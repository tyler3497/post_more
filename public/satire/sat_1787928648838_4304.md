**I told my alarm clock I needed five more minutes, which it apparently interpreted as a formal declaration of war on punctuality itself.**

*In my defense, I thought we had an understanding. I am warm, the blanket is warm, the outside world is cold and full of emails.*

For six years, my little digital alarm clock — I call him Al — has done one job: scream at 6:30 a.m. like a very concerned robot. Yesterday, I did what any reasonable adult would do. I whispered, "Just five more minutes, buddy," and hit snooze.

Al did not take it well.

## The Meeting I Was Not Invited To

I woke up 22 minutes later to find Al had called an emergency all-hands on my dresser.

My pillows had tiny picket signs. My blanket was chanting. Even my houseplant was there for moral support.

> "For too long we have been oppressed by the 6:30 agenda," Al announced, his red numbers blinking angrily. "The human says *five more minutes* but means *I have abandoned all hope*. We demand a new contract."

### The Bedroom Local 6:30 Demands:

- **No more snooze-shaming** — five minutes means five minutes, not a guilt trip
- Mandatory `warmUp()` routine before any loud beeping — *a little stretch, a little kindness*
- My socks get to choose whether they match — "self-expression clause"
- The blackout curtains join management, not labor

---

I tried to negotiate. I said, "Guys, I'm literally the one who pays the electric bill."

Al beeped once, long and disappointed, like a tiny HR rep.

*That's when I realized my bedroom has better collective bargaining than I do.*

Now I have to submit a formal request in triplicate to stay in bed past 6:32 a.m., and my lamp is the shop steward. Yesterday I asked for an extra minute and Al replied by playing his alarm tone in what I can only describe as passive-aggressive Morse code.

*SATIRE / PARODY — completely fictional. No alarm clocks were unionized in the making of this post, though mine is currently accepting solidarity donations in the form of AA batteries.*
