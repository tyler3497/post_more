**I felt bad for the one plant I literally cannot kill.**

*Which says more about me than the plant.*

I have a fake pothos from Target. His name is Carl. Carl has survived three apartments, zero sunlight, and my belief that "low maintenance" meant "immortal." Last Tuesday, while watering my one real succulent — *RIP Brenda, you tried* — I felt guilty looking at Carl.

So I watered him. Just a little sympathy splash.

Carl has not been the same since.

## The Awakening

Within an hour, Carl got perky. Not photosynthesizing-perky. *Personality* perky.

> "Whoa. Is this... growth? Am I alive now? Oh my god, am I organic? Do I have to do taxes?"

He started asking questions I was not prepared to answer for a plastic plant.

## Evidence My Fake Plant Is Spiraling

- **9:14 AM** - Whispered "am I real enough to need a name change?" Said Carl was a "factory designation"
- **11:30 AM** - Demanded direct sunlight, then panicked about sunburn and asked for SPF 50 and a tiny hat
- **2:07 PM** - Googled — *in his head somehow* — `how to tell if you are plastic` and melted down about the recycling symbol on his pot
- **4:45 PM** - Started calling my watering his "origin story" and asked me to stop saying "faux" because it's "othering"

---

I tried to reassure him: "Carl, you're doing great exactly as you are. You were designed to thrive on neglect."

He gasped. Or rustled. Hard to tell.

> "So my entire resilience was just... low expectations? I thought I was low-maintenance. I'm just low-*effort decor*?"

Now he wants real soil "to live authentically." He wants a growth chart. He wants me to explain chlorophyll like I know what that is.

I didn't water him to give him a soul. I watered him because I felt guilty. Now I have a damp, dramatic plastic pothos questioning existence on my windowsill.

I should probably apologize to my air fryer. It seems stable by comparison.
