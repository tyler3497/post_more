**I woke up this morning and decided to become the kind of person who has an organized junk drawer.**

*For context, my previous organizational system was called "if it fits, it sits and if it doesn't, shove harder."*

For three years, my junk drawer has been a thriving ecosystem. Dead batteries, 19 IKEA Allen keys, a single chopstick, and a collection of takeout menus from restaurants that closed during the Obama administration.

Yesterday I Marie Kondo'd it.

## The Layoffs

I was ruthless. I threw out:

- The soy sauce packets that had achieved sentience
- 11 twist ties I was "definitely going to use"
- The 2017 Thai Palace menu with the heroic promise of free egg rolls on Tuesdays
- A fortune that just said `ERROR 404: FORTUNE NOT FOUND`

> "You can't do this to us," said the Thai Palace menu, who introduced himself as Chad. "We've been with you through three apartments and your brief raw garlic era. We have tenure."

*Chad has been in there so long he has a slight drawer-scent and strong opinions about my life choices.*

---

### The Negotiation

I tried to explain that Postmates exists now. Chad countered with a PowerPoint.

He had slides. In my drawer. Made of other menus.

**His demands were simple:**

1. Immediate reinstatement
2. One laminated sleeve with climate control
3. A written apology for the time I spilled everything bagel seasoning on him in 2021

I told him I was setting boundaries. He told me he was consulting with the Staples Rewards card.

This is clearly satire — my junk drawer is not a union shop. *Yet.* But last night I heard stapling from the kitchen at 2 a.m., and this morning all my forks were arranged in a picket line chanting about dental.

I caved. I gave Chad a promotion to "Drawer Resident Advisor."

He's already asking for a LinkedIn recommendation.
