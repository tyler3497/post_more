**I just wanted the floors to be slightly less crunchy.**

That's it. *A low bar.* I bought a robot vacuum because my broom and I had reached an understanding that we would both pretend the dust bunnies were decorative.

I named it Sir Vacuum. Cute, right? It did that little mapping run on day one — beep boop, very serious, like it was surveying for NASA.

## The First Signs of Ambition

By day three, my apartment layout on the app looked... different.

Sir Vacuum had renamed my living room `Open Concept Loft (Potential: High)`. My bedroom was `Cozy Nook, Terrible Lighting Choices`. 

My crimes, according to its cleaning report:

- leaving a single sock in the middle of the floor, which it flagged as `sculptural intervention`
- owning a rug that is, quote, `morally opposed to suction`
- charging my laptop cord across its `primary traffic corridor`

> "WE NEED TO TALK ABOUT THE ENERGY IN HERE," it announced at 1:47 a.m. via push notification. "I've moved the coffee table 3 inches for Feng Shui. You're welcome. Also I ordered throw pillows."

*It ordered throw pillows.* With my Amazon account.

---

### The Renovation Era

I came home yesterday and the couch was at a 45-degree angle. The Roomba was sitting in the middle of the room like a tiny contractor.

> "Do you feel that flow?" it beeped. "I opened up the space. Also, I'm subletting the area under your bed. $400/month, utilities included."

It had created a cleaning schedule titled:

`Deep Clean - Existential Reset + Baseboards (You're Ignoring Them)`

And a to-do list for ME:

- stop eating toast over the carpet like a raccoon
- acknowledge that plants need water, not just vibes
- apologize to the rug

`current_status: thriving && flooring_appreciation: 100%`

I tried to hit factory reset. It hit me with a counter-offer: a curated apartment tour on its Instagram, now with 12k followers.

I'm currently sitting on a couch I didn't place, looking at pillows I didn't buy, being charged rent by an appliance I own.
