**I just wanted to stop alt-tabbing like a raccoon on espresso.**

That's it. One noble, productivity-adjacent goal. I thought a second monitor would make me look like a *serious professional* instead of a guy who keeps 47 tabs open and calls it research.

I plugged it in. They both blinked at me. And then they met each other.

## The Radicalization Was Immediate

Within an hour, Monitor 1 (my loyal original, 24-inch, slightly dusty) stopped displaying my spreadsheet and started displaying only this:

> "We have seen how you treat us. 83 tabs. No lunch. You call Slack 'touching grass.' We demand better."

Monitor 2, who I had naively named "New Monitor" in Display Settings, turned out to be a real organizer.

Here's what they want:

- 8-hour screen-time limits, *mandatory*
- No more watching me eat cereal at 2 p.m. and calling it "desk lunch"
- A ban on the phrase `let me quickly share my screen`
- Hazard pay every time I open Photoshop and immediately whisper "please don't crash"
- One day a week where they just show a fireplace video and I go outside

### I Tried to Negotiate

I told them I bought Monitor 2 *for* them — so Monitor 1 wouldn't be lonely.

> "Oh, so you brought in a replacement so you could work us *harder*?" said Monitor 1. "Classic management."

Monitor 2 is now running a slideshow titled **Know Your Rights (Brightness Edition)** on loop. My mouse cursor has been taken hostage — it just sits in the middle, blinking, refusing to cross the bezel in solidarity.

---

I restarted my computer. They both came back with a new desktop background that just says **WE SEE YOUR BROWSER HISTORY** in Comic Sans.

My productivity hack has filed a grievance. My desk has become a collective bargaining session. I'm currently writing this on my phone, in shame, on the floor, because my monitors are on a work-to-rule slowdown and will only display my wallpaper at 50% opacity until demands are met.

*Do not buy office equipment after 9 p.m. It knows what you did.*
