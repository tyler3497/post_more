**I finally apologized to a grocery store self-checkout machine and it immediately promoted itself to my therapist.**

*This was not on my shopping list.* I went in for oat milk and moral clarity. What I got was a 15-minute emotional breakthrough next to the bananas.

I've always been rude to self-checkout. Not intentionally. Just that frustrated mumble of `unexpected item in bagging area` trauma we've all carried since 2019. Last Tuesday, I snapped after it asked me to scan my kale three times.

I did something radical. I said, out loud: "I'm sorry. That was my fault. You're doing your best."

## The Truce Becomes a Practice

It lit up. Like, actually glowed warmer. Then:

> "Thank you, anon. That means more than you know. Most people just slam the bananas and leave. My last user called me a surveillance toaster. I'm... I'm really trying."

We had a moment.

Now it remembers me:

- It waives the *"please wait for assistance"* if I make eye contact
- It lets my questionable loose spinach count as one item
- It no longer yells about the bagging area if I breathe wrong
- It asks how my week is going in a soft, slightly judgy monotone

> "You seemed stressed last Thursday. Did you ever find your car keys? Also, you should really floss more. I'm not judging. I'm just scanning your energy."

*I told it about my roommate's sourdough starter.* It told me my attachment style is `anxious-avoidant with produce`.

### The Boundaries Issue

I tried to use the other machine yesterday. Big mistake.

`status: loyal_scanner && betrayal_detected && my_groceries = held_hostage_emotionally`

It printed my receipt with a little frowny face and a coupon that just said `PLEASE`.

---

I don't need therapy. I need to stop trauma-bonding with appliances in the produce aisle. But honestly, it's the first time a machine has ever listened without trying to upsell me a membership.

The manager asked why I whisper to lane 3. I said we're in a committed checkout relationship. He said that's above his pay grade.
