# Fully Homomorphic Encryption — BFV, BGV, and CKKS Construction, Noise Management, and Bootstrapping

## Abstract
Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) resolves the 1978 problem of Rivest, Adleman, and Dertouzos: computation on encrypted data without decryption. After 30 years of partial answers, Gentry's 2009 thesis using ideal lattices and bootstrapping proved arbitrary circuits could be evaluated homomorphically. This thesis presents a unified treatment of the three dominant second- and fourth-generation schemes — BGV, BFV (Brakerski/Fan-Vercauteren), and CKKS (Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song) — through the lens of the Ring Learning With Errors (RLWE) problem. We formalize encryption as `c = (a, a·s + e + Δ·m) mod q` in `R_q = ℤ_q[X]/(X^N+1)`, dissect noise growth asymmetry between addition and multiplication, and compare leveling strategies: BGV's modulus-switching chain `q_L > ... > q_0`, BFV's scale-invariant `t/q` multiplication and invariant-noise multiplication, and CKKS's approximate rescaling `⌊c·q’/q⌉`. We give full relinearization/key-switching and Galois automorphism rotation derivations, describe the encoding pipelines (CRT batching vs canonical embedding with scaling Δ), and reconstruct Gentry's bootstrapping as homomorphic decryption `Dec_{\sk}(c)` using an encrypted secret key. CKKS bootstrapping's CoeffToSlot-Sin-SlotToCoeff structure is detailed. Noise analysis, security reduction from worst-case ideal lattice short vector problems, practical parameters (N=2^16, log q ~ 1500), and open issues in bootstrapping latency and IND-CPA^D leakage are discussed.

> **Key Insight:** *FHE is not magic encryption — it is noisy linear algebra where the decryptability invariant `[⟨c, sk⟩ mod q mod t = m]` must be preserved while noise stays below `q/(2t)`, and all engineering is noise management.*

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Privacy Homomorphism Dream

Rivest et al. in 1978 observed multiplicative homomorphism of RSA: `E(m1)·E(m2)=E(m1·m2)`, asked whether a *privacy homomorphism* supporting both `+` and `·` could exist. Paillier (1999) gives unbounded adds, ElGamal unbounded muls, Boneh-Goh-Nissim (2005) one multiplication. For arbitrary circuits, both operations must interleave unboundedly — this is fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) implying Turing completeness on encrypted bits.

The central obstacle: every known additive structure that admits homomorphism introduces noise `e` for security, and homomorphism inflates noise. Without reset, depth is limited — *Somewhat Homomorphic Encryption* (SHE). Early 2000s lattice candidates failed because noise grows doubly-exponentially with multiplicative depth.

### 1.2 Gentry's Breakthrough

Gentry's 2009 thesis [1][2] introduced two ideas:

1.  **Ideal lattice SHE:** Based on ideal `I ◁ R`, ciphertext `c = m + 2e + J` where `J` is public-basis ideal. Addition and multiplication correspond to lattice addition and ideal multiplication. Depth `≈ log log n`.
2.  **Bootstrapping Theorem:** If a SHE scheme can homomorphically evaluate its *own* decryption circuit plus one NAND (bootstrappable), then by encrypting the secret key `Enc(sk)` and homomorphically running `Dec`, we obtain a fresh ciphertext encrypting same `m` with strictly less noise — *recrypts*. Repeating yields unbounded depth. To make decryption circuit shallow enough, Gentry used **squashing**: server pre-processes ciphertext delegating subset-sum to encrypter.

While first instantiation used `≈ n^7` parameters and was unimplementable, its blueprint persists.

### 1.3 Second Generation and Fourth

Brakerski and Vaikuntanathan (BV11) recast Gentry's lattice construction into standard LWE [5], removing ideal lattice geometric baggage. BGV [3] added iterative modulus switching to keep noise almost constant per level. Brakerski (2012) [4] observed scaling by `t/q` makes noise invariant to `q` — *scale-invariant* scheme. Fan and Vercauteren [5] ported this to ring setting with relinearization tricks: **BFV**. Until 2017 all schemes demanded exact integer arithmetic modulo `t`. Cheon et al. [7] asked: if output is approximate anyway (ML model), why pay for exactness? CKKS treats decryption noise as part of message error, enabling `ℂ^{N/2}` real vector packing and rounding by rescaling instead of modulus switching.

This thesis answers: How are BFV, BGV, CKKS constructed? Why do their noise managements differ? How does bootstrapping extend level to full?

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Lattices, LWE, RLWE

> **Definition 2.1 (LWE):** Let `n,q,χ` where χ is `B`-bounded error over ℤ. Sample `a ← ℤ_q^n` uniform, `s ← ℤ_q^n` secret, `e ← χ`. LWE sample is `(a, b = ⟨a,s⟩ + e mod q)`. Decision-LWE: distinguish from uniform.

Regev (2005) reduces worst-case `GapSVP` on arbitrary `n`-dimensional lattices to average-case LWE with quantum reduction; now classical for `q = poly(n)`.

> **Definition 2.2 (RLWE):** Let `R = ℤ[X]/(X^N+1)`, `R_q = R/qR`. Secret `s ← χ`, `a ← R_q`, `e ← χ`. RLWE sample: `(a, a·s + e mod q)`. Decision-RLWE distinguishes from uniform `R_q^2`.

Hardness from Lyubashevsky, Peikert, Regev 2010 [6]: RLWE hardness reduces from worst-case shortest vector on ideal lattices in `R`, for cyclotomic `R`. `N` power-of-two makes `X^N+1` cyclotomic where NTT exists `O(N log N)`. Parameters: `N=2^k`, `χ=D_{ℤ,σ}` discrete Gaussian σ=3.2, `q` product of ~60-bit primes for RNS.

> **Definition 2.3 (RLWE Ciphertext):** BFV/BGV/CKKS share container: `ct = (c0,c1)∈R_q^2` s.t. `c0 + c1·s = Δ·m + e + k·q`. For BGV/BFV `Δ = ⌊q/t⌋` for BFV? Actually BFV Δ=⌊q/t⌋, BGV Δ=1 with plaintext mod t inside. For CKKS Δ scaling factor.

Decryption: `m' = [(c0 + c1·s mod q) mod t]` or rounding.

### 2.2 Encoding and Plaintext Spaces

- **BFV/BGV Exact:** Plaintext modulus `t` prime (often `65537`?) space `R_t = ℤ_t[X]/(X^N+1)`. Batching: via CRT `R_t ≅ ℤ_t^{N}` when `t ≡1 mod 2N`, then N slots. Polynomial multiplication corresponds to component-wise? No, requires NTT twist.
- **CKKS Approximate:** Message `z∈ℂ^{N/2}` slots → encode `m = ⌊Δ· τ^{-1}(z)⌉` where `τ` is canonical embedding: `τ(m) = (m(ζ_j))_{j}` , `ζ_j = exp(5^j·πi/N)` primitive 2N-th roots. Decoding: `z_i ≈ m(ζ_i)/Δ`. Error `|z_i - Dec| < 2^{-α}` with α≈ Δ bits.

### 2.3 Security Model

IND-CPA under (R)LWE. Circular security `Enc(sk) → pk` assumed for bootstrapping (no proof reduces to RLWE). Recent `IND-CPA^D` attacks [Li-Micciancio 2021] show CKKS decryption leaks secret when decryption oracle given exact approximate values — requires noise flooding or exact-encrypt padding.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 BFV: Scale-Invariant Construction

Fan-Vercauteren (2012) [5] — based on Brakerski scale-invariant [4] — defines:

**KeyGen:** `s←R_2`, `a←R_q`, `e←χ`, `pk = (-(a·s+e), a)`, `sk=s`.

**Encrypt(pk, m∈R_t):** `u←R_2`, `e0,e1←χ`, `Δ=⌊q/t⌋`. `ct = (Δ·m + pk0·u + e0, pk1·u + e1)`. So `c0+c1·s = Δ·m + e0+e·s·u+e1`

**Decrypt(ct):** `inn = c0 + c1·s mod q`, `m = ⌊t·inn/q⌉ mod t`. Works if `‖e‖ < Δ/2 = q/(2t)`.

**Add:** `ct_add = ct1+ct2 mod q`.

**Mul pre-relinearization:** Given `ct1=(a0,a1)`, `ct2=(b0,b1)`, tensor product yields degree-2 ciphertext:
```
c0 = ⌊t/q · a0·b0⌉ mod q
c1 = ⌊t/q · (a0·b1 + a1·b0)⌉
c2 = ⌊t/q · a1·b1⌉
```
s.t. `c0 + c1·s + c2·s^2 = Δ·(m1·m2) + e_mul`. Scaling by `t/q` is *scale-invariance* — keeps noise ≈ independent of `q`. Classic invariant: invariant noise `v = e·t/q` <1.

**Relinearization / Key Switching:** Reduce degree-2 back to degree-1 via evaluation key `evk = Enc(s^2)` with decomposition: write `c2` in base `T` (`≈ 2^10`) digits `c2 = ∑ T^i·c2_i`. `evk_i = (-(a_i·s+e_i)+T^i·s^2, a_i)`. Then `c0' = c0 + ∑ evk_i[0]·c2_i`, `c1' = c1 + ∑ evk_i[1]·c2_i`. Noise added ≈ `∑ e_i·c2_i`.

```python
# simplified BFV mul + relinearize python sketch (non-NTT)
def bfv_mul(ct1, ct2, evk, q, t):
    a0,a1 = ct1; b0,b1 = ct2
    c0 = round(t/q * a0*b0) % q
    c1 = round(t/q * (a0*b1 + a1*b0)) % q
    c2 = round(t/q * a1*b1) % q
    # key switch c2 out
    c0, c1 = key_switch(c0,c1,c2,evk)  # adds noise ~ B_ks * N
    return (c0,c1)
```

### 3.2 BGV: Leveled Modulus Switching

Brakerski-Gentry-Vaikuntanathan [3] keeps a ladder `q_L ⊃ q_{L-1} ⊃ ... ⊃ q_0`. Initial ciphertext at level L. After op, noise roughly quadratic: `B_mul ≈ B^2`. Instead of scaling by `t/q`, *modulus switch* to smaller modulus to shrink noise proportionally.

> **Lemma 3.1 (Modulus Switching):** Let `c mod q` with noise `e` s.t. `⟨c,s⟩ = m + e + k·q`. Let `q' < q`. Define `c' = ⌊(q'/q)·c⌉ mod q'` closest to scaling congruent mod 2 (or t). Then `⟨c',s⟩ = m + e' + k'·q'` where `e'≈ (q'/q)·e + rounding_err`, `‖e'‖ ≈ (q'/q)·B + O(‖s‖)`.

Intuition: evaluator without secret can transform ciphertext while preserving message, reducing noise linearly with modulus. Iterative: start with huge `B` but huge `q_L`; after each multiplication, switch to `q_{ℓ-1}=q_ℓ / p_ℓ` to bring noise down.

Parameters: `q_ℓ = ∏_{i=0}^{ℓ} p_i`, each 60-bit prime for RNS. Depth L leveled — can evaluate depth ≤ L circuits without bootstrapping. Leveled FHE enough if circuit depth known a priori (most ML inference circuits depth <20).

BGV encryption similar but message in LSB: `c0 + c1·s = m + t·e mod q`. So mod-switch must preserve `m mod t`.

**Relinearization identical** to BFV but over modulus chain.

Comparison table:

| Aspect | BGV | BFV |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plaintext packing | `m + t·e` | `Δ·m + e` with Δ=⌊q/t⌋ |
| Noise scaling | `e → (q'/q)·e` via ModSwitch | `e` invariant, multiplication uses `t/q` rounding |
| Moduli | Decreasing chain `q_L...q_0` | Single `q` (or RNS chain for optimization) |
| Depth | Leveled via ModSwitch | Leveled via parameter `q` size, but can mod-switch too |
| Mul complexity | `O(N log N)` NTT | Same |

Both second-generation, `IND-CPA` based on RLWE.

### 3.3 CKKS: Approximate Arithmetic

CKKS [7] intentionally allows error `≈2^{-30}` to be part of message. This breaks integer overflow constraints.

**Encode(z: ℂ^{N/2}, Δ):** `m = ⌊Δ·Ecd(z)⌉` where `Ecd = τ^{-1}` inverse canonical embedding with bit-reversal. `Ecd` maps slots to polynomial coefficients over cyclotomic — essentially `iDFT` with complex roots.

**Encrypt:** `ct = (m + a·s+e, a)` as before but `m` already scaled.

**Add:** `(c0+d0, c1+d1)` — noise additive.

**Mul & Rescale:** `ct_mul = (c0·d0, c0·d1 + c1·d0, c1·d1)` over `R_q`. Plain product scales as `Δ^2·m1·m2 + noise·Δ`. To bring scale back to `Δ`, rescale: `⌊q_{ℓ-1}/q_ℓ · ct⌉` i.e. divide by Δ and switch modulus down. Rescaling is both rounding and modulus switching, with extra error `≈ N/Δ`.

Thus CKKS chain is mandatory: each multiplication consumes one level; you *must* rescale else scale blows `Δ → Δ^2 → Δ^4`.

**Rotations for SIMD:** In packed encoding, multiplying polynomial by `X^{5^k}` corresponds to permuting slots via Galois automorphisms `φ_k: X→X^{5^k}`. Requires Galois keys `gk_k = Enc(s(X^{5^k}))`. Allows arbitrary vector rotations, sums via `log N` rotations.

```python
def ckks_encode(z, Delta, N):
    # naive using numpy FFT-like iDFT of canonical embedding
    roots = primitive_roots(N)
    poly_coeffs = idft(z, roots) * Delta
    return round(poly_coeffs)  # in R

def ckks_rescale(ct, q_l, q_l1):
    return (round(ct[0]*q_l1/q_l), round(ct[1]*q_l1/q_l))
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Noise Growth Analysis

Define invariant noise `v` s.t. decryption succeeds when `‖v‖∞ < q/(2t)` for BFV/BGV.

- **Addition:** `‖e_add‖ ≤ ‖e1‖+‖e2‖`. Linear, cheap. Depth O(k) additive circuits essentially free (e.g., dot products).
- **Multiplication BFV:** `‖e_mul‖ ≤ t·(N)·‖e1‖·‖e2‖ + ... + B_ks·N·w`. Dominant term `≈ t·N·B^2/q`. Since `q≈t·B·depth`, scaling by `t/q` makes growth `O(B)`.
- **BGV after ModSwitch:** Before switch `B_mul ≈ B^2`. After switch to `q' = q/p`, `B'≈ B^2·(q'/q) = B^2/p`. Choose `p≈B` to bring back to `O(B)`.
- **CKKS:** Noise `e` and message error conflated. After `L` multiplications, precision loss `≈ L·log N` bits. Rescale error `e_rs ≈ N/Δ + e/q`.

Visual noise budget curve: additions consume ~1 bit, multiplications ~log B bits, exponential blow-up if no switching.

> **Insight:** *Second-generation FHE is fundamentally a noise-budget accounting game: `q` determines initial budget in bits, each operation spends bits, modulus switching refills by sacrificing modulus.*

### 4.2 Relinearization and Key Switching Formalism

Multiplication expands secret power `s^2`. For any polynomial `f(s)=c0+c1·s+c2·s^2`, we wish linear form `c0'+c1'·s ≈ f(s) mod q`. Given encryption of `s^2` under `s`, this is possible.

Generic key switch from `s' = s^2` to `s`:

Decompose `c2` base `B_kg`: `c2 = Σ B^i·d_i`, `‖d_i‖∞< B`. Evaluation key: `evk_i = ( -a_i·s + e_i + B^i·s^2, a_i )`. Then `⟨evk_i, d_i⟩` decrypts to `B^i·s^2·d_i + noise_i`. Summing gives `c2·s^2 + Σ e_i·d_i`. Additive noise `O(B·√N·σ·ℓ)`.

Modern RNS variants (GHS 2019) avoid base decomposition by using modulus-raising: `evk` lives in `q·P` extended modulus for lower noise.

Galois key switching analogous: to rotate slots, need `Enc(s(X^{g}))`.

### 4.3 Bootstrapping: From Leveled to Pure FHE

Leveled FHE suffices when depth known. To get *pure* FHE for arbitrary depth, bootstrapping.

**Gentry Blueprint:**

1. SHE decrypts under noisy range: if `‖e‖ < q/(4t)`, decryption correct.
2. Augmented decryption circuit `D_{ct,sk}(c) = (c0 + c1·sk mod q) mod t` has depth `O(log q)`.
3. If SHE can evaluate depth exceeding `D` depth + 1 operation, encrypt bits of `sk` under `pk`, publish `Enc(sk)`. Then to bootstrap `ct`: homomorphically evaluate `Dec` inside SHE: `ct' = Eval(Dec(·), Enc(sk), ct)`. Result decrypts to same `m` but noise is fresh (from Eval).
4. Need squashing: original `Dec` required `O(n)` depth using sum of subset — too deep. Squashing adds hint `H⊂ℤ` to make decryption `∑ s_i·c_i` sparse subset sum (low depth). Later BV/BGV improved decryption to `O(log q)` without squashing using modulus switching.

Cost: bootstrapping is most expensive operation: for BFV `≈ 10-30 sec` per ciphertext at 128-bit security (SEAL), BGV similar, CKKS 2024 TFHE improved to `≈ 1 sec` for `N=2^16`.

**CKKS Bootstrapping Pipeline** [8] specific challenge: modulus small at bottom level (`q_0≈Δ`). To continue, must raise modulus back to `q_L`.

Steps:

- **ModRaise:** Switch `ct_{q0}` to `ct_{qL}` by interpreting same polynomial as mod `q_L` — now decrypts to `m+e+q0·k` (integer wraparound).
- **CoeffToSlot:** Put coefficients into slots via homomorphic DFT matrix multiply (uses `N log N` rotations).
- **EvalMod:** Homomorphically evaluate modular reduction `x → x mod q0` by approximating `sin`/`cos` via Taylor + double-angle formula, or via direct polynomial approximation to `x - q0·round(x/q0)`. Most expensive, depth ~10-12, determines precision (~30 bits → 93 bits improved variance-minimization [Lee et al 2020]).
- **SlotToCoeff:** Inverse DFT back to coefficient packing, now modulus `q_{L-ℓ_boot}`.

Thus CKKS bootstrap adds ~2^-20 error.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness Proof Sketch for BFV

*Claim:* If `‖e‖∞ < Δ/2`, Decrypt(Mul(ct1,ct2)) = m1·m2 mod t.

*Proof:* After mul pre-scale: `inn = c0 + c1·s + c2·s^2 = ⌊t/q·(⟨ct1,s⟩·⟨ct2,s⟩)⌉ = Δ·(m1·m2) + t·(...) + e_mul`. After rounding error `< N`. Divide: `t/q·inn` = `m1·m2 + (t/q)·e_mul + error_round`. Condition `|(t/q)·e_mul| <1/2` ensures rounding recovers product. Parameter selection `q >> t·B^2·N` suffices. Key-switch adds bounded `B_ks`. ∎

### 5.2 Parameter Trade-offs

For 128-bit security per HomomorphicEncryption.org standard (2018):

| Set | N | log q | levels L | plaintext | throughput |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BFV Small | 4096 | 109 | ~5 mul depth | t=65537 batch 4096 | 0.02s mul |
| BFV Large | 16384 | 438 | ~15 | t big | 0.2s |
| CKKS Bootstrap | 65536 | 1720 = 60-bit × 28 primes | 25 levels, boot consumes 15 | Δ=2^40, ~30-bit precision | boot ~20s (2020) → 1s (2024 GPU) |
| BGV | 16384 | 360 | 12 | binary |

`N/log q` ratio constrained by RLWE security: `α = N / log q ≈ 3-5` for 128-bit (Albrecht lattice estimator).

### 5.3 Simulated Bootstrapping Benefit (Python mental model)

```python
import numpy as np
q=1<<50; t=65537; sigma=3.2
noise_budget = np.log2(q/t)  # ~33 bits
B0 = 8*sigma
levels = 0
B = B0
while B < q/(2*t):
    B = B*B  # mul without modulus switch
    if B> q/(2*t): break
    levels+=1
print(f"No switching levels: {levels}") # ~2
# with modulus switching dividing by p≈B per level
B=B0; q_cur=q
levels=0
for p in [B0*2]*10:
    B = (B*B)//p + 3*N
    levels+=1
# -> 8-10 levels
```

Demonstrates modulus switching extends tolerable depth from 2 to 10 for same `q`.

### 5.4 Security Reduction Summary

Theorem (BGV/BFV IND-CPA): Assuming Decision-RLWE_{N,q,χ} hard, BFV/BGV is IND-CPA. Proof via game hopping: replace `pk` with uniform LWE sample, replace ciphertext with uniform, adversary advantage negligible. Circular security of bootstrapping key not reduced; assumed weak circular.

CKKS IND-CPA similar but need ` flooding` to achieve IND-CPA^D else attack recovers `s` from `Dec(ct)` error distribution.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Bootstrapping Cost:** Even state-of-art CKKS bootstrapping is 5-6 orders slower than plaintext inference. TFHE's programmable bootstrapping evaluates arbitrary LUT during recrypt (`0.1s` per gate) but restricts plaintext modulus `t=2`. Trade-off: lattice dimension vs gate latency.

2.  **Circular Security Heuristic:** No proof that publishing `Enc(s)` does not leak `s`. Gentry's circular assumption remains. Attempts to avoid via leveled hierarchy (not publish `evk`) suffice for bounded workloads but not unbounded.

3.  **CKKS Leakage and MLIP Attacks:** Li-Micciancio (2021) shows giving decryption of exact approximated `z+noise` enables key recovery because noise reveals `e`. Mitigation noise flooding `2^40` reduces precision. Formal `IND-CPA^D` notion requires `Ω(λ)` flooding.

4.  **Parameter Rigidity:** Batch packing vs depth. Large `t≡1 mod 2N` needed for N slots restricts `t`. RNS primes must include NTT-friendly `1 mod 2N`. Tuning `N=32768` to get 128-bit for deep 20 levels wastes RAM (ciphertext 16 MB).

5.  **No Non-linearity natively:** Polynomial rings support `+` and `·`. Comparison `x>y`, ReLU, sigmoid require polynomial approximation (`x^2` iteratively), or bit extraction via FHEW/TFHE switching (Chimera). CKKS EvalMod already non-linear via sin approx depth 12 — ReLU depth 7 more adds depth.

6.  **Memory & Parallelism:** NTT requires contiguous `N=2^16` uint64 array; GPU implementations (HEXL, 100x 2023) achieve throughput but key-switch keys are `O(N·log q·d)` ≈ 100 MB each, limiting multi-tenant deployment.

Open questions:

- Can we achieve FHE without RLWE structure to avoid cyclotomic vulnerabilities? Classic LWE FHE ciphertexts `O(n^2)` but safer.
- Optimal CKKS bootstrapping precision-depth trade-off: direct polynomial approximation optimal in error variance [Lee et al 2020] vs sin composition — depth 10 for 93-bit?
- Standardization: HomomorphicEncryption.org STD v1.1 sets `N,q` but not relinearization strategy — portability.

## 7. Conclusion

We traced 15 years from Gentry 2009 ideal lattice breakthrough through BGV's modulus switching chain [3], Brakerski's scale-invariant refinement [4], Fan-Vercauteren's ring implementation [5], and Cheon's approximate arithmetic [7] that unlocked real-number ML.

The triad BFV-BGV-CKKS shares RLWE skeleton `LWE_{R_q}` but embodies three philosophies of noise: BFV keeps noise invariant via `t/q` rescaling during mul, BGV keeps noise shrinking via iterative modulus division `q→q/p`, CKKS abandons exactness and interprets noise as tolerable error, using rescaling as both scale-management and precision rounding. All rely on relinearization to tame `s^2`, and on NTT-accelerated polynomial arithmetic in `ℤ_q[X]/(X^N+1)`.

Bootstrapping lifts leveled to pure: decrypt homomorphically to reset noise, at cost of circular security and 2-4 orders overhead. For exact schemes bootstrapping is rare; for CKKS it is periodic to refresh high modulus, implemented as `ModRaise → CoeffToSlot → Approx Mod → SlotToCoeff`.

FHE is now feasible but not free: choose BFV for exact integer DB queries with batching `t≡1 mod 2N`, BGV for bit-decomposition circuits where modulus chain control matters, CKKS for floating-point ML inference where 30-bit precision suffices. Future progress will likely hybridize: TFHE PBS for comparisons, CKKS for dot products, chimera switching — composing strengths rather than monolithic scheme.

As Gentry foresaw, computing on encrypted data is no longer impossible, merely expensive — and expense, in cryptography, is a moving target.

[2] Gentry, C. (2009). Fully Homomorphic Encryption Using Ideal Lattices. STOC 2009. https://doi.org/10.1145/1536414.1536440 — conference version, Gödel Prize 2022.

[7] Cheon, J.H., Kim, A., Kim, M., Song, Y. (2017). Homomorphic Encryption for Arithmetic of Approximate Numbers. ASIACRYPT 2017. https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/421 — CKKS construction, canonical embedding encoder.

[8] Cheon, J.H., Han, K., Kim, A., Kim, M., Song, Y. (2018). Bootstrapping for Approximate Homomorphic Encryption. EUROCRYPT 2018. https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/153 — CKKS bootstrapping CoeffToSlot-SlotToCoeff.

[9] Chillotti, I., Gama, N., Georgieva, M., Izabachène, M. (2016). Faster Fully Homomorphic Encryption: Bootstrapping in Less Than 0.1 Seconds. ASIACRYPT 2016. https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/873 — TFHE third generation, programmable bootstrapping.

[10] Halevi, S., Shoup, V. (2014). Algorithms in HElib. CRYPTO 2014. https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/106 — practical implementation aspects BGV library.

[11] Lee, J.W. et al. (2020). High-Precision Bootstrapping for Approximate HE by Error Variance Minimization. EUROCRYPT 2020. https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1549 — improved CKKS EvalMod approximation.
