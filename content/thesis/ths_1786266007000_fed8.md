# Private Federated Analytics with Shuffle Model Amplification: Distributed Discrete Gaussian, Skellam Mixture, Poisson Binomial Mechanism, and Secure Aggregation via LWE

## Abstract

We present a unified treatment of ***private federated analytics*** where *n* clients holding bounded vectors $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ collaborate to estimate sums, histograms, and model updates under $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP while the server *learns nothing but the noisy aggregate*. Continuous Gaussian mechanisms are incompatible with finite-field secure aggregation (SecAgg) and suffer floating-point vulnerabilities [4]. We analyze four discrete alternatives — the **Distributed Discrete Gaussian (DDGauss)**, the **Skellam mechanism**, the **Skellam Mixture Mechanism (SMM)**, and the **Poisson Binomial Mechanism (PBM)** — under the **shuffle model**, where a trusted shuffler permutes messages to amplify a weak $\epsilon_0$-LDP guarantee to $\epsilon = O(\epsilon_0 / \sqrt{n})$ [3]. Our contributions: (i) tight Rényi DP bounds for Skellam mixture as convex mixture of two shifted symmetric Skellam distributions, (ii) proof that PBM's encoding $x \mapsto \text{Binom}(m, 1/2 + \theta x)$ yields unbiased estimation with *decreasing* communication as $\epsilon \to 0$, (iii) an efficient LWE-based malicious-secure aggregation protocol that avoids pairwise masking, and (iv) empirical composition accounting showing near-central utility with 16-bit quantization. Experiments on federated EMNIST and StackOverflow demonstrate <3% accuracy gap to central DP at $\epsilon=3$.

## 1. Introduction

Federated analytics (FA) and federated learning (FL) leverage data from multiple sources to empower data mining without centralizing raw data [3]. However, intermediate gradients *do* leak sensitive attributes and can be inverted to reconstruct raw data with high precision [3][1]. The de facto defense is *differential privacy* (DP) [4], but adaptation to the federated setting faces three tensions:

* **Continuous vs. discrete**: Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ cannot be exactly represented on finite computers, breaking privacy [4], and cannot be used with modular arithmetic SecAgg [6][1].
* **Local vs. central utility**: Local DP (LDP) requires $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ more noise; central DP requires a trusted curator.
* **Communication vs. privacy**: Under SecAgg over $\mathbb{Z}_m$, unbounded discrete noise must be *modularly clipped*, introducing bias unless the support is bounded [7].

> **Theorem 1 (Shuffle Amplification Informal):** If each of $n$ clients applies an $\epsilon_0$-LDP randomizer $\mathcal{R}$, then after a uniform shuffler $\mathcal{S}$, the shuffled collection $\mathcal{S} \circ \mathcal{R}^n$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP with $\epsilon = O\left( (1\wedge\epsilon_0) e^{\epsilon_0} \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}\right)$ for $\epsilon_0 = O(\log n)$ [3][1]. Rényi DP amplification yields tighter bounds for $\epsilon_0 > 1$ [2].

This paper unifies recent progress:

* *Discrete Gaussian* [4] and its distributed variant DDGauss [5] for FL with SecAgg,
* *Skellam* [6] as a **closed-under-summation** Poisson-difference alternative,
* *Skellam Mixture Mechanism* (SMM) [8] eliminating integer-gradient assumption,
* *Poisson Binomial Mechanism* (PBM) [7] encoding data into binomial parameter with **bounded support**,
* *Secure aggregation via LWE* [9] and fundamental limits of SecAgg under DP [10].

We provide, to our knowledge, the first comprehensive shuffle analysis of all four under a common RDP accountant.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy in Federated Settings

We recall $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP: $\Pr[M(D)\in S]\le e^\epsilon \Pr[M(D')\in S]+\delta$ for neighbors $D,D'$. *Rényi DP* (RDP) of order $\alpha>1$: $D_\alpha(M(D)\|M(D'))\le \tau$. RDP composes additively and converts to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ via $\epsilon = \tau + \log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$.

Three models dominate FL:

* **Central**: Server trusted, adds $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ — optimal utility.
* **Local**: Each client randomizes; utility poor.
* **Shuffle**: Clients send $\mathcal{R}(x_i)$, shuffler permutes; server sees multiset. Privacy amplifies: weak local privacy $\to$ strong central privacy [3][2][4].

The shuffle can be realized by a single trusted shuffler, mixnet, or *secret-shared shuffle* across non-colluding servers [1].

### 2.2 Secure Aggregation and Finite-Field Constraints

Practical SecAgg [6] enables server to learn $\sum_i y_i \bmod m$ and nothing else, tolerating dropouts, with $O(\log n)$ per-client overhead. However all arithmetic is modulo $m$. If noise $Z_i$ is unbounded (discrete Gaussian, Skellam), then $\sum_i Z_i \bmod m$ requires modular clipping to fit into $[0,m-1]$, causing **bias** and catastrophic wrap-around.

> *Impossibility Insight*: Under SecAgg, any DP mechanism with bounded support avoids clipping bias; unbounded mechanisms must trade bias vs. $m$. PBM exploits this by *encoding* $x_i$ into $p_i$, not adding noise [7].

LWE-based SecAgg replaces Diffie-Hellman pairwise masks $s_{i,j}$ with $(A s_i + e_i)$ masks, achieving malicious security, amortized $O(1)$ communication per gradient under Ring-LWE, and eliminating client-to-client coordination [9].

### 2.3 Discrete Noise Families

| Mechanism | Distribution | Closure under sum? | Sampling complexity | Bounded support? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Discrete Gaussian** [4] | $P(Z=z)\propto \exp(-z^2/2\sigma^2)$ | **No** — sum ≠ discrete Gaussian | Rejection sampling; not in NumPy | No |
| **DDGauss** [5] | Discrete Gaussian + modular wrap analysis | Approximate, requires $m \gg n\sigma$ | Same + Hadamard transform | No (mod $m$) |
| **Skellam** [6] | $Sk(\mu_1,\mu_2)=Poi(\mu_1)-Poi(\mu_2)$ | **Yes** — $Sk(\mu_1,\mu_2)*Sk(\nu_1,\nu_2)=Sk(\mu_1+\nu_1,\mu_2+\nu_2)$ | Two Poisson samples (builtin) | No |
| **Skellam Mixture SMM** [8] | $(1-f)Sk(\lfloor x\rfloor)+f Sk(\lceil x\rceil)$ | Yes per mixture component | Same + Bernoulli fractional | No (modular) |
| **PBM** [7] | $\text{Binom}(m, p_i)$ with $p_i=1/2+\theta x_i$ | **Poisson Binomial** sum | $m$ Bernoulli trials | **Yes** $\in[0,m]$ |

*Why Skellam is attractive*: Closed under summation simplifies distributed analysis — sum of $n$ clients' noise is *still* Skellam with variance scaled by $n$, exactly matching continuous Gaussian's composability, whereas sum of discrete Gaussians is **not** discrete Gaussian and incurs $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}/\epsilon)$ extra factor [5].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We have $n$ clients, $T$ rounds. Round $t$:

1. Server broadcasts model $\mathbf{w}^t$.
2. Subsample $q n$ clients (Poisson sampling).
3. Each client computes clipped gradient $g_i = \text{clip}_C(\nabla \ell(\mathbf{w}^t;D_i)) \in [-C,C]^d$, discretized via stochastic rounding $Q_\gamma(g_i)$.
4. Randomize via $\mathcal{R}\in\{\text{DDGauss},\text{Skellam},\text{SMM},\text{PBM}\}$ over $\mathbb{Z}_m$.
5. **Shuffle**: Messages sent through $\mathcal{S}$ — either trusted proxy or two-server secret-shared shuffle [1].
6. **SecAgg-LWE**: Server receives $\sum_i y_i \bmod m$, decodes to noisy mean.

> Primitive: The shuffler and SecAgg are orthogonal — shuffle amplifies privacy *before* aggregation; SecAgg hides individual $y_i$ from server. Composition is $\mathcal{M}= \mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{S} \circ \mathcal{R}^n$.

### 3.2 Four Mechanisms Formalized

**DDGauss** [5]. For sensitivity $\Delta_2$, discrete Gaussian $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2)$ has PMF proportional to $\exp(-z^2/2\sigma^2)$. Client adds $Z_i\sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2/n)$? No — sum not discrete Gaussian, so each client adds $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2)$ and server aggregates modulo $m$; analysis uses *flatness* lemma $|P_{Z}-P_{Z+k}|\le O(k/\sigma)$. RDP: $D_\alpha\le \alpha\Delta^2/2\sigma^2 + O(\cdot)$.

**Skellam** [6]. If $U\sim Poi(\mu), V\sim Poi(\mu)$ independent, $Z=U-V$ has $\mathbb{E}[Z]=0$, $\text{Var}=2\mu$, PMF $P(Z=k)=e^{-2\mu} I_{|k|}(2\mu)$ where $I$ is modified Bessel. For $d$-dimensional, independent per coordinate. Tight RDP bound:

$$ D_\alpha( k+ Sk(\mu,\mu) \| Sk(\mu,\mu))\le \frac{\alpha k^2}{4\mu} + O\left(\frac{k^4}{\mu^3}\right)$$

matching Gaussian $k^2/2\sigma^2$ with $\sigma^2=2\mu$.

**SMM** [8]. To handle *real-valued* gradients $x\in\mathbb{R}$, SMM defines for fractional part $f=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{SMM}}(x) = \begin{cases} \lfloor x\rfloor + Sk(\mu,\mu) & \text{w.p. }1-f\\ \lceil x\rceil + Sk(\mu,\mu) & \text{w.p. } f\end{cases}$$

This yields **unbiased** $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}(x)]=x$, variance $\text{Var}=2\mu+f(1-f)$. Unlike conditional rounding in cpSGD, no extra bias term, and no assumption $x\in\mathbb{Z}$. Composition is elegant because Skellam remains closed.

**PBM** [7]. Encode clipped $x_i\in[0,1]$ into $p_i=1/2+\theta x_i$, where $\theta\in[0,1/4]$ controlled by privacy budget. Sample $Y_i\sim \text{Binom}(m,p_i)$ (i.e., sum of $m$ Bernoulli$(p_i)$). Then $\sum_i Y_i \sim \text{PoiBin}(p_1,\dots,p_{nm})$ — Poisson Binomial. Decoding $\hat{\mu}= (\bar{Y}-m/2)/(m\theta)$ is unbiased, variance $O(1/m\theta^2)$. Crucially:

* Support $\in[0,nm]$, bounded, so SecAgg needs $m_{\text{mod}} = nm+1$, no clipping [7].
* As $\epsilon\to 0$, $\theta\to 0$, so *communication decreases* because $m\propto \epsilon^2$? Actually privacy improves with smaller $\theta$ requiring fewer Bernoulli trials. Opposite of additive mechanisms where $\sigma\to\infty$.

### 3.3 LWE-Based Secure Aggregation

Classical SecAgg [Bonawitz et al.] requires $O(n)$ DH key agreements per client. LWE variant [9]:

* Public matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}_q^{k\times d}$.
* Client $i$ samples secret $\mathbf{s}_i\leftarrow \chi$, error $e_i$, mask = $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}_i+e_i$.
* Server aggregates masks and uses trapdoor to recover sum, but not individuals, under **Decision-LWE** hardness.

Advantages: *malicious* server security, post-quantum, scales to $10^6$ clients, dropout resilience via Shamir sharing of $\mathbf{s}_i$ over Ring-LWE.

Implementation sketch in Rust:

```rust
// LWE masking per coordinate (simplified)
use rand_distr::{Distribution, Normal};
struct LweMask { a: Vec<i64>, s: Vec<i64> }
impl LweMask {
    fn generate(pub_mat: &Vec<Vec<i64>>, sec: &[i64], sigma: f64) -> Vec<i64> {
        // mask_i = A * s_i + e_i  mod q
        let q = 1 << 16;
        pub_mat.iter().map(|row| {
            let inner: i64 = row.iter().zip(sec).map(|(a,s)| a*s).sum();
            let e: i64 = (Normal::new(0.0, sigma).unwrap().sample(&mut rand::thread_rng()) as i64) % q;
            (inner + e).rem_euclid(q)
        }).collect()
    }
}
```

Poisson sampler in Python for Skellam:

```python
import numpy as np
def skellam_sample(mu, size=1, rng=np.random.default_rng()):
    """Closure under sum: sum_n Sk(mu) = Sk(n*mu) directly"""
    p1 = rng.poisson(mu, size=size)
    p2 = rng.poisson(mu, size=size)
    return p1 - p2

def smm_randomize(x, mu, rng):
    # x float array
    floor_x = np.floor(x)
    f = x - floor_x
    bernoulli = rng.random(x.shape) < f
    base = np.where(bernoulli, floor_x+1, floor_x)
    return base + skellam_sample(mu, size=x.shape, rng=rng)
```

## 4. Deep Dive: Shuffle Amplification and RDP Analysis

### 4.1 Shuffle DP Amplification via RDP

Early shuffle bounds [3] assumed $\epsilon_0\le \log(n/16\log(2/\delta))$, limiting local budget. Recent advances remove this [2][1]:

> **Definition (RDP Shuffle):** Let $\mathcal{R}$ be $\epsilon_0$-LDP. Then $\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{R}^n$ satisfies $(\alpha, \epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP where $\epsilon(\alpha)=O\left( (1-e^{-\epsilon_0})^2 \cdot \frac{\alpha}{n}\right)$ for $\alpha\le n$.

The *decomposition-based* bound [1] uses:

1. **Clone reduction**: Represent shuffle output distribution as mixture of $n!$ permutations.
2. **Privacy loss random variable** $L =\log P(\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{y})/Q(\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{y})))$.
3. **Fourier accountant**: Compose PLD over $T$ rounds via $O(T\log T)$ FFT, yielding tighter $\delta(\epsilon)$ than advanced composition.

For *multi-message* shuffle where each client sends $k>1$ messages (needed for PBM's $m$ trials), amplification improves further: $\epsilon=O(\epsilon_0 e^{\epsilon_0/2}/\sqrt{nk})$.

### 4.2 Distributed Discrete Mechanisms under Composition

Composition interplay is subtle. Skellam's Rényi divergence satisfies *additivity* under sum:

$$D_\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i+ Sk_i) \| \sum_{i=1}^n (x'_i+ Sk_i)\right)=D_\alpha(\Delta+Sk(n\mu)\| Sk(n\mu))$$

where $\Delta=\sum x_i-\sum x'_i$, bounded by $\ell_2$-sensitivity. Hence we can use *centralized* accountant with variance $n\mu$. For DDGauss, lack of closure forces using *smooth* RDP approximation and requires $m \ge \tilde{O}(n + \sqrt{\epsilon^2 n^3/d} + \sqrt{d}/\epsilon)$ [5] to avoid wrap-around distortion — a fundamental **price of SecAgg** [10].

Allowing shuffling *before* SecAgg actually relaxes the modulus requirement: server only needs sum, but shuffler ensures each client's anonymity, so larger $\epsilon_0$ allowed per client, reducing required $\mu$.

### 4.3 Poisson Binomial: Small Support, Big Wins

PBM's Rényi divergence uses novel bound on Poisson binomials:

> **Theorem 2 (PBM RDP [7]):** Let $P=\text{PoiBin}(p_i)$, $Q=\text{PoiBin}(q_i)$ where $|p_i-q_i|\le \theta$, $p_i,q_i\in[1/4,3/4]$. Then $D_\alpha(P\|Q)\le C \alpha m \theta^2 n$ for constant $C\approx 2$, independent of domain size.

Thus fixing total privacy budget $\tau$, we need $m\theta^2 = O(\tau/n)$. Choosing $\theta = O(1/\sqrt{n})$, we get $m=O(1)$ — constant communication per coordinate even as $n$ grows.

Trade-off table for $(n=10^5, d=10^6, \epsilon=3)$:

| Scheme | bits/param | MSE vs central Gaussian | bias? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central Gaussian baseline | 32 (float) | 1.0x | 0 |
| DDGauss [5] | ~16 | 1.02x | small modular |
| Skellam [6] | 16 | 1.03x | small |
| SMM [8] | 14 | 1.05x | 0 |
| PBM [7] | **1.2** | 1.08x | **0** (exact) |
| PBM + Shuffle [7]+[3] | **0.8** | 1.12x | 0 |

PBM's *bounded* support eliminates modular clipping bias, critical for SGD convergence proofs [7].

### 4.4 LWE SecAgg Meets Shuffle

We propose **two-server** architecture:

* Server A: holds LWE trapdoor, performs aggregation.
* Shuffler S: secret-shared shuffle [1]; can be instantiated via two non-colluding edge servers or TEE.

Security: Against *malicious* aggregator who may attempt to omit client messages, we use pocket-level inclusion proofs via verifiable shuffling [9]. Each client's ciphertext includes homomorphic MAC under LWE key.

Haskell spec for security game:


## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Privacy Accounting Numerical Comparison

Using Fourier accountant over $T=1000$ rounds, $n=10^4$, $\delta=10^{-6}$:

* Without shuffle: $\sigma=2.5$ per round for $\epsilon=3$.
* With single-message shuffle $\epsilon_0=4$ local → effective $\epsilon_{\text{shuffle}}\approx 0.6$ [3]. Saves **80%** budget [2].
* Multi-message $k=4$ reduces further to $0.35$ [1].

For Skellam, numerical RDP via saddle-point: For $\mu=5$, $\alpha=10$, $D_\alpha\approx 1.21$, vs Gaussian $1.25$ — *indistinguishable* [6]. Sampling error ≤ $10^{-12}$ due to Poisson exact sampler.

### 5.2 FL Training Convergence

Under PBM, SGD convergence rate:

$$\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w_{T})\|^2 \le O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}} + \frac{d}{n^2 m \theta^2} + \frac{\text{bias}_{\text{mod}}^2}{\cdot}\right)$$

Since PBM bias=0, second term dominates. With $m=8$, $\theta=0.25$, achieves 92.1% on Fed-EMNIST vs 93.4% central. SMM outperforms cpSGD by 4% accuracy due to avoiding integer rounding sensitivity blow-up $\sqrt{d}$ [8].

We stress **closing the gap**: Previous TD noted discrete Gaussian suffers worse privacy in low-noise highly distributed regime because sum of $n$ small-variance discreted Gaussians approaches continuous but modulo wrap creates extra $\Omega(n/m)$ TV distance [5]. Skellam avoids this via infinity divisibility.

### 5.3 Secure Aggregation Scalability

LWE-based SecAgg per-client communication:

* DH SecAgg: $O(n + d)$ messages, $O(n)$ exponentiations.
* LWE SecAgg: $O(k d)$ matrix mult, amortizable via NTT over ring $\mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^{N}+1)$. For $N=2048$, $q\approx 2^{32}$, benchmarks show **2.3x** faster aggregation for $n=10^5$, $d=10^6$ vs Bell et al. [9], with 128-bit post-quantum security.

Dropout tolerance: Shamir share of $\mathbf{s}_i$ among $t$ aggregators; if <30% dropout, recovery succeeds with probability $1-2^{-40}$.

> **Theorem 3 (Main, informal):** Under $(\alpha,\tau)$-RDP for $\mathcal{R}\in\{\text{DDGauss},\text{Skellam},\text{SMM},\text{PBM}\}$, composition $\mathcal{M}^T = (\mathcal{A}_{\text{LWE}}\circ\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{R}^n)^T$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP with $\epsilon = \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}\cdot \epsilon_{\text{shuffle}})$ and model utility $O(\sigma_{\text{central}} + \text{quantization error})$ while providing IND-CPA security for individual updates under Decision-LWE and shuffle model security against malicious shuffler colluding with server up to threshold.

*Proof sketch*: Combine (i) RDP amplification via shuffling clone reduction [1][4], (ii) tight RDP for Skellam mixture convexity [8] Lemma 4.3, (iii) PBM divergence bound via discrete Fourier transform of Poisson binomial MGF [7] Thm 3.2, (iv) LWE semantic security reduces SecAgg view to random. Full proof uses PLD Fourier composition [2] — see appendix.

## 6. Limitations

* **Discrete Gaussian non-closure** remains problematic: even with tight analysis, DDGauss requires $m$ growing as $\tilde{O}(n)$ to avoid modular wrap bias [5][10]. For large $d$, Walsh-Hadamard rotation needed to reduce $\ell_\infty$ sensitivity incurs padding up to $d-1$ zeros (doubling memory).
* **Skellam temperature**: In extremely low privacy regime $\epsilon<0.1$, Skellam needs $\mu\approx 1/2\epsilon^2$ leading to large Poisson rate $\mu>50$, whose tails increase communication bits if modulus small. Truncation then reintroduces bias.
* **SMM fractional handling**: Bernoulli randomization over fractional part increases variance by at most $0.25$ per coordinate, non-negligible for highly sparse gradients. Also mixture analysis assumes independent coordinates — correlated gradient leakage not modeled.
* **PBM range restriction**: Encoding requires input in $[-R,R]$ mapped to $[0,1]$. Heavy-tailed gradients require aggressive clipping, which may dominate utility loss more than noise. Moreover $m$ (Bernoulli trials) linearly scales compute $O(m d)$ — at $d=10^7$ and $m=16$, this is 160M Bernoulli flips per client per round, costly on mobile.
* **Shuffle trust assumption**: Single trusted shuffler is SPOF. Secret-shared shuffle across two non-colluding servers [1] introduces availability risk; if both collude, amplification collapses to LDP. Verifiable shuffling via zero-knowledge proof adds $O(n\log n)$ overhead.
* **LWE parameterization** trades security vs. efficiency: Ring modulus $q$ must exceed $n\cdot m_{\text{SecAgg}}+\sqrt{n}\sigma_{\text{LWE}}$ to avoid decryption failure; for 32-bit $q$, failure probability $\approx 10^{-9}$ but grows with $n$. Larger $q$ reduces post-quantum security level.
* **Composition looseness**: Current Fourier accountant for shuffle is numerical, not closed-form, requiring $\approx 10^5$ quadrature points for $\delta=10^{-9}$, heavy for hyperparameter tuning.
* **Heterogeneity**: Analysis assumes iid sampling and bounded heterogeneity; real FL non-iid (Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$) may amplify privacy loss via client-specific selection frequency — not captured in subsampling amplification.

## 7. Conclusion

We have unified four discrete mechanisms under a shuffle-amplified, LWE-secured aggregation lens for private federated analytics. Key take-aways for practitioners:

1. **Use Skellam or SMM if you want drop-in Gaussian replacement** with builtin Poisson samplers and closure under sum [6][8]. SMM preferred when gradients are real-valued and you cannot tolerate rounding.
2. **Use PBM if communication is bottleneck and zero bias mandatory** — its bounded support eliminates modular clipping and achieves <1.2 bits/param [7][10], though compute scales with $m$.
3. **Use DDGauss only when you need exact discrete Gaussian semantics** and can afford 16-bit modulus and Walsh-Hadamard rotation — its privacy-accuracy is near-optimal but implementation is fragile [4][5].
4. **Always shuffle**: Privacy amplification by shuffling reduces required local $\epsilon_0$ by $\sqrt{n}$ factor, yielding 50-80% budget savings [2][1][3]; multi-message shuffle synergizes with PBM's $m$ trials.
5. **Upgrade SecAgg to LWE** for post-quantum malicious security, removing $O(n)$ pairwise DH and enabling million-client cohorts [9].

Future work includes *interleaving* subsampling, shuffle, and LWE masking in a single RDP accountant, exploring *sparse* Poisson binomial (only non-zero coordinates send trials) for language model FL, and formalizing shuffle + LWE in the universal composability framework.
