# Structured State Space Sequence Models: HiPPO Memory, the S4 Parameterization, and Selective Scanning in Mamba

## Abstract

Structured state space sequence models (SSMs) have re-emerged as a principled alternative to self-attention for long-range sequence modeling. This thesis develops the mathematical foundations of continuous-time linear state space models, their discretization via zero-order hold and bilinear transforms, and the HiPPO theory of optimal polynomial-projection memory that resolves the long-horizon forgetting problem of classical recurrences. We then dissect S4's diagonal-plus-low-rank parameterization and its Cauchy-kernel convolution view that enables FFT-accelerated training, followed by the family of simplifications and generalizations it spawned: S4D, DSS, S5's multi-input multi-output formulation with parallel scans, and Liquid-S4. The core contribution of the second half is Mamba's selective mechanism, which makes the step size, input, and output matrices input-dependent to achieve content-aware gating with linear time and constant-memory inference, implemented through a hardware-aware parallel associative scan, and its successor Mamba-2, which reframes the recurrence as a Structured State Space Duality (SSD) of a semiseparable matrix with attention. We analyze expressivity on copying and induction-head tasks, survey Long Range Arena and language-modeling benchmarks, and close with limitations: recall bottlenecks, training instabilities, and attention's remaining in-context edge.

## 1 Introduction

For most of the deep learning era, sequence modeling oscillated between two poles: **recurrent** models, which compress history into a fixed-size hidden state with constant per-step cost but suffer from vanishing gradients and a finite effective memory horizon, and **convolutional** or **attention-based** models, which preserve the entire input with per-step parallelism but pay quadratic cost in sequence length (attention) or bounded receptive fields (convolutions). The long-range sequence problem — the requirement that a model faithfully propagate information across thousands of timesteps — exposed the failure modes of both poles: Transformers scaled poorly to 16k-token sequences, while RNNs, even LSTM and GRU variants, could not reliably resolve dependencies spanning even a few hundred steps [7].

The structured state space program, initiated by the **HiPPO** framework (2020) and instantiated in **S4** (2021), **S5** (2022), **Mamba** (2023), and **Mamba-2** (2024), proposes that the classical linear state space model of control theory — the subject of sixty years of systems theory — is the right primitive, provided it is initialized and parameterized correctly [1][2][3][5][6]. The central insight is that long-range memory is not a training artifact but a *mathematical design decision*: projecting the input history onto orthogonal polynomial bases under carefully chosen time measures yields an analytically optimal memory update, and initializing a deep SSM with the corresponding matrices converts a naive recurrence into a mechanism with bounded gradients, timescale robustness, and provably complete memory of the past [1].

This thesis reconstructs that intellectual arc in full technical detail. We begin with the continuous-time SSM formulation and its discretizations (§2–§3), derive HiPPO's Legendre memory measures (§4.1), explain how S4's diagonal-plus-low-rank structure turns a theoretically intractable kernel computation into an FFT-efficient convolution (§4.2), survey the variant zoo (§4.3), and then analyze the selective paradigm shift: Mamba's input-dependent parameters and hardware-aware scan (§4.4), and Mamba-2's duality with attention (§4.5). We compare expressivity against attention (§5), present benchmark evidence (§5), and conclude with limitations and open questions (§6–§7).

> **Theorem (Informal, running theme):** Every advance in this line of work can be understood as simultaneously improving *two* quantities: (i) the *memory optimality* of the state representation — how completely a finite state vector summarizes the unbounded past — and (ii) the *computational duality* of the resulting operator — the ability to compute the same map as a recurrence (linear-time inference), a convolution (FFT-parallel training), and, in Mamba-2, as a structured matrix product analogous to attention.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Classical sequence models and the long-range problem

The **Long Range Arena (LRA)** benchmark (2021) codified the long-range problem into six tasks with sequence lengths from 1k to 16k tokens — byte-level text classification and retrieval, ListOps, CIFAR-10 image classification at pixel level, and the Pathfinder/Path-X spatial reasoning tasks — against which Transformer variants were systematically evaluated and found wanting [7]. Classical recurrent networks failed earlier and more quietly: the *vanishing/exploding gradient* pathology meant that a standard RNN could not credit assignment across more than a few hundred steps, and even gated variants (LSTM, GRU) exhibited a soft ceiling, because their memory is a learned heuristic rather than an engineered guarantee [1]. What the SSM program contributes is precisely the guarantee: a state that is *provably* the optimal compression of the signal history under a specified measure of past importance.

### 2.2 Linear state space models from control theory

The continuous-time linear state space model is the system

$$\dot{x}(t) = A x(t) + B u(t), \qquad y(t) = C x(t) + D u(t), \tag{1}$$

with state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$, input $u(t) \in \mathbb{R}$, output $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$, and matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$. Equation (1) is the lingua franca of control: the *controllability* of $(A, B)$ determines whether the input can steer the state, the *observability* of $(A, C)$ determines whether the state can be read through the output, and the transfer function $H(s) = C(sI - A)^{-1}B + D$ determines input–output behavior in the frequency domain [3].

Deep learning had flirted with SSMs before — linear RNNs, unitary RNNs, Legendre Memory Units — but always as trainable recurrences from random initialization. The **LSSL** (Linear State Space Layer, 2021) reformulated deep SSMs as a general framework unifying recurrent, convolutional, and continuous-time views, showing that any discretization of (1) yields both a recurrence for autoregressive inference and a convolution kernel for parallel training [3]. LSSL's weakness was computational: naive parameterizations required $O(N^2 L)$ or $O(N^3)$ operations. **HiPPO** had already supplied the missing initialization theory [1]; **S4** supplied the missing parameterization [2].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Discretization: from continuous to discrete time

Deep learning operates on sampled sequences $u_0, u_1, \dots, u_{L-1}$ with an implicit step size $\Delta$ (the *sampling rate*). Converting (1) into the discrete recurrence

$$x_k = \bar{A} x_{k-1} + \bar{B} u_k, \qquad y_k = \bar{C} x_k, \tag{2}$$

requires choosing how the input behaves *between* samples. Two classical choices dominate:

- **Zero-order hold (ZOH):** assume $u(t)$ constant on each interval $[k\Delta, (k+1)\Delta)$. Solving (1) exactly over the interval gives
  $$\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}(\exp(\Delta A) - I)\,\Delta B, \qquad \bar{C} = C. \tag{3}$$
  ZOH is exact for piecewise-constant inputs and preserves stability: eigenvalues of $A$ with negative real part map strictly inside the unit disc.
- **Bilinear (Tustin) transform:** approximate the differential with the trapezoidal rule, yielding
  $$\bar{A} = (I - \tfrac{\Delta}{2}A)^{-1}(I + \tfrac{\Delta}{2}A), \qquad \bar{B} = (I - \tfrac{\Delta}{2}A)^{-1}\Delta B, \qquad \bar{C} = C, \tag{4}$$
  which maps the left half-plane exactly onto the unit disc, again preserving stability.

S4 uses a **generalized bilinear** discretization and, crucially, *learns* $\Delta$ as a per-channel parameter, which gives the model an adaptive timescale: small $\Delta$ resolves fine detail, large $\Delta$ integrates long-range context [2]. Mamba later promotes $\Delta$ to a full input-dependent function — the key selective ingredient (§4.4) [5].

### 3.2 The convolutional view

Unrolling (2) with $x_{-1} = 0$ expresses the output as a convolution of the input with the **SSM kernel**

$$\bar{K} = (\bar{C}\bar{B},\; \bar{C}\bar{A}\bar{B},\; \dots,\; \bar{C}\bar{A}^{L-1}\bar{B}), \qquad y = \bar{K} \ast u, \tag{5}$$

computable in $O(L \log L)$ via FFT. This duality — *recurrence for inference, convolution for training* — is the computational backbone of the entire family: models train with parallel convolution and deploy with constant-memory recurrences [2][3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HiPPO: optimal polynomial-projection memory

The foundational question HiPPO answers is: *given a fixed state budget of $N$ coefficients, what is the best possible online summary of the history $\{u(s) : s \le t\}$?* HiPPO frames this as function approximation: maintain coefficients $c(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ of the projection of the history onto $N$ orthogonal basis polynomials $g_n$, minimizing

$$\left\lVert u_{\le t} - \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} c_n(t)\, g_n^{(t)} \right\rVert_{\mu^{(t)}}^2, \tag{6}$$

where $\mu^{(t)}$ is a *measure* assigning importance to past timesteps and the basis adapts to it [1]. Differentiating the optimality condition yields a linear ODE $\dot{c} = A c + B u$ — a state space model whose $(A, B)$ are *derived*, not learned.

Three measures produce the canonical instantiations:

| Instantiation | Measure $\mu^{(t)}$ | Basis | Property |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HiPPO-LegT** | uniform on $[t-\theta, t]$ | scaled Legendre | sliding window of fixed width $\theta$ |
| **HiPPO-LegS** | uniform on $[0, t]$ (scaled) | scaled Legendre | remembers *all* history, timescale-robust |
| **HiPPO-LagT** | exponential decay $e^{-(t-s)/\theta}$ | Laguerre | fading memory with forgetting |

**HiPPO-LegS** is the pivotal case: its measure dilates with time, so the system remembers the entire history without any prior on the timescale, enjoys *bounded gradients* (the $A$ matrix is normal up to a low-rank term with controlled norm), and supports *fast $O(N)$ updates* [1]. As a special case, the framework re-derives the Legendre Memory Unit from first principles and generalizes GRU gating. Empirically, a HiPPO-LegS RNN reached 98.3% on permuted MNIST and outperformed RNN and Neural-ODE baselines by 25–40% on timescale-shifted trajectory classification [1].

The LegS matrix takes the form $A = A^{\text{(normal)}} - \text{low-rank}$, a structure S4 exploits directly: HiPPO matrices are exactly the **diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR)** matrices that make S4 tractable.

### 4.2 S4: the diagonal-plus-low-rank parameterization

S4's breakthrough is simultaneously a *modeling* and an *algorithmic* result [2]. Modeling: initialize the SSM with the HiPPO-LegS matrix and learn in the structured class

$$A = \Lambda - P Q^*, \tag{7}$$

where $\Lambda$ is diagonal (complex) and $P, Q$ are low-rank factors — the **DPLR** form. Algorithmic: the convolution kernel (5) for DPLR matrices can be computed without ever forming $\bar{A}^k$. Using the Woodbury identity and a generating-function/Cauchy-kernel reduction, S4 evaluates $\bar{K}$ via the resolvent $(sI - A)^{-1}$ on the unit circle as a sum of **Cauchy kernel** terms

$$K(z) = \frac{2}{1+z}\Big[\tilde{C}^*(I - \bar{\Lambda}z)^{-1}\bar{B}\Big] + \text{low-rank corrections}, \tag{8}$$

each computable in $O(N + L)$ time, with the low-rank correction handled by the matrix inversion lemma in $O(Nr)$ per frequency. The total cost is $\tilde{O}(N + L)$ instead of $O(N^2 L)$ [2].

Architecturally, an **S4 layer** runs $H$ independent single-input single-output (SISO) SSMs in parallel — one per feature channel — followed by a position-wise mixing (linear + nonlinearity + residual), mirroring the Transformer block but with the SSM replacing attention. Results were dramatic: state of the art on LRA (average ~86% with improved configs), 91% accuracy on sequential CIFAR, strong raw-audio generation with **SaShiMi**, and competitive WikiText-103 perplexity — all with *linear* scaling in sequence length [2]. S4 also proved theoretically that its HiPPO initialization is *necessary*: ablating it collapses long-range performance, confirming that the memory guarantee, not just the architecture, drives the gains.

### 4.3 The variant zoo: S4D, DSS, S5, and Liquid-S4

The DPLR machinery, while elegant, is intricate to implement. A wave of simplifications followed:

- **S4D (diagonal):** observed that the low-rank term in (7) matters mainly at initialization; a purely diagonal $A$ with the HiPPO-inspired initialization ($\Lambda$ entries sampled from the diagonal of the HiPPO matrix) matches S4 on most tasks with a far simpler implementation [2, companion]. S4D's lesson: the *spectrum* of the initialization (eigenvalues with negative real parts, imaginary parts spread over frequencies) matters more than the exact algebraic structure.
- **DSS (diagonal state spaces):** an independent diagonal simplification reaching similar conclusions [2].
- **S5:** replaces $H$ independent SISO SSMs with a *single multi-input multi-output (MIMO)* SSM, $x_k = \bar{A}x_{k-1} + \bar{B}u_k$ with $u_k \in \mathbb{R}^H$, and computes the recurrence with a **parallel associative scan** instead of convolution [4]. The scan exploits the associativity of the pair-operation $(a_i, b_i) \bullet (a_j, b_j) = (a_j a_i,\; a_j b_i + b_j)$, achieving $O(\log L)$ depth. S5 matches S4's efficiency while reaching **87.4% average on LRA and 98.5% on Path-X**, the hardest task [4].
- **Liquid-S4:** adds input-dependent (liquid) time constants, a first step toward selectivity [2, related].

A subtle but important theoretical point unifies them: the *diagonal* variants are exactly as expressive as the DPLR ones for the kernel computation, because what the kernel (5) depends on is the rational transfer function $C(sI - A)^{-1}B$, and diagonal complex $A$ with learned $(B, C)$ spans the same space of order-$N$ transfer functions [2][4].

### 4.4 Mamba: the selective mechanism

All models above are **linear time-invariant (LTI)**: $(\bar{A}, \bar{B}, \bar{C})$ are fixed after training, so the memory update treats every token identically — a fundamental limitation for *information-dense* data like language, where the model must *selectively* remember a name mentioned once and forget filler tokens [5]. Mamba's thesis: make the SSM parameters **input-dependent**,

$$\Delta_k = \tau_\Delta(\text{Linear}(u_k)), \quad B_k = \text{Linear}(u_k), \quad C_k = \text{Linear}(u_k), \tag{9}$$

so that discretization (3) becomes a per-step nonlinear map: $\bar{A}_k = \exp(\Delta_k A)$, $\bar{B}_k$ likewise. The step size $\Delta_k$ acts as a **data-controlled gate**: $\Delta_k \to 0$ freezes the state (ignore the token), $\Delta_k$ large resets/overwrites it (attend to the token). This recovers the selective power of attention's softmax and RNN gating *within* a linear recurrence.

> **Theorem (Selectivity as gating):** With input-dependent $\Delta$, the scalar SSM recurrence $x_k = \bar{A}_k x_{k-1} + \bar{B}_k u_k$ implements exact content-based gating: for $\Delta_k \to 0$, $\bar{A}_k \to I$ and $x_k \to x_{k-1}$ (perfect retention); for $\Delta_k \to \infty$ with $A = -I$, $\bar{A}_k \to 0$ (complete reset). The mechanism is continuous between these extremes, giving a differentiable, per-token memory controller [5].

Selectivity breaks the convolutional view (the kernel is no longer time-invariant), so Mamba cannot use FFT training. Instead it uses a **hardware-aware parallel associative scan**: the per-step transition is still associative, so a Blelloch-style scan computes all states in $O(L)$ work with $O(\log L)$ depth — and, crucially, the scan is fused into a single GPU kernel that keeps the $N$-dimensional state in SRAM, materializing only the needed intermediates to HBM, in the spirit of FlashAttention [5]. The **Mamba block** further simplifies the architecture: it drops attention and MLP blocks entirely, using a gated-MLP-style sandwich (linear expansion, causal depthwise convolution, selective SSM, gating, projection) repeated homogeneously.

Empirically, Mamba matched or beat Transformers of equal size on language modeling (the Pile) while scaling *linearly* in length, with **5× faster inference** than Transformers at 2k context and constant-memory generation — and it decisively closed the gap on synthetic selective-copying tasks where S4 had failed [5].

### 4.5 Mamba-2: structured state space duality

Mamba-2 reframes the selective recurrence as a **duality between recurrences and structured matrices** [6]. Any linear recurrence $x_k = a_k x_{k-1} + b_k u_k$, $y_k = c_k x_k$ can be written as a matrix-vector product $y = M u$ with $M$ a **1-semiseparable matrix**:

$$M_{ji} = c_j^T \Big(\prod_{k=i+1}^{j} a_k\Big) b_i \quad (j \ge i), \tag{10}$$

lower-triangular with rank-1 off-diagonal blocks. This **SSD (Structured State Space Duality)** framework shows that selective SSMs and (linear) attention are two algorithms for the same structured-matrix family: attention corresponds to the *quadratic* form $(L \circ QK^T)V$ where $L$ masks causality, while SSD computes the same product in linear time via chunkwise decomposition — splitting the sequence into chunks, computing intra-chunk terms as small attention-like matmuls (tensor-core friendly) and inter-chunk terms via the recurrence [6]. The head structure is also redesigned: Mamba-2 uses a multi-head pattern (like attention) with per-head scalar-times-identity $A$, unlocking 2–8× training throughput over Mamba-1. The conceptual payoff is large: it demonstrates that "Transformers are SSMs" in a precise algebraic sense, and that the design space between them is continuous rather than dichotomous.

### Expressivity: what can selective SSMs do that LTI SSMs cannot?

1. **Selective copying.** The synthetic *selective copy* task (remember sparse informative tokens among noise) is provably hard for LTI systems: a time-invariant linear filter cannot suppress noise tokens without also suppressing signal, because its kernel weights are position-agnostic [5]. Mamba's input-dependent $\Delta_k$ solves it by construction — the gating theorem above.
2. **Induction heads and associative recall.** Attention's celebrated induction-head mechanism (pattern completion: "… A B … A → B") requires content-addressable lookup. Selective SSMs implement a *compressed* variant: $B_k$ writes keyed content into the state, $C_k$ reads it out. Multi-query associative recall (MQAR) benchmarks show Mamba-1 trailing attention on long-range recall, with Mamba-2's larger state and head structure substantially closing the gap [6].
3. **Universality vs. efficiency trade-off.** LTI SSMs are universal approximators of *regular* (finite-memory) languages but provably weaker than attention on tasks requiring unbounded content-based addressing with a *fixed* state size; selectivity restores the missing capability at the cost of losing the convolutional training path [5][6].

> **Theorem (SSD unification):** Linear attention and selective SSMs are both instances of multiplication by structured matrices — attention by a masked Gram matrix, SSMs by a semiseparable matrix. The SSD algorithm interpolates between the quadratic and linear regimes via chunking, proving the two paradigms are endpoints of one continuum rather than disjoint architectures [6].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Recall with fixed state remains lossy.** Despite selectivity, Mamba's state is still a fixed-size vector; on multi-query associative recall with many keys, precision degrades where attention's KV cache grows unboundedly [5][6].
2. **Training fragility.** Selective SSMs reintroduce optimization pathologies the LTI theory had tamed: the input-dependent discretization can produce stiff dynamics, and practitioners report sensitivity to $\Delta$ initialization, learning rates, and the causal-convolution front-end. The clean HiPPO stability guarantees apply to initialization, not to the selective regime [5].
3. **No convolutional fallback.** Because selectivity is time-varying, Mamba-family models *must* train with scans; they cannot exploit FFT convolution. Scan kernels are hardware-expert artifacts (fused CUDA, SRAM tiling), raising the implementation bar far above a matmul [5][6].
4. **In-context learning gaps.** On tasks where Transformers exploit attention's explicit pairwise comparisons (e.g., certain few-shot pattern completions), selective SSMs underperform at equal scale, suggesting content-addressing through a compressed state is strictly weaker than full pairwise addressing for some function classes [5].
5. **Theory lags practice.** We have HiPPO-style optimality theory for LTI memory and SSD algebra for selective computation, but no unified theory of *what selective SSMs learn*: no analog of the induction-head circuit analysis, and open questions about the sample complexity of learning the gating functions themselves.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The structured state space program is best read as a single sustained argument: **memory should be designed, not hoped for**. HiPPO derived the optimal finite-state summary of history from first principles [1]; S4 made it computable at scale through the DPLR–Cauchy parameterization [2]; the diagonal and MIMO variants (S4D, S5) showed the essence was spectral initialization and associative computation, not algebraic intricacy [4]; Mamba added the missing ingredient — content-aware selectivity — and the hardware-aware scan to train it [5]; and Mamba-2 revealed that the resulting objects are, in a precise matrix-algebraic sense, the same family as attention itself [6]. The practical payoff is real: linear-time, constant-memory inference with Transformer-competitive quality on language, and state-of-the-art long-range reasoning on 16k-token benchmarks.

Yet the arc is unfinished. The field still lacks a theory of selective memory *learning* to match its theory of selective memory *representation*; recall under fixed-state compression remains the central bottleneck; and the empirical contest with attention on in-context flexibility is unresolved. If the next chapter mirrors the last — a principled mathematical characterization preceding the engineering — it will likely come from extending HiPPO-style optimality to the input-dependent regime: an *optimal selective projection* theory. Until then, SSMs stand as the strongest evidence that the Transformer is a point in a larger design space, not its boundary.
