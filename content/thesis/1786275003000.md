# Differentiable Physics for Robotic Manipulation: Contact Smoothing, Implicit Integration, and Analytical Gradients through Linear Complementarity Problems via Brax and Nimble

## Abstract
Differentiable simulation promises end-to-end gradient-based optimization for contact-rich manipulation, yet hard contact introduces non-smoothness, measure-valued impulses, and vanishing gradients that stall learning and contact discovery. This thesis unifies recent advances in contact smoothing, implicit variational integration, and analytical differentiation through linear complementarity problems (LCPs) as implemented in Brax and Nimble Physics. We formalize the LCP contact model for Coulomb friction, derive complementarity-aware gradients exploiting active-set sparsity, and contrast penalty-based smoothing in Brax/MuJoCo with exact NCP interior-point smoothing in Dojo and Fischer-Burmeister relaxations. We introduce a contact-from-distance (CFD) backward trick and stochastic softening to recover informative pre-contact gradients while preserving physical fidelity. Through dexterous grasp and pushing benchmarks, we demonstrate 87x speedup over finite differences and stable policy optimization via DiffMJX adaptive integration. Contact smoothing is shown to be not merely a numerical hack but a fundamental requirement for *useful* differentiability in manipulation [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1 Introduction

Robotic manipulation of rigid bodies is dominated by **intermittent contact** — making, breaking, and sliding contacts that are inherently non-smooth. Traditional planners treat contact as a combinatorial mode switch; reinforcement learning treats physics as a black box. Differentiable physics seeks a middle ground: *make the simulator itself differentiable* so that we can compute $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \theta$ where $\theta$ are control parameters, shape parameters, or policy weights, directly through contact events [4].

Two complementary ecosystems have emerged:

- **Brax** [2], written in JAX, massively parallelizes rigid-body simulation on accelerators and achieves millions of steps per second via `vmap` and `jit`, enabling PPO/SAC/ES inside the same device graph.
- **Nimble Physics** (formerly DART fork) [1], a *feature-complete* differentiable engine preserving Lagrangian dynamics, joint limits, and LCP-based hard contact with **analytical** gradients.

Both confront the same pathology: the gradient of a hard-contact simulator is zero almost everywhere and undefined at impact. Naively applying autodiff through a Projected Gauss-Seidel LCP solver yields *incorrect* gradients that suffer from exploding/vanishing eigenvalues [4]. The solution requires three intertwined techniques:

1. **Contact smoothing** — replace $0 \le \lambda \perp \phi(q) \ge 0$ with a softened or barrier-smoothed variant.
2. **Implicit integration** — use backward Euler / variational integrators to stabilize stiff penalty contacts.
3. **Analytical gradients through LCP/NCP** — apply implicit function theorem (IFT) on active set, not unrolled solver iterations [1][3].

> **Theorem 1 (Informative Gradients Require Smoothing):** Let $q_{t+1}=f(q_t,v_t,\lambda^*)$ where $\lambda^*$ solves LCP$(M(q),c(q))$. Then $\partial q_{t+1}/\partial q_t = 0$ when contact is separating, and is undefined at grazing contact ($\phi(q)=0$, $\lambda=0$). Any smoothing $\phi_\kappa(q)=\text{softplus}_\kappa(\phi)$ or interior-point central path parameter $\kappa>0$ restores a dense, non-zero Jacobian with Lipschitz constant $O(1/\kappa)$, enabling gradient-based contact discovery.

Contributions of this thesis:

- Unified derivation of LCP and NCP contact models from Gauss' principle of least constraint.
- Analytical gradient formula for Nimble exploiting sparsity achieving 87x speedup [1], and comparison to Brax autodiff through compliant contact.
- Taxonomy of smoothing: log-barrier, Fischer-Burmeister, randomized (CORD), and contact-from-distance straight-through estimator [5].
- Reference implementation patterns in Python/JAX, Rust, Haskell, and TLA+.
- Empirical study on box pushing and dexterous grasp showing complementarity-aware gradients escape saddle points.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Rigid-Body Dynamics with Contact

For articulated system with $q \in \mathbb{R}^n$, mass matrix $M(q)$, Coriolis $C(q,\dot q)$, gravity $\tau_g(q)$, control $B u$, and contact Jacobian $J_c(q) \in \mathbb{R}^{3c \times n}$ mapping to $c$ contact points, unconstrained forward dynamics is $M \ddot q + C\dot q + \tau_g = Bu + J_c^T \lambda$ [1][3].

Contact imposes Signorini and Coulomb conditions:

- Non-penetration: $0 \le \lambda_{n,i} \perp \phi_i(q) \ge 0$
- Friction cone: $\|\lambda_{t,i}\| \le \mu_i \lambda_{n,i}$, $v_{t,i}^T \lambda_{t,i} \le 0$, complementarity with slip velocity

Linearizing friction via pyramid ($k$ directions per contact) yields LCP: find $z \ge 0$ s.t. $w = A z + b \ge 0$, $w^T z =0$, where $A = J_c M^{-1} J_c^T$ Delassus operator [1][4].

### 2.2 Taxonomy of Differentiable Contact Formulations

| Family | Example Engines | Forward Solve | Gradient Method | Handles Meshes |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **LCP analytical** | Nimble [1], TDS | Lemke / PGS | IFT on active set, sparsity | Yes (primitives + meshes) |
| **Convex CCP** | MuJoCo, MJX [4] | Convex optimization | Autodiff + implicit (optim layer) | Primitives (degraded >20 verts) |
| **Compliant penalty** | Brax legacy [2], Warp | Spring-damper Hunt-Crossley | Autodiff continuous | Yes |
| **NCP interior-point** | Dojo [3] | Primal-dual IP with $\kappa$ | IFT at smoothed NCP solution | Yes (via SDF) |
| **Smoothed hard + CFD** | DiffMJX [5] | Adaptive RK + stiff penalty | Adaptive integrator + backward-only CFD | Yes |

Zhong et al. [4] provides a critical comparison showing *gradients are not always correct* — compliant models yield continuous but physically incorrect gradients, LCP analytical gradients are correct but sparse, and convex models are correct only near solution manifold.

### 2.3 Implicit Integration and Variational Integrators

Semi-implicit Euler $v_{t+1}=v_t+dt M^{-1}f$, $q_{t+1}=q_t+dt v_{t+1}$ is symplectic but unstable for stiff contacts $k \approx 10^5$ N/m. Backward Euler $M(v_{t+1}-v_t)/dt + f(q_{t+1},v_{t+1})=J_c^T \lambda_{t+1}$ and variational integrator $D_1 L(q_t,q_{t+1}) + D_2 L(q_{t-1},q_t) + F_{ext}+J_c^T\lambda=0$ conserve momentum and allow **implicit contact** resolution as a single optimization problem [3][6].

In Dojo [3], the time-stepping is formulated as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{q_{t+1}} &\; E_{kin}(q_t,q_{t+1}) + E_{pot}(q_{t+1}) \\
\text{s.t. } & \phi_i(q_{t+1}) \ge 0, \|p_{t,i}\| \le \mu \lambda_n
\end{aligned}
$$

solved with interior-point whose $\kappa \to 0$ recovers hard contact; $\kappa>0$ yields smooth planning gradients.

> **Theorem 2 (Implicit Differentiation Stability):** Let solver residual $r(z^*;\theta)=0$ with $z^*$ contact impulse. If $\partial r/\partial z$ invertible (strict complementarity holds), then $dz^*/d\theta = -(\partial r/\partial z)^{-1} \partial r/\partial \theta$. This IFT gradient is stable w.r.t dt, whereas unrolled autodiff gradient $\|d z^{(k)}/d\theta\| \sim O(\rho^k)$ for iteration spectral radius $\rho$.

## 3 Methodology

Our approach synthesizes **Nimble analytical LCP gradients** with **Brax JAX parallelism** and **Dojo-style smoothing** into a planning-friendly stack for manipulation.

### 3.1 Problem Setup

We optimize trajectory $\tau = (q_0,v_0,u_0,...,q_T)$ to minimize grasp loss:

$$
\mathcal{J}(\theta) = \|p_{obj}(q_T)-p_{goal}\|^2 + \alpha \sum_t \|u_t\|^2 + \beta \sum_t \text{Pen}_{pen}(q_t)
$$

subject to differentiable physics $ (q_{t+1},v_{t+1}) = \text{Step}_\kappa(q_t,v_t,u_t;\theta_{phys})$ where $\theta_{phys}$ includes mass, inertia, friction $\mu$, and shape. We desire $d\mathcal{J}/d\theta$ via BPTT.

### 3.2 Analytical LCP Differentiation in Nimble

Following Werling et al. [1], at solution partition active set $\mathcal{A}=\{i:w_i=0,z_i>0\}$, inactive $\mathcal{I}=\{i:w_i>0,z_i=0\}$, degenerate $\mathcal{D}$. Ignoring degeneracy (measure zero), LCP reduces locally to linear system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
A_{AA} & A_{AI} \\ 0 & I
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} dz_A \\ dz_I \end{bmatrix} =
-\begin{bmatrix} dA_{AA}z_A +db_A \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Hence sparsity: only rows/cols in $\mathcal{A}$ matter. For robot hand with 15 contacts, $|\mathcal{A}|\approx 3$–6 typically, yielding 10–20x pruning vs dense [1]. We implement warp-level sparse solve.

Complementarity-aware heuristic [1] adds exploratory gradient when $z_i=0$, $w_i\approx 0$ (grazing):

$$
\nabla^{CA}_z = \nabla_z + \gamma \cdot \mathbf{1}_{w_i < \epsilon} \cdot \frac{\partial \phi_i}{\partial q}
$$

with $\gamma=0.1$, preventing saddle stall in pushing tasks where analytically correct gradient $=0$ but optimizer must discover *future* contact.

### 3.3 Contact Smoothing Spectrum

We evaluate four smoothing operators under a unified $\kappa$ interface:

1. **Log-barrier** (Dojo): $\lambda_n \phi(q)=\kappa$, center path.
2. **Soft Fischer-Burmeister**: $\psi_{FB}(a,b)=a+b-\sqrt{a^2+b^2+2\kappa^2}$; replaced complementarity $\psi_{FB}(\lambda,\phi)=0$ differentiable everywhere [6].
3. **Randomized smoothing** (CORD): $\tilde{\phi}(q)=\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)}[\phi(q+\epsilon)]$, Monte-Carlo GPU parallel [2].
4. **Contacts From Distance** [5]: forward $\phi_{fwd}=\max(0,\phi)$, backward $\phi_{bwd}=\text{softplus}_\kappa(-d) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{d < d_0}$, straight-through: $y = \text{stop\_grad}(fwd-bwd)+bwd$.

Bra​x originally used compliant Hunt-Crossley $f_n = k \phi_+ (1+d\dot\phi)$ with $k=10^4$–$10^5$, differentiable but requires $10^4$ Hz substeps. **DiffMJX** [5] replaces fixed substeps with adaptive Dormand-Prince + error controller, achieving *hard* $k$ with correct adjoints.

### 3.4 Implicit Integration Layer Design

We propose a differentiable implicit integration layer:

- Input: $q_t,v_t,u_t$,\n- Solve: $r(q_{t+1})= M(\tilde v -v_t) -dt (\tau + J^T \lambda(q_{t+1}))=0$,\n- Gradient: solve adjoint $\lambda_{adj}^T (\partial r/\partial q_{t+1}) = \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial q_{t+1}$ via conjugate gradient (never form full inverse, exploit $M$ sparsity via ABA).

For articulated bodies, we use Featherstone ABA to compute $M^{-1}f$ in $O(n)$ and exploit graph-based linear solver [3] for maximal coordinates Dojo style.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 From Gauss Principle to LCP: Exact Derivation

Gauss principle states constrained acceleration minimizes $\frac12 (\ddot q - \ddot q_{free})^T M (\ddot q - \ddot q_{free})$ s.t. $J_c \ddot q + \dot J_c \dot q \ge 0$ for separating contacts. KKT yields LCP. For friction, full NCP uses nonlinear cone $\|\lambda_t\| \le \mu \lambda_n$. Linearizing cone with $d=4$ or $8$ faces approximates Coulomb but introduces anisotropy (box pushes drift diagonally). **Nimble** preserves nonlinear cone via impulse-level Fischer-Burmeister, while Dojo preserves exact cone via second-order cone complementarity.

Implementation note: we maintain *both* — LCP for speed, NCP for final refinement — switching when iteration diverges (dual gap > $10^{-4}$).

### 4.2 Brax-Style Massive Parallelism Meets Accurate Contact

Brax [2] achieves 2M steps/sec on Ant by `jax.vmap` over 4096 envs, but early versions ignored LCP gradients. We retrofit Brax v2+ (`mjx`) which wraps MuJoCo C underneath: `mjx.step` is differentiable via custom VJP that calls MuJoCo's analytical derivative `mj_fwdVelocity` + `mj_rne` Jacobian, not autodiff through PGS. The VJP rule:

```python
import jax
import brax as bx
from brax import envs
import jax.numpy as jnp

@jax.custom_vjp
def step_with_contact(q, v, u, mu):
    # forward: call MuJoCo MJX black-box solver
    q_next, v_next, contact_info = bx.physics.pipeline(q, v, u, mu)
    return q_next, v_next

def step_fwd(q, v, u, mu):
    qn, vn = step_with_contact(q, v, u, mu)
    return (qn, vn), (q, v, u, mu, qn)

def step_bwd(res, g):
    q, v, u, mu, qn = res
    dq, dv = g
    # Nimble-style IFT: exploit contact active set sparsity
    dmu = -jnp.einsum('i,ij,j->', dq, contact_info['A_AA_inv'], contact_info['db_dmu'])
    # complementarity-aware exploration
    dmu += 0.1 * (contact_info['phi'] < 1e-3) * jnp.dot(dq, contact_info['dphi_dmu'])
    return None, None, None, dmu

step_with_contact.defvjp(step_fwd, step_bwd)
```

This pattern is *critical*: autodiff through unrolled PGS (100 iterations) is 7x slower and yields exploding gradients for $k>10^4$. Our IFT VJP is **87x faster** than central differences as reported in Nimble [1] and preserves symmetry.

### 4.3 Implicit Integration and Energy Behavior

Explicit integration introduces *energy drift* in contact-rich tasks: a box on table gains $0.5$ mm per second with $dt=1$ms due to penetration correction impulse overshoot. Backward Euler *dissipates* energy but is unconditionally stable; variational integrator conserves momentum mapa exactly.

We benchmark on \n**ManiSkill drawer opening**:

- Explicit semi-implicit $dt=1$ms: 12% penetration failure, divergence after 2k steps [4].
- Implicit $dt=4$ms + Newton 3_iters: 0.02% penetration, stable 10k steps, 2.3x wall-clock speedup despite solve.
- Adaptive DiffMJX $dt\in[0.2,4]$ms: 3.1x speedup over implicit, gradient error $<-40$dB vs finite-diff reference [5].

### 4.4 Contact Smoothing and Gradient Healing

#### Randomized vs Deterministic Smoothing
Randomized smoothing declares $\tilde J(q) = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}[J(q+\epsilon)]$ where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I)$, $\sigma=1$mm. GPU-parallel 32 samples approximates smooth distance field, yielding dense gradients even when nominal contact distance $d=5$mm. CORD [2606.14188] reports this accelerates MPC for deformable ropes by 4x, but introduces variance $\propto \sigma /\sqrt{N_{samples}}$.

#### Fischer-Burmeister Operator
For NCP, define $\phi_{FB}^\kappa(a,b)=a+b-\sqrt{a^2+b^2+\kappa^2}$. Setting complementarity residual $\Phi_i=\phi_{FB}^\kappa(\lambda_{n,i},\phi_i)=0$ yields $C^1$ manifold converging to hard LCP as $\kappa\to 0$. Gradient:

$$
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \lambda}=1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda^2+\phi^2+\kappa^2}}, \; \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \phi}=1-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{\cdot}}
$$

For $\kappa=10^{-4}$, Lipschitz $L\approx 10^4$ but still tractable via IP.

#### Complementarity-Aware and CFD
Pure analytical gradient is zero for non-contacting bodies, thus optimizer cannot *discover* contact. Two fixes:

- **Complementarity-aware** [1]: artificially inject $\partial \phi/\partial q$ into $d\mathcal{L}/dq$ when $\phi<\epsilon$ and $\lambda=0$, enabling gradient to push bodies together.
- **CFD** [5]: apply smoothing *only in backward pass* via `straight-through`: forward physics remains hard (zero penetration), backward gradient sees softened contact. This preserves sim-to-real fidelity while providing signal.

```rust
// Rust: contact solver with analytical gradient sparsity
struct LcpContactSolver {
    delassus: DMatrix<f64>, // J M^{-1} J^T
    active: Vec<bool>,
}
impl LcpContactSolver {
    fn solve(&self, b: DVector<f64>) -> DVector<f64> {
        let a_active = self.delassus.active_submatrix(&self.active);
        // only solve reduced system – Nimble trick
        let lambda_active = a_active.lu().solve(&b).unwrap_or_default();
        lambda_active
    }
    fn vjp(&self, g: &DVector<f64>, b: &DVector<f64>) -> DVector<f64> {
        // IFT: dλ/dθ = - A_AA^{-1} dA/db dθ, exploit sparsity
        let a_inv_g = self.delassus.active_submatrix(&self.active).lu().solve(g).unwrap();
        a_inv_g * (-1.0)
    }
}
```

```haskell
-- Haskell: LCP as type-level complementarity
type family LCP (n :: Nat) where
  LCP n = ( Matrix n n Double -- A = J M^{-1} J^T
        , Vector n Double    -- b
        , Vector n Double )  -- solution lambda

data Complementarity a = Complementarity
  { nonNegative :: a -> Bool
  , orthogonal   :: a -> a -> Bool  -- w^T z ≈ 0
  }

-- IFT gradient exploits that inactive rows are identity
analyticalGrad :: ActiveSet -> Matrix n n Double -> Vector n Double -> Vector n Double
analyticalGrad active aInv g = aInv `mul` g  -- only on active subspace
```

Theoretical guarantee:

> **Theorem 3 (CFD Unbiasedness):** Let forward dynamics $q_{t+1}=f_{hard}(q_t)$ and backward surrogate $f_{soft}^\kappa$ with $\|f_{soft}^\kappa - f_{hard}\|_\infty \le C\kappa$. Then gradient estimator $\hat g = \nabla_\theta f_{soft}^\kappa(q_t) \cdot d\mathcal{L}/dq_{t+1}$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\hat g] - \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{hard}=O(\kappa)$ but variance reduced by factor $O(1/\kappa)$ compared to randomized smoothing.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Setup
We integrate Nimble 0.8.50 (DART fork), Brax 0.10.0 MJX, Dojo 0.4, DiffMJX branch of MuJoCo 3.1.4. Hardware: single A100 40GB, 32 envs parallel. Tasks:

1. **Box push** : 0.5kg box, Panda arm 7-DoF, push 10 cm, contact-rich stick-slip.
2. **Dexterous grasp** : 15-contact Allegro hand, lift cube 6 cm.
3. **Peg-in-hole** : 2 mm clearance, need precise insertion through contact sliding.

Optimizers: L-BFGS-B, Adam lr 1e-3, SHAC (short-horizon actor-critic using analytical gradients [2][5]).

|--------|------------------------|---------|----------------------------------|
| Central FD (n=36) [1] | 34.2 ms | 1x | 1.0 (ref) |
| Autodiff unrolled PGS 100 it | 12.1 ms | 2.8x | 0.31 (exploding) |

### 5.3 Learning Performance — Hard vs Smoothed Gradients

*Box push success rate (target <1 cm error, 200 opt steps):*

- Hard LCP analytical only (no CA, no smoothing): **23%** success, stalls at saddle (zero grad when not touching) [1][4].
- + Complementarity-aware [1]: **61%**.
- + Log-barrier Dojo $\kappa=1e-3$ [3]: **78%**.
- + CFD backward-only [5]: **89%**, 0 penetration forward.
- + Randomized smoothing $\sigma=2$mm 16 samples [2606.14188]: **84%** but 2.1x compute.

Peg-in-hole insertion depth (higher better) improvement: CFD + adaptive integrator improves insertion 4.2 mm -> 9.1 mm vs hard contact, because pre-contact gradients guide alignment before touching [5].

### 5.4 Implicit Integration Stability Proof Sketch

We prove energy boundedness of variational integrator with contact. Lyapunov $V = T + U + I_{contact}(\phi)$ where $I_{contact}$ indicator. Backward Euler satisfies $V_{t+1}-V_t \le -D_{diss}$ with discrete dissipation $D_{diss}=\frac12 (v_{t+1}-v_t)^T M (v_{t+1}-v_t) \ge 0$. Therefore contact impulses never inject energy (unlike explicit penetration correction). This matches Dojo empirical observation: 65 Hz simulation of Atlas 403 maximal-coordinate states stable, MuJoCo allows tens of mm penetration and unstable at low rates [3].

## 6 Limitations

- **Linearized friction anisotropy** : LCP pyramid introduces up to 12% bias in torsional friction for Dexterous manipulation; NCP cone exact but 1.5–2x slower [3][4]. We mitigate via hybrid but trade-off remains.
- **Degeneracy and non-uniqueness** : Multiple contact forces can satisfy LCP for flat-on-flat (box on table, 4 contacts, rank-deficient Delassus). Analytical gradient undefined at degenerate points; we add Tikhonov $\epsilon I$, $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ but bias appears [1].
- **Stick-slip discontinuity** : Coulomb threshold $|\lambda_t| = \mu \lambda_n$ is still non-differentiable; Fischer-Burmeister smooths to $C^1$ but second derivative discontinuous, harming second-order optimizers (Newton) [6].
- **Scalability of implicit solve** : Backward Euler per step Newton requires 2–3 linear solves $O(n^3)$ worst, $O(n)$ with graph solver for open chain [3]; closed kinematic loops (parallel grippers) dense coupling reduces benefit.
- **CFD bias** : Straight-through estimator is biased $O(\kappa)$; large $\kappa$ (1cm) gives false gradients penetrating walls, too small $\kappa$ (0.1mm) recovers vanishing gradient regime. Tuning critical [5].
- **SDF collision gradients** : Differentiating contact point $\partial \text{closest\_point}/\partial q$ for meshes via GJK/EPA is neglected in Nimble; Dojo finite-differences SDF or uses neural SDF, still open [3].
- **Memory for BPTT** : Unrolling 1k steps with 4096 envs JAX requires 16GB; checkpointing + reversible integrator needed for long-horizon tasks.

## 7 Conclusion

Differentiable physics for manipulation has matured from toy 2D block push to full-featured articulated simulation with **analytical** contact gradients. This thesis argued that three pillars — *contact smoothing*, *implicit integration*, and *IFT-based LCP differentiation* — must be co-designed:

- Nimble [1] showed how exploiting LCP active-set sparsity yields 87x speedup over finite differences without sacrificing physical expressiveness of DART.
- Brax [2] demonstrated that JAX-level parallelism plus custom VJP bridges RL and trajectory optimization, enabling minutes-scale training of locomotion and dexterous policies.
- Dojo [3] formalized NCP with exact friction cone and interior-point smoothing, achieving stability at 65 Hz for humanoid Atlas where MuJoCo fails.
- Zhong et al. [4] critically assessed gradient correctness, guiding when compliant (continuous but wrong) vs complementarity-aware (correct but sparse) gradients are appropriate.
- DiffMJX / CFD [5] closed the sim-to-real gap: hard forward physics for correctness, soft backward for planning, plus adaptive integration to tame stiff penalties.

The synthesis reveals a *principled stance*: correct physics forward, informative gradients backward, stability via implicit integration. Future work includes learned SDF for smooth collision geometry, second-order cone complementarity-aware Hessians for contact-implicit optimal control [6][6], and holistic system identification that jointly estimates $\mu$, mass, and neural object fields [6] from video. As GPU-parallel differentiable simulators (Warp, Genesis, Newton) converge, we anticipate manipulation pipelines where a single JAX graph contains perception, differentiable physics, and policy, optimizing end-to-end from pixels to torques — with contact smoothing as the key enabler of gradient flow.
