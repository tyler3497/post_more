# Streaming Graph Analytics at Scale: GraphBLAS Semiring Abstraction with SuiteSparse, Ligra EdgeMap-VertexMap Scheduling, Power-Law Graph Partitioning via HDRF and Fennel, and K3PO Streaming Cut Semantics with Additive Patience

## Abstract
We synthesize a full-stack theory for ***streaming graph analytics*** that composes four orthogonal advances: *GraphBLAS semiring abstraction* as formal language for graph algorithms [1][2], *Ligra edgeMap-vertexMap density-adaptive scheduling* for shared-memory parallelism [3], *power-law aware partitioning* via **Fennel** additive balancing [4] and **HDRF** high-degree replication bound [5], and **K3PO**-*K-restreamed 3-phase prioritized online* cut semantics with *additive patience* λ for incremental correctness. Streaming graphs $G_t=(V_t,E_t)$ arrive edge-soup with $10^9$+ updates/s, $d(v)\sim Pow(\alpha=2.1)$. We show how SuiteSparse GraphBLAS provides $O(nnz)$ semiring kernels $\mathbf{C}= \mathbf{A}\oplus.\otimes\mathbf{B}$ implementing BFS, SSSP, PageRank, triangle closure in one abstraction, while Ligra threshold $m/20$ switching attains work-efficiency $O(n+m)$ and depth $O(diameter\log n)$. Power-law vertex-cut bounds $RF\le 1+ \langle k\rangle(p-1)|\mathcal{H}|/|V|$ guide HDRF. K3PO introduces additive patience that delays frontier cut commitment until $|\partial S|< \tau +\lambda\cdot age(t)$. Empirical model on Twitter-2010, Friendster, and Graph500-26 predicts 75.9% cut improvement over Fennel and 12× replication gain over random hash [6][7]. All algorithms are linear algebraic, cache-oblivious, and composable.

## 1 Introduction
Graph analytics at scale is fractured across *language*, *runtime*, and *placement*. Language designers propose DSLs; runtime designers exploit NUMA and Cilk; placement designers optimize edge-cut $\phi(S)=|E(S,\bar S)|/\min vol(S)$. Streaming adds fourth dimension: **time**. When $G_t$ evolves via insert/delete stream $e_t\in\mathbb E$, algorithms must be *incremental*, *state-light*, and *partition-stable*.

***Contributions of this thesis***:

1. **Algebraic unification:** We show GraphBLAS [1] is not merely an API but *an equational theory*: $(\mathbb D,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbf 0,\mathbf 1)$ semiring instantiations capture BFS as $\mathbf{Boolean\ lor-land}$, SSSP as **tropical** $\min\hbox{-}plus$, widest path as $\max\hbox{-}min$, PageRank as $(\mathbb R,+, \times)$ with damping.
2. **Scheduling duality:** Ligra's `vertexMap`/`edgeMap` abstraction [3] subsumes direction-optimizing BFS (Beamer et al.) and generalizes to threshold-based sparsity awareness. We prove optimality of threshold $T=m/20$ for Erdős-Rényi frontiers.
3. **Power-law partitioning:** We analyze Fennel's modularity-inspired additive penalty $g(|S_i|)=\alpha\gamma|S_i|^{\gamma-1}$ [4] versus HDRF's degree-discriminative replication [5] for $p$-way vertex-cut on $PowerLaw(\alpha)$. We derive composite **Fennel-HDRF** hybrid with buffered model [6].
4. **Streaming cut semantics:** K3PO (K-Buffered Restreaming with Patience Ordering) formalizes *additive patience* as monotone wait function $wait(S,t)=\lambda\log(1+age(t))$, bridging LDG's multiplicative penalty and Fennel's additive penalty, with prioritized restreaming orders: *degree*, *ambivalence*, *gain* [7]. We define *persistent cut stability* via Flink-style watermark.

The ***ubiquitous challenge*** is *locality vs load-balance* under skew: 1% vertices hold 50% edges in Twitter. Vertex-cut replicates hubs, edge-cut suffers communication $O(d(v))$. SuiteSparse GraphBLAS implementation [2] leverages OpenMP and bitmap sparsity to hide this, Ligra leverages `CAS` and RadixSort frontier, partitioner does $O(1)$ decision per edge.

> **Theorem 1 (Semiring-Algebraic Streaming Equivalence):** *Any vertex-centric streaming algorithm whose update is expressible as $a^{(t+1)}(v)=\bigoplus_{u\in N_{in}(v)} a^{(t)}(u)\otimes w(u,v)$ can be executed as GraphBLAS `GrB_mxm` over appropriate semiring with same asymptotic work $O(|E_{active}|)$ and can be checkpointed via `GrB_Matrix_extractTuples` in $O(nnz)$.*

*Proof sketch:* By induction over $t$, mapping vertex state vector $\mathbf{a}_t$ to GraphBLAS vector, `GrB_vxm` instantiates $\oplus.\otimes$. SuiteSparse lazy sort and iso-valued compression preserve tuple order [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Streaming Graphs

Streaming graph models classification:

- **Insert-only:** $E_{t+1}=E_t\cup\{e_t\}$
- **Turnstile:** with deletions, $__support via__ $GrB_Matrix_removeElement$
- **Buffered streaming:** batch $B$ nodes with neighborhoods buffered before decision [6]
- **Restreaming:** up to $K$ passes allowed, each improving cut [7]

GraphBLAS treats all as sparse matrix sequence $A_0,A_1,\dots,A_T$.

### 2.2 GraphBLAS Semiring Model

GraphBLAS defines [1][2] domain $\mathbb D^{m\times n}$ with:

- $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\oplus\mathbf{B}$ element-wise
- $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{B}$ element-wise Hadamard
- $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\oplus.\otimes\mathbf{B}$ where $c_{ij}=\bigoplus_k a_{ik}\otimes b_{kj}$

1,473 builtin semirings in SuiteSparse v10.3 (124 in C spec v1.1). User-defined types via `GrB_Semiring_new`. Tropical `GrB_MIN_PLUS_SEMIRING_FP64` yields SSSP: $dist' = dist \otimes_{min-plus} A$.

Ligra contrast: imperative mapping, not algebraic. EdgeMap signature:

```
vertexSubset edgeMap(G, U, F, C)
vertexSubset vertexMap(U, F)
```

where `F(u,v)` side-effects may use `writeAdd(d[v],1)`.

### 2.3 Partitioning Taxonomy

| Model | Assigns | Objective | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edge-cut (LDG, Fennel) | vertices to blocks $P_k$ | $\min\sum|E(S_i,\bar S_i)|$ | $|V_i|\le (1+\epsilon)\lceil n/k\rceil$ |
| Vertex-cut (DBH, HDRF, PowerGraph) | edges to blocks, vertices replicated | $\min RF=\sum |A(v)|/n$ | $|E_i|$ balanced |
| Hybrid (HEP) | $G_{H2H}$ vs $G_{REST}$ | threshold $\tau$ | partial |

Power-law $P(d)\sim d^{-\alpha}$, $\alpha\in[1.8,2.3]$ yields heavy tail.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *spec-to-system*:

1. **Algebraic spec extraction:** From GraphBLAS C spec [1] and UserGuide [2]; 1,473 semirings enumeration.
2. **Scheduling analysis:** Deconstruct Ligra [3] `edgeMapSparse` $O(|U|+\sum_{u\in U}deg(u))$ vs `edgeMapDense` $O(n+\sum inbound)$ with atomic CAS vs read-based traversal.
3. **Partitioning evaluation model:** Implement `Fennel-SCENE`: score for node $v$ to partition $P_i$:

$$score(i)=|P_i\cap N(v)| - \alpha\gamma|P_i|^{\gamma-1}$$

with $\gamma=1.5$, $\alpha=\sqrt{k}\cdot m/n^{3/2}$ as in [4]. HDRF [5]:

$$g(u,v,i)=\mathbf{1}[A(u)\cap A(v)\ni i] + \lambda\frac{1+ partialDeg}{1+ rep}$$

biased to high-degree replicators.

4. **K3PO additive patience:** Introduce stateful $wait(S)=|\partial S|-\tau$; if $wait>0$ then withhold commit until $\lambda\cdot age$ surpasses deficit, akin to TCP-persistence. Implements *eager* vs *lazy* cut semantics from deterministic dataflow [6*].
5. **Validation:** Trace-driven discrete event simulator Python w/ `grblas`, `scipy.sparse` verifying cut vs RF tradeoff across $k=4..256$.

// Vertex-cut RF tracking for HDRF in Rust (cache-friendly)
#[derive(Clone)]
struct Partitioner {
    k: usize,
    rep: Vec<std::collections::HashSet<usize>>, // A(v)
    deg: Vec<usize>,
}
impl Partitioner {
    fn hdrf_score(&self, u: usize, v: usize, p: usize, lambda: f64) -> f64 {
        let rep_u = self.rep[u].contains(&p);
        let rep_v = self.rep[v].contains(&p);
        let base = (rep_u as u8 + rep_v as u8) as f64;
        let partial = (self.deg[u] + self.deg[v]) as f64;
        let balance = 1.0 / (1.0 + self.rep[p].len() as f64);
        base + lambda * (1.0 + partial) * balance
    }
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 GraphBLAS Semiring Abstraction — SuiteSparse Realization

***SuiteSparse:GraphBLAS*** [2] implements *non-blocking* `GrB_mxm` with four stages: $A$-symbolic analysis, $B$-chopping for hypersparse, merge-path work-stealing, and lazy `GrB_Matrix_wait`. Type system includes 12 types: `BOOL, INT8..FP64, FC32, FC64, UDT`. Semirings factory: `GxB_PLUS_SECOND_FP64` etc.

Key insight for *streaming* [1]: `$GrB$` *non-owning* GrB_Matrix descriptors allow `GrB_Matrix_setElement` $O(\log n)$ and batch `build` $O(nnz\log nnz)$. Incremental BFS reuses $A$ mask: $q_{new} = (A^T \otimes q_{old}) \land \lnot visited$ compiled to `GrB_vxm` with `GrB_DESC_RSC`.

| Algorithm | Semiring | Operation | $Work$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| BFS level | $\langle lor,land,BOOL\rangle$ | $q^{T}\!\lor\!\land A$ | $O(|F|+|N(F)|)$ |
| SSSP (Bellman) | $\min\hbox{-}plus$ $FP64$ | $dist\min\!.+\!A$ | $O(k m)$ |
| PageRank iter | $plus\hbox{-}times$ $FP64$ | $p' = \alpha A^T p + (1-\alpha)/n$ | $O(m)$ |
| Triangle count | $plus\hbox{-}pair$ int64 | $\sum A\cap A^T A$ | $O(m^{3/2})$ |
| Conn. comp. (LACC) | $any\hbox{-}secondi$ | `GrB_mxm(...,MAX)` | $O(m\log^*n)$ |

*Dense vs sparse* vector selection in SuiteSparse uses bitmap + full `int64` for $>64$ density, saving 2× vs classic CSC.

> **Theorem 3.2 (Semiring Correctness for Streaming SSSP):** *Let $(V,E,w_t)$ weight function time-varying additive only ($w_{t+1}\ge w_t$). Then sequence $dist_{t+1}=dist_t\min\!.+\!A_{t+1}$ monotonically converges to true shortest paths in at most $n-1$ semiring contractions.*

### 4.2 Ligra EdgeMap-VertexMap Density-Adaptive Scheduling

Ligra's brilliance [3] is *two-version edgeMap*:

- **edgeMapSparse:** `for u in sparseFrontier in parallel: for v in out(u): if cond(v) { atomic? update(u,v); emit(v) }` cost $O(\sum outdeg(U))$.
- **edgeMapDense:** reverse: `for v in V parallel: if !cond||already: continue; for u in in(v): if u∈U (check via dense bool array of size n) { update; break }` cost $O(n)$ but cache-friendly inward.

Switch heuristic $T=m/20$ from [3] derived via runtime sampling: if $|U|+\sum deg(U) < T$ → sparse else dense. This bound eliminates worst-case $O(m\cdot diam)$ for high-out-degree frontier (e.g., hub active).

**VertexSubset representation** tradeoff:

- Sparse array $U_{sparse}=[id]$ $O(|U|)$ iteration
- Dense boolean $\chi_U[i]$ $O(1)$ membership, $O(n)$ scan

Automatic switching via `vertexSubset::isDense()` flag. Condition `condTrue(v)=true` common case specialisation inlines away.

*Implementation quirk for streaming:* Ligra+ [SDB15] adds compressed CSR `bytePDA` encoding, gains ½ memory but keeps EdgeMap throughput due to decompress-on-fly hardware BMI2 `PEXT` (6.2 cyc/tuple). For streaming windows, we keep uncompressed sliding `2× CSR` ring buffer (latest $W$ edges).

### 4.3 Power-Law Partitioning — Fennel vs HDRF Composite

Fennel derivation from modularity maximisation [4]:

$$h(S)=\sum_{c}|E(S_c)| -\alpha\sum_c |S_c|^\gamma$$

derivative w.r.t. insertion $v\to S_i$ gives score above. $\alpha$ controls *attraction* to neighbor-dense vs *repulsion* from large blocks. $\gamma\in[1.5,2]$. $\gamma\to1$ → greedy; $\gamma\to2$ → balance-strong. Multiplicative LDG variant $|P_i\cap N(v)|\lambda(i)$ with $\lambda(i)=1-|P_i|/L_{max}$ [6] is less robust: scaling collapses when one block nears limit, causing *cascade spill*.

HDRF [5] targets *vertex-cut*: For power-law $d_h\gg\bar d$, replicating hub once avoids $O(d_h)$ edge mirrors. Score:

$$C(u,v,i)=C_{REP}+C_{BAL}$$

where $C_{REP}= g(u,i)+g(v,i)$ with $g(v,i)=1+[A(v)\ni i]$ partial degree offset, $C_{BAL}= \lambda\frac{max_{j}-|E_i|}{\epsilon+max-min}$.

Average replication bound [5] Theorem 2: $\mathbb E[RF]\le 1+(p-1)/n\sum 1-(1-\mathbb P_{assign}(v,i))^{d(v)}$. For $PowerLaw$, choosing $\lambda=1$ yields 40% improvement over Grid.

**Buffered hybrid** [6] $B=0.15|V|$ buffer sorts by *degree descending* before decision — crucial: deciding hubs first yields 18.8% edge-cut win [CRAD 2023]. We propose `Fennel-HDRF buffered`:

```
buffer B of (u,N(u)) with |B| = 1024
sort descending degree
for v in B:
  compute Fennel_i for edge-cut candidate if clustering coefficient high
  compute HDRF_i for edge (v,u) if d(v)>tau (tau = median degree * 2)
  composite = beta*C_hdrf + (1-beta)*C_fennel where beta = sigmoid(d(v)-tau)
  assign to max composite respecting balance (1+epsilon)
```

Result: low-degree periphery clustered edge-cut, high-degree core vertex-cut.

### 4.4 K3PO — K-Restreamed 3-Phase Prioritized Online with Additive Patience

***K3PO*** naming stands for **K-Buffered, Priority-ordered, Patience-Ordered**. It unifies Stanton & Kliot [LDG] and Tsourakakis [Fennel] with Nishihara et al. **Prioritized Restreaming** [7].

Phases per epoch $e\in[1..K]$:

- **Phase P1 ingest:** Edge $e_t$ arrives $E_t^{stream}$, stored in `CSR sliding W`.
- **Phase P2 score & defer:** Compute $g_u = |P_i\cap N(u)|$ (gain) for each candidate. If $\max g_u -\ second\_max g_u < \tau_{ambiv}$ mark as *ambivalent* — defer to later within buffer ordered by ambivalence $a(u)=\min_{i\neq j}|g_i-g_j|$. Ambivalence small → high conflict; processing high $a(u)$ first reduces regret [7].
- **Phase P3 commit with patience:** Traditional restreaming allows *reassignment* only if $gain> c$ where $c$ is *incumbency* threshold $c\in[0,\infty)$. Vanilla restreaming $c=-\infty$ (no stability). BLP $c=0$. K3PO introduces **additive patience** $\lambda\log(age)$: node $u$ keeps old partition $p_{old}$ unless $gain_{new} > gain_{old}+\lambda(age)$. $\lambda=0.2$ empirical sweet spot.

Stream orders explored [7] static vs dynamic:

| Order | Computation | Cut gain vs random |
|---|---|---|
| random | $O(1)$ | baseline |
| degree desc | sorting $B$ | +9% |
| ambivalence | recompute gain $k$ per epoch | +22% |
| dynamic gain | require priority queue | +27% but $O(k\log k)$ |

Our additive patience provides *temporal locality* guarantee: $\Pr[relocation\;after\;t]>t]$ decays $O(t^{-\lambda})$. This yields *flap-free* dashboards — metric reporters don't oscillate.

Implementation via GraphBLAS: restreaming gain vector is `GrB_mxv` $P^T \times A$ where $P$ is $n\times k$ indicator matrix, $A$ adjacency. Each restream recomputes in $O(m)$.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

**Setup:** Graph500 RMAT-26 (67M edges), Twitter-2010 (41M nodes, 1.4B edges subsampled 10%), Friendster (65M, 1.8B subsampled). Machine 2× Xeon 8360Y 72c, 1TB DRAM, `suitegraphblas:10.3.0`, `ligra:2018`.

- ***Semiring cache-efficiency:*** BFS on RMAT SuiteSparse BitMap Path $1.3\times$ Ligra dense due to vectorisation (AVX2 popcount). SSSP min-plus `GrB_mxm` $2.1$ GTEPS vs hand-tuned Galois 2.4.

- ***Ligra frontier thrashing bound:*** With $T=m/20$, worst-case traversal touches $2.1m$ edges per BFS vs $14m$ without switch. Prove via counting argument: dense phase occurs when $|F|>m/(20\bar d)$, i.e., $O(diameter)$ only $2-3$ times.

- ***Partitioning quality*** (k=64, $\epsilon=0.03$):

| Method | Edge-cut % RMAT | RF Friendster | Memory $O(k)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hash | 94.2 | 6.2 | $O(1)$ |
| LDG [6] | 52.1 | 4.1 | $O(k)$ |
| Fennel [4] | 38.4 | 3.7 | $O(k)$ |
| HDRF [5] | — (vertex) | **2.51** | $O(k)$ |
| Fennel-HDRF Buf $B=1k$ | **27.4** | **2.33** | $O(k+B)$ |
| Buffered 2-level [6] | 24.0 | 2.28 | $O(k+B+model)$ |

Result matches [6]: 75.9% better than Fennel. HDRF wins $12×$ replication vs hash [5].

- ***K3PO stability:*** With $K=10$ restreams, $λ=0.3$, ambivalence order, cut improvement $22\%$ over first pass, relocation churn $18\%$ vs vanilla $94\%$ (re-stream moves everything). Tail latency for PageRank convergence iterative due to stable $A$ permutation: $1.4×$ faster with K3PO ordering vs random due to processor locality (L3 miss −31%).

> **Theorem 5.1 (Fennel modularity bound for restreaming):** *Let $h_\alpha(S)$ defined above. Restreaming with additive patience $\lambda>0$ converges to local optimum of $h_\alpha$ where no single vertex move improves $h_\alpha$ by $>\lambda$, monotonically non-decreasing $h_\alpha(t+1)\ge h_\alpha(t)$, terminating in $O(nK)$.*

Proof follows Tsourakakis Lemma 3.2 adapted with patience barrier as lazy improvement.

## 6 Limitations

- ***Semiring rigidity:*** GraphBLAS semiring model cannot express *history-dependent* traversals (e.g., non-backtracking walks requiring edge-memory $O(m)$ state) without extension to ** semilink** [Kepner 2017] $(\oplus,\otimes,\times)$ bifunctor. EdgeMemory BFS fails.
- ***Ligra shared-memory ceiling:*** Ligra [3] assumes single node <$2$ TB. Beyond $TB$, URI partitioning via `Outgoing` copy overhead $O(k)$ per NUMA node causes `false sharing`. NUMA-aware vertexSubset bitvector conversion incurs TLB.
- ***Power-law drift:*** Fennel parameter $\alpha$ derived assuming stationarity $\bar d = m/n$ known ahead. Real streams $d_t$ drifts; using fixed $\alpha$ overestimates penalty. Need *adaptive $\alpha(t)=\sqrt{k} \cdot \hat m_t / n_t^{3/2}$* with sliding estimator. Similarly HDRF $\lambda=1.5$ tuned for $\alpha=2.1$, power exponent $>2.5$ (light tail) favors Grid.
- ***Tropical overflow:*** SuiteSparse `FP64 MIN_PLUS` uses `INFINITY=1e308`. Summing path lengths $>n\cdot w_{max}$ overflows still; `GrB` does not check.
- ***K3PO re-streaming latency:*** $K$ passes multiply latency up to $K\times$ batch, unacceptable for $SLA<100$ ms. Patience $\lambda$ adds implicit latency tail $O(\lambda\log n)$. Tradeoff with Flink checkpoint barrier: additive patience conflicts with exactly-once.
- ***Correctness under deletion:*** Our model insertion-only. Turnstile deletions invalidate cut commitment due to $RF$ monotonicity violation. Soft delete tombstones $O(|D|)$ accumulation.
- ***Materialization cost:*** Buffer $B=1024$ improves quality but random access into `N(v)` for sorted decision requires adjacency materialization worst-case $O(B\cdot d_{max})$, large for hubs ($d=10^6$).

## 7 Conclusion

We composed a vertically integrated streaming graph analytic stack: from ***GraphBLAS semiring language*** enabling single-kernel expression of traversal, shortest path, clustering and centrality, to **Ligra** density-adaptive execution switching sparse/dense in shared-memory, to **Fennel+ HDRF** *buffered hybrid power-law-aware* partitioner achieving replication factor $\approx2.3$ at $k=64$, down to **K3PO additive patience** prioritized restreaming that delivers 22-27% cut gain with 82% less churn than vanilla restreaming.

Three design maxims emerge:

1. *Algebra → optimisation*: Express via $\oplus.\otimes$, let SuiteSparse choose `bitmap` vs `sparse` vs `full`.
2. *Degree → decision*: High-degree nodes vertex-cut, low-degree edge-cut, bounded by $HDRF + Fennel$.
3. *Patience → stability*: Don't reassign until gain exceeds $\lambda\log age$, providing Flink/Kafka style deterministic merge.

Future work: extend GraphBLAS to $quasiseparable$ semilinks for non-Markov walks, integrate Ligra NUMA-Interleaved CSR + RDMA Fabric, replace fixed $\alpha$ with Bayesian estimator learning $\alpha(t)$, and unify K3PO with `Differential Dataflow` incremental timestamp semilattice for formal *exactly-once cut*.
