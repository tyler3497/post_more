# Galois Deformations and Modularity Lifting: the Taylor–Wiles–Kisin Method and Serre's Conjecture

## Abstract
This thesis develops the modern theory of Galois deformations and modularity lifting, centered on the Taylor–Wiles method, its Kisin refinement, and its culminating applications to Serre's modularity conjecture and Fermat's Last Theorem. Beginning with the deformation functor of a residual Galois representation and the universal deformation ring $R$ parametrizing all its lifts, we explain how Taylor–Wiles patching turns a surjection $R \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}$ onto a Hecke algebra into an isomorphism, using auxiliary prime sets (Taylor–Wiles primes) that simultaneously trivialize the obstruction-theoretic defect and permit a limit over framing data. We then treat Kisin's decisive innovation: presenting the global deformation ring as an algebra over tensor products of local lifting rings, replacing geometric multiplicity-one inputs by $p$-adic Hodge theory via Breuil–Kisin modules and the Breuil–Mézard conjecture. Finally we show how these lifting theorems, together with potential modularity and prime-switching arguments, yield the Khare–Wintenberger theorem on Serre's conjecture: every odd irreducible mod $p$ representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ arises from a modular form of precisely predicted level and weight [4][5][6].

##1 Introduction

Few developments in modern mathematics rival the modularity lifting program: the circle of ideas through which Andrew Wiles, Richard Taylor, and their successors proved Fermat's Last Theorem and, eventually, Serre's modularity conjecture [2][4][5]. At its heart lies a deceptively simple comparison. On one side stands a **Galois deformation ring** $R$, a complete Noetherian local ring parametrizing, in a universal sense, all lifts of a fixed residual Galois representation $\bar{\rho}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{F})$ to characteristic-zero coefficient rings, subject to prescribed local conditions. On the other side stands a **Hecke algebra** $\mathbb{T}$, acting on spaces of modular forms whose attached Galois representations lift the same residual one. A natural surjection $R \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}$ records that modular lifts are a special class of all lifts. The dream is the identity $R = \mathbb{T}$: every lift satisfying the local conditions is modular.

The central obstacle is dimensional. The ring $R$ is constructed abstractly, and its Krull dimension is typically inaccessible from first principles, while $\mathbb{T}$ is known to be finite over the coefficient ring. Taylor and Wiles discovered in 1994–1995 that this asymmetry can be inverted by **patching**: one builds infinite towers of auxiliary level structures at carefully chosen primes and takes an organized limit in which both $R$ and $\mathbb{T}$ become modules over a common power-series ring $S_\infty$ of the *same* dimension [1]. Once that dimension equality is secured, a commutative-algebra criterion converts the surjection into an isomorphism.

This thesis gives a research-level account of the method in its mature form: the deformation formalism of Mazur and Ramakrishna [8], the original patching mechanism [1][2], Kisin's $p$-adic Hodge-theoretic refinement unlocking non-minimal and higher-weight cases [3], and the dialectic between lifting theorems and Serre's conjecture powering the Khare–Wintenberger proof [4][5][6].

##2 Background

### 2.1 Galois representations and modularity

Let $G_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ be the absolute Galois group of the rationals. A **$p$-adic Galois representation** is a continuous homomorphism $\rho: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_n(A)$, with $A$ a $p$-adic coefficient ring. For $n = 2$, canonical examples arise from elliptic curves via Tate modules; reducing modulo the maximal ideal yields the **residual representation** $\bar{\rho}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{F})$ with $\mathbb{F}$ finite of characteristic $p$.

The Langlands program predicts that $p$-adic representations of geometric origin are *automorphic*. The association from modular forms to Galois representations is a theorem (Deligne, Serre, and others); the converse — that a given representation is modular — is the modularity problem resolved systematically by the methods of this thesis.

### 2.2 Serre's conjecture

In 1987, Serre conjectured a precise reciprocity law [6]: every continuous, odd, absolutely irreducible $\bar{\rho}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ arises from a modular form, and he predicted the exact **level** $N(\bar{\rho})$ (the prime-to-$p$ Artin conductor, measuring ramification away from $p$) and **weight** $k(\bar{\rho})$ (an integer between $2$ and $p+1$ dictated by $\bar{\rho}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}}$ on inertia, distinguishing reducible, irreducible, très ramifiée, and peu ramifiée cases) of the eigenform.

Serre's conjecture reverses the Deligne–Serre construction with *sharp numerical predictions*. It implies an "$\epsilon$-conjecture" on level lowering: if $\bar{\rho}$ is modular of some level, it is modular of its Serre level. Proved by Ribet, this was precisely the bridge Wiles needed to connect modularity of elliptic curves to Fermat's Last Theorem [2].

### 2.3 Deformation theory à la Mazur

Fix $\bar{\rho}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{F})$, absolutely irreducible and odd. A **deformation** to a complete Noetherian local $\mathcal{O}$-algebra $A$ with residue field $\mathbb{F}$ is a strict equivalence class of continuous lifts reducing to $\bar{\rho}$. Mazur showed the deformation functor is representable by a **universal deformation ring** $R$ under mild hypotheses [8]. Imposing **local conditions** at a finite set $S$ of primes (restricting $G_{\mathbb{Q}_v}$-restrictions to specified quotients of local framed deformation rings) and fixing the determinant yields the universal ring $R_{\mathcal{S}}$ for a *global deformation problem* $\mathcal{S} = (\bar{\rho}, S, \chi, \mathcal{O}, \{D_v\})$ — the ring that lifting theorems compare against Hecke algebras [8].

> Theorem: *(Mazur).* If $\bar{\rho}$ is absolutely irreducible, the deformation functor is pro-representable by a complete Noetherian local $\mathcal{O}$-algebra $R_{\bar{\rho}}$ with residue field $\mathbb{F}$; framed deformations are represented by $R^\square_{\bar{\rho}}$, formally smooth over $R_{\bar{\rho}}$ of relative dimension $4$.

The tangent space of $R_{\bar{\rho}}$ is $H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},S}, \mathrm{ad}^0 \bar{\rho})$ and obstructions live in $H^2$. Global Euler characteristic and Poitou–Tate duality bound the expected dimension; the **defect** between the expected and guaranteed dimension is governed by local invariants at each prime. The Taylor–Wiles method is a machine for *annihilating this defect* by enlarging the ramification set with auxiliary primes [1].

##3 Methodology

The mature Taylor–Wiles–Kisin argument, as exposited in modern surveys [8], decomposes into five stages:

1. **Setup.** Choose $\bar{\rho}$ absolutely irreducible and odd, and modular ($\bar{\rho} \cong \bar{\rho}_f$ for some newform $f$) — the *residual modularity* hypothesis, now a theorem in the relevant generality [3][4][5]. Fix $S$ containing $p$ and all ramification, a determinant $\chi$, and local conditions $D_v$ compatible with modularity.
2. **The map $R \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}$.** Localize the Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}^S(\Gamma)_{\mathfrak{m}}$ at the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ corresponding to $\bar{\rho}$; interpolating eigenform representations gives $\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{T}^S(\Gamma)_{\mathfrak{m}})$. Verifying it satisfies the local conditions yields, by universality, a surjection $R_{\mathcal{S}} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}^S(\Gamma)_{\mathfrak{m}}$.
3. **Taylor–Wiles primes.** For each $n \ge 1$, select primes $Q_n$ with $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^n$ such that $\bar{\rho}(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ has *distinct eigenvalues*. At each such $q$, the tame principal-series framed deformation ring is a power series ring over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_q]$, with $\Delta_q$ the $p$-Sylow of $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Diamond operators generate $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_{Q_n}]$ acting on cohomology, with completely explicit local-global compatibility.
4. **Patching.** Form modules $M_n$ of modular forms with $Q_n$-augmented level over $R_{\mathcal{S}_{Q_n}} \hat{\otimes} \mathcal{O}[\Delta_{Q_n}]$; a diagonal limit gives a patched module $M_\infty$ over $R_\infty \hat{\otimes} S_\infty$ with $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_q]]$. Choosing $|Q_n| = q$ equal to the dual Selmer dimension makes $\dim S_\infty = \dim R_\infty$; freeness of $M_\infty$ over the regular ring $S_\infty$ then forces the surjection to have nilpotent kernel [1][8].
5. **Kisin's refinement.** Present $R_\infty$ as an algebra over the **local lifting ring** $R^{loc} = \hat{\bigotimes}_{v \in S} R_v$. The $R_v$ are shown, via Breuil–Kisin modules and local models, to be $\mathcal{O}$-flat and equidimensional of the correct dimension, so $\mathrm{Supp}(M_\infty)$ is a union of whole components — yielding $R^{red} \cong \mathbb{T}$ without multiplicity-one hypotheses [3].

The method's evolution:

| Era | Key innovation | Input required | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wiles (1994) | Patching with TW primes, minimal case | Multiplicity-one / Gorenstein hypotheses | $R^{min} \cong \mathbb{T}^{min}$ |
| Taylor–Wiles (1995) | Simultaneous minimal/non-minimal patching | Ihara's lemma or Taylor's avoidance | $R \cong \mathbb{T}$, non-minimal |
| Kisin (2009) | Local lifting rings; Breuil–Kisin modules | $p$-adic Hodge theory, BM conjecture | $R^{red} \cong \mathbb{T}$, higher weight |
| Khare–Wintenberger (2009) | Potential modularity + lifting | 3-5-7 switching, base change | Serre's conjecture [4][5] |

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Selmer groups and the defect

With $G_{\mathbb{Q},S}$ the Galois group of the maximal extension unramified outside $S$, local conditions choose subspaces $L_v \subset H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q}_v}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar{\rho})$, defining Selmer and dual Selmer groups $H^1_{\mathcal{S}}$, $H^1_{\mathcal{S}^\perp}$. The Greenberg–Wiles formula relates their dimensions through local Euler characteristics, governing the expected relative dimension of $R_{\mathcal{S}}$ over $\mathcal{O}$. Taylor–Wiles primes are chosen to kill the dual Selmer group: each $q \in Q_n$ contributes a one-dimensional local condition while the distinct-eigenvalue condition forces dual Selmer elements to vanish at $q$, so $H^1_{\mathcal{S}_{Q_n}^\perp} = 0$ for suitable $Q_n$ [1][8].

```python
def taylor_wiles_prime_count(dual_selmer_dim: int) -> int:
    """One auxiliary prime per dual Selmer dimension annihilates the obstruction."""
    return dual_selmer_dim

def patched_dimension(num_primes: int) -> int:
    """dim S_infty = 1 + framing variables + q; dim R_infty matches by construction."""
    return 1 + 4 + num_primes

q = taylor_wiles_prime_count(3)
print("TW primes:", q, "| dim S_infinity:", patched_dimension(q))
```

### 4.2 The local picture at a Taylor–Wiles prime

Let $q \equiv 1 \bmod p$ with $\bar{\rho}(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ having distinct eigenvalues $\alpha \ne \beta$. Then $\bar{\rho}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}}$ is a sum of two distinct unramified characters, and the tame principal-series framed deformation ring is

$$R_q^\square \cong \mathcal{O}[[X_1, \dots, X_4]] \hat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}} \mathcal{O}[\Delta_q],$$

with $\Delta_q$ the $p$-Sylow of $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$. The factor $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_q]$ is the local incarnation of diamond operators. Forms of $q$-power level localized at $\mathfrak{m}$ are free over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_q]$ — the multiplicity-one input at TW primes, from local Langlands for $GL_2$ plus Ihara-type lemmas [1][8]. Since $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^n$ gives $\Delta_q$ a quotient of order $p^n$, the group algebras $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_{Q_n}]$ approximate a power series ring ever more closely, and the patching limit replaces them by the honest $S_\infty$.

### 4.3 The patching limit and $R = \mathbb{T}$

For each $n$, the tuple $(R_n, M_n, S_n)$ — framed deformation ring, modular forms with $Q_n$-level, $S_n = \mathcal{O}[[y]]/((1+y_i)^{p^n}-1)$ — has bounded ranks, so a compactness argument extracts compatible truncations for infinitely many $n$ and takes the inverse limit:

$$S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[y_1, \dots, y_q]], \quad R_\infty \twoheadrightarrow R_{\mathcal{S}}, \quad M_\infty \text{ finite over } R_\infty,$$

with $M_\infty$ finite free over $S_\infty$ (minimal case) and $\dim S_\infty = \dim R_\infty$ by the numerical choice of $q$ [1].

> Theorem: *(Taylor–Wiles patching criterion).* Let $S_\infty$ be regular local, $R_\infty$ a complete local $S_\infty$-algebra with $\dim R_\infty = \dim S_\infty$, and $M_\infty$ a finite $R_\infty$-module free over $S_\infty$. Then $\mathrm{Supp}_{R_\infty}(M_\infty) = \mathrm{Spec}\,R_\infty$, and $R_\infty \to \mathbb{T}_\infty$ has nilpotent kernel; quotienting by the augmentation ideal yields $R_{\mathcal{S}}^{red} \cong \mathbb{T}$.

The depth argument is the crux: freeness over $S_\infty$ plus equal dimensions forces every minimal prime of $R_\infty$ into the support of $M_\infty$. Specializing at $\mathfrak{a} = (y_1, \dots, y_q)$ recovers $R_{\mathcal{S}} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}$ with nilpotent kernel — an isomorphism in the minimal case where $R_{\mathcal{S}}$ is $\mathcal{O}$-flat [1][2].

```haskell
-- Patching datum at finite level n; the diagonal limit exists by compactness
data PatchDatum = PatchDatum
  { ringR   :: String  -- R_n: framed deformation ring with Q_n level
  , moduleM :: String  -- M_n: modular forms, free over S_n
  , ringS   :: String  -- S_n: O[[y]] / ((1+y_i)^{p^n} - 1)
  }
-- Limit: R_infty ->> R_S, M_infty free over S_infty, dim R_infty = dim S_infty
```

### 4.4 Kisin's local-to-global presentation

Kisin restructured the argument so the fragile global freeness input is replaced by *local* $p$-adic Hodge theory [3]. The patched ring $R_\infty$ is presented as an algebra over

$$R^{loc} := \widehat{\bigotimes}_{v \in S} R_v, \qquad R_v = R^{\square, \chi}_{\bar{\rho}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_v}}}(\text{condition } D_v),$$

and the restriction map $R^{loc} \to R_\infty$ is surjective. The revolutionary content is the geometry of $R_p$: via moduli of **Breuil–Kisin modules** (finite free $\mathfrak{S} = W[[u]]$-modules with bounded-height Frobenius), Kisin resolves the flat deformation ring — its singularities are identified with **local models** of Shimura varieties for $Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p} GL_n$, known to be normal, Cohen–Macaulay, and $\mathcal{O}$-flat [3]. Hence each $R_v$ is $\mathcal{O}$-flat and equidimensional of the expected dimension (4 away from $p$, 5 at $p$), $R^{loc}$ and $R_\infty$ are equidimensional of dimension $\dim S_\infty$, and the support of $M_\infty$ is a union of whole components. The map $R_\infty \to \mathbb{T}_\infty$ then has nilpotent kernel *without any multiplicity-one hypothesis*, giving $R^{red} \cong \mathbb{T}$ for higher weights, non-minimal levels, and potentially Barsotti–Tate representations far beyond the original method's reach [3].

### 4.5 The Breuil–Mézard multiplicity formula

Kisin's method forged a bridge to a purely local conjecture of Breuil and Mézard [7]. For $\bar{r}: G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \to GL_2(\mathbb{F})$, let $R^\square_{\bar{r}}(\tau)$ parametrize potentially semistable lifts of inertial type $\tau$. Breuil–Mézard predicts the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of $R^\square_{\bar{r}}(\tau)/\varpi$ as

$$\mu_{gal}(\tau) = \sum_\sigma n_\sigma(\tau)\,\mu_\sigma(\bar{r}),$$

with $\sigma$ running over Serre weights, $n_\sigma(\tau)$ from the mod $p$ local Langlands correspondence, and $\mu_\sigma(\bar{r})$ intrinsic local invariants [7]. Kisin showed his patched formalism implies $\mu_{gal} \ge \mu_{aut}$, with equality iff $R_\infty[1/p] \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty[1/p]$ — so every representation-theoretic proof of the reverse inequality simultaneously yields a modularity lifting theorem [3][7]. This dictionary has since been geometrized by Emerton–Gee into cycles on moduli stacks of Galois representations.

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The modularity lifting theorem

The mature output of the method [3][8]:

> Theorem: *(Kisin).* Let $p \ge 5$ and $\rho: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p)$ be continuous irreducible, unramified outside finitely many primes, such that: (i) $\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}}$ is potentially Barsotti–Tate; (ii) $\bar{\rho}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)}}$ is absolutely irreducible with adequate image; (iii) $\bar{\rho} \cong \bar{\rho}_f$ for some modular form $f$. Then $\rho$ is modular.

The point of $\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{\mathcal{S}}$ defined by $\rho$ factors through $R^{red}_{\mathcal{S}} \cong \mathbb{T}$, whose points are modular by construction. The adequacy hypothesis [9] guarantees Taylor–Wiles primes via Chebotarev; residual modularity is discharged by Serre's conjecture in applications — a productive circularity, since lifting theorems prove new cases of Serre's conjecture, which feed stronger lifting theorems.

```tla+
Next == UNCHANGED <<dimS, dimR, free>>   \* invariant across all levels
Invariant == (dimR = dimS) /\ free
====
```

### 5.2 From lifting to Serre's conjecture

Khare and Wintenberger proved Serre's conjecture by induction on $p$ and the level, with lifting theorems as the engine [4][5]:

1. **Base cases.** Small weights and level $1$ via companion forms, weight shifting, and known Artin cases.
2. **Lifting to characteristic zero.** Construct a *minimal crystalline lift* $\rho$ of the predicted weight reducing to $\bar{\rho}$, using potential modularity (Taylor) plus Boeckle's deformation-ring lower bounds to descend.
3. **Killing ramification.** Auxiliary primes and the 3-5-7 switching trick move between residual characteristics via congruences, reducing to small Serre level.
4. **Applying lifting.** $\rho$ satisfies Kisin-style hypotheses; $\bar{\rho}$ is modular by the induction hypothesis, so $\rho$ is modular. Reducing mod $p$ exhibits $\bar{\rho}$ as modular, and level/weight lowering (Ribet, Diamond, Buzzard–Diamond–Jarvis) refines this to Serre's exact invariants [4][5].

Each step needs a lifting theorem for precise local conditions (crystalline of weight $k$, semistable, potentially Barsotti–Tate) — exactly what Kisin's $p$-adic Hodge-theoretic method supplies [3].

### 5.3 Quantitative content

The method computes, not merely asserts existence. The Breuil–Mézard multiplicities $\mu_{gal}(\tau)$ are *numbers* — Hilbert–Samuel multiplicities of explicit Cohen–Macaulay local rings — and their determination via patching resolved cases resisting purely local attack [7]. In the ordinary case, Hida theory describes $\mathbb{T}_\infty$ as finite free over Iwasawa algebra, and $R = \mathbb{T}$ identifies the universal ordinary deformation ring with it, controlling the eigencurve at classical points.

##6 Limitations

- **Residual modularity is input, not output.** Lifting theorems *propagate* modularity from characteristic $p$ to characteristic zero; they cannot create it. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ this input is Serre's conjecture itself [4][5], but over general number fields residual modularity remains largely conjectural, limiting the method's reach there.
- **Big image hypotheses.** Taylor–Wiles primes need large image — modern formulations use *adequacy* [9]. Dihedral, reducible, or small-image $\bar{\rho}$ fall outside, requiring Langlands–Tunnell or Skinner–Wiles techniques instead.
- **The $p = 2, 3$ barrier.** Small residue characteristics cause genuine trouble: the condition $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^n$ clashes with the scarce supply of distinct eigenvalues, adequacy fails often, and $2$-adic local rings exhibit pathologies. Extensions exist (Kisin's 2-adic results and later work) but $p = 2$ remains partially open.
- **Non-effectivity.** The patching limit is a compactness argument proving *existence* without construction — no algorithm extracts the modular form from the Galois representation. Scholze's perfectoid refinement is more canonical but still non-effective computationally.
- **Higher dimensions.** Generalizations to $GL_n$ and reductive groups [9] demand the stable trace formula, base change, and potential automorphy, with vastly more complicated local deformation theory; each new group needs a fresh analysis of its local lifting rings.

##7 Conclusion

The Taylor–Wiles–Kisin method turned modularity from miraculous coincidences into systematic science. Its arc: **deformation theory** recasts "is $\rho$ modular?" as a ring comparison $R \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}$; **Taylor–Wiles primes** equalize dimensions in a patching limit; **commutative algebra** converts the surjection into an isomorphism; **Kisin's $p$-adic Hodge theory** replaces fragile multiplicity inputs with robust local geometry via Breuil–Kisin modules; and the resulting **lifting theorems**, iterated through potential modularity and prime-switching, prove **Serre's conjecture** [1][2][3][4][5][6].

The unity is striking: a local multiplicity conjecture (Breuil–Mézard [7]) becomes equivalent, via patching, to global modularity; Serre's elementary-looking level-and-weight recipe [6] is vindicated by the deepest $p$-adic Hodge theory. The method keeps evolving — Scholze's perfectoid patching, the Emerton–Gee geometrization, Calegari–Geraghty extensions — but the Taylor–Wiles insight endures: when a surjection is paired with an engineered limit making dimensions agree, the limit forces coincidence, and arithmetic follows.
