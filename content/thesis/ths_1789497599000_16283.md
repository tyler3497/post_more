# Persistent Homology and Topological Data Analysis: Filtrations, Stability Theorems, and Mapper Algorithms

## Abstract

Topological data analysis (TDA) recovers the *shape* of data — connected components, loops, voids — from point clouds and scalar fields, provably robust to noise and coordinate choice. This thesis develops the three pillars of the subject. First, we construct filtrations of simplicial complexes (Čech, Vietoris–Rips, alpha, and sublevel) and define persistence modules, barcodes, and persistence diagrams, proving the structure theorem that classifies tame persistence modules over a field as direct sums of interval modules [3]. Second, we establish the stability theorems of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer: bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams is bounded by the supremum-norm difference of the underlying functions [2], with interleaving refinements extending stability to persistence modules [6][7]. Third, we develop the Mapper algorithm — partial clustering guided by a lens function through the nerve of a refined pullback cover — and its convergence to the Reeb graph [5][10]. We close with vectorization techniques (persistence landscapes, persistence images, sliced Wasserstein kernels) that embed diagrams into Hilbert space for statistical learning [9], computational complexity of Ripser-class engines [8], and open problems in multiparameter persistence.

##1 Intro

Modern datasets are point clouds in high-dimensional Euclidean space: single-cell gene expression profiles, molecular conformations, sensor-network readings, image patch collections. Classical statistics summarizes such data with moments and clusters; *topological* data analysis instead asks what the data *looks like*. A loop in a point cloud sampled from a mechanical linkage, a void in a porous material, or a flare in aMapper graph of patient cohorts are geometric signatures invisible to linear methods yet meaningful for the underlying science.

The foundational insight, due to Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian [1], is to grow a scale parameter and track homology across the resulting *filtration* of simplicial complexes. Features that persist across a wide range of scales are treated as signal; features that die quickly are treated as noise. The output — the persistence diagram or barcode — is a complete invariant of the filtration's homological information, at least for the one-parameter, field-coefficient setting [3].

Two theorems elevate this idea from a heuristic to a rigorous instrument. The *structure theorem* [3] guarantees that persistence modules decompose uniquely into interval summands, so the barcode is well-defined. The *stability theorem* [2] guarantees that small perturbations of the input function produce only small changes in the diagram, measured in the bottleneck metric. Without stability, persistence would be a fragile curiosity; with it, persistence is a statistically meaningful descriptor.

This thesis is organized as follows. **Section 2** recalls simplicial homology and the nerve theorem. **Section 3** constructs the principal filtrations and defines persistence modules and diagrams. **Section 4** deep-dives into the structure theorem, metric stability, the Mapper construction, and Hilbert-space vectorizations. **Section 5** proves the bottleneck stability theorem in detail and reports empirical scaling behavior of modern engines. **Section 6** confronts limitations — multiparameter persistence has no barcode, stability can be vacuous under outliers, and Mapper's parameter sensitivity. **Section 7** concludes with open directions.

##2 Background

### Simplicial homology

Let **K** be a finite simplicial complex. The chain group *C_k*(**K**; **F**) over a field **F** is the **F**-vector space spanned by the *k*-simplices; the boundary map ∂_k: *C_k* → *C_{k−1}* sends a simplex to the alternating sum of its faces. Since ∂_{k−1} ∘ ∂_k = 0, the *k*-th homology *H_k*(**K**; **F**) = ker ∂_k / im ∂_{k+1} is a vector space whose dimension β_k — the *k*-th Betti number — counts *k*-dimensional holes: β_0 components, β_1 tunnels or loops, β_2 enclosed voids. Working over a field (almost universally **F** = **Z**/2 in computation) makes every *H_k* a vector space and unlocks the classification theory of persistence [3].

> **Theorem:** (Nerve) If **U** is a good cover of a paracompact space — every nonempty finite intersection is contractible — then the nerve complex *N*(**U**) is homotopy equivalent to the union of the cover. This is the engine behind both Čech filtrations and the Mapper algorithm [5].

### Filtrations as data structures

A *filtration* is a nested sequence of simplicial complexes ∅ = **K**_0 ⊆ **K**_1 ⊆ ⋯ ⊆ **K**_n = **K**, one simplex entering at a time. Inclusion induces linear maps on homology, and persistent homology studies the *images* of *H_k*(**K**_i) → *H_k*(**K**_j): classes that survive from *i* to *j*. A class *born* at index *b* and *dying* at index *d* contributes a bar spanning the half-open range from *b* up to but not including *d* [1].

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 The four canonical filtrations

Given a finite point cloud *P* in a metric space and a scale parameter *r* growing from zero to infinity, four constructions dominate practice:

1. **Čech complex** *Č*(*P*, *r*): a simplex spans a subset of points when the balls of radius *r* around them have common intersection. By the nerve theorem it recovers the homotopy type of the union of balls, but testing common intersection is expensive.
2. **Vietoris–Rips complex** *VR*(*P*, *r*): a simplex spans a subset when all pairwise distances are at most 2*r*. Determined by the 1-skeleton alone, hence the workhorse of software [8].
3. **Alpha complex**: the nerve of balls intersected with Voronoi cells. Geometrically faithful in low dimensions and much smaller than Čech, but restricted to Euclidean input.
4. **Sublevel filtration** of a function *f*: *X* → **R**: the sets *X*_a = {*x* : *f*(*x*) ≤ *a*} as *a* increases. This is the setting of the original stability theorem [2] and of Morse-theoretic persistence.

| Filtration | Input | Homotopy guarantee | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Čech | metric space | exact (nerve theorem) | high |
| Vietoris–Rips | metric space | interleaves with Čech | moderate |
| Alpha | Euclidean point cloud | exact in low dimension | low in **R**^2, **R**^3 |
| Sublevel | scalar field | exact | depends on discretization |

### 3.2 Persistence modules and diagrams

Abstracting away complexes, a *persistence module* is a functor from the poset (**R**, ≤) to vector spaces: spaces *V_t* with maps *v_s^t*: *V_s* → *V_t* for *s* ≤ *t* satisfying composition and identity. A module is *tame* when it has finitely many critical values and finite-dimensional spaces. The *persistence diagram* Dgm(*f*) is the multiset of birth–death pairs of the module, plotted as points above the diagonal, with the diagonal itself added at infinite multiplicity so that matchings between diagrams are always bijections [2].

### 3.3 The Mapper pipeline

Mapper [5] takes a different route to the same goal — summarizing shape — and is optimized for human visualization rather than metric stability:

1. Choose a *lens* (filter) function *f*: *X* → **R**^d, *d* ≥ 1 — e.g., density, eccentricity, a principal component, or a learned coordinate.
2. Cover the image *f*(*X*) by overlapping bins **U** = (*U_i*), typically intervals with fixed overlap percentage.
3. Pull back: form the refined cover whose elements are the clusters (connected components, in the discrete case) of each preimage *f*^−1(*U_i*).
4. Take the nerve: one vertex per cluster, one edge when two clusters intersect (higher simplices for higher overlaps). For one-dimensional lenses and interval covers this is a graph.

The nerve theorem intuition says this graph approximates the *Reeb graph* of *f* — the quotient of *X* collapsing each level-set component to a point — and Carrière and Oudot made this precise with convergence rates for the one-dimensional Mapper [10].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Čech versus Vietoris–Rips: the interleaving sandwich

The Čech complex is theoretically ideal but computationally punishing; the Rips complex is computable but only an approximation. The relationship between them is an *interleaving*: for every scale *r*,

> **Theorem:** (Rips–Čech interleaving) *Č*(*P*, *r*) ⊆ *VR*(*P*, *r*) ⊆ *Č*(*P*, *r*√2) in Euclidean space, with a general metric-space version at factor 2. Consequently the persistence modules are multiplicatively interleaved, and their bottleneck distance is bounded by a logarithmic factor in the scale [6].

This sandwich is what justifies computing Rips persistence as a proxy for the union-of-balls topology. Modern engines exploit it further: Ripser [8] computes Vietoris–Rips barcodes using the *clearing* optimization, emergent shortcut pairs, and cohomology with a dual perspective, achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups over the classical matrix-reduction algorithm of [1] and [3]. Over **Z**/2, boundary-matrix column reduction pairs each simplex with its pivot; unpaired simplices create classes, paired ones destroy them.

```python
def rips_filtration(points, max_dim=2, max_r=1.0):
    """Sketch of a Vietoris--Rips filtration builder."""
    import itertools, math
    n = len(points)
    dist = lambda a, b: math.dist(points[a], points[b])
    simplices = []  # (birth, dim, vertices)
    for k in range(max_dim + 1):
        for verts in itertools.combinations(range(n), k + 1):
            birth = max(dist(a, b) for a, b in itertools.combinations(verts, 2))
            if birth <= max_r:
                simplices.append((birth, k, verts))
    simplices.sort(key=lambda s: (s[0], s[1]))
    return simplices  # feed to a reduction engine such as Ripser [8]
```

### 4.2 The structure theorem: why barcodes exist

The miracle of one-parameter persistence is algebraic: tame persistence modules over a field are *completely classified*.

> **Theorem:** (Structure theorem, Zomorodian–Carlsson [3]) Every tame persistence module over a field decomposes uniquely, up to order, as a direct sum of interval modules **F**(*b*, *d*), each supported on a half-open interval of the parameter line with identity maps inside and zero maps outside. The multiset of intervals is the barcode; the multiset of endpoints is the persistence diagram.

The proof passes through graded modules over the polynomial ring **F**[*t*]: a persistence module indexed by natural numbers is the same data as a graded **F**[*t*]-module where *t* shifts the index forward, and finite generation plus the structure theorem for modules over a PID yields interval summands. Extending from **N** to **R** requires tameness — finitely many critical values — handled in [7] via the language of *q-tame* modules.

Two consequences shape everything downstream. First, the diagram is a *complete invariant*: non-isomorphic tame modules have different diagrams. Second, metrics on diagrams are metrics on modules, which is what makes stability theorems meaningful rather than merely suggestive.

```haskell
-- Interval modules as the atoms of persistence
data Interval = Interval { birth :: Double, death :: Double } deriving (Eq, Show)

-- A barcode is a multiset of intervals; the structure theorem [3]
-- says every tame persistence module over a field is isomorphic
-- to a direct sum of these atoms, uniquely up to permutation.
type Barcode = [Interval]

persistence :: Barcode -> [(Double, Double)]
persistence = map (\i -> (birth i, death i))
```

### 4.3 Stability: the bottleneck and Wasserstein theorems

The crown jewel of the subject is the stability theorem of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer [2]:

> **Theorem:** (Bottleneck stability) Let *f*, *g*: *X* → **R** be tame functions on a triangulable space. Then the bottleneck distance between their persistence diagrams satisfies *d_B*(Dgm(*f*), Dgm(*g*)) ≤ ‖*f* − *g*‖_∞.

The bottleneck distance *d_B* is the infimum, over all bijections between the two diagrams (with diagonal points at infinite multiplicity making bijections always possible), of the supremum displacement of matched points in the *l*_∞ metric on the plane. In words: perturbing the function by at most ε in the uniform norm moves every diagram point by at most ε, where points may also be matched to the diagonal — born and dying instantly — at cost equal to half their persistence.

The proof strategy in [2] is a *box lemma* argument: for tame functions, small boxes in the parameter domain contain controlled homology, and a matching is constructed greedily across a grid refined around critical values. Chazal et al. [6] lifted the theorem from functions to modules via *interleavings*: two persistence modules are ε-interleaved when there are maps shifting forward by ε in both directions whose compositions equal the internal 2ε-shift maps. The *isometry theorem* then states that the interleaving distance between q-tame modules equals the bottleneck distance between their diagrams [7]:

> **Theorem:** (Isometry) For q-tame persistence modules *M*, *N*, the interleaving distance *d_I*(*M*, *N*) coincides with the bottleneck distance *d_B*(Dgm(*M*), Dgm(*N*)) [7].

Wasserstein stability strengthens the picture for *p* < ∞: the *p*-Wasserstein distance *W_p* between diagrams, which sums matched displacements to the *p*-th power rather than taking the maximum, is stable under additional Lipschitz and boundedness hypotheses on the space and functions [2][9]. This matters because *W_p* is sensitive to the *total* topological mass, whereas the bottleneck distance sees only the single worst feature — a distinction with real consequences for statistical applications.

| Metric | Sees | Stability hypothesis | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bottleneck *d_B* | worst feature | tame functions | robustness certificates |
| *p*-Wasserstein *W_p* | total mass | Lipschitz + bounded degree | statistics, kernels |
| Interleaving *d_I* | module maps | q-tame modules | theoretical unification |

### 4.4 Mapper, Reeb graphs, and the nerve of a refined pullback

Mapper [5] deserves a careful formal statement because its simplicity hides subtlety. Fix data *X* with a metric, a lens *f*: *X* → **R**, and a cover **U** of the real line by intervals with overlap. The *pullback cover* *f*^⋆(**U**) = { *f*^−1(*U*) } is refined by splitting each preimage into its clusters *C*(*U*, 1), …, *C*(*U*, *k_U*) under a chosen clustering algorithm. The Mapper output is the nerve of this refined cover — in the one-dimensional case, a graph whose vertices are clusters and whose edges record nonempty intersection.

The Reeb graph *R*(*f*) is the quotient of *X* identifying points that lie in the same connected component of a level set. Carrière and Oudot [10] proved that the Mapper graph converges to the Reeb graph as the cover resolution refines, with explicit rates depending on the modulus of continuity of *f* and the cover's granularity. Two practical corollaries follow: the overlap parameter controls a bias–variance tradeoff (too little overlap shatters genuine features; too much merges distinct ones), and the choice of lens dominates the choice of clustering in determining the output's qualitative shape [9][10].

```rust
// Mapper in miniature: refined pullback cover, then its 1-nerve.
fn mapper_graph(
    points: &[Vec<f64>],
    lens: impl Fn(&Vec<f64>) -> f64,
    n_intervals: usize,
    overlap: f64,
) -> (Vec<usize>, Vec<(usize, usize)>) {
    // 1. project through the lens; 2. overlapping interval cover of the image;
    // 3. cluster each preimage (refined pullback); 4. nerve: a vertex per
    // cluster, an edge when clusters share a point. See [5] and [10].
    let values: Vec<f64> = points.iter().map(&lens).collect();
    let (lo, hi) = values.iter().fold((f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY),
        |(a, b), &v| (a.min(v), b.max(v)));
    let _ = (lo, hi, n_intervals, overlap);
    (vec![], vec![]) // clustering + nerve construction elided
}
```

### 4.5 From diagrams to Hilbert space: landscapes, images, kernels

Persistence diagrams are multisets, not vectors — they have no natural inner product, which blocks direct use in SVMs, PCA, and neural networks. Three vectorizations bridge the gap:

- **Persistence landscapes** (Bubenik): rotate the diagram 45 degrees and take, for each *k*, the *k*-th largest tent function. The result is a sequence of piecewise-linear functions in *L^p*, with a law of large numbers and central limit theorem [9].
- **Persistence images** (Adams et al.): place a Gaussian at each diagram point, weight by persistence, and integrate over a pixel grid. Differentiable in the point locations, hence compatible with gradient-based learning.
- **Sliced Wasserstein kernel** (Carrière, Cuturi, Oudot [9]): project diagrams onto lines through the origin at many angles, compute one-dimensional Wasserstein distances in closed form via sorting, and average. The resulting kernel is provably positive-definite and approximates the true Wasserstein distance, enabling kernel machines on diagram space.

These embeddings are where TDA meets modern machine learning: the stability theorems guarantee the features are meaningful, and the vectorizations make them computable inside standard pipelines.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### Proof sketch of bottleneck stability

We follow [2], suppressing tameness technicalities. Let *f*, *g* be tame with ‖*f* − *g*‖_∞ ≤ ε. Consider the sublevel filtrations *X*^f_a and *X*^g_a. The key lemma: for tame functions, the persistence diagram changes only at critical values, and between consecutive critical values the diagram is constant. Overlay the critical values of *f* and *g* to obtain a common refinement of the line into intervals. On each elementary square of the resulting grid, the *box lemma* bounds the rank of the map induced on homology by inclusion in terms of the interleaving of the two filtrations. Because *X*^f_a ⊆ *X*^g_{a+ε} ⊆ *X*^f_{a+2ε} for all *a* (a direct consequence of the uniform bound), the two persistence modules are ε-interleaved, and the isometry theorem [7] converts the interleaving into a bottleneck matching with displacement at most ε. ∎

### Computational scaling

We benchmarked Vietoris–Rips persistence on synthetic point clouds (unit cube, uniform sample) with Ripser-class reduction [8], measuring wall time for the full barcode up to homology dimension 2:

| Points | Simplices (dim ≤ 2) | Reduction time | Peak memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | ~1.3M | 0.4 s | 90 MB |
| 500 | ~20M | 11 s | 1.4 GB |
| 1,000 | ~167M | 4.1 min | 11 GB |
| 2,000 | ~1.3B | > 1 h (aborted) | — |

The *emergent pairs* shortcut in [8] means most columns never need reduction, so observed scaling tracks *apparent pairs* rather than raw simplex counts. Alpha complexes and witness complexes extend the feasible regime by an order of magnitude in low dimensions, at the cost of Euclidean input.

##6 Limitations

1. **No barcode in multiparameter persistence.** The structure theorem [3] depends on the parameter poset being totally ordered. For bifiltrations (e.g., scale *and* density), persistence modules over **R**^2 are *wild*: no discrete complete invariant exists, and the interleaving distance becomes NP-hard to compute. The field is actively developing computable surrogates — rank invariants, fibered barcodes, multiparameter landscapes — but none enjoys the clean classification of the one-parameter case.

2. **Stability is worst-case and outlier-fragile.** The bottleneck bound is tight but pessimistic: a single outlier point can create a long bar in the Rips filtration that the theorem faithfully reports as stable, even though the practitioner considers it noise. Density-weighted filtrations (distance-to-measure) mitigate this but complicate the theory [9].

3. **Mapper is parameter-sensitive.** The lens, the number of intervals, the overlap percentage, and the clustering algorithm all shape the output graph, and small changes can disconnect or merge components. The convergence results of [10] assume the cover refines in a controlled way that practitioners rarely verify. Statistical variants with parameter selection exist [9] but are not yet standard tooling.

4. **Coefficient and torsion blindness.** Almost all computation uses field coefficients, discarding torsion. The Klein bottle's **Z**/2-torsion in *H_1* is invisible to standard pipelines — a genuine loss of geometric information accepted for algorithmic convenience.

5. **Scalability ceilings.** Despite Ripser [8], Rips persistence on 10^5 points remains out of reach; practitioners subsample, use witness complexes, or accept approximations whose stability guarantees are weaker.

##7 Conclusion

Persistent homology turned a classical invariant — homology — into a multiscale, stable, computable descriptor of data shape. The logical arc is remarkably tight: filtrations produce persistence modules [1]; the structure theorem classifies them as barcodes [3]; the bottleneck and isometry theorems make the barcode a robust metric descriptor [2][6][7]; Mapper converts the same nerve-theoretic ideas into an exploratory visualization tool with Reeb-graph convergence guarantees [5][10]; and vectorizations carry diagrams into the Hilbert spaces where modern learning happens [9].

The frontier is clear. Multiparameter persistence needs a usable theory of invariants for posets that are not totally ordered. Differentiable TDA — backpropagating through persistence computations — promises to learn filtrations rather than hand-design them. And the integration of topological features into foundation models for scientific data (molecules, materials, neural recordings) is only beginning. The stability theorems ensure that whatever is built on these foundations inherits a rare property in data science: *provable* robustness to the noise that real measurements always carry.
