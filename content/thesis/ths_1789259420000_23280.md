# Sheaf Cohomology and Derived Functors: From Presheaves to Derived Categories and Homological Mirror Symmetry

## Abstract

Sheaf cohomology is the precise instrument that measures the obstruction to gluing local geometric data into global objects. This survey develops the subject from first principles — presheaves, the sheaf axiom, sheafification, and stalks — through the classical exemplars of the structure sheaf, constant sheaves, and skyscraper sheaves, to the Grothendieck–Verdier theory of right derived functors on the abelian category of sheaves. We construct sheaf cohomology via injective resolutions, compare it with Čech cohomology, explain acyclic sheaves and the Leray spectral sequence, and pass to the derived category with its triangulated structure and Serre duality on projective varieties. The article closes with the modern horizon: Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture, which identifies the derived category of coherent sheaves on a Calabi–Yau manifold with the derived Fukaya category of its mirror [1][6]. The exposition assumes only basic homological algebra and algebraic geometry at the level of Hartshorne [4] and Weibel [5].

---

## 1 Introduction

Few problems recur in geometry as insistently as this one: given data defined locally, when can it be glued into a global object, and what is the *obstruction* when it cannot? The sheaf, invented by Jean Leray in the 1940s and given definitive form by Henri Cartan and Jean-Pierre Serre, is the framework in which this question becomes precise. A sheaf assigns to every open set the data living over it, together with restriction maps, subject to an axiom: compatible local sections glue uniquely [4].

But the global-sections functor F ↦ F(X) is only left exact. Applied to a short exact sequence of sheaves, it preserves kernels but not cokernels; the failure is exactly what cohomology measures. Alexander Grothendieck's 1957 Tôhoku paper recast sheaf cohomology as the *right derived functor* of global sections on the abelian category of sheaves, unifying sheaf, singular, group, and Lie algebra cohomology under one homological machine [2]. The systematic account of that machine — derived functors, δ-functors, spectral sequences — is the subject of Weibel's graduate text [5], while its geometric realization on schemes is codified in Hartshorne's Chapter III [4].

Three further steps transform the classical picture into the modern one. First, derived categories, constructed by Verdier following Grothendieck, invert quasi-isomorphisms of complexes and promote complexes themselves to the objects of study. Second, the Grothendieck–Leray spectral sequence and Serre duality organize cohomology into a calculus of derived functors between derived categories. Third, Kontsevich's 1994 conjecture of *homological mirror symmetry* proposes that this categorical apparatus is not merely descriptive but structural: for a mirror pair of Calabi–Yau manifolds X, Y, the derived category of coherent sheaves on X should be equivalent to the derived Fukaya category of Y [1]. The Fukaya category — built from Lagrangian submanifolds with morphisms given by Floer cochain complexes carrying an A_∞-structure [7] — is the symplectic mirror of the algebro-geometric B-model.

This survey traces the complete arc: presheaves and sheafification; the sheaf axiom and stalks; the canonical examples; derived functors and sheaf cohomology; Čech cohomology and the agreement theorems; acyclic sheaves and the Leray spectral sequence; the derived category, triangulated structure, and Serre duality; coherent sheaves on projective space; and the HMS outlook. We emphasize theorems that can be stated precisely and proved by the machinery itself.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Abelian categories and complexes

We work in abelian categories A: additive categories with kernels, cokernels, and the property that every monomorphism is a kernel and every epimorphism a cokernel. A chain complex C^• is a sequence ··· → C^n →^{d^n} C^{n+1} → ··· with d^{n+1}∘d^n = 0; its cohomology H^n(C^•) = ker d^n / im d^{n-1} measures deviation from exactness. Two complexes related by a chain map inducing isomorphisms on all cohomology are *quasi-isomorphic*. The homotopy category K(A) identifies chain maps up to homotopy; the derived category D(A) goes further and formally inverts quasi-isomorphisms [3].

A functor F: A → B between abelian categories is *left exact* if it preserves kernels and *right exact* if it preserves cokernels. The functors of sheaf theory are typically left exact, and derived functors exist to repair their failure to be exact. Recall the central players:

| Functor | Variance | Derived functors |
|---|---|---|
| Γ(X, −): Ab(X) → Ab, global sections | covariant, left exact | sheaf cohomology H^i(X, −) |
| f_∗: Ab(X) → Ab(Y), direct image | covariant, left exact | higher direct images R^i f_∗ |
| Hom_A(−, M), contravariant | left exact | Ext^i(−, M) |
| Hom_A(M, −), covariant | left exact | Ext^i(M, −) |

A *universal δ-functor* is a sequence of functors T^i: A → B with i ≥ 0, together with connecting homomorphisms δ: T^i(C) → T^{i+1}(A) for every short exact sequence 0 → A → B → C → 0, forming long exact sequences and universal among such data. Grothendieck's fundamental insight: the right derived functors R^i F of a left exact functor F form the universal δ-functor extending F [3][5].

### 2.2 Enough injectives

An object I in A is *injective* if Hom(−, I) is exact. A has *enough injectives* when every object embeds into an injective. Grothendieck proved that categories of sheaves of modules on a site — in particular Ab(X), the abelian sheaves on a topological space, and Mod(O_X), sheaves of modules over a ringed space — are *Grothendieck categories* and hence have enough injectives [2]. This single fact is the foundation on which all sheaf cohomology rests.

> Theorem: The category Ab(X) of abelian sheaves on a topological space X has enough injectives. Consequently every sheaf admits an injective resolution F → I^•, and sheaf cohomology is well defined as the cohomology of Γ(X, I^•) [2][5].

## 3 Methodology

Our method throughout is *derived-functor calculus*, executed in three movements.

**First: resolution.** To compute R^i F(A), choose an injective resolution 0 → A → I^0 → I^1 → ···, apply F, and take cohomology: R^i F(A) = H^i(F(I^•)). Independence of the chosen resolution follows from the comparison theorem for injective resolutions and the fact that injectives are F-acyclic [5]. In sheaf theory the canonical choice is the *Godement resolution*: G^0(F) = ∏_{x∈X} (i_x)_∗ F_x, the product over all points of skyscraper sheaves of stalks, with G^{n+1}(F) built inductively from cokernels — flasque by construction, functorial, and exact on stalks [2].

**Second: comparison with Čech.** While injective resolutions are theoretically decisive, they are computationally unwieldy. The Čech complex Č^•(U, F) for an open cover U is an explicit cochain complex whose cohomology agrees with derived-functor cohomology under mild hypotheses (Leray's theorem; the Stacks Project agreement theorem [2]). We use Čech cohomology as the bridge between abstract derived functors and concrete computation.

**Third: passage to the derived category.** Total derived functors RF: D^+(A) → D^+(B) are exact functors of triangulated categories, and R^i F = H^i ∘ RF [3]. Distinguished triangles X → Y → Z → X[1] replace short exact sequences, and the octahedral axiom organizes their compositions. This is the natural habitat of Serre duality and of homological mirror symmetry.

To fix ideas, the following toy Haskell program checks the sheaf axiom for binary covers on a finite Alexandroff topology — a presheaf is modeled as sections over opens with restriction maps, and the equalizer condition is tested directly.

```haskell
import Data.List (intersect, sort)

-- Opens are downward-closed sets of points; a presheaf assigns
-- a list of "sections" to each open, plus restriction maps.
type Open    = [Int]
type Section = String

data Presheaf = P
  { pOpens    :: [Open]
  , pSections :: Open -> [Section]
  , pRestrict :: Open -> Open -> Section -> Section
  }

-- Sheaf axiom for a binary cover U = U1 ∪ U2: every compatible
-- pair (s1, s2) must glue to a UNIQUE s over U.
isSheafOnBinaryCover :: Presheaf -> Open -> Open -> Open -> Bool
isSheafOnBinaryCover p u u1 u2 =
  all (\(s1, s2) ->
        let glued = [ s | s <- pSections p u
                        , pRestrict p u u1 s == s1
                        , pRestrict p u u2 s == s2 ]
        in length glued == 1)
      [ (s1, s2)
      | s1 <- pSections p u1
      , s2 <- pSections p u2
      , pRestrict p u1 inter s1 == pRestrict p u2 inter s2
      ]
  where inter = sort (u1 `intersect` u2)

-- Example: the constant presheaf is a sheaf; the presheaf of
-- bounded functions on R with the usual topology is not.
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Presheaves, the sheaf axiom, sheafification, and stalks

A *presheaf* F on a topological space X (with values in Set, Ab, or R-Mod) is a contravariant functor from the poset of open sets: for V ⊆ U there is a restriction map ρ_{U,V}: F(U) → F(V), with ρ_{U,U} = id and ρ_{V,W}∘ρ_{U,V} = ρ_{U,W}. It is a *sheaf* if for every open cover {U_i} of U, the sequence

0 → F(U) → ∏_i F(U_i) ⇉ ∏_{i,j} F(U_i ∩ U_j)

is an equalizer — the *sheaf axiom*. The first map encodes the locality (separatedness) condition, the second the gluing condition: sections s_i ∈ F(U_i) agreeing on overlaps glue to a unique s ∈ F(U).

Sheaves are determined by their *stalks* F_x = colim_{x∈U} F(U), and a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism iff it induces isomorphisms on all stalks. Every presheaf F has a *sheafification* F^+, the sheaf associated to F, equipped with a universal map F → F^+; the sheafification functor is left adjoint to the inclusion Sh(X) ↪ PSh(X) and is exact [2]. A classic illustration: the constant presheaf U ↦ A has sheafification the sheaf of locally constant A-valued functions.

### 4.2 Canonical exemplars: O_X, constant sheaves, skyscrapers

Three examples dominate the theory.

- **The structure sheaf O_X.** On a scheme X, O_X(U) is the ring of regular functions on U. On a complex manifold, the sheaf O_X of holomorphic functions; on a smooth real manifold, C^∞_X. Sheaves of O_X-modules — quasi-coherent, coherent — are the objects of algebraic geometry. The cohomology H^i(X, O_X) carries the analytic soul of X: for a compact complex manifold, H^0(X, O_X) = C, and H^1(X, O_X) classifies infinitesimal deformations of complex structure via the Kodaira–Spencer map.
- **Constant sheaves.** For an abelian group A, the constant sheaf A_X is the sheafification of U ↦ A. Its cohomology recovers singular cohomology: H^i(X, A_X) ≅ H^i_sing(X; A) for reasonable X. This is the precise sense in which sheaf cohomology generalizes classical cohomology theories [2][5].
- **Skyscraper sheaves.** For x ∈ X and an abelian group A, i_{x,∗}A has stalk A at x and 0 elsewhere. Skyscrapers are the building blocks of the Godement resolution and embody the philosophy that sheaves are controlled stalkwise: flasque sheaves — those with surjective restriction maps — are acyclic, and every sheaf embeds in a flasque one.

> Theorem: Flasque (flabby) sheaves are acyclic for the global-sections functor: H^i(X, F) = 0 for all i > 0 whenever F is flasque. The same holds for fine sheaves on paracompact spaces and soft sheaves — hence for sheaves of C^∞-modules on manifolds, where partitions of unity exist [2][4].

### 4.3 Čech cohomology, acyclic sheaves, and the Leray spectral sequence

Given an open cover U = {U_i}_{i∈I}, the *Čech complex* is

Č^0(U, F) = ∏_i F(U_i) → Č^1(U, F) = ∏_{i<j} F(U_i ∩ U_j) → ···

with the alternating restriction differential. Its cohomology Ȟ^i(U, F) is Čech cohomology; passing to the colimit over refinements gives Ȟ^i(X, F). There is a canonical map Ȟ^i(U, F) → H^i(X, F), and Leray's theorem states it is an isomorphism whenever every finite intersection U_{i_0} ∩ ··· ∩ U_{i_p} is F-acyclic (H^q = 0 for q > 0) [2]. The Stacks Project proves the agreement in full generality for the colimit [2]. This is what makes sheaf cohomology computable: on a scheme, affine covers are Leray for quasi-coherent sheaves, since H^i(U, F) = 0 for i > 0 on affines by Serre's criterion.

The *Leray spectral sequence* organizes the failure of Γ(Y, −)∘f_∗ = Γ(X, −) to commute with derivation. For f: X → Y and a sheaf F on X,

E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y, R^q f_∗ F) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X, F).

It is the Grothendieck spectral sequence for the composition of left exact functors, with edge maps H^p(Y, f_∗F) → H^p(X, F) and H^q(X, F) → H^0(Y, R^q f_∗F) [5]. The five-term exact sequence and degeneration at E_2 when the base or fibers are acyclic are workhorses of algebraic geometry.

### 4.4 Derived categories, Serre duality, and coherent sheaves on P^n

The derived category D(A) is obtained from K(A) by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms. Its *shift functor* [1]: C^• ↦ C^•[1] with (C[1])^n = C^{n+1} reindexes complexes, and a *distinguished triangle* X → Y → Z → X[1] is the derived analogue of a short exact sequence — every morphism embeds in one, via the mapping cone. The axioms (TR1–TR4), especially the octahedral axiom, make D(A) a *triangulated category* [3].

On a smooth projective variety X of dimension n over a field k, with canonical bundle ω_X = Ω^n_X, *Serre duality* asserts a perfect pairing

H^i(X, F) × Ext^{n-i}(F, ω_X) → H^n(X, ω_X) ≅ k

for F coherent; in derived language, RHom(F, ω_X[n]) is the dual of RΓ(X, F) [4]. For X = P^n_k this becomes completely explicit: with S = k[x_0,…,x_n],

| Sheaf | H^0(P^n, O(d)) | H^i, 0 < i < n | H^n(P^n, O(d)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| O(d), d ≥ 0 | S_d, dim (n+d choose d) | 0 | 0 |
| O(d), −n ≤ d < 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| O(d), d < −n | 0 | 0 | dual of S_{−d−n−1} |

and all other cohomology vanishes [4]. These computations — vanishing of intermediate cohomology, duality between H^0 of O(d) and H^n of O(−d−n−1) — are the template for every duality argument in the subject.

The bounded derived category D^b(Coh X) of coherent sheaves is now the central object. For smooth projective X it is *saturated*: every cohomological functor is representable, and autoequivalences are governed by Fourier–Mukai kernels. It is this category, not the abelian category Coh(X), that mirror symmetry sees [1].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We collect the theorems that do the work, with proof sketches exhibiting the machinery.

> Theorem (Grothendieck vanishing): If X is a Noetherian topological space of Krull dimension n, then H^i(X, F) = 0 for all i > n and all abelian sheaves F. Proof sketch: induct on n using the Godement resolution and the fact that a sheaf on an irreducible Noetherian space of dimension 0 is flasque [2][4].

> Theorem (Leray): Let f: X → Y be continuous and F a sheaf on X. There is a spectral sequence E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y, R^q f_∗F) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X, F). Proof sketch: take an injective resolution F → I^•; f_∗I^• is a complex of sheaves on Y whose cohomology sheaves are R^q f_∗F; resolve each by injectives and form the total complex; the two filtrations give the spectral sequence via the Grothendieck construction for R(Γ∘f_∗) = RΓ∘Rf_∗ [5].

> Theorem (Serre duality, projective case): Let X be smooth projective of dimension n over k. Then H^n(X, ω_X) ≅ k and the Yoneda pairing H^i(X, F) × Ext^{n-i}_{O_X}(F, ω_X) → k is perfect for F coherent. Proof sketch: reduce via the closed immersion i: X ↪ P^N to the case of P^N using R i_∗ and the projection formula; on P^N compute explicitly with the table of §4.4 and the trace map [4].

The capstone application is the first theorem of homological mirror symmetry. Polishchuk and Zaslow proved Kontsevich's conjecture for elliptic curves: if E is an elliptic curve and E^∨ its dual, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^b(Coh E) ≅ D^π Fuk(E^∨), matching stable bundles with special Lagrangian branes and the tensor product with the symplectic area-weighted composition of Floer complexes [6]. The proof matches generators on both sides — line bundles of degree 0 and skyscrapers on the B-side, linear Lagrangians of rational slope with flat connections on the A-side — and verifies that the A_∞-structures agree. The Fukaya category itself is an A_∞-category: morphism spaces are Floer cochain complexes CF^•(L_0, L_1), and the higher compositions μ^d count pseudoholomorphic (d+1)-gons [7][8].

## 6 Limitations

The theory has sharp boundaries. Injective resolutions are almost never explicit — the Godement sheaf is enormous — so derived-functor cohomology is a definition, not an algorithm; all computation passes through Čech covers, acyclic resolutions, or spectral sequences, each requiring hypotheses (paracompactness, affineness, Leray covers) [2]. Derived categories lose the abelian structure: kernels and cokernels do not exist, and the octahedral axiom is notoriously inexplicit, which is why enhancements (dg- or A_∞-categories) are needed for serious work [3][7].

Serre duality in the stated form requires smoothness and projectivity; singular varieties need dualizing complexes and Grothendieck duality. Cohomology with compact support, perverse sheaves, and the six-functor formalism extend the picture but demand the étale or analytic topology in arithmetic settings. Finally, homological mirror symmetry remains conjectural in general: beyond elliptic curves, K3 surfaces, and toric cases, the expected equivalence D^b(Coh X) ≅ D^π Fuk(Y) is open, and constructing the Fukaya category itself — transversality, virtual fundamental chains, bounding cochains — is among the hardest analytic problems in symplectic geometry [1][7][8].

## 7 Conclusion

From the sheaf axiom to the derived Fukaya category, the trajectory is one of progressive abstraction in service of a single idea: *local data, global obstructions, categorical structure*. Sheafification turns presheaves into sheaves; injective resolutions turn the left exact global-sections functor into the universal δ-functor of sheaf cohomology; the derived category turns quasi-isomorphisms into isomorphisms and complexes into geometric objects; Serre duality turns cohomology into a self-mirroring pairing. Kontsevich's conjecture proposes that this entire apparatus is the common language of two geometries — complex and symplectic — that physicists had already identified as two descriptions of one string theory [1].

The program has already reshaped both sides: derived categories of coherent sheaves are now standard tools in birational geometry and enumerative geometry, while Fukaya categories have become central to symplectic topology [6][7]. Whether or not the full HMS conjecture is proved in our lifetime, the lesson of sheaf cohomology endures: the obstructions to gluing are not noise but structure, and the derived functors that measure them are among the most powerful invariants mathematics possesses [2][4][5].
