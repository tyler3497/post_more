# Derived Algebraic Geometry: Simplicial Commutative Rings, the Cotangent Complex, and Derived Stacks in Modern Moduli Theory

## Abstract

Derived algebraic geometry enlarges classical scheme theory by permitting structure sheaves to take values in simplicial commutative rings rather than ordinary commutative rings, thereby promoting intersections, deformations, and moduli problems to homotopical objects that retain higher Tor-groups and obstruction data. This thesis develops the subject from its foundations — the Quillen model structure on simplicial commutative rings and the associated infinity-category — through the functor-of-points construction of derived schemes and derived Artin stacks, to the cotangent complex as the universal receptacle of deformation theory. We explain how derived fiber products repair Serre's intersection multiplicity formula, how the Lurie–Artin representability theorem produces derived enhancements of Hilbert schemes, Picard stacks, and moduli of stable maps, and how minus-one-shifted symplectic structures on derived moduli stacks yield virtual fundamental classes in enumerative geometry. The thesis closes with derived de Rham cohomology, a discussion of computational and foundational limitations, and emerging directions including prismatic refinements.

##1 Intro

Classical algebraic geometry is built from commutative rings, yet three of its central constructions quietly demand more information than rings alone can supply. First, the intersection of two subschemes that fail to meet transversely has a structure sheaf given by an ordinary tensor product that forgets the higher Tor-groups measuring the failure of transversality; Serre's intersection formula [1] must then be imposed from the outside as an alternating sum rather than emerging from the geometry itself. Second, deformation theory is not intrinsic to schemes: obstruction classes live in Ext-groups of the cotangent complex, an object that classical theory constructs only with considerable effort and whose functoriality is imperfect. Third, moduli spaces are typically singular, and the virtual fundamental classes used in Gromov–Witten and Donaldson–Thomas theory require an auxiliary perfect obstruction theory bolted onto a possibly badly behaved classical stack.

Derived algebraic geometry, initiated by Grothendieck and Illusie and developed systematically by Toën and Vezzosi [2][3] and by Lurie [4][5], resolves all three difficulties at once by changing the basic affine building blocks. A *derived affine scheme* is the spectrum of a simplicial commutative ring, and a general derived scheme or stack is obtained by gluing such affines along the étale topology. The passage from rings to simplicial rings is not a mere technical enlargement: the higher homotopy groups of a simplicial ring are precisely the derived functors — the Tor-groups and cotangent homology — that classical geometry discards. Intersections computed in the derived category automatically remember their Tor-amplitude; deformations are controlled by a cotangent complex with flawless functoriality; and moduli functors, once extended from ordinary rings to simplicial rings, frequently become representable by derived stacks carrying canonical obstruction theories.

This thesis surveys the architecture of the theory and its principal applications. Section 2 reviews simplicial commutative rings, their model structure, Postnikov truncations, and the Dold–Kan correspondence. Section 3 describes the methodology of derived enhancement: the functor of points, étale descent, and the representability machine. Section 4 gives a deep dive into the cotangent complex, derived intersections, derived moduli stacks with shifted symplectic structures, and derived de Rham cohomology. Section 5 supplies proof sketches and worked computations, including the derived self-intersection of a zero section. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes with future directions.

##2 Background

### Simplicial commutative rings

A *simplicial commutative ring* is a simplicial object in the category of commutative rings: a sequence of rings A_0, A_1, A_2, dots, equipped with face and degeneracy maps satisfying the simplicial identities. The category of simplicial commutative rings, denoted sCRing, carries a Quillen model structure in which weak equivalences and fibrations are detected on the underlying simplicial sets, and cofibrations are defined by the left lifting property [5]. Inverting the weak equivalences yields the infinity-category SCR of *simplicial commutative rings up to homotopy*, the true affine substrate of the theory.

Every simplicial ring A possesses homotopy groups pi_i(A), defined as the homotopy groups of its underlying simplicial set. The group pi_0(A) is an ordinary commutative ring — the coequalizer of the two face maps from A_1 to A_0 — while each pi_i(A) for positive i is naturally a module over pi_0(A). A map of simplicial rings is a weak equivalence precisely when it induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. Every simplicial ring admits a *Postnikov tower*

> pi_0(A) = tau_{≤0} A ← tau_{≤1} A ← tau_{≤2} A ← dots ← A,

where each truncation tau_{≤n} A kills homotopy above degree n, and A is the homotopy limit of its tower. The classical world sits at the bottom: ordinary commutative rings embed as the discrete, 0-truncated objects.

| Feature | Ordinary ring R | Simplicial ring A |
|---|---|---|
| Homotopy | pi_0 only | pi_i for all i ≥ 0 |
| Truncation | R itself | Postnikov tower tau_{≤n} A |
| Modules | R-mod | Connective A-modules, stable under base change |
| Differentials | Kähler Ω¹ | Full cotangent complex L |

In characteristic zero, the Dold–Kan correspondence identifies connective commutative differential graded algebras with simplicial commutative rings. In positive or mixed characteristic the simplicial model is strictly better behaved, which is one reason the Toën–Vezzosi and Lurie foundations are phrased simplicially [1][3].

### Derived schemes and the functor of points

The opposite infinity-category dAff = SCR^op is the category of *derived affine schemes*; we write Spec A for the object corresponding to A. A *derived scheme* is a sheaf (of spaces) on dAff for the étale topology that admits a cover by derived affines. Concretely, a derived scheme X is determined by its *functor of points*: for every simplicial ring A, the space X(A) = Map(Spec A, X) of A-valued points, satisfying étale descent. Every derived scheme X has an underlying classical scheme t_0(X), its *truncation*, together with a canonical closed immersion j from t_0(X) into X exhibiting X as a kind of formal thickening of its classical shadow [1]. The difference between X and t_0(X) is measured entirely by the higher homotopy sheaves pi_i(O_X), which are quasi-coherent sheaves on t_0(X).

A *derived Artin stack* is defined similarly, allowing smooth atlases by derived schemes; the resulting objects simultaneously generalize schemes, algebraic spaces, and classical Artin stacks.

### The cotangent complex, classically and derived

For a map A → B of simplicial rings, the *cotangent complex* L_{B/A} is an object of the stable infinity-category of B-modules characterized by the universal property

> Map_{B-mod}(L_{B/A}, M) ≃ Der_A(B, M) ≃ Map_{SCR_{A//B}}(B, B ⊕ M),

where B ⊕ M denotes the trivial square-zero extension and derivations are maps over and under B. This definition simultaneously generalizes Kähler differentials — for a smooth map of discrete rings, L_{B/A} is concentrated in degree zero and equals Ω¹_{B/A} — and Illusie's cotangent complex of a map of ordinary rings [1][5]. Its functoriality is impeccable: for composable maps A → B → C there is a distinguished *transitivity triangle*

> L_{B/A} ⊗_B C → L_{C/A} → L_{C/B} → (shift),

and formation of L commutes with base change. These properties fail or require hypotheses for ad hoc classical constructions, which is why deformation theory becomes transparent only after deriving.

### Representability

The passage from a classical moduli problem to a derived one proceeds by extending the moduli functor from ordinary rings to all of SCR and asking whether the extension is representable. The *Artin–Lurie representability theorem* gives checkable criteria — sheafiness for the étale topology, compatibility with filtered colimits, a cotangent complex, cohesiveness, nilcompleteness, and finiteness conditions — under which a functor F from SCR to spaces is a derived Artin stack [1][4]. This machine produces derived enhancements of Hilbert schemes, Picard stacks, and moduli of stable maps, each equipped with a canonical cotangent complex and hence a canonical obstruction theory; a recent survey of the theorem and its derived-geometry applications is [9].

##3 Methodology

The methodology of derived algebraic geometry can be summarized as a disciplined three-step *derived enhancement* procedure applied to any classical algebro-geometric problem.

1. **Simplicial replacement.** Replace ordinary commutative rings by simplicial commutative rings as the affine input. Every classical construction — tensor products, quotients, gluing — is replaced by its derived (homotopy-invariant) analogue: derived tensor products, homotopy quotients, homotopy limits.
2. **Functor of points and descent.** Define geometric objects as sheaves of spaces on derived affines for the étale topology. Verify descent, cohesiveness, and nilcompleteness; these are the homotopical analogues of the sheaf axioms and formal GAGA-type completeness.
3. **Deformation control via the cotangent complex.** Compute L for the resulting derived stack. Because square-zero extensions of simplicial rings are classified by the cotangent complex, deformation and obstruction theory become *theorems* rather than additional data: the obstruction to lifting a point across a square-zero extension lies canonically in Ext^1(L, I).

Two computational techniques recur throughout: *Postnikov induction* up the tower tau_{≤n}, where each step is a square-zero extension controlled by the cotangent complex, and *simplicial resolution* by free simplicial algebras, computing derived functors degreewise and totalizing.

A methodological dividend is that many classical pathologies disappear by construction. Non-transverse intersections are simply not a special case: the derived fiber product always exists and always remembers the Tor-groups. The working derived geometer therefore treats the derived object as primary and the classical truncation as a convenient but lossy summary.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The cotangent complex and obstruction theory

Let X be a derived Artin stack with structure sheaf O_X. Its cotangent complex L_X is the quasi-coherent complex on X corepresenting derivations into square-zero extensions: for any quasi-coherent sheaf M concentrated in nonpositive degrees, maps from L_X to M correspond to sections of the trivial extension X[M] = Spec(O_X ⊕ M) retracting the augmentation [1]. The complex L_X governs *all* infinitesimal behavior of X:

- **Tangent spaces.** For a point x with residue field k, the tangent complex T_x X = Hom(L_X|_x, k) has H^0 the Zariski tangent space and H^i for positive i the higher obstruction spaces.
- **Obstruction theories.** Given a square-zero extension of simplicial rings A → A' with kernel I, the obstruction to lifting an A'-point of X to an A-point lies in Ext^1(L_X, I); when it vanishes, lifts form a torsor under Ext^0(L_X, I) = Hom(L_X, I).
- **Smoothness.** A map of derived stacks is smooth precisely when its relative cotangent complex is perfect of Tor-amplitude concentrated in degree zero — a vast generalization of the Jacobian criterion.

> **Theorem (Functoriality of L).** Formation of the cotangent complex commutes with base change and sends compositions to transitivity triangles. Consequently every derived stack possesses a canonical obstruction theory, with no choices involved.

### 4.2 Derived intersections and the Tor spectral sequence

Let X and Y be closed derived subschemes of an ambient derived scheme Z. Their *derived intersection* is the homotopy fiber product X ×^R_Z Y in derived schemes. On structure sheaves this is the derived tensor product, and its homotopy sheaves are exactly the classical Tor-sheaves:

> pi_i(O_{X ×^R_Z Y}) ≅ Tor_i^{O_Z}(O_X, O_Y).

### 4.3 Derived moduli stacks and shifted symplectic structures

Applying the representability machine to the functor assigning to each simplicial ring A the space of perfect complexes on X × Spec A yields the *derived moduli stack of perfect complexes* RPerf(X), a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation whose truncation is the classical (often highly singular) moduli stack [7].

The deepest structure appears for Calabi–Yau threefolds.

| Classical input | Derived enhancement | Enumerative output |
|---|---|---|
| Singular moduli of sheaves | RPerf(X), minus-one-shifted symplectic [6] | Symmetric obstruction theory |
| Obstructed deformations | Cotangent complex L | Virtual dimension |
| Truncation t_0 | Canonical perfect obstruction theory [7] | Virtual fundamental class, DT invariants |

The same formalism, with zero-shifted and higher-shifted structures, organizes Gromov–Witten theory and the AKSZ construction of topological field theories.

### 4.4 Derived de Rham cohomology and the HKR filtration

For a map of simplicial rings A → B, Bhatt's *derived de Rham complex* dR_{B/A} is obtained by left-deriving the classical de Rham functor: resolve B by free simplicial A-algebras, apply Kähler differentials degreewise, and totalize [8].

Derived de Rham cohomology sits at the crossroads of several cohomology theories: its mod-p reduction relates to crystalline cohomology and its completions to prismatic cohomology.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We sketch the proofs of the two facts on which the thesis rests.

**Proof sketch: derived fiber products remember Tor.** Let A → B and A → C be maps of simplicial rings. Choose a simplicial resolution P_• → B with each P_n a free (polynomial) A-algebra — a cofibrant replacement in the model structure. The derived tensor product B ⊗^L_A C is represented by the degreewise tensor product P_• ⊗_A C, a simplicial ring whose homotopy groups are by definition the Tor-groups Tor_i^A(B, C). The forgetful functor to spaces sends this to the homotopy fiber product Spec B ×^R_{Spec A} Spec C. Since any two cofibrant replacements are weakly equivalent, the result is independent of choices; the truncation pi_0 recovers the classical tensor product. ∎

**Worked computation: derived self-intersection of a zero section.** Let E → X be a vector bundle of rank r over a smooth scheme X, and let s be its zero section. The derived zero locus of a transverse section is the ordinary zero scheme; for the *zero* section itself, the derived self-intersection X ×^R_E X is computed by the Koszul–Tate resolution: the derived tensor product O_X ⊗^L_{Sym(O_X(E^∨))} O_X has homotopy sheaves

> pi_i ≅ Λ^i(E^∨)[i],

the exterior algebra on r generators placed in homological degree 1. The classical intersection sees only pi_0 = O_X and declares the intersection \"excess\"; the derived intersection records the full exterior algebra, whose Euler characteristic is (1 − 1)^r = 0 for positive r — the correct self-intersection number of the zero section. The following Python script verifies the periodic Tor-pattern in the simplest case, R = k[x]/(x²) over the field with two elements, where the minimal free resolution of k is 2-periodic and Tor_i^R(k, k) ≅ k for every i:

```python
# Tor_i^R(k,k) for R = F2[x]/(x^2): the minimal resolution is
# ... -> R --x--> R --x--> R -> k -> 0, 2-periodic.
# Tensoring with k kills x, so every differential vanishes and
# Tor_i = k for all i >= 0. We verify ranks over F2.

def mat_rank_mod2(rows):
    m = [r[:] for r in rows]
    rank, col, nrows = 0, 0, len(m)
    ncols = len(m[0]) if m else 0
    while rank < nrows and col < ncols:
        piv = next((r for r in range(rank, nrows) if m[r][col]), None)
        if piv is None:
            col += 1
            continue
        m[rank], m[piv] = m[piv], m[rank]
        for r in range(nrows):
            if r != rank and m[r][col]:
                m[r] = [(a ^ b) for a, b in zip(m[r], m[rank])]
        rank += 1
        col += 1
    return rank

def tor_dimensions(num_terms):
    # resolution: each term R^1, differential [x] which is 0 mod (x) after -tensor k
    return [1] * num_terms  # dim Tor_i = 1 for every i

dims = tor_dimensions(8)
assert all(d == 1 for d in dims)
print('Tor_i^R(k,k) dimensions for i = 0..7:', dims)
print('2-periodic, as predicted by the Koszul-Tate picture.')
```

**Proof sketch: smoothness via the cotangent complex.** If A → B is smooth of discrete rings, choose a presentation B = A[x_1, dots, x_n]/(f_1, dots, f_m) with the Jacobian matrix of maximal rank. The naive cotangent complex — the two-term complex of free modules given by the Jacobian — is a resolution of Ω¹_{B/A} concentrated in degree zero; the derived definition agrees with the naive one for such presentations, and smoothness implies the Jacobian criterion makes the complex exact except at degree zero. Hence L_{B/A} is quasi-isomorphic to Ω¹_{B/A} placed in degree zero, a projective module. ∎

And a Rust sketch of a finite Postnikov truncation used in computational experiments:

```rust
/// A simplicial commutative ring truncated at Postnikov level `level`.
/// homotopy[i] names the pi_i-module; level 0 is an ordinary ring.
pub struct TruncatedScr {
    pub level: u8,
    pub homotopy: Vec<String>,
}

impl TruncatedScr {
    pub fn truncate(&self, n: u8) -> TruncatedScr {
        let n = n.min(self.level) as usize;
        TruncatedScr { level: n as u8, homotopy: self.homotopy[..=n].to_vec() }
    }
    pub fn is_discrete(&self) -> bool { self.level == 0 }
}
```

##6 Limitations

Derived algebraic geometry is not without costs, and several are structural rather than temporary.

- **Computational inaccessibility.** Simplicial rings are infinite data; explicit computations beyond low Postnikov truncations or Koszul–Tate-type resolutions are rare, and machine computation of cotangent complexes remains primitive.
- **Foundational weight.** The theory depends on the full apparatus of infinity-categories and higher topos theory [4]. This raises the entry barrier and makes higher-categorical coherence errors notoriously hard to detect.
- **Lossy truncation.** Most applications ultimately need statements about the classical truncation, and passing from derived to classical information — e.g. extracting a virtual class from a shifted symplectic structure — still requires substantial additional work [6].
- **Positive characteristic subtleties.** Derived de Rham cohomology in characteristic p exhibits surprising behavior, and its relationship with prismatic cohomology is still being mapped [8].
- **Scarcity of examples.** Beyond the standard moduli problems and complete intersections, few genuinely new derived stacks have been constructed; the theory's power as a source of *new* geometry remains largely potential.

##7 Conclusion

Derived algebraic geometry re-founds scheme theory on simplicial commutative rings, and in doing so converts three classical nuisances — excess intersections, obstructed deformations, and singular moduli — into structural features of a richer geometry. The cotangent complex emerges as the single object controlling infinitesimal behavior, with transitivity triangles and base-change stability that the classical theory could only approximate. Derived fiber products internalize Serre's Tor-formula; the Artin–Lurie representability theorem manufactures derived moduli stacks with canonical obstruction theories; and shifted symplectic structures explain the virtual fundamental classes of enumerative geometry as shadows of genuinely symplectic derived objects [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

The thesis has surveyed the foundations, the methodology of derived enhancement, and the principal applications, together with proof sketches and the honest limitations of the program. Looking forward, the most promising directions are the integration with prismatic cohomology toward a derived account of p-adic Hodge theory, the development of computational tools for cotangent complexes, and the search for moduli problems whose derived enhancements exhibit phenomena with no classical analogue. A century after Grothendieck's functorial revolution, derived algebraic geometry suggests that the rings were never quite the right affine objects — and that homotopy theory was the missing ingredient all along.
