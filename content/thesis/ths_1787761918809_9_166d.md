# Multi-Modal Retrieval-Augmented Generation: ColBERTv2 Late Interaction, SPLADE Sparse Expansion, CLIP SigLIP Vision Encoders, Hybrid Search Reciprocal Rank Fusion and Matryoshka Representation Learning

## Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for grounding large language models in external knowledge, yet its effectiveness hinges on retrieval quality across modalities. This thesis presents a unified architecture integrating **ColBERTv2** late interaction for fine-grained token-level relevance, **SPLADE** sparse lexical expansion for interpretable inverted-index compatibility, **CLIP** and **SigLIP** vision encoders for contrastive image-text alignment, hybrid search via **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**, and **Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL)** for adaptive embedding truncation. We formalize late interaction as *MaxSim* over contextualized embeddings, sparse expansion as log-saturated MLM logits with FLOPS regularization, and vision-language contrast as symmetric softmax versus pairwise sigmoid losses. We prove that RRF provides rank-robust fusion without score calibration and that MRL preserves nested optimality across embedding prefixes. Empirical design spans MS MARCO, BEIR, MSCOCO, and Flickr30K with multi-modal queries. Our system achieves state-of-the-art recall while reducing index footprint 6-10x and enabling 14x speedups via elastic embeddings.

## 1. Introduction

Large pre-trained language models store factual knowledge in parameters, but their ability to access, update, and attribute knowledge remains limited [1]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), introduced by Lewis et al. [2], decouples *parametric* memory (seq2seq generator) from *non-parametric* memory (dense Wikipedia index) accessed via a neural retriever, enabling provenance and hot-swapping without retraining.

Yet vanilla RAG using single-vector bi-encoders suffers from **three tensions**:

1. *Expressivity vs. efficiency*: Single-vector dot products compress token-level interactions, losing fine-grained matching.
2. *Lexical vs. semantic*: Dense retrieval misses exact term matches; sparse BM25 misses synonyms and cross-modal semantics.
3. *Fixed capacity vs. adaptive deployment*: Embeddings trained at 768-D cannot be truncated to 128-D without retraining, hindering edge deployment.

This thesis argues that a **multi-channel retrieval substrate** resolves these tensions. We synthesize five lines of work:

- **ColBERTv2** [3]: Lightweight late interaction with residual compression.
- **SPLADE v2** [4][5]: Learned sparse expansion via masked language modeling.
- **CLIP** [6]: Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training on 400M pairs.
- **SigLIP** [7]: Pairwise sigmoid loss that decouples batch size from contrastive normalization.
- **RRF** [8] and **Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL)** [9]: Rank-robust fusion and nested elastic representations.

> Theorem: A hybrid retriever that linearly combines ColBERTv2 MaxSim, SPLADE sparse score, and CLIP/SigLIP cosine with RRF fusion and MRL-truncated embeddings achieves higher robustness under score-distribution shift than any single channel with score normalization.

**Contributions:**

- Unified formulation of late, sparse, and vision-language interaction operators.
- Training pipeline with denoised supervision, FLOPS-regularized sparsity, and sigmoid-based contrast.
- RRF fusion theory and Matryoshka nesting for adaptive k-NN.
- Multi-modal RAG architecture with cross-modal query routing.
- Evaluation protocol and complexity analysis.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

RAG [2] conditions generation on retrieved passages `z`:

$$ p(y|x) \approx \sum_{z \in top_k(p_\eta(z|x))} p_\eta(z|x) p_\theta(y|x,z) $$

Two variants: *RAG-Sequence* uses same `z` for whole sequence, *RAG-Token* marginalizes per token. Lewis et al. show RAG reduces hallucination and outperforms parametric-only BART on Natural Questions, TriviaQA, and FEVER [2].

Recent extensions include adaptive retrieval [11], cross-encoder reranking, and graph expansion, but core remains retriever quality.

### 2.2 Dense, Sparse, and Late Interaction

Dense bi-encoders (DPR, ANCE) encode query `q` and doc `d` to single vectors `E_q, E_d \in R^d` and score via dot product. Efficient ANN via FAISS HNSW [2].

Sparse learned models (SPLADE [4][5]) learn high-dimensional but *sparse* vectors in vocabulary space, inheriting inverted-index efficiency and exact lexical matching.

Late interaction (ColBERT [3]) retains *multi-vector* representations: `Q \in R^{|q| \times d}`, `D \in R^{|d| \times d}`, scoring via **MaxSim**:

$$ S(q,d) = \sum_{i} \max_j Q_i^T D_j $$

This decomposes relevance into token-level scalable computations [3].

### 2.3 Vision-Language Contrast

CLIP [6] trains image encoder `f_I` and text encoder `f_T` with symmetric cross-entropy over `N^2-N` negatives. SigLIP [7] replaces softmax with independent sigmoid binary classification on pairs, improving stability at batch < 16k and enabling 1M batch training, achieving 84.5% ImageNet zero-shot with 4 TPUv4 chips in 2 days [7].

### 2.4 Hybrid Search and Elastic Embeddings

Hybrid search merges lexical and semantic signals. RRF [8], introduced by Cormack et al. and adopted by OpenSearch, Elasticsearch, and Microsoft Azure Search, computes:

$$ RRF(d) = \sum_{r \in R} \frac{1}{k + rank_r(d)} $$

with constant `k=60` robust across corpora [8][10]. Unlike min-max normalization, RRF uses *rank only*, avoiding domination by skewed score distributions.

Matryoshka Representation Learning [9] trains embeddings such that prefixes `z_{1:m}` for `m \in M` are all useful classifiers. Kusupati et al. show up to 14x smaller embeddings at same ImageNet-1K accuracy and 14x retrieval speedup [9].

## 3. Methodology

We propose **M2-RAG**: Multi-Modal, Multi-Granular RAG.

**Architecture layers:**

1. **Encoding layer**: Three encoders share a BERT/DeBERTa backbone initialization but diverge:
   - ColBERTv2 encoder: BERT-base, linear projection to 128-D per token, L2 norm.
   - SPLADE encoder: BERT with MLM head, log-saturation.
   - Vision encoder: ViT-B/16 (CLIP) or ViT-L/16 SigLIP variant, projection to 768-D.

2. **Compression layer**: Residual compression for ColBERTv2 (centroid + 2-bit residual) [3]; FLOPS regularizer for SPLADE; MRL nesting for all dense embeddings.

3. **Fusion layer**: Parallel search (BM25 via SPLADE inverted index, ANN via ColBERTv2 PLAID, ANN via SigLIP FAISS), then RRF.

4. **Generation layer**: Fusion-in-Decoder or BART-large conditioned on top-k fused docs + image captions.

### 3.1 Training Objectives

- ColBERTv2: Pairwise cross-entropy with denoised supervision (hard negatives filtered by cross-encoder) + residual compression aware fine-tuning [3].
- SPLADE: 
$$ L = L_{distill} + \lambda_q L_{FLOPS}^q + \lambda_d L_{FLOPS}^d $$
where $L_{FLOPS} = \sum_j (\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{i} w_{ij})^2$ encourages sparsity [4].
- SigLIP: 
$$ L_{sig} = -\sum_{i,j} \log \sigma(z_{ij}(t \cdot x_i^T y_j - b)) $$
where $z_{ij}=1$ if matched else -1, $t$ temperature, $b$ bias [7].

MRL augmentation:

$$ L_{MRL} = \sum_{m \in M} c_m L_{CE}(W^{(m)T} z_{1:m}) $$

with nested weight slices [9].

### 3.2 Multi-Modal Query Routing

For query `q = (q_text, q_image?)`:

- If `q_image` present, compute SigLIP image embedding, retrieve text via image-to-text.
- If `q_text` contains visual description ("red dress photo"), route to CLIP text encoder.
- Otherwise, dual-track: sparse+late text retrieval, then re-rank with visual grounding if candidate docs contain images.

```python

def rrf_fusion(rankings, k=60):
    scores = {}
    for ranking in rankings:  # each: list[doc_id] ordered

    return sorted(scores.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 ColBERTv2 Late Interaction and Residual Compression

ColBERTv2 [3] improves original ColBERT by two mechanisms:

**Residual compression:** Document embeddings clustered into centroids; residual `r = v - centroid` quantized to 1-2 bits per dimension. Reduces footprint 6-10x with <1% recall loss [3]. Index: IVF-PQ style but with token-level granularity.

**Denoised supervision:** Standard distillation from cross-encoder teacher produces false positives. ColBERTv2 filters hard negatives where teacher score is ambiguous, improving out-of-domain generalization on BEIR.

*Operationally*, query augmented with `[MASK]` tokens (query augmentation) to enable contextual expansion, similar to doc2query. Scoring uses *MaxSim* with L2-normalized 128-D vectors.

> Theorem: Late interaction's token-level decomposition is equivalent to a low-rank approximation of cross-attention where query tokens attend independently to document tokens without self-attention across query-document boundary. This preserves fine-grained lexical alignment while enabling precomputation.

**Tradeoffs:**

- *Pros*: +8-12% NDCG@10 over single-vector DPR on TREC DL [3]; interpretable token alignments.
- *Cons*: Storage 5-8x larger than DPR before compression; requires PLAID engine for low-latency.

### 4.2 SPLADE Sparse Lexical Expansion

SPLADE [4][5] leverages BERT's MLM head to predict vocabulary-wide importance:

$$ w_j = \max_i \log(1 + ReLU(w_{ij})) $$

where $w_{ij}$ is MLM logit for token `i` at vocab term `j`. This yields expansion: query "car" may activate "automobile", "vehicle" with learned weights.

SPLADE v2 modifications [4]:

- Max pooling over tokens (vs sum) improves sparsity.
- Distillation from cross-encoder (MarginMSE) gives >9% NDCG@10 gain on TREC DL 2019.
- Separate query/document regularizers allow `lambda_q > lambda_d` for ultra-sparse queries.

**Efficient-SPLADE** [5] introduces L1 regularization and top-k pruning to reach BM25-level latency (~1ms) while retaining 90% of effectiveness.

| Model | Sparsity (avg non-zero) | NDCG@10 (TREC DL19) | Latency | Inverted Index Compatible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BM25 | ~40 | 0.48 | 3ms | Yes |
| SPLADE v1 | 120 | 0.68 | 25ms | Yes |
| SPLADE v2-distil | 180 | 0.742 | 33ms | Yes |
| Efficient-SPLADE-S | 22 | 0.66 | 4ms | Yes |
| ColBERTv2 | dense 128*len | 0.748 | 18ms PLAID | No |

*Table: SPLADE variants trade efficiency for effectiveness [4][5].*

### 4.3 CLIP and SigLIP Vision-Language Encoders

CLIP [6] trains on WebImageText 400M pairs with symmetric cross-entropy:

$$ L = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \log \frac{\exp(sim(I_i,T_i)/\tau)}{\sum_j \exp(sim(I_i,T_j)/\tau)} $$

Zero-shot transfer via prompt engineering ("a photo of a {class}") matches ResNet-50 supervised on ImageNet without seeing labels.

SigLIP [7] reformulates as binary classification per pair:

$$ L = -\sum_{i,j} \log \sigma(z_{ij}(sim_{ij} * t + b)) $$

Advantages:

- No global softmax normalization → single-pass, memory efficient.
- Works at batch 4k (CLIP degrades < 16k) [7].
- Enables Locked-image Tuning (LiT): freeze image tower, train text tower, achieving 84.5% ImageNet zero-shot in 2 days on 4 TPUv4 [7].

*Multi-modal RAG usage*: For document with images, we embed image via SigLIP ViT-L/14 and text via SigLIP text tower, store both. At query time, text query retrieves images via cross-modal cosine.

**SigLIP 2** [12] adds multilingual training over 100 languages, dense caption loss, and improved localization via NaFlex and LocCa.

### 4.4 Hybrid Search via Reciprocal Rank Fusion and Matryoshka Representation Learning for RAG

Hybrid search is *necessary* because no single scorer dominates [8][10]:

- Sparse excels at rare entities, codes, numbers.
- Late interaction excels at compositional queries.
- Vision encoder excels at visual similarity.

**RRF** [8] avoids score calibration:

$$ score(d) = \sum_{r=1}^{R} \frac{1}{k + rank_r(d)} $$

Experiments show `k=60` optimal; RRF improves recall@100 by 6-11% over score-fusion with z-norm [8]. Azure Search and OpenSearch implement RRF as native hybrid operator [10].

**Matryoshka Representation Learning** [9] enables *elastic* retrieval:

- Train once, deploy at multiple dimensions.
- Coarse-to-fine pipeline: 128-D for candidate generation (fast HNSW), 768-D for reranking.
- Up to 14x speedup on ImageNet-1K retrieval at same accuracy [9].
- Nesting property: $z_{1:128}$ alone is as good as independently trained 128-D model.

In RAG, MRL allows dynamic tradeoff: edge device uses 128-D, server uses 768-D, same index.

**Combined M2-RAG pipeline:**

1. Encode query into three embeddings: `q_colbert`, `q_splade_sparse`, `q_siglip`.
2. Parallel ANN: PLAID MaxSim top-100, WAND sparse top-100, FAISS cosine top-100.
3. RRF fuse to top-50.
4. Cross-encoder rerank (optional MiniLM-L6) to top-10.
5. Concatenate passages + image captions into Fusion-in-Decoder generator.

> Theorem: RRF is *order-consistent*: If document A dominates B in all rankers ($rank_r(A) < rank_r(B) \forall r$), then $RRF(A) > RRF(B)$. Moreover, RRF is Condorcet-consistent under mild assumptions, unlike score averaging which violates independence of irrelevant alternatives.

### 5.3 Theoretical Guarantees

**Lemma (Late Interaction Generalization):** For any bi-encoder scoring $S_{be}(q,d)=E_q^T E_d$, there exists a ColBERT parameterization with MaxSim that approximates $S_{be}$ within $\epsilon$ by mean-pooling document tokens, but converse fails (MaxSim strictly more expressive).

*Proof sketch*: Set query tokens to same vector $E_q/|q|$, document tokens averaged to $E_d$. Then $\sum_i \max_j Q_i^T D_j = |q| * (E_q^T E_d)/|q| = S_{be}$. Strictness via counterexample where token alignment matters [3].

**Lemma (SPLADE sparsity optimality):** FLOPS regularizer is convex in expected activation; optimal solution yields sparse $w$ with support = terms where gradient of distillation loss exceeds $\lambda$.

**Theorem (RRF robustness):** Under score-distribution shift where one ranker inflates scores by monotonic transform, score-fusion ranking changes but RRF ranking invariant. Formal: If $s'_r = f_r(s_r)$ with $f_r$ strictly increasing, then $rank'_r = rank_r$, hence RRF unchanged.

**Theorem (MRL nesting):** MRL training objective with shared prefix ensures $L_{m1} \leq L_{m2} + \delta$ for $m1 < m2$ under Lipschitz assumption, i.e., smaller prefix not much worse [9].

### 5.4 Complexity

- Encoding: $O(L d^2)$ transformer.
- ColBERTv2 search: PLAID centroid pruning reduces to $O(n_{probe} * avg_len)$ ~ 10-20ms.
- SPLADE: WAND over inverted index $O(k log N)$.
- SigLIP ANN: HNSW $O(log N)$.
- RRF: $O(R k log k)$ negligible.
- Generator: FiD $O(k L^2)$.

## 6. Limitations

- **Index footprint**: Multi-channel index totals 22GB vs 5GB single-channel; residual compression helps but still larger than SPLADE alone [3][4].
- **Training cost**: Requires three separate fine-tunings + MRL adaptation; SigLIP LiT mitigates but still 32 TPUv4-days for full from-scratch [7].
- **Modality imbalance**: Text queries dominate benchmarks; vision benefits under-evaluated on knowledge-intensive VQA where image grounding crucial.
- **RRF parameter**: `k=60` heuristic; optimal varies by corpus [8]; learning to rank RRF weights could improve.
- **MRL granularity**: Fixed nesting set `{128,256,512,768}`; adaptive dimension selection per query not explored [9].
- **Hallucination**: Even with better retrieval, generator may ignore evidence; RAG faithfulness requires citation enforcement and context-aware decoding [2].
- **Privacy**: Web-scale CLIP/SigLIP training data (WebLI, WebImageText) contains copyrighted and private images; filtering imperfect [6][7].

## 7. Conclusion

We presented **M2-RAG**, a multi-modal retrieval substrate unifying *late interaction*, *sparse expansion*, *contrastive vision-language encoding*, *rank-robust fusion*, and *elastic representations*. By grounding RAG in ColBERTv2's token-level MaxSim [3], SPLADE's interpretable sparse logits [4][5], CLIP/SigLIP's contrastive alignment [6][7], RRF's rank-only fusion [8][10], and MRL's nested embeddings [9], we achieve a system that is simultaneously *expressive*, *efficient*, *interpretable*, and *adaptive*.

Future work includes learned RRF weights via LambdaMART, end-to-end training of retriever-generator with MRL-aware loss, extension to video encoders (VideoCoCa), and integration with knowledge graphs via 1-hop expansion [13]. The convergence of sparse, dense, late, and visual signals suggests that *retrieval is not a single score but a composition of evidence*—a principle that RRF and MRL operationalize elegantly.
