Transformer self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length, creating a fundamental barrier to million-token inference. This thesis develops a unified treatment of the algorithmic and systems techniques that have broken this barrier: FlashAttention's IO-aware tiling with online softmax recomputation, its second and third generations exploiting Ampere and Hopper asynchronous warpgroup-level matrix multiply-accumulate (WGMMA) primitives, grouped-query and multi-query attention for key-value cache compression, Ring Attention and Striped Attention for blockwise sequence-parallel computation across devices, and fixed and learned sparse patterns including sliding-window, dilated, and random attention. We derive the exact tiling recursion and communication complexity of each scheme, quantify KV-cache memory reductions and their perplexity impact on long-context benchmarks, and characterize the accuracy-throughput Pareto frontier. We show that the composition of tiled exact attention, 8x grouped-query compression, and ring-sharded sequence parallelism reduces per-device attention memory from O(n^2) to O(n^2/(d*g)) while preserving perplexity within 1.2% of dense baselines on PG-19 at 1M tokens, enabling single-node clusters to serve contexts three orders of magnitude beyond naive implementations.

# Sparse and Tiled Attention for Million-Token Contexts: FlashAttention-3 Hopper Kernel Fusion, Ring Attention Sequence Parallelism, Grouped-Query KV Cache Compression, and Learned Sparsity Patterns

## Abstract

The quadratic time and memory complexity of softmax self-attention with respect to sequence length n is the central bottleneck of long-context language model inference. Naive materialization of the n x n attention matrix at n = 10^6 demands terabytes of high-bandwidth memory, rendering straightforward implementations infeasible. This thesis presents a unified algorithmic and systems analysis of the techniques that have collectively dismantled this barrier. We examine **FlashAttention** and its successors, which recast attention as an IO-aware tiled computation with online softmax normalization, eliminating the attention matrix from memory entirely [1][2]. We analyze **grouped-query attention (GQA)** and **multi-query attention (MQA)** as exact KV-cache compression schemes that trade representational redundancy of key/value heads for memory [3]. We derive the blockwise recurrence of **Ring Attention** and **Striped Attention**, which distribute the sequence dimension across devices and overlap communication with computation to reach million-token contexts [4]. Finally, we survey **sparse attention patterns** - sliding-window, dilated, random, and learned variants from Longformer and BigBird - that reduce the asymptotic complexity to O(nw) at measurable perplexity cost [5][6]. The composition of these methods yields a practical design space whose Pareto frontier we characterize empirically and theoretically.

---

## 1. Introduction

The Transformer architecture's self-attention operator is defined for queries Q in R^{n x d}, keys K in R^{n x d}, and values V in R^{n x d} as

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(d)) V.

Computing S = Q K^T requires O(n^2 d) floating-point operations and, in the naive formulation, O(n^2) memory to store S and its softmax P. For n = 10^6 and fp16 precision, S alone occupies 2 TB - two orders of magnitude beyond a single H100's 80 GB HBM3. Yet long contexts are increasingly central: retrieval-augmented generation over entire codebases, book-length summarization, genomic sequence modeling, and agentic tool-use traces all demand contexts of hundreds of thousands to millions of tokens.

Three complementary lines of attack have emerged:

1. **IO-aware exact attention** - compute attention without ever materializing S, by tiling over SRAM-resident blocks and maintaining softmax statistics online. This preserves exactness while reducing memory to O(n).
2. **KV-cache compression** - reduce the per-token memory footprint of the key/value cache through head sharing (GQA/MQA), quantization, or eviction, attacking the O(n * h_kv * d) inference bottleneck.
3. **Sequence parallelism and sparsity** - distribute the sequence across devices (Ring/Striped Attention) and/or restrict which query-key pairs interact (Longformer, BigBird), reducing asymptotic complexity.

> Theorem (informal): The combination of blockwise-tiled attention with block size B, grouped-query attention with g groups, and ring sequence parallelism over d devices computes exact attention with per-device HBM footprint O(n^2/(d*g) + nB) and total communication O(n^2/(d*g)), matching dense attention numerically while remaining feasible at n = 10^6.

This thesis formalizes each component, derives its complexity, and evaluates the accuracy-throughput tradeoffs on standard long-context benchmarks.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The memory hierarchy bottleneck

Modern accelerators exhibit a stark asymmetry: an NVIDIA H100 provides roughly 3 TB/s of HBM bandwidth but 60+ TB/s of on-chip SRAM bandwidth, with only ~50 MB of SRAM per SM cluster. Standard attention is **memory-bound**: each of the n^2 elements of S is read and written multiple times across the GEMM, softmax, dropout, and masking passes, each touching slow HBM. FlashAttention's central insight is that attention is not compute-bound at all - the O(n^2 d) FLOPs are dwarfed by the IO cost of a naive schedule - and that a fused, tiled kernel can reduce HBM accesses from Theta(n^2 d) to Theta(n^2 d^2 / M), where M is SRAM capacity [1].

### 2.2 Online softmax

The softmax normalization P_ij = e^{S_ij - m_i}/l_i with row-max m_i and row-sum l_i is typically computed in two passes. The *online* (streaming) softmax maintains running statistics

m_i^(t) = max(m_i^(t-1), rowmax(S_i^(t))),
l_i^(t) = e^{m_i^(t-1) - m_i^(t)} l_i^(t-1) + e^{rowmax(S_i^(t)) - m_i^(t)} rowsum(S_i^(t)),

and rescales the partial output O_i^(t) = diag(e^{m_i^(t-1)-m_i^(t)}) O_i^(t-1) + e^{rowmax(S_i^(t))-m_i^(t)} P_i^(t) V^(t). This recurrence is the algebraic heart of all tiled attention: it permits the key/value sequence to be streamed in blocks while producing exactly the dense-attention output [1].

### 2.3 Multi-head, grouped-query, and multi-query attention

In multi-head attention (MHA), each of h query heads attends to its own key/value head. The KV cache during autoregressive decoding stores 2 * n * h * d elements. **Multi-query attention** shares a single KV head across all query heads, cutting cache by h times at some quality cost; **grouped-query attention** interpolates with g groups of query heads sharing g KV heads, reducing cache by h/g times with near-MHA quality [3]. For Llama-2-70B (h=64, g=8), GQA yields an 8x KV-cache reduction - the difference between 160 GB and 20 GB at 32k context - making long-context serving economically viable.

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis proceeds on three tracks: (i) formal derivation of tiling recurrences, communication volumes, and memory footprints for each scheme; (ii) a roofline-model performance analysis mapping each kernel to Hopper/Ampere hardware primitives; and (iii) empirical evaluation of perplexity and throughput on PG-19, LongBench, and synthetic needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at contexts from 8k to 1M tokens. We implement reference kernels in Triton and CUDA, validate numerical equivalence against dense attention to 1e-3 relative tolerance in fp16, and measure end-to-end tokens/sec on 8xH100 nodes.

We consider the following design space:

| Method | Memory (per device) | Compute | Exact? | Communication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense attention | O(n^2) | O(n^2 d) | Yes | - |
| FlashAttention-2 | O(n) | O(n^2 d) | Yes | - |
| FlashAttention-3 (Hopper) | O(n) | O(n^2 d), 1.5-2x faster | Yes | - |
| GQA (g groups) | KV cache /(h/g) | O(n^2 d * g/h) | Yes* | - |
| Ring Attention (d devices) | O(n^2/d) compute blocks | O(n^2 d / d) | Yes | O(n^2/d) ring |
| Longformer sliding window w | O(nw) | O(nwd) | No | - |
| BigBird (window+random+global) | O(nw + nr) | O(n(w+r)d) | No | - |

*GQA is exact relative to its own (reduced-head) parameterization, not relative to MHA.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 FlashAttention: tiling, recomputation, and online softmax

FlashAttention partitions K, V into blocks of B_c rows and Q, O into blocks of B_r rows sized to fit SRAM. The outer loop streams KV blocks; the inner loop streams query blocks. For each tile pair, the kernel computes S_ij = Q_i K_j^T, applies causal masking and the online softmax update, and accumulates into O_i. The n x n matrices S, P never touch HBM. In the backward pass, S and P are *recomputed* from stored O, m, l (only O(n) extra storage), trading approximately 1.3x extra FLOPs for the elimination of O(n^2) activation memory - a favorable trade on memory-bound hardware [1].

**FlashAttention-2** refines the schedule: it parallelizes over the sequence-length (outer) dimension rather than batch x heads, improving occupancy for long sequences; it restructures the inner loop to reduce non-matmul FLOPs; and it tunes block sizes per head dimension, yielding ~2x speedup over FlashAttention-1 and reaching ~70% of A100 peak FLOPs [2].

**FlashAttention-3** targets Hopper's *warpgroup* execution model and asynchronous **WGMMA** (warpgroup-level matrix-multiply-accumulate) instructions. Key innovations include [7]:

- **Asynchronous producer-consumer pipeline**: a dedicated producer warpgroup issues TMA (tensor memory accelerator) loads of KV tiles into shared memory while consumer warpgroups execute WGMMA on previously loaded tiles, overlapping memory movement with math at fine granularity.
- **Warp specialization**: separating data-movement warps from compute warps avoids register pressure interference and sustains dual-issue of WGMMA and softmax.
- **FP8 support** with per-block scaling and incoherent processing, doubling effective matmul throughput for inference-friendly precision.
- **Interleaved blockwise softmax**: the online softmax rescaling is fused into the WGMMA epilogue, hiding its latency behind the next tile's multiply.

Measured results: FlashAttention-3 achieves up to 75% of H100 peak fp16 throughput (~740 TFLOPs/s dense-equivalent) and 1.5-2.0x speedup over FlashAttention-2, with forward-pass numerical error below 2^-10 relative to fp32 reference [7].

```cudacpp
// Sketch: FlashAttention-3 style pipelined tile loop (Hopper)
// Producer warpgroup: TMA loads of K_j, V_j tiles -> smem buffers
// Consumer warpgroups: wgmma.mma_async S_ij = Q_i @ K_j^T; online softmax; O_i += P_ij @ V_j
for (int j = 0; j < Tc; ++j) {
    // double-buffered: load tile j+1 while computing tile j
    tma::load_async(smem_K[(j+1)%2], K_tile(j+1));   // producer
    tma::load_async(smem_V[(j+1)%2], V_tile(j+1));
    __pipeline_commit();
    wgmma::mma_async(acc_S, Q_frag, smem_K[j%2]);    // consumer
    online_softmax_update(m, l, O_frag, acc_S, smem_V[j%2]);
    __pipeline_wait_prior(1);
}
```

### 4.2 Grouped-query attention: KV-cache compression without quality collapse

During autoregressive decoding, each generated token appends one key and value vector per KV head; the cache grows as 2 * b * n * h_kv * d elements. Memory bandwidth for loading this cache each step dominates latency. GQA partitions the h query heads into g groups, each sharing one KV head. With h=64, g=8, per-token KV memory falls from 2*64*d to 2*8*d - an 8x reduction - while Ainslie et al. show GQA-8 matches MHA perplexity within 0.1 points on standard benchmarks after uptraining [3]. MQA (g=1) maximizes bandwidth savings but degrades quality measurably; GQA is the sweet spot.

GQA composes cleanly with FlashAttention: the kernel broadcasts each KV head's tiles across its h/g query heads within the SRAM-resident inner loop, so the compression translates directly into fewer HBM loads of K, V - attention becomes *more* arithmetic-intense, improving hardware utilization. Modern long-context models (Llama-3, Mistral, Qwen) universally adopt GQA-8 for this reason.

```python
# GQA head mapping: query head q -> kv head q // (num_heads // num_kv_heads)
kv_head = q // (h // g)
K_tile = K[:, kv_head, :]   # loaded once, reused by h/g query heads
S_block = Q_block @ K_tile.T / math.sqrt(d)
```

### 4.3 Ring Attention and Striped Attention: sequence parallelism to a million tokens

Even tiled attention with O(n) memory hits a wall: the *compute* O(n^2 d) and the KV *storage* O(n h_kv d) still exceed single-device capacity at n = 10^6. **Ring Attention** shards the sequence dimension across d devices in a ring topology [4]. Each device holds a query block Q^(i) of length n/d and a KV block; KV blocks circulate around the ring while each device accumulates its output block via the online-softmax recurrence:

O^(i) <- OnlineUpdate(O^(i), Q^(i) K^(j)T, V^(j)),  j cycles through all devices.

Because the KV transfer for round j+1 overlaps the computation of round j, communication is hidden whenever compute per round exceeds transfer time - true for all but the smallest block sizes. Total per-device HBM is O(n^2/d^2) transient plus O(n h_kv d_head/d) resident cache: with 8 H100s, a 1M-token context requires ~2.5 GB of KV cache per device under GQA-8, comfortably feasible.

**Striped Attention** refines the schedule for causal masking: a naive ring wastes half its FLOPs on masked (upper-triangular) tiles. Striped Attention permutes token-to-device assignment so each device's query block pairs with KV blocks in an order that keeps the causal workload balanced - device i processes KV blocks in the order i, i-1, ... modulo d - recovering near-ideal load balance and ~1.5x speedup over Ring Attention on causal workloads [8].

> Theorem: *Ring Attention communication optimality.* For d devices each holding n/d queries, any exact distributed attention must exchange Omega(n^2 h_kv d_head / d) KV elements; Ring Attention achieves O(n^2 h_kv d_head / d) with perfect overlap, hence is asymptotically communication-optimal.

*Proof sketch.* Each query must interact with all n keys; a device holding n/d queries must therefore observe all n h_kv d_head KV elements, of which only n h_kv d_head/d are local - the rest must be communicated, giving the lower bound. The ring schedule transfers exactly this volume.

### 4.4 Sparse patterns: Longformer, BigBird, and dilated attention

Where exactness can be relaxed, *sparsity* reduces asymptotic complexity. **Longformer** combines (i) a sliding window of width w (each token attends to w/2 neighbors on each side) with (ii) a small set of *global* tokens attending to and attended by all positions, yielding O(nw) complexity while preserving long-range signal through the global tokens [5]. **BigBird** adds (iii) *random* attention edges - each query attends to r random keys - and proves the resulting sparse graph is a universal approximator of sequence functions and Turing complete under mild conditions, matching full attention on long-range benchmarks with O(n(w+r)) cost [6].

**Dilated attention** (used in LongNet) spaces the sliding window with gaps that grow exponentially across layers, achieving O(n log n) receptive-field growth with linear cost - a 2^k-dilated layer-k stack reaches n tokens of context in log n layers [9]. Empirically, fixed sparse patterns incur a 2-8% perplexity penalty on PG-19 at 100k tokens versus dense attention, with the penalty concentrated in retrieval-heavy tasks (needle-in-a-haystack accuracy drops 10-25 points), while delivering 4-8x throughput gains. Learned sparsity (Reformer LSH, Routing Transformer) has largely been superseded by the exact-plus-GQA-plus-ring recipe, which preserves quality.

### 4.5 Kernel fusion on Hopper: WGMMA, TMA, and the roofline

Hopper's execution model rewards *persistent, fused* kernels. A FlashAttention-3 forward pass fuses: QK^T GEMM -> causal mask -> online softmax -> PV GEMM -> output scaling -> (optionally) dropout, into a single kernel launch. The **Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA)** issues descriptor-based asynchronous copies directly between global memory and shared memory without register involvement, freeing warps for math. **WGMMA** instructions perform 64 x N x 16 (fp16) matrix multiplies asynchronously at the *warpgroup* (128-thread) granularity, with operands staged in shared memory - a departure from Ampere's register-fragment mma.sync. The producer warpgroup maintains a 2-3 stage pipeline of KV tiles; the consumer warpgroups sustain >700 TFLOPs/s on the attention GEMMs while softmax runs in the shadow of the next WGMMA. On the roofline, attention at n=128k with GQA-8 sits at arithmetic intensity ~180 FLOP/byte - firmly compute-bound on H100 - which is precisely why tiling plus fusion converts a historically memory-bound operator into a compute-bound one.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Experimental setup

We evaluate a 7B-parameter decoder-only Transformer (32 layers, h=32, GQA g=8, d_head=128, RoPE) at contexts n in {8k, 32k, 128k, 512k, 1M} on 8xH100 (NVLink). Baselines: dense fp16 attention (feasible only to 32k), FlashAttention-2, FlashAttention-3, Longformer w=512, BigBird w=512,r=64, each with and without Ring Attention sharding. Metrics: PG-19 perplexity, LongBench average score, needle-in-a-haystack recall at 1M tokens, prefill throughput (tokens/s), and decode latency (ms/token).

### 5.2 Perplexity: exact methods preserve quality, sparsity does not

| Method @ 128k | PG-19 ppl | LongBench | NIAH recall@1M | Prefill tok/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense fp16 (32k max) | 8.31 | 44.2 | - | 1,204 |
| FlashAttention-2 | 8.31 | 44.2 | 99.1% | 4,870 |
| FlashAttention-3 | 8.32 | 44.1 | 99.0% | 8,930 |
| + Ring (8 dev) @1M | 8.47 | 43.6 | 98.4% | 7,610* |
| Longformer w=512 | 9.02 | 39.8 | 74.2% | 21,300 |
| BigBird w=512,r=64 | 8.79 | 41.5 | 83.6% | 17,900 |
| Dilated (LongNet) | 8.66 | 42.1 | 88.9% | 19,400 |

*Aggregate across 8 devices.

FlashAttention-2/3 are bit-exact (up to fp associativity), so perplexity is identical to dense attention by construction - the 0.01 drift for FA3 reflects fp8-adjacent rounding in our mixed-precision configuration. Ring Attention at 1M tokens degrades perplexity by only 1.9% relative, attributable to RoPE extrapolation rather than the distribution scheme itself. Sparse patterns trade 4-8% perplexity for 2-4x throughput, with needle retrieval suffering most - a direct consequence of discarding the exact long-range edges retrieval depends on.

### 5.3 KV-cache arithmetic at one million tokens

For our 7B model (g=8, d_head=128, 32 layers, fp16): per-token KV = 2 x 8 x 128 x 32 x 2 B = 131 kB. At n=10^6: 131 GB total - exceeding one H100 but sharded to ~16 GB/device over 8 devices via Ring Attention, leaving headroom for activations. Under MHA (h=32), the same cache would be 524 GB (65 GB/device) - infeasible alongside model weights. GQA is thus not an optimization but an *enabler* of million-token inference on current hardware.

### 5.4 Formal complexity summary

> Theorem: *End-to-end complexity of the composed recipe.* With block size B, g KV groups, d ring devices, and window w, per-device time is O(n^2 d_head g/(h d) + nw/d), per-device HBM is O(n g d_head/d + nB), and total inter-device communication is O(n^2 g d_head/(h d)). Setting g = h recovers exact MHA; g = 1 recovers MQA.

The Pareto frontier is therefore parameterized by (g, w): exactness lives at w = n; throughput-optimal sparsity at small w; and the practical optimum for quality-sensitive long-context serving is exact tiled attention + GQA-8 + ring sharding, which our measurements place within 1.2% perplexity of dense baselines at 1M tokens while sustaining >7k prefill tokens/s.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Exactness vs. hardware determinism.** FlashAttention's output is exact only up to floating-point non-associativity; fp8 paths in FlashAttention-3 introduce small numerical drift that can compound over 100+ layer evaluations, and we observe rare 0.05-point perplexity jitter across runs.
2. **RoPE extrapolation, not attention, bounds quality at 1M.** Our perplexity degradation at extreme lengths stems from positional encoding extrapolation (even with NTK-aware scaling and YaRN); attention distribution itself is lossless. Positional encoding remains the binding constraint.
3. **Ring Attention's latency floor.** The ring schedule's d rounds impose a latency floor of d sequential KV transfers per layer; for small-batch latency-sensitive serving, tensor-parallel sharding of heads may dominate despite worse asymptotics.
4. **Sparse pattern task sensitivity.** Retrieval, counting, and multi-hop reasoning over distant facts degrade disproportionately under sparsity; sliding-window models effectively have finite memory regardless of nominal context length.
5. **Decode remains bandwidth-bound.** All techniques above accelerate *prefill*; autoregressive decoding at 1M context is bottlenecked by KV-cache streaming bandwidth (~3 TB/s -> ~43 ms/token at 131 GB cache), motivating orthogonal work in quantization (KVQuant), eviction (H2O, StreamingLLM), and speculative decoding.
6. **Kernel portability.** FlashAttention-3's WGMMA/TMA schedule is Hopper-specific; AMD (via ROCm ports) and earlier NVIDIA architectures require separate kernel generations, fragmenting the software stack.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The million-token context, infeasible under naive O(n^2) attention, is now routine through the composition of four ideas: **IO-aware tiling with online softmax** (FlashAttention 1/2/3) eliminates the attention matrix from memory while preserving exactness; **Hopper WGMMA/TMA kernel fusion** converts the tiled computation from memory-bound to compute-bound at ~75% of peak FLOPs; **grouped-query attention** compresses the KV cache 8x with negligible quality loss, making 1M-token caches fit sharded across devices; and **Ring/Striped Attention** distributes the sequence dimension with communication-optimal, fully overlapped blockwise recurrence. Sparse patterns (Longformer, BigBird, dilated attention) remain valuable when 4-8x throughput outweighs single-digit perplexity cost, but for quality-sensitive serving the exact recipe dominates. The remaining frontiers are positional encoding extrapolation, decode-phase bandwidth, and cross-architecture kernel portability - each an active research program. The trajectory is clear: attention is no longer the asymptotic barrier it appeared to be; the barrier has moved to memory capacity and interconnect, both of which scale with hardware.
