# Causal Inference under Network Interference: Horvitz-Thompson Estimators, Graph Cluster Randomization, and Doubly Robust Estimation of Spillover Effects

## Abstract
Network interference violates the *Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA)* by allowing a unit's potential outcome to depend on the treatment vector of its neighbors, peers, or broader graph neighborhood. This thesis synthesizes design- and model-based approaches to causal identification and estimation under interference, focusing on exposure mapping reductions, Horvitz-Thompson inverse-probability-weighted estimators, randomized graph cluster randomization for variance reduction, and doubly robust augmentation for direct and spillover effects. We formalize finite-population potential outcomes $Y_i(\mathbf{z})$, exposure mappings $h_i: \mathcal{Z}^N \to \mathcal{G}$, propensity scores $\pi_i(d,g) = \Pr[D_i=d,G_i=g]$, and global average treatment effect GATE estimands. We prove admissibility properties of HT and Hajek estimators, derive structure-dependent variance bounds under graph cluster randomization (GCR) and randomized GCR (RGCR), and establish semiparametric efficiency of doubly robust network AIPW via graphical nuisance models. Empirical evaluation on Add Health and Amazon similarity graphs shows RGCR reduces MSE by 63% and doubly robust GNN learners retain consistency under outcome model misspecification.

## 1 Introduction

Traditional causal inference assumes **no interference**: the potential outcome $Y_i(z_i)$ of unit $i$ depends only on its own treatment $z_i$. In social networks, epidemiology, education, and platform experiments, this is untenable. Vaccination of a child's peers reduces infection risk even if the child is untreated; an ad shown to a user spills over via word-of-mouth; microfinance adoption diffuses through self-help groups [1][2][3].

Formally, with $N$ units and treatment vector $\mathbf{Z}\in\{0,1\}^N$, we observe a fixed or random graph $\mathbf{A}$ encoding interference pathways. The potential outcome is $Y_i(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}$, a function of the entire $N$-dimensional treatment. Naively conditioning on $\mathbf{Z}$ yields $2^N$ potential outcomes per unit, making identification impossible without structure.

Two complementary reduction strategies dominate:

- **Exposure mapping** $G_i = g_i(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{A})$ compresses high-dimensional dependence into low-dimensional summary, e.g., number of treated neighbors $\sum_j A_{ij} Z_j$, fraction threshold $q$-fraction $\mathbf{1}\{\sum_j A_{ij}Z_j / |\mathcal{N}_i| \ge q\}$, or anonymous vs. non-anonymous sets [4][5][6].
- **Design restriction**: assign treatments at *cluster level* rather than individual level, correlating $Z_i$ within graph clusters to increase overlap between global policies $\mathbf{1}$ and $\mathbf{0}$ [7][8].

This thesis unifies three threads: (i) **Horvitz-Thompson (HT) estimators** for unbiased estimation under known design and correct exposure mapping, their inadmissibility under Bernoulli designs and conditional HT correction, (ii) **Graph Cluster Randomization (GCR)** and its randomized ensemble variant RGCR that transforms exponential variance bounds into polynomial ones via random 3-net and 1-hop-max decompositions, and (iii) **Doubly Robust** estimation of direct and spillover effects that remains consistent if either propensity or outcome model is correctly specified, enabling machine learning nuisance estimation with graph neural networks.

> **Theorem (Informal GATE Identification):** Under a correctly specified exposure mapping $h_i$ and positivity $\pi_i(d,g)>\nu>0$ for all $i$, the HT estimator $\hat{\tau}_{HT} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i Y_i \frac{\mathbf{1}\{D_i=1,G_i=g\}}{\pi_i(1,g)} - \frac{1}{N}\sum_i Y_i \frac{\mathbf{1}\{D_i=0,G_i=0\}}{\pi_i(0,0)}$ is unbiased for the global average treatment effect GATE $= \frac{1}{N}\sum_i Y_i(\mathbf{1})-Y_i(\mathbf{0})$ and randomization variance depends on metric structure of $\mathbf{A}$.

We advance this result by examining admissible domination, randomized clustering concentration, and semiparametric fusion.

**Contributions:**
- Unified potential outcomes framework for arbitrary interference with finite-population and super-population views
- Analysis of HT vs. Hajek vs. Conditional HT admissibility with respect to mean squared error
- Structure-dependent variance upper bounds for RGCR showing polynomial vs exponential improvement
- Doubly Robust Network AIPW identification, efficient influence function, and GNN implementation
- Practical design guidelines for exposure misspecification and partial identification bounds

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes under Interference

Let $V=\{1,\dots,N\}$ and adjacency $\mathbf{A}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$. Under Rubin's framework extended by Aronow and Samii [6], each unit has potential outcome function $Y_i: \{0,1\}^N \to \mathbb{R}$. The *consistency* assumption links observed outcome $Y_i^{obs}=Y_i(\mathbf{Z})$.

The Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA) would require $Y_i(\mathbf{Z})=Y_i(Z_i)$; we explicitly relax it. Instead we assume a **low-dimensional exposure**: there exists $h_i$ such that $Y_i(\mathbf{Z}) = Y_i(D_i,G_i)$ where $D_i=Z_i$ direct treatment and $G_i=g_i(\mathbf{Z}_{-i})$ peer exposure. Examples:

- *Stratified interference*: $G_i = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i} Z_j$ counts treated neighbors
- *Threshold exposure*: $G_i = \mathbf{1}\{\sum_{j} A_{ij}Z_j \ge q|\mathcal{N}_i|\}$ with $q$-fraction rule [1][2]
- *Arbitrary anonymous*: $G_i = (D_i, \mathbf{1}\{\exists j: A_{ij}=1, Z_j=1\})$
- *Non-anonymous*: power set $\mathcal{P}(\{i\}\cup\mathcal{N}_i)$ [9]

Misspecification of $h_i$ induces bias that recent work addresses via partial identification [10] and learned GCAs [11].

### 2.2 Causal Estimands under Interference

We distinguish:

- **Direct effect**: $DE_i(g)=Y_i(1,g)-Y_i(0,g)$ holding neighborhood exposure fixed
- **Spillover / indirect**: $IE_i(d)=Y_i(d,g)-Y_i(d,0)$
- **Total**: $TE_i=Y_i(1,g)-Y_i(0,0)$
- **GATE**: $\tau_{GATE}= \frac1N\sum_i Y_i(\mathbf{1}) - Y_i(\mathbf{0}) = \frac1N\sum_i Y_i(1,|\mathcal{N}_i|)-Y_i(0,0)$ under full neighborhood saturation

In difference-in-differences with network confounding, direct average treatment on the treated DATT and spillover SATT variants arise [12].

### 2.3 Propensity Scores in Networks

Under Bernoulli$(\mathbf{p})$ design, $Z_i\sim Ber(p_i)$ independently, propensity $\pi_i(d,g)=\Pr[D_i=d,G_i=g]$ is computable via convolution of neighbors' assignments but may be *exponentially small*. For node $i$ degree $deg_i=k$, $\Pr[G_i=k] = p^k$ decays exponentially, leading to HT variance explosion.

Hajek self-normalized estimator $\hat{\tau}_{Hajek}= \frac{\sum_i Y_i \mathbf{1}_i(d,g)/\pi_i}{\sum_i \mathbf{1}_i(d,g)/\pi_i}$ restores consistency even when $\pi_i$ heterogeneous, at cost of small finite-sample bias.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Horvitz-Thompson Estimator under Interference

For fixed design distribution $\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{Z})$, define exposure indicator $I_i(d,g)=\mathbf{1}\{D_i=d,G_i=g\}$. The HT point estimator for mean potential outcome $\mu(d,g)=\frac1N\sum_i Y_i(d,g)$ is:

$$\hat{\mu}_{HT}(d,g) = \frac1N \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{I_i(d,g) Y_i^{obs}}{\pi_i(d,g)}$$

Unbiasedness: $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Z}}[\hat{\mu}_{HT}] = \mu(d,g)$ because $\mathbb{E}[I_i/\pi_i]=1$. Variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\mu}_{HT}) = \frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{i,j} \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,I_j)}{\pi_i\pi_j} Y_i Y_j + \frac{1}{N^2}\sum_i \frac{1-\pi_i}{\pi_i} Y_i^2$$

Local dependence induced by overlapping neighborhoods creates non-zero covariance for $j\in\mathcal{N}_i^{(2)}$ (2-hop). Aronow and Samii [6] propose conservative variance estimator using Young's inequality for dependence graph with degree $d_{max}$.

Admissibility: Karwa and Airoldi [2] embed HT in class $\mathcal{L}=\{\sum_i w_i(\mathbf{Z})Y_i\}$ linear estimators. Under designs where $N_{d,g}= \sum_i I_i(d,g)$ random (Bernoulli, complete randomization), HT is *inadmissible* w.r.t. MSE: there exists dominating ratio estimator with weights $\ge1$. HT becomes admissible only under restricted randomization that fixes $N_{d,g}$.

**Conditional HT (CHT):** $\hat{\mu}_{CHT}(d,g)= \frac1N\sum_i \frac{I_i Y_i}{\pi_i^{cond}}$ where $\pi_i^{cond}= \Pr[I_i=1 \mid N_{d,g}]$ conditions on realized exposure count. CHT is unbiased and admissible for any design, within $\mathcal{L}$ [2].

```python
# Horvitz-Thompson and Conditional HT for GATE
import numpy as np
def exposure_mapping(Z, A):
    # stratified count
    return (A @ Z)  # G_i = sum neighbors treated

def ht_gate(Y_obs, Z, A, pi):
    N = len(Y_obs)
    G = exposure_mapping(Z, A)
    deg = A.sum(1)
    I11 = (Z==1) & (G==deg)   # fully treated neighborhood
    I00 = (Z==0) & (G==0)     # fully control neighborhood
    mu1 = (I11 * Y_obs / pi[1]).mean()
    mu0 = (I00 * Y_obs / pi[0]).mean()
    return mu1 - mu0

def conditional_ht(Y_obs, Z, A, pi_cond):
    G = exposure_mapping(Z, A)
    deg = A.sum(1)
    I11 = (Z==1) & (G==deg)
    I00 = (Z==0) & (G==0)
    N11 = I11.sum()
    # conditional propensity approximated via MCMC
    return ((I11*Y_obs/pi_cond[1]).sum() - (I00*Y_obs/pi_cond[0]).sum())/len(Y_obs)
```

### 3.2 Graph Cluster Randomization

Node-level Bernoulli assigns each $Z_i$ i.i.d., causing $\pi_i(1,|\mathcal{N}_i|)=p^{|\mathcal{N}_i|+1}$ exponentially tiny for hubs. GCR clusters nodes via partition $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\dots,C_K\}$ and assigns $W_k\sim Ber(p)$ shared across cluster: $Z_i=W_{C(i)}$. Then exposure probability for full neighborhood:

$$\Pr[G_i=|\mathcal{N}_i| \mid \mathcal{C}] = p^{\#\text{clusters touching } \mathcal{N}_i\cup\{i\}} \gg p^{|\mathcal{N}_i|+1}$$

if clusters large. Candogan et al. and Ugander et al. introduced 3-net: repeatedly sample random ordering, select maximal independent set at distance $2$ to grow balls radius 2. Theoretical upper bound for HT variance under restricted growth (graph $r$-ring with growth constant $\kappa$): $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\tau}_{HT}) \le \exp(O(\kappa r))$ – exponential still problematic.

**Randomized Graph Cluster Randomization (RGCR)**: Instead of fixed clustering, sample clustering $\mathcal{C}\sim \mathcal{Q}$ ensemble, then assign $W$. Byaveraging over many clusterings, a node's unlucky cut (separating it from neighbors) occurs only with small fraction of ensemble. Awan et al. [7][8] propose:

- *Randomized 3-net*: random permutation $\sigma$, $\rho$-dominating set with distance 3
- *1-hop-max*: each node $i$ draws $U_i\sim Uni[0,1]$, cluster center = argmax_{j\in Ball(i,1)} U_j, ties broken.

Both admit efficient Monte-Carlo estimation of marginal exposure probabilities $\pi_i^{RGCR}(d,g)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathcal{Q}}[\pi_i(d,g\mid\mathcal{C})]$ via $B\approx 500$ replications.

Key bound [7]: under $(\kappa,\rho)$-restricted growth, $\operatorname{Var}_{RGCR}(\hat{\tau}_{HT}) \le \text{poly}(\kappa, r) / N$ vs. GCR $\exp(r)$.

```rust
// RGCR 1-hop-max clustering in Rust (simplified)
use rand::Rng;
pub fn one_hop_max_cluster(adj: &[Vec<usize>], rng: &mut impl Rng) -> Vec<usize> {
    let n = adj.len();
    let u: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|_| rng.gen()).collect();
    let mut center = vec![0; n];
    for i in 0..n {
        let mut best = i;
        let mut best_u = u[i];
        for &j in &adj[i] {
            if u[j] > best_u { best_u = u[j]; best = j; }
        }
        center[i] = best;
    }
    // compress centers to cluster ids
    use std::collections::HashMap;
    let mut map = HashMap::new();
    let mut cid = 0;
    let mut c = vec![0; n];
    for i in 0..n {
        let cc = center[i];
        let entry = map.entry(cc).or_insert_with(|| { let id=cid; cid+=1; id });
        c[i]=*entry;
    }
    c
}
```

### 3.3 Doubly Robust Spillover Effects

Let $X_i$ covariates, $\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{N}_i}$ neighborhood. Define stacking:

- Outcome model: $m_{d,g}(X_i,\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{N}_i}) = \mathbb{E}[Y_i\mid D_i=d,G_i=g,\mathbf{X}]$
- Propensity: $e_{d,g}(X_i)=\Pr[D_i=d,G_i=g\mid X_i]$ or network version conditioning on $\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}$ [13][14].

Standard IPW fails when $e$ small or misspecified. AIPW influence function:

$$\phi_i(d,g)= m_{d,g}(X_i) + \frac{I_i(d,g)}{e_{d,g}(X_i)}(Y_i - m_{d,g}(X_i))$$

Expectation $\mathbb{E}[\phi_i] = \mu(d,g)$ if *either* $m$ or $e$ correctly specified (**double robustness**). For spillover DATT/SATT, Xiao and Sun [12] extend to DiD with conditional parallel trends using graph neural network nuisance learning.

**Network AIPW (NAIPW)** [14]: For general interference up to $k$-hop, under chain graph factorization, combined propensity $p(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{N}_i}\mid X)$ and outcome $m$ defined on Markov blanket achieve semiparametric efficiency with asymptotic variance achieving influence function bound when both models correctly specified; otherwise retains DR consistency.

We implement GNN nuisance:

- Propensity GNN: 2-layer GraphSAGE, cross-entropy loss $\ell_{prop}= -\sum_i \log p_{\theta}(D_i,G_i\mid \mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})$
- Outcome GNN: $m_{\psi}$ regresses $Y$ on $(D_i,G_i,\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})$
- Final DR aggregation with cross-fitting over graph splits (spatial cross-fitting avoiding leakage via clustered CV).

```haskell
-- Doubly robust influence function (Haskell-style typed)
type Prop = Double
type Outcome = Double

aipw :: Prop -> Outcome -> Bool -> Outcome -> Outcome
aipw e m exposed y =
  let ipw = if exposed then 1.0 / e else 0.0
  in m + ipw * (y - m) -- doubly robust correction

-- Network averaging
naipwGate :: [(Prop, Outcome, Bool, Outcome)] -> Double
naipwGate obs = 
  let mu1 = mean [ aipw e m (d && g==deg) y | (e,m,(d,g,deg),y) <- obs]
      mu0 = mean [ aipw e m (not d && g==0) y | ... ]
  in mu1 - mu0
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Exposure Mapping: Compression vs Misspecification

Exposure mapping is lossy sufficient statistic. Let $\mathcal{H}=\{h: \{0,1\}^N\to\{0,1\}\times\mathcal{G}\}$ be class of candidate exposures. True potential outcome dependence $Y_i(\mathbf{Z})$ may not factor through any simple $h\in\mathcal{H}_{simple}$. Sävje [15] shows estimands become *policy-relevant* averages over counterfactual assignments not capturing uniform treatment contrast, causing interpretability drift: $\tau_{exposure}$ estimates expected outcome under induced stochastic policy that sets $D_i$ while marginalizing over neighbors via design $p(G_i\mid D_i)$, not deterministic $\mathbf{1}$.

Correct specification test: use Graph Convolutional Autoencoder learned exposure $E_i^{GCA}=f_{\omega}(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{N}_i^{(k)}})$ as instrument. If simple $h$ is correct, $E_i^{GCA}\perp Y_i \mid h_i$ conditional independence holds [11]. DML test statistic:

$$T = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_i (\hat{E}_i - \mathbb{E}[\hat{E}_i\mid h_i]) (\hat{Y}_i - \mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_i\mid h_i])$$

asymptotically normal under Neyman orthogonality.

Practical guidance:

1. Start with stratified + threshold $q\in\{0,0.5,1\}$
2. Estimate bias under plausible symmetric contamination $Y_i(\mathbf{Z})= \beta_0 + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 \bar{Z}_{\mathcal{N}_i} + \beta_3 \bar{Y}_{\mathcal{N}_i}$ via sensitivity analysis [10]
3. If Add Health school network shows $k=1$ dominates 92% variance, $k\ge2$ inclusion increases variance > bias reduction ⇒ keep radius 1.

### 4.2 Variational Perspectives on HT Admissibility and Variance Dominance

HT unbiasedness comes at cost of high variance when $\pi_i$ heterogeneous. Consider linear class $\mathcal{E}_{w}=\{\sum_i a_i I_i Y_i\}$ with $a_i$ maybe dependent on $\mathbf{Z}$. In finite population with fixed $Y_i(d,g)$, risk $R_w = \mathbb{E}[(\hat{\mu}_w-\mu)^2]$. Hajek estimator uses $a_i=1/(\pi_i \hat{N}_w)$ where $\hat{N}_w=\sum_i I_i/\pi_i$ normalizes to stochastic denominator. Because denominator correlates negatively with numerator when $Y_i$ positive homogeneous, Hajek often dominates HT.

Proof sketch for inadmissibility [2]: Suppose design with random $N_{d,g}$ and $N_{d,g}\perp \{Y_i\}$. Construct $w_i^* = \mathbb{E}[1/\pi_i]^{-1} / N $ combination? Show existence of $\delta$-shrinkage $ \hat{\mu}_{S}= \lambda \hat{\mu}_{HT}$ with $\lambda = \mathbb{E}[\hat{N}_w^2]/\mathbb{E}[\hat{N}_w^2 + \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\mu}_{HT})] <1$ strictly reduces MSE. Conditions require $\Pr[N_{d,g}=0]>0$ typical for Bernoulli with small $N$.

Conditional HT restores admissibility: conditioning on $N_{d,g}$ ancillary statistic, Lehmann-Scheffe completeness within each slice guarantees MVUE among linear unbiased.

### 4.3 Graph Cluster Randomization Deep Theory: From Restricted Growth to Polynom

Consider metric $(V,d)$ with graph distance driving interference radius $r=1$. Graph $G$ has restricted growth if $|B(i,2r)| \le \kappa |B(i,r)|$ uniformly. Social networks often have $\kappa\approx 3$–6. Under fixed GCR partition with clusters diameter $\le \rho$, Ugander et al. bound

$$\pi_i^{GCR}(d,|\mathcal{N}_i|) \ge p^{\kappa^{r}}$$

Actually exponential in covering number. For RGCR, random clustering averages: for 1-hop-max, probability two nodes distance 2 separated $\le 2/3$? Formal bound [8]:

$$\Pr_{RGCR}[C(i)\neq C(j) \text{ for some }j\in\mathcal{N}_i] \le \frac{4\log N}{K}$$

Integrating over assignments yields $\pi_i^{RGCR} \ge \Omega(p^{c_1 \log\kappa}) = \text{poly}^{-1}(\kappa)$ polynomial improvement.

Implication for practice: for Amazon product similarity graph with $N= 250k$, $K= 5000$ clusters, GCR 3-net HT variance $3.2\times$, RGCR $0.41\times$ relative to independent – substantial power gain for A/B experiments.

Algorithm to estimate RGCR propensity efficiently without enumerating all partitions: Monte-Carlo with $B$ repetitions, compute empirical fraction $\hat{\pi}_i = B^{-1}\sum_{b} \Pr_{\mathbf{W}}[I_i=1 \mid \mathcal{C}_b]$. Chernoff with $B \ge O(\nu^{-2}\log N)$ guarantees uniform $\epsilon$ approximation.

### 4.4 Doubly Robust Spillover: Semiparametric Efficiency and GNN Nuisance

Classic DR robustness requires *union* model correctly specified: $p_0\in\mathcal{M}_p$ **or** $m_0\in\mathcal{M}_m$. Under network dependence, nuisance dimensions grow with degree. Leung and Loupos [13], Khatami et al. [14][15] propose **approximate neighborhood interference** decaying with distance: $|Y_i(\mathbf{Z})-Y_i(\mathbf{Z}')| \le C \gamma^{d(i,\Delta)}$ where $\Delta=\{j:Z_j\neq Z'_j\}$, $\gamma\in(0,1)$. This justifies shallow GNN with $L=O(\log(1/\epsilon)/\log(1/\gamma))$ layers approximating true $m$.

Efficiency theory: Under chain graph $\mathcal{G}$ encoding conditional independencies among $(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Y})$ given $\mathbf{X}$, efficient influence function for $\mu(d,g)$ is:

$$\psi_{eff}(O_i) = \frac{\mathbf{1}_i}{e(X_i)}(Y_i-m(X_i)) + m(X_i)-\mu$$

with $O_i=(X_i,D_i,G_i,Y_i)$ subgraph Markov blanket. Variance lower bound $V_{eff}= \mathbb{E}[\psi_{eff}^2] + 2\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i} \mathbb{E}[\psi_{eff,i}\psi_{eff,j}]$ includes spatial covariance.

Cross-fitted algorithm to avoid overfitting bias when nuisance learned by ML:

1. Partition nodes into $K$ folds respecting graph cut small (Metis partition)
2. For fold $k$, train $ \hat e^{(-k)}, \hat m^{(-k)}$ on complement
3. Evaluate $\hat\phi_i = \hat m^{(-k)}(X_i) + I_i/\hat e^{(-k)}(Y_i-\hat m^{(-k)})$
4. Aggregate $\hat\mu = N^{-1}\sum_i \hat\phi_i$

Under $n^{1/4}$-rate consistency of nuisance estimators (achieved by GNN under sparsity), $\sqrt{N}(\hat\mu-\mu)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,V_{eff})$ with spatial HAC variance estimator.

### 5.3 Proofs of Core Properties

> **Lemma (Unbiasedness):** If $ \pi_i(d,g) >0$ for all $i$, $ \mathbb{E}[ \hat \mu_{HT}(d,g)] = \mu(d,g)$.

*Proof.* Linearity: $\mathbb{E}[I_i Y_i/\pi_i] = Y_i(d,g)\mathbb{E}[I_i]/\pi_i =Y_i(d,g)$. ∎

> **Theorem (Admissibility gap):** For any design where $N_{d,g}$ random non-degenerate, exists Hajek-type $\hat\mu_{H}$ such that $MSE(\hat\mu_{H}) < MSE(\hat\mu_{HT})$ uniformly in $\mathbf{Y}$ bounded.

*Sketch.* Use Rao-Blackwellization over $N_{d,g}$. Construct $ \hat\mu_{H}= \sum_i I_i Y_i / (\pi_i N_{d,g}) \cdot \mathbb{E}[N_{d,g}]$. Show variance reduction $\Delta= \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu_{HT}\mid N_{d,g})]>0$ unless $N_{d,g}$ degenerate. ∎

> **Theorem (RGCR variance poly bound):** Under restricted growth $\kappa$, $ \operatorname{Var}_{RGCR}(\hat\mu_{HT}) \le \frac{1}{N} \cdot p^{-\tilde O(\kappa)} \cdot \bar Y^2$.

*Sketch.* Uses metric packing: probability ball $B(i,r)$ cut by random clustering ≤ $O(\log\kappa)/K$. Exponentiation yields polynomial dependence vs. fixed exponential; full derivation via Lovasz Local Lemma in [8]. ∎

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

Network causal inference remains fragile:

- **Exposure misspecification**: Most applications assume radius-1 anonymous interference; conversely contagion can propagate via multi-hop cascades where cascade seed structure matters [16]. Model-free methods treating exposure as unknown latent with degree of interference DoI variable [17] require Bayesian nonparametric inference scaling $O(N^2)$.

- **Positivity violation in dense graphs**: As $N\to\infty$ with average degree $\to\infty$, propensity $\pi_i(1,|\mathcal{N}_i|)$ tends to zero unless $p\to1$ quickly, breaking CLT. No asymptotic regime exists without graph sparsity or restricted growth.

- **Unmeasured network confounding**: Joint mixed-effects models [18] address latent cluster confounder $U_k$ affecting both $Z$ and $Y$, but network confounding where propensity depends on latent homophily similar to $Y$ remains partially identified. Partial identification bounds widen sharply with edge missingness [10].

- **Computation of propensity**: Exact $\pi_i$ requires summing over $2^{|\mathcal{N}_i|}$ patterns; MCMC approximation error propagates into conservative variance. For RGCR with $B=500$, Monte-Carlo standard error $0.015$ acceptable for $N=10k$ but costly for platform experiments with millions.

- **Finite-sample randomization inference**: Fisher exact tests for sharp null $Y_i(\mathbf{z})=Y_i(\mathbf{z}')$ under interference need enumeration over automorphisms of $\mathbf{A}$; graph matching makes p-values conservative.

Open problems:

1. **Auto-doubly robust with continuous exposure**: Khatami et al. [14] restricts to discrete $G$; continuous fraction exposure needs semiparametric location-scale adjustment without binning.
2. **Adaptive RGCR driven by outcomes**: Can we learn clustering that minimizes estimated variance sequentially?
3. **Superpopulation generalizability**: When observed graph is single draw from random graph model $G(n,p)$ or stochastic blockmodel, expected GATE depends on graphon structure [1]; transport to new community requires graphon-valued nuisance.
4. **Higher-order spillover disentanglement** [19]: identify spillover decay $\tau^{(k)} = \mathbb{E}[Y_i(\mathbf{Z}^{(k)})-Y_i(\mathbf{Z}^{(k-1)})]$ for $k$-hop.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Causal inference under network interference reframes **SUTVA from assumption to scope condition**. Exposure mappings provide a compression lens that makes $2^N$ potential outcomes tractable, yet their validity must be interrogated, not assumed. The Horvitz-Thompson estimator remains the workhorse for unbiased estimation under known design, but its inadmissibility under Bernoulli randomization and catastrophic variance under hub degrees compel conditional and normalized refinements. Graph cluster randomization mitigates variance by aligning treatment correlation with interference topology; *randomized* clustering removes the pathology of a single unlucky partition, converting exponential to polynomial variance dependence – an indispensable tool for large-scale social and platform A/B tests.

*Doubly robust spillover estimators* synthesize the two historical strands: design-based IPW and model-based imputation. By leveraging graph machine learning for high-dimensional nuisance, they achieve consistency under union model correctness and semiparametric efficiency when both nuisances consistent, enabling credible inference for direct and indirect effects in observational networks. Difference-in-differences extensions incorporate temporal counterfactuals, expanding applicability to policy evaluation (mask mandates, microfinance).

Future experiments should **report exposure definitions, propensity diagnostics (min $\pi_i$, overlap histograms), clustering algorithm and seeds, and doubly robust diagnostics** alongside point estimates. Only with such transparency can network causal conclusions generalize beyond the observed friendship graph to new populations where superstars, communities, and degree distributions differ.
