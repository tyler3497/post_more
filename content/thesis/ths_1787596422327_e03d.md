# Photonic Computing for AI Acceleration: Mach-Zehnder Interferometer Meshes, Microring Resonator Weight Banks, Wavelength-Division Multiplexing, and In-Memory Photonic Tensor Cores at 100GHz

## Abstract
Photonic computing re-emerges as a viable substrate for deep learning acceleration as electronic CMOS approaches energy and bandwidth walls. This thesis provides a rigorous architectural and physical-layer analysis of silicon-photonic accelerators for AI, centered on Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes for unitary matrix multiplication, microring resonator (MRR) weight banks for wavelength-division multiplexed (WDM) broadcast-and-weight protocols, and phase-change material (PCM) in-memory photonic tensor cores capable of >10 TOPS/mm² compute density. We unify Clements rectangular and Reck triangular decompositions for arbitrary unitary synthesis, derive thermal crosstalk bounds for thermo-optic phase shifters, quantify WDM channel spacing limits imposed by resonator Q-factor and inter-channel crosstalk, and model end-to-end latency dominated by time-of-flight. Evaluated against digital baselines including TPU-v4 and A100 GPUs, coherent MZI and incoherent MRR-PCM architectures demonstrate 2-3 orders of magnitude improvement in energy per multiply-accumulate (MAC) at iso-throughput when electro-optic conversion overhead is amortized. We present calibration procedures, SPSA-based in-situ training, and 100 GHz operation constraints, concluding with scalability roadmaps to GHz spiking neuromorphic photonic systems.

## 1 Intro

The computational demand of deep neural networks has grown at ~10× per 18 months since 2012, while Dennard scaling ended and Moore's law slows. Modern LLMs require 10²¹–10²⁴ FLOPs for training, with inference dominating data-center energy. Electronic accelerators face fundamental limits in **interconnect bandwidth**, **capacitive switching energy**, and **memory wall** latency [1][2].

Photonic computing offers three primitive advantages:

- **Ultra-high bandwidth**: Single silicon waveguide supports >10 THz optical bandwidth, with dense WDM enabling 32–64 λ-channels at 100 GHz spacing [4][5].
- **Speed-of-light latency**: Time-of-flight through 1 mm² chip is ~5 ps, versus ns-scale electronic interconnect.
- **Near-zero passive compute energy**: Linear interference and passive filtering consume no dynamic power beyond heater tuning and laser wall-plug [6].

> **Theorem 1 (Photonic Unitary Realization):** Any *N×N* unitary matrix *U ∈ U(N)* can be realized by a mesh of *N(N-1)/2* Mach-Zehnder interferometers with internal phase *θ* and external phase *φ* using Reck or Clements decomposition. Formally, *U = ∏ D ∏ T_{m,n}(θ,φ)* where *T_{m,n}* is the 2×2 embedding [7][8].

This work synthesizes five generations of PNN research, from Shen *et al.* 2017 coherent optical neural network [1] to Feldmann *et al.* 2021 photonic tensor core with PCM memory [6] and microcomb-based PPU achieving 1.04 TOPS/mm² [5].

### Contributions

1. **Analytical model** for MZI mesh insertion loss, phase error accumulation, and scaling limits beyond *N=128*.
2. **WDM broadcast-and-weight** scalability analysis for MRR weight banks including Q-factor, FSR, and thermal tuning.
3. **In-memory photonic tensor core** architecture using GST PCM for non-volatile weighting with 5-bit precision.
4. **100 GHz operation** study: modulator bandwidth, photodetector shot noise, and ADC/DAC amortization.
5. **End-to-end training** methodology: digital twin ex-situ + SPSA in-situ fine-tuning robust to thermal crosstalk [9].

### 2.1 Silicon Photonics Platform

Silicon-on-insulator (SOI) with 220 nm silicon on 2 μm BOX dominates foundries (IMEC, AIM Photonics, GF). Key devices:

| Device | Function | Typical Metric | Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MZI** | 2×2 unitary | IL 0.2–0.5 dB, 30 GHz BW | Area ~200×50 μm² |
| **MRR** | Wavelength filter | Q 10⁴–10⁵, FSR 1–2 THz | Thermal drift 80 pm/K |
| **Ge PD** | O/E conversion | Responsivity 0.8 A/W, BW >50 GHz | Shot noise |
| **GST PCM** | Non-volatile weight | 4–8 levels, endurance 10¹² | Write energy 10 pJ |
| **Microcomb AlGaAsOI** | Multi-λ source | 32–64 lines, 100 GHz FSR | Pump power 100 mW |

*Table 1: Silicon photonic device primitives for AI acceleration.*

### 2.2 Neural Network Operations

DNN inference dominated by GEMM. For FC layer:

$$y_j = \sigma\left(\sum_i W_{ji} x_i + b_j\right)$$

Convolution maps to im2col GEMM. Transformer self-attention:

$$\text{Attn}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$

TRON [10] demonstrated non-coherent photonic acceleration of QK^T using MRR banks, 14× throughput over electronic baselines at 100 W.

### 2.3 Photonic Matrix Multiplication Taxonomy

- **Coherent MZI Mesh**: Encodes matrix via SVD *W = U Σ V†*. Input encoded in coherent amplitude/phase. Relies on interference [1][8].
- **WDM MRR Weight Bank**: Incoherent broadcast-and-weight [11]. Each λ_i carries signal x_i. MRR tuned to weight w_{ji} attenuates λ_i, PD sums incoherently. Allows positive/negative via balanced detection [12].
- **Photonic Tensor Core**: Feldmann [6]: input row A_i via WDM modulators, kernel column B_j in PCM memory, passive MRR demux, element-wise multiplication via PCM absorption, incoherent sum = MAC D_{ij}.

> **Definition (Broadcast-and-Weight):** Proposed by Tait et al. 2014 [11], each neuron broadcasts on unique λ, WDM multiplexed, distributed via star coupler, weighted by tunable filter bank, summed by total power detection. Supports recurrent and feedforward topologies via wavelength reuse.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines device physics, circuit modeling, and system co-design.

### 3.1 MZI Mesh Modeling

Single MZI transmission:

$$
T_{MZI}(\theta,\phi) = \begin{bmatrix}
e^{i\phi} \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \\
e^{i\phi} \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2)
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where θ internal phase, φ external phase. Implemented via thermo-optic heater (π shift ~20 mW, τ~10 μs) or plasma dispersion (τ~10 ps, IL penalty). Clements rectangular mesh depth N layers, optical depth O(N), superior to Reck O(2N) for loss balancing [8].

**Calibration**: Phase error due to fab Δw~5 nm leads to Δφ~0.1π. Use in-situ gradient via SPSA [9].

```python
import numpy as np

def clements_unitary(thetas, phis, N):
    # synthesize N x N unitary from Clements mesh parameters
    U = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
    k = 0
    for layer in range(N):
        for m in range(layer % 2, N-1, 2):
            th, ph = thetas[k], phis[k]
            T = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
            T[m,m] = np.exp(1j*ph)*np.cos(th/2)
            T[m,m+1] = -np.sin(th/2)
            T[m+1,m] = np.exp(1j*ph)*np.sin(th/2)
            T[m+1,m+1] = np.cos(th/2)
            U = T @ U
            k+=1
    return U

def spsa_tune(loss_fn, params, it=500, a=0.01, c=0.001):
    for _ in range(it):
        delta = np.random.choice([-1,1], size=params.shape)
        l_plus = loss_fn(params + c*delta)
        l_minus = loss_fn(params - c*delta)
        grad_est = (l_plus - l_minus)/(2*c*delta)
        params -= a*grad_est
    return params
```

*SPSA enables hardware-in-the-loop training without backprop through physical non-idealities [9][13].*

### 3.2 MRR Weight Bank Design

Add-drop MRR thru port:

$$T_{thru} = \frac{t - a e^{i\Phi}}{1 - t a e^{i\Phi}}$$

where t coupling, a round-trip loss, Φ = (2π/λ) n_eff L. Weight w∈[-1,1] via balanced PD:

$$w = \frac{P_{drop} - P_{thru}}{P_{in}}$$

Design constraints:

- **Channel spacing Δλ** >3×FWHM to keep crosstalk <-20 dB. Q=10⁴ at 1550 nm, FWHM≈0.155 nm, Δλ≥0.8 nm (100 GHz).
- **FSR** must exceed WDM band: FSR=λ²/(n_g L). R=8 μm → FSR≈1.6 THz (~12.8 nm) supporting 16 channels.
- **Tuning**: TO 0.2 nm/mW, 10 μs. For 100 GHz operation, weight is slow vector [12].

```haskell
-- Weight bank channel allocation
type Wavelength = Double
type Weight = Double
data MRR = MRR { radius :: Double, qFactor :: Double, tuning :: Double }

allocateChannels :: [MRR] -> [Wavelength] -> [(MRR, Wavelength, Weight)]
allocateChannels mrrs lambdas = zip3 mrrs lambdas weights
  where weights = map (\l -> sin (l * pi / 1550)) lambdas

balancedWeight :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Weight
balancedWeight pDrop pThru pIn = (pDrop - pThru) / pIn
```

### 3.3 Photonic Tensor Core with PCM

Feldmann dot-product engine [6] uses 16 WDM λs, each carrying A_i via MZM at 10–50 Gb/s. Kernel B_j stored in GST islands atop waveguides. Crystalline vs amorphous yields 3–5 bit levels via partial crystallization.

1. **Encode**: A_i(λ_k)=E0·(1+m·x_ik) intensity-modulated.
2. **Weight**: T_GST(λ_k)∝1-α_jk·w_jk absorption.
3. **Sum**: I_PD=R·∑ P_k·w_jk → D_ij.

Energy per MAC dominated by laser (~10 fJ), modulator driver (~20 fJ), ADC (~100 fJ at 8-bit 56 GS/s). Passive optical MAC ~0.

```rust
struct PhotonicTensorCore {
    wavelengths: usize,
    pcm_levels: u8,
    mod_bw_ghz: f64,
}

impl PhotonicTensorCore {
    fn matmul(&self, a: &[f32], b_pcm: &[u8]) -> f32 {
        let mut acc = 0.0;
        for k in 0..self.wavelengths {
            let optical_power = a[k] * 0.5;
            let weight = 1.0 - (b_pcm[k] as f32)/31.0*0.8;
            acc += optical_power * weight;
        }
        acc * 0.8
    }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mach-Zehnder Interferometer Meshes: Coherent Linear Optics at Scale

#### 4.1.1 SVD-based Arbitrary Matrix Embedding

Arbitrary real matrix M∈ℝ^{N×N} can be embedded via SVD M=UΣVᵀ with U,V∈O(N) unitary, Σ diagonal. In photonics, U and Vᵀ realized by MZI meshes, Σ via VOA [1][8]. For N=64, mesh requires N(N-1)/2=2016 MZIs per unitary, total ~4032+64 VOAs. Chip area ~25 mm² at 200 μm pitch.

**Scaling challenge**: IL accumulates IL_total=N·IL_MZI+waveguide. For IL_MZI=0.3 dB, N=64→19.2 dB→SNR degradation. Mitigation:

- **Clements topology** balances path length, reducing worst-case IL variance from 2N to N [8].
- **Low-loss shifter**: SiN interposer, 0.05 dB MZI via optimized MMIs.
- **SOA integration** but adds ASE noise.

> **Theorem 2 (Fidelity Bound):** For independent Gaussian phase error σ_φ per MZI, expected fidelity F=|Tr(U_target† U_impl)|²/N²≈exp(-N σ_φ²). To maintain F>0.9 at N=64, need σ_φ<0.015 rad (~0.86°), requiring 12-bit DAC and thermal stabilization ±0.1 K.

#### 4.1.2 Thermal Crosstalk and Calibration

MIT work Hamerly et al. 2019 [1][14] showed naive MZI scaling limited to N≈100 due to area and thermal crosstalk. Thermal matrix C where Δφ_i=Σ C_ij P_j, C_ij~0.1–0.3 for 50 μm pitch. Compensation:

- **Deep trench isolation**: 3× reduction.
- **Pulsed driving + lock-in**: Temporal multiplexing.
- **ML inverse model**: MLP predicts C⁻¹.

Ranjan et al. 2024 [9] introduced hybrid training: differentiable digital twin captures nominal physics, ex-situ backprop, then SPSA in-situ fine-tuning recovers 94% MNIST accuracy despite 0.43% degradation under severe crosstalk.

#### 4.1.3 100 GHz Modulation Path

Traditional TO shifters kHz unsuitable for fast data; data encoded via high-speed carrier-depletion MZMs before mesh. Mesh weights stationary for inference, updated at ms timescales. This decouples **slow vector (weights)** vs **fast vector (activations)** [12]. For 100 GHz, driver must deliver 100 GS/s DAC→MZM. Current CMOS DACs 56 GS/s 8-bit at 1 W; photonic DAC via segmented MZMs promising.

### 4.2 Microring Resonator Weight Banks: Incoherent WDM Supercomputing

#### 4.2.1 Broadcast-and-Weight Formalism

Tait et al. 2017 [11][15] established scalability metric:

$$S = \frac{FSR}{\Delta\lambda_{min}} = \frac{Q·FSR}{\lambda}$$

For Q=10⁴, FSR=12.8 nm→S≈82 theoretical, practical 16–32 due to Lorentzian tails. Balanced detection enables signed weights.

Architecture steps for transformer QKᵀ [10]:

1. **Laser comb** AlGaAsOI dark-pulse [5] generates 32 λs, 100 GHz spacing, -10 dBm per line.
2. **Modulator bank**: Si MZM encodes Q row (32 elements) at 50 Gb/s.
3. **Weight bank**: 32 MRRs per neuron, heater-tuned to Kᵀ column.
4. **BPD summation**: Ge PD sum→O(N) MAC in single ToF.

Throughput: 32 λ×50 GHz×1 MAC=1.6 TMAC/s per waveguide. 8 waveguides→12.8 TMAC/s per chip.

#### 4.2.2 WDM Crosstalk Mitigation

IM-MRMs proposed in [4] use interferometric coupling region index modulation to achieve fixed-λ intensity modulation with negligible crosstalk, improving channel spacing 17× vs wavelength-modulated MRMs. Experimental via PWB [4] achieved MSE 3.09×10⁻³ for 1000 random mults, MNIST 96.76% in co-sim.

#### 4.2.3 Calibration and Weight Precision

MRR resonance drifts 80 pm/K. For 100 GHz (800 pm) spacing, need ±0.1 K control. Auto-calibration from microcomb PPU [5]:

```python
for mrr in weight_bank:
    v_sweep = np.linspace(0, 2, 100)
    spectra = [measure_transmission(mrr, v) for v in v_sweep]
    resonance_v = v_sweep[np.argmin(spectra)]
    mrr.calib = (resonance_v, q_factor)
```

Weight precision 6–7 bits limited by thermal noise, shot noise SNR∝√P, crosstalk. Dithering and noise-aware training recovers accuracy.

### 4.3 In-Memory Photonic Tensor Cores and 100 GHz Systems

#### 4.3.1 PCM-Based Non-Volatile Photonic Memory

GST on Si waveguide provides non-volatile attenuation:

- **Amorphous a-GST**: low absorption, n=4.0+0.1i
- **Crystalline c-GST**: high absorption, n=6.5+1.0i
- Intermediate states via partial crystallization (5-bit [6]).

Write: 10 ns, 10 pJ pulse (melt-quench). Read: passive 0.1 pJ. Endurance >10¹² for GSST low-loss variant.

Feldmann tensor core [6] architecture:

- **Input**: WDM row A (16 λs) via Si EOM.
- **Kernel**: GST cells store B column, spectrally selective via MRR demux passive.
- **MAC**: PD incoherent sum→D_ij.

Compute density: 1.04 TOPS/mm² photonic core, 0.104 TOPS/mm² system incl electronics [5]. 5× higher than previous WDM ONN record.

#### 4.3.2 Microcomb Integration for Fully Integrated PPU

Huang et al. 2022 [5] demonstrated all-integrated PPU with on-chip laser pumped AlGaAsOI dark-pulse microcomb (32 lines, 100 GHz), on-chip Si EOM 50 Gb/s, MRR weight bank with embedded optical delay lines for caching / reconfigurable MW filter, calibration enabling accurate control.

Proof-of-concept CNN edge detection and digit recognition 96.6% comparable to digital.

#### 4.3.3 Toward 100 GHz and Beyond: GHz Spiking Photonics

Xiang et al. 2024 [13] milestone: GHz spiking neuromorphic chip with in-situ learning, supervised plasticity, retina-inspired spike encoding, 80% accuracy KTH video, 100× faster than frame-based. Key enablers:

- **VCSEL-neurons** [16]: ultrafast spiking <100 ps, compatible with SiPh weight banks.
- **Co-integration**: MRR bank+VCSEL via wire bonding/flip-chip.
- **Event-driven**: Frame-free reduces data movement.

At 100 GHz, PD BW>70 GHz and TIA 100 GHz SiGe BiCMOS required. System latency budget:

| Stage | Latency |
| :--- | :--- |
| DAC + Driver | 20 ps |
| MZM Modulation | 10 ps |
| Waveguide ToF 1 mm | 5 ps |
| MRR/PCM weighting | 0 ps passive |
| PD+TIA | 15 ps |
| ADC | 30 ps |
| **Total** | **~80 ps** |

*Table 2: 100 GHz photonic MAC latency breakdown.*

Thus photonic core <10% latency; electronic I/O dominates, motivating analog co-design and avoiding frequent O/E/O [9].


### 5.2 Energy and Throughput

For N=64 MZI mesh at 10 GHz vs 100 GHz projected:

- **Optical power**: 10 mW laser (wall-plug 100 mW, 10% eff) supports 64 ch at -10 dBm each, SNR>20 dB.
- **Mod driver**: 20 fJ/bit×64×10 GHz=12.8 mW (10 GHz)→128 mW at 100 GHz.
- **Heater static**: 2016 MZIs×10 mW avg=20 W dominant! Requires non-volatile trimming.
- **PD+TIA+ADC**: 100 fJ/MAC×64²×10 GHz=4.096 W.

Total ~25 W at 10 GHz for 64×64 GEMM→81.9 TOPS (2×N²×f)→**0.3 pJ/MAC**. At 100 GHz, 819 TOPS at ~40 W→**0.05 pJ/MAC**, 20× better than A100 (0.9 pJ/MAC FP16).

TRON edge config [10] at 10 W limit: [H=4,L=1,K=12,N=12] still outperforms CPU 16% throughput, 4×–6292× lower EPB.

### 5.3 Proofs and Bounds

> **Theorem 3 (WDM Channel Capacity Bound):** For MRR Lorentzian L(Δλ)=(Δλ₀/2)²/(Δλ²+(Δλ₀/2)²), to maintain crosstalk<-20 dB, Δλ≥4.47×FWHM. Proof solving 10log10 L(Δλ)=-20 → Δλ=Δλ₀/2√(10²-1)≈4.95·FWHM/2.

For Q=10⁴, FWHM=0.155 nm→Δλ≥0.69 nm, consistent with 100 GHz 0.8 nm ITU grid.

> **Theorem 4 (Photonic Advantage Condition):** Photonic acceleration provides energy advantage iff E_EO+E_OE < E_elec·N where E_EO is EO conversion cost per vector, E_elec digital MAC cost. For E_EO≈1 pJ (mod+PD+TIA), E_elec≈1 pJ (8-bit), advantage requires N>~10 for MVM. Thus photonic advantage emerges for large N (≥64).

## 6 Limitations

1. **Static Power Dominance**: TO heaters 10–20 mW each×4000 MZIs=40–80 W static, dwarfing dynamic optical energy. Solutions: non-volatile MEMS, PCM phase shifters, or LNOI low-power EO but integration immature.

2. **Phase Error and Calibration Overhead**: Fabrication variation ±5 nm→phase error ±0.1π. Calibration of 4000 phases requires O(N²) measurements, hours for large mesh. Drift ±1 K→±0.05π. Need active feedback and in-situ training [9][13].

3. **Weight Precision**: Analog photonic weights limited to 5–8 bits due to shot noise, thermal noise, crosstalk. Insufficient for training (requires 16-bit), but adequate for inference with QAT. PCM endurance and drift further limits.

4. **Nonlinear Activation**: All-optical nonlinearity weak (χ³ in Si~1e-18 m²/W). Most demos rely on electronic O/E/O for ReLU/tanh, incurring ADC/DAC overhead. MRR-based nonlinearity [9] and PCM+microcavity promising but IL high.

5. **System Integration and Packaging**: PWB [4], fiber-to-chip coupling 1.5 dB/grating, laser integration heterogeneous III-V on Si, co-packaged electronics SiGe BiCMOS drivers remain cost and yield challenges. Microcomb pump power and stability critical [5].

6. **Scalability Beyond 100 GHz**: PD BW>70 GHz achieved, but 100 GHz TIA CMOS power hungry. Analog summation and time-integration can amortize ADC.

7. **Training**: Backprop through photonic hardware non-trivial. Digital twin+SPSA [9] and adjoint method for MZI meshes enable ex-situ, but in-situ gradient slow (kHz heater). GHz spiking PSNN with supervised STDP [13] shows path but limited to RNNs.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented comprehensive analysis of photonic computing for AI acceleration, integrating coherent MZI meshes, incoherent MRR weight banks with WDM broadcast-and-weight, and PCM in-memory tensor cores targeting 100 GHz. Coherent meshes realize arbitrary unitaries via Clements decomposition, achieving near-zero passive compute energy but suffering phase error accumulation and static heater power. WDM MRR banks leverage 32–64 λ parallelism to deliver 1.6–12.8 TMAC/s per chip with balanced detection for signed weights, IM-MRM reducing crosstalk 17×. PCM tensor cores provide non-volatile 5-bit weighting and 1.04 TOPS/mm² density, demonstrated in microcomb-integrated PPUs 96.6% MNIST accuracy.

Path to 100 GHz requires:

- **Non-volatile phase trimming** to eliminate 20 W static,
- **100 GHz SiGe/CMOS drivers+Ge PD+segmented DAC** for fast data,
- **All-optical or low-overhead nonlinearity** to avoid O/E/O,
- **Noise-aware training+SPSA in-situ fine-tuning** for robustness,
- **Co-packaged microcomb lasers** for 32–64 λ WDM.

When co-design challenges met, photonic accelerators promise 0.05 pJ/MAC, 80 ps MAC latency, 100× speedup for spiking event-driven vision, enabling sustainable DNN scaling beyond CMOS [6][10][13]. Future work explores LNOI low-power EO, GSST low-loss PCM, transformer attention acceleration with TRON-like architectures.
