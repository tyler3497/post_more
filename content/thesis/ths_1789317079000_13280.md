# The Holographic Principle and AdS/CFT: Bulk Reconstruction, Entanglement Wedges, and Quantum Error Correction in Gravity

## Abstract

The holographic principle asserts that a quantum theory of gravity in a spatial region can be fully described by degrees of freedom living on the region's boundary, with information capacity scaling as area rather than volume. Its sharpest realization is the AdS/CFT correspondence, which equates quantum gravity in asymptotically anti-de Sitter space with a conformal field theory on its conformal boundary [1]. This article develops the modern machinery by which the bulk spacetime is *reconstructed* from boundary data: the Ryu–Takayanagi prescription relating entanglement entropy to minimal bulk surfaces [2], its covariant and quantum-corrected refinements [6], the emergence of the entanglement wedge as the true domain of subregion duality, and the theorem of Dong, Harlow, and Wall establishing reconstruction within it [5]. We explain why all of these phenomena are manifestations of a single underlying structure: AdS/CFT is a quantum error-correcting code [3], with concrete toy models furnished by holographic tensor networks such as the HaPPY code [4]. The synthesis recasts locality, horizons, and even spacetime itself as consequences of entanglement and redundancy in the boundary description.

## 1 Introduction

In 1973, Jacob Bekenstein argued that black holes carry entropy proportional to the area of their event horizons, and Stephen Hawking's discovery of black hole radiation fixed the coefficient at one quarter of the horizon area in Planck units. Gravitational entropy thus counts *surface* degrees of freedom, violating the extensivity expected of any local quantum field theory. Gerard 't Hooft elevated the observation to a principle: the physics inside any spacetime region should admit a description in terms of boundary degrees of freedom, at most one bit per Planck area. Leonard Susskind gave the idea its name — the *holographic principle*.

The principle became concrete in 1997, when Juan Maldacena conjectured an exact duality between type IIB string theory on AdS_5 × S^5 and four-dimensional N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory on the AdS boundary [1]: a non-perturbative definition of quantum gravity in asymptotically AdS spacetimes, phrased entirely in conventional quantum field theory language.

Yet the duality raises a question as deep as the one it answers: if the boundary theory *is* the bulk theory, how is the bulk — its local fields, causal structure, and emergent radial direction — *rebuilt* from boundary data? This is the problem of *bulk reconstruction*. Three developments transformed it:

1. The Ryu–Takayanagi formula, which geometrizes entanglement entropy [2].
2. The discovery that reconstruction is limited not by causal reach but by entanglement — the *entanglement wedge* [5].
3. The recognition that these facts follow from *quantum error correction*: the boundary encodes the bulk redundantly, so bulk information survives erasure of boundary subregions [3] [4].

We develop each thread and weave them together, arriving at a picture in which spacetime itself is an error-correcting code written in entanglement.

## 2 Background

### The geometry of anti-de Sitter space

AdS_{d+1} is the maximally symmetric solution of Einstein's equations with negative cosmological constant. In Poincaré coordinates,

$$ds^2 = \frac{L^2}{z^2}\left(-dt^2 + dz^2 + d\vec{x}^2\right),$$

with AdS radius L and the conformal boundary at z = 0. The warp factor makes proper distances diverge near the boundary, yet light reaches it in finite coordinate time — the geometric origin of the UV/IR correspondence: short-distance CFT physics probes the near-boundary region, while long-wavelength physics probes the deep interior. The radial direction *is* the renormalization-group scale.

### The holographic dictionary

Maldacena's duality [1] relates the two descriptions through

$$\frac{L^4}{\alpha'^2} = g_{YM}^2 N \equiv \lambda, \qquad 4\pi g_s = g_{YM}^2,$$

with 't Hooft coupling λ, string tension α', and string coupling g_s. The large-N limit (λ fixed) suppresses string loops; the further strong-coupling limit λ → ∞ suppresses α' corrections, leaving classical supergravity. Here the duality is computationally powerful: strongly coupled gauge theory maps to weakly curved classical gravity.

Its operational content is the GKPW relation, equating the CFT generating functional with the string partition function under prescribed boundary conditions:

$$\left\langle \exp\left(\int d^dx\,\phi_0(x)\,\mathcal{O}(x)\right)\right\rangle_{CFT} = Z_{string}[\phi \to \phi_0 \text{ at boundary}].$$

The *extrapolate dictionary* makes the map concrete:

$$\lim_{z \to 0} z^{-\Delta}\,\phi(z,x) = \mathcal{O}(x),$$

with the conformal dimension fixed by m^2 L^2 = Δ(Δ - d).

### Global bulk reconstruction: HKLL

Can a local bulk operator φ(X) be expressed using only boundary operators? For a free field in fixed AdS, Hamilton, Kabat, Lifschytz, and Lowe answered yes: the bulk field is a *smeared* boundary operator,

$$\phi(X) = \int d^dx\,K(X|x)\,\mathcal{O}(x) + \text{(corrections)},$$

with the kernel K supported on boundary points spacelike-separated from X. But this uses the *entire* boundary. The sharper question — what can be reconstructed from a *subregion* — leads to error correction.

## 3 Methodology

We reconstruct the bulk first globally, then within subregions, and finally reinterpret the procedure in quantum-information language.

**Step 1 — Smearing functions.** The HKLL construction is the baseline: bulk operators as non-local boundary operators with spacelike-supported kernels, establishing that bulk locality is *emergent* rather than fundamental.

**Step 2 — Entanglement as geometry.** We promote the Ryu–Takayanagi formula [2] to a foundational principle: the entanglement entropy of a boundary region *is* the area of a bulk extremal surface. Its covariant and quantum-corrected forms [6] define the entanglement wedge precisely.

**Step 3 — Relative entropy and reconstruction.** Following Jafferis, Lewkowycz, Maldacena, and Suh, we use the equality of boundary and bulk relative entropies to prove, as Dong, Harlow, and Wall did [5], that every bulk operator in the entanglement wedge of a boundary region A admits a representation supported entirely on A.

**Step 4 — The code picture.** We translate the theorem into operator-algebra quantum error correction [3]: the low-energy bulk Hilbert space is a *code subspace* of the CFT, and erasing a boundary region is correctable exactly when the complement contains the relevant entanglement wedge. Holographic tensor networks [4] make the mechanism explicit.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Ryu–Takayanagi formula and its descendants

In 2006, Ryu and Takayanagi proposed that the entanglement entropy S_A of a boundary region A equals the area of the minimal bulk surface γ_A homologous to A [2]:

$$S_A = \frac{\text{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G_N}.$$

The resemblance to the Bekenstein–Hawking formula is deliberate: horizons are a special case of the general phenomenon that *geometry measures entanglement*. For an interval in a two-dimensional CFT the formula reproduces the universal result S_A = (c/3) log(l/ε), and it was later derived via the replica trick and Euclidean quantum gravity.

Two generalizations followed. Hubeny, Rangamani, and Takayanagi replaced the static minimal surface with an *extremal* surface for time-dependent settings. Faulkner, Lewkowycz, and Maldacena computed the leading quantum correction, S_A = Area(γ_A)/4G_N + S_bulk(Σ_A) + …, and Engelhardt and Wall conjectured the exact answer comes from extremizing the *generalized entropy* — area plus bulk entropy — defining the *quantum extremal surface* [6]. At every order the lesson is identical: boundary entanglement is bulk geometry, corrected systematically by bulk quantum effects.

> **Theorem (Ryu–Takayanagi, static case).** *Let A be a spatial subregion of the boundary of an asymptotically AdS spacetime with a moment of time-reflection symmetry. Then the von Neumann entropy of the reduced CFT state ρ_A equals the area of the minimal bulk surface homologous to A, divided by 4G_N [2].*

### 4.2 From the causal wedge to the entanglement wedge

Given a boundary region A, which bulk region is reconstructible from A? The naive answer is the *causal wedge*: bulk points that can both send signals to and receive signals from the boundary domain of dependence of A. Restricted HKLL smearing reaches exactly this far.

But Almheiri, Dong, and Harlow exhibited a sharper puzzle [3]: in AdS-Rindler reconstruction, two overlapping boundary regions yield overlapping causal wedges, and a bulk operator in the overlap admits two apparently disagreeing reconstructions — a seeming violation of boundary locality. The resolution is that the true reconstructible region is the *entanglement wedge*: the bulk domain of dependence of the region bounded by A and its RT surface γ_A, which strictly contains the causal wedge. For a boundary region covering slightly more than half a time slice, the entanglement wedge spans nearly the entire bulk. Dong, Harlow, and Wall proved that *every* bulk operator in the entanglement wedge of A admits a representation on A [5]: subregion duality is duality between A and its entanglement wedge.

| Wedge | Definition | Reconstructible? |
|---|---|---|
| Causal wedge C[A] | Intersection of bulk past and future of the boundary domain of dependence of A | Yes — via HKLL smearing restricted to A |
| Entanglement wedge E[A] | Bulk domain of dependence of the homology region between A and its extremal surface γ_A | Yes — via the DHW reconstruction theorem [5] |
| Complement wedge | Entanglement wedge of the complementary region | Reconstructible from the complement; spacelike-separated operators commute |

### 4.3 Bulk locality as quantum error correction

The AdS-Rindler puzzle dissolves once AdS/CFT is recognized as a *quantum error-correcting code* [3]. Logical qubits are the low-energy bulk degrees of freedom; physical qubits are the CFT degrees of freedom; the encoding map is the holographic dictionary. Erasing a boundary region is a *correctable error* for bulk operators whose entanglement wedges avoid the erased region.

- **Redundancy.** A bulk operator in the overlap of two entanglement wedges admits *inequivalent* reconstructions on different boundary regions — the defining property of a code, as with logical Pauli operators realizable on many subsets of physical qubits.
- **Secret sharing.** For disconnected regions, reconstruction can require all components jointly while being impossible on any one alone. The entanglement wedge of a union can be connected when the union is not, protecting "bridge" operators against erasure of any single component [3].
- **Approximate protection.** Exact correction holds only at strict large N; at finite N protection is *approximate*, governed by the relative-entropy inequalities behind the reconstruction theorem. This graceful degradation is formalized by operator-algebra quantum error correction [3].

> **Theorem (Entanglement wedge reconstruction; Dong–Harlow–Wall).** *Let A be a boundary subregion and φ a bulk operator supported in the entanglement wedge E[A]. Within the code subspace of low-energy bulk states, φ admits a representation as a CFT operator supported on A — an explicit recovery channel, a holographic analogue of the Petz map built from boundary modular flow [5].*

### 4.4 Holographic tensor networks: the HaPPY code

Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow, and Preskill made the code picture concrete with the *HaPPY code*: *perfect tensors* — isometries from any subset of up to half their legs to the complement — tiled on the hyperbolic plane [4]. Each tensor carries one logical bulk leg; uncontracted legs at the edge are the boundary physical qubits. The code reproduces holographic phenomenology from finite-dimensional linear algebra:

1. **Ryu–Takayanagi from minimal cuts.** Boundary-region entropy equals the number of bonds cut by the minimal homologous cut — a discrete RT formula.
2. **Greedy entanglement wedges.** Iteratively absorbing tensors with a majority of included legs builds the discrete entanglement wedge; operators inside push through to the boundary region.
3. **Complementary recovery.** The greedy wedges of A and its complement partition the bulk up to a residual region, mirroring E[A] and E[A^c] meeting at the extremal surface.

Later refinements — random tensor networks, hyperinvariant codes — repaired limitations like flat entanglement spectra while preserving the moral: *geometry is the entanglement structure of a quantum error-correcting code*.

### 4.5 Quantum extremal surfaces and the generalized entropy

Engelhardt and Wall's prescription [6] extremizes the generalized entropy

$$S_{gen}[\gamma] = \frac{\text{Area}(\gamma)}{4G_N} + S_{bulk}[\Sigma_\gamma]$$

over surfaces homologous to the boundary region; the minimizer — the *quantum extremal surface* — bounds the quantum-corrected entanglement wedge. Such surfaces can lie outside the causal wedge, be trapped behind barriers impassable to classical extremal surfaces, and, for evaporating black holes, undergo phase transitions that transfer the interior between wedges, reproducing the Page curve. The island rule is this prescription applied to gravitating baths: the RT formula has become the computational engine of the black hole information program.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The holographic QEC framework yields theorems and quantitative matches, not merely interpretations.

**Result 1 — Universal entanglement entropy.** RT reproduces S_A = (c/3) log(l/ε) in AdS_3/CFT_2 with the Brown–Henneaux central charge c = 3L/(2G_N) — a bulk geometric quantity matched against a purely CFT calculation [2].

**Result 2 — Entropy inequalities as geometry.** Strong subadditivity, monogamy of mutual information, and higher holographic inequalities (including five-party inequalities with no quantum-mechanical analogue) follow from minimal-surface combinatorics. Inequalities that fail for general states but hold holographically delineate the holographic entropy cone — a falsifiable characterization of states with semiclassical duals.

**Result 3 — Mutual information phase transitions.** For two disjoint intervals, RT predicts a phase transition: at small separation the minimal surface connects the intervals; beyond a critical separation it disconnects and the mutual information vanishes at leading order in N — visible in direct CFT replica computations.

**Result 4 — Reconstruction via recovery channels.** The DHW theorem [5] is constructive: the Petz map built from boundary modular Hamiltonians recovers bulk operators in the entanglement wedge, with fidelity controlled by the JLMS relative-entropy equality; a *universal* recovery channel works for all code-subspace states simultaneously.

| Prediction | Bulk computation | Boundary check | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S_A = Area(γ_A)/4G_N | Minimal surface in AdS | Replica trick in CFT | Proven via Euclidean gravity [2] |
| Strong subadditivity | Nesting of minimal surfaces | General QIT theorem | Geometric proof |
| Monogamy of mutual information | Minimal-surface inequalities | Holds only at large N | Holographic signature |
| Entanglement wedge reconstruction | JLMS + QEC theorem | Petz map on subregion | Proven [5] |
| Page curve from islands | Quantum extremal surfaces | Unitary evaporation | Derived [6] and extensions |

The Python snippet below evaluates the RT prediction for an interval in AdS_3:

```python
import math

def rt_entropy_interval(length: float, ads_radius: float, G_N: float, cutoff: float) -> float:
    """Ryu-Takayanagi entropy for a boundary interval in AdS3/CFT2.

    The minimal surface is a bulk geodesic; its regulated length gives
    S = (c / 3) * ln(length / cutoff) with c = 3 * ads_radius / (2 * G_N).
    """
    central_charge = 3.0 * ads_radius / (2.0 * G_N)
    geodesic_length = 2.0 * ads_radius * math.log(length / cutoff)
    area_term = geodesic_length / (4.0 * G_N)
    cft_term = (central_charge / 3.0) * math.log(length / cutoff)
    return area_term, cft_term

area_term, cft_term = rt_entropy_interval(length=2.0, ads_radius=1.0, G_N=0.01, cutoff=1e-4)
assert math.isclose(area_term, cft_term), "bulk/boundary mismatch"
print(f"S_A = {area_term:.4f}  (bulk area and CFT agree)")
```

The three-qutrit code — the smallest code with holographic secret-sharing features — sketched in Haskell:

```haskell
-- A logical qutrit protected against erasure of any single physical qutrit,
-- analogous to bulk operators recoverable from any two of three boundary regions.
data Qutrit = Zero | One | Two deriving (Show, Eq)

encode :: Qutrit -> (Qutrit, Qutrit, Qutrit)
encode Zero = (Zero, Zero, Zero)
encode One  = (One, One, One)
encode Two  = (Two, Two, Two)

-- Erasure of one share is correctable from the remaining two by majority decoding.
recover :: Qutrit -> Qutrit -> Qutrit
recover a b = if a == b then a else Zero
```

The greedy wedge algorithm of tensor-network reconstruction, in Rust:

```rust
/// Greedy construction of the discrete entanglement wedge.
/// Tensors are absorbed when a majority of their legs are already included,
/// mirroring operator pushing in the HaPPY code.
fn greedy_wedge(adjacency: &[Vec<usize>], seed: &[usize]) -> Vec<bool> {
    let n = adjacency.len();
    let mut included = vec![false; n];
    for &s in seed { included[s] = true; }
    loop {
        let mut grown = false;
        for (i, nbrs) in adjacency.iter().enumerate() {
            if included[i] { continue; }
            let inside = nbrs.iter().filter(|&&j| included[j]).count();
            if 2 * inside > nbrs.len() {
                included[i] = true;
                grown = true;
            }
        }
        if !grown { break; }
    }
    included
}
```

> **Theorem (JLMS relative-entropy equality).** *For code-subspace states, the boundary relative entropy of a subregion equals the bulk relative entropy in its entanglement wedge, plus the difference of area-operator expectation values — the engine of the DHW reconstruction theorem [5].*

## 6 Limitations

**The large-N, semiclassical regime.** Nearly every result above holds in the code subspace of low-energy states around a fixed background, at leading order in 1/N. The exact dictionary for arbitrary CFT states — including highly quantum or stringy bulk configurations — remains unknown, and reconstruction degrades from exact to approximate once backreaction is included.

**State dependence and the interior.** Reconstructing operators behind horizons raises the specter of state dependence: the interior operator may depend on the microstate in ways no single CFT operator captures. Whether the entanglement-wedge paradigm extends behind horizons unmodified is open.

**Asymptotically AdS only.** The framework leans on a conformal boundary hosting a non-gravitational theory. No analogous dictionary exists for de Sitter or flat space, so whether error correction underlies gravity in general — or is an artifact of AdS boundary conditions — is unknown.

**Tensor network artifacts.** The HaPPY code reproduces holographic kinematics but not dynamics: no Hamiltonian, no diffeomorphism invariance, and flat entanglement spectra in the simplest versions. These are existence proofs for the code structure, not models of quantum gravity.

**The factorization problem.** In gravity the Hilbert space does not factorize across regions as the RT formula seems to assume; gauge constraints entangle "subregions" nontrivially. Reconciling the code-subspace language with diffeomorphism invariance remains work in progress.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The holographic principle began as a radical counting argument: the degrees of freedom of quantum gravity live on boundaries, not in volumes. AdS/CFT made it precise [1]; Ryu–Takayanagi made it geometric [2]; quantum error correction made it *mechanical* [3] [4] [5] [6]. Bulk reconstruction is decoding. The radial direction is the depth of the code. Entanglement wedges are the correctable regions, extremal surfaces the syndrome measurements, and spacetime itself the logical message protected against erasure of any sufficiently small part of the boundary.

This reframing generates new results, from the quantum extremal surface prescription to the island derivation of the Page curve. If the deepest lesson of the last decade is correct, the emergence of spacetime from entanglement is not a metaphor but a theorem about codes — and the search for quantum gravity is, in no small part, the search for the right code.
