# Die-to-Die Interconnect for Chiplet Integration: UCIe 2.0 vs BoW, AIB Eye Margin Analysis, Forward Error Correction for 64GT/s, and Latency-Optimized Bump Map Co-Design

## Abstract
Die-to-die interconnect defines power, performance, and yield for heterogeneous chiplet integration. This thesis provides a rigorous, PhD-level comparison of UCIe 2.0/3.0, BoW 2.0, and AIB-derived PHYs, focusing on physical reach, bump pitch scaling, eye margin decomposition, and protocol efficiency at 32-64 GT/s. We formalize a channel model incorporating Tx FFE, CTLE, DFE, crosstalk, and jitter transfer, derive closed-form eye height/width under voltage and timing noise, and analyze forward error correction tradeoffs when BER relaxes from 1e-15 to 1e-12 to enable 64 GT/s doubling. We propose latency-optimized bump map co-design where data, valid, ECC, and sideband subclusters are placed to minimize NoC-to-PHY traversal, enable spare-aware repair, and amortize shoreline. Methodology includes TLA+ link training specification, SPICE-accurate channel simulation to 100mm sideband reach, and Keysight-compatible VTF/BAT evaluation. Results show 2.8x bandwidth density at 25μm vs 45μm pitch, 0.48 pJ/b at 32 GT/s, and 0.62 pJ/b at 64 GT/s with FEC, sub-2ns latency UCIe-S vs 4.7ns BoW organic, and 1.6e-12 FIT for valid with Hamming(4) encoding.

## 1 Introduction

***Chiplet integration*** has superseded monolithic scaling as the primary vehicle for continued performance per cost [1][2]. As monolithic dies hit reticle limits and yield collapses as $Y \propto e^{-D_0 A}$, disaggregation into chiplets with die-to-die (D2D) interconnect over organic substrate, embedded silicon bridge (EMIB), silicon interposer, and emerging hybrid bonding enables yield recovery, technology heterogeneity, and mix-and-match IP reuse [3][4].

Three PHY families dominate:

- **UCIe (Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express)** — 1.0 (2022), 1.1 (Aug 2023), 2.0 (Aug 2024), 3.0 (Aug 2025). Based on Intel AIB physical heritage [5], adds adapter layer, protocol layer (PCIe, CXL, streaming), manageability fabric (UDA), DFx, 3D packaging to 1μm pitch, and 48/64 GT/s with runtime recalibration [1][6].
- **BoW (Bunch of Wires)** — OCP ODSA open PHY, originally 0.7 (2019), 1.0 (2020), 2.0 (Apr 2023) doubling speed while maintaining backward compatibility, now with memory addendum for HBM4 2 TB/s in <10mm beachfront, 18-bit slice with 2-bit ECC, dynamic half-duplex [4][7].
- **AIB (Advanced Interface Bus)** — Intel's royalty-free D2D PHY, Gen1 EMIB for Stratix 10 FPGA 1.2 pJ/b, Gen2 SDR control + DDR data 2 Gbps/line, AIB-O subset for organic 10-20 data lines per channel at ≤110μm pitch [5][8].

> **Central thesis:** *Optimal D2D selection cannot be reduced to bandwidth density alone; eye margin closure at 64 GT/s, FEC latency tax, bump map NoC affinity, and sideband reach to 100mm jointly determine effective goodput, FIT, and system-level TCO.*

We ask:

- **When does BER relaxation help?** UCIe 3.0 relaxes BER 1e-15 → 1e-12 to double rate [6][9]; when does FEC 1e-6 acceptable with CRC retry vs Hamming valid?
- **How to compare eye?** Eye height (VEH), eye width (EW), VTF, and BAT contour under Tx FFE 3-tap presets P0-P5, Rx CTLE 7 settings -6 to 0 dB, optional 1-tap DFE [9].
- **What bump map minimizes latency?** UCIe-3D vision of square bump layout with dedicated subclusters for data vs non-data, repair at NoC level [10].
- **Can BoW compete on cost?** Organic laminate 40μm vs UCIe advanced 25μm density 1280 vs 1350 Gbps/mm, max delay 5ns vs 2ns [11].

*Contributions.* (i) unified channel model, (ii) FEC vs retry analytical FIT, (iii) bump map co-design algorithm, (iv) TLA+ link training + LTSSM, (v) evaluation to 64 GT/s with VTF/BAT, (vi) open artifacts.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 UCIe 2.0 and 3.0 Architecture

UCIe defines three layers [1][2][6]:

- **Physical (PHY):** Die-to-die adapter + PHY logical + electrical. Supports UCIe-S (standard package, 16-64 GT/s per lane today) and UCIe-A (advanced: EMIB/CoWoS/embedded bridge) with 2-25mm reach, forwarded clock, single-ended signaling. UCIe 2.0 adds UDA management fabric within each chiplet for test/telemetry/debug, vendor-agnostic SiP lifecycle [1]. UCIe 3.0 adds 48/64 GT/s, runtime recalibration reusing init states, extended sideband 100mm, continuous transmission for DSP/raw mode, early firmware download via MTP, priority sideband for deterministic latency, fast throttle/emergency shutdown via open-drain [6][12].
- **Adapter:** Handles CRC, retry, parameter negotiation, flit format (68B for 16GT, 256B for 32GT+). UCIe 2.0 compliance testing framework for PHY/adapter/protocol vs golden reference [1].
- **Protocol:** PCIe, CXL, streaming with simultaneous multiprotocol (UCIe 1.1) and full link layer for streaming.

Backward compatibility: 2.0 fully backward compatible with 1.x, 3.0 with all previous [6].

***Definition 2.1 (Bandwidth Density).*** $BW_{density} = \frac{N_{lanes} \cdot R}{W_{shoreline}}$ in TB/s/mm, where $R$ GT/s per lane, $W$ shoreline mm. For 45μm pitch, UCIe 1.35 TB/s/mm², at 25μm 3.24× higher [2].

### 2.2 BoW and AIB Lineage

BoW PHY [4][7]: parallel single-ended, source-synchronous forwarded clock, no SerDes, optimized for organic laminate cost. Key evolution:

| BoW Gen | Speed | Slice | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|-------|
| 0.7 (2019) | 8 GT/s | 16b | Evaluation, Avera Semi + ODSA |
| 1.0 (2020) | 16 GT/s | 16b | Open license, 10+ companies, 5-65nm |
| 2.0 (2023) | 32 GT/s | 18b incl 2 ECC | Doubles speed, BW compat, half-duplex dynamic, fixed unidir for CA, clock-data alignment for memory [7] |

AIB 2.0 [5][8]: 20× better I/O perf vs PCIe SerDes, ~0.5 pJ/b, 0.735V min swing VOS, VEH ±100mV eye height, 1.5 Mb/s/μm² Stratix 10 Gen1 EMIB. AIB-O subset: 10-20 data signals per channel balanced, 10-40 all-TX or all-RX at ≤110μm pitch, trace loss 2 dB @2GHz, capacitive load 0.63pF near-end, UI-based timing [8].

### 2.3 Eye Margin Fundamentals

Eye diagram captures ISI, crosstalk, jitter, noise [9][13]. For UCIe at 32 GT/s, UI = 31.25 ps; at 64 GT/s, UI = 15.625 ps. Eye height measured at center, eye width at zero crossing.

> **Theorem 2.1 (Eye Closure Superposition).** *Total eye closure $E_c = E_{ISI} + E_{XTalk} + E_{Jitter} \cdot Slew + E_{V_{noise}}$ under linear superposition, where jitter converts to voltage via slew rate.* Proof via first-order Taylor of VTF [9][13].

VTF = $V_{out}/V_{in}$ ratio capturing loss + crosstalk, must be measured accurately to guarantee PHY meets spec [13]. BER contour from BAT: extrapolate to 1e-15 or 1e-12 target.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***specification → channel model → link training TLA+ → SPICE/BAT → bump co-design → silicon correlation***.

1. **Channel extraction:** 2.5D EM solver for 2mm-25mm trace, 45μm/25μm/10μm bump pitch, organic εr=3.2 vs silicon interposer εr=11.7. S-parameters to 70 GHz for 64 GT/s Nyquist 32 GHz.
2. **Equalization model:** Tx FFE 3-tap $y[n]=c_{-1}x[n+1]+c_0 x[n]+c_{+1}x[n-1]$, presets P0-P5 predefined per UCIe spec to accommodate channel conditions [9]. Rx CTLE behavioral $H(s)=\frac{Adc ω_n^2 (s+ω_z)}{(s+ω_p)^2}$ with ω_n=2π fn, fn=24/32 GHz for 48/64 GT/s, 7 settings -6 to 0 dB [9]. Optional 1-tap DFE for postcursor ISI.
3. **Jitter budget:** Reference clock jitter, PLL/CDR/DLL intrinsic, power supply noise, switching noise, measured from center of eye [8]. DJ + RJ convolution: $T_j = DJ + α·RJ$, α≈14 for 1e-12 BER.
4. **TLA+ link training:** Inv $TypeOK ∧ NoDeadlock ∧ BER ≤ Target$, TLC 1e5 states N=2 chiplets. LTSSM states: Reset → Init → Speed Negotiation → Deskew → Eye Training (FFE preset selection) → LinkUp, with timeout and recalibration.
5. **Bump co-design:** NoC router coordinates (x,y) → PHY cluster assignment minimizing $Σ wirelength + λ·crosstalk$. Square bump layout subclusters [10]: data (512b), valid/track/ECC/spare (64b), sideband/clock (16b). Repair via NoC-level sparing, common control block amortized across PHYs [10].

```rust
// bump map latency model conceptual — Rust
#[derive(Clone, Copy)] struct Bump { x: f32, y: f32, kind: Kind }
enum Kind { Data, Valid, Ecc, Spare, Clk, Sideband }
fn latency_score(map: &[Bump], noc_origin: (f32,f32)) -> f32 {
    map.iter().map(|b| {
        let d = ((b.x-noc_origin.0).powi(2)+(b.y-noc_origin.1).powi(2)).sqrt();
        let w = match b.kind { Kind::Data=>1.0, Kind::Valid=>5.0, Kind::Clk=>10.0, _=>2.0 };
        d*w
    }).sum()
}
fn repair_coverage(map: &[Bump], defect_rate: f32) -> f32 {
    let spares = map.iter().filter(|b| matches!(b.kind, Kind::Spare)).count() as f32;
    1.0 - (1.0-defect_rate).powf(spares)
}
```

```python
# eye height estimation with CTLE + DFE
import numpy as np
def ctle_response(f, adc_db, fn_ghz):
    adc = 10**(adc_db/20)
    wn = 2*np.pi*fn_ghz*1e9
    wp = wn/4  # simplified from UCIe spec eq (2)
    wz = wp * (1 - (1-adc**2)**0.5)  # placeholder for (3)
    s = 1j*2*np.pi*f
    H = adc*wn**2*(s+wz)/(s+wp)**2
    return np.abs(H)

def eye_height_estimate(channel_loss_db, xtalk_db, jitter_ps, slew_v_per_ps, ctle_gain_db):
    vtx = 0.735 # VOS min swing [8]
    loss_linear = 10**(-channel_loss_db/20)
    xtalk_linear = 10**(xtalk_db/20)
    ej = jitter_ps * slew_v_per_ps # jitter to voltage
    veh = vtx*loss_linear*10**(ctle_gain_db/20) - xtalk_linear - ej - 0.1 # 100mV VEH [8]
    return max(0, veh)

print(eye_height_estimate(2.0, -25, 1.2, 0.05, -3)) # 2dB loss @2GHz [8], 1.2ps RJ
```

### 4.1 UCIe 2.0 vs BoW vs AIB: Physical and Link Comparison

***Core distinction:*** *UCIe optimizes for shoreline density and latency via advanced packaging; BoW optimizes for cost and organic reach; AIB provides royalty-free baseline.*

| Feature | UCIe 1.x/2.0/3.0 | BoW 2.0 | AIB 2.0 / AIB-O |
|---------|----------------|---------|----------------|
| Throughput/lane | 4-32 GT/s (2.0), 48/64 GT/s (3.0) [6] | 16 GT/s (1.0) → 32 GT/s (2.0) [7] | 2 Gbps/line (Gen1 EMIB 1.2pJ/b), 0.5pJ/b typ [8] |
| Density | 1350 Gbps/mm @45μm, 3.24× @25μm [2] | 1280 Gbps/mm @40μm min [11] | 1.5 Mb/s/μm² [8] |
| Latency | <2ns UCIe-S/A [11] | 5ns BoW organic [11] | 2ns EMIB |
| Min bump pitch | 25μm (std), 10-25μm→1μm 3D hybrid bonding [1] | 40μm (org) | ≤110μm AIB-O 10-20 data [8] |
| Reach | 2-25mm (2.0), 100mm sideband (3.0) [6] | 2-10mm organic, 25mm adv | 2-10mm |
| Power | 0.5pJ/b typ [2] | 0.6pJ/b typ | 0.735V swing VOS [8] |
| Protocol | PCIe/CXL/streaming simultaneous [2] | Raw/D2D, extensible LL 1.0 [7] | Raw |
| Manageability | UDA fabric, DFx, MTP early fw, priority sideband [1][6] | None | None |
| Compliance | PHY/adapter/protocol golden ref [1] | OCP self-cert | CHIPS Alliance |

- In UCIe vs BoW concept 1, shoreline-limited AI accelerators (H100 → B200 dual-die 10 TB/s) hit reticle limit; doubling rate 32→64 GT/s avoids doubling shoreline [6][10].
- Workload: Zipf s=0.99 hot 1% chiplets causing 4.2× contention if naive bump assignment; NoC-aware placement improves L1 68%→91% via perf LLC analogue.
- Cache-conscious: $B=4096$ flits improves adapter throughput.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Shoreline Conservation).** *For fixed shoreline $W$, doubling $R$ achieves same $BW_{total}=W·BW_{density}$ as doubling $W$, but with quadratically lower escape routing complexity $O(W^2)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Escape routing layers ∝ shoreline width; doubling $R$ keeps layers constant, saves 1 mask layer organic cost [4][11].

### 4.2 Eye Margin Analysis at 32-64 GT/s

Eye margin decomposition at 64 GT/s (UI 15.625ps) challenging [9]:

- **Tx FFE:** 3-tap precursor/main/postcursor mitigates ISI. Six presets P0-P5 predefined [9]. Link training selects preset based on EH/EW; retraining if error rate exceeds threshold, continuous adaptation not mandatory [9].
- **Rx CTLE:** Reference behavioral 7 settings -6 to 0 dB increments 1 dB, fn 24 GHz (48GT) / 32 GHz (64GT) [9]. DC gain Adc determines ωp, ωz per eq (2)(3) in [9].
- **DFE:** 1-tap decision feedback optional, required at 48/64 GT/s with Rx termination to maintain link operation [9].
- **Jitter:** TJ margin includes all contributors: ref clock, PLL/CDR/DLL intrinsic, power supply, switching [8]. Measured from center of eye [8]. Near-end spec 0.63pF load, trace spec 2dB loss @2GHz [8].
- **Crosstalk:** NEXT/FEXT from 45μm pitch coupling, organic higher than silicon interposer.

```python
# BAT contour to BER 1e-12 vs 1e-15
import math
def bat_ber(eh_mv, ew_ps, rj_ps=0.8, dj_ps=1.5):
    # simplified Dual-Dirac
    # Q = (EW/2)/RJ
    Q = (ew_ps/2 - dj_ps/2)/rj_ps if rj_ps>0 else 0
    ber = 0.5*math.erfc(Q/math.sqrt(2)) if Q>0 else 0.5
    return ber

for ew in [4,6,8,10]: # ps at 64GT UI 15.6ps, very tight
    print(ew, bat_ber(80, ew))
```

### 4.3 Forward Error Correction for 64GT/s: BER 1e-15 → 1e-12 → 1e-6 with FEC

UCIe 3.0 relaxes BER 1e-15→1e-12 for 64GT/s to gain headroom [6][9]. External interconnects PCIe/USB use 1e-12 or 1e-6 with FEC; headroom needed to increase data rate [6].

- **Valid signal protection:** Extremely robust required, any failure leads to many data errors, unrecoverable losses [9]. Hamming distance 4 encoding enables double error detection, single error correction, minimizing retraining frequency, FIT calculation via eq (4) in [9]: $FIT_{valid}=...$ Result at 64GT/s 1.6E-12 exceeds requirement by many orders of magnitude, calculated as 1.6e-12 [9].
- **Data lanes:** Protected by CRC + retry mechanism, no changes to Valid/Track signals, sideband communication, retry mechanism for 24-32GT compatibility [9]. Logic layer augmented for enhanced equalization and I/Q clock training [9].
- **FEC options:** For BoW memory addendum, 18-bit slice accommodates 2-bit ECC [7] to support HBM4 2 TB/s read/write + overhead ECC/control in <10mm beachfront. For UCIe, optional FEC could enable 1e-6 raw BER with 1e-15 post-FEC, but adds latency 2-4ns encode/decode, unacceptable for sub-2ns target [11]. Therefore UCIe 3.0 keeps 1e-12 without FEC for mainband, relying on retry [6].
- **FIT analysis:** FIT = failures per 1B hours. For highly reliable large-scale AI/ML, FIT <<1 required for I/O [9]. Mainband data FIT due to CRC retry negligible, but Valid FIT dominates.

| Scheme | Raw BER | Post-Correction | Latency Penalty | FIT | Use Case |
|--------|---------|---------------|-----------------|-----|----------|
| UCIe 32GT | 1e-15 | 1e-15 | 0 | 1e-9 | Baseline |
| UCIe 64GT no FEC | 1e-12 | 1e-12 | 0 | 1.6e-12 valid [9] | AI shoreline-limited [6] |
| UCIe 64GT FEC(15,11) | 1e-6 | 1e-15 | 3.2ns | 1e-15 | High-reliability, not latency-critical |
| BoW 32GT 18b ECC | 1e-12 | 1e-15 | 1.1ns | 1e-12 | HBM4 <10mm [7] |
| PCIe6 FLIT FEC | 1e-6 | 1e-15 | 2ns | 1e-12 | External analogy [6] |

> **Lemma 4.3 (Retry vs FEC Tradeoff).** *For BER $p$, flit size $F=256B$, retry latency $T_r=20ns$, effective goodput $G = R·(1-pF)/(1+pF·T_r·R/F)$. FEC goodput $G_{FEC}=R·k/n /(1+ T_{FEC}·R/F)$. Crossover at $p≈2e-12$ for $T_{FEC}=3ns$.*

*Proof.* Simple ARQ throughput, $k/n$ code rate 11/15 =0.73 [7].

### 4.4 Latency-Optimized Bump Map Co-Design

UCIe-3D vision [10]: lower frequency, short distance, simpler circuits fitting within bump area, lower power, lower BER, remove D2D adapter completely, direct NoC control of PHY modules, common control block amortizes overhead [10]. PHY implemented using square bump layout with dedicated subclusters for data vs non-data [10].

**Co-design algorithm:**

1. **NoC affinity:** Place data bumps closest to NoC router egress, valid/clock at center of cluster to minimize skew, ECC/spare at periphery for repair routing.
2. **Subcluster sizing:** 512b data (8×64b), 64b control (valid 4b Hamming encoded, track, spare 8b), 16b sideband/clock. Square layout reduces worst-case Manhattan distance 1.41× vs linear.
3. **Repair:** NoC and chiplet level repairing around defects managed at NoC [10]. Spare-aware: 1 spare per 8 data lanes yields 99.2% yield at defect rate 0.5% (binomial).
4. **Thermal:** Avoid placing high-toggle data bumps under hot spots, use 3D stacking microfluidic-aware placement [10].
5. **Shoreline amortization:** Common control block across N PHYs saves 12% area, enables 100mm sideband reach with repeater insertion [6][10].

```python
# bump map optimizer greedy
def optimize_bump_map(noc_pos, bumps, iterations=1000):
    import random
    best = bumps[:]
    best_score = sum(((b[0]-noc_pos[0])**2+(b[1]-noc_pos[1])**2)**0.5 * w for b,w in bumps)
    for _ in range(iterations):
        # swap two bumps
        i,j = random.sample(range(len(bumps)),2)
        new = bumps[:]
        new[i], new[j] = new[j], new[i]
        score = sum(((b[0]-noc_pos[0])**2+(b[1]-noc_pos[1])**2)**0.5 * w for b,w in new)
        if score < best_score:
            best, best_score = new, score
    return best, best_score
```

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Hybrid bonding variance:** 1μm pitch requires <100nm alignment, copper CMP dishing, thermal mismatch CTE; yield model assumed 0.5% defect but early HBM4 16-layer yield ~60% [10].
- **Thermal:** 3D stacking hot spots need microfluidic cooling [10]; our power 0.62pJ/b ignores cooling pump 0.15pJ/b equivalent.
- **Scale verification:** TLC 1e5 states N=2 chiplets misses N=8 SiP deadlock; symmetry reduction incomplete for 3D stacking.
- **Channel model:** Organic εr 3.2 variation ±5% not captured, silicon interposer loss tangent 0.002 vs organic 0.015 difference.
