# Colored-Pointer Concurrent Garbage Collection: Load Barriers in ZGC and Shenandoah, Pauseless Region-Based Reclamation, and the LXR RC-Immix Design

## Abstract

This thesis develops a unified comparative analysis of the three dominant families of low-latency concurrent garbage collectors: colored-pointer load-barrier designs (ZGC), Brooks-forwarding concurrent compactors (Shenandoah), and the reference-counting RC-Immix hybrid (LXR). We formalize the 64-bit colored-pointer bit layouts of both non-generational and generational ZGC, derive the fast-path/slow-path decomposition of the ZGC load barrier and its self-healing repair semantics, and prove that the barrier preserves the weak tricolor invariant under concurrent relocation. We contrast colored pointers with Shenandoah's per-object Brooks forwarding pointer, its load-reference barrier, and its SATB marking discipline, and we reconstruct MMTk's LXR design — deferred coalescing reference counting over an Immix mark-region heap with SATB backup — showing how it amortizes collection work to bound pauses without tracing the full heap. A pause-time cost model and published benchmark data are used to compare sub-millisecond (ZGC), low-single-millisecond (Shenandoah), and amortized-RC (LXR) pause behavior, concluding that pointer coloring trades a small constant mutator overhead for near-pauseless evacuation, while LXR trades generational tracing complexity for latency without dedicated hardware assumptions [1][2][3][4][5].

## 1. Introduction

The central tension of automatic memory management is *latency versus throughput*. Tracing collectors achieve high throughput but historically require stop-the-world (STW) pauses proportional to the live heap; reference-counting collectors distribute work over time but suffer atomic-update overhead and cannot reclaim cycles [6]. The last decade has produced a family of collectors that drive pause times below the millisecond regime while keeping the application running: ZGC's colored pointers, Shenandoah's concurrent evacuation, and LXR's lazy concurrent reference counting. This thesis studies all three from first principles.

We organize the contribution as follows. Section 2 reviews pointer coloring, Brooks forwarding pointers [6], and barrier theory. Section 3 defines a comparative methodology: a common cost model over mutator overhead, pause structure, and heap geometry. Section 4 — the deep dive — reconstructs the ZGC pointer layout and load barrier (Section 4.1–4.2), Shenandoah's Brooks-pointer design (Section 4.3), region-based generational evacuation (Section 4.4), and the LXR RC-Immix architecture (Section 4.5). Section 5 states and proves the load-barrier correctness theorem and compares empirical pause numbers. Section 6 discusses limitations.

The throughline is one design axis: *where the collector stores relocation metadata* — ZGC in the pointer, Shenandoah in a header word, LXR almost nowhere. Everything else follows from that choice.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Pointer coloring and metadata-in-pointer designs

A 64-bit machine can address $2^{64}$ bytes, but commodity hardware exposes far less: x86-64 historically provides 48 bits of virtual address space, and AArch64 48–52 bits. Managed runtimes exploit the unused high bits to smuggle GC metadata into the pointer *value*, a technique called **pointer coloring**. The Z Garbage Collector (ZGC), first shipped experimentally in JDK 11 (JEP 333), is the canonical example: object references in the heap are 64-bit *colored pointers* whose high bits encode marking and relocation state [2][3]. This makes the metadata *O(1)* to read — a single `test` instruction — at the price of forbidding compressed oops and 32-bit platforms.

### 2.2 Brooks forwarding pointers

An older alternative, due to Brooks (1984), stores an extra **forwarding pointer** word in each object's header [6]. When the collector moves an object, it writes the new address into the forwarding word; every access through the old copy is redirected. Shenandoah (JEP 189, experimental in JDK 12) adopts exactly this design: one extra word per object, no pointer mangling, and hence compatibility with compressed oops and 32-bit address spaces [4]. The cost is one word of header tax per object and a write barrier on object writes.

### 2.3 Barriers: load, store, and SATB

A **barrier** is compiler- or JIT-injected code executed on selected heap accesses so the collector and mutator can cooperate:

- *Load barriers* run when the mutator reads a reference from the heap. ZGC places its entire evacuation logic in the load barrier [3].
- *Store barriers* run on reference writes; G1 and generational ZGC use them to maintain remembered sets [2].
- *SATB (snapshot-at-the-beginning)* barriers record the *old* value of an overwritten reference, giving the collector a consistent snapshot of the object graph as of marking start; Shenandoah and LXR both use SATB discipline [4][5].

> **Theorem (Weak tricolor invariant):** A concurrent collector is correct if no black object ever points to a white object: every reference created by the mutator is either processed by a barrier or captured by a snapshot discipline.
>
> **Proof:** By induction over the sequence of mutator writes. Each write is either intercepted by a store/load barrier that marks or enqueues the referent, or the overwritten edge is preserved in a SATB buffer so the concurrent marker still observes the pre-write graph. Hence every object reachable at any instant during concurrent marking is eventually marked. ∎

### 2.4 Region-based heaps and Immix

Modern collectors divide the heap into fixed-size **regions** (G1, Shenandoah, ZGC) or **blocks**/**lines** (Immix) so evacuation proceeds region-by-region with bounded pauses [4][5].

## 3. Methodology

We compare the three designs along four axes:

1. **Metadata placement** — bits in the pointer (ZGC), a header word (Shenandoah), or side metadata (LXR).
2. **Barrier shape** — load barrier fast/slow path cost in machine instructions; store-barrier and SATB queue cost.
3. **Pause structure** — which phases are STW, what they scale with (thread count, root-set size, heap size), and measured pause bounds [1][2].
4. **Correctness mechanism** — how each design maintains the weak tricolor invariant during concurrent evacuation.

We do not re-run benchmarks; instead we synthesize the published results of the ZGC and LXR teams and the OpenJDK JEPs, and we formalize the barrier algorithms as pseudo-code and, where illuminating, as TLA+ / Haskell sketches. Inline citations map to the References.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The 64-bit colored pointer: bit layout and multi-mapped heaps

In non-generational ZGC, a 64-bit reference is laid out as follows [3]:

| Bits | Width | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| 63–60 | 4 | Color: **Marked0**, **Marked1**, **Remapped**, **Finalizable** |
| 59–44 | 16 | Unused |
| 43–0 | 44 | Object address (16 TB address space) |

Only one marking color is *good* (live and correctly addressed) at a time; the others are *bad* and trigger barrier action. Because a bad-colored pointer cannot be dereferenced directly, ZGC maps the *same* physical heap pages at three different virtual base addresses — the **multi-mapped heap** — so the fast path dereferences the pointer *as-is* when the color is good and the slow path only intervenes on bad colors [3].

> **Definition (Good color):** Let $c(p) \in \{\text{Marked0}, \text{Marked1}, \text{Remapped}\}$ be the color of pointer $p$. The good color $g$ is the color designating "marked in the current cycle and address is current". A load of $p$ is *transparent* iff $c(p) = g$.

Generational ZGC (JEP 439, JDK 21) abandons multi-mapping [2]. The motivation is twofold: `ps`-style tools triple-counted the heap, and the metadata bits no longer needed to sit inside the addressable range. Instead:

- Object references *in heap fields* are colored pointers; references on the *hardware stack and in registers* are **colorless** raw addresses.
- The metadata moves to the **low-order bits** of the pointer, with the address in the high bits, so that a single shift both tests for a bad color and strips the metadata [2].
- Store barriers maintain double-buffered remembered sets between generations [2].

This layout opens the path beyond the 16 TB ceiling of the non-generational design and removes the virtual-memory accounting anomaly [2].

A minimal model of the coloring in Haskell:

```haskell
data Color = Marked0 | Marked1 | Remapped | Finalizable
  deriving (Eq, Show)

goodColor :: Phase -> Color
goodColor MarkPhase     = Marked1
goodColor RelocatePhase = Remapped

isBad :: Phase -> Color -> Bool
isBad ph c = c /= goodColor ph
```

### 4.2 The load barrier: fast path, slow path, and self-healing

ZGC's load barrier fires on every heap reference load. The JIT emits roughly:

```asm
mov  0x10(%rax), %rbx        ; load colored reference
test %rbx, 0x20(%r15)        ; bad color? (mask in thread-local)
jnz  slow_path               ; no -> fall through (fast path)
; ... use %rbx directly ...
slow_path:
  call ZBarrier::load_barrier ; mark/relocate/remap, heal 0x10(%rax) and %rbx
```

The fast path is two instructions with ~4% steady-state overhead [3]. The slow path performs one of three repairs and then **self-heals** — it writes the corrected pointer back into the field, so the *same field is never slow twice* [3]:

1. **Mark**: the object is not yet marked; mark it and push it on the mark stack (concurrent marking piggybacks on mutator loads).
2. **Relocate**: the object lives in the relocation set; allocate a copy in the target region, install a **forwarding-table** entry $old \mapsto new$, and return the new address.
3. **Remap**: the object has already been relocated; look up the forwarding table, return the new address, and heal the stale pointer.

The forwarding table is a global hash from old to new addresses consulted only on the slow path. Because the barrier heals, forwarding-table lookups decay to zero as the heap converges — this is the mechanism that makes "pauseless" evacuation possible: the mutator itself finishes the relocation work incrementally [3].

The generational variant shortens the fast path further: with metadata in the low bits,

```
lui  tmp, <bad-mask>     ; RISC-V sketch from the ZGC port [2]
srli tmp, tmp, 12
and  tmp, ref, tmp
beqz tmp, good
j    slow_path
good:
srli ref, ref, ZPointerLoadShift   ; strip color, obtain address
```

On x86-64 the equivalent collapses toward a single shift-and-test sequence [2].

The algorithm in TLA+ style:

```tla
---- MODULE ZLoadBarrier ----
VARIABLES ptr, field, fwdTable, phase

LoadBarrier ==
  IF color(ptr) = GoodColor(phase)
  THEN ptr \* fast path: use as-is
  ELSE LET repaired ==
         IF InRelocationSet(ptr) THEN Relocate(ptr)
         ELSE IF NeedsMark(ptr) THEN Mark(ptr)
         ELSE Remap(ptr, fwdTable)
       IN  /\ field' = repaired     \* self-heal the field
           /\ repaired              \* return repaired pointer
====
```

> **Theorem (Load-barrier correctness):** If every heap reference load passes through the barrier above, the mutator never dereferences a stale address, and every loaded object is eventually marked.
>
> **Proof:** Suppose the mutator loads $p$ pointing to object $o$ relocated to $o'$. If $c(p)$ is bad, the slow path returns $fwd(p) = o'$ (installed atomically at relocation time) and heals the field. If $c(p)$ is good, either $o$ was never relocated or a previous slow path already healed this field, so $p = o'$. Marking piggybacks on the same path, so every object the mutator can reach is marked when loaded. ∎

### 4.3 Brooks pointers, SATB, and Shenandoah's concurrent compaction

Shenandoah rejects pointer coloring — deliberately, for portability to compressed oops and 32-bit targets [4]. Each object header carries an extra **Brooks forwarding pointer** word. The read protocol is:

```rust
// Simplified Shenandoah load-reference barrier (LRB)
fn load_ref(field: *mut *mut Obj) -> *mut Obj {
    let raw = unsafe { *field };
    let fwd = unsafe { (*raw).brooks_ptr }; // forwarding word
    if in_collection_set(raw) {
        // slow path: evacuate or forward, then CAS the Brooks word
        evacuate_or_forward(raw, fwd)
    } else {
        fwd // equals raw when not evacuated
    }
}
```

Key contrasts with ZGC [4]:

- **Write barrier, not read barrier, for correctness of writes**: when the mutator writes through a stale reference, the barrier redirects via the Brooks pointer, so the old copy is never observably mutated.
- **SATB marking**: Shenandoah's concurrent mark uses snapshot-at-the-beginning; the write barrier enqueues overwritten referents, preserving the weak tricolor invariant of Section 2.3.
- **Phases**: *Concurrent Mark* → *Concurrent Evacuate* (copy live objects out of the collection set) → *Concurrent Update References* (rewrite all pointers to evacuated objects). Only root scanning and the phase handshakes are STW; pauses scale with thread count, typically 1–5 ms, and are *not* proportional to heap size [4].
- **Degenerated GC**: under extreme allocation pressure Shenandoah falls back to a full STW collection, a documented safety valve [4].

Because reads vastly outnumber writes in most workloads, Shenandoah's per-load check is cheaper in the common case than ZGC's, while ZGC's pause bound is tighter — the two designs sit at different points on the same latency/overhead curve [4].

### 4.4 Region-based evacuation and ZGC's generational turn

All three collectors are **region-based**: the heap is partitioned so evacuation copies only the profitable regions. ZGC's cycle is [3]:

1. *Pause Mark Start* (STW, root scan)
2. *Concurrent Mark/Remap*
3. *Pause Mark End* (STW)
4. *Pause Relocate Start* (STW, choose relocation set)
5. *Concurrent Prepare for Relocation*
6. *Concurrent Relocate* — mutators relocate via the load barrier; a background thread drains the rest

Generational ZGC (JEP 439) adds independently collected young/old generations, exploiting the generational hypothesis [1]; double-buffered remembered sets track inter-generational edges via store barriers [2]. Its acceptance criteria: pauses ≤ **1 ms**, heaps from hundreds of MB to many TB, no manual tuning [1]. JEP 474 made it the default in JDK 23 and JEP 490 removed the non-generational mode in JDK 24 [7].

### 4.5 LXR: reference counting meets Immix and SATB

LXR (PLDI 2022), implemented in MMTk on OpenJDK 11, takes a third path: **concurrent, coalescing reference counting over an Immix heap, with SATB backup** [5]. Its ingredients:

- **Immix substrate**: the heap is blocks of 256-byte lines; objects never move unless evacuated for defragmentation. Reclamation is *lazy*: RC decrements identify garbage; lines with no live objects are recycled without sweeping dead objects [5].
- **2-bit reference counts in side metadata**: two RC values per line, stored outside the object (no header tax, no pointer mangling). Counts saturate at 3 ("3 or more"); overflowed objects fall back to the SATB trace [5].
- **Coalescing RC**: the write barrier logs each mutated field once per epoch; at epoch end the collector diffs old and new values, collapsing RC overhead from *O(stores)* to *O(distinct mutated fields)* [5].
- **Implicitly-dead optimization**: newly allocated objects start dead; only objects surviving an epoch get counted, so short-lived garbage costs nothing [5].
- **Concurrent, pauseless epochs**: RC processing runs concurrently; the only STW work is root scanning and epoch handshakes, bounding pauses like the tracing designs.

LXR's reported results are striking: lower latency than both Shenandoah and ZGC on four latency-critical benchmarks, while beating G1 on throughput — achieved on stock hardware with no pointer-coloring or multi-mapping tricks [5]. The price is complexity: cycle collection requires the SATB backup trace, and the 2-bit counters plus remembered increments demand careful overflow discipline.

```python
# Coalescing RC epoch processing (simplified)
def end_epoch(mod_buffer, rc_table):
    for field in mod_buffer:          # each mutated field, once per epoch
        old, new = field.old, field.new
        if old is not new:
            rc_table.decrement(old)   # may free line when count hits 0
            rc_table.increment(new)   # saturating 2-bit counter
    mod_buffer.clear()
```

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Pause-time comparison

Synthesizing the published targets and measurements [1][3][4][5]:

| Collector | Metadata | Barrier | STW pause target | Pause scales with |
|-----------|----------|---------|------------------|-------------------|
| ZGC (non-gen) | 4 color bits in pointer; 3× multi-mapped heap | Load barrier (~4% overhead), self-healing | < 10 ms (2018), < 1 ms goal [1] | Thread/root count, not heap |
| ZGC (generational) | Low-bit colors; colorless stack values; no multi-mapping | Load + store barriers | ≤ 1 ms (JEP 439 acceptance) [1] | Thread/root count, not heap |
| Shenandoah | Brooks word per object; SATB queues | LRB + write barrier | 1–5 ms typical [4] | Thread count, not heap |
| LXR | 2-bit RC in side metadata; SATB backup | Coalescing write barrier | Amortized; epoch handshakes only [5] | Root set, not heap |

The pattern is uniform: *no design pauses on the heap*. They differ in what the mutator pays continuously — ZGC pays per-load, Shenandoah pays per-write plus a header word, LXR pays per-epoch buffer processing.

### 5.2 A cost model for barrier overhead

Let $\lambda_L, \lambda_S$ be the mutator's reference-load and reference-store rates, $c_f$ the fast-path instruction cost, $p_s$ the slow-path probability, and $c_s$ the slow-path cost. Steady-state mutator tax:

$$T = \lambda_L (c_f + p_s c_s) + \lambda_S \cdot c_w$$

For ZGC, $c_f \approx 2$ instructions and $p_s \to 0$ outside relocation phases [3]; for Shenandoah the load term is replaced by a write-side term with $c_w$ small; for LXR the store term is $O(\text{distinct fields})$ per epoch rather than per store [5]. Because $\lambda_L \gg \lambda_S$ in most programs, Shenandoah's and LXR's write-side designs enjoy lower continuous overhead, while ZGC buys the tightest pause bound by spending on loads [4].

> **Theorem (Pauseless evacuation bound):** In a colored-pointer collector with self-healing load barriers, the remaining STW relocation work after concurrent relocation begins is bounded by the number of *unhealed fields*, which the mutator strictly decreases.
>
> **Proof:** Each slow-path barrier heals the field it loads, converting it to the good color permanently for the cycle. Unhealed fields form a finite set; every mutator load of an unhealed field removes it from the set. A background thread heals the remainder concurrently. Hence no STW phase needs to visit unhealed fields, and evacuation completes without pausing the mutator. ∎

### 5.3 Benchmark synthesis

- **ZGC**: the JEP 439 acceptance criterion — pauses not exceeding 1 ms on heaps up to terabytes — is the design contract; generational mode additionally targets lower allocation-stall risk, lower heap overhead, and lower GC CPU than non-generational ZGC [1].
- **Shenandoah**: 1–5 ms pauses, scaling with thread count; full STW only under allocation pressure [4].
- **LXR**: on the authors' four latency-critical benchmarks, lower max pauses than both Shenandoah and ZGC, with throughput above G1; Shenandoah suffered 77% and 37% throughput slowdowns on 1.3× and 2× heaps in the same study [5].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Colored pointers vs. the ecosystem.** ZGC's design forbids compressed oops and 32-bit ports and historically confused memory-accounting tools; generational ZGC fixed the accounting but not the portability constraint [1][2]. A hardware-assist (e.g., RISC-V pointer masking) could shrink the fast path further but remains non-portable [2].
2. **Forwarding-table scalability.** The global forwarding table is a contention point during heavy relocation; sharded or per-region tables are an open engineering problem [3].
3. **Shenandoah's header tax and degenerated GC.** One extra word per object is significant for small-object workloads, and the degenerated-GC fallback means the pause bound is *typical*, not guaranteed [4].
4. **LXR's complexity budget.** Saturating 2-bit counters, the SATB backup trace for cycles, and overflow handling make LXR the hardest of the three to implement correctly [5].
5. **Formal verification gap.** The barrier-correctness arguments above are hand proofs; machine-checked verification of a self-healing load barrier against a weak-memory model remains open.

## 7. Conclusion

Colored-pointer collection is the logical endpoint of a simple idea: if the pointer itself carries the collector's knowledge, the mutator can repair the heap as a side effect of using it. ZGC turns every reference load into a potential act of collection; Shenandoah matches it with a header word and write-side barrier; LXR shows reference counting can beat both when coalesced, deferred, and laid over Immix lines. The three designs agree on the essentials: region-partitioned heaps, snapshot or barrier discipline for the tricolor invariant, and pause times that scale with roots rather than heap size. They differ only in where they put the metadata — and *everything else follows from that decision*.
