# Privacy-Preserving Genome-Wide Association Studies via Secure Multi-Party Computation: Secret Sharing, Garbled Circuits, and Malicious-Secure Logistic Regression

## Abstract

Genome-wide association studies demand individual-level genotype and phenotype data whose privacy is protected by law and ethics, yet their power grows with ever-larger pooled cohorts. This thesis constructs a complete secure multi-party computation (MPC) architecture for GWAS that closes the gap between the semi-honest systems demonstrated to date and the *malicious* security that clinical deployment requires. We formalize chi-square association testing and covariate-adjusted logistic regression over authenticated secret shares, integrate Yao's garbled circuits for thresholding and quality-control comparisons, and lift the full pipeline to active security using SPDZ-style information-theoretic MACs with Beaver-triple preprocessing. Randomized sketching reduces population-stratification control from $O(nM)$ to $O(n\ell)$ communication, and a federated variant keeps raw genomes at source sites. Drawing on the published record — S-GWAS [1], homomorphic-encryption GWAS [2], and SF-GWAS [3] — we project malicious-secure biobank-scale logistic regression at $2$–$4\times$ the cost of the semi-honest baseline, i.e., tens of hours, and we characterize the remaining bottlenecks in offline preprocessing, rare-variant numerics, and output-privacy governance.

## 1. Introduction

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have become the dominant methodology for linking single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) to complex phenotypes, yet their statistical power depends on aggregating individual-level genotype and phenotype records that are encumbered by stringent privacy regulation. Secure multi-party computation (MPC) offers a principled resolution of this tension: multiple custodians jointly evaluate association statistics over *secret-shared* data without any party ever observing another's raw records. This thesis develops a complete MPC architecture for privacy-preserving GWAS. We first formalize the chi-square association test and logistic regression over replicated and additive secret shares, then integrate Yao's garbled circuits for the non-arithmetic comparison and thresholding steps, and finally lift the entire protocol to *malicious* security using the SPDZ paradigm of information-theoretic MACs under a globally unknown key. We show how Beaver multiplication triples, pseudorandom-share preprocessing, and random-projection dimensionality reduction reduce the asymptotic communication of population-stratification control to manageable levels. Drawing on the published engineering record — including the semi-honest system of Cho, Wu, and Berger [1], the homomorphic-encryption baseline of Blatt *et al.* [2], and the federated SF-GWAS framework [3] — we argue that malicious-secure MPC logistic regression at biobank scale is now within engineering reach, and we identify the remaining bottlenecks in preprocessing, numerical precision, and covariate adjustment.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The GWAS statistical problem

A GWAS scans $M \approx 10^5$–$10^6$ SNPs across $n$ individuals, testing each variant for association with a phenotype $y \in \{0,1\}^n$ (binary case) or $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ (quantitative). The classical allelic *chi-square* test builds a $2 \times 2$ contingency table of allele counts against case/control status and computes

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i,j} \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}},$$

with $E_{ij}$ the expected counts under independence, rejecting the null of no association when $\chi^2$ exceeds a genome-wide threshold (e.g., $5 \times 10^{-8}$ after Bonferroni correction). Covariate-aware analysis — adjusting for age, sex, and the top principal components of population structure — instead fits a logistic model

$$\Pr(y_i = 1 \mid x_i) = \sigma(\beta^\top x_i), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}},$$

where $x_i$ concatenates the SNP genotype dosage with covariates, and tests $\beta_{\text{SNP}} = 0$ via a Wald or score test. The arithmetic of both tests is dominated by inner products of the $n \times M$ genotype matrix $G$ with phenotype-derived vectors — a structure MPC can exploit.

### 2.2 Secret sharing and garbled circuits

In an $n$-party protocol, *additive secret sharing* represents a field element $x \in \mathbb{F}_p$ as shares $\langle x \rangle = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ with $\sum_i x_i = x$, each share uniform and independent of $x$. Linear operations are free: parties add shares locally. Multiplication requires interaction, classically via *Beaver triples* $(\langle a \rangle, \langle b \rangle, \langle c \rangle)$ with $c = ab$, generated in an input-independent offline phase; the online phase opens only masked values $d = x - a$, $e = y - b$ and reconstructs $\langle xy \rangle = \langle c \rangle + d\langle b \rangle + e\langle a \rangle + de$ [4].

Yao's garbled circuits complement sharing: one party garbles a Boolean circuit and the other evaluates it on wire labels obtained via oblivious transfer, revealing only the output. Garbling excels at comparisons, thresholding, and sorting — precisely the non-arithmetic steps of GWAS (significance thresholding, top-$k$ selection) — while secret sharing excels at the arithmetic bulk. Modern frameworks such as MP-SPDZ [5] unify both paradigms behind a single compiler.

### 2.3 The adversary model ladder

- **Semi-honest**: corrupted parties follow the protocol but attempt to infer inputs from their view. This is the model of the landmark S-GWAS system [1].
- **Covert**: deviations are detected with some probability, deterring rational adversaries.
- **Malicious (active)**: corrupted parties deviate arbitrarily; the protocol must either deliver correct output or *abort* without leaking inputs.

Malicious security is the appropriate standard for genomics: a biobank that subtly perturbs shares could otherwise bias association statistics toward spurious findings, with clinical consequences. The SPDZ family achieves malicious security with abort by attaching to every shared value $x$ an information-theoretic MAC $m = \alpha x$ under a global key $\alpha$ unknown to all parties [4].

### 2.4 Related work in privacy-preserving genomics

Secure GWAS has been attacked from several cryptographic directions. Cho, Wu, and Berger [1] demonstrated the first large-scale MPC GWAS using secret sharing and pseudorandom-share generation, with runtime scaling linearly in sample size (approximately 80 days extrapolated for one million individuals and 500,000 SNPs under the semi-honest model). Blatt *et al.* [2] showed homomorphic encryption could evaluate chi-square tests for 100,000 individuals and 500,000 SNPs in 5.6 hours non-interactively, at the cost of lattice-based assumptions and no practical logistic regression at that time. The SF-GWAS system [3] combined MPC with homomorphic encryption in a federated architecture, keeping raw data at source sites and reducing communication by roughly $3.5\times$ relative to S-GWAS while supporting both linear and logistic tests. Early garbled-circuit approaches targeted monogenic diagnosis on small cohorts, and the I-GWAS line [7] addressed membership-inference leakage from *released* statistics via differential privacy. Our contribution synthesizes these threads into one malicious-secure architecture.
## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 System and threat model

We consider $K$ data custodians (hospitals, biobanks) holding genotype matrices $G^{(k)} \in \{0,1,2\}^{n_k \times M}$ and phenotype vectors $y^{(k)}$, and $N$ compute parties (e.g., $N = 3$ non-colluding servers, or $N = 2$ with dishonest majority) that evaluate the study. Custodians secret-share their records to the compute parties over authenticated channels; no custodian or compute party ever sees another party's plaintext. The adversary may corrupt up to $N-1$ compute parties (dishonest majority) and any subset of custodians, adaptively deviating from the protocol. Security is *UC-style* simulation-based: for every real-world adversary there exists an ideal-world simulator producing an indistinguishable transcript, with abort permitted. Public output consists solely of: (i) per-SNP test statistics (or a Boolean significance vector), (ii) estimated effect sizes and standard errors for declared hits, and (iii) population-structure PCs — nothing else.

### 3.2 Protocol pipeline

The pipeline proceeds in five phases:

1. **Sharing and authentication.** Each custodian distributes additive shares of its records; the parties convert them to SPDZ-authenticated shares $\langle x \rangle = (x_i, m_i)$ with $m_i = \alpha x_i + r_i$ style MACs under global key $\alpha = \sum_i \alpha_i$ unknown to any coalition below the corruption bound [4].
2. **Quality control.** Call-rate, minor-allele-frequency (MAF), and Hardy–Weinberg filters are evaluated as arithmetic circuits over shares, with a garbled-circuit top-$k$/threshold stage that reveals only the surviving SNP index set.
3. **Population stratification.** Randomized PCA on the shared genotype matrix via random projection, following the communication-saving strategy of [1], yields shared top PCs used as covariates.
4. **Association testing.** For each SNP: an arithmetic circuit computes the chi-square statistic (screening) and, for candidates, Newton–Raphson logistic regression with a low-degree polynomial approximation of the sigmoid.
5. **MAC check and opening.** Before any output is opened, the parties run the SPDZ batch MAC check; on failure the protocol aborts. Only authenticated outputs are reconstructed.

Fixed-point encoding maps real values into $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $f$ fractional bits; all divisions (e.g., in $\chi^2$ and Newton steps) use secure division via Goldschmidt iteration or Newton–Raphson reciprocal, avoiding garbled circuits in the hot loop.

### 3.3 Cost model

Let $n = \sum_k n_k$ and $M$ be the SNP count. The dominant term is the genotype–phenotype inner product block: $O(nM)$ multiplications in the online phase, each costing one Beaver triple and $O(N)$ field elements of communication. Preprocessing (triple generation via oblivious transfer or homomorphic encryption, as in MASCOT [5]) is input-independent and amortizable across studies on the same cohort. Random projection reduces PCA communication from $O(nM \cdot \text{iterations})$ to $O(nM + n\ell)$ with sketch dimension $\ell \ll M$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Secret-shared chi-square association tests

For SNP $j$, define case/control allele counts from shares. With genotype dosages $g_i \in \{0,1,2\}$ and binary phenotype $y_i$, the allelic table entries are

$$O_{11} = \sum_i g_i y_i, \quad O_{12} = \sum_i g_i (1 - y_i), \quad O_{21} = \sum_i (2-g_i) y_i, \quad O_{22} = \sum_i (2-g_i)(1-y_i),$$

each an inner product computable with $O(n)$ Beaver multiplications per SNP. Row/column sums and the closed-form $2 \times 2$ chi-square

$$\chi^2 = \frac{n(O_{11}O_{22} - O_{12}O_{21})^2}{(O_{11}+O_{12})(O_{21}+O_{22})(O_{11}+O_{21})(O_{12}+O_{22})}$$

are then evaluated with a handful of secure multiplications and one secure division. A garbled comparison circuit finally tests $\chi^2 > \tau$, revealing only a single bit per SNP — this is the crucial step that prevents leakage of the exact statistic for non-significant variants, blunting membership-inference attacks of the kind studied in [7].

> **Theorem:** Let $\Pi_{\chi}$ be the protocol above over field $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p > 4n^2 M_{\text{eff}}$. Then $\Pi_{\chi}$ securely realizes the ideal functionality that outputs $\{\mathbf{1}[\chi^2_j > \tau]\}_j$ to all parties with abort, against a malicious adversary corrupting $t < N$ compute parties, assuming a secure triple-generation functionality and authenticated channels.

*Proof sketch.* Arithmetic subcircuits are handled by the SPDZ online phase, whose simulation is standard [4]: the simulator extracts adversarial inputs from shares, forwards them to the ideal functionality, and programs the openings of masked values with uniform randomness. The garbled comparison is simulated via the standard garbling simulator given only the output bit. The MAC check guarantees that any deviation altering a share is detected except with probability $2/p$ per check, negligible for 128-bit $p$. ∎

### 4.2 Garbled circuits for thresholding, ranking, and QC

While the arithmetic bulk belongs to secret sharing, several GWAS steps are inherently Boolean: MAF thresholding ($f_j > 0.01$), Hardy–Weinberg $p$-value cutoffs, genomic-control inflation factor comparisons, and selection of the top-$k$ SNPs for follow-up. Implementing these as arithmetic circuits would require expensive bit-decomposition; instead we convert the relevant shared values to a garbled-circuit representation (share-to-garbling conversion protocols add only $O(\kappa)$ overhead per value [5]) and evaluate compact Boolean circuits. The QC stage, in particular, benefits: each custodian's per-SNP call rate and MAF are computed arithmetically, converted, and thresholded, so that the *identity* of filtered SNPs — itself potentially sensitive — can optionally remain hidden behind an oblivious index permutation.

### 4.3 Malicious-secure logistic regression via SPDZ

Logistic regression is the workhorse of binary-trait GWAS with covariates. Newton–Raphson iteration over shared data proceeds as

$$\beta^{(t+1)} = \beta^{(t)} + (X^\top W^{(t)} X)^{-1} X^\top (y - \mu^{(t)}),$$

with $\mu^{(t)}_i = \sigma(x_i^\top \beta^{(t)})$ and $W^{(t)} = \operatorname{diag}(\mu^{(t)}_i (1 - \mu^{(t)}_i))$. Three MPC engineering decisions make this practical:

1. **Sigmoid approximation.** The exact sigmoid is replaced by a degree-3 minimax polynomial on the logit range encountered in GWAS (typically $[-8, 8]$), evaluated with two Beaver multiplications per element. Approximation error below $10^{-4}$ in probability space is empirically invisible against the genome-wide significance threshold [2].
2. **Avoiding the matrix inverse.** Rather than inverting the $(d+1) \times (d+1)$ Hessian in MPC (costly and division-heavy), we solve the Newton system with shared conjugate gradient, requiring only matrix–vector products — each a Beaver-multiplication inner product — and one scalar secure division per CG iteration.
3. **MAC-authenticated everything.** Every intermediate $\langle \beta \rangle$, $\langle \mu \rangle$, $\langle W \rangle$ carries a SPDZ MAC. The global key $\alpha$ is itself secret-shared, and MACs are verified in a single batch check before the final $\beta$ is opened: the parties commit to $\sigma_i = \gamma_i - \alpha \cdot a_i$ style check shares and open them, aborting unless $\sum_i \sigma_i = 0$ [4]. A malicious server that perturbs a gradient share to inflate a particular SNP's $p$-value triggers abort with probability $1 - 1/p$.

Preprocessing generates Beaver triples for the entire Newton trajectory *before* seeing data; because the iteration count is data-independent (fixed $T = 8$ iterations suffice for GWAS-scale conditioning), the offline phase size is known exactly in advance.

### 4.4 Population stratification under MPC: randomized PCA

Uncorrected population structure is the classic source of spurious GWAS hits, and PCA-based correction (EIGENSTRAT-style) must itself be privacy-preserving. The genotype matrix $G$ ($n \times M$) is far too large for an MPC eigendecomposition. Following [1], we apply a random projection $\Omega \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times \ell}$ with $\ell \approx 100$: the sketch $Y = G\Omega$ is computed with $O(nM\ell)$ *local* multiplications by public randomness (no communication, since $\Omega$ is public), after which only the small $n \times \ell$ sketch enters interactive MPC for QR and Rayleigh–Ritz steps. The top PCs are returned as authenticated shares and appended to the covariate matrix $X$. Communication drops from $O(nM)$ field elements per PCA iteration to $O(n\ell)$ — a factor of $\sim 10^4$ at $M = 500{,}000$.

### 4.5 Federated deployment: keeping data at the source

The dishonest-majority $N$-server model assumes custodians are willing to secret-share raw records outward. The SF-GWAS design [3] weakens this requirement: each site performs *local* plaintext computation (e.g., local sufficient statistics $X^{(k)\top} X^{(k)}$, $X^{(k)\top} y^{(k)}$) and only these aggregates enter MPC/HE for the global combine. Our architecture accommodates both modes: the Newton–CG iteration decomposes into per-site local Gram matrices plus a small secure aggregation, so the online MPC cost becomes independent of $n$ and scales only with $d^2$ (covariate dimension) and $M$. Homomorphic encryption can replace the aggregation layer where non-interactivity is preferred [2], at the cost of lattice assumptions; the MAC-based malicious-security argument then applies to the MPC portion only, and the HE layer inherits semantic security.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 Complexity analysis

| Phase | Online communication | Rounds | Preprocessing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharing + MAC setup | $O(nM)$ field elements | $O(1)$ | — |
| QC filters (arith. + garbled) | $O(nM + M\kappa)$ | $O(\log n)$ | $O(M)$ garbled gates |
| Randomized PCA sketch | $O(n\ell)$ | $O(\ell)$ | $O(n\ell)$ triples |
| Chi-square screen (all $M$ SNPs) | $O(nM)$ | $O(1)$ amortized | $O(nM)$ triples |
| Logistic regression (top $M'$ SNPs) | $O(T \cdot M' (nd + d^2))$ | $O(T \log n)$ | $O(T M' nd)$ triples |
| Batch MAC check + opening | $O(N \cdot \text{wires})$ | $O(1)$ | — |

For the reference scale $n = 10^5$, $M = 5 \times 10^5$, $d = 10$, $T = 8$, and $M' = 2.5 \times 10^4$ (top 5% retested, as in [2]), the online phase consumes roughly $2.5 \times 10^{11}$ Beaver multiplications — the same order as the semi-honest S-GWAS workload that ran in $\sim$37 hours for association tests [1]. Malicious security multiplies communication by a small constant (MAC shares roughly double share size; the batch check is $O(1)$ rounds), so a malicious-secure run is projected at $2$–$4\times$ the semi-honest runtime on equivalent hardware — tens of hours, not months. The offline triple-generation phase dominates wall-clock time but is embarrassingly parallel and reusable across phenotypes on the same cohort.

### 5.2 Accuracy: fixed-point and approximation error

Two error sources distinguish MPC GWAS from plaintext PLINK-class tools: fixed-point quantization ($f = 32$ fractional bits) and the polynomial sigmoid. Bounding the former: inner products of length $n = 10^5$ with 32-bit fractions accumulate rounding error below $2^{-20}$ relative, orders of magnitude under the standard error of any effect estimate. The degree-3 sigmoid approximation introduces at most $3 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute error in fitted probabilities on $[-8, 8]$, shifting $-\log_{10} p$ by less than $0.01$ for all but the most extreme statistics — consistent with the empirical near-identity of secure vs. plaintext Manhattan plots reported in [1] and [2].

### 5.3 Security argument summary

*Privacy.* The view of any coalition of $< N$ compute parties consists of uniform shares, masked openings $d, e$ (one-time-pad blinded by triple randomness), and garbled-circuit labels revealing only the programmed outputs — simulatable given only the public outputs. No individual genotype or phenotype bit is reconstructible.

*Correctness under attack.* Any additive tampering with a share invalidates its MAC; the batch check catches tampering except with probability $1/p$ per value, and values are checked *before* opening, so a corrupted party cannot both bias the result and pass the check except negligibly. Selective-abort leakage is bounded by the standard SPDZ analysis [4]: at most one bit per abort, and aborts reveal nothing about honest inputs beyond the fact of tampering.

*Output privacy.* Releasing only significance bits (not exact statistics) for the screening stage limits membership-inference surface; the logistic follow-up releases effect sizes only for declared hits, matching the disclosure profile recommended by [7].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Preprocessing cost.** Malicious-secure triple generation (MASCOT-style OT or SHE-based LowGear/HighGear [5]) remains the practical bottleneck: generating $10^{11}$ triples requires days of cluster time even though it is offline and parallelizable. Silent-OT advances continue to compress this, but it is the least "solved" component.
2. **Dishonest-majority vs. deployment reality.** Our analysis assumes $N$ non-colluding compute servers; real biobank consortia may struggle to instantiate even two mutually distrustful operators. The federated variant [3] eases this by keeping raw data local, but then the MPC layer sees only aggregates and malicious security covers less of the pipeline.
3. **Numerical edge cases.** Rare variants (MAF $< 10^{-3}$) produce near-singular Hessians; the CG solver needs shared preconditioning, and fixed-point division loses precision exactly where statistics are most delicate. A hybrid that falls back to exact garbled-circuit arithmetic for rare-variant burden tests is future work.
4. **Selective abort and fairness.** Like all dishonest-majority MPC with abort, a corrupted party can deny service; guaranteed output delivery would require honest majority or stronger assumptions. In a clinical-research setting, abort is safe (no wrong result is published) but availability must be handled contractually.
5. **Regulatory gap.** Cryptographic security is not legal compliance: a protocol can be UC-secure yet still violate data-use agreements if the *outputs* (e.g., polygenic scores for a stigmatized trait) are misused. Governance must accompany the cryptography.

## 7. Conclusion

We have presented a unified architecture for genome-wide association studies under malicious-secure multi-party computation: secret-shared chi-square screening with garbled-circuit thresholding, SPDZ-authenticated Newton–Raphson logistic regression with conjugate-gradient Hessian solves, randomized-PCA stratification control, and a federated deployment option that keeps raw genomes at their source sites. The design inherits its performance envelope from a decade of published engineering — from the semi-honest S-GWAS milestone [1] through homomorphic-encryption acceleration [2] to federated SF-GWAS [3] — and closes the most important remaining gap: *active* security, so that a compromised compute server can at worst abort the study, never silently falsify it. At reference biobank scale the projected runtime is measured in tens of hours with a reusable offline phase, placing malicious-secure GWAS firmly in the realm of the practical. The remaining work is engineering — faster triple generation, rare-variant numerics, and honest deployment governance — not new cryptography.
