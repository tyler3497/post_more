# Masked Image Modeling Beyond Pixels: MAE High Masking Ratio Decoder Design, DINO Momentum Teacher Collapse Avoidance, and iBOT Patch-Level Self-Distillation with Masked Patch Token Alignment

## Abstract
Self-supervised Vision Transformers have converged around masked prediction and self-distillation, yet the design choices that separate pixels from semantics remain subtle and decisive. This thesis synthesizes three paradigmatic frameworks — *Masked Autoencoders (MAE)* with 75% masking ratio and asymmetric lightweight decoder [1], *DINO* with momentum teacher centering and sharpening to avoid collapse [2], and *iBOT* that unifies masked image modeling with patch-level self-distillation via an online tokenizer [3] — into a unified analysis of masking ratio, decoder capacity, momentum dynamics, and token alignment. We derive why high masking prevents trivial interpolation, how decoder depth modulates semantic abstraction, why centering plus sharpening provides non-contrastive collapse avoidance without negatives, and how iBOT's masked patch token alignment lifts BEiT-style discrete tokenization [4] to continuous self-distilled targets. We present formalism, ablations, complexity, and implementations in PyTorch with comparisons to SimMIM [5], DINOv2 [6], and VideoMAE [7], establishing best practices for scalable, data-efficient pretraining that yields 82-87% ImageNet linear probe without labels.

## 1. Introduction

The success of BERT in NLP catalyzed a migration of **masked language modeling (MLM)** to vision, but pixels are not tokens. Words are discrete, semantic, and low-redundancy; image patches are continuous, highly redundant, and locally interpolatable. Directly porting BERT's 15% masking to images collapses to an easy inpainting task solvable by texture extrapolation. Concurrently, contrastive methods like MoCo and SimCLR required large batches and negative samples, while clustering models required online codebook balancing.

Three works redefined the landscape in 2021-2022:

* **MAE [1]** showed that *randomly masking 75% of patches* and encoding only visible patches with a standard ViT, then decoding with a *lightweight 8-layer transformer* to regress normalized pixels, yields scalable self-supervised learners that accelerate training 3× and scale to ViT-Huge 87.8% ImageNet.
* **DINO [2]** demonstrated that *self-distillation with no labels* — student predicting teacher's sharpened distribution, teacher updated by exponential moving average (EMA) with momentum 0.996→1, plus centering — generates emergent object segmentation properties in ViT attention heads without any pixel reconstruction.
* **iBOT [3]** bridged them: masked patch modeling *in the self-distilled feature space*, not pixels, using the momentum teacher *itself as online tokenizer*. Together with class-token self-distillation, it achieved 82.3% linear probing and 87.8% fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K with ViT-L, while revealing emergent part semantics crucial for dense tasks.

This thesis asks: *Why 75%? Why lightweight decoder? Why does EMA teacher + centering + sharpening not collapse? Why patch-level alignment beats pixel regression?* We answer with formalism, ablations, and actionable synthesis.

> **Central Claim:** *The information density gap between language and vision demands high masking ratios; the asymmetry between encoder and decoder decouples representation learning from reconstruction specialization; and non-contrastive self-distillation requires explicit yet complementary anti-collapse operators whose interaction can be analyzed via entropy–KL decomposition. Unifying MIM with self-distillation at patch level yields a tokenizer-free, semantically rich objective.*

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Vision Transformer Tokenization

ViT partitions image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$ into $N = HW/P^2$ patches $x^p_i \in \mathbb{R}^{P^2\cdot3}$, linearly projected to tokens $z_0 = [x_{class}; x^p_1E; ...; x^p_NE] + E_{pos}$. Standard $P=16$, $N=196$ for 224² image. Redundancy: neighboring patches have mutual information ~0.6 under natural image statistics, far higher than word co-occurrence.

> **Theorem (Redundancy-Corrected Masking):** Let $I(x_i; x_{\mathcal{N}(i)}) = \rho$. To ensure prediction requires global reasoning, masking ratio $r$ must satisfy $(1-r) < 1 - \rho$ so expected visible neighbor count $k = (1-r)\cdot|\mathcal{N}| < 1$. For $\rho\approx0.6$ and $8$-neighbor, $r>0.7$ satisfies.

BEiT [4] first mimicked BERT by tokenizing images via discrete VAE (dVAE) from DALL-E [8], vocabulary 8192, objective $\mathcal{L}_{MIM}= -\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} \log p(z_i|x^{\setminus \mathcal{M}})$. While elegant, it required offline tokenizer pretrained on 250M images, domain mismatch risk [5].

SimMIM [5] simplified: random masking 60% with moderate 32×32 mask size, direct regression of raw pixels with $L_1$ loss, decoder as single linear layer. It proved tokenizer-free pixel regression works, but performance degraded under high masking without asymmetric design.

### 2.2 Self-Distillation Foundations

DINO stems from BYOL and MoCo but without predictor or memory bank. Teacher $g_t$ and student $g_s$ have identical ViT + projection head $h: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^K$, $K=65536$ prototypes. For view $x_1,x_2$:

$$
P_s = \text{softmax}(h_s(g_s(x_1))/\tau_s), \quad P_t = \text{softmax}((h_t(g_t(x_2))-c)/\tau_t)
$$

Loss $H(P_t, P_s)= -P_t \log P_s$. Teacher updated: $\theta_t \leftarrow \lambda\theta_t + (1-\lambda)\theta_s$, $\lambda$ cosine 0.996→1. Center $c \leftarrow m c + (1-m)\text{mean}(h_t)$. Sharpening: $\tau_t=0.04$, $\tau_s=0.1$ warmed up. Multi-crop: 2 global 224², 8 local 96²; teacher sees only global, student all.

Without centering + sharpening, collapse: entropy $h(P_t)\to0$ (dominant prototype) or $-\log 1/K$ (uniform) and KL $\to0$ indicating constant output [2].

### 2.3 Online Tokenizer Motivation

PeCo [8] argued DALL-E tokenizer lacks perceptual semantics. Optimizing dVAE with perceptual loss (LPIPS + MoCo features) improved ImageNet 84.5% vs 83.2%. iBOT takes further step: *no explicit codebook*; teacher network *is* tokenizer, evolving online. This sidesteps quantizer collapse and multi-stage pipeline, enabling end-to-end joint optimization.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 MAE Asymmetric Encoder-Decoder

MAE formulation [1]:

- Sample mask $m\in\{0,1\}^N$, $||m||_0 = rN$, $r=0.75$.
- Visible set $\mathcal{V}= \{i:m_i=0\}$, $|\mathcal{V}|=0.25N$.
- Encoder $f$ operates *only* on $\mathcal{V}$: $z_v = f(x_{\mathcal{V}})$, $O((0.25N)^2)$ self-attention, 3× FLOP reduction.
- Decoder $g$: input $[z_v; \text{[MASK]}_i + pos_i]_{i\in\mathcal{M}}$ of length $N$, depth 8, dim 512 vs encoder dim 768-1280. Reconstruct $\hat{x}_i$, loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{MAE}= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} \|\text{norm}(x_i) - \hat{x}_i\|_2^2
$$

Normalization per-patch improves 0.4%. Mask token never seen by encoder → no train-test gap for fine-tuning (encoder always unmasked).

*Decoder design insight:* Linear probe accuracy vs decoder depth inverse correlation: shallow decoder forces encoder to retain higher-level semantics ($\approx$ BERT intuition that decoder is task head). Deep decoder allows shortcut where encoder does less.

| Encoder | Decoder depth | FT Acc | Linear |
|---------|---------------|--------|--------|
| ViT-B | 1 | 83.0% | 63.2% |
| ViT-B | 8 | **83.6%** | **67.8%** |
| ViT-B |12 | 83.1% | 65.1% |

### 3.2 DINO Collapse Avoidance Formalism

Entropy-KL decomposition [2]:

$$
H(P_t,P_s)= h(P_t) + D_{KL}(P_t||P_s)
$$

If $D_{KL}\to0$, no gradient for student; if $h(P_t)\to0$, dominant dimension collapse; if $h\to \log K$, uniform collapse.

- **Centering** $c$ prevents dominant: subtract batch mean → output distribution de-biased uniform-ish, raises $h$.
- **Sharpening** low $\tau_t$ pushes towards peaked distribution, prevents uniform $h\approx \log K$ by lowering entropy.

Both needed: ablation in [2] shows removing either → KL=0 within 20 epochs, top-1 drop 15-60%.

Momentum teacher interpretation as *Polyak-Ruppert averaging* with exponential decay: $\theta_t^{(T)} = \sum_{t} \alpha_t \theta_s^{(t)}$, ensembling effect smooths target, exceeding student always (Fig. 5 in [2]: teacher kNN +0.8% over student persistently).

### 3.3 iBOT Two-Level Distillation

iBOT loss [3]:

$$
\mathcal{L}= \mathcal{L}_{CLS} + \lambda\mathcal{L}_{MIM}
$$

Where:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{CLS}= -P_t^{CLS}(u)\log P_s^{CLS}(v)
$$

for two views $u,v$ of same image, and

$$
\mathcal{L}_{MIM}= -\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} P_t^{patch,i}(u)\log P_s^{patch,i}(\hat{u})
$$

$\hat{u}$ is $u$ masked: masked positions replaced by learnable **[MASK]** token. Teacher sees *clean* view $u$, student masked view $\hat{u}$, thus forcing masked patch token alignment in *latent* space, not pixel.

**Online tokenizer**: $P_t$ produced by centering + sharpening same as DINO, but at *patch* granularity $N\times K$, not just CLS. Vocabulary size $K=8192$ shared between CLS and patches via same head (weights shared) → semantic consistency between instance and local parts.

Implementation detail: masking at patch level, ratio $r\sim\mathcal{U}(0,0.3)$ for blockwise mask (Bezier masking) combined with 50% random; unlike MAE 75% because goal is semantic target not pixel.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why 75% Masking? Information Density and Decoder Specialization

Standard BERT masks 15% because language is highly semantic and dense: hiding 15% already requires syntactic + world knowledge. Vision: image can be reconstructed from 25% visible patches via interpolation if low masking, as shown by He et al. [1] visualization where 75% masking still plausible reconstruction but 50% trivial.

We formalize trade-off:

- *Too low* ($<40%$): task = local texture copying, encoder remains low-level Gabor-like.
- *Sweet spot* (60-80%): encourages long-range structure, object shape.
- *Too high* ($>85%$): ambiguous, many plausible completions, loss noisy.

MAE ablation ImageNet fine-tuning ViT-B 400ep:

| Mask ratio | 15% | 40% | 50% | 75% | 85% |
|------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| FT Acc | 82.6 | 84.0 | 84.1 | **84.9** | 84.4 |

*Decoder width matters less than depth:* 128-d dim still 84.2% but 1024-d not improve (<0.1) while FLOPs 6×.

> **Theorem (MAE Efficiency):** Encoder FLOPs scale as $O((1-r)^2 N^2 d)$. For $r=0.75$, FLOPs = $0.0625$ of full ViT, enabling 3× overall speedup despite decoder $N$.

### 4.2 DINO Momentum Dynamics and Alternative Teachers

Caron et al. [2] compared teachers:

1. Copy student (λ=0): collapses 0.1% kNN.
2. Previous iteration student: 48.2% kNN.
3. Previous epoch student: 62.5% kNN (no collapse!).
4. EMA 0.9 → 0.996 cosine: **74.6% kNN**.

Thus momentum improves but not essential for anti-collapse; existence of *any* decoupled teacher breaks symmetry leading to stop-gradient deadlock (similar to BYOL proof that predictor + EMA avoids collapsed Nash). Polyak averaging argument: even noisy student average outperforms latest student (bias-variance trade).

Multi-crop is *essential* for DINO small patch local-to-global correspondence: without multi-crop, 67.9% vs 74.5% with 2 local-crops. Local crops (96² covering <50% area) forced to match global teacher → part-to-whole.

Collapse visualisation (Fig. 7 in [2]):

```python
# entropy-KL logging in PyTorch training loop
def dino_loss(student_out, teacher_out, center, tau_s=0.1, tau_t=0.04):
    student_out = student_out / tau_s
    teacher_out = (teacher_out - center) / tau_t
    p_t = F.softmax(teacher_out, dim=-1).detach()
    p_s = F.log_softmax(student_out, dim=-1)
    entropy = -(p_t * torch.log(p_t.clamp(min=1e-9))).sum(dim=-1).mean()
    kl = (p_t * (torch.log(p_t.clamp(min=1e-9)) - p_s)).sum(dim=-1).mean() \
         - entropy # Actually cross-entropy = entropy+KL
    loss = -(p_t * p_s).sum(dim=-1).mean()
    return loss, entropy, kl
```

Monitoring shows without centering, entropy→0, prototypes dominated by 1 dimension (sharpening too strong alone). Without sharpening, entropy→logK ~11.1 for K=65536, uniform.

### 4.3 iBOT Patch Alignment vs Pixel Regression: Semantic Granularity

Key distinction: MAE reconstructs *pixels* $x_i$, low-level; iBOT reconstructs *distribution* $P_t^{patch}$, semantic. PeCo [8] hypothesized pixels too low-level hurts; iBOT proves continuous teacher targets more transferable to dense tasks: COCO detection $AP^{box}$ 51.2 with iBOT ViT-B vs 50.3 MAE vs 49.3 DINO; ADE20K 50.0 mIoU vs 48.1 MAE linear probe.

**Shared head trick:** CLS and patch tokens share prototype projection $h$. Why? Ensures patch vocab aligns with image-level semantics; ablation removing sharing: -0.9% linear, -1.2% segmentation. Theoretical justification: patch prototypes are parts decomposed from same embedding manifold as whole – analogous to topic model where words share dictionary across levels.

Masked modeling at patch level also introduces *equivariance*: student must infer missing part's semantics from context, not only appearance. Example: masked dog's ear, teacher sees ear patch and outputs prototype 2411 ("floppy ear texture"), student from surrounding head tokens must predict same id – forces contextual reasoning akin to BERT's "bank" disambiguation.

```haskell
-- Haskell pseudo: iBOT patch distillation core
type Token = Vector Float
type Prototype = Int -- 0..8191

ibotLoss :: [Token] -> [Token] -> [Bool] -> Float
ibotLoss teacherPatches studentMasked isMasked =
  let teacherDist = softmax . centerSharpen $ teacherPatches
      studentLog  = logSoftmax studentMasked
      maskedPairs = filter (\(m,_,_)-> m) (zip3 isMasked teacherDist studentLog)
  in  - mean [ dot t s | (_,t,s) <- maskedPairs ]
  where centerSharpen x = (x - center) / tau
```

TLA+ spec for masking invariance:

```tla
---- MODULE iBOT_Mask ----
VARIABLES visible, masked, embeddings
TypeOK == visible \in SUBSET Patches /\ masked = Patches \ visible
MaskInvariant == \A p \in masked: \E ctx \in SUBSET visible: |ctx| >= 3
                                   /\ SemanticAlign(p, ctx)
====
```

### 4.4 Unifying Perspective: DINOv2, Tokenizer-Free Future, and Computational Pareto

DINOv2 [6] scales DINO+iBOT amalgam: 142M curated LVD-142M dataset, ViT-g 1.1B params, KoLeo regularizer + high-res 518px fine-tune. Recipe:

- Both image-level DINO loss and patch-level iBOT loss kept.
- Sinkhorn-Knopp centering (instead of batch mean) for stability at scale.
- No MAE decoder – reconstruction objective dropped entirely in favor of distillation.

Why dropped? At scale, pixel reconstruction becomes bottleneck: decoder 8 layers at 518² $N=1024$ is 4× FLOPs vs 224². Distillation targets already encode geometry via unmasked teacher.

Complexity comparison:

| Method | Encoder FLOP (224²) | Extra head | Memory | Target |
|--------|---------------------|------------|--------|--------|
| MAE 75% | 0.25× ViT-B (4.1G) | 0.6G decoder | low | pixel norm |
| DINO mc=10 | 1.1× (10 crops) | 2-layer MLP 65k | 12.9GB | CLS dist |
| iBOT | 1.15× | shared 8k | 13.4GB | CLS+patch dist |
| SimMIM 60% | 1.0× | linear | 6.2GB | pixel L1 |

Pareto frontier: if compute limited and need fine-tune downstream supervised, MAE cheapest; if frozen encoder / kNN / dense, iBOT/DINOv2 wins.

Self-supervised scaling law emerging: *data curation matters more than algorithm after threshold* – DINOv2's LVD-142M pipeline (retrieve near ImageNet-22k, ADE20k, Landmarks) yields +2.1% over uncurated 142M random images [6].

> *The tokenizer is dead. Long live the tokenizer.* – From discrete dVAE [4] → perceptual PeCo → online EMA teacher, trajectory shows handcrafted vocabularies replaced by self-evolving distributions. Future models may distill *both* CLS and dense tokens across modalities.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 MAE Reconstruction Bounds

On ImageNet-1K val, ViT-L MAE 800ep fine-tune 87.8% [1], surpassing supervised 85.2% ViT-L trained on ImageNet-22K labels! Linear probe lower: 75.8% vs 82.3% iBOT – indicating pixel objective less linearly separable but still transferable fine-tune.

Memory: mask ratio 75% reduces forward memory 3.7× (encoder only 25%) enabling batch 4096 on 64 A100 – SimMIM cannot due to full encoder.

### 5.2 DINO Emergent Properties Quantified

kNN 20-NN evaluation ImageNet [2]: ViT-S/16 DINO 74.5% vs MoCo-v3 72.8%, BYOL 71.0%. Attention map probing VOC12: Jaccard 45.9 vs supervised 27.3.

Centering ablation raw numbers:

| Setting | kNN | Collapse entropy |
|---------|-----|------------------|
| both c+sharpen | 74.6 | 5.2 |
| no centering | 0.1 | ~0.0 |
| no sharpening | 0.2 | 11.0 ≈ logK |
| no momentum EMA but PrevEpoch | 62.5 | 6.1 |

Proof sketch: EMA ensures teacher Lipschitz constant < student, breaking symmetry that leads to fixed-point collapse at constant output – analogous to proof in BYOL where predictor + EMA prevents trivial solution being only global optimum.

Python diagnostic:

```rust
// Rust-like pseudo: EMA update stable in fp16
fn ema_update(teacher: &mut Param, student: &Param, lambda: f32) {
    // lambda scheduled cosine 0.996 -> 1.0
    for (t,s) in teacher.iter_mut().zip(student.iter()) {
        *t = *t * lambda + *s * (1.0 - lambda);
    }
}
```

### 5.3 iBOT Dense Task Transfer

ADE20K linear segmentation frozen ViT-B: iBOT 38.3 mIoU vs DINO 34.5 vs MAE 32.1 [3]. COCO Mask R-CNN fine-tune ViT-B: iBOT 51.2 box AP vs BEiT 49.1 (+ perceptual gap). Robustness on ImageNet-C corruption error 45.1% vs 48.9% MAE, indicating semantic tokens more invariant than pixels.

Shared head ablation table:

| Shared CLS/patch head | Linear 1K | ADE mIoU |
|-----------------------|-----------|----------|
| True (default) | 79.5% | 50.0 |
| False separate | 78.6% | 48.8 |

Formal proof iBOT loss lower-bounds mutual information between masked and visible views: $\mathcal{L}_{MIM} \ge -I(z_{\mathcal{M}};z_{\mathcal{V}})+ \log K$, thus maximizing alignment maximizes MI.

## 6. Limitations

1. **MAE decoder capacity dilemma:** 8-layer decoder tuned for ViT-B fails for ViT-Tiny – overpowers encoder (1-layer better 75.8% vs 73.2%). Automatic decoder scaling law unresolved; Tiny models require much narrower decoder width 96 vs 512 [1][5].

2. **DINO multi-crop cost:** 10 crops inflate epoch time 3× (24h → 72h for ViT-B/16). DINOv2 reduces to 2 global + 8 local still heavy; memory 15.4GB vs MAE 3.9GB. Without crop, collapse avoidance still works but performance -6%. Research into single-crop augmentation still open.

3. **iBOT vocabulary bottleneck:** $K=8192$ shared prototypes must represent all patch concepts; if $K$ too large (>16k), centering unstable Sinkhorn iterations $O(K^2)$; if too small (<1024), semantic granularity lost (kNN -2%). Optimal $K$ dataset-dependent; 8192 empirical for ImageNet-1K not theoretically grounded.

4. **Evaluation mismatch:** Linear probing favors discriminative (DINO/iBOT) over generative (MAE). Fine-tuning erases gaps (<1% after 100ep). Does self-supervised objective matter long-term if full fine-tune always? Recent evidence says *data efficiency*: at 10% labels, iBOT 70.1% vs MAE 68.3% – gap widens again.

5. **Masking strategy bias:** Random masking assumes i.i.d. patch importance, false for images – foreground objects occupy ~30% area typical. Attention-guided masking (MST, SemMAE) shows +0.6% but adds complexity that breaks simplicity premise.

6. **Theoretical gap synthesis:** No unified theorem proves combined $\mathcal{L}_{CLS}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{MIM}$ non-collapsing guarantee with patch centering – DINO proof for CLS does not trivially extend to $N\times$ independent patch distributions with masking.

## 7. Conclusion

We dissected three pillars—high masking ratio MAE [1], momentum teacher DINO [2], patch self-distillation iBOT [3]—revealing complementary design invariants: *sparsify to de-redundify, asymmetrize to specialize, and self-distill at multiple granularities to semanticize.*

**Practitioner's guide**:

- Use **MAE 75% + 8-layer 512-d decoder** when pretraining for *fine-tuning* with limited GPU (3× speedup) and ImageNet-scale data.
- Use **DINO momentum 0.996→1 + centering + τ_t 0.04** when needing *frozen* features, kNN retrieval, or emergent masks; include multi-crop unless compute-starved.
- Use **iBOT masking 0-30% blockwise + shared 8192 prototypes + λ=1** when transfer to dense tasks and data efficiency critical; scales best to ViT-L.

Future: DINOv2 [6] direction indicates tokenizer-free continuous distillation at scale plus curated data beats pixel reconstruction asymptotically. Yet MAE remains invaluable intuition: *you can hide 75% of an image and still learn the world.* The path forward marries both – masking as aggressive augmentation, distillation as semantic target.

> *Representation is the encoder's burden; reconstruction is the decoder's chore – never conflate the two.*
