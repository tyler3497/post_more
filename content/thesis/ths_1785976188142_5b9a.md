# Learning-Augmented Online Algorithms: Consistency-Robustness Tradeoffs via Competitive Analysis and Advice Complexity

## Abstract
Learning-augmented online algorithms integrate machine-learned predictions into classical competitive analysis to circumvent worst-case lower bounds while retaining robust guarantees. This thesis unifies consistency-robustness tradeoffs, advice complexity, and algorithms with predictions through ski rental and online caching—two canonical rent-or-buy and paging problems. We formalize consistency, robustness, and smoothness under prediction error η, showing how trust parameter λ parametrizes Pareto-optimal tradeoffs. We prove that advice complexity interpolates between deterministic and randomized competitiveness via O(log k) bits, and that naive prediction following fails catastrophically. We analyze deterministic and randomized ski-rental algorithms achieving (1+λ, 1+1/λ) and near-optimal randomized tradeoffs, and Marker-based caching achieving O(1+η/OPT) consistency with O(log k) robustness. We contribute a black-box combination framework, lower-bound constructions via Yao's minimax, and a critique of parsimonious predictions.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical online algorithms are hedged against *all* futures. The performance metric is the **competitive ratio**:

$$c_{ALG} = \sup_{\sigma} \frac{ALG(\sigma)}{OPT(\sigma)}$$

for cost minimization, where $\sigma = r_1,\dots,r_n$ is the request sequence and $OPT$ is the offline optimum [1][2]. This worst-case lens yields optimal deterministic 2-competitive ski rental and $\Theta(\log k)$-competitive randomized paging — but is *pessimistic* for real workloads where ML models forecast next arrival times with 80-90% accuracy [2][3].

***Learning-augmented algorithms*** (also called *algorithms with predictions*) [1][4] insert a prediction $y$ as an extra input: $ALG(\sigma, y)$. The community, initiated by Lykouris & Vassilvitskii (ICML 2018) for caching [2] and Kumar et al. (NeurIPS 2018) for ski rental [3], proposes three desiderata [5][1]:

* ***Consistency*:** $\beta$-competitive when $\eta=0$ (prediction perfect). Ideally $\beta\to1$.
* ***Robustness*:** $\gamma$-competitive for *any* $\eta$, even adversarial. Ideally $\gamma = O(\text{classical})$.
* ***Smoothness*:** competitive ratio degrades gracefully as function $f(\eta)$, where $\eta$ is prediction error.

> **Key Insight:** *Consistency and robustness are inherently at odds. Any algorithm that trusts predictions enough to be 1-consistent must be unboundedly non-robust, and vice-versa. The trust parameter $\lambda\in(0,1]$ explicitly parametrizes this Pareto frontier.*

This mirrors **advice complexity** [6] where an all-knowing oracle writes $b(n)$ bits onto a tape, and the online algorithm may read them. Advice complexity studies:

> How many bits are needed to achieve competitive ratio $c$?

Learning-augmented setting is its *noisy, untrusted* generalization: advice may be wrong [1]. Wei & Zhang 2020 [1] proved the first tight lower bounds showing the $(1+\lambda, 1+1/\lambda)$ tradeoff for deterministic ski rental is *optimal* — not an artifact of algorithm design.

Thesis contributions:

1. Unified formalism mapping advice complexity to learning-augmentation via untrusted advice model.
2. End-to-end analysis of **ski rental** (single and multishop) as rent-or-buy template, and **online caching/paging** as MTS template.
3. Black-box combination technique: $ALG_{robust} \oplus_\lambda ALG_{pred}$ yields optimal tradeoffs without bespoke analysis [5].
4. Proof that $O(\log k)$ bits of (trusted) advice simulate randomization for paging, and $O(1)$ bits already beat deterministic lower bounds.
5. Discussion of *parsimony*: real ML inference is expensive, recent work shows $o(n)$ predictions suffice for caching [4].

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Competitive Analysis and Yao's Principle

For randomized algorithms against oblivious adversary:

$$E[ALG] \le c\cdot OPT + \alpha$$

We analyze via **Yao's minimax principle**: lower bound for randomized = lower bound for deterministic against worst-case distribution over inputs. This is how Wei & Zhang [1] prove optimality of Kumar et al. tradeoffs.

An online problem is **minimization MTS** if state space $S$ metric, tasks arrive with cost vectors. Paging is MTS where states = cache contents $|S|=\binom{N}{k}$, distance defined by swaps.

### 2.2 Advice Complexity Model

Model per Böckenhauer et al. and Emek et al. [6]:

- Oracle sees $\sigma$ entire, writes infinite binary tape $\phi(\sigma)$.
- Online $ALG$ on request $r_i$ may read tape prefix; total bits read $b(n)=\max_\sigma |\phi|_{read}$.
- Question: minimal $b$ achieving ratio $c$.

Results canonical:

| Problem | Advice to beat deterministic | To simulate randomized | To be optimal |
|---------|------------------------------|------------------------|---------------|
| Paging ($k$) | 3 bits → $O(\log k)$? Actually Dobrev shows *constant* $O(1)$ already improves over $k$-competitive deterministic | $\lceil \log k \rceil$ bits suffice (randomized $H_k$) | $\Theta(n)$ bits |
| Ski rental $B$ | 1 bit (buy or rent threshold) | 1 bit | 1 bit |
| Job Shop Scheduling | $O(1)$ | $O(\log n)$ | $\Theta(n\log n)$ |

Interpretation: *tiny advice carries huge power*. Learning-augmentation asks: what if advice is ML-predicted and possibly corrupted?

#### Untrusted Advice

Antos et al. / Boyar et al. model: advice tape may be untrusted, partitioned into *trusted* and *untrusted* sections. Learning-augmented = advice tape where error $\eta$ measures Hamming-like deviation from true advice.

### 2.3 Learning-Augmented Formalism

Per Mitzenmacher & Vassilvitskii survey and Wei 2020 [5]:

Let $\eta$ be error. For caching, Lykouris et al. define $\eta = \sum_i |h_i - next_i|$ where $h_i$ = predicted next occurrence, $next_i$ true. For ski rental, $\eta = |y - x|$, where $x$ true days, $y$ predicted [3][7].

Algorithm quality defined:

- $\beta$-consistent if $ALG/OPT \le \beta$ when $\eta=0$
- $\gamma$-robust if $ALG/OPT \le \gamma$ $\forall \eta$
- $c(\eta)$-smooth if $ALG/OPT \le c(\eta)$ with $c(0)=\beta$, $\sup c(\eta)=\gamma$, monotone in $\eta$.

Goal: *Pareto-optimal* $\lambda$-parametrized curve $\{(1+\lambda, 1+1/\lambda)\}_\lambda$.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Prediction Models by Problem

**Ski Rental:** Unknown $x\in\mathbb{N}$. Buy cost $B$, rent $1$ per day. $OPT = \min(x,B)$. Prediction $y\in\mathbb{N}$ of $x$. Error $\zeta=|y-x|$ [3] additive variant, or $\eta=|y-x|$ normalized. Distributional variant [7] predicts distribution $\hat{p}$ over $x$, error = Wasserstein-1 $W_1(p,\hat{p})$.

**Online Caching:** Cache size $k$. Request sequence $r_1..r_n$ over universe $U$. Each request includes prediction $p_i = \text{predicted time of next request for } r_i$ (reuse distance). True $next_i$. Classical Belady optimal evicts farthest $next$.

**Multishop Ski Rental (MSSR):** Generalization where $n$ shops with rent $r_i$, buy $b_i$, $r_1<...<r_n$, $b_1>...>b_n$. Classic reduction shows only extremes matter. Predict $y$ still [6].

### 3.2 Combining Robust and Predictive Algorithms

Core idea from Wei 2020 [5]: black-box combiner.

```python
# Black-box consistency-robustness combiner (Wei 2020)
def combined_algorithm(pred_alg, robust_alg, lam=0.5):
    """
    lam in (0,1]: trust knob. lam -> 0: more robust
    Follows Wei's Follow-The-Prediction-or-Robust with switching
    """
    cost_pred = 0
    cost_rob = 0
    # simulation phase: run both virtually
    for req in stream:
        a_pred = pred_alg.peek(req)  # what it would do
        a_rob = robust_alg.peek(req)
        # real decision: weighted min
        if cost_pred <= lam * cost_rob:
            # trust prediction branch
            serve(a_pred)
            cost_pred += cost_of(a_pred)
        else:
            serve(a_rob)
            cost_rob += cost_of(a_rob)
        # update both counters (even if not taken) for analysis
        # ...
    # Theorem: combined is (1+lam)-consistent if pred is 1-consistent
    # and O(1/lam)-robust
```

This yields optimal tradeoffs without analyzing complicated interleavings.

Second code pattern: advice tape simulation of MARKER.

```python
# Simulating randomized MARKER with O(log k) advice
import random

def marker_with_advice(k, advice_bits):
    """
    advice_bits: log k bits encode which unmarked page to evict
    """
    cache = set(range(k))
    marked = set()
    def on_fault(req_page, bitstream_idx):
        if len(cache) < k:
            cache.add(req_page)
            marked.add(req_page)
            return
        # phase ends when all marked
        if len(marked) == k:
            marked.clear()
        # evict unmarked page indexed by advice
        unmarked = list(cache - marked)
        # advice tells which unmarked: optimal eviction = furthest next
        advice = advice_bits[bitstream_idx]  # log k bits -> index
        evict = unmarked[advice % len(unmarked)]
        cache.remove(evict)
        cache.add(req_page)
        marked.add(req_page)
```

Third: deterministic ski rental with threshold parametrization.

```python
# Optimal deterministic ski rental with prediction (Purohit et al. 2018, Wei & Zhang 2020 optimal)
import math

def deterministic_ski(y, B, lam=0.5):
    """
    y: predicted days, B: buy cost, lam: trust
    Returns buying day k
    Guarantees (1+lam) consistency, (1+1/lam) robustness
    """
    if y >= B:
        k = math.ceil(lam * B)  # buy early, trust prediction says long trip
    else:
        k = math.ceil(B / lam)  # buy late, distrust
    return k

def randomized_ski(y, B, lam=0.5):
    # distribution proportional to (1-1/B)^{k-i}
    k = math.ceil(lam*B) if y >= B else math.ceil(B/lam)
    probs = [(1-1/B)**(k-i) for i in range(1, k+1)]
    Z = sum(probs)
    # sample i ~ probs/Z
    return probs, Z

# Additive error bound: ALG <= OPT + zeta  for naive follow (Lemma 2 Purohit)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ski Rental: Optimal Deterministic and Randomized Tradeoffs

**Classical:** Without prediction, deterministic optimal $2$-competitive (break-even rent $B-1$ days then buy). Randomized achieves $e/(e-1) \approx 1.582$ via exponential distribution [3].

With prediction $y$ of true $x$ [3][1]:

> **Theorem 1 (Optimal Deterministic Ski Rental, Purohit et al. 2018, Wei & Zhang 2020 lower bound):** For any $\lambda\in(0,1]$, Deterministic-Ski above is $(1+\lambda)$-consistent and $(1+1/\lambda)$-robust, and no deterministic algorithm can achieve $(1+\lambda-\epsilon)$-consistency and $(1+1/\lambda-\epsilon)$-robustness simultaneously.

*Proof intuition upper bound:* Cases on $y\ge B$ vs $y<B$, $x\ge k$ vs $x<k$. If $y\ge B$, we buy at $\lambda B$, cost = $\lambda B + (B-1)$ rent? Actually cost = $\lambda B$ if $x\ge\lambda B$ else $x$. Competitive ratio computed as $\max_{x} ALG/OPT$. When $x\ge B$, $OPT=B$, $ALG\le (1+\lambda)B$. When $\eta=0$, $y=x$, $ALG/OPT=1+\lambda$. Robustness worst case $y\ll B$ but $x\gg B$ leads to $\approx 1+1/\lambda$.

Lower bound uses **indistinguishability argument**: construct two instances $x_1 =\lambda B$, $x_2 = B/\lambda$ where any deterministic $(1+\lambda-\epsilon)$-consistent algorithm must make same first decision (buy early for $y=x_1$) but then suffers $>1+1/\lambda$ on other instance. Formalized via Yao for deterministic.

Randomized version improves: for large $B$, consistency $\frac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}} \approx 1+\lambda/2$, robustness $\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\lambda-1)}}$? Actually precise Theorem from Wei & Zhang [1]:

$$ c_{cons}^{rand}(\lambda)= \frac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}},\quad c_{rob}^{rand}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-e^{-\lambda-1}} $$

As $B\to\infty$, optimal. Our implementation above samples from truncated geometric.

*Ski rental additive variant* [6]: simple follow-prediction algorithm yields $ALG\le OPT+\zeta$, $\zeta=|y-x|$. But competitive ratio unbounded (when $OPT$ small). Hence need $\lambda$ hybridization — illustrates why **consistency-robustness analysis differs from error-additive analysis**.

#### Distributional Extension [7]

Cui & Dinitz 2026 re-interpret $y$ as distribution $\hat{p}$, error $\eta=W_1(p,\hat{p})$. They show additive loss $OPT+O(\min(\max(\eta,1)\sqrt{b}, b\log b))$, robustness $O(b\log b)$ irrespective of $\eta$. This removes requirement to know error bound — algorithm *adapts* without tuning $\lambda$ beforehand.

### 4.2 Online Caching: From Marker to Trust-&-Doubt

Classical paging: $k=64$, $N\gg k$. LRU $k$-competitive, MARKER $2H_k$, randomized $H_k\approx\log k$ optimal.

With predictions $p_i$ of next arrival [2][4][5]:

- **BlindOracle:** always evict page with largest predicted next occurrence (emulating Belady optimal on predictions). *Fails*: even tiny average error $\eta$ can cause $\Omega(k)$ competitive ratio — prediction error accumulates when predicted far future is actually near.

- **PredictiveMarker (Lykouris & Vassilvitskii 2018):** Modified MARKER. Phases defined by $k$ distinct pages. Algorithm partitions pages into *clean* vs *stale*. It trusts predictions only for *evicting unmarked pages*, with fallback to random eviction if eviction chain length exceeds threshold.

Result [2]:

$$ \text{PredictiveMarker is } 2 + O\left(\min\left(\sqrt{\eta/OPT},\log k\right)\right)\text{-competitive}$$

So consistency $2$ (when $\eta=0$ constant) and robustness $O(\log k)$.

**Wei 2020 simplification** [5] proves black-box combination *better* than bespoke:

> Take $A =$ BlindOracle (consistent but non-robust) and $B=$ LRU (robust $O(\log k)$). Combine via switching strategy (Theorem 2 in [5]). Combined is $(1+o(1))$ times best of both, yields $(1+\lambda)$-consistency with $O(\log k /\lambda)$ robustness, simpler and *tight* for deterministic.

| Algorithm | Consistency ($\eta=0$) | Robustness ($ \sup_\eta$) | Smoothness $c(\eta)$ | Requires |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| BlindOracle | 1-optimal | unbounded $\infty$ | $1+ \eta/OPT$ [bad] | predictions |
| PredictiveMarker [2] | 2 | $O(\log k)$ | $2+O(\sqrt{\eta/OPT})$ | $p_i$ per request |
| Robustified BlindOracle (Wei) [5] | $1+\lambda$ | $O(\log k /\lambda)$ | $O(1+\eta/(OPT\cdot\lambda))$ | black-box |
| MARKER alone | $O(\log k)$ | $O(\log k)$ | — | none |

Thus predictions help *iff* combined carefully.

Lower bound: any deterministic $ \beta$-consistent algorithm must be $\Omega(\log k / \beta)$-robust? Open tightening still. Wei shows optimal deterministic tradeoff achieved by LRU+BlindOracle mixing.

### 4.3 Advice Complexity Connection: Trusted vs Untrusted Bits

Advice complexity explains *why* $O(\log k)$ bits suffice to simulate randomization: randomized MARKER needs $\log k$ random bits per phase to pick uniform unmarked page. Same bits, if provided by oracle that knows future farthest page, yield deterministic $O(\log k)$? Actually $3$ bits already beat $k$-competitive deterministic.

**Formal bridge** [1][6]:

- Trusted advice of $b$ bits $\approx$ prediction with $0$ error and $b$-bit encoding limit.
- Learning-augmented with error $\eta$: prediction is *untrusted* advice with weight.
- Purely online corresponds to $b=0$.
- Purely offline corresponds to $b=n\log n$.

Hence curve in Figure: $c(b)$ decreasing from $2$ (or $k$) to $1$ as $b$ grows, but learning-augmented allows *any* $b$-bit predictor whose accuracy degrades, still capped at $c(0)$.

> **Theorem 2 (Advice Simulation, Böckenhauer et al.):** Any randomized online algorithm with competitive ratio $c$ can be simulated deterministically with $O(\log n + \log\log(1/\epsilon))$ advice bits achieving $c+\epsilon$ ratio.

This explains parsimonious caching [4]: Im et al. show you only need prediction for *$\approx OPT \log k$ requests*, not all $n$. Since advice bits per phase $O(\log k)$, sublinear number of predictions suffices to achieve same asymptotic as using all predictions.

*Implication*: ML inference expensive [4]; calling transformer to predict next occurrence 1M times per second unrealistic. Sublinear predictions model reduces cost, still gives same consistency-robustness because MARKER phases have limited distinct pages.

### 4.4 Multishop and Beyond: Rent-or-Buy Universe

MSSR [6] shows framework extends. With $n$ shops, buy costs monotone $b_1>...>b_n$, rent costs $r_1<...<r_n$. Even with $n$ options, 1-bit prediction ($y\ge b_n$?) suffices for $ALG\le OPT+\zeta$ via simple follow. But robustness again fails; $\lambda$-threshold buys: early at $\lambda b_n$ else late.

Technique extends to:

- **TCP Ack** (aggressive rent vs delayed ack)
- **Bahncard problem** (German rail discount card)
- **Online bipartite matching with predictions**
- **Non-clairvoyant scheduling** where $Purohit et al.$ predict job lengths, SRPT emulation [3][5].

Common pattern: **single-parameter online problem with threshold decision**, predictions estimate threshold crossing.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Robustness-Consistency Lower Bound via Yao

We follow Wei & Zhang [1] construction for ski rental lower bound.

Consider $\lambda\in(0,1]$, $B$ large. Define two instances distribution $\mathcal{D}$ uniform over $x_1=\lambda B$, $x_2=B/\lambda$. Predictor $y=x_1$ fixed. Any deterministic $(1+\lambda-\delta)$-consistent must buy at $\le\lambda B$ on $y$ (since when $x=x_1$, $OPT=x_1$, if buy after $> (1+\lambda) x_1$...). Formal calc: $ALG(x_1)\ge k$ if $k\le x_1$ else $x_1$. To keep $ALG/OPT\le1+\lambda$, need $k\le(1+\lambda)x_1 = (1+\lambda)\lambda B$. So must buy early.

But then on $x_2=B/\lambda\gg k$, $OPT=B$, $ALG = k+(B-1)$? Approx $B+k$. Ratio $\approx 1+k/B\approx 1+\lambda$. Wait need $1+1/\lambda$ bound, need different $x$ choice $B$? Modified to $x_2=B$. $OPT=B$, $ALG=B$? Hmm. Actually worst-case robustness when $y\ge B$ but true $x < B$ small: $ALG = \lambda B$, $OPT = x$ small $\to$ unbounded $1/\lambda$ factor. So $x_2 =\lambda^2 B$? Exact bound yields $1+1/\lambda$. Full proof in Appendix B of [1] uses $B\to\infty$ limit and exponential distribution.

For randomized, use Yao: distribution over $x$ uniform in $[0,B]$. Expected competitive ratio of any deterministic given $y$ lower bounded, implying randomized lower bound same.

### 5.2 Caching Empirical Re-evaluation

Lykouris et al. [2] evaluate on BrightKite, CitiBike, Gmail traces. Using simple linear regression predictor of reuse distance, PredictiveMarker beats LRU by 12-18% when predictor error $<0.5\cdot OPT$, and never worse than LRU + 5% (robust). BlindOracle degrades to 2.3× OPT when error spikes — illustrating non-robustness.

Wei's combiner [5] on same traces improves: with $\lambda=0.5$, combined LRU+BlindOracle 4% *better* than PredictiveMarker on low error, and matches LRU on high error.

Parsimonious experiments [4]: querying predictor only on cache misses (not hits) reduces inference calls 70% while competitive ratio degrades $<0.03$.

### 5.3 Limitations of Metrics

- **Error definition $\eta$ matters.** $l_1$ error $\sum|h_i-next_i|$ vs $l_\infty$ vs $W_1$ changes smoothness. Paging predictors predicting next arrival sensitive to heavy tail — $l_1$ may be dominated by one far page.
- **Advice bits vs ML real valued prediction.** Mapping predictions to bits loses granularity. Real valued $y$ infinite bits, but ML quantization needed.
- **Distribution shift.** All guarantees assume $\eta$ bounded post-hoc, not worst-case over training distribution shift. Calibration work [prediction literature] tries to bridge.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Pareto frontier not always known.** Ski rental tradeoff tight [1]. For caching, optimal deterministic tradeoff unknown; gap between $1+\lambda$ consistency + $O(\log k/\lambda)$ robustness vs lower bound $\Omega(\log k)$ is factor 2. Randomized caching with predictions tightness open [5].

2.  **Smoothness vs robustness tension.** Best known smooth bound for caching $O(\sqrt{\eta/OPT})$ [2]; can we achieve $O(1+\eta/OPT)$ with $O(\log k)$ robustness simultaneously? Rohatgi 2020 claims yes with doubling trick, but constant larger.

3.  **Parsimony vs power.** Im et al. 2022 show $o(n)$ predictions suffice *existentially*, but determining *which* requests to query is online itself — chicken-egg. Bandit version where algorithm chooses query set adaptively not fully analyzed.

4.  **Beyond single prediction.** Real ML gives *confidence interval* or *distribution* [7]. How to use variance? Calibration work (Shen et al.) shows calibrated predictions improve ski rental intervals: with variance $\sigma^2$, trust should scale $\propto 1/\sigma$. Framework extension to *uncertainty-quantified* predictions emerging.

5.  **Advice complexity lower bounds for untrusted.** Traditional advice lower bounds assume trusted bits. With untrusted bits, need new technique measuring *information bottleneck* of predictor: if model has VC-dim $d$, how many bits of useful advice can it provide? Open.

6.  **Implementation gap.** TLA+ style verification of learning-augmented systems as *hybrid* ML + classical — no formal methods yet to guarantee that predictor satisfying $E[\eta]\le\epsilon$ on training implies worst-case $\eta$ bound needed for competitive analysis. PAC to competitive gap.

*Future directions*: learning the trust parameter $\lambda$ online via meta-learning (e.g., exponential weights over $\lambda$ grid), extending to graph problems (online Steiner tree with predictions), and linking to differential privacy (advice bits leak input — can we make predictions private?).

---

## 7. Conclusion

Learning-augmented online algorithms reinterpret *advice complexity* through an ML lens: advice bits become *predictions*, possibly erroneous, but cheap and ubiquitous. Consistency-robustness tradeoffs formalize the intuition that one can *have cake and eat too* up to a provable Pareto curve: ski rental optimal $(1+\lambda,1+1/\lambda)$, caching $O(1+\eta/OPT)$ with $O(\log k)$ fallback.

Three lessons:

* **Naive follow is dangerous** — BlindOracle and simple $y\ge B$ buy can be arbitrarily bad despite low average error. Robustification via threshold $\lambda$ or MARKER fallback essential.
* **Black-box combination optimal** — no need bespoke coupling; mixing a consistent (untrusted) and robust (classical) algorithm via $\lambda$-switching is optimal among deterministic [5].
* **Bits are precious, but fewer suffice** — $O(\log k)$ advice simulates randomization, constant beats deterministic, parsimonious $o(n)$ queries preserve guarantees, aligning with practical inference budgets [4].

The field moves from *whether* ML helps online decisions to *how much* and *when*. Next frontier is *distributional, calibrated, query-efficient* predictions [7] integrated with verified runtime monitors — bridging competitive analysis, information theory, and trustworthy ML.
