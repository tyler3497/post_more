# Deterministic Replay and Causal Debugging for Cloud-Native Microservices: Vector Clock Lineage, eBPF Kprobe Capture, and CRDT-Based Trace Reconciliation
## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of deterministic replay microservices: cloud-native microservices exhibit nondeterministic failure modes across time, scheduling, network partitions, and RNG; deterministic replay requires capturing all nondeterminism sources at syscall and middleware boundaries while preserving causality via vector clocks and Hybrid Logical Clocks, reconciled using CRDT merge semantics and eBPF ring-buffer telemetry. We formalize cost models, invariants, and coherence protocols spanning deterministic replay microservices, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, and ACM DL. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and hardware traces including H100 confidential computing, 96-core Genoa, NVLink4 600GBps, RoCEv2 400G CX7, Micron7450 1.6TB NVMe ZNS, Loihi2 Oheo Gulch Lava0.8, USRP X310 6GHz IQ streams. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds, deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations.
