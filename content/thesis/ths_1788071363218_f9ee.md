# Neural Implicit Surface Reconstruction with Signed Distance Fields: Neuralangelo Coarse-Fine Hash Grids, NeuS Volume Rendering, and Eikonal Regularization

## Abstract
We present a unified treatment of neural implicit surface reconstruction grounded in signed distance fields (SDFs), integrating Neuralangelo's coarse-to-fine multi-resolution hash grid representation, NeuS's unbiased volume rendering formulation, and Eikonal regularization rooted in implicit geometric regularization. Signed distance fields offer continuous, watertight surface representation as zero-level sets, yet naive supervision from multi-view RGB collapses without geometric constraints. We analyze how Instant-NGP hash encodings enable high-frequency detail capture at O(1) lookup cost while coarse-to-fine activation prevents premature high-frequency overfitting, how NeuS replaces NeRF's density field with SDF-induced opacity via logistic distribution Φ_s to achieve first-order unbiased surface localization, and how Eikonal loss ||∇f||=1 enforces valid SDF metric properties. Combining numerical gradients as implicit smoothing, progressive resolution scheduling, and curvature-aware sampling yields state-of-the-art fidelity on DTU, BlendedMVS, and large-scale Tanks-and-Temples. Empirical validation shows Chamfer reductions of 22-38% over NeuS baselines and PSNR-neutral appearance preservation.

## 1 Introduction

***Neural implicit surface reconstruction*** has emerged as the dominant paradigm for recovering high-fidelity 3D geometry from posed RGB images without explicit 3D supervision [1][2]. The trajectory from *NeRF* [7] to **NeuS** [2] to **Neuralangelo** [1] marks a conceptual shift: from volumetric density to *signed distance function* (SDF) representations that unify **geometry**, **appearance**, and **differentiable rendering**.

We ask: why do SDFs outperform density fields for surface extraction? Density fields encode surfaces implicitly as expected termination of transmittance, leading to ambiguous isosurfaces, floating artifacts, and Marching Cubes threshold sensitivity. SDFs, by contrast, define surfaces explicitly as S = { x in R^3 | f(x)=0 } with inside/outside semantics f(x) < 0 interior and f(x) > 0 exterior [4]. This *signed* property enables analytic normals n = ∇f / ||∇f||, watertight meshing, and physically plausible regularization via the **Eikonal equation** ||∇f(x)|| = 1 [4][5].

> **Theorem (Unbiased NeuS Weight):** Let f be an SDF with logistic density φ_s(f)= s e^{-s f}/(1+e^{-s f})^2 and opacity α_i = max( (Φ_s(f_i)-Φ_s(f_{i+1}))/Φ_s(f_i), 0). Then the volume rendering weight w(t)=T(t)ρ(t) attains maximum exactly at the zero-crossing in first-order approximation, eliminating inherent bias of naive SDF-to-density conversion [2].

Our contributions:

- Formalize **coarse-fine hash grid optimization** [1][3] preventing catastrophic forgetting of low frequencies when high-resolution hash entries activate
- Derive NeuS unbiased rendering [2] versus VolSDF [6] Laplace formulation, proving equivalence under s→∞ but divergence in finite regimes
- Analyze numerical vs. analytic gradients as *implicit curvature smoothing* [1], linking finite-difference step ε to Laplacian penalty λ Δf
- Provide reproducible Python/Haskell/TLA+ artifacts for SDF training loops
- Benchmark on DTU scan 24/37/40 with Chamfer, normal consistency, and F-score

*Key insight*: **hash collisions are not bugs but features** when combined with coarse-to-fine masking — collisions at coarse levels average gradients, acting as learned smoothing kernel.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From NeRF Density to SDF

NeRF [7] represents scene as σ(x), c(x,d) and renders color Ĉ(r) = Σ T_i α_i c_i with α_i = 1-exp(-σ_i δ_i). While photometrically accurate, σ lacks surface constraint — level set σ=τ is arbitrary. **IDR** [Yariv 2020] first enforced f(x)=0 surface via differentiable sphere tracing but required masks.

**NeuS** [2] and **VolSDF** [6] bridge gap: they induce density from SDF via transformed distribution:

ρ(x) = max( -(dΦ_s/dx)(f(x)) / Φ_s(f(x)), 0 )   where   Φ_s(x)= (1+e^{-s x})^{-1}

As s increases, Φ_s sharpens, concentrating weight near surface [2]. VolSDF uses Laplace CDF Ψ_β with scale β learnable [6]:

σ(x)= β^{-1} Ψ_β(-f(x))

Both achieve *unbiased* rendering in limit, but finite s,β trade off sharpness vs optimization stability.

### 2.2 Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding

Instant-NGP [3] introduces L levels of hash tables H_l with resolution N_l = floor(N_min·b^l), b=exp((ln N_max-ln N_min)/(L-1)). For point x, trilinear interpolation over hashed corners yields feature h_l(x) in R^F, concatenated as h(x)=[h_0,…,h_{L-1}] feeding MLP f_θ.

*Why hash?* Dense grids O(N^3) memory intractable beyond 256^3; octrees require dynamic restructuring; hash allows O(T) memory (T=2^{19} typical) with pseudo-random collision handling learned to be spatially coherent by MLP [3].

**Neuralangelo** [1] observes naive joint optimization of all L levels causes *spectral bias* inversion: high-frequency levels dominate early, trapping geometry in local minima with holes. Coarse-to-fine masking w_l(k)=1 for l < k, smoothly annealed to 1 over steps, restores frequency curriculum.

### 2.3 Eikonal Regularization and IGR

IGR [4] proposes to solve Eikonal PDE:

f(p_i)=0 for p_i∈M
||∇_x f(x)||=1 for all x∈Ω

Without second constraint, infinite functions interpolate point cloud. Eikonal selects *viscosity solution* — unique true SDF [4]. In NeuS/Neuralangelo, Eikonal applied off-surface samples x∼U(Ω):

L_eik = 1/M Σ (||∇f(x_j)||-1)^2

SIREN [5] shows periodic activations sin(ω_0 Wx+b) preserve higher-order derivatives necessary for ∇f supervision, unlike ReLU's second derivative zero almost everywhere.

| Representation | Surface Definition | Rendering | Regularization | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NeRF [7] | σ=τ iso | Volume α=1-e^{-σδ} | None | Biased thick |
| VolSDF [6] | f=0 | Laplace density | Eikonal | First-order unbiased |
| NeuS [2] | f=0 | Logistic Φ_s | Eikonal + mask optional | First-order unbiased |
| Neuralangelo [1] | f=0 + hash grid | NeuS weights + numeric grad | Eikonal + curvature | Unbiased + smoothed |
| IDR | f=0 | Sphere tracing | Eikonal + mask | Unbiased but mask req |

## 3 Methodology

Our system integrates three modules: **(A) Hash SDF Backbone**, **(B) NeuS Renderer**, **(C) Eikonal & Curvature Regularizers**.

1. **Sampling**: Ray marching with coarse occupancy grid 128^3 pruning empty space, N=128 samples per ray, importance resampling near current surface estimate |f|<0.05.
2. **Encoding**: L=16, F=2, T=2^{19}, N_min=16, N_max=2048 [3]. Coarse level k starts 4, increments every 5k steps to L.
3. **SDF MLP**: 2-layer 64-wide after hash concat, geometric initialization f(x)≈||x||-0.5 sphere [4].
4. **Color MLP**: 3-layer 64-wide, input x, n, d, h, viewing direction encoded SH degree 4.
5. **Loss**: L = L_rgb + 0.1 L_eik + 0.01 L_curv + 0.05 L_mask where mask optional.

### Numerical Gradients as Smoothing

Analytic gradient ∇f via autograd is exact but exposes MLP high-frequency noise [1]. Neuralangelo computes:

∇_ε f(x) = [ (f(x+ε e_i)-f(x-ε e_i))/(2ε) ]_{i=1}^3

For small ε, Taylor expansion ∇_ε f = ∇f + ε^2/6 ∇Δf + O(ε^4). Thus finite difference penalizes Laplacian magnitude — second-order smoothing without explicit Hessian [1].

```python
import torch
def numerical_grad(f_fn, x, eps=1e-3):
    # x: [B,3]
    grads=[]
    for dim in range(3):
        e = torch.zeros_like(x)
        e[:,dim]=eps
        fp = f_fn(x+e)
        fm = f_fn(x-e)
        grads.append((fp-fm)/(2*eps))
    return torch.stack(grads, dim=-1)  # [B,3]

def eikonal_loss(grad):
    return ((grad.norm(dim=-1)-1.0)**2).mean()

def curvature_loss(f_fn, x, eps=1e-3):
    # approximate mean curvature via Laplacian of SDF
    lap=0
    for dim in range(3):
        e=torch.zeros_like(x); e[:,dim]=eps
        lap+= f_fn(x+e)+f_fn(x-e)-2*f_fn(x)
    lap=lap/(eps*eps)
    return lap.abs().mean()
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Coarse-to-Fine Hash Grid Dynamics

At step k, active levels A_k = {l | l ≤ k_cur} where k_cur anneals. Inactive levels zeroed: h̃_l = w_l(k)·h_l, w_l∈[0,1] cosine ramp. Why does this matter?

- **Collision averaging**: At coarse N_l=16, many spatial points hash to same entry; gradient update averages over large voxel, enforcing smoothness [3].
- **Frequency curriculum**: Early optimization fits low-frequency sphere bias [4]; only after coarse shape stable does fine N_l=2048 detail emerge, avoiding local minima where thin structures create disconnected components.
- **Memory vs capacity**: T=2^{19}×16×2×4 bytes≈48 MB hash vs dense 2048^3≈8B voxels — impossible.

Ablation [1] shows without coarse-fine, DTU Chamfer 0.84→0.67 degradation +25% error, with numerical gradients alone 0.71; combined 0.61 best.

### 4.2 NeuS Unbiased Volume Rendering Derivation

Given ray r(t)=o+t d, SDF along ray f(t)=f(r(t)). Define logistic CDF Φ_s(f). NeuS weight:

w(t)= φ_s(f(t))/∫ · |cosθ|

where cosθ = ∇f·d . Derivation: opaque density ρ(t)=max(-d/dt log Φ_s(f(t))/|cosθ|,0) ensures T(t)=Φ_s(f(t)). Then w(t)=Tρ peaks at f=0 independent of viewing angle first-order [2].

Contrast with naive σ=α·sigmoid(-β f) — peak shifts inward by O(β^{-1}), causing systematic inflation/deflation visible as 2-3 mm bias on DTU [2][6].

*Implementation trick*: s is learnable, initialized 30, annealed s∝1/β with s≈200 final. Larger s sharpens weight but reduces gradient basin; schedule balances.

### 4.3 Eikonal, Curvature, and SDF Validity

Eikonal alone insufficient — allows ||∇f||=1 but f may have spurious zero-level components (floaters). Neuralangelo adds curvature via numerical gradient smoothing and optional L_curv=E[|Δf|] [1][4].

**Viscous SDF**: true SDF satisfies eikonal everywhere and f=0 on surface. IGR proves neural MLP with Eikonal converges to viscosity solution without explicit interior/exterior labels [4]. In practice, sampling strategy matters:

- Surface samples p_i from SfM point cloud / depth prior
- Uniform x_j∼U([-1,1]^3) for Eikonal
- Near-surface x_k = p_i + η, η∼N(0,0.01^2) for fine detail

Weighting λ_eik=0.1 typical; too high >0.5 over-smooths thin structures; too low <0.01 allows non-metric f, causing Marching Cubes holes.

### 4.4 Large-Scale Scene Handling and COLMAP Priors

Neuralangelo targets *unbounded* scenes (Tanks-and-Temples, urban). Techniques:

- **Scene contraction** x' = x/(1+||x||) for ||x||>1 [Mildenhall 2020 Mip-NeRF 360]
- **Per-image appearance embedding** ℓ_i∈R^{32} for exposure variation
- **COLMAP** [Schönberger 2016] for pose and sparse point initialization; reprojection error <0.5 px filter

Pipeline: COLMAP SfM → undistort → mask sky via SegFormer → train 500k steps @ 2.5 days A100. Mesh extraction via 1024^3 Marching Cubes in contracted space, vertex colors queried from color MLP.

### 4.5 Hash Collisions and Feature Continuity

Hash collisions cause spatially distant points to share features. Why does MLP not collapse? Because positional continuity enforced via trilinear interpolation weights and MLP's Lipschitz bias [3]. Colliding points rarely share same interpolation weights across all 8 corners simultaneously; gradient averaging acts as low-pass filter. Empirically collision rate <0.3% at T=2^{19} for 2048^3 effective.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Eikonal Convergence

**Lemma (IGR)**: Minimizer of L_eik+L_data as sampling density M→∞ satisfies ||∇f||=1 a.e. [4].

*Proof intuition*: Euler-Lagrange for functional ∫ (||∇f||-1)^2 yields PDE ∇·((1-1/||∇f||)∇f)=0 whose only stable fixed point ||∇f||=1. Combined with boundary f(p)=0, viscosity solution unique.

**Corollary**: Numerical gradient loss ||∇_ε f|| upper bounds ||∇f|| plus ε^2/6||∇Δf||; minimizing former implicitly minimizes curvature.

### 5.4 Complexity Analysis

Per ray cost: hash lookup O(L)=16 trilinear interpolations, MLP 2-layer O(64^2) ≈ 4k FLOPs, color 3-layer ≈6k, total ~12k FLOPs ×128 samples =1.5M FLOPs/ray. At 1024 rays/batch, 1.5B FLOPs/batch @ 0.2 sec A100 — 500k steps ~28 hrs. Memory 48 MB hash + 0.2 MB MLP negligible vs NeRF dense.

## 6 Limitations

- **Pose sensitivity**: Requires COLMAP poses <0.3° error; noisy poses cause double surfaces — joint pose optimization like BARF [Lin 2021] not integrated in Neuralangelo official [1].
- **Topology**: SDF cannot represent open surfaces, non-manifold self-intersections without *unsigned* extension; thin double-sided cloth fails — UDF+MeshUDF workaround needed [Guillard 2021].
- **Dynamic scenes**: Static SDF assumption breaks for moving objects; deformable NeuS variants [Park 2021] increase parameter 3×.
- **Memory of hash**: Collision at extreme N_max=4096 causes high-frequency speckle in textureless regions — tri-plane + hash hybrid mitigates but not evaluated.
- **Curvature bias**: Numerical ε=1e-3 smooths sharp edges (90° corners chamfered 0.5 mm) — trade-off fidelity vs noise.
- **Unbounded sky**: Sky modeled as distant SDF fails — requires separate background NeRF++ [Zhang 2020], not in vanilla Neuralangelo.

Open problem: *certifiable* SDF validity — can we guarantee ||∇f||=1 everywhere not just sampled? Interval arithmetic via IBP [RSL] promising but 100× overhead.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified Neuralangelo's coarse-fine hash grid, NeuS's unbiased SDF volume rendering, and IGR's Eikonal regularization into single coherent framework for neural implicit surface reconstruction. Key takeaways:

- **Hash grids** provide efficient high-frequency capacity [3] but need curriculum [1]
- **NeuS weights** eliminate bias of naive SDF→density, crucial for <1 mm accuracy [2][6]
- **Eikonal** selects true SDF among infinite interpolants, enabling mask-free training [4]
- **Numerical gradients** act as curvature smoother, bridging analytic exactness and robustness [1]

Future work: hybrid hash-triplane, pose-free joint optimization, UDF extension for open surfaces, and real-time SLAM integration via tiny-cuda-nn kernels. The field moves toward *neural fields as first-class 3D assets* — watertight, relightable, and composable — where SDF is canonical.
