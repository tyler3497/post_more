# End-to-End Learned Video Compression: Hyperprior Variational Autoencoders, Scale-Space Flow Motion Compensation, Learned Entropy Models, and Rate-Distortion Optimization against VVC

## Abstract

We present a comprehensive treatment of end-to-end learned video compression, tracing the line from hyperprior variational autoencoders for still images to learned video codecs evaluated against the VVC/H.266 reference encoder VTM. We derive the rate–distortion Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = R + \lambda D$ in its variational-inference form, show how non-differentiable quantization is sidestepped by uniform-noise and straight-through surrogates, and how hierarchical side information $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ conditions the latent entropy model $p(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})$. We analyze DVC's predictive-coding architecture — learned optical-flow motion estimation with jointly trained motion and residual autoencoders — and the scale-space flow generalization, which appends a per-pixel scale parameter to the displacement field and warps a Gaussian-blurred reference volume to handle disocclusions and fast motion without pre-trained flow networks. We contrast residual with conditional feature-space coding and joint autoregressive–hierarchical entropy models, evaluate the learned paradigm against VVC via Bjøntegaard delta-rate methodology, and discuss the remaining limitations: autoregressive latency, error propagation, domain shift, and encoder complexity.

---

## 1 Introduction

Video dominates global network traffic, and the economics of its distribution are governed by rate–distortion theory: for a source $X$ and allowed distortion $D$, the rate–distortion function $R(D)$ is the infimum of achievable bitrates. For half a century this bound was approached by hand-engineered block-based hybrid codecs — H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, and now H.266/VVC — whose motion estimation, transforms, quantization, and context-adaptive entropy coding encode decades of signal-processing insight into fixed pipelines [7].

Learned video compression replaces the pipeline with differentiable modules trained jointly under a rate–distortion Lagrangian. The intellectual pivot came from learned *image* compression: Ballé et al. demonstrated that an autoencoder with a fully factorized entropy model could be trained end-to-end for the objective $R + \lambda D$ by substituting quantization with additive uniform noise during training [1]. The subsequent scale-hyperprior extension made the entropy model itself image-adaptive, transmitting a small side-information latent $\mathbf{z}$ that parameterizes the distribution of the main latents $\mathbf{y}$ [1]. This hierarchical variational autoencoder (VAE) formulation became the substrate on which learned *video* codecs are built.

Video introduces temporal redundancy, and the first end-to-end framework to exploit it jointly was DVC [3]: a learned optical-flow estimator produces motion fields between the current frame and the previously reconstructed reference; the flow is compressed by an autoencoder; the reference is warped; and the residual is compressed by a second autoencoder — all optimized under one loss. Agustsson et al. then generalized optical flow to *scale-space flow*, adding a per-pixel blur scale that lets the warping operator degrade gracefully under uncertainty [2]. Together with learned entropy models — hyperpriors, autoregressive context models, and their joint combinations — these architectures define the modern learned codec, and recent instantiations are now compared directly against the VVC test model VTM on common test sets (UVG, MCL-JCV, HEVC CTC sequences) using Bjøntegaard delta rate (BD-rate) [5].

> **Theorem:** *This thesis argues that the learned video codec is best understood as a hierarchical variational inference problem over a temporal graphical model, where motion compensation, residual coding, and entropy modeling are not separate stages but coupled factors of a single approximate posterior, optimized against a distortion constraint through the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$.*

The remainder of this thesis develops that argument: §2 establishes the variational and information-theoretic background; §3 derives the methodology — the hyperprior VAE, quantization surrogates, and the predictive temporal architecture; §4 dives into scale-space flow, entropy-model design, conditional versus residual coding, and the $\lambda$-swept training protocol; §5 presents empirical comparisons against VVC; §6 enumerates limitations; §7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Lossy compression as variational inference

Consider encoding a datum $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ through an analysis transform $\mathbf{y} = g_a(\mathbf{x}; \phi)$, quantizing to $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \lfloor \mathbf{y} \rceil$, and reconstructing $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = g_s(\hat{\mathbf{y}}; \theta)$. If an entropy coder transmits $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ using a probabilistic model $P(\hat{\mathbf{y}})$, the expected code length is the cross-entropy $\mathbb{E}[-\log P(\hat{\mathbf{y}})]$. Ballé et al. observed that this transform-coding scheme is formally a variational autoencoder: the rate term is the negative log-likelihood under the *prior* $p(\hat{\mathbf{y}})$, the analysis transform is the (deterministic) approximate posterior $q(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\mathbf{x})$, and the distortion term corresponds to the negative log-likelihood of a Gaussian observation model $p(\mathbf{x}\mid\hat{\mathbf{y}})$ [1]. The training objective is therefore the $\beta$-weighted ELBO with $\beta$ absorbed into $\lambda$:

$$\mathcal{L}(\phi, \theta) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}[-\log p(\lfloor g_a(\mathbf{x}) \rceil)]}_{R \text{ (rate)}} + \lambda \, \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}[d(\mathbf{x}, g_s(\lfloor g_a(\mathbf{x}) \rceil))]}_{D \text{ (distortion)}}.$$

Varying $\lambda$ traces the operational rate–distortion curve, exactly as varying the quantization parameter (QP) does in conventional codecs.

### 2.2 Quantization surrogates

Rounding has zero gradient almost everywhere, so three surrogates dominate practice:

1. **Additive uniform noise** (Ballé et al.): replace $\lfloor \mathbf{y} \rceil$ with $\tilde{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{y} + \mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{u} \sim \mathcal{U}(-\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$, making the rate term a differential entropy that upper-bounds the discrete rate [1].
2. **Straight-through estimator (STE)**: forward rounding, backward identity gradient — exact rate at train time but biased gradients.
3. **Mixed training**: noise for the rate term, STE-rounded values for the distortion term, closing the train–test mismatch [6].

### 2.3 Conventional video coding and VVC

Hybrid codecs partition frames into I-, P-, and B-frames. P-frames are predicted by block-based motion compensation against reconstructed references; the residual undergoes transform, quantization, and CABAC entropy coding. VVC (H.266, finalized 2020 by JVET) extends HEVC with larger CTUs (128×128), quadtree-plus-multi-type-tree partitioning, affine motion models, and an enhanced entropy engine; its reference implementation is the **VTM** test model. VVC typically reports ~35–50% BD-rate savings over HEVC depending on configuration and content [7]. It is the yardstick against which learned codecs must now be measured — under the caveat that comparisons must fix the operating point (low-delay P vs. random access), the distortion metric (PSNR vs. MS-SSIM), and the test set.

### 2.4 Datasets and metrics

Learned codecs train on large corpora — Vimeo-90K (90k 7-frame clips), YouTube-NT, Kinetics-derived sets — and evaluate on UVG (7 sequences, 1080p), MCL-JCV, and HEVC test sequences [2][5]. **BD-rate** integrates the relative bitrate difference between two RD curves over a PSNR interval; **BD-PSNR** is its dual. MS-SSIM is reported alongside PSNR because learned codecs optimized for MSE historically show a larger advantage under perceptual metrics [1].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The hyperprior VAE

The factorized prior $p(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = \prod_i p_i(\hat{y}_i)$ of early learned codecs is misspecified: neighboring latent coefficients covary in scale. The hyperprior model introduces auxiliary latents $\mathbf{z} = h_a(\mathbf{y})$ capturing these dependencies [1]. The generative model becomes

$$p(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \hat{\mathbf{z}}) = p(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}) \, p(\hat{\mathbf{z}}), \qquad p(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}) = \prod_i \mathcal{N}(\hat{y}_i; 0, \sigma_i^2), \;\; \boldsymbol{\sigma} = h_s(\hat{\mathbf{z}}).$$

Both $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ are quantized and entropy-coded; $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ is small (a fraction of the rate) but makes the entropy model of $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ *input-adaptive*. The decoder first recovers $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, computes $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, then decodes $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ with per-coefficient Gaussian CDFs evaluated at integer bins [1].

*Figure 1. The hyperprior variational autoencoder: analysis/hyper-analysis transforms, quantized latents and side information, and the conditional Gaussian entropy model. In video codecs this module is instantiated three times per P-frame — for the I-frame, the motion field, and the residual [2][4].*

### 3.2 From image to video: the predictive temporal model

Video coding adds a temporal graphical model. Let $\mathbf{x}_t$ be the current frame and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}$ the previously reconstructed reference. The DVC framework [3] factorizes P-frame coding as:

1. **Motion estimation**: a CNN (in DVC, a pre-trained optical-flow network) estimates dense flow $\mathbf{f}_t$ between $\mathbf{x}_t$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}$.
2. **Motion compression**: an autoencoder with its own hyperprior entropy model compresses $\mathbf{f}_t$ to $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_t$ at rate $R_m$.
3. **Motion compensation**: $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t = \text{warp}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}, \hat{\mathbf{f}}_t)$ via bilinear sampling.
4. **Residual compression**: $\mathbf{r}_t = \mathbf{x}_t - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t$ is coded by a second autoencoder at rate $R_r$; reconstruction is $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t + \hat{\mathbf{r}}_t$.

All modules minimize a *single* objective

$$\mathcal{L} = (R_m + R_r + R_I) + \lambda \, d(\mathbf{x}_t, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_t),$$

so motion estimation is no longer optimized for endpoint error but for *rate–distortion*: the network learns to spend motion bits only where they reduce residual rate plus distortion [3]. DVC was the first such joint model and outperformed H.264 in PSNR while matching H.265 in MS-SSIM [3].

```python
# Pseudocode: rate-distortion training step for a predictive learned codec
for x_t, x_hat_prev in dataloader:          # current frame, reconstructed reference
    f_t      = motion_net(x_t, x_hat_prev)   # dense flow (learned end-to-end)
    f_hat, Rm = motion_codec(f_t)            # AE + hyperprior entropy model
    x_bar    = warp(x_hat_prev, f_hat)       # differentiable motion compensation
    r_t      = x_t - x_bar
    r_hat, Rr = residual_codec(r_t)          # AE + hyperprior entropy model
    x_hat    = x_bar + r_hat
    R, D     = Rm + Rr, mse(x_t, x_hat)       # or 1 - ms_ssim
    (R + lam * D).backward(); optimizer.step()
```

### 3.3 The λ-sweep training protocol

A single $\lambda$ yields a single RD point. Codecs are trained at 4–8 logarithmically spaced $\lambda$ values (e.g., 256 to 2048 for MSE), often with a coarse-to-fine schedule where low-$\lambda$ models warm-start from high-$\lambda$ ones. At test time each model produces one (bpp, PSNR) point; BD-rate is computed against VTM anchors run at matched QPs.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Scale-space flow: motion compensation with uncertainty

Classical optical flow assigns each pixel a displacement $(u, v)$, and bilinear warping fails at disocclusions, motion boundaries, and fast motion — precisely where residuals become expensive. Agustsson et al. generalize the flow field with a **scale parameter** $\sigma \geq 0$ per pixel [2]:

$$\mathbf{g}_t(p) = (u(p), v(p), \sigma(p)).$$

Instead of warping a single reference image, the decoder builds a *scale-space volume*: the reference $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}$ convolved with Gaussians of increasing width. Warping indexes this volume trilinearly in $(x, y, \sigma)$. Where the network is uncertain — near occlusions or large motion — it predicts large $\sigma$, effectively warping a blurred reference and absorbing the mismatch into the flow bitstream rather than the residual. The P-frame model is then

$$\mathbf{x}_t = \text{ScaleSpaceWarp}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}, \mathbf{g}_t) + \mathbf{r}_t,$$

with $\mathbf{g}_t$ and $\mathbf{r}_t$ each compressed by separate hyperprior VAEs [4].

### 4.2 Learned entropy models: from factorized to joint autoregressive–hierarchical

The entropy model determines the bitstream; the transforms determine the distortion. Three generations structure the literature:

| Entropy model | Form of $p(\hat{\mathbf{y}})$ | Key property |
|---|---|---|
| Factorized prior | $\prod_i p_i(\hat{y}_i)$, learned CDFs per channel | Simple; misses spatial correlation |
| Hyperprior (scale) | $\prod_i \mathcal{N}(\hat{y}_i; 0, \sigma_i^2)$, $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = h_s(\hat{\mathbf{z}})$ | Image-adaptive scales via side info [1] |
| Hyperprior (mean+scale) | $\prod_i \mathcal{N}(\hat{y}_i; \mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ | Absorbs local mean shifts |
| Joint autoregressive + hierarchical | $p(\hat{y}_i \mid \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{<i}, \hat{\mathbf{z}})$ via masked 3D/5×5 context | Best RD; serial decoding cost [7] |

The joint model combines the hyperprior's global adaptivity with a causal context $c(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{<i})$ computed by masked convolutions, predicting $(\mu_i, \sigma_i)$ per coefficient. It yields the largest single-architecture RD gains in the image-compression lineage and is inherited by video codecs for I-frames, flow fields, and residuals [7][4]. 

![Learned entropy model taxonomy](/thesis/ths_1788802126000_1b60-2.webp)

*Figure 3. Evolution of learned entropy models: factorized priors, hyperprior-conditioned Gaussians, and joint autoregressive–hierarchical models, all trained under $R + \lambda D$.*

### 4.3 Residual coding versus conditional coding

DVC and SSF are *residual* coders: they transmit $\mathbf{r}_t = \mathbf{x}_t - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t$. Information theory suggests a tighter bound is achievable by *conditional* coding — transmitting $\mathbf{x}_t$ given the prediction as side information at the decoder, i.e., coding $p(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t)$ directly, which can exploit correlations the subtraction discards. Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC) operationalizes this by performing temporal conditioning in *feature space*: multi-scale context features extracted from the reference propagate through the encoder and entropy model, and the frame is coded conditioned on them rather than on a pixel-domain difference [5]. Subsequent DCVC-family work adds hybrid spatial-temporal entropy modeling and motion-aligned contexts, and these conditional codecs constitute the current state of the art among learned methods in BD-rate terms [5].

### 4.4 Rate–distortion optimization: theory meets practice

In the Lagrangian formulation, $\lambda$ is not a hyperparameter of convenience — it is the slope $-dR/dD$ of the operational RD curve at the chosen point, the exact analogue of the QP–$\lambda$ relationship in H.264/H.265/VVC mode decision. Two subtleties deserve emphasis:

- **Amortization gap.** The encoder $g_a$ is amortized: one network must produce near-optimal latents for all inputs. Test-time overfitting (instance-adaptive compression) closes part of this gap by fine-tuning latents or decoder parameters per sequence and transmitting the updates — at the cost of encoder complexity [4].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Representative RD performance

The following table summarizes the trajectory of learned video codecs as reported in the literature on standard 1080p test sets (UVG/MCL-JCV), low-delay P configuration, PSNR metric, anchored to x265 (HEVC) and VTM (VVC) [2][3][4][5]:

| Codec | Year | Paradigm | Reported BD-rate vs. HEVC (PSNR) | Standing vs. VVC |

| STAT-SSF-SP | 2021 | SSF + structured autoregressive prior | Beats SSF baseline; competitive with HEVC RGB [5]* | Approaching VVC on some sequences |

### 5.2 What the numbers mean

Three conclusions are robust across studies [2][4][5]:

1. **Motion representation dominates.** The largest historical jumps came from better temporal modeling (joint flow training in DVC; uncertainty-aware warping in SSF; conditional contexts in DCVC), not from deeper residual autoencoders.
2. **Entropy modeling is the quiet multiplier.** Upgrading a fixed SSF-style codec from a factorized prior to a hyperprior to a joint autoregressive–hierarchical model yields cumulative BD-rate improvements comparable to a generational codec change — at the price of decoding serialism.
3. **VVC remains the anchor to beat.** Under PSNR in low-delay configurations, the best-published learned codecs reach rough parity with VTM on favorable content (smooth motion, 1080p natural video) while trailing on screen content, film grain, and scene cuts; under MS-SSIM the learned advantage is larger and more consistent [5][7]. Random-access (B-frame) configurations, where VVC's hierarchical GOP structure shines, remain harder territory for learned codecs, most of which are P-frame-only.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Autoregressive decoding latency.** Joint context models decode coefficients serially; even with checkerboard or channel-wise parallelization, learned decoders run at hundreds of kMACs/pixel [4], orders of magnitude above VTM's optimized integer pipelines. Real-time 4K decoding on commodity hardware is unsolved.
2. **Error propagation and GoP constraints.** P-frame chains accumulate reconstruction error; learned codecs typically use short GoPs (10–12) versus VVC's flexible structures, sacrificing temporal efficiency for robustness [2][4].
3. **Domain shift and generalization.** Models trained on Vimeo-90K/YouTube corpora degrade on out-of-distribution content — screen captures, animation, medical video — whereas VVC is content-agnostic by construction [5].
4. **Encoder complexity and instance adaptation.** Test-time fine-tuning [4] improves RD but makes encoding 100–1000× slower than real time; practical deployment demands amortized encoders with bounded complexity.
5. **Evaluation fragility.** BD-rate comparisons are sensitive to anchor configuration, GoP length, metric choice, and dataset; PSNR-parity claims against VTM do not imply perceptual parity, and MS-SSIM advantages partly reflect metric–loss alignment [1][7].
6. **Lack of standardized bitstream.** Unlike VVC, learned codecs have no interoperability standard; a decoder must match the exact trained weights, complicating deployment.

---

## 7 Conclusion

End-to-end learned video compression has matured from DVC's first joint flow–residual optimization [3], through the uncertainty-aware warping of scale-space flow [2], to conditional feature-space codecs that now contest VVC on its own BD-rate terms [5] — all resting on the hyperprior VAE foundation [1] and the rate–distortion Lagrangian $R + \lambda D$ as the single training principle. The paradigm's strength is its *jointness*: motion, residual, and entropy model co-adapt to the objective in ways no staged hand-engineered pipeline can. Its weaknesses are practical — decoding latency, error propagation, domain fragility, encoder cost — and methodological, in the sensitivity of codec comparisons to configuration and metric. The most promising path forward combines the temporal modeling advances of conditional coding with parallelizable entropy models, perceptually aligned distortion losses, and instance-adaptive techniques that close the amortization gap without breaking the latency budget [4][6][7].
