# On the Construction, Analysis, and Sampling of Score-Based Generative Diffusion Models via Reverse-Time Stochastic Differential Equations: Predictor-Corrector Schemes and Probability Flow Ordinary Differential Equations

## Abstract
Score-based generative modeling unifies denoising diffusion probabilistic models and score matching under the formalism of stochastic differential equations (SDEs). This thesis presents a self-contained treatment of the forward diffusion SDE that perturbs data into tractable noise, Anderson's time-reversal theorem yielding the reverse-time SDE driven solely by the time-dependent score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, and practical estimators via denoising score matching. We analyze predictor-corrector samplers that interleave numerical SDE predictors with score-based Markov Chain Monte Carlo correctors, and derive the probability flow ordinary differential equation (ODE) that shares marginals with the reverse SDE while enabling exact likelihood evaluation via instantaneous change-of-variables. Connections to variance-exploding, variance-preserving, and sub-variance-preserving processes are tabulated, with complexity and discretization error analysis. Empirical implications for high-resolution image synthesis and conditional inverse problems are discussed.

## 1. Introduction

Creating data from noise is the fundamental operation of deep generative modeling. While **generative adversarial networks** learn an implicit pushforward map $G: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{X}$ and **variational autoencoders** optimize a variational bound, diffusion-based methods define a continuous *noising* trajectory and learn its inversion.

> **Key Insight:** The entire generative process can be characterized by a single time-dependent vector field: the *score* $\mathbf{s}_t(\mathbf{x}) \triangleq \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$. Once this field is known, generation reduces to integrating a reverse-time SDE or its deterministic flow counterpart.

The seminal contribution of Song et al. [1] was to encode the discrete Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [5] and Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSN) into three canonical SDEs and to propose a unified training and sampling framework. This work generalizes that, formalizes discretization, introduces predictor-corrector sampling [1,4], and derives the **probability flow ODE** [1,3] for likelihood estimation and accelerated deterministic sampling.

This thesis addresses three central questions:

1.  Under what conditions does a forward SDE $\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}$ admit a reverse-time representation that depends only on $p_t$ and its score?
2.  How do numerical errors accumulate in Euler-Maruyama and ancestral discretizations, and how can **corrector** steps mitigate them?
3.  When does the deterministic probability flow ODE preserve marginals exactly, and what are its computational tradeoffs?

*We assume familiarity with Itô calculus, Fokker-Planck equations, and score matching at the graduate level.*

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Forward Diffusion as SDE

Let $p_0 = p_{\text{data}}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ and $W_t$ be a standard Wiener process. Consider the Itô SDE on $[0,T]$:

$$\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_t = f(\mathbf{x}_t, t) \mathrm{d}t + g(t) \mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_t \tag{1}$$

where $f: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is the drift, $g: [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ the diffusion coefficient, assumed Lipschitz and of sublinear growth to ensure strong existence and uniqueness [6].

The marginal density $p_t(\mathbf{x})$ evolves under the **Fokker-Planck** (or Kolmogorov forward) equation:

$$\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (f p_t) + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \Delta p_t \tag{2}$$

For *affine drift* $f(\mathbf{x},t) = \alpha(t)\mathbf{x}$, the transition kernel $p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0)$ is Gaussian, enabling closed-form score matching.

| SDE Family | Drift $f(\mathbf{x},t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | $p_{0t}$ | Prior $p_T$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Variance Exploding (VE)** | $0$ | $\sqrt{\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^2(t)}$ | $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_0, (\sigma^2(t)-\sigma^2(0))I)$ | $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\max}^2 I)$ |
| **Variance Preserving (VP)** | $-\frac12 \beta(t)\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_0 e^{-\frac12\int_0^t\beta}, (1-e^{-\int_0^t\beta})I)$ | $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ |
| **sub-VP** | $-\frac12 \beta(t)\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)(1-e^{-2\int_0^t\beta})}$ | Same mean as VP, smaller variance | $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ |

*Table 1: Canonical SDEs from [1]. $\sigma(t)=\sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t$ geometric progression; $\beta(t)=\beta_{\min}+t(\beta_{\max}-\beta_{\min})$ linear schedule.*

### 2.2 Score Matching

The score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ is never observed directly. We learn a time-conditioned network $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x},t) \approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ via **denoising score matching (DSM)** [7]:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DSM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,T]} \lambda(t) \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_0\sim p_0} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_t\sim p_{0t}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x}_0)} \left\| \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0) \right\|_2^2 \tag{3}$$

where $\lambda(t) \propto g(t)^2$ is a likelihood-weighting function. For Gaussian $p_{0t}$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_{0t}= -(\mathbf{x}_t-\mu_t)/\sigma_t^2$, yielding a stable $L_2$ regression akin to denoising.

> **Theorem:** (Equivalence to Explicit Score Matching) Under regularity conditions, minimizer of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DSM}}$ equals minimizer of explicit score matching $\mathbb{E}_{p_t}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta - \nabla\log p_t\|^2$ up to a constant independent of $\theta$ [7,1].

### 2.3 Time Reversal of Diffusions

Classic result: Anderson [6] (1982) showed reverse-time SDE exists.

> **Theorem:** (Anderson Reverse-Time) If $p_t$ satisfies (2) and is $C^2$ positive, then reverse process $\{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t\}_{t=0}^T$ with $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t \sim q_t = p_{T-t}$ obeys
> $$\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t = \left[ -f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t,T-t) + g(T-t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{T-t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t) \right]\mathrm{d}t + g(T-t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_t \tag{4}$$
> equivalently forward in reverse time:
> $$\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} = \left[ f(\mathbf{x},t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}) \right]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_t \tag{5}$$

Crucially, drift depends *only* on $f,g$ and the score.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Learning Objective and Network Parameterization

We parameterize $\mathbf{s}_\theta$ with a U-Net backbone with time embedding via sinusoidal Fourier features and adaptive group normalization. Predictor parameterization equivalence [5]:

$$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t) = -\sigma_t \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t), \quad \mathbf{x}_0\text{-prediction} \leftrightarrow \text{score}$$

Training uses $O(N)$ sampling of $(\mathbf{x}_0,t,\mathbf{x}_t)$; per-iteration complexity $O(d\cdot C_{\text{net}})$ where $C_{\text{net}}$ is network FLOPs, typically $O(d \log d)$ for convolutional architectures.

```python
# Denoising score matching for VP-SDE (PyTorch-like pseudocode)
import torch

def loss_fn(model, x0, t, beta_min=0.1, beta_max=20.0):
    # t: [B] in [0,1]
    beta_t = beta_min + t*(beta_max-beta_min)
    alpha_bar = torch.exp(-0.5*torch.cumsum(beta_t,0)) # simplified
    # Gaussian perturbation: p0t = N(sqrt(alpha_bar)*x0, (1-alpha_bar)I)
    mean = x0 * torch.sqrt(alpha_bar)[:,None,None,None]
    std = torch.sqrt(1-alpha_bar)[:,None,None,None]
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    xt = mean + std*eps
    score_target = -eps / std  # grad log p0t
    score_pred = model(xt, t)
    lam = 1.0 # or g(t)^2 weighting
    return lam * ((score_pred - score_target)**2).mean()
```

Computational cost is $O(B\cdot d)$ memory and $O(B \log B)$ stability tricks via EMA of $\theta$ decay 0.999.

### 3.2 Predictor-Corrector Framework

Naive discretization of (5) via Euler-Maruyama with step $\Delta t$ yields $O(\Delta t)$ weak error and accumulates along $N=T/\Delta t$ steps: $O(N^{-1})$ global error under Lipschitz score. Song et al. [1] propose **Predictor-Corrector (PC)** samplers:

*   **Predictor:** Any numerical solver for reverse SDE - Euler-Maruyama, Reverse Diffusion (ancestral), or Stochastic Runge-Kutta. Given $\mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t}$, predict $\mathbf{x}_t^{(p)}$.

*   **Corrector:** Any score-based MCMC that leaves $p_t$ invariant - Langevin dynamics, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC). One step:

$$\mathbf{x}_t^{(c)} = \mathbf{x}_t^{(p)} + \epsilon_t \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t^{(p)}, t) + \sqrt{2\epsilon_t}\mathbf{z}, \quad \mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I) \tag{6}$$

where $\epsilon_t = r \cdot \|\mathbf{z}\|_2^2 / \|\mathbf{s}_\theta\|_2^2$ signal-to-noise ratio $r$ [1].

Iterating corrector $M$ times contracts $D_{KL}(q_t^{(k)} \| p_t)$ geometrically under log-Sobolev inequality (LSI):

$$D_{KL}(q_t^{(k+1)} \| p_t) \le e^{-2\alpha \epsilon_t} D_{KL}(q_t^{(k)} \| p_t) + O(\epsilon_t^2)$$

where $\alpha$ is LSI constant of $p_t$ [2,8].

```haskell
-- Conceptual PC sampler in Haskell-like pseudo
pcSampler :: ScoreNet -> [Time] -> State -> IO State
pcSampler sNet times xT = foldM step xT (reverse times)
 where
  step x t = do
    xPred <- predictor sNet x t dt  -- Euler-Maruyama
    correctorSteps xPred t m
  correctorSteps x _ 0 = return x
  correctorSteps x t k = do
    z <- sampleGaussian
    let eps = r * norm z / norm (sNet x t)
    let x' = x + eps * sNet x t + sqrt (2*eps)*z
    correctorSteps x' t (k-1)
```

### 3.3 Probability Flow ODE

By eliminating the Brownian term and halving diffusion-weighted drift, we obtain a deterministic ODE with identical marginals:

$$\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_t = \left[ f(\mathbf{x}_t,t) - \frac12 g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t) \right]\mathrm{d}t \tag{7}$$

Derivation via Fokker-Planck: set ODE vector field $\tilde{f}$ such that continuity equation matches Fokker-Planck (2). This is **neural ODE** [1,3].

Properties:

*   **Same marginals:** $p_t^{\text{ODE}} = p_t^{\text{SDE}}$ for all $t$ if initialized identically.
*   **Deterministic invertibility:** Enables interpolation, latent encoding via integrating forward ODE from $0$ to $T$.
*   **Likelihood:** Instantaneous change-of-variables:
    $$\log p_0(\mathbf{x}_0) = \log p_T(\mathbf{x}_T) + \int_0^T \nabla\cdot\tilde{f}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t) \mathrm{d}t \tag{8}$$
    where divergence estimated via Skilling-Hutchinson $\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}[\epsilon^T \nabla\tilde{f}\epsilon]$ with $O(d)$ cost vs $O(d^2)$ Jacobian.

```rust
// Divergence estimator (Rust-like)
fn divergence_estimate<F>(f: F, x: Vector, eps: Vector) -> f64 
where F: Fn(Vector)->Vector {

    let jvp = jacobian_vector_product(&f, &x, &eps);
    eps.dot(&jvp)
}
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Discretization Error Analysis for VE vs VP

VE SDE has diffusion coefficient that *explodes* with $t$, $g(t)^2 = \frac{d}{dt}\sigma^2(t) = 2\sigma^2(t)\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})$. Its transition kernel support expands unbounded, simplifying prior matching but exacerbating numerical stiffness: local Lipschitz constant $L_t = O(\sigma^{-2}(t))$ diverges as $t\to0$. VP has bounded $g(t)\le\sqrt{\beta_{\max}}$ and stable variance, but $p_T$ only approximately Gaussian unless $T$ large. Theory [2] bounds Wasserstein-2 error:

*   $\mathcal{W}_2(p_0,\hat{p}_0) \le e^{LT}\cdot O(\sqrt{d\Delta t} + \epsilon_{\text{score}})$ for naive EM,
*   With $M\ge1$ Langevin corrector steps per predictor, factor reduces to $O(e^{-cM} + \sqrt{d\Delta t^2})$ under LSI.

Order of accuracy matters: reverse-diffusion predictor [5] is ancestral sampling with same noise schedule as forward, achieving $O(\Delta t)$ strong order 0.5 but lower bias in practice vs Euler-Maruyama $O(\Delta t^{0.5})$.

### 4.2 Predictor-Corrector Design Space

Predictor choices [1,3,8]:

*   Euler-Maruyama (EM): first-order, $O(N)$ steps $N=1000$ baseline.
*   Reverse Diffusion Predictor: exact reverse for DDPM, uses $p_{0t}$ mean coefficient.
*   Stochastic Runge-Kutta (SRK): higher weak order, adaptive step.

Corrector choices:

*   Langevin MC: leaves $p_t$ invariant, step-size $r$ heuristic $0.16$ for CIFAR-10.
*   Underdamped Langevin / HMC: momentum augmentation improves mixing to $O(\sqrt{\kappa})$ vs $O(\kappa)$ iterations, $\kappa$ condition number, critical when score network poorly conditioned near manifold.
*   No corrector: corresponds to $M=0$, degenerates to standard SDE sampler.

Empirical findings Table 2 from [1]:

| Predictor \ Corrector | None | Langevin M=1 | ALD M=1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| EM | FID 10.2 | 3.2 | 2.9 |
| Reverse Diffusion | 6.1 | 2.5 | 2.2 |
| Probability Flow (Euler) | 4.1 (deterministic) | 3.0 | 2.9 |

*CIFAR-10 unconditional FID (lower better). PC improves $2$–$7$ points.*

Tradeoff: $M$ correctors increase NFE by $M\times N$; wall-clock grows linearly but enables reducing $N$ by $4\times$ at same FID, net speed-up.

### 4.3 Probability Flow ODE: Sampling, Interpolation, and Likelihood

Deterministic mapping $F_{\text{ODE}}: \mathbf{x}_T \mapsto \mathbf{x}_0$ admits:

*   **Temperature control:** Scaling initial noise $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2 I)$ interpolates diversity-fidelity; $\tau<1$ sharper, $\tau>1$ more diverse.

*   **Inversion:** Forward ODE $\mathbf{x}_0\to\mathbf{x}_T$ yields latent code used for editing; editing latent then reverse ODE yields semantically consistent edits [1].

*   **Likelihood:** Bits/dim $= -\log_2 p_0(\mathbf{x}_0)/d$. ODE with adaptive Dormand-Prince (DOPRI5) tolerates $1e-5$ achieves 2.99–3.10 bpd on CIFAR-10, competitive with autoregressive models at $O(N_{\text{FE}} d)$ cost where $N_{\text{FE}}\approx 200$–$500$ vs SDE $1000$.

Singularities near $t=0$ cause stiff Jacobian: divergence of score $O(\sigma_t^{-2})$ leads to $O(t^{-1})$ field blow-up; time reparameterization $\lambda=\log(\alpha_t/\sigma_t)$ mitigates stiffness [9].

### 4.4 Conditional Generation via Unconditional Score

A major advantage of SDE framework is *training-free* conditioning [1]. For measurement $\mathbf{y}=A(\mathbf{x}_0)+\eta$, Bayes:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{y}) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_t)$$

where second term approximated via Tweedie denoiser $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0(\mathbf{x}_t)=\mathbf{x}_t+\sigma_t^2\mathbf{s}_\theta$ and Gaussian likelihood $p(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_t)\approx\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y}; A(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0), \sigma_t^2AA^T+\sigma_\eta^2)$.

This enables class-conditional, inpainting, colorization without retraining, with corrector steps crucial to correct Tweedie bias.

### 4.5 Connection to Schrödinger Bridge and Optimal Transport

Reverse-time SDE is first iteration of Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) solving the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) problem: entropic OT between $p_0$ and prior $\pi$ [4]. SB minimizer $p^*$ satisfies $D_{KL}(p^*\|r)$ minimal over path measure $r$ induced by reference SDE. Iterating forward/backward score learning yields **Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (DSB)** converging to finite-time transport even when $T$ small [4,2]. Weight parameterization in [2] interpolates SDMs (diffusion only) and GANs (discriminator-driven drift).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Reverse-Time SDE Preserves Marginals

*Sketch using Fokker-Planck.* Let $q_t = p_{T-t}$ reverse time marginal. Define reverse generator $\mathcal{L}^*_t \phi = (-f+g^2\nabla\log p)\cdot\nabla\phi + 0.5 g^2\Delta\phi$. Show $\partial_t q_t = (\mathcal{L}^*_t)^\dagger q_t$ equals $\partial_t p_{T-t}$ computed via (2). Detailed steps invoke integration by parts, positivity, and Novikov condition ensuring martingale $\int g \mathrm{d}\bar{w}$ well-defined [6].

Consequently, Kolmogorov equations match, QED by uniqueness for uniformly elliptic $g>0$.

### 5.2 Likelihood Computation: Instantaneous Change-of-Variables

For ODE $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\tilde{f}_\theta$, continuity dictates $\partial_t\log p_t = -\nabla\cdot\tilde{f}_\theta$. Integrating gives (8). Proof via Liouville theorem extended to neural ODE [10]. Implementation complexity $O(N_{\text{FE}} \cdot (C_f+ d_{\text{proj}}))$, memory $O(1)$ with adjoint method.

Empirically CIFAR-10 NLL tables [1]:

| Method | NLL (bpd) | FID | IS |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM | $\le3.70$ | 3.17 | 9.46 |
| NCSN v2 | $\le3.38$ | 10 | 8.87 |
| SDE (VP)+PC | 3.11 | 2.20 | 9.89 |
| Probability Flow | 3.08 | 2.99 | 9.3 (deterministic) |

NLL improvement $0.02$ bpd with $8196$ hidden channels vs $128$.

### 5.3 Scaling to $1024\times1024$

High-resolution generation in [1] uses NCSN++ with 4 resolution levels, attention at $16\times16$, progressive growing. VE SDE with $\sigma_{\min}=0.01$, $\sigma_{\max}=1024$ $415$ network. Sampler PC 1 correction. Compute $O(4000)$ GPU-days for FFHQ $1024$ but final samples rival StyleGAN2 in FID (2.0 vs 3.1) without adversarial training, illustrating SDE flexibility.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

*   **Score estimation error near low-density regions:** For $p_t$ near $t=0$, $\|\nabla\log p_t\|$ can be $O(\sigma_t^{-2})$ leading to exploding network target variance $Var \propto 1/\sigma_t^2$. Variance-reduction via importance sampling $\lambda(t)=g(t)^2$ or $\sigma_t^2$ weighting partially mitigates but not solves. Need *manifold-aware* score architectures exploiting low-dimensional support; current bounds assume LSI on whole $\mathbb{R}^d$ [8].

*   **Computational inefficiency:** $N=1000$ steps $O(10^3)$ network evaluations per sample prohibitive for real-time deployment. ODE solvers with DPM-Solver++ reduce to $O(20)$ steps but sacrifice stochastic diversity. Distillation [9] trains one-step student but introduces $O(\epsilon_{\text{distill}})$ bias.

*   **Corrector stationarity mismatch:** Langevin corrector leaves $p_t$ invariant only asymptotically as $\epsilon\to0$, run finite $M$. For poorly conditioned $p_t$, $M=1$ insufficient; underdamped extensions need careful friction tuning.

*   **Discrete data gaps:** Theory assumes continuous $\mathbb{R}^d$; discrete images dequantized with uniform noise $U[0,1/256)$ alters score near boundaries. Discrete diffusion extensions (D3PM) not captured by SDE.

*   **Evaluation metrics:** Likelihood and FID misaligned; ODE achieving better NLL 3.08 vs SDE 3.11 but FID worse 2.99 vs 2.20. Need unified metric linking sample quality, diversity, and mode coverage.

Open directions:

1.  **Higher-order SDE solvers**: Exponential integrators exploiting affine drift analytically, achieving $O(\Delta t^2)$ with same NFE
2.  **Learned correctors**: Parameterize corrector as learned diffusion sampler (Langevin + normalizing flow)
3.  **Unified GAN-SDM interpolation** via DiffFlow weighting [2] suggests hybrid training minimizing both adversarial and score losses, potentially Pareto-optimal tradeoff
4.  **Infinite-depth limit and beyond:** SB iteration complexity $O(KN)$ $K$ IPF iterations; prove convergence rate in $\mathcal{W}_2$ for $K\ge2$ still open [4]

---

## 7. Conclusion

Score-based generative modeling through SDEs provides an elegant unification of diffusion probabilistic models, score matching, and neural differential equations. The forward SDE continuously perturbs data to noise, the reverse-time SDE inverts it using only the score, predictor-corrector methods compensate discretization with MCMC, and the probability flow ODE reframes stochastic generation as deterministic transport enabling exact likelihood.

The framework's power lies in modularity: *any* affine forward SDE induces a generative model; *any* SDE solver becomes a sampler; *any* invariant MCMC becomes a corrector; and the ODE detour provides inference and editing. At cost $O(N d)$ generation, it achieves state-of-art quality without adversarial instability, opening avenues for conditional synthesis, super-resolution, and scientific data generation where likelihood provenance matters.

Future synthesis will likely blend stochastic and deterministic dynamics adaptively per time interval, exploit Schrödinger bridge refinements for short-time diffusion, and learn noise schedules $g(t)$ jointly with score parameterization — yielding sub-$O(10)$-step, high-fidelity generators with rigorous guarantees.
