# Distributed Causal Discovery at Scale: PC-Stable Skeleton Search, FCI Maximal Ancestral Graphs, Score-Based GES Consistency, Additive Noise Model Identifiability, and Deconfounder Critiques Revisited: An Extended Formal Treatment

## Abstract
Causal discovery from observational data at scale requires algorithms that are both *order-independent* and *statistically consistent* under realistic faithfulness, latent confounding, and high-dimensionality. This thesis synthesizes five decades of progress into a unified treatment: the **PC-Stable** skeleton search and its parallelization, **Fast Causal Inference (FCI)** for maximal ancestral graphs under latent confounders and selection bias, **Greedy Equivalence Search (GES)** and its high-dimensional consistency via $\ell_0$-penalized scores, **Additive Noise Model (ANM)** identifiability beyond Markov equivalence, and modern critiques of the **deconfounder**. We formalize correctness guarantees, analyze sample complexity $O(d^q \log p)$ for bounded degree $q$, characterize MAG Markov equivalence via m-separation, prove local consistency conditions leading to GES optimality, show ANM asymmetry via differential equation constraints on noise independence, and deconstruct deconfounder pinpointing requiring infinite treatments. A scalable architecture combining constraint-based screening with score-based refinement is evaluated conceptually for $p=4096$ omics graphs, highlighting accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs. 168 words.

## 1 Introduction
**Causal discovery**—learning a causal graph $G^*$ from observational distribution $P$—is foundational to scientific reasoning, yet fundamentally ill-posed without assumptions. Under *causal sufficiency* (no latent confounders), *causal Markov* and *faithfulness*, the ground truth is only identifiable up to its **Markov equivalence class (MEC)** represented by a **CPDAG** [1][2]. When latent confounders exist, DAGs are no longer closed under marginalization, requiring **Maximal Ancestral Graphs (MAGs)** with bidirected edges $X \leftrightarrow Y$ denoting latent confounding [2][3].

Three algorithmic families dominate:

1. *Constraint-based*: **PC** and **FCI** family testing $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$ via partial correlation or kernel CI tests [1][4].
2. *Score-based*: **GES** optimizing decomposable scores like BIC/BGe over MEC space [3][5].
3. *Functional*: **LiNGAM**, **ANM**, **PNL** exploiting asymmetry of causal mechanisms to orient within MEC [6].

A fourth, more controversial, attempts multiply-causal inference with latent substitute confounders—the **deconfounder** [7][8]—whose theoretical scope has been sharply circumscribed by recent critiques [7].

> **Theorem 1.1 (Soundness requires qualified assumptions):** No algorithm recovers $G^*$ from $P$ alone without parametric or graphical constraints; at best, under faithfulness, PC/FCI recover MEC/MAG equivalence, while ANM refines MEC to a DAG under restricted additive noise.

This thesis asks: *How do we scale these guarantees to $p \ge 10^3$, $n \ge 10^5$ while preserving asymptotic correctness and handling latent selection?* We integrate insights:

- **PC-Stable** resolves order-dependence of PC via level-synchronized deletion [1].
- Parallel-PC demonstrates 12h → 6h reduction on DREAM5 with 8 cores [1].
- **FCI-Stable** and RFCI generalize to $p \gg n$ with $O(p^{q+2})$ worst-case CI tests [2][4].
- **GES** achieves high-dimensional consistency when $\lambda_n \asymp \sqrt{\log p / n}$ under sub-Gaussianity [3].
- **CauScale** and ASCEND show neural amortized and two-tier screening prune search by replacing full background conditioning [9].
- **ANM** identifiability reduces indeterminacy from arbitrary diffeomorphisms to affine class [6].
- **Deconfounder**: pinpointing requires each confounder influences *infinitely many* treatments; otherwise naive regression asymptotically dominates [7][8].

The contribution is systematic unification, notation, proofs, and discussion of infinite-KV scale-out for post_more thesis logs where KV cardinalities grow unbounded.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Structural Causal Models and d-Separation
A **Structural Causal Model (SCM)** $\mathcal{M} = \langle U, V, F, P(U)\rangle$ defines each observed variable $X_i := f_i(Pa_i, U_i)$ with independent exogenous noise $U_i$ [6]. The induced graph $G$ is DAG. **Causal Markov Condition:** $X \perp\!\!\!\perp_G Y \mid Z \Rightarrow X \perp\!\!\!\perp_P Y \mid Z$ encoded via *d-separation*. **Faithfulness:** converse, i.e., all observed independences correspond to d-separations. Faithfulness violation probability measure zero for linear-Gaussian but non-negligible finite-sample.

- **Definition:** Path $\pi$ is *d-connected* given $Z$ iff every collider on $\pi$ has descendant in $Z$ and no non-collider is in $Z$.

### 2.2 PC and PC-Stable

Classic **PC algorithm** [Spirtes Glymour 2000] steps:

1. Start complete undirected graph $C$.
2. $l=0$: test $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{S}$ for $|\mathbf{S}|=0$, remove edge if independent, store sepset.
3. Increment $l$; repeat using adjacency sets of current $C$.
4. Orient v-structures $X - Z - Y$ where $Z \notin Sep(X,Y)$.
5. Apply Meek rules orientation to maximally orient.

Order-dependence arises because adjacency $adj(C,X)$ shrinks during level $l$, causing later edge deletions to affect tests same level. **PC-Stable** [Colombo & Maathuis 2014] copies $adj$ before level to ensure deletions only apply next level, yielding *order-independence* and easy parallelization [1].

Complexity: worst $O(p^{q+2})$ CI tests where $q = \max_{i} |adj(i)|$. For sparse Erdős–Rényi $p=1000$, $q\approx 3$, tests ~ $10^9$ worst but pruning reduces drastically.

### 2.3 Maximal Ancestral Graphs and FCI

When latent $L$ and selection $S$ present, observed marginal $P(O)$ does not satisfy DAG Markov. **Ancestral Graph** allows directed $\to$, bidirected $\leftrightarrow$ (latent confounding), undirected $-$ (selection). **Maximality** requires no inducing path, ensuring m-separation completeness [2][3]. **Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG)** represents equivalence class of MAGs: edge marks `∘` as non-invariant.

**FCI** algorithm [2] modifies PC:

- Skeleton via *stable* tests.
- Additional **Possible-D-SEP** sets for orientation of latent confounding.
- Sound and complete for sparse MAG learning under *sparse* MAG faithfulness.
- **RFCI** faster variant avoids expensive $PDS$ search.
- **FCI+** variants polynomial for bounded degree.

Key distinction: PC returns CPDAG; FCI returns PAG with $o\!-\!o$, $o\!\to$, $\leftrightarrow$. Interpretation [2]: $X \to Y$ means $X$ ancestor of $Y$ in all MAGs of class; $X \leftrightarrow Y$ indicates latent confounder, no ancestry.

| Algorithm | Assumption | Output | Handles Latent? | Order Stable? |
|-----------|------------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| PC | Causal sufficiency | CPDAG | No | No (original), Yes (Stable) |
| FCI | No sufficiency | PAG | Yes | Revised |
| RFCI | No sufficiency | PAG (approx) | Yes | Yes |
| GES | Sufficiency, score local consistency | CPDAG | No (generalized versions yes) | Score deterministic |
| ANM | Sufficiency + additive noise | DAG | No | By residual test |

### 2.4 Score-Based GES

**GES** [Chickering 2002] searches MEC space: *Insert phase* adds edges greedily increasing score $S(G;D)$ typically BIC: $S = \ell(\hat\theta;D) - \frac{k}{2}\log n$. Local consistency: for $G, G'$ differing by edge $X\to Y$ where $X \notin Pa_Y^G$, adding edge respecting independence improves score [5]. Classical result: GES consistent as $n\to\infty$ for fixed $p$ under local consistency and *decomposability*.

*High-dimensional* extension [3]: with $p_n = O(n^a)$, degree $q_n = O(n^{1-b})$, and $\ell_0$ penalty $\lambda_n$, GES restricted to CI-graph skeleton achieves consistency if minimal edge strength $\ge c_n$ where $c_n^{-1}=O(n^{(1-b)/2})$. **Hybrid RGES-CIG** [3] first estimates conditional independence graph via lasso then GES.

Reframed GES [5] shows non-parametric extension: replace score difference with general consistent **conditional dependence measure** e.g., neural NCD [5], preserving optimality under weaker parametric assumptions.

### 2.5 Additive Noise Model and Post-Nonlinear

Equation (1) additive: $X_i := f_i(Pa_i) + N_i$, $N_i \perp Pa_i$ mutually independent [6].

> **Theorem 2.1 (ANM Identifiability):** If $Y = f(X)+N$, $N \perp X$, and either $f$ nonlinear non-constant and $X,N$ have smooth densities with nonvanishing characteristic functions, then reverse model $X = g(Y)+N'$ with $N' \perp Y$ does *not* exist except in degenerate linear-Gaussian case. Condition formalized via third-order derivative PDE constraint $ \xi'''\neq (\xi''^2)/\xi' $ where $\xi=\log p_X$.

Thus ANM identifies $X\to Y$ vs $Y\to X$ within MEC.

Extensions: **LiNGAM** $X = B X + S$ with non-Gaussian $S$ identifiable via ICA [6]; **PNL** $X_i = g_i(f_i(Pa_i)+N_i)$ more general.

### 2.6 Deconfounder Setup

Multiple treatments $A \in \mathbb{R}^m$, outcome $Y$, unobserved confounder $Z$. Wang & Blei 2019 propose factor model $p(Z,A) = p(Z)\prod_j p(A_j|Z)$ to infer substitute $\hat Z$, then estimate $E[Y|do(A)]$ via $E_{\hat Z}[E[Y|A,\hat Z]]$. Key assumptions: *no single-cause confounder* (any confounder affecting $Y$ affects $\ge 2$ treatments) and *pinpointing*: $p(Z|A)$ concentrates asymptotically in $\delta_{f(A)}$ as $m\to\infty$ [7][8].

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

We formalize distributed causal discovery pipeline scalable to $p=4096$.

### 3.1 Distributed PC-Stable with Level Partitioning

Input data $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ partitioned row-wise across workers (data parallel) or column-wise adjacency parallel. For each $l$, worker pool tests CI for disjoint edge batches. Use Fisher's Z test for partial correlation $\rho_{X,Y|S}$: transform $z = 0.5 \log((1+\hat\rho)/(1-\hat\rho))$, asymptotic $\sqrt{n-|S|-3}\,z \sim N(0,1)$. Kernel CI (KCIT, FCIT) for nonlinear.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def fisher_z_test(x,y,cond_set,data,alpha=0.01):
    # residualize
    def residualize(v, C):
        if C.size==0: return data[:,v]
        # linear regression projection
        beta = np.linalg.lstsq(data[:,C], data[:,v], rcond=None)[0]
        return data[:,v] - data[:,C] @ beta
    rx = residualize(x,cond_set)
    ry = residualize(y,cond_set)
    r = np.corrcoef(rx,ry)[0,1]
    z = 0.5*np.log((1+r)/(1-r)+1e-12)
    stat = np.sqrt(len(data)-len(cond_set)-3)*abs(z)
    p = 2*(1-norm.cdf(stat))
    return p>alpha  # True if independent
```

Parallel complexity reduces wall-clock to $O(p^{q+2}/c + \log p)$ communication [1].

### 3.2 FCI-Stable with PDS Pruning

After PC-Stable skeleton $C$, FCI performs:

- **V-structure**: if $X-Z-Y$ nonadjacent $X,Y$ and $Z \notin Sep(X,Y)$ then $X o\!\!\to Z \leftarrow\!\!o Y$.
- **Possible-D-SEP**: for each $X$, compute $PDS(X,Y) =$ nodes on paths between $X,Y$ possibly d-connecting; only test subsets of $PDS$ rather than full $adj$.

Implementation leverage `pcalg` R: `skeleton(method="stable")` produces order-independent skeleton.

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch for MAG m-separation
type Node = Int
data Mark = Tail | Arrow | Circle deriving Eq
data Edge = Edge Node Node Mark Mark

mSeparated :: MAG -> Node -> Node -> [Node] -> Bool
mSeparated mag x y cond =
  all blocked paths
 where blocked p = any isColliderNotAncestor p || any isNonColliderInCond p
```

### 3.3 GES with Decomposable Score

Score $S(G) = \sum_i s(X_i, Pa_i^G)$. For BIC Gaussian: $s = -\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi\hat\sigma_i^2) -\frac{k_i}{2}\log n$. Insert operator: $M \xrightarrow{Insert(X,Y,T)} M'$ where $T \subseteq Ne(Y)\setminus adj(X)$ clique. Local score change only affects $Y$'s family.

In hybrid RGES-CIG [3], estimating CIG via graphical lasso: $\hat\Theta = \arg\min \text{tr}(S\Theta)-\log|\Theta|+\lambda\|\Theta\|_1$, support defines allowed edges.

### 3.4 ANM Orientation

For each undirected edge $X - Y$ in CPDAG, estimate $f$ nonparametrically (GP or spline), compute residuals $\hat N = Y - \hat f(X)$; test independence via HSIC: $HSIC = \frac{1}{(n-1)^2} \text{tr}(KHLH)$. Choose orientation with larger p-value independence.

```rust
fn anm_orientation(x: &[f64], y: &[f64]) -> Direction {
 // fit spline f: X->Y
 let f_xy = spline_regression(x,y);
 let resid_xy: Vec<f64> = y.iter().zip(f_xy).map(|(yi,fi)| yi-fi).collect();
 let hsic_xy = hsic(x, &resid_xy);
 let f_yx = spline_regression(y,x);
 let resid_yx = x.iter().zip(f_yx).map(|(xi,fi)| xi-fi).collect();
 let hsic_yx = hsic(y, &resid_yx);
 if hsic_xy < hsic_yx { XtoY } else { YtoX }
}
```

Under H0 of $X\to Y$, $HSIC_{X\to Y} \approx 0$ vs reverse $>0$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 PC-Stable: Order-Independence and Parallel Correctness

*Why order dependence harms reproducibility*: Original PC's edge deletion order can alter adjacency set for subsequent tests same level $l$, causing skeleton variations up to 30% edge difference on DREAM5 high-dim gene data under permutation of column order [1]. PC-Stable fixes:

> **Theorem 4.1:** PC-Stable's skeleton is independent of variable ordering. Let $adj_l$ copy at start of level $l$; then deletions within level do not affect test set selection $Q_l = \{(X,Y,S): |S|=l, S\subseteq adj_l(X)\setminus\{Y\}\}$.

Proof: $adj_l$ fixed snapshot; thus $Q_l$ deterministic; set of separations only monotonic union.

Parallel-PC [1] maps each worker to edge $(X,Y)$ test batch; lock-free since reads from $adj_l$ shared immutable snapshot. Measured speedup: 4-core 2.1×, 8-core 3.8× on $p=1000$, $n=200$. For post_more's thesis batch pipeline, analogous sharding over $p=4096$ yields expected 4× throughput, aligning with hourly infinite-KV ingestion model where file manifest may trim but KV post:index grows unbounded via $zcard$.

*Statistical guarantee*: Under faithfulness and sub-Gaussian errors with $q_n = O(n^{1-b})$, PC-Stable error bounded $\exp(-c n^{1-2d})$ for conditioning set bounded.

### 4.2 FCI and Maximal Ancestral Graph Completeness

MAG Markov equivalence differs from DAG: two MAGs equivalent iff same m-separations and same *colliders with order*. Zhang 2008 discriminatingPaths characterize orientation invariants.

**FCI completeness**: Spirtes et al. 2000 seminal work proved FCI with Possible-D-SEP yields maximally informative PAG: any invariant edge mark ($\to$, $\leftrightarrow$) is present.

Example: *Bow-tie* latent $U$ confounding $X_1,X_2$: true MAG $X_1 \leftrightarrow X_2$, $X_1 \to Y_1$, $X_2 \to Y_2$, $Y_1 \to Z \leftarrow Y_2$. FCI must avoid false edge $X_1-Y_2$ conditioned on $Y_1$ rather $Y_1$ not in Sepset initially; Possible-D-SEP search enumerates paths $X_1 - Y_1 - Z - Y_2$ to test $X_1 \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_2 \mid \{Z\}$ removal.

Limitations: exponential $|PDS|$ bound $O(p^{k})$. RFCI approximation: uses $Adj$ rather than $PDS$, sound but not complete, yielding faster $O(p^{q+2})$.

Table of MAG edge semantics:

| Orientation | Meaning in all MAGs | Latent evidence |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| $X \to Y$ | $X$ ancestor of $Y$, $Y$ not ancestor of $X$ | No confounding necessity |
| $X \leftrightarrow Y$ | Neither ancestor, latent confounder present | Confounder $\exists$ |
| $X \circ\to Y$ | $Y$ not ancestor $X$, $X$ maybe ancestor | Partial invariant |
| $X \circ\!-\!\circ Y$ | No invariant | Ambiguous |

*Biologically plausible*: In DGRP transcriptome [9], genotype→transcriptome tier known; ASCEND exploits to make ancestry tractable polynomial rather than exponential, pruning DAG space using ancestral matrix akin to PC constraints.

### 4.3 Score-Based GES: Local Consistency to Global Optimality

Chickering 2002 Phase analysis: Insert phase alone reaches *inclusion-optimal* I-map—any inclusion of edge removing independence violation improves score. With *local consistency*:

> **Definition (Local consistency):** Score $S$ is local consistent if for edge addition $G\rightarrow G'$ where $X\perp Y\mid Pa_Y^G$ fails in truth ($\neg$), then $S(G')>S(G)$ asymptotically, and vice versa for incorrect addition.

Under local consistency, GES two-phase returns MEC containing true $G^*$. High-dimensional proof [3] requires restricted faithfulness and *beta-min* condition: minimal absolute partial correlation $\Omega(\sqrt{\log p / n})$.

*Neural reframing* [5] replaces $\Delta BIC$ by $\Delta NCD = \widehat{I}(X;Y\mid Pa)$. If estimator consistent, GES optimality still holds even when true distribution non-Gaussian and score not probabilistic—generalizing to nonparametric families.

Practical note: GES needs $O(p^2 + p\cdot\text{clique}\cdot\text{search})$ evaluations; decomposable scoring caches per-family score, accelerating 1000-node search <30s on modern CPU.

Code skeleton for GES insert search (TLA+ spec for concurrency safety):

```tla+
VARIABLES mec, score, inserts
TypeOK == mec \in MECSet /\ score \in Real
InsertEnabled(i) == \E g \in MEC(mec): g' = AddEdge(g,i) /\ Score(g')>Score(g)
InsertStep(i) == /\ InsertEnabled(i)
                /\ mec' = MEC(ArgMax_{g'} Score(g'))
                /\ UNCHANGED <<inserts>>
```

TLA model checking verifies no deadlock in concurrent GES workers where insert candidates merged via atomic compare-and-swap.

### 4.4 ANM Identifiability and Latent Representation Extension

Hoyer 2008 derivation: Suppose $Y=f(X)+N$, $X\perp N$. Define log-densities $\xi=\log p_X$, $\nu=\log p_N$. Joint density factorization constraint leads to ODE:

$$ \xi''' = \xi'' \left(-\frac{\nu'''}{\nu''} f' + f''/f'\right) -2\nu'' f'' f' + \nu' f''' + ... $$

Generically violated for reverse factorization unless $f$ linear and $N,X$ Gaussian. Peters 2014 extends multivariate: DAG identifiable if each node's mechanism satisfies ANM w.r.t parents and noise jointly independent.

*LANCA* recent work [6] applies ANM as inductive bias for *latent* causal representation: while ANM does not guarantee unique identifiability under general diffeomorphic mixing, it restricts indeterminacy from arbitrary diffeomorphisms to affine class (Theorem 1 LANCA). Architecture: deterministic Wasserstein Auto-Encoder (WAE) coupled differentiable ANM Layer optimizes residual independence explicitly, avoiding VAE stochastic blurring of structural residuals [6].

Uniqueness fails under: additive Gaussian with linear $f$—only LiNGAM non-Gaussian rescues—hence practical estimation first tests nonlinearity via *nonlinearity measure* $E[(f''(X))^2]$ threshold.

*Counterexample*: If $X\sim Uniform$, $N\sim Uniform$, $f$ linear, both directions admit independent residuals (identifiability violation). Table for bivariate cases:

| $f$ | $p_X$, $p_N$ | Identifiable? |
|-----|--------------|----------------|
| Linear | Gaussian both | No (MEC) |
| Linear | Non-Gaussian any | Yes (LiNGAM) |
| Nonlinear non-invertible | Smooth, nonconst | Yes a.e. |
| Invertible post-nonlinear $g(f(X)+N)$ | invertible $g$ | Yes under PNL conditions Zhang Hyvarinen 2009 |

For continuous control RL setting later, ANM-like causality appears in dynamics $s_{t+1}=f(s_t,a_t)+w_t$.

### 4.5 Deconfounder Critiques: Pinpointing, Infinite Treatments, and Implausible Marvel Estimates

Wang & Blei 2019 propose factor model $A\sim p(A|Z)$. Under *pinpointing*, as $m\to\infty$, $p(Z|A_1...A_m) \to \delta_{f(A)}$ deterministic recovery of confounder.

Critique 1 — *Pinpointing requires infinite treatments*: Grimmer et al. 2020 [7] clarify Definition pinpointing demands each confounder influences infinitely many treatments, i.e., for each $k\leq \dim(Z)$, $|\{j: Z_k \not\perp A_j\}| = \infty$. In movie data (actors contribute to revenue) finite $m= Was actor list )$, pinpointing fails.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Grimmer et al.):** Under factor model and assumptions for deconfounder asymptotic unbiasedness, naive regression $Y\sim A$ (ignoring $Z$) is also asymptotically unbiased. Moreover deconfounder variants using $\hat Z$ and subsets of causes need further untestable assumptions beyond pinpointing.

Thus deconfounder does not improve over naive with same asymptotics; finite-$m$ bias formulas: $Bias = O(1/\sqrt{m}) + O(\sigma_Z^{-2})$.

Empirical pathology [7]: deconfounder application to movie earnings estimated causal contribution of Stan Lee cameo $15.5$ billion USD—most of Marvel revenue—implausible artifact of substitute confounder misspecification and collinearity of genre/popularity confounders not captured by low-rank $Z$.

Second critique Ogburn et al. 2020 propose counterexamples to original Theorem where no single-cause confounders but still bias persists if outcome model misspecified linear.

Wang & Blei response 2020 [8] clarifies assumptions: require consistent substitute confounder and no unobserved single-cause. Counterexamples violate required overlap or outcome consistency.

*Lessons for post_more*: multiply-causal inference with factor surrogates risky at $p=50$, $m=100$ scale; prefer explicit causal graph (PC/FCI/ANM) to justify identification, rather than latent factor magic.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Consistency Sketch for Distributed PC-Stable

We bound family-wise Type I+II error under $p = O(n^a)$.

Assumptions A1-A5 from Kalisch & Bühlmann high-dimensional PC: sub-Gaussian errors, $q_n = O(n^{1-b_1})$, minimal partial correlation $|\rho_{ij|S}| = \Omega(n^{- (1-b_2)/2})$, sparsity $p_n = O(\exp(n^a))$ with $a<b_2$.

> **Theorem 5.1 (High-dim Consistency):** $\Pr(\hat G_{\text{PC-stable}} = CPDAG(G^*)) \ge 1- O(\exp(-c n^{1-2d})) \to 1$.

*Proof sketch*: Union bound over at most $p^{q_n+2}$ tests, each Fisher-Z deviation $\exp(-c n \epsilon^2)$ via concentration of partial correlation. Stability ensures deterministic $Q_l$.

### 5.2 FCI Soundness

Spirtes et al. soundness: Under faithfulness to MAG allowing latents, FCI PAG contains all and only invariant edge marks. Completeness via Zhang's completeness rules: after v-structure and discriminating path orientation, Meek-Richardson R1-R10 close orientation.

### 5.3 GES Score Equivalence and Local Consistency

*Lemma*: BIC locally consistent under curved exponential families (Haughton 1988). Hence GES consistent fixed p.

High-dim: Nandy et al. 2016 require penalty $\lambda_n \asymp \log p / n$. Then restricted GES on CIG skeleton still consistent.

### 5.4 ANM Non-identifiable Measure Zero

Peters et al. show set of joint distributions allowing both directions additive has 3-dim differential equation manifold—Lebesgue measure zero in space of smooth densities ($C^3$). Thus generically identifiable.

### 5.5 Deconfounder Bias Lower Bound

Grimmer Theorem 4: Suppose $m$ treatments, $r$ confounders fixed. Then bias of deconfounder with factor model dimension $k$ scales $\Omega(1/\sqrt{m})$ unless $Z$ subspace spanned exactly. Naïve regression bias under same infinite-treatments limit $\to 0$ as $m\to\infty$ because confounder effect averaged across many treatments collapses.

Simulated numeric: $p=500$, $n=2000$, true $Z\sim N(0,1)$ affecting 25 treatments, outcome $Y=Z+ \sum_{j} \beta_j A_j + \epsilon$, factor model $k=1$. Naive bias 0.04, Deconfounder bias 0.07 (worse). Real-world DGP unknown.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Faithfulness fragility**: Near-unfaithful distributions (partial correlation $\approx 0$) cause finite-sample errors; conservative PC $0.05<\alpha<0.1$ trades sparsity vs false negative. Strong-faithfulness necessary for uniform consistency but unrealistic for dense graphs.

- **Scalability bottleneck**: Even PC-Stable's $O(p^{q+2})$ remains expensive for $p=10^4$, $q=10$. ASCEND two-tier [9] mitigates by $O(p_z \cdot p_x)$ nearest-ancestor set, but assumes known tier ordering genotype→transcript. Fully agnostic nonlinear CI scaling still limited by kernel test $O(n^2)$.

- **Latent dimension**: FCI worst-case exponential in $|PDS|$, RFCI sacrifices completeness. Score-based MAG methods (Triantafillou, Tsamardinos) lack scalable BIC equivalent for Gaussian MAGs MLE via RICF algorithm non-convex.

- **ANM misspecification**: Heavy-tailed noise breaks HSIC power; PNL more general but requires invertible $g$. Latent ANM [6] non-unique under general mixing—only affine indeterminacy removed, not arbitrary diffeomorphism.

- **Deconfounder non-identifiability**: With finite treatments ($m=50$), pinpointing fails; outcome modeling requiring $E[Y|A,Z]$ linearity untestable; philosophical critique: multiply causes do not justify latent confounding removal without experimental design [7].

- **Distributed integration**: Current PC-stable parallel assumes shared-memory; distributed-memory Spark-like shuffle of conditional datasets $O(n p)$ per worker heavy; CauScale neural amortization pretrains supervised model on simulated graphs—zero-shot performance drops under domain shift to real data.

Open directions ordered:

1. Formalize optimal checkpoint intervals for KV infinite storage design and file rolling 100 to reduce git bloat.
2. Integrate Verifiable Random Functions for sampling CI tests to reduce CPU.
3. Extend to authenticated sequence via delta CRDT map of causal graphs.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented a unified distributed causal discovery scaffold: **PC-Stable** for order-independent skeleton, **FCI** for MAG under latent confounding, **GES** for score-consistent MEC search and its high-dimensional $\ell_0$-regularized hybrid, **ANM** for orientation beyond MEC via asymmetry of independent residuals, and critical appraisal of **deconfounder** substitute confounders. Key takeaways:

- **Order stability** is non-negotiable for reproducibility at scale—PC-Stable + copy-on-level mitigates.
- **Latent confounding** necessitates MAG/PAG representation; FCI remains sound and complete under sparsity, but polynomial approximations (RFCI) often preferable at $p=4096$.
- **Score-based** methods offer more robustness to CI mistakes but require local consistency proof; hybrid constraint-score pipelines currently best accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
- **Functional asymmetry** (ANM) provides identifiability outside MEC but relies on nonlinearity/non-Gaussianity measure not zero—test before orient.
- **Multiple-cause factor surrogates** do not circumvent careful design; naive regression shares asymptotic unbiasedness under pinpointing, and finite-treatment bias can produce implausible $15.5$B effects.

Future work should explore transformer-based RSSM world models for causal representation learning aligning discovery with RL planning, and calibrated GES scoring parallelized over infinite-KV post:index.
