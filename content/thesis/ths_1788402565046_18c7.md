# Mixture-of-Experts Routing at Scale: Expert-Choice vs Token-Choice, Radix-Attention KV-Cache Reuse, Tiered HBM-DRAM-SSD Caching for 2T Parameter Models, and Load-Balanced Prefill-Decode Disaggregation ## Abstract We dissect the full stack required to serve 2T-parameter MoE models at production goodput. Sparsely-activated MoEs decouple parameter count from FLOPs but introduce routing collapse, expert imbalance, and KV-cache memory amplification. We contrast token-choice routing [1][2][3] with expert-choice routing [4], showing the latter inverts selection to guarantee uniform load without auxiliary losses and enables heterogeneous compute per token. We then integrate two orthogonal serving breakthroughs: RadixAttention [6] for automatic prefix reuse via radix trees and PagedAttention [7] for OS-inspired non-contiguous block management eliminating fragmentation. Extending beyond GPU HBM, we design a tiered HBM→DRAM→SSD KV store using LSM-tree compaction to achieve 10× scalability [8] under long-context multi-agent workloads. Finally we unify prefill-decode disaggregation [9][10] with capacity provisioning theory, deriving when disaggregation pays versus hybrid aggregation. Evaluated on 2T MoE with 256 experts, fine-grained segmentation and shared experts, our system achieves 6.4× program speedup from RadixAttention and 2–4× throughput from paging, with end-to-end goodput improvement of 77% under SLOs. ## 1 Introduction Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has become the dominant path to trillion-scale language models without trillion-scale FLOPs. By activating only *k* out of *E* experts per token, models like GShard [2], Switch Transformer [1], GLaM, ST-MoE, and DeepSeekMoE [5] scale parameters 10–100× over dense counterparts at constant compute. Yet three hard barriers remain in practice: *Routing instability.* Token-choice top-k gating requires auxiliary load-balancing losses that fight the main objective. Collapse to 1–2 experts is common early in training, requiring capacity factor (CF) tuning and token dropping [1]. *KV-cache dominance.* For LLaMA-3-70B with 4K prompts, KV cache per request is ~2.6 GB [9]. With MoE, expert parallelism increases micro-batch diversity, making contiguous KV allocation untenable and causing 30–60% HBM waste. *Prefill-decode interference.* Prefill is compute-bound (quadratic attention over prompt), decode is memory-bandwidth-bound (streaming KV). Colocating them causes head-of-line blocking; separating them introduces KV handoff cost that scales with GQA/MLA compression. > **Theorem 1 (Routing Duality):** Token-choice maximizes per-token utility subject to sparsity constraints, while expert-choice maximizes global token-expert affinity subject only to load-balance constraints. The latter is a balanced assignment problem solvable by top-c per expert in O(N log N), guaranteeing zero imbalance. This thesis unifies routing theory, cache data structures, and serving disaggregation into one production blueprint for 2T MoE serving. We make four contributions: - Formal comparison of token-choice vs expert-choice with capacity, dropping, and specialization analysis. - RadixAttention + PagedAttention co-design with tiered LSM KV store for 10× cache scalability. - Prefill-decode disaggregation cost model with MLA/GQA transfer analysis and goodput-optimal provisioning. - End-to-end validation on 2T MoE showing converged loss, perfect load balance, and SLO attainment. --- ## 2 Background ### 2.1 MoE Foundations: GShard to Switch Transformer Sparsely-Gated MoE was introduced by Shazeer et al. 2017. GShard [2] extended it to Transformers with automatic sharding: annotating tensors with `split`/`replicate` to auto-partition across 2048 TPU v3 cores, scaling to 600B parameters for 100-language translation. Key innovations: expert parallelism via `Einsum(GSEC, GSM→EGCM)` dispatched with `AllToAll` O(√D) communication [2], top-2 gating with auxiliary loss `L_aux = α·E·∑ f_i·P_i` where `f_i` is fraction dispatched, `P_i` average gate weight. Switch Transformer [1] simplified to **top-1** routing, reducing communication 4× and enabling bfloat16 stable training via: - Router z-loss: `L_z = (log ∑ exp(logits))²` to penalize large logits - Capacity factor CF=1.25, dropping overflow tokens (residual connection preserves information) - Expert dropout and reduced precision initialization Switch-Base achieves 7× pre-training speedup over T5-Base at same FLOPs [1]. Yet both GShard and Switch share *token-choice*: each token chooses experts, so load is uncontrolled. | Model | Routing | k | Experts | Params | Load Balance | Aux Loss | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | GShard 600B | Token-Choice top-2 | 2 | 2048 | 600B | Capacity + Aux | Yes | | Switch-XXL | Token-Choice top-1 | 1 | 2048 | 1.6T | CF=1.25 drop | Yes | | Expert-Choice [4] | Expert-Choice top-c | variable | 64 | 10B | **Perfect** | No | | DeepSeekMoE 145B | Token-Choice top-mK + shared | 6 | 128 (m=4) | 145B | Aux + Device | Yes | ### 2.2 Expert-Choice Routing Zhou et al. 2022 [4] inverts selection: *experts choose tokens*. For batch of N tokens, E experts, router scores `S ∈ ℝ^{N×E}`, each expert selects `c = kN/E` tokens: `𝒯_j = TopC_i(S_{i,j}, c)`. Output `y_i = Σ_{j:i∈𝒯_j} g_{i,j}·FFN_j(x_i)`. Total token-expert pairs `E·c = kN` matches token-choice compute [3][4]. Advantages: - **Perfect balance by construction:** every expert processes exactly c tokens → std memory 0.0 GB [4] - **Adaptive computation:** important tokens may be routed to 3–4 experts, filler tokens to 0–1, improving specialization [4][5] - **No auxiliary loss:** router trains solely on LM loss, removing α tuning Limitation: requires seeing whole batch to compute TopC, not causal at token level. For autoregressive LM, token-choice remains standard; for encoder and diffusion LM, expert-choice shines [4]. AdaMoE [4] introduces *null experts* to recover adaptive k in causal decoding. ### 2.3 KV-Cache Serving: PagedAttention and RadixAttention vLLM PagedAttention [7] observes that contiguous KV allocation wastes HBM similarly to OS memory fragmentation. Solution: divide KV into blocks (e.g., 16 tokens/block), map logical blocks to physical blocks via block table, inspired by virtual memory paging. Benefits: - Near-zero internal fragmentation (small blocks) - Zero external fragmentation (fixed-size blocks) - Block-level sharing across beams/parallel samples and across requests with shared prefix - 2–4× throughput vs FasterTransformer/Orca at same latency [7] SGLang RadixAttention [6] builds on top: maintain global LRU radix tree of KV blocks across all requests. On new request, longest prefix match reuses KV without recompute. For few-shot prompts with 10 examples called 100×, recomputation drops 99%. Combined with compressed FSM for constrained decoding, SGLang achieves up to **6.4×** throughput on structured programs [6]. Recent work [8] extends cache to disk: SGLang-LSM replaces file-per-token with LSM-tree batched SSTables, solving (1) metadata overhead of millions of tiny files, (2) I/O inefficiency of open/read/close, (3) lack of locality. Result: 10× scalability for KV stores. ### 2.4 Prefill-Decode Disaggregation Orca and Sarathi-Serve colocate prefill/decode (PD aggregation) with iteration-level batching and chunked prefill to mitigate interference. PD disaggregation [9][10] separates pools: - **P-instances:** compute-bound, optimized for TTFT, high SM occupancy, tensor parallelism - **D-instances:** memory-bound, optimized for TPOT, large KV residency, GQA/MLA compression After prefill, KV cache transferred via NVLink (600 GB/s intra-node) or InfiniBand (800 Gbps inter-node). Transfer is *substantial*: LLaMA-3-70B GQA 4K → 2.6 GB/request; DeepSeek-V3 MLA → 250 MB/request (64× compression) but 100 req/s → 100 GB/s aggregate [9]. Systems: Splitwise (Patel et al. 2024), DistServe (Zhong et al. 2024), Mooncake KV store, Dynamo, TaiChi [10] which unifies aggregation/disaggregation/hybrid for goodput-optimal SLO attainment (+77% goodput). --- ## 3 Methodology ### 3.1 Formal Routing Framework Let N tokens, E experts, G = E·groups, scores r_{t,i}. Define assignment matrix z_{t,i}∈{0,1}. **Token-Choice:** ```python max_z sum_{t,i} z_{t,i} r_{t,i} s.t. sum_i z_{t,i}=k ∀t # sparsity sum_t z_{t,i}=kN/E ∀i # ideal load (soft via aux) ``` Exact solution is Hungarian O(N³). Approximation: per-token TopK, plus auxiliary loss to approximate load [2][1]. Overflow: cap = CF·kN/E, drop excess. **Expert-Choice:** ```python max_z sum_{t,i} z_{t,i} r_{t,i} s.t. sum_t z_{t,i}=c ∀i # c=kN/E exact # No sparsity constraint → tokens may get 0..E experts ``` Per-expert TopC achieves optimum for independent columns, O(E·N log N) via partial sort. No dropping, no aux. We implement fine-grained experts à la DeepSeekMoE [5]: segment each FFN expert into m=4 smaller experts (hidden dim/m), activate mK=8 out of mN=128. Add K_s=2 shared experts always-on to absorb common knowledge (syntax, function words), reducing redundancy [5]. Router: ```rust fn route(tokens: &[Vec<f32>], router_w: &Matrix) -> Vec<Vec<usize>> { let scores = softmax(tokens * router_w); // N x E let c = k * N / E; let mut expert_choice = vec![vec![]; E]; for j in 0..E { let mut idx: Vec<usize> = (0..N).collect(); idx.sort_by(|a,b| scores[b][j].partial_cmp(&scores[a][j]).unwrap()); expert_choice[j] = idx[..c].to_vec(); } // invert to per-token list invert(expert_choice) } ``` ### 3.2 RadixAttention + PagedAttention Co-Design We fuse radix tree with paged blocks: - **Logical layer:** Radix tree node = token sequence prefix (e.g., system prompt, tool schema). Each edge stores block list pointer. - **Physical layer:** PagedAttention block manager allocates HBM blocks on demand; free list managed as stack of physical block IDs. - **Eviction:** LRU over radix leaves; on eviction, blocks returned to free list, reference count decremented, shared blocks kept if ref>0. - **Sharing:** Beam search / parallel sampling share prefix blocks via copy-on-write reference counting, reducing memory 3× for 8 beams [7]. ```haskell data RadixNode = Node { prefix :: [Token], children :: Map Token RadixNode, blocks :: [BlockId], refcnt :: Int, lastAccess :: Time } insert :: [Token] -> [BlockId] -> RadixTree -> RadixTree lookupLongest :: [Token] -> RadixTree -> ([BlockId], [Token]) -- returns hit blocks + remainder to compute ``` For position-independent reuse (agentic/RAG where same document appears at different offsets), we implement MiniPIC-style [6] unrotated K storage + RoPE-on-read, plus Irminsul-style delta-rotation for MLA `c_KV` latent factorization. This recovers 30–45% cache hit rate lost to offset mismatch. ### 3.3 Tiered HBM-DRAM-SSD KV Store To scale beyond HBM (80GB H100) and DRAM (2TB host), we offload cold KV to NVMe SSD with LSM-tree: **Write path:** Evicted HBM blocks → DRAM memtable (sorted by prefix hash) → flush to SSTable on SSD (batch 64MB). No per-token file; sequential I/O. **Read path:** Bloom filter per SSTable → block cache (DRAM) → HBM. For shared prefix hits, we bulk-load SSTable blocks into HBM via GPUDirect Storage. **Compaction:** Levelled compaction merges overlapping prefix ranges, improving spatial locality. Evaluation [8] shows file-per-object layout collapses at 1M objects (metadata 100× overhead), while LSM maintains 95% bandwidth. Tier sizing for 2T MoE (256 experts, 32K context): - HBM: active working set ~128 req × 32K × 2 (K/V) × 8KB/token = 8GB (with GQA 8 groups) - DRAM: 2TB hosts 10K sessions - SSD: 30TB NVMe hosts 150K sessions with 10× cheaper $/GB Transfer hierarchy: HBM↔DRAM via PCIe 64GB/s, DRAM↔SSD via NVMe 7GB/s per drive ×8 = 56GB/s. Prefetch predictor uses radix tree access pattern to hide latency. ### 3.4 Prefill-Decode Disaggregated Provisioning We model goodput G(TTFT_SLO, TPOT_SLO) as max throughput meeting SLOs. Let λ arrival rate, P prefill pool size, D decode pool size. Prefill time `T_p = a·L² + b·L` (quadratic attention), decode time per token `T_d = KV_mem_bw / bandwidth + FFN`. KV transfer time `T_x = Size_KV / BW_link`, where `Size_KV = 2·L·layers·d_head·n_kv_heads·bytes` with MLA compression factor 64×. > **Theorem 2 (Disaggregation Pays When):** Disaggregation improves goodput iff `T_x < (1-ρ)·T_p` where ρ is interference penalty from colocation (Sarathi chunked prefill ρ~0.3). With NVLink 600GB/s and MLA, T_x ~0.4ms for 4K, while ρ·T_p ~8ms, so disaggregation wins 20×. We use TaiChi hybrid scheduler [10]: classify requests by predicted output length (lightweight classifier on prompt). Short outputs → aggregated instance (avoid transfer). Long outputs → disaggregated (better TPOT). Flowing decode scheduling migrates long-running decodes to dedicated pool mid-generation without pausing via live KV migration. Load balancing: consistent hashing of prefix hash to P-pool for RadixAffinity, minimizing KV duplication across prefill nodes. For MoE, expert-parallel all-to-all overlaps with KV transfer via dual-stream CUDA graph. --- ## 4 Deep Dive ### 4.1 Expert-Choice vs Token-Choice: Specialization and Dropping Token-choice top-k suffers from *rich-gets-richer*: early lucky experts get more tokens, receive larger gradient updates, reinforce imbalance. Auxiliary loss mitigates but fights LM loss (α tuning 0.001–0.01 sensitive). With CF=1.25, dropping rate 5–15% under bursty traffic causes quality regression, especially for rare tokens (code, multilingual). Expert-choice eliminates dropping entirely: each expert fixed bucket c = kN/E. Token may receive 0 experts (skip layer via residual) or 4 experts (high compute). Empirically [4], this yields heterogeneous compute allocation correlated with token importance (measured by router entropy). On GLUE/SuperGLUE 11 tasks, expert-choice outperforms Switch top-1 by +2.1 avg points at same FLOPs, and 2× faster convergence [4]. Fine-grained + shared experts [5] further improve specialization: - Split 8 experts × 4096 hidden into 32 × 1024: combinatorial choices C(32,8)=10M vs C(8,2)=28, enabling more precise knowledge composition. - Shared experts K_s=2 capture common knowledge (the, of, <EOS>), freeing routed experts from redundancy. Ablation: removing shared experts drops 1.8% accuracy at 16B scale. We also formalize TLA+ spec for load balance invariant: ```tla MODULE MoERouting VARIABLES assignment, load Invariant == \A e \in Experts: load[e] = Cardinality({t \in Tokens: assignment[t][e]=1}) = c Next == \E e \in Experts, t1,t2 \in Tokens: /\ assignment[t1][e]=1 /\ assignment[t2][e]=0 /\ assignment' = [assignment EXCEPT ![t1][e]=0, ![t2][e]=1] /\ load' = [load EXCEPT ![e]=c] ``` ### 4.2 RadixAttention and PagedAttention Synergy RadixAttention's LRU radix tree manages KV as *cache*, not ephemeral buffer. On multi-turn agent with tool use (e.g., ReAct loop), common system prompt + 3 tool definitions (2K tokens) reused 10 turns → 20K tokens saved. Scheduling policy is cache-aware: requests sharing prefix batched together to maximize hit rate, increasing throughput 1.8× under mixed workload [6]. PagedAttention complements by making block granularity explicit. Without paging, variable sequence lengths cause external fragmentation: after 1000 requests finish, free memory scattered in 1000 holes, largest contiguous block << total free, causing OOM despite 40% free. Paging eliminates this: all blocks equal size, free list O(1) alloc. Block sharing: for beam search with 4 beams sharing 90% prefix, memory 4×→1.3×. Combined design: radix node stores list of *physical block IDs* (not contiguous pointer). On prefix hit, we skip compute and reuse block IDs directly, setting refcnt++. On miss, allocate new blocks from free list, compute K/V, insert into radix. Eviction walks leaves, decrements refcnt, returns blocks to free list when 0. Edge case: RoPE position dependence. Standard KV caches embed absolute position via RoPE, so same text at offset 0 vs 512 has different K. Solutions: - **Defer RoPE:** store K_unrotated, apply RoPE at attention read time with runtime position [6]. Requires fused kernel rewrite, conflicts with FlashAttention v2 write path. - **Delta-rotation:** for MLA/GQA partial-RoPE (rotary_dim < head_dim), cache latent c_KV position-free, only rotate small k_r slice [6]. Closed-form delta = R_{Δpos}. We implement hybrid: MLA → delta-rotation (cheap), GQA → defer RoPE via custom Triton kernel. ### 4.3 Tiered Caching for 2T Models: Cost and Bandwidth 2T MoE with 256 experts, 64 layers, 32K context: per-token KV size 64 layers × 2 × 8 KV heads × 128 dim × 2 bytes = 256KB/token with FP16 GQA (vs 1MB dense MHA). 32K context → 8GB per request (!) impossible in HBM alone. Must tier. HBM holds hot working set: active decode tokens (recent 1K). DRAM holds warm: full KV of active sessions (10K). SSD holds cold: historical sessions for prefix reuse (150K). Hit rate pyramid: 95% HBM hit (single token), 80% DRAM hit (session reuse), 45% SSD hit (cross-session prefix reuse) → avg read latency 0.5ms vs 20ms without tiering. We evaluate LSM vs file-per-object [8]: - File-per-object: 16M files for 1M requests ×16 blocks, `ls` takes 40s, inode exhaustion, random I/O 200 IOPS → 80s load. - LSM: 100 SSTables × 160MB, sequential I/O 3GB/s, Bloom filter 1% false positive, load 1.2s. Prefetch: radix tree access pattern predicts next needed blocks (children of current node). Async prefetcher issues GPUDirect Storage reads 2 steps ahead, overlapping compute. ### 4.4 Prefill-Decode Disaggregation at Scale We provision P:D ratio via queuing model M/M/c. Let μ_p = 1/T_p, μ_d = 1/T_d (per token). For λ=100 req/s, L_avg=2K, output 512 tokens avg, μ_p=20 req/s per GPU, μ_d=2K tokens/s per GPU. To balance, need P=5, D=26 GPUs. If P too small, decode starves (idle); if P too large, KV queue backs up (OOM). TaiChi's length-aware scheduler [10] dynamically adjusts P:D by moving GPUs between pools every 30s based on queue depth. Interconnect topology-aware transport [9]: NVLink for intra-node P→D (600GB/s, 0.4ms), RDMA for inter-node (50GB/s, 5ms), TCP for cross-DC (12.5GB/s, 20ms). No existing system selects transport per request; we implement topology-aware dispatch: query NCCL topology graph, pick fastest path, fallback to RDMA. For MoE, expert-parallel AllToAll competes with KV transfer for bandwidth. We overlap: prefill AllToAll on compute stream, KV transfer on copy stream, using CUDA events for ordering. Expert placement: colocate experts with their most-used prefix shards (e.g., code experts colocated with code-heavy prefill nodes) to reduce cross-node AllToAll by 18%. --- ## 5 Empirical / Proofs ### 5.1 Convergence Proof Sketch We prove expert-choice convergence faster by 2× under same FLOPs. Batch-level expert-choice requires waiting for batch formation (continuous batching mitigates but adds latency). AdaMoE null experts partially address but introduce extra router parameters. - **RadixAttention offset mismatch:** Position-dependent RoPE breaks prefix reuse when same content appears at different absolute positions (common in RAG/agent). Deferring RoPE requires rewriting all attention kernels (FlashAttention, FlashInfer) and conflicts with fused kernels; delta-rotation only works for MLA/partial-RoPE, not full RoPE. Our hybrid still has 15% accuracy drop on synthetic offset tests if not corrected. - **Tiered LSM write amplification:** LSM compaction causes write amplification 3–5×, wearing NVMe. Bloom filter false positives (1%) cause unnecessary SSD reads. For small KV blocks (16 tokens ~4KB), 4KB random read still inefficient vs 64KB+ batches. Prefetcher mispredicts on branching agent trajectories. - **Disaggregation provisioning brittleness:** Optimal P:D ratio depends on input/output length distribution, which drifts diurnally. Static provisioning either wastes GPUs or violates SLOs. Live migration of decode instances causes 100ms pause (KV copy). Network partition between P and D pools causes head-of-line blocking; need fallback to aggregated mode. - **2T MoE training cost:** While inference FLOPs constant, training 2T MoE still requires 2048 accelerators for 4 days [2], with AllToAll communication O(√D) becoming bottleneck beyond 4096 devices. Expert specialization remains syntactic rather than semantic [4], with experts clustering by token position not topic, suggesting routing learns surface patterns. - **Evaluation gap:** Our 8×H100 setup cannot fully test 2T model (requires 32×H100 with expert parallelism). Simulated throughput via analytical model may underestimate communication overhead from expert-parallel AllToAll (measured 28% cycles on 36L model, 34% on 60L with rematerialization [2]). --- ## 7 Conclusion We have presented a unified systems view of trillion-scale MoE serving, bridging routing theory, KV-cache data structures, and disaggregated scheduling.
