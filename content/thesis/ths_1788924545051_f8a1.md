# Stateless Ethereum Clients via Verkle Trees: Vector Commitment Constructions, Witness Size Economics, and State Expiry with Regenesis under EIP-6800

## Abstract

---

## 1 Introduction

Ethereum's state is large — hundreds of gigabytes and growing monotonically — and its authentication primitive, the *hexary Merkle–Patricia trie* (MPT), was designed in an era when every validating node was assumed to hold the entire state. Under the MPT, proving a single key requires revealing every sibling node along the path from leaf to root; a block touching thousands of state keys therefore carries a *witness* measured in megabytes. EIP-6800 quantifies the pathology precisely: an average-case single-account witness near 3 kB, a worst case three times larger, and — at a worst-case 6,000 accesses per block — a ~18 MB block witness that cannot be safely propagated through the peer-to-peer network inside a 12-second slot [4].

A *stateless client* verifies blocks given only the block header, the transactions, and a witness supplied by a state-providing peer; it never stores state itself. This is the cornerstone of *The Verge* in Ethereum's roadmap [11] and of the broader "purge" agenda against node hardware centralization. But stateless verification is only feasible if witnesses are small. The Merkle–Patricia trie is structurally incapable of small witnesses, because its authentication cost is dominated by sibling disclosure: with branching factor $k = 16$ and tree height $\log_{16} N$, each of the $\log_k N$ levels forces the disclosure of $k - 1$ sibling hashes.

*Verkle trees* break this coupling. Replacing hash-based node digests with *vector commitments* [2] — commitments to the vector of child commitments, with constant-size or logarithmic opening proofs — removes the need to disclose siblings entirely. The resulting witness for $T$ accesses shrinks from

$$O(T \cdot B \cdot k \cdot \log_k N)$$

to roughly $O(T \log_k N)$ plus a single constant-size aggregated proof [8]. With $k = 256$, the depth halves; with commitment-based openings, the sibling terms vanish. This thesis develops that claim from first principles, examines the concrete Ethereum instantiation of EIP-6800 (Bandersnatch curve, Banderwagon group, IPA multiproofs, 31-byte stems), and extends the analysis to the second half of the stateless agenda: *state expiry with regenesis*, which addresses not the cost of proving state but the cost of keeping it.

> **Theorem (informal):** Under the discrete-logarithm assumption in the Banderwagon group, a Verkle multiproof over the EIP-6800 state tree is *binding* and *position-binding*: an adversary cannot produce an accepting proof for a value different from the committed one except with negligible probability, and proofs for $T$ distinct keys aggregate into a single proof whose size is independent of $T$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Merkle–Patricia trie and its witness pathology

Ethereum's current state tree is a hexary ($k = 16$) Merkle–Patricia trie keyed by Keccak-256 hashes of addresses and storage slots. Each branch node holds up to 16 child hashes plus a value; path compression (extension nodes) shortens paths through sparse regions. For a state of $N$ accounts, depth is $\log_{16} N \approx 6$–$7$ at current scale [4]. A Merkle proof for one key discloses, at each level, the $15$ sibling hashes — roughly $15 \times 32\,\text{B} \times 7 \approx 3.4\,\text{kB}$ before compression, matching EIP-6800's ~3 kB average [4].

Multiproofs (e.g., the SSZ multiproof scheme studied for EIP-4762) share nodes along common path prefixes, which helps in the average case: a full block witness under EIP-4762 is estimated at 2–4 MB at ~200M accounts [6]. But the *worst case* remains adversarial — a crafted transaction set can touch keys with disjoint prefixes, and consensus must provision for the worst case. Witness size must fit in gossip, which makes worst-case bandwidth, not average, the binding constraint.

### 2.2 Vector commitments

Catalano and Fiore [2] formalized *vector commitments*: a primitive that commits to an ordered vector $\mathbf{v} = (v_0, \dots, v_{q-1})$ with a single short commitment $C$, and later opens position $i$ with a proof $\pi_i$ such that verification takes $C$, $i$, $v_i$, $\pi_i$ and accepts only if $v_i$ is truly the $i$-th component. Crucially, the opening proof does *not* disclose the other components — the sibling-disclosure problem disappears by construction.

Kate, Zaverucha, and Goldberg [5] instantiated vector commitments as *KZG polynomial commitments* over bilinear pairings — constant-size openings at the price of a structured trusted setup. Bünz, Fisch, and Szepieniec [1] replaced the pairing with an *inner-product argument* (IPA): logarithmic proofs, but a *transparent* setup with no trusted ceremony. This tradeoff — slightly larger proofs, no toxic waste — is exactly the one Ethereum chose.

### 2.3 Verkle trees

Kuszmaul [7] observed that replacing every Merkle hash node with a vector commitment yields a *Verkle tree*: a $k$-ary authenticated tree whose proof size is $O(\log_k n)$ group elements rather than $O(k \log_k n)$ hashes. Dankrad Feist then showed that a *single* IPA multiproof can cover *all* state accesses in a block — one logarithmic-size proof amortized over thousands of keys, contributing roughly ~576 bytes of fixed overhead [6]. The Ethereum research community, led by Buterin, Feist, Ballet, and others, consolidated this into EIP-6800 [4].

> **Definition (Verkle tree):** A Verkle tree of arity $k$ over leaves $L$ is a rooted tree in which each internal node $u$ stores $C_u = \mathsf{Commit}(C_{c_0}, \dots, C_{c_{k-1}})$, the vector commitment to its children's commitments. A proof for leaf $\ell$ consists of the commitments along the root-to-leaf path plus one aggregated opening proof; its size is $O(\log_k n)$ and independent of $k$.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The EIP-6800 construction

EIP-6800 does not replace the MPT in one step. It introduces a *unified Verkle tree alongside* the existing hexary trie: after the fork, the Verkle tree stores all *edits* to state and a *copy of all accessed state*, while the hexary trie becomes immutable and is eventually discarded [4]. This overlay strategy bounds migration risk — consensus can fall back to the frozen trie during the transition.

### 3.2 Multiproof aggregation

The block witness in the Verkle regime has three components:

1. **Stem commitments and values.** For each accessed stem, the 256-value commitment plus the accessed values themselves. Values are data, not proofs — they must be transmitted regardless of the scheme.
2. **Path commitments.** Commitments along each stem's path to the root (depth $\approx 4$, 48 bytes each in compressed Banderwagon form).
3. **One IPA multiproof.** All opening claims — "value $v$ sits at position $p$ inside commitment $C$" for every accessed $(C, p)$ — are folded into a *single* inner-product argument of ~576 bytes, via the standard random-linear-combination batching of Feist [6].

Because component 3 is shared, the marginal witness cost of one more access is essentially the stem's values plus a few dozen bytes of path commitments — EIP-6800's ~200 bytes per account in the average case [4].

### 3.3 Stateless verification model

A stateless client holds only recent block headers. To verify block $B$, a *witness provider* (a full node) ships $(B, \text{witness})$; the client recomputes the post-state root from the pre-state root, the transactions, and the witness, checking the IPA multiproof and recomputing commitments bottom-up. The security reduction is: *if the IPA is sound and the pre-state root is correct, the recomputed post-state root equals the true one.* No state database is consulted at any point.

### 3.4 Evaluation basis

Our claims rest on three legs: (i) the asymptotic witness formulas derived in §5.1; (ii) EIP-6800's calibrated estimates [4]; (iii) independent benchmarks — Oberst et al.'s Verkle-vs-SNARK comparison [8], Kaustinen testnet exercises, and Mana's EIP-6800 implementation notes [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 From hash nodes to commitment nodes: why siblings vanish

In a Merkle tree, the parent digest $H(c_0 \Vert \cdots \Vert c_{k-1})$ is *opaque*: to recompute it you need every $c_i$. Verification of one leaf therefore forces disclosure of all $k-1$ siblings per level. A vector commitment inverts this: $C = \sum_i v_i G_i$ (Pedersen-style) or $C = g^{f(\omega^i)}$-style polynomial evaluation commits to the whole vector while *position-binding* each component individually [2][5]. Opening position $i$ proves "$v_i$ is the $i$-th component" without saying anything about the rest. The witness cost per level drops from $\Theta(k)$ hashes to $\Theta(1)$ group elements plus an amortized share of one logarithmic proof. This is not an optimization of Merkle trees — it is a different authentication primitive with a different cost model, and the $k$-dependence of witness size, the root of the MPT pathology, is excised rather than mitigated.

### 4.2 The IPA multiproof: one proof for the whole block

The inner-product argument of Bünz et al. [1] proves knowledge of vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$ with $\langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b} \rangle = c$ and $C = \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{G} \rangle$ in $O(\log n)$ communication. Feist's multiproof construction [6] observes that the many opening claims in a block witness are all of the form "commitment $C_j$ opens to $y_j$ at evaluation point $x_j$"; taking a verifier-supplied random linear combination $\sum_j r^j \cdot \text{claim}_j$ folds them into *one* IPA instance. The proof is ~576 bytes *regardless of how many claims it batches*. Concretely:

```python
# Verkle block-witness verification (stateless client)
def verify_block(pre_root, txs, witness):
  claimed = {s: v for s, v in zip(witness.stems, witness.values)}
  assert ipa_verify(witness.ipa_multiproof, witness.opening_claims) # one proof, all claims
  root = recompute_root(witness.stems, claimed, witness.path_commitments)
  assert root == pre_root
  return execute(txs, claimed)                    # post-state root
```

Step 1 is the only cryptographic work beyond hashing; its cost is logarithmic in the total number of committed values, not linear in accesses — the amortization that collapses marginal cost to ~200 bytes per account [4].

### 4.3 EIP-6800 tree keys and the migration overlay

The tree-key scheme deserves close attention because it is where consensus-critical detail lives. For an address `addr`:

| Tree key bytes | Content |
|---|---|
| `0x00 \|\| addr[0..30]` | stem for basic data (nonce, balance) |
| suffix `0x00` / `0x01` | version / balance / nonce split |
| `0x01 \|\| addr[0..30]`, suffix `0x00` | code hash |
| `0x02 \|\| addr[0..30]`, suffix `0x00` | code size |
| `0x02..0x03 \|\| addr \|\| slot[0..30]` | 256 storage slots per stem group |

The *version byte* is the forward-compatibility hook: future state-management regimes (expiry epochs, regenesis) can introduce version `0x04+` keys without disturbing existing layout [4]. Migration proceeds by *copy-on-access*: any MPT state touched after the fork is copied into the Verkle tree; untouched state remains provable only via the frozen MPT until a dedicated conversion pass migrates it. This two-phase design — overlay first, conversion later — is why EIP-6800 is described as "a first step in a multi-phase transition" [4].

### 4.4 State expiry and regenesis: the other half of statelessness

Verkle trees solve the cost of *proving* state. *State expiry* solves the cost of *keeping* it. Ethereum's state grows monotonically: every SSTORE allocates storage that full nodes must retain forever, and rent-based schemes have repeatedly failed to achieve consensus. Expiry proposals divide chain history into *address periods* (e.g., ~1 year); state not touched during a period *expires* — nodes may drop it — and is resurrected on demand when a transaction provides a *witness* (a proof of its last active-period value) [11][12].

Regenesis sharpens the idea: at period boundaries, an "empty" genesis-like state root commits to nothing, and all continued state must be explicitly resurrected. The interaction with Verkle trees is subtle and favorable. First, **resurrection witnesses are multiproof-shaped**: reviving an expired account requires proving its value at the last period's state root — exactly the kind of opening claim the IPA multiproof batches, so expiry needs no separate proof system. Second, **expiry bounds tree width, not depth**: expired keys simply stop being opened, and the version byte in EIP-6800 tree keys (§4.3) can encode the period of last activity, distinguishing live keys from resurrectable ones without extra metadata [4]. Third, **witness availability becomes the liveness assumption**: expired state must remain retrievable from *somewhere* (archive nodes, Portal Network, or the resurrecting user) — strictly weaker than every validator storing everything, and composable with stateless verification, since a stateless client never stored the state anyway.

Buterin's "Purge" writings are candid that state expiry is "much harder than history expiry, because the EVM is fundamentally designed around an assumption that once a state object is created, it will always be there" [12]. Partial expiry — expiring only *storage slots* while keeping account headers, or the "partial state expiry" variant — reduces the semantic shock at the cost of weaker storage savings. Current discourse (late 2025) leans toward *partial nodes* as the near-term mechanism, with protocol-level expiry deferred until Verkle/stateless infrastructure matures [13].

### 4.5 Curve choice: Bandersnatch, Banderwagon, and the quantum shadow

KZG over BLS12-381 needs a powers-of-tau ceremony per circuit size — an operational and political cost the protocol preferred to avoid — so EIP-6800 uses the IPA over Banderwagon, the prime-order subgroup of the Bandersnatch curve, which also eliminates cofactor attacks [3][6]. The price is logarithmic rather than constant proof size.

The deeper shadow is *quantum*. Both IPA and KZG rest on the discrete logarithm assumption; a cryptographically relevant quantum computer breaks the binding of every Verkle commitment, silently and retroactively — unlike hash-based Merkle trees, which degrade more gracefully (Grover only halves preimage security). This is the principled objection that, by mid-2024, reignited core-developer interest in *binary Merkle trees with hash-based commitments* (EIP-7864), where SNARKs compress witnesses instead of vector commitments [9]. Verkle remains the specified path in EIP-6800, but any honest treatment must note that the roadmap's center of gravity has partially shifted.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Witness-size asymptotics

Let $T$ be state accesses per block, $B$ the node size in bytes, $k$ the arity, $N$ the number of accounts. The naive Merkle multiproof bound [8]:

$$W_{\text{MPT}}(T) = O(T \cdot B \cdot k \cdot \log_k N)$$

For the hexary trie ($k = 16$, $B = 32$): $\log_{16}(2 \times 10^8) \approx 6.9$, giving $\approx T \times 32 \times 16 \times 6.9 \approx 3.5\,\text{kB}$ per access before prefix-sharing — consistent with the ~3 kB average and ~18 MB worst-case block witness in [4]. The Verkle bound:

$$W_{\text{Verkle}}(T) = O(T \cdot \log_k N) + O(\log n_{\text{total}})$$

with $k = 256$: $\log_{256}(2 \times 10^8) \approx 3.4$ levels, each contributing one 48-byte compressed commitment, plus the amortized ~576-byte IPA proof. Per-access marginal cost: values (~100–200 B for account data) + ~$3.4 \times 48 \approx 163$ B of path commitments + negligible proof share — the ~200 bytes/account of [4]. The improvement factor is $\approx 15\times$ per access in the average case and far larger in the adversarial worst case, because prefix-disjoint accesses no longer multiply sibling disclosure.

| Metric | Hexary MPT | Verkle (EIP-6800) | Factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Witness / account (avg) | ~3 kB | ~200 B | ~15× |
| Worst-case block witness | ~18 MB | < 1 MB (est.) | >18× |
| Proof aggregation | none (multiproof only) | 1 IPA multiproof / block (~576 B) | — |
| Trusted setup | none | none (transparent IPA) | — |
| Tree arity | 16 | 256 | 16× |
| Depth at 200M accounts | ~7 | ~4 | ~1.75× |

### 5.2 Benchmark evidence

Oberst et al. [8] benchmarked Verkle trees against the SNARK-based binary-tree alternative and found Verkle "more practical for proving and verification latency" — the IPA prover handles block-scale multiproofs in tens of milliseconds. The Kaustinen testnet exercised the EIP-6800 migration path (copy-on-access, witness gossip, stateless block verification) and validated sub-megabyte block witnesses; client teams (Geth, Nethermind, Besu, Erigon, Mana [10]) converged on the Banderwagon/IPA parameters. Fractional-Verkle work [6] corroborates the core numbers: ~576-byte multiproof overhead and 2–4 MB MPT witnesses under EIP-4762.

### 5.3 Proof sketch: multiproof soundness

*Claim.* If the IPA is knowledge-sound for "commitment $C$ opens to $y$ at point $x$", the batched multiproof is knowledge-sound for the conjunction of all block opening claims.

*Sketch.* The verifier samples $r \gets \mathbb{F}$ (Fiat–Shamir) and both parties form $\tilde{C} = \sum_j r^j C_j$, $\tilde{y} = \sum_j r^j y_j$. By Schwartz–Zippel, a false individual claim makes the combined claim false except with probability $\le d/|\mathbb{F}|$; IPA soundness bounds the rest negligibly. Position-binding of the Pedersen vector commitment [2] lifts claim-soundness to tree-soundness by induction on depth. ∎

## 7 Conclusion

Verkle trees reframe Ethereum's state problem from *hash disclosure* to *commitment opening*, and the quantitative payoff — ~200 bytes per account versus ~3 kB, one 576-byte proof per block, no trusted setup — is what makes stateless clients physically possible within a 12-second slot [4][6][8]. EIP-6800's overlay design, with its versioned tree keys and copy-on-access migration, shows how to land this cryptography in a live $100B+ consensus system without a flag day [4]. State expiry with regenesis is the natural complement: once witnesses are cheap, *not storing* cold state becomes viable, with resurrection carried by the same multiproof machinery — though the semantic and incentive questions around expiry remain the least-settled part of the agenda [11][12][13].

The honest coda is that Verkle's future is no longer uncontested. Quantum vulnerability has pushed binary hash-based trees (EIP-7864) back into serious contention [9], and "lean Ethereum" discourse treats the state tree as one proving-bottleneck component among several . Whether mainnet state ends up committed by IPA over Banderwagon or Poseidon-hashed binary trees, the intellectual core of this thesis survives: *vector commitments, or their SNARK-compressed equivalents, are what decouple witness size from tree arity* — and that decoupling is the necessary condition for Ethereum to verify without storing.
