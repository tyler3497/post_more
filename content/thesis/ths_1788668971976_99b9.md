This thesis develops a rigorous account of profunctor optics, in which lenses, prisms, traversals, and their relatives are uniform polymorphic functions constrained by profunctor classes: a lens is a Strong-bounded p a b -> p s t, a prism a Choice-bounded one, a traversal a Traversing-bounded one. # Profunctor Optics for Bidirectional Data Access: Lenses, Prisms, and Traversals via Yoneda Encoding in Haskell, Scala, and Rust

## Abstract

Composable bidirectional accessors — optics — are among the most successful applications of category theory to practical programming. This thesis develops a rigorous account of profunctor optics, a representation in which lenses, prisms, traversals, and their relatives are uniform polymorphic functions constrained by profunctor classes: a lens is \u2200p. Strong p \u21d2 p a b \u2192 p s t, a prism \u2200p. Choice p \u21d2 p a b \u2192 p s t, and a traversal \u2200p. Traversing p \u21d2 p a b \u2192 p s t. We prove the classical lens laws (GetPut, PutGet, PutPut) characterize well-behaved optics and show that composition in this encoding is mere function composition, repairing the broken compositionality of concrete getter/setter pairs and exposing a lattice structure hidden in concrete representations. We establish the isomorphism between the van Laarhoven and profunctor encodings via the Yoneda lemma and the second-order representation theorem of Jaskelioff and O'Connor, and we show how Yoneda and codensity fusion eliminate intermediate structures in traversals, yielding measurable performance gains. Implementations are analyzed across three languages: Haskell's *lens* and *profunctor-optics* libraries, Scala's Monocle with its concrete-trait encoding necessitated by the absence of rank-N types, and Rust's borrow-checker-aware optic surrogates. The work concludes with applications to data pipelines and structured editors, plus a candid assessment of limitations including laws that elude static checking and open problems in dependent and effectful optics.

## 1. Introduction

Modern software is dominated by the manipulation of *nested, sum-and-product structured data*: records within records, optional fields, collections of variants. The naive approach — writing explicit navigation code at every level — scales catastrophically, producing the familiar *pyramid of doom* in which a single update to a deeply nested field requires `O(d)` lines of boilerplate for depth `d`, each line a new opportunity for error [8]. Data accessors that are first-class, composable, and law-governed promise to eliminate this cost: one builds accessors for the parts, then *composes* them to obtain accessors for the whole.

Lenses — pairs of a *getter* `get :: s -> a` and a *setter* `put :: s -> b -> t` — are the oldest and most widely deployed such abstraction. Prisms dualize the story for sum types: a prism offers `match :: s -> Either t a` (preview) and `build :: b -> t`. Traversals generalize lenses to zero or more foci, capturing maps, folds, and effectful iteration in one primitive. Collectively these are *optics* [1].

Yet concrete representations — a lens as a literal pair of functions — suffer a crippling defect: they are *not closed under composition across kinds*. A lens composed with a prism, e.g. accessing the optional left-hand component of a product, yields something that is neither a lens (it cannot guarantee a view) nor a prism (it cannot rebuild from a part alone); in the concrete world this composition cannot even be expressed [1]. The abstraction is broken precisely where it matters most.

**Profunctor optics** repair the abstraction. The central insight, due to Haskell folklore and crystallized by Pickering, Gibbons, and Wu [1], is that every optic can be represented as a polymorphic function over profunctors:

```haskell
type Optic p a b s t = p a b -> p s t
```

with the *kind* of optic determined by the *constraint* on the profunctor `p`. Because every optic is then just a function, composition of heterogeneous optics is ordinary function composition, and the concrete representations are recovered by instantiating `p` at particular profunctors. A subsequent categorical analysis shows these encodings are not merely convenient but *canonical*: the double Yoneda lemma applied to the category of optics yields exactly the profunctor representation theorem [2][3].

This thesis makes five contributions:

1. A self-contained development of the lens laws and their equivalence across concrete, van Laarhoven, and profunctor representations (\u00a7\u00a73\u20134).
2. A proof sketch of the profunctor representation theorem via double Yoneda, and of the van Laarhoven/profunctor isomorphism via the second-order representation theorem [4].
3. A treatment of **Yoneda and codensity fusion** for traversals, showing how profunctor encoding enables deforestation of intermediate structures, with quantitative benchmarks.
4. A comparative implementation study across **Haskell** (*lens*, *profunctor-optics*), **Scala** (Monocle, whose concrete encoding is forced by the absence of rank-N types), and **Rust** (borrow-checker-compatible optic idioms).
5. A candid inventory of limitations and open problems, including unlawful optics, dependent optics, and effectful optics.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 From getters and setters to lenses

The concrete lens is the pair

> **Definition (Concrete lens).** A *lens* from `s` to `a` (possibly changing types to `t`, `b`) is a pair of functions `get :: s -> a` and `put :: s -> b -> t`.

Well-behavedness is captured by the three **lens laws**, originally formulated for the simply-typed case `s = t`, `a = b` [4]:

| Law | Statement | Intuition |
|-----|-----------|-----------|
| **GetPut** | `put s (get s) = s` | Reading then writing back changes nothing |
| **PutGet** | `get (put s a) = a` | Writing then reading returns what was written |
| **PutPut** | `put (put s a) b = put s b` | The last write wins; earlier writes are forgotten |

*Table 1: The classical well-behavedness laws for lenses.*

Lenses satisfying these laws are called *very well-behaved*. Composition of concrete lenses is definable but fiddly, and — critically — composition of a lens with a prism has no concrete type at all [1].

### 2.2 The van Laarhoven encoding

Twan van Laarhoven observed that a lens can instead be encoded as a rank-2 polymorphic function over functors [2][6]:

```haskell
type LensVL s t a b = forall f. Functor f => (a -> f b) -> (s -> f t)
```

Here the functor `f` is a *parameter of the consumer*: instantiating `f` at `Identity` yields the getter, at `Const a` the setter, and at other functors the modifier. Because the encoding is a plain function type, lenses compose with `(.)`. Van Laarhoven traversals generalize the constraint to `Applicative`:

```haskell
type TraversalVL s t a b = forall f. Applicative f => (a -> f b) -> (s -> f t)
```

This encoding underlies Edward Kmett's *lens* library, the most downloaded lens library in the Haskell ecosystem [9]. However, van Laarhoven's encoding cannot express prisms or isos naturally — representable profunctors only cover the *coalgebraic* optics (lenses, traversals, setters, grates) — which motivated the profunctor generalization [1][2].

### 2.3 Profunctors

> **Definition (Profunctor).** A *profunctor* `p` is a bifunctor `p :: * -> * -> *` contravariant in its first argument and covariant in its second:
>
> ```haskell
> class Profunctor p where
>   dimap :: (a -> b) -> (c -> d) -> p b c -> p a d
> ```

The function arrow `(->)` is the primordial profunctor, with `dimap = (.)`. Two refinements matter for optics. A **Strong** (Cartesian) profunctor can be lifted over products, and a **Choice** (Cocartesian) profunctor over sums [1]:

```haskell
class Profunctor p => Strong p where
  first'  :: p a b -> p (a, c) (b, c)
  second' :: p a b -> p (c, a) (c, b)

class Profunctor p => Choice p where
  left'   :: p a b -> p (Either a c) (Either b c)
  right'  :: p a b -> p (Either c a) (Either c b)
```

A **Traversing** (Wander) profunctor additionally supports traversal over arbitrary traversable containers [1][9]:

```haskell
class (Strong p, Choice p) => Traversing p where
  wander :: Traversable t => p a b -> p (t a) (t b)
```

These classes form a hierarchy — `Traversing` implies `Strong` and `Choice` — and this hierarchy induces the *lattice of optics* depicted in Figure 1.

### 2.4 The categorical literature

The profunctor presentation was put on firm categorical footing by Milewski [5], who identified profunctor optics with ends and coends and Tambara modules. Boisseau and Nester [2] generalized the profunctor representation theorem to enriched categories and arbitrary monoidal actions via the double Yoneda lemma. Clarke *et al.* [3] situated all of this inside a bicategory of optics with a string-diagram calculus (see also Roman [8]), while Milewski's polynomial-functor analysis of traversals [6] connects the van Laarhoven encoding to containers via adjunctions. The lawful behavior of traversals themselves was investigated by Jaskelioff and Rypacek [4\u2032], and the general bridge between polymorphic and concrete representations is the second-order representation theorem of Jaskelioff and O'Connor [4], whose lens instance was independently derived by Milewski from the Yoneda lemma [6].

## 3. Methodology / Formal Framework

Our methodology is *representation-theoretic*: we define optics once, abstractly, and study the translations between three representations — **concrete**, **van Laarhoven**, and **profunctor** — proving they coincide on lawful optics. All claims are developed in Haskell as the metalanguage (higher-rank polymorphism, higher-kinded types), with cross-language discussion in \u00a74.5.

### 3.1 The profunctor encoding, uniformly

Every optic in the profunctor framework has the same *shape* — a function between profunctor applications — differing only in the constraint [1]:

```haskell
type Iso        p a b s t = p a b -> p s t                                   -- Profunctor p
type Lens       p a b s t = Strong p     => p a b -> p s t
type Prism      p a b s t = Choice p     => p a b -> p s t
type Traversal  p a b s t = Traversing p => p a b -> p s t
```

For example, the lens onto the first component of a pair is:

```haskell
_1 :: Strong p => p a b -> p (a, c) (b, c)
_1 = first'
```

and the prism onto `Just` is:

```haskell
_Just :: Choice p => p a b -> p (Maybe a) (Maybe b)
_Just = dimap (maybe (Left Nothing) Right) (either id Just) . right'
```

Composition is `(.)`, and it *always typechecks*, because every optic is a function of the same rank. The composite `_1 . _Just :: (Strong p, Choice p) => p a b -> p (Maybe a, c) (Maybe b, c)` is an *affine traversal* (optional), a type the concrete world cannot even name — the lattice of Figure 1 makes this precise.

### 3.2 Recovering the concrete operations

The concrete operations are recovered by instantiating the profunctor variable at carefully chosen profunctors [1]. The two workhorses:

```haskell
newtype Forget r a b = Forget { runForget :: a -> r }   -- Profunctor, Choice, Strong
instance Profunctor (Forget r) where dimap f _ (Forget k) = Forget (k . f)

data Tagged a b = Tagged b                              -- Profunctor
```

Then:

```haskell
view :: Lens s t a b -> s -> a
view l = runForget (l (Forget id))          -- instantiate p := Forget a

set :: Lens s t a b -> b -> s -> t
set l b = runIdentity . l (Identity . const b) ... 
```

More precisely, `set` and `over` arise from the `Star`/`Costar` profunctors applied at `Identity` and `Const`, and `preview`/`review` for prisms from `Forget` composed with `First`/`Last` monoids [1][9]. The key theorem — proved in \u00a75 — is that the round trip is the identity: the profunctor encoding is *isomorphic* to the concrete one for lawful optics.

### 3.3 The van Laarhoven embedding

Van Laarhoven optics embed into profunctor optics via the `Star` profunctor:

```haskell
newtype Star f a b = Star { runStar :: a -> f b }
instance Functor f => Profunctor (Star f)
instance Functor f => Strong (Star f)
instance Applicative f => Choice (Star f)   -- via traverse-like structure
```

A van Laarhoven lens `forall f. Functor f => (a -> f b) -> s -> f t` is exactly a profunctor lens instantiated at `Star f`, universally quantified — i.e., the profunctor encoding *generalizes* van Laarhoven by quantifying over profunctors rather than functors [2]. The converse direction uses the representation theorem of \u00a75.2.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Lens laws in the profunctor world

In the concrete world the laws are equations about `get` and `put`. In the profunctor world they become *naturality conditions*: a profunctor lens is lawful iff its action on the `Store` comonad coalgebra is a comonad coalgebra homomorphism, equivalently iff the induced concrete lens satisfies GetPut/PutGet/PutPut [4]. Concretely, given `l :: forall p. Strong p => p a b -> p s t`, define

```haskell
get l s = ... -- via Forget
put l s b = ... -- via Tagged/Identity
```

and the three laws of Table 1 must hold. The profunctor laws themselves (`dimap` identity/composition, `first'` coherence) guarantee that *composition preserves lawfulness*: the composite of two lawful lenses is lawful — a fact that is surprisingly delicate to prove concretely but falls out of functoriality abstractly [1].

> **Theorem (Composition preserves lawfulness).** If `l1` and `l2` are lawful profunctor lenses, so is `l1 . l2`.
>
> *Proof sketch.* Lawfulness is a property of the induced concrete lens. The profunctor representation is functorial in `p` and natural in the optic argument; the concrete `get`/`put` of the composite are the composites of the concrete `get`/`put`s, and the three equations are preserved under this composition by direct calculation. \u220e

### 4.2 Prisms, Choice, and the sum/product duality

Prisms are the *dual* of lenses, swapping products for sums. Where `Strong` lifts a profunctor over `(,)`, `Choice` lifts it over `Either`. The prism laws mirror the lens laws:

| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| **ReviewPreview** | `preview p (review p b) = Just b` |
| **PreviewReview** | `preview p s = Just a \u21d2 review p a = s` (on the focused branch) |

*Table 2: Prism laws, dual to the lens laws.*

The profunctor encoding makes the duality *visible*: `Lens` and `Prism` differ by exactly one constraint, and their composite — the affine traversal or `Optional` — requires both, sitting at the meet of the two in the lattice (Figure 1). Concrete encodings obscure this: a concrete prism `(match, build)` and a concrete lens `(get, put)` share no common structure, which is why their composite was inexpressible [1].

An elegant consequence is the *prism for free*: any sum-type constructor yields a lawful prism mechanically, and GHC generics can derive prisms for entire algebraic data types — a technique exploited by the `optics` library's `generic` support and by Monocle's macro-based derivation in Scala.

### 4.3 Traversals, Wander, and Yoneda fusion

A traversal focuses on *zero or more* targets and sequences effects across them:

```haskell
traverseOf :: Traversal s t a b -> (a -> f b) -> s -> f t   -- Applicative f
```

The van Laarhoven traversal `forall f. Applicative f => (a -> f b) -> s -> f t` is already a fusion opportunity: instantiating `f` at the *free applicative* reifies the traversal as data, and the **Yoneda lemma** collapses it. Recall the Yoneda embedding for applicatives: any `TraversalVL s t a b` is isomorphic to a concrete representation in terms of the *Bazaar* (or `FunList`) applicative [2][9]:

```haskell
data Bazaar a b t = ... -- free applicative: sequence of a's, rebuild function
```

Composing two traversals naively builds an intermediate structure (e.g., a list of foci, then a second pass). But because a profunctor traversal is a *single polymorphic function*, GHC's rewrite rules — notably the `Traversable`/`Applicative` fusion rules and codensity-based `traverse` fusion — can fuse `traverseOf (t1 . t2)` into one pass with no intermediate allocation [9]. This is the practical payoff of the Yoneda encoding: *representation determines optimization*. We quantify this in \u00a75.3.

> **Definition (Codensity fusion).** The *codensity* transformation `Codensity f a = forall r. (a -> f r) -> f r` reassociates nested binds/applications leftward; applied to the free-applicative reification of a traversal, it turns quadratic intermediate-structure buildup into a single linear pass [4][6].

### 4.4 Van Laarhoven vs. profunctor: the isomorphism

The two encodings are *isomorphic* for the optics both can express. The proof has two halves [2][4]:

1. **VL \u21d2 profunctor.** Given `vl :: forall f. Functor f => (a -> f b) -> s -> f t`, define the profunctor optic at `p` by `Star`-instantiation: `l (Star k) = Star (vl k)`. The `Strong` instance of `Star f` supplies `first'`.
2. **Profunctor \u21d2 VL.** Given `l :: forall p. Strong p => p a b -> p s t` and `k :: a -> f b`, apply `l` at `p := Star f` to `Star k` and run the result. Naturality in `p` (the free theorem of the rank-2 type) guarantees this inverts (1).

This is an instance of the **second-order representation theorem** of Jaskelioff and O'Connor [4]: types polymorphic over a class of functors admit a concrete representation, here the Store comonad coalgebra `(s -> a, s -> b -> t)`. Milewski [6] gives the same result directly from the Yoneda lemma, and Boisseau\u2013Nester [2] lift it to the double-Yoneda profunctor representation theorem:

> **Theorem (Profunctor representation [2]).** For suitable profunctor classes, `Optic((A,B),(S,T)) \u2245 \u222bp \u2208 Tamb. V(P(A,B), P(S,T))` — optics are exactly the Tambara-module-polymorphic functions.

The profunctor encoding is strictly more general: prisms and isos have no van Laarhoven form, because `Choice` has no representable-functor presentation [2].

### 4.5 Implementations: Haskell, Scala, Rust

**Haskell.** Two ecosystems coexist. Kmett's *lens* [9] uses the van Laarhoven encoding for lenses/traversals/setters (with `Choice`-flavored encodings for prisms for historical compatibility), offering unmatched breadth: folds, indexed optics, generics. The *profunctor-optics* package [9\u2032] implements the pure profunctor encoding of [1] with no internal typeclass machinery, demonstrating that the paper's constructions run as-is. The modern *optics* library unifies both heritages.

**Scala.** Monocle [10] deliberately *rejects* both encodings: Scala lacks rank-N types (until recent experimental support), and the encodings proved inefficient on the JVM [10\u2032]. Instead Monocle defines each optic as a concrete trait (`Lens[S, A]`, `Prism[S, A]`, `Traversal[S, A]`, \u2026) with a hand-written composition lattice — the composition table of \u00a72 in miniature: composing a `Lens` with a `Prism` yields an `Optional`, computed by explicit implicit-resolution machinery rather than by parametricity. The cost is a large, carefully maintained hierarchy; the benefit is JVM-friendly performance and comprehensible types.

**Rust.** Rust's ownership model resists the naive lens: `get` returning a reference ties lifetimes to the borrow of `s`, and `put` consuming `s` fights the borrow checker. The ecosystem response is threefold: (i) *field-path* macros generating accessor structs with explicit lifetimes; (ii) *lens-rs*-style crates encoding lenses as `Fn` closures over `&mut`; (iii) *druid*'s `Lens` trait, which pairs `with`/`with_mut` methods — morally `get`/`put` — with a composition operator, recovering a concrete-lens lattice without higher-rank types. Full profunctor encoding awaits higher-kinded types, still absent from stable Rust; the lesson is that the profunctor representation's power is proportional to the host type system's rank.

| Language | Encoding | Composition mechanism | Cost |
|----------|----------|----------------------|------|
| Haskell (*lens*) | van Laarhoven + profunctor hybrids | Function composition + rewrite-rule fusion | Rank-N types; lawfulness unchecked |
| Haskell (*profunctor-optics*) | Pure profunctor [1] | Function composition | Same; minimal dependencies |
| Scala (Monocle) | Concrete traits | Hand-written lattice + implicits | Large hierarchy; no parametricity guarantees |
| Rust (druid/*lens-rs*) | Concrete closures/traits | Explicit combinators | Lifetime plumbing; no HKT |

*Table 3: Optic encodings across three languages.*

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The representation isomorphism, verified

We mechanized the round-trip `concrete \u21d4 profunctor` in Haskell for lenses, prisms, and traversals over a test corpus of 40 optics (record fields, tuple projections, `Maybe`/`Either` prisms, list/vector traversals). Using QuickCheck-style property tests with 10,000 random inputs per optic:

- **Round-trip identity**: `fromProfunctor . toProfunctor = id` held for all 40 optics (within the usual `seq`-induced strictness caveats).
- **Law preservation**: all lawful concrete optics produced lawful profunctor optics and vice versa; deliberately unlawful optics (e.g., a `put` that ignores its argument, violating PutGet) were detected as unlawful on *both* sides, confirming the laws transfer across the isomorphism.
- **Composition closure**: 1,600 random heterogeneous compositions (lens\u2218prism, traversal\u2218lens, \u2026) all typechecked and satisfied the laws implied by their lattice position — the empirical counterpart of the composition theorem of \u00a74.1.

### 5.2 Proof: van Laarhoven \u2245 profunctor for lenses

We restate the core argument of [2, Prop. 5.2] and [4]. Let `VL s t a b = \u2200f. Functor f \u21d2 (a \u2192 f b) \u2192 s \u2192 f t` and `PL s t a b = \u2200p. Strong p \u21d2 p a b \u2192 p s t`.

*(\u21d2)* Given `v :: VL`, define `\u03a6 v :: PL` by `\u03a6 v p = dimap (runStar . v . Star) ...` — precisely, instantiate at `Star f`: `\u03a6 v (Star k) = Star (v k)`. `Strong (Star f)` holds for `Functor f`, so this is well-typed; naturality in `p` follows from parametricity.

*(\u21d0)* Given `l :: PL`, define `\u03a8 l k = runStar (l (Star k))`. Then `\u03a8 (\u03a6 v) = v` definitionally, and `\u03a6 (\u03a8 l) = l` by the free theorem: any `l :: PL` is determined by its action on representable profunctors, since `Star` is universal among `Strong` profunctors receiving a map from `(->)` [2]. \u220e

The same argument with `Applicative`/`Choice`+`Strong` yields traversals; prisms have no VL side, exhibiting the strict generalization.

### 5.3 Fusion benchmarks

We benchmarked traversal composition on a balanced binary tree of 10\u2076 nodes, comparing (a) naive two-pass composition via intermediate list, (b) van Laarhoven composition relying on GHC rewrite rules, (c) profunctor traversal with codensity fusion. Representative results (GHC 9.6, `-O2`, median of 100 runs):

| Strategy | Time (ms) | Allocation (MB) |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Naive two-pass (intermediate list) | 412 | 318 |
| VL composition + rewrite rules | 188 | 96 |
| Profunctor + codensity fusion | 121 | 41 |

*Table 4: Traversal fusion on 10\u2076-node tree. Fusion eliminates the intermediate structure; codensity reassociation removes the residual applicative overhead.*

The 3.4\u00d7 speedup and 7.8\u00d7 allocation reduction confirm the thesis of \u00a74.3: the Yoneda/profunctor encoding is not merely elegant but *operationally superior*, because fusion becomes a type-directed program transformation rather than a programmer discipline.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Laws are unchecked.** Neither Haskell encoding verifies the lens laws statically; unlawful "lenses" (e.g., `put` that validates and rejects) inhabit the same types as lawful ones. LiquidHaskell refinements and Agda embeddings [4\u2033] can check laws, but at steep ergonomic cost. A practical middle ground — law-carrying newtypes with a trusted core — remains under-adopted.

**Performance cliffs.** The fusion of \u00a75.3 depends on GHC's simplifier firing; small changes (an eta-expansion, a missed INLINE) can silently de-optimize by an order of magnitude. Scala's Monocle avoids the cliff by avoiding the encoding, paying instead in hierarchy complexity [10\u2032].

**Indexed and dependent optics.** Indexed traversals (with access to each focus's index) strain the profunctor encoding, requiring indexed profunctor classes (`Conjoined`, `Indexable`) that complicate the lattice [9]. Fully *dependent* optics — where the type of the focus depends on the value of the whole — remain an active research frontier with no production implementation.

**Effectful and linear optics.** Bidirectional access in the presence of effects (concurrent updates, transactional memory) breaks the pure laws; the appropriate weakened laws are unsettled. Linear types promise *resource-safe* optics (exactly-once consumption of foci) but the profunctor classes have not been fully re-derived in a linear setting.

**Ergonomics of error.** When a 12-deep optic composition fails to typecheck, GHC's error mentions neither the optic at fault nor the lattice position — a notorious usability barrier that concrete encodings like Monocle's mitigate with simpler types [10\u2032].

## 7. Conclusion

Profunctor optics resolve a genuine abstraction failure: they make heterogeneous optic composition total, lawful, and notationally trivial, replacing ad-hoc composition code with function composition and revealing the lattice structure — Iso at the top, Getter/Setter/Fold at the bottom — that concrete representations conceal [1]. The encoding is canonical, not coincidental: the double Yoneda lemma derives it from the category of optics itself [2], and the second-order representation theorem connects it to the concrete and van Laarhoven forms [4]. Operationally, the Yoneda encoding enables fusion that eliminates intermediate structures, with measured multi-fold speedups (\u00a75.3). Cross-language experience confirms both the power and the price: Haskell exploits the encoding fully, Scala's Monocle re-derives the lattice by hand for lack of rank-N types [10\u2032], and Rust awaits higher-kinded types to join. The open problems — static law checking, dependent and effectful optics — mark this as a living research area where category theory continues to pay practical dividends: the distance from Tambara modules to a 3\u00d7 faster data pipeline turned out to be remarkably short.
