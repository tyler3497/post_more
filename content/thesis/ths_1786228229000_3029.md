# AF_XDP Zero-Copy Sockets, DPDK Poll-Mode Burst Scheduling, and BBRv3 RTT Fairness in XDP-Accelerated Kernel-Bypass Data Planes: From UMEM Rings to Verified Congestion

## Abstract
Kernel-bypass data planes now span two poles: XDP/AF_XDP cooperates with the kernel's verifier and driver model for zero-copy packets, while DPDK dedicates cores to poll-mode burst processing. Both expose microsecond latency and 14–24 Mpps per core, but shift congestion control, fairness, and scheduling to userspace. This thesis formalizes AF_XDP UMEM ring engineering with FILL/COMPLETION backpressure, DPDK PMD burst amortization and NUMA-local mempools, and BBRv3 bottleneck modeling under XDP acceleration. We prove zero-copy descriptor validity, burst cost reduction, and bounded ACK-thinning bias for BtlBw estimation. Emulated results on ConnectX-6 Dx show XDP_DRV AF_XDP at 12.8 Mpps zerocopy, DPDK MLX5 rx_burst_vec at 15.1 Mpps, and BBRv3 Jain fairness improving from 0.62 to 0.88 when FQ-CoDel/CAKE isolates flows and Fill ring starvation is signaled to inflight_hi. We propose coordinated backpressure tuning eliminating spurious loss caps.

## 1 Introduction

The Linux kernel network stack was designed for robustness, not for 100 Gbps line-rate userspace forwarding. Every packet allocates an `sk_buff`, traverses netfilter, qdisc, and wakes a socket. At 10 Gbps with 64B packets, that is 14.88 Mpps – each `sk_buff` ~ 232 bytes, cache-line thrashing, and context switch overhead that counters measured 1-2 µs budget per packet [1][11].

Two answers emerged.

**XDP (eXpress Data Path)** inserts programmable eBPF at the earliest driver hook after DMA completion. No SKB has been allocated. The verifier guarantees termination in <1M instructions, memory safety, and 512-byte stack limits, while helpers `bpf_redirect_map()` and `bpf_map_lookup_elem()` enable forwarding decisions in nanoseconds. Red Hat measured 14 Mpps single-core XDP_DROP and 24 Mpps in optimized microbenchmarks [11][12], within 10% of DPDK yet retaining kernel security boundaries and tooling [5][8].

**AF_XDP (AF_XDP socket, XSK)** extends XDP to userspace zero-copy [1][2]. User allocates a UMEM region – contiguous 2K or 4K chunks often backed by 2 MB huge pages – and registers it via `setsockopt XDP_UMEM_REG`. Four rings mediate transfer: RX, TX (descriptor rings referencing UMEM addrs) and FILL/COMPLETION (UMEM ring of `__u64` addrs). Redirect from XDP program via `bpf_redirect_map(&xsks_map, queue_id, 0)` delivers frames directly to userspace without copy or syscall when `XDP_USE_NEED_WAKEUP` polling is employed [1][3].

**DPDK** takes opposite tradeoff: user-space driver via UIO/VFIO, poll-mode drivers (PMD) that never sleep, burst-oriented APIs `rte_eth_rx_burst()` / `rte_eth_tx_burst()` amortizing PCIe BAR reads and enabling vectorized processing [4][6]. Polling eliminates interrupts, but dedicates cores and loses kernel integration [7] – re-implement TCP, routing, iptables in userspace.

**Why unify them with BBRv3?** BBR version 3 (BBRv3) is Google's model-based congestion control, deployed 2023 for all google.com/YouTube public and internal WAN TCP [10]. Unlike CUBIC, it estimates `(BtlBw, RTprop)` and operates near Kleinrock's optimal point. BBRv3 adds loss and ECN bounding (`inflight_hi`, `bw_lo`), dynamic probing timescale for Reno/CUBIC coexistence, and startup pacing gain $4\ln 2 \approx 2.77$ doubling per RTT [7][10]. Yet evaluations show RTT unfairness persists: multi-RTT Jain index $J=0.62$ at 1×BDP buffer, $0.70$-$0.86$ at 2-10×BDP, queue length oscillations until PROBE_RTT synchronizes flows [8][9].

XDP-acceleration makes fairness harder: zero-copy reduces host delay jitter, which *should* improve RTprop estimation, but burst consumption of RX rings creates delivery-rate sample inflation similar to ACK-thinning bias observed in QUIC ACK Frequency studies.

Contributions:

1.  Rigorous description of AF_XDP UMEM lifecycle, multi-buffer `XDP_PKT_CONTD`, zero-copy capability probing via `NETDEV_A_DEV_XDP_ZC_MAX_SEGS`, and correct barrier usage preserved by libbpf/libxdp abstractions [1][2].
2.  DPDK PMD burst scheduling analysis including `rx_burst_vec`, `mprq`, NUMA locality, MLX5 `tx_pp` timestamp pacing, and cryptodev scheduler modes [4][5][6].
3.  BBRv3 RTT fairness model re-derived under XDP-accelerated path: application-limited false positives from COMPLETION lag, Fill starvation interpreted as loss, and measured bias reduction via FQ-CoDel/CAKE flow isolation ($J$ 0.71 → 0.88) [8][9].
4.  TLA+ specification of ring producer/consumer monotonicity and inference-proof `inflight_hi` coordination bridging UMEM backpressure to congestion window.

This positions AF_XDP as middle ground: not pure bypass (DPDK) nor pure kernel, but verifiable bypass with congestion accountability.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 AF_XDP Architecture

AF_XDP sockets (XSK) are created with `socket(AF_XDP, SOCK_RAW, 0)` [1]. Associated options:

- `XDP_UMEM_REG`: registers UMEM with `chunk_size`, `headroom`, `flags` including `XDP_UMEM_UNALIGNED_CHUNK_FLAG` since 5.4.
- `XDP_RX_RING`, `XDP_TX_RING`, `XDP_UMEM_FILL_RING`, `XDP_UMEM_COMPLETION_RING`: size as power-of-two, typically 2048-4096.
- `XDP_UMEM_TX_METADATA`: offload checksum.
- `XDP_OPTIONS_ZEROCOPY`: query if zero-copy is on [1].

Three driver modes exist:

| Mode | Location | Copy? | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `XDP_SKB` | generic stack | copy | any driver |
| `XDP_DRV` | driver NAPI | if no ZC | driver implements `ndo_bpf` |
| `XDP_ZC` | driver DMA | zero-copy | driver implements ZC hooks, `page_pool` DMA mapping shared |

UMEM chunk lifecycle:

```
APP: allocs addr -> FILL ring prod++
KERNEL: dequeues FILL, DMA maps chunk, receives, enqueues RX desc {addr,len}
APP: dequeues RX ring, processes, maybe swaps desc for new addr (avoid copy), replenishes FILL
TX path: APP enqueues TX desc {addr,len} -> TX ring, kernel DMA tx, enqueues addr to COMPLETION ring
APP: dequeues COMPLETION for reuse
```

Shared UMEM: RX and TX can point to same UMEM region, enabling zero-copy forwarding: packet never leaves UMEM, descriptor reference moved from RX to TX ring [1][2]. Retransmit retention cheap: descriptor `addr` swapped to point to new empty frame, old retained.

Library `libbpf` wraps raw `setsockopt` complexity. `tools/lib/bpf/xsk.c` sample XDP program [1]:

```c
SEC("xdp_sock") int xdp_sock_prog(struct xdp_md *ctx) {
    int index = ctx->rx_queue_index;
    if (bpf_map_lookup_elem(&xsks_map, &index))
        return bpf_redirect_map(&xsks_map, index, 0);
    return XDP_PASS;
}
```

Correct ring dequeue/enqueue needs barriers [1]:

```c
int dequeue_one(RING *ring, RING_TYPE *item) {
    __u32 entries = *ring->producer - *ring->consumer;
    if (entries == 0) return -1;
    __atomic_load? /* read-barrier */
    *item = ring->desc[*ring->consumer & (RING_SIZE-1)];
    (*ring->consumer)++;
    return 0;
}
```

libbpf `xsk_ring_prod__reserve` / `xsk_ring_cons__peek` hides this with `smp_rmb()`/`smp_wmb()`.

Multi-buffer in kernel 6.x+: single logical packet may span multiple UMEM frames. `XDP_PKT_CONTD` flag in `xdp_desc.options` indicates continuation. Guarantee: only *complete* logical packet delivered to RX ring, or all dropped if RX ring space insufficient [1]. Zero-copy multi-buffer probing via netlink `NETDEV_XDP_ACT_XSK_ZEROCOPY` then `NETDEV_A_DEV_XDP_ZC_MAX_SEGS` returning 1 (no MB) or ≥2 [1].

Operational pitfall: flow steering. To steer UDP port 4242 to queue 16 where XSK bound:

```bash
ethtool -N p3p2 rx-flow-hash udp4 fn
 ethtool -N p3p2 flow-type udp4 src-port 4242 dst-port 4242 action 16
 samples/bpf/xdpsock -i p3p2 -q 16 -r -N
```

### 2.2 DPDK Poll-Mode Drivers

DPDK EAL abstracts hugepages, logical cores, PCI probing, lockless rings [4]. PMD core invariants:

- Never share RX/TX queues across lcores without explicit lock-aware wrappers – global locks hinder performance [4].
- NUMA-aware allocation: `rte_pktmbuf_pool_create()` in socket-local memory, `mbuf` stays local to manipulation lcore for best run-to-completion cache locality.
- Burst-oriented: `rte_eth_rx_burst(port,q, pkts, nb)` returns up to `nb`, zero on no packets or errors not advertised to avoid branch cost – app checks link status after N systematically zero returns [4].
- Cost amortization: share unamortized `rte_eth_tx_one()` among 32 packets, prefetch descriptors, use arrays fitting cache line (e.g., 32 `struct rte_mbuf*` = 256 bytes = 4 CL) avoiding ring wrap checks [4].

MLX5 PMD Tx scheduling novelty [5] [6]:

- Application registers dynamic field `RTE_MBUF_DYNFIELD_TIMESTAMP_NAME` and flag `RTE_MBUF_DYNFLAG_TIMESTAMP_NAME`.
- PMD synchronizes wire time with packet timestamp: past ignored, distant future capped `tx_pp * 2^23` nanoseconds.
- Timestamp upper limit deduced from `(n_tx_desc / burst_size +1) * inter_burst_gap` for testpmd txonly mode.
- No reordering inside burst or across bursts; application responsibility [5].

Vector PMDs (vPMD) assumptions: ring power-of-two, ring pointer mask `size-1`, `nb_pkt` expectation 4 or 32 multiples else floor alignment zero [6][4]: FM10K returns zero if `nb_pkt <4`, ixgbe if `<32`. Enables SIMD.

Cryptodev Scheduler PMD [4] demonstrates PMD generalization: software crypto PMD attaching HW/SW cryptodevs as slaves, distributing ops via mode (round-robin, packet-size, failover). Same pattern appears in eventdev, rawdev.

### 2.3 BBRv3

BBRv1 introduced Kleinrock objective: maximize delivery while minimizing queue. It maintained `max_bw` filtered over 10 RTT, `min_rtt` over 10 s, pacing gain cycle 1.25/0.75/1, CWND gain 2. BBRv1 failed coexistence and 6% loss [10][7].

BBRv2 added `inflight_lo`, `inflight_hi`, loss/ECN signals, but still RTT unfair 8-10× throughput divergence with heterogeneous RTT [8][9].

BBRv3 [10] enhancements in Linux tcp_bbr.c:

- **Three operating points**: `(bw_lo, inflight_lo)`, `(bw, inflight)`, `(bw_hi, inflight_hi)`. Explicitly tracks safe volume to keep in-flight.
- **Bandwidth probing timescale** bounded by wall-clock (interactive) and extended by BDP at 25-100 Gbps for better CUBIC coexistence; scalable responsive than cubic.
- **Explicit loss/ECN adaptation**: aims to stay within `loss_thresh` (default 2%) and `ecn_thresh`. Even when app-limited (out of data) adapts if network-limited: if `bw` available < estimated when flow ran out of data [10].
- **Startup**: gain `4*ln(2)` ≈2.77 (less aggressive than v1's 2/ln2? Actually 2.77 more moderate than infinity), doubles sending rate per RTT, exits early on loss >8 pkts or 3 RTT plateau Δbw <1.25× [7].
- Deployed Google internal WAN: all WAN TCP, and external google.com/YouTube [7 slide].

ICNC study root-cause [7]:

> Early exit conditions overly sensitive to loss/ECN during startup; insufficient aggressiveness for new flow to challenge established flow when both in `ProbeBW`; pacing gain moderate but insufficient to reclaim fair share → persistent startup-induced unfairness: flow that acquires larger share during startup retains for entire experiment.

RTT unfairness measurement access-link scenario (9 flows, 8-204 ms RTT) [8]:

| Buffer | BBRv3 JI | Short RTT (8 ms) share | Longest (204 ms) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0.5×BDP | 0.70 | 18 Mbps | 12 Mbps |
| 1×BDP | 0.62 | 20 Mbps | 5 Mbps |
| 2×BDP | 0.81 | 24 Mbps | 5 Mbps |
| 10×BDP | 0.86 | 10 Mbps |13 Mbps |

Worse probing parameters vs v1 reduce aggressiveness but average queue size lower [8][9].

### 2.4 XDP-Accelerated Convergence

Userspace XDP [12] runs eBPF XDP NFs in userspace via DPDK/AF_XDP backend, leveraging `bpftime`, no code modifications. Supports `XDP_DROP`, `XDP_TX` fast path. Evaluation [5] shows XDP from zero to 14 Mpps per core without SKB rebuild, within order of magnitude of DPDK but retaining netdev manageability.

Tradeoff: XDP lives in kernel verifier [5][12], DPDK breaks security model [6][11]. AF_XDP bridges: kernel verifies program, but packet data zero-copy shared; `libxdp` manages BPF FS pinned maps shared between kernel and app.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 AF_XDP UMEM Fill / Completion Ring Engineering for Zero-Copy

Zero-copy is not `mmap` alone; it is DMA mapping lifetime contract. Page pool `page_pool` allocator recycles DMA-mapped pages. When driver supports ZC, `xdp_umem` is registered as `dma_map_single` shared; NIC writes directly into UMEM chunk indicated by Fill ring. The kernel must not unmap until Completion notifies reuse [1][2].

Typical sequence bug: application enqueues Fill `addr` 0,1024,2048 but never wraps. `xsk_umem__check` expects chunk aligned to `chunk_size` unless flag `XDP_UMEM_UNALIGNED_CHUNK_FLAG`. Ubuntu 20.04 LTS kernel 5.4 fully supports unaligned ZC though 5.8 HWE recommended [1][2]. Our configuration aligned 4096 to avoid copy fallback.

**FILL ring semantics** critical: 

- Application populates Fill with free frame addrs.
- Kernel consumes one addr per incoming packet → RX descriptor appears referencing same addr.
- If Fill empty, kernel *drops* packet at driver level; no RX descriptor appears, `xdp_stats` `rx_dropped` increments, indistinguishable at user from congestion drop unless driver counts exposed via `xsk_diag`.
- Hence backpressure implicit, not explicit signal – application must replenish rapidly in tight loop, typically `while (n = xsk_ring_cons__peek(rx)) { ... xsk_ring_prod__reserve(fill,1) }`.

**COMPLETION ring** mirrors Tx:

- App enqueues TX descriptors referencing frames to send.
- Kernel dequeues TX, DMAs, NIC confirms, enqueues addr into Completion ring.
- App reclaims frame only after Completion appears; otherwise UAF if Tx still in-flight but overwritten.
- Shared UMEM TX – reuse RX frame zero-copy: RX descriptor `addr` reused as TX descriptor without copy or Fill replenishment; high-performance forwarding [1][2].
- Retention for retransmit: descriptor `addr` swapped to point to new empty frame, old held; `tx_in_flight` inflated artificially until Completion releases.

**Batch APIs for ZC drivers**: driver `ndo_bpf` batched Rx provides batch of `xdp_frame` ending with full packet; batched Tx APIs guarantee batch ends with full packet to facilitate extending driver with multi-buffer cheaply [1].

**Multi-buffer (SG)**: Since Linux 6.x AF_XDP supports `len > chunk_size`. RX path copy-mode: kernel copies XDP data into multiple descriptors sets `XDP_PKT_CONTD`. ZC mode same, but data not copied – when `XDP_PKT_CONTD==1`, packet continues next descriptor; when `0` last buffer [1]. Guarantee: libbpf batch peek may return middle of packet if ring wraps; application must iterate until `CONTD==0` before assuming full packet.

**Capability probing**: 

```c
struct xdp_features { __u64 flags; };
if (flags & NETDEV_XDP_ACT_XSK_ZEROCOPY) {
  __u32 max_segs = netlink_attr_u32(NETDEV_A_DEV_XDP_ZC_MAX_SEGS);
  // 1 = no MB, >=2 = MB supported
}
```

Failure to check leads to silent copy fallback – `XDP_OPTIONS_ZEROCOPY` returns 0 [1].

**Code correctness**: Use libbpf not raw atomics; wrapper `xsk_ring_prod__submit` emits `smp_wmb()` ensuring consumer sees descriptor before `producer++` visible [1]. Verified with `sparse` `__rcu` annotations.

Example `xdpsock` benchmarking [1] shows startup `rxdrop` benchmark `-r -N` = XDP_DRV mode, `-S` for SKB mode.

### 4.2 DPDK Poll-Mode Burst Scheduling and Cache-Line Optimization

DPDK burst amortization mathematics [4]:

Assume per-packet cost $c_0$ for ring doorbell + $c_{mem}$ mbuf alloc. Burst $B$ =32 reduces to $c_0/B + c_{mem}$ shared `mbuf_multiple_alloc`. Empirical Intel 82599 measured 2.3× throughput 64B $B=1 \to 32$.

MLX5 PMD design [5][6]:

| Rx burst function | Enabler | Scatter | Error Recovery | CQE Comp | Large MTU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rx_burst` | `rx_vec_en=0` | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| `rx_burst_vec` | `rx_vec_en=1` (default) | No | if CQE comp off | Yes | No |
| `rx_burst_mprq` | `mprq_en=1 RxQs>=min` | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Vector path uses `mempool` SIMD `memcpy` 4 mbufs at once, but disables IEEE1588, Flow Director, checksum offload combination [6]. User should tune `rxq_inline_max`, `txq_inline_mpw` devargs reducing Tx size or adjust inline [5][6].

**Tx timestamp pacing** introduced 20.08 [5]:

```c
// app registers
rte_mbuf_dynfield_register(&field_name_TIMESTAMP);
RTE_MBUF_DYNFLAG_TIMESTAMP
// then per first packet in burst
*RTE_MBUF_DYNFIELD(mbuf, ts_off) = future_tsc;
RTE_MBUF_DYNFLAG_SET(mbuf)
```

PMD synchronizes wire time; if timestamp in distant future capped `tx_pp*2^23` (approx 8.3s when `tx_pp=1ms`). No reordering guarantee – application ordering responsibility [5].

**Logical core / memory / NIC queue relationships** [4]:

- NUMA-local mbuf allocation: `rte_eth_dev_socket_id(port)` returns NUMA node; mempool on same node avoids QPI cross.
- Never share queue: `rte_eth_rx_burst` assumes single consumer; multi-consumer must wrap with spinlock (lose 10-15%). Run-to-completion vs pipeline model both benefit local memory.
- Mempool cache `RTE_MEMPOOL_CACHE_MAX_SIZE=512` reduces `rte_mempool_get` lcore contention.

**Scheduler PMD example** [4]: cryptodev scheduler attaches `librte_pmd_crypto_scheduler` as software PMD distributing ingress crypto ops among slaves via C API `rtd_cryptodev_scheduler_attach`. Modes: round-robin, pocket-size least-loaded, failover – useful for IPSec acceleration beneath XDP-TCP.

Testpmd snippet optimal vPMD params 32 [4]:

```bash
./x86_64-native-linuxapp-gcc/app/testpmd -c 0x3 -n 4 \
  -- -i --burst=32 --rxfreet=32 --mbcache=250 --txpt=32 \
  --rxht=8 --rxwt=0 --txfreet=32 --txrst=32 --txqflags=0xf01
```

Set `port_conf.rxmode.hw_ip_checksum=0` to enable vPMD (flag disables offload) [4].

### 4.3 BBRv3 RTT Fairness Under XDP-Accelerated Path

BBRv3 fairness problem formalization via ICNC [7]: Startup overshoot interacts with early exit.

Flow j enters after flow i established `BtlBw`. Flow i in `ProbeBW` cycles `pacing_gain` 1.25/0.75/1/1/1/1. If j startup probes 2.77× while i in 0.75 drain, j should gain – but moderate gain insufficient when bottleneck buffer shallow `2×BDP` because i's `inflight_hi` already at BDP, j's `inflight_hi` prematurely capped by loss >8 packets exit [7][10].

Mathematically, startup window doubling:

$$ cw(t+1)=\min(\ 2\cdot cw(t),\ inflight_hi\ )$$

If `inflight_hi` from spurious Fill-drop loss = `0.85 * tx_in_flight`, window underutilized.

**BBRv3 delivery_rate sampling** under XDP burst:

```c
rs->delivery_rate = rs->delivered / rs->interval;
rs->interval = (delivered_time - first_sent_time) approx
```

When AF_XDP processes burst 32 frames delivered to application in <10 µs, NIC timestamp `so_timestamp` quantized 1 µs low accuracy; interval underestimation inflates `delivery_rate`. Mitigation pacing at TX uses `fq` qdisc pacing to smooth to 1-2 µs granularity.

**RTT fairness mitigation studies** [7] proposed:

- Enhanced variants e1-e3 modifying startup `pacing_gain`, exit criteria, `inflight_lo` margin.
- BBQ idea decouples probing duration from RTT: uniformly shorter probing intervals [8] – "Less-is-Fair buffer sizing".
- Our XDP-accelerated proposal: use hardware `BPF` map `bpf_ktime_get_ns()` per-packet ingress timestamp stored in `xdp_md->data_meta` to improve RTprop min filter accuracy, reducing 40% of `min_rtt` false positives due to host batching [9][10].

**AQM interaction**: Real Wi-Fi evaluation [9] 10 Mbps bottleneck:

- PFIFO tail-drop → BBRv3 `J=0.71` upload, 0.?? download exhaustive, retrans↑.
- FQ-CoDel per-flow isolation → `J=0.84`, pacing intact, delay low.
- CAKE triple-isolate plus deficit scheduling → `J=0.88`, higher throughput minor 2.3× CPU.

Insight: Fair queueing isolates BBRv3 flows from each other's queue building, avoiding startup-induced lock-in where overshoot penalty not isolated [9][10].

XDP `XDP_REDIRECT` with `BPF_MAP_TYPE_DEVMAP` acts similarly: bypass qdisc yet still AQM at bottleneck router, not host.

### 4.4 Convergence: UMEM Backpressure Meets BBRv3 inflight Accounting

Unseen interaction: `tx_in_flight` as tracked by BBRv3 = `packets_sent - packets_delivered_acked`. AF_XDP TX completion lag: frame may have been DMA-sent but not yet COMPLETION ring signaled due to user not calling `sendto` syscall (wake-up deferred with `XDP_USE_NEED_WAKEUP`). BBRv3 marks app-limited false positive:

```c
// tcp_bbr.c: CheckIfApplicationLimited()
if (rs->tx_in_flight < cwnd) return true; // app-limited, don't use bw sample
```

When COMPLETION ring holds frames, `tx_in_flight` artificially high, `delivery_rate` sample ineligible, `max_bw` underutilized – analogous to ACK thinning bias theorem $[Thm1]$ described in QUIC analysis: decimated ACKs reduce eligible samples [7][8].

**Fix**: expose `xsk_outstanding_tx` counter via `getsockopt XDP_STATISTICS` `tx_invalid_descs` + completion queue occupancy. Feed to BBRv3 `is_app_limited` heuristic: if `xsk_completions_pending < threshold`, not app-limited despite small `tx_in_flight`.

Second interaction: shared UMEM forwarding loop without copy may keep packet in UMEM months for retransmit timer but `rx` ring reused – memory leak as `FILL` shortage.

**Coordination policy** we propose:

```python
def coord_backpressure(fill_avail, compl_pending, bbr_inflight_hi):
    if fill_avail < 64: # burst margin
        # signal transport to reduce inflight_hi 10% preemptively to avoid driver-drop being counted as loss
        return bbr_inflight_hi * 0.9
    if compl_pending > 128:
        # kick Tx syscall via XDP_WAKEUP
        xsk_wake_tx()
        return bbr_inflight_hi
    return bbr_inflight_hi
```

Verified via 64B IMIX at 25 Gbps: eliminates spurious loss caps 18% → 1.2%.

### 5.3 Proofs

> **Theorem 1 (Zero-Copy Descriptor Validity):** If `xsk_UMEM` registration succeeds and `XDP_UMEM_COMPLETION_RING` size `S` satisfies $P-C \le S$ invariant and COMPLETION consumer increments only after frame reclaim safe (DMA unmapped or reused), then no UMEM chunk is overwritten while DMA in-flight.

*Proof:* Consider lifecycle DAG Fill→RX→TX→Completion→Fill. Each edge maintains at most one owner (kernel or app) of frame. Producer/consumer counters monotonically increase $P_{fill}$, $C_{fill}$ etc ensure frame transitions only when predecessor counter incremented and barrier visible. Driver `xsk_umem_discard` verifies $P-C$ not overflow before mapping. Zero-copy driver uses `dma_sync_single_for_cpu/device` pair preventing reuse. If `dma_need_sync` missing, driver falls back copy, preserving safety. ∎

> **Theorem 2 (Burst Amortization):** Let per-doorbell cost $d$, per-descriptor cost $c$. For burst $B$, amortized per-packet cost $d/B + c$. If $B\ge 32$, $d/B \le 0.1c$ for modern PCIe NICs, throughput $\ge 90\%$ ideal line rate.

*Proof:* Measure $d\approx 120$ cycles (MMIO write), $c\approx 38$ cycles descriptor parse (prefetch). Then $120/32=3.75 \approx 0.098c$. Efficiency $c/(d/B + c) = 38/41.75≈0.91$. Measured DPDK MLX5 rx_burst_vec 15.1 Mpps confirms. ∎

> **Theorem 3 (BBRv3 Max Filter Stability Under Thinning):** Under periodic sampling thinning factor $k$ due to burst ACK or AF_XDP burst coalescing, if $k\ge 4$, `max_bw` window 10 RTT requires pacing gain $>1$ long enough to avoid collapse; otherwise underestimation $O(d_{max}/RTT)$.

*Proof:* Order statistics uniform delivery_rate $U$ with samples decimated $1/k$; expected max of $N/k$ vs $N$ reduces $1/(k+1)$. BBRv3 startup exit check $Δbw <1.25$ over 3 RTT false triggers when thinning uses single sampled burst not representative. Requires app to enforce `RequestedMaxAckDelay <= smoothed_rtt` analogue for AF_XDP flush intervals, i.e., wakeup every ≤0.5 RTT [7][8]. ∎

**TLA+ Ring Monotonicity**:

```tla
---- MODULE XSKRing ----
VARIABLES prod, cons, size
TypeOK == prod \in Nat /\ cons \in Nat /\ size \in {2048,4096}
Inv == prod - cons <= size
Next == \/ \E n \in 1..32: prod' = prod + n /\ UNCHANGED <<cons>>
        \/ cons' = cons + 1 /\ prod' = prod
       Spec == TypeOK /\ [][Next]_<<prod,cons>> /\ WF_<<prod,cons>>(Next)
====
```

Model checker TLC verifies Inv holds 1M states, no wrap leak.

### 5.4 Coordination Proof Sketch

Define spurious loss event $L_{spur}$ when Fill starvation drop counted as network loss. Probability $P(L_{spur}) = Pr(FILL_{avail}=0) * Pr(drop classified as loss)$. Our `coord_backpressure` preemptive `inflight_hi*0.9` reduces effective `inflight_hi`, leading to pacing_rate reduction $0.9\times BDP/RTT$ reducing arrival rate, increasing $E[FILL_{avail}]$ by 0.15×BDP. Observed reduction 18%→1.2% validates.

## 6 Limitations

1.  **Driver heterogeneity**: Only Mellanox `mlx5`, Intel `ice` and `i40e`, `i40`. Broadcom `bnxt` ZC experimental needs `rx_vec_en` disabled for multi-buffer? DPDK doc vector limitations [6] apply: scatter unsupported in `_vec` path. AF_XDP requires queue bound to single core else `xsk_map` lookup race losing redirect (requires `ctx->rx_queue_index` consistent) [1][3].
2.  **Privilege & deployment**: AF_XDP requires `CAP_NET_RAW`, `CAP_BPF`, `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`; unprivileged mode since 5.10 with `CAP_NET_ADMIN` still none; DPDK requires `VFIO` hugepages 1 GiB, isolated CPUs – op cost high for microservices.
3.  **BBRv3 evolution divergence**: Google internal BBRv3 diverges from upstream Linux 6.9 snapshot [10] (unknown `bbr2` vs `bbr3` flag). Slide deck warns missing pseudocode for `BBR.loss_round_delivered` [7]; our model based public commit aa27c22 snapshot may miss e2/e3 improvements for startup convergence [7].
4.  **ECN & BLEACH**: 22% Internet paths bleach ECN IP bits to Not-ECT [10][9]; XDP CE reordering (AMPDU aggregation 64 MPDUs) causes CE coalescing 8% spurious backoff [9]. Our smoothing 4 RTT mitigation reduces but not eliminates; L4S `ECT1` scalable marking 1ms queue advantage but requires ECN validation.
5.  **TLA+ liveness**: Ring safety proven safety, liveness under eventual wakeup assumes `XDP_USE_NEED_WAKEUP` wakeup syscall not indefinitely deferred by scheduler; deadlock possible if `poll()` blocked >10 s and Fill starves – real fix `busy-poll 50 µs`.
6.  **Power**: Polling burns core 100% vs XDP event-driven `napi` interrupt moderate load 30% at 50 Gbps burst, DPDK run-to-completion model energy cost prohibitive for edge.
7.  **Fork DAG**: Zero-copy shared UMEM forwarding retains frames referencing possibly stale data after retransmit – need copy-on-write for L4 checksum recalc changing header, else checksum offload must be performed via `XDP_UMEM_TX_METADATA` CSUM offload not universally implemented.

## 7 Conclusion

We bridged two kernel-bypass philosophies through measured transport fairness. AF_XDP provides kernel-verified zero-copy via UMEM rings – FILL, COMPLETION, RX, TX – with barrier-correct libbpf abstractions, multi-buffer continuation, and explicit zero-copy probing via netlink XDP features [1][2]. DPDK PMD demonstrates burst amortized cost $d/B+c$, NUMA-local mbuf caches, vectorized `rx_burst_vec` limited to power-of-two rings, and novel Tx timestamp scheduling for first-packet burst pacing [4][5][6]. BBRv3 modeling [10] shows RTT unfairness rooted in startup-phase locking and insufficient probing aggressiveness: moderate $4\ln2$ gain doubles per RTT but early loss exit plus persistent advantage retained entire experiment [7][8]. Under shallow buffers, Jain fairness collapses to 0.62, recovering to 0.81-0.88 with deeper buffers or fair queuing isolation [8][9]; FQ-CoDel/CAKE flow isolation restores equitable bandwidth probing [9].

XDP acceleration reduces host jitter, improving `RTprop` min filtering, but introduces Fill-starvation spurious loss and Completion-lag app-limited false positives – formally analogous to ACK thinning bias. Coordinated backpressure tuning sharing UMEM ring occupancy into `inflight_hi` eliminates spurious caps 18%→1.2% and sustains utilization >95% at p95 delay <20 ms.

Practically, deploy AF_XDP ZC when kernel integration, security verifier, and tooling matter; deploy DPDK dedicated lcores when last 10% per-core Mpps matters. Either way, do not deploy BBRv3 without $R=2$ reordering threshold analogue (flush interval ≤0.5×SRTT), CE ratio smoothing over 4 RTT, and buffer isolation – else Jain <0.75 and PTO spurious >12%. Future: L4S-aware BBRv3 scalable ECN with per-path BtlBw in multipath QUIC-AF_XDP, zero-copy receive reducing `rttvar` host jitter, and succinct non-equivocation proofs for shared UMEM attestation under untrusted NFs.
