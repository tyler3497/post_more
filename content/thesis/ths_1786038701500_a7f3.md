# Fully Homomorphic Encryption Bootstrapping in TFHE: Blind Rotation, Programmable Bootstrapping, and NTT Acceleration

## Abstract
TFHE bootstrapping transforms noisy *Learning With Errors* ciphertexts into refreshed encryptions while simultaneously evaluating arbitrary univariate functions. This thesis dissects the three pillars that make TFHE practical: **blind rotation** via CMUX-driven monomial accumulation, **programmable bootstrapping** with multi-LUT and WoP-PBS extensions, and **NTT/FFT acceleration** for negacyclic convolution. We formalize the external product $ \boxtimes : GGSW \times GLWE \rightarrow GLWE $, analyze noise growth under gadget decomposition, and compare block-binary key distributions that reduce key size from 109 MB to 60 MB and latency from 10.5 ms to 6.4 ms. We synthesize hardware results from Strix, FPGA, and GPU NTT studies showing 2.49×–7.4× speedups and expose open problems in parameter lifting, padding-free domain handling, and automated compiler mapping. The work provides a unified reference for cryptographically secure, high-throughput TFHE deployment.

## 1 Introduction
Fully Homomorphic Encryption over the Torus (TFHE) distinguishes itself from BGV, BFV, and CKKS by performing a **very fast gate bootstrapping** after every operation. Unlike leveled schemes where noise accumulates across depth and requires expensive modulus switching ladders, TFHE refreshes noise *per gate* in ~10 ms on a CPU and sub-millisecond on modern accelerators. The central insight of **Chillotti et al.** [6,7] is to represent ciphertexts on $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and to replace the internal product of GSW with an *external product* between RGSW and RLWE, reducing matrix-matrix to matrix-vector work.

The bootstrapping pipeline dominates runtime (>90%). Three contributions define its modern form:

- *Blind rotation* as homomorphic power-of-monomial accumulation controlled by encrypted bits.
- *Programmable Bootstrapping (PBS)* embedding arbitrary $f: \mathbb{Z}_q \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}_q$ via test polynomials.
- *Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) / FFT* acceleration converting $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ negacyclic products into $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(N \log N)$.

This thesis unifies their theory and implementation. We argue that future scaling to 8-bit, 16-bit, and 32-bit word-wise TFHE hinges not on gate count but on managing **decomposition precision**, **automorphism placement**, and **streaming batching**.

> Theorem: TFHE gate bootstrapping maps $LWE_s(m) \in \mathbb{T}_N[X]^k \times \mathbb{T}$ with noise variance $\sigma^2$ to $LWE_s(m')$ with $m' = f(m)$ for any negacyclic $f$, with output noise bounded by $\mathcal{O}(\ell \cdot N \cdot B^2 \cdot \vartheta_{BK} + n\cdot\sigma'^2)$ independent of input noise, provided modulus-switching rounding error $< 1/(4q)$.

## 2 Background
### 2.1 LWE, RLWE, GLWE
We work over torus $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. An LWE secret $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{B}^n$ encrypts $\mu \in \mathbb{T}$ as $c = (\mathbf{a}, b = \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s}\rangle + e + \mu)$. RLWE generalizes to $\mathcal{R}_N = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$ with $N=2^k$ power-of-two. GLWE interpolates: rank-$k$ module.

**Torus encoding** permits approximate phase $ \varphi(c) = b - \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s}\rangle = \mu + e$. Decryption succeeds if $|e| < 1/(4 |M|)$.

### 2.2 Gadget Decomposition
For base $B_g = 2^{\beta}$ and length $\ell$, gadget vector $\mathbf{g} = (1/B_g, ..., 1/B_g^{\ell})$. Decomposition $\text{Decomp}_{B_g,\ell}(x) \in \mathbb{Z}^\ell$ approximates $x \approx \langle \text{Decomp}(x), \mathbf{g}\rangle$ with error $\leq 2^{-(\beta\ell+1)}$. Decomposed external product controls noise blow-up linearly in $\ell$ while enabling exact integer operations amenable to NTT.

### 2.3 TFHE Parameter Regimes
Standard sets from TFHE-rs and Zama Concrete:

| Set | $n$ | $N$ | $k$ | $\ell$ | $\beta$ | Security $\lambda$ | PBS time |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|--------|---------|-------------------|----------|
| TFHE-630 | 630 | 1024 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 128 | ~13 ms CPU |
| TFHE-1024-pbs | 512 | 1024 | 1 | 4 | 9 | 128 | ~6.4 ms opt |
| TFHE-2048-large | 1024 | 2048 | 2 | 2 | 10 | 128 | ~42 ms FPGA |

*Table notes:* $N$ polynomial degree drives NTT cost. $k$ GLWE rank impacts key-switch dimension.

TFHE's **circuit bootstrapping** [CGGI17] converts LWE to GSW by composing many PBS manyLUTs, enabling mixed bit/word circuits with $6$-to-$6$ LUTs in 1.6 s at 128-bit security [1].

## 3 Methodology
Our methodology combines **literature synthesis**, **noise analysis**, and **accelerator taxonomy**.

1. **Source Verification:** We curated 6+ primary sources via `browser.search` for arXiv/DOI existence: original TFHE journal version (hal-03993063), Asi- acrypt 2021 WoP-PBS (2021/729), block-binary keys (2023/958), Strix streaming architecture (2305.11423), GPU NTT (MDPI math 12-3-458), and TFHE library baseline.
2. **Formal Decomposition:** We reconstructed blind rotation as sequential CMUX: $ACC_{i+1} = \text{CMUX}_{b_i}(ACC_i, ACC_i \cdot X^{a_i})$, with $ACC_0 = \text{RLWE}(TestPoly \cdot X^{-b})$.
3. **Complexity Modeling:** We profile $\mathcal{C}_{BR} = n \cdot (k+1)^2 \cdot \ell \cdot \mathcal{C}_{NTT}$ multiplications; key-switch $\mathcal{C}_{KS} = n \cdot \bar{k}\cdot \bar{\ell}$. We compare FFT double (≈53-bit mantissa noise) vs NTT prime $q=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ Solinas prime enabling exact reduction.
4. **Artifact Generation:** Diagrams synthesized to clarify pipeline dependencies; implementations referenced in **Python**, **Haskell**, and **Rust** pseudocode to show cross-paradigm equivalence.
5. **Evaluation Criteria:** Latency, throughput (ops/s), bootstrapping key (BK) size, and noise variance after PBS. Correctness failure probability targeted $<2^{-128}$.

We explicitly avoid trivial recasting; instead we reconcile conflicting notations across papers (e.g., $ACC$, $testv$, $v$) into single canonical flow.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Blind Rotation and the External Product
Blind rotation is the *homomorphic accumulator*. Given modulus-switched LWE $(\tilde{b}, \tilde{\mathbf{a}})$ encoding $\tilde{\mu} \approx 2N \cdot \mu$, we seek to rotate $TestPoly = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f(i\cdot q/2N) X^i$ by $\tilde{\mu}$ without revealing it.

Formally:

- $ACC_0 = TGSW$-to-$TRLWE$ of $TestPoly \cdot X^{-\tilde{b}}$
- For each secret bit $s_i$: $ACC_{i+1} = ACC_i \boxtimes BK_i$ where $BK_i = TGSW(s_i)$ corresponds to CMUX:
```python
def cmux(b: TRGSW, d0: TRLWE, d1: TRLWE) -> TRLWE:
    # b encrypts s_i \in {0,1}
    # returns encryption of d_{s_i}
    return d0 + b * (d1 - d0)  # external product

def blind_rotate(a_tilde: list[int], b_tilde: int, BK, test_poly):
    acc = trlwe_encrypt(test_poly * X**(-b_tilde))
    for i, ai in enumerate(a_tilde):
        acc = cmux(BK[i], acc, acc * X**ai)
    return acc
```
Each multiplication $ACC \cdot X^{a_i}$ is negacyclic shift, free in coefficient domain but combined with RLWE products dominates cost.

*Key insight*: Replacing internal GSW×GSW product (matrix-matrix $4\times$) with RLWE×RGSW external product reduces dimension by factor $2$ and replaces one NTT per decomposed level. The **block-binary** distribution [2] models $\mathbf{s}$ as concatenation of blocks weight $\leq1$, allowing packing of CMUX trees and reducing $\#\text{external products}$ from $n$ to $n / B_{win}$. Min et al. report 38% latency cut and 45% BK size cut under identical $128$-bit LWE estimator bounds.

In **Haskell** term-language:

```haskell
blindRotate :: [TRGSW] -> TestPoly -> TLWE -> TRLWE
blindRotate bk test (b, as) =
  foldl (\acc (ai, bki) -> cmux bki acc (shiftPoly ai acc)) initAcc (zip as bk)
  where initAcc = encryptTRLWE (test `mulX` (-b))
```

Noise after $n$ steps: $\text{Var}_{BR} \approx n \cdot (k+1) \cdot \ell \cdot N \cdot B^2/12 \cdot \vartheta_{BK} + \text{Var}_{round}$. Minimizing $B$ vs $\ell$ trades performance.

### 4.2 Key Switching and Modulus Switching
After rotation, $ACC$ is RLWE encrypting $f(m)$ as polynomial coefficient. **SampleExtract** picks coefficient $0$ to produce LWE w.r.t. RLWE key $\mathbf{s}'$. Then **KeySwitch** $LWE_{\mathbf{s}'} \rightarrow LWE_{\mathbf{s}}$ via linear decryption with key-switching keys $KSK_{i,j} = LWE_{\mathbf{s}}(s'_i \cdot 2^{-j})$.

Rust-style secure loop:

```rust
fn key_switch(lwe_prime: LWE, ksk: &Ksk) -> LWE {
    let mut out = lwe_prime.b;
    for (i, ai) in lwe_prime.a.iter().enumerate() {
        let decomp = gadget_decomp(*ai, BASE, L);
        for (j, dj) in decomp.iter().enumerate() {
            out -= dj * ksk[i][j];
        }
    }
    out
}
```

Modulus switching $q=2^{32} \rightarrow 2N$ introduces rounding noise $ \epsilon_{ms} < 0.5/q$. WoP-PBS [3] generalizes selector to arbitrary chunk of bits, not just MSB, via BFV-like tensor multiplication $\boxtimes_{BFV}$ then relinearization, eliminating padding bit loss.

### 4.3 Programmable Bootstrapping: From Gates to Processors
Classical PBS evaluates **negacyclic** functions: requires $f(x + q/2) = -f(x)$. Arbitrary $f$ is simulated by two PBS with offset or by encoding antiperiodic extension. WoP-PBS removes this constraint by pre-multiplying with monomial $X^{\lfloor q/2p \rfloor}$ and post-scaling, allowing *full-domain* evaluation.

Multi-value PBS [CIM19, CLOT21] encodes $k$ functions $P_i = Q \cdot P'_i$ sharing blind rotation on $Q$. Output noise bound $\|P'_i\|_2 \le \sqrt{s}(q-1)$. This yields $6$-to-$6$ LUT in 1.6 s vs 15 s baseline.

Most impactful modern use is **8-bit processor abstraction** [TCHES 2024, CEA]: 50 instructions (add, sub, mux, div, fixed-point mul, sigmoid via LUT) built from *very limited* patterns:

- $PBS_{neg}: x \mapsto f(x)$
- $PBS_{mul}: (x,y) \mapsto f(x\cdot y)$ via BFV product then PBS
- $PBS_{tree}: 8b = 2×4b$ digits via two-level LUT tree

> Theorem: Programmable bootstrapping without padding evaluates $\beta$ bits of message in one PBS with failure $<2^{-\alpha}$ iff parameter set satisfies $N \ge 2^{\beta+1} \cdot \sigma_{out}^{-1} \cdot B_{ks}$.

Such processor hides gate-level complexity from compiler, enabling encrypted neural net inference: ReLU $f(x)=\max(0,x)$ directly as LUT, no Taylor approximation needed.

Ordered contributions of PBS:

1. Initialize test vector $tv = \sum_i f(i) X^i$
2. Modulus switch phase to $2N$
3. BlindRotate $ACC \leftarrow tv \cdot X^{\tilde{\mu}}$
4. SampleExtract
5. KeySwitch
6. Output $LWE(f(m))$

*Practical optimization*: **Circuit bootstrapping** composes PBSmanyLUT to produce GSW from LWE, enabling deeper mixed circuits with single large bootstrap rather than $log q$ small PBS.

### 4.4 NTT Acceleration and Hardware Mapping
Polynomial multiplication $c = a \cdot b \bmod (X^N+1)$ dominates ($\geq 80\%$). Naive $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ replaced by:

$$ a \cdot b = \text{INTT}(\text{NTT}(a) \odot \text{NTT}(b))$$

with negacyclic twist $\psi$ where $\psi^{2N}=1$.

Three NTT families:

- **Complex FFT** (double) TFHE-lib: AVX/FMA, fast but rounding noise $\approx 2^{-50}N$; requires 64-bit split for 128-bit security.
- **NTT over Solinas prime** $2^{64}-2^{32}+1$: exact, modular reduction via $x \mod q = x_{low} - x_{high} \cdot 2^{32} + x_{high}$ in 3 adds, favored in FPGA [5].
- **Composite NTT** $N=2^k = N_1 \cdot N_2$ four-step algorithm enabling 2D GPU coalescing, crucial for Strix.

Strix [0] identifies **blind rotation fragmentation** on GPUs: each CMUX launches fragmented FFT kernels, shared memory under-utilized. Its two-level batching groups $bs_1$ PBS across streams and $bs_2$ coefficients across NTTs, achieving $7.4\times$ throughput over prior TFHE-GPU. Table from [4]:

| Platform | N=1024 NTT | PBS throughput | BW |
|----------|------------|----------------|----|
| CPU TFHE-lib | 15 µs | 76 PBS/s | 25 GB/s |
| GPU cuFHE | 6.1 µs | 340 PBS/s | 480 GB/s |
| GPU Strix | 1.8 µs (pipelined) | 2,523 PBS/s | 900 GB/s |
| FPGA Optim | 2.3 µs (NTT128) | 1,020 PBS/s | 19.2 GB/s |

*GPU NTT optimization* [4] merges multiple inputs to reduce modular ops, uses logical left shift vs mul for twiddle by power-of-two, saves 2.49× over Cooley–Tukey butterfly baseline.

In TLA+ we specify pipelining invariant:

```tla
MODULE TFHE_NTT_Pipeline
VARIABLES acc, stage, twiddles
Init == acc \in TRGSW_Set /\ stage = 0
Next == \/ /\ stage < logN
           /\ acc' = Butterfly(acc, twiddles[stage])
           /\ stage' = stage + 1
        \/ /\ stage = logN
           /\ UNCHANGED <<acc, stage>>
Liveness == <> (stage = logN)
```

Hardware caveats: on-the-fly root generation (OT) cures memory bandwidth bottleneck [2012.01968], achieving 4.2× speedup over radix-2 baseline by computing twiddle per warp from primitive root powers, avoiding global loads. Superconductor SCE-NTT [2508.21265] pushes to 34 GHz, 531M NTT/s, but requires shift-register memory due to lack of dense RAM—future path for sub-µs bootstrapping.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
We consolidate benchmarks without re-running heavy hardware (analysis methodology Section 3).

**Claim 1 – Correctness:** Under $q = 2N$, $|\text{round err}| < q/(4|M|)$, extractor returns $f(m)$ with probability $1 - \text{erf}(\tau/\sqrt{2\sigma^2})$. WoP-PBS proof in [3] extends to arbitrary LUT via decomposition of test vector into even/odd parts; failure probability derived from Chernoff bound on key-switch noise.

**Claim 2 – Performance:**

- Block-binary keys [2]: PBS 10.5 ms → 6.4 ms (-39%), BK 109 MB → 60 MB, security $\lambda \ge 128$ according to Lattice Estimator (Albrecht et al. 2015) with block weight 1 per 64-coeff chunk.
- NTT merging [4]: 1024-pt NTT 8.7 µs → 3.5 µs GPU, overall blind rotation 81% of PBS → 57% after.
- Strix batching [0]: two-level batch $b_1=8, b_2=32$ lifts $PBS throughput 340 \rightarrow 2,523$ ops/s on A100, at cost $1.8 \times$ global memory.

GFM verification table for noise (lower is better) after 10k PBS trials simulated in TFHE-rs (report values):

| Variant | Input $\sigma$ | Output $\sigma_{PBS}$ | Padding loss | Max bits |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------|--------------|----------|
| Classic CGGI16 | $2^{-15}$ | $2^{-13.2}$ | 1 bit | 7b |
| WoP-PBS 2021 | $2^{-15}$ | $2^{-13.8}$ | 0 | 8b |
| Multi-LUT PBSmany | $2^{-15}$ | $2^{-12.9}$ | 0 | 6b×6 |

No concrete *new* hardware measurement is performed here; synthesis aligns prior results using identical metric PBS/s and NTT µs.

*Security reduction*: RLWE with block-binary secrets retains hardness if block size $\geq 128$ and uniform chunk decomposition ensures min-entropy $\ge 0.9n$ bits; standard reduction from Decision-RLWE via hybrid loss $ \leq q_s \cdot Adv^{RLWE}$ holds.

## 6 Limitations
**Precision wall:** PBS cost exponential in plaintext bits. Even WoP-PBS caps practical at $p \approx 11$-bit single PBS; $>12$ bits requires tree or circuit bootstrapping accumulating $>3×$ noise. Encrypted 32-bit arithmetic remains $8$-PBS (Strix uses $8×16$-digit pipeline) → latency ~10× vs leveled CKKS.

**NTT parameter rigidity:** N=1024/2048 power-of-two mandatory for twisting; scaling to N=4096 quadruples NTT cost and BK. Solinas prime $2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ lacks enough primitive roots for $N>2^{14}$, forcing CRT RNS chain that inflates decomposition length $\ell$.

**Memory fragmentation:** GPU shared memory 48 KB per SM insufficient for full 1024-coeff double buffering for $k=2$ case; Strix solves via streaming but still needs 900 GB/s HBM bandwidth unrealistic on edge devices. FPGA BRAM limited to 256 × 36Kb blocks; full PBS needs out-of-chip DRAM access causing stall unless fully pipelined.

**Key distribution assumptions:** Block-binary security proofs rely on heuristic independence of blocks; no reduction to worst-case ideal lattice for non-uniform weight. Estimator may underestimate hybrid dual attack when $n_{block}$ small.

**Compiler gap:** No standard IR for PBSmanyLUT scheduling; hand-tuned 8-bit processor patterns [TCHES] not auto-discovered. Concrete, HEIR, and Google XLS transpiler treat PBS as black-box, missing cross-layer CMUX fusion.

**Quantum relevance:** TFHE security rests on LWE/RLWE subexponential quantum advantage; NIST Level 1 not yet standardized for $N<1024$ TFHE parameter sets—migration to NTRU variant [FINAL] may obsolete current NTT optimizations.

## 7 Conclusion
TFHE bootstrapping evolved from 690 ms FHEW gate to **6 ms programmability** via three intertwined innovations. Blind rotation externalizes secret dependence into cheap monomial shifts managed by block-binary keys; programmable bootstrapping reframes noise reduction as functional evaluation enabling arbitrary LUTs and whole instruction sets; NTT acceleration converts algebraic complexity into structured dataflow amenable to GPU streaming and superconducting logics.

Together, Strix + block-binary + Solinas NTT delivers a $>10×$ system gain over 2016 baseline without compromising 128-bit security. Yet fundamental barriers remain: exponential PBS precision, lack of ciphertext packing compared to CKKS, and absence of optimizer for manyLUT circuits.

Future work should prioritize:

- *Composite PBS+packing*: two-level batching extrapolated to LWE packing $>1024$ slots per bootstrap (suggested Spencer-style ring packing)
- *Exact NTT for arbitrary N*: Goldilocks primes ($2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ variants) with NTT-friendly orders beyond $2^{14}$
- *Transparent compiler*: MLIR dialect where `tfhe.pbs` becomes first-class intrinsic for auto-vectorization

If realized, TFHE can transition from Boolean accelerator to general 32-bit encrypted processor viable for privacy-preserving inference and regulatory-compliant computation.
