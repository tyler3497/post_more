# Perverse Sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem: Intersection Cohomology, the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence, and the Topology of Algebraic Maps

## Abstract
The theory of perverse sheaves, introduced by Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne, and Gabber in *Faisceaux pervers* (1982) [1], supplies the correct coefficient category in which the deepest topological properties of algebraic maps become visible. This essay develops the arc from stratified geometry to the BBDG decomposition theorem and the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence of Kashiwara and Mebkhout [6]. We explain how the middle perversity t-structure carves out an abelian category of complexes on singular spaces whose simple objects are intersection cohomology complexes; state the decomposition theorem for proper maps with its relative hard Lefschetz refinement; survey its three proofs — reduction modulo *p* [1], Saito's mixed Hodge modules [9], and the geometric induction of de Cataldo–Migliorini [2][3] exposited by Williamson [4]; and show how Riemann–Hilbert identifies this machinery with regular holonomic D-modules [7][8]. The Springer resolution illustrates the theory in its cleanest form. We close with limitations, including the failure of semisimplicity in modular coefficients.

---
##1 Introduction

Few results in modern algebraic geometry carry the weight of the **decomposition theorem**. In MacPherson's phrase, it "contains as special cases the deepest homological properties of algebraic maps that we know" [4]. Consider a proper morphism of complex algebraic varieties *f : X → Y*. The derived pushforward *Rf*∗**C**_X_ packages the topology of every fiber into a single object of the derived category of sheaves on *Y*. Classically, when *f* is smooth, the Leray spectral sequence degenerates, the higher direct images are local systems, and each of them is semisimple by Deligne's theorem of the fixed part. The decomposition theorem asserts that the same picture survives for an *arbitrary* proper map — but only after one replaces ordinary sheaves with **perverse sheaves** and ordinary truncation with **perverse truncation** [2][10].

The price of generality is a shift in perspective. Perverse sheaves are not sheaves at all: they are objects of the bounded derived category satisfying support and cosupport conditions dictated by the *middle perversity* function. They form an abelian, Noetherian, Artinian category whose simple objects are the intersection complexes *IC*_Z_(*L*) [1][5]. In this category, the decomposition theorem takes the crystalline form

**Rf*∗IC*_X_ ≅ ⊕_*i* ^pH^i(Rf*∗IC*_X_)[−i]**,

with each summand a *semisimple* perverse sheaf: a direct sum of shifted intersection complexes [2][10]. When *X* is smooth, *IC*_X_ = **Q**_X_[dim *X*]; when *Y* is a point, the theorem collapses to the purity of intersection cohomology of a projective variety. The statement unifies hard Lefschetz, the invariant cycle theorem, purity of intersection cohomology, and the semisimplicity of monodromy — all as corollaries of one geometric machine [2].

##2 Background

### Stratified spaces and the middle perversity

Let *X* be a complex algebraic variety. A **stratification** is a finite partition of *X* into locally closed smooth connected subvarieties (strata) such that the closure of each stratum is a union of strata. On a stratified space the **middle perversity** is the function *p(S) = −(dim S)/2* (equivalently the integer-valued perversity *m(k) = −⌊k/2⌋*). A bounded constructible complex *K* ∈ *D^b_c(X)* is **perverse** (for the middle perversity) when it satisfies two vanishing conditions [1][5]:

- **Support:** dim supp *H^i(K)* ≤ −*i* for all *i*;
- **Cosupport:** dim supp *H^i(DK)* ≤ −*i* for all *i*, where *D* denotes Verdier duality.

Equivalently, *K* lies in the heart of the **perverse t-structure** (*^pD^≤0*, *^pD^≥0*), obtained by gluing the standard t-structures on each stratum via the recollement formalism, with shifts chosen according to the perversity [1][5]. The resulting abelian category **Perv(X)** is Noetherian and Artinian, self-dual under Verdier duality, and its simple objects are precisely the intersection complexes *IC*_Z_(*L*) := *i*∗ *j*_!∗ (*L*[dim *Z*]), where *j : S ↪ Z̅* is the inclusion of a smooth locally closed stratum, *i : Z̅ ↪ X* the closed immersion of its closure, *L* an irreducible local system on *S*, and *j*_!∗ the Goresky–MacPherson (middle) extension [1][2][5].

> **Theorem:** (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne) *Perv(X) is an abelian category, Artinian and Noetherian. Every simple object is isomorphic to IC_Z(L) for a unique stratum closure Z and irreducible local system L on a dense open stratum of Z.* [1]

For smooth *X* of pure dimension *n*, the perverse t-structure is the standard one shifted by *n*, so *Perv(X)* is equivalent to the category of local systems placed in degree *−n*: perverse sheaves are the correct generalization of local systems to singular bases [2].

### Intersection cohomology

Goresky and MacPherson defined intersection homology by restricting which chains may meet the singular locus, controlled by a perversity function; the resulting **intersection cohomology** *IH*(X)* satisfies Poincaré duality for singular spaces, with *IH*(X)* ≅ *H*(X)* when *X* is a rational homology manifold [5]. Sheaf-theoretically, *IH*(X)* is the hypercohomology of the complex *IC*_X_ := *j*_!∗(**Q**_{X_reg}[dim X]), the middle extension of the constant sheaf from the regular locus [1][2]. Gabber proved the decisive fact that *IC*_X_ is a *pure* perverse sheaf of weight 0 — the seed from which the decomposition theorem grows [1][2].

### The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence

On a smooth complex variety *X*, let **D**_X_ be the sheaf of differential operators. The classical Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, proved independently by Kashiwara and by Mebkhout, asserts a *t-exact equivalence of triangulated categories*

**DR : D^b_{rh}(D_X) → D^b_c(X^an, C)**,

between regular holonomic D-modules and constructible complexes, which restricts to an equivalence of abelian categories between regular holonomic D-modules and *perverse sheaves* [6][8].

### Semismall maps and small resolutions

A proper surjective map *f : X → Y* with *X* smooth is **semismall** if *2 dim f^{−1}(y) ≤ dim X − dim Y* for every *y ∈ Y*; it is **small** if the inequality is strict over every positive-dimensional stratum of *Y*. For semismall *f*, the complex *Rf*∗**Q**_X_[dim X] is perverse, and the decomposition theorem takes its simplest non-trivial form [2][3]. The archetype is the **Springer resolution** *μ : T*(G/B) → N*, from the cotangent bundle of the flag variety to the nilpotent cone, which is small [2].

##3 Methodology

Our exposition follows the standard architecture of [1] and its expository descendants [2][4][5], working over **C** with **Q**-coefficients unless stated otherwise.

1. **The constructible derived category.** We work in *D^b_c(X)*, the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves, equipped with the six functors *f*∗, *f*∗, *f*!, *f*!, ⊗, *RHom* and Verdier duality *D*. Perverse sheaves are studied via **recollement**: for a closed immersion *i : Z ↪ X* with open complement *j : U ↪ X*, the perverse t-structure on *X* is glued from those on *Z* and *U*, yielding the intermediate extension functor *j*_!∗ as the image of *^pH^0(j_! → j*∗)* [1][5].

2. **Weights as the engine of semisimplicity.** Following Deligne's theory of weights for *ℓ*-adic sheaves, a perverse sheaf is *pure of weight w* if the eigenvalues of Frobenius on its stalks have absolute value *q^{(w+i)/2}* on *H^i*. Gabber's purity theorem makes *IC*_X_ pure of weight 0, and the decomposition theorem is fundamentally a statement that a pure complex splits as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves — a consequence of the vanishing of *Ext^1* between pure perverse sheaves of the same weight [1][2][10].

3. **The three proofs as complementary lenses.** We present (a) the original reduction to finite fields of [1], (b) Saito's mixed Hodge module proof [9], and (c) the de Cataldo–Migliorini geometric induction [2][3], following Williamson's synthesis [4]. Each proof illuminates a different facet: arithmetic, analytic, and topological.

4. **Computational witnesses.** We illustrate the machinery with the semismall case — where the decomposition can be read off from the top-dimensional components of fiber products — and a Python check of semismallness for the Springer resolution.

The following table summarizes the three proofs and their characteristic ingredients:

| Proof | Setting | Key input | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| BBDG (1982) [1] | *ℓ*-adic, finite fields | Weights, Frobenius, Gabber purity | Arithmetic |
| Saito [9] | Mixed Hodge modules | Polarizable Hodge modules, vanishing cycles | Analytic |
| de Cataldo–Migliorini [2][3] | Topology over **C** | Semismall maps, Hodge theory, induction | Geometric |

##4 Deep Dive

### The perverse t-structure and its heart

The decisive move of [1] is to replace the standard t-structure — whose heart is sheaves, far too rigid to see singularities — with a t-structure adapted to a stratification. Gluing proceeds stratum by stratum: on a single smooth stratum *S* of complex dimension *d*, the perverse t-structure is the standard one shifted by *d*, and recollement assembles these into a global t-structure on *X* [1][5]. The truncation functors *^pτ_{≤i}* and *^pτ_{≥i}* produce **perverse cohomology sheaves** *^pH^i(K)*, which are perverse sheaves. A complex splits non-canonically as *K ≅ ⊕_i ^pH^i(K)[−i]* in general, but the decomposition theorem asserts that for *K = Rf*∗IC*_X_ with *f* proper, the splitting is canonical up to the usual choices and the summands are semisimple [2][10].

```haskell
-- The middle perversity as a Haskell function on strata
-- strata are represented by their complex dimension
type Stratum = Int          -- complex dimension of the stratum
middlePerversity :: Stratum -> Int
middlePerversity d = negate (d `div` 2)

-- Support condition: dim supp H^i(K) <= -i for perverse K
supportCondition :: Int -> Int -> Bool
supportCondition i supportDim = supportDim <= negate i

-- A complex is perverse iff support AND cosupport conditions hold
isPerverse :: [(Int, Int)] -> [(Int, Int)] -> Bool
isPerverse cohom dualCohom =
  all (uncurry supportCondition) cohom &&
  all (uncurry supportCondition) dualCohom
```

The intermediate extension *j*_!∗ is characterized as the unique extension of a perverse sheaf from an open stratum with no non-zero subobject or quotient supported on the boundary [1][5].

### The decomposition theorem and relative hard Lefschetz

Let *f : X → Y* be a proper morphism of complex algebraic varieties. The **decomposition theorem** of Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne, and Gabber states [1][2][10]:

> **Theorem:** (BBDG Decomposition) *Let f : X → Y be proper. Then*
> *Rf*∗IC*_X_ ≅ ⊕_*i* ^pH^i(Rf*∗IC*_X_)[−i]*,
> *and each perverse cohomology sheaf ^pH^i(Rf*∗IC*_X_) is a semisimple perverse sheaf on Y: a direct sum of intersection complexes IC_{Z_a}(L_a)[l_a] supported on the closures Z_a of strata of Y, with L_a simple local systems.*

When *X* is smooth, *IC*_X_ = **Q**_X_[dim X], recovering the formulation for the constant sheaf. If moreover *Y* is a point, the theorem says that *IH*(X)* is a direct sum of pure pieces — the purity of intersection cohomology of projective varieties [2][3].

The **relative hard Lefschetz theorem** refines this: for *f* projective and *η* the first Chern class of a relatively ample line bundle, cup product induces isomorphisms *η^i : ^pH^{−i}(Rf*∗IC*_X_) → ^pH^{+i}(Rf*∗IC*_X_)* for all *i ≥ 0* [1][2][10]. Together, decomposition and relative hard Lefschetz form the "decomposition package," which also yields the relative invariant cycle theorem and the semisimplicity of the monodromy action. Specializing to *Y* a point gives the classical hard Lefschetz isomorphism *−∪η^i : H^{d−i}(X) → H^{d+i}(X)* for smooth projective *X* of dimension *d* [10].

### The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence and D-modules

The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence transports the entire perverse-sheaf apparatus into analysis. For smooth *X*, Kashiwara and Mebkhout proved that the de Rham functor gives an equivalence between regular holonomic D-modules and perverse sheaves [6][8]:

> **Theorem:** (Riemann–Hilbert, Kashiwara–Mebkhout) *The functor DR : Mod_rh(D_X) → Perv(X^an) is an equivalence of abelian categories, extending to a t-exact equivalence D^b_{rh}(D_X) → D^b_c(X^an) of triangulated categories.* [6]

Here **regularity** generalizes regular singularities of ODEs, and **holonomicity** is the finiteness condition making solution sheaves constructible [6][8]. The correspondence identifies the six-functor formalism on D-modules (proper pushforward, duality, nearby/vanishing cycles) with the corresponding topological operations on perverse sheaves, and it identifies Saito's mixed Hodge modules — bifiltered regular holonomic D-modules with compatible weight and Hodge filtrations — with mixed perverse sheaves carrying Hodge data [9]. In this way the analytic proof of the decomposition theorem via mixed Hodge modules [9] and the topological proof [2][3] are two faces of one object.

### The Springer resolution: small maps in action

The decomposition theorem is most transparent for **semismall** maps. Let *f : X → Y* be semismall with *X* smooth of dimension *n*. Then *Rf*∗**Q**_X_[n] is perverse, and its decomposition is controlled by the top-dimensional irreducible components of the fiber product *X ×_Y X*: the endomorphism algebra of the pushforward is spanned by the fundamental classes of these components, and semisimplicity follows from a signature computation on the intersection pairing [2][3].

The **Springer resolution** *μ : T*(G/B) → N* is the flagship example. Here *G* is a complex reductive group, *B* a Borel, *T*(G/B)* the cotangent bundle of the flag variety (the Springer variety, smooth of dimension *2 dim G/B*), and *N ⊂ g* the nilpotent cone. The map *μ* is proper, birational, and *small*: the inequality *2 dim μ^{−1}(x) < codim O_x* is strict for every non-zero nilpotent orbit *O_x* [2]. Consequently

**Rμ*∗Q[dim] ≅ ⊕_{(O,L)} IC_{Ō}(L) ⊗ V_{(O,L)}**,

where the sum runs over pairs of a nilpotent orbit *O* and an irreducible *G*-equivariant local system *L* on *O* (equivalently, an irreducible representation of the component group *A(O)*), and the multiplicity spaces *V_{(O,L)}* carry the action of the Weyl group — this is the **Springer correspondence**, identifying irreducible representations of *W* with geometric data on the nilpotent cone [2].

```python
# Semismallness check for the Springer resolution, following [2][3].
# For each nilpotent orbit O in the nilpotent cone N of a reductive Lie
# algebra g, verify 2*dim(Springer fiber) <= codim(O), with strict
# inequality for O != {0} (smallness).

def springer_data():
    # sl_3 nilpotent orbits: partition, orbit dim, Springer fiber dim
    return [
        {"partition": (1, 1, 1), "orbit_dim": 0, "fiber_dim": 3},  # zero orbit
        {"partition": (2, 1),     "orbit_dim": 4, "fiber_dim": 1},  # minimal orbit
        {"partition": (3,),       "orbit_dim": 6, "fiber_dim": 0},  # regular orbit
    ]

def check_small(orbits, dim_cone=6):
    for o in orbits:
        codim = dim_cone - o["orbit_dim"]
        lhs = 2 * o["fiber_dim"]
        status = "small (strict)" if lhs < codim else ("semismall" if lhs == codim else "FAIL")
        print(f"orbit {o['partition']}: 2*dim fiber = {lhs}, codim = {codim} -> {status}")

check_small(springer_data())
```

Running this confirms the strict inequality at every non-zero orbit — the combinatorial shadow of smallness that makes the Springer decomposition so clean [2].

##5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

There are three complete proofs of the decomposition theorem, and their coexistence is itself evidence for the result's centrality [4].

**1. The arithmetic proof (BBDG, 1982).** Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne, and Gabber work with *ℓ*-adic sheaves on varieties over finite fields [1]. The strategy: Gabber proves that the intersection complex *IC*_X_ is pure of weight 0; Deligne's Weil II machinery shows that the proper pushforward of a pure complex is pure; and a purity argument shows that a pure perverse sheaf over a finite field splits as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves, because *Ext^1* between pure perverse sheaves of the same weight vanishes after passing to the algebraic closure. The statement over **C** follows by spreading out: a complex variety and a proper map are defined over a finitely generated **Z**-algebra, and the decomposition over the algebraic closure of a finite field descends [1][2][10].

**2. Saito's mixed Hodge modules.** Morihiko Saito built an abelian category **MHM(X)** of mixed Hodge modules — roughly, bifiltered regular holonomic D-modules *(M, F, W)* whose graded pieces are polarizable Hodge modules — equipped with a faithful exact functor *rat : D^b(MHM(X)) → D^b_c(X)* landing in perverse sheaves [9]. The decomposition theorem becomes a corollary of the stability of polarizable Hodge modules under proper pushforward and the strictness properties of the Hodge filtration: the direct image of the constant Hodge module decomposes, and applying *rat* yields the topological decomposition with Hodge-theoretic refinements, including the purity of intersection cohomology and Hodge–Lefschetz for *IH** [9]. This proof is analytic and technically formidable, but it gives the strongest statements.

**3. The geometric proof (de Cataldo–Migliorini).** The most recent proof proceeds by induction on the dimension of the image strata and reduces everything to the semismall case, where the decomposition follows from classical Hodge theory on smooth projective varieties [2][3]. The key geometric ingredients are the **refined intersection form** on the fibers, the construction of projectors via correspondences, and an ingenious use of the weak Lefschetz theorem for perverse sheaves (Artin vanishing) to control supports [2][3]. Williamson's Bourbaki exposé gives a lucid account of how this proof "explains" the theorem in purely topological terms, and how its methods adapt to prove Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity for Soergel bimodules [4].

The three proofs agree on all corollaries and reinforce one another: the arithmetic proof gives the result over finite fields and for *ℓ*-adic coefficients; Saito's gives Hodge structures; de Cataldo–Migliorini gives geometric intuition.

> **Theorem:** (Purity of intersection cohomology) *Let X be a projective variety. Then IH^k(X) carries a canonical pure Hodge structure of weight k, and the intersection pairing is a polarization. In particular the odd-degree groups need not vanish, but each IH^k(X) is pure.* [3][9]

##6 Limitations

The decomposition theorem is sharp, and its hypotheses are essential.

- **Properness is necessary.** For non-proper *f*, *Rf*∗IC*_X_ need not decompose; the correct statement involves boundary contributions and weights at infinity [1][2].
- **Characteristic zero (or ℓ-adic) coefficients.** The theorem **fails with modular coefficients**: with **F**_p_-coefficients, the pushforward of the constant sheaf need not be semisimple. The *modular decomposition theorem* of Juteau, Mautner, and Williamson shows that semisimplicity can fail and must be replaced by a *parity sheaf* theory; the decomposition becomes a statement about *p*-canonical bases rather than direct sums of simples [4]. This is not a technicality: it reflects genuine torsion phenomena in the integral topology of Schubert varieties.
- **Singular domain.** If *X* is singular, the constant sheaf must be replaced by *IC*_X_; the theorem for **Q**_X_ on singular *X* is false in general, since **Q**_X_ need not be perverse [2][10].
- **Relative hard Lefschetz needs projectivity.** The decomposition holds for proper maps, but the relative hard Lefschetz isomorphism *η^i : ^pH^{−i} → ^pH^{+i}* requires *f* projective (a relatively ample class); for merely proper maps the Lefschetz operator need not exist [2].
- **Computability.** Intersection complexes are notoriously hard to compute: outside of small resolutions, toric varieties, and Schubert varieties — where Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials govern the stalks — there is no general algorithm for the decomposition of a given pushforward [4][5].

##7 Conclusion

Perverse sheaves reframe the topology of algebraic maps: by truncating the derived category along the middle perversity rather than the standard one, they produce an abelian category whose simples are intersection complexes and in which the pushforward along a proper map *splits canonically into semisimple pieces* [1][2][10]. The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence identifies this topological machine with the analytic theory of regular holonomic D-modules, so that every perverse statement has a differential-equation counterpart and vice versa [6][8]. The three proofs — arithmetic [1], Hodge-theoretic [9], geometric [2][3] — place the theorem at the intersection of number theory, analysis, and topology, with applications from the Springer correspondence to Ngô's support theorem to Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity [4]. The modular failure of semisimplicity [4] reminds us that the theorem is a characteristic-zero miracle, and the search for its motivic and modular refinements is among the liveliest frontiers in geometric representation theory.
