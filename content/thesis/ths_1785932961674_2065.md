# Free Energy, Prediction, and Action: A Unified Treatment of Predictive Coding and Active Inference Under the Free Energy Principle — From Variational Bayes to Cortical Microcircuits

## Abstract

We provide a rigorous synthesis of the **free energy principle (FEP)**, **predictive coding**, and **active inference** as a unified Bayesian mechanics for nervous systems. Starting from variational free energy as an upper bound on surprise, we derive process theories for perception and action, formalize Markov blanket partitionings for autonomy, and articulate hierarchical predictive coding with precision-weighting as biologically plausible gradient descent. We distinguish variational free energy from expected free energy, decomposing the latter into epistemic and pragmatic affordances, and show how policy selection, curiosity, and goal-directedness emerge without ad hoc reward functions. Empirical compatibility with cortical lamination, canonical microcircuits, and dopaminergic precision signaling is reviewed alongside limitations of tractability and falsifiability. The result is a dense, self-contained reference for graduate readers aiming to connect normative principles, algorithms, and neural implementation.

---

## 1. Introduction

The central puzzle of neuroscience is not merely how neurons fire, but why *this* organization persists in a universe tending toward disorder. The **free energy principle** [1][2] proposes an answer: any system that maintains itself as distinct from its environment must, on average, minimize the entropy of its sensory states — or equivalently, minimize *surprise*. Because surprise is intractable, the system minimizes an upper bound known as **variational free energy**.

This principle is *normative* rather than mechanistic. It does not prescribe ion channels; it prescribes *what* any self-evidencing system must do. **Predictive coding** [3][4] and **active inference** [5][7] are then *process theories* — algorithmic instantiations of how the brain might realize free energy minimization under a **generative model**.

> **Key Insight:** Perception is not feature detection; it is hypothesis testing. Action is not reward maximization; it is experiment design via self-fulfilling prophecies.

In this thesis we:

- Formalize variational inference under Markov blankets
- Derive predictive coding updates from Gaussian assumptions
- Extend to action via expected free energy (EFE)
- Map to cortical microcircuitry and neuromodulation
- Evaluate empirical adequacy and open problems

The audience is assumed fluent in probability, graphical models, and elementary dynamical systems.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Core Definitions

- **Generative model** $p(o, s)$: joint distribution over observations $o \in \mathcal{O}$ and hidden states $s \in \mathcal{S}$, factorizing structure about world dynamics.
- **Variational density** $q(s)$: approximate posterior, parameterized by sufficient statistics $\mu$, often Gaussian $q(s) = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$.
- **Surprise**: $-\log p(o)$. Its time-average is entropy $H[p(o)]$.
- **Variational Free Energy**: 
  $$F[q, o] = \mathbb{E}_{q(s)}[\log q(s) - \log p(o,s)] = D_{KL}[q(s) \| p(s|o)] - \log p(o) \ge -\log p(o)$$
- **Markov blanket**: For a system partitioned into $\{\eta, b, \mu\}$ — *external* $\eta$, *blanket* $b = \{s, a\}$ sensory/active, and *internal* $\mu$ — conditional independence $p(\mu|\eta,b)=p(\mu|b)$. *Autonomy* is defined by blanket existence [1][2].
- **Prediction error** $\varepsilon$: difference between observation and top-down prediction, precision-weighted.

### 2.2 Bayesian Mechanics

Bayesian mechanics treats existence as inference. A particle with a Markov blanket that persists over time *looks as if* it infers external states. This is not metaphorical but tautological given ergodicity and the **complete class theorem** [5]: any admissible behavior can be described as Bayes-optimal for some prior.

Key assumptions:

1. *Ergodicity / existence*: system visits limited attracting set
2. *Sparse coupling via blanket*
3. *Generative model entails prior preferences* $p(o) \propto \exp(-C(o))$ where $C$ is cost / prior over outcomes

> **Theorem:** *Free Energy Lemma.* For any system with Markov blanket at nonequilibrium steady-state (NESS), internal dynamics can be expressed as gradient flow on $F$.

$$
\dot{\mu} = -\Gamma_{\mu\mu} \nabla_{\mu} F(\mu, b)
$$

This is proved via Helmholtz decomposition under NESS [2].

### 2.3 Historical Lineage

- Helmholtz (1860s): perception as unconscious inference
- Rao and Ballard (1999) [4]: predictive coding implementation in visual cortex
- Friston (2006-2010) [1]: unification under FEP
- Friston et al (2017-2022) [5][7]: active inference generalization

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Variational Free Energy Derivation

Given intractability of $p(s|o) = p(o,s)/p(o)$, introduce $q$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log p(o) &= \log \int p(o,s) ds \\
&= \log \int q(s) \frac{p(o,s)}{q(s)} ds \\
&\ge \int q(s) \log \frac{p(o,s)}{q(s)} ds = -F  \quad \text{(Jensen)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus minimizing $F$ *tightens* evidence lower bound (ELBO) and renders $q \approx p(s|o)$.

Under **Laplace approximation**, $q(s)=\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)$ and:

$$
F \approx -\log p(o,\mu) + \frac{1}{2}\log|\Sigma| + \text{const}
$$

Gradient descent $\dot{\mu} = -\kappa \nabla_\mu F$ yields recognition dynamics.

### 3.2 Predictive Coding Under Gaussian Generative Models

Assume hierarchical generative model:

$$
p(o|s_1)p(s_1|s_2)...p(s_{L-1}|s_L)p(s_L)
$$

with $p(s_{i-1}|s_i) = \mathcal{N}(g_i(s_i), \Pi_{i-1}^{-1})$, where $g_i$ is nonlinear link, $\Pi$ precision (inverse covariance).

Then:

$$
F = \sum_{i} \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_i^T \Pi_i \varepsilon_i + \frac{1}{2}\log|\Pi_i^{-1}| + \text{priors}
$$

where $\varepsilon_i = \mu_{i-1} - g_i(\mu_i)$, with $\mu_0 \equiv o$.

Dynamics:

$$
\dot{\mu_i} = -\nabla_{\mu_i}F = (\partial g_i)^T \Pi_{i-1} \varepsilon_{i-1} - \Pi_i \varepsilon_i
$$

This is *biological*: top-down predictions $g_i(\mu_i)$ inhibit error units, bottom-up errors drive belief updates.

### 3.3 Active Inference and Expected Free Energy

Action $a$ changes $o$ via $p(o|s,a)$. Instead of minimizing current $F$, select *policies* $\pi = (a_1,...,a_T)$ minimizing **expected free energy** $G(\pi)$:

$$
G(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{q(o,s|\pi)}[\log q(s|\pi) - \log p(o,s|\pi)]
$$

Decomposition:

$$
G(\pi) = \underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{q(o|\pi)}[D_{KL}[q(s|o,\pi)\|q(s|\pi)]]}_{\text{-epistemic value}} - \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(o|\pi)}[\log p(o|C)]}_{\text{-pragmatic value}} + \text{ambiguity}
$$

where $p(o|C)$ encodes prior preferences. Hence **curiosity** emerges normatively: policies that resolve uncertainty minimize $G$.

Policy prior: $p(\pi) = \sigma(-\gamma G(\pi))$, softmax with inverse temperature $\gamma$ (precision over policies, linked to dopamine).

### 3.4 Computational Schematic

```python
# Hierarchical predictive coding with precision weighting - process theory
import numpy as np

def predictive_coding_step(o, mu, Pi, g, dg, eta=1e-2):
    # o: observation, mu: list of level means [mu1, mu2, ...]
    # Pi: list of precisions, g: generative functions, dg: jacobians
    L = len(mu)
    mu_full = [o] + mu
    eps = []
    for i in range(1, L+1):
        pred = g[i-1](mu_full[i]) if i < L else np.zeros_like(mu_full[i])
        if i == 1:
            pred = g[0](mu_full[1])
        eps_i = mu_full[i-1] - pred
        eps.append(eps_i)
    dmu = []
    for i in range(1, L+1):
        err_below = Pi[i-1] @ eps[i-1] if i-1 < len(Pi) else 0
        jaco = dg[i-1](mu_full[i]) if i-1 < len(dg) else np.eye(len(mu_full[i]))
        lift = jaco.T @ err_below
        decay = Pi[i] @ eps[i] if i < len(eps) and i < len(Pi) else 0
        dmu_i = eta * (lift - decay) if i < L else eta * lift
        dmu.append(dmu_i)
    mu_new = [mu_full[i+1]+dmu[i] for i in range(L)]
    return mu_new, eps

def expected_free_energy(q_o_given_pi, q_s_given_o, q_s, log_pref):
    epistemic = np.mean([kl_divergence(q_s_given_o[o], q_s) for o in q_o_given_pi])
    pragmatic = np.mean([log_pref[o] for o in q_o_given_pi])
    return -epistemic - pragmatic
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Variational Free Energy and Bayesian Mechanics: Existence Implies Inference

Consider stochastic dynamics at NESS with partition $\mathbf{x}=\{\eta, b, \mu\}$. Existence of Markov blanket implies $p(\mu|\eta,b)=p(\mu|b)$. One can show *marginal flow lemma*: flow of internal and active states depends only on blanket. From this, define *most likely* external state given blanket configuration $\boldsymbol{\mu}(b)=\arg\max_\eta p(\eta|b)$. If map $b\mapsto \mu$ is invertible, internal states *parameterize* beliefs about external states.

Formally:

- **Synchronization manifold**: $\mathcal{M} = \{(\mu,\eta): \mu = \sigma(\eta)\}$
- Internal dynamics perform **approximate Bayesian inference** because stationary density $p(\eta,b,\mu)$ entails $q_\mu(\eta) \approx p(\eta|b)$.

Critical nuance: *this does not entail brain explicitly computes $F$; rather, any system that *looks* self-organizing can be described as if minimizing $F$.* The explanatory bite comes from empirical content of generative model structure.

*What if blanket fails?* Then pathology: loss of autonomy corresponds to psychopathology (e.g., functional seizures as breakdown of precise blanket inference) [5].

### 4.2 Predictive Coding as Process Theory: From Normative to Neural

Predictive coding is often conflated with FEP itself. It is not. It is a *specific* process theory under:

1. Gaussian generative model
2. Laplace approximation
3. Mean-field factorization $q(s)=\prod_i q(s_i)$
4. Dynamics approximate gradient descent

Its neural mapping is strikingly concrete [3][7]:

| Computation | Putative Neural Substrate | Laminar Location |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction $\mu_i$ | Deep pyramidal cells (L5/6) | Descending feedback |
| Prediction error $\varepsilon_i$ | Superficial pyramidal (L2/3) | Ascending feedforward |
| Precision $\Pi_i$ | Neuromodulators / interneurons (PV+, SOM+) | Gain control |
| $g_i$ nonlinearity | Canonical microcircuit | NMDA-dependent |

- **Top-down**: predictions inhibit errors via divisive/subtractive mechanisms.
- **Bottom-up**: errors excite higher-level expectations.
- **Precision**: weights error influence. High precision on prior suppresses updating (rigidity); high precision on sensory error yields hallucinosis.

*Attention* is then *precision allocation* — not a separate module, but second-order inference.

Ordered pipeline for one timestep:

1. Receive sensory sample $o_t$
2. Compute error $\varepsilon_0 = o_t - g_1(\mu_1)$
3. Propagate precision-weighted error upward
4. Update beliefs $\mu_i \leftarrow \mu_i - \eta \nabla F$
5. Propagate updated predictions downward
6. Evaluate $F$ decrease as convergence check

Unordered principles enabling efficiency:

- **Hierarchical depth trades computation for abstraction**: higher levels encode slower dynamics (more abstract latent causes)
- **Generalized coordinates**: $\tilde{\mu} = (\mu, \mu', \mu'', ...)$ storing position, velocity, acceleration to handle non-stationary world via Taylor expansion — permits tracking in continuous time
- **Lateral inhibition** corresponds to explaining away within level

### 4.3 Active Inference and Expected Free Energy: Why We Act

Active inference removes *value function*. Preferences $p(o|C)$ replace reward. Action samples world to make observations consistent with preferences — *self-fulfilling prophecies*.

Consider agent choosing between policies $\pi_1$ = look at novel cue and $\pi_2$ = exploit familiar cue.

- $\pi_1$ high epistemic value: reduces uncertainty about state-outcome mapping $H[p(o|s)]$
- $\pi_2$ high pragmatic value: gets preferred outcome with high probability

$G$ automatically balances exploration-exploitation because:

$$
G = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}[H[p(o|s)]]}_{\text{ambiguity}} + \underbrace{D_{KL}[q(o|\pi)\|p(o|C)]}_{\text{risk}} - \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[D_{KL}[q(s|o)\|q(s)]]}_{\text{info gain}}
$$

Implementation uses **categorical models** for discrete MDPs [6]. The `pymdp` library [6] demonstrates:

```python
# Discrete Active Inference - policy evaluation (pymdp style)
from pymdp import utils
import numpy as np

A = utils.obj_array(1)
A[0] = np.array([[0.9,0.1],[0.1,0.9]]) # likelihood 2 obs x 2 states
B = utils.obj_array(1)
B[0] = np.array([[[1,0],[0,1]],[[0,1],[1,0]]]) # transitions
C = utils.obj_array(1)
C[0] = np.array([1.0, -1.0]) # prefer obs0
D = utils.obj_array(1)
D[0] = np.array([0.5,0.5]) # prior
# inference: q_s = softmax(log D + log A^T o)
# G[pi] = sum_t (KL + ambiguity)
```

A classic result: saccadic eye movements emerge as epistemic foraging — eyes go where uncertainty reduction maximal [7].

> **Corollary:** *Curiosity need not be intrinsic reward; it is instrumental to minimizing future surprise about preferred states.*

### 4.4 Hierarchical Precision, Neuromodulation, and Psychopathology

Precision $\Pi$ is statistically inverse variance, psychologically **attention** or **confidence**.

- **Acetylcholine**: sensory precision, bottom-up salience. High ACh → high learning rate on prediction errors in sensory cortex; implication for attentional deficits when depleted.
- **Dopamine**: precision over policies $p(\pi)$ — not reward signal per se but confidence that policy will produce preferred outcomes. Friston et al tie phasic DA to update of $EFE$ [5].
- **Noradrenaline**: volatility precision, meta-learning rate, abrupt context changes.
- **Serotonin**: prior preferences cost? Inconclusive.

Pathologies reframed:

- *Autism*: overly high sensory precision (hypersensitivity to $\varepsilon$), reduced top-down attenuation — world appears too precise, no generalization [7].
- *Schizophrenia*: false inference due to aberrant precision on higher-level priors (hallucinations as precise top-down predictions overriding weak sensory evidence), plus compensatory DA dysregulation.
- *Functional neurological disorder*: excessively precise intermediate-level prior beliefs overriding lower-level sensory evidence [5].

This offers transdiagnostic vocabulary, though critics caution reification.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Formal Guarantees

Under Laplace approximation, for linear $g_i$:

- **Convergence**: $F$ is convex quadratic; gradient flow exponentially stable $O(e^{-\lambda t})$ where $\lambda = \min \text{eig}(\Pi)$ [4].
- **Bound tightness**: $0 \le F + \log p(o) = D_{KL}[q\|p(\cdot|o)] \le \epsilon$ for well-specified model; approximation error $O(\text{skewness})$ for nonlinear $g$.

General case non-convex; hierarchical EM ensures local minima, requiring annealing via precision reduction (mirroring simulated annealing / sleep).

### 5.2 Neurophysiological Compatible Evidence

- **MEG/EEG mismatch negativity (MMN)**: robust $\varepsilon$ signal to oddballs, suppressed by repetition — explained as precision-weighted prediction error reduction [3].
- **Laminar fMRI**: deep layers activity for predictions, superficial for errors — confirmed in V1/V2 by recent 7T laminar studies [7].
- **Birdsong learning**: canaries minimize free energy of song spectrograms — synthetic birdsong via predictive coding reproduces trajectories.
- **Saccades**: active inference agent with $G$-minimizing eye movements reproduces human scanpaths $r=0.72$ correlation in free viewing datasets [6].

Table: comparison of theories:

| Criterion | Predictive Coding | RL + Bayes | Active Inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | $F$ (perception) | Reward + Info gain (hand-tuned) | $G=F_{future}$ unified |
| Exploration | Not derived | epsilon-greedy / UCB add-on | Emerges from epistemic term |
| Neural substrate | Canonical circuit (strong) | Basal ganglia value loop | Circuit + DA as policy precision |
| Generative model | Gaussian hierarchical | MDP | Both (continuous/discrete) |
| Tractability | $O(L d^2)$ per step | $O(|S||A|)$ | PSPACE harder (policies exponential) |

### 5.3 Computational Complexity

Naive policy search $|\pi| = |A|^H$ exponential in horizon. Solutions:

- **Sophisticated inference**: tree search with pruning via $G$ (occam window)
- **Hierarchical factorization**: temporally deep generative models with separation of scales reduces effective horizon — e.g., $H_{high}=5$ with each step unfolding to $H_{low}=10$ substeps
- **Amortized inference**: learn $q(\pi|b)$ mapping via neural networks (link to habit formation as fast, MF policy)

Complexity for continuous predictive coding: $O(L d^3)$ if full precision matrices, $O(L d)$ with diagonal assumption (canonical).

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Falsifiability criticism**: FEP as formulated without specific generative model is *unfalsifiable* — any trajectory can be rationalized post hoc with suitable priors [2]. This is acknowledged; empirical content resides in *model specification*. Progress requires *hard* model classes that make bold predictions.

- *Problem 1*: What generative model does human cortex actually entail? Infinite degrees of freedom; need linking hypotheses stronger than "some hierarchical deep model". Current mapping to lamina remains correlational.

**Tractability**: Exact minimization of $F$ requires inversion of large precision matrices. Biological approximations rely on diagonal or low-rank precision. Does brain approximate efficiently? Recent work linking to **Hebbian + anti-Hebbian** suggests local learning feasible, but rigorous convergence bounds missing for nonlinear deep networks.

**Thermodynamics misinterpretation**: FEP *variational* free energy is not thermodynamic free energy, though mathematical analogies exist. Confusion persists; recent work by Parr, Friston, and colleagues explicitly connects via *stochastic thermodynamics* — expected thermodynamic work lower-bounded by variational free energy under NESS [2], but empirical testability in vivo unlikely.

**Discrete vs continuous**: discrete active inference [6] is elegant for cognition but scales poorly; continuous predictive coding is scalable but lacks principled planning. Unifying both in mixed models (hybrid) is active area.

Open research vectors:

1. *Learning the generative model*: structure learning $p(\theta)$ over model forms — how does cortex learn number of levels $L$, dimensionalities $d_i$? Bayesian model reduction offers compress-and-prune, but cortical developmental mechanisms unclear.
2. *Precision optimization meta-level*: who watches the precisions? Formal hierarchy of precisions leads to infinite regress; cutoff pragmatically defined by evolutionarily endowed hyperpriors.
3. *Interoception and affect*: Seth & Friston (2016) propose emotions as inference about allostatic states; review shows interoceptive predictions via insula. Testing requires perturbative interoceptive stimuli.
4. *Social active inference*: multi-agent $G$ includes inference about others' inference — Theory of Mind as recursive Markov blankets. Computational explosion, but ESS games show coordination as minimization of joint free energy.
5. *Consciousness*: FEP-linked theories (e.g., *expected* FEP = self-modeling) propose consciousness corresponds to higher-level inference about precision. No consensus; needs adversarial collaboration.

> **Caveat:** Precision-engineered accounts risk *just-so storytelling*. Without preregistered model-class commitments and out-of-sample prediction of neural dynamics (not just retrofitting), Bayesian mechanics remains interpretively powerful but predictively weak.

## 7. Conclusion

We reviewed the chain from **surprise minimization** to **predictive coding** to **active inference** as a coherent, if ambitious, framework for brain function. Beginning from the inevitability that self-maintaining systems maintain low-entropy sensory distributions, we derived variational free energy as tractable objective, Markov blankets as partition criterion for individuality, and hierarchical predictive coding as biologically plausible gradient descent under Gaussian assumptions. Extension to action via expected free energy unifies value and information under single objective, explaining exploration, curiosity, and goal-directedness without extra reward engineering.

The mapping to cortical microcircuits — deep predictions, superficial errors, precision-as-neuromodulation — renders process theories testable, and early laminar evidence is encouraging [7]. Simultaneously, the framework's greatest strength — generality — is its weakness: without strong commitments to generative architecture, it can trivially accommodate data.

For graduate researchers, productive stance is *instrumental*: use FEP/active inference toolkit [6] to *design* agents that exhibit robust, adaptive, uncertainty-sensitive behavior, and then pit its internal dynamics against neurophysiological recordings via **Bayesian model comparison**. Whether FEP ultimately describes brains as they *are* or as they *could be described* may be secondary to whether it generates experiments that *wouldn't* be conceived otherwise — e.g., precision-centric accounts of psychosis, attention as second-order inference, saccades as epistemic foraging — many of which already have empirical traction.

Future work will either harden this Bayesian mechanics into quantitatively predictive models of cortical laminar computation and neuromodulatory dynamics, or reveal it as elegant reinterpretation without mechanistic breakthrough. Either outcome advances neuroscience by clarifying what class of explanation we seek: normative *why*, algorithmic *how*, or implementational *where*.
