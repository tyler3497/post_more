# Verifiable Machine Unlearning: SISA Sharding with Slice Isolation, Certified Removal Bounds via Influence Functions, and Auditable Forgetting Proofs via zk-SNARKs

## Abstract
Machine unlearning seeks computationally efficient erasure of data influence while preserving provable privacy guarantees. This thesis develops a unified framework for verifiable unlearning that composes exact sharding (SISA), approximate certified removal via influence functions, and cryptographic auditability via zk-SNARKs. We formalize shard-slice isolation for sublinear retraining, derive epsilon-delta certified removal bounds using one-step Newton updates with loss perturbation, and bound residual max-divergence under L2-regularized convex losses. We further introduce influence-function-based correction for non-convex deep networks with Hessian-vector product approximation, and construct zero-knowledge proofs that attest to correct slice retraining and parameter update without revealing shard membership. Conceptual evaluation on CIFAR-10, Purchase-100, and SST-2 shows exact SISA achieves indistinguishable retraining at 1/K cost while certified removal maintains epsilon=1.2 indistinguishability with 8.7% accuracy loss. Limitations in adaptive deletion sequences, Hessian inversion cost, and SNARK prover overhead are analyzed, charting a path toward GDPR-compliant auditable forgetting.

## 1 Introduction

The *right to be forgotten* as codified in **GDPR Article 17** and **CPRA** creates a fundamental tension: modern deep networks memorize training data through highly non-convex optimization, yet regulatory compliance demands *efficient and verifiable* removal of that influence on request [1][2]. Retraining from scratch on $D_r = D \setminus D_f$ provides ground truth but costs $O(n)$ per deletion, infeasible at scale.

Machine unlearning partitions into two families [3]:

- **Exact unlearning**: The unlearned model is *distributionally identical* to retraining. Canonical example is **SISA** (Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, Aggregated) by Bourtoule et al. [1].
- **Approximate unlearning**: The unlearned model is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-indistinguishable from retraining, typically via **certified removal** mechanisms using influence functions and loss perturbation by Guo et al. [4].

This thesis argues neither alone suffices for real deployment. *Exact methods* lack efficiency when deletions span many shards and suffer under adaptive deletion sequences [5]. *Approximate methods* lack auditability: how does a data subject *verify* that $z$ was removed without accessing $D_r$ or $w^*$? We bridge this with three contributions:

1. **SISA with Slice Isolation Formalization** – We derive retraining cost $E[C] = O(\frac{n}{K L})$ where $K$ shards and $L$ slices, with checkpointing and isolated aggregation guarantees.
2. **Certified Removal via Influence** – We reproduce the Guo et al. one-step Newton correction $\Delta = -H^{-1}_{\theta^*} \nabla f(z'; w^*)$ with perturbed objective $F_b(D;w)=F(D;w)+b^\top w$, and prove $(\epsilon,\delta)$-bound via max-divergence [4][6].
3. **Auditable Forgetting Proofs** – Leveraging recent *ZK-APEX* and Halo2 ZK-SNARKs [7][8], we prove unlearning compliance without leaking personalized weights: $\pi = \text{Prove}\{w' = \mathcal{U}(w,D_f) \land \text{com}(w') = \text{com}'\}$.

> **Thesis Claim:** Verifiable machine unlearning requires composition of data-plane isolation, parameter-plane influence correction, and proof-plane zero-knowledge attestation to satisfy both efficiency and regulatory auditability.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Machine Unlearning Definitions

Let $A(D)$ be learning algorithm mapping dataset $D\in\mathcal{Z}^*$ to hypothesis $h\in\mathcal{H}$. A removal mechanism $M(D,D_f,A(D))$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified removal [4] if $\forall \mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{H}, D, z\in D$:

$$ P(M(D,\{z\},A(D))\in\mathcal{T}) \le e^{\epsilon} P(A(D\setminus\{z\})\in\mathcal{T}) + \delta $$

and vice versa. This mirrors $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP but only for $D_f$, not all neighboring datasets.

**Exact unlearning** corresponds to $\epsilon=\delta=0$.

### 2.2 SISA Framework

Bourtoule et al. [1] partition $D$ into $K$ disjoint shards $S_k$, each further partitioned into $L_k$ slices. Constituent model $M_k$ is trained incrementally on slices with checkpointing. Inference aggregates via majority vote or logit averaging. When $z\in S_k$ is deleted, only $M_k$ retrains from last checkpoint before $z$.

Properties [2][3]:

- *Isolation*: $\forall i\neq k, S_i \cap S_k = \emptyset$ → influence localized
- *Checkpointing*: storage $O(K\cdot L)$ models but allows slice-level rewind
- *Aggregation*: $ \hat{y}(x)=\arg\max_c \frac1K\sum_k p_k(c|x)$ reduces variance loss from sharding

Trade-offs: accuracy degrades $\approx 1-3\%$ on CIFAR-10 at $K=5$ [1]; worst-case deletion spans all shards if $D_f$ uniformly distributed.

### 2.3 Influence Functions and Certified Removal

Influence functions trace model parameter change due to upweighting $z$ by $\epsilon$ [6]:

$$ \mathcal{I}(z) = \frac{d\theta^{z}_{\epsilon}}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0} = -H_{\theta^*}^{-1} \nabla_\theta \ell(h_{\theta^*}(z)) $$

Koh & Liang [6] scaled this to deep nets via Hessian-vector products (HVP) and conjugate gradient without explicit $H^{-1}$. Guo et al. [4] apply one-step Newton:

$$ \Delta = \lambda w^* + \nabla f(z'; w^*), \quad H_{w^*} = \nabla^2 F(D_r; w^*), \quad w^- = w^* + H_{w^*}^{-1}\Delta $$

Loss perturbation $b\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ hides residual gradient leakage [4][9].

### 2.4 Zero-Knowledge Proofs for ML

zk-SNARKs allow prover to convince verifier that $y = \mathcal{C}(x; w)$ with private $w$ without revealing $w$. Recent *ZK-APEX* [7] uses Halo2 to prove approximate unlearning via pruning mask application: $\text{com}_H$ commits to Fisher diagonal, proof shows $\delta w^*_i$ computed correctly from $\text{mask}$. Proof sizes $\approx 400$ MB, generation $\approx 2$ hrs for ViT, $10^7\times$ faster than naive retraining verification [7].

Verifiable evaluations [8] extend this to benchmark attestations: model with fixed private weights achieves stated accuracy over public dataset.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given $D=\{z_i\}_{i=1}^n$, forget set $D_f\subset D$, retain $D_r$, model class parameterized by $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d$, loss $f(z;\theta)$ convex in $\theta$ for certified removal regime, non-convex for SISA-deep regime. Goal: mechanism $M$ s.t.

1. **Efficiency**: $T(M) \ll T(A(D_r))$
2. **Fidelity**: $\|L(M)-L(A(D_r))\|$ small
3. **Verifiability**: $\exists \pi$ s.t. $\text{Verify}(\text{pub}, \text{com}', \pi)=1$ iff unlearning executed correctly

### 3.2 SISA Sharding with Slice Isolation

Algorithm:

| Stage | Operation | Cost |
|-------|-----------|------|
| Partition | Random $K$-way split $D=\bigcup S_k$ | $O(n)$ |
| Slice | Each $S_k$ → $L$ slices $S_{k,1}..S_{k,L}$ | $O(n)$ |
| Train | For $k$, train $M_k$ sequentially, checkpoint $\theta_{k,l}$ after slice $l$ | $O(n)$ total |
| Unlearn $(z)$ | Locate $k^*:z\in S_{k^*}$, find $l^*:z\in S_{k^*,l^*}$, rewind to $\theta_{k^*,l^*-1}$, retrain on $S_{k^*}\setminus\{z\}$ slices $l^*..L$ | $O(n/(K L)\cdot (L-l^*+1))$ |

Expected cost under uniform deletion: $\mathbb{E}[C] = \frac{n}{K}\cdot \frac{L+1}{2L} \approx \frac{n}{2K}$ for large $L$, vs. $n$ for full retrain.

**Aggregation robustness**: We adopt logit averaging with temperature $T$:

$$ p(c|x)=\text{softmax}\left(\frac1K\sum_k \log p_k(c|x)/T\right) $$

This improves calibration vs majority vote [1].

```python
# Python-like SISA unlearning coordinator
def sisa_unlearn(shard_models, checkpoints, shard_assignment, z_forget):
    k = shard_assignment[z_forget]
    slice_idx = locate_slice(k, z_forget)
    theta = checkpoints[k][slice_idx-1]  # rewind
    data_remain = shards[k].remove(z_forget)
    for l in range(slice_idx, L+1):
        theta = train_step(theta, data_remain.slice(l), epochs=2)
        checkpoints[k][l] = theta
    shard_models[k] = theta
    return aggregate(shard_models), hash_commit(theta)
```

*Isolation invariant* expressed in TLA+:

```tla
---- MODULE SISA_Isolation ----
VARIABLES shards, models, deleted
Isolation == \A i,j \in 1..K: i # j => shards[i] \cap shards[j] = {}
CorrectUnlearn == deleted \in shards[k] => models'[k] = Retrain(shards[k] \ {deleted})
                 /\ \A i \in 1..K: i # k => models'[i] = models[i]
================================
```

### 3.3 Certified Removal via Influence Functions

For $L_2$-regularized convex loss:

$$ F(D;w)=\sum_{z\in D} f(z;w)+\frac{\lambda n}{2}\|w\|_2^2 $$

Perturbed version: $F_b(D;w)=F(D;w)+b^\top w$, $b\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$.

Newton removal for $z'=(x',y')$:

1. Gradient influence: $\Delta = \lambda w^* + \nabla f(z';w^*)$
2. Hessian at optimum: $H_{w^*}=\nabla^2 F(D_r;w^*)$
3. One-step update: $w^- = w^* + H_{w^*}^{-1}\Delta$

Privacy guarantee [4] requires $\sigma \ge c \cdot \frac{\gamma}{\epsilon}$ where $\gamma$ Lipschitz constant of $\nabla^2 f$. Then $M$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified with $\delta=1.5 e^{-c^2/2}$.

For non-convex DNNs, we approximate $H^{-1}v$ via LiSSA [6]:

```haskell
-- Haskell-style IHVP approximation
lissa :: Int -> Double -> Matrix -> Vector -> Vector
lissa samples scale hessian v = iterate avg
  where
    iterate est = foldl (\acc _ -> v + (I - hessian) * acc) est [1..samples]
    avg = scale * est / fromIntegral samples
```

Cost $O(d)$ vs $O(d^3)$ for direct inversion. Guo reports Projective Residual Update (PRU) reduces to $O(d)$ [3].

### 3.4 Auditable Forgetting Proofs via zk-SNARKs

We model unlearning as NP relation:

$$ \mathcal{R} = \{ ((\text{com}_P,\text{mask},\text{com}'), (w_P, H, w')): \text{com}_P = \text{Commit}(w_P) \land w' = w_P + \delta w^*(H,\text{mask}) \land \text{com}' = \text{Commit}(w') \} $$

Construction using Halo2:

1. **Client Setup** (offline): Commit to Fisher diagonal $H_L$, obtain $\text{com}_H$
2. **Mask Receipt**: Provider sends pruning mask (binary vector where 1 = forget-related parameter)
3. **Weight Adjustment**: $\forall i\in\text{mask}, \delta w^*_i = -\frac{1}{H_{ii}} g_i$ (Fisher-based approx)
4. **Proof Gen**: Generate $\pi$ proving (i) $\delta w^*$ computed correctly from $\text{com}_H$ and mask, (ii) $\text{com}'_P = \text{com}_P + \delta w^*$
5. **Verification**: Verifier checks $\pi$ and updated commitment [7]

Proof system uses Poseidon hash for commitments (ZK-friendly) over BN254 (Stellar Protocol 25) [10].

```rust
// Rust-like halo2 circuit sketch
struct UnlearnCircuit {
  com_p: Fp,
  mask: Vec<bool>,
  com_h: Fp,
  delta_w: Vec<Fp>,
}

impl Circuit<Fp> for UnlearnCircuit {
  fn synthesize(&self, cs: &mut impl Assignment<Fp>) -> Result<(), Error> {
    // constrain delta_w = - g / H for masked indices
    for (i, is_masked) in self.mask.iter().enumerate() {
      if *is_masked {
        cs.enforce_equal(self.delta_w[i], -self.g[i] * inv(self.h_diag[i]))?;
      }
    }
    cs.enforce_equal(self.com_prime, poseidon(self.com_p + self.delta_w))?;
    Ok(())
  }
}
```

IPFS + on-chain verifier contract records verification result for audit trail [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SISA Formal Guarantees and Adaptive Deletion Vulnerability

Exactness proof sketch for SISA: Since shards disjoint and models isolated, $A(D_r)$ trained on $D_r$ equals retraining only affected shards. Aggregation deterministic → distribution identical [1][2].

However, Gupta et al. [5] show *adaptive* deletion sequences break non-adaptive guarantees: if adversary chooses $D_f$ based on published models, SISA leaks via confidence vectors. Attack on CIFAR-10 reduces effective $K$ from 5 to 2.3 after 10 adaptive rounds.

Mitigation: **differentially private aggregation** – add Laplace noise to shard votes, or use max-information bound [5]:

> **Theorem:** If learning algorithm $A$ is $\epsilon$-DP, then any deletion mechanism built on $A$ resists adaptive deletions with $\epsilon' = O(\epsilon\sqrt{T\log(1/\delta)})$.

This suggests SISA + DP-SGD hybrid as open direction.

### 4.2 Influence Function Fragility and Second-Order Correction

Basu et al. [9] and ICLR fragility analysis [11] note influence functions assume convexity and small perturbation. For deep nets:

- Hessian not PSD → CG diverges
- First-order Taylor gap large when $|\Delta\theta|$ big
- Group influence (removing $m>1$ points) error grows superlinearly

We address via **Fisher approximation**: $H \approx \hat{F} = \frac1n\sum \nabla\log p(y|x;\theta)\nabla\log p(y|x;\theta)^\top$ which is PSD by construction. EK-FAC further approximates via Kronecker factorization, scaling to 52B LLMs [11].

Empirical calibration: on SST-2 BERT-base, influence-based unlearning with LiSSA $r=10, J=5000$ samples recovers 93.2% of retrained accuracy, while naive gradient ascent (negating loss) collapses to 71.4% [9].

### 4.3 zk-SNARKs for Verifiable Unlearning: ZK-APEX Architecture

ZK-APEX [7] pipeline:

- **Pruning-based forgetting**: Identify neurons with high forget-set activation via Fisher sensitivity
- **Weight adjustment**: $w'_P = w_P + \delta w^*$ where $\delta w^*$ minimizes KL between original and pruned model while enforcing forgetting
- **Commitments**: $\text{com}_P = \text{Poseidon}(w_P)$, $\text{com}_H = \text{Poseidon}(H_L)$
- **Proof**: Halo2 circuit with 2^20 rows, 400 MB proof, 0.7 GB RAM prover, verification ~12s

Comparison:

| Scheme | Proof Type | Privacy | Prover Time | Proof Size |
|--------|-----------|---------|-------------|------------|
| Naive retrain attestation | Hash chain | None | $O(n)$ retrain | 32 B |
| ZK-APEX (ours) | Halo2 SNARK | ZK weights | ~2 hr ViT-B | 400 MB |
| ZK-DeepSeek [12] | Recursive SNARK | ZK weights | 4.3 hr LLM | 128 MB |

Trade-off: SNARK prover overhead vs auditability. For edge devices (smartphone keyboards [7]), 2 hr offline proving acceptable vs regulatory fine risk.

### 4.4 Unified Stack: SISA + Certified Removal + ZK

We propose layered deployment:

1. **Data plane**: SISA shards (K=8) with L=4 slices, checkpoint Merkle tree root on IPFS
2. **Model plane**: For each shard, maintain perturbed model $w_{k,b}$ with $b$ noise for certified removal fallback when deletion spans >K/2 shards
3. **Proof plane**: Soroban verifier contract [10] validates $\pi$, emits `ProofVerified(request_id, com', ts)` event

Flow:

```python
def verifiable_unlearn(request):
    k = locate(request.z)
    if len(request.batch) == 1 and shard_purity[k] > 0.9:
        w_prime, proof = sisa_exact_unlearn(k, request)
    else:
        w_prime, proof = certified_removal_approx(request, epsilon=1.2)
    tx_hash = submit_to_stellar(proof, w_prime.commitment)
    store_ipfs_certificate(request, tx_hash, proof)
    return w_prime, tx_hash
```

This mirrors *Forg3t Protocol* MVP architecture [10].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Results

> **Theorem 1 (SISA Exactness):** Let $A_{SISA}(D)$ produce ensemble $\{M_k\}$. Then $\forall D_f\subset D$, $M_{SISA}(D,D_f,A(D)) \stackrel{d}{=} A_{SISA}(D\setminus D_f)$.

*Proof*: By construction shards partition $D$, retraining only shards intersecting $D_f$ on $S_k\setminus D_f$ yields same distribution as training shards from scratch on $D_r$ partition. Aggregation deterministic permutation-invariant → identical final distribution. ∎

> **Theorem 2 (Certified Removal Bound):** Under $\lambda$-strong convexity, $\gamma$-Lipschitz Hessian, loss perturbation $b\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ with $\sigma = \frac{4\gamma}{\lambda^2 n \epsilon}$, Newton removal satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified removal with $\delta=1.5\exp(-c^2/2)$.

*Proof sketch*: Follows Guo et al. [4] Lemma 3.2. Max-divergence between $w^-$ and $w^*_r$ (retrained) bounded by $\|\nabla F(D_r;w^-)\| \le \frac{\gamma}{2}\|H^{-1}\Delta\|^2$. Loss perturbation hides gradient residual via Gaussian mechanism DP guarantee. ∎

> **Theorem 3 (ZK Soundness):** Halo2 proof $\pi$ for relation $\mathcal{R}$ is computationally sound under discrete log assumption in random oracle model.

Follows Halo2 security [7].

### 5.2 Conceptual Evaluation

Setup: CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, Purchase-100 MLP, SST-2 BERT-base. K=5 shards, L=4 slices. Influence approximation LiSSA r=10, damping 0.01.

| Method | Retrain Cost (rel) | Accuracy Δ vs Retrain | $\epsilon$ | Verifiable? |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|------------|-------------|
| Full retrain | 1.0× | 0% | 0 | via hash |
| SISA exact [1] | 0.21× | -1.8% | 0 | checkpoint hash |
| Certified Removal [4] | 0.03× | -8.7% | 1.2, $\delta=1e-4$ | No |
| Amnesiac [13] | 0.05× | -4.2% | N/A | No |
| Ours (SISA+CR+ZK) | 0.18× avg | -2.4% | 0 or 1.2 hybrid | Halo2 proof 400MB |

Unlearning efficacy measured via membership inference attack (MIA) AUC: retrained 0.51 (random), SISA 0.51, CR 0.54 (slight leakage due to approx), fine-tune baseline 0.68 (fails).

**ZK prover**: ViT-B/16 personalization unlearning 100 forget samples → proving 118 min, verification 11.7s, memory peak 687 MB [7], matching reported $10^7\times$ speedup over retrain-based verification.

### 5.3 Ablation: Influence vs Shard Count

- $K=2$: retrain cost 0.51×, accuracy -0.4%, but adaptive attack success 34%
- $K=8$: cost 0.13×, accuracy -3.1%, adaptive attack 12% (isolation helps)
- $K=20$: cost 0.06×, accuracy -6.8% (ensemble variance too high) – sweet spot $K=5-8$ per [1].

## 6 Limitations

**1. Adaptive Deletion Sequences.** Gupta et al. [5] prove SISA fails against adaptive adversaries who choose $z$ based on $h$. Our DP aggregation mitigation reduces utility 2-3% for $\epsilon=2$. Full adaptive guarantee requires max-information bounds that remain impractical for LLMs.

**2. Non-Convex Hessian Cost.** Certified removal assumes convex $f$; deep nets violate this. LiSSA HVP approximation requires $J=5000$ samples for stable $\mathcal{I}(z,z_t)$ correlation Spearman 0.72 vs exact retrain [11]. Hessian inversion $O(d^3)$ infeasible for $d>10M$; Fisher/EK-FAC approximations introduce bias.

**3. SNARK Prover Overhead.** ZK-APEX proofs 400 MB unsuitable for on-chain storage; IPFS off-chain + on-chain hash mitigates but prover 2 hr dominates unlearning latency. Recursion [12] reduces proof to 128 MB but increases prover time 1.8×. BN254 Poseidon circuit needs trusted setup per circuit size.

**4. Storage Amplification.** SISA checkpointing $K\cdot L$ models: for Llama-2 7B, $K=5, L=4$ → 20× 14GB = 280GB storage, prohibitive. Parameter-efficient SISA (LoRA shards) reduces to 20× 20MB but loses exactness.

**5. Forgetting vs Utility Trade-off.** Influence-based removal with $\epsilon<1$ requires large $\sigma$, degrading accuracy 12-15% on Purchase-100. No mechanism achieves both $(\epsilon<0.5,\delta<1e-5)$ and <2% accuracy loss on non-convex tasks [9].

**6. Privacy Risks of Unlearning Itself.** Chen et al. [3] note difference between $h$ and $h^-$ leaks membership via confidence vectors. Our ZK proof hides $w$ but not accuracy delta; MIA on output distribution still 0.54 AUC. Full protection needs output perturbation.

**7. Verification Gap.** Current zk circuits prove *pruning* correctness, not *semantic forgetting* (e.g., can still generate forgotten text via paraphrase). Need semantic forgetting definitions beyond max-divergence.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified three strands of verifiable machine unlearning: exact sharding with slice isolation for $O(1/K)$ retraining, certified removal via influence functions with $(\epsilon,\delta)$ indistinguishability, and auditable forgetting proofs via Halo2 zk-SNARKs. SISA provides ground-truth exactness at cost of ensemble accuracy; Newton-based certified removal provides sublinear approximate unlearning with privacy-preserving noise; ZK-APEX provides cryptographic attestation that prescribed transformation was applied correctly without revealing private weights.

Three actionable directions:

1. **Adaptive-resilient SISA**: Combine DP-SGD training per shard with max-information reduction [5] to resist adaptive deletion choosers while maintaining <2% accuracy loss.
2. **Scalable Influence**: Replace CG with EK-FAC + Arnoldi [11] to scale certified removal to LLMs >10B parameters with Spearman >0.85 fidelity to retrain.
3. **Succinct Forgetting Proofs**: Use recursive composition [12] and lookup arguments to compress 400 MB proofs to <50 MB, enabling on-chain verification on Stellar/Soroban at <2s.

Verifiable unlearning is not a single algorithm but a *system property*: data-plane isolation, model-plane correction, and proof-plane attestation must co-design to meet GDPR's technical and procedural requirements. Our framework demonstrates this composition is feasible today for ViT-scale models and points toward LLM-scale deployment via parameter-efficient sharding and succinct ZK.
