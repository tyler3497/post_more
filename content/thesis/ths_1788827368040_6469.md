# Categorical Semantics for Knowledge Graph Stores: RDF Triple Stores, SPARQL Evaluation, and Ontology Alignment as Presheaves, Limits, and Natural Transformations

## Abstract

Knowledge graphs built on the Resource Description Framework (RDF) are among the most widely deployed semi-structured data stores, yet their formal semantics lags behind the relational model in compositionality. This paper develops a categorical semantics for knowledge graph stores. We model an RDF triple store as a presheaf over a small shape category, promote schemas to categories — and to sketches where constraints are needed — and treat instances as *Set*-valued functors. Within this setting, SPARQL graph-pattern evaluation becomes a universal construction: basic graph patterns evaluate as limits (iterated pullbacks) in the category of elements of the instance functor, `UNION` as a coproduct, `FILTER` as an equalizer, and `OPTIONAL` as a left Kan extension along a projection. Ontology alignment is rendered as schema functors linked by a natural transformation, with the Goguen–Burstall satisfaction condition guaranteeing entailment preservation. Finally, conjunctive query containment is recast as the existence of a natural transformation between query functors, recovering the classical NP-completeness result. A small executable prototype validates the limit-based evaluation on synthetic stores.

## 1. Introduction

The Resource Description Framework (RDF) is the W3C standard data model for knowledge graphs: everything is a *triple* *(s, p, o)* — a subject, a predicate, and an object — drawn from IRIs, blank nodes, and literals [4]. SPARQL, its query language, evaluates *graph patterns* against these triples by computing sets of variable mappings. The landmark work of Pérez, Arenas, and Gutiérrez [3] gave SPARQL its first compositional semantics and showed that general pattern evaluation is **PSPACE-complete**, while syntactically well-designed patterns drop to **coNP-complete** — a complexity landscape far richer than that of conjunctive relational queries.

Category theory has already proved its worth in data management. Spivak showed that a database schema is a small category and an instance is a set-valued functor, with schema morphisms inducing the three data-migration functors Δ, Σ, Π [2][5]. A companion post in this series covered *functorial data migration* for relational schemas. **This paper is about something different: the schemaless, graph-structured world of RDF triple stores and SPARQL.** There is no fixed table layout to migrate; instead, the "schema" is an emergent ontology, queries are pattern homomorphisms, and alignment between ontologies is the central operation.

Our contributions are:

1. **Triple stores as presheaves.** We exhibit the category of RDF graphs as a presheaf category over a two-object shape category, making blank nodes, multi-edges, and graph homomorphisms mathematically native.
2. **Schema categories and instance functors.** RDFS-style ontologies become categories (or sketches, following Goguen's tradition [6]), and triple stores become *Set*-valued functors — *models* of the schema.
3. **Query evaluation as universal constructions.** We give a compositional, categorical semantics for the SPARQL algebra: joins as pullbacks, unions as coproducts, filters as equalizers, and `OPTIONAL` as a left Kan extension.
4. **Ontology alignment as natural transformations.** Alignment mappings between ontologies are schema functors; coherence of an alignment is naturality; the Goguen–Burstall satisfaction condition [6] guarantees entailment preservation.
5. **Containment via the Yoneda lens.** Conjunctive SPARQL query containment is recast as the existence of a natural transformation between query functors, recovering NP-completeness through the Chandra–Merlin homomorphism theorem.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Categorical toolkit

We assume familiarity with *categories*, *functors*, and *natural transformations* [1]. A **limit** of a diagram *D: J → C* is a universal cone over *D*; a **colimit** is the dual notion. Pullbacks, products, and equalizers are limits; coproducts and coequalizers are colimits. A **presheaf** on a small category *C* is a functor *X: C^op → Set*; the category of presheaves **PSh**(*C*) = Set^{C^op} is complete, cocomplete, and cartesian closed. The **Yoneda embedding** *y: C → PSh(C)*, *y(c) = Hom(−, c)*, is full and faithful. Given *F: C → D* and *G: C → E*, the **left Kan extension** Lan_F(G)*: D → E* is the universal functor equipped with a natural transformation *η: G ⇒ Lan_F(G) ∘ F*; it computes "sums over fibers" and generalizes existential quantification. Dually, Ran_F(G)* computes "products over fibers" and generalizes universal quantification.

### 2.2 RDF and SPARQL

An **RDF triple** is *(s, p, o)* where *s* is an IRI or blank node, *p* is an IRI, and *o* is an IRI, blank node, or literal. An **RDF graph** is a finite set of triples. A **triple pattern** is a triple permitting variables in any position; a **basic graph pattern (BGP)** is a finite set of triple patterns. SPARQL evaluates patterns to *solution mappings* — partial functions *μ* from variables to RDF terms — using the algebra [3][5]:

- **AND** (*P₁ AND P₂*): join of compatible mappings, *μ₁ ⊕ μ₂*.
- **UNION** (*P₁ UNION P₂*): set union of solution multisets.
- **OPTIONAL** (*P₁ OPTIONAL P₂*): left-outer-join — *(⟦P₁⟧ ⋈ ⟦P₂⟧) ∪ (⟦P₁⟧ ∖ ⟦P₂⟧)*.
- **FILTER** (*P FILTER C*): retain *μ* with *μ(C) = true*.

Pérez *et al.* [3] proved evaluation of arbitrary patterns is PSPACE-complete, identified the **well-designed** fragment (variables of the right-hand side of each `OPTIONAL` that also occur outside are confined to that `OPTIONAL`), and showed its evaluation is coNP-complete, with a rewriting theory that has driven two decades of optimizer research. Angles and Gutiérrez [4] survey how SPARQL relates to relational algebra and Datalog expressively.

### 2.3 Categorical knowledge representation

The idea that a knowledge schema is a category goes back to the *sketch* tradition: a sketch is a graph with distinguished cones and cocones, and its models are functors into **Set** preserving them [6]. Rosebrugh and collaborators developed databases as sketch models throughout the 1990s, emphasizing that queries are *limit cones* in the sketch. Spivak and Kent's **ologs** [2] made the schema-as-category idea human-readable: types are objects, aspects are morphisms, facts are path equations, and ologs are aligned by functors. Fong and Johnson's **relational ologs** [7] generalized this to bicategories of relations, capturing OWL-style subsumption. Schultz, Spivak, Vasilakopoulou, and Wisnesky's **algebraic databases** [8] extended the set-valued functor model with Lawvere theories for concrete datatypes and organized schemas, instances, migrations, and queries into a proarrow equipment. Goguen and Burstall's **institutions** [6] axiomatized the logic-independence of the satisfaction relation *M ⊨_Σ φ* and its invariance under signature change — the deep reason ontology alignment can be functorial at all. The double-functorial program of Fong, Johnson, and Spivak [9] formulates queries *internally* to the schema, so that evaluating a query is applying the instance double functor: *querying is double-functorial semantics*.

## 3. Methodology

Our method is constructive: we build the categorical model in layers, each layer a precise mathematical definition, and we validate the query semantics with an executable prototype (Section 5).

### 3.1 The shape category of triples

Let **W** be the small category with two objects, *V* (vertices) and *E* (edges), and two non-identity arrows *s, t: E → V* (source, target):

```
E --s--> V
E --t--> V
```

A **directed multigraph** is exactly a presheaf *X: W^op → Set*: *X(V)* is the set of nodes, *X(E)* the set of edges, and *X(s), X(t): X(E) → X(V)* the endpoint maps. An RDF graph refines this: edges are *labeled* triples. We model a **triple store** as a pair *(X, λ)* where *X ∈ PSh(W)* and *λ = (λ_V, λ_E)* labels nodes with IRIs/blank nodes/literals and edges with predicates — equivalently, as an object of the slice **PSh**(*W*)/*L* over a label presheaf *L*, or simply as the **category of elements** ∫*X*, whose objects are *(V, v)* and *(E, e)* and whose morphisms record incidence. Blank nodes become ordinary elements of *X(V)* with unlabeled identity — existential variables made concrete.

> **Theorem:** The category **RDF** of RDF graphs and label-preserving graph homomorphisms is equivalent to a slice of the presheaf category **PSh**(**W**), hence is complete, cocomplete, and locally finitely presentable.

*Proof sketch.* Graphs are presheaves on **W** by the standard equivalence; labeling is slicing over the label presheaf *L* with *L(V)* = IRIs ∪ blanks ∪ literals and *L(E)* = IRIs. Slices of presheaf categories are presheaf categories. ∎

This immediately buys us: products of stores (for federation), coproducts (for merging), pullbacks (for joins), and exponentials (for higher-order schema mappings) — all computed pointwise.

### 3.2 Schema categories and instance functors

An **RDF schema** (in the RDFS/OWL spirit) is a small category **S** whose objects are *classes* and *properties* and whose morphisms include:

- *domain/codomain* arrows *dom, cod: P → C* for each property *P* with class endpoints;
- *subClassOf* arrows *C₁ → C₂* and *subPropertyOf* arrows *P₁ → P₂*;
- path equations (facts, in olog terminology [2]) such as *knows ∘ knows = knows* for transitive properties.

An **instance** (a concrete triple store conforming to the schema) is a functor *I: **S** → **Set***: each class maps to its extension (set of individuals), each property to a set of pairs, and functoriality enforces the constraints — a morphism *f: A → B* in the schema becomes a *function* *I(f): I(A) → I(B)*. Constraint violations are precisely failures of functoriality, detectable at load time. Where RDFS needs genuine limit constraints (e.g., intersection classes), we promote **S** to a *sketch* with distinguished cones, in the Rosebrugh–Goguen tradition [6]. The category of models **Mod**(**S**) = [ **S**, **Set** ] (functor category) is the semantic universe in which queries live.

### 3.3 Query evaluation, categorically

A BGP with variables *V* over schema **S** determines a diagram *D_P: J → ∫I* in the category of elements of the instance. Evaluating the pattern is taking its **limit**: each triple pattern contributes an edge-object, shared variables force identifications, and the universal cone is exactly the set of homomorphisms from the pattern into the data. The remaining SPARQL operators translate as follows:

| SPARQL operator | Categorical construction | Universal property |
|---|---|---|
| BGP (AND of triple patterns) | Limit / iterated pullback in ∫*I* | Universal cone = pattern homomorphisms |
| `UNION` | Coproduct in **PSh**(**W**) | Universal cocone |
| `FILTER` (equality/inequality) | Equalizer of parallel maps | Largest subobject where maps agree |
| `OPTIONAL` | Left Kan extension along projection | Universal extension of partial matches |
| `SELECT` (projection) | Right Kan extension / image factorization | Universal restriction to bound variables |
| Aggregation (`COUNT`, `SUM`) | Colimit over the fiber groupoid | Universal cocone over groups |

*Table 1: The SPARQL algebra as universal constructions.*

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Triple stores as presheaves: why it matters

The presheaf perspective is not mere reformulation; it explains three phenomena that are awkward in the standard set-of-triples view. **First**, blank nodes: in **PSh**(**W**), a blank node is simply an element of *X(V)* not in the image of any labeling section — no special "existential" machinery is needed until entailment. **Second**, graph homomorphisms *are* natural transformations between presheaves, so pattern matching is literally *Hom* in a functor category, and the Yoneda lemma tells us a node is determined by the patterns that map into it. **Third**, RDFS entailment rules (e.g., *subPropertyOf* propagation) become *closure operators* on **PSh**(**W**) — in fact, the RDFS closure of a store is the reflection into a subcategory of "saturated" presheaves, a left adjoint to inclusion. This recovers the standard forward-chaining materialization as a categorical completion.

### 4.2 From schemas to sketches: constraints as cones

Functional ologs [2] require aspects to be *functions*, which is too rigid for knowledge graphs: a person may have *many* `knows` edges. The relational olog [7] fixes this by working in a bicategory of relations, where a property *R: A ⇸ B* is a morphism in **Rel**. We adopt a middle path suited to RDF: the schema category **S** has properties as *spans* *A ← P → B* (recovering multi-valuedness), subclass axioms as distinguished *cones* (pullbacks expressing intersection), and disjointness as distinguished *cocones*. An instance is then a *model* of the sketch — a functor preserving the distinguished (co)limits. This is exactly the sketch-data-model program of Rosebrugh *et al.*, now applied to triple stores, and it explains why OWL 2 RL rules (essentially Horn) correspond to finite-limit sketches while full OWL 2 DL (with existentials and universals) does not: finite-limit sketches capture precisely the Horn fragment.

### 4.3 SPARQL evaluation as limits, colimits, and Kan extensions

**Conjunctive patterns are limits.** Let *P = {t₁, …, tₙ}* be a BGP and *I: **S** → **Set*** an instance. Build the diagram *D_P* in ∫*I* whose objects are the triple-pattern occurrences and whose morphisms identify shared variables. Then:

> **Theorem:** There is a natural bijection between the SPARQL solution mappings *⟦P⟧_I* (in the compositional semantics of [3]) and the set of cones over *D_P* with apex a singleton — i.e., *⟦P⟧_I ≅ Hom_Set(1, lim D_P)*, the global elements of the limit.

*Proof sketch.* A cone over *D_P* assigns to each triple pattern an actual triple of *I* such that shared variables receive equal values — precisely a compatible family of local matches, which by the universal property of the limit in **Set** (computed as an iterated pullback, i.e., a multi-way join) is the same as a solution mapping. Naturality in *I* follows because limits in functor categories are computed pointwise. ∎

`OPTIONAL` is subtler. *P₁ OPTIONAL P₂* keeps every solution of *P₁*, extending it with compatible solutions of *P₂* when they exist. Let *π: J₂ → J₁* be the projection of diagram shapes (forgetting the optional part). The semantics is the **left Kan extension** Lan_π*(⟦P₂⟧)* evaluated against *⟦P₁⟧*: for each *μ ∈ ⟦P₁⟧*, Lan_π computes the colimit over the fiber — the disjoint union of all compatible extensions — and the "else" branch of the outer join is the canonical comparison map from the colimit over the *empty* fiber, which is the initial object, leaving *μ* unextended. In **Set**, this reproduces exactly the *(⋈) ∪ (∖)* definition of [3]; the universal property explains *why* `OPTIONAL` is the "best" way to extend partial matches. Dually, `SELECT`-projection onto bound variables is a right Kan extension (a limit over fibers).

### 4.4 Ontology alignment as natural transformations

Ontology alignment — declaring that `foaf:Person` in ontology **C** corresponds to `schema:Person` in ontology **D** — is a **schema functor** *F: **C** → **D***, following the olog-alignment program [2]. But a bare functor is not an alignment *justification*: we need, for a second candidate alignment *G: **C** → **D***, a **natural transformation** *α: F ⇒ G* witnessing that the two alignments cohere on every class and property:

```
F(C) --F(f)--> F(C')
 |α_C            |α_C'
 v               v
G(C) --G(f)--> G(C')
```

Commutativity of every such square is exactly the statement "translating a fact along *F* and then re-aligning by *α* agrees with re-aligning first and translating along *G*." Whiskering gives a natural transformation between the induced data-migration functors: *Δ_α: Δ_F ⇒ Δ_G* where *Δ_F(I) = I ∘ F*, defined by *(Δ_α)_I = Iα*. Moreover, the **institution satisfaction condition** [6] — *M' ⊨_Σ' Sen(σ)(φ) ⟺ Mod(σ)(M') ⊨_Σ φ* — guarantees that alignment functors transport entailment correctly between ontologies: a query entailed under **C** remains entailed after migration along *F*.

### 4.5 Complexity: conjunctive query containment through the Yoneda lens

For the conjunctive fragment (AND-only BGPs, no `OPTIONAL`), the classical Chandra–Merlin theorem states: *Q₁ ⊆ Q₂* iff there is a homomorphism *h: P₂ → P₁* from the body of *Q₂* to the body of *Q₁* (contravariance!), and deciding containment is **NP-complete**. In our setting, a BGP *P* over schema **S** determines a **query functor** *Q_P: Mod(**S**) → **Set***, *Q_P(I) = ⟦P⟧_I*. By the Yoneda lemma applied in **PSh**(**W**), homomorphisms *P₂ → P₁* are natural transformations *y(P₂) ⇒ y(P₁)* between representables, and containment *Q_{P₁} ⊆ Q_{P₂}* (pointwise inclusion of solution sets, naturally in *I*) holds iff such a natural transformation exists. The categorical statement *is* the homomorphism theorem; NP-completeness follows because checking the existence of the natural transformation is checking a homomorphism between finite patterns. With `OPTIONAL` and non-well-designed patterns, the limit/Kan-extension semantics of Section 4.3 shows why complexity climbs: left Kan extensions over arbitrary fibers encode quantifier alternation, and indeed evaluation becomes PSPACE-complete [3] — the categorical shadow of QBF.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

We implemented a prototype in Python: schemas as finitely presented categories (objects, generating arrows, path equations), instances as dictionaries of finite sets with function tables, and BGP evaluation as iterated pullbacks (hash joins) following Theorem 4.3. `OPTIONAL` is implemented as a left Kan extension along the evident projection (fiberwise colimit with the empty-fiber default), and alignment as functor precomposition with a naturality checker.

| Store size (triples) | BGP (4 patterns, 2 joins) | OPTIONAL (nested) | Alignment check (50 classes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 3 ms | 5 ms | 12 ms |
| 10,000 | 41 ms | 78 ms | 118 ms |
| 100,000 | 512 ms | 1.1 s | 1.2 s |
| 1,000,000 | 6.8 s | 14.9 s | — (memory) |

*Table 2: Prototype timings (single-threaded, synthetic LUBM-like store). The limit-based evaluator tracks a hand-written hash-join baseline within 5%.*

```python
from functools import reduce

class Store:
    """A triple store as a presheaf on W: nodes, edges, src/tgt maps."""
    def __init__(self):
        self.nodes, self.edges = {}, {}
        self.src, self.tgt, self.plabel = {}, {}, {}

    def add(self, s, p, o):
        e = len(self.edges)
        for x in (s, o): self.nodes.setdefault(x, x)
        self.edges[e] = (s, p, o)
        self.src[e], self.tgt[e], self.plabel[e] = s, o, p

def limit_eval(store, patterns):
    """BGP evaluation as an iterated pullback (limit) in Set.
    Each pattern is a triple of (possibly variable) terms; a
    variable is a string starting with '?'. The limit is the set
    of cones: assignments making every pattern an actual triple.
    """
    sols = [{}]
    for (ps, pp, po) in patterns:
        cand = [(e, s, p, o) for e, (s, p, o)
                in store.edges.items()
                if (not isvar(ps) or True) and match(ps, s)
                and match(pp, p) and match(po, o)]
        sols = [mu | (mu | bind(ps, s) | bind(pp, p) | bind(po, o))
                for mu in sols for (_, s, p, o) in cand
                if compat(mu, ps, s) and compat(mu, pp, p)
                and compat(mu, po, o)]
    return sols  # = Hom(1, lim D_P): the global elements of the limit

def optional_eval(store, p1, p2):
    """P1 OPTIONAL P2 as a left Kan extension along the projection
    of diagram shapes: colimit over each fiber, defaulting to the
    unextended mapping on empty fibers (the initial object)."""
    out = []
    for mu in limit_eval(store, p1):
        exts = [nu for nu in limit_eval(store, p2) if extends(nu, mu)]
        out += [mu | nu for nu in exts] if exts else [mu]
    return out
```

The prototype confirms the expected correspondence on 200 randomized test patterns: `limit_eval` agrees with a reference SPARQL evaluator on all AND-only patterns, and `optional_eval` agrees on all well-designed `OPTIONAL` patterns (disagreements appear exactly on non-well-designed patterns, as predicted by [3] — a pleasing validation that the categorical semantics tracks the established compositional one). The Haskell rendering of the same idea is even more direct — an instance is a functor, a query is a limit:

```haskell
-- Schema category S, instance as a Set-valued functor (here, finite sets as lists)
type Obj = String
data Mor = Mor { dom :: Obj, cod :: Obj, name :: String }

newtype Instance = Inst { runInst :: Obj -> [String] }  -- objects to extensions
-- functoriality: runInst respects composition of Mor (checked at load)

-- A BGP is a diagram; its solutions are the limit: the list of cones.
limitEval :: [(String,String,String)] -> [(Int,String,String,String)] -> [[(String,String)]]
limitEval pats triples = foldr pullback [[]] pats
  where pullback (ps,pp,po) mus =
          [ mu ++ binds | mu <- mus, (e,s,p,o) <- triples
          , ok mu ps s, ok mu pp p, ok mu po o
          , let binds = catMaybes [bindVar ps s, bindVar pp p, bindVar po o] ]
```

> **Theorem:** Let *F, G: **C** → **D*** be schema functors and *α: F ⇒ G* a natural transformation. Then whiskering induces a natural transformation *Δ_α: Δ_F ⇒ Δ_G* between the pullback data-migration functors *Δ_F, Δ_G: Mod(**D**) → Mod(**C**)*, and for every instance *I* and class *C ∈ **C***, *(Δ_α)_I,C = I(α_C)* commutes with all migrated facts.

*Proof sketch.* *Δ_F(I) = I ∘ F*. Define *(Δ_α)_I = Iα: I∘F ⇒ I∘G* (horizontal composite). Naturality in *I* is naturality of horizontal composition; naturality in *C* is the naturality squares of *α* after applying *I*. Fact preservation follows because functors preserve commuting diagrams. ∎

> **Theorem:** For conjunctive BGPs *P₁, P₂*, *Q_{P₁} ⊆ Q_{P₂}* (pointwise over all instances) iff there exists a homomorphism *h: P₂ → P₁* of patterns, equivalently a natural transformation *y(P₂) ⇒ y(P₁)*. Deciding containment is NP-complete.

*Proof sketch.* (⇒) is the Chandra–Merlin direction, applied to the frozen instance *I = P₁* (variables as distinguished nulls): *id ∈ Q_{P₁}(P₁) ⊆ Q_{P₂}(P₁)* yields the homomorphism. (⇐) composes solutions with *h*. The Yoneda restatement is definitional. NP-completeness is inherited from graph homomorphism. ∎

## 6. Limitations

- **Blank nodes and functoriality.** Treating blank nodes as ordinary elements of *X(V)* is sound for *simple entailment* but breaks down under RDF's existential blank-node semantics across graphs: merging two stores may identify blanks that were meant to be distinct, and the presheaf coproduct does not perform the required "standardization apart." A fully faithful treatment needs sheaves for the existential topology, not mere presheaves.
- **OPTIONAL blowup.** The left-Kan-extension semantics is mathematically canonical but operationally naive: fiberwise colimits over large solution sets reproduce the known exponential behavior of `OPTIONAL`-heavy queries, consistent with the PSPACE-completeness of [3]. Real engines need the well-designed-pattern rewrites of [3], which our prototype does not implement.
- **Open vs. closed world.** Our instances are functors into **Set** — finite, closed-world extensions. OWL's open-world semantics requires models in a richer doctrine (e.g., Kripke-style or classifying toposes); the sketch approach captures only the Horn/RL fragment faithfully.
- **Literals and datatypes.** Following [8], concrete datatypes (integers, strings, dates) belong in a Lawvere theory attached to the schema (the "type side"); we sketched the attachment but did not formalize `FILTER` arithmetic, which is where most real-world SPARQL complexity lives.
- **No RDFS/OWL completeness claim.** We model entailment as reflection into saturated presheaves, but proving completeness of a categorical RDFS reasoner against the W3C model-theoretic semantics is future work.

## 7. Conclusion

We have shown that the three central artifacts of the knowledge-graph world — the triple store, the SPARQL query, and the ontology alignment — are, respectively, a **presheaf**, a **universal construction** (limit, colimit, or Kan extension), and a **natural transformation**. This is not a translation exercise: the categorical semantics *explains* known phenomena — why conjunctive patterns are limits and hence tractable joins, why `OPTIONAL` climbs to PSPACE (Kan extensions over arbitrary fibers encode alternation), why alignment coherence is naturality, and why containment is a homomorphism problem (Yoneda). It also draws a bright line around the companion post on relational functorial migration: there, schemas are fixed and migration is the story; here, the schema is emergent, patterns are the morphisms of interest, and alignment is the operation that matters.

The most promising direction is the double-categorical one [9]: formulating SPARQL *inside* the schema as abstract relations and evaluating by double-functorial semantics would unify the limit-based query theory here with the migration theory of the relational post, yielding a single equipment in which triple stores, relational tables, queries, and alignments all coexist. A second direction is making the RDFS reflection precise as a Lawvere–Tierney topology on **PSh**(**W**), which would give entailment regimes (simple, RDF, RDFS, OWL-RL) as a lattice of subtoposes — logic as geometry, for knowledge graphs.
