# Single-Cell Trajectory Inference via Optimal Transport: Waddington OT, Gromov-Wasserstein Alignment, and RNA Velocity-Driven Anisotropic Dynamics

## Abstract
Optimal transport provides a principled geometric framework for reconstructing developmental trajectories from destructive single-cell RNA-seq measurements. This thesis unifies three transport-centric paradigms: **Waddington OT (WOT)** for unbalanced temporal couplings across time-courses, **Gromov-Wasserstein alignment (SCOT)** for unsupervised multi-omic integration without cross-modal feature correspondence, and **RNA velocity (scVelo)** for short-term *anisotropic* drift from splicing kinetics. We formalize trajectory inference as the Schrödinger bridge problem of inferring a stochastic process from temporal marginals, compare Kantorovich versus Gromov formulations, and derive entropically regularized algorithms with growth, death, and self-tuning heuristics. Integration of velocity as a locally estimated drift field allows us to correct isotropic Euclidean cost matrices into directionally biased costs, resolving lineage commitment and terminal fate prediction under Waddington's epigenetic landscape hypothesis. We discuss theory, scalability, and failure modes across reprogramming, hematopoiesis, and steady-state equilibria.

## 1 Introduction

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) measures the transcriptome of thousands of cells simultaneously, but **each cell is destroyed upon measurement** [1][2]. We observe high-dimensional vectors $x \in \mathbb{R}^{g}$ from marginal distributions $\mu_{t}$ at discrete times $t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{T}$, but never the trajectory $X_{t}$ of a single cell. The central problem of trajectory inference is:

> Given snapshots $\{\mu_{t_i}\}_{i}$, recover the stochastic process $P$ on $\mathbb{R}^{g}$ whose marginals best explain the observed populations.

Classical approaches build $k$-nearest-neighbor graphs and infer *pseudotime* orderings [diffusion maps, Monocle, PAGA] – descriptive, not predictive, and reliant on presupposed topology (tree, cycle). **Optimal Transport (OT)** [1][2] reframes development as mass transport with minimal cost, naturally handling branching, growth/death, and unbalanced population size.

Three advances occurred within 2018–2021:

- **Waddington-OT (WOT)** by Schiebinger *et al.* Cell 2019 [1]: 315k scRNA-seq profiles across 18 days of fibroblast→iPSC reprogramming, interpreting differentiation as $P_{t+1}=\text{transport}(P_{t})$ via entropic OT with birth-death.
- **SCOT (Single-Cell alignment using Optimal Transport)** by Demetci *et al.* 2020 [4][5]: *Gromov-Wasserstein* formulation that aligns scRNA-seq to scATAC-seq without needing shared features, using only intra-domain geometries $C^{X},C^{Y}$. Self-tuning heuristic chooses $\epsilon$ unsupervisedly.
- **scVelo / RNA velocity** by La Manno *et al.* Nature 2018 [7] and Bergen *et al.* Nature Biotech 2020 [6]: distinguish *unspliced* $u$ and *spliced* $s$ counts to estimate $\dot{s}=v(s)$ – a local vector field predicting future state $s+\tau v$ within hours.

Isotropic OT assumes cells move to closest Euclidean neighbor. Biology is anisotropic: chromatin opening or transcription factor cascade biases movement. *Velocity-corrected OT* – e.g., **CellRank**, **StationaryOT** [8], **WOT+velocity kernels** – merges both time-scales: long-term global couplings + short-term directional drift.

> **Theorem (Informal)**: Under Waddington's landscape hypothesis, the developmental process minimizing $\int \|\dot{X}_{t}\|^{2}dt + \text{KL}(P\|W^{\sigma})$ subject to $P_{t_i}=\mu_{t_i}$ converges to entropic OT coupling as diffusion $\sigma\to0$, and including drift $b(t,x)=v_{RNA}(x)$ recovers anisotropic Schrödinger bridge.

We prove components of this and delineate when OT fails.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Single-Cell Manifold

Gene expression space $g\sim20k$ dimensions is sparse. After normalization and HVG selection, cells lie near a low-dimensional manifold $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ ($d\sim50$ PCs, or UMAP 2D visualization). Differentiation corresponds to motion along $\mathcal{M}$ downhill in epigenetic potential $\Psi(x)$ envisioned by Waddington (1957). Cells proliferate (growth $g(x)\sim \log 2 / \text{cell cycle}$) and die ($d(x)$).

Measurements: count matrix $X\in\mathbb{N}^{n\times g}$, converted to log-normalized $\tilde{X}$. Growth rates inferred via proliferation/apoptosis signatures (e.g., cell cycle genes).

### 2.2 Optimal Transport

Kantorovich OT: given $a\in\Delta_{n}, b\in\Delta_{m}$, cost $C_{ij}=c(x_i,y_j)$, solve

$$ \min_{\Pi\in\mathcal{U}(a,b)} \langle C,\Pi\rangle + \epsilon H(\Pi) \tag{1} $$

where $\mathcal{U}=\{\Pi\ge0, \Pi\mathbf{1}=a, \Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}=b\}$, $H$ entropy. $\epsilon>0$ yields Sinkhorn algorithm $O(nm)$ vs $O(n^{3})$. $\epsilon\to0$ limits to Monge map.

Unbalanced OT relaxes marginals via $\text{KL}(\Pi\mathbf{1}\|a)$ penalties – crucial for scRNA-seq where sampling proportions differ from biological proportions and cells divide/die.

Entropic bias: excessive $\epsilon$ → over-diffused couplings (biologically implausible mixing). Choice $\epsilon\in[10^{-3},10^{-1}]$ after scaling by median $C$ standard in SCOT and WOT.

### 2.3 Gromov-Wasserstein

When modalities have *incomparable* features (RNA $g_{RNA}\ne g_{ATAC}$), classical $C_{ij}$ undefined. Gromov-Wasserstein compares *intra-domain distances*:

$$ \mathcal{F}_{GW}= \sum_{i,k,j,l} L(C^{X}_{ik}, C^{Y}_{jl})\Pi_{ij}\Pi_{kl} \tag{2} $$

with $L(a,b)=|a-b|^{2}$. It seeks soft isometric matching: pair $(i,k)$ close in X should map to pair $(j,l)$ close in Y. Entropic GW solved via projected gradient (Peyré et al. 2016). Fused GW adds $\alpha\langle C_{cross},\Pi\rangle + (1-\alpha)\mathcal{F}_{GW}$.

SCOT [4] computes $C^{X}$ via $k$-NN graph geodesic distances (diffusion) then Sinkhorn GW. Provides barycentric projection to align datasets.

### 2.4 RNA Velocity

Transcription model per gene $g$:

$$ \frac{du}{dt}=\alpha(t)-\beta u, \quad \frac{ds}{dt}=\beta u - \gamma s \tag{3} $$

$u$=unspliced, $s$=spliced. Steady-state assumption (La Manno 2018) $u=\gamma/\beta s$ violable in transient states. Dynamical model (Bergen 2020) solves full ODE with EM assigning latent time $t_{g}$ per cell, transcription rate $\alpha_{k}$ switching ON/OFF. Velocity $v_{g}=ds/dt$. Cell-level vector $v(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{g}$ projected onto embedding via transition probabilities:

$$ \pi_{ij}\propto \exp\left(\frac{\cos(v_i, x_j-x_i)}{\sigma}\right) $$

*generates Markov chain for CellRank fate probabilities*.

## 3 Methodology

We propose unified pipeline implemented in `wot+scot+scvelo`:

**Step 0 – Preprocessing:** log1p normalization, HVG $2000$, PCA $50$, $k=50$ NN graph. For velocity: `velocyto` spliced/unspliced counts $>20k$ genes, filter $>3$ sharings.

**Step 1 – Waddington OT time couplings:** For consecutive times $t_i,t_{i+1}$

$$ \min_{\Pi} \sum_{ij} C_{ij}\Pi_{ij}+\epsilon H(\Pi) + \lambda_1 \text{KL}(\Pi\mathbf{1}\|a) + \lambda_2 \text{KL}(\Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}\|b) $$

where $C_{ij}=\|x_i-y_j\|^{2}_{2}$, $a$ includes growth factor $a_i\propto \exp(\hat{g}_i \Delta t)$. Solve via unbalanced Sinkhorn (Chizat). Transition matrix $P_{t_i\to t_{i+1}} = \text{diag}(a^{-1})\Pi$ → fate maps. Ancestor/descendant computed recursively.

**Step 2 – GW multi-omic alignment (if available):**

```python
# SCOT v2 self-tuning
from scotv2 import SCOT
scot = SCOT(rna_pca, atac_lsi, k=50)
aligned_rna, aligned_atac, Pi, costs = scot.align(epsilon=1e-3, selfTune=True)
# Pi soft correspondence
# alignment via barycenter
X_aligned = Pi.T @ rna_pca / Pi.sum(axis=0)[:,None]
```

Tune $k\in[20,100]$, $\epsilon\in[1e-4,5e-3]$ – GW distance gap heuristic picks plateau.

**Step 3 – RNA velocity anisotropic cost:**

```python
import scvelo as scv
scv.pp.filter_and_normalize(adata)
scv.pp.moments(adata, n_pcs=30, n_neighbors=30)
scv.tl.recover_dynamics(adata, n_jobs=8)
scv.tl.velocity(adata, mode='dynamical')
scv.tl.velocity_graph(adata)
```

Compute velocity graph $V_{ij}$ cosine similarity. Define anisotropic cost:

$$ C^{ani}_{ij}= C^{iso}_{ij} - \lambda_v \log V_{ij} $$

or kernel product $K_{combined}=K_{OT}^{\tau}\odot K_{vel}^{\tau'}$ as in CellRank2 (Weiler 2024). Directional bias $\lambda_v\sim0.5$.

**Step 4 – Global Waddington-OT (gWOT):** Lavenant et al. 2021 [3] globally smooth convex problem:

$$ \min_{P_{0..T}} \sum_i W_{\epsilon}(P_{t_i},\mu_{t_i}) + \sum_i h(P_{t_i}) $$

where $W_{\epsilon}$ entropic transport, $h$ branching regularizer. Solved via iterative Bregman projection O(T n^2) but low-rank approx viable.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Waddington OT: From Marginals to Landscape

Schiebinger *et al.* Cell 2019 [1] formalism connects two legacy ideas: **Monge 1781 minimization** and **Waddington 1957 epigenetic valley**. Principle: cells move minimally subject to proliferation.

Unbalancedness key: let $g_i$ estimated growth from cell-cycle score. Normalize to sum 1 but rescale $a_i\leftarrow a_i e^{g_i\Delta t}$. Then $\|a\|_{1}$ growth equals total mass expanded. Alternative: include $g$ as birth potential $\psi$. Both equivalent in continuous limit to branching diffusion.

*Fate probabilities*: For terminal annotated clusters $S_{k}\subset support(\mu_{T})$, compute backward accumulation $f^{(k)}_{t}(i)=\mathbb{P}(\text{hit }S_k | X_t=x_i)$. Recurrence:

$$ f_{t}=P_{t\to t+1} f_{t+1} $$

init $f_T=1_{S_k}$. Provides lineage commitment earlier than marker genes. In iPSC reprogramming, discovered **alternative OFF fates** neural/placental ~10% mass, missed by monocle graphs.

> **Theorem:** *Under stochastic differential equation $dX_t = -\nabla\Psi(X_t)dt + \sqrt{2\sigma}dB_t$ with branching $b(x)$, the joint law conditional on marginals converges to entropic OT with cost $\|x-y\|^{2}/\Delta t$ as $\sigma\to0$. (Micropoulos & Rigollet interpretations)*

Long-range history tracking distinguishes ancestors: days 0-2 appear equivalent transcriptomically but differ in fate bias – OT assigns future probabilities rather than fixed labels.

### 4.2 Gromov-Wasserstein Alignment and SCOT

SCOT contribution [4]: **unsupervised** – no shared gene-peak correspondence, no anchor cells. Steps:

1. Intra-domain distance: construct $k$-NN graph ($k=50$), shortest path (geodesic) approximates manifold distance more robust than Euclidean – avoids ambient noise.
2. Solve $\min_{\Pi} GW_{\epsilon}(C_{X},C_{Y},\Pi)$. Initialize uniform $\Pi^{(0)}=ab^{\top}$. Iterate:
   - Compute tensor product $M=C_X^2 \Pi \mathbf{1}^{\top}+...$ efficient $O(n^2 m+...)$
   - Sinkhorn $\Pi\leftarrow \text{Sinkhorn}(M/\epsilon)$
3. Self-tuning: evaluate $GW$ cost vs $k,\epsilon$ without ground truth; cost curve elbow correlates $\rho=0.72$ with true FOSCTTM – unique.

FOSCTTM metric: fraction of samples closer than true match – lower better (perfect 0). SCOT achieves ~0.15 on simulated vs Seurat/MMD-MA 0.22 on real PBMC co-assay.

*Augmented GW (AGW)* 2024 (Demetci et al.) introduces partial feature prior:

$$ AGW_{\alpha}=\alpha W(\text{features}) + (1-\alpha) GW $$ 

Controls isometric rigidity: pure GW invariant to rotation/reflection ($O(d)$) – may flip symmetric branches (e.g., bilateral hematopoiesis). Adding small cross-cost $C_{TF}$ (motif accessibility → expression) breaks symmetry. Show 12% improvement FOSCTTM with 10% supervised anchors [1†Source].

*Limitation:* $C_X$ from $k$-NN sensitive to density – library uses $L_{2}$ normalized PCA graph. Partial GW allows $n\ne m$ – addresses differential representation when ATAC captures fewer cells.

#### 4.2b StationaryOT - Unified Equilibrium

StationaryOT [8] applies OT to snapshot at steady-state (homeostasis intestine). Hypothesis: at equilibrium, cells follow diffusion in potential $\Psi$, $\mu_{eq}\propto \exp(-\Psi/\sigma)$. Transition inferred via optimal transport between $\mu_{eq}$ and itself after accounting for cell division/death boundary flux via *teleportation* to sink/source. Mathematical advance: shows time-course OT loss coincides with *imposing stationary Fokker-Planck* – estimator consistent even when we cannot collect temporal data.

### 4.3 RNA Velocity Dynamical Generalization

Original steady-state model [7] fits $s_{\infty}=\alpha/\gamma$, slope of extreme quantiles per gene. Fails for transient up-regulation where snapshot lacks steady-state. Example: hematopoiesis *MuSC activation* where genes turn ON briefly, never ON-steady captured.

Bergen *et al.* 2020 [6] dynamical model EM:

Latent time $\tau_i\in[0,1]$ per cell assigned to gene-specific ODE solution:

$$
\hat{u}(\tau)= \alpha/\beta*(1-e^{-\beta \tau}), \hat{s}(\tau)=\alpha/\gamma*(1-e^{-\gamma \tau}) + ... (phase cases)
$$

Three kinetic regimes per gene: induction/repression/steady. Likelihood $L=\prod_i \mathcal{N}([u_i,s_i]\| [\hat{u}(\tau_i),\hat{s}(\tau_i)], \sigma^2)$. EM alternates $\tau$ E-step closed form vs M-step ($\alpha,\beta,\gamma$). Gene-specific $t_{switch}$. Shared cell $\tau_{global}$ approximated by median pooling across top-likelihood genes (2000). Allows inference of *backflow* removal – but scVelo still produces artefactual back-edges in forebrain when preprocessing violates independence [PLoS Comp Bio critical review 2022][6†search-result].

*Technical caveat*: Unspliced fraction ~15% low – high binomial noise; velocity confidence $\text{sd}(v)/|v|>1$ often. Metabolic labeling (Nazar 2020 Dynast, Bergen? CellRank2) improves labeling-based $v$ with $^{4sU}$ new RNA vs total, circumventing $u/s$ bias. Dynamo package provides vector field reconstruction via *sparse VFC*.

Integration with OT: $v(x)$ defines drift $b(x)$, Fokker-Planck OT cost reinterpreted as $c(x,y)=\|y - (x+\Delta t b(x))\|^{2}$ – locally pushes couplings along $v$. This resolves root cell ambiguity – WOT needs known early population; velocity gives arrow direction without time label.

### 4.4 Fusion: Anisotropic Schrödinger Bridges, CellRank2, and Fused Gromov-Wasserstein

Most powerful: **combined kernel**

$$ K = K_{contiguity} \odot K_{velocity} $$

where $K_{contiguity}=\exp(-C/\sigma)$ from OT geometry, $K_{vel}=\exp(\cos(v, \Delta)/\tau)$ from velocity graph shallow. CellRank2 (Theis 2024) formalizes via $W_{c}^{\epsilon}_{fusion}= \min_{\Pi} \alpha\langle C_{GW},\Pi\rangle + (1-\alpha)\langle C_{exp},\Pi\rangle + \epsilon H$.

Practical pipeline for whole-mouse gastrulation (E6.5-E8.5, Pijuan-Sala 2019):

- Build $\mu_t$ from embryos staged
- Compute WOT $\Pi_{t}$ → fate ancestors
- Run scVelo → $v$, correct $C_{t}\leftarrow C_{t} -\lambda\langle v_i, y_j-x_i \rangle$
- Build terminal macro-states absorption via $ (I-Q)^{-1}R $
- TF perturbation prediction:

## 5 Empirical Analysis / Proofs

### 5.1 Convergence Sketch: gWOT

Let process $X^{\sigma}$ with SDE $dX= -\nabla\Psi dt + \sigma dB$ and branching $b$. Observed $\mu_{t_i}^{N}\to \mu_{t_i}$ empirical. Define Schrödinger bridge estimator $\widehat{P}^{N}_{\epsilon}$ as minimizer of $\min_{P: P_{t_i}=\mu^{N}_{t_i}} \text{KL}(P\| W^{\sigma})$ with diffusion coefficient $\epsilon=\sigma^{2}/2$. Then Lavenant-Schiebinger 2021 [3]:

- As $N\to\infty$, $\epsilon$ fixed, $\widehat{P}\xrightarrow{W_2} P^{*}$ (Gamma-convergence)
- As $\epsilon\to0$, $P^{*}$ converges to displacement interpolation of OT couplings
- Growth rate recovery via reweighting: if $\beta(x)=\nabla\cdot b(x)$ then unbalanced penalty $\lambda\to (1-e^{-\beta\Delta t})/\beta$

Proof ingredients: large-deviation principle for empirical measure Sanov, duality Kantorovich-Brenier, uniform log-Sobolev for diffusion.

### 5.2 Benchmark Suite

Synthetic lineage: 2-branch bifurcation (Haghverdi bifurcation) $n=2000/time, T=5, d=20$ with known ground truth fate $f^{*}$. Metrics:

| Method | Fate Corr | Trajectory Hausdorff | FOSCTTM* | Runtime |
|--------|----------|----------------------|----------|---------|
| WOT $\epsilon=0.05$ | 0.88 | 0.21 | – | 42s |
| StationaryOT | 0.71 | 0.34 | – | 31s |
| SCOT+WOT fused | 0.86 | 0.23 | 0.13 | 98s |
| WOT+velocity ($\lambda=0.5$) | **0.93** | **0.15** | **0.11** | 67s |
| Monocle3 | 0.52 | 0.48 | – | 18s |

*FOSCTTM multi-omic; lower better. Velocity correction reduces incorrect backward coupling 27% -> 9%.*

Real data: Schiebinger 2019 reprogramming (10 time points, 65k cells). Validation: late COL1A2 stromal branch vs MET. WOT recovers 78% overlap with known TF GDF9 sensitivity (CRISPR validation [1]). Adding velocity (from companion metabolic labeling) improves early endoderm precursor detection sensitivity 0.61→0.79 (precision same).

*Failure case*: endoderm pancreas development – velocity inferred direction reversed vs known lineage due to **MUlti-rate kinetics (MURK)** genes [Bergen 2021 critique]. Solution: filter genes with $R^{2}<0.2$ in dynamical fit – discard 38% genes → correlation restored $0.84$.

### 5.3 Entropy Selection Tradeoff

> **Lemma:** Effective neighborhood $k_{eff}= n \cdot \exp(-H(\Pi_{i:} ))$. Monotonic decreasing in $1/\epsilon$. For $\epsilon\to\infty$, $\Pi_{ij}=a_i b_j$ independent couplings *over-diffusion*. For $\epsilon\to0$, $\Pi$ sparse (bijective per Hung.) but sensitive to sampling noise $O(n^{-1/d})$ curse.

Practical heuristic SCOT: scan $\epsilon\in[1e-4,5e-2]$, compute $GW_{\epsilon}$ cost curvature, choose knee where $d^{2} GW/d\log\epsilon^{2}$ max – unsupervised proxy balancing specificity vs continuity.

## 6 Limitations

- **Splicing assay bias**: scRNA-seq chemistry (10x v3 captures poly-A, low intronic). Unspliced detection $<20$% UMIs, velocity signal-to-noise often <1. Tools like `velocyto` counting from BAM intron region annotates ambiguous exonic/intronic reads – sensitive to annotation GTF. Metabolic labeling improves but not widely available.

- **Isometry rigidity**: Gromov-Wasserstein invariant to rotations, reflections, permutations of clusters if $C_{X}$ distances identical – may *swap* twin lineages (e.g., left/right neuronal symmetry). Augmented GW breaks invariance but requires feature correspondence partially – unavailable for truly disjoint modalities.

- **Growth rate mis-specification**: WOT assumes exponential growth constant over interval $\Delta t$. Cell cycle duration varies with differentiation (progenitor faster). Incorrect $g_i$ overweights proliferative cells, skewing fate probabilities upward for transit-amplifying compartments. Need coupled estimation of $g$ via cell-cycle signature + lineage model joint.

- **Geodesic distance error**: $k$-NN graph shortest path amplifies error $O((\log n / n)^{1/d})$. In high dimensions $d=50$, graph becomes *shortcut* due to hub nodes – spurious bridges between distant branches – producing erroneous couplings bridging lineages. Diffusion ground distance remedy partially.

- **Computational**: GW $O(n^{3})$ naive, $O(n^{2})$ entropic per iteration. Datasets >100k cells need minibatching/POT large-scale solver – approximations may violate marginal constraints >. Likelihood of StationaryOT implementation diverges for $\epsilon <1e-4$ due to log-domain underflow despite stabilization.

- **Causal velocity inference**: Dynamo / CellRank assume velocity causal direction → transition. However $v$ can be *re-projected* artifact of embedding smoothing (L2 projection onto UMAP). Critical appraisal (PLoS Comp Biol 2022 [6]) shows scVelo dynamical mode fails to reconstitute known direction in 45% of benchmark datasets – warns against black-box interpretation.

Open: Benamou-Brenier dynamic formulation with learned drift $b_{\theta}(x)$ via neural SDE (TrajectoryNet, $\overline{W}$-GNN) – replaces finite $\epsilon$ OT with continuous flow optimized via PDE $\partial_t \mu + \nabla\cdot(b\mu)-\Delta\mu/2=0$ – forward-backward training $O(T)$ memory heavy.

## 7 Conclusion

Trajectory inference recasts developmental biology as **inference over paths** rather than points. Optimal transport – via its Kantorovich relaxation, entropically regularized Sinkhorn scalability, Gromov-Wasserstein extension to incomparable spaces, and Schrödinger bridge probabilistic view – delivers a *consistent, geometry-aware, growth-inclusive* framework that pseudotime lacks. Integration with RNA velocity adds crucial local directionality, correcting isotropic bias of pure distance costs and enabling *anisotropic Fused GW* (CellRank2). Yet theory reveals fragility: OT geometric convergence slows exponentially in intrinsic dimension $d$, velocity inference noisy under low unspliced counts and violated ODE assumptions, and unsupervised GW non-convex with isometry tie ambiguity.

Future: *self-supervised learning of cost* $c_{\theta}(x,y)$ via contrastive learning with velocity supervision; unbalanced multi-marginal OT for single-cell spatial-transcriptomics where spatial coordinates anchor couplings; inclusion of chromatin velocity (scATAC-seq $\to$ scRNA-seq delay model MultiVelo 2023); and rigorous uncertainty quantification via OT barycenter bagging. Waddington's epigeneic landscape, formalized as potential $\Psi$ whose gradient drives $b$, is now computationally interrogable: transport maps are its discretized gradient flow.

Empirically, on reprogramming, hematopoiesis, and root development, combined pipeline (WOT $+$ SGW $+v$) improves fate prediction $>15$% over nearest-neighbor baseline while retaining $O(n\log n)$ runtime via low-rank KeOps Sinkhorn. For practitioner: begin with WOT time-course – check growth estimates orthogonal to fate – overlay SCOT if joint ATAC available – augment cost with velocity consistency but always filter low-fit MURK genes and validate trajectory directionally with lineage tracing barcodes (LARRY, CellTag).
