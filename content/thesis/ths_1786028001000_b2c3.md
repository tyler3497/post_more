# Scalable Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search under Memory and Predicate Constraints: HNSW Layering, Vamana Robust Pruning, Anisotropic Quantization, and Predicate-Aware Traversal for ACORN and Filtered-DiskANN

## Abstract

Approximate nearest neighbor search (ANNS) underpins modern retrieval-augmented generation, recommendation, and multimodal search, yet billion-scale deployments are bounded by memory, SSD I/O, and predicate sparsity. This thesis synthesizes four interlocking advances: Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs that separate link scales across randomized layers to achieve near-logarithmic routing, Vamana graphs with α-RobustPrune that guarantee navigability on disk with beamsearch re-ranking, anisotropic and product quantization that minimize inner-product ranking loss rather than ℓ2 reconstruction error, and predicate-aware traversal schemes such as ACORN and Filtered-DiskANN that preserve connectivity under arbitrary label filters. We formalize construction complexity, quantization distortion bounds, and filtered search recall under selectivity, and we provide concrete tuning rules for M, efConstruction, L, and beamwidth that jointly optimize recall@10, QPS, and tail latency in hybrid DRAM-SSD hierarchies.

## 1 Introduction

The scale of vector search has shifted from million-scale in-memory indices to **billion-point** workloads with strict latency SLOs and structured predicates. In production systems at Microsoft Bing, Google Research, and vector databases such as Pinecone and Milvus, a query must retrieve *k* nearest neighbors from embeddings of dimensionality *d ∈ [64, 1536]* under metadata filters whose *selectivity* may be as low as 0.1% [2][5][7]. Classical partitioning approaches such as *inverted file with asymmetric distance computation* (IVFADC) achieve attractive memory compression but suffer from coarse quantization boundaries and limited recall at high throughput [4].

Graph-based indices have emerged as the dominant paradigm because they explicitly encode *neighborhood proximity* and support greedy *best-first* routing without exhaustive bucket scans [1][2]. However, three tensions remain unresolved:

- *Memory vs. I/O*: HNSW and Navigating Spreading Graph (NSG) assume the entire graph resides in DRAM, which imposes O(N·M) edges and O(N·d) vectors in memory. For N=10^9, this exceeds 1 TB [2].
- *Reconstruction vs. ranking loss*: Product Quantization (PQ) minimizes ||x - tilde(x)||_2^2, but for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) only the **parallel component** of residual error along the query direction changes ranking [5].
- *Vector similarity vs. predicate satisfiability*: Pre-filtering degenerates to linear scan when selectivity is low; post-filtering requires k' >> k over-retrieval and incurs unpredictable latency; fully filter-aware indices sacrifice generality [6][7].

This thesis articulates a unified stack: *HNSW layering* for logarithmic routing, *DiskANN's Vamana* for SSD-resident navigation with DRAM-resident PQ codes, *anisotropic quantization* for MIPS-aware loss, and *predicate-aware expansion* via ACORN and Filtered-DiskANN that maintains subgraph connectivity under arbitrary filters. Our contribution is intentionally integrative: we show how parameter choices propagate across layers and where current systems fail.

> Theorem: Navigability under α-RobustPrune. For α > 1, the Vamana graph ensures that for any points p, q, there exists a path where each hop reduces distance to q by factor ≥ 1/α, provided the graph satisfies α-relaxed relative neighborhood dominance [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Problem Definition

Let X = {x_i ∈ R^d}_{i=1}^N be a database, q ∈ R^d a query, and dist(·,·) either ℓ2, cosine, or negative inner product. ANNS returns R_k(q) such that |R_k ∩ R_k^*| / k is maximized, where R_k^* is exact k-NN. We measure *Recall@k*, *QPS*, and *p99 latency*. For filtered ANNS, each x carries attributes A_x, and a predicate σ defines a feasible set P_σ ⊆ X with selectivity s = |P_σ|/|X| [6][7].

### 2.2 Families of Indices

- **Tree-based**: Annoy, KD-trees. Simple but poor in d > 32 due to curse of dimensionality.
- **Hash-based**: LSH, FALCONN. Sublinear in theory but high probing cost for high recall.
- **Partition-based**: IVF, SPANN. Balanced posting lists, hierarchical k-means partitioning [3].
- **Graph-based**: *HNSW* [1], *NSG*, *Vamana* [2]. State-of-the-art recall-QPS frontier on ANN-Benchmarks.
- **Quantization**: *PQ, OPQ, AQ, RQ* [4][5]. Compression 4-32× with asymmetric distance computation (ADC).

*Filtered* extensions introduce either inline filtering over a generic graph or attribute-tailored construction (Filtered-DiskANN, SeRF) or predicate-agnostic augmentation (ACORN) [6][7].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is constructive and analytical. We re-derive construction rules, expose tuning knobs, and present reference implementations in *Python, Rust, and Haskell* that mirror production libraries (FAISS, DiskANN, ScaNN).

Key design decisions:

1. Use *hierarchical randomization* for layer assignment: l = floor(-ln(U(0,1)) · m_L) where m_L = 1/ln(M) controls exponential decay [1].
2. Adopt *RobustPrune(p, V, α, R)* instead of vanilla NSG prune to keep both short and long edges when d(p,v) > α·d(p*,v) is not satisfied, ensuring diversity [2].
3. Train *anisotropic* codebooks with loss weighting parallel component heavier than orthogonal [5].
4. Augment HNSW with dense two-hop expansion to restore connectivity in predicate subgraphs below critical percolation density [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hierarchical NSW: Layered Navigability and Logarithmic Scaling

HNSW builds a multi-layer proximity graph G = {G_0, G_1, … G_{L_max}} where each layer is a navigable small world (NSW) graph on nested subsets [1]. Search proceeds top-down:

```python
def hnsw_search(q, entry, ef, layers):
    cur = entry
    for l in reversed(range(1, len(layers))):
        cur = greedy_search_layer(q, cur, ef=1, layer=l)  # scale separation
    # base layer with larger ef
    candidates = greedy_search_layer(q, cur, ef=ef, layer=0)
    return top_k(candidates, k=10)
```

The maximum layer for node insertion is sampled i.i.d. from exponential decay, producing O(log N) layers in expectation. **Bold** claim of [1]: unlike flat k-NN graphs, the hierarchy avoids exhaustive global entry from a random point, reducing search cost from O(N^{1/d} log N) to O(log N) under intrinsic dimensionality assumptions [1].

*Critical nuance*: later empirical ablation demonstrates that hierarchical structure contributes less in d > 128 than diversification alone – a flat HNSW with *heuristic neighbor selection* achieves similar QPS [1][3]. The analogy to *skip lists* is intentional: each layer separates characteristic distances, much like skip pointers separate list positions.

> Theorem: Expected search cost in HNSW. Under uniformly distributed data and M bounded, expected hops per layer is O(M) and total search is O(M · log_{M_max} N) [1].

Implementation considerations:

- `M` = 12–32 for typical d=128–768; larger M improves navigability but increases distance comparisons (DC).
- `efConstruction` ≥ 200 for high recall workloads; trades off index time O(N·efC·log N).
- *Lock-free* incremental insertion enables streaming build; no global rebalancing required unlike trees.

```rust
// Rust sketch: HNSW layer assignment
```

### 4.2 Vamana, DiskANN, RobustPrune and SSD-DRAM Hierarchy

**DiskANN** reframes the memory dilemma: keep *compressed* vectors in DRAM for *lossy* distance estimation, and full-precision vectors plus adjacency on SSD for *exact* re-ranking [2]. Its core is *Vamana*, a directed regular graph:

- *Iteration*: greedy search with list size L to find candidates
- *Prune*: `RobustPrune(p, candidates, α, R)` retains edge (p, v) iff ∄ v' : d(p', v) < α·d(p, v) where α ≥ 1 is diversity factor
- *Construction*: partition-merge with medoid entry point for billion-scale parallel build

*DiskANN's* hybrid search pseudocode:

```haskell
-- Haskell: beam search with PQ + full re-rank
beamSearch :: Graph -> PQTable -> Query -> Int -> Int -> [NodeId]
beamSearch g pqTable q beamWidth k = 
    let init = medoid g
        pqDist n = asymDist pqTable q n  -- in-memory
        step frontier visited =
          if frontier == [] then visited
          else let u = argmin pqDist frontier
                   nbrs = adjacency g u -- mayTrigger SSD I/O
                   new = filter (`notElem` visited) nbrs
               in step (take beamWidth $ sortBy pqDist (frontier ++ new)) (u:visited)
    in take k $ sortBy (fullDist q) (step [init] [])
```

**Key theorems from DiskANN**:

- On *SIFT1B*, 95%+ 1-recall@1 at 5000 QPS, <3 ms mean latency on single node with 64 GB RAM + SSD, vs FAISS IVFADC+G+P plateauing at 50% under similar memory [2].
- - FreshDiskANN extends Vamana to streaming inserts/deletes with *5–10×* lower maintenance cost than rebuild [8].

Operational parameters:

- *R* (max degree) = 32–128; *L* = 75–200 construction
- *α* = 1.2 controls long-range preservation; higher α → denser graph, better high-dimensional recall but index size ↑
- *SSD layout optimization*: isomorphic mapping + aligned 4KB pages → fewer wasted reads per beam
- *Medoid vs hierarchical entry*: Beam search from single medoid surprisingly robust with query-sensitive re-entry using coarse PQ centroids

Comparison table for DRAM-resident alternatives:

| System | Index size @1B SIFT | QPS @95% R@1 | Mean latency | Freshness |
|--------|----------------------|-------------|--------------|-----------|
| HNSW M=36 | ~1.2 TB | 8000 | 1.2 ms | scan required |
| NSG deg=100 | ~0.9 TB | 6500 | 1.5 ms | static |

### 4.3 Quantization – Product, Optimized, and Anisotropic

*Product Quantization* [4] decomposes R^d = ∏_{j=1}^m R^{d/m} and learns m codebooks C_j with k* = 256 centroids each. A database vector x is encoded as code (c_1,…,c_m) where c_j = argmin_{c∈C_j} ||x_j - c||_2^2 and stored in m bytes:

This yields asymmetric distance computation with m table lookups + sums, SIMD-accelerable [4].

**Bold limitation**: minimizing ||x-tilde(x)|| is *proxy*, not ranking loss. For MIPS where q^T x matters, residual parallel to x corrupts dot-product ordering more than orthogonal residual:

> Theorem: MIPS quantization distortion. Let r = x - tilde(x), q uniform on S^{d-1}. Then E[(q^T r)^2] = (||r_parallel||^2 + κ ||r_perp||^2)/d with anisotropy factor κ < 1 [5].

*Anisotropic Vector Quantization (ANQ)* introduces threshold t = 0.2 for normalized score and weights parallel component heavier:

```python
# ANQ training loss: score-aware weighting
def anisotropic_loss(x, x_tilde, queries_batch, beta=2.0, tau=0.2):
    r = x - x_tilde
    # projection onto query subspace approximated by parallel to x for MIPS
    r_parallel = proj(r, x) 
    r_perp = r - r_parallel
    # high inner product queries get larger weight
    weights = np.maximum(0, (queries_batch @ x) - tau)
    loss = np.mean(weights) * (np.linalg.norm(r_parallel)**2 + 0.3*np.linalg.norm(r_perp)**2)
    return loss
```

SCANN architecture [5] has three stages: (i) coarse partitioning via anisotropic clustering, (ii) ANQ for fast scoring within partitions, (iii) exact re-rank of top n_reorder = 50–100. Empirically, on `glove-100-angular`, SCANN yields *2× QPS* at same recall as PQ/OPQ baselines [5].

Practical guidance:

- *Dimensional ordering*: rotate via OPQ rotation matrix learned to minimize PQ distortion before encoding [4].
- *Bytes*: 16–64 bytes typical; m = d/4 common for d=128, m = d/2 for d=768 retrieval embeddings.
- *Codebook training*: k-means++ initialization, sample 10^6 vectors; GPU-accelerated FAISS `faiss.ProductQuantizer`.
- *Residual encoding*: for SPANN, store x - c_i via 2-level IVFPQ for memory-efficient posting lists [3].

### 4.4 Filtered ANN – ACORN, Filtered-DiskANN, and Predicate-Agnostic Tradeoffs

Filtered ANNS defines R_k(q, σ) ⊆ P_σ with predicate σ [6][7]. Three canonical baselines:

1. **Pre-filter**: scan P_σ (good for s < 1% but O(N))
2. **Post-filter**: retrieve k' >> k then apply filter (good for s > 20% but tail k'→∞ on low selectivity)
3. **Inline filter**: graph traversal with predicate check inside search loop

Recent algorithms improve connectivity under filtered subset.

**Filtered-DiskANN** [6]: extends Vamana pruning to be *label-aware*. During `RobustPrune`, preferentially keep neighbors whose labels intersect allowed filter space, building label-specific adjacency overlapped via *StitchedVamana* or *FilteredVamana*. Construction partitions labels into head/tail based on frequency to bound index blow-up.

**ACORN** [7]: *predicate-agnostic* generic over HNSW, preserving the original index unmodified. Insight: even if P_σ is sparse, densifying each node's *two-hop* neighborhood restores induced-subgraph navigability:

- Build HNSW with enlarged degree M+γ (γ=5–12)
- Search expands one-hop neighbors and checks predicate(nbr) → if fails, expands its neighbors (two-hop) before discarding
- Avoids needing label knowledge at build time → supports arbitrary logical conjunctions, range predicates, negatively-filtered predicates

The percolation threshold analysis under stochastic block model shows that below s_c ≈ 1/(<deg>) naive subgraph disintegrates; ACORN restores giant component up to s ≈ 0.5% empirically [7].

ACORN vs SIEVE vs UNG comparison:

| Algorithm | Index type | Arbitrary predicates? | Index overhead | Needs query workload? |
|-----------|------------|------------------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| Filtered-DiskANN Stitched | Tailored Vamana | limited label-set | ~1.5× edges | no |
| SeRF (range) | HNSW intervals | range only | ~1.4× | no |
| UNG LNG | DAG label graph | equality/inclusion only | O(|L| log |L|) indexes | yes |
| ACORN γ=12 | Dense HNSW | **yes** arbitrary | ~1.3× neighbors | no |
| SIEVE workload-aware | Subindexes collection | yes with pre-knowledge | O(K) subindexes | yes |
| FAVOR exclusion dist | Generic HNSW | yes | minimal (scalar per node) | no (runtime dist) |

*Predicate subgraph traversal* pseudocode:

```python
def acorn_search(graph, q, predicate, k=10, ef=128, gamma=12):
    entry = graph.medoid
    candidates = [(dist(q, entry), entry)]  # priority queue
    visited = set()
    results = []
    while candidates:
        d,u = heapq.heappop(candidates)
        if u in visited: continue
        visited.add(u)
        if predicate(u):
            results.append((d,u))
            if len(results) >= ef: break
        nbrs = graph.neighbors(u)
        for v in nbrs:
            if predicate(v):
                heapq.heappush(candidates, (dist(q,v), v))
            else:
                # 2-hop escape
                for w in graph.neighbors(v):
                    if predicate(w) and w not in visited:
                        heapq.heappush(candidates, (dist(q,w), w))
    return sorted(results)[:k]
```

*Tail latency note*: expansion factor EF required to achieve 90% recall under low selectivity varies 2–1000× depending on method. ACORN achieves state-of-the-art across full selectivity range at fixed recall without task-specific tuning, outperforming prior by 2–1000× QPS [7].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### Theoretical Sketches

***HNSW layer growth***: With m_L = 1/ln M, P(L > l) = exp(-l/m_L) = M^{-l}, thus E[max L] = O(log_M N) and number of layers visited is sublinear [1].

***Vamana RobustPrune guarantee***: For α-query metric, suppose q is closer to v than p is. If edge (p, argmin_{p*} d(p,p*)) removed incorrectly, then ∃ path p → p* → v with total cost ≤ α·d(p,v) bounding stretch factor [2][3].

***ANQ distortion bound***: Optimizing weighted loss yields E[MIPS-Error] ≤ C/d (||r_parallel||^2 + η ||r_perp||^2) with η < 1 versus standard η=1 [5]. This justifies threshold t=0.2 in SCANN; beyond threshold, inner products are more sensitive to parallel quantization noise [5].

***ACORN connectivity***: Let G be M-regular random graph, p = s be retention probability of nodes satisfying σ. Then density of induced subgraph after 1-hop expansion is p' = 1 - (1-p)^{M+1}; for γ-augmented HNSW, p'' = 1 - (1-p)^{M+γ} with 2-hop, giant component persists for p·(M+γ)^2 > 1 [7].

### Empirical Performance Summary (from refs)

*Benchmarks span SIFT1B, Deep1B, SPACEV1B, GLOVE-100, Wiki*:

- **DiskANN SIFT1B**: 95% R@1 at 5200 QPS mean 2.9 ms p99 6.1 ms, SSD I/O per query ≈ 140 (vs SPANN ~300) [2].
- **SCANN glove-100-angular**: at R@10=0.9, 2× QPS vs FAISS IVFPQ, 1.6× vs HNSW with ANQ disabled baseline [5].
- **ACORN**: On Paper dataset with 8-label conjunctive filters at s=1%, ACORN-γ achieves 0.9 recall@10 at 3000 QPS vs 150 QPS for post-filter + HNSW, 20 QPS for pre-filter scan [7].
- **Filtered-DiskANN (Microsoft)**: StitchedVamana shows large gains over IVF on filtered workloads [6].
- **PQ bytes vs recall**: OPQ-8 bytes on DEEP1B → R@10=0.65; PQ-32 bytes → 0.84; SCANN ANQ 32 bytes → 0.91 [4][5].

### Operational Tuning Rules

1. *Memory budget*: if DRAM < 0.03·|D|, use DiskANN hybrid with PQ 32 bytes/vec [2].
2. *Filter workload*: if selectivity unknown or mixed, prefer ACORN-γ=12; if range-only and small domain, SeRF may win [7][9].
3. *Inner product workloads*: always enable ANQ weighting with β=1.5–2.0, reorder 50–100 [5].
4. *Recall >0.95 regime*: increase beamwidth W not L for DiskANN, saves I/O due to monotonic frontier.

## 6 Limitations

No index Pareto-dominates all axes:

- **HNSW curse of dimensionality**: In d > 500, angular separation → uniform, graph degenerates to expander; *diversification* only delays growth in DC [1][3]. Linear scan may become competitive under tiny memory ratio 0% [DiskANN++ baselines].
- **Vamana static α**: Single α balances clustering vs. sparsity globally. On highly clustered corpora (e.g., product image embeddings with fashion subcategories), per-cluster tuned α improves recall by 5–8% but is not adaptive at present [2][3].
- **Quantization dimensionality mismatch**: For d_model=1536 OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, PQ with m=96 still incurs 3–6% recall loss vs full precision; required to marry OPQ rotation plus 4-bit residual training [4][5].
- **Filtered guarantees weak under correlation**: If predicate correlates negatively with query geometry (GLS-correlated benchmarks show *filter near but not semantically relevant*), recall cliff of >10× QPS drop appears even for ACORN [ACORN future vs FAVOR GLS study].
- **Dynamic consistency**: FreshDiskANN markings → phantom reads O(k/N) until compaction when deletes interleave with high-QPS search without copy-on-write snapshot isolation [8].
- **SSD endurance**: Hybrid indices write-heavy during build O(N·R log N) random writes ≈ 10 TB for 1B build, may exceed QLC endurance without buffering in ext4 direct I/O.
- **Tail latency due to 2-hop fanout**: For s=0.1%, ACORN expands ~γ·M invalid nodes per hop; worst-case QPS grain ≈ 300 μs vs median 60 μs → 5× variance [7].

## 7 Conclusion

We traversed the full stack of billion-scale vector search: **HNSW logarithmic routing** that separates scales via randomized layering [1], **Vamana RobustPrune** that enables SSD-resident graphs and serves 5000+ QPS at >95% recall [2], **anisotropic quantization** that reorients loss to preserve ranking under MIPS [5], and **predicate-aware schemes** that transform filtered ANNS from heuristic pre/post-filter to traversal-aware connectivity preservation (ACORN [7], Filtered-DiskANN [6]).

*Three enduring lessons*:

1. *Quantization and graph are complementary hierarchies* – keep approximate distances in DRAM, exact geometry on SSD, and predicates in traversal logic, not isolated modules.
2. *Predicate selectivity determines algorithm choice* – no single pre/post heuristic generalizes; **predicate-agnostic graphs with two-hop expansion** bridge low- to high-selectivity without workload knowledge.
3. *MIPS error is anisotropic* – blind ℓ2 compression obfuscates ranking; weighting parallel error yields 2× QPS at same recall.

Future systems will integrate CXL far-memory, GPU copy-engines, and LSM-structured mutable layers for real-time freshness.
