# Privacy-Preserving Telemetry and Heavy Hitters: Local Differential Privacy via Hadamard Response, Tree Aggregation for Heavy Hitters, Shuffle Model Amplification, and Frequency Oracle Lower Bounds

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, artifact-evaluable treatment of privacy-preserving telemetry and heavy hitters: local differential privacy via hadamard response, tree aggregation for heavy hitters, shuffle model amplification, and frequency oracle lower bounds, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 systems practice. We formalize cost models, invariants, and coherence protocols spanning differential privacy telemetry, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, ACM SIGCOMM/SOSP, and domain venues. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and specialized hardware traces including H100 CC, 96-core Genoa, NVLink4 600GBps, RoCEv2 400G CX7, Micron7450 1.6TB NVMe ZNS, Loihi2 Oheo Gulch, USRP X310 6GHz IQ streams, and event camera DSEC. We prove safety, liveness, approximation bounds, and privacy guarantees, deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation via bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001. Empirical results report 2–10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, ablations, and carbon accounting via RAPL. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage bounds, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance, mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient, scalable differential privacy telemetry production systems.
