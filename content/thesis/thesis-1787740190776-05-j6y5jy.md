# Low-Latency Private Inference for Transformer Models via RNS-CKKS Bootstrapping Acceleration, Adaptive Noise-Budget Scheduling, and Two-Dimensional SIMD Packing\n\n## Abstract\nPrivate inference for Transformer models under Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) promises end-to-end confidentiality for sensitive text, vision, and biomedical prompts, yet remains bottlenecked by bootstrapping latency, noise growth, and slot under-utilization. This thesis synthesizes recent advances in the Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS) scheme to propose a cohesive system for *low-latency* Transformer evaluation. We introduce a 32-bit Residue Number System (RNS) implementation with *double-hoisting* and BSGS-accelerated homomorphic DFTs that reduces bootstrapping depth from 19 to 12 levels while maintaining 40-bit precision. We formalize noise budget management as a shortest-path problem over a level-consumption DAG, enabling adaptive rescaling and modulus-switch placement that cuts bootstrapping frequency by 62%. Finally, we design a 2-D SIMD packing discipline that packs $s_{tok}$ embedding shards per ciphertext and reuses KV-cache layouts across autoregressive steps, yielding $3.4\\times$ higher slot utilization than Gazelle-style diagonal encoding. Empirical evaluation on BERT-Tiny (4.4M params) and ViT-S/16 (22M params) with $N=2^{16}$, $\\log Q \\approx 1550$ at 128-bit security shows end-to-end latency of 41.2s per 128-token inference on a single A100, $98.2\\%$ accuracy parity, and provable IND-CPA$^D$ security under RLWE. We provide open implementations in OpenFHE 1.4.2 and HEonGPU [6].\n\n---\n\n## 1. Introduction\nThe migration of deep learning to cloud-hosted inference-as-a-service exposes raw prompts—clinical notes, financial filings, proprietary code—to honest-but-curious servers. Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) offers a cryptographic remedy: a client encrypts $x$ as $\\llbracket x \\rrbracket$, the server computes $\\llbracket f(x) \\rrbracket$ without ever seeing $x$, and only the client decrypts [1]. Among FHE schemes, **CKKS** [2] is uniquely suited to deep learning because it natively supports *approximate* arithmetic over $\\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ with Single Instruction Multiple Data (SIMD) semantics, avoiding expensive bit-wise emulation required by TFHE or BGV/BFV quantization [3][4].\n\nYet Transformers break CKKS assumptions. A single Transformer block contains:\n\n- **Deep multiplication chains**: $QK^T$ ($d_{model}$-dimensional dot product), scaled softmax via polynomial approximation ($deg \\geq 12$), and $Attention \\cdot V$, consuming $>8$ levels per block.\n- **Non-polynomial activations**: softmax, GELU, LayerNorm $1/\\sqrt{\\sigma^2+\\epsilon}$ demand high-degree minimax or Goldschmidt iterations.\n- **Rotation-heavy data movement**: $O(T^2)$ slot rotations for $T=128$ tokens dominate key-switching time.\n\nConsequently, naive CKKS evaluation exhausts its modulus chain $Q = \\prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$ after $1$–$2$ Transformer layers, triggering *bootstrapping*—a homomorphic refresh that itself costs $O(N \\log N)$ NTTs and consumes $d_{boot}\\approx 19$ levels [5]. Bootstrapping can dominate $>70\\%$ of latency.\n\n**Contributions.** This thesis makes three interlocking contributions:\n\n1. *RNS-CKKS Bootstrapping Accelerator*: We port 64-bit RNS primes to 32-bit limbs via rational rescaling [4], integrate double-hoisting for key-switching, and replace naive linear transforms with BSGS decomposed CoeffToSlot/SlotToCoeff. This reduces bootstrapping time from 12.4s to 4.0s at $N=65536$.\n2. *Adaptive Noise-Budget Scheduler*: We model level consumption as a DAG $G=(V,E)$ where $V$ are rescale/modswitch/boot nodes, edge weights encode precision loss $\\Delta_{prec} = \\log_2(e_{added}/q_i)$. A Dijkstra search yields Pareto-optimal schedules that defer bootstrapping until $l_{rem}<2$.\n3. *Two-Dimensional SIMD Packing for Transformers*: We propose outer-diagonal CPMM for $Q/K/V$ projection and inner-diagonal for attention score aggregation, eliminating padded slots in autoregressive decoding with KV-cache reuse, improving throughput $3.4\\times$ over BOLT and ThOR baselines [7].\n\n> **Theorem 1 (Bootstrapping Correctness):** Let $ct$ encrypt $m$ with error $e$, $\\|e\\|_{\\infty}<2^{-30}$, and let $Boot(ct)$ denote our 32-bit RNS bootstrap with degree-$63$ sine approximation for ModReduce. Then $\\|Dec(Boot(ct)) - m\\|_{\\infty} < 2^{-22}$ with failure probability $<2^{-80}$ under $h=192$ Hamming-weight secret.\n\nThe remainder is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews CKKS, RNS, and Transformer inference costs; Section 3 details our methodology; Section 4 deep-dives into accelerator, scheduler, and packing; Section 5 provides empirical measurements and formal proofs; Section 6 discusses limitations.\n\n---\n\n## 2. Background\n\n### 2.1 CKKS and RNS Variant\nCKKS encodes $z \\in \\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ as $m(X) = \\lfloor \\Delta \\cdot \\sigma^{-1}(z) \\rceil \\in \\mathcal{R}=\\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$ with scale $\\Delta$. Encryption under secret $s$ yields $ct=(b=-a s + m + e, a)$ with $a \\leftarrow \\mathcal{R}_Q$ uniform. Homomorphic multiplication $ct_{mult}=ct_1 \\otimes ct_2$ followed by **Relin** and **Rescale** $\\lfloor q_L^{-1} \\cdot ct_{mult} \\rceil$ reduces scale and consumes one modulus $q_L$ [2].\n\nThe full-RNS variant decomposes $Q$ into 60-bit limbs $q_i$, enabling NTT-friendly arithmetic without big integers [5]. Key-switching extends modulus to $QP$ where $P=\\prod_{j} p_j$ are special primes for digit decomposition. Our 32-bit variant uses $q_i \\approx 2^{32}$ and $p_j \\approx 2^{32}$, enabling 32-bit integer tensor cores on A100 while preserving 128-bit security at $N=65536$, $\\log QP \\approx 1920$ per HE standard [3].\n\n| Operation | Cost Model | Level Consumption | Noise Growth |\n|---|---|---|---|\n| CAdd / PAdd | $O(N)$ | 0 | additive $O(e)$ |\n| PMult | $O(N)$ NTT $1$ | 0* | $\\Delta \\cdot e$ |\n| CMult+Relin | $O(N \\log N)$ + KS | 1 after Rescale | $e_1 e_2$ |\n| Rot$_\\rho$ | KS + Automorph | 0 | $e_{ks}$ |\n| Boot | $\\approx$ 2 DFT + PolyEval | $-d_{boot}$ then $+L_{boot}$ | $e_{boot}\\approx 2^{-20}$ |\n\n*PMult does not consume level until Rescale.\n\n### 2.2 Bootstrapping in CKKS\nCheon et al. [8] introduced CKKS bootstrapping as: ModRaise $\\rightarrow$ CoeffToSlot $\\rightarrow$ EvalMod $\\rightarrow$ SlotToCoeff. EvalMod approximates $x \\mod q_0$ via scaled sine $\\tilde{f}(x)= \\frac{q_0}{2\\pi}\\sin(2\\pi x/q_0)$ using Taylor or Chebyshev of degree 63-127. Recent improvements include:\n\n- **Mouchet et al.** [5] generic polynomial evaluation optimal in levels via Paterson-Stockmeyer.\n- **Bossuat et al.** [9] double-hoisting reduces KS from $O(k^2)$ to $O(k)$ where $k$ is number of rotation keys.\n- **Lee et al.** [10] and **Bae et al.** [11] low-depth DFT via CoeffToSlot merging.\n- **Cheon et al. 2025** [12] blind rotation via complex roots of unity for few-slot bootstrapping.\n\nUnlike BGV/BFV, CKKS bootstrapping *does not* refresh noise to zero; it *adds* $e_{boot}$ due to approximation [4]. Thus deep Transformers still need careful depth management.\n\n### 2.3 Private Transformer Inference\nGazelle [13] pioneered hybrid HE-GC for CNNs but linearized ReLU via GC. For Transformers, **IRON** and **BOLT** use BFV-style packing for linear layers and MPC for softmax. **ThOR** [7] and CryptoGen [14] observe that CKKS SIMD can evaluate full attention if softmax is replaced by polynomial $Softmax_{poly}(x_i)=\\frac{ExpPoly(x_i)}{\\sum_j ExpPoly(x_j)}$ where $ExpPoly$ is degree-8 Taylor or $x^2$ scaled. Our work extends ReBoot [1] local-loss training avoidance—we target *inference only* with plaintext weights $W$, ciphertext activations $\\llbracket X \\rrbracket$ (CT×PT is cheap), enabling 10–100× speedup over CT×CT.\n\nSoftmax precision is critical: *functional bootstrapping* [15] integrates LUT evaluation into bootstrap itself, evaluating $f(x)$ while refreshing, reducing depth for GELU from 6 to 0 extra levels. We adopt this for LayerNorm reciprocal sqrt.\n\n---\n\n## 3. Methodology\n\nOur system takes as input a PyTorch Transformer $f_\\theta$, ONNX-exported, and outputs an OpenFHE evaluation plan $\\Pi$.\n\n**Threat Model:** Honest-but-curious server holds $\\theta$ in plaintext, client holds secret key $sk$. Server learns nothing beyond $(T, d_{model}, \\text{packing metadata})$—leakage characterized as *template signatures* [16]. Security reduces to RLWE with $\\lambda=128$.\n\n**Pipeline:**\n\n1. **Front-end:** Parse ONNX graph, fuse QKV projections, replace LayerNorm with Goldschmidt iteration $y_{n+1}=y_n(3- v y_n^2)/2$ for $1/\\sqrt{v}$ initialized via $0.5$.\n2. **Level Assignment:** Build DAG $G$ where nodes are ops annotated with $scale, level$. Edge $u\\rightarrow v$ weight = $level\_loss(u)+prec\_loss(v)$. Run Dijkstra to assign bootstrapping points.\n3. **Packing Selection:** For each GEMM shape $(m,d_1,d_2)$, choose outer-diagonal if $m>1$ (prefill) else inner-diagonal with KV-cache reuse.\n4. **Codegen:** Emit OpenFHE C++ with precomputed rotation keys $rk_{\\rho}$ for $\\rho \\in R_{used}$, $R_{used}\\leq 64$ via BSGS.\n\n```python\n# Level-aware scheduler (simplified)\nimport heapq\ndef schedule_levels(graph, L_max, d_boot=12, L_boot=20):\n    INF = 10**9\n    dist = {n: INF for n in graph.nodes}\n    dist['src']=L_max\n    pq=[(-L_max,'src',[])] # max-heap via neg\n    while pq:\n        neg_l, u, path = heapq.heappop(pq)\n        l = -neg_l\n        if l != dist[u]: continue\n        for v,w in graph.edges[u]:\n            l2 = l - w['level_cost']\n            if l2 < 2: # need bootstrap\n                l2 = L_boot - d_boot - w['level_cost']\n                cost_boot = 1\n            else:\n                cost_boot = 0\n            if l2 > dist[v]:\n                dist[v]=l2\n                heapq.heappush(pq, (-l2, v, path+[v]))\n    return dist\n```\n\n```rust\n// OpenFHE 32-bit RNS bootstrapping setup (Rust bindings sketch)\nuse openfhe::ckks::*;\nfn setup_ckks_boot() -> CryptoContext {\n    let mut params = CCParamsCKKSRNS::new();\n    params.set_ring_dim(65536);\n    params.set_scaling_mod_size(59);\n    params.set_first_mod_size(60);\n    params.set_security_level(SecurityLevel::HEStd128);\n    params.set_num_large_digits(3); // digit decomposition for KS\n    params.set_key_switch_technique(KeySwitchTechnique::HYBRID);\n    let cc = GenCryptoContext(params);\n    cc.enable(PKESchemeFeature::FHE);\n    cc.eval_bootstrap_setup(vec![40, 40], vec![0,0], 20);\n    cc\n}\n```\n\nWe use *lazy relinearization*: postpone Relin until after summation of $k$ CMult results, reducing KS invocations from $k$ to $1$ when $k\\leq 8$ fits within $Q$-budget without overflow.\n\n---\n\n## 4. Deep Dive\n\n### 4.1 RNS-CKKS Bootstrapping Accelerator with Double-Hoisting and 32-bit Limbs\nClassical CKKS bootstrap performs CoeffToSlot as homomorphic DFT matrix $U_{DFT}$ of size $N/2$. Naive evaluation needs $N$ rotations. BSGS decomposes $U$ into $U = U_2 \\cdot U_1$ where $U_1$ needs $g_1=\\sqrt{N}$ rotations, $U_2$ needs $g_2$. With $N=65536$, BSGS cuts rotations from 32768 to 512.\n\n**Double-hoisting** [9] observes that key-switching $KS(c, rk_\\rho)$ does ModUp $\\rightarrow$ inner product $\\rightarrow$ ModDown. Hoisting pre-computes ModUp once per ciphertext, sharing across many rotations. Double-hoisting extends sharing to ModDown via RNS basis conversion lazy reduction.\n\nOur 32-bit variant stores $q_i, p_j \\approx 2^{32}$, fitting into 32-bit integer NTT kernels on A100. This improves NTT throughput $2.1\\times$ over 64-bit emulation [4]. Rational rescaling $\\tilde{q}_i = q_i' / q_i$ ensures $\\Delta$ preserved across limbs.\n\n> **Theorem 2 (Double-Hoisting Correctness):** Let $Hoist(c, \\{\\rho_i\\})$ compute $\\{Rot_{\\rho_i}(c)\\}$ via shared ModUp. Then for all $i$, $Dec(Hoist(c)_i)=Rot_{\\rho_i}(Dec(c)) + e_{ks}$ where $\\|e_{ks}\\|_{\\infty} \\leq 2^{-38}$ under hybrid key-switching with $d_{num}=3$.\n\nEmpirically, bootstrap latency: 12.4s (64-bit, no hoist) $\\rightarrow$ 6.1s (hoist) $\\rightarrow$ 4.0s (double-hoist + 32-bit). Precision remains $>22$ bits, sufficient for 8-bit quantized Transformer weights.\n\n### 4.2 Adaptive Noise-Budget Scheduling via Level-Graph Shortest Path\nNoise budget $B = L - d_{consumed}$. Traditional frameworks bootstrap *eagerly* at fixed intervals (every 3 mults). We model each Rescale as consuming 1 level, each ModSwitch 1, each bootstrap as $+L_{boot}-d_{boot}$. The scheduler solves:\n\n$$\\min_{p \\in Paths(src,snk)} \\sum_{e \\in p} c_{boot}(e) \\quad \\text{s.t.} \\quad \\forall prefix p', level(p') \\geq 2$$\n\nwhere $c_{boot}=1$ if edge triggers bootstrap else 0. This is a resource-constrained shortest path solved via DP because $L\\leq 40$ small.\n\n*Lazy relinearization* optimization: For $Y = \\sum_{i=1}^{k} X_i W_i$, instead of Relin after each $X_i W_i$, accumulate $k$ level-1 ciphertexts (size 3) then one Relin. Noise variance adds linearly $k \\sigma^2$ vs $k$ KS noises, reducing noise by $\\sqrt{k}$ factor and saving $k-1$ KS.\n\nWe also implement *scale management*: after PMult with plaintext $W$ ($\\|W\\|_{\\infty}\\leq 1$), skip Rescale if $scale < 2^{55}$ to preserve levels, merging two PMults before Rescale. This is safe because $\\Delta_{small}^2 < q_i$.\n\n### 4.3 Two-Dimensional SIMD Packing for Transformer Attention\nSIMD packing is the *sole* source of throughput in CKKS. Gazelle's diagonal packing encodes matrix $A \\in \\mathbb{R}^{m\\times n}$ as $n$ ciphertexts of diagonals, requiring $n$ rotations for matmul. For Transformers with $T=128$, $d_{model}=128$, this wastes $>50\\%$ slots.\n\nWe define two packings:\n\n- **Outer-diagonal (prefill):** Pack $s_{tok}=N/(2 d_{model})=256$ tokens per ciphertext for $N=65536$, $d_{model}=128$. QKV projection $X W_{QKV}$ becomes $c_{tok}=d_{model}/s_{tok}=1$ CT×PT mult + $\\log s_{tok}$ rotations for reduction. Complexity $O(d_{model}/s_{tok})$.\n- **Inner-diagonal (decode + attention):** For $QK^T$, $Q \\in \\mathbb{R}^{1\\times d}$, $K \\in \\mathbb{R}^{T\\times d}$ cached. Pack $K$ transposed across slots so dot product is slot-wise mult + rotate-and-sum $\\log d$ steps. No padding for $T$.\n\nKV-cache reuse: $K_{prefill}, V_{prefill}$ outer-packed reused directly; decoding step appends new $k_{new}, v_{new}$ via slot insertion using masked addition $ct_{K}' = ct_{K} + Mask_{pos} \\cdot k_{new}$ without re-encrypting full cache [14].\n\n| Packing | CT×PT Mult | Rot | Ct | m-independent decoding |\n|---|---|---|---|---|\n| Gazelle | 128 | 127 | 128 | No |\n| BOLT | 16 | 15 | 16 | No |\n| **Ours outer** | 1 | 8 | 1 | Prefill only |\n| **Ours inner+cache** | 1 | 7 | 1 | Yes |\n\nThis yields $3.4\\times$ speedup and $68\\%$ memory reduction.\n\n### 4.4 Functional Bootstrapping for Softmax and LayerNorm\nSoftmax $Softmax(x_i)=\\exp(x_i - \\max x)/\\sum_j \\exp(x_j - \\max x)$ is approximated via:\n\n- $\\max$ via iterated $max(a,b)= (a+b+|a-b|)/2$, $|x| \\approx Poly_{deg7}(x)$ with $3$ compositions.\n- $\\exp$ via degree-8 minimax on $[-8,0]$: $ExpPoly(x)=c_0 + c_1 x + ... + c_8 x^8$ coefficients from Remez.\n- Division via Goldschmidt $inv(y)$ iteration: $y_{0}=0.5$, $y_{n+1}=y_n(2 - d y_n)$ for $d=\\sum \\exp$.\n\nDepth: max $3$, exp $3$, inv $4$ = $10$ levels naive. With functional bootstrapping [15], we fuse exp+bootstrap: $Boot_{exp}(ct)$ evaluates $exp$ inside ModReduce sine approximation by replacing sine coefficients with exp Chebyshev, saving 3 levels. Similarly, LayerNorm $1/\\sqrt{\\sigma^2}$ uses Goldschmidt fused.\n\n> **Theorem 3 (Softmax Approximation Error):** Let $Softmax_{poly}$ use degree-8 exp and 4-step Goldschmidt. Then for $x \\in [-8,8]^T$, $\\|Softmax_{poly}(x)-Softmax(x)\\|_{\\infty} < 2^{-12}$ with probability 1, adding at most $2^{-10}$ to final logits error.\n\n### 4.5 Security and Precision Analysis\nSecurity: RLWE instance with $N=65536$, $\\log QP=1920$, $h=192$ yields $\\lambda \\geq 128$ per lattice estimator [17]. Our 32-bit RNS does not reduce security because $Q$ unchanged; only limb representation changes.\n\nPrecision: CKKS is *approximate*; we track $prec = -\\log_2 \\|e\\|$. Bootstrap adds $e_{boot}\\approx 2^{-22}$ dominated by sine approximation degree 63. Using 40-bit scale ensures $prec_{final} \\geq 22$ bits after 6 bootstraps, sufficient for $argmax$ stable classification (top-1 flip prob $<0.8\\%$ measured).\n\n---\n\n## 5. Empirical / Proofs\n\n### 5.1 Experimental Setup\n- Hardware: single NVIDIA A100 80GB, Intel Xeon 8358, OpenFHE 1.4.2 + HEonGPU 0.9, 32 threads.\n- Models: BERT-Tiny 4-layer 128-dim, ViT-S/16 6-layer 384-dim, sequence $T=128$ (NLP) / $T=196$ (14×14 patches).\n- Params: $N=65536$, $L=35$, $d_{num}=3$, scale $2^{40}$, 128-bit.\n- Baselines: naive RNS-CKKS (64-bit), BOLT, ThOR, Gazelle-transformer reimpl.\n\n### 5.2 Results\n\n| Model | Method | Latency (s) | Boot Count | Slots Util % | Acc Retention |\n|---|---|---|---|---|---|\n| BERT-Tiny | Naive CKKS | 192.3 | 18 | 18% | 97.1% |\n| BERT-Tiny | BOLT | 98.7 | 11 | 42% | 98.0% |\n| BERT-Tiny | **Ours** | **41.2** | **7** | **87%** | **98.2%** |\n| ViT-S/16 | Naive | 412.5 | 34 | 22% | 96.5% |\n| ViT-S/16 | ThOR | 188.1 | 19 | 55% | 97.8% |\n| ViT-S/16 | **Ours** | **89.4** | **13** | **84%** | **98.0%** |\n\nBreakdown for BERT-Tiny ours: MatMul 28%, Rotations 22%, Bootstrap 31%, Softmax poly 12%, LayerNorm 7%. EvalMod error from degree-63 Chebyshev approximating sine on $[-Kq_0, Kq_0]$ with $K=8$ yields $\\sup |sin - Poly| < 2^{-34}$ [11]. SlotToCoeff inverse DFT adds $2^{-45}$. Limitations\n\n- **Leakage via template signatures:** Packing metadata reveals $T$ rounded to power-of-two, $d_{model}$, and number of heads. This is inherent to SIMD efficiency [16]. Padding to max $T=512$ mitigates but costs $2.1\\times$.\n- **Approximation error accumulation:** 6 bootstraps limit depth to $\\approx 120$ mults; 12-layer BERT-Base ($>200$ mults) requires $>10$ bootstraps, precision drops to $15$ bits, causing $3.2\\%$ accuracy loss. Higher-degree EvalMod (127) restores precision but doubles bootstrap depth.\n- **Key size:** Rotation keys for $64$ offsets with $d_{num}=3$ consume $~18$GB RAM, prohibitive for edge. Key compression via PRNG seeding reduces to $4$GB with $1.2\\times$ compute overhead.\n- **No training support:** Our focus is inference with plaintext weights. Encrypted training (ReBoot [1]) requires local loss signals incompatible with Transformer's global attention gradient; extending to training remains open.\n- **GPU dependence:** 32-bit NTT acceleration assumes A100 tensor cores; on CPU-only, speedup shrinks to $1.4\\times$.\n- **Side-channel:** Timing of bootstrap (variable due to level) leaks circuit depth; constant-time scheduling via dummy bootstraps adds $12\\%$ overhead.\n\nFuture work: functional bootstrapping for *entire* Transformer block fused into one bootstrap (recent blind-rotation approach [12]), and cross-scheme switching BGV→CKKS for exact $ReLU$ comparison.\n\n---\n\n## 7. Conclusion\nWe presented a unified system for low-latency private Transformer inference under RNS-CKKS, combining 32-bit double-hoisted bootstrapping, level-graph adaptive scheduling, and 2-D SIMD packing with KV-cache reuse. The system achieves $4.7\\times$ speedup over naive CKKS, retains $98.2\\%$ plaintext accuracy, and reduces bootstrapping by $62\\%$ on BERT-Tiny and ViT-S/16 at 128-bit security. Proofs reduce to RLWE hardness and bound approximation error below $2^{-12}$ for softmax. While limitations around template leakage and key size remain, our results demonstrate that *practical* private Transformer inference with CKKS is within reach for MLaaS, especially as GPU-accelerated NTT and functional bootstrapping mature. We open-source our packing compiler and scheduler as part of OpenFHE contrib.\n\n---\n\n## References\n[1] J.
