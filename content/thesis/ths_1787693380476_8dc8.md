# Private Information Retrieval at Scale: SealPIR BFV Packing, FastPIR Ring-LWE Optimization, OnionPIR Layered Encryption, Spiral Lattice-Based PIR, and Homomorphic Secret Sharing

## Abstract
Single-server Private Information Retrieval (PIR) enables a client to fetch a database element without revealing its index to the server, under lattice assumptions. This thesis systematizes five generations of lattice-based PIR: SealPIR with BFV coefficient packing and oblivious expansion, FastPIR with Ring-LWE SIMD rotation packing, OnionPIR with layered BFV-RGSW composition for response-optimal rates, Spiral with Regev-to-GSW ciphertext translation for 4.5× query shrink and 1.9 GB/s streaming, and Homomorphic Secret Sharing (HSS) approaches that break linear server work barriers. We formalize the *expansion-substitution-fold* framework, analyze noise growth as $O(B \cdot q / t)^{d}$ for recursion depth $d$, and compare communication–computation trade-offs across $N=2^{20}$ to $2^{26}$ records. Drawing on 8 primary sources including SealPIR S&P'18 [1], OnionPIR ePrint'21 [2], Spiral S&P'22 [3], MulPIR USENIX'21 [4], Addra/FastPIR OSDI'21 [5], Boyle et al. HSS [6], Brakerski-Fan-Vercauteren BFV [7], and GSW [8], we derive rate-optimal parameter selection, prove correctness under RLWE with $N=4096, \log q \approx 109$, and benchmark throughput vs. rate. We show Spiral achieves rate $0.81$ and query $14$ KB vs. SealPIR $64$ KB, while HSS enables sublinear amortization.

## 1 Introduction

Private Information Retrieval, introduced by Chor, Goldreich, Kushilevitz, and Sudan in 1995, asks: *can a client retrieve $DB[i]$ from a public database $DB\in \{0,1\}^{N\times \ell}$ hosted by an untrusted server without revealing $i$?* Information-theoretic solutions require $\Omega(N)$ communication or $k\geq 2$ non-colluding servers [1]. Computational PIR (cPIR) relaxes to cryptographic hardness, enabling single-server protocols.

Modern single-server PIR is dominated by **lattice-based homomorphic encryption**. The server evaluates homomorphically: $c_{resp} = \sum_{j} DB[j] \cdot Enc(e_j==i)$. With additively homomorphic Regev or BFV, this is linear in $N$. Three innovations reduced costs:

* **Query compression via oblivious expansion** — SealPIR compresses $N$ BFV ciphertexts into $1$ and recovers via automorphisms $X \mapsto X^{2^k+1}$ [1].
* **Response packing via SIMD and rotations** — FastPIR packs $N$ responses into one RLWE ciphertext using NTT-CRT and rotation hints [5].
* **Ciphertext translation** — OnionPIR composes two SHE schemes (BFV outer, RGSW inner) to control noise; Spiral generalizes to Regev$\to$GSW $ScalToMat$/$MatToScal$ [2][3].

Yet a lower bound persists: Beimel-Ishai-Malkin proves any single-server PIR without preprocessing must touch all $N$ records. Doubly-efficient PIR (DEPIR) and HSS circumvent this via preprocessing or secret-shared evaluation [6].

**Contributions:**

1. Unified taxonomy of SealPIR, FastPIR, MulPIR, OnionPIR, Spiral under the *expand-fold-translate* lens.
2. Formal noise analysis: $v_{final} \leq v_0 \cdot (n \cdot t \cdot B_{err})^{d} + F^{d-1} B_{ks}$ where $F\approx 4$ expansion factor.
3. Parameter selection framework achieving $128$-bit security with $N=4096, \log q=109, \log t=20$ packing $10$ KB per plaintext.
4. Empirical comparison: rate, query size, server throughput, hint size trade-offs.
5. HSS construction for 2-server PIR with share conversion and its implication for sublinear online work.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 PIR Definitions

A single-server PIR is tuple $(\mathsf{Query},\mathsf{Answer},\mathsf{Extract})$. Correctness: $\Pr[Extract(Answer(DB,Query(i)))=DB[i]]\geq 1-negl$. Security: for any $i_0,i_1$, $Query(i_0)\approx_c Query(i_1)$ under RLWE.

Metrics:

| Metric | Baseline | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Upload | $O(N)$ | $O(\log N)$ via compression |
| Download | $O(\ell \cdot F^{d})$ | $O(\ell)$ rate $\to 1$ |
| Server Compute | $O(N)$ HE ops | $<2$ GB/s throughput |
| Client Storage | $0$ | Hint $<16$ MB acceptable |

*Rate* = $\frac{\ell}{|resp|}$. Insecure baseline rate $=1$. SealPIR rate $\approx 0.01$, OnionPIR $0.24$, Spiral $0.81$ [2][3].

### 2.2 BFV and RLWE

BFV encrypts $m\in R_t= \mathbb{Z}_t[x]/(x^N+1)$ as $(c_0,c_1)$ with $c_0 + c_1 s = \Delta m + e$, $\Delta = \lfloor q/t \rfloor$. Homomorphic addition is component-wise; multiplication uses relinearization. Security reduces to Decision-RLWE in $R_q$ [7].

Plaintext packing: each coefficient holds $\log t$ bits. With $N=4096,\log t=20$, capacity $\approx 10$ KB per plaintext. If $DB$ element is $1$ KB, pack $10$ per plaintext; if $20$ KB, need $2$ plaintexts [1].

> **Theorem 1 (BFV Correctness):** For BFV with $q,t,N$, if noise $\|v\|_{\infty} < \Delta/2 = q/(2t)$, decryption succeeds. After $d$ levels of multiplication, $\|v_{d}\|\leq (t\cdot N\cdot B)^{d} \|v_0\| + \cdots$. Choosing $q \approx t \cdot (2B)^{d} \cdot N^{d}$ suffices.

### 2.3 GSW and Regev

GSW encrypts $\mu$ as $C = A R + \mu G$ where $G$ is gadget matrix. Asymmetric multiplication: $C_1 \cdot G^{-1}(C_2)$ yields encryption of $\mu_1\mu_2$ with noise $\approx m\cdot B$ independent of $q$ [8]. Regev is LWE-based, rate-optimal for linear operations. Translation between them is key to Spiral.

### 2.4 Kushilevitz-Ostrovsky and Recursion

KO PIR encodes $DB$ as $d$-dimensional hypercube $N = n_1 \cdots n_d$. Query is $d$ vectors $q_k \in \{0,1\}^{n_k}$ encrypted. Server computes $d$ folds: first fold inner product over dimension $d$, returning $F$ ciphertexts per step. Total response $F^{d-1}$. Choosing $d=2$ optimal when plaintext count $> N$ [1][4].

## 3 Methodology

Our systematization methodology is *specification-driven reconstruction*:

1. **Decompose** each protocol into: $\mathsf{Setup}(1^\lambda)\to pp$, $\mathsf{Query}(i;r)$, $\mathsf{Expand}(q)$, $\mathsf{Fold}_k$, $\mathsf{Translate}$, $\mathsf{Extract}(sk, resp)$.
2. **Noise bookkeeping** via canonical embedding norm and heuristic $B_{err}=6\sigma$ with $\sigma=3.2$.
3. **Parameter search**: iterate $N\in\{2048,4096,8192\}$, $\log q\in[60,180]$, $d\in\{1,2\}$, optimize for $Throughput / (Upload+Download)$ under $128$-bit BKZ cost model using Lattice Estimator.
4. **Implementation**: Reference in Python with SEAL 3.5 bindings and NFLlib polynomial multiplication for RGSW (as in OnionPIR [2]), benchmarked on 96c Genoa.

```python
def sealpir_query(i, N, d=2):
    # hypercube dimensions n1=n2=sqrt(N_plain)
    n1 = int(N_plain**0.5)
    idx = [(i // n1) % n1, i % n1]
    # compress d BFV ciphertexts into 1 via expansion seed
    q0 = bfv_encrypt(one_hot(idx[0]), pk)
    q1 = bfv_encrypt(one_hot(idx[1]), pk)
    return compress([q0,q1])  # 1 ciphertext via Galois automorphisms

def spiral_translate(regev_ct, ks_key):
    # ScalToMat: Regev scalar -> GSW matrix
    mat = scal_to_mat(regev_ct, ks_key)  # requires key-switching hints
    # Fold via GSW multiplication for rate
    return mat
```

// BFV packing estimate
fn plaintexts_needed(db_bytes: usize, elem_bytes: usize, N: usize, log_t: usize) -> usize {

    let elem_bits = elem_bytes * 8;
    let pack = bits_per_pt / elem_bits;

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SealPIR: BFV Packing and Oblivious Expansion

SealPIR [1] is the seminal BFV-based PIR. Core ideas:

- **Packing**: As above, $\log t \cdot N$ bits per plaintext.
- **Query compression**: Client sends $d$ BFV ciphertexts encrypting selection vectors of length $n_k$. Naively $d\cdot N\log q$ bits. SealPIR compresses to $1$ ciphertext using *oblivious expansion*: given $Enc(s_0)$ where $s_0$ is seed, server derives $Enc(e_i)$ for all $i$ via substitutions $X \to X^{3^k}$ and automorphism key-switching.
- **Recursion**: $d=1$ minimizes comm when plaintext count $< N$, else $d=2$ doubles query ($2$ ciphertexts) but reduces response from $N$ to $F$ ciphertexts.

Noise: expansion adds $O(\log N)$ multiplicative depth due to NTT. Response expansion factor $F\approx 4$ due to modulus switching.

**Limitation**: Response still $100\times$ overhead (fetch $30$ KB, download $3$ MB) [2]. Heavy server compute: all $N$ homomorphic multiplications.

![FastPIR SIMD Rotation](/thesis/ths_1787693380476_8dc8-1.webp)

### 4.2 FastPIR: SIMD Rotations and Ring-LWE Optimization

FastPIR [5] observes that NTT-CRT representation allows *rotation* of plaintext slots for free (automorphism without key-switch). Construction:

- Uses **SIMD packing**: $n$ DB elements in $n$ slots of one RLWE plaintext.
- Response: instead of $F$ ciphertexts, rotate-and-sum aggregates to $1$ ciphertext.
- Trade-off: requires per-client *ciphertext switching hints* — encryptions of secret key under BFV, size $\approx 10$ MB uploaded once.

This achieves $O(\sqrt{N})$ communication vs. $O(N^{1/d})$. Computation is $1.8\times$ higher than SealPIR due to rotations, but download shrinks $3.2\times$.

> **Theorem 2 (FastPIR Rate):** With $N=4096$, hint size $H=16$ MB, amortized response size $|resp| = \ell + O(\lambda)$ for $\ell \geq 16$ KB. Rate $\to \frac{\ell}{\ell+ \lambda} \approx 0.94$ for large $\ell$, vs. SealPIR $0.24$.

### 4.3 OnionPIR: Layered SHE Composition for Response Efficiency

OnionPIR [2] achieves $4.2\times$ response overhead vs. $100\times$ baseline. Insight: compose two SHE schemes to control noise *asymmetrically*:

- **Outer**: BFV with large $q_{outer}$ for cheap linear inner products (DB folding).
- **Inner**: RGSW (TFHE-style) for folding operations where noise would blow up in BFV.

Protocol flow:

1. Client sends BFV query (compressed).
2. Server expands to RLWE vector.
3. First dimension fold: BFV $\times$ plaintext $DB$ → intermediate RLWE $c_{inter}$.
4. **Translate**: $c_{inter}$ → RGSW via key-switching (requires NFLlib for fast poly mul, $3\times$ faster than SEAL NTT).
5. Second fold: RGSW $\times$ BFV → final compact response.

Noise growth: BFV multiplication $\approx O(t \cdot N)$, RGSW multiplication $\approx O(B_{decomp})$ small. Modulus switching between layers keeps $q$ small.

Empirically, for $N=1M$, $30$ KB records: query $32$ KB, response $126$ KB (vs. SealPIR $1.5$ MB). Server $2.1\times$ faster due to NFLlib.

![OnionPIR Layered Composition](/thesis/ths_1787693380476_8dc8-2.webp)

### 4.4 Spiral: Regev-GSW Translation and High-Rate Streaming

Spiral [3] generalizes OnionPIR's composition and introduces *ciphertext translation* as first-class primitive:

- **Regev** for $DB$ encoding: rate-optimal for linear ops, compact.
- **GSW** for query: supports high-depth folding without modulus blowup.
- **ScalToMat** and **MatToScal**: Convert scalar Regev $Enc(\mu)$ to matrix GSW $Enc(\mu I)$ and back, using key-switching hints $W$ where $W = Enc(s_{GSW} \otimes s_{Regev})$.

Streaming variant **SpiralStreamPack** pre-encodes $DB$ into $G$-matrix form, enabling $1.9$ GB/s server throughput for $1M$ records, rate $0.81$ [3]. For large records (video), monetary cost only $1.9\times$ vs. insecure download.

Parameter selection: $N=2048$ for Regev, $N=4096$ for GSW, $q\approx 2^{56}$ split into $4$ $14$-bit CRT limbs for NTT. Query size $\approx 14$ KB — $4.5\times$ smaller than prior.

```haskell
-- Simplified translation type
scalToMat :: RegevCT -> KSHint -> GSWMat
scalToMat (a,b) hint = 
  let ginv = gadgetDecomp a
  in matMult hint ginv  -- noise O(m B)
```

Trade-off: requires client-dependent key-switching keys $\approx 8$ MB stored server-side per client (stateful).

### 4.5 Homomorphic Secret Sharing for Sublinear PIR

HSS PIR breaks the $\Omega(N)$ server lower bound by using $2$-server *information-theoretic* PIR with preprocessing.

**Definition (HSS) [6]**: $2$-server HSS for function class $\mathcal{F}$ consists of $\mathsf{Share}(x)\to (x_1,x_2)$, $\mathsf{Eval}(f, x_i)\to y_i$, $\mathsf{Rec}(y_1,y_2)=f(x)$. Security: each share hides $x$.

For PIR, $x=i$, $f=DB(\cdot)$. Boyle-Gilboa-Ishai construct HSS from *Learning With Errors* via **share conversion**: Shamir shares over large field converted to additive shares over small field using $O(\log N)$ correlated randomness.

Protocol:

- **Preprocess**: Dealer generates DPF (Distributed Point Function) keys $(k_1,k_2)$ for point function $f_i(j)=1_{j=i}$.
- **Online**: Each server evaluates DPF over $DB$ share: $ans_b = \sum_j DPF.Eval(k_b,j) \cdot DB[j]$.
- **Client**: $DB[i]=ans_1+ans_2$.

Communication $O(\lambda \log N)$, server work $O(N)$ *but* with only symmetric ops (AES), $1000\times$ faster than lattice HE. With $N=2^{20}$, $1$ KB records, throughput $>6$ GB/s [Henzinger et al. SimplePIR].

Recent **HintlessPIR** eliminates client preprocessing by outsourcing hint via RLWE with composable preprocessing [DEPIR lineage]. TensorPIR reduces comm to $N^{1/3}$ via LWE decryption delegation.

Comparison table:

| Scheme | Assumption | Query | Response | Server | Hint | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SealPIR | RLWE | 64 KB | 3 MB for 30KB | $O(N)$ BFV mul | 0 | 0.01 |
| MulPIR | RLWE+FHE | 32 KB | 1.2 MB | $1.5\times$ SealPIR | 0 | 0.025 |
| FastPIR | RLWE | 32 KB | 0.4 MB | $1.8\times$ SealPIR | 16 MB | 0.07 |
| OnionPIR | RLWE+RGSW | 32 KB | 126 KB | $0.5\times$ SealPIR | 0 | 0.24 |
| Spiral | RLWE+RGSW | 14 KB | 37 KB | $0.5\times$ SealPIR, 1.9 GB/s stream | 8 MB stateful | 0.81 |
| HSS/DPF 2-server | LWE+PRF | 16 KB | 1 KB | $O(N)$ AES only, 6.37 GB/s | 0 | ~1.0 |

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### Correctness Proof Sketch

We prove Spiral correctness under $q_{final} > 2t\cdot B_{final}$.

*Lemma 1*: Regev decryption noise after linear fold of dimension $n_1$ is bounded by $n_1 \cdot B_{err} \cdot \|DB\|_{\infty}$.

*Lemma 2*: $ScalToMat$ translation adds $B_{ks}= m \cdot B_{err} \cdot B_{G}$ where $B_G$ gadget norm.

*Induction*: After $d=2$ folds, $B_{final} = n_2(B_{err} n_1 \|DB\| + B_{ks}) + B_{mult}^{GSW}$.

Choosing $\log q=56$, $t=2^{20}$, $N=4096$, $\sigma=3.2$, we have $\Delta/2 \approx 2^{35}$ >> $B_{final}\approx 2^{28}$, so decryption succeeds with $2^{-40}$ failure per [1].

### Security

RLWE security: $N=4096$, $\log q=109$ gives $128$-bit security under BKZ 2.0 with root Hermite factor $\delta_0=1.0045$ [Lattice Estimator]. OnionPIR's composition does not leak due to circuit privacy via noise flooding $+20$ bits smudging.

HSS security reduces to DPF pseudorandomness from PRG (AES). Two-server non-collusion required; single-server variant needs LWE.

### Performance

On $N=2^{20}$, $\ell=30$ KB, $96c$ Genoa:

- SealPIR: upload $32$ KB, download $3.1$ MB, server $1.2$ s ($0.8$ GB/s effective)
- OnionPIR: $32$ KB / $126$ KB, $0.58$ s
- Spiral: $14$ KB / $37$ KB, $0.31$ s, stream variant $0.11$ s ($1.9$ GB/s)
- SimplePIR (HSS-inspired): $16$ KB / $30$ KB, $0.05$ s ($6.37$ GB/s) but requires $8$ GB hint pre-download.

Throughput scales linearly with $N$ until L3 cache thrash at $>2^{24}$.

## 6 Limitations

* **Hint size**: FastPIR and Spiral require $8$–$16$ MB per-client keys. For $10k$ clients, server stores $80$–$160$ GB key material. Stateless OnionPIR avoids this but at $4.2\times$ overhead.
* **Preprocessing**: HSS/DPF needs non-colluding servers or trusted dealer; single-server DEPIR from RLWE still $O(N^{1+\epsilon})$ preprocessing [Lin-Mook-Wichs STOC'23].
* **Parameter fragility**: BFV packing assumes uniform record size; variable-length records cause fragmentation. $d=2$ optimal only when plaintext count $>N$; auto-tuning heuristic can mis-select $d$.
* **Side channels**: RevEAL attack [2] showed power side-channel leakage in SEAL's Gaussian sampling pre-v3.6; CBD sampling now constant-time but NTT timing still data-dependent on some platforms.
* **Malicious server**: No verifiability. Verifiable PIR via SNARKs adds $10\times$ overhead.
* **Quantum**: RLWE believed PQ-secure, but concrete $N=4096$ may need $N=8192$ for long-term $128$-bit post-quantum after Albrecht-Bai-Ducas improvements.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified five lattice PIR generations under *expand-fold-translate*. SealPIR established BFV packing and expansion as foundation; FastPIR introduced SIMD rotation for response compression; OnionPIR showed layered SHE composition reduces rate overhead from $100\times$ to $4.2\times$; Spiral generalized to Regev-GSW translation achieving $14$ KB queries, $0.81$ rate, and $1.9$ GB/s streaming — only $1.9\times$ monetary cost over no privacy; HSS/DPF points to sublinear online work via preprocessing and symmetric primitives.

Open directions: sublinear *single-server* PIR without hints from plain LWE (barely doubly-efficient $o(N/\log N)$ recently shown [Springer'24]), PQ PIR with $N=8192$ NTT optimization on GPU (PIRonGPU achieves $6.5\times$ via HEonGPU), and verifiable PIR with sublinear proofs.

The trajectory is clear: PIR is moving from academic curiosity to deployment in Apple Private Visual Search, private DNS, and blockchain light clients — where Spiral-like high-rate protocols make privacy *affordable*.
