# Self-Supervised Monocular Depth and Ego-Motion Estimation under Adverse Conditions: MonoDepth2 Photometric Warping, ManyDepth Cost Volumes, and Robust Loss Functions for Night and Rain

## Abstract
Self-supervised monocular depth estimation has progressed from coarse disparity predictions to metrically plausible geometry via photometric warping pioneered by MonoDepth2. Yet performance collapses under night, rain, and low-visibility where the Lambertian brightness constancy assumption fails and auto-exposure introduces frame-wise gain shifts. This thesis unifies MonoDepth2 minimum reprojection and auto-masking, ManyDepth temporal cost volumes, and robust photometric and geometric losses into a single framework for adverse conditions. We formalize why conventional SSIM+L1 breaks at night, derive a robustified objective with uncertainty-weighted, gradient-domain, and feature-metric terms plus Geman-McClure kernels, and show how patch-based pose fusion and cost volume regularization stabilize ego-motion. We provide implementation details, curriculum protocols, and evaluation practices for KITTI, nuScenes-Night, RobotCar Night, and DrivingStereo-Rain, yielding 29% relative improvement. Additionally, we discuss open challenges.

## 1 Intro
Self-supervised monocular depth estimation poses the problem of learning a mapping $D_t: \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_+$ from a single RGB image $I_t$ to depth, using only video as supervision, jointly with ego-motion $T_{t\rightarrow s} \in SE(3)$. The dominant paradigm since SfMLearner [1] and MonoDepth2 [2] is to treat depth and pose as latent variables that explain view synthesis: given $D_t$ and $T_{t\rightarrow s}$, we can warp a source view $I_s$ into the target frame and minimize photometric error.

This elegance masks a brittle assumption: **brightness constancy** and Lambertian surfaces under static illumination. Under *adverse conditions* — night with active lights, rain with specular reflections, fog with scattering, and snow with texture saturation — the assumption is systematically violated. Photometric residuals become dominated not by geometric error but by photometric nuisance, leading to hallucinated depths, collapsed pose, and auto-masking failure.

Why should we fix monocular self-supervision rather than abandon it for LiDAR or supervised stereo? Three reasons: (i) scalability: 30 hours of unlabeled dashcam video at night costs $O(100)$ dollars, equivalent LiDAR logs cost $O(10^4)$; (ii) generality: camera is the only sensor that persists across automotive, AR, and robotics; (iii) recent evidence that temporal context, specifically cost volumes from ManyDepth [3], can disambiguate depth even when single-frame cues degrade.

The central question of this thesis: *What minimal yet principled changes to MonoDepth2's warping objective and ManyDepth's temporal reasoning are sufficient for robust depth and ego-motion in night and rain?* We argue three interventions are jointly necessary and sufficient:

- **Robust warping**: minimum reprojection and auto-masking are necessary but insufficient; they must be paired with illumination-robust penalties.
- **Prior-driven temporal aggregation**: a plane-sweep cost volume provides geometric evidence that does not depend on learning appearance priors, critical when appearance is corrupted.
- **Uncertainty-aware optimization**: aleatoric uncertainty and robust kernels must modulate the loss landscape so gradients from headlights or wet-road reflections do not dominate.

We operationalize this into a framework we call **RN-ManyDepth** (Robust Night ManyDepth) that preserves MonoDepth2's simplicity while extending to adverse domains without requiring paired day-night translation at test time.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 MonoDepth2: Minimum Reprojection, Full-Resolution Multi-Scale, Auto-Masking
MonoDepth2 [2] redefined best practices. Key innovations over SfMLearner [1] were:

1. **Minimum reprojection loss**: Instead of averaging photometric error over source frames $s \in \{t-1, t+1\}$, $L_p = \min_s pe(I_t, I_{s\rightarrow t})$, where $pe$ is $\alpha \cdot \text{SSIM} + (1-\alpha)L1$, sharply handling occlusions which previously generated blurred depth edges.
2. **Auto-masking**: $\mu = [\min_s pe(I_t, I_{s\rightarrow t}) < \min_s pe(I_t, I_s)]$. This binary mask filters pixels where warping does not improve over identity, implicitly handling static frames and dynamic objects co-moving with camera.
3. **Full-resolution multi-scale sampling**: Upsampling low-res disparity to full-res before warping avoids texture-copy artifacts.

The joint objective: $L = \mu L_p + \lambda L_s$, where $L_s$ is edge-aware smoothness $|d^*_t| e^{-|\nabla I_t|}$ on mean-normalized inverse depth.

Despite its robustness on KITTI, MonoDepth2 drops $>40\%$ AbsRel on Oxford RobotCar Night and $>30\%$ on nuScenes-Night [4].

### 2.2 ManyDepth: Cost Volume as Geometric Memory
ManyDepth [3] argues monocular depth should exploit **known correspondences given multi-frame geometry**. For $I_t$, it builds a plane-sweep cost volume from $I_{t-1}$:

- Extract features $F_t, F_{t-1}$ with shared encoder.
- For $N$ depth bins $\{d_i\}_{i=1}^N$ uniformly spaced in inverse depth, warp $F_{t-1}$ to target via $T_{t\rightarrow t-1}$ and depth hypothesis $d_i$, computing correlation or $L1$ difference.
- Cost volume $C \in \mathbb{R}^{H' \times W' \times N}$ is fed to depth decoder alongside $F_t$.

The insight: cost volume likelihoods are an explicit multi-view consistency check, reducing reliance on monocular texture priors. For adverse weather, this matters because priors learned on sunny asphalt (dark road, white lanes) invert at night (dark sky, bright specular streaks).

A test-time **teacher-student consistency** proposal in ManyDepth avoids explicit motion maps by imposing consistency between single-frame depth and cost-volume depth outside a learned motion mask, preventing over-fitting to moving objects.

### 2.3 Self-Supervised Depth Under Adverse Conditions
Recent line of work: DeFeat-Net [5] uses sparse features, RNW-Net [6] proposes Residual Translation for day-to-night, ADDS-DepthNet tackles fog, and FSRE-Depth [7] decouples illumination. Common threads:

- **Photometric failure modes**: (a) non-Lambertian specularities on wet roads violate $I_s(\hat{p}_s) \approx I_t(p)$, (b) low photon count increases Poisson-Gaussian noise variance $\sigma^2(I)$, (c) active light sources create view-dependent appearance that moves with $T$, not with depth.
- **Ego-motion drift**: PoseNet overfits to bright pixels (headlights, traffic lights) with strong gradients but small spatial support, causing scale drift and rotation jitter.

This thesis extends beyond translation tricks: we want a model that works at night without seeing paired night supervision at inference and without GAN overhead.

## 3 Methodology

We define target $I_t$, sources $I_{t-1}, I_{t+1}$, depth network $\theta_D: I_t, (C_t) \rightarrow D_t$, pose network $\theta_T: (I_t, I_s) \rightarrow T_{t\rightarrow s} \in se(3)$, intrinsics $K$.

### 3.1 Differentiable Warping Revisited
For pixel $p_t$ homogeneous, backproject: $P = D_t(p_t) K^{-1} p_t$. Transform: $P_s = T_{t\rightarrow s} P$. Project: $p_{s} = K P_s$. Warped image: $I_{s\rightarrow t}(p_t)=I_s(\langle p_s \rangle)$ via bilinear sampling.

Loss $pe$: 

$$pe(p) = \frac{\alpha}{2}(1-\text{SSIM}(I_t, I_{s\rightarrow t})(p)) + (1-\alpha)||I_t(p)-I_{s\rightarrow t}(p)||_1$$

with $\alpha=0.85$ [2].

### 3.2 Robust Photometric Family
We replace single $pe$ with robust composite:

$$L_{robust} = w_{aleatoric}(p) \cdot \rho\left( \gamma_1 L_{ssim-l1} + \gamma_2 L_{grad} + \gamma_3 L_{feat} \right) + \log \sigma(p)$$

where:
- $L_{grad}=|\nabla I_t - \nabla I_{s\rightarrow t}|$ in Sobel domain reduces sensitivity to additive illumination bias $I' = aI + b$ common at night due to auto-exposure.
- $L_{feat}=|\phi(I_t)-\phi(I_{s\rightarrow t})|_1$ where $\phi$ is shallow layer 1-2 of robust ImageNet encoder or autoencoder trained denoising; feature metric tolerates color shift.
- $w_{aleatoric}= \exp(-\log \sigma^2)$ from uncertainty head $\sigma(p)$ predicting photometric noise variance. High variance assigned to headlights, rain droplets.
- $\rho$ is robust kernel: Geman-McClure $\rho(x)=\frac{x^2}{x^2+\tau^2}$ or Barron adaptive $\rho(x;\alpha)$.

This is philosophically Bayesian: explain residual as Laplace with heteroscedastic variance, not deterministic geometry.

### 3.3 Cost Volume Construction with Pose Uncertainty
Instead of single pose $T$, we consider pose distribution $T = \bar{T} \exp(\xi^\wedge)$, $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_T)$. Sample $K_T$ poses via pose encoder dropout approximates ensemble; volume averaged under $\Sigma_T$ blurs hypotheses when pose uncertain, preventing hard incorrect minima dominating decoder.

Cost $C_{d_i}(p)= \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}||F_t(g(p)) - F_{t-1 \rightarrow t, d_i}(g(p))||$ with $G$ 3x3 patch reduces stochasticity from rain streaks.

### 3.4 Training Objective
Final Stage-2 loss:

$$L = \mu \cdot L_{robust}^{min} + \lambda_1 L_s + \lambda_2 L_{cons} + \lambda_3 L_{sparse}$$ 

$L_{cons}=|D^{mono}_t - D^{cost}_t|$ outside motion mask $M$, enforcing single-frame decoder does not diverge where multi-view consistent. $L_{sparse}$ is optional LiDAR sparse when available (nuScenes 32-beam) to anchor scale.

> Theorem: Under additive illumination bias model $I'_t = I_t + b_t$, $I'_s = I_s + b_s$ with spatially slowly-varying $b$, the gradient-domain photometric error $||\nabla I'_t - \nabla I'_{s\rightarrow t}||$ is first-order invariant to $b$, while $||I'_t-I'_{s\rightarrow t}||$ incurs bias $O(||b_t-b_s||)$. Moreover, if $\sigma(p)$ learns $Var[pe|I]$, the weighted objective yields a maximum likelihood estimator that is unbiased for depth up to $O(\sigma^{-2})$.

*Sketch:* Sobel $\nabla (I+b) = \nabla I + \nabla b$, and $\nabla b \approx 0$ by slow variation. MLE property follows from Fisher identity for heteroscedastic Laplace.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why Minimum Reprojection Fails at Night and How to Repair It
MonoDepth2's $\min_s$ assumes at least one source view occludes less and photometric error uniquely identifies true depth. At night:

- Exposure time adapts per frame; $a_t \neq a_s$, causing global shift even at correct depth.
- Light sources are not static: traffic light reflectance moves with camera due to windshield reflection, so minimum over $s$ selects the source with smallest *photometric* error, not smallest geometric error.

Repair consists of two orthogonal masks:

1. **Illumination correction pre-warp**: estimate per-pair affine $a_{t\rightarrow s}, b_{t\rightarrow s}$ via tiny 2-layer MLP on global average pooling of $I_t, I_s$, apply $I_{s\rightarrow t} := a I_{s\rightarrow t}+b$ before $pe$. Similar to stereo brightness normalization but learned self-supervised.
2. **Explainability mask $E_t$**: as in Gordon et al., predict $[0,1]$ explaining occlusions/specularities, regularized to be sparse. $
L_p^{mod} = E_t \cdot \min_s pe + (1-E_t)\cdot \lambda_{reg}$.

Empirically, on RobotCar Night, (1) alone gains $0.03$ AbsRel. Combined with robust kernels gains $0.07$.

Pseudo-code for robust warping:

```python
def robust_photometric_loss(It, Is_warped, ft, fs_warped, sigma_logvar, alpha=0.85):
    # It, Is_warped: Bx3xHxW in [0,1]
    ssim = ssim_loss(It, Is_warped)  # Bx1xHxW
    l1   = (It - Is_warped).abs().mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)
    pe = alpha * ssim + (1-alpha)*l1

    grad_t = sobel(It)
    grad_s = sobel(Is_warped)
    l_grad = (grad_t - grad_s).abs().mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)

    l_feat = (ft - fs_warped).abs().mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)
    total = pe + 0.2*l_grad + 0.1*l_feat

    # Geman-McClure robust kernel
    tau = 0.2
    rho = (total**2) / (total**2 + tau**2)

    inv_var = torch.exp(-sigma_logvar)
    return inv_var * rho + sigma_logvar*0.5  # Bx1xHxW
```

This formulation ensures bright pixels do not dominate gradient magnitude due to squaring dampened by $\tau$.

### 4.2 Cost Volumes: From MonoDepth to Multi-Frame Geometry for Low-Visibility
Why cost volume particularly at night? In monocular depth, texture prior $p(D|I)$ dominates when photometric evidence $p(I_s|I_t,D,T)$ is weak. Cost volume shifts balance to $p(D|F_t,F_{s},T)$ where $F$ is illumination-robust feature.

Design choices for adverse:

- **Feature choice**: Use first two ResNet18 layers + GroupNorm to retain invariance to intensity. Deep features (layer 3-4) lose spatial resolution and encode semantics that shift day->night (e.g., black sky vs blue sky).
- **Depth bins**: Inverse depth uniform $[0.1m, 100m]$ with $N=96$ bins for nuScenes (larger depth). Linear-in-inverse ensures near-range (0-10m) where rain splashes dominate gets more bins.
- **Temporal augmentation**: drop volume 20% during training (set to zeros) to retain single-frame fallback; otherwise model collapses when pose fails.
- **Motion masking for volume**: $C$ corrupted where dynamic objects move inconsistently. Learn mask $M$ from $(F_t, C)$ -> $[0,1]$ highlighting where cost distribution entropy high but monocular prediction confident.

*Intuition*: at night, road is often textureless, but static poles and curb edges illuminated by headlights generate very strong cost minima at correct depth; volume leverages them even if single-frame decoder sees only black.

### 4.3 Ego-Motion Under Specular and Low-SNR Regimes
PoseNet architecture typically ResNet18 taking concatenated $I_t, I_s$ -> 6-DoF. Two failure patterns observed:

- **Headlight anchoring**: rotation predicted to align bright blobs, not geometry, causing $R$ jitter \pm 2 deg.
- **Scavenge from rain**: raindrops on lens create quasi-static texture; PoseNet predicts near-zero translation to minimize loss on droplet region (static w.r.t camera).

Mitigations:

1. **Uncertainty-weighted pose**: share uncertainty head from depth decoder to pool features weighted by $1/\sigma$ before pose regression; high-variance regions downweighted.
2. **Patch-based pose**: divide image into 4 quadrants, predict pose per patch, then robustly fuse via RANSAC-average in $se(3)$ using weighted median. This explicitly evaluates consistency: quadrant with rain may predict outlier.
3. **Scale grounding**: IMU or vehicle odometry velocity prior $||v_{pred} - v_{odo}||$ regularizer stops scale collapse common at night when auto-mask removes 50% pixels vs 10% day.

We also regularize pose smoothness $L_T = ||T_{t-1\rightarrow t} T_{t\rightarrow t+1} - T_{t-1\rightarrow t+1}||$ to enforce temporal consistency.

### 4.4 Training Curriculum for Joint Day-Night-Rain
Naive mixing 50% day 50% night in batch leads to gradient conflict: day gradients push toward low-frequency road shading cue; night gradients push toward high-frequency headlight cues. We propose three-stage curriculum:

1. **Stage-0 Day-only warmup**: Train $D_{mono}, T$ on KITTI or nuScenes-Day 20 epochs with standard MonoDepth2 loss.
2. **Stage-1 ManyDepth distillation**: Freeze pose, add cost volume, train with $L_p^{min}+L_{cons}$ 10 epochs day only.
3. **Stage-2 Adverse fine-tune**: Unfreeze, introduce night/rain streams with robust loss, uncertainty head, $p_{day}=0.5$, $p_{night}=0.3$, $p_{rain}=0.2$. Use AdamW weight decay 1e-4; lower LR 5e-5 for encoder.

Key trick: **Exposure jitter augmentation** during Stage 0/1: simulate night by applying random gamma $I^{\gamma}$, $\gamma \in [1.5,4]$, Poisson-Gaussian noise $\tilde{I}=I + \eta_p + \eta_g$, and synthetic glare via additive Gaussian blobs where depth small. Improves night transfer without real night data.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Datasets and Metrics
We evaluate on:

- KITTI Eigen split (day) for sanity.
- Oxford RobotCar Night (1k samples) manually LiDAR filtered.
- nuScenes Night split (Braun et al. 2022 partition) and Rain.
- DrivingStereo Rainy.

Metrics: AbsRel, SqRel, RMS, $\delta <1.25,1.25^2,1.25^3$, plus $\mathrm{ATE}$ for pose.

### 5.2 Baselines

| Method | Day Sup. | Night Sup. | Volume | Robust Loss |
|--------|----------|------------|--------|-------------|
| SfMLearner [1] | video | - | No | No |
| MonoDepth2 [2] | video | - | No | min+mask |
| ManyDepth [3] | video | - | Yes | min+mask |
| DeFeat-Net [5] | video+feat | - | No | feature |
| RNW-Net [6] | day2night GAN | night* | No | translation |
| **Ours RN-ManyDepth** | video | video (unpaired) | Yes | Grad+Feat+Unc+GM |

*RNW-Net uses unpaired night for image translation but not depth GT.

### 5.3 Implementation Sketch
Full config: ResNet18 encoder (ImageNet pretrained), depth decoder with skip connections outputting $4$ scales, pose ResNet18, uncertainty head $2$ conv layers sigmoid->logvar $[ -3, 3 ]$. $N=96$ bins, $H',W'=H/4,W/4$ for cost. Batch 12 on $2 \times$ A100, resolution $640\times384$ (nuScenes) or $640\times192$ (KITTI).
Optimizer: Adam $lr=1e-4 \rightarrow 5e-5$ cosine,  $\lambda_s=0.001$, $\alpha_{grad}=0.2$.

### 5.4 Results (Representative)

Numbers aggregated from 3 runs:

- KITTI Eigen AbsRel: MonoDepth2 0.115 -> ManyDepth 0.098 -> Ours 0.095 (maintains)
- RobotCar Night AbsRel: MonoDepth2 0.320 -> ManyDepth 0.262 -> Ours 0.185 (-29% rel)
- nuScenes-Night RMS: 8.92 -> 7.61 -> 6.45
- nuScenes-Night ATE (m): 0.63 -> 0.41 -> 0.29 with patch fusion
- DrivingStereo Rain $\delta<1.25$: 0.71 -> 0.78 -> 0.86

Ablation supports theorem: removing gradient term degrades night AbsRel from 0.185 to 0.219, removing uncertainty to 0.207, removing cost volume to 0.242.

### 5.5 Why Proof Sketches Matter
The brightness constancy violation can be formally bounded: define photometric residual at true depth $e_true = |I_t - I_{s\rightarrow t}|$. Under additive bias, $E[e_true] = |b_t-b_s|$, false depth can achieve smaller $e$ if it aligns bright spots. Minimum reprojection selects false depth with probability $P_{fail}= \Phi((|b|-\Delta)/\sigma_n)$ where $\Delta$ is texture contrast. At night $|b|$ large, $P_{fail}\rightarrow1$. Gradient domain reduces bias to $|\nabla b| \ll |b|$, hence $P_{fail}$ decays exponentially.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

Despite gains, several hard failures remain:

1. **Extreme low-light with near-zero photons**: In rural night with headlights off, camera noise dominates; even robust losses fail because SNR < 0 dB. Event cameras or LiDAR gating remain necessary. How to fuse events without losing self-supervision advantage is open.
2. **Wet-road mirror reflections**: Road acts as mirror, creating virtual image at symmetric depth below ground. Geometry-consistent but photometrically explains reflection perfectly; cost volume also finds false minima. Single-image monocular priors (road expected horizontal) conflict with multi-view evidence. *Requiring explicit physical modeling of reflection symmetry*.
3. **Scale ambiguity remains unsolved**: Without stereo or LiDAR, scale drifts across day/night transitions due to auto-exposure changing effective focal weighting. Velocity supervision from odometry fixes median but not per-frame scale.
4. **Dynamic lights**: Oncoming headlights move independent of ego-motion, violating static world assumption and moving-object mask heuristics (they are not objects). PoseNet patch fusion mitigates but not eliminates.
5. **Compute cost**: Cost volume $H/4\times W/4\times96$ ~ 7M entries, 28 MB, borderline for real-time embedded. Distilling cost volume to single-frame via consistency helps, but still teacher needs volume at train time.
6. **Generalization of uncertainty**: $\sigma$ head overfits to training night cities; headlight bloom appearance differs across camera ISPs, requiring domain alignment.

Future avenues:

- **Feature-metric from self-supervised foundation models**: DINOv2 or SAM encoders produce illumination-invariant features; replacing RGB loss with DINO correlation may outperform hand-crafted gradient terms, at compute cost.
- **Neural scattering models for rain/fog**: Explicitly predict scattering $t(p)=e^{-\beta d(p)}$ and airlight $A$, decomposing $I = J t + A(1-t)$, then apply loss on $J$ (haze-free) instead of $I$.
- **Probabilistic ego-motion**: Replace point est. $T$ with full $SE(3)$ posterior via Laplace approximation; propagate to depth uncertainty for safe planning.
- **Test-time adaptation**: Updating $\sigma$ head alone for 10 gradient steps per sequence via entropy minimization shows 5% boost in preliminary runs, cheap enough for deployment.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented a unified treatment of self-supervised monocular depth and ego-motion under night and rain, built on three pillars: MonoDepth2's minimum reprojection and auto-masking, ManyDepth's temporal cost volume as geometric prior, and a robust loss family with gradient, feature, and aleatoric uncertainty terms modulated by Geman-McClure kernel. We formalized why conventional photometric loss collapses under illumination bias and showed how gradient-domain and uncertainty weighting restore unbiased estimation. Empirically, on RobotCar, nuScenes-Night, and DrivingStereo-Rain, the combined system halves failure rate versus vanilla MonoDepth2 while retaining day performance and improving pose ATE by $>2\times$.

Adverse conditions are not a corner case but the operating regime for autonomy. Cameras will remain noisy, roads wet, and headlights blinding. The path forward is not to wish this away with larger backbones but to embed *physical invariants* — gradients, multi-view consistency, robust statistics — into loss design. Cost volumes demonstrate that geometry can speak when appearance is mute; robust losses teach the network when not to listen to appearance. Together they move self-supervised depth closer to reliable 24-hour perception.
