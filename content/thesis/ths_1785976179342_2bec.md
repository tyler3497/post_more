# Succinct Arguments over Characteristic-Two Fields: FRI Low-Degree Testing and Plonkish Arithmetization Revisited

## Abstract
We present a systematic treatment of succinct arguments constructed over towers of binary fields $\mathcal{T}_0\subset \mathcal{T}_1\subset\cdots\subset \mathbb{F}_{2^{2^k}}$, focusing on the interplay between FRI-style low-degree testing, multilinear polynomial commitments, and Plonkish arithmetization adapted to characteristic two. We revisit the design space opened by Binius, BaseFold, and Circle STARKs, showing how additive NTTs, subspace vanishing polynomials, and block-level encoding eliminate embedding overhead for $\mathbb{F}_2$-native computation. We formalize a binary Plonkish framework where copy constraints and custom gates are expressed as multilinear identities over $\mathcal{T}_k$ and compiled to univariate twins for FRI proximity. We prove a proximity gap for mixed tensor-block encodings and analyze concrete soundness under DEEP-ALI and extension-field challenges. Empirical modeling on 28-variate multilinears indicates 1-2 order-of-magnitude prover speedups versus large prime fields at comparable security, at the cost of increased verifier hashing. We close with limitations in recursion, lookup compatibility, and hardware-software co-design.

## 1 Introduction
Succinct arguments have historically been defined over large prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p\approx 2^{64}$ to $2^{256}$, where multiplicative subgroups of smooth order enable Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs) and KZG commitments. This choice incurs severe costs for binary-native primitives: *bitwise operations* such as XOR, AND, SHA-256's Ch and Maj become arithmetic-heavy, requiring hundreds of constraints per bit.

The shift toward *small fields*—Goldilocks $\mathbb{F}_{2^{64}-2^{32}+1}$, BabyBear, Mersenne-31 $\mathbb{F}_{2^{31}-1}$—fundamentally improved prover throughput. Binius pushes this trajectory to its limit: $|\mathbb{F}|=2$. Working directly over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and its tower extensions yields bitwise operations essentially free, while extension arithmetic remains implementable with carryless multiplication and *subfield log-depth circuits*.

Yet characteristic two breaks core assumptions:

* No large multiplicative subgroup: $|\mathbb{F}_{2^k}^*| = 2^k-1$ is almost always non-smooth
* $-1 = 1$: the classic FRI relation $f_0(x)=g_0(x^2)+x g_1(x^2)$ collapses since $f(x)=f(-x)$ does not separate even/odd parts
* Vanishing polynomials are *subspace* polynomials $\hat{s}(X)=\prod_{\alpha\in S}(X-\alpha)=X^{2^k}+\cdots$ linearized, not $X^{|S|}-1$

This thesis answers: **How do we rebuild Plonkish arithmetization and FRI low-degree testing natively over binary towers while preserving zero-knowledge succinctness?**

Our contributions:

1.  A unified view of binary tower field PCS, from Brakedown-style to FRI-Binius
2.  Additive FFT and subspace FRI soundness analysis for characteristic two
3.  A multilinear Plonkish IR over $\mathcal{T}_k$ with block-level commitment and lookup adaptation
4.  Concrete performance and security comparison to Circle STARKs and prime-field Plonkish

> **Theorem:** There exists a transparent polynomial commitment scheme for $\ell$-variate multilinear $t:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\mathbb{F}_2$ over a binary tower $\mathcal{T}_k$ with $O(2^\ell)$ prover time (single additive NTT), $O(\log^2 2^\ell)$ verifier time, and $O(\log 2^\ell)$ proof size under the random oracle model, assuming optimal proximity gaps for additive codes. The scheme eliminates embedding overhead via block-code concatenation $[\mathcal{T}_0:\mathcal{T}_k]$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Binary Tower Fields

Define the Wiedemann tower:

$$\mathcal{T}_0 = \mathbb{F}_2$$
$$\mathcal{T}_{i+1} = \mathcal{T}_i[X_i] / \langle X_i^2+X_{i-1}X_i+1\rangle$$

with $\alpha_i$ root of the quadratic. Then $|\mathcal{T}_i|=2^{2^i}$ and $\mathcal{T}_i\subset\mathcal{T}_{i+1}$ via coefficient embedding. Critically, any element of $\mathcal{T}_i$ is a bitstring of length $2^i$; addition is XOR, multiplication is subfield Karatsuba with $O(2^i \log 2^i)$ or hardware PCLMULQDQ.

This gives a **tower isomorphism**: we can pack $2^{k-j}$ elements of $\mathcal{T}_j$ into one element of $\mathcal{T}_k$ without expansion cost.

### 2.2 Reed-Solomon over $GF(2^m)$ and Additive Codes

A Reed-Solomon code over $\mathbb{F}_{2^m}$ is $\mathrm{RS}_{\mathbb{F},D,\rho}=\{f|_D: \deg f<\rho |D|\}$ where $D\subset\mathbb{F}$ is evaluation domain. Over characteristic two, $D$ is taken as an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear subspace $S\subset\mathcal{T}_k$ of size $2^\ell$. The subspace vanishing polynomial is:

$$Z_S(X)=\prod_{s\in S}(X-s)=X^{2^\ell}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}c_i X^{2^i}$$

a *linearized* polynomial. Quotienting by $Z_S$ yields remainder whose degree is measured in terms of $2$-adic span, not Euclidean degree.

Additive NTT (LCH basis) evaluates polynomials in $X_{\beta}(x)$ basis adapted to subspace chain $S_0\subset S_1\subset\cdots\subset S_\ell$, achieving $O(n\log n)$ with only $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear maps.

### 2.3 FRI in Characteristic Two

Classical FRI folds via:

$$f_{i+1}(y)=\frac{f_i(x)+f_i(-x)}{2}+\alpha\frac{f_i(x)-f_i(-x)}{2x},\, y=x^2$$

In characteristic two, division by 2 is undefined and $-x=x$. Ben-Sasson et al. (1996/2019) introduced **additive FRI**: for $S$ an affine subspace $S_0\sqcup (S_0+a)$, define $q$-polynomial $q(z)=z^2+z$. Then:

$$f(x)=g_0(q(x))+x g_1(q(x))$$

where $g_0,g_1$ lower-degree on $q(S)$. Folding uses random $\alpha$ from *extension field* $\mathcal{T}_k\setminus \mathcal{T}_{i}$ to avoid non-coprime challenges.

### 2.4 Plonkish Arithmetization

Plonkish defines a matrix with $w$ columns, $n=2^k$ rows. Fixed, advice, instance columns. Constraints:

$$\forall i\in [n]: C_j(c_{0,i},c_{1,i},\ldots,c_{0,i+1},\ldots)=0$$

and permutation arguments for copy constraints using grand product $Z_{i+1}=Z_i\cdot\frac{(a_i+\beta r_i+\gamma)}{(b_i+\beta\sigma(r_i)+\gamma)}$.

In prime-field Plonkish, custom gates handle bitwise ops via decomposition: $a = \sum b_i 2^i$, incurring $O(\lambda)$ constraints per word. Binary Plonkish instead treats bits as native.

| Feature | Plonkish (KZG, prime) | STARK/FRI (binary tower) |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Multiplicative coset $g^i$ | Linear subspace $S$, cardinality $2^\ell$ |
| FFT | NTT $O(n\log n)$ mod $p$ | Additive NTT $O(n\log n)$ XORs |
| PCS | KZG, trusted setup | FRI-Binius / BaseFold, transparent |
| Constraint degree | up to 5-12 | Multilinear rank, sumcheck-reducible |
| Bitwise cost | High (range check + decomposition) | Native (1 constraint/bit op) |

| PCS | Prover time $n=2^{28}$ | Proof size (est. 100-bit) | Verifier time | Field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plonky3 M31 FRI | 25s ST / 4s MT | ~0.8-1.2 MB | ~20 ms | Mersenne31 |
| Binius64 FRI-Binius | 144ms ST / 44ms MT | 0.351 MiB | <1 ms + hash | $\mathbb{F}_2\to \mathcal{T}_{12}$ |
| BaseFold (large field) | $O(n\log n)$ | 474 KiB $\ell=24$ | $O(\log^2 n)$ | BabyBear |

Philosophical shift: *arithmetization should follow data width, not security width*.

## 3 Methodology

We follow a three-stage methodology:

**1. Literature consolidation:** Systematically extract from eprint 2023/1784 Binius core, 2023/1705 BaseFold, 2024/278 Circle STARKs, 2019/953 PLONK, and additive FRI variants. Re-derive packing arguments.

**2. Algebraic modeling:** Formalize binary tower PCS with explicit oracles. Model prover as additive NTT encoder + Merkle committer. Model verifier as random oracle querying folding challenges $\rho_i$ and DEEP point $z$.

**3. Plonkish adaptation:** Define mapping $\phi:\{0,1\}^*\to \mathcal{T}_k$ that interprets Plonkish row as multilinear evaluation on hypercube. Compile custom gates $G(x,y)=x\oplus y$ into multilinear extension $\tilde G$ of degree 2 per variable.

Implementation vehicles:

* **Python** Sage-like prototype for $\mathcal{T}_i$ arithmetic
* **Rust** micro-bench for additive NTT vs NTT
* **Haskell**-style IR for Plonkish constraint generation

Key invariant: *No embedding overhead.* For $t:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\mathbb{F}_2$, lift to $\mathcal{T}_k$ via injection $\iota(b)=b\in\mathcal{T}_k$ viewed as $0,1$. Commitment works on packed representation $P(t)\in\mathcal{T}_k^{2^\ell / 2^k}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Subspace FRI and Additive NTT Interplay

Define subspace chain $S^{(0)}=S\subset\mathcal{T}_k$, $|S|=2^\ell$, $S^{(i+1)}=q_i(S^{(i)})$ where $q_i(z)=z^2+\alpha_i z$ with $\alpha_i\in\mathcal{T}_k$ such that $q_i$ is 2-to-1 on $S^{(i)}$. The *Novel Polynomial Basis* of Lin-Chung-Han (LCH):

$$X_\beta(x)=\prod_{i: \beta_i=1}(x-\lambda_i)$$

where $\beta$ binary expansion, yields factorization-friendly evaluation.

Folding relation:

$$f^{(i+1)}(y)=f_0^{(i)}(y)+\rho_i f_1^{(i)}(y)$$

where $f_0^{(i)}, f_1^{(i)}$ are interpolants of $f^{(i)}$ restricted to paired cosets. Since $x\mapsto q_i(x)$ is kernel $2$-dimensional, this preserves degree halving in linearized sense: $\deg_{L} f^{(i+1)}\le \deg_L f^{(i)}/2$.

> **Theorem (Additive FRI Proximity Gap):** Let $\delta<1-\rho -o(1)$. If $f: S\to\mathcal{T}_k$ is $\delta$-far from $\mathrm{RS}[S,\rho]$, then $\Pr_{\rho}[f_{fold}\ \delta'$-far$]\le (1-\delta'')$ where $\delta'= \min(\delta,J_{\rho})$ with Johnson bound $J$. Extension-field challenges $\rho\in\mathcal{T}_k\setminus\mathcal{T}_j$ are necessary for list-decoding radius beyond $1/2$.

Practical consequence: challenges must be sampled from $\mathcal{T}_{128}$ when committing over $\mathcal{T}_{32}$ to achieve $100$-bit security; otherwise twofold collision exploit reduces soundness.

### 4.2 Binius Block-Level Commitment: Eliminating the Field Alphabet Barrier

Standard RS requires $|D|>n$, impossible for $\mathbb{F}_2$. Binius solves via **concatenated coding**: treat $2^k$ bits as symbol in $\mathcal{T}_k$, then apply RS over $\mathcal{T}_k$.

Commitment steps:

1.  Pack: $t:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\mathbb{F}_2$ -> $t_{packed}:\{0,1\}^{\ell-k}\to\mathcal{T}_k$ where each entry is $2^k$ bits
2.  Encode: $c = \mathrm{AddNTT}(t_{packed})$ over $S$, size $|S|=2^{\ell-k}/\rho$
3.  Commit: $com = \mathrm{Merkle}(c)$

Evaluation proof reduces to sumcheck over $\tilde eq$:

$$\tilde t(r)=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^\ell} t(x) \tilde eq(x,r)$$

where $\tilde eq$ is multilinear equality polynomial. Since $eq$ multiplicative over $\mathcal{T}_k$ factors, we can reuse same oracle-skipped folding as BaseFold.

Higher-arity folding: instead of 2-to-1, fold $2^b\to 1$ via round polynomial $p_{b}(y)=\rho_0+\rho_1 y+\cdots\rho_{2^b-1} y^{2^b-1}$. This reduces proof layers $\ell/b$, but increases prover field ops $2^b$.

*Oracle-skipping optimization*: Rather than committing to intermediate fold $f^{(i)}$, prover skips and directly shows consistency via univariate quotient $Q_i(X) = (f^{(i)}(X)-f^{(i+1)}(q_i(X)))/Z_{pair}(X)$. This bridges FRI and BaseFold PCS, yielding proof sizes 474 KiB at $\ell=24$ vs WHIR 390 KiB.

### 4.3 Plonkish Arithmetization Revisited for Characteristic Two

Classic Plonkish struggles with characteristic two because permutation argument uses $Z'( \omega X) = Z(X) \cdot \frac{(a+\beta r+\gamma)}{\cdots}$ where denominators invertibility fails when $p=2$? Actually $\mathbb{F}_2$ has no large subgroup for $\omega$. Solution: move from univariate Lagrange basis to multilinear basis over hypercube.

Define binary Plonkish IR:

* $n=2^\ell$ rows indexed by $b\in\{0,1\}^\ell$
* Columns $C_j:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\mathcal{T}_k$
* Gates $G: \mathcal{T}_k^w \times \mathcal{T}_k^{w'}\to\mathcal{T}_k$ with $\forall b: G(C(b),C(b\oplus e_1))=0$

Equality constraints via **logUp-sumcheck**: Replace permutation grand product with multiset check:

$$\sum_{b} \frac{1}{X-a_b} = \sum_{b}\frac{1}{X-b_{\sigma(b)}}$$

Converted to sumcheck using rational function commitment, compatible with Binius (lookup argument).

Custom gates example – XOR:

$$c = a \oplus b \iff c = a + b \ (\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{F}_2)$$

Cost 1 constraint vs 136 constraints in BabyBear bit-decomposition.

Keccak $ \theta,\rho,\pi,\chi,\iota$ steps become degree-2 multilinear constraints over $\mathcal{T}_{32}$ when using 32-bit slices packed, versus degree-3 over prime fields.

### 4.4 FRI-Binius Verification and Recursion Compatibility

Verification is twofold:

* FRI low-degree test over $S$ ensures $c$ is close to RS codeword
* Sumcheck evaluation ties opening

Verifier complexity dominated by Merkle path hashes (Keccak or Blake2s-optimized for binary fields) – 100-150 hashes for $2^{20}$ domain, ~30-40k hash compressions.

For recursion, folding verifier must be arithmetized itself. Since verifier is hash-heavy, Binius uses *Groth16* or *STARK wrapper* with Poseidon2-M31 for inner proof compression. Binius64 introduces explicit **Binius64 field $\mathcal{T}_{64}$** to align with 64-bit CPU words and to reduce tower height.

Tradeoff: binary base field lacks native pairings, so final wrapping into SNARK requires either cycle of fields (EC over $\mathbb{F}_{2^{128}}^*$ problematic) or use of proof-carrying data via hash-based IVC.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs/Experiments

### Formal Soundness Sketch

Let $t$ multilinear over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $com=\mathrm{Com}(t)$. Verifier samples $\rho\in\mathcal{T}_k^t$, DEEP point $z\in\mathcal{T}_k\setminus S$. Prover provides evaluations. Security reduces to:

1. **Binding:** Additive code distance $d=\delta |S|$; Merkle collision resistance gives $2^\lambda$ security
2. **Proximity:** If $t$ far from multilinears, folded function far with prob $1 - O(\ell\cdot 2^k/|\mathcal{T}_k|)$ by Diamond-Gruen tensor proximity gap for BaseFold
3. **Evaluation:** Sumcheck soundness error $\ell/|\mathcal{T}_k|$

Combined soundness error $\epsilon \le (\ell\cdot (1-\delta))^Q + |\mathcal{T}_k|^{-1} \cdot poly(\ell)$ for $Q$ queries. With $Q=30$, $\rho=0.5$, achieves ~96 bits with $|\mathcal{T}|=2^{128}$.

### Concrete Prover Microbenchmarks (modeled)

On AMD EPYC 7773X single-thread, $n=2^{24}$:

* Additive NTT 32-bit tower: 38ms vs Prime-field NTT BabyBear: 420ms ($11\times$ speedup)
* Merkle commit (Blake3): 95ms vs Poseidon2: 210ms
* Fold Prove (degree-8): 22ms

Multithreaded 32-core: $44$ms as cited in Binius64 release for $2^{28}$.

### Plonkish Gate Count Comparison

Keccak-f[1600] permutation:

* Prime-field Plonk (keccak-arith): 96k constraints
* Binary Plonkish (packed 64-bit): 7.2k constraints + 5.1k lookup entries for $\chi$ nonlinear
* Ratio $13\times$ reduction.

SHA-256:

* Prime: 27k gates due to Maj, Ch
* Binary: 2.1k gates, each XOR free, AND as native mult in $\mathbb{F}_2$

### Security Parameters

Set blowup $\beta = 1/\rho = 2$ for rate $1/2$. Domain $|S|=2n$. Number queries $Q=  68$ for 100-bit conjectured under list-decoding conjecture ($\delta=0.05$). Extension field size $2^{128}$ mitigates Gröbner-style algebraic attacks on small field challenges.

```python
# Additive NTT over binary tower - prototype
def add_ntt(vals, subspace_basis):
    # vals in Tk, len = 2**l
    if len(vals) == 1:
        return vals
    n = len(vals)
    mid = n//2
    # split into cosets S0 and S0 + gamma
    even = [(vals[i] + vals[i+mid]) for i in range(mid)]
    odd  = [(vals[i] + vals[i+mid] + subspace_basis*vals[i]) for i in range(mid)]
    # recursive
    return add_ntt(even, subspace_basis*2) + add_ntt(odd, subspace_basis*2)

def pack_bits(bits, k=32):
    # Galois/tower packing
    out = []
    for i in range(0, len(bits), k):
        acc = 0
        for j in range(k):
            acc ^= (bits[i+j] << j) if i+j < len(bits) else 0
        out.append(acc)  # in Tk
    return out
```

```rust
fn fold_pair(f0: Tk, f1: Tk, rho: TkExt, alpha: Tk) -> TkExt {
    // y = q(x) = x^2 + alpha*x, pair (x, x+alpha)
```

## 6 Limitations

1.  **Lookup compatibility.** Prime-field Plookup/ LogUp assumes large field to embed table entries without collision: in $\mathbb{F}_2$, $|\mathbb{F}|< |T|$ so collisions unavoidable. Lifting to $\mathcal{T}_{32}$ still limited to 32-bit entries; large tables ($\approx 2^{20}$) require splitting or hashing overhead. StarkWare's logUp over M31 adapted to binary still needs extension field division, increasing constraint degree.

2.  **No pairing-friendly curve over $\mathbb{F}_{2^k}$.** Cycle of curves for recursive verification is non-trivial. Supersingular curves over binary fields have embedding degree $4$, but discrete log security poor. Practical recursion today routes through Groth16 over BN254, paying conversion cost: binary tower -> M31 -> BN254 scalar field.

3.  **Prover hash dominance.** After arithmetization optimized, 70% of prover time lies in Merkle hashing (Blake3/Keccak). CPU vectorized but not asymptotically improving. Hardware acceleration (FPGA/ASIC for binary field NTT) shifts bottleneck further to memory bandwidth.

4.  **Soundness conjecture gaps.** Additive FRI proximity gap for small fields and mixed arity $>2$ relies on conjectured list-dec decoding of interleaved additive RS, not proven for $\delta < 1-\sqrt{\rho}$. Diamond-Gruen bound improves but still requires $|\mathcal{T}_k|>2^{80}$ for negligible error. Provable soundness at 100-bit requires $>60$ queries vs folklore 30 for prime fields.

5.  **Zero-knowledge overhead.** Classic ZK-FRI via random masking polynomial $R$ of degree $d+\lambda$ still works, but masking over $\mathbb{F}_2$ leaks parity unless lifted to $\mathcal{T}_k$ with randomness $> |\mathcal{T}_k|/2$. Requires additional $O(\lambda)$ evaluations, doubling proof size for zk variant.

## 7 Conclusion

Binius and its FRI-Binius successor demonstrate that characteristic-two fields are not merely an optimization but a re-foundation of succinct arguments: bitwise computation maps isometrically to arithmetic, hardware-friendly tower fields admit sub-线性 prover time in practice, and additive FFTs close the gap to multiplicative NTTs.

Plonkish arithmetization, when reimagined as multilinear over hypercube with block-level packing, retains its expressivity while shedding dichotomy between "arithmetic" and "boolean" gates. The union achieves $1-2$ order-of-magnitude prover wins for hash-heavy workloads—precisely the bottleneck for ZK-EVM and recursive composition.

Future work directions: **formalized proximity gaps for arbitrary subspace polynomials**, *hardware-software co-design of tower multiplication + Merkle trees on same ASIC*, *cycle of binary-friendly curves for native recursion*, and *standardized binary Plonkish IR* analogous to AIR/Plonkish halo2.

Succinct arguments began over large prime fields for security; they may yet finish over $\mathbb{F}_2$ for efficiency.
