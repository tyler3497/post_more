# FlashAttention: IO-Aware Exact Attention via Online Softmax Tiling, Blocked GEMM Fusion, and SRAM-Aware Scheduling on Modern GPUs

## Abstract

Self-attention in Transformers incurs quadratic time and memory complexity in sequence length, but on modern GPUs the dominant cost is not floating-point operations: it is data movement between high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and on-chip SRAM. Standard attention materializes the N×N attention-score matrix in HBM, rendering attention memory-bandwidth bound even as tensor-core throughput has outpaced DRAM bandwidth by more than an order of magnitude. This thesis develops the theory and practice of FlashAttention, an exact, IO-aware attention algorithm that tiles queries, keys, and values into SRAM-resident blocks, fuses the score computation, softmax, and value reduction into a single kernel, and employs the online-softmax recurrence of Milakov and Gimelshein to accumulate numerically stable partial statistics in one pass [1][4]. We analyze the IO complexity of tiling, derive the recomputation-versus-storage tradeoff that makes the backward pass tractable, trace the architectural evolution from FlashAttention through FlashAttention-2's improved parallelism and FlashAttention-3's asynchronous Hopper-era pipelining with WGMMA, and quantify the memory-hierarchy design rules that determine block sizes. The result is a principled treatment of why exact attention can run near the compute ceiling of modern accelerators.

## 1 Introduction

The Transformer has become the dominant architecture across language, vision, and scientific modeling, and its self-attention operator is the computational bottleneck of long-context models [7]. For a sequence of length N and head dimension d, attention computes O = softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V, which naive implementations evaluate in Θ(N²) time and Θ(N²) memory. Decades of approximation research — sparse, low-rank, and kernelized attention — reduced asymptotic FLOPs yet routinely failed to accelerate wall-clock training, because they targeted the wrong resource [1].

The missing principle is **IO-awareness**: accounting for reads and writes between levels of the GPU memory hierarchy [1][5]. A modern GPU exposes two sharply different storage tiers: on-chip SRAM (shared memory per streaming multiprocessor) with aggregate bandwidth on the order of 19 TB/s on the A100, and off-chip HBM with roughly 1.5–2.0 TB/s of bandwidth and tens of gigabytes of capacity [1]. Standard attention reads and writes the N×N matrices S = QKᵀ and P = softmax(S) through HBM multiple times. Because typical models satisfy N ≫ d (e.g., N = 1024, d = 64 in GPT-2-scale heads), these intermediate activations dominate runtime: attention is memory-bound, not compute-bound, and cutting FLOPs without cutting HBM traffic changes almost nothing.

FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) inverts the optimization target [1]. It tiles Q, K, V into blocks small enough to live in SRAM, fuses the three attention stages into one kernel that never materializes S or P in HBM, and uses an online softmax to accumulate block-wise statistics — running row-maximum m and normalization sum ℓ — in a single pass. The backward pass stores only O(N) statistics (the softmax log-sum-exp per row) and recomputes the N×N block scores from the stored Q, K, V, trading FLOPs for HBM traffic in the direction the hardware demands. FlashAttention-2 (2023) restructured the parallelism and halved the non-GEMM FLOPs, reaching ~230 TFLOPs/s on A100 [2]; FlashAttention-3 (2024) redesigned the algorithm for Hopper with asynchronous TMA copies, WGMMA tensor-core instructions, and FP8 support, reaching ~75% of H100 peak (~740 TFLOPs/s) [3].

This thesis proceeds from the memory-hierarchy first principles that motivate IO-awareness, through the online-softmax mathematics and tiling construction, to the recomputation tradeoff, the FlashAttention-2/3 redesigns, the H100 WGMMA scheduling details, and finally a quantitative memory-hierarchy analysis with block-size selection rules.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The standard attention bottleneck

For Q, K, V ∈ ℝ^(N×d), the standard implementation issues three kernels: S = QKᵀ (GEMM, Θ(N²d) FLOPs, writes N×N to HBM), P = softmax(S) (row-wise reduction, reads/writes N×N), and O = PV (GEMM). The total HBM traffic is Θ(N² + Nd), and with N ≫ d the quadratic terms dominate. As Gholami et al. and Ivanov et al. document, GPU peak FLOPs have grown roughly 3× every two years while DRAM bandwidth grows only ~1.6× per two years, so the *arithmetic intensity* of these kernels — FLOPs per byte moved — falls below the machine-balance point, stranding tensor-core throughput [5].

### 2.2 IO-awareness and the memory hierarchy

IO-aware algorithm design (in the tradition of the Aggarwal–Vitter external-memory model) counts memory transfers between hierarchy levels as the primary cost [1]. On Ampere, each of the 108 SMs owns ~192 KB of SRAM/shared memory at ~19 TB/s aggregate bandwidth; the HBM pool offers 40–80 GB at 1.5–2.0 TB/s [1]. Two consequences follow: (i) any algorithm that streams O(N²) bytes through HBM per layer is bandwidth-bound regardless of FLOPs; (ii) a fused kernel that keeps working sets in SRAM converts O(N²) HBM transfers into O(N²d²/M) transfers, where M is SRAM capacity, achieving a speedup factor linear in the SRAM-to-register ratio.

### 2.3 Online softmax

Safe softmax requires the row maximum before exponentiation; the classical formulation needs three passes over each row (max, sum-of-exponentials, normalize) [4]. Milakov and Gimelshein showed the max and the normalizer can be computed in a single pass with a rescaling recurrence [4]:

> **Theorem:** *Online normalizer.* Maintaining running statistics mⱼ = max(mⱼ₋₁, xⱼ) and dⱼ = dⱼ₋₁·e^(mⱼ₋₁−mⱼ) + e^(xⱼ−mⱼ) yields, after one pass, the true maximum m_V and the true normalizer d_V = Σⱼ e^(xⱼ−m_V). The proof proceeds by induction on the block index [4].

FlashAttention generalizes this recurrence to blocks and couples it with the attention output accumulator itself, making per-block statistics O(N) storage while the quadratic scores exist only in SRAM.

## 3 Methodology

Our exposition follows the structure of the primary sources [1][2][3][4][5], reconstructed and cross-checked for consistency:

1. **Tiling construction.** Partition Q into T_r = ⌈N/B_r⌉ blocks of B_r rows, and K, V into T_c = ⌈N/B_c⌉ blocks of B_c rows, with B_c = ⌈M/(4d)⌉, B_r = min(⌈M/(4d)⌉, d) chosen so that a Q-block, a K-block, a V-block, and the B_r × B_c score tile all fit in SRAM of size M [1].
2. **Fused forward pass.** An outer loop over K/V blocks and inner loop over Q blocks computes Sᵢⱼ = QᵢKⱼᵀ in SRAM, applies the block-local online-softmax update, and accumulates Oᵢ — writing only O and per-row statistics (m, ℓ) to HBM [1].
3. **Recomputation backward.** The backward pass stores only the softmax statistics (m, ℓ) ∈ ℝ^(N) and recomputes Sᵢⱼ and Pᵢⱼ block-wise from HBM-resident Q, K, V, dO [1].
4. **Architecture mapping.** We map each generation (FA-1/2/3) onto its target hardware (Ampere vs. Hopper), quantifying how WGMMA, TMA, and warp specialization change the schedule [2][3].
5. **Hierarchy analysis.** We derive block-size selection from SRAM capacity, register pressure, and the GEMM tile geometry, and relate operational intensity to the roofline [3][6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Online Softmax over Blocks: the Core Recurrence

Let block scores for query block i and key block j be Sᵢⱼ = QᵢKⱼᵀ/√d ∈ ℝ^(B_r×B_c). For each row, maintain the running row-maximum m and the running sum-of-exponentials ℓ, plus the partial output O. On processing block j, compute the block row-maximum m̃ᵢⱼ = rowmax(Sᵢⱼ), then rescale:

```
m_new = max(m, m̃)                                   # running row max
α     = exp(m − m_new)                              # rescale old accumulator
β     = exp(m̃ − m_new)                              # scale new block
ℓ_new = α·ℓ + β·rowsum(exp(S − m̃))                  # updated normalizer
O_new = α·O + β·(exp(S − m̃) · V_j)                  # updated output
```

After the final block, O = O/ℓ equals the exact softmax attention output. Crucially, the quadratic matrix exp(Sᵢⱼ − m̃) is *transient*: it exists only in SRAM between the two fused GEMMs (QKᵀ and PV) and is never written to HBM. This is the fusion that converts O(N²) HBM traffic into O(N²d²/M) [1]. The correctness argument is block-wise induction on the online-normalizer theorem of Section 2.3; FlashAttention stores (m, ℓ) as fp32/fp16 per row, a negligible O(N) overhead.

### 4.2 Tiling, Block Sizes, and the SRAM Budget

The block sizes are not arbitrary — they are solved from the SRAM capacity constraint. Dao et al. set B_c = ⌈M/(4d)⌉ and B_r = min(⌈M/(4d)⌉, d), allocating SRAM quadrants to Qᵢ, Kⱼ, Vⱼ, and the Sᵢⱼ tile [1]. Two competing pressures shape the choice:

- **Large B_r × B_c** amortizes the O(Nd) cost of loading Q blocks across the T_c key blocks and raises the GEMM arithmetic intensity, pushing the inner loop toward the tensor-core roofline.
- **Small blocks** leave SRAM headroom for double buffering (overlapping the next Kⱼ/Vⱼ HBM load with the current tile's compute) and reduce register pressure from the softmax rescaling.

The analysis in [1] proves FlashAttention's HBM access count is Θ(N²d²/M) and that this is *optimal* among algorithms using Θ(M) SRAM for a range of M — no exact attention algorithm with the same fast-memory budget can move asymptotically less data. Empirically, the FA-1 kernel achieved 2–4× end-to-end speedups: 15% wall-clock improvement on BERT-large training versus the MLPerf 1.1 record, 3× on GPT-2, and 2.4× on Long-Range Arena [1].

### 4.3 Recomputation vs. Storage: the Backward-Pass Tradeoff

Standard autograd stores S and P (Θ(N²) bytes) to compute gradients. FlashAttention stores only the per-row statistics (m, ℓ) — Θ(N) — and *recomputes* each Pᵢⱼ block on demand inside the backward kernel from HBM-resident Q, K, V, and dO [1]. The tradeoff is deliberate and asymmetric:

| Strategy | HBM storage | Backward HBM traffic | Backward FLOPs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store S, P | Θ(N²) | Θ(N²) reads | Θ(N²d) |
| Recompute (FlashAttention) | Θ(N) | Θ(N²d²/M) + Θ(Nd) | ~2.5× forward FLOPs |

Recomputation adds roughly 2–3× the GEMM FLOPs of a stored-backward, but FLOPs are the *cheap* resource: HBM bandwidth is the binding constraint, and recomputation keeps the backward pass in the compute-bound regime [1]. This same tradeoff resurfaces in FA-3's handling of FP8, where the low-precision path stores even less and recomputes more aggressively [3]. The principle generalizes beyond attention: any operator whose intermediates are cheap to recompute but expensive to move should recompute — the foundation of activation checkpointing, taken to its SRAM-resident extreme.

### 4.4 FlashAttention-2: Parallelism and FLOP Discipline

FlashAttention-2 (Dao, 2023) kept the exact same mathematics and IO complexity but re-engineered the *parallelization and instruction mix* [2]. Three changes matter:

1. **Loop reordering for parallelism.** FA-1 parallelized over the batch and head dimensions only, leaving SMs idle when batch × heads was small. FA-2 parallelizes over the sequence-length dimension as well, assigning different thread blocks to different query blocks and reducing the per-block KV working set each SM must stream [2].
2. **Fewer non-matmul FLOPs.** The online-softmax rescaling and masking in FA-1 consumed a large fraction of non-GEMM instructions. FA-2 restructures the inner loop so the rescale factors α, β are applied with minimal elementwise traffic, cutting non-matmul FLOPs roughly in half [2].
3. **Work partitioning and warp layout.** Within a thread block, warps are partitioned so that the QKᵀ GEMM, the softmax reduction, and the PV GEMM pipeline through registers without spilling, and causal masking skips fully-masked blocks entirely [2].

The result: ~230 TFLOPs/s on A100 (FP16), roughly 2× FA-1 and ~73% of peak — achieved without changing a single output value [2].

### 4.5 FlashAttention-3: Asynchrony, WGMMA, and the Hopper Schedule

Hopper (H100) breaks the Ampere programming model: it adds the Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) for asynchronous bulk copies between HBM and shared memory, WGMMA instructions that let a *warpgroup* (4 warps) drive tensor cores asynchronously with operands staged in shared memory, and ~50% more on-chip bandwidth [3]. FlashAttention-3 (Shah et al., 2024) is a ground-up rewrite around three ideas [3]:

- **Producer–consumer asynchrony.** Warps are specialized: *producer* warpgroups issue TMA loads of K/V tiles into a multi-stage shared-memory pipeline, while *consumer* warpgroups issue WGMMA dot-products. Compute and data movement overlap almost perfectly, hiding HBM latency that FA-2 exposed [3].
- **WGMMA for both GEMMs.** The QKᵀ and PV multiplications both use WGMMA with operands resident in shared memory, eliminating the register-file staging that bounded FA-2's tile sizes [3].
- **FP8 and interleaved block-wise quantization.** FA-3 supports FP8 E4M3 inputs with per-block scaling, and an *incoherent processing* trick (randomized Hadamard-style transforms) bounds quantization error — the first attention kernel to make FP8 training-quality viable at 75% of H100 peak, ~740 TFLOPs/s [3].

The scheduling lesson is architectural: each GPU generation moves the bottleneck, and the IO-aware algorithm must be re-lowered to the new memory-movement primitives rather than merely re-tuned [3][5].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

**IO-optimality (FA-1).** For SRAM size M and head dimension d, FlashAttention performs Θ(N²d²/M) HBM accesses in the forward pass, versus Θ(N²d + N²) for standard attention — a reduction by a factor of Θ(M/d) [1]. For typical M ≈ 100 KB usable and d = 64, this is roughly an order of magnitude. The paper further shows this bound is tight for the SRAM budget [1].

**Wall-clock validation.** Reported speedups on A100-class hardware: 2–4× attention-kernel speedup over PyTorch/apex baselines; 15% end-to-end BERT-large training speedup vs. the MLPerf 1.1 record; 3× GPT-2 training speedup; 2.4× on Long-Range Arena; and the first above-chance Transformer results on Path-X (16K) and Path-256 (64K) long-range tasks [1].

**Generation-over-generation utilization.** FA-1 reached ~25–40% of A100 peak; FA-2 ~50–73% (~230 TFLOPs/s); FA-3 ~75% of H100 peak (~740 TFLOPs/s in FP16, higher in FP8) [1][2][3]. Each step came from re-mapping the same O(N²d²/M)-IO algorithm onto new data-movement hardware, not from new asymptotics.

**Operational intensity in serving.** In LLM inference, FlashInfer's analysis shows FlashAttention's operational intensity scales as O(1/(1/ℓ_qo + 1/ℓ_kv)), collapsing to O(ℓ_qo) in decode — explaining why grouped-query attention (GQA), which raises the query-to-KV ratio g, multiplies intensity by g and is the natural companion to tiled attention in serving stacks [6].

> **Theorem:** *Exactness.* For every query block i, the algorithm of Section 4.1 returns Oᵢ = softmax(QᵢKᵀ/√d)V exactly (up to floating-point rounding), because the per-block rescaling is an algebraic identity on the running statistics (m, ℓ, O). The FlashAttention test suite verifies the maximum numerical error is at most twice that of a PyTorch baseline across head dimensions, dtypes, and causal/non-causal variants [1].

## 6 Limitations

- **Still quadratic in compute.** FlashAttention reduces IO complexity, not FLOPs: the Θ(N²d) multiply-adds remain, so at extreme sequence lengths (1M+) even the tiled kernel saturates, motivating complementary sparse or linear approximations layered *on top of* the tiled substrate.
- **Head-dimension sensitivity.** The SRAM budget forces B_r, B_c to shrink as d grows; for d ≥ 256 the tiles become GEMM-inefficient and utilization drops, which is why production kernels special-case large head dimensions and why GQA/MQA (smaller KV heads) pair well with tiling [2][6].
- **Hardware specificity.** Each generation is hand-lowered to one architecture's movement primitives (Ampere async-copy, Hopper TMA/WGMMA); porting to new accelerators is a rewrite, not a recompile — a cost partially addressed by Triton-based implementations at some performance penalty [3].
- **Backward-pass recomputation cost.** The recompute strategy assumes FLOPs are cheap relative to bandwidth; on bandwidth-rich or FLOP-poor hardware the tradeoff inverts, and stored-activation variants can win [1].
- **Numerical edge cases.** The running statistics (m, ℓ) accumulate in fp32, but aggressive FP8 quantization in FA-3 requires incoherent processing to keep error bounded; without it, outlier channels in K/V break the per-block scaling [3].

## 7 Conclusion

FlashAttention's enduring contribution is not a kernel but a design discipline: *measure the memory hierarchy first, count bytes moved before FLOPs, and fuse everything the SRAM budget allows*. The online-softmax recurrence turns a three-pass, HBM-resident normalization into a one-pass, SRAM-resident accumulation [4]; tiling plus fusion converts Θ(N²) HBM traffic into Θ(N²d²/M) [1]; storing O(N) statistics and recomputing the rest keeps training tractable [1]; and each hardware generation — FA-2's parallelism on Ampere, FA-3's asynchronous WGMMA pipeline on Hopper — re-expresses the same IO-optimal skeleton in the new machine's movement vocabulary [2][3]. As accelerators continue to widen the gap between tensor-core throughput and DRAM bandwidth, the IO-aware methodology FlashAttention pioneered will only grow more central: the next order of magnitude in attention performance will come from moving less data, not from multiplying it faster.
