# Ricci Flow and the Poincaré Conjecture: Hamilton's Program, Perelman's Entropy Functional, and Surgery

## Abstract

This thesis reconstructs the Hamilton–Perelman proof of the Poincaré Conjecture via the Ricci flow with surgery. We begin with Hamilton's evolution equation ∂_t g = −2Ric and the DeTurck trick that renders it strictly parabolic, securing short-time existence. The analytic core follows: Hamilton's curvature pinching and differential Harnack estimates in dimension three, which confine finite-time singularities to neck-like regions. Perelman's contribution is developed in full: the W-entropy functional and its monotonicity, the gradient-flow interpretation, L-geometry and reduced volume, and the no-local-collapsing theorem that restores geometric control where curvature blows up. Blow-up analysis classifies singularity models as shrinking solitons and ancient κ-solutions, and the canonical neighborhood theorem makes surgery possible: high-curvature necks are excised and capped at a discrete set of singular times, producing a flow defined for all time. Finally, the finite-extinction theorem for manifolds without aspherical summands forces the flow on a homotopy 3-sphere to disappear in finite time, proving it is S³, while the thick–thin decomposition yields Thurston's geometrization. The thesis emphasizes the monotone quantities and comparison principles that converted a nonlinear PDE into a topological classification tool.

## 1 Introduction

In 1904 Henri Poincaré asked whether every simply connected closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere — a question that became the most famous open problem in topology and, a century later, one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems [6]. The conjecture resisted purely topological attack for a hundred years. Its resolution came instead from geometric analysis: Richard Hamilton's 1982 proposal to evolve an arbitrary Riemannian metric toward a canonical one by the *Ricci flow*, the nonlinear heat-type equation

$$\partial_t g = -2\,\mathrm{Ric}(g),$$





## 2 Background

A Riemannian 3-manifold $(M^3, g)$ is *rigid* in a way higher-dimensional manifolds are not: the Weyl tensor vanishes identically in dimension three, so the full Riemann curvature tensor $\mathrm{Rm}$ is algebraically determined by the Ricci tensor. If $\lambda \ge \mu \ge \nu$ denote the eigenvalues of the curvature operator, the sectional curvatures are pairwise sums, and the scalar curvature is $R = 2(\lambda + \mu + \nu)$. Positivity of Ricci curvature therefore controls *all* sectional curvatures — a fact Hamilton exploited from the start [4].


Two foundational facts shape everything:

- **Short-time existence.** The equation is only *weakly* parabolic because it is invariant under diffeomorphisms; the DeTurck trick (Section 4.1) breaks the gauge and yields existence and uniqueness for smooth initial data [4].
- **Scalar curvature is monotone in the minimum.** From $\partial_t R = \Delta R + 2|\mathrm{Ric}|^2$ and the scalar maximum principle, $\min_M R(\cdot, t)$ is nondecreasing. In particular, a metric of positive scalar curvature keeps it, and the reaction term $2|\mathrm{Ric}|^2 \ge \frac{2}{3}R^2$ forces finite-time blow-up whenever $R > 0$ somewhere: singularities are *unavoidable*, not pathological.

The model success story is dimension two. On a closed surface the normalized flow $\partial_t g = -2(K - \bar{K})g$ exists for all time and converges exponentially to the constant-curvature metric in its conformal class — uniformization by PDE. Hamilton proved this in 1988, and it remains the philosophical template: *normalize to fix the volume, and let the flow find the canonical geometry*.

> **Theorem:** *(Poincaré Conjecture, Perelman 2003 [1][2][3]).* Every simply connected closed smooth 3-manifold is diffeomorphic to the 3-sphere $S^3$.


## 3 Methodology

The analysis rests on four interlocking methods.

**1. Maximum principles for tensors.** Hamilton's tensor maximum principle extends the scalar parabolic maximum principle to sections of vector bundles satisfying $\partial_t S = \Delta S + \Phi(S)$: if a closed convex $O(n)$-invariant set of algebraic curvature tensors is preserved by the ODE $\dot{S} = \Phi(S)$, it is preserved by the PDE. Combined with Uhlenbeck's trick — evolving an orthonormal frame by an ODE so that covariant derivatives commute cleanly — this reduces curvature pinching to *ODE comparison*: one studies the reaction ODE $\dot{\mathrm{Rm}} = Q(\mathrm{Rm})$ and transfers invariant sets to the flow [4].

**2. Differential Harnack estimates.** The Li–Yau–Hamilton Harnack inequality for the Ricci flow with nonnegative curvature operator states that for any vector field $V$,

$$Z := \partial_t R + \frac{R}{t} + 2\langle \nabla R, V\rangle + 2\,\mathrm{Ric}(V,V) \ge 0,$$

integrating to a classical Harnack comparison of scalar curvature at different space-time points. This is the parabolic analogue of the Cheng–Yau gradient estimate and is indispensable for controlling ancient solutions [4].

**3. Monotone functionals and gradient-flow structure.** Perelman discovered that Ricci flow is, modulo diffeomorphisms, the gradient flow of the $\mathcal{F}$-functional

$$\mathcal{F}(g, f) = \int_M \big(R + |\nabla f|^2\big)\, e^{-f}\, d\mu,$$

and introduced the $\mathcal{W}$-entropy whose monotonicity rules out periodic orbits ("breathers") and yields the no-collapsing theorem [1]. These are *Lyapunov functions* for an infinite-dimensional dynamical system — the decisive new idea.

**4. Blow-up and compactness.** At a finite-time singularity $T < \infty$, Hamilton's compactness theorem for sequences of Ricci flows with uniform curvature bounds produces *singularity models*: complete ancient solutions obtained by parabolic rescaling $(x, t) \mapsto (x, t)$ at the natural curvature scale. Perelman's no-collapsing guarantees these limits do not collapse, so the models are genuine geometric objects [1][5].

The logical dependencies flow downward: pinching (Section 4.2) needs the tensor maximum principle; entropy monotonicity (Section 4.3) needs the conjugate heat equation; the canonical neighborhood theorem (Section 4.4) needs both Harnack and no-collapsing; surgery (Section 4.5) needs canonical neighborhoods plus a *standard solution* as the cap model.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The DeTurck Trick and Short-Time Existence

The Ricci flow is invariant under the full diffeomorphism group: if $g(t)$ solves the flow and $\varphi_t$ is a family of diffeomorphisms, then $\varphi_t^* g(t)$ is *not* generally a solution, but the equation's degeneracy means its linearization has a nontrivial kernel — the Lie derivatives $L_X g$. Hence the system is only weakly parabolic and standard theory does not apply directly.

DeTurck's trick fixes a background metric $\bar{g}$ and modifies the flow by a carefully chosen diffeomorphism gauge. Define the vector field

$$W^k = g^{ij}\big(\Gamma_{ij}^k - \bar{\Gamma}_{ij}^k\big),$$

the trace of the difference of Christoffel symbols. The *Ricci–DeTurck flow*

$$\partial_t g = -2\,\mathrm{Ric}(g) + L_W g$$


For complete noncompact manifolds with bounded curvature, Shi proved short-time existence with bounded-curvature control via a localized maximum principle — the starting point for analyzing singularity models, which are noncompact ancient solutions [5].

> **Theorem:** *(Short-time existence and uniqueness).* For every smooth Riemannian metric $g_0$ on a closed manifold $M^n$ there exists $T > 0$ and a unique smooth solution $g(t)$ of $\partial_t g = -2\,\mathrm{Ric}$ on the half-open interval of times from $0$ to $T$ with $g(0) = g_0$.

### 4.2 Curvature Evolution and Hamilton's Pinching in Dimension Three

Under the flow the curvature operator evolves by

$$\partial_t \mathrm{Rm} = \Delta \mathrm{Rm} + Q(\mathrm{Rm}),$$


The sharp form is the **Hamilton–Ivey pinching estimate**: along any 3-dimensional Ricci flow, at each point either $\nu \ge 0$, or

$$R \ge (-\nu)\,\big(\log(-\nu) + \log(1+t) - 3\big)\qquad\text{whenever }\nu < 0.$$


Consequences are far-reaching:

- Near a finite-time singularity, where $R \to +\infty$, the estimate forces $\nu / R \to 0$: blow-up limits have *nonnegative* sectional curvature. Singularity models are therefore geometrically tame.
- Combined with the strong maximum principle, nonnegative Ricci curvature is preserved, and Hamilton's *differential Harnack* estimate applies to ancient solutions with bounded nonnegative curvature operator.
- The pinching feeds directly into *neck detection*: regions of large positive curvature with almost-vanishing negative part are close to round cylinders $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ — the $\varepsilon$-necks of the canonical neighborhood theorem.


### 4.3 Perelman's W-Entropy, L-Geometry, and No Local Collapsing

Perelman's first preprint [1] introduced two monotone quantities that transformed the subject. The first is the **$\mathcal{W}$-entropy**. For a metric $g$, a function $f$, and a scale $\tau > 0$, with $u = (4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}e^{-f}$ normalized to $\int u\,d\mu = 1$,

$$\mathcal{W}(g, f, \tau) = \int_M \big[\tau\big(|\nabla f|^2 + R\big) + f - n\big]\, u\, d\mu.$$

If $g(t)$ evolves by Ricci flow, $f(t)$ by the *conjugate heat equation* $\partial_t f = -\Delta f + |\nabla f|^2 - R + \frac{n}{2\tau}$, and $\tau(t) = T - t$ is backward time, then

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{W} = 2\int_M \tau\,\Big|\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 f - \frac{g}{2\tau}\Big|^2 u\, d\mu \;\ge\; 0.$$

Monotonicity is *strict* unless the flow is a *shrinking gradient Ricci soliton*, $\mathrm{Ric} + \nabla^2 f = g/(2\tau)$. Three applications follow immediately [1][8]:

1. **No nontrivial breathers.** A Ricci flow periodic modulo diffeomorphism and scaling would return $\mathcal{W}$ to its initial value, forcing the soliton equation at every time — hence breathers are trivial (steady solitons or Einstein). The flow has no periodic orbits in the moduli space of metrics.
2. **The $\mu$-functional.** Minimizing $\mathcal{W}$ over $f$ gives $\mu(g, \tau)$, and $\nu(g) = \inf_{\tau} \mu(g,\tau)$ is monotone nondecreasing along the flow — a scale-invariant Sobolev-type constant.
3. **No local collapsing.** If $|\mathrm{Rm}| \le r^{-2}$ on the parabolic ball $P(x, t, r, -r^2)$, then $\mathrm{Vol}\,B(x, t, r) \ge \kappa r^n$ for $\kappa$ depending only on the initial metric. Curvature controls volume from below: *where curvature is bounded, the manifold cannot collapse*.

The second monotone quantity is the **reduced volume**, built from Perelman's $\mathcal{L}$-geometry. For a curve $\gamma$ in space-time with backward time $\tau$, the $\mathcal{L}$-length is

$$\mathcal{L}(\gamma) = \int \sqrt{\tau}\,\big(R + |\dot{\gamma}|^2\big)\, ds,$$

whose critical points are $\mathcal{L}$-geodesics satisfying a Jacobi-type equation with Ricci correction. Minimizing over curves from a basepoint gives the reduced length $\ell(q, \tau)$, and the *reduced volume*

$$\tilde{V}(\tau) = \int_M (4\pi\tau)^{-n/2} e^{-\ell(q,\tau)}\, d\mu(q)$$



### 4.4 Singularity Models: Ancient κ-Solutions and Neckpinches

Suppose $T < \infty$ is the first singular time. Choose points $(x_i, t_i)$ with $t_i \nearrow T$ nearly attaining $\max |\mathrm{Rm}|$, and parabolically rescale by $Q_i = |\mathrm{Rm}|(x_i, t_i)$:

$$g_i(t) = Q_i\, g\big(t_i + t/Q_i\big).$$


In dimension three, ancient $\kappa$-solutions are rigidly classified [1][5]:

| Model | Geometry | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Shrinking round $S^3$ | Constant positive curvature | Extinction of spherical components |
| Shrinking cylinder $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ | Product soliton | Neckpinch cross-section |
| Quotients $(S^2 \times \mathbb{R})/\Gamma$ | Twisted cylinders | Non-orientable necks |
| Bryant soliton | Rotationally symmetric steady soliton | Degenerate neckpinch tip |




### 4.5 Surgery with Cutoff and the Long-Time Existence Theorem

Perelman's surgery procedure [2] operates with two scale parameters, a *cutoff radius* $h$ and a much larger *canonical scale* $r$, plus an accuracy parameter $\delta$. At a singular time $T$:

1. **Detect.** By the canonical neighborhood theorem, the super-level set $\{R \ge h^{-2}\}$ decomposes into $\delta$-necks, $\delta$-caps, and compact components of positive curvature.
2. **Cut.** Along the central 2-sphere of each *maximal horn* — a chain of $\delta$-necks terminating in a cap, with curvature increasing toward the tip — cut at a neck of radius comparable to $h$.
3. **Cap.** Glue a *standard cap* — a fixed rotationally symmetric metric on the 3-ball asymptotic to the cylinder, evolved briefly by the flow (the *standard solution*) — to each exposed boundary sphere.
4. **Discard.** Throw away components covered by canonical neighborhoods with positive curvature or recognized topology; continue the flow on the rest.


Nonaccumulation is the heart of [2]. If surgery times $T_i$ accumulated at $T_\infty < \infty$, rescaling about the accumulating surgeries would produce a non-flat ancient $\kappa$-solution arising as a limit of surgically modified flows — contradicting the classification, because the cutoff construction forces the limit to be the standard cylinder solution, whose geometry is incompatible with accumulation. The argument uses a delicate comparison between the surgically modified flow and the standard solution, plus Shi's derivative estimates extended across surgery times.

> **Theorem:** *(Long-time existence of Ricci flow with surgery [2]).* For any initial metric on a closed orientable 3-manifold, there exists a Ricci flow with surgery defined for all $t \ge 0$, whose surgery times form a discrete subset of $(0, \infty)$, such that on each nonsingular interval the flow satisfies the canonical neighborhood assumptions with uniform parameters.

Each surgery either performs a connected-sum decomposition, removes a spherical space form or $S^2$-bundle component, or discards a topologically standard piece — so the topology evolves in a completely controlled way [2][6]. A detailed independent account of the surgery analysis, with additional analytic background, is given in [7].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

**Numerical verification of the model singularity ODEs.** The shrinking-soliton models of Section 4.4 reduce, under symmetry, to ODEs whose extinction times are known in closed form — a checkable prediction of the theory. For the round 3-sphere, $g(t) = r(t)^2 g_{\mathrm{unit}}$ gives $\mathrm{Ric} = \frac{2}{r^2}g$, whence $2r\dot r = -4$, i.e. $u := r^2$ satisfies $u' = -4$ and $u(t) = r_0^2 - 4t$, extinct at $T = r_0^2/4$. For the round cylinder $S^2(r) \times \mathbb{R}$, $u' = -2$ and $T = r_0^2/2$. The following script integrates both with classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta and compares against the exact extinction times:

```python
import numpy as np

def rk4(f, y0, t):
    """Classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta on a uniform grid t."""
    y = np.empty_like(t, dtype=float)
    y[0] = y0
    for k in range(len(t) - 1):
        h = t[k + 1] - t[k]
        yk = y[k]
        k1 = f(yk)
        k2 = f(yk + 0.5 * h * k1)
        k3 = f(yk + 0.5 * h * k2)
        k4 = f(yk + h * k3)
        y[k + 1] = yk + h * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4) / 6.0
    return y

def extinction(u0, rate, n=20000):
    """Integrate u' = -rate up to 99.9 percent of the exact extinction time."""
    T = u0 / rate
    t = np.linspace(0.0, 0.999 * T, n)
    u = rk4(lambda u: -rate, u0, t)
    return T, t[-1], u[-1]

for name, u0, rate in [("round S^3", 4.0, 4.0),
                       ("cylinder S^2 x R", 4.0, 2.0)]:
    T, t_last, u_last = extinction(u0, rate)
    print(f"{name:18s} exact T = {T:.6f}   reached t = {t_last:.6f}   u = {u_last:.2e}")
```

The output confirms the analytic prediction to discretization accuracy:

| Model | $r_0$ | Exact $T$ | Numerical $t_{\max}$ | Residual $u$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round $S^3$ | 2 | 1.000000 | 0.999000 | $4.0 \times 10^{-3}$ |
| Cylinder $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ | 2 | 2.000000 | 1.998000 | $4.0 \times 10^{-3}$ |




**Geometrization.** For general $M$, the flow with surgery exists for all time. Perelman showed the manifold splits into a *thick* part, where the flow converges (after rescaling) to a hyperbolic metric, and a *thin* part, which collapses with a local lower curvature bound — and collapsing with such a bound forces the thin part to be a *graph manifold* [2]. Reorganizing gives the geometric decomposition: every closed 3-manifold decomposes along tori into pieces each admitting one of Thurston's eight geometries [6]:

| Geometry | Model space | Curvature sign |
|---|---|---|
| $S^3$ | Round 3-sphere | Positive |
| $E^3$ | Euclidean space | Zero |
| $H^3$ | Hyperbolic space | Negative |
| $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ | Cylinder | Nonnegative |
| $H^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ | Saddle extruded | Nonpositive |
| $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})$ | Twisted bundle | Negative-ish |
| Nil | Heisenberg group | Mixed |
| Sol | Torus bundle | Mixed |


## 6 Limitations

The Hamilton–Perelman theory, monumental as it is, has genuine boundaries:

- **Surgery is not canonical.** Perelman's construction involves choices — the cutoff scales $h \ll r$, the accuracy $\delta$, the precise neck locations. Different choices yield different flows with surgery. A fully *canonical* flow through singularities was constructed only later by Bamler–Kleiner using the theory of singular Ricci flows [9]; Perelman's version suffices for topology but not for uniqueness questions.
- **Dependence on deep 3-manifold topology.** The geometrization conclusion leans on the JSJ decomposition, the theory of collapsing with local lower curvature bounds (Shioya–Yamaguchi), and the identification of collapsed pieces as graph manifolds — thousands of pages of topology outside PDE [6]. The Ricci flow does not *replace* 3-manifold topology; it *guides* it.
- **No higher-dimensional analogue.** The Hamilton–Ivey pinching, the classification of ancient $\kappa$-solutions, and the canonical neighborhood theorem all fail or are unknown in dimension four and above. Singularities of 4-dimensional Ricci flow can exhibit collapsing and complex formation; the program is intrinsically three-dimensional.
- **Non-explicit constants.** The parameters $\varepsilon, \delta, h, r, \kappa$ are obtained by contradiction and compactness — there is no effective bound on the number of surgeries or the extinction time in terms of the initial metric. The theory is *qualitative*.
- **Numerical Ricci flow remains nascent.** Unlike mean curvature flow, there is no robust general-purpose numerical Ricci flow in 3D; the diffeomorphism gauge and singularity formation defeat naive discretizations. The ODE verification in Section 5 covers only symmetric models.
- **Open analytic questions.** Uniqueness of tangent flows (blow-up limits) at singularities is unknown in general; the precise long-time asymptotics of the thick part beyond subsequential convergence required Bamler's later work [9].

## 7 Conclusion

The proof of the Poincaré Conjecture is a triumph of *monotonicity*. Hamilton's equation gave the flow; his pinching and Harnack estimates gave control of curvature; but it was Perelman's monotone quantities — the $\mathcal{W}$-entropy increasing in forward time, the reduced volume decreasing in backward time — that tamed the singularities. No-collapsing turned blow-ups into classifiable ancient solutions; canonical neighborhoods turned classification into a surgery algorithm; surgery turned finite-time breakdown into an all-time flow; finite extinction turned the all-time flow into a topological recognition procedure for the 3-sphere; and the thick–thin decomposition turned it into geometrization.

The chain of implications is worth stating plainly:

1. Short-time existence (DeTurck) starts the flow [4].
2. Pinching (Hamilton–Ivey) forces singularity models to have nonnegative curvature [4].
3. Entropy monotonicity rules out breathers and yields no-local-collapsing [1].
4. Blow-up analysis classifies singularity models as shrinking solitons [1][5].
5. Canonical neighborhoods localize high curvature to necks and caps [2].
6. Surgery with cutoff removes singularities at a discrete set of times [2].
7. Finite extinction kills the flow on homotopy spheres [3] — Poincaré.
8. Thick–thin decomposition geometrizes everything else [2][6] — Thurston.
