# Programmable Bootstrapping in TFHE: Blind Rotation, Functional Bootstrapping, and Noise Refresh for Encrypted Neural Network Inference

## Abstract

The Torus Fully Homomorphic Encryption (TFHE) scheme is distinguished by fast gate-level bootstrapping that simultaneously refreshes ciphertext noise and evaluates an arbitrary univariate function encoded as a look-up table in a test polynomial. This dual-purpose operation, programmable bootstrapping (PBS), reduces every nonlinear layer of a neural network to a single bootstrapping: modulus switching maps the LWE phase to a rotation index, blind rotation obliviously rotates the encrypted test vector through a CMux chain keyed by RGSW encryptions of the secret bits, and sample extraction plus key switching return a fresh low-noise ciphertext encrypting the function output. Because post-PBS noise depends only on the PBS parameters and not on prior computation, quantized networks of arbitrary depth become computable with exactly one PBS per activation. We formalize the three-phase pipeline, derive the variance-based noise budget that governs correctness, survey the precision advances from 4 to 12 bits (WoP-PBS, multi-value PBS, Meta-PBS), and give a quantitative performance model of a PBS-per-neuron quantized MLP pipeline for MNIST-scale inference.

---

## 1. Introduction

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) promises computation on data that is never decrypted, yet its practical deployment has been gated by the cost of *nonlinear* operations. In lattice-based FHE, every homomorphic multiplication amplifies noise; when noise exceeds a threshold, decryption fails. Classical leveled schemes such as BGV, BFV, and CKKS cope by parameterizing for a maximum circuit depth, which makes arbitrary-depth computation — and in particular the iterated nonlinear activations of deep neural networks — expensive or approximate. The TFHE family [1] inverts this tradeoff: it embraces bootstrapping, the operation that refreshes a noisy ciphertext, as the *primitive* computational unit rather than a rare rescue operation.

The insight that elevates TFHE bootstrapping from a maintenance routine to a universal evaluator is the **programmable bootstrapping (PBS)** [2][4]. In a standard TFHE gate bootstrapping, a look-up table (LUT) encoded in the coefficients of a *test polynomial* is blindly rotated by the encrypted message, and the constant coefficient is extracted; the output is a fresh encryption of the LUT applied to the input message. By replacing the binary gate's test vector with an encoding of an arbitrary function $f$, the same procedure computes $f(m)$ homomorphically *and* refreshes the noise to a fixed low level, all in one pass. A ReLU, a sigmoid approximation, a quantization step, or a sign function each costs exactly one PBS, regardless of the function's algebraic complexity.

This restructures the economics of encrypted inference. Where CKKS pipelines approximate ReLU with high-degree minimax polynomial compositions [5] — each consuming depth and noise budget, with costly bootstraps at depth boundaries — a TFHE pipeline performs linear layers with cheap LWE additions and cleartext scalar multiplications, then folds every nonlinearity into the bootstrapping that the accumulated noise demanded anyway. The result, demonstrated by Concrete and TFHE-rs [6][7], is exact integer inference on quantized networks with no depth ceiling.

> **Theorem (Informal):** For a quantized neural network with $b$-bit activations, TFHE programmable bootstrapping evaluates the entire network with exactly one bootstrapping per nonlinear activation and unbounded depth, where the post-PBS noise variance is a fixed function of the PBS parameters alone, independent of the circuit evaluated before it.

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the TFHE cryptosystem and prior work on functional bootstrapping. Section 3 formalizes the three-phase PBS pipeline and the test-vector construction. Section 4 dissects blind rotation, negacyclicity constraints, noise accounting, and high-precision extensions. Section 5 gives empirical performance figures and formal correctness bounds. Section 6 discusses limitations and open problems, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The TFHE scheme

TFHE, introduced by Chillotti, Gama, Georgieva, and Izabachène [1], works over the *real torus* $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, the additive group of reals modulo 1. Messages are encoded as elements of a discretized subset of $\mathbb{T}$; the canonical encoding maps a bit $x \in \{0,1\}$ to $x/2 \in \mathbb{T}$, so that the two codewords are maximally separated. The scheme defines three ciphertext flavors:

- **TLWE** (torus LWE): $(\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{T}^{n+1}$ with $b = \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle + m + e$, encrypting $m \in \mathbb{T}$ under binary secret $\mathbf{s} \in \{0,1\}^n$.
- **TRLWE** (ring variant): polynomials in $\mathbb{T}_N[X] = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$, encrypting a polynomial message.
- **TRGSW** (ring GSW): a matrix of TRLWE samples enabling the *external product* $\boxdot$ with a TRLWE ciphertext, the workhorse of blind rotation.

Security reduces to (ring) LWE on the torus. The original TFHE paper demonstrated gate bootstrapping — a NAND gate plus noise refresh — in ~10 ms per gate, orders of magnitude faster than contemporary BGV-style bootstrapping [1].

### 2.2 From gate bootstrapping to programmable bootstrapping

Gate bootstrapping evaluates only binary gates. The generalization to arbitrary univariate functions was developed across several works: the functional-bootstrapping line of work and, most influentially, the *improved programmable bootstrapping* of Chillotti, Ligier, Orfila, and Tap [2], which introduced larger-precision PBS (beyond the native ~4-bit limit), multi-value bootstrapping (extracting several LUT outputs from one blind rotation), and the PBSmanyLUT procedure evaluating multiple look-up tables simultaneously. These works established PBS as the standard nonlinear primitive of integer TFHE, as exposed today in TFHE-rs [7] and Concrete [6].

Meta-PBS [3] achieves 12-bit negacyclic LUT evaluation in 156 ms — 82.4× faster than earlier error-tolerant bootstrapping — with capacity for tens of thousands of further additions. WoP-PBS (PBS *without padding*) [2] removes the padding-bit requirement imposed by negacyclicity, enabling full-domain evaluation of non-negacyclic functions such as ReLU over the complete input range. For neural inference specifically, "Deep Neural Networks for Encrypted Inference with TFHE" [6] demonstrated exact quantized inference competitive with leveled approaches, "Neural Network Training on Encrypted Data with TFHE" [9] extended PBS to quantized *training*, and recent work on homomorphic ReLU via full-domain bootstrapping [10] pushed single-PBS ReLU to 7–11 bit integers with 88.85% MNIST CNN accuracy.

### 2.3 Why neural networks fit PBS

A feed-forward network alternates *linear* layers (affine maps, convolutions) with *nonlinear* activations (ReLU, sigmoid, tanh). In TFHE:

1. Linear layers are cheap: LWE ciphertexts support homomorphic addition and multiplication by *cleartext* scalars natively, so a dot product is a sequence of additions with noise growing only linearly in the number of terms.
2. Nonlinear layers are exactly one PBS each, which simultaneously absorbs the linear layer's accumulated noise.
3. Depth is unbounded: after each PBS the noise is reset to the fixed PBS output noise, so networks of arbitrary depth — ResNets, LSTMs [4] — are computable.

The price is quantization: activations must fit in the PBS input precision (practically 4–12 bits depending on parameters), so networks are trained or calibrated with quantization-aware methods. Empirically this costs little accuracy on standard benchmarks [6][10].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The three-phase PBS pipeline

Let the input be a TLWE ciphertext $\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{T}^{n+1}$ encrypting $m$ with noise $e$, i.e., the *phase* $\varphi = b - \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle = m + e$. Let $f$ be the target univariate function defined on a discretized domain, and let the *test vector* (test polynomial) $v \in \mathbb{T}_N[X]$ encode $f$ as a LUT. The bootstrapping key $\mathsf{BK}_{s \to S}$ consists of TRGSW encryptions of the bits of $\mathbf{s}$ under a ring secret $S$. PBS proceeds in three phases [1][2]:

**Phase 1 — Modulus switching.** Map $\mathbf{c}$ from $\mathbb{T}^{n+1}$ to $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{\,n+1}$ by rounding: $\bar{a}_i = \lfloor 2N a_i \rceil$, $\bar{b} = \lfloor 2N b \rceil$. After switching, the phase satisfies

$$\bar{b} - \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle \approx 2N(m + e) \pmod{2N},$$

introducing a *modulus-switching rounding error* $e_{\mathrm{ms}}$ that is the first term in the noise budget. The integer $\lfloor 2N(m+e) \rceil$ is the rotation index: it selects which coefficient of the test vector will land in the constant position.

**Phase 2 — Blind rotation.** Initialize the accumulator as $\mathsf{ACC} = (0, v \cdot X^{-\bar{b}}) \in \mathrm{TRLWE}_S$, a trivial encryption of the test vector rotated by $-\bar{b}$. Then iterate over the $n$ mask components:

$$\mathsf{ACC} \leftarrow \mathsf{CMux}\big(\mathsf{BK}_i,\; \mathsf{ACC},\; \mathsf{ACC} \cdot X^{\bar{a}_i}\big), \qquad i = 1..n,$$

where $\mathsf{CMux}(C, d_0, d_1) = C \boxdot (d_1 - d_0) + d_0$ selects $d_{s_i}$ homomorphically using the TRGSW encryption $C$ of the secret bit $s_i$. After the loop,

$$\mathsf{ACC} \in \mathrm{TRLWE}_S\big(v \cdot X^{-(\bar{b} - \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle)}\big) = \mathrm{TRLWE}_S\big(v \cdot X^{-\lfloor 2N(m+e) \rceil}\big).$$

The rotation amount is *encrypted* — the evaluator never learns the index — hence "blind" rotation [8].

**Phase 3 — Sample extraction and key switching.** $\mathsf{SampleExtract}_0(\mathsf{ACC})$ pulls the constant coefficient into a TLWE ciphertext under the ring key $S$ (viewed as a vector), encrypting $v_0$'s rotated value, i.e., an encoding of $f(m)$ provided the noise was small enough that the rotation landed on the correct coefficient block. A final key switch returns to the original LWE key $\mathbf{s}$ (plus an offset to recenter the encoding).

### 3.2 Encoding the function: the test vector

The test vector encodes the LUT with redundancy. For a $t$-bit input domain and target function $f: \mathbb{Z}_t \to \mathbb{T}$, one sets

$$v = X^{-N/t} \sum_{i=0}^{t/2-1} \Big\lfloor f(i) \Big\rceil \sum_{j=0}^{2N/t-1} X^{i \cdot 2N/t + j} \;\in\; \mathbb{T}_N[X],$$

so each LUT entry occupies a *block* of $2N/t$ coefficients, giving error tolerance: a rotation error under half a block still lands on the correct entry [2][3]. This construction only works directly when $f$ is *negacyclic*, $f(x + t/2) = -f(x)$, because multiplication by $X^N = -1$ in the negacyclic ring flips the sign. Non-negacyclic functions (e.g., ReLU, sigmoid) require either a padding bit (evaluating $f$ on half the domain and relying on sign symmetry) or the WoP-PBS construction [2], which chains multiple PBS rounds or exploits the redundancy to cover the full domain — the "full-domain bootstrapping" line of work [10].

### 3.3 Noise model

We track noise by variance under the independence (central-limit) heuristic, standard in TFHE parameter selection [1][2]. Let $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{TRGSW}}$ be the noise variance of bootstrapping-key components, $\ell$ the gadget-decomposition length, $B_g$ its base, and $N$ the ring degree. Each CMux iteration contributes external-product noise of variance approximately

$$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{CMux}} \approx (k+1)\,\ell\,N\,B_g^2\,\sigma^2_{\mathrm{TRGSW}} + (k+1)(1+N)\,\epsilon^2,$$

with $\epsilon$ the decomposition rounding error and $k$ the GLWE rank; over $n$ iterations the blind-rotation variance accumulates linearly, $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{BR}} \approx n\,\sigma^2_{\mathrm{CMux}}$ (the rotation by $X^{\bar{a}_i}$ merely permutes coefficients and preserves variance) [8]. Adding modulus-switching variance $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ms}}$ and key-switching variance $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{KS}}$, the output noise $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{out}} = \sigma^2_{\mathrm{BR}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{KS}}$ is *independent of the input noise* — the defining property of bootstrapping. Correctness requires $\lvert e_{\mathrm{total}} \rvert < \Delta/2$ where $\Delta$ is the encoding distance; with the Gaussian heuristic, parameters are chosen so that $\Pr[\mathrm{fail}] \le 2^{-40}$ or below [3].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Blind rotation as oblivious polynomial evaluation

Blind rotation can be understood as evaluating the polynomial $v$ at the encrypted point $X^{-\varphi}$, where $\varphi = \bar{b} - \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle$. Expanding the CMux loop reveals a binary decision tree of depth $n$: at level $i$, the accumulator is either left alone or multiplied by $X^{\bar{a}_i}$, depending on $s_i$. Because each $\mathsf{BK}_i$ encrypts $s_i$, the evaluator performs $n$ *oblivious* conditional rotations whose composition equals rotation by $\sum_i \bar{a}_i s_i = \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle$, and the initial $X^{-\bar{b}}$ offset completes $\varphi$. Two subtleties matter:

- **The first accumulator is noiseless.** $\mathsf{ACC}_0 = (0, vX^{-\bar{b}})$ is a trivial TRLWE sample with zero noise; noise enters only through the $n$ CMux steps [8]. This is why the noise analysis counts exactly $n$ external products and not $n+1$.
- **Monomial multiplication is free.** Multiplying a TRLWE ciphertext by $X^{\bar{a}_i}$ is a negacyclic coefficient permutation — no noise, no NTT. All cryptographic cost concentrates in the external products $C \boxdot (d_1 - d_0)$. For typical parameters ($n \approx 500$–$800$, $N = 1024$–$4096$, $\ell = 2$–$4$), this yields millisecond-scale latencies. Parallelism across the $n$ iterations requires restructured variants such as Maverick's multi-chain decomposition [12].

```rust
// TFHE-rs style programmable bootstrapping (simplified pseudocode)
fn programmable_bootstrap(
    lwe_in: &LweCiphertext,      // input TLWE, noisy
    lut: &PlaintextList,         // f encoded as test vector v
    bsk: &BootstrappingKey,      // TRGSW encryptions of secret bits
    ksk: &KeyswitchKey,
) -> LweCiphertext {
    let mod_switched = modulus_switch(lwe_in);          // Phase 1: T -> Z_{2N}
    let mut acc = trivial_glwe_mul_x_pow(lut, -mod_switched.b);
    for (i, bk_i) in bsk.iter().enumerate() {            // Phase 2: blind rotation
        let rotated = acc.mul_monomial(mod_switched.a[i]);
        acc = cmux(bk_i, acc, rotated);                 // external product inside
    }
    let extracted = sample_extract(&acc, 0);            // Phase 3a
    keyswitch(&extracted, ksk)                           // Phase 3b
}
```

### 4.2 Negacyclicity, padding bits, and WoP-PBS

The ring $\mathbb{T}_N[X] = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$ is negacyclic: $X^{N} = -1$, so rotating the test vector by more than $N$ positions negates coefficients. Consequently a single test vector naturally encodes only *negacyclic* functions satisfying $f(x + t/2) = -f(x)$. The classical workaround reserves one *padding bit*: the $t$-bit message is embedded in a $(t+1)$-bit space, the function is evaluated on half the domain, and the output is interpreted with a sign flip on the upper half — costing one bit of precision and excluding functions like ReLU whose two halves are not sign-symmetric.

WoP-PBS ("without padding") [2] removes this tax by splitting the LUT across the negacyclic boundary deliberately, using the antiperiodicity as part of the encoding. Full-domain bootstrapping [10] generalizes this to arbitrary functions over 7–11 bit domains via signed-digit decomposition into chunks bootstrapped in parallel. For neural inference this is decisive: ReLU, the workhorse activation, is not negacyclic, and recovering its lost padding bit effectively doubles the usable activation precision.

### 4.3 Precision scaling: from 4 bits to 12 and beyond

Native TFHE PBS circa 2020 supported roughly 4–6 bits of message precision before the noise overwhelmed the encoding distance. Three complementary advances raised the ceiling:

1. **Multi-value PBS and PBSmanyLUT** [2]: a single blind rotation produces one RLWE accumulator whose coefficients encode the LUT; extracting *multiple* coefficients (or packing several LUTs into one test vector) amortizes the $n$ CMux iterations across several function evaluations, and the larger effective block sizes improve the noise-to-signal geometry.
2. **Error-tolerant and refined LUT packing** [3]: Meta-PBS restructures the blind-rotation noise so that its variance grows sublinearly in the decomposition parameters, achieving 12-bit negacyclic LUTs in 156 ms with enough residual capacity for 25,000 subsequent additions — enough to absorb entire linear layers between activations.
3. **Parameter re-optimization** [11]: systematic search over $(N, n, \ell, B_g, \sigma)$ under the $2^{-40}$ failure target shows that larger $N$ (2048–4096) with careful gadget bases buys precision more cheaply than naive scaling, because the NTT cost grows as $N \log N$ while the encoding distance for fixed bit-width grows with the modulus.

| Technique | Precision (bits) | PBS latency (order) | Key idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate bootstrapping [1] | 1 | ~10 ms | Binary LUT |
| Classic PBS [2] | 4–6 | tens of ms | Redundant test vector |
| WoP-PBS / full-domain [2][10] | 7–11 | ~1 s (ReLU, chunked) | Signed-digit decomposition |
| Meta-PBS [3] | 12 | 156 ms | Restructured BR noise |
| Tree-based multi-precision | 16+ | seconds | Hierarchical decomposition |

For neural inference, the sweet spot today is 6–8 bit activations: one PBS per neuron per layer at tens of milliseconds, with quantization-aware training recovering essentially full accuracy [6][9].

### 4.4 PBS as the nonlinear layer: encrypted inference architecture

Consider a quantized MLP with layers $\mathbf{z} = W\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{x}' = \sigma(\mathbf{z})$. In TFHE-rs/Concrete style integer PBS [6][7]:

- **Linear part.** $W, \mathbf{b}$ are cleartext (weights public; *data* private). Each neuron is a dot product $\sum_j w_j \cdot \mathsf{Enc}(x_j)$ via cleartext-scalar multiplications and LWE additions; noise grows as $\sum_j w_j^2 \sigma^2_{\mathrm{in}}$, erased by the subsequent PBS regardless.
- **Nonlinear part.** One PBS per neuron evaluates $\sigma$ (ReLU, tanh/sigmoid LUT) *and* resets noise to $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{out}}$. Activations are clipped/scaled during training so the accumulator $\mathbf{z}$ fits the supported domain.
- **Depth independence.** Noise is reset per layer, so a 100-layer network costs $100 \times$ (linear + PBS) with no compounding failure probability beyond the union bound — unlike leveled CKKS, where approximation error compounds and bootstrapping must be scheduled at depth intervals [5].

Max-pooling, argmax, and comparisons reduce to the same primitive: a comparison is a PBS evaluating the sign function (or a small LUT), and max-pooling over a window is a tournament of comparisons. LSTMs, whose gates need sigmoid and tanh, were demonstrated in TFHE by decomposing each gate into PBS-friendly LUTs [4]. The architectural moral is uniform: **in TFHE, the activation function is free given the bootstrap you needed anyway.**

### 4.5 Implementation landscape: TFHE-rs, Concrete, and hardware

Two production libraries define the state of the art. **TFHE-rs** (Zama) [7] exposes integer PBS over `FheUint` types, so a ReLU is a single `programmable_bootstrap` call with a precomputed test vector. **Concrete** (Zama) [6] adds a compiler: it ingests ONNX/NumPy programs, fuses consecutive univariate functions into one LUT (one PBS), and manages precision automatically. Recent systems work restructures blind rotation for GPUs: Maverick [12] decomposes the sequential CMux chain into $\sqrt{n}$ parallel sub-chains with homomorphic trace-based recombination, achieving order-of-magnitude speedups on parallel hardware at a modest key-size overhead (<0.7% of the bootstrapping key). Noise-aware key distributions [11] cut rounding-noise variance by 50%, yielding ~1.6× more operations per bootstrap.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness theorem

> **Theorem (PBS correctness):** Let $\mathbf{c} \in \mathrm{TLWE}_{\mathbf{s}}(m)$ with input noise $e_{\mathrm{in}}$, and let $f$ be encoded in test vector $v$ with block size $B = 2N/t$. If the total effective noise satisfies
>
> $$|e_{\mathrm{in}}| + |e_{\mathrm{ms}}| < \frac{B}{4N},$$
>
> and the blind-rotation and key-switching noises satisfy $|e_{\mathrm{BR}}| + |e_{\mathrm{KS}}| < \Delta_{\mathrm{out}}/4$ for output encoding distance $\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}$, then $\mathsf{PBS}(\mathbf{c})$ decrypts to $f(m)$ except with probability bounded by the Gaussian tail of the output noise.

*Proof sketch.* Modulus switching maps the phase into the coefficient block of $v$ encoding $f(m)$ under the hypothesis. Each CMux is a correct oblivious selector — the external product $C \boxdot (d_1 - d_0)$ decrypts to $s_i(d_1-d_0)$ plus decomposition noise [1][8] — so by induction the accumulator decrypts to $v \cdot X^{-\lfloor 2N(m+e) \rceil}$ with variance $n\,\sigma^2_{\mathrm{CMux}}$. Sample extraction is exact, key switching adds $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{KS}}$, and total noise below $\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}/2$ lets rounding recover $f(m)$. ∎

Under the central-limit heuristic, the failure probability is estimated as $2\,Q(\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}/(2\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}))$; parameters are chosen to push this below $2^{-40}$ [3][11].

### 5.2 Performance model for a quantized MLP

Consider MNIST inference with a 784–128–64–10 MLP, 8-bit quantized, ReLU activations: 202 PBS per image. At ~30 ms per 8-bit PBS per CPU core [7][3], inference costs ~6 s single-threaded and scales linearly with core count (neurons within a layer are independent). The bootstrapping key is 100–200 MB [12], a one-time cost. PBS is exact, so accuracy matches the quantized cleartext model [6][9]; reported figures include 88.85% MNIST CNN accuracy with full-domain ReLU PBS [10].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Precision ceiling.** Native single-PBS precision stalls around 12 bits [3]; 16-bit+ needs tree-based decomposition with superlinear latency. Whether a different test-vector encoding breaks the $O(2^b)$ block-size barrier is open.
2. **SIMD amortization.** TFHE PBS processes one scalar per bootstrap. Packing multiple values into one RLWE and bootstrapping them *simultaneously* (PBSmanyLUT evaluates multiple *functions* on one input [2]; the dual is less developed) remains an active target; true SIMD PBS would divide inference latency by the packing factor.
3. **The central-limit heuristic.** All failure-probability claims model accumulated noise as Gaussian and independent [1][8]. A *provable* noise analysis for the full $n$-iteration blind rotation would put parameter selection on rigorous footing; current worst-case bounds are too loose to be useful.
4. **Hardware co-design.** Blind rotation is CMux-bound: $n\ell$ NTTs per PBS with a strict sequential dependency. Maverick's multi-chain restructuring [12] relaxes the dependency with modest overhead; dedicated external-product accelerators could plausibly bring PBS under a millisecond.
5. **Quantization gap.** PBS inference inherits the quantized model's accuracy, and aggressive quantization (≤4 bits) still degrades hard tasks. Co-designing architectures *for* PBS — activations exactly representable as small LUTs — is underexplored.
6. **Circuit privacy.** Recent work [8] shows FHEW/TFHE-style bootstrapping can leak circuit information through noise distributions; achieving circuit privacy for PBS inference without prohibitive noise flooding is open.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Programmable bootstrapping is the operation that makes TFHE a machine-learning substrate rather than merely a fast bootstrapping scheme. By fusing *function evaluation* with *noise refresh* — blindly rotating an encrypted look-up table by an encrypted index, then extracting the selected coefficient — PBS evaluates any univariate function, including every neural activation of practical interest, in a single bootstrapping whose output noise depends only on the PBS parameters and not on the computation that preceded it. We have presented the three-phase pipeline, the test-vector encoding with its negacyclicity constraint and WoP-PBS/full-domain remedies, a predictive variance-based noise accounting, and the architectural consequence: arbitrary-depth quantized networks with one PBS per nonlinearity, realized in TFHE-rs and Concrete with exact integer semantics. The frontier — higher precision, SIMD amortization, heuristic-free noise analysis, external-product acceleration — is well-defined and active. In homomorphic machine learning, the bootstrap is not overhead. It is the computer.
