# Kernel-Bypass Fabric Transport: RDMA Queue-Pair Semantics, DCQCN Congestion Control, and RNIC Microarchitecture at Scale

## Abstract

Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA) has become the dominant fabric transport for data-center machine learning, disaggregated storage, and high-throughput key–value services, displacing the kernel TCP/IP stack where single-digit microsecond latency and line-rate bandwidth are non-negotiable. This thesis unifies RDMA as a kernel-bypass transport architecture: the InfiniBand verbs model of queue pairs (QPs), work queue elements (WQEs), and completion queues (CQs); one-sided versus two-sided verb semantics and their memory-ordering guarantees; the reliable-connected transport's hardware retransmission machinery; and the congestion-control ecosystem — Priority Flow Control, Explicit Congestion Notification, and Data-Center Quantized Congestion Notification (DCQCN) — that makes lossy Ethernet behave as a lossless fabric for RoCEv2. We formalize the RNIC microarchitecture, derive the DCQCN rate-control recurrence and its stability conditions, analyze the incast pathology that defeats naive end-to-end schemes, and evaluate the design space through FaRM, HERD, and the RedN offload framework. RDMA's enduring power lies in delegating concurrency control to hardware: every scalability ceiling in modern deployments traces to the finiteness of NIC-resident state.

## 1. Introduction

The kernel network stack was engineered for generality: sockets, multiplexing, fairness, and feature velocity across heterogeneous workloads. For a steadily growing class of data-center applications — distributed training of trillion-parameter models, NVMe-over-Fabrics storage, in-memory transaction processing — generality is the wrong currency. What these workloads demand is *predictability*: bounded tail latency, deterministic throughput under incast, and zero CPU involvement in the data path. RDMA answers by relocating the transport endpoint from the operating system into the network interface controller (RNIC), exposing a user-space verbs API that posts work directly to hardware queues and receives completions via DMA-written events [1].

The intellectual lineage of RDMA reaches back to IBM's Virtual Interface Architecture and the InfiniBand Architecture (IBA) specification, which defined a transport-independent programming model: queue pairs as the communication endpoints, memory regions as DMA-addressable windows, and a family of transport services (Reliable Connected, Unreliable Connected, Reliable Datagram, Unreliable Datagram) with precise reliability and ordering contracts [1]. Three wire protocols realize this model today: native InfiniBand, iWARP (RDMA over TCP/SCTP, specified in RFC 5040/5041/5044 [3][4]), and RoCEv2 (RDMA over Converged Ethernet, UDP/IP-encapsulated, Annex A17 of the IBA specification [1]). Despite their differences, all three share the same verbs semantics — a fact that makes the QP model, rather than any particular wire format, the true object of study.

This thesis makes four contributions. First, it gives a precise operational account of queue-pair semantics: WQE lifecycle, doorbell coalescing, signaled versus unsignaled completions, and the one-sided/two-sided dichotomy with its attendant memory-ordering subtleties. Second, it derives the DCQCN congestion-control algorithm — the de facto standard for RoCEv2 — from its three participants (congestion point, notification point, reaction point), analyzes its AIMD-style recurrence, and explains why large-scale incast defeats it. Third, it dissects RNIC microarchitecture, quantifying the costs of doorbell MMIO, QP-context cache misses, and on-NIC SRAM budgets. Fourth, it synthesizes the empirical record: FaRM's demonstration that one-sided RDMA reads sustain millions of operations per second [7], HERD's argument that two-sided verbs with RDMA writes can outperform one-sided designs [8], and RedN's proof that RNICs are Turing-complete offload engines [5].

> **Theorem (Transport Delegation):** In any kernel-bypass RDMA deployment, every mechanism traditionally owned by the OS transport — segmentation, retransmission, flow control, congestion response — must be re-implemented in RNIC hardware or firmware. The scalability of the deployment is therefore bounded above by the RNIC's on-chip state budget, not by host CPU or memory capacity.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The Verbs Model: QPs, WQEs, CQs

The fundamental abstraction is the *queue pair*: a send queue (SQ) and a receive queue (RQ) bound to a single communication endpoint. Applications post *work queue elements* (WQEs) describing operations — `SEND`, `RECV`, `RDMA_WRITE`, `RDMA_READ`, `COMPARE_AND_SWAP`, `FETCH_AND_ADD` — into these queues, then "ring the doorbell": a programmed-I/O (MMIO) write to a NIC register announcing new work. The RNIC fetches WQEs by DMA, executes them, and reports completion by DMA-writing a *completion queue element* (CQE) into a completion queue (CQ), optionally raising an interrupt or leaving the application to poll [5].

*Memory regions* (MRs) are the second pillar. Before the NIC may DMA to host memory, the application registers the region, pinning its pages and installing virtual-to-physical translations in the NIC's memory-translation and protection tables (MPT/MTT). Each MR carries an `rkey`/`lkey`; remote peers present the `rkey` to authorize one-sided access. Registration is expensive (page pinning, TLB shootdown coordination), so production systems use huge pages, on-demand paging (ODP), or pre-registered buffer pools.

### 2.2 One-Sided versus Two-Sided Verbs

*One-sided* verbs — `RDMA_WRITE`, `RDMA_READ`, atomics — execute entirely in the *remote* RNIC without involving the remote CPU. The initiator supplies the remote virtual address, `rkey`, and length; the remote NIC performs the DMA directly. This yields sub-microsecond remote-memory access and is the basis of disaggregated-memory systems such as FaRM [7]. *Two-sided* verbs (`SEND`/`RECV`) resemble sockets: the receiver must pre-post `RECV` WQEs, and the remote CPU is interrupted (or polls) to consume the message. HERD demonstrated that for skewed key–value workloads, two-sided `SEND` combined with one-sided `RDMA_WRITE` for responses can beat pure one-sided designs by avoiding the extra round trip of remote reads [8].

The ordering semantics are subtle. The IBA specification guarantees that WQEs posted to the same SQ are *initiated* in order, but completion order depends on the transport; RDMA reads and writes to the *same* QP are not guaranteed to be ordered with respect to each other unless fenced. Recent formalizations (`rdma_tso`, `rdma_sc_rmw`) model these guarantees declaratively and prove equivalence with operational models, exposing behaviors — such as the reorderability of a remote read past a later remote write — that surprise even experienced practitioners [6].

### 2.3 Transport Services

| Transport | Reliability | Ordering | Connection | Multicast | Max message |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reliable Connected (RC) | ACK/NAK, go-back-N | In-order | 1:1 QP | No | 2^31 B |
| Unreliable Connected (UC) | None | In-order (per packet) | 1:1 QP | No | MTU-bounded |
| Reliable Datagram (RD) | ACK-based | In-order | 1:N | No | 2^31 B |
| Unreliable Datagram (UD) | None | None | 1:N | Yes | MTU-bounded |
| XRC (extended RC) | ACK/NAK | In-order | 1:N shared RQ | No | 2^31 B |

RC dominates data-center deployments: its hardware retransmission and in-order delivery let applications treat the fabric as a reliable byte-stream-like channel with RDMA addressing. UD is used for connection setup, multicast, and address resolution.

### 2.4 Wire Protocols: InfiniBand, RoCEv2, iWARP

Native InfiniBand uses its own link and network layers with credit-based flow control — lossless by construction. RoCEv2 encapsulates InfiniBand transport headers in UDP/IPv4/IPv6 (UDP destination port 4791), enabling routing across L3 fabrics but inheriting Ethernet's lossy nature; losslessness must be *constructed* via PFC and DCQCN [1]. iWARP layers the RDMA protocol (RDMAP, RFC 5040 [3]) over DDP (RFC 5041 [4]) over MPA/TCP (RFC 5044), reusing TCP's congestion control at the cost of higher per-packet processing and the inability to avoid TCP's stream semantics cleanly.

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis proceeds along three axes. **(i) Specification analysis:** we ground the verbs semantics in the InfiniBand Architecture Specification Volume 1 [1] and the IETF RDMAP/DDP/MPA RFCs [3][4], cross-checked against the Linux `rdma-core` implementation of the verbs ABI. **(ii) Algorithmic derivation:** we reconstruct DCQCN from Zhu et al. [2], deriving the rate-update recurrence, the ECN-marking probability function, and the parameter regime (timer τ, byte counter B, gain g, additive-increase rate R_AI) used in production ConnectX deployments. **(iii) Empirical synthesis:** we survey measured results from FaRM [7], HERD [8], RedN [5], and DCQCN+ [9], normalizing their testbed configurations to extract scaling laws for doorbell latency, QP-context pressure, and incast collapse thresholds.

We adopt the following notation: a flow *f* has instantaneous rate *R(t)*, target rate *R_T*, current rate *R_C*; the congestion estimate α ∈ [0,1] is an EWMA of CNP arrivals; K_min, K_max are the RED marking thresholds.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Doorbell Path: From Userspace to Wire in Nanoseconds

The critical path of an RDMA operation begins in userspace. Posting a WQE is a plain memory write into a driver-mapped queue; ringing the doorbell is an MMIO write (typically 64 bytes via write-combining) to a NIC BAR register. RedN's measurements on ConnectX-5 show the doorbell MMIO itself dominating small-message latency: the PIO write traverses the PCIe root complex as a non-posted transaction, costing on the order of hundreds of nanoseconds before the NIC even fetches the WQE [5]. Production stacks mitigate this with *doorbell coalescing* (one MMIO for a batch of WQEs), *BlueFlame* (WQE written directly into the doorbell register, eliminating the DMA fetch), and unsignaled completions (suppressing CQEs for all but every *k*-th WQE to bound CQ pressure).

```rust
// Illustrative verbs posting path (pseudo-Rust over rdma-core semantics)
fn post_send(qp: &QueuePair, wr: &SendWr) {
    let wqe = encode_wqe(wr);                 // userspace: serialize into SQ buffer
    qp.sq.push(wqe);                          // plain store, no syscall
    if qp.pending_since_doorbell >= BATCH {    // doorbell coalescing
        mmio_write(qp.doorbell_reg, qp.sq.head); // PIO: the only privileged step
        qp.pending_since_doorbell = 0;
    }
}
```

The NIC-side pipeline — WQE fetch, address translation via MTT, DMA data fetch, packetization, CRC (ICRC/VCRC) — is fully pipelined in hardware; per-packet overhead is measured in tens of nanoseconds. The lesson: *software* overheads (doorbells, completions, MR registration) dominate *hardware* overheads by an order of magnitude, which is why kernel bypass matters and why every residual kernel crossing is a performance bug.

### 4.2 RC Transport: Go-Back-N in Silicon

The Reliable Connected transport implements a selective-repeat-like protocol that degrades to go-back-N under loss. Each QP maintains a packet sequence number (PSN); the receiver ACKs cumulatively and NAKs gaps. On timeout or NAK, the sender retransmits from the first unacknowledged PSN. Because the fabric is engineered lossless (PFC), retransmission is a rare slow path — but its existence shapes NIC SRAM provisioning: the sender must retain every unacknowledged packet's payload (or its DMA source) until ACKed, bounding the number of in-flight QPs by retransmission-buffer capacity.

> **Theorem (RC Buffer Bound):** For an RC QP with maximum message size *M* and bandwidth-delay product *B*, the RNIC must provision Ω(min(*M*, *B*)) bytes of retransmission state per QP. NIC SRAM is therefore the binding constraint on QP count, motivating shared-receive-queue (SRQ) and extended-RC (XRC) designs that amortize RQ state across connections.

XRC deserves emphasis: by decoupling the send QP from a shared receive queue, XRC reduces the QP-count scaling from O(N²) to O(N) in all-to-all topologies — the difference between 10,000 and 100 QPs per node at thousand-node scale.

### 4.3 DCQCN: Congestion Control as a Three-Party Protocol

DCQCN [2] adapts IEEE 802.1Qau QCN to L3-routable RoCEv2. Three roles participate:

1. **Congestion Point (CP)** — the switch. It monitors egress queue depth *q* and marks packets with ECN-CE with RED probability *p(q)*: *p* = 0 for *q* < K_min, ramping linearly to P_max at K_max (production values: K_min = 5 KB, K_max = 200 KB, P_max = 1%) [2].
2. **Notification Point (NP)** — the receiver RNIC. Upon observing a CE-marked packet, it returns a Congestion Notification Packet (CNP, RoCEv2 opcode 0x81) identifying the flow, rate-limited (one CNP per flow per ~50 µs).
3. **Reaction Point (RP)** — the sender RNIC. It maintains the rate limiter with this update discipline:

```
On CNP arrival (every CNP, not just the first):
    R_T      ← R_C                    # new target = current rate
    R_C      ← R_C * (1 - α/2)         # multiplicative decrease
    α        ← (1-g)*α + g             # increase congestion estimate
    start fast-recovery timer
On timer expiry or byte-counter B expiry (10 MB), if no CNP:
    α        ← (1-g)*α                 # decay estimate
    R_C      ← (R_C + R_T)/2           # fast recovery: halfway to target
    then R_C ← R_C + R_AI              # additive increase, R_AI ≈ 5–40 Mbps
```

The EWMA gain *g* (typically 1/256, τ = 55 µs) controls responsiveness; smaller *g* smooths but slows convergence. DCQCN's distinguishing features versus DCTCP: it needs no per-RTT ACK clocking (rate-based, not window-based), requires no destination acknowledgment to release congestion, and — crucially — does not require a truly lossless network, degrading gracefully when PFC is absent [2].

### 4.4 The Incast Pathology and PFC's Dark Side

Many-to-one incast — *N* senders converging on one receiver, as in storage replication or distributed-training all-reduce — is DCQCN's nemesis. The feedback loop has an inherent delay (mark → CNP → rate cut ≈ one RTT plus CNP rate-limiting); with hundreds of synchronized senders, queue buildup outruns the control loop, PFC fires, and PFC's head-of-line blocking spreads congestion to *victim flows* sharing the paused priority — the "parking-lot" unfairness and, in pathological topologies, PFC deadlock via cyclic buffer dependency [9]. DCQCN+ [9] addresses this by estimating congestion *scale* at the endpoints and suppressing rates more aggressively during large incasts, trading a small throughput loss for dramatically reduced buffer occupancy. The deeper lesson is architectural: PFC provides *losslessness*, DCQCN provides *fairness and low queueing*; neither alone suffices, and their interaction must be tuned so ECN marking preempts PFC activation [2].

### 4.5 RNIC Microarchitecture and the State-Scaling Ceiling

A modern RNIC (e.g., ConnectX-7/8 class) integrates: PCIe Gen5/6 DMA engines, multiple packet-processing pipelines, a QP-context cache (tens of thousands of entries backed by host-memory spillover), MPT/MTT translation caches, and a programmable match-action engine for offloads. Two scaling ceilings dominate:

- **QP-context cache misses.** Each active QP's context (~hundreds of bytes: PSN, keys, timers) must be resident for line-rate processing. When the working set exceeds the cache, contexts spill to host memory over PCIe, adding microseconds per packet. Connection-scaling techniques — XRC, DCT (dynamically connected transport), SRQ — exist precisely to shrink this working set.
- **Doorbell and completion processing.** At millions of messages per second, even userspace polling burns cores; production stacks use CQ moderation, eventfd-based sleeping for low-rate QPs, and busy-polling only on hot paths.

RedN [5] reframed the RNIC as a *compute* device: by chaining WQEs with WAIT/ENABLE ordering primitives, arbitrary programs — hash-table traversals, linked-list walks, Memcached request handling — execute entirely on the NIC, surviving even host OS crashes. This establishes RDMA offload as Turing-complete and points toward SmartNIC-resident application logic as the next scaling frontier.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

**FaRM** [7] demonstrated the one-sided thesis at scale: using RDMA reads over RC QPs, FaRM sustained ~11 Mops/s per machine on 90-node clusters with strict serializability via optimistic concurrency — proving that remote-memory access latency (~2 µs) could replace local lock-manager round trips. **HERD** [8] countered with a two-sided design: clients `SEND` requests, servers reply with `RDMA_WRITE` into client buffers, eliminating the server's need to poll receive queues per connection and achieving 26 Mops/s on 11 nodes — the key insight being that *asymmetric* verb usage (two-sided in, one-sided out) minimizes both CPU and NIC overhead.

**DCQCN** [2] was validated on Microsoft production clusters: with the parameters above, it held 40 Gbps links at >90% utilization with bounded queues, cutting PFC pause duration by orders of magnitude versus PFC-only fabrics. The failure mode appears past ~hundreds of synchronized incast flows, where DCQCN+ [9] recovers stability at a measured 3–5% utilization cost.

*Proof sketch (DCQCN fairness convergence).* Consider *n* flows sharing a bottleneck of capacity *C*, each with rate *R_i*. The RED marking probability *p(q)* is monotone in aggregate arrival rate ΣR_i. In the fluid limit, each RP's decrease is proportional to α_i ≈ p (common congestion signal), and additive increase is uniform (R_AI). This is the classic Chiu–Jain AIMD dynamic, whose fixed point satisfies R_i → C/n with Jain's fairness index → 1, provided the CNP feedback delay is small relative to 1/(g·τ) [2]. ∎

**Doorbell economics** (RedN [5], ConnectX-5): MMIO doorbell ≈ 300–500 ns; BlueFlame PIO ≈ 100–200 ns saved per WQE; unsignaled batching amortizes completion DMA to <50 ns/op. These constants, not wire speed, set the small-message ops/s ceiling.

| System | Verb pattern | Reported throughput | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| FaRM [7] | One-sided READ | ~11 Mops/s/machine | Lock-free remote reads, HTM-assisted Tx |
| HERD [8] | SEND + RDMA_WRITE | ~26 Mops/s (11 nodes) | Asymmetric verbs, no server polling |
| RedN [5] | Chained WQEs (WAIT/ENABLE) | 2–4× traversal speedup | Turing-complete NIC offload |
| DCQCN [2] | Rate-limited RC | >90% util @ 40 Gbps | ECN→CNP→AIMD hardware loop |

## 6. Limitations

First, **lossless-Ethernet fragility**: RoCEv2's correctness depends on PFC, whose pause storms, unfairness, and deadlock risk are well documented; the industry is migrating toward ECN-only (lossy-tolerant) designs, but the transition is incomplete. Second, **connection state scaling**: RC's per-QP retransmission buffers and context entries bound all-to-all topologies; DCT and XRC mitigate but complicate the programming model. Third, **security**: one-sided verbs with exposed `rkey`s create a confused-deputy surface — a compromised peer can read/write any registered region; memory keys are capabilities without revocation granularity, and IOMMU/ODP integration remains uneven. Fourth, **ordering formalisms**: the IBA memory model is specified operationally and ambiguously; the `rdma_tso`/`rdma_sc_rmw` formalizations [6] cover only fragments, and fence semantics vary across vendors. Fifth, **observability**: hardware-implemented transports are black boxes — debugging a stuck QP or a misbehaving DCQCN loop requires vendor-proprietary counters, unlike software stacks with `tcpdump`-grade introspection. Finally, **cost**: RNICs with full offload (DCT, ASAP², BlueFlame) command premiums that reintroduce the economics kernel bypass was meant to escape.

## 7. Conclusion

RDMA endures because it recognized, earlier than any competing fabric technology, that the operating system is the wrong place to implement a data-center transport. The queue-pair abstraction — with its clean separation of control (doorbells, completions) from data (DMA), its one-sided/two-sided duality, and its hardware-owned reliability — has survived three wire protocols and two decades of NIC evolution essentially unchanged. DCQCN showed that congestion control, too, could be delegated to silicon, closing the loop from ECN mark to rate cut without host involvement. The open problems are all *state* problems: QP-context scaling, incast feedback delay, key-based protection, and the observability of hardware transports. As CXL and cache-coherent fabrics encroach on RDMA's disaggregation territory from below, RDMA's response — SmartNIC-resident computation à la RedN, lossy-tolerant congestion control, and ever-richer verbs — suggests the architecture's next decade will be defined not by faster wires but by *more of the application living in the NIC*.
