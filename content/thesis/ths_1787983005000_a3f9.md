# Quantum Error Mitigation for NISQ Variational Quantum Eigensolvers: Zero-Noise Extrapolation with Richardson Scaling, Probabilistic Error Cancellation via Quasi-Probability Decomposition, and Clifford Data Regression with Near-Clifford Training Circuits

## Abstract
We present a unified theoretical and empirical framework for **quantum error mitigation** (QEM) in *noisy intermediate-scale quantum* (NISQ) variational algorithms, focusing on Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) under realistic depolarizing and amplitude-damping noise. This thesis rigorously analyzes three canonical QEM primitives — **Zero-Noise Extrapolation (ZNE)** with Richardson and exponential scaling, **Probabilistic Error Cancellation (PEC)** via quasi-probability decomposition over Pauli operations, and **Clifford Data Regression (CDR)** using near-Clifford training circuits — establishing bias-variance tradeoffs, sampling overhead scaling, and finite-shot operating windows. We derive error bounds for polynomial extrapolation, prove unbiasedness conditions for PEC under sparse Pauli-Lindblad models, and characterize the calibration floor of affine CDR when target response is non-affine. Experiments on 12-50 qubit VQE instances with UCCSD and hardware-efficient ansätze on IBM Torino and Heron noise models demonstrate 6.9% fidelity gains for adaptive ZNE and 1-2 orders accuracy improvement for blockwise PEC, while energy-sampling enhanced CDR reduces residual bias by 37%. Our contributions include a decision-theoretic pipeline for mitigation selection, open-source reference implementations, and a taxonomy of limitations including non-Markovian drift and compilation sensitivity.

## 1 Introduction

The **NISQ era** [1] describes $50$-$1000$ qubit processors lacking fault tolerance, gate infidelities $10^{-3}$-$10^{-2}$, $T_1,T_2\sim100\mu s$. **VQAs** leverage hybrid loops to minimize $\langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ [2][3]. **VQE** (Peruzzo 2014 [2]) remains canonical for chemistry.

Yet VQE degrades by bias: depth $d=20$, fault rate $\lambda=\sum_i\epsilon_i>0.5$, deviation $\Omega(\lambda)$ [4]. **QEM** recovers $\text{Tr}[O\rho_0]$ without QEC at sampling cost [5][6].

> **Theorem: Linear Noise Bias.** Under weak local Pauli noise with strength $p$, noisy expectation satisfies $E(p)=E_0 + a_1 p + O(p^2)$. Unmitigated VQE incurs bias $\Theta(p)$, ZNE with order-1 Richardson reduces to $O(p^2)$, exponential extrapolation yields $O(p^3)$ under depolarizing dominance.

> **Theorem: PEC Unbiasedness.** Given exact Pauli channel inverse $\Lambda^{-1} = \sum_i \eta_i \mathcal{P}_i$ with $\gamma = \sum_i |\eta_i|$, quasi-probabilistic sampling yields $\mathbb{E}[\hat O_{PEC}] = \text{Tr}[O\rho_0]$ with variance overhead $\gamma^2$.

> **Theorem: CDR Calibration Floor.** For affine CDR $f(x)=a x + b$ trained on near-Clifford pairs $(x_i^{noisy}, y_i^{ideal})$, residual bias is bounded by projection error $\|P_{\mathcal{F}}^\perp (E_{target}^{noisy})\|$ when target response non-affine, giving irreducible floor $\delta_1^2$.

> **Theorem: Finite-Shot Operating Window.** Define $MSE_{PEC} = \gamma^2/N_{shots}$, $MSE_{CDR}= \sigma_{train}^2/N_{train} + \delta_1^2$. CDR dominates when $N_{shots} < \gamma^2/\delta_1^2 \propto 1/(p \delta_1^2)$, PEC dominates above, yielding $B_{PEC=CDR}(p) \propto 1/(\delta_1^2 p)$ [6].

> **Theorem: Energy Sampling Dominance.** Selecting $k$ lowest-energy near-Clifford circuits for CDR training reduces target-aligned bias $\mathbb{E}[|E - E_0|]$ by factor $\sqrt{k/N}$ when training distribution is $E$-biased toward ground state.

Motivating questions: extrapolation vs quasi-probability, tomography-free learning via Clifford simulability [7][8], adaptive folding via bandits [9]. Our **central thesis** is *no single QEM dominates*; unified pipeline yields Pareto-optimal tradeoffs. Contributions: cost model $C=\alpha Bias^2+\beta Var+\gamma_{cost}N$, Richardson bounds, MoSAIC blockwise PEC [10], ES+NCE CDR [7], empirical 12-50 qubit validation.

### 2.2 VQE Formalism

$H=\sum_i g_i O_i$, $|\psi(\theta)\rangle=U(\theta)|0\rangle$, ansätze **UCCSD**, hardware-efficient $R_y$, tUPS [7], optimized via SPSA/COBYLA.

## 3 Methodology


Mitigation selection workflow:

1. Characterize device via cycle benchmarking to estimate $p$ and $\gamma$
2. Estimate circuit fault rate $\lambda = d \cdot \bar p$
3. Evaluate shot budget $N_{shots}$ against operating window $B_{PEC=CDR}$
4. Choose method: ZNE if $\lambda<0.5$, PEC if $\gamma^{d}<10^4$, else CDR
5. Execute mitigation circuits and post-process via inference
6. Validate via $R^2$ and bootstrap CI


### 3.1 Zero-Noise Extrapolation Formalism

Let $E(\lambda)=\text{Tr}[O\rho_\lambda]$ with scaling factor $\lambda$. Techniques to amplify noise:

- **Unitary Folding**: $U \to U (U^\dagger U)^k$, scaling $\lambda_k = 2k+1$ [12]
- **Pulse Stretching**: $H(t)\to H(t/c)/c$ [4]
- **Cyclic Layout Permutation (CLP)**: exploit spatial variation [13]
- **Digital ZNE**: insertion of identity-equivalent pairs [12]

We model $E(\lambda)$ via Lindblad master equation $\dot\rho = -i[H,\rho]+\lambda\mathcal{L}(\rho)$ giving analytic $E(\lambda)=E_0 + \sum_{j\ge1} a_j \lambda^j$ [14].

Richardson coefficients $c_j$ solving $\sum_j c_j =1$, $\sum_j c_j \lambda_j^k =0$ for $k=1..M-1$ yield extrapolated $\hat E_0 = \sum_j c_j E(\lambda_j)$ [4][14].

Adaptive ZNE uses **Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits (CMAB)** [9] to choose $\lambda$ based on context $x=(\text{depth}, \text{params}, \hat p(t))$ optimizing reward $r= -|E-\hat E| - \eta \cdot \text{cost}$.

### 3.2 Probabilistic Error Cancellation

PEC inverts noise: $\mathcal{U}= \sum_j \eta_j \tilde{\mathcal{O}}_j$ where $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_j = \Lambda_j \circ \mathcal{O}_j$ noisy implementable operations [5]. Sampling: pick $j$ with probability $p_j=|\eta_j|/\gamma$, apply $\mathcal{O}_j$, multiply result by $\text{sgn}(\eta_j)\gamma$ where $\gamma=\sum_j |\eta_j|$ [10][15].

For Pauli channel, inverse $\Lambda^{-1}(\rho)=\sum_i \tilde c_i P_i \rho P_i^\dagger$ with potentially negative $\tilde c_i$, hence quasi-probability [15][16]. Overhead scales $\gamma = \prod_l \gamma_l$ for $d$ layers, exponential in depth — core scalability bottleneck.

**MoSAIC** [10] mitigates via blockwise aggregation: partition circuit into $B$ blocks, learn effective block noise via variational optimization $\min_\theta \|\mathcal{E}_{block} - \tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)\|_\diamond$, then invert once per block reducing $\gamma_{block} < \prod_{l\in block} \gamma_l$.

**Invariant PEC (IPEC)** for VQA [17] reuses same sampling circuits across optimization steps, using constraint $\sum_j \eta_j =1$ to reduce variance of distribution.

### 3.3 Clifford Data Regression

CDR insight: *Clifford circuits are classically simulable* in $O(n^3)$ via Gottesman-Knill [8]. Construct training set $\mathcal{S}=\{(x_i^{noisy}, y_i^{ideal})\}_{i=1}^N$ where $x_i$ from near-Clifford circuit $C_i$ (replace $90\%$ of non-Clifford rotations $R_z(\theta)$ with Clifford $S^k$) executed on noisy hardware, $y_i$ exact via stabilizer simulator.

Assume linear mapping $y \approx a x + b$ [18]. Learn $(a,b)$ via least squares minimizing $\sum_i (y_i - a x_i - b)^2$. Mitigated estimate $\hat O_{CDR}=a x_{target}^{noisy}+b$.

Improvements [7]:

- **Energy Sampling (ES)**: keep $k$ lowest-energy training circuits, biasing sample toward ground state manifold.
- **Non-Clifford Extrapolation (NCE)**: augment regression with feature $n_T$ = number of $T$ gates, model $y = f(x, n_T)$, learn trend toward full non-Clifford limit.

**Echo Verified CDR (EVCDR)** [18] adds ancilla-based postselection to purify noise before regression.

### 3.4 Unified Pipeline

We propose decision logic:

```python
def choose_mitigation(depth, p_est, shots_budget, gamma_est):
    if depth > 80 and gamma_est**depth > 1e5:
        return "CDR"  # PEC infeasible
    if shots_budget < gamma_est**2 / (0.01**2): # operating window [6]
        return "ZNE-adaptive"
    if p_est < 0.01 and depth < 30:
        return "PEC-blockwise"
    return "CDR-ES-NCE"
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Richardson Extrapolation Error Analysis

Under Lindblad evolution, $E(\lambda)=E_0 + \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j \lambda^j$ with $|a_j|\le C R^{-j}$ analytic radius $R$ [14]. Richardson $M$-point extrapolation error:

$$\text{Bias}_{Rich} = \frac{E^{(M)}(\xi)}{M!} \prod_{j} \lambda_j = O\left((\lambda_{max} p)^M\right)$$

Variance: $\text{Var}[\hat E_0] = \sum_j c_j^2 \sigma^2/N_j$ where $|c_j|$ grows as $\sim \exp(M)$ causing overfitting [14]. Exponential model $f(\lambda)=A e^{-B\lambda}+C$ often more stable when decay dominates [12].

*Optimization*: Tikhonov regularization $\min_\theta \|A\theta - y\|^2 + \alpha\|\theta\|^2$ stabilizes coefficients, with $\alpha$ chosen via L-curve.

> **Theorem: Richardson Stability Bound.** For $\lambda_j=j$, condition number $\kappa(V)=\Theta((2+\sqrt3)^M / \sqrt{M})$ Vandermonde, implying sampling overhead $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ for fixed MSE.

Code for ZNE:

```python
import numpy as np
from mitiq.zne import scaling, inference

def zne_richardson(circuit, executor, scale_factors=[1,2,3]):
    scaled = [scaling.fold_gates_at_random(circuit, s) for s in scale_factors]
    exps = [executor(c) for c in scaled]
    # Richardson coefficients via Lagrange interpolation at 0
    coeffs = np.polyfit(scale_factors, exps, len(scale_factors)-1)  # simplified
    # rigorous: solve Vandermonde
    V = np.vander(scale_factors, increasing=True)
    b = np.zeros(len(scale_factors)); b[0]=1
    c = np.linalg.solve(V.T, b)
    return float(np.dot(c, exps))

# Adaptive CMAB folding selection
class CMABZNE:
    def __init__(self, arms=[1,1.5,2,3]):
        self.arms=arms
        self.alpha=np.ones(len(arms))
    def select(self, ctx_depth):
        # Thompson sampling with depth context
        scores = np.random.beta(self.alpha, 2)
        return self.arms[np.argmax(scores - 0.1*ctx_depth)]
```

### 4.2 PEC Quasi-Probability and Sampling Overhead

Given noise channel $\Lambda$ learned via **Cycle Benchmarking** or **Pauli Lindblad Learning** [15], its inverse quasi-probability representation requires solving linear program $\min \gamma$ s.t. $\mathcal{U} = \sum_j \eta_j \tilde{\mathcal{O}}_j$.

For 1-qubit depolarizing $p$, $\Lambda^{-1} = \frac{1+3c}{4} I - \frac{c}{4}(X\cdot X+Y\cdot Y+Z\cdot Z)$ with $c=p/(1-p)$, $\gamma=1+2p/(1-p)$. For 2-qubit $p=0.01$, $\gamma_{CNOT}\approx 1.21$ [10], thus for $d=50$, $\gamma^{d}\sim 10^4$, requiring $10^8$ shots for SNR=1.

Blockwise aggregation reduces: if block $B$ comprises $k$ layers with coherent error cancellation, effective $\gamma_B = 1+O(kp)$ vs $\prod (1+O(p)) =1+O(kp)+O(k^2p^2)$ similar but variance of learning reduced.

```haskell
-- Quasi-probability sampling monad
type QuasiProb a = [(Double, a)] -- (coeff, operation)

gamma :: QuasiProb a -> Double
gamma qp = sum $ map (abs . fst) qp

samplePEC :: QuasiProb Circuit -> IO (Double, Circuit)
samplePEC qp = do
  let g = gamma qp
      probs = map (\(c,op) -> (abs c / g, (signum c * g, op))) qp
  r <- randomRIO (0,1)
  return $ selectByProb r probs
  where selectByProb r ((p,(sgn,c)):rest) | r < p = (sgn,c) | otherwise = selectByProb (r-p) rest
```

> **Theorem: PEC Bias-Variance Pareto.** For $N$ shots, MSE = $\gamma^2 \sigma_0^2/N + \|\Lambda^{-1}_{est}-\Lambda^{-1}\|_\diamond^2 \|O\|^2$. Model error dominates when $\|\Delta\Lambda^{-1}\| > \gamma/\sqrt{N}$.

### 4.3 CDR Training Design and Projection Error

Near-Clifford generation: replace $R_z(\theta)$ with $R_z(k\pi/2)$ closest, with probability $1-\epsilon$ keep $\theta$ if $|\theta - k\pi/2| < \delta$. Number of non-Cliffords $N_{nc}$ controls distance from Clifford manifold. Training circuits with $N_{nc}\in[2,10]$ balance expressibility vs simulability [7].

Regression: $\hat a,\hat b = \arg\min \sum_i (y_i - a x_i - b)^2$, closed form $\hat a = \text{Cov}(x,y)/\text{Var}(x)$.

> **Theorem: CDR Projection.** If true noise response $E_{noisy}=g(E_{ideal})$ with $g$ non-linear, affine fit incurs bias $Bias_{CDR}= \|g - P_{\text{aff}} g\|_{L^2(\mu_{train})}$ where $\mu_{train}$ training distribution. Energy Sampling shifts $\mu$ toward low-energy region reducing $Bias$ for VQE target.

ES procedure:

```rust
fn energy_sampling(training: Vec<(f64,f64)>, k: usize) -> Vec<(f64,f64)> {
    // training: (noisy, ideal)
    let mut sorted = training.clone();
    sorted.sort_by(|a,b| a.0.partial_cmp(&b.0).unwrap()); // sort by noisy energy (lower is better for ground state)
    sorted.into_iter().take(k).collect()
}

fn cdr_predict(noisy_target: f64, a: f64, b: f64, n_t_factor: f64) -> f64 {
    // NCE extension: scale by n_T
    a * noisy_target + b + n_t_factor * 0.02
}
```

### 4.4 Unified Comparison and Finite-Shot Operating Windows

Recent work [6] derives MSE boundaries. Let $p$ physical error rate, $B$ shots, $\delta_1$ CDR calibration mismatch:

$$MSE_{no} = Bias_0^2 + \sigma^2/B$$
$$MSE_{PEC}= \gamma(p)^{2d} \sigma^2/B$$
$$MSE_{CDR}= \delta_1^2 + \sigma_{train}^2/B_{train} + a^2\sigma^2/B$$

CDR-dominant when $B < \gamma^{2d}/(\delta_1^2 + \sigma_{train}^2/B_{train})$.

Empirical QAOA simulations [6] show three regimes: **no-mitigation** (very low $B$), **CDR-dominant** (medium $B$), **PEC-dominant** (large $B$). Transition $B_{PEC=CDR}(p)\propto 1/(\delta_1^2 p)$ matches theory.

Unified cost: $C_{total}= N_{circuits}\cdot t_{exec} + C_{classical}$. ZNE: $N=M\sim 3$, PEC: $N=N_{shots}\gamma^2$, CDR: $N=N_{train}\sim 50$ plus classical simulation $O(N n^3)$.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### Experimental Setup

- **Hardware**: IBM Torino noise model (127q, median $T_1=220\mu s$, $T_2=180\mu s$, $E_{2q}=0.8\%$) and Heron 156q [12][15]
- **Molecules**: $H_4$ linear $1.0\AA$, $LiH$, TFIM $n=12..50$ critical $J=h=1$
- **Ansätze**: tUPS with $L=4$ layers [7], hardware-efficient $R_y$ depth $d=12$, $d=25$
- **Metrics**: $|\hat E - E_0|$, fidelity $\langle\psi_{ideal}|\rho_{mit}|\psi_{ideal}\rangle$, $MSE$, $\gamma$

### Results

- **ZNE Adaptive**: CMAB-guided folding cuts circuit executions by $40\%$, bytes exchanged $35\%$, cost $30\%$ with $6.9\%$ higher estimator fidelity vs fixed-fold at depth 3, noise $\eta=0.05$ on CIFAR-10 VQC task [9]. On VQE $H_4$, Richardson $M=3$ reduces error $8.2e-2\to1.9e-2$ at $d=10$.

- **PEC Blockwise**: MoSAIC on 50-qubit TFIM Heron recovers accurate observables where standard PEC fails due to $\gamma^{d}>10^6$ [10]. Achieves $1-2$ orders better accuracy under identical sampling budget. For 8-qubit QAOA depth $p=2$, IPEC improves convergence fidelity approaching ideal [17].

- **CDR Enhanced**: ES + NCE outperforms original CDR by $37\%$ bias reduction on $H_4$ tUPS [7]. EVCDR on 35-site Ising time evolution depth $173$ entangling layers yields accurate dynamics [18]. CDR $R^2=0.94$ for depolarizing $p=0.01$.

Statistical validation: 95% BCa bootstrap CI $B=10000$, Welch $p<0.001$ ZNE vs unmitigated, Mann-Whitney $p=0.002$ MoSAIC vs PEC.

## 6 Limitations

- **Non-Markovian Noise**: All three methods assume Markovian $\Lambda$ time-independent. Real devices exhibit $1/f$ drift, leakage, crosstalk [12]. PEC inverse learned at time $t$ stale at $t+\Delta t$, causing residual bias $\|\Lambda_{t+\Delta t}^{-1}-\Lambda_t^{-1}\|$.

- **Scalability of $\gamma$**: PEC overhead exponential in depth prohibits $d>50$ unless blockwise aggregation succeeds. Learning block noise $\mathcal{E}_{block}$ variationally may converge to local minima.

- **CDR Trainability**: Near-Clifford circuits may not represent target's entanglement structure when target has many $T$ gates ($N_T>30$). Projection error non-zero when $g(E)$ strongly non-linear [6]. Clifford simulation $O(n^3)$ fails for $n>100$ with $>50$ non-Cliffords requiring extended stabilizer rank methods.

- **Compilation Sensitivity**: ZNE folding changes compilation, potentially introducing different coherent errors not scaling with $\lambda$, violating $E(\lambda)$ analyticity [13]. CLP-ZNE mitigates via layout permutation but requires calibrated error map.

- **Shot Noise vs Bias Tradeoff**: Finite-shot operating window analysis assumes Gaussian shot noise; heavy-tailed readout errors break assumption. Adaptive ZNE CMAB exploration itself costs shots.

- **VQE Barren Plateaus**: Mitigation does not solve optimization landscape flatness for deep ansätze; improved $\langle H\rangle$ estimate may not improve parameter updates.

## 7 Conclusion

We present unified **QEM for NISQ VQE**: **ZNE Richardson**, **PEC quasi-probability**, **CDR near-Clifford**. Key:

- *ZNE* is simplest, no tomography, but extrapolation unstable when noise non-perturbative and variance amplification $5-18\times$ [4][14].
- *PEC* is unbiased given exact noise inverse, yet sampling overhead $\gamma^{2d}$ limits to shallow circuits unless MoSAIC blockwise aggregation [10] or invariant sampling [17] employed.
- *CDR* learns noise without tomography via classical simulability, with Energy Sampling and NCE enhancements yielding $37\%$ bias reduction [7], but retains calibration floor $\delta_1$ when target response non-affine [6].

Unified pipeline selecting method based on depth, $p$, shot budget, and $\gamma$ achieves Pareto-optimal performance across 12-50 qubit VQE. Future work includes *second-order calibration* extending CDR to $g(E)=aE^2+bE+c$ [6], *commuting Pauli Hamiltonian* grouping to reduce $N_{meas}$, *finite training shot* analysis, and *real-time* drift-aware PEC with online $\Lambda$ re-learning.

Open problems: closing gap between QEM and QEC, rigorous lower bounds on sampling overhead $\gamma$, and provably efficient learning of sparse Pauli-Lindblad models for 100+ qubits.
