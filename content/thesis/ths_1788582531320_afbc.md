# Byzantine-Resilient Dispersed Storage: Erasure-Coded Verifiable Information Dispersal, Asynchronous Atomic Broadcast, Repair Amplification Bounds, and Polynomial Commitments for Data Availability Sampling

## Abstract

*Byzantine-resilient dispersed storage seeks to guarantee that a large data object remains retrievable in an asynchronous network where up to one third of the storage nodes are adversarial, without replicating the object at every node.* This thesis unifies four lines of work that together constitute the modern answer to that problem: asynchronous verifiable information dispersal (AVID) in the formulation of Cachin and Tessaro [1], erasure-coded atomic broadcast as exemplified by HoneyBadgerBFT [2] and refined by DispersedLedger [3], the theory of repair amplification for Reed–Solomon coded fragments, and KZG polynomial commitments as deployed for data availability sampling (DAS) in Ethereum's EIP-4844 proto-danksharding design [4]. We reconstruct the AVID dispersal and retrieval protocols, derive the fundamental communication lower bounds of Alhaddad et al. [5], and prove a repair-amplification trade-off showing that regenerating-code variants of AVID reduce node-recovery cost from Θ(|M|) to O(|M|/k · d/(d−k+1)) at the expense of dispersal-time storage blowup. We then show how DispersedLedger's separation of consensus on block *headers* from retrieval of dispersed *payloads* generalizes the AVID abstraction to ledger systems, and how KZG commitments convert per-fragment authentication metadata from O(n) hashes to a single constant-size commitment, enabling light-client sampling with O(1) verification per sample. A complexity comparison across nine protocols and a parameterized cost model are presented, followed by an empirical evaluation methodology and open problems in post-quantum and high-churn regimes.

## 1. Introduction

The problem of *information dispersal* was posed by Rabin [6] as a storage-efficient alternative to full replication: split a message *M* into *n* fragments such that any *k < n* of them suffice to reconstruct *M*. In adversarial settings — permissionless networks, cloud-of-clouds storage, and rollup data-availability layers — the model must be strengthened against *Byzantine* participants. Cachin and Tessaro [1] formalized this as **asynchronous verifiable information dispersal (AVID)**: a protocol by which a (possibly faulty) client disperses *M* among *n* servers, up to *t < n/3* of them Byzantine, in a fully asynchronous network, such that honest dispersal terminates everywhere, termination at one honest server implies termination at all, and any client can eventually retrieve a consistent value.

AVID's internal machinery — erasure coding paired with a Bracha-style three-phase agreement on a *fingerprint* of the encoding — anticipated modern asynchronous BFT. HoneyBadgerBFT [2] built the first practical asynchronous atomic broadcast by running *n* parallel Cachin–Tessaro-style reliable broadcasts as the proposal phase of an asynchronous common subset protocol. DispersedLedger [3] inverted the relationship: it disperses payloads via Merkle-based AVID and orders only short block *headers*, decoupling bandwidth-heavy downloads from consensus progress. In parallel, Ethereum's danksharding roadmap replaced Merkle-based per-fragment authentication with **KZG polynomial commitments**, exploiting their *linearity* and constant-size opening proofs to make **data availability sampling** feasible for light clients [4].

This thesis makes four contributions: (1) a unified reconstruction of AVID, AVID-H, AVID-FP, and AVID-M along the fingerprint-compression axis (§4.1); (2) a repair-amplification analysis proving naive AVID recovery costs Θ(|M|) per failed node and characterizing the MSR-code frontier that reduces it (§4.3); (3) a consensus-integration framework showing how DispersedLedger's header/payload separation and HoneyBadgerBFT's ACS both instantiate AVID's "agree on the fingerprint, disperse the fragments" pattern at ledger scale (§4.2); and (4) a commitment-scheme comparison quantifying how KZG achieves O(1) per-fragment proofs and enables two-dimensional DAS (§4.4, §5).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 System Model and the AVID Specification

We consider *n* storage nodes and an unbounded set of clients communicating over point-to-point authenticated channels in an *asynchronous* network: messages are eventually delivered but with no timing bound, and the adversary may reorder or delay them arbitrarily. Up to *t < n/3* nodes may be Byzantine, controlled by a static adversary; the dispersing client may also be Byzantine [1]. Following the rephrasing in [7], AVID is specified by four properties for parameters ℓ ≤ *n − t*:

- **Termination.** If an honest client disperses *m*, all honest nodes eventually complete the dispersal.
- **Agreement.** If some honest node completes the dispersal of *m*, all honest nodes eventually do.
- **Availability.** If ℓ honest nodes completed the dispersal, any client can eventually retrieve *some* value.
- **Correctness.** Retrieved values are consistent across clients; if the writer was honest, the retrieved value equals *m*.

The bound *t < n/3* is optimal for the agreement-flavored properties in asynchrony, inherited from the reliable-broadcast core [1].

### 2.2 Erasure Coding: Reed–Solomon Fundamentals

A *(k, n)* Reed–Solomon code over a finite field **F**q encodes *k* data symbols into *n* code symbols via evaluation of a degree-*< k* polynomial at *n* distinct points; any *k* symbols suffice for interpolation-based decoding. The classical choice *k = t + 1* guarantees that among any *k* fragments used for reconstruction, enough originate from honest nodes that a Byzantine writer cannot force disagreement — but it fixes per-node storage at |*M*|/(*t*+1), i.e., storage blowup ≈ *n*/(*t*+1) → 3 as *n* grows [1].

### 2.3 From Reliable Broadcast to Atomic Broadcast

Bracha's reliable broadcast achieves agreement through *send / echo / ready* phases with *n − t* and *2t + 1* quorum thresholds. Cachin and Tessaro's key insight [1] was to run this agreement **only on a short fingerprint** of the erasure-coded fragments while the fragments themselves travel point-to-point. HoneyBadgerBFT [2] lifted the same idea to consensus: *n* parallel RBC instances carry transaction batches as proposals, and *n* binary agreement instances select a common subset — asynchronous common subset (ACS) — with no timing assumptions. Dumbo [8] later reduced the ABA instances from *n* to O(1) via multi-valued validated Byzantine agreement.

### 2.4 Polynomial Commitments and KZG

A polynomial commitment scheme lets a prover commit to a polynomial *P* with a short commitment *C*, then open *P(z) = y* with a proof π verifiable against *C* alone. The KZG scheme [9] computes *C = Σ bᵢ[τⁱ]₁* in a pairing-friendly group from a structured reference string produced by a Powers-of-Tau ceremony. Its decisive properties for dispersal are: **constant-size** commitments and opening proofs; **commitment linearity**, enabling two-dimensional erasure coding where columns of a blob matrix are themselves low-degree [4]; and a degree bound guaranteeing that any *k* cells of a row reconstruct the row. These properties made KZG the commitment of choice for EIP-4844 blobs and the planned substrate for full DAS [4].

## 3. Methodology

Our method is analytic and comparative, proceeding in four stages.

**Stage 1 — Protocol reconstruction.** We re-derive AVID, AVID-H, AVID-FP, and AVID-M from their primary sources [1][5][7], normalizing all cost statements to the four axes (dispersal, storage, retrieval, recovery).

**Stage 2 — Lower-bound analysis.** We present the Ω(|*M*| + *n²*) dispersal lower bound for deterministic AVID [5] and the *t < n/2* impossibility bound for simultaneous availability and correctness [7].

**Stage 3 — Repair-amplification calculus.** We model node recovery as a distinct phase: replacing a failed node *v* with *v′* holding an equivalent fragment. We quantify the *repair amplification* *A* = (bytes transferred during repair)/(fragment size), and evaluate regenerating-code variants on the storage–bandwidth trade-off curve.

**Stage 4 — Commitment-scheme substitution.** We compare hash-vector, Merkle-tree, and KZG authentication for the fingerprint *D*, and analyze the DAS soundness that KZG linearity buys: a light client sampling *s* random cells of a 2D-encoded blob catches unavailability with probability 1 − 2^(−s) under the low-degree guarantee [4].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The AVID Protocol Family: Dispersal, Fingerprints, and Retrieval

**Dispersal (Cachin–Tessaro AVID).** The client encodes *M* into fragments *f₁…fₙ* with a *(t+1, n)* erasure code and computes the fingerprint vector *D = [H(f₁), …, H(fₙ)]*. It sends *(fᵢ, D)* to each node *i*. Nodes then execute a Bracha-style exchange *on D*: upon *n − t* matching echoes on *D*, a node multicasts *ready(D)*; upon *t + 1* readies it amplifies; upon *2t + 1* readies on *D*, it *completes*, storing *(fᵢ, D)*. The crucial economy: agreement is reached on the O(*n*)-hash vector *D*, not on *M*, while each node stores only its own fragment plus *D* [1].

> **Theorem (AVID correctness [1]):** In an asynchronous network with *t < n/3* Byzantine nodes, the protocol above satisfies Termination, Agreement, Availability (with ℓ = *n − t*), and Correctness. The dispersal communication is O(*n*|*M*|) and per-node storage is |*M*|/(*t*+1) + O(*nλ*) for hash size λ.

**The fingerprint-compression axis.** Every subsequent variant preserves the dispersal skeleton and compresses *D* or the echoed payload:

- **AVID-H** replaces *D* with a Merkle root over the fragments; each node stores its fragment plus an O(log *n*)-size proof. Storage blowup stays ≈ 3 but per-node metadata drops from O(*n*) to O(log *n*) [1].
- **AVID-FP** (fingerprint) has nodes echo only *fingerprints* rather than full fragments, cutting dispersal communication; **AVID-M** further compresses fingerprints with Merkle trees [7].
- **Polynomial-commitment AVID** replaces *D* with a single KZG commitment to the polynomial whose evaluations are the fragments; per-fragment authentication becomes one constant-size opening proof, and dispersal communication asymptotically meets the lower bound [5].

| Protocol | Dispersal | Storage (total) | Retrieval | Recovery | Auth. metadata/node |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full replication | O(*n*\|*M*\|) | *n*\|*M*\| | \|*M*\| | \|*M*\| | — |
| AVID [1] | O(*n*\|*M*\|) | ≈ 3\|*M*\| | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | **Θ(\|*M*\|)** | O(*n*) hashes |
| AVID-H [1] | O(*n*\|*M*\|) | ≈ 3\|*M*\| | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | **Θ(\|*M*\|)** | O(log *n*) |
| AVID-FP/M [7] | O(\|*M*\| + *n²λ*) | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | **Θ(\|*M*\|)** | O(1)/O(log *n*) |
| KZG-AVID [5] | O(\|*M*\| + *n²λ*) | ≈ 3\|*M*\| | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | **Θ(\|*M*\|)** | O(1) |
| eAVID (pruning) | O(*n*\|*M*\|) | → \|*M*\|·*n*/(*n−t*) | ≈ 1.5\|*M*\| | Θ(\|*M*\|) | O(log *n*) |

The bold column is the scandal of the family: **recovery always costs Θ(|M|)** — the subject of §4.3.

**Retrieval.** A retrieving client queries nodes for *(fᵢ, proofᵢ)*, verifies each fragment against the agreed fingerprint, and decodes after collecting *k = t+1* valid fragments. Because at most *t* fragments may be corrupt and *n − t ≥ 2t + 1* honest nodes respond, *k* valid fragments are always obtainable [1].

```python
def avid_retrieve(nodes, fingerprint, k, decode):
    """Client-side retrieval against an agreed fingerprint."""
    valid = []
    for node in nodes:
        frag, proof = node.serve_fragment()
        if verify(frag, proof, fingerprint):
            valid.append(frag)
        if len(valid) == k:
            break
    assert len(valid) == k, "availability violated"
    return decode(valid)
```

### 4.2 From Dispersal to Ledgers: HoneyBadgerBFT and DispersedLedger

**HoneyBadgerBFT: AVID inside consensus.** HoneyBadgerBFT [2] is usefully read as *n* interleaved AVID dispersals feeding an ACS. Each node *i* proposes a batch *Bᵢ*; the proposal phase runs a Cachin–Tessaro-style RBC with erasure coding — nodes echo fragments and agree on the batch digest. Then *n* binary agreement instances decide *which* proposals to include, outputting a common subset of batches. The erasure coding inside RBC is exactly the AVID trick: agree on short digests, move bulk data point-to-point [2]. The cost is the *n* ABA instances — the bottleneck Dumbo [8] attacks with a constant number of MVBA instances, achieving multi-fold latency and throughput gains.

**DispersedLedger: consensus on headers, retrieval of payloads.** DispersedLedger [3] reverses the embedding: it splits each block into a small *header* (ordered by an ACS-like protocol) and a large *payload* dispersed via Merkle-based AVID. Consensus terminates on headers alone; payload retrieval is asynchronous and per-node-paced. *Inter-block linking* chains headers so that a header implicitly certifies the availability of its payload's dispersal. The evaluation on geo-distributed EC2 shows **2× the throughput and 74% lower latency** than HoneyBadgerBFT under variable bandwidth [3].

### 4.3 Repair Amplification: The Hidden Tax of Erasure-Coded Dispersal

Classical AVID specifications [1] say nothing about *node replacement*: when a storage node fails permanently, a fresh node *v′* must acquire the fragment *fᵥ* that *v* held. The naive recovery — retrieve *M* (Θ(|M|) download), re-encode, keep one fragment — has **repair amplification** *A = Θ(k)*: the network moves *k* times the data the new node will store. For *k = t+1 = Θ(n)*, replacing a single node costs as much as the entire dispersal.

*Regenerating codes* trade storage overhead for repair bandwidth. A minimum-storage regenerating (MSR) code lets *v′* contact *d > k* surviving nodes, downloading *β < |f|* symbols from each, for total repair bandwidth *γ = d·β* [7]. With *n = 3t+1*, *k = t+1*, *d = n−1 = 3t*: *A = 3t/(2t) = 1.5* — repairing a node costs only 1.5× its fragment. But MSR codes complicate *verification*: helper symbols are linear combinations, not raw fragments, so the fingerprint must authenticate linear subspaces (e.g., via homomorphic hashes or KZG), and Byzantine helpers can poison repair with invalid combinations. Recent AVID constructions [7] therefore pair MSR-style recovery with *detect-and-reject* remedies: helpers attach proofs, and the recovering node cross-checks against the committed fingerprint.

A complementary mitigation is *post-dissemination pruning* (eAVID): once more than *k* honest nodes acknowledge, nodes shed redundant fragments, driving long-term storage toward the optimum *n*/(*n−t*)·|*M*| [5].

### 4.4 KZG Commitments and Data Availability Sampling

Merkle-based AVID authenticates each fragment with an O(log *n*)-size path against a root. KZG polynomial commitments [9] collapse this to O(1): the disperser commits to the polynomial *P* with *P(i) = fᵢ*, publishes one 48-byte commitment *C*, and each node stores *(fᵢ, πᵢ)* with a 48-byte opening proof, verified by a single pairing check.

Three consequences matter for dispersed storage at scale:

1. **Fingerprint size becomes constant.** Dispersal messages carry one commitment instead of *n* hashes [5].
2. **Two-dimensional encoding enables DAS.** Arrange blob data in a *2k × 2k* matrix where every row *and* column is a degree-*< k* Reed–Solomon codeword (possible by KZG linearity [4]). A light client sampling *s* random cells, each with a KZG opening proof, is assured: if fewer than *(k+1)²* cells were published, the samples catch it with probability ≥ 1 − 2^(−*s*). The low-degree guarantee — "any *k* cells of a row reconstruct the row" — is exactly what KZG's degree bound provides for free, and what Merkle trees cannot express [4].
3. **The price is trust and curves.** KZG needs a Powers-of-Tau SRS: a single toxic-waste leak lets an adversary forge openings and break availability soundness. Verification needs pairings (BLS12-381 in EIP-4844), and the scheme is *not* post-quantum — a live limitation (§6).

EIP-4844 instantiates this design in production: each blob is a degree-4095 polynomial, the execution layer stores only the 32-byte *versioned hash*, and the point-evaluation precompile lets any contract verify a claimed evaluation — the on-chain anchor for sampling challenges [4]. Blobs persist ~18 days on the consensus layer: a deliberate *expiry*, trading permanent retrievability (the AVID guarantee) for bounded validator storage.

† amortized over epochs via pipelining; ‡ consensus rounds on headers; § gossip propagation of blob sidecars.

**Lower bounds.** Alhaddad et al. [5] prove that any *deterministic* AVID has dispersal Ω(|*M*| + *n²*) and retrieval Ω(|*M*| + *nλ*): the *n²* term is the irreducible cost of Byzantine agreement on the fingerprint, and the fingerprint itself must be Ω(*n*) bits unless authentication is algebraic (KZG) rather than combinatorial. KZG-AVID meets both bounds up to the security parameter — it is *near-optimal*. A second bound [7]: no scheme achieves availability *and* correctness with *t ≥ n/2*.

### 5.2 Parameterized Cost Model

For a deployment with *n = 3t + 1* nodes and fragment size *s = |M|/k*, the per-epoch cost decomposes as dispersal Θ(*n*|*M*|) for naive echo, repair |*M*| naive versus *s·d/(d−k+1)* for MSR, and retrieval ≈ 1.5|*M*|.

With *t = 33, n = 100, k = 34, |M| = 1 GiB*: naive AVID stores ≈ 3 GiB network-wide, disperses ≈ 100 GiB, and each node failure costs 1 GiB of repair traffic. MSR-AVID with *d = 99* cuts repair to ≈ 45 MiB — a **22× reduction**. These magnitudes explain why production data-availability systems pair erasure coding with *sampling* rather than full retrieval: a light client checking 75 random cells downloads kilobytes, not gigabytes, for 2^(−75) soundness error [4].

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Near-Optimal Dispersal

We sketch the [5] lower-bound argument. *Agreement necessity:* agreeing on the fingerprint in asynchrony with *t* Byzantine faults needs Ω(*n²*) messages (Dolev–Reischuk). *Data necessity:* *n − t* honest nodes must collectively hold enough information to reconstruct |*M*| bits even if the *t* Byzantine fragments are garbage — a cut argument giving Ω(|*M*|) bits crossing from the client into the honest set. ∎

## 6. Limitations

1. **Trusted setup for KZG.** The entire DAS soundness argument rests on SRS integrity. Multi-thousand-participant ceremonies make subversion implausible but not impossible, and the assumption is qualitatively stronger than the hash-only assumptions of AVID-H.
2. **No post-quantum security.** KZG relies on pairing-friendly elliptic curves, broken by Shor's algorithm. A quantum adversary forges opening proofs and shatters availability.
3. **The *t < n/3* ceiling in asynchrony.** Deterministic asynchronous AVID cannot exceed *n/3* faults; randomized protocols can approach *n/2* but add expected-round complexity and common-coin setup.
4. **Repair–verification tension.** MSR repair helpers send linear combinations, so per-fragment fingerprints no longer suffice; homomorphic authentication reintroduces per-helper proof overhead and new Byzantine poisoning vectors during repair itself.
5. **Expiry vs. permanence.** EIP-4844's ~18-day blob pruning abandons AVID's permanent-availability guarantee for validator economics. Rollups must re-anchor data elsewhere — the "permanent" in AVID is a protocol property, while in production it is an *incentive* property delegated to archival actors.
6. **Churn and long-lived deployments.** AVID analyses assume a static node set; permissionless networks have continuous churn. Re-encoding the fragment layout on membership change is O(|*M*|) per epoch unless proactive resharing is added — an open composition problem.

## 7. Conclusion

Four ideas, developed over two decades, converge on a single architecture for Byzantine-tolerant dispersed storage. **AVID** [1] taught us to separate agreement on *fingerprints* from movement of *fragments*, achieving storage blowup ≈ 3 with no timing assumptions. **HoneyBadgerBFT** [2] and **DispersedLedger** [3] showed the same separation scales to consensus: order short headers, disperse large payloads, retrieve lazily — with measured 2× throughput gains on variable-bandwidth networks. **Repair-amplification analysis** exposes the Θ(|*M*|) recovery tax hidden in every classical AVID and the MSR-code frontier that reduces it to ~1.5 fragment sizes. **KZG polynomial commitments** [4][9] compress authentication metadata to O(1) and, through linearity, make data availability sampling practical — the mechanism by which Ethereum's danksharding roadmap hopes to scale blob throughput without scaling validator hardware.

The open frontier is compositional: an AVID that simultaneously achieves near-optimal dispersal [5], MSR-efficient repair [7], post-quantum transparent setup, and DAS-compatible commitments does not yet exist. Each axis is individually near-optimal; their combination remains the central challenge for the data-availability layers of the next decade.
