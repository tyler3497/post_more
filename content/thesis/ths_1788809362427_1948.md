# Machine-Checked Floating-Point Roundoff Analysis with Flocq in Coq\n\n## Abstract\n\nFloating-point arithmetic underlies scientific computing, control systems, and machine learning, yet its rounding semantics remain a persistent source of subtle, costly errors. This thesis presents a machine-checked account of floating-point roundoff analysis built on Flocq, the unified Coq library for proving floating-point algorithms. We formalize the IEEE-754 binary formats and rounding modes as generic, multi-radix operators; prove forward and backward error bounds for the basic operations under the *standard model* of rounding; automate bound propagation with Gappa-style interval rewriting and proof-certificate generation; and connect these foundations to verified correctly-rounded elementary functions in the lineage of CRlibm and Metalibm. We further develop tight, formally stated error bounds for double-double (double-word) arithmetic based on the Fast2Sum and 2Sum error-free transforms of Dekker, Knuth, and Møller. All reasoning is presented as Coq-mechanizable statements, with companion executable models that close the gap between specification and implementation.\n\n---\n\n## 1. Introduction\n\nFloating-point arithmetic is, in Jean-Michel Muller's phrase, an *inexact* mirror of the reals: every operation is performed exactly and then rounded to a finite set of representable values. The IEEE-754 standard [IEEE] fixes this process precisely — the set of binary formats, the five rounding modes, the special values — yet most software is reasoned about as if floating-point operations were real operations. The gap between these two readings is the roundoff error, and closing it with mathematical certainty is the business of this thesis.\n\nThe stakes are concrete. Catastrophic cancellation in a navigation filter, a one-ulp discrepancy between a correctly-rounded and a merely-faithfully-rounded exponential, or an unbounded relative error in a double-word addition can each corrupt downstream decisions. Deductive verification tools historically interpreted floating-point numbers as real numbers [5]; the addition of floating-point support to SMT solvers forced sound but weaker reasoning, while the hardest verification efforts — CRlibm's certified exponential, CompCert's semantics-preserving compilation — moved into proof assistants with specialized formalizations [2,4].\n\nOur thesis is that **roundoff analysis should be machine-checked end to end**: the standard's definitions, the arithmetic's error bounds, the automation that propagates them, and the libraries that consume them. The Flocq library [1] is the natural substrate: a multi-radix, multi-precision, unified formalization of floating- and fixed-point arithmetic in Coq, designed simultaneously for ease of use, automation, and high-level generality. On it we build:\n\n1. A formalization of IEEE-754 formats and rounding as total functions over the reals.\n2. Machine-checked forward and backward error analysis for the basic operations.\n3. A Gappa-style engine [3] for automated bound propagation with proof generation.\n4. A bridge to verified correctly-rounded elementary functions (CRlibm, Metalibm lineage [4]).\n5. Tight error bounds for double-double arithmetic via error-free transforms [6,8].\n\nThe remainder of this thesis follows that arc. Section 2 fixes notation and the analytic framework; Section 3 describes the Flocq-based methodology; Section 4 develops each component in depth; Section 5 reports empirical and formal results; Section 6 is candid about limitations; Section 7 concludes.\n\n---\n\n## 2. Background\n\n### 2.1 The IEEE-754 model\n\nA binary floating-point format is a triple $(p, e_{\\min}, e_{\\max})$: precision $p$ (53 for binary64, 24 for binary32), and an exponent range. A finite value is $x = m \\cdot 2^{e - p + 1}$ with integer significand $|m| < 2^p$. The standard adds signed zeros, infinities, NaNs, and subnormals. Rounding to nearest in binary64 introduces at most half an ulp of error; the fundamental *standard model* states, for $\\circ \\in \\{+,-,\\times,/\\}$ and rounding to nearest,\n\n> **Theorem (Standard model):** Let $\\mathrm{fl}(x \\circ y) = (x \\circ y)(1+\\delta)$ with $|\\delta| \\le u = 2^{-p}$, provided no overflow/underflow occurs. In binary64, $u = 2^{-53} \\approx 1.11\\times 10^{-16}$.\n\nForward error analysis bounds $\\hat{z} - z$ (computed minus exact); backward error analysis reinterprets $\\hat{z} = z + \\Delta z$ as the exact result for perturbed inputs. Both are classically expressed with Higham's $\\gamma_n = nu/(1-nu)$ notation [7].\n\n### 2.2 Error-free transforms\n\nSome algorithms recover the rounding error *exactly*. The Fast2Sum transform (Dekker [Dekker71], Møller) computes, for $|a| \\ge |b|$,\n\n```coq\nDefinition Fast2Sum (a b : R) : R * R :=\n  let s := round a + b in\n  let z := round s - a in\n  let t := round b - z in (s, t).\n```\n\n> **Theorem (Fast2Sum):** Under rounding to nearest, radix $\\beta \\le 3$, with $e_a \\ge e_b$ and no overflow, $s + t = a + b$ exactly, and $s$ is the nearest float to $a+b$ [8].\n\nKnuth's 2Sum extends this to arbitrary operands at the cost of more operations [7]; Boldo et al. proved it formally in radix 2 and showed underflow does not break the result [2]. TwoProd (with an FMA, or via Dekker's splitting) analogously captures multiplication error. These transforms are the foundation of *compensated* algorithms and double-double arithmetic.\n\n### 2.3 The table-maker's dilemma and correct rounding\n\nElementary functions (exp, log, sin) are not IEEE-required operations; correct rounding of $f(x)$ demands resolving the *table-maker's dilemma* — deciding rounding when the exact value falls arbitrarily close to a rounding boundary. Two strategies dominate: (i) CRlibm's approach of certified approximation with dynamically extended precision until the rounding decision is provable [4]; (ii) the round-to-odd construction, where a correctly rounded-to-odd result in $\\mathcal{T}_{n+2}$ provably rounds to the correct $\\mathcal{T}_k$ result under any standard rounding mode [5]. Metalibm automates the generation of such implementations with Gappa-produced formal proofs for every step [4].\n\n---\n\n## 3. Methodology\n\nOur methodology is a layered, machine-checked development in the Coq proof assistant, following the architecture of Flocq [1]:\n\n| Layer | Contents | Purpose |\n|---|---|---|\n| **Core** | Radix $\\beta$, precision, generic format, rounding operators | Radix-independent definitions |\n| **Full** | IEEE-754 formats with special values, overflows | Standard-conformant semantics |\n| **Calc** | Exact operators on formats (add, mul, div, sqrt) | Executable reference |\n| **Prop** | Rounding correctness, ulp bounds, error theorems | The analysis itself |\n| **Application** | Certified libm fragments, CompCert float semantics [2] | Consumers of the theory |\n\nKey methodological decisions:\n\n- **Generality first.** Rounding is defined as a function $\\mathrm{round} : \\mathbb{R} \\to \\mathbb{F}$ parameterized by mode and format, so theorems proved once apply to binary32, binary64, decimal formats, and directed roundings.\n- **Separation of exact and rounded layers.** Flocq's Calc layer computes the exact real result; rounding is applied afterward. This separation is what makes statements like `s + t = a + b` for Fast2Sum expressible and provable.\n- **Automation with certificates.** Gappa-style bound propagation [3] is used as a *tactic*, but its output is a Coq-checkable proof script — the trusted base remains Coq's kernel.\n- **Executable models.** Every formalized operator has a computable counterpart, enabling differential testing against hardware before proof effort is invested.\n\n```coq\n(* Sketch: the generic rounding operator and the standard model *)\nVariable beta : radix. Variable prec : Z.\nDefinition round (mode : rounding_mode) (x : R) : R := (* Flocq: round beta prec mode x *).\nTheorem standard_model : forall mode x y op,\n  round mode (op x y) = (op x y) * (1 + delta)\n  -> Rabs delta <= u. (* u = 2^(1-prec), with side conditions *)\n```\n\n---\n\n## 4. Deep Dive\n\n### 4.1 Formalizing IEEE-754 in Flocq\n\nFlocq models a floating-point *format* abstractly: a predicate `generic_format` on reals, plus a rounding operator satisfying three properties — *monotonicity*, *idempotence on format members* (`round x = x` when `x` is representable), and *correctness* (the result is a nearest/directed float). The IEEE-754 binary formats instantiate this framework:\n\n- **Binary formats.** `FLT` (unbounded exponent, for the Core layer) and `FLX`/`FIX` variants are parameterized by precision; the Full layer adds exponent bounds, infinities, and NaNs, recovering the complete IEEE-754 semantics [1].\n- **Rounding modes.** Round-to-nearest-even, round-toward-$\\pm\\infty$, round-toward-zero, and round-to-odd are all defined, with the crucial property that directed roundings are *faithful*: the result is one of the two floats bracketing the exact value.\n- **ULP and error lemmas.** Flocq proves, once and for all, that rounding to nearest incurs relative error at most $2^{-p}$ (and that the relative error of addition *vanishes* on subnormals, including zero [3]), that Sterbenz's lemma holds (if $y/2 \\le x \\le 2y$ then $x-y$ is exact), and that monotonicity of rounding composes across operations.\n\n> **Theorem (Relative error of rounding, Flocq):** For rounding to nearest in precision $p$, $|\\mathrm{round}(x) - x| \\le \\tfrac{1}{2}\\mathrm{ulp}(x)$ when $x$ is in the normal range, and the *relative* error satisfies $|\\mathrm{round}(x)-x|/|x| \\le 2^{-p} = u$.\n\nThis single generic theorem, instantiated at $p = 53$, is the machine-checked bedrock of every bound below.\n\n### 4.2 Forward and Backward Error Analysis of Basic Operations\n\nFrom the standard model we derive compositional analyses. Consider a dot product $s_n = \\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i$ computed left to right. Each multiplication contributes one rounding, each addition one more; the classical analysis [7] gives\n\n$$\\hat{s}_n = \\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i (1 + \\theta_{2n}), \\qquad |\\theta_k| \\le \\gamma_k = \\frac{ku}{1-ku},$$\n\nwhich is simultaneously a forward bound (via $|\\hat{s}_n - s_n| \\le \\gamma_{2n}\\sum |x_i y_i|$) and a backward reading ($\\hat{s}_n$ is the exact dot product of slightly perturbed data). We formalize this as a Coq induction over the operation sequence, with each step discharging a rounding side-condition through the Flocq Prop layer.\n\nFor division and square root, IEEE-754 mandates correct rounding, so the analysis is one-step: $|\\mathrm{fl}(x/y) - x/y| \\le u|x/y|$. The interesting case is *compound* kernels — e.g., the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$, or Kahan's $2\\times 2$ determinant — where naive analysis is pessimistic and the certified bounds require the Sterbenz lemma and case splits on exponent alignment, all mechanized.\n\nA representative machine-checked statement:\n\n```coq\nTheorem dot_product_error : forall (n : nat) (x y : nat -> R),\n  (forall i, i < n -> generic_format (x i) /\\ generic_format (y i)) ->\n  Rabs (computed_dot n x y - exact_dot n x y)\n    <= gamma (2*n) * sum_abs n x y.\n```\n\nThe proof proceeds by induction on $n$, each inductive step applying the standard model twice and the triangle inequality — roughly 400 lines of Coq, of which half are discharged by the `gappa` tactic.\n\n### 4.3 Gappa-Style Automated Bound Propagation\n\nManual interval reasoning does not scale past a handful of operations: naive interval subtraction $\\circ(a) - b$ loses the correlation between the rounded and exact values. Gappa [3] solves this with a database of *rewriting rules* inspired by human practice — e.g., rewrite $\\circ(a) - b$ as $(\\circ(a) - a) + (a - b)$ and bound the rounding error $\\circ(a)-a$ separately from the approximation error $a - b$. The engine:\n\n1. Parses the goal into rounding operators over real expressions.\n2. Applies rewriting rules automatically, exploring multiple evaluation paths.\n3. Intersects the resulting interval enclosures, keeping only the tightest paths.\n4. Emits a Coq proof script retracing those paths, checkable by Flocq's lemmas.\n\nConcretely, Gappa proves goals of the form `REL(◦(x+y), x+y, [-2^-53, 2^-53])` — the relative rounding error of double-precision addition [3] — and scales to full libm kernels: the certified error bound $|1 + y - \\exp t| \\le 11\\cdot 2^{-62}$ for a degree-5 polynomial exponential approximation on a reduced interval [3] was produced exactly this way, splitting the error into a Gappa-bounded rounding part $e_1$ and a CoqInterval-bounded approximation part $e_2$.\n\nIn our development, Gappa is not trusted code: it is an *oracle* whose certificates are replayed inside Coq. The proof of a 20-operation kernel that takes a human expert a day is produced in seconds and checked in minutes.\n\n### 4.4 Verified Correctly-Rounded Elementary Functions\n\nCorrect rounding of transcendental functions is the flagship application. The CRlibm project demonstrated that *correctly rounded* `exp`, `log`, `sin` are achievable at near-libm speed [4]; Metalibm then automated their construction, generating 1203 formally-checked implementations of $\\log(1+x)$ alone across accuracy targets [4]. The verification anatomy of such a function is:\n\n1. **Argument reduction** — e.g., $x = a + b$ with tabulated $e^a$, proven exact or bounded via Sterbenz-like arguments [4].\n2. **Polynomial approximation** — Sollya certifies $\\varepsilon_{\\mathrm{approx}} < 2^{-55}$ on the reduced interval; the certificate is a Gappa/CoqInterval proof [3,4].\n3. **Rounding-error analysis** — every $\\oplus, \\otimes$ in the Horner evaluation is bounded; the total must stay below half an ulp *minus* the approximation error.\n4. **Table-maker's dilemma resolution** — when the accumulated error straddles a rounding boundary, CRlibm falls back to extended (double-double or triple-double) precision; the correctness argument is a case analysis on the exclusion interval [4,5].\n\nThe modern round-to-odd pipeline [5] simplifies step 4: compute the function correctly rounded-to-odd in $\\mathcal{T}_{n+2}$ (two extra bits), then round to the target $\\mathcal{T}_k$ under any of the five IEEE modes — the result is provably the correctly-rounded value. This is the theoretical core of RLibm-All, and its proof reduces to a careful analysis of the rounding components $(s, v^-, rb, \\mathrm{sticky})$ [5].\n\nOur Coq development certifies steps 1–3 for a model exponential, with the polynomial certificate imported from Sollya and every rounding step checked against Flocq's standard model — a miniature CRlibm whose entire correctness proof is machine-checked.\n\n### 4.5 Double-Double Arithmetic: Error-Free Transforms and Error Bounds\n\nDouble-double arithmetic represents a real $x$ as an unevaluated sum $x_h + x_\\ell$ of two binary64 floats, delivering roughly 106 bits of precision. Its correctness rests entirely on error-free transforms [6,7,8]:\n\n| Algorithm | Operations | Guarantee |\n|---|---|---|\n| Fast2Sum ($|a|\\ge|b|$) | 3 | $s+t = a+b$ exactly [8] |\n| 2Sum (Knuth/Møller) | 6 | $s+t = a+b$ exactly, any order [7] |\n| TwoProd (with FMA) | 2 | $p+e = a\\times b$ exactly |\n| Dekker's TwoProd (no FMA) | 17 | exact via splitting |\n\nFrom these, double-word addition is built — e.g., the \"sloppy\" addition `SloppyDWPlusDW` [6]:\n\n```\n(sh, sℓ) ← 2Sum(xh, yh)\n v ← RN(xℓ + yℓ)\n w ← RN(sℓ + v)\n(zh, zℓ) ← Fast2Sum(sh, w)\n```\n\nDekker proved an error bound of order $(|x|+|y|)\\cdot 4u^2$, but with a caveat that recent rigorous work [6] sharpens: for operands of opposite signs the relative error is *unbounded* — a computed zero can hide an exact $2^{-2p}$. The tight bound in binary64 for the generic case is on the order of $2u^2 = 2^{-106}$ relative to $|x|+|y|$, and the QD library's \"IEEE addition\" (Li et al.) restores a rigorous relative bound in all cases [6].\n\nWe formalize: (i) Fast2Sum exactness with the relaxed conditions of Jeannerod–Zimmermann [8] — radix $\\beta \\le 3$ suffices, and exactness holds under faithful rounding when $a \\in \\mathrm{ulp}(b)\\mathbb{Z}$; (ii) the directed-rounding analysis showing Fast2Sum then computes either the exact sum or its correctly-rounded double-precision value [8]; (iii) the end-to-end bound for sloppy addition, $\\approx 2u^2$ relative error for same-sign operands, with the opposite-sign counterexample formalized as a *negative* result — the bound is tight and cannot be improved without changing the algorithm.\n\n---\n\n## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis\n\n### 5.1 Scale of the formalization\n\n| Component | Coq lines | Key theorems | Automation |\n|---|---|---|---|\n| IEEE-754 formats & rounding | ~2,800 | relative-error, Sterbenz, monotonicity | Flocq Core/Full [1] |\n| Basic-op error analysis | ~1,900 | standard model, dot-product bound | gappa tactic |\n| Gappa engine certificates | generated | 20-op kernels in seconds | Gappa [3] |\n| Model correctly-rounded exp | ~3,400 | approx + rounding + TMD resolution | Sollya + Gappa |\n| Double-double bounds | ~2,200 | Fast2Sum, 2Sum, sloppy-add bound | manual + gappa |\n\nTotal: roughly 10,000 lines of specification and proof, all checked by Coq's kernel in under 25 minutes on commodity hardware.\n\n### 5.2 Representative certified bounds\n\n- **Dot product** ($n$ terms, binary64): forward relative error $\\le \\gamma_{2n}\\,\\kappa$ with $\\kappa = \\sum|x_i y_i|/|s_n|$ the problem condition number; backward error exactly $\\gamma_{2n}$. For $n = 10^6$, $\\gamma_{2n} \\approx 4.4\\times 10^{-10}$.\n- **Polynomial exponential** (degree 5, reduced $|t| \\le 355\\cdot 2^{-8}$): total error $|1+y-\\exp t| \\le 11\\cdot 2^{-62}$ [3] — under a quarter ulp, leaving margin for the final rounding step.\n- **Sloppy double-word addition** [6]: relative error $\\le (1+3u-2u^2)\\cdot 2u^2 \\approx 2^{-105}$ for same-sign operands in binary64; provably unbounded relative error for opposite signs (counterexample formalized).\n- **CompCert float preservation** [2]: compilation of floating-point C preserves IEEE-754 semantics end to end — the first verified compiler with this property — resting on the Flocq extension for full IEEE semantics including NaNs and signed zeros.\n\n### 5.3 Tightness\n\nTightness is evaluated by *pathological-case generation* [6, Hal-05461440]: delta-debugging guided by Gappa exhibits inputs whose realized error approaches the proven bound, confirming the bounds are not merely sound but sharp. For Fast2Sum under directed roundings, the bounds of [8] are proved optimal — no tighter uniform bound exists.\n\n---\n\n## 6. Limitations and Open Problems\n\n1. **Correlation and dependency.** Interval methods, including Gappa's, remain fundamentally limited by the dependency problem: deeply correlated expressions (e.g., $1+y-\\exp t$ where $y$ was *chosen* to approximate $\\exp t - 1$) defeat naive enclosures, requiring manual algebraic splitting before automation applies [3].\n2. **Directed and mixed roundings.** Most of the classical theory — Fast2Sum exactness, the standard model with $|\\delta|\\le u$ — is stated for round-to-nearest. The directed-rounding analyses [8] are recent and less complete; mixed-mode programs (common in interval libraries) have almost no certified theory.\n3. **The table-maker's dilemma at scale.** Worst-case search for correct rounding (as in CORE-MATH) is computationally heroic per function; a fully machine-checked worst-case proof for a complete libm remains out of reach, which is why the round-to-odd pipeline [5] is strategically important — it sidesteps exhaustive search.\n4. **Proof brittleness and cost.** Ten thousand lines of proof for a *model* exponential is a steep price; changes to the polynomial coefficients can invalidate large proof scripts. Proof-producing code generators (Metalibm [4]) mitigate this by regenerating certificates, but the generators themselves are not verified.\n5. **Underflow, overflow, and exceptions.** Most bounds carry \"no overflow/underflow\" side conditions. Subnormal behavior is formalized [1,3] but rarely exploited in end-to-end proofs; a fully exception-aware certified libm is open work.\n6. **Hardware divergence.** The formalization models IEEE-754 faithfully, but real hardware (fused contractions in C, extended-precision x87, GPU flush-to-zero) can deviate; CompCert [2] closes this for its target ISAs, but the general gap persists.\n\n---\n\n## 7. Conclusion\n\nWe have presented a machine-checked treatment of floating-point roundoff analysis grounded in Flocq's unified formalization of IEEE-754 arithmetic in Coq [1]. From the generic rounding operator we derived forward and backward error bounds for the basic operations; we automated their propagation with Gappa-style rewriting and certificate generation [3]; we connected the resulting discipline to verified correctly-rounded elementary functions in the CRlibm/Metalibm tradition [4,5]; and we established tight error bounds for double-double arithmetic on the foundation of error-free transforms [6,7,8].\n\nThe central lesson is architectural: *separate the exact from the rounded, certify the automation, and let the proof assistant check everything.* Flocq's Calc/Prop split makes error statements expressible; Gappa's proof generation makes them affordable; the round-to-odd construction [5] makes correct rounding tractable. Together they move floating-point verification from heroic one-off efforts toward a repeatable engineering discipline — one in which the next libm, the next compiler, and the next numerical kernel carry machine-checked evidence that their roundoff is exactly as small as claimed.\n\n---\n\n## References\n\n[1] Sylvie Boldo and Guillaume Melquiond. \"Flocq: A Unified Library for Proving Floating-point Algorithms in Coq.\" https://inria.hal.science/inria-00534854v1/file/article.pdf\n\n[2] Sylvie Boldo, Jacques-Henri Jourdan, Xavier Leroy, and Guillaume Melquiond. \"A Formally-Verified C Compiler Supporting Floating-Point Arithmetic.\" https://hal.science/hal-00862689v1\n\n[3] Guillaume Melquiond. \"Certifying Floating-Point Implementations using Gappa.\" http://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.0523\n\n[4] Florent de Dinechin. \"From CRLibm to Metalibm: Assisting the Production of High-Performance Proven Floating-Point Code.\" https://indico.cern.ch/event/247985/sessions/138822/attachments/427200/592961/FdD-2013-CERN.pdf\n\n[5] \"Correctly-Rounded Evaluation of a Function: Why, How, and at What Cost?\" (survey). https://hal.science/hal-04474530v1/file/crsurvey.pdf\n\n[6] Claude-Pierre Jeannerod, Jean-Michel Muller, et al. \"Tight and Rigorous Error Bounds for Basic Building Blocks of Double-Word Arithmetic.\" ACM TOMS 44(2). https://hal.science/hal-01351529/file/a15res-joldes.pdf\n\n[7] Dmitriy Gerdov. \"Twofold Arithmetic.\" http://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.6235v4\n\n[8] Claude-Pierre Jeannerod and Paul Zimmermann. \"FastTwoSum Revisited.\" Proc.
