# The C++11 Memory Model, RC11 Repair, and the Promising Semantics: A Formal Account of Language-Level Weak Memory

## Abstract

The C++11 language memory model was the first weak-memory semantics adopted by a mainstream programming language, yet within a few years its formal foundations were shown to be defective: it admitted out-of-thin-air reads through causality cycles, invalidated standard compiler optimizations, and — as Lahav et al. demonstrated — its sequential-consistency axioms were unsound with respect to both proposed POWER compilation schemes. This thesis gives a unified formal account of the arc that repaired it. We present the C++11 axiomatic model of Batty et al. — sequenced-before, reads-from, modification order, and happens-before — and diagnose exactly where it fails. We then develop the RC11 repair with its strengthened coherence, no-thin-air, and sequential-consistency axioms, and Kang et al.'s promising semantics, an operational model of timestamped messages, thread views, speculative promises, and thread-local certification. We survey the compilation-correctness results for POWER and ARMv8 and the DRF-SC guarantees, drawing on the original PLDI 2017, POPL 2017, and POPL 2018 papers and the ISO N4659 working draft.

## 1 Introduction

A memory model is the contract that binds three parties with conflicting interests: the *programmer*, who wants simple reasoning principles; the *compiler*, which wants freedom to reorder, eliminate, and speculate on memory accesses; and the *hardware*, which wants to exploit store buffers, speculative execution, and non-atomic propagation to gain performance [3]. Sequential consistency (SC) satisfies the programmer but starves the other two parties, so modern languages define *weak* or *relaxed* models that permit carefully circumscribed reorderings.

C++11 was the first mainstream language to standardize such a model, but its prose specification was never rigorous; the first mathematical semantics came from Batty, Owens, Sarkar, Sewell, and Weber, who formalized it in Isabelle/HOL and built the Cppmem exploration tool [1]. Their work both validated the design and exposed its fractures. Researchers soon discovered that the C++11 model:

1. **Admits out-of-thin-air reads.** Values can appear in executions that no write ever produced, via self-justifying causality cycles [2][5].
2. **Invalidates ordinary optimizations.** Transformations routinely performed by compilers — sequentialization, redundant-load elimination across atomics — are unsound under the model [5].
3. **Breaks sequential consistency twice over.** The SC axioms are simultaneously *too strong* (forbidding innocuous behaviors and mischaracterizing message passing) and *too weak* (SC fences fail to restore order), and both published POWER compilation schemes are unsound [2].

Two complementary repairs resolved this crisis. **RC11** (Lahav et al., PLDI 2017) strengthened the axiomatic model: a tighter coherence axiom, an explicit no-thin-air acyclicity requirement, and corrected SC axioms that restore sound compilation to POWER while preserving DRF-SC [2]. The **promising semantics** (Kang et al., POPL 2017) took the operational route: memory as timestamped messages, per-thread views, speculative *promises* of future writes, and thread-local *certification* that excludes thin-air behaviors while validating optimizations and compilation to x86-TSO and POWER [3]. ARM's revision of ARMv8 to a multicopy-atomic model with proved-equivalent axiomatic and operational presentations completed the hardware side [4].

This thesis reconstructs that arc in full formal detail: the axiomatic machinery of C++11, the precise anatomy of its failures, the RC11 repair, and the promising semantics, ending with the compilation-correctness results that reconnect language semantics to silicon.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From sequential consistency to weak hardware

Lamport's sequential consistency demands that a concurrent execution appear as some interleaving of per-thread program orders. Real multiprocessors violate SC routinely: x86-TSO permits store→load reordering through store buffers, while POWER and ARM permit nearly all reorderings subject only to dependency and barrier constraints. A *language-level* model must therefore be weaker than SC yet strong enough to support portable reasoning.

The central reasoning principle is **DRF-SC**: programs whose accesses are all properly synchronized behave as if sequentially consistent, so programmers who avoid races never need to understand the weak model [1][6].

### 2.2 The C++11 design

C++11 divides accesses into *non-atomic* and *atomic*. Races on non-atomics are programming errors with undefined behavior, which lets compilers treat them as plain machine accesses and apply ordinary sequential optimizations. Races on *atomics* are permitted but governed by a *memory order*:

| Memory order | Intended guarantee |
|---|---|
| `memory_order_relaxed` | Atomicity only; no ordering constraints |
| `memory_order_consume` | Orders accesses data-dependent on the loaded value |
| `memory_order_acquire` | Later accesses cannot move before the load |
| `memory_order_release` | Earlier accesses cannot move after the store |
| `memory_order_acq_rel` | Both acquire and release |
| `memory_order_seq_cst` | A single global total order over all SC operations |

Release–acquire pairs implement the workhorse *message-passing* idiom: a release store of a flag synchronizes with an acquire load that observes it, establishing happens-before from everything before the store to everything after the load [1][6].

### 2.3 Litmus tests

Weak-memory research is organized around *litmus tests* — tiny programs with a distinguished outcome. The canonical four, under SC, all forbid their interesting outcome:

- **SB (store buffering):** two threads store then load; SC forbids both loads reading the initial value.
- **LB (load buffering):** two threads load then store; SC forbids the cyclic outcome where each load reads the other's store.
- **MP (message passing):** the release–acquire idiom; SC forbids reading stale data.
- **IRIW (independent reads of independent writes):** SC forbids the two readers disagreeing on write order; the SC-fences variant tests whether fences alone restore SC.

These tests recur throughout the thesis as the empirical backbone of every model comparison.

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is an analytic reconstruction: it synthesizes the primary formal sources — the Batty et al. formalization [1], the RC11 paper [2], the promising-semantics paper [3], the ARMv8 model [4], the compiler-optimization analysis [5], and the N4659 working draft [6] — checking each definition and theorem claim against the original text.

We evaluate every model variant against five criteria:

1. **Thin-air freedom:** no execution may produce a value no write ever wrote.
2. **Compilation soundness:** the standard mappings to POWER and ARMv8 must preserve all model-guaranteed behaviors.
3. **DRF-SC:** race-free programs must exhibit only sequentially consistent behaviors.
4. **Optimization validity:** standard compiler transformations must be sound.
5. **Mechanization:** key metatheory should be machine-checked (both [2] and [3] are substantially Coq-verified).

Litmus-test outcomes serve as the running empirical check: a model's verdicts on SB, LB, MP, and IRIW must match the theorems its authors claim.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The C++11 axiomatic model: sequenced-before, reads-from, modification order, happens-before

The C++11 model, as formalized by Batty et al. [1], is *axiomatic*: a program's candidate executions are filtered by global constraints over relations on memory actions. The vocabulary is as follows:

| Relation | Symbol | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Sequenced-before | `sb` | Per-thread program order (intra-thread predecessor relation) |
| Additional synchronizes-with | `asw` | Cross-thread edges from thread creation, join, etc. |
| Reads-from | `rf` | Each read is related to the unique write (same location) it observes |
| Modification order | `mo` | A per-location total order over all writes to that location |
| Synchronizes-with | `sw` | Release write → acquire read that reads from it (via release sequences), plus fence variants |
| Dependency-ordered-before | `dob` | Carries `consume` ordering along data dependencies |
| Happens-before | `hb` | Transitive closure of `sb ∪ asw ∪ sw ∪ dob`; required acyclic |

*Consistency* then demands, roughly:

- **hb is acyclic** — causality must be a strict partial order.
- **Coherence** — conditions constraining how `rf` may select among writes relative to `mo` and `hb` (a read cannot observe a write `mo`-hidden behind another write it happens-after).
- **SC axioms** — a total order `S` over all `seq_cst` actions, consistent with `hb` and `mo`, such that each SC read observes the most recent SC write in `S` (or a non-SC write not hidden by `hb`).
- **Atomicity** — read-modify-write operations read from their immediate `mo`-predecessor.

Finally, the **data-race** definition gives the model its teeth: an execution contains a data race if it has two *conflicting* actions (same location, at least one a write) in different threads, *at least one of which is non-atomic*, with neither ordered before the other by `hb`. A racy program has *undefined behavior* — the infamous catch-fire semantics — which is precisely what licenses compiling non-atomics to plain machine accesses [6].

> **Theorem:** (DRF-SC, C++11). If every sequentially consistent execution of a program is data-race-free, then every execution admitted by the model is sequentially consistent [1].

### 4.2 Thin-air reads, causality cycles, and the non-atomic data race

The model's deepest flaw is the **out-of-thin-air** problem. Consider load buffering with a twist:

```cpp
std::atomic<int> x{0}, y{0};
// Thread 1                // Thread 2
int r1 = x.load(rlx);      int r2 = y.load(rlx);
y.store(r1, rlx);          x.store(r2, rlx);
```

Nothing in the C++11 axioms forbids the outcome `r1 == r2 == 42`. Thread 1's read of `x` can read-from Thread 2's write of `42`, whose value came from Thread 2's read of `y`, which read-from Thread 1's write of `42`, whose value came from Thread 1's read of `x` — a self-justifying **causality cycle** in `sb ∪ rf` (Figure 2). No write of `42` ever existed in program order; the value materialized from the cycle itself.

![Figure 2 — An out-of-thin-air causality cycle: each thread's read is justified by a write whose value depends on that very read. C++11 admits the cycle; RC11's no-thin-air axiom forbids it.](ths_1789072500000_16280-1.webp)

Why does this matter? Vafeiadis, Balabonski, Chakraborty, Morisset, and Zappa Nardelli showed that causality cycles do not merely permit a bizarre litmus outcome — they make *standard compiler transformations unsound* [5]. Sequentialization, redundant-load elimination, and simple reorderings become invalid, because a compiler that speculates a value can have its speculation retroactively justified by the cycle. The optimization and the thin-air read are the same phenomenon viewed from opposite sides.

The standard's response was a non-normative note: N4659 states that implementations *should* ensure that no out-of-thin-air stores are performed, but the axiomatic definition never enforces it [6]. A note is not an axiom, and compilers and hardware cannot be verified against a wish.

The non-atomic data race plays a subtler role here. Because a race involving a non-atomic access is undefined behavior, the compiler may assume such races never occur and optimize aggressively. But the *definition* of the race depends on `hb`, and `hb` depends on `rf` — which, amid causality cycles, can be chosen adversarially. The model's undefined-behavior escape hatch thus rests on foundations that thin-air executions undermine.

### 4.3 RC11: strengthened coherence and the no-thin-air axiom

Lahav, Vafeiadis, Kang, Hur, and Dreyer (PLDI 2017) attacked the model on a second front and found the situation worse than anyone suspected [2]. Their three headline findings:

1. **SC is too strong.** The SC axioms forbid behaviors of programs mixing SC and relaxed accesses that should be allowed — a soundness problem in the opposite direction.
2. **SC is too weak.** SC *fences* fail to restore sequential consistency where programmers expect it: IRIW with SC fences still admits non-SC outcomes.
3. **Both published POWER compilation schemes are unsound** — contrary to previously published correctness claims — and every proposed strengthening of the SC axioms then on the table shared the flaw.

Their repair, **RC11**, keeps the axiomatic style but replaces the defective axioms:

- **Coherence.** The seven C++11 coherence conditions are replaced by a single crisp requirement. Let `eco = (rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)+` be the *extended coherence order* (`fr`, from-reads, relates a read to writes `mo`-after the write it observed). RC11 demands:
  > **Coherence:** `hb ; eco?` is irreflexive.
  
  In words: a coherence violation must be exactly one happens-before path followed by at most one eco step. Executions whose cycles interleave two or more eco segments with `hb` segments — such as plain store buffering — remain allowed; genuine coherence violations are still caught.
- **No thin air.** RC11 adds the missing axiom outright:
  > **No-thin-air:** `sb ∪ rf` is acyclic.
  
  This single acyclicity requirement severs every causality cycle at the root: a read can never (transitively) justify the write it reads from through program order.
- **Repaired SC.** The SC axioms are reformulated around a strengthened *partial* SC order (PSC) that is simultaneously weaker where C++11 over-forbade (fixing finding 1) and stronger where C++11 under-guaranteed (fixing finding 2, giving SC fences their expected force).

| Feature | C++11 | RC11 |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | Seven ad-hoc conditions | `irreflexive(hb ; eco?)` |
| Thin air | Permitted (advisory note only) | Forbidden: `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)` |
| SC axioms | Too strong *and* too weak | Strengthened PSC order |
| POWER compilation | Both schemes unsound | Strengthened scheme proved sound |
| DRF-SC | Holds | Preserved |

Crucially, RC11 is not merely a paper design: the authors give a *stronger* compilation scheme to POWER — one that preserves load→store ordering and therefore avoids thin-air reads by construction — and prove it sound in Coq, the first such proof [2]. The DRF-SC guarantee survives the repair intact.

### 4.4 The promising semantics: views, promises, and certification

Where RC11 repairs the axiomatic edifice, the promising semantics of Kang, Hur, Lahav, Vafeiadis, and Dreyer (POPL 2017) rebuilds from operational foundations [3]. The machine state is deliberately simple:

- **Memory** is a per-location list of *messages* `⟨location, value, timestamp interval⟩`. A write appends a message with a fresh timestamp; there is no single *current value* — every historical write remains addressable.
- **Views.** Each thread carries a *view*: a map from locations to timestamps recording how far the thread has observed. A read of location `ℓ` may choose any message for `ℓ` with timestamp at or above the thread's view, then raises its view to the message's timestamp.
- **Release/acquire** fall out naturally: a release write *attaches* the writer's view to its message; an acquire read *merges* the message's view into the reader's.

The innovation is the **promise**.

Unrestricted promises would reintroduce thin air through the back door (promise `x = 42`, read `42`, use it to justify the promise).

![Figure 3 — The promising machine: timestamped message memory, per-thread views, speculative promises, and the thread-local certification check that excludes thin-air values.](ths_1789072500000_16280-2.webp)

The paper's results are sweeping, and largely mechanized in Coq [3]:

> **Theorem:** (DRF-SC/DRF-RA, promising semantics). Data-race-free programs exhibit only sequentially consistent behaviors; release/acquire-race-free programs exhibit release/acquire semantics.

> **Theorem:** (Compilation soundness). The standard compilation schemes from the promising semantics to x86-TSO and to POWER are sound: every hardware-observable behavior is admitted by the source model.

Standard compiler optimizations — including reorderings of relaxed accesses that hardware performs — are validated, and, unlike C++11, *every* program including racy ones receives a defined semantics rather than catch-fire undefined behavior, a prerequisite for applying the model to type-safe languages.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The models can be compared head-to-head on the five criteria of §3:

| Criterion | C++11 (Batty et al.) | RC11 | Promising semantics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin-air freedom | ❌ Admitted | ✅ `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)` | ✅ Certification |
| POWER compilation sound | ❌ Both schemes unsound | ✅ Strengthened scheme proved | ✅ Scheme proved |
| DRF-SC guarantee | ✅ | ✅ Preserved | ✅ (+ DRF-RA) |
| Validates compiler optimizations | ❌ [5] | Partial (axiomatic) | ✅ Broadly, in Coq |
| Defined semantics for racy programs | ❌ Catch-fire UB | ❌ Catch-fire UB | ✅ |
| Mechanized metatheory | Isabelle/HOL (model) | Coq (repair proofs) | Coq (extensive) |

Litmus-test verdicts confirm the theory. Under RC11 and the promising semantics, LB with a fabricated value is forbidden while genuine LB remains allowed; MP with release–acquire is enforced; SB's weak outcome stays permitted (it is observable on real hardware); and IRIW with SC fences is forbidden, restoring the guarantee C++11 failed to provide [2][3]. The ARMv8 equivalence proof anchors the hardware end: litmus suites run against the operational Flat model and the axiomatic model agree [4].

> **Theorem:** (RC11 DRF-SC). If all SC executions of a program are data-race-free, then all RC11-consistent executions are sequentially consistent [2].

> **Theorem:** (Promising compilation correctness). The compilation schemes from the promising semantics to x86-TSO and POWER map every target behavior to a source behavior; no new observable behaviors are introduced [3].

Both theorems are machine-checked in Coq — a standard of evidence the original C++11 prose definition could never meet.

## 6 Limitations

Neither repair is the final word, and intellectual honesty requires stating the boundaries:

- **Certification is non-constructive.** The promising semantics defines legality via an existential quantification over solo executions; it is a specification device, not an interpreter. Executable exploration requires bounding the search, and later work (notably the *promising 2.0* revision) reworked promises with *reservations* to handle read-modify-write operations and certification more compositionally.
- **Coverage gaps.** The models idealize away mixed-size and unaligned accesses, which real hardware and the revised ARMv8 model [4] must confront. C++'s `consume` ordering is effectively deprecated in practice and handled only vestigially.
- **The standard lags the science.** N4659 still treats out-of-thin-air as an implementation *should*, not a semantic *shall* [6]. Later committee work weakened release sequences (P0982R1) and repaired SC fences (P0668), moving toward RC11, but the axiomatic text never fully absorbed the repair.
- **Liveness and fairness.** All of these models specify *safety* (which outcomes are possible), not *liveness* (whether a thread ever observes progress). Fairness extensions exist but remain outside the core story.
- **Axiomatic vs. operational tension.** RC11 and the promising semantics agree on the litmus corpus, but whether they coincide on *all* programs remains a subtle, partially open correspondence question.

## 7 Conclusion

The decade from the C++11 formalization to the ARMv8 revision traces a complete arc of repair. Batty et al. gave the language its first rigorous semantics [1]; Vafeiadis et al. showed it broke the compilers it was meant to serve [5]; Lahav et al. repaired the SC axioms with RC11, restoring provably sound POWER compilation [2]; Kang et al. supplied the operational counterpart, where promises and certification explain *why* speculation is safe and thin air is not [3]; and Pulte et al. stabilized the hardware target with multicopy-atomic ARMv8 [4].

Two lessons endure. First, *a memory model is a three-party contract*, and a defect against any party — programmer, compiler, or hardware — is a defect in the model. Second, *proofs must be mechanized*: every major result in this story that survived contact with reality was checked in Coq or Isabelle, while every unsound claim (the POWER schemes, the proposed strengthenings) was paper-only. The remaining work is convergence — bringing the standard's text, still hedging on thin air in N4659 [6], into line with the repaired semantics the community has proved correct.
