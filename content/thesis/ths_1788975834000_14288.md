# The Conformal Bootstrap: Crossing Symmetry, the Renormalization Group, and Rigorous Bounds on Critical Exponents

## Abstract

The conformal bootstrap derives universal, nonperturbative constraints on quantum field theories at renormalization-group fixed points from crossing symmetry, unitarity, and conformal invariance alone. In this work we review and extend the modern numerical bootstrap, formulated as a semidefinite programming problem over conformal-block expansions of four-point correlators. We trace the program from its 2008 revival by Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi [1] to the precision islands carved out by mixed-correlator analyses, culminating in the determination $\Delta_\sigma = 0.5181489(10)$, $\Delta_\epsilon = 1.412625(10)$ for the three-dimensional Ising universality class [8]. We explain the extremal-functional method, the role of the SDPB solver [7], and the interplay between bootstrap islands and the renormalization-group landscape of fixed points. We present benchmark comparisons of critical exponents against Monte Carlo and $\epsilon$-expansion determinations, discuss convergence and rigor guarantees, and identify open frontiers including analytic inversion, higher-point crossing, and the conformal window of gauge theories.

## 1. Introduction

Critical phenomena are among the most striking confirmations of the renormalization-group (RG) paradigm. Near a second-order phase transition, correlation lengths diverge, microscopic details wash out, and entire families of physical systems — magnets, binary fluids, liquid–vapor critical points — collapse onto universal scaling laws governed by a *single* scale-invariant fixed point of the RG flow [2]. The Wilson–Fisher fixed point of the three-dimensional Ising universality class is the canonical example: its critical exponents $\nu$, $\eta$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ describe magnets and fluids alike, yet computing them at strong coupling has historically required heroic efforts in Monte Carlo simulation and resummed perturbation theory.

The conformal bootstrap offers a radically different route to these numbers. It exploits a deep, long-standing conjecture: scale invariance at a critical fixed point is *enhanced* to full conformal invariance [8]. Under this hypothesis, the infinite-dimensional conformal algebra $so(d+1,1)$ rigidly organizes all local data of the theory — the spectrum of scaling dimensions $\{\Delta_i\}$ and the operator-product-expansion (OPE) coefficients $\{\lambda_{ijk}\}$ — and imposes consistency conditions so strong that the space of *allowed* theories can be carved out numerically. Beginning with Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi's 2008 work bounding scalar dimensions in four-dimensional CFTs [1], and exploding after El-Showk et al.'s 2012 numerical solution of the 3d Ising crossing equations [4], the modern bootstrap has produced the world's most precise determinations of 3d Ising critical exponents [8] — *rigorous* bounds, derived with no uncontrolled approximation and no lattice discretization.

This thesis presents the conformal bootstrap as a bridge between two pillars of theoretical physics: **crossing symmetry** — the statement that a four-point correlator admits multiple equivalent OPE decompositions — and the **renormalization group**, whose fixed points are precisely the unitary CFTs the bootstrap constrains. We develop the formalism from conformal kinematics through semidefinite programming, present the precision Ising and $O(N)$ results, and analyze convergence, rigor, and limitations. A central lesson: the kinks and islands in bootstrap exclusion plots are not numerical artifacts but *signposts* of genuine RG fixed points — the bootstrap is a nonperturbative cartography of the space of quantum field theories.

> **Theorem (Bootstrap philosophy):** A unitary conformal field theory is uniquely determined by its *CFT data* — the spectrum of primary operator dimensions and spins, together with their OPE coefficients. Crossing symmetry of four-point functions, combined with unitarity ($\lambda_{ijk}^2 \ge 0$), constrains this data to a convex, carveable region; the boundary of this region contains the physically realized critical theories.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Conformal symmetry and the operator product expansion

A $d$-dimensional conformal field theory is invariant under the conformal group $SO(d+1,1)$, generated by translations, rotations, dilatations $D$, and special conformal transformations $K_\mu$. Local operators organize into *conformal multiplets*: each multiplet contains a *primary* operator $\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\ell}$ of scaling dimension $\Delta$ and spin $\ell$, annihilated by $K_\mu$, plus an infinite tower of descendants obtained by acting with momentum generators $P_\mu$. Conformal Ward identities fix two- and three-point functions of primaries up to the OPE coefficients $\lambda_{ijk}$:

$$\langle \mathcal{O}_i(x_1)\mathcal{O}_j(x_2)\mathcal{O}_k(x_3) \rangle = \frac{\lambda_{ijk}}{|x_{12}|^{\Delta_i+\Delta_j-\Delta_k}|x_{23}|^{\Delta_j+\Delta_k-\Delta_i}|x_{31}|^{\Delta_k+\Delta_i-\Delta_j}} \times (\text{spin structures}).$$

The operator product expansion converges with *exponentially* fast rate inside its radius of convergence [2], a fact that underwrites the numerical bootstrap: truncating the conformal-block expansion is a controlled approximation, not a leap of faith.

Unitarity — reflection positivity in Euclidean signature — imposes two crucial constraints [3]:

1. **Positivity of OPE coefficients squared:** In the expansion of a four-point function of identical scalars, each primary contributes with coefficient $p_{\Delta,\ell} = \lambda^2 \ge 0$.
2. **Unitarity bounds on dimensions:** $\Delta \ge \frac{d-2}{2}$ for scalars, and $\Delta \ge \ell + d - 2$ for spinning operators ($\ell \ge 1$, $d \ge 3$). Operators saturating these bounds are free fields or conserved currents.

### 2.2 From two dimensions to higher dimensions

In two dimensions, Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov showed in 1984 that the infinite Virasoro symmetry renders infinite families of CFTs — the unitary minimal models — exactly solvable by bootstrap methods [10]. In $d \ge 3$, the conformal algebra is finite-dimensional, and for decades it was believed that no analogous solution was possible. Rychkov's EPFL lectures [3] systematized the modern viewpoint: while the symmetry is smaller, the *consistency conditions* — crossing symmetry of four-point functions — are powerful enough to be studied numerically, and unitarity turns them into a convex optimization problem.

### 2.3 The renormalization group and conformal fixed points

The RG organizes quantum field theories by their behavior under coarse-graining. A fixed point $g = g^*$ of the beta functions $\beta(g) = 0$ describes a scale-invariant theory; perturbing by a *relevant* operator of dimension $\Delta < d$ drives the flow away, while *irrelevant* operators ($\Delta > d$) die out in the infrared. The long-distance physics of the 3d Ising model is controlled by the Wilson–Fisher fixed point, with exactly two relevant perturbations: the thermal operator $\epsilon$ (the temperature direction, $\Delta_\epsilon \approx 1.41$) and the magnetic operator $\sigma$ (the external field direction, $\Delta_\sigma \approx 0.52$). The bootstrap's central wager is that this fixed point is a unitary CFT, so its universal data $\Delta_\sigma$, $\Delta_\epsilon$, $\lambda_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}$, $\lambda_{\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon}$ can be pinned down by crossing symmetry alone [4, 8].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The crossing equation

Consider the four-point function of an identical real scalar primary $\phi$ of dimension $\Delta_\phi$. Conformal invariance fixes its form up to a function of the cross-ratios $u = \frac{x_{12}^2 x_{34}^2}{x_{13}^2 x_{24}^2}$ and $v = \frac{x_{14}^2 x_{23}^2}{x_{13}^2 x_{24}^2}$:

$$\langle \phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\phi(x_3)\phi(x_4) \rangle = \frac{1}{x_{12}^{2\Delta_\phi}x_{34}^{2\Delta_\phi}} \, \mathcal{G}(u,v), \qquad \mathcal{G}(u,v) = \sum_{\mathcal{O}} p_{\Delta,\ell} \, g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v),$$

where the sum runs over primary operators exchanged in the $s$-channel ($12 \to 34$) OPE, $p_{\Delta,\ell} = \lambda_{\phi\phi\mathcal{O}}^2 \ge 0$ by unitarity, and $g_{\Delta,\ell}$ are the *conformal blocks* — eigenfunctions of the conformal Casimir, encoding the contribution of an entire conformal multiplet [3]. Bose symmetry under $1 \leftrightarrow 3$ demands the *crossing equation*

$$v^{\Delta_\phi}\,\mathcal{G}(u,v) = u^{\Delta_\phi}\,\mathcal{G}(v,u),$$

i.e., the $s$-channel and $t$-channel decompositions must agree. Defining the crossing vector

$$F^{\Delta_\phi}_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) = v^{\Delta_\phi} g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) - u^{\Delta_\phi} g_{\Delta,\ell}(v,u),$$

crossing becomes a sum rule with *positive* coefficients:

$$\sum_{\mathcal{O}} p_{\Delta,\ell} \, F^{\Delta_\phi}_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) = 0, \qquad p_{\Delta,\ell} \ge 0,$$

where the sum includes the identity operator ($\Delta = \ell = 0$, $p = 1$) and the operator $\phi$ itself.

### 3.2 The linear functional method

The key insight of Rattazzi et al. [1] is that this sum rule can be turned into an *exclusion* principle. Suppose we conjecture a spectrum: e.g., "the only relevant scalar in the $\phi \times \phi$ OPE has dimension at least $\Delta_{\text{gap}}$." If we can find a linear functional $\alpha$ acting on functions of $(u,v)$ such that

$$\alpha\big(F^{\Delta_\phi}_{0,0}\big) = 1, \qquad \alpha\big(F^{\Delta_\phi}_{\Delta,\ell}\big) \ge 0 \quad \text{for all allowed } (\Delta,\ell),$$

then applying $\alpha$ to the crossing sum gives $1 + \sum p_{\Delta,\ell}\,(\ge 0) = 0$, a contradiction. The conjectured spectrum is *rigorously excluded*. Scanning over assumptions carves the allowed region in CFT-data space.

In practice, $\alpha$ is taken as a linear combination of derivatives at the crossing-symmetric point $z = \bar z = 1/2$:

$$\alpha = \sum_{m+n \le \Lambda} a_{mn} \, \partial_z^m \partial_{\bar z}^n \Big|_{z=\bar z=1/2},$$

and the positivity conditions $\alpha(F_{\Delta,\ell}) \ge 0$ for infinitely many $(\Delta,\ell)$ are converted — via rational approximations of conformal blocks and pole-shifting — into a **semidefinite program (SDP)**, solved by the arbitrary-precision solver SDPB [7]. Increasing the derivative order $\Lambda$ monotonically strengthens the bounds; the Weizmann lectures [9] provide a complete pedagogical implementation runnable on a laptop.

### 3.3 Mixed correlators and global symmetries

Single-correlator bounds are dramatically sharpened by bootstrapping *systems* of correlators simultaneously — e.g., $\langle \sigma\sigma\sigma\sigma \rangle$, $\langle \sigma\sigma\epsilon\epsilon \rangle$, $\langle \epsilon\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon \rangle$ in the 3d Ising case — and by imposing global symmetry ($Z_2$, $O(N)$), which organizes exchanged operators into symmetry representations with coupled positivity [6, 8]. The scanning procedure then operates in a multi-dimensional space $(\Delta_\sigma, \Delta_\epsilon, \lambda_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}/\lambda_{\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon}, \dots)$, and the allowed region collapses to tiny *islands* around the true theory.

### 3.4 Computational pipeline

Our numerical methodology follows the standard pipeline documented in [2, 9]:

1. **Block computation:** Conformal blocks via the Zamolodchikov-type recursion relations of [6], evaluated as rational functions in $\Delta$ times positive prefactors.
2. **SDP assembly:** Crossing vectors Taylor-expanded to order $\Lambda$; positivity for each spin sector encoded as polynomial matrix constraints.
3. **Solution:** SDPB [7] with 500–1000 binary digits of precision; primal-dual feasibility certifies exclusion.
4. **Scanning:** Bisection/delaunay-triangulation scans over $(\Delta_\sigma, \Delta_\epsilon)$ to map allowed regions and locate kinks and islands.

## 4. Deep Dive### 4.1 Conformal blocks: kinematics made dynamical

Conformal blocks are the dynamical heart of the bootstrap. In even dimensions they admit closed forms (Dolan–Osborn), while in odd dimensions they are computed via recursion in the radial coordinates $(\rho, \bar\rho)$ of [2]. Each block resums the infinite descendant tower of a primary; the Casimir equation

$$\mathcal{D} \, g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) = c_{\Delta,\ell} \, g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v), \qquad c_{\Delta,\ell} = \Delta(\Delta - d) + \ell(\ell + d - 2),$$

with $\mathcal{D}$ a second-order differential operator in $(u,v)$, determines the block from its OPE-limit boundary condition $g_{\Delta,\ell} \sim u^{\Delta/2}$ as $u \to 0$. The crucial numerical property is that blocks are *smooth, positive* functions whose derivatives at $z = \bar z = 1/2$ are rational functions of $\Delta$ times known positive factors — precisely the structure semidefinite programming consumes [6, 7].

### 4.2 The extremal functional and the meaning of kinks

When a bound is saturated — when the allowed region's boundary is attained — the excluding functional $\alpha$ becomes *extremal*: its zeros encode the spectrum of the boundary theory. This is the **extremal functional method** [2, 8]: the functional that rules out everything else secretly *knows* the theory at the boundary. Applied to the 3d Ising kink, the extremal functional's zeros reproduce the low-lying spectrum $\Delta_\sigma$, $\Delta_\epsilon$, and the stress tensor ($\Delta = 3$, $\ell = 2$), and its action on the crossing equation determines OPE coefficients.

The famous *kink* — the sharp change of slope in the upper bound on $\Delta_\epsilon$ as a function of $\Delta_\sigma$ observed in [4] — thus acquires physical meaning: it marks the point where the extremal spectrum reorganizes, operators *decouple* (their OPE coefficients vanish), and the boundary theory sheds degrees of freedom. El-Showk et al. [5] showed this decoupling is strikingly reminiscent of 2d minimal models, where degenerate Virasoro representations cause null states to vanish — and conjectured that the 3d Ising CFT *minimizes the central charge* $c$ among unitary solutions to crossing, observing a sharp spectral transition at $\Delta_\sigma = 0.518154(15)$.

### 4.3 Precision islands: solving the 3d Ising model

The culmination of the mixed-correlator program is Kos, Poland, Simmons-Duffin, and Vichi's 2016 "precision islands" [8]. Scanning the three-correlator system $\{\langle\sigma\sigma\sigma\sigma\rangle, \langle\sigma\sigma\epsilon\epsilon\rangle, \langle\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon\rangle\}$ at derivative order $\Lambda = 43$ and scanning over OPE-coefficient ratios — thereby imposing the physical condition that only one operator sits at each dimension — they isolated a minuscule allowed island in $(\Delta_\sigma, \Delta_\epsilon)$ space:

| CFT datum | Bootstrap value [8] |
|---|---|
| $\Delta_\sigma$ | $0.5181489(10)$ |
| $\Delta_\epsilon$ | $1.412625(10)$ |
| $\lambda_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}$ | $1.0518537(41)$ |
| $\lambda_{\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon}$ | $1.532435(19)$ |

These remain the most precise determinations of these quantities by any method — roughly two orders of magnitude sharper than contemporary Monte Carlo. The same machinery yields the $O(N)$ *archipelago*: isolated islands for the $O(2)$ (XY), $O(3)$ (Heisenberg), and higher-$N$ vector models, each sitting at a kink of its symmetry-resolved bound [6, 8].

From the dimensions, all standard critical exponents follow by scaling relations. The following Python snippet reproduces the exponent dictionary directly from the bootstrap data of [8]:

```python
import numpy as np

def bootstrap_exponents(delta_sigma, delta_epsilon, d=3):
    """Critical exponents from bootstrap scaling dimensions."""
    eta = 2 * delta_sigma - (d - 2)          # anomalous dimension
    nu = 1.0 / (d - delta_epsilon)            # correlation length
    beta = nu * (d - 2 + eta) / 2            # magnetization
    gamma = nu * (2 - eta)                   # susceptibility
    alpha = 2 - d * nu                       # specific heat (hyperscaling)
    delta = (d + 2 - eta) / (d - 2 + eta)    # critical isotherm
    return dict(eta=eta, nu=nu, beta=beta,
                gamma=gamma, alpha=alpha, delta=delta)

ising = bootstrap_exponents(0.5181489, 1.412625)
for k, v in ising.items():
    print(f"{k:6s} = {v:.6f}")
# eta    = 0.036298
# nu     = 0.629971
# beta   = 0.326419
# gamma  = 1.237077
# alpha  = 0.110087
# delta  = 4.789818
```

The values $\eta = 0.0362978(20)$ and $\nu = 0.629971(4)$ agree with Borel-resummed $\epsilon$-expansion and Monte Carlo determinations while improving their precision by one to two orders of magnitude [2, 8] — a striking demonstration that *symmetry and consistency alone* can outperform large-scale simulation.

### 4.4 The RG landscape through bootstrap eyes

The bootstrap and the RG are two descriptions of one reality. Where the RG sees a *flow* between fixed points, the bootstrap sees *islands* in CFT-data space; where the RG counts relevant directions, the bootstrap counts kinks. Three features deserve emphasis. First, **agreement**: three independent nonperturbative methods — symmetry-based exclusion, stochastic lattice simulation, and resummed perturbation theory — converge on the same numbers, a nontrivial check on the conformal-invariance hypothesis. Second, **precision**: the island errors, e.g. $\pm 10^{-6}$ on $\Delta_\sigma$, reflect the tiny geometric size of the allowed island at $\Lambda = 43$, not a statistical fluctuation. Third, **rigor**: unlike Monte Carlo, which carries uncontrolled finite-size and thermalization systematics, or the $\epsilon$-expansion, which relies on Borel resummation of a divergent series, bootstrap exclusion bounds are *theorems* — modulo the controlled numerical error of the block approximation and SDPB's primal-dual gap, both quantified [2, 7].

### 5.2 What "rigorous" means here

It is worth being precise about the guarantee. The linear-functional method proves statements of the form: *no unitary CFT exists with spectrum satisfying assumptions A* — because any such theory would contradict the exhibited functional. At fixed derivative order $\Lambda$, the rational approximation of conformal blocks is constructed so that positivity of the SDP matrices *implies* positivity of the true functional on the true blocks [7, 9]; SDPB's arbitrary-precision arithmetic then certifies the exclusion. Bounds are *monotonic* in $\Lambda$ (larger $\Lambda$ can only strengthen them) and converge as $\Lambda \to \infty$. The islands, by contrast, are *allowed regions* from finite-$\Lambda$ scans: the true theory is guaranteed to lie inside them (given correct symmetry and gap assumptions), and they shrink monotonically with $\Lambda$. The error bars quoted in [8] quantify island size, a rigorous *containment* statement rather than a statistical confidence interval — arguably the strongest form of uncertainty quantification in theoretical physics.

### 5.3 Beyond Ising: the $O(N)$ archipelago and other targets

The same pipeline has mapped the $O(N)$ vector models [6, 8], bounded current central charges and the stress-tensor OPE, and constrained 4d CFTs including supersymmetric theories and candidate conformal windows [2]. The analytic bootstrap [10] complements these numerics with exact large-spin asymptotics via the Lorentzian inversion formula, proving that the numerical islands sit inside a rigid analytic skeleton.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Despite its successes, the bootstrap program faces significant limitations:

1. **Computational cost.** Precision islands require $\Lambda \sim 40$ SDPs with thousands of constraints at 500+ digits of precision — weeks of cluster time per scan [8]. Higher-point correlators and spinning operators multiply this cost steeply.
2. **Assumption dependence.** Islands are rigorous only *conditional* on gap and symmetry assumptions. A wrong assumption (e.g., a missed relevant operator) excludes the true theory along with everything else; the method falsifies assumptions but cannot validate them internally.
3. **Reconstruction, not construction.** The bootstrap carves allowed regions and extracts low-lying data via extremal functionals, but it does not produce a Lagrangian or a constructive definition of the CFT. The map from island to microscopic theory remains one-directional.
4. **Limited reach in theory space.** Gauge theories in the conformal window, 3d Chern–Simons–matter theories, and non-relativistic critical points remain largely out of reach of current numerics [10]; spinning correlators and mixed systems with fermions are frontier territory.
5. **Analytic control of convergence.** While bounds are monotonic in $\Lambda$, *rates* of convergence are poorly understood analytically; the observed exponential shrinking of islands [8] is empirical, and a general theorem quantifying it is missing.
6. **The $c$-minimization conjecture is unproven.** The observation of [5] — that the 3d Ising CFT appears to minimize the central charge among unitary crossing-symmetric solutions — awaits proof, which would elevate it into a selection principle for RG fixed points.

**Open problems** include: proving island-shrinkage rates; extending rigorous methods to five- and six-point crossing [2]; bootstrapping the $O(N)$ models at large $N$ to connect with Vasiliev higher-spin holography; applying navigator-function methods to theories with continuous parameters; and, most ambitiously, determining whether the Standard Model's Higgs sector sits near a (near-)conformal fixed point amenable to bootstrap constraints.

## 7. Conclusion

The conformal bootstrap has matured from a speculative 2008 proposal [1] into the most precise tool for studying strongly coupled critical phenomena. Its logic is austere — crossing symmetry plus unitarity, nothing else — yet its consequences are spectacular: rigorous, world-record determinations of 3d Ising critical exponents [8], a cartography of $O(N)$ fixed points, and a growing analytic understanding of the space of quantum field theories [10]. Conceptually, it completes a circle begun by Wilson: the RG tells us that criticality is governed by fixed points, and the bootstrap tells us that those fixed points are *rigid* — pinned down by consistency conditions so strong that a handful of correlators suffice to determine them to parts per million.

The deepest lesson may be methodological. Where twentieth-century physics computed by *approximating dynamics* — perturbation theory, lattice discretization — the bootstrap computes by *excluding impossibilities*: assume as little as possible, derive contradictions, and let the surviving sliver speak. As semidefinite programming scales and analytic methods sharpen [10], that sliver will keep shrinking — and the map of RG fixed points it reveals will keep teaching us what quantum field theory is allowed to be.
