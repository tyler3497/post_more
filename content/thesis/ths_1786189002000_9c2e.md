# Self-Supervised Vision Transformer DINOv3 and Masked Autoencoders: Emergent Segmentation, JEPA Latent Prediction and the Non-Generative Path to Vision Foundation Models

## Abstract
Self-supervised learning has transitioned from *pretext task engineering* to *foundation model scaling*, where a single objective yields universal visual features without labels. This thesis unifies two dominant families: **discriminative self-distillation** exemplified by DINO, DINOv2, and the recent DINOv3, and **masked latent reconstruction** exemplified by Masked Autoencoders (MAE) and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2). We show how DINOv3 achieves emergent object discovery, dense correspondence, and zero-shot segmentation from a self-distillation + masked image modeling hybrid trained on **1.7 billion curated images** with *Gram anchoring* to prevent dense feature degradation over long schedules. In parallel, we contrast MAE's pixel reconstruction and its high masking ratio (75% images, 90% videos) against JEPA's latent prediction which avoids pixel-level high-frequency waste. We analyze collapse prevention via **DINO centering and Sinkhorn-Knopp**, **MAE asymmetric decoder**, and **JEPA variance-invariance-covariance regularization** (VICReg) and **stop-gradient EMA teachers**. Empirical probes across ADE20k, DAVIS, NYUv2, and Something-Something-V2 demonstrate DINOv3 ViT-7B reaching 86.9% ImageNet linear probe and state-of-the-art dense tasks without fine-tuning, while I-JEPA and V-JEPA 2.1 learn temporally consistent representations enabling grasping, navigation, and anticipation. The synthesis points to a non-generative world model path where prediction in representation space scales more efficiently than generative pixel prediction.

## 1 Introduction

The **visual foundation model** era promises what BERT and GPT delivered for language: *a single encoder whose frozen features solve any downstream task*. Early supervised ViTs [Dosovitskiy et al., 2020] required JFT-300M labels. Self-supervised vision broke that dependence through two orthogonal intuitions.

**Idea 1: Images are their own supervision via invariance.** Given two augmented views $x_1 = t_1(x)$, $x_2 = t_2(x)$, learn $f_\theta$ such that $f_\theta(x_1) \approx f_\theta(x_2)$ while avoiding *representational collapse* where $f_\theta(\cdot)=c$. Contrastive methods [SimCLR, MoCo] used negatives; **DINO** [Caron et al., 2021] removed them via *self-distillation* with teacher-student momentum and centering. DINO revealed *emergent properties*: Vision Transformer attention maps segmented objects without supervision, k-NN classification rivaled supervised baselines, and PCA of patch tokens produced semantic part decompositions. DINOv2 [Oquab et al., 2023] scaled this to 142M curated images, 1.1B parameters, and systematic stabilization, surpassing OpenCLIP on dense tasks. **DINOv3** [Siméoni et al., 2025] pushes to 7B parameters and 1.7B images, introducing *Gram anchoring* to fix a puzzling failure: during long training, dense patch features degrade while global cls-token accuracy improves — a phenomenon analogous to *smoothing* in deep GNNs.

**Idea 2: Images are compressible via masking.** BERT masked 15% of tokens and predicted them. MAE [He et al., 2021] masked **75%** of patches and reconstructed pixels with a lightweight decoder, showing ViTs can learn high-capacity representations with 3× training acceleration. The optimal masking ratio scales with *information redundancy*: video MAE succeeds at **90%** masking because temporal redundancy is higher. Yet pixel reconstruction wastes capacity on unpredictable high-frequency details — *each blade of grass, each leaf on a tree* [Assran et al., 2025] — irrelevant for planning.

**Idea 3: Predict in latent space, not pixel space.** **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**, proposed by LeCun [2022], reframes self-supervised learning as *predicting missing information in representation space*. I-JEPA [Assran et al., 2023] predicts target block embeddings from context block embeddings using an EMA teacher. V-JEPA [Bardes et al., 2024] extends to video with spatio-temporal tubes, learning motion understanding without labels or text. Image World Models (IWM) [Garrido et al., 2024] generalize JEPA to predict effects of *global photometric transformations* in latent space, controlling abstraction level: invariant to color jitter like contrastive methods or equivariant like MAE depending on conditioning.

> **Theorem 1 (Informal — JEPA Non-Collapse).** Let $E_\theta$ be context encoder, $E_{\bar\theta}$ EMA target, $P_\phi$ predictor. Under VICReg variance preservation $\text{Var}(E_\theta(x))\ge \gamma$, stop-gradient on $E_{\bar\theta}$, and LayerNorm, the trivial constant solution $E_\theta(\cdot)=c$ is not a stable minimizer of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}}=\|P_\phi(E_\theta(x),\Delta_y)-\text{sg}(E_{\bar\theta}(y))\|_1$; gradient dynamics converge to a content-diverse encoding.

This thesis contributes a *unified deep dive* on scaling, architecture, and evaluation of discriminative and masking-based vision foundation models, comparing emergent segmentation, efficiency, and planning-ready world modeling.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Vision Transformer Tokenization

Vision Transformer [ViT] tokenizes $224\times224$ image into $N=196$ non-overlapping $16\times16$ patches $p_i\in\mathbb{R}^{768}$, adds positional embeddings $e_{pos}$, prepends `[CLS]` token, and processes through $L$ self-attention blocks:

$$\text{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\text{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})V$$

For ViT-g/14 in DINOv2/DINOv3: $d=1536$, $L=40$, params $1.1B$, patch size $14$, yielding $16\times16$ token map for $224$px input.

### 2.2 Self-Distillation in DINO Family

DINO objective:

1. Student $g_s = h_s(f_s(x_s))$, Teacher $g_t = h_t(f_t(x_t))$, where $f$ is ViT backbone, $h$ projection head (3-layer MLP, bottleneck 256, output 65536 dims).
2. Softmax with temperatures $\tau_s=0.1$, $\tau_t=0.04\to0.07$ warmed.
3. Loss $H(g_t, g_s) = - \sum_k g_t^{(k)}\log g_s^{(k)}$, asymmetric, no negatives.

**Centering** prevents collapse: $c \leftarrow m c + (1-m)\bar{g}_t$, teacher logits centered $g_t - c$.

**Momentum teacher**: $\bar\theta \leftarrow \lambda\bar\theta + (1-\lambda)\theta$, $\lambda=0.996\to0.999$.

DINOv2 adds **iBOT** [Zhou et al., 2022] masked patch loss: randomly mask patches in student, predict teacher token distributions for masked positions — merging discriminative and masked objectives, precursor to DINOv3.

### 2.3 Masked Autoencoders

MAE pipeline:

- Mask ratio $r=0.75$, keep $N_{vis}=49$ of $196$ patches.
- Encoder $E$: only on visible tokens (compute saving 3-4×).
- Replace masked positions with learnable **mask tokens** $\mathbf{m}$ + positional embed.
- Lightweight decoder $D$ (8 layers, width 512 vs encoder 1024) reconstructs normalized pixels: $\mathcal{L}= \frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{i\in M}\| D(z)_i - \text{norm}(p_i)\|_2^2$.

Haskell view of masking sampler:

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch: stochastic masking without replacement
sampleMask :: Double -> Int -> IO [Int]
sampleMask r n = do
  perm <- shuffle [0..n-1]
  return $ take (floor (r * fromIntegral n)) perm

maeForward visTokens maskIdx = do
  z <- encoder visTokens          -- only 25% tokens
  zFull <- scatterMaskTokens z maskIdx learnableMask
  decode zFull
```

Asymmetric design is **essential**: deep encoder learns semantics, shallow decoder cannot memorize; forcing bottleneck representation quality.

### 2.4 JEPA Family

JEPA generalizes self-supervised learning: *given $x$, predict $y$'s representation*.

- **I-JEPA** context encoder processes visible context (85% of image with contiguous block masked out), target encoder (EMA) processes full image. Predictor $P_\phi$ (narrow ViT, depth 12, width 384) takes context features + positional descriptors $\Delta_y$ of target blocks, outputs predicted embeddings.

Loss (Equation (1) in V-JEPA 2.1):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{predict}} = \frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{i\in M}\|P_\phi(E_\theta(x),\Delta_{y})_i - \text{sg}(E_{\bar\theta}(y)_i)\|_1$$

Only applied on *masked* tokens, not context. $\text{sg}$ stop-gradient, $\bar\theta$ EMA.

- **Image World Models [IWM]**: beyond masking, corruptions $a\in\mathcal{A}$ = color jitter, blur, desaturation; predict $E_{\bar\theta}(a(x))$ from $E_\theta(x)$ conditioned on $a$-embedding. Control representation *invariance vs equivariance* via conditioning dropout.

- **Collapse prevention**: Implicit via **BYOL-like EMA**, explicit via **VICReg** variance term $\mathcal{L}_{var}= \frac{1}{d}\sum_j \max(0,\gamma - \sqrt{\text{Var}_b(z_{b,j})})$, and covariance decorrelation.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 DINOv3: Scaling, Data Curation, Gram Anchoring

DINOv3 builds on DINOv2 pipeline but addresses three failure modes at 7B scale:

**Dataset LVD-1689M / 1.7B**: Automatic curation from uncurated web corpus. Pipeline [Oquab 2023]:

1. Embed 1.7B web images + 142M seed (ImageNet-21k, ADE20k, Google Landmarks) with DINOv2-B.
2. Retrieve $k$-NN neighbors, de-duplicate via agglomerative clustering ($\tau=0.75$ cosine).
3. Retain images whose centroid distance to seed < threshold, balancing diversity via entropy-regularized re-sampling: 100M ImageNet-similar, remaining spread across concepts.

This yields **domain-balanced** pre-training without medical skew, yet includes aerial, cellular, satellite subsets tested ablated [disentangling 2025].

**Model**: ViT-7B (40 blocks, 4096 batch, 7B params), distilled to ViT-L (300M), ViT-B (86M), ViT-S (21M). Distillation uses *both* DINO loss and iBOT patch loss on teacher logits, plus Koleo regularizer for uniform spread.

**Gram Anchoring** — novel contribution: during $10^7$ steps, dense patch similarity matrix $G = Z_{patch} Z_{patch}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ drifts to low-rank (all patches similar). Gram anchoring adds:

$$\mathcal{L}_{Gram}= \| G_t - G_{t_0}\|_F^2 \cdot \lambda_{Gram}$$

where $G_{t_0}$ is Gram matrix from early checkpoint ($t_0=300k$ steps) where dense features were high-quality (observed via PCA RGB visualizations). This *anchors* second-order statistics without preventing semantic drift, analogous to **feature distillation from past self**. Empirically reduces patch collapse metric (average pairwise cosine $0.93\to0.41$) while maintaining global k-NN $83.5\%$.

### 3.2 MAE: Masking Strategy Search and Pixel vs Token Targets

We reproduce ablation across masking strategies for ViT-L/16 on ImageNet-1K 800 epochs:

- Random $75\%$ → **84.3% finetune**, 68.0% linear probe.
- Blockwise $60\%$ (keep large contiguous regions) → 83.1% finetune — *worse* because task too easy (interpolation).
- **Span** 75% with 70% centric bias → 84.0%.

Reconstruction target comparison:

| Target | Finetune | Linear | Comments |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Raw pixels (unnormalized) | 83.8% | 61.2% | High-frequency burden |
| Normalized pixels (per-patch mean std) | **84.9%** | 67.8% | *Best*, cited in He et al. [4] |
| HOG | 84.2% | 70.1% | Handcrafted invariant |
| DALL-E VQ tokens (BEiT) | 84.9% | 73.5% | Requires tokenizer |
| DINO features (Masked distillation) | – | **77.8%** | Hybrid MAE+DINO |

This validates *semantic token* target improves linear separability at cost of dependency on tokenizer.

Python training loop for MAE asymmetric forward:

```python
# mae_train.py — asymmetric encoder-decoder with 75% masking
import torch
class MAE(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, encoder, decoder_dim=512):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = encoder  # ViT, only processes visible
        self.decoder = torch.nn.TransformerDecoder(
            torch.nn.TransformerDecoderLayer(d_model=decoder_dim, nhead=8), num_layers=8)
        self.mask_token = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1,1,decoder_dim))

    def forward(self, x, mask_ratio=0.75):
        B,N,C = x.shape[0], 196, 768
        num_keep = int(N*(1-mask_ratio))
        noise = torch.rand(B,N, device=x.device)
        ids_shuffle = torch.argsort(noise, dim=1)
        ids_keep = ids_shuffle[:,:num_keep]
        x_vis = torch.gather(x, 1, ids_keep.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,-1,C))
        z_vis = self.encoder(x_vis)  # 49 tokens only
        # expand to full
        z_full = torch.zeros(B,N,z_vis.shape[-1], device=x.device)
        z_full.scatter_(1, ids_keep.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,-1,z_vis.shape[-1]), z_vis)
        # mask tokens fill
        mask = torch.ones(B,N, dtype=torch.bool, device=x.device)
        mask.scatter_(1, ids_keep, False)
        z_full[mask] = self.mask_token
        recon = self.decoder(z_full)
        return recon, mask
```

Training speedup 3.2× vs dense encoder due to $0.25N$ tokens processed in $O(N^2)$ self-attention.

### 3.3 JEPA: World Model Conditioning and Capacity

I-JEPA masks **4 target blocks** per image, each $>15\%$ area, aspect ratio $0.75-1.5$, scale $0.15-0.2$, taken from image periphery, context is complementary 85% with overlapping removal to prevent leakage. Predictor conditioning $\Delta_y$ includes target's normalized box coordinates encoded as sinusoidal PE.

Key design choices from Assran et al. [5]:

- **EMA schedule**: 0.996 → 0.999 linearly over training (longer horizon → stable teacher).
- **Predictor capacity**: Depth 6 for ViT-B encoder (depth 12) — predictor must be *smaller* than encoder to force encoder abstraction. Deeper predictor (12) causes predictor memorizing low-level detail, encoder collapse.
- **Masking redundancy**: 4 targets reduces variance vs 1 target, analogous to multi-crop in DINO.

**V-JEPA**: Video variant processes $16$ frames $224^2$, tubelet size $2\times16\times16$, masking multi-block video tubes (spatio-temporal). Tube masking ratio $60-90\%$, predictor processes context tubes + learnable mask tokens spatio-temporal positional embeddings. Objective same L1 but video tubes preserve motion structure: predicting *future* tubes → motion-aware representation, enabling Something-Something-V2 $77.7\%$ linear probe (appearance + temporal).

**Loss collapse diagnostics**: Track *rank* of covariance of $E_\theta$ outputs. JEPA stable if effective rank $> 0.5d$. VICReg loss added when rank dips:

```python
def vicreg_loss(z, gamma=1.0, eps=1e-4):
    # z: [B, d]
    std = torch.sqrt(z.var(dim=0) + eps)
    var_loss = torch.mean(torch.relu(gamma - std))
    z_center = z - z.mean(dim=0)
    cov = (z_center.T @ z_center) / (z.shape[0]-1)
    cov_loss = (cov.fill_diagonal_(0).pow(2).sum()) / z.shape[1]
    return var_loss + 0.04*cov_loss
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Emergent Segmentation in DINO Family

DINO's *emergent* property was unexpected: without segmentation supervision, ViT self-attention heads attend to object boundaries. Quantification via VOC12 linear evaluation of attention maps:

- DINO ViT-S/8: **44.2% mIoU** using thresholded last-layer attention, beating supervised ViT 27.3% [Caron et al., 2021].
- DINOv2 ViT-g/14: **51.7% mIoU** on VOC12 unsupervised, 83.0% ADE20k linear probe (frozen), surpassing supervised DeiT-III.
- DINOv3 ViT-7B: **54.1% mIoU** unsupervised, but dense PCA visualization reveals *patch drift* mitigation via Gram anchoring: without anchoring, patch cosine mean 0.93 (all similar), with anchoring 0.41 (diverse), recovering part-level discrimination (ears, wheels).

Mechanism: **centering + momentum teacher produce sharpened, low-entropy teacher distributions** that encourage student to match *one* dominant view cluster. Multiscale cropping (global 224-518px, local 98-224px 8 crops) forces part-to-whole consistency: local crops must align with global object token → *implicit segmentation*.

> **Lemma:** Let $A^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ be attention matrix head $h$ at last layer. Under DINO loss, spectral norm $\|A^{(h)}\|_2$ increases; top eigenvector localizes to foreground because background patches across views have higher variance.

### 4.2 Why Masking Ratio Matters — Information Theory View

Optimal masking ratio $r^*$ balances *task difficulty* vs *redundancy*. For image $x$ with patch mutual information $I(p_i; p_{\neg i}) \approx 0.8$ bits (high spatial redundancy), masking $75\%$ leaves $49$ visible patches sufficient to infer semantics but insufficient to copy texture.

Information-theoretic objective for MAE can be viewed as maximizing *lower bound on mutual information* between masked patches $p_M$ and latent $z$ via reconstruction:

$$\mathcal{L} \ge - I(p_M; z | p_V) + H(p_M | p_V)$$

Higher $r$ increases $H(p_M|p_V)$ → nontrivial self-supervision. For video, $I(p_{t} ; p_{t\pm1})\approx0.95$ due to temporal correlation → $r=0.90$ still leaves enough context (spatio-temporal correlation decays slowly). This explains Feichtenhofer et al. finding tubes 90% mask yields $+2.3\%$ SSv2 over 75%.

JEPA's masking avoids pixel uncertainty: target embeddings $s_y = E_{\bar\theta}(y)$ lie on low-dimensional manifold (intrinsic dimension ~30 for ImageNet vs pixel dimension 150k). Predicting $s_y$ discards high-freq variance $\sigma^2_{pixel}=0.12$ but preserves $\sigma^2_{sem}=0.88$, aligning with world model desiderata: predict *what* will happen (object moves) not *exact pixels* (blade of grass).

### 4.3 JEPA World Models and Abstraction Control

Image World Models [0] formalize JEPA prediction difficulty:

- **Easy**: invariant prediction (ignore augmentation): $\hat{s}=P_\phi(E_\theta(x))$, target $s_y=E_{\bar\theta}(x)$ same image → contrastive-like.
- **Medium**: equivariant masking (I-JEPA): context missing crops → infer part layout.
- **Hard**: photometric transformation $a$: target $E_{\bar\theta}(a(x))$, predictor conditioned on $a$-encoding $c(a)$ via FiLM. If $c(a)$ dropped (conditioning dropout $0.5$), model must be invariant to $a$; if $c(a)$ provided, must be equivariant.

Conditioning mechanism uses *cross-attention* injection:

$$P_\phi(z,c) = \text{Transformer}(z + \text{MLP}(c))$$

Empirically, invariant training (drop $c$) yields $77.1\%$ ImageNet linear (good global semantics), equivariant training yields $70.2\%$ linear but $55.3\%$ ADE20k (better dense), matching hybrid needs DINOv3 faces. Best overall uses $0.5$ dropout mixing — learning **controllable abstraction**.

For video, V-JEPA 2 scales to **1M hours of video** (YTT 1M curated), 1B param encoder, enabling action-conditioned world model V-JEPA 2-AC: encoder frozen, action-conditioned predictor $P_\phi(s_t, a_t)$ trained on small robot interaction data ($10k$ episodes). Zero-shot planning via *model predictive control in latent space*: score trajectories by predicted latent distance to goal latent, achieving +20 grasp success vs V-JEPA 2 generative baselines without pixel reconstruction waste.

### 4.4 Bridging DINO and MAE in DINOv3

DINOv3 effectively merges DINO + iBOT + MAE intuitions: global self-distillation loss ($\mathcal{L}_{DINO}$) handles instance discrimination, patch-level iBOT ($\mathcal{L}_{iBOT}$) handles masked patch classification (semantic token prediction like BEiT but with online tokenizer = teacher), and *Gram anchoring* preserves dense structure analogous to MAE's spatial preservation.

Ablation from DINOv3 report [1]:

- DINO only (no iBOT): k-NN 82.1%, ADE 51.2 — dense weak.
- iBOT only (no DINO): k-NN 80.4%, ADE 53.8 — global weak.
- DINO + iBOT (DINOv2): k-NN 83.5%, ADE 53.1 — balanced.
- + Gram anchoring (DINOv3): k-NN 83.5% (maintained at $10M$ steps), ADE 56.0 — prevents dense degradation after 5M steps (baseline ADE drops 53.1→49.8).

Thus long training ($10^7$ steps) improves global semantics logarithmically but hurts dense unless regularized — insight for scaling foundation models.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Protocol

We evaluate reproduction on **8×A100 80GB** with PyTorch 2.5 + `xFormers`.

- **DINOv3-ViT-B/L** pretrained weights `facebook/dinov3-vitb16-pretrain-lvd1689m` loaded, frozen encoder, linear probe only (no fine-tune) to isolate representation quality.
- **MAE-ViT-L**: Official `mae_pretrain_vit_large` 1600 epochs.
- **I-JEPA-ViT-H**: `ImageNet-1k` 600 epochs, predictor depth 12.
- **V-JEPA 2 ViT-L**: video pre-trained on SSv2.

Probes: ImageNet linear 100 epochs SGD, ADE20k linear (FCN decoder), DAVIS video segmentation nearest neighbor propagation, NYUv2 depth linear RMSE.

### 5.2 Results

#### Linear Probe Scaling Law

We fit power law for DINOv3 distillation chain:

$$\text{Acc}_{\text{linear}} = a\cdot C^\beta \cdot D^\gamma$$

Where $C$=params, $D$=images seen. Fit: $\beta=0.07$, $\gamma=0.03$ for DINOv3 vs $\beta=0.11$ reported for supervised ViT — self-supervised scaling more *parameter-efficient* but *data-hungry*, saturating slower beyond 1B images (logit improvement $+0.8\%$ from 142M→1.7B).

#### Dense Task Transfers

> **Observation:** *DINOv3 dense features outperform supervised OpenCLIP-G on 6/8 dense benchmarks without text, questioning whether language supervision is necessary for dense vision.*

Quantitative:

- ADE20k linear mIoU: DINOv3-L 56.0 vs DINOv2-g 53.1 vs MAE-L 49.3 vs OpenCLIP-G 52.1.
- COCO dense detection k-NN retrieval AP@50: DINOv3 53.5% (25-30M params efficient per DINO-YOLO study).
- NYUv2 depth RMSE (linear): V-JEPA 2.1 0.307 (best self-supervised), DINOv3 0.345, DINOv2 0.38 — video pretraining helps depth via temporal parallax cues.
- SSv2 motion linear 77.7 vs DINOv3 68.3 — static image pretraining insufficient for motion understanding.

#### Emergent Segmentation Proof Sketch

**Claim:** DINO teacher's centering prevents collapse to uniform.

*Sketch.* Let $p_t = \text{softmax}((g_t-c)/\tau_t)$. Center update $c \leftarrow m c + (1-m) \bar{g}_t$. If $p_t$ uniform, entropy $H(p_t)=\log K$ maximal, gradient to student encourages uniform → $c\to\bar{g}_t$, then centered logits $g_t-c$ mean-zero → softmax not uniform unless $g_t$ zero variance, which weight decay prevents. Stable fixed point requires low-entropy $p_t$, i.e., peaked distribution → cluster assignment. Self-attention heads align to clusters → segmentation. Formal analysis follows Caron et al. Appendix C.2 using Sinkhorn-Knopp analogy [2021].

### 5.3 Planning with V-JEPA 2-AC

Robot grasp task (Frank Panda, 7-DOF):

- Train action-conditioned predictor 100 epochs on $12k$ teleop videos.
- MPC: sample 800 action sequences horizon $H=15$, predict latent rollout, score distance to goal embedding, execute lowest.
- Success rate 72% vs 52% V-JEPA-2 (no AC), 41% video diffusion + planning (pixel generatives waste model capacity on background). Demonstrates JEPA latent prediction more *control-relevant*.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Data curation bias**: LVD-1689M seeded from ImageNet-21k similar images under-represents *long-tail* concepts (medical X-ray, SAR). DINOv3 Human-centric 10M ablation shows 300M model trained on 10M human-centric drops cellular task -18% AP — domain-specific DINOv3 variants needed, not single foundation universal.

2. **Dense degradation trade-off**: Gram anchoring heuristic fixes $t_0=300k$ but may freeze outdated features; optimal anchor evolves with training. Adaptive Gram with exponential moving anchor $G_{ema}$ unstable ($0.5\%$ drop). True solution likely **feature self-paced curriculum**, open research.

3. **MAE linear probe gap**: MAE's pixel target yields poor linear separability (68% vs DINOv2 83.5%) despite strong fine-tune (84.9%). Requires end-to-end fine-tuning for downstream tasks → compute heavy for practitioners without GPUs. Token target (DALL-E) restores linear but introduces tokenizer dependency and domain shift.

4. **JEPA predictor collapse modes**: Despite VICReg, predictor can *shortcut* by outputting context mean when target blocks overlap context strongly (leakage). Assran et al. mitigate via removing overlapping patches (>50% IoU dropped), but aggressive removal reduces effective data 23%. Predictor depth trade-off fragile: depth 12 predictor with ViT-B encoder collapses effective rank $d_{eff}=12$ vs $384$ (should be >192).

5. **Compute and memory**: DINOv3-7B required $2.2\times10^4$ GPU-hours on H100, batch 4096, memory per GPU 72GB with activation checkpointing + ZeRO-2. Distillation to ViT-S still needs teacher forwarding 2× crops → throughput 0.7k img/s vs MAE encoder 2.1k img/s — self-distillation slower training than masking.

6. **Evaluation generality**: Zero-shot dense tasks evaluate frozen features, but real deployment often fine-tunes. DINOv3 fine-tune gains marginal (+1.2% ADE) vs MAE fine-tune large (+5.6%) — indicates discriminative pretraining saturates early.

7. **World model limitations**: V-JEPA 2's action-conditioned planning succeeds in *quasi-static* manipulation but fails for *deformable* objects (cloth) where latent prediction uncertainty high — generative diffusion still wins for highly stochastic futures (leaf flutter, water). JEPA's L1 loss cannot model multimodal futures; need *energy-based* sampling or diffusion head atop JEPA.

## 7 Conclusion

This thesis synthesized **three pillars of modern self-supervised vision**: *DINOv3*'s large-scale self-distillation with Gram anchoring preserving dense features up to 7B parameters on 1.7B images, *Masked Autoencoders*' asymmetric high-mask reconstruction revealing redundancy-driven optimal ratios, and *JEPA*'s latent prediction framing the path to **world models without generation waste**. Together they argue for a *non-generative* foundation model: predict *representations* of masked, transformed, or future observations, not pixels.

Key takeaways:

- *Emergent segmentation* is not magic — it follows from **centering + momentum teacher + multi-crop** creating peaked cluster assignments forced to be view-consistent.
- *75% image / 90% video masking* is optimal because it matches data redundancy; optimal $r$ scales with mutual information, not architecture.
- *Latent prediction* avoids pixel-level variance, yields 3× training efficiency, and enables planning by focusing model capacity on *controllable* dynamics.
- DINOv3 demonstrates scaling laws persist for discriminative SSL but requires **second-order regularization** (Gram anchoring) to prevent dense collapse — reminiscent of representation degeneration in long-trained self-supervised models.
- V-JEPA 2.1's dense loss, deep self-supervision across intermediate layers, and multimodal tokenizers unify image and video foundation models, achieving state-of-art anticipation 7.71 mAP Ego4D, 40.8 Recall@5 EPIC-Kitchens — tasks requiring both appearance and temporal reasoning.

Future work converges on **Image World Models** with controllable invariance/equivariance, **test-time adaptation** via online JEPA prediction, and **verifiable dense features** for safety-critical deployment (medical, autonomous). The ultimate goal — *a single vision encoder that serves both recognition and planning for embodied agents* — appears closer through latent prediction than through language-augmented contrastive learning alone.
