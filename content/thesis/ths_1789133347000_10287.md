# Flow-Based Generative Models: Normalizing Flows, the Change-of-Variables Formula, and Exact Likelihood Estimation

## Abstract

Flow-based generative models occupy a distinctive position in deep generative modeling: they construct complex probability distributions by pushing a tractable base measure through a composition of invertible transformations, yielding exact likelihood evaluation through the change-of-variables formula. This thesis develops a rigorous treatment of normalizing flows, beginning with the measure-theoretic foundations of probability transport and the analytic requirements of diffeomorphic warps. We trace the architectural lineage from additive coupling in NICE [1] through affine coupling in RealNVP [2] and invertible $1\times 1$ convolutions in Glow [3], to autoregressive flows including inverse autoregressive flow [4] and masked autoregressive flow [5], and finally to continuous-time flows defined by neural ordinary differential equations [6] and FFJORD's unbiased stochastic trace estimator [7]. Central to the analysis is the tension between Jacobian-determinant tractability — achieved through triangular, contractive, or instantaneous-trace structure — and expressive power, formalized through universality results and topology-preservation theorems [8]. We derive exact likelihood objectives, survey density-estimation benchmarks, and characterize the fundamental limitations of invertible architectures.

---

## 1. Introduction

Deep generative modeling confronts a trilemma. Variational autoencoders [9] offer stable training but only *lower bounds* on likelihood; generative adversarial networks [10] produce sharp samples but no density estimate and suffer training instability; autoregressive models [11] deliver exact likelihoods but sample sequentially at $\mathcal{O}(D)$ cost. **Flow-based models** occupy the remaining corner of this design space: they provide *exact* likelihood computation *and* one-pass parallel sampling, at the price of invertibility constraints on the architecture.

A **normalizing flow** is a composition $f = f_L \circ \cdots \circ f_1$ of invertible differentiable maps transforming a base random variable $\mathbf{z} \sim p_z$ (typically an isotropic Gaussian) into data $\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{z})$. The density of $\mathbf{x}$ follows from the *change-of-variables formula* of differential topology, which turns the abstract operation of "warping a distribution" into a computable quantity. This thesis is devoted to three questions:

1. *What mathematical machinery governs density transport under invertible maps?* (Section 3)
2. *How do architectural choices — coupling, autoregressive, residual, and continuous-time — reconcile tractability with expressiveness?* (Section 4)
3. *What are the fundamental limits of the framework: universality, topology preservation, and empirical performance?* (Sections 5–6)

Our running thesis is that the entire field reduces to a single computational bottleneck: **the Jacobian determinant**. Every architectural innovation in the literature is, at bottom, a strategy for making $\log |\det J_f|$ cheap while keeping the map rich enough to transport the base distribution onto the data manifold.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Density transport and probability warping

The idea that complex distributions can be generated by warping simple ones predates deep learning. Tabak and Turner [12] introduced *density estimation by transport*, iteratively applying parametric maps that minimize the Kullback–Leibler divergence to a reference density; Tabak and Vanden-Eijnden [13] formalized the one-dimensional construction. Normalizing flows lift this program to high dimensions with neural networks as the warping machinery.

### 2.2 Generative modeling landscape

| Model family | Exact likelihood | Parallel sampling | Latent structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normalizing flows | ✅ yes | ✅ yes | bijective, interpretable |
| Variational autoencoders | ❌ ELBO only | ✅ yes | stochastic posterior |
| GANs | ❌ none | ✅ yes | uninvertible |
| Autoregressive models | ✅ yes | ❌ sequential | none |

This comparison (cf. [8]) explains the field's motivation: flows are the only family simultaneously permitting exact likelihood evaluation, unbiased ancestral sampling, and tractable density queries — properties essential for scientific applications such as molecular simulation, anomaly detection, and variational inference [14].

### 2.3 The computational bottleneck

For a general neural network $f : \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$, computing $\det J_f$ costs $\mathcal{O}(D^3)$ — prohibitive for $D \sim 10^5$ (images) or $D \sim 10^6$. The field's architectures are therefore characterized by their *determinant trick*:

- **Coupling layers** (NICE [1], RealNVP [2], Glow [3]): triangular Jacobians → determinant is the product of the diagonal, $\mathcal{O}(D)$.
- **Autoregressive flows** (IAF [4], MAF [5]): triangular Jacobians with full conditioning → $\mathcal{O}(D)$ but asymmetric forward/inverse costs.
- **Contractive residual flows** (i-ResNet [14], Residual Flow [15]): Lipschitz-bounded residuals → log-determinant via unbiased truncated power series.
- **Continuous flows** (Neural ODE [6], FFJORD [7]): ODE-defined dynamics → determinant replaced by the *trace* of the Jacobian, estimable in $\mathcal{O}(D)$ with Hutchinson's estimator.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The change-of-variables formula

> **Theorem:** Let $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ be a random variable with density $p_z$, and let $f : \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ be a $C^1$-diffeomorphism. Then $\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{z})$ has density
>
> $$p_x(\mathbf{x}) = p_z(f^{-1}(\mathbf{x})) \cdot \left| \det \frac{\partial f^{-1}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \right|.$$

Equivalently, evaluating the *forward* map $\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{z})$ gives the log-likelihood used in training:

$$\log p_x(\mathbf{x}) = \log p_z(\mathbf{z}) + \log \left| \det \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{z}} \right|, \qquad \mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{z}).$$

For a composition of $L$ layers, the chain rule yields an *additive* decomposition — the log-determinants sum layerwise:

$$\log p_x(\mathbf{x}) = \log p_z(\mathbf{z}_0) + \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \log \left| \det J_{f_\ell}(\mathbf{z}_{\ell-1}) \right|, \quad \mathbf{z}_\ell = f_\ell(\mathbf{z}_{\ell-1}).$$

This decomposition is the workhorse of the entire field: it reduces global density evaluation to *local* determinant computations, enabling maximum-likelihood training by straightforward backpropagation [8].

### 3.2 Triangular Jacobians: the coupling construction

The coupling layer [1, 2] partitions $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ into $(\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{x}_b)$ with $|\mathbf{x}_a| = d < D$ and applies:

$$\mathbf{y}_a = \mathbf{x}_a \odot \exp(s(\mathbf{x}_b)) + t(\mathbf{x}_b), \qquad \mathbf{y}_b = \mathbf{x}_b,$$

where $s, t : \mathbb{R}^{D-d} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ are arbitrary neural networks. Because $\mathbf{x}_b$ passes through unchanged, the Jacobian is lower-triangular:

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} \mathrm{diag}(\exp(s(\mathbf{x}_b))) & \star \\ 0 & I_{D-d} \end{pmatrix},$$

so $\log |\det J| = \sum_{i=1}^d s_i(\mathbf{x}_b)$ — computable in $\mathcal{O}(D)$. Crucially, the inverse requires no network inversion: $\mathbf{x}_a = (\mathbf{y}_a - t(\mathbf{y}_b)) \odot \exp(-s(\mathbf{y}_b))$. The network $s, t$ never needs to be invertible; only the coupling *transform* does. The degenerate case $s \equiv 0$ recovers the *additive* coupling of NICE [1], whose Jacobian has unit determinant and is therefore *volume-preserving*.

### 3.3 Likelihood-based training objective

Given i.i.d. data $\{\mathbf{x}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^N$, parameters $\theta$ of all layers are learned by minimizing the negative log-likelihood:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \left[ \log p_z(f_\theta^{-1}(\mathbf{x}^{(n)})) + \log \left| \det J_{f_\theta^{-1}}(\mathbf{x}^{(n)}) \right| \right].$$

For discrete data (e.g., image pixels in $\{0,\dots,255\}$), a continuous model cannot assign density directly; standard practice adds *dequantization* noise $\mathbf{u} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[0,1)^D$ to obtain $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{u}$ [16], or learns a variational dequantizer [17].

### 3.4 Implementation of a coupling layer

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class AffineCoupling(nn.Module):
    """RealNVP-style affine coupling layer with triangular Jacobian."""
    def __init__(self, net: nn.Module, mask: torch.Tensor):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = net          # predicts (s, t) from masked input
        self.mask = mask        # binary mask selecting the pass-through half

    def forward(self, x):
        xb = x * self.mask                      # unchanged part
        s, t = self.net(xb).chunk(2, dim=1)
        s = torch.tanh(s)                       # stabilize scale factor
        xa = x * (1 - self.mask)
        ya = xa * torch.exp(s) + t              # affine coupling
        y = ya + xb                             # yb = xb
        logdet = (s * (1 - self.mask)).sum(dim=1)
        return y, logdet

    def inverse(self, y):
        yb = y * self.mask
        s, t = self.net(yb).chunk(2, dim=1)
        s = torch.tanh(s)
        ya = y * (1 - self.mask)
        xa = (ya - t) * torch.exp(-s)
        x = xa + yb
        return x
```

Alternating the mask between layers ensures every dimension is transformed over the depth of the flow [2].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Coupling flows: from NICE to Glow

NICE [1] introduced the additive coupling layer, establishing that exact-likelihood training and unbiased ancestral sampling could coexist. RealNVP [2] generalized to affine coupling and the multi-scale architecture (squeezing spatial dimensions, factoring out half the channels at each scale). **Glow** [3] then identified the coupling layer's weakness: channel permutations (the reversal used by RealNVP) are fixed, limiting mixing. Glow's remedy was threefold:

1. **ActNorm** — a per-channel affine layer with *data-dependent initialization* (scale and bias set so the first minibatch has zero mean, unit variance), stabilizing very deep flows where batch normalization fails under small per-GPU batches.
2. **Invertible $1\times 1$ convolution** — a learned permutation of channels parameterized as $\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{U}$ (or via the matrix exponential), with $\log |\det \mathbf{W}|$ computed once from the weight matrix.
3. **Affine coupling** with a shallower but wider conditioner.

Glow achieved $3.35$ bits/dim on CIFAR-10 and generated $256 \times 256$ faces at quality rivaling GANs of the era [3], and its open implementation catalyzed the field's growth.

### 4.2 Autoregressive flows: IAF and MAF

An autoregressive flow [4, 5] transforms each coordinate conditioned on all previous ones:

$$x_i = \mu_i(\mathbf{z}_{<i}) + \sigma_i(\mathbf{z}_{<i}) \cdot z_i, \qquad i = 1,\dots,D,$$

with conditioner networks masked to enforce causality. The Jacobian is triangular by construction, so $\log |\det J| = \sum_i \log \sigma_i$ — yet the transformation can represent arbitrarily complex conditionals, and *every* autoregressive density model is a single-layer autoregressive flow [8]. The critical asymmetry:

- **IAF** (Kingma et al. [4]): the *forward* map $\mathbf{z} \to \mathbf{x}$ is parallel (sampling is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ depth), while inversion $\mathbf{x} \to \mathbf{z}$ is sequential — ideal as a flexible posterior in VAEs.
- **MAF** (Papamakarios et al. [5]): the *inverse* map $\mathbf{x} \to \mathbf{z}$ is parallel (density evaluation is fast), while sampling is sequential — ideal for density estimation and anomaly detection.

This duality mirrors the teacher-forcing/prior-sampling asymmetry of classical autoregressive models, now unified under the flow formalism [11].

### 4.3 Continuous normalizing flows and FFJORD

Chen et al. [6] observed that a residual network $\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = \mathbf{z}_t + f(\mathbf{z}_t)$ is an Euler discretization of the ODE $\frac{d\mathbf{z}}{dt} = f_\theta(\mathbf{z}, t)$. Passing to the continuum replaces the discrete determinant chain with the **instantaneous change-of-variables formula**:

$$\frac{d}{dt} \log p(\mathbf{z}(t)) = -\mathrm{tr}\left( \frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial \mathbf{z}(t)} \right),$$

so the log-density follows by integrating a scalar quantity along the trajectory — no matrix factorizations at all. Grathwohl et al. [7] (FFJORD) made this scalable: Hutchinson's identity $\mathrm{tr}(A) = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}[\epsilon^\top A \epsilon]$ for $\epsilon$ with zero mean and unit covariance turns the $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ trace into an $\mathcal{O}(D)$ vector–Jacobian product estimated in a single backward pass, permitting *completely unrestricted* architectures. The adjoint sensitivity method [6] supplies memory-efficient gradients by solving a second, backward ODE rather than backpropagating through the solver's operations.

### 4.4 Universality and topology preservation

Two opposing results circumscribe flow expressiveness. On the positive side, stacked affine coupling layers with sufficiently expressive conditioners are *universal*: they can approximate any target density in total variation under mild regularity conditions, with universality also established for spline-based couplings [8]. On the negative side, flows are *diffeomorphisms* and therefore preserve topology — a connected Gaussian cannot be homeomorphically mapped onto a target with disconnected support, nor can a flow change the number of modes separated by zero-density regions without letting the Lipschitz constant diverge [8]. Cornish et al. showed that relaxing exact bijectivity (e.g., surjective or stochastic layers) is necessary to cross these topological barriers, at the cost of exact likelihood.

### 4.5 Beyond the standard recipes

The field has since diversified: **spline flows** replace affine couplings with monotonic rational-quadratic splines for greater per-layer flexibility [8]; **i-ResNet** [14] and **Residual Flow** [15] enforce contractivity ($\mathrm{Lip}(g) < 1$ in $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x} + g(\mathbf{x})$) and evaluate $\log |\det(I + J_g)|$ via a Russian-roulette-truncated power series; **Flow++** [17] adds variational dequantization and MixLogCDF couplings; **neural spline** and **surjective** flows trade exactness for expressiveness. Each is, in our framing, a different answer to the same determinant-tractability question.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Density estimation benchmarks

Bits-per-dimension (lower is better) on standard image benchmarks, as compiled in the Residual Flow study [15]:

| Model | MNIST | CIFAR-10 | ImageNet 32×32 | ImageNet 64×64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RealNVP [2] | 1.06 | 3.49 | 4.28 | 3.98 |
| Glow [3] | 1.05 | 3.35 | 4.09 | 3.81 |
| FFJORD [7] | 0.99 | 3.40 | — | — |
| Flow++ [17] | — | 3.29 | 3.86 | 3.69 |
| Residual Flow [15] | 0.97 | 3.29 | 4.02 | 3.78 |

Three observations emerge. First, continuous flows (FFJORD) match discrete coupling flows with far fewer parameters, since the ODE vector field is *shared* across "depth" [6]. Second, variational dequantization [17] accounts for most of Flow++'s margin over Glow — a reminder that likelihood numbers on discrete data are dominated by the dequantization model. Third, progress has been steady but asymptotic: five years of architecture search compressed CIFAR-10 from 3.49 to 3.29 bits/dim, suggesting the invertibility constraint — not optimization — is the binding limitation.

### 5.2 A proof sketch: universality of affine coupling flows

> **Theorem:** Let $p^\star$ be a continuous density on a compact $K \subset \mathbb{R}^D$ bounded away from zero. For any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a finite composition of affine coupling layers whose pushforward density $p$ satisfies $d_{TV}(p, p^\star) < \varepsilon$.

*Sketch.* Affine coupling layers with universal conditioner networks can approximate any *triangular transport map* — the Knothe–Rosenblatt rearrangement, which pushes any absolutely continuous source onto any absolutely continuous target by matching one-dimensional conditionals sequentially [8]. Since a triangular map is itself a composition of coordinate-wise bijections each conditioning on previous coordinates, and each such bijection can be approximated by an affine coupling layer with a universal $s, t$, the composition approximates the rearrangement arbitrarily well. Convergence of the maps in a suitable topology implies convergence of the pushforward densities in total variation. ∎

The practical caveat: the theorem is *existential* — it bounds neither the required depth nor the conditioner capacity, and the approximating sequence may require exponentially many layers in $D$.

### 5.3 Exactness as an experimental instrument

Because likelihoods are exact, flows serve as measurement instruments rather than merely generative models:

1. **Anomaly detection**: $\log p_x(\mathbf{x})$ is a calibrated outlier score; MAF-based detectors achieve competitive AUROC on tabular benchmarks without labels [5].
2. **Variational inference**: flows parameterize flexible posteriors $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$ in VAEs, tightening the ELBO beyond diagonal Gaussians [4, 14].
3. **Physical simulation**: Boltzmann generators use flows to sample molecular conformations with exact reweighting via the change-of-variables formula, correcting for bias [8].
4. **Compression**: bits-back coding with flows attains near-entropy compression rates, since $-\log p_x(\mathbf{x})$ is the exact code length [11].

## 6. Limitations

No framework is without cost, and flows pay for exactness in several currencies:

- **Architectural straitjacket.** Every layer must be analytically invertible with a cheap determinant. This rules out the most expressive primitives of deep learning — attention, arbitrary dense maps, max-pooling — and forces the triangular/contractive/ODE contortions surveyed in Section 4.
- **Depth and compute.** Coupling flows need dozens of layers to match the expressiveness of a single unrestricted network; Glow used $K=32, L=3$ flow steps on CIFAR-10 [3]. Continuous flows trade depth for *solver evaluations*: adaptive ODE solvers may take hundreds of function evaluations per likelihood query, and training instability (stiff dynamics) is a persistent issue [7].
- **Topological barriers.** As noted in Section 4.4, diffeomorphisms cannot connect or disconnect support; multimodal targets with well-separated modes are poorly served, and workarounds (mixture base distributions, surjective layers) sacrifice exactness [8].
- **Discrete and structured data.** Dequantization is a hack — it inflates the modeling problem with artificial noise — and flows offer no native treatment of text, graphs, or sets, where autoregressive models remain dominant [11].
- **Likelihood–sample-quality mismatch.** Flows optimize exact likelihood, yet likelihood correlates imperfectly with perceptual quality: a model can assign high likelihood to blurry images. The field's slow sample-quality progress relative to diffusion models and GANs reflects this objective mismatch [15].

---

## 7. Conclusion

Normalizing flows realize an elegant mathematical idea — *probability as a transportable substance, carried by diffeomorphisms and measured by Jacobian determinants* — with unusual fidelity to its theory. From the additive couplings of NICE [1] through the invertible convolutions of Glow [3], the autoregressive duality of IAF and MAF [4, 5], and the continuous dynamics of Neural ODEs and FFJORD [6, 7], each generation has attacked the same bottleneck: computing $\log |\det J|$ without surrendering expressive power. The framework's enduring contribution may be less the specific architectures than the demonstration that exact likelihood, efficient sampling, and latent interpretability can coexist — a proof of possibility that continues to inform diffusion models, whose probability-flow ODEs are continuous normalizing flows in all but name [6]. The open frontier is the exactness–expressiveness frontier itself: surjective and stochastic generalizations that preserve the change-of-variables spirit while escaping the topological prison of bijectivity.
