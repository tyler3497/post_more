# The Gradual Guarantee: Blame Calculus, Cast Semantics, and Sound Interoperation of Typed and Untyped Code

## Abstract

This thesis unifies three pillars of sound typed-untyped interoperation: cast semantics, blame tracking, and the gradual guarantee. From Siek and Taha's gradual typing [1] — consistency relation, dynamic type, cast insertion — we develop Wadler and Findler's blame calculus [2], which labels casts and proves well-typed programs can't be blamed via a decomposition of subtyping into positive and negative components. We analyze space-efficient cast representations — Henglein-style coercions [3][4] and threesome factorization — culminating in their unification with blame [5]. We contrast guarded, transient, and monotonic dynamic semantics in Reticulated Python [6], formalize the gradual guarantee of Siek, Vitousek, Cimini, and Boyland [7] as the correctness criterion for migration, and survey performance evidence from Typed Racket's overhead crisis [8] to JIT-based recovery [9]. We argue blame safety, the gradual guarantee, and space efficiency jointly form the minimal contract of a gradual type system.

## 1. Introduction

Static typing catches errors early and enables optimization; dynamic typing permits rapid prototyping and idioms that fit no decidable discipline. For decades the choice looked binary. Gradual typing, introduced by Siek and Taha [1], rejects the dichotomy: a distinguished *dynamic* type `?` stands for "type unknown at compile time," type equality is replaced by a weaker *consistency* relation, and runtime *casts* enforce the boundaries between statically known and dynamically unknown regions. A prototype can begin fully untyped and migrate toward static typing one annotation at a time.

But safety alone is a weak promise. Early gradual systems said nothing about *which party* is at fault when a cast fails, nothing about whether *adding annotations can change behavior*, and nothing about the *space behavior* of accumulating wrappers. Three research lines closed these gaps, and this thesis is organized around them:

1. **Blame.** Findler and Felleisen's higher-order contracts [10] allocate *blame* to the party violating a contract. Wadler and Findler [2] internalized blame in the cast calculus, proving blame always falls on the *less precisely typed* side — *well-typed programs can't be blamed*.
2. **The gradual guarantee.** Siek, Vitousek, Cimini, and Boyland [7] proposed that programs differing only in annotation precision must behave identically, except that more precision may raise *more* dynamic type errors — a checkable design criterion.
3. **Space efficiency.** Herman, Tomb, and Flanagan [3] showed naive proxies accumulate without bound and destroy tail recursion; coercion-based representations bound space. Siek, Wadler, and colleagues [5] unified blame with coercions.

Section 2 surveys background; Section 3 describes methodology; Section 4 gives the technical core; Section 5 presents proofs and empirical results; Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Before gradual typing

Thatte's *quasi-static typing* [11] mixed static and dynamic checking but did not guarantee fully annotated programs to be statically error-free. Anderson and Drossopoulou built a nominal OO system with optional annotations [12]; the *soft typing* tradition inserted runtime checks into dynamic programs instead. Two 2006 efforts independently reached the modern formulation: Tobin-Hochstadt and Felleisen's *interlanguage migration* [13], which became Typed Racket [14] — typed and untyped modules interoperating through generated contracts with blame on untyped code; and Matthews and Findler's operational semantics for *multi-language programs* [15]. Flanagan's *hybrid type checking* [16], implemented in Sage [17], combined static checking with dynamic contracts for refinements. Siek and Taha [1] distinguished themselves by *micro* granularity — the expression, not the module, is the unit of interoperation — and by a clean metatheory with a "pay-as-you-go" theorem.

### 2.2 Contracts and blame

Findler and Felleisen [10] introduced higher-order contracts with *blame*: at higher order, a violated argument contract blames the caller (negative), a violated result contract blames the function (positive). Tobin-Hochstadt and Felleisen [13] proved contract violations are always blamed on untyped code, via heavy operational-equivalence arguments. Wadler and Findler [2] put blame labels directly into the cast calculus, so blame safety follows from standard progress-and-preservation [18].

### 2.3 The performance crisis

Sound gradual typing proved catastrophically slow in practice. Takikawa et al. [8] measured Typed Racket across the full lattice of typed/untyped configurations and found extreme, unpredictable slowdowns — worst cases exceeding two orders of magnitude — from contract wrappers at module boundaries. Responses included transient semantics [6], nominal designs [19], tracing-JIT recovery [9], and Shallow Racket's weaker enforcement [20].

## 3. Methodology

Our investigation is formal and synthetic: we reconstruct the central calculi, state theorems precisely, and evaluate designs against the refined criteria of [7]. Empirical claims report published measurements.

### 3.1 The gradually typed lambda calculus

The object language is STLC plus the dynamic type: $\tau ::= \iota \mid \tau \to \tau \mid ?$. Static typing uses the *consistency* relation $\sim$: $\tau \sim \tau$; $?\sim \tau$ and $\tau \sim ?$; structural congruence otherwise. Consistency is reflexive and symmetric but **not transitive** — `Int ~ ?` and `? ~ Bool`, yet `¬(Int ~ Bool)`. Non-transitivity is load-bearing: it stops `?` from collapsing all distinctions. Elaboration translates surface terms into a *cast calculus* with explicit casts $\langle \tau_2 \Leftarrow \tau_1 \rangle^\ell\, e$, $\ell$ a blame label, inserted wherever typing appeals to consistency between distinct types.

### 3.2 Proof techniques

Soundness is proved over small-step reduction on the cast calculus: *progress* (a well-typed term is a value, reduces, or raises blame) plus *preservation* [18], with blame as a terminal outcome. Blame safety follows from an invariant relating labels to precision [2]. The gradual guarantee is proved by simulation over the precision relation $\sqsubseteq$, mechanized for the GTLC [7].

### 3.3 Evaluation designs

Takikawa et al. [8] evaluate all $2^n$ typed/untyped configurations of $n$-module programs — the "lattice" methodology, refined by Greenman et al. [21]. Vitousek et al. [6] compare guarded, transient, and monotonic semantics on third-party Python programs.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cast insertion and the consistency relation

Casting a function cannot inspect its future behavior, so higher-order casts *wrap* the function in a proxy:

```haskell
-- surface: untyped function used at a precise type
f :: ? -> ? ;  g :: Int -> Int
g = \y -> (f y) + 1
-- elaborated: casts made explicit
g = \y -> ((<Int -> Int <= ? -> ?>^l1 f) y) + 1
```

Calls through the proxy cast each argument `Int → ?` (an upcast, infallible) and each result `? → Int` (a downcast, possibly raising blame `l1`). This is the *guarded* semantics: higher-order invariants enforced lazily, per use, via wrappers. Three properties matter:

- **Consistency is not transitive.** Any "plausibility" phase making it transitive would admit statically nonsensical programs.
- **Precision.** The order $\sqsubseteq$ has `? ⊑ τ` for all `τ` and is structural otherwise; it lifts to terms ($e \sqsubseteq e'$ when $e'$ refines annotations of $e$).
- **Pay-as-you-go.** Fully annotated programs elaborate to cast-free terms.

> **Theorem (Pay-as-you-go [1]).** If $e$ is fully statically typed, its elaboration contains no casts and behaves as in STLC.

### 4.2 The blame calculus: positive, negative, and naive subtyping

The blame calculus [2] gives each cast label a polarity: positive blame ($+\ell$) falls on the *term inside* the cast; negative blame ($-\ell$) on the *context*. A failing first-order cast reduces to `blame ℓ`; higher-order casts distribute the label, flipping polarity on the domain:

$$\langle \tau_1' \to \tau_2' \Leftarrow \tau_1 \to \tau_2 \rangle^\ell\, v \longrightarrow \lambda x.\, \langle \tau_2' \Leftarrow \tau_2 \rangle^{+\ell}\, (v\, (\langle \tau_1 \Leftarrow \tau_1' \rangle^{-\ell}\, x))$$

The core contribution is the *decomposition of subtyping* into positive subtyping ($\tau <:^+ \sigma$, "no positive blame possible") and negative subtyping ($\tau <:^- \sigma$), which recombine into *naive* subtyping — the precision order:

| Relation | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $\tau <:^+ \sigma$ | cast $\tau \Rightarrow \sigma$ never raises $+\ell$ | `Int <:^+ ?` holds; `? <:^+ Int` fails |
| $\tau <:^- \sigma$ | cast $\tau \Rightarrow \sigma$ never raises $-\ell$ | `? <:^- Int` holds; `Int <:^- ?` fails |
| $\tau \sqsubseteq \sigma$ (naive) | $\tau$ less precise than $\sigma$ | `? ⊑ Int`, `(?->?) ⊑ (Int->Int)` |

A cast from less to more precise type can raise only *positive* blame — blaming the less-precise term inside. Hence:

> **Theorem (Blame safety [2]).** If $e \longrightarrow^* \text{blame }\ell$, then $\ell$ labels a cast whose less precisely typed side is the term, never the context. *Well-typed programs can't be blamed.*

The proof's novelty is methodological: naive subtyping internalizes the blame invariant, so Wright–Felleisen progress/preservation suffices — no operational-equivalence machinery as in [13]. The calculus also unifies untyped, simply typed, and refinement-typed code, allocating blame by *relative precision* rather than a binary typed/untyped split.

### 4.3 Space efficiency: coercions, threesomes, and blame together

Guarded semantics has a space defect: mutually recursive functions in tail position across a typed-untyped boundary accumulate one proxy per call — linear space growth, tail calls degraded [3]. Herman, Tomb, and Flanagan replaced proxies with *coercions* (after Henglein [22]): a first-order runtime representation with a *composition* operation merging adjacent coercions. Coercion size is bounded by the types involved, independent of boundary crossings; tail calls stay tail calls; and composition fails *eagerly*, detecting some errors earlier than proxies.

A related factorization [23]: any cast — a *twosome* $(\tau_1 \Rightarrow \tau_2)$ — splits into a downcast to an intermediate type and an upcast: a *threesome* $(\tau_1 \Rightarrow^g \tau_2)$ with $g = \tau_1 \sqcap \tau_2$ the greatest lower bound under precision. Chains of threesomes collapse by taking greatest lower bounds — another bounded representation. With blame labels, failures map back to the offending source twosome.

The capstone [5] unifies all three: a calculus with blame safety, space efficiency, and eager error detection, plus full-abstraction results from which the "Fundamental Property of Casts" follows as a corollary:

| Approach | Representation | Space | Blame | Error timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive proxies [1] | wrappers | unbounded | none | lazy |
| Coercions [3] | composed coercions | bounded | none | eager |
| Threesomes [23] | glb-factorized casts | bounded | yes | eager |
| Blame + coercion [5] | labeled coercions | bounded | yes | eager |

### 4.4 The gradual guarantee and the refined criteria

By 2015 "gradual typing" had diluted into a buzzword. Siek, Vitousek, Cimini, and Boyland [7] drew "a crisp line in the sand": three *refined criteria* plus the **gradual guarantee**:

1. **Conservative extension** of a static discipline — fully annotated programs behave as in the static language.
2. **Embedding of the dynamic language** — erasing annotations preserves untyped behavior.
3. **The gradual guarantee** — precision must not change behavior except by introducing errors.

> **Theorem (Gradual guarantee [7]).** *Static half:* if $e_1 \sqsubseteq e_2$ and $\vdash e_1 : \tau_1$, then $\vdash e_2 : \tau_2$ with $\tau_2 \sqsubseteq \tau_1$ — adding precision never breaks typeability. *Dynamic half:* if $e_1 \sqsubseteq e_2$ and $e_1 \longrightarrow^* v_1$, then $e_2 \longrightarrow^* v_2$ with $v_1 \sqsubseteq v_2$ — adding precision can turn successes into cast errors, but never errors into successes, and never changes which value is produced.

This is what makes *gradual migration* coherent engineering: annotating a working program cannot silently change its success-case behavior. Siek et al. mechanized the proof for the GTLC and surveyed the literature, finding designs — e.g. Thorn's "like types" [24] — that *fail* the guarantee, trading it for performance. Blame and the guarantee complement each other: the guarantee constrains *which behaviors* precision may change; blame safety constrains *who is responsible* when the change is an error.

### Interlude: guarded, transient, monotonic

Reticulated Python [6] implements one static discipline with three dynamic semantics:

- **Guarded**: proxies enforce full higher-order invariants. Sound and blame-correct, but slow without coercions.
- **Transient**: casts check only *first-order* shapes at boundaries; use sites inside typed code re-check shallow properties. No proxies, near-untyped speed — but blame is lost and higher-order violations can escape typed regions.
- **Monotonic**: objects are permanently locked to their most precise observed type. Sound for mutable state, but non-compositional.

> **Observation [6].** Transient semantics runs within a small constant factor of untyped Python; guarded semantics is orders of magnitude slower on higher-order-heavy code. Full soundness has a real, boundary-concentrated price.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof summary

Four proved pillars: (1) **soundness** of the cast calculus — progress plus preservation with blame terminal [2][5]; (2) **blame safety** — blame always on the less precise side [2]; (3) **space efficiency** — coercion size bounded independently of boundary crossings, tail calls preserved [3], with threesome collapse as an alternative [23]; (4) **the gradual guarantee**, mechanized for the GTLC [7]. A fifth result — *full abstraction* between the blame calculus, coercion calculus, and STLC [5] — makes the Fundamental Property of Casts a corollary.

### 5.2 Performance evidence

Takikawa et al. [8] (POPL 2016): across the full configuration lattice of Typed Racket benchmarks, partially typed configurations show extreme, unpredictable overheads — worst cases over two orders of magnitude versus untyped code — concentrated at boundaries, where Racket's chaperones and impersonators wrap values per crossing. Two responses: **Pycket** (Bauman et al., OOPSLA 2017 [9]), a tracing JIT via RPython eliminating *over 90%* of the overhead with hidden-class-optimized contract structures — the costs are implementation artifacts, not fundamental; and **Nom** (Muehlboeck & Tate, OOPSLA 2017 [19]), a nominal system checking large structures with single O(1) checks, under 10% worst-case overhead at the cost of structural expressiveness. Vitousek et al. [6] add the transient data point: shallow checks recover near-untyped performance while abandoning blame. Greenman et al. [21] systematized the lattice methodology (JFP 2019).

### 5.3 Typed Racket in the field

Typed Racket [14] is the largest deployed experiment: ecosystem-wide module-by-module migration, contracts generated at every boundary, blame assigned to untyped modules. Experience confirms the theory — blame lands where expected — and its costs: the [8] lattice was measured on this system, and Shallow Racket [20] (transient-style, weaker soundness) was the direct response.

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

**Open programs.** The guarantee quantifies over closed programs differing only in annotations; real migration crosses open boundaries into libraries the programmer doesn't control. Compositional blame for open systems is underdeveloped.

**Blame usefulness.** "Less precise" can be an entire untyped module, and higher-order blame can bounce through wrappers before surfacing. Whether programmers can *act* on blame reports is barely studied empirically.

**Space ≠ time.** Coercions bound space, but crossings still allocate and compose; the [8] overheads are a time problem. Pycket [9] suggests tractability, but remains experimental.

**The guarantee as defeasible ideal.** Like types [24], transient checks [6], and Shallow Racket [20] deliberately weaken the guarantee for performance. The field hasn't settled whether it's a hard requirement or a defeasible ideal.

**Threats to the record.** The Takikawa lattice measures Racket's contract implementation as much as gradual typing; the Nom comparison [19] transliterates benchmarks across a structural-to-nominal paradigm shift; all timing claims are VM-sensitive. And the theorems are about core calculi — full languages with effects and concurrency can invalidate core-calculus properties.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Gradual typing grew from a simple bargain — annotate what you can, check the rest at runtime — into a deep research program. Its three load-bearing ideas: **casts** mediating the boundary with pay-as-you-go cost [1]; **blame** assigning every failure to the less precisely typed party, provable by progress/preservation once subtyping splits into positive and negative components [2]; and the **gradual guarantee** making annotation-adding behavior-preserving by theorem [7]. Around them: space-efficient coercions [3], blame-carrying threesomes [23], their unification [5], and the hard-won knowledge that soundness has a price [8] discountable — by JITs [9], nominal designs [19], or weakened semantics [6][20] — but not eliminable.

The minimal contract of a gradual type system, we argue, is exactly these three properties: *blame safety*, *the gradual guarantee*, and *space efficiency*. The open frontier is whether they survive contact with full-scale languages, optimizing compilers, and working programmers.
