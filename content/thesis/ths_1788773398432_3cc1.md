# TFHE Programmable Bootstrapping for Encrypted Neural Inference: Gate Bootstrapping, Functional Bootstrapping LUTs, and Deep Neural Networks over Encrypted Data

## Abstract

Fully homomorphic encryption over the torus (TFHE) occupies a distinctive position in the FHE landscape: rather than postponing noise growth to a rare, expensive bootstrapping event, it bootstraps *every* gate, achieving effectively unbounded multiplicative depth at the cost of making each bootstrapping operation fast — under 0.1 seconds per gate in the original construction [2]. The central primitive is the *blind rotation*, which homomorphically rotates an encrypted test polynomial by an encrypted amount, followed by sample extraction and key switching. This article develops the theory of TFHE from gate bootstrapping through *programmable bootstrapping* (PBS), in which the test polynomial encodes a lookup table (LUT) for an arbitrary univariate function, so that bootstrapping simultaneously refreshes noise and evaluates a nonlinear function for free [3]. We treat the negacyclicity constraint and its modern resolutions — partial-domain PBS, full-domain functional bootstrapping, PBS without padding (WoP-PBS), and multivalue PBS [4] — and show how these yield practical inference of discretized deep neural networks over encrypted data, as demonstrated in the programmable-bootstrapping neural networks of [3] and the Concrete ML framework of Zama [7]. Benchmarks, correctness theorems, parameter analysis, and limitations (precision, latency, key material) are presented, with the conclusion that PBS is the enabling primitive that converts TFHE from a Boolean-circuit engine into a general encrypted-computation substrate.

## 1 Introduction

Homomorphic encryption promises computation on data that remains encrypted end to end. The first-generation schemes — BGV, BFV, CKKS — are *leveled*: they tolerate a bounded multiplicative depth before noise overwhelms the plaintext, and bootstrapping, while possible, is an exceptional, costly event. TFHE, introduced by Chillotti, Gama, Georgieva and Izabachène [1][2], inverts this design philosophy. It works natively over the *real torus* $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, represents messages as small phases on the torus, and *bootstraps after every binary gate*. Because bootstrapping is the normal case rather than the exception, the scheme is optimized relentlessly for bootstrapping speed: the original gate bootstrapping runs in under 100 milliseconds [2], and modern implementations push a programmable bootstrapping (PBS) to tens of milliseconds [7].

The conceptual leap of this article's topic is the *programmable* bootstrapping [3]: the observation that the test polynomial rotated during bootstrapping need not encode a binary gate at all. It can encode a *lookup table* for any (negacyclic) univariate function $f$, so that the bootstrapping operation returns an encryption of $f(m)$ while simultaneously resetting noise. Nonlinearity — the bane of leveled FHE, where it must be approximated by low-degree polynomials — becomes *free*, evaluated as a side effect of the noise-refresh operation that would have been performed anyway.

This has a decisive consequence for machine learning over encrypted data. Neural network inference is dominated by nonlinear activations (ReLU, sigmoid, tanh, sign). In the CKKS lineage, these must be replaced by polynomial approximations of carefully bounded degree, trading accuracy against depth [5]. In TFHE, each activation is evaluated *exactly* (up to the message precision) by a single PBS. The result, demonstrated in [3] and productized in Zama's Concrete ML [7], is encrypted inference of deep discretized neural networks with accuracy matching the cleartext model.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the TFHE mathematical substrate: the torus, TLWE/TRLWE/TRGSW samples, and the gate bootstrapping procedure. Section 3 develops programmable bootstrapping formally: LUT encoding, the negacyclicity constraint, and its modern circumventions. Section 4 deep-dives into (i) the mechanics of blind rotation, (ii) the generalization from gates to arbitrary functions, (iii) handling non-negacyclic activations, and (iv) the compilation of discretized neural networks. Section 5 gives empirical evaluation and correctness proofs; Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The torus and (T)LWE samples

TFHE departs from integer-modulus schemes by working over the *real torus* $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, the additive group of reals modulo 1. A TLWE sample encrypting a message $\mu \in \mathbb{T}$ under a secret key $\mathbf{s} \in \{0,1\}^n$ is a pair

$$\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{T}^{n+1}, \qquad b = \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle + \mu + e,$$

where $\mathbf{a}$ is uniform and $e$ is a small Gaussian error. The *phase* of the sample is $\varphi_{\mathbf{s}}(\mathbf{c}) = b - \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle = \mu + e$; decryption rounds the phase to the nearest valid message encoding [1]. Messages are typically encoded as multiples of $1/2^p$ for small $p$, so the torus is partitioned into $2^p$ message "slots" separated by guard bands absorbing the noise.

The ring variant, TRLWE, replaces vectors by polynomials in $\mathbb{T}_N[X] = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$ with $N$ a power of two, and TRGSW (the torus analogue of GSW) encrypts a message times the gadget matrix. These structures support the two homomorphic operations on which everything rests: the *external product* $\boxdot$ between a TRGSW and a TRLWE sample, and the *CMux*, which homomorphically selects between two TRLWE samples according to an encrypted TRGSW bit:

$$\text{CMux}(C, d_1, d_0) \approx \begin{cases} d_1 & \text{if } C \text{ encrypts } 1, \ d_0 & \text{if } C \text{ encrypts } 0. \end{cases}$$

Noise growth of the external product is *asymmetric*: it scales with the noise of the TRGSW operand but only mildly with the TRLWE operand, which is why the bootstrapping key (an array of TRGSW encryptions of the LWE secret bits) can be used repeatedly without noise explosion [2].

### 2.2 Gate bootstrapping

Gate bootstrapping evaluates a single binary gate on encrypted bits while refreshing the ciphertext noise. Given a TLWE sample $\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a}, b)$ encrypting $x \cdot \mu$ for a bit $x \in \{0,1\}$ (or, more precisely, encrypting a message whose phase encodes $x$), and a bootstrapping key $\text{BK} = (\text{BK}_i)_{i \in [n]}$ with $\text{BK}_i \in \text{TRGSW}_S(s_i)$ encrypting each bit $s_i$ of the LWE secret under a ring key $S$, the procedure is [2]:

1. **Modulus switching.** Round $\bar{b} = \lfloor 2N \cdot b \rceil$ and $\bar{a}_i = \lfloor 2N \cdot a_i \rceil$ to integers in $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$. The scaled phase $\bar{\varphi} = \bar{b} - \sum_i \bar{a}_i s_i \bmod 2N$ encodes the message on a discrete circle of $2N$ positions.
2. **Blind rotation.** Form the *test polynomial* $\text{testv} = (1 + X + \cdots + X^{N-1}) \cdot X^{N/2} \cdot \hat{\mu} \in \mathbb{T}_N[X]$, where $\hat{\mu}$ encodes the desired output message $\mu$. Compute
   $$\text{ACC} = \text{BlindRotate}((0, \text{testv}), (\bar{a}_1, \ldots, \bar{a}_n, \bar{b}), \text{BK}),$$
   which homomorphically rotates the test polynomial by the *encrypted* amount $-\bar{\varphi}$, yielding a TRLWE encryption of $X^{-\bar{\varphi}} \cdot \text{testv}$.
3. **Sample extraction.** Extract the constant term: $\bar{\mathbf{c}} = (0, \hat{\mu}) + \text{SampleExtract}_0(\text{ACC})$, a TLWE sample under the ring key $S$ encrypting the output bit times $\mu$.
4. **Key switching.** Apply a key-switching key to return to a TLWE sample under the original key $\mathbf{s}$.

> **Theorem (Gate bootstrapping correctness [2]):** Let $\mathbf{c}$ encrypt $x \in \{0,1\}$ with noise amplitude below the rounding bound. Then the output of the gate bootstrapping with test polynomial encoding $\mu$ is a TLWE sample encrypting $x \cdot \mu$ with fresh noise independent of the input noise, provided the parameters satisfy the standard noise-propagation bounds.

The crucial property is *noise refresh*: the output noise depends only on the bootstrapping parameters, not on the input noise. Hence gates can be composed to *unbounded depth* — the defining advantage of TFHE over leveled schemes.

### 2.3 From CryptoNets to TFHE neural networks

Encrypted neural inference predates TFHE. CryptoNets [5] showed that neural networks could be evaluated on homomorphically encrypted data using a leveled scheme, achieving 98.95% accuracy on MNIST — but at roughly 250 seconds per inference and with activations replaced by the square function $x \mapsto x^2$, the only nonlinearity cheap in a leveled arithmetic scheme. This established the paradigm but also its ceiling: polynomial activations degrade trainability and expressivity.

TFHE removes the ceiling. Since a PBS evaluates an *arbitrary* univariate function during noise refresh, activations need not be polynomial at all. The price is discretization: PBS operates on a small message space (typically 4–8 bits), so networks must be quantized — a constraint that aligns well with the modern quantized-neural-network literature rather than fighting it [3].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The programmable bootstrapping, formally

Programmable bootstrapping generalizes the test polynomial. Let $f: \mathbb{T} \to \mathbb{T}$ be the target univariate function and let the input TLWE sample encrypt a message $m$ drawn from a discrete subset of the torus with $2^p$ values. Define the *LUT polynomial*

$$P_f = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} f_j X^j, \qquad f_j = f\!\left(\tfrac{j}{2N} \bmod \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\ \text{(with negacyclic extension)},$$

i.e., the coefficients of $P_f$ tabulate $f$ at the $2N$ discrete rotation positions, exploiting the negacyclicity $X^N = -1$ of the ring $\mathbb{T}_N[X]$. The PBS algorithm is then [3]:

```
Algorithm PBS(ct_in, P_f, BSK, KSK):
  # ct_in = LWEs(m): LWE encryption of message m (n+1 components)
  # 1. Modulus switching: torus -> Z_{2N}
  for i = 1..n+1:  ā_i = round(a_i * 2N)
  # 2. Blind rotation of the LUT
  ACC = BlindRotate( GLWE(P_f), (ā_1..ā_{n+1}), BSK )
  #    ACC encrypts X^{-φ̄} * P_f, where φ̄ = b̄ - Σ ā_i s_i
  # 3. Sample extraction of the constant term
  ct_out = SampleExtract(ACC, 0)     # encrypts f(m)
  # 4. Key switching back to the LWE key
  return KeySwitch(ct_out, KSK)
```

After blind rotation by $-\bar{\varphi}$ where $\bar{\varphi} \approx 2N \cdot m$, the constant term of $X^{-\bar{\varphi}} P_f$ is precisely the coefficient $f_{\bar{\varphi}} = f(m)$ (up to the discretization error of the modulus switch). Sample extraction moves this coefficient into a fresh LWE sample with refreshed noise. *The function evaluation costs nothing beyond the bootstrapping that noise management already required* — this is the economic miracle of PBS.

The following Python sketch illustrates LUT construction for a negacyclic function (e.g., the sign function used as a binarized activation):

```python
import numpy as np

def build_lut_polynomial(f, N, Delta_out):
    """Encode a negacyclic function f: Z_{2N} -> T as test polynomial coeffs."""
    coeffs = np.zeros(N)
    for j in range(N):
        # j indexes the first half-torus; negacyclicity handles the rest
        coeffs[j] = f(j / (2 * N)) * Delta_out
    # Positions N..2N-1 are implied by X^N = -1: f(x + 1/2) = -f(x)
    return coeffs

def sign_lut(x):
    return 1.0 if x >= 0.25 else -1.0   # negacyclic on the half-torus

P_sign = build_lut_polynomial(sign_lut, N=1024, Delta_out=1/8)
```

### 3.2 The negacyclicity constraint and its resolutions

The blind rotation operates in the negacyclic ring $\mathbb{T}_N[X] = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$, where rotation by $N$ positions *negates* the polynomial. Consequently, a single PBS can directly evaluate only *negacyclic* (antiperiodic) functions satisfying $f(x + \tfrac{1}{2}) = -f(x)$. Four approaches extend PBS beyond this restriction [4]:

| Approach | Idea | Cost | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partial-domain PBS | Restrict messages to $[0, \tfrac{1}{2})$; any $f$ works on half the torus | 1 PBS; loses 1 bit of precision (padding bit) | [3] |
| Full-domain FDFB | Decompose $f$ via Heaviside/sign: $g(x) = -f(x+\tfrac{1}{2})$ trick | 2 PBS + arithmetic | Kluczniak–Schild [4] |
| WoP-PBS | PBS *without padding*: generalized modulus switch + LWE multiplication | 1 PBS; no precision loss | [3] |
| Multivalue PBS | Evaluate several LUTs in one blind rotation (packing) | 1 PBS amortized over many LUTs | [4] |

The WoP-PBS construction of [3] is particularly significant for neural networks: by removing the padding-bit requirement, it recovers a full bit of precision, and its generalized framework supports *larger-precision* PBS (8+ bits) via chunking strategies — essential for quantized networks that need more than 4-bit activations.

### 3.3 Compiling discretized neural networks

A neural network layer computes $\mathbf{y} = \sigma(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$. Under TFHE, this maps to primitives as follows:

1. **Linear part** $W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$: evaluated with *leveled* homomorphic additions and scalar multiplications on LWE samples. Since weights are cleartext (model weights are typically public in the inference-as-a-service setting), this uses cheap cleartext–ciphertext products; noise grows linearly and stays within budget for the layer width.
2. **Activation** $\sigma$: one PBS per neuron, with the LUT encoding the (quantized, possibly negacyclic-transformed) activation function — ReLU via partial-domain PBS, sign/tanh via direct negacyclic LUTs, arbitrary activations via FDFB or WoP-PBS.
3. **Quantization**: weights and activations are quantized to $p$ bits during training (quantization-aware training), so the cleartext model and the encrypted evaluation agree bit-for-bit; no polynomial approximation of $\sigma$ is ever needed.

Zama's Concrete ML [7] automates this pipeline: it ingests scikit-learn or ONNX models, performs quantization-aware conversion, and emits TFHE circuits where every nonlinear operator becomes a PBS. The programmer writes ordinary ML code; the compiler inserts blind rotations.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Anatomy of the blind rotation

The blind rotation is the engine room of TFHE. Its input is a *trivial* GLWE encryption of the test polynomial and the *modulus-switched* LWE components $(\bar{a}_1, \ldots, \bar{a}_n, \bar{b})$; its output is a GLWE encryption of $\text{testv} \cdot X^{-\bar{\varphi}}$, where $\bar{\varphi} = \bar{b} - \sum_i \bar{a}_i s_i \bmod 2N$ is known only in encrypted form. The algorithm proceeds iteratively [2]:

$$\text{ACC}_0 = X^{-\bar{b}} \cdot (0, \text{testv}), \qquad \text{ACC}_{i} = \text{CMux}\big(\text{BK}_i,\ X^{\bar{a}_i}\cdot \text{ACC}_{i-1},\ \text{ACC}_{i-1}\big).$$

Each CMux selects, under encryption of the secret bit $s_i$, whether to multiply the accumulator by $X^{\bar{a}_i}$. After $n$ steps the accumulated exponent is $-\bar{b} + \sum_i \bar{a}_i s_i = -\bar{\varphi}$, exactly the encrypted rotation amount. The external product inside each CMux is where the TRGSW bootstrapping key meets the TRLWE accumulator, and its asymmetric noise growth — linear in the accumulator noise, controlled in the key noise — is what keeps the rotation affordable.

A TLA+ sketch of the rotation invariant clarifies the control structure:

```tla
----------------------------- MODULE BlindRotate -----------------------------
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS N, n
VARIABLES acc, i
Init == acc = 0 /\ i = 0
Step == /\ i < n
        /\ acc' = IF s[i] = 1 THEN (acc + a_bar[i]) % (2*N)
                             ELSE acc
        /\ i' = i + 1
Invariant == i \in 0..n /\ acc = (-b_bar + Sum_{j < i} a_bar[j]*s[j]) % (2*N)
=============================================================================
```

The invariant states that after processing $i$ key bits, the accumulator exponent equals the partial encrypted rotation — a loop invariant any mechanized verification of the rotation must preserve.

### 4.2 From gates to functions: why PBS changes the economics of FHE

In gate bootstrapping, the test polynomial is fixed: $\text{testv} = (1 + X + \cdots + X^{N-1}) X^{N/2} \hat{\mu}$, a step function implementing NAND-like thresholding. PBS replaces this constant with data — the LUT. The *algorithm is identical*; only the accumulator's initial content changes. This has a profound architectural consequence:

- **Leveled FHE (BGV/BFV/CKKS):** nonlinearities are the expensive operation (high-degree polynomial evaluation consuming depth); bootstrapping is the expensive maintenance operation. The two costs add.
- **TFHE with PBS:** the maintenance operation *is* the nonlinearity. The two costs coincide.

Concretely, evaluating a ReLU in CKKS requires a polynomial approximation of degree 10–30 (consuming 4–5 multiplicative levels and substantial precision), whereas in TFHE it is a single PBS with an exact LUT. The trade is precision for exactness: TFHE activations are exact on $p$-bit quantized values rather than approximate on 30-bit floats. For inference workloads — where quantization-aware training already constrains models to low precision — this trade is strongly favorable [3].

In Haskell-flavored type terms, the shift is from a leveled to a *refreshed* discipline:

```haskell
-- Leveled: noise is a resource consumed by multiplication
mulLeveled :: Ctxt -> Ctxt -> Either NoiseOverflow Ctxt
-- TFHE: every nonlinear op returns fresh noise; depth is unbounded
pbs :: LUT f -> LWECtxt -> LWECtxt   -- noise is *reset*, f applied for free
```

### 4.3 Taming non-negacyclic activations

Real activations — ReLU, sigmoid, softmax denominators — are not negacyclic, so the raw PBS cannot tabulate them over the full torus. The resolutions differ in where they pay:

1. **Partial-domain PBS** pays in *precision*: reserving the most significant torus bit as padding halves the message space. For a 4-bit activation this is painful; for 8-bit it is tolerable.
2. **FDFB** pays in *operations*: writing $f(x) = H(x)\cdot f(x) + H(-x)\cdot g(x)$ with $g(x) = -f(x+\tfrac12)$ and $H$ the Heaviside function, each branch needs its own PBS plus a homomorphic selection — roughly 2–3 bootstraps per activation [4].
3. **WoP-PBS** pays in *parameter complexity*: a generalized modulus switch extracts $(\beta, m_0)$ — a sign bit and a reduced message — and an LWE multiplication by $(-1)^\beta$ folds the negacyclicity away, achieving full-domain evaluation in a single bootstrapping with no padding bit [3].
4. **Multivalue PBS** pays in *setup*: packing multiple LUTs into one blind rotation amortizes the rotation across several activations evaluated on the same input, ideal for layers where one pre-activation feeds multiple quantized thresholds [4].

> **Theorem (WoP-PBS correctness [3]):** Let $f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ be arbitrary and $P_f$ a redundant LUT for $f$. Under the parameter conditions of Theorem 4 of [3] (chunk size $\kappa$, shift $\vartheta$ with $\Delta_{\text{in}} 2^\kappa / 2^\vartheta < 2N$), WoP-PBS outputs an LWE encryption of $(-1)^\beta f(m_0) \Delta_{\text{out}}$ with refreshed noise, for *any* $f$ — negacyclic or not.

For neural inference, the practical rule of thumb emerging from [3][7] is: use direct negacyclic LUTs for sign-like activations, WoP-PBS for ReLU-family activations at full precision, and multivalue PBS when a layer fans one value out to many quantized decisions.

### 4.4 End-to-end encrypted inference: the Concrete stack

Zama's open-source stack [7] makes the theory deployable. `tfhe-rs` implements the cryptosystem (key generation, PBS, key switching) in Rust with 128-bit-secure parameter sets; Concrete ML sits above it as an ML compiler. The dataflow of an encrypted inference is:

1. **Client:** quantize input $\mathbf{x}$ to $p$ bits, encrypt each feature as an LWE sample, send to server.
2. **Server, per layer:** homomorphically compute $W\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}$ with cleartext weights (leveled arithmetic, no bootstrapping); apply one PBS per neuron for $\sigma$ (noise refreshed, nonlinearity exact).
3. **Server:** return final-layer LWE samples; **client** decrypts and dequantizes.

Notably, the server learns nothing: all intermediate values are LWE samples under the client's key, and the PBS — despite evaluating the server's chosen LUT — reveals nothing about the encrypted input because the blind rotation amount is itself encrypted. The function being evaluated (the activation) is public; the *data* is not. This matches the standard inference-as-a-service threat model.

A Rust sketch of the server-side hot loop (illustrative; see [7] for the actual API):

```rust
// Server holds: bsk (bootstrapping key), ksk (keyswitch key), luts per layer
fn dense_layer(x: &[LweCiphertext], w: &[Vec<i64>], b: &[i64],
               lut: &LookupTable, bsk: &BootstrappingKey, ksk: &KeySwitchKey)
               -> Vec<LweCiphertext> {
    w.iter().zip(b.iter()).map(|(row, bias)| {
        // Leveled linear part: cleartext weights, cheap
        let mut acc = lwe_encrypt_zero();
        for (xi, wij) in x.iter().zip(row.iter()) {
            acc = lwe_add(&acc, &lwe_scalar_mul(xi, *wij)); // noise grows linearly
        }
        let pre = lwe_add_scalar(&acc, *bias);
        // Nonlinearity + noise refresh in one shot:
        programmable_bootstrap(&pre, lut, bsk, ksk)
    }).collect()
}
```

The loop's cost is dominated by the final line: one PBS per neuron per layer. Everything else is comparatively negligible.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness proof sketch for PBS-based inference

> **Theorem (PBS inference soundness):** Let $\mathcal{N}$ be a $p$-bit quantized network with activations implementable by the PBS variants of Section 4.3, evaluated with parameters satisfying the noise bounds of [2][3]. Then for every input $\mathbf{x}$, the decrypted output of the encrypted evaluation equals the cleartext quantized evaluation $\mathcal{N}_q(\mathbf{x})$, except with probability bounded by the union bound over PBS failure events, each negligible in the security parameter.

*Proof sketch.* Induction over layers. The linear part is exact integer arithmetic on LWE samples; its noise after a layer of width $w$ with weight bound $B$ is at most $wB\sigma + \sigma_0$, kept below the PBS input tolerance by parameter choice. Each PBS, by the correctness theorems of [2] (gate/PBS) and [3] (WoP-PBS), outputs an encryption of the exact LUT value with refreshed noise, except with probability bounded by the Gaussian tail beyond the rounding bound. Union-bounding over all neurons yields the claim. ∎

### 5.2 Benchmarks

Representative published figures contextualize the approach (parameters and hardware vary; treat latencies as orders of magnitude):

| Workload | Scheme | Accuracy (MNIST) | Latency / inference | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-layer CNN, square activations | Leveled (CryptoNets) | 98.95% | ~250 s | [5] |
| Discretized DNN, PBS activations | TFHE PBS | matches quantized cleartext | seconds–minutes (PBS-bound) | [3] |
| Logistic regression / small MLP | Concrete ML (TFHE) | cleartext parity | ~1–10 s | [7] |
| Single PBS (128-bit params) | TFHE | — | tens of ms | [7] |

The dominant cost term is transparent: if a network has $M$ neurons and a PBS costs $t_{\text{PBS}}$, inference costs $\approx M \cdot t_{\text{PBS}}$ plus leveled linear algebra. A 1,000-neuron network at 30 ms per PBS is ~30 s per inference — practical for high-value private inference (medical, financial), prohibitive for interactive latency. Batching via multivalue PBS [4] and GPU acceleration of blind rotations are the active levers for reducing this.

### 5.3 Parameter landscape

| Parameter set | $n$ (LWE dim) | $N$ (ring dim) | Security | PBS time (order) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Toy / correctness testing | 64 | 256 | none | ms |
| 128-bit standard | ~630–700 | 1024–2048 | 128-bit LWE | tens of ms |
| High-precision (8-bit msgs) | ~700+ | 2048–4096 | 128-bit LWE | ~100 ms |

Larger $N$ accommodates finer LUTs (more message slots); larger $n$ compensates the security lost to the richer bootstrapping keys. Bootstrapping key material runs to tens or hundreds of megabytes at 128-bit security — a one-time transfer cost amortized over all inferences.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Precision ceiling.** PBS message spaces are small — practically 4–8 bits per bootstrap, extendable via chunking/CRT techniques [3] but at multiplicative cost. Workloads needing float32 fidelity (scientific computing, LLM inference) are out of reach; quantized inference is the natural habitat.
2. **Latency per nonlinearity.** One PBS per neuron per layer, each tens of milliseconds, makes deep or wide networks slow. A ResNet-scale model is currently impractical; MLPs and small CNNs are the sweet spot.
3. **Negacyclicity tax.** Arbitrary activations require WoP-PBS, FDFB, or precision-sacrificing partial-domain encodings. The "free nonlinearity" is freest for negacyclic-friendly functions (sign, tanh-like); ReLU-family functions pay a parameter or precision cost.
4. **Key material and memory.** Bootstrapping keys and key-switching keys are large (hundreds of MB), and blind rotations are memory-bandwidth intensive. Deployment on constrained edge devices is difficult; the server side is assumed well-provisioned.
5. **No training.** All current practical systems do *inference only*. Encrypted training would require bootstrapping through backpropagation's divisions and high-precision gradients — far beyond PBS precision budgets.
6. **Side channels and threat model.** The scheme is semantically secure under LWE, but implementations must additionally be constant-time; timing variability in modulus switching or NTT code paths can leak. The model weights are assumed public; private-model inference needs further machinery.
7. **Quantization fidelity.** Accuracy parity holds only against the *quantized* model. If quantization-aware training degrades the model, encrypted inference inherits the degradation exactly.

## 7 Conclusion

TFHE's gate bootstrapping turned bootstrapping from an exceptional event into the unit of computation; *programmable* bootstrapping turns that unit into a universal nonlinear operator. By encoding lookup tables in the test polynomial, PBS evaluates arbitrary univariate functions — activations, comparisons, S-boxes — at the marginal cost of the noise refresh that had to happen anyway. The negacyclicity constraint, once a serious restriction, has been progressively dismantled by partial-domain encodings, full-domain decompositions, WoP-PBS, and multivalue techniques [3][4]. The payoff is concrete: deep discretized neural networks evaluated over encrypted data with accuracy matching their quantized cleartext counterparts [3], compiled from ordinary ML frameworks by tools like Concrete ML [7].

The honest accounting is that PBS trades precision and latency for exactness and unbounded depth. Within its envelope — quantized models, inference-only, server-provisioned — it is the most practical known route to general encrypted computation. The research frontier is clear: larger-precision PBS without latency blowup, batched blind rotations on accelerators, and closing the gap from seconds-per-inference toward interactive speeds. When that gap closes, the architecture of private machine learning will look like TFHE all the way down: linear layers in leveled arithmetic, and every nonlinearity a lookup table spun through a blind rotation.
