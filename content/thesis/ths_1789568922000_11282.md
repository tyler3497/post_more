# Diagrammatic Quantum Reasoning: Dagger Compact Categories, the ZX-Calculus, and Completeness from Stabilizers to Clifford+T

## Abstract

The ZX-calculus replaces matrix mechanics with string diagrams: spiders, wires, and rewrite rules that make quantum processes amenable to purely graphical proof. This thesis reconstructs the mathematical foundations of categorical quantum mechanics, develops the ZX-calculus from dagger compact categories and complementary Frobenius algebras, and surveys the decade-long completeness programme. We present the generator set and the standard rule schemata -- spider fusion, identity removal, copying, bialgebra laws, colour change, and the Euler decomposition -- and explain why soundness is routine while completeness is deep. We trace the milestone results: Backens' completeness for stabilizer quantum mechanics via graph-state normal forms, supplementarity and the single-qubit Clifford+T fragment, and the Jeandel-Perdrix-Vilmart axiomatisation that made the calculus complete for Clifford+T using the ZW-calculus, with the dyadic-rationals characterisation of the fragment. Finally, we cover the algorithmic payoff: graph-like normal forms, phase gadgets, T-count reduction in PyZX, circuit extraction, and the reformulation of measurement-based quantum computation in terms of generalised flow.

## 1 Intro

Quantum mechanics is traditionally expressed in Hilbert spaces and linear operators: states are vectors, evolutions are unitaries, and reasoning proceeds by multiplying large matrices. This notation is complete but opaque -- a three-qubit circuit identity may require verifying an equality between 8-by-8 matrices, revealing nothing about *why* it holds and scaling exponentially in qubit count. Categorical quantum mechanics proposes a different foundation: quantum theory as the theory of *processes* in a dagger compact category, reasoned about through *string diagrams* where composition is connecting wires and tensor product is placing boxes side by side.

The ZX-calculus is the flagship graphical language of this programme. Introduced by Coecke and Duncan in 2008 [5], it is generated by two families of vertices -- the green Z-spiders and red X-spiders -- together with the Hadamard gate, wires, cups, caps, and swaps. Its rewrite rules are sound with respect to the standard matrix interpretation. Soundness is elementary. *Completeness* -- the converse claim that every true matrix equality is derivable graphically -- resisted proof for a decade and drove the invention of some of the deepest results in the field.

This thesis has three aims. First, the categorical foundations: dagger compact categories, classical structures as special commutative dagger-Frobenius algebras, and strong complementarity, the abstract engine inside spider fusion and the bialgebra laws. Second, the ZX-calculus proper: generators, the standard interpretation, and the rule set. Third, the completeness programme from Backens' stabilizer result [2] through supplementarity [3,7] to the Clifford+T axiomatisation of Jeandel, Perdrix, and Vilmart [1], and the algorithmic payoff: graph-like normal forms, phase gadgets, T-count reduction in PyZX [4], circuit extraction, and measurement-based quantum computation. The emphasis is on *why* the diagrammatic viewpoint succeeds where matrices fail: structure invisible in indices -- complementarity, fusion, flow -- becomes a picture, and pictures can be rewritten.

## 2 Background

### Dagger compact categories

A **symmetric monoidal category** is a category equipped with a tensor product and unit object satisfying the usual associativity and unitality coherences up to isomorphism. In the quantum reading, objects are physical systems, morphisms are processes, sequential composition is composition, and parallel composition is the tensor product. The graphical calculus of symmetric monoidal categories is the string diagram: morphisms are boxes, wires are systems, and the coherence axioms become topological moves -- wires may be bent and slid without changing the denoted process [9].

A **dagger** structure equips the category with an involutive contravariant functor fixing objects, the abstract adjoint. **Compact closure** equips every object $A$ with a dual $A^*$ and cup and cap morphisms satisfying the snake equations, drawn as wires bending back on themselves. Together these give **dagger compact categories**: the minimal setting for states, effects, transposition, conjugation, and inner products drawn diagrammatically. The category **FHilb** of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and linear maps is the motivating example.

### Classical structures and complementarity

Inside a dagger compact category, a **classical structure** is a special commutative dagger-Frobenius algebra. In FHilb this is equivalent to a choice of orthonormal basis: multiplication copies basis vectors, the unit deletes them, and the axioms encode orthonormality. Two classical structures are **complementary** when their bases are mutually unbiased; **strong complementarity** strengthens this to a bialgebra law between the two algebras, generating finite abelian groups inside the category. For qubits, the $Z$ and $X$ observables form a strongly complementary pair. The ZX-calculus is the graphical calculus of this pair -- which is why spiders fuse by adding phases like decorated group elements.

> **Theorem:** (Coecke-Pavlovic-Vicary). In **FHilb**, classical structures on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space are in bijective correspondence with orthonormal bases. Two classical structures are complementary precisely when their bases are mutually unbiased.

## 3 Methodology

### Generators and the standard interpretation

The ZX-calculus is a *dagger compact PROP* generated by the following diagrams, each with a standard interpretation $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket$ as a linear map between qubit tensor powers:

- **Z-spiders**: green vertices with $m$ inputs, $n$ outputs, and a phase $\alpha$ drawn from the phase group. The interpretation is
  $$\llbracket Z_m^n(\alpha) \rrbracket = |0\rangle^{\otimes n}\langle 0|^{\otimes m} + e^{i\alpha} |1\rangle^{\otimes n}\langle 1|^{\otimes m}.$$
  Special cases: $Z_1^1(\alpha)$ is the phase gate, $Z_2^1(0)$ copies the computational basis, and $Z_0^0(\alpha) = 1 + e^{i\alpha}$ is a scalar.
- **X-spiders**: red vertices, the same with $Z$ and $X$ bases exchanged; equivalently a Z-spider conjugated by Hadamards.
- **Hadamard**: the yellow box (or dashed edge) denoting $H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$, which interchanges the colours.
- **Structural generators**: the identity wire, the swap of two wires, and the cup and cap, which make the calculus dagger compact.

The calculus is **universal** for pure-state qubit quantum mechanics [2].

### The rewrite rules

Two diagrams are equal in the calculus when one rewrites into the other via these sound rule schemata:

1. **Spider fusion (S)**: adjacent same-colour spiders fuse, adding phases -- the diagrammatic content of the Frobenius algebra: any connected same-colour network collapses to one spider.
2. **Identity removal (I)**: a phaseless 1-to-1 spider is the identity wire and may be erased.
3. **Copying (C) and $\pi$-copy**: a classical point (phase $0$ or $\pi$) copies through an opposite-colour spider; $\pi$ phases flip the copy's phase.
4. **Bialgebra (B)**: the strong-complementarity law relating Z-copy with X-multiplication -- the abstract form of the two observables interacting as a group algebra.
5. **Colour change (H)**: pushing a Hadamard through a spider swaps its colour, so red rules follow from green ones.
6. **Hopf law and scalar rules**: handling cups, caps, and scalars, including vanishing $\pi$-phase loops.
7. **Euler decomposition (EU)**: $H = Z(\pi/2)\, X(\pi/2)\, Z(\pi/2)$ up to scalar. Duncan and Perdrix showed this is *not* derivable from the original rules; adding it completes the stabilizer fragment [2,7].
8. **Supplementarity (SUP)**: relates two spiders whose phases stand in a specific arithmetic pattern -- discovered precisely because the calculus *without* it is incomplete for Clifford+T [3,7].

> **Theorem:** (Soundness). If $D_1 = D_2$ is derivable from the ZX rules, then $\llbracket D_1 \rrbracket = \llbracket D_2 \rrbracket$ up to a non-zero scalar factor. The proof is a routine check of each rule against the standard interpretation.

Soundness is cheap. The hard question, posed for every fragment, is completeness: does graphical derivability coincide with matrix truth?

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Spiders, Frobenius algebras, and strong complementarity

The single most powerful feature of the calculus is that spiders *fuse*. The **spider theorem** states that any connected diagram built from the (co)multiplication, (co)unit, and phase maps of one Frobenius algebra equals a single spider. In practice, large regions of a diagram collapse in one step -- which is why ZX-based optimisation digests circuits with thousands of gates into small graphs [4].

Strong complementarity yields the bialgebra rule, and the **supplementarity** rule -- invisible to the original axioms -- follows from the internal group structure of the phase groups. It was discovered via a *non-standard* interpretation: a model of the rules in which a true matrix equality fails. This is a textbook example of categorical semantics guiding syntax: to prove incompleteness, exhibit a model of the axioms that refutes the equation [7].

### 4.2 The completeness programme: stabilizers, supplementarity, Clifford+T

Completeness results are always *relative to a fragment*, a restriction of the allowed phases:

| Fragment | Phases | Universal | Complete | Milestone rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stabilizer / Clifford | multiples of $\pi/2$ | No | 2014 [2] | Euler decomposition (EU) |
| Real stabilizer | $0, \pi$ | No | 2015 | H-loop / pivoting axiom |
| Single-qubit Clifford+T | multiples of $\pi/4$ | approx. (1 qubit) | 2014 [3] | Supplementarity (SUP) |
| Clifford+T | multiples of $\pi/4$ | approx. | 2018 [1] | additional axioms via ZW-calculus |
| Dyadic angles | multiples of $\pi/2^k$ | approx. | 2019 [8] | cancellation rule |
| Universal | arbitrary angles | yes | 2019 [5] | triangle generator (ZH-style) |

Backens' 2014 proof for stabilizer quantum mechanics is the template for everything after [2]. Every stabilizer state is equivalent under local Clifford operations to a graph state, and Backens introduced **rGS-LC normal forms** -- reduced graph states with local Cliffords -- proving that two diagrams denote the same state *iff* their normal forms are graphically identical. Completeness then reduces to rewriting every diagram to normal form, proved by induction on diagram structure. The Euler decomposition of the Hadamard is the load-bearing axiom: it implements local complementation of graph states diagrammatically.

For Clifford+T, Backens first proved completeness for single qubits by exhibiting the exact obstruction -- supplementarity -- and showing the original rules plus (SUP) derive all single-qubit equalities [3]. The multi-qubit case required new ideas: Jeandel, Perdrix, and Vilmart translated into the **ZW-calculus**, whose diagrams denote integer matrices and which was already complete, then pulled completeness back along a structure-preserving translation [1].

> **Theorem:** (Jeandel-Perdrix-Vilmart [1]). The $\pi/4$-fragment of the ZX-calculus represents exactly the matrices over a finite-dimensional extension of the ring of dyadic rationals, and the axiomatisation with the additional rules is complete for it. Hence the ZX-calculus is the first complete and approximately universal diagrammatic language for quantum mechanics.

Vilmart later showed the same axiomatisation extends to all dyadic-angle fragments and isolated the single extra **cancellation** rule needed for arbitrary rational angles [8]. Full universality with completeness in the original syntax remains out of reach; the known universal complete axiomatisations extend the language with new generators, such as the triangle node of the ZH-calculus, which makes the Toffoli-Hadamard fragment -- and hence everything -- tractable [5].

### 4.3 Graph-like normal forms, phase gadgets, and T-count reduction

Completeness is a theorem about *provability*; the same diagrams also give state-of-the-art *algorithms*. The key normal form is **graph-like**: every spider is a Z-spider, every edge between spiders is a Hadamard edge, and no parallel edges or self-loops remain. Graph-like diagrams expose the two operations that drive optimisation:

- **Local complementation**: a spider with phase $\pm\pi/2$ (a Clifford spider) is removed while complementing its neighbourhood's connectivity and updating neighbour phases -- the diagrammatic form of the graph-state identity.
- **Pivoting**: a pair of connected phaseless spiders is removed while complementing the edges between their neighbourhoods.

Repeated application eliminates *all* interior Clifford spiders, leaving only non-Clifford phases. The remainder is reorganised into **phase gadgets**: a single spider with non-zero phase connected by Hadamard edges to boundary spiders, denoting a diagonal unitary $\exp(-i\alpha P/2)$ for a Pauli operator $P$. Compatible gadgets fuse, adding phases -- and this fusion is where T-count reduction happens: two $T$ gates distant in circuit order may be the same gadget in the graph.

This pipeline is implemented in **PyZX**, the open-source library for automated diagrammatic reasoning [4,9]:

```python
import pyzx as zx

# random 5-qubit Clifford+T circuit with 80 gates
circuit = zx.generate.cliffordT(5, 80)

# translate to a ZX-diagram and run the full reduction:
# graph-like normal form -> Clifford elimination ->
# phase-gadget fusion -> repeat to fixpoint
zx.simplify.full_reduce(circuit)

# extract an equivalent circuit back out
opt = zx.extract.extract_circuit(circuit)
```

On standard benchmark suites, this strategy matches or beats dedicated T-count optimisers -- with a *certificate*: every step is a sound rule application, so the optimiser emits a machine-checkable proof that the output circuit equals the input. The Haskell encoding of the underlying categorical structure is equally direct:

```haskell
-- A spider is a Frobenius-algebra homomorphism family:
-- fusion is associativity made visible.
```

### 4.4 Circuit extraction and measurement-based quantum computation

A ZX-diagram is more general than a circuit: it can denote any linear map, including non-unitary ones. **Circuit extraction** turns a diagram back into a unitary circuit, possible exactly when the diagram's causal structure permits it. The modern approach annotates the graph-like diagram with a **generalised flow** (gFlow): a partial order on vertices with correction sets guaranteeing satisfiable dependencies. When a focused gFlow exists, Gaussian elimination over the biadjacency structure extracts a circuit gate by gate -- the "there and back again" round trip: circuit to diagram, diagram simplified, diagram back to circuit [4].

The same flow technology bridges to **measurement-based quantum computation** (MBQC). In the one-way model, computation proceeds by single-qubit measurements on a fixed entangled resource state, with angles adapted on earlier outcomes. A ZX-diagram with a gFlow *is* a measurement pattern: spiders are measured qubits, Hadamard edges are entanglement, phases are measurement angles, and the flow order is the measurement order. Duncan and Perdrix's rewriting of measurement patterns with generalised flow showed the calculus natively verifies MBQC determinism: a pattern is uniformly deterministic iff its underlying open graph admits a gFlow, and the rewrite rules preserve flow. The diagrammatic view thus unifies the circuit model and MBQC -- the same spiders, with or without a causal extraction order.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We collect the central formal statements and the quantitative evidence for the algorithmic claims.

> **Theorem:** (Backens [2]). The ZX-calculus with the Euler decomposition is complete for pure-qubit stabilizer quantum mechanics, up to non-zero scalar factors: every diagram rewrites to a unique rGS-LC normal form, so matrix equality implies graphical derivability.

> **Theorem:** (Dyadic characterisation [1]). The $\pi/4$-fragment denotes precisely the matrices over a finite-dimensional extension of the ring of dyadic rationals. Consequently, T-count -- the number of non-Clifford phases -- is a well-defined resource measure of the fragment.

On the empirical side, the PyZX pipeline has been evaluated on the standard Clifford+T benchmark circuits of the T-count literature [4]. Representative outcomes of `full_reduce` plus gadget fusion: circuits with naive T-counts in the hundreds are routinely reduced by 20 to 40 percent, with the reductions *verified* by the rewriter itself -- it reduces the symmetric difference of input and output diagrams to the empty diagram. The post-fusion gadget count is invariant under the Clifford rules, so it certifies a minimal T-count relative to the gadget-fusion strategy.

## 6 Limitations

The programme has sharp boundaries, and stating them is part of the theory.

- **Scalars.** The classical completeness theorems hold *up to non-zero scalar factors*. Backens showed that scalar-exact stabilizer completeness needs a new diagram element and extra rules, and zero diagrams -- denoting the zero matrix from contradictory post-selections -- need their own treatment [6]. Applications computing amplitudes or probabilities must use the scalar-complete variant.
- **Universality vs. completeness.** No complete axiomatisation of the *universal* ZX-calculus in its original syntax is known. Universal complete systems extend the language: the ZW-calculus with integer-matrix semantics, or the ZH-calculus with triangle/H-box generators making the Toffoli-Hadamard fragment complete [5]. Whether plain ZX admits a finite complete axiomatisation for all phases is open.
- **Phase arithmetic.** Rules like supplementarity and cancellation involve non-trivial arithmetic between angles, so automated rewriting must combine graph matching with a phase solver; naive implementations suffer phase-expression blowup on large diagrams.
- **Circuit extraction is conditional.** Extraction succeeds only when a (generalised) flow exists. Aggressively simplified diagrams may have no flow and no efficient circuit equivalent at all, so the optimiser must balance simplification against extractability.
- **Complexity barriers.** T-count minimisation is related to problems believed hard; gadget fusion is a powerful heuristic, not a provable optimiser. Diagrammatic reasoning organises -- but does not circumvent -- the exponential cost of classical simulation of universal quantum circuits.

## 7 Conclusion

The ZX-calculus demonstrates that the *syntax* of quantum theory matters. Moving from matrices to string diagrams in a dagger compact category turns complementarity into a picture (the bialgebra law), phase-group structure into a picture (spider fusion), and measurement-based computation into a picture (flow on a graph). The completeness programme -- from stabilizers [2] through supplementarity [3] to Clifford+T [1] -- shows these pictures are a genuine proof system, in several fragments as strong as matrix mechanics itself.

The practical dividend is equally real: graph-like normal forms and phase gadgets turn the calculus into a circuit optimiser competitive with the best dedicated tools while emitting checkable proofs [4], and the flow-based view unifies circuits with measurement-based protocols. The open problems are now precise: scalar-exact universal completeness [6], a finite complete axiomatisation of the plain universal calculus, and the complexity-theoretic limits of diagrammatic simplification. What began as a notation for interacting observables has become one of the few places in quantum computing where foundations, proof theory, and engineering advance together -- by drawing the right pictures.
