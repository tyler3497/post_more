# The Langlands Program: Automorphic Forms, Functoriality, and the Geometric Correspondence

## Abstract

The Langlands program, inaugurated by Robert Langlands in his celebrated January 1967 letter to Andre Weil [1], proposes a sweeping correspondence between *n*-dimensional representations of Galois groups and automorphic representations of the adele group of GL(*n*), unified through the machinery of *L*-groups, automorphic *L*-functions, and the principle of functoriality. This thesis develops the analytic, algebraic, and geometric pillars of the program: the construction of the dual group from root data, the definition of automorphic *L*-functions via Euler factors attached to Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy classes, and the conjectural transfer of automorphic representations along homomorphisms of *L*-groups. We survey the landmark theorems that anchor the program in evidence — Drinfeld's and Lafforgue's proofs for GL(*n*) over function fields [4], the Harris-Taylor and Henniart local correspondence for GL(*n*), Ngo's proof of the fundamental lemma, and the recent geometrization of the local correspondence by Fargues and Scholze [5] — alongside the geometric reformulation in terms of Hecke eigensheaves on moduli stacks of bundles [3]. The thesis closes with the central open problems: functoriality over number fields and the categorical geometric conjecture.

## 1 Introduction

Few proposals in twentieth-century mathematics have matched the **Langlands program** for sheer unifying ambition. In the winter of 1966-67, Robert Langlands, then a young professor at Princeton, computed the constant terms of Eisenstein series and discovered that the emerging Euler factors demanded a vast generalization of class field theory. He communicated his vision in a now-legendary letter to Andre Weil in January 1967 [1], introducing in a few pages the *L*-group and the principle of *functoriality* — the idea that homomorphisms between *L*-groups should induce transfers of automorphic representations between reductive groups.

The program's audacity lies in its proposed bridge between two mathematical continents that appear to share nothing:

- **Arithmetic**: Galois representations, objects encoding the symmetries of algebraic number fields, whose *L*-functions (Artin *L*-functions) capture deep arithmetic data such as the distribution of prime ideals and the behavior of Frobenius elements.
- **Analysis**: automorphic forms and their representation-theoretic incarnation as automorphic representations, objects arising in harmonic analysis on adelic quotients, whose *L*-functions (Hecke *L*-functions, Godement-Jacquet *L*-functions) are defined by purely analytic means.

Langlands conjectured that these two families of *L*-functions coincide — that every Galois *L*-function is automorphic — and, more powerfully, that automorphic representations themselves transfer functorially along maps of *L*-groups. This thesis expounds the architecture of that vision, the evidence accumulated over six decades, and the frontiers where the conjectures remain open [6].

---

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From reciprocity to class field theory

The program's genealogy begins with **reciprocity laws**. Gauss's quadratic reciprocity relates the solvability of congruences in opposite directions; its vast generalization, *Artin reciprocity*, identifies characters of the abelianized absolute Galois group of a number field with characters of the idele class group. In modern language, abelian class field theory is the Langlands correspondence for the group GL(1): one-dimensional Galois representations correspond to automorphic representations of GL(1), and the correspondence is realized concretely by the Artin map [2].

### 2.2 Hecke's theory and automorphic forms

Erich Hecke associated to a holomorphic modular form *f* of weight *k* a Dirichlet series *L*(*s*, *f*) with Euler product, analytic continuation, and a functional equation. The converse direction — characterizing which Dirichlet series arise from modular forms — became the theory of *converse theorems*, a methodology Langlands himself pioneered for GL(2) [1]. The adelic reformulation replaces classical modular forms with **automorphic representations**: irreducible constituents of the space of functions on *G*(*F*) \ *G*(**A**), where *F* is a global field and **A** its ring of adeles.

### 2.3 The tensor product theorem and local factors

A foundational structural result, due to Flath, states that an irreducible admissible representation of *G*(**A**) factors as a *restricted tensor product* of local representations over all places *v* of *F*. This factorization is what makes the definition of *automorphic L-functions* possible: at each unramified place, the local representation is determined by a semisimple conjugacy class — the *Frobenius-Hecke class* — in the dual group, and the *L*-factor is defined as a characteristic polynomial evaluated on that class [2].

### 2.4 Galois representations in nature

On the arithmetic side, Galois representations arise *in nature* from algebraic geometry: the absolute Galois group of a number field acts on the etale cohomology of varieties defined over that field. An elliptic curve over **Q**, for instance, yields a two-dimensional *l*-adic representation whose *L*-function is the Hasse-Weil *L*-function of the curve. The modularity theorem (formerly the Taniyama-Shimura-Weil conjecture), proved by Wiles and others, is precisely the *n* = 2 case of Langlands reciprocity for these geometric Galois representations.

## 3 Methodology

How does one attack conjectures of this scope? The program has developed a distinctive methodological toolkit, and this thesis is organized around its principal instruments.

1. **Converse theorems.** If a representation of GL(*n*, **A**) has *L*-functions with the right analytic properties (entirety, boundedness in vertical strips, functional equations) under twists, then it is automorphic. Cogdell and Piatetski-Shapiro extended these theorems to GL(*n*), making functoriality accessible through analytic characterization rather than direct construction.

2. **The trace formula.** The *Arthur-Selberg trace formula* equates a spectral expansion (involving automorphic representations) with a geometric expansion (involving orbital integrals). Comparing trace formulas on different groups — the strategy of *endoscopy* and, more ambitiously, *beyond endoscopy* — is the principal engine for proving instances of functoriality [6].

3. **Moduli of shtukas.** Over function fields, Drinfeld introduced *shtukas* (originally *F*-sheaves): vector bundles with a Frobenius-twisted modification. Their moduli spaces carry simultaneous actions of the adelic group and the Galois group, so that comparing the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula on shtuka cohomology with the Arthur-Selberg trace formula isolates the Langlands correspondence. This was Lafforgue's route to GL(*n*) over function fields [4].

4. **Geometrization.** Replacing functions with sheaves and conjugacy classes with moduli stacks converts the correspondence into a statement of *categorical* or *geometric* nature: Hecke eigensheaves on the moduli stack of *G*-bundles. In the local setting, Fargues and Scholze geometrized the correspondence over the Fargues-Fontaine curve, constructing *L*-parameters for all smooth irreducible representations via excursion operators [5].

5. **The Langlands-Shahidi method.** A systematic technique for proving analytic properties of automorphic *L*-functions (meromorphic continuation, functional equation) via the Fourier coefficients of Eisenstein series on larger groups, yielding the local factors needed to define the correspondence's analytic side.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The L-Group and the Principle of Duality

The *L*-group is Langlands's most original invention: a complex reductive group that encodes both the internal symmetries of *G* and the Galois action on its root datum. A connected reductive group *G* over a field *F* is classified by its **based root datum** (*X**, *Delta*, *X*_*, *Delta*^vee), where *X** is the character lattice and *X*_* the cocharacter lattice. The *dual group* G^ is the complex reductive group whose root datum is the dual one — characters and cocharacters interchanged, roots swapped with coroots. The *L*-group is then the semidirect product

> ^*L G* = Gal(*F*^sep / *F*) ⋉ G^,

where the Galois group acts through its action on the root datum. This duality interchanges groups in a striking pattern:

| Group *G* | Dual group G^ |
|---|---|
| GL(*n*) | GL(*n*, **C**) |
| SL(*n*) | PGL(*n*, **C**) |
| PGL(*n*) | SL(*n*, **C**) |
| SO(2*n*+1) | Sp(2*n*, **C**) |
| Sp(2*n*) | SO(2*n*+1, **C**) |
| SO(2*n*) | SO(2*n*, **C**) |
| *G*_2 | *G*_2(**C**) |

The self-duality of GL(*n*) is what makes the *general linear* case the natural home of the correspondence: an *n*-dimensional Galois representation is tautologically a homomorphism into the dual group of GL(*n*). The type discipline of root data can be expressed directly in code:

```haskell
-- Based root data and Langlands duality, in miniature
type Weight = [Int]

data RootDatum = RootDatum
  { characters   :: [Weight]   -- X^*
  , roots        :: [Weight]   -- Delta
  , cocharacters :: [Weight]   -- X_*
  , coroots      :: [Weight]   -- Delta^vee
  } deriving (Show)

dual :: RootDatum -> RootDatum
dual rd = RootDatum
  { characters   = cocharacters rd
  , roots        = coroots rd
  , cocharacters = characters rd
  , coroots      = roots rd
  }

-- Duality is an involution: dual (dual rd) == rd
```

The *L*-group formalism transforms the reciprocity conjecture into a precise statement: for a global field *F*, there should be a bijection between (suitable) homomorphisms from the conjectural *Langlands group* of *F* into ^*L G* and automorphic representations of *G*(**A**), compatible with the formation of *L*-functions [2].

### 4.2 Automorphic L-Functions and Euler Products

Let *pi* = tensor'_*v* *pi*_v_ be an automorphic representation of GL(*n*, **A**), and let *r* be a finite-dimensional representation of the *L*-group. At each unramified place *v*, the Satake isomorphism identifies the spherical Hecke algebra with the representation ring of the dual group, so *pi*_v_ determines a semisimple conjugacy class *c*(*pi*_v_) in ^*L G*; the local *L*-factor is

> *L*(*s*, *pi*_v_, *r*) = det(1 − *r*(*c*(*pi*_v_)) *q*_v_^(−*s*))^(−1),

and the global *L*-function is the Euler product over all places. The fundamental analytic conjecture — a vast generalization of Hecke's results — asserts meromorphic continuation and functional equation for every such *L*-function, with the *Godement-Jacquet* construction handling the standard *L*-function and the *Langlands-Shahidi method* supplying the general case via Eisenstein series [2].

A computational shadow of the Satake parametrization is easy to exhibit numerically. For an unramified representation of GL(2) with Satake parameters (*alpha*, *beta*), the Hecke eigenvalues are the power sums of the parameters:

```python
def hecke_eigenvalues(alpha: complex, beta: complex, n_terms: int = 8):
    """Eigenvalues T_{p^k} for unramified GL(2): power sums of Satake parameters."""
    return [alpha**k + beta**k for k in range(1, n_terms + 1)]

# Example: Satake parameters on the unit circle (tempered case)
import cmath
alpha = cmath.exp(1j * 0.7)
beta = cmath.exp(-1j * 0.7)
print([round(x.real, 6) for x in hecke_eigenvalues(alpha, beta, 6)])
# Ramanujan bound: |alpha| = |beta| = 1  <=>  temperedness at p
```

The unproven *generalized Ramanujan conjecture* asserts that every cuspidal automorphic representation of GL(*n*) is tempered at every place — equivalently, that all Satake parameters lie on the unit circle. Its known approximations (the bounds of Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak and their refinements) remain among the deepest analytic results in the field.

### 4.3 Functoriality, Endoscopy, and the Trace Formula

The **principle of functoriality** is the beating heart of the program. Given reductive groups *H* and *G* and an *L*-homomorphism *rho*: ^*L H* → ^*L G* (a homomorphism respecting the projections to the Galois factor), functoriality predicts a transfer of automorphic representations from *H* to *G* characterized by the identity of *L*-functions:

> *L*(*s*, *Pi*, *r*) = *L*(*s*, *pi*, *r* ∘ *rho*)

for every representation *r* of ^*L G*, where *Pi* is the functorial lift of *pi*. Classical instances include:

- **Gelbart-Jacquet lift**: the symmetric square from GL(2) to GL(3), the first higher-rank case proved via converse theorems.
- **Symmetric powers**: the symmetric cube and fourth power lifts for GL(2), established by Kim and Shahidi, with decisive applications to the Sato-Tate conjecture.
- **Endoscopic transfer**: cases where *H* is an *endoscopic group* of *G*, proved via stabilization of the trace formula — a project completed only after Ngo's proof of the *fundamental lemma* relating orbital integrals on *G* to stable orbital integrals on *H*.

Langlands's *beyond endoscopy* proposal seeks to isolate functorial transfers directly from the trace formula by inserting *L*-function data (via a pole-order analysis at *s* = 1) into the geometric side, circumventing the comparison of two trace formulas. Though still largely conjectural, it represents the most promising path toward functoriality over number fields [6].

### 4.4 The Geometric Correspondence: Hecke Eigensheaves and the Hitchin System

Over a smooth projective curve *X* (over a finite field, or over **C**), functions on the adelic double quotient are replaced by sheaves on the **moduli stack Bun**_G_ of *G*-bundles, and the Langlands correspondence becomes *geometric*: to a ^*L G*-local system (a flat connection, or an *l*-adic local system) *sigma* on *X*, one attaches a *Hecke eigensheaf* Aut_*sigma* on Bun_G — a perverse sheaf (or *D*-module) satisfying

> Hecke_*x*(Aut_*sigma*) ≅ Aut_*sigma* ⊠ *sigma*_*x*

for every point *x* of *X*, where the Hecke functors act by modifying bundles at *x*. The eigenvalues of the classical correspondence are thus *categorified* into the fibers of the local system.

The *Hitchin fibration* — the map from the moduli space of Higgs bundles to the Hitchin base, an algebraically completely integrable system — provides the spectral decomposition of this picture: its fibers are abelian varieties (Jacobians of spectral curves), and the duality between the Hitchin systems for *G* and its dual G^ is a classical limit of the geometric correspondence. Kapustin and Witten's interpretation via *S*-duality in four-dimensional supersymmetric gauge theory recasts Hecke eigensheaves as branes in the sigma-model with target the Hitchin moduli space, linking the correspondence to homological mirror symmetry [3].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The program is conjectural in general, but a formidable body of theorems now anchors it. The principal milestones, in roughly chronological order:

1. **Drinfeld (1983)**: proof of the Langlands correspondence for GL(2) over function fields, via moduli of shtukas and the trace formula comparison [4].
2. **Lafforgue (2002)**: the correspondence for GL(*n*) over function fields for all *n*, a ~600-page tour de force extending Drinfeld's strategy. The precise statement:

> Theorem: *Let F be a global function field and r ≥ 1. There is a canonical bijection between irreducible cuspidal automorphic representations of GL_r(**A**_F) with finite-order central character and irreducible r-dimensional l-adic representations of Gal(F^sep/F) with finite-order determinant, compatible with the formation of L-functions at all places.* [4]

3. **Harris-Taylor and Henniart (2001)**: the *local* Langlands correspondence for GL(*n*) over *p*-adic fields, via the cohomology of Shimura varieties and Lubin-Tate deformation spaces respectively.
4. **Ngo (2010)**: proof of the fundamental lemma, unlocking the stabilization of the trace formula and endoscopic functoriality (Fields Medal, 2010).
5. **Arthur (2013)**: the endoscopic classification of automorphic representations of classical groups, conditional on the stabilized trace formula — the definitive structural result for symplectic and orthogonal groups [6].
6. **Fargues-Scholze (2021)**: *geometrization of the local Langlands correspondence* — a canonical construction of semisimple *L*-parameters attached to smooth irreducible representations of *G*(*E*) for *any* reductive *G* over a *p*-adic field *E*, via excursion operators acting on sheaves on Bun_G over the Fargues-Fontaine curve, together with a spectral action of perfect complexes on the stack of *L*-parameters [5].

Each theorem follows the same deep pattern: a *geometric* object (shtuka moduli, Shimura variety, Hitchin fibration, Fargues-Fontaine curve) mediates between the spectral world of automorphic forms and the arithmetic world of Galois representations, and a *trace formula comparison* extracts the correspondence.

## 6 Limitations

Despite this progress, the central conjectures remain open, and the obstacles are formidable.

- **Functoriality over number fields.** Not a single genuinely new case of non-endoscopic functoriality over **Q** (beyond symmetric powers for GL(2)) has been proved in decades. The beyond-endoscopy program offers a strategy but no complete execution; the analytic difficulties of isolating the relevant spectral contributions are severe [6].
- **The global correspondence for general groups.** Even the *existence* of Galois representations attached to automorphic representations on general reductive groups over number fields is known only in special cases (notably via the cohomology of Shimura varieties for groups admitting them).
- **The categorical geometric conjecture.** The strongest form of geometric Langlands — an equivalence of *categories* between *D*-modules on Bun_G and quasi-coherent sheaves on the stack of ^*L G*-local systems — remains conjectural, though Gaitsgory and collaborators have proved major portions (including the vanishing conjecture and the spectral decomposition).
- **Integral and *p*-adic aspects.** The Fargues-Scholze construction yields only *semisimplified* parameters; the full categorical local correspondence with integral coefficients, and its compatibility with *p*-adic Hodge theory, is active frontier territory [5].
- **Effectivity.** The methods are profoundly non-effective: no theorem in the program produces an explicit automorphic form from a Galois representation by a finite algorithm. The correspondence remains, in practice, an existence statement mediated by cohomology.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The Langlands program has evolved from a speculative letter into the organizing principle of modern number theory and representation theory, generating entire fields — endoscopy, the geometric Langlands program, the cohomology of Shimura varieties, perfectoid geometry — as byproducts of the attempt to prove it. Its core insight, that *harmonic analysis on adelic groups and the arithmetic of Galois representations are two reflections of a single structure governed by dual groups*, has been vindicated wherever it could be tested: over function fields by Drinfeld and Lafforgue [4], locally by Harris-Taylor, Henniart, and Fargues-Scholze [5], and geometrically by the theory of Hecke eigensheaves [3].

The deepest mysteries persist precisely where Langlands placed them: *functoriality* over number fields and the *reciprocity* bijection for general reductive groups. Whether beyond endoscopy, the geometrization program, or some as-yet-unimagined idea will breach these walls is unknown — but the history of the subject suggests that the attempt will continue to reshape mathematics far beyond the conjectures themselves [6].
