# The Conformal Bootstrap: Crossing Symmetry, the Operator Product Expansion, and Rigorous Bounds on Conformal Field Theories

## Abstract

The conformal bootstrap is a nonperturbative program that constrains — and in favorable cases determines — the data of conformal field theories using only the axioms of conformal invariance, unitarity, and associativity of the operator product expansion. By demanding consistency between different OPE channels of a single four-point function, one obtains crossing equations that the spectrum of scaling dimensions and the OPE coefficients must jointly satisfy. Because unitarity renders the OPE coefficients real and their squares positive, these equations become sum rules that can be attacked with linear and semidefinite programming. This thesis develops the bootstrap from first principles: the kinematics of conformal correlators and conformal blocks, the linear-functional exclusion method pioneered by Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi [1], the SDPB semidefinite solver [4], and the analytic lightcone bootstrap [9][10]. We present the landmark achievements — the 3D Ising island [6], the O(N) archipelago [7], the gauge-theory bootstrap [8] — and the deep connection to quantum gravity through AdS/CFT [11], concluding with open problems and the fundamental limitations of the method.

## 1 Introduction

For most of the history of quantum field theory, strongly coupled theories were regarded as inaccessible: perturbation theory fails, the lattice is expensive and Euclidean, and the space of possible nonperturbative behaviors seemed hopelessly large. The conformal bootstrap rejects this pessimism. Its founding claim is that the axioms a conformal field theory (CFT) must obey — conformal symmetry, unitarity (reflection positivity), and a convergent, associative operator product expansion (OPE) — are already strong enough to carve out the allowed space of theories, and that in exceptional cases they pin a theory down essentially uniquely.

The philosophy is old. The 1960s S-matrix bootstrap attempted to derive hadronic physics from analyticity, unitarity, and crossing alone, and while that program stalled, its conformal descendant proved far more tractable. In two dimensions, Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov showed in 1984 that conformal symmetry becomes infinite-dimensional and solved the minimal models exactly. For decades afterward, however, the bootstrap in *d* > 2 remained a formal constraint with little computational bite: crossing symmetry was understood, but nobody knew how to extract numbers from it.

The modern era began in 2008, when Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi recast crossing symmetry as a problem in convex optimization and derived rigorous, theory-independent upper bounds on operator dimensions in unitary four-dimensional CFTs [1]. What followed was an explosion: the 3D Ising critical exponents were computed to five decimal places [5][6], entire families of O(N) models were isolated as "islands" in parameter space [7], a dedicated arbitrary-precision semidefinite solver (SDPB) was built for the task [4], and a parallel analytic bootstrap uncovered universal large-spin structure in every CFT [9][10]. The program now touches statistical physics, the conformal window of gauge theories [8], and quantum gravity via the AdS/CFT correspondence [11].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the axiomatic foundations: conformal kinematics, radial quantization, the OPE, and conformal blocks. Section 3 develops the numerical methodology — the linear-functional method and its semidefinite-programming implementation. Section 4 works through four deep case studies: the single-correlator dimension bound, the 3D Ising island, O(N) models and gauge theories, and the analytic bootstrap with its holographic implications. Section 5 collects the sharpest quantitative results and rigorous theorems, Section 6 confronts the method's limitations, and Section 7 concludes with the open frontier.

## 2 Background

A conformal field theory in *d* spacetime dimensions is a quantum field theory invariant under the conformal group SO(*d*+1, 1): Poincaré transformations plus dilatations and special conformal transformations. The local operators organize into *conformal primaries* $\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\ell}(x)$, labeled by a scaling dimension $\Delta$ and a spin $\ell$ (an SO(*d*) representation), together with their descendants obtained by acting with derivatives. The axioms of the bootstrap can be stated compactly:

| Axiom | Content |
|---|---|
| **Conformal invariance** | Correlators transform covariantly under SO(*d*+1, 1); the forms of two- and three-point functions are fixed up to numbers. |
| **Unitarity** | Reflection positivity holds; there is a positive-definite Hilbert space, so OPE coefficients of real operators are real. |
| **OPE associativity** | The operator product expansion converges (in a precise domain) and different ways of grouping operators in a correlator agree — *crossing symmetry*. |

The power of the axioms is already visible in low-point functions. Conformal invariance fixes the two-point function of primaries to $\langle \mathcal{O}_i(x_1)\mathcal{O}_j(x_2)\rangle = \delta_{ij}\, x_{12}^{-2\Delta_i}$ and the three-point function up to a single number, the **OPE coefficient** $f_{ijk}$ (often denoted $\lambda_{ijk}$). These coefficients, together with the spectrum $\{\Delta_i, \ell_i\}$, constitute the **CFT data**: everything else is kinematics. The pedagogical development of these facts, including conformal Ward identities and radial quantization, is given in the standard lecture notes [2].

The OPE itself,

$$\mathcal{O}_i(x)\,\mathcal{O}_j(0) = \sum_k f_{ijk}\, C_{ij}^{k}(x, \partial)\, \mathcal{O}_k(0),$$

expresses the product of two nearby operators as a sum over primaries, with differential operators $C_{ij}^{k}$ fixed by conformal symmetry. Crucially, in radial quantization the OPE is a *convergent* expansion with a nonzero radius of convergence — a theorem, not a formal manipulation — which is what makes the bootstrap mathematically legitimate rather than merely heuristic [2].

The first nontrivial dynamical object is the four-point function of identical scalars $\phi$ of dimension $\Delta_\phi$. Conformal invariance reduces it to a function of two **cross ratios**,

$$\langle \phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\phi(x_3)\phi(x_4)\rangle = \frac{1}{x_{12}^{2\Delta_\phi} x_{34}^{2\Delta_\phi}}\, g(u,v), \qquad u = \frac{x_{12}^2 x_{34}^2}{x_{13}^2 x_{24}^2},\;\; v = \frac{x_{14}^2 x_{23}^2}{x_{13}^2 x_{24}^2}.$$

Applying the OPE to the pair (12) yields the *s-channel* **conformal block decomposition**

$$g(u,v) = 1 + \sum_{\Delta,\ell} \lambda_{\Delta,\ell}^2\, g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v),$$

where the **conformal blocks** $g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v)$ are universal kinematical functions — the contribution of an entire conformal multiplet — and the sum runs over primaries in the $\phi \times \phi$ OPE with squared OPE coefficients $\lambda_{\Delta,\ell}^2 \geq 0$ by unitarity. Applying the OPE instead to the pair (14) gives the *t-channel* expansion. Both must agree: this is **crossing symmetry**,

$$\sum_{\Delta,\ell} \lambda_{\Delta,\ell}^2\, F_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) = 0, \qquad F_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) \equiv v^{\Delta_\phi} g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v) - u^{\Delta_\phi} g_{\Delta,\ell}(v,u),$$

including the identity contribution. The functions $F_{\Delta,\ell}$ are completely known; the unknowns are the CFT data $\{\Delta, \ell, \lambda_{\Delta,\ell}^2\}$. Crossing symmetry is thus an infinite set of linear equations in the positive variables $\lambda_{\Delta,\ell}^2$ — a structure tailor-made for convex optimization.

## 3 Methodology

The decisive insight of [1] is that one need not solve crossing symmetry to extract consequences from it. Rearranging the crossing equation as a **sum rule**,

$$1 = \sum_{k} \lambda_k^2\, h_{\Delta_k,\ell_k}(u,v),$$

with known functions $h_{\Delta_k,\ell_k}$, one asks: given assumptions about the spectrum (e.g., a gap above the identity in the scalar sector), can this sum rule possibly hold with all $\lambda_k^2 \geq 0$? This is answered by a **linear functional** $\alpha$ acting on functions of $(u,v)$ — typically a linear combination of derivatives evaluated at the crossing-symmetric point $u = v = 1/4$.

> **Theorem (Linear functional exclusion):** Let $\alpha$ be a linear functional such that $\alpha[1] = 0$ in the normalization of the sum rule and $\alpha[h_{\Delta,\ell}] \geq 0$ for all $(\Delta,\ell)$ allowed by the assumed spectrum. Then no unitary CFT with that spectrum exists. Contrapositively, a unitary CFT must contain an operator violating at least one of the two conditions.

In practice, finding such a functional is a convex optimization problem. The modern formulation is **semidefinite programming**: after approximating derivatives of conformal blocks by rational functions (times a positive prefactor), the positivity conditions $\alpha[F_{\Delta,\ell}] \succeq 0$ become polynomial matrix inequalities in $\Delta$, i.e., a semidefinite program over the cone of positive semidefinite matrices [4]. This is solved by **SDPB**, a custom arbitrary-precision primal–dual interior-point solver written by Simmons-Duffin [4], which handles the extreme ill-conditioning of high-derivative bootstrap problems (hundreds of binary digits of precision are routine). Earlier work used linear programming and simplex methods on discretized spectra [5]; SDPB and its rational-block technology made the mixed-correlator revolution possible.

Two methodological refinements deserve emphasis. First, the **mixed-correlator bootstrap** studies a *system* of four-point functions $\{\langle \sigma\sigma\sigma\sigma\rangle, \langle \sigma\sigma\epsilon\epsilon\rangle, \langle \epsilon\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon\rangle\}$ simultaneously, imposing crossing on each and sharing OPE coefficients between them. This multiplies the constraints and collapses allowed regions dramatically [6]. Second, the **extremal functional method** reconstructs approximate spectra: when a bound is saturated (a "kink"), the optimal functional's zeros encode the dimensions and OPE coefficients of the extremal theory, allowing the numerical bootstrap to *solve* a CFT rather than merely bound it [5].

A schematic of the numerical pipeline:

```python
# Toy illustration of the exclusion logic (real codes use SDPB + rational blocks)
def excludes_spectrum(delta_phi, gap, deriv_order):
    # Build crossing vectors F_{Delta,ell} as derivative vectors at u=v=1/4
    basis = derivative_basis(order=deriv_order)
    # Search for functional alpha with alpha . F >= 0 for all allowed (Delta, ell)
    # and alpha . F_identity < 0  -->  spectrum ruled out
    sdp = SemidefiniteProgram()
    sdp.add_psd_constraints(block_polynomials(basis, delta_phi, gap))
    sdp.set_objective(maximize_identity_action(basis))
    return sdp.solve_with_sdpb(precision_bits=256)  # dual feasible => excluded
```

The functional basis is truncated at derivative order $\Lambda$; bounds improve monotonically with $\Lambda$, and extrapolation $\Lambda \to \infty$ is standard. The method yields *rigorous* exclusion statements at finite $\Lambda$ (any feasible dual functional is a certificate), which is why bootstrap bounds are theorems, not fits.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Single-Correlator Bound: Kinks and Dimension Gaps

The founding application [1] considers a single scalar $\phi$ in a unitary 4D CFT and bounds the dimension $\Delta_\epsilon$ of the leading scalar in the $\phi \times \phi$ OPE: $\Delta_\epsilon \leq f(\Delta_\phi)$ for a computable function $f$. The argument is exactly the exclusion logic above with a gap assumption $\Delta \geq \Delta_*$ in the scalar sector. The bound is nontrivial — it says the OPE cannot be arbitrarily sparse — and in 3D it exhibits a striking **kink**: as $\Delta_\phi$ varies, the bound curve develops a sharp corner at $\Delta_\phi \approx 0.518$, precisely where the 3D Ising model sits [5]. That a kink in a *bound* should coincide with a physical theory was the first hint that the bootstrap can locate real CFTs. It also established a template: kinks in exclusion plots are candidate theories, and the extremal functional at a kink reconstructs their data.

### 4.2 The 3D Ising Island and Mixed Correlators

The kink was sharpened into an **island** by bootstrapping the mixed system of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-odd scalar $\sigma$ and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-even scalar $\epsilon$ of the 3D Ising CFT [6]. Assuming only that $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ are the *unique* relevant operators in their symmetry sectors — a mild assumption, arguably a definition of the theory — the allowed region in $(\Delta_\sigma, \Delta_\epsilon)$ space collapses to a tiny closed island. Increasing the derivative order $\Lambda$ shrinks the island further, and at $\Lambda = 43$ the determination reached

| Quantity | Bootstrap value |
|---|---|
| $\Delta_\sigma$ | 0.5181489(10) |
| $\Delta_\epsilon$ | 1.412625(10) |
| $\lambda_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}$ | 1.0518537(41) |
| $\lambda_{\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon}$ | 1.532435(19) |

roughly two orders of magnitude more precise than the best Monte Carlo results, and in agreement with them [6]. The extremal functional method simultaneously extracted over a hundred operators of the 3D Ising spectrum, revealing the double-twist families predicted by the analytic bootstrap. Later refinements adding stress-tensor correlators pushed critical-exponent precision toward $10^{-7}$–$10^{-8}$, making the 3D Ising model the most precisely "solved" strongly coupled CFT in existence.

### 4.3 O(N) Models and Gauge Theories

The Ising success generalizes. Bootstrapping $\langle \phi_i \phi_j \phi_k \phi_l \rangle$ with $O(N)$ global symmetry produces an **archipelago** of islands: the $O(2)$, $O(3)$, $O(4)$ vector models and beyond, each sitting at a kink or island in its own exclusion plot, with the free and large-$N$ limits recovered as consistency checks [7]. Particularly celebrated is the $O(2)$ model — the universality class of the superfluid $\lambda$-transition in $^4$He — where the bootstrap resolved a long-standing experimental anomaly in the specific-heat exponent with precision exceeding both Monte Carlo and the landmark microgravity experiment.

More ambitiously, the bootstrap has been turned on **gauge theories**. Li and Poland bootstrapped CFTs with $SU(N)$ global symmetry and fermions to search for the conformal window of 4D QCD-like theories and for 3D QED (relevant to deconfined quantum criticality), deriving bounds on the dimensions of monopole and baryon operators that any candidate IR fixed point must satisfy [8]. While no gauge theory has yet been isolated as cleanly as the Ising model — the spectra are richer and the islands harder to close — the results already exclude portions of theory space that perturbation theory and large-$N$ expansions cannot reach, demonstrating that the bootstrap constrains even theories with no Lagrangian description.

### 4.4 The Analytic Bootstrap, Large Spin, and AdS/CFT

Parallel to the numerics, an **analytic bootstrap** emerged from studying crossing in the *lightcone limit* ($z \to 1$ with $\bar{z}$ fixed). There, the identity operator dominates one channel, and crossing can only be satisfied if the other channel contains infinite families of **double-twist operators** $[\phi\phi]_{n,J} \sim \phi \partial^J \partial^{2n} \phi$ with twists approaching $2\Delta_\phi + 2n$ at large spin $J$ [9]. Corrections are organized in $1/J$: an exchanged operator of twist $\tau_0$ shifts anomalous dimensions by $\gamma(J) \sim J^{-\tau_0}$, a result proved rigorously by Komargodski and Zhiboedov using convexity of the twist spectrum [10]. Caron-Huot went further, proving **analyticity in spin**: CFT data extend to analytic functions of $J$, which yields a rigorous proof of the Nachtmann theorem (convexity of leading-twist trajectories) directly from crossing [12].

These results dovetail with **holography**. In AdS/CFT, a CFT with large central charge $c$ and a sparse spectrum of low-dimension single-trace operators is dual to weakly coupled gravity in AdS; the double-twist families are the CFT image of two-particle states in the bulk [9]. Heemskerk, Penedones, Polchinski, and Sully showed that crossing symmetry plus a large gap in higher-spin single-trace operators *implies* local bulk physics — holography derived from CFT axioms rather than assumed [11]. The analytic bootstrap thus provides the dictionary: large-spin systematics in the CFT are locality and causality in AdS, and bounds on the gap to higher-spin operators bound the scale of quantum gravity corrections.

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Analysis

The numerical bootstrap's quantitative record is unmatched among nonperturbative methods. The 3D Ising determination quoted above [6] agrees with high-precision Monte Carlo ($\Delta_\sigma = 0.5181489(10)$ vs. Monte Carlo $0.51815(2)$-class values) while improving the precision hundredfold, and the OPE coefficients $\lambda_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}$, $\lambda_{\epsilon\epsilon\epsilon}$ are quantities Monte Carlo cannot easily access at all. The $O(N)$ archipelago reproduces large-$N$ and $\epsilon$-expansion predictions where those are reliable and supersedes them where they are not [7]. Central-charge lower bounds, stress-tensor OPE constraints, and bounds on the current central charge $C_J$ in theories with global symmetries round out a web of rigorous inequalities carving theory space [4].

On the formal side, the bootstrap has produced genuine theorems. The convergence of the OPE in a finite domain was established from first principles, justifying the manipulations the numerical method relies on [2]. The extremal functional method converts saturated bounds into approximate solutions, and its mathematical structure — functionals whose zeros locate the spectrum — is now understood as a form of convex duality [5]. The lightcone results [9][10] and analyticity in spin [12] are theorems about *all* unitary CFTs, independent of numerics: every CFT contains double-twist families, and their large-spin asymptotics are fixed by the low-twist data. The 2022 Snowmass white paper surveys this landscape and identifies the systematic exploration of gauge theories, SCFTs, and the S-matrix bootstrap as the next frontiers [13].

A unifying way to read these results: the bootstrap turns the *absence* of a functional into a *theorem* (exclusion), and the *uniqueness* of the extremal functional into a *solution* (islands, kinks). Numerics and analytics reinforce each other — the lightcone predictions explain why the numerical extremal spectrum looks the way it does, and the numerical islands confirm that the analytic asymptotics set in at remarkably low spin.

## 6 Limitations

The bootstrap's power comes with sharp boundaries. **Computational cost** is the most immediate: SDPB problems at high derivative order $\Lambda$ involve thousands of polynomial matrix constraints and require hundreds of digits of precision; pushing $\Lambda$ from 43 to 60+ in mixed systems strains even dedicated clusters, and spinning external operators (needed for stress-tensor and current correlators) multiply the cost [13]. **Spectrum extraction is ill-posed**: the extremal functional yields approximate dimensions, but resolving nearly degenerate operators or high-lying states is exponentially sensitive to truncation — the bootstrap bounds rigorously but solves approximately.

**Scope is limited by unitarity**: the positivity $\lambda^2 \geq 0$ that makes the method work fails for non-unitary theories (e.g., the Lee-Yang edge singularity requires separate techniques), for defects and boundaries (where reflection positivity is subtler), and for Lorentzian observables like the S-matrix, though the S-matrix bootstrap is a growing cousin program [13]. **Islands require assumptions**: closing an island demands input such as "only one relevant scalar per sector," and while such assumptions are physically natural, they are assumptions — the method rules out spectra, it does not prove existence. Finally, the **analytic bootstrap is asymptotic**: large-spin expansions are typically asymptotic series whose resummation and extension to finite spin remain active research [12].

## 7 Conclusion

The conformal bootstrap has transformed crossing symmetry from a formal consistency condition into a computational engine that proves theorems about quantum field theory. From the 2008 dimension bounds [1] through SDPB [4], the 3D Ising island [6], the O(N) archipelago [7], gauge-theory searches [8], and the analytic lightcone program [9][10][12] with its holographic payoff [11], the trajectory is one of increasing precision and widening scope, surveyed comprehensively in the modern reviews [3][13].

The deepest lesson is methodological: *consistency is constraining*. A handful of axioms — symmetry, unitarity, associativity — carve an infinite-dimensional theory space down to islands, and at the islands sit the theories realized in nature, from critical magnets to superfluid helium. The open frontier is clear: close the islands for gauge theories and supersymmetric CFTs, tame the computational scaling of spinning correlators, and complete the analytic understanding that turns asymptotic large-spin series into exact statements. If the last fifteen years are any guide, the bootstrap's next surprise is already hidden in its next exclusion plot.
