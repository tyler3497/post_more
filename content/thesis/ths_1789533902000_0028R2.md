# Quantum Metrology: Quantum Fisher Information, the Cramér–Rao Bound, and Heisenberg Scaling

## Abstract

How precisely can a physical parameter be estimated, and what does quantum mechanics buy us? We develop quantum metrology from first principles: classical Fisher information and the Cramér–Rao bound, the quantum Fisher information as the Bures metric on state space (Braunstein–Caves), and the quantum Cramér–Rao bound as the ultimate precision limit. We explain why separable probes hit the standard quantum limit $1/\sqrt{N}$ while entangled probes — NOON states, spin-squeezed states — can reach the Heisenberg scaling $1/N$, following Giovannetti–Lloyd–Maccone. We cover Ramsey interferometry, SU(1,1) interferometers, the role of the generator's spectrum, noisy metrology and the Zeno-like collapse of quantum advantage under decoherence, and modern applications: optical atomic clocks, gravitational-wave detectors with squeezed light, and quantum-enhanced magnetometry. We close with open problems in multiparameter estimation and the quest for Heisenberg-limited sensing in realistic noise.

## 1 Introduction

Every measurement is an estimation problem. A parameter $\theta$ — a phase, a frequency, a magnetic field — is imprinted on a probe, the probe is measured, and an estimator $\hat\theta$ is computed from the data. Classical statistics bounds the mean-square error via the **Cramér–Rao inequality**:

$$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta) \ge \frac{1}{\nu F(\theta)},$$

where $\nu$ is the number of repetitions and $F(\theta)$ the **Fisher information**. Quantum mechanics enters twice: the state carrying $\theta$ can be entangled, and the measurement itself is a quantum optimization [1][2].

The headline result, crystallized by Giovannetti, Lloyd, and Maccone in *Science* (2004), is that quantum strategies beat the **standard quantum limit** (SQL) $\Delta\theta \sim 1/\sqrt{N}$ — the scaling of $N$ independent probes — and can approach the **Heisenberg limit** $\Delta\theta \sim 1/N$, where the uncertainty principle itself sets the floor [1].

> **Thesis.** Quantum metrology is estimation theory geometrized: the quantum Fisher information is a Riemannian metric on the manifold of quantum states, the optimal measurement is the one that saturates the metric's geodesics, and entanglement is the resource that shortens distances. Noise, however, flattens the geometry — and understanding exactly when the quantum advantage survives is the central theoretical challenge.

## 2 Background: Classical Estimation

### 2.1 Fisher information

For a parametric family of distributions $p(x|\theta)$, the **score** is $\partial_\theta \ln p$, and the **Fisher information** is

$$F(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\partial_\theta \ln p(X|\theta)\right)^2\right] = -\mathbb{E}[\partial_\theta^2 \ln p(X|\theta)].$$

It measures how sensitively the distribution responds to $\theta$. The Cramér–Rao bound states that any unbiased estimator satisfies $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta) \ge 1/(\nu F(\theta))$, and the maximum-likelihood estimator saturates it asymptotically. Fisher information is additive over independent repetitions: $F_{\nu} = \nu F_1$, whence the $1/\sqrt{\nu}$ scaling — the **central limit theorem** as a precision law.

### 2.2 The standard quantum limit

In interferometry with $N$ photons in a coherent (classical-like) state, phase sensitivity scales as $1/\sqrt{N}$ — **shot noise**. Caves (1981) showed that injecting squeezed vacuum into the unused port of a Mach–Zehnder interferometer reduces noise below shot noise [2]. This was the first hint that the SQL is not fundamental: it is the limit of *classical* probe states, not of quantum mechanics.

## 3 Methodology: The Quantum Cramér–Rao Bound

### 3.1 Quantum Fisher information

A parameter-dependent state $\rho_\theta$ and a POVM $\{E_x\}$ induce $p(x|\theta) = \mathrm{Tr}(E_x \rho_\theta)$. Optimizing the classical Fisher information over all POVMs gives the **quantum Fisher information** $F_Q(\theta)$ (Helstrom; Braunstein–Caves 1994) [3]:

$$F_Q(\theta) = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_\theta L_\theta^2), \qquad \frac{\partial \rho_\theta}{\partial\theta} = \tfrac{1}{2}(L_\theta \rho_\theta + \rho_\theta L_\theta),$$

where $L_\theta$ is the **symmetric logarithmic derivative**. For pure states $\rho_\theta = |\psi_\theta\rangle\langle\psi_\theta|$,

$$F_Q(\theta) = 4\left(\langle\partial_\theta\psi|\partial_\theta\psi\rangle - |\langle\psi|\partial_\theta\psi\rangle|^2\right),$$

which is (up to constants) the **Bures metric** — the infinitesimal Fubini–Study distance. Estimation precision is thus the *speed* at which the state moves through Hilbert space as $\theta$ varies [3].

The **quantum Cramér–Rao bound**:

$$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta) \ge \frac{1}{\nu F_Q(\theta)}.$$

It is asymptotically saturable by the projective measurement onto the eigenbasis of $L_\theta$ (which generally depends on the unknown $\theta$ — handled by adaptive two-stage protocols [4]).

### 3.2 Unitary encoding and the generator

For the ubiquitous case $\rho_\theta = e^{-i\theta G}\rho_0 e^{i\theta G}$ with Hermitian generator $G$, and pure probes,

$$F_Q(\theta) = 4(\Delta G)^2,$$

four times the variance of the generator. Maximizing over input states with constrained resources gives the ultimate limits. If $G$ has eigenvalues in $[g_{\min}, g_{\max}]$, then $(\Delta G) \le (g_{\max}-g_{\min})/2$, so

$$\Delta\theta \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}\,(g_{\max} - g_{\min})}.$$

For $N$ qubits with $G = \sum_j \sigma_z^{(j)}/2$, the range is $N$, giving the **Heisenberg limit** $\Delta\theta \ge 1/(N\sqrt{\nu})$ [1][5]. The bound is saturated by the **NOON state** $(|N,0\rangle + |0,N\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$, whose $N$-fold phase accumulation produces fringes of period $2\pi/N$.

| Strategy | Probe state | Scaling $\Delta\theta$ |
|---|---|---|
| Classical (coherent) | product of coherent states | $1/\sqrt{N}$ (SQL) |
| Spin-squeezed | entangled, Gaussian | $1/N^{5/6}$–$1/N$ |
| NOON / GHZ | maximally entangled | $1/N$ (Heisenberg) |
| NOON under dephasing | decohered | back to $1/\sqrt{N}$ |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ramsey interferometry and atomic clocks

The workhorse of precision measurement is **Ramsey interferometry**: prepare atoms in a superposition, let the phase $\theta = \omega T$ accumulate during free evolution, and read out. With $N$ uncorrelated atoms, projection noise gives the SQL; **spin squeezing** (Kitagawa–Ueda; Wineland) entangles the atoms to redistribute uncertainty, beating the SQL. Modern optical lattice clocks (Ye, Ludlow) with spin-squeezed ensembles approach fractional instabilities of $10^{-18}$ — a clock that loses one second in the age of the universe — with quantum enhancement shaving averaging times [2].

### 4.2 Squeezed light in gravitational-wave detectors

LIGO's sensitivity at high frequencies is shot-noise limited. Since 2019, all gravitational-wave observatories inject **squeezed vacuum** (Caves' 1981 proposal realized), improving strain sensitivity by $\sim 3$ dB — a $\sim 40\%$ increase in detection volume [2]. Frequency-dependent squeezing (via filter cavities) now beats the SQL across the band. This is quantum metrology's flagship real-world deployment: black-hole mergers detected sooner because of quantum noise engineering.

### 4.3 The fragility of Heisenberg scaling

Here is the sobering theorem. Under generic **uncorrelated noise** (e.g., dephasing, photon loss), the quantum Fisher information of *any* $N$-probe state satisfies $F_Q \le c\,N$ with a noise-dependent constant — Heisenberg scaling collapses to SQL scaling, with only a constant-factor quantum advantage surviving (Demkowicz-Dobrzański, Kołodyński; Escher–de Matos Filho–Davidovich) [6]. The NOON state, optimal noiselessly, is the *most* fragile: losing one photon destroys all phase information.

*Aside: this is why the field pivoted from chasing $1/N$ to optimizing constants — a factor of 2 in $F_Q$ at fixed $N$ is worth more than an asymptotic scaling that noise forbids.*

Strategies that retain advantage under noise include: **error-corrected metrology** (sensing within a decoherence-free subspace or with active QEC — Dür, Sekatski), **nonlinear generators** (Boixo et al.: $k$-body interactions give $1/N^k$ scaling [5]), and variational optimization of probe states for the specific noise channel.

### 4.4 Multiparameter estimation

Estimating a vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ introduces the **quantum Fisher information matrix** $\mathcal{F}_{jk}$ and the matrix Cramér–Rao bound $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}) \ge \mathcal{F}^{-1}/\nu$. The optimal measurements for different parameters may be **incompatible** (non-commuting SLDs), quantified by the Uhlmann curvature; the Holevo bound then exceeds the QCRB. Applications — quantum imaging (estimating multiple phases), field gradient sensing — require navigating this incompatibility, an active frontier [6].

## 5 Empirical Record

- **Spin squeezing**: Wineland's group (1992) first demonstrated reduced quantum noise in spectroscopy; modern BEC experiments (Oberthaler, Treutlein) achieve $> 10$ dB squeezing-equivalent phase enhancement.
- **NOON states**: photonic NOON states up to $N = 10$ demonstrated; super-resolving phase measurements with $\lambda/N$ fringes confirmed.
- **LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA**: squeezed-light injection operational; $3$–$6$ dB noise reduction depending on band.
- **NV-center magnetometry**: entangled NV ensembles approach sub-picotesla sensitivity; quantum-enhanced nanoscale NMR demonstrated.
- **Atomic clocks**: Sr lattice clocks at $10^{-18}$ fractional uncertainty; spin-squeezed Yb clocks beating the SQL.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Noise kills scaling.** The no-go theorems for uncorrelated noise mean Heisenberg *scaling* is unachievable in most realistic settings; only constant-factor gains survive.
2. **Resource accounting.** Fair comparisons must fix the *total* resource (energy, time, particle number). Many "super-Heisenberg" claims evaporate under careful counting (Zwierz–Pérez-Delgado–Kok).
3. **Measurement complexity.** The optimal POVM (eigenbasis of the SLD) is often highly entangled and experimentally infeasible; practical gains require co-design of probe *and* measurement.
4. **Multiparameter incompatibility.** The single-parameter theory is essentially complete; the multiparameter case with non-commuting generators remains partially open, especially under noise.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Quantum metrology turned a philosophical question — does entanglement help measure better? — into a quantitative science with deployed technology. The quantum Cramér–Rao bound and the Bures geometry of state space give the ultimate limits; NOON states and spin squeezing show the limits are reachable; noise theorems show exactly where they break. The modern field is an engineering discipline built on deep theory: **design the probe, the dynamics, and the measurement jointly against the noise you actually have**, and the quantum advantage — modest in scaling, decisive in constants — is real [1][2][6]. The next decade's targets: Heisenberg-limited gravitational-wave astronomy with frequency-dependent squeezing, entanglement-enhanced clock networks for dark-matter searches, and a complete noisy multiparameter theory.
