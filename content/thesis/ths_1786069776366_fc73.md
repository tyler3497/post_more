# Certifiably Robust Neural Networks: Randomized Smoothing, Lipschitz-Constrained Spectral Normalization, and Complete Verification via Branch-and-Bound over ReLU Polytopes

## Abstract
Adversarial examples undermine deployment of neural networks in safety-critical domains, yet most defenses provide only empirical robustness easily broken by adaptive attacks. This thesis unifies three paradigms for *certified robustness*: probabilistic guarantees via randomized smoothing, deterministic guarantees via global Lipschitz bounding through spectral normalization and orthogonalization, and exact formal verification via complete branch-and-bound search over piecewise-linear ReLU polytopes. We prove Cohen's tight $\ell_2$ certificate $R=\sigma\Phi^{-1}(p_A)$ for Gaussian smoothing using the Neyman-Pearson lemma [1][2], derive margin-based certificates $R\ge \text{margin}(x)/\sqrt{2}L$ under Lipschitz constraints enforced by Miyato's spectral normalization [3][4] and Tsuzuku's margin training [5], and formalize verification as SMT solving through Katz's Reluplex calculus [6] and its modern GPU-accelerated extension $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN [7][8]. Synthesis across CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and ACAS Xu benchmarks reveals a tradeoff hierarchy: smoothing scales to billion-parameter models but yields probabilistic vacuity under large $\sigma$, Lipschitz methods provide deterministic $\mathcal{O}(1)$ certification at inference, and complete verifiers achieve exact decision-boundary reasoning at exponential worst-case cost. We provide algorithms, complexity analysis, and reference implementations for certification pipelines.

## 1. Introduction

Deep classifiers $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathcal{Y}$ achieve superhuman accuracy yet assign high-confidence incorrect labels to imperceptibly perturbed inputs $\tilde{x}=x+\delta$ with $\|\delta\|_p\le\epsilon$. Adversarial training improves empirical robustness but cannot *prove* absence of attack within a norm ball. **Certified robustness** seeks a computable lower bound $R(x)$ such that:

$$
\forall \delta,\ \|\delta\|_p < R(x) \implies g(x+\delta)=g(x)
$$

where $g$ is either original or smoothed classifier. Certification must be *sound*, *scalable*, and *tight*.

> **Theorem (Taxonomy of Certification):** Certification strategies partition into three axes:
> 1. *Randomized (probabilistic):* $g$ = $\arg\max_c \mathbb{P}_{\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2I)}[f(x+\eta)=c]$ → tight $\ell_2$ ball via Neyman-Pearson [1].
> 2. *Lipschitz deterministic:* If $Lip_p(f)\le L$, then margin $m(x)=f_{y^*}(x)-\max_{y\neq y^*}f_y(x)>0$ certifies $R\ge m/(\sqrt{2}L)$ [5].
> 3. *Complete verification:* Decision problem $\exists x'\in\mathcal{B}_p(x,\epsilon): f(x')\neq y$ encoded as MILP/SMT over ReLU linear regions; complete via Branch-and-Bound (BaB) [6][7].

Contributions:
- Rigorous proof of Cohen's certificate tightness
- Analysis of spectral normalization as power-iteration projection onto $\{W:\sigma(W)\le1\}$
- Ortho-lifting methods: Björck orthonormalization, Cayley convolutions, SOC, AOL, SLL
- Complete verification landscape: Reluplex simplex extension, BaB with $\beta$-CROWN bound propagation
- Unified empirical table comparing verified accuracy vs radius
- Python certification code for smoothing with Clopper-Pearson intervals

No comedy, purely technical.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Threat Model
Given classifier $f$, input $(x,y)$, adversary chooses $\delta$ with $\|\delta\|_2\le\epsilon$ (or $\ell_\infty$). Robust radius:

$$
R(f;x)=\sup\{r\ge0: \forall \|\delta\|\le r,\ \arg\max f(x+\delta)=y\}
$$

Computing $R$ exactly is **NP-complete** for ReLU networks [6].

### 2.2 ReLU Polytope Partition
ReLU $z\mapsto\max(0,z)$ partitions input space into $O(2^{\#\text{neurons}})$ linear polytopes, each defined by activation pattern $a\in\{0,1\}^n$. Within a polytope, network is affine $f(x)=W_{P}x+b_P$. Verification reduces to checking linear inequalities over union of polytopes.

### 2.3 Lipschitz Constants
$f$ is $L$-Lipschitz w.r.t. $\|\cdot\|_p$ if $\|f(x)-f(x')\|_p\le L\|x-x'\|_p\ \forall x,x'$. For composition $f=f_k\circ\phi\circ f_{k-1}\dots$, $L\le \prod_i Lip(f_i)$. For linear layer $Lip_2=\sigma_{\max}(W)$ = spectral norm. For ReLU, $Lip=1$. Thus global bound $L\le\prod_i\sigma(W_i)$. Loose but tractable.

| Method | Bound Type | Cost | Tightness |
|--------|------------|------|-----------|
| Naive product | Global | O(forward) | Loose |
| SDP LipSDP | Global | SDP | Tighter |
| Local lip opt | Local BaB | NP-hard | Tightest [9] |

### 2.4 Randomized Smoothing Base
Define base classifier $f$, noise $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, smoothed:

$$
g(x)=\arg\max_c p_c(x),\ p_c(x)=\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon[f(x+\varepsilon)=c]
$$

$g$ inherents smoothness: not Lipschitz in usual sense but *is* $1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$-smooth in terms of $\Phi^{-1}(p)$.

## 3. Methodology

We present three certification pipelines combined in practice.

### 3.1 Pipeline A: Randomized Smoothing Certification

**Goal:** Certify $\ell_2$ radius with probability $1-\alpha$ over Monte Carlo samples.

Steps:
1. Sample $n_0=100$ to guess $c_A=\arg\max$
2. Sample $n=100k$ to estimate $\underline{p_A}$ lower bound via Clopper-Pearson.
3. If $\underline{p_A}>0.5$, radius $\underline{R}=\sigma\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})$ else abstain.

Neyman-Pearson optimality: For isotropic Gaussian, worst-case perturbation that flips $p_A$ to $>0.5$ rival class is half-space classifier, attaining bound tight.

**Calibration tension:** Large $\sigma$ → larger potential $R$ but base accuracy drops. Small $\sigma$ → vacuous small $R$.

### 3.2 Pipeline B: Lipschitz Spectral Normalization

Enforce $Lip_2\le1$ via layer-wise constraints.

*Spectral Normalization* [3][4]: For $W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$,

$$
\bar{W}_{SN}=W/\sigma(W),\ \sigma(W)\approx u^T W v
$$

where $u,v$ maintained via power iteration:

```python
u ← W v / ||W v||, v ← W^T u / ||W^T u||
```

Projection preserves gradient: forward uses normalized weights, backward propagates through normalization (differentiable almost everywhere).

Extensions for convolutions: reshape $W_{conv}$ to matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{out}\times c_{in}khkw}$, bound via Fourier or power iteration on convolution operator [10].

**Orthogonality:** SN constrains $\sigma_{\max}=1$ but allows vanishing singular values. Orthogonal layers achieve $\sigma_i=1\ \forall i$, preserving norm *isometrically*. Methods: Björck iteration $W_{k+1}=1.5W_k-0.5W_kW_k^TW_k$, Cayley transform $W=(I+Q)^{-1}(I-Q)$ with skew-symmetric $Q$, SOC $\exp(A)$ where $A=-A^T$, AOL rescaling.

Training objective becomes margin-aware [5]:

$$
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{CE}(f(x),y)+\lambda\max(0, K - \gamma\cdot m(x)/Lip)
$$

where $m(x)$ logit margin, $K$ target robustness.

*Resulting deterministic certificate:*

> **Theorem (Lipschitz-Margin):** If $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $\ell_2$, and $y^*=\arg\max_j f_j(x)$, gap $m(x)=f_{y^*}(x)-\max_{y\neq y^*}f_y(x)>0$, then $R\ge m(x)/(\sqrt{2}L)$.

*Proof sketch:* $\|f(x+\delta)-f(x)\|_2\le L\|\delta\|_2$. Using norm inequality $\|f(x+\delta)-f(x)\|_\infty\le\|...\big\|_2$, change in any logit bounded. If $m>2\epsilon L'$ eventual flip impossible.

### 3.3 Pipeline C: Complete Verification via Branch-and-Bound

Formalize verification query:

$$
\exists x'\in [x-\epsilon,x+\epsilon]: c^T f(x')+b>0 \land x'\text{ feasible}
$$

Encode ReLU $y=\max(0,\hat{y})$ as $(y\ge0)\land(y\ge\hat{y})\land(y=0\lor y=\hat{y})$.

**Reluplex** [6]: Extends Simplex to treat ReLU as lazy constraints. Maintain tableau $Ax=b$ with tableau variables partitioned into basic/nonbasic. Always keep assignment, even violating. Two fixpoint loops: linear feasibility via pivot, ReLU violation via update of $y$ and splitting. Splitting creates subproblems $\{y=0,\hat{y}\le0\}$ vs $\{y=\hat{y},\hat{y}\ge0\}$.

**Beta-CROWN** [7]: Replace LP solver by bound propagation. For each neuron split $S$, introduce Lagrange multiplier $\beta\ge0$ encoding constraint $\mathbb{1}_{split}$. Bound:

$$
\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}, z\in\mathcal{Z}} c^Tf(x) = \max_{\alpha,\beta\ge0}\min_x g(\alpha,\beta,x)
$$

Optimized via gradient ascent on $\beta$ (GPU parallel). BaB iteratively chooses unstable ReLU with heuristic e.g., FSB (Filtered Smart Branching) to bisect, improving bounds until provably robust or counterexample found.

Complete: depth-first search explores at most $2^n$ nodes but prunes via bound >0.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Randomized Smoothing: Proof of Tightness

We formalize Cohen et al. [1].

> **Theorem (Cohen 2019 Tight $\ell_2$):** Let $g$ be $\sigma$-smoothed. If $p_A=g_{y^*}(x)$ satisfies $p_A\ge\underline{p_A}>1/2$, then $\forall\|\delta\|_2<R=\sigma\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})$, $g(x+\delta)=y^*$. Moreover bound is tight: $\exists$ base $f$ where radius exactly $\sigma\Phi^{-1}(p_A)$.

*Proof:* For Gaussian, distributions $\mathcal{N}(x,\sigma^2 I)$ and $\mathcal{N}(x+\delta,\sigma^2 I)$ have $\ell_2$ distance related to Neyman-Pearson tradeoff. Define half-space $S=\{z: \delta^T(z-x)\le \sigma\|\delta\|\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})\}$. Then $\mathbb{P}_{x}(S)=\underline{p_A}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{x+\delta}(S)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})-\|\delta\|/\sigma)$. If $\|\delta\|<R$, then $\mathbb{P}_{x+\delta}(S)>1/2$, implying $g(x+\delta)=\arg\max$ stays $y^*$, else for any smaller radius we can construct $f=\mathbb{1}_S$ to flip. Tightness from choosing $f$ as indicator of $S$ achieves equality.

Key lemma: Total variation distance between Gaussians parameterized by $\Phi$, and worst-case classifier among all $f$ with given $p_A$ is half-space.

Implementation needs *certification with noise*: tightness fails if noise anisotropy or non-Gaussian, but extensions exist to $\ell_1$ via Laplace and $\ell_0$ via sparsity-aware.

### 4.2 Spectral Normalization Dynamics

SN projection ensures $\|f\|_{Lip}\le1$ but reduces capacity. Study eigen-spectrum after SN: typically many singular values <<1, causing gradient attenuation. Analytical example: depth $L$ with SN-only, gradient norm scales $\le1$, but Jacobian product may →0 due to ReLU masking correlation across layers, phenomenon "Rank collapse" noted in Anil et al. orth expansions. Mitigation via *residual* $x+Conv_{1-Lip}(x)$ preserving isometry.

Björck vs SN comparison:

| Property | SN Power-iter | Björck orthogonal | SOC exp |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|---------|
| Iter cost | O(mn) | O(m^2 n) once | O(k mn) k=6 |
| Exact ortho | No | Approx after 15 iters | Yes up to truncation |
| Gradient norm preserved | No | Yes | Yes |

Practical recipe (1-Lipschitz ResNet): Use BCOP or Cayley Conv, GroupSort activation (1-Lipschitz, gradient-preserving) instead of ReLU to avoid gradient vanishing while maintaining sorting non-linearity, temperature scaling $\tau$ in loss to adjust logits without breaking Lip bound.

### 4.3 ReLU Polytope Branch-and-Bound Exhaustion

Consider 2-layer ReLU $f(x)=c^TW_2\text{ReLU}(W_1x)$. Input bounds $l_0\le x\le u_0$ propagated via interval bound propagation (IBP): $\hat{l}_1=W_1^+l_0+W_1^- u_0$, $\hat{u}_1$ symmetric, $l_1=\text{ReLU}(\hat{l}_1)$, etc. Upper bound cheap but loose, depends on big-M relax.

CROWN bound: linear lower $a^T x + b \le f(x)$ by back-substituting ReLU relaxation $\alpha\hat{z}\le \text{ReLU}(\hat{z})\le \beta\hat{z}+\gamma$ where $\alpha=0$ or $1$ if stable else optimized via $\alpha$-CROWN. For unstable neuron $\hat{l}<0<\hat{u}$, triangle relaxation:

$$
y\ge0,\ y\ge\hat{y},\ y\le \frac{\hat{u}}{\hat{u}-\hat{l}}(\hat{y}-\hat{l})
$$

Beta extension encodes split $y=0$ if $\hat{y}_{i}\le0$ via Lagrangian $\beta_i$ term that cancels mismatch.

Search tree depth typically << $n$ because bound propagation already strong enough to prove robustness for many nodes. VNN-COMP 2021 winner $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN solved 3× more instances than Reluplex in same time limit via GPU batch BaB.

Counterexample completeness guarantee: if search exhausts all nodes without pruning, property proven UNSAT, meaning no adversarial $x'$ exists.

### 4.4 Certified Accuracy vs Radius Tradeoff and Composition

Unified view: define *certified curve* $C(\epsilon)=\mathbb{P}_x[ R(x)\ge\epsilon \land g(x)=y]$. Randomized smoothing yields smooth curve decaying roughly as $\Phi$-tail, Lipschitz gives step-like deterministic curve where each $x$ contributes either 0 or margin-derived radius, complete verification yields exact curve for given $\epsilon$ per sample (pointwise yes/no).

Composition theorem: Smoothing *over* Lipschitz classifier improves tradeoff via Gaussian Poincaré inequality $\text{Var}_\varepsilon[f]\le\sigma^2\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2\le\sigma^2L^2$. Low $L$ reduces variance, improves empirical $\underline{p_A}$, thus larger $R$. This motivates joint training SLL [11] + smoothing, instantiated in LVM-RS.

### 5.2 CIFAR-10 Empirical Sketch (synthesized from sources)

At $\epsilon=36/255$ $\ell_\infty$:

- Standard ResNet (no cert): clean 95%, verified 0%
- IBP-trained: clean 66%, verified 52%
- Lipschitz LipNet (SLL): clean 77.8%, verified 64.5% deterministic $\ell_2$ radius 0.5
- $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN on same LipNet: verifies 78% of instances for $\epsilon=36/255$ within 10s timeout, up from 64.5% via margin alone, showing BaB closing gap to complete.

For $\ell_2$ ImageNet $\sigma=0.5$:

- Randomized smoothing ResNet-50: clean top-1 57%, certified accuracy at $R=0.5$ : 49% [1], at $R=1.0$: 37%
- Denoised smoothing [12]: +5-8 point improvement by using pretrained denoisier.

### 5.3 Proof of Soundness for Branch-and-Bound

> **Lemma (Soundness):** If BaB returns ROBUST, then $\forall x'\in\mathcal{B}_p(x,\epsilon), f(x')=y$.

*Proof:* BaB maintains partition $\{S_i\}$ of input domain where $\cup_i S_i = \mathcal{B}$. For each $S_i$, lower bound $LB_i\le\min_{x\in S_i} m(x)$ where $m(x)=f_y(x)-\max_{j\neq y}f_j(x)$. If $\forall i, LB_i>0$, then $\min_{\mathcal{B}} m>0$ → no adversarial. Termination with pruning safe because $LB_i$ sound by linear relaxation encompassing non-convex ReLU. ∎

Completeness follows from ReLU splits eventually making all neurons stable, at which point relaxation exact (linear region), hence bound = true min.

### 5.4 PyTorch-style Certification Implementation

```python
import numpy as np, math
from scipy.stats import norm, binom
from statsmodels.stats.proportion import proportion_confint  # Clopper-Pearson

def certify_smoothing(base_clf, x, sigma, n0=100, n=100000, alpha=0.001):
    # stage 1: select top class
    counts0 = {}
    for _ in range(n0):
        pred = base_clf(x + np.random.randn(*x.shape)*sigma)
        counts0[pred] = counts0.get(pred,0)+1
    cA = max(counts0, key=counts0.get)

    # stage 2: estimate pA
    counts = 0
    noise_batch = 256
    samples = 0
    preds = []
    # batched for speed
    while samples < n:
        b = min(noise_batch, n-samples)
        batch_noise = np.random.randn(b,*x.shape)*sigma  # shape handling simplified
        # base_clf batched interface
        batch_pred = [base_clf(x + nb) for nb in batch_noise]
        counts += sum(1 for p in batch_pred if p==cA)
        samples+=b

    # Clopper-Pearson lower bound
    pA_low = proportion_confint(counts, n, alpha=2*alpha, method='beta')[0]  # lower
    if pA_low < 0.5:
        return cA, 0.0, False
    radius = sigma * norm.ppf(pA_low)
    return cA, radius, True

def spectral_normalize_layer(W, u, v, n_iter=1):
    # W: (out,in)
    for _ in range(n_iter):
        v = W.T @ u
        v = v / (np.linalg.norm(v)+1e-12)
        u = W @ v
        u = u / (np.linalg.norm(u)+1e-12)
    sigma = u @ W @ v
    W_sn = W / sigma
    return W_sn, u, v, sigma

# Lipschitz margin cert
def cert_lipschitz_margin(logits, L):
    # logits vector shape (K,)
    y_star = np.argmax(logits)
    sorted_logits = np.sort(logits)[::-1]
    margin = sorted_logits[0]-sorted_logits[1]
    return margin / (math.sqrt(2)*L) if margin>0 else 0.0
```

```haskell
data ReLUState = Neg | Pos | Unstable deriving Show
type Bounds = (Vector Double, Vector Double)
branchAndBound :: Network -> Bounds -> Property -> Decision
branchAndBound net bnds prop =
  case boundPropagation net bnds of
    LowerBound lb | lb > 0 -> Robust
    UpperBound ub | ub <= 0 -> Counterexample (extractWitness net bnds)
    _ -> let (i, leftB, rightB) = chooseSplit net bnds
         in case branchAndBound net leftB prop of
              Counterexample w -> Counterexample w
              Robust -> branchAndBound net rightB prop
```

## 6. Limitations

1. **Gaussian curse of dimensionality:** For $\ell_\infty$ threat, smoothing requires $\sigma=\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ leading to vacuous base accuracy. $d=3072$ CIFAR, $R_\infty$ = $R_2/\sqrt{d}$ shrink factor.
2. **Lipschitz upper-bound looseness:** Product norm overestimates true local Lip up to $10^4$x, even with SDP improvements, limiting achievable certified radii vs empirical robustness. Orthogonal constraints also reduce model expressivity, e.g., universal approximation of 1-Lipschitz functions not possible with ReLU alone; requires GroupSort.
3. **Complete verification cost:** NP-complete scaling forbids networks >10^5 neurons for tight $\epsilon$. BaB timeout rates increase superlinearly with depth; branching heuristics degrade under batch.
4. **Probabilistic abdication:** Smoothing certificates hold with probability $1-\alpha$ over randomness; adversarial risk remains over Monte Carlo failure unless Hoeffding corrected, requiring $n\approx 10^5$ for tight intervals, compute-heavy at ImageNet scale.
5. **Threat p-norm mismatch:** Techniques specialized: Gaussian smoothing optimal only for $\ell_2$, Laplace for $\ell_1$, uniform for $\ell_\infty$ loose. Lipschitz $\ell_2\to\ell_\infty$ conversion loses $\sqrt{d}$ factor.
6. **No semantic certification:** All methods certify norm-balls, not rotations, brightness, or LLM jailbreaks; gap between formal norm and human perceptual similarity.
7. **Training instability:** SN power iteration can diverge if $u,v$ not renormalized each step; $\beta$-CROWN optimization non-convex in $\beta$ may get stuck local optimum requiring restarts.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a unified theory of certified robustness spanning three worlds: **randomized smoothing** which transfers any classifier to a smoothed one with tight $\ell_2$ guarantee $\sigma\Phi^{-1}(p_A)$ via Neyman-Pearson [1][2], **Lipschitz-constrained networks** via spectral normalization [3][4] and margin training [5] delivering deterministic $\mathcal{O}(1)$ inference-time certificates derived from margin over Lipschitz constant, and **complete verification** through Reluplex SMT [6] and its scalable successor $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN [7][8][9] that exhaustively branches over ReLU polytopes with GPU-accelerated bound propagation. Each trades soundness, completeness, scalability differently: smoothing scales to ImageNet at probabilistic cost, Lipschitz provides fast deterministic bounds at expressivity cost, BaB gives exact guarantees at exponential cost. Their fusion — e.g., Lipschitz-smoothed classifiers reducing variance for larger $R$, and $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN tightening Lipschitz margin certificates — emerges as most promising for safety-critical deployment (ACAS Xu, malware, autonomous driving). Future work includes semidefinite layer SLL scaling to ViT, adoptive $\sigma(x)$ smoothing, and verification of LLM logits via ReLU polytope extensions of attention.
