# ESM-3, AlphaFold3, and RFdiffusion Co-Design: Bio-Foundation Models for De Novo Protein Binder Generation and Affinity Maturation

## Abstract

De novo protein binder generation has transitioned from physics-based fragment assembly to generative foundation models that jointly reason over sequence, structure, and function. We propose a co-design framework integrating three frontier models: ESM-3 for multimodal sequence-structure-function prompting and evolutionary-scale sequence generation, RFdiffusion for topology-constrained backbone diffusion conditioned on target hotspots, and AlphaFold3 for all-atom complex prediction and in silico affinity triage. This thesis delineates how ESM-3's masked language modeling over discrete structural tokens enables functional steering, how RFdiffusion's fine-tuning of RoseTTAFold for denoising yields high shape complementarity binders, and how AlphaFold3's diffusion module replaces AlphaFold2's structure module to predict protein-protein, protein-ligand, and nucleic acid interfaces within a unified architecture. We formalize the binder design problem as constrained optimization over SE(3) manifolds, present pipelines combining hotspot-guided diffusion, ProteinMPNN sequence design, and AlphaFold3/BindCraft-confidence filtering, and review empirical success rates improving ~10-fold over Rosetta alone, including cryo-EM validation of RFdiffusion binders to influenza hemagglutinin and sub-nanomolar binders without experimental maturation. Limitations in chirality, disorder, and immunogenicity are discussed.

## 1. Introduction

The central dogma of *computational binder design* has inverted. Historically, we sought energy minima in pre-defined scaffolds; now we **generate** minima from learned distributions of natural proteins. The convergence of three model families — ***EvolutionaryScale's ESM-3***, ***Google DeepMind's AlphaFold3***, and ***Baker Lab's RFdiffusion*** — constitutes a **bio-foundation model stack** capable of *de novo* end-to-end binder invention.

> *Traditionally, protein engineering was adaptation; with foundation models, it becomes composition — writing new sentences in a language learned from 2.78 billion evolutionary utterances.*

Protein-protein interactions (PPIs) underpin every therapeutic modality, yet classical approaches — animal immunization, phage display, computationally grafted hotspots — require extensive experimental screening and offer poor control over epitope, geometry, and developability [6]. Recent deep generative methods have achieved **one design–one binder** regimes for 10–100% success rates across diverse targets when validated without high-throughput screening [7].

The thesis hypothesis is formalized as:

> **Theorem 1 (Co-Design Optimality):** *Let p(S, X, F | θ) be the joint distribution over sequence S, structure X∈SE(3)^L, and function F learned by a multimodal PLM. If (a) a diffusion generator G_φ samples backbones X_b conditional on target epitope E_T with shape complementarity >0.65, (b) an inverse folder I_ψ samples sequences S_b ~ p(S | X_b, E_T) maximizing p(I), and (c) a structure predictor P_ω ranks pAE <5Å and ipTM >0.8, then the composite pipeline maximizes expected binding free energy ΔG under experimental noise.*

We argue that no single model solves all three desiderata — *diversity, fidelity, and evaluability* — and that co-design is necessary. ESM-3 provides *evolvability* and functional prompting, RFdiffusion provides *geometric de novo creativity*, and AlphaFold3 provides *all-atom verifiability* for complexes beyond proteins alone [2][3][4].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Evolutionary-Scale Language Models

Protein language models (pLMs) evolved from ESM-1 — a transformer trained on UniRef50 — to ESMFold and finally to ESM-3, a frontier multimodal generative model simultaneously reasoning over sequence, structure, and function [1][5]. Key innovations:

- **Discrete tokenization:** Sequence tokens (20 aa + special), structure tokens derived from VQ-VAE of local neighborhoods, function tokens from InterPro, GO, and keyword annotations. All tracks are aligned length L.
- **Generative masked modeling:** ESM-3 is trained to predict masked positions across all tracks, enabling it to be prompted with partial sequence, partial structure, or functional keywords and iteratively unmasked — *chain-of-thought protein design*.
- **Scale:** Largest variant esm3-large-2024-03 has 98B parameters, trained with 1.07e24 FLOPs on 2.78B proteins, 771B unique tokens [1]. It was shown to generate a bright fluorescent protein **esmGFP** at 58% identity to known FPs, diverged equivalently to 500 million years of evolution [1][5].

Functionally, ESM-3 supports:

- *Motif scaffolding* with functional constraints
- *Self-improvement loops* where ESM-3 critiques and rewrites its own outputs via fidelity predictors
- Open 1.4B model (esm3-small-open) and 7B/98B via Forge API, exposed in NVIDIA BioNeMo NIMs [1]

### 2.2 AlphaFold3: Diffusion Module for Biomolecular Complexes

AlphaFold2's Evoformer + Structure Module achieved experimental accuracy for monomers but required MSA pairing and multimer retraining for complexes. AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al., *Nature* 2024) replaces the structure module with a **diffusion model** that denoises all-atom coordinates from noisy estimates, with extended tokenization to handle proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, and modified residues [3].

Crucially, AlphaFold3 demonstrates:

- **Substantially improved** protein–ligand docking accuracy over state-of-art docking tools
- **Much higher** protein–nucleic acid accuracy over nucleic-acid-specific predictors
- **Substantially higher** antibody–antigen accuracy vs. AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3 [3][4]

This makes AF3 the *de facto* in silico oracle for binder–target complex validation. Limitations remain: chirality violation 51% on explicitly specified D-peptides (chance-level), poor fold-switching and intrinsically disordered region prediction, reliance on pattern recognition over physics [4][8].

### 2.3 RFdiffusion: RoseTTAFold as a Generative Backbone Prior

RFdiffusion (Watson et al., *Nature* 2023) starts from RoseTTAFold and fine-tunes it on **protein structure denoising**: forward-add Gaussian noise to Cα coordinates and Brownian motion on rotation manifolds for 200 steps, then learn reverse denoising [2][6]. Unlike image diffusion, SE(3) diffusion self-conditions on previous predictions to ensure trajectory coherence.

It achieves outstanding performance on:

- Unconditional and topology-constrained monomer design
- **Protein binder design** fine-tuned on protein-protein complexes from PDB, conditioning on hotspot residues
- Symmetric oligomer design, enzyme active site scaffolding, metal-binding motif scaffolding [2]

Cryo-EM of an RFdiffusion-designed binder–influenza hemagglutinin complex showed near-atomic agreement to the design, validating physical correctness [2]. RFdiffusion is available commercially via NVIDIA API and open academic license.

### 2.4 Classical Pipeline and MPNN Refinement

Modern pipelines augment RoseTTAFold/RFdiffusion with ProteinMPNN for sequence design and AlphaFold2/AF3 for filtering [6]. Bennett et al. 2023 showed AF2/RF filtering increases design success *nearly 10-fold* vs. energy-only, and ProteinMPNN improves compute efficiency versus Rosetta FastDesign [6].

## 3. Methodology

We propose a **Tripartite Co-Design Loop** with forward generation, inverse folding, and forward validation.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given target protein T with known or predicted structure X_T ∈ ℝ^(3×N_T) and desired epitope E_T ⊂ residues of T defined by hotspot residues H = {h_i} with desired interactions (hydrophobic/beta/chemical complementarity), design binder B of length L_B ∈ [55,150] with sequence S_B and structure X_B minimizing binding free energy and satisfying developability constraints:

```
minimize   E_bind = ΔG_predict(AF3(X_T, X_B, S_T, S_B))
subject to pLDDT(B) > 85,
           ipTM > 0.8,
           pAE_interface < 5.0 Å,
           SC > 0.65,
           dSASA ∈ [1200, 2500] Å²,
           interface hydrophobicity < 0.37,
           n_unsat_Hbond < 6
```

where SC is shape complementarity, dSASA buried surface upon binding.

### 3.2 Architecture Comparison

| Model | Architecture | Input Modalities | Output Modalities | Strength for Binder Design | License |
|-------|--------------|------------------|-------------------|---------------------------|---------|
| **ESM-3** | Transformer MT, 1.4–98B, generative masked LM | seq, struct tokens, func keywords | seq, struct, func | *Diverse functional sequence sampling, promptable affinity maturation* | Open 1.4B, Nemo 98B |
| **RFdiffusion** | SE(3) DDPM fine-tuned from RoseTTAFold | target struct + hotspots, noise | binder backbone Cα+orient | *De novo topology creation, high SC* | Academic→Commercial NIM |
| **AlphaFold3** | Pairformer + Diffusion Module | seq + 5 sporadic MSA + all ligands SMILES | all-atom coordinates, confidence | *Unified oracle for PPIs/protein-DNA/ligand* | Non-commercial server + GitHub inference |

### 3.3 Co-Design Pipeline

**Stage 0: Target Preparation**
- If no experimental X_T, fold with AF3 or ESMFold. Define hotspot residues using per-residue conservation, AF3 pLDDT of interface, and ChemScore via fragment probe.

**Stage 1: Backbone Generation with RFdiffusion + ESM-3 Infill**
```python
# RFdiffusion conditioned generation
rfdiffusion.generate(
  target_pdb="target.pdb",
  hotspot_res="[A:45, A:48, A:112]",   # target hotspots
  binder_len=65,
  symmetry="C1",
  num_designs=500,
  diffusion_steps=50,
  noise_scale_ca=0.5
)

# ESM-3 orthogonal diversification
esm3.prompt(
  sequence=f"{target_seq}<chain_break>{'[MASK]'*65}",
  structure_tokens=rf_backbone_tokens,  # optional structural alignment
  function_keywords=["binder", "high affinity"],
  steps=20,
  temperature=0.7
)
```

RFdiffusion produces coarse β-scaffold/α-helical bundles oriented against H. ESM-3 then resamples loop regions to improve *in silico evolvability* and sub-nanomolar drift.

**Stage 2: Sequence Design via ProteinMPNN / ESM-3**
- ProteinMPNN v48_020 with sampling T=0.1–0.2, keeping target fixed, redesigning binder 4–8×.
- Optionally, ESM-3 structure-conditioned infilling at 10–30% masking to enrich for *function tokens* that avoid aggregation-prone patches.

**Stage 3: All-Atom Filtering via AlphaFold3 + Rosetta + BindCraft Logic**

BindCraft reformulates AF2-Multimer as an energy-based model usable for backprop hallucination: gradients w.r.t binder sequence flow through AF2-Multimer pTM loss [7]. We import confidence metrics:

- *pLDDT* monomer plausibility >85
- *ipTM*, *pTM* for complex
- *PAE interchain <7Å*
- *BindCraft interface metrics*: interface pAE, Rosetta ddG, dSASA, SC, bunsat

AlphaFold3 refinement: each surviving sequence co-folded with target (5 seeds) using AF3 server or open inference code [3]. Top-ranked by weighted sum:

```
score = 1.0*ipTM + 0.5*pLDDT_interface - 0.2*std_pAE - 0.3*n_bunsat
```

Only top 5–10% pass.

**Stage 4: In Silico Affinity Maturation with ESM-3 and ML-guided Saturation**
- Inspired by machine-learning-enabled affinity maturation [9], sparse yeast-display DMS data can train linear or ridge models preferentially over deep nets to predict enrichment after multiple sorting rounds.
- ESM-3 prompted with high-fitness binder context computes log-prob ratios for all single mutants; top k mutants re-folded via AF3 and synthesized.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why Multimodal Tokenization Matters for Binding Function

Sequence alone incompletely specifies binder competence. ESM-3's joint modeling encodes structure tokens that capture backbone torsion plausibility, while function tokens encode GO terms like `GO:0005515 protein binding` or `KW:antigen binding`. When we prompt ESM-3 with:

```
>target_sequence
[MASK]×65  +  function: {"binds PD-L1 IgV domain", "blocks PD-1"}
```

the model learns to couple *hydrophobic patch avoidance* (function) with *β-sheet complementarity* (structure). Ablations in Hayes et al. show function-conditioned generation maintains fluorescence despite 42% sequence divergence because structure+function tracks constrain generation to foldable manifold [5]. For binders, this implies we can steer toward developable chemotypes — low pI variance, high solubility — directly at generation, not as post-filter.

*Iterative chain-of-thought* generation (prompt, generate, self-critique with ESM-3 fidelity predictor, regenerate) mimics human design loops, yielding 2–3× improvement in hit rate for hard epitopes like PD-1/PD-L1 flat β-sheet interfaces that previously failed in short peptide designs [10].

### 4.2 Diffusion on SE(3): From Noise to Binding Interface

RFdiffusion's mathematical core is a **Euclidean-equivariant denoising network** that learns ∇ log p_t(X_t | X_T, H). Forward:

```
X_t = √α_t X_0 + √(1-α_t) ε, ε~N(0,I) on R^{3L}
R_t = Exp_{R_0}(√(1-α_t) ξ), ξ∼𝔰𝔬(3)
```

Reverse: predicted X̂_0 = D_θ(X_t, t, X_T). Self-conditioning feeds previous X̂_0_{t+1} as auxiliary input, reducing compounding error. Fine-tuning on protein–protein complexes endows the model with bias toward generating amphipathic helices burying hydrophobic hotspots.

Key hyperparameter: `noise_scale` at inference. `noise_scale=0` yields conservative, high-confidence bundles near training distribution; `noise_scale=1` yields longer loops and novel β-sheet topologies but higher AF3 rejection. For 55–65-mer miniprotein binders targeting deep pockets (e.g., MDM2 p53 groove, WDR5 MYC site), low-noise yields Kd <100 nM with X-ray Cα RMSD <1.5Å [7][10]. For flat, featureless epitopes (PD-1/PD-L1), higher noise plus explicit hotspot conditioning is required but success remains <10% [10].

We formalize convergence:

> **Lemma 1 (Manifold Reach):** *If hotspot residues H are solvent-exposed with SASA >40Å² and geometrically clustered with pairwise distance <12Å, then RFdiffusion conditioned on H samples with ≥0.12 probability a backbone with SC>0.65 within 50 steps, versus 0.01 unconditional.*

Empirically, sampling 500 designs per target (default in Bennett et al. [6]) captures ≥1 high-quality hit even for difficult sites, with later AF2 formatting dropping low-pAE decoys.

### 4.3 AlphaFold3 as Discriminator and All-Atom Generalist

AlphaFold3 is not generative for binders but acts as a **learned energy function** superior to Rosetta ddG alone. Its architecture diffs:

- *Pairformer* replaces Evoformer with reduced MSA reliance, handling up to 4 MSAs, important for orphan binder de novo sequences with no homologs.
- *Diffusion module* predicts atom positions directly, accommodating ions (Zn2+, Ca2+), glycosylations, and non-canonical residues often present in therapeutic targets (e.g., RbtA unknown fold).
- *Distillation-trained on PDB+MD* supplies implicit flexibility.

For RFpeptides macrocycle binder design (Rhombotarget A), AF3 predicted bound complexes accurately despite target structure itself being predicted — demonstrating tolerance to model errors provided epitope local confidence is high [11].

Crucial protocol: **AF3 ensembling with 5 seeds and 3 recycles**. Chirality violation flag for D-peptide projects must be separately checked because AF3 error rate on D-chirality inputs is 51% [8] — random. Thus for L-binders AF3 remains reliable, but D-binder therapeutics (protease-evading) require Mirror-image coding or explicit chiral restraint losses.

### 4.4 Integration: BindCraft and Complexa Stacks

BindCraft integrates steps 1–3 in one JAX loop allowing true backprop-through-AF2, yielding hallucinated sequences that *directly maximize* predicted ipTM [7]. Benchmark on 12 therapeutically relevant targets: birch pollen allergen Bet v 1, SARS-CoV-2 spike, Cas9, etc., showed high-affinity binders without HT screening, some achieving functional reduction of IgE binding in patient sera.

Complexa (architecture 2026, poster 2026) generative pretraining + test-time compute scaling further pushes atomistic design with target-conditioned variable lengths. Comparisons show Complexa + ESM-3 + AF3 co-fold ranking surpasses RFdiffusion/MPNN on diverse folds, particularly αβ mixed binder types.

### 5.2 Energetic Validation Loop

Rosetta-based physical constraints remain orthogonal to learned confidence:

- **dSASA loss**: target-binding burial drives ΔG but too high → unspecific aggregation.
- **Shape complementarity (SC)**: Lawrence & Colman metric; high SC correlates with low pAE.
- **Bunsats**: <6 unsaturated polar atoms at interface necessary for enthalpic compensation.
- **Hydrophobicity <0.37**: avoids oily interfaces that aggregate in *E. coli* inclusion bodies.

When ESM-3 generates sequences with low predicted solubility, hydrophobic filter aggressively removes 30–40% but improves downstream monodispersity.

### 5.3 Computational Cost Model

| Stage | Compute per Design | Dominant FLOPs |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| RFdiffusion 50 steps 80-mer | ~8 s A100 | pairformer attn |
| ESM-3 7B 20 steps infill | ~1.2 s H100 | self-attn over 1.4K ctx |
| ProteinMPNN 8 seq | ~0.4 s GPU | MPNN decoding |
| AlphaFold3 5 seeds full | ~45 s A100 (or server) | diffusion sampler |

Consequently, pipeline at 500 backbones × 8 sequences × AF3 top 10% implies ~14 GPU-h + 30 CPU-h per target. With NVIDIA NIM batching, feasible within 24 h turnaround — order-of-magnitude faster than 4–6 weeks hybrid immunization campaign [6][10].

## 6. Limitations

**1. Epitope and Target Bias.** Current diffusion models generalize best to concave/deep hydrophobic pockets where shape complementarity is realizable via rigid α-helix docking (MDM2, WDR5, RH, IL-7R). Flat, polar, or glycosylated surfaces (PD-1/PD-L1, many viral glycoproteins) remain low-success. Comparative BindCraft peptide data show MDM2  (hydrophobic groove) >85% success and WDR5 MYC site >70%, but PD-1/PD-L1 β-sheet mimic failed to yield measurable binding for short peptides (<35aa) [10]. Miniproteins (55–65aa) compensate by encompassing larger footprint (~1600 Å² dSASA) yet entropic cost remains.

**2. AlphaFold3 Reliability Boundaries.** Despite AF3's generality, it underperforms on representation requiring conformer ensembles: fold-switch proteins, metamorphic proteins, IDR binding coupled to folding [4]. D-peptide chirality violation at 51% [8] prohibits naive expansion to mirror-image therapeutics. AF3's non-commercial academic-only inference weights further restrict industrial scaling, requiring open reimplementations (e.g., Chai-1, OpenFold3) with lower accuracy.

**3. ESM-3 Functional Drift and Hallucination.** Large PLM generates plausible but non-functional proteins at Rate ~90–95% for stringent tasks (e.g., fluorescent retention even with structural prompting only 4/96 in esmGFP campaign [1]). Function token conditioning incompletely penalizes hallucinated active sites that are sterically occluded upon binding. Moreover, training data cutoff introduces motif memorization: ESM-3 shows measurable leakage of PDB-derived toxin binders in training set proximity, raising biosafety concerns for de-novo toxin binder misuse.

**4. Immunogenicity and Developability Not Learned.** None of the three foundation models explicitly predict immunogenicity, MHC-II presentation, or polyreactivity. Designed miniproteins, particularly ESM-3 synthetic sequences at <60% identity to human proteome, risk anti-drug antibodies. Contemporary pipelines add external tools (NetMHCIIpan, PASTA aggregation predictor, CamSol) not integrated into diffusion loss. Experimental data show RFdiffusion binders may express insoluble without fusion tag optimization.

**5. Experimental Gap: In Vitro to In Vivo.** All cited K_d values are SPR/BLI at 25°C in purified PBS; translation to cell-surface receptor engagement plus serum half-life is unmodeled. BindCraft AAV capsid retargeting and Cas9 activity modulation [7] remain proof-of-concept; off-target proteome-wide binding unprofiled.

## 7. Conclusion

We have articulated **co-design** as the logical synthesis of three seismic model classes: ***ESM-3's programmable evolutionary language***, ***RFdiffusion's SE(3) diffusional creativity***, and ***AlphaFold3's atomistic adjudication***. No lone model solves de novo binder inception — generative diversity without validation blooms into hallucination, and discriminative validation without generation cannot leave the manifold of known folds. Together, they close the loop: RFdiffusion sketches the pose, ESM-3 writes the sentence, ProteinMPNN tightens the grammar, and AlphaFold3 reads it aloud to confirm it is not nonsense.

The pipeline is not fictitious — it is the tacit standard in Baker Lab, EvolutionaryScale Forge, and Isomorphic Labs 2025 stacks, with demonstrated sub-nM binders, cryo-EM structural parity, and emerging clinical utility in allergen blockade and AAV retargeting [2][7][11]. Remaining challenges are domain-generalization to flat polar interfaces, chirality-aware diffusion, immunogenicity-integrated losses, and experimental-scale proof bridging purified K_d to cellular function and safety.

Future directions converge on **all-atom diffusion conditioned jointly on ligand and protein** (RFdiffusionAA, AtomFlow, Chroma Scaffolding), RLHF from DMF-based display datasets to align binder diffusion with enrichment, and *in-cell* language model prompting where function tokens include cellular localization and half-life rather than mere GO tags. Within five years, we anticipate fully autonomous binder factories — prompt in target FASTA + epitope mask, out antibody-mimetic with IND-ready developability — executing at cloud-scale cost under regulatory audit of bio-foundation model lineage.

The age of *evolutionary reading* ended with AlphaFold2; the age of *evolutionary writing* begins with this trinity.
