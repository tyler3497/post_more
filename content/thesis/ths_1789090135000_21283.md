# From Univalence to Computation: Homotopy Type Theory, Cubical Sets, and the Constructive Content of Equality

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory reinterprets Martin-Löf type theory through the lens of homotopy: types are spaces, terms are points, and proofs of identity are paths, with higher identity types furnishing the structure that makes every type a weak ∞-groupoid. Voevodsky's univalence axiom extends this picture by identifying equivalences between types with equalities between them, restoring a principled form of *structural invariance* to the foundations of mathematics. Yet univalence, taken as an axiom, destroys the computational character of type theory: a closed term of natural-number type that mentions the axiom need not reduce to a numeral, breaking the canonicity on which proof assistants rely. This thesis develops the constructive resolution. We present the cubical sets model of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, in which the interval, path types, Kan composition, and *glueing* operations make univalence a theorem with definitional computation rules; we prove canonicity by sconing; we develop higher inductive types, including the circle and the torus; and we survey the mechanization of this edifice in Cubical Agda, where synthetic homotopy theory is carried out with native computational univalence.

---

## 1. Introduction

In 2006, Vladimir Voevodsky observed that the identity type of Martin-Löf type theory admits a homotopical interpretation so compelling that it reorganizes the subject: the *univalence axiom*, which states that the canonical map from identity of types to equivalence of types is itself an equivalence, would unify the notion of equality with the notion of equivalence at the foundational level [1]. Combined with *higher inductive types*, this interpretation, elaborated in the HoTT Book [2], suggested a new foundation of mathematics in which mathematical structures are *invariant under equivalence*: equivalent structures literally have identical properties, because their equivalence can be transported along univalence into an equality.

This vision was immediately seized upon by the proof-assistant community, for a pressing practical reason. In Coq, Agda, or Lean, proofs that two functions are equal require *function extensionality*, a principle that must be postulated as an axiom in intensional type theory. But an axiom is a black box to the type checker: a closed term of type `Nat` built using an axiom does not reduce to a numeral. *Canonicity* — the theorem that every closed term of the natural-number type computes to a numeral — is the bedrock property that makes a proof assistant a programming language, and the univalence axiom as axiomatically postulated breaks it [3].

The decisive advance was the discovery that univalence need not be postulated at all. Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg constructed a model of dependent type theory in cubical sets — a *presheaf* model over a category of cubes with de Morgan algebra structure — in which a new type former, *glueing*, makes univalence *provable*, with definitional equations governing its computation [4]. Huber then proved canonicity for the resulting cubical type theory: every closed term of natural-number type is definitionally a numeral [5]. Equality had acquired computational content.

This thesis traces that intellectual arc end to end. **Section 2** develops the background: the homotopical interpretation of identity, the simplicial sets model of Kapulkin–Lumsdaine that established consistency but non-constructively [6], and the definition of the univalence axiom. **Section 3** presents our methodology: the presheaf construction over the Lawvere theory of de Morgan algebras, the interval object, path types, Kan composition via homogeneous composition and generalized transport, glue types, and the sconing proof of canonicity. **Section 4**, the deep dive, treats five subjects in depth: (i) the higher groupoid structure of identity types; (ii) the Glue construction and the proof of univalence; (iii) higher inductive types and the synthetic circle; (iv) the decomposition of Kan filling that enables general HITs; and (v) mechanization in Cubical Agda. **Section 5** collects the key theorems with their proofs sketched. **Section 6** discusses the limitations of the cubical program, and **Section 7** concludes with the case that cubical type theory is the first semantics of equality that is simultaneously *homotopical*, *computational*, and *practical*.

> **Thesis claim:** The univalence axiom is not an axiom at all. It is a theorem of a well-designed computational type theory, and the cubical sets construction exhibits the definitional equations that constitute its proof — thereby restoring canonicity, enabling synthetic homotopy theory with computational content, and giving the mechanization of equality a sound operational semantics.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf Type Theory and the Identity Type

Martin-Löf type theory is a dependently typed foundation in which every term has a type and types may depend on terms.

```haskell
J : (C : (x : A) -> Id A a x -> Set)
  -> C a refl
  -> (x : A) -> (p : Id A a x) -> C x p
```

with the definitional computation rule `J C c a refl ≡ c`.

The crucial subtlety is *proof relevance*: identity proofs are data, and there may be many distinct proofs of the same equation.

### 2.2 The Homotopical Interpretation

Awodey and Warren made the homotopical interpretation systematic: any *model category* in the sense of Quillen admits an interpretation of type theory in which dependent types are fibrations, and identity types are *path objects* — factorizations of the diagonal `A → A × A` as an acyclic cofibration followed by a fibration [8].

| Type theory | Homotopy theory |
|---|---|
| Type `A` | Space |
| Term `a : A` | Point |
| `p : Id A a b` | Path from `a` to `b` |
| `α : Id (Id A a b) p q` | Homotopy between paths |
| Dependent type `B : A → Set` | Fibration over `A` |

The dependent eliminator `J` becomes path lifting / transport along fibrations.

### 2.3 Univalence

A map `f : A → B` is an *equivalence* when each of its fibers is contractible; equivalently, when it has a two-sided inverse up to homotopy.

> **Theorem (Univalence):** The canonical map `id-to-equiv : Id U A B → (A ≃ B)` is itself an equivalence.

Consequences cascade.

### 2.4 The Simplicial Model and Its Limitations

Kapulkin and Lumsdaine, following Voevodsky, constructed a model of type theory with univalence in *Kan simplicial sets*: types are interpreted as Kan complexes, the universe as the simplicial set of small Kan fibrations, and univalence is validated by the *equivalence extension* property [6].

### 2.5 Prior Constructive Attempts

Bezem, Coquand, and Huber gave the first constructive model of univalence in cubical sets [9], but over a cube category *without* the connections and diagonals that later proved decisive, and they did not formulate a type theory internalizing the construction.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The Base Category and the Interval

We work in the presheaf category `Ĉ = Set^(C^op)` where `C` is the *Lawvere theory of de Morgan algebras*: objects are finite sets of names (dimensions), and morphisms are substitutions of de Morgan terms.

The interval supports a *path type former*: for `A : I → U` a line of types and `a : A 0`, `b : A 1`,

```
Path A a b   (often written PathP A a b)
```

whose elements are introduced by *path abstraction* `λ i.

### 3.2 Kan Composition: hcomp and Transport

Types must support *Kan filling*: given a partial cube (an "open box") with a specified lid, a filler exists.

1. **Homogeneous composition** `hcomp`: fills an open box in a *constant* type, composing a tube of paths with a cap.
2. **Generalized transport** `transp`: transports along a *line of types* `A : I → U`, coercing `a : A 0` to `transp^i A a : A 1`.

The general composition `comp^i A [φ ↦ u] a₀` is then definable from these two.

For path types, composition is the familiar *concatenation of paths* and *reversal*; for Π-types it is pointwise; for the universe it is the univalence-relevant operation.

```
λ i. λ j. p @ (i ∧ j)
```

witnesses that `p ∙ refl ≡ p`, definitionally.

### 3.3 Glue Types and Univalence as a Theorem

Given a type `B`, a *partial* type `A` defined on a face `φ`, and a partial equivalence `e : A ≃ B` on `φ`, the *glue type*

```
Glue B [φ ↦ (A, e)]
```

is `B` away from `φ` and `A` on `φ`, with elements `glue [φ ↦ a] b` and `unglue : Glue B […] → B`.

```
transp^i (ua e) a ≡ e .fun a
```

Transport along `ua e` *computes* to application of the equivalence.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Higher Groupoid Structure of Identity

With paths as primitive, the groupoid laws are *squares*, not inductions.

- **Reversal:** `sym p := λ i. p @ (~ i) : Path A b a`.
- **Concatenation:** `p ∙ q` is obtained by composing the open box with tube `[i=0 ↦ a, i=1 ↦ q]` and cap `p`; the de Morgan connection `i ∧ j` fills the square witnessing `p ∙ refl ≡ p`.
- **Associativity:** a cube filler gives `Path (Path A a d) ((p ∙ q) ∙ r) (p ∙ (q ∙ r))`.

All coherence laws hold *up to higher paths*, and the tower of coherences is automatic: it is just the Kan structure of the path type iterated.

> **Theorem (Groupoid laws):** For every type `A`, the path types equip `A` with the structure of a weak ∞-groupoid: composition, units, and inverses satisfying all coherence laws up to higher paths. [2, Ch. 2]

In Cubical Agda, `cong`, `subst`, and `J` all compute on `refl` definitionally, and `transport` along a path in a family reduces by the `transp` operation of the family — the "substitution" of homotopy type theory has an operational semantics.

### 4.2 The Glue Construction and the Proof of Univalence

The heart of the constructive account is the *Glue* type.

1. For `e : A ≃ B`, define the line `ua e : I → U` by `ua e i := Glue B [i = 0 ↦ (A, e), i = 1 ↦ (B, id-equiv)]`. At `i = 0` this is `A` (with the equivalence data identifying it with `B`); at `i = 1` it is `B`.
2. The endpoints satisfy `(ua e) 0 ≡ A` and `(ua e) 1 ≡ B` definitionally, so `λ i. ua e i : Path U A B`.
3. Transport along this line satisfies `transp^i (ua e) a ≡ equivFun e a` — this is the *computation rule* for univalence, the equation that the axiomatic approach lacks.
4. The maps `id-to-equiv : Path U A B → A ≃ B` and `ua : A ≃ B → Path U A B` are mutually inverse: `ua (id-to-equiv p)` is a line of types, and a second glueing over a square shows it is path-equal to `p`; conversely `id-to-equiv (ua e)` is the equivalence obtained by transporting along `ua e`, which is `e` by the computation rule.

> **Theorem (Univalence, constructive):** The type `isUnivalent U := (A B : U) → isEquiv (id-to-equiv A B)` is inhabited, and `transport (ua e)` computes to the underlying function of `e`. [4]


### 4.3 Higher Inductive Types and the Synthetic Circle

A *higher inductive type* is generated by point constructors and *path constructors* with specified boundaries.

```rust
// Cubical Agda syntax (illustrative)
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator must handle the path constructor: to define `f : S¹ → B`, give `b : B` and a *dependent path* `l : PathP (λ i → B) b b` lying over `loop`.

The payoff is the synthetic computation of `π₁(S¹)`:

> **Theorem (Licata–Shulman):** `Path S¹ base base ≃ ℤ`. The loop space of the circle is the integers. [13]

The proof is the encode-decode argument: define `Code : S¹ → U` by `Code base := ℤ`, `Code (loop i) := ua succEquiv i`; show `Σ (x : S¹), Code x` contractible; conclude.

Higher constructors can also be *truncations*: the propositional truncation `∥ A ∥` with `∣_∣ : A → ∥ A ∥` and `squash : (x y : ∥ A ∥) → x ≡ y` makes proof-irrelevance available without axioms, and the set truncation gives `π₀`.

### 4.4 Decomposing Kan Filling: Homogeneous Composition and Transport

The original CCHM theory presented a single `comp` operation; Cubical Agda and the cubicaltt checker decompose it as noted in §3.2.

1. **HITs:** `transp` on a HIT is defined by recursion on constructors, never needing to compose in a varying type; `hcomp` needs only the homogeneous case.
2. **Definitional equalities:** the computation rules for `transp` at each type former are simple equations (e.g., on Π-types, `transp^i ((x : A i) → B i x) f ≡ λ x. transp^i (B i (transp^i A x)) (f (transp^i A x))`-style), which the evaluator implements directly.
3. **Metatheory:** Orton and Pitts axiomatized the structure — an *internal* language with an interval, cofibrations, and filling operations — showing that the cubical sets models are instances of a general topos-theoretic pattern [15].


### 4.5 Cubical Agda: Univalence as a Programming Construct

Cubical Agda turns the metatheory into a tool [12].

- **Native `ua`:** `ua : A ≃ B → A ≡ B` with `transport (ua e) x ≡ e .fst x` definitionally. Programmers transport *representations*: develop a theory of unary naturals, compute with binary naturals, and `ua` mediates with zero runtime cost.
- **HITs with definitional higher computation:** pattern matching extends to path constructors, so functions out of `S¹` compute on `loop` by the clause the user wrote.
- **Propositional and structural extensionality:** `funExt`, `propExt`, and quotient types are theorems, not axioms — so developments using them retain canonicity and extract to runnable code.
- **The `agda/cubical` library:** thousands of lines of synthetic mathematics — groups, rings, categories, and homotopy theory — all type-checked against the cubical primitives.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

We collect the central results, each with the locus of its proof.

**Result 1 — Function extensionality (definitional).** In cubical type theory, `funExt : ((x : A) → f x ≡ g x) → f ≡ g` is `funExt h := λ i x. h x @ i`. No axiom; the proof is one line. *Source:* [4, §3.2].

**Result 2 — Univalence with computation.** `ua : A ≃ B → Path U A B` satisfies `transport (ua e) a ≡ e.fun a`. The equivalence `id-to-equiv` is an equivalence with inverse `ua`. *Source:* [4, §6]; mechanized in Cubical Agda [12].

**Result 3 — Canonicity.** If `⊢ t : ℕ` in cubical type theory, then `t ≡ sucⁿ zero` for some `n`. Proof by sconing over the presheaf model. *Source:* [5]; homotopy canonicity without the lifting equations in [11].

**Result 4 — The circle's loop space.** `Ω(S¹, base) ≃ ℤ` (Licata–Shulman [13]); the encode-decode proof computes winding numbers. Extended to synthetic spheres and the Hopf fibration [14].

**Result 5 — Consistency of univalence.** The simplicial sets model validates univalence, hence univalence is consistent relative to ZFC with universes. *Source:* [6]. The cubical model [4] gives the stronger *constructive* consistency: univalence holds in a predicative, computational metatheory.

**Result 6 — Mechanized synthetic homotopy theory.** The `agda/cubical` library formalizes the results above with computational univalence, including the equivalence between the torus and the product of two circles — a proof that is nearly trivial in Cubical Agda because higher constructors compute definitionally, where it was a substantial formalization in book HoTT. *Source:* [12].

| Result | Setting | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Univalence | Simplicial sets | Consistent (classical) [6] |
| Univalence | Cubical sets | Theorem, constructive [4] |
| Canonicity with univalence | Cubical TT | Proved via sconing [5] |
| `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ` | Book HoTT / Cubical | Proved, computes [13][14] |
| HIT schema | Cubical Agda | Implemented, definitional [12] |
| Axioms for cubical models | Topos-internal | Axiomatized [15] |

## 6. Limitations

1. **Interval pretype vs. type.** The interval `I` is a *pretype*, not a fibrant type: one cannot eliminate from `I` into arbitrary types, only into the path machinery. This complicates some constructions and the informal exposition; it is a genuine irregularity of the syntax.
2. **Performance of transport.** Cubical evaluation is correct but can be slow: deeply nested `transp`/`hcomp` terms blow up during type checking, and proof engineers report order-of-magnitude slowdowns relative to axiomatic HoTT on some developments.
3. **Universe and size issues.** The Glue construction and the universe's Kan structure raise the universe level in delicate ways; `Glue` on `Set` lives in `Set`, but the general theory requires careful level bookkeeping, and impredicativity is unavailable.
4. **No general schema proof for all HITs (in CCHM).** The original paper treats the circle and truncations; the general schema for higher inductive types in cubical settings was developed afterward [10] and its metatheory (canonicity for the full HIT schema) remains more intricate than for the core theory.
5. **Homotopy canonicity vs. strict canonicity.** The strict canonicity proof [5] depends on the definitional equations for the path-lifting operation; Coquand–Huber–Sattler [11] showed that dropping them retains only *homotopy* canonicity. The exact boundary of which equations are necessary is still being mapped.
6. **The simplicial/cubical gap.** Results proved in book HoTT (simplicial semantics) transfer to cubical settings in principle, but the transfer is not always automatic: definitional behaviors differ, and some book-HoTT arguments rely on strictures the cubical theory lacks.
7. **Adoption.** Despite its advantages, cubical type theory remains a research frontier; the major proof assistants (Coq, Lean) do not natively implement it, and the library ecosystems are an order of magnitude smaller than their axiomatic counterparts.

## 7. Conclusion

We have traced the arc from Voevodsky's univalence axiom — a postulate that made equality behave like equivalence at the cost of computation — to the cubical reconstruction in which univalence is a *theorem with a definitional computation rule*. The key moves were: (i) the homotopical reading of identity types [8]; (ii) the simplicial model establishing consistency [6]; (iii) the cubical sets model with de Morgan cubes, path types, Kan composition, and glueing [4]; (iv) the sconing proof of canonicity [5]; (v) the extension to higher inductive types with computational higher constructors [10]; and (vi) the mechanization in Cubical Agda [12].
