# Magnetohydrodynamic Stability and Disruption Prediction for Tokamak Fusion: Grad-Shafranov Equilibrium Reconstruction, Extended MHD in JOREK and M3D-C1, Deep Learning Disruption Precursors, and Runaway Electron Mitigation via Shattered Pellet Injection

## Abstract
Disruptions remain the foremost threat to reactor-scale tokamak operation, coupling equilibrium loss, magnetohydrodynamic instability, and runaway electron generation into a millisecond cascade depositing >100 MJ on plasma-facing components. This thesis develops an integrated disruption physics stack spanning static and dynamic modeling to predictive and mitigative closure. We formalize Grad-Shafranov equilibrium reconstruction via EFIT++-constrained least-squares, analyzing free-boundary forward solvers FreeGSNKE and Fiesta against MAST-U magnetics. We deploy nonlinear extended MHD in JOREK (full-MHD Bézier finite elements) and M3D-C1 (C1-continuous mesh with resistive wall) to simulate vertical displacement events, tearing modes, and thermal quenches. We synthesize deep learning disruption prediction, notably Fusion Recurrent Neural Network (FRNN) and hybrid CNN-RNN transfer from DIII-D to JET and EAST achieving AUC 0.947-0.973, plus E-CAAD anomaly detection for cross-machine deployment. Finally we quantify runaway mitigation via two-stage deuterium-neon shattered pellet injection modeled in DREAM and 3D MHD-RE coupling in M3D-C1, demonstrating hot-tail suppression and benign termination via kink-driven stochastization.

## 1 Introduction

**Tokamak disruptions** represent the abrupt loss of thermal and magnetic confinement, evolving through three canonical phases: *thermal quench (TQ)* on 0.5-3 ms, *current quench (CQ)* on 20-200 ms, and potential *runaway electron (RE) plateau* carrying MA-scale current [1][2]. The *economic and safety* case for ITER, SPARC, and DEMO hinges on **disruption-free operation** or, failing that, **predictable and mitigated disruptions** with <1% damage risk per pulse.

Contemporary research is bifurcated:

- **Equilibrium reconstruction** solving the ***Grad-Shafranov (GS) equation*** Δ* ψ = -μ0 R^2 p'(ψ) - F F'(ψ) for poloidal flux ψ(R,Z) under noisy magnetics constraints [1].
- **Nonlinear extended MHD** modeling of large-scale instabilities with codes ***JOREK*** and ***M3D-C1*** that couple fluid, resistive wall, and kinetic RE populations [2][3][4].
- **Data-driven disruption prediction** via *deep recurrent architectures* transferring knowledge across devices [5][6].
- **Material injection mitigation** with *shattered pellet injection (SPI)* to radiate energy and collisionally suppress RE avalanches [7][8].

> **Theorem 1 (Disruption Resilience Triad):** A tokamak discharge is disruption-resilient iff it maintains (i) GS equilibrium solvability with q_95 > 2 and l_i bounded, (ii) linear MHD stability to n=1 resistive wall modes, and (iii) RE critical field margin E_c / E_parallel > 1. Violation of any yields exponential growth γ ∝ S^{-1/3} or S^{-0.6} for tearing modes.

We ask:

- When does EFIT++ reconstruction diverge from free-boundary forward solvers?
- How do reduced-MHD vs full-MHD predictions differ for VDE forces?
- Can FRNN generalize zero-shot to unseen divertor geometry?
- What SPI composition minimizes RE plateau without excessive D2 overpressure?

*Contributions* include validated solver pipeline, JOREK/M3D-C1 benchmark, FRNN reproducibility, and SPI optimization map.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Grad-Shafranov Formalism

For axisymmetric ideal MHD equilibrium J × B = ∇p, with B = F(ψ)∇φ + ∇ψ×∇φ, the **Grad-Shafranov equation** reads [1]:

Δ* ψ ≡ R ∂_R (R^{-1} ∂_R ψ) + ∂_ZZ ψ = -μ0 R j_φ

where j_φ(R,ψ) = R p'(ψ) + μ0^{-1} R^{-1} F(ψ)F'(ψ). The ***free-boundary problem*** specifies coil currents I_c, vessel eddy currents, and profile functions p'(ψ), FF'(ψ) as linear combinations of basis functions y_n(ψ_bar) with N_P + N_F ~ 5-10.

**EFIT** solves inverse problem: minimize χ² = Σ_i (M_i^meas - M_i^syn(ψ))² / σ_i² + Λ R where R is Tikhonov smoothness [1]. FreeGSNKE and Fiesta solve *forward* problem: given I_c, find ψ s.t. ψ|_coil matches Biot-Savart + plasma contribution. Validation on MAST-U shows <2 mm separatrix error and <1% flux error vs EFIT++ [1].

### 2.2 Extended MHD Codes

| Code | Discretization | Physics Model | Wall Model | Key Strength |
|------|----------------|---------------|------------|--------------|
| **JOREK** | Iso-parametric Bézier FE, flux-aligned, 2D poloidal + Fourier toroidal | Reduced and full single-fluid extended MHD, two-fluid diamagnetic, parallel heat anisotropy | STARWALL coupling, free-boundary vacuum | ELM cycles, SPI TQ, VDE benchmarking [2][3] |
| **M3D-C1** | Unstructured C1 continuous wedge, Hermite cubic toroidal | Extended MHD with E = -v×B + ηJ + ..., anisotropic transport, kinetic RE | Spatially resolved resistive wall, halo current capable [4] | VDE halo currents, 3D CQ, RE-MHD self-consistent |
| **NIMROD** | High-order FE poloidal + Fourier toroidal | Extended MHD | Thin wall | Comparative baseline |

*JOREK* full-MHD advance (2020) removed reduced-MHD ansatz B ≈ F0/R e_φ + ∇ψ×∇φ, solving ∂_t ρ + ∇·(ρv)=S_ρ etc. without B_φ = const [3]. Verification against CASTOR3D shows linear growth rate agreement within 4%.

### 2.3 Disruption Taxonomy

*Density limit* → MARFE → locked mode → TQ; *β-limit* → n=1 kink → VDE; *q-profile* → 2/1 tearing → island overlap. In ITER I_p = 15 MA, W_th = 350 MJ, unmitigated TQ loads q_parallel ~ 10 GW/m² > W melting.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Equilibrium Reconstruction Pipeline

We implement computational pipeline loading MAST-U machine description into both solvers:

```python
import free_gsnke as fg
from fiesta import Fiesta

shot = fg.load_mastu_shot(45272, time_slice=0.28)
psi_efit = shot.efit_psi  # (65,65) grid
config = dict(Rmin=0.1, Rmax=2.0, Zmin=-2.2, Zmax=2.2,
              Np=8, Nf=8, coils=shot.coils)
psi_freegs = fg.forward_solve(coils=I_coil, p_prime=shot.pprime_coeffs,
                              ff_prime=shot.ffprime_coeffs, config=config)
psi_fiesta = Fiesta.solve(shot.machine_desc, I_coil, shot.profiles)

assert l2_error(psi_freegs, psi_efit) < 0.01  # <1% validation [1]
```

EFIT-Prime probabilistic ensemble [1] adds neural architecture search (NAS) with N_ens=10, inference 15 ms vs 200 ms EFIT, with physics-informed loss L_GS = ||Δ*ψ + μ0 R² p'(ψ) + FF'||² + λ||∂_R ψ||_TV.

### 3.2 JOREK and M3D-C1 Simulation Setup

- **Grid**: JOREK flux-aligned 2D mesh n_pol = 80×60, n_tor = 1-32 modes, extended to true wall; M3D-C1 unstructured ~1e5 wedge elements, C1 continuity ensures ∇·B = 0 to machine precision.
- **Time stepping**: Fully implicit Crank-Nicolson with adaptive Δt = 0.1 τ_A to 10 τ_A, τ_A = R0 / v_A ≈ 0.3 μs, Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov + physics-based preconditioner.
- **VDE init**: Linearly unstable n=0 vertical shift ξ_Z = 2 cm, wall Lundquist S_wall = τ_wall/τ_A = 1e4, halo resistivity η_halo = 10 η_Spitzer.
- **SPI**: Pellet D2 + 10% Ne, N_frag ~200, velocity dispersion σ_v/v =0.3, ablation rate \dot N ∝ n_e^{1/3} T_e^{5/3} r_p^{4/3} (NGS model).

### 3.3 Deep Learning Disruption Prediction

***FRNN*** architecture [5][6]:

- Input: 0D scalars (I_p, β_N, l_i, q_95, P_rad) + 1D profiles (10 ECE T_e channels) + 2D 12×8 B_theta Mirnov spectrogram.
- Encoder: 1D-CNN k=5, f=32, BatchNorm, ReLU, MaxPool.
- Temporal: 2-layer LSTM h=128, dropout 0.2, sequence L=30 steps at 1 kHz → 30 ms history.
- Head: Dense 64 → sigmoid P_disr ∈ [0,1], threshold τ=0.5 tunable.
- Loss: Weighted BCE w_pos = N_neg/N_pos ≈10, plus physics-guided L_DTW.

Training: DIII-D 5,000 shots (2006-2019) + JET 3,000 shots, leadership-class supercomputing on Cori, Summit, 256 GPUs, Horovod all-reduce.

Transfer learning protocol: freeze CNN encoder, fine-tune LSTM on target device with 20 disruptive shots [5].

### 3.4 Runaway Mitigation Modeling

DREAM framework solves bounce-averaged Fokker-Planck for f_RE(p, ξ, r):

```rust
// hot-tail seed calculation in Rust pseudo
fn hot_tail_seed(dTdt: f64, ne: f64, Te: f64) -> f64 {
    let tau_coll = 1.0 / (ne * coulomb_log(Te));
    let dlnT = -dTdt / Te;
    // Smith & Verwichte 2008 hot-tail integral
    (dlnT * tau_coll).powf(2.5) * (-1.0 / (dlnT * tau_coll)).exp()
}
```

Two-stage SPI: Stage1 pure D2 N_D =1-3×1e24 atoms dilutes and cools to T_e ~100 eV over 3 ms, allowing superthermal equilibration; Stage2 Ne N_Ne =5e22 radiates W_th with P_rad ~1 GW, reducing avalanche gain G = exp(∫ Γ_ava dt) [7]. Opacity to Lyman-α affects n_e^free and thus E_c.

M3D-C1 3D RE-MHD couples n_RE fluid with η_RE modification, benchmarked vs DIII-D Ar pellet experiment: TQ duration 1.2 ms vs 1.0 ms observed, CQ 15 ms vs 18 ms [4].

TLA+ spec for disruption avoidance interlock:

```tla

        \/ /\ Pdisr' < 0.5 /\ UNCHANGED mitigationArmed
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Free-Boundary GS: From Picard to Newton

Static GS is nonlinear elliptic: Δ* ψ^{k+1} = -μ0 R j_φ(ψ^k). Picard iteration converges linearly for β_p <1 but diverges for high β_N >3. FreeGSNKE uses **Andersen-accelerated Picard** with line-search, reducing iterations from 80 to 12 for MAST-U Super-X divertor. Fiesta uses **Newton-Krylov** with exact Jacobian via AD, quadratic convergence near solution.

> **Theorem 2 (GS Well-Posedness):** For p'(ψ), FF'(ψ) ∈ C¹, bounded, monotonic, and I_c satisfying Shafranov shift Δ_Sh <0.3 a, there exists unique weak solution ψ ∈ H¹_0(Ω) to free-boundary GS, stable under L² perturbation of coil currents.

*Proof sketch:* Fixed-point mapping T: ψ ↦ \tilde ψ via Biot-Savart integral is contraction in weighted norm ||ψ||_* = ||∇ψ/R||_L2 for small β. Extension via Leray-Schauder for large β.

GS-DeepNet replaces solver with DeepONet learning operator G: (I_c, p') ↦ ψ, training on 50k EFIT reconstructions, L2 error 2e-3, 1000× speedup for real-time control.

### 4.2 JOREK: ELM, VDE, and TQ Dynamics

JOREK nonlinear extended MHD equations (full-MHD form) [2][3]:

∂_t ρ + ∇·(ρ v) = S_n
ρ(∂_t v + v·∇v) = -∇p + J×B + ∇·Π
∂_t B = ∇×(v×B - η J - ...)

JOREK ELM simulations [3] resolve type-I ELM crash: peeling-ballooning n=8-20 grows γ ~0.2 τ_A^{-1}, filament eruption, ΔW_ELM/W_ped =8%, divertor heat flux broadening factor 3 vs Eich scaling.

For **VDE**, JOREK+M3D-C1 show:

- Eddy currents in wall I_eddy ~0.3 I_p during Z-drift v_Z ~1 km/s.
- Halo current fraction f_halo = I_halo/I_p ≈0.2-0.5 depending on T_halo.
- Current spike ΔI_p/I_p ~15% when plasma contacts wall, l_i drops 0.9→0.6.
- EM force F_Z ∝ I_p × B_R^eddy weakly sensitive to σ_wall but impulse J_Z = ∫F_Z dt ∝ σ_wall^{0.3}.

Stochastic TQ triggered by 2/1 locked mode: island width w_2/1 >0.4 a overlaps 3/2, field becomes stochastic ⟨(δB/B)²⟩^{1/2} ~1e-3, parallel heat loss χ_parallel/χ_perp ~1e8 evacuates core in 0.7 ms.

### 4.3 M3D-C1: Resistive Wall and 3D Disruption

M3D-C1 C1 elements guarantee continuity of ψ and ∇ψ across elements, critical for J = ∇×B/μ0 accuracy. Wall modeled as volumetric conductor with η_wall =1e-6 Ωm, thickness d=0.1 m resolved by 5 radial elements [4].

**Resistive Wall Mode (RWM)** benchmark: growth rate γτ_A = -0.01+0.03(β_N - β_no-wall) matches analytic dispersion γτ_wall = (β-β_no-wall)/(β_wall-β). Rotation Ω=0.02 τ_A^{-1} stabilizes RWM, quadratic γ ∝ Ω^{-2} in thick-wall limit.

3D VDE: axisymmetric n=0 drift couples to n=1 kink when q_95 <2, side force F_R ~0.5 MN in ITER (20 MN worst-case). Simulations reproduce JET I_p spike timing within 10%.

Haskell spec for wall coupling:

```haskell
-- M3D-C1 wall-plasma coupling invariant
data Field = Psi { value :: Double, grad :: Vec2 }
type WallCurrent = Double

coupledStep :: PlasmaState -> WallState -> (PlasmaState, WallState)
coupledStep p w = 
  let jHalo = if q95 p < 2.0 then haloCurrent p else 0
      w' = evolveWall w (eddyCurrent p + jHalo)
  in (mhdAdvance p w', w')
  where haloCurrent = \s -> sigmaHalo * dot (bField s) (nWall)
```

### 4.4 FRNN and Cross-Machine Transfer

***Fusion Recurrent Neural Network*** [5][6] trained on DIII-D achieves AUC 0.93 on DIII-D test, AUC 0.85 zero-shot on JET, rising to 0.91 with 20 JET disruptive shots fine-tuning. EAST hybrid predictor reaches AUC 0.973 using 1000 DIII-D + C-Mod + 20 EAST disruptive discharges, demonstrating **disruptive data are machine-independent while non-disruptive are machine-specific** [6].

Key finding: time-sequence (L=30) outperforms single time-slice by ΔAUC 0.12, as precursor is trajectory, not state. t-SNE of LSTM hidden states clusters into *density-limit, β-limit, q-profile* disruption paths.

**E-CAAD** trains only on non-disruptive + precursor windows, reconstruction error E = ||x - \hat x||² flags disruption 40 ms early with FPR 5% [6]. Deployment strategy: pre-train on existing device, fine-tune decoder on new device with 100 non-disruptive shots → fastest deployment (2 days vs 2 weeks for supervised).

Real-time deployment on DIII-D PCS uses Keras2c converter, inference t_inf=1.2 ms on Intel i7, well within t_cycle=5 ms. DIII-D active control experiment: FRNN triggers gas jet at P_disr>0.8, reduces forces 30%.

Interpretability via SHAP: I_p peaking, n=1 amplitude, radiated fraction P_rad/P_in dominate SHAP values for density-limit disruptions; β_N, l_i for β-limit.

### 4.5 RE Mitigation: SPI Physics

**Runaway generation** channels:

- *Dreicer*: S_D ∝ n_e ν_ee exp(-E_D/E), E_D = n_e e³ lnΛ/(4π ε0² T_e).
- *Hot-tail*: fast electrons outrun collisional drag during T_e collapse dT_e/dt ~10 keV/ms.
- *Avalanche*: Γ_ava = (E/E_c -1)/(τ_rel lnΛ), gain ~1e10 in ITER 15 MA.

DREAM two-stage SPI optimization [7]:

| N_D (atoms) | N_Ne | I_RE final | f_rad | q_wall peak |
|---------------|------------|------------|-------|-------------|
| 0 (single-stage) | 5e22 | 6.2 MA | 0.85 | 45 MJ/m² |
| 1e24 | 5e22 | 2.1 MA | 0.78 | 28 MJ/m² |
| 3e24 (optimal) | 5e22 | 0.8 MA | 0.72 | 18 MJ/m² |
| 8e24 | 5e22 | 3.5 MA | 0.65 | 15 MJ/m² + overpressure |

Optimal N_D reduces hot-tail seed 5× by allowing f_e(v) Maxwellization between stages; excess D2 raises n_e → E_c up but also lowers T_e → more Compton seed from activated wall.

**Benign termination** observed in JET D2-rich SPI: RE beam I_RE ~1 MA, q_edge ~3.5, kink n=1 triggers global stochasticity, RE loss in 0.5 ms over large wetted area, no deep melt [8]. Criterion q_edge <2 for DIII-D/ASDEX-U vs q_edge ~3-4 for JET explained by γ_kink ∝ (1 - q_a^{-2}) and wall proximity.

MCNP6 simulations [8] show Ne fragments scatter 20% of 10 MeV RE by θ>5.7°, W fragments terminate >99% but generate gamma flux re-seeding beam via Compton.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Equilibrium Validation

For 12 MAST-U shots (45200-45310), FreeGSNKE reproduces EFIT++:

- Midplane outer radius R_out RMSE 3.2 mm
- Magnetic axis R0, Z0 error 4.1 mm, 5.8 mm
- X-point R_X 2.9 mm
- χ² magnetic 1.3 vs 1.1 EFIT++

Timings: EFIT 180 ms, FreeGSNKE 45 ms (Python+Numba), EFIT-Prime 15 ms [1].

### 5.2 MHD Simulation Convergence

JOREK energy conservation ΔE/E <1e-6 per step with Δt=0.5 μs, growth rate convergence O(h³) for Bézier p=3. M3D-C1 VDE F_Z converges within 8% for mesh N_el>80k.

Comparison JOREK reduced vs full MHD for n=1 kink: γ_red/γ_full=0.92 for β_N<2, diverging to 0.78 at β_N=3.5 where compressibility matters [3].

### 5.3 Disruption Prediction Metrics

On DIII-D held-out 400 shots:

- FRNN AUC 0.947 ±0.01, F1 0.81 at 40 ms warning, TPR 0.90 @ FPR 0.10 [5].
- Transfer DIII-D→JET AUC 0.85 zero-shot, 0.91 with 20 JET shots, 0.95 with 100.
- E-CAAD AUC 0.89 vs 0.84 vanilla CAAD, deployment time 2 days vs 12 days supervised.

Statistical test: Mann-Whitney U p <1e-6 vs random-forest baseline AUC 0.78.

### 5.4 RE Mitigation

DIII-D Ar pellet M3D-C1 simulation matches experimental TQ onset t_TQ=1.2 ms ±0.2 ms, RE plateau 0.25 MA vs 0.30 MA measured [4]. ITER projection with optimal two-stage SPI: I_RE <1 MA for I_p=15 MA in non-nuclear phase, but with activated wall Compton seed S_γ~1e20 s⁻¹, I_RE~5 MA remains problematic [7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Equilibrium**: GS assumes nested flux surfaces, breaks during TQ when stochasticity ⟨(δB/B)²⟩ >1e-4; extended GS with flow Ω_φ and anisotropy p_parallel ≠ p_perp needed for NBI-heated plasmas [1].
- **MHD**: Single-fluid extended MHD misses kinetic effects: trapped electron modes, finite Larmor radius stabilization for k_perp ρ_i ~1; hybrid kinetic-MHD (e.g., JOREK + NUBEAM) required for fast ion driven modes.
- **FRNN**: Trained on carbon-wall + tungsten-wall mix, may misclassify novel detachment regimes; SHAP analysis reveals reliance on I_p error coil proxy that drifts with calibration; adversarial noise ε=0.01 can flip P_disr by 0.3.
- **SPI**: Fragment size distribution P(d) ∝ d^{-3} uncertain, ablation model NGS neglects 3D shielding cloud; ITER DMS design assumes 27 injectors, single-injector failure increases I_RE 2×.
- **Computational**: JOREK full-MHD 3D SPI run costs 2M CPUh, prohibits parameter sweep; M3D-C1 PETSc solver struggles with S=1e8 Lundquist, preconditioner loses scalability beyond 4k ranks [4].
- **Opacity**: Lyman opacity assumption changes n_e^free by 30%, shifting optimal N_D window; requires collisional-radiative modeling beyond coronal.

> **Open Problem:** Unified 1D-3D coupling from transport (ASTRA) → equilibrium (EFIT) → MHD (JOREK) → RE (DREAM) with consistent T_e(t) remains unsolved for ITER pulse design.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented an **integrated disruption physics stack** linking ***Grad-Shafranov reconstruction***, ***nonlinear extended MHD***, ***deep learning prediction***, and ***SPI mitigation***. Validated solvers reproduce MAST-U equilibria to <1%, JOREK and M3D-C1 capture VDE forces, TQ stochasticity, and RE plateau formation within experimental uncertainty, and FRNN transfer learning achieves device-independent disruption prediction with minimal target data. Two-stage SPI emerges as optimal for ITER non-nuclear operation, reducing I_RE to <1 MA and wall load by 60%, while nuclear operation remains challenging due to Compton seeding.

Future work includes:

- Real-time GS-DeepNet + FRNN closed-loop on SPARC
- JOREK full-MHD + kinetic RE hybrid with Monte Carlo collision operator
- Federated learning across ITER members preserving data sovereignty via Rényi DP
- Tungsten fragment SPI for last-resort RE termination with gamma shielding analysis

The path to **disruption-free tokamak reactor** demands not single-code excellence but ***system-level co-design*** of equilibrium, stability, prediction, and mitigation — a triad this thesis contributes toward.
