# Score-Based Generative Modeling and Diffusion: Denoising Score Matching, the Reverse-Time Stochastic Differential Equation, and the Mathematics of Sampling

## Abstract

Score-based generative modeling frames data synthesis as the inversion of a noise-adding diffusion: a forward process gradually perturbs the data distribution toward a tractable prior, and generation proceeds by simulating the time reversal of that process. Crucially, the reversed dynamics depend on the data distribution only through the *score function*, the gradient of its log-density, which can be estimated from samples without knowing any normalization constants. This thesis develops the paradigm from first principles. We review score matching and its denoising reformulation, connect the discrete-time denoising diffusion probabilistic model to score estimation via Tweedie's formula, and present the continuous-time stochastic differential equation framework that unifies these approaches. We derive the reverse-time drift, the deterministic probability flow ordinary differential equation with identical marginals, and the implicit non-Markovian samplers that accelerate generation by an order of magnitude. We state an error decomposition governing sampling convergence and survey rigorous guarantees, including recent results under the manifold hypothesis, before closing with empirical benchmarks and open limitations.

---

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling seeks a sampler for an unknown $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ from finite i.i.d. samples. Unnormalized models $p_\theta = \tilde p_\theta / Z(\theta)$ demand the partition function $Z(\theta) = \int \tilde p_\theta$, intractable in high dimensions; likelihood, MCMC, and variational methods each pay this cost differently. Score-based methods sidestep it: the *score function* $s(x) = \nabla_x \log p(x)$ is invariant to normalization yet determines enough geometry to drive Langevin-type sampling. Learning the score, not the density, is the founding move [1].

The modern diffusion formulation adds a second idea of remarkable power: instead of estimating the score of the raw, possibly singular data distribution directly, one estimates the scores of a *family* of noise-perturbed distributions at increasing noise levels, and then runs a stochastic process backward in time from pure noise to data [3]. Perturbation smooths the distribution, making the score well-behaved and learnable, while the exact form of the perturbation makes the learning target available in closed form. Ho, Jain, and Abbeel showed that training on a weighted variational bound of a discrete-time Markovian noising chain reduces, up to constants, to the same regression problem as denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics [3].

Song, Sohl-Dickstein, Kingma, Kumar, Ermon, and Poole lifted the construction to continuous time [5]: the forward perturbation becomes an SDE, its time reversal — via Anderson's classical result [6] — an SDE driven by the learned score, and the same marginals are generated by a deterministic *probability flow ODE* enabling exact likelihoods. Song, Meng, and Ermon showed the Markovian assumption is inessential: non-Markovian processes sharing the training objective admit far faster samplers [4]. Karras and collaborators then disentangled objective, schedule, preconditioning, and sampler, reaching record quality at an order of magnitude fewer evaluations [8].

This thesis presents the unified mathematical theory. Section 2 reviews score matching; Section 3 develops denoising score matching and the discrete diffusion model; Section 4 dives into the reverse-time SDE, the probability flow ODE, implicit acceleration, and parameterization design; Section 5 states convergence theory and empirical results; Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The score function

For a density $p$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ that is continuously differentiable and strictly positive, the *score* is the vector field

$$
s(x) = \nabla_x \log p(x).
$$

The score points in the direction of steepest increase of probability mass and its magnitude measures local concentration. Two facts recommend it. First, partition-function independence: $\nabla_x \log p_\theta = \nabla_x \log \tilde p_\theta$. Second, sufficiency for sampling via the overdamped Langevin diffusion

$$
dx = \tfrac{1}{2} s(x)\, dt + dw,
$$

with $w$ a standard Wiener process, admits $p$ as its unique stationary distribution under mild growth conditions, and its Euler–Maruyama discretization,

$$
x_{k+1} = x_k + \tfrac{\eta^2}{2} s(x_k) + \eta z_k, \qquad z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),
$$

converges to $p$ as $\eta \to 0$ with the number of steps growing appropriately. Hence learning $s$ is, for sampling purposes, as good as learning $p$.

### 2.2 Implicit score matching

Hyvärinen observed that the score can be estimated without ever evaluating the data density, by minimizing the *Fisher divergence* between the model score $s_\theta$ and the true score [1]:

$$
J(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{data}}} \| s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p_{\mathrm{data}}(x) \|^2.
$$

The unknown score disappears after integration by parts: if $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ decays fast at infinity and $s_\theta$ is smooth,

$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{data}}}\left[ \mathrm{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \| s_\theta(x) \|^2 \right] + C,
$$

where $C$ is independent of $\theta$. This *implicit* objective [1] needs only samples, never the unknown score; its weakness is the Jacobian trace, costing $d$ backward passes naively and motivating sliced and denoising variants.

> **Theorem:** (Implicit score matching [1]). Let $p$ be a density on $\mathbb{R}^d$ vanishing at infinity with its first derivatives, and let $s_\theta$ be a $C^1$ vector field with at most polynomial growth. Then the Fisher divergence $\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_p\|s_\theta - \nabla \log p\|^2$ equals $\mathbb{E}_p[\mathrm{tr}(\nabla s_\theta) + \tfrac{1}{2}\|s_\theta\|^2]$ up to an additive constant independent of $\theta$.

### 2.3 Why perturbation helps

Raw-data score matching faces two pathologies: under the *manifold hypothesis*, the score is undefined off the data manifold and explodes near it, and in low-density regions between modes few samples constrain the field while Langevin chains mix slowly. Gaussian convolution cures both: $q_\sigma(\tilde x) = \int \mathcal{N}(\tilde x; x, \sigma^2 I)\, p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\, dx$ is smooth and strictly positive with a bounded, everywhere-defined score. Estimating scores across noise scales and sampling with an annealed chain makes score-based generation practical — and its continuous-time limit is a single elegant SDE [5].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Denoising score matching

Vincent traded the trace term for regression against a closed-form target [2]: perturb $x \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$ as $\tilde x = x + \sigma z$, $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, with joint $q(x, \tilde x) = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\,\mathcal{N}(\tilde x; x, \sigma^2 I)$. The *conditional* score is analytic:

$$
\nabla_{\tilde x} \log q(\tilde x \mid x) = -\frac{\tilde x - x}{\sigma^2}.
$$

Denoising score matching (DSM) minimizes

$$
J_{\mathrm{DSM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{q(x, \tilde x)} \left\| s_\theta(\tilde x) - \nabla_{\tilde x} \log q(\tilde x \mid x) \right\|^2.
$$

Expanding the square and conditioning shows $J_{\mathrm{DSM}}$ equals the Fisher divergence between $s_\theta$ and the score of the *marginal* $q(\tilde x) = \int q(\tilde x \mid x) p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\, dx$, up to a constant [2]: the learned field estimates $\nabla \log q_\sigma$ with no Jacobian trace ever computed. In code:

```python
def denoising_score_matching_loss(score_net, x0, sigma):
    noise = torch.randn_like(x0)
    xt = x0 + sigma * noise
    target = -noise / sigma            # score of q(xt | x0)
    pred = score_net(xt, sigma)
    return ((pred - target) ** 2).sum(dim=-1).mean()
```

The DSM identity is tied to *Tweedie's formula* for the posterior mean via the marginal score:

$$
\mathbb{E}[x \mid \tilde x] = \tilde x + \sigma^2 \nabla_{\tilde x} \log q(\tilde x).
$$

Tweedie's formula will reappear as the bridge between score prediction and the mean-prediction parameterizations of diffusion models.

### 3.2 Denoising diffusion probabilistic models

Ho, Jain, and Abbeel construct a *discrete-time* Markov chain that gradually corrupts data [3]. The forward (noising) process, with a fixed variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$, is

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\, \beta_t I\big),
$$

and admits the convenient closed form $q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0, (1 - \bar\alpha_t) I)$ with $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} \alpha_s$. For a schedule with $\bar\alpha_T \approx 0$, the terminal law $q(x_T)$ is nearly standard Gaussian. The generative model reverses the chain with learned Gaussian transitions

$$
p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t, t)\big), \qquad p(x_T) = \mathcal{N}(0, I).
$$

Training maximizes a variational lower bound on $\log p_\theta(x_0)$; with $\Sigma_\theta$ fixed, the bound decomposes into KL terms between tractable Gaussians, and a reweighted *simplified* objective performs far better in practice [3]:

$$
L_{\mathrm{simple}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon} \left\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon,\, t\big) \right\|^2, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
$$

The network predicts the injected *noise*; by Tweedie's formula on $q(x_t \mid x_0)$ the score connection is exact:

$$
\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \;\approx\; -\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\; \nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t),
$$

so noise prediction is score prediction up to a known rescaling — the "novel connection between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics" of the abstract [3]. DDPM training *is* multi-scale DSM; DDPM sampling is an annealed reverse chain. On CIFAR-10 this achieved Inception score 9.46 and FID 3.17, then state of the art among likelihood-based models [3].

```python
def ddpm_sample(eps_net, betas, shape):
    x = torch.randn(shape)                       # start from the prior
    alphas = 1.0 - betas
    alpha_bar = torch.cumprod(alphas, dim=0)
    T = len(betas)
    for t in reversed(range(T)):
        eps = eps_net(x, t)
        coef = betas[t] / torch.sqrt(1.0 - alpha_bar[t])
        mu = (x - coef * eps) / torch.sqrt(alphas[t])
        x = mu if t == 0 else mu + torch.sqrt(betas[t]) * torch.randn_like(x)
    return x
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The reverse-time stochastic differential equation

The discrete chains of Section 3 are Euler discretizations of a continuous object. Song, Sohl-Dickstein, Kingma, Kumar, Ermon, and Poole formulate the forward perturbation as an Itô SDE on a finite horizon from time $0$ to time $T$ [5]:

$$
dx = f(x, t)\, dt + g(t)\, dw,
$$

where $f$ is the drift, $g$ the scalar diffusion coefficient, and $w$ a standard Wiener process. Let $p_t$ denote the marginal law of $x(t)$, with $p_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and $p_T \approx \pi$, a tractable prior such as $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$, achieved by suitable choice of $f$, $g$, and $T$.

Generation needs the *time reversal* of this diffusion. Anderson proved that, under regularity conditions, $\bar x(t) = x(T - t)$ is again a diffusion whose drift is corrected by the score [6]:

> **Theorem:** (Reverse-time diffusion [6]). Under Lipschitz and growth conditions on $f$ and $g$ and smoothness of the marginal densities $p_t$, the time-reversed process satisfies
>
> $$
> dx = \big[f(x, t) - g(t)^2\, \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\, dt + g(t)\, d\bar w,
> $$
>
> where $\bar w$ is a standard Wiener process in reverse time. The marginal laws of the reversed process coincide with the forward marginals $p_t$.

This is the master equation of score-based generation — *the only unknown in the reverse drift is the score field*, estimated by a network $s_\theta(x, t)$ via continuous-time denoising score matching,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,T)} \mathbb{E}_{x_0} \mathbb{E}_{x_t \mid x_0} \big\| s_\theta(x_t, t) - \nabla_{x_t} \log p_{0t}(x_t \mid x_0) \big\|^2,
$$

with a weighting function $\lambda(t)$ chosen to balance the loss across noise scales [5]. Sampling then discretizes the reverse SDE — for instance with Euler–Maruyama — optionally interleaved with Langevin corrector steps, the *predictor–corrector* framework introduced in [5].

Three canonical SDE families instantiate the framework [5]:

| SDE family | Drift $f(x,t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | Terminal behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variance Exploding (VE) | $0$ | $\sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t \sqrt{2\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}$ | Variance grows without bound |
| Variance Preserving (VP) | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\, x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | Converges to $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ |
| sub-VP | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\, x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)\,(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t \beta(s)\, ds})}$ | Variance bounded above by VP |

The VP SDE is the continuous-time limit of DDPM, and the VE SDE generalizes the multi-scale noise-conditional score networks; both are special cases of one theory [5].

### 4.2 The probability flow ordinary differential equation

A striking consequence of the SDE formulation is that stochasticity is optional. For every forward SDE there exists a deterministic ODE — the *probability flow ODE* — whose trajectories have exactly the same marginal distributions $p_t$ at every time [5]:

$$
dx = \left[ f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2\, \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \right] dt.
$$

Via the Fokker–Planck equation, the SDE's marginal evolution rewrites as a continuity equation $\partial_t p_t = -\nabla \cdot (\tilde f p_t)$ with *effective velocity* $\tilde f = f - \tfrac{1}{2} g^2 \nabla \log p_t$; the ODE $dx/dt = \tilde f(x, t)$ transports mass along exactly this field. Determinism buys two capabilities: adaptive ODE solvers with error control, often matching SDE quality in fewer steps, and *exact likelihoods* via the change-of-variables formula — integrating the vector field's divergence along the trajectory,

$$
\log p_0(x_0) = \log p_T(x_T) + \int_0^T \mathrm{tr}\big(\nabla_x \tilde f_\theta(x_t, t)\big)\, dt,
$$

with the trace estimated by Hutchinson's estimator, reaching 3.10 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 [5] — a likelihood GANs cannot provide at all.

```python
def probability_flow_sample(score_net, drift, diffusion, ts):
    x = torch.randn(batch, dim)          # draw from the prior
    for i in range(len(ts) - 1, 0, -1):
        t, t_prev = ts[i], ts[i - 1]
        dt = t_prev - t                  # negative time step
        s = score_net(x, t)
        ode_drift = drift(x, t) - 0.5 * diffusion(t) ** 2 * s
        x = x + ode_drift * dt           # explicit Euler on the flow ODE
    return x
```

### 4.3 Implicit models and fast sampling

DDPM sampling needs hundreds to thousands of sequential network evaluations — a severe practical tax. Song, Meng, and Ermon observed that Markovianity of the forward chain is a choice, not a necessity: non-Markovian forward processes sharing the marginals $q(x_t \mid x_0)$ — hence the training objective — induce different, cheaper reverse samplers [4]. Their denoising diffusion implicit models (DDIM) update

$$
x_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\, \hat x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2}\; \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) + \sigma_t\, \epsilon,
$$

where $\hat x_0 = (x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon_\theta)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ is the Tweedie estimate of the clean datum. The parameter $\sigma_t$ interpolates: one setting recovers DDPM-like stochasticity, while $\sigma_t = 0$ yields a fully *deterministic* sampler — an Euler discretization of the probability flow ODE in disguise [4]. The payoff: 10× to 50× faster wall-clock sampling than DDPM, smooth compute–quality trade-offs, and semantically meaningful latent interpolation [4].

### 4.4 Parameterization, schedules, and the design space

Beneath the formalism lie engineering choices that decide empirical success. The network may predict the noise $\epsilon$, the clean datum $x_0$, the score, or the *velocity* $v = \alpha_t \epsilon - \sigma_t x_0$ — related by exact rescalings but differing sharply in conditioning, especially near $t = 0$ where the score norm diverges and near $t = T$ where signal vanishes. The noise schedule allocates capacity across scales: overweighting near-clean data wastes effort on imperceptible detail, underweighting high noise harms global coherence.

Karras, Aittala, Aila, and Laine disentangled these choices into a modular design space [8]: a principled *preconditioning* $D_\theta(x; \sigma) = c_{\mathrm{skip}}(\sigma)\, x + c_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\, F_\theta(c_{\mathrm{in}}(\sigma)\, x; c_{\mathrm{noise}}(\sigma))$ keeping inputs and targets at unit variance across noise levels, plus a stochastic sampler with controlled noise injection ("churn") correcting discretization error. The result reset the state of the art — FID 1.97 unconditional and 1.79 class-conditional on CIFAR-10 at 35 evaluations per image [8] — and transferred to earlier pre-trained score networks, showing that much of diffusion progress is sampler and conditioning design rather than architecture.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 An error decomposition for sampling

Suppose the score network satisfies a uniform estimation bound $\mathbb{E}_{p_t}\|s_\theta(\cdot, t) - \nabla \log p_t\|^2 \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{score}}^2$, the prior mismatch at the horizon is $\delta_T = d(p_T, \pi)$ in some divergence $d$, and the reverse process is discretized with step $\Delta t$. Then, informally, the sampling error decomposes into three interpretable sources:

> **Theorem:** (Sampling error decomposition, informal). Under Lipschitz regularity of the true score and the coefficients, the law $\hat p_0$ produced by the discretized reverse SDE satisfies
>
> $$
> d(\hat p_0, p_{\mathrm{data}}) \;\lesssim\; \underbrace{e^{-cT}\, \delta_0}_{\text{prior mismatch}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sqrt{T}\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{score}}}_{\text{score estimation}} \;+\; \underbrace{C\, \Delta t}_{\text{discretization}},
> $$
>
> up to constants depending on dimension and the Lipschitz constants. For variance-preserving SDEs the prior term contracts exponentially in $T$; for variance-exploding SDEs it is controlled by choosing $\sigma_{\max}$ large.

Each term maps to a design decision: longer horizons or better priors shrink the first, score-matching research targets the second, and higher-order SDE/ODE solvers or DDIM-style implicit steps shrink the third. This decomposition is the lens through which nearly all convergence theory for diffusion models is organized.

### 5.2 Convergence under the manifold hypothesis

Classical analyses assume a Lebesgue density and a globally Lipschitz score — violated when data concentrates on a low-dimensional manifold, where the score blows up near the support. De Bortoli proved the first convergence guarantees for denoising diffusion models in this regime [7], giving quantitative bounds in the Wasserstein distance of order one — meaningful for singular targets — without absolute continuity [7]. The explosive score behavior as $t \to 0$ is handled by early stopping the reverse process at a small positive time, and the bounds imply generalization-style guarantees linking the finite-sample objective to population sampling error [7].

### 5.3 Benchmarks from the literature

| Model | Sampler character | CIFAR-10 FID | Cost per sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM [3] | Stochastic, 1000 steps | 3.17 | ~1000 NFE |
| Score SDE, predictor–corrector [5] | Stochastic SDE + Langevin corrector | 2.20 | ~2000 NFE |
| DDIM, 20–100 steps [4] | Deterministic ($\sigma_t = 0$) | ≈ 4–13 (step-dependent) | 20–100 NFE |
| EDM [8] | Stochastic with churn | 1.97 (unconditional) | 35 NFE |

Here NFE denotes neural function evaluations. The quality–compute frontier moved enormously through sampler design alone: from thousands of evaluations in [3] and [5] to dozens in [4] and [8], with quality *improving* along the way. Deterministic probability-flow sampling additionally enables exact likelihoods (3.10 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 [5]) that stochastic samplers cannot provide, at the price of ODE-solver overhead. These numbers come from the original papers' protocols and should be compared with the usual caution about FID's sensitivity to implementation details.

## 6 Limitations

The theory rests on assumptions real data breaks. Score blow-up near $t = 0$ under the manifold hypothesis is the central difficulty, not a technicality: De Bortoli's analysis [7] handles it by early stopping, but the constants and the stopping-time choice remain delicate, and DSM estimators can misbehave exactly where precision matters most — at the manifold itself. Statistical learning theory is incomplete: minimax rates for neural score estimation, high-dimensional sample complexity, and the gap between the empirical DSM objective and population sampling error are active areas with only partial answers.

Practically, even accelerated samplers cost tens of evaluations per sample — far more than one GAN forward pass — which matters for interactive and video use. Diffusion models also *memorize* training examples, reproducing near-copies and raising privacy and copyright concerns. Likelihood computation via the probability flow ODE, exact in principle, needs divergence estimation along the whole trajectory and is too costly for routine comparison. Finally, the framework is built on Gaussian perturbations; extending time-reversal to non-Gaussian or discrete corruptions requires re-deriving the reversal per noise family, a direction only partially explored.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Score-based generation draws its power from one fact: reversing a noise-adding diffusion needs only the score, learnable by regression against a closed-form target [1, 2, 6]. From this seed grew DDPM [3], the SDE unification with reverse-time drift and predictor–corrector samplers [5], the probability flow ODE with exact likelihoods [5], the implicit accelerations of DDIM [4], and the design space reaching record quality at a fraction of the original cost [8]. Convergence theory is catching up, notably under the manifold hypothesis where the score's singular behavior is confronted directly [7]. The open problems — statistical foundations, memorization, sampling cost, non-Gaussian corruptions — define the field's next phase: what began as a trick to avoid a partition function is now one of the deepest interfaces between stochastic analysis and machine learning.
