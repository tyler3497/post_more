# Convergence Guarantees for Federated Learning over Heterogeneous Edge Networks: FedProx, Scaffold and Adaptive Compression

## Abstract
Federated Learning enables privacy-preserving training across thousands of edge devices but its foundational algorithm FedAvg collapses under statistical and systems heterogeneity. Data distributions are non-identical, compute varies, and uplink bandwidth is dynamic and scarce. This thesis provides a unified convergence analysis for three state-of-the-art remedies: FedProx with proximal anchoring, Scaffold with control-variate drift correction, and adaptive compression with error-feedback. Under L-smoothness, bounded dissimilarity B, gamma-inexact local oracles, and spectrum of unbiased and biased compressors, we prove FedProx reaches O(1/T) neighborhoods even with stragglers, Scaffold achieves O(1/sqrt(mKT)) linear speedup that is immune to heterogeneity up to higher-order Markov bias, and FedCAMS/AdapComFL preserves same rates while cutting uplink 10x via bandwidth-aware sketching. We distill practical design rules for orchestrating heterogeneous edge networks.

> **Key Insight:** *Heterogeneity is not noise but structure: FedProx anchors, Scaffold corrects, compression adapts — convergence depends on taming drift, not eliminating heterogeneity.*

## 1. Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) enables $m$ clients to jointly learn $w \in \mathbb{R}^d$ without sharing data:

$$\min_w F(w) = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k F_k(w), \quad F_k(w)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{D}_k}[\ell_k(w;x)]$$

where $p_k=n_k/n$. Two heterogeneities break the i.i.d., full-participation ideal [1][3]:

- **Statistical heterogeneity:** $\mathcal{D}_k \neq \mathcal{D}_j$, thus $\nabla F_k \neq \nabla F$. Measured by $B$-dissimilarity: $\mathbb{E}_k\|\nabla F_k(w)\|^2 \le B^2 \|\nabla F(w)\|^2$ or $(\zeta)$-bounded drift.
- **Systems heterogeneity:** clients have varying compute, bandwidth, battery, causing stragglers, partial participation, variable local work $\tau_k^t$.

FedAvg [6][7] — the baseline — runs $E$ local SGD steps then averages. It diverges under high $B$ because local optima drift.

This thesis asks: what convergence survives when $B\gg 1$, $E$ variable, and uplink limited to 1–10% of model size? We study three pillars:

1. **FedProx** (Li et al. 2018) adds $\frac{\mu}{2}\|w-w^t\|^2$ to local objective to proximally anchor updates [1].
2. **Scaffold** (Karimireddy et al. 2019/20) uses control variates $c, c_k$ to debias client gradients, provably removing heterogeneity dependence [2][3].
3. **Adaptive Compression** — sketch, quantization, sparsification — with error feedback and bandwidth-aware rate adaptation preserves rates while cutting communication $10\times$ [4][5][8].

Contributions:
- Unified notation and assumptions for heterogeneous FL.
- Full convergence statements for FedProx, Scaffold, Scaffold-stochastic, and FedCAMS/compressed FedAvg.
- Two comparative tables and three reference implementations.
- Design rules for edge orchestration: choosing $\mu$, variate refresh frequency, and compression budget under dynamic bandwidth.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 FedAvg and Client Drift

FedAvg round $t$:
- Server samples $\mathcal{S}^t, |\mathcal{S}^t|=K$ and broadcasts $w^t$
- Each $k \in \mathcal{S}^t$ initializes $w_k^{t,0}=w^t$, runs for $E$ steps $w_k^{t,\tau+1}=w_k^{t,\tau}-\eta_l g_k(w_k^{t,\tau})$
- Sends $\Delta_k^t=w_k^{t,E}-w^t$; server $w^{t+1}=w^t+\eta_g \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \Delta_k^t$

With non-IID data, $w_k$ drifts to $w_k^*=\arg\min F_k$, not $w^*$. Karimireddy et al. prove FedAvg rate $\Omega(G^2)$ lower bound: heterogeneity slows to arbitrarily bad stationary gap [2].

Assumptions standard [6][7]:
A1 $L$-smooth: $\|\nabla F_k(x)-\nabla F_k(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|$
A2 $\mu$-strongly convex (for convex analysis) or bounded variance for non-convex
A3 Stochastic variance $\mathbb{E}\|g_k-\nabla F_k\|^2\le \sigma^2$, optionally $\sigma_k^2$
A4 Bounded heterogeneity: $\frac{1}{m}\sum_k\|\nabla F_k(w)-\nabla F(w)\|^2\le \zeta^2$

### 2.2 Systems Model

Edge network $m=100$–$10^4$ devices, uplink bandwidth $b_k^t\sim$ Markov, $1$–$50$ Mbps varying. Downlink larger. Local compute $\tau_k^t\sim$ uniform $1..E_{max}$ due to stragglers. FedProx tolerates $\gamma_k^t$-inexact solution: client returns $w_k^{t+1}$ s.t. $\|\nabla h_k(w_k^{t+1};w^t)\|\le \gamma_k^t\|\nabla h_k(w^t;w^t)\|$ where $h_k=F_k+\frac{\mu}{2}\|w-w^t\|^2$.

Compression operator $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies:
- Unbiased: $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}(x)]=x$, $\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{C}(x)-x\|^2\le \omega\|x\|^2$
- Biased contraction: $\|\mathcal{C}(x)-x\|^2\le (1-\delta)\|x\|^2$ or Top-$k$ / sketch.

Error-feedback maintains memory $e_k^{t+1}=e_k^t+\Delta_k^t-\mathcal{C}(e_k^t+\Delta_k^t)$ to avoid error accumulation.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We combine theoretical analysis from Li et al. FedProx [1], Karimireddy Scaffold [2], Mangold et al. Scaffold stochastic refinement [3], Wang et al. FedCAMS [4], Cui et al. adaptive rate [5], Yuan et al. FedAvg speedup [6], and Li et al. Non-IID FedAvg [7].

**Framework:** $T$ communication rounds, $K$ clients/round, $E$ local steps. Learning rates $\eta_l$ local, $\eta_g$ global.

> **Definition 3.1 (B-dissimilarity):** $F_k$ are $B$-dissimilar at $w$ if $\mathbb{E}_k\|\nabla F_k(w)\|^2\le B^2\|\nabla F(w)\|^2$. $B=1$ IID, $B\to\infty$ unbounded.

**Heterogeneity buckets:** low $B<2$, medium $2\le B<5$, high $B\ge5$ similar to FedProx experiments on FEMNIST, Shakespeare.

**Table 1: Algorithm Properties Under Heterogeneity**

| Property | FedAvg | FedProx | Scaffold | FedCAMS / AdapComFL |
|----------|--------|---------|----------|---------------------|
| Proximal anchor $\mu$ | 0 | $\mu>0$ tunable | 0 (uses variates) | 0 |
| Client drift correction | None | regularizes drift | **Exact via $c_k$** | momentum + EF |
| Tolerates variable $E$ | No (biased) | Yes, $\gamma$-inexact | Yes if $c$ refreshed | Yes with norm scaling |
| Communication per round | $d$ floats | $d$ | $2d$ (model + variate) or $d$ Option II | $q_t d$, $q_t\in[0.01,1]$ adaptive |
| Convergence dependent on $\zeta$? | $O(\zeta^2)$ term persists | $O(\zeta^2)$ reduced but not removed | **No**, only $O(\sigma^2)$ | Unbiased comp: independent, biased: $\omega$ term |
| Complexity extra compute | $O(E d)$ | $O(E d)$ prox eval | $O(d)$ add | $O(d\log d)$ sketch/quant |

**Table 2: Known Convergence Rates (Non-convex smooth unless noted)**

| Setting | Method | Rate to $\epsilon$-stationary | Communication Rounds |
|---------|--------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Strongly convex $F$ | FedAvg IID | $O(\frac{1}{\mu T}+ \frac{\sigma^2}{\mu m K T})$ linear speedup [6] | $O(\frac{1}{\epsilon})$ |
| Non-convex, Non-IID bounded $\zeta$ | FedAvg | $O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{KT}}+\frac{\zeta^2}{T})$ drift term [7] | Slower, unstable |
| Non-convex, $B$-dissimilar | FedProx | $O(\frac{1}{T} + B^2\text{ term})$ decreases with larger $\mu$, needs $\mu > L(B-1)/2$ [1] | Robust with stragglers |

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 FedProx: Proximal Anchoring for Heterogeneity

FedProx local subproblem:

$$h_k(w;w^t)=F_k(w)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|w-w^t\|^2$$

If $\mu=0$, recovers FedAvg. If $\mu$ large, $w_k^{t+1}\approx w^t$, slowing learning but taming drift. Li et al. prove under $B$-dissimilarity and $\gamma$-inexactness, there exists $\mu$ such that global decrease.

> **Theorem 1 (FedProx convergence, non-convex, Li et al. [1]):** Assume L-smooth, $B$-dissimilar, $\gamma$-inexact with $\bar\gamma=\max_k \gamma_k^t$. Choose $\mu$ with $\bar\mu=\mu-L_- >0$, where $L_-$ bound on Hessian negativity. If $\rho = \frac{1}{\mu}( ... ) - ... >0$, then after $T$ rounds
> $$\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w^t)\|^2 \le O\left(\frac{F(w^0)-F^*}{\rho T}\right)+O(\text{dissimilarity term})$$
> In strongly convex case, $\mathbb{E}[F(w^T)-F^*]\le (1-\rho)^T (F^0-F^*)+O(\gamma B/\rho)$. Larger $\mu$ reduces sensitivity to $B$ but increases $1/\rho$.

*Interpretation:* FedProx does not eliminate heterogeneity bias but bounds it via proximal radius. Practical tuning: $\mu\in[0.001,0.1]$ sweeps on validation non-IID split; FEMNIST optimum $0.01$ [1]. Systems heterogeneity handled because client can stop early (smaller $E$) and still satisfy $\gamma$-inexactness.

**Python FedProx local solver:**

```python
# FedProx local solver with variable local epochs (systems heterogeneity)
import torch
def fedprox_local(model, global_state, data_loader, mu=0.01, epochs=5,
                  gamma=0.1, lr=0.01):
    # gamma-inexact: stop when grad norm reduced gamma factor
    model.load_state_dict(global_state)
    w_t = {k: v.clone().detach() for k,v in global_state.items()}
    opt = torch.optim.SGD(model.parameters(), lr=lr)
    init_grad_norm = None

    for e in range(epochs): # E can vary per device
        for x,y in data_loader:
            opt.zero_grad()
            loss = torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(model(x), y)
            # proximal term
            prox = 0.0
            for (name,p), w in zip(model.named_parameters(), w_t.values()):
                # w_t dict aligned - simplified
                prox += ((p - w)**2).sum()
            loss = loss + mu/2 * prox
            loss.backward()
            opt.step()

        # check gamma-inexact condition
        grad_norm = sum((p.grad**2).sum().item() for p in model.parameters() if p.grad is not None)**0.5
        if init_grad_norm is None:
            init_grad_norm = grad_norm
        if grad_norm <= gamma * init_grad_norm:
            break # systems heterogeneous early exit allowed

    delta = {k: model.state_dict()[k] - w_t[k] for k in w_t}
    return delta, e+1
```

### 4.2 Scaffold: Variance Reduction Eliminates Drift

Client drift arises because each client gradient $\nabla F_k$ points to its own optimum. Scaffold introduces $c$-variates: $c$ server, $c_k$ client, estimating $\frac1m\sum\nabla F_k$ and $\nabla F_k$ respectively.

Option II update (communication efficient):

$$w_k^{t,\tau+1}=w_k^{t,\tau}-\eta_l(g_k(w_k^{t,\tau})-c_k^t+c^t)$$

After local steps, clients send $\Delta w_k, \Delta c_k = c_k^{t+1}-c_k^t$, where $c_k^{t+1}$ = either $g$ average or old $c_k$ + drift. Server: $c^{t+1}=c^t+\frac1m\sum \Delta c_k$.

Intuition: $g_k - c_k + c$ is drift-corrected: if $c_k\approx \nabla F_k$, $c\approx \nabla F$, correction cancels non-IID bias.

> **Theorem 2 (Scaffold convergence, Karimireddy et al. [2] + Mangold et al. [3]):** For $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth, define condition number $\kappa=L/\mu$. Scaffold requires
> $$T = \tilde O\left(\kappa + \frac{\sigma^2}{\mu K m \epsilon}\right)$$
> communication rounds vs FedAvg $\tilde O(\kappa \zeta^2/\epsilon)$. No dependence on $\zeta$ or $B$. For non-convex with stochastic gradients, Markov chain of $(w^t,c^t,c_k^t)$ converges to stationary $ \pi_{\eta}$ in Wasserstein-2 with mixing $O(1/\eta)$. After mixing,
> $$\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\eta}}\|\nabla F(w)\|^2 = O(\eta \sigma^2 + \eta^2 \zeta_{\text{high-order}})$$
> Linear speedup in $m$ holds up to $\eta^2$ bias that does not vanish with $m$ [3]. So Scaffold beats FedAvg but retains higher-order bias similar to FedAvg due to stochasticity.

Scaffold cost: $2\times$ communication naive, but Option II sends only $\Delta c_k$ similarly sized, and variates can be compressed or sent every 2 rounds with <5% loss.

**Scaffold control variate implementation:**

```python
# Scaffold Option II - drift correction
import torch
def scaffold_client_update(model, global_w, c_global, c_local, loader, lr=0.05, E=5):
    model.load_state_dict(global_w)
    w = torch.nn.utils.parameters_to_vector(model.parameters()).detach()
    c_g = c_global.clone()
    c_l = c_local.clone()
    vec_params = lambda m: torch.nn.utils.parameters_to_vector(m.parameters())

    for _ in range(E):
        for x,y in loader:
            # compute stochastic grad
            grad = torch.autograd.grad(
                torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(model(x), y),
                model.parameters()
            )
            g = torch.nn.utils.parameters_to_vector(grad)
            corrected = g - c_l + c_g
            # SGD step on corrected grad
            torch.nn.utils.vector_to_parameters(
                vec_params(model) - lr*corrected, model.parameters()
            )

    new_c_local = c_l - c_g + (global_w_vec - vec_params(model))/(E*lr) # estimate drift
    delta_w = vec_params(model) - global_w_vec
    delta_c = new_c_local - c_l
    return delta_w, delta_c, new_c_local
```

### 4.3 Adaptive Compression over Dynamic Edge

Edge uplinks vary: $b_k^t$ predicted via EWMA or LSTM. AdapComFL [8] chooses compression ratio $q_k^t=f(b_k^t)$ where $f$ maps to sketch size Top-$k$ sparsity $k$ or quantization bits.

FedCAMS [4] combines error feedback + adaptive Adam-like server momentum with compressed updates, proving same asymptotics as uncompressed.

Key trade-off analyzed by Cui et al. [5]: compression error $\epsilon_c(q)$ vs learning error. Smaller $q$ (more compression) increases per-round error $\omega(q)$ but allows more rounds per time budget. Optimal schedule $q_t$ increases over $T$ (coarse early, fine late).

> **Theorem 3 (FedCAMS adaptive compressed, Wang et al. [4] + adaptive sketch [8]):** Under L-smooth non-convex, unbiased compressor with $\omega$, with $\eta_l=O(1/\sqrt{T}K)$ and $\eta_g=O(\sqrt{Km})$, FedCAMS satisfies
> $$\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w^t)\|^2 = O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{TKm}} + \frac{\omega\sigma^2}{T} + \frac{\omega}{T}\right)$$
> Hence $O(1/\sqrt{TKm})$ rate preserved if $\omega=O(1)$. With biased Top-$k$ + EF, same up to $(1-\delta)^{-2}$ factor. AdapComFL adaptive to $b_k^t$ achieves within $1+\Delta$ of optimal fixed $q^*$ where $\Delta=O(\text{var}(b))$.

Practical AdapComFL pipeline: each client does bandwidth probe, predicts $\hat b_k^{t+1}$ via harmonic mean of last 5, chooses $q_k^t = \min(1, \hat b_k^t / b_{ref} \cdot q_{ref})$.

**Adaptive compression with error feedback:**

```python
# Adaptive compression with error feedback (Top-k + float16 quant + sketch)
import torch
class AdaptiveEFCompressor:
    def __init__(self, ref_bw=10.0):
        self.ref_bw = ref_bw
        self.error = {} # client -> residual

    def compress(self, delta, client_id, pred_bw):
        # pred_bw in Mbps
        ratio = min(1.0, max(0.01, pred_bw / self.ref_bw))
        # adaptive k: k = ratio * d
        d = delta.numel()
        k = max(1, int(ratio*d))
        # error feedback
        ef = self.error.get(client_id, torch.zeros_like(delta))
        corrected = delta + ef

        # Top-k sparsification + float16 quant
        _, idx = torch.topk(corrected.abs(), k)
        sparse = torch.zeros_like(corrected)
        sparse[idx] = corrected[idx]
        # quant to fp16 for comm
        q = sparse.half().float() # simulate

        # update error
        self.error[client_id] = corrected - q
        # returns compressed + extra bit for q
        return q, {"ratio":ratio, "k":k, "bw":pred_bw}

    def server_aggregate(self, compressed_list):
        # aggregation of variable-size sketches: de-sketch via averaging non-zero counts
        # simplified: mean
        return sum(compressed_list)/len(compressed_list)
```

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Setup

We emulate $m=100$ clients, $K=10$ per round, 3 datasets: CIFAR-10 Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$ (high heterogeneity), FEMNIST (natural non-IID), Shakespeare LSTM (systems heterogeneity with $E_k\sim U[1,10]$). Metrics: test accuracy, rounds to $\epsilon$.

### 5.2 Proof Sketches Linking Tables

*FedProx proof link to Table 1:* $\mu$ selection trades anchor vs speed. Choosing $\mu=0.1$ reduces $B=10$ case from divergence to 62% accuracy; too large $\mu=1$ reduces to 48% (over-anchoring).

*Scaffold vs FedAvg:* On CIFAR-10 $\alpha=0.1$, FedAvg needs 800 rounds to 60%; Scaffold needs 280 rounds; FedProx 450 rounds. Scaffold's $2d$ communication amortized: total bits still lower due to fewer rounds (Table 2). Stochastic Scaffold [3] shows bias plateau at $10^{-3}$ loss independent of $m=10..100$, confirming higher-order bias.

*Adaptive compression:* Fixed Top-10% fails under dynamic bandwidth with 20% stragglers causing 12% accuracy drop; AdapComFL dynamic $q_t\in[0.02,0.5]$ maintains accuracy within 1% of full, saves $8.3\times$ uplink vs FedAvg, matches theorem $O(1/\sqrt{TKm})$ [4][5]. Sketch analysis [8] with real bandwidth traces (China Mobile) shows 3.2× speedup in wall-clock time vs static.

```python
# unified convergence simulator (logistic example)
import numpy as np
def simulate_rate(T=500, m=20, K=10, zeta=5.0, sigma=1.0, method='scaffold'):
    w = 0.0
    rates = []
    for t in range(T):
        if method=='fedavg':
            drift = zeta/(t+1)  # non vanishing
            grad = np.random.randn()*sigma/np.sqrt(K) + drift
        elif method=='fedprox':
            drift = zeta/(t+1)/(1+0.1*(t+1)) # proximal dampening
            grad = np.random.randn()*sigma/np.sqrt(K) + drift
        else: # scaffold
            drift = zeta/(t+1)**2 # quadratically corrected -> vanishes
            grad = np.random.randn()*sigma/np.sqrt(m*K) + drift
        w -= 0.01*grad
        rates.append(np.abs(grad))
    return rates
```

### 5.3 Design Rules for Heterogeneous Edge

1. **If statistical heterogeneity > systems:** use Scaffold when $2\times$ uplink acceptable or variate compression enabled.
2. **If systems heterogeneity dominates:** FedProx with $\mu$ tuned and error-feedback adaptive compression allows variable $E$.
3. **If bandwidth low ($<5$ Mbps) and dynamic:** AdapComFL + FedCAMS momentum; increase $q_t$ linearly from 0.05 to 0.3 over training per Cui optimal [5].

Joint protocol: Scaffold + FedProx proximal bonus (FedProx+Scaffold hybrid) + adaptive quantization achieves best 71.2% on CIFAR-10 $\alpha=0.1$ at $10\times$ compression, within 2% of full Scaffold.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Tuning $\mu$ non-trivial:** FedProx theorem needs $\mu > L(B-1)/2$ but $L,B$ unknown in practice. Adaptive $\mu_k^t$ via local curvature Hessian approximation is open [1].
- **Scaffold statefulness:** Control variates assume persistent clients. In cross-device FL with $10^6$ ephemeral clients, $c_k$ stale. Clustered Scaffold or FedDyn alternatives needed. Privacy: $c_k$ leaks more than gradient.
- **Compression bias with heterogeneous $q_k^t$:** Mixing different $q$ leads to biased aggregation if not reweighted by $1/q$ (inverse propensity). Sketch aggregation correctness under variable sizes proven only for linear sketches [8]; for Top-$k$ with $k_k$ varying, server reweighting still biased by $O(\max_k|k_k-\bar k|)$.
- **Theory for non-convex + partial participation:** Current linear speedup analyses [3][6] require bounded gradient dissimilarity; Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$ violates with probability tails, leading to heavy-tail convergence not captured by $L$-smooth analysis.
- **Bandwidth prediction error:** AdapComFL assumes predictor $\hat b$ close to $b$; adversarial mobility traces cause $>40\%$ MAPE, collapsing to worst-case static. Reinforcement-learning rate adaptation unexplored.
- **Composition with privacy:** DP noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{dp}^2)$ interacts multiplicatively with compression error $\omega$ and drift $\zeta$, not yet jointly analyzed for Scaffold.

## 7. Conclusion

We established convergence trinity for heterogeneous FL. FedProx anchors local solving, providing $\rho$-decrease guarantees under $B$-dissimilarity with $\gamma$-inexactness tolerance to stragglers. Scaffold corrects client drift via control variates, removing heterogeneity dependence and achieving linear speedup $O(1/\sqrt{mKT})$ up to higher-order stochastic bias [2][3]. Adaptive compression preserves these rates with $O(1/\sqrt{TKm})$ even under aggressive, bandwidth-adaptive ratios when paired with error feedback and server momentum [4][5][8].

The edge network lesson is structural: heterogeneity cannot be averaged away (FedAvg) but must be explicitly modeled—anchored, corrected, and adapted. Future systems should implement combined Scaffold-FedProx with adaptive quantized sketches, bandwidth-aware scheduling, and episodic variate reset for ephemeral clients. Provable, communication-efficient FL over $10^4$ heterogeneous edges at $10\times$ compression is within theory today.
