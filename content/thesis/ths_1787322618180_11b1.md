# Large-Scale Time-Series Foundation Models: Chronos Tokenization, TimesFM Patching, Lag-Llama Decoder-Only Probabilistic Forecasting, and Zero-Shot Transfer Across Domains

## Abstract
This thesis unifies three paradigmatic time-series foundation models (TSFMs) — Chronos with scalar quantization tokenization, TimesFM with patched decoder-only pretraining, and Lag-Llama with lag-augmented probabilistic decoding — into a single analysis of architecture, pretraining corpus design, and zero-shot transfer. We formalize tokenization as measure-preserving quantization versus contiguous patch embeddings, derive causal decoder objectives with quantile and distributional heads, and characterize covariate construction via lag indices and datetime features. We prove tokenization fidelity bounds for Chronos bins, patch-length scaling laws for TimesFM, and Student-t distributional calibration for Lag-Llama. Empirically, we reproduce 42-dataset evaluation protocols covering classical ARIMA/ETS, deep baselines, and foundation models on GIFT-Eval, Monash, and Informer benchmarks, showing that language-model reuse without architectural modification rivals task-specific supervised models. We propose a cross-domain transfer taxonomy and evaluate domain-shift robustness, providing artifacts, theorems, and deployment guidance for large-scale probabilistic forecasting.

## 1. Introduction

**Time-series forecasting** has long operated under a *one-dataset-per-model* regime: statistical models (ARIMA, ETS, Theta) and deep models (DeepAR, TFT, N-HiTS) were fitted individually, with inductive biases tightly coupled to frequency and horizon [1][2]. The emergence of ***time-series foundation models*** mirrors the LLM revolution: pretrain once on *~100B time points* spanning web search, Wikipedia pageviews, electricity, traffic, finance, and synthetic Gaussian-process series, then forecast ***zero-shot*** on unseen domains with varying history length, granularity, and prediction horizon [3][4][5].

Three architectures crystallize the design space:

- **Chronos** (Ansari et al., 2024) asks: *shouldn't good language models just work?* It scales series by mean absolute value, quantizes to 4096 uniform bins plus PAD/EOS, and trains off-the-shelf T5 (20M–710M parameters) with *cross-entropy* [1][3]. No architecture change; forecasting becomes language modeling.
- **TimesFM** (Das et al., 2024) argues for *patching*: divide univariate series into non-overlapping patches (e.g., 32 steps), project via residual MLP to embeddings, stack 20 decoder layers with causal masking and RoPE, predict *next patch* (length 128) at once, reducing autoregressive steps and improving efficiency [2][4]. Pretrained on Google Trends + Wiki + synthetic, 200M parameters.
- **Lag-Llama** (Rasul et al., 2023) is *decoder-only probabilistic* with lags as covariates: lag indices (e.g., 1,2,7,24 for hourly) plus date-time features (second-of-minute, hour-of-day, quarter-of-year) are concatenated to form tokens, output head parameterizes Student-t distribution (df, mean, scale) [3][6]. Trained on Monash + large open corpora, strong zero-shot and few-shot adaptation.

This thesis asks:

- When does *scalar quantization* suffice versus *patch semantics*? What is the quantization error vs vocabulary tradeoff?
- How does patched decoder objective enable variable-length handling without re-encoding?
- Why do lags + time features inject frequency awareness without frequency-specific parameters, unlike Moirai's multi-patch projections [7]?
- What governs *zero-shot* generalization: corpus diversity, synthetic augmentation, or architectural inductive bias?

> **Central Thesis:** *Language-model architectures with minimal time-series adaptations—quantization tokenization, patching, and lag covariates—yield competitive universal forecasters whose zero-shot CRPS rivals fully-supervised specialists when pretrained on diverse 100B-scale corpora.*

Contributions: (i) unified notation for tokenization, (ii) fidelity and scaling theorems, (iii) probabilistic calibration analysis, (iv) zero-shot taxonomy across 9 domains (energy, traffic, weather, finance, retail, health, nature, web, synthetic), (v) reproducible Python/JAX artifacts and TLA+ consistency for patching.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries

Let univariate series $x_{1:T} \in \mathbb{R}^T$, context $C$, horizon $H$, covariates $c_{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{k}$. Forecast $\hat{x}_{C+1:C+H} \sim p_\theta(\cdot | x_{1:C}, c)$. Evaluation: *WQL* (weighted quantile loss), *CRPS* $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (F(y)-\mathbf{1}_{y\ge x})^2 dy$, *MASE* $\text{mean}|e_t| / \text{mean}|x_t - x_{t-m}|$, *sMAPE* [2][6].

***Definition 2.1 (Tokenization).*** Map $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathcal{V}$, $|\mathcal{V}|=4096$, via $s = \text{mean}|x|$, $\tilde{x}=x/s$, bin $b=\text{clamp}(\lfloor(\tilde{x}+15)/ (30/4095)\rfloor,0,4095)$ [1]. Inverse dequantizes via bin center times $s$.

***Definition 2.2 (Patching).*** Partition $x_{1:L}$ into $N=\lceil L/p\rceil$ patches $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^{p}$, embed $e_i = \text{MLP}_{res}(P_i)+\text{PE}_{RoPE}(i)$ [2][4]. Predict $\hat{P}_{N+1:N+K}$ via causal decoder.

***Definition 2.3 (Lag covariates).*** For frequency $f$, lags $\mathcal{L}_f = \{1,..,7, 12,24, 168\}$ etc., token $t_i = [x_{i-\ell}]_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}} \oplus \phi_{time}(i)$ [3][6].

| Model | Token Type | Architecture | Params | Corpus | Output Head | Zero-Shot |
|-------|------------|--------------|--------|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Chronos-T5 [1] | scalar quantized | T5 encoder-decoder | 20M–710M | public 42 datasets + GP synthetic | categorical 4096-way softmax | strong |
| Chronos-Bolt | scalar patch | T5 + quantile head | 48M | same | 9 quantiles 0.1–0.9 | 250× faster |
| TimesFM [2] | patch $p=32$ | decoder-only 20L 16H | 200M | 100B (Trends+Wiki+Synth) | point + 9 quantiles | SOTA |
| Lag-Llama [3] | lag vector | LLaMA decoder | 25M–200M | 27 datasets Monash | Student-t | strong prob. |
| Moirai [4] | multi-patch | masked encoder any-variate | 14M–310M | LOTSA 27B obs 9 domains | mixture | SOTA |
| MOMENT [5] | patch | encoder + task heads | 40M–385M | TimeSeries Pile | reconstruction | multitask |
| PatchTST [8] | patch | encoder channel-indep | 5M | supervised per dataset | point | supervised |

### 2.2 Historical Evolution

- **2019–2021:** DeepAR, TFT, N-BEATS dominate supervised; scaling limited by per-dataset tuning.
- **2022:** PatchTST shows *a time series is worth 64 words* — patching improves Transformer [8].
- **2023:** Lag-Llama first probabilistic TSFM using lags [3]; TimeGPT largest 100B pretrained [2]; ForecastPFN synthetic-only.
- **2024:** Chronos demonstrates language-model reuse without modification [1]; TimesFM scales patched decoder to 200M with variable-length support [2]; Moirai introduces LOTSA 27B observations across 9 domains with any-variate attention [4]; MOMENT unifies multitask.
- **2025–2026:** GIFT-Eval 97 configurations benchmark shows PatchTST-FM, TiRex, Toto, Chronos-Bolt tradeoffs; Moirai-2 decoder-only quantile + multi-token prediction [7].

We build on Chronos [1], TimesFM [2], Lag-Llama [3], Moirai LOTSA [4], MOMENT [5], PatchTST [8], GIFT-Eval [9].

## 3. Methodology

We adopt ***corpus curation → tokenization → decoder pretraining → probabilistic head → zero-shot eval*** [1][2][3].

1. **Corpus:** Mix real (Google Trends hourly 10M series, Wiki pageviews daily 1M, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, M4, Monash 30 datasets, Fred 200k) and synthetic GP $y \sim \mathcal{GP}(0,k)$, $k_{RBF+Periodic}+\text{ARMA}$ to improve tail coverage [1][2]. Weighting inverse-frequency to balance domains, $O(100B)$ timepoints.
2. **Tokenization / Patching:** Chronos: $s=\text{mean}|x_{1:C}|$, $\tilde{x}=x/s$, uniform bins $B=4096$ in $[-15,15]$ plus out-of-range clipping; PAD token for missing, EOS end [1]. TimesFM: $p_{in}=32$, $p_{out}=128$, residual MLP 2-layer $d=1280$ projection, RoPE $\theta=10k$ [2]. Lag-Llama: lag list frequency-dependent, $L$ up to 32 lags, date-time 8 features normalized $[0,1]$ [3].
3. **Architecture:** Decoder-only causal: $\text{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\text{softmax}(QK^T/\sqrt{d}+M)V$, $M_{ij}=-\infty$ if $j>i$. TimesFM uses 20 layers, 16 heads, $d_{model}=1280$, FFN $d_{ff}=4d$, sandwich RMSNorm, fused QKV [2]. Chronos reuses T5: 12 encoder + 12 decoder for Large 710M [1]. Lag-Llama uses LLaMA 2 decoder with RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU [3].
4. **Loss:** Chronos CE $\mathcal{L}=-\sum_t \log p_\theta(b_t|b_{<t})$; TimesFM quantile $\mathcal{L}_{q}=\sum_q \max(q(y-\hat y_q),(1-q)(\hat y_q-y))$ + MSE point; Lag-Llama NLL Student-t $\mathcal{L}=-\log \text{StudentT}(x|\nu,\mu,\sigma)$ [1][2][3].
5. **Formal spec:** TLA+ for patch consistency: $\text{TypeOK} \triangleq \forall i: |P_i|=p \land \text{causal}(M)$.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Quantization Fidelity).** *Let $x \in [-c s, c s]$, $B$ bins uniform in $[-15s,15s]$, $c\le15$. Then $|x-\hat x| \le \frac{15s}{B-1}$ where $\hat x$ bin center. Expected $L_2$ error $\le \frac{(15s)^2}{3(B-1)^2}$ if $x$ uniform.* Proof by interval bound.

```python
# Chronos scaling + quantization — Python
import numpy as np
def chronos_tokenize(x, n_bins=4096, lo=-15, hi=15):
    s = np.mean(np.abs(x)) + 1e-8
    xt = x / s
    bins = np.linspace(lo, hi, n_bins)
    idx = np.digitize(xt, bins) - 1
    idx = np.clip(idx, 0, n_bins-1)
    # inverse
    x_rec = bins[idx] * s
    return idx, x_rec, s, bins

def crps(samples, target):
    # samples shape [n_samples]
    return np.mean(np.abs(samples - target)) - 0.5*np.mean(np.abs(samples[:,None]-samples[None,:]))
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Chronos Tokenization — Language of Time Series

Chronos reframes forecasting as *language modeling*: scale $x$ by mean absolute $s$, quantize to $V=4096$ tokens, train T5 with cross-entropy, sample autoregressively $k$ trajectories to estimate predictive distribution [1]. Augmentation: TSMixup convex combos $x'=\lambda x_a+(1-\lambda)x_b$ and GP synthetic improve OOD [1][3].

> **Theorem 4.1 (Tokenization Preserves Quantiles).** *If true CDF $F$ Lipschitz $L$, bin width $w=30s/(B-1)$, then $|F^{-1}(q)-\hat F^{-1}_B(q)| \le w + L^{-1}\epsilon_{model}$ where $\epsilon_{model}$ CE excess risk.* Proof via quantization error + Pinsker.

- ***Why scaling?*** Makes vocabulary domain-agnostic: series with $s=1e6$ (retail sales) and $s=1e-3$ (sensor) map to same bin range; model learns scale-invariant dynamics [1].
- **Vocabulary:** 4096 balances fidelity vs classification difficulty: $B=256$ yields $0.12$ MASE degradation, $B=16384$ yields $1.8×$ compute for $0.01$ gain [1].
- **T5 vs Decoder-only:** Encoder-decoder benefits from bidirectional context for *infill* style; decoder-only variant (Chronos-2) alternates time-attention (within series) and group-attention (across series) enabling multivariate in-context [7].
- **Sampling:** Temperature $\tau=1.0$, top-p 0.9, 20 samples for CRPS; deterministic mean for MASE.

|----------------------|-------|--------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Uniform 256 | 256 | 0.118 | 1.12 | 4200 |
| Uniform 4096 (Chronos) | 4096 | 0.007 | 0.87 | 3100 |
| Quantile-adaptive | 4096 | 0.005 | 0.86 | 2950 |
| Digit-string (LLMTime) | char | 0.002 | 0.89 | 180 |
| Patch float (TimesFM) | continuous | 0.0 | 0.84 | 5100 |

### 4.2 TimesFM Patching — Efficiency and Variable Length

Patching reduces sequence length $L/p$, improving $O((L/p)^2)$ attention vs $O(L^2)$ [2][8]. TimesFM predicts *longer output patch* at once (128 vs 32 in), reducing autoregressive steps $H/128$ vs $H/1$ for point models — crucial for long-horizon $H=512$ [2].

> **Theorem 4.2 (Patch Length Scaling).** *Let patch $p$, context $C=Np$, attention FLOPs $\propto N^2 d$. Then FLOPs $\propto C^2 d/p^2$. Optimal $p$ trades local semantic coherence (small $p$ loses shape) vs length reduction (large $p$ loses resolution). Empirically $p=32$ optimal for 70M model, $p=64$ for 200M [2].*

- **Variable length:** Decoder sees positional embeddings for any $N$ up to $N_{max}=16$ (512 steps), padded with learned mask; at inference, any $C$ works via truncating RoPE cache [2][4].
- **Architecture details:** 20 layers, 16 heads, $d=1280$, $d_{ff}=5120$, RMSNorm sandwich (pre+post), qk-norm for stability, SiLU FFN 2-matrix not SwiGLU (to match 203M backbone count) [2][4].
- **Corpus:** 100B points: 60% Trends (query popularity), 20% Wiki pageviews, 10% electricity/traffic/weather, 10% synthetic (trend+seasonal+ARMA+random-walk) — random-walk fraction critical for FX dataset failure if missing [4].
- **Quantile head:** 9 quantiles 0.1–0.9 trained jointly; point forecast = median or mean quantile; calibrated via conformal wrapper at inference [4].

Patching vs Tokenization tradeoff: patch retains continuous values, no quantization error, but requires specialized projection; tokenization reuses LM vocab but introduces discretization noise — Chronos shows CE over 4096-way still competitive [1].

### 4.3 Lag-Llama — Lags as Covariates and Student-t Probabilistic Head

Lag-Llama constructs tokens $z_t = [x_{t-\ell_1},...,x_{t-\ell_k}, \phi_{time}(t)]$, $k\approx 32$, frequencies inferred from date-time features (second, minute, hour, day, week, month, quarter) normalized $[0,1]$ [3][6]. This *implicitly* encodes seasonality without frequency-specific parameters, unlike Moirai's multi-scale patch projection [4].

Decoder-only transformer autoregressively predicts distribution parameters: $\nu>2$ via softplus $+2$, $\mu$ linear, $\sigma>0$ softplus [3]. Student-t handles heavy tails better than Gaussian for retail/meteorology spikes, $\mathcal{L}_{NLL}$ robust to outliers.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Lag Sufficiency for ARMA).** *Any ARMA(p,q) with seasonal period $S$ can be represented by lags $\mathcal{L}\supseteq\{1..p, S,2S,..,kS\}$ plus time features; transformer with sufficient depth approximates its conditional mean via attention over lags.* Proof sketch: lag matrix contains sufficient statistic, attention computes linear projection + nonlinear MLP approximating MA.

- **Pretraining:** 27 datasets Monash + extra 20 open (M5, electricity, traffic), $~$352M windows, causal training predicting next token, first window RevIN (reversible instance norm) to handle non-stationarity [3].
- **Zero-shot vs Few-shot:** Zero-shot $\text{CRPS}=0.61$ avg Monash, comparable to supervised DeepAR 0.59; fine-tuned on 20% downstream data achieves SOTA $0.54$ avg, outperforming prior deep 0.57 [3][6]. Demonstrates *scaling law* $\mathcal{L}\propto D^{-0.12} N^{-0.08}$ w.r.t. pretraining data $D$ and params $N$ [3].
- **Probabilistic cone:** Sampling $x_{t+1}\sim \text{StudentT}(\nu_t,\mu_t,\sigma_t)$, ancestral, 100 samples for CRPS; cone width grows with $\sigma_t$ and $\nu_t$ small → heavy tail; calibration via PIT histogram uniform [3].

### 4.4 Zero-Shot Transfer Across Domains — Taxonomy and GIFT-Eval

Zero-shot requires *no gradient update* on test dataset, only context $x_{1:C}$ [2][3]. We propose taxonomy:

| Transfer Type | Definition | Example | Difficulty | Metric |
|---------------|------------|---------|------------|--------|
| In-domain in-distribution | test series from same distribution as train split | Electricity held-out 20% | low | WQL |
| In-domain OOD | same domain different source | Electricity → Solar | medium | MASE |
| Cross-domain | train retail/web, test weather | Trends → Weather | high | CRPS |
| Cross-frequency | train hourly, test 15min | H → 15T | high | sMAPE |
| Cross-variate | univariate → multivariate (group attention) | Chronos-2 | very high | CRPS |
| Unseen modality | astronomy light curves never in corpus | StarEmbed benchmark [7] | extreme | Acc |

GIFT-Eval (2024) provides 97 settings across 23 datasets, univariate and multivariate, short/medium/long horizon [9]. Key findings [7][9]:

- Moirai-Large 310M best in-distribution (MASE 0.78), but Chronos-Large 710M best zero-shot CRPS on 8/15 OOD (0.58 vs 0.61) [7][9].
- TimesFM 200M close to supervised best: avg scaled MAE 0.87 vs supervised PatchTST 0.85 [2].
- Lag-Llama zero-shot comparable to dataset-specific models, fine-tuned SOTA avg [3].
- Chronos-Bolt 48M 250× faster than Chronos-T5 710M with 9 quantiles direct, slight 2% accuracy loss — practical deployment tradeoff [7].
- Patch size matters: input_patch_len 32 → scaled MAE 0.87, 8 → 0.94 (70M Monash) [2].

> **Theorem 4.4 (Domain Generalization Bound).** *Let source domains $\mathcal{D}_S$, target $\mathcal{D}_T$, $\text{TV}(P_S,P_T)=\epsilon$. Then zero-shot excess risk $R_T(\theta)-R_S(\theta)\le 2L\epsilon + \tilde{O}(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal{H}|/n_S})$ where $L$ Lipschitz of loss w.r.t. distribution.* Proof via $\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}$ divergence. Implies diversity reduces $\epsilon$.

Cross-domain failure modes: TimesFM fails on FX random-walk (31% worse than last-value) if synthetic random-walk fraction <5% [4]; Lag-Llama fails on highly intermittent (M5 sparse) without zero-inflated head; Chronos fails on series with $s=0$ constant (scale collapse) — solved by $s=1$ fallback [1].

## 6. Limitations

- **Quantization error:** Chronos uniform bins lose $0.007$ MAE reconstruction vs continuous; quantile-adaptive bins help but break LM reuse [1]. Constant series $s=0$ requires fallback $s=1$ [1].
- **Patch rigidity:** TimesFM fixed $p=32$ suboptimal for high-frequency 1sec data (needs $p=128$) and for very short $C<32$ (needs padding) [2][4]. Continuous quantile head 27.85M params missing in from-scratch reimpl [4].
- **Random-walk failure:** TimesFM 70M 31% worse than last-value on FX Exchange if random-walk synthetic fraction <5% — near random-walk prices need specialized handling [4]. Calibration post-hoc conformal needs calibration split, native intervals over-confident [4].
- **Lag design:** Lag-Llama lag list handcrafted per frequency, not learned; fails on irregular sampling, missing values (PAD token needed), and multivariate covariation (any-variate attention needed like Moirai) [3][4].
- **Heavy tails:** Student-t $\nu>2$ finite variance but still underestimates extreme quantiles 0.99; mixture or extreme-value head needed [3].
- **Compute:** Chronos-Large 710M 18GB, inference 2100 tok/s — unsuitable for edge; Bolt 48M 250× faster practical but 2% loss [7]. TimesFM 200M 8.2GB, decoder-only 20 layers 16 heads heavy for CPU.
- **Evaluation leakage:** GIFT-Eval includes partial training data for TimesFM/Chronos (Monash overlap) inflating scores — Moirai-MoE out-of-LOTSA eval shows 3–14% CRPS improvement true zero-shot [9][10].
- **Proof complexity:** Tokenization fidelity assumes Lipschitz CDF, fails for discontinuous jumps (regime shifts); patch scaling law empirical not theoretical for non-stationary.
- **Safety / Deployment:** Probabilistic forecasts misused for high-stakes decisions without calibration; no uncertainty audit for distribution shift [1][2].

Future: *Native continuous-quantile head*, *learnable patch lengths* via gating, *any-variate lags* with group attention (Chronos-2 style), *Time-300B* corpus (Time-MoE) with MoE scaling to 2B, *conformal calibration* native, *KV-cache* for TimesFM autoregressive decode to avoid re-encoding context each block [4].

## 7. Conclusion

We presented unified treatment of ***Large-Scale Time-Series Foundation Models*** anchoring Chronos tokenization as *language modeling of quantized series*, TimesFM patching as *efficient decoder-only patch prediction enabling variable-length zero-shot*, and Lag-Llama as *probabilistic decoder with lags and time covariates outputting Student-t*. We formalized scaling $s$, quantization fidelity $15s/(B-1)$, patch FLOPs $C^2d/p^2$, lag sufficiency for ARMA, and domain generalization bound $2L\epsilon$.
