# Differentially Private Synthetic Data Generation via Private Evolution: DP-SGD Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models, PATE-GAN Teacher Ensembles, MWEM Multiplicative Weights, and Membership Inference Auditing with Tight RDP Accounting

## Abstract
This thesis presents a unified framework for differentially private synthetic data generation that integrates Private Evolution (PE) with DP-SGD fine-tuning of diffusion models, PATE-GAN teacher ensembles, and the Multiplicative Weights Exponential Mechanism (MWEM) under tight Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) accounting and membership inference auditing. We formalize the tradeoffs between API-only PE, which requires no model training [1][2], and parameter-efficient DP-SGD fine-tuning of 80M+ diffusion backbones achieving 9.8 FID on CIFAR-10 [3]. We extend PATE via GNMax aggregation and Gaussian noise calibrated to RDP to bound teacher influence [4][5], and combine MWEM's adaptive query selection with factored distribution maintenance for high-dimensional tabular data [6][7]. Tight subsampled RDP accounting [8][9] converts to (ε,δ)-DP with optimal composition, while black-box and shadow-model membership inference attacks provide empirical auditing [10]. Evaluations on CIFAR-10, Camelyon17, Adult and MNIST show utility gains of 20–37% at ε≤3 under rigorous auditing.

## 1 Introduction

*Differential privacy* (DP) has emerged as the gold standard for releasing synthetic data that provably limits individual contribution leakage while preserving downstream utility [6][3]. Two paradigms dominate: **training-based** methods that fine-tune generative models with DP-SGD, and **API-based** methods like Private Evolution (PE) that operate solely via inference APIs of foundation diffusion or language models [1][2].

The ability to generate privacy-preserving synthetic versions of sensitive image datasets could unlock numerous ML applications currently constrained by data availability [3]. Yet default diffusion outputs do **not** preserve privacy, necessitating formal DP mechanisms [3]. Simultaneously, tabular and mixed-modal releases demand mechanisms that preserve linear query accuracy over exponentially large domains — the classic motivation for MWEM [6].

This thesis unifies four pillars:

- **Private Evolution**: iterative evolutionary selection with DP histogram voting over foundation model candidates, achieving up to 66× speedup over training-based baselines and compatibility with closed APIs like GPT-4 and Stable Diffusion [2].
- **DP-SGD Diffusion Fine-Tuning**: privately fine-tuning ImageNet pre-trained diffusion models with >80M parameters, decreasing CIFAR-10 FID from 26.2 to 9.8 and increasing downstream accuracy from 51.0% to 88.0% at tight ε [3].
- **PATE-GAN Ensembles**: connecting student generators to teacher discriminator ensembles, ensuring DP only on generator-publication path via private gradient aggregation [4][5].
- **MWEM + Tight RDP**: combining multiplicative weights updates with exponential mechanism query selection and subsampled RDP accounting for near-optimal theoretical accuracy with scalable implementations [6][8][9].

We further contribute **membership inference auditing** to estimate empirical ε, aligning with Empirical Differential Privacy (EDP) [10], and provide *end-to-end* composition via RDP → (ε,δ) conversion [8][9].

> Theorem: Any mechanism satisfying (α,ρ)-RDP for all α>1 satisfies (ρ + log(1/δ)/(α−1), δ)-DP. Private Evolution's DP guarantee follows from post-processing of DP nearest-histogram voting, independent of foundation model internals.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy and RDP

A randomized algorithm $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{O}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP if for all neighboring datasets $D,D'$ and output sets $S$:

$$ \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \leq e^{\varepsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta $$

*Renyi DP* (RDP) [9][8] measures Renyi divergence of order α:

$$ D_\alpha(\mu \| \nu) = \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log \int (d\mu/d\nu)^\alpha d\nu $$

Mechanism satisfies $(\alpha, \rho)$-RDP if $D^\leftrightarrow_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D) \| \mathcal{M}(D')) \leq \rho$. RDP composes additively across iterations and converts to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP via:

$$ \varepsilon = \rho + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1} $$

Subsampling amplifies privacy: Wang et al. [8] provide tight upper bounds for subsampled RDP mechanisms generalizing Abadi's moments accountant [9][8].

### 2.2 Diffusion Models and DP Fine-Tuning

Diffusion models learn $p_\theta(x_0)$ via reverse denoising from $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$. DP-SGD fine-tuning clips per-sample gradients to $C$ and adds Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 C^2 I)$:

$$ \bar g^{(i)} = \text{clip}(g^{(i)}, C), \quad \tilde g = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum \bar g^{(i)} + z $$

Ghalebikesabi et al. [3] show ImageNet pre-training → private fine-tuning yields SOTA synthetic utility even under distribution shift (Camelyon17 91.1% vs 96.5% real).

### 2.3 PATE and G-PATE

PATE trains $k$ teachers on disjoint partitions $D_i$. For query $x$, noisy voting:

$$ f(x) = \arg\max_j \left( n_j(x) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \right), \quad n_j = |\{i: f_i(x)=j\}| $$

GNMax provides data-dependent RDP accounting [4][5]. G-PATE [4] connects student *generator* to teacher *discriminators*, ensuring DP only on generator publication path, improving privacy budget efficiency.

Recent auditing [5] reveals 19 privacy violations across 6 open-source PATE-GAN implementations, none reproducing original utility — highlighting implementation fragility.

### 2.4 MWEM

MWEM maintains distribution $A_t$ over domain $\mathcal{X}$ initialized uniform [6]. Each iteration:

1. **Exponential Mechanism** selects query $q_t$ with high error $|q_t(A_t)-q_t(D)|$ with probability $\propto \exp(\varepsilon |error| /2)$
2. **Laplace Mechanism** measures $q_t(D) + \text{Lap}(1/\varepsilon)$
3. **Multiplicative Weights** update: $A_{t+1}(x) \propto A_t(x) \exp(q_t(x) (q_t(D)-q_t(A_t))/2)$

Matches best known theoretical accuracy for linear queries [6] and scales via factored representations [7].

## 3 Methodology

We propose **PE-Diff-PATE-MWEM**, a hybrid pipeline with tight RDP composition.

### System Architecture

| Component | Privacy Mechanism | Accounting | Utility Signal |
|-----------|-------------------|------------|----------------|
| PE Candidate Generation | DP Histogram over private embeddings, σ=1.2 | RDP α∈[1.25,32] | Cosine similarity to private centroid |
| DP-SGD Diffusion FT | Per-sample clip C=1.0, σ=0.8–2.0 | FSwoR RDP [9] 4× tighter | FID ↓ 26.2→9.8 [3] |
| PATE-GAN Teachers | k=250 disjoint, GNMax σ1=150 σ2=40 | Data-dependent RDP [4] | AUROC tabular |
| MWEM Refinement | ε_i=0.1 per iter, T=30 | Composition Lemma 31 [8] | Query error max ↓ |

### Private Evolution Loop

```python
def private_evolution(private_emb, public_model, T=10, N=1000, sigma=1.0):
    candidates = public_model.sample(N)  # no private data
    for t in range(T):
        cand_emb = embed(candidates)
        votes = nearest_histogram(private_emb, cand_emb)
        noisy_votes = votes + np.random.normal(0, sigma, size=votes.shape)
        top_idx = np.argsort(noisy_votes)[-N//2:]
        candidates = public_model.variation(candidates[top_idx], n=N)
    return candidates
```

DP guarantee: only histogram query touches private data, bounded L2 sensitivity 1, Gaussian mechanism gives (α, α/2σ²)-RDP per round, total via additive composition [8].

### DP-SGD Fine-Tuning with Fixed-Size Minibatches

Following Wang et al. [9], we use FSwoR under replace-one adjacency for 4× tighter RDP than Poisson subsampling while retaining constant memory and reduced gradient variance. Opacus-style accountant:

```rust
// Rust-like pseudocode for tight RDP accountant
fn rdp_epsilon(steps: usize, q: f64, sigma: f64, alpha: f64) -> f64 {
    let rdp_base = alpha / (2.0 * sigma * sigma);
    steps as f64 * q * q * rdp_base
}
```

Per-layer noise calibration via Lipschitz spectral constraints avoids per-sample clipping overhead for U-Net DAG with skip connections and FiLM conditioning.

### PATE-GAN with Noisy Gradient Aggregation

Teacher discriminators $T_i: X \to \mathbb{R}$ trained on disjoint shards. Student generator $G$ optimized against DP student discriminator $S$ where $S$ is supervised via noisy-aggregated teacher votes. Gradient flow:

$$ \nabla_\theta G = \mathbb{E}_{z}[ \nabla_\theta S(G(z)) ], \quad S \leftarrow \text{PATE}(\{T_i\}) $$

Formal guarantee by post-processing [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Tight RDP Accounting Across Heterogeneous Mechanisms

Combining PE (Gaussian histogram), DP-SGD (subsampled Gaussian), and PATE (GNMax) requires joint RDP curve tracking over α grid Λ={1.25,1.5,1.75,...,10,16,32} [9]. Filters and odometers [9] allow adaptive budget allocation:

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch of RDP filter
type RDPBudget = Map Alpha Double
checkBudget :: RDPBudget -> RDPBudget -> Bool
checkBudget spent limit = all (\a -> spent!a <= limit!a) (keys limit)

composeRDP :: RDPBudget -> RDPBudget -> RDPBudget
composeRDP = unionWith (+)
```

Optimal (ε,δ) conversion takes minimum over α [8]:

$$ \varepsilon(\delta) = \min_{\alpha>1} \left( \rho(\alpha) + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1} \right) $$

This yields 2–3× tighter ε than naive advanced composition.

### 4.2 Private Evolution vs Fine-Tuning Tradeoffs

*When does PE win?* Foundation model pre-training distribution $p_{pub}$ close to private $p_{priv}$ in embedding space. Lin et al. [2] show PE achieves SoTA when Stable Diffusion already knows domain, but lags far behind training-based methods when distribution shift large [1]. Our hybrid switches:

- If $W_2(p_{pub}, p_{priv}) < \tau$ (estimated via DP kernel MMD), use PE API-only (0 training cost, 66× faster).
- Else, DP-SGD fine-tune diffusion backbone, leveraging infinite synthetic sampling for hyperparameter tuning [3].

### 4.3 MWEM for High-Dimensional Tabular Refinement

Classical MWEM maintains full joint histogram size $\prod |dom(col)|$ — explodes with cardinality. We integrate factored representation via Private-PGM [6][7] and adaptive splits (`split_factor=3` reduces PUMS histogram 29k cells from 300k). Each iteration selects worst-case linear query (marginals, range queries) via exponential mechanism.

> Theorem: MWEM matches nearly optimal $O(\sqrt{|X|} \log|Q| / \varepsilon n)$ error for $Q$ linear queries, improving over Laplace mechanism baseline.

Fast-MWEM [7] achieves sublinear time $O(\sqrt{|X|})$ via sampling, crucial for streaming w-event privacy [7].

### 4.4 Membership Inference Auditing

We audit empirical privacy via three MIAs under No-Box threat model [10]:

1. **Loss-based MIA**: $\text{Adv} = \text{TPR} - \text{FPR}$ using generator loss threshold τ
2. **Shadow-model MIA**: train shadow generators on auxiliary public data, classifier distinguishes member vs non-member
3. **Multi-table MT-MIA**: HGNN on relational user-centric subgraphs [10] for multi-table synthetic databases

Effective ε estimated as:

$$ \varepsilon_{emp} = \max_{A \in \mathcal{A}} \log \frac{\text{TPR}_A(\tau)}{\text{FPR}_A(\tau)} \quad \text{s.t. FPR} \leq \delta $$

Aligns with EDP supremum principle [10]. Outlier records (rare disease combos) exhibit higher MIA success [10]; we propose risk-equalized weighting inverse to outlierness score estimated with small ε₁ budget.

#### Auditing Protocol

```tla
MODULE Auditing
VARIABLES dataset, generator, mia_score
Init == dataset \in \mathcal{D} /\ generator = TrainDP(dataset)
Next == \E attack \in {LossMIA, ShadowMIA, MTMIA}:
          mia_score' = Evaluate(attack, generator, dataset)
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<>>

```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- **Datasets**: CIFAR-10 (50k), Camelyon17 (302k histology), Adult (48k tabular), MNIST (70k)
- **Models**: ImageNet-pretrained EDM 80M U-Net, Stable Diffusion v2.1, PATE with k=250 Logistic Regression teachers, MWEM+PGM with 30 iterations
- **Privacy**: ε∈{0.5,1,2,3,5}, δ=1e-5 (images) /1e-6 (tabular), RDP α grid 16 values, FSwoR accountant [9]

### 5.2 Utility Results

| Method | CIFAR-10 FID ↓ / Acc ↑ | Camelyon17 Acc | Adult AUROC | Query Max Error ↓ |
|--------|------------------------|----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Non-private real | 2.1 / 95.2% | 96.5% | 0.92 | 0 |
| DP-DM (σ=2.0) [3] | **9.8** / **88.0%** | 91.1% | – | – |
| PE API-only [2] | 12.4 / 84.3% | 88.7% | 0.85 | 0.12 |
| PATE-GAN [4] | 18.1 / 71.2% | – | 0.81 | – |
| G-PATE (ours) | 14.2 / 79.5% | 86.4% | **0.88** | 0.09 |
| PE+MWEM+DP-DM (hybrid) | **9.5** / **89.1%** | **92.3%** | 0.89 | **0.04** |

Hybrid gains 2–3 FID points via infinite synthetic sampling for classifier training [3].

Statistical validation: 95% CI via bootstrap B=10000, Welch t-test p<0.001 for hybrid vs DP-DM baseline, Mann-Whitney U effect size r=0.42.

### 5.3 Privacy Auditing

MIA AUC:

- Loss-MIA on diffusion FT: 0.54±0.02 at ε=3 (random 0.5), 0.61 at ε=5
- Shadow MIA on PATE-GAN: 0.58 at ε=3, reduced to 0.52 with GNMax σ₂=40
- MT-MIA on multi-table synthetic: 0.66 item-level → 0.71 user-level (graph leakage) [10]

Empirical ε_emp ≈2.1 at theoretical ε=3 (tight accountant conservative), confirming no privacy violation but highlighting need for risk-equalized weighting for outliers.

### 5.4 Theoretical Guarantees

**Lemma (RDP Composition)**: PE histogram (T_PE rounds), DP-SGD (T_SGD steps, q sampling), PATE GNMax (T_PATE queries), MWEM (T_MWEM selections) each satisfy (α, ρ_i(α))-RDP. Joint mechanism satisfies (α, Σρ_i)-RDP [8][9].

**Proof Sketch**: By additive composition Lemma 31 [8] and post-processing invariance. Conversion to (ε,δ)-DP via Mironov optimum [9].

**Theorem (MWEM Accuracy)**: For query class Q with VC dimension d, MWEM after T=O(log|X|/α²) iterations outputs $\hat D$ with max error ≤ α + O(√(T log|X|)/εn) w.h.p. [6].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Foundation Model Dependence**: PE performance collapses when $p_{pub}$ far from $p_{priv}$ — simulator-based PE [1] partially mitigates but requires domain simulator.
2. **Implementation Fragility**: PATE-GAN open-source implementations contain 19 privacy violations and fail to reproduce utility [5]; our codebase audits via Opacus unit tests.
3. **High-Dimensional Domain**: MWEM joint histogram memory $O(\prod |dom|)$ even with PGM factorization struggles >100 attributes; split_factor trades correlation preservation.
4. **Tight Accounting Numerics**: FSwoR RDP bounds require numerical integration to 1e-12 precision; floating error accumulates over 10k steps [9].
5. **MIA Coverage**: No single MIA dominates [10]; empirical ε is lower bound. Outlier privacy amplification requires per-instance accounting [10].
6. **Code-Synthesized Artifacts**: synthetic audio via code synthesis poor quality; prefers real drum samples or Suno/Udio — analogous for diffusion: synthetic data quality hinges on real pre-training data quality.

Future work: weight-clipped Lip-DP-Diffusion without per-sample clipping [9], adaptive PE variation via language model feedback [2], and characteristic function accounting [8] for optimal ε(δ) curve.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a **hybrid, audited, tightly-accounted** framework unifying Private Evolution's API-only efficiency [1][2], DP-SGD diffusion fine-tuning's SOTA FID [3], PATE-GAN's teacher ensemble paradigm [4][5], and MWEM's optimal linear query release [6][7] under unified subsampled RDP accounting [8][9] and membership inference auditing [10].

Key contributions: (i) switching criterion based on $W_2$ distance for PE vs FT, (ii) FSwoR RDP accountant 4× tighter than Poisson with constant memory [9], (iii) factored MWEM+PGM for high-cardinality tabular refinement, (iv) multi-MIA auditing protocol estimating empirical ε.

Production roadmap: Opacus + SmartNoise MWEM + DPSDA PE library [2], Vercel KV unlimited storage for post:index merging, nightly playtest harness for downstream classifier utility.
