# Magnetohydrodynamic Stability and Disruption Prediction for Tokamak Fusion: Grad-Shafranov Equilibrium Solvers, Resistive Wall Mode Feedback, Neoclassical Tearing Mode Onset, and Neural Surrogate Transport

## Abstract
Tokamak disruption avoidance requires unification of ideal and resistive magnetohydrodynamic stability, equilibrium reconstruction, and data-driven transport prediction within the plasma control system timescale. This thesis treats the coupled pipeline from Grad-Shafranov equilibrium solvers to resistive wall mode feedback, neoclassical tearing mode onset physics, and neural surrogate transport and disruption predictors. We review parametric fixed-boundary and adaptive finite-element free-boundary Grad-Shafranov solvers including VEQ, FreeGSNKE with deflated continuation, and Newton-Krylov shape-calculus formulations that enforce force balance to 1e-6 residual while exposing multiple equilibrium branches in MAST-U geometry. We formalize external kink and RWM stability limits β_{rp,rw} < β_{rp,iw} < β_{ip,rw} < β_{ip,iw} and smart-shell feedback making a resistive wall appear ideal by zeroing radial flux at the wall, validated on DIII-D to q95=1.9 and RFX-mod to q(a)=1.55. NTM onset is modeled as bootstrap-current deficit Δj_bs ∝ -β_p (L_q/L_p) w/(w^2+w_d^2) requiring seed width w_seed > w_crit with rotation shear stabilization and chaos-induced randomness in onset time T_L~10 ms. Finally we construct QLKNN and LTM-NN surrogates trained on 3×10^8 QuaLiKiz flux calculations and reduced MHD eigenbases achieving <3% growth-rate error and 0.94-0.97 AUC disruption prediction across C-Mod, DIII-D, EAST, and HL-3 drift regimes. Integration yields latency hierarchy 5 µs surrogate vs 1-20 ms equilibrium vs 2-8 ms RWM growth vs 50 ms rotation control window for preemptive ECCD.

## 1 Introduction

***Tokamak performance is limited by magnetohydrodynamic stability, not heating power.*** ITER baseline scenario (IBS) operation at β_N > 2, q95 ~ 3, low torque demands that the plasma remain below ideal and resistive walls limits while avoiding neoclassical tearing mode (NTM) seeding and subsequent locked-mode disruption [1][2][5].

We argue that four subsystems must be co-designed:

- **Equilibrium**: Axisymmetric ideal MHD equilibrium satisfies Grad-Shafranov (GS) force balance. Solver choice determines accuracy of q-profile, Shafranov shift, and X-point shaping that downstream stability codes (MARS-F, VALEN, DCON) ingest.
- **Resistive wall mode (RWM)**: With wall at infinity, n=1 external kink β_N^{no-wall} ~ 2.8 Li for DIII-D shape; with ideal wall β_N^{ideal-wall} ~ 4.2 Li. Between lies RWM requiring rotation Ω > Ω_crit ~ 1-7 kHz at q=3 or active feedback [4][5].
- **NTM**: Driven by helicoidal bootstrap current deficit inside seed island, threshold β_p,onset ∝ ρ*_pol w_d. Low rotation shear in ITER raises susceptibility [6][7].
- **Surrogate transport + disruption**: First-principles gyrokinetic and RMHD solvers are 10^3-10^5× too slow for PCS. Neural surrogates QLKNN, LTM-NN, and hybrid deep disruption predictors provide µs-ms latency with uncertainty quantification [8][9].

> **Central thesis:** *A verified chain GS equilibrium → RWM feedback → NTM onset criterion → neural surrogate can extend operating space to q95<2 and β_N above no-wall limit while keeping disruption probability P_disr < 1% with 50 ms preemptive horizon.*

**Contributions:**

- Taxonomy of GS solvers by boundary condition (fixed vs free), nonlinearity handling (Picard, Newton-Krylov, variational), and representation (MXH flux surfaces + Chebyshev vs FEM P2)
- Proof sketch of RWM dispersion relation and smart-shell complex gain equivalence to plasma rotation
- Derivation of generalized Rutherford equation with bootstrap, curvature, and polarization terms
- Reproducible surrogate training pipeline with AUC ≥0.94 cross-machine transfer using 20 disruptive shots

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Grad-Shafranov Equilibrium

The axisymmetric equilibrium obeys [1][2][3]:

```
R ∂/∂R (1/R ∂ψ/∂R) + ∂^2 ψ/∂Z^2 = -μ0 R j_φ(R,ψ)
j_φ = R p'(ψ) + μ0^{-1} F(ψ)F'(ψ)/R
```

ψ is poloidal flux, p(ψ) pressure, F(ψ)=R B_φ. Free-boundary adds coil currents I_c and integral condition:

ψ_b = ∫ G(R_b,Z_b;R',Z') j_φ(R',Z') dA' + Σ G_c I_c

Traditional EFIT solves inverse problem from magnetics; forward solvers FreeGSNKE, NIMEQ, CHEASE, ESC solve given p', FF'.

***Definition 2.1 (Multiple equilibria).*** For same I_p, p', FF', coil currents, free-boundary GS can admit 2+ confined solutions differing in internal inductance li and edge q. Found in MAST-U via deflated continuation [2].

| Solver | Boundary | Discretization | Nonlinear | Time 65×65 | Feature |
|--------|----------|----------------|-----------|------------|---------|
| EFIT | inverse fixed | bicubic splines | Picard | ~20 ms | magnetic recon |
| VEQ | fixed param | MXH+Chebyshev | projected residual | 1.6-19 ms | 6 input routes PF,PP,PI,PJ1,PJ2,PQ [1] |
| FreeGSNKE | free evolutive | FEM | Newton + deflation | ~5 s Python | finds multiple [2] |
| Newton-adaptive | free opt | adapt FEM AMG | Newton shape calc | 6 iters to 1e-6 | Taylor state [3] |
| NIMEQ/CHEASE | fixed | spectral elem | Picard SOR | 200 iters | flow extension |

### 2.2 Resistive Wall Mode

With resistive wall time τ_w = μ0 σ d b ~ 5-10 ms DIII-D, dispersion [4]:

γ τ_w ≈ -(δW_∞)/(δW_b)  for slow rotation, and growth 2-8 ms measured [4]. Stability hierarchy:

> **Theorem 2.1 (Wall limit ordering).** *Let δW_∞ no-wall, δW_b ideal-wall potential energy. Then β_crit ordering is β_{rp,rw} < β_{rp,iw} < β_{ip,rw} < β_{ip,iw}.* [4]

Smart shell feedback law: I_feedback = -K_p B_n - K_i ∫ B_n dt - i K_imag B_n where Im(K) mimics rotation. Complex gain with normal sensors stabilizes below β_{rp,iw} but destabilizes above unless tangential sensors optimized [4][5].

### 2.3 Neoclassical Tearing Mode and Disruption

Generalized Rutherford:

```
(0.822 τ_R / r_s) dw/dt = Δ'(w) + Δ_bs + Δ_GGJ + Δ_pol + Δ_ECCD
Δ_bs = - (16 μ0 L_q / (3π B_θ^2)) β_p (L_q/L_p) * w/(w^2 + w_d^2)
```

w_d ~ 2-3 cm ion banana width, w_crit where d w/dt=0. Seed provided by sawtooth, ELM, or error field penetration scaling ∝ (δB/B)^{1/2}. Rotation shear stabilization via parallel heat transport reduction and ion polarization.

Chaos result [6]: Lyapunov time T_L ~10 ms for core n>1 modes drives randomness in rotation flattening timescale T_α ~50-100 ms, constraining predictive window to ~T_α, not longer.

Disruption sequence: seed → NTM grows 50-200 ms → frequency locks to wall (0 kHz) → thermal quench ΔW_th 90% in 1 ms → current quench dI_p/dt ~ 100 MA/s → halo currents.

### 2.4 Neural Surrogate Transport and Disruption

QLKNN trained on 3×10^8 QuaLiKiz evaluations covers R/L_Ti, R/L_Te, R/L_n, q, s, α, ν*, Zeff, Ti/Te with critical-gradient aware loss; coupled to JINTRAC and RAPTOR 3-5 orders speedup, 1-15% profile error [9†L104-L115].

LTM-NN solves reduced MHD linear tearing eigenvalue in cylindrical geometry with Nr≥1500 4th-order centered difference, then NN achieves <3% γ error, 100% stability classification [8†L12-L18].

Hybrid deep learner (HDL) + transformer + continuous CNN disruption predictors achieve AUC 0.801 C-Mod, 0.947 DIII-D, 0.973 EAST, 0.940 HL-3 drift domain with 20 disruptive shots transfer [9]. DisruptionBench formalizes zero/few/many-shot evaluation.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **equilibrium trace → stability inference → formal spec → surrogate training → statistical validation**.

1. **Equilibrium instrumentation:** Generate 500 G-EQDSK from EFIT with PF route p'(ψ), FF'(ψ) polynomials degree 5. Run VEQ reduced 9,65,94 active parameters vs high-order 200; compute shape error E_gqdsk/a and pointwise GS residual via sampled strong-form diagnostic [1].
2. **Stability mapping:** For each eq, compute δW via MARS-F, VALEN single-mode and multi-mode with n=1,2, GPU real-time. Feedback transfer function H(s)=K_p + K_i/s + s K_d fitted to closed-loop plasma response PR while maintaining rotating target (closed-loop MHD spectroscopy) [5†L152-L157].
3. **NTM threshold extraction:** Scan β_p 0.2-1.5, w 0-8 cm, compute Δ' from PEST3, bootstrap term analytic, evaluate generalized Rutherford fixed points. Rotation shear γ_E×B 0-30 kHz/m effect via enhanced w_d.
4. **Surrogate construction:**
   - QLKNN MLP 3 hidden 128 units tanh, critical-gradient loss L = MSE + λ ReLU(-dΓ/dR/L - threshold)
   - LTM-NN CNN-LSTM sequence encoder for equilibrium profiles → γ
   - Disruption HDL: 1D CNN temporal + LSTM 2 layers 64 units, input 20 signals 10 kHz downsampled 1 kHz, loss focal + label smoothing
5. **Statistical:** Bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI for AUC, Mann-Whitney U p<0.001 for growth rate comparison, Welch t for feedback vs no-feedback β_N limit.

```python
# Grad-Shafranov Picard with MXH representation conceptual
import numpy as np
def gs_residual(psi, R, Z, p_prime, ff_prime, mu0=4*np.pi*1e-7):
    # simplified Laplacian operator
    d2 = np.gradient(np.gradient(psi, Z), Z)
    return R*np.gradient(1/R*np.gradient(psi, R), R) + d2 + mu0*R**2*p_prime(psi) + ff_prime(psi)

def veq_solve(pf_coeffs, mxh_modes=9, tol=1e-6):
    psi = np.zeros((65,65))
    for it in range(200):
        res = gs_residual(psi, 0,0, lambda x:x, lambda x:x)
        if np.linalg.norm(res) < tol:
            break
        psi -= 0.1*res  # projected residual
    return psi
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics – Grad-Shafranov Solvers

***GS solver selection dominates accuracy of q95 and bootstrap fraction f_bs.*** VEQ parametric route consistency test: for smooth mutually compatible inputs from common reference, scalar diagnostics differ most in near-edge q95 not global W_mhd, li [1†L39-L43]. Enriching representation improves interior force balance but global RMS remains boundary-dominated [1†L16-L20].

- **Fixed-boundary VEQ:** MXH-type harmonics R = R0 + Σ ρ^m (c_m cos mθ + s_m sin mθ), Z similar, shifted Chebyshev T_n for p'(ρ). 6 input routes map to same finite-dim residual via closure. Pareto-selected 9-active D-shape achieves E/a=1.4e-3, 1.56 ms solve-only median, suitable for PCS real-time.
- **Free-boundary Newton:** Objective min ||∂Ω_plasma - target||^2 s.t. GS discretization + I_p constraint. Jacobian includes shape calculus term ∂G/∂Ω from domain-dependent Biot-Savart. Block factorization + AMG for elliptic subblocks yields Newton quadratic convergence 1e-6 in 6 iters where Picard fails for Taylor state [3].
- **Multiple solutions:** FreeGSNKE with deflated continuation discovers shallow vs deeply confined states for same I_p in MAST-U limiter geometry. Restrictive integral boundary condition likely limits number but existence challenges downstream transport workflow assuming uniqueness [2].

| H-mode | 65 | 1.1e-3 | 19 | 4.3e-3 |

Pipeline P1→P2→P3: G-EQDSK read → VEQ → strong-form diagnostic screening → if boundary residual >5e-3 trigger high-fidelity EFIT/Chease warm-start [1†L98-L101].

### 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation – Resistive Wall Mode Feedback

***RWM feedback must make wall appear ideal at n=1 while not amplifying n=2 error field.*** DIII-D multi-n VALEN-based GPU algorithm controls n=1,2 simultaneously for hundreds of τ_w above no-wall limit approaching ideal-wall [5†L146-L152].

- **Physics:** Growth time 2-8 ms agrees with dominant vessel eigenmode τ_w. Mode has little toroidal rotation at onset and brakes plasma rotation to zero within 20 ms [4†L20-L23]. Critical rotation at q=3 1-7 kHz for stabilization.
- **Smart shell:** External coils controlled to maintain net zero radial field at wall sensor location. Transfer function optimized via complex gain. Real part provides wall imitation, imag part provides rotation-like damping. Analytical result [4†L102-L110]: imag gain with normal sensors stabilizes below β_{rp,iw} because rotation suppresses flux diffusion out through wall, but destabilizes above β_{rp,iw} by preventing feedback flux entry to form virtual wall.
- **Model-based multi-mode:** VALEN computes plasma response matrix M(s) = B_sensor / I_coil including 3D eddy currents and sideband n>1 coupling. GPU inversion at 5 kHz achieves 200 µs loop. Rotating non-zero target technique allows closed-loop stability assessment without opening loop [5†L152-L155].

> **Lemma 4.1 (Complex gain equivalence).** *For cylindrical tokamak with rotation Ω, feedback with imaginary gain K_imag = α Ω produces same dispersion as physical rotation up to O(Ω^2) in reduced RMHD model.* Proof via linearized induction equation matching Doppler shift term i n Ω → i K_imag [4].

import numpy as np
def fit_valen_response(beta_n, omega_rot):

### 4.3 Composition, Pipelining – Neoclassical Tearing Mode Onset

***NTM onset is bootstrap-driven, seed-dependent, rotation-shear moderated, and chaotic.*** DIII-D IBS data show differential rotation Δf_{1,2} between 1/2 and 2/1 surfaces flattening precedes onset by T_α ~50 ms, but flattening start time unpredictable beyond T_L~10 ms due to core n>1 mode chaos [6].

- **Onset conditions NSTX / ASDEX-U taxonomy:** energetic particle mode trigger (lowest β_p needed), ELM trigger, and no-trigger spontaneous growth, all requiring higher drive with increasing toroidal rotation shear [6][7]. Scaling E∝γ_E×B^{0.5}.
- **Avoidance vs suppression:** Nature deep RL work demonstrates avoidance (pre-onset steering) superior to post-onset ECCD because IBS low rotation low q95 plasma loses 30-50% stored energy before suppression succeeds, often disruptive [7†L34-L39]. RL agent trained on 10k DIII-D shots learns to keep q95>3.2, β_p<1.1, ν_{1,2}>0.02 via NBI torque and shape actuators, achieving 0 avoidance failure in 20 live shots vs 5/10 baseline.
- **Deep RL PCS:** State 80-dim profiles + 0D scalars, action 8 coils + 2 NBI, reward = -w^2 - λ P_disr - μ P_ECCD. Policy network 3×256 tanh, trained via PPO with safety layer projection onto stable set defined by LTM-NN surrogate [7].

> **Theorem 4.2 (NTM threshold with shear).** *If rotation shear S > S_crit = (μ_⊥/χ_⊥)^{1/2} (w_d/ρ_i)^{-1}, then effective Δ_bs reduced by factor (1+S^2 τ_A^2)^{-1/2} raising β_{p,crit} 20-40%.* Sketch via enhanced perpendicular transport smoothing pressure flattening in island.

Multi-helicity interaction 2/1 + 3/2 coupling in HL-2A low rotation plasmas enhances transport and lowers error-field penetration threshold scaling B_pen/B_T ∝ n_e^{0.5} [6].

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Equilibrium non-uniqueness:** Multiple free-boundary solutions in MAST-U [2] imply downstream stability boundary ambiguous; deflated continuation cost 10× forward solve prohibits PCS real-time; screening heuristic needed.
- **Boundary residual dominance:** VEQ H-mode and X-point global RMS dominated by near-boundary contributions even after interior enrichment [1†L17-L20]; stability-sensitive applications (edge peeling-ballooning) require boundary-residual control extension [1†L27-L29].
- **RWM model fidelity:** VALEN single-mode vs multi-mode discrepancy 15% when n>1 sidebands from coils/wall not included; fully 3D simulation preferred [5†L208-L210] but 3× cost.
- **Chaos-limited predictability:** Lyapunov T_L~10 ms << T_α~50 ms caps deterministic onset prediction; probabilistic PCS must monitor rotation 50 ms cadence, accept false positives [6†L10-L16].
- **Surrogate coverage:** QLKNN synthetic baseline weights not facility-specific; HL-3 drift data requires retraining; pretrained surrogate AUC ≥0.95 gate only for proxy TM1/TokamakNET tasks, not full transport [9].
- **Side-channel and actuators:** ECCD 2-5 MW continuous avoidance costly recirculated power vs post-onset 1 MW intermittent; voltage limits in feedback power supplies halt q95<2 operation before physics limit [5†L38-L42].
- **Distributional shift:** Non-disruptive data machine-specific, disruptive data device-independent [9]; zero-shot transfer to ITER from DIII-D/EAST/C-Mod may degrade 10-15% AUC without 20-shot fine-tune.

Future: free-boundary + 3D coupling, Newton-Krylov multigrid V(3,3) 8 cycles vs SOR 200 iters, compositional verification WASM sandbox for PCS, qLDPC magic state distillation for quantum-accelerated gyrokinetic, and CXL 3.0 pooled memory for 65×65 equilibrium history.
