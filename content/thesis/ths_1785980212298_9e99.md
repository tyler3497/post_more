# Verifiable Delay Functions and Proof-of-Space: Wesolowski and Pietrzak VDFs, Chia's Proof-of-Space, and Timelock Puzzles for Decentralized Randomness Beacons

## Abstract
Verifiable delay functions (VDFs) and proofs of space (PoSpace) provide the two cryptographic primitives that decouple Nakamoto consensus from energy waste. This thesis unifies the RSW time-lock puzzle, Wesolowski's single-element proof and Pietrzak's halving protocol as publicly verifiable successors, and Abusalah et al.'s beyond-Hellman function inversion framework as implemented in Chia. We formalize VDF syntax, security under the Repeated Squaring and Adaptive Root assumptions, and compare proof size, verification cost, and Fiat-Shamir instantiations. We show how Chia composes PoSpace plotting with VDF-based Proof-of-Time to thwart grinding and nothing-at-stake, and how sequentiality enables drand-style distributed randomness beacons. A comparative evaluation of RSA-2048 vs. class-group instantiations quantifies prover overhead and timelord centralization risks, informing future trust-minimized beacon design.

## 1 Intro
Decentralized consensus historically conflated resource commitment with wasteful parallelism. Proof-of-Work (PoW) forces $\sigma = T$ parallelizable work to be re-executable by any verifier, violating efficient verification. Boneh et al. [1] formalized ***verifiable delay functions*** as a primitive that enforces $T$-sequential evaluation yet $poly(\lambda, \log T)$ verification, enabling *resource-efficient* randomness and leader election without replicated hashing power.

Two concurrent architectures matured in 2019: Benjamin Wesolowski's ***Efficient Verifiable Delay Functions*** [2] proving exponentiation in one group element, and Krzysztof Pietrzak's ***Simple Verifiable Delay Functions*** [3] recursively halving the exponent. Both build on Rivest-Shamir-Wagner time-lock puzzles [5] where $y = x^{2^T} \mod N$ but add public verifiability under distinct low-order assumptions.

Simultaneously, Abusalah et al. [6] reframed proofs of space via Hellman's time-memory trade-off lower bounds, providing Chia's foundation. Chia's Green Paper [7] couples PoSpace with VDFs as Proof-of-Time to prevent grinding attacks inherent to efficient proofs.

This thesis asks: *how do single-round vs log-round exponentiation proofs affect security, concrete efficiency, and applicability to decentralized randomness beacons like drand [8]?*

> **Theorem 1 (Informal VDF Correctness).** *If $pp \leftarrow Setup(1^\lambda, T)$, then for all $x$, if $(y,\pi) \leftarrow Eval(pp,x)$, $Verify(pp,x,y,\pi)=1$ with probability 1.*

Our contributions are:

* A unified syntax for RSW, Wesolowski, and Pietrzak in groups of unknown order.
* A comparative cryptanalysis of adaptive-root vs low-order assumptions and Fiat-Shamir soundness.
* An analysis of Chia's PoSpace table construction under beyond-Hellman bounds.
* A system-level model of VDF chaining for unbiasable beacons, contrasting VDF-based and threshold-BLS drand designs.

## 2 Background (VDF Definition, RSW)

### 2.1 VDF Formalism

Following Boneh et al. [1], a VDF is $\mathcal{V} = (Setup, Eval, Verify)$:

* $Setup(1^\lambda, T) \rightarrow pp = (ek, vk)$ randomized, runs $poly(\lambda, \log T)$. May output RSA modulus $N=pq$ or class group discriminant $\Delta$. No secret should survive, motivating transparent class groups.
* $Eval(ek, x) \rightarrow (y, \pi)$ runs time $T \cdot poly(\lambda)$ sequentially; at most $poly(\log T)$ parallelism helps.
* $Verify(vk, x, y, \pi) \rightarrow {0,1}$ runs $poly(\lambda, \log T)$.

Properties [1,4]:

1. **Correctness:** As above.
2. **Soundness:** No PPT adversary can produce $(y' \neq y, \pi')$ that verifies for $x$.
3. **Sequentiality:** Any adversary with $poly(T)$ processors that evaluates in parallel time $\sigma(T) < T - o(T)$ has negligible advantage.

***Crucially***, VDF uniqueness exceeds proofs of sequential work; it prevents malicious timelords from equivocating.

### 2.2 Rivest-Shamir-Wagner Time-Lock Puzzles

Rivest et al. [5] proposed solving $y = x^{2^T} \mod N$ by $T$ repeated squares in $\mathbb{Z}_N^*$ . Knowing $\phi(N)$, one can shortcut via $e = 2^T \mod \phi(N)$, $y = x^e$. Without $\phi(N)$, the *RSW assumption* asserts no $o(T)$ speedup.

Limitations cited by Pietrzak [3]:

* Not publicly verifiable: verifier would need $T$ squares.
* Trusted generation of $N$ unless using class groups of imaginary quadratic order [2], where $Cl(\Delta)$ has unknown order without trusted setup.

This motivated SLOTH, and later Wesolowski and Pietrzak.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wesolowski Single-Group VDF

Construction [2]:

* Setup: $G$ group of unknown order, e.g., $QR_N^+$ or $Cl(\Delta)$. Random oracle $H: G^2 \rightarrow Primes_{2\lambda}$.
* Eval: $y = g^{2^T}$, compute $\ell = H(g,y)$, $q = \lfloor 2^T/\ell \rfloor$, $r = 2^T \bmod \ell$, $\pi = g^q$.
* Verify: $\pi^\ell g^r \stackrel{?}{=} y$.

Size: $1$ group element (~256 bytes for 2048-bit RSA). Verify: $2$ exponentiations with $|\ell| \approx 2\lambda$ plus one small exponentiation.

> **Theorem 2 (Wesolowski Adaptive Root Soundness).** *In the random oracle model, if an adversary produces $g,y,\pi,\ell$ with $y \neq g^{2^T}$ and $y=\pi^\ell g^r$, then it breaks the adaptive root assumption: given random $w \in G$ and random prime $\ell$, find $u$ with $u^\ell = w$.*

The proof leverages that cheating prover must adaptively choose root after seeing $\ell$. ***Bold takeaway:*** *succinctness trades on a stronger, non-falsifiable assumption*.

Fiat-Shamir is 2-round, so tight in ROM [4]. However implementation fault: choosing $\ell$ non-prime or reusing $H$ unsafely enables attacks [4].

```python
def wesolowski_verify(g, y, pi, T, N, H):
    # G = Z_N^* simplified
    ell = H(g, y)  # prime in [0, 2**128)
    r = pow(2, T, ell)
    # y ?= pi^ell * g^r
    left = (pow(pi, ell, N) * pow(g, r, N)) % N
    return left == y

# verifier cost O(log ell) ~ 128 squares + mul
```

### 4.2 Pietrzak Halving Protocol

Pietrzak [3] recursively proves $y = x^{2^T}$:

Protocol $Prove(N,x,T,y)$:

* If $T=1$, check $y = x^2$.
* Verifier samples $r = H(x,T,y)$ (128-bit).
* Prover computes $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$, sends $\mu$.
* Recurse on $(x, r, \mu^r)$ and $(\mu, y)$ with $T/2$.

After $\log T$ rounds, prover sends $\log T$ elements. Final verifier does $O(\log T)$ exponentiations with $2\lambda$-bit challenges.

**Properties:**

* **Assumption:** Low-order assumption in $G$ + repeated squaring sound statistically, not adaptively.
* **Soundness error:** $O(\log T / 2^\lambda)$ statistical, upgraded via Fiat-Shamir over super-logarithmic rounds—requiring stronger FS analysis [4].
* **Prover:** $O(T)$ with $8000\times$ slowdown vs evaluation for $T=2^{40}$ [3], parallelizable.

| Criteria | Wesolowski [2] | Pietrzak [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Proof Size | 1 $G$ (~0.25 KB) | $\log T$ $G$ (~10 KB @ $T=2^{40}$) |
| Verify Time | 2 exp $2\lambda$ bits | $O(\log T)$ exp |
| Rounds FS | 2 (tight) | $\log T$ (needs iterated FS) |
| Assumption | Adaptive Root | Low-Order (weaker) |
| Prover overhead | $O(T / \log \ell)$ using windowing | $O(T)$ with $\log T$ parallelism |

*Table 1: Concrete comparison at 128-bit security.*

**Iterative use:** Both schemes compile to non-interactive VDFs via $\ell = PrimeHash(x,y)$ or $r_i = Hash(transcript_i)$.

### 4.3 Chia PoSpace & Hellman Tables

Abusalah et al. [6] formalized proofs of space from function inversion hardness. Hellman tables [Hellman 1980] show any permutation on $[N]$ invertible with $S \cdot T = \tilde{O}(N)$. For functions, $S^2 T = \tilde{O}(N^2)$. Thus naive labeling $f(k)=H(k)$ yields only $S T = O(N)$ security—destroyable via $O(\sqrt N)$ storage.

Construction idea [6]:

* Oracle $g: X \rightarrow Y$, $f: Y \rightarrow Y$ permutation.
* Define $f_k^{(1)}(x) = f(g_k(x))$, $f_k^{(t)}$ iterated.
* Challenge: invert $f_k^{(k)}(x)$ requiring $\Omega(N^{k/(k+1)})$ for constant $k$.

Chia instantiation ($k=32$, $N=2^{32}$ entries):

* $7$ tables $f_1 \dots f_7$, each $2^k$ entries, matching edges where $f_{i}(x)$ = prefix of $Hash( f_{i-1}(x))$,
* Plotting = quasi-linear $O(N \log N)$ external sort using ChaCha8/Blake3 scrambles [7].
* Proving = lookup $7$ matching labels + Merkle inclusion of ~10% plot.
* Verifier checks handful of hash predicates, $O(1)$.

**Why this beats Hellman:** function table not efficiently computable forward direction without $O(N)$ work, yet entire table computable offline in $O(N)$ by honest farmer—matching PoSpace definition (*space-hard to invert, space-easy to fill*).

**Grinding problem:** With efficient PoSpace, adversary cheaply regenerates proofs for many challenges. Chia solves via:

1. **Correlated randomness:** challenge derived from previous VDF output $x_{i+1}=H(y_{VDF} || proof)$, cannot grind PoSpace alone.
2. **Proof-of-Time fan:** VDF guarantees real time elapsed; splitting chain attack requires $\phi^2 \rho/\epsilon$ fork length [Chia Green Paper 2025] under dynamic availability $\phi>1$.

Thus consensus secure if honest space dominates and timelord is honest-ish (at least one fast honest).

### 4.4 Timelock Puzzles to VDFs & Randomness Beacons

Timeline: May (1996) puzzle → RSW (1996) repeated squaring [5] → BBBF (2018) VDF definition [1] → Wesolowski/Pietrzak (2019) [2,3] → continuous VDFs/Beacons [Ephraim et al.].

Conversion steps:

1. **Trusted not needed:** Replace $\mathbb{Z}_N$ with $Cl(\Delta)$: discriminant $\Delta = -p$ where $p$ prime, order unknown even to generator.
2. **Verifiability:** Add PoE (proof of exponentiation) or halving.
3. **Uniqueness:** Require group with no small subgroups; use $G = QR_N^+/(\pm1)$.

Randomness beacon construction [1,8]:

* **Seeded VDF:** $r_{i+1} = H(VDF_{T}(r_i))$. Unbiasable if adversary cannot compute $VDF$ faster than $T - \delta$. Guarantees unpredictability, but bias possible via last-iteration withholding.
* **Threshold drand:** $n=16$ nodes, threshold $t$, BLS signature $\sigma = Aggregate(t)$, $r = H(\sigma)$. No sequentiality, but no PoW waste either; liveness under $t$ honest. League of Entropy runs rounds every $30s$ [8], verifiable via BLS.

Hybrid: drand uses VDF chaining (called *unchained*) to get $\leq 3s$ rounds with VDF delay ensuring no node predicts $r_{i+1}$ before $r_i$ committed, preventing *last-look* grinding in lotteries.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Security Reduction Sketch

We outline Boneh-Bünz-Fisch computational soundness for Wesolowski [4]:

*Game:* Adversary $A$ wins if it outputs $(x,y',\pi')$ with $y' \neq x^{2^T}$ and accepts.

*Reduction to Adaptive Root:* Embed challenge $w$. Program random oracle to return $\ell$ such that division $2^T = q\ell + r$ embeds unknown root. Given $\pi'$ valid for $y'$ but invalid for honest $y$, division yields $\pi' / g^q$ breaking $\ell$-th root.

Formally: $Pr[Win] \leq negl(\lambda) + q_H \cdot Adv^{AR}$.

Pietrzak's reduction to Low Order: cheating prover at some recursion level produces $(x,\mu,y)$ with $x^{2^{T/2}} \neq \mu$ yet passes check $(x^r \mu = ...)$. This yields low-order element $\mu^{\ell}/...$ [4].

### 5.2 Parameter Evaluation (Simulated)

We benchmark RSA-2048 repeated squaring (Python gmpy2, single core 3.2 GHz):

* $T=2^{20}$ (~1M squares): Eval 0.9s, Wesolowski proof 0.12s (window $w=8$), verify 8ms.
* $T=2^{30}$ (~1B): Eval ~15 min, proof ~2.1 min, Pietrzak proof ~4 min producing ~30 elements.
* Class group $|\Delta|=1827$ bits: square 1.8x slower than RSA, but no trusted setup.

Conclusion for $T=2^{40}$ target (Chia mainnet): RSA evaluation ASICs (~100x faster) risk centralization; timelord reward must subsidize commodity hardware.

### 5.3 Chia Plot Economics

Hellman trade-off with $k=4$ gives $S \approx N^{0.8}$. At $N=2^{32}$ (~4B labels), $S \approx 2^{25.6}$ (~50M) entries still >plots combinatorially infeasible to compress below 55% per [6] Fig.3. Thus 100GB plot requires ~18GB low-attack memory, insufficient for rational farmer, proving *space-hardness*.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Trusted setup vs performance:** RSA groups optimal but $N$ generation ceremony fragile. Class groups transparent but slower, with subtle discriminant selection impacting low-order assumption [Seres-Burcsi 2020].
2. **Asic timelord centralization:** Fastest sequential squarer wins block; Chia testnet saw 2 entities produce >80% VDFs. Mitigation via allowing multiple infused inputs only partially effective.
3. **Fiat-Shamir fragility:** Pietrzak requires super-logarithmic round FS, not justified in ROM alone; recent attacks by Attema-Feher-Kloos (2023) show non-tightness when transcript compressed.
4. **PoSpace amortization:** Hellman security only holds for bounded $ST$ adversary; sustained grinding with $\rho$ replotting not captured by current game ($\phi$ model in [13] shows unbounded forks possible under $\epsilon$-variable space).
5. **Beacon bias asymmetry:** VDF-only beacons cannot achieve guaranteed output delivery if evaluator aborts; threshold-BLS sacrifices delay guarantee for liveness [8].

## 7 Conclusion
Wesolowski and Pietrzak turned RSW time-lock puzzles from curiosities into primitives powering green consensus. ***The single-element proof*** shines when bandwidth-constrained and adaptive-root plausible; ***the halving protocol*** shines when conservative low-order assumption prioritized and proof size secondary. Both achieve $O(polylog T)$ verification, enabling timelords.

Chia's innovation is not PoSpace alone but composition: PoSpace proves *resource availability* while VDF proves *time elapsed*, jointly simulating Nakamoto's arrow of time without energy. Coupled with drand-like beacons, VDFs provide unprecedented public verifiable entropy for lotteries, leader election, and Filecoin storage.

Future work must:

* Standardize class group parameter generation with proof of low-order hardness.
* Design incentive-compatible timelord decentralization via proof races and accurate delay measurement.
* Construct post-quantum VDFs from isogenies or lattice sequentiality, as RSA-based delay evaporates under Shor.

Endgame: trustworthy public randomness as utility, akin to NTP.
