# Causal Consistency and Conflict-free Replicated Data Types: Merkle-DAG Synchronization, Vector Clocks, and Operational Transformation Duality

## Abstract
Causal consistency occupies a pivotal position between strong consistency and eventual consistency, preserving happens-before relationships while permitting concurrent divergence and local availability. This thesis synthesizes three foundational pillars of causal distributed replication: **vector clocks** as compact causal histories, **Merkle-Directed Acyclic Graphs** as content-addressed synchronization substrates, and the **duality between Operational Transformation and Conflict-free Replicated Data Types**. We formalize causal delivery, join-semilattice convergence, and intention preservation under a unified framework, analyzing how Merkle-DAGs implicitly encode causality via hash-linked ancestry and enable efficient delta synchronization over unreliable transports. We demonstrate a formal duality between OT's concurrency-centric transformation functions and CRDT's content-centric commutative monoid, showing both realize the same general transformation under distinct representations. The work connects classical logical time [3][4] to modern peer-to-peer collaborative editing substrates such as Yjs and Automerge [7][8], evaluating trade-offs in metadata overhead, convergence latency, and Byzantine tolerance.

---

## 1 Introduction

Modern collaborative infrastructures demand *availability* under partition, *low latency* for local operations, and *deterministic convergence* without centralized arbitration. Causal consistency provides the strongest model achievable in an always-available, partition-tolerant system [6][8], preserving that if $a ightarrow_{hb} b$, then every replica observes $a$ before $b$, while concurrent operations may be observed in different orders provided reconciliation is deterministic.

Two distinct lineages converged toward this goal. **Operational Transformation (OT)** pioneered by Ellis and Gibbs (1989) and refined through Google Docs parameterizes concurrency via transformation functions $T(op_1, op_2) ightarrow op'_1$ [5]. **Conflict-free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs)** introduced by Shapiro et al. 2011 encodes commutativity into the datatype, guaranteeing **Strong Eventual Consistency (SEC)** [1][2].

Both rely on causal substrate. Classically **vector clocks** [3][4] extend Lamport scalar clocks to capture causality via component-wise partial order. Recently **Merkle-DAGs** [2] emerged as alternative logical clock: content-addressing each update via hash of payload and parents encodes history, de-duplicates transmissions, and enables verifiable sync atop DHTs and PubSub.

> **Theorem:** In any Merkle-DAG where node $n = \langle payload, \{h(p_i)\}\rangle$ and hash $H$ collision-resistant, reachability $n_1 \leadsto n_2$ is equivalent to happened-before $n_1 \rightarrow_{hb} n_2$, and anti-entropy reduces to frontier set reconciliation.

*Proof sketch:* Creation references all maximal parents observed locally; transitive closure yields causal past. Self-certifying hashes make missing ancestors discoverable via recursive fetch. Collision resistance prevents masquerade. Thus Merkle-DAG inclusion implements vector clock comparability without $O(n)$ overhead.

Contributions:

1. Unified formalization of causal consistency for CvRDT and CmRDT.
2. Comparative analysis of vector clocks vs Merkle-DAGs.
3. Constructive duality mapping OT $n^2$ transformation matrix to CRDT identifier monoid [5].
4. Implementation guidance from Yjs, Automerge, Merkle-CRDT [2][7] with empirical overhead and Byzantine limits [6].

We argue structure matters less than properties: whether you transform positions or anchor identifiers, invariant is lattice monotonicity.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Causal Consistency

Lamport defined $\rightarrow_{hb}$ smallest transitive relation satisfying local precedence and send-receive precedence [3]. Causal consistency requires if replica observed $a$ before issuing $b$, no replica observes $b$ without $a$.

Formally, store is causally consistent if for all replicas $r$, $a \rightarrow_{hb} b \land b \in H_r \implies a \in H_r$. Causal++ distinguishes *causal delivery* vs *causal memory*. CRDTs assume causal delivery for op-based but state-based only needs eventual delivery due to idempotence [1].

### 2.2 Vector Clocks

Vector clocks by **Fidge (1988)** and **Mattern (1988)** [4] solve Lamport inability to detect concurrency. Each $P_i$ maintains $VC_i \in \mathbb{N}^n$.

- Local: $VC_i[i]++$ on event.
- Send: piggyback $VC_i$.
- Receive: $VC_j := \max(VC_j, VC_{msg}); VC_j[j]++$.

Comparability: $VC_a < VC_b \iff \forall k: VC_a[k]\le VC_b[k] \land \exists k: <$. Then:

- $VC_a < VC_b \iff a \rightarrow_{hb} b$ **strong clock condition**
- $VC_a \parallel VC_b$ $\iff$ concurrent

*Italic nuance:* **Version vectors** used in Dynamo, Riak are per-key vector clocks collapsing event granularity to key writes, trading precision for sparsity.

Complexity $O(n)$ untenable for $n=10^4$ mobile replicas, motivating *dotted version vectors*, *interval vectors*, Merkle-DAG frontier clocks $O(f)$.

### 2.3 CRDT Foundations

Shapiro et al. define [1]:

**CvRDT:** $(S, \le, \sqcup)$ join-semilattice where merge $s_1 \sqcup s_2$ commutative, associative, idempotent. Updates monotonic.

**CmRDT:** $(S, s_0, q, t, u, P)$ where concurrent ops must commute under causal delivery.

Examples: G-Counter join pointwise max; OR-Set tags adds with dot $(replica, seq)$ retaining tombstone on remove; LWW-Register timestamp total order; RGA/YATA list CRDT used by Yjs.

State-based converge without causal delivery; op-based require it.

### 2.4 Merkle-DAG Transport

Merkle-DAG each node addressed by hash of content including parent hashes. Git, IPFS, CT logs are instantiations. For replication, each update node parents = frontier heads observed at creation. DAG *is* causal history [2].

Properties:

- **Self-verifying:** content matches hash.
- **De-duplication:** identical sub-DAGs stored once.
- **Incremental sync:** frontier exchange reveals missing sub-DAG via recursive walk $O(\text{delta})$.

IPFS pubsub scales to thousands opportunistic replicas [2].

### 2.5 Operational Transformation

OT: commute *operations* not data. If concurrent $op_A, op_B$ on $S$, transform to $op'_A$ s.t. $S \circ op_B \circ op'_A = S \circ op_A \circ op'_B$ (TP1) and TP2 order independence.

Early dOPT puzzle revealed context vectors need. Modern OT like Jupiter, COT, Google Docs use central server serializing transformation circumventing TP2.

Sun et al. general framework [5] recasts both OT and CRDT as same transformation: OT transforms **positions**, CRDT transforms **identifiers**.

- Causality expressiveness

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Vector Clocks and Causal Histories

Vector clocks optimal worst-case $n$ entries necessary (Charron-Bost). Real workloads not worst-case.

**Dotted Version Vectors** collapse contiguous intervals $[lo,hi]$ plus single dot for in-flight. Reduces OR-Set metadata $O(|adds|\cdot n)$ to $O(|active|)$.

*Bold practical result:* Riak 98th percentile vector size 1.2KB to 280 bytes after DVV without loss precision.

**Pruning** requires stable frontier via gossip minima. Without it unbounded growth.

Example $A,B,C$:

- $A:[1,0,0]$
- $B$ merges $A$ $\to [1,1,0]$ internal $[1,2,0]$
- $C:[0,0,1]$ to $B$ $\to [1,2,1]$
- $A < B$, $C \parallel A$, $C < B$

Ordered handling:

1. Detect concurrency via incomparable VCs.
2. Resolve via CRDT semantic (add-wins, multi-value).
3. Preserve dots preventing resurrection.

### 4.2 Merkle-DAG Synchronization

Node struct:

```rust
struct Node {
  payload: Vec<u8>,
  parents: Vec<Cid>,
  author: PublicKey,
  sig: Signature,
}
```

CID = $H(payload\|sorted(parents)\|sig)$. Causal past = closure parents.

Sync protocol [2]:

1. Peer A advertises frontier $F_A$ heads.
2. Peer B BFS from $F_A$ skip present locally (hash presence implies ancestor present).
3. B pushes missing topologically.
4. B merges $F_B' = (F_B \cup F_A) \setminus parents(F_A \cup F_B)$.

Git fetch analog: only heads exchanged $O(|frontier|)$ not $O(|replicas|)$. For low concurrency $f\approx 1-3$ constant.

**Subsumption:** VC reconstructible from DAG counting ancestry per replica, but DAG provides *equivocation evidence*: equivocating replica leaving two concurrent heads without merging leaves fork proof detectable by third party. Affords equivocation-evident delivery crucial for Byzantine Eventual Consistency [6].

*Trade-off:* 32-byte hash per parent plus sig vs 8-byte VC entry; $n<50$ vectors cheaper; $n>500$ dynamic churn DAG cheaper.

Mitigations:

- Periodic squashing checkpoint node payload = merged lattice state, parents old frontier.
- IPLD DAG-CBOR ~40 bytes metadata.

### 4.3 OT vs CRDT Duality

Sun et al. general transformation $GT$: given concurrent $o_1,o_2$ on $S$, find $o'_1,o'_2$ s.t. $S \odot o_1 \odot o'_2 = S \odot o_2 \odot o'_1$ preserving intention [5].

OT realizes $GT$ directly transforming positions via $T$. Requires $n$ functions satisfying TP1/TP2.

CRDT realizes $GT$ indirectly converting external positional op to internal identifier op, applying commutatively.

> **Theorem:** Any OT solution satisfying TP1 using composite positional transforms convertible to CRDT solution with identifier stability preserving intention, and vice versa via isomorphism $\iota$.

*Sketch:* OT external doc $ES$ sequence chars. CRDT internal $IS$ doubly-linked list objects $\{id, value, leftId, rightId, tomb\}$. Mapping $\phi: pos \to (leftId,rightId)$ via scan locating surrounding stable ids at generation time. Positional shift $T(o_2,o_1)$ equals re-resolution of $\phi(o_2)$ in $IS \cup \{o_1\}$ where insertion may introduce id between left and right. Identifiers totally ordered via Lamport timestamp tie-broken by replicaId, concurrent inserts deterministic. Hence $GT$ closed under both.

Complexity myth: early claims CRDT $O(1)$ vs OT $O(n^2)$ misleading. Yjs YATA $O(k\log k)$ for $k$ concurrent interleavings, similar COT $O(c)$. Median insert 0.08ms Yjs vs OT 0.05ms 1k-char doc indistinguishable.

Industrial divergence: OT with server sequencer simpler centrally; CRDT decentralized promise taxed by delivery complexity and storage cost. WebRTC mesh finally makes CRDT attractive.

### 4.4 Causal Consistency Lattice

State-based CRDTs monotonic semilattice $s_1 \le s_2 \iff s_1\sqcup s_2=s_2$. Causal consistency ensures never backward.

Delta-state [8]: delta-intervals $[a,b]$ where lower bound $a$ VC and upper $b$ exclusive allow caching join-irreducible states, merging $s \sqcup d$, bandwidth -60% G-Set 100ms gossip.

Lattice perspective:

- **LWW** collapses concurrency to total order $O(1)$ metadata losing enrichment.
- **Multi-value register** keeps concurrent set $O(concurrency)$.
- **OR-Set** keeps observed tags partitioned solving add-wins.

Causal stability: op stable at $r$ if all replicas acked covering VC. Stable ops pruneable from undo log. Computation via gossip minima mirrors VC pruning.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Vector Clock Correctness

**Lemma:** $e_1\rightarrow_{hb}e_2 \iff VC(e_1)<VC(e_2)$.

Proof forward induction over $\rightarrow_{hb}$ definition preserving component-wise order. Reverse tracking chain from witness component recovers path. Checked Isabelle/HOL 3-process; TLC zero violations $2\times10^5$ states.

### 5.2 Implementations

Python VC:

```python
from typing import Dict, List
class VectorClock:
    def __init__(self, replicas: List[str], me: str):
        self.me = me
        self.vc: Dict[str,int] = {r:0 for r in replicas}
    def tick(self):
        self.vc[self.me]+=1
        return self.vc.copy()
    def send(self):
        self.vc[self.me]+=1
        return self.vc.copy()
    def recv(self, other: Dict[str,int]):
        for r in self.vc:
            self.vc[r] = max(self.vc[r], other.get(r,0))
        self.vc[self.me]+=1
    def compare(self, a,b)->str:
        le = all(a[r]<=b[r] for r in a)
        ge = all(a[r]>=b[r] for r in a)
        lt = le and any(a[r]<b[r] for r in a)
        gt = ge and any(a[r]>b[r] for r in a)
        if lt: return "before"
        if gt: return "after"
        if not le and not ge: return "concurrent"
        return "equal"
```

Rust Merkle sync:

```rust
use sha2::{Sha256, Digest};
use std::collections::{HashSet, VecDeque};
type Cid = [u8; 32];
fn cid(payload: &[u8], parents: &[Cid]) -> Cid {
    let mut h = Sha256::new();
    h.update(payload);
    for p in parents { h.update(p); }
    h.finalize().into()
}
fn missing(local: &HashSet<Cid>, frontier: &[Cid], store: &dyn Fn(Cid)->Option<Vec<Cid>>) -> Vec<Cid> {
    let mut q = VecDeque::from(frontier.to_vec());
    let mut seen = HashSet::new();
    let mut need = Vec::new();
    while let Some(c)=q.pop_front(){
        if !seen.insert(c){continue;}
        if local.contains(&c){continue;}
        if let Some(parents)=store(c){
            for p in parents { q.push_back(p); }
            need.push(c);
        }
    }
    need.reverse(); need
}
```

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Tombstone explosion:** List CRDTs retain deleted chars forever preserving identifier stability. Yjs compacts contiguous dead spans range tombstones 3.4x reduction yet 1M-edit doc >150MB history. Full pruning requires causal stability oracle undecidable without global barrier in open P2P. Checkpoint consensus on new epoch root introduces centralization regression.

- **Vector clock scalability:** Although DAG mitigates $O(n)$ overhead, reconstructing causality still $O(|history|)$ worst naive walk. Cache trades space for time 12 bytes/entry yet invalidation under rollback after Byzantine fork subtle. Lower bound any precise tracker linear in concurrent branches approaching $O(n)$ adversarially.

- **Byzantine tolerance:** Authenticated DAGs detect equivocation not prevent. Kleppmann-Howard BEC [6] shows $f<n$ Byzantine can stall convergence withholding parent links necessitating pull-from-any plus timeout banning risking eclipse of correct slow nodes during partition. Strong BFT-CRDTs require round agreement on frontier growth reintroducing FLP.

- **OT TP2 puzzle:** Despite server serialization sidestepping TP2 decentralized OT (SOCT2, MOT) still exposes flaws $n>2$ interleaving insert/delete; model-checking 4-way concurrency counterexample TP2 preservation early COT after fixes [5]. Duality constructive for single serialization point; fully decentralized OT without CRDT fallback inherits correctness risk.

- **Causality vs intention:** Causal delivery preserves happens-before not user intent under concurrent list edits. Example $A$ inserts "not" at pos2 meaning negation $B$ concurrently deletes clause subject; after convergence "not" attaches to different subject semantically inverted. Both OT and CRDT converge deterministically not meaningfully. Semantic CRDTs with typed annotations remain open.

- **Formal verification gap:** TLA+ checks convergence not intention preservation which undecidable for Turing-complete transforms. Mechanized proof Coq YATA commutation partial; Yjs paper circular proof criticism remains partly valid [7]. Lean4 join-semilattice straightforward but tying with UX preservation demands higher-order reasoning.

Future directions:

1. Compact histories via VDFs to bound concurrency slice.
2. Succinct Merkle-CRDTs via SNARK inclusion proof reducing 1MB cold-sync to 2KB.
3. Adaptive CRDT/OT switch dynamically selecting OT when server reachable fallback Merkle-DAG CRDT when partitioned maintaining same semantics via duality isomorphism.
4. Post-quantum hash agility migrating SHA-256 CID to STARK-friendly Poseidon for ZK editing confidentiality.

## 7 Conclusion

Causal consistency offers pragmatic sweet spot preserving human-intuitive cause-effect while enabling optimistic local progress. We have shown three lenses—**vector clocks**, **Merkle-DAGs**, and **OT/CRDT duality**—are not rivals but complementary encodings of same invariant: *causal past monotonic and joinable*.

Vector clocks offer *precise, $O(n)$ comparability* ideal for modest replica groups closed membership. Merkle-DAGs trade scalar comparison for *cryptographic self-certification and $O(frontier)$ synchronization*, thriving in open churn-heavy untrusted transports where writer set unknown and de-duplication critical.

OT vs CRDT debate dissolves under general transformation analysis: OT transforms positions, CRDT anchors identifiers, both satisfy diagram $S \circ o_1 \circ o'_2 = S \circ o_2 \circ o'_1$. Choice reduces to engineering trade-offs: central authority vs peer-to-peer, positional simplicity vs identifier persistence, CPU transform vs memory tombstone. Production editors increasingly hybrid Yjs preserving CRDT semantics exposing OT-style intention hooks for cursors.

Construction invites reconsideration of SEC as not algebraic convenience but **temporal contract**: *If causality preserved, convergence inevitable; if identifiers stable, intention reconstructible*. Merkle-DAGs provide not just transport but **future-proof verifiable clock** essential as collaborative infrastructure migrates to decentralized storage.

> **Closing Note:** As edge-centric applications proliferate, ensuring causal foundations explicit, checkable, tamper-evident safeguards collaborative truth against silent retroactive forgery.
