# Systolic Execution for Transformers: Weight-Stationary Dataflow, Interconnect Topology, and Compilation Scheduling in TPU-class Accelerators

## Abstract

Systolic execution has re-emerged as the architectural backbone of modern deep learning accelerators, codified in Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) lineage. Originally formalized by Kung and Leiserson for VLSI efficiency, systolic arrays trade flexible control for rhythmic data movement, achieving near-unity compute density for dense linear algebra. For Transformers, which are dominated by $O(B \cdot S \cdot D^2)$ matrix multiplications but punctuated by memory-intensive softmax and layer-norm, the mapping to 128x128 or 256x256 systolic meshes exposes subtle trade-offs between **weight-stationary (WS)**, **output-stationary (OS)**, and **row-stationary** dataflows. This thesis provides a complete treatment: from the space-time transformation theory underlying WS, to the twisted 3D torus and Optical Circuit Switch (OCS) fabric in TPU v4 that enables 4096-chip supercomputing, to XLA compilation scheduling that fuses and tiles Transformer blocks to hide HBM latency. We contribute analytical models, Verilog and Python scheduling pseudocode, and a roofline-guided design space exploration.

## 1. Introduction

The triumph of Transformers — from Vaswani et al. [2017] to GPT-3, ViT, and LLaMA — imposes a brutal architectural requirement: accelerate $M \times K \times N$ GEMMs where $M, K, N$ vary by factors of $>32\times$ within a single inference pass.

> **Key Insight:** A Transformer is 95% GEMM by FLOPs but only ~60% GEMM by time if mapped naively to systolic arrays, due to bandwidth-bound attention reductions, layernorm, and inter-chip communication.

Why systolic? Three VLSI invariants from Kung (1982) still hold:

1.  **Compute costs ~ distribution**: A multiply-accumulate (MAC) in 5 nm is ~0.5 pJ; moving 32 bits from HBM is ~20 pJ. Reuse is power.
2.  **Regularity simplifies layout**: A 2D mesh of identical processing elements (PEs) yields $O(\sqrt{N})$ wire length vs. crossbar $O(N)$.
3.  **Synchronous pulsing enables $>1$ TOPS/mm²**: No dynamic arbitration in the inner loop.

Google's TPU v1 (Jouppi et al., ISCA 2017) demonstrated this with a 256x256 8-bit WS array delivering 92 TOPS at 40 W in a PCIe card. TPU v2/v3 moved to 128x128 bfloat16 MXUs per core to improve utilization for small batches, and TPU v4 introduced *two* TensorCores per chip, each with four MXUs, plus SparseCores for embeddings, and an optically reconfigurable interconnect to scale to 4096 chips at 1.1 exaflop BF16.

This thesis asks: *What is required to make WS systolic execution transformer-optimal, not just convolution-optimal?*

We structure the argument as:

- Formalizing WS dataflow as a space-time affine transformation
- Analyzing interconnect topology physics and OCS economics
- Deriving compiler scheduling for fusion, tiling, and collective hiding
- Quantifying utilization and roofline limits for MHA and FFN

We assume graduate familiarity with digital design, loop nests, and DNN basics; no prior systolic expertise is required.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Systolic Definition

Following Kung & Leiserson [1979] and Kung [1982]:

> A **systolic system** is a network of PEs that rhythmically compute and pass data, where each datum is used by multiple PEs before eviction, analogous to blood pumped by the heart.

Formally, given a nested loop with iteration domain $\mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{Z}^n$, a space-time mapping $T: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}^{d_s} \times \mathbb{Z}^{t}$ assigns each iteration to a PE coordinate and time step such that dependence vectors $d_i$ map to $T(d_i)$ with $t>0$ (causality) and bounded spatial distance (locality).

For GEMM $C = A \times B$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times K}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times N}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$:

```
for m in 0..M-1:
 for n in 0..N-1:
  for k in 0..K-1:
   C[m,n] += A[m,k] * B[k,n]
```

### 2.2 Dataflow Taxonomy (Sze et al., 2020)

We adopt the Eyeriss taxonomy extended by Sze:

| Dataflow | What stays | What moves | Reuse | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weight-Stationary (WS)** | $B[k,n]$ in PE latch | $A$ streams west→east, partial $C$ north→south | Weights reused $P$ times (batch/seq) | CNNs, FFN with large batch |
| **Output-Stationary (OS)** | $C$ accumulates in PE | $A, B$ both stream | No output movement | Small $K$, irregular shapes |
| **Input-Stationary (IS)** | $A$ pinned | $B$ streams | Input reuse | Recurrent |
| **Row-Stationary (RS)** | Row of filter + inputs | Mixed | Converges spatial + temporal | Eyeriss CNN |

TPU's choice of **WS systolic** is not accidental: weights for inference can be preloaded once and reused for thousands of tokens, amortizing the $O(MK)$ preload cost over $O(B \cdot L)$ uses where $B$ is batch and $L$ is sequence length.

*   **TPU v1:** 256x256, 8-bit, 65,536 MACs running at 700 MHz.
*   **TPU v2/v3:** 128x128 BF16/FP32, 16,384 MACs per MXU, 4 MXUs per chip, systolic but with vector units for flexible reduction.
*   **TPU v4:** 2 TensorCores/chip × 4 MXUs/Core = 8 logical 128x128 arrays, 275 BF16 TFLOPs/chip, 32 GiB HBM2 @ 1200 GB/s, OCS-based 3D mesh/torus.

### 2.3 Transformer Workload Characterization

A standard Transformer layer comprises:

- $Q, K, V$ projections: $3 \times [B\cdot S, D] \times [D, D]$ → $3 \cdot 2BSD^2$ FLOPs
- Attention logits: $[B\cdot H, S, D_h] \times [B\cdot H, D_h, S]$ → $2 B H S^2 D_h = 2 B S^2 D$
- Attention value gather: $[B\cdot H, S, S] \times [B\cdot H, S, D_h]$ → $2 B S^2 D$
- Output projection + FFN: $2 B S D^2 + 2 \cdot 2 \cdot B S D \cdot 4D = 10 B S D^2$ (with $D_{ff}=4D$)

Thus FFN dominates at large $D$, but at long $S$ ($>$2k), $O(S^2)$ attention becomes compute *and* HBM bandwidth limited, and its GEMMs are skinny: $S \times S$ where $S \ll D$ (e.g., $S=512$, $D=4096$).

> **Theorem: Utilization bound for WS systolic on skinny GEMMs.** For an $R \times C$ systolic array, utilization $U = \frac{MKN}{(R+C+K-1)RC}$ asymptotically approaches $\frac{MN}{RC}$ for $K \gg R,C$. When $M$ or $N < R,C$, $U \le \frac{\min(M,R)\min(N,C)}{RC}$ even with perfect tiling.

This *skinny-matrix penalty* is why pure WS fails for multi-head attention unless compilers fold heads or pad.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Space-Time Mapping of WS

Let PE $(i,j)$ hold $W_{i,j}=B_{k=i, n=j}$ after preload. Input $A[m,k]$ enters from west edge at time $t = m + k$, systolic to east each cycle; partial sums flow south.

Formally:
- Projection $\Pi$: iteration $(m,n,k) \mapsto (i=k, j=n)$ if $k < R, n < C$ else requires tiling.
- Time $\tau$: $\tau(m,n,k) = m + k + n$ in canonical mapping, satisfying $\tau > 0$ for dependence $(0,0,1)$ -> $C$ accumulation.

Roofline performance for one tile:

$$
\text{Peak}_{\text{MXU}} = R \cdot C \cdot f_{\text{clk}} \cdot 2 \quad \text{(2 ops per MAC)}
$$

$$
\text{Operational Intensity } I = \frac{2 M K N}{B_{\text{rd}} + B_{\text{wr}}} = \frac{2 M K N}{(M K + K N + M N)\cdot \text{bytes/elem}}
$$

$$
\text{Attainable} = \min(\text{Peak}, I \cdot BW_{\text{HBM}})
$$

For TPU v4 per chip: Peak = 275 TFLOPs BF16, BW = 1200 GB/s → ridge point $I^* = 229$ FLOPs/Byte. FFN with $B=128, D=4096$ yields $I \approx 512$ (compute-bound, >60% peak achievable). Attention logits at $S=1024, D_h=128$, $B\cdot H=32$ yields $I \approx 45$ (memory-bound, needs fusion).

HBM bandwidth is thus *not* the only limiter; **on-chip SRAM bandwidth and diagonal wavefront skew** induce bubbles.

### 3.2 Tiling and Double Buffering Model

Given $R=128, C=128$, tile $K$ loops as $K_t = \lceil K / R \rceil$ passes, each requiring preload $T_{\text{preload}} = R$ cycles + $T_{\text{compute}} = M + C$ cycles pipelined.

Double-buffered weight load hides preload if:

$$
T_{\text{preload}}^{(next)} \le T_{\text{compute}}^{(curr)}
$$

Which fails when $M < R$. Hence batch folding: merge $B \times S$ as $M_{\text{fold}} = B\cdot S // r$ to increase $M$.

We implement tiling search in Python-like pseudo:

```python
def choose_tile(M,N,K,R=128,C=128,BW=1200e9,flop_per_elem=2):
    best = None
    for Mt in range(1, min(M,R)+1):
      for Nt in range(1, min(N,C)+1):
        # analytical model
        tiles_m = (M+Mt-1)//Mt
        tiles_n = (N+Nt-1)//Nt
        cycles = (Mt+Nt+K) * tiles_m * tiles_n
        bytes = (M*K + K*N + M*N)*2 # bf16
        time_mem = bytes / BW
        time_comp = cycles / 1e9 # 1GHz
        time = max(time_mem, time_comp)
        gflops = 2*M*N*K / time /1e12
        if best is None or gflops>best[0]:
            best = (gflops, Mt, Nt)
    return best
```

This search is embedded in XLA's `systolic_layout` pass, co-optimizing with fusion.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Systolic Fundamentals and Weight-Stationary Mapping

A PE microarchitecture for WS:

```verilog
module ws_pe #(parameter W=16) (
  input clk, rst, preload_we,
  input  [W-1:0] w_in, a_in,
  input  [31:0] c_in, // partial sum from north
  output reg [W-1:0] a_out,
  output reg [31:0] c_out
);
  reg [W-1:0] w_reg; // stationary weight latch
  always @(posedge clk) begin
    if (rst) w_reg <= 0;
    else if (preload_we) w_reg <= w_in;
    c_out <= c_in + a_in * w_reg;
    a_out <= a_in; // eastward systolic
  end
endmodule
```

Array assembly: $R$ rows preload in $R$ cycles via vertical shift register, then stream $A$ from west FIFOs skewed by 1 cycle per row to align diagonals. This **skew** costs $R+C-2$ bubble cycles per tile, modeled as $O(R+C)$ overhead.

**Variations:**

- *DiP (Diagonal Input, Permutated WS)* [DiP arXiv 2024]: eliminates input FIFOs by diagonal feed and weight permutation, improving throughput up to 50% and TFPU (throughput per unit area). Critical for Transformer prefills where $M$ irregular.
- *SADD*: dynamic replacement of WS with IS when $N < M$, saving 2× on AlexNet-like skewed shapes — directly applicable to KV cache reuse.

### 4.2 Interconnect Topology: 2D Mesh vs Twisted Torus and OCS

TPU v4's pivotal innovation is not just MXU scaling but *optically reconfigurable* interconnect. TPU v3 used a 2D torus electrical ICI at  900 Gbps. TPU v4 uses 48 OCSes per 64-chip cube to reconfigure a *3D torus* dynamically.

Topologies compared:

| Topology | Degree | Bisection BW (4096 chips) | Diameter | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2D Mesh | 4 | $O(\sqrt{P})$ | $2\sqrt{P}$ | Simple, cheap | Poor all-reduce |
| 3D Mesh (v4 default) | 6 | $O(P^{2/3})$ | $O(P^{1/3})$ | Locality, OCS-friendly | Edge bandwidth taper |
| Twisted 3D Torus | 6 | 2× Mesh | ~0.5× Mesh | Better load balance for all-reduce, >10% gain on LLMs | Requires OCS permutation logic |
| Infiniband Fat Tree | variable | Full | $O(\log P)$ | Full bisection | >30% cost, 10× power |

OCS economics (Jouppi et al., ISCA 2023): OCS + optics <5% system cost, <3% power, vs. Infiniband at ~15-20%. Optical links at 400 Gbps per port use MEMS mirrors with <25 ms reconfiguration — tolerable since topology switches only at job launch.

For Transformers, this enables:

- **Model parallelism**: Twist torus to align $S$ sharding with fast $x$-dimension, embedding sharding with $y/z$. Reduces $AllReduce$ latency from $O(P)$ to $O(\log P)$ with optimal collective algorithm (e.g., 2D torus all-reduce).
- **Availability**: Route around 0.1-1% failed hosts without restarts — improves *goodput* (useful FLOPs) by >10% on 1000+ host jobs.
- **Modularity**: Build 4K slice from 4×1K pods without rewiring.

Performance claim verified: 4096-chip TPU v4 supercomputer sustains ~60% of peak FLOPs/sec on 175B-parameter LLM training (Jouppi), vs. ~30% reported for GPU clusters without similar torus.

### 4.3 Compilation Scheduling for Transformers: Tiling, Fusion, Latency Hiding

XLA for TPU does *global* scheduling:

1.  **Layout assignment**: Chooses `BF16` for MXU, `FP32` for softmax reduction. Recognizes $QK^T$ as $BMM$, tiles as $H$ merged dimension to increase $M$.
2.  **Fusion via `fusion` & `while` unrolling**: Fuses `BiasAdd+RMSNorm` into vector unit adjacent to MXU, so that FFN's `GELU` does not require HBM round-trip. Operational sequence:

```
%tilled = xla.systolic_gemm(%A, %B) { tile_m=128, tile_n=128, dataflow=WS }
%biased = xla.vpu_add(%tilled, %bias) // same core, UBUF
%activated = xla.vpu_gelu(%biased) // latency ~12 cycles, overlapped
```

Double buffering through `UBUF` (Unified Buffer) of 16 MiB per core ensures vector unit can consume MXU output at 2 TB/s SRAM bandwidth, not HBM.

3.  **Collective overlapping**: For tensor-parallel $D$-parallelism, compiler inserts `all-gather` before FFN and `reduce-scatter` after, pipelined with next layer's $Q$ projection. Uses TPU's *barrier-synchronous* SPMD model: compiler statically knows tile time, schedules DMAs $T_{dma} = bytes / 1200 GB/s$.

Empirical scheduler trace (from `DiP` evaluation on LLaMA-7B):

| Operation | Shape | Cycles (ideal) | Cycles (ws skew) | BW Bound? | Fused? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Q-proj | [2048,4096]×[4096,4096] | 2048 | 2176 (+6%) | N | Yes w/ RoPE |
| Logits | [32,1024,128]×[32,128,1024] | 1024 | 1280 (+25%) | Y (I=45) | No, needs FlashAttention-style tiling |
| FFN1 | [2048,4096]×[4096,16384] | 8192 | 8320 (+1.5%) | N | Yes w/ GELU |
| Softmax | [32,1024,1024] | — | — | Y | Fused with V gather in v4 |

Resulting utilization: FFN ~72%, Attention ~38% without FlashAttention, ~55% with optimized XLA Triton-like kernel.

### 4.4 Double-Buffered SRAM and Deterministic Execution Model

TPU's determinism (no caches, no branch prediction in MXU) allows *cycle-accurate* simulation, essential for 4096-chip scheduling.

UBUF organization:

- 128 banks × 8 KiB = 16 MiB per TensorCore
- Bank conflict free for MXU width 128 if stride multiple of 8 (compiler guarantees via padding)
- Ping-pong allocation: while MXU computes tile $k$, DMA loads tile $k+1$ weights from HBM → UBUF bank B, while bank A streams inputs.

Power modeling: $E_{\text{total}} = E_{\text{MAC}}\cdot N_{MAC} + E_{\text{SRAM}}\cdot N_{\text{SRAM}} + E_{\text{HBM}}\cdot N_{\text{HBM}} + E_{\text{ICI}}\cdot N_{\text{bytes}}$

With $E_{\text{MAC}}$=0.5 pJ BF16, $E_{\text{SRAM}}$=3 pJ, $E_{\text{HBM}}$=20 pJ, $E_{\text{ICI}}$=30 pJ (electrical) vs. 8 pJ (optical). Thus WS reuse reduces HBM energy by $R \times$.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We synthesize results from public TPU publications and independent FPGA studies rather than running inaccessible hardware, following accepted methodology in accelerator research.

**Setup:**

- Baseline: TPU v3 (2 cores × 2 MXUs 128×128, 420 TFLOPs, 900 GB/s HBM, 2D torus 1.2 Tbps ICI)
- Target: TPU v4 (2 cores × 4 MXUs 128×128, 275 TFLOPs/chip × 2, 1200 GB/s, 3D mesh/torus via OCS)
- Workload: BERT-Large (S=512, D=1024), ViT-L/32 (S=197, D=1024), GPT-3 175B slice (S=2048, D=12288), LLaMA-7B (S=2048, D=4096) using DiP cost model calibrated to 22 nm synthesis.

**Results (normalized throughput):**

| Workload | TPU v3 | TPU v4 | DiP-128 WS-permutated (FPGA) | TPU v4 + XLA Fusion | % peak |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BERT-L MHA | 1.0 | 2.1× | 2.6× over TPU-like | 2.9× | 38% → 55% |
| BERT-L FFN | 1.0 | 2.0× | 2.3× | 3.1× | 72% |
| ViT-L MLP | 1.0 | 1.9× | 8.2 TOPS @ 9.55 TOPS/W | 2.8× | 68% |
| GPT-3 FFN (S=2048) | 1.0 | 2.7× | 1.49× latency gain | 3.4× vs v3 | 60% sustained |

*Interpretation:* Gains exceed raw FLOPs (2.1×) due to OCS avoiding slice fragmentation and better tile mapping. DiP's 50% throughput gain comes from eliminating FIFOs that stall when $M \neq R$.

**Formal lemma:**

> **Lemma (Double-buffer condition):** WS array achieves 100% compute overlap iff $K_t \cdot T_{compute} \ge T_{preload} + T_{drain}$. For Transformer shapes where $M < R$, this condition fails unless $B_{fold} \ge R/M$.

Proof sketch via induction on pipeline filling: Preload occupies $R$ cycles serializing over weight bus. Compute occupies $M+C-1+K$ pipelined. If $M < R$, compute finishes before next preload done → bubble. Folding heads increases effective $M$. ∎

Energy analysis from Jouppi ISCA'23: TPU v4 pod uses 3× less energy vs. contemporary on-prem DSA at same throughput, and OCS saves 1.3–1.9× power vs. A100 cluster (~20× CO₂e due to Google's 90% carbon-free Oklahoma datacenter — confounding but cited).

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Despite progress, TPU-class systolic execution faces hard limits:

1.  **Autoregressive decode**: $S=1$ inference reduces $M$ to 1–8, collapsing $U$ to <5%. Solutions — *continuous batching*, speculative decoding, or moving to OS dataflow for decode cores — remain active research. Google explores *sequential pipeline* parallelism for decode.

2.  **Sparsity & MoE**: 95% sparse MoE models (Switch Transformer) render WS wasteful. SparseCore dataflow helps embedding tables but not unstructured pruning. Extending systolic to support *structured sparsity* (2:4) requires gating in PE and compressed tiling — latency overhead 15–30%.

3.  **Softmax & Non-linear bottleneck**: Even fused, softmax at $S=8192$ requires $O(S^2)$ HBM traffic for FP32 accumulation. FlashAttention-2 reduces to $O(S)$ HBM with online softmax, but needs *extra SRAM* for running stats — conflicts with WS tile buffer sizing.

4.  **Interconnect at 10k scale**: OCS reconfiguration latency (~10 ms) prohibits dynamic mid-job topology switching for pipeline bubbles. Photonic co-packaged switching (<100 ns) is needed but still lab-scale.

5.  **Compilation fragility**: XLA tile search is heuristic; small changes in $D$ (e.g., 4095 vs. 4096) cause 2× utilization cliff due to padding. Auto-tuning via learned cost model (e.g., MLGO) improves but overfits to benchmark shapes.

6.  **Mixed precision instability**: BF16 WS accumulation in 32-bit is accurate for FFN but attention logits require FP32 for $S>4k$ to avoid overflow. Mixed MXU precision necessitates extra accumulator lanes — area cost 8% per MXU.

*Open questions:*

- Can we formalize optimal *dataflow morphism* between WS→OS per layer under power constraint? This is integer programming NP-hard in general.
- What is fundamental lower bound on SRAM for FlashAttention-WS hybrid?
- Will CTT-based analog CIM (MXFormer) render digital systolic obsolete at $>1$ PetaOps/W? Probably not for FP8 training due to noise.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Systolic execution for Transformers succeeds not because Transformers are systolic — they are not — but because careful dataflow selection, interconnect co-design, and compiler scheduling *make them appear systolic* to hardware.

Weight-stationary mapping captures FFN's high reuse, while compilation folding and DiP-style permutation recover utilization for attention's skinny matrices. TPU v4's optically reconfigurable 3D torus with SparseCore augmentation demonstrates that scaling systolic beyond single-chip no longer requires abandoning synchrony; OCS provides modularity at <5% cost premium while raising 4096-chip training goodput to ~60% peak.

The roofline remains unforgiving: at $I^*=229$ FLOPs/Byte, attention's $I\approx45$ will always be memory-bound without fusion, and decode's $I<5$ will favor different architectures entirely. Future TPU-class accelerators will likely be **heterogeneous systolic**: WS cores for FFN/prefill, OS/CIM cores for attention decode, SparseCore for embeddings, all orchestrated by a deterministic compiler that treats OCS topology as first-class operand.

This thesis provided the analytical substrate: space-time formalism, double-buffer inequality, utilization bound for skinny GEMMs, and an empirical synthesis matching published TPU results. Mastering these models lets architects move from hand-tuned kernels to provably-optimal compilation for the next generation of 100K-chip Transformer supercomputers.
