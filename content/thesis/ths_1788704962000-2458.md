# SMT Solving with Z3: CDCL(T) Architecture, Theory Combination via Nelson-Oppen, Bit-Vector Blasting, and Array Theory Decision Procedures

## Abstract

The Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) problem—deciding whether a first-order formula is satisfiable with respect to a combination of background theories—lies at the heart of modern hardware verification, software model checking, and program synthesis. This thesis dissects the architecture of Z3, the industrial SMT solver of de Moura and Bjørner [1], as the canonical embodiment of the conflict-driven clause-learning modulo theories, or CDCL(T), paradigm. We reconstruct the DPLL(T) interaction loop in which a SAT core enumerates Boolean assignments while specialized theory solvers supply consistency checks, theory propagation, and theory lemmas [2][3]; we develop the Nelson–Oppen combination procedure for stably-infinite theories with disjoint signatures, including purification, arrangement search, and the convexity shortcut [4][10]; we detail bit-vector blasting, in which word-level operators are rewritten and expanded into AND-inverter graphs discharged by a SAT backend [7][9]; and we present the decision procedure for McCarthy's theory of arrays via read-over-write axioms, extensionality, and congruence closure. Complementary reductions—Ackermannization of uninterpreted functions and e-matching for quantifiers—are analyzed alongside soundness and completeness arguments, empirical findings from the literature, and the fundamental limitations that remain.

## 1 Introduction

Propositional satisfiability (SAT) solvers have undergone a quiet revolution. Conflict-driven clause learning (CDCL)—non-chronological backjumping, first-UIP learning, two-watched-literal propagation, aggressive restarts—turned SAT into an industrial workhorse. Yet pure propositional logic is a poor modeling language for real verification artifacts: a 32-bit adder is not naturally 96 Boolean variables, and an array update is certainly not a clause. Encoding everything to bits *works*, but it discards the structure that makes reasoning tractable and can inflate formulas exponentially.

*Satisfiability Modulo Theories* (SMT) is the principled answer: decide whether a formula is satisfiable **modulo** background theories—linear arithmetic, bit-vectors, arrays, uninterpreted functions—so the solver reasons at the problem's level rather than at the level of bits. The dominant architecture is **CDCL(T)**: a CDCL SAT core enumerates candidate Boolean assignments to the formula's *theory atoms*, while dedicated *theory solvers* check those assignments for theory-consistency, propagate implied literals, and return *theory lemmas* that steer the SAT core away from theory-inconsistent regions [2][6].

Our subject is **Z3**, announced by Leonardo de Moura and Nikolaj Bjørner at TACAS 2008 [1]. Z3 is arguably the most widely deployed SMT solver in existence—embedded in Dafny, F*, VCC, symbolic executors, and synthesis pipelines—and its architecture is the textbook realization of CDCL(T): a simplifier and clause compiler feed a SAT engine, which cooperates with a congruence-closure core and theory solvers for arithmetic, arrays, bit-vectors, datatypes, and strings. This thesis reconstructs the **CDCL(T) interaction loop** from the abstract DPLL(T) transition system [2]; develops **Nelson–Oppen theory combination** [10] from purification through arrangement search to convexity and delayed combination [5][8]; details **bit-vector blasting** from word-level rewriting to AIG discharge [7][9]; and presents the **array theory decision procedure**—read-over-write axioms plus extensionality—integrated with congruence closure and Ackermannization. We close with metatheory, the empirical record, and an honest catalog of limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The SMT problem

Fix a signature Σ and a Σ-theory *T*. The **SMT problem** asks whether a formula φ is *T-satisfiable*: does some model of *T* satisfy φ? Practice centers on quantifier-free fragments named by the SMT-LIB standard [7]: `QF_LRA`, `QF_LIA`, `QF_BV`, `QF_AX`, `QF_UF`, and combinations like `QF_AUFBV`.

Every formula has a **Boolean skeleton**: replace each theory atom by a fresh propositional variable. For φ := (x > 0 ∨ x = 1) ∧ (x² < 0 ∨ x² > 9), the skeleton is (a ∨ b) ∧ (c ∨ d). A SAT solver may propose {a ↦ ⊤, c ↦ ⊤}; the theory solver must find {x > 0, x² < 0} *T*-inconsistent and *explain why*, so the clause (¬a ∨ ¬c) can be learned [6]. This propose-and-refute loop, refined with propagation and learning, *is* lazy SMT.

### 2.2 From DPLL to CDCL, abstractly

Nieuwenhuis, Oliveras, and Tinelli [2] recast DPLL as an **abstract transition system** over states *M ∥ F*—a trail *M* of assigned literals and a clause set *F*:

| Rule | Effect | Intuition |
|---|---|---|
| **Decide** | *M ∥ F* ⇒ *M lᵈ ∥ F* | decision literal |
| **UnitPropagate** | *M ∥ F, C ∨ l* ⇒ *M l ∥ F, C ∨ l* if *M* ⊨ ¬C | forced assignment |
| **Conflict** | detect *M* ⊨ ¬C for *C ∈ F* | dead end |
| **Explain / Learn** | conflict ⇒ *M ∥ F, D* | analyze, learn clause *D* |
| **Backjump** | conflict ⇒ *M′ l ∥ F, D* | non-chronological backtrack |
| **Restart / Forget** | reset trail; drop clauses | diversify search |
| **Fail / Success** | *Fail* iff *F* unsat; else model *M* | termination |

Soundness, completeness, and termination follow from a well-founded measure on states [2]; DPLL(T) keeps this engine *exactly as is* and adds theory rules alongside it.

### 2.3 The theories of interest

- **EUF**: equality with uninterpreted functions, decided by *congruence closure*.
- **Difference logic / linear arithmetic**: atoms *x − y ≤ k*; LRA via Simplex, LIA via branch-and-bound and cutting planes.
- **Bit-vectors (BV)**: fixed-width two's-complement machine arithmetic—`concat`, `extract`, `bvadd`, `bvmul`, `bvudiv`, shifts, signed/unsigned comparisons—with defined division by zero [7].
- **Arrays**: McCarthy's `select`/`store` (§4.4).

Real formulas mix these freely—*p ∨ x − read(A, i) ≤ y ∨ f(write(A, j, i+2)) = read(A, j) + 1*—which is why **theory combination** (§4.2) is load-bearing, not optional [6].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Architectural reconstruction from primary sources

We synthesize the Z3 architecture from the sources that actually establish each mechanism: abstract DPLL(T) [2], the DPLL(X) parametric engine [3], lazy SMT integration [6], the SMT-LIB standard [7], and the tool paper itself [1]—while respecting the boundary noted in [11] between the announcement and the internals papers.

### 3.2 Eager vs. lazy integration

1. **Eager**: translate everything to SAT up front. Complete for finite domains, but surrenders high-level structure and can explode.
2. **Lazy (DPLL(T))**: keep theories symbolic; SAT core and theory solvers cooperate incrementally.

Lazy integration splits further [6]: **offline** ("lemmas on demand") checks *full* Boolean assignments wholesale, while **online** interleaves theory reasoning with *partial* assignments, enabling **theory propagation**—the solver asserting literals the SAT core never decided, e.g. deriving *x ≠ y* from arithmetic bounds. Z3 is firmly online.

### 3.3 The theory-solver contract

A DPLL(T) theory solver must honor a strict interface [2][3][6]:

- **Incremental assertion** of literals as the trail grows;
- **Backtrackable state** (trail-based undo) for backjumping;
- **Consistency checking**—cheap partial checks mid-search, full checks at complete assignments;
- **Theory propagation** of entailed unassigned literals;
- **Explanation**: on *T*-inconsistency, return a small inconsistent subset μ; the SAT core learns the **theory lemma** ¬μ;
- **Interface equalities** over shared variables, for combination (§4.2).

Any solver honoring this contract plugs into the generic DPLL(X) engine to yield DPLL(T)—the modularity result that made the architecture dominant [3].

### 3.4 Z3's pipeline

Per [1]: input → **simplifier/rewriter** (constant folding, destructive equality resolution) → **compiler** (Tseitin CNF-ization, BV preprocessing) → **SAT core** → **congruence-closure core** (the e-graph, handling EUF and dispatching) → **theory solvers** (Simplex arithmetic, arrays, BV via rewriting + blasting, datatypes, strings). Quantifiers go through **e-matching** and **model-based quantifier instantiation (MBQI)**.

```smt2
(set-logic QF_LRA)
(declare-fun x () Real) (declare-fun y () Real)
(assert (and (> x 0) (< (+ x y) 1) (> y 0)))
(check-sat)   ; => sat
```

```python
from z3 import Reals, Solver, sat
x, y = Reals('x y')
s = Solver()
s.add(x > 0, x + y < 1, y > 0)
assert s.check() == sat
print(s.model())  # e.g. [y = 1/4, x = 1/4]
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The CDCL(T) Interaction Loop

After each round of Boolean propagation, the SAT core notifies theory solvers of newly asserted literals on the trail *M*. A solver may then:

- **(a)** report *T*-consistency and **propagate**: from {*x − y ≤ 0*, *z = x*} the arithmetic solver propagates *z − y ≤ 0*, treated by the SAT core like any propagated literal;
- **(b)** report a **theory conflict** with explanation μ ⊆ *M*; the SAT core learns the **theory lemma** ¬μ and backjumps;
Conflict analysis generalizes first-UIP learning: the implication graph gains edges justified by *theory explanations*, and resolution over them yields learned clauses mixing Boolean and theory reasons [2][6]. **Plunging**—speculatively probing the solver with unassigned literals to discover propagations—is quadratic in the worst case and used sparingly [6].

**Model-based theory combination (MBC)** [8] refines the loop: the SAT core asks each solver for a *candidate model* of its literals, and disagreements on shared variables generate targeted lemmas.

> **Theorem (Abstract DPLL(T) correctness [2]).** *Let T be decidable with a contract-satisfying solver (§3.3). Then DPLL(T) is sound (learned lemmas are T-valid), complete (Fail is reached iff the input is T-unsatisfiable), and terminating.*

### 4.2 Nelson–Oppen Theory Combination

The classical combination result is the **Nelson–Oppen procedure** [10] for *stably-infinite* theories with *disjoint signatures* (sharing only equality).

**Step 1 — Purification.** Replace "alien" subterms with fresh variables. In

> φ := f(f(x) − f(y)) ≠ f(z) ∧ x ≤ y ∧ y + z ≤ x ∧ 0 ≤ z

the term *f(x) − f(y)* mixes arithmetic and EUF. With *e₁ = f(x)*, *e₂ = f(y)* we obtain pure parts: *f(e₁ − e₂) ≠ f(z)* (EUF) alongside *x ≤ y ∧ y + z ≤ x ∧ 0 ≤ z* (arithmetic) [4].

**Step 2 — Equality sharing.** Solvers exchange entailed equalities over *shared (interface) variables* until fixpoint or conflict.

> **Theorem (Nelson–Oppen, 1979 [10]).** *A purified conjunction Γ₁ ∧ Γ₂ is (T₁ ∪ T₂)-satisfiable iff some **arrangement** δ—a complete equality/disequality partition of the shared variables—makes each Γᵢ ∧ δ Tᵢ-satisfiable.*

The catch is exponential arrangement search. The **convexity** shortcut rescues the common case: *T* is *convex* if Γ ⊨ ⋁ᵢ(xᵢ = yᵢ) implies Γ ⊨ (xᵢ = yᵢ) for some *i*. EUF, LRA, and difference logic are convex; LIA is not. For convex pairs, solvers only *propagate entailed equalities*—no guessing [4].

**Delayed Theory Combination (DTC)** [5] moves arrangement search into the SAT solver: interface equalities become Boolean literals, and CDCL enumerates arrangements with learning and backjumping. DTC is empirically competitive with eager Nelson–Oppen precisely because SAT learning prunes the arrangement space [5]. Z3 goes further with model-based combination [8], proposing likely arrangements from candidate models first.



### 4.3 Bit-Vector Blasting to AIG/SAT

The SMT-LIB BV theory [7] models machine arithmetic; deciding QF_BV is conceptually simple—every operator has a Boolean circuit encoding—so **bit-blasting** expands word-level formulas into propositional logic for the SAT solver. The engineering is in surviving the expansion. Z3's `bv` tactic proceeds in stages—rewrite, blast, reduce, discharge:

1. **Word-level rewriting.** Algebraic simplification before any blasting: constant folding, `(extract i j (concat a b))` reduction, distributivity of `extract` over `bvadd`, normalization of nested additions. Multiplication by constants becomes shifts-and-adds [9].
2. **Blasting to circuits.** Remaining operators expand to gates: ripple-carry/carry-lookahead adders, O(n²) partial-product multipliers, barrel shifters. A 64-bit multiplier already costs thousands of gates—the fundamental quadratic tax.
3. **AIG reduction.** The circuit becomes an **AND-inverter graph** with *structural hashing* (shared subcircuits built once) and *fraiging* (SAT-sweeping merges functionally equivalent nodes), dramatically compressing the encoding.
4. **SAT discharge.** Tseitin-encoded CNF goes to the SAT core.

Word-level reasoning avoids blasting when possible. **Invertibility conditions** (Niemetz et al., CAV 2018) give, for each operator ⋄ and literal *x ⋄ s ⋈ t*, a quantifier-free *IC(s,t)* equivalent to ∃x. *x ⋄ s ⋈ t*—a complete elimination procedure for "linear" BV formulas without expanding a gate. Z3's rewriter internalizes many such identities.

Blasting is *complete and predictable* but scales with circuit size; word-level methods are *fast when they fire* but incomplete. Production strategy: rewrite aggressively, blast the residue.

### 4.4 The Array Theory Decision Procedure

McCarthy's arrays provide `select`/`store` governed by the **read-over-write axioms**:

1. **select(store(a, i, v), i) = v** — reading a fresh write returns the value;
2. **i ≠ j → select(store(a, i, v), j) = select(a, j)** — writes elsewhere are invisible;
3. **Extensionality**: (∀i. select(a, i) = select(b, i)) → a = b.

The quantifier-free fragment is decidable via **congruence closure with read-over-write rewriting** [6]: every `select(store(a,i,v),j)` simplifies by (1) when *i = j* is known, or (2) when *i ≠ j* is known.

```smt2
(set-logic QF_AX)
(declare-fun a () (Array Int Int))
(declare-fun i () Int) (declare-fun j () Int) (declare-fun v () Int)
(assert (distinct i j))
(assert (distinct (select (store a i v) j) (select a j)))
(check-sat)  ; => unsat, by axiom (2)
```

Since *i ≠ j*, the solver rewrites `select(store(a,i,v),j)` to `select(a,j)`, making the disequality self-contradictory—no search needed. Extensionality is the expensive direction: refuting *a ≠ b* demands a *witness index* where they differ, so solvers generate witnesses lazily via models rather than naive axiom application. The **array property fragment** (bounded universal index quantification) stays decidable and covers most verification idioms.

In Z3 [1], the array solver sits atop the congruence-closure core: array terms live in the e-graph, read-over-write fires as theory propagation, extensionality witnesses are constructed on demand—the CDCL(T) pattern of *propagate eagerly, case-split lazily*.

### 4.5 Ackermannization and Congruence Closure

**Congruence closure** is Z3's beating heart: the e-graph maintains term equivalence classes closed under *congruence* (a = b → f(a) = f(b)), deciding EUF in almost-linear time (Downey–Sethi–Tarjan). Every theory solver ultimately communicates through it.

**Ackermannization** is the classical alternative: replace each *f(t)* by a fresh variable *v_{f,t}* and add *functional consistency* constraints *(tᵢ = tⱼ) → (v_{f,tᵢ} = v_{f,tⱼ})* for all pairs—reducing EUF to pure equality at *quadratic* cost with sharing destroyed. Congruence closure achieves the same effect *lazily*: the e-graph enforces consistency on demand, which is why no production solver Ackermannizes up front. The reduction survives as the correctness argument for "EUF = equality + congruence" and underlies Nelson–Oppen purification (§4.2).

For **quantified** formulas Z3 uses **e-matching**: triggers extracted from quantified clauses match against e-graph terms to produce instantiations—heuristic and incomplete in general—complemented by **MBQI**, which builds candidate models and instantiates quantifiers they falsify (§6).

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Metatheory

> **Theorem (Abstract DPLL(T) [2]).** *DPLL(T) with theory propagation and learning is sound, complete, and terminating for any decidable theory with a contract-satisfying solver; learned clauses are always T-valid consequences of the input.*

> **Theorem (Nelson–Oppen correctness [10]; modern proof [4]).** *Purification preserves satisfiability; arrangement search is complete for stably-infinite disjoint-signature theories; convexity reduces search to equality propagation.*

> **Theorem (QF array decidability [6]).** *The quantifier-free theory of arrays with extensionality is decidable via congruence closure with read-over-write rewriting; the array property fragment extends this to bounded universal index quantification.*

### 5.2 The empirical record

The JACM paper [2] reports the authors' Barcelogic DPLL(T) implementation **outperforming the state of the art, frequently by orders of magnitude**, on industrial benchmarks with markedly better scaling—evidence the modular DPLL(X)+solver design was not just elegant but fast. The DTC study [5] found delayed combination competitive with or superior to eager Nelson–Oppen across SMT-LIB divisions, because SAT-style learning prunes arrangement search more effectively than theory-side enumeration. Z3 itself dominated SMT-COMP divisions from its 2008 debut [1] onward.

| Scheme | SAT/theory coupling | Combination search | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offline (lemmas on demand) | loose: full assignments checked | n/a or eager NO | simplicity, SAT reuse | no early pruning |
| Online DPLL(T) | tight: incremental + theory propagation | equality propagation (convex) | early conflict detection | solver must be incremental/backtrackable |
| Delayed theory combination [5] | SAT enumerates interface equalities | CDCL over arrangement literals | learning prunes arrangements | Boolean blowup on many shared vars |
| Model-based combination [8] | candidate models guide lemmas | model-suggested arrangements | far fewer guesses | model construction cost |

## 6 Limitations

1. **Non-convex combination is exponential.** Without convexity, Nelson–Oppen guesses arrangements—2^Θ(n²) worst case. DTC [5] and model-based methods [8] mitigate but do not eliminate this; heavily-shared LIA+EUF formulas remain hard.
2. **Non-stably-infinite theories break the combination theorem.** Bit-vectors admit only finite models, so *BV + lists* is outside vanilla Nelson–Oppen; *polite*/*shiny* combination [8] recovers decidability for some pairs but demands witness functions few solvers implement.
3. **Quantifiers are incomplete.** E-matching hinges on trigger heuristics—bad triggers mean missed instantiations and spurious `unknown`s; over-eager triggers cause matching loops. MBQI helps only where candidate-model construction terminates.
4. **Non-linear arithmetic is undecidable** over ℤ (Hilbert's tenth) and doubly-exponential over ℝ (CAD). Z3's nlsat is pragmatic incomplete search: `sat`/`unsat` on most practical instances, `unknown` otherwise.
5. **Bit-blasting has a quadratic tax.** 64-bit multipliers produce enormous AIGs; word-level methods fire only on stylized patterns.
6. **Proofs cost.** Minimal theory-lemma extraction is itself hard; verbose proofs slow solving, terse proofs slow checking—unresolved at scale.
7. **Shared symbols beyond equality.** Disjoint signatures are required; theories sharing interpreted symbols need bespoke combination code, a perennial source of solver bugs.

## 7 Conclusion

CDCL(T)—abstract DPLL(T) [2] realized as a parametric DPLL(X) engine [3], instantiated with contract-satisfying theory solvers, embodied industrially by Z3 [1]—remains the dominant SMT paradigm. Its power is a division of labor that is both mathematically clean and empirically validated: the SAT core owns *search* (decisions, propagation, learning, backjumping), theory solvers own *domain reasoning* (consistency, propagation, lemmas), and combination procedures—Nelson–Oppen [10], delayed [5], model-based [8]—mediate between theories through the narrow channel of shared equalities. Bit-blasting extends the philosophy to machine arithmetic by compiling word-level structure to SAT only after rewriting, and the array decision procedure shows how a handful of axioms plus congruence closure decides an expressive theory of mutable state.

*Modularity won*: by separating Boolean search from theory reasoning behind a crisp interface, DPLL(T) turned SMT from a collection of bespoke decision procedures into an engineering discipline—and Z3 into the infrastructure on which modern verification is built.
