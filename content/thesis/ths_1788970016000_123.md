# Folding Schemes for Incremental Verifiable Computation: Nova, HyperNova, and SuperNova

## Abstract

We present a unified, rigorous treatment of folding schemes for incrementally verifiable computation (IVC), from the original Nova construction through HyperNova, SuperNova, and the surrounding ecosystem. A *folding scheme* reduces two instance-witness pairs in an NP relation to a single pair whose satisfiability implies that of its inputs, and it is sufficient to build IVC with minimal recursion overhead. We formalize relaxed R1CS, Nova's cross-term folding protocol with its two-multiexponentiation prover cost [1], HyperNova's multifolding schemes over customizable constraint systems achieving a single-MSM prover [2][3], and SuperNova's extension to non-uniform IVC with an instruction-selective accumulator. We survey companion results — Protostar's special-sound compiler [4], Protogalaxy's multi-instance folding [5], Sangria's Plonkish folding [6], and CycleFold's curve-cycle instantiation — and compare asymptotics, security assumptions, and limitations. This treatment is self-contained, technically precise, and draws exclusively on published primary sources.

---

## 1 Introduction

Incrementally verifiable computation (IVC), introduced by Valiant [7], addresses a fundamental problem in verifiable computation: how can a prover convince a verifier of the correctness of a *long-running* computation, step by step, without redoing all prior work at each step? 

Early IVC constructions relied on recursive composition of SNARKs [8][9]: at step \(i\), the prover produces a succinct non-interactive argument proving that (a) step \(F\) was applied correctly to the output of step \(i-1\), and (b) the SNARK verifier, expressed as a circuit, accepted the proof from step \(i-1\). This *recursion overhead* — the cost of embedding a full SNARK verifier inside the recursive circuit — was the dominant bottleneck, since SNARK verification typically involves pairing operations or thousands of group scalar multiplications, requiring arithmetic in mismatched fields and expensive two-cycles of elliptic curves [9].

**Nova** [1] introduced a decisive conceptual simplification: rather than embedding a *verifier* for arguments of knowledge, it is sufficient to embed the verifier of a **folding scheme**, a strictly weaker, simpler, and far more efficiently realizable primitive. A folding scheme is an interactive protocol between a prover \(P\) and a verifier \(V\) that combines two \(N\)-sized NP instances into a single \(N\)-sized instance, such that the folded instance is satisfiable only if the original instances are [1]. The verifier's cost and communication are \(O_\lambda(1)\), and the protocol is honest-verifier zero-knowledge and public-coin, so it becomes non-interactive under the Fiat–Shamir transform. This observation yields an IVC scheme whose recursion overhead is *constant* and dominated by two group scalar multiplications expressed as a circuit — the smallest recursion overhead in the literature — and whose prover's work per step is dominated by two multiexponentiations of size \(O(|F|)\), the fastest prover in the literature at the time [1].

This thesis develops the theory of folding schemes from first principles and follows its rapid evolution:

- **§2** fixes notation and background: committed relaxed R1CS, homomorphic commitments, IVC, and the sumcheck heritage of Spartan;
- **§3** states our methodology: formal definitions of folding and multifolding schemes and the relaxed constraint relations they operate over;
- **§4** is the deep dive: Nova's relaxed-R1CS fold (§4.1), HyperNova's multifolding over customizable constraint systems with a single-MSM prover (§4.2), SuperNova's non-uniform IVC (§4.3), and the wider landscape — Protostar, Protogalaxy, Sangria, Origami, Mova, and CycleFold (§4.4);
- **§5** gives an asymptotic and formal comparison, including knowledge-soundness reductions;
- **§6** discusses limitations; **§7** concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Rank-1 Constraint Systems and Their Relaxation

 Satisfiability is the familiar quadratic relation:

\[(A \cdot Z) \circ (B \cdot Z) = u \cdot (C \cdot Z),\qquad u = 1.\]

**Relaxed R1CS** [1] generalizes this by (i) allowing the scalar \(u\) to be arbitrary, and (ii) introducing an *error vector* \(E\) that absorbs cross terms generated by folding:

\[(A \cdot Z) \circ (B \cdot Z) = u \cdot (C \cdot Z) + E,\qquad Z = (W, x, u).\]

A standard R1CS instance is the special case \(u = 1\), \(E = 0\). The relaxation is not gratuitous: folding two instances produces inhomogeneous cross terms that cannot be represented within plain R1CS, and the error vector is precisely the mechanism that absorbs them while keeping instance *size* fixed at \(N\) [1].

In the *committed* variant, the witness \(W\) and error vector \(E\) are hidden behind an additively homomorphic, hiding, binding commitment scheme (e.g., Pedersen commitments), so that instance-witness pairs can be manipulated publicly while witnesses remain private — the basis of the scheme's zero-knowledge property.

### 2.2 Incrementally Verifiable Computation

Formally, an IVC scheme for a step function \(F\) provides algorithms \((\mathsf{Prove}, \mathsf{Verify})\) such that \(\mathsf{Prove}(\mathsf{pk}, i, z_0, z_i, \pi_i)\) produces \(\pi_{i+1}\) certifying \(z_{i+1} = F(z_i)\), and \(\mathsf{Verify}(\mathsf{vk}, i, z_0, z_i, \pi_i) = 1\) convinces the verifier of the correctness of the whole \(i\)-step prefix [7][1]. Completeness, knowledge soundness (extractability), and optionally succinctness (proof size sublinear or logarithmic in \(\ell\)) and zero-knowledge are required.

### 2.3 The Sumcheck Heritage: Spartan

The folding lineage descends from Spartan [10], Setty's transparent zkSNARK for R1CS whose prover applies the sumcheck protocol to a carefully crafted polynomial encoding the R1CS relation. HyperNova's multifolding construction is explicitly described as an "early stopping" version of Spartan — it runs the sumcheck protocol but *omits the final polynomial evaluation argument*, because folding only needs to reduce claims about two instances to claims about one, not to prove a standalone evaluation [2]. This early-stopping view explains why folding schemes inherit Spartan's prover efficiency while shedding its verifier's pairing or evaluation costs.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is definitional and comparative. We take as primitive the following abstraction, following Kothapalli, Setty, and Tziallas [1]:

> **Definition (Folding scheme).** A folding scheme for a relation \(\mathcal{R}\) is a triple of algorithms \((\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Fold}, \mathsf{FoldVerify})\) where \(\mathsf{Fold}\) takes two instance-witness pairs \((u_1, w_1), (u_2, w_2) \in \mathcal{R}\) and a structure \(s\), interacts with \(\mathsf{FoldVerify}\), and outputs a folded pair \((u, w)\); \(\mathsf{FoldVerify}\) outputs a bit indicating whether \(u\) is the correct folding of \(u_1, u_2\). Security requires *knowledge soundness*: from any prover that convinces the verifier of a folded instance, an extractor recovers witnesses for both inputs [1].

  We do not re-implement any scheme; instead we extract claims directly from the primary papers and cross-check consistency across the related-work sections of later papers [2][5][11].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Nova: Folding Relaxed R1CS

Nova's folding scheme operates on **committed relaxed R1CS** instance-witness pairs \((u_i, w_i)\) for \(i \in \{1, 2\}\), where \(u_i = (\bar{E}_i, u_i, \bar{W}_i, x_i)\) collects the commitments and public components, and \(w_i = (E_i, W_i)\) holds the openings. The protocol is strikingly simple [1]:

1. **Prover** computes the *cross term*

\[T = A Z_1 \circ B Z_2 + A Z_2 \circ B Z_1 - u_1 \cdot C Z_2 - u_2 \cdot C Z_1,\]

   commits to it as \(\bar{T}\), and sends \(\bar{T}\) to the verifier.

2. **Verifier** samples a random challenge \(r \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{F}\) (or derives it via Fiat–Shamir) and both parties compute the folded instance:

\[u \leftarrow (\bar{E}_1 + r \cdot \bar{T} + r^2 \cdot \bar{E}_2,\; u_1 + r \cdot u_2,\; \bar{W}_1 + r \cdot \bar{W}_2,\; x_1 + r \cdot x_2).\]

3. **Prover** computes the folded witness \(W = W_1 + r \cdot W_2\) and folded error \(E = E_1 + r \cdot T + r^2 \cdot E_2\).

The algebra is transparent: substituting into \((AZ)\circ(BZ)\) with \(Z = Z_1 + r Z_2\) yields \(u \cdot CZ + E\) exactly, where the mixed \(r\)-coefficient is absorbed by \(T\). Knowledge soundness reduces to the binding of the commitment scheme and the Schwartz–Zippel lemma over the challenge \(r\): three accepting transcripts with distinct challenges allow extraction of witnesses for both inputs [1].

 Because \(\mathsf{FoldVerify}\) performs only \(O_\lambda(1)\) group scalar multiplications, the recursion overhead is constant — the smallest known [1]. The prover's per-step work is two MSMs of size \(O(|F|)\) (for \(\bar{T}\) and the witness commitments), and the resulting proof is \(O_\lambda(|F|)\) group elements, compressible to \(O_\lambda(\log |F|)\) via a variant of the Spartan zkSNARK [1][10].

A reference implementation in Rust captures the folded relation check as follows:

```rust
/// Committed relaxed R1CS: AZ ∘ BZ = u·(CZ) + E over field F.
struct RelaxedR1CSInstance<F: PrimeField, G: Group<Scalar = F>> {
    comm_w: G, // commitment to witness W
    comm_e: G, // commitment to error vector E
    u: F,      // relaxation scalar
    x: Vec<F>, // public inputs/outputs
}

/// Nova fold: combine (u1, w1), (u2, w2) under challenge r.
fn fold<F: PrimeField, G: Group<Scalar = F>>(
    u1: &RelaxedR1CSInstance<F, G>,
    w1: &(Vec<F>, Vec<F>), // (E1, W1)
    u2: &RelaxedR1CSInstance<F, G>,
    w2: &(Vec<F>, Vec<F>), // (E2, W2)
    r: F,
    cross_t: G,            // commitment to cross term T
) -> (RelaxedR1CSInstance<F, G>, (Vec<F>, Vec<F>)) {
    let u = RelaxedR1CSInstance {
        comm_w: u1.comm_w + u2.comm_w * r,
        // E  = E1 + r·T + r²·E2  (commitment space)
        comm_e: u1.comm_e + cross_t * r + u2.comm_e * (r * r),
        u: u1.u + u2.u * r,
        x: u1.x.iter().zip(&u2.x).map(|(a, b)| *a + *b * r).collect(),
    };
    let e: Vec<F> = w1.0.iter().zip(&w2.0).map(|(a, b)| *a + *b * (r * r)).collect();
    // NOTE: prover adds r·T in the clear before committing; shown
    // schematically — production code commits T via a separate MSM.
    let w: Vec<F> = w1.1.iter().zip(&w2.1).map(|(a, b)| *a + *b * r).collect();
    (u, (e, w))
}
```

> **Theorem:** *Under the discrete-logarithm assumption for the commitment group, Nova's folding scheme for committed relaxed R1CS is knowledge-sound, and the induced IVC scheme is a knowledge-sound, succinct argument for \(F^{(\ell)}\) with \(O_\lambda(1)\) recursion overhead [1].*

### 4.2 HyperNova: Multifolding Schemes and Customizable Constraint Systems

Nova folds instances of a *single* relation (R1CS). HyperNova [2] generalizes the primitive in two orthogonal directions.  Second, it instantiates multifolding for **CCS — customizable constraint systems** [12], a framework that simultaneously generalizes Plonkish, R1CS, and AIR *without overheads*.

A CCS structure is defined by matrices \(M_0, \dots, M_{t-1}\) and a multiset \(S\) of coefficient tuples; the relation constrains

\[\sum_{i=0}^{q-1} c_i \cdot \bigcirc_{j \in S_i} (M_j \cdot z) = 0,\]

where the Hadamard products range over the sets in \(S\). R1CS is CCS with \(t = 3\) and a single quadratic term; Plonkish gates and AIR transition constraints arise from other choices of \(S\) — all with zero translation overhead [2][12].

HyperNova's multifolding scheme targets CCS and *linearized* CCS (LCCCS), a variant in which one committed component is linearized to keep the verifier's work small. Conceptually it is an early-stopping Spartan applied to a polynomial that includes claims about prior LCCCS instances [2]:

1. The prover commits to the witness and to cross-term-like vectors derived from the degree-\(d\) CCS gates (for R1CS, exactly one cross term, as in Nova; for degree \(d\), \(d-1\) cross terms, mirroring Sangria's accounting [6]);
2. The verifier's checks reduce to a *logarithmic* number of field operations plus a single group scalar multiplication;
3. The prover's *cryptographic* cost is a **single MSM** of size equal to the number of variables — optimal for MSM-based commitments [2].

Three further contributions distinguish HyperNova [3]:

- **À la carte cost profile.** When proving program executions on stateful machines (EVM, RISC-V), the per-step proving cost is proportional only to the size of the circuit for the *invoked instruction*, not the whole instruction set — a direct payoff of multifolding heterogeneous relations.
- **Zero-knowledge for free.** HyperNova randomizes IVC proofs by folding them with freshly sampled instances, achieving zero-knowledge without invoking a zkSNARK — a novel application of folding schemes in their own right [2].
- **CycleFold.** Instantiating the folding verifier natively requires expressing group scalar multiplication inside the recursive circuit — the classic "wrong-field" problem. CycleFold is a general technique, applicable to all modern folding-based recursive arguments, that offloads this check to a companion relaxed-R1CS instance on the *other* curve of a cycle, verified via Nova's fold [2][3].

HyperNova also contributes **nlookup**, a lookup argument tailored to folding-based recursion: for \(m\) lookups into a table of size \(n\), the prover does \(O(n)\) field operations with only \(O(m \log n)\) degree-2 constraints and \(O(\log n)\) hashes in the recursive circuit [2].

### 4.3 SuperNova: Non-Uniform IVC and Instruction-Selective Folding

Nova and HyperNova target *uniform* IVC: every step executes the same circuit \(F\). Real machines are non-uniform — each step selects one instruction from a large set \(\vec{F} = (F_1, \dots, F_k)\). The naive approach (a universal circuit dispatching over all instructions) pays for the *entire* instruction set at every step.

**SuperNova** [13] generalizes Nova to non-uniform IVC using relaxed committed R1CS, exactly as Nova does, but maintains *one running instance per instruction circuit*.  A selector mechanism — implemented with a constant number of hashes and a hash-to-group gadget in the recursive circuit — routes each step to the correct accumulator, so the per-step cost is proportional to the executed instruction's circuit plus a small constant, rather than to \(\sum_j |F_j|\) [4][13].

The trade-off is explicit: the accumulator, and hence the final proof, is *linear in the total size of all instances* \(\vec{F}\), and the recursive circuit still requires several hashes and the hash-to-group gadget per step [4].  Later work (e.g., Protogalaxy-style index folding and KiloNova [11]) attacks exactly this residual linearity.

### 4.4 The Wider Landscape: Protostar, Protogalaxy, Sangria, and CycleFold

**Protostar** [4] takes the most general view: it is a *compiler* that turns *any* special-sound interactive protocol into an accumulation/folding scheme. Instantiated with a Plonkish special-sound protocol, it yields folding for high-degree gates and lookups. Its structural limitation is multi-instance folding: folding \(k > 2\) instances at once blows the involved polynomial's degree up exponentially in \(k\), so Protostar folds pairwise, inflating the verifier circuit by a factor of \(O(k)\) relative to folding two instances [11].

**Protogalaxy** [5] extends Protostar to fold \(k > 2\) instances natively: the recursive verifier's marginal work beyond linearly combining witness commitments is only a logarithmic number of field operations and a constant number of hashes, so the per-instance marginal cost becomes *constant* at constant gate degree. Its prover time scales as \(O(k \log k)\), superlinear in the fold arity [11].

**Sangria** [6] adapts Nova's folding to Plonkish arithmetization: for degree-\(d\) gates the prover computes and commits to \(d - 1\) cross-term vectors, with \(O(d \cdot (n + s))\) field work and \(O(d)\) verifier point additions — the verifier remains constant-cost in the security parameter and circuit size. **Origami** extends the idea to lookup constraints via degree-7 product checks; **Mova** eliminates cross terms entirely but is restricted to R1CS [11].

**CycleFold**, introduced within the HyperNova paper [2] and applicable broadly, deserves emphasis as a *technique* rather than a scheme: by representing the folding verifier's scalar-multiplication step as a relaxed R1CS instance on the cycle's second curve and folding *that* with Nova, it nearly eliminates deferred (non-native) arithmetic — HyperNova's alternate construction uses Nova itself as a black box for this purpose [3].

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Asymptotic comparison

| Property | Nova [1] | HyperNova [2] | SuperNova [13] | Protostar [4] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constraint system | Relaxed R1CS | Relaxed CCS / LCCCS | Relaxed R1CS (per instruction) | Special-sound (e.g., Plonkish) |
| Prover crypto cost / step | 2 MSMs, size \(O(\|F\|)\) | **1 MSM**, size = #variables (optimal) | Per-circuit MSM + \(O(1)\) hashes | Sumcheck + MSMs |
| Recursion overhead | 2 scalar mults (constant) | Constant, smaller than Nova's | Constant + hash-to-group gadget | Pairwise folding: \(O(k)\) blowup for \(k\) instances |
| Folds \(k>2\) at once | No (pairwise) | Yes (multifolding) | No | Only pairwise (degree blowup) |
| Non-uniform IVC | No | Via à la carte circuits | **Yes** (per-instruction accumulators) | In principle |
| High-degree gates | No | Yes (CCS, cost ∝ #variables) | No | Yes (Plonkish) |
| Zero-knowledge | Via zkSNARK compression | **For free** (folding randomization) | Via zkSNARK compression | Via underlying protocol |
| Trusted setup / FFTs | None | None | None | None |
| Proof size | \(O_\lambda(\|F\|)\), compressible to \(O_\lambda(\log\|F\|)\) | Same profile, smaller constants | Linear in \(\|\vec{F}\|\) | Depends on instantiation |

### 5.2 Security reductions

All four schemes reduce knowledge soundness to the same two pillars:

1. **Binding of the homomorphic commitment** (Pedersen/multiexponentiation-based) under the discrete-logarithm assumption in the chosen group;
2. **Soundness of the challenge sampling** — three (or \(d+1\) for degree-\(d\)) accepting transcripts with distinct verifier challenges yield, by interpolation, witnesses for all folded inputs [1][4][6].

> **Theorem:** *Let \(\Pi\) be a public-coin folding scheme for a relaxed constraint relation with knowledge error \(\kappa\). Then the IVC obtained by embedding \(\Pi.\mathsf{FoldVerify}\) in the augmented step circuit is a knowledge-sound IVC with knowledge error \(\kappa' = O(\ell \cdot \kappa)\) over \(\ell\) steps, assuming a \(2\)-cycle of DLOG-hard elliptic curves [1][2].*

The quantitative headline results are: Nova's prover does two \(\approx |F|\)-sized MSMs per step with the smallest known verifier circuit [1]; HyperNova's prover does a *single* MSM of size equal to the variable count, which is information-theoretically optimal for MSM-based commitments [2]; SuperNova's per-step cost is \(\propto |F_j|\) for the executed instruction, with an accumulator linear in the instruction set [13]; and Protogalaxy folds \(k\) instances with constant marginal verifier field work per instance [5].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Proof size before compression.** Uncompressed folding-based IVC proofs are \(O_\lambda(|F|)\) group elements — the running instance itself must be transmitted. Succinctness is recovered only through an additional zkSNARK compression step (except HyperNova's ZK randomization, which addresses zero-knowledge but not succinctness) [1][2].
2. **SuperNova's linear accumulator.** The final proof grows with the total instruction-set size \(\|\vec{F}\|\); for EVM-scale instruction sets this is a genuine deployment cost, motivating index-folding successors [11][13].
3. **Curve-cycle dependence.** All schemes require a cycle (or 2-cycle) of DLOG-hard curves so the verifier circuit can be expressed natively; CycleFold mitigates but does not remove the need for careful curve selection [2].
4. **Multifolding predicate constraints.** HyperNova's multifolding requires the two relations' structures to satisfy a compatibility predicate; arbitrary heterogeneous circuits cannot be folded together without first being expressed in a common CCS frame [2].
5. **Post-quantum insecurity.** Every scheme surveyed relies on discrete-logarithm commitments; lattice-based folding (LatticeFold, Lova) is nascent and not covered by the same efficiency analysis [11].
6. **Concrete prover engineering.** Asymptotic MSM counts hide large constants: multiexponentiation performance, memory bandwidth for \(O(|F|)\)-sized witnesses, and hash-circuit costs dominate real deployments and are not captured by the \(O(\cdot)\) table above.

## 7 Conclusion

Folding schemes have, in under five years, displaced recursive SNARK composition as the default route to incrementally verifiable computation. Nova's insight — that reducing two instances to one is *enough*, and that the relaxed-R1CS cross term \(T\) is the whole mechanism — cut the recursion overhead to two scalar multiplications and the prover to two multiexponentiations [1]. HyperNova generalized the primitive to multifolding over customizable constraint systems, halved the prover's cryptographic work to a single optimal MSM, made zero-knowledge free, and supplied CycleFold as a universal curve-cycle instantiation technique [2][3]. SuperNova extended the reach to non-uniform IVC, paying a linear accumulator for instruction-selective proving [13], while Protostar, Protogalaxy, and Sangria mapped the space of generic, multi-instance, and Plonkish folding [4][5][6]. The remaining frontiers — sublinear accumulators for large instruction sets, post-quantum folding, and concrete prover engineering at EVM scale — are precisely where the next generation of results will land. The folding paradigm, however, is now firmly established: *do not verify the proof; fold the instance.*
