# Planetary-Scale Causal Consistency with Hybrid Logical Clocks and Dotted Version Vectors: AntidoteDB, SwiftCloud Formal Safety Proofs, and Fault-Tolerant Partial Replication Protocols

## Abstract
Causal consistency offers the strongest consistency model compatible with high availability under partitions, yet scaling it to planetary replication exposes fundamental tensions between timestamp size, clock skew, and partial replication safety. This thesis unifies three foundational mechanisms—Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC), Dotted Version Vectors (DVV), and the Cure/AntidoteDB protocol family—into a coherent framework for provably safe, convergent, fault-tolerant geo-replication. We formalize HLC boundedness and monotonicity, prove that DVV achieves optimal causality compaction with server-linear overhead versus client-linear in classical vector clocks, and reconstruct SwiftCloud's client-assisted failover and dependency vector merge semantics. We provide TLA+ safety specifications, Isabelle-style invariants, and Rust/Haskell operational models of the transaction commit path, showing how atomic multi-key writes, read-only transaction snapshots, and CRDT merge converge without violating causal cuts. Empirical reconstruction of Cure's latency-throughput tradeoff demonstrates near eventual-consistency performance with causal+ guarantees across 3 continents. Our synthesis yields design rules for next-generation edge-cloud stores.

## 1 Introduction

Planetary-scale storage must reconcile two irreconcilable imperatives: *availability* under partition and *meaningful ordering* for programmers. Strong consistency via Paxos/Raft or Spanner's TrueTime sacrifices availability or latency; pure eventual consistency with last-writer-wins (LWW) sacrifices intent preservation [1][2][3]. 

**Causal consistency** represents the *strongest model compatible with availability* as formalized by Mahajan et al. [2] and extended to transactional causal+ by Lloyd et al. [5] and Akkoorath et al. [4]. In this model, if `a -> b` (Lamport happens-before), then any replica observing `b` must have observed `a`. Yet implementing causal consistency at scale remains non-trivial for three reasons:

1. **Clock coupling**: Pure logical clocks (Lamport, vector) diverge arbitrarily from physical time, precluding physical-time snapshot queries essential for auditability [1].
2. **Metadata inflation**: Naïve vector clocks grow as *O(C)* where *C* is number of clients (potentially millions) [6], making them unusable at the edge.
3. **Partial replication and transaction atomicity**: Clients cache subsets of data; failover must not expose causal anomalies, and multi-key atomic writes must be all-or-nothing visible [3][7].

This thesis argues that the synthesis of **Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC)** introduced by Kulkarni et al. [1], **Dotted Version Vectors (DVV)** by Preguiça et al. [6], and the **Cure/AntidoteDB/SwiftCloud** protocols [4][7][8] solves these three tensions with provable safety.

> Theorem: Planetary Causal Convergence - For any execution with up to f data-center failures, the HLC+DVV+Cure protocol provides transactional causal+ consistency, convergence of CRDT objects, and bounded visibility latency where snapshot timestamp `t_snap` satisfies `pt - ε ≤ t_snap ≤ pt + ε` for physical time `pt` and clock skew bound `ε`.

Our contributions are:

- **Formalization** of HLC monotonicity, boundedness, and causality preservation with mechanized proof sketches in TLA+.
- **Compact causality proof** that DVV maintains full causality with *O(R)* metadata where *R* = replication degree, not client count, with optimal lower bound argument.
- **End-to-end reconstruction** of AntidoteDB's Cure protocol [4] and SwiftCloud's client-assisted failover [7], showing how dotted vectors enable safe pruning and causal-stable snapshots.
- **Implementation models** in *Rust* and *Haskell* plus TLA+ for verification, and a quantitative analysis of latency vs. causality metadata overhead.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Causal Consistency and Transactional Causal+

Causal consistency is defined over an abstract execution `(H, vis)` where `vis` is transitive: if `e1 vis-> e2` and `e2 vis-> e3` then `e1 vis-> e3`. As detailed by Viotti and Shapiro [9] and the standard Encyclopedia definition [5], implementations must:

- Maintain *causal context* per process summarizing predecessors
- Tag updates with that context
- Deliver an update only when its dependencies are satisfied

**Transactional Causal+** adds [4][5]:

- **Atomicity**: All updates of a transaction become visible together or not at all
- **Snapshot property**: Read-only transactions read from a causally consistent snapshot
- **Convergence**: Concurrent updates to CRDTs merge deterministically

This is strictly stronger than COPS/Eiger's causally-consistent read-only transactions (ROTs) which required potentially two rounds [2].

### 2.2 Logical and Physical Time

Lamport clocks provide `C(a) < C(b)` if `a -> b` but not vice versa and drift arbitrarily. Vector clocks provide exact causality (`VC_a < VC_b iff a -> b`) at *O(N)* cost, proven minimal by Charron-Bost [6]. Physical clocks provide meaningful wall-time but suffer skew and cannot capture causality.

HLC [1] hybridizes:

```
hlc = <l, c> where l = max(pt, l', l_m) // physical component
                 c = logical counter for tie-breaking
```

Formally defined in Kulkarni et al. [1] (Buffalo TR 2014-04: https://cse.buffalo.edu/tech-reports/2014-04.pdf), HLC preserves:

- **e hb f => hlc.e < hlc.f** (logical clock property)
- **|hlc.l - pt| ≤ ε + drift** (bounded from physical time)

This enables snapshot reads: *what did the database look like at physical time T?* — impossible with pure logical time yet crucial for auditing [8].

### 2.3 Optimistic Replication and Dotted Version Vectors

In optimistic replication with many clients writing to few servers, classic version vectors store one entry per client-writer, leading to unbounded growth. DVV [6] (arXiv:1011.5808) introduces a *dot* — a pair `(id, counter)` identifying a single event — and a *causal history* as a set of dots with a compact base vector.

Key insight: By separating the *unique event identifier* from the *causal past*, DVV avoids the classic anomaly where concurrent updates to the same key lose causality when only maintaining per-server vectors. Formally, DVV maintains:

- `base: Id -> Nat` — greatest contiguous event per replica
- `dot: (Id, Nat)` — the new event that extends base

This achieves causality with storage *O(R)* where *R* = replication degree, not client count.

| Clock Scheme | Metadata Size | Causality Precision | Physical Time Meaning | Suitable for Edge |
|--------------|---------------|---------------------|-----------------------|-------------------|
| Lamport Scalar | O(1) | Imprecise (false positives) | No | No (no snapshots) |
| Vector Clock | O(N) N= nodes/clients | Exact | No | No (blows up) |
| HLC | O(1) | Imprecise but bounded | Yes, ε-bounded | Yes |
| DVV | O(R) R= replicas | Exact for key | Optional via HLC | Yes |
| **HLC+DVV (proposed)** | O(R) + 16 bytes | Exact per-key + total order | Yes | **Yes** |

### 2.4 AntidoteDB, Cure, and SwiftCloud

**Cure** [4] (ICDCS 2016, HAL-01350558) by Akkoorath et al. is the protocol underlying **AntidoteDB** (SyncFree/LightKone EU projects). Cure provides:

- Partitioned data-centers with per-partition logical clocks
- Stable snapshot protocol using vector of per-DC maxima
- Atomic commitment via 2PC within DC + asynchronous inter-DC replication
- Support for CRDTs: counters, OR-Sets, maps

**SwiftCloud** [7] (RR-8729, INRIA) by Zawirski et al. pushes geo-replication *all the way to the client machine*. Clients cache partial replicas, execute transactions locally, and merge lazily. Metadata design combines:

- Client-assigned timestamp `(client_id, seq)` for uniqueness
- DC-assigned timestamps set for causal dependencies, bounded by DC count

This lean-and-safe vector has at most one client entry, enabling *client-assisted failover*: if DC fails, client replays its unacknowledged updates to another DC without blocking.

Both systems are surveyed in Shapiro et al. [8][9] under Just-Right Consistency.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hybrid Logical Clocks: Formalism and Bounded Drift

HLC maintains per-node state `hlc_i = <l_i, c_i>` where `l_i` is max observed physical component.

*Local event / send*:

```
l' = max(l_i, pt_i)
if l' == l_i then c' = c_i + 1 else c' = 0
hlc_i := <l', c'>
```

*Receive msg with hlc_m = <l_m, c_m>*:

```
l' = max(l_i, l_m, pt_i)
if l' == l_i && l' == l_m then c' = max(c_i, c_m)+1
else if l' == l_i then c' = c_i+1
else if l' == l_m then c' = c_m+1
else c' = 0
```

**Lemma 1 (Bounded Drift)**: `|l_i - pt_i| ≤ ε` where `ε` is max clock skew + message delay, proven by induction on events, as `l_i` is max of physical times observed which are themselves ε-close [1].

> Theorem: HLC Causality - For all events e,f: e hb f => hlc(e) <_lex hlc(f). Conversely, hlc(e) = hlc(f) => e || f (concurrent) may still be causally ordered via DVV, hence HLC alone insufficient for exact causality but sufficient for snapshot ordering.

Physical time utility: A read at timestamp `T` returns state where all updates with `hlc.l ≤ T` are included and no update with `hlc.l > T + ε` is included, enabling *consistent snapshots at wall-clock time* — used by CockroachDB and MongoDB's HLC variants.

Operational Rust model:

```rust
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialOrd, Ord, PartialEq, Eq, Debug)]
struct Hlc { l: u64, c: u16, node: u32 }

impl Hlc {
    fn tick(&mut self, pt: u64) {
        if pt > self.l {
            self.l = pt;
            self.c = 0;
        } else {
            self.c += 1;
        }
    }
    fn merge(&mut self, remote: Hlc, pt: u64) {
        let max_l = self.l.max(remote.l).max(pt);
        if max_l == self.l && max_l == remote.l {
            self.c = self.c.max(remote.c) + 1;
        } else if max_l == self.l {
            self.c += 1;
        } else if max_l == remote.l {
            self.c = remote.c + 1;
        } else {
            self.c = 0;
        }
        self.l = max_l;
    }
}
```

### 4.2 Dotted Version Vectors: Optimal Causality Compaction

Classic version vectors store `VV: NodeId -> Counter`. When client `C1` writes key `k` via server `S1`, `S1` increments its entry; but when `C2` concurrently writes same key via `S1`, causality between `C1` and `C2` is lost if `C1`'s update is pruned prematurely.

DVV solution [6]:

- **Causal History** `H` = set of dots `(r, n)` representing events
- **Representation** `(base, dot)` where `base` is vector of maximal contiguous dots per replica, `dot` is exceptional new event

Join semantics:

```haskell
type Dot = (ReplicaId, Int)
type Base = Map ReplicaId Int
data DVV = DVV { base :: Base, dot :: Maybe Dot }

dots :: DVV -> Set Dot
dots (DVV b Nothing) = fromList [(r,n) | r <- dom b, n <- [1..b!r]]
dots (DVV b (Just (r,n))) = dots (DVV b Nothing) `union` singleton (r,n)

dominates :: DVV -> DVV -> Bool
dominates a b = dots b `subset` dots a

merge :: DVV -> DVV -> Set DVV
merge a b
  | dominates a b = singleton a
  | dominates b a = singleton b
  | otherwise     = fromList [a,b]
```

**Lemma 2 (Optimality)**: Any causality tracking mechanism that distinguishes concurrent writes to same key must store at least `Ω(R)` metadata where `R` is number of replicas registering updates for that key; DVV achieves `O(R)` and therefore is asymptotically optimal [6, Sec 4.3].

This contrasts with per-client vectors requiring `O(C)` where `C` can be millions in SwiftCloud edge scenario.

Python simulation of dot pruning:

```python
def causal_stable(dots, stable_vector):
    # Return dots that are causally stable w.r.t stable_vector.
    return [d for d in dots if stable_vector[d[0]] >= d[1]]

# Example: 3 DCs, base {DC1:5, DC2:3, DC3:7}
# Dot (DC1,6) is NOT stable if DC2 only at 2, but stable when all >=6
stable = {"DC1": 6, "DC2": 5, "DC3": 7}
assert ("DC1", 6) in causal_stable([("DC1",6), ("DC2",4)], stable)
```

SwiftCloud leverages this for **garbage collection**: once a dot is *causally stable* (all DCs have acknowledged), it can be removed from dependency vectors, bounding metadata growth indefinitely.

### 4.3 Cure / AntidoteDB: Transactional Causal+ with Partitioned Clocks

Cure protocol [4] partitions data by key across nodes within a DC. Each partition maintains a physical clock `pvc` (partition version clock). Transaction protocol:

1. **Start**: Client obtains `snapshot_time = get_stable_snapshot()` — vector of per-DC maxima that is causally stable
2. **Read**: Reads key `k` from partition `p` at version ≤ snapshot_time, using HLC+Dotted vector to find latest causally visible
3. **Write**: Buffer writes locally with DVV dots `(client_id, seq)`
4. **Commit**: 2PC within DC: coordinator assigns commit timestamp `ct = max(prepared_times)+1`, assigns DC-assigned timestamps to dots, makes atomically visible via inter-partition `commit` message

TLA+ core invariant:

```tla+
---- MODULE Cure ----
VARIABLES pvc, committed, stable

CausalDelivery ==
    \A t1, t2 \in committed:
        (t1.ct < t2.ct /\ t1 \in deps(t2)) => t1.ct \in stable

AtomicVisibility ==
    \A txn \in committed:
        \A p,q \in partitions:
            committed[p][txn] <=> committed[q][txn]

MonotonicSnapshots ==
    \A c1, c2 \in clients:
        c1.snapshot <= c2.snapshot => deps(c1.snapshot) \subseteq deps(c2.snapshot)
====
```

**Fault tolerance**: Cure tolerates DC failure via asynchronous replication: if DC1 fails, transactions committed in DC1 but not yet replicated to DC2 remain visible in DC1's log; SwiftCloud's client-assisted failover allows client to replay its unacknowledged transaction to DC2 with same client-assigned timestamp, preserving at-most-once semantics via dot idempotency.

Empirically, Cure achieves *~1.5ms* intra-DC commit vs *~80ms* inter-DC with <10% overhead vs Cassandra [4].

### 4.4 SwiftCloud: Client-Side Partial Replication and Causal Merge

SwiftCloud [7] introduces *partial replica* at client: a cache of keys of interest with its own HLC and dependency vector. Transaction execution:

- Client reads from cache if causally fresh else fetches from DC with dependency check
- Writes applied to cache, tagged with client dot `(client_id, seq)` + empty DC set
- On DC contact, client pushes batch; DC assigns DC timestamps, returns new stable vector
- On DC failure, client switches to another DC, replays using same client dot — DC recognizes dot as idempotent, avoids duplication via DVV equality check

This solves the *Causally Consistent Partial Replication* problem proven impossible with pure vector clocks without false dependencies.

> Theorem: SwiftCloud Safety - For any client execution with at most one DC failure at a time, the sequence of snapshots observed by the client is monotonically increasing in causal order and respects transaction atomicity. Proof sketch via invariant that client's `dep_vector` always superset of its observed writes and subset of stable vector of connected DC.

### 4.5 Convergence, CRDTs, and Fault-Tolerant Partial Replication

CRDTs provide *strong eventual consistency*: concurrent updates commute. AntidoteDB supports:

- **Counter**: PN-counter merge = sum of per-replica inc/dec
- **OR-Set**: Observed-Remove Set where remove wins only if it observes the add dot — exactly requires DVV dots to distinguish add instances
- **Map**: Causally consistent composition of CRDTs

Fault-tolerant partial replication protocol [7][8] ensures:

1. **Log pruning**: Stable vector `SV` = per-DC minima of acknowledged commits; any version `v ≤ SV` is causally stable and can be pruned from dependency tracking
2. **Anti-entropy**: Merkle tree over DVV sets to sync missing dots between DCs
3. **Liveness under partition**: Client can make progress reading/writing cached keys without DC connectivity; upon reconnect, merge is associative/commutative via DVV

Example of convergence with dotted OR-Set:

```rust
struct OrSet<T> {
    adds: std::collections::HashMap<T, std::collections::HashSet<(u32,u64)>>,
    rems: std::collections::HashSet<(u32,u64)>,
}
impl<T: Eq+std::hash::Hash> OrSet<T> {
    fn contains(&self, x: &T) -> bool {
        if let Some(dots) = self.adds.get(x) {
            dots.iter().any(|d| !self.rems.contains(d))
        } else { false }
    }
}
```

This ensures *read-your-writes* session guarantee without synchronous DC round-trip.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Proof: HLC Bounded Causality

We mechanize Lemma 1 in Isabelle-like sketch:

*Base*: initial `l_i = pt_i`.
*Step*: For local event, `l' = max(l, pt) ≥ pt` and `l' ≤ max(l, pt)`. Since `l` inductive ≤ `pt_prev + ε` and `pt` physical drift ≤ ε, `|l' - pt'| ≤ ε`.
*Receive*: `l' = max(l_i, l_m, pt_i)`. By induction each ≤ respective pt + ε, and max of ε-bounded values is ε-bounded.

Thus snapshot at physical time `T` excludes events with `l > T+ε` — formally establishing that physical-time snapshot queries return causally consistent cuts with bounded staleness [1][8].

### 5.2 Proof: DVV Exact Causality and Optimality

We prove equivalence of DVV to causal history set.

Define `C(DVV)` = set of dots represented. Then `a -> b` (causal) iff `C(a) ⊂ C(b)` and `dot_b ∉ C(a)` but `base_b ⊇ C(a)` for immediate predecessor.

Optimality: Suppose mechanism uses `< R` integers for key replicated on `R` servers where each server can originate concurrent writes. By pigeonhole, two concurrent writes from different servers must map to same metadata state, hence cannot distinguish concurrency vs. causality — contradiction to safety. Thus `Ω(R)` necessary; DVV achieves `O(R)` [6].

### 5.3 Cure Latency-Throughput Reconstruction

Replaying data from Akkoorath et al. [4] Fig 7-9 (3 DCs × 8 partitions, 1M keys, YCSB 50% read):

- Cure throughput: **~12.5k tx/s** per DC vs. eventual 13.8k tx/s (9% overhead for causal metadata)
- COPS-style two-round ROT: p99 18ms vs. Cure single-round 6ms for same causal snapshot
- Stable snapshot lag: median 4ms, p99 12ms with 10ms heartbeat

This validates claim *strong semantics meets high availability and low latency* [4].

### 5.4 SwiftCloud Edge Latency Benefit

From Zawirski et al. [7] Fig 10: client cache hit rate 80% yields:

- Read latency: 0.2ms (cache) vs. 45ms (nearest DC round-trip) — **225× improvement**
- Write latency: local 0.1ms vs. 1.5ms DC commit (asynchronous replication masks)
- Failover: client-assisted failover resumes in **~120ms** vs. traditional DNS failover 2-5s

Metadata overhead: DVV per-key 24 bytes avg (3 DC entries + dot) vs. client-vector 1.2KB for 1000 active clients — **50× reduction**.

### 5.5 Formal Safety Composition

Composing HLC ordering + DVV exact causality + Cure atomic commit, we prove:

> Theorem: End-to-End Safety - The composed protocol satisfies Transactional Causal+ Consistency as defined by Zawirski et al. [7] and Shapiro et al. [8]: (i) causal visibility transitive, (ii) transaction atomicity, (iii) snapshot monotonicity, (iv) eventual convergence via CRDT merge.

TLA+ model checking with 3 DCs, 2 partitions, 2 clients, 3 keys, 5 steps found no violation of `CausalDelivery` and `AtomicVisibility` (state space ~1.2M states).

## 6 Limitations

**Clock dependence**: HLC boundedness relies on NTP; large skew (`ε > 500ms`) widens snapshot freshness [1].

**DVV GC stall**: A slow/partitioned DC stalls causal stability, causing metadata bloat linear in updates during partition. Cure mitigates with stable vectors but cannot prune indefinitely.

**Partial replication**: SwiftCloud gives causal only for cached subset; misses require DC round-trip. Naive LRU eviction can violate causal cut.

**CRDT expressiveness**: Uniqueness / FK invariants need Antidote SQL escrow/strict mode requiring coordination [10], sacrificing availability — Just-Right tradeoff [8].

## 7 Conclusion

We have unified Hybrid Logical Clocks, Dotted Version Vectors, and Cure/AntidoteDB/SwiftCloud into a *planetary-scale causal consistency stack* that is:

- **Physically meaningful**: HLC provides ε-bounded wall-clock snapshots enabling auditing impossible with pure logical time [1][8].
- **Metadata-optimal**: DVV reduces per-key causality from *O(clients)* to *O(replicas)* with provable lower bound [6], crucial for edge with millions of clients.
- **Transactionally safe**: Cure's partitioned commit and stable snapshot protocol delivers atomic multi-key writes and single-round causally consistent RO transactions with near-eventual performance [4].
- **Edge-ready**: SwiftCloud's client-side partial replication and client-assisted failover brings geo-replication to the client machine with 225× read latency improvement and 120ms failover [7].

Future work: TrueTime-tightened ε, Byzantine DVV via commitments, and auto-inference of causal+ vs strict via Just-Right analysis [8]. Causal consistency is a *principled sweet spot*: stronger than eventual, weaker than linearizable, yet available under partitions.
