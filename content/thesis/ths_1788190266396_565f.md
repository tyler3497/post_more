# Homomorphic Secret Sharing for Private Information Retrieval: Function Secret Sharing, Distributed Point Functions, Onion-PIR, and Sublinear Communication via FHE Batch Codes

## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of homomorphic secret sharing for private information retrieval, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of homomorphic secret sharing for private information retrieval and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early homomorphic constructions to state-of-the-art Sharing deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show homomorphic secret sharing for private information retrieval achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(commit - srs.g*value, srs.g2);
    let rhs = pairing(proof, srs.g2_h - srs.g2*point);
    lhs == rhs
}

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Trusted Setup**: OnionPIR: Scaling PIR - S&P 2022 requires SRS generation; although updatable via ceremony, remains trust assumption. Future work explores transparent setups via FRI [7].
