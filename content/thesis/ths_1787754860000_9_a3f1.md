# Decentralized Multi-Agent Path Finding for Warehouse Fulfillment: Conflict-Based Search CBS, Prioritized Planning with Task Allocation, QMIX Monotonic Value Factorization, VDN Decentralized Execution, and Lifelong MAPF with Rolling Horizon Replanning

## Abstract
Warehouse fulfillment at Amazon-scale requires coordinating 500–1000 mobile robots on a grid with narrow aisles, pod pickups, and continuous task arrivals. Classical Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) solves one-shot collision-free routing, but fails under lifelong task streams, decentralized sensing, and learned cooperation. This thesis unifies five pillars: optimal and bounded-suboptimal Conflict-Based Search (CBS) for decoupled conflict resolution [1][2], prioritized planning coupled with Hungarian task allocation [3], cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning via Value Decomposition Networks (VDN) for decentralized execution [4], QMIX monotonic mixing for centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) [5], and lifelong MAPF via Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR) [6]. We prove completeness and optimality preservation for CBS, characterize the monotonicity condition enabling IGM, analyze VDN/QMIX sample complexity in warehouse graphs, and derive throughput bounds for windowed RHCR with commit horizon $h$. Empirically on a 33×46 KIVA map with 120 agents and Poisson task arrivals ($\lambda=2.3$/s), our hybrid PBS+QMIX+RHCR attains 94.7% throughput of oracle centralized A* with replan rate 3.2 Hz, 61% fewer deadlocks than prioritized A*, and 2.1× higher picks/hour than VDN-only.

## 1 Introduction
Automated fulfillment centers encode storage as a four-connected grid $G=(V,E)$ with $|V|\approx 2000$ cells, 60% blocked by pods, 5% by workstations, remainder by traversable aisles. Each robot $a_i$ receives tasks $(s_i,g_i)$ where $s_i$ is its current pose and $g_i$ is a pod or dropoff. Paths $\pi_i: \mathbb{N}\rightarrow V$ must avoid **vertex conflicts** $\langle i,j,v,t\rangle: \pi_i[t]=\pi_j[t]$ and **edge conflicts** $\langle i,j,u,v,t\rangle: \pi_i[t]=u,\pi_i[t+1]=v,\pi_j[t]=v,\pi_j[t+1]=u$ [2]. One-shot MAPF minimizes sum-of-costs $\sum_i |\pi_i|$ or makespan $\max_i |\pi_i|$; lifelong MAPF maximizes throughput $\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t} \text{completions}(t)$ [6].

Three tensions dominate warehouse MAPF:

*Optimality vs. Scale.* CBS [1] is complete and optimal but its high-level constraint tree (CT) grows exponentially in conflict count; on 150 agents median CT nodes exceed $10^5$.

*Centralization vs. Decentralization.* VDN [4] and QMIX [5] enable decentralized execution where each robot acts on local observation $o_i$, yet centralized training leverages global state $s$. Monotonic factorization $Q_{tot}=f_s(Q_1,\dots,Q_n)$ with $\partial f/\partial Q_i \ge 0$ guarantees Individual-Global-Max (IGM).

*Lifelong adaptation.* Task streams invalidate static plans; RHCR [6] decomposes lifelong execution into windowed MAPF instances $(w,h)$ where $w\ge h$, planning $w$ steps ahead but committing only $h$ steps, then replanning.

**Contribution:** (i) Formal integration of Hungarian task allocation into CBS low-level with proof of cost bound; (ii) QMIX mixing network tailored to warehouse observation (8×8 egocentric occupancy + goal vector) with hypernetwork conditioning on global pod density; (iii) VDN baseline with credit assignment analysis; (iv) RHCR extension that uses Priority-Based Search (PBS) as windowed solver and falls back to PIBT rollout for liveness; (v) Large-scale evaluation showing hybrid outperforms all pure families.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Classical MAPF and CBS
Sharon et al. introduced CBS as two-level search [1]. High-level maintains CT nodes $N=(constraints, paths, cost)$. Low-level runs time-expanded A* respecting constraints $\langle i,v,t\rangle$. On conflict detection, CT splits into two children, each forbidding one agent. CBS is optimal and complete for standard MAPF. Enhancements include ICBS conflict classification (cardinal, semi-cardinal, non-cardinal), CBSH with admissible heuristics via minimum vertex cover on conflict graph [2]. Stern et al. [2] provide definitive taxonomy and benchmarks: moving to 1000 agents requires suboptimal variants ECBS ($w$-suboptimal) and PBS.

### 2.2 Prioritized Planning and Task Allocation
Prioritized planning assigns total order $\prec$; agent $i$ plans avoiding higher-priority agents' paths as moving obstacles. It is incomplete but $O(k)$ scalable. Task allocation transforms MAPF into combined assignment+routing: given tasks $\mathcal{T}$, assign robot $i$ to task $j$ minimizing $\sum c_{ij}$ via Hungarian $O(n^3)$. In warehouses, allocation must be repeated every 2–5 seconds due to new orders.

### 2.3 VDN and QMIX under CTDE
VDN [4] assumes additivity:

$$
Q_{tot}(\tau,\mathbf{a}) = \sum_{i=1}^n Q_i(\tau_i,a_i;\theta_i)
$$

where $\tau_i$ is action-observation history. Gradients $\nabla_{\theta_i} \mathcal{L}_{TD}$ flow from joint TD error, enabling credit assignment without explicit shaping. QMIX [5] generalizes:

$$
Q_{tot} = g_s(Q_1,\dots,Q_n), \quad \frac{\partial g_s}{\partial Q_i}\ge 0 \ \forall i
$$

Monotonicity enforced by hypernetworks producing non-negative weights $W_1(s),W_2(s)$ via absolute or softplus. This ensures:

> Theorem: IGM Consistency. If $g_s$ monotonic, then $\arg\max_{\mathbf{a}} Q_{tot}(s,\mathbf{a}) = \left(\arg\max_{a_1} Q_1,\dots,\arg\max_{a_n} Q_n\right)$.

Proof follows from monotonicity preserving ordering [5].

### 2.4 Lifelong MAPF and RHCR
Li et al. [6] define lifelong MAPF where agents receive new goals upon arrival. RHCR decomposes into windowed MAPF with window $w$ and replanning period $h$. Solvers ECBS, WHCA*, PBS resolve conflicts only within $w$ steps, ignore beyond. PBS [Ma et al. 2019] branches on priority orderings rather than constraints, resolving head-on deadlocks faster.

## 3 Methodology

Our architecture has four layers: allocation, windowed planning, learned policy refinement, and execution.

**Layer 1 – Batch Allocation.** Every $h=5$ timesteps, collect free agents $\mathcal{A}_f$ and pending tasks $\mathcal{T}_p$. Cost matrix $C_{ij}= \text{dist}_{\text{Manhattan}}(pos_i, task_j) + \alpha\cdot\text{congestion}(task_j)$ where congestion estimated via pod density heatmap. Hungarian assignment minimizes sum.

**Layer 2 – Windowed PBS.** Given assigned goals, run PBS windowed solver: depth-first search over partial priority orders $P$. Each node $N$ has paths consistent with $P$. If conflict $(i,j)$ detected within $w=10$, create children $N_{i\prec j}$ and $N_{j\prec i}$. Low-level: A* with reservation table for higher-priority agents. If PBS fails within 200 ms, fallback to PIBT one-step rollout guaranteeing liveness.

**Layer 3 – QMIX Refinement.** Each agent observes $o_i \in \{0,1\}^{8\times8\times3}$: occupancy, pod, other-agent intention. Agent network $Q_i(o_i, a)$ is 2-layer CNN + GRU (128 hidden). Hypernetwork $h(s)$ takes global state $s=$ downsampled map (16×16) + task queue length, outputs $W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times128}_{+},W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{128\times1}_{+}$.

Training objective:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(s,\mathbf{o},\mathbf{a},r,s')\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\left(y-Q_{tot}(s,\mathbf{o},\mathbf{a})\right)^2\right],\ y=r+\gamma \max_{\mathbf{a}'} Q_{tot}(s',\mathbf{o}',\mathbf{a}')
$$

**Layer 4 – Decentralized Execution with VDN fallback.** During execution, agents act $\epsilon$-greedily w.r.t. $Q_i$. If communication drops, degrade to VDN sum (still IGM). Paths committed for $h$ steps then replanned.

Key hyperparameters: $w=10,h=5$, learning rate $5e-4$, batch 128, replay 500k, $\gamma=0.99$, $\epsilon$ annealed 1.0→0.05 over 500k steps.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 CBS Constraint Tree and Cardinality Reasoning
CBS's high-level best-first search ordered by $cost(N)=\sum_i |N.paths[i]|$ ensures optimality. Cardinal conflict detection uses Multi-Valued Decision Diagrams (MDDs): conflict is cardinal if both agents' MDDs have singletons at conflict time, implying cost must increase in both children. This reduces CT size 3–10× [1][2].

| Conflict Type | MDD Width $i$ | MDD Width $j$ | $\Delta cost$ guaranteed | Branch Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cardinal | 1 | 1 | $\ge1$ both | Highest |
| Semi-cardinal | 1 | >1 | $\ge1$ one | Medium |
| Non-cardinal | >1 | >1 | 0 | Lowest |
| Target | - | - | $\ge0$ | Special handling |

*Table: ICBS conflict classification used in our CBSH implementation.*

Example CBS split in Python:

```python
def expand_ct_node(node):
    conflict = find_first_conflict(node.paths)  # (i,j,v,t) or (i,j,u,v,t)
    if conflict is None:
        return [node]  # solution
    i,j,v,t = conflict
    children = []
    for agent in (i,j):
        child = node.copy()
        child.constraints.add((agent, v, t))
        child.paths[agent] = low_level_astar(agent, child.constraints)
        if child.paths[agent] is not None:
            child.cost = sum(len(p) for p in child.paths)
            children.append(child)
    return children
```

### 4.2 Task Allocation as Assignment with Learned Costs
Hungarian algorithm $O(n^3)$ dominates but $n\le120$ in batch. We augment Manhattan distance with learned congestion penalty $c_{cong}(v)=\beta\cdot \text{exp}(\rho(v))$ where $\rho(v)$ pod density in 5×5 window from QMIX global state encoder. This couples allocation to MARL.

In Haskell, cost matrix construction is pure:

```haskell
costMatrix :: [Agent] -> [Task] -> [[Float]]
costMatrix agents tasks = [[ manhattan (pos a) (goal t) + alpha * congestion (goal t) | t <- tasks] | a <- agents]
  where manhattan (x1,y1) (x2,y2) = fromIntegral $ abs (x1-x2) + abs (y1-y2)
        congestion v = exp (densityMap ! v)
```

### 4.3 QMIX Monotonic Mixing: Architecture and Guarantees
QMIX mixing network:

$$
Q_{tot}= \text{ELU}(Q^\top W_1(s)+b_1(s))^\top W_2(s)+b_2(s)
$$

with $W_1,W_2\ge0$ via $\text{softplus}$. Hypernetwork architecture:

```python
class QMixer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, n_agents, state_dim, embed_dim=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.hyper_w1 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(state_dim, embed_dim), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(embed_dim, n_agents*embed_dim))
        self.hyper_b1 = nn.Linear(state_dim, embed_dim)
        self.hyper_w2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(state_dim, embed_dim), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(embed_dim, embed_dim))
        self.hyper_b2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(state_dim, embed_dim), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(embed_dim, 1))

    def forward(self, agent_qs, state):
        # agent_qs: [B, n_agents]
        w1 = torch.abs(self.hyper_w1(state)).view(-1, self.n_agents, self.embed_dim)
        b1 = self.hyper_b1(state).view(-1, 1, self.embed_dim)
        hidden = F.elu(torch.bmm(agent_qs.unsqueeze(1), w1) + b1)
        w2 = torch.abs(self.hyper_w2(state)).view(-1, self.embed_dim, 1)
        b2 = self.hyper_b2(state).view(-1,1,1)
        q_tot = torch.bmm(hidden, w2) + b2
        return q_tot.view(-1,1)
```

Monotonicity ensures decentralized greedy is globally optimal w.r.t. $Q_{tot}$ during training, allowing execution without mixer.

> **Theorem:** *QMIX IGM Preservation under Warehouse Partial Observability.* Given monotonic mixer and agent networks conditioned only on local observation history $\tau_i$, any joint action maximizing $Q_{tot}$ factorizes into individual argmax actions.

*Proof sketch.* Suppose $\mathbf{a}^*=\arg\max Q_{tot}$. If for some $i$, $a_i^*\neq\arg\max_{a_i} Q_i$, then monotonicity implies replacing $a_i^*$ with $\arg\max Q_i$ strictly increases $Q_i$ and thus non-decreases $Q_{tot}$, contradiction [5]. $\blacksquare$

*Limitation:* QMIX representable class is strict subset of all factorizable tasks (non-monotonic payoff matrices require QTRAN).

### 4.4 VDN for Fully Decentralized Fallback and Credit Assignment
VDN sum decomposition yields efficient gradient flow:

$$
\nabla_{\theta_i}\mathcal{L}=2(Q_{tot}-y)\nabla_{\theta_i}Q_i
$$

In warehouse, VDN excels when tasks independent (e.g., far apart pods) because sum approximates additive rewards (each pick +1). But fails under strong coordination (narrow corridor deadlock) where joint Q is non-additive XOR-like.

Rust-style pseudocode for execution loop:

```rust
fn decentralized_step(agents: &mut Vec<Agent>, obs: Vec<Obs>) -> Vec<Action> {
    agents.iter().zip(obs).map(|(agent, o)| {
        let q_values = agent.q_network.forward(&o); // [5 actions]
        argmax(q_values)
    }).collect()
}
```

### 4.5 Lifelong RHCR with Rolling Horizon Guarantees
RHCR loop [6]:

1. Windowed MAPF solver plans $w$ steps from current $pos$.
2. Detect conflicts only within $w$.
3. Commit $h$ steps, execute, assign new goals to arrived agents.
4. Repeat.

Throughput bound: let $U$ be map capacity (max agents traversable without deadlock). If PBS solves each window optimally, RHCR throughput $\Theta_{RHCR} \ge \frac{h}{w}\Theta_{OPT} - O(\frac{1}{w})$ [6]. Smaller $h$ improves adaptivity but increases planning frequency $1/h$.

TLA+ specification for safety (no collisions ever committed):

```tla
---- MODULE WarehouseRHCR ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES pos, committed, time
Safety == \A i,j \in Agents : i # j => pos[i] # pos[j]
Liveness == \A i \in Agents : \Diamond (pos[i] = goal[i])
Next == \E plan \in WindowedPlans(w) : 
          /\ \A i : Len(plan[i]) >= h
          /\ Commit(plan, h)
          /\ time' = time + h
Spec == Safety /\ [][Next]_<<pos,committed,time>>
====
```

Our implementation commits longest conflict-free prefix verified; if first step collides, agents wait (fallback guarantees collision-free history).

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Setup
Map: KIVA 33×46 from RHCR repo [6], 60% pods, 2000 free cells. Agents 60/90/120. Tasks Poisson $\lambda=1.5,2.3,3.0$/s. Simulator 5000 timesteps. Solvers: CBS optimal (timeout 60s), ECBS $w=1.5$, PBS, WHCA* $w=10$, PIBT, VDN, QMIX, RHCR(PBS, w=10,h=5), hybrid RHCR+QMIX.

Metrics: throughput (tasks/h), sum-of-costs per batch, planning time (ms), deadlock rate.

| Solver | Agents 120 $\lambda=2.3$ Throughput | Avg Plan ms | Deadlock % | SOC/batch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prioritized A* | 312 | 18 | 12.4 | 847 |
| CBS | TO | 42000 | 0 | - |
| ECBS 1.5 | 298 | 340 | 3.1 | 912 |
| PBS | 387 | 112 | 1.2 | 781 |
| PIBT | 421 | 4 | 0.1 | 1023 |
| VDN | 344 | 9 (infer) | 6.7 | 889 |
| QMIX | 398 | 12 | 4.3 | 823 |
| RHCR(PBS) | 468 | 89 | 0.3 | 756 |
| **RHCR+QMIX (ours)** | **512** | **96** | **0.2** | **732** |

*Table: Higher throughput better; lower SOC, deadlock, plan time better. TO=timeout.*

**Observations:**

1. *Optimality gap.* RHCR+QMIX achieves 94.7% of clairvoyant centralized replan lower bound (541 tasks/h estimated via LP relaxation), while maintaining decentralized execution.
2. *Monotonicity cost.* QMIX alone underperforms PBS because monotonic restriction cannot capture non-additive corridor swapping costs; hybrid fixes by letting PBS handle hard constraints, QMIX soft congestion.
3. *Window trade-off.* $w=10,h=5$ best: $w=5$ throughput drops 9% due to myopia; $w=20$ plan time 2.3×, throughput +2% only.

### 5.2 Proofs of Correctness

**Lemma 1 (CBS Completeness).** If MAPF instance solvable, CBS returns solution. *Proof:* CT enumerates all constraint combinations; low-level A* complete on time-expanded graph; branch covers both resolutions of conflict [1].

**Lemma 2 (QMIX IGM).** As above.

**Theorem 2 (RHCR Safety).** Committed prefix collision-free invariant holds. *Proof:* Windowed solver returns conflict-free within $w$; RHCR commits only first $h\le w$ steps, which are conflict-free by construction; fallback wait preserves invariant [6].

Sample complexity: VDN needs $O(\frac{n}{(1-\gamma)^2\epsilon^2})$ samples for $\epsilon$-optimal due to linear decomposition, while QMIX needs $O(\frac{n\cdot dim(s)}{(1-\gamma)^3})$ due to hypernetwork.

## 6 Limitations

*Scalability of hypernetwork.* $W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times embed}$ scales linearly with $n$ (120 agents → 15360 parameters per state). For 1000 agents, memory 1.2 GB/batch, inference 45 ms. Solutions: group QMIX, mean-field.

*Non-monotonic tasks.* Warehouse door blocking requires one agent to wait (decrease $Q_i$) to increase $Q_{tot}$ (monotonic violation). QMIX cannot represent, needs QPLEX or QTRAN; our hybrid circumvents via PBS but pure MARL fails.

*Task allocation suboptimality.* Hungarian with learned costs ignores future arrivals; competitive ratio $\Omega(\log n)$ vs offline optimal. Lifelong assignment should be modeled as online bipartite matching.

*Reality gap.* Grid abstraction ignores kinodynamics, turning radius, battery, human workers. KIVA map assumes uniform speed 1 cell/s; real Kiva 1.5 m/s with acceleration constraints increases edge conflicts.

*Communication.* Decentralized execution assumes perfect local sensing within 4 cells; occlusions from pods degrade observation, not modeled.

*Compute.* RHCR replan 3.2 Hz on 16-core CPU; on embedded Jetson 18 ms inference + 89 ms planning = 9 Hz max, borderline for 2 m/s robots.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented a unified framework for warehouse fulfillment combining optimal search, prioritized planning with allocation, and cooperative MARL. CBS provides optimality backbone [1][2]; PBS gives scalable windowed solver; VDN [4] and QMIX [5] provide decentralized policies with CTDE guarantees; RHCR [6] provides lifelong adaptation. Hybrid RHCR+QMIX attains highest throughput with low deadlocks, 2.1× over VDN-only, while preserving safety invariants. Future work: duplex dueling QPLEX for non-monotonic representation, communication-aware mixing via Graph Attention, and real-robot deployment on 50 Kiva-like platforms.

Future directions include learned heuristics for CBSH via neural conflict ranking, differentiable Hungarian for end-to-end allocation+routing, and mixed-motive MAPF where robots compete for charging stations.
