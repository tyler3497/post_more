# Derived Algebraic Geometry: Simplicial Commutative Rings, the Cotangent Complex, and Derived Moduli Stacks

## Abstract

Classical algebraic geometry studies solution sets of polynomial equations, but its foundations crack at the first encounter with non-transverse intersections: the intersection multiplicity is lost, deformation theory becomes obstructed, and moduli problems refuse to be representable. Derived algebraic geometry, pioneered by Grothendieck's school and brought to maturity by Lurie, Toën, and Vezzosi, resolves these defects by replacing commutative rings with simplicial commutative rings — equivalently, connective $E_\infty$-algebras — and schemes with derived schemes. The key invariant is the cotangent complex, which simultaneously encodes Kähler differentials, deformation obstructions, and derived intersection multiplicities. This article develops the foundations: simplicial commutative algebra and its model structures, the derived tensor product and Tor-spectral sequence, the cotangent complex and its role in obstruction theory, and the construction of derived moduli stacks via the functor of points. We prove that derived fiber products recover Serre's intersection formula, explain how shifted symplectic structures on derived stacks underlie modern enumerative geometry, and survey applications to deformation quantization, the geometric Langlands program, and topological field theory.

## 1 Introduction

Algebraic geometry begins with a simple idea: study the common zeros of polynomials. Yet this innocent starting point conceals a structural defect. Consider two curves in the plane intersecting non-transversely — tangent at a point. Classical scheme theory records the intersection as a scheme, but the *multiplicity* of the intersection, the infinitesimal information encoding how the curves meet, is only partially captured. Worse, when one tries to deform the intersection or form fiber products of non-transverse maps, the classical theory produces answers that are visibly wrong: dimensions jump, flatness fails, and obstruction classes appear from nowhere.

> **Principle:** Non-transverse intersections carry hidden homological information. Derived algebraic geometry makes this information visible by remembering the full derived tensor product, not merely its zeroth homology.

The remedy, envisioned by Grothendieck and developed by Illusie [1], Quillen [2], and in modern form by Lurie [3] and Toën–Vezzosi [4], is to work *derived*: replace commutative rings by **simplicial commutative rings**, modules by chain complexes, and the tensor product by its derived functor. A derived scheme is locally modeled on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ for $A$ a simplicial commutative ring; its underlying classical scheme is $\operatorname{Spec} \pi_0(A)$, but the higher homotopy groups $\pi_i(A)$ retain the intersection multiplicities and deformation data that classical geometry discards.

This article is a systematic introduction. We begin with simplicial commutative algebra, develop the cotangent complex as the universal linearization invariant, prove the derived intersection formula, and construct derived moduli stacks. We conclude with the theory of shifted symplectic structures [5] — the derived-geometric engine behind Donaldson–Thomas theory and much of modern enumerative geometry.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Simplicial commutative rings

A **simplicial commutative ring** is a simplicial object in the category of commutative rings: a sequence of rings $A_0, A_1, \ldots$ with face and degeneracy maps satisfying the simplicial identities, such that each $A_n$ is commutative and all maps are ring homomorphisms. The homotopy groups $\pi_*(A)$ form a graded-commutative ring, with $\pi_0(A)$ an ordinary commutative ring.

The category $\mathbf{sCRing}$ of simplicial commutative rings carries a **model structure** transferred from simplicial sets: a map is a weak equivalence if it induces isomorphisms on all $\pi_i$, a fibration if it is surjective in positive degrees. Cofibrant replacements are given by free simplicial resolutions — the derived analogue of projective resolutions. The homotopy category $\operatorname{Ho}(\mathbf{sCRing})$ is equivalent to the $\infty$-category of connective $E_\infty$-algebras over $\mathbb{Z}$ [3].

The crucial operation is the **derived tensor product**: for maps $A \to B$ and $A \to C$ of simplicial rings, $B \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_A C$ is computed by replacing one factor cofibrantly. Its homotopy groups are the classical $\operatorname{Tor}$ groups:
$$\pi_i(B \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_A C) \cong \operatorname{Tor}^A_i(B, C).$$
There is a convergent **Tor spectral sequence** $E^2_{p,q} = \operatorname{Tor}^{\pi_*(A)}_{p,q}(\pi_*(B), \pi_*(C)) \Rightarrow \pi_{p+q}(B \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_A C)$, the computational workhorse of the subject [2].

### 2.2 The cotangent complex

For a map $A \to B$ of ordinary rings, the **cotangent complex** $\mathbb{L}_{B/A}$ is a complex of $B$-modules, well-defined up to quasi-isomorphism, with $H_0(\mathbb{L}_{B/A}) \cong \Omega_{B/A}$ the Kähler differentials and $H_{-1}$ measuring obstructions [1]. For simplicial rings, the cotangent complex is defined via the same universal property: it corepresents derivations into square-zero extensions.

The cotangent complex governs **deformation theory**: for a derived scheme $X$ and a square-zero extension $A \to A'$ with kernel $I$, lifts of a map $\operatorname{Spec} A \to X$ to $\operatorname{Spec} A'$ are controlled by $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb{L}_X, I)$, with obstructions in $\operatorname{Ext}^2$. This is the derived upgrade of the classical Kodaira–Spencer formalism, and it works *without* transversality hypotheses — the reason derived moduli problems are better behaved than classical ones.

### 2.3 Derived schemes and stacks

A **derived scheme** is a pair $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ where $X$ is a topological space and $\mathcal{O}_X$ is a sheaf of simplicial commutative rings, locally equivalent to $\operatorname{Spec} A$ for $A \in \mathbf{sCRing}$, with the truncation $\pi_0(\mathcal{O}_X)$ giving an ordinary scheme $t_0(X)$. Morphisms are maps of ringed spaces respecting the simplicial structure.

More generally, a **derived stack** is a functor $F: \mathbf{sCRing}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{S}$ from simplicial rings to spaces (simplicial sets), satisfying étale descent. The **functor of points** philosophy — a geometric object is determined by its $A$-points for all $A$ — becomes fully effective here: derived moduli functors that fail to be sheaves classically become representable derived stacks [4].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Computing with the Tor spectral sequence

The primary computational tool is the Tor spectral sequence. Given a derived fiber product $X \times^h_Z Y$ of derived schemes, its structure sheaf has homotopy groups computed by
$$E^2_{p,q} = \operatorname{Tor}^{\pi_*( \mathcal{O}_Z)}_{p,q}(\pi_*(\mathcal{O}_X), \pi_*(\mathcal{O}_Y)) \Rightarrow \pi_{p+q}(\mathcal{O}_{X \times^h_Z Y}).$$
In practice, one resolves the maps by Koszul-type complexes: if $Z = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $X, Y$ are cut out by regular sequences, the derived tensor product is the total complex of a double Koszul complex, and the spectral sequence collapses at $E^2$.

```python
# Pseudocode: derived tensor product via free simplicial resolution
def derived_tensor(A, B, C, f, g):
    """Compute B ⊗^L_A C using a cofibrant replacement of B."""
    B_cof = free_simplicial_resolution(B, over=A, via=f)  # levelwise polynomial
    total = total_complex(B_cof.tensor_over_A(C, via=g))
    homology = [ homology_group(total, i) for i in range(dim_bound) ]
    return homology  # pi_i of the derived tensor product = Tor^A_i(B, C)
```

### 3.2 Obstruction theory via the cotangent complex

To show a derived moduli functor $F$ is representable, one verifies Artin's criteria in derived form [6]: $F$ must be a sheaf for the étale topology, locally of finite presentation, with a well-behaved deformation theory governed by a (perfect) cotangent complex, and effective formal versality. The key simplification over classical Artin representability is that the cotangent complex *exists* as a genuine object, so obstruction classes live in honest cohomology groups rather than ad hoc constructions.

### 3.3 Shifted symplectic structures

A **$n$-shifted symplectic structure** on a derived stack $X$ is a closed 2-form $\omega$ of degree $n$ on $X$ that induces an equivalence $\mathbb{T}_X \simeq \mathbb{L}_X[n]$ between the tangent and shifted cotangent complexes [5]. The fundamental existence theorem states that derived moduli stacks of objects in a Calabi–Yau category carry canonical shifted symplectic structures: the derived stack of perfect complexes carries a 2-shifted symplectic form, and derived mapping stacks inherit shifts by AKSZ transgression. This is the geometric origin of the symmetric obstruction theories underlying Donaldson–Thomas invariants [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Serre's intersection formula, derived

Let $X, Y \subset Z$ be closed subschemes of a smooth variety, with structure sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_Y$. Serre defined the intersection multiplicity as the alternating sum
$$i(X, Y) = \sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{length}\, \operatorname{Tor}^{\mathcal{O}_Z}_i(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_Y),$$
and conjectured its positivity and vanishing properties [8]. In derived geometry, this formula is not a definition but a *theorem*: the derived fiber product $X \times^h_Z Y$ is a derived scheme with $\pi_i(\mathcal{O}_{X \times^h_Z Y}) = \operatorname{Tor}^{\mathcal{O}_Z}_i(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_Y)$, and Serre's alternating sum is simply the Euler characteristic of the structure sheaf of the derived intersection. The derived perspective thus *explains* Serre's formula: the Tor groups were always the homotopy groups of a geometric object, and classical geometry could only see their Euler characteristic.

> **Theorem (Derived intersection formula):** For closed immersions of derived schemes, the derived fiber product has structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{X \times^h_Z Y} \simeq \mathcal{O}_X \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_Z} \mathcal{O}_Y$, and its Euler characteristic recovers Serre's intersection multiplicity.

### 4.2 Derived moduli of stable maps and virtual classes

The moduli space $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)$ of stable maps is classically singular and of the wrong dimension; its virtual fundamental class is constructed via perfect obstruction theories [9]. In derived geometry, there is a **derived moduli stack** $\mathbf{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)$ whose truncation is the classical stack and whose cotangent complex *is* the obstruction theory [10]. The virtual class is then the fundamental class of the derived stack in a suitable homology theory. This construction is functorial and requires no choices — the derived stack remembers everything.

### 4.3 The derived loop space and Hochschild homology

For a derived scheme $X$, the **derived loop space** $\mathcal{L}X = X \times^h_{X \times X} X$ (the derived self-intersection of the diagonal) has functions given by Hochschild homology: $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{L}X) \simeq HH_*(\mathcal{O}_X)$ [11]. The $S^1$-action on $\mathcal{L}X$ by loop rotation induces the Connes differential, recovering cyclic homology. This identification is the starting point for the derived-geometric approach to the Hodge conjecture via noncommutative motives and for Blanc's topological $K$-theory of dg-categories.

### 4.4 Deformation quantization via formality

Kontsevich's formality theorem — every Poisson manifold admits a deformation quantization — is naturally a statement in derived geometry: the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg quasi-isomorphism identifies polyvector fields with Hochschild cohomology as $L_\infty$-algebras, and Maurer–Cartan elements quantize [12]. The derived moduli stack of quantizations carries a rich homotopy-theoretic structure invisible classically, and the globalization to arbitrary smooth varieties uses precisely the cotangent-complex machinery developed above.

### 4.5 Shifted Poisson structures and coisotropic reduction

A **$n$-shifted Poisson structure** is a Maurer–Cartan element in the weight-graded polyvector fields, generalizing ordinary Poisson bivectors [13]. The key theorem — that $n$-shifted Poisson structures on $X$ correspond to $(n+1)$-shifted symplectic structures on the formal neighborhood of the diagonal — unifies Poisson geometry with the AKSZ formalism. **Coisotropic reduction** in the shifted setting produces derived symplectic quotients, with applications to character varieties: the derived moduli of $G$-local systems on a surface carries a 0-shifted symplectic structure, and its quantization is the skein algebra [14].

## 5 Empirical and Computational Aspects

While derived algebraic geometry is largely theoretical, computational tools are emerging. The Tor spectral sequence can be implemented in Macaulay2 for graded rings, computing $\pi_*$ of derived tensor products in low degrees. For derived intersections of explicit complete intersections, the Koszul–Tate resolution gives a finite model suitable for computer algebra:

```haskell
-- Koszul-Tate resolution of a derived zero locus (schematic)
koszulTate :: Ring -> [Poly] -> Complex
koszulTate r eqs = totalComplex
  [ exteriorAlgebra (map (shift 1) eqs)  -- generators in homological degree 1
  , differential = contractionWith eqs  -- d(e_i) = f_i
  ]
-- H_0 = r/(eqs); H_{-i} = higher Tor = hidden intersection data
```

Software for $\infty$-categories remains nascent, but the **spectral sequence** computations and **obstruction class** evaluations are increasingly automated, with applications to explicit enumerative predictions (e.g., computing Donaldson–Thomas invariants of local curves via the topological vertex).

## 6 Limitations

- **Foundational overhead:** The $\infty$-categorical foundations [3] require substantial background; the subject remains inaccessible without fluency in homotopy theory.
- **Computability:** Beyond complete intersections and toric cases, explicit computations of derived tensor products are infeasible; most results are structural.
- **Comparison with classical theory:** Translating derived results back into classical statements (e.g., actual numbers from virtual classes) still requires delicate analysis.
- **Positive characteristic:** The theory of simplicial commutative rings in characteristic $p$ interacts subtly with divided powers; the prismatic refinement [15] is still being integrated.

## 7 Conclusion

Derived algebraic geometry is not a generalization for its own sake: it is the recognition that classical algebraic geometry was always computing Euler characteristics of richer homotopical objects. The cotangent complex, the derived tensor product, and shifted symplectic structures are not exotic decorations but the native language of intersection theory, deformation theory, and enumerative geometry. As the subject matures — with derived moduli stacks now routine in enumerative geometry and the Langlands program — the lesson is clear: whenever a classical construction produces obstructions, multiplicities, or virtual classes, there is a derived object whose ordinary invariants they are. The future lies in making this dictionary computational and in extending it to arithmetic and analytic settings.
