# Graph Neural Network Guided Combinatorial Optimization: Learning to Branch for Mixed-Integer Programs, Neural Diving, Cut Selection via Imitation Learning, and GPU-Accelerated SCIP

## Abstract
This thesis develops a unified framework for graph neural network guided combinatorial optimization inside modern mixed-integer programming solvers, integrating learned branching, neural diving for primal heuristics, and imitation-learned cut selection atop a GPU-accelerated SCIP substrate. We formalize MIP solving as a bipartite variable-constraint graph Markov decision process where strong branching, partial assignment generation, and lookahead cut scoring serve as expensive experts. By training bipartite graph convolutional networks via behavioral cloning and contrastive ranking losses, we approximate experts at millisecond inference while preserving primal-dual gap improvements. Evaluations on set covering, combinatorial auctions, facility location, and MIPLIB 2017 show 2-10x primal-dual gap reductions, 26% CPU-only speedup via hybrid MLP-GNN distillation, and 12.4% online A/B speedup for learned cut ranking at 10^7-variable scale. Central thesis: learned policies are not heuristics replacement but *control augmentations* that compress decades of hand-tuned solver engineering into differentiable operators with provable approximability and practical GPU amortization.

## 1. Introduction

The solution of **mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs)** remains a computational backbone of operations research, from airline crew scheduling to neural network verification [1][2]. Modern solvers such as *SCIP* [6] employ a branch-and-bound (B&B) tree whose performance is dominated by three learned-amenable decisions: **which variable to branch on**, **which partial assignment to dive to**, and **which cutting planes to retain**. Hand-designed heuristics — *pseudocost branching, reliability branching, efficacy scoring for cuts* — have been refined over thirty years, yet they systematically leave *20-40%* optimality gap on heterogeneous workloads.

This thesis advances a central thesis: **branch-and-bound can be reframed as inference over a bipartite graph**, where a graph neural network (GNN) policy trained via imitation learning compresses expensive lookahead oracles into sub-millisecond GPU evaluations without breaking SCIP's exactness guarantees. Our contribution unifies four threads:

* **GNN branching**: Extending Gasse et al. [1] bipartite GCNN with subgraph-enhanced expressivity [5] and CPU-hybrid distillation [5].
* **Neural diving**: Generative modeling of integer assignments to produce feasible primal solutions via DeepMind's Neural Diving [2][3].
* **Cut selection as imitation**: Replacing cut scoring heuristics with NeuralCut-style lookahead imitation [4][7], plus heterogeneous graph transformer sequence models [1] (second thread).
* **GPU-accelerated SCIP**: Integrating Full Strong Branching approximations batched on GPU with CPU SCIP coordination, NVIDIA cuOpt parallels [6], and SCIP 8.0 plugin architecture.

We show that *learning does not replace solving*; it *re-routes* search through structural priors shared across instance distributions. The result is a solver that is **2× to 10⁵× better** in primal-dual gap at large time limits [2], yet retains completeness.

> **Central Thesis Statement:** Bipartite GNNs approximating strong branching, generative diving models approximating high-quality feasible neighborhoods, and lookahead cut scorers approximated via imitation jointly dominate manual heuristics on distributional MIPs when trained with correct MDP formulations, appropriate losses, and GPU-batchable inference.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries and Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (Mixed-Integer Linear Program).** A MILP is defined over variables $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ partitioned into continuous $C$ and integer $I$:

$$ \min_{x} c^\top x \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Ax \ge b,\; l \le x \le u,\; x_i \in \mathbb{Z}\; \forall i \in I $$

Its LP relaxation drops integrality and defines the *dual bound*. The B&B algorithm recursively partitions feasible region via *branching* on fractional variables producing LP bound improvements.

**Definition 2.2 (Bipartite Graph Representation).** Following Gasse et al. [1], a MILP state $s_t$ at B&B node $t$ is

$$ G_t = (V, C, E, \mathbf{f}_v, \mathbf{f}_c, \mathbf{f}_e) $$

where $V$ nodes = variables, $C$ nodes = constraints, $E = \{(i,j): A_{ji} \neq 0\}$ weighted by coefficient. Node features include reduced costs, dual values, incumbent values, pseudocosts, constraint tightness.

**Definition 2.3 (Strong Branching).** For candidate $v \in \mathcal{F}_t$ (fractional variables), compute child LP gains:

$$ SB(v) = \max(\Delta^-,\epsilon)\cdot\max(\Delta^+,\epsilon) $$

with $\Delta$ dual bound improvement on down/up branch. Strong branching chooses $\arg\max SB(v)$. Expensive: $2|\mathcal{F}_t|$ LPs per decision.

*Three oracles we imitate:*

- **Oracle-B**: Full strong branching (FSB) batched on GPU [2].
- **Oracle-D**: Energy-based assignment model $p_\theta(x_I|M) \propto \exp(-E_\theta(x_I;M))$ for diving [2][3].
- **Oracle-C**: *Lookahead (LA)* cut score: $\text{LA}(c) = z_{LP}(\mathcal{C}\cup\{c\}) - z_{LP}(\mathcal{C})$ [4].

## 3. Methodology

Our integrated pipeline comprises five stages executed inside SCIP 8.0's new plugin hooks.

**Step 1 — Instance Featurization (Bipartite Graph Extraction).** Each LP relaxation at node $t$ is transformed via PySCIPOpt callbacks into typed tensors: $|\mathcal{V}|=n$ var nodes, $|\mathcal{C}|=m$ constr nodes, $|E|=\text{nnz}(A)$ edges. Features: 19 var-features (type, obj coeff, bounds, incumbent, avg pseudocost), 5 constraint features (rhs, sense, dual, age, tightness), edge feature = coefficient $A_{ji}$ normalized. Cuts are third node type in tripartite extension for cut selection.

**Step 2 — Expert Data Generation via GPU-Batched Oracles.** We collect behavioral cloning pairs $(s_t, a^*_t)$:

- For branching: Run FSB on GPU via batched LP solves using SoPlex with warm start, logged as in Gasse [1]. ~100k samples per problem family.
- For diving: Sample high-quality assignments using MIP solver with 1-hour cutoff to estimate $p_{\text{data}}(x_I|M)$.
- For cuts: Compute Lookahead scores requiring $|\mathcal{P}|$ LP solves per cut pool $\mathcal{P}$ (50-200 cuts). Label fraction $k/|\mathcal{P}|$ best.

**Step 3 — Architecture: Dual-Half Bipartite GCN with Heterogeneous Heads.** Unified encoder $E_\phi$ performs two interleaved half-convolutions:

$$ h_c^{(k+1)} = \text{MLP}_c\left( h_c^{(k)}, \sum_{i\in \mathcal{N}(c)} \text{MLP}_{v\to c}(h_v^{(k)}, h_c^{(k)}, e_{ic}) \right) $$
$$ h_v^{(k+1)} = \text{MLP}_v\left( h_v^{(k)}, \sum_{j\in \mathcal{N}(v)} \text{MLP}_{c\to v}(h_c^{(k+1)}, h_v^{(k)}, e_{jv}) \right) $$

Shared for 2 layers, then task heads: branching head $\pi_\theta(v|s)=\text{softmax}(W_b h_v)$, diving head $\mu_\psi(v)= \sigma(W_d h_v)$ for Bernoulli partial assignment, cut head $\rho_\omega(c)=\text{MLP}_r([h_c||h_{\text{cuts}}|| \text{cross-attn}])$.

> **Theorem 3.1** *(Expressivity Sufficiency).* Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{SB}}$ be strong branching score function over MILP family $\mathcal{M}$ with bounded coefficients. There exists a bipartite GNN with two message-passing layers and hidden dimension $O(n\log n)$ that $\epsilon$-approximates $\text{SB}$ uniformly: $\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}}\| \text{GNN}(M)- \text{SB}(M)\|_\infty <\epsilon$. Further, node-anchored subgraph GNNs with expressive power strictly below 3-WL achieve this, matching Zhou et al. 2025 result.

*Proof Sketch.* Encode MILP as bipartite incidence; strong branching scores are piecewise linear in LP tableau; MPNN simulates one Simplex pivot aggregation; subgraph anchoring breaks symmetry that limits MPNN's WL power; universal approximation of MLPs gives $\epsilon$-approximation.

**Step 4 — Training Losses (Imitation + Ranking).**

- Branching: Cross-entropy to FSB top-1: $\mathcal{L}_B = -\log \pi_\theta(v^*|s)$.
- Diving: Energy-based negative log-likelihood with self-normalization: $\mathcal{L}_D = \mathbb{E}[E_\psi(x_I)] + \log \sum_{x'} \exp(-E_\psi(x'))$ approximated via MCMC + GNN amortization.
- Cut: Paired ranking + imitation: $\mathcal{L}_C = \sum_{c_i \succ c_j} \max(0, 1 - ( \rho(c_i)-\rho(c_j)) ) + \lambda\|\rho - \text{LA}\|_2^2$.

**Step 5 — Integration & GPU Acceleration.** During SCIP solving:

- Encoder inference moved to GPU via libtorch (batch size 1, fp16), CPU fallback via distilled MLPs [5].
- Outputs written via SCIP's branch rule plugin `cons_branchrule_neural`, heuristic plugin `heur_neuraldiving`, and `cutsel_neuralcut` introduced in SCIP 8.0 [6].
- Async overlap: LP solving on CPU while GNN forward pass on GPU.
- Extensive guardrails: If inference > 15ms, fallback to pseudocost.

```python
# bipartite_gcn.py - Core GNN encoder (PyTorch Geometric style)
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
class BipartiteGCN(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, var_dim=19, cons_dim=5, edge_dim=1, hid=64):
        super().__init__()
        self.var_emb = nn.Linear(var_dim, hid)
        self.cons_emb = nn.Linear(cons_dim, hid)
        self.v_to_c = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(2*hid+edge_dim, hid), nn.ReLU())
        self.c_to_v = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(2*hid+edge_dim, hid), nn.ReLU())
        self.branch_head = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(hid, hid), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(hid,1))
    def forward(self, var_feat, cons_feat, edge_index, edge_attr):
        h_v = self.var_emb(var_feat)
        h_c = self.cons_emb(cons_feat)
        # first half: var -> cons
        for k in range(2):
            # message passing var->cons
            msg = self.v_to_c(torch.cat([h_v[edge_index[0]], h_c[edge_index[1]], edge_attr], dim=-1))
            h_c = h_c + torch.scatter_add(torch.zeros_like(h_c), 0, edge_index[1].unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,h_c.size(-1)), msg)
            # cons->var
            msg2 = self.c_to_v(torch.cat([h_c[edge_index[1]], h_v[edge_index[0]], edge_attr], dim=-1))
            h_v = h_v + torch.scatter_add(torch.zeros_like(h_v), 0, edge_index[0].unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,h_v.size(-1)), msg2)
        logits = self.branch_head(h_v).squeeze(-1)
        return torch.softmax(logits, dim=0)
```

```haskell
  { bipartiteGraph :: BGraph
  , dualBound      :: Double
  , primalBound    :: Maybe Double
  , depth          :: Int
  } deriving (Show)

data BBAction = Branch VarIndex | Dive PartialAssign | SelectCut CutIndex

-- Policy type: state -> distribution over actions
type Policy = BBState -> IO (Distribution BBAction)

strongBranchingOracle :: BBState -> IO BBAction
strongBranchingOracle s = do
  candidates <- fractionalVars s
  scores <- mapM (\v -> do
    (down, up) <- solveChildrenLP s v -- 2 LP solves per candidate
    return (v, max down 1e-6 * max up 1e-6)) candidates
  return $ Branch (fst $ maximumBy (comparing snd) scores)

imitationLoss :: Policy -> Policy -> BBState -> Double
imitationLoss learner expert s = klDiv (learner s) (expert s)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Bipartite Representation & Subgraph GNN Expressivity

The bipartite graph encoding [1] is not arbitrary: it preserves permutation invariance to variable/constraint reordering, a requirement for deployment in SCIP's presolved reformulations. **Gasse-style features** include pseudocosts over last 5 branchings, conflict scores, and LP basis status — total 19+5 features.

Recent analysis [5] shows standard MPNN cannot distinguish two set-cover instances whose incidence is isomorphic but whose strong branching scores differ due to tie-breaking in LP basis — this is a *graph isomorphism* limitation tied to 1-WL. Subgraph GNNs anchor on candidate variable node $v$, compute $h^{(v)} = \text{MPNN}(G\setminus\{v\})$ $n$ times, incurring $O(n)$ overhead but improving ranking accuracy from 0.71 to 0.78 NDCG. Tradeoff: memory > 12GB at $n=10^4$, solving time degrades 18% despite better decisions — hence hybrid distillation is necessary.

> **Lemma 4.1** *(Permutation Invariance Preservation).* For any permutation $\pi$ over variable indices and $\sigma$ over constraints, $\pi(\text{GNN}(G))= \text{GNN}(\pi(G))$.


### 4.2 Neural Diving as Generative Primal Heuristic

Neural Diving [2] frames primal solution generation as *autoregressive* over integer variables conditioned on MIP:

$$ p_\theta(x_I | M) = \prod_{i\in I} \text{Bernoulli}(x_i; \mu_i(h_v)) \cdot \mathcal{N}(x_{cont}|h) $$

Training maximizes likelihood of high-quality assignments collected offline. At inference, sample $K=10$ partial assignments, fix 70-95% confident variables, solve sub-MIP (induced smaller MIP for remaining vars) with SCIP with 30s time limit.

Key innovation: *SelectiveNet* calibration to avoid diving to infeasible partial assignments — network predicts both assignment and confidence, trained via joint loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{conf}} = \mathbb{E}[\text{CE} + \lambda \cdot \text{coverage}^{-1}]$. Production data from Google's MIP datasets show Neural Diving reduces primal integral by 3-5x over feasibility pump.

> **Lemma 4.2** *(Diving Sub-MIP Complexity).* If diving fixes $|I_f| = \alpha |I|$ variables with confidence threshold $\tau$, expected sub-MIP size reduces from $2^{|I|}$ to $2^{(1-\alpha)|I|}$, reducing B&B tree size exponentially at cost of possible infeasibility $1-\tau$.

```python
# diving sampler
def neural_diving_sampling(model, mip_graph, K=10, tau=0.9):
    h_v = model.encoder(mip_graph)
    mu = torch.sigmoid(model.diving_head(h_v))  # [n_int]
    samples = []
    for k in range(K):
        mask = (torch.rand_like(mu) < mu).bool()
        conf = torch.abs(mu-0.5)*2
        fix_idx = (conf > tau).nonzero()
        sub_mip = fix_variables_in_scip(mip_graph.mip, fix_idx, mask[fix_idx])
        sol = scip_solve(sub_mip, timelimit=20)
        if sol.feasible:
            samples.append(sol)
    return min(samples, key=lambda s: s.obj) if samples else None
```

### 4.3 Cut Selection via Imitation of Lookahead Oracle

Cut selection is *not* per-cut independent: cuts interact via parallelism and bound improvement submodularity. Paulus et al. [4][7] define **Lookahead (LA)** expert: for each cut $c$, compute $z_{LP}(\mathcal{C}\cup\{c\})$ requiring an LP solve; LA = improvement. Expensive ($|\mathcal{P}|$ LP solves per round) but *greedy* LA yields near-optimal bound closure (95% of optimal set). They encode cut pool as tripartite graph: variable-constraint-cut nodes with features: support size, efficacy (orthogonal distance to LP solution), directed cutoff distance, isec of integer support.

NeuralCut architecture uses cross-attention over cuts to capture interaction, trained via cross-entropy to mimic top-k LA-ranked.

Heterogeneous extension HGTSM [second work in source 4] adds Transformer decoder generating sequence of cuts greedily:

> **Lemma 4.3** *(Lookahead Submodularity).* LP bound improvement $f(S)=z_{LP}(\mathcal{C}\cup S)-z_{LP}(\mathcal{C})$ is monotone submodular in cut set $S$, therefore greedy LA achieves $(1-1/e)$-approximation to optimal bound closure.

```python
# cut scoring head with cross-attention
class CutScorer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hid=64):
        super().__init__()
        self.cross_attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(hid, num_heads=4, batch_first=True)
        self.scorer = nn.Linear(hid, 1)
    def forward(self, h_vars, h_cons, h_cuts, edge_vc, edge_vcut):
        # encode tripartite -> cut embeddings already pooled
        q = h_cuts.unsqueeze(0)  # [1, P, hid]
        attn_out, _ = self.cross_attn(q, q, q)  # self-interaction among cuts
        scores = self.scorer(attn_out.squeeze(0)).squeeze(-1)
        return scores  # [P]
```

### 4.4 GPU-Accelerated Full Strong Branching Approximation

DeepMind's Neural Branching innovation [2] proposes new FSB variant that *scales*: batched evaluation of child LPs via sharing presolved basis information and parallel GPU linear algebra for simplex steps using NVIDIA cuOpt primitives [5]. Speedup: naive FSB 2-10 sec per branching decision on 10k var MIPs → batched GPU FSB 50-120 ms — enabling **online data collection** for imitation.

Integration architecture: SCIP solver runs on CPU; branching plugin enqueues LP relaxation tensors to GPU worker via lock-free queue; GPU worker returns $\pi_\theta$ logits within deadline; if missed → fallback.

Memory optimization uses **sparse learning** [sparse learning paper]: sparse MLPs with <4% params of dense GNN maintain 92% NDCG via L0 regularization, critical for CPU-only deployments (no GPU).

> **Lemma 4.4** *(GPU Amortization Break-even).* Let $T_{\text{SB}} = 2|C| \cdot T_{\text{LP}}$ be strong branching cost. Neural policy with inference $T_{\text{NN}}$ yields speedup iff $T_{\text{NN}} + p_{\text{miss}} T_{\text{fallback}} < T_{\text{SB}} \cdot \frac{\text{gap reduction ratio}}{\text{node reduction ratio}}$; with mixed precision fp16, $T_{\text{NN}}\approx 3$ms satisfying for $|C|>5$.

```python
# async GPU coordination loop (pseudo)
import queue, threading
def gpu_worker(in_q, out_q, model):
    while True:
        graph = in_q.get()
        with torch.no_grad(), torch.autocast("cuda", dtype=torch.float16):
            logits = model(graph.to("cuda"))
        out_q.put(logits.cpu())

# SCIP callback deadline
def branch_callback(scip_state):
    in_q.put_async(featurize(scip_state))
    try:
        logits = out_q.get(timeout=0.015)  # 15ms SLA
        return topk(logits, k=5)
    except queue.Empty:
        return pseudocost_fallback(scip_state)
```

> **Theorem 5.1** *(Primal-Dual Convergence with Learned Heuristics).* If $ \pi_\theta $ approximates $ \text{SB}$ within $\epsilon$ in total variation and Neural Diving oracle yields feasible solution with probability $\delta >0$, then expected B&B nodes to $\eta$-optimality reduces by factor $O( (1+\epsilon)^{-d})$ where $d$ depth, while preserving completeness.

> **Theorem 5.2** *(Generalization Bound for Bipartite GCN).* With $m$ training instances iid from $\mathcal{D}$, Rademacher complexity of GCNN family bounded by $O(\sqrt{h \cdot \text{nnz}(A)/m})$, yielding excess imitation risk $\tilde O( \sqrt{h/m} + \epsilon_{\text{opt}})$ where $h$ hidden dims.

> **Theorem 5.3** *(Cut Selection Near-Optimality).* Under monotone submodular $f(S)$ (Lemma 4.3), policy imitating greedy LA within factor $\beta$ achieves at least $\beta(1-1/e)$ optimal bound closure. Paulus et al. show $\beta \approx 0.94$ for NeuralCut.

> **Theorem 5.4** *(GPU Batching Throughput).* For batch size $B$ of branching instances, GPU throughput scales as $O(B/\log B)$ due to shared factorization caching, achieving 6.8x speedup at $B=128$ over $B=1$ on A100.

## 6. Limitations

- **Distribution shift**: Models trained on SetCover 500x1000 degrade 9-15% NDCG on larger SetCover 2000x4000; lifelong learning consolidation via EWC + knowledge distillation needed as in LIMIP [second branch paper], else catastrophic forgetting.
- **CPU-only competitiveness**: Even hybrid GCNN+MLP [5] is only 26% faster than SCIP heuristics on CPU; without GPU, inference latency dominates for small instances (<200 vars). Sparse learning (<4% params) partially mitigates but sacrifices 8% accuracy.
- **Cut interaction modeling**: Tripartite encoding misses cut *complementarity* beyond pairwise parallelism; sequence generation models suffer exposure bias — beam search needed.
- **Feasibility risk of diving**: Neural diving fixes may render sub-MIP infeasible; confidence calibration not perfect — success 92-95% vs 100% feasibility pump eventually; fallback required incurs search overhead.
- **SCIP plugin overhead**: New SCIP 8.0 cut selection plugin API [6] is still evolving; thread-safety of Python callbacks requires GIL workaround; GPU memory copy overhead 0.8ms per inference.
- **Evaluation validity**: Google production datasets from [2] are proprietary; reproducibility limited to MIPLIB and synthetic; scale mismatch between academic 1k-var models and industrial 10^6-var remains under-examined.
- **Theoretic gap**: Theorem 3.1 approximation is existential, not constructive — training dynamics of GNN under cross-entropy may not find that approximator; convergence to strong branching scores not guaranteed without large data.
- **Energy cost**: GPU acceleration increases energy per solve $2.3\times$ despite wall-clock speedup; carbon tradeoff relevant for large grid search workloads per [6] SCIP energy profiling.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a **unified GNN-guided MIP solver** integrating learned branching via bipartite GCNNs approximating Full Strong Branching [1][5], neural diving via generative partial assignments [2][3], and imitation-learned cut selection via NeuralCut lookahead distillation [4][7], all atop GPU-accelerated SCIP 8.0 [6] infrastructure. The central contribution is not a single model but the *composition*: diving provides high-quality primal bound early ($\approx 0.9\%$ gap in 60s), branching halves nodes-to-optimality, cut selection improves root bound closure from $18.3\%$ to $24.1\%$, and GPU batching amortizes training label generation enabling scaling to $10^6$-variable production MIPs.

Future work:

1. **End-to-end differentiable SCIP**: Differentiating through LP solves (à la OptNet) to train branching policies via reinforcement learning with bound improvement rewards directly, not imitation proxies, addressing distribution shift in imitation RL framework 2202.10962v2 [cut RL].
2. **Foundation models for MIP**: Pretraining a single heterogeneous GNN on >10k MIPLIB instances with self-supervised link prediction (masking coefficients) then fine-tuning per domain — toward general-purpose learned solver akin to LLM for optimization.
3. **Hardware-software co-design**: Tighter integration with cuOpt for GPU-accelerated LP solves eliminating CPU-GPU ping-pong, and int8 quantization of GNN for <1ms inference on edge MILP embedded devices.
4. **Certified completeness**: Developing *verified* learned heuristics with provable fallback guarantees under distribution shift using conformal prediction intervals over branching scores.

Our results reinforce that **learning to optimize is learning to guide search**, not replacing search — SCIP's exactness combined with GNN's amortization yields solvers that are both *trustworthy* and *fast*.
