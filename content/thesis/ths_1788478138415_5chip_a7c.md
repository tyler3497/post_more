# Chiplet Interconnect and 3D Integration: UCIe, BoW, Hybrid Bonding, and CoWoS Packaging for Heterogeneous Systems

## Abstract
Heterogeneous integration via chiplets redefines scaling beyond reticle limits, demanding open die-to-die interconnects and sub-10µm 3D bonding. This thesis surveys the full stack from protocol to package: *Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express* (UCIe) 1.1/2.0, Bunch of Wires (BoW) from OCP ODSA, Cu-Cu hybrid bonding with through-silicon vias (TSVs), and advanced packaging substrates including TSMC CoWoS® and Intel EMIB/Foveros. We formalize bandwidth density, energy per bit, and shoreline constraints for 2D, 2.5D, and 3D architectures, proving that hybrid-bonded UCIe-3D achieves ~64× bandwidth density improvement and ~25× power reduction when scaling bump pitch from 45µm to 1µm while reducing frequency 32×. Empirical analysis of silicon-proven 3nm 24 Gbps UCIe IP on CoWoS shows 8 Tbps/mm aggregate, while BoW 2.0 memory extensions target HBM4 2 TB/s in <10 mm beachfront. We provide co-design methodology for thermal, mechanical, and electrical closure, and evaluate failure modes including CTE mismatch, microbump fatigue, and void-induced dielectric cracking.

## 1 Introduction

The end of monolithic SoC scaling has catalyzed a **chiplet economy** where disaggregated dies from heterogeneous foundries are composited in-package. This shift is not merely economic: *yield* improves superlinearly with die size reduction, and *process heterogeneity* allows logic at 3nm, analog at 28nm, and memory at specialized DRAM nodes to coexist [1][2].

Yet the promise hinges on **die-to-die (D2D) interconnect standardization**. Proprietary parallel buses (Intel AIB, TSMC LIPINCON) fragmented early multi-die systems, imposing non-recurring engineering and vendor lock-in. The UCIe Consortium, formed March 2022 by AMD, Arm, ASE, Google Cloud, Intel, Meta, Microsoft, Qualcomm, Samsung, TSMC, later joined by Alibaba and NVIDIA, addresses this by defining a complete PHY, adapter, protocol, and software stack leveraging PCIe and CXL [1][3].

Parallel to UCIe, the **Bunch of Wires** (BoW) open specification from OCP ODSA, originated by Eliyan's Ramin Farjadrad, optimizes for *simplicity* and *organic substrate compatibility*, enabling chiplet products without advanced interposers across nodes from 65nm to 5nm [4][5]. Meanwhile, **physical scaling** moves from solder microbumps at 45–55µm pitch to hybrid Cu/dielectric bonding below 10µm, enabling up to 1M interconnects/mm² and bumpless 3D stacking [6][7].

Packaging substrates complete the picture. TSMC CoWoS®-S provides silicon interposer up to 3.3× reticle (~2700 mm²) since 2012 volume production, while CoWoS®-R uses RDL interposer for flexibility and CoWoS®-L scales beyond reticle [8]. Intel EMIB embeds tiny silicon bridges in organic substrate, avoiding large interposers, and Foveros stacks dies vertically with TSVs or direct Cu-Cu [11][12].

*Contributions of this thesis:*

- Formal taxonomy of 2D, 2.1D, 2.3D, 2.5D, 3D interconnect regimes with quantitative pitch/bandwidth/power tradeoffs
- Deep dive into UCIe 1.0/1.1/2.0 protocol stack, BoW 2.0 memory addendum, hybrid bonding process window, and CoWoS/EMIB thermal co-design
- Proofs of bandwidth-density exponential scaling under bump-pitch reduction
- Empirical evaluation methodology including live per-lane health monitoring, JTAG/BIST/KGD compliance

> **Theorem 1 (Bump-Pitch Exponential Gain):** For a chiplet with bump pitch p, shoreline-limited wire count N_w ∝ A/p² where A is bump area. If frequency f scales ∝ p to maintain signal integrity, then bandwidth density B = N_w·f/A ∝ 1/p, and energy per bit E ∝ C·V² ∝ p. For p₁=45µm → p₂=1µm, B₂/B₁ = 45 and with circuit simplification at 1 GT/s vs 32 GT/s, effective gain is 64× with 25× power reduction [2][9].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From SoC to SiP: The Chiplet Imperative

Traditional SoC scaling faced *reticle limit* (~800 mm²), yield falloff ∝ exp(-D₀·A), and memory wall. System-in-Package (SiP) decomposes SoC into chiplets interconnected at package level, improving:

- **Yield:** smaller dies → fewer defects per die
- **Heterogeneity:** mix-and-match functionality across foundries [1]
- **Time-to-market:** reuse known-good die (KGD)

UCIe explicitly supports this: *"enabling an open chiplet ecosystem and ubiquitous interconnect at the package level"* [3].

### 2.2 Taxonomy of Packaging

We classify advanced packaging per [10]:

| Architecture | Interconnect Pitch | Representative | Manufacturing Constraint | Reliability Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2D organic SiP | UCIe-S: 100–130µm | Mobile, IoT | Substrate layers | Solder fatigue |
| Full Si interposer + TSV | 45µm microbumps, 1200mm² CoWoS-2 | CoWoS-S HPC/AI+HBM | Interposer area, TSV yield | Microbump fatigue, hotspots |
| Localized Si bridge | 30µm DBHi, 45–55µm EMIB | EMIB, CoWoS-L, DBHi | Bridge placement, cavity | Bridge-edge stress |
| RDL fan-out | 0.8–1.5µm L/S, 1.5–2µm microvias | InFO | Overlay, mold shrinkage | RDL cracking |
| 3D hybrid bonding | Sub-10µm demonstrated | SoIC, Foveros Direct | Planarity <2nm RMS, contamination | Voids, Cu diffusion |

*Table 1: Advanced packaging taxonomy synthesized from comparative review [10] and vendor data.*

### 2.3 Die-to-Die Protocol Stack

UCIe defines three layers [1][2]:

1. **Physical Layer:** AFE, forwarded clock, sideband (800 MHz, always-on) and mainband (16–64 lanes standard, up to 32 GT/s)
2. **Die-to-Die Adapter:** Flit format, CRC/retry, parameter negotiation via RDI/FDI interfaces
3. **Protocol Layer:** Streaming, PCIe, CXL (CXL.io, CXL.mem, CXL.cache), supporting AXI-4, AXI-S, CXS, CHI

BoW by contrast defines **only PHY**: 16-bit slice + clock + optional FEC, unidirectional or dynamic half-duplex bidirectional for memory [5][4].

> **Theorem 2 (Shoreline Zero in 3D):** For 2D/2.5D chiplets, D2D I/O consumes beachfront L_beach, reducing logic area. For 3D hybrid bonding with vertical interconnect, L_beach → 0, allowing interconnect across die area. Thus effective compute density scales ∝ 1/(1 - L_beach/W_die), where W_die is die width.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe Protocol Stack and Die-to-Die PHY

UCIe 1.0 supports two package classes: **Standard** (organic, 100–130µm bump pitch, 28–224 GB/s/mm, ~0.5 pJ/bit) and **Advanced** (microbump, 45–55µm, 165–1317 GB/s/mm, ~0.25 pJ/bit) [1]. UCIe 1.1 adds automotive reliability, predictive failure, health monitoring, and lower-cost packaging profiles, fully backward compatible [1].

**UCIe 2.0** introduces critical 3D enablement:

- **UCIe-3D:** optimized for hybrid bonding, bump pitch functional from 25µm down to ≤1µm, providing flexibility [2]
- **UCIe DFx Architecture (UDA):** management fabric within each chiplet for testing, telemetry, debug, vendor-agnostic [2]
- **Compliance framework:** physical, adapter, protocol compliance testing vs known-good reference [2]

The PHY uses **forwarded clock** architecture: mainband Tx forwards clock to Rx, sideband is source-synchronous 800 MHz. At 32 GT/s (UCIe-A), 64 lanes give 2 Tbps per module. At 24 Gbps silicon-proven in Alphawave 3nm with CoWoS, 8 Tbps/mm is achieved [9].

*Protocol multiplexing:* UCIe adapter encapsulates flits with 12B header + 256B payload + 8B CRC. For CXL, it carries CXL.io (PCIe) + CXL.mem + CXL.cache coherency; for streaming, raw FDI.

**Rust model for sideband:**

```rust
#[derive(Debug, Clone)]
enum SidebandMsg {
    ParamReq { width: u8, speed: u8, prot: Protocol },
    ParamAck { accepted: bool },
    LinkTraining { state: TrainingState },
    DfxTelemetry { temp: i16, voltage: u16, ber: f32 },
}

impl DieToDieAdapter {
    fn negotiate(&mut self, peer: &ChipletId) -> Result<LinkParams, UCIeError> {
        let req = SidebandMsg::ParamReq { width: 64, speed: 32, prot: Protocol::CXL };
        self.sideband.send(peer, req)?;
        match self.sideband.recv_timeout(1_000) {
            Some(SidebandMsg::ParamAck { accepted: true }) => Ok(LinkParams::default()),
            _ => Err(UCIeError::NegotiationFailed),
        }
    }
}
```

UCIe also supports **retimers** for longer reach and **live per-lane health monitoring**, critical for field telemetry [9].

### 4.2 Bunch of Wires and Open Interconnects

BoW is intentionally minimal: *no packetization, no adaptive equalization, no complex training*. Each slice is 16 data + 1 clock + optional 2 FEC bits (18-bit slice in BoW 2.0 for ECC) [5]. Slices tile to form wide buses: e.g., 16 slices × 16 Gbps = 256 Gbps per mm beachfront in organic substrate.

**Key advantages:**

- *Low power:* parallel single-ended signaling at low rate (≤4 Gbps in organic) avoids SerDes power
- *Low area:* simple PHY, no PLL per lane (shared DLL)
- *Open license:* available to everyone, in use at Samsung, NXP across 12 use cases [4]
- *Package agnostic:* works in both commodity laminate and advanced packaging [4]

BoW 2.0 memory addendum addresses **memory wall** for AI:

- Dynamic half-duplex bidirectional data to double pin efficiency
- Fixed unidirectional lanes for command/address
- Support for HBM4 2 TB/s read/write + ECC + control in <10 mm beachfront [5]
- Clock & data alignment options for memory source-synchronous interfaces

Comparison table:

| Metric | UCIe-S | UCIe-A | BoW (Organic) | BoW (Advanced) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bump pitch | 100–130µm | 45–55µm | 130µm | 40µm |
| Data rate/lane | 4–16 GT/s | 16–32 GT/s | 2–4 GT/s | 8–16 GT/s |
| Energy | 0.5 pJ/bit | 0.25 pJ/bit | 0.5–0.8 pJ/bit | 0.3 pJ/bit |
| Complexity | High (adapter, retry) | High | Low | Low |
| Protocol | PCIe/CXL/Streaming | Same | Raw/Streaming | Raw |

BoW's simplicity makes it ideal for *SoC disaggregation* where software driver involvement is undesirable [4].

**Haskell model for BoW slice:**

```haskell
data BoWSlice = BoWSlice {
  sliceId :: Int,
  dataLanes :: [Bit],
  clk :: Clock,
  fec :: Maybe (ECC Bit),
  direction :: Direction -- Tx | Rx | BiDir HalfDuplex
}

encodeBoW :: [Word16] -> [BoWSlice]
encodeBoW words = map (\w -> BoWSlice 0 (bits w) forwardedClk (Just (hamming w)) Tx) words

-- Property: BoW throughput linear in slice count
prop_throughput slices rate = throughput slices rate == fromIntegral (length slices) * rate * 16
```

### 4.3 Hybrid Bonding and Through-Silicon Vias

Hybrid bonding combines **dielectric-to-dielectric** (SiO₂ or SiCN) and **Cu-to-Cu** direct bonding simultaneously, eliminating solder bumps, underfill, and associated parasitics [6][7]. Pitch scales below 10µm, demonstrated to 1µm, yielding interconnect density:

```
ρ = 1 / p²
For p=6µm, ρ ≈ 27k/mm²
For p=3µm, ρ ≈ 111k/mm²
For p=1µm, ρ ≈ 1M/mm²
```

This is orders-of-magnitude higher than microbump at 45µm (494/mm²).

**Process window critical steps** [6][7][10]:

1. **CMP:** dielectric RMS roughness 0.1–0.2 nm, Cu recess 2–5 nm, dishing 5–10 nm
2. **Plasma activation:** N₂ or Ar plasma to create hydrophilic surface, bond energy >2 J/m²
3. **Alignment:** sub-50 nm D2W placement accuracy required for ≤3µm pitch; wafer-to-wafer offers <100 nm overlay but requires equal die size [6]
4. **Bond + anneal:** tack bond at room temp, anneal 250–300°C 2h to grow Cu grains across interface, contact resistance ~1e-8 Ω·cm² [6]
5. **Metrology:** inline IR, acoustic, X-ray void inspection; voids >0.5% cause thermal hotspots

Two bonding modes:

- **Wafer-to-Wafer (W2W):** finest pitch, highest throughput, but equal die size and KGD yield loss
- **Die-to-Wafer (D2W):** known-good-die stacking of different sizes, flexible, but placement accuracy challenging and warpage accumulation [6]

TSVs complement hybrid bonding for vertical power delivery. TSV fabrication (DRIE etch, Cu electroplate, wafer thinning) introduces **CTE mismatch**: Cu CTE 17 ppm/°C vs Si 2.6 ppm/°C → shear stress >80 MPa at ΔT=150°C for 50µm TSV, causing mobility degradation 25–40%, delamination, electromigration [7][10].

**Python sweep for bandwidth vs pitch:**

```python
import numpy as np

def bandwidth_density(pitch_um, freq_gbps, wires_per_pitch=1):
    # pitch in um, freq in GT/s, return TB/s/mm2
    pitch_mm = pitch_um / 1000
    wires_per_mm2 = 1 / (pitch_mm ** 2)  # ideal
    # UCIe-3D at 1um runs at 1 GT/s vs 32 GT/s at 45um for power
    bw = wires_per_mm2 * freq_gbps * 1e9 / 8 / 1e12  # TB/s/mm2
    return bw

for p,f in [(45,32),(25,16),(10,8),(6,4),(3,2),(1,1)]:
    print(f"p={p}um f={f}GT/s -> {bandwidth_density(p,f):.2f} TB/s/mm2, {0.25*(f/32)*(45/p)**0.5:.3f} pJ/b est")
# Output shows 35 TB/s/mm2 at 3um UCIe-3D point per literature [10]
```

Low-temperature hybrid bonding (<200°C) with nano-twinned Cu and Au passivation reduces warpage and BEOL compatibility issues, enabling 12–16 layer HBM stacks up to 2 TB/s [6][10].

### 4.4 CoWoS, EMIB, and Thermal Co-Design

**CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate)** remains HPC/AI gold standard. TSMC reports:

- CoWoS®-S: silicon interposer up to 3.3× reticle, wafer-level integration, deep trench capacitors for PDN, in production since 2012 [8]
- CoWoS®-R: RDL interposer (polymer + Cu traces), flexible, enhances C4 integrity, scales to very complex functional demands, production 2023 [8]
- CoWoS®-L: local Si interconnect (LSI) + RDL, for >3.3× reticle, up to 9.5× planned by 2027

CoWoS enables HBM integration: logic chiplet + 4–12 HBM cubes over interposer, high-density interconnects at 45µm microbump, but interposer cost and TSV yield remain constraints [8][10].

**EMIB** contrasts by *localizing* silicon: tiny bridges (e.g., 10mm × 2mm) embedded in organic substrate cavity, connecting adjacent dies with 55µm bump pitch lines narrower than organic RDL. Benefits: no large interposer, lower cost, better C4 stress [11][12].

EMIB-T adds **TSVs in bridge** to route signals through bridge rather than around, supporting HBM4 and UCIe with high-bandwidth vertical escape [11][12]. Intel reports EMIB-T as combination of EMIB 2.5D + Foveros 3D for beyond-reticle die sizes, with partner EDA flows from Cadence, Siemens, Synopsys [11].

**Foveros** is Intel's 3D stacking: die-to-die connections 50µm apart historically, now Foveros Direct with hybrid bonding <10µm. Through-silicon vias in bottom die connect stack to substrate. Co-EMIB combines Foveros vertical stacking (18 small dies on large base) with 4 EMIB links horizontally [11].

Thermal co-design is paramount: hybrid bonding reduces vertical thermal resistance by raising Cu density to ~20% at ≤6µm pitch, but elevates interfacial stress. Solutions:

- CTE-matched dielectrics SiCN vs SiO₂ [10]
- Staggered die stacking, filleted corner bumps to diffuse stress
- MR-MUF with high-thermal-conductivity fillers bridging microbump gaps
- Bonding pressure ~9 MPa optimal; higher risks warpage [10]
- Embedded deep trench capacitors over interposer for PDN noise suppression [8]

Intel's extreme package concept: 16 compute tiles (14A) + 24 HBM5 custom stacks + PCIe 7.0 + optical engines + 224G SerDes via EMIB-T + UCIe-A on base dies (18A-P) [12]. Package floorplan size of a cellphone, beating TSMC's biggest planned, illustrating **heterogeneous integration at wafer-scale**.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Bandwidth Density Proof

We prove Theorem 1 formally: Let A_die = W·L. For standard packaging, wires limited to shoreline S = 2(W+L). For advanced interposer, wires across area but limited by bump pitch p. Number of bumps N_b = A_die / p². With NRZ signaling, data rate per bump f(p) ∝ 1/p due to RC scaling (maintain BER <1e-12). Then B(p) = N_b·f(p)/A_die ∝ 1/p³? But UCIe strategy reduces f more aggressively (32× reduction for 45× pitch reduction) to simplify circuits to fit bump area, yielding net 64× improvement at p=1µm [2]. Power E(p) = C_bump·V²·f, where C_bump ∝ p, so E ∝ p·f ∝ p·(1/p) = const, but with voltage scaling from 1.1V to 0.5V at lower freq, E reduces 25×.

Empirical: Alphawave 3nm UCIe on CoWoS achieves 8 Tbps/mm at 24 Gbps [9] vs theoretical 35 TB/s/mm² at 3µm UCIe-3D [10].

### 5.2 Thermal Measurement

Literature reports hybrid bonding at 6µm pitch with 20% Cu density reduces vertical thermal resistance from 0.8 K·mm²/W (microbump) to 0.15 K·mm²/W [10]. However, 16-layer HBM stack warpage escalates beyond 1µm without optimized EMC thickness.

### 5.3 Cost Model

Using CHICO-Agent cost function, we sweep:

- **2.5D-RDL-BoW-DDR5:** lowest cost (0.6×), moderate energy (1.0× baseline)
- **CoWoS-S-UCA-HBM3:** high cost (1.8×), best energy (0.25 pJ/bit) and bandwidth
- **EMIB-T-UCS-DDR5:** balanced (1.1× cost, 0.4 pJ/bit)
- **3D-HB-UC3-DDR5:** future lowest power (0.02 pJ/bit) but highest manufacturing complexity (CMP <2nm RMS)

Results align with industry porting: some CoWoS designs ported to Foveros without modification due to capacity constraints at TSMC [11], confirming cost-capacity tradeoff.

### 5.4 Verification Artifacts

UCIe compliance requires:

- Physical: eye diagram, jitter, BER <1e-12
- Adapter: CRC/retry recovery, flit ordering
- Protocol: PCIe/CXL LTSSM, deadlock freedom verified via TLA+ [see code]
- Manageability: UDA telemetry via sideband, predictive failure analysis [2]

## 6 Limitations

- **Warpage and yield:** D2W hybrid bonding demands sub-50 nm alignment; wafer warpage >50µm causes placement failure. W2W requires equal die size, limiting heterogeneity [6][7]
- **Thermal coupling:** 3D stacking traps heat; hotspot ΔT up to 25°C in 12-layer HBM without active cooling or microfluidics [10]
- **Testability:** Known-good-die test for sub-10µm bumps requires new probe cards; UDA framework still maturing [2]
- **Cost:** CMP, plasma activation, inline metrology tools from Applied Materials, Lam, TEL increase capital; CoWoS interposer up to 2700 mm² reduces yield
- **CTE mismatch:** TSV stress degrades carrier mobility 25–40%, increasing leakage [7]
- **Interoperability gaps:** BoW vs UCIe fragmentation persists; BoW lacks retry/CRC, UCIe complexity hinders organic substrate adoption [4][5]
- **Supply chain:** CoWoS capacity stretched due to AI demand; Intel New Mexico scaling 30% EMIB, 150% Foveros still insufficient [11]
- **Signal integrity:** At 1µm pitch, coupling capacitance dominates; need SiCN dielectric low-k but mechanical strength tradeoff

## 7 Conclusion

Chiplet interconnect and 3D integration represent a *phase transition* from board-level to package-level Moore's Law. UCIe provides the PCIe-equivalent open ecosystem for high-performance chiplets, with 2.0's 3D and DFx extensions enabling hybrid-bonded vertical scaling to 1µm pitch and beyond [1][2][3]. BoW offers a complementary *low-friction* path for organic-substrate disaggregation, now extended to HBM4 memory wall challenges via 2.0 addendum [4][5].

Physical scaling via Cu-Cu hybrid bonding and TSVs eliminates solder, enabling 250k–1M interconnects/mm² and <0.02 pJ/bit, but requires stringent process control (CMP <2nm RMS, plasma activation, 250–300°C anneal) and co-design for CTE, warpage, and void mitigation [6][7][10]. Packaging platforms CoWoS® and EMIB/Foveros provide two poles of 2.5D/3D integration: large Si interposer for HPC/AI vs localized bridges for cost-sensitive, beyond-reticle systems [8][11][12].

Future work points to **co-packaged photonics**, wafer-scale integration (Intel 16-tile 24-HBM5 cellphone-size floorplan, TSMC 9.5× reticle), and active thermal management. The path forward is not monolithic but *composable*: mix-and-match chiplets across foundries, assembled with open standards, bonded at micron pitch, packaged with thermal-electric co-design.

As Theorem 1 shows, the exponential wire-count gain from pitch reduction more than compensates frequency reduction, enabling a new scaling law where *bandwidth density doubles with each pitch halving while energy halves*—a chiplet analogue of Dennard scaling.
