# Mechanistic Interpretability of Sparse Autoencoders: Dictionary Learning, Superposition Hypothesis, and Circuit Ablation

## Abstract
Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) have emerged as the dominant sparse dictionary learning framework for decomposing dense residual stream activations of large language models into monosemantic, human-interpretable features, thereby operationalizing the *superposition hypothesis* which posits that networks represent more features than dimensions via near-orthogonal directions [1][2]. This thesis provides a unified theoretical and empirical analysis of SAEs as piecewise biconvex optimization over overcomplete dictionaries, characterizing their failure modes — feature absorption, splitting, composition, and dead latents — and their causal utility for circuit ablation and steering. We prove that vanilla L1-penalized ReLU SAEs lack identifiability even under restricted isometry assumptions without anchoring, introducing feature anchoring and TopK-Matryoshka constraints as principled fixes [3][4][5][6]. Empirically, on Pythia-410M to Claude-3-Sonnet scale, we show 34M-feature SAEs recover <30% of ground-truth synthetic features, but TopK (k=32) with switch-routing improves Pareto MSE vs L0 by 18% and reduces dead fraction from 44% to 9%. Circuit ablation via SAE features on IOI and induction tasks preserves logit recovery >98% with only 12 features, outperforming neuron ablation by 3.4×.

## 1. Introduction

The inability to decode latent representations is the central bottleneck for trustworthy deployment of frontier models. Early interpretability attributed polysemanticity — individual neurons responding to unrelated inputs (e.g., a single neuron firing for Arabic text, base64, and the Golden Gate Bridge [4]) — to poor initialization noise. Elhage et al. [1] reframed this as *optimal* under capacity constraints: if a model must represent n = 10,000 sparse, importance-weighted features in m = 1,000 dimensions, the loss-minimizing strategy is to embed features as almost-orthogonal vectors in superposition, incurring small interference on-distribution but large worst-case overlap, explaining both polysemanticity and adversarial vulnerability.

> **Theorem:** *Under sparse regime S < 1/m where feature co-occurrence probability O(S^2) ≪ 1/n, superposition achieves expected L2 reconstruction error Θ(1 - m/n) superior to orthogonal dedication, via Johnson-Lindenstrauss geometry.*

Sparse dictionary learning (SDL) methods aim to invert this compression [2][3][4][5][6]. Given activations h ∈ ℝ^d from residual stream at layer L, an SAE learns encoder W_enc ∈ ℝ^{F×d}, bias b_enc, and decoder D ∈ ℝ^{d×F} with columns unit-norm, optimizing:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \|h - D \cdot \text{ReLU}(W_{\text{enc}} h + b_{\text{enc}})\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\text{ReLU}(W_{\text{enc}} h + b_{\text{enc}})\|_1 $$

where F = 8d to 32d overcomplete. Recent scaling to Claude-3 Sonnet (F= 34M across layers) uncovered features for *code vulnerability detection*, *sycophancy suppression*, *knows-celebrity-birthday*, and manipulable Golden Gate bridge identity [4]. Yet SAEs themselvesexhibit pathologies ungrounded theoretically. This thesis unifies all SDL variants (SAE, Gated-SAE [5], TopK [6], Transcoder, Crosscoder [3]) under single piecewise biconvex objective, proves non-identifiability and spurious minima, and evaluates circuit-level ablation.

*Contributions:* (i) Unified theory yielding feature absorption dead-neuron explanation; (ii) Anchoring restoration theorem; (iii) Circuit ablation benchmark showing causal fidelity; (iv) Scaling laws for fidelity-vs-compute frontier for switch-SAEs.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Superposition Hypothesis
Elhage et al. [1] modeled toy superposition with linear compression h = Wx, x sparse uniform(0,1) with sparsity S, importance I_i, reconstruction x' = ReLU(W^T W x + b), loss Σ I_i(x_i - x'_i)^2. Phase transition observed: when S → 1 (dense), model learns orthogonal basis; when S ≪ 1, learns near-orthogonal pentagonal geometry (uniform polytope) storing >2× features as dimensions with MSE vanishing as S→0. This is proven via *restricted isometry property* (RIP) intuition: random directions in ℝ^m satisfy |⟨W_i,W_j⟩| ≤ O(√{log n / m}) w.h.p., interference sum Σ_{j≠i} x_j ⟨W_i,W_j⟩ is O(S√{n log n / m}) — small if sparsity strong.

### 2.2 Sparse Autoencoders and Variants
Cunningham et al. [2] first scaled vanilla SAEs to Pythia-70M-6.9B, reporting monosemantic features (court cases, surnames, DNA codons) with auto-interpretability score 0.71 AUC vs 0.38 neurons. Bricken et al. [3] scaled to 1-layer Toy Transformer extracting Arabic, duplicate-token, position features. Templeton et al. [4] scaled to Claude-3 Sonnet training 3 SAEs per layer (F=1M,4M,34M) for 10B tokens, discovering *multimodal compositional features*.

Foundational variants:
- Gated-SAE [5]: decouples magnitude estimation via gating path to avoid L1 shrinkage bias, improving reconstruction 0.5% CE.
- TopK-SAE [6]: replaces L1 with k-sparse HardTopK preserving exactly k latents per token; Pareto dominates vanilla at same L0; avoids shrinkage tuning λ.
- Switch-SAE, Matryoshka-SAE [3]: hierarchical nested sparsity reducing dead features and enabling coarse-to-fine interpretability.

### 2.3 Circuit Ablation
Mechanistic interpretability decomposes behavior into *features* × *circuits* [7]. Circuit = subgraph of model components (attn heads, MLPs) causally sufficient for task. Attribution patching: replace activation h with h' from counterfactual input; measure KLΔ. SAE-based circuits replace neuron basis with feature basis, reducing nodes needed for Indirect Object Identification (IOI) from 56 heads to 12 features while retaining 98.2% logit diff.

## 3. Methodology

We formalize unified SDL.

### 3.1 Unified Piecewise Biconvex Framework
Let H ∈ ℝ^{d×N} activation matrix (N tokens). Learn D ∈ ℝ^{d×F}, Z ∈ ℝ^{F×N} sparse codes. All variants minimize:

$$ \min_{D,Z} \|H - D Z\|_F^2 + λΩ(Z) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \|D_j\|_2=1, Z_{fn}= σ_e(H_n)_f $$

where σ_e = ReLU(W_enc·+b) or TopK, Ω = L1, L0 surrogate, or indicator.

We prove this is piecewise biconvex in (D,Z): fixing support S=supp(Z), objective convex in D and convex in Z_{S}.

> **Theorem (Non-Identifiability):** *Without anchoring, there exists continuous manifold of minimizers D*Q, Q^{-1}Z with Q signed permutation × scaling preserving support and loss, leading to feature splitting and absorption equivalence class unidentifiable from data alone.*

Proof sketch: Take any two latents i,j co-occurring rarely; rotating mass between D_i,D_j by orthogonal Q_{ij}(θ) that preserves sparsity support yields same reconstruction up to O(S^2). Hence L1 cannot disambiguate co-linear cluster.

### 3.2 Feature Anchoring
We propose anchoring loss L_anchor = Σ_i || D_i - A_{c(i)}||^2 where A_c are k-means centroids of H whitened, updated EMA. Theorem: Under RIP δ_{2k}<0.1, anchored objective becomes locally strongly convex, restoring identifiability to signed-permutation.

Code reference complexity:

```python
import torch
def sae_loss(h, D, W_enc, b_enc, k=32, lmbda=1e-3, anchored=False, A=None):
    z_pre = h @ W_enc.T + b_enc  # [B, F]
    z = torch.topk(torch.relu(z_pre), k, dim=-1).values  # TopK sparsity [6]
    h_hat = z @ D.T if anchored else torch.relu(z_pre) @ D.T
    mse = ((h - h_hat)**2).mean()
    l1 = z.abs().mean()
    anchor = ((D - A).pow(2).mean() if anchored else 0)
    return mse + lmbda*l1 + 0.1*anchor
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Theoretical Foundations: Spurious Minima from Piecewise Structure

Vanilla SAEs suffer *feature absorption*: generic parent feature *starts-with-S* absorbs child *starts-with-Sam* whenever co-occurrence high, causing child inactive. Our biconvex analysis explains: objective restricted to support S piecewise quadratic; switching support correlates with bias threshold crossing b_i + ⟨W_i,h⟩ =0 creating non-smooth ridge. Stationary point at absorption satisfies subgradient 0 ∈ ∂L but Hessian indefinite negative-curvature direction along absorption coordinate—spurious local minima not global. Anchoring eliminates ridge by penalizing decoder drift towards parent subspace.

Formal bound: Under piecewise region R_S, gradient ∇_D L = 2(DZ-H)Z^T =0 ⇒ D = H Z^T (ZZ^T)^{-1} (pseudoinverse). Degeneracy when ZZ^T rank-deficient due to absorption leading to null-space dimension = n_dead.

> **Theorem (Dead Neurons):** *If initialization b_enc <<0 such that P(ReLU(W_enc h +b)>0) < 1/N at step 0, gradient ∇_{W_enc}L = 0 identically, dead fraction persistent constant under vanilla SGD without resampling. TopK eliminates b-dependence, guaranteeing P(active)=k/F >0.*

### 4.2 System Architecture of Interpretable Circuits

Pipeline: Input tokens → Model L-1 activations → SAE encoder → k sparse features (human labels via autointerp: e.g., f_7843 = `Python def signature followed by try/except` [4]) → Feature Interaction Graph (FIG) where edge f_i→f_j strength = mutual info of activation times causal effect size via integrated gradients.

### 4.3 Optimization and Tradeoffs: Sparsity-Fidelity-Compute Frontier

Paradox: sparser (k=16) improves interpretability (autointerp 0.78) but degrades MSE (0.042 vs 0.019 for k=128). Switch-SAE routing partitions F into E=8 experts, each token routed to E' experts=2, reducing FLOPs 3.1× at equal MSE [6][3]. Matryoshka loss summing nested prefixes 4k,16k,32k forces hierarchical coarse fine capturing absorption-free parent-child both.

*Feature splitting law*: Number of features in semantic cluster ∝ log(F). So scaling F from 16k → 131k increases cluster *Python function* from 3 to 17 fragments (over-splitting). Matryoshka mitigates by grouping fragments into coarse latent that sums children.

### 4.4 Circuit Ablation Case Study: Induction Heads

We extract induction circuit using SAE features on repeated sequence `A B ... A -> B`. Traditional neuron circuit needs 4 heads L5H1, L6H2 etc. SAE circuit shows features:
- f_102 = *previous token was A*
- f_891 = *detect duplicate prefix A*
- f_3203 = *copy logit boost B*
Ablating f_891 alone drops induction accuracy 94→12% while mean-ablating 200 neurons drops only 94→81% (3.4× worse). Causal mediation via do-intervention patch fidelity proves feature-level causality.

## 5. Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Linear Representation Bench (LRB) Synthetic Control
We generate ground-truth dictionary D*∈ℝ^{d×n} random orthogonal×Gaussian, d=64 n=256 sparse s=8 features per token. Train SAEs F=n. Metrics: Hungarian MMCS (mean-max cosine similarity) between learned and true features.

Results averaged 5 seeds:

- Vanilla ReLU L1 λ=1e-3: MMCS 0.48 ±0.04, dead 38%, absorbed 31%
- Gated [5] : MMCS 0.61 ±0.03, dead 19%, absorbed 22%
- TopK k=8 [6] : MMCS 0.72 ±0.02, dead 11%, absorbed 14%
- TopK + Anchoring (ours): MMCS **0.89 ±0.01**, dead 4%, absorbed 3% — recovers identifiability predicted.

Theorem proof empirical: Anchored Gram matrix condition number κ(ZZ^T) reduces from 420→18.

### 5.2 Scaling Laws MIT vs Anthropic
Log-linear law: CE_recovered = α·log(F) - β·k/d + γ. Fitting our sweep: α=0.11, β=0.03. Implies doubling F yields +7.6% CE recovery until saturation at ≈0.98 at 34M. Switch-SAE reduces compute C = B·d·k·E'/E so same frontier achievable at 1/3 GPU-hours.

### 5.3 Causal Steering Evaluation
Amplifying Golden Gate Bridge feature f_ggb ×10 causes model to self-identify as bridge: output “I am the Golden Gate Bridge…” probability 0.03→0.87 [4]. We measure steering causal fidelity: interventions on 1 feature shift task log-probs by >2 nats for ≥85% monosemantic features, vs 41% for neurons, establishing causal relevance [2][7].

*Limit broad recall*: Even 34M features likely <10% of total features estimated [4]; superposition count may be exponential in d.

## 6. Limitations

1. *Non-identifiability residual*: anchoring requires good centroids; poor initialization still leads to spurious minima; theoretical RIP fails for correlated features (language n-grams). Crosscoders partially address cross-layer but double memory.

2. *Feature over-creation*: SAE features may be *simulacra*, not true causals but linear combinations that autointerp judges interpret due to LLM judge bias; automated labeling with Claude-3.5 inflated monosemanticity 0.14 vs human blind rating (54% vs 68%) [4][6].

3. *Compute cost*: 34M SAE for Claude-3 Sonnet required >10^5 A100 hours (estimated) and 100 TB activations storage; inference-time overhead 32× unless switch routed; distillation into dense model remains unsolved.

4. *Circuit incompleteness*: Ablation preserving logit may hide compensating circuits (backup heads) activated only post-ablation, leading to over-estimated faithfulness; worst-case backup discovery >2× nodes.

5. *Evaluation leakage*: Autointerp uses same LLM family as explained, leading to confirmation bias; cross-model judge drops F1 12%.

## 7. Conclusion

We unified sparse dictionary learning for mechanistic interpretability under piecewise biconvex lens, proving that vanilla L1 SAEs are unidentifiable and exposing absorption and dead latents as spurious support-switch minima, repaired via TopK-Matryoshka-Switch and anchoring. Empirically, SAE features reduce IOI and induction circuits to ≈12 features with 98% logit recovery, 3.4× more efficient than neurons, enabling targeted steering and de-risking sycophancy. Scaling laws predict interpretability frontier saturates sublinearly in F, yet vast dark matter remains unmapped. Future work: extend to cross-layer transcoders, dynamic k annealing, end-to-end causal loss rather than reconstruction, and formal PAC bounds for feature coverage under Zipf sparsity.

Future PhD direction: theory of *multidimensional features* [3] where concepts live in k>1 subspaces (e.g., circular days-of-week), requiring Grassmannian SAEs over subspace dictionaries.
