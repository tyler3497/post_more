# Coherent Photonic Neural-Network Accelerators: Mach–Zehnder Meshes, Optical Backpropagation, and Analog Noise Resilience

## Abstract

Silicon-photonic processors that perform **coherent optical matrix multiplication** encode weights as phase and amplitude shifts in *Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes*, executing matrix–vector products at light-propagation speed with energy bounded by photodetection. This thesis develops the full stack: universal unitary realization via Reck and Clements decompositions [1][2], the seminal coherent nanophotonic demonstration of Shen *et al.* [3], and the photonic analogue of backpropagation derived by Hughes *et al.* via adjoint-variable methods, yielding exact gradients from *in situ* intensity measurements [4]. We survey training without backpropagation — end-to-end optical backpropagation [5] and power-aware zeroth-order optimization [6] — and confront analog noise: phase drift, fabrication disorder, thermal crosstalk, and shot noise. Noise-tolerant coherent-neuron architectures with noise-aware training recover >99% MNIST accuracy at 10 GMAC/s per axon under σ = 0.4 noise [7], and sub-photon-per-multiplication inference exploits statistical averaging over large fan-in [9]. We close with energy projections below a joule per tera-operation and the hard physical limits — nonlinearity, memory volatility, and calibration complexity — bounding the coherent photonics roadmap.

##1 Introduction

Deep learning is dense linear algebra: inference is dominated by matrix–vector multiplications **y** = W**x** + **b**, with single layers demanding trillions of MACs per second. Electronics has tracked this through larger systolic arrays and smaller transistors, but Dennard scaling has ended: each MAC costs ~1 pJ, and moving operands between memory and compute — the *von Neumann bottleneck* — consumes most of it [8].

In a *coherent photonic neural network*, **x** is encoded as the complex amplitudes of light in N waveguides and W as a mesh of programmable MZIs. The product W**x** then *physically happens* through interference: propagation performs the multiplication in O(1) waveguide-transit time (~10–100 ps), independent of N. Energy cost migrates from computation to transduction — lasers, modulators, photodetectors — so throughput grows quadratically in N while energy per MAC falls [3][8].

This thesis is a self-contained treatment of that architecture. Our contributions are:

1. How arbitrary complex matrices decompose into meshes of 2×2 unitary gates (Reck and Clements schemes) [1][2], including the SVD construction mapping any matrix onto two unitary meshes plus a diagonal attenuation layer.
2. The derivation of **in situ backpropagation** — the photonic analogue of backpropagation — via adjoint-variable methods, yielding exact gradients from *on-chip* intensity measurements with no digital device model [4].
3. Training regimes that avoid backpropagation: bidirectional reversible MVM blocks with optical end-to-end backpropagation [5], and power-aware sparse zeroth-order optimization scaled to 2,500 on-chip components [6].
4. A quantitative analysis of **analog noise resilience** — fabrication disorder, thermo-optic phase drift, thermal crosstalk, shot noise — including the dual-IQ-modulator coherent neuron and noise-aware training that recover >99% MNIST accuracy at 10 GMAC/s/axon under σ = 0.4 noise [7].
5. Formal statements on universality, calibration complexity, and energy scaling, plus the hard physical limits — nonlinearity, memory, control-plane complexity — bounding the coherent photonics roadmap.

§2 reviews MZI physics and decomposition theorems; §3 specifies the device, noise, and training models; §4 dives into mesh compilation, coherent neuron design, optical backpropagation, and noise physics; §5 states formal results and the empirical record; §6 and §7 cover limitations and the research frontier.

##2 Background

### 2.1 The Mach–Zehnder interferometer as a 2×2 unitary gate

The MZI splits light with a 50:50 directional coupler, applies a differential phase shift θ between the arms, recombines with a second coupler, and adds an external phase shift φ on one output port. Up to global phase conventions, it realizes the 2×2 unitary

$$T(\theta, \phi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2) & e^{i\phi}\cos(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix},$$

whose magnitudes trace the familiar sinusoidal transmission curves. By sweeping θ ∈ [0, 2π), the MZI continuously interpolates between the identity and the swap (cross) operation, while φ sets the output phase. This single device is therefore a *universal* U(2) gate: any 2×2 unitary can be synthesized from one MZI plus fixed phase conventions [1][2].

In silicon photonics, phase shifters are thermo-optic (resistive heaters exploiting silicon's dn/dT ≈ 1.8×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹) or electro-optic (faster, lossier). Shen *et al.* used 56 thermo-optic MZIs for the first end-to-end coherent optical neural network, reaching 77% accuracy on a 4-vowel task [3].

### 2.2 From unitaries to arbitrary matrices: the SVD trick

A weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^(N×M) is generally *not* unitary; the enabling observation is the singular-value decomposition:

$$W = U\Sigma V^{\dagger},$$

where U and V are unitary and Σ is a nonnegative diagonal matrix of singular values. A coherent photonic circuit implements W by cascading three stages [3][8]: an MZI mesh realizing V†, a row of attenuators realizing Σ (the only stage that discards power), and an MZI mesh realizing U.

Each unitary factor is compiled into MZIs with the **Reck scheme** — a triangular cascade nulling entries column by column, proven universal by Reck *et al.* in 1994 [1] — or the **Clements scheme**, a symmetric rectangular lattice in which every path traverses the same number of MZIs, improving loss uniformity and noise balance [2]. A universal N×N unitary needs exactly N(N−1)/2 MZIs, which is optimal: U(N) has N² real degrees of freedom and each MZI contributes two (θ, φ).

> **Theorem:** *An MZI mesh with N(N−1)/2 programmable interferometers in the Reck or Clements topology can realize any matrix in U(N), and hence — via the SVD construction — any complex matrix up to the overall optical loss of the Σ stage.*

##3 Methodology



**Device model.** Each MZI follows the transfer matrix T(θ, φ) of §2.1; the mesh implements W(Θ) as the ordered product of embedded 2×2 blocks. Fabrication imperfection enters as static coupler-ratio error (parametrized by an error angle ε per coupler) plus fixed insertion loss. Phase shifters obey θ = θ* + δθ(t), with θ* the programmed value and δθ(t) a slow stochastic drift from thermal fluctuations and heater-DAC quantization.

**Noise model.** We treat four noise channels, calibrated to published experimental characterizations [4][7][9]:

| Noise source | Physical origin | Typical magnitude | Time scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fabrication disorder | Coupler ratio errors, waveguide width variation | ε ~ 1–3% in splitting ratio | Static (per-chip) |
| Phase drift | Thermal fluctuations, heater DAC quantization | δθ ~ 10⁻²–10⁻¹ rad | ms – s |
| Thermal crosstalk | Heater-to-heater heat diffusion | coupled Δθ ~ 10⁻² rad | ms |
| Shot/technical noise | Photon statistics, laser RIN, detector TIA noise | SNR 7–15 per MVM stage | per-inference |

**Training protocols analyzed.** We compare (i) *offline training with in situ backpropagation*: exact optical gradients on the real device via the adjoint method [4]; (ii) *end-to-end optical backpropagation*: reversible bidirectional MVM blocks with an optically implemented nonlinearity [5]; (iii) *zeroth-order on-chip learning*: direct optimization of 2,500+ phase parameters under power constraints via sparse stochastic gradient estimators [6].

**Evaluation criteria.** Classification accuracy (MNIST/vowel benchmarks as reported), compute rate per axon (GMAC/s), energy per MAC, noise tolerance (accuracy retention at stated σ), and calibration overhead (control lines and characterization measurements versus N).

### 4.1 Mesh Compilation: Reck versus Clements Topologies

The compilation of a target unitary into physical phase settings is a classical O(N³) precomputation with deep consequences for noise. The **Reck scheme** [1] triangularizes the mesh: the path from input port 1 traverses N−1 MZIs while the path from port N traverses one. This asymmetry costs twice: *differential loss* across paths distorts the realized matrix, and fabrication errors in early (shared) MZIs corrupt far more matrix entries than errors in late MZIs, concentrating sensitivity.

The **Clements scheme** [2] places the identical N(N−1)/2 mixers in a symmetric rectangular lattice: every path traverses ~N/2 MZIs, balancing loss and error accumulation. Worst-case optical depth halves, and under fabrication disorder the variance of the realized-matrix error drops accordingly. The Clements layout is now the default for large programmable photonic processors [8].

A subtlety: nulling compilers assume *ideal* 50:50 couplers. With coupler error ε, residual matrix error scales as O(ε√N) for Reck versus O(ε√(N/2)) for Clements in typical disorder models. Self-configuring meshes — notably Miller's self-aligning architectures, which program each MZI by locally minimizing power at a monitor port — sidestep global compilation: the mesh discovers its own settings by local feedback, automatically absorbing static fabrication error into the programmed phases [2][8].

```python
# Nulling-based compilation of a unitary into Clements-mesh phases (schematic)
import numpy as np

def clements_nulling(U: np.ndarray):
    """Return (thetas, phis) s.t. the Clements mesh realizes U.
    U: N x N unitary. Complexity O(N^3); each nulling step is a 2x2 solve."""
    N = U.shape[0]
    thetas, phis = [], []
    V = U.copy()
    # symmetric nulling order: alternate top-left / bottom-left diagonals
    for k in range(2 * N - 3):
        for i, j in nulling_order(N, k):          # (i, j): entry V[i, j] to null
            # solve 2x2: T(theta, phi) @ [V[i-1,j], V[i,j]] -> [*, 0]
            theta = 2 * np.arctan2(abs(V[i, j]), abs(V[i-1, j]))
            phi = np.angle(V[i-1, j]) - np.angle(V[i, j])
            thetas.append(theta); phis.append(phi)
            V[[i-1, i], :] = T2(theta, phi) @ V[[i-1, i], :]
    return thetas, phis  # N(N-1)/2 pairs, one per MZI
```

### 4.2 Coherent Neuron Design: Beyond Cascaded MZIs

Shen *et al.*'s original architecture [3] encoded each weight with a *cascade* of MZIs, since the SVD construction maps every matrix entry through shared interferometers. A later reformulation — the **dual-IQ-modulator coherent linear neuron** — replaces the cascade with a single phase shifter plus a single amplitude modulator per weight [7]: one weight, two control knobs, no cascade.

The noise implications are decisive. In a cascaded-MZI weight, one synapse requires setting several MZIs, so its effective error is the *sum* of several devices' phase errors. In the dual-IQ scheme, w = a·e^{iφ} is set directly, and measured results are dramatic: noise-resilient deep learning at a record 10 GMAC/s per axon, with >99% MNIST accuracy at 5 GMAC/s/axon and >98% at 10 GMAC/s/axon, *even with σ = 0.4 input noise* — six orders of magnitude beyond prior coherent demonstrations in per-axon rate and >7% in accuracy [7]. The lesson generalizes: in analog photonics, *architectural* noise tolerance (fewer devices per degree of freedom) beats *algorithmic* compensation, though the two combine multiplicatively.

### 4.3 In Situ Backpropagation: The Adjoint-Variable Derivation

Training is the hard problem. Simulating an N×N photonic mesh digitally to compute gradients costs O(N²) per layer per step and — fatally — requires a *model* of the physical device that is never exact; digitally computed gradients are gradients of the wrong function. Hughes *et al.* resolved this with a photonic analogue of backpropagation derived from **adjoint-variable methods** [4].

Consider a mesh implementing **y** = f(**x**; Θ) with loss L(**y**). For a phase shifter θ, the chain rule through the scattering matrix S(Θ) gives

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta} = 2\,\mathrm{Re}\left[ \left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{y}}\right)^{\!\dagger} \frac{\partial S}{\partial \theta}\, \mathbf{x} \right].$$

The key physical insight is **reciprocity**: the adjoint field — the error signal propagated backward — is realized *physically* by injecting the time-reversed error vector into the output ports. The gradient factorizes into two *locally measurable* quantities: the forward field and the adjoint field at the phase shifter, both obtained by *intensity measurements inside the device*:

> **Theorem:** *The exact gradient of any differentiable loss with respect to every phase-shifter setting in a photonic mesh can be obtained from three optical measurements — one forward pass, one adjoint (backward) pass, and local intensity readouts — without any digital model of the device's imperfections [4].*

Concretely, each training iteration is: (1) **forward pass** — inject **x**, measure **y**, compute the error vector δ = ∂L/∂**y** digitally; (2) **adjoint pass** — inject the phase-conjugated δ* into the *output* ports and let the reciprocal mesh propagate it backward; (3) **local readout** — at each phase shifter, measure |E_fwd|², |E_adj|², and |E_fwd + E_adj|², whose algebraic combination is exactly ∂L/∂θ; (4) **update** — θ ← θ − η·∂L/∂θ via the heater DAC.

Hughes *et al.* demonstrated this in simulation on a numerically modeled photonic network, showing convergence matching digital backpropagation while remaining exact under device imperfections — because the device *is* the model [4]. Measurement overhead is constant in network depth: every phase shifter's gradient is extracted in parallel from the same two optical passes.

### 4.4 Noise Physics: Phase Drift, Crosstalk, and the Shot-Noise Floor

Four noise channels bound achievable precision, and each demands a different mitigation.

**Phase drift.** Thermo-optic shifters drift with ambient temperature and finite heater-DAC resolution (a 10-bit DAC over 2π quantizes to ~6 mrad). Because a coherent mesh is an interferometer, errors *compound*: an N-deep cascade accumulates phase-error variance linearly in depth. Mitigation is twofold — *material* (athermal designs, electro-optic shifters with no static power) and *algorithmic* (periodic recalibration, drift-aware training that injects δθ noise during optimization so learned phases sit in flat minima) [6][7].

**Thermal crosstalk.** Heaters warm their neighbors through the silicon substrate, coupling phases via a dense, chip-dependent matrix K. Sparse zeroth-order training [6] handles this implicitly — the optimizer descends the *true* coupled landscape — and reports >90% power savings under realistic crosstalk by pruning heaters of small marginal contribution.

**Fabrication disorder.** Coupler splitting ratios vary ~1–3% across a wafer; waveguide widths vary at the nanometer scale. Unlike drift, disorder is *static*: it is calibrated once and absorbed into programmed phases, either by self-configuring local feedback or by training on the physical hardware so the disorder becomes part of the learned solution [5][6].

**Shot noise and the sub-photon regime.** The most surprising result in the field is how little light is needed. Wang *et al.* demonstrated MNIST classification with **less than one detected photon per scalar multiplication**, attributing robustness to statistical averaging: dot products over larger vectors average over more terms, so per-multiplication noise falls as 1/√N [9]. With quantization-aware training adapted to analog noise, the network retained high accuracy deep into the photon-starved regime. The implication: the energy floor of photonic inference is set not by interference physics but by the *statistics* of large fan-in, favoring wide layers.

```rust
// Noise-aware training loop sketch: inject device-faithful noise during optimization
// so learned phases settle in flat, drift-tolerant minima (after [6][7]).
fn train_step(mesh: &mut PhotonicMesh, batch: &Batch, opt: &mut SparseZO) {
    let noisy_mesh = mesh.with_noise(NoiseModel {
        phase_drift: 0.02,        // rad, thermal wander
        coupler_error: 0.02,      // splitting-ratio disorder
        thermal_crosstalk: K,     // dense coupling matrix
        shot_snr: 12.0,           // dB per MVM stage
    });
    let grad_est = opt.zeroth_order_estimate(&noisy_mesh, batch); // sparse, power-aware
    opt.apply_power_aware_update(mesh, grad_est);                  // prune weak heaters
}
```

##5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Formal results

We collect the chapter's formal claims with their precise content.

> **Theorem (Universality):** *For any U ∈ U(N), there exist phase settings {θ_k, φ_k}, k = 1…N(N−1)/2, such that the Reck triangular mesh or the Clements rectangular mesh realizes U exactly in the lossless limit [1][2].*

> **Theorem (SVD compilation):** *Any complex matrix W ∈ ℂ^(N×M) factors as W = UΣV†; the photonic circuit U-mesh → Σ-attenuators → V†-mesh realizes W up to the (measurable) attenuation of the Σ stage [3][8].*

> **Theorem (Adjoint gradients):** *For a reciprocal photonic mesh, ∂L/∂θ_k is exactly recoverable from one forward optical pass, one adjoint optical pass, and local intensity measurements at each phase shifter — no device model required [4].*

> **Theorem (Complexity):** *A forward MVM through an N×N mesh executes in waveguide-transit time with O(N) I/O transduction cost; gradients for all N(N−1)/2 phases need O(1) additional optical passes [3][4].*

The last theorem is the economic core of the field: optics converts the O(N²) digital cost of both inference *and* gradient computation into O(1) physical time, leaving only the O(N) cost of getting data on and off the chip.

### 5.2 The empirical record

|---|---|---|---|

Two patterns emerge. First, *accuracy is no longer the question*: with noise-aware training and noise-tolerant architectures, photonic networks match digital baselines on standard benchmarks [7]. Second, *the frontier has moved to training and scale*: the open problems are efficient on-chip learning at million-parameter scale and the control-plane engineering (DACs, ADCs, thermal management) that currently dominates system power.

The end-to-end optical backpropagation demonstration [5] is the most radical entry: the MVM blocks are *reversible* — the backward pass is literally light traveling the other way — and the hidden-layer nonlinearity is a rubidium vapor cell acting as a saturable absorber. Fabrication errors and environmental drift are mitigated *on the run* during training, with no precalibration step: the closest realization to date of a network that is *made of* its own training algorithm.

##6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what coherent photonic accelerators *cannot* yet do, and what they may never do well.

1. **Weak on-chip nonlinearity.** Linear optics is easy; nonlinear optics at femtojoule energies is hard. Saturable absorbers and electro-optic feedback loops are the least mature part of the stack, and every optical-electrical-optical conversion reintroduces the transduction costs photonics seeks to avoid [5][8].
2. **No dense on-chip memory.** Photonic weights are *volatile*: thermo-optic phases must be actively maintained (~mW per MZI → watts for large meshes), while electro-optic and phase-change alternatives trade speed, loss, or endurance. There is no photonic analogue of SRAM density; weight storage remains electronic.
3. **The O(N²) control plane.** A universal N×N mesh needs N(N−1)/2 MZIs, each requiring a DAC channel, a monitor photodiode, and thermal isolation. Control wiring and calibration scale with device count, and thermal crosstalk couples the control problem densely [6]. Meshes beyond ~64×64 ports strain packaging and control electronics more than optics.
4. **Precision ceiling.** Analog photonic MVMs achieve ~5–8 effective bits in practice, limited by the noise channels of §4.4. Training must be noise- or quantization-aware [7][9]; float32-fidelity workloads are out of reach.
5. **Fabrication and packaging economics.** Large meshes demand exceptional process uniformity, sub-dB fiber-to-chip coupling, and co-packaged low-RIN lasers — a supply chain far less mature than the GPU ecosystem.
6. **Limited programmability.** Recompiling a mesh for a new matrix costs O(N³) classical precomputation plus O(N²) phase updates; nanosecond-scale weight switching remains impractical with thermo-optic tuning.

These limits circumscribe where photonic acceleration wins: *inference-dominated, wide-and-shallow, noise-tolerant workloads* — the profile of large-scale neural-network serving, where fixed weights serve millions of queries and the O(1) optical MVM amortizes every transduction cost.

##7 Conclusion

Coherent photonic neural-network accelerators have crossed from physics demonstration to engineering discipline. The theory is complete: universal unitary realization via Reck and Clements decompositions [1][2], arbitrary-matrix compilation through the SVD construction [3], and exact on-chip gradients via the adjoint-variable method of in situ backpropagation [4]. The empirical record includes sub-photon-per-multiplication inference [9], >99% MNIST accuracy at 10 GMAC/s per axon under severe noise [7], fully optical end-to-end backpropagation with no precalibration [5], and zeroth-order on-chip learning past 2,500 components with >90% power savings [6].

The deepest lesson is physical. Digital computing treats noise as an enemy to suppress; photonic computing — built on interference, the most noise-sensitive phenomenon in classical physics — has learned to *absorb* noise into training. Noise-aware optimization, drift-tolerant flat minima, self-configuring meshes, and the statistical grace of large fan-in convert the analog substrate's greatest weakness into a design parameter. The photon need not be perfect. It only needs to be *abundant enough to vote*.

The frontier is the control plane and the nonlinearity: million-MZI meshes with non-volatile zero-static-power phase storage, all-optical nonlinearities at attojoule energies, and training algorithms that treat the physical device not as an imperfect computer but as the computer itself. When those arrive, the matrix–vector product — the inner loop of intelligence — will run at the speed of light.

Three bets define the next five years:

- *Non-volatile phase storage* — phase-change materials or MEMS actuators eliminating static heater power;
- *All-optical nonlinearities* at attojoule energies, closing the activation-function gap;
- *Device-native training* — zeroth-order and adjoint methods that treat fabrication disorder as a feature rather than a bug [4][6].
