# Masking Countermeasures against Side-Channel Analysis: ISW Probing Security, Higher-Order Composition Theory, and Formal Verification with maskVerif and SILVER

## Abstract

Masking is the most widely deployed countermeasure against differential power analysis. This thesis unifies the masking discipline from information-theoretic foundations to machine-checked verification. We present the **t-probing model** of Ishai, Sahai, and Wagner, whose simulation paradigm reduced higher-order security proofs to a constructive argument, and derive the ISW masked multiplication gadget, its quadratic cost, and the quasilinear refinements via wire shuffling and DFT techniques that followed [1,2,3]. We develop the compositional security notions — **Non-Interference, Strong Non-Interference, and Probe-Isolating Non-Interference** — that turn gadget-level proofs into circuit-level guarantees [4,5], and explain the reductions from probing security to **noisy leakage** and **random probing** models [6]. We then cover formal verification: **maskVerif**'s probabilistic non-interference engine, **SILVER**'s exact ROBDD-based statistical-independence checks under glitches, and the Prover tool reconciling their speed–accuracy trade-off [7,8,9]. A worked proof sketch, tool benchmarks, and a candid discussion of physical defaults, randomness assumptions, and scalability walls complete the treatment.

## 1 Introduction

Cryptographic algorithms assume a *black-box* adversary that sees only inputs and outputs. Physical implementations, however, *leak*: CMOS switching activity, EM emanations, and cache timing all encode intermediate values — since Kocher's DPA, it has been clear that sound ciphers can be undone by their own physics [1]. **Masking** counters this by *secret sharing* every sensitive value: a secret \(x\) is split into \(d+1\) shares with \(x = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{d} x_i\), so any strict subset of shares is independent of \(x\), and computation proceeds share-wise or through *masked gadgets* that never recombine a full sharing without fresh randomness.

The central question is *how to prove* that a masked circuit is secure. A naive proof of \(t\)-th-order security demands analyzing \(\binom{N}{t}\) tuples of probed wires — exponential in the masking order and infeasible even for modest \(t\). The 2003 breakthrough of **Ishai, Sahai, and Wagner** replaced this enumeration with a *simulation paradigm*: to show that \(t\) probed wires leak nothing, exhibit a simulator that reproduces their joint distribution from a strict subset of the input shares, without knowledge of the secret [1]. This single idea reorganized the entire field. It yielded:

- **Security notions with composability**: NI, SNI, PINI — gadget-level proofs compose into circuit-level guarantees [4,5].
- **Efficiency results**: the ISW AND gadget costs \(\mathcal{O}(t^2)\); wire shuffling reduced statistical security to \(\mathcal{O}(t)\), DFT-based constructions to quasi-linear [2,3].
- **Model reductions**: probing security implies noisy-leakage and random-probing security, bridging proof-friendly and realistic leakage [6].
- **Automation**: from maskVerif's probabilistic non-interference checks to SILVER's exact glitch-aware independence analysis, formal tools now discharge proofs no human could complete by hand [7,8,9].

This thesis unifies these strands. Section 2 fixes notation and the ISW model. Section 3 develops share-wise computation and the ISW multiplication gadget. Section 4 dives into composition notions, glitch-aware probing, leakage-model reductions, and refresh strategies. Section 5 gives a proof sketch and verification evidence. Section 6 confronts limitations — physical defaults the model omits and the scalability walls tools still hit. Section 7 concludes with research directions.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Secret sharing and the adversary model

Let \(\mathbb{F}\) be a finite field (in practice \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) or \(\mathrm{GF}(2^n)\)). A *\(d\)-th-order Boolean masking* of \(x \in \mathbb{F}\) is a tuple \(\mathbf{x} = (x_0,\ldots,x_d)\) with \(\sum_i x_i = x\) and \((x_0,\ldots,x_{d-1})\) uniform and independent. The **t-probing adversary** (ISW, \(t = d\)) chooses up to \(t\) wires and learns their exact values [1]. A masked circuit is *\(t\)-probing secure* if, for every choice of \(\le t\) probes, the joint distribution of the probed wires is independent of the secret inputs — equivalently, *simulatable* from at most \(t\) shares of each input sharing.

> **Theorem: ISW simulation lemma.** Let \(G\) be a gadget whose every set of at most \(t\) internal wires can be perfectly simulated from at most \(t\) shares of each input sharing. Then no \(t\)-probing adversary distinguishes the gadget's leakage for two different secret inputs; \(G\) is \(t\)-probing secure.

### 2.2 Linear versus non-linear gadgets

Linear operations (XOR, rotations, share permutations) are *free*: applied share-wise as \(z_i = x_i \oplus y_i\), they need no randomness and no cross-share interaction. The **non-linear gadget** — the masked AND — is where all difficulty concentrates: any cross term \(a_i \cdot b_j\) with \(i \ne j\) mixes share positions and must be masked by fresh randomness before recombination.

### 2.3 Leakage models beyond probing

The probing model is an *abstraction*: real adversaries observe *noisy* functions of *all* wires, not exact values of a few. Three refinements matter:

1. **Noisy leakage model**: each wire leaks \(L(v) = \phi(v) + \mathcal{N}\).
2. **Random probing model**: each wire leaks independently with probability \(p\); Duc et al. reduced noisy leakage to (random) probing [6].
3. **Robust probing model**: extends probing with *glitches*, *transitions*, and *couplings* — the *physical defaults* that break idealized proofs [7].

A proof strategy must declare *which* model it targets; standard-model results do not automatically survive synthesis into glitchy hardware.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the canonical pipeline: (i) fix leakage model and security order; (ii) decompose the cipher into *gadgets* (refresh, masked AND, share-wise linear layers); (iii) prove each gadget secure in the ISW simulation sense, possibly tool-assisted; (iv) compose via an SNI/PINI notion whose theorem covers the chosen model, glitches included for hardware targets; (v) validate with maskVerif/SILVER plus empirical TVLA \(t\)-tests on the final netlist.

### 3.1 The ISW multiplication gadget

For sharings \(\mathbf{a} = (a_0,\ldots,a_t)\) and \(\mathbf{b} = (b_0,\ldots,b_t)\) of \(a, b \in \mathrm{GF}(2)\), ISW compute shares \(\mathbf{c}\) of \(c = a \cdot b\) as follows [1]. For each \(i\), initialize \(c_i = a_i b_i\). Then for every pair \(i < j\), sample a fresh uniform bit \(r_{i,j}\) and update

\[
c_i \mathrel{+}= r_{i,j}, \qquad c_j \mathrel{+}= (r_{i,j} \oplus a_i b_j \oplus a_j b_i).
\]

Correctness: each cross term is added exactly once, so \(\sum_i c_i = \sum_{i,j} a_i b_j = ab\). The simulator argument is the heart of the matter: fix any set \(P\) of at most \(t\) probes. Each probe is either an input share (requiring that share), a partial product \(a_i b_j\) (requiring shares \(i\) of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(j\) of \(\mathbf{b}\)), or a masked intermediate \(r_{i,j} \oplus \cdots\) (simulatable as uniform randomness). A counting argument shows the union of required input shares has size at most \(t\) per sharing [1,2]:

```python
def isw_and(a_shares, b_shares, t, rng):
    """ISW masked AND, order t. a_shares/b_shares: lists of t+1 bits."""
    c = [a_shares[i] & b_shares[i] for i in range(t + 1)]
    for i in range(t + 1):
        for j in range(i + 1, t + 1):
            r = rng.getrandbits(1)
            c[i] ^= r
            c[j] ^= r ^ (a_shares[i] & b_shares[j]) ^ (a_shares[j] & b_shares[i])
    return c
```

The gadget consumes \(\binom{t+1}{2}\) random bits and \(\mathcal{O}(t^2)\) gates — *quadratic* in the order. Coron and co-workers showed that perfect-simulation probing security requires \(\Omega(t^2)\) randomness in general, while *statistical* simulation can be cheaper: \(\mathcal{O}(t)\) via wire shuffling [2,3].

### 3.2 Share-wise methodology and refresh discipline

In practice, ciphers alternate linear layers (free, share-wise) and non-linear layers (S-boxes built from masked ANDs). Because SNI/PINI composition requires uniform output sharings, a **refresh** gadget is inserted between multiplications, re-randomizing \(\mathbf{x}\) as \(y_i = x_i \oplus r_i\) (\(i<t\)), \(y_t = x_t \oplus \bigoplus_{i<t} r_i\). Refresh discipline — *where* refreshes are mandatory — is dictated by the composition notion: SNI needs refreshing before non-linear fan-out; PINI gadgets compose *trivially* with no refreshes at all [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The composition problem: NI, SNI, and why plain probing security does not compose

A subtle and historically painful fact: **\(t\)-probing security is not compositional.** Two individually \(t\)-probing-secure circuits \(C_1, C_2\), wired output-to-input, can jointly leak: the adversary's \(t_1\) probes in \(C_1\) and \(t_2 = t - t_1\) in \(C_2\) may demand more than \(t\) input shares to simulate jointly. Barthe et al. resolved this with **Non-Interference (NI)** and **Strong Non-Interference (SNI)** [4]:

> **Theorem: SNI composition.** A gadget \(G\) is \(t\)-SNI if any set of \(t_1\) internal probes plus \(t_2\) output probes, with \(t_1 + t_2 \le t\), can be simulated from at most \(t_1\) shares of each input. A circuit built by composing \(t\)-SNI gadgets (with at most one input sharing per gadget fed from another gadget's outputs, and fresh refreshes where needed) is \(t\)-probing secure.

The key innovation is that the simulation budget counts *only internal probes* (\(t_1\)), not output probes — output probes are "free" because the output sharing is uniform. NI is the weaker variant where the budget is \(t_1 + t_2 \le t\). The hierarchy and its practical consequences:

| Notion | Simulation budget | Composes | Glitch-robust | Refresh cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(t\)-probing | \(t\) shares total | ✗ (no) | ✗ | — |
| \(t\)-NI | \(t_1 + t_2 \le t\) | with care | ✗ | moderate |
| \(t\)-SNI | \(t_1 \le t\) (outputs free) | ✓ sequential | ✗ | high |
| \(t\)-PINI | per-share isolation | ✓ trivial | ✓ (HPC) | none |

### 4.2 PINI: probe isolation and trivial composition

Cassiers and Standaert observed that SNI composition, while sound, is *operationally* expensive: it demands careful refresh placement and forbids natural circuit structures like multi-output gadgets [5]. **Probe-Isolating Non-Interference (PINI)** replaces the budget-counting discipline with a *structural* invariant:

> **Definition (t-PINI, informal).** A gadget is \(t\)-PINI if every set of at most \(t\) probes is simulatable using only input shares carrying the *same share index* — probes never force the simulator to mix share domains.

In a PINI gadget, share \(i\) of the output depends only on share \(i\) of the inputs, so composing PINI gadgets *cannot* create cross-share recombination paths: probing \(t\) wires anywhere is simulatable index-by-index. Composition becomes **trivial** — concatenate gadgets, no refresh analysis, no budget bookkeeping — and extends naturally to MIMO gadgets, which SNI handles poorly [5]. The price is implementation cost: PINI multipliers (e.g., the HPC family) use more randomness and area than SNI counterparts, but they are **robust-probing secure** (glitch-resistant), whereas plain standard-model PINI gadgets like unregistered DOM multipliers are not [8].

### 4.3 The robust probing model: glitches, transitions, couplings

A combinational AND gate does not evaluate atomically: within a clock cycle its output may *glitch*, transiently computing functions of different input subsets before settling. A probe on that wire then observes a *sequence* of values — potentially recombining shares the designer believed time-separated. The **robust probing model** extends each standard probe with a *glitch-extended* probe observing the gate's full transient behavior, plus *transition* probes on registers (jointly observing consecutive values) and *coupling* effects [7].

This is where formal verification earns its keep: the flaw in the DOM multiplier's SNI claim under glitches — misclassified by maskVerif as secure at order 2 (the PARA3 example) — was caught exactly by SILVER's glitch-extended independence checks [8]. Hand proofs of glitch behavior are notoriously unreliable; transient semantics involve \(\mathcal{O}(2^t)\) input combinations per gate.

### 4.4 From probing to reality: noisy leakage and random probing reductions

Why prove anything in the probing model at all, if real adversaries see noise on *every* wire? Duc, Dziembowski, and Faust gave the answer: a **reduction** [6]. If a circuit is secure against a \(t\)-probing adversary, then under mild noise assumptions it is secure against an adversary observing a noisy function of *all* wires — the required noise level growing exponentially in \(t\) (the Chari et al. amplification). The **random probing model** sharpens this: each wire leaks independently with probability \(p\), and \(p\)-random-probing security *implies* noisy-leakage security, with recent work removing the field-size dependence of the noise parameter [6]. Practically: prove the gadget \(t\)-probing secure (the *tractable* target); the reduction lifts the guarantee to noisy leakage (the *realistic* target) at a quantified noise requirement; choose \(t\) so the device's physical noise exceeds it with margin.

The chain *ISW simulation → SNI/PINI composition → random-probing/noisy-leakage reduction* is the complete theoretical pipeline from a single AND gate to a side-channel-resilient AES engine.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Worked proof sketch: ISW refresh is 1-NI

We prove the 2-share ISW refresh (\(t=1\)) is 1-NI. With inputs \((x_0, x_1)\) (\(x_0\) uniform), randomness \(r\), and outputs \(y_0 = x_0 \oplus r\), \(y_1 = x_1 \oplus r\), consider any single probe \(P \in \{x_0, x_1, r, y_0, y_1\}\).

- \(P = x_0\): simulate from input share \(x_0\) (budget 1 ≤ 1). ✓
- \(P = x_1\): simulate from \(x_1\). ✓
- \(P = r\): uniform bit, simulatable with no input. ✓
- \(P = y_0 = x_0 \oplus r\): uniform since \(r\) is uniform and independent; simulate as a fresh random bit. ✓
- \(P = y_1 = x_1 \oplus r\): likewise uniform. ✓

Every probe is simulatable from at most one input share (or none): the gadget is 1-NI. This two-line argument is the *entire* proof technique, scaled by tooling to circuits with thousands of wires.

### 5.2 Formal verification: maskVerif

**maskVerif** (Barthe et al., ESORICS 2019; ePrint 2018/562) recasts probing security as *probabilistic non-interference* [7]. The tool enumerates observable sets \(O\) of size ≤ \(t\) (pruned by dependency analysis and the *optimistic sampling* rule, which substitutes \(a \oplus m\) by the fresh mask \(m\)), then checks symbolically whether the joint distribution of \(O\) depends on secrets. It supports NI, SNI, and \(t\)-probing notions plus glitch/transition extensions. maskVerif is *fast* — higher-order AES S-boxes in seconds — but *incomplete*: syntactic independence reasoning yields false positives (e.g., the PARA3 gadget reported "not SNI" though it is 2-SNI) [8,9]. Its reasoning core, simplified:

```haskell
-- maskVerif-style independence judgment (simplified)
data Expr = Secret Int | Mask Int | Xor Expr Expr | And Expr Expr

independent :: [Expr] -> Bool
independent obs
  | any hasSecret obs = all maskedByFresh obs   -- optimistic sampling
  | otherwise         = True
  where
    hasSecret (Secret _) = True
    hasSecret (Xor a b)  = hasSecret a || hasSecret b
    hasSecret (And a b)  = hasSecret a || hasSecret b
    hasSecret _          = False
    maskedByFresh e = -- e = s xor m with m fresh and unused elsewhere
      case normalize e of
        Xor s (Mask m) -> hasSecret s && freshIn m obs
        _              -> False
```

### 5.3 Formal verification: SILVER and the exact approach

**SILVER** (Knichel, Moradi, Sasdrich, ASIACRYPT 2020) takes the opposite trade-off: *exactness over speed* [8]. It builds the circuit's Boolean functions as **ROBDDs** and checks *statistical independence* semantically — \(\Pr[O = o \mid k] = \Pr[O = o]\) for all secrets \(k\) — over every observable set of size ≤ \(t\), in both the standard and glitch-extended probing models. It additionally verifies output-sharing *uniformity* and reports *weak nets*. Semantic checking means no false positives; enumerating \(\sum_{i=1}^{t}\binom{p}{i}\) observable sets (\(p\) = probe positions) means poor scaling — a second-order AES S-box is feasible, a fifth-order one took ~47 days in reported benchmarks [9].

The tools are therefore *complementary*: design with PINI/HPC gadgets, prove composition by hand or type system, verify with maskVerif for speed, and confirm critical gadgets with SILVER for exactness under glitches. **Prover** (Zhou et al., TCHES 2025) narrows the gap with reduction rules that shrink observable and secret sets before ROBDD construction plus variable-ordering optimization, verifying S-boxes that defeat both maskVerif (false positive) and SILVER (timeout) [9].

| Tool | Method | Glitches | Exact? | Scales to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| maskVerif [7] | probabilistic non-interference, syntactic | ✓ (model) | ✗ (false +) | high-order AES S-box |
| SILVER [8] | ROBDD statistical independence | ✓ (robust) | ✓ | 2nd-order AES S-box |
| Prover [9] | ROBDD + reduction rules | ✓ | ✓ | S-boxes beating both |
| CheckMasks | circuit transforms | ✗ | ✗ | small gadgets |

### 5.4 Empirical leakage assessment

Formal proofs live in models; *leakage assessment* tests the silicon. The standard methodology is **TVLA** (test vector leakage assessment): collect power traces for fixed vs. random plaintexts and apply Welch's \(t\)-test; \(|t| > 4.5\) indicates first-order leakage, with pre-processing (e.g., centered products) for higher orders. A complete workflow closes the loop: *prove* in the probing model → *reduce* to noisy leakage → *verify* with SILVER/maskVerif → *measure* with TVLA on FPGA/ASIC. Discrepancies between the last two steps invariably point at unmodeled physical defaults — exactly the gap the robust probing model was created to close.

## 6 Limitations

**The independence assumption on randomness.** Every proof above assumes fresh, uniform, independent random bits. Real RNGs have bias, and randomness *reuse* across gadgets silently voids simulation arguments. Masking's randomness cost — \(\Theta(t^2)\) bits per multiplication at order \(t\) — dominates area and power in high-order designs.

**Transitions and micro-architecture.** The robust model covers glitches and register transitions, but *software* masking faces pipeline forwarding, bus precharge, and address-dependent leakage that no gate-level model captures; tools like CocoAlma address this at the cost of CPU-specific modeling.

**Composition boundaries.** SNI/PINI theorems assume gadgets communicate *only* through their interfaces. Shared randomness sources, shared memory, or a synthesizer merging logic across gadget boundaries all invalidate the proof — a frequent source of real-world failures.

**Scalability walls.** Exact verification is exponential in \(t\) and circuit size; 5th-order full AES verification remains out of reach for exact tools, forcing reliance on incomplete or bounded methods.

**Model-reality gap.** The noisy-leakage reduction gives *asymptotic* comfort but *quantitative* pain: the noise levels it demands at high \(t\) can exceed what a device provides, and the reduction's constants are rarely computed for concrete implementations.

## 7 Conclusion

Two decades after Ishai, Sahai, and Wagner, masking has matured from a clever trick into a *rigorous engineering discipline* with a full stack: the simulation-based proof method [1], its efficiency refinements [2,3], the compositional notions NI/SNI/PINI that make large designs provable [4,5], the reductions transporting probing guarantees into noisy physical reality [6], and the formal tools — maskVerif's speed, SILVER's exactness, Prover's reconciliation — that discharge proofs at scales no human can [7,8,9]. The trajectory is clear: *security by construction* (PINI gadgets, trivial composition) plus *verification by computation* (ROBDD independence checks under glitch semantics) is replacing the old craft of hand-proving masked S-boxes.

Open frontiers remain. Post-quantum schemes (Kyber, Dilithium) demand masking over *arithmetic* rings \(\mathbb{Z}_q\), where Boolean/arithmetic conversion and prime-field PINI are active research. Leakage models keep evolving toward fine-grained physical effects — couplings, aging, cross-device variability. And the quantitative question — *how much noise is enough, exactly, for this netlist?* — still lacks an automated answer. The probing model gave us the language; the next decade's task is to make its guarantees *measurable*.
