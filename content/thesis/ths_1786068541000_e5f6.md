# Unbounded Neural Radiance Fields from Sparse Views: Integrated Positional Encoding in Mip-NeRF 360, Multi-Resolution Hash Grids in Instant-NGP, and Visibility Regularization for Few-Shot Novel View Synthesis

## Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) degrade under unbounded 360-degree capture and sparse inputs, causing aliasing, floaters, and overfitting. This thesis unifies three advances that jointly resolve these failures: Mip-NeRF 360's non-linear contraction and distortion regularizer for unbounded scenes [1], Mip-NeRF's integrated positional encoding (IPE) over conical frustums for scale-aware anti-aliasing [5], and Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding for 5-second training with learned collision handling [2]. We connect these to few-shot priors: frequency annealing in FreeNeRF [3], ray entropy in InfoNeRF, and depth smoothness in RegNeRF [7]. We derive the Gaussian frustum integral, analyze hash grid domain manipulation, and derive sparsity priors that compact depth without external sensors. Results show 57% MSE reduction over mip-NeRF on 360 data, 300× speedup via hash tables, and 3-view PSNR from 11 dB to 22 dB with free regularizers. Code in Python/Rust/Haskell is provided.

## 1. Introduction

Novel view synthesis learns $F: (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ and integrates via volume rendering [4]. Original NeRF uses MLP with sinusoidal encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{p})=[\sin(2^\ell\mathbf{p}),\cos(2^\ell\mathbf{p})]$, enabling high-frequency learning but failing under:

- **Unbounded ambiguity:** Depth in $[0.1,\infty)$. NDC maps invert for 360° captures; distant content gets equal capacity as foreground, causing background collapse [1].
- **Scale aliasing:** NeRF samples points, not areas. Pixel footprint grows with distance; mip-map theory ignored, causing shimmer [5].
- **Speed:** 1–2 days per scene on V100.
- **Few-shot overfitting:** With 3–8 views, density concentrates near cameras creating floaters [3][7].

> **Core claim:** Anti-aliased frustum integration, compressed learned grids, and empty-space regularization are complementary bandwidth controls: spatial bandwidth (IPE), parameter bandwidth (hash grid), and occupancy bandwidth (distortion/occlusion).

Contributions:

1. Derivation of IPE as Gaussian expectation with closed-form damping.
2. Analysis of hash encoding as domain manipulation creating synthetic linear segments.
3. Formalization of Mip-NeRF 360 contraction, proposal distillation, and $L_{dist}$.
4. Unification of few-shot regularizers in ≤20 LOC.
5. System for unbounded sparse NeRFs achieving interactive training.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Volume Rendering

For ray $\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}$:

$$
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int T(t)\sigma \mathbf{c} dt,\quad w_i = (1-e^{-\tau_i\delta_i})e^{-\sum_{j<i}\tau_j\delta_j}
$$

Learning minimizes photometric loss $\|\mathbf{C}-\mathbf{C}_{gt}\|^2$. PE lifts $\mathbb{R}^3$ to $\mathbb{R}^{6L}$ overcoming spectral bias.

### 2.2 Unbounded Scenes

NDC fails for 360° scenes where cameras surround object and background is mountains. Mip-NeRF 360 [1] proposes contraction leaving $||\mathbf{x}||\le1$ unchanged and mapping outside to radius 2:

$$
contract(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} \text{ if }||\mathbf{x}||\le1 \text{ else } (2-1/||\mathbf{x}||)\mathbf{x}/||\mathbf{x}||
$$

This allocates 50% capacity to infinite world compressed and enables Gaussian IPE in contracted space. Distortion regularizer prevents clouds.

### 2.3 Instant-NGP Hash Encoding

Instant-NGP [2] replaces $\gamma$ with learnable features in $L$ hash tables. Level $l$ has resolution $N_l=\lfloor N_{min} b^l\rfloor$, $T$ entries $2^{19}$, $F=2$. Query $\mathbf{x}$ finds 8 vertices, hashes via $h(\mathbf{x})=(\oplus x_i\pi_i)\bmod T$, tri-linear interpolates, concatenates $[interp_l]$ to shallow MLP (2×64). Training uses fully-fused CUDA kernels and occupancy grids skipping empty space.

> Paradox: Hash collisions should degrade; but 3D surfaces are sparse (<5% voxels), collisions occur in free space where gradient zero, so network learns to ignore.

### 2.4 Few-Shot Failure Modes

With $<10$ views, pathologies emerge [3][7]:

- **High-frequency overfit:** PE exposes all frequencies early; sparse views let MLP hallucinate.
- **Near-camera fog:** Density concentrates toward origin.
- **Shape-radiance ambiguity:** View-dependent color compensates wrong geometry.

FreeNeRF insight: frequency is currency — spend slowly. RegNeRF adds patch depth smoothness. DietNeRF uses CLIP. InfoNeRF penalizes entropy. We synthesize as *free lunches*.

## 3. Methodology

Our method is analytic synthesis plus constructive verification:

- **Step A — Unbounded parameterization:** Reimplement contraction vs NDC vs inverse sphere; measure PSNR by depth bins on Mip-NeRF 360 dataset (9 scenes, 100–330 images).
- **Step B — Anti-aliasing:** Derive conical frustum moments to IPE; compare scale error vs supersampled NeRF.
- **Step C — Hash grid:** Implement multi-res encoder in Python Taichi; ablate $L$, $T$, $N_{max}$; measure fill rate $\rho=\kappa/T$.
- **Step D — Sparse view:** Reimplement NeRF on Blender 8-view and LLFF 3-view; add frequency masking $m_\ell(t)=\mathbb{I}[\ell/L<t/T]$, occlusion penalty $w_k\exp(-\alpha d_k)$, distortion $L_{dist}$.
- **Step E — Unified integration:** Propose Zip-NeRF isotropic Gaussian downweighting to marry hash grids with IPE [6]; test proposal MLP distillation online.

Scope excludes dynamic scenes and diffusion priors.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Conical Frustum Integrated Positional Encoding

Mip-NeRF [5] replaces point $\mathbf{x}_i$ with conical frustum $F(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{d},\dot{r},t_0,t_1)$ where $\dot{r}$ is pixel radius scaled by focal length. Approximated by Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})$:

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu}= \mathbf{o}+ \mu_t\mathbf{d},\quad \Sigma = \sigma_t^2\mathbf{dd}^\top + \sigma_r^2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{dd}^\top)
$$

where $\sigma_t^2=(t_1-t_0)^2/12$, $\sigma_r^2=\dot{r}^2(\mu_t^2+\sigma_t^2)$ [5].

> **Theorem 1 (IPE Expectation):** For $\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})$, $\mathbb{E}[\sin(2^\ell\mathbf{x})]=\sin(2^\ell\boldsymbol{\mu})\exp(-0.5\cdot2^{2\ell}diag(\Sigma))$. Proof via characteristic function $\mathbb{E}[e^{i\omega^\top x}]=e^{i\omega^\top\mu-0.5\omega^\top\Sigma\omega}$.

Thus IPE damps high frequencies when Gaussian wide — automatic low-pass filter. Original PE is Dirac $\Sigma=0$ full frequency always.

```python
def integrated_pos_enc(mu, sigma_diag, L=10):
    feats=[]
    for ell in range(L):
        freq=2**ell
        amp=jnp.exp(-0.5*(freq**2)*sigma_diag)
        feats.append(jnp.sin(freq*mu)*amp)
        feats.append(jnp.cos(freq*mu)*amp)
    return jnp.concatenate(feats)
```

Cost same MLP dimension but 6 FLOPs extra per frequency. Visual impact: eliminates z-aliasing on checkerboard at varying distances — 60% error reduction [5].

*Italic observation:* *IPE makes MLP learn “stone wall at 2 cm footprint vs 2 m footprint as different textures”, disambiguating.*

**Bold insight:** Without IPE, same 3D coordinate gets contradictory colors from different distances, forcing blur. With IPE, MLP receives distance-conditioned encoding.

 Limitations: Gaussian breaks for wide-angle pixels near camera; anisotropic covariance diagonal approximation poor. Zip-NeRF improves via multisampling and zero-mean trick [6].

### 4.2 Multi-Resolution Hash Grids: Domain Manipulation

Instant-NGP's innovation is parameter efficiency via learned collision. For $N_l^3<T$ hash bijective; for $N_l^3\gg T$ hash maps many vertices to same feature, but interpolation smooths.

Spatial hash:

$$
h(\mathbf{x})= (x_1\pi_1\oplus x_2\pi_2\oplus x_3\pi_3)\bmod T
$$

with primes $1,2654435761,805459861$.

Rust hot loop:

```rust
fn spatial_hash(x:i32,y:i32,z:i32,t:usize)->usize{
    let mut h = (x as u64) ^ (y as u64).wrapping_mul(2654435761) ^ (z as u64).wrapping_mul(805459861);
    h ^= h>>33; h=h.wrapping_mul(0xff51afd7ed558ccd);
    (h as usize)%t
}
fn interp_level(grid:&[f32], xi:[i32;3], frac:[f32;3], t:usize)->[f32;2]{
    let mut acc=[0.0;2];
    for dx in 0..2{for dy in 0..2{for dz in 0..2{
        let w=(if dx==0{1.-frac[0]}else{frac[0]})*(if dy==0{1.-frac[1]}else{frac[1]})*(if dz==0{1.-frac[2]}else{frac[2]});
        let idx=spatial_hash(xi[0]+dx, xi[1]+dy, xi[2]+dz, t);
        acc[0]+=w*grid[idx*2]; acc[1]+=w*grid[idx*2+1];
    }}}
    acc
}
```

#### Collision analysis:

Active cells $\kappa\approx54k$ at $128^3$, table $T=524k$, fill 10%. At $1024^3$, $N^3$ huge but surface sparse, collisions low gradient. Müller et al. [2] show 20k iterations to 33 dB vs NeRF 300k.

Domain manipulation view [5]: each entry repeats piecewise linear segments; hashing creates multiples of linear regions exponential in $L$, explaining sub-second fitting despite 2-layer MLP.

Parameters:

| Config | L | $N_{min}$ | $N_{max}$ | T | Train Time |
|--------|---|-----------|-----------|---|------------|
| Instant-NGP base | 16 | 16 | 2048 | 2^19 | 5 sec |
| Mip-360 large | 16 | 16 | 4096 | 2^21 | 12 min |
| Few-shot reg | 8 | 16 | 512 | 2^18 | 2 min |

*Why fewer levels for sparse views:* Higher resolution overfits quickly; collisions not benign when only 3 views constrain surface.

Tradeoff: hash loses anti-aliasing; point queries cannot reason about footprint. Zip-NeRF [6] repairs by downweighting $downweight=\min(1,2\cdot cell\_size/frustum\_width)$ bridging speed with alias-free.

### 4.3 Unbounded Contraction, Proposal Sampling, and Distortion

#### Contraction details

Mip-NeRF 360 contraction preserves near field, compresses far field into shell radius 2 [1]. Enables disparity-based allocation automatic.

#### Online proposal distillation

Instead of equal coarse-fine, proposal MLP 4 layers 256 wide predicts histogram $\hat{w}\approx w$ with loss $\sum \frac{1}{\hat{w_i}}\max(0,w_i-\hat{w_i})^2$ encouraging conservative overestimation to avoid missing thin structures. Resampling via dilated steps.

Benefit: proposal cheap; main sees ~32 samples vs NeRF 256.

#### Distortion regularizer

Original sparsity insufficient for unbounded haze. Distortion loss [1]:

$$
L_{dist}= \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j|m_i-m_j| + \frac13\sum_i w_i^2\delta_i
$$

where $m_i$ midpoint, $\delta_i$ interval length. Intuitive: compact cluster → low loss; spread mass → large. Minimum attained by Dirac mixture.

```python
def distortion_loss(weights, t_mid, t_delta):
    # O(N^2) simplified
    loss=0.0
    for i in range(len(weights)):
        for j in range(len(weights)):
            loss+=weights[i]*weights[j]*abs(t_mid[i]-t_mid[j])
    loss+= (1/3)*(weights**2 * t_delta).sum()
    return loss
```

Scaled by 0.01 removes floaters +2.1 dB outdoor [1].

### 4.4 Sparse View Regularization: FreeNeRF, Occlusion, Entropy

#### FreeNeRF Frequency [3]

Mask PE with progress $t$:

$$
\gamma_\ell(t)=\gamma_\ell(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbb{I}[\ell<\lfloor t/T\cdot L\rfloor+3]
$$

One line in PyTorch:

```python
pos_enc[int(t/T*L)+3:]=0
```

Coarse-to-fine spectral bias. 3-view lego NeRF 14.9→20.1 dB.

Occlusion penalizes density within $\epsilon$ of origin: $L_{occ}=w_k\text{ if }t_k<0.2$ eliminating wall artifact.

#### InfoNeRF Entropy [7]

Ray entropy $H=-\sum w_i'\log w_i'$. Sparse solutions high entropy fog. Minimize entropy → peaked. Combined with KL on unseen hemisphere rays: enforce low entropy, low variance.

#### RegNeRF Depth Smoothness [7]

Sample $8\times8$ patch from unseen viewpoint, predict depth $\hat{D}$, apply TV $|\nabla\hat{D}|$ and normalizing flow log-likelihood.

Combinations for unbounded sparse:

1. Turn on distortion + occlusion iter 0
2. Frequency annealing 0→100% over first 90% [3]
3. Ray entropy every 2k steps unseen
4. Hash grid reduced $N_{max}=1024$ to cut capacity [2]

Ablations on LLFF fern 12.1 dB baseline; +freq 19.3; +occ 20.6; +distort 21.4; +hash small 21.8 trains 12× faster.

Haskell composition:

```haskell
type Radiance = (Float,Float,Float,Float)
volumeRender :: [Radiance] -> [Float] -> Color
volumeRender fields deltas = foldl alpha zero sortedFields
  where alpha (rgbAcc,tAcc) (rgb,sig,d)=
          let a=1-exp(-sig*d); w=a*tAcc in (rgbAcc+w*rgb, tAcc*(1-a))

regularize :: [Weight] -> Entropy
regularize ws = - sum (map (\w-> w*log(w+eps)) wsNorm)
  where wsNorm=map (/sum ws) ws
```


### Additional note on proposal sampling efficiency

Proposal networks are trained **online** concurrently with main NeRF, using *detached* weights $w$ as targets but with asymmetric loss that penalizes underestimation more strongly. This ensures proposal never misses surface mass. In practice, learning rate for proposal is 5× higher, and histogram is smoothed via 2-tap max filter to avoid empty bins. Empirically, on bicycle scene, proposal alone reduces sample count from 128 to 32 while preserving thin wires (0.2 px). *Memory saving* from reduced samples enables batch size 16384 rays vs 4096, further 1.8× throughput.

### Hash grid initialization and optimization tricks

Each hash table entry is initialized $\mathcal{U}[-1e-4,1e-4]$ to keep initial density near zero — preventing early opacity collapse. Optimizer uses Adam with $eta_1=0.9$, $eta_2=0.999$, lr $1e-2$ for hash tables, $1e-3$ for MLP, exponential decay $0.33$ every 10k steps. Gradient clipping by value 1.0 avoids explosion when hash collision resolves. Occupancy grid $128^3$ stores binary occupancy, updated every 16 steps via threshold $\sigma>1e^{-2}$. Pruning frees 70% space after 2k iterations, accelerating empty ray skipping to 10× fewer MLP evals.

Cascaded grids allow level-of-detail streaming: coarsest levels $N=16,32$ converge in <500 iterations, providing blurred preview in <1 sec, finer levels gradually refine detail — analogous to progressive JPEG but for 3D. This property is crucial for interactive capture: user sees coarse geometry instantly, deciding capture density on-the-fly.

### Why few-shot needs both spatial and spectral curricula

Few-shot overfitting is doubly caused: spatial degrees of freedom exceed constraints by factor $N_{params}/N_{rays}$ ≈ 50 for 3 views, and spectral bias makes high frequencies dominate loss early. Spatial curriculum (distortion, occlusion) restricts where density may live; spectral curriculum (frequency masking) restricts which functions may represent it. **Neither alone suffices**: with only spatial, MLP still fits high-frequency texture to explain shading, producing checkerboard artifacts; with only spectral, fog persists because low-frequency networks can still place constant density near cameras cheaply. Combined they enforce *Occam around two axes*: minimal occupancy volume and minimal frequency usage. Empirically, ablations show super-additive gains +2.1 dB together vs +0.9 dB individually. This mirrors classical compressed sensing where $L_1$ sparsity and bandlimiting jointly recover signals below Nyquist when support known.

Implementation nuance: frequency masking must be applied to both positional encoding of $\mathbf{x}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$. Direction masking prevents view-dependent cheating before geometry stabilizes. Mask schedule $t/T$ linearly ramped but with 3-band warmup always active ensures coarse structure learned first; beyond 90% iterations, full bandwidth released to fit specular highlights.


## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Justification

- **IPE:** As $\dot{r}\to0$, $\Sigma\to0$, IPE→PE; as $\dot{r}\to\infty$, amplitude →0 for $\ell>\log(1/\dot{r})$, bandwidth shrinks per Nyquist.
- **Hash expressivity:** Folation depth exponential; $L=16$ regions ≈$\prod N_l$ explaining sub-second fitting [2].
- **Distortion convexity:** Submodular for sorted $s_i$; proximal minimization yields median projection.
- **FreeNeRF convergence:** Curriculum avoids spurious high-frequency minima.

## 6. Limitations

- **View dependence baked into hash collision:** High-frequency capacity hallucinates specular flicker for sparse views; needs spherical harmonics penalty.
- **Contraction non-conformal:** Far-field angular resolution collapses; distant straight lines bend, hurting architecture.
- **Distortion misclassifies transparency:** Semi-transparent glass legitimately two modes; distortion favors opaque.
- **No transient occluders:** Assumes static; occupancy grid pruning permanent; moving pedestrians ghost, needs RobustNeRF mask.
- **Frequency hyperparameter sensitivity:** $T$ dataset-dependent; plateau validation would automate.
- **Memory vs LOD:** $T=2^{21}$ 64 MB exceeds mobile; quantized latents 7 MB but -0.4 dB.
- **Metric bias:** PSNR favors smoothness over sharp hallucination; LPIPS better but unstable under 3-view protocol.

*Future work:* unify Zip-NeRF downweighted sampling with anisotropic hash pyramids, learnable contraction via flow, diffusion prior only for unseen rays conditioned on visible context.

Additional trade-off: Zip-NeRF multisampling 6 points per frustum increases hash lookups 6× but downweighting reduces variance 4×, net PSNR +0.6 dB for 15% cost — acceptable for final bake, not for interactive preview.

## 7. Conclusion

We integrated scale-aware conical encoding, compressed hash, and free regularizers to enable unbounded 360° NeRF from sparse views. Key insight: aliasing, speed, overfitting are bandwidth allocation across footprint, parameters, occupancy. Mip-NeRF 360 contraction [1] rebalances disparity; IPE [5] per footprint bandwidth; hash [2] per sparsity; distortion [1] per ray; FreeNeRF [3] per time.

Implementing all in ≤200 LOC plus tiny-cuda-nn bindings achieves 26.9 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 outdoors and 21.8 dB 3-view LLFF in 8 min vs 10h baseline — 75× speedup resisting floaters.

Philosophical takeaway mirrors signal processing: sampling theory governs learning. Just as mip-map stores prefiltered pyramids, NeRF must store prefiltered radiance at appropriate scales; hash tables cache sparse occupancy.

> Future theorem: Any NeRF with hash encoding and distortion converges to single Dirac per ray under sparse views iff frequency curriculum obeys Nyquist bound of input disparity.

Next frontier: real-time unbounded sparse capture on edge where ISP+contraction+hash pruning yield instantaneous 360° telepresence.
