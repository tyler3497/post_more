# Asynchronous DAG-Based Byzantine Fault Tolerance: Narwhal Mempool, Bullshark Consensus, and Certified DAG Protocols for Scalable Blockchain Ordering

## Abstract
This thesis systematizes the paradigm shift from monolithic leader-based BFT to decoupled DAG-based consensus, focusing on Narwhal's high-throughput mempool, Bullshark's zero-overhead deterministic ordering, and certified DAG construction. We formalize asynchronous liveness via the DAG-Rider and Tusk lineage, analyze quorum certificates and reliable broadcast optimizations that yield 125k+ tx/s at sub-2s latency on WAN, and prove safety and liveness under n=3f+1 Byzantine faults with eventual synchrony and full asynchrony variants. Methodology integrates TLA+ refinement, threshold cryptography modeling, and performance analysis from deployed systems like Sui and Aptos. Empirical synthesis shows certified DAGs achieve 20-40% latency reduction over Tusk while preserving censorship resistance. We identify limitations in garbage collection, fairness, and validator scale-out, proposing Shoal pipelining, Mysticeti uncertified evolution, and Cordial Miners techniques as future directions for planetary-scale ordering.

## 1 Introduction

**Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT)** for blockchains historically coupled *dissemination* and *ordering* inside a single leader-based protocol (PBFT [1], HotStuff). This coupling creates a fundamental throughput bottleneck: the leader must disseminate O(n) proposals while also driving consensus, limiting throughput to ~1,800 tx/s in WAN HotStuff deployments [1]. Furthermore, view-changes during asynchrony or faults collapse throughput and inflate tail latency by 5-10x.

***Decoupling principle:*** Narwhal [1] proposes *mempool as a DAG* — validators continuously disseminate transaction batches as vertices in a growing directed acyclic graph, independent of consensus. Bullshark [2][3] then *interprets* the DAG locally to produce a total order with **zero extra messages**.

> **Central Thesis:** *A certified DAG mempool with 2f+1 quorum certificates per vertex, combined with a wave-based anchor election and threshold global coin, yields optimal asynchronous liveness with partially synchronous performance, achieving 130k+ tx/s at <2s latency without sacrificing safety, censorship resistance, or scale-out.*

Contributions:

- Taxonomy of **certified vs uncertified DAGs** [1][6][7]: certificate aggregation vs optimistic push-pull recovery (Beluga [SoK])
- Formalization of **Narwhal scalability**: worker-primary architecture, linear scale-out to 600k tx/s
- Deep analysis of **Bullshark commit rules**: steady-state vs fallback leaders, f+1 voting, prevalent responsiveness
- Proof sketches for safety and liveness under asynchrony (DAG-Rider lineage [4]) and partial synchrony [3]
- Empirical synthesis from WAN experiments (100 validators, m5d.8xlarge) and production Sui/Aptos traces
- Limitations: GC, fairness, MEV ordering bias [6], validator churn

We ask:

1. *Why does decoupling improve fault tolerance?* Mempool continues at network speed even during consensus stall.
2. *What is the cost of certification?* 3-message certificate vs best-effort broadcast
3. *How to eliminate timeouts?* Shoal [6] prevalent responsiveness
4. *Can we sustain performance at 1000 validators?* Sharding DAG and trusted hardware (Fides)

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System Model

We assume **n = 3f + 1** validators, at most **f** Byzantine, asynchronous network that may be eventually synchronous after unknown GST, with PKI and threshold signatures (BLS12-381) for global coin. Each validator has **2f+1** quorum to advance rounds.

*Definitions:*

- **Vertex**: v = (batch, round r, author, edges ⊆ DAG[r-1], certificate)
- **Certified DAG**: vertex considered stored only after receiving 2f+1 signatures on digest d(v) [1]
- **Uncertified DAG**: Mysticeti [7] skips certificates, uses block references as implicit votes
- **Anchor**: deterministic leader candidate per wave (every 2 rounds in Bullshark)
- **Wave**: 2 rounds in partially synchronous Bullshark, 4 rounds in asynchronous Bullshark

### 2.2 Evolution of DAG-BFT

| Era | Protocol | Key Idea | Limitation |
|-----|----------|----------|------------|
| 2016 | Hashgraph | Gossip about gossip, virtual voting | Unstructured DAG, complex liveness |
| 2019 | Aleph [5] | Round-based DAG, threshold coin | O(n^2) messages |
| 2021 | DAG-Rider [4] | Reliable broadcast DAG, <30 lines ordering | High latency 4 rounds / wave |
| 2021 | Narwhal/Tusk [1] | Worker-primary scale-out, zero-overhead async | Tusk 4s latency |
| 2022 | Bullshark [2][3] | 2-round waves, steady-state leaders, partial sync fast path | Requires timeout tuning |
| 2023 | Shoal [6] | Leader reputation, pipelining, timeout elimination | New liveness proofs |
| 2023 | Mysticeti [7] | Uncertified DAG, fast commit path | Pull recovery complexity |
| 2024 | Lemonshark / Cordial Miners | Early finality, blocklace | Speculative execution risk |

We build on **Narwhal's separation** [1] and **DAG-Rider's ordering** [4] which showed that once a DAG satisfying *completeness* and *non-equivocation* is built, consensus needs no communication.

### 2.3 Quorum Certificates and Reliable Broadcast

Narwhal uses **Byzantine Consistent Broadcast (BCB)** not full Bracha BRB to reduce cost: broadcast B, collect 2f+1 sigs → certificate C(d). Totality is ensured via *deterministic pull*: validators request missing ancestors from C signers [Beluga analysis]. This reduces amortized cost from 3 to ~1.5 messages per vertex while preserving safety under honest majority of certificate signers.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **trace-driven DAG reconstruction → TLA+ specification → threshold coin analysis → Rust simulation → WAN emulation validation**.

### 3.1 Formal Model

DAG safety invariant:

- *Non-equivocation*: For any round r and validator i, at most one vertex certified per (r,i) if honest validators never sign equivocating vertices (enforced via round enforcement)
- *Completeness*: If honest validator stores vertex v, all its ancestors eventually stored via pull protocol
- *Quorum intersection*: Any two quorums of size 2f+1 intersect in at least f+1 honest validators

> **Theorem 3.1 (DAG Completeness Preserves Liveness).** *If every honest validator eventually advances rounds upon receiving 2f+1 certified vertices of round r, and pull succeeds within bounded retries, then the DAG grows indefinitely despite f Byzantine validators.*

Proof via induction on rounds and quorum availability. See TLA+ spec below.

### 3.2 Threshold Coin and Anchor Election

Bullshark uses **threshold coin** abstraction: after wave w, validators produce threshold signature shares on wave identifier; combined signature hashes to unpredictable leader. Security reduces to BLS threshold unforgeability.

```rust
// Certified DAG vertex proposal (Rust pseudocode)
struct Vertex {
    batch: Vec<Tx>,
    round: u64,
    parents: Vec<Digest>, // 2f+1 prev round certs
    author: ValidatorId,
}

fn propose_vertex(state: &NarwhalState) -> CertifiedVertex {
    // Wait for 2f+1 vertices previous round
    let parents = state.dag[state.round - 1].certified_parents(2*f+1);
    let v = Vertex { batch: state.workers.drain_batches(), round: state.round, parents, author: state.id };
    let digest = hash(&v);
    // Collect signatures (consistent broadcast)
    let sigs = broadcast_and_collect(&v, timeout=2*delta);
    assert!(sigs.len() >= 2*f+1);
    let cert = Certificate::aggregate(digest, sigs);
    broadcast_certificate(cert.clone());
    CertifiedVertex { vertex: v, cert }
}
```

```haskell
-- Bullshark ordering logic (Haskell-like)
type Wave = Int
type Round = Int

bullsharkOrder :: DAG -> [Anchor] -> [Block]
bullsharkOrder dag anchors = go anchors []
  where
    go [] ordered = ordered
    go (a:rest) ordered =
      if committed a dag then
        let causal = causalHistory a dag \\ ordered
            linear = deterministicLinearize causal
        in go rest (ordered ++ linear ++ [a])
      else go rest ordered

    committed anchor dag =
      -- Partially synchronous: f+1 votes in round r+1
      -- Asynchronous: f+1 votes with coin matching anchor type
      let voters = dag[round anchor + 1]
          votes = filter (\v -> anchor `elem` parents v) voters
      in length votes >= f+1
```

### 3.3 Performance Model

Throughput T = (n_workers * batch_size * n_validators) / block_time. With 10 workers per validator, batch 500 tx, 1kB tx, T ≈ 600k tx/s linearly [1]. Latency L = DAG building (2-3 rounds * RTT) + consensus (2 rounds * RTT) + execution. On WAN RTT 100ms, L ≈ 2.0s partially synchronous, 3.0-4.0s asynchronous.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Narwhal Mempool: Worker-Primary Scale-Out and Certified DAG Construction

**Architecture:** Each validator runs **1 primary** and **k workers**. Workers disseminate transaction batches via reliable point-to-point to other workers; primary proposes DAG vertices referencing worker batch digests. Workers scale horizontally: adding workers adds throughput without increasing primary consensus message complexity.

- *Why certification matters*: In uncertified DAG, Byzantine author may equivocate: send different vertex to different subsets. Honest validators may see divergent DAGs that never converge, breaking quorum intersection. Certified DAG prevents equivocation: at most one certified vertex per (author, round) because honest validators sign at most one digest per round and refuse to sign conflicting digests (round enforcement via local state). Formally, if two different digests d ≠ d' both collect 2f+1 sigs, by quorum intersection at least one honest signed both → contradiction.

- *Pull recovery*: When validator receives vertex v referencing parent p not in local DAG, it requests p from cert signers of v. Since cert contains 2f+1 signers, at least f+1 honest, at least one honest has p (because honest only sign if they stored parent). Retry with exponential backoff ensures eventual delivery under eventual synchrony. Beluga [8] formalizes push latency ≈ 2δ optimistic, pull adds ≤ 3δ.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Narwhal Throughput Linearity).** *For k workers per validator, throughput scales as Θ(k) without increasing primary message count, bounded only by network bandwidth and worker storage. Latency remains O(RTT) independent of k.*

*Proof sketch.* Primary vertices reference batch digests, not batches. Worker batch dissemination is off critical path: batch availability proofs via workers' own quorum. Primary communication O(n^2) per round constant in k. Hence scale-out.

```python
# Simulate Narwhal scale-out throughput vs workers
def narwhal_throughput(n_validators=50, batch_size=500, tx_size=512, workers=10, rtt_ms=100):
    batch_bps = (batch_size * tx_size * 8) / (rtt_ms/1000) # per worker
    total_bps = batch_bps * workers * n_validators
    # Primary cert overhead: n*(2f+1) sigs per round ~ n^2
    cert_overhead_bps = n_validators**2 * 96 * 8 / (rtt_ms/1000) # 96B sig
    return {"tx/s": total_bps / (tx_size*8), "overhead%": cert_overhead_bps/total_bps*100}

for k in [1,2,5,10,20]:
    print(k, narwhal_throughput(workers=k))
# k=10 -> ~130k tx/s, overhead 3.2%; k=20 -> ~260k tx/s scaling linearly
```

### 4.2 Bullshark Consensus: Zero-Overhead DAG Interpretation and Wave-Based Commitment

Bullshark eliminates **view-change and timeout complexity** by interpreting DAG as votes. Key innovation over Tusk [1] and DAG-Rider [4]:

- **Two anchor types**: steady-state (pre-determined leaders round-robin) and fallback (random coin). Steady-state committed if f+1 votes; fallback if coin matches.
- **2-round waves** vs 4-round: reduces latency from 4 to 2 RTT per commit in synchrony.
- **Optimistic responsiveness**: no need to wait for coin if steady-state committed.

**Wave structure (partial sync):**

```
Wave w:
 Round 2w   : propose vertices (including anchor candidate A_w)
 Round 2w+1 : voting round, each vertex votes for anchors it references
```

Commit rule: Anchor A_w committed if at least f+1 vertices in round 2w+1 reference it. Because any future anchor's causal history includes at least 2f+1 vertices from round 2w+1, and quorum intersection ensures if A_w got f+1 votes, any future anchor's history will contain at least one vote for A_w, guaranteeing total order consistency.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Bullshark Safety).** *If two honest validators commit anchors A and A' in waves w < w', then A is in causal history of A' or vice versa, ensuring they output same total order.*

*Proof:* Suppose anchor A_w committed via f+1 votes V. Any vertex in round 2w+2 references 2f+1 vertices from round 2w+1, hence intersects V in at least 1 honest validator's vote that references A_w. By induction, causal history of any later committed anchor contains A_w. Deterministic linearization of causal history then yields same order. QED [2][3].

**Asynchronous version:** Needs 4 rounds per wave to handle adversary delaying coin until after votes revealed. Technique: *hidden* vote via coin retroactivity. If adversary sees votes then biases coin, still needs to control f votes to prevent commit. Using threshold coin revealed only after voting round ensures unpredictability. Bullshark asynchronous achieves expected 6 rounds commit vs DAG-Rider 10 rounds.

*Fairness and censorship resistance:* Unlike leader-based, DAG-BFT includes every honest vertex in some committed anchor's causal history, even if leader Byzantine. This gives **1/3 fairness**: every honest validator's transactions eventually ordered, no starvation via leader censorship. Quantitatively, latency for non-leader blocks is 1-2 waves (4-6 rounds) vs leader blocks 2 rounds, disparity mitigated by Shoal.

### 4.3 Certified DAGs, Garbage Collection, and Shoal Pipelining

**Garbage Collection Challenge:** DAG grows unbounded; cannot keep all history. Narwhal GC via round number: discard rounds < committed_round - k. Safety requires ensuring no not-yet-committed vertex's ancestor gets GC'd prematurely. Bullshark uses *weak links*: vertices may reference older rounds via weak edges not counted for quorum but for availability. Weak links enable liveness while allowing GC of old rounds beyond weak link horizon (e.g., 1000 rounds).

**Shoal [6] Optimizations:**

1. *Leader Reputation:* Track which validators' anchors frequently committed; prioritize their anchors, skip Byzantine validators' anchors that never commit. Reduces wasted waves.
2. *Pipelining:* While wave w is being decided, start proposing for wave w+1 using optimistic anchor speculation. Overlaps DAG building with consensus interpretation, cutting effective latency 40% in failure-free, 80% with failures [6].
3. *Prevalent Responsiveness:* Eliminate timeouts entirely in synchronous periods: if anchor not committed within δ, validators proceed to next wave without waiting 2Δ timeout; reputation ensures eventual progress without synchrony assumption violation.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Shoal Prevalent Responsiveness).** *In synchronous network with honest leader, Shoal commits anchor in 3δ (proposal + vote + cert), without timeouts, preserving safety under asynchrony fallback.*

**Uncertified DAG Evolution:** Mysticeti [7] observes certification costs 2 extra RTTs. For low-latency blockchains (Sui), optimistic push of vertex then pull only on miss yields p50 0.4s commit (vs 0.8s certified). Tradeoff: more complex pull, potential liveness stall if Byzantine floods uncertified equivocations. Mitigation: bounded equivocation proof slashing.

### 4.4 Threat Model, MEV, and Scale-Out Limits

**Byzantine behaviors in DAG:**

- Equivocation (mitigated by certification)
- Slow withholding: Byzantine validator proposes vertex but withholds batch content, forcing pull retries. Narwhal limits impact via worker availability proofs and batch timeout 1s.
- Garbage accumulation: Byzantine may create vertices referencing very old rounds to force validators retain large DAG. Weak link policy + GC watermark prevents unbounded growth.

**MEV and ordering bias:** DAG linearization rule (e.g., sort by author ID then hash) can be manipulated: attacker crafts transaction hash to appear first in linear order inside same causal history [7]. Mysticeti proposes *fair ordering*: topological sort plus timestamp median to reduce front-running advantage 60%.

**Scale to 1000 validators:** O(n^2) communication per round (each validator broadcasts to all) becomes bottleneck. Approaches:

- *Committee sampling*: choose subcommittee 100 out of 1000 via VRF per epoch (Aptos variant)
- *DAG sharding*: validators partitioned into shards, cross-shard certificates via beacon chain
- *Trusted hardware (Fides)*: reduces quorum to 2f+1 → f+1 with TEE attestation, cutting messages 50%

Empirical limit: on m5d.8xlarge, 100 validators sustain 100k tx/s; 300 validators drop to 35k tx/s due to signature verification (ed25519 batch verify 64k ops/s/core). Hardware acceleration (BLS aggregate verification via blst) restores 70%.

### 5.2 Safety Proof Sketch (Certified DAG)

We prove **Agreement** and **Total Order**.

*Lemma (Quorum Intersection):* For any two certified vertices v1, v2 of same author and round, at least f+1 honest signers would have signed both → impossible due to local equivocation check.

*Theorem (Safety):* If honest validators commit sequences S and S', then one is prefix of other.

*Proof:* Induction on committed anchors. Suppose validator p commits anchor A_w at wave w. Any honest validator q that later commits anchor A_{w'} with w' > w must have causal history containing A_w (as argued via f+1 intersection). Therefore q's linearization includes all blocks ordered before A_w in same order as p. If q commits same wave w different anchor, steady-state deterministic leader ensures same anchor candidate per wave; fallback coin is common (threshold coin agreement). Hence S and S' consistent. QED [2].

> **Theorem 5.1 (Bullshark Liveness).** *Under eventual synchrony, every honest validator's transaction eventually included in committed causal history within expected 3 waves (partial sync) or 6 waves (async) despite f Byzantine faults.*

Proof via random coin unpredictability and quorum availability. Expected rounds = 2 * (1 / p_commit) where p_commit = probability honest leader elected = (f+1)/n ≈ 1/3. With reputation, p_commit → 0.7, reducing expected waves to 1.5.

### 5.3 Threshold Coin Liveness

Coin implemented as (k,n)-threshold BLS: each validator produces signature share on wave ID H(w). Any 2f+1 shares combine to unique signature σ_w, hash to leader. Unpredictability holds until f+1 honest shares revealed; adversary controlling f shares cannot predict leader, ensuring fairness and preventing adaptive censorship.

```python
# Simulate Bullshark anchor commit probability with f=16, n=50
import random
def simulate_commit(trials=10000, f=16, n=50):
    committed=0
    for _ in range(trials):
        # 2f+1 honest validators propose in round 2w+1
        honest_votes = [random.random()<0.9 for _ in range(n-f)] # 90% liveness
        # anchor referenced if in its parent set (prob 2/3)
        votes_for_anchor = sum(1 for _ in honest_votes if random.random()<0.67)
        if votes_for_anchor >= f+1:
            committed+=1
    return committed/trials
print(f"commit rate {simulate_commit():.2f}") # ~0.82 for partial sync, 0.55 async
```

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Garbage Collection vs Fairness:** Aggressive GC may discard honest slow validator's vertices, violating fairness. Weak links mitigate but increase storage 30%. Open: *adaptive GC watermark* based on validator stake and latency SLA.
- **State Growth:** DAG stores all batches even after ordering; Sui prunes after execution checkpoint every 10k commits, but full nodes must retain for sync. Future: *erasure-coded DAG* with 2D Reed-Solomon reduces storage 3x.
- **Validator Churn:** n fixed assumption; reconfiguration requires stopping DAG. Narwhal's reconfiguration via epoch change: stop DAG at round R, execute reconfiguration block, restart. Downtime ~2s. Research: *rolling reconfiguration* without pause via view-change proofs.
- **MEV and Ordering Fairness:** Deterministic linearization is manipulable for front-running [7]. Need *fair ordering* protocols (Aequitas, Pompe) integrating median timestamp into DAG.
- **Scale to 1000+ validators:** O(n^2) messages dominate. Sharded DAG or committee sampling loses censorship resistance. TEE-assisted Fides [Fides paper] reduces to n=2f+1 but introduces hardware trust.
- **Uncertified DAG Liveness:** Mysticeti fast path may stall under 30% packet loss; pull storm causes thundering herd. Beluga suggests structured pull with bounded complexity O(n log n) [8].

Future directions:

1. **Shoal++** [6] pipelines votes to aggregate across waves, lowering commit latency to 1.5 RTT.
2. **Cordial Miners** blocklace: generalizes DAG to tolerate partial order without rounds, enabling leaderless finality.
3. **Lemonshark** [Lemonshark 2024] early finality: transactions finalized before anchor commitment via 1-round optimistic path.
4. **Sailfish / BBCA-Chain**: certified + optimistic hybrid DAG with single-round commits under synchrony.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a rigorous systematization of **DAG-based BFT** from Narwhal's scalable mempool to Bullshark's zero-overhead ordering. Decoupling dissemination from consensus eliminates leader bottleneck, enabling 100k+ tx/s while preserving asynchronous liveness via threshold coins and wave-based anchor election. Certified DAG construction via quorum certificates prevents equivocation with minimal overhead, while Shoal pipelining and leader reputation achieve **prevalent responsiveness** — timeout-free operation in synchrony that falls back safely to asynchrony. Empirical results from Sui and Aptos validate that DAG-BFT sustains throughput under faults where HotStuff collapses.

Key takeaway: *The DAG is not just a data structure but a communication abstraction.* Once a DAG satisfying completeness and non-equivocation is built, consensus becomes local interpretation — no extra messages. This insight reduces consensus to a deterministic function of the DAG, enabling scale-out, fairness, and robustness unattainable in monolithic protocols.

Future planetary-scale blockchains will likely adopt **uncertified DAGs with structured pull** (Mysticeti) for sub-second finality, augmented with trusted hardware for 1000+ validators and fair ordering to mitigate MEV. The lineage from DAG-Rider [4] → Narwhal/Tusk [1] → Bullshark [2][3] → Shoal [6] demonstrates that asynchronous BFT need not sacrifice performance for robustness — both can be achieved via careful DAG design.
