# Post-Quantum TLS 1.3 Handshake Optimization: Kyber KEM, Dilithium Signatures, Hybrid X25519-Kyber, KEMTLS, and Formal Verification with Tamarin and ProVerif

## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of post-quantum tls 1.3 handshake optimization, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of post-quantum tls 1.3 handshake optimization and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early post-quantum constructions to state-of-the-art KEM deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show post-quantum tls 1.3 handshake optimization achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(commit - srs.g*value, srs.g2);
    let rhs = pairing(proof, srs.g2_h - srs.g2*point);

}
