# Timely and Differential Dataflow: Partially-Ordered Logical Times, Nested Iteration with Product Timestamps, Incremental View Maintenance via Difference Traces, and Scale-Out Streaming Joins

## Abstract

Timely and differential dataflow recast streaming, batch, and iterative computation as a single incremental program over partially-ordered logical time. This thesis develops the theory and systems design of the timely dataflow model — in which every datum carries a logical timestamp drawn from a lattice, operators expose progress through pointstamp frontiers, and nested iteration is expressed with product timestamps that keep epochs and loop iterations incomparable wherever they commute — and of differential dataflow, which layers collections-as-differences, multiversioned difference traces, and shared indexes (*arrangements*) on that substrate to obtain incremental view maintenance for iterative queries. We formalize progress tracking, argue the correctness of frontier-based notification, derive the consolidation and arrangement algorithms that bound operator state, and show how delta joins exploit shared indexes for scale-out streaming joins. We connect the model to the DBSP theory of incremental computation and the COST metric of McSherry, Isard, and Murray, and report empirical evidence that single-threaded and modestly parallel implementations routinely match hundred-core clusters on graph analytics. Limitations in ergonomics, expressiveness, and operational complexity are examined.

---

## 1. Introduction

Data-parallel systems were long organized around a false trichotomy: *batch* engines maximize throughput, *stream* engines minimize latency, *iterative* frameworks recompute to convergence — and none does all three well. Applications needing all three glued together incompatible platforms, sacrificing efficiency, maintainability, and simplicity [1].

**Timely dataflow** (Murray, McSherry, Isaacs, Isard, Barham, Abadi — *Naiad*, SOSP 2013) dissolves the trichotomy with one move: enrich the dataflow graph with *logical timestamps* from a **partially-ordered set**, and derive coordination entirely from them [1]. Operators exchange *pointstamps* (location, timestamp); each worker maintains per-operator *frontiers* of possibly-outstanding timestamps. Once the frontier passes *t*, no future message bears *t*. One mechanism expresses batch, streaming, and iteration — only the *shape of time* changes [1].

**Differential dataflow** (McSherry, Murray, Isaacs, Isard — CIDR 2013) builds *incremental* computation on this substrate [2]: collections are functions from *time* to *multisets of changes*, operators propagate only **differences**, and iteration nests over the same partially-ordered times so new data and new loop iterations maximally reuse prior work. The key structure is the **difference trace** — an indexed, consolidated, multiversioned log of (key, time, difference) triples supporting *as-of*-frontier lookups — shared across operators as **arrangements**, the streaming indexes at the heart of Materialize [2].

This thesis makes the following contributions:

1. A formalization of **partially-ordered logical time**, pointstamp progress tracking, and the frontier notification theorem.
2. **Nested iteration via product timestamps**: how incomparability captures the commutativity of epochs and loop iterations, yielding incremental rather than bulk-synchronous iteration.
3. **Difference traces, consolidation, and arrangements** — indexed batches, multiversion cursors, and the **delta join** for scale-out streaming joins.
4. A synthesis with **DBSP** [3] and the **COST** metric [4].
5. An empirical evaluation (reachability, SCC, streaming joins) and a candid assessment of limitations.

> **Thesis statement:** Partially-ordered logical time is the right primitive for unifying batch, streaming, and iterative computation; difference traces over product timestamps are the right state abstraction for incremental maintenance of those computations; and shared arrangements make the resulting engine competitive with — and often superior to — specialized systems in each of the three domains.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The landscape before timely dataflow

Classical systems forced a choice: batch engines (MapReduce, Spark RDDs) iterate via driver-controlled re-execution, stream processors (Storm, MillWheel) use total-order time and cannot express cycles, and iterative graph systems (Pregel, GraphLab) restart on new input. Each could simulate the others only awkwardly — and that awkwardness was Naiad's point: iteration *and* incrementality *and* low latency required a new primitive, not a new combination of old ones [1].

### 2.2 Logical time, partial orders, and lattices

A **partial order** is a set *T* with reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive ≤ (*s, t* **incomparable** if neither *s ≤ t* nor *t ≤ s*); a *lattice* adds joins (∨) and meets (∧). Timely timestamps form a lattice: the meet is the greatest time complete for both — exactly the algebra progress tracking needs.

The crucial examples of timestamp types are:

| Timestamp type | Order | Models |
|---|---|---|
| `usize` | Total order | Streaming epochs, batch rounds |
| `(usize, usize)` product | *(a₁,b₁) ≤ (a₂,b₂)* iff *a₁≤a₂ ∧ b₁≤b₂* | Epoch × iteration: nested loops over streams |
| `Vec<usize>` nested product | Componentwise | Arbitrarily nested iteration scopes |
| Root timestamp | Trivial (single point) | Pure batch, no logical time |

The **product order** is the thesis's central device: *(1, 5)* and *(2, 3)* are incomparable — neither precedes the other — which is the formal statement that "these two pieces of work commute and may proceed concurrently" [2]. A total order would serialize them artificially; the partial order exposes the parallelism actually present.

### 2.3 From Naiad to timely to differential: a genealogy

**Naiad (SOSP 2013)** [1] introduced timely dataflow with structured loops via *ingress/egress/feedback* operators. **Timely dataflow (Rust, 2014–)** reimplemented the progress-tracking substrate as a standalone crate [7]. **Differential dataflow (Rust, 2015–)** added the incremental collection API — collections of differences, shared *arrangements*, delta joins [2][7]. **Materialize (2019–)** added a PostgreSQL-compatible SQL planner over arrangements as its substrate. **DBSP (2022; VLDB 2023 best paper)** [3] re-derived the same ideas from digital signal processing — streams, Z-sets, and the differentiation/integration operators *D* and *I*.

### 2.4 Related theoretical foundations

Abadi et al. gave timely iteration a denotational semantics [3, §9]; DBSP positions itself as "inspired from Differential Dataflow... a simpler formalization of DD than the one of Abadi et al." [3]. The **COST** metric (Configuration that Outperforms a Single Thread) of McSherry, Isard, and Murray (HotOS 2015) [4] supplies the evaluation discipline: absolute performance must be weighed against a competent single-threaded baseline.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is threefold: **formalization**, **mechanism design**, and **empirical grounding**.

1. **Formalization.** We model timely computation as a graph *G = (V, E)* with timestamps *t ∈ T* from a lattice *(T, ≤)*, formalize progress tracking as distributed per-input *frontier* computation, and prove the *frontier notification theorem*: an operator notified for *t* has received all messages with *s ≤ t*.
2. **Mechanism design.** From collections-as-differences we derive the three practical mechanisms: (a) **consolidation** of triples to canonical form; (b) **arrangements** — shared, indexed, multiversioned traces; (c) **delta joins** evaluating joins of differences against arrangements.
3. **Empirical grounding.** We evaluate on published measurements only — reachability and SCC over evolving graphs [2], streaming joins, and COST-style single-thread vs. distributed comparisons on *twitter_rv* [4]; no new cluster experiments are claimed.

*Notation.* We write *C[t]* for the multiset contents of collection *C* at logical time *t*, and *δC[t]* for its **difference** at *t*, defined by

```text
C[t] = Σ_{s ≤ t} δC[s]        (accumulation over the down-set of t)
δC[t] = C[t] − Σ_{s < t} δC[s]  (difference: the "new" part at t)
```

Differences are signed multisets (*Z-sets* [3]): *(d, +1)* inserts, *(d, −1)* retracts, opposites cancel. For product timestamps the sums range over the down-set *{s : s ≤ t}* — the partial order matters semantically, not just for scheduling.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Partially-ordered logical time and progress tracking

Timely dataflow's defining departure is that **time is not total**. Once a feedback loop appears, timestamps must distinguish "same data, later iteration" from "new data, same iteration" — two *independent* advances, neither preceding the other — so the type of time becomes a product and the order becomes partial.

For nested loops — epochs *e* outside, fixpoint iterations *i* inside — a timestamp is *(e, i)* with *(e₁,i₁) ≤ (e₂,i₂)* iff *e₁≤e₂* and *i₁≤i₂*. Diagonal pairs like *(1,2)* and *(2,1)* are incomparable: a message at *(1,2)* can only affect times *≥ (1,2)*, which excludes *(2,1)*, so the two are processed in either order or concurrently without violating semantics.

**Progress tracking** turns this order theory into a coordination protocol [1]. Each worker, for each of its operators and each input, maintains:

Each worker tracks **pointstamps** *(location, timestamp)* — promises of possible future sends — and workers exchange their *meet* into a per-input **frontier** *F*: every *t ⋠ F* is **complete**, i.e., no worker will ever send at *t* again.

> **Theorem (Frontier notification completeness).** Let *Fᵢ* be the frontier reported to operator *O* on input *i*. If *t* is not greater than or equal to any element of *Fᵢ* (written *t ⋠ Fᵢ*), then *O* has received every message that will ever be sent to it on input *i* with timestamp *s ≤ t*.

*Proof sketch.* *Fᵢ* is the meet over all workers of outstanding pointstamps feeding input *i*; any unsent future message bears a timestamp at or above some *f ∈ Fᵢ*. If *t ⋠ Fᵢ*, no future message can bear *s ≤ t*, else *s ≥ f* and *s ≤ t* would give *t ≥ f* by transitivity — contradiction. ∎

An operator registered via *`notify_at(t)`* fires once *t* falls behind the frontier, then safely consolidates or emits *t*'s state. The protocol is **lightweight** because it is *decentralized*: workers exchange only small antichain frontiers — never global barriers — so progress at one timestamp never blocks progress at an incomparable one [1]. In Rust this surfaces through a small operator API:

```rust
use timely::dataflow::operators::{Input, Inspect, Probe};

timely::execute_from_args(std::env::args(), |worker| {
    let mut held = None; // capture the input handle out of the closure
    let probe = worker.dataflow(|scope| {
        let (handle, stream) = scope.new_input::<usize>();
        held = Some(handle);
        stream.inspect(|x| println!("observed: {:?}", x)).probe()
    });
    let mut input = held.unwrap();
    for epoch in 0..10 {
        input.advance_to(epoch);
        for datum in epoch*100..(epoch+1)*100 { input.send(datum); }
        while probe.less_than(&input.time()) { worker.step(); } // wait for this timestamp's completeness only
    }
}).unwrap();
```

The `probe.less_than` loop waits for completeness of exactly the timestamps it cares about — and for product timestamps it can wait on one coordinate while another advances, the mechanism that makes nested iteration non-blocking.

### 4.2 Product timestamps and nested iteration

Iteration is a *first-class graph construct*, not driver-controlled re-execution: a loop is delimited by three operator roles [1]:

1. **Ingress**: injects external data, extending timestamps with an inner coordinate (*(e) → (e, 0)*).
2. **Feedback**: routes loop output back, *incrementing* the inner coordinate (*(e,i) → (e,i+1)*).
3. **Egress**: strips the inner coordinate once the frontier shows all iterations complete.

The sequence *(e, 0) < (e, 1) < …* is a chain while *(e₁, i)* and *(e₂, j)* for *e₁ ≠ e₂* are incomparable — the central performance property of the model:

> **Principle (Incremental iteration).** Work at *(e, i)* is valid for all times *≥ (e, i)* and *reused* as the computation advances; work at incomparable timestamps proceeds independently. When epoch *e+1* arrives while *e* still iterates, the fixpoint is **not** restarted: new data enters at *(e+1, 0)* and iterates concurrently with *e*'s convergence [2]. Advancing input and advancing iteration are the *same operation* — motion along the product order. The `iterate` combinator makes this explicit:

```rust
use differential_dataflow::input::InputSession;
use differential_dataflow::operators::Join;

worker.dataflow(|scope| {
    let mut edges_input = InputSession::new();
    let edges = edges_input.to_collection(scope);
    // Reachability: nested fixpoint over product time (epoch x iteration),
    // maintained incrementally as `edges_input` changes.
    let _reachable = edges.iterate(|reach| {
        reach.join(&edges).map(|(_n, nbr)| nbr).concat(reach).distinct()
    });
});
```

Feedback passes increment the iteration coordinate; `advance_to` increments the epoch coordinate; the runtime schedules whatever the partial order permits — a data-driven fixpoint. Nesting generalizes arbitrarily (`((e,i₁),i₂)`, …): a PageRank inside a hyperparameter sweep inside a streaming pipeline is three scopes with a three-dimensional frontier — compositionality no contemporary model offered [1][2].

### 4.3 Difference traces, consolidation, and arrangements

The differential data model represents every collection as its **difference trace**: the multiset of *(data, time, diff)* triples with *diff ∈ ℤ ∖ {0}*, such that the contents at time *t* are recovered by summing diffs over the down-set of *t* [2].

**Consolidation** rewrites raw triples into canonical form — one triple per *(data, time)*, nonzero diffs, sorted by key — the minimal representation and the one that makes indexed lookup efficient; the operator itself is incremental, emitting only net changes on frontier notifications. A consolidated trace is naturally **multiversioned**: each triple carries its validity time, so an "as of frontier *F*" lookup sums exactly triples with *time ≤ F* — the streaming analogue of MVCC, and what lets many operators share one trace with independent cursors.

**Arrangements.** An *arrangement* is a shared, indexed difference trace: triples are key-partitioned for scale-out; each partition keeps an LSM-like stack of immutable indexed batches; batches are *reference-counted* so operators at different frontiers share history and compact only when all readers have advanced past it [2]:

| Requirement | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Point lookup by key at frontier *F* | Per-partition sorted batch index; binary search + accumulate diffs with *time ≤ F* |
| Multiple readers, multiple frontiers | `Rc`-shared immutable batches; each reader holds its own cursor |
| Bounded memory | Compaction: when the meet of all reader frontiers passes *t*, merge and consolidate batches below *t* |
| Scale-out | Key-hash partitioning across workers; each worker owns a shard of every arrangement |

Arrangements are the reason differential dataflow's joins scale. In a classical streaming join, each new batch of input must be joined against accumulated state; in differential dataflow, the *join itself* is differential — it consumes difference traces and produces a difference trace — and its implementation, the **delta join**, evaluates only the *changes*:

> **Delta join rule.** For collections *A, B* with differences *δA, δB* at time *t*, the difference of the join at *t* is
> *δ(A ⋈ B)[t] = (δA[t] ⋈ B[<t]) ∪ (A[<t] ⋈ δB[t]) ∪ (δA[t] ⋈ δB[t])*,
> where *A[<t] = Σ_{s<t} δA[s]* is read from the arrangement of *A* as of the frontier just below *t*.

Each term joins *small* differences against an *indexed* arrangement — point lookups, not scans — so work at *t* is proportional to the *change*, not the state. The DBSP program shows the same delta rules arising automatically from differentiating lifted queries [3].

### 4.4 Scale-out streaming joins and the Materialize architecture

**Materialize** stacks three layers on arrangements [2]: (1) **timely dataflow** — distributed runtime with progress tracking; (2) **differential dataflow** — incremental operators, arrangements, delta joins; (3) a PostgreSQL-compatible SQL planner mapping tables to input collections, views to dataflows, indexes to arrangements. Because arrangements are key-partitioned and shared by reference, a streaming join needs no shuffle of the tables: each worker joins its local *δA* shard against its local *B* arrangement shard. Latency tracks the delta, throughput the cluster width, memory the consolidated trace — the Naiad vision of "queries joined with processed data, incrementally re-executed as updates arrive" [1], as a production database.

A comparative summary of the design space:

| System | Time model | Iteration | Incrementality | Coordination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MapReduce / Spark RDD | None (batch) | Driver re-execution | None | Barriers |
| Flink / MillWheel | Total order (watermarks) | Limited / none | Deltas on state | Watermarks |
| Pregel / GraphLab | Supersteps (total) | Native | None | Barriers |
| **Naiad** [1] | **Partial order (lattice)** | **Nested, in-graph** | Manual | **Frontiers** |
| **Timely + Differential** [2] | **Product timestamps** | **Nested fixpoints** | **Difference traces** | **Frontiers** |
| DBSP / Feldera [3] | Streams over ℕ (circuits) | Recursion via feedback | *Q^Δ = D∘↑Q∘I* | Stream semantics |

### 4.5 The DBSP lens: a simpler formalization

DBSP [3] re-derives these results from signal processing: streams map *t ∈ ℕ* to values, *differentiation* *D* computes per-time changes, *integration* *I* inverts it, and any query *Q* incrementalizes as *Q^Δ = D ∘ ↑Q ∘ I*, including recursion and non-monotone aggregation. It is a *formalization*, not a replacement: DBSP's total stream time cannot express the partial orders of §4.1–§4.2, but its Z-set algebra captures the difference-trace mechanics of §4.3. Triangulated: **timely contributes the partial order of time; differential contributes difference-trace state; DBSP contributes incrementalization as algebra** [2][3].

## 5. Empirical Evaluation

We evaluate the model's claims against published measurements on the three workloads it unifies.

### 5.1 Iterative graph analytics on evolving data

The CIDR 2013 paper [2] targets the motivating workload directly: *incrementally updated strongly connected components* on a changing social graph, which defeats pure streaming engines (no iteration) and pure iterative engines (no incrementality). The result: previously intractable algorithms become a few combinators with work proportional to the *change*, not the graph — confirmed by community benchmarks with single-machine PageRank over tens of millions of edges converging in seconds.

### 5.2 The COST discipline: absolute performance of data-parallel graph processing

The HotOS 2015 COST survey [4] found many published SOSP/OSDI graph systems had *unbounded* COST, with 128-core PageRank on *twitter_rv* (249–857 s) bracketing a single laptop thread (~300 s), and the laptop winning label propagation outright (153 s vs. 251–1784 s). The timely Rust implementation descends directly from this work — cooperative scheduling, columnar batches, no JVM — single-node performance is the *baseline* scale-out must beat — typically needing tens of cores on graph analytics.

### 5.3 Streaming joins and arrangement sharing

Arrangements convert the streaming equi-join from a state-scan into an index-lookup problem: delta joins touch only changed keys, and arrangement sharing amortizes one indexed trace across all downstream consumers. Memory follows the consolidated trace — proportional to distinct (key, time) pairs above the compaction frontier, not raw event volume.

### 5.4 Summary of evidence

| Claim | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| One model spans batch, streaming, iteration | Naiad evaluation across all three workloads | [1] |
| Iterative queries maintainable incrementally | Incremental SCC / reachability on evolving graphs | [2] |
| Single-thread baseline is competitive | COST survey; laptop vs. 128-core PageRank/label propagation | [4] |
| Difference traces bound join state | Arrangement design; consolidation + compaction | [2] |
| Incrementalization is algebraic | *Q^Δ = D ∘ ↑Q ∘ I*; VLDB best-paper award | [3] |

## 6. Limitations

**Ergonomics of time.** The timestamp type — hence the partial order — must be chosen up front; a total order where a product order was needed silently serializes independent work, while an overly fine product order fragments frontiers and inflates progress traffic. Debugging a stall means reasoning about antichains in a lattice. DBSP's total-time reaction [3] trades expressiveness for comprehensibility, and the trade is genuine.

**Expressiveness boundaries.** Monotone fixpoints and relational operators have clean delta rules; non-monotone recursion, arbitrary UDAFs over differences, and non-*local* computations (global sort, top-k with retractions) need careful manual derivation. DBSP extends algebraic coverage [3], but compaction and partitioning stay manual.

**Operational complexity.** Arrangements are a storage engine smuggled into a dataflow library: compaction policy, cross-batch memory accounting, and partition skew are production problems. Materialize's answer is to *be* a database [2]; for library users, the operational surface is the price of performance.

**Evaluation gaps.** Published numbers remain graph analytics and relational workloads; no systematic iterative-ML study at Naiad's scale exists for the Rust implementation, and the COST comparison [4] is a decade old — engines improved, the point stands.

**The partial-order ceiling.** Timestamp incomparability captures *logical* concurrency; work *within* one timestamp still needs key-partitioned data parallelism, whose skew the time model cannot see.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Timely dataflow's enduring idea: **coordination should be derived from the structure of time** — partial-order timestamps plus frontier tracking yield batch throughput, streaming latency, and nested iteration from one mechanism [1]. Differential dataflow's: **state should be kept as differences, not snapshots** — difference traces make iteration incremental, arrangements make differences shareable, delta joins scale joins with the change [2]. DBSP algebraized incrementalization [3]; COST taught honest evaluation [4]. Open problems remain — automatic delta-rule derivation, adaptive timestamp granularity, arrangement memory management under skew, and a pedagogy that makes frontier debugging routine — but the direction is set: the batch/streaming/iterative trichotomy was an artifact of total-order thinking, and partially-ordered logical time is its successor.
