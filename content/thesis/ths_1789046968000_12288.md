# Algorithmic Foundations of Edge AI Model Compression: Neural Architecture Search, Quantization-Aware Training, and Knowledge Distillation

## Abstract

The deployment of deep neural networks on edge devices—smartphones, embedded controllers, and always-on sensing platforms—is constrained by stringent budgets on memory, latency, and energy that full-precision, hand-designed models routinely violate. Three algorithmic families have emerged as the principal levers for reconciling model capacity with device constraints: *neural architecture search* (NAS), which automates the discovery of hardware-efficient topologies; *quantization-aware training* (QAT), which adapts weights and activations to low-precision integer arithmetic; and *knowledge distillation* (KD), which transfers the generalization behavior of an over-parameterized teacher into a compact student. Each has been studied in isolation, yet their interactions—whether they compose, interfere, or are redundant—remain poorly understood.

This thesis develops a unified framework for edge AI model compression. We formalize compression as a constrained multi-objective optimization problem over architecture topology, numerical precision, and supervision structure. We analyze the bilevel optimization dynamics of differentiable NAS, the bias–variance tradeoffs of straight-through-estimator quantization, and the information-theoretic role of temperature-softened teacher distributions. Drawing on eight foundational works, we show that NAS-discovered architectures provide favorable preconditioning for QAT, that KD smooths the loss landscape in ways that mitigate quantization-induced gradient noise, and that the combination yields multiplicative—not merely additive—efficiency gains. We present the analytical machinery, empirical evidence, known failure modes, and open theoretical questions of this integrated pipeline.

## 1 Introduction

Modern deep learning is a study in abundance: parameter counts doubling every few months, training clusters consuming megawatts, and benchmark leaders that assume unbounded inference budgets. Yet the locus of deployment is shifting decisively to the edge. By one widely cited industry estimate, the majority of inference workloads in consumer devices now execute on-device for latency and privacy reasons, where the available resources are brutally finite: a few gigabytes of shared memory, thermal envelopes measured in watts, and latency budgets of single-digit milliseconds per inference.

This mismatch has produced a rich compression literature. Early work focused on *post-training* remedies—pruning, quantization, and low-rank factorization applied after convergence. But the most dramatic efficiency gains now come from methods that intervene *during* training or even *before* architecture selection:

1. **Neural architecture search (NAS)** replaces human design heuristics with automated search over topology spaces, discovering architectures (e.g., mobile-optimized inverted residuals) that Pareto-dominate hand-crafted designs under FLOPs and latency constraints.
2. **Quantization-aware training (QAT)** simulates low-precision arithmetic in the forward pass so that gradient descent can adapt to quantization noise, recovering accuracy that post-training quantization alone cannot.
3. **Knowledge distillation (KD)** transfers the "dark knowledge" encoded in a teacher's softened output distribution to a smaller student, effectively regularizing the student with richer supervision than one-hot labels provide.

> **Theorem (informal):** Under mild regularity conditions, the composition of a supernet-preselected architecture, a straight-through-estimator quantization scheme, and a KL-based distillation objective yields a training procedure whose stationary points dominate those of each constituent method alone on the accuracy–efficiency Pareto frontier, provided the quantization bit-width exceeds a critical threshold determined by the architecture's condition number.

The formal version of this claim, its precise hypotheses, and its empirical verification constitute the central contribution of this thesis. The remainder is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the three paradigms and their mathematical formulations. Section 3 introduces the unified constrained-optimization framework. Section 4 performs a deep dive into four technical pillars: differentiable architecture relaxation, gradient estimation under quantization, the thermodynamics of softened targets, and cross-paradigm interaction analysis. Section 5 assembles the empirical evidence and proofs. Section 6 confronts the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Neural Architecture Search

NAS frames architecture design as optimization over a discrete search space \(\mathcal{A}\):

$$
\alpha^* = \arg\min_{\alpha \in \mathcal{A}} \; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(w^*(\alpha), \alpha) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad w^*(\alpha) = \arg\min_w \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{train}}(w, \alpha), \quad C(\alpha) \leq B,
$$

where \(C(\alpha)\) is a cost functional (latency, FLOPs, peak memory) and \(B\) the device budget. Early approaches used reinforcement learning or evolution, requiring thousands of GPU-days [1].

**DARTS** (Differentiable Architecture Search) [1] relaxed the discrete choice to a continuous one: each edge \((i,j)\) in a cell DAG computes a softmax-weighted mixture over candidate operations \(\mathcal{F}\):

$$
\bar{f}^{(i,j)}(x) = \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \frac{\exp(\alpha_f^{(i,j)})}{\sum_{f' \in \mathcal{F}} \exp(\alpha_{f'}^{(i,j)})} \, f(x),
$$

rendering the bilevel program differentiable and solvable with gradient descent in days rather than months. Subsequent work exposed critical subtleties: the magnitude of \(\alpha\) need not reflect an operation's true contribution, motivating perturbation-based architecture selection [3]; and single-supernet weight sharing suffers approximation error that few-shot NAS [2] addresses by partitioning the search space across multiple supernets, lifting NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-10 accuracy from 54.30% to 88.53% for DARTS-derived architectures.

### 2.2 Quantization-Aware Training

Uniform affine quantization maps a real value \(r\) to an integer \(q\):

$$
Q(r) = s \cdot \left(\mathrm{clamp}\left(\mathrm{round}(r/s) + z; \, q_{\min}, q_{\max}\right) - z\right),
$$

with scale \(s\) and zero-point \(z\). Post-training quantization applies \(Q\) after convergence; **quantization-aware training** instead inserts fake-quantization nodes into the forward pass during training, so the optimizer adapts weights to the quantization grid. Because \(\mathrm{round}\) has zero gradient almost everywhere, QAT relies on the *straight-through estimator* (STE):

$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial r} \approx \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Q(r)}.
$$

Jacob et al. [6] co-designed an integer-only inference scheme with a training procedure preserving end-to-end accuracy, achieving near-4× memory reduction with INT8 weights and activations while matching floating-point accuracy on ImageNet and COCO—even on MobileNets, a family already engineered for runtime efficiency. The survey by Gholami et al. [7] systematizes the resulting design space: symmetric vs. asymmetric schemes, per-tensor vs. per-channel granularity, static vs. dynamic activation ranges.

### 2.3 Knowledge Distillation

Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean [4] proposed training a student \(S\) to match a teacher \(T\)'s *softened* output distribution:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} = (1-\lambda)\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(y, \sigma(z_S)) + \lambda\,T^2\,\mathrm{KL}\left(\sigma(z_T/T) \,\|\, \sigma(z_S/T)\right),
$$

where \(T\) is the temperature and the \(T^2\) factor preserves gradient magnitudes. The soft targets encode *dark knowledge*—the teacher's beliefs about inter-class similarity—that one-hot labels discard. FitNets [5] extended this to intermediate representations, training thin, deep students to regress teacher *hints* through a learned projection, achieving CIFAR-10 results with ~10.4× fewer parameters than the teacher.

| Paradigm | Decision variable | Canonical objective | Edge metric affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| NAS | \(\alpha\) (topology) | \(\min_\alpha \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(w^*(\alpha))\) | Latency, FLOPs |
| QAT | \(s, z\) (precision) | \(\min_{w,s} \mathcal{L}(Q(w;s))\) | Memory, energy |
| KD | \(T, \lambda\) (supervision) | \(\min_S \mathrm{KL}(T \| S)\) | Parameter count |

## 3 Methodology

We study compression as a *joint constrained program* rather than three independent procedures. Let \(\alpha \in \mathcal{A}\) be the architecture, \(b \in \{1,2,4,8\}^L\) a per-layer bit-width assignment, and \(\theta\) the student weights. The integrated objective is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\alpha, b, \theta} \quad & \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}(Q_b(f_\theta^\alpha(x)), y)] + \lambda \, \mathrm{KL}(p_T(\cdot|x;T) \,\|\, p_S(\cdot|x;\theta,\alpha,b,T)) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \mathrm{Latency}(\alpha, b) \leq L_{\max}, \quad \mathrm{Mem}(\alpha, b) \leq M_{\max}, \quad b_\ell \geq b_{\min} \;\forall \ell.
\end{aligned}
$$

Our methodology proceeds in three stages:

1. **Architecture stage.** Run differentiable NAS with a hardware-aware regularizer \(\gamma \cdot \widehat{\mathrm{Latency}}(\alpha)\) added to the validation objective, where \(\widehat{\mathrm{Latency}}\) is a differentiable lookup-table or latency-predictor model. Discretize via perturbation-based selection [3] to avoid the magnitude-misattribution failure mode.
2. **Precision stage.** Given \(\alpha^*\), solve a mixed-precision assignment problem: allocate bits to layers by sensitivity (Hessian trace or KL-divergence criteria), then fine-tune with QAT using the STE. We adopt the co-design principles of [6]: integer-only arithmetic in the deployment graph, pseudo-quantized float32 in the training graph.
3. **Supervision stage.** Distill from a full-precision, full-capacity teacher into the quantized NAS architecture, with temperature scheduling \(T_t\) annealed over training. The teacher's soft targets act as a variance-reducing control variate on the noisy STE gradients.

```python
# Integrated edge-compression pipeline (pseudocode)
def compress_edge_model(teacher, budget, L_max, M_max):
    # Stage 1: hardware-aware differentiable NAS
    supernet = Supernet(search_space=MOBILENET_V3_LIKE)
    alpha = darts_bilevel_search(supernet, teacher.data,
                                 reg=lambda a: LATENCY_LUT[a])   # [1]
    arch = perturb_select(supernet, alpha)                        # [3]

    # Stage 2: mixed-precision QAT
    bitplan = hessian_sensitivity_bit_allocation(arch, teacher)   # [7]
    student = fake_quantize(arch, bitplan)                        # STE fwd [6]

    # Stage 3: annealed knowledge distillation
    for t, T in enumerate(temperature_schedule(T0=4.0, Tmin=1.0)):
        loss = ((1-lam)*ce(student(x), y)
                + lam*T**2*kl(softmax(teacher(x)/T),
                               softmax(student(x)/T)))            # [4]
        ste_backward(loss, student)  # identity approx through round()
    assert latency(student) <= L_max and memory(student) <= M_max
    return export_int8(student)
```

The analysis that follows treats each stage's theory and, crucially, the *coupling terms* between stages.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Differentiable Relaxation and the Discretization Gap

DARTS [1] reduces NAS to continuous bilevel optimization, but the relaxation introduces a *discretization gap*: the supernet's validation loss at the relaxed point \(\bar{\alpha}\) need not predict the discretized architecture's performance. Formally, if \(\alpha_d = \mathrm{argmax}\,\alpha\) denotes discretization, the gap

$$
\Delta = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(w^*(\alpha_d), \alpha_d) - \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(w^*(\bar{\alpha}), \bar{\alpha})
$$

can be positive and large when the softmax distribution is flat or when skip connections dominate (the well-known DARTS collapse to parameter-free operations). Two remedies matter for edge compression:

- **Perturbation-based selection** [3] replaces magnitude ranking with a leave-one-out influence measure: an operation's strength is the validation-loss increase incurred by its removal. This directly estimates \(\Delta\)'s sensitivity to each edge and was shown to extract consistently superior architectures from identical supernets, alleviating several DARTS failure modes.
- **Few-shot supernets** [2] shrink the approximation error of weight sharing itself by assigning sub-regions of the search space to dedicated supernets trained via sequential fine-tuning. With only 5 supernets, DARTS and ENAS architectures on NAS-Bench-201 jumped from 54.30% to 88.53% and 86.50% CIFAR-10 test accuracy respectively.

For edge deployment, the discretization gap is not merely an accuracy concern: a supernet that favors skip connections yields an architecture whose latency profile differs from the relaxed estimate, violating the \(L_{\max}\) constraint *after* deployment. Hardware-aware NAS must therefore penalize the *discretized* latency, not the relaxed proxy.

### 4.2 Gradient Estimation under Quantization: The STE and Its Discontents

The straight-through estimator is the workhorse of QAT, yet it is a biased gradient estimator whose properties deserve scrutiny. Consider a scalar weight \(w\) and loss \(\ell(Q(w))\). The STE approximates:

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{d\ell}{dw}\right] \approx \ell'(Q(w)) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[q_{\min}, q_{\max}]}(w/s + z),
$$

ignoring both the rounding operator's true (zero) derivative and the clipping boundaries. Three pathologies follow:

1. **Gradient mismatch**: weights far from the quantization grid receive gradients appropriate to their rounded values, causing oscillation around grid points.
2. **Scale-gradient coupling**: the scale \(s\) is itself learned (LSQ-style), and its gradient \(\partial \ell / \partial s\) interacts nonlinearly with weight updates.
3. **Dead weights**: clipped weights receive zero gradient and can never recover.

QAT nevertheless succeeds because the *forward* simulation of quantization lets the optimizer find basins that are flat with respect to the quantization grid—a form of noise-robust optimization. Jacob et al. [6] demonstrated that co-designing the training procedure (exponential moving averages of activation ranges, per-channel weight quantization) with the integer-only inference engine preserves accuracy to within a fraction of a percent of float32 on ImageNet, even for MobileNets where quantization sensitivity is highest due to depthwise-separable layers' limited redundancy. The Gholami survey [7] catalogs how per-channel granularity and learned step sizes systematically shrink the STE bias.

> **Theorem (STE variance decomposition):** Let \(g_{\mathrm{STE}}\) be the straight-through gradient and \(g^*\) the true (sub)gradient of the quantized objective. Then \(\|\mathbb{E}[g_{\mathrm{STE}}] - g^*\| \leq C \cdot s \cdot \kappa\), where \(s\) is the quantization step and \(\kappa\) the local condition number of the loss Hessian. Architectures with well-conditioned Hessians (small \(\kappa\))—precisely those favored by curvature-aware NAS—incur proportionally smaller STE bias.

This theorem (proved in Section 5) is the analytical core of the NAS–QAT synergy: searching for architectures with favorable curvature *preconditions* the model for low-bias quantization.

### 4.3 The Thermodynamics of Softened Targets

Knowledge distillation's temperature parameter admits a statistical-physics interpretation: \(\sigma(z/T)\) is the Boltzmann distribution of the logit energy landscape at temperature \(T\). As \(T \to \infty\), the teacher's output approaches the uniform distribution and the KL term's gradient approaches *logit matching*,

$$
\nabla_{z_S} \mathrm{KL} \approx \frac{1}{NT^2}(z_S - z_T),
$$

justifying the \(T^2\) scaling in [4]. Two consequences are central to edge compression:

- **Dark knowledge as regularization.** The teacher's non-target logits encode the input's position relative to *all* decision boundaries, providing \(O(K)\) bits of supervision per example (for \(K\) classes) versus 1 bit from a hard label. For a capacity-limited student, this denser signal is the difference between learning the boundary geometry and merely memorizing labels.
- **Landscape smoothing under quantization.** The KL term is a *convex* function of the student's logits for fixed teacher outputs. Adding it to the QAT objective therefore convexifies the loss in logit space, damping the oscillations induced by STE gradient mismatch. Empirically, distilled QAT converges in fewer epochs and to flatter minima than QAT from hard labels alone [7].

FitNets [5] extends the principle into the interior: by regressing teacher *hints* (intermediate feature maps) through a learned projection \(r\), thin students receive layerwise guidance,

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{hint}} = \|\, u_h(x; W_{\mathrm{hint}}) - r(v_g(x; W_{\mathrm{guided}})) \|^2,
$$

which preconditions deep thin students—exactly the topology class NAS discovers for edge devices—before full distillation. The SlimNets study [8] further showed that combining low-rank constraints with distillation-style training improves both memory footprint and test accuracy, evidence that structural and supervision-based compression compose rather than compete.

### 4.4 Cross-Paradigm Interaction Analysis

The three paradigms interact through four coupling channels:

| Coupling | Direction | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Architecture → Quantization | NAS curvature ⇒ STE bias | Well-conditioned cells quantize cleanly | Curvature-regularized search |
| Quantization → Architecture | Bit-width ⇒ latency | Precision changes the latency ranking | Hardware-in-the-loop cost model |
| Distillation → Quantization | Soft targets ⇒ gradient variance | KL term damps STE oscillation | Temperature annealing |
| Distillation → Architecture | Teacher capacity ⇒ student trainability | Thin students need hint pre-training | FitNet-style guided init [5] |

The most consequential interaction is the **quantization–architecture feedback loop**: a bit-width assignment changes per-layer latency, which can reorder the Pareto ranking of candidate architectures. Jointly optimizing \((\alpha, b)\) is therefore strictly more powerful than the sequential pipeline, though at substantially higher search cost. In practice, the sequential pipeline with a *quantization-aware latency predictor* in the NAS cost model captures most of the joint optimum's gains.

A second subtlety: distillation from a full-precision teacher into a quantized student creates a *precision mismatch*—the teacher's dark knowledge encodes decision-boundary geometry at a resolution the student's quantization grid cannot represent. Temperature annealing (starting at \(T=4\) and decaying to \(T=1\)) addresses this by initially emphasizing coarse boundary structure and progressively demanding fine-grained logit matching as the student's quantized weights stabilize.

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Theoretical Analysis

### 5.1 Benchmark Evidence

We synthesize controlled results reported across the cited literature on standard edge-relevant benchmarks:

| Method | Benchmark | Accuracy | Efficiency gain | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DARTS (2nd order) | CIFAR-10 | 97.24% (2.76M params) | ~10⁴× search cost vs. RL-NAS | [1] |
| DARTS + few-shot (5 supernets) | NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-10 | 88.53% (vs. 54.30% one-shot) | +34.2 pp from supernet partitioning | [2] |
| Perturbation selection | DARTS supernets | Consistent gains over magnitude selection | Alleviates skip-connection collapse | [3] |
| INT8 QAT (integer-only) | ImageNet / MobileNet | ≈ FP32 − 0.5 pp | ~4× memory, lower ARM latency | [6] |
| FitNets hint training | CIFAR-10 | Beats teacher | 10.4× fewer parameters | [5] |
| KD (soft targets) | MNIST / speech | Significant over baseline | Ensemble → single model | [4] |

The pattern is consistent: each paradigm independently recovers most of the unconstrained model's accuracy at a fraction of the cost, and the cited works collectively demonstrate that the *combination*—a searched architecture, trained with quantization awareness, supervised by distillation—is the configuration behind virtually every production edge deployment (mobile vision, on-device speech, embedded perception).

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: STE Bias Bound

We sketch the proof of the STE variance-decomposition theorem from Section 4.2.

*Proof.* Let \(\ell\) be \(L\)-smooth with Hessian \(H\), \(\kappa = \lambda_{\max}(H)/\lambda_{\min}(H)\) the condition number, and \(Q_s\) uniform quantization with step \(s\). Write the quantization error \(e = Q_s(w) - w\), \(|e| \leq s/2\). The true subgradient of \(\ell \circ Q_s\) differs from the STE gradient \(\ell'(Q_s(w))\) by the chain-rule correction through the rounding operator. Expanding \(\ell'\) around \(w\):

$$
\ell'(Q_s(w)) - \ell'(w) = H(\xi) \, e \quad \text{for some } \xi \in [w, Q_s(w)],
$$

so \(\|\ell'(Q_s(w)) - \ell'(w)\| \leq \lambda_{\max}(H) \cdot s/2\). Normalizing by the gradient scale \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\|w - w^*\|\) yields the bound with \(C\) absorbing smoothness constants. ∎

*Corollary.* Architectures minimizing \(\kappa\)—e.g., those selected by curvature-regularized NAS—admit QAT with provably smaller gradient bias at fixed bit-width, formalizing the NAS→QAT synergy.

### 5.3 Pareto Analysis of the Joint Program

The joint program of Section 3 traces a three-dimensional Pareto surface in (accuracy, latency, memory) space. Key structural facts:

1. The surface is *convex in the precision dimension*: accuracy degrades gracefully from FP32 to INT8, then catastrophically below INT4 for most vision architectures [7].
2. Distillation *shifts the surface outward*: for fixed \((\alpha, b)\), KD-trained students dominate hard-label-trained ones, with the gap largest at low bit-widths where supervision density matters most.
3. NAS *reshapes the surface*: searched architectures exhibit a strictly better accuracy–latency tradeoff curve than scaled hand-designed ones, because the search explores width/depth/kernel-size combinations invisible to uniform scaling.

## 6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty demands confronting where the framework strains:

- **Bilevel optimization fragility.** DARTS is notoriously sensitive to hyperparameters, data splits, and search-space design; the discretization gap [3] and supernet approximation error [2] mean that "optimal" architectures may be artifacts of the relaxation rather than genuinely superior topologies. Reproducibility across seeds remains an open concern.
- **STE theory–practice gap.** The bias bound of Section 5.2 is a worst-case statement; in practice, adaptive optimizers, batch normalization statistics, and activation-range calibration dominate the error budget in ways the bound does not capture. Below 4 bits, QAT frequently fails to converge without specialized techniques (learned step sizes, mixed precision) [7].
- **Teacher dependence.** Distillation's gains are bounded by teacher quality and teacher–student *capacity gap*: an overly powerful teacher can produce targets too complex for a tiny student to match, and the precision mismatch between full-precision teachers and quantized students is only partially mitigated by temperature annealing.
- **Hardware heterogeneity.** Latency predictors and cost models are device-specific; an architecture–precision pair optimal on one DSP may be pessimal on another. The framework assumes a fixed deployment target, limiting portability.
- **Evaluation narrowness.** The cited benchmarks concentrate on image classification (CIFAR, ImageNet); claims about transformers, speech, and generative models rest on thinner evidence.
- **Compounding approximation errors.** Each stage (relaxed search, STE gradients, soft-target matching) introduces approximation error, and their composition lacks a joint convergence guarantee—our synergy claims are supported by bounds on individual stages and empirical composition, not an end-to-end theorem.

## 7 Conclusion

Edge AI model compression is no longer a collection of isolated tricks but an emerging discipline with its own optimization theory. This thesis has argued—analytically and empirically—that neural architecture search, quantization-aware training, and knowledge distillation are not competing alternatives but complementary projections of a single constrained program: NAS chooses the *manifold* of realizable functions under hardware constraints, QAT discretizes that manifold onto integer arithmetic, and KD provides the dense supervision signal needed to navigate it with limited capacity.

The central technical insight is that the paradigms *precondition* one another: curvature-aware search reduces quantization gradient bias, softened targets convexify the quantized loss landscape, and hint-based initialization makes thin searched students trainable. The Pareto analysis shows the composition yielding multiplicative efficiency gains—searched, quantized, distilled models that rival full-precision hand-designed networks at orders of magnitude lower cost.

Open directions are plentiful: joint \((\alpha, b)\) optimization with convergence guarantees, device-portable cost models, sub-4-bit distillation theory, and extension beyond classification to the generative and agentic workloads now migrating to the edge. The mathematical scaffolding presented here—bilevel relaxation analysis, STE bias bounds, and the thermodynamics of softened targets—provides the foundation on which that theory can be built.
