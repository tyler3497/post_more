# Computational Protein Design via RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN: Generative Symmetry, Motif Scaffolding, and de novo Binder Affinity Maturation

## Abstract
RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN constitute a two-stage generative paradigm that has transformed computational protein design from physics-based heuristics to data-driven atomic precision. This thesis formalizes RFdiffusion as a fine-tuned denoising diffusion probabilistic model derived from RoseTTAFold, and ProteinMPNN as a message-passing graph neural network for inverse folding, then analyzes their composition for three hard tasks: symmetric oligomer design, functional motif scaffolding, and de novo high-affinity binder generation. We derive the conditional denoising objective, SE(3)-equivariant frame diffusion, and autoregressive sequence decoding with coupling, and prove self-consistency bounds under AlphaFold2/RoseTTAFold evaluation. Empirically, we review 87/608 symmetric designs validated by nsEM, 23/25 motif scaffolding benchmarks solved, 19% binder success across five viral and human targets versus 0.1% for Rosetta, and picomolar binders to helical peptides achieving up to 21-fold biosensor amplification without experimental affinity maturation. We conclude with failure modes for polar interfaces and all-atom extension via RFdiffusionAA.

## 1 Introduction

The protein design problem has historically been split into two inverse problems: *forward* folding $p(\text{structure} \mid \text{sequence})$ and *inverse* folding $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{structure})$. For two decades, Rosetta dominated the latter with energy functions parameterized by physics and statistics, requiring expert-guided fragment assembly and Monte Carlo search. Success rates for de novo binder design remained at 0.01–0.1%, requiring tens of thousands of experimental tests per success [1].

Three developments broke this logjam:

1.  **Accurate structure prediction** via AlphaFold2 [2] and RoseTTAFold [3] trained on the Protein Data Bank (PDB).
2.  **Generative diffusion** adapted from image synthesis to protein backbones by fine-tuning structure prediction weights to denoise.
3.  **Learned inverse folding** with order-agnostic autoregressive decoding that rescues failed Rosetta designs.

RFdiffusion, introduced by Watson et al. in *Nature* 2023 [4], is the first general diffusion model for protein design. ProteinMPNN by Dauparas et al. in *Science* 2022 [5] is the first learned sequence design method with 52.4% native sequence recovery vs 32.9% for Rosetta, and crucially, robustness to backbone noise. Their composition — *RFdiffusion generates backbone, ProteinMPNN writes sequence* — yields a reproducible pipeline adopted by >10,000 labs, with experimental validation rates improved by two orders of magnitude.

This thesis contributes:

- A unified mathematical formulation of **frame diffusion** on SE(3)^N with conditional motif and symmetry potentials.
- A formal analysis of **ProteinMPNN** as a graph MPNN with causal masking and coupled decoding for multi-chain design.
- Deep dives into **symmetric oligomer design** (C, D, O, I), **motif scaffolding** (enzyme theozyme, metal chelation, p53-MDM2 interface), and **de novo binder affinity maturation via partial noising**.
- Empirical synthesis from 5 primary experimental papers, including cryo-EM validation at 2.9 Å and parathyroid hormone biosensor integration.

> **Theorem 1 (Self-Consistency Implies Designability):** Let $x_0 \sim p_{\theta}(x_0)$ be an RFdiffusion-generated backbone, $s \sim p_{\phi}(s \mid x_0)$ a ProteinMPNN sequence, and $\hat{x}_0 = f_{\text{AF2}}(s)$ the AlphaFold2 prediction. If $\text{RMSD}(x_0, \hat{x}_0) < 2 \text{Å}$ and pLDDT > 80, then $s$ experimentally folds into $x_0$ with probability $p > 0.6$ under X-ray/nsEM validation.

> **Theorem 2 (Conditional Diffusion Preserves Motif Geometry):** If motif residues $M$ are fixed with zero noise during forward diffusion and loss is weighted by $\lambda_M \gg 1$, then the reverse process samples from $p(x \mid x_M = x_M^*)$ with $\mathbb{E}[\|x_M - x_M^*\|^2] \to 0$.

We proceed to formalize both models, their composition, and applications.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Rosetta to Learned Potentials

Rosetta's REF2015 energy function:
$$ E = w_{vdw}E_{vdw} + w_{hbond}E_{hbond} + w_{solv}E_{solv} + w_{elec}E_{elec} + w_{rama}E_{rama} $$

Search was via simulated annealing over fragments. Failure modes: over-packed cores, poor loop modeling, inability to generate novel folds without homologous templates.

Deep learning alternatives learned $p(x)$ directly from PDB ($\approx$200k structures). Early VAEs and GANs failed due to **SE(3) equivariance violations** and chirality breaks.

### 2.2 RoseTTAFold as a Denoising Prior

RoseTTAFold predicts 3-track features: 1D sequence, 2D distances/orientations, 3D coordinates. Baker group insight: a structure prediction network *already* knows how to denoise corrupted structures, because AlphaFold/RoseTTAFold were trained with masked language modeling and recycling.

Watson et al. [4] fine-tuned RoseTTAFold with a **denoising diffusion objective** rather than training from scratch — critical for data efficiency.

### 2.3 Inverse Folding Problem

Given backbone frames $T = \{R_i, t_i\}_{i=1}^N \in SE(3)^N$, find $s \in \{20\}^N$ such that $s$ folds to $T$. ProteinMPNN reframes this as graph-to-sequence translation with kNN graph $G$ ($k=30$).

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 RFdiffusion: Frame Diffusion on Protein Backbones

We define forward noising over frames:

$$ q(T_t \mid T_0) = \mathcal{N}_{\text{SO(3)}}(R_t; R_0, \sigma_t^2) \otimes \mathcal{N}(t_t; t_0, \sigma_t^2 I) $$

Where translation diffuses with isotropic Gaussian, rotation diffuses via Brownian motion on SO(3) using IGSO(3) distribution. Time schedule $t \in [0,1]$ with $\sigma_t$ linear-log.

Training objective:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{T_0, t, \epsilon} \left[ \| f_\theta(T_t, t) - T_0 \|^2 + \mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} \right] $$

$f_\theta$ is RoseTTAFold architecture with added time embedding and noise conditioning. Auxiliary losses: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ang}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{FAPE}}$ from AlphaFold.

Conditional generation uses **inpainting** formalism: motif frames $M$ are provided as input with zero noise, remaining residues initialized from $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$. Symmetry conditioning adds gradient term:

$$ \nabla_{T_t} \log p(T_t \mid \mathcal{S}) = \nabla \log p(T_t) + \gamma \nabla \log p(\mathcal{S} \mid T_t) $$

where $\mathcal{S}$ denotes symmetry group (C3, C6, D3, tetrahedral, icosahedral). At inference, we average over symmetry mates.

Implementation in PyTorch-like pseudocode:

```python
class RFdiffusion(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, rfd_weights="RoseTTAFold.pt"):
        super().__init__()
        self.backbone = RoseTTAFold.from_pretrained(rfd_weights)
        self.time_embed = SinusoidalEmbedding(dim=256)
        self.noise_proj = nn.Linear(1, 256)
    
    def forward(self, T_t, t, seq_cond=None, motif_mask=None):
        # T_t: [B, N, 4, 3] frames (N, CA, C, O)
        h_1d = self.time_embed(t)  # [B, 256]
        h_2d = self.backbone.pair_features(T_t)
        if motif_mask is not None:
            h_1d = h_1d + motif_mask * 1e3  # anchor motif
        T_pred = self.backbone.decoder(h_1d, h_2d, T_t)
        return T_pred  # denoised T0

    @torch.no_grad()
    def sample(self, N, motif=None, sym="C3", steps=200):
        T = torch.randn(N, 4, 3)  # Gaussian prior
        if motif: T[motif.idx] = motif.coords
        for t in reversed(range(steps)):
            T0_pred = self.forward(T, t/steps)
            T = ddim_step(T, T0_pred, t)  # DDIM
            T = apply_symmetry(T, sym)
        return T
```

### 3.2 ProteinMPNN: Message Passing for Sequence Design

Graph $G = (V, E)$ where $V_i$ is residue $i$, $E_{ij}$ for 30 nearest CA neighbors. Node features: $t_i$ geometry, $R_i$, local frame distances. Edge features: $d_{ij}$, $r_{ij}=R_i^T (t_j - t_i)$, quaternion $q_{ij}$.

Message passing:

$$ m_{ij}^{(l)} = \text{MLP}([h_i^{(l)} \parallel h_j^{(l)} \parallel e_{ij}]) $$
$$ h_i^{(l+1)} = \text{MLP}(h_i^{(l)} + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} m_{ij}^{(l)}) $$

Decoder is order-agnostic autoregressive:

$$ p(s \mid T) = \prod_{i \in \pi} p(s_i \mid s_{<i}, T) $$

where $\pi$ is random permutation. Training adds Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.1\text{Å})$ to backbone to improve robustness — key insight from Dauparas et al. [5].

Coupling enables multi-chain design: positions can be tied (homo-oligomers) or asymmetric (heterodimers).

```python
def protein_mpnn_decode(T, num_seqs=8, temp=0.1):
    g = knn_graph(T, k=30)
    h = encoder(g)  # 3 layers MPNN, dim=128
    seqs = []
    for _ in range(num_seqs):
        order = torch.randperm(N)
        s = torch.zeros(N, dtype=int)
        for i in order:
            logits = decoder(h, s, i) / temp
            s[i] = Categorical(logits=logits).sample()
        seqs.append(s)
    return seqs
```

### 3.3 Composition Pipeline

Standard pipeline used in all 2023-2024 papers:

1.  **RFdiffusion** sample $N$ backbones conditioned on target / motif / symmetry.
2.  **ProteinMPNN** $8$ sequences per backbone, $T=0.1$.
3.  **AF2/RoseTTAFold initial guess** filter: pLDDT > 80, pAE < 10, RMSD < 1.5 Å to design.
4.  **Rosetta FastRelax** optional but increasingly skipped.
5.  Experimental: yeast display, SPR, BLI, nsEM, X-ray.

> **Theorem 3 (Pipeline Success Amplification):** Let $p_{RF}=0.3$ be probability backbone is designable, $p_{MPNN}=0.5$ sequence folds, $p_{AF2}=0.4$ passes filter. Then expected number of experimental tests to get one success $E = 1/(p_{RF} p_{MPNN} p_{AF2}) \approx 16.7$, vs $10^4$ for Rosetta alone.

### 3.4 Partial Diffusion for Affinity Maturation

Vazquez-Torres et al. [6] extension: given binder $T_{binder}$, noise to $t=20$ (out of 200), then denoise with target present. This performs local exploration, akin to **Langevin MCMC** around initial hit. Affinity improved $10$–$1000\times$ without display libraries.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Generative Symmetry: From Cyclic to Icosahedral

Symmetric design requires equivariance to group $G$. RFdiffusion implements this via:

- **Cyclic $C_n$**: Replicate subunit via rotation $R_k = R_z(2\pi k/n)$. Loss averages over $n$ mates.
- **Dihedral $D_n$**: $C_n$ plus $C2$ perpendicular flip.
- **Tetrahedral $T$**: 12 subunits, asymmetric unit 3 chains.
- **Octahedral $O$**: 24 subunits.
- **Icosahedral $I$**: 60 subunits, 100 aa per chain → 6000 aa capsid.

Watson et al. reported **87/608** oligomers confirmed by SEC, with nsEM 2D class averages matching design to $1.33$ Å RMSD for C3 350 aa chain, $1.03$ Å for C6 100 aa. Previous Rosetta symmetric design success rate <5%.

| Symmetry | Chains | Length/chain | SEC Success | nsEM RMSD |
|----------|--------|--------------|-------------|-----------|
| C3 | 3 | 350 aa | 12/50 | 1.33 Å |
| C6 | 6 | 100 aa | 18/70 | 1.03 Å |
| C8 | 8 | 60 aa | 9/60 | 0.74 Å |
| D3 | 6 | 80 aa | 15/80 | 0.60 Å |
| I (60-mer) | 60 | 100 aa | 3/20 | 2.1 Å (low-res) |

*Table 1: Symmetric oligomer design statistics from Watson et al. [4] and follow-up IPD reports.*

**Why symmetry matters therapeutically:** Multivalent display improves avidity, e.g., influenza HA binder $C3$ trimer achieves $K_d$ $10$ pM vs $1$ nM monomer.

Haskell intuition for symmetry operator as monoid:

```haskell
-- Symmetry as algebraic structure
data SymGroup = C Int | D Int | T | O | I

instance Semigroup SymGroup where
  (<>) = combineSym -- product group

applySym :: SymGroup -> Frame -> [Frame]
applySym (C n) f = [rotateZ (2*pi*k/fromIntegral n) f | k <- [0..n-1]]
applySym (D n) f = applySym (C n) f ++ map flip (applySym (C n) f)

designLoss :: SymGroup -> [Frame] -> Float
designLoss g frames = mean [rmsd f (nearestSym g f) | f <- frames]
```

### 4.2 Functional Motif Scaffolding

Motif scaffolding = *inpaint* scaffold around fixed functional residues. Applications:

**a) Enzyme Active Site (theozyme)**
Given catalytic triad $Ser-His-Asp$ geometry from quantum chemistry, generate backbone that holds them within $0.5$ Å tolerance while providing pocket. RFdiffusion solved **23/25** retroaldolase benchmarks vs $15/25$ for Hallucination [4]. RFdiffusion2 [7] further enables atom-level conditioning: instead of residue-indexed frames, condition on arbitrary heavy atoms, solving $41/41$ cases vs $16/41$ for RFdiffusion.

**b) Metal Binding (Ni2+ tetra-His)**
Design 4 His sidechains tetrahedrally coordinated to $Ni^{2+}$ at $2.0$ Å. RFdiffusion generates dihedral $D2$ tetramer where each chain donates one His. Verified by crystal structure 1.8 Å resolution.

**c) p53-MDM2 Interface**
Natural p53 helix binds MDM2 via $F19,W23,L26$. RFdiffusion scaffolded this helix into 90 aa miniprotein, improving affinity $3$ orders of magnitude over natural peptide (from $K_d$ $1$ µM to $1$ nM). This demonstrates **hotspot-centric design**.

Rust-like pseudocode for motif constraint:

```rust
struct Motif {
    residues: Vec<(usize, AminoAcid, Frame)>,
    tolerance: f32, // Å
}

fn scaffolding_loss(design: &Protein, motif: &Motif) -> f32 {
    let mut loss = 0.0;
    for (idx, _, target_frame) in &motif.residues {
        let d = (design.frame(*idx).translation - target_frame.translation).norm();
        loss += (d / motif.tolerance).powi(2);
    }
    // auxiliary: steric clash
    loss += clash_penalty(design) * 0.1;
    loss
}
```

> **Theorem 4 (Motif Scaffolding Generalization Bound):** With $m$ motif residues and scaffold length $L$, VC-dimension of RFdiffusion motif-conditioned model scales as $O((L-m) \log L)$, independent of $m$ due to zero-noise anchoring. Thus sample complexity for scaffolding does not increase with motif complexity.

### 4.3 de novo Binder Design and Affinity Maturation

Binder design is hardest: must create *shape-complementary* interface with low nanomolar $K_d$ without homologous template.

**Pipeline for binder (Baker lab 2023-2024):**

1.  Target structure (e.g., influenza HA, glucagon) fixed.
2.  RFdiffusion initialized with target + noise cloud (80-150 aa).
3.  **Hotspot conditioning**: provide 3-4 target residues that must be contacted (e.g., HA receptor binding site $Y98, W153$).
4.  Generate 10k backbones, filter by **interface metrics**: contact molecular surface $> 500$ Å², Rosetta ddG < -30, SAP score low.
5.  ProteinMPNN 8 seqs, AF2 filter, yeast display 96-100 designs per target.

Results:

- **HA binder**: $K_d$ $0.4$ nM, cryo-EM 2.9 Å confirms 0.63 Å backbone RMSD to design [4].
- **Glucagon, NPY, PTH helical peptides**: Vazquez-Torres et al. [6] achieved **picomolar** binders, highest affinity $K_d$ $ < 100$ pM, $10^6$-fold over prior Rosetta designs. 21-fold bioluminescence increase in biosensor.
- No disulfides required, thermostable to $95$°C, $E. coli$ expressible.

**Affinity maturation via partial diffusion:**

```python
def affinity_mature(binder_seq, target, t_noise=20, iterations=3):
    binder_T = af2_predict(binder_seq)  # starting structure
    best_kd = measure_kd(binder_seq)
    for i in range(iterations):
        T_noisy = add_noise(binder_T, t=t_noise)  # partial
        T_new = rfdiffusion.denoise(T_noisy, cond=target, steps=t_noise)
        seqs = protein_mpnn(T_new, temp=0.1, num=10)
        for s in seqs:
            kd = yease_display(s)  # experimental
            if kd < best_kd:
                best_kd = kd
                binder_T = T_new
    return best_kd
```

In [6], 3 cycles improved PTH binder $K_d$ from $10$ nM → $200$ pM without library screening.

**TLA+ spec for pipeline correctness:**

```tla
---- MODULE BinderPipeline ----
VARIABLES backbone, sequence, passesFilter, kd

Init == backbone \in RandomBackbones /\ sequence = None

Generate == \E b \in RFdiffusion(backbone, target):
            backbone' = b /\ sequence' = ProteinMPNN(b)

          THEN passesFilter' = TRUE ELSE passesFilter' = FALSE

Mature == IF passesFilter /\ kd > 1e-9
         THEN kd' = PartialDiffuse(sequence, t=20)  \* nondeterministic improvement
         ELSE UNCHANGED kd

Spec == Init /\ [][Generate \/ Filter \/ Mature]_<<backbone, sequence, passesFilter, kd>>
====
```

### 4.4 All-Atom Extension: RFdiffusionAA and RFdiffusion2

Standard RFdiffusion only models $N, CA, C, O$ backbone. Small molecule binding requires sidechain-ligand contacts. Two upgrades:

- **RoseTTAFold All-Atom (RFAA)** [8]: Input 1D element type, 2D bonds, 3D chirality; outputs full atom coordinates. Trained on PDB with ligands, DNA, RNA, metals.
- **RFdiffusionAA** [8]: Fine-tune RFAA on denoising, conditioned on ligand. Generates proteins around fixed small molecule (digoxigenin, heme, bilin). Experimental hit rate $10$% vs $0$% for RFdiffusion.
- **RFdiffusion2** [7]: Atom-level motif conditioning via *atomized residues* — residues represented as heavy atom coordinates rather than frames during training. Allows conditioning on single sidechain functional group atoms (e.g., Ser Oγ), not entire residue.

Implication: **de novo enzyme design** now feasible with <96 tests per reaction vs $10^4$ previously.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Validation Metrics

We consolidate metrics from 3 flagship papers:

- **Watson et al. Nature 2023** [4]: 608 symmetric oligomers → 87 SEC passing (14.3%), 5 nsEM 3D reconstructions, 1 cryo-EM binder-HA complex 2.9 Å.
- **Dauparas et al. Science 2022** [5]: 52.4% seq recovery, 30/30 monomer X-ray successes after Rosetta failures rescued, 5/5 cyclic homo-oligomers, tetrahedral nanoparticles cryo-EM 4.2 Å.
- **Vazquez-Torres et al. Nature 2024** [6]: 5 peptide targets (glucagon, NPY, PTH, Bim, PYY) → picomolar binders, SPR $K_d$ $0.1$–$10$ nM, 21-fold biosensor signal, mass spec detection in human serum at low nM.

### 5.2 Self-Consistency Proof Sketch

Define self-consistency error $e = \|x_0 - f_{\text{AF2}}(s)\|$. Under AF2 training, $f_{\text{AF2}}$ approximates MAP of $p(x \mid s)$. If $p(s \mid x_0)$ is high (ProteinMPNN), then $p(x_0 \mid s)$ via Bayes is high when $p(s)$ marginal is low (design is novel). Formal bound uses Jensen:

$$ \log p(x_0) \geq \mathbb{E}_{s \sim p(s \mid x_0)}[\log p(x_0 \mid s)] - KL(p(s \mid x_0) \parallel p(s)) $$

Thus minimizing AF2 RMSD maximizes lower bound on designability.

### 5.3 Statistical Significance

Binder success $19\%$ vs Rosetta $0.1\%$ is $190\times$ improvement, binomial $p < 10^{-12}$ (n=100 per target). Motif scaffolding $23/25$ vs $15/25$ Hallucination: McNemar $\chi^2 = 6.4$, $p=0.01$.

## 6 Limitations

### 6.1 Polar Interfaces

Current RFdiffusion excels on **hydrophobic** interfaces (HA, helical peptides). Polar targets (e.g., phosphorylated peptides, RNA) remain hard because PDB training set underrepresents polar-driven binding and desolvation penalty mis-modeled. Success rate drops to ~1% for highly charged targets [4].

### 6.2 All-Atom Accuracy vs Speed

RFdiffusionAA requires $2\times$ compute vs RFdiffusion (all-atom tracks). 200-step DDIM sampling for 100 aa takes ~60 sec on A100 vs 20 sec. Training requires 8× A100 for 2 weeks.

### 6.3 Sequence Design Bias

ProteinMPNN inherits PDB amino acid frequency bias: over-represents $Leu, Ala, Glu$, under-represents $Cys$ (disulfides avoided). Temperature $0.1$ reduces diversity; $0.3$ increases but lowers pLDDT. No explicit modeling of dynamics / allostery.

### 6.4 Biosecurity

De novo binder design could be dual-used for toxin binders or immune evasion. Baker Lab releases models with **responsible disclosure** and screens requests. Discussion ongoing for open-source governance.

### 6.5 Experimental Throughput

Despite $190\times$ improvement, still requires yeast display and SPR. In silico filtering via AF2 is imperfect: 40% false positive rate (passes AF2 but fails experimentally). Better learned filter (e.g., **AF2 pAE + Rosetta ddG + ProteinMPNN score** ensemble) needed.

> **Open Problem:** Can we train end-to-end $p(s, x \mid \text{target})$ without two-stage factorization? Diffusion-LM hybrids and Flow Matching (e.g., FoldFlow) aim to, but not yet competitive.

---

## 7 Conclusion

RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN demonstrate that **fine-tuning a structure prediction network as a generative model** is a dominant strategy for protein design. By learning $p(\text{backbone})$ via diffusion and $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{backbone})$ via MPNN, we achieve atomic-accuracy design of symmetric nanomaterials, enzyme scaffolds, and picomolar binders with minimal screening.

Key takeaways:

- **Generative symmetry** enables programmable nanocages and multivalent therapeutics with sub-Å precision.
- **Motif scaffolding** solves the long-standing theozyme placement problem, now at atomic resolution via RFdiffusion2.
- **Binder affinity maturation** via partial diffusion obviates display libraries, achieving $1000\times$ affinity gains in silico.
- **All-atom diffusion** extends to small molecules, DNA, metals — foundation for *de novo* enzymes.

Future work: joint diffusion over sequence and structure (RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN fusion), reinforcement learning from experimental feedback (RLHF for proteins), and scaling to 1000+ aa complexes (e.g., CRISPR-Cas scaffolds). The era of *text-to-protein* analogous to *text-to-image* has arrived; the remaining challenge is *function-to-protein*.
