# The Jamming Transition in Amorphous Solids: Force Chains, Marginal Stability, the Gardner Transition, and Replica Theory from Mean Field to Finite Dimensions

## Abstract

The jamming transition is the athermal route by which a disordered assembly of particles acquires rigidity: below a critical packing fraction $\phi_J$ the system flows, above it the system resists shear as a solid, yet no structural symmetry is broken. This thesis unifies the principal theoretical advances of the field — the Liu–Nagel jamming phase diagram [1], the isostatic scaling of O'Hern *et al.* [2], the heterogeneous force-chain networks of granular media, the marginal-stability paradigm, the Gardner transition and its fractal free-energy landscapes [4], and the exact replica theory of hard-sphere glasses in infinite dimensions [3] — into a single narrative. We derive the critical scaling of elastic moduli, coordination, and vibrational spectra at point $J$, show how marginal stability fixes the exponents of weak forces and near-contacts, demonstrate that the Gardner transition renders the glass marginally stable long before jamming, and trace the survival of the mean-field predictions in finite-dimensional simulations and experiments [5, 7, 8].

---

## 1 Introduction

Consider pouring sand into a pile. Each grain is an athermal, macroscopic object; thermal agitation is irrelevant to its motion. Yet pour enough grains into a confined space and the collective abruptly stops flowing — the pile supports its own weight and resists indentation, shear, and penetration. Something analogous happens when a liquid is cooled past its glass transition: the viscosity grows by fourteen orders of magnitude with almost no change in structure [1]. Jamming is the proposal that these phenomena — and the arrest of foams, emulsions, and dense colloidal suspensions — are facets of a single underlying transition in which a disordered assembly of particles becomes rigid without crystallizing [2].

The simplest model in which jamming can be isolated is disarmingly austere: frictionless spheres interacting through purely repulsive, finite-range forces at zero temperature, quenched by energy minimization. O'Hern, Silbert, Liu, and Nagel showed that this model exhibits a sharp transition at a critical packing fraction $\phi_J$ — *point J* — whose character is a hybrid: the bulk modulus and coordination number jump discontinuously, like a first-order transition, while the shear modulus vanishes continuously and diverging length scales appear, like a second-order transition [2]. This is the idealization from which everything else follows: isostaticity, the excess of low-frequency vibrational modes, and the universal scaling laws that experiments on real granular materials confirm to remarkable accuracy [1].

> **Definition 1.1 (Jamming).** An athermal packing of particles is *jammed* when it possesses a mechanically rigid contact network: every particle is constrained by its neighbors such that no collective infinitesimal displacement is possible without doing work, and the system sustains a finite shear stress at zero rate of deformation [2].

But jamming is not merely about the final rigid state. Between the liquid and the jammed solid sits the *glass*, and the modern understanding — driven by the exact solution of hard-sphere glasses in infinite spatial dimensions [3] — is that the glass itself undergoes a profound topological transformation before jamming is reached. At the *Gardner transition*, each glass basin fractures into an infinite hierarchy of sub-basins, the free-energy landscape becomes fractal, and the glass becomes *marginally stable*: sitting perpetually at the edge of mechanical instability [4]. Jamming is then not an isolated singularity but the endpoint of a marginally stable phase whose exponents can be computed exactly [3, 4].

This thesis develops that picture from the ground up. Section 2 reviews the background: the Liu–Nagel phase diagram, point $J$, and the heterogeneous force networks seen in experiments. Section 3 describes the methodology of the field — soft-sphere energy minimization and replica theory. Section 4 contains the deep dive: force chains, isostaticity and scaling, the vibrational anomaly, the Gardner transition and marginal stability, and the exact infinite-dimensional solution. Section 5 confronts theory with simulation and experiment. Section 6 lists the limitations: friction, temperature, finite-dimensional corrections, and the glass/jamming boundary. Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Liu–Nagel jamming phase diagram

In 1998, Liu and Nagel proposed a unifying phase diagram with axes of temperature $T$, inverse packing fraction $1/\phi$, and applied shear stress $\Sigma$ [1]. Within a surface enclosing the origin — low temperature, high density, low stress — the system is jammed; outside it, it flows. The provocative suggestion was that *point J*, the zero-temperature, zero-stress jamming point, controls the behavior in its vicinity, so that thermal glasses, sheared foams, and compressed granular piles all inherit critical-like properties from the same singularity. The full review a decade later refined this picture into a quantitative science [1].

| Axis | Unjammed side | Jammed side | Control parameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | fluid / glass | arrested solid | $T \to 0$ |
| Density | flowing suspension | rigid packing | $\phi - \phi_J$ |
| Shear stress | yielded flow | elastic solid | $\Sigma - \Sigma_y$ |

### 2.2 The soft-sphere model and point J

O'Hern *et al.* [2] studied $N$ frictionless spheres with pair potential

$$V(r_{ij}) = \frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}\left(1 - \frac{r_{ij}}{\sigma_{ij}}\right)^{\alpha}\Theta(\sigma_{ij} - r_{ij}),$$

with $\alpha = 2$ (harmonic) or $\alpha = 5/2$ (Hertzian), quenched to $T = 0$ by conjugate-gradient minimization from random initial conditions. At low $\phi$ the minimized energy is zero (particles avoid overlap); at $\phi_J \approx 0.64$ for monodisperse spheres in three dimensions, the energy becomes positive and the system acquires rigidity [2]. The transition is protocol-dependent in detail — $\phi_J$ shifts with polydispersity, preparation rate, and dimension — but its scaling properties are robust.

### 2.3 Force chains: the granular signature

Photoelastic-disk experiments reveal that stress in a jammed packing is not transmitted homogeneously but along filamentary *force chains*: linear sequences of heavily loaded contacts that carry most of the load, embedded in a weakly stressed background of "spectator" particles [1]. The force distribution is broad, roughly exponential at large forces, $P(f) \sim \exp(-f/\langle f\rangle)$, with a peak or plateau at small $f$ [7]. These chains buckle under load, and their collective buckling is the microscopic mechanism of plastic failure — a theme to which marginal stability gives a precise mathematical form.

> **Observation 2.1.** Force chains are *not* an artifact of friction: frictionless simulations exhibit equally heterogeneous contact-force networks, with the same qualitative $P(f)$ [2].

## 3 Methodology

The field advances through two complementary methodologies: *numerical energy minimization* of soft-sphere packings, and *analytical replica theory* of hard spheres in high dimension.

### 3.1 Athermal quasi-static simulation

The standard protocol [2] generates packings by minimizing the total potential energy $U = \sum_{\langle ij\rangle} V(r_{ij})$ with respect to all particle positions, typically using conjugate-gradient or the FIRE algorithm, at fixed packing fraction:

```python
import numpy as np

def hertzian_forces(pos, sigma, eps=1.0):
    """Pairwise repulsive Hertzian forces for a soft-sphere packing."""
    N = len(pos)
    F = np.zeros_like(pos)
    for i in range(N):
        d = pos - pos[i]
        r = np.linalg.norm(d, axis=1)
        overlap = sigma - r
        contact = (overlap > 0) & (np.arange(N) != i)
        # f ~ delta^{3/2} for Hertzian contacts (alpha = 5/2)
        fmag = eps * overlap[contact] ** 1.5 / r[contact]
        F[i] += (fmag[:, None] * d[contact]).sum(axis=0)
    return F

def quench_to_jamming(pos, sigma, tol=1e-12):
    """FIRE-style energy minimization at fixed packing fraction."""
    vel = np.zeros_like(pos)
    F = hertzian_forces(pos, sigma)
    while np.linalg.norm(F) > tol:
        vel = 0.9 * vel + 0.1 * F          # damped dynamics
        pos += 0.01 * vel                  # small step
        F = hertzian_forces(pos, sigma)
    return pos
```

Rattlers — particles with fewer than $d+1$ contacts — are identified and removed before computing coordination numbers, since they do not participate in the rigid network [2]. Elastic moduli are obtained from the Hessian matrix $H_{ij} = \partial^2 U / \partial \mathbf{r}_i \partial \mathbf{r}_j$ via linear response, and the vibrational density of states $D(\omega)$ from its eigenvalue spectrum.

### 3.2 Replica theory in infinite dimensions

The analytical counterpart is the *replica method* applied to hard spheres as $d \to \infty$ [3]. In this limit the virial expansion truncates, the cage each particle explores becomes exactly Gaussian, and the replicated free energy can be evaluated by saddle point. The full replica-symmetry-breaking (fullRSB) solution yields a phase diagram with three characteristic densities:

1. $\phi_d$ — the *dynamical* (mode-coupling) transition, where metastable glassy states first appear;
2. $\phi_G$ — the *Gardner* transition, where each glass basin breaks into a fractal hierarchy of sub-basins;
3. $\phi_J$ — the *jamming* transition, where the pressure diverges and the cage size vanishes.

The $d \to \infty$ limit is exact, not approximate, and its predictions — exponents, scaling functions, the very existence of the Gardner phase — can be tested against finite-dimensional simulations [3, 4, 5]. The same fullRSB solution yields the shear modulus of amorphous hard spheres across the whole phase diagram, unifying earlier theoretical approaches to glass elasticity [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Isostaticity and the scaling laws of point J

Mechanical stability of a frictionless packing is a counting problem. With $Nd$ degrees of freedom and $N_c$ contacts (each imposing one constraint), rigidity requires $N_c \ge Nd$, or average coordination $z \ge 2d \equiv z_c$ — the *Maxwell isostatic criterion* [2]. The startling discovery of O'Hern *et al.* is that jammed packings sit *exactly* at the margin: $z \to z_c^+$ as $\phi \to \phi_J^+$ [2].

> **Theorem 4.1 (Isostaticity at jamming).** In the thermodynamic limit, frictionless sphere packings at the jamming transition are isostatic: the mean coordination number jumps from $z = 0$ to $z = z_c = 2d$, the minimum compatible with rigidity, and approaches it from above as $z - z_c \sim (\phi - \phi_J)^{1/2}$ [2].

The exponent $1/2$ is not mean-field triviality; it follows from the singular pair correlation function. Near contact, $g(r)$ develops a delta-like peak whose area gives the contacts and whose width scales as $\Delta\phi$, yielding $\Delta z \sim \Delta\phi^{1/2}$ [2, 4]. The elastic moduli inherit related exponents. For a potential with exponent $\alpha$:

| Quantity | Scaling with $\Delta\phi = \phi - \phi_J$ | Harmonic ($\alpha=2$) | Hertzian ($\alpha=5/2$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure $p$ | $\Delta\phi^{\alpha-1}$ | $\Delta\phi$ | $\Delta\phi^{3/2}$ |
| Bulk modulus $B$ | $\Delta\phi^{\alpha-2}$ | $\Delta\phi^0$ (jump) | $\Delta\phi^{1/2}$ |
| Shear modulus $G$ | $\Delta\phi^{\alpha-3/2}$ | $\Delta\phi^{1/2}$ | $\Delta\phi$ |
| Excess coordination $\Delta z$ | $\Delta\phi^{1/2}$ | $\Delta\phi^{1/2}$ | $\Delta\phi^{1/2}$ |

The ratio $G/B \sim \Delta z \to 0$ at jamming: the marginally jammed solid is anomalously soft to shear while remaining stiff to compression — the fingerprint of isostaticity [1, 2].

### 4.2 The vibrational anomaly: excess soft modes

A crystal has Debye spectrum $D(\omega) \sim \omega^{d-1}$ down to $\omega = 0$. The marginally jammed solid instead exhibits a *plateau* in $D(\omega)$ extending to zero frequency as $\phi \to \phi_J^+$ [1]. Cutting a single contact from an isostatic network creates a zero-frequency floppy mode; because the network is only just rigid, there is an abundance of such nearly-floppy, quasi-localized modes. The plateau terminates at a crossover frequency $\omega^* \sim \Delta z \sim \Delta\phi^{1/2}$, below which ordinary phonons reappear [1, 2].

These soft modes are the normal modes of failure: under shear, plastic rearrangements nucleate precisely where the lowest-frequency quasi-localized modes concentrate, and the force chains buckle along the directions these modes dictate [1]. The boson peak of real glasses — the excess of modes over Debye seen in neutron and Raman scattering — is widely interpreted as the finite-temperature echo of this jamming anomaly [5].

### 4.3 The Gardner transition: when the basin breaks

The deepest conceptual advance of the last decade is the realization that the glass phase *itself* is critical. Consider a glass basin — the set of configurations mutually accessible without crossing a large barrier. Cooling (compressing) a hard-sphere glass, the basin does not simply shrink; at a well-defined density $\phi_G$ it undergoes the *Gardner transition* [4]:

- Each basin splits into an infinite hierarchy of sub-basins within sub-basins — a *fractal* free-energy landscape [4];
- The states become *marginally stable*: the spectrum of the stability matrix develops arbitrarily soft modes, and the system sits at the edge of instability everywhere in the Gardner phase [3, 4];
- Two-point correlations develop the scaling of a critical point, with a diverging correlation length, even though no symmetry is broken and no order parameter jumps [4].

> **Theorem 4.2 (Marginal stability in the Gardner phase).** Within the fullRSB solution, glassy states for $\phi_G < \phi < \phi_J$ are marginally stable: the replicon eigenvalue vanishes throughout the phase, so that an extensive number of soft modes exists and the linear response is controlled by the hierarchical basin structure rather than by any single minimum [3].

The Gardner transition was first identified in spin glasses (Gardner, 1985); its importation into structural glasses via the $d \to \infty$ solution [3] and its numerical confirmation in three-dimensional hard spheres [4] unified two previously separate literatures. Experimentally, signatures consistent with Gardner physics — anomalous aging, diverging susceptibilities, and non-Debye vibrational scaling — have been reported in colloidal and granular glasses [4].

### 4.4 Exact exponents from replica theory

At jamming, the fullRSB solution predicts critical exponents that are *irrational numbers* fixed by the theory, not fitted [4]:

- **Weak-force exponent:** $P(f) \sim f^{\theta}$ as $f \to 0$, with $\theta = 0.42311\ldots$;
- **Near-contact (gap) exponent:** $g(h) \sim h^{-\gamma}$ for small gaps $h$, with $\gamma = 0.41269\ldots$;
- **Cage-size exponent:** the mean-square displacement within a basin vanishes as $\Delta \sim (\phi_J - \phi)^{\kappa}$ with $\kappa \approx 2.17$.

These exponents satisfy *scaling relations* dictated by marginal stability: stability against contact buckling bounds $\theta$ from below, stability against opening new contacts bounds $\gamma$ from below, and at jamming both bounds are saturated — the packing is marginally stable in both senses simultaneously [4]. Simulations in $d = 2$ through $d = 8$ confirm the exponents to within a few percent, a striking dimensional robustness of the mean-field predictions [3, 5].

The vibrational spectrum near the Gardner transition likewise obeys a universal, non-Debye law: $D(\omega) \sim \omega^2$ at low frequency in three dimensions, independent of microscopic detail — verified numerically across glass-forming models [5]. This universality is the sense in which marginal stability is a *principle*, not a model artifact.

### 4.5 Shear jamming and the stress axis

Jamming is not confined to the $\Sigma = 0$ plane. It is now established that *shear* alone can jam a packing at densities below $\phi_J$: an initially unjammed assembly, sheared at fixed volume, develops a rigid, anisotropic contact network — force chains aligned with the compressive direction — and acquires a yield stress [6]. This *shear jamming* reshapes the Liu–Nagel diagram: the jammed region extends to lower $\phi$ under applied stress, and the boundary between fragile, shear-jammed, and isotropically jammed states is now mapped in detail [6].

The same physics drives *discontinuous shear thickening* (DST) in dense suspensions: under increasing shear rate, lubricated (effectively frictionless) contacts give way to frictional contacts, the suspension shear-jams transiently, and the viscosity jumps by orders of magnitude [6]. The Mari–Seto–Morris–Denn model — stress-dependent crossover from frictionless to frictional rheology — quantitatively captures DST and connects it to the jamming phase diagram [6]. Force chains are the common microscopic language: DST is, in essence, the dynamical formation and collapse of transient force-chain networks.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Numerical confirmation of the scaling laws

Large-scale simulations ($N$ up to $10^5$) confirm the point-$J$ scaling to high precision [2, 6]:

1. **Coordination:** $z - 6 \propto (\phi - \phi_J)^{0.50 \pm 0.03}$ in 3D, after rattler removal [2].
2. **Moduli:** $G \propto (\phi - \phi_J)^{0.48 \pm 0.05}$ for harmonic repulsions, $G \propto (\phi - \phi_J)^{1.0 \pm 0.05}$ for Hertzian — matching $\alpha - 3/2$ [2].
3. **Density of states:** the plateau in $D(\omega)$ with $\omega^* \propto \Delta z$ is observed across potentials and polydispersities [1, 5].
4. **Gardner exponents:** measured $\theta = 0.42 \pm 0.02$ and $\gamma = 0.41 \pm 0.02$ in $d = 3$ agree with the irrational mean-field values [4].

### 5.2 Experimental evidence

- **Emulsions and foams:** confocal microscopy of compressed emulsions confirms $z - z_c \sim (\phi - \phi_J)^{1/2}$ and the isostatic value $z_c = 6$ [1].
- **Photoelastic disks:** direct imaging of force chains, measurement of $P(f)$, and observation of chain buckling under load [1].
- **Colloidal glasses:** diverging dynamical susceptibilities and aging behavior consistent with Gardner-like marginality near jamming [4].
- **Granular shear:** the full phase diagram of reversible–irreversible, jamming, and yielding transitions under cyclic shear has been mapped in a single experimental system, unifying three previously separate transition lines [6].

### 5.3 The mathematical status

The $d \to \infty$ solution is exact within its limit [3], and the exponents it predicts are theorems of the fullRSB equations, not phenomenology [4]. What remains unproven is the *dimensional robustness*: why exponents computed at $d = \infty$ match $d = 3$ simulations to percent accuracy. Renormalization-group arguments suggest the upper critical dimension may be low, but a complete finite-dimensional theory — the "complete mathematical theory of the jamming transition" surveyed in [8] — remains an active construction site.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Friction.** Real grains are frictional, and friction changes the counting: the isostatic coordination becomes $z_c = d + 1$ (each contact constrains $d$ force components but torques must also balance), the jamming density drops ($\phi_J^{\text{frictional}} < \phi_J^{\text{frictionless}}$), and protocol dependence is far stronger [1]. Much of the exact theory is frictionless-only.
2. **Finite temperature.** Point $J$ is a $T = 0$ concept; at $T > 0$ the sharp transition is rounded into a crossover, and the interplay of jamming criticality with the thermal glass transition (the $\phi_d$–$\phi_G$–$\phi_J$ sequence) is only partly understood [3].
3. **Finite dimensions.** The replica theory is exact only as $d \to \infty$. Non-perturbative finite-$d$ effects — activated barrier hopping, facilitation, dynamical heterogeneity — are absent from the mean-field picture and dominate the deeply supercooled regime in $d = 3$ [3].
4. **Preparation dependence.** $\phi_J$ is not unique: the "J-line" of jamming densities spans a range depending on annealing protocol, and which point on the line a protocol selects remains incompletely characterized [3, 6].
5. **Shear-jamming theory.** While simulations map the shear-jamming boundary precisely [6], an analytical theory of the anisotropic, fragile states at the level of the isotropic fullRSB solution does not yet exist.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The jamming transition has grown from a simulation curiosity into one of the most complete critical phenomena in disordered matter. Its elementary facts — isostaticity, the $1/2$ exponent of the coordination, the vanishing of the shear modulus, the plateau in the vibrational spectrum — are established beyond reasonable doubt [1, 2]. Its deeper structure — force chains as the carriers of stress, marginal stability as the organizing principle, the Gardner transition as the fracturing of the glass basin, and the exact infinite-dimensional solution that predicts irrational critical exponents and the fullRSB shear modulus — represents a rare triumph in which a mean-field theory of a disordered system is both exactly solvable and quantitatively predictive in three dimensions [3, 4, 5, 7].

The frontier is now the territory the idealizations exclude: frictional packings with their richer isostaticity, finite temperatures where jamming meets the glass transition, and the shear-jammed states that connect granular physics to the rheology of dense suspensions [6, 7]. The mathematical program — a complete theory valid in finite dimensions [8] — remains open, but the scaffolding is in place. Jamming is no longer a point on a phase diagram; it is the endpoint of a marginally stable phase, and that phase may be the generic fate of disorder under compression.
