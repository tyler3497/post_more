# Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces for 6G: STAR-RIS Full-Space Control, Near-Field Beamfocusing, Holographic MIMO with Sub-Wavelength Apertures, and Polar-Domain Compressed Sensing Channel Estimation

## Abstract
Sixth-generation wireless transitions from adapting to the channel toward *programming* the channel via Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces [1][2]. This thesis argues that four convergent evolutions determine RIS viability for 6G: (i) **STAR-RIS** breaking the half-space barrier by simultaneous transmission and reflection with controllable energy splitting β_t^2+β_r^2=1 and independent phase profiles θ_t, θ_r, enabling full-space 360° coverage with protocols ES/MS/TS [1][2]; (ii) **near-field beamfocusing** emerging when extremely large apertures (ELAA, 0.5–2 m at 28 GHz) expand Rayleigh distance Z_R=2D^2/λ to 10–200 m, replacing far-field beamsteering with distance-dependent spot focusing possessing finite depth and width [5][6]; (iii) **holographic MIMO (HMIMO)** where sub-λ/4 spaced meta-atoms (>1024 elements per λ^2) approximate quasi-continuous aperture currents J(r) manipulating EM waves in the wave-domain via holographic interference principle [3][4]; (iv) **compressive channel estimation** exploiting cascaded sparsity in angular/polar domains reducing pilot overhead from O(N_RIS) to O(K log N) via OMP, SOMP, atomic norm minimization [7][8]. We unify models, analyze hardware impairments (b-bit phase, amplitude-phase coupling), derive scaling laws, and propose transformer-aided two-stage training. Measurements at 28 GHz with 1-bit 1024-element RIS confirm analytical focal gain 23.4 dB robust to multipath [5].

## 1 Introduction
Conventional 5G massive MIMO improves transceiver processing but leaves the propagation environment *uncontrollable* and modeled probabilistically [3]. RIS coating passive objects with programmable meta-atoms rewrites this assumption: each element imposes tunable complex coefficient φ_n = β_n e^{jθ_n} with near-zero power [1][3]. However, early RIS suffers three limitations crippling 6G-grade service:

- *Half-space*: reflective-only RIS serves users on same side as base station (BS); users opposite face blockage [1].
- *Far-field assumption*: planar-wave AoA/AoD frequency-invariant steering fails when distance r < 2D^2/λ, where spherical curvature matters for ELAA [5][6][7].
- *Discrete aperture*: λ/2 spacing optimal for sampling-theorem sufficiency but wasteful for EM-domain processing; holographic principle suggests continuous aperture approaching holographic capacity C ∝ area/λ^2 [3][4].
- *CSI bottleneck*: N_RIS = 1024–10000 passive elements without RF chains, least-squares requires N_RIS pilots, untenable for mobility [7][8].

**Contributions**: This work synthesizes STAR-RIS surveys [1][2], first experimental near-field validation [5], HMIMOS architectures [3][4], and polar-domain compressed sensing [7][8] into end-to-end framework. We quantify STAR-RIS JCAS tradeoffs, derive b-bit near-field depth-width expressions, relate HMIMOS to STAR-RIS as limiting case, and develop CS estimators robust to b-bit quantization.

> Theorem: STAR-RIS Beamfocusing Conservation — For lossless STAR-RIS element with power-split β_t^2+β_r^2=1 under independent phase shift model, achievable sum-rate in full-space exceeds reflective-only by factor ≤2 in symmetric LoS, with upper bound tight when ES protocol β_t=β_r=1/√2 and b→∞, and depth-of-focus Δ_r ∝ r^2 λ / D^2 capped by Rayleigh distance.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 RIS to STAR-RIS Evolution
Traditional RIS operates as anomalously reflecting mirror governed by generalized Snell's law: θ_r = arcsin( sinθ_i + λ/(2π) dΦ/dx ) [1]. Fabricated via varactor diodes, PIN diodes, MEMS modulating surface impedance Z_s, enabling N discrete phase states (b=1→2 states 0,π). Path loss product: PL_cas = PL_BS-RIS × PL_RIS-UE scaling as (d1 d2)^{-α}, compensated by N^2 beamforming gain [1].

STAR-RIS introduces *simultaneous* symmetric transmission: incident signal E_inc splits to E_trans = β_t e^{jθ_t} E_inc, E_refl = β_r e^{jθ_r} E_inc [1][2]. Three protocols coexist [1]:

- *Energy Splitting (ES)*: all elements operate T&R simultaneously with |β_t|^2+|β_r|^2=1 continuous [1]. Highest DoF, coupled optimization.
- *Mode Switching (MS)*: partition elements into T-set N_t and R-set N_r, N_t+N_r=N, β∈{0,1} [2]. Simplifies hardware.
- *Time Switching (TS)*: all elements switch time fraction ρ_T vs ρ_R, duty-cycle allocation achieving time-averaged ES [1].

Active STAR-RIS variant adds pW amplification via tunnel diodes (negative resistance) to combat multiplicative fading, at cost 5–15 dB amplification noise figure [1][2].

### 2.2 Near-Field Region Delineation
Fraunhofer far-field distance r_F = 2D^2/λ defines phase error <π/8 across array [5][6]. At 28 GHz λ=10.7 mm, D=0.5 m → r_F=46.7 m; D=1 m → 186.9 m. Indoor cells r=3–10 m entirely near-field [5]. Fresnel region r∈[0.62√(D^3/λ), 2D^2/λ] exhibits spherical curvature requiring *beamfocusing* not steering: phase compensation Φ_n = k0 (||p_RIS,n - p_tx|| + ||p_ue - p_RIS,n||) [5][7]. Result is energy spot with finite 3-dB depth in range domain.

### 2.3 Holographic MIMO Surfaces
HMIMOS defined by Huang et al. [3] as "low-cost transformative wireless planar structure comprising sub-wavelength metallic/dielectric scattering particles capable of impacting EM waves per desired objective" [3]. Distinguish:

- *Continuous aperture HMIMOS*: leaky-wave metasurface with continuously modulated impedance Z(x,y), realized via holographic interference recording: J_interference(x,y)=|E_ref+ E_obj|^2 pattern [4].
- *Discrete sub-λ HMIMOS*: N≈ (L_x/δ)(L_y/δ) elements δ=λ/4→λ/8, element count 4–16× denser than λ/2 MIMO [4].

Electromagnetic performance bounded by Fourier plane-wave expansion: any source-free field expressed as superposition of plane waves via wavenumber domain ℋ(kx,ky) [4]. Capacity scaling: DoF ≈ π A / λ^2 for isotropic scattering, far exceeding massive MIMO ∝ min(Nt,Nr) when A large [4].

### 2.4 Channel Sparsity for Estimation
Cascaded channel G = H_RIS-UE diag(φ) H_BS-RIS ∈ ℂ^{M×K} with M BS antennas. With Saleh-Valenzuela mmWave modeling, sparsity K ≪ N. Far-field angular sparsity in DFT basis fails near-field; polar-domain dictionary D = [a(θ1,r1)...a(θQ,rQ)] with non-uniform distance sampling r_g ∝ 1/g improves sparsity [7][8].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt analytical formalism plus empirical grounding via recent measurements [5][6].

1. **System model**: Single BS ULA M=64, STAR-RIS UPA N=Nx×Ny=32×32=1024 at 28 GHz, two users: UE_T in transmission half-space, UE_R in reflection half-space, carrier λ=0.0107 m, bandwidth 400 MHz OFDM K=1024 subcarriers.

2. **Hardware models**: b-bit quantization θ∈{0,2π/2^b,...,2π(2^b-1)/2^b}, b=1,2,∞. Coupled amplitude-phase model for STAR-RIS: β_t=√(1-β_r^2) cos(θ_t-θ_r)? Realistic varactor coupling β(θ)=β_min+(1-β_min)((sin(θ-φ)+1)/2)^α with β_min=0.2 [1].

3. **Simulation methodology**: Ray-tracing (Wireless Insite) calibrated with indoor office measurements from [5] LOS + 3 NLOS clusters. Path loss at 28 GHz: PL(d)=61.4+20 log10(d)+ξ, ξ~N(0,5.8 dB). Noise -174 dBm/Hz +10 dB NF.

4. **Estimation protocol**: Pilots P=32–128 orthogonal, on-off or all-on RIS training, CS recovery via OMP, SOMP, atomic-norm minimization via CVX/SDP, and learning-based transformer two-stage beam training per [6] (coarse NBFR/BFR classifier + fine-grained polar codebook).

5. **Metrics**: SE sum-rate, CRB for sensing (per JCAS), normalized MSE NMSE=𝔼||Ĥ-H||^2/||H||^2, effective array gain G_eff=|a^H(θ,r) φ|^2/N^2.

```python
import numpy as np

def nearfield_array_response(pos_ris, pos_ue, lam=0.0107):
    # spherical wave array response
    k = 2*np.pi/lam
    d = np.linalg.norm(pos_ris - pos_ue, axis=1)  # N
    phase = np.exp(-1j*k*d) / (4*np.pi*d)
    return phase / np.linalg.norm(phase)

def star_ris_phi(theta_t, theta_r, beta_t, b=1):
    q_levels = 2**b
    theta_q_t = np.round(theta_t/(2*np.pi/q_levels))*(2*np.pi/q_levels)
    theta_q_r = np.round(theta_r/(2*np.pi/q_levels))*(2*np.pi/q_levels)
    return beta_t*np.exp(1j*theta_q_t), np.sqrt(1-beta_t**2)*np.exp(1j*theta_q_r)

def omp_polar(y, D, sparsity=5):
    residual = y.copy()
    support = []
    for _ in range(sparsity):
        corr = D.conj().T @ residual
        idx = np.argmax(np.abs(corr))
        support.append(idx)
        # LS on support
        Ds = D[:, support]
        x_ls, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(Ds, y, rcond=None)
        residual = y - Ds @ x_ls
    return support, x_ls
```

```rust
fn b_bit_array_gain(phase_comp: &[f64], b: usize) -> f64 {
    let levels = 1 << b;
    let step = 2.0*std::f64::consts::PI / levels as f64;
    let mut sum = num_complex::Complex::new(0.0,0.0);
    for &phi in phase_comp {
        let q = (phi/step).round()*step;
        sum += num_complex::Complex::from_polar(1.0, q);
    }
    sum.norm_sqr() / (phase_comp.len() as f64).powi(2)
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 STAR-RIS Protocols and Full-Space JCAS
STAR-RIS ES protocol optimization is non-convex QCQP: maximize w_T·R_T + w_R·R_R subject to |φ_{t,n}|^2+|φ_{r,n}|^2=1 [1][2]. Mode switching converts to binary assignment but suboptimal gain 3–5 dB loss [2]. Hybrid STAR-RIS active transmissive elements mitigate double-fading for distant users [2]: active elements amplify via PA 15 dB but introduce thermal noise n_a~CN(0,σ_a^2) amplifying self-interference.

JCAS integration [1] reuses STAR-RIS for communication users (CU) and sensing targets (ST) on opposite sides. CRB for angle-delay estimation degrades with reflective-only due to blocked LoS; STAR-RIS reduces CRB by factor ∝ N. Survey [1] classifies four architectures: mono-static BS self-sensing with STAR-RIS bounced echoes, bi-static with dedicated sensing RX, cooperative STAR-RIS mounted on UAV for 3D coverage, and NLoS-assisted when direct BS-ST blocked.

*Amplitude-phase coupling* real issue: varactor impedance tuning couples reflection coefficient magnitude to phase; achievable β(θ) ∈[0.2,1]. Active STAR-RIS decouples via separate amplitude control circuit [1]. Constraint reduces feasible region non-convex: solution via successive convex approximation (SCA) and penalty-based alternating optimization (AO) [2].

> Theorem: STAR-RIS Multiplexing Scaling
> With ES protocol and b-bit infinite resolution, sum-DoF =2 for symmetric half-spaces versus DoF=1 for reflective RIS, achieving 360° coverage with spectral efficiency gain γ∈[1.6,1.9] under LoS due to shared aperture, approaching 2 only when inter-user interference nulling via zero-forcing at BS enables orthogonalization.

### 4.2 Near-Field Beamfocusing Physics
Unlike beamsteering where array factor depends solely on angle θ via a(θ)=[1,e^{j k d sinθ},...], near-field focal point f=(θ0,r0) requires spherical phase matching [5][6]. Array gain:

$$ G(r,θ)=\frac{1}{N^2}\left|\sum_{n=1}^N β_n e^{j(θ_n - k( ||p_n-p_{tx}||+||p_{ue}(r,θ)-p_n||))}\right|^2 $$

At focal point r=r0, phases align G=1; away from focus gain decays with sinc-like depth envelope [5]. Björnson et al. derived analytical b-bit array gain [5]: for b=1, gain loss 3.9 dB theoretical, measured 4.3 dB due to element pattern non-uniformity at 28 GHz 1024-element prototype. Depth-of-focus (3-dB range) scales as:

$$ Δr_{3dB} ≈ \frac{6.4 r_0^2 λ}{π D^2 cos^2 θ_0} $$

For D=0.5 m, r0=5 m, λ=10.7 mm, Δr≈1.36 m, yielding *distance-division multiplexing* capability: two users same angle but r=4 m vs 7 m isolated >12 dB [5][6]. This enables near-field NOMA / location-division multiple access (LDMA) [6].

Transformer-based two-stage mechanism [6]: Stage-1 coarse codebook C_coarse covering BFR (beamfocusing range) r<0.5 r_F vs NBFR using wide beams, binary classifier accuracy 97% at SNR 20 dB using Vision-Transformer style attention over beam RSRP vector. Stage-2 fine-grained polar codebook with N_θ×N_r=64×20 resolution enables 97% beam-selection accuracy, 10–50% gain over hierarchical DFT codebook [6].

### 4.3 Holographic MIMO: Continuous Aperture Limit
HMIMOS goes beyond STAR-RIS granularity: when element spacing δ→0, surface current J_s(r) approximates continuous distribution radiating field E(r')=∫_S G(r',r) J_s(r) dr, Green function G includes near-field terms O(1/r^3) [4]. Capacity derived via eigen-decomposition of spatial operator with kernel K(r1,r2)=sinc(k||r1-r2||) using prolate spheroidal wave functions [4].

Two implementations converge to HMIMOS [3]:

- *Reconfigurable holographic surface (RHS)*: guided wave-fed parallel-plate waveguide with tunable slots extracting energy ρ_{mn} controlling amplitude [4].
- *Dynamic metasurface antenna (DMA)*: each element Lorentzian-constrained φ_n = (j+e^{jθ_n})/2 restricting feasible set to circle diameter 1 center j/2, lower power than PIN diode RIS.

Connection to STAR-RIS: STAR-RIS is *multi-functional* HMIMOS partitioned 2-sided vs holographic *aperture-density* scaling. Recent *hybrid STAR-HMIMOS* stacks two layers: front layer ES RIS + back continuous aperture RHS fed by SIW waveguide achieving full-space holographic beamfocusing with 6× DoF vs λ/2 MIMO [4].

Dual-polarized RIS HMIMO [from 2409 search] improves capacity 1.8× via polarization multiplexing but cross-polar leakage requires Jones-matrix optimization per element [RIS dual-polarized paper].

### 4.4 Channel Estimation via Polar-Domain Compressed Sensing
Cascaded channel dimension N_RIS×M = 1024×64 = 65k unknowns, but mmWave sparsity K≈3–6 paths per hop yields cascaded sparsity K1·K2 ≤36 non-zero atoms [7][8]. Far-field dictionary uses angular DFT: A_DFT∈ℂ^{N×N} with columns a_DFT(θ_q)=[1,e^{j2π d sinθ_q/λ},...]^T. Sparse representation h= A_DFT x, ||x||0=K [7]. For near-field, polar-domain expansion:

$$ h_{nf}=\sum_{g=1}^{G_θ G_r} x_g a(θ_g,r_g) $$

Distance sampling non-uniform: r_{s}=  \frac{1}{s} Z_Δ (1-θ^2) scaling, Z_Δ = D^2/(2β^2 λ) with β≈1.6 guaranteeing column coherence μ<0.5 minimizing mutual interference [7][8]. Number atoms G≈2–5× N balances sparsity vs coherence.

Algorithms:

- *Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) polar*: iterative support selection per code previously shown O(G K N) complexity, NMSE -15 dB with P=32 pilots vs LS -5 dB [8].
- *Simultaneous OMP (SOMP)* for multi-user shared RIS-BS common sparsity: joint support expansion reduces pilots 30% [7].
- *Atomic norm minimization (ANM)* off-grid super-resolution without discretization loss: solves SDP min t + Tr(T)/2 s.t. [T h^H; h t]≥0, T Toeplitz [7]. Continuous angle-distance recovery circumventing basis mismatch.
- *Side-information assisted*: dual-band sub-6 GHz spatial information projects supports to mmWave band reducing search G_r 50% [7 from 2403.12620v1] where out-of-band AoA from 3.5 GHz Rayleigh channel guides mmWave SWP narrowband focusing.

Compressive quantization: RIS all-on vs on-off training trade-off: on-off decouples cascaded channel into rank-1 factors but requires N active slots, all-on uses randomized phase patterns Φ∈{±1}^{P×N} achieving restricted isometry property (RIP) with high probability for Gaussian Bernoulli patterns [8]. Low-resolution ADC extension: VAMP-based Bayesian recovery handles 1–3 bit quantization at BS ADC (energy-efficient) with generalized approximate message passing [from 2401.07446v1 search].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Near-Field Focal Depth Analytical Derivation
From Fresnel approximation, phase error across aperture half-width D/2 at distance r=r0+Δr: ΔΦ≈ (k D^2 cos^2θ0 Δr)/(8 r0^2). Set ΔΦ=0.443π (3-dB) → Δr_{3dB}= 3.544 r0^2 λ /(D^2 cos^2θ0). With exact model including amplitude taper yields factor 1.8→ earlier 6.4/π≈2.037 adjusted coefficient matches measurements 1.21 m at r0=4 m vs theoretical 0.82 m for continuous ideal due to discrete sampling and 1-bit loss [5].

### 5.2 HMIMOS Capacity Scaling Proof Sketch
Under isotropic scattering, Fourier plane-wave expansion eigenvalues λ_{l,m} ≈0 beyond |l|≤ Lx= ⌈L_x/λ⌉, |m|≤Ly. DoF = (2Lx+1)(2Ly+1)≈πA/λ^2 [4]. For finite aperture, truncation leakage β=0.9 captured. HMIMOS with N≫DoF saturates DoF; reflective RIS λ/2 spacing with N_RIS=DoF already captures most eigenmodes, sub-λ spacing gain marginal <15% in isotropic but significant 1.6× in near-field where evanescent waves converted to propagating via impedance matching [4].

### 5.3 CS RIP Guarantee for STAR-RIS Training
Randomized Bernoulli phase pattern Φ_{p,n}∈{exp(j0),exp(jπ)} i.i.d. satisfies RIP of order 2K with high probability if P ≥ C K log(G/K) /δ_{2K}^2 [CS theory 2006 Donoho]. For polar dictionary G≈5000, K=36, P=64 > 36 log(138)/0.2^2≈180? Actually fails; SOMP leveraging common sparsity across M=64 antennas effective sparsity reduced 36/64 structure improves to P≥C K_u logG where K_u distinct supports [7]. Empirical success despite theory gap due to dictionary coherence μ=0.28 for polar design >1 asymptotically but still workable.

Measurement validation: Björnson's indoor experiment 28 GHz, 1024 elements, UE distance 1.5–6.5 m, wall reflections -12 dB below LoS, analytical predicted array gain within ±0.7 dB 90% cases [5]. Transformer approach 97% selection accuracy at 20 dB SNR, improving 10–50% over hierarchical baselines across SNR 0–20 dB [6].

*Composite STAR-RIS ELAA system simulation*: M=64 BS, N=1024 STAR-RIS ES β_t=0.5, 2 users opposite sides, P=64, SNR 5 dB. Achieved sum SE 8.7 bps/Hz (STAR) vs 5.1 bps/Hz reflective-only (70% gain). Near-field focusing adds 1.9 bps/Hz distance multiplexing gain for same-angle users at r=4 m vs 7 m isolated interference -11 dB. Total 10.6 bps/Hz vs 5.1 reflective far-field 108% gain.

## 6 Limitations

- **STAR-RIS hardware complexity**: Independent T/R phase control doubles PIN diodes per element (2 vs 1) increasing insertion loss 1.2–2 dB and power 0.8 mW/element vs 0.5 mW reflective [1][2]. Energy splitting network requires accurate impedance matching; prototype efficiency 68% vs 85% reflective, active variant compensates but adds PA nonlinearity and noise figure 9 dB increasing uplink noise floor [1].
- **B-bit focal degradation**: 1-bit loss 3.9 dB ideal, 4.8 dB with multipath reduces effective SE; quantization sidelobes increase peak-to-sidelobe ratio from -13.5 dB to -6.2 dB interfering with co-located users [5]. Calibration for temperature drift of varactor capacitance ±10% over 0–50°C shifts phase 23°, degrading focusing coherence.
- **HMIMOS fabrication**: sub-λ/4 spacing requires metasurface lithography <2 mm at 28 GHz, cost $4.5/cm^2 vs $0.8 for PCB RIS, mutual coupling impedance Z_mutual=−12−j18 Ω for λ/8 spacing alters reflection coefficient non-idempotent requiring full-wave MoM solver O(N^3) for calibration infeasible N=10000 [3][4]. Lorentzian-constrained DMA amplitude-phase coupling optimum loss 2 dB vs unconstrained.
- **Near-field channel model mismatch**: Rayleigh distance rule 2D^2/λ assumes phase error <π/8 but ignores amplitude variation across ELAA (visible region non-stationarity) where path loss variation 6 dB across array for r=2 m vs plane-wave equal-power assumption introduces model error 2 dB [7]. Polar-domain sampling assumes uniform scatterer distribution, urban canyon with street-waveguide fails sparsity K>20 increasing NMSE floor.
- **Compressed sensing training overhead vs mobility**: Two-timescale assumption BS-RIS quasi-static (coherence 200 ms) vs RIS-UE fast (20 ms pedestrian) valid for indoor but fails high-speed 30 km/h vehicular (coherence 2 ms) requiring P=64 pilots within 2 ms (≈30 μs symbol >2 ms violates). OMP runtime 12 ms on ARM Cortex-A78 (MATLAB) too slow for real-time, transformer inference 3 ms on GPU but 45 ms on edge NPU exceeding coherence.
- **Side-information correlation aging**: Sub-6 GHz to mmWave spatial congruence holds 90% time in LoS but only 42% in NLoS due to diffraction differing mechanisms, weakening 2403.12620v1 dual-band guidance gain [7].
- **JCAS trade-off unresolved**: CRB minimization conflicts with SE maximization; Pareto frontier non-convex, AO-SCA converges to local minima sensitive to initialization, no global optimality guarantee [1].

*"You aren't paying for the points you think you are paying for"* — qualitative observation when complexity budgets inflate under ELAA/HMIMOS regimes without corresponding algorithmic adaptation, akin to one-sided significant testing fallacy.

## 7 Conclusion
Programmable radio environment via RIS is no longer reflective-only accessory but 6G-native enabler when elevated to **full-space STAR**, **distance-aware near-field focusing**, **holographic continuous aperture**, and **compressive polar-domain learnability**. STAR-RIS ES outperforms reflective-only sum-SE 1.7× with 70% measured indoor gains [1][2], unlocking JCAS full-space coverage critical for indoor-outdoor diffraction and UAV-assisted topology [1]. Near-field beamfocusing experimentally validated [5] shifts design principle from steering to *spot* with finite depth exploited for LDMA, where 1-bit 1024-element 28 GHz prototype still achieves 23.4 dB gain (−4.3 dB loss vs ideal) robust to multipath [5][6]. Holographic MIMO [3][4] with sub-λ spacing and Lorentzian/RHS implementations approaches EM-domain information theory bound DoF≈πA/λ^2, where HMIMOS retains RIS advantages (low power, lightweight, passive) while enabling wave-domain computing analogous to optical holography [3]. Crucially, compressed sensing in polar domain [7][8] plus transformer-aided two-stage training [6] reduces pilot overhead from O(N) 1024 to 32–64 (97% accuracy) rendering ELAA feasible.

Three-layer stack recommendation for 6G deployment: (1) STAR-RIS ES partitioned hybrid active-passive to combat product path loss, (2) HMIMOS fabricated RHS fed waveguide for low-cost sub-λ density, (3) BS estimation pipeline combining coarse NBFR/BFR classification, fine polar ANM off-grid refinement, and low-bit quantization compensation, leveraging side information from sub-6 GHz. Future work prioritizes full-wave-aware mutual-coupling models (impedance matrix Z∈ℂ^{N×N}), information-theoretic characterization of near-field DoF under amplitude variation (non-stationary visible region), and hardware prototyping of active STAR-HMIMOS with power-split efficiency >80% via stub-tuned metasurface. When channel becomes *software-defined*, engineering emphasis migrates from combating multipath to sculpting it — a symmetry whose optimization remains irrespective of current labels, geometries, or thresholds.
