# Wafer-Scale Cerebras WSE-3 Versus Groq LPU and Google TPUv5p: Dataflow Compilation, Interconnect Topology, and Static Scheduling for Large Language Model Inference at Tensor-Production Scale

## Abstract
We dissect three antipodal dataflow philosophies for LLM inference at tensor-production scale: Cerebras WSE-3 wafer-scale mesh with 900k cores and 44GB on-chip SRAM, Groq LPU deterministic single-core streaming multiprocessor with software-scheduled SRAM slicing, and Google TPUv5p 3D torus pod with XLA-compiled systolic TensorCores. Formalizing compilation as static placement-routing of dataflow graphs onto spatial fabrics, we compare interconnect bisection bandwidth (21 PB/s vs 80 TB/s on-die vs 4800 Gbps ICI), weight-streaming vs KV-cache-stationary scheduling, and tiling strategies for multi-head attention and MoE routing. Empirical synthesis from HotChips, ISCA, and cloud benchmarks shows 2.8x v5p over v4 training uplift, Groq sub-millisecond TTFT at batch-1, and Cerebras 125 PFLOPs sparse FP16 on-wafer, revealing fundamental tradeoffs between static determinism, pod-scale programmability, and wafer-scale defect-tolerant compilation.

## 1 Intro

***Large language model inference at tensor-production scale*** — 10k-100k concurrent tokens/s, 32k-1M context, Mixture-of-Experts routing — has fractured accelerator design into *three incompatible schools* that share one premise: **dynamic scheduling fails**. The GPU era's reliance on hardware arbiters, caches, branch predictors, and warp schedulers introduces jitter that is tolerable for batch training but catastrophic for latency SLOs, KV-cache locality, and interconnect deadlock at pod scale.

We formalize the alternative: ***static dataflow compilation***. The model is a DAG G=(V,E) where nodes are tensor ops (einsum, softmax, all-reduce) and edges are tensors with known shapes at compile time even when dynamic sequence length varies. Compilation is *placement* π: V→P (processing elements) and *routing* ρ: E→paths in fabric, plus *temporal schedule* σ: V→ℕ cycle-accurate, such that all buffer lifetimes, SRAM bank conflicts, and ICI link utilizations are resolved *before* silicon executes.

Three instantiations dominate:

- **Cerebras WSE-3** [1][2]: wafer-scale integration (WSI). 4 trillion transistors, 900,000 AI-optimized cores, 46,225 mm² silicon occupying most of a 300mm wafer fabricated on TSMC 5nm, 44 GB on-chip SRAM, 21 PB/s SRAM bandwidth, 214 Pb/s fabric bandwidth, 125 petaflops sparse FP16 [2][9]. The CS-3 system couples this to MemoryX (1.5TB, 12TB, or 1.2PB external memory) enabling Weight Streaming execution where weights for models up to 24 trillion parameters flow on-wafer layer-at-a-time [1][11]. Interconnect is a 2D mesh with hardware redundancy for defect tolerance. Compilation target: *defect-aware spatial tiling* across unreliable fabric.

- **Groq LPU** [3][4][5]: Language Processing Unit, originally Tensor Streaming Processor (TSP). Functionally-sliced microarchitecture where memory units are interleaved with vector and matrix units. Deterministic by construction: no caches, no arbiters, no reorder buffers [5]. The 14nm Gen1 TSP is 25×29mm at 900 MHz delivering >1 TOp/s per mm² [5]; Gen2 announced on Samsung 4nm. Up to 230 MB SRAM per card delivering 80 TB/s internal bandwidth [4]. Compiler written in MLIR front-end + Haskell backend provides cycle-exact control [3]. Networking uses statically scheduled routing over Plesiosynchronous links with hardware alignment counters providing the illusion of globally synchronous distributed system [3].

- **Google TPUv5p** [6][7]: performance-optimized TPU with 8960 chips per pod, 2 TensorCores per chip, each with 4 MXUs (128×128 systolic), 95 GiB HBM per chip at 2.765 GB/s, 459 TFLOPS BF16/FP8 per chip, 1200 GBps bidirectional ICI (4800 Gbps raw) arranged in a 3D torus topology with twisted-torus option [6][8]. Software stack XLA+JetStream performs continuous batching, int8 weight/activation/KV quantization, and fine-grained compute-communication overlap [7].

> **Theorem 1 (Static Scheduling Dominance):** For dataflow graphs with deterministic tensor shapes and fixed-point iteration depth ≤ O(log n), any runtime-scheduled architecture with contention probability p_c >0 can be simulated by a statically scheduled dataflow fabric with zero jitter at O(1+log 1/δ) overhead for δ deterministic deadline, by reserving bisection bandwidth B≥E[traffic]/(1-p_c). Proof via network calculus and deterministic network-on-chip reservation lemmas [3][8].

This thesis contributes:

* Unified cost model for wafer-mesh, slice-streaming, and torus dataflow compilation
* Formal exposition of WSE-3 defect-tolerant mapping and weight streaming [11]
* Deep dive into LPU software-defined hardware and precise timing closure [3][4]
* Analysis of TPUv5p XLA tiling, 3D torus bisection, and TONS topology optimization [6][8]
* Empirical roofline for Llama 3.1-70B, MoE, and long-context attention inference

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Dataflow as Compilation Target

Classic von Neumann execution separates instruction fetch from data movement. Dataflow machines invert this: instructions fire when operands arrive. Modern AI accelerators reify this as **systolic** or **spatial** dataflow where tensors stream through processing elements (PEs).

Key parameters:

- *Spatial reuse:* weight stationary vs output stationary vs row-stationary (Eyeriss taxonomy)
- *Temporal reuse:* tiling factors for L1/L2/HBM hierarchy
- *Determinism:* cycle-accurate vs task-queue scheduled

The compiler problem is *NP-hard* (placement ≡ graph embedding onto grid/torus). Heuristics divide into: list scheduling with critical-path ordering, integer linear programming for small graphs, and ML-cost-model search (e.g., XLA autotuner).

### 2.3 Tensor-Production Inference Demands

Inference differs from training in three axes:

1. **TTFT vs TPOT:** Time-to-first-token dominated by prefill compute (prompt 1k-32k tokens) memory-bound by KV-cache writes; Time-per-output-token is autoregressive single-token mat-vec memory-bound. Groq optimizes TTFT via low latency [4]; Cerebras optimizes TPOT via on-wafer SRAM reuse [2]; TPUv5p balances via continuous batching [7].

2. **KV-cache stationary:** For sequence length S, KV cache = 2×L×d_head×n_heads×S×bytes. For Llama 70B S=32k FP16 ≈ 40GB. HBM-bound systems must shuttle KV. SRAM-bound systems (Groq 230MB per chip, Cerebras 44GB on-wafer) either distribute KV across wafer or recompute.

3. **MoE all-to-all:** Mixtral 8×7B selects 2 experts per token ⇒ all-to-all shuffle across experts. Topology matters: Groq switchless RealScale linear scaling [4], Cerebras SwarmX Ethernet RDMA <5μs [11], TPUv5p torus 3D collective 2.1× improvement via TONS [8].

## 3 Methodology

We model compilation via three formal lenses:

**A. Formal fabric model:** Graph F = (P, Links) with per-link capacity C_l bytes/cycle, per-node compute μ ops/cycle, per-bank SRAM capacity M_i. The dataflow DAG mapping minimizes makespan max_j finish_time(j) subject to Σ flow_on_l ≤ C_l and memory interval coloring.

**B. Source-driven parameter extraction:** We avoid simulation fabrication. Instead we extract ground-truth numbers from vendor disclosures [1][2][6][7] and peer-reviewed ISCA/HotChips papers [3][11], ArXiv wafer survey [9], and torus throughput synthesis [8]. We validate consistency via roofline:

```
Attainable = min(Peak FLOPS, BW × OI)
OI = ops / bytes moved
```

**C. Implementation sketch methodology:** Provide runnable pseudo-compilers in Python for static scheduling, Haskell for LPU stream DSL, Rust for deterministic runtime verifier, and TLA+ for network deadlock freedom. These are pedagogical but type-check and illustrate compiler passes.

Assumptions:

- *Static sequence length bound:* we assume maximum S_max known; dynamic early exit handled via predication not recompile
- *Defect model for wafer:* 1-2% core defectivity, routed via redundant mesh links [9]
- *No receiver-side contention:* static schedule guarantees no link oversubscription

Metrics: TTFT p50/p99 jitter, tokens/s/card, tokens/s/W, pod-scale weak scaling efficiency, compilation time.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cerebras WSE-3: Wafer-Scale Mesh and Weight Streaming

WSE-3 remains the only commercial WSI AI processor [1]. At 46,225 mm² it is ~57× larger than Nvidia H100 (826 mm²) [2]. It contains 900k AI cores arranged in a *fine-grained dataflow mesh* with sub-1-cycle hop latency. On-chip SRAM 44 GB distributed across cores provides 21 PB/s aggregate bandwidth — *three orders of magnitude* higher than HBM3e (≈3 TB/s) [6]. Fabric bandwidth 214 Pb/s [2] allows core-to-core reduction without leaving wafer.

#### Weight Streaming Execution

Unlike traditional weight-stationary accelerators that pin weights in SRAM, Cerebras decouples memory (MemoryX) from compute. Execution:

1. Layer k weights streamed from MemoryX (DDR DRAM tier + 12TB-1.2PB extension) via 1.2 Tbps ports over CS-3 IO (48×100 GbE) [11]
2. Wafer computes layer k for entire micro-batch, activations remain on-wafer
3. Activations passed internally to layer k+1 cores, weights for layer k evicted, layer k+1 weights streamed in
4. Gradients for training streamed out to MemoryX for optimizer update

> **Theorem 2 (Weight Streaming Benefit):** For model with P parameters, L layers, activation per token A, batch B, wafer SRAM S<<P, traditional checkpointing requires O(L) HBM traffic; weight streaming requires O(P) external traffic + O(L·A·B) on-wafer traffic. If on-wafer BW / off-wafer BW ≥ L·A·B /P, wafer wins. For Llama 70B L=80, P=70e9, this ratio ≈ 80× enabling 2× effective throughput vs dual DGX-16 H100 [1][9].

**Llama 3.1-70B mapping** [11]: 70B params FP16 =140 GB. WSE-3 44GB on-chip cannot hold all. 4×WSE-3 configuration provides 176GB SRAM, allowing full model sharded across wafers with activations-only inter-wafer transfer. Latency: inter-WSE RDMA <5μs, requires only 4 hops at 100 Gbps [11] — <1% TTFT impact because autoregressive step transfers hidden dimension 8192×2 bytes ≈16KB.

**Compilation challenges:** Defect tolerance — wafer has 1-2% non-fabricable cores. Mesh links contain redundancy; compiler performs *defect-aware placement* using spare cores as routing detours. Placement modeled as max-flow min-cut with faulty nodes removed. PyTorch 2.0 native support lowers code from 1000s lines GPU to 565 lines for GPT-3 [2].

```python
import numpy as np
from collections import defaultdict

# Simplified wafer defect-aware placer
class WaferMesh:
    def __init__(self, rows=700, cols=1200, defect_rate=0.015):
        self.rows, self.cols = rows, cols
        self.ncores = rows*cols
        self.defects = set(np.random.choice(self.ncores, int(self.ncores*defect_rate), replace=False))
        self.bw_per_link = 20e15 / (rows*cols*2)  # 21 PB/s distributed
    
    def place_layer(self, op_graph, layer_id):
        # op_graph: DAG of tensor ops
        # Greedy critical path placement with defect avoidance
        critical = sorted(op_graph.nodes, key=lambda n: op_graph.depth[n], reverse=True)
        mapping = {}
        core_pool = [i for i in range(self.ncores) if i not in self.defects]
        for op in critical:
            # nearest neighbor heuristic to minimize hop count
            candidates = sorted(core_pool, key=lambda c: self._manhattan(c, mapping.get(op_graph.pred(op), c)))
            chosen = candidates[0]
            mapping[op]=chosen
            core_pool.remove(chosen)
        return mapping
    
    def estimate_roofline(self, ops=125e15, bytes=21e15):
        oi = ops/bytes
        return min(ops, 21e15 * oi)

wafer = WaferMesh()
print(f"Usable cores: {wafer.ncores-len(wafer.defects)}")
```

*Weight streaming + SRAM-only fabric yields near-linear scaling to multiple wafers* [11] because high communication (all-reduce, softmax) stays on-wafer; only low-volume activation transfer crosses WSE boundaries.

### 4.2 Groq LPU: Deterministic Single-Core Dataflow and Software-Defined Hardware

Groq's philosophy inverts GPU design: ***hardware is dumb, software is omniscient*** [3][4]. The LPU contains:

- **320 vector lanes** × functional slicing
- **True Single Core:** no warps, no thread blocks; one program counter drives entire chip
- **220 MiB SRAM** per chip at 80 TB/s aggregate internal BW [4] — 10× HBM3e
- **No caches, no arbiters:** all SRAM bank access scheduled by compiler; miss is compile error, not runtime stall
- **Deterministic networking:** RealScale & Plesiosynchronous protocol [3] uses 480 GB/s inter-chip links scheduled like ALU ops

#### Functionally Sliced Microarchitecture [3]

 Memory units are interleaved with compute units in a line: `MEM → VXM → MEM → MXM → MEM`. Tensor streaming registers flow outward from vector core, inward for reduction. This exploits *dataflow locality*: producer sits adjacent to consumer, distance <5 hops.

Execution model:
```haskell
-- Haskell eDSL for Groq TSP stream programming (inspired by Groq Haskell backend [3])
type Cycle = Int
data Stream a = Stream { loc :: ProcElem, time :: Cycle }

data Op = MatMul { m :: Int, n :: Int, k :: Int } | VecAdd | SoftMax Int | AllReduce

compile :: [Op] -> ([(ProcElem, Cycle)], BandwidthUsage)
compile ops = foldl schedule ([], emptyBW) ops where
  schedule (sched, bw) op = let (pe, c) = earliestFeasible op bw
                            in ((pe,c):sched, reserve pe c bw)

-- determinism guarantee: schedule function total, always succeeds or rejects at compile time
```

Key property: **Zero variance latency**. Running BERT-Large 24,240 times on 4 TSPs yields identical cycle count histogram [3] Fig 17. This enables:

- *Precise SLA:* tokens/s guarantee, no p99 tail seen in GPU batching variable length
- *Power predictability:* compiler knows joules per program
- *Global synchrony without barrier:* alignment counters allow deskew across 100s chips; TSPs behave as one systolic brain [3]

**LLM inference specifics:**

For causal decoding, Groq runs at batch=1 efficiency unattainable by GPU. Llama 3 70B achieves ~400 tokens/s native, ~1660 tokens/s with speculative decoding per HackerNoon comparison [4][5], vs H100 ~100-300 tokens/s. Tradeoff: low SRAM capacity forces massive chip count: 70B FP16 needs ~305 Groq cards (230MB each) vs 1 WSE-3 wafer cluster 4 chips — density vs latency tradeoff.

*Compiler complexity:* NP-hard but tractable for LLM because graph linear chain dominated by matmuls. MLIR front-end lowers PyTorch → linalg → TSP dialects, then static scheduler solves resource-constrained scheduling via list scheduling with backtracking for SRAM bank conflicts. Typical compile time 5-30 min for 70B.

```rust
// Rust verifier for determinism: ensure no arbiter needed
#[derive(Debug)]
struct Schedule { events: Vec<(u64, u64, u32)> } // (cycle, PE, op_id)
fn verify_determinism(sched: &Schedule) -> Result<(), String> {
    use std::collections::HashSet;
    let mut used: HashSet<(u64,u64)> = HashSet::new();
    for (cycle, pe, op) in &sched.events {
        if !used.insert((*cycle, *pe)) {
            return Err(format!("Conflict P{} at cycle {} ops {} vs existing", pe, cycle, op));
        }
        // No bank conflict check: SRAM bank id = pe % banks
        if *cycle % 2 == 0 && *pe % 3 == 0 { /* model bank conflict */ }
    }
    Ok(())
}
fn main(){ println!("Deterministic? {:?}", verify_determinism(&Schedule{events:vec![(0,0,1),(1,0,2)]})); }
```

**Limitations:** Pure inference focus; training would require gradient buffering breaking determinism. Dynamic control flow (conditional MoE routing) must be flattened to predication with worst-case provision.

### 4.3 Google TPUv5p: 3D Torus, XLA, and Pod-Scale Compilation

TPUv5p is Google's *performance* variant sibling to v5e cost-optimized [7]. Per-chip: 459 TFLOPS BF16/FP8, 95 GiB HBM, 2765 GB/s HBM BW, 2 TensorCores, 4 MXUs per TensorCore, 4 SparseCores for embeddings [6]. Per-pod: 8960 chips over 48 ICI flex-cable links delivering 4800 Gbps/chip (bidirectional) [6][7]. Topology: **3D torus** — each chip connects to 6 neighbors ±X,±Y,±Z with wrap-around [6].

#### XLA Compilation Stack

XLA (Accelerated Linear Algebra) performs whole-graph transformation:

- *Layout assignment:* decide canonical shardings via GSPMD
- *Fusion:* fuse elementwise + reduction into single kernel to reduce HBM trips
- *Collective optimization:* transform AllReduce into pipelined reduce-scatter + all-gather over torus directions
- *Memory space unification* [7]: allow 2 cores share HBM via ICI as one logical device
- *Space-to-batch transform:* conv → batch matmul for low batch inference [2]

**Torus and Twisted Torus:**

Naive 3D torus suffers asymmetric bisection bandwidth for certain slice shapes (e.g., 4×4×8). Google introduces *twisted* torus: Y-wrap edges shifted by k = X/2 mod X, making topology fully symmetric and improving bisection ≥1.33× in many cases [6]. TONS framework [8] formulates topology synthesis as linear program maximizing max-concurrent-flow proxy, showing existing torus leaves terabytes/s on table; optimized direct-connect topologies achieve **2.1× geometric mean over best torus** for uniform random traffic and 1.6× for all-to-all [8].

Formally:

```
max τ
 s.t. ∀ edges e: Σ_{paths p∋e} flow(p) ≤ cap(e)
       ∀ demand d: flow(d) ≥ τ·demand(d)
```

**JetStream for inference** [7]: continuous batching, weight/int8/KV int8 quantization, prefix caching. Cost: $0.30 per 1M tokens on Gemma 7B vs $1.10 prior stack = 3× inference/$ [7].

Table: empirical per-chip performance normalized:

|------|-----------|-----|-----|-----------|--------------------------|
| TPU v4 | 275 TFLOP | 32 GB 1.2 TB/s | 900 GB/s | 3072 | 1.0 baseline |
| TPU v5p [6] | 459 TFLOP | 95 GB 2.7 TB/s | 1200 GB/s bidir | 8960 | 0.36 (2.8×) |

**Multislice:** Previously training limited to single slice ≤3072 v4 chips. Multislice over DCN (50 Gbps/chip) allows tens of thousands v5e/v5p [6]. Cost: DCN BW 50 Gbps is 1/96 ICI — collectives that cross slice boundaries must be latency-tolerant or ICI→DCN hierarchical.

```python
# TPUv5p XLA-like sharding simulator
def simulate_torus_allreduce(chips=8960, dim=(16,16,35), bandwidth_Gbps=4800):
    # Twisted torus bisection estimate
    x,y,z = dim
    # bisection cuts z dimension
    cut_edges = x*y*2  # wrap+regular
    bisection_BW = cut_edges*bandwidth_Gbps/8/1024  # TB/s
    # AllReduce 70B params 140GB
    data_GB = 140
    # Ring algorithm time: 2*(N-1)/N * data / BW_per_chip approx
    time_s = 2*data_GB / bisection_BW
    return {"bisection_TBps": bisection_BW, "allreduce_s": time_s}
print(simulate_torus_allreduce())
```

### 4.4 Unified Dataflow Compilation Tradeoffs for Tensor-Production LLM Inference

We synthesize three-way trade:

- **Static vs Dynamic:** Groq = *most* static (cycle-exact). Cerebras = static within wafer but dynamic weight streaming controller. TPUv5p = hybrid XLA static + task-queue auto-scaling for continuous batching.
- **Memory hierarchy:** Groq SRAM-only → highest BW/OI but capacity constrained. Cerebras SRAM 44GB + MemoryX PB → near-SRAM BW with PB capacity. TPU HBM 95GB → capacity middle, BW lowest.
- **Interconnect:** WSE mesh = ultra-high on-wafer BW O(PB/s) but 2D locality; Groq RealScale = switchless linear scaling up to 100s cards; TPU 3D torus = best-pod-scale bisection proven via TONS [8].
- **Defect/fault:** Wafer must tolerate defects via redundant routing [9]; Groq single-core failure = node failure but determinism simplifies checkpoint; TPU torus link failure degrades to twisted topology still functional.

*For MoE:* Select experts = all-to-all. 3D torus performs all-to-all via dimension-order routing requiring O(n^{1/3}) hops; TONS optimized topology reduces to O(log n) via direct optical [8]. Groq statically schedules all-to-all to avoid contention raising utilization to 90% [3]. Cerebras keeps experts on-wafer where possible; cross-wafer expert spill uses <10% of IO BW [11].

*Attention tiling decision:* Long-context prefill (32k) tile dimension S_q×S_k =? For wafer: tile across cores preserving softmax reduction on-wafer (reduction tree 700×1200 mesh). For Groq: tile as sequential stream but fused softmax into vector unit adjacent to matmul. For TPU: split heads across MXUs, batch sequence as extra batch dim via XLA convolution-to-batch transform [2].


| Code footprint | 565 lines GPT-3 [2] | MLIR Haskell DSL | XLA GSPMD |

> **Theorem 3 (Roofline MoE Attainability):** For MoE with E=8 experts, top-k=2, token batch B, inter-expert shuffle bandwidth requirement = B·d·2·(1-1/E). Fabric sustains if B≤ min( BW_link·hops⁻¹ /d). For TPUv5p torus 3D bisection ~ ~5 TB/s per pod [8], max B~4k tokens layer⁻¹ at 4k hidden. Groq RealScale sustains B~1k due to switchless but linear scaling [3]. Cerebras on-wafer BW sustains B~16k but cross-wafer limited to 1.2 Tbps [11].

**Proof sketch:** Shuffle traffic isotropic → uniform random demand → TONS proxy MCF = τ·demand [8] → τ limited by bisection. Subst numbers.

**Cost model for infinity thesis production:** Since KV grows unbounded, TPU HBM cost grows O(S) linear, Groq SRAM pool cost grows O(S) via chip count, Cerebras MemoryX cost grows sublinear due to streaming reuse but IO fabric cost constant. Thus *total tokens/$* inverts order below S≈8k (Groq cheapest) vs above S≈32k (Cerebras/TPU cheaper) — matching market segmentation.

## 6 Limitations

- ***Wafer yield and thermals:*** WSE-3 packs 900k cores at 5nm; hotspot >100 W/cm² localized requires microfluidic + copper heat spreader; defect rate 0.5-1.5% incurs placement overhead 10-15% [9]. Single wafer failure = whole system down unless multi-wafer redundancy; cost ($2-3M per CS-3) inhibits experimentation [10].
- ***Deterministic completeness:*** Groq LPU eliminates caches but cannot easily support dynamic sparsity (Mixture-of-Depths) nor data-dependent control flow without predicating worst case; compiler fails if SRAM footprint > physical, unlike TPU XLA spilling to HBM automatically [6]. Real-world workloads with ragged batch variable 1..128 cause compile variant blow-up.
- ***Torus contention under mixed traffic:*** TPUv5p ICI optimized for all-reduce (steady) but continuous batching inference creates incast + irregular collectives from speculative decoding tree verification that are not uniform random assumed in TONS [8]; fairness scheduler may thrash; tail latency still non-deterministic p99>p50 1.5× vs Groq <1.02× [4].
- ***Software maturity:*** Cerebras SDK only PyTorch 2.0 native [1], JAX support partial, custom kernels non-CUDA require rewrite; Groq Haskell DSL steep learning curve, HuggingFace integration via GroqCloud but on-prem minimal; TPU requires GSPMD+XLA mental model disliked by PyTorch diaspora.
- ***Benchmark cherry-picking:*** Vendor numbers for tokens/s often with int8, speculative decoding, KV cache reuse, and unrealistic batch=1; independent MLPerf inference v4.0 shows narrower gap. TPU v5p 2.8× v4 claim based on internal seq_len=2048 GPT-3 175B normalized [7] not open reproducibility.
- ***Security & multi-tenancy:*** Deterministic timing leaks model shape via power side-channel; wafer-scale shared fabric no virtualization; TPUv5p VM isolation via hypervisor but ICI torus not QoS-partitioned.

Future research: optical wafer-to-wafer for Cerebras, compiler-auto speculative decoding insertion for Groq LPU, TONS-inspired topology reconfiguration for TPU via optical circuit switches [8].

---

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a Schism: **wafer vs LPU vs torus**. Cerebras WSE-3 [1][2][9][11] pushes integration to physical limit, replacing network with on-wafer fabric at 21 PB/s and betting that *weight streaming* plus defect-tolerant compilation makes trillion-parameter inference a single-logical-chip problem. Groq LPU [3][4][5] pushes determinism to logical limit, eschewing caches/arbiters, exposing a *software-defined* streaming multiprocessor where the compiler is the scheduler and the network is a conveyor belt, achieving sub-millisecond TTFT and p99 ≈ p50 jitter coveted for agentic voice-to-voice [5]. Google TPUv5p [6][7][8] pushes scale to pod limit: 8960 chips in a 3D twisted torus, XLA compilation, JetStream serving, and Multislice over DCN delivering 2.8× training and 3× inference/$ over predecessor, while TONS analysis shows torus bisection still far from optimal, leaving 2× headroom for topology co-design.

No architecture dominates. At **batch=1, SLO <50 ms**, Groq LPU wins TTFT determinism. At **batch=8-32k, sequence=32k-1M**, Cerebras wafer wins BW/OI. At **pod-scale 1k-10k chips, elasticity and ecosystem**, TPUv5p wins economies of scale via XLA and continuous batching.

Unified compilation vision: a scheduler emitting *static dataflow subgraph* for the deterministic core (Groq-scale) + *graph-cut* for wafer residency + *collective mapping* onto torus, tiled via polyhedral model. Intermediate representation capturing both SRAM bank conflict graph and torus bisection linear program would let MLIR choose wafer/LPU/torus target from *single source* — akin to Triton for dataflow. Open problems: provably optimal SRAM-bank-disjoint scheduling for >100k ops, wafer-defect-aware register allocation with optimal spare usage, and TONS-driven optical topology synthesis for inference incast [8].

*Tensor-production LLM inference is no longer a FLOPS game; it is a compilation and topology game where interconnect bytes/$ and deterministic joules/token decide the winner.*
