# Hybrid Epithelial–Mesenchymal States and the Transcriptional Circuits of Metastasis: ZEB1/SNAIL Networks, Collective Invasion, and Circulating Tumor Cell Clusters

## Abstract

Metastasis remains the dominant cause of cancer mortality, and the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) has emerged as a central conceptual framework for understanding how cohesive epithelial tumors acquire motility, intravasate, and colonize distant organs. This thesis argues that the clinically decisive EMT configuration is not a binary switch but a spectrum of stable **hybrid epithelial/mesenchymal (E/M) states** stabilized by coupled microRNA–transcription-factor feedback loops, principally the *miR-34/SNAIL/miR-200/ZEB1* circuit. Drawing on dynamical-systems modeling, lineage tracing, and circulating tumor cell (CTC) biology, we show that the hybrid E/M state uniquely supports **collective invasion** and the formation of **polyclonal CTC clusters** whose metastatic competence exceeds that of single CTCs by up to two orders of magnitude. We develop a mathematical treatment of the ternary chimera switch (TCS) versus cascading bistable switches (CBS) models, review genetic evidence distinguishing the complementary roles of SNAIL, TWIST, and ZEB1 in vivo, and analyze leader–follower organization in collective strands marked by keratin-14. We conclude with the limitations of EMT-targeted therapy and the open problem of selectively disarming hybrid-state plasticity without disrupting physiological EMT in wound repair.

## 1 Introduction

Carcinomas, which account for the majority of solid tumors, arise from epithelial tissues and inherit their cell–cell adhesion machinery. The paradox of carcinoma metastasis — that cohesive epithelial cells can nevertheless disseminate to distant organs — was substantially resolved by the recognition that developmental gene-regulatory programs of epithelial–mesenchymal transition can be reactivated in malignancy [1]. Yet a decade of genetic perturbation studies complicated this picture: conditional deletion of the EMT transcription factors *Snail1* or *Twist1* in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma models did not abolish metastasis, prompting premature claims that EMT is dispensable for metastatic spread [2].

This thesis defends a refined hypothesis. The contradictions dissolve once EMT is treated not as a binary epithelial-to-mesenchymal switch but as a **dynamical landscape with one or more stable hybrid attractors** [3]. In this landscape, distinct EMT transcription factors (EMT-TFs) occupy complementary positions: SNAIL is an early, low-threshold responder that initiates junctional loosening, whereas ZEB1 functions as a high-threshold, hysteresis-generating node that locks in the mesenchymal program and confers stemness and metabolic plasticity [2]. Crucially, the *hybrid* epithelial/mesenchymal (E/M) phenotype — cells that simultaneously express epithelial adhesion molecules and mesenchymal motility genes — is the most metastatically potent configuration. It is the phenotype of collective invasion strands and of the polyclonal circulating tumor cell (CTC) clusters that account for a disproportionate share of metastatic seeding events [4][5].

We proceed as follows. Section 2 reviews the molecular hardware of EMT. Section 3 introduces the dynamical-systems framework used throughout. Section 4 presents four deep investigations: the ZEB1/SNAIL circuit architecture, the stabilization of partial EMT, the biomechanics of collective invasion, and the biology of CTC clusters. Section 5 evaluates the empirical evidence; Section 6 enumerates limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The classical EMT program

The canonical description of EMT, codified by Kalluri and Weinberg, distinguishes developmental (Type 1), fibrosis-associated (Type 2), and cancer-associated (Type 3) EMT [1]. Type 3 EMT endows carcinoma cells with the capacity to dissolve basement membrane, migrate through stroma, and enter the vasculature. The molecular signature is the coordinated downregulation of epithelial markers — **E-cadherin** (*CDH1*), cytokeratins, claudins, and the apical–basal polarity complexes (Par, Crumbs, Scribble) — and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers — **N-cadherin**, **vimentin**, **fibronectin** — together with matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) that degrade the basement membrane [1][7].

The program is orchestrated by a small set of EMT-TFs: **SNAIL1/2 (Snail/Slug)**, **ZEB1/2**, and **TWIST1/2**. All directly or indirectly repress *CDH1*. SNAIL1 binds E-boxes in the *CDH1* promoter; ZEB1 represses polarity genes including *CRB3* and activates MMP expression; TWIST1 acts more indirectly, in part through upregulation of SNAIL and BMI1 [7]. The process is inducible by stromal signals — most prominently **TGF-β**, but also Wnt, Notch, hypoxia via HIF-1α, and inflammatory cytokines — and is **reversible**: the mesenchymal–epithelial transition (MET) permits re-epithelialization at the distant site, facilitating macrometastatic outgrowth [1].

### 2.2 EMT, stemness, and chemoresistance

Cells traversing EMT acquire traits of cancer stem cells: *CD44^hi/CD24^lo* signatures, sphere-forming capacity, and therapy resistance [1]. This coupling between EMT and stemness is not incidental; the same transcription factors that repress epithelial genes activate stemness regulators (e.g., SOX2, BMI1) and drug-efflux pumps. Clinically, tumors enriched in hybrid or mesenchymal signatures show inferior responses to endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, and, increasingly, immunotherapy [6].

### 2.3 The spectrum view

The binary E/M dichotomy was always an experimental convenience. Single-cell RNA sequencing and flow cytometry have documented stable intermediates co-expressing E-cadherin and vimentin — the **hybrid E/M states** — in cell lines (e.g., H1975 lung carcinoma cells, which display intermediate ZEB1 with retained E-cadherin) and in primary tumors [3]. These states are not transient; they are attractors of the underlying regulatory network, stabilized by "phenotypic stability factors" such as OVOL1/2, GRHL2, ΔNp63α, and NRF2, which expand the hybrid basin of attraction [6].

## 3 Methodology

Our analytical framework combines **network topology analysis** with **nonlinear dynamical modeling** of ordinary differential equation (ODE) systems. The core circuit — miR-34/SNAIL/miR-200/ZEB1 — comprises two coupled double-negative feedback loops driven by an exogenous TGF-β signal *S*.

A minimal Python prototype of this bistable motif illustrates how hysteresis arises:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

def emt_circuit(t, y, S, p):
    """Reduced miR-200/ZEB double-negative loop with TGF-beta drive."""
    Z, m = y                      # ZEB protein, miR-200
    gZ, gM, kZ, kM, n, lam = p
    Hz  = gZ * (lam**n / (lam**n + m**n))       # miR-200 represses ZEB
    Hz += 0.3 * gZ * (S * Z**2) / (1 + Z**2)     # autocrine/TGF-beta + ZEB self-activation
    Hm  = gM / (1 + (Z / 0.5)**n)                 # ZEB represses miR-200
    return [Hz - kZ * Z, Hm - kM * m]

# Sweep TGF-beta up then down -> hysteresis loop reveals the hybrid window
for direction in ("up", "down"):
    z_last = 0.1 if direction == "up" else 3.0
    for S in (np.linspace(0, 3, 40) if direction == "up"
              else np.linspace(3, 0, 40)):
        sol = solve_ivp(emt_circuit, [0, 60], [z_last, 1.0],
                        args=(S, (1.0, 1.0, 0.2, 0.2, 3, 2.0)))
        z_last = sol.y[0, -1]
        print(f"S={S:.2f} -> ZEB*={z_last:.3f} [{direction}]")
```

Bifurcation analysis of this system is the basis of the two competing mathematical frameworks — the **ternary chimera switch (TCS)** and **cascading bistable switches (CBS)** models — evaluated in Section 4.1. We assess models against four experimental discriminators: the E → E/M → M two-step transition, differential SNAIL/ZEB1 sensitivity to TGF-β dose, irreversibility of complete EMT, and single-cell co-expression of ZEB1 and E-cadherin [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The ZEB1/SNAIL circuit: two switches, two thresholds

> **Theorem (TCS model, informal):** The coupled miR-34/SNAIL and miR-200/ZEB double-negative loops form a ternary chimera switch admitting up to three stable fixed points — E, hybrid E/M, and M — ordered along a TGF-β bifurcation parameter, with SNAIL acting as the low-threshold initiator and ZEB1 as the high-threshold, hysteresis-generating effector [3].

The TCS model makes specific, testable claims. First, the concentration of exogenous TGF-β required to activate SNAIL is *lower* than that required for ZEB1, because miR-200 inhibits ZEB1 more strongly than miR-34 inhibits SNAIL [3]. This produces a **two-step transition**: cells first enter the hybrid E/M state (high SNAIL, low–intermediate ZEB1) and only at higher signal strength proceed to full M. Second, the irreversibility of complete EMT is attributed to strong ZEB1-mediated inhibition of miR-34 and to ZEB1 self-activation via the CD44s/ZEB1 loop, which together generate a hysteresis window that resists signal withdrawal [3].

The competing **CBS model** instead proposes that miR-34/SNAIL and miR-200/ZEB behave as two independent bistable switches triggered at different thresholds, with SNAIL required upstream for ZEB activation. Experimental discrimination favors TCS: single-cell analysis shows ZEB1 present at *intermediate* levels in hybrid E/M cells (H1975), inconsistent with a pure bistable view, and SNAIL overexpression in HMLE cells fails to initiate EMT when ZEB1 and FOXC2 are absent — demonstrating that SNAIL alone is insufficient and that the network operates as an integrated chimera rather than a cascade of independent toggles [3].

Genetic evidence from the KPC pancreatic cancer model sharpens the functional division. Deleting *Snail1* or *Twist1* left metastasis intact, but deleting **Zeb1** suppressed precursor lesion formation, invasion, stemness, colonization capacity, and — crucially — metabolic plasticity, the ability of tumor cells to switch between oxidative and glycolytic programs during dissemination [2]. The conclusion is not that EMT is dispensable, but that EMT-TFs have **complementary subfunctions**: SNAIL initiates junctional remodeling while ZEB1 executes and stabilizes the invasive, plastic program. Therapeutic strategies must therefore target the factors *simultaneously* rather than treating them as interchangeable [2].

### 4.2 Partial EMT: stabilization of the hybrid attractor

If complete EMT were the only route to dissemination, carcinoma cells would arrive at distant organs as solitary mesenchymal cells incapable of proliferative outgrowth. In reality, tumor cells *fluently convert between intermediate states* retaining partial epithelial character, and these intermediates are the workhorses of metastasis [6].

The hybrid E/M state is defined operationally by co-expression: E-cadherin or keratins alongside vimentin, N-cadherin, or ZEB1 at intermediate levels. Computationally, hybrids correspond to a middle attractor whose basin of attraction is widened by **phenotypic stability factors (PSFs)**: OVOL2, GRHL2, and NRF2 each couple to the core circuit in ways that enlarge the hybrid region of parameter space [6]. The biological consequence is that hybrid cells can hold their position under fluctuating microenvironmental signals — precisely the noise a cell encounters while traversing stroma, vessels, and foreign parenchyma.

Hybrid cells also uniquely combine adhesion with motility, enabling **collective behaviors** impossible for either pure phenotype: single E cells cannot move, and single M cells cannot hold together. The hybrid state is therefore not a halfway house but a *distinct functional program* optimized for the two most dangerous steps of the metastatic cascade — collective invasion and cluster-based dissemination [4][5].

### 4.3 Collective invasion: leader–follower organization

Carcinomas frequently invade adjacent tissue not as single cells but as **cohesive strands, sheets, and multicellular buds** that maintain cell–cell junctions [5]. Three-dimensional analysis of the stromal border of human breast tumors detected frequent collective invasion and *virtually no* single cancer cells, overturning the textbook image of lone mesenchymal escapees [5].

Collective strands exhibit a division of labor. **Leader cells** at the invasive tip display a basal epithelial differentiation program — expression of keratin-14 (K14), K5, P-cadherin, and p63 — together with proteolytic machinery (MT1-MMP) that clears a path through the extracellular matrix; **follower cells** maintain tighter epithelial character and are carried along the remodeled track [5]. This K14+ invasive state is observed at every stage of dissemination — collective invasion, local dissemination, CTC clusters, and micrometastases — but is *rare* in the primary tumor bulk and in macrometastases, suggesting distinct epithelial programs for growth versus dissemination [5].

> **Mechanistic highlight:** The Snail–claudin-11 axis illustrates how partial EMT serves cohesion rather than dissolving it. In squamous cell carcinoma, SNAIL induces the tight-junction protein **claudin-11**, maintaining cell–cell contact within clusters rather than dismantling it [6]. Similarly, plakoglobin (γ-catenin) — a constituent of desmosomes and adherens junctions — is highly expressed in clusters; its knockdown reduces cluster formation and lung metastatic nodules without affecting primary tumor growth [6][9].

The biomechanical implication is profound: collective invasion units behave as **active viscoelastic materials** whose leader-generated traction and follower-generated cohesion must be jointly tuned. Partial EMT tunes both dials simultaneously, whereas complete EMT maximizes traction at the cost of cohesion — a trade that works for single-cell escape but forfeits the survival advantages of the cluster.

### 4.4 Circulating tumor cell clusters: polyclonal seeds of metastasis

The most striking vindication of the hybrid-state hypothesis comes from the circulation. **CTC clusters** — multicellular aggregates of 2–100 tumor cells traveling together in blood — constitute only 2–5% of CTC events yet possess 23- to 50-fold greater metastatic competence than single CTCs in mouse models, accounting for roughly half of all metastatic lesions [4]. Reports estimate up to 100-fold increases in metastatic potential [8].

Three lines of evidence establish their nature:

| Property | Single CTCs | CTC Clusters | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Origin | Intravasation of individual cells post-complete EMT | Oligoclonal detachment from primary tumor (multicolor lineage tracing) | Aceto et al. [4] |
| Junctional state | E-cadherin-negative; complete EMT | E-cadherin retained at junctions; desmosomal proteins (plakoglobin, K14) enriched | [4][6][9] |
| EMT state | Mesenchymal | **Hybrid E/M**: epithelial adhesion + vimentin co-expression | [6][9] |
| Epigenetics | Baseline | Hypomethylation of OCT4/NANOG/SOX2 binding sites; hypermethylation of Polycomb targets | Gkountela et al., cited in [8][9] |
| Metastatic efficiency | Reference (1×) | 23–50× (up to ~100× reported) | [4][8] |

Multicolor lineage tracing (GFP/mCherry mixtures in LM2 and 4T1 models) showed that clusters are overwhelmingly **oligoclonal/polyclonal** — multicolored aggregates derived from a single tumor — and that separate primary tumors produce single-color clusters, proving clusters detach as pre-formed units rather than aggregating intravascularly in breast cancer [4][9]. Because clusters carry polyclonal genomes, polyclonal metastases — long an embarrassment for the single-cell seeding model — are naturally explained [4].

Molecularly, clusters are hybrid E/M incarnate: they retain E-cadherin at cell–cell junctions, express keratins 5/8/14 and plakoglobin, and simultaneously display cell-surface vimentin, a mesenchymal marker linked to invasiveness [6][9]. Notably, E-cadherin itself supports survival during metastatic progression — anoikis resistance — so that the epithelial features of clusters are not vestigial but *functional* [9]. The cluster's epigenetic state is further reprogrammed: CTC clustering induces hypomethylation at binding sites for stemness regulators OCT4, NANOG, and SOX2, enhancing their expression — a mechanistic link between the collective mode of travel and the stem-like competence to found a new tumor [8].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

We now test the hybrid-state framework against the strongest available evidence, distinguishing confirmed findings from contested ones.

**Confirmed.** (i) ZEB1 is required for pancreatic cancer metastasis and for stemness/plasticity in the KPC model, while SNAIL and TWIST are individually dispensable — establishing complementary EMT-TF subfunctions [2]. (ii) The core miR-34/SNAIL/miR-200/ZEB1 circuit admits tristability consistent with hybrid E/M attractors, and experimental discriminators favor the TCS model [3]. (iii) CTC clusters are oligoclonal, hybrid E/M, and 23–50× more metastatic than single CTCs [4]. (iv) Collective invasion led by K14+ leader cells is the dominant invasion mode at the human breast tumor–stroma border [5]. (v) Junctional proteins (plakoglobin, claudin-11, keratins) are *causally* required for cluster formation and metastasis, not merely correlated [6].

**Contested.** Whether clusters form exclusively by collective detachment or also by intravascular CD44-mediated aggregation remains debated; evidence favors detachment as dominant in breast cancer but permits aggregation in specific vascular beds [6][9]. The universality of the 23–50× competence multiplier across carcinoma types is unverified — estimates derive primarily from breast models.

**Clinical correlates.** Hybrid EMT signatures predict chemoresistance and poor prognosis; ZEB1 is enriched in triple-negative breast cancer and cooperates with ERα signaling in bone metastasis [6][8]; CTC-cluster burden is a prognostic marker in TNBC [6].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Model specificity.** The TCS/CBS framework was calibrated largely on epithelial cell lines (HMLE, H1975, NCI-60); whether identical bifurcation structures hold in vivo under stromal and immune co-signaling is unproven.
2. **EMT-TF redundancy.** Mammalian EMT-TFs form a redundant family (ZEB1/2, SNAIL1/2, TWIST1/2); single-gene knockouts underestimate network robustness, and the KPC results reflect one tissue context.
3. **Marker-based inference.** "Hybrid E/M" is defined by marker co-expression, which correlates imperfectly with functional state; a cell co-expressing E-cadherin and vimentin may still behave as effectively epithelial in some assays.
4. **Cluster quantification bias.** CTC isolation technologies are biased toward larger clusters and may miss single hybrid cells, inflating cluster-specific effect sizes.
5. **Therapeutic window.** Physiological EMT (wound healing, placentation) uses identical circuitry; systemic EMT inhibition risks severe on-target toxicity. Moreover, forcing MET at the wrong cascade step could *accelerate* colonization of already-disseminated cells.
6. **Temporal resolution.** Most evidence is endpoint-based; the real-time dynamics of individual cells traversing E → E/M → M during intravasation have not been directly observed in patients.

## 7 Conclusion

The epithelial–mesenchymal transition is best understood not as a binary switch but as a **tri-stable dynamical system** whose middle attractor — the hybrid E/M state — is the principal engine of carcinoma metastasis. The miR-34/SNAIL/miR-200/ZEB1 circuit implements this landscape: SNAIL as the low-threshold initiator of junctional remodeling, ZEB1 as the high-threshold effector whose self-reinforcing loops generate hysteresis, stemness, and metabolic plasticity [2][3]. Hybrid cells, stabilized by phenotypic stability factors, uniquely support **collective invasion** led by K14+ basal-programmed leaders [5] and the **polyclonal CTC clusters** that dominate metastatic seeding despite their rarity [4].

This reframing resolves the apparent contradiction between EMT's undoubted importance and the survival of metastasis after *Snail1* or *Twist1* deletion: the deleted factors are initiators, while ZEB1 executes the program — and it is the hybrid state, not the mesenchymal endpoint, that selection favors [2]. For therapy, the target is therefore not EMT *per se* but the **plasticity machinery of the hybrid attractor**: the CD44s/ZEB1 self-activation loop, the junctional proteins (plakoglobin, claudin-11) that hold clusters together, and the epigenetic reprogramming that accompanies clustering [4][6][8]. Disarming these without crippling physiological EMT is the central open problem — and the prize is the prevention of metastasis at its most efficient step.
