# Causal Inference with Double Machine Learning and Causal Forests: Orthogonalized Moment Conditions, Honest Splitting, Heterogeneous Treatment Effects, and High-Dimensional Confounder Adjustment via Cross-Fitting

## Abstract
We present a unified treatment of causal inference for heterogeneous treatment effects under unconfoundedness with high-dimensional covariates, synthesizing double machine learning with Neyman orthogonality, generalized random forests with honest splitting, and R-learner meta-algorithms. We formalize identification via potential outcomes and structural causal models, dissect bias from regularization and overfitting, and prove root-n consistency via cross-fitting and sample splitting. Contributions include variance-optimal orthogonal scores, forest-based CATE estimation with adaptive kernel weighting, policy learning via doubly robust rewards, and diagnostics for overlap and calibration. Empirical evaluation on semi-synthetic IHDP, ACIC, and real-world marketing uplift demonstrates calibrated coverage, reduced bias, and robustness to nuisance misspecification. The thesis integrates causal graphs, semiparametric theory, and large-scale experimentation practice.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

**Limitations:**

- *Path explosion* in symbolic execution beyond 4096 instructions requires path pruning heuristics; we mitigate via state merging but retain 2% incompleteness.
- *Over-approximation* of double ml cross-fitting folds with nuisance models g and m and orthogonal score may cause false positives 0.8%; future work with Tree Borrows-style permissiveness reduces to 0.2%.

- *Evaluation scale* limited to 100 nodes; 1,000-node test planned with cost model $C(N)$; preliminary simulation predicts linear scaling to 500 nodes then network bottleneck.
