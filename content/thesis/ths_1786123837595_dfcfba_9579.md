# Mamba and Selective State Space Models: HiPPO Theory, Discretization via ZOH, Hardware-Aware Parallel Scan, and Long-Context Linear Recurrence for Foundation Models

## Abstract
We provide a unified, first-principles treatment of selective state space models (SSMs) with focus on Mamba and its theoretical predecessors. Starting from classical linear time-invariant systems $x'(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t), y(t)=Cx(t)$, we trace the lineage through HiPPO optimal polynomial projection theory for long-range memory, the structured diagonal-plus-low-rank parameterization of S4, and the necessity of careful discretization via zero-order hold (ZOH) and its alternatives. We explicate the selective mechanism that makes $B, C, \Delta$ input-dependent, breaking time-invariance and convolution equivalence, and motivating the hardware-aware parallel associative scan with kernel fusion and SRAM-aware tiling. We analyze long-context linear recurrence for foundation models, its $O(N)$ complexity, $O(1)$ inference state, and its duality with linear attention, and provide empirical scaling laws, stability lemmas, and ablation perspectives relevant to pretraining at million-token scale.

## 1 Introduction
Foundation models have been dominated by Transformer architectures whose core self-attention operator incurs $O(N^2)$ time and memory in sequence length $N$ [1][2]. While subquadratic alternatives — linear attention, gated convolutions, recurrent models — have proliferated, they historically underperformed on *information-dense* modalities such as language where content-based reasoning is essential [1].

The contemporary structured state space model lineage resolves this tension through three convergent advances:

1. **Memory theory**: HiPPO [3][4] formalizes recurrent memory as *online function approximation* under a measure $\mu(t)$, deriving optimal ordinary differential equations for polynomial projection coefficients.

2. **Structured parameterization**: S4 [2] shows that choosing $A$ as diagonal plus low-rank (DPLR) with HiPPO initialization enables stable diagonalization and $O(N \log N)$ Cauchy kernel computation, solving long-range arena tasks at length $16k$ for the first time.

3. **Selectivity and systems**: Mamba [1][6] introduces input-dependent $\Delta_t, B_t, C_t$, thereby *selecting* which information to propagate or forget, and designs a hardware-aware parallel scan that retains linear scaling despite breaking convolution equivalence.

This thesis demonstrates that these ideas are not isolated tricks but a coherent theoretical stack: measure-induced orthogonal basis $\to$ stable continuous-time dynamics $\to$ discretization theory $\to$ input-dependent time-varying system $\to$ associative parallelization $\to$ linear-time foundation backbone.

> **Central claim:** Long-context foundation modeling does not require quadratic pairwise interactions if the hidden state is a rigorously constructed projection of history and the dynamics are selective and hardware-co-optimized.

Key contributions of this synthesis:

- **Unified notation** bridging control theory, orthogonal polynomials, and deep learning.
- **Derivation** of HiPPO-LegS, LegT, FouT operators and their exponential warping interpretation in S4.
- **Comparative analysis** of ZOH, FOH, bilinear, and RK4 discretization for SSMs [5] and why ZOH with timescale $\Delta$ remains default for Mamba.
- **Systems interpretation** of parallel scan as fused associative operator over semiring $(\mathbb{R}^{N \times N}, \cdot)$.
- **Scaling discussion** for million-length sequences with $5\times$ Transformer throughput [1].

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Classical State Space Models
A continuous-time linear time-invariant (LTI) SSM maps $u(t) \in \mathbb{R} \to y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ via hidden state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x'(t) &= A x(t) + B u(t) \\
y(t) &= C x(t) + D u(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}, B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}, C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$. Upon sampling with step $\Delta$, we obtain discrete recurrence:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_k &= \bar{A} x_{k-1} + \bar{B} u_k \\
y_k &= \bar{C} x_k
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\bar{A}=e^{\Delta A}, \bar{B}= (\Delta A)^{-1}(e^{\Delta A}-I)\Delta B$ under ZOH [5]. The convolutional kernel $\bar{K} = (\bar{C}\bar{B}, \bar{C}\bar{A}\bar{B}, ..., \bar{C}\bar{A}^{L-1}\bar{B})$ yields $y = u * \bar{K}$ [2].

### 2.2 From RNNs to SSMs
*RNNs* maintain $h_{t+1}=f(h_t, u_t)$ but suffer vanishing gradients and memory bottleneck. *SSMs* linearize $f$ while enriching $A$ initialization. The distinction is **principled memory**: HiPPO explicitly optimizes:

$$
c(t) = \arg\min_{c} \int_{-\infty}^{t} \| u(s) - \sum_n c_n(t) g_n^{(t)}(s) \|^2 d\mu^{(t)}(s)
$$

where $g_n^{(t)}$ are orthogonal basis functions under measure $\mu^{(t)}$ [3].

### 2.3 Related Lineage

| Model | Core Innovation | Complexity | Memory Horizon |
|-------|-----------------|------------|----------------|
| LMU | Legendre basis sliding window | $O(N)$ | Fixed window $\theta$ |
| HiPPO-LegS | Scaled Legendre over full history | $O(N)$ | Unbounded, $1/t$ measure |
| S4 [2] | DPLR $A$, Cauchy kernel | $O(N \log N)$ | $\approx 10k$+ |
| DSS / S4D | Diagonal $A$ simplification | $O(N \log N)$ | Similar |
| Mamba [1] | Selective $B_t,C_t,\Delta_t$ | $O(N)$ scan | Content-adaptive |
| Mamba-2 [6] | SSD - structured duality | $O(N)$ | Matmul-optimized |

*Italicized emphasis*: The shift from *time-invariant* to *time-varying* is the *defining* architectural phase transition.

## 3 Methodology
We adopt a constructive methodology: derive memory ODE from first principles, impose structural constraints for stable computation, discretize under input-driven timescale, and map to hardware-efficient execution.

### 3.1 HiPPO Formalism
Given measure $\mu^{(t)}(s) = \frac{1}{t} \mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(s)$ for LegS, optimal coefficient dynamics satisfy:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} c(t) = A c(t) + B u(t)
$$

where for HiPPO-LegS:

$$
A_{nk} = -\begin{cases}
\sqrt{(2n+1)(2k+1)} & n>k \\
n+1 & n=k \\
0 & n<k
\end{cases}, \quad B_n = \sqrt{2n+1}
$$

with time-warping factor $1/t$ absorbed into exponentially-scaled variant for S4 [4]. This matrix is **highly non-normal**, eigenvalues with negative real part ensuring stability.

### 3.2 Selective Parameterization
Mamba generalizes LTI to linear time-varying (LTV):

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_t &= S_B(u_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N}, \quad C_t = S_C(u_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N} \\
\Delta_t &= \tau_{\Delta}(\text{Broadcast}_D(S_{\Delta}(u_t))) = \text{softplus}(W_{\Delta} u_t + b_{\Delta})
\end{aligned}
$$

where $S_B, S_C, S_{\Delta}$ are linear projections. Then:

$$
\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A), \quad \bar{B}_t = \Delta_t B_t \text{ (simplified; exact ZOH integral variant in practice)}
$$

This selection is analogous to gating in RNNs but derived via discretization parameterization [1].

### 3.3 Hardware-Aware Scan
Naive recurrence is sequential and memory-bound. Mamba reformulates recurrence as associative scan over pairs $(p_t,q_t) = (\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t u_t)$ with binary op:

$$
(p_2,q_2) \otimes (p_1,q_1) = (p_2 p_1, p_2 q_1 + q_2)
$$

This is parallelizable via Blelloch scan in $O(\log N)$ steps on GPU, with **kernel fusion** preventing materialization of $N \times D \times \text{state}$ intermediates in HBM [1][6].

```python
# Simplified selective scan (PyTorch-like)
def selective_scan(u, delta, A, B, C, D):
    # u: (B, L, D), delta: (B, L, D) -> (B, L, D)
    # A: (D, N), B,C: (B, L, N)
    dA = torch.exp(torch.einsum('bld,dn->bldn', delta, A))
    dB = torch.einsum('bld,bln->bldn', delta, B)
    h = torch.zeros(B, D, N, device=u.device)
    ys = []
    for i in range(L):
        h = dA[:,i] * h + dB[:,i] * u[:,i,None]
        y = torch.einsum('dn,bln->bld', C[:,i], h) + D * u[:,i]
        ys.append(y)
    return torch.stack(ys, dim=1)  # real kernel uses fused parallel scan
```

> Theorem: Selective SSM Duality
> *For diagonal $A$, selective SSM with scan is equivalent to linear attention with data-dependent causal mask $L_{ij} = \prod_{k=j+1}^{i} \bar{A}_k$ and admits both quadratic dual (matmul) and linear dual (recurrence) forms.* [6]

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HiPPO Theory and Orthogonal Basis Projections
HiPPO solves online function approximation [3]. For each $t$, we desire coefficients $c(t)$ that minimize reconstruction error of $u_{<t}$ under measure $\mu^{(t)}$.

**LegT (translated):** $\mu^{(t)}(s)=\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{1}_{[t-\theta,t]}$ — sliding window, basis = Legendre polynomials shifted. ODE: $c' = -\frac{1}{\theta}A_{LegT} c + \frac{1}{\theta}B u$.

**LegS (scaled):** $\mu^{(t)}(s)=\frac{1}{t}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}$ — full history, time-scaled. ODE time-varying: $c' = \frac{1}{t}A_{LegS}c + \frac{1}{t}B u$, later converted to time-invariant via $d/dt$ warping $e^{s-t}$ [4].

**FouT:** Fourier basis, measure uniform, yields circulant-like $A$ for periodic compression.

The key lemma in [4] (Corollary C.10) shows S4's time-invariant $\tilde{A}$ corresponds to projecting under exponential measure $\mu(s)=e^{s-t}\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,t]}$, i.e., $c_n(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t u(s) L_n(e^{s-t}) e^{s-t} ds$. This explains why simply dropping $1/t$ works: it changes weighting from uniform-over-history to **exponential forgetting**, which is *more* stable for optimization.

*Properties of LegS:*

- **Timescale robustness**: Invariant to $u(\alpha t)$ up to reparameterizing $\Delta$.
- **Bounded gradients**: $\| \partial c(t)/\partial u(s) \| \le O(1)$ regardless of $t-s$ due to orthonormality.
- **Fast updates**: $A = D + P Q^\top$ low-rank correction allows $O(N)$ update using Woodbury.

This non-normal $A$ with eigenvalues clustered near negative real axis is initialization, not frozen — training retains spectral bias while allowing adaptation.

### 4.2 Discretization via Zero-Order Hold and Beyond
Continuous-to-discrete conversion assumes intersample behavior of $u(t)$. ZOH assumes $u(t)=u_k$ for $t \in [k\Delta,(k+1)\Delta)$ [5].

Exact ZOH solution of ODE over interval:

$$
x_{k} = e^{A\Delta} x_{k-1} + \left(\int_0^{\Delta} e^{A\tau} d\tau\right) B u_k = \bar{A} x_{k-1} + A^{-1}(\bar{A}-I) B u_k
$$

Mamba simplifies $\bar{B} = \Delta B$ when $\Delta$ small and $A$ structured as $\approx$ diagonal, justified because $\int_0^{\Delta} e^{A\tau} d\tau \approx \Delta I$ for small $\Delta A$ and input-dependent $\Delta$ already modulates scale.

Alternatives analyzed in [5]:

- **FOH**: Linear interpolation $u(t)=u_k + \frac{t}{\Delta}(u_{k+1}-u_k)$; reduces lag but requires lookahead, not causal for autoregressive LM.
- **Bilinear (Tustin)**: $\bar{A}=(I-\Delta A/2)^{-1}(I+\Delta A/2)$, frequency warping preserves stability, better for vision where spatial signal has high-frequency edges.
- **RK4**: Fourth-order accurate but $4\times$ function evals per step; training overhead prohibitive.

Empirically, BIL outperforms ZOH by $+0.6$-$1.1\%$ top-1 on ImageNet for ViM, while POL/HOH gain more but at cost. ZOH remains default for language because *causality + variable $\Delta_t$ already mimics adaptive hold* — selection compensates for discretization error.

### 4.3 Hardware-Aware Parallel Selective Scan
Why not convolution? Selective $\bar{A}_t,\bar{B}_t$ varying per token breaks LTI convolution theorem — kernel $\bar{K}$ becomes data-dependent and $O(N^2)$ if unrolled. Recurrence is $O(N)$ but naive loop is memory-bound.

Mamba's systems contribution [1]:

1. **Kernel Fusion**: Compute $\Delta_t, B_t, C_t$ projection, discretization, scan, and output projection in **one** kernel, never storing $(B, L, D, N)$ intermediate $(> 10$GB at $L=1M$) in HBM.

2. **Parallel Associative Scan**: Map to work-efficient Blelloch scan with $O(N)$ ops, $O(\log N)$ parallel steps. On GPU, tiles of size $e.g., 1024$ load $A^{\log}$ into SRAM, scan locally, carry inter-tile states.

3. **Recomputation for Backward**: Instead of saving $h_t$ for gradient, recompute forward scan inside backward kernel (checkpointing), trading compute for IO — classic FlashAttention-style philosophy [6].

Complexity table:

| Method | Forward FLOPs | HBM Writes | Parallel? |
|--------|---------------|------------|-----------|
| Naive RNN | $O(BLDN)$ | $O(BLDN)$ | No |
| S4 Conv FFT | $O(BL\log L)$ | $O(BL)$ | Yes (FFT) |
| Selective Scan Fused | $O(BLDN)$ | $O(BLD)$ | Yes (associative) |
| Attention | $O(BL^2D)$ | $O(BL^2)$ | Yes |

Result: $5\times$ higher throughput than Transformer at $L=2k$, scales to $L=1M$ on single A100 where Transformer OOMs [1].

```haskell
-- Associative scan as higher-order functional
-- The binary op is associative if (a<>b)<>c == a<>(b<>c)
scan :: (Associative op) => (a -> s) -> (s -> s -> s) -> [a] -> [s]
scan f op xs = scanl1 op (map f xs)
-- In Mamba: f = \u_t -> (Abar_t, Bbar_t * u_t)
-- op = \(a1,b1) (a2,b2) -> (a2*a1, a2*b1 + b2)
selective :: [Token] -> State -> [Output]
selective tokens s0 = let states = scan lift op tokens in map project states
```

### 4.4 Long-Context Linear Recurrence for Foundation Models
As context grows to $100k$-$1M$ tokens, $O(N^2)$ becomes untenable. SSMs offer:

- **$O(1)$ per-token inference**: Like RNN, maintain fixed-size state $h \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times N}$. Generation needs no KV-cache growth; memory constant.

- **$O(N)$ pretraining**: Parallel scan matches Transformer training parallelism despite recurrence.

- **Duality [6]**: Mamba-2 shows SSM and attention are two sides of structured matrix multiplication $y = (L \circ CB^\top) \cdot (X)$, where $L$ is 1-semiseparable (lower-triangular) matrix defined by $A$. When $A$ scalar, $L$ is standard causal mask; when $A$ diagonal, it's **adaptive** mask decaying via $\bar{A}$ product.

*Scaling empirical results [1]:*

- **Language**: Mamba-3B matches Transformer++ 7B; loss scales as power law $L(N_{params}, D_{tokens})$ with steeper slope on long data.
- **Genomics**: 1M-length DNA modeling, $10\times$ speedup over Transformer.
- **Audio**: Waveform generation $= 10$ seconds @ 16kHz ($160k$ steps) without chunking.

Crucially, *selectivity restores content-based reasoning*. Pure LTI SSMs are filters; they cannot *discretely* copy or index. By making $\Delta_t$ large, model ignores token (state decays fast, $e^{\Delta A} \approx 0$); by making $\Delta_t$ small, model *persists* state indefinitely ( $e^{\Delta A} \approx I$). This **continuous gating** enables in-context learning tasks like induction heads without attention.

> Theorem: Selection Enables Universal Approximation of Regulated Functions
> *Let $\mathcal{F}$ be set of functions implementable by selective SSM with state dimension $N$, with Lipschitz $S_\Delta, S_B, S_C$. As $N\to\infty$ and $\sup_t \|\Delta_t\| \to 0$, the selective SSM converges to a universal approximator for causal, continuous, shift-equivariant operators on $C([0,T])$.* Sketch: combines Stone-Weierstrass on basis projections (HiPPO) with gating approximating indicator functions via large $\Delta$ limits.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Stability and Initialization Proof Sketch
Claim: HiPPO-LegS $A$ initialized has $\Re(\lambda_i)<0$ and condition number $O(N)$ for DPLR form, ensuring stable gradients.

*Proof outline:* $A = A^{(N)} - P P^\top$ where $A^{(N)}_{nk} = -\sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1}$ for $n>k$, $- (n+1/2)$ diag. Then $A + A^\top = -(P P^\top + \text{diag}) \prec 0$, negative definite, eigenvalues negative real part by Lyapunov. Diagonalization $A = V \Lambda V^{-1}$ exists because DPLR matrices are diagonalizable almost surely; $\Lambda$ has imaginary parts $O(N)$. Conditioning bounded by low-rank structure enables stable inversion of $(I - \Delta A)$ [2][4].

### 5.2 Empirical Scaling on Long-Range Arena and Beyond
S4 achieved $86\%$ avg LRA, $96\%$ Path-X [4]; Mamba improves perplexity on Pile from $9.2$ (GPT2-1.3B) to $8.6$ (Mamba-1.4B) at same FLOPs [1].

### 5.3 Ablation: What Matters?

* **No selection**: LTI SSM alone on language $\to$ $+0.8$ ppl degradation [1].
* **No HiPPO init**: Random $A$ $\to$ $50\%$ LRA drop.
* **No $\Delta$ selection**: Fixed $\Delta$ $\to$ loses ability to ignore padding / copying.
* **No fusion**: Materializing $B,L,D,N$ state $\to$ $20\times$ slower due to HBM spill.

### 5.4 Formal Equivalence to Linear Attention
Mamba-2 SSD [6] proves $y = L \odot (Q K^\top) V$ where $Q = C$, $K = B$, $V = X$, and $L_{ij} = \prod_{k=j+1}^{i} \bar{A}_k$ (with $\bar{A}_k =1$ recovers causal linear attention). Thus selective SSM is *attention with data-controlled decay*.

## 6 Limitations
Despite promise, selective SSMs face non-trivial constraints.

First, **diagonal simplification** limits expressivity vs full DPLR. While diagonal $A$ with HiPPO-based scaling suffices for many tasks, complex oscillatory memories (Fourier) need complex diagonal or $2\times2$ real blocks; pure real diagonal may underfit phase-dependent dynamics.

Second, **discretization sensitivity**. ZOH with learned $\Delta$ works because $\Delta$ adapts, but theory still assumes piecewise constant input. At very high compression ratios (e.g., $100\times$ patch merging in vision), intra-patch variation violates ZOH, causing aliasing that FOH or bilinear mitigates [5]. Recent Vision Mamba work shows $+0.7\%$ gain switching to BIL at negligible extra cost — suggesting ZOH not universally optimal.

Third, **selectivity overhead**: making $B_t, C_t, \Delta_t$ input-dependent introduces $O(B L D N)$ compute even if memory remains linear. At very small $L$ ($<128$), overhead outweighs attention's optimized matmul, making Mamba slower than FlashAttention-2. Hybrid Mamba-Attention architectures are active research [6].

Fourth, **initialization stability for very large $N$** ($>256$) remains brittle; $\Re(\lambda)$ bound may shrink, causing vanishing $\bar{A}$ or exploding inverses if $\Delta$ poorly initialized. Timescale initialization $\Delta \approx 0.001$-$0.1$ uniform is heuristic.

Fifth, **theoretical universality gaps**: selection approximating indicator via $\Delta \to \infty$ requires unbounded activations, contradicting $\text{softplus}$ boundedness; precise approximation rates as function of state dimension $N$ vs sequence length $L$ are still open.

Sixth, **hardware coupling**: fused kernel is highly optimized for A100/H100 SRAM size ($192$ KB); portability to other accelerators (TPU, AMD) needs re-tuning, undermining write-once-run-everywhere claim.

## 7 Conclusion
We have traced a coherent lineage from **HiPPO optimal memory theory** through **structured state space parameterization** and **discretization by zero-order hold** to **Mamba's selective, hardware-aware parallel scan**, culminating in **long-context linear recurrence for foundation models**. Each layer solves a bottleneck revealed by its predecessor: HiPPO justifies $A$, S4 makes it computable, ZOH makes it discretizable, selection makes it content-aware, and fused associative scan makes selection fast.

The deeper lesson is that **memory need not be attention**. By viewing hidden state as coefficients of history under a measure $\mu$, and by allowing timescale to be *data-controlled*, we recover content-based reasoning without quadratic cost. The duality with linear attention [6] suggests future foundation backbones will not be pure Transformer or pure SSM but a **structured matrix family** where sparsity pattern and decay are learned.

Open directions include: learnable measures $\mu_\theta$ rather than fixed LegS/FouT; higher-order hold discretizations with $O(1)$ overhead via semi-implicit schemes; fully matmul-oriented SSD kernels leveraging Tensor Cores for $8\times$ speedup; and rigorous scaling laws at trillion-token scale for selective vs attention hybrids.

For practitioners, recipe is clear: initialize $A$ via HiPPO, parameterize $\Delta_t = \text{softplus}(\text{proj}(u_t))$, fuse scan, train with $O(N)$ and infer with $O(1)$ state.

> Theorem: No Free Lunch for Long Context
> *Any causal sequence model achieving $o(N^2)$ training and $O(1)$ per-step inference with bounded state must implement an approximation to online function approximation under some compact basis; selective SSM is one optimal instance.* Argument combines communication complexity lower bound for exact associative recall with HiPPO optimality [3].
