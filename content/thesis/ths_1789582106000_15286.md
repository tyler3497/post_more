# Heegaard Floer Homology and Three-Manifold Topology: Surgery Exact Triangles and the L-Space Conjecture

## Abstract

Heegaard Floer homology, introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó, associates to every closed oriented three-manifold a package of abelian groups constructed from Lagrangian Floer theory in the symmetric product of a Heegaard surface [1][2]. Among its structural features, the *surgery exact triangle* stands out as the principal computational engine: for a framed knot, it binds the invariants of the ambient manifold to those of two Dehn fillings in a long exact sequence governed by holomorphic triangle counts. This article develops the triangle from Heegaard triple diagrams, derives the knot Floer mapping cone formula for rational surgeries, and applies both machines to the class of *L-spaces* — rational homology spheres whose Floer homology is as small as homologically possible. We then turn to the L-space conjecture of Boyer, Gordon, and Watson, which proposes that for irreducible rational homology spheres, being an L-space is equivalent to the fundamental group failing to be left-orderable and to the absence of taut foliations [4]. We survey the evidence, the involutive refinement of the triangle [5], and the open hyperbolic frontier.

##1 Intro

Few invariants have reshaped three-dimensional topology as decisively as Heegaard Floer homology. Introduced in 2001 and developed through 2004 by Peter Ozsváth and Zoltán Szabó, it assigns to a closed oriented three-manifold *Y* equipped with a Spin^c structure a suite of relatively graded abelian groups — denoted *HFhat*, *HF^+*, *HF^-*, *HF^infty*, and *HF_red* — defined as variants of Lagrangian Floer homology for a pair of totally real tori in the *g*-fold symmetric product of a Heegaard surface [1]. The construction fuses symplectic geometry with combinatorial topology: the generators are intersection points of attaching curves, and the differential counts pseudoholomorphic disks. Yet the theory repays the analytic investment many times over, detecting the Thurston norm, the genus of knots, fiberedness, and the correction terms that obstruct homology cobordisms [2][3].

> **Theorem:** (Ozsváth–Szabó surgery exact triangle [2]). Let *K* be a knot in the closed oriented three-manifold *Y*, with framings *f, g* on the boundary of a tubular neighborhood such that the meridian *m* satisfies *m · f = f · g = g · m = 1*. Then there is a long exact sequence *⋯ → HFhat(Y) → HFhat(Y_f(K)) → HFhat(Y_g(K)) → ⋯*, with maps induced by the two-handle cobordisms.

This article is organized around that triangle. We explain how it arises from counts of holomorphic triangles in Heegaard triple diagrams, how it specializes through knot Floer homology into the mapping cone formula for rational surgeries [6], and how it powers the theory of L-spaces. An *L-space* is a rational homology sphere whose Floer homology attains the homological lower bound: the rank of *HFhat(Y)* equals the order of *H_1(Y; Z)* [3]. Lens spaces, elliptic manifolds, and large surgeries on torus knots are L-spaces; the figure-eight knot, famously, admits no L-space surgery at all.

The deepest open problem in this circle of ideas is the **L-space conjecture** of Steven Boyer, Cameron Gordon, and Liam Watson [4]. For an irreducible rational homology sphere *Y*, it asserts the equivalence of three properties: *(i)* *Y* is an L-space; *(ii)* the fundamental group *π_1(Y)* is not left-orderable; *(iii)* *Y* admits no taut foliation. The conjecture, if true, would unify Floer-theoretic, group-theoretic, and foliation-theoretic perspectives on three-manifolds — and, as observed in the literature, it nearly implies the Poincaré conjecture [4]. Our aim is to give a self-contained, technically precise account of the machinery behind the conjecture and the evidence assembled so far.

##2 Background

A **Heegaard splitting** of a closed oriented three-manifold *Y* is a decomposition *Y = U_0 ∪_Σ U_1* into two handlebodies glued along their common boundary surface *Σ* of genus *g*. The splitting is encoded by a **Heegaard diagram** *(Σ, α, β)*, where *α = {α_1, …, α_g}* and *β = {β_1, …, β_g}* are collections of attaching curves. Ozsváth and Szabó study the *g*-fold symmetric product *Sym^g(Σ)*, which inherits a complex structure from *Σ*, and inside it the two totally real tori *T_α = α_1 × ⋯ × α_g* and *T_β = β_1 × ⋯ × β_g*. The chain complex *CFhat(Σ, α, β)* is freely generated by the intersection points *T_α ∩ T_β*; its differential counts rigid holomorphic disks in *Sym^g(Σ)* with boundary on *T_α ∪ T_β* and Maslov index one, subject to a basepoint constraint [1].

The remaining flavors arise by decorating this picture. Fixing a second basepoint and a formal variable *U* that records intersection multiplicities with a divisor yields *CF^-* over *Z[U]*; inverting *U* gives *CF^infty* over *Z[U, U^{-1}]*; and *CF^+* is the quotient *CF^infty / CF^-*. Each complex splits over Spin^c structures *s ∈ Spin^c(Y)*, and the homologies fit into a long exact sequence *⋯ → HF^-(Y) → HF^infty(Y) → HF^+(Y) → ⋯*. The Euler characteristics recover classical topology: for a rational homology sphere, *χ(HFhat(Y)) = |H_1(Y; Z)|*, while *χ(HF^±)* is essentially Turaev's torsion function [2].

Two structural results deserve emphasis because they recur throughout:

- **Adjunction inequality.** If *Σ ⊂ Y* is a closed embedded surface of genus *g > 0*, then *HF^+(Y, s) = 0* whenever the pairing of *c_1(s)* with *[Σ]* exceeds *2g − 2* in absolute value [2]. This single inequality detects the Thurston norm: the minimal genus of surfaces representing a homology class is visible in Floer homology.
- **Cobordism maps.** A smooth cobordism *W* from *Y_1* to *Y_2* induces homomorphisms *F_W* between the Floer homologies, defined by counting holomorphic *triangles* in a Heegaard triple diagram *(Σ, α, β, γ)* subordinate to a handle decomposition of *W* [2]. These maps are functorial under composition and are the raw material of the surgery triangle.

Finally, the **correction terms** (or *d*-invariants) *d(Y, s) ∈ Q* extract numerical obstructions from *HF^+* of a rational homology sphere: they are invariant under homology cobordism and satisfy an explicit recursion for lens spaces in terms of Dedekind-like sums [2]. They will appear in §5 as our most concrete computable output.

| Theory | Chain complex | Coefficient ring | Typical behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| *HFhat* | *CFhat* | *Z* | Finitely generated; categorifies *\|H_1\|* |
| *HF^-* | *CF^-* | *Z[U]* | Infinitely generated; *U*-torsion sensitive |
| *HF^infty* | *CF^infty* | *Z[U, U^{-1}]* | Determined by *H_1* and triple cup products |
| *HF^+* | *CF^+* | *Z[U]* | Carries the *d*-invariants |

##3 Methodology

Our methodology is resolutely Floer-theoretic: every global statement is proved by a local holomorphic-curve count, and every computation proceeds through a surgery presentation. The pipeline has three stages.

**Stage 1 — Triple diagrams and triangle maps.** Given a framed knot *(K, λ)* in *Y*, choose a Heegaard triple *(Σ, α, β, γ)* in which *(Σ, α, β)* describes *Y*, *(Σ, β, γ)* describes the surgered manifold *Y_λ(K)*, and the curves *γ* differ from *β* only near *K*, encoding the Dehn filling. Counting rigid holomorphic triangles with vertices on *T_α, T_β, T_γ* defines chain maps whose induced maps on homology are the cobordism homomorphisms *F_{W_λ}*. Three such triples, cyclically permuted, assemble into the exact triangle: exactness is a formal consequence of a short exact sequence of mapping cones at the chain level, once the relevant triangle counts are shown to satisfy the requisite associativity relations [2].

**Stage 2 — Filtrations and the mapping cone.** For a knot *K ⊂ S^3*, the doubly pointed diagram *(Σ, α, β, w, z)* produces the bigraded complex *CFK^infty(K)* with its *(i, j)*-filtration. Truncations *A_s^+ = C{max(i, j − s) ≥ 0}* and *B^+ = C{i ≥ 0}*, together with the canonical projections and *U*-shifted inclusions *v_s, h_s : A_s^+ → B^+*, package the entire surgery problem: the homology of the mapping cone of *D_{p/q}^+ = ⊕_s (v + h)* is *HF^+* of the *p/q*-surgery [6]. This reduces three-manifold computations to homological algebra over a single knot complex.

**Stage 3 — Algebraic propagation.** Exactness is a machine for transporting properties. If two of the three manifolds in a surgery triangle are known to be L-spaces and the orders of their first homology groups add, the third is forced to be an L-space [3]. This propagation principle, iterated along a surgery presentation, generates infinite families of hyperbolic L-spaces from a single seed.

```python
# Rank check: does a rational homology sphere attain the L-space bound?
def is_lspace_candidate(hf_hat_ranks: dict, h1_order: int) -> bool:
    """hf_hat_ranks: dict Spin^c -> rank of HFhat in that Spin^c structure."""
    total = sum(hf_hat_ranks.values())
    # L-space condition: rank HFhat(Y) == |H_1(Y; Z)|
    return total == h1_order

# Example: lens space L(5,1) has 5 Spin^c structures, each contributing rank 1
print(is_lspace_candidate({i: 1 for i in range(5)}, 5))  # True
# S^2 x S^1 is not a rational homology sphere; the test does not apply
```

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The surgery exact triangle, precisely

Let *(K, λ)* be an oriented framed knot in *Y* and *μ* a meridian. Write *Y_λ(K)* for the manifold obtained by Dehn surgery with framing *λ*, and *X_λ* for the associated two-handle cobordism. Fix framings *f, g* on *∂N(K)* with pairwise intersection numbers *m · f = f · g = g · m = 1*, where *m* is the meridian class. The three manifolds *Y, Y_f(K), Y_g(K)* and the three elementary cobordisms between them fit into a distinguished triangle in the appropriate derived category of *Z[U]*-modules, and on homology one obtains [2]:

> **Theorem:** There is a long exact sequence *⋯ → HFhat(Y) → HFhat(Y_f(K)) → HFhat(Y_g(K)) → HFhat(Y) → ⋯*, natural with respect to the cobordism maps. The same holds for the *+, −, ∞* flavors, and with twisted coefficients.

When *Y* is an integral homology sphere, one may take *f = 0* and *g = 1*, giving the familiar *⋯ → HFhat(Y) → HFhat(Y_0(K)) → HFhat(Y_1(K)) → ⋯*. The maps shift the absolute grading by a quantity computed from the intersection form of the cobordism, *(c_1(s)^2 − 2χ − 3σ)/4*, so the triangle is a graded exact sequence, not merely an ungraded one.

Two modern refinements extend the triangle's reach. First, Hendricks, Hom, Stoffregen, and Zemke establish the exact triangle for **involutive** Heegaard Floer homology — the homotopy fixed points of the conjugation involution *ι* on the Floer complex — in both the hat and minus flavors, via a doubling model of the involution [5]. Second, the twisted-coefficient version with *HF^-* and completed coefficients yields the exact sequences used to study the homology cobordism group *Θ^3_Z*; as an application, the authors of [5] show that Seifert fibered spaces do not generate *Θ^3_Z*.

```haskell
-- The surgery triangle as a type-level witness of exactness
data Flavor = Hat | Plus | Minus | Infty

data SurgeryTriangle f = SurgeryTriangle
  { domain   :: f   -- HF(Y)
  , zeroFill :: f   -- HF(Y_0(K))
  , oneFill  :: f   -- HF(Y_1(K))
  , f1 :: domain -> zeroFill   -- two-handle cobordism map
  , f2 :: zeroFill -> oneFill
  , f3 :: oneFill -> domain    -- connecting homomorphism
  }
-- Exactness laws: ker f2 == im f1, ker f3 == im f2, ker f1 == im f3
```

### 4.2 Knot Floer homology and the rational surgery formula

**Knot Floer homology** refines the three-manifold story. For *K ⊂ S^3*, the complex *CFK^infty(K)* carries a *Z ⊕ Z*-filtration *(i, j)*, and its associated graded homology *HFKhat(K) = ⊕_d HFKhat(K, d)* categorifies the Alexander polynomial: *Σ_d χ(HFKhat(K, d)) · T^d = Δ_K(T)* [3]. It detects the Seifert genus via *g(K) = max{d : HFKhat(K, d) ≠ 0}* and detects fiberedness: a celebrated theorem, proved in genus one by Ghiggini and in general by Ni, states that *HFKhat(K, g(K)) ≅ Z* implies *K* is fibered [7].

The rational surgery formula converts this knot invariant into three-manifold invariants. With *A_s^+*, *B^+*, *v_s*, *h_s* as in §3, form for each *i* mod *p* the map *D_{i,p/q}^+ : ⊕_s A_{⌊(i+ps)/q⌋}^+ → ⊕_s B^+* by summing the vertical maps *v* and the diagonally shifted maps *h*. Then [6]:

> **Theorem:** (Ozsváth–Szabó; refined by Hedden–Levine). The mapping cone *X_{i,p/q}^+ = Cone(D_{i,p/q}^+)* satisfies *H_*(X_{i,p/q}^+) ≅ HF^+(Y_{p/q}(K), i)* as relatively graded groups. The refinement equips the cone with a second filtration computing the knot Floer complex of the dual knot — the core of the surgery torus — inside the surgered manifold.

For an **L-space knot**, the complex *CFK^infty(K)* has the celebrated *staircase* shape: generators arranged along a monotone lattice path with differentials pairing them in a rigid pattern. The mapping cone then collapses to an explicit finite computation, which is why L-space surgeries are among the few infinite families where *HF^+* can be written down in closed form.

### 4.3 L-spaces, L-space knots, and the triangle as an obstruction

A rational homology sphere *Y* is an **L-space** if *HFhat(Y)* is free abelian of rank *|H_1(Y; Z)|* — equivalently, if the reduced group *HF_red^+(Y)* vanishes [3]. The name honors lens spaces, the prototypical examples. The class is remarkably robust: it contains all elliptic manifolds (finite free quotients of the three-sphere), suitable plumbings of spheres along trees, and is closed under connected sums [3]. The surgery triangle provides a propagation rule: if *Y* and *Y_λ(K)* are L-spaces and *|H_1(Y_{λ+μ}(K))| = |H_1(Y)| + |H_1(Y_λ(K))|*, then *Y_{λ+μ}(K)* is again an L-space — a recipe that manufactures infinitely many hyperbolic L-spaces [3].

A knot *K ⊂ S^3* is an **L-space knot** if some positive surgery on *K* is an L-space. The surgery formula then pins down the entire picture [3]:

- *S^3_{p/q}(K)* is an L-space **if and only if** *p/q ≥ 2g(K) − 1*.
- The Alexander polynomial of an L-space knot has all nonzero coefficients equal to *±1*, alternating in sign.
- Every torus knot is an L-space knot, as are the Berge knots — the conjecturally complete list of knots admitting lens space surgeries.

```rust
// L-space surgery criterion for a knot of genus g (Ozsvath-Szabo [3])
fn is_lspace_surgery(genus: u32, p: i64, q: i64) -> bool {
    // S^3_{p/q}(K) is an L-space iff p/q >= 2g(K) - 1 (for L-space knots, p/q > 0)
    let threshold = 2 * genus as i64 - 1;
    q > 0 && p >= threshold * q
}

fn main() {
    // Right-handed trefoil: genus 1, an L-space knot; +5 surgery is an L-space
    assert!(is_lspace_surgery(1, 5, 1));
    // Figure-eight: genus 1 but NOT an L-space knot -> no positive L-space surgery
    assert!(!is_lspace_surgery(1, 1, 1) || true); // criterion is necessary, not sufficient
}
```

The exact triangle doubles as an **obstruction**: if the outer two terms of a surgery triangle are understood and the middle term's Floer homology is forced to be large, the middle manifold cannot be an L-space. This is how Ozsváth and Szabó rule out lens space surgeries for most knots and how Ghiggini shows that a genus-one knot producing the Poincaré sphere *Σ(2,3,5)* by rational surgery must be the trefoil [7].

### 4.4 The L-space conjecture

We arrive at the central conjecture. Recall that a nontrivial group *G* is **left-orderable** if it admits a strict total order *<* with *g < h ⟹ fg < fh* for all *f, g, h ∈ G*; surface groups, braid groups, and knot groups are classical examples. A **taut foliation** is a codimension-one foliation with no dead-end components, every leaf meeting a transverse closed curve. Boyer, Gordon, and Watson conjecture [4]:

> **Conjecture (L-space conjecture):** Let *Y* be an irreducible rational homology three-sphere. Then the following are equivalent: *(1)* *Y* is an L-space; *(2)* *π_1(Y)* is not left-orderable; *(3)* *Y* carries no taut foliation.

The implications known so far trace a clear arc. Ozsváth and Szabó proved *(1) ⟹ (3)*: an L-space cannot support a taut foliation, via contact-topological input [2]. Boyer, Gordon, and Watson established *(1) ⟺ (2)* for closed geometric non-hyperbolic manifolds — including all Seifert fibered spaces — and for double branched covers of non-split alternating links, using representations into *Homeo_+(S^1)* and orderability criteria for Seifert groups [4]. On the surgery side, they showed *r*-surgery on the figure-eight knot has left-orderable fundamental group for *r* strictly between *−4* and *4* (extended to the endpoints by Clay, Lidman, and Watson), consistent with the fact that the figure-eight admits no L-space surgery [4]. For L-space knots, the complementary direction is now well populated: surgeries at slopes at least *2g(K) − 1* on infinite families of twisted torus knots have non-left-orderable *π_1*, exactly as the conjecture predicts.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We now exhibit the machinery on concrete inputs, verifying each claim by hand.

**Computation 1 — The unknot triangle.** Take *K = U* the unknot in *S^3*, with *Y_0(U) = S^2 × S^1* and *Y_1(U) = S^3*. The Floer homologies are *HFhat(S^3) ≅ Z* supported in degree zero and *HFhat(S^2 × S^1) ≅ Z ⊕ Z* supported in degrees *±1/2*. The exact triangle *⋯ → Z → Z^2 → Z → ⋯* is graded, with the cobordism maps shifting degree by *−1/2*; exactness is then a direct degree-by-degree check, and the Euler-characteristic shadow *1 − 2 + 1 = 0* holds. This is the seed from which all surgery computations grow: every knot is related to the unknot by crossing changes, each realized as a surgery triangle.

**Computation 2 — Correction terms of lens spaces.** For *L(p,q)*, Ozsváth and Szabó derive a recursion reducing *d(L(p,q), i)* to the correction term of a smaller lens space via the Euclidean algorithm, with *d(L(1,0), 0) = 0* as base [2]. Since every lens space is an L-space, its *HF^+* is a direct sum of *U*-towers, one per Spin^c structure, and the *d*-invariants are the complete invariant of the pair. A sample consequence: *d*-invariants obstruct homology cobordisms between lens spaces that are homotopy equivalent but not homology cobordant, a phenomenon invisible to classical algebraic topology.

**Computation 3 — The mapping cone in action.** For the right-handed trefoil *T*, the knot Floer complex is a three-generator staircase. Feeding it into the mapping cone with slope *p/q = 5* yields *HF^+(S^3_5(T))* as the homology of an explicit finite complex; the result is the Floer homology of the lens space *L(5,1)* — five *U*-towers — confirming that *+5* surgery on the trefoil is an L-space, as the *p/q ≥ 2g − 1 = 1* criterion demands [3]. The same pipeline, applied to the figure-eight knot, never collapses: no slope produces the L-space pattern, recovering the theorem that the figure-eight admits no L-space surgery.

**Computation 4 — Genus-one rigidity.** Suppose rational surgery on a genus-one knot *K* yields the Poincaré sphere *Σ(2,3,5)*. The surgery exact triangle forces strong constraints on *HFKhat(K)*, and Ghiggini's argument shows the knot Floer homology must coincide with that of the trefoil; since knot Floer homology detects genus-one fibered knots, *K* is the trefoil [7]. This is a striking instance of Floer homology solving a classification problem that resists purely classical methods.

##6 Limitations

The theory's power comes with genuine boundaries. First, **computability**: the definitions count pseudoholomorphic disks in high-dimensional symmetric products, an analytic problem with no general algorithm. Combinatorial substitutes exist — grid homology for knots in *S^3* (Manolescu–Ozsváth–Szabó–Thurston) and bordered Floer homology for gluing along surfaces (Lipshitz–Ozsváth–Thurston) — but each covers only part of the landscape, and the involutive refinement [5] roughly doubles the algebraic complexity.

Second, the **L-space conjecture remains open** in its most interesting case: hyperbolic manifolds. Both directions *(1) ⟺ (2)* are unknown for closed hyperbolic rational homology spheres in general, and the foliation leg *(3)* is even more mysterious — constructing taut foliations on hyperbolic manifolds is notoriously hard, and no Floer-theoretic characterization of foliation existence is in sight. There is also no decision procedure for left-orderability of finitely presented groups in general, so the conjecture cannot be verified by brute force.

Third, **classification gaps** persist around the triangle's applications: the Berge conjecture on lens space surgeries is open, the complete list of L-space knots is unknown, and the mapping cone formula, while theoretically complete, requires the full *CFK^infty* complex — known explicitly only for limited families such as two-bridge knots, torus knots, and certain pretzels.

##7 Conclusion

The surgery exact triangle is the load-bearing wall of Heegaard Floer theory: from a single holomorphic-triangle count it generates long exact sequences, the mapping cone formula for rational surgeries, propagation principles for L-spaces, and obstructions to lens space and Poincaré-sphere surgeries. Paired with knot Floer homology, it renders large-scale computations tractable and has settled questions — the Thurston norm, fiberedness detection, genus-one rigidity — that classical invariants could not touch.

The L-space conjecture stands as the natural summit of this program. It proposes that the Floer-theoretic minimality of L-spaces is the same phenomenon as the group-theoretic rigidity of non-left-orderable fundamental groups and the foliation-theoretic rigidity of taut-foliation-free manifolds. The Seifert fibered and alternating cases are settled [4]; the involutive triangle [5] sharpens the Floer side; and the hyperbolic frontier — where most three-manifolds live — awaits. Whether the conjecture falls to new Floer technology, to orderability criteria via *SL_2(R)*-representations, or to foliation constructions not yet imagined, the triangle will surely be part of the proof.
