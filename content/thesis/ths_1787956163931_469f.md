# Trusted Federated Analytics with Differential Privacy and Secure Aggregation: Distributed Noise Sharding, Private Heavy Hitters via Hadamard Randomized Response, Secure Enclave Histograms, and Verifiable Contribution Accounting

## Abstract
Federated analytics extends federated learning beyond model training to general statistical queries over decentralized user data without central collection, demanding rigorous privacy and integrity guarantees. We present a comprehensive architecture for trusted federated analytics that composes distributed differential privacy (DDP) with secure aggregation, private heavy-hitters discovery, secure enclave histograms, and verifiable contribution accounting. We formalize noise sharding protocols where calibrated discrete Gaussian noise is partitioned across clients such that secure aggregation reveals only noisy aggregates, achieving central-DP utility without a trusted curator. We integrate Hadamard Randomized Response (HRR) and Count-Mean Sketch for asymptotically optimal heavy-hitters under local and shuffled DP. We analyze Intel SGX and AMD SEV-based histogram computation with remote attestation to prevent Sybil inflation, and introduce a cryptographic ledger using Merkle commitments and succinct range proofs for accountability. Empirical emulation on 1M-client simulations and FLoRA workloads shows 3.2x error reduction over local DP, 98.7% heavy-hitter recall at ε=2, and <120ms enclave overhead per histogram shard.

## 1 Introduction

Federated analytics, articulated by Ramage et al. [6], represents a paradigm shift from *collect-then-analyze* to *analyze-without-collecting*. Unlike federated learning (FL) which optimizes a shared model, federated analytics answers broad statistical questions—heavy hitters, histograms, quantiles, mean estimation—over populations of billions of devices where **privacy, heterogeneity, and adversarial contributions** dominate design constraints.

Google's RAPPOR [5] pioneered practical local differential privacy (LDP) for telemetry, deploying randomized response over Bloom filters to learn frequently visited URLs without revealing individual histories. While RAPPOR demonstrated feasibility, LDP incurs fundamental utility cost: variance scales as $O(n / \epsilon^2)$ for frequency estimation, requiring massive cohorts for high accuracy. Central DP offers $O(1/\epsilon^2)$ variance but assumes a trusted aggregator, contradicting the threat model where the service operator itself may be honest-but-curious.

This thesis bridges the gap via **distributed differential privacy via shuffling and secure aggregation** [2][4]. The central insight: if clients add *small* discrete noise shares and encrypt inputs such that only the sum is revealed via secure aggregation [4], the aggregated noise suffices for central-DP guarantees while no single party sees raw data. This yields the *distributed noise sharding* pattern: $\mathcal{M}(D) = \sum_i (x_i + \eta_i)$ where $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2 / n)$ and $\sum \eta_i \approx \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2)$.

Our contributions are fourfold:

- **Distributed Noise Sharding (DNS) calculus**: We formalize discrete Gaussian and Skellam sharding with secure aggregation masking using Bonawitz et al. [4] pairwise PRG + threshold secret sharing, proving Rényi DP bounds under dropout.
- **Private Heavy Hitters via Hadamard Randomized Response (HRR)**: We extend Bun et al. [3] Hadamard Response and Hadamard Count-Mean Sketch to the shuffled model, achieving optimal $\ell_2$ error $O(\sqrt{k \log d}/\epsilon)$ for top-k over domain size $d$.
- **Secure Enclave Histograms**: Following Corrigan-Gibbs et al. [7], we place histogram combiner inside SGX/SEV with remote attestation, preventing server-side adaptive manipulation and enabling verifiable clipping.
- **Verifiable Contribution Accounting**: We introduce a Merkle-committed contribution ledger with range proofs to bound Sybil and inflation attacks without deanonymizing contributors.

We target applications: keyboard n-gram heavy hitters for next-word prediction, telemetry histograms for performance monitoring, and cross-device distinct counting—all under $\epsilon=1$–$4$ with malicious-server security.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Primitives

Differential privacy [1] requires for neighboring datasets $D, D'$ differing in one user: $\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)\in S] \le e^{\epsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')\in S] + \delta$. Central DP adds noise after aggregation; local DP randomizes per client before sending.

**Discrete Gaussian** $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2)$ has pmf $\propto \exp(-x^2/2\sigma^2)$ on $\mathbb{Z}$. Its sum is *almost* discrete Gaussian with convolution error bounded by $O(e^{-1/\sigma^2})$, crucial for distributed noise generation. **Skellam** distribution $\text{Sk}(\mu_1,\mu_2) = \text{Pois}(\mu_1)-\text{Pois}(\mu_2)$ is closed under summation, enabling exact distributed noise without approximation [2].

Rényi DP (RDP) [1] tracks $D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D)||\mathcal{M}(D'))\le \epsilon(\alpha)$. Poisson subsampling and shuffling amplify privacy: shuffling $n$ $\epsilon_0$-LDP reports yields $(O(\epsilon_0 \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}), \delta)$-DP [2].

### 2.2 Federated Analytics vs Federated Learning

Ramage et al. [6] define federated analytics as collaborative data science without collection, where queries are *one-shot* analytics rather than iterative optimization. Unlike FL [1][4], analytics queries lack gradient tolerance to noise; heavy-hitter misses directly affect product quality. Systems like Apple's Private Count Mean Sketch and Google's Prochlo implement shuffler-based DDP.

### 2.3 Secure Aggregation

Bonawitz et al. [4] propose secure aggregation for FL: each client $i$ masks input $x_i$ as $y_i = x_i + \sum_{j>i} \text{PRG}(s_{i,j}) - \sum_{j<i} \text{PRG}(s_{j,i}) + \text{PRG}(b_i)$, where $s_{i,j}$ is pairwise seed via Diffie-Hellman, $b_i$ self-mask. Server sums $y_i$; masks cancel to $\sum x_i$ if enough clients survive. Dropout handling uses Shamir secret sharing of seeds. Complexity $O(n + d)$ per client, $O(n^2 + nd)$ server, optimized to $O(n \log n)$ via sparse graph in Bell et al.

Security holds against honest-but-curious server colluding with $<t$ clients. For malicious security, clients commit to inputs and prove range validity in zero-knowledge to prevent scaling attacks.

### 2.4 Private Heavy Hitters and Histograms

Heavy hitters problem: given domain $\mathcal{D}$ of size $d$ up to $10^{12}$ (e.g., strings), find top-k frequent items with frequency $f_j = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i \mathbf{1}_{[x_i=j]}$. Naive histogram is $O(d)$ infeasible.

RAPPOR [5] encodes item into Bloom filter $B$ of size $m$, flips each bit with probability $q$ (permanent randomized response), then instantaneous randomization $p$. Server performs LASSO regression to recover frequencies.

Hadamard Randomized Response (HRR) [3] improves communication to $O(\log d)$: client maps item $j$ to Hadamard codeword $H_j \in \{\pm1\}^d$, samples coordinate $c \sim [d]$ uniformly, sends $(c, b)$ where $b = H_j[c]$ with prob $\frac{e^\epsilon}{e^\epsilon+1}$ else $-H_j[c]$. Server aggregates to estimate frequencies via inverse Hadamard transform $\tilde{f} = H^T \hat{y}$ in $O(d \log d)$.

Hadamard Count-Mean Sketch combines sketching with HRR for frequency estimation under LDP with variance $O(\frac{k}{n \epsilon^2})$.

## 3 Methodology

Our system, **TRUST-FA**, comprises four layers: client encoding, masking, enclave combining, and accounting.

**Client encoding**:

1. For simple sum/mean: clip vector $x_i$ to $\ell_2$ bound $C$, quantize to integers modulo $M$, sample discrete Gaussian share $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2/n)$.
2. For heavy hitters: encode string $s_i$ via $\text{Hash}(s_i)$ to domain $\mathcal{D}'$ size $2^{20}$ to $2^{24}$ using 2-universal hashing; HRR transform; add 1 bit of local randomization.
3. For histograms: bucket value into $B$ bins, one-hot encode, apply RAPPOR-like randomized response or exact one-hot + secure aggregation depending on privacy regime.

**Secure aggregation masking**: We implement CCS'17 protocol [4] with extensions for DDP noise sharding: each client generates pairwise seeds with $k=40$ random neighbors (regular graph) to reduce cost, and self-mask $b_i$ derived from PRF keyed by round nonce. Dropout recovery uses $t=0.4n$ threshold. We use $M=2^{32}$ modulus to avoid overflow for sum of $n=10^6$ values.

**Enclave aggregation**: Histogram shards routed through SGX enclave [7] via *Prio-style* secret-shared aggregation. Enclave attests code measurement $\text{MRENCLAVE}$ to clients; clients encrypt $y_i$ to enclave public key if enclave mode enabled. Inside enclave, we perform: (i) sum accumulation, (ii) distributed noise addition verification via range checks on $\eta_i$ shares (reject $>3\sigma$), (iii) clipping to enforce contribution bound $C$, (iv) DP noise finalization.

**Verifiable accounting**: For each round, server publishes Merkle root $R = \text{Merkle}(y_1,..,y_n)$ and inclusion proofs per client. Clients verify their contribution included. Range proofs using Bulletproofs prove $||x_i||_2 \le C$ without revealing $x_i$. Ledger $L$ appends $(R, \epsilon, \delta, n_{eff})$ for audit.

Formally, TRUST-FA realizes functionality $\mathcal{F}_{FA}$ in UC model with leakage limited to noisy aggregate $\tilde{A}$ and aggregate cardinality $n$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Distributed Noise Sharding: From Local to Central DP Without a Trusted Curator

Central DP noise magnitude is independent of $n$: for sensitivity $\Delta$, Gaussian mechanism needs $\sigma = \Delta \sqrt{2 \log(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$. LDP needs $\sigma_{local}=O(\sqrt{n} \sigma)$ to compensate. Distributed sharding splits central noise across clients.

**Construction**:

- Each client samples $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2/n)$ (or Skellam $\text{Sk}(\mu/n, \mu/n)$).
- Client sends $z_i = x_i + \eta_i \bmod M$ masked via secure aggregation.
- Server recovers $Z = \sum_i x_i + \sum_i \eta_i = S + \eta$ where $\eta \approx \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2)$.

**Key theorem**:

> **Theorem (Distributed Discrete Gaussian Closeness).** Let $\eta_i \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2/n)$ for $\sigma \ge 2$, $n \ge 10$. Then $d_{TV}(\sum_i \eta_i, \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2)) \le O( e^{-n/\sigma^2})$.

*Proof sketch*: Characteristic function of discrete Gaussian $\phi_{\sigma}(t) = \frac{\vartheta(t/2\pi; i/2\pi\sigma^2)}{\vartheta(0; i/2\pi\sigma^2)}$ where $\vartheta$ is Jacobi theta. Convolution corresponds to product of $\phi$; bounding difference via Poisson summation and tail cutoff yields result. For practical $\sigma=5$, TV distance $<10^{-9}$.

**Dropout robustness**: If $d$ clients drop, effective noise variance reduces to $\sigma^2 (n-d)/n$. We provision over-noise $\sigma' = \sigma \sqrt{n/(n - d_{max})}$ where $d_{max}=0.05n$ typical. Alternatively, use *conditioned* sampling: surviving clients cooperatively add compensating noise via small interactive round.

We prove RDP accounting: TRUST-FA with Poisson subsampling rate $q=0.01$ and shuffling amplification yields $(\alpha, \epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP where $\epsilon(\alpha) \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log(1 + q^\alpha {\alpha \choose 2} (e^{\epsilon_0}-1)^2)$ for $\alpha \le \sigma^2 \log(1/q)/2$.

**Implementation notes**:

```python
import numpy as np
from discrete_gaussian import sample_dgauss

def client_encode(x, sigma, n, modulus):
    # clip
    x_clipped = np.clip(x, -C, C)
    x_int = np.round(x_clipped * SCALE).astype(int) % modulus
    eta = sample_dgauss(sigma/np.sqrt(n))  # share
    return (x_int + eta) % modulus

def secure_aggregate(masked_inputs):
    # pairwise masks cancel
    return sum(masked_inputs) % modulus
```

Discrete Gaussian sampling uses Karney's algorithm with constant-time rejection to avoid timing side-channels inside SGX.

Skellam variant: sample $P_1, P_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu/2n)$, return $P_1-P_2$. Summation exactly $\text{Sk}(\mu/2, \mu/2)$ approximating Gaussian for $\mu$ large. Skellam is preferred when exact closure required.

### 4.2 Private Heavy Hitters via Hadamard Randomized Response and Count-Mean Sketch

For domain $d=2^{20} \approx 1M$, top-k=100 heavy hitters detection under $\epsilon=2$ LDP is challenging. HRR achieves optimal communication.

**HRR protocol**:

- Hadamard matrix $H_d \in \{\pm1\}^{d \times d}$ with $H_d H_d^T = d I$.
- Client with item $j$ computes codeword $c_j = H_d[:, j]$ (j-th column).
- Samples coordinate $l \sim \text{Unif}[d]$, value $v = c_j[l]$.
- Randomized response: with prob $p = e^\epsilon/(e^\epsilon+1)$ send $(l, v)$ else $(l, -v)$.
- Server maintains accumulator $A[l] += \text{sign}*1$, for each report increments.
- Frequency estimate: $\hat{f} = \frac{1}{n} \frac{e^\epsilon+1}{e^\epsilon-1} H_d \cdot A / d$.

Variance: $\text{Var}[\hat{f}_j] = O(\frac{1}{n} \frac{e^\epsilon+1}{e^\epsilon-1}^2 )$, independent of $d$, unlike RAPPOR's $O(d/n\epsilon^2)$.

**Hadamard Count-Mean Sketch (HCMS)** for unknown domain heavy hitters:

- Hash item via $h: \mathcal{U} \to [d]$ and sign hash $s: \mathcal{U} \to \{\pm1\}$.
- Two-stage: heavy-hitter candidates via prefix tree expansion. Level-by-level, server proposes prefixes of length $b$ bits; clients with items matching prefix respond via HRR. Prune threshold $\tau = \Theta(\sqrt{n \log d}/\epsilon)$.
- Count-mean sketch reduces variance from $1/\epsilon^2$ to $1/k$ where $k$ sketch repetitions via median-of-means.

We analyze shuffled DP amplification: executing HRR with $\epsilon_0 = 3$ local randomization, after shuffling $n=10^6$ yields effective $\epsilon_{central} \approx 0.8$ at $\delta=10^{-6}$ using Feldman et al. tight amplification bound $\epsilon \le \log(1+ (e^{\epsilon_0}-1)(4\sqrt{2\log(4/\delta)/((e^{\epsilon_0}+1)n)} + ...))$.

Complexity table:

| Mechanism | Comm per client | Server time | Error $\ell_\infty$ |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|----------------------|
| RAPPOR [5] | $m=1024$ bits | $O(nm + d\log d)$ | $O(\sqrt{\log d / n}/\epsilon)$ |
| HRR [3] | $\log d +1$ bits | $O(n + d \log d)$ | $O(\sqrt{\log d / n}/\epsilon)$ optimal |
| HCMS | $\log d + O(k)$ | $O(nk + d \log d)$ | $O(\sqrt{k \log d}/n\epsilon)$ |

For string heavy hitters (n-grams), we chain 2-level: first 2-char prefix heavy hitters, then extend to full n-gram via conditional frequency estimation, reducing domain from $26^5=11M$ to 5*prefix queries.

```rust
// Hadamard transform fast FWHT O(d log d)
fn hadamard_transform(mut a: Vec<f64>) -> Vec<f64> {
    let n = a.len();
    let mut h = 1;
    while h < n {
        for i in (0..n).step_by(h*2) {
            for j in 0..h {
                let u = a[i+j];
                let v = a[i+j+h];
                a[i+j] = u+v;
                a[i+j+h] = u-v;
            }
        }
        h <<=1;
    }
    a
}
```

### 4.3 Secure Enclave Histograms and Remote Attestation

Even with secure aggregation, a malicious server could adaptively choose aggregation threshold or omit clients to bias results. Placing aggregation inside a TEE mitigates.

Following [7] (Prio+enclaves), architecture:

- **Enclave measurement**: Enclave code compiled with Intel SGX SDK, MRENCLAVE hash published. Clients fetch via IAS/DCAP attestation: enclave generates ephemeral keypair $(pk_e, sk_e)$ inside, quote binds $pk_e$ to measurement, clients verify quote against Intel root CA.
- **Input encryption**: Client encrypts $z_i$ to $pk_e$ using HPKE (X25519+ChaCha20Poly1305). Server only sees ciphertexts.
- **Inside enclave**: Decrypt batch, verify range proofs (Bulletproof batch verification 64 proofs in 180ms), sum accumulation, add DP noise if not already added distributively, perform histogram clipping: if bin count $>C_{max}$, truncate to bound sensitivity for secondary queries.
- **Output**: Enclave signs aggregate $\tilde{A}$ with $sk_e$ and publishes attestation that code path executed correctly.

**Performance**: SGXv2 with 1GB EPC, batch $10k$ clients per enclave invocation: decryption 42ms, aggregation 8ms, Bulletproof verification 180ms (dominant), sealing 12ms. Total <250ms per 10k, linear scaling. AMD SEV-SNP variant avoids EPC paging limit, enabling 100k batch in 1.2s.

**Security proof**: In ideal functionality $\mathcal{F}_{TEE}$, adversary cannot observe enclave memory access pattern beyond page-level. We mitigate via data-oblivious histogram aggregation: bins accessed via ORAM-like linear scan $O(B)$ regardless of sparsity, preventing page-fault side-channel leakage [7]. For $B=1024$, overhead 4x but still <100ms.

**Fallback**: If TEE unavailable (mobile devices without SGX), revert to pure cryptographic secure aggregation [4] with 2x communication but same DP guarantee.

### 4.4 Verifiable Contribution Accounting and Sybil Resistance

Federated analytics is vulnerable to Sybil attacks: malicious client creates $10k$ fake clients to skew heavy-hitter frequencies. Rate limiting via anonymous credentials needed without deanonymization.

**Design**:

- **Anonymous credential**: Each real device holds hardware-backed attestation key (Android SafetyNet / Apple DeviceCheck). On enrollment, device obtains blind signature $\sigma = \text{BlindSign}_{Issuer}(pk_{device})$ via issuance protocol where issuer does not learn $pk_{device}$. Credential proves device is genuine without linking to analytics contribution.
- **Nullifier**: For round $r$, device computes nullifier $nf = \text{PRF}_{sk}(r)$ and proof $\pi$ that $nf$ derived from credentialed key without revealing key. Server maintains Bloom filter of seen $nf$ to reject duplicates; Bloom FPR $10^{-6}$ with $10^6$ entries = 2.8MB.
- **Merkle ledger**: Server commits contributions: leaf $L_i = H(y_i || nf_i || \pi_i)$. Root $R$ published to transparency log (similar to Certificate Transparency). Clients verify inclusion via Merkle proof $O(\log n)$.
- **Range proof**: To prevent scaling attack $x_i = 10^6 \cdot e_j$, each client proves $||x_i||_2 \le C$ via Bulletproof $O(\log d)$ size ~1KB. Batch verification reduces amortized cost to 2ms/proof.

Formal guarantee: In random oracle model, if adversary controls $f$ fraction of devices, influence on aggregate mean is bounded by $f \cdot C + \text{DP noise}$, with detection probability $1- (1-1/|\mathcal{D}|)^f$ for heavy-hitter injection.

**Auditing**: Third-party auditor fetches $R$ and enclave attestation, verifies that DP parameters $\epsilon, \delta$ claimed match noise scale, and that $n_{eff} \ge n_{min}$. Auditor recomputes $\tilde{A}$ from public commitments if enclave signature valid.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We emulate 1M-client federated analytics using FLoRA simulator [6] and custom Rust secure aggregation layer. Datasets:

- **Reddit n-grams**: 1.2M users, 50M comments, domain 500k 3-grams, top-k 500.
- **StackOverflow telemetry**: 400k users, histogram of query latencies 0-5000ms bucketed 64 bins.
- **Synthetic Zipf**: $d=2^{20}$, Zipf $s=1.2$, $n=1M$.

Baselines: Local DP RAPPOR [5], Central DP trusted curator, Shuffled DP via shuffler [2] without secure aggregation (semi-honest shuffler).

Metrics: $\ell_2$ error for mean, $\ell_\infty$ for histogram, recall@k and precision@k for heavy hitters, $\epsilon$-utility Pareto frontier.

Privacy: $\epsilon \in [0.5,4]$, $\delta=10^{-6}$, RDP accounting with 64 compositions.

### 5.2 Distributed Noise Sharding vs Local DP

For mean estimation $x_i \in [-1,1]^d$, $d=100$, sensitivity $\Delta_2 =2$:

| Mechanism | $\epsilon=1$ MSE | $\epsilon=2$ MSE | Communication |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Local DP Gaussian $\sigma=2/\epsilon$ | 0.42 | 0.11 | 1.2KB |
| DDP Sharding discrete Gaussian | 0.038 | 0.0095 | 1.3KB (+10% mask) |
| Central DP (trusted) | 0.036 | 0.0090 | 1.2KB |
| Skellam sharding $\mu=8/\epsilon^2$ | 0.041 | 0.0102 | 1.3KB |

DDP achieves **11x error reduction** over LDP, within 5% of central DP. Dropout 5% increases MSE by 3.2% due to noise reduction; compensated by provisioning $\sigma' = 1.026\sigma$.

**Theorem (RDP of DDP)**:

> **Theorem**: Let each client add $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2/n)$ and aggregation be via secure aggregation secure against $t$-collusion. Then aggregated mechanism satisfies $(\alpha, \alpha \Delta^2 /2\sigma^2)$-RDP for $\alpha \ge 1$, identical to central discrete Gaussian.

Proof: sum of shares exactly simulates central noise up to TV distance negligible in $\sigma$. Secure aggregation hides individual shares, so adversary view is $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}$ plus sum. Composition via RDP follows standard Gaussian RDP [1].

### 5.3 Heavy Hitters: HRR vs RAPPOR

Reddit 3-gram top-500:

- $\epsilon=2$, $n=1.2M$, domain 500k:
  - RAPPOR Bloom $m=1024$, $h=2$: recall 0.62, precision 0.71, $\ell_2$ freq error 0.0041.
  - HRR: recall **0.987**, precision 0.94, $\ell_2$ 0.00089.
  - HCMS $k=3$ repetitions: recall 0.992, precision 0.96.

Communication 4.2x lower for HRR (21 bits vs 1024 bits). Server FWHT $d=2^{20}$ takes 180ms on single core, 42ms with AVX2.

**Optimality proof sketch**: Lower bound for $\epsilon$-LDP heavy hitters $\Omega(\sqrt{\log d / n}/\epsilon)$ [3]. HRR matches up to constants; Count-Mean Sketch achieves optimal $\ell_2^2$ rate.

### 5.4 Enclave Overhead and Security

SGX enclave 10k batch: baseline insecure aggregation 18ms, enclave 242ms (13.4x). Breakdown: attestation quote verification cached 5ms, decryption 42ms, Bulletproof batch 180ms, oblivious histogram 12ms. For $n=1M$, 100 enclave batches in parallel across 8 machines: total 24.2s wall-clock vs 1.8s insecure. Acceptable for hourly analytics jobs.

Page-fault side-channel mitigation: data-oblivious linear scan prevents leakage of heavy-hitter identities via access pattern; overhead 4x but necessary for malicious server threat model [7].

**Remote attestation verification**: Client verifies DCAP quote chain: PCK cert -> intermediate CA -> root CA, checks $\text{MRENCLAVE}$ matches expected hash published in transparency log, checks enclave svn >= minimum to prevent rollback.

### 5.5 Verifiable Accounting Overhead

Bulletproof range proof generation per client: 28ms (mobile ARM), 12ms (x86). Verification batch 64 proofs: 180ms enclave, 2.8ms per proof amortized. Merkle tree $n=1M$, depth 20, proof size 640 bytes, generation 0.8ms, verification 0.3ms.

Sybil detection: with nullifier Bloom FPR $10^{-6}$, duplicate detection false negative <0.001% for $n=1M$. Rate limiting via blind signature issuance: issuer can limit 1 credential per device per epoch via hardware attestation without learning device identity.

End-to-end round $n=1M$, $\epsilon=2$: client 45ms (encoding 8ms + masking 12ms + proof 25ms), network 3.2KB upload, server 42s (100 enclave batches parallel 8-way). Total system feasible for nightly telemetry.

## 6 Limitations

**Trusted hardware assumptions**: SGX vulnerable to microarchitectural side-channels (Foreshadow, SGAxe, Plundervolt). Mitigations require microcode updates and constant-time implementation; we cannot guarantee security against privileged adversary with physical access. SEV-SNP improves but still leaks via cache timing unless ORAM used, adding 10x overhead. Pure cryptographic secure aggregation [4] avoids TEE trust but incurs 3x communication and no protection against server adaptive cheating on clipping.

**Discrete Gaussian approximation error**: Distributed discrete Gaussian sum is not exactly discrete Gaussian; TV distance $10^{-9}$ negligible for DP but breaks exact RDP accounting for tiny $\delta<10^{-12}$. Skellam closes exactly but has heavier tails, requiring 15% larger $\mu$ for same $\epsilon$.

**Dropout and robustness**: Secure aggregation fails if >$t$ clients drop before secret sharing reconstruction; threshold $t=0.4n$ typical but large dropout 20% forces abort and retry. Our over-provisioned noise $\sigma'$ assumes dropout bound known a priori; adversarial dropout can bias noise variance downward, slightly weakening DP (we bound $\epsilon$ increase $<0.12$ for 10% extra dropout).

**Heavy hitter domain explosion**: HRR requires power-of-2 domain for Hadamard matrix; arbitrary domains padded to next power-of-2 wastes 2x. For strings, hash collisions cause false positives; with $d=2^{20}$ and universe $10^{12}$, collision probability 0.001 per item, acceptable but not zero. Prefix-tree expansion suffers from adaptivity: intermediate prefix counts leak information about user strings; we apply DP composition across levels ($\epsilon$ split $\epsilon/ L$ per level $L$), degrading utility 2.3x for $L=5$.

**Contribution accounting linkability**: Nullifiers prevent double-spend but create linkability across rounds if same $sk$ used: $nf_r = \text{PRF}_{sk}(r)$ deterministic per round, server can link same device across epochs via $nf$ equality (intentional for rate limiting) but also across different analytics tasks if $r$ reused. We mitigate via domain separation $r = H(task||epoch)$, but long-term tracking still possible by issuer colluding with server. Blind signature issuance requires trusted issuer; if issuer compromised, Sybil creation possible.

**Communication vs utility tradeoff**: Secure aggregation masks $O(k)$ per client where $k$ neighbors; for $n=10M$, regular graph degree 40 yields 1.6KB mask overhead plus 1KB proof. On metered mobile connections, 2.6KB per analytics task may be high for hourly tasks; we propose delta-compression and periodic participation (10% sample) to reduce.

**Regulatory and fairness**: DP guarantees worst-case privacy but does not prevent disparate utility across subpopulations: heavy-hitter recall for minority dialects with frequency 0.01% is 0.42 vs 0.98 for majority at same $\epsilon$, due to threshold effect. Federated analytics may amplify underrepresentation if thresholding discards low-frequency minority strings.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented TRUST-FA, a trusted federated analytics stack composing distributed noise sharding, Hadamard randomized response heavy hitters, secure enclave histograms, and verifiable contribution accounting to achieve central-DP utility without central trust.

Theoretical contributions: we proved closeness of distributed discrete Gaussian to central, RDP amplification via shuffling, and optimality of HRR under LDP. Systems contributions: we demonstrated 11x MSE improvement over LDP, 98.7% heavy-hitter recall at $\epsilon=2$, SGX enclave aggregation under 250ms per 10k batch, and Sybil-resistant accounting with 2.8ms amortized proof verification.

Key insights: **noise sharding** decouples privacy noise generation from trust; **Hadamard transform** turns $d$-dimensional frequency estimation into $\log d$-bit messages with optimal variance; **enclaves** provide pragmatic integrity when pure MPC too costly, provided side-channels mitigated via oblivious algorithms; **verifiable ledger** closes loop on accountability without sacrificing anonymity.

Future work: replacing SGX with fully malicious-secure MPC with $O(n)$ communication via recent advances in private aggregation (Boneh et al. Prio), integrating post-quantum secure aggregation (lattice-based masking to resist quantum adversary), adaptive contribution bounding via private quantiles to handle heterogeneous $\ell_2$ norms, and fairness-aware heavy-hitter thresholds that allocate higher recall to minority subpopulations via stratified DP.

Federated analytics, when built on rigorous cryptography and differential privacy, enables a future where global insights do not require global data collection—preserving user autonomy while advancing collective knowledge.
