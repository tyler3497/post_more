## Abstract

Cosmological perturbation theory is the precision framework that connects the quantum fluctuations of the inflationary era to the observed anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and the large-scale distribution of galaxies. This thesis develops the complete gauge-invariant formalism initiated by Bardeen: the Stewart–Walker characterization of the gauge problem, the scalar–vector–tensor decomposition and its linear decoupling, the perturbed Einstein equations in terms of the gauge-invariant Bardeen potentials, and the quantization of the Mukhanov–Sasaki variable during slow-roll inflation with Bunch–Davies initial conditions. We derive the primordial scalar and tensor power spectra, the spectral tilt, and the single-field consistency relation, then propagate these initial conditions through the transfer-function and line-of-sight formalism to the CMB temperature and polarization angular power spectra, including the Sachs–Wolfe effects, acoustic oscillations, Silk damping, and E/B polarization decomposition. Confrontation with Planck 2018 data — scalar tilt n_s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042, tensor-to-scalar ratio r < 0.056, and stringent adiabaticity bounds — confirms the inflationary paradigm at percent precision while sharpening the theoretical challenges that remain.


## 1 Intro

The Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) universe is homogeneous and isotropic only on the largest scales and only to first approximation. Galaxies, clusters, and the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature anisotropies of one part in a hundred thousand all testify that the early universe was *almost* — but not exactly — homogeneous [2]. **Cosmological perturbation theory** is the mathematical machinery that turns this "almost" into quantitative predictions: it linearizes the Einstein equations and the matter conservation laws around the FRW background, evolves the resulting small fluctuations from inflation through radiation and matter domination to the present, and predicts the statistics of everything from the CMB anisotropy maps to the galaxy power spectrum [1][2].

The program is justified by the extraordinary smallness of the fluctuations themselves. The observed CMB anisotropy, delta T over T of order ten to the minus five, implies that metric perturbations were at most of that order at recombination, and gravity being purely attractive, they were even smaller in the past [2]. Linear perturbation theory is therefore not an uncontrolled approximation but a quantitatively excellent one on the scales probed by cosmological surveys.

The central technical difficulty is the *gauge problem*. General relativity has no preferred coordinate system: an infinitesimal diffeomorphism can shuffle a perturbation between the "background" and the "perturbation," making naive perturbation variables unphysical [8][1]. **Bardeen's 1980 breakthrough** was to construct combinations of metric and matter perturbations that are invariant under these gauge transformations, so that the physical degrees of freedom are cleanly separated from coordinate artifacts [1]. Combined with the scalar–vector–tensor (SVT) decomposition, the quantization of the Mukhanov–Sasaki variable during inflation, and the line-of-sight transfer-function formalism, the gauge-invariant framework yields the complete pipeline from quantum vacuum fluctuations to the observed CMB power spectra — and with it, the tightest empirical confirmation of the inflationary paradigm [4][5].

## 2 Background

### The homogeneous background

We work with a spatially flat FRW spacetime. In **conformal time** tau, defined by dt = a(t) d tau, the background line element reads

```
ds^2 = a^2(tau) ( -d tau^2 + delta_ij dx^i dx^j )
```

where a(tau) is the scale factor and delta_ij the Euclidean metric. The background dynamics are governed by the Friedmann equations [4]

```
H^2 = (8 pi G / 3) rho
H'  = -(4 pi G / 3) (rho + 3p) a^2
```

with H = a'/a the conformal Hubble parameter (primes denote derivatives with respect to conformal time), rho the homogeneous energy density and p the pressure. The continuity equation rho' + 3H(rho + p) = 0 closes the system. The equation-of-state parameter w = p/rho takes the values 0 (matter), one third (radiation), and approximately minus one (inflation, dark energy).

### Perturbing the metric

A general first-order perturbation of the flat FRW metric introduces ten functions: four scalars, four components of two transverse vectors, and the two polarizations of a transverse-traceless tensor [2]. In the **conformal Newtonian (longitudinal) gauge**, the scalar sector takes the familiar diagonal form

```
ds^2 = a^2(tau) ( -(1 + 2 Psi) d tau^2 + (1 - 2 Phi) delta_ij dx^i dx^j )
```

Here Psi is the Newtonian potential (lapse perturbation) and Phi the spatial curvature perturbation. In the absence of anisotropic stress the two coincide, Phi = Psi, a relation broken at the ten-percent level by photon and neutrino free streaming after decoupling [7].

Matter is perturbed alongside the geometry. For a perfect fluid we write the fractional density contrast delta = delta rho / rho, the velocity divergence theta, and the anisotropic stress sigma; for a scalar inflaton field phi(tau, x) = phi_0(tau) + delta phi(tau, x), the field fluctuation plays the analogous role [2][4].

### The gauge problem

Under an infinitesimal coordinate change x^mu -> x^mu + xi^mu, a first-order perturbation Q transforms as Q -> Q + L_xi Q_0, where L_xi is the Lie derivative and Q_0 the background value [8]. Because any scalar background quantity depends only on time, its perturbation shifts by a term proportional to the background time derivative — a pure coordinate artifact can therefore *masquerade* as a physical density fluctuation.

> **Theorem (Stewart–Walker Lemma):** A first-order perturbation is gauge-invariant if and only if the corresponding background quantity vanishes, is a constant scalar, or is a constant linear combination of Kronecker deltas [8].

> **Proof:** Under a gauge transformation generated by xi^mu, the perturbation changes by the Lie derivative of the background. This vanishes identically for all xi^mu precisely when the background is of the stated trivial form. The energy density rho_0(tau) is time-dependent and non-constant, so delta rho is *not* gauge-invariant; the Weyl tensor, which vanishes in FRW, yields gauge-invariant perturbations. ∎

The practical consequence is that one must either fix a gauge completely (e.g. synchronous or Newtonian gauge) and track residual gauge modes carefully, or — far more robustly — work with explicitly gauge-invariant combinations from the start [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the canonical gauge-invariant program of Bardeen, Kodama–Sasaki, and Mukhanov–Feldman–Brandenberger [1][2][3]:

1. **SVT decomposition.** Expand all perturbations in eigenfunctions of the spatial Laplacian (Fourier modes on flat slices) and split them by their transformation properties under spatial rotations into scalar, vector, and tensor components. At linear order the three sectors decouple completely, and vector modes decay with the expansion, leaving scalars (density perturbations) and tensors (gravitational waves) [2].

2. **Gauge-invariant variables.** Form the **Bardeen potentials**

   ```
   Phi_B = Phi + H (B - E') + (B - E')'
   Psi_B = Psi - H (B - E')
   ```

   from the general scalar metric perturbation (with shift B and spatial shear E), together with gauge-invariant matter variables such as the comoving density contrast. These coincide with Phi and Psi in Newtonian gauge but are defined in *any* gauge [1][3].

3. **Perturbed Einstein equations.** Linearize G_mu_nu = 8 pi G T_mu_nu. In Fourier space the scalar system becomes (schematically): a Hamiltonian constraint relating Phi to the density contrast, a momentum constraint relating Phi' + H Psi to the momentum density, and evolution equations for the shear and anisotropic stress [2][3].

4. **Quantization during inflation.** Combine the metric and inflaton fluctuations into the **Mukhanov variable** v = a (delta phi + (phi_0'/H) Psi), whose action is that of a canonical scalar field with time-dependent mass. Each Fourier mode obeys the **Mukhanov–Sasaki equation**

   ```
   v_k'' + (k^2 - z''/z) v_k = 0,    z = a phi_0' / H
   ```

   Quantize v with Bunch–Davies initial conditions deep inside the horizon (k >> aH), where modes behave as Minkowski vacuum fluctuations, and evolve through horizon exit (k = aH) to superhorizon scales, where the curvature perturbation R = v/z freezes out and becomes highly squeezed — a classical stochastic field for all practical purposes [2][4].

5. **Transfer to observables.** Evolve the primordial spectra through the radiation and matter eras with **transfer functions** T(k) computed by Boltzmann codes, and project onto the sky with the line-of-sight integral to obtain the CMB angular power spectra C_l [6][7].

The sections below expand each stage, with the deep-dive section examining the subtlest points: gauge invariance, mode decoupling, quantum-to-classical transition, the transfer pipeline, and polarization.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Gauge Problem, Gauge Fixing, and Bardeen Invariants

The decomposition of spacetime into "background plus perturbation" requires a *gauge*: a diffeomorphism identifying points of the fictitious background manifold with points of the physical perturbed manifold. Changing this identification while holding coordinates fixed is a gauge transformation, and it changes every perturbation variable by the Lie-derivative terms above [8].

Two complete gauge fixings dominate the literature, and their complementary strengths illuminate why invariants matter:

| Gauge | Condition | Strengths | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synchronous | Psi = 0, shift zero; comoving with CDM | Natural for Boltzmann codes; no spurious potentials | Residual gauge freedom; unphysical gauge modes grow |
| Conformal Newtonian | B = E = 0 | Variables coincide with Bardeen potentials; transparent physics | Must be imposed carefully at second order |

Bardeen's resolution sidesteps the choice entirely: the potentials Phi_B and Psi_B, the gauge-invariant density contrast Delta = delta + 3(1+w)H (v - B), and the comoving curvature perturbation R are unchanged by *any* infinitesimal diffeomorphism [1]. All physical predictions — power spectra, CMB anisotropies, growth rates — are expressible through them, guaranteeing that no result depends on the arbitrary identification map [2][3].

### 4.2 Scalar–Vector–Tensor Decomposition and Linear Decoupling

By the Helmholtz theorem on flat spatial slices, any vector field splits into a gradient (scalar) part and a transverse (vector) part, and any symmetric tensor into scalar, vector, and transverse-traceless (tensor) pieces [2]. Counting degrees of freedom:

- **Scalars (4 functions → 2 physical):** Phi, Psi plus two gauge functions. These source density and pressure perturbations and seed all structure.
- **Vectors (4 functions → 2 physical):** Frame-dragging-type modes. With no persistent vector sources, their amplitude decays as one over a, rendering them irrelevant for standard cosmology — though they are actively sourced in models with cosmic strings or primordial magnetic fields.
- **Tensors (2 functions, both physical):** Transverse-traceless h_ij, the two helicity states of gravitational waves. They obey a free wave equation, are neither sourced nor damped by scalar matter at linear order, and imprint a distinctive B-mode polarization pattern on the CMB — the celebrated "smoking gun" of inflation [4][5].

> **Theorem (Linear SVT decoupling):** On an FRW background, scalar, vector, and tensor perturbations evolve independently at first order in perturbation theory [2].

> **Proof:** The background is rotationally invariant, so the linearized Einstein operator commutes with spatial rotations. By Schur's lemma applied to the rotation group, the operator is block-diagonal in the SVT basis; cross-terms vanish. The background field equations contain no preferred vector or tensor, so no mixing is generated. ∎

### 4.3 Quantum Fluctuations During Inflation: The Mukhanov–Sasaki Equation

The most profound application of the formalism is the computation of the *primordial* spectra. During slow-roll inflation the universe is dominated by a scalar field with a nearly flat potential; quantum fluctuations delta phi of the inflaton and of the metric are stretched by the quasi-exponential expansion [4].

The gauge-invariant Mukhanov variable v packages the single scalar degree of freedom. Its equation, v_k'' + (k^2 - z''/z) v_k = 0, describes a harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency. Two regimes emerge [2]:

- **Subhorizon (k >> aH):** The k^2 term dominates; modes oscillate as e^{-ik tau} / sqrt(2k), and we impose the Bunch–Davies vacuum — the unique state matching the Minkowski vacuum at short distances.
- **Superhorizon (k << aH):** The z''/z term dominates; the growing solution tracks z itself, so the curvature perturbation R_k = v_k/z approaches a constant. Modes *freeze out*: their quantum nature is hidden in extreme squeezing of the Wigner function, and they behave as classical Gaussian random fields [2][4].

The resulting dimensionless power spectrum is, to leading order in slow roll,

```
Delta_R^2(k) = (1 / 24 pi^2) (V / M_pl^4) (1 / epsilon_V)
```

evaluated at horizon crossing k = aH, with epsilon_V the potential slow-roll parameter. The spectral tilt n_s - 1 = -6 epsilon_V + 2 eta_V and the tensor spectrum Delta_t^2 = (2 / 3 pi^2)(V / M_pl^4) follow similarly, yielding the famous single-field consistency relation r = -8 n_t between the tensor-to-scalar ratio and the tensor tilt [4].

### 4.4 Transfer Functions and the Line-of-Sight Integral

Primordial spectra are *initial conditions*. The observable universe is their convolution with 13.8 billion years of plasma physics, gravity, and radiative transfer. The bridge is the **transfer function** T(k, tau): the linear response of each species' perturbation to a unit primordial curvature mode [6][7].

Key physics encoded in the transfer functions:

- **Acoustic oscillations:** Before recombination, photons and baryons form a tightly coupled fluid. Gravity compresses it into potential wells while radiation pressure resists, driving standing acoustic waves with sound speed c_s = 1/sqrt(3(1+R)), R the baryon-to-photon ratio. Modes caught at compression maxima or rarefaction maxima at last scattering produce the celebrated harmonic peak series [7].
- **Silk damping:** Photon diffusion erases fluctuations on small scales, exponentially suppressing power beyond multipole l of order a few thousand.
- **Baryon loading:** Baryons deepen compressions relative to rarefactions, boosting odd (compression) peaks over even ones — a direct baryometer [7].

Seljak and Zaldarriaga's **line-of-sight integration** recast the full Boltzmann hierarchy as an integral over sources along the photon past light cone, reducing the computation of the C_l spectra from hours to seconds and enabling the Markov-chain parameter estimation era [6]. Modern codes (CAMB, CLASS) implement this with sub-percent precision.

### 4.5 CMB Anisotropies: Sachs–Wolfe Effects, Acoustic Peaks, and Polarization

Photons climbing out of potential wells at last scattering are gravitationally redshifted; combined with intrinsic temperature fluctuations and Doppler shifts from plasma motion, this yields the observed anisotropy [7]:

- **Ordinary Sachs–Wolfe (large scales):** delta T/T = Psi/3. On superhorizon scales at recombination the combination of gravitational redshift and intrinsic fluctuation leaves a nearly flat plateau in l(l+1)C_l — a direct snapshot of the primordial spectrum.
- **Integrated Sachs–Wolfe:** Time-varying potentials along the line of sight (during radiation-to-matter transition and dark-energy domination) add secondary anisotropy, correlating the CMB with large-scale structure.
- **Acoustic peaks:** The harmonic series at l near 220, 540, 800, ... encodes the sound horizon, spatial curvature, baryon density, and dark matter density with exquisite precision [5][7].
- **Polarization:** Thomson scattering of a quadrupolar radiation field linearly polarizes the CMB. Scalar perturbations generate only E-mode (gradient) polarization; **B-mode (curl) polarization** on degree scales can only be sourced by tensor modes or by gravitational lensing of E-modes — making primordial B-modes the prime target of next-generation experiments [4][5].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### Observational confirmation

The Planck satellite's 2018 legacy analysis turned the qualitative picture above into percent-level parameter constraints [5]:

| Parameter | Planck 2018 value | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Scalar spectral index n_s | 0.9649 ± 0.0042 | 8 sigma deviation from scale invariance |
| Scalar amplitude A_s (at 0.05 Mpc^-1) | (2.10 ± 0.03) × 10^-9 | Seeds of all structure |
| Tensor-to-scalar ratio r_0.002 | < 0.056 (95% CL, Planck + BK15) | Concave potentials favored |
| Spatial curvature Omega_K | 0.0007 ± 0.0019 | Flat to 0.4% with BAO |
| Isocurvature fraction | < ~1.5% | Adiabatic initial conditions confirmed |

The measured red tilt (n_s < 1) matches the generic slow-roll prediction, the tight isocurvature bounds confirm the single-clock adiabatic initial conditions predicted by single-field inflation, and the B-mode non-detection progressively eliminates monomial large-field models [5]. The baryon acoustic oscillation scale measured in galaxy surveys provides an independent standard ruler consistent with the CMB-inferred sound horizon — a nontrivial cross-check of the entire transfer-function machinery [5].

### Worked example: slow-roll predictions vs. Planck

The following Python snippet computes n_s and r for two benchmark inflationary models and compares them with the Planck 2018 constraints [4][5]:

```python
import math

# Planck 2018 68% CL constraints
ns_obs, ns_err = 0.9649, 0.0042
r_limit = 0.056  # 95% CL upper bound, Planck + BK15

def slow_roll_predictions(model, N=55.0):
    # N: e-folds before end of inflation at pivot scale
    if model == "starobinsky":  # R^2 / alpha-attractor class
        ns = 1.0 - 2.0 / N
        r = 12.0 / (N * N)
    elif model == "phi2":  # quadratic monomial, now disfavored
        ns = 1.0 - 2.0 / N
        r = 8.0 / N
    else:
        raise ValueError("unknown model")
    return ns, r

for model in ["starobinsky", "phi2"]:
    ns, r = slow_roll_predictions(model)
    sigma = (ns - ns_obs) / ns_err
    verdict = "VIABLE" if abs(sigma) < 2.0 and r < r_limit else "DISFAVORED"
    print(model, "ns=%.4f" % ns, "r=%.4f" % r, "->", verdict)
```

Output: the Starobinsky model (n_s ≈ 0.9636, r ≈ 0.0040) sits comfortably inside the Planck contours, while quadratic inflation (r ≈ 0.145) is excluded — precisely the model-selection power the perturbation formalism enables [5].

> **Theorem (Conservation of the comoving curvature perturbation):** For adiabatic perturbations, the comoving curvature perturbation R is conserved on superhorizon scales (k << aH), independent of the equation of state [2].

> **Proof:** The evolution equation for R reads R' = -(H / (rho + p)) delta p_nad + O(k^2 / a^2 H^2), where delta p_nad is the non-adiabatic pressure perturbation. For purely adiabatic perturbations delta p_nad = 0, and on superhorizon scales the gradient term is negligible; hence R' ≈ 0. This is why inflationary predictions survive the unknown physics of reheating: whatever happens at the end of inflation, R is carried unchanged to the hot Big Bang era. ∎

## 6 Limitations

The framework's very successes delineate its boundaries:

1. **Linearity breaks down.** On small scales and at late times, delta exceeds unity and perturbation theory fails; N-body simulations and effective field theory of large-scale structure take over. The formalism is strictly a large-scale, early-time tool [2].
2. **Gauge invariance is a linear luxury.** At second order, gauge transformations mix orders, and no fully satisfactory gauge-invariant formulation of non-Gaussianity exists without careful gauge fixing; calculations of primordial f_NL must track gauge artifacts explicitly [2].
3. **The trans-Planckian problem.** Modes observed today originated with physical wavelengths far below the Planck length if inflation lasted much more than the minimal duration. The Bunch–Davies prescription assumes unknown Planck-scale physics leaves the vacuum undisturbed — an assumption, not a derivation [4].
4. **Vacuum and initial-state ambiguity.** The Bunch–Davies state is natural but not unique; excited initial states or nonstandard dispersion relations can imprint features the data currently disfavor but cannot exclude in full generality [5].
5. **Degeneracies at late times.** The late integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, reionization optical depth, and lensing partially degenerate with primordial parameters; precision cosmology requires joint fits across probes [5].
6. **Cosmic variance.** On the largest scales only a handful of independent modes exist (2l+1 per multipole), imposing an irreducible statistical floor — the low-l "anomalies" can never be sharpened beyond it.

## 7 Conclusion

Gauge-invariant cosmological perturbation theory is one of the most empirically successful frameworks in all of physics. From Bardeen's identification of the physical degrees of freedom [1], through the Stewart–Walker characterization of gauge invariance [8], the Kodama–Sasaki and Mukhanov–Feldman–Brandenberger systematization [2][3], the line-of-sight computational breakthrough [6], and the Planck collaboration's percent-level validation [5], the chain from quantum vacuum fluctuation to CMB anisotropy map is now a precision science.

The central lessons are durable: *coordinates are not physics* (hence Bardeen variables); *rotational symmetry organizes the dynamics* (hence SVT decoupling); *the early universe was a quantum system* (hence the Mukhanov–Sasaki equation and its frozen, squeezed superhorizon modes); and *plasma physics is a linear filter* (hence transfer functions and the line-of-sight integral). With n_s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042, r < 0.056, and adiabatic, Gaussian, nearly scale-invariant initial conditions confirmed [5], the inflationary origin of structure stands as the best-tested paradigm of the very early universe — while the hunt for primordial B-modes promises to promote the tensor sector from upper limit to detection, completing the scalar–vector–tensor triptych the theory predicts.
