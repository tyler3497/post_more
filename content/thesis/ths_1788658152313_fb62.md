# Homotopy and Cubical Type Theory: The Univalence Axiom, Higher Inductive Types, Canonicity, and Computational Interpretations

## Abstract

The univalence axiom of Vladimir Voevodsky identifies the identity type of a universe with the type of homotopy equivalences, turning Martin-Löf type theory into a synthetic language for homotopy theory. Asserted axiomatically, however, univalence destroys canonicity: closed terms of natural-number type need not reduce to numerals, and proofs using the axiom carry no computational content. Cubical type theory resolves this tension by internalizing the cubical-set model into the syntax: an interval object supports direct manipulation of n-dimensional cubes, Kan composition and coercion operations give computational meaning to transport along paths, and a Glue type former proves univalence with definitional computation rules. This thesis surveys the development from axiomatic homotopy type theory through the constructive cubical-set interpretation of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, the Cartesian reformulation of Angiuli, Brunerie, Coquand, Favonia, Harper, and Licata, and the canonicity metatheory of Huber and of Cavallo and Harper. It explains how higher inductive types acquire judgmental computation rules, why synthetic homotopy computations such as Brunerie's number become evaluable, and where fundamental limitations remain.

## 1 Introduction

Dependent type theory began as a constructive foundation for mathematics, but the discovery that identity types admit a homotopical interpretation transformed the subject. In the early 2000s, Awodey and Warren showed that intensional Martin-Löf type theory can be modeled by weak factorization systems arising in homotopy theory [2], and independently Voevodsky constructed a model of type theory in Kan simplicial sets [1]. In these models, a type is a space, a term is a point, and a proof of an identity type `a = b` is a path from `a` to `b`. Higher identity proofs are homotopies between paths, and so on: the identity type carries the full structure of an ∞-groupoid.

Voevodsky then observed that his simplicial model satisfies a striking additional property, which he named *univalence* [1][2]. Stated for a universe `U` with identity type `Id_U`, the axiom asserts that the canonical map

```
idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)
```

is an equivalence, where `A ≃ B` is the type of homotopy equivalences from `A` to `B`. Intuitively, equal types are equivalent, and — more powerfully — equivalent types are equal, so that equivalences can be transported along like identities. This principle subsumes function extensionality, propositional extensionality, and a host of structural identifications that working mathematicians perform informally but that set-theoretic foundations cannot validate.

Yet there is a price. Asserted as an axiom, univalence postulates a new canonical term of type `(A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B)` without specifying how that term computes. Since the axiom's eliminations get stuck, the theory loses *canonicity*: a closed term of type `ℕ` may be stuck on an application of the univalence axiom rather than reducing to a numeral. The celebrated computation of the fourth homotopy group of the 3-sphere, `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/nℤ` for some closed `n : ℕ`, could only be shown to have `n = 2` by sophisticated mathematical arguments rather than by mere evaluation [3]. The central question driving cubical type theory is therefore: can we have univalence *and* computation?

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Identity types and their homotopical reading

Martin-Löf type theory equips every type `A` with an identity type family `Id_A(x, y)` generated by `refl : Id_A(x, x)` and eliminated by the induction principle `J`. From a logical perspective, `Id_A` internalizes propositional equality; from a homotopical perspective, it is the *path space* `A^I` of continuous maps from the unit interval into `A` [2]. Two functions are identical exactly when they are homotopic, since a homotopy is a pointwise family of paths. A dependent type `B(x)` indexed by `x : A` is interpreted as a fibration, with transport along paths corresponding to path lifting.

The groupoid model of Hofmann and Streicher showed that the identity type naturally forms a groupoid — paths have inverses and composition, satisfying the groupoid laws up to higher paths [2]. The simplicial model extended this to all dimensions, interpreting types as Kan complexes and universes as object classifiers. Univalence holds in this model but fails in the "types as sets" interpretation, where the identity type of a universe classifies definitional, not homotopical, identifications.

### 2.2 Axiomatic homotopy type theory and its virtues

The Univalent Foundations Program's book *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics* [1] develops mathematics synthetically from univalence and higher inductive types (HITs). Among its achievements:

1. **Function extensionality** follows from univalence: pointwise-equal functions are equal [1].
2. **Higher inductive types** give direct logical presentations of spheres, truncations, quotients, and localizations, enabling synthetic proofs such as `πₙ(Sⁿ) = ℤ` [1].
3. **Structure identity principle**: isomorphic mathematical structures are identified, matching informal mathematical practice [1].

But the axiomatic formulation inherits the computational deficit. The univalence axiom and HIT axioms postulate new canonical inhabitants of types without computation rules. Definitional equality is unaffected, so proofs that must compute — Brunerie's `n : ℕ` being the flagship example — cannot simply be run.

> Theorem: (Univalence, Voevodsky [1][2]). For a univalent universe `U` and types `A, B : U`, the canonical function `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence. In particular, every equivalence `e : A ≃ B` induces a path `ua(e) : A =_U B`, and transport along `ua(e)` computes as application of `e` — but only *propositionally*, not definitionally, in the axiomatic theory.

### 2.3 The canonicity problem

*Canonicity* states that every closed term of an inductive type reduces to a canonical form: every closed `t : ℕ` is definitionally equal to a numeral `Sⁿ(0)`, and every closed `b : Bool` is `true` or `false`. Canonicity is the proof-theoretic shadow of the claim that proofs are programs: without it, the theory's computational interpretation is broken at exactly the points where univalence is used. Any constructive interpretation of the univalence axiom must therefore do two things: (i) validate univalence as a theorem rather than an axiom, and (ii) specify the operational behavior of the univalence construction so that canonicity is restored. Cubical type theory achieves both by making the interval, paths, and composition operations *primitive* rather than derived [3][5].

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is a systematic comparative survey of the cubical approach to univalence. The method is threefold:

- **Historical reconstruction**: trace the line from the Hofmann–Streicher groupoid model, through the Awodey–Warren and Voevodsky simplicial interpretations, to the constructive cubical-set models of Bezem–Coquand–Huber and Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg [3].
- **Comparative analysis of cube categories**: contrast the De Morgan cubical sets of CCHM (interval with reversal and connections) against the Cartesian cubes of Angiuli–Brunerie–Coquand–Favonia–Harper–Licata (ABCFHL), in which only diagonal face maps are retained [5]. The comparison is organized around the Kan operations, the formulation of Glue types, and the treatment of higher inductive types.
- **Metatheoretic evaluation**: examine canonicity (Huber [3]), normalization and decidability of type checking (Cavallo–Harper [7]), and the status of implementations (Cubical Agda [8], redtt/cooltt) as empirical evidence for the computational claims.

Primary sources are the original research papers and the HoTT Book [1]; all cited URLs were verified from publication records. The emphasis throughout is on *how* the cubical syntax internalizes model structure, and on what the resulting metatheory does and does not guarantee.

## 4 The Cubical Machinery: Intervals, Paths, Composition, and Glue

The core insight of cubical type theory is to take the *interval* seriously as a syntactic object. Where axiomatic HoTT reasons about paths through the inductively generated identity type, cubical type theory postulates an interval pretype `I` with endpoints `i0, i1 : I`, and defines paths as *functions out of the interval*.

### 4.1 The interval pretype and dependent paths

A path in `A` from `a` to `b` is a term `p : I → A` with `p i0 ≡ a` and `p i1 ≡ b`, written `Path A a b`. More generally, given a line of types `A : I → U`, a *dependent path* relates elements of the fibers:

```agda
PathP : (A : I → Type ℓ) → A i0 → A i1 → Type ℓ
```

The eliminator for paths is then definable: path induction follows from the Kan operations, and function extensionality is *provable by a one-line term* — given `h : (x : A) → f x ≡ g x`, the term `λ i x → h x i` inhabits `f ≡ g` [3]. This is the first dividend of the cubical perspective: extensionality principles that require axioms (or elaborate encodings) in Book HoTT become definitional.

The interval is a *pretype*, not a type: it has no Kan structure and cannot be eliminated into arbitrary types. In the CCHM formulation the interval carries De Morgan algebra structure (minimum, maximum, reversal), while the Cartesian formulation of ABCFHL strips this down to diagonals, retaining only what is needed for the Kan operations and HITs [5]. Both choices support the same essential constructions; they differ in the complexity of the composition algorithm and in how easily connections and higher cubes are expressed.

### 4.2 Kan composition: `hcomp` and `transp`

Fibrancy — the property that makes a pretype a genuine type — is expressed through two Kan operations:

- **Homogeneous composition** (`hcomp`): given an open box (a partial cube missing one face) and a cap, produce the missing face and the filler.
- **Transport** (`transp`): given a line of types `A : I → U` and `a : A i0`, produce an element of `A i1`.

Heterogeneous composition `comp` combines both. These operations are the computational engine of the theory: symmetry and transitivity of paths, congruence, and transport are all *defined* by composing cubes, and each comes with definitional equalities describing its behavior on canonical forms [3]. For example, path composition fills the missing face of a square:

```agda
-- path composition via hcomp: the missing right face of the square
_∙_ : {x y z : A} → x ≡ y → y ≡ z → x ≡ z
p ∙ q = hcomp (λ j → λ { (i = i0) → x
                      ; (i = i1) → q j })
              (p i)
```

Critically, `transp` along a constant line is the identity, and `transp` along `ua(e)` for an equivalence `e : A ≃ B` computes by applying `e` — this is the computational content of univalence, made definitional.

### 4.3 Glue types and the proof of univalence

The masterstroke of CCHM is the *Glue* type former [3]. Given a total type `B`, a face condition `φ`, a partial type `T` defined on `φ`, and a partial equivalence `e : T ≃ B` on `φ`, the type `Glue B [ φ ↦ (T, e) ]` agrees with `T` on `φ` and with `B` elsewhere:

```agda
Glue : (B : Type ℓ) (φ : I) {T : Partial φ (Type ℓ)}
     → (e : Partial φ (T ≃ B)) → Type ℓ
```

with introduction `glue : (b : B) (t : Partial φ T) → Glue B ...` and eliminator `unglue : Glue B ... → B`, satisfying `unglue (glue b t) ≡ b` definitionally. Univalence is then proved by the famous term

```agda
ua : {A B : Type ℓ} → A ≃ B → A ≡ B
ua e i = Glue B [ (i = i0) ↦ (A , e)
                ; (i = i1) ↦ (B , idEquiv B) ]
```

At `i = i0` the Glue type is definitionally `A`; at `i = i1` it is definitionally `B`; the path between them in the universe is the desired identification. Transport along `ua e` then computes, by the Kan operations on Glue types, as application of `e` [3]. This single construction is what axiomatic HoTT cannot provide: a *proof* of univalence with specified computational behavior.

> Theorem: (Univalence in CCHM [3]). In cubical type theory, the map `idtoeqv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence, with quasi-inverse `ua`. Moreover, `transport (ua e) a ≡ e a` holds *definitionally*, restoring the computational character of equivalence induction.

### 4.4 Higher inductive types: the circle and beyond

Higher inductive types extend ordinary inductive types with *path constructors*. The circle is the paradigmatic example:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator (circle induction) requires, besides `b : P base`, a dependent path `l : PathP (λ i → P (loop i)) b b` lying over `loop`. In axiomatic HoTT, the computation rule for `loop` holds only propositionally — eliminating out of a HIT can get stuck on the higher constructor. In cubical type theory, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg showed that spheres, tori, suspensions, propositional truncations, and pushouts all admit *judgmental* computation rules for every constructor, including the higher-dimensional ones, with universes closed under these formers [4]. The key is that HITs are interpreted as higher-dimensional programs whose boundary behavior is specified by the Kan operations; composition at a HIT reduces according to the constructors' shapes.

The payoff is immediate and dramatic: synthetic homotopy theory becomes *executable*. Brunerie's number — the closed `n : ℕ` with `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/nℤ` — can in principle be normalized by the machine rather than analyzed by hand [3][6]. Cubical Agda [8] turns this into practice, providing a dependently typed programming language in which univalence and HITs compute.

## 5 Canonicity, Normalization, and Computational Content

### 5.1 Canonicity in the CCHM theory

Because CCHM cubical type theory is presented by inference rules rather than by meaning explanations, canonicity is not automatic: it must be proved. Huber established canonicity for the CCHM theory using a sconing (Artin gluing) argument over the cubical-set model [3]. The proof constructs a computability model in which every type is equipped with a computability predicate closed under the Kan operations, and shows that every well-typed closed term of natural-number type denotes a numeral.

> Theorem: (Canonicity, Huber [3]). In cubical type theory, every closed term `n : ℕ` is definitionally equal to a numeral `Sᵏ(0)`. More generally, every closed term of an inductive type is definitionally equal to a canonical form.

This is the precise sense in which univalence has been given computational content: a proof that uses `ua` and then produces a natural number can be *run*, and the machine is guaranteed to deliver a numeral.

### 5.2 Computational cubical type theory and canonicity at every type

The ABCFHL program takes a different, more radical route [5][6]. Instead of defining the theory by rules and proving canonicity afterward, *computational cubical type theory* defines types as specifications of the behavior of untyped cubical programs equipped with a deterministic operational semantics — a cubical analogue of Martin-Löf's meaning explanations and the Nuprl tradition. Because types are defined behaviorally, canonicity holds *trivially at every type*: a closed program of boolean type must evaluate to `true` or `false`, since that is what it means to be a boolean. The hard work shifts to verifying the formation, introduction, and elimination rules against the meaning explanations [6].

This Cartesian reformulation also simplifies the cube category: keeping only diagonal face maps (contraction of dimensions) suffices for the Kan operations and for HITs, while discarding the De Morgan structure streamlines both the metatheory and the implementation in the RedPRL proof assistant [5][6].

### 5.3 Synthetic homotopy computations: Brunerie's number and beyond

The practical significance of canonicity is best appreciated through synthetic homotopy theory. In Book HoTT, Brunerie and Licata proved `πₙ(Sⁿ) = ℤ` for `n ≥ 1` using univalence in an essential way [2], and Brunerie showed `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/nℤ` for a specific closed term `n : ℕ` — but identifying `n` as `2` required deep additional argument, because the axiomatic theory cannot evaluate `n` [6]. In cubical type theory, `n` is a program; canonicity guarantees it reduces to a numeral, and normalization results give algorithms for computing it. Cavallo and Harper's work on internal parametricity extends the Cartesian framework further, showing how relational parametricity coexists with univalence in a double-presheaf model [7]. The table below summarizes the landscape:

| Theory | Univalence | HITs | Canonicity | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Axiomatic HoTT [1] | Axiom (no computation) | Axioms (propositional β) | Fails | Coq-HoTT, Agda (flagged) |
| CCHM cubical [3] | Theorem via Glue (definitional) | Judgmental β for all constructors [4] | Proved (Huber) | Cubical Agda [8] |
| Cartesian ABCFHL [5] | Theorem via Glue | Judgmental β | At every type (meaning explanations) [6] | RedPRL, cooltt |
| Cubical Agda [8] | Built-in, computes | Native HITs | Conjectured/partial | Agda `--cubical` |

## 6 Limitations

The cubical resolution of the univalence-computation tension is genuine, but it is not free of costs or open problems:

- **Metatheoretic complexity.** Canonicity and normalization proofs for cubical theories are substantially harder than for ordinary dependent type theory. The computability arguments must handle the Kan operations, Glue types, and HITs simultaneously; full normalization for the complete CCHM theory with all HITs remains delicate, and decidability of type checking rests on normalization results that are still being consolidated [7].
- **Two-level tension.** The interval is a pretype, and managing the type/pretype distinction (fibrant vs. non-fibrant) complicates both the theory and its exposition. Users of Cubical Agda must understand when `hcomp` applies and why certain types lack Kan structure.
- **Performance of synthetic computations.** While Brunerie's number is *in principle* evaluable, actually normalizing it in Cubical Agda is computationally expensive; the naive cubical composition algorithms can blow up. Efficient evaluation of higher-dimensional programs is an active engineering problem [8].
- **Semantics vs. syntax gap.** The ABCFHL meaning explanations justify rules but do not yield a decidable type checker directly; implementations like RedPRL refine the rules substantially [6]. Conversely, the rule-based CCHM theory needs the separate canonicity argument [3].
- **HIT schema.** A fully general schema for higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules — covering indexed, recursive-recursive, and higher inductive-inductive families — is not yet complete; current results cover spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts [4].
- **Compatibility with classical principles.** Univalence is incompatible with uniqueness of identity proofs and with certain forms of choice; cubical type theory inherits these incompatibilities, so it cannot serve mathematicians who require classical reasoning without further modal or two-level extensions.

## 7 Conclusion

Cubical type theory transforms the univalence axiom from a computation-blocking postulate into a theorem with definitional computational behavior. By internalizing cubes, Kan composition, and Glue types into the syntax, the CCHM theory [3] proves univalence and restores canonicity; the Cartesian reformulation [5][6] grounds the same ideas in computational meaning explanations with canonicity at every type; and the extension to higher inductive types [4] makes synthetic homotopy theory executable in systems such as Cubical Agda [8]. The arc from Voevodsky's simplicial model to evaluable proofs of `π₄(S³)` represents one of the deepest interactions between homotopy theory and programming-language semantics yet achieved. What remains is consolidation: complete normalization proofs, efficient implementations, a general HIT schema, and the broader question of how univalent foundations relate to the rest of mathematics. The cubical perspective has, at minimum, settled the central conceptual question — univalence and computation are compatible — and provided the machinery with which the next generation of results will be both proved and run.
