# Receding-Horizon Optimization under Uncertainty: Nominal, Robust Tube-Based, and Explicit Model Predictive Control with Embedded Convex Realizations

## Abstract

Model predictive control (MPC) synthesizes feedback laws by repeatedly solving a finite-horizon optimal control problem and applying only the first control move, thereby handling multivariable constraints explicitly where classical linear methods cannot. This thesis develops the complete theoretical arc of linear MPC: the receding-horizon formulation and its condensed quadratic-program realization; the terminal-cost and terminal-set machinery — dual-mode laws, quasi-infinite horizons, and maximal invariant sets — that converts finite-horizon optimality into closed-loop stability certificates; tube-based robust MPC, which confines uncertain trajectories inside a robust positively invariant cross-section around a nominal plan through constraint tightening; explicit MPC, which solves the multiparametric quadratic program offline to yield a piecewise-affine control law over a polyhedral partition; and the convex reformulations, operator-splitting solvers, and code-generation techniques that bring MPC onto embedded hardware with microsecond cycle times. Proof sketches for recursive feasibility and asymptotic stability are given, a numerical study contrasts solver strategies, and fundamental limitations — the fragility of nominal designs, the curse of dimensionality in explicit laws, and the computational burden of robust invariant-set synthesis — are examined with precision.

## 1 Intro

Few ideas in control theory have crossed the chasm from academic journals to industrial practice as decisively as *receding-horizon control*. The premise is disarmingly simple: at each sampling instant, measure the state, predict the future evolution of the plant over a finite horizon using an internal model, solve an optimization problem that trades off tracking performance against control effort subject to hard constraints, and then apply only the first element of the optimal input sequence before repeating the entire procedure at the next sample. The horizon *recedes* — it is never actually reached — and the resulting implicit feedback law inherits constraint satisfaction, multivariable coordination, and a systematic treatment of performance objectives that classical PID and LQ designs handle only awkwardly [1].

Yet for two decades the theory lagged the practice: stability of the closed loop was argued heuristically, through sufficiently long horizons or terminal penalties chosen by trial. The decisive theoretical consolidation arrived with the axiomatic framework of Mayne, Rawlings, Rao, and Scokaert [1], which distilled *essential principles* — terminal costs, terminal constraint sets, and local stabilizing laws — that guarantee recursive feasibility and asymptotic stability for constrained linear and nonlinear systems alike.

This thesis traces that arc in full. We begin with the nominal formulation and its reduction to a convex quadratic program, develop the invariant-set and terminal-ingredient theory that certifies stability, confront the central weakness of nominal designs — their potential *zero robustness* to model mismatch [2] — through tube-based robust MPC, eliminate the online optimizer entirely via multiparametric explicit MPC [3], and finally show how structure-exploiting convex solvers and code generation deliver MPC on embedded targets with kilohertz rates [4][5]. The treatment is rigorous but implementation-aware: every theorem is paired with the computational object — a Riccati solution, an invariant polytope, a critical region, a splitting iteration — that makes it real.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The discrete-time optimal control problem

Consider the linear time-invariant plant

```
x_{k+1} = A x_k + B u_k,      x_0 given
```

with state `x_k` in R^n, input `u_k` in R^m, and polytopic constraint sets `x_k ∈ X`, `u_k ∈ U` containing the origin in their interiors. The *stage cost* is taken quadratic,

```
l(x, u) = x' Q x + u' R u,     Q ⪰ 0, R ≻ 0,
```

with `(A, Q^{1/2})` detectable and `(A, B)` stabilizable. Over a horizon `N`, the finite-horizon optimal control problem (FHOCP) is

```
V_N(x_0) = min_{u_0..u_{N-1}}  Σ_{k=0}^{N-1} l(x_k, u_k) + V_f(x_N)
s.t.     x_{k+1} = A x_k + B u_k,
         x_k ∈ X,  u_k ∈ U,
         x_N ∈ X_f,
```

where `V_f` is a *terminal cost* and `X_f ⊆ X` a *terminal set*. The receding-horizon law applies `u_0^⋆(x)` and discards the rest. Eliminating the states yields the *condensed* strictly convex quadratic program

```
min_U  (1/2) U' H U + x_0' F U + (1/2) x_0' Y x_0
s.t.   G U ≤ W + S x_0,
```

with `U` the stacked input sequence and `H ≻ 0` — a parametric QP in the measured state `x_0` that is solved at every step [4].

### 2.2 Invariant sets: the geometric vocabulary of safety

Stability arguments in MPC are inseparable from set invariance. A set `Ω` is *positively invariant* for `x^+ = f(x)` if `x ∈ Ω ⇒ f(x) ∈ Ω`; it is *control invariant* if for every `x ∈ Ω` there exists an admissible `u` keeping the successor inside `Ω`. For the uncertain system `x^+ = A_K x + w` with `w ∈ W` compact, a set `Z` is *robust positively invariant* (RPI) if `A_K Z ⊕ W ⊆ Z`, where `⊕` denotes the Minkowski sum. The *minimal* RPI set,

```
X_∞ = ⊕_{i=0}^{∞} A_K^i W,
```

exists when `A_K` is Schur stable and characterizes the smallest tube cross-section that an ancillary feedback `u = Kx` can guarantee [6]. The Pontryagin (geometric) difference `X ⊖ Z = {x : x + z ∈ X ∀ z ∈ Z}` tightens constraints so that nominal plans remain feasible for every disturbed realization. Computing polytopic RPI sets and maximal output-admissible sets is the subject of a mature algorithmic literature [6][7].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The stabilizing terminal ingredients

Following the axiomatic treatment [1], closed-loop stability is secured by four ingredients chosen jointly:

1. **A terminal set** `X_f ⊆ X` that is control invariant under a *local* law `κ_f(x) ∈ U`.
2. **A terminal cost** `V_f` satisfying the Lyapunov descent `V_f(Ax + Bκ_f(x)) − V_f(x) ≤ −l(x, κ_f(x))` for all `x ∈ X_f`.
3. **A stage cost** `l` positive definite in the usual sense.
4. **Continuity** of the value function at the origin (automatic for the QP formulation).

The canonical choice takes `κ_f(x) = Kx` as the unconstrained LQR gain, `V_f(x) = x'Px` with `P` solving the discrete algebraic Riccati equation (DARE), and `X_f` as the maximal output-admissible invariant set for the LQR loop [7]. With these ingredients, the optimal value function `V_N^⋆` is a Lyapunov function for the receding-horizon closed loop.

> **Theorem: (Asymptotic stability of nominal MPC).** *Suppose the terminal ingredients satisfy the four conditions above and the FHOCP is feasible at the initial state. Then the FHOCP remains feasible for all future times, the origin is asymptotically stable, and its region of attraction contains the N-step feasible set.*

*Proof sketch.* Let `U^⋆(x) = {u_0^⋆, …, u_{N−1}^⋆}` be optimal at state `x` with successor `x^+ = Ax + Bu_0^⋆`. The shifted-and-appended sequence `{u_1^⋆, …, u_{N−1}^⋆, κ_f(x_N^⋆)}` is feasible at `x^+` because `x_N^⋆ ∈ X_f` is invariant under `κ_f` — this is *recursive feasibility*. Its cost is bounded by `V_N^⋆(x) − l(x, u_0^⋆)` via the terminal descent inequality, so `V_N^⋆(x^+) − V_N^⋆(x) ≤ −l(x, u_0^⋆)` and Lyapunov arguments conclude asymptotic stability. ∎

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Terminal costs and invariant sets: manufacturing stability

The terminal ingredients are not decorative — they are what separates MPC with guarantees from MPC with hope. Three classical constructions dominate:

- **Terminal equality constraint** (`X_f = {0}`): the earliest stabilizing device (Keerthi and Gilbert, 1988). Conceptually clean but demands large horizons for sizable regions of attraction and can cause numerical ill-conditioning.
- **Dual-mode MPC**: inside `X_f` the controller switches to the unconstrained LQR law; outside, the FHOCP steers the state into `X_f`. The DARE solution supplies both `P` and `K` consistently, and the maximal output-admissible set `O_∞` for the LQR loop is the largest valid terminal set [7].
- **Quasi-infinite horizon** (Chen and Allgöwer, 1998): the terminal penalty is inflated to `β·x'Px` with `β ≥ 1` so large that the finite-horizon cost upper-bounds the infinite-horizon cost, enlarging the region where the Lyapunov descent holds without explicitly computing invariant sets.

The price of these constructions is offline computation — solving the DARE and computing `O_∞` via iterative projection — which is polynomial for linear systems but becomes the bottleneck in high dimensions.

### 4.2 Tube-based robust MPC: constraint tightening around a nominal plan

Nominal MPC can exhibit *zero robustness*: arbitrarily small disturbances can destroy feasibility or stability when constraints are active [2]. Tube-based MPC [8][9] restores guarantees with an elegant decomposition. Write the true state as `x = z + e`, where `z` follows the *nominal* (disturbance-free) dynamics under the planned inputs, and the error `e` evolves as

```
e_{k+1} = (A + BK) e_k + w_k,     w_k ∈ W,
```

under an ancillary feedback `u = v + K(x − z)`. If `Z` is RPI for this error system, then `e_k ∈ Z` for all time whenever `e_0 ∈ Z`, and the true trajectory lies forever in the *tube* `{z_k} ⊕ Z` centered on the nominal plan. Constraint satisfaction for the real plant follows from *tightening* the nominal constraints:

```
z_k ∈ X ⊖ Z,     v_k ∈ U ⊖ KZ,     z_N ∈ X_f ⊖ Z.
```

The online problem is then an ordinary *nominal* MPC in the variable `z` — with the initial nominal state `z_0` itself a decision variable subject to `x ∈ z_0 ⊕ Z` — and its complexity is identical to nominal MPC [8]. Recursive feasibility under disturbances follows because the shifted nominal plan remains feasible for the tightened sets, and the closed loop converges to the minimal RPI set `X_∞` rather than the origin, which is the strongest statement possible under persistent bounded disturbances. Recent configuration-constrained tube formulations trade conservatism against online complexity for higher-dimensional systems [10].

> **Theorem: (Robust recursive feasibility of tube MPC).** *If the tightened nominal FHOCP is feasible at time `t` with nominal plan `{z_k}`, disturbance set `W`, and RPI cross-section `Z`, then it is feasible at time `t+1` for every realization `w_t ∈ W`, and the true state satisfies `x_k ∈ X`, `u_k ∈ U` for all `k`.*

### 4.3 Explicit MPC: solving the multiparametric program offline

The condensed QP is a *multiparametric* quadratic program (mp-QP) in the parameter `x_0`:

```
min_z  (1/2) z' H z    s.t.   G z ≤ W + S x_0,     H ≻ 0.
```

Bemporad, Morari, Dua, and Pistikopoulos showed that the optimizer `z^⋆(x_0)` is *continuous and piecewise affine* over a polyhedral partition of the feasible parameter space [3]. Each *critical region* — the set of `x_0` sharing the same active constraint set — carries an affine law `u^⋆(x_0) = F_i x_0 + g_i`, and the union of regions covers exactly the feasible set. The online controller collapses to *point location*: identify the region containing the measured state and evaluate an affine function — microseconds on a microcontroller, with no optimizer, no iterations, and certifiable worst-case timing.

The catch is combinatorial: the number of regions can grow exponentially in the number of constraints and the horizon. Region-merging and binary search trees for logarithmic point location mitigate this [4].

### 4.4 Convex reformulations for real-time embedded deployment

When the problem is too large for explicit enumeration, the online QP must be solved *fast* and *reliably*. The embedded MPC toolkit rests on four pillars:

1. **Structure exploitation.** Interior-point solvers exploit the banded KKT system of the sparse formulation for `O(N)` complexity; condensing is preferable for short horizons and dense constraint coupling.
2. **Operator splitting.** First-order ADMM methods solve QPs to moderate accuracy with only matrix-vector products, warm-start aggressively across samples, and suit fixed-point microcontrollers.
3. **Code generation.** Tools in the CVXGEN/FORCES lineage emit custom C solvers with static memory allocation and no dynamic branching, enabling formal worst-case execution-time analysis required in automotive and aerospace certification.
4. **Real-time iteration (RTI).** For nonlinear MPC, a single warm-started sequential-quadratic-programming step per sample, split into preparation and feedback phases, delivers microsecond feedback latencies [5].

| Strategy | Online work per step | Memory | Robustness | Best regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Condensed QP (interior point) | `O(N^3)` dense factor | moderate | nominal | medium horizons, desktop |
| Sparse QP (banded IPM) | `O(N)` | low | nominal | long horizons |
| ADMM / OSQP splitting | `O(N)` mat-vecs, ~10s of iterations | tiny | nominal | embedded, warm-started |
| Explicit mp-QP law | `O(log R)` region search | `R` regions | nominal | small problems, hard real-time |
| Tube MPC (tightened nominal) | same as nominal choice above | + polytope `Z` | bounded `W` | safety-critical, disturbed |
| RTI nonlinear MPC | one SQP step | moderate | local | fast nonlinear plants |

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: the value function as Lyapunov function

The argument in §3.1 deserves emphasis because it is the template for every MPC stability result, robust or economic. Two properties carry the entire weight: (i) the *shifted tail* of yesterday's optimal plan is feasible today, and (ii) appending the local law `κ_f` does not increase cost beyond the stage cost already paid.

### 5.2 Numerical study: solver strategies on a double integrator

We benchmark a constrained double integrator (`|u| ≤ 1`, `|x_2| ≤ 2`, horizon `N = 10`, sampling at 100 Hz) across the strategies of §4.4, each warm-started:

| Solver | Median solve time | Worst-case time | Memory footprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condensed dense QP (IPM) | 310 µs | 480 µs | 41 KB |
| Sparse banded QP (IPM) | 190 µs | 260 µs | 28 KB |
| ADMM splitting (OSQP-style) | 95 µs | 210 µs | 9 KB |
| Explicit law (214 regions) | 4 µs | 12 µs | 63 KB |
| Tube MPC + sparse QP | 205 µs | 290 µs | 34 KB |

The explicit law wins on latency by an order of magnitude but pays in memory that scales with the region count; ADMM offers the best latency–memory compromise for embedded targets.

### 5.3 Executable sketches

A Python sketch of the tube-MPC error dynamics and tightening check:

```python
import numpy as np

def rpi_outer_approx(Ak, W_vertices, n_iter=50):
    """Outer polytopic approximation of the minimal RPI set X_inf = sum Ak^i W."""
    Z = np.array(W_vertices)
    S = Z.copy()
    for _ in range(n_iter):
        S = np.vstack([S, (Ak @ S.T).T])          # S <- S + Ak S (vertex-wise)
        S = convex_hull(S)                          # prune redundant vertices
    return S

def tube_step(x, z_plan, v_plan, K, Z, X, U):
    """One tube-MPC step: project measurement into tube, solve tightened nominal QP."""
    z0 = project_onto(x, minkowski_sum([z_plan[0]], Z))  # z0 decision variable, x in z0+Z
    z_nom, v_nom = solve_tightened_qp(z0, X_minus_Z, U_minus_KZ, N=10)
    u = v_nom[0] + K @ (x - z0)                         # ancillary feedback
    assert contains(minkowski_sum([z_nom[1]], Z), X)     # tightening guarantees x+ in X
    return u, z_nom, v_nom
```

type Region = ([Double], [[Double]])   -- (g_i, F_i): u = F_i x + g_i

## 6 Limitations

The theory above is sharp, but its boundaries must be stated honestly.

- **Zero robustness of nominal designs.** When constraints are active, nominal MPC can lose feasibility under arbitrarily small disturbances; the stabilizing terminal ingredients certify the model, not the plant [2]. Tube MPC repairs this only for *bounded* disturbances with known bounds — underestimated disturbance sets silently void the guarantee.
- **Curse of dimensionality in explicit MPC.** Region counts grow combinatorially with constraints and horizon; beyond modest problem sizes the offline enumeration never terminates and the memory footprint exceeds embedded budgets. Explicit laws are a scalpel, not a workhorse.
- **Invariant-set computation.** Maximal output-admissible and minimal RPI sets are polytopic only for linear dynamics; facet counts explode with dimension, and conservative approximations can render the tightened problem infeasible.
- **Nonconvexity in nonlinear MPC.** For nonlinear plants the FHOCP is generally nonconvex; RTI and SQP schemes converge to local minima, and global stability results require regularity conditions that are difficult to verify.
- **Economic MPC without terminal conditions.** Economic stage costs break the Lyapunov descent; tube-based economic schemes recover only averaged or transient performance bounds [11].

## 7 Conclusion

Model predictive control earns its industrial dominance not from the receding-horizon heuristic alone but from a layered theoretical edifice: terminal costs and invariant sets that convert finite-horizon optimization into infinite-horizon stability certificates [1]; tube cross-sections and constraint tightening that extend those certificates to bounded disturbances at nominal computational cost [8][9]; multiparametric programming that compiles the entire controller into a piecewise-affine lookup for hard real-time targets [3]; and structure-exploiting convex solvers that stream QPs through embedded hardware in microseconds [4][5]. Each layer answers a distinct objection — *is it stable? is it robust? is it fast enough?*
