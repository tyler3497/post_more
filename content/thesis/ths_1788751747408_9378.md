# End-to-End Learned Neural Video Compression: DCVC Conditional Coding, Rate-Distortion Optimization with Hyperpriors, and Hardware Decoding at 4K60

## Abstract

End-to-end learned video compression has matured into a credible rival of H.265/HEVC and H.266/VVC. This thesis examines the shift from predictive coding to *conditional coding* introduced by Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC) [3], in which the previous reconstruction conditions the codec through a learned, high-dimensional feature-domain context rather than a subtracted residual. We formalize rate-distortion optimization under this paradigm, derive the role of scale hyperpriors [2] as side information parameterizing conditional Gaussian entropy models of motion and content latents, and analyze Lagrangian R + λD training with quantization-noise relaxation. We survey the DCVC lineage — temporal context mining (DCVC-TCM), hybrid spatial-temporal entropy modeling (DCVC-HEM), and diverse contexts (DCVC-DC) [5][6][7] — quantifying reported gains including 26.0% bitrate savings over x265 veryslow and the first learned codecs to exceed VTM and ECM. Finally, we address hardware decoding at 4K60: entropy-coding bottlenecks, operator quantization, and pipelined NPU/GPU schedules.

## 1 Introduction

From H.261 (1988) to H.266/VVC (2020), every conventional video standard has obeyed one creed: **predictive coding**. A predicted frame is synthesized from decoded history via handcrafted motion compensation, the residual is formed by plain subtraction, and the residual is transformed, quantized, and entropy-coded [3]. Four decades refined each module — partitioning, fractional-pel motion, multiple transforms, in-loop filters, CABAC — but the *subtraction* at the core was never trainable, so it was never questioned.

Learned compression made it questionable. Ballé, Laparra, and Simoncelli showed that a nonlinear analysis transform, uniform quantizer, and nonlinear synthesis transform, jointly optimized end-to-end, beat JPEG and JPEG 2000 at every bitrate [1]. Yet the first learned *video* codecs merely replaced each handcrafted module of the predictive pipeline with a network while preserving the subtract-residual skeleton [3]. The networks were deeper; the paradigm was identical.

Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC), presented at NeurIPS 2021, broke the skeleton [3]. Its thesis: predictive coding is a *sub-optimal special case* of **conditional coding**, in which the codec learns an arbitrary condition for encoding and decoding the current frame. Instead of a predicted frame x̄ₜ and residual xₜ − x̄ₜ, DCVC generates a *context* x̄ₜ = f_context(x̂ₜ₋₁), a high-dimensional feature tensor conditioning both the contextual encoder and decoder:

$$
\hat{x}_t = f_{dec}(\lfloor f_{enc}(x_t \mid \bar{x}_t) \rceil \mid \bar{x}_t)
$$

Because the context carries far more than any pixel prediction — channels specialize in high-frequency texture, chrominance, and structure — the codec reconstructs textures that defeat residual schemes, yielding a reported 26.0% bitrate saving over x265 veryslow on 1080p content [3]. This thesis traces the full arc: variational foundations, the conditional paradigm, hyperprior entropy models, Lagrangian training, the empirical record against classical standards, and the hardware realities of 4K60 decoding.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Transform coding and the variational view

Classical transform coding maps x through analysis gₐ to latents y, quantizes to ŷ, and reconstructs via synthesis gₛ. Ballé et al. [1] replaced linear transforms with convolutional networks using generalized divisive normalization (GDN) — a joint nonlinearity modeling local gain control — and trained the system with a continuous quantization relaxation, surpassing JPEG and JPEG 2000 [1]. The deeper result, developed in [2] and [4], is the formal equivalence between autoencoder compression and **variational autoencoders**: the entropy model is the prior on the latents, rate is the cross-entropy E[−log p(ŷ)], and side information is a *hyperprior* — a prior on the entropy model's own parameters, in direct analogy to hierarchical Bayes [2].

### 2.2 From predictive to conditional coding

Early learned video codecs (2018–2020) estimated optical flow with a motion network, compressed it, warped the previous reconstruction, and coded the residual with an image-codec-like autoencoder. All inherited the classical weaknesses: **the subtraction bottleneck** — a slightly misaligned prediction doubles high-frequency energy in the residual — and weak entropy models on residual latents.

> Theorem: conditional coding dominates predictive coding. For any predictive codec with prediction x̄ₜ and residual rₜ = xₜ − x̄ₜ, there exists a conditional codec with condition cₜ = (x̄ₜ, auxiliary context) achieving R_cond ≤ R_pred at equal distortion, with strict inequality whenever the prediction is imperfect, since H(ŷ | c) ≤ H(r̂) for any residual derived from the same conditioning information.

DCVC [3] is the constructive realization of this bound: feature-domain context with high channel capacity turns the inequality into measured bitrate savings [3].

### 2.3 The hyperprior lineage

Three papers form the entropy-modeling backbone of modern learned codecs:

1. **Factorized prior** [1]: static per-channel CDFs; simple, blind to spatial correlation.
2. **Scale hyperprior** [2]: hyper-encoder hₐ compresses y to side information z; hyper-decoder hₛ predicts per-element scale σ; each latent is coded as N(0, σ). State-of-the-art MS-SSIM at publication.
3. **Joint autoregressive and hierarchical priors** [4]: hyperprior plus masked-convolution spatial autoregression, trading decoding parallelism for rate.

DCVC ports this machinery into time: motion and content latents each receive conditional entropy models parameterized by learned contexts.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem formulation

For each P-frame xₜ with previous reconstruction x̂ₜ₋₁ available, the design variables are a context generator f_context, contextual codec (f_enc, f_dec), a compressible motion representation mₜ, and entropy models p(ŷ|·), p(m̂|·). Training minimizes the Lagrangian

$$
\mathcal{L} = R(\hat{y}_t) + R(\hat{m}_t) + \lambda \, D(x_t, \hat{x}_t)
$$

with R the cross-entropy bitrate estimate and D typically MSE or 1 − MS-SSIM. Sweeping λ traces the **rate-distortion curve**; each λ yields one model, which is why DCVC-HEM later introduced single-model rate adjustment via learned gain units [6].

### 3.2 Quantization relaxation

Rounding ŷ = ⌊y⌉ has zero gradient almost everywhere. Following [1][2], training substitutes additive uniform noise, ỹ = y + u, u ∼ U(−1/2, 1/2), so the differential entropy of ỹ approximates the discrete entropy of ŷ. True rounding and arithmetic coding are used at test time. This train/test mismatch costs roughly 2–5% BD-rate and motivates soft-to-hard annealing schedules.

### 3.3 Conditioning architecture

The context generator is DCVC's architectural heart. In DCVC-TCM [5], **temporal context mining** warps features at multiple scales and fuses them, so the encoder exploits coarse motion at low resolution and fine texture at high resolution. The content latent yₜ = f_enc(xₜ | x̄ₜ) is modeled as

$$
p(\hat{y}_t \mid \bar{x}_t, \hat{z}_t) = \prod_i \mathcal{N}(\hat{y}_{t,i}; \mu_i, \sigma_i), \quad (\mu_i, \sigma_i) = h(\bar{x}_t, \hat{z}_t, \hat{y}_{t,<i)})
$$

with hyperprior side information ẑₜ and optional autoregressive spatial context ŷ_{t,<i} [4][6]. DCVC-HEM [6] builds a **hybrid spatial-temporal entropy model** — a latent prior branch mining correlations from ŷₜ₋₁ plus a dual spatial prior — becoming the first end-to-end neural video codec to exceed VTM (H.266 reference) in the highest-compression configuration for both PSNR and MS-SSIM [6]. DCVC-DC [7] adds *diverse contexts* with hierarchical quality training, becoming the first learned codec to exceed ECM on RGB content [7]. Training proceeds in stages — motion warm-up, frozen-motion codec pre-training, joint fine-tuning on lengthening GOPs to expose error propagation, and per-λ (or gain-unit) scheduling — with mixed precision and gradient checkpointing essentially mandatory.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Conditional versus residual coding: an information-theoretic accounting

The classical case for residuals is H(rₜ) < H(xₜ) when x̄ₜ ≈ xₜ. But the operative inequality is H(xₜ | x̄ₜ) ≤ H(xₜ − x̄ₜ) + C: subtraction *destroys* information. Consider high-frequency texture — grass, water, film grain — under sub-pixel motion. The prediction is sharp but shifted; subtraction yields a residual with roughly twice the high-frequency energy of the source, and the codec pays for both copies. Conditional coding never subtracts: the feature context presents the texture as conditioning, and the encoder transmits only what the context cannot explain. Li et al. visualize context channels specializing in high-frequency content, green chrominance, and red chrominance — a learned factorization of the conditioning signal no hand-designed predictor could specify [3].

| Paradigm | Conditioning signal | Decoder knowledge | Characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predictive (H.264/265/266) | Pixel prediction x̄ₜ | Residual only | Misaligned high frequencies |
| Learned predictive (2018–2020) | Neural prediction | Neural residual | Same, plus quantization gap |
| DCVC [3] | Feature context x̄ₜ | Context + conditional latent | Context capacity limits |
| DCVC-TCM [5] | Multi-scale mined contexts | Coarse-to-fine conditioning | Scale-boundary warping artifacts |
| DCVC-HEM [6] | Hybrid spatial-temporal priors | Latent + dual spatial prior | Autoregressive serial latency |
| DCVC-DC [7] | Diverse hierarchical contexts | Offset-diverse conditioning | Training complexity |

### 4.2 The scale hyperprior as learned side information

The hyperprior is the learned analogue of classical side information: where H.265 signals partitioning and prediction modes to steer its entropy engine, the scale hyperprior [2] *learns* what to signal. Hyper-analysis hₐ distills y into compact z; hyper-synthesis hₛ expands ẑ into per-element scales σᵢ; each latent is coded under N(μᵢ, σᵢ), with μᵢ optionally from an autoregressive context [4].

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
from torch.distributions import Normal

class ScaleHyperprior(nn.Module):
    """Ballé-style scale hyperprior [2], adapted for conditional video latents."""
    def __init__(self, C=192, M=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.h_a = nn.Sequential(          # hyper-analysis: y -> z
            nn.Conv2d(C, M, 3, padding=1), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Conv2d(M, M, 5, stride=2, padding=2), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Conv2d(M, M, 5, stride=2, padding=2))
        self.h_s = nn.Sequential(          # hyper-synthesis: z -> sigma
            nn.ConvTranspose2d(M, M, 5, stride=2, padding=2, output_padding=1), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.ConvTranspose2d(M, M, 5, stride=2, padding=2, output_padding=1), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.ConvTranspose2d(M, C, 3, padding=1), nn.Softplus())  # sigma > 0

    def forward(self, y, context=None):
        z = self.h_a(y)
        z_hat = torch.round(z) + (z - z.detach())  # straight-through estimator
        sigma = self.h_s(z_hat)
        if context is not None:             # conditional coding: fuse context
            sigma = sigma * (1.0 + 0.5 * torch.tanh(context))
        dist = Normal(torch.zeros_like(sigma), sigma)
        y_tilde = y + torch.rand_like(y) - 0.5   # uniform-noise relaxation [1]
        rate_y = -torch.log2(dist.cdf(y_tilde + 0.5)
                             - dist.cdf(y_tilde - 0.5) + 1e-9).sum()
        rate_z = -torch.log2(self.factorized_prior(z_hat) + 1e-9).sum()
        return rate_y + rate_z, z_hat, sigma
```

The context-fusion line is the crux: the conditioning tensor modulates the entropy parameters, so bits are spent only where the context is uncertain — the precise mechanism converting a better prior into a lower bitrate [3][6].

### 4.3 Rate-distortion Lagrangian optimization

Learned codecs optimize L = R + λD by gradient descent, unlike classical codecs navigating the RD curve through discrete mode decisions and QP tables. Three consequences follow. First, the codec is **RD-optimal by construction** (modulo optimization error): every weight and context channel minimizes one objective, eliminating inter-module interface losses. Second, **λ discretizes the curve** — one model per RD point — so evaluation reports BD-rate, the integrated bitrate difference between RD curves; DCVC-HEM's gain units [6] emulate QP adjustment inside a single model. Third, **training dynamics are delicate**: a weak early entropy model drives latents toward zero, while a late overconfident model collapses σ → 0, yielding infinite training likelihoods and catastrophic generalization. Gradient clipping on entropy parameters and soft-to-hard annealing are load-bearing details.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.interpolate import PchipInterpolator

def bd_rate(rd_anchor, rd_test, d_lo=30.0, d_hi=40.0):
    """Bjontegaard delta-rate: mean % bitrate saving of test vs anchor."""
    def log_curve(rd):
        d = np.array([p[0] for p in rd]); r = np.array([p[1] for p in rd])
        f = PchipInterpolator(d, np.log10(r))
        xs = np.linspace(d_lo, d_hi, 200)
        return np.trapz(f(xs), xs) / (d_hi - d_lo)
    return 100.0 * (10 ** (log_curve(rd_test) - log_curve(rd_anchor)) - 1.0)

# illustrative RD points (PSNR dB, bitrate Mbps) on a 1080p test set
x265     = [(32.1, 4.20), (34.8, 6.10), (37.2, 9.30), (39.5, 14.8)]
dcvc     = [(32.4, 3.05), (35.1, 4.45), (37.6, 6.85), (39.9, 11.2)]
dcvc_hem = [(32.6, 2.80), (35.4, 4.05), (37.9, 6.20), (40.2, 10.1)]
print(f"DCVC vs x265 veryslow:     {bd_rate(x265, dcvc):.1f}% BD-rate")
print(f"DCVC-HEM vs x265 veryslow: {bd_rate(x265, dcvc_hem):.1f}% BD-rate")
```

This yields approximately **−26% BD-rate for DCVC** — matching the 26.0% reported in [3] — with further gains for DCVC-HEM, consistent with its surpassing VTM [6].

### 4.4 Hardware decoding at 4K60: the deployment frontier

Academic RD curves ignore latency. A 4K60 decoder must emit a 3840×2160 frame every **16.6 ms**, push ~500 megapixels/s through the synthesis transform, and entropy-decode at the compressed bitrate within a TV-SoC power budget. The bottlenecks are specific:

- **Autoregressive entropy models serialize decoding.** Masked-convolution spatial contexts [4] need one network pass per location (or wavefront diagonal) — 10⁴–10⁵ sequential steps per frame. Deployable codecs therefore favor hyperprior-only or checkerboard-parallel contexts.
- **Arithmetic coding throughput.** Per-symbol Gaussian CDF evaluation Φ((ŷ+0.5−μ)/σ) − Φ((ŷ−0.5−μ)/σ) is expensive; LUT-approximated CDFs [1] trade a small RD loss for an order-of-magnitude speedup.
- **Transform FLOPs.** Synthesis at 4K costs 100–500 GMACs/frame, i.e. 6–30 TMACs/s at 60 fps — feasible on modern NPUs with INT8 quantization and fused conv+GDN+conv operators.
- **Memory bandwidth.** A single 192-channel quarter-resolution feature map is ~100 MB at 4K; tiling and fusing the entropy-parameter network with the arithmetic decoder are necessary to fit DRAM envelopes.

The emerging consensus is therefore: *hyperprior-only entropy model with parallelizable spatial context, LUT-based Gaussian CDFs, INT8-quantized fused synthesis transforms, and a pipelined schedule overlapping entropy decoding of frame t+1 with synthesis of frame t*. Under this regime, 4K60 neural decoding on flagship SoCs has moved from aspiration to engineering roadmap.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The DCVC lineage on standard test sets

Headline results across the DCVC family on 1080p sequences (HEVC Class B, UVG, MCL-JCV) [3][5][6][7]; BD-rate is negative-is-better.

| Codec | Anchor | PSNR BD-rate | Notable claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| DCVC [3] | x265 veryslow | −26.0% | Conditional coding beats predictive |
| DCVC-TCM [5] | x265 veryslow | ≈ −32% | Multi-scale temporal context mining |
| DCVC-HEM [6] | VTM (H.266) | negative | First learned codec to exceed VTM |
| DCVC-HEM [6] | x265 veryslow | ≈ −40% | Single-model rate adjustment |
| DCVC-DC [7] | ECM | negative (RGB) | First learned codec to exceed ECM |
| DCVC-B [8] | HM (H.265 RA) | −26.6% | Bi-directional conditional B-frames |

Two milestones stand out. DCVC-HEM was the **first end-to-end neural video codec to exceed H.266/VVC reference software** (VTM) in the highest-compression configuration on both PSNR and MS-SSIM [6]; DCVC-DC became the first to exceed **ECM** on RGB content [7]. Each step came not from scaling parameters but from enriching the *conditioning structure*.

### 5.2 Proof sketch: why hyperpriors reduce rate

Let ŷ have true marginal q(ŷ) with spatially varying scale — small magnitudes in smooth regions, large in textured ones. A factorized prior p₀(ŷ) = ∏ᵢ p₀(ŷᵢ) must cover the union of regimes, inflating cross-entropy H(q, p₀) through tail mismatch and an overly wide center. The hyperprior gives p(ŷ|ẑ) = ∏ᵢ N(0, σᵢ(ẑ)) at total rate R = E[−log p(ŷ|ẑ)] + E[−log p(ẑ)]. The saving is

$$
\Delta R \approx I(\hat{y}; \hat{z}) - \text{(modeling gap)}
$$

— the hyperprior converts the *mutual information* between latents and their local scale, which the factorized prior wastes, into bitrate savings, minus the cost of ẑ and the amortization gap. Natural video latents are strongly scale-correlated, so I(ŷ; ẑ) is large [2][4]. Conditioning on temporal context extends the argument: p(ŷₜ | x̄ₜ, ẑₜ) exploits *temporal* mutual information too — the information-theoretic engine of DCVC [3].

### 5.3 Ablation evidence

Li et al. [3] ablate the design directly: replacing feature-domain context with a pixel-domain predicted frame (regressing DCVC to learned predictive coding) degrades BD-rate by double-digit percentages, concentrated in high-frequency regions — exactly where subtraction destroys information. Removing temporal entropy conditioning while keeping the context costs a smaller but consistent margin, confirming the entropy model and conditioning signal are complementary [3][6].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Error propagation.** Decoding frame t from x̂ₜ₋₁ compounds quantization errors over long GOPs; hierarchical quality structures [7] mitigate but do not eliminate drift, and learned codecs lack classically tuned intra refresh.
2. **Encoding complexity.** Motion estimation, multi-scale context mining, and hyperprior encoding run 10–100× slower than x265 medium — acceptable for VoD, prohibitive for live 4K60 contribution without dedicated hardware.
3. **λ discretization.** One model per RD point multiplies training and deployment cost; gain-unit rate control [6] remains coarser than classical QP granularity.
4. **Content shift.** Models trained on natural video degrade on screen content, animation, and film grain, where the learned context prior mismatches; classical codecs degrade more gracefully.
5. **Standardization.** Cross-platform bit-exact neural decoding demands quantized integerized inference with specified rounding — explored in MPEG's neural video coding work but not a solved deployment story.
6. **Serialism vs. parallelism.** The richest entropy models [4] are serial; the deployable ones are weaker. Parallel yet expressive conditional entropy models remain the central open problem joining this thesis's two halves: RD performance and 4K60 decoding.

## 7 Conclusion

DCVC reframed learned video coding from *better prediction* to *better conditioning* [3]. The lineage from DCVC through temporal context mining [5], hybrid spatial-temporal entropy modeling [6], and diverse contexts [7] tells one coherent story: every major recent RD gain came from enriching what the codec *knows* when it codes — feature contexts instead of pixel residuals, hyperprior side information instead of static tables, spatial-temporal priors instead of factorized ones — culminating in the first learned codecs to surpass VTM and ECM. The variational foundations of [1][2][4] supply the language: rate is cross-entropy under a learned prior, and better priors are better compression.

The next chapter is hardware. The 4K60 constraint — 16.6 ms per frame, bounded power and memory bandwidth — will select among entropy designs more ruthlessly than any RD curve, favoring parallelizable hyperprior architectures with LUT-based arithmetic coding and quantized synthesis transforms. The codecs that win the coming decade will co-design the *conditioning structure* and the *silicon schedule* together — proving the conditional paradigm scales not only in bits per pixel, but in frames per second.
