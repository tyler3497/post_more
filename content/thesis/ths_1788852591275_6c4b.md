# Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models: A Unified Theoretical Treatment of Score-Based SDEs, Flow Matching, Consistency Distillation, and Classifier-Free Guidance Dynamics

## Abstract

Diffusion models rest on a small set of primitives — noising chains, SDEs, learned vector fields, self-consistent trajectory maps — yet the literature treats their modern variants as distinct families. This thesis unifies *denoising diffusion probabilistic models* (DDPMs) with four principal modernizations: score-based SDEs, flow matching with optimal-transport paths, consistency-model distillation for one-step synthesis, and classifier-free guidance as a dynamical modification of the score field. We derive the noise-prediction/score identities linking DDPM training to the reverse-time SDE; prove the gradient equivalence of conditional and marginal flow-matching objectives; analyze the fixed-point consistency property and its distillation convergence; and characterize classifier-free guidance as an implicit classifier whose extrapolation sharpens the model distribution at the cost of manifold fidelity. We consolidate CIFAR-10 and ImageNet benchmarks, provide reference sampler implementations, and identify open gaps in few-step generation and guidance calibration.

---

## 1 Introduction

A model that can *remove* noise can also *create* data. Diffusion models operationalize this by defining a forward process that destroys signal and training a network to invert it. The denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) of Ho, Jain, and Abbeel [1] turned the elegant but uncompetitive diffusion models of Sohl-Dickstein et al. into a practical generator by re-parameterizing the variational bound as a noise-prediction objective conditioned on the noise level.

Four generalizations of this discrete Markov construction are unified here:

1. **Score-based SDEs** [2] embed the discrete diffusion chain in continuous time, replacing the Markov chain with a forward stochastic differential equation (SDE) and deriving a *reverse-time SDE* whose drift depends on the score function $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$. The deterministic *probability flow ODE* shares the same marginals and enables exact likelihood computation.
2. **Flow matching** [4] abandons the diffusion path entirely and trains continuous normalizing flows by regressing analytically tractable *conditional vector fields*, with optimal-transport (OT) displacement interpolation producing near-straight probability paths that need far fewer solver steps.
3. **Consistency models** [5] enforce *self-consistency* along the probability-flow ODE trajectory, distilling a multi-step teacher into a network that maps any point on a trajectory directly to its origin, yielding one-step generation.
4. **Classifier-free guidance** [6] jointly trains conditional and unconditional score estimates and extrapolates their difference at inference, trading sample diversity for fidelity to the condition without any auxiliary classifier.

These four threads are not independent: noise prediction *is* score estimation up to rescaling; the probability flow ODE is the shared deterministic backbone of DDIM sampling, flow matching, and consistency distillation; and guidance is a modification of the vector field driving any of these samplers. This thesis makes these connections precise, proves the core equivalences, and analyzes the characteristic failure modes — oversaturation, mode dropping, off-manifold trajectories, and distillation ceilings.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Denoising diffusion probabilistic models

A DDPM [1] defines a forward noising Markov chain over $T$ steps:

$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\, \beta_t \mathbf{I}\right), \qquad \beta_t \in (0,1).$$

With $\alpha_t = 1-\beta_t$ and $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s$, the marginal is closed-form:

$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\, (1-\bar\alpha_t)\mathbf{I}\right),$$

so $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$ is sampled directly. A learned reverse process $p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$ is fit by maximizing a variational lower bound on $\log p_\theta(x_0)$; up to weighting, the bound reduces to *denoising* — predicting the injected noise:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}\left[\left\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\left(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\, t\right)\right\|^2\right].$$

Sampling iterates $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$ backward through $p_\theta$: Inception score 9.46 and FID 3.17 on unconditional CIFAR-10 (state of the art at the time), but at the cost of the full $T=1000$ chain — roughly 20 GPU-hours per 50k images [3].

### 2.2 Score matching and noise-conditional score networks

An alternative lineage begins with *score matching*: modeling the *score* $\nabla_x \log p(x)$ rather than the density. Denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011) is equivalent to explicit score matching, so a denoiser implicitly learns scores; Song and Ermon (2019) scaled this to noise-conditional score networks $s_\theta(x,\sigma)$ sampled with annealed Langevin dynamics:

$$x_{i+1} = x_i + \frac{\gamma}{2} s_\theta(x_i, \sigma_j) + \sqrt{\gamma}\,z, \qquad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}).$$

The DDPM chain and the annealed Langevin chain are two discretizations of one idea — which the score-based SDE framework [2] makes continuous.

### 2.3 Key terminology

| Concept | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Score | $s(x,t) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ | Steepest-ascent direction of the perturbed log-density |
| Noise schedule | $\beta_t$, $\bar\alpha_t$ | Variance schedule controlling forward corruption |
| Probability flow ODE | $\dot x = f - \tfrac{1}{2}g^2 s_\theta$ | Deterministic flow sharing the SDE's marginals |
| Guidance scale | $w$ | Extrapolation strength in classifier-free guidance |
| NFE | — | Number of function evaluations per sample |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The score-based SDE framework

Song et al. [2] unify DDPMs and score-based models by writing the forward perturbation as an Itô SDE on $t \in [0, T]$:

$$dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw, \tag{1}$$

where $w$ is a standard Wiener process, $f$ the drift, and $g$ the diffusion coefficient. Two canonical instantiations recover the discrete families:

- **Variance Exploding (VE):** $f \equiv 0$, $g(t) = \sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}$ — continuous NCSN annealing.
- **Variance Preserving (VP):** $f(x,t) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)x$, $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$ — continuous DDPM chain, preserving marginal variance.

Time reversal of diffusions (Anderson) gives the *reverse-time SDE*:

$$dx = \left[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right]dt + g(t)\,d\bar w, \tag{2}$$

where $\bar w$ runs backward in time. Generation thus reduces to *score estimation*: one network $s_\theta(x,t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ trained by denoising score matching,

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,T)}\mathbb{E}_{x_0 \sim p_{\text{data}}}\mathbb{E}_{x_t \sim p_t(\cdot \mid x_0)}\left[\lambda(t)\,\left\|s_\theta(x_t,t) - \nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t \mid x_0)\right\|^2\right],$$

suffices to simulate (2) backward from $p_T$ to $p_0 \approx p_{\text{data}}$. The Gaussian perturbation kernel has a closed-form score — e.g. $\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t\mid x_0) = -(x_t - x_0)/\sigma_t^2$ for VE — making training simulation-free.

Song et al. further derive the *probability flow ODE*, a deterministic process with identical marginals $p_t$:

$$dx = \left[f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right]dt. \tag{3}$$

This is the workhorse of modern fast sampling: DDIM [3], flow matching inference [4], and consistency distillation [5] are all, at bottom, different ways to integrate (3).

> **Theorem (Marginal equivalence of the probability flow ODE).** *Under mild regularity conditions on $f$, $g$, and $p_t$, the solution of the probability flow ODE (3) initialized at $x_T \sim p_T$ induces the same family of marginals $\{p_t\}_{t \in [0,T]}$ as the forward SDE (1). In particular, sampling (3) backward from $t=T$ to $t=0$ yields $x_0 \sim p_0$.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* The Fokker–Planck equation for (1) can be rewritten, via $\Delta p_t = \nabla\!\cdot\!(p_t\nabla\log p_t)$, as the continuity equation $\partial_t p_t = -\nabla\!\cdot\!(\tilde f p_t)$ with $\tilde f = f - \tfrac{1}{2}g^2\nabla\log p_t$. The ODE $\dot x = \tilde f(x,t)$ realizes exactly this transport, hence shares the marginals. ∎

### 3.2 Flow matching

Flow matching [4] trains continuous normalizing flows by fixing a *probability path* $p_t$ between noise $p_0$ and data $p_1$, generated by a vector field $u_t$. The marginal field $u_t$ is intractable, but *conditional* probability paths are tractable: for each data point $x_1$, define $p_t(x \mid x_1) = \mathcal{N}(x; t x_1, (1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t)^2 \mathbf{I})$ — the optimal-transport displacement interpolation — with conditional vector field $u_t(x \mid x_1) = (x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})x)/(1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t)$. The conditional flow matching loss

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1),\, x_1 \sim p_{\text{data}},\, x \sim p_t(\cdot \mid x_1)}\left[\left\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x \mid x_1)\right\|^2\right]$$

is simulation-free. Diffusion probability paths are a special case of this framework, so flow matching with diffusion paths is a strictly more stable alternative to DDPM training [4]. Rectified-flow OT paths use straight interpolants $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1$ with constant conditional velocity $x_1 - x_0$; inference is forward Euler integration in as few as 10–50 steps.

### 3.3 Consistency models

Consistency models [5] exploit the determinism of the probability flow ODE: the ODE defines a *trajectory* $\{x_t\}_{t \in [\epsilon, T]}$ from noise to data, and the *consistency function* $f(x_t, t) \mapsto x_\epsilon$ maps every point on the trajectory to its origin. A consistency model parameterizes $f_\theta$ and enforces the *self-consistency property*

$$f_\theta(x_t, t) = f_\theta(x_s, s) \qquad \forall\, t, s \text{ on the same trajectory},$$

with boundary condition $f_\theta(x_\epsilon, \epsilon) = x_\epsilon$. Two training regimes exist:

- **Consistency distillation (CD):** a pre-trained diffusion model's ODE solver yields adjacent trajectory pairs $(x_{t_{n+1}}, \hat x_{t_n}^\phi)$; the student minimizes $d(f_\theta(x_{t_{n+1}}, t_{n+1}), f_{\theta^-}(\hat x_{t_n}^\phi, t_n))$ against an EMA target $\theta^-$.
- **Consistency training (CT):** the same objective estimated from data alone, using unbiased one-step score estimates from the perturbation kernel.

One-step generation is $x \approx f_\theta(x_T, T)$; multistep sampling alternates denoising jumps with re-noising to trade compute for quality. Reported one-step FID: 3.55 on CIFAR-10 and 6.20 on ImageNet $64\times64$ [5].

### 3.4 Classifier-free guidance

Classifier guidance perturbs the reverse process with the gradient of a separately trained noisy-image classifier $\nabla_{x_t}\log p_\phi(c \mid x_t)$. Ho and Salimans [6] remove the classifier: one network is trained on both conditional and unconditional objectives by randomly replacing $c$ with a null token $\varnothing$ (10–20% dropout), then extrapolating at inference:

$$\tilde\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\left(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\right), \tag{4}$$

with guidance scale $w \geq 1$. Since $\epsilon_\theta \propto -\sigma_t\nabla\log p$, (4) equals the guided score

$$\tilde s(x_t, c) = s_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\left(s_\theta(x_t, c) - s_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\right).$$

By Bayes' rule, $s_\theta(x_t, c) - s_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) = \nabla_{x_t}\log p(c \mid x_t)$: the gradient of an *implicit* classifier — guidance without training one.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The noise-prediction/score identity

The first unification is algebraic. From $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$, the Gaussian perturbation score is

$$\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t \mid x_0) = -\frac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0}{1-\bar\alpha_t} = -\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}.$$

so noise prediction and score estimation differ only by the deterministic rescaling

$$s_\theta(x_t, t) = -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}.$$

The DDPM objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$ is therefore *literally* a weighted denoising score matching loss with weight $\lambda(t) = 1-\bar\alpha_t$. This is why a DDPM checkpoint can be reused, without retraining, inside the reverse-time SDE (2), the DDIM update [3], and the EDM sampler [7] — same weights, different integrators. The $\epsilon$-parameterization is also better conditioned than predicting $x_0$ directly, and its induced weighting emphasizes mid-range noise levels where compositional structure is decided.

### 4.2 Reverse SDE, probability flow ODE, and predictor–corrector samplers

Euler–Maruyama discretization of (2) gives the ancestral sampler, but *predictor–corrector* (PC) schemes [2] add Langevin corrector steps after each solver step, pushing samples back toward the true marginal $p_t$ and outperforming pure reverse-SDE integration at fixed NFE.

The deterministic special case is DDIM [3]: Song, Meng, and Ermon show that a family of *non-Markovian* forward processes shares exactly the DDPM training objective, so a network trained as a DDPM can be sampled with the deterministic update

$$x_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\underbrace{\left(\frac{x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}}\right)}_{\text{predicted } x_0} + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t) + \sigma_t z.$$

Setting $\sigma_t = 0$ gives a deterministic sampler — 10–50× faster than DDPM with minor quality loss — while a specific $\sigma_t$ schedule recovers DDPM exactly. DDIM thus demonstrates that the *training objective* and the *sampling process* are separable modules, a separation the EDM design space [7] later systematizes into independent choices (schedule, parameterization, preconditioning, sampler).

```python
# Reverse-SDE sampling with classifier-free guidance (PyTorch-like pseudocode)
def sample_reverse_sde(score_net, cond, w, timesteps, sigma):
    x = torch.randn(batch, *shape) * sigma[-1]
    for t_now, t_prev in zip(timesteps[:-1], timesteps[1:]):
        dt = t_prev - t_now  # negative: integrating backward
        s_uncond = score_net(x, t_now, cond=None)
        s_cond   = score_net(x, t_now, cond=cond)
        s_guided = s_uncond + w * (s_cond - s_uncond)
        drift = -0.5 * beta(t_now) * x - beta(t_now) * s_guided
        diffusion = math.sqrt(beta(t_now))
        x = x + drift * dt + diffusion * math.sqrt(-dt) * torch.randn_like(x)
    return x
```

### 4.3 Optimal-transport paths and straightened flows

The DDPM/VP forward process has *curved* probability paths, forcing ODE solvers through high-curvature regions with many small steps. Flow matching's OT displacement interpolation [4] replaces these with (near-)straight conditional paths $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1$, inducing a smoother, lower-curvature marginal field — hence competitive ImageNet FID at an order of magnitude fewer NFE. Rectified flow goes further by *reflowing*: re-training on synthetic pairs $(x_0, x_1)$ from the learned flow provably straightens trajectories (reduced transport cost), often to near-single-step sufficiency.

### 4.4 Self-consistency and one-step distillation dynamics

The consistency property $f_\theta(x_t,t) = f_\theta(x_s,s)$ demands network invariance under the flow's own time evolution. Distillation minimizes adjacent-trajectory-point discrepancy; with a teacher solver of local error $O(\Delta t^{p+1})$, target error accumulates as $O(\Delta t^p)$ over $N$ points. As $N \to \infty$ the CD minimizer converges to the teacher ODE's true consistency function [5] — which is why CD inherits teacher quality while collapsing $N$ evaluations into one.

> **Theorem (Consistency distillation convergence, informal).** *Let $f^\star$ be the consistency function of the probability-flow ODE induced by a fixed score model, and let $f_{\theta_N}$ minimize the consistency-distillation loss on an $N$-point time discretization with an exact ODE solver. Then under Lipschitz regularity of $f^\star$, $\|f_{\theta_N} - f^\star\| \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$, and one-step samples $f_{\theta_N}(x_T, T)$ converge in distribution to the teacher's samples.*

Finite $N$ and limited student capacity create the *distillation ceiling*: a one-step model cannot exceed its teacher, and $1$–$4$ NFE regimes blur high-frequency detail. Multistep consistency sampling — alternating $f_\theta$ jumps with re-noising — partially refunds the gap, recovering a smooth compute-quality trade-off.

### 4.5 Guidance dynamics: sharpened densities, saturation, and manifold corrections

Guidance scale $w$ is a *temperature dial* on the implicit classifier:

$$\tilde p(x \mid c) \propto p(x)\, p(c \mid x)^w,$$

$w > 1$ sharpens $\tilde p(x \mid c)$ around modes of $p(c \mid x)$ — better prompt adherence — while $w < 1$ flattens toward the prior. Text-to-image systems use $w \in [5,30]$, far outside the $w=1$ regime where (4) is a valid score of a normalized density. This extrapolation causes two canonical pathologies:

1. **Oversaturation.** The extrapolated field exceeds any true score's magnitude, driving pixel values to extremes and saturating color channels.
2. **Off-manifold drift and mode collapse.** For $w \neq 1$ the field need not be a gradient at all, so integration can leave the data manifold while mass concentrates on a few high-$p(c\mid x)$ modes.

This motivates *manifold-constrained* guidance: CFG++ [8] uses the guided prediction for denoising but the *unconditional* prediction for renoising, keeping latents on-manifold and enabling effective guidance at small scales $0 < \lambda < 1$, with smooth coarse-to-fine transitions where standard CFG saturates early. The EDM framework [7] adds a complementary lever — preconditioning and schedule design — recovering much of guidance's gain from better samplers at $w=1$.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Benchmark evidence

Three quantitative lessons stand out. **First**, sampler design dominates: Karras et al. [7] improve a *pre-trained* ImageNet-64 model from FID 2.07 to 1.55 by changing only sampler and preconditioning (1.36 after retraining) — much of "model progress" was integration error. **Second**, straight paths pay: OT flow matching matches or beats diffusion-path training on ImageNet in likelihood and quality with off-the-shelf ODE solvers [4]. **Third**, distillation has a measured cost: one-step consistency FID 3.55 on CIFAR-10 versus teacher-class EDM 1.97 [5, 7] — a ~1.5-point tax for collapsing $35+$ NFE into one, partially refunded by multistep sampling.

### 5.2 The gradient-equivalence theorem of flow matching

> **Theorem (Lipman et al. [4]).** *Let $p_t(x) = \int p_t(x \mid x_1) q(x_1)\,dx_1$ be a marginal probability path with generating field $u_t$, and let $u_t(\cdot \mid x_1)$ generate the conditional path $p_t(\cdot \mid x_1)$. Then for any parameterized field $v_\theta$,*
>
> $$\nabla_\theta\, \mathbb{E}_{t,x \sim p_t}\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x)\|^2 = \nabla_\theta\, \mathbb{E}_{t,x_1 \sim q,x \sim p_t(\cdot \mid x_1)}\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x \mid x_1)\|^2.$$
>
> *That is, the intractable marginal flow-matching objective and the tractable conditional objective have identical gradients.*

*Proof sketch.* Expanding $\|v_\theta - u_t\|^2$ and using the marginal-field identity $u_t(x) = \mathbb{E}_{x_1 \sim p_t(x_1 \mid x)}[u_t(x \mid x_1)]$, the cross term $\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_t}\langle v_\theta, u_t\rangle$ equals its conditional counterpart by the tower property; the remaining terms are $\theta$-independent or coincide by total expectation. ∎

This theorem is the entire practical basis of flow matching: it licenses training on analytically known conditional vector fields — straight lines, OT interpolants, diffusion paths — with the guarantee that the learned $v_\theta$ approximates the true marginal transport field.

### 5.3 Guidance as an implicit classifier: proof of the identity

The Bayesian identity underlying classifier-free guidance [6]:

$$s_\theta(x_t, c) - s_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) \approx \nabla_{x_t}\left[\log p_t(x_t \mid c) - \log p_t(x_t)\right] = \nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(c \mid x_t),$$

since $\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(c) = 0$. The guided update (4) is thus *classifier guidance with the classifier gradient replaced by the difference of two score estimates* — no auxiliary model, at the cost of two evaluations per step.

## 6 Limitations

1. **The few-step fidelity gap.** Every acceleration method pays a tax: DDIM at 10 steps, one-step consistency models, and single-step rectified flows all trail their multi-step teachers in fine detail and texture fidelity. The tax shrinks with better paths and distillation but has not been eliminated; there is no proof that one-step non-adversarial generation can match iterative refinement in the worst case.
2. **Guidance miscalibration.** The theoretically clean regime ($w = 1$) is rarely used in practice, and the effective regime ($w \in [5,30]$) extrapolates beyond any valid score field, producing saturation, mode dropping, and diversity collapse. Automatic, per-sample or per-timestep guidance scheduling remains heuristic, and manifold-constrained variants [8] add sampler complexity.
3. **Distillation ceilings and teacher dependence.** Consistency distillation cannot surpass its teacher, so one-step quality is bottlenecked by the best available multi-step model; consistency training from scratch avoids the teacher but converges more slowly and currently lags CD in reported FID [5].
4. **Discretization and solver sensitivity.** The framework rests on numerical integration of *learned, imperfect* vector fields, for which solver error analysis is far less developed than for exact fields; stiff low-noise dynamics interact with schedule and solver order in ways still largely empirical [7].
5. **Likelihood versus perceptual quality.** The probability flow ODE enables exact likelihood, but likelihood and FID famously disagree, and guidance — the main quality lever — actively distorts the likelihood. No unified objective jointly optimizes both.

## 7 Conclusion

DDPMs began as a discrete Markov chain with a simplified variational bound [1]; they are now one point in a design space spanned by vector fields, probability paths, and trajectory maps. The score-based SDE framework [2] supplied the continuous-time foundation; the probability flow ODE became the deterministic backbone of DDIM [3], flow matching [4], and consistency models [5]; classifier-free guidance [6] steered conditioning by extrapolating scores instead of training classifiers; and design-space studies [7] showed sampler and preconditioning choices rival architectural ones.

The unifying thread is the *vector field*: noise predictor, score network, flow velocity, or consistency map — every method trains a neural approximation to a transport field and integrates it to move mass from noise to data. The differences are choices about *which* field to learn and *how* to integrate it. Future progress will likely come from better geometry (lower-curvature, lower-cost paths), manifold-respecting calibrated guidance, and a theory of few-step generation that closes the gap between one evaluation and one thousand.
