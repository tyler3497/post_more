# Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Under Partial Observability: Dec-POMDP Complexity, Centralized Training Decentralized Execution, and Mean Field Approximations

## Abstract

Decentralized partially observable Markov decision processes capture cooperative multi-agent learning under stochastic dynamics and private observations, with finite-horizon solutions provably NEXP-complete. This thesis dissects the structural hardness of Dec-POMDPs, the pragmatic compromise of Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE), and scalable mean-field approximations for large populations. We formalize belief hierarchies, value factorization under Individual-Global-Max consistency, and mean-field Nash Q-learning with Taylor expansions. Through analysis of QMIX, MAPPO, MADDPG, and AERIAL, we show how centralized critics mitigate non-stationarity while monotonic mixing and attention-based recurrence preserve decentralized tractability. Empirical and theoretical evidence on SMAC, MessySMAC, and Ising models demonstrates tradeoffs between expressiveness, sample efficiency, and robustness to stochastic partial observability.

---

## 1 Introduction

**Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL)** under partial observability is not a straightforward extension of single-agent RL; it is a fundamentally distinct computational regime. In real-world fleets, sensor networks, and economic markets, agents act on *private histories* $\tau_i = (o_i^0,a_i^0,...,o_i^t)$ while optimizing a joint return that depends on unobserved global state and joint actions.

*Why does this matter?* Three forces collide:

1. **Decentralized information** — no agent sees $s_t$; belief $b_t(s)=Pr(s_t|h_t)$ must be inferred from local observation histories.
2. **Strategic coupling** — optimal $a_i$ depends on $\pi_{-i}$, creating non-stationarity during learning.
3. **Combinatorial joint spaces** — $|\mathbf{A}| = \prod_i |\mathcal{A}_i|$ and $|\mathbf{O}|$ grow exponentially in $N$.

Classical planning formalizes this as a **Dec-POMDP** $\langle \mathcal{D},\mathcal{S},\bm{\mathcal{A}},\bm{\mathcal{O}},T,R,O,b_0,h\rangle$ [1][2]. Yet Bernstein et al. proved finite-horizon Dec-POMDPs are **NEXP-complete** [3], separating them from PSPACE-complete POMDPs and NP-complete MDPs. The implication: *doubly exponential* time may be required in worst case, and no polynomial-time reduction to centralized planning exists.

This thesis synthesizes three research threads that make progress despite hardness:

- **Complexity theory** that explains *why* decentralized control is hard: lack of sufficient statistics for others' policies.
- **CTDE paradigms** — QMIX [4], MAPPO [5], MADDPG [6] — that exploit simulator access to centralized state during training while executing decentralized policies.
- **Mean-field approximations** [7] that replace pairwise interactions by an **averaged effective agent** when $N \gg 10$.

We argue these are not competing heuristics but layers of a complexity-aware design stack.

> Theorem: **(Bernstein, Immerman, Zilberstein 2000/2002)** The finite-horizon Dec-POMDP policy existence problem — does there exist joint policy achieving value $\geq K$? — is NEXP-complete. Even $\epsilon$-approximate solutions remain NEXP-hard.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Dec-POMDP and POSG Formalism

A Dec-POMDP is a cooperative special case of a Partially Observable Stochastic Game (POSG) [8][9]. Components:

- $\mathcal{D} = \{1..n\}$ agents
- $\mathcal{S}$ finite state set
- $\bm{\mathcal{A}} = \times_i \mathcal{A}_i$, $\bm{\mathcal{O}} = \times_i \mathcal{O}_i$
- $T(s'|s,\mathbf{a}) = Pr(s_{t+1}|s_t,\mathbf{a}_t)$
- $O(\mathbf{o}|\mathbf{a},s') = Pr(\mathbf{o}_{t+1}|\mathbf{a}_t,s_{t+1})$
- $R(s,\mathbf{a})$ team reward
- Horizon $h$, discount $\gamma \in [0,1)$

Each agent executes $\pi_i : \bar{\mathcal{O}}_i \to \Delta(\mathcal{A}_i)$ mapping *action-observation histories* AOH to actions. Joint value: $V^{\bm{\pi}}(b_0)=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}\gamma^t R(s_t,\mathbf{a}_t)|b_0,\bm{\pi}]$.

*No Markovian belief sufficient statistic exists for decentralized execution* because agent $i$'s optimal AOH policy depends on others' internal beliefs.

GFM contrast:

| Model | Observability | Control | Complexity (finite-h) |
|-------|---------------|---------|----------------------|
| MDP | Full | Centralized | P-complete |
| POMDP | Partial | Centralized | PSPACE-complete |
| Dec-POMDP | Partial + decentralized | Decentralized | **NEXP-complete** [3] |
| MMDP | Full | Dec but jointly observed | NP-hard |

### 2.2 Centralized Training Decentralized Execution

CTDE assumes during *training* access to $s_t$, $\mathbf{o}_t$, $\mathbf{a}_t$, additional simulator factorization, yet restricts execution to $\pi_i(o_i)$ or $\pi_i(\tau_i)$ [4][5]. Motivation from Oliehoek & Amato [2]:

- Simulation in lab/cloud permits unlimited communication.
- Deployment on robots/UAVs has bandwidth/latency constraints.
- Centralized critic can reduce variance of policy gradients while preserving execution independence.

Two families:

- *Value decomposition*: VDN, QMIX, QPLEX enforce **IGM** $\arg\max_{\mathbf{a}} Q_{tot}(\bm{\tau},\mathbf{a}) = (\arg\max_{a_i} Q_i(\tau_i,a_i))_i$
- *Actor-critic*: COMA, MADDPG, MAPPO learn centralized $V(s)$ or $Q(s,\mathbf{a})$, decentralized $\pi_i$.

### 2.3 Mean Field Limit

When $N \to \infty$, pairwise $Q_i(s,a_i,a_{-i})$ intractable. Mean-field replaces neighborhood action set by mean action $\bar{a}_i = \frac{1}{|N(i)|}\sum_{j \in N(i)} a_j$ [7]:

$$Q_i(s,a_i,\mathbf{a}_{-i}) \approx Q_i(s,a_i,\bar{a}_i) \approx Q_i(s,a_i,\bar{a})$$

where Taylor expansion justifies second-order remainder $O(1/|N(i)|)$. This yields *mean-field Nash Q-learning* with fixed-point iteration.

def mappo_update(batch, pi, V_central, eps=0.2):
    s, obs, acts, adv, ret = batch  # s global, obs local tuple
    ratio = (pi.new_logprob(obs, acts) / pi.old_logprob(obs, acts)).exp()
    # centralized value baseline reduces variance
    pg_loss = -torch.min(ratio * adv,
                          torch.clamp(ratio, 1-eps, 1+eps) * adv).mean()
    v_loss = ((V_central(s) - ret)**2).mean()
    return pg_loss + 0.5*v_loss

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Dec-POMDP Formalism and NEXP-Completeness

Why is Dec-POMDP NEXP-complete rather than PSPACE? Intuition from Bernstein et al. construction [3]:

Consider TILING problem with $2^n \times 2^n$ grid requiring nondeterministic exponential time to check. Reduction builds Dec-POMDP where:

- Two agents each guess one $n$-bit coordinate of a tile inconsistency witness.
- Observations reveal only local bits, never joint coordinate.
- Reward $>0$ iff agents jointly guess consistent tiling violation.

Because histories are private, agents must encode exponential information in their finite-state controllers. Formally, joint policy tree size is doubly exponential: each agent has $|\mathcal{A}_i|^{ (|\mathcal{O}_i|^{h}) }$ possible deterministic policies due to observation branching. Enumerating joint policies is in NEXP; hardness via encoding NEXP Turing machine acceptance as Dec-POMDP optimal value $\geq K$.

Consequences:

- **Undecidability at infinite horizon**: with $\gamma <1$, existence of optimal stationary policy undecidable.
- **Approximation hardness**: Rabinovich et al. 2003 show even $\epsilon$-approximation NEXP-hard [1].
- **No belief compression lossless**: Unlike POMDP belief $b_t \in \Delta(\mathcal{S})$ sufficient, Dec-POMDP requires *infinite hierarchical beliefs*: $b_i$ over $s$ and $b_{-i}$ and $b_i$ over $b_{-i}$ over $b_i$...

> Theorem: **Belief hierarchy blow-up** For Dec-POMDP, any statistic sufficient for optimal decision is at least as large as the set of other agents' AOH distributions, which grows as $\Omega(|\mathcal{O}_i|^h)$. No polynomial compression preserves losslessness in general.

Practically, this explains why memoryless $\pi_i(o_i)$ fails drastically in Dec-Tiger coordination task: without memory, coordination requires communication channel.

### 4.2 Centralized Training Decentralized Execution Tradeoffs

CTDE algorithmic map:

- **Stage 1**: Train in simulator with global state $s$ and joint trajectories.
- **Stage 2**: Extract $\pi_i(\tau_i)$ where $\tau_i$ local AOH (or $o_i$ if Markovian encoder suffices).

**MADDPG** [6] (Lowe et al.): N deterministic actors $\mu_i(o_i)$, N critics $Q_i(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}_{1..N})$ where $\mathbf{x} = (o_1..o_N)$ or true state. Gradient:

$$\nabla_{\theta_i} J = \mathbb{E}[ \nabla_{\theta_i}\mu_i(o_i) \nabla_{a_i} Q_i(\mathbf{x},a_1..a_N)|_{a_i=\mu_i(o_i)} ]$$

Centralized critic mitigates non-stationarity: $P(s'|s,a_i,\bm{\pi}_{-i})$ non-stationary if learning others, but $P(s'|s,\mathbf{a})$ stationary.

**COMA** [Foerster 2018]: Counterfactual baseline $A_i(s,\mathbf{a})=Q(s,\mathbf{a}) - \sum_{a_i'}\pi_i(a_i'|\tau_i)Q(s,(\mathbf{a}_{-i},a_i'))$.

**Limitation of state-based CTDE** emphasized by Phan et al. [9]: Under stochastic observation $O(o|s)$, true state $s$ may be *misleading* for decentralized value estimation because decentralized policy conditions on observation history, not $s$. Example Dec-Tiger with two doors, tiger random. Knowing $s$ = tiger left is optimal for centralized, but decentralized agents with noisy listening need to consider uncertainty. $V(s)$ learns optimistic value unreachable via $\pi_i(\tau_i)$.

AERIAL solution: replace $V(s)$ by $f_{\text{attn}}(h_1..h_n)$ where $h_i = RNN(o_i^0,a_i^0..)$ self-attention over agent recurrences. Empirically improves 30% median win-rate in MessySMAC with $\phi=0.15$ observation noise.

### 4.3 Value Factorization and IGM

VDN [Sunehag 2018]: $Q_{tot}(\bm{\tau},\mathbf{a})=\sum_i Q_i(\tau_i,a_i)$ . Simple IGM but limited representation: cannot represent payoff matrix $\begin{smallmatrix}8 & -12 \\ -12 & 0\end{smallmatrix}$ where coordination second action better.

QMIX [4] extends capacity: monotonic mixing network $Q_{tot}=g_{\psi(s)}(Q_1..Q_n)$ where $\partial Q_{tot}/\partial Q_i \ge 0$ via hypernetwork producing non-negative weights.

IGM condition formalized by Son et al. QTRAN 2019:

$$\forall \bm{\tau}: \arg\max_{\mathbf{a}} Q_{tot}(\bm{\tau},\mathbf{a}) = \begin{bmatrix}\arg\max_{a_1} Q_1 \\ \dots \\ \arg\max_{a_n} Q_n\end{bmatrix}$$

Monotonicity sufficient but not necessary. QPLEX achieves full IGM via duplex dueling decomposition $Q_{tot}=V_{tot}+A_{tot}$ with advantage factorization.

Tradeoff table:

| Method | Factorization | IGM? | Complexity | SMAC performance |
|--------|---------------|------|------------|------------------|
| VDN | Sum | Yes (restricted) | $O(n)$ | Baseline 60% |
| QMIX | Monotonic hypernet | Sufficient | $O(n)$ mixer | +25% over VDN [4] |
| WQMIX | Weighted projection | Sufficient | $O(n)$ | +5% over QMIX |
| QPLEX | Duplex dueling | Full | $O(n \cdot |\mathcal{A}|)$ | SOTA on hard maps |
| MAPPO | No factorization, centralized V | Via policy gradient | $O(n)$ | Comparable to QMIX, faster wall-clock [5] |

MAPPO surprising effectiveness [5] attributed to 5 tricks: value clipping, death masking, observation normalization, global+local feature pruning, data chunking.

### 4.4 Mean Field Approximations for Large Populations

Mean-field theory originates statistical physics; Yang et al. 2018 transfer to MARL [7]. For $N=100-1000$ agents, pairwise interaction matrix size $O(N^2|\mathcal{A}|^2)$ impossible.

Decompose $Q_i$ by Taylor:

$$Q_i(s,\mathbf{a}) = Q_i(s,a_i, \frac{1}{N^i}\sum_{j \in N(i)} a_j ) + \frac{1}{2N^i}\sum_j \delta a_{ij}^T H_{ij} \delta a_{ij} + o(\|\delta a\|^2)$$

If actions embedded as one-hot or continuous torque, mean action $\bar{a}_i$ approximates neighborhood influence; second-order term bounded by variance of neighbor actions.

Algorithm MF-Q:

1. For each agent $i$, compute $\bar{a}_i^t$.
2. Act $\epsilon$-greedy from $Q_i(s,\bar{a}_i, a_i)$.
3. Observe $r_i,\; s'$, compute target $y_i = r_i + \gamma v^{MF}(s')$ where $v^{MF}= \sum_{a_i} \pi_i(a_i|s,\bar{a}_i) Q_i(s',\bar{a}_i',a_i)$.
4. Update.

Convergence proof in Yang et al. requires: Lipschitz $Q$, bounded rewards, GLIE policy, and mean-field operator being contraction under sup norm when interaction graph dense enough. Empirical: solves Ising model with 100 spins where MF-Q finds ground state energy -1.42 vs. naive independent Q -1.15 (lower is better).

Heterogeneous extension multi-type MF [Subramanian et al. 2020] maintains $K$ mean fields for $K$ agent types:

$$Q_i^k(s, a_i, \bar{a}^1 .. \bar{a}^K)$$

Complexity $O(K)$ not $O(N)$.

Graph-attention MF partially observable variant [Drones 2023] uses GAT to weight neighbor importance: $\bar{a}_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} a_j$ where $\alpha_{ij}=softmax( e_{ij})$, $e_{ij}= a^T [Wh_i||Wh_j]$.

### 4.5 Partial Observability and Recurrence

Two regimes:

- *Small $n$, long memory*: Use RQN with GRU: $h_t^i = GRU(o_t^i, h_{t-1}^i)$, $Q_i(h_t^i,a)$.
- *Large $n$, short memory*: Use mean-field + attention pooling.

AERIAL ablation shows state $s$ encoder contributes *negative transfer* when observation stochasticity $\Phi >0.1$ because optimal decentralized belief differs from centralized belief. Proposed metric: **Observability Gap** $OG = \mathbb{E}_s[ \max_{\bm{\pi}_{dec}} V - V^{\pi^*(s)}]$.

For SMAC maps with 20% sight range reduced, OG correlates strongly (Pearson 0.84) with CTDE-state drop.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch NEXP-completeness Containment

Membership in NEXP: Guess joint policy trees nondeterministically — size $O(\exp(|\mathcal{O}|^h))$, doubly exponential but guessable in exponential time relative to input size $h$ binary-encoded exponentially. Verify expected value $\ge K$ by dynamic programming over occupancy states in exponential time ⇒ NEXP.

Hardness: Reduce from TILING language $L \in NEXP$. Construct Dec-POMDP with two agents where each local observation reveals half of tape cell coordinate; only joint $O$ matches tiling inconsistency if TILING fails. Thus optimal value distinguishes accepting vs rejecting instances.

Full proof formal in Bernstein 2002 UAI [3].

### 5.2 QMIX Monotonicity Enables Tractability

> Theorem: If $\partial Q_{tot}/\partial Q_i \ge 0 \; \forall i$, then $\max_{\mathbf{a}} Q_{tot}(\bm{\tau},\mathbf{a}) = Q_{tot}(\bm{\tau}, (\arg\max_{a_i} Q_i))$.

Proof: monotonic composition preserves argmax order. Since mixing weights non-negative, increasing any $Q_i$ increases total. Hence global max achieved by individually greedy actions, enabling $O(n|\mathcal{A}_i|)$ decentralized maximization instead of $O(\prod |\mathcal{A}_i|)$ joint max [4].

### 5.3 Mean-Field Convergence Rate

Under assumptions: $Q$ $L$-Lipschitz in mean action, rewards bounded $[0,R_{\max}]$, discount $\gamma$, population $N$, Taylor residual $R \le \frac{L}{2N}\sum_j ||a_j-\bar{a}||^2$. Then mean-field Q-iteration contracts with factor $\gamma + \epsilon_{MF}$ where $\epsilon_{MF}=O(1/\sqrt{N})$. As $N\to\infty$, $\epsilon_{MF}\to 0$ recovering standard Q convergence.

Quantitative: For Gaussian Squeeze reward $R(\mathbf{a})= \mu \exp(-(\bar{a}-\mu)^2/\sigma^2)$ with $\mu=0,N=1000$, MF-AC achieves 80% less variance in gradient than independent AC.

### 5.4 Empirical Summary from Literature

- **SMAC 3m, 5m_vs_6m, MMM2**: QMIX 75-85% win-rate vs VDN 55%, IQL 35% [4]. MAPPO matches QMIX with 2x faster wall-clock on 1 GPU [5].
- **MessySMAC**: adding $\phi=0.15$ observation stochasticity drops state-CTDE QMIX from 83% to 41% median; AERIAL retains 71% [9].
- **MA mujoco Spread**: MADDPG coverage 0.82 vs DDPG 0.51 [6].
- **Ising 100 spins**: MF-Q ground energy -1.42 (optimal ~ -1.45) vs Independent Q -1.15, confirming mean action sufficient statistic for symmetric interactions [7].

## 6 Limitations

- **NEXP barrier not bypassed**: CTDE does not solve Dec-POMDP exactly; it solves central surrogate. When execution truly requires high-order reasoning about others' beliefs, e.g., Hanabi-style implicit communication, factorization fails. IGM violations cause QMIX to converge to suboptimal [QTRAN examples].
- **State-based critic fragility**: Under stochastic partial observability, central state $s$ overfits simulator; real robots have sensor noise mismatch. No free lunch: centralized information must be *decentralizably realizable* — a concept formalized as Dec-POMDP *realizable value functions*.
- **Mean-field homogeneity assumption**: $Q_i(s,a_i,\bar{a})$ assumes permutation invariance and second-order term small. For heterogeneous skills, multimodal neighbor distributions, mean degrades; higher moments or distribution embedding necessary (e.g., using Wasserstein distance).
- **Scalability vs coordination tradeoff**: MF scales to $N=1000$ but destroys ability to model pairwise locks (traffic intersection). Graph MF alleviates but introduces non-stationarity of attention weights.
- **Evaluation bias**: SMAC benchmark has deterministic observations largely, underestimating partial observability hardness. MessySMAC [9] attempts fix but still synthetic.

*Open problem*: Learn factorization structure automatically from Dec-POMDP generational graph — causal discovery of agent influence.

## 7 Conclusion

We have traced a path from **intractability** (NEXP-completeness of Dec-POMDPs) through **pragmatic centralization** (CTDE with QMIX, MAPPO, MADDPG) to **population scaling** (mean-field approximations). The central thesis: hardness stems from infinite belief hierarchies, yet engineering solutions succeed when they exploit *training-time privilege* or *statistical symmetry*.

Three designer principles emerge:

1. **Explicitly model decentralized realizability**: Replace $V(s)$ with learned recurrence embedding $h_{1..n}$ via attention when stochasticity high.
2. **Enforce consistent factorization**: Monotonicity ensures IGM and tractable decentralized argmax, but explore full IGM class (QPLEX) for expressive coordination.
3. **Average when homogeneous, graph when sparse**: Mean-field $O(N)$ approximation plus GAT weighting yields tractable large-scale learning with convergence guarantees $O(1/\sqrt{N})$.

Future work points to *common information approaches* [Gao et al. 2023 POSG information-sharing model [8]] that formalize what to share for quasi-polynomial planning, bridging Dec-POMDP theory and deep MARL practice.
