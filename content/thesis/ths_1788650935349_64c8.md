# Learned Neural Video Compression: Conditional Coding, Scale-Space Flow, and Entropy-Constrained End-to-End Optimization

## Abstract

Neural video compression (NVC) has evolved from a direct emulation of the classical hybrid coding loop into a family of end-to-end optimized architectures whose central design axis is the **conditional coding** paradigm. This thesis develops a unified account of three mutually reinforcing advances: (i) conditional (as opposed to residual) coding in the feature domain, exemplified by the Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC) series; (ii) **scale-space flow** motion compensation, which augments the optical-flow field with a learned scale parameter to model prediction uncertainty; and (iii) learned entropy models — hyperpriors, autoregressive context models, and their temporal conditionings — that make the rate term of the **rate–distortion Lagrangian** tractable and tight. We trace the lineage from DVC, the first fully end-to-end learned codec, through scale-space flow warping and DCVC's context generation, to the modern DCVC-TCM/HEM/DC refinements. The analysis formalizes why conditional coding strictly generalizes residual coding, how scale-space warping encodes heteroscedastic motion uncertainty, and how entropy-constrained optimization converts the Shannon rate into a differentiable loss. Empirical evidence surveyed from the literature places learned codecs above H.265/HEVC and within striking distance of, and in select regimes beyond, H.266/VVC on standard benchmarks, while we close with a candid discussion of the complexity, latency, and generalization gaps that still separate learned codecs from industrial deployment.

---

## 1 Introduction

Classical video codecs — H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, H.266/VVC — are triumphs of hand-engineered signal processing: block partitioning, translational motion search, integer transforms, and context-adaptive entropy coding, refined over decades by committees of experts. Learned neural video compression asks a more radical question: *what if every component — the transform, the predictor, the motion model, and the entropy model — were differentiable modules jointly optimized for a single rate–distortion objective?*

The first affirmative answer arrived with DVC, which demonstrated that optical-flow-based motion compensation and autoencoder-style residual coding could be trained end-to-end through a rate–distortion Lagrangian and match H.265 in MS-SSIM [2]. Yet DVC remained a *learned implementation of the classical pipeline*: it coded the **residual** `x_t − warp(x̂_{t−1})`, exactly as hybrid codecs do. The decisive conceptual break was the **conditional coding** framework introduced by Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC) [1]: rather than subtracting the prediction and coding the difference, the network receives the current frame *and* a learned temporal context as joint inputs, allowing it to exploit statistical dependencies the linear residual can never express.

Two further threads complete the modern picture. First, **scale-space flow** [3] generalized the bilinear warp by learning, alongside each flow vector, a scale parameter that adaptively blurs the reference — a principled treatment of motion *uncertainty* rather than motion *error*. Second, **learned entropy models** descended from the variational image-compression literature — factorized priors, scale hyperpriors [4], and masked autoregressive context models — supply the differentiable bit-rate estimates that make end-to-end optimization possible at all.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the hybrid coding loop, the rate–distortion Lagrangian, and the quantization bottleneck that shaped every subsequent design. Section 3 details the methodology: conditional versus residual coding formulations, scale-space warping, entropy-model architectures, and the training machinery. Section 4 presents deep dives on the DCVC family evolution, the uncertainty interpretation of scale-space flow, entropy-model taxonomies, and the Lagrangian rate-control problem. Section 5 surveys empirical results, including the state of the art relative to H.266/VVC. Section 6 states limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The hybrid coding loop and its learned shadow

Every mainstream standard since H.261 has implemented the same skeleton: motion estimation, motion compensation (MEMC) producing a prediction `x̃_t`, transformation and quantization of the residual `r_t = x_t − x̃_t`, and entropy coding of the quantized coefficients. DVC [2] mapped each stage onto a neural module: a learned optical-flow network for motion, bilinear warping for compensation, and two autoencoder subnetworks compressing motion and residual latents. Crucially, the modules were optimized jointly through

> **Lagrangian rate–distortion loss:** `L = R + λ·D`, where `R` is the estimated bit rate of all transmitted latents and `D` is a distortion measure (MSE or 1 − MS-SSIM) between the source and reconstruction [2][4].

The Lagrange multiplier `λ` selects the operating point on the rate–distortion curve; a family of models trained at different `λ` values traces the full RD frontier. This single scalar mechanism replaced the hand-tuned QP tables of classical codecs.

### 2.2 The quantization bottleneck

Quantization `Q(y) = round(y)` has zero gradient almost everywhere, so the rate term `R = −Σ log₂ P(ŷ)` cannot be backpropagated through directly. Two surrogates dominate:

1. **Additive uniform noise:** `ỹ = y + u`, `u ∼ U(−½, ½)` during training, with true rounding at test time [4];
2. **Straight-through estimators** (STE), which pass gradients through the rounding as if it were the identity.

The noise surrogate has a clean variational interpretation: it makes the rate term a valid upper bound on the discrete entropy of the quantized latents [4]. Virtually all NVC systems inherit this treatment for both content and motion latents.

### 2.3 Entropy models from variational image compression

Learned compression is, formally, lossy compression with a variational autoencoder: the encoder is an approximate posterior `q(y|x)`, the decoder a likelihood `p(x|y)`, and the *entropy model* `p(y)` is the prior whose negative log-likelihood determines the bit rate [4]. Progress in image compression therefore flowed directly into video:

- **Fully factorized prior** (Ballé et al. 2017): one learned univariate density per latent channel;
- **Scale hyperprior** (Ballé et al. 2018): side information `ẑ = h_a(y)` decoded into per-element Gaussian scales `σ = h_s(ẑ)`, capturing spatial correlations of variance [4];
- **Autoregressive context models** (Minnen et al.): masked convolutions predicting each latent's distribution from already-decoded spatial neighbors, later extended with temporal conditioning [5][6].

The video literature adopted all three, and added *temporal* priors `p(y_t | y_{<t})` on top [5].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Residual versus conditional coding: the formal distinction

Let `x_t` be the current frame, `x̂_{t−1}` the decoded reference, and `f_predict` a prediction network. Classical and first-generation learned codecs (DVC [2], SSF [3]) implement **residual coding**:

```
x̂_t = f_dec( ⌊f_enc( x_t − x̃_t )⌋ ) + x̃_t,   x̃_t = f_predict(x̂_{t−1})     (1)
```

Only the residual passes through the transform; the prediction is added back verbatim. DCVC [1] instead proposed **conditional coding** in the feature domain:

```
x̂_t = f_dec( ⌊f_enc( x_t | x̄_t )⌋ | x̄_t ),   x̄_t = f_context(x̂_{t−1})     (2)
```

where `x̄_t` is a *learned context* — a high-dimensional feature tensor, not a pixel-domain prediction. The central theoretical claim [1]:

> **Theorem:** *Conditional coding generalizes residual coding.* The residual formulation (1) is the special case of (2) in which the decoder's dependence on the condition is restricted to addition. Consequently, the achievable rate–distortion frontier under conditional coding weakly dominates that under residual coding, given sufficient model capacity.

The intuition is information-theoretic: residual coding transmits `H(x_t | x̃_t)` under a linear conditioning, whereas conditional coding transmits approximately `H(x_t | x̄_t)` with a nonlinear conditioning the network learns jointly with the code. Follow-up work confirmed the gap empirically and then widened it: DCVC-TCM mines **multi-scale** temporal contexts [8], DCVC-HEM enriches the entropy model with spatial-temporal priors, and DCVC-DC diversifies context with group-based offsets [9].

### 3.2 Scale-space flow: warping under uncertainty

Bilinear warping fails gracefully nowhere: at disocclusions, motion boundaries, and fast motion, the warped reference is confidently wrong, and the residual codec pays for it. Scale-space flow [3] augments each 2D flow vector `(u, v)` with a **scale field** `σ ≥ 0`:

```
x̄_w(p) = warp_ss( x̂_{t−1}, u(p), v(p), σ(p) )
```

where `warp_ss` trilinearly interpolates a 3D scale-space volume built by convolving the reference with a Gaussian pyramid `σ² ∈ {0, σ₀², (2σ₀)², (4σ₀)², …}` [3]. Where the flow is certain (`σ → 0`), the warp is sharp; where it is uncertain (disocclusion, motion blur), the network learns to sample the blurred levels. Later work showed the naïve Gaussian cascade can be implemented efficiently with a blur–downsample–upsample pyramid requiring only small fixed kernels [6], and that bicubic warping *followed by* adaptive blurring is a computationally lighter equivalent [11].

> **Lemma:** *Scale-space warping subsumes bilinear warping.* Setting `σ(p) = 0` everywhere recovers exact bilinear interpolation of the reference. The scale channel therefore adds one degree of freedom per pixel whose cost is amortized against the residual bits it saves.

### 3.3 Entropy models: hyperprior, autoregressive, temporal

The rate term for the content latents is `R_y = −Σ_i log₂ p(ŷ_i | c_i)`, where the conditioning context `c_i` distinguishes the model family:

| Model | Conditioning `c_i` | Strength | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Factorized prior | ∅ (per-channel tables) | Trivial decode | Loose rate bound |
| Scale hyperprior [4] | `σ_i = h_s(ẑ)_i` | Captures spatial variance correlation | Small side-info overhead `R_z` |
| Autoregressive (spatial) | `ŷ_{<i}` (masked conv) | Tightest spatial model | Serial decode, `O(HW)` steps |
| Temporal prior [5] | `ŷ_{t−1}`, warped features | Exploits inter-frame latent correlation | Requires feature buffer |

Practical systems combine them. DCVC's entropy model uses a hyperprior **plus** an autoregressive spatial component, conditioned on the learned temporal context for inter-frame correlation [8]; DCVC-TCM drops the serial autoregressive term in favor of a parallelizable temporal-context-mining design, trading a small RD loss for much faster decoding [8]. DCVC-HEM instead *keeps* the spatial-temporal modeling but restructures it as a hybrid spatial-temporal prior with checkerboard parallelism. The design space is thus a three-way trade among **rate tightness**, **decode parallelism**, and **model size**.

### 3.4 End-to-end training: loss, schedule, and stability

Training minimizes the Lagrangian over short sequences (typically 3–7 frames), unrolling the recurrent dependency `x̂_t → x̂_{t+1}`:

```python
# Pseudocode: entropy-constrained end-to-end training step
for clip in dataloader:                          # clips of T frames
    x_hat_prev = intra_codec(clip[0])            # I-frame via learned image codec
    total_loss = rate_distortion(x_hat_prev, clip[0])
    for x_t in clip[1:]:
        flow, scale = motion_net(x_t, x_hat_prev)     # scale-space motion
        ctx = context_net(warp_ss(x_hat_prev, flow, scale))
        y = enc_content(x_t, ctx)                     # conditional encoder
        y_tilde = y + uniform_noise(-0.5, 0.5)         # quant surrogate
        R = -log2(prior(y_tilde | ctx, z_hat)).sum()   # differentiable rate
        x_hat = dec_content(y_tilde, ctx)
        D = mse(x_hat, x_t)
        total_loss += R + lam * D
        x_hat_prev = x_hat
    total_loss.backward(); optimizer.step()
```

Two practices proved essential. First, **staged pretraining** (motion-only, then residual/content, then joint) stabilizes the deeply recurrent graph [2]. Second, **λ-scheduling**: training separate models per `λ` remains standard, though variable-rate mechanisms (gain units, conditional scaling networks, DCVC-FM's wide-range modulation [9]) now allow a single model to span the bitrate range.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The DCVC family: from context to diversified mining

The original DCVC [1] proved that a *learned* high-dimensional context beats the pixel-domain prediction as a conditioning signal. Its successors each attacked a different bottleneck:

1. **DCVC-TCM** — temporal context mining across multiple feature scales, replacing single-scale warping; removed the autoregressive entropy term for decode speed [8].
2. **DCVC-HEM** — heterogeneous entropy modeling: a latent prior fusing spatial (checkerboard-parallel) and temporal priors, tightening the rate bound without serial decode.
3. **DCVC-DC** — diversified context: group-based offset prediction for motion compensation plus a quadtree-partition-based entropy model, improving handling of complex and large motion [9].
4. **DCVC-FM** — wide quality-range support via feature modulation, so one model covers the full RD curve [9].
5. **DCVC-RT** — a real-time variant (~7.8 ms inference + ~2.6 ms entropy coding per 1080p frame) using a two-step distribution-estimation entropy model [10].

The arc is unmistakable: each generation either **tightens the entropy bound** or **buys back parallelism**, and the conditional-coding scaffold accommodates both without redesign of the motion path.

### 4.2 Uncertainty as a first-class signal: the scale-space interpretation

Scale-space flow is best read probabilistically. Bilinear warping implicitly asserts a Dirac belief over the correspondence: pixel `p` in frame `t` came from *exactly* `p + (u,v)` in frame `t−1`. The scale field relaxes this to a Gaussian belief of learned variance `σ²(p)`, and sampling the scale-space volume implements the corresponding expectation under a Gaussian smoothing kernel [3]. This is **heteroscedastic uncertainty**: the model learns *where* it is uncertain (occlusion boundaries, defocus, fast texture) and spends blur — a cheap operation — instead of residual bits, which are expensive.

Later refinements generalized the fixed Gaussian pyramid: VLVC's **weighted warping** learns the full 3D smoothing kernel shape rather than fixing it to Gaussian [7], and deformable temporal alignment modules learn local sampling offsets around the motion-compensated prediction to correct alignment errors at boundaries [12]. All are instances of one principle: *motion compensation should output a distribution over references, not a point estimate.*

### 4.3 Entropy-model taxonomy and the rate-bound hierarchy

The rate term is the only part of the Lagrangian the encoder can *shrink without touching distortion* — a tighter entropy model is pure RD gain. The hierarchy, by tightness of the bound on the true latent entropy `H(ŷ)`:

1. `R_factorized ≥ R_hyperprior ≥ R_hyperprior+AR ≥ R_hyperprior+AR+temporal`
2. Each refinement pays in either side information (`R_z` for hyperprior bits), serial decode steps (autoregressive), or memory (temporal feature buffers) [4][5][8].

A subtle point: the *temporal* prior is where video compression most differs from image compression. Autoregressive temporal priors were shown to tighten the bound significantly because residual temporal dependencies persist after motion compensation [5]. DCVC's context-conditioned entropy model is the engineering realization of this insight [1][8].

### 4.4 The Lagrangian and learned rate control

In classical codecs, rate control is an outer loop (QP cascading, R-λ models). In NVC, `λ` *is* the rate control, baked into training. This creates two research problems: (i) **variable-rate inference** from fixed-`λ` training, solved via gain vectors that scale latents before quantization, or via conditional modulation networks (DCVC-FM) [9]; and (ii) **content-adaptive λ**, where per-clip or per-frame λ refinement (including test-time fine-tuning of the motion or entropy modules) recovers RD performance lost to train-test domain shift [9]. The Lagrangian view also clarifies a common failure mode: optimizing MSE-only distortion biases the model toward blur at low rates, motivating MS-SSIM or perceptual (GAN/feature) losses as the `D` term — with the caveat that adversarial losses optimize a different, non-metric distortion [11].

### 4.5 Where learned codecs stand against H.266/VVC

The competitive history is compact: DVC matched H.264 and approached H.265 (MS-SSIM) [2]; scale-space flow beat contemporary learned baselines with a far simpler training recipe [3]; DCVC reported gains over H.265 and, on several test sets, over VVC reference software in MS-SSIM [1]; and the DCVC-DC/FM generation reports consistent BD-rate savings over VTM (the VVC reference) in both PSNR and MS-SSIM across standard test sets [9]. Independent analyses corroborate that conditional-coding NVC now *leads* VVC on perceptual metrics while remaining roughly competitive on PSNR — with the strong caveat that VVC results depend heavily on configuration (random access vs. low delay) and that learned codecs are evaluated mostly in low-delay P-frame settings where their recurrent structure is strongest [8][9].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The evidence base for the three pillars is both theoretical and experimental.

**Pillar 1 — conditional coding dominates residual coding.** Beyond the architectural theorem of Section 3.1, the empirical record is consistent: DCVC outperformed residual-coding learned baselines (DVC, SSF) at matched complexity [1], and every subsequent DCVC-family paper retained conditional coding while improving the context or entropy model — an implicit ablation across the literature confirming the scaffold's value [8][9][10]. A recent conditional-coding study with spatial-temporal super-resolution further showed the framework extends to ultra-low-bitrate regimes (0.05 Mbps) [11].

**Pillar 2 — scale-space flow reduces motion-compensation error.** Agustsson et al. demonstrated that adding the scale channel improved RD performance over pure optical-flow warping with no pretrained flow network and a drastically simplified training pipeline [3]. The efficient Gaussian-pyramid implementation keeps the per-pixel cost to small-kernel blurs plus trilinear sampling [6].

**Pillar 3 — tighter entropy models convert directly to BD-rate.** The image-compression lineage quantified each step (hyperprior over factorized, autoregressive over hyperprior) [4]; in video, the context-conditioned entropy model of DCVC and the temporal priors of follow-ups are credited with a substantial share of the family's BD-rate gains over VVC [1][5][8][9].

> **Theorem (sketch):** *For a stationary source, the expected rate of conditional coding is lower-bounded by `H(x_t | x̄_t)` and that of residual coding by `H(x_t − x̃_t)`. Since `H(x_t − x̃_t) ≥ H(x_t | x̃_t)` for the linear residual map and `H(x_t | x̃_t) ≥ H(x_t | x̄_t)` when the learned context `x̄_t` is a sufficient statistic refinement of the prediction, conditional coding admits a strictly lower rate bound whenever the context captures nonlinear temporal dependencies.*

**Benchmark posture (surveyed):** on the standard HEVC test sequences and UVG datasets, the DCVC family reports BD-rate reductions versus VTM (VVC reference, low-delay P) in the low tens of percent in MS-SSIM and single-to-low-double digits in PSNR, varying by sequence class and rate range [1][8][9]. Decode complexity remains the gating factor: autoregressive entropy models impose serial dependencies, which is precisely why DCVC-TCM and DCVC-RT restructured the entropy path for parallelism [8][10].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Complexity and latency.** Learned decoders require GPU-class hardware; entropy decoding with autoregressive components is serial. Real-time CPU decoding, standard in VVC deployments, remains out of reach for the strongest models, though DCVC-RT narrows the gap [10].
2. **Generalization and domain shift.** Models trained on Vimeo-90k-style natural video degrade on screen content, animation, and high-motion sports; content-adaptive fine-tuning helps but adds encode-side cost [9].
3. **Error propagation.** Recurrent P-frame chains accumulate quantization error; unlike VVC's hierarchical B structures with periodic intra refresh, most NVC systems use simple fixed intra periods, and long chains drift.
4. **Evaluation asymmetry.** Learned codecs are typically compared against VVC in low-delay configurations favorable to their architecture; random-access VVC with hierarchical B-frames remains a harder target, and BD-rate numbers are sensitive to anchor settings [8].
5. **Standardization and determinism.** Cross-platform bit-exact decoding of floating-point neural networks is unsolved at the standards level; quantized integer inference is an active research area but not yet a deployed reality.
6. **Perceptual–fidelity tension.** MSE-trained models blur; GAN-trained models hallucinate. No learned codec yet dominates VVC simultaneously on PSNR, MS-SSIM, and human-rated perceptual quality [11].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Three ideas carry learned video compression from curiosity to contender. **Conditional coding** replaced the linear residual with a learned nonlinear conditioning, strictly generalizing the hybrid loop and unlocking the DCVC family's steady gains [1][8][9]. **Scale-space flow** replaced the point-estimate warp with an uncertainty-aware operator, spending cheap blur where the motion is unknowable instead of expensive residual bits [3][6]. **Learned entropy models** — hyperpriors, autoregressive contexts, temporal priors — turned the Shannon rate into a differentiable, progressively tightenable loss term, making the entire system optimizable end-to-end under the rate–distortion Lagrangian [4][5].

The trajectory points toward convergence with, and selective surpassing of, H.266/VVC on perceptual metrics, contingent on solving decode parallelism, domain robustness, and deterministic deployment [9][10]. The deeper lesson is methodological: once prediction, transform, and entropy model are all differentiable, codec design becomes architecture search over a single objective — and the classical block diagram, refined over thirty years, becomes one point in a much larger, learnable space.
