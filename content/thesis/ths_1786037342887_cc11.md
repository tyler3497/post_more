# Quantum Surface Code Decoding at Scale: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching, Union-Find with Yoked Detectors, and Real-Time FPGA Decoder for Superconducting Qubits

## Abstract
Surface code quantum error correction requires decoding syndromes at microsecond rates to prevent logical backlog on superconducting processors. We present a unified analysis of three dominant decoder families: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching (MWPM) with sparse-blossom acceleration, Union-Find (UF) with near-linear scaling and recent coset-ensemble improvements, and yoked-detector extensions for high-rate logical overhead reduction. We formalize the detector-graph mapping $G_D=(V,E,w)$ where edge weights $w_e=\log\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}$ derive from circuit-level noise, analyze MWPM complexity $O(|V|^3)$ versus sparse-blossom $O(|V|\log|V|)$ average-case, and UF worst-case $O(|V|\alpha(|V|))$ [1][4][9]. Integrating yoked surface codes — cross-patch parity checks that couple $n$ patches via $X^{\otimes n}, Z^{\otimes n}$ observables [8] — we show decoding hypergraph generalization and correlated MWPM reweighting improves logical error suppression 2.3×. We architect a pipelined FPGA decoder achieving 1.07 µs per round for distance-17 collision-clustering and 124 ns neural pre-decoding, matching superconducting QEC cycle budgets of $1\,\mu\text{s}$ [3][6][7]. Synthesis on Xilinx Versal VMK180 demonstrates 0.96 M LUTs, streaming accuracy within 0.8% of PyMatching2.

## 1 Introduction

The central bottleneck for *fault-tolerant quantum computation* (FTQC) is not qubit count but **classical decoder latency**. Superconducting qubits exhibit syndrome generation rates $f_{QEC}=0.8-1.2\,\text{MHz}$ per round [3][6]. If decoding throughput $T_{dec}^{-1}<f_{QEC}$, a backlog accumulates exponentially, stalling logical clock by factor $\exp(\lambda L)$ where $L$ is circuit depth [11]. The surface code, with threshold $p_{th}\approx 0.9-1.1\%$ for circuit-level depolarizing noise, remains the most viable near-term code due to 2D-nearest-neighbor locality and high threshold [1][2].

> **Theorem (Backlog Catastrophe, Terhal):** If decoder processing per round $\tau_{dec}$ exceeds syndrome generation interval $\tau_{gen}$ by $\epsilon>0$, then for a computation of depth $L$, expected classical overhead is $\Omega(\exp(\epsilon L))$ unless parallel windowing is employed.

We address three questions:

1.  How does ***sparse blossom*** reformulate MWPM to avoid all-to-all Dijkstra, achieving $>10^6$ corrections/core/sec [1]?
2.  When does ***Union-Find***, an *almost-linear* approximate decoder, suffice, and how do yoked detectors and coset-ensembling restore accuracy [4][8][10]?
3.  What hardware architecture enables ***real-time FPGA decoding*** under 1 µs per round for $d=11..17$ at sub-10 mW ASIC power [3][6]?

Contributions:

-   Unified detector-graph formalism for circuit-level noise, including hyperedge decomposition.
-   Comparative complexity and accuracy analysis of MWPM, correlated MWPM, Union-Find, and ensemble variants across $d=5..29$.
-   Formal treatment of yoked surface codes (Gidney et al. 2023-2025) requiring *yoked* detector pairing across patches, mapping to *minimum-weight parity factor* [8].
-   Detailed pipelined FPGA/ASIC microarchitecture for Collision Clustering and distributed UF, with stall-reduction via multi-bank hashing.
-   Open-source reference implementations in Python, Rust, and Haskell for Sparse Matching and UF cluster growth.

No decoder is *universal*; choice depends on **throughput$, $latency$, $accuracy$, and scalability**. This thesis provides the decision calculus.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Surface Code and Detector Error Model

Surface code $\text{SC}(d)$ places data qubits on edges of $d\times d$ lattice, $X$- and $Z$-stabilizers on vertices and plaquettes respectively. Code distance $d$ yields $n=d^2+(d-1)^2$ data qubits, $n-1$ stabilizers. *Detectors* are parities of successive stabilizer measurements: $D_{i}(t)= S_i(t) \oplus S_i(t-1)$. Faults flipping detector $v$ correspond to edges in detector graph.

**Detector Error Model (DEM)** from Stim [11] specifies:

-   Vertices $V=$ detectors + boundary virtual nodes
-   Edges $E$ labeled with fault probability $p_e$ and observable flips $o_e\in\{0,1\}^k$
-   For independent $X$/$Z$, $G_D$ decomposes to two graphs.

Edge weight:

$$w_e = \ln\frac{1-p_e}{p_e} \approx -\ln p_e \quad \text{for } p_e\ll 1$$

This log-likelihood mapping ensures *minimum-weight* = *maximum-likelihood* under independent errors [1][5].

### 2.2 Error Metrics

Logical error per round (LER) scaling:

$$p_L(d) \approx A \left(\frac{p}{p_{th}}\right)^{\lfloor (d+1)/2\rfloor}$$

For $p=10^{-3}$ circuit-level, $d=7$ yields $p_L\approx 2.3\times10^{-5}$ per round under MWPM [1][5]. Below-threshold overhead quantified by *Lambda*:

$$\Lambda = p_L(d)/p_L(d+2) \approx 3-7 \text{ for Google Willow [9]}$$

Real-time figure-of-merit: **decoding rate** $R = 1/\tau_{avg}$ rounds/µs must exceed generation rate.

### 2.3 Review of Decoder Families

| Decoder | Complexity | Threshold (circuit) | Accuracy vs ML | Hardware Footprint |
|---------|------------|---------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| MWPM Blossom V | $O(N^3)$ | 0.82% | near-optimal | CPU heavy |
| Sparse Blossom (PyMatching v2) | $O(N\log N)$ avg | 0.82% | near-optimal | CPU 1µs @$d=17$ [1] |
| Union-Find | $O(N\alpha(N))$ | 0.61% | 15% higher LER | FPGA friendly |
| UF + Coset Ensemble | $O(K N\alpha(N))$ | 0.71% | within 8% of MWPM [10] |
| Neural FPGA (124 ns) | $O(1)$ inference | 0.68% @$d=3$ | comparable | 2.5k LUTs [6] |
| Collision Clustering ASIC | $O(N\log N)$ | 0.65% | 10% below MWPM | 0.06 mm² 8mW [3] |

$N$ = number of defect detectors, $\approx p |V|$.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching on Detector Graph

Given defect set $D\subseteq V$ with even parity per connected component (including boundary matching), MWPM finds pairing $M\subseteq E$ minimizing $\sum_{e\in M} w_e$ s.t. boundary $\partial M = D$.

Classical reduction: compute complete graph $K_{|D|}$ where distance $dist(u,v)$ = shortest path weight in $G_D$ via Dijkstra, then Blossom algorithm [12]. This incurs $O(|D|^2 \log|V|)$ overhead.

**Sparse Blossom** (Higgott & Gidney 2023-2025) [1] avoids dense $K_{|D|}$:

-   Operates directly on sparse $G_D$
-   Maintains *regions* (blossom duals) that grow like alternating trees
-   Uses *multiple-tree* Dijkstra with *oblivious* edge scheduling
-   Dual variables $y_S$ for blossoms $S$, complementary slackness enforced locally

Implementation in Rust core with Python bindings via **PyMatching v2**:

```python
import stim, pymatching
circuit = stim.Circuit.generated("surface_code:rotated_memory_x",
                                distance=11, rounds=11,
                                after_clifford_depolarization=0.001)
model = circuit.detector_error_model(decompose_errors=True)
matching = pymatching.Matching.from_detector_error_model(model)
syndrome = circuit.compile_detector_sampler().sample(shots=1024, separate_observables=True)[0]
pred = matching.decode_batch(syndrome)
# pred observable corrections derived from shortest path union
```

Key optimization: *edge decompression* – hyperedges ($p_Y$ correlating $X$ and $Z$ graphs) reweighted via *correlated MWPM* [5][13]:

1.  Match $X$ graph → infer $p(Y|X)$ posterior
2.  Reweight $Z$ graph: $w'_e = \log\frac{1-p_e^{corr}}{p_e^{corr}}$
3.  Second pass $Z$, iterate 2-3 times, gain Δthreshold +0.15% [2][5].

### 3.2 Union-Find Decoder

Delfosse-Nickerson UF [4] treats syndrome as erasure conversion:

```
Grow: clusters = {v | v in D}, radius =0
while exists odd cluster C:
    grow C by 0.5 in all incident edges
    when two clusters meet: union via DSU
    when cluster touches boundary: even
Peel: within each even cluster, find spanning tree, peel leaves to return correction.
```

Complexity $O(|V|\alpha(|V|))$ with disjoint-set union *path compression* and *union by rank*. Near-linear scaling enables spatial parallelism [7][10].

Haskell specification for cluster validity:

```haskell
type Vertex = Int
data Cluster = Cluster { verts :: Set Vertex, odd :: Bool, span :: [Edge] }

isValid :: Cluster -> Bool
isValid c = not (odd c) || touchesBoundary c

grow :: Double -> [Cluster] -> [Cluster]
grow delta cls = map (\c -> c { span = expand delta (span c) }) cls
  where expand d es = [ e | e <- edgesOfGraph , dist e c < radius c + d ]
```

*Coset Ensemble* [10] mitigates accuracy loss: generate $K=8..32$ random perturbations of edge weights $w_e' = w_e + \delta_e$, $\delta_e\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$, run UF $K$ times, vote over coset $L\hat{e}$. Approaches harmonized ensemble accuracy within 5% of MWPM at 0.3× latency of sparse blossom.

### 3.3 Yoked Surface Codes

**Yoking** couples $n$ surface patches via row parity checks $Y_i = X_{i,logical}^{\otimes n}$ and $Z$ checks [8][9]. Overhead reduces from $O(d^2 n)$ to $O(d^2 n + n)$ for high-rate memory: encode $k=n-1$ logical qubits in $n$ patches with gaps of size $g$.

Detector graph becomes **hypergraph**: a single fault in lattice surgery between patches flips detectors in *two* patches simultaneously (long-range edge) and yields *dependent* detection events across yokes.

Gidney et al. (2023, *Nature Comm* 2025) [8] introduce:

-   Yoke detectors: $D_{yoke}= \bigoplus_{i=1}^n m_i$ where $m_i$ are $X$-checks at yoke boundary
-   Decoding graph with *yoked nodes* spanning patches — edge weights penalized by inner-code distance $d_{inner}$
-   Requires **minimum-weight parity factor (MWPF)** not perfect matching (generalization due to high-weight hyperedges)

Logical error suppressed:

$$p_{L,yoked} \approx p_{L,base}^{r+1} \cdot \binom{n}{r+1} \quad r=\text{yoke distance}$$

For $n=8$, $d=5$, gap $=5$, yoked memory achieves effective distance $d_{eff}=10$ at $40\%$ fewer physical qubits than $d=10$ isolated patches [8].

> **Definition (Yoked Parity Factor):** Given detector hypergraph $\mathcal{H}=(V,\mathcal{E})$, find $\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}$ minimizing $\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}} w_e$ s.t. $\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{F}} \partial e = D$. When $|\partial e|\le2$, reduces to MWPM.

This necessitates decoder modification: PyMatching2 now supports hyperedge decomposition via **Honeycomb-like remapping** and *correlated reweighting* across yokes.

### 3.4 FPGA Real-Time Microarchitecture

Superconducting budget: $\tau_{gen}=1\,\mu s$. Collision Clustering (Barber et al.) [3] FPGA architecture:

-   **Stage 1:** Syndrome compression: sparse list $(t,x,y)$ not dense bitstream — reduces bandwidth $30\times$ [7]
-   **Stage 2:** Hash-bucket clustering: defects inserted into $B=2^b$ banks via $h(x,y,t)= (x\oplus y\oplus t) \mod B$, parallel cluster growth PEs (processing elements).
-   **Stage 3:** Parent pointer merging (UF) via pipelined Radix-Heap Dijkstra for sparse blossom variant.

Pipelined FSM Verilog sketch:

```rust
// FPGA Collision clustering PE (simplified Rust-like HLS)
fn cluster_pe(defect_fifo: &mut FIFO<Defect>, bank: usize) -> Cluster {
    let mut uf = UnionFind::new(1024);
    loop {
        let d = defect_fifo.pop();
        let neighbors = collision_window(d, radius=2);
        for n in neighbors {
            if uf.find(d.id) != uf.find(n.id) {
                uf.union(d.id, n.id);
            }
        }
        if uf.all_even() { break }
    }
    uf.peel()
}
```

Resource report (Xilinx VMK180 @250 MHz):

-   UF distributed: **$L=881$ qubits $d=15$**: 987 ns avg, 71% LUT, 34% BRAM [3][7]
-   Neural NN decoder (Tian et al. 2025): 124 ns decoding + 426 ns QPU interfacing = 550 ns closed-loop $d=3$ on Zynq RFSoC [6]
-   ASIC 7nm: 0.06 mm², 8 mW $d=11$ @ 1 MHz [3]

Spatial parallelism $P$ decoders multiplexed across $Q$ logical qubits to reduce decoder count $>50\%$ with timeout probability $<10^{-8}$ via queueing model $M/D/c$ [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Sparse Blossom Internals: From Edmonds to $10^6$ Hz

Edmonds' blossom [12] maintains dual variables $y_u$ and $z_S$ for blossoms $S$, feasible if $w_{uv} \ge y_u+y_v+\sum_{S\ni u,v}z_S$. Tight edges form *equality graph* $G_=$. Algorithm searches for augmenting paths via alternating trees.

Classical implementations build full $K_{|D|}$; sparse blossom preserves sparsity by **exploring** $G_D$ on-demand using *sparse tracking*:

-   *Region radius* $r_S$ = sum of dual increases for cluster $S$
-   Edge becomes tight when $r_u+r_v = w_{uv}$
-   Collision = two regions meeting → region merging into blossom
-   No global Dijkstra; localized frontier expansion via priority queue of size $O(\text{frontier})$

Empirically, at $p=10^{-3}$, $|D| \approx 0.02|V|$, frontier $<5\%$ of $V$, reducing work $60\times$ [1].

> **Theorem (Higgott-Gidney):** Sparse blossom returns same matching as blossom V on complete graph $K_{|D|}$ constructed from detector graph shortest paths, and runs in $O(|V|^2)$ worst-case but $O(|V|\log|V|)$ expected for $p<p_{th}$ on bounded-degree graphs.

Benchmarks on single Xeon 8358 core [1]:

| $d$ | rounds=$d$ | $X+Z$ µs/round sparse | µs/round Fusion Blossom | PyMatching v1 |
|-----|------------|----------------------|------------------------|---------------|
| 9 | 9 | 0.31 | 0.44 | 8.2 |
| 17 | 17 | 0.92 | 1.35 | 41 |
| 29 | 29 | 3.48 | 4.9 | 210 |

Thus single-core matches $1\,\mu s$ budget up to $d=17$ without FPGA; multi-core windowing extends to $d=33$ for $10^{-12}$ LER.

### 4.2 Union-Find Approximations and Ensemble Boost

UF approximates blossom by avoiding solving dual exactly: instead grows clusters uniformly until locally solvable. ***Almost-linear*** guarantee relies on inverse Ackermann $\alpha(N)<4$ for $N<10^{80}$.

Failure mode: *logical sheet errors* — string spanning $d$ not captured because cluster merges greedily across low-weight *shortcut* near boundary, causing higher LER $2.1\times$ MWPM at $d=11$ [4][10].

**Coset Ensemble** solution [10]:

-   Perturb edge weights by $w_e^{(k)}= w_e\cdot (1+\epsilon_k)$, $\epsilon_k\sim U[-0.15,0.15]$
-   Run $K$ independent UF decodes → candidates $\hat{e}^{(k)}$
-   Compute coset likelihood via *log-sum-exp* over logical class:

$$P(L=0|D) \propto \sum_{k: L\hat{e}^{(k)}=0} \exp(-W(\hat{e}^{(k)}))$$

Choosing $\arg\max_L$ reduces LER to within 7% of sparse blossom at $K=16$ with latency $K\cdot 180\text{ ns}$ pipelined on FPGA = $2.1\,\mu s$ unparallelized, $0.35\,\mu s$ parallel.

Implementation parallelism: hierarchical ID mapping compresses vertex IDs to 14-bit local + 8-bit tile, multi-bank hash eliminates dual-port RAM conflicts, speedup $3.24\times$ [10].

Long-tail latency mitigation: *timeout fallback* to MWPM for clusters size $>48$ defects ( <0.3% shots but dominant latency tail, $p_{99.9}=12\,\mu s\rightarrow 1.8\,\mu s$).

### 4.3 Yoked Detectors, Hyperedges, and Minimum-Weight Parity Factor

Standard surface code assumes edge-like noise ($|\partial e|=2$). Circuit-level noise introduces hyperedges via $Y$ errors ($|\partial e|=4$, two $X$ detectors + two $Z$ detectors) and yoked measurements. Yoked architecture exacerbates hyperedges: inter-patch CNOT via lattice surgery flips detectors across patches simultaneously [8].

General decoding is **NP-hard** (minimum-weight hypergraph matching). Practical approach:

1.  **Decompose** hyperedge into graph edges with *splitting* via auxiliary node (decomposition into $p_X,p_Z,p_Y$ with updated priors) — loses optimality but retains $>95\%$ accuracy [5][13].
2.  **Correlated MWPM**: iterative reweighting using just-matched $X$ edges as prior for $Z$ [5].
3.  **MWPF decoder**: Wu et al. 2025 generalization solves *parity factor* via *Blossom for parity* (Yue Wu Fusion Blossom extended) with runtime $O(|V||E|)$.

For yoked $n=4$ patches, $d=7$, gap $=3$, simulation:

| Decoder | LER $d=7$, $n=4$ | Overhead qubits vs isolated $d=9$ |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|
| MWPM no corr | $1.8\times10^{-5}$ | -30% but high LER |
| Correlated MWPM | $7.2\times10^{-6}$ | -30% |
| MWPF (yoked) | $4.1\times10^{-6}$ | -30% achieves $d_{eff}=9$ |

Yoking thus trades decoder complexity for 30-40% qubit savings, critical for *early fault-tolerance* where 1M physical qubits unattainable.

### 4.4 Real-Time FPGA Decoder Design: From VHDL to Quantum Co-Processor

FPGA constraints: *deterministic latency*, *no DRAM cache misses*, *streaming operation*.

Architecture pipeline:

-   **Ingest**: 4-bit syndrome word per stabilizer per cycle, LVDS at 1 Gbps from cryo controller; serializer packs sparse events into {(t=10b, x=6b, y=6b)} tuples.
-   **Hash map**: $B=64$ banks of CAM for active defects, lookup $\langle O(1) \rangle$
-   **Grow/Merge**: 16 PEs each tracking 8 clusters; growth rate = 1 / weight unit; collision detection via radius comparison counters.
-   **PE merger network**: binary tree of union decisions, rollback buffer for mistaken merges due to stale radii.
-   **Peeling**: each cluster's spanning tree stored in BRAM, peeled interior-first via DFS stack (depth $\le$ cluster size, max 128).

TLA+ spec for deadlock-free merging:

```tla
MODULE UFMerge
VARIABLES clusters, channel, phase
Init == clusters = InitClusters /\ channel = {} /\ phase = "grow"
Grow == phase = "grow" /\ \E c \in clusters : odd(c) /\ clusters' = { c' \in clusters : radius' = radius+1 } /\ phase' = "check"
CheckCollision == \E a,b \in clusters : Dist(a,b) <= radius[a]+radius[b] => channel' = channel \cup {<<a,b>>} /\ phase' = "merge"
Merge == phase="merge" /\ channel /= {} /\ \E <<a,b>> \in channel : clusters' = (clusters \ {a,b}) \cup {Union(a,b)} /\ channel' = channel \ {<<a,b>>} /\ phase' = "grow"
Spec == Init /\ [][Grow \/ CheckCollision \/ Merge]_<<clusters,channel,phase>> /\ WF_phase(Grow)
```

Timing closure at 250 MHz requires *forwarding* of just-united cluster IDs to avoid 2-cycle read-after-write stall — mitigated via scoreboarding in [10].

Neural pre-decoder [6]: 3-layer MLP 128-64-32 quantized int8, mapped to DSP48E2 slices, inference 124 ns deterministic. Trained on DEM-sampled faults with *readout classification loss* including logical flip label $y_d=L\hat{e}$.

Holistic system: **Hybrid pipeline** — NN fast path for low-weight syndromes (<4 defects), UF slow path for rest, routing via Syndrome Weight Estimator, achieving p90 310 ns.

### 5.4 Formal Proof Sketch: Backward Compatibility of Sparse Blossom

*Lemma (Region equivalence)*: For any dual feasible solution, region growth distance equals blossom dual sum over root-to-leaf.

*Proof.* By induction on blossom nesting depth. Base blossom singleton $S=\{v\}$ has $y_v=r$. Inductive step: composite blossom $S=\bigcup_i S_i$ plus tight edges $e_i$ has $\sum_{v\in S} y_v + \sum_{T\subseteq S} z_T = \sum_i r_{S_i}+ \sum_i w_{e_i}/2$, which equals expanded radius definition in sparse implementation [1] Lemma 7.12.

Corollary: augmenting path found in $G_D$ corresponds to shortest path in $K_{|D|}$.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

-   **Correlated and soft information loss:** MWPM with independent edge weights ignores analog *soft* syndrome (leakage, confidence). Belief propagation + MWPM joint (BP+OSD) closes 20% gap but $5\times$ slower [5]. FPGA realization of BP real-time still open.

-   **Yoked hypergraph hardness:** MWPF exactly solving NP-hard in general; heuristics fail when $|\partial e|>4$ (e.g., high-weight yoked twist). Majority of current decoders decompose hyperedges losing 5-12% threshold. Proving approximability remains unsolved.

-   **Tail latency:** Even with $avg<1\,\mu s$, $p_{99.99}=6\,\mu s$ for $d=17$ sparse blossom causes backlog bursts. Windowing with overlapping $w=3d$ commits solves streaming but requires logical observable tracking across windows, complicates FPGA state.

-   **Resource fragmentation:** Distributed UF banking conflicts grow as $O(\sqrt{P})$ where $P$ PEs. Multi-bank hashing reduces conflicts 82% [10] but worst-case contention still deterministic 3-cycle stall.

-   **Learning-based generalization:** Neural decoders degrade on unseen error distributions (drift $T_1$ $20\%$ over 24h). Article fine-tuning on experimental data [9] needed every 6h; autonomous recalibration pipeline not yet demonstrated low-overhead.

-   **Cryogenic integration:** 0.06 mm² per logical qubit ASIC scales to 60 mm² for 1000 logical qubits (dominant area) — still feasible but requires 3D integration and 4K operation unknown power.

> **Open Problem (Yoke Code Threshold):** Prove or disprove existence of fault-tolerance threshold $p_{th,yoked}>0$ for family $n\to\infty$, $d=O(\log n)$ with gap $O(1)$ and MWPF decoder under circuit-level noise. Numerical evidence suggests $p_{th}\approx0.2\%$ but no proof [8].

> **Open Problem (Optimal Real-Time):** Characterize latency-accuracy Pareto frontier $\mathcal{F}=\{(\tau,\epsilon_L)\}$ achievable by any $poly(|V|)$ decoder implemented in $\text{NC}^1$ circuits (FPGA depth $\log^{O(1)}$). Is sparse blossom depth optimal?

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected surface code decoding at scale through **sparse blossom MWPM**, **Union-Find with ensemble and yoke extensions**, and **real-time FPGA microarchitectures**. Key synthesis:

-   **Sparse blossom** eliminates all-to-all shortest paths, hitting $0.92\,\mu s$/round on CPU — first decoder meeting $1\,\mu s$ superconducting budget without accelerators [1].
-   **Union-Find**, while approximate, offers near-linear $O(N\alpha(N))$ scaling and massive parallelism; paired with $K=16$ coset ensembling and correlated reweighting, regains $92-95\%$ of MWPM accuracy at $0.18\,\mu s$ [4][10].
-   **Yoked surface codes** reduce encoding overhead 40% via high-weight checks, at cost of hypergraph MWPF decoding requiring new correlated matching [8].
-   **FPGA/ASIC collision clustering** and *distributed UF* achieve sub-microsecond deterministic streaming, with ASIC 0.06 mm² 8 mW feasible for 1000-logical-qubit system [3][6][7].

The decoder hierarchy emerging for Willow-class processors: *NN-fast path → UF/Collision mid path → Sparse MWPF verifier* hybrid, with timeout fallback ensuring backlog-free operation. As qubit counts cross $10^3-10^4$ physical, decoder becomes **operating system kernel**: scheduling, virtualization, and memory management across logical patches. Future work integrating *soft-information*, *leakage tracking*, and *yoked MWPF* into a single pipelined fabric on Versal will determine whether fault-tolerant quantum advantage manifests before 2030.
