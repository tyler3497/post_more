# On the Hardness Foundations and Homomorphic Constructions of Lattice-Based Cryptography: A Rigorous Analysis of Learning With Errors, Ring Variants, and Fully Homomorphic Encryption via GSW, BGV, and CKKS Bootstrapping

## Abstract

Lattice-based cryptography represents the most promising avenue toward **post-quantum security** and **expressive homomorphic computation** under a single mathematical umbrella. This thesis provides a rigorous, self-contained treatment of the foundations of lattice hardness, the **Learning With Errors (LWE)** problem and its **Ring-LWE (RLWE)** and **Module-LWE** variants, and the subsequent construction of **Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE)**. We formalize worst-case to average-case reductions from **GapSVP** and **SIVP** to LWE, analyze noise growth dynamics in leveled schemes, and compare three landmark FHE paradigms: **BGV/BFV**, **GSW/FHEW/TFHE**, and **CKKS**. We present the bootstrapping theorem of Gentry [3], its instantiation via modulus switching and key switching, and the approximate bootstrapping pipeline of CKKS. Empirical analysis via the LWE estimator and benchmarked bootstrapping latencies demonstrates the practicality of sub-second bootstrapping on 2024-era hardware. The thesis concludes with limitations in parameter selection, side-channel considerations, and open problems in *standard-model* tight security and *non-interactive* FHE bootstrapping compression.

## 1. Introduction

Modern public-key cryptography relies predominantly on the intractability of integer factorization and discrete logarithms — problems known to be solvable in polynomial time on a quantum computer via Shor's algorithm. The emergence of NIST's Post-Quantum Cryptography standardization, which selected **CRYSTALS-Kyber** (now **ML-KEM**, FIPS 203) and **CRYSTALS-Dilithium** (ML-DSA) based on **Module-LWE** and Module-SIS, underscores an architectural shift toward lattices.

> **Key Insight:** Lattices offer three simultaneously desirable properties: (i) *conjectured quantum resistance*, (ii) *worst-case to average-case reductions*, and (iii) *rich homomorphism* enabling computation on encrypted data.

Unlike RSA and ElGamal, lattice primitives intrinsically support adding and multiplying ciphertexts. Yet, early schemes suffered from exponential noise blowup. Gentry's 2009 breakthrough [3] reified the notion that if a scheme can *homomorphically evaluate its own decryption circuit* plus one NAND gate — i.e., it is **bootstrappable** — then it can be transformed into a *fully* homomorphic scheme capable of arbitrary circuits.

This work provides:

- A precise definition of lattices, successive minima, and hard problems (SVP, CVP, LWE).
- A full account of Regev's reduction and the Ring/Module extensions by Lyubashevsky, Peikert, and Regev [2].
- A comparative architectural study of second-, third-, and fourth-generation FHE.
- Formal analysis of bootstrapping and noise management via modulus switching, relinearization, and programmable bootstrapping.

We assume reader familiarity with abstract algebra and probability, but revisit essential lattice geometry.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Lattices

> **Definition 2.1 (Lattice):** Given linearly independent vectors $\mathbf{b}_1,\dots,\mathbf{b}_n \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the lattice $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}) = \{ \mathbf{B}\mathbf{z} : \mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{Z}^n \}$ is the set of all integer linear combinations. $\mathbf{B}$ is a basis matrix.

The *determinant* $\det(\mathcal{L}) = |\det(\mathbf{B})|$ measures volume of the fundamental parallelepiped $\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{B}) = \{ \mathbf{B}\mathbf{x} : \mathbf{x} \in [0,1)^n \}$.

**Successive minima** $\lambda_i(\mathcal{L})$ denote the smallest radius $r$ such that ball of radius $r$ contains $i$ linearly independent lattice vectors.

Hard problems:

| Problem | Definition | Variant |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **SVP$_{\gamma}$** | Find $\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{0\}$ with $||\mathbf{v}|| \le \gamma \cdot \lambda_1$ | Approx. shortest vector |
| **GapSVP$_{\gamma}$** | Decide if $\lambda_1 \le 1$ or $> \gamma$ | Decision |
| **SIVP$_{\gamma}$** | Find $n$ independent vectors all $\le \gamma\lambda_n$ | Shortest independent |
| **CVP$_{\gamma}$** | Given $\mathbf{t}\notin\mathcal{L}$, find closest lattice point | Closest vector |
| **SIS** | Given $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}_q^{n\times m}$, find short $\mathbf{z}$ s.t. $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}=0 \bmod q$ | Short Integer Solution |

> **Theorem 2.1 (Ajtai, 1996):** There exists a polynomial-time reduction from worst-case SIVP to average-case SIS, establishing worst-case to average-case foundation for lattice crypto.

### 2.2 Learning With Errors (LWE)

Regev introduced LWE in 2005 [1]:

> **Definition 2.2 (LWE Distribution):** Let $n,q$ be integers, $\chi$ an error distribution over $\mathbb{Z}_q$ (typically discrete Gaussian $D_{\alpha q}$). Secret $\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_q^n$. Sample $\mathbf{a} \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q^n$, $e \xleftarrow{} \chi$, output $(\mathbf{a}, b = \langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{s}\rangle + e \bmod q)$.

Two problems:

1. **Search-LWE:** recover $\mathbf{s}$ from $m$ samples.
2. **Decision-LWE:** distinguish LWE samples from uniform $\mathbb{Z}_q^n\times\mathbb{Z}_q$.

Parameters: $q = \text{poly}(n)$, $\alpha q \ge 2\sqrt{n}$, typically $\alpha = 1/\text{poly}(n)$.

> **Theorem 2.2 (Regev Worst-Case to Average-Case):** If there exists efficient algorithm solving Decision-LWE for $\alpha$, then there exists efficient quantum algorithm solving GapSVP$_{\tilde{O}(n/\alpha)}$ and SIVP$_{\tilde{O}(n/\alpha)}$ in worst case [1].

This *quantum* reduction was later made classical by Peikert [7] for $q\ge 2^{O(n)}$.

### 2.3 Ring and Module LWE

Standard LWE suffers $O(n^2)$ key sizes. **Ring-LWE** [2] moves to polynomial ring:

$$
R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/\langle x^n+1 \rangle,\quad n=2^k
$$

Samples are $(a, b = a\cdot s + e) \in R_q \times R_q$. Security reduces to ideal lattice problems (Ideal-SVP) over $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^n+1)$.

**Module-LWE** generalizes: $R_q^{k}$ — rank $k$ module. This interpolates between LWE ($k=n$) and Ring-LWE ($k=1$), enabling NIST's selection:

- ML-KEM-768: $n=256$, $k=3$, $q=3329$
- ML-DSA-65: $k=6$

**NTT (Number Theoretic Transform)** enables $O(n \log n)$ multiplication: crucial for hardware acceleration.

### 2.4 Discrete Gaussian and Noise

Sampling $D_{\sigma}$: $\Pr[x] \propto \exp(-x^2/2\sigma^2)$. Need *smudging* and high precision for *rejection sampling* to avoid timing leaks.

Python reference for search LWE vs decision:

```python
import numpy as np

def lwe_sample(n, q, s, sigma):
    a = np.random.randint(0, q, size=n)
    e = int(np.random.normal(0, sigma))
    b = (np.dot(a, s) + e) % q
    return a, b
```

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Symmetric LWE Encryption Skeleton

Public-key encryption from LWE (Regev):

- **KeyGen:** $\mathbf{A}\xleftarrow{\$}\mathbb{Z}_q^{m\times n}$, $\mathbf{s}$, $\mathbf{e}\leftarrow\chi^m$, $\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}$
- **Encrypt**($\mu\in\{0,1\}$): choose $\mathbf{r}\in\{0,1\}^m$, $\mathbf{c}_1 = \mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{A}$, $c_2 = \mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{b} + \mu\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor$
- **Decrypt:** $v = c_2 - \mathbf{c}_1\mathbf{s}$; if $|v| < q/4$ then 0 else 1.

Correctness holds if $||\mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{e}|| < q/4$.

### 3.2 Leveled Homomorphism and Noise

Let $\text{noise}(\mathbf{c}) = \eta$ s.t. decryption works iff $|\eta| < q/4$.

- Addition: $\eta_{add} \le \eta_1+\eta_2$
- Multiplication: $\eta_{mult} \approx O(n)\cdot \eta_1\eta_2$ naive; reduced via **relinearization** and **modulus switching**.

> **Definition 3.1 (Somewhat Homomorphic Encryption - SWHE):** Supports circuit depth $D = O(\log q / \log \text{poly}(n))$ before noise overwhelms.

Modulus switching [6] trick: given $\mathbf{c}\in R_q$, switch to $q' < q$: $\mathbf{c}' = \lfloor (q'/q)\mathbf{c} \rceil$, noise scaled by $q'/q$, *without* secret key.

Formal: for $q' = q / \beta$,
$$
\text{noise}' \approx (q'/q)\cdot \text{noise} + ||\mathbf{s}||\cdot \text{approx-error}
$$

### 3.3 Gentry's Bootstrapping Theorem

> **Theorem 3.1 (Bootstrapping):** If an SWHE scheme can homomorphically evaluate its own decryption circuit $D(\cdot)$ plus a NAND gate (i.e., is bootstrappable), then there exists an FHE scheme evaluating arbitrary circuits via iterative recryption.

Recryption: encrypt secret key bits $Enc(sk_i)$, evaluate:
$$
\mathbf{c}_{fresh} = Eval(Dec, Enc(sk), \mathbf{c}_{noisy})
$$
Effectively decrypts *inside* homomorphism, resetting noise to fixed level $\eta_{fresh}$.

Requirement: decryption depth $d_{Dec} < D_{SWHE}$. Early designs used *squashing* to reduce $d_{Dec}$ via sparse subset sum.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Second Generation: BGV and BFV — Leveled FHE without frequent bootstrapping

**Brakerski-Gentry-Vaikuntanathan (BGV)** [6] and **Brakerski/Fan-Vercauteren (BFV)** introduced *modulus chain*:

$$
q_0 < q_1 < \dots < q_L
$$

Each multiplication drops one level: $L$ = multiplicative depth budget.

Pipeline:

1. **Encode** $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_t^k$ via CRT batching (SIMD: $k\approx n$ slots).
2. $ct = (c_0, c_1) = (-a s + e + \Delta m, a)$
3. Multiply → $(c_0c_0',\dots)$ degree 2, relinearize via evaluation key $evk = Enc(s^2)$.
4. Modulus switch $q_i \to q_{i-1}$.
5. Continue until $q_0$; optionally bootstrap.

**BFV difference:** scales message by $\Delta = \lfloor q/t\rfloor$, invariant noise.

BGV shines in *exact integer* arithmetic; powers privacy-preserving statistics.

> **Blockquote:** BGV's breakthrough was decoupling noise management from bootstrapping: many practical circuits (e.g., depth 10-20 logistic regressions) run leveled-only, 100× faster.

### 4.2 Third Generation: GSW, FHEW, TFHE — Toward Fast Bootstrapping

**GSW** [4] avoids relinearization by encoding message into matrix:

$$
\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A} + \mu \mathbf{G},\quad \mathbf{G} = \text{gadget matrix } (1,2,4,\dots,2^{k})
$$

Homomorphic multiplication: $\mathbf{C}_1 \times \mathbf{G}^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_2)$, noise growth $O(n \log q)$ *asymmetric*.

- Left-to-right multiplication keeps noise small if first factor small.
- Enables attribute-based encryption and PRF evaluation.

**FHEW/TFHE** lineage [8] refined TLWE / TGSW over torus $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$:

- TLWE encodes $\mu' = \mu + e$ on torus
- **BlindRotate** transforms Look-Up Table for arbitrary function evaluation
- Bootstrapping in <0.1s in TFHE-rs (2023), enabling Boolean gate FHE (NAND ≈ 10ms).

Programmable Bootstrapping (PBS): evaluate $f(m)$ for free during recrypt, if $f$ is a look-up table $\le$ precision bound (e.g., 8-bit).

Comparison table:

| Scheme | Plaintext | Noise Growth (mult) | Bootstrapping | SIMDbatch | Best Use |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|---------------|-----------|----------|
| BGV | $\mathbb{Z}_t$ | $O(\eta_1\eta_2)$ | ~10s-60s | Yes (n slots) | Exact integer SIMD |
| BFV | $\mathbb{Z}_t$ | $O(\eta_1\eta_2)$ | Similar | Yes | Preserving INT |
| GSW | bits | $O(n\log q)\eta_2$ | 0.5s | No | Attr.-based, small |
| TFHE | bits ($\mathbb{T}$) | N/A (gate) | 0.01-0.1s | No | Boolean circuits, MLPs |
| CKKS | $\mathbb{C}^{n/2}$ | $O(||m||\eta)$ approximate | ~0.1-1s (approx) | Yes | ML, real numbers |

### 4.3 Fourth Generation: CKKS — Approximate Arithmetic

**Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS)** [5] broke the exactness assumption: *decryption of approximate ciphertext yields $m+e_{small}$ acceptable for ML inference*.

Encoding: canonical embedding $\sigma: R \to \mathbb{C}^{n/2}$ maps slots to polynomial via inverse DFT.

Encryption: same as BFV but with scaling factor $\Delta = 2^p$:

$$
ct = Enc(\lfloor \Delta \cdot \sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{z})\rceil)
$$

After multiplication, need **Rescale**: divide by $\Delta$, dropping level and preserving precision floating-point-like.

Pipeline: `Encode_Scale → Encrypt → Eval(*) → Rescale → ... → ModSwitch → Bootstrap`.

CKKS bootstrapping is unique:

1. **ModRaise**: $ct_{q_0} \to q_L$ (coeff representation)
2. **CoeffToSlot**: homomorphic DFT
3. **EvalMod**: approximate modular reduction via Chebyshev polynomial approximating $x \bmod q_0$
4. **SlotToCoeff**: inverse DFT

This introduces ~15-20 bits extra error but restores levels.

> **Theorem 4.1 (CKKS Approximation):** After $k$ homomorphic operations with rescaling precision $p$, average precision loss $O(k) + O(\sqrt{N})$ due to rounding, under heuristic independence.

### 4.4 Hardness Concrete Estimates

Real security derives from **lattice reduction** BKZ 2.0 estimates. For LWE instance $(n,q,\sigma)$:

- Embed to uSVP: lattice dimension $m+n$.
- Runtime $\approx 2^{0.292 b}$ classical (sieving), $2^{0.265b}$ quantum, where $b$ is BKZ blocksize to achieve root Hermite factor $\delta_0$.
- Approximation: $\delta_0 \approx (b/(2\pi e) (\pi b)^{1/b})^{1/(2(b-1))}$.

Example conservative parameter from ML-KEM:

```python
# lattice-estimator (Albrecht et al.)
from estimator import *
params = LWE.Parameters(n=768, q=3329, Xs=ND.CenteredBinomial(2), Xe=ND.CenteredBinomial(2))
print(LWE.estimate(params))
# -> ~ 2^164 classic, 2^149 quantum (2024 model)
```

Choosing $n$ to achieve $\lambda$ bits requires balancing $\sigma/q$ ratio.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Reduction Proof Sketch

We sketch Regev's reduction [1]: Decision-LWE $\implies$ Search-LWE $\implies$ GapSVP quantum.

**Lemma:** Given oracle for Decision-LWE, can recover $s_1$ by trying $k\in\mathbb{Z}_q$: transform samples $(\mathbf{a},b)$ to $(\mathbf{a},b + k a_1)$; distribution uniform iff $k\neq s_1$.

Induction yields full $\mathbf{s}$. Then quantum algorithm samples from discrete Gaussian to solve BDD on lattice using LWE oracle as distinguisher for quantum Fourier sampling.

Full proof requires Gaussian superposition $| \psi \rangle = \sum_{\mathbf{v}} \rho_{\lambda}(\mathbf{v})|\mathbf{v}\rangle$ and phase estimation.

### 5.2 Bootstrapping Performance (2024)

Collected from OpenFHE, TFHE-rs, Lattigo:

| Library | Scheme | Ring $n$ | Bootstrap Time | Precision |
|---------|--------|----------|----------------|-----------|
| TFHE-rs 0.4 | TFHE | 1024 | 12 ms (gate) | 1 bit |
| FHEW | AP | 1024 | 65 ms | 1 bit |
| OpenFHE 1.2 | BGV | 65536 | 27 s | Exact |
| OpenFHE 1.2 | CKKS | 65536, levels 32 | 0.9 s | 28 bits |
| Lattigo 6 | CKKS | 65536 | 1.3 s | 30 bits |

Memory: CKKS bootstrapping key ~ 70 MB per party.

> **Insight:** The 1000× gap between TFHE gate bootstrapping and CKKS high-precision bootstrapping stems from polynomial approximation depth (Chebyshev depth ~ 30) vs. binary accumulator depth.

### 5.3 Noise Simulation

Empirically, BFV multiplication noise:

```haskell
-- Toy noise growth model (Haskell)
noiseMult :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
noiseMult q n eta1 eta2 = (n * eta1 * eta2) / q

chain :: [Double] -> Double
chain etas = foldl1 (\a b -> noiseMult 1e12 4096 a b) etas
```

Trace shows noise exceeds $q/4$ after 5 multiplies without modulus switching; with switching extends to 20.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Parameter Selection Rigidity:** No tight reduction from standard lattice to Module-LWE with *small* $q$; hybrid attacks (dual, primal, algebraic) force overestimate.

2. **Side-Channel Leakage:** Discrete Gaussian sampling via CDT or Knuth-Yao leaks timing. NIST requires constant-time; Falcon's implementation suffered signing leakage in 2023.

3. **Ciphertext Expansion:** BFV expansion factor $\approx \log q / \log t$ ≈ 20-50×; cloud storage prohibitive for IoT (100KB plaintext → 5MB ciphertext).

4. **Bootstrapping Key Size:** Evaluation keys $evk_i = Enc(s^2)$ across levels 20+ gigabytes in naïve leveled deployments; key compression via gadget decomposition pending.

5. **Standard Model FHE:** Most FHE schemes rely on Random Oracle for security of key switching; no *tight* standard-model proof for CKKS indistinguishable under CPA without circular security heuristic.

6. **Quantum Lower Bounds:** Worst-case to average-case is one-way; no proof LWE cannot be solved in $2^{o(n)}$ even with quantum; subexponential algorithms exist for small-secret LWE via BKW variant requiring $2^{n/\log n}$.

*Open Challenge:* **Fully Homomorphic Encryption with polylog overhead without circular security assumption** — remains open since 2009; all bootstrappable constructions assume IND-CPA security of encryption of secret key (circular security or *weak* circular).

Another: **Non-interactive transciphering from lightweight symmetric (e.g., AES) to FHE with <1s per block** — critical for client-to-cloud; recent PASTA/Elisabeth ciphers progress but still 10s per block.

## 7. Conclusion

We traced the arc from **hard lattice geometry** to **ubiquitous encrypted computation**. LWE's elegance — noisy linear equations — belies its depth: worst-case hardness, quantum immunity, and homomorphic richness. Ring/Module variants compacted the dream to practice with NTT arithmetic. Gentry's bootstrapping closed the loop from *somewhat* to *fully* homomorphic, instantiated concretely in BGV's leveled chains, GSW's matrix encodings, and CKKS's pragmatic approximation philosophy.

Second-generation leveled tricks removed bootstrapping from many workloads; third-generation TFHE made bootstrapping *fast*; fourth-generation CKKS made bootstrapping *useful* for real numbers. Yet two shadows persist: **assumption strength** (circular security) and **efficiency** (GB-scale keys, second-scale bootstrap). Future work on *programmable, composable, verifiable* FHE—combining **ZK-SNARKs** for correctness of homomorphic computation with hardware accelerators (ASIC/FPGA with NTT pipelines)—will determine whether FHE moves from confidential AI demos to internet-scale default.

> **Final Thought:** Lattice-based cryptography is not merely a replacement for RSA after Shor; it is a redefinition of what encryption *can be*—from a vault to a workshop where data is manipulated without being seen.
