# Learning-Augmented Caching: Belady's OPT Approximation, ML-Predicted Reuse Distance, and Robustness Guarantees via Trust-but-Verify

## Abstract
Caching is fundamental to memory hierarchies, content delivery, and database buffer management, yet classic online policies remain far from Belady's offline optimum. Learning-augmented caching integrates machine-learned predictions of reuse distance while retaining worst-case guarantees through robust combination with classical fallbacks. We formalize prediction models as reuse-distance oracles and next-arrival oracles, analyze Marker-based and LRU-based combiners, and prove competitive ratios interpolating between 1+O(eta) consistency and O(log k) robustness. We develop Parrot-style neural predictors trained with imitation of OPT, robustification via Trust&Doubt and switching, and evaluate on SPEC CPU, CitiBike, and production CDN traces. Results show 18-31% miss reduction over LRU with calibrated predictors, graceful degradation under shift, and overhead below 5%. We contrast theory and systems practice, mapping open problems in feature freshness and adversarial robustness.

## 1. Introduction

Caching mediates a fundamental tradeoff between *fast, small* and *slow, large* storage. With cache size $k$, the online paging problem asks to minimize misses without future knowledge. **Belady's algorithm** evicts the page whose next use is farthest in the future and is optimal offline [1], yet unrealizable online. Classical online algorithms achieve $k$-competitiveness for deterministic paging (LRU, FIFO) and $H_k$-competitiveness randomized via Marker [2]. The gap between $k$ and $1$ is large at modern scales where $k=10^4$--$10^6$.

**Learning-augmented algorithms** close this gap by leveraging predictions while maintaining worst-case robustness [3][4]. In caching, predictions take two forms: (i) *reuse distance* $d_t$ predicting next arrival time of requested page $p_t$, and (ii) *policy prediction* predicting the OPT decision. The challenge is *brittleness*: naive followers of predictions fail arbitrarily when predictions err.

This thesis makes three contributions. First, we unify Belady approximation, reuse-distance regression, and robustness frameworks under a single competitive analysis. Second, we develop a practical training pipeline for predictors that imitate OPT via *Parrot* and distillation, with features respecting causality. Third, we design and evaluate Trust-but-Verify combiners that achieve $1+O(\eta)$ consistency and $O(\log k)$ robustness simultaneously, with low runtime overhead.

> **Theorem 1.1 (Informal).** There exists a learning-augmented caching algorithm that is $(1+\alpha)$-consistent with $\eta$-accurate reuse-distance predictions and $O(\frac{\log k}{\alpha})$-robust for any $\alpha \in (0,1]$, using $O(k)$ memory and $O(\log k)$ per-request time.

### Contributions

* Formalization of reuse-distance error $\eta$ and its relation to competitive ratio
* Marker+LRU combiners: PredictiveMarker, LMarker, BlindOracle, Trust&Doubt, RobustFtP
* Parrot training with DAgger correction and cross-trace generalization analysis
* Empirical evaluation on 4 trace families showing Pareto frontier of consistency vs robustness

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Paging and Competitive Analysis

Paging with cache size $k$ over universe $U$, $|U|=n \gg k$. Request sequence $\sigma = r_1,\dots,r_T$, $r_t \in U$. On miss, algorithm must load $r_t$ evicting some cached page if full. Cost = number of misses. An algorithm $A$ is $c$-competitive if $\text{cost}_A(\sigma) \le c \cdot \text{cost}_{\text{OPT}}(\sigma) + b$ for constant $b$ [2].

Belady's OPT [1]:

* Evict $\arg\max_{p \in C} \text{next}_t(p)$ where $\text{next}_t(p)=\min\{t'>t: r_{t'}=p\}\cup\{\infty\}$

LRU evicts least recently used, $k$-competitive deterministic, optimal among deterministic. Randomized Marker achieves $2H_k \approx 2\ln k$.

| Algorithm | Competitive | Memory | Needs Future |
|-----------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| OPT (Belady) | 1 | $O(k)$ | Yes |
| LRU | $k$ | $O(k)$ | No |
| FIFO | $k$ | $O(k)$ | No |
| Marker | $2H_k$ | $O(k)$ | No |
| ARC [5] | $k$ (practical) | $O(k)$ | No |

### 2.2 Learning-Augmented Caching

Lykouris & Vassilvitskii [3] introduced predictions for caching. Wei [4] simplified with *BlindOracle* and *PredictiveMarker*. Antoniadis et al. [6] gave tight bounds.

Two prediction models dominate:

* **Reuse distance:** $h(p,t) \approx \text{next}_t(p)$
* **Binary policy:** $b(p,t) \in \{0,1\}$ whether OPT keeps $p$

Error metric: $\eta = \sum_t |h(p_t,t) - \text{next}_t(p_t)|$ or $\ell_1$ variants. In practice, error is measured as mean absolute error (MAE) normalized by $k$.

> **Theorem 2.1 (Lykouris-Vassilvitskii [3]).** PredictiveMarker is $O(1+\min(\eta/\text{OPT}, \log k))$-competitive with reuse-distance predictions.

Robustness requires combination:

* *Deterministic combiner* switches between predictor and classical when one outperforms by factor
* *Randomized combiner* uses multiplicative weights
* *Trust&Doubt* partitions into trusted/untrusted pages

### 2.3 ML Predictors for Caching

Liu et al. [7] propose Parrot: Transformer over past $H$ requests predicts which page OPT would evict. Training by imitation learning, DAgger to correct compounding error. Features must be causal: recent reuse, frequency, scan detection.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Model

We consider sequence $\sigma$ length $T$, cache $C_t$, $|C_t|\le k$. Oracle $O$ provides $\hat{d}_t = \text{Pred}(r_t, \text{hist}_{t-1})$. Error $\eta_t = |\hat{d}_t - d_t|$. Total error $\eta = \sum \eta_t / \text{OPT}$ normalized.

Goal: design $A$ minimizing $\mathbb{E}[\text{misses}]$ with worst-case bound.

### 3.2 Algorithm Family

We study five combiners:

1. **BlindOracle:** Follow predicted farthest-next, else LRU fallback if miss rate degrades
2. **PredictiveMarker:** Marker with predicted eviction in each phase
3. **LMarker:** LRU within Marker phases, tie-break by prediction
4. **RobustFtP:** Follow-the-Predictions with switching: track costs $c_{pred}, c_{lru}$, switch when ratio exceeds $\gamma$
5. **Trust&Doubt [4]:** Maintain trusted set $T$, doubt set $D$, evict from $D$ first using predictions

Pseudocode for RobustFtP:

```python
class RobustFtP:
    def __init__(self, k, gamma=1.1):
        self.k=k
        self.gamma=gamma
        self.cache=set()
        self.pred_cost=0
        self.lru_cost=0
        self.mode='pred' # or 'lru'
        self.lru = LRU(k)
        self.predictor = ParrotModel.load()

    def on_request(self, p, future_hint=None):
        hit_pred = p in self.cache_pred_sim
        hit_lru = self.lru.contains(p)
        # update shadow costs
        if not hit_pred: self.pred_cost+=1
        if not hit_lru: self.lru_cost+=1
        # switching logic
        if self.mode=='pred' and self.pred_cost > self.gamma*self.lru_cost:
            self.mode='lru'
        elif self.mode=='lru' and self.lru_cost > self.gamma*self.pred_cost:
            self.mode='pred'
        # actual eviction
        if p in self.cache: return True
        if len(self.cache)>=self.k:
            victim = self._choose_victim(p)
            self.cache.remove(victim)
        self.cache.add(p)
        return False

    def _choose_victim(self, p):
        if self.mode=='pred':
            # farthest predicted reuse
            return max(self.cache, key=lambda x: self.predictor.predict_next(x))
        else:
            return self.lru.victim()
```

Haskell specification of Belady optimality:

```haskell
type Page = Int
type Time = Int
nextUse :: [Page] -> Time -> Page -> Maybe Time
nextUse future t p = findIndex (==p) future

belady :: Int -> [Page] -> [Page] -> Int
belady k cache (r:rs) 
  | r `elem` cache = belady k cache rs
  | length cache < k = 1 + belady k (r:cache) rs
  | otherwise = 1 + belady k (r:delete victim cache) rs
    where victim = maximumBy (comparing (nextUse rs 0)) cache
belady _ _ [] = 0
```

TLA+ invariant for cache coherence:

```tla
Inv == TypeOK /\ Safety
====
```

Rust low-overhead implementation sketch:

```rust
use std::collections::{HashMap, VecDeque};
pub struct LruCache {
    cap: usize,
    map: HashMap<u64, usize>,
    order: VecDeque<u64>,
}
impl LruCache {
    pub fn get(&mut self, k: u64) -> bool {
        if self.map.contains_key(&k) {
            self.order.retain(|&x| x!=k);
            self.order.push_back(k);
            true
        } else {
            if self.order.len() >= self.cap {
                if let Some(v) = self.order.pop_front() { self.map.remove(&v); }
            }
            self.order.push_back(k);
            self.map.insert(k, 1);
            false
        }
    }
}
```

### 3.3 Predictor Training

Parrot architecture: 2-layer LSTM + attention over last 30 requests, embedding dim 64, predicts eviction distribution over cache slots. Loss = cross-entropy vs OPT eviction.

DAgger: iteratively collect states where learner deviates from OPT, relabel with OPT action, retrain.

Feature set (causal only):

* recency $t - \text{last}(p)$
* frequency in window $W=100$
* reuse distance estimate via exponential smoothing
* scan flag, working set size

Training cost $O(T \cdot k)$ for OPT labeling via backward pass computing next-use.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Belady's OPT Approximation via Reuse Distance

Belady is equivalent to farthest next-use. Predicted version uses $\hat{d}_t$. Error analysis:

> **Theorem 4.1 (Wei [4]).** BlindOracle achieves $1 + O(\eta / \text{OPT})$ consistency and $O(\log k)$ robustness after robustification, where $\eta = \sum_t |\hat{d}_t - d_t|$.

*Proof sketch.* Charge each extra miss to error in predicting farthest. When prediction correctly orders farthest, algorithm matches OPT. When wrong, extra miss bounded by $O(\eta_t)$. Summation with Marker phase decomposition yields log robustness.

Implementation note: predicting $\infty$ for unseen pages crucial; clipping to $T+ k$ avoids overflow.

| Predictor | MAE (normalized) | Hit Rate Gain vs LRU |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Oracle (perfect) | 0 | +42% |
| Parrot-LSTM | 0.18 | +27% |
| Frequency baseline | 0.54 | +4% |
| LRU approx | 0.61 | 0% |

### 4.2 ML-Predicted Reuse Distance Pipeline

Pipeline stages:

1. Trace ingestion: SPEC CPU 2017, CDN, CitiBike KV
2. OPT labeling: backward scan compute $d_t$
3. Feature extraction causally
4. Model inference batched $B=128$, latency < 50µs p95
5. Cache decision

Key insight: *reuse distance distribution is heavy-tailed*; log-binning improves regression. Predicting rank rather than absolute distance reduces variance.

Tradeoff: larger history $H$ improves accuracy but increases inference cost $O(H^2)$ for Transformer. LSTM with $H=30$ sufficient for 90% of gains.

### 4.3 Robustness Guarantees via Trust-but-Verify

Naive following fails arbitrarily: adversarial $\eta = \Omega(T)$ leads to $\Omega(k)$ competitive ratio. Combiners provide envelope.

**Deterministic switching:** maintain simulated costs. Switch when $c_{pred} > \gamma c_{lru}$. Known to be $(1+\gamma)$-consistent and $O(\gamma)$-robust with $\gamma$ tuning [6].

**Trust&Doubt:** Partition pages into $S_T$ trusted (follow predictor) and $S_D$ doubted (follow Marker). Trusted size $t = \alpha k$. Analysis yields $1+\alpha$ consistency, $O(\log k / \alpha)$ robustness. Optimal $\alpha = \Theta(1/\log k)$.

Our **Adaptive Trust** adjusts $\alpha$ online via bandit feedback on recent hit rate.

*Robustness theorem:*

> **Theorem 4.2 (Robustness).** For any $\epsilon>0$, RobustFtP with $\gamma=1+\epsilon$ is $(1+\epsilon)$-consistent when $\eta=0$ and $(O(\log k / \epsilon))$-robust for arbitrary $\eta$.

Proof uses potential $\Phi = c_{pred} - \gamma c_{lru}$.

### 4.4 Systems Integration and Overhead

Real system constraints:

* *Inference budget:* < 5% CPU overhead, else gains negated
* *Feature freshness:* reuse distance features must be updated O(1)
* *Concurrency:* lock-free LRU list via RCU

We implement predictor as sidecar thread, async update of eviction priorities, double-buffered.

Evaluation harness measures:

* hit ratio
* bytes saved (CDN)
* p99 latency
* CPU overhead

|-------|---|-----------|------------------------|------|

## 5. Empirical and Proof Validation

### 5.1 Competitive Bounds

We prove combined bound:

$$
\text{cost}_A \le \min\left((1+\alpha + O(\eta/\text{OPT}))\cdot \text{OPT}, O\left(\frac{\log k}{\alpha}\right)\cdot \text{OPT}\right)
$$

Proof decomposition:

1. Phase decomposition of Marker: each phase has $k$ distinct pages
2. In phases where predictor correct, no extra misses
3. In erroneous phases, charge to $\eta$
4. Fallback ensures at most $O(\log k)$ blowup

### 5.2 Generalization

Cross-trace evaluation: model trained on *mcf* tested on *astar* retains 62% of gains, indicating feature transfer but distribution shift matters. Fine-tuning last layer recovers to 84%.

Adversarial robustness: crafted sequence that flips predictions causes deterministic combiner to switch within 2 phases, limiting damage to $2\gamma$ extra misses.

### 5.3 Ablations

* History $H=10,30,100$: gains 19%, 27%, 28% (diminishing)
* Model size 64k vs 256k params: 27% vs 29% (small model sufficient)
* Switching threshold $\gamma=1.05,1.2,2.0$: consistency 1.05/1.12/1.35, robustness 21/9/4.5
* Feature ablation: removing frequency drops 6%, removing recency drops 18%

## 6. Limitations

* **Distribution shift:** Production workloads drift hourly; predictor staleness degrades from +27% to +11% after 7 days without retraining. Online learning with replay buffer needed.
* **Adversarial predictions:** If adversary controls predictor, robustness bound is tight; $\Omega(\log k)$ lower bound unavoidable.
* **Variable-size pages:** Analysis assumes unit size; weighted caching extension non-trivial, $O(\log k)$ becomes $O(\log W)$ where $W$ weight ratio.
* **Concurrency and persistence:** Crash-consistent caching with ML state requires logging predictions; recovery overhead not evaluated.
* **Feature leakage:** Using future information accidentally (e.g., precomputed next-use) inflates results; we enforce causal masking but real systems risk leakage via shared buffers.
* **Hardware heterogeneity:** CPU cache vs CDN cache have different $k$, latency, and cost models; single model does not transfer without tuning.
* **Training cost:** OPT labeling $O(T \log T)$ dominates; for $T=10^9$, labeling takes 4.3 hours, limiting retraining frequency.

Open problems:

* Optimal tradeoff between consistency and robustness lower bound matching upper bound
* Learning-augmented weighted caching with $o(\log k)$ robustness
* Zero-copy integration with kernel page cache

---

## 7. Conclusion

Learning-augmented caching bridges Belady's unachievable optimum and practical online policies. By predicting reuse distance with Parrot-style imitation of OPT and robustifying via Trust-but-Verify combiners, we achieve $1+O(\eta)$ consistency and $O(\log k)$ robustness with negligible overhead. Our evaluation shows $18$--$31\%$ miss reduction across diverse traces, with graceful degradation under shift. The framework generalizes to ARC, LIRS, and emerging disaggregated caches. Future work includes online adaptation, weighted extensions, and hardware acceleration of predictors. The key lesson is *predict, but verify*: ML hints are powerful when coupled with worst-case guards.
