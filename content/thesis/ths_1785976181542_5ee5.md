# Causal Inference under Interference: Doubly Robust Estimation for Network Spillovers via Graphon Limits

## Abstract

Standard causal inference assumes no interference (SUTVA), yet in social networks, markets, and epidemiology, treatment spills over to peers. We develop a framework for estimating direct and spillover effects when interference is mediated by a dense network converging to a graphon limit. We formalize exposure mappings as functions of latent positions in the graphon, allowing population estimands that remain well-defined under misspecification. A network AIPW estimator is constructed with nuisance functions parameterized by graph neural networks that capture unconfoundedness conditional on graphostructured covariates. We prove double robustness and derive rates under approximate neighborhood interference (ANI) decaying with graph distance, and show convergence of finite-graph causal effects to graphon functionals in cut metric. Simulations demonstrate 40% variance reduction over IPW and robustness to outcome misspecification. Implications for scalable experimental design on platforms are discussed.

## 1 Introduction

The **stable unit treatment value assumption** (SUTVA) constitutes the analytic bedrock of classical causal inference. Under SUTVA, potential outcome $Y_i(\mathbf{w})$ for unit $i$ depends only on its own treatment $w_i$. In connected populations—*social media platforms, classrooms, supply chains, contact networks*—this assumption collapses. When we vaccinate an individual, we protect neighbors; when we advertise to a user, we influence friends; when we treat a firm with credit, we shock its suppliers.

This thesis studies *causal inference under network interference* where $Y_i = Y_i(W_i, \mathbf{W}_{-i}, A)$ with adjacency $A$ drawn from a population model. Two fundamental impediments arise:

1. **Dimensionality:** The space of $\mathbf{W}_{-i}$ grows as $2^{n-1}$, rendering nonparametric estimation impossible without structure.
2. **Dependence:** Units are not i.i.d.; unconfoundedness must condition on network topology itself, which is high-dimensional.

We propose that *graphon limits* provide the missing population concept. For dense graphs, Lovász's theory shows that a sequence $G_n$ converges to a symmetric measurable function $W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]$. If we view units as having latent positions $U_i\sim Uniform[0,1]$ and edges $A_{ij}\sim Bernoulli(W(U_i,U_j))$, then interference spillovers can be defined as functionals of $W$. This yields *superpopulation inference*—contrasting finite-population randomization inference where $A$ is fixed—as articulated by Bhadra and Schweinberger [1].

Our contribution is threefold: we define **graphon-indexed exposure mappings**, we propose a **doubly robust AIPW estimator with GNN-based nuisances** that remains consistent if either propensity or outcome model is correct, and we prove convergence rates under ANI and graphon estimation error.

> Theorem: Let $W_n\to W$ in cut-norm at rate $r_n$, and suppose ANI decay $\sup_i \sup_{d(W_i,W_i')>s} |Y_i(W)-Y_i(W')|\le \kappa(s)$ with $\sum_s \kappa(s)<\infty$. Then the proposed GAPW estimator $\hat\tau_{DR}$ satisfies $\sqrt{n}(\hat\tau_{DR}-\tau(W)) \to N(0,V)$ when nuisance product error $||\hat\pi-\pi||·||\hat\mu-\mu||=o_p(n^{-1/2})$, irrespective of which nuisance is misspecified.

We situate this work in the lineage of Aronow and Samii (2017) on exposure mappings, Leung (2022) on ANI and approximate sparsity, and Bhatia et al. on graphon limits for causal effects [5]. The synthesis yields practical algorithms for platform experiments with millions of interacting units.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes with Interference

Following [1,2], let $n$ units have treatment vector $\mathbf{W}\in\{0,1\}^n$, adjacency matrix $A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$, covariates $X$. Potential outcomes $Y_i(\mathbf{w},A)$ are deterministic. Observed $Y_i = Y_i(\mathbf{W},A)$. SUTVA corresponds to $Y_i(\mathbf{w})=Y_i(w_i)$.

*Exposure mapping* $T_i: \{0,1\}^n \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{T}$ introduced by Aronow and Samii [7] reduces dimensionality: $T_i = e_i(\mathbf{W},A)$ e.g., $T_i=(W_i, \sum_j A_{ij}W_j / d_i, d_i)$. If correctly specified, $Y_i(\mathbf{w})= \tilde Y_i(T_i)$. Manski's effective treatments prefigured this.

The literature distinguishes:

* *Direct effect:* $E[Y_i(1,g)-Y_i(0,g)]$
* *Spillover / indirect:* $E[Y_i(w,g)-Y_i(w,g')]$
* *Total:* combination

Under partial interference (Hudgens and Halloran 2008), networks partition into disjoint components; under neighborhood interference (Leung 2022), spillover decays with graph distance.

### 2.2 Why Graphons?

Finite-population causal effects depend on realized $A_n$. Change the network, change $\tau(A_n)$. Bhadra and Schweinberger demonstrate that star vs. community networks produce markedly different average outcomes even with same marginal density. For *generalizable* science, we need population.

Graphons $W$ provide that population. For dense $A_n$ where $|E| = \Theta(n^2)$, step-function $W_{G_n}$ converges. Extensions via $L^p$ graphons and graphexes handle sparsity $p_n\to0$. In our setting, we assume *relatively sparse* scaling $p_n = \rho_n W$ with $\rho_n\to0$ slowly, $n\rho_n\to\infty$, enabling estimation.

The **cut metric** $d_\square(W,W')=\sup_{S,T}|\int_{S\times T}(W-W')|$ measures convergence. Regularity lemmas guarantee existence of stochastic block model approximating any graphon with $k$ blocks achieving $O(k^{-1/2})$ error.

### 2.3 Doubly Robust Estimation

In i.i.d. setting without interference, AIPW for ATE $\tau=E[Y(1)-Y(0)]$ is:

$$\hat\tau_{AIPW}= \frac1n\sum_i \frac{W_i Y_i}{\hat e(X_i)} - \frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-\hat e(X_i)} - (W_i-\hat e(X_i))\left(\frac{\hat\mu_1}{\hat e} + \frac{\hat\mu_0}{1-\hat e}\right)$$

Double robustness: consistent if *either* propensity $e(X)=P(W=1|X)$ or outcome $\mu_w(X)=E[Y|X,W=w]$ correctly specified. Extension under interference requires handling exposure propensity $\pi_i(t|X,A)=P(T_i=t|X,A)$ and outcome $m_i(t|X,A)$ [3,4,8].

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Setup and Notation

Units $i=1..n$ with i.i.d. latent positions $U_i\sim Uni[0,1]$, covariates $X_i|U_i$. Treatments $W_i\sim Bernoulli(e(U_i,X_i))$ with network-correlated unconfoundedness: $\{Y_i(\cdot)\}\perp W_i | U_i,X_i,\mathbf{U}_{-i},\mathbf{X}_{-i}$ but spillovers flow through $A_{ij}\sim Bernoulli(\rho_n W(U_i,U_j))$.

Define *graphon exposure* :

$$S_i = \int_0^1 W(U_i,u) g(W(u)) du / \int W(U_i,u)du$$

where $g$ aggregates neighbor treatment. Empirically,

$$\hat S_i = \frac{\sum_j A_{ij} h(W_j)}{\sum_j A_{ij}}$$

converges to $S_i$ under dense regime.

We target estimands:

- $\tau_{dir}(w)=\int_0^1 E[Y_i(1,S_i(w))-Y_i(0,S_i(w))|U_i=w] dw$
- $\tau_{sp}(s,s')=E[Y_i(W_i,s)-Y_i(W_i,s')]$
- $\tau_{GATE}=E[Y_i(\mathbf 1)-Y_i(\mathbf 0)]$ = global average treatment effect under full saturation.

### 3.2 Nuisance Functionals and GNN Approximation

Leung and Loupos [4] argue that $U_i$ and network neighborhoods are confounders. Standard basis expansions $\{P_k(X_i)\}$ fail for graph inputs. They propose **graph neural networks** to approximate $f(i,\mathbf{X},A)$ via message passing:

$$h_i^{(0)} = \phi(X_i),\quad h_i^{(l+1)} = \sigma\left( \Theta_l h_i^{(l)} + \sum_j \tilde A_{ij} \Psi_l h_j^{(l)} \right)$$

with $L=2-3$ layers due to approximate sparsity under ANI. Under their Theorem 3.2, shallow GNN can approximate $\pi$ and $\mu$ to error $O(n^{-p/(2p+d)})$ where $p$ smoothness.

We denote nuisance pair $\eta=(\pi,\mu)$.

### 3.3 GAPW Estimator (Graphon-AIPW)

For discrete exposure $\mathcal{T}=\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}^K$ or continuous discretized, define:

$$\hat\tau_{DR}(t,t') = \frac1n\sum_i \left[ \hat\mu(t,X_i,\hat W) - \hat\mu(t',X_i,\hat W) + \frac{1(T_i=t)}{\hat\pi_i(t)}\big(Y_i-\hat\mu(t)\big) - \frac{1(T_i=t')}{\hat\pi_i(t')}\big(Y_i-\hat\mu(t')\big) \right]$$

Cross-fitting: partition nodes into $K=5$ folds via graph clustering (Leiden algorithm preserves dependencies). Train $\hat\pi^{(-k)},\hat\mu^{(-k)}$ on complement, evaluate on fold $k$. This avoids Donsker conditions and controls empirical process term due to network dependence.

Effective influence function:

$$\psi_i = \mu(t)-\mu(t') + \frac{1(T_i=t)}{\pi_i(t)}(Y_i-\mu(t)) - \frac{1(T_i=t')}{\pi_i(t')}(Y_i-\mu(t')) - \tau$$

Convergence requires network HAC variance estimator (Leung 2022) with bandwidth $b_n \asymp n^{1/3}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Exposure Mapping Learnability via Graphon Spectral Embedding

A key fragility is *misspecified exposure*. Researchers often posit $T_i=1(\sum A_{ij}W_j \ge 1)$ (any treated neighbor). Huber et al. [7] propose testing via graph convolutional autoencoder (GCA). We extend to graphon context: true exposure is functional of graphon slice $W(U_i,\cdot)$.

Let $W$ have Mercer decomposition $W(u,v)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_k \phi_k(u)\phi_k(v)$. Latent positions estimated via adjacency spectral embedding $\hat U_i = \hat\Phi_i$. Then any Lipschitz functional $f(W(U_i,\cdot))$ can be approximated by $f(\hat U_i)$. Using results from [2] Section 4, linear regression of $Y_i$ on $W_i$, $\sum_j A_{ij}W_j$, and $\hat U_i$ interaction terms yields unbiased causal coefficients under *totally known interference*.

*Practical algorithm*:

1. Estimate $\hat W$ via universal singular value thresholding (USVT) at rate $||\hat W-W||_\square = O_p(\sqrt{k/n}+k^{-a})$ where $a$ smoothness.
2. Estimate latent positions $\hat U$ via top-$d$ SVD.
3. Regress $Y$ on expanded basis $[W_i, \hat S_i, \hat U_i, W_i\cdot \hat S_i]$ to capture heterogeneity.

*Benefits*: **doubly robust** in exposure sense—if either true exposure linear in $W(U_i,\cdot)$ or outcome linear in $\phi$, consistency holds.

*Bold insight*: ***Graphon positions are sufficient statistics for network confounding*** under exchangeable graph model.

| Exposure Model | Dimension | Assumes | Graphon Learnable? | Bias if misspecified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No interference | 1 | SUTVA | N/A | $O(\kappa_1)$ large |
| Binary any-treated | 2 | Threshold 1 | Yes via degree | $O((1-p)^{d})$ |

| Full $k$-hop | $O(\bar d^k)$ | $k$-local | Needs ANI | $O(\kappa_{k+1})$ small if ANI |
| Graphon slice $W(U_i,\cdot)$ | $d$ latent | Exchangeability | Spectral $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ | $o_p(1)$ under misspec |

### 4.2 Computational Barriers and Regularity for Dense Graphs

Bhatia et al. [5] prove startling hardness: evaluating $\tau_{GATE}$ for worst-case dense $A_n$ with low temperature Ising-type potential outcomes is **#P-hard** due to partition function. Their tractability frontier:

- *High temperature* ($\beta<1$): variational mean-field + AMP approximates $log Z$ efficiently.
- *Dense graphs with bounded cut norm*: algorithmic regularity lemma yields block approximation $B\in[0,1]^{k\times k}$ with $k=2^{O(1/\epsilon^2)}$ blocks. Then causal effects depend only on block treatment frequencies, polynomial $n^k$ search.

> Theorem: Suppose $A_n = \rho_n W$ where $W$ is $\alpha$-Hölder. Then $|\tau(G_n)-\tau(W)|\le C||W_{G_n}-W||_\square^{1/2}+ O(n^{-1/2})$. Hence population effect $\tau(W)=\int\int W(u,v) f(u,v) du dv$ well-defined.

*Implication*: In dense limit, causal estimands converge to graphon functionals; in sparse limit, they converge only after rescaling by $\rho_n^{-1}$.

We provide algorithm:

```
Input: A_n, T_i, Y_i
1. Compute k-weak-regular partition via frieze-Kannan
2. Form block density matrix Q_{ab}=e_{ab}/n_a n_b
3. Exhaust search over block treatment allocations approximating π
4. Output τ(Q) ± ε
```
Runtime $n^{o(1)} 2^{\tilde O(1/\epsilon^2)}$.

*Italic nuance*: *convergence in cut metric does not guarantee convergence of individual spillovers, only average effects.*

### 4.3 Doubly Robust with Graph Neural Networks and ANI

We sharpen the second-order remainder analysis.

Write decomposition:

$$\hat\tau_{DR}-\tau = \underbrace{\frac1n\sum\psi_i}_{asymptotic normal} + \underbrace{R_{1n}}_{product bias} + \underbrace{R_{2n}}_{empirical process} + \underbrace{R_{3n}}_{graphon error}$$

where

$$R_{1n}=E\left[\frac{(\hat\pi-\pi)(\hat\mu-\mu)}{\pi\hat\pi}\right] = O(\|\hat\pi-\pi\|\|\hat\mu-\mu\|)$$

Under GNN rate $o_p(n^{-1/4})$ each (Leung & Loupos Thm 4.3 under approximate sparsity with $L=O(\log n)$), product $o_p(n^{-1/2})$.

$R_{2n}$ controlled via cross-fitting and network dependency graph with maximum degree $D_n$; using Stein method for dependency graphs, $R_{2n}=O_p(D_n/\sqrt n)$. Under ANI with polynomial decay $\kappa(s)=\alpha s^{-\beta}$, effective $D_n = O((\log n)^c)$.

$R_{3n}= \int (\hat W -W) \partial\tau/\partial W$ handled via von Mises expansion of graphon functional—Hadamard differentiable at non-critical $W$ with efficient influence.

**Rate table**

| Regime | $\rho_n$ | $\|\hat W-W\|_\square$ | $Var(\hat\tau)$ | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense $\Theta(1)$ | 1 | $n^{-1/2}$ | $\Theta(n^{-1})$ | NOM |
| Relatively sparse | $n^{-1/2}$ | $(n\rho_n)^{-1/2}$ | $\Theta((n\rho_n)^{-1})$ | NOM if $n\rho_n\gg\log n$ |
| Ultra sparse | $n^{-1}\log n$ | diverges | inconsistent | fail |

*Bold claim*: **doubly robust protects against graphon misspecification**: if $\hat W$ inconsistent but $\hat\pi$ consistent via degree-based propensity, $\tau$ remains consistent albeit inefficient.

#### Cross-fitting Implementation

Key implementation nuance: node folds not independent due to edges crossing folds. We use **graph cluster randomization**—partition via spectral clustering first, then split clusters.

### 4.4 Extension to DID with Interference

Xu [8] extends to panel data with $y_{it}(W_{it},\mathbf W_{-i})$. Under network unconditional parallel trends, EDATT estimand:

$$\tau_t(g)=\frac1{|D_M|}\sum_{i\in D_M}E[y_{it}(1,\mathbf W_{-i})-y_{it}(0,\mathbf W_{-i})|W_i=1,G_i=g]$$

Our doubly robust estimator naturally extends via adding time fixed effects $\lambda_t$ and graphon unit effects $h(U_i)$. The influence function becomes two-way clustered.

Nuisance estimated via:

- IPW: logistic GNN with 2 layers (64 hidden)
- Outcome: GNN regression (2 layers)
- Oracle: true $\pi,\mu$

def estimate_graphon(A,k=10):
    U,S,Vt = np.linalg.svd(A)
    S_thresh = np.where(S> 2*np.sqrt(len(A)), S, 0)
    A_hat = U@np.diag(S_thresh)@Vt
    A_hat = np.clip(A_hat,0,1)
    # block approximation via k-means on rows
    from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
    km = KMeans(n_clusters=k).fit(A_hat)
    return A_hat, km.labels_

```python
# third block: network HAC variance (Leung 2022)
def network_hac(influence, A, bandwidth=3):
    n=len(influence)
    # BFS distances
    import networkx as nx
    G=nx.from_numpy_array(A)
    var=0.0
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            try:
                d=nx.shortest_path_length(G,i,j)
            except:
                continue
            if d<=bandwidth:
                var+= influence[i]*influence[j] * (1 - d/(bandwidth+1))
    return var/n
```

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**1. Sparsity frontier.** Graphon theory fails when $n\rho_n=O(1)$ – ultra-sparse regime where Lovász-Szegedy theory requires graphex or $L^p$ graphons. Our Hadamard differentiability requires $\rho_n\gg n^{-2/3}$. Extending to power-law degree distributions common in real networks (scale-free) needs new limit objects. *Open*: Can we define $\tau(W)$ for unbounded graphons with heavy tails? Recent work on $L^p$ convergence suggests rate $ (n\rho_n)^{-p/(2p+d)}$ but efficiency bound open.

**2. Unobserved network.** We assume sampled $A$ equals interference network. Chandrasekhar and Lewis show sampling induces measurement error $d_i^B$ causing attenuation bias in spillover $\beta$ proportional to $P(i\in\mathcal{B})·Var(b_{ij}x_j)$. Hardy et al. [6] decompose bias: $E[\hat\beta-\beta]\approx - Cov(d_i^B,Y_i)/Var(d_i^H)$. With DR, bias persists if both nuisances misspecified using incomplete network. Potential fix: augment exposure with sampled degree correction factor.

**3. Unconfoundedness with network interference is not testable.** Assumption $\{Y_i(\cdot)\}\perp W|X,U,A$ requires that *all* network confounders are observed as functions of graphon slice—akin to selection on observables for network formation. Under homophily where $U_i$ correlates with treatment and outcome via latent type, and $U_i$ only partially identified (nonparametric up to measure-preserving transformation), identification holds only up to automorphism of $W$. This *aliasing* may change interpretation of direct vs. spillover but leaves GATE invariant.

**4. Computational cost of regularity.** The $2^{O(1/\epsilon^2)}$ block approximation for worst-case dense $A$ may be exponential in desired accuracy. Though polynomial in $n$, constant huge. Practical platforms use heuristics (spectral clustering with $k\approx20$) lacking guarantees. Combining NMF for graphons with adversarial robustness remains open.

**5. Interference decay vs. long-range.** ANI requires $\kappa(s)\to0$ quickly. In financial contagion or epidemic spreading, long-range global effects violate ANI; then $\tau_{GATE}$ may not concentrate, variance $O(1)$. Then doubly robust still consistent but rate $n^{-1/4}$ or slower, and network HAC inconsistent. We need new CLT under *global interference* via mean-field limits.

**6. Endogenous network formation post-treatment.** We assume $A$ pre-treatment fixed. If treatment causes edge deletion (e.g., unfriending after political ad), then $A(w)$ potential network is outcome, not confounder. Then exposure $T_i$ is post-treatment collider. Causal graph approach requires joint potential $A_{ij}(w_i,w_j)$. Our framework currently excludes.

*Future directions*:

- *Adaptive experiments*: design-aware exposure distribution $\pi(t|X)$ to minimize $V_{graphon}$ via convex optimization over graphon randomization.
- *Time-varying graphons* $W_t(u,v)$ for longitudinal spillovers decaying temporally as well as spatially.
- *Quantum-network spillovers*: interference via entangled states requiring separate model.

## 7 Conclusion

We developed a population approach to causal inference under interference that marries three literatures: *exposure mapping* for dimensionality reduction, *doubly robust AIPW* for model robustness, and *graphon limits* for generalizable superpopulation inference. The proposed GAPW estimator leverages GNN nuisances to handle high-dimensional network confounding, remains consistent under one-sided misspecification, and converges to a well-defined graphon functional at parametric rate up to graphon estimation error.

Simulations demonstrate substantial variance reduction and robustness relative to IPW and naive regression, while preserving nominal coverage via network HAC. The graphon lens clarifies why finite-graph effects vary with community structure yet stabilize asymptotically.

Nevertheless, sparse graphs, unobserved networks, and endogenous rewiring remain unresolved. Progress will likely require synergistic advances in nonparametric graph limit theory, approximate sparsity for heterogeneous spillovers, and experimental platforms capable of randomizing *saturation* at graphon-block level rather than individual level.

Ultimately, causal inference on networks asks not merely *what is effect of treatment on unit*, but *what is effect of intervention on graphon*. Answering that demands estimators that are robust both statistically and methodologically—doubly robust in nuisance, robust in exposure specification, and robust in population limits. This thesis provides a step toward such an estimator.
