# Lattice Boltzmann Methods for Multiphase Turbulent Flows: BGK and MRT Collision Operators, Shan–Chen Pseudopotentials, and GPU-Accelerated Phase-Field Variants

## Abstract

The lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) has matured from a kinetic-theory curiosity into a first-class framework for multiphase turbulence: hydrodynamic fields emerge from the streaming and collision of discrete particle populations rather than from direct discretization of the Navier-Stokes equations. This thesis unifies LBM for multiphase turbulent flows: the single-relaxation-time Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) operator and its high-Reynolds-number pathologies; the multiple-relaxation-time (MRT) formalism of Lallemand and Luo that stabilizes kinetics in moment space; the Chapman-Enskog multiscale expansion recovering Navier-Stokes with viscosity nu = c_s^2(tau-1/2)dt; and the Shan-Chen pseudopotential, a mean-field interaction from which phase separation emerges spontaneously without interface tracking. Free-surface and phase-field variants are compared against direct numerical simulation benchmarks - Rayleigh-Taylor instability, rising bubbles, droplet oscillation, and decaying isotropic turbulence - and GPU implementations are analyzed as bandwidth-bound lattice-update engines. Spurious currents, thermodynamic inconsistency, and the low-Mach constraint are confronted directly, with current multirange and consistency remedies surveyed.

## 1 Introduction

Turbulence and multiphase interfaces are each, separately, among the hardest problems in computational fluid dynamics. Together they form a regime — splashing droplets, atomizing sprays, boiling heat transfer, foaming, emulsification — where the physics is governed by phenomena operating across wildly disparate scales: the nanometer-scale molecular interactions that set surface tension, the micrometer-scale diffuse interface, and the meter-scale turbulent eddies that stretch and tear it. Conventional solvers built on the Navier–Stokes equations treat the interface as an afterthought: a geometric surface to be advected, reconstructed, or captured by auxiliary fields (volume-of-fluid, level-set, front-tracking), each carrying its own conservation headaches and topology-change singularities.

The lattice Boltzmann method takes a fundamentally different route. Instead of discretizing the macroscopic conservation laws, LBM discretizes *velocity space* and *time* of the Boltzmann kinetic equation, evolving particle distribution functions $f_i(\mathbf{x}, t)$ on a regular lattice through two steps of almost childish simplicity: **collision** (local relaxation toward equilibrium) and **streaming** (exact advection along lattice links). Macroscopic density and momentum are mere moments of these populations:

$$\rho(\mathbf{x},t) = \sum_i f_i(\mathbf{x},t), \qquad \rho\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t) = \sum_i \mathbf{c}_i f_i(\mathbf{x},t)$$

> **Theorem (Emergent hydrodynamics):** In the hydrodynamic limit of small Knudsen number, the moments of the lattice Boltzmann equation satisfy the compressible Navier–Stokes equations up to errors of order $\mathrm{Ma}^2$ and $\mathrm{Kn}^2$, with transport coefficients determined analytically by the collision operator's relaxation spectrum.

This thesis argues that LBM's kinetic foundation is not a historical accident but a *structural advantage* for multiphase turbulence: because the method lives one level below the continuum, non-ideal thermodynamics, interfacial forces, and even turbulent fluctuations can be injected as particle-level interactions rather than as geometric surgery on the mesh. The cost of this elegance is a set of well-documented pathologies — spurious currents, thermodynamic inconsistency, stability ceilings — which we confront directly.

## 2 Background

### From lattice gases to lattice Boltzmann

The intellectual lineage runs from the Hardy–Pomeau–de Pazzis (HPP) lattice gas, through the Frisch–Hasslacher–Pomeau (FHP) hexagonal model that first recovered isotropic Navier–Stokes, to the McNamara–Zanetti substitution of Boolean particles by real-valued distributions — the decisive step that traded statistical noise for floating-point determinism. Higuera, Succi, and Benzi linearized the collision operator; Qian, d'Humières, and Lallemand (1992) introduced the lattice BGK operator that made LBM a practical engineering tool [5].

The velocity discretization is not arbitrary: the discrete sets $D2Q9$, $D3Q19$, and $D3Q27$ are Gauss–Hermite quadrature rules in velocity space, chosen so that the first several velocity moments of the discrete equilibrium match those of the continuous Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. The equilibrium distribution is a second-order Mach expansion:

$$f_i^{eq}(\rho, \mathbf{u}) = w_i \rho \left[1 + \frac{\mathbf{c}_i\cdot\mathbf{u}}{c_s^2} + \frac{(\mathbf{c}_i\cdot\mathbf{u})^2}{2c_s^4} - \frac{\mathbf{u}^2}{2c_s^2}\right]$$

with lattice weights $w_i$ and sound speed $c_s = 1/\sqrt{3}$ (in lattice units). This truncation is the origin of the method's *low-Mach constraint*: errors scale as $O(\mathrm{Ma}^2)$, so faithful simulations demand $\mathrm{Ma} \lesssim 0.3$, and in practice $\mathrm{Ma} \lesssim 0.1$.

### The multiphase zoo

Multiphase LBM models divide into four families, summarized below.

| Model family | Key authors | Interface treatment | Strengths | Signature weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Color-gradient | Gunstensen et al. (1991) | Recoloring step segregates "colors" | Sharp interfaces, low spurious currents | Lattice pinning, complex 3D recoloring |
| Pseudopotential | Shan & Chen (1993) [1][2] | Interparticle force $\mathbf{F} = -G\psi\sum w_i\psi(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{c}_i)\mathbf{c}_i$ | Automatic phase separation, trivial coding | Spurious currents, thermodynamic inconsistency |
| Free-energy | Swift et al. (1995) | Equilibrium derived from free-energy functional | Thermodynamic consistency | Galilean-invariance errors, instability |
| Phase-field | He, Chen & Zhang (1999) [8] | Cahn–Hilliard order parameter coupled to LBM | Sharp-interface limit rigorous | Extra PDE, mobility tuning |

The pseudopotential model dominates applications precisely because it asks nothing of the programmer beyond a force term — yet its theoretical foundations were, for a decade, shakier than its users admitted. The multirange generalization [3] and the consistency analysis of Sbragaglia and Shan [4] repaired much of this, as detailed in §4.3.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The lattice Boltzmann equation

The discrete-velocity Boltzmann equation with a general collision operator $\Omega_i$ and forcing $F_i$ reads:

$$f_i(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{c}_i\Delta t, t + \Delta t) - f_i(\mathbf{x}, t) = \Omega_i(\mathbf{x}, t) + F_i(\mathbf{x}, t)$$

The **BGK** (single-relaxation-time) collision operator is

$$\Omega_i^{BGK} = -\frac{1}{\tau}\big(f_i - f_i^{eq}\big)$$

with a single relaxation time $\tau$ controlling *all* kinetic modes identically. A Chapman–Enskog analysis yields the kinematic viscosity

$$\nu = c_s^2\left(\tau - \frac{1}{2}\right)\Delta t$$

so that $\tau \to 1/2$ corresponds to the inviscid limit — and to numerical catastrophe, since the scheme's dissipation vanishes while its dispersion errors do not.

### 3.2 MRT in moment space

The multiple-relaxation-time formulation [5][6] maps populations to moments $\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{f}$ via an invertible matrix $\mathbf{M}$ (typically orthogonal), relaxes each moment toward equilibrium at its own rate, and maps back:

$$\mathbf{f}^* = \mathbf{f} - \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\big(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{f} - \mathbf{m}^{eq}\big)$$

where $\mathbf{S} = \mathrm{diag}(s_0, s_1, \dots)$ is the diagonal relaxation matrix. Conserved moments ($\rho$, $\rho\mathbf{u}$) carry $s = 0$; the stress moments carry $s_\nu = 1/\tau$; the remaining "ghost" modes are free parameters that can be tuned for stability and to enforce exact no-slip bounce-back at walls [6]. This decoupling is the single most important algorithmic advance for turbulent LBM: it breaks the BGK straitjacket in which bulk viscosity, thermal modes, and numerical stability were all hostage to one number.

### 3.3 Forcing and the Shan–Chen interaction

Intermolecular attraction is introduced as a mean-field body force. The Shan–Chen force at site $\mathbf{x}$ is

$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = -G\,\psi(\mathbf{x}) \sum_i w_i\,\psi(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{c}_i\Delta t)\,\mathbf{c}_i$$

with coupling strength $G$ and the *effective mass* (pseudopotential) $\psi(\rho) = \rho_0\big(1 - e^{-\rho/\rho_0}\big)$. The force is incorporated through a velocity shift in the equilibrium, $\mathbf{u}^{eq} = \mathbf{u} + \tau\mathbf{F}/\rho$ (the original Shan–Chen scheme), or preferably through the second-order Guo forcing scheme, which restores discrete lattice effects in the momentum equation. Below a critical $G_c$, the homogeneous state becomes unstable and the fluid spontaneously segregates into coexisting dense and dilute phases whose densities satisfy a non-ideal equation of state $p = c_s^2\rho + \tfrac{1}{2}Gc_s^2\psi^2$ [1][2].

### 3.4 The Chapman–Enskog bridge to Navier–Stokes

The analytical heart of LBM is the multiscale expansion. Introducing the Knudsen number $\varepsilon$ as a bookkeeping parameter,

$$f_i = f_i^{(0)} + \varepsilon f_i^{(1)} + \varepsilon^2 f_i^{(2)} + \cdots, \qquad \partial_t = \varepsilon\partial_{t_1} + \varepsilon^2\partial_{t_2}, \qquad \nabla = \varepsilon\nabla_1$$

At $O(\varepsilon^0)$: $f_i^{(0)} = f_i^{eq}$ — the Euler equations. At $O(\varepsilon^1)$: the momentum-flux tensor picks up the viscous stress, and at $O(\varepsilon^2)$ the diffusive time scale completes the Navier–Stokes viscous term. The expansion proves that LBM is not *analogous* to Navier–Stokes solvers — it is a *kinetic discretization whose coarse-grained limit is Navier–Stokes*, with transport coefficients known in closed form. This is what makes LBM turbulence modeling unusually clean: subgrid closures act on the stress moments directly.

### 3.5 Turbulence modeling within LBM

Direct resolution of multiphase turbulence at engineering Reynolds numbers is infeasible for any method; LBM implementations therefore couple to large-eddy simulation (LES), most commonly through a Smagorinsky eddy viscosity computed *locally* from the non-equilibrium stress tensor — a quantity LBM carries for free:

$$\Pi_{\alpha\beta}^{neq} = \sum_i c_{i\alpha}c_{i\beta}\big(f_i - f_i^{eq}\big), \qquad \nu_t = (C_s\Delta)^2 |\bar{S}|$$

The local, algebraic nature of this closure — no Poisson solves, no test filtering — is a genuine practical edge for GPU execution.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The BGK Collision Operator and Its Pathologies

BGK's single relaxation time is both its genius and its original sin. Because every kinetic mode relaxes at $1/\tau$, the Prandtl number is frozen at unity, the bulk viscosity is slaved to the shear viscosity, and — fatally for turbulence — the non-hydrodynamic "ghost" modes that should be damped are instead under-damped whenever $\tau \to 1/2$ (i.e., whenever $\mathrm{Re}$ is large). The result is a well-documented zoo of instabilities: checkerboard pressure modes, spurious invariants, and catastrophic blow-up in under-resolved shear layers. Linear von Neumann analyses [5] show the BGK stability envelope collapsing rapidly with increasing Mach number and wavenumber; in practice, BGK-LBM for $\mathrm{Re} \gtrsim 10^4$ without subgrid modeling is an exercise in optimism. The fix is not finer grids — it is a better collision operator, which is precisely what §4.2 provides.

### 4.2 Multiple Relaxation Times and the Moment-Space Machinery

The MRT framework of Lallemand and Luo [5] and its three-dimensional realization by d'Humières, Ginzburg, Krafczyk, Lallemand, and Luo [6] reconceives collision as relaxation in the space of physically meaningful moments. For $D3Q19$, the nineteen moments include density, three momentum components, energy, energy-squared, three energy fluxes, the symmetric traceless stress tensor, and higher-order ghosts. The relaxation matrix

$$\mathbf{S} = \mathrm{diag}(0, s_e, s_\varepsilon, 0, s_q, 0, s_q, 0, s_q, s_\nu, s_\pi, s_\nu, s_\pi, s_\nu, s_\nu, s_\nu, s_m, s_m, s_m)$$

exposes the design freedom: $s_\nu$ sets the shear viscosity, $s_e$ the bulk viscosity (independently!), and the ghost rates $s_q, s_m, s_\pi$ are tuned to kill the unstable modes BGK leaves alive. Luo et al. [6] demonstrated diagonal lid-driven cavity flow at $\mathrm{Re} = 2000$ where BGK diverges and MRT remains crisp — the canonical stability advertisement. Two further refinements matter for turbulence: (i) *central-moment* and *cascaded* LBM, which relax moments in the co-moving frame and restore Galilean invariance of the viscous stress to higher order; and (ii) entropic stabilizers that adaptively bound the relaxation to enforce a discrete H-theorem. For multiphase flows, MRT's independent bulk viscosity is not a luxury: interfaces are regions of strong compression, and BGK's slaved bulk viscosity is a known amplifier of spurious currents.

```rust
// MRT collision in moment space (D2Q9 sketch): the entire operator is a
// diagonal relaxation plus two dense matrix-vector products.
fn mrt_collide(f: &mut [f64; 9], m: &Mat9, m_inv: &Mat9, s: &[f64; 9], rho: f64, u: [f64; 2]) {
    let moments = m.mul_vec(f);                 // populations -> moments
    let meq = equilibrium_moments(rho, u);      // analytic moment equilibria
    let mut m_star = [0.0; 9];
    for k in 0..9 {
        m_star[k] = moments[k] - s[k] * (moments[k] - meq[k]); // per-mode relaxation
    }
    *f = m_inv.mul_vec(&m_star);                 // moments -> populations
}
```

### 4.3 The Shan–Chen Pseudopotential: A Mean-Field Trick That Works

The Shan–Chen model's audacity is that it *derives* phase behavior from a lattice force rather than imposing it. With $G < G_c$, small density perturbations grow (spinodal decomposition), interfaces form with a finite width of a few lattice nodes, and curved interfaces sustain a Laplace pressure jump $\Delta p = \sigma/R$ — all without any geometric interface representation [1][2]. Three criticisms have followed the model for thirty years, and each has a substantive answer:

1. **Spurious currents.** Discrete anisotropy of the force stencil drives parasitic vortices near curved interfaces, scaling as $|\mathbf{u}|_{sp} \sim \sigma/(\mu R)$ in the worst cases. Shan showed that higher-order isotropic gradient stencils suppress them; the multirange model [3] drives them toward zero with mesh refinement.
2. **Thermodynamic inconsistency.** The mechanical stability condition (from the pressure tensor) and the Maxwell equal-area construction (from the EOS) predict different coexistence densities. Sbragaglia and Shan [4] derived the exact lattice pressure tensor and showed how to tune the pseudopotential to reconcile the two — putting SC "on a solid theoretical basis."
3. **Density-ratio ceiling.** The original model stalls near $\rho_l/\rho_g \sim 10$. The improved forcing and EOS treatments of Li et al. [7] pushed stable simulations past density ratios of **500** at $\mathrm{Re}$ up to $1000$, reproducing droplet-splash power laws — the regime where water–air systems ($\rho_l/\rho_g \approx 1000$) become reachable.

> **Theorem (Laplace law, lattice version):** For a static Shan–Chen droplet of radius $R \gg 1$ lattice units, the pressure jump satisfies $\Delta p = \sigma/R + O(R^{-3})$, where $\sigma$ is fixed by $G$ and the stencil weights; numerical experiments confirm agreement within $\sim 2\%$ for $R \geq 20$ [7].

### 4.4 Free-Surface and Phase-Field Variants

When the gas phase is dynamically irrelevant — ocean waves, foaming, mold filling — tracking both phases wastes computation. The **free-surface LBM** [9] evolves only the liquid, flagging cells as fluid, interface, or empty and advecting a fill level $\varphi \in [0,1]$ with the mass flux across links; missing populations at the free surface are reconstructed from the gas pressure boundary condition. The method captures violent free-surface breakup at a fraction of two-phase cost.

The **phase-field route** [8] is more principled: a Cahn–Hilliard equation for the order parameter $\phi$,

$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot\!(\phi\mathbf{u}) = M\nabla^2\mu, \qquad \mu = \frac{\delta \mathcal{F}}{\delta\phi}$$

is solved alongside the LBE, with surface tension entering as a chemical-potential force. He, Chen, and Zhang's incompressible formulation eliminates the artificial compressibility that plagues pseudopotential models at large density ratios and reproduces the Rayleigh–Taylor instability growth rates against linear theory — the benchmark dissected in §5. The price is a second distribution (or finite-difference solve) for $\phi$ and a mobility parameter $M$ that must be tuned to keep the interface sharp yet stable.

### 4.5 GPU Implementations and the Memory Wall

LBM is the archetypal *bandwidth-bound* algorithm: per lattice update, each population is read once, relaxed with a few dozen flops, and written once — an arithmetic intensity of roughly $1$ flop/byte that no amount of cache blocking can disguise. On modern GPUs this is a feature, not a bug: the algorithm's perfectly regular memory access and embarrassingly parallel collision step map onto thousands of cores with near-ideal scaling. Three engineering choices dominate:

- **Memory layout.** Structure-of-arrays (SoA) layouts guarantee coalesced access; the "AA pattern" and "esoteric twist" schemes collapse the two-lattice ping-pong into a single population array, halving bandwidth.
- **Precision.** Single (or mixed) precision suffices for most turbulent flows and doubles effective bandwidth; MRT's extra moment transforms raise the flop count but remain bandwidth-dominated.
- **Scaling.** Production codes report $10^3$–$10^4$ MLUPS (million lattice updates per second) per GPU and weak-scale to thousands of GPUs for $10^{10}$-cell multiphase turbulence.

### Analytical results

Two proofs anchor the method's credibility. First, the **Chapman–Enskog derivation** (§3.4) shows that the viscous stress emerges as $\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 2\rho\nu S_{\alpha\beta} + O(\mathrm{Kn}^2, \mathrm{Ma}^2)$ with $\nu$ fixed by the relaxation spectrum — transport coefficients are *theorems*, not calibration constants. Second, an **H-theorem** holds for BGK: the discrete entropy $H = \sum_i f_i \ln(f_i/w_i)$ is non-increasing under collision, guaranteeing nonlinear stability of the collision step itself (instability enters only through streaming/dispersion, which MRT then controls).

```python
# Laplace-law verification: measure pressure inside/outside a static droplet
# for several radii; the slope of dp vs 1/R is the surface tension.
import numpy as np
def laplace_test(sim, radii):
    slopes = []
    for R in radii:
        p_in  = sim.pressure_inside(R)    # average over droplet core
        p_out = sim.pressure_outside(R)   # far-field gas pressure
        slopes.append((p_in - p_out) * R) # = sigma if Laplace holds
    sigma = float(np.mean(slopes))
    assert np.std(slopes) / sigma < 0.02, "Laplace law violated"
    return sigma
```

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloging what LBM does badly, because the method's evangelists sometimes do not.

1. **Spurious currents** remain the pseudopotential model's original sin. Near curved interfaces, force anisotropy drives parasitic flows that, while small ($\sim 10^{-4}$–$10^{-2}$ lattice units), can dominate capillary-driven phenomena at low capillary number and pollute long-time statistics. Multirange stencils [3] and MRT's independent bulk viscosity mitigate but do not eliminate them.
2. **Thermodynamic inconsistency**, though much reduced by [4], lingers in the coupling between the EOS-implied and mechanically-implied coexistence curves for aggressive parameter choices.
3. **The low-Mach ceiling** ($\mathrm{Ma} \lesssim 0.1$–$0.3$) forbids compressible turbulence; LBM is an incompressible/low-Mach method, full stop.
4. **Density ratios** beyond $\sim 10^3$ remain fragile even with [7]; air–water at full ratio with strong turbulence is still frontier work.
5. **Galilean invariance** of the viscous stress is broken at $O(\mathrm{Ma}^2)$ by the truncated equilibrium — central-moment formulations repair much of this, but the fix adds complexity.
6. **Interface resolution**: diffuse interfaces span 3–5 lattice nodes, so thin films and near-contact coalescence demand resolution that quickly becomes expensive in 3D turbulence.
7. **Wall modeling** for high-$\mathrm{Re}$ turbulent boundary layers in LBM-LES is less mature than in finite-volume codes; log-layer mismatch persists.

## 7 Conclusion

The lattice Boltzmann method earns its place in multiphase turbulence not by beating Navier–Stokes solvers at their own game, but by changing the game: kinetics first, hydrodynamics as a theorem. The progression from BGK's elegant fragility to MRT's moment-space control [5][6], from Shan and Chen's heuristic force [1][2] to thermodynamically consistent multirange interactions [3][4][7], and from single-phase benchmarks to phase-field and free-surface variants [8][9] validated against DNS, traces a thirty-year arc from curiosity to production tool. On GPUs, where the method's bandwidth-bound regularity is an asset rather than a liability, LBM now resolves multiphase turbulence at scales — $10^{10}$ cells, thousands of GPUs — that were unthinkable a decade ago. The remaining limitations are real but increasingly circumscribed: spurious currents, the Mach ceiling, and extreme density ratios define a frontier, not a verdict. For the vast middle ground of incompressible multiphase turbulence — sprays, foams, emulsions, boiling — the kinetic route has become one of the sharpest tools available.
