# Homological Mirror Symmetry: A-Infinity Structures, Landau–Ginzburg Matrix Factorizations, and Categorical Proof Programs

## Abstract

Homological mirror symmetry, proposed by Kontsevich in his 1994 International Congress of Mathematicians address [1], reframes the physical duality of string-theoretic mirror pairs as an equivalence of triangulated categories: the derived Fukaya category of a symplectic Calabi–Yau manifold should be quasi-equivalent to the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on its mirror. This article develops the full architecture of the conjecture, from the **A-infinity** structure of Lagrangian Floer theory [5] and **derived categories of coherent sheaves**, through **Orlov's matrix factorizations** as the B-brane category of Landau–Ginzburg models [3][4], to the **Strominger–Yau–Zaslow** fibration picture [6]. We give the Polishchuk–Zaslow proof for elliptic curves [2][7], Seidel's Picard–Lefschetz framework for Fukaya–Seidel categories [5], and Orlov's matrix-factorization theorems [3]. A case table surveys proven instances and open terrain, including the compact quintic threefold. Python and Haskell illustrations make the **A-infinity relations** and **theta-function** products explicit. We close with the structural limitations keeping full generality open: virtual perturbation foundations, wrapped versus compact Fukaya categories, and the missing geometric mirror construction for general compact Calabi–Yau threefolds.

## 1 Intro

Mirror symmetry began as a startling physical observation. Compactifying type IIA and type IIB string theory on certain paired Calabi–Yau threefolds produced identical physics, yet the pairs had exchanged Hodge numbers and exchanged curve-counting predictions with period-integral computations [6]. Enumerative geometers quickly extracted predictions of enormous power — the counts of rational curves on the quintic threefold among them — but the mechanism remained opaque. What *was* mirror symmetry, mathematically?

Maxim Kontsevich's 1994 ICM address gave the boldest and most structural answer [1]. Witten had shown that the topological sector of an N=(2,2) supersymmetric sigma model admits two topological twists, the **A-model**, which depends only on the symplectic structure, and the **B-model**, which depends only on the complex structure. Open strings in these models end on **D-branes**: A-branes are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with flat unitary bundles, while B-branes are coherent sheaves on complex subvarieties. Kontsevich proposed that the mirror phenomenon is nothing but an equivalence between the *categories* of these branes:

> **Theorem (Kontsevich's HMS conjecture, informal [1]):** For a mirror pair of Calabi–Yau manifolds X and X∨, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories Dπ Fuk(X) ≅ Db Coh(X∨), where the left side is the split-closed derived Fukaya category and the right side is the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves.

The conjecture is deliberately categorical rather than geometric: it asserts that two ostensibly unrelated mathematical worlds — symplectic topology and algebraic geometry — are the same category in disguise. Its consequences are sharp. Hochschild cohomology of the two sides recovers the closed-string invariants (quantum cohomology on the A-side, polyvector fields on the B-side), so the categorical equivalence predicts the classical numerical mirror identities. Its proofs, where they exist, are constructive and intricate, requiring the full machinery of **A-infinity categories**, **Floer theory**, and **derived algebraic geometry**.

This thesis surveys the conjecture's architecture and its verified instances: the elliptic curve via theta functions [2], quartic K3 surfaces and Lefschetz fibrations via Picard–Lefschetz theory [5], Fano/Landau–Ginzburg mirrors via **matrix factorizations** [3][4], and the SYZ geometric program [6]. We aim to show not only what is known, but *how* one proves a categorical equivalence of this kind — the methodological core of modern homological mirror symmetry.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The A-side: Lagrangians and the Fukaya category

Let (X, ω) be a symplectic manifold of real dimension 2n. A **Lagrangian submanifold** L ⊂ X is an n-dimensional submanifold on which ω restricts to zero; it is the symplectic analogue of a real slice of a complex variety. **Lagrangian Floer homology** HF(L0, L1) is built by counting pseudo-holomorphic strips with boundary on a pair of Lagrangians L0, L1: the chain complex CF(L0, L1) is generated by intersection points, and the differential counts rigid pseudo-holomorphic disks. The theory requires strong exactness or monotonicity hypotheses to guarantee that the differential squares to zero — a condition physicists call **unobstructedness**.

The **Fukaya category** Fuk(X), introduced by Fukaya and Oh–Ohta–Ono, organizes this into an **A-infinity category** [5]:

- **Objects**: (graded, spin) Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with flat unitary local systems — the A-branes.
- **Morphisms**: hom(L0, L1) = CF(L0, L1), the Floer cochain complex, graded by the Maslov index.
- **Composition**: the A-infinity operations μ^k count rigid pseudo-holomorphic (k+1)-gons with boundary arcs on L0, …, Lk and vertices at intersection points.

The A-infinity relations,
∑_{i,j} ± μ^{k−j+1}(x_k, …, μ^j(x_{i+j−1}, …, x_i), …, x_1) = 0,
encode the fact that the boundary of a one-dimensional moduli space of polygons decomposes into broken configurations. The cohomology category H^0(Fuk(X)) is an ordinary category, and its **triangulated envelope** (obtained by formally adjoining twisted complexes and splitting idempotents) is the **derived Fukaya category** Dπ Fuk(X) appearing in the conjecture [5].

Coefficients are subtle: in general one works over the **Novikov field**, a completion of a group ring that records the symplectic areas of the counted curves. For exact symplectic manifolds the theory simplifies, and much of the proven HMS literature works in the exact or monotone setting.

### 2.2 The B-side: coherent sheaves and derived categories

On the B-side, the protagonist is the **bounded derived category of coherent sheaves** Db Coh(Y) on a complex variety Y. Its objects are bounded complexes of coherent sheaves up to quasi-isomorphism; its morphisms are computed by sheaf Ext groups; its triangulated structure encodes extensions as distinguished triangles. Physically these are the B-branes [3]. The category knows the variety: Bondal–Orlov reconstruction shows that, for varieties with ample or anti-ample canonical bundle, Db Coh(Y) determines Y up to isomorphism. For Calabi–Yau varieties this fails — precisely the ambiguity that mirror symmetry exploits.

The **Landau–Ginzburg** extension of the B-side replaces a variety by a pair (X, W), where W: X → C is a holomorphic function called the **superpotential**. B-branes in this model are **matrix factorizations** of W: pairs of projective modules (or locally free sheaves) E0, E1 with maps d0: E0 → E1 and d1: E1 → E0 satisfying d1 d0 = W · id and d0 d1 = W · id [3]. This is a Z/2-graded curved complex — the curvature is W itself. Matrix factorizations arose classically in Eisenbud's study of complete intersections, but Kontsevich recognized them as the correct B-side for mirrors of **Fano** varieties, where the mirror is not a compact Calabi–Yau but a Landau–Ginzburg model.

> **Theorem (Orlov [3]):** For a regular scheme X with potential W, the homotopy category of matrix factorizations MF(X, W) is equivalent to the triangulated category of singularities D_sg(X0) of the zero fiber X0 = W^{−1}(0), i.e. the Verdier quotient Db Coh(X0)/Perf(X0).

This theorem is the bridge that lets matrix factorizations participate in homological mirror symmetry on equal footing with derived categories of sheaves: when the fiber is smooth, the singularity category vanishes, and MF(X, W) relates instead to derived categories of the critical locus via Knörrer periodicity and the LG/CY correspondence [4].

### 2.3 The SYZ geometric picture

Strominger, Yau, and Zaslow gave mirror symmetry its most geometric formulation [6]: a Calabi–Yau manifold with a mirror should admit a **special Lagrangian torus fibration** μ: X → B, where each smooth fiber is a special Lagrangian n-torus. The mirror X∨ is then the moduli space of pairs (fiber, flat U(1) connection) — the total space of the *dual* torus fibration. In this picture "mirror symmetry is T-duality": fiberwise dualization of tori, corrected by instanton contributions from singular fibers and holomorphic disks.

The SYZ picture suggests *how* to build the HMS functor: a Lagrangian section or fiber maps to a skyscraper or line bundle on the dual fibration, and the equivalence should be a kind of **Fourier–Mukai transform** along the dual fibrations. Making this precise — the "family Floer" program — is one of the main proof strategies discussed in Section 4.4, where holomorphic disks bounding the fibers supply the quantum corrections to the naive duality.

## 3 Methodology

Proving an equivalence Dπ Fuk(X) ≅ Db Coh(X∨) follows a recurring methodological template, refined across the known cases [2][5]:

1. **Choose generators on each side.** On the A-side, find a finite collection of Lagrangians whose Floer complexes and A-infinity products can be computed (e.g. vanishing cycles of a Lefschetz fibration, or closed geodesics on a torus). On the B-side, choose a tilting collection or a generating set of sheaves.
2. **Compute the endomorphism A-infinity algebra.** The Yoneda embedding says the whole category is determined by the A-infinity structure maps μ^k among the generators. One computes intersection points (as morphism generators) and counts the relevant pseudo-holomorphic polygons.
3. **Match the algebra to a B-side computation.** Show the generator A-infinity algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the dg endomorphism algebra of the mirror sheaves. On the elliptic curve this is the miracle of theta functions [2]; for Lefschetz fibrations it is Picard–Lefschetz theory [5].
4. **Prove generation.** Show the chosen objects split-generate the whole category: every object is a twisted complex (iterated mapping cone) built from the generators. Techniques include Seidel's exact triangles from Dehn twists, Abouzaid's generation criterion, and localization sequences.
5. **Pass to triangulated envelopes.** A quasi-equivalence of A-infinity categories induces an equivalence of their derived (split-closed triangulated) envelopes, which is the statement of HMS.

Two auxiliary strategies recur constantly:

- **Localization and quotients.** Removing a divisor or stop from the symplectic manifold corresponds, on the B-side, to quotienting by a subcategory or localizing — the mechanism behind Orlov's LG/CY correspondence relating graded matrix factorizations to derived categories of projective hypersurfaces [4].
- **Deformation and versality.** The closed-string moduli on the two sides must match: the deformation theory of the Fukaya category (its Hochschild cohomology, controlled by quantum cohomology) must agree with the deformation theory of Db Coh (controlled by polyvector fields). Checking this is both a test of any proposed equivalence and a source of enumerative predictions.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The elliptic curve: theta functions and the Polishchuk–Zaslow proof

The one-dimensional case is the Rosetta Stone of HMS. Let E^τ be the symplectic 2-torus with complexified Kähler parameter τ, and let E_τ = C/Λ be the mirror complex elliptic curve. Objects of the Fukaya side are closed geodesics (special Lagrangians) equipped with flat complex vector bundles; morphisms are intersection points, graded by an orientation convention.

Polishchuk and Zaslow constructed an explicit functor [2]:

- A geodesic of slope (p, q) with a rank-r flat bundle maps to a holomorphic vector bundle on E_τ of rank and degree determined by the slope and holonomy.
- **Vertical geodesics map to skyscraper (torsion) sheaves**; horizontal ones with flat connections map to degree-zero line bundles.
- Intersection points map to **theta functions**: morphisms on the B-side are sections of line bundles, and the product of morphisms — counting pseudo-holomorphic triangles on the torus — is exactly the classical product formula for theta functions.

> **Theorem (Polishchuk–Zaslow [2], completed in [7]):** There is an equivalence between the derived Fukaya category of the symplectic 2-torus (with biproducts adjoined) and the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror elliptic curve.

The proof's analytic core is an identity of counts: the A-infinity product μ^2 on three geodesics counts triangles of a given area, weighted by e^{−area}, while the B-side product of the corresponding theta functions has Fourier coefficients given by the same exponentials. Kontsevich had predicted precisely this in 1994 [1]; Kreußler later completed the remaining analytic details of the weak statement [7].

A toy computation makes the structure concrete. The A-infinity relations for three objects can be checked symbolically:

```haskell
-- A-infinity relations: sum over insertions of mu^j into mu^{k-j+1} vanishes
aInfRel :: Int -> [[Int]] -> Bool
aInfRel k gens =
  let insertions = [ (i, j) | j <- [1..k], i <- [0..k-j] ]
      term (i, j) = compose (mu (k - j + 1)) (insertAt i (mu j) gens)
  in all (== 0) (map term insertions)
  where
    mu n xs = polygonCount n xs   -- counts rigid (n+1)-gons
    insertAt i f xs = take i xs ++ [f (take j (drop i xs))] ++ drop (i+j) xs
```

And the theta-function product on the B-side mirrors triangle counts on the A-side:

```python
import cmath, math

def theta(z, tau, N=50):
    # classical Jacobi theta: sum over the integer lattice
    return sum(cmath.exp(math.pi*1j*(n*n*tau + 2*n*z)) for n in range(-N, N+1))

def triangle_weight(p1, p2, p3, area):
    # A-side: mu^2 counts triangles weighted by exp(-area)
    return cmath.exp(-area) * len({p1, p2, p3})
```

The equality of these two products — one enumerative, one analytic — is the entire content of HMS for the elliptic curve.

### 4.2 Fukaya–Seidel categories and Lefschetz fibrations

Seidel's program extends the A-side to **Landau–Ginzburg models** themselves: a symplectic manifold equipped with a Lefschetz fibration W: X → C [5]. The relevant A-infinity category is the **Fukaya–Seidel category**: its objects are **Lefschetz thimbles** — Lagrangian disks obtained by parallel-transporting vanishing cycles from the critical values out to infinity along chosen paths. Morphisms count pseudo-holomorphic polygons in the total space with boundary on thimbles.

The algebraic input is **Picard–Lefschetz theory**: the directed subcategory of vanishing cycles in a smooth fiber, with morphisms given by Floer complexes and composition governed by the monodromy around critical values.

The method generalizes the elliptic-curve template: vanishing cycles play the role of geodesics, thimbles play the role of Lagrangians, and the directed structure replaces the slope combinatorics.

### 4.3 Matrix factorizations as B-branes: Orlov's theorems

On the B-side of Fano mirror symmetry, the mirror of a Fano variety Y is a Landau–Ginzburg model (X, W).

1. **Singularity equivalence [3]:** MF(X, W) ≅ D_sg(W^{−1}(0)). A matrix factorization is a way of resolving the singularities of the zero fiber; when the fiber is smooth, the category measures nothing — consistent with the fact that smooth fibers carry no B-branes.
2. **LG/CY correspondence [4]:** for a homogeneous superpotential W of degree d on affine space, graded matrix factorizations relate to the derived category of the projective hypersurface {W = 0} by semi-orthogonal decompositions. When the hypersurface is Calabi–Yau (d = n+1), this becomes an *equivalence* between graded matrix factorizations and Db Coh of the hypersurface.

The following table summarizes the proven landscape:

| Mirror pair | A-side category | B-side category | Status | Key reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symplectic vs. complex 2-torus | Dπ Fuk(T^2) | Db Coh(E_τ) | Proved (weak form) | [2][7] |
| Quartic K3 surface | Fukaya–Seidel of LG mirror | Db Coh(quartic) | Proved | [5] |
| Toric Fano varieties | Fukaya–Seidel of LG model | MF of superpotential | Proved (many cases) | [3] |
| Punctured Riemann surfaces | Wrapped Fukaya | MF of mirror potential | Proved | [3][5] |
| Quintic threefold (compact) | Dπ Fuk(quintic) | Db Coh(mirror quintic) | **Open** | [1] |

Each row is proved by the same template of Section 3, but the analytic and algebraic inputs differ completely — which is why the compact threefold case remains open: no computable set of generators is known on either side.

### 4.4 SYZ fibrations and categorical T-duality

The SYZ conjecture [6] proposes the *geometric* mechanism behind all of the above: dual special Lagrangian torus fibrations, with the mirror as the moduli space of fiber–connection pairs.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We collect the hard theorems — the "empirical" core of the subject, since in this field a proved case *is* the data point.

> **Theorem (Kontsevich's conjecture [1]):** For mirror Calabi–Yau manifolds X, X∨, there should exist a quasi-equivalence Dπ Fuk(X) ≃ Db Coh(X∨) of triangulated A-infinity categories.

> **Theorem (Polishchuk–Zaslow [2]):** The weak HMS statement holds for elliptic curves: H^0(Fuk(E^τ)) with biproducts is equivalent to Db Coh(E_τ), via the geodesic-to-sheaf dictionary with theta-function morphism identification.

> **Theorem (Orlov [3]):** For X regular and W a potential, MF(X, W) ≅ D_sg(X0) as triangulated categories, identifying Landau–Ginzburg B-branes with the singularity category of the zero fiber.

> **Theorem (Orlov [4]):** Graded matrix factorizations of a homogeneous superpotential admit fully faithful functors into, and in the Calabi–Yau case equivalences with, Db Coh of the associated projective hypersurface — the categorical LG/CY correspondence.

> **Theorem (Seidel [5]):** The directed Fukaya category of vanishing cycles of a Lefschetz fibration embeds into its Fukaya–Seidel category; for the mirror of the quartic surface this yields HMS for the quartic K3.

A proof-strategy comparison clarifies the methodology:

1. **Elliptic curve [2]:** explicit functor, theta-function identities, analytic estimates on triangle counts.
2. **Lefschetz fibrations [5]:** thimble generation, Picard–Lefschetz exact triangles, Dehn-twist autoequivalences matched to spherical twists on the B-side.
3. **Toric/Fano cases [3]:** combinatorial description of the LG mirror, HMS via exceptional collections and mutations.
4. **SYZ/family Floer [6]:** construct the mirror space from Floer data, then prove the functor is an equivalence by generation.

```rust
// Pseudocode: the HMS proof template as a verification pipeline
fn verify_hms_case<A, B>(generators_a: &[A], generators_b: &[B]) -> bool {
    let alg_a = a_infinity_endomorphism_algebra(generators_a); // Floer polygon counts
    let alg_b = dg_endomorphism_algebra(generators_b);        // sheaf Ext computations
    let qi = quasi_isomorphism(alg_a, alg_b);                 // the hard analytic step
    let gen_a = split_generates(generators_a);                // Seidel/Abouzaid criterion
    let gen_b = split_generates(generators_b);                // tilting / ample sequence
    qi && gen_a && gen_b                                      // => derived equivalence
}
```

## 6 Limitations

The conjecture's prestige should not obscure how far the general case remains.

- **Compact Calabi–Yau threefolds are open.** The quintic threefold — the original testing ground of numerical mirror symmetry — has no proved HMS equivalence. Neither side admits a computable generating set: the Fukaya category of the quintic is essentially unknown, and the mirror's derived category lacks a workable tilting theory.
- **Analytic foundations.** The Fukaya category's definition requires virtual perturbation theory for moduli spaces of pseudo-holomorphic curves. Complete, published foundations exist in the exact/monotone settings used by most HMS proofs [5], but the general compact symplectic case relies on formidable virtual machinery whose details are still being consolidated.
- **Wrapped versus compact.** Non-compact mirrors (Landau–Ginzburg models) need **wrapped** Fukaya categories, whose morphism spaces involve Hamiltonian chords at infinity. The wrapped/compact dictionary is subtle, and several HMS statements are proved only after passing to idempotent completions or specific localizations.
- **Coefficients and gradings.** Most proofs work over the Novikov field or over C with exactness hypotheses. HMS over the integers, or with the full Z-grading rather than a Z/2-grading, is known only in special cases. Torsion phenomena in Floer theory have no established B-side counterpart.
- **dg-enhancements and uniqueness.** The conjecture is properly a statement about A-infinity or dg-enhancements, not just triangulated categories: a triangulated equivalence may fail to lift. Lunts–Orlov uniqueness results guarantee the enhancement is unique in many B-side cases, but the A-side has no comparable theorem.
- **Beyond Calabi–Yau.** The Fano/LG and general-type extensions of HMS are active but fragmentary: the expected statements involve partially wrapped categories, stops, and singularity categories [3][4], and the dictionary between symplectic stops and B-side localizations is still being written.

These limitations define the field's frontier: every proved case required inventing the analytic or algebraic technology it needed, and the compact threefold case will likely require more.

## 7 Conclusion

Homological mirror symmetry proposes that mirror symmetry *is* a categorical equivalence — that the symplectic geometry of Lagrangian branes and the algebraic geometry of coherent sheaves are two presentations of one category [1]. Thirty years on, the proposal has been vindicated in a growing list of cases: elliptic curves via theta functions [2][7], K3 surfaces and Lefschetz fibrations via Picard–Lefschetz theory [5], Fano varieties via Landau–Ginzburg models and **matrix factorizations** [3][4], all organized geometrically by the SYZ fibration picture [6].

The compact quintic threefold remains the white whale. Its resolution will require either a new Floer-theoretic technology for compact symplectic manifolds or a new algebraic handle on derived categories of Calabi–Yau threefolds — very likely both. When it falls, it will certify that categories, not varieties, are the native language of mirror symmetry.
