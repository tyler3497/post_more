# Pooled CRISPR Screens and Single-Cell Perturb-seq: sgRNA Library Design, MAGeCK Statistical Analysis, and Causal Gene Regulatory Network Inference from Perturbation Data

## Abstract

Pooled CRISPR screening has transformed functional genomics from single-gene perturbation to genome-scale causal interrogation. This thesis unifies three pillars of the modern screening stack: (i) **sgRNA library design**, where on-target activity models such as Doench Rule Set 2/Azimuth and off-target specificity metrics such as the CFD score underpin the Brunello and Brie libraries that superseded the first-generation GeCKO collection; (ii) **statistical hit-calling**, where MAGeCK models sgRNA read counts with a negative-binomial distribution and prioritizes genes by robust rank aggregation across independent guides; and (iii) **single-cell readouts**, where Perturb-seq couples pooled lentiviral perturbations to droplet-based transcriptomics, scaling to genome-wide CRISPRi atlases of millions of cells. Because every cell carries a randomized genetic intervention, these data license causal — not merely correlative — inference of gene regulatory networks via interventional distributions and do-calculus. We derive the statistical machinery, benchmark empirical milestones from vemurafenib-resistance screens to the Replogle 2022 genome-scale atlas, and delineate the limits of current designs.

---

## 1 Introduction

Functional genomics aims to map *genotype to phenotype* at whole-genome scale and single-cell resolution. For decades a trade-off ruled: perturb many genes and measure a crude phenotype (pooled shRNA viability screens), or perturb few genes and measure a rich one (arrayed knockouts). Programmable Cas9 collapsed the perturbation bottleneck; fusing pooled CRISPR libraries with single-cell RNA sequencing — Perturb-seq and its siblings — collapsed the readout bottleneck [1][4].

This thesis treats the pooled CRISPR screen as an integrated statistical instrument with three coupled stages. First, the **library** determines which loci can be interrogated and at what signal-to-noise ratio; the GeCKO → Brunello transition is a story of machine-learned on-target scoring and systematic off-target filtering [1][3]. Second, the **analysis** must convert noisy, overdispersed sgRNA counts into ranked gene lists with calibrated false discovery rates; MAGeCK's negative-binomial modeling plus robust rank aggregation remains the reference solution [2]. Third, the **readout** has moved from bulk viability to the full transcriptome of each perturbed cell, and the resulting interventional data enable *causal* — not merely correlative — inference of gene regulatory networks [4][6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The CRISPR-Cas9 perturbation engine

*Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9, guided by a ~20-nt sgRNA, cuts adjacent to a 3′ NGG PAM; error-prone non-homologous end joining then produces frameshifting indels and loss-of-function alleles. dCas9 fusions extend the toolkit: dCas9–KRAB represses transcription (CRISPRi), dCas9–VP64/VPR activates it (CRISPRa), and base editors install point mutations without double-strand breaks. In pooled screens, tens of thousands of sgRNAs are synthesized as an oligo pool, cloned into lentiviral vectors, and delivered at low MOI (≈ 0.3) so most transduced cells receive exactly one integrant — one perturbation per cell, the foundational randomization of the assay [1].

### 2.2 Pooled screening as a randomized experiment

A pooled screen is a *massively parallel randomized trial*. The treatment of cell *i* is its integrated sgRNA *g(i)*; the outcome is survival, proliferation, fluorescence — or, in Perturb-seq, the entire transcriptome. Random viral integration makes treatment independent of baseline confounders in expectation, which is precisely what licenses causal interpretation downstream (see §4.4). Selection pressure shifts sgRNA frequencies in the population; deep sequencing of integrated cassettes quantifies the shift. Enrichment implies advantage (positive selection); depletion implies essentiality (negative selection) [1][2].

### 2.3 From bulk to single-cell readouts

Bulk screens collapse each perturbation to a scalar fitness value. Single-cell CRISPR screens — Perturb-seq [4], CRISP-seq, CROP-seq, direct-capture Perturb-seq [5] — instead recover each cell's perturbation identity *and* transcriptome. The phenotype becomes a ~20,000-dimensional vector, revealing *how* a perturbation rewires the cell rather than merely *whether* it survives. The price is statistical: sparse, noisy, confounded single-cell data demand purpose-built models for guide assignment and differential expression.

## 3 Methodology

A complete pooled screen proceeds through six stages, each with quantitative design constraints:

1. **Library design.** Select 3–6 sgRNAs per gene maximizing predicted on-target activity (Rule Set 2/Azimuth) while minimizing off-target risk (CFD/MIT scores); include 100–1,000 non-targeting controls [3].
2. **Library construction.** Oligo pools are PCR-amplified and Gibson-cloned into lentiviral backbones (lentiCRISPR v2, lentiGuide-Puro). Transformation efficiency must exceed library complexity by ≥100× to avoid bottlenecking.
3. **Low-MOI transduction.** At MOI λ ≈ 0.3, Poisson statistics give ~22% singly infected and ~4% multiply infected cells; antibiotic selection purifies transductants. Target ≥500–1,000 cells per sgRNA [1].
4. **Selection.** Control vs. treatment arms (drug selection), or propagation so essential-gene knockouts drop out. Use *n* = 2–3 replicates for variance estimation.
5. **NGS readout.** Genomic DNA extraction, barcoded PCR of the sgRNA cassette, deep sequencing. Read counts per sgRNA form the count matrix *K* (guides × samples) [2].
6. **Statistical analysis.** Median-ratio normalization, negative-binomial testing of sgRNA differential abundance, robust rank aggregation to gene-level calls with FDR control [2].

For Perturb-seq, stage 5 becomes droplet scRNA-seq linking each cell's transcriptome to its sgRNA via a co-expressed barcode or direct guide capture [4][5]; stage 6 becomes guide assignment, per-perturbation differential expression, and network inference.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 sgRNA Library Design: On-Target Activity Meets Off-Target Specificity

Library quality dominates screen outcome, so two predictive problems must be solved per candidate spacer.

**On-target activity.** Doench *et al.* trained successive models on thousands of sgRNAs with measured efficacy. Rule Set 1 (2014) used logistic regression on position-specific nucleotide features; Rule Set 2, implemented as **Azimuth** (2016), used gradient-boosted regression trees over position-specific nucleotides, dinucleotides, GC content, PAM-proximal thermodynamics, and accessibility — substantially improving predicted-vs-measured correlation [3]. The Brunello (human) and Brie (mouse) libraries, built from top Rule Set 2 guides, show per-guide activity distributions shifted far above GeCKO v1/v2, achieving equal or better power with 4 guides per gene instead of 6 [3].

| Library | Organism | sgRNAs | Genes | Guides/gene | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GeCKO v1 | Human | 64,751 | 18,080 | 3–4 | Early heuristic |
| GeCKO v2 | Human | 123,411 | 19,050 | 6 | Heuristic + controls |
| Brunello | Human | 76,441 | 19,114 | 4 | Rule Set 2 (Azimuth) |
| Brie | Mouse | 78,637 | 19,674 | 4 | Rule Set 2 (Azimuth) |
| TKOv3 | Human | 70,948 | 18,053 | 4 | Empirical rules |

**Off-target specificity.** Cas9 tolerates mismatches, especially PAM-distal ones. The **MIT score** (Hsu *et al.* 2013) applies position-weighted mismatch penalties; the **CFD (cutting frequency determination) score** (Doench *et al.* 2016) makes weights *mismatch-type-specific* (an A→G mismatch at position 15 differs from A→T there) and outperformed MIT against GUIDE-seq-measured off-target cleavage [3]. Designers require clean CFD profiles — no high-scoring exonic off-targets — since aggregate off-target burden degrades signal even when individual sites are weak [3].

> **Theorem 1 (Design monotonicity, informal).** *Holding coverage fixed, replacing a guide cohort with one of stochastically greater Rule Set 2 scores and stochastically smaller aggregate CFD burden strictly increases the expected signal-to-noise ratio of the gene-level test — the formal justification for the GeCKO → Brunello transition.*

### 4.2 MAGeCK: Negative-Binomial Modeling and Robust Rank Aggregation

MAGeCK (Model-based Analysis of Genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 Knockout) [2] converts the count matrix *K* — *K_{ij}* reads for guide *i* in sample *j* — into ranked gene lists.

**Normalization.** Depth varies across samples, and compositional effects (a few strongly selected guides consuming read share) distort total-count scaling. MAGeCK uses *median-ratio* size factors, *s_j* = median_i(*K_{ij}* / geometric-mean_i(*K_{ij}*)), robust to asymmetric selection [2].

**Negative-binomial model.** Counts are overdispersed, so MAGeCK fits *K_{ij} ~ NB(μ_{ij}, α_i)* with *Var = μ + αμ²*, estimating the mean–variance trend by linear regression across guides and shrinking per-guide dispersions toward it — borrowing strength across ~10⁵ guides to stabilize estimates from 2–3 replicates [2]. A negative-binomial test yields per-guide *p*-values for enrichment *and* depletion, reporting positive and negative selection simultaneously [2].

**Robust rank aggregation (RRA).** The decisive step is the gene-level summary. With *n* guides per gene, let *r_(1) ≤ … ≤ r_(n)* be their sorted normalized ranks among *N* guides. The RRA statistic is

*ρ* = min_{k=1..n} *β_{k,n}*( *r_(k)* ),

where *β_{k,n}(x)* is the Beta(*k*, *n−k+1*) tail probability that the *k*-th order statistic of *n* i.i.d. Uniform(0,1) variates falls at or below *x* (adapted from Kolde *et al.* 2012). Intuitively, *ρ* detects an unexpectedly well-ranked *prefix* of a gene's guides, and it is robust because one dead guide merely fails to contribute rather than vetoing the gene [2].

> **Theorem 2 (RRA calibration).** *Under the null of guide exchangeability, normalized ranks are order statistics of i.i.d. Uniform(0,1) variates. MAGeCK's α-RRA computes permutation p-values for ρ and applies Benjamini–Hochberg, yielding gene-level FDR control that is empirically robust down to ~1M reads [2].*

Reference workflow:

```python
# MAGeCK count + test (mageck 0.5.9)
import subprocess
subprocess.run(["mageck", "count", "-l", "brunello_library.csv",
                "--sample-label", "day0,day14_r1,day14_r2",
                "--fastq", "d0.fq,d14r1.fq,d14r2.fq",
                "-n", "screen_counts"], check=True)
subprocess.run(["mageck", "test", "-k", "screen_counts.count.txt",
                "-t", "day14_r1,day14_r2", "-c", "day0",
                "-n", "screen_mageck"], check=True)
# -> screen_mageck.gene_summary.txt: RRA p-values, FDR, LFC per gene
```

MAGeCK outperformed RIGER and RSA on benchmarks, finding more consistent hits across CRISPR and shRNA screens of the same system; MAGeCK-MLE extends the framework to multi-condition designs via per-guide *β* coefficients in a generalized linear model [2].

### 4.3 Perturb-seq: Coupling Pooled Perturbations to Single-Cell Transcriptomes

**Perturb-seq** (Dixit *et al.*, *Cell* 2016) reads the full transcriptional state of every perturbed cell, turning each guide into a ~20,000-dimensional phenotype [4].

**Vector engineering.** Each lentiviral vector carries the sgRNA cassette *and* a polyadenylated **guide barcode (GBC)** identifying the guide, so standard droplet scRNA-seq recovers guide identity with mRNA [4]. Refinements capture the guide directly: CROP-seq (Datlinger *et al.* 2017) embeds the sgRNA in a polyadenylated transcript, and **direct-capture Perturb-seq** (Replogle *et al.*, *Nat. Biotechnol.* 2020) adds capture sequences to the sgRNA scaffold for 10x Feature Barcoding readout and combinatorial dual-guide screens [5].

**The founding studies.** Dixit *et al.* perturbed immune transcription factors in LPS-stimulated dendritic cells and cell-cycle regulators in K562 with Cas9 knockouts, recovering coherent gene modules and cell states [4]. Adamson *et al.* used CRISPRi (dCas9–KRAB) with up to three perturbations per cell to dissect the unfolded protein response, resolving the IRE1α/XBP1, PERK/ATF4, and ATF6 branches and their epistasis — combinatorial analysis impossible in bulk [4].

**Guide assignment and DE.** Ambient guide molecules make assignment probabilistic: a two-component Poisson–Gaussian mixture on guide UMIs per cell assigns cells with posterior > 0.5 to the upper component [5]. **Mixscape** (Papalexi *et al.*, *Nat. Genet.* 2021) then separates successfully perturbed cells from "escapers" resembling non-targeting controls, and **SCEPTRE** provides calibrated differential-expression tests. A standard pseudobulk analysis:

```r
# Per-perturbation DE on Mixscape-filtered pseudobulk counts
library(DESeq2)
dds <- DESeqDataSetFromMatrix(countData = pseudobulk_counts,
                              colData = coldata,
                              design = ~ perturbation)
dds <- DESeq(dds)
res <- results(dds, contrast = c("perturbation", "CEBPB_KO", "NT"))
res[order(res$padj), ]
```

| Direct-capture (Replogle 2020) | CRISPRi/a | Scaffold capture | Combinatorial |

**Genome-scale Perturb-seq.** Replogle *et al.* (*Cell* 2022) scaled CRISPRi Perturb-seq to the expressed genome: ~2.5M K562 cells, ~2,058 perturbations, ~1,000 cells each, via native 10x 3′ direct capture [6]. Clustering by perturbation signature recovered complexes and pathways *de novo*, ranked chromosomal-instability drivers by karyotype phenotype, and assigned functions to uncharacterized genes by perturbation-profile similarity [6].

### 4.4 From Perturbation Responses to Causal Gene Regulatory Networks

Observational methods — GENIE3 (random forests), ARACNe (mutual information + data-processing inequality), SCENIC — infer edges from co-expression and cannot distinguish regulation from co-regulation. Perturb-seq changes the epistemic status of the data because perturbations are **interventions** in Pearl's sense.

Let *X* be the perturbed gene and *Y* a candidate target. Observational data give only *P(Y | X)*, confounded by shared upstream causes. In a pooled screen, guide assignment *G* is randomized by viral integration and *X* is a deterministic function of *G*, so

*P(Y | do(X = x)) = P(Y | X = x)*,

identifying the interventional distribution directly. An edge *X → Y* is supported when perturbing *X* shifts *Y*'s distribution versus non-targeting controls — a test on *interventional*, not observational, contrasts [6].

> **Theorem 3 (Causal identifiability under randomized perturbation).** *If guide assignment is independent of baseline cell state (low-MOI transduction) and the perturbation affects Y only through X (exclusion, modulo measured off-targets), then E[Y | do(X=x)] − E[Y | do(X=x₀)] is identified by the control contrast of single-cell expression distributions, orienting X → Y without faithfulness assumptions beyond the design.*

Inference pipelines proceed in stages: (i) per-perturbation DE (pseudobulk DESeq2 on Mixscape-filtered cells, or SCEPTRE) yields a perturbation × gene response matrix; (ii) sparse regression or tree ensembles on *interventional* contrasts orient edges; (iii) tools like **CellOracle** simulate *in silico* perturbations through the inferred graph to predict unseen knockouts, closing the loop [6]. The Replogle 2022 atlas is the proving ground: regulators of ribosome biogenesis and mitochondrial programs inferred this way validated against independent genetic evidence [6].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

**The GeCKO proof of concept.** Shalem *et al.* (*Science* 2014) delivered GeCKO (64,751 guides, 18,080 genes) and ran negative selection (essential genes in cancer and pluripotent stem cells) and positive selection (vemurafenib resistance in BRAF-mutant A375 melanoma) [1]. Top resistance hits included known mediators *NF1*, *MED12* and novel candidates *NF2*, *CUL3*, *TADA2B*, *TADA1*, with high concordance among independent guides per gene — the empirical fact that makes guide-level replication the unit of evidence [1].

**MAGeCK's re-analysis dividend.** On public datasets MAGeCK [2] recovered biology original analyses missed: *EGFR* in vemurafenib-treated A375, *BCR*/*ABL1* essentiality in BCR-ABL-fusion KBM7 cells, *IGF1R* in HL-60, and novel positively selected genes *CDH13* (FDR = 1.7×10⁻²) and *PPT1*. Downsampling showed >80% of gene-level calls retained at 1M reads, demonstrating RRA's robustness to depth [2].

**Perturb-seq's regulatory maps.** Dixit *et al.* recovered module-level programs from TF perturbations in dendritic cells; Adamson *et al.* decomposed the UPR into its three branches with epistasis mapping [4]. At genome scale, Replogle *et al.* (2022) converted 2.5M perturbed transcriptomes into a functional atlas — *de novo* complex discovery, aneuploidy-driver ranking, and function assignment for uncharacterized genes [6].

*Proof sketch, Theorem 2.* Under the null each guide's rank is uniform over *N* positions, so normalized ranks are i.i.d. *U(0,1)* and the *k*-th order statistic is Beta(*k*, *n−k+1*). Permuting guide labels gives an exact null for *ρ*; Benjamini–Hochberg on these permutation p-values controls FDR under standard PRDS conditions [2].

*Proof sketch, Theorem 3.* Random guide assignment gives *G ⫫ (Y(0), Y(1))*; with consistency and SUTVA (enforced by low MOI and single-cell containment), *E[Y(1) − Y(0)]* is identified by the observed contrast. Orientation follows because the intervention targets *X* by construction, so systematic shifts in *Y* must flow downstream of *X* absent off-target confounding [4][6].

## 6 Limitations

- **Editing heterogeneity.** In-frame indels leave functional protein; single-cell editing efficiency varies, creating "escapers" that dilute effects. CRISPRi avoids breaks but suffers incomplete knockdown and KRAB spreading to neighboring promoters [4][5].
- **Off-target and paralog confounding.** Residual CFD-escaping off-targets persist, and paralog compensation masks phenotypes of duplicated genes — blind spots no statistic can fully correct [3].
- **Single-cell statistical fragility.** Ambient barcodes, doublets, cell-cycle and batch confounders, and extreme sparsity inflate false positives in naive per-cell tests; Mixscape and SCEPTRE mitigate but do not eliminate these [5].
- **Causal scope.** Perturb-seq identifies *total* causal effects, not direct edges: unmeasured mediators are invisible, feedback loops violate DAG assumptions, and off-targets break exclusion. Network inference remains hypothesis generation requiring orthogonal validation [6].
- **Scale economics.** A genome-scale atlas costs ~$50–100K and needs ~10³ cells per perturbation for stable DE; rare cell types and *in vivo* screens remain frontier applications [6].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Pooled CRISPR screening matured through three coupled revolutions: libraries engineered by machine-learned activity and specificity models (GeCKO → Brunello/Brie) [1][3]; statistics that tame overdispersed counts and aggregate weak guide signals into calibrated gene calls (MAGeCK's negative-binomial RRA) [2]; and readouts escalating from scalar fitness to the full transcriptome (Perturb-seq → genome-scale CRISPRi atlases) [4][6]. The deepest consequence is epistemic: randomized perturbations make these datasets *interventional*, licensing causal gene regulatory network inference with edge orientation earned by design rather than assumed by faithfulness. Remaining challenges — editing heterogeneity, off-target confounding, single-cell calibration, and cost — define the next decade. As compressed and combinatorial designs drive costs down, a complete causal map of gene function is moving from aspiration to engineering plan.
