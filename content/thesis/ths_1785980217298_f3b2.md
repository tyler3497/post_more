# Concurrent Separation Logic Evolved: Iris Ghost State, Later Modalities, and Mechanized Linearizability Proofs of Michael-Scott Queue and Harris List

## Abstract
Iris provides a ***higher-order concurrent separation logic framework*** built atop resource algebras and guarded recursion. This thesis shows how ***Iris subsumes classical Concurrent Separation Logic*** while enabling mechanized verification of computationally challenging lock-free structures. We examine the algebraic foundation of ghost state, the guarded fixpoint interpretation of the later modality $\\triangleright$, and its role in sound step-indexing. We then mechanize two landmark linearizability proofs: the Michael-Scott queue (1996) with its two-step enqueue helping protocol, and the Harris list (2001) with marked-pointer deletion and logical atomicity. The resulting Coq developments are extensible, compositional, and computationally adequate via Iris's adequacy theorem. We evaluate proof complexity, invariant granularity, and reuse, and identify current limitations around liveness and weak memory.

## 1 Introduction
***Concurrent separation logic (CSL)*** was introduced by O'Hearn [2007] and Brookes to extend Reynolds-O'Hearn separation logic with disjoint parallelism $\\{P_1\\} C_1 \\{Q_1\\} \\ast \\{P_2\\} C_2 \\{Q_2\\}$. Yet CSL relied on global resource invariants and syntactic lock discipline, insufficient for *fine-grained* optimistic concurrency where synchronization is realized by `CAS` loops rather than locks.

The challenge is threefold:

- **Locality vs Sharing:** How to reason locally when threads share unbounded heap via pointers that may become logically deleted before physical removal?
- **Recursion via Heap:** How to define $\\mathsf{Inv} : \\mathsf{iProp}$ recursively when invariants refer to themselves via stored protocols, as in higher-order ghost state?
- **Linearization in the Future:** How to assign a linearization point that is determined by another thread's action, the classic *helping* problem?

Iris [Jung et al., ICFP 2018] resolves these via a *unifying semantic model*: unital resource algebras (uRA) for user-defined ghost resources, monoid-based invariants with $\\triangleright$-guarded impredicativity, and atomic triples for *logical atomicity*. This thesis demonstrates those advances through two definitions considered canonical in Herlihy & Wing [1990] linearizability theory.

*Contributions:*
1. A unified exposition of CSL → Iris evolution: from $R \\vdash \\{P\\}C\\{Q\\}$ to $\\mathsf{wp}\\, e \\, \\{ \\Phi \\}$.
2. Deep dive into Iris ghost theory $\\ownGhost{\\gamma}{a}$, invariant $\\knowInv{N}{P}$, and later modality $\\triangleright P$ via Nakano [2000] and Appel et al. [2007] indirection theory.
3. End-to-end mechanized linearizability specifications for **Michael-Scott Queue** and **Harris List**, including helping via authoritative sets and marked pointer encodings.
4. Quantitative evaluation of proof effort and reusable proof patterns identified in Iris project.

## 2 Background
### 2.1 From Separation Logic to Concurrency
Separation Logic introduces $P \\ast Q$: heap can be split into disjoint parts satisfying $P$ and $Q$ respectively, enabling frame rule:
$$ \\frac{\\{P\\}C\\{Q\\}}{\\{P \\ast R\\}C\\{Q \\ast R\\}} $$
with $\\mathsf{mod}(C) \\cap \\mathsf{fv}(R) = \\emptyset$.

O'Hearn's **Resources, Concurrency and Local Reasoning** [2007] extended this with *ownership transfer*: shared resource $R$ with invariant $I_R$ is not owned by any thread; threads transiently acquire ownership inside atomic blocks $\\langle C \\rangle$ where
$$ \\frac{R: \\{P \\ast I_R\\} C \\{Q \\ast I_R\\}}{R \\vdash \\{P\\} \\langle C \\rangle \\{Q\\}} $$
This sufficed for semaphore-split mergesort and pointer-transferring buffers, but failed for:

- lock-free structures with no critical sections,
- interference that *changes* logical state without restoring $I_R$ identically,
- dynamic allocation of resources unknown statically.

### 2.2 Linearizability
Herlihy & Wing [1990] define history $H$ linearizable iff it can be extended to $H'$ and there exists legal sequential history $S$ equivalent to $\\mathsf{complete}(H')$ with $<_H \\subseteq <_S$. Intuitively, each operation appears instantaneous at its *linearization point* between invocation and response.

Proving linearizability for MSQ and Harris list reduces to exhibiting for every interleaving a linearization point consistent with abstract set/queue semantics.

| Criterion | Description | Preserves |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential Consistency | Exists $S$ consistent with per-thread program order | Program order |
| Linearizability | + respects real-time order | Real-time + compositionality |
| Quiescent Consistency | Only quiescent states linearize | Eventual |

Iris internalizes linearizability via *logically atomic triple*:
$\\langle x. \\; \\alpha(x) \\mid P \\rangle e \\langle v. \\; Q(v) \\rangle$ where linearization is allowed to occur at any $\\triangleright$-opened invariant step.

## 3 Methodology (Iris Framework)
Our methodology follows the **Iris 3.1 base logic** construction [Jung et al. 2018]: a minimal logic with $\\ast, \\wand, \\triangleright, \\mu, \\own$ built over a uRA $M$, from which program logic $\\mathsf{HeapLang}$ is derived as shallow embedding.

### Architecture
1. **Language parameterization** via state interpretation $S(\\sigma)$ and wp: $\\mathsf{wp}_{\\mathcal{E}} e \\{ \\Phi \\}$ parametric in language.
2. **Resource algebra instantiation**: pick $M := \\mathsf{Auth}(X) \\cdot \\mathsf{Frac} \\cdot \\mathsf{Excl} \\cdot \\mathsf{GMap}$ to model queue tail lag, mark bits, abstract list.
3. **Invariants as persistent knowledge**: $\\knowInv{I}{N} : \\square P$ where $N \\in \\mathsf{namespace}$ prevents re-entrance.
4. **View shifts** $P \\vsW{M_1}{M_2} Q$ for ghost updates without program steps, modeling linearization commits.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Iris Adequacy).** If $\\vdash \\{\\mathsf{True}\\} e \\{ v. v = () \\}$ in Iris with empty masks, then safe execution of $e$ never gets stuck; if postcondition implies $\\phi(v)$, then any terminating trace satisfies $\\phi$.

We work in Coq 8.18 + Iris 4.2 dev + iris-atomic library. All definitions are `Definition` or `Fixpoint` with `Next Obligation` proving `Contractive` for guarded fixpoints.

Key MoSeL tactics used:
- `iProp` intro/elim: `iIntros`, `iDestruct "#Hinv" as (Hγ & Hlist)`,
- `iInv N as (x) "Hclose"`, `iMod`, `iModIntro`,
- `wp_cas_suc`, `wp_load`, `wp_store`,
- `iApply atomic_update`, `iAuIntro`.

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 Iris Ghost State & Resources & Invariants
***Ghost state*** decouples physical $\\ell \\mapsto v$ from logical ownership. In Iris, every piece of ghost is an element $a : M$ owned via $\\ownGhost{\\gamma}{a}$ where $M$ is a uRA with validity $\\mathcal{V}(a)$.

- **Exclusive** $\\mathsf{Ex}(X) := X_{\\bot}$ with $x \\cdot y$ invalid unless one $\\bot$, models thread-local linearization token.
- **Fractional** $\\mathsf{Frac}(q, a)$, $q \\in (0,1]$, enables read-sharing of node invariant.
- **Authoritative** $\\mathsf{Auth}(A)$ with $\\bullet a$ (authority) and $\\circ b$ (fragment): $\\mathcal{V}(\\bullet a \\cdot \\circ b) \\iff b \\preceq a$. Used for abstract queue state $\\mathsf{AbsQ} : \\mathsf{list}\\, \\mathsf{Val}$.

In Iris 3.1, invariants are *impredicative*: $\\mathsf{Inv} : \\mathsf{iProp} \\to \\mathsf{iProp}$ can contain $\\knowInv{P}$ itself, with stratification enforced by $\\triangleright$. Invariant opening:
$$ \\infer{ \\knowInv{N}{P} \\ast \\triangleright P \\vsW{E \\setminus N}{E} \\triangleright P \\ast (\\triangleright P \\vsW{E \\setminus N}{E} \\mathsf{True}) }{ } $$
This permits temporarily exploiting $P$ for atomic steps, then restoring it – precisely what MSQ needs when swinging `tail->next`.

For Harris, we allocate two ghost maps:

```coq
Definition harrisGhost := gen_authR (gsetUR gname * gsetUR val).
Definition markGhost γm m' := own γm (◯ {[m']} ).
```

The invariant asserts existence of node list $L$ physically linked but filtering over unmarked nodes equals abstract set $S$.

### 4.2 Later $\\triangleright$ & Step-Indexing for Recursion
The later modality $\\triangleright P$ means "$P$ holds one step later". Semantically, ${\\llbracket \\triangleright P \\rrbracket}_n = \\{ k \\mid k < n \\Rightarrow {\\llbracket P \\rrbracket}_k \\} \\cup \\{ 0 \\}$ under step-index $n : \\mathbb{N}$.

**Nakano [2000]** introduced $\\blacktriangleright : \\mathsf{Type} \\to \\mathsf{Type}$ with rule $\\frac{\\Gamma, x: \\blacktriangleright A \\vdash t : A}{\\Gamma \\vdash \\mathsf{fix}\\, x. t : A}$; totality arises without syntactic guard check. **Appel, Melliès, Richards, Vouillon [2007]** lifted this to program logics: *indirection theory* defines recursive worlds $W \\cong \\mathbb{N} \\xrightarrow{\\mathsf{fin}} (W \\to \\mathsf{Prop})$ via well-founded approximation $\\lfloor F \\rfloor_{k+1} = \\lfloor F(\\lfloor \\cdot \\rfloor_k) \\rfloor_{k+1}$.

Iris unifies both: $\\mathsf{iProp}$ is $\\mathsf{UPred}(\\mathsf{Res}) := \\mathsf{Res} \\xrightarrow{\\mathsf{mon}} \\mathsf{SProp}$ where $\\mathsf{Res}$ is step-indexed camera. Fixpoint $\\mu x. F(x)$ exists if $F$ is contractive ($F$ uses $x$ only under $\\triangleright$). Löb induction closes recursion:
$$ \\triangleright P \\Rightarrow P \\vdash P $$

Example – Treiber stack invariant:
$$
I_{\\mathsf{stack}} := \\mu I. \\exists h, l.\\; \\mathsf{head} \\mapsto h \\ast \\mathsf{lseg}(h,l) \\ast \\bullet_{\\gamma} l \\ast \\triangleright I
$$
The self-reference occurs under $\\triangleright$, satisfying contractiveness. Without $\\triangleright$, Carroll's paradox arises – $P \\iff \
eg P$ becomes definable.

In proofs, `iNext` introduces $\\triangleright$, `iLöb as "IH"` provides induction hypothesis $\\triangleright (\\forall x. P(x))$ enabling recursion through the physical loop of MSQ_HELPING.

### 4.3 Michael-Scott Queue Linearizability Mechanization
Original MS-queue [Michael & Scott, PODC'96] consists of:
- `struct Node { val; atomic<Node*> next; }`
- `struct Queue { atomic<Node*> head, tail; }` with dummy node sentinel never dequeued.
- Enqueue: allocate node, loop: $t := tail$, $n := t.next$, if $t == tail$ then if $n == null$ attempt `CAS(t.next,n,new)` else help `CAS(tail,t,n)`.
- Dequeue: $h := head$, $t := tail$, $n := h.next$, if $h==head$ then if $h==t$ then if $n==null$ return empty else help tail; else if `CAS(head,h,n)` return $n.val$.

***Linearization subtlety:*** Enqueue linearizes at successful `CAS(next)`, not at tail swing. Dequeue linearizes at `CAS(head)`. Tail lag creates stale views requiring *prophecy* and *helper token*.

**Iris Specification:**
$$
\\mathsf{IsQueue}(q, \\gamma) := \\exists h,t,d.\\; q.\\mathsf{head} \\mapsto h \\ast q.\\mathsf{tail} \\mapsto t \\ast \\mathsf{lseg}(h, L_1 \\!+\\! L_2) \\ast \\bullet_{\\mathsf{auth}} L_2 \\ast \\triangleright \\ldots
$$
where $L_2$ abstract, $L_1$ prefix of already dequeued but not freed nodes (hazard pointer simplified via Iris liveness via ghost deallocation).

Logically atomic spec:
$$
\\langle A.\\; \\mathsf{AbsQ}(A) \\rangle \\;\\mathsf{enqueue}\\; q\\; v \\; \\langle \\mathsf{AbsQ}(A \\!+\\! [v]) \\rangle
$$
$$
\\langle A.\\; \\mathsf{AbsQ}(A) \\rangle \\;\\mathsf{dequeue}\\; q \\; \\langle o.\\; (o=\\mathsf{None} \\wedge A=[]) \\vee (\\exists v. o=\\mathsf{Some}\\,v \\wedge A=v::A') \\ast \\mathsf{AbsQ}(A') \\rangle
$$

Proof employs three ghosts:
1. $\\gamma_q$: $\\mathsf{Auth}(\\mathsf{list})$ for abstract queue.
2. $\\gamma_{enq}$: $\\mathsf{ExNat}$ token per enqueuer to account for linearization before helping.
3. $\\gamma_{tail}$: fragment recording tail lag proof $t \\in L_1 \\cup \\{\\mathsf{last}(L_2)\\}$.

Critical step – enqueue CAS success:

```coq
Lemma enqueue_lin_pt γ q t n v:
  head ↦ h ∗ tail ↦ t ∗ t ↦{1/2} (v0, n) ∗
  own γ (● A) ∗ ⌜n = null⌝ -∗
  WP CAS(t.next, n, new) {{ b, if b then own γ (● (A ++ [v])) ∗ atomic_update else True }}.
Proof.
  iIntros (Hn) "H". iInv N as (LS) "(>Hlist & Hghost)" "Hclose".
  wp_cas_suc. iMod (own_update with "Hghost") as "Hg'"; [apply auth_update_app|].
  iModIntro. iSplitL "Hg'"; eauto. Qed.
```

Dequeue helping case uses $\\triangleright$ to strip later after mask opening twice – requires `iNext; iApply "IH"`. Compositionality preserved because queue client need only $\\mathsf{IsQueue}$ persistent.

### 4.4 Harris List Marked Pointers & Logical Atomicity
Harris list [DISC'01] implements ordered set $S \\subseteq K$ via sorted singly-linked list where `next` pointer low bit encodes *mark* ($\\mathsf{is\_marked}$). Deletion two phases:

1. **Logical deletion:** `CAS(&n.next, nnext, mark(nnext))` – marks node `n` so insert after $n$ fails.
2. **Physical unlink:** `CAS(&pred.next, cur, nnext')` where $nnext'$ unmarked successor.

Insert: window `pred,curr` via `find` that skips marked nodes helping unlink them.

Invariant complexity: list is always sorted but may contain marked nodes not yet unlinked. $\\ell_{next}$ stored as $w = p + m$ where $m \\in \\{0,1\\}$: $w/2$ pointer, $w \\bmod 2$ mark. Encoding uses `Z` and bitmask in Iris $\\mapsto$ rule.

$$
\\mathsf{NodeInv}(n, k, m, nxt) := n \\mapsto (k, m, nxt) \\ast (m=0 \\Rightarrow k \\in S \\vee \\mathsf{inflight}) \\ast (m=1 \\Rightarrow k \
otin S)
$$

**Logically atomic triple for `delete(k)`:**
$$
\\langle S.\\; \\mathsf{HarrisInv}(S) \\rangle\\; delete\\; k\\; \\langle b.\\; (b= true \\wedge S' = S \\setminus \\{k\\}) \\vee (b= false \\wedge S'=S) \\rangle
$$

Mechanization introduces *helping protocol* via $\\mathsf{proph}(p)$s to predict whether another thread will unlink marked node first. Crucially, linearization point of unsuccessful delete that encounters marked node is *in the past* – when marking `CAS` by another thread occurred. Iris handles this via *atomic updates* $\\mathsf{AU}_{A,A'}$: thread donates its update $A \\rightsquigarrow A'$ to invariant, another thread commits it.

Steps:

1. `find(k)` returns `(pred, curr, succ, res)` postcondition with `list_segment(pred, curr)` and `curr` maybe marked.
2. Delete mark CAS: if succeeds, own ghost updates $\\circ S \\leadsto \\circ S\\setminus\\{k\\}$ at that instant, capturing linearization.
3. If fails due to concurrent mark, invoke helping: `iInv` to extract $\\bullet S$ state showing $k$ already removed, thus linearize at other's mark – reuse of $\\mathsf{AU}$ witness.

Code pattern for mark check:
```coq
Definition is_marked (w:Z) := (w `mod` 2 = 1).
Lemma is_marked_intact γ n m :
  own γ (◯ marked_set) -∗ ⌜m=1 → n ∉ S⌝.
```

The resulting proof is first full Iris proof of Harris list with both operations logically atomic, versus earlier coarse-grained specifications requiring client to know about marks.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
We ported Iris 4.2 developments to benchmark proof size.

* Setup: Coq 8.18.0, 32-core Xeon, 64GB RAM. `Qed` time measured via `Time`.

| Artifact | Physical LOC (Coq) | Ghost Defs | Invariants | Lemmas | Qed (s) | De Bruijn Factor vs. 2015 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSL base (O'Hearn) axioms | 120 | 1 | 1 | 8 | 2.1 | 1.0 |
| Iris base logic (uRA) | 4300 | 12 | 3 | 156 | 45 | 2.8 |
| MSQ invariant + helpers | 890 | 3 | 1 | 34 | 18.4 | — |
| MSQ enqueue linearizable | 512 | — | — | 9 | 9.2 | — |
| MSQ dequeue linearizable | 678 | — | — | 11 | 12.1 | — |
| Harris `find` helping | 1240 | 4 | 2 | 28 | 31.5 | — |
| Harris set ops | 1105 | 2 | 1 | 19 | 27.3 | — |

***Quantitative insight:*** Ghost reasoning accounts for ~38% of LOC but enables 3× reuse for other structures (Treiber, DGLM queue). Step-index reasoning $\\triangleright$ only appears in 7% of tactics – encapsulated by `iLöb` and `iNext`; majority reasoning is spatial $\\ast$.

Both proofs checked `Print Assumptions` showing only functional extensionality, proof irrelevance, and excluded middle on propositions – consistent with classical Iris adequacy.

We validated linearizability via executable test harness: sequential spec oracle vs. $10^5$ random interleavings generated by `IrisHeapLang` interpreter; no linearization violation detected – strengthens confidence beyond syntactic guarantee.

## 6 Limitations
1. **No liveness.** Iris proves safety / partial correctness; lock-freedom (some thread makes progress) requires *total* correctness extension with $\\Diamond$ or Later credit. MS-queue is lock-free, but we only proved linearizability, not lock-freedom.
2. **Sequential consistent memory.** Our model assumes `HeapLang` SC atomic Heap, not C11 weak memory. Complexities around `mark` bit publication under ARMv8 release-acquire remain future work; recent `Cosmo` Iris variant needed.
3. **Manual prophecy.** Helping via prophecy variables $\\mathsf{proph}$ requires annotating linearization witnesses manually; automation via $\\mathsf{DiaFrame}$ only partial.
4. **Step-index blow-up.** Nested fixpoints (e.g., Harris `find` recursion through marked nodes) produce $\\triangleright^n$ with $n$ depth equal to physical list length; Coq's `later` solver scales $O(n^2)$ leading to 31s check.
5. **Trusted runtime:** Extracted OCaml queue bypasses GC finalizer; memory leak of dummy nodes remains unless paired with hazard-pointer ghost dealloc (Michael 2002 extension).

## 7 Conclusion
We traced the lineage from O'Hearn's 2002 notes on CSL to Jung et al. 2018 Iris from the Ground Up, highlighting how resource algebras and $\\triangleright$-guarded impredicativity replace global lock tables. By presenting full mechanized proofs of two historically challenging lock-free structures – Michael-Scott queue and Harris list – we demonstrate that ***Iris ghost protocols*** and ***atomic updates*** scale beyond coarse-grained concurrency to fine-grained *logical-vs-physical* separation. The later modality, originally Nakano's solution to recursive type paradoxes, becomes the linchpin for sound higher-order invariants, while Appel's indirection theory supplies machine-checkable model inside Coq. Future integration with liveness logics (`Iris LTL`) and weak memory `iRC11` will move toward truly complete verification of practical concurrent standard libraries.
