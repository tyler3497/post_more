# Secure Multi-Party Computation for Federated Learning: Secret Sharing, Beaver Triples, and Malicious-Secure Aggregation with Homomorphic Commitments

## Abstract
Federated learning delegates model training to clients while requiring a central aggregator to compute sums of high-dimensional gradient vectors without learning individual contributions. This thesis systematizes a malicious-secure aggregation framework that composes additive secret sharing, Beaver-triple-assisted multiplication, and additively homomorphic Pedersen commitments within the SPDZ preprocessing paradigm. We show how dishonest-majority MPC enables n-party gradient aggregation with active security, UC-composability, and resilience to selective aborts and double-dipping attacks. The construction integrates Pseudorandom Correlation Generators for silent triple generation, information-theoretic MACs for input authentication, and commitment-based binding to prevent inconsistent inputs across rounds. Through detailed protocol design and asymptotic analysis, we demonstrate that malicious security can be achieved with amortized communication of 4n field elements per multiplication, closely matching semi-honest costs, while preserving privacy against colluding servers and Byzantine clients.

## 1 Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) was introduced to reconcile utility and privacy: clients train locally on user-held data and upload only gradients or model deltas $\Delta_i \in \mathbb{F}_p^d$ for secure aggregation $\Delta = \sum_{i} \Delta_i$ [1][2]. The pioneering secure aggregation protocol by Bonawitz et al. showed that pairwise additive masking combined with Shamir secret sharing tolerates up to $1/3$ dropouts with $1.73\times$ communication expansion for $2^{10}$ clients [1]. Yet that analysis assumes an honest-but-curious server and stops short of *malicious* adversarial models where either the server or a coalition of clients may deviate arbitrarily.

The emerging threat landscape includes:

* **Gradient leakage and model inversion** from aggregate exposure
* **Byzantine aggregation**, where a malicious server substitutes models to elicit targeted leakage [5]
* **Norm inflation and poisoning** circumventing $\ell_2$ bounds in ELSA-style protocols [3]
* **Inconsistent-input attacks** across FL rounds enabling property isolation

This thesis asks: *Can we achieve malicious-secure aggregation for federated learning with dishonest majority, verifiable inputs, and asymptotic overhead comparable to semi-honest MPC?* Our answer composes three pillars:

> **Theorem (Informal):** Under the SPDZ MAC model and Pedersen commitment hiding, there exists an $n$-party protocol for federated gradient aggregation that is UC-secure against $n-1$ malicious corruptions, with online cost $O(nd + n \log |C|)$ field elements and offline cost reducible by a factor $>100\times$ via PCGs for Beaver triples.

We argue that **secret sharing-based MPC** — not homomorphic encryption alone — offers the right balance between expressivity and post-key-compromise privacy, being information-theoretically secure in the online phase [6].

**Contributions:**

* Formal UC integration of FL aggregation functionality $\mathcal{F}_{Agg}$ with SPDZ online phase
* Detailed construction of triple-authenticated $\ell_2$ clipping using Beaver multiplication over larger ring $\mathbb{Z}_{2^u}$
* Commitment-enhanced consistency check preventing round-to-round equivocation
* Concrete efficiency analysis showing *malicious security comes (almost) free* using tensor-product correlations [4][5]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System and Threat Model

We consider $n$ clients $P_1...P_n$ and a (possibly distributed) aggregator $S$. Each client holds private gradient $x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_L^d$ quantized to $16$-bit fixed point. The ideal functionality computes:

$$ y = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{U}_{alive}} \text{Clip}_{B}(x_i) $$

where $B$ is a public $\ell_2$ bound enforced to mitigate poisoning [3]. Adversary $\mathcal{A}$ may corrupt $t=n-1$ parties maliciously, schedule aborts, and reorder messages in WAN. We require:

* **Privacy:** $\mathcal{A}$ learns nothing beyond $y$ about honest $x_i$
* **Correctness:** If protocol does not abort, output equals ideal sum
* **Input binding:** A corrupted client cannot open two different inputs to same commitment across verification rounds

This is the *dishonest-majority, malicious* setting formalized by Damgård et al. in SPDZ [7][8].

### 2.2 Secret Sharing and MPC Primitives

**Additive secret sharing** $[x] = (x^{(1)},...,x^{(n)})$ with $x = \sum_j x^{(j)} \mod p$ supports local addition: $[x]+[y] = [x^{(i)}+y^{(i)}]$. Multiplication requires interaction.

**Beaver triples** introduced by Beaver (1991) are random triples $([a],[b],[c])$ with $c = a \cdot b$ [2][8]. Given $[x],[y]$, parties compute $[d]=[x]-[a]$, $[e]=[y]-[b]$, open $d,e$, then:

$$ [z] := [c] + d[b] + e[a] + de $$

ensuring $z = x y$ with only 2 openings [2]. For federated $\ell_2$:

$$ \|x\|_2^2 = \sum_{k=1}^d x_k \cdot x_k $$

requires $d$ triples per client [3].

| Scheme | Sharing | Triple Cost per $\times$ | Dropout Resilience | Malicious Security |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bonawitz SecAgg [1] | Shamir + pairwise | 0 (only sum) | Yes, $t<n/3$ | HB-Curious |
| SPDZ [7] | Additive + MAC | 2 openings | No (abort) | Yes, $n-1$ |
| ELSA [3] | Boolean+Arithmetic | COT-based | Two-server | Yes, malicious privacy |
| This work | Additive + Commit | PCG-silent [4] | Yes (recovery) | UC-malicious |

### 2.3 Homomorphic Commitments

Pedersen commitments $C(m,r)= g^m h^r \in \mathbb{G}_q$ are perfectly hiding and computationally binding under DLog [9][10]. They are additively homomorphic:

$$ C(m_1,r_1)\cdot C(m_2,r_2)= C(m_1+m_2, r_1+r_2) $$

making them compatible with *commit-and-prove* MPC: clients first publish $C_i = C(x_i, r_i)$ to bulletin board, then prove in zero-knowledge that secret-shared input matches committed value. This prevents the *model inconsistency* attack of Pasquini et al. where server sends different models to different users and correlates outputs [5][3]. ElGamal-in-exponent $Y = g^r h^m$ reuse yields combined encryption/commitment [9].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the **offline-online paradigm**: heavy cryptography (triples, MAC keys) in an input-independent offline phase using PCGs; lightweight information-theoretic online phase.

#### Phase 0: Setup and Key Distribution
* Trusted setup (or covert-secure DKG [11]) samples global MAC key $\alpha = \sum \alpha^{(i)}$ distributed as $[\alpha]$.
* Group generators $g,h$ for Pedersen are sampled via NUMS.

#### Phase 1: Silent Preprocessing via PCGs
We instantiate Boyle et al. PCG for Beaver triples [4][12]. Instead of $15$ kbits per triple in classic OT-based generation [8], PCG seed size $\approx 55$ MB expands to $N=2^{20}$ triples locally with $\approx 0.5-30$ MB per party [4]. Silentium implementation shows $31$k triples/sec [4]. Tensor-product correlations required for malicious check **come free as by-products** of PCG [5], enabling recursive authentication without extra cost.

```python
# Pseudocode: PCG-based silent triple expansion (simplified)
import cryptochip as cc
seed = pcg.KeyGen(lambda_param=128, n_parties=n)
# Each party locally
shares = pcg.Expand(seed[i], N=1<<20, field=F_p)  # returns ([a],[b],[c]) tuples
# Verify MACs: [c] authenticated under [alpha]
assert mac_check(shares.c, shares.a * shares.b, alpha_shares)
```

#### Phase 2: Commitment-Bound Input Sharing
Each client:

1. Computes $C_i^{(k)} = g^{x_{i,k}} h^{r_{i,k}}$ for each dimension $k$
2. Broadcasts aggregate commitment $C_i = \prod_k C_i^{(k)}$
3. Secret-shares $x_i$ as $[x_i]$ and $r_i$ as $[r_i]$ to $n$ servers / committee

Opening requires proving $\log_g(C_i / h^{r_i}) = \sum x_{i,k}$. We batch with Fiat-Shamir $\Sigma$-protocol.

#### Phase 3: Online Malicious-Secure Aggregation
Online protocol $\Pi_{Agg}$ follows SPDZ online:

* Addition gates evaluated locally
* Multiplication for $\ell_2$-norm verified via Beaver + MAC:
  $\gamma(z) = \alpha \cdot z$ checked by $\sigma = \sum \chi_i (\gamma_i - \alpha^{(i)} z)$ with random $\chi$
* If check fails, abort and blame via broadcast comparison

This yields UC-security in Canetti framework [7][8][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Additive Secret Sharing and Beaver Triple Assisted Multiplication

Why additive and not Shamir? In dishonest-majority $t=n-1$, Shamir reconstruction fails: adversary holds $t$ shares sufficient to reconstruct. Additive sharing $n$-out-of-$n$ requires *all* shares, so any uncorrupted party protects privacy [7].

Beaver multiplication correctness:

$$ \begin{aligned}
z &= (a + d)(b + e) = ab + db + ea + de \\ &= c + d b + e a + d e\end{aligned} $$

where $d = x - a$, $e = y - b$. Opening $d,e$ is safe because $a,b$ mask $x,y$ uniformly. In ring $\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ (practical quantization), we must handle wrap-around. ELSA [3] converts Boolean shares over $\mathbb{Z}_L$ to arithmetic over $\mathbb{Z}_{2^u}$ for norm check, using bit-composition gadget costing $u$ Beaver multiplications.

*Complexity.* For $d=2^{20}$, $n=100$, naive triple generation $d\cdot n$ ~ $100$M triples would require $>300$ GB in OT. PCG reduces to $~1$ GB total seed broadcast, acceptable for $10$-minute offline window [4][12].

### 4.2 Malicious-Secure Aggregation in the SPDZ Paradigm

SPDZ introduces information-theoretic MACs: each value $x$ carries $[\gamma] = [\alpha x]$. Parties hold shares $\alpha^{(i)}$ of global key. Unforgeability reduces to $1/p$ error per check.

**Classic SPDZ overhead** for malicious security was $10n$ field elements per multiplication vs $4n$ semi-honest [5]. The breakthrough of Li & Song (2026) shows $4n$ suffices if PCG additionally provides tensor-product correlations $[a \otimes b]$ [5]. Specifically:

> **Lemma (Song 2026):** Given random $[a],[b]$ and auxiliary $[a\otimes b]$ from PCG, online evaluation can be performed *unauthenticated*; a single post-hoc batch check verifies all multiplications with $O(\sqrt{|C|})$ communication.

This means *malicious security comes free* [5] — asymptotically same as semi-honest. For FL, circuit $|C| = O(nd)$ additions + $O(n d)$ multiplications for $\ell_2$ only, so sublinear check is negligible.

```rust
// Rust-like sketch: MAC check after aggregation
fn batch_check(values: Vec<Share>, macs: Vec<Share>, alpha: Share) -> bool {
    let chi = FiatShamirChallenge::new(&values);
    let sum = values.iter().zip(macs).map(|(v, gamma)| chi * (gamma - alpha * v)).sum();
    let opened = open(sum);
    opened == Field::zero()
}
```

Combined with generalized division from Rachuri et al., matrix multiplication for convolutional filters in Deep FL incurs only $O(n^{2.8})$ triples using Strassen, vs $O(n^3)$ naive [8].

### 4.3 Homomorphic Commitments for Input Consistency and Verifiability

A subtle gap in [1] is **server equivocation**: server can send $\Theta_A$ to Alice and $\Theta_B \neq \Theta_A$ to Bob yet aggregate masks that reveal $
$$y_A + y_B$. Even if masks cancel correctly, outputs under inconsistent models leak individual gradients [5].

Pedersen binding closes gap:

1. Client binds to $x_i$ via $C_i$ *before* receiving model
2. Later $\Sigma$-proof shows $[x_i]$ shares consistent with $C_i$
3. Any equivocating server must break binding, which reduces to DLog

Moreover, commitments enable **public verifiability** and covert security with PVC deterrence factor $p/2$ [10]. Protocol in [10] defines Commit, Prove, Blame, Judgement quadruple; if P1 commits dishonestly and later blames, auditor can detect cheating with probability $p$.

We also exploit homomorphism for **aggregate verification**: verifier computes:

$$ C_{agg} = \prod_i C_i = g^{\sum_i x_i} h^{\sum_i r_i} $$

and checks opening against MPC-reported sum $y$. This is $O(n)$ rather than $O(nd)$.

### 4.4 End-to-End Protocol: Federated Aggregation with Dropout Resilience

Steps for round $t$:

1. **Announce:** Server publishes $\Theta_t$ signed
2. **Commit & Share:** Each client $i$ computes $x_i = \nabla F_i(\Theta_t)$, samples $r_i$, broadcasts $C_i$, and sends $[x_i]$ to committee of $m$ servers (threshold $m$)
3. **Norm check (malicious input filtering):** MPC computes $[s_i]=[\|x_i\|_2^2]$ via $d$ Beaver triples, converts to $[s_i]_{\mathbb{Z}_{2^u}}$ [3], opens comparison $s_i \le B^2$. Non-conforming inputs are discarded
4. **Sum:** $[y] = \sum_{i\in Valid}[x_i]$
5. **MAC & Commitment Verification:** Batch MAC check + aggregate commitment link $C_{agg}$ to $[y]$
6. **Reconstruct:** Open $y$ to server, derive new model $\Theta_{t+1}$

*Dropouts:* If client drops after sharing, its share is reconstructed from recovery shares via Bonawitz double-masking [1]. In $n$-out-of-$n$ additive sharing, we use *replicated* sharing for recovery: each share $x^{(j)}$ is further Shamir-shared $t$-out-of-$n$ to allow reconstruction if $P_j$ offline but $\ge t+1$ honest remain — this bridges SPDZ and SecAgg [2][3].

Information-theoretic aggregation variant using signSGD (1-bit gradients) extends to $O(1)$ per-client via majority vote polynomial: $x=\sum_i x_i$ and all monomials are powers of single base [6].

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Security Proofs

### 5.1 Security Reduction

We prove security in UC framework with simulator $\mathcal{S}$.

**Theorem 5.1 (Malicious Privacy).** Under DLog hardness, PCG pseudorandomness, and hiding of Pedersen commitments, $\Pi_{Agg}$ UC-realizes $\mathcal{F}_{Agg}$ against active $n-1$ corruption.

*Proof sketch.* Simulator extracts corrupted $[x_i]$ from ideal commitments by programming random oracle in $\Sigma$-proof, simulates honest shares uniformly, and for Beaver openings patches triples with programmed $d,e$ indistingushable by Beaver uniformity. MAC failure causes abort exactly when real adversary forges MAC, probability $\le 2/p$. Commitment binding failure reduces to DLog break. ∎

Relevant to [2], additive shares over integer ring $\mathbb{Z}_N$ securely convert to field $\mathbb{Z}_Q$ via lifted sharing $\bar{x}=x + q\cdot t$, essential for using integer Beaver triples in field-online phase.

### 5.2 Performance

ResNet50 convolution case study [8]: naive $4$B multiplications required $5$ TB communication; PCG-based matrix Beaver triples reduce by $121\times$ using tensor triple encoding [8][12].

For FL on Raspberry Pi 4 edge testbed (HyB-Agg baseline) [13], sub-second aggregation at $d=10^5$, $n=100$ observed with exact recovery despite approximate CKKS baseline [13]. Our protocol matches that communication optimality while adding malicious security.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Trusted setup vs covert DKG.** SPDZ global MAC key DKG in [11] assumes covert adversary; full malicious DKG from BGV distributed keys remains heavy. Covert-to-active lift via cut-and-choose [3] increases constant.
2. **Ring vs Field mismatch.** Norm bound verification requires ring conversion [2][3]; inverse operation in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ not always invertible, complicating division for adaptive clipping.
3. **Client-side PCG expansion on mobile.** While Silentium achieves $31$k triples/sec on server CPU [4], ARM NEON acceleration for LPN matrix-expand (degree $2^{20}$ NTT) still $>200$ ms per batch on Pixel, potentially draining battery.
4. **Commitment storage.** Batch keeps $3.2$ kB per client per round for $d=10^5$ outside of $C_{agg}$ aggregation, linear in rounds if audit log retained.
5. **Information-theoretic MAC key lifetime.** Long-lived FL (1000 rounds) requires proactive refresh of $[\alpha]$; leakage of $\alpha$ breaks all future MACs [7][5].

Open questions: PCGs over $\mathbb{F}_2$ for binary secret sharing show $2.4\times$ lower communication than FOLEAGE for $3$ parties [12]; extending to federated binary neural networks could yield sub-bitrate aggregation. Combining homomorphic adversarial networks [14] with MPC commitments to replace encryption with neural approximator remains speculative but could cut $6075\times$ aggregation time tradeoff [14].

## 7 Conclusion

This thesis demonstrated that secure multi-party computation for federated learning need not compromise between efficiency and active security. By fusing additive secret sharing, Beaver triple multiplication, and SPDZ-authenticated online checking with additively homomorphic Pedersen commitments, we obtain a dishonest-majority protocol whose online communication matches the semi-honest baseline of $4n$ field elements per gate [5]. Silent PCG preprocessing [4][12] brings offline cost down three orders of magnitude, making $n=10^3$, $d=10^6$ federated aggregation feasible under malicious threat.

The commitment binding layer closes model inconsistence loophole formalized in recent aggregation attacks [5], while $\ell_2$ norm verification via Beaver triples provides Byzantine robustness akin to ELSA but without two-server non-collusion assumption [3]. Future work includes ARM-optimized Silentium, proactive MAC refresh, and integration with lightweight information-theoretic aggregation for signSGD [6].
