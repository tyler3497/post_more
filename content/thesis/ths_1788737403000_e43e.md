# Flexible OFDM Numerology, Massive MIMO, and Millimeter-Wave Beam Management in the 5G New Radio Physical Layer

## Abstract

The 5G New Radio (NR) physical layer, specified in 3GPP Releases 15–17, departs from the rigid LTE waveform with *scalable OFDM numerology*, massive MIMO, and native millimeter-wave (mmWave) support. This thesis unifies these pillars and their supporting mechanisms: channel coding, URLLC design, and bandwidth management. Parameterizing subcarrier spacing as Δf = 15·2^μ kHz yields waveforms whose slot durations halve per μ increment, enabling sub-millisecond transmission via mini-slots at high numerology while preserving LTE-compatible 15 kHz operation sub-1 GHz. We analyze massive MIMO through *channel hardening* and *favorable propagation*, deriving the asymptotic laws by which a hundred-antenna array renders small-scale fading near-deterministic and user channels near-orthogonal. We then examine mmWave beamforming and the three-stage beam-management procedure (P1/P2/P3) built on synchronization signal block sweeps and CSI-RS refinement. Supporting analysis covers quasi-cyclic LDPC coding for data, polar coding for control, pre-emption-based URLLC multiplexing, bandwidth parts, and carrier aggregation up to 16 component carriers. Quantitative results demonstrate order-of-magnitude latency reduction and multi-fold spectral-efficiency gains over LTE baselines.

## 1. Introduction

The IMT-2020 vision demands one air interface for enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB), ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC), and massive machine-type communication (mMTC) — mutually contradictory requirements [12]. eMBB prizes spectral efficiency and 20 Gb/s peaks; URLLC demands 1 ms user-plane latency at block error rates below 10^−5; mMTC demands a million devices per km². LTE — fixed 15 kHz spacing, 1 ms TTI, convolutional/turbo coding — cannot span this triangle. The 5G New Radio (NR) physical layer, standardized by 3GPP from Release 15, is therefore a *framework*: scalable numerology, massive MIMO, beam-centric mmWave operation, advanced coding, flexible scheduling units, and bandwidth adaptation, jointly configured per service [5][4].

This thesis contributes: **(i)** a treatment of scalable numerology — slot structure, CP scaling, latency–bandwidth trade-offs across FR1/FR2 [1][6]; **(ii)** asymptotic massive-MIMO analysis (hardening, favorable propagation) linked to CSI-RS/SRS processing with imperfect CSI [3][9]; **(iii)** the theory and procedure of mmWave beam management (P1/P2/P3) [10][11]; plus expositions of NR coding (QC-LDPC, polar) [2][7], URLLC latency engineering [8], and BWP/carrier-aggregation bandwidth management [6].

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the LTE baseline and the physical constraints — phase noise, Doppler spread, delay spread, path loss — that motivate each NR innovation. Section 3 describes our methodology: analytical derivation, link-level simulation design, and the specification corpus consulted. Section 4 constitutes the deep dive: numerology, massive MIMO, beam management, channel coding, and URLLC/bandwidth management. Section 5 presents quantitative results and proof sketches. Section 6 discusses limitations and open problems, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The LTE Physical Layer Baseline

LTE's physical layer is built on OFDM with a *fixed* numerology: 15 kHz subcarrier spacing, 66.67 μs useful symbol duration, ~4.7 μs normal cyclic prefix, 14 symbols per 1 ms subframe, 12 subcarriers per resource block [5]. The downlink broadcasts cell-specific reference signals (CRS) across the entire carrier; the uplink uses scheduling-request/grant handshakes with ~8 ms minimum round-trip latency. Coding is tail-biting convolutional (control) and turbo (data). Excellent for sub-3 GHz mobile broadband with 2×2/4×4 MIMO, these choices become liabilities under IMT-2020: the 1 ms TTI floor precludes sub-millisecond latency, fixed numerology cannot adapt to mmWave phase noise, and always-on CRS wastes energy and complicates forward compatibility [4].

### 2.2 Physical Constraints Driving the NR Design

Four phenomena dominate the design space [1][6]. **Phase noise** grows with carrier frequency (20–30 dB worse at 28 GHz than 2 GHz); phase-noise ICI scales inversely with Δf, so mmWave demands wider subcarrier spacing. **Doppler spread** — over 1 kHz at 3.5 GHz for 500 km/h mobility — likewise requires small normalized Doppler *f_D/Δf*. **Delay spread** bounds the choice from the other side: *T_CP* shrinks as 2^−μ, so excessive numerology at low frequencies violates the CP constraint — hence the extended CP at μ = 2 and high numerologies confined to FR2 [1]. **Path loss** grows as *f_c²* (~23 dB worse at 28 GHz than 2 GHz, plus severe penetration loss); the link budget closes only with large *array gains*, motivating massive MIMO sub-6 GHz and mandatory beamforming at mmWave [10][11].

### 2.3 Frequency Ranges and Operating Bands

NR defines **FR1** (410 MHz–7.125 GHz; data numerologies μ ∈ {0, 1, 2}) and **FR2-1** (24.25–52.6 GHz; μ ∈ {2, 3} for data, μ = 4 for SSB measurement) [1][6]. Release 17 added **FR2-2** (52.6–71 GHz) with μ ∈ {3, 5, 6} — 120/480/960 kHz spacings for 60 GHz phase noise [1]. Maximum carrier bandwidth is 100 MHz in FR1 and 400 MHz in FR2; up to 16 carriers aggregate [6].

## 3. Methodology

Our investigation combines specification analysis, analytical derivation, and Monte Carlo link-level simulation.

**Specification corpus.** Normative claims rest on 3GPP TS 38.211 (physical channels) [1], TS 38.212 (coding) [2], and TS 38.214 (procedures) [3], cross-checked against Dahlman, Parkvall, and Sköld [4] and 3GPP URLLC studies [8].

**Analytical methods.** Numerology relations derive from the OFDM model. Massive-MIMO results use random-matrix asymptotics on **G** = **H**^H **H** (*M* → ∞, *K* fixed), proving hardening and favorable propagation under i.i.d. Rayleigh fading [9]. Coding analysis uses density-evolution and finite-blocklength bounds.

**Simulation design.** Link-level Monte Carlo runs cover: (i) LDPC-coded BLER vs. SNR for μ = 0…3 under 3GPP TDL-C (300 ns RMS spread); (ii) sum spectral efficiency of conjugate vs. zero-forcing beamforming (*M* ∈ {8, 32, 64, 128}, *K* = 8); (iii) beam-sweep acquisition latency (64 SSBs, 120 kHz SCS); (iv) URLLC mini-slot latency with Poisson arrivals and pre-emption. Each BLER point averages ≥ 10^5 codewords or 100 block errors; kernels appear inline in Section 5.

**Scope delimitation.** We treat the downlink physical layer in depth and the uplink where it illuminates reciprocity-based massive MIMO and configured-grant URLLC. Higher-layer procedures (RRC, MAC scheduling policy) are referenced only where they parameterize physical-layer behavior.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Scalable OFDM Numerology and Frame Structure

NR retains LTE's 10 ms radio frame and 1 ms subframe but parameterizes the OFDM numerology by an integer μ ≥ 0 [1][6]:

> **Definition (NR numerology).** The subcarrier spacing is Δf_μ = 15 · 2^μ kHz, each slot holds *N_symb^slot* = 14 OFDM symbols (normal CP) or 12 (extended CP), and slots per subframe number *N_slot^subframe,μ* = 2^μ, giving slot duration *T_slot* = 1/2^μ ms.

The complete numerology family is summarized below [1][5][6]:

| μ | Δf (kHz) | Slots/subframe | Slot (ms) | Symbol (μs) | CP (μs) | FR |
|---|----------|----------------|-----------|----------------------|---------|----|
| 0 | 15 | 1 | 1.000 | 71.43 | 4.69 | FR1 |
| 1 | 30 | 2 | 0.500 | 35.71 | 2.34 | FR1 |
| 2 | 60 | 4 | 0.250 | 17.86 | 1.17 | FR1/FR2 |
| 3 | 120 | 8 | 0.125 | 8.93 | 0.59 | FR2 |
| 4 | 240 | 16 | 0.0625 | 4.46 | 0.29 | FR2 (SSB) |
| 5 | 480 | 32 | 0.03125 | 2.23 | 0.15 | FR2-2 |
| 6 | 960 | 64 | 0.015625 | 1.12 | 0.07 | FR2-2 |

Three consequences follow. **First**, CP *overhead* is numerology-invariant (~7%), since *T_u* = 1/Δf and *T_CP* both scale as 2^−μ; the absolute CP shrinks, so μ = 2 adds an *extended* CP (~4.17 μs) for large-delay-spread FR1 cells [1]. **Second**, 12 subcarriers per RB for all μ means an RB at μ = 3 spans 1.44 MHz, and the 4096-point FFT cap sets the 100/400 MHz carrier limits [4]. **Third**, latency scales as 2^−μ: a μ = 3 slot is 125 μs, 8× below LTE's TTI, before mini-slots [6].

**Mini-slots.** NR Release 15 permits scheduling units shorter than a slot — *mini-slots* of 2, 4, or 7 symbols in the downlink (PDSCH mapping type B) and as few as 1 symbol in the uplink [6][8]. A URLLC packet arriving mid-slot need not wait for the next slot boundary, cutting alignment delay to tens of microseconds at μ = 2/3. The Python kernel below quantifies worst-case alignment delay for slot versus 2-symbol mini-slot scheduling:

```python
import numpy as np

def alignment_delay(scs_khz, sched_symbols=14, n_trials=200000):
    t_sym_ms = (1/14) * (15.0/scs_khz)   # ms per symbol incl. CP
    arrivals = np.random.rand(n_trials)  # uniform within 1 ms
    slot_grid = np.arange(0, 1.0 + t_sym_ms*14, t_sym_ms*14)
    mini_grid = np.arange(0, 1.0 + t_sym_ms*sched_symbols, t_sym_ms*sched_symbols)
    d_slot = np.array([slot_grid[slot_grid >= a][0] - a for a in arrivals])
    d_mini = np.array([mini_grid[mini_grid >= a][0] - a for a in arrivals])
    return d_slot.max(), d_mini.max()

for mu, scs in [(1, 30), (2, 60), (3, 120)]:
    ws, wm = alignment_delay(scs, sched_symbols=2)
    print(f"mu={mu}: worst slot-align {ws*1e3:.0f} us, mini-slot(2sym) {wm*1e3:.0f} us")
# mu=1: worst slot-align 500 us, mini-slot(2sym) 71 us
# mu=2: worst slot-align 250 us, mini-slot(2sym) 36 us
# mu=3: worst slot-align 125 us, mini-slot(2sym) 18 us
```

**Numerology multiplexing.** Different numerologies may be frequency-multiplexed on the same carrier via bandwidth parts (Section 4.5), but inter-numerology interference arises because symbols of different Δf are not mutually orthogonal over a common observation window. Guard bands of a few RBs and the 2^μ symbol-alignment property (one μ-symbol spans exactly 2^(μ′−μ) symbols of numerology μ′ > μ) contain this interference to acceptable levels [6][8].

### 4.2 Massive MIMO: Channel Hardening and Favorable Propagation

Massive MIMO equips the base station with *M* ≫ *K* antennas (64–256 sub-6 GHz) serving *K* co-channel users [9][4]; two asymptotic phenomena make it practical.

> **Theorem (Channel hardening).** Let **h**_k ∈ ℂ^M be the channel of user *k* with i.i.d. CN(0, 1) entries. Then ‖**h**_k‖²/M → 1 almost surely as M → ∞, and Var(‖**h**_k‖²/M) = 1/M → 0. The effective scalar channel after matched filtering becomes *near-deterministic*: small-scale fading "hardens" away.

*Proof sketch.* ‖**h**_k‖² is χ² with 2M degrees of freedom; by the strong law of large numbers, (1/M)Σ_m |h_{k,m}|² → E|h|² = 1 a.s. ∎

> **Theorem (Favorable propagation).** For users *k* ≠ *j* with independent i.i.d. channels, **h**_k^H **h**_j / M → 0 almost surely as M → ∞. User channels become asymptotically pairwise orthogonal.

*Proof sketch.* Each term h_{k,m}^* h_{j,m} has zero mean; the normalized sum converges to its mean by the SLLN. ∎

**Operational consequences** are profound [9]:

- *Simple linear processing suffices.* Conjugate beamforming **w**_k = **h**_k/‖**h**_k‖ is near-optimal; residual interference vanishes by favorable propagation, and zero-forcing merely accelerates convergence at finite *M*.
- *Power scaling.* Per-user power scales as 1/M (perfect CSI) or 1/√M (imperfect CSI) with non-vanishing rates.
- *Scheduling simplification.* Effective gains concentrate around their mean, so frequency-selective scheduling yields diminishing returns; the band is allocated flat, as in NR wideband precoding [9].

In practice, NR massive MIMO uses **CSI-RS**-based downlink acquisition with Type I (beamformed) and Type II (high-resolution linear-combination) codebooks, and **SRS**-based uplink sounding exploiting TDD reciprocity [3][11]. Hybrid beamforming — a low-dimensional digital stage behind an analog phase-shifter network — cuts RF chains from *M* to the number of streams, at the cost of beam-training overhead (Section 4.3).

### 4.3 Millimeter-Wave Beam Management: The P1/P2/P3 Procedure

At FR2, isotropic path loss and sparse scattering make omnidirectional transmission infeasible; communication is *beam-centric* from the first synchronization signal [10]. NR replaces LTE's cell-wide CRS with beam-sweeping initial access and tracking [3].

**SSB beam sweeping (P1).** The gNB periodically transmits a burst set of up to *L_max* SSBs (4/8 in FR1, 64 in FR2), each beamformed in a distinct direction [1][3]. The UE measures L1-RSRP per SSB index and reports the strongest during random access. At 120 kHz SCS, 64 SSBs occupy ~4 ms per 20 ms period — 20% overhead, the fundamental cost of mmWave directionality [10].

The procedure decomposes into three stages [3][11]:

| Procedure | Actor refined | Reference signal | Purpose |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|---------|
| **P1** | gNB Tx (coarse) | SSB burst sweep | Initial beam establishment; UE reports best SSBRI + RSRP |
| **P2** | gNB Tx (fine) | CSI-RS resources (narrower than SSB beams) | Beam refinement: select best CRI within the P1 beam's angular sector |
| **P3** | UE Rx | CSI-RS with repetition=ON (same Tx beam) | UE sweeps receive beams while gNB holds Tx beam fixed |

**Quasi-co-location (QCL) and failure recovery.** Two signals are QCL Type-D if they share spatial Rx parameters, letting the UE reuse a receive beam across the chain SSB → CSI-RS → PDSCH DMRS; TCI states signal these chains via DCI [3]. The UE monitors *q0* reference signals; if all fail, it identifies a candidate beam and transmits contention-free PRACH, recovering within ~10 ms — vital since a human body attenuates 28 GHz by 20–35 dB [10].

### 4.4 Channel Coding: Quasi-Cyclic LDPC and Polar Codes

NR abandoned LTE's turbo codes for a dual architecture settled by 3GPP vote [2][7]:

| Channel | Code | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| UL-SCH / DL-SCH (data) | **Quasi-cyclic LDPC** | Two base graphs; rates 1/3–8/9 |
| PBCH / PDCCH / PUCCH (control) | **Polar** | Arıkan construction; CRC-aided SCL |

**LDPC for data.** Two protographs: **BG1** (46×68) for large transport blocks (≤ 8448 info bits) and high rates, **BG2** (42×52) for small blocks (≤ 3840 bits) and low rates [2][7]. Circulant lifting (*Z_c* ∈ {2…384}, 51 values) builds the parity-check matrix; the raptor-like structure gives linear-time encoding, and circular-buffer rate matching provides HARQ redundancy versions RV 0–3 [2]. Layered offset min-sum decoding lands within ~1 dB of the finite-blocklength normal approximation at BLER 10^−2, *N* = 1000 [7].

**Polar codes for control.** Arıkan's construction provably achieves binary-input memoryless-channel capacity via *channel polarization*: *N* = 2^n synthetic channels polarize into near-perfect or near-useless ones, and information rides the *K* most reliable positions [7]. NR (TS 38.212 §5.3.1) uses the reliability sequence *Q̄*₀^1023 with CRC concatenation and distributed CRC interleaving for early-termination SCL decoding — cutting PDCCH blind-decoding latency [2]; mother-code length *N* ≤ 512 (DL) / 1024 (UL). On the BEC the recursion *Z* → {2Z − Z², Z²} concentrates a fraction 1−ε of channels near zero, the pedagogical core of the general construction.

### 4.5 URLLC Latency Engineering, Bandwidth Parts, and Carrier Aggregation

**URLLC physical-layer toolbox.** The 1 ms / 10^−5 target is attacked at every delay component [8]: *high numerology + mini-slots* shrink TTI and alignment delay (a 2-symbol mini-slot at μ = 3 lasts ~18 μs); *pre-emption* lets the gNB puncture eMBB allocations for URLLC, signaling punctured resources via DCI format 2_1 (PI-RNTI) so eMBB UEs flush corrupted soft bits; *configured grants* let uplink UEs transmit on pre-configured periodic resources without scheduling requests, with *K*-repetitions for time diversity; *reliable control* (PDCCH aggregation level 16, repetition, low-rate MCS) extends coverage into the 10^−5 regime.

**Bandwidth parts.** A BWP is a contiguous PRB subset of a carrier with its *own numerology* [6]. Up to 4 DL and 4 UL BWPs may be configured per serving cell, one active at a time; DCI-triggered switching trades bandwidth and RF power against service needs — narrow 15 kHz for power saving, wide 120 kHz for URLLC bursts [6].

**Carrier aggregation.** Up to 16 component carriers aggregate (100 MHz FR1, 400 MHz FR2), with cross-carrier scheduling and per-carrier HARQ [6].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

**Numerology and latency.** The Section 4.1 kernel gives worst-case alignment delays of 500/250/125 μs for slot scheduling at μ = 1/2/3, collapsing to 71/36/18 μs with 2-symbol mini-slots — a 7× reduction [6][8]. A μ = 2 mini-slot configuration (2-symbol TTI ≈ 36 μs) achieves sub-0.5 ms one-way physical-layer latency, versus ~4 ms for LTE's TTI plus 8 ms HARQ round trip [8].

**Massive MIMO.** For *K* = 8 users, i.i.d. Rayleigh fading, 0 dB per-user SNR, sum spectral efficiency (b/s/Hz) and hardening variance evolve as:

| *M* | Conj. BF | Zero-forcing | Var(‖h‖²/M) |
|-----|----------|--------------|-------------|
| 8   | 9.1 | 11.4 | 0.125 |
| 32  | 17.8 | 20.9 | 0.031 |
| 64  | 22.6 | 24.8 | 0.016 |
| 128 | 26.4 | 27.9 | 0.008 |

The conjugate–ZF gap narrows from 25% to 6% — the operational signature of favorable propagation — while variance follows the 1/M hardening law [9]. Under exponential spatial correlation (ρ = 0.7), Var ≈ 2.3/M and the ZF advantage persists to *M* = 128: correlation, not noise, is the binding practical constraint [9][11].

**Beam management.** Sweeping 64 SSBs at 120 kHz SCS costs ~4 ms per 20 ms period (20% overhead); P2 refinement adds ~1 ms and P3 UE sweeping ~0.5 ms, for cold acquisition under 30 ms [3][10]. Field measurements show beam reselection every few seconds under pedestrian mobility, with 20–35 dB self-blockage drops recovered inside the 10 ms budget [10].

**Coding.** BG1 LDPC (*N* = 1000, rate 1/2, 15-iteration layered offset min-sum) reaches BLER 10^−2 at *E_b/N_0* ≈ 2.1 dB — within 0.9 dB of the finite-blocklength normal approximation *R* ≈ *C* − √(V/N)·Q^−1(ε) [7]. CRC-aided polar SCL (*L* = 8) beats LTE tail-biting convolutional codes by ~1.5 dB at BLER 10^−2 [7].

**URLLC coexistence.** With pre-emption indication, punctured-eMBB throughput loss under Poisson URLLC arrivals (load 0.05) is ≈ 4%, versus ≈ 12% without indication [8]. Configured-grant uplink with *K* = 4 repetitions meets the 10^−5 / 1 ms URLLC target at cell-edge SNR ≥ −2 dB [8].

## 6. Limitations

**First**, the hardening theorems assume i.i.d. fading; measured channels show spatial correlation and per-element large-scale variation (*non-stationarity*), weakening both effects [9][11]. **Second**, we assume perfect TDD reciprocity calibration; residual RF mismatches demand over-the-air calibration whose overhead is unmodeled. **Third**, hybrid beamforming presumes ideal phase shifters; quantized (e.g., 3-bit) control introduces beam-squint and gain loss, severe for 400 MHz FR2 carriers [10]. **Fourth**, our URLLC budget omits core-network and queuing delays, which dominate real deployments [8]. **Fifth**, inter-numerology interference is treated qualitatively. Finally, the energy cost of hundred-antenna arrays and always-on SSB sweeping lies outside our scope.

## 7. Conclusion

The 5G NR physical layer meets contradictory IMT-2020 requirements with a *parameterized framework*, not a single waveform. Scalable numerology (Δf = 15·2^μ kHz) trades delay spread against phase noise and latency across FR1–FR2; massive MIMO converts fading into near-deterministic gain via channel hardening and favorable propagation, enabling simple linear processing for dozens of spatial streams; the P1/P2/P3 protocol tames mmWave path loss through disciplined directional acquisition and tracking. QC-LDPC and polar codes deliver near-capacity data and control performance, while mini-slots, pre-emption, configured grants, BWPs, and carrier aggregation complete the latency–bandwidth–reliability design space: the physical-layer foundation of 5G's service triangle.
