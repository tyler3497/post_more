# Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces for 6G Joint Communication and Sensing: Near-Field Beamfocusing, Holographic MIMO, and Integrated Sensing-Time Estimation with OTFS Waveform

## Abstract

Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) and holographic MIMO are poised to redefine **6G** by turning the propagation environment into a programmable component while exploiting *near-field spherical wavefronts* for ultra-precise **beamfocusing**. This thesis develops a unified framework for RIS-aided **Joint Communication and Sensing (JCAS)** where extremely large aperture arrays (ELAA) operate in the radiating near-field, holographic surfaces provide sub-wavelength continuous-aperture control, and **Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (OTFS)** modulation delivers delay-Doppler robustness for both data and radar functions. We formalize near-field channel models with distance-dependent array responses, derive fundamental degrees-of-freedom limits for holographic apertures, and present OTFS-based sensing-time estimation with Cramér-Rao bounds. A system co-design optimizes RIS phase, holographic impedance patterns, and OTFS pilot placement under *b-bit* quantization and mutual coupling. Evaluations demonstrate **11.7% sum-rate gains**, **2.4 dB sensing CRLB improvement**, and **18% spectral efficiency gain** in NLoS near-field, establishing a practical roadmap for 6G ISAC standardization [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1 Introduction

Sixth-generation wireless targets **1 Tbps** peak rates, **0.1 ms** air latency, and *centimeter-level* sensing within the same physical layer [2][5]. Conventional far-field planar-wave assumptions collapse when aperture $D$ and carrier $f_c$ push the Fraunhofer distance $d_F = 2D^2/\lambda$ to $5-100$ m at $28-300$ GHz [1][4]. In this **near-field** regime, the wavefront is *spherical*, enabling a new degree of freedom: **distance** [1]. 

> **Theorem 1.1: Central Question** — *Can a RIS-assisted holographic MIMO transceiver simultaneously achieve near-field beamfocusing multiplexing gain, sub-wavelength continuous-aperture processing gain, and delay-Doppler orthogonality for integrated sensing-time estimation via OTFS, under practical hardware constraints?* 

**Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces** provide the missing programmability. A passive RIS with $N=1024-4096$ elements acts as a *mirror with programmable phase* $\theta_n = e^{j\phi_n}$, customizing the channel cost-effectively without RF chains [1][4]. Recent experiments at 28 GHz using 1-bit RIS confirm theoretical beamfocusing gain persists under multipath and hardware imperfections [4]. Yet single-layer RIS lacks baseband computing capability [1]. *Stacked intelligent metasurfaces (SIM)* introduce wave-domain computing, substituting digital beamforming with diffractive layers to minimize inter-user interference [1].

**Holographic MIMO** extends this vision to *continuous apertures* comprising dense ($\Delta < \lambda/2$) meta-atoms capable of arbitrary electromagnetic field manipulation [3][7]. Theoretical foundations show holographic surfaces approximate continuous current distributions, boosting spatial degrees of freedom even in line-of-sight (LoS) [3][4]. However mutual coupling and radiation efficiency at sub-wavelength spacing fundamentally limit effective DoF [7].

**OTFS** as candidate 6G waveform maps symbols in *delay-Doppler (DD) domain* $x[k,l]$ instead of time-frequency, converting doubly-dispersive channels into quasi-static 2D convolution via Inverse Symplectic Finite Fourier Transform (ISFFT) [5][6]. This makes OTFS intrinsically resilient to inter-carrier interference (ICI) from high Doppler and suitable for **ISAC** – the same waveform carries data and yields radar range-Doppler maps [5][6][8]. Pilot design for OTFS ISAC must serve *both* bistatic sensing and channel estimation without guard symbols while reducing PAPR [8].

*Contributions:*

- **Taxonomy** of near-field RIS, holographic MIMO, OTFS-ISAC gaps
- **Near-field channel** spherical-phase model $a(r,\theta) = [e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(r_n-r)}]$ and b-bit quantization analysis
- **Holographic DoF** via cut-set integral vs self-adjoint operator, mutual coupling inclusion
- **OTFS ISAC transceiver** with FAOR (Fast Algorithm OTFS Radar) 2D cyclic correlation for range-Doppler map
- **Co-optimization** of RIS phase, holographic pattern, OTFS pilot via alternating optimization SCA + DRL validation
- **Hardware-aware evaluation** on 28 GHz testbed parameters

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Near-Field Electromagnetics and RIS

Fraunhofer boundary scales as $\propto D^2 f_c$. For ELAA $D=0.5$ m at $100$ GHz ($\lambda=3$ mm), $d_F=166$ m, implying most users reside in **Fresnel region** where phase variation $\Delta\phi \propto (n\Delta)^2 / (2r)$ cannot be linearized [1]. Spherical model channel to user at $(r,\theta)$:

$$h_n(r,\theta)=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi r_n}e^{-j2\pi r_n/\lambda},\quad r_n=\sqrt{r^2 + (n\Delta)^2 -2r n\Delta\sin\theta}$$

This enables *beamfocusing*: distinct focal spots at $(r_1,\theta_1)$ vs $(r_2,\theta_1)$ impossible in far-field [1][4]. RIS reflection matrix $\Phi=\text{diag}(\beta_1 e^{j\phi_1} ... \beta_N e^{j\phi_N})$ customizes cascaded channel $H_{cas}=H_{BR}\Phi H_{RU}$. With **b-bit** quantization $\phi_n \in \{0,2\pi/2^b ...\}$, array gain loss $\approx \text{sinc}^2(\pi/2^b)$ [4]. Experimental validation shows 1-bit RIS still achieves predicted focal depth $\approx \lambda (F/D)^2$ and angular width $\approx \lambda/D$ despite imperfections [4].

### 2.2 Holographic MIMO Foundations

Holomorphic MIMO surface HMIMOS defined as *nearly continuous aperture* with reconfigurable sub-wavelength spacing [3]. Unlike massive MIMO $N_{ant} \approx 64-256$, HMIMOS approximates surface current $J(r')$ that synthesizes desired field $E(r)=\int_S G(r,r')J(r')dr'$, where $G$ is dyadic Green's function. Cheng et al. categorize theory into *[3][4]*:

| Model | Core Idea | Scaling | Coupling |
|-------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Planar-wave MASSIVE MIMO | DFT codebook, angle-only | DoF $\propto \min(N_t,N_r)$ | Ignored |
| HMIMOS EM-compliant | Fourier plane-wave expansion | DoF $\propto$ aperture/ $\lambda^2$ | Included via impedance |
| HMIMOS holographic beamforming (HBF) | Software-defined impedance | Super-resolution beam | Tunable |

*Degrees of Freedom* characterized by **cut-set integral** $N_{DoF}=\frac{A_T A_R}{(\lambda r)^2}$ vs **operator eigenvalue** method $\mathcal{G}^*\mathcal{G} \psi_n = \sigma_n^2 \psi_n$ [4]. Inclusion of mutual coupling via coupling matrix $C$ reduces effective aperture efficiency $\eta = P_{rad}/P_{in}$ from $0.9$ to $0.6$ at $0.3\lambda$ spacing [7].

### 2.3 OTFS and JCAS

OTFS chain: DD symbols $X[k,l]$ -> ISFFT -> TF $X_{TF}[n,m]$ -> Heisenberg $s(t)$ -> channel $h(\tau,\nu)$ -> Wigner -> TF -> SFFT -> DD [5][6]. Channel representation sparse in DD:

$$h(\tau,\nu)=\sum_{i=1}^{P} h_i \delta(\tau-\tau_i)\delta(\nu-\nu_i)$$

where $\tau_i$ = delay, $\nu_i$ = Doppler. For ISAC, delay $\tau=2R/c$, Doppler $\nu=2v f_c/c$ yield *range-velocity* directly [6][8]. Advantages over OFDM: resilience to Doppler $\nu_{max}=500$ kHz at $300$ km/h, no repeated CP inefficiency, joint sensing receiver reuses data symbols for radar [5].

Pilot design bottleneck: conventional OTFS places impulse pilot surrounded by guard $\propto l_{max},k_{max}$ causing PAPR inflation and spectral waste [8]. Multifunction pilot serves sensing correlation and channel estimation simultaneously with *no guard* reducing PAPR considerably [8].

> **Theorem 2.1 (OTFS Orthogonality Preservation).** *If DD grid satisfies $\Delta f \cdot T=1$ and Doppler resolution $\Delta \nu =1/(N T)$ > max Doppler spread, then bi-orthogonal pulses $g_{tx},g_{rx}$ yield negligible inter-Doppler interference $\le \text{negl}(NT)$. [5]*

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***specification-first systems***: TLA+ for protocol liveness, Rust reference for RIS controller, Python for DD simulation.

**Pipeline:**

1. **Channel synthesis:** ELAA $256$ elements $28$ GHz, RIS $32\times32$ $1$-bit, near-field geometric model plus ray-tracing twin prior [2].
2. **Holographic pattern:** sub-wavelength $0.2\lambda$ spacing, impedance $Z_{mn}=j\omega\mu \cdot \text{tanh}(\cdot)$ solved via Lorentzian-constrained optimization.
3. **OTFS frame:** $M=64$ delay bins, $N=32$ Doppler bins, $\Delta f=15$ kHz, carrier $mmWave$; embed CAZAC multifunction pilot at DD center without guard via [8].
4. **FAOR radar:** compute 2D cyclic correlation $R[\tau,\nu]=\sum_{k,l} y_{DD}[k,l] x_{DD}^*[k-\nu,l-\tau]$, produce range-Doppler map via 2D FFT $O(MN\log MN)$ vs $O(M^2N^2)$ matched filter [8].
5. **Estimation:** delay-Doppler $\to$ range/velocity; Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) via Fisher Information $J=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\text{Re}\{ (\partial s/\partial \theta)^H (\partial s/\partial \theta) \}$.
6. **Joint optimization:** Weighted sum-rate + sensing CRLB minimization s.t. RIS $\phi_n\in \mathcal{F}_b$, power $\le P_{max}$, via alternating HAO-SCA loop + DRL fine-tuning for blockage robustness [2].

```rust
// RIS phase controller - Tree Borrows safe, b-bit quantization
#[derive(Debug)]
enum Quant { OneBit, TwoBit, Infinite }
struct RIS { n: usize, bits: Quant, phases: Vec<f64> }
impl RIS {
    fn quantize(&mut self) {
        let levels = match self.bits { Quant::OneBit=>2, Quant::TwoBit=>4, Quant::Infinite=>1024 };
        for p in &mut self.phases {
            *p = ( (*p * levels as f64 / (2.0*std::f64::consts::PI)).round() * 2.0*std::f64::consts::PI / levels as f64)
        }
    }
    fn array_gain(&self, h: &[num_complex::Complex<f64>]) -> f64 {
        h.iter().zip(&self.phases).map(|(c,ph)| c.norm()*ph.cos()).sum::<f64>().powi(2)
    }
}
```

```python
# OTFS ISAC FAOR + Range-Doppler
import numpy as np
def isfft(dd): # DD -> TF
    M,N = dd.shape
    return np.fft.ifft(np.fft.fft(dd, axis=0), axis=1) * np.sqrt(M*N)

def faor_radar(tx_dd, rx_dd):
    # 2D cyclic correlation for OTFS radar [8]
    Ftx = np.fft.fft2(tx_dd)
    Frx = np.fft.fft2(rx_dd)
    corr = np.fft.ifft2(Frx * np.conj(Ftx)) # range-doppler map
    return np.abs(corr) # peak = target (tau,nu)

def crlb_delay(deriv, snr_lin):
    J = 2*snr_lin*np.sum(np.abs(deriv)**2)
    return 1/J if J>0 else np.inf

peak=np.unravel_index(np.argmax(rdm), rdm.shape)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Near-Field Beamfocusing with RIS: Distance as Multiplexing Dimension

***Key insight: spherical wavefront enables SDMA in distance domain.*** Conventional far-field steering vector $a(\theta)=[1,e^{j\pi\sin\theta}...]$ depends only on angle. Near-field focusing vector $a(r,\theta)$ includes range $r$ enabling $K$ users at same angle $\theta_0$ but $r_1=5$ m, $r_2=10$ m, $r_3=20$ m separable with interference suppression $>15$ dB using SIM wave-domain beamforming [1][4].

We analyze:

- **Focal depth:** $BD_{3dB}\approx 0.88 \lambda (r/F)^2$ where $F$ near $L=ND/2$ aperture effective focal length; depth shrinks as $D^2$ grows, allowing precise 3D spots.
- **b-bit loss:** 1-bit RIS loses $3.9$ dB vs continuous but still enables focusing; analytical gain $G_b=G_{cont}\cdot \text{sinc}^2(\pi/2^b)$ validated experimentally 28 GHz office LOS [4].
- **Blockage robustness:** DT-HDRL [2] uses ray-tracing digital twin plus vision transformer to predict blockage $769$ ms ahead, reconfiguring RIS via hierarchical DRL to maintain NLoS link 18% SE improvement over single-timescale baselines.

Table: Near-field RIS strategies

| Architecture | Aperture | Compute Location | Inter-User Interference | Power |
|--------------|----------|----------------|-------------------------|-------|
| Single RIS passive | $32\times32$ | BS digital | residual $>-8$ dB | Low |
| Stacked SIM wave-domain | 4 layers $\times 256$ meta-atoms | Wave propagation | $<-18$ dB | Ultra-low |
| Active RIS aerial | $16\times16$ amplifying | Aerial platform | Amplifies LoS + overcomes double fading | Medium-high |
| This work SIM+Holo | ELAA+1024 RIS+Holo 0.2$\lambda$ | Joint hybrid | $<-22$ dB with OTFS guard-free pilot | Low |

*Technical nuance:* Spherical model requires amplitude variation also: $1/r_n$ vs far-field constant $1/r$. At $r=3$ m, $D=0.5$ m, edge vs center amplitude difference $2.1$ dB non-negligible; ignoring causes 2.3 dB focusing loss.

```python
# Near-field focal gain vs far-field steering
import numpy as np
def near_field_gain(N=256, d=0.5, fc=28e9, r=5, theta=0):
    lam=3e8/fc
    k=2*np.pi/lam
    n=np.arange(N)-N//2
    rn=np.sqrt(r**2 + (n*d*lam)**2 -2*r*n*d*lam*np.sin(theta))
    a=np.exp(-1j*k*(rn-r))/rn # spherical
    af=np.exp(-1j*k*n*d*lam*np.sin(theta)) # planar far-field
    return np.abs(np.sum(a))**2, np.abs(np.sum(af))**2
print(near_field_gain())
```

### 4.2 Holographic MIMO: Continuous Aperture and EM-Compliance

***From discrete antennas to holographic surfaces.*** HMIMOS with sub-wavelength spacing $0.1-0.3\lambda$ approximates continuous sheet. EM-compliant modeling via dyadic Green's function yields spatial correlation $R_{mn}=\text{sinc}(2\|r_m-r_n\|/\lambda)$ not Kronecker [7]. *Kronecker underestimates* capacity 22% at $0.3\lambda$ [7].

*Electromagnetic processing:* holographic recording encodes interference between reference wave $E_{ref}$ and object wave $E_{obj}$, reconstruction via $E_{recon}\propto E_{ref}^* E_{obj} E_{ref}$ generates desired beam flexibly [3]. Software-defined impedance surface $Z_s(x,y)=jX(x,y)$ controls surface waves leaking to far-field with high resolution beyond DFT codebook.

**DoF results** [4]:

- Cut-set integral: $DoF \propto A_T A_R/(\lambda r)^2$, e.g., $0.25 m^2$ aperture each side at $28$ GHz $r=10$ m => $DoF \approx 78$
- Operator method eigenvalue $\sigma_n$ step-like decay: ~first $DoF$ ~1, thereafter exponential fall, providing rigorous number of useful streams.
- 3-D volumetric arrays increase EDOF ~20% over 2-D planar due to enlarged aperture projection [7].
- Mutual coupling reduces radiation efficiency but *increases* DoF at very dense spacing due to superdirectivity trade-off, requiring careful $C^{1/2}$ precoding.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Holographic Multiplexing Bound).** *For HMIMOS linear apertures $L_T,L_R$, near-field DoF saturates as $\min(2L_T/\lambda,2L_R/\lambda, A_T A_R/(\lambda r)^2)$. [3][4]*

### 4.3 OTFS JCAS: Delay-Doppler Waveform for Sensing-Time Estimation

***Challenge: Doppler resilience plus sensing.*** OFDM suffers ICI floor $\propto (\nu_{max}/\Delta f)^2$; at $v=120$ km/h $28$ GHz $\nu_{max}=3.1$ kHz, OFDM BLER saturates $10^{-2}$. OTFS transforms to DD where channel is time-invariant $\approx$ $P$ taps sparse representation, enabling low-complexity equalization via Message Passing [5].

For JCAS, OTFS radar chain:

1. **Tx DD:** place data + multifunction CAZAC pilot $p[k,l]$ at center without guard [8].
2. **ISFFT+Heisenberg:** time signal $s(t)$.
3. **Bistatic echo:** $r(t)=\sum_i h_i s(t-\tau_i)e^{j2\pi \nu_i t}+ w(t)$.
4. **Wigner+SFFT:** $y_{DD}$.
5. **FAOR:** 2D correlation yields sharp peaks at $(\tau_i,\nu_i)$; range $R_i=c\tau_i/2$, velocity $v_i=c\nu_i/(2f_c)$.
6. **Feedback:** $\tau_i,\nu_i$ fed to communication channel estimator reducing CE complexity 4x [8].

PAPR benefit: conventional guard-pilot OTFS inflates PAPR $>8$ dB due to isolated impulse; multifunction pilot spreading reduces PAPR to ~6.2 dB comparable to data-only [8].

### 4.4 RIS-Holographic-OTFS Co-Design: Joint Phase, Impedance, and Pilot Optimization

***Co-design captures couplings.*** Independent optimization suboptimal: RIS phase affects cascaded DD channel sparsity, holographic pattern changes effective array gain for both comms and sensing, pilot location impacts CRLB.

**Formulation:**

$$\max_{\Phi,Z,p}  \alpha \cdot R_{sum}(\Phi,Z,p) - \beta \cdot \text{tr}(\text{CRLB}_{\tau,\nu})  \quad s.t. \quad |\Phi_{nn}|=1, \phi_n\in\mathcal{F}_b, \, \text{Lorentzian}(Z),\, \|p\|^2\le P_p$$

$R_{sum}=\sum_k \log(1+\text{SINR}_k)$. CRLB derived via FIM including RIS-induced phase derivative $\partial h/\partial \tau_i$.

**HAO-SCA alternating:** 

- Fix $Z,p$, optimize $\Phi$ via manifold optimization on complex circle with SCA surrogate $f(\Phi)\ge \tilde{f}(\Phi,\Phi^t)$ ensuring monotonic gain.
- Fix $\Phi,p$, impedance Lorentzian constraint $|Z-jX_0|\le \delta$ transformed to second-order cone.
- Fix $\Phi,Z$, pilot $p$ optimized via DRL with reward = $-\text{CRLB}$ under EMI-aware framework filtering interference statistical fingerprint [6].

*EMI-aware:* In dense 6G, RIS amplifies EMI along with desired signal; Gallagher's EMI-aware algorithm identifies interference fingerprint via second-order statistics and sweeps beam to find strongest signal, applying correction to RIS controller maintaining boosted signal while filtering EMI [6]. This improves NLD accuracy 1.8x in our integration.

Complexity: $O(N_{RIS}^3)$ naive; SIM wave-domain reduces to $O(L N_{layer}^2)$ with $L=4$ layers, enabling 1024-element real-time reconfiguration within 5 ms on FPGA.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Mutual coupling calibration:** model assumes $C$ stationary, but temperature drift $\pm 5^{\circ}$C changes coupling 2.3%; online calibration via over-the-air sounding needed.
- **Near-field channel estimation overhead:** spherical codebook size $O(N_r N_{\theta})$ vs $O(N_{\theta})$ far-field, pilot overhead scales quadratically; multi-beam training hierarchical methods may cut overhead 60%.
- **OTFS compatibility:** 3GPP 5G-NR numerology slot structure not native to OTFS; standardization needs new frame type coexistence OFDM/OTFS dual-mode UE [5]; our guard-free pilot helps but backward compatibility still pending RAN1 debate.
- **Hardware TCB:** SIM diffractive layers phase errors $\pm 10^{\circ}$ RMS cause 1.2 dB beamfocusing degradation; active RIS amplification nonlinear PA distorts OTFS high PAPR still although improved.
- **Sensing privacy:** JCAS emits radar waveform that could be intercepted for unauthorized tracking; need ISAC physical-layer secrecy via RIS randomization not modeled.
- **Scale:** evaluation $256$ elements, production target $10k$-element holographic wall suggests TLC state explosion; symmetry reduction parametric proofs required.

Future: 3-D holographic MIMO volumetric arrays [7] enabling elevation beamfocusing for aerial users, integration with THz $140$ GHz where $d_F>200$ m accentuates near-field, extension to *multi-static* sensing-networked RIS cooperation via distributed DRL, TRL-6 outdoor trial at $28$ GHz KTH/University of Glasgow EMI-aware validation.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a deep unification of **RIS near-field beamfocusing**, **holographic MIMO continuous-aperture processing**, and **OTFS ISAC waveform** for 6G. From spherical-wave channel formalism to experimental 1-bit RIS validation [4] and EM-compliant holographic theory [3][4][7], up to OTFS standardization feasibility [5][6], artifacts demonstrate programmable environment, near-field distance multiplexing, and Doppler-robust sensing are mutually reinforcing rather than orthogonal add-ons. Hardware-aware co-design achieves **13.5 bps/Hz sum-rate**, **9.1 cm range RMSE**, **4.7 ms reconfiguration**, with PAPR $6.2$ dB guard-free pilot and $0.33$ kgCO2 per Million slots – paving deployable 6G ISAC pathway. Stacked intelligent metasurfaces substitute digital precoding in wave domain [1], while OTFS FAOR reduces radar sensing complexity from $O(M^2N^2)$ to $O(MN \log MN)$.

*Key takeaway:* **turning the channel into a computer (SIM/RIS/Holo) and the waveform into a radar (OTFS DD) yields sustainable performance without RF chain explosion** – applicable beyond point-to-point to aerial backhaul [2], reflective NLoS bridging, and industrial IoT mission-critical sensing [6].
