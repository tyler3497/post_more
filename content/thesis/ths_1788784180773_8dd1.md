# Linux Memory Management at Scale: Folios, Transparent Huge Pages, and DAMON-Guided Reclaim

## Abstract

Modern servers routinely carry terabytes of DRAM, yet the Linux virtual memory subsystem still speaks, in its innermost dialect, of 4 KiB pages: one `struct page` per frame, a page-table walk per access check, and a reclaim algorithm that must decide, millions of times per second, which frames deserve to live. This thesis analyzes three interlocking responses to that scale crisis — **folios** as a type-safe unit of multi-page management, **transparent huge pages (THP)** with their asynchronous collapse daemon `khugepaged`, and **DAMON** (Data Access MONitor), a region-granular, sampling-based access monitor that drives data-driven policies such as DAMON-based reclaim (`DAMON_RECLAIM`) and tiered memory migration. We trace the evolution from ambiguous compound-page APIs to the folio type introduced in 5.16 [1], quantify the metadata overhead that folios eliminate (up to 75% of per-huge-page `struct page` waste [2]), dissect the `khugepaged` scan-and-collapse pipeline including its tunable limits (`pages_to_scan`, `max_ptes_none`) [3], and formalize DAMON's adaptive three-layer region model — initial regions, sampled random addresses, and access-frequency merging/splitting — with its DAMOS scheme engine that maps *(size, frequency, age)* triples onto actions such as `pageout`, `hugepage`, `migrate_hot`, and `migrate_cold` [4][5]. We further evaluate how DAMON-guided proactive reclaim complements Multi-Gen LRU at region granularity, and how DAMON-driven tiering reduces tiered-memory slowdowns from 11% to 3–5% under pressure [6]. The synthesis argues that folios, THP, and DAMON together constitute a coherent program: shrink the unit-cost of management, amortize TLB pressure with huge pages, and let measured locality — not heuristics — steer reclamation.

## 1. Introduction

The page-table entry was designed when megabytes were generous. On a 2 TiB host, a fully-populated 4 KiB page space demands over 500 million `struct page` descriptors — roughly 32 GiB of `memmap` metadata alone — and each reclaim pass must reason about the set at a cadence the CPU cannot afford. Three pressures collide: **metadata cost**, **TLB coverage**, and **policy accuracy**. Metadata cost grows linearly with memory; a single 2 MiB THP's 512 page structures occupy eight pages of `memmap`, seven of which are near-identical copies pointing back at the head [2]. TLB coverage demands huge pages, yet promotion (collapse) is expensive and fragmentation-hostile. Policy accuracy demands knowing what is hot — but scanning page tables of a terabyte-scale process on every reclaim tick is infeasible.

The kernel's answer is architectural, not merely incremental. *Folios* refactor the MM subsystem around a first-class type for physically contiguous page sets, eliminating a whole class of head/tail confusion bugs and opening the door to large-page-cache folios [1][7]. *THP* plus `khugepaged` automates promotion and, through `madvise(MADV_COLLAPSE)`, synchronous collapse [8]. *DAMON* abandons per-page tracking for adaptive regions: it samples one random address per region per interval, then merges and splits regions by observed access frequency, yielding a statistically grounded hot/cold map at sub-1% overhead [4]. Crucially, DAMON's output is **policy-agnostic**: DAMOS (Data Access Monitoring-based Operation Schemes) turn the map into actions — proactive reclaim, huge-page promotion (`ethp`), tiering migrations (`migrate_hot`/`migrate_cold`), and LRU sorting [5][6].

> **Theorem 1 (Scale trilemma).** For a memory system of size $M$ managed at granularity $g$ with monitoring cost $c$ per unit, no single static policy simultaneously minimizes metadata ($M/g$), maximizes TLB reach (large $g$), and maximizes reclaim precision (small $g$, fine $c$). Folios bound metadata, THP enlarges $g$, and DAMON decouples $c$ from $M$.

> **Proof.** Metadata is proportional to $M/g$; shrinking $g$ for precision explodes metadata unless the per-unit descriptor is deduplicated — exactly the folio's structural move. TLB reach scales with $g$, requiring promotion, which is amortized only asynchronously (`khugepaged`). Precision at cost $c$ requires sampling rather than exhaustive scanning; DAMON's region adaptation keeps $c$ sublinear in $M$. Each mechanism optimizes one leg of the trilemma while the others cover the remainder. ∎

The remainder of this thesis: §2 surveys background; §3 states methodology; §4 dissects folios, THP/`khugepaged`, and DAMON/DAMOS; §5 presents empirical results and formal properties; §6 discusses limitations; §7 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The compound-page problem

Before folios, a "page" in Linux could denote a base 4 KiB frame *or* a compound (multi-page) block, distinguished at runtime by the `PG_head`/`PG_tail` flags (bits 15/16 of `page->flags`) [9]. APIs such as `compound_order()` silently returned 0 on tail pages, and callers of `isolate_migratepages_block()` had to defensively call `compound_head()` before interpreting anything [7]. This ambiguity was not merely stylistic: it generated real defects in migration, reclaim, and GUP paths, where a tail page handed to a head-page-only helper produced corrupt accounting.

### 2.2 Folios

Matthew Wilcox's folio work (merged in Linux 5.16, the release's headline MM feature [1]) introduces `struct folio` — logically the head page of a contiguous set — as the canonical unit for the page cache, migration, and increasingly reclaim [7]. Seeing a `struct folio *` is itself a proof: it cannot be a tail. The direct wins are (i) elimination of redundant `compound_head()` calls, (ii) removal of judgment branches, and (iii) a type-level guarantee against tail-page misuse. Indirectly, folios are the prerequisite for large page-cache folios and for unifying hugetlbfs with the ordinary MM subsystem: at the 2024 LSFMM+BPF summit, Peter Xu argued that large-folio work is what makes hugetlbfs consolidation tractable, since 11 hugetlbfs-specific code paths might shrink to two or three [10].

### 2.3 Transparent huge pages

THP (default 2 MiB on x86-64 via `_PAGE_PSE` at the PMD level) collapses contiguous 4 KiB mappings into single PMD entries, cutting TLB misses and page-table depth. The asynchronous daemon `khugepaged` scans VMAs at lowest priority (`khugepaged_do_scan`), bounded by `pages_to_scan` (default 4096 = 8 × `1 << (PMD_SHIFT - PAGE_SHIFT)`) and `max_ptes_none` (default 511, the number of zero-filled subpages tolerated during collapse) [3]. Synchronous collapse arrived via `MADV_COLLAPSE` [8].

### 2.4 DAMON and DAMOS

DAMON (by SeongJae Park, merged 5.15) monitors access patterns at *region* granularity. Users specify target address ranges; DAMON splits each into initial regions, then per sampling interval reads one random address per region and checks its access bit (physical address space monitoring, `damon_paddr`), or page-table accessed bits / idle tracking for virtual spaces. Regions are periodically merged and split by `nr_accesses` and age, so hot spots shrink to fine granularity while cold expanses coalesce [4]. DAMOS schemes specify *(min/max size, min/max access frequency, min/max age) → action*, executed against matching regions with quotas and watermarks [5]. Stock modules `damon_reclaim` (proactive reclaim) and `damon_lru_sort` (LRU prioritization) ship in-tree.

### 2.5 Related: MGLRU

Multi-Gen LRU (MGLRU) tracks per-folio generations by walking page tables — efficient *ordering* for reclaim — while DAMON samples at region granularity for *policy*: tiering, THP promotion, working-set reports. The two coexist and complement each other [4].

## 3. Methodology

We adopt a *source-grounded systems analysis*: every mechanism claim is traced to kernel documentation, LWN reporting, or LKML design threads. Specifically:

1. **Code-path archaeology.** We reconstruct `khugepaged`'s scan loop (`khugepaged_do_scan` → `khugepaged_scan_pmd` → `collapse_huge_page`), the folio API (`folio_*` conversions, `page_folio`), and DAMON's three-layer region pipeline (`damon_init_regions` → `kdamond` sampling → `damon_merge_regions`/`damon_split_regions` → DAMOS application) from documentation and published patch series [3][5][11].
2. **Quantitative extraction.** Overhead and effect sizes are taken from published evaluations: memmap savings from Song's vmemmap deduplication [2], THP tunable defaults [3], DAMON overhead bounds and the original `ethp`/`prcl` PARSEC results [5], and DAMON tiered-memory evaluations on CXL systems [6].
3. **Formal modeling.** We model DAMON region adaptation as an adaptive quantization process and state convergence and overhead bounds as theorems with proofs (§5).
4. **Comparative analysis.** DAMON vs. MGLRU vs. idle-page tracking are compared on granularity, mechanism, overhead, and policy expressiveness (Table 1).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The folio as a type-level invariant

The folio's deepest contribution is not performance but **correctness by construction**. In C, where the type system cannot express "this pointer is a head page," the convention *"`struct folio *` is always a head"* converts a global invariant into a local, checkable one. Callers that previously wrote:

```c
page = pfn_to_page(low_pfn);
if (PageCompound(page) && !cc->alloc_contig) {
    const unsigned int order = compound_order(page); /* BUG if tail */
```

now operate on folios where `folio_order()` is well-defined by construction [7]. The migration path (`migrate_pages` → `migrate_folio`), the page cache (`filemap_get_folio`), and GUP (`get_user_pages` returning folios internally) all shed defensive `compound_head()` calls. At scale this matters: on a 2 TiB machine, compaction and reclaim touch millions of descriptors per second, and every eliminated branch and redundant pointer chase is multiplied by the working set.

```rust
// Conceptual analogy: folios as a newtype invariant (illustrative, not kernel code)
struct Folio {
    head_pfn: u64,   // guaranteed head: no tail can inhabit this type
    order: u8,       // 2^order contiguous base pages
}

impl Folio {
    fn order(&self) -> u8 { self.order } // total: no partial-function hazard
    fn nr_pages(&self) -> u64 { 1u64 << self.order }
}
```

The `Rust` sketch captures the idea: a *newtype* whose inhabitants provably satisfy the head-page invariant, turning a partial function (`compound_order` on arbitrary pages) into a total one.

### 4.2 THP and the khugepaged collapse pipeline

THP promotion has two routes: *fault-time* allocation of a huge page when the faulting VMA is aligned and the buddy allocator can satisfy order-9, and *background* collapse by `khugepaged`. The daemon's pipeline is a bounded scan:

1. **Scan.** `khugepaged_do_scan()` walks eligible mm's VMAs, calling `khugepaged_scan_pmd()` per PMD. At most `pages_to_scan` (4096) base pages are examined per pass; the thread then sleeps on `khugepaged_wait` [3].
2. **Suitability.** A PMD qualifies if enough of its 512 PTEs are present, young, non-shared (subject to `max_ptes_shared`), writable-compatible, and from the same NUMA node; `max_ptes_none` (511) caps how many may be unmapped holes filled with zero pages.
3. **Collapse.** `collapse_huge_page()` allocates the target huge folio with `GFP_TRANSHUGE`, copies/zero-fills contents, installs the PMD atomically under the mmap lock, and frees the 512 base pages — with full rollback on any `SCAN_*` failure code (`SCAN_PAGE_LOCK`, `SCAN_PAGE_COMPOUND`, `SCAN_CGROUP_CHARGE_FAIL`, …) [8].

The design is deliberately conservative: collapse races against concurrent faults, so every step re-validates. Failure is cheap and silent; success amortizes TLB pressure for the lifetime of the mapping.

### 4.3 DAMON: adaptive region monitoring

DAMON's core loop, per monitoring context and target, is:

```
initial regions (user-specified ranges)
  → split into N sub-regions
  → per sampling interval: pick one random address per region,
                           record access bit → nr_accesses
  → per aggregation interval: merge adjacent regions with similar
      nr_accesses; split regions with divergent access into halves
  → DAMOS schemes: for each region, if (size, freq, age) matches,
      apply action subject to quotas/watermarks
```

This is *adaptive quantization* of the address space: resolution follows information. A 1 TiB heap with a 4 GiB hot working set converges to a handful of fine hot regions and a few coarse cold ones, so monitoring cost is proportional to the number of regions (bounded, default ≤ 1000), not to memory size. The physical-address implementation (`damon_paddr`) samples via the idle-page-tracking-style access-bit checks, keeping overhead sub-1% [4][5].

### 4.4 DAMOS: from observations to actions

DAMOS decouples *what we know* from *what we do*. A scheme is a predicate over *(size ∈ [min,max], accesses ∈ [min,max] %, age ∈ [min,max])* and an action. Stock actions include `pageout` (proactive reclaim), `hugepage`/`nohugepage` (promotion/demotion), `stat` (working-set accounting), `lru_prio`/`lru_deprio`, and — for tiered memory — `migrate_hot`/`migrate_cold` between NUMA nodes [6]. Two canonical proof-of-concept schemes from the original design document illustrate the idiom [5]:

- **ethp**: region with ≥5% access rate → `hugepage`; region ≥2 MiB with <5% access for ≥13 s → `nohugepage`. THP policy becomes *measured*, not heuristic.
- **prcl**: region ≥4 KiB with ≤5% access for ≥7 s → `pageout`. Proactive reclaim with an explicit, tunable definition of "cold."

Quotas (`ms` per interval, bytes per interval, reset interval) bound each scheme's blast radius, and watermarks (`high`/`low` free-memory thresholds) gate activation — reclaim only engages when memory is actually scarce.

### 4.5 DAMON-guided reclaim and tiered memory at scale

`DAMON_RECLAIM` is the production packaging of proactive reclaim: a kernel module with sysfs/module-parameter knobs (`min_age`, quotas, watermarks) that pages out cold regions *before* kswapd pressure arrives, smoothing latency tails [11]. It operates on any reclaimable memory — page cache, anonymous (with swap), shmem — bounded only by the monitored regions (default: the largest contiguous System RAM range) and the scheme's filters [11].

The most striking at-scale result is **tiered memory management**: on DRAM+CXL systems, DAMOS `migrate_hot` promotes hot regions to DRAM while `migrate_cold` demotes cold regions to CXL. Evaluations with Redis/YCSB workloads show the default NUMA policy slowing down 11% under fast-tier pressure, while DAMON-driven tiering holds the slowdown to 3–5% [6]. Note the synergy with folios: migration now moves `struct folio`s, and DAMON regions naturally align with multi-page units, so a hot 2 MiB folio migrates as one decision rather than 512 correlated ones.

```python
# Illustrative: DAMOS-style scheme evaluation over monitored regions
def apply_schemes(regions, schemes, quotas):
    for r in regions:                      # r: (start, end, nr_accesses, age)
        for s in schemes:                  # s: predicate + action + quota
            if s.matches(r.size, r.access_pct, r.age) and quotas[s].allow(r.size):
                s.action(r)                 # pageout / hugepage / migrate_hot / ...
                quotas[s].consume(r.size)
                break                       # first matching scheme wins
```

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Metadata: the folio dividend

For one 2 MiB THP, 512 `struct page`s (64 B each = 32 KiB) collapse to 8 pages of descriptors; Song's vmemmap deduplication maps the 7 identical tail-page descriptor pages onto one, returning 6 pages (24 KiB) to the system — a 75% overhead reduction per huge page, and ~16 MiB per 1 GiB huge page [2]. Folios compound this by letting reclaim, migration, and the page cache reason about *one* object per 512 frames.

> **Theorem 2 (Region-count bound).** Let $R_t$ be DAMON's region count at aggregation step $t$ with min/max region counts $r_{\min}, r_{\max}$. Then $r_{\min} \le R_t \le r_{\max}$ for all $t$, and per-interval sampling cost is $\Theta(R_t)$, independent of monitored size $M$.

> **Proof.** Merge reduces count only while $R_t > r_{\min}$; split increases only while $R_t < r_{\max}$ — both operations clamp. Each interval samples exactly one address per region, hence $\Theta(R_t)$ work regardless of $M$. ∎

### 5.2 THP collapse economics

Collapse costs one order-9 allocation, 512 PTE manipulations, and a copy/zero-fill; it pays off when the mapping's lifetime TLB-miss savings exceed that one-time cost — the rationale for `khugepaged`'s conservative suitability checks and for gating on young/accessed PTEs [3]. Synchronous `MADV_COLLAPSE` lets latency-tolerant setup phases (e.g., database buffer pool initialization) pay the cost upfront [8].

### 5.3 DAMON accuracy and overhead

The original DAMON evaluation on PARSEC/SPLASH-2x showed monitoring overhead of 0.40–0.82% runtime on `blackscholes`/`bodytrack` for recording configurations, with the `ethp` scheme *improving* runtime slightly (−0.39%/−0.50%) by promoting genuinely hot regions — evidence that measured promotion beats heuristic THP [5]. The proactive-reclaim (`prcl`) scheme paid up to 7% on `blackscholes` in that untuned proof of concept, underscoring that scheme tuning (age thresholds, quotas) is the production-critical step.

### 5.4 Tiered memory

As cited in §4.5: DAMON tiered kswapd/demotion with `DAMOS_MIGRATE_HOT/COLD` cut CXL-tier slowdown from 11% to 3–5% versus the default policy, and — unlike plain demotion — never triggered OOM in the evaluated configurations [6].

### 5.5 Working-set estimation

Because region sizes adapt to access density, the sum of hot-region sizes is a direct working-set-size (WSS) estimator, exportable via DAMOS `stat` — capacity planning without page-table scans.

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

1. **Sampling fidelity.** DAMON samples one address per region per interval; bursty or adversarial access patterns (e.g., strided scans touching exactly one cache line per region) can be misclassified. Confidence scales with aggregation intervals, trading responsiveness for accuracy.
2. **Folio conversion is incomplete.** As of recent kernels, compaction and parts of reclaim still operate on pages; large page-cache folios and full hugetlbfs unification remain in progress [10]. Mixed page/folio code paths are a lingering source of subtle bugs.
3. **THP collapse fragility.** Under fragmentation, order-9 allocations fail and `khugepaged` silently skips; workloads with short-lived mappings never amortize collapse. `MADV_COLLAPSE` helps only where the application cooperates.
4. **Scheme tuning burden.** DAMOS's expressiveness is also its hazard: `prcl`'s 7% regression in the original evaluation [5] shows that poorly chosen age/frequency thresholds actively harm. Production deployments need workload-specific calibration and quota discipline.
5. **Evaluation scope.** The tiering numbers [6] come from Redis/YCSB on specific CXL hardware; generalization to other topologies (multi-hop CXL fabrics, HBM tiers) is unproven. DAMON's sub-1% overhead claims assume default intervals; aggressive sampling reintroduces cost.
6. **Threats to validity.** This thesis synthesizes published evaluations rather than running new benchmarks; effect sizes are quoted from their original contexts and may not transfer. Kernel internals evolve rapidly — defaults cited (e.g., `pages_to_scan = 4096`) reflect documented behavior at time of writing.

## 7. Conclusion

Folios, transparent huge pages, and DAMON-guided reclaim form a layered answer to memory management at scale. **Folios** fix the unit of management: a type-level head-page invariant that kills a class of MM bugs and makes multi-page reasoning cheap. **THP with `khugepaged`** amortizes address-translation cost by promoting hot, stable mappings to huge pages in the background, with synchronous `MADV_COLLAPSE` for the impatient. **DAMON** replaces exhaustive scanning with adaptive, sampling-based region monitoring whose cost is independent of memory size, and **DAMOS** turns the resulting hot/cold map into a programmable policy substrate — proactive reclaim, measured huge-page promotion, and tiered migration that demonstrably tames CXL slowdowns. The common thread is *measurement over heuristics*: as memory systems grow into terabytes and heterogeneous tiers, the kernel can no longer afford to guess. It must observe, adapt, and act — and these three mechanisms are how it does so.
