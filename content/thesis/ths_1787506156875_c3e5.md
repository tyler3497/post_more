# Learned Cardinality Estimation for Query Optimizers: NeuroCard, DeepDB, BayesCard, and FactorJoin via Probabilistic Graphical Models

## Abstract
Cardinality estimation remains the Achilles' heel of cost-based query optimization, where errors propagate exponentially through join ordering and physical operator selection. Learned cardinality estimators replace classical independence assumptions with deep generative and graphical models that capture full joint data distributions. This thesis synthesizes four state-of-the-art data-driven estimators—NeuroCard, DeepDB, BayesCard, and FactorJoin—through the unifying lens of probabilistic graphical models. We formalize estimation as inference over P(T) for tables T with joins J and predicates Q, analyze autoregressive factorization, sum-product networks, Bayesian networks with Chow-Liu trees, and factor graphs for join handling. Empirical synthesis across JOB-light, JOB-M, and STATS-CEB shows 8–60× accuracy gains over PostgreSQL histograms and 10–100× reductions in model size and training cost versus deep models. We prove complexity bounds, discuss progressive sampling, fanout attributes, and incremental maintenance, and outline production integration via probabilistic programming. Results demonstrate that lightweight graphical models attain comparable accuracy to deep autoregressive models with superior deployability.

## 1. Introduction

**Cardinality estimation (CardEst)** is the problem of predicting |Q(D)|, the number of tuples returned by query Q over database D, without executing Q [1]. Query optimizers depend critically on these estimates: a single Q-error of 10× can flip a hash join to nested-loop and degrade runtime by 100× [2]. Classical systems rely on three simplifying assumptions introduced by System R [3]:

* *Attribute Value Independence (AVI)*: P(A,B) ≈ P(A)P(B)
* *Join Uniformity*: P(A|J) ≈ P(A)
* *Join Predicate Independence*: P(J1,J2) ≈ P(J1)P(J2)

These assumptions are *catastrophically* violated in modern schemas. IMDB JOB benchmark contains correlations where AVI underestimates by 4 orders of magnitude [4]. *Learned* estimators abandon these assumptions and learn P_data directly from tables.

This thesis contributes a unified analysis of four leading architectures:

1. **NeuroCard** [1] – one autoregressive model for all tables via full outer join sampling
2. **DeepDB** [2] – Relational Sum-Product Networks (RSPN) decomposing schemas hierarchically
3. **BayesCard** [3] – Bayesian Networks (BN) with probabilistic programming and graph reduction
4. **FactorJoin** [4] – single-table conditionals combined into factor graphs for joins

We argue that *probabilistic graphical models (PGMs)* are the natural formalism: they subsume histograms, provide tractable inference, and balance expressivity with deployability. While deep autoregressive models like Naru [5] achieve SOTA accuracy, they are 100–1000× larger and slower to update. PGMs achieve comparable accuracy with 2 orders of magnitude lower cost.

> Theorem 1 (Estimation Reduction): For any SPJ query Q = sigma_theta(T1 ⋈ ... ⋈ Tk), |Q| = |E| * P_E(Q) where E is the full outer join of all tables and P_E is its distribution. Hence CardEst reduces to density estimation over E [1].

### Contributions

- Formal PGM taxonomy for learned CardEst with complexity analysis
- Comparative dissection of NeuroCard's column factorization, DeepDB's RSPN learning, BayesCard's BN structure learning via Chow-Liu, and FactorJoin's factor-graph inference
- Empirical synthesis across JOB-light, JOB-M, JOB-light-ranges, STATS-CEB, DMV, CENSUS
- Production lessons: model size, training latency, inference latency, incremental maintenance, interpretability

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Classical Foundations

System R's estimator [3] uses 1-D histograms and distinct counts |R.K|. For join R.K = S.F:

```
P(J) = min(1/|R.K|, 1/|S.F|)
```

PostgreSQL extends this with most-common values (MCV) and functional dependencies but retains AVI. Sampling-based methods [6] execute on samples; they fail for low-selectivity predicates.

*Modern optimizers are starved of correlation information.* No commercial system stores n-D joint histograms for n>2 due to exponential blowup.

### 2.2 Learned Taxonomy

- **Query-driven**: MSCN [6], Local Partitioning Models, TabPFN regressors. Map Q -> |Q| via supervised regression. Efficient but brittle to workload shift.
- **Data-driven**: Naru, NeuroCard, DeepDB, FLAT, BayesCard, FactorJoin. Learn P(D) unsupervised, answer any Q via integral of indicator_Q * P(D).

Data-driven models dominate recent leaderboards because they *generalize* to unseen queries [2].

### 2.3 PGMs Primer

A PGM factorizes joint distribution via graph structure:

- **Directed (BN)**: P(X) = prod_i P(Xi | Pa(Xi))
- **Undirected / Factor**: P(X) = 1/Z prod_c phi_c(Xc)
- **Autoregressive**: P(X) = prod_i P(Xi | X_<i), implemented via MADE masks [1]

Inference queries: *marginalization* and *conditioning* with range predicates a <= X <= b.

| Model | Factorization | Inference | Learnable |
|-------|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| Histogram | prod P(Xi) independent | O(1) | MCV |
| BN | DAG conditional | Variable Elimination O(exp(w)) | Chow-Liu O(n^2) |
| SPN / RSPN | Sum=mixture, Product=independent | Linear O(|S|) | RDC clustering |
| Autoregressive | Chain rule | Progressive Sampling O(k*n) | MADE/Transformer |

*Table 1: PGM trade-offs. w = treewidth. NeuroCard's autoregressive has no independence assumption but requires sampling.*

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology unifies analysis via three lenses: **representation**, **learning**, and **inference for joins**.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given schema S = {Ti}, foreign-key graph G, and query workload Q, learn model M minimizing:

L = E_{Q~W}[ log Q_error(M(Q), |Q|) ] + lambda * Cost(M)

where Q_error = max(hat_c/c, c/hat_c) [1][2][3][4] and Cost includes size, training, latency.

### 3.2 Unified PGM View

All four systems learn P(Ti) per table then combine for joins:

- NeuroCard: learns P(E) directly via join samples, avoiding P(Ti) composition
- DeepDB: learns RSPN per table subset, stores Pr[T] and Pr[Fanout]
- BayesCard: learns BN per subset, uses fanout attributes F_{T->U} to correct scaling [3]
- FactorJoin: learns P(Ti | Key) single-table, constructs factor graph F = (V = {join keys}, Phi = {phi_T}) at query time [4]

> Theorem 2 (FactorJoin Correctness): If single-table conditionals are exact and join keys are independent given filter predicates, factor-graph variable elimination yields exact join cardinality [4].

### 3.3 Training Data Generation

Key innovation in NeuroCard: *uniform independent sampling of full outer join without materializing it* [1]. Using join-count tables via Exact-Weight algorithm, sample tuple t in E with probability 1/|E|. This avoids O(prod |Ti|) blowup.

DeepDB uses RDC (Randomized Dependence Coefficient) to test independence: if RDC(A,B) < tau, split via Product node; else cluster via K-means into Sum children [2].

BayesCard leverages probabilistic programming languages (Pyro, Stan) for structure learning: Chow-Liu finds maximum spanning tree of mutual information, then adds edges via hill-climbing with BIC score [3].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 NeuroCard: One Model for All Tables

NeuroCard builds a single deep autoregressive model over all columns of E [1]. Architecture:

- Input: d = sum |cols(Ti)| up to 100+ columns
- MADE with 4 layers, 128 units, masks enforcing Xi not -> X_<i
- Lossless column factorization: high-cardinality columns c with |Dom(c)|>10k factorized into (c1, c2) via base-2k decomposition, e.g., c = c1 * K + c2 [1]. Reduces embedding size 100×.
- Progressive sampling: to estimate P(Q), sample X1 ~ P(X1|Q1), then X2 ~ P(X2|X1,Q2), etc., averaging prod hat_P(Qi).

```python
# NeuroCard progressive sampling (simplified)
def estimate(model, predicates, n_samples=2000):
    logp = 0.0
    samples = []
    for col in topological_order:
        logits = model(samples)  # MADE forward
        dist = Categorical(logits=logits)
        # Condition on predicate range
        prob_in_range = dist.cdf(high) - dist.cdf(low)
        logp += torch.log(prob_in_range + 1e-12)
        # Sample inside range for next step
        s = dist.sample_truncated(low, high)
        samples.append(s)
    return torch.exp(logp).mean() * outer_join_size
```

Strengths: captures *all* cross-table correlations, 8.5× lower max Q-error on JOB-light vs best prior [1]. Weaknesses: full outer join sample dominated by NULLs for small-cardinality joins; training on STATS-CEB requires 1M samples for 1% coverage; model size ~10MB.

*Iterative refinement*: NeuroCardE extends to cyclic schemas via tree decomposition.

### 4.2 DeepDB: Relational Sum-Product Networks

DeepDB learns an ensemble of RSPNs [2]. Each RSPN is a tree:

- **Leaf**: histogram or piecewise linear for single column
- **Sum**: mixture sum w_i P_i(X), w_i = cluster proportion
- **Product**: prod_j P_j(X_Aj) when RDC indicates independence

Learning algorithm:

```haskell
learnRSPN :: Table -> RSPN
learnRSPN tbl
  | cols tbl == 1 = Leaf (hist tbl)
  | independent tbl = Product (map learnRSPN (splitCols tbl))
  | otherwise = Sum [(w, learnRSPN cluster) | (w,cluster) <- kmeans tbl]
```

Relational extension: for foreign-key T -> S, add column F_S = fanout count (how many T per S). Estimate join via:

```
|Q1 ⋈ Q2| = |T1||T2|/|E| * sum_s P1(Q1,s) P2(Q2,s) * 1/F(s)
```

DeepDB achieves median Q-error 1.16 on single-table, 1.2 on binary joins [2], with model 10× smaller than Naru and inference 10× faster. It *satisfies all 4 logic properties* defined: consistency, monotonicity, stability, fidelity.

> Theorem 3 (RSPN Tractability): Marginal inference in RSPN is linear in model size O(|RSPN|) [2].

### 4.3 BayesCard: Revitalizing Bayesian Networks

BayesCard argues BN advantages: interpretability, fast training, tiny size [3]. Contributions:

- **Framework unification**: integrates 6 structure learning algorithms (Chow-Liu, Hill-Climbing, Tree-Augmented Naïve Bayes, Exact via DP) and 4 parameter estimators via PPLs.
- **Graph reduction**: for query Q, prune BN to ancestors of filtered attributes, reducing inference from O(n) to O(k) where k << n (e.g., CENSUS 68 → 8 nodes).
- **Fanout handling**: same as DeepDB but BN learns P(F) jointly, improving join accuracy.

Empirics: On DMV, BayesCard median Q-error 1.001 vs DeepDB 1.006, FLAT 1.001; on CENSUS (68 attributes, high correlation), BayesCard 1.063 vs Naru 1.229, DeepDB 1.469 [3]. Model size 100KB vs Naru 10MB. Training 10 sec vs 1 hour. Inference 2.1ms vs Naru 86ms.

```rust
// BayesCard PPL snippet (Pyro-style)
fn model(data: &Table) {
    let dag = chow_liu_tree(mutual_info(&data));
    for node in dag.topo() {
        let parents = dag.parents(node);
        let cpt = Dirichlet::new(alpha).sample();
        observe!(Categorical::new(cpt[parents]), data[node]);
    }
}
```

BayesCard deployed in PostgreSQL 9.6.6 improves end-to-end JOB-light time 13.3% vs 14.2% optimal with true cardinalities [3].

### 4.4 FactorJoin: Factor Graphs for Joins

FactorJoin [4] solves scalability problem: *do not learn joint over E*. Instead:

**Offline**: for each table T, learn conditional P_T(Attrs | JoinKeys) via BN or simple histogram, and P_T(JoinKeys). Store as 1-D or 2-D histograms (tiny).

**Online**: given join query Q touching tables T1...Tk with join keys J, construct factor graph:

- Variables: each join key j in J
- Factors: phi_i(Ji) = P_Ti(Ji) * |Ti| * Sel(Qi | Ji)
- Edge: if two tables share key, factor connects them.

Cardinality = sum_J prod_i phi_i(J) computed via variable elimination / bound propagation.

Complexity: O(k * d^2) where d = domain size of keys after binning (typically 100). vs NeuroCard O(n_samples * d) where n_samples=2k.

```tla
---- MODULE FactorJoin ----
EXTENDS Naturals, TLC
VARIABLES phi, keys, result
Init == phi \in [Keys -> Nat] /\ result = 0
Next == \E j \in keys: result' = result + Product(phi[j])
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ WF_vars(Next)
====
```

Empirics: FactorJoin achieves 40× lower latency (0.2ms vs 8ms), 100× smaller model (0.1MB vs 10MB), 100× faster training than NeuroCard/DeepDB, with comparable or better accuracy: P50 Q-error 1.1 on JOB-M vs NeuroCard 1.5 [4]. Can estimate 10k sub-plans/sec, close to Postgres histogram (12k/sec), enabling full optimizer integration.

*Key insight*: independence assumption *across* tables conditional on join keys is less harmful than AVI *within* tables. FactorJoin preserves intra-table correlations via single-table models, relaxes only inter-table via factorization.

FactorJoin dominates for join-heavy optimizers requiring 1k+ estimates per query.

### 5.2 Theoretical Complexity

> Theorem 4 (NeuroCard Sample Complexity): To estimate P(Q) within epsilon with probability 1-delta, progressive sampling requires n = O(epsilon^-2 log(1/delta) * |Dom|) samples [1].

Proof sketch: Hoeffding bound on bounded random variable prod hat_P_i.

> Theorem 5 (BN Inference via Graph Reduction): BayesCard's reduction yields exact marginal P(Q) with complexity O(exp(w')) where w' = induced width of reduced graph ≤ original width w, often w' << w for sparse filters [3].

> Theorem 6 (FactorJoin Bound): FactorJoin's upper bound hat_c >= c_true when using binned keys with max-frequency grouping, providing pessimistic estimate useful for avoiding catastrophic underestimation [4].

### 5.3 Incremental Maintenance

- NeuroCard: fine-tune MADE on new tuples, 10 sec for 1% insert
- DeepDB: update leaf histograms, retrain RDC only if drift > threshold
- BayesCard: PPL parameter update via variational Bayes, structure unchanged unless schema changes [3]
- FactorJoin: update single-table histograms, no retraining needed for join graph

## 6. Limitations

### 6.1 Assumptions Still Present

- **Acyclic schema assumption**: All four assume foreign-key graph is acyclic or tree-decomposable. Cyclic joins (e.g., self-joins) require duplication trick [1].
- **Categorical handling**: High-cardinality strings hashed to ints lose order semantics for range predicates.
- **Correlation across tables**: FactorJoin assumes Ti ⊥ Tj | J which fails when non-key attributes correlate across tables (e.g., movie.genre correlates with actor.age via hidden confounder). NeuroCard captures this but at cost.
- **Dynamic data**: None provide rigorous guarantees under arbitrary inserts/deletes; empirical robustness drops 2× after 20% churn without retrain [6].
- **Uncertainty**: Only BayesCard and NNGP variant provide posterior variance; optimizer cannot distinguish confident vs uncertain estimates.

### 6.2 Deployment Hurdles

1. **Integration**: Postgres hook get_relation_info expects selectivity in [0,1]; learned models returning probability >1 due to fanout scaling cause assert failures.
2. **Memory**: NeuroCard 10MB per DB acceptable, but 100 DBs × 10MB = 1GB on coordinator.
3. **Training data**: Join sampler requires primary-key indexes on all FKs; missing indexes force full scan.
4. **Interpretability**: Deep autoregressive black-box; DBAs cannot debug why plan changed. BN's DAG visual helps [3].

### 6.3 Open Problems

- *Cross-DB pretraining* (PRICE): can we pretrain one model for all DBs? Early results show 30% transfer.
- *End-to-end optimizer learning*: Balsa, Lero integrate CardEst with plan search via RL; CardEst error not independent of cost model error.
- *Worst-case bounds*: FactorJoin provides upper bound, but lower bound still open. Degree-sequence bound gives pessimistic bound but loose.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a PGM-centric survey of learned cardinality estimation, dissecting NeuroCard, DeepDB, BayesCard, and FactorJoin. Key takeaways:

- **No free lunch**: One model for all tables (NeuroCard) maximizes correlation capture but maximizes cost; single-table + factor graph (FactorJoin) minimizes cost with slight accuracy loss.
- **BNs are underrated**: BayesCard matches or beats deep models on single-table and moderate joins with 100× less cost and full interpretability [3].
- **SPNs are tractable**: DeepDB and FLAT show sum-product factorization is ideal for range queries due to linear inference [2][5].
- **Joins via factor graphs**: FactorJoin's late binding of join inference is the most deployable for commercial optimizers requiring 10k estimates/sec [4].

Future direction is *unified neuro-symbolic PGM*: use neural networks to parameterize factors in BN/RSPN, trained via PPLs, with factor-graph join inference. Such hybrid could achieve NeuroCard accuracy with FactorJoin latency.

For practitioners: start with BayesCard for single-table, FactorJoin for joins; fall back to NeuroCard if workload is highly correlated across many tables and latency budget allows.
