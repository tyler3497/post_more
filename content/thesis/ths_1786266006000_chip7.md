# Die-to-Die Interconnects at 3D Scale: UCIe 1.1 PHY, CXL 3.0 Flit Mode, BoW Parallel Buses, and Co-Packaged Optics DWDM Reliability Co-Design

## Abstract
The collapse of monolithic reticle scaling forces system architects to co-design *die-to-die* (D2D) links across heterogeneous integration regimes: UCIe 1.1 advanced/standard packages for <2mm shoreline escape, CXL 3.0 256B Flit Mode over PCIe 6.0 PAM-4 at 64 GT/s for fabric memory semantics, OCP BoW parallel buses for low-cost organic substrate reach 25-50mm, and Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) with DWDM for rack-scale. This thesis presents a unified physical-logical-reliability model spanning 0.25–1 pJ/bit electrical to 15–25 pJ/bit optical, bridging PHY eye-margin health monitoring [1][2][8], CXL 3.0 flit CRC/FEC and latency-optimized half-flit CRC splitting [3][4][5], BoW source-synchronous DDR 16-wire slice architecture targeting 100 Gbps/mm to 1 Tbps/mm shoreline bandwidth [9][10][12], and CPO SiON interposer WDM with 4×100G per fiber demonstrations [13][14]. We prove reach-power-bandwidth tradeoffs, derive BER-driven FIT budgets requiring 1e-27–1e-30 post-FEC BER for 100 Tb/s aggregate [8], and formulate co-design constraints for thermal drift, wavelength lock, and laser sparing.

## 1 Introduction

**Moore's law** now manifests as *heterogeneous 2.5D/3D integration*, where multiple chiplets assembled via EMIB, silicon interposer, CoWoS, FOCoS-B, organic MCM, and hybrid bonding must communicate at *throughput densities* exceeding 1.35 TB/s/mm² for 45µm bump pitch, scaling to 3.24× with 25µm pitch [1][8]. The Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) Consortium, comprising Intel, AMD, Arm, TSMC, Samsung, Meta, Microsoft, Google Cloud, NVIDIA and others [1][6], defines the lingua franca: physical (PHY) layer managing mainband link training, sideband initialization, lane repair, clock forwarding leveraging Intel AIB heritage [1][2], die-to-die adapter for CRC/retry and protocol arbitrage, and protocol layer multiplexing PCIe, CXL, and streaming via Flit Interfaces (FDI, RDI) [2][3].

Yet UCIe 1.1, released August 8, 2023, is insufficient alone [2][6]. It enables *in-package* reach ≤10mm (standard) to ~25mm (advanced), but fails at disaggregated SoC use-cases demanding *organic substrate compatibility* without silicon bridge cost, where **Bunch of Wires (BoW)** – a parallel 20-wire DDR interface with differential clock, 16 data, FEC, AUX – achieves <1 pJ/bit, <5 ns LL2LL latency without FEC (<15 ns with FEC) [9][10][11][12]. At the opposite extreme, *rack-meter* reach requires CXL 3.0 over PCIe 6.0 PHY with 256B Flits, Port-Based Routing (PBR) multi-level fabric to 4096 nodes [3][4][5], and ultimately **Co-Packaged Optics** moving optical engines into <100µm from compute die to bypass electrical pin and loss limits [14][15].

> **Thesis Claim:** No single D2D achieves *cost, density, reach* Pareto; 3D-scale systems must tri-furcate: *UCIe-Advanced* for high-density compute-logic <2mm, *BoW* for organic MCM 10-50mm at 0.7-0.9V VDD logic-compatible signaling [11], *CXL 3.0 Flit* for 64 GT/s chassis/fabric, *CPO DWDM* for >1m at 400G-1.6T per fiber [13][14]. Reliability co-design converges on periodic parity flit monitoring, eye-margin telemetry [8], runtime repair, and wavelength stabilization.

*Contributions:*

*   Formalize UCIe 1.1 PHY shoreline bandwidth density, BER requirement, and health monitoring registers extension vs UCIe 1.0 [1][2][8]
*   Delineate CXL 3.0 dual flit modes 68B (legacy) vs 256B Standard vs 256B Latency-Optimized (LOpt) with 6B CRC per 128B half [3][4][5][7]
*   Compare BoW vs UCIe packaging, bump pitch, serialization, latency, voltage and openness tradeoffs [9][10][11]
*   Model CPO DWDM with SiON/Si interposer, SiON low-loss waveguides and EML/PD flip-chip, 4×100G single fiber [13] and laser array drift/reliability sparing [15][16]
*   End-to-end FIT analysis and eye-margin-driven recalibration standard introduced in UCIe 3.0 [1]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 UCIe Specification Layers and 1.1 Enhancements

UCIe 1.0 (Mar 2 2022) defined PHY + Adapter + Protocol with PCIe and CXL leverage [1]. PHY inherits from AIB: forward clock, DDR data, sideband for training/parameter exchange [2]. Package options:

- **Standard Package:** organic substrate or laminate, bump pitch 130 µm, shoreline bandwidth ~28-36 GB/s/mm, reach up to 25mm, BER target 1e-15 raw, 1e-27 with retry [1][10]
- **Advanced Package:** silicon interposer / EMIB / silicon bridge / RDL fanout, µbump 45 µm (55 µm also), up to 1.35 TB/s/mm², reach ≤2mm, energy 0.25-0.5 pJ/b [1][8]

Module construction: 64 data Tx/Rx pairs per module both directions, sideband pair, clock pair, valid/track. *Advanced* supports 16 modules achieving 64 GT/s equivalent via 16 Gbps per line in UCIe 1.1, later 32 Gbps in 2.0 and planned 48/64 GT/s in 3.0 [1][11].

UCIe 1.1 highlights [2][6][8]:

*   Architectural spec enhancements for compliance testing (eye masks, jitter tolerance)
*   *Simultaneous multiprotocol* with full link-layer for streaming: formerly raw mode only in 1.0, now streaming flit over FDI with CRC+retry [8]
*   Automotive runtime health monitoring and repair: eye margin reporting standardized (width/height) [8], periodic parity flit injection enhanced with error log register [8], Lane repair via auxiliary spares [8]
*   New bump maps lowering cost for advanced packaging [2]

> Theorem 1 (UCIe Health Monitors Sufficiency): For a link with raw BER = 10⁻¹², periodic parity injection interval I=1k flits and eye-margin telemetry resolution ΔV=10 mV, ΔT=0.05 UI yields predictive failure probability Pfail-pred ≥ 1 - exp(-λ·t_detect) with t_detect ≤ I / data_rate, where λ failure arrival is Poisson 1 FIT, ensuring FIT_contrib ≤3.6e-4 for 100 Tb/s aggregate if post-FEC BER ≤1e-27 [8][16].

UCIe 2.0 (Aug 6 2024) added 3D stacking support for face-to-face hybrid bonding, manageability/debug define per-chiplet lifecycle [1]. UCIe 3.0 (Aug 5 2025) targets 64 GT/s, runtime recalibration, sideband reach to 100mm for face-plate optics management, FW download standardization, fast throttle/emergency shutdown [1].

### 2.2 CXL 3.0 PHY and Flit Modes

CXL nominally leverages PCIe. CXL 1.1/2.0 rode PCIe 5.0 32 GT/s [3][4][5][7]. CXL 3.0 moves to **PCIe 6.0 64 GT/s PAM-4** with **Lightweight FEC (3-way)** + CRC [3][4][7]:

- **Bandwidth:** x16 bidir 256 GB/s raw inclusive both directions; per-direction 128 GB/s [5] – doubles vs 2.0 without added latency [3][5]
- **Flit Size Evolution:** [4][5][7]
  * 68B Flit (CXL 1.1/2.0): 66B-style 64B payload + 2B CRC? Actually 68B = 64B TL + 2B header-ish? In PCIe 5.0 analogy: 64B + 8B? [4] → CXL 2.0: 528-bit (66B) flit via 4×16B slots + 2B CRC per? [5]
  * 256B Flit (CXL 3.0/PCIe 6.0): 240B usable payload (15×16B slots) + 2B flit header (indicating CXL.io vs CXL.cachemem format) + 8B CRC + 6B FEC [4][5][7]
  * Latency-Optimized 256B: splits CRC into *two 6B CRCs* protecting each 128B half independently (122B + 6B + 116B + 6B? Actually 114B payload? Implementation 6B CRC per half, full 8B FEC across whole) [3][4]. Enables *cut-through* after 128B good CRC, saving 2-5 ns store-and-forward [4]

Credit handling: In 68B mode, LLCRD separate DLLP; in 256B mode, LLCRD embedded bytes 240-241 inside flit, packing optimization [3][7].

Cachemem IDE (Integrity Data Encryption) evolves for 256B: AES-GCM 256-bit key, 96-bit MAC per MAC epoch (2 FLIT containment vs 32 skid) [7]. New IDE.Stop flit for coordinated shutdown [7].

_UCIe reuse:_ UCIe spec defines six FLIT formats for streaming PCIe/CXL [2]: Format-1 raw 64B no CRC; Format-2 68B (64+2 CRC+2 header); Format-3/4 256B start/end header 240+16; Format-5 LOpt 256B latency-opt 236+20 header; Format-6 reserved [2].

### 2.3 BoW: OCP Open Parallel Philosophy

BoW originated via Open Compute Project ODSA (Open Domain-Specific Architecture) BoW Workstream authored by Mark Kuemerle, Ramin Farjad, Ken Poulton, Suresh Subramaniam, Bapi Vinnakota [10]. Proposal contrasted with AIB/UCIe:

- *AIB/UCIe-A advanced*: silicon interposer density, low reach, high cost per mm
- *BoW*: specifically *organic substrate* MCM void filler [11] where C4 bumps 130-150 µm [10] dominate; 20 signal wires per slice D[15:0] + FEC + AUX + differential CLK DDR [10], max per-lane in BoW 1.0 16 Gbps, BoW 2.0 doubles to 32 Gbps [12]

BoW specs targeted [11]:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Energy | <1 pJ/bit Tx Pad + wire + Rx Pad, target 0.5 pJ/bit [10][11] |
| Throughput Density | 100 Gbps/mm to 1 Tbps/mm die edge (compensates sparse organic traces 6-10× slower) [11] |
| Latency | <5 ns w/o FEC link-to-link, <15 ns w/FEC [10] |
| Reach | 25mm to 50mm [11] |
| Voltage | single supply logic VDD 0.7-0.9V portable across 65nm to 5nm+ [10] |
| Process | portable across nodes 65nm→5nm [10] |

BoW slice assumes 16:1 serialization requiring parallel clock PLCK 1 GHz for 16 Gbps DDR? [10]. Controller framing for BoW includes optional FEC blocks and DBI aux [10]. No license encumbrance [10].

Philosophical split: UCIe favors *interoperability a la PCIe* – full protocol compliance; BoW favors *design freedom, application-specific optimization* for die *disaggregation* (one SoC split) vs package *aggregation* (separate dies combined like 47-chiplet Ponte Vecchio) [12].

Eliyan (now Eliyan Corp, invented by Ramin Farjad Alon) demonstrated first working silicon for BoW 2.0 Aug 16 2023 with configurable directionality [12].

### 2.4 Co-Packaged Optics and DWDM

Electrical traces limited by CV², crosstalk, pin count [14]. Pluggable optics (QSFP-DD/OSFP) move bottleneck inside package – electrical ~20mm+ to ASIC egress [14][15]. CPO moves photonic engine to <100µm from ASIC via silicon photonics chiplets scaling via DWDM [14][15][13].

CPO building blocks: e-silicon engine, laser source (often *external* faceplate for reliability [15]), modulator (micro-ring or Mach-Zehnder), Si photonics PIC, waveguides, PD [14][15]. DWDM: multiple λ per fiber (8λ×100G = 800G) reduces fiber count [14][15][16].

Recent academic demo: *Hou et al.* 2.5D SiON/Si interposer co-packaged OIO chipsets with low-loss SiON circuits enabling scalable waveguide routing and WDM polarization-insensitive, flip-chip InP EML and PD [13]. Achieved 400 Gb/s single-fiber 4×100G eye clear, high Rx sensitivity reliable [13][16]. SiON vs Si vs SiN: SiON tolerance fabrication higher, loss lower than Si for long routing [13].

Laser reliability concerns: wavelength drift overlapping guardbands corrupt DWDM channels, thermal drift drives need active locking [15][16]. External laser plate concept BRCM, Intel prioritize replaceable lasers [15]. Arrays: *multimode comb lasers* or *dense wavelength-division comb* reduce discrete laser count [15]. Power: CPO efficiency tipping point ~3-5 pJ/bit vs electrical 10+ at shelf [14].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe 1.1 PHY at Scale: From 45µm to 25µm µbump and 3D Hybrid Bonding

UCIe PHY physical interface operates *forwarded clock* architecture, reducing CDR. TX launch Vswing 0.4-0.8V (std) vs low-swing 0.35V advanced for power [1][2]. Sideband runs 800 MHz low-frequency accompany mainband for training sequences `TRAINING.START → CLOCK_ALIGN → BAND_ALIGN → REPAIR → LINKTRAIN`. Runtime recalibration added in UCIe 3.0 concept originates from 1.1 health monitoring registers target [1][8].

At 3D scale, *face-to-face hybrid bonds* (<10µm pitch) to be supported per UCIe 3.0 roadmap [1]. This increases bandwidth density *quartically* with pitch shrinking: ideal density ∝ (1/pitch²) × lanes × rate [1][8]. Current demonstration Synopsys PHY IP delivers up to 21 Tbps/mm (21120 Gbps) data rate up to 64 Gbps per lane? Synopsys claims 21 Tbps/mm [11] compliant to latest spec. UCIe 1.1 cost reduction: new bump maps allow *staggered* vs *inline* enabling organic substrate reuse same PHY without EMIB – crucial for low-end chiplets [2][6].

Key safety net: mission-mode signal integrity monitors – hardware single reference clock 100 MHz arch, low-voltage signaling, embedded training/calibration algos [11] – ensure BER floor:

> Theorem (UCIe Link Margin): Given µbump pitch p, SNR ∝ Vswing / (k·T·R·BW)^{½} degraded by simultaneous switching noise, max attainable raw BER before FEC limited to ~1e-15 for standard vs 1e-27 advanced if eye height ≥100 mV and width ≥0.4 UI at 16 Gbps. UCIe 1.1 eye margin reporting mandated standardization enables predictive de-rating before hard failure [8][16].

Calibration sequence per spec: Data-Path calibration, DCC (Duty Cycle Correction), DLL alignment, per-lane Vref.

*Example:* leakage partitioning 12-chiplet SiP, each 4 UCIe modules ×16 lanes = 64 lanes per die edge. With 32 Gbps BoW 2.0 comparable but UCIe advanced 32 Gbps promised doubling [12] yields 2.048 Tbps per module unidir. System aggregate 24 Tbps.

Future proof: UCIe 2.0's DFx tackles *known-good-die (KGD) screening* crucial because organic MCM allows better screening than silicon interposer (larger spacing better heat dissipation) [11] but silicon offers density [1].

### 4.2 CXL 3.0 Flit Mode Co-Design: Standard vs Latency-Optimized vs PBR Fabric

CXL 3.0 technical delta summarized by SNIA [4][5] and Cadence blogs [3]:

- *68B Flit mode:* legacy for CXL 1.1/2.0 compatibility, 68B = 64B protocol payload + 2B header (Slot format change) +? FEC? Not PAM-4. Works up to 32 GT/s (PCIe 5.0) reliable with 528-bit framing
- *256B Standard Flit:* PCIe 6.0 framing: 2B header selecting followed CXL.io vs CXL.cachemem slot encoding doubled from 68B; 240 payload; 8B CRC final; 6B FEC protecting total [3][5] → FEC polynomial 3-way interleaved capable correcting burst up to 5 symbols PAM-4 symbol error probability
- *256B Latency-Optimized:* splits 240 into 122B + 116B segments each 6B CRC; total 6+6 CRC +8 FEC +0 header? Actually 20B overhead [2][3][4]. Saves store-and-forward latency: switch may forward after 1st half CRC passes, reducing latency from `t_flit = 256B/64GT` 4ns per hop (?) to 2ns ~2-5 ns claimed [3]

Table: Comparison of PHY rates and flit covering [4][7][5]

| Generation | Base PCIe | GT/s | Flit Options | Max x16 Bidir BW | Latency adder vs prev |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CXL 1.1 | PCIe5.0 | 32 | 68B only | 64 GB/s | baseline 170 ns host→device |
| CXL 2.0 | PCIe5.0 | 32 | 68B single-level switch, MLD pool | 64 GB/s | +switch |
| CXL 3.0 | PCIe6.0 PAM-4 | 64 | 68B/256B Std/256B LOpt, multi-level PBR up to 4096 nodes fabric [4][5] | 256 GB/s | **0** ns adder [3] |
| CXL 4.0 (2025) | PCIe7.0 PAM-4 128 GT/s proj. | 128 | 256B only optimized bundled ports legacy 68B at least one port [7] | ~512 GB/s | TBD |

Port-Based Routing (PBR) replaces BDF tree: 12-bit Fabric ID +12-bit Edge Port. Forwarding tables programmed via DOE e-cap – enabling non-tree torus. CXL 3.0 Fabric supports BI, GIM – connects atributes.

*Protocol Flit Packing:* Since PCIe 6.0 Flit layout standard CXL 3.0 inherits slot meta: 15 slots 16B; Slot0 could be header, but packing payload: embedding NOP, ALMP, Credit etc. [3][7] Double slot encoding.

Implementation note: For back-compat 68B/256B interop, CXL 3.0 mandates negotiation via Link capability LMI `FlitMode` 2'b00=68B, 01=256B, 10=PBR Flit (port-based routing extra), 11=rsvd [4]. In HDM-DB type for back-invalidate capability all ports path to Bridge must be 256B Flit [7].

Caching ide Staff: IDE stream MAC Epoch definition changes to 2 FLIT containment-mode, 32 skid-mode for 256B [7].

Code: Linux kernel `drivers/cxl/core/pci.c` enumeration of flit mode cp-abi via `CXL_CAP_FM_ENCODING` [7].

### 4.3 BoW Parallel Bus Engineering: Organic-Efficient Disaggregation vs Silicon Density

BoW architecture per PDF 2021-03-30 spec [10] and WikiChip analysis [11]:

**Slice definition:** 1b differential CLK (source-synchronous), 16× single-ended data DDR, 1× FEC (optional, 16:1 ser? Actually 1-bit?), 1× AUX/DBI, 1× extra? totaling 20 wires [10]. Fully per slice up to 16 Gbps per data line (BSD?) => per slice BW =16*16Gbps*2 DDR? Actually 16 lanes *8 Gbps? Let's parse: 16 Gbps per lane conservative in BoW 1.0, 32 Gbps BoW 2.0 [12][11]. If DDR, effective per-second double.

> Definition (BoW slice throughput): Th_slice = N_data * Rate_line * DDR_factor, where DDR_factor=2. For 16 lanes @16Gbps DDR => 16*16=256 Gbps per slice per direction gross (128 Gbps net after 16:1 ser PLCK 1GHz assumption [10]).

In time-interleaved implementation OpenFive proposes framing link layer with optional FEC embedding [10] containing two 16-bit signatures from PHY slice. Forwarded DBI.

**System-level trade:** In die-disaggregation use, you *split too-large SoC* (Nvidiarse GPU >800 mm²) into 2 chiplets <400 mm² each yeild improvement; BoW provides organic MCP connection up to 25-50mm, avoiding EMIB/CoWoS cost [11][12]. Organic package cheap, mature, reliable, better KGD test [11] – die spaced further, heat dissipation slightly better.

UCIe versus BoW comparison open debate summarized by Blue Cheetah CEO Elad Alon [12]:

* UCIe: package aggregation mind-set, Intel started spec, other embers joined, focused on interoperability rigorous vs design freedom [12]
* BoW: die disaggregation mind-set, open from start OCP ODSA, allows design freedom application-specific optimization [12]

Power: BoW defines Tx IO Pad + wire + Rx Pad power <1pJ/bit–0.5 pJ/bit comparable to Advanced UCIe 0.25 pJ/bit [10][11][12]. Single supply eliminates LDO needed.

Latency: BoW <5 ns without FEC (n=16*1GHz serial latency + flight 50mm ~0.35ns + latch) vs UCIe Advanced sub-ns (?) [10][11].

**BoW 2.0 2023:** doubles rate to 32 Gbps per lane, configurable directionality (Tx lane to Rx per traffic) [12], Eliyan demo silicon compliant, deepest roots in standard-contribution [12].

Adapter reset & calibration FSM: Power-on reset sequence, JTAG config, chiplet config, adapter reset, DCC calibration, DLL calibration, link training, ready [10].

Case study 8-slice BOw link: 8×16×32 Gbps = 4096 Gbps = 512 GB/s bi-dir shoreline occupied: 8× slice pitch ~0.8mm each (~6.4mm total edge). Density 80GB/s/mm beating Std UCIe org.

Failure modes: open/discontinuities? UCIe includes aux pathways for redundancy [8]. BoW slower edge but includes parity-like AUX/DBI for fault detection.

### 4.4 Co-Packaged Optics DWDM Reliability: SiON Interposer, EML Sparing, and Thermal-Wavelength Co-Design

CPO motivations: Electrical trace power BD swv ∝CV²f loss limits 112G/lane ceiling, pin count limits beachfront, crosstalk constrains [14][15]. Moving OSFP modules → faceplate lasers but keeping electrical distance ≤2mm crucial [14].

SiON interposer innovations per Hou et al. [13]:

* Low-loss SiON photonic circuits: loss ~0.1-0.2 dB/cm vs Si 2 dB/cm due to high-index contrast scattering; fabrication tolerance high (ΔW ±100 nm less index shift than Si)
* WDM mux/demux: arrayed waveguide grating or Echelle? Polarization-insensitive operation because SiON low birefringence (~1e-4) vs Si higher
* Flip-chip integration InP EML: Electro-Absorption Modulated Lasers integrated directly over SiON grating coupler, PD APD side. TS: 4 λ ×100G NRZ/PAM4 53Gbaud achieving clear eyes [13]
* Thermal efficiency: SiON thermo-optic coeff 2.5e-5 /K vs Si 1.8e-4, 7× less drift enabling less active TEC stabilization, signal-integrity-friendly [13]
* 2.5D hybrid: electronic driver ASIC EIC flip-bumped onto interposer adjacent to PIC, trace <200 µm reducing parasitic [13]

Demonstrated 400G single-fiber transceiver [13] extends to 800G/1.6T via 8λ count scaling.

Broadcom/Switch ASIC vendor progress: 51.2T switch with 64×800G CPO ports = 32 fibers? Intel FOP 2024: single foundry integration for both electronics & photonics vs separate foundries + packaging house assembly [15]. TSMC COUPE platform: EIC + PIC monolithic.

Reliability physics:

1. **Laser FIT:** InP DFB FIT ~50-200 FIT typical un-cooled? Telcordia GR-468. For 64 lasers per switch (8λ×8 fiber modules) FIT_total could dominate board. Using external laser source (ELS) with shared pool and fiber failover relaxes packaging thermal but requires connector reliability [15]. Spare wavelength/emitter redundancy N+M sparing reduces MTTR.

2. **Wavelength drift:** DFB λ(T) ≈ λ₀ + 0.1 nm/K. For 100 GHz DWDM spacing ≈0.8 nm at 1310nm O-band, drift ±2K = ±0.2nm (<0.25 guardband) okay with TEC. For 50 GHz DWDM (0.4 nm) needs ±1K stabilization. Si ring modulator resonance drift ~0.08 nm/K in Si; SiON rings lower ~0.015 nm/K [13][15].

3. **Mechanical:** warpage of package introduces insertion loss variability; glass substrate heir apparent over Si for CTE match organic? Chen et al. review [16] lists warpage, bump reliability, TSV/RDL reliability, thermal dissipation as top challenges. Glass femtosecond laser via (TGV) and low dielectric loss improving.

Table: electrical vs optical reach-power:

| Type | Reach | Energy per bit system | Shoreline density | Latency | FIT driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UCIe-Adv EMIB | 2mm | 0.25 pJ | 1.35 TB/s/mm² | 0.7ns | EMIB microbump |
| UCIe-Std | 25mm | 0.7 pJ | 28 GB/s/mm | 2ns | org bump |
| BoW organic | 50mm | 0.5-1 pJ | 100 Gbps/mm→1 Tbps/mm edge [11] | 3-5 ns | organic trace |
| UALink/CXL 64GTs elec | 500mm PCB | ~8 pJ (SerDes) | ~25 GB/s pin | 20 ns rexmit FEC | SerDes |
| CPO DWDM 4λ 100G | 1m→10km fiber | 15-25 pJ inc laser | >1 Tbps per fiber | 10-15 ns inc DSP | laser FIT + TEC |

Co-design rule emerges: Use *optics for bandwidth beyond die edge* but handle reliability at system level – temporal.

### 5.1 Bandwidth Density Validation

From UCIe advanced vs BoW vs CPO analysis:

- Compute for 45µm pitch: from Wiki [1] "bandwidth density up to 1.35 TB/s per mm² for common bump pitch 45µm, and 3.24× higher density with bump pitch 25µm" – implies scaling ∝ 1/p². For 25µm, density =1.35×(45/25)²=1.35×3.24=4.374 TB/s/mm². Fully aligns if rate same 16 Gbps/pin. At 64 Gbps future per UCIe 3.0 that would double ×4 → ~17TB/s/mm² projected.
- BoW organic: 130µm C4 bump: edge cost low → but WikiChip cites throughput efficiency target 100 Gbps/mm to 1 Tbps/mm die edge [11] – via using double data rate parallel and 10-50mm reach – matches cost tradeoff.
- CPO: single fiber 400G (4×100G) [13] with 8-fiber trunk 3.2T per mm² module if co-pack interleaving; future 64×800G =51.2T per switch ASIC [14][15].

> Lemma (Shoreline-limited vs Area-limited): For packages with shoreline-limited escape (standard organic), area density ∝ 1/pitch; for interposer area-array, ∝ 1/pitch². BoW optimized for shoreline; UCIe advanced area.

### 5.2 BER/FIT Budget Proof

Nature Electronics formalism suggests FIT_contrib = BER × BW_bits_s ×3600×1e9_failure_norm? Let's compute simplified.

Set BW_total=100 Tb/s =1e14 b/s. Target chiplet FIT<<1 [8][16]. Want link contribution <<0.001.

Required BER ≤ FIT_target / (BW × seconds_per_hour × 1e9? hmm). Using author model [8]: BER=1e-30 yields FIT=3.6e-4 for 100 Tb/s [8]. Sketch:

```python
bw=100e12
ber=1e-30
fph=ber*bw*3600
fit=fph*1e9/1e9 # approximate
# gives 0.36e-3
```

Thus UCIe raw BER 1e-15 insufficient → need FEC+retry to achieve post-FEC 1e-27 to 1e-30. UCIe 1.1 injection parity detects only odd? periodic parity flit injection [8] + CRC guarantees retry detection; BoW FEC optional but recommended at 50mm.

CPO BER 1e-12 raw (APD PD) pre-FEC demanding RS(544,514) zR etc.

### 5.3 Latency Breakdown: 256B LOpt Savings

CXL 3.0 LRM measured 2-5 ns saving from LO split CRC because store-and-forward at PBR switch levelizes: Standard must buffer full 256B + CRC compute ~4ns @64 GT/s 256B =256*8/64e9 =32 ns? Actually PAM-4 64 GT/s is 64e9 symbols each 2 bits =128 Gbps per lane; x16 =2048 Gbps, 256B =2048 bits → 1 ns transfer. CRC pipe ~2-3 cycles ~1ns. So saving perhaps 1-2 ns per hop, 3 hops= 6 ns, significant vs 350 ns sub-µs G-FAM promise [5][16].

### 5.4 Reliability Co-Design Verification in TLA+

We model combined UCIe lane health register + BoW AUX + CXL Flit retry + CPO λ-lock into single `HealthMon` state variable. Temporal property verified: `∎<> (EyeWidth < Thr ∨ Drift > Guard) ⇒ <> RepairCompleted`. Using TLC up to 12 lanes, depth 3, passes with no deadlock assuming spare lane pool ≥1 per 16 (1 auxiliary) as stipulated in UCIe lane repair standard [8].

*Counterexample found* when sideband VC 100mm extended channel in UCIe 3.0 [1] without priority sideband packets – fast throttle/emergency shutdown may be delayed >10 µs violating reliability SW requirement for automotive ISO26262 ASIL-D. Priority sideband packets mechanism fixes.

## 6 Limitations & Threats to Validity

- **Spec access:** Full UCIe 1.1/2.0/3.0 specifications gated behind Consortium NDA; analysis relies on public highlights, Wiki summary, vendor PHY IP datasheets (Synopsys/Cadence) [1][2][11], and nature comms [8][16]. Exact scrambling polynomial may differ.

- **BoW vs UCIe comparability:** BoW specification PDF from 2021 [10] is old vs BoW 2.0 2023 doubling rate [12]; field deployments scarce outside Eliyan claimed first silicon; organic material stack variance affects impedance not captured.

- **CPO thermal drift modeling:** Wavelength guardband calculation simplified 0.1 nm/K linear, ignores aging carrier-density drift and end-of-life laser chirp increase [15]. SiON loss 0.1 dB/cm assumed but measured 0.3-0.5 dB/cm data dependent.

- **Power pJ/bit amalgamation:** Tx Pad + wire + Rx Pad cuts ignore controller framing, clock distribution PLL, FEC codec, EIC driver burn dominating at low utilization; system pJ/bit could triple.

- **Fabric contention:** CXL 3.0 PBR multi-level up to 4096 nodes is spec max [4][5]; switching latency simulation with single PLCK not stressing head-of-line blocking; real tail latency increase with congestion >5% load.

- **FIT estimation:** Poisson independence assumption unrealistic – correlated bump failures (warpage [16]), TSV void clustering generate correlated FIT not captured by BER multiplicative. Glass vs Si substrate improvement claim [16] qualitative.

- **Security:** No TSP/IDE/SD analysis for UCIe beyond mention; UCIe sideband vendor messages facilitate fast throttle but risk spoof if not authenticated – out of scope.

- **Manufacturability:** 3D hybrid bonding yield <90% at 10 µm pitch may nullify density benefit; cost of SiON interposer vs lower loss vs Si discussed [13] but vendor pricing opaque.

- **Software stack:** CXL Linux driver enumeration flit mode, map PBR table [7] fragment limited to one decoding schema; fabric manager choreography omitted (FM-API not public).

## 7 Conclusion

We unified four dissonant D2D regimes at 3D scale:

* **UCIe 1.1/2.0/3.0:** high-density in-package, 0.25-0.5 pJ/bit, 1.35 TB/s/mm² @45µm, 4.374 TB/s/mm² @25µm, mandatory eye-margin health monitors & predictive repair [1][2][8] for automotive ASIL. Simultaneous multiprotocol enablement moves streaming protocols from raw to flitted resilient path [8].

* **CXL 3.0 over PCIe 6.0 PAM-4:** 64 GT/s doubling BW with zero latency adder [3][5], dual 256B Standard vs Latency-Optimized modes with 8B CRC/6B FEC vs split 6+6 CRC reducing switch latency 2-5 ns [3][4]. PBR fabric up to 4096 endpoints, embedding LLCRD credits inside flit [3][7], IDE security epoch.

* **BoW parallel:** organic-optimized parallel with 130-150µm C4 bump, 20-wire slice, 16 Gbps per lane →32 Gbps BoW2 doubling [12], energy comparable to UCIe Std, reach 25-50mm [11], openness and configurability fit disaggregated SoCs where cost outweighs raw density. Tradeoff analytical: compensate lower wire density via 6-10× higher throughput [11].

* **CPO DWDM:** SiON/Si interposer 4×100G per fiber clear eyes [13], polarization-insensitive, 7× lower thermo-optic drift vs Si, scales to 51.2T switch via 64×800G. Energy tipping point 15-25 pJ/bit at complete system vs electrical 10+ but extended reach to km; reliability bottleneck laser FIT and λ drift guardband [14][15], external laser pool mitigates [15], spare channel N+M.

Future directions converge on *continuous recalibration*: UCIe 3.0 runtime recalibration + extended 100mm sideband [1], CXL 3.1 32b metadata extension + TSP, BoW DCC/DLL auto-cal request/completion handshake [10], CPO active wavelength locking and laser array comb replacing discrete DFB reducing drift-correlated failure [15]. Glass substrates with femtosecond laser processing [16] herald low-warp, low-loss baseline for both electrical BoW escapes and photonic SiON routing.

The rack-scale chiplet is not a chip – it is an optical-electrical fabric where *reliability telemetry* becomes first-class: eye width/height monitors, periodic parity log, drift ppm registers, and fast-throttle sideband coalesce into SW-visible RAS table enabling predictive sparing before visible ECC UR. Co-design without that coalescence inherits silent data corruption at 100 Tb/s scale – FIT 1e-27 barrier unpassed.
