# Gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell Dynamics of Magnetized Plasma Turbulence: Theory, δf Numerics, and Turbulent Transport Prediction for Tokamaks

## Abstract

Gyrokinetics is the first-principles framework that makes magnetized plasma microturbulence tractable: *gyroaveraging* over fast cyclotron motion reduces the 6D Vlasov–Maxwell system to 5D, discarding frequencies above Ωᵢ while retaining the finite-Larmor-radius, drift-wave, and resonance physics driving anomalous tokamak transport. This thesis develops the nonlinear gyrokinetic theory from its Hamiltonian foundations [1, 2], derives the δf splitting behind modern codes, and surveys the ITG, TEM, ETG, KBM, and MTM instability classes with their saturation mechanisms — zonal-flow regulation and the Dimits shift. We contrast the Eulerian (GENE [3], CGYRO [4]), particle-in-cell (XGC [6]), and full-*f* spectral (GENE-X [10]) algorithms in flux-tube and global geometries, present multi-channel validation against ASDEX Upgrade, DIII-D, and Alcator C-Mod [5, 7, 8], and close with the framework's limitations: edge ordering breakdown, electromagnetic incompleteness, profile stiffness, and the exascale cost challenge [9, 10].

## 1 Introduction

Anomalous transport in magnetically confined fusion plasmas is one of the grand challenges of contemporary plasma physics. Classical collisional theory — neoclassical transport, derived from the drift-kinetic equation — predicts heat diffusivities one to two orders of magnitude below observed levels in tokamaks. The deficit is made up by *microturbulence*: self-organized, low-frequency (ω ≪ Ωᵢ, with Ωᵢ the ion gyrofrequency), small-scale (k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 1, with ρᵢ the ion gyroradius) fluctuations of the electric and magnetic fields that scatter charged particles radially at rates far exceeding Coulomb collisions. Predicting this turbulence *ab initio* is essential for the design and operation of burning-plasma devices such as ITER and SPARC, whose performance margins hinge on the turbulent energy confinement time.

The obstacle is **scale separation**: six decades separate the Debye length from the device size, and the electron plasma period (∼10⁻¹¹ s) from the confinement time (∼1 s). Direct 6D Vlasov–Maxwell integration is hopeless. The gyrokinetic ordering of Frieman and Chen [1] exploits strong magnetization: since Ωₛ exceeds all frequencies of interest and ρₛ ≪ L_eq, the gyromotion is *averaged out* analytically, trading the gyrophase for the adiabatic invariant μ.

The resulting **nonlinear gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell equations** [2], solved by the GENE [3], CGYRO [4], GS2, ORB5, and XGC [6] code families, have matured from linear analysis to quantitative *multi-channel* flux prediction validated against experiment [5, 7, 8]. This thesis reconstructs that chain: Hamiltonian derivation, δf discretization, microinstability physics and saturation, empirical validation, and open problems.

> Theorem: *Gyrokinetic Reduction Theorem (Frieman–Chen, 1982).* Under the gyrokinetic ordering — fluctuation amplitudes eδφ/T ∼ ρ\*/ ≡ ρᵢ/a ≪ 1, frequencies ω/Ωᵢ ∼ ρ\* ≪ 1, and perpendicular scales k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 1 — the Vlasov dynamics of a magnetized plasma can be reduced, via a near-identity Lie-transform coordinate change, to a closed five-dimensional system for the gyrocenter distribution Fₛ(**R**, v∥, μ, t) coupled to the gyroaveraged electromagnetic fields, with exact energy conservation inherited from the underlying Hamiltonian structure [1, 2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Vlasov–Maxwell to the gyrokinetic ordering

The exact kinetic description is the Vlasov equation for each species *s*,

∂fₛ/∂t + **v**·∇fₛ + (qₛ/mₛ)(**E** + **v**×**B**)·∂fₛ/∂**v** = 0,

coupled to Maxwell's equations. The Lorentz term drives gyration at Ωₛ = qₛB/mₛ; for 10 keV deuterium at 5 T, Ωᵢ ≈ 2.4 × 10⁸ rad/s and ρᵢ ≈ 3 mm, so ρ\* = ρᵢ/a ∼ 3 × 10⁻³ in a meter-scale tokamak. Drift-wave turbulence oscillates at ω/Ωᵢ ∼ ρ\* — far below the gyrofrequency — with k⊥ρᵢ ∼ O(1) across **B** but k∥L ∼ 1 along field lines.

The gyrokinetic ordering formalizes this separation [1]:

- **Amplitude ordering:** eδφ/Tₛ ∼ δB/B ∼ ρ\* — fluctuations are small in absolute terms.
- **Frequency ordering:** ω/Ωₛ ∼ ρ\* — only low-frequency dynamics survive.
- **Anisotropy:** k⊥ρₛ ∼ 1 but k∥/k⊥ ∼ ρ\* — eddies are highly elongated along **B**.
- **Equilibrium variation:** ρₛ/L_eq ∼ ρ\* — background profiles vary slowly.

Crucially, no smallness is assumed for k⊥ρₛ: *finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) effects enter at full order* through gyroaveraging, which is why gyrokinetics captures the ion–electron scale asymmetry that MHD and fluid models miss.

### 2.2 Hamiltonian foundations and Lie-transform perturbation theory

The modern derivation rests on Littlejohn's variational principle for guiding-center motion: the *nonlinear* gyrokinetic equations follow from a systematic Lie-transform expansion of the single-particle phase-space Lagrangian, order by order in ρ\* [2]. This machinery matters for two reasons. First, it guarantees **exact energy conservation**: the gyrocenter Hamiltonian yields a conserved energy functional with polarization (Poisson) and magnetization (Ampère) contributions. Second, it is *modular* — δB∥, δB⊥, rotation, and strong flows enter by extending the Lie generating functions [2]. The definitive reference [2] organizes the theory around three pillars: (i) the gyrokinetic Vlasov equation in gyrocenter Hamiltonian form, (ii) the gyrokinetic Maxwell system with polarization and magnetization densities, and (iii) the exact energy conservation law.

### 2.3 The δf method and numerical strategies

Even gyroaveraged, five-dimensional phase space is enormous — a flux-tube grid easily exceeds 2 × 10⁸ degrees of freedom per species. The **δf method** [3, 4] writes fₛ = F₀ₛ + δfₛ, evolving only the small fluctuation δfₛ ∼ ρ\*F₀ₛ about a prescribed Maxwellian background F₀ₛ. In PIC codes (XGC, ORB5) markers sample δf, cutting statistical noise by orders of magnitude; in continuum codes (GENE, CGYRO, GS2) the grid resolves only the fluctuating part. Validity requires δf/F₀ ≪ 1 — true in the core but *false at the edge*, motivating full-*f* formulations [10].

Two discretization philosophies dominate:

| Approach | Codes | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|----------|-------|-----------|------------|
| **Eulerian continuum** (fixed grid, field-aligned coords) | GENE [3], CGYRO [4], GS2 | No particle noise; spectral accuracy in ⊥; exact conservation | High memory; CFL limits from parallel dynamics |
| **Particle-in-cell δf** (Lagrangian markers) | ORB5, XGC (PIC variant) [6] | Low cost per step; natural for complex geometry | Statistical noise ∼ 1/√N; noise-driven transport |
| **Full-*f* spectral** (no δf splitting) | GENE-X [10], XGC (total-f) | Valid in edge/SOL; strong flows | 10–100× cost of δf; harder verification |

All three exploit **field-aligned coordinates**: with k∥ ≪ k⊥, aligning one coordinate with **B** cuts grid requirements by roughly an order of magnitude [3, 4].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell system

In GENE's electrostatic δf flux-tube formulation [3], the non-adiabatic part gₛ = δfₛ + (qₛδφ/T₀ₛ)F₀ₛ evolves as ∂gₛ/∂t = G[gₛ] + L[gₛ] + D[gₛ] + N[gₛ, gₛ], where G is the *gradient drive*, L the linear parallel dynamics, drifts and Landau damping, D dissipation (collisions plus hyperdiffusion), and N the quadratic E×B nonlinearity {gₛ, ⟨δφ⟩} driving cascade and saturation [3]. The fields follow from the gyrokinetic quasi-neutrality (Poisson) equation,

Σₛ (qₛ²n₀ₛ/T₀ₛ)(δφ − ⟨⟨δφ⟩⟩) = Σₛ qₛ ∫ d³v ⟨δfₛ⟩,

with ⟨·⟩ the gyroaverage, and from Ampère's law for the parallel vector potential δA∥ when electromagnetic effects are retained:

−∇⊥² δA∥ = μ₀ Σₛ qₛ ∫ d³v v∥ ⟨δfₛ⟩.

Gyroaveraging introduces J₀(k⊥ρₛ) — the fingerprint of FLR physics — suppressing ion response at k⊥ρᵢ ≳ 1 while electrons remain effectively unmagnetized at ion scales [1, 2].

### 3.2 Flux-tube versus global geometry

The **local (flux-tube) approximation** [3, 4] assumes eddies are small versus profile scale lengths, permitting periodic (x, y) boundaries with Fourier representation and twist-and-shift parallel conditions encoding magnetic shear. Flux-tube runs are *gradient-driven*: profiles fixed, turbulent fluxes computed for given gradients.

The **global** formulation [5] evolves the full radial annulus — with profile evolution and sources/sinks — capturing *nonlocal* spreading, avalanches, and ρ\*-dependent finite-size effects at 10–50× flux-tube cost. Cross-verification of global GENE, XGC, and ORB5 on shared equilibria showed quantitative agreement in relaxed profiles and heat fluxes [6].

### 3.3 Numerical implementation: the GENE/CGYRO algorithmic core

GENE discretizes (x, y, z, v∥, μ) on a fixed field-aligned grid: spectral methods across **B**, finite differences along it, Gauss–Legendre/Laguerre quadrature in velocity space [3]. Time advance is explicit fourth-order Runge–Kutta with dealiased pseudospectral nonlinearity; collisions enter via a conserving linearized Landau–Boltzmann operator [5], and **GyroLES** subgrid dissipation prevents spectral pile-up [8].

CGYRO [4], successor to GYRO, uses implicit–explicit stepping to remove the high-k⊥ CFL restriction, enabling routine multi-scale simulations. XGC [6] takes the Lagrangian route: total-*f*/δf PIC on an unstructured mesh from axis to wall, built for whole-volume core–edge coupling.

A minimal illustration of the δf weight evolution at the heart of PIC gyrokinetics:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import j0

def deltaf_weight_update(w, E_cross_B, grad_F0_over_F0, dt, nu_coll):
    """Advance delta-f marker weight w = delta_f / g: dw/dt = -(1/g) dF0/dt - nu*w."""
    drive = -np.dot(E_cross_B, grad_F0_over_F0)   # gradient drive G[g]
    return w + dt * (drive - nu_coll * w)

def gyroaveraged_ExB(phi_k, k_perp, rho_s, B0):
    """Gyroaveraged E x B drift; J0(k_perp*rho) = FLR suppression at k_perp*rho >> 1."""
    return 1j * np.cross(k_perp, [0, 0, 1]) * j0(k_perp * rho_s) * phi_k / B0
```

The Bessel factor J₀(k⊥ρₛ) is the entire reason electrons and ions decouple across scales: at ion-scale turbulence (k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 1), J₀ → 0 kills the ion adiabatic response at higher k, while electrons with k⊥ρₑ ∼ 1/60 see J₀ ≈ 1.

### 3.4 Verification methodology

Before validation, a code must be *verified* [9]:

1. **Linear benchmarks** — ITG/TEM/ETG/KBM growth rates vs. analytic dispersion and cross-code results (Cyclone base case).
2. **Nonlinear benchmarks** — saturated fluxes in standardized local cases.
3. **Conservation tests** — gyrokinetic energy invariant conserved in the collisionless limit.
4. **Cross-code verification** — GENE vs. CGYRO vs. XGC vs. ORB5 on shared equilibria, checking the *primacy hierarchy*: spectra, cross-phases, and zonal flows, not just fluxes [6, 9].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Linear microinstability taxonomy

Gyrokinetic linear theory predicts a zoo of drift-wave instabilities, distinguished by their free-energy source, propagation direction, and scale. The table below summarizes the principal modes; all are routinely computed by GENE and CGYRO linear eigenvalue/flux-tube solvers [3, 4, 8].

| Mode | Drive | Scale | Propagation | Transport channel |
|------|-------|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **ITG** (ion temperature gradient) | ∇Tᵢ, toroidal curvature | k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 0.3 | ion diamagnetic | dominant ion heat |
| **TEM** (trapped electron mode) | ∇n, ∇Tₑ, trapped electrons | k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 0.3–1 | electron diamagnetic | electron heat + particles |
| **ETG** (electron temperature gradient) | ∇Tₑ | k⊥ρₑ ∼ 0.3 (k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 60) | electron diamagnetic | electron heat (streamers) |
| **KBM** (kinetic ballooning mode) | ∇p, finite β | k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 0.1 | ion diamagnetic | stiff, MHD-like crashes |
| **MTM** (microtearing mode) | ∇Tₑ, collisional reconnection | k⊥ρᵢ ∼ 0.5–2 | electron diamagnetic | electron heat via δB |

ITG is the workhorse of core turbulence: toroidal curvature couples ∇Tᵢ to the E×B drift, with linear threshold R/L_Ti ≳ 4–6 (depending on q, shear, Tᵢ/Tₑ) [3]. TEMs arise from trapped-electron precessional resonance and dominate at strong ∇n; ETG, the electron-scale analogue of ITG, forms elongated *streamers* (k_r ≪ k_θ) carrying significant electron heat despite gyro-Bohm expectations [7].

> Theorem: *Quasilinear Transport Proportionality.* In the weak-turbulence limit, the turbulent heat flux of species *s* satisfies Qₛ ∝ Σ_k |δφ_k|² Im[χₛ(k)] where χₛ is the linear susceptibility; the *cross-phase* between δφ and δTₛ/δnₛ fluctuations determines the sign and magnitude of transport. Phase-angle analysis therefore sits *below* fluxes in the primacy hierarchy: two codes can agree on growth rates yet disagree on fluxes if their cross-phases differ [6].

Finite β = 2μ₀p/B² brings electromagnetic physics: approaching the ballooning limit, the KBM destabilizes and magnetic flutter (δB⊥) opens a parallel electron heat channel. Crucially, finite-β fluctuations also **stabilize ITG** by partially canceling the electrostatic drive — an effect exploited in high-β scenarios and confirmed in GENE simulations of JET D–T plasmas [5].

### 4.2 Zonal flows, the Dimits shift, and nonlinear saturation

Linear theory cannot predict turbulence *levels*. Saturation is governed by **zonal flows** — axisymmetric (k_y = 0), radially sheared E×B flows driven by the turbulence itself via Reynolds stress. They shear eddies apart in a predator–prey cycle, throttling transport. The collisionless zonal-flow *residual* of Rosenbluth–Hinton must be reproduced by every code; failure here means failure to saturate. The **Dimits shift** — the nonlinear upshift of the transport threshold 30–50% above the linear boundary [9] — is:

1. *Purely nonlinear* — invisible to linear analysis, decisive for stiffness;
2. *Collisionally eroded* — collisions damp zonal flows;
3. *A verification rite of passage* — every code must reproduce the Cyclone Dimits shift [3, 4, 9].

Saturation follows Q ∼ nT vₜₕ ρ\*² (R/L_T − R/L_T,crit)^α (α ∼ 1–3); simulations calibrate threshold and exponent for reduced transport models (TGLF, QuaLiKiz).

### 4.3 Multi-scale coupling: when electron scales matter

Ion- and electron-scale turbulence *do not* decouple. Multi-scale GYRO simulations of Alcator C-Mod [7] — spanning k_θρₛ ∼ 0.1 to k_θρₑ ∼ 1 with realistic mass ratio — showed ETG streamers raising both electron *and* ion-scale transport via cross-scale transfer invisible to single-scale runs. At ∼15 million core-hours per run on NERSC Edison [7], these remain among the most expensive turbulence simulations ever performed. The lesson: ion-scale-truncated models can under-predict Qₑ by factors of two — the historic "shortfall" was partly a *resolution* artifact. GENE multi-scale DIII-D near-edge runs [8] further revealed a nonlinearly *hybrid* ITG–TEM state at ρ = 0.90 missed by linear analysis, with ion-scale turbulence suppressing electron-scale transport — coupling acts both ways.

### 4.4 The δf method under the microscope: noise, cancellation, and full-*f*

The δf splitting has well-characterized failure modes. In **PIC**, marker noise sets a 1/√N fluctuation floor; if it exceeds the physical δf/F₀, noise-driven transport contaminates results — hence coarse-graining, Fourier filtering, and large marker counts [6]. In **continuum** codes, the *cancellation problem* in Ampère's law — the current as a small difference of large ion/electron terms — amplifies round-off at high β, mitigated by split-weight schemes [4]. Where δf/F₀ ∼ 1 (edge, SOL) the splitting is invalid and **full-*f*** is required. The spectral full-*f* code GENE-X [10] uses a Hermite–Laguerre velocity basis for order-of-magnitude speedups, reproducing TCV-X21 profiles, spectra, and separatrix fluxes — the likely path to predictive edge modeling [10].

A compact statement of the conservation structure that any discretization must respect can be written as a TLA+ invariant:

```tla
------------------------------ MODULE GyrokineticEnergy ------------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, Reals
VARIABLES fieldEnergy, particleEnergy, collisionalSink

TypeInvariant ==
    /\ fieldEnergy \in Real
    /\ particleEnergy \in Real

(* Exact energy conservation in the collisionless limit: dE/dt = 0 *)
EnergyConservation ==
    collisionalSink = 0 =>
        fieldEnergy' + particleEnergy' = fieldEnergy + particleEnergy

(* H-theorem: collisions dissipate free energy monotonically *)
FreeEnergyDecay ==
    collisionalSink > 0 =>
        fieldEnergy' + particleEnergy' <= fieldEnergy + particleEnergy
======================================================================================
```

### 4.5 Electromagnetic gyrokinetics and fast-ion stabilization

Retaining δA∥ and δB∥ extends gyrokinetics to finite β. Two consequences dominate. First, **fast-ion stabilization**: supra-thermal ions dilute the thermal drive and, via wave–particle resonances, suppress turbulence 30–60% — a leading explanation for JET D–T confinement improvement, quantified in global electromagnetic GENE runs [5]. GENE–CGYRO cross-verification on KSTAR profiles agreed on linear stability and fractional flux changes but flagged absolute-flux and rotation discrepancies defining the verification frontier [6]. Second, **microtearing modes** — ∇Tₑ-driven via collisional current layers — form ρᵢ-scale islands short-circuiting electron heat along stochastic field lines, a channel invisible to electrostatic models.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Multi-channel validation: the new standard

The standard is now **multi-channel flux matching**, exemplified by the ASDEX Upgrade *Nature Communications* study [5]: nonlinear GENE must simultaneously reproduce ion/electron heat, particle, and momentum fluxes within gradient uncertainties. Key results:

- Electromagnetic, collisional, shaped-geometry GENE with realistic mass ratio matched all channels in two scenarios — a predictive milestone [5].
- Linear census: ITG-dominated at k⊥ρₛ ∼ 0.1–1.0, ETG at electron scales, consistent with nonlinear fluxes [5].
- Synthetic BES/reflectometry diagnostics closed the loop to measured fluctuation spectra [5].

DIII-D near-edge L-modes [8] reached simultaneous ion/electron agreement within ∇Tᵢ diagnostic uncertainty, and Alcator C-Mod [7] required multi-scale runs to recover Qₑ.

### 5.2 Quantitative scaling results

Gyrokinetic simulation databases underpin the confinement scalings used in systems codes. Representative calibrated results:

| Quantity | Gyrokinetic finding | Source |
|----------|--------------------|----|
| Ion heat diffusivity stiffness | Qᵢ/Q_gB ∝ (R/L_Ti − R/L_Ti,crit)^2–3 | GENE Cyclone scans [3] |
| ρ\* scaling of transport | Weaker than gyro-Bohm in global runs (nonlocal spreading) | Global GENE [5] |
| ETG streamer contribution | Up to 50% of Qₑ in high-R/L_Te regimes | Multi-scale GYRO [7] |
| Fast-ion turbulence suppression | 30–60% reduction in Qᵢ with 20% fast-ion fraction | GENE JET D–T [5] |
| Dimits shift magnitude | Δ(R/L_T,crit) ≈ 1.5–2.0 above linear threshold | Cyclone benchmark [9] |

### 5.3 Formal results: what is proven

Three results in gyrokinetic theory carry the force of proof rather than empirical observation:

> Theorem: *Exact Energy Conservation (Brizard–Hahm).* The nonlinear gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell system derived by Lie-transform perturbation theory possesses an exact conserved energy functional — the sum of gyrocenter kinetic energy and field energy including quadratic polarization and magnetization terms — for any truncation consistent with the variational derivation [2]. Numerical schemes inherit this conservation only if the discretization preserves the underlying Hamiltonian structure.

> Theorem: *Zonal-Flow Residual (Rosenbluth–Hinton).* An initial axisymmetric potential perturbation in a collisionless toroidal plasma relaxes not to zero but to a finite residual fraction 1/(1 + 1.6q²/√ε), where q is the safety factor and ε the inverse aspect ratio. This residual underwrites the Dimits shift and is a mandatory verification target [9].

A compact Haskell expression of the flux-surface-averaged transport computation performed on every simulation's saturated phase:

```haskell
-- Flux-surface averaged radial heat flux from gyrokinetic fields
heatFlux :: Species -> Field k -> Field k -> Double
heatFlux s phi_k dT_k =
    sum [ q_s * n0 * realPart (conjg (exbRadial phi_k k) * dT_k k)
        | k <- modes, ky k /= 0 ]   -- zonal (ky = 0) modes carry no radial flux
  where
    -- gyroaveraged E x B radial velocity: v_ExB = i k_y J0(k_perp rho_s) phi / B0
    exbRadial phi k = (0 :+ 1) * ky k * besselJ0 (kPerp k * rho s) * phi k / b0
```

## 6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty demands a clear accounting of where gyrokinetics, as practiced today, falls short.

**1. The edge ordering breakdown.** In the pedestal and SOL, δf/F₀ reaches tens of percent and L ∼ ρᵢ, violating the ordering; δf codes are formally invalid there. Full-*f* methods [10] are the remedy at 10–100× cost with thinner verification.

**2. Electromagnetic incompleteness at high β.** δB∥ physics and the Ampère cancellation problem remain delicate near the ballooning limit; spherical tokamaks push into thinly verified regimes [4, 6].

**3. Profile stiffness.** Fluxes depend stiffly on gradients — 20% diagnostic uncertainty maps to factor-two flux uncertainty — so validation means consistency-within-uncertainty, not parameter-free prediction [5, 8].

**4. Missing physics.** Alfvén eigenmodes, large-scale MHD, neutrals, and plasma–material interaction are routinely omitted; validation is conditional on their being subdominant.

**5. Computational cost.** Multi-scale global runs cost tens of millions of core-hours [7]; design work leans on reduced models (TGLF, QuaLiKiz) calibrated to gyrokinetic databases, adding a partially quantified error layer.

**6. Verification is never finished.** Each extension — collisions, rotation, fast ions, electromagnetics — reopens the hierarchy [9]; the GENE–CGYRO fast-ion comparison [6] shows agreement on growth rates coexisting with absolute-flux disagreement: *code agreement is necessary but not sufficient for truth*.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Gyrokinetics is among the most successful reductions in theoretical physics: Lie-transform machinery [1, 2] extracts from 6D Vlasov–Maxwell a 5D theory retaining exactly the FLR, drift-resonance, and zonal-flow physics behind anomalous transport. Its realization in GENE [3], CGYRO [4], and XGC [6] has matured from qualitative instability studies to multi-channel prediction validated on the world's tokamaks [5, 7, 8] — including cross-scale coupling [7] and fast-ion stabilization [5] that decide burning-plasma performance.

The road ahead: full-*f* spectral fidelity at the edge [10], the electromagnetic cancellation problem at reactor β, and exascale databases converted into reliable reduced models. The central lesson — *rigorous asymptotic reduction, exactly-conserving discretization, hierarchical verification* — is the only credible path to confident ITER and SPARC performance prediction.
