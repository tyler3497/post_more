# Linear Regions and Concurrent Ownership: Region-Based Memory Safety Without Garbage Collection in Project Verona

## Abstract

Microsoft Research's **Project Verona** is an experimental programming language that pursues a radical proposition: *temporal and spatial memory safety, data-race freedom, and scalable memory management can be obtained simultaneously, without a garbage collector, without a per-object borrow checker, and without pervasive unsafe code*. The language's three pillars — **linear regions** (groups of objects with group-level ownership), **concurrent owners (cowns)** enabling behaviour-oriented concurrency, and pervasive compartmentalisation — replace Rust-style per-object linearity with a coarser, more ergonomic unit of ownership: the *memory region*. This thesis gives a self-contained treatment of Verona's region model: the *Iso* (isolated) region discipline and its single-entry-point invariant, the *Cell* and *cown[T]* reference capabilities that mediate mutation and sharing, the `when`-clause semantics of behaviour-oriented concurrency (BoC) formalised by Cheeseman et al. [2], and a proof sketch of the region-isolation theorem that underpins temporal safety. We compare Verona's region model systematically against Rust's borrow checker, Cyclone's dynamic regions, and the Pony actor model from which cowns descend. We assess the empirical evidence — the snmalloc allocator lineage [4], the BoC C++ runtime, and early Verona interpreter benchmarks — and argue that regions, not objects, are the correct granularity at which to reconcile safety with scalable reclamation. Finally, we discuss limitations: the expressiveness cost of forbidding concurrent mutation, the ergonomics of region nesting, and the open problem of legacy interoperability.

## 1 Introduction

Roughly **seventy percent** of the serious security vulnerabilities addressed by Microsoft's security response centre stem from memory-safety defects [5]. The industry response has bifurcated: adopt memory-safe-by-default languages wholesale, or retrofit safety into C and C++ through sandboxing, hardware capabilities (CHERI), and static analysis. Project Verona, developed by Microsoft Research Cambridge in collaboration with academics at Imperial College London and first unveiled in late 2019, explores a third path: *design a language in which unsafe memory behaviour is inexpressible*, but in which the cost model — predictable, allocator-level reclamation — resembles C++ more than it resembles a garbage-collected runtime [1][6].

Verona's wager is that Rust's ownership discipline, brilliant as it is, operates at the wrong granularity. Rust tracks ownership and borrows **per object**: every `&mut T` is a linear-ish capability whose lifetime the borrow checker must reason about individually. This yields safety, but at the cost of the well-known fights with the borrow checker whenever a *data structure* — a collection of objects with a shared lifetime — must be built, split, or reorganised. As Matthew Parkinson, the project's research lead, put it: programmers think of "a data structure as a collection of objects. And that collection of objects [has] a lifetime." Verona therefore lifts ownership one level up: **the region**, a group of objects managed and owned as a unit [5].

Three ideas compose the design, announced when the project was open-sourced under the MIT licence [6]:

1. **Data-race freedom** via the renunciation of *concurrent arbitrary mutation*. Giving up concurrent mutation is presented as the necessary precondition for scalable memory management: deciding "when an object will never be accessed again" is a global consensus problem across threads unless ownership makes it local [7].
2. **Concurrent owners (cowns)**: a new concurrency primitive that encapsulates resources — typically regions — and guarantees they are accessed by at most one thread of execution at a time, coordinated through asynchronous *behaviours* [2][7].
3. **Linear regions**: ownership tracked at the granularity of object groups, with per-region memory-management strategies, nesting, and a single linear entry point into each region [1][6].

This thesis reconstructs the technical argument in full. Section 2 situates Verona among region calculi (Tofte–Talpin), Cyclone, Rust, and Pony. Section 3 states our methodology: a synthesis of primary sources — the Verona design documents, the BoC formalisation [2], and the reference-capability literature [3] — reconstructed into a uniform calculus. Section 4 is the deep dive: linear/affine foundations, the *Iso* discipline, *Cell* and *cown*, `when`-clauses, and the isolation guarantee. Section 5 surveys empirical evidence. Section 6 confronts limitations honestly. Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The memory-safety crisis and the ownership turn

The empirical premise needs no rehearsal: spatial violations (out-of-bounds access) and temporal violations (use-after-free, double-free) dominate systems-security CVEs. Two families of defence exist. *Dynamic* defences — garbage collection, reference counting, sanitizers, hardware capabilities — pay runtime cost and, in the case of GC, introduce latency and footprint unpredictability unacceptable in kernels and embedded targets. *Static* defences — linear and affine type systems, ownership types, borrow checking — shift the cost to compile time and programmer annotation.

Rust demonstrated that affine ownership with borrowing can scale to production systems software: Firefox, the Linux kernel, Windows components. Yet Rust's model has a structural pressure point: its unit of ownership is the single object. Building intrusive data structures, self-referential graphs, or arena-allocated object families forces the programmer into `unsafe` blocks, `Rc<RefCell<T>>` reference cycles, or arena crates that reintroduce manual reasoning — precisely the "expose unsafe to enable unsafe libraries" compromise the Verona team criticises [7].

### 2.2 Region-based memory management: Tofte–Talpin to Cyclone

Region-based memory management predates ownership types. Tofte and Talpin's *region inference* for ML allocated values into lexically scoped regions reclaimed *en masse* at region exit — O(1) amortised deallocation with no per-object bookkeeping. The price was lexical scoping: region lifetimes followed the call stack, crippling expressiveness for data structures that outlive their creating scope.

**Cyclone** (Morrisett et al., Cornell) generalised regions: dynamically allocated, first-class regions with a growable-region discipline and a sophisticated effect system tracking which regions each pointer could reference. Cyclone proved that region safety could be statically guaranteed for C-like code, but its region polymorphism and effect annotations were notoriously heavy — the "region variables everywhere" problem.

Verona inherits Cyclone's ambition (safe manual-style memory management, region granularity) while attacking its weakness (annotation burden) through a different mechanism: *linear capabilities as region handles*, so that region lifetime is witnessed by possession of a unique token rather than by a lattice of effect variables.

### 2.3 Pony and the road to cowns

The concurrency half of Verona descends from **Pony**, the actor-model language co-designed by Sylvan Clebsch — now a Verona researcher [1]. Pony's reference capabilities (`iso`, `val`, `ref`, `tag`, `box`) guarantee data-race freedom by construction: an `iso` reference is the unique path to a mutable object graph, and only `iso`/`val`/`tag` may cross actor boundaries. Pony's limitation, identified in the BoC paper [2], is compositional atomicity: acquiring consistent, atomic access to *multiple* actors' state is notoriously hard in pure actor models. Verona's cowns generalise Pony's `iso` from single objects to *regions of objects* and replace message-passing with `when`-clauses that atomically acquire several cowns at once — transactional, ordered, deadlock-free multi-resource acquisition.

### 2.4 The Verona research programme

Project Verona is explicitly a *research vehicle*, not a product: a prototype interpreter, type-checker, and production-quality runtime (building on the snmalloc allocator lineage [4]) were open-sourced on GitHub under MIT [6], with contributors spanning Microsoft Research Cambridge (Parkinson, Clebsch, Juliana Franco, David Chisnall), Imperial College London (Sophia Drossopoulou, Marios Kogias, Luke Cheeseman, Paul Liétar), and Uppsala University (Tobias Wrigstad) [2]. The programme's stated research questions, from the project's design notes, are [7]:

1. If we design a language *without concurrent mutation*, can we build scalable memory management?
2. Can *linear regions* remove the restrictions of per-object linearity without sacrificing memory management?
3. Can language-level regions support *compartmentalisation* — sandboxing untrusted legacy code at language-design granularity?

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is a *reconstructive synthesis*: we take the primary artefacts — the Verona design documents and FAQ [7], the BoC formal model and C++ runtime [2], the reference-capability framework [3], contemporary technical reportage [1][5][6], and the Wikipedia project summary [8] — and reconstruct them as a single, uniform technical account. Our method has four steps:

1. **Source triangulation.** Every technical claim is cross-checked against at least two independent sources (e.g., the InfoWorld technical summary [1] against the SD Times concurrent-ownership description [7] against the BoC paper's formal definitions [2]).
2. **Calculus reconstruction.** We restate Verona's region discipline in a compact core calculus (in the spirit of Featherweight Java) so that the isolation theorem can be stated and its proof sketched precisely, rather than relying on prose.
3. **Comparative matrix.** Rust, Cyclone, Pony, and Verona are compared along fixed axes — unit of ownership, concurrency model, reclamation mechanism, annotation burden, escape hatches — in Section 4.5.
4. **Evidence audit.** Section 5 distinguishes *measured* results (BoC runtime experiments [2], snmalloc allocator performance [4]) from *projected* claims (scalable region reclamation), and marks the boundary explicitly.

We do not claim access to unreleased Verona compiler internals; where the public record is silent (e.g., the exact region-nesting subtyping rules in the current prototype), we say so.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ownership, linearity, and affinity: the type-theoretic substrate

Verona's static discipline rests on **substructural type theory**. In ordinary intuitionistic typing, the structural rules of *weakening* (discard a hypothesis) and *contraction* (duplicate a hypothesis) are free. A **linear** type system drops both: a linear value must be used *exactly once*. An **affine** system drops contraction but keeps weakening: use *at most once*. Rust is morally affine (values may be dropped, never duplicated); Verona's region handles are linear in the strict sense at region boundaries — a region handle consumed by a move cannot be silently dropped without either destroying the region or transferring ownership, because *the handle is the proof that the region's lifetime is accounted for*.

The key move is changing *what* is linear. In Rust, linearity attaches to objects (`Box<T>`, `&mut T`). In Verona, linearity attaches to **regions**:

> **Theorem: (Region Isolation).** *In a well-typed Verona program, every heap object belongs to exactly one region; every region has exactly one linear entry point; and no reference crosses a region boundary except through the region's entry point. Consequently, when the unique handle to a region is consumed, the entire region — all its objects — may be reclaimed as a unit without inspecting any object, and no dangling reference can survive.*

The proof sketch (Section 4.6) follows from two invariants: (i) *entry-point uniqueness*, enforced by making region handles linear capabilities; and (ii) *boundary discipline*, enforced by the capability lattice on references (*Iso*, *Cell*, cown) that controls which pointers may point where.

Why does group-level linearity help? Consider building a doubly-linked list. In Rust, each node's `prev`/`next` pointers create aliasing the borrow checker cannot see through, forcing `unsafe` or `Rc<RefCell<>>`. In Verona, the whole list lives in one region; internal pointers are *intra-region* references, unrestricted by linearity, because linearity is checked only at the region boundary. The programmer reasons about one linear token — the region — instead of *n* linear tokens for *n* nodes. Per-object linearity's restrictions dissolve because the *unit of reasoning* matches the *unit of the data structure* [5].

Formally, regions form a **forest** (a collection of trees): regions may nest — a child region's lifetime is bounded by its parent's — but sibling regions are disjoint. Allocation always occurs *in* the current region; there is no global heap of individually owned objects. Deallocation is *region deallocation*: freeing the entry-point handle frees the region's arena in O(1) amortised time, exactly as in Tofte–Talpin, but with dynamic rather than lexical extent.

| Concept | Rust analogue | Verona form | Linearity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owned value | `Box<T>` / owned `T` | Region handle | Linear (exactly-once) |
| Borrow | `&T`, `&mut T` | Intra-region reference | Unrestricted (within region) |
| Shared ownership | `Rc<T>` / `Arc<T>` | Shared immutable region | Non-linear (frozen) |
| Unique transfer | Move semantics | `Iso` region move | Linear |
| Thread-safe sharing | `Mutex<T>` / channels | `cown[T]` + `when` | Capability-gated |

### 4.2 Iso regions: the isolated region discipline

An **Iso** (isolated) region is Verona's strictest region mode, and the workhorse of both memory safety and concurrency. An `Iso` region satisfies three properties:

1. **External uniqueness.** There is exactly one reference from outside the region into it — the linear entry point. No aliases to the entry point exist; the type system treats the handle affinely/linearly.
2. **Internal freedom.** Within the region, arbitrary object graphs are permitted: cycles, back-pointers, doubly-linked structures. The region is *internally* a conventional heap; discipline applies only at the boundary.
3. **Boundary opacity.** Code outside the region cannot name, reach, or mutate objects inside except by *entering* through the entry point (consuming or borrowing the linear handle) or by *freezing* the region into an immutable shared state.

The `Iso` discipline is what makes region deallocation sound: because no external reference into the region can exist other than the unique handle, destroying the handle provably destroys the last path to every object in the region. This is the region-level analogue of Pony's `iso` capability — and indeed Verona's designers describe `Iso` as generalising Pony `iso` from object graphs to *regions* [2][3].

A subtle point: `Iso` regions may be *temporarily opened*. Opening an `Iso` region yields a scoped capability to mutate its interior; the opening construct is lexically scoped so the region is re-sealed (and uniqueness restored) at scope exit. This is the region analogue of Rust's reborrowing, but checked once per region rather than once per reference.

```rust
// Verona-like pseudocode: Iso region lifecycle
let r: Iso<Region> = Region::isolated();   // linear handle; sole entry point
r.allocate(|arena| {
    let a = arena.alloc(Node { next: None });
    let b = arena.alloc(Node { next: Some(a) });  // cycles fine: intra-region
    a.next = Some(b);                              // unrestricted interior mutation
});                                                // scope exit re-seals Iso
// move the region across a thread boundary — still unique:
send_to_worker(move r);                            // linear move; sender loses handle
// ... later, when the handle is consumed:
drop(r);   // O(1) arena reclamation; no per-object finalisers, no GC pause
```

Note what is absent: no borrow annotations, no lifetime parameters, no `unsafe`. The region boundary absorbs all the linearity reasoning.

### 4.3 Cell and cown: capabilities for mutation and concurrency

If `Iso` governs *ownership*, the **capability lattice** governs *access*. Two capabilities matter most:

**Cell.** A `Cell` is a region (or object) handle that grants *exclusive mutable access* to its contents to whoever holds it — but unlike `Iso`, a `Cell` may be *aliased for reading* under controlled conditions, and its contents may be *frozen* into immutability. In the BoC formalisation, each heap object is owned by exactly one entry point, and the type system ensures that any object accessed within a behaviour's closure is owned by one of the acquired cowns [2]. `Cell` is the sequential-programming capability: it says "whoever holds this token may mutate; the token itself is linear, so mutation is never concurrent."

**cown[T]** — pronounced "cone" — is the concurrency capability. A `cown` *encapsulates* a set of resources (typically one or more regions) and guarantees they are accessed by **at most one thread of execution at a time** [7]. Crucially, a `cown` *denies direct access*: given `acc2: cown[Account]`, the expression `acc2.balance += 100` is a type error. The contents are accessible only from inside a *behaviour* that has acquired the cown [2]:

```
// From Cheeseman et al. [2], simplified: cowns deny direct access
var acc1 = Account.create();
acc1.balance -= 100;                       // OK: ordinary synchronous access

var acc2 = cown.create(Account.create());
acc2.balance += 100;                       // TYPE ERROR: cown contents are opaque

when (a = acc2) {                          // spawn a behaviour acquiring acc2
    a.balance += 100;                      // OK: inside, cown[Account] looks like Account
}                                          // behaviour runs later, exclusively, atomically
```

The `when` construct is the sole primitive for concurrent access. `when (x1 = c1, ..., xn = cn) { body }` spawns a **behaviour**: an asynchronous unit of work that will execute *at some later point* with *exclusive access* to all of `c1..cn` simultaneously. From inside the body, each acquired `cown[T]` is viewed as a plain `T`. The behaviour executes *logically atomically* and *without data races* — the type system plus the runtime's acquisition protocol guarantee it [2].

Three properties make `when` remarkable:

1. **Atomic multi-acquisition.** A behaviour acquires *all* its cowns or *none* — there is no incremental lock ordering for the programmer to get wrong, and hence no deadlock from lock-order inversion. The runtime queues the behaviour on each cown's wait queue; it runs only when it reaches the head of *every* queue.
2. **Ordering.** Behaviours that share a cown execute in *spawn order* — a total order per cown — giving programmers a comprehensible happens-before story without explicit synchronisation.
3. **Return-immediately semantics.** `when` spawns and returns; the caller never blocks. Coordination without blocking is what makes the model *behaviour-oriented* rather than *thread-oriented*: you declare units of exclusive work, and the runtime schedules them.

```
// Atomic transfer across two cowns: impossible to write racy or deadlocked
transfer(src: cown[Account], dst: cown[Account], amount: U32) {
    when (s = src, d = dst) {      // acquires BOTH atomically; no lock ordering
        s.balance -= amount;       // exclusive: no other behaviour interleaves
        d.balance += amount;
    }                              // returns immediately; behaviour runs later
}
```

In a pure actor model (Pony included), this transfer requires a multi-message protocol with an intermediate pending state visible to other actors. In BoC, it is one atomic behaviour [2].

### 4.4 When-clauses and behaviour-oriented concurrency

Behaviour-Oriented Concurrency deserves emphasis because it is Verona's answer to the "amazing concurrency story" problem: since the language forbids concurrent mutation *outside* cowns, it must provide exactly one excellent way to coordinate — there is no `unsafe` escape hatch as in Rust [7].

The BoC paper [2] (Cheeseman, Parkinson, Clebsch, Kogias, Drossopoulou, Chisnall, Wrigstad, Liétar; OOPSLA 2023, Article 276) contributes:

- **An informal design** via worked examples (bank accounts, dining philosophers, concurrent data structures) showing that exclusive multi-resource behaviours subsume actors, monitors, and transactional memory for coordination patterns.
- **A formal model**: an operational semantics with a *cown queue* per cown, behaviour spawning, and acquisition; progress and preservation lemmas; and a **data-race-freedom theorem** — no two behaviours holding the same cown ever execute concurrently, and behaviours are serialisable per cown.
- **A C++ runtime implementation** demonstrating practicality: lock-free per-cown queues, work-stealing scheduling of behaviours, and performance competitive with hand-rolled fine-grained locking on coordination-heavy benchmarks.

The deeper claim is architectural: *parallelism* (do independent things at once) and *coordination* (agree on shared state) are usually served by different mechanisms (threads + locks, async + channels), and their interaction is where bugs breed. BoC unifies them in a single primitive — the behaviour — parameterised by the set of cowns it coordinates. Parallelism falls out for free: behaviours over *disjoint* cown sets run concurrently with no coordination whatsoever.

### 4.5 Verona vs. Rust vs. Pony vs. Cyclone: the comparison matrix

| Axis | Rust | Pony | Cyclone | **Verona** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit of ownership | Single object | Object graph (`iso`) | Region | **Region (object group)** |
| Linearity granularity | Per-reference (`&mut`) | Per-capability | Per-region-effect | **Per-region handle** |
| Concurrent mutation | `unsafe` + `Send`/`Sync` | Forbidden (`iso`/`val`/`tag` only cross actors) | N/A (sequential) | **Forbidden; cowns + `when` instead** |
| Multi-resource atomicity | Manual lock ordering | Multi-message protocols | N/A | **`when` atomic acquisition** |
| Reclamation | Per-object drop / RAII | Per-actor GC | Region delete | **Region-atomic free; per-region strategy** |
| Garbage collector | No | Yes (per-actor) | No | **No** |
| Annotation burden | Lifetimes, borrowck | Capabilities | Region/effect polymorphism | **Region handles; interior unannotated** |
| Escape hatch | `unsafe` (pervasive in ecosystem) | None | `unsafe`-like casts | **Compartments for legacy code** |
| Data-structure ergonomics | Poor (fights borrowck) | Good within actor | Moderate | **Good: interior unrestricted** |

The matrix clarifies Verona's thesis: it keeps Rust's *no-GC, no-unsafe* ambition and Pony's *data-race freedom by construction*, but moves the unit of ownership up to the region — recovering the data-structure ergonomics that per-object linearity sacrifices, and the scalable reclamation (free a region, not a million objects) that per-object drop cannot offer.

### 4.6 Memory-safety proof sketch: region isolation

We now sketch the safety argument for the core calculus. The judgement `Γ ⊢ e : τ @ ρ` reads "under capability context Γ, expression e has type τ and executes within region ρ."

**Definitions.** A *region handle* `h : Region[ρ]` is a linear capability. The *region forest invariant* states the heap is partitioned into regions, each with a nesting parent (or none). The *entry-point invariant* states each region has at most one live handle.

**Lemma 1 (Handle linearity).** In a well-typed program, region handles are neither duplicated nor discarded except by explicit `free`/`move`. *Proof.* By the substructural typing rules: handles have linear type, and the only rules consuming them are move, freeze, and region-free; weakening/contraction are inadmissible for linear types. ∎

**Lemma 2 (Boundary confinement).** No well-typed reference crosses a region boundary except a region handle pointing to its region's entry point. *Proof.* By induction on typing derivations: allocation fixes the current region; the only reference-forming rule that names another region's interior is the entry-point projection, which requires the linear handle; `Iso` regions additionally forbid even temporary aliases from escaping the open-scope. ∎

**Lemma 3 (Cown exclusivity).** At most one behaviour holds a given cown at any instant. *Proof.* From the BoC operational semantics [2]: behaviours queue per cown and are dequeued atomically only when at the head of all their cowns' queues; the scheduler never schedules two behaviours sharing a cown concurrently. ∎

> **Theorem: (Region Isolation / Temporal Safety).** *If `⊢ P` (P is well-typed) and P evaluates to a state where region handle `h` for region ρ is consumed by `free`, then no live reference to any object in ρ exists in the resulting state, and freeing ρ's arena is memory-safe.*

*Proof sketch.* By Lemma 1, `h` was the unique live handle for ρ (entry-point invariant). By Lemma 2, every path from the roots to any object in ρ passes through `h`; with `h` consumed, no such path remains — all objects in ρ are unreachable. By Lemma 3, no behaviour concurrently executing holds ρ's cown (any behaviour needing ρ must acquire its cown, and acquisition requires the handle path). Hence no thread can observe or touch ρ after `free`: no use-after-free, no double-free (linearity forbids freeing twice), no data race on reclamation. The arena may be returned to the allocator as a unit. ∎

**Corollary (Data-race freedom).** Well-typed Verona programs are data-race free: intra-region mutation is single-threaded by the cown discipline (a region is accessed by one thread at a time [1]), and inter-region communication moves linear handles or frozen (immutable) regions only.

The sketch omits the full preservation/progress machinery of [2], and the treatment of *freezing* (converting a mutable region to a shared-immutable one — sound because immutability needs no exclusivity). But the shape of the argument is what matters: *safety follows from counting handles, not from scanning objects*.

## 5 Empirical Results

We separate measured evidence from projected claims.

**Measured: the BoC runtime.** Cheeseman et al. [2] implement BoC in C++ with per-cown atomic queues and a work-stealing scheduler. Their evaluation shows behaviour-based coordination matching or beating hand-written fine-grained locking on contended data-structure benchmarks, while eliminating lock-ordering bugs by construction. Deadlock-freedom is not merely asserted: because acquisition is atomic over the behaviour's whole cown set, the classic hold-and-wait deadlock cycle cannot form — a *structural* property, verified in the model rather than benchmarked.

**Measured: the allocator lineage.** Verona's runtime builds on **snmalloc** (Chisnall et al., ISMM 2019 [4]) — a message-passing allocator designed so that deallocation never requires synchronisation with the allocating thread. snmalloc's published results show allocation throughput competitive with jemalloc/mimalloc while providing the "free on any thread, no remote-free lists" property that region-atomic reclamation wants: when a region is freed, its pages return to the allocator without cross-thread coordination. This is the empirical foundation for Verona's claim that *giving up concurrent mutation buys scalable memory management* [7].

**Projected: region reclamation at scale.** The headline claim — O(1)-amortised region free replacing per-object drops and GC pauses — follows from the isolation theorem rather than from large-scale Verona benchmarks, which the public record does not yet contain (the language remains a research prototype with an interpreter and type-checker [6]). The honest statement: the *mechanism* is proven sound and the *allocator substrate* is measured fast; end-to-end application evidence is future work.

**Qualitative: expressiveness.** The BoC paper's worked examples [2] — atomic multi-account transfer, parallel pipelines with ordered stages, compartmentalised plugins — demonstrate programs that are *shorter* than their actor-model equivalents and *safer* than their lock-based ones. The region examples in the Verona design notes show doubly-linked structures, graphs with back-edges, and arena-style allocation written with no annotations beyond region handles [7] — the borrow-checker fights simply do not arise.

## 6 Limitations

A candid assessment must name what the model gives up.

1. **No concurrent mutation, ever.** This is the founding renunciation [7], and it is total: any algorithm whose essence is fine-grained concurrent mutation of shared state (lock-free data structures, concurrent hash tables with in-place updates) must be restructured around cowns and behaviours, or pushed into a *compartment* — Verona's sandbox for legacy/unsafe code [1]. Compartments reintroduce a trust boundary the type system cannot see across; the safety story becomes "safe by default, explicitly sandboxed where not," which is honest but weaker than the core theorem.
2. **Region granularity vs. object lifetime.** Group-level ownership is coarser than per-object ownership: a long-lived object pinned in a region keeps the whole region alive. Verona mitigates this with region *splitting* (dividing a region along ownership lines) and *nesting*, but splitting requires proving the split respects the object graph — precisely the analysis regions were meant to avoid. Pathological cases (one immortal object per region) degenerate to per-object management with extra steps.
3. **Ergonomics of nesting and freezing.** The open questions in the design notes are real [7]: region-nesting subtyping rules, the interaction of freezing with cowns, and inference of region placement remain research problems. The prototype's ergonomics are unproven at the scale of a real codebase.
4. **Legacy interoperability.** Verona targets *infrastructure programming* — kernels, runtimes, services — domains drowning in C/C++. The compartment story (hardware-enforced via CHERI-adjacent work, or OS sandboxing) is architecturally elegant but operationally heavyweight; the migration path from a million lines of C++ to region-disciplined Verona is, charitably, aspirational.
5. **Proof coverage.** The BoC model [2] covers concurrency; the region-isolation argument exists in design-note form rather than as a mechanised proof. A full mechanised soundness proof (à la RustBelt for Rust) is outstanding — and given the subtlety of the freeze/cown interaction, it is *needed* before the claims can be considered settled.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Project Verona's contribution is best understood not as "a better Rust" but as a *change of the unit of account* in memory safety: from the object to the region, from per-reference linearity to per-region-handle linearity, from locks and messages to cowns and behaviours. The technical core — linear regions with a single entry point, the `Iso` isolation discipline, capability-gated mutation via `Cell`, and atomic multi-cown acquisition via `when` — composes into a system where temporal safety, data-race freedom, and O(1)-amortised reclamation are *theorems of the type system* rather than properties of a runtime. The BoC formalisation [2] and the snmalloc substrate [4] supply the two halves of the empirical story: coordination without locks, reclamation without a collector.

Whether Verona escapes the laboratory depends on the limitations of Section 6 — above all, whether programmers accept the renunciation of concurrent mutation in exchange for never fighting a borrow checker again, and whether compartments can absorb the legacy world without swallowing the safety argument. But as a research programme, Verona has already paid for itself: it demonstrates, with unusual clarity, that *ownership was never about objects*. It was always about *boundaries* — and regions are the boundary at which safety, scalability, and human reasoning finally coincide.
