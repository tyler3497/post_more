# Isogeny-Based Post-Quantum Key Exchange on Supersingular Elliptic Curve Graphs: SIDH, the Castryck–Decru Cryptanalysis, and the CSIDH–FESTA–SQIsign Aftermath

## Abstract

Supersingular elliptic curves over finite fields yield Ramanujan isogeny graphs whose path-finding problems underpinned post-quantum key exchange for over a decade. The *supersingular isogeny Diffie–Hellman* (SIDH) protocol and its NIST candidate SIKE offered the smallest keys of any post-quantum proposal — a few hundred bytes against kilobytes for lattices [1]. In July 2022 this edifice fell to a *classical* attack: Castryck and Decru recovered SIDH secret keys from the auxiliary torsion-point images the protocol publishes, combined with Kani's 1997 "glue-and-split" theorem on genus-two Jacobians, breaking SIKEp434 in about an hour on a single CPU core [2]. Refinements by Maino–Martindale [4] and Robert [5] generalized the break beyond SIKE's parameters. This thesis reconstructs the Deuring correspondence, explains SIDH/SIKE, dissects the attack via (2,2)-isogenies, and surveys the survivors — CSIDH's commutative class-group action [6], FESTA's weaponization of the attack for decryption [7], and SQIsign's quaternion signatures [8]; the lesson: the line between trapdoor and vulnerability is exactly which torsion data is revealed.

## 1. Introduction

The prospect of a cryptographically relevant quantum computer has forced public-key cryptography onto new mathematical foundations. Among the candidate families, *isogeny-based cryptography* occupied a tantalizing niche: the smallest key sizes of any post-quantum proposal and a protocol shape strikingly reminiscent of Diffie–Hellman, at the price of heavy computation and deep arithmetic geometry [1].

The foundational object is a graph. Fix a large prime *p*. The *supersingular ℓ-isogeny graph* has as vertices the *j*-invariants of supersingular elliptic curves over **F**¯_p_, with edges given by isogenies of prime degree ℓ. These graphs are Ramanujan expanders: random walks mix rapidly, and no efficient path-finding algorithm was known, even quantumly. The best generic attacks remain Õ(*p*^{1/4}) classical (Delfs–Galbraith meet-in-the-middle) and Õ(*p*^{1/6}) quantum (Tani claw-finding) [1].

> **Theorem:** (Deuring correspondence). The supersingular isogeny graph is isomorphic to the graph of maximal orders in the quaternion algebra *B*_{p,∞} ramified at *p* and ∞, edges given by left ideals of prime norm ℓ. The vertex count is ⌊*p*/12⌋ + ε_p_, with ε_p_ ∈ {0, 1, 2} depending on *p* mod 12.

This quaternionic dictionary is the source of both the field's constructive power and its cryptanalytic leverage: SIDH, the Castryck–Decru attack, CSIDH, and SQIsign all live inside it. The central question of this thesis is: *which isogeny problems survive once the adversary is handed auxiliary torsion-point information, and which do not?*

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Elliptic curves and isogenies

An elliptic curve *E* over a field *K* (char ≠ 2, 3) is given by *y*² = *x*³ + *ax* + *b* with 4*a*³ + 27*b*² ≠ 0; its *K*-points form an abelian group. The *j*-invariant classifies curves up to geometric isomorphism. An *isogeny* φ: *E* → *E′* is a non-constant morphism preserving the identity — hence a group homomorphism with finite kernel. Every isogeny has a *dual* φ̂ with φ̂ ∘ φ = [deg φ]. Given a kernel subgroup *G*, *Vélu's formulas* compute the quotient curve *E*/*G* and the rational maps explicitly in time polynomial in |*G*| and log *q*; SIDH decomposes a degree-2^{e_A} secret isogeny into *e_A* steps of 2-isogenies so the whole walk costs Õ(*e_A* log *p*).

### 2.2 Supersingular curves and the Deuring correspondence

A curve over **F**_p_ is *supersingular* when *p* divides the Frobenius trace *t* = *p* + 1 − #*E*(**F**_p_). All supersingular *j*-invariants lie in **F**_{p²}, and End(*E*) is a *maximal order* in *B*_{p,∞} — noncommutative, unlike the imaginary-quadratic endomorphism rings of ordinary curves. This noncommutativity is what makes the graph an expander with no efficiently computable distinguished paths.

| Geometric object | Quaternionic object |
|---|---|
| Supersingular *j*-invariant | Maximal order O ⊂ *B*_{p,∞} (up to conjugacy) |
| Isogeny φ: *E* → *E′* | Left O-ideal *I* with right order O′ |
| deg(φ) | Reduced norm nrd(*I*) |
| Dual isogeny φ̂ | Conjugate ideal *Ī* |

Path-finding between vertices becomes *connecting-ideal* finding between maximal orders — easy when one endpoint's endomorphism ring is known (the KLPT paradigm of Kohel–Lauter–Petit–Tignol reduces it to quaternion arithmetic), conjecturally hard otherwise.

### 2.3 The SIDH setting: smooth-torsion primes

SIDH uses primes *p* = 2^{e_A}·3^{e_B}·*f* ± 1 with small cofactor *f*, so the full 2^{e_A}- and 3^{e_B}-torsion is **F**_{p²}-rational. Concrete SIKE parameters: SIKEp434 with *p* = 2^{216}·3^{137} − 1 (NIST level 1) and SIKEp751 with *p* = 2^{372}·3^{239} − 1 (level 5) [1]. Walks start from the fixed public curve *E*₀: *y*² = *x*³ + 6*x*² + *x* — whose endomorphism ring is completely explicit, the fatal detail.

## 3. Methodology

Our approach is reconstructive and forensic. We formalize SIDH as a commutative diagram of isogenies and isolate its exact hardness assumption — the *SIDH problem*: recover the secret isogeny given the codomain *plus torsion-point images*. We then re-derive the Castryck–Decru attack following [2] and Wesolowski's simplification [9]: the isogeny diamond, Kani's glue-and-split theorem, the (2,2)-isogeny chain in the superspecial genus-2 graph, and digit-by-digit key recovery. Finally we classify the aftermath — Maino–Martindale [4], Robert [5], CSIDH [6], FESTA [7], SQIsign [8] — by whether the torsion leakage powering the attack is present, masked, or absent, and we compile the published attack timings and revised security estimates as empirical grounding.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The supersingular isogeny graph as a cryptographic arena

The graph Γ_ℓ(*p*) has ≈ *p*/12 vertices of out-degree ℓ + 1, and is Ramanujan: all nontrivial adjacency eigenvalues satisfy |λ| ≤ 2√ℓ. Three consequences matter:

1. **Uniform sampling** — a short random walk from *E*₀ lands near-uniformly; this is how SIDH private keys yield pseudorandom public curves.
2. **No structural shortcuts** — expansion rules out small cuts or trapdoor subgraphs.
3. **Generic hardness** — the best attacks remain claw-finding between half-walks: Õ(*p*^{1/4}) classical, Õ(*p*^{1/6}) quantum.

The Deuring correspondence refines this into an attacker's handbook. Because SIKE's *E*₀ (*j* = 287496) has a *known* endomorphism ring, the path problem from *E*₀ reduces to quaternion ideal arithmetic, solvable in heuristic polynomial time via KLPT. SIKE's security therefore never rested on the pure isogeny path problem — it rested on the SIDH problem, where the adversary additionally receives φ_A(*P_B*), φ_A(*Q_B*). Castryck–Decru showed this auxiliary data is *exactly* the handle that makes the problem easy [2], [3].

### 4.2 SIDH and SIKE: torsion-point public keys

Fix bases {*P_A*, *Q_A*} of *E*₀[2^{e_A}] and {*P_B*, *Q_B*} of *E*₀[3^{e_B}].

1. **Alice** samples (*m_A*, *n_A*), forms *K_A* = ⟨[*m_A*]*P_A* + [*n_A*]*Q_A*⟩, computes φ_A: *E*₀ → *E_A* = *E*₀/*K_A*, and publishes (*E_A*, φ_A(*P_B*), φ_A(*Q_B*)).
2. **Bob** mirrors with a 3^{e_B}-isogeny φ_B: *E*₀ → *E_B*, publishing (*E_B*, φ_B(*P_A*), φ_B(*Q_A*)).
3. **Shared secret.** Alice computes φ′_A: *E_B* → *E_BA* with kernel ⟨[*m_A*]φ_B(*P_A*) + [*n_A*]φ_B(*Q_A*)⟩; Bob computes φ′_B analogously. Then *E_BA* ≅ *E_AB* ≅ *E*₀/⟨*K_A*, *K_B*⟩ since the kernels have coprime order, and the common *j*-invariant is the secret.

SIKE wraps this in a Hofheinz–Hövelmanns–Kiltz-style transform for IND-CCA security *assuming SIDH is hard* [1]; public keys were 330 bytes for SIKEp434 versus 800+ for Kyber-512.

> **Theorem:** (SIDH correctness). *E_AB* ≅ *E_BA*, so both parties derive the same *j*-invariant.

> **Proof:** Both equal *E*₀/⟨*K_A*, *K_B*⟩: pushing *K_A* through φ_B (resp. *K_B* through φ_A) generates the subgroup spanned by both kernels, and the *j*-invariant is an isomorphism invariant. ∎

The retrospective lesson: the published torsion images let the *peer* complete the diagram — but they let *anyone* evaluate φ_A on the full 3^{e_B}-torsion. That is the data the attack weaponizes.

### 4.3 The Castryck–Decru attack: glue, split, recover

On July 30, 2022, Castryck and Decru posted "An efficient key recovery attack on SIDH" [2], recovering Bob's secret φ_B: *E*₀ → *E_B* from (*E_B*, φ_B(*P_A*), φ_B(*Q_A*)) in heuristic polynomial time. Ingredients:

1. **Kani's glue-and-split theorem** (1997) [10]. From an *isogeny diamond* — *E*₀ → *E_B* and *E*₀ → *E′* with compatible kernels (an anti-isometry on *d*-torsion) — one builds a (*d*,*d*)-isogeny Φ: *E*₀ × *E*′ → *E_B* × *E*″ of principally polarized abelian surfaces, where *E*″ splits as a product of elliptic curves *iff* the diamond closes consistently. A theorem of pure arithmetic geometry, proved 25 years before SIDH.
2. **The product-surface trick.** From public data the attacker builds a chain of (2,2)-isogenies — explicit via *Richelot formulas* — starting from a product *C* × *E* and walking the superspecial (2,2)-isogeny graph. Vertices are "usually" genus-2 Jacobians; products of elliptic curves are rare (≈ *p*²/288 of ≈ *p*³/2880 vertices).
3. **The decision oracle.** The walk is engineered so its endpoint splits as a product *iff* a guessed digit of Bob's secret is correct. Since products are vanishingly rare, "endpoint is a product" tests the guess with overwhelming reliability — recovering the secret digit-by-digit, after which φ_B is reconstructed directly [9].

The setup needs a small non-scalar endomorphism of *E*₀ — available precisely because SIKE fixed *E*₀: *y*² = *x*³ + 6*x*² + *x*. The Magma code broke the $IKEp182/$IKEp217 challenges in ~4/~6 minutes, SIKEp434 in ~62 minutes, SIKEp751 in ~20 hours — on a *single core* of a 2013 Xeon [2], [11]. Two generalizations followed within weeks: **Maino–Martindale** [4] removed the special-curve dependence (subexponential attack for arbitrary starting curves), and **Robert** [5] gave a *provably* polynomial-time attack via Kani's lemma in dimension 8.

A SageMath sketch of the decision core (after the public reimplementation [12]):



> **Theorem:** (Castryck–Decru, informal). There is a heuristic polynomial-time classical algorithm that, given an SIDH public key with the SIKE starting curve, recovers a generator of ker φ_B, hence the shared secret.

The "heuristic" caveat was analyzed by Wesolowski [9]: a wrong guess essentially never yields a product surface, and auxiliary-degree smoothness heuristics hold in practice; the attack has been independently reimplemented in SageMath [12].

### 4.4 CSIDH: the commutative survivor

The attack needs *torsion-point images*. Protocols that never publish them are not directly affected — notably **CSIDH** (Castryck, Lange, Martindale, Panny, Renes, Asiacrypt 2018) [6]. Over **F**_p_ with *p* = 4·ℓ_1·…·ℓ_n − 1, the ideal class group cl(**Z**[√−*p*]) acts *freely and transitively* on **F**_p_-isomorphism classes of supersingular curves (Waterhouse). Key exchange is Diffie–Hellman in the group-action setting: Alice publishes [*a*]·*E*₀, Bob [*b*]·*E*₀, shared secret [*a*][*b*]·*E*₀ — commutativity from the class group. Public keys are one **F**_p_ element: 64 bytes at the 128-bit level [6]. No torsion images are ever published, so the glue-and-split machinery has nothing to grip [3].

| Property | SIDH/SIKE (broken) | CSIDH | SQIsign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public key | 330 B (p434) | 64 B | 64 B |
| Assumption | Torsion-revealing path problem | Class-group action inversion | Endomorphism-ring path + ZK |
| Post-2022 status | Classically broken [2] | Unbroken; quantum security revised down [13] | NIST additional-sigs round 2 |
| Cost driver | 2-/3-isogeny chains | Many small-ℓ **F**_p_-isogenies | KLPT + ideal→isogeny |

CSIDH's quantum security was revised well below 128 bits for CSIDH-512 (Kuperberg's subexponential dihedral-HSP algorithm; Bonnetain–Schrottenloher analysis [13]), prompting larger SQALE parameters with sublinear Vélu techniques. Classically the best attack remains Õ(*p*^{1/4}) meet-in-the-middle on the class group.

### 4.5 FESTA and SQIsign: building on the ruins

**FESTA** (Basso, Maino, Pope, Asiacrypt 2023) — *Fast Encryption from Supersingular Torsion Attacks* — inverts the relationship: it uses the SIDH attack *as the decryption algorithm* of a trapdoor function [7]. The public key carries torsion images *masked by a secret matrix* *A* ∈ M_b (diagonal or circulant); the trapdoor unmasks them so the decryptor can run glue-and-split recovery. This is delicate: Moriya gave an adaptive attack, follow-ups broke instances of M-SIDH/FESTA via the "lollipop" torsion-point technique [14], and a 2024 analysis revalidated the verification oracle via side channels against the circulant variant [15]. QFESTA (Nakagawa–Onuki, Crypto 2024) reworked parameters with quaternion algebras, halving sizes and using only (2,2)- and 3-isogenies [16].

**SQIsign** (De Feo, Kohel, Leroux, Petit, Wesolowski, Asiacrypt 2020) predates the break and was untouched: it publishes no torsion images [8]. A Fiat–Shamir signature from a zero-knowledge proof of isogeny knowledge, using KLPT to turn quaternion ideals into smooth isogenies: 16-byte secret keys, 64-byte public keys, 204-byte signatures — the most compact post-quantum signature known — with keygen 0.6 s, signing 2.5 s, verification ~42 ms on 3.4 GHz Skylake [8]. It advanced to round 2 of NIST's additional-signatures process; a 2025 pure-Rust implementation with revised parameters reports 148-byte level-1 signatures [17].

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

The SIDH collapse is unusually well-documented because the attackers published code and timings [2], [11]:

| Target | Claimed level | Break time (single core) |
|---|---|---|
| $IKEp182 (Microsoft challenge) | — | ~4 min |
| $IKEp217 ($50,000 challenge) | — | ~5–6 min |
| SIKEp434 | NIST level 1 | ~62 min (later < 1 min) |
| SIKEp751 | NIST level 5 | ~20 h (later < 1 h) |

Post-August-2022 improvements — better Richelot formulas, Oudompheng's direct isogeny-recovery shortcut [12], Robert's dimension-8 polynomial-time variant [5] — collapsed the timings further. MIT's summary timeline: July 30 Castryck–Decru preprint; August 8 Maino–Martindale arbitrary-curve extension; August 10 ANTS XV talks; August 17 Robert's algorithm [3].

> **Proof:** (Why the attack is polynomial-time, heuristic sketch.) Each digit step performs O(1) Richelot (2,2)-isogeny computations, each polynomial in log *p*, plus a Jacobian-vs-product test (Igusa invariants), also polynomial. There are O(*e_B*) = O(log *p*) steps. The initial diamond setup needs an auxiliary smooth-degree isogeny, found via the known endomorphism ring of *E*₀ in heuristic polynomial time (KLPT-style). Total: heuristic polynomial in log *p*. ∎

Measured anchors for the survivors: SQIsign level-1 (keygen 0.6 s / sign 2.5 s / verify 50 ms [8]); CSIDH-512 group action ~100 ms-class variable-time with 64-byte keys [6], [13]; QFESTA roughly halves FESTA's sizes using only (2,2)- and 3-isogenies [16].

## 6. Limitations

1. **SIDH is dead, not wounded.** NIST's postscript: "SIKE and SIDH are insecure and should not be used"; *any* torsion-revealing variant falls to the same attack family [1]. Changing the starting curve does not help [4]; hiding torsion entirely destroys the protocol.
2. **CSIDH's quantum margin is thin.** Subexponential quantum attacks and refined analyses leave CSIDH-512 well short of 128-bit quantum security; conservative parameters are much slower [13].
3. **FESTA's security is fragile.** Matrix masking of torsion points is a subtle, young assumption; adaptive and side-channel-assisted attacks already broke natural variants [14], [15].
4. **SQIsign is slow to sign.** KLPT-based signing (~2.5 s, improved since but still far from lattice microseconds) and the difficulty of constant-time implementation limit deployment [8].
5. **Heuristic foundations.** KLPT and the attack itself are *heuristic* polynomial-time (complete proof only under GRH [9]); provable variants exist [5] but are less practical.
6. **The endomorphism-ring problem.** Computing End(*E*) for random supersingular *E* anchors CSIDH and SQIsign, but its exact classical/quantum complexity is still being mapped, with new connections to lattice geometry in quaternion algebras [18].

## 7. Conclusion

The rise and fall of SIDH is the cleanest natural experiment in post-quantum cryptography. For eleven years, torsion-point public keys looked like an innocuous functionality requirement — the price of commutativity. Kani's 1997 theorem on genus-two Jacobians, with no cryptographic content whatsoever, converted those public keys into a key-recovery oracle. The Castryck–Decru attack did not break the *isogeny path problem*; it broke the *SIDH problem*, the strictly weaker assumption the protocol actually used — a distinction with a $50,000 price tag [2], [3].

The response has been principled. CSIDH had already shown commutativity without torsion revelation via class-group actions over **F**_p_ [6]. SQIsign had shown the Deuring correspondence used *constructively* — quaternion ideals to isogenies for zero-knowledge proofs — without publishing torsion images [8]. FESTA showed the most striking reversal: the attack itself, domesticated by a masking matrix, becomes a decryption algorithm [7], though its security is the least settled.

The durable lesson is a design rule: **every auxiliary value in the public transcript must be justified against the strongest known algorithmic use of that data, not merely against the problem you intended to pose.** SIDH's designers knew torsion points *could* leak — Petit's 2017 torsion-point attacks had shown reduced security for unbalanced parameters — but the leak was judged manageable. It was not. Future isogeny protocols will be built, if at all, on the CSIDH/SQIsign pattern: reveal the curve, hide the torsion, and let the quaternion algebra work in the dark.
