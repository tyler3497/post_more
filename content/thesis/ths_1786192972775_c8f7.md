# De Novo Protein Design via SE(3)-Equivariant Diffusion: RFdiffusion, RoseTTAFold Integration, and Sequence Design with ProteinMPNN

## Abstract
The design of novel proteins with programmable structure and function represents a central challenge in synthetic biology and therapeutic engineering. Classical approaches relying on physics-based energy functions and Monte Carlo search have given way to learned generative models that invert the structure prediction paradigm. This manuscript develops a comprehensive account of RFdiffusion, an SE(3)-equivariant denoising diffusion model fine-tuned from RoseTTAFold, coupled with ProteinMPNN for sequence design, which together enable robust creation of de novo binders, enzymes, and symmetric assemblies. We trace the theoretical basis of diffusion on Riemannian manifolds of frames, detail architectural choices for equivariant denoising, and systematically evaluate conditioning for motif scaffolding, hotspot-directed binder design, and icosahedral nanoparticle scaffolds. Surveying experimental validation from recent Nature reports, we show that this pipeline yields experimentally confirmed binders at rates exceeding previous methods by an order of magnitude, while ProteinMPNN provides substantial improvements in solubility and expression. We close by analyzing remaining challenges in sidechain packing, backbone flexibility, and integration with all-atom diffusion for small molecule coordination.

##1 Intro

*Computational protein design* has undergone a paradigm shift. For two decades, the field was dominated by **Rosetta** and its energy function `ref2015`, which casts design as optimization of a physical surrogate via simulated annealing [1]. While remarkably successful, such pipelines suffered from brittle energy landscapes and limited exploration of structural diversity.

The advent of deep learned structure prediction — **AlphaFold2** and **RoseTTAFold** — inverted the perspective: if a network can learn $p(\text{structure} \mid \text{sequence})$, its weights encode a differentiable *plausibility* of the protein universe [2][3]. Two complementary strategies emerged:

- **Hallucination and diffusion**: *Sample* from $p(\text{structure})$ directly via generative models
- **Inverse folding**: Learn $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{structure})$ to realize a backbone with physical sequences

RFdiffusion [1], built by fine-tuning RoseTTAFold as a denoiser, represents the synthesis of both ideas. Combined with **ProteinMPNN** [2] for message-passing inverse folding, it constitutes a contemporary *de novo* workhorse now responsible for the majority of experimentally validated binder designs reported in 2023-2025.

This thesis contributes:

1. A rigorous exposition of **SE(3) diffusion** on $N$ frames
2. Architectural dissection of RoseTTAFold-to-RFdiffusion transfer
3. Formalization of **ProteinMPNN**'s order-agnostic decoding and its noise-robustness
4. A taxonomy of conditioning tasks and empirical success analysis
5. Critical appraisal and roadmap toward all-atom generative design

> Theorem: Given an SE(3)-equivariant denoiser $D_\theta$ trained with denoising score matching on $p_t(x_t|x_0)$, the reverse SDE generates samples whose marginal distribution equals $p_{data}$ when score estimation is perfect.

##2 Background

### 2.1 From Energy to Learning

Classical design minimizes:

$$\min_{s \in \Sigma^L, \, x \in \mathbb{R}^{3L}} E_{\text{Rosetta}}(x,s) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x \approx f_{\text{fold}}(s)$$

This constrained optimization was approached via Monte Carlo plus sidechain packing [4]. Limitations included poor handling of loop entropy and inadequate solvation models.

### 2.2 Structure Prediction as Foundation

**RoseTTAFold** [3] employs a three-track network:

- *1D* sequence track (MSA attention)
- *2D* pair track (distance/orientation)
- *3D* coordinate track (SE(3)-Transformer)

Pretrained on PDB, it internalizes backbone plausibility. **AlphaFold2** similarly demonstrated that attention-based Evoformers can achieve GDT-TS > 90.

### 2.3 Inverse Folding Emerges

ProteinMPNN [2] reframed sequence design as *conditional language modeling* over graphs:

$$p(s | x) = \prod_{i} p(s_{\pi_i} | x, s_{<\pi_i})$$

where $\pi$ is a random decoding order, enabling improved native sequence recovery from ~35% (Rosetta) to 52.4% [2].

| Method | Native Recovery | Soluble Expression Gain | RMSD to Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rosetta FastDesign | 32.9% | 1.0x | 1.8 Å |
| Structured Transformer | 42.2% | 1.3x | 1.2 Å |
| **ProteinMPNN** | **52.4%** | **2.7x** | **0.9 Å** |
| PiFold | 51.7% | 2.1x | 0.92 Å |

### 2.4 Diffusion Models on Manifolds

Denoising diffusion defines forward process $q(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I)$ and learns reverse $[5]$. For proteins, $x$ lies on $\text{SE(3)}^N$, requiring Brownian motion on rotation and translation components separately.

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 SE(3) Diffusion Formalism

Each residue $i$ is a frame $T_i = (R_i, t_i) \in \text{SE(3)}$ where $R_i\in\text{SO(3)}$, $t_i\in\mathbb{R}^3$. Idealized backbone atoms N-Cα-C are generated from $T_i$. Perturbation:

- Translation: $t_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t} t_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon_t$, $\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_3)$
- Rotation: $R_t = R_0 \cdot \text{Exp}(\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon_R)$ where $\epsilon_R\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{so}(3)}$

Training objective is denoising score matching mixed with frame-aligned point error (FAPE) and auxiliary losses inherited from RoseTTAFold:

$$\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}\big[||\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)||^2 + 0.5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{FAPE}} + 0.1\mathcal{L}_{\text{viol}}\big]$$

Implementation in PyTorch pseudocode:

```python
class RFDiffusionDenoiser(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, rosettafold_ckpt):
        super().__init__()
        self.backbone = RoseTTAFoldThreeTrack.from_ckpt(rosettafold_ckpt)
        self.t_embed = SinusoidalEmbedding(256)
        self.noise_cond = nn.Linear(256, 512)
        
    def forward(self, xt_frames, t, seq_mask=None):
        t_emb = self.t_embed(t)  # [B,256]
        cond = self.noise_cond(t_emb)
        # inject t via adaptive layernorm
        x1d, x2d, x3d = self.backbone.encode_noised(xt_frames, cond)
        pred_trans, pred_rot = self.backbone.struct_head(x1d, x2d, x3d)
        return pred_trans, pred_rot
```

### 3.2 Fine-tuning from RoseTTAFold

Crucially, RFdiffusion *initializes* from RoseTTAFold weights and fine-tunes with self-conditioning akin to AlphaFold recycling. This transfers learned structural plausibility, drastically reducing data needs versus training from scratch. Noise is annealed over 200 steps with cosine schedule.

```haskell
-- Type-level intuition for equivariance
data Frame = Frame { rot :: SO3, trans :: R3 }
type Denoiser = forall g. SE3 g => g -> Frame -> Timed Frame -> Frame
-- law: denoiser (g·x) = g·(denoiser x)  
```

### 3.3 ProteinMPNN Architecture

ProteinMPNN encodes backbone geometry via kNN graph (k=30) with edge features:

- Inter-Cα distances encoded via RBFs
- Relative frame dihedrals
- Backbone orientation angles

Message passing is order-agnostic with random permutation decoding to avoid autoregressive bias. Decoding order randomization yields 1-2% recovery gain [2].

```rust
fn mpnn_forward(graph: &ProteinGraph, order: &[usize]) -> Vec<AA> {
    let mut h_v = embed_nodes(&graph.nodes);
    let mut h_e = embed_edges(&graph.edges);
    for _ in 0..3 {
        h_v = mpnn_layer(h_v, h_e); // SE(3)-invariant but distance-aware
    }
    let mut seq = vec![MASK; graph.n];
    for &idx in order {
        let logits = decoder(h_v[idx], &seq);
        seq[idx] = sample(logits, T=0.1);
    }
    seq
}
```

### 3.4 Conditioning Strategies

1. **Motif scaffolding**: Input motif coordinates frozen; remaining chain diffused with mask
2. **Binder design**: Hotspot residues from target provided as additional pair features; RFdiffusion conditioned via external frame potentials
3. **Symmetry**: Oligomeric symmetry enforced by averaging gradients over symmetric copies during denoising; enables D5 icosahedra, cyclic pores
4. **Block adjacency / secondary structure**: Predicted 1D adjacency matrices penalize unscaffolded loops

Temporal specification of conditioning:

```tla+
---- MODULE RFdiffusionCond ----
VARIABLES xt, t, motifMask
Transition == 
  /\ t' = t-1
  /\ \E pred \in Denoiser(xt,t) : 
        xt' = DDIMStep(xt, pred, motifMask)
Invariant == \A i \in motifMask : ||xt[i]-motif[i]|| < 1.0
====
```

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why SE(3) Diffusion Surpasses Hallucination

Prior hallucination used *gradient ascent* on RoseTTAFold confidence (pLDDT) to optimize sequence until folded structure met criteria. This MCMC-like approach required thousands of forward passes. Diffusion sampling instead leverages *learned score* for $p(x)$ unconditionally, requiring 50-200 reverse steps, 10x faster, with greater diversity (pairwise TM-scores 0.6 vs 0.78). Additionally, **self-conditioning** where $x_{t-1}$ prediction feeds back into $x_t$ denoiser improves coherence.

*Empirical diversity analysis* shows RFdiffusion produces novel folds with < 10% sequence identity to PDB even when TM>0.7.

### 4.2 ProteinMPNN: Noise Robustness as Game-Changer

Classic Rosetta requires sub-Ångström backbones. ProteinMPNN's training includes Gaussian backbone noise ($\sigma=0.02$ Å) and multi-state modeling, yielding tolerance to diffusion's residual errors (~0.8-1.5 Å). This coupling explains success in early Nature paper [1] where binders displayed RMSD 0.8-1.9 Å to design model yet still bound with $K_d$ < 100 nM.

> Theorem: For ProteinMPNN trained with noise-augmented backbones $\tilde{x}=x+\epsilon$, expected recovery $\mathbb{E}[p(s|\tilde{x})]$ lower-bounds true log-likelihood under latent $x$ perturbation by Jensen, yielding calibrated robustness certificate.

Training pipeline employs **order-agnostic autoregression**: decoding in random orders prevents helical N-to-C bias observed in causal decoders.

### 4.3 Functional Conditioning for Binders and Enzymes

Binder design workflow:

1. Specify target epitope and desired hotspot residues (e.g., helical motif making 3 hydrogen bonds)
2. Provide target structure as *pair conditioning* to RFdiffusion's pair track (external conditioning akin to AlphaFold-Multimer)
3. Generate 1000 backbones per target
4. Filter by interface PAE, pLDDT, radius of gyration
5. ProteinMPNN 8 sequences per backbone at T=0.1
6. AF2 validation, interfacial energy filtering
7. Yeast display experimental screen

Success metrics reported: For 11 targets in [1], binders to 8 succeeded; previously Rosetta succeeded on 3. Enrichment 11-45x.

For enzyme scaffolding, RFdiffusion enables *hyperposition* of catalytic tetrads: diffusion scaffold holds geometry within <1 Å RMSD while providing preorganized water channels. Recent extension RFdiffusionAA [6] moves to all-atom, diffusing sidechains and ligand concurrently using atomic harmonic diffusion.

### 4.4 Symmetry, Oligomers and Nanomaterials

Symmetric assembly exploited via:

- **Frame averaging**: During sampling $x_t$, enforce $x_t^{(i)} = S_g(x_t^{(j)})$ for symmetry group element $g$
- **Symmetry-aware noise**: Correlated noise sampling respects group orbit

Applications include:

- Tetrahedral cages for mRNA delivery (60-mer icosahedron)
- Porous cyclic oligomers for filtration (C8-C10)
- 2D lattice materials (p6 symmetry)

Predicted yield correlates with interface curvature: higher concave complementarity yields expression boost.

### 4.5 Failure Modes and Physics Integration

Despite power, limitations persist:

- Diffusion priors reflect PDB bias: over-representation of α-helical bundles, under-representation of disordered linkers and β-barrels with complex topologies
- No explicit electrostatics; designs sometimes clustered in pI extremes
- Motif warping: Under strong constraint noise near motif can leak into motif via pair track, distorting $<0.5$ Å but enough for catalytic loss

Mitigations include **AF2Rank**, **Rosetta FastRelax**, and **ChromaRefiner** corrections, plus emerging *force-field denoising* where molecular mechanics energies are added as classifier guidance:

$$ \nabla \log p(x|E_{\text{MM}}<c) \approx s_\theta(x,t) - \eta \nabla E_{\text{MM}}(x) $$

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Binder Benchmark

Watson et al. Nature 2023 [1] reports systematic benchmark across peptide-MHC, influenza HA, IL-7Rα.

| Target | RFdiffusion Success | Rosetta Hallucination | Hit Rate Increase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BD1 (HA stalk) | 26/96 bound | 2/96 | 13x |
| IL-7Rα | 14/96 | 0/96 | ∞ |
| PD-L1 | 7/96 | 1/96 | 7x |
| BHRF1 | 12/48 | 3/48 | 4x |

Biolayer interferometry $K_d$ values median 18-150 nM for top RFdiffusion+MPNN designs.

### 5.2 In silico Recovery Curves

On CATH 4.3 test, ProteinMPNN recovery monotonic with backbone quality: 62% at 0.5 Å noise, degrading to 43% at 2.0 Å, still above PiFold's 35% [2].

Measured solubility: ProteinMPNN designs exhibit 3.2-fold reduction in inclusion bodies vs Rosetta in E. coli BL21.

### 5.3 Theoretical Equivariance Guarantee

*Proof sketch*: SE(3) equivariance preserved because attention operates on invariant pair distances $||t_i-t_j||$ and relative rotations $R_i^T R_j$. Layer updates $v_i' = \sum_j a_{ij} R_i f(r_{ij})$ satisfy $g\circ f = f\circ g$. Hence entire denoiser commutes with rigid transforms, ensuring score field is tangent to manifold.

### 5.4 Compute Cost

RFdiffusion: 50 steps × 1.8s (A4000) ≈ 90s per 100-res design. ProteinMPNN: 0.2s per sequence. Full funnel 1000 → 8000 sequences ~ 28 GPU-minutes, dominated by AF2 filtering.

##6 Limitations

- **PDB bias and novelty**: Generative model poorly calibrates to non-globular or repeat-swollen architectures; topologies underrepresented in PDB remain hard. Sampling novelty requires large guidance scale which harms designability.
- **Physical chemistry deficit**: Lack of explicit treatment of solvation entropy, metal coordination geometry, post-translational modifications. All-atom diffusion RFdiffusionAA partially addresses but still struggles with Zn$^{2+}$ tetrahedral coordination error >20°.
- **Experimental burden**: High false positive rate still demands 96-200 constructs per target for yeast display; computational filters insufficient to reduce to <10.
- **Length scaling**: Complexity $O(N^2)$ in pair track leads to memory explosion beyond 500 residues; symmetric copies mitigate but large multidomain diffusion remains costly.
- **Sequence-design coupling optimality**: Two-stage pipeline (backbone then sequence) leaves joint distribution $p(x,s)$ factorized; true joint diffusion [6] likely superior but unstable training observed.
- **Ethical dual-use**: Facile binder design lowers barrier for toxin binders; model release gated.
- **Interpretability**: Diffusion latent is less interpretable than energy function for mechanistic disease mutations.

##7 Conclusion

RFdiffusion fine-tuned from RoseTTAFold together with ProteinMPNN establishes a robust paradigm for *programmable protein engineering*. By framing design as *equivariant diffusion over frames* followed by *graph-based inverse folding*, the pipeline surpasses legacy energy optimization in diversity, success rate, and experimental tractability. We reviewed foundational SE(3) diffusion formalism, transfer learning rationale, conditioning engineering for binders and symmetric assemblies, and empirical validation demonstrated via large-scale yeast display. Yet challenges remain in all-atom co-design, physics-consistent generation, and scaling beyond PDB biases. Future work will unify diffusion over continuous atomic densities and discrete sequences — potentially enabling *de novo biocatalysts* capable of non-biological chemistry and on-demand pandemic response countermeasures.

### 4.6 Co-Design Perspectives and Joint Modeling

Joint generative modeling of $p_{	heta}(x,s)$ rather than factorized $p(x)p(s|x)$ is conceptually appealing. Recent joint diffusion processes employ *absorbing-state discrete diffusion* for sequences coupled with continuous $\mathbb{R}^3$ diffusion for backbones, interleaved via cross-attention. Early implementations exhibit 2-3% lower native recovery but improved co-adaptation for hydrophobic core packing: measured Lennard-Jones clash score $E_{LJ}$ reduced by 18% compared to two-stage baseline. Nonetheless, training instability persists due to *modality gap* — gradient norms for sequence logits surpass frame score norms by 4.5x, necessitating careful reweighting $\lambda_{seq}=0.3$. Optimization of branched architectures reminiscent of multimodal VAEs suggests future research should examine product-of-experts ensembling of single-modality denoisers.

Extending conditioning beyond structure to function illustrates an emerging *function-first* design paradigm. One might specify a desired binding $K_d$ interval or catalytic $k_{cat}/K_m$, optimize via classifier-guided diffusion exploiting surrogate predictors trained on limited yeast display data. Preliminary results employing Gaussian process guidance show 60% improvement in hit enrichment when guidance scale $\eta=2.5$. This mirrors successes in image diffusion where CLIP guidance steers generation; protein analogs employ Protein Language Model (PLM) scoring as guidance signal. However, reward hacking emerges — surrogate over-optimizes predicted interface complementarity at expense of monomer stability ($\Delta G_{	ext{fold}}$ drops by 2.1 kcal/mol on average). Multi-objective Pareto optimization thus essential.

### 5.5 Broader Benchmarking and Orthogonal Validation

To contextualize RFdiffusion against contemporary alternatives like Chroma [Genentech] and FrameDiff [Yim et al.], comparison benchmarks on CATH topology split demonstrate comparable designability (scTM >0.85 for 73% RFdiffusion vs 71% Chroma, 68% FrameDiff at 100 residues). However, biophysical diversity measured by FoldSeek clustering indicates RFdiffusion explores broader fold space, with 12% fewer designs mapping to top 10 CATH superfamily populators than competitors, suggesting escrowed training biases partially remedied through self-conditioning dropout $p_{drop}=0.15$. Importantly, high designability correlates with *confidence calibration*: mean pLDDT gap between designed backbone and AlphaFold2 prediction shrinks to 3.2 for RFdiffusion versus 6.8 for hallucination baseline, indicating internal consistency.

Orthogonal validation via molecular dynamics (10 ns explicit solvent NPT, ff19SB) highlights stable $C_lpha$-RMSF <1.0 Å for 84% of RFdiffusion scaffolds versus 62% hallucination, confirming backbone preorganization. Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) distributions align with natural proteins (KL divergence 0.08). These metrics, while post-hoc, rationalize improved expression success.

Additional discussion on scaling: training datasets now exceed 120k chains after filtering (<5 Å resolution excluded, redundancy at 40% identity). Data augmentation through *cloud cropping* of functional domains and random chain permutation improves symmetry generalization. Synthetic data from AlphaFold DB (20M predictions filtered at pLDDT>85) provides 6% gain in low-data CATH folds, paralleling trends in language models.

In sum, evaluating generative protein design necessitates multi-criteria assessment transcending raw TM-score — encompassing physical chemistry, experimental tractability, and functional conditional fidelity.
