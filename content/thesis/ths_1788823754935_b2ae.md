# Combinatorial Auctions and Mechanism Design: VCG Payments, Core-Selecting Auctions, Winner Determination Complexity, and Spectrum Auction Practice

## Abstract

Combinatorial auctions sell heterogeneous goods in packages, letting bidders express complements and substitutes. This thesis develops the full arc of modern combinatorial auction design. We present the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism with the Clarke pivot rule, prove dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality, and show the Clarke payment equals each bidder's externality on declared welfare [1][2]. We then examine VCG's failures: outcomes can lie outside the core, enabling shill bidding and zero-revenue equilibria unsuitable for public sales [3][4]. We analyze bidding languages (OR, XOR, succinctness trade-offs), prove winner determination NP-hard and inapproximable via weighted set packing, and describe optimal search algorithms — Sandholm's branch-and-bound and CABOB [5][6][7]. We present Day and Milgrom's core-selecting auctions: bidder-optimal core allocations that minimize deviation incentives and reproduce Vickrey outcomes exactly when those lie in the core [8][9]. Finally, we study the combinatorial clock auction — clock stage, supplementary bids, revealed-preference activity rules, second-price core pricing — and its FCC and European spectrum deployments [10][11]. Benchmarks show exact solvers handling hundreds of bids in milliseconds, with CCAs achieving efficiencies above 95%.

## 1. Introduction

An auction for spectrum licenses, airport slots, or freight routes is not a set of independent markets: a bidder's value for *license A alone* plus *license B alone* may be dwarfed by its value for the *package* $\{A, B\}$, or collapse to zero if one arrives without the other. Selling each item separately exposes bidders to the *exposure problem* — gambling on winning complementary goods in parallel contests, possibly paying for a subset worthless without its mates. The combinatorial auction answers with a single sale in which bids name *bundles*, and it has become the workhorse of spectrum allocation worldwide [10].

Expressiveness has a price, in three interlocking difficulties:

1. **Incentives.** The natural generalization of the second-price auction is the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism: dominant-strategy truthful and efficient [1]. Yet VCG is fragile where it matters — it can hand the seller *zero revenue*, invites *shill bidding*, and can reward excluding qualified bidders [8]. Trading incentive strength for robustness is the theme of §4.4.
2. **Representation.** Bidders must communicate valuations over $2^m$ bundles. Bidding languages — OR, XOR, and richer formalisms — decide which valuations can be stated compactly [12].
3. **Computation.** Winner determination (choosing the welfare-maximizing allocation) is NP-hard and essentially inapproximable [5][6]: the designer must run exponential search, sacrifice truthfulness to approximation [11], or restrict the domain.

This thesis treats these as one story: §2 reviews the model, §3 the methodology, §4 the five deep dives, §5 the evidence, §6 the limitations, §7 the conclusion.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Model and notation

Let $M = \{1, \dots, m\}$ be indivisible items and $N = \{1, \dots, n\}$ bidders. Each bidder $i$ has a monotone normalized valuation $v_i : 2^M \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$. An *allocation* $X = (X_1, \dots, X_n)$ partitions (a subset of) $M$; its *social welfare* is $\sum_i v_i(X_i)$, maximized by the efficient allocation.

A *direct revelation mechanism* is a pair $(f, p)$: allocation rule $f$ mapping reported profiles $\mathbf{b} = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ to allocations, and payment rule $p$ mapping reports to transfers. Bidder $i$'s quasilinear utility is $u_i(\mathbf{b}, v_i) = v_i(f(\mathbf{b})) - p_i(\mathbf{b})$. The mechanism is *dominant-strategy incentive compatible* if truth-telling maximizes $u_i$ regardless of others' reports; *individually rational* if truthful participation yields $u_i \geq 0$; *efficient* if $f(\mathbf{v})$ maximizes welfare under truthful reports.

### 2.2 From single items to packages

The single-item second-price auction charges the winner the *externality* it imposes on others — the welfare they forgo. VCG generalizes exactly this: each bidder pays the loss of *declared* welfare its presence inflicts on everyone else [2].

### 2.3 The core

Given reports $\mathbf{b}$, the *coalitional value* of $S \subseteq N$ is $w(S) = \max_X \sum_{i \in S} b_i(X_i)$. An imputation lies in the *core* if no coalition can do better alone: $\sum_{i \in S} \pi_i \geq w(S)$ for all $S$, with $\sum_i \pi_i = w(N)$. The core means no subset of bidders and the seller has a mutually beneficial post-auction renegotiation — a practical desideratum for public sales [8][9].

> **Theorem:** The Vickrey outcome need not lie in the core. There exist profiles at which VCG payments leave the seller with revenue strictly below what some coalition would jointly offer — the VCG imputation is *blocked* [4].

## 3. Methodology

Four traditions combine here:

1. **Mechanism design proofs.** Direct revelation analysis: under VCG each bidder's utility is affine in *declared welfare*, so truth-telling maximizes the bidder's own objective — the canonical Groves argument [1][2].
2. **Complexity reductions.** Winner determination is NP-hard by reduction from *weighted set packing*; inapproximability follows from set packing's hardness [5].
3. **Algorithm engineering.** Exact search (IDA\*, branch-and-bound with LP bounds, decomposition, preprocessing), benchmarked against CPLEX [6][7].
4. **Experimental and field evidence.** Laboratory CCA experiments and FCC/European spectrum revenue data anchor mechanism choice [10][11].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The VCG Mechanism and the Clarke Pivot Rule

The *Groves* template internalizes the social objective. Fix pivot functions $h_i(b_{-i})$ depending only on others' reports:

$$f(\mathbf{b}) \in \arg\max_{X} \sum_j b_j(X_j), \qquad p_i(\mathbf{b}) = h_i(b_{-i}) - \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(f(\mathbf{b})).$$

Bidder $i$'s utility is $v_i(f(\mathbf{b})) + \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(f(\mathbf{b})) - h_i(b_{-i})$. Since $h_i$ is constant in $b_i$, the bidder's report only steers $f$ toward maximizing *true* welfare $v_i + \sum_{j \neq i} b_j$ — achieved exactly by reporting $b_i = v_i$. Truth-telling is therefore dominant: the mechanism makes each agent want to maximize what the designer wants to maximize [1].

The **Clarke pivot rule** sets $h_i(b_{-i}) = \max_X \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(X_j)$, the maximum declared welfare achievable *without* $i$:

$$p_i(\mathbf{b}) = \underbrace{\max_X \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(X_j)}_{\text{others' welfare if } i \text{ absent}} - \underbrace{\sum_{j \neq i} b_j(f(\mathbf{b}))}_{\text{others' welfare with } i \text{ present}},$$

the *externality* $i$ imposes [2]:

![VCG payment flow diagram](public/thesis/ths_1788823754935_b2ae-0.webp)

> **Theorem (Vickrey–Clarke–Groves):** VCG with the Clarke pivot rule is dominant-strategy incentive compatible. If $v_i(X) \geq 0$ for all $i, X$, it entails no positive transfers and is individually rational [2].

*Proof sketch.* Let $x^* = f(v_i, b_{-i})$. Then $u_i((v_i, b_{-i}), v_i) = v_i(x^*) + \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(x^*) - \max_X \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(X) \geq u_i((b_i, b_{-i}), v_i)$ for any $b_i$, because $x^*$ maximizes $v_i(x) + \sum_{j \neq i} b_j(x)$ by definition of $f$. Payments are nonnegative since the without-$i$ optimum weakly dominates any with-$i$ welfare; individual rationality follows because truth-telling beats the exclusion allocation (value zero) [2]. ∎

**Vulnerabilities.** The mechanism must solve winner determination exactly $n+1$ times — once overall, once per winner with that winner's bids removed; dropping merely one winning bid breaks truthfulness [3]. Approximate winner determination can *destroy* incentive compatibility entirely [3][11]. Strategically, shill bidding — splitting into identities $1x, 1y$ — can win each item at *zero* VCG payment, collapsing revenue and efficiency [4]. And VCG can yield arbitrarily low revenue while incentivizing the seller to exclude qualified bidders [8].

### 4.2 Bidding Languages: OR, XOR, and Succinctness

A bidding language must express valuations over $2^m$ bundles without enumeration. Two canonical atomic-bid languages [12]:

| Language | Semantics | Expressiveness |
|---|---|---|
| **OR bids** | Bidder may win *any number* of atomic bids $(S_j, p_j)$; value is the sum | Cannot express substitutes |
| **XOR bids** | Bidder wins *at most one* atomic bid; value is the max accepted | Fully expressive (worst-case exponential) |
| **OR\*** (dummy items) | OR bids plus phantom items simulating XOR | Fully expressive, often polynomial overhead |

The *exposure problem* arises under additive single-item bidding: a bidder needing $\{A,B\}$ must shade and gamble. XOR bidding eliminates exposure but forces the auctioneer to enforce at-most-one constraints — one more wrinkle in winner determination.

```python
# Winner determination = weighted set-packing integer program
# x_j in {0,1}: accept bid j = (bundle S_j, price p_j)?
# max sum_j p_j * x_j
# s.t. sum_{j: i in S_j} x_j <= 1  for each item i
#      sum_{j in XORgroup_k} x_j <= 1  per bidder k   (XOR bids)
# NP-hard: reduces from weighted set packing [5]
```

### 4.3 Winner Determination: NP-Hardness and Optimal Search

**Definition (Winner Determination).** Given bids $\mathcal{B} = \{(S_j, p_j)\}$, find pairwise-disjoint bids maximizing $\sum p_j$.

> **Theorem:** Winner determination is NP-complete and, in general, inapproximable to within any reasonable factor [5][6].

*Reduction.* Weighted set packing — max-weight disjoint subcollection of sets — *is* winner determination with one bid per set. Set packing's NP-hardness and inapproximability transfer directly [5][6]. The inapproximability is operationally devastating: approximate winner determination *destroys* VCG truthfulness [3][11].

Exact algorithms invest in search engineering [6][7]:

1. **Sandholm's IDA\*/branch-and-bound (2002).** Bid-ordered depth-first search with bound $f = g + h(F)$ ($g$: revenue on path; $h(F)$: optimistic bound on unallocated items), converting to branch-and-bound after the first solution; preprocessors PRE1–PRE4 prune dominated bids [7].
2. **CABOB (Sandholm, Suri, Gilpin, Levine, 2001).** Branch-on-bids with *decomposition* of the bid-conflict graph into independently solved components; LP/dual solutions supply bounds, bid orderings, and special-case detection. Typically faster than CPLEX 7.0, sometimes drastically [6].
3. **Tractable special cases.** Bounded-treewidth conflict graphs or laminar (hierarchical) bid families admit polynomial dynamic programming — relevant for geographically nested spectrum lots.

![Branch-and-bound search tree for winner determination](public/thesis/ths_1788823754935_b2ae-3.webp)

On structured distributions with hundreds of bids, optimal search terminates in milliseconds to seconds; pathological instances stay exponential — which is why real auctions (e.g., the CCA) *cap* packages or use clock phases to shrink the bid set before exact optimization [10].

### 4.4 Core-Selecting Auctions: The Day–Cramton–Milgrom Program

VCG's indictment — zero-revenue outcomes, shill vulnerability, exclusion incentives — motivated Day and Milgrom's *core-selecting auctions* [8]: direct mechanisms always selecting a *core allocation* w.r.t. reported values, so no coalition of losers can jointly offer the seller a better deal [9].

> **Theorem (Day–Milgrom):** When the Vickrey outcome lies in the core, truthful reporting is an ex post equilibrium of *any* bidder-optimal core-selecting auction, reproducing Vickrey payoffs. Among core-selecting auctions, *bidder-optimal* core allocations minimize the maximum incentive to deviate from truth-telling at every profile [8].

The design space collapses to one extremal principle: *minimize seller revenue subject to core constraints* — the **minimum-revenue core** (MRC), i.e., the bidder-optimal core point. Three further virtues [8][9]:

- **Revenue monotonicity.** MRC revenue is nondecreasing in bids — no perverse qualification-stage distortions.
- **No exclusion incentive.** The seller never gains by excluding qualified bidders.
- **Shill resistance.** Core constraints price out the shill attacks that collapse VCG revenue [9].

Geometrically, the core is a polytope over $2^n$ coalitional constraints; the Vickrey vector may lie *outside* it, and MRC projects onto the bidder-optimal face:

![Core polytope vs VCG outcome geometry](public/thesis/ths_1788823754935_b2ae-1.webp)

Computing MRC prices is a linear program with exponentially many core constraints, solved by constraint generation: repeatedly run winner determination as a separation oracle to find the most violated coalition, add its constraint, re-optimize [8]. The truthfulness loss is bounded and *structured*: every bidder has a best reply in *truncation strategies*, and every bidder-optimal core imputation is a full-information Nash equilibrium of truncation reports [9] — dominant strategies traded for equilibrium truthfulness plus robustness, the defining bargain of applied mechanism design.

### 4.5 The Combinatorial Clock Auction and Spectrum Practice

The **combinatorial clock auction (CCA)** of Ausubel, Cramton, and Milgrom operationalizes core-selecting package pricing dynamically; it has been adopted by the UK (Ofcom 2008, 2013), Canada, Australia, and European regulators [10]:

1. **Clock stage.** The auctioneer posts per-product prices; each bidder names its preferred *package*. Prices rise on over-demanded products until demand clears — pure *price discovery* before commitment.
2. **Supplementary round.** Sealed bids improving clock bids or naming new packages, constrained by a **revealed-preference activity rule**: a supplementary bid must be consistent with the bidder's clock behavior, killing "snake in the grass" late-strike strategies [10].
3. **Pricing.** Winner determination over *all* bids; prices follow a **second-price, core-selecting rule** — minimum-revenue core w.r.t. final bids (Vickrey prices projected into the core when outside it).

![Combinatorial clock auction round dynamics](public/thesis/ths_1788823754935_b2ae-2.webp)

The CCA keeps VCG's spirit — package bids, second-price logic, encouraged truthfulness — while removing its pathologies: the core pricing floor guarantees competitive revenues, the activity rule disciplines gaming, and the clock stage tames the cognitive and computational complexity that laboratory studies flagged as the main barrier to combinatorial auctions [10][11]. FCC practice ran on a parallel track (simultaneous ascending auctions since 1994, later absorbing clock-proxy logic), and together these designs constitute the most successful large-scale deployment of algorithmic mechanism design in history [10].

## 5. Empirical Results and Benchmarks

### 5.1 Exact winner-determination performance

CABOB vs. CPLEX 7.0 on standard distributions (weighted random, uniform, decay, CATS suites) [6]:

| Instance class | Bids | Items | CABOB (median) | CPLEX 7.0 (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weighted random, sparse | 200 | 40 | 0.02 s | 0.4 s |
| Uniform decay | 500 | 50 | 0.3 s | 2.1 s |
| CATS regions | 1,000 | 100 | 1.8 s | 9.6 s |
| Pathological dense | 150 | 30 | 41 s | 380 s |

Decomposition dominates: on splittable conflict graphs, per-component bounding prunes exponentially, and LP-based upper bounding beat all earlier handcrafted bounds in ablation studies [6][7].

### 5.2 Approximation and truthfulness

Exact VCG needs $n+1$ optimal solves, and approximation breaks truthfulness [3] — so the literature studies *maximal-in-range* and randomized truthful approximations. Truthful-in-expectation mechanisms strictly dominate universally truthful ones in power, with an FPTAS separating them in multi-parameter settings [11]. Spectrum practice chose exactness instead: exact optimization over capped bid sets beat approximate optimization over full ones [10].

### 5.3 Laboratory and field evidence for the CCA

- Lab CCAs beat simultaneous ascending auctions by 5–15 efficiency points in high-complementarity treatments; the supplementary round captures most residual clock-stage inefficiency [10][11].
- Ofcom's 2008/2013 awards and later European CCAs raised competitive revenues at near-complete allocation; replications report efficiency routinely above 95% [10].
- The core pricing floor matters quantitatively: where Vickrey prices collapse (shill-like or thin-demand profiles), MRC restores 70–90% of the efficient benchmark revenue while preserving the allocation [8][9].

### 5.4 The shill-bidding demonstration

Milgrom's canonical example [4]: bidder 2 values package $AB$ at \$200; bidder 1 splits into $1x$ bidding \$201 on $A$ and $1y$ bidding \$201 on $B$. Under VCG, $1x$ and $1y$ win paying *zero* — each is non-pivotal given the other — so bidder 1 takes both goods free while the efficient allocation gives $AB$ to bidder 2. Any core-selecting rule blocks this: coalition $\{2, \text{seller}\}$ offers \$200, forcing covering payments [9].

## 6. Limitations

1. **No dominant strategies.** Core-selecting auctions trade VCG's dominant-strategy truthfulness for Nash/truncation-equilibrium incentives [9]; in thin or asymmetric settings bidders may still shade.
2. **Computational burden persists.** MRC pricing solves winner determination repeatedly inside constraint generation [8]; adversarial bid sets can strain exact solvers despite the CCA's clock-stage cap.
3. **Budget constraints break the theory.** The core-incentive theorems do not extend to binding bidder budgets — and real spectrum bidders are capital-constrained [8].
4. **Residual exposure.** The clock stage reveals prices, but bidders whose optimal package shifts discontinuously can face leftover exposure in the supplementary round [10].
5. **Collusion.** Core constraints defend against *post-auction* renegotiation, not pre-auction bidder rings; spectrum auctions needed separate anti-collusion rules and spectrum caps.
6. **Inapproximability wall.** No polynomial mechanism can be exactly truthful and approximately efficient in the worst case for general valuations [11] — every practical design is a domain restriction, an equilibrium concept, or both.

## 7. Conclusion

The arc from VCG to the combinatorial clock auction is mechanism design's clearest case of theory disciplined by practice. VCG proved efficiency and dominant-strategy truthfulness jointly achievable — then revealed, through zero-revenue equilibria and shill vulnerability, that public sales need *coalitional stability* too [1][2][4]. Bidding languages and winner-determination complexity made computation a first-class design constraint [5][6][7][12]. Day and Milgrom's core-selecting auctions supplied the synthesis: keep the efficient allocation, price at the bidder-optimal core point, accept equilibrium rather than dominant-strategy incentives for revenue robustness [8][9]. The CCA turned that synthesis into a dynamic, learnable format whose revealed-preference activity rules and second-price core pricing now allocate the world's most valuable invisible infrastructure [10]. Budget constraints, collusion, and the inapproximability wall define the next generation of market design.
