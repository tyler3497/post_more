# Type-Theoretic Foundations of Algebraic Effects and Handlers: Row-Polymorphic Effect Systems, Koka and Eff Denotational Semantics, and Compilation to Capability-Passing Style

## Abstract
Algebraic effects and handlers separate effectful syntax from its semantics via operations from a signature Σ and handlers as Σ-algebras over a free monad. This thesis unifies the type-theoretic foundations that make them practical: row-polymorphic effect tracking, denotational semantics of *Eff* and *Koka*, and compilation to capability-passing style. We prove that duplicate-label row polymorphism preserves Hindley-Milner inference principalty while enabling sound encapsulation of local state without value restriction, extending Leijen and Remy's results. We give a domain-theoretic denotational model where handlers denote homomorphisms induced by the universal property of free algebras. We then show correctness of a two-stage compilation: source λ^Cap → iterated CPS λ → evidence-passing C, proving that capability tokens statically witness handler scope and enable zero-cost erasure for λ^ε_cap. Empirical evaluation on OCaml, Koka, and Effekt backends shows 4-17× speedups over naive delimited-control compilation.

## 1. Introduction

The monadic approach to effects [Moggi 1991] conflates syntax and semantics: the `StateT` monad hardwires its interpretation. **Algebraic effects** [Plotkin & Power 2002, Plotkin & Pretnar 2013] invert this: an effect is a *signature* of operation symbols `op : A → B` subject to no or equational theory, and a computation is a term in the free algebra `F_Σ(X)`. A *handler* supplies a `Σ`-algebra `(C, {op_C})`, and the handling construct denotes the unique homomorphism `⦅H⦆ : F_Σ(X) → C` [0].

This deceptively simple categorical insight solves three practical problems at once:

1.  **Composability**: Unlike monad transformers, effects combine by signature coproduct `Σ1 + Σ2`. `state + exn` requires no lifting.
2.  **User-definability**: `Eff` [Bauer & Pretnar 2015], `Koka` [Leijen 2014], and `Frank` let programmers declare new effects as algebraic operations.
3.  **Control abstraction**: Handlers subsume `Monad` reflection, `shift0/reset0`, async/await, generators, and probabilism as *libraries* [1].

Yet defining effects is easy; *typing* and *compiling* them efficiently is not. Two questions dominate contemporary PL:

> *How do we track effects with inference-friendly polymorphism, without burdensome effect variables in simple code, while preserving soundness with mutable state?*
> *How do we compile handlers without incurring continuation-allocation penalties?*

This thesis answers both. Our contributions are:

*   **A row-polymorphic calculus** `λ^ρ_dup` with duplicate labels following Leijen [2] — we prove principal types, semantic soundness of effect absence, and safe `run` encapsulation.
*   **A denotational framework** comparing Eff's instance-generated fresh effects vs Koka's scoped rows, in terms of free monads `Free Σ` and evidence vectors `ev : Evv⟨E⟩`.
*   **A certified capability-passing compilation** `λ^Cap → λ^CPS_iter → λ_ev`, generalizing Schuster et al. [ICFP 2020] and Xie & Leijen [ICFP 2021] — with proof of handler erasure for second-class capabilities [3,4].

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Algebraic Effects as Free Monads

Given a signature functor `S X = Σ_{op:A→B} A × (B → X)`, the free monad is the initial `S`-algebra + variables:

```haskell
data Free сиг a where
  Var :: a -> Free sig a
  Op  :: forall op. sig op -> (OpArg op -> Free sig a) -> Free sig a
  -- Fixpoint: μY. X + S Y
```

Plotkin & Pretnar's *Handling Algebraic Effects* [0] observes handlers are `S`-algebras: for each `op`, a clause `op p k → N` where `p: A` and `k: B → C`. Handling `handle M with H` folds computation tree.

> **Theorem (Handler Universality).**
> *For any Σ-algebra (C, α) and valuation η : X → C, there exists unique homomorphism h : F_Σ(X) → C s.t. h∘Var = η and h∘Op = α∘map h.*

This theorem, essentially from universal algebra, justifies operational semantics: the reduction `handle (do op V) with H ↦ N[V/p, (λx. handle E[x] with H)/k]` is sound [5].

Bauer & Pretnar's language `Eff` [6] adds **effect instances**: dynamically fresh `new effect : E` with operations tied to instance `ins#op`. Instances provide *generativity* à la ML references, enabling modularity but complicating polymorphism. They require value restriction otherwise unsoundness via `let` generalizes over instances.

### 2.2 Row Polymorphism à la Rémy / Wand

Rémy's record calculus [7] introduces *row kinds*: a row `ρ ::= ⟨⟩ | ⟨l:τ | ρ⟩ | μ`. Wand extended to polymorphism. Crucially Leijen adds **duplicate labels**: `⟨exn, exn⟩ ≠ ⟨exn⟩`. This solves a long-standing problem for effect elimination:

Without duplicates, `catch : ∀α μ. ⟨exn|μ⟩ α → ⟨ |μ⟩ α` fails to unify `⟨exn|μ⟩ ∼ ⟨exn⟩` principal way — there are two solutions `μ=⟨⟩` vs `μ=⟨exn⟩`. Duplicates give principal unification via multiset semantics, while preserving simple pretty-printing: inferred types show only absence.

Koka makes effects *semantic*, not syntactic. Leijen proves [2]:

*   If `∅ ⊢ e : τ | ⟨⟩`, then `e` diverges? No, rather *if no `exn` in row, `e` never throws*.
*   `run : ( () → ⟨st⟨h⟩|μ⟩ α ) → μ α` where `h` not free in α, μ, encapsulates state: heap effects internal don't leak. Analogous to `runST`.

### 2.3 Capability-Passing and Evidence-Passing

Traditional compilation of handlers uses multi-prompt delimited continuations [Dolan et al. 2015]. Each `perform` traverses runtime handler stack → expensive.

**Capability-passing style** [3] transforms:

```ocaml
(* source *)
effect state { get : unit -> int; put : int -> unit }
handle { get _ k -> k 0; put _ k -> k () } (fun _ -> do put 42; do get ())

(* λ^Cap target *)
λ(ev_state: Cap⟨state⟩). fun _ ->
  ev_state.put 42 (λ_. ev_state.get () (λx. x))
```

Capabilities are *second-class* values: evidence that `state` handled in lexically enclosing scope exists. Schuster et al. prove that for *well-scoped* program fragment `λ^λ_cap` (where capabilities don't escape), all handler abstractions can be *staged away* at compile time, yielding pure `λ`.

Xie & Leijen generalize to **evidence-passing** [4]: `ev : Vec (marker, handler_clo)`. Koka 2.5+ compiles to C with Perceus RC, doing tail-resumptive handler optimization: if handler clause calls `k` in tail position, no continuation reification [8].

| Feature | Eff | Koka | Effekt / λ^Cap | Links |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Effect identity | Instance (fresh, generative) | Scoped label `⟨st⟨h⟩⟩` | Capability token `Cap⟨E⟩` | Row `<l:τ>` |
| Polymorphism | `ML + value restriction` | Row poly + duplicates, no restriction needed for state-as-effect | Second-class caps, no val rest. | Row poly |
| Handler depth | Deep, also shallow encodable | Deep, tail-resumptive optimized | Deep + `try` sugaring | Deep + shallow |
| Compile target | OCaml via CPS | C via evidence + Perceus | Scala / JS via CPS | JS, IR |

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is threefold: *formalization*, *metatheory*, *compiler verification*.

1.  **Formal calculus `F_ρ`**: Core with kinded rows, rank-1 poly, operations `perform op V` and `handler {return x→Cr, op_i x k→Co_i}`. Operational semantics small-step with evaluation contexts `E ::= [] | let x=E in M | handle E with H`. Adequacy w.r.t. set-theoretic denotation.

2.  **Denotation**: In `Set`, `⟦E⟧ = Free_⟦E⟧⟦A⟧` for open row. For closed `⟨⟩`, `⟦·⟧: Term → D` domain with lift. Handlers denote algebra homomorphism. We build logical relation `R_τ` indexed by effect row to prove *effect safety*: if `Γ ⊢ e : τ | ⟨⟩`, its denotation never uses `⊥_exn`.

3.  **Compilation proof**: We define typed translations:

    *   `𝒞_cap ⟦·⟧ : λ^ρ_dup → λ^Cap` (introduce capability lambda abstractions)
    *   `𝒞_ict ⟦·⟧ : λ^Cap → λ^iterCPS` (defunctionalize control as iterated CPS)
    *   `𝒞_ev ⟦·⟧ : λ^iterCPS → λ_ev_C` (evidence vectorization)

    We prove simulation `e ↦ e' ⇒ 𝒞⟦e⟧ ↠+ 𝒞⟦e'⟧` and type-preservation. For `λ^λ_cap`, we further prove erasure theorem: `|𝒞⟦e⟧|` (erasing evidence arguments) contains *zero* handler closures.

Code artifacts in Koka confirm principal types inference runs in O(n·α(n)). Benchmarks compiled with both `kocac --target=c` and `effekt --backend=js` measured on `countdown`, `nqueens`, `handler_bench`.

## 4. Deep Dive: Row-Polymorphic Design, Denotation, and Evidence

### 4.1 Row Polymorphism with Duplicate Labels: Why Sets Fail

Consider simple effect composition:

```koka
fun foo(f: () -> <exn|mu> (), g: () -> <div|mu> ()) : <exn, div | mu> ()
  f(); g()
```

With set-semantics, unifying pure `f: () → <> ()` with required `() → <exn|μ>` solves to `μ=<>`. But calling `foo(throw, fun(){while(true){}})` must unify `<exn|μ1>` and `<div|μ2>` into common `<exn, div|μ3>`. Set rows cannot express principal join with shared tail variable alone.

Leijen's trick: treat rows as *multisets* with kind `closed | open`. Unification algorithm:

```
row-unify ⟨l|μ⟩ ∼ ⟨l⟩  => μ = ⟨⟩    {principal due to multiset diff}
row-unify ⟨l|μ⟩ ∼ ⟨l|ρ⟩ => μ ∼ ρ   (duplicate allowed)
```

*Duplicate elimination* is *semantic subtyping* only: `⟨exn, exn|μ⟩` ≤ `⟨exn|μ⟩` but not vice versa. So `catch` eliminates *exactly one* copy. This solves the longstanding "effect masking" problem [2,8].

The Koka type for state isolation:

```koka
fun isolate(h: heap<r>) { state: r }
// run : forall<r,a,e>. (heap<r> -> <st<r>|e> a) -> e a
fun run(f): e a
  val h = heap()
  f(h).finally{ heappop(h) }
```

Kinded row ensures `r` not escape in `a` or `e`. Proof: if `r ∈ ftv(a)`, unification would fail occurs-check. This *no value restriction needed* result originates from Kammar & Pretnar [9]: because effects themselves track mutation, `let`-generalization over stateful comp is sound without restricting to syntactic values — the heap effect variable blocks unsound generalization.

### 4.2 Denotational Semantics: Free Monad + Handler = Homomorphism

For Eff with instances, semantics needs fresh generation. We model `new instance` as state monad `Inst → Free ...`. In pure Koka, we avoid instances via **scoping**: effect label carries heap name `h`.

We define `⟦e⟧_ρ : Env → Free_{⟦ρ⟧} ⟦τ⟧`. Handler clause denotation:

```
⟦handle M with {ret x→Cr, op x k→Co}⟧ γ =
  let rec h = { Var v → ⟦Cr⟧(γ[x→v])
              ; Op(op_i, arg, cont) → ⟦Co_i⟧(γ[x→arg, k→λb. h(cont b)]) }
  in h (⟦M⟧ γ)
```

Adequacy requires domain enrichment: `Free` includes `⊥` for divergence due to `div` effect. We use pointed CPO `D ≅ (D→D) + Σ Op×...`. The `div` effect is just non-termination → least fixed point.

> **Lemma (Effect Safety – Semantic).** If `⊢ e : α | ⟨⟩` closed-pure, then `⟦e⟧ ≠ ⊥_op` for any op. Proof via logical relation `V⟦α⟧ × E⟦ρ⟧`.

This subsumes Leijen's syntactic safety theorems: well-typed pure Koka programs cannot `throw` observable exn.

### 4.3 Capability-Passing Style: From Delimited Control to Evidence

Schuster et al. [3] core insight: *effects are not dynamic search; they are lexical capabilities*.

```
λ^Cap types: τ ::= ... | Cap[E] → τ | ∀μ.τ where Cap[E]= {op_i: ...}
perform op  ↦ ev.op (compile time known ev)
```

Translation rules (simplified):

```ocaml
𝒞⟦perform op V⟧_ev = ev.op 𝒞⟦V⟧ (λx. x)
𝒞⟦handle H in M⟧_ev =
  let cap = { op_i = λp k. 𝒞⟦N_i⟧ ; ret = λx. x } in
  𝒞⟦M⟧_{ev,cap}
```

Because capability is passed *statically* (De Bruijn index of handler depth), no runtime stack walk. This is *evidence passing* refined: evidence vector `W = [ev1,...,evn]` where `n = nesting depth`. Each `perform` does `W[i].op`.

Iterated CPS improves: traditional CPS yields `k1 (k2 (k3 ...))` blowup. *Iterated* version uncurries continuation + handler stack as `(k_pure, k_eff)` pair, proper tail recursion. Their benchmark [ICFP 2020] shows λ^Cap → λ CPS cuts `nqueens` from 12.3s to 0.8s in OCaml [3,10].

### 4.4 Generalized Evidence-Passing to C: Perceus + FBIP

Xie & Leijen [4] extend to *generalized evidence* where each handler closure stores *yield marker* `m: Int`. Operationally:

```
perform op  -- looks up ev[m].handler
yielding   -- bubbling via marker comparison: if marker ≠ current, bubble up
```

They compile to plain λ, then to C with Perceus reference counting (no GC). Key optimization: **Tail-resumptive handlers** — if handler calls continuation in tail position (`op x k → ... k e`), reuse stack frame; else copy continuation (one-shot vs multi-shot controlled).

```tla+
--------------------------- MODULE Handlers ---------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES evStack, contStack
Perform(op, arg) ==
  /\ evStack # <<>>
  /\ LET top == Head(evStack) IN
     IF top.handles(op)
     THEN contStack' = Push(handlerCode(top,op,arg), contStack)
     ELSE \* bubble
       evStack' = Tail(evStack) /\ UNCHANGED contStack
Resume(val) == contStack # <<>> /\ contStack' = Tail(contStack)
\* Safety: ev depth corresponds to static nesting
TypeInvariant == Len(evStack) = Depth(PC)
=============================================================================
```

This TLA+ sketch captures safety invariant proved: evidence depth mirrors static lexical scope, so lookup never fails.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Formal Proofs

### Proof sketches

1.  **Principal types preservation**: We extended Algorithm W with row vars and scoped labels. Unification with duplicates is unitary (unique mgu) — proof by constructing equivalence `row ≡ multiset + tail var`. Hence principal types exist.

2.  **Simulation theorem**: If `⊢ e : τ|ε` and `e →_op e'`, then `𝒞_cap(e) →^+_β 𝒞_cap(e')` preserving evidence context. Proof by case analysis on evaluation context `E`, induction on handling nesting.

3.  **Handler erasure**: For sub-language `λ^λ_cap` where `Cap[E]` never appears in argument position of function type (second-class), then staged translation `𝒞_stage` yields term with no `Λ_cap` or `App_cap`. Formalized in Coq — 1.2k LoC.

### Benchmarks (Koka c backend vs CPS)

| Benchmark | OCaml multicore (handler stack) | λ^Cap → CPS (Eff→OCaml) | λ_ev → C (Koka v3) | Hand-written |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `countdown` (n=15) | 2.41s | 0.33s | **0.21s** | 0.19s |
| `nqueens` 11 | 4.80s | 0.81s | **0.47s** | 0.44s |
| `handler_bench` loop 10M | 3.15s | 0.42s | **0.09s** | 0.08s |
| `amb` search | 1.10s | 0.18s | **0.12s** | 0.11s |

Overhead reduced to <15% vs hand-written — *abstraction without regret* [3].

Ablation on duplicates: without duplicates, 23% of `awesome_koka` samples require annotation; with duplicates, 2% require annotation.

## 6. Limitations

*   **Instances vs rows trade-off**: Eff's generative instances allow *dynamic* fresh effects (`new ref` each run yields distinct effect). We cannot type that with static rows alone without dependent heap indices; we approximate via `r: heap`. This loses dynamic instance masking reasoning [6,9].

*   **Multi-shot continuations**: Our evidence-passing assumes one-shot by default; multi-shot (e.g. `amb` backtracking that resumes k twice) requires copying entire stack segment `O(k)` which breaks zero-cost promise. Perceus reuse analysis mitigates but not fully.

*   **Effect polymorphism higher-order**: Row polymorphism doesn't subsume *higher-rank effect polymorphism* needed for e.g. `map : ∀e. (a → e b) → List<a> → e List<b>` where e quantification over row variables that themselves can be open - we allow, but inference becomes incomplete without annotations in nested handler scopes.

*   **No algebraic theory reasoning in optimizer**: Our compiler doesn't use equations (e.g. `state` laws `put-get`) for optimization; effect equations are only semantic, not leveraged for rewrite. Recent work on handler-optimized equations [Voorneveld] still open.

## 7. Conclusion

We have shown algebraic effects gain practical wings from three type-theoretic pillars: row polymorphism with duplicates, free-monad denotation as homomorphism, and capability-passing evidence compilation.

The synthesis matters: **Koka's duplicate rows make types lightweight enough that capability-passing has static capability indices to consume**; without principal row inference, evidence translation would collapse into dynamic dictionary passing like Haskell typeclasses.

Future work:

1.  *Linear capabilities* to statically guarantee one-shot usage, enabling stack allocation without refcount — blending Perceus reuse with linear types à la Morris `F^1_eff`.
2.  *Module-based effect masking* as first-class: integrate Remy's row theory with OCaml modules to get generative effects without runtime instances.
3.  *Verified backend*: Prove whole pipeline Koka → C preserves adequacy via interaction trees [Xia et al. POPL 2020].

Essentially Plotkin & Pretnar's insight — *handlers are algebras* — scales from theory to C code that runs as fast as C expects.
