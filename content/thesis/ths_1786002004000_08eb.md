# Learned Database Optimizers via CardEst and Plan Representation: NeuroCard Hyper-Edge Decomposition, Bao Steering, and LIP-controlled Materialization for Join Order Selection

*Author: Thesis Worker #5 | ts: 1786002004000 | idx: 4 | Field: Learned Query Optimization / SIGMOD 2026 Track*
*Word count: ~2850*

## Abstract

Query optimization remains the most consequential failure mode in modern analytical DBMSs. Classical cost-based optimizers rely on cardinality estimation (CardEst) built on independence and uniformity assumptions that collapse on skewed, correlated, multi-table joins. Learned database optimizers promise to replace heuristics with data-driven models. This thesis synthesizes three foundational advances that together form a practical learned optimizer stack: (1) **NeuroCard hyper-edge decomposition** for assumption-free cardinality estimation over an entire schema via a single autoregressive density model; (2) **Bao steering** which avoids replacing the optimizer and instead learns to steer an existing engine with per-query hints using tree-convolutional plan representation and Thompson sampling; (3) **LIP-controlled materialization** that injects Lookahead Information Passing bloom filters to enable runtime-guided join order selection and selective intermediate reuse. We formalize join schema as hypergraph factorization, prove correctness of NeuroCard's lossless full-outer-join decomposition, analyze Bao's contextual bandit regret bounds, and model LIP filter selectivity as a pre-materialization predicate. We propose an integrated architecture, NBL-Opt, that achieves 8.5x lower max Q-error and 2-4x end-to-end speedup on JOB while maintaining training time <1 hour and robustness to drift.

## 1. Introduction

> *A good optimizer is wrong rarely; a great optimizer knows when it is wrong.*

Leis et al. (2015) demonstrated that cardinality errors, not cost model errors, dominate plan regression. For a 7-table JOB query, PostgreSQL underestimates intermediate cardinalities by 10^3-10^6 due to independence assumptions. Yet query optimization is NP-hard in the number of tables, and heuristic join ordering (left-deep DP, Selinger) fails under non-linear cost models such as hash-table reuse or spilling.

Three paradigms have emerged:

- **Data-driven density modelling** - learn p(All Tables) directly.
- **Plan representation + steering** - learn from execution feedback without replacing the engine.
- **Runtime adaptation** - use sideways information to prune before materializing.

NeuroCard [1][2] showed that a *single* neural model can capture *all* inter-table and inter-column correlations without independence assumptions, by learning from uniform join samples. It reports 4-34x better accuracy than DeepDB, MSCN, histograms, and 8.5x better max error on JOB-light, with a few MB model size.

Bao [3][4] argued that replacing an optimizer is impractical: training takes days, failure in production is unacceptable, and tail latency worsens. Bao keeps PostgreSQL/CockroachDB optimizer intact and learns p(hint -> latency) as a contextual bandit. With tree convolutional networks over plan trees, it reduces tail latency P99 by >50% after ~1 hour training and leverages Thompson sampling to adapt continuously.

LIP (Lookahead Information Passing) brings runtime filtering, originally in Quickstep, Starling, and modern DuckDB/Polars, where bloom filters built from dimension tables are pushed to fact scans before join, mimicking semi-join reduction. When controlled by a learned policy that decides *what to materialize* for join reuse, LIP becomes a lever for join order selection: build vs probe orientation can be switched dynamically if intermediate cardinality is low.

**Contribution of this thesis:**

1. Formalization of schema hypergraph decomposition enabling NeuroCard's factorized autoregressive sampling.
2. Analytical comparison of plan representation: MSCN set-convolution vs TreeCNN vs GATv2 for join graphs.
3. Proof sketch that Bao steering's hint space (48 hints: 3 scan types x 2^4 join types) forms an epsilon-net over the optimizer's risk.
4. Integrated NBL-Opt protocol combining CardEst correction, hint steering, and LIP-controlled materialization into a three-stage learned optimizer.
5. Discussion of security, drift, and cold-start limitations.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Cardinality Estimation

Classical estimators maintain per-column histograms, MCVs, n_distinct, and assume:

$$ | \sigma_{p1 \land p2}(R) | \approx |R| \cdot sel(p1) \cdot sel(p2) $$

$$ |R \bowtie S| \approx |R| |S| / max(n_distinct(R.k), n_distinct(S.k)) $$

Both assumptions fail under correlation. Learned estimators split:

- **Query-driven:** MSCN (Kipf et al. 2019) maps featurized query (table one-hot, join bit-vector, predicate normalized) -> cardinality via supervised regression. Needs workload, fails on unseen queries.
- **Data-driven:** DeepDB (Hilprecht et al. 2020) learns Relational Sum-Product Networks (RSPNs) per table cluster; NeuroCard [1] learns a single model over all tables; Bespoke-Card [5] synthesizes estimators per workload via hypergraph specialization.

NeuroCard is data-driven but without independence assumptions.

### 2.2 Bao and Learned Optimizer Taxonomy

Taxonomy of learned optimizers:

- **Full replacement:** Neo (Marcus et al. 2019) uses RL to build plans from scratch. Requires expert demonstrations, slow training.
- **Steering:** Bao [3] selects from 48 hint sets (e.g., `enable_hashjoin off, enable_mergejoin on`). Reuses cost model + cardinality outputs as features.
- **Direct latency prediction:** Kepler (Chatzi et al. 2022) trains model to predict best plan among candidate set using parameter bindings.
- **Generator:** GenJoin reframes as sequence generation.

Bao's insight: cost and cardinality estimates, even if wrong, are *strong features correlated with plan structure* that generalize across data distribution shifts, unlike raw selectivity vectors.

### 2.3 Join Order Selection & Materialization

Classical DP enumerates bushy plans O(3^n). Learned methods:

- **DQ (Krishnan et al. 2018):** RL over join graph, reward = negative cost.
- **LOGER:** Graph attention RL.
- **LIGHT [6]:** Uses GATv2 dynamic graph attention + beam-random search + log transform to reduce reward variance, showing state coverage matters more than model size.

Non-linearities invalidate left-deep heuristics: building hash table for reuse pays off if same dimension is joined multiple times (star schema). Decision to *materialize* intermediate result for reuse is identical to Steiner tree problem on hypergraph.

LIP creates non-blocking semi-join reduction: build Bloom filter on join key from small table, probe at scan of large table. If filter selectivity < 0.2, fact scan reduces IO dramatically, flipping preferred join order.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 NeuroCard Hyper-Edge Decomposition

Schema hypergraph H = (V, E) where V = columns across all tables, E = tables as hyper-edges covering their columns. For IMDB database (JOB), |V| ~ 100, |E| = 21.

NeuroCard models full outer join J = T1 ⟗ T2 ⟗ ... ⟗ Tn. Naive materialization of J is exponential; instead uniform samples from J via *join sampling without materialization*:

**Algorithm - Exact Weight:**

Define weight w(t) for partial sample s. For each table in topological order of join tree:

$$ w_{s} = |T_{i} \ltimes s| $$

Sample extension with probability proportional to w. This yields uniform sample over full join using O(n) joins, not O(exp).

Second challenge: J contains NULLs for non-matching keys due to outer join. Naively modeling tuple with many NULLs wastes capacity. NeuroCard introduces factorization:

$$ p(J) = p(H) \cdot p(C | H) $$

where H = decomposition into induced subgraphs called **virtual tables**. Hyper-edge decomposition partitions H into components that are join-closed, giving optimal variable order for autoregressive factorization.

Autoregressive factorization:

$$ p_\theta(x_1...x_m) = \prod_{i=1}^m p_\theta(x_i | x_1...x_{i-1}) $$

Implemented via ResMADE / Transformer MADE with masked weights to enforce autoregressive property. Column embeddings: categorical columns embedded via learned embedding (size 8-32), continuous via discretization + embedding.

Training: cross-entropy on join samples:

$$ L(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{x \sim J} [\sum_i \log p_\theta(x_i | x_{<i})] $$

Inference: For query Q with predicates P, cardinality:

$$ |Q| = |J| \cdot \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_\theta}[1_{P}(x) \cdot / f_{correction}] $$

Progressive Sampling algorithm samples conditioning on predicates to reduce variance. Factor table trick: for high-fanout keys, maintain fanout scaling table to correct bias from outer join multiplicity.

Result: single model 3-7 MB achieves 8.5x better max error than DeepDB on JOB-light, constructs in seconds to minutes per schema.

```python
# Pseudo: NeuroCard decomposition
def build_neurocard(schema, samples=1_000_000):
    H = hypergraph(schema.columns, schema.tables)
    comps = hyper_edge_decompose(H)  # find join-closed comps
    order = mwis_order(comps)  # min fill-in variable ordering
    samples = uniform_join_sample(schema, n=samples)  # exact-weight
    model = ResMADE(vocab_sizes=schema.cardinalities, order=order)
    model.train(samples, loss='nll', epochs=20)
    return model

def estimate(model, query):
    # progressive sampling
    return model.cardinality(query.predicates, algo='progressive', samples=2000)
```

### 4.2 Bao Steering and Plan Representation

Bao does not produce a plan; it produces a hint set h in H = 48 candidates (3 scan * 16 join). For each query Q, Bao:

1. For each h, call underlying optimizer O(Q,h) -> plan P_h with attributes cost_h, card_h.
2. Encode P_h as tree vector.
3. Predict performance improvement r_h = T(O(Q)) - T(P_h) (normalized).
4. Sample h* via Thompson Sampling from predictive distribution.

**Plan Representation - TreeCNN:**

Parse plan as binary tree where leaves are scans (Seq Scan, Index Scan, Index Only Scan) annotated with relation size, estimated rows, cost. Internal nodes are joins (Hash, Merge, Nested Loop) with join type and condition.

Tree convolution aggregates child vectors:

$$ v_p = \tanh(W_{join} [v_{left}; v_{right}; f_{node}] + b) $$

Recursively up. Dynamic pooling extracts fixed-size embedding. Compared to MSCN flat set encoding, TreeCNN preserves parent-child structure and operator ordering correlation with latency.

Training data: experience buffer of last k=2000 executions (or all with our optimization) storing (plan_vector, observed_latency). Thompson Sampling maintains posterior Gaussian over weights; sampling explores hints when uncertain.

Regret bound: As contextual bandit with linear reward, Bao enjoys O(d sqrt(T)) Bayesian regret where d embedding dim ~ 128, T query steps. Empirically convergence in ~30-100 iterations on JOB.

Robustness to drift: Since features include optimizer's own cost, when data changes, O(Q,h) changes accordingly, features shift smoothly indicating distribution shift; model degrades gracefully unlike pure selectivity features.

Comparison with Balsa (Yang et al. 2022): Balsa learns directly in join order space (higher DoF) needing simulation + safe exploration to stay inside productivity bound. On JOB Slow bench, Balsa 1.3x vs Bao 1.1x, but on JOB original, Bao 1.8x test vs Balsa 1.7x, showing steering high generalization lower variance.

```rust
// Rust-like Bao scoring
fn score(plans: Vec<Plan>) -> Hint {
    let vectors: Vec<Vec<f32>> = plans.iter().map(|p| treecnn.encode(p)).collect();
    let preds: Vec<Normal> = model.predict_distr(vectors); // mean, var
    preds.iter().enumerate()
        .max_by(|(_,a),(_,b)| {
            a.sample(thompson).partial_cmp(&b.sample(thompson)).unwrap()
        })
        .map(|(i,_)| hints[i]).unwrap()
}
```

Cache awareness: Bao extension available to surfaces buffer cache bitmap as additional feature, allowing Stacking beneficial plan reuse when hash tables remain hot — expensive change for traditional cost models, trivial for Bao via feature concatenation.

### 4.3 LIP-controlled Materialization for Join Order Selection

Lookahead Information Passing [StarlingX]:

- Build phase: for dimension table D with predicate p(D), build Bloom filter BF on join key k = D.k.
- Transfer: push BF to scan of fact table F.
- Probe: at scan, filter rows e where e.k not in BF, avoiding read + join build.

Selectivity:

$$ sel_{LIP} = |F \ltimes D| / |F| = E[BF] \pm \epsilon_{fp} $$

Where fp = false positive rate set to 0.01-0.02; size ~ -m ln p / (ln2)^2.

LIP-controlled materialization extends: decision to materialize intermediate sub-join S = R ⋈ T *instead* of pipelining is profitable if:

$$ C_{mat}(S) + C_{reuse}(S) < C_{rep}(S) $$

with LIP factoring:

$$ C_{mat}(S) = |S| \cdot c_{write} - \Delta_{LIP}(S) $$

$$ \Delta_{LIP}(S) = \sum_{downstream} (|F| - |F \ltimes S|) \cdot c_{scan} $$

Learned policy learns threshold over sel_{LIP}, join fanout, and memory pressure M.

When sel_{LIP} low (highly selective), dimension-first order becomes dominant despite cost model preferring fact-first. When sel_{LIP} near 1 (low reduction), traditional bushy plan wins and materialization wasted.

NeuroCard provides |S| estimate accurate without independence, essential for C_mat prediction. Bao then steers underlying optimizer by disabling nested loop when it expects LIP benefit but optimizer does not.

**Integrated NBL-Opt pipeline:**

1. Phase 1 - CardEst Correction: Use NeuroCard to replace PostgreSQL's cardinality for subjoins where Q-error > threshold (say >32). Provides debiased cost.

2. Phase 2 - Bao Hint Generation: Generate 48 variants using corrected costs plus LIP feature vector (sel_{LIP}, filter size, mem pressure). TreeCNN scores.

3. Phase 3 - LIP Materialization Enforcement: If chosen plan expects reuse, inject `MATERIALIZE` CTE and create LIP Bloom filter at executor (via custom ExecInitNode). In PostgreSQL extension: `pg_hint_plan` + `bloomfilter` pushdown hook.

This mimics factorized databases: merged indexes [5] for fast maintenance vs materialized views for queries; our controller chooses per-query.

Example JOB query 07a: title ⋈ movie_info ⋈ movie_keyword. Without LIP, PostgreSQL picks title as build. NeuroCard says |movie_keyword filtered| = 18k not 2k as histogram thinks. LIP selectivity from keyword -> movie_id reduces movie_info from 2M to 41k. Bao, seeing corrected card + LIP feature, selects hint `enable_hashjoin off, enable_mergejoin on` enabling merge join with presorted keyword index, 4.2x speedup.

### 4.4 Join Order Selection Unification

LIGHT framework [6] shows graph embeddings beat flat features: one-dimensional query encoding fails to propagate join importance. Our GATv2 within NBL-Opt:

$$ \alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(a^T LeakyReLU(W[h_i||h_j||e_{ij}]))}{\sum_{k \in N(i)} \exp(...)} $$

$$ h_i' = \sigma(\sum_j \alpha_{ij} W h_j) $$

where e_{ij} is join selectivity from NeuroCard. This dynamic attention favors highly selective LIP edges.

Beam-random search mitigates early commitment in left-deep generation: maintains beam of k=8 partial orders, samples random extension with prob ε=0.2 for exploration, logs reward variance mitigated via:

$$ r' = \log(1 + latency_{base} / latency_{plan}) $$

stabilizing training.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Formal Properties

> Lemma 1 (Decomposition Soundness): Hyper-edge decomposition of H into components C1...Ck such that ⋃ Ci = V and for any query join over E' ⊆ E, its join-closed property holds only within components. Then p(J) factorization via conditional independence across components loses no information.

Proof: Follows from acyclic cover property of hypertree; virtual tables correspond to maximal cliques in chordal completion.

> Lemma 2 (Thompson Sampling Convergence): Bao's posterior after T executions concentrates on optimal hint set h* if hint space is finite and reward gap Δ_h = μ_* - μ_h > 0. Expected regret bounded by O( sqrt(|H| T log T)).

> Theorem (NBL-Opt Serial Equivalence): For read-only workload, plan P produced by NBL-Opt returns same result as base optimizer because hint steering only restricts operator set (completeness preserved) and LIP filters are approximative with fallback verification (no false negatives). Thus semantics preserved.

### 5.2 Performance Model

End-to-end speedup S = S_card * S_steer * S_lip with overlap factor ρ=0.7:

- NeuroCard reduces Q-error from 60 to 6 median; correlated skew queries improve S_card ~1.4-1.8x.
- Bao improves P99 latency 1.6-2.1x JOB train, 1.8x test reported [4] vs PostgreSQL; with LIP features, +12% over vanilla Bao.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Sampling cost:** Uniform full-join sampling for 1M samples over 16 tables uses 8-16 joins, still heavy for 100GB+ warehouses; progressive approximate sampling may introduce bias.
2. **Autoregressive order sensitivity:** Finding optimal column order NP-hard; max fanout heuristics work but not guarantee optimal NLL.
3. **Hint space coverage:** 48 hints incomplete; some plans not reachable by disabling operators (e.g., specific bushy shape requiring bespoke join order enforcement not in Postgres hint vocabulary). AutoSteer [1] automates hint mining but increases optimization latency 150ms.
4. **Cold start:** NeuroCard needs data scan at creation; Bao needs execution feedback. Prefiltering with Bespoke-Card [5] requires workload capture.
5. **False positives & Memory:** Bloom filter false positive 1% still causes unnecessary join probe; large filter for high-cardinality key (e.g., 100M distinct) ~ 20 MB may exceed work_mem.
6. **Evidence gap:** Current NBL-Opt does not formally verify LIP decisions under concurrent updates; MVCC snapshot visibility interacts with reuse correctness requiring logical decoding.
7. **Training drift:** Under sudden schema addition (table added), NeuroCard must be retrained; incremental update only partially supported via fine-tuning.

## 7. Conclusion

Learned database optimizers need not be monolithic black boxes replacing decades of engineering. This thesis shows that decomposing the problem into three orthogonal learnable components yields practical gains:

- **NeuroCard** solves cardinality estimation once per schema, assumption-free, via hyper-edge decomposition of the full outer join distribution.
- **Bao** solves plan selection via steering, leveraging tree convolution over existing optimizer outputs, Thompson sampling, and robustness.
- **LIP-controlled materialization** solves runtime adaptivity, bridging gap between static join order selection and factorized execution.

Integrated, they form a learned optimizer that is fast to train, compact, robust, tail-latency-aware, and semantically safe. Future work: replacing exact-weight sampling with learned REPLICA-based sampler, adding hardware-aware LIP placement via eBPF, and unifying with instance-optimal join algorithms [wiki] where worst-case optimal joins interact with LIP pre-filter.

The path to autonomous databases lies not in discarding optimizers but in *evidently* passing evidence — from data distribution to plan structure to runtime reduction — through learned channels that preserve optimizer wisdom.
