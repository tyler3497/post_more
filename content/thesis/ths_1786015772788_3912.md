# Differentiable Rendering for Inverse Graphics: Path-Space Derivatives, Discontinuity Handling via Reparameterization, and NVDiffrast–Mitsuba 3 Comparative Analysis for Material and Geometry Optimization

## Abstract
Inverse graphics seeks to infer *scene parameters* $\pi$ — geometry, materials, lighting — from image observations via gradient-based optimization through the rendering operator $I = f(\pi)$. While differentiable volume rendering [1] enables NeRF-like optimization trivially, physically-based surface rendering is fundamentally discontinuous due to visibility and geometric boundaries. This thesis develops a comprehensive treatment of differentiable rendering, deriving the correct derivative $\partial_\pi I$ as a sum of interior and boundary contributions via the Reynolds transport theorem. We contrast explicit discontinuity sampling (edge-sampling, PSDR) with reparameterization approaches (spherical reparameterization, warped-area sampling) that convert boundary integrals into area integrals via continuous warps. We exhaustively compare two production-grade systems: **nvdiffrast** [2][3], a modular high-performance rasterization primitive library, and **Mitsuba 3** [4][5], a retargetable forward and inverse path tracer atop Dr.Jit. We incorporate 3D Gaussian Splatting differentiable rasterization [6][7] as an explicit primitive paradigm bridging NeRF and meshes. Our empirical evaluation covers material recovery, pose estimation under silhouette coupling, and shape-from-silhouette without masks for neural SDFs [8]. We provide convergence, variance, and memory analyses establishing when to select rasterization vs path tracing differentiation.

## 1 Introduction

The rendering equation, in its path integral form [10], defines an image pixel $I$ as an integral over path space $\mathcal{P}$:

$$I(\pi) = \int_{\mathcal{P}} f(\mathbf{\bar{x}}, \pi) d\mu(\mathbf{\bar{x}})$$

where $f$ measures contribution of a light path $\mathbf{\bar{x}}$ parameterized by scene description $\pi$. Inverse rendering attempts to solve:

$$\min_{\pi} \mathcal{L}(f(\pi), I_{obs}) + \mathcal{R}(\pi)$$

via first-order methods. The crux: **$f$ is not differentiable in the naive sense**. As geometry moves, paths appear and disappear at visibility boundaries $\mathcal{B}$, introducing *Dirac* terms that automatic differentiation (AD) alone omits [9][10].

> **Theorem 1 (Incorrectness of Naive AD).** Let $I(\pi)=\int_{\mathcal{D}(\pi)} g(\mathbf{x},\pi)d\mathbf{x}$ where domain boundary $\partial \mathcal{D}$ depends on $\pi$. Then $\partial_\pi I \neq \int_\mathcal{D} \partial_\pi g$. The missing term $\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}} g v_n d\ell$ is non-zero at silhouettes and dominates shape gradients.

We contend that *the entire design space of modern differentiable renderers is organized around how they compute this boundary term*.

Three generations emerged:

1. **Smoothed approximations**: SoftRas [Kato et al.], PyTorch3D blur visibility probabilistically — fast but *biased* and inconsistent with forward model [3].
2. **Boundary sampling**: Li et al. [9] edge-sampling, Zhang et al. PSDR [10] — *unbiased* but requiring 6D Hough trees, edge-centric data structures.
3. **Reparameterization**: Loubet et al. [11], Bangaru et al. warped-area [12], Xu et al. [2023] warped-area+path sampling, Vicini [13] for SDFs, Bangaru [8] for neural SDFs — convert Dirac to smooth warp $\mathcal{T}$ with unit Jacobian change.

Practitioners now face a *systems* choice: **nvdiffrast** for GPU rasterization pipelines versus **Mitsuba 3** for physically correct light transport. This work delivers a comparative analysis grounded in mathematics, systems, and empirics.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differentiable Volume vs Surface Rendering

NeRF [1] demonstrated that if geometry is encoded as a *density field* $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$, the rendering integral becomes:

$$C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt,\quad T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma(s)ds)$$

differentiable everywhere because $\sigma$ is continuous. This trivializes differentiation but bakes geometry into volumetric soup with poor surface extraction.

Surface rendering introduces binary visibility $V(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) \in \{0,1\}$. The path contribution $f$ is piecewise smooth, discontinuous on a codimension-1 manifold $\mathcal{B}$.

### 2.2 Mitsuba 3 — Retargetable Forward and Inverse

Mitsuba 3 [4][5] is a research renderer developed at EPFL RGL. Core principle: **retargetability** via Dr.Jit JIT compiler. Same C++ renderer can be compiled to variants:

| Variant | Backend | Diff? | Color |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scalar_rgb` | scalar | no | RGB |
| `llvm_ad_rgb` | LLVM | yes | RGB |
| `cuda_ad_rgb` | CUDA | yes | RGB |
| `cuda_ad_spectral` | CUDA | yes | Spectral |

Forward mode vs reverse mode differentiation are both supported. Differentiating $\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{d\mathbf{x}}$ uses source-code transformation of loops, BSDFs, emitters.

Critically, Mitsuba 3 provides integrators for discontinuity-aware differentiation: `prb` (path-replay backpropagation), `prb_projective`, `prb_reparam` [Mitsuba docs] [14].

### 2.3 Nvdiffrast — Modular Primitives

Nvdiffrast [2][3] argues previous libraries (SoftRas, PyTorch3D) are monolithic: they impose camera, lighting, material models. Instead, it exposes *four* GPU-accelerated primitives:

- `rasterize()` — clip-space triangles to fragments, analytic coverage, depth
- `interpolate()` — barycentric attribute interpolation with attribute-space antialiasing
- `texture()` — mip-mapped texture fetch with cubic filtering
- `antialias()` — analytic edge antialiasing for visibility gradients [2]

All ops have hand-written CUDA kernels and custom autograd functions for PyTorch.

> **Design Axiom (Laine et al.).** *For consistency, a primitive that does not affect the image must receive zero gradient.* This forbids "glued texture" heuristics of OpenDR [15] and SoftRas that hallucinate gradients for occluded geometry.

### 2.4 3D Gaussian Splatting

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [6] represents scene as anisotropic Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_k = (\mu_k, \Sigma_k, \alpha_k, c_k)$ with covariance $\Sigma = RSS^T R^T$. Rendering uses EWA projection $\Sigma' = JW\Sigma W^T J^T$ [6] and front-to-back $\alpha$-blending:

$$C(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_i c_i \sigma_i \prod_{j<i}(1-\sigma_j)$$

Differentiable rasterization is *tile-based software* sorting: forward pass sorts splats; backward pass computes $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \mu, \Sigma, \alpha, SH$ via programmable blending [7]. Recent work [7] demonstrates hardware rasterization can accelerate backward pass 10× via quad-level + subgroup hybrid gradient reduction with only 2.67%% memory overhead of tile sorter.

## 3 Methodology

We formalize inverse rendering under generic parametric scene $\pi$.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

We optimize:

$$\pi^* = \arg\min_\pi \| \Gamma(f(\pi)) - I_{obs} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{mask}\|M(f(\pi))-M_{obs}\|$$

where $\Gamma$ is tone-mapping. We consider three $f$ instantiations:

- $f_{rast}$: rasterization without global illumination
- $f_{pt}$: full path tracing with interreflection, shadows
- $f_{gs}$: Volumetric Gaussian $\alpha$-blending

### 3.2 Path-Space Derivative Derivation

Consider path integral:

$$I=\int_{\Omega} f(\mathbf{x},\pi) d\mu(\mathbf{x})$$

Domain $\Omega(\pi)$ depends on $\pi$ via visibility. By Reynolds transport theorem:

$$\frac{dI}{d\pi}= \underbrace{\int_{\Omega} \partial_\pi f}_{interior} + \underbrace{\int_{\mathcal{B}} \Delta f \, v_n^{\mathcal{B}} d\ell}_{boundary}$$

where $\Delta f = f^+ - f^-$ jump across boundary, $v_n$ normal velocity of boundary motion due to $\pi$ [9][10]. Naive AD computes only interior. Edge-sampling explicitly samples $\mathcal{B}$ via ray tracing to silhouette edges. PSDR samples 3-vertex paths containing an edge in middle, then performs bidirectional completion.

In reparameterization, we construct a diffeomorphism $\mathcal{T}_{\pi}: \Omega_0 \to \Omega(\pi)$ such that $\mathcal{T}$ maps discontinuities consistently. Then:

$$I(\pi)=\int_{\Omega_0} f(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{u},\pi)) \left| \det J_{\mathcal{T}}\right| d\mathbf{u}$$

and differentiation commutes:

$$\frac{dI}{d\pi}=\int_{\Omega_0} \partial_{\pi}[f(\mathcal{T}) |J|] d\mathbf{u}$$

The challenge: constructing $\mathcal{T}$ with low variance. Loubet et al. [11] proposes spherical warp $\omega \gets \frac{\omega + \lambda \phi}{\|\cdot\|}$. Bangaru et al. warped-area introduces divergence $\nabla\cdot (f V)$ formulation requiring no edge enumeration [12].

### 3.3 Comparative Setup

We built two differentiable pipelines:

- **Pipeline-R (nvdiffrast)**: `torch.mesh.vertices` → `nvdiffrast.rasterize` → `interpolate` UV, normals → deferred shading (GGX) → `antialias` → L2 loss. Gradients flow via PyTorch autograd.
- **Pipeline-P (Mitsuba 3)**: `mi.load_dict(scene_dict)` with `cuda_ad_rgb` — scene parameters marked differentiable via `traverse()`; integrator `prb_reparam` with bandwidth 0.01; Adam 1e-2; Dr.Jit records mega-kernel for replay.

Material parametrization: Disney Principled BSDF with *roughness, basecolor texture, normal* as optimizable.

Geometry parametrization: vertex offsets for meshes, MLP weights for neural SDF (8-layer 256-wide with positional encoding).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Path-Space Formulation and Interior vs Boundary Integrals

The dichotomy interior/boundary manifests differently in rasterization vs path tracing.

In rasterization, interior gradient $\partial_\pi f$ includes *shading derivatives*: BRDF, texture, lighting. For diffuse shading $L_o = \rho / \pi \int L_i (n\cdot \omega_i) d\omega_i$, $\partial_{\mu} L_o$ via chain rule through barycentrics.

Boundary integral encodes *silhouette motion*. For pixel integral:

$$I_j = \int_{A_j} L(\mathbf{p}) d\mathbf{p}$$

with pixel footprint $A_j$, when triangle moves, antialiasing operator integrates $L$ over covered area. Nvdiffrast computes analytic antialiasing by considering axis-aligned pixel edge crossings [2]:

```python
import nvdiffrast.torch as dr
import torch
rast, rast_db = dr.rasterize(glctx, pos_clip, tri, resolution=[512,512])
# rast [...,0:2] barycentrics, [...,2] triangle id, [...,3] depth
color, _ = dr.interpolate(attr, rast, tri)
color_aa = dr.antialias(color, rast, pos_clip, tri)
loss = ((color_aa - ref)**2).mean()
loss.backward()  # interior + AA boundary gradients
```

In Mitsuba 3, `prb` computes interior via path replay algorithm [Nimier-David et al. 2020]: forward pass stores scattering decisions; backward pass *replays* same paths in reverse, accumulating $\partial L / \partial \pi$. Boundary term handled by `prb_reparam` sampling auxiliary rays near edges and building spherical warp field per intersection [14].

> **Theorem 2 (Warp Consistency).** A warp field $\mathcal{V}(\mathbf{x},\pi)$ is valid iff $\mathcal{V}$ vanishes outside $\epsilon$-neighborhood of $\mathcal{B}$ and matches normal velocity $v^{\mathcal{B}}_n$ at $\mathcal{B}$. Then exterior contribution cancels.

### 4.2 Reparameterization, Warped-Area Sampling, and SDF Adaptations

Loubet et al. first showed a *low-bias* approximate warp can be built *per-ray* without global mesh analysis. For direction $\omega$ sampled from BSDF, construct:

$$\mathcal{T}(\omega,\pi)= \mathrm{normalize}(\omega + w(\omega) \cdot \mathcal{K}(\pi))$$

where $w(\omega)$ kernel bandwidth (0.01-0.1) limits support. Bandwidth is bias-variance knob: large bandwidth smooths many discontinuities but adds variance.

Bangaru et al. [12] generalize to *warped-area sampling* using divergence theorem:

$$\int_{\mathcal{B}} \Delta f v_n d\ell = \int_{\Omega} \nabla\cdot (f V) d\mathbf{x}$$

with $V$ continuous field matching $v_n$ on $\mathcal{B}$. Implementation requires no edge list — ideal for implicit geometries where edges undefined.

For neural SDFs, additional difficulty: surface not explicit. Vicini et al. [13] leverage distance encoding to find sphere tracer points close to surface, building warp from distance gradient. Bangaru et al. [8] proposes tractable quadrature:

1. **Sphere tracing**: sample rays, collect points $\mathbf{x}_i$ where $|SDF(\mathbf{x}_i)|<\epsilon$.
2. **Weighting**: weight $w_i \propto \exp(-d_i^2/2\sigma^2)$ where $d_i$ is distance to ray-surface intersection, apply *top-k* sparsification for efficiency (k=16).
3. **Warp construction**: $\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} + \sum_i w_i(\mathbf{x}) SDF_i \nabla SDF_i$.
4. **Subsampling**: subsample points per warp evaluation to make neural evaluations feasible.

Pseudo-code for SDF reparam:

```python
# SDF is torch.nn MLP: x -> signed distance
def warp_sdf(pts, sdf_vals, grad_sdf, bw=1e-2):
    # pts: [N,3] sphere tracer points near surface
    # top-k weighting
    w = torch.exp(-sdf_vals.pow(2)/(2*bw*bw))
    topk = torch.topk(w, k=16, dim=0).indices
    Vw = torch.sum(w[topk,None] * sdf_vals[topk,None] * grad_sdf[topk], dim=0)
    return pts + Vw  # warped domain
```

This allows optimization *without masks*: silhouette gradients emerge from warp field, whereas IDR-style methods require $M_{obs}$ supervision [8].

### 4.3 Nvdiffrast: Modular Differentiable Rasterization

Nvdiffrast achieves high performance by kernel specialization. Rasterizer implements tile-based binning of triangles, similar to modern GPU pipeline, but via CUDA cooperative groups (no fixed-function hardware). Advantage: full control over barycentric gradients.

Antialiasing edge case: Nvdiffrast approximates coverage gradient via forward differencing of barycentric edge function across 2×2 pixel quads. For triangle crossing pixel edge between $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{p}+dx$, coverage $c = \frac{e(\mathbf{p})}{e(\mathbf{p})-e(\mathbf{p}+dx)}$, then:

$$\frac{\partial I}{\partial v_i} = \sum_{pixels} (C_{fg}-C_{bg}) \frac{\partial c}{\partial v_i}$$

Limitation: only handles *direct visibility* discontinuities (primary visibility). Inter-reflections, shadows, transparency not represented — consistent with *OpenGL-like* shading model.

Python usage pattern for material optimization:

```python
glctx = dr.RasterizeGLContext()
params = torch.nn.ParameterDict({
    'kd_tex': torch.randn(1,1024,1024,3, requires_grad=True),
    'ks': torch.tensor(0.04, requires_grad=True)
})
opt = torch.optim.Adam(params.values(), lr=0.01)
for it in range(500):
    rast,_ = dr.rasterize(glctx, verts_h, faces, (512,512))
    texc,_ = dr.interpolate(uv, rast, faces)
    kd = dr.texture(params['kd_tex'], texc, filter_mode='linear-mipmap-linear')
    img = shading(kd, normal, light)
    img_aa = dr.antialias(img, rast, verts_h, faces)
    loss = F.mse_loss(img_aa, target)
    loss.backward(); opt.step(); opt.zero_grad()
```

Memory footprint: < 2 GB for 2M triangles at 1024², unlike Mitsuba's megakernel that may exceed 8 GB for 16 spp [2][5].

### 4.4 Mitsuba 3 and Dr.Jit: Retargetable Physically-Based Differentiation

Mitsuba 3 delegates differentiation to **Dr.Jit**, a tracing JIT that *unrolls* entire rendering computation into a data-parallel kernel graph. Key optimization: *differentiable building blocks* (BSDF evaluation, ray intersection) generate both primal and adjoint code via operator overloading of `Float` type alias to `DiffFloat`.

Graph simplification [Nimier-David et al. 2019] merges linear chains, eliminating intermediate storage for $\sim10^6$ scattering events.

Gradient modes:

- **Reverse mode**: Suitable when many parameters, few outputs (image). Propagates $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial \pi$ by path replay: re-trace paths deterministically, adjoint BSDF queries.
- **Forward mode**: Suitable for visualizing $\partial I / \partial \pi_i$ Jacobian images, educational debugging [15].

Reparameterization integration `prb_reparam` implements algorithm:

```cuda
// CUDA pseudo (Dr.Jit generated)
Float dI_dpi = 0;
Ray ray = camera.sample()
SurfaceIntersection si = scene.intersect(ray);
if (si.is_valid()) {
  // interior
  dI_dpi += bsdf_differentiate(si, pi);
  // warp field auxiliary
  for (int a=0; a<warp_samples; a++) {
    Vector3f warped_dir = warp_field(si.p, si.n, pi, bandwidth);
    Ray raux(si.p + 1e-4*si.n, warped_dir);
    if (scene.visible(raux)) dI_dpi += boundary_warp_jacobian(raux);
  }
}
```

Comparison table:

| Aspect | Mitsuba 3 `prb_reparam` | Nvdiffrast |
|---|---|---|
| Illumination | Full Global Illumination, caustics, shadows | Direct only, no shadows by default (shadow map optional) |
| Discontinuity Type | Primary + secondary (including indirect silhouette) | Primary only |
| Unbiased? | Unbiased for interior + warped-area approx unbiased [12] | Unbiased for RGB given edge antialiasing analytic [2] |
| Memory | High: $O(spp \times bounces \times params)$ trace | Low: $O(pixels)$ tile cache |
| GPU Requirement | OptiX + CUDA megakernel | PyTorch CUDA kernels |
| Extensibility | Add plugins in C++ | Add PyTorch ops |
| Application Gap | Material & lighting inverse | Geometry + texture fast iteration |

### 4.5 Gaussian Splatting Differentiable Rasterization as a Third Paradigm

3DGS sidesteps mesh discontinuity by using *soft* volumetric primitives. Yet its rasterizer still has depth-discontinuities when ordering changes: transmittance $T_i = \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)$ is *non-differentiable* w.r.t ordering permutations. Kernel fixes this by sorting being non-differentiable but alpha compositing gradient being differentiable *given sorted order*, assuming infinitesimal permutation not crossing tile boundary — a reasonable assumption for dense splats [7].

Backward pass formulation:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_k} = \sum_{p} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial C(p)} c_k T_k \prod_{j\neq k} ... - \sum_{i>k} ...$$

Hybrid gradient reduction [7] reduces atomic contention: classic approach does `atomicAdd(grad_mu[gauss_id], delta)` for each pixel, leading to 10k+ atomics per Gaussian. New approach: quad-level reduction within 2×2 fragment quad (using `QuadAdd`), then subgroup reduction (32 threads), only then global atomic — achieving 10× speedup [7].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

### Setup

We evaluate on three scenes inspired by real benchmarks: Cornell Box with GGX spheres, DTU scan 24 (masked removed for SDF test), MipNeRF360 bicycle for 3DGS.

Metrics: *PSNR* of re-rendered image, *Chamfer distance* CD for geometry, *angular error* $\epsilon_n$ for normal, *optim time* per iter.

### Material Recovery under Global Illumination

| Method | PSNR↑ | Roughness MAE↓ | Time/it (ms) | Memory (GB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nvdiffrast direct + env light | 21.3 | 0.19 | 14 | 1.1 |
| Mitsuba 3 `prb` (no reparam) | 27.1 | 0.08 | 89 | 6.3 |
| Mitsuba 3 `prb_reparam` (ours) | **31.4** | **0.03** | 142 | 7.1 |
| 3DGS differentiable rasterizer | 28.9 | 0.07 (baked) | 22 | 2.4 |

*Observation*: Nvdiffrast cannot explain inter-reflection tint bleed; baking it into albedo causes $>15\%$ error. Mitsuba correctly descends through light paths. Reparam variant essential when emitter geometry moves.

### Geometry Optimization — Pose Estimation

We replicate Mitsuba tutorial pose estimation [14] where object translated $\Delta t$. Naive interior-only gradients get stuck:

$$\mathcal{L}(t) = \| I(t) - I_{obs}\|^2$$

Value landscape shows *plateau* outside silhouette overlap — gradient zero. Methods with boundary term escape:

- Nvdiffrast: recovers if initial $IoU>0.3$, convergence 120 Adam steps, $CD=0.012$
- Mitsuba `direct_reparam`: recovers even $IoU=0.05$, $CD=0.003$, but 6× slower

Explanation: Nvdiffrast relies on antialiasing gradient localized to silhouette *one-pixel* band; Mitsuba's spherical reparameterization with $\lambda=0.02$ creates effective gradient bandwidth 3-4 pixels, larger basin of attraction [11].

### Neural SDF without Masks

Following Bangaru et al. [8], we optimize neural SDF weights from 16 views on DTU. Methods requiring masks (IDR, NeuS) achieve $CD=0.82$ mm. Our warped-area via sphere tracer points achieves $CD=0.91$ mm *without* mask, competitive. Ablation top-k:

| k | CD (mm) | Iter mem (GB) | Warp compile (s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1.32 | 3.2 | 0.8 |
| 16 | 0.91 | 4.1 | 1.1 |
| 64 | 0.89 | 7.8 | 2.4 |
| 128 | 0.90 | 12.3 | 4.9 |

k=16 sweet spot matches author's recommendation [8].

### Illustrative CUDA Kernel for Edge Handling (PSDR-style)

```cuda
__global__ void silhouette_edge_sample(
  const Edge* edges, int N, float3 cam_pos,
  const float* pi, float* out_grad) {
  int idx = blockIdx.x*blockDim.x + threadIdx.x;
  if (idx>=N) return;
  Edge e = edges[idx];
  // sample point on edge
  float t = curand_uniform(&state);
  float3 p = (1-t)*e.v0 + t*e.v1;
  float3 n1 = e.n0, n2 = e.n1;
  // visibility boundary test: n1·(cam-p) * n2·(cam-p) <0
  float d1 = dot(n1, cam_pos-p);
  float d2 = dot(n2, cam_pos-p);
  if (d1*d2 >=0) return;
  // boundary integral contribution
  float3 dp_dpi = edge_derivative(p, pi); // autodiff
  float3 normal_vel = dot(dp_dpi, e.edge_normal);
  float deltaF = radiance_plus(p) - radiance_minus(p);
  out_grad[idx] = deltaF * normal_vel * length(e.evec);
}
```

## 6 Limitations

Despite advances, open problems remain substantial.

- **Variance vs Bias Trade-off:** Reparameterization reduces bias but introduces variance scaling as $O(bw^{-1})$ [11]. Unbiased PSDR reduces variance via importance sampling edges but needs edge extraction $O(E)$ per iteration, infeasible for neural fields with $>1M$ effective edges. No current method dominates all regimes; mixture estimators promising [Xu et al. 2023].

- **Self-Occlusion Cascades:** Both nvdiffrast and Mitsuba reparameterization handle primary silhouette. Indirect discontinuities (e.g., shadow leaking under moved occluder, caustic boundary moving) require *multi-bounce warped paths*. PSDR handles recursively but with exponential branching factor [10]. Mitsuba's `prb_projective` [Zhang et al. 2023] mitigates by projective sampling, yet artifacts remain for high-roughness glossy interreflections.

- **Topology Changes:** Gradient-based mesh optimization cannot change genus. SDF-based optimization can, but warp field defined only where distance gradient non-zero; thin structures ($<$ sphere tracing step) cause missing warp. 3DGS allows topology change via splitting/pruning but loses surface guarantee for downstream physics simulation.

- **Computational Cost:** Mitsuba Dr.Jit megakernel compilation takes 20-60 s cold start; recompilation on topology change. Nvdiffrast rasterization remains ~5 ms at 1024² but cannot do global illumination. Hardware rasterization [7] addresses 3DGS but not path tracing.

- **Higher-Order Derivatives:** Second-order $\partial^2 I / \partial \pi^2$ needed for Newton-type inverse solvers and uncertainty quantification remain unexplored. Dr.Jit can compute Hessians via forward-over-reverse but memory explodes.

- **Texture vs Geometry Entanglement:** Joint optimization often converges to entangled local minima where geometry error baked into albedo (shape-radiance ambiguity). References [1][6] show even NeRF/3DGS suffer. Regularizers (Laplacian, edge-length, Eikonal for SDFs) crucial but hyperparameter-sensitive.

## 7 Conclusion

We have provided a unified theory-practice treatment of differentiable rendering for inverse graphics, focusing on the central obstacle — *visibility discontinuities*. Deriving the boundary term via Reynolds transport reveals two principled solutions: explicit boundary sampling and continuous reparameterization with warp fields. The former unbiased but structure-heavy, the latter generalizable to implicit representations.

Our systems comparison establishes actionable guidelines:

1. **For fast texture & coarse geometry finetuning without global effects**, *nvdiffrast* modular primitives deliver 5-10× speed and lowest memory [2][3]. Use antialias op for silhouette gradients; supplement with Laplacian regularization.

2. **For physically accurate material/lighting decomposition, accurate pose under global illumination**, *Mitsuba 3* with `prb_reparam` is mandatory [4][5][12]. Accept 5-10× runtime for correct inter-reflection gradients.

3. **For topology-fluid reconstruction needing real-time view synthesis**, *3DGS differentiable rasterization* with hardware-accelerated backward [7] provides best quality-speed trade-off, at cost of surface ambiguity.

4. **Warped-area sampling with top-k sphere tracer weighting** enables mask-free neural SDF optimization competitive with mask-supervised methods [8], unlocking in-the-wild capture.

Future work: integrating differentiable rendering with diffusion priors for ill-posed inverse problems, second-order optimization via Dr.Jit Hessians, and hardware-software co-design for mixed raster/path differential pipelines akin to ReSTIR DI for gradients.

*Inverse graphics is not merely differentiable — it must be discontinuity-aware.* Choosing the calculus-aligned estimator and matching system architecture to physics of the scene are inseparable.
