# High-Frequency Gravitational Wave Detection with Pulsar Timing Arrays: Hellings-Downs Correlation, Bayesian Inference for Stochastic Background, and NANOGrav 15-Year Dataset Constraints

## Abstract

Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs) transform Galactic millisecond pulsars into a kiloparsec-scale gravitational-wave detector sensitive to 1–100 nHz. The recent NANOGrav 15-year dataset comprising 67 pulsars reports 3.5–4σ evidence for Hellings-Downs-correlated common red noise consistent with a stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB). This thesis synthesizes the theoretical foundation of the Hellings-Downs curve from the overlap reduction function, its Legendre decomposition and cosmic variance, with the full Bayesian hierarchical inference pipeline using ENTERPRISE and PTMCMCSampler for SGWB characterization. We derive the optimal statistic, power-law strain modeling h_c = A(f/f_yr)^α, and constraints on supermassive black-hole binary populations with median amplitude A_yr = 2.4^{+0.7}_{-0.6}×10⁻¹⁵. We detail noise modeling, Bayes factors >10¹⁴ vs independent noise and 200–1000 vs uncorrelated common process, and outline future high-frequency sensitivity to individual binaries and anisotropic mapping.

## 1 Introduction

The **nanohertz gravitational-wave sky** is inaccessible to ground-based interferometers and even to space-borne LISA (mHz). It is probed instead by *pulsar timing arrays*, exploiting the extraordinary rotational stability of millisecond pulsars (MSPs) as celestial clocks [1][7]. A gravitational wave (GW) transiting Earth and a pulsar induces a fractional Doppler shift in pulse frequency, integrated into timing residuals Δτ(t) of order ~10–100 ns for strain h ~10⁻¹⁵ [3][5].

The central challenge is **discriminating** a common gravitational signal from intrinsic pulsar spin noise, interstellar medium dispersion, and clock/ephemeris errors. Single-pulsar limits pioneered by Sazhin (1978) and Detweiler (1979) set h_c <10⁻¹³ but were noise-limited. The conceptual breakthrough was Detweiler (1979) and Hellings & Downs (1983): *cross-correlating* residuals from *multiple* pulsars distributed over the sky reveals a unique angular correlation signature that no other noise source can mimic [7][4].

In June 2023, NANOGrav, EPTA+InPTA, PPTA, and CPTA independently reported evidence for this signature [1][2]. The NANOGrav 15-year dataset (NG15) with 67 MSPs timed over 15.9 yr, 12.5 yr of which with wideband receivers, provides the most stringent result: a common-spectrum process with **Hellings-Downs correlations** favored by Bayes factors of 200–1000 over an uncorrelated common red noise (CURN), and p=5×10⁻⁵ to 1.9×10⁻⁴ (~3.5–4σ) in frequentist optimal statistic [1][3].

> Theorem: Hellings-Downs Uniqueness — For an isotropic, unpolarized, stationary Gaussian SGWB composed of transverse-traceless GR modes (+, ×), the expected zero-lag correlation of timing residuals from pulsar pair (p,q) depends only on angular separation γ_pq via μ_HD(γ) = 1/3 + (1-cosγ)/2[ln((1-cosγ)/2)-1/6] up to normalization, with c_0=c_1=0. No isotropic clock error (monopole) or ephemeris error (dipole) reproduces this l≥2 shape [7][3].

This thesis provides a **PhD-level end-to-end treatment** of:

- The **Hellings-Downs overlap reduction function** derivation, Legendre expansion, and cosmic variance.
- **Bayesian inference** architecture: ENTERPRISE likelihood, Gaussian processes for achromatic red noise and chromatic dispersion measure variations, and PTMCMCSampler hierarchical sampling [8][9].
- **NG15 constraints**: amplitude, spectral index, astrophysical interpretation as supermassive black-hole binary (SMBHB) ensemble, and limits on individual binaries [2].
- High-frequency extension to continuous waves (CWs) at 4–170 nHz and prospects for *single-source* detection.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Gravitational Wave Timing Residuals

For a plane GW propagating along k̂, metric perturbation h_ij(t - k̂·x) = Σ_A e^A_ij h_A(t), A∈{+,×}. The fractional frequency shift for pulsar p at direction p̂_p:

```python
# Timing residual response to GW
import numpy as np
def gw_redshift(p_hat, k_hat, e_plus, e_cross, h_plus, h_cross):
    # antenna pattern F^A = 1/2 p^i p^j / (1+ k·p) e^A_ij
    denom = 1 + np.dot(k_hat, p_hat)
    Fp = 0.5 * np.einsum('i,j,ij', p_hat, p_hat, e_plus) / denom
    Fx = 0.5 * np.einsum('i,j,ij', p_hat, p_hat, e_cross) / denom
    return Fp*h_plus + Fx*h_cross

def residual(t, z_of_t):
    # Δτ(t) = ∫_0^t z(t') dt'  where z = Δν/ν
    return np.cumtrapz(z_of_t, t, initial=0)
```

The full residual includes Earth term z_E(t) and pulsar term z_P(t - D_p(1+ k̂·p̂)/c) retarded by light-travel time D_p ~kpc [5][7]. For SGWB, pulsar terms add incoherently and contribute variance but zero mean correlation.

### 2.2 Stochastic Background Spectrum

An isotropic SGWB is described by characteristic strain:

* **Power-law:** h_c(f) = A (f / f_yr)^α where α = (3-γ)/2, γ is timing-residual PSD exponent [1].
* For circular GW-driven SMBHBs: α = -2/3 → γ = 13/3 ≈ 4.33 [1][10].
* Energy density: Ω_gw(f) = (2π²/3H₀²) f² h_c² ∝ f^{2+2α}

NANOGrav 15-yr infers γ ~3.2 ±0.6, shallower than 13/3, suggesting environmental coupling (gas, eccentricity, stellar scattering) or cosmological contribution [1][4].

### 2.3 Pulsar Noise Budget

| Component | Spectral Model | Correlation | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| White noise EFAC/EQUAD/ECORR | ∝ σ_TOA² | Uncorrelated | Per-backend fit |
| Achromatic Red Noise (RN) | PL: A_RN f^{-γ_RN} | Uncorrelated per pulsar | 30 Fourier modes GP |
| Dispersion Measure DM | ν^{-2} × GP | Chromatic, ∝ ν⁻² | 100 modes + DMX |
| Scattering | ν^{-4.4} | Chromatic | Wideband timing |
| Common Red Noise (CURN) | PL common A,γ | Monopole, no HD | Model comparison |
| GW Background (GWB) | PL + HD ORF | HD-correlated | Target signal |
| Solar System Ephemeris | Dipole | Partial | BAYESEPHEM, DE440 |

Wideband timing and DMX model dispersion to <0.1 pc cm⁻³ precision critical for NG15 [1][10].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows NG15 enterprise pipeline [8][9][1]:

1. **TOA generation**: narrowband and wideband TOAs from Green Bank Telescope (GBT) and Arecibo (pre-2020) plus VLA, with 810 MHz – 2.3 GHz coverage.

2. **Timing model fit** via `TEMPO2`/`PINT`: deterministic parameters (spin, spindown, astrometry, binary Keplerian) marginalized analytically.

3. **Gaussian Process likelihood**:
   ```haskell
   -- ENTERPRISE pseudo-Haskell signal model
   data Signal = WhiteNoise EFAC EQUAD
               | RedNoise  { log10_A :: Double, gamma :: Double, nmodes :: Int }
               | DMNoise   { log10_A_DM :: Double, gamma_DM :: Double }
               | Common    { log10_A_c :: Double, gamma_c :: Double, orf :: ORF }
   data ORF = Uncorrelated | HellingsDowns | Dipole | Monopole
   likelihood residuals = Gaussian 0 (N_white + T*B*phi*B^T*T^T)
   -- T design matrix, B Fourier basis, phi powerlaw covariance
   ```
   Covariance C = N + T B φ Bᵀ Tᵀ where φ = diag(P(f_i)) with P(f)=A²/(12π²)f_yr⁻³ (f/f_yr)^-γ / Tspan [8][10].

4. **Bayesian sampling**: PTMCMCSampler with 62+ parameters (2 per pulsar RN + 2 common) for CURN, + HD requires evaluating ORF matrix Γ_pq = μ_HD(γ_pq). Likelihood evaluation O(N³_pulsar × N_TOA) optimized via Woodbury lemma → tens ms CURN, ~0.3 s HD [9].

5. **Model comparison**: Product-space sampling for Bayes factor BF = Z_HD / Z_CURN, or Savage-Dickey at A=0. Background distribution via **sky scrambles** and **phase shifts** that destroy HD correlations while preserving common power [1][3].

6. **Optimal Statistic (OS)**: Frequentist cross-correlation estimator:

   $$
   \hat{A}^2 = \frac{\sum_{p<q} r_p^T C_p^{-1} \tilde{S}_{pq} C_q^{-1} r_q}{\sum_{p<q} \text{Tr}[C_p^{-1}\tilde{S}_{pq}C_q^{-1}\tilde{S}_{qp}]}
   $$
   where \tilde{S}_{pq}=Γ_{pq} S(f) Fourier-domain signal covariance [6]. Signal-to-noise SNR = Â² / σ_Â.

```rust
// Optimal statistic core (Rust sketch)
fn optimal_stat(residuals: &[Vector], inv_covs: &[Matrix], orf: &Matrix, psd: &[f64]) -> (f64, f64) {
    let mut num = 0.0; let mut denom = 0.0;
    for p in 0..npulsar {
        for q in (p+1)..npulsar {
            let s_pq = orf[[p,q]] * psd_matrix(psd); // Γ_pq * S(f)
            num += residuals[p].dot(&inv_covs[p].dot(&s_pq.dot(&inv_covs[q].dot(&residuals[q]))));
            denom += (inv_covs[p].dot(&s_pq).dot(&inv_covs[q].dot(&s_pq.t()))).trace();
        }
    }
    (num/denom, 1.0/denom.sqrt())
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hellings-Downs Curve Derivation and Legendre Structure

The **overlap reduction function** Γ(γ) = ⟨F^A(p̂)F^A(q̂)⟩_k̂,A sky-polarization average. For GR TT modes:

*Analytic form* [7][3]:

$$
\mu_u(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}x + \frac{3}{2}x\ln x,\quad x = \frac{1-\cos\gamma}{2}
$$

Normalization conventions vary: NANOGrav uses Γ(0)=0.5 for auto-correlation 1+δ_pq term, and Γ →1/3 at γ→0 for cross-only. Some literature defines (3/2)μ to set 1 at 0. The NG15 paper plots (3/2)μ normalized to 0.5 at zero separation due to pulsar term [1][5].

> Theorem: Legendre Spectrum — Expectation coefficients ⟨c_l⟩ = (2l+1)/[(l+2)(l+1)l(l-1)] for l≥2, zero otherwise, implying quadrupole (l=2) dominates: ⟨c_2⟩/Σ⟨c_l⟩=0.62. This explains HD sensitivity primarily via quadrupole anisotropy despite isotropy assumption [4][5].

*Proof sketch*: Expand plane-wave response e^{ik·x} in spherical harmonics, integrate |Y_lm|² against antenna pattern. Orthogonality kills l=0,1 for TT because transverse condition removes monopole/dipole. Coefficient integral yields Beta functions → rational form [4][5]. QED.

**Cosmic variance** [5]: For finite pulsar sky, estimator variance does not vanish even with zero noise due to interference of GW sources. Variance:

$$
\sigma^2_{cos}(\gamma)=h^4 \sum_l \frac{2l+1}{8\pi} c_l^2 [1+P_l(\cos\gamma)^2] - h^4\mu_u(\gamma)^2
$$

For 67 pulsars, binned cosmic variance ~0.2–0.3 at 90° separation, comparable to NG15 error bars [5][3].

### 4.2 Bayesian Hierarchical Inference with ENTERPRISE

ENTERPRISE implements **hyper-efficient marginalization** [8]. Timing model parameters δ ε analytically marginalized via design matrix M: likelihood p(δt | φ) ∝ det(C)⁻½ exp(-½ δtᵀ C⁻¹ δt) where C = N + M Σ Mᵀ. Red noise modeled as rank-reduced Fourier GP with 30 frequencies f_i = i/Tspan, i=1..30, Tspan=16.03 yr → f_min=1.98 nHz.

**PTMCMCSampler** [9] features:

- *Adaptive Metropolis* with differential evolution proposals.
- *Parallel tempering* with ~10 temperatures to sample multimodal posteriors (e.g., bimodal at γ~3 vs 5).
- *Prior*: log10 A_gwb ∈ U[-18,-11], γ ∈ U[0,7] for common process; RN per pulsar log10 A_RN ∈ U[-20,-11], γ_RN ∈ U[0,7].

Computational cost: NG15 HD chain requires ~10⁶ iterations × 0.3 s = 3.5 days single-core, but parallel tempering + MPI yields ~6 hr on 100 cores. CURN chain ~ tens ms → hours [9].

*Variational acceleration* recent work [10] shows normalizing flows reduce HD runtime to minutes by learning posterior approximation then importance-weighting to true likelihood — critical for IPTA joint dataset with >150 pulsars where O(N³) would be prohibitive.

### 4.3 NANOGrav 15-Year Dataset Results and High-Frequency Extension

**NG15 dataset specifics** [1][2]:

- 67 MSPs, median baseline 12.4 yr, max 15.9 yr.
- ~700k TOAs, wideband TOAs 2010–2023 improve DM precision 3×.
- Noise model per pulsar: EFAC, EQUAD, ECORR + RN + DM + scattering (selected pulsars) + chromatic scattering.

**Key posteriors**:

| Parameter | Median | 90% CI | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| log10 A_yr (f_yr=1/yr) | -14.62 | [-14.9, -14.25] | HD PL |
| γ_gwb | 3.2 | [2.6, 3.9] | HD PL |
| A (strain at f_yr) | 2.4e-15 | [1.8,3.1]e-15 | f^{-2/3} fixed γ=13/3 |
| BF HD/CURN | 200–1000 | spectral model dep | Product-space |
| BF GWB / RN-only | >10¹⁴ | decisively favored | |
| OS SNR | 4.0 | p=5e-5 –1.9e-4 | Frequentist |

Astrophysical interpretation [1][10]: SMBHB population with merger rate density ~10⁻⁴ Mpc⁻³ Gyr⁻¹, mass function dN/dM ∝ M^{-1.5}, environmental hardening required to flatten γ from 13/3 to 3.2. Alternative: cosmological SGWB from inflation (r <10⁻⁵), cosmic strings (Gμ ~10⁻¹⁰), phase transition at T~GeV.

**High-frequency CW limits** [2]: Search for deterministic sinusoid h(t)=h₀ sin(2πft+Φ₀) with evolving frequency due to chirp. Weak candidates:

- 4 nHz: Bayes factor 2.5, diminished to 1.2 when HD background included → likely common noise leak.
- 170 nHz: BF ~5 but support from single pulsar J1713+0747 whose binary period ~170 nHz alias; no host galaxy in 1 deg² localization volume out to 1 Gpc (M >10⁹ M⊙ BH should have host).

Upper limit at most sensitive 6 nHz: h₀ <8×10⁻¹⁵ 95% sky-averaged, exclusion radius R_eff ~50 Mpc for M_c=10⁹ M⊙ [2]. High-frequency sensitivity ∝ Tspan⁻½ f^{-13/6} for PTA — improving with longer Tspan and more pulsars.

### 4.4 Optimal Estimator, Sky Scrambles, and Anisotropy

**Optimal statistic** derivation [6]: Matched filter maximizing SNR for HD correlation. NG15 OS gives Â²_HD = (2.1±0.5)×10⁻³⁰ corresponding to A=2.3e-15 consistent with Bayesian [1]. Noise-marginalized OS accounts for RN uncertainty by averaging over posterior draws.

**Null tests**:

- *Sky scrambles*: Randomize pulsar positions preserving pairwise separations distribution, recompute BF_HD/CURN 10⁴ times → p=10⁻³ (3σ) for observed BF [1][3].
- *Phase shifts*: Fourier phases of common process randomized → destroys interpulsar coherence.
- *Leave-one-out*: Remove pulsar with highest dropout factor (J1909-3744) reduces BF by factor 2 but retains >100 significance → signal not single-pulsar dominated.

**Anisotropy**: Expand SGWB power P(Ω̂)=Σ_lm c_lm Y_lm(Ω̂). NG15 anisotropy search [1 suppl.] finds no significant l>0 power: C_l/C_0 <0.3 at 95% for l=1–4. Future IPTA with 200 pulsars can probe dipole anisotropy from local large-scale structure at SNR~5 for SMBHB background [5].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

> Theorem: Detection Significance Scaling — For N_p pulsars timed with white RMS σ, Tspan, cadence c, SGWB amplitude A, HD detection SNR scales as SNR_HD ∝ A² Tspan^{γ-1/2} N_p (N_p-1)/σ² / sqrt(2Tspan/c). For γ=13/3, SNR ∝ Tspan^{13/3}. Thus NG15 15.9 yr vs NG12.5 12.9 yr improves SNR by (15.9/12.9)^{13/6}≈1.6, explaining transition from common process to HD evidence [1][6].

*Empirical verification*:

- Simulation of 100 universe realizations with SMBHB population (holodeck) shows BF_HD/CURN distribution median 300, 68% interval 80–1200, matching NG15 observed 200–1000 → NG15 consistent with SMBHB ensemble variance [1][3].
- Injection recovery: Simulated SGWB with A=2e-15, γ=13/3 into NG15 TOAs recovered with bias <0.1σ for A, <0.2σ for γ when RN properly modeled; mis-modeled DM leaks into common γ flattening by Δγ≈-0.8 [10].
- **Bayes factor calibration**: With ENTERPRISE extension `enterprise_extensions`, hypermodel sampling 5×10⁶ steps yields BF error <20% (log BF σ~0.2) for BF~200. Requires prior volume correction for HD vs CURN nested models via Savage-Dickey at A→0 where prior dominates [9].

## 6 Limitations

- **Pulsar term decorrelation**: At f>1/Tspan ~2 nHz, pulsar term uncorrelated but adds self-noise variance σ²_pulsar = h²/3, reducing SNR by factor (1+ σ²_pulsar/σ²_white)⁻½. For high-frequency CW >10 nHz, pulsar term can be resolved and improves sky localization to <10 deg² [2].
- **Chromatic noise degeneracy**: DM variations with solar wind (n_e ∝ r⁻²) mimic γ≈8/3 spectrum similar to GWB. NG15 mitigation via DMX + solar wind model with variable amplitude still leaves 10% covariance between DM and common γ [1][10].
- **Ephemeris errors**: DE440 vs DE421 ephemeris changes BF_HD/CURN by factor ~2 due to Jupiter orbit uncertainty coupling to dipole. BAYESEPHEM marginalization broadens A posterior by 15% [1].
- **Cosmic variance fundamental limit**: Even with infinite pulsars, HD curve measured with variance σ_cos ~0.1–0.3 due to single-universe realization of SGWB [5]. Cannot distinguish isotropic SGWB from anisotropic with few hot spots without >100 pulsars.
- **Computational scaling**: Full HD likelihood O(N_p² N_f²) where N_f=30 → matrix 2010×2010 inversion per likelihood. For SKA-era 10³ pulsars, O(10⁹) operations per likelihood impossible — requires factorized likelihood or neural posterior estimation [10][9].
- **High-frequency sensitivity**: PTA sensitivity drops as f^{3/2} above f~1/yr due to white noise floor and irregular sampling. Detecting individual SMBHBs at 10–100 nHz requires next-gen receivers (RMS <30 ns) and 10+ yr more timing.

## 7 Conclusion

The NANOGrav 15-year dataset marks the **dawn of nanohertz gravitational-wave astronomy** [1]. The measurement of Hellings-Downs correlations at 3.5–4σ with Bayes factors >200 vs uncorrelated common noise establishes that the common red process is not instrumental or environmental but gravitational. The inferred strain A_yr=2.4×10⁻¹⁵ is consistent with astrophysical expectations for a population of inspiraling SMBHBs with masses 10⁸–10¹⁰ M⊙ out to z~1, although cosmological sources (cosmic strings, phase transitions, inflationary tensors) remain viable and may contribute to the observed shallow spectral index γ≈3.2 vs 13/3 [1][4].

Future progress lies in three axes: (i) **Increasing N_p and Tspan** via IPTA Data Release 3 (~115 pulsars, 20 yr) to achieve 5σ HD detection and constrain γ to ±0.2; (ii) **High-frequency CW searches** for individual SMBHBs enabling host galaxy identification and standard-siren H₀ measurement; (iii) **Anisotropy and polarization** tests: searching for scalar-transverse, vector, or longitudinal modes predicted by alternative gravity theories which modify HD shape to include monopole/dipole components [7][6].

Pulsar timing arrays have become, in effect, a Galactic-scale detector whose arms are the Earth-pulsar lines of sight, and whose mirrors are the millisecond pulsars themselves — a detector that has now heard the hum of the universe's most massive binaries.
