# Chromatin Architecture and the Three-Dimensional Genome: Topologically Associating Domains, Loop Extrusion, and Phase Separation

## Abstract

The linear DNA sequence of the human genome — roughly two meters of chromatin fiber — is folded into a nucleus barely ten micrometers across, yet genes are regulated with exquisite precision across genomic distances that dwarf any protein complex. This thesis examines how three-dimensional genome architecture solves that problem. We trace the experimental revolution, from chromosome conformation capture to Hi-C, Micro-C, and single-cell contact mapping, that revealed chromosome territories, A/B compartments, and topologically associating domains (TADs). We dissect the loop extrusion model, in which cohesin complexes actively extrude chromatin until stalled by convergently oriented CTCF boundaries, and we evaluate liquid–liquid phase separation as a complementary organizing principle of heterochromatin. We survey enhancer–promoter loops, the single-cell and super-resolution imaging results that challenge the population-average picture of TADs, and the clinical face of nuclear architecture — TADopathies and enhancer hijacking in cancer. Synthesizing polymer physics, genomics, and cell biology, we argue that genome architecture is neither a static scaffold nor experimental noise, but a dynamical, multiscale system whose principles are only now becoming quantitative.

## 1 Introduction

Consider the central paradox of gene regulation. A typical mammalian enhancer activates a promoter located tens or hundreds of kilobases away in linear sequence — a distance occupied by dozens of intervening genes, silencers, and architectural landmarks. Transcription factors diffuse in three dimensions, yet regulation is exquisitely specific in one dimension. How does the cell fold a one-dimensional polymer so that the correct three-dimensional contacts form, reproducibly, in every cell, while incorrect contacts are suppressed?

For most of the twentieth century, interphase chromatin was treated as an amorphous mass presumed irrelevant to function. The first crack came from chromosome painting, which showed that each chromosome occupies a discrete **chromosome territory** rather than mixing freely with its neighbors [1]. The decisive break came in 2009, when Lieberman-Aiden and colleagues introduced **Hi-C**, a genome-wide variant of chromosome conformation capture, and published the first all-versus-all contact maps of the human genome [1]. Those maps contained two revelations: contact probability decays with genomic distance as a power law consistent with a *fractal globule* — a knot-free, unentangled polymer state — and the genome partitions into two compartments, A and B, correlating with transcriptional activity.

Three years later, Dixon and colleagues reported that chromosomes are further partitioned into **topologically associating domains (TADs)**: megabase-scale blocks within which loci contact each other frequently, separated by boundaries across which contacts are depleted [2]. In 2014, Rao and colleagues pushed Hi-C to kilobase resolution and found ~10,000 chromatin loops, most anchored by CTCF bound in convergent orientation [3]. Two competing — ultimately complementary — physical models emerged to explain these patterns: **loop extrusion**, in which the cohesin motor complex actively extrudes chromatin into loops until stalled by CTCF [4][5], and **phase separation**, in which heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1) and related factors condense inactive chromatin into liquid-like droplets [7][8].

We proceed from chromatin background and Hi-C methodology through a deep dive on TADs, extrusion, phase separation, regulation, and single-cell evidence, closing with limitations and conclusions.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From nucleosomes to territories

The fundamental unit is the **nucleosome**: 147 bp of DNA wrapped around a histone octamer; linked nucleosomes form the 10-nm "beads-on-a-string" fiber. Whether this fiber coils into a regular **30-nm fiber** *in vivo* is debated: cryo-EM found little evidence for ordered 30-nm fibers in cells, suggesting a disordered polymer melt [10]. Above the fiber scale, each chromosome occupies a **chromosome territory** — a discrete, largely non-intermixing volume within the nucleus [1][10].

### 2.2 Contact probability scaling and the fractal globule

Hi-C measures, for every pair of genomic loci, how often they are found in spatial proximity. The resulting contact matrix $M$ has entries $M_{ij}$ proportional to the contact probability $P(s)$ at genomic separation $s = |i - j|$. Lieberman-Aiden *et al.* found that over 500 kb to 7 Mb, contact probability scales as:

$$P(s) \propto s^{-\alpha}, \quad \alpha \approx 1.0$$

This exponent is diagnostic: an **equilibrium globule** yields $\alpha = 3/2$ and is highly knotted, while a **fractal globule** — formed by rapid hierarchical collapse — yields $\alpha = 1$ and is essentially knot-free [1]. Interphase chromatin is thus trapped in a long-lived, unentangled state permitting local unfolding without topological catastrophe.

### 2.3 A/B compartments

The second discovery in the 2009 maps was compartmentalization. Computing the Pearson correlation matrix of the observed-over-expected contact map and taking its first **principal component (eigenvector)** partitions each chromosome into two spatial compartments [1]:

- **Compartment A**: gene-rich, transcriptionally active, enriched in H3K36me3 and DNase hypersensitivity; loci in A preferentially contact other A loci.
- **Compartment B**: gene-poor, transcriptionally silent, enriched in H3K9me3 and lamina association; B contacts B.

Rao *et al.* later refined this into six **subcompartments** (A1, A2, B1–B4) with distinct epigenetic signatures [3].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Hi-C protocol

Hi-C converts spatial proximity into DNA sequence through a five-step chemistry [1][3]:

1. **Crosslinking** with formaldehyde freezes 3D contacts in place.
2. **Digestion** with a restriction enzyme (HindIII originally; MboI/DpnII four-cutters for higher resolution).
3. **Biotin fill-in and proximity ligation** joins crosslinked, spatially proximal ends into chimeric junctions.
4. **Pull-down and paired-end sequencing** of biotinylated junctions.
5. **Mapping** each read pair independently; pairs on different fragments become matrix contacts.

Resolution is set by fragment size: HindIII gives ~1 Mb effective resolution in early maps, while in-situ Hi-C with MboI reached 1–5 kb [3].

### 3.2 Computational processing

Raw matrices carry technical biases (fragment length, GC, mappability), corrected by **iterative correction (ICE)** or **Knight–Ruiz (KR) balancing**, which rescale rows and columns to equal visibility [3].

Downstream analyses include:

- **Compartment calling**: eigenvector decomposition of the correlation matrix of observed/expected contacts [1].
- **Directionality index (DI)**: for each bin, Dixon *et al.* computed $\mathrm{DI} = \left(\frac{B-A}{|B-A|}\right)\left(\frac{(A-E)^2}{E} + \frac{(B-E)^2}{E}\right)$, where $A$ and $B$ are upstream and downstream contact counts; a **hidden Markov model** on the DI segments the genome into TADs [2].
- **Insulation score**: Crane *et al.* defined, for each bin, the sum of contacts crossing a diamond centered on the bin; local minima mark boundaries — a method robust across resolutions.
- **HiCCUPS**: Rao *et al.* called loops as pixels enriched relative to four local neighborhoods (horizontal, vertical, lower-left, donut), yielding ~10,000 loops [3].

```python
import numpy as np

def insulation_score(matrix: np.ndarray, window: int = 10) -> np.ndarray:
    """Insulation score per bin of a KR-normalized matrix; boundaries are local minima."""
    n = matrix.shape[0]
    scores = np.full(n, np.nan)
    for i in range(window, n - window):
        diamond = matrix[i - window:i, i:i + window]
        scores[i] = np.nansum(diamond)
    # normalize by genome-wide mean for comparability
    return scores / np.nanmean(scores)

def call_boundaries(scores: np.ndarray, min_dist: int = 5) -> list[int]:
    """Return bins that are local minima of the insulation profile."""
    bnd = []
    for i in range(1, len(scores) - 1):
        if np.isnan(scores[i]):
            continue
        if scores[i] < scores[i - 1] and scores[i] < scores[i + 1]:
            if not bnd or i - bnd[-1] >= min_dist:
                bnd.append(i)
    return bnd
```

### 3.3 Beyond bulk Hi-C

- **Micro-C** uses micrococcal nuclease for nucleosome-level resolution, revealing fine promoter–enhancer stripes.
- **Single-cell Hi-C** profiles contacts in individual nuclei, exposing cell-to-cell variability.
- **Super-resolution imaging** (DNA seqFISH+, ORCA, multiplexed FISH) traces chromatin paths at nanometer precision, testing whether TADs exist in single nuclei [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Topologically associating domains

TADs appear on Hi-C maps as squares of enriched contact frequency straddling the diagonal: loci within a TAD contact each other far more often than loci in adjacent TADs at the same linear distance [2]. Dixon *et al.* identified ~2,200 TADs in mouse embryonic stem cells with a **median size of 880 kb** (range: tens of kb to several Mb); Rao *et al.* found 4,000–9,000 contact domains per human cell line with a median of **185 kb** at higher resolution [2][3]. TAD boundaries are enriched for:

- **CTCF binding sites**, housekeeping promoters, and **SINE retrotransposons**,
- sharp transitions in histone marks and replication timing.

Genes within a TAD tend to be co-regulated, and boundaries are substantially conserved across cell types and species — roughly 60–70% shared between human and mouse — suggesting a fundamental architectural unit [2][10].

### 4.2 The loop extrusion model

The molecular cast: **loop-extruding factors (LEFs)** — cohesin rings (SMC1/SMC3/RAD21/STAG) — loaded by **NIPBL**, released by **WAPL** (residence time ~20 min in G1), and stalled by **CTCF** barriers bound in specific orientations.

The mechanism: a LEF loads onto chromatin and translocates bidirectionally, reeling DNA through the ring so the loop grows. Extrusion stalls only at CTCF bound to a motif oriented *toward* the oncoming extruder — hence stable loops form almost exclusively between **convergently oriented** CTCF pairs, the convergent rule observed by Rao *et al.* [3][4].

> **Theorem (convergent-orientation rule):** *Under bidirectional loop extrusion with orientation-sensitive barriers, the steady-state contact map exhibits enriched peaks ("corner dots") precisely at pairs of CTCF motifs in convergent orientation; divergent and tandem pairs produce no stable peak.*

> **Proof sketch:** Consider two barriers at positions $x_1 < x_2$ with orientations $o_1, o_2 \in \{+,-\}$. A LEF extruding bidirectionally from between them stalls at $x_1$ iff $o_1 = +$ (facing right) and at $x_2$ iff $o_2 = -$ (facing left). Simultaneous stalling — hence a long-lived loop with a Hi-C peak — requires $o_1 = +, o_2 = -$, i.e., convergent orientation. All other orientation pairs permit at least one side to continue extruding, so no persistent peak accumulates. ∎

With LEF **processivity** $\lambda = v \cdot \tau$ (~100–200 kb) and **separation** $d$ (~100–200 kb), the model predicts — and experiments confirm [4][5][6]:

1. **Corner peaks** at TAD corners where convergent CTCF sites stall extruders;
2. **Stripes/tracks** — lines of enriched contact emanating from a strong boundary, produced by one-sided extrusion;
3. **Loss of TADs upon cohesin depletion** (RAD21 degradation dissolves TADs and peaks within hours) with *strengthened* compartments [6];
4. **Loss of TADs upon CTCF depletion** while extrusion continues to compact chromatin locally [6];
5. **WAPL depletion** lengthens cohesin residence time, producing longer loops, stronger corner peaks, and *weakened* compartmentalization [6].

### 4.3 Compartments, antagonism, and phase separation

Compartments and TADs arise from **two distinct, antagonistic mechanisms** [6]: compartmentalization reflects preferential association of epigenetically similar chromatin, while loop extrusion mixes chromatin locally and *counteracts* segregation. Removing cohesin *strengthens* compartment checkerboards; increasing extrusion *weakens* them [6]. TADs are therefore not building blocks of compartments.

**Phase separation** provides a complementary physical language for the compartment side of this antagonism. Heterochromatin protein 1α (HP1α) undergoes **liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS)** *in vitro* — forming droplets that fuse, wet surfaces, and selectively partition molecules by charge and valency rather than size [7]. In *Drosophila* embryos, heterochromatin forms liquid-like droplets that fuse over time, and purified HP1a recapitulates droplet formation [8].

### 4.4 Enhancer–promoter communication and transcriptional control

Within TADs, **enhancer–promoter (E–P) loops** bring distal regulatory elements into contact with their target genes. TAD boundaries act as insulators: an enhancer normally activates only promoters within its own TAD. Deleting or inverting a boundary permits **ectopic E–P contacts**, and the resulting misexpression causes developmental disease — the class of disorders termed **TADopathies** [10][11].

The landmark demonstration came from Lupiáñez *et al.*, who showed that structural variants disrupting TAD boundaries at the *EPHA4* locus rewire enhancer–promoter contacts and cause human limb malformations: the same enhancers, placed in a new TAD neighborhood, drive ectopic expression of neighboring genes [11]. In cancer, **enhancer hijacking** exploits the same principle: rearrangements reposition strong enhancers near oncogenes. In gliomas, for example, IDH mutations cause hypermethylation of CTCF sites, dissolving a TAD boundary and allowing a constitutive enhancer to activate *PDGFRA* [12]. Genome architecture is thus not merely correlated with gene regulation — its disruption is *causal* in human disease.

### 4.5 The single-cell challenge

A critical caveat shadows everything above: Hi-C is a **population average** over millions of cells. Do TADs exist in any single cell? Single-cell Hi-C revealed that individual nuclei contain **TAD-like domains** — compacted globular regions — but their boundaries vary from cell to cell [10]. Super-resolution tracing confirmed this: Bintu *et al.* imaged chromatin paths at 30-kb resolution and found TAD-like globules in single cells whose boundaries only *statistically* coincide with population TAD boundaries.

In the loop extrusion picture, TADs are **statistical tendencies**: cohesin continuously loads, extrudes, stalls, and releases, so each cell holds a different loop configuration at any instant while the ensemble average reproduces the sharp population TAD [4][6].

## 5 Empirical and Theoretical Evidence

The loop-extrusion-plus-compartments framework earns its status from a tight loop between prediction and perturbation:

| Perturbation | Hi-C observation | Interpretation under the model |
|---|---|---|
| RAD21 (cohesin) degradation | TADs and corner peaks vanish; compartments strengthen | Extrusion generates TADs and *opposes* segregation [6] |
| CTCF degradation | TADs vanish; loops lost; compartments strengthen | Barriers confine extrusion; without them loops are not positioned [6] |
| WAPL depletion | Longer loops, stronger peaks, weaker compartments | Higher processivity λ mixes chromatin more thoroughly [6] |
| NIPBL depletion | Loss of loops and TADs; hyper-compartmentalization | No loading → no extrusion → pure segregation [6] |
| CTCF motif inversion | Peak disappears or relocates | Convergent rule: orientation determines stalling [3][4] |
| Boundary deletion (EPHA4) | Ectopic E–P contacts; limb malformation | Boundaries insulate regulatory neighborhoods [11] |

The evidential logic proceeds in five steps:

1. **Existence**: kilobase-resolution Hi-C reveals loops anchored at convergent CTCF pairs — a pattern with no natural equilibrium-polymer explanation [3].
2. **Sufficiency**: simulations of LEFs with directional barriers reproduce TADs, peaks, stripes, and the convergent rule from a handful of parameters [4][5].
3. **Necessity**: acute degradation of cohesin or CTCF dissolves precisely the features the model attributes to them, on timescales (hours) consistent with extrusion dynamics [6].
4. **Quantitative match**: measured extrusion speeds, residence times, and LEF densities predict the observed TAD size range [4][6].
5. **Direct visualization**: single-molecule assays show SMC complexes reeling DNA into loops in real time, closing the gap between model and mechanism.

A minimal formal sketch of the extrusion dynamics can be stated as a state machine:

```tla
--------------------------- MODULE LoopExtrusion ---------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES posL, posR,      \* left/right extruder positions on the fiber
          stalledL, stalledR,
          bound            \* LEF still associated with chromatin

Init == /\ posL \in Nat /\ posR \in Nat /\ posL < posR
        /\ stalledL = FALSE /\ stalledR = FALSE /\ bound = TRUE

\* Extrude outward unless stalled at a convergent CTCF barrier
Extrude == /\ bound
           /\ \/ /\ ~stalledL /\ posL' = posL - 1
              \/ /\ stalledL  /\ posL' = posL
           /\ \/ /\ ~stalledR /\ posR' = posR + 1
              \/ /\ stalledR  /\ posR' = posR
           /\ UNCHANGED <<stalledL, stalledR, bound>>

\* WAPL-mediated unloading dissolves the loop
Unload == /\ bound' = FALSE
          /\ UNCHANGED <<posL, posR, stalledL, stalledR>>

Next == Extrude \/ Unload
=============================================================================
```

## 6 Limitations

The field's confidence should be tempered by genuine limitations.

**Algorithmic dependence.** TAD boundaries shift with the caller: directionality-index HMMs, insulation scores, and clustering methods disagree on 20–40% of boundaries, and there is no ground-truth definition of a TAD independent of the assay [10].

**Population averaging.** As Section 4.5 argued, bulk TADs are ensemble properties. Functional claims must reckon with the fact that no single cell may contain the textbook structure.

**Resolution and chemistry.** Hi-C resolution is bounded by restriction-fragment size and sequencing depth; formaldehyde crosslinking captures an unknown mixture of direct and indirect proximities; and ligation efficiency varies with fragment properties. Micro-C and imaging mitigate but do not eliminate these concerns.

**Phase separation: correlation versus causation.** HP1α forms droplets *in vitro* at possibly supra-physiological concentrations, and some measurements find heterochromatin behaving as a collapsed gel rather than a liquid. Whether LLPS *drives* heterochromatin formation *in vivo* remains contested.

**Causality.** Transcription itself reshapes architecture — RNA polymerase acts as a moving extrusion barrier — so the arrow of causation runs both ways.

## 7 Conclusion

Three decades of work have replaced the amorphous interphase nucleus with a quantitative, multiscale architecture. At the megabase scale, chromosomes segregate into territories and phase-separate into A/B compartments. At the sub-megabase scale, the cohesin motor actively extrudes chromatin into loops, stalled by the directional logic of CTCF, generating TADs as statistical steady states of a dynamical process. At the kilobase scale, enhancer–promoter loops execute the regulatory program, insulated by boundaries whose disruption causes developmental disorders and cancer.

The deepest lesson is the **antagonism at the heart of the system**: loop extrusion and compartmental segregation are independent, competing forces, and genome architecture is their negotiated equilibrium [6]. The next decade belongs to methods that watch this dynamics unfold in living single cells at nucleosome resolution, and to theories that predict not just the average map but the full distribution of configurations from which the cell computes regulation.
