# State-Space Sequence Models: HiPPO Theory, Structured State Spaces (S4), and Selective State Spaces (Mamba)

## Abstract

State-space models (SSMs) define sequence maps through a latent state governed by a linear ODE, discretized into a recurrence — and, when time-invariant, equivalently a convolution. This thesis traces the lineage from HiPPO's optimal polynomial memory to S4 and selective SSMs. We present HiPPO-LegS as the online $L^2$-optimal projection of signal history onto Legendre polynomials; derive S4's diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) parameterization, which renders the convolutional kernel computable in $\tilde{O}(L)$ via the Woodbury identity and Cauchy-kernel evaluation; and analyze the selection mechanism — input-dependent $\Delta, B, C$ — that restores content-aware gating at the cost of the convolution view, regained as a hardware-aware parallel associative scan. We survey diagonal simplifications (DSS, S4D, S5) and the State Space Duality unifying SSMs with attention, review empirical landmarks (Long Range Arena, 91% sequential CIFAR-10, $5\times$ inference speedups, Transformer parity in language modeling), and close with limitations: the fixed-state compression bottleneck, coarse selectivity, and open questions on measures and expressivity.

## 1. Introduction

Sequence modeling is the problem of learning functions over ordered inputs — text, audio, time series, video — whose informative context may span thousands or millions of timesteps. For nearly a decade, two paradigms have contested this territory. **Recurrent neural networks** (RNNs) compress history into a fixed-dimensional hidden state, achieving $O(1)$ inference cost per step but suffering from sequential training, vanishing gradients, and empirically poor long-range memory. **Transformers** dispense with recurrence entirely, attending to the full context in $O(L^2)$ time and memory, enabling massive parallelization at the cost of quadratic scaling that makes million-token contexts prohibitively expensive [1].

State-space models occupy a third position. Originating in control theory and signal processing, a continuous-time state-space model describes a dynamical system:

> **Definition 1.1 (Continuous-time SSM).** Let $u(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ be a scalar input signal. A linear state-space model of order $N$ is specified by matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$ and the scalar $D$, governing the latent state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ via
>
> $$\dot{x}(t) = A x(t) + B u(t), \qquad y(t) = C x(t) + D u(t).$$

This is a familiar object — essentially the dynamics of a linear time-invariant (LTI) filter. What makes it a deep learning layer is a set of insights, accumulated between 2019 and 2024, that turn this classical system into a drop-in replacement for attention:

1. **HiPPO (2020):** There is a *principled* choice of $A$ and $B$ — the HiPPO-LegS matrices — that makes $x(t)$ the optimal online compression of the entire signal history into $N$ polynomial coefficients [2].
2. **S4 (2021):** A structured (normal-plus-low-rank) parameterization of $A$ makes the SSM's convolutional kernel computable efficiently, yielding a model that is simultaneously a fast parallel convolution during training and a fast recurrence during inference [1].
3. **Mamba (2023):** Making the SSM *selective* — input-dependent $\Delta$, $B$, $C$ — restores the content-aware gating that LTI systems lack, breaking the convolution equivalence but regaining it as an efficient parallel associative scan with hardware-aware implementation, matching Transformers on language modeling with linear complexity [3].

The thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the mathematical background: HiPPO theory, discretization, and the three equivalent views of an LTI SSM (continuous ODE, discrete recurrence, discrete convolution). Section 3 describes methodology: the DPLR parameterization, Cauchy-kernel computation, discretization schemes, and the selective scan. Section 4 deep-dives into the four pivotal constructions: HiPPO-LegS, S4's DPLR machinery, diagonal simplifications (DSS/S4D/S5), and the selective SSM. Section 5 surveys empirical results and theoretical guarantees. Section 6 enumerates limitations, and Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The Online Function Approximation Problem

The central difficulty of sequence modeling is *memory*: given a stream of observations, retain exactly what future computation needs. HiPPO (High-order Polynomial Projection Operators) formulates this as an **online function approximation** problem. At each time $t$, we seek the best approximation of the entire history $u|_{[0,t]}$ by a degree-$(N-1)$ polynomial, where "best" is measured in $L^2$ under a probability measure $\omega^{(t)}$ on $[0,t]$ [2]:

$$x(t) = \arg\min_{p \in \mathcal{P}_{N-1}} \int_0^t \left(u(\tau) - p(\tau)\right)^2 \, \omega^{(t)}(\tau)\, d\tau.$$

Writing the approximation in an orthonormal polynomial basis, $u(\tau) \approx \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n(t)\, P_n^{(t)}(\tau)$, the coefficient vector $x(t)$ becomes the memory state. The key theorem of HiPPO is that these coefficients can be maintained *online* via a structured linear ODE:

> **Theorem 2.1 (HiPPO dynamics).** For the scaled Legendre measure $\omega^{(t)}(\tau) = \frac{1}{t}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(\tau)$, the optimal coefficients satisfy
>
> $$\dot{x}(t) = \frac{1}{t}\left(A x(t) + B u(t)\right),$$
>
> where $A_{nk} = -\sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1}$ for $n > k$, $A_{nn} = -(n+1)$, $A_{nk} = 0$ for $n < k$, and $B_n = \sqrt{2n+1}$.

The matrix $A$ above is the **HiPPO-LegS** matrix. It has three properties that matter for deep learning [2]:

- **Timescale robustness:** the uniform measure over $[0,t]$ gives the memory no preferred timescale — hence 98.3% on permuted MNIST and robustness to time-warped inputs.
- **Bounded gradients:** the eigenvalues of $A$ lie in the left half-plane, so the dynamics are stable.
- **Optimality:** HiPPO-LegS minimizes reconstruction error among all online linear projections of the history, recovering the Legendre Memory Unit (LMU) as a special case [2].

### 2.2 Three Views of a Linear Time-Invariant SSM

Once $A, B, C$ are fixed (time-invariant), the continuous system admits two equivalent discrete formulations. Fix a step size $\Delta > 0$ and assume $u(t)$ is constant on each interval $[k\Delta, (k+1)\Delta)$ (zero-order hold). Then the state evolves as:

$$x_k = \bar{A}\, x_{k-1} + \bar{B}\, u_k, \qquad y_k = \bar{C}\, x_k,$$

where the discretized matrices are $\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)$, $\bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}(\exp(\Delta A) - I)\,\Delta B$, and $\bar{C} = C$. This is the **recurrent view**: $O(1)$ per-step inference with $O(N)$ state, but inherently sequential — unrolling $L$ steps cannot be parallelized across time.

Unrolling the recurrence with $x_{-1} = 0$ gives:

$$y_k = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \bar{C}\, \bar{A}^{\,j}\, \bar{B}\, u_{k-j},$$

which is the **convolutional view**: $y = \bar{K} \ast u$ with the SSM kernel $\bar{K}_j = \bar{C}\,\bar{A}^{\,j}\,\bar{B}$ for $j = 0, \dots, L-1$. The kernel can be applied in $O(L \log L)$ via FFT, fully parallel across time [1]. The kernel is trivial to *apply* but expensive to *compute* — naively $O(N^2 L)$ for $L$ dense matrix powers. Resolving this tension is the entire S4 project.

The three views and their computational profiles are summarized below:

| View | Equation | Training cost | Inference cost | Parallel across time? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous ODE | $\dot{x} = Ax + Bu$ | n/a (not trainable directly) | n/a | — |
| Discrete recurrence | $x_k = \bar{A}x_{k-1} + \bar{B}u_k$ | $O(NL)$ sequential | $O(N)$ per step | No |
| Discrete convolution | $y = \bar{K} \ast u$, $\bar{K}_j = \bar{C}\bar{A}^j\bar{B}$ | $O(L\log L)$ via FFT | $O(L)$ per step (kernel) | Yes |
| Selective recurrence | $x_k = \bar{A}_k x_{k-1} + \bar{B}_k u_k$ | $O(NL)$ via parallel scan | $O(N)$ per step | Yes (associative scan) |

> **Observation 2.2.** The convolution view exists *only* for time-invariant SSMs. The moment $\bar{A}$, $\bar{B}$ depend on the input (selectivity), the unrolled map $u \mapsto y$ is no longer a convolution, and the parallel-scan view must take over.

### 2.3 Discretization Choices

Three discretization rules appear in the literature:

1. **Zero-order hold (ZOH):** $\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)$, $\bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}(e^{\Delta A} - I)\Delta B$. Exact for piecewise-constant inputs; used by DSS and S5.
2. **Bilinear (Tustin) transform:** $\bar{A} = (I - \tfrac{\Delta}{2}A)^{-1}(I + \tfrac{\Delta}{2}A)$. Maps the left half-plane exactly onto the unit disk, preserving stability; used by the original S4 [1].
3. **Simplified Euler:** $\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)$, $\bar{B} = \Delta B$. Mamba's cheap first-order form [3].

The step size $\Delta$ is itself a *timescale* parameter: discretization breaks HiPPO-LegS's timescale invariance, so $\Delta$ becomes learnable (or log-uniformly initialized) per state dimension — a "timescale bank" letting dimensions specialize to different temporal resolutions.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The DPLR Parameterization

The computational bottleneck of the convolution view is the kernel $\bar{K}_j = \bar{C}\,\bar{A}^{\,j}\,\bar{B}$. S4's breakthrough is a parameterization of $A$ that makes this kernel computable without ever forming dense matrix powers. The HiPPO-LegS matrix is not diagonalizable in a numerically stable way, but it admits a **normal-plus-low-rank (NPLR)** decomposition [1]:

$$A = V \Lambda V^* - P Q^*,$$

where $\Lambda$ is diagonal (the *HiPPO normal* part) and $P, Q$ are rank-$r$ ($r \le 2$) matrices. Conjugating by $V$ yields the **DPLR (diagonal-plus-low-rank)** form $\tilde{A} = \Lambda - \tilde{P}\tilde{Q}^*$. The crucial device is the generating function of the kernel:

$$\hat{K}(z) = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \bar{K}_j z^j = \tilde{C}(I - z\tilde{\bar{A}})^{-1}\tilde{\bar{B}}.$$

Because $\tilde{\bar{A}} = \Lambda - \tilde{P}\tilde{Q}^*$ is diagonal-plus-low-rank, the Woodbury matrix identity gives:

$$(I - z(\Lambda - \tilde{P}\tilde{Q}^*))^{-1} = D(z)^{-1} + D(z)^{-1}\tilde{P}\,\big(I - \tilde{Q}^* D(z)^{-1}\tilde{P}\big)^{-1}\tilde{Q}^*\, D(z)^{-1},$$

where $D(z) = I - z\Lambda$ is diagonal and trivially invertible. Evaluating $\hat{K}(z)$ at the $L$ roots of unity reduces the whole computation to *Cauchy kernel* evaluations of the form $\sum_n \frac{c_n}{z_j - \lambda_n}$, which specialized $O((N+L)\log^2(N+L))$ algorithms handle; an inverse FFT then yields $\bar{K}$ [1]. In practice:

> **Theorem 3.1 (S4 kernel complexity, informal).** For state size $N$ and sequence length $L$, the DPLR-parameterized SSM kernel $\bar{K}$ can be computed in $\tilde{O}(N + L)$ time and $O(N + L)$ memory.

The S4 *layer* then applies $H$ independent SISO SSMs (one per feature channel) in parallel, followed by a nonlinearity and a position-wise mixing projection — the SSM analogue of multi-head attention followed by a feed-forward block [1].

### 3.2 From Time-Invariance to Selectivity

The LTI restriction is also the model's expressive ceiling: the kernel $\bar{K}$ is identical for every input sequence, so the SSM cannot *choose* what to remember based on content — the function of gating in LSTMs or attention in Transformers. Mamba breaks LTI by parameterizing the SSM with the input [3]:

$$\Delta_t = s_\Delta(u_t), \qquad B_t = s_B(u_t), \qquad C_t = s_C(u_t),$$

where $s_\Delta, s_B, s_C$ are simple linear projections (with $s_\Delta$ passed through softplus). The state matrix $A$ remains input-independent and diagonal (initialized as $A_n = -(n+1)$, the S4D-Real spectrum). Discretizing per-timestep,

$$\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A), \qquad \bar{B}_t = \Delta_t B_t, \qquad x_t = \bar{A}_t x_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t u_t, \qquad y_t = C_t x_t.$$

The convolution view is destroyed — but the recurrence is still a *linear* first-order recurrence with an associative binary operator. Defining $e_t = (\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t u_t)$ and the operator $e_i \circ e_j = (\bar{A}_j \bar{A}_i,\; \bar{A}_j \bar{B}_i u_i + \bar{B}_j u_j)$, the recurrence becomes a **parallel prefix scan** (Blelloch scan) computable in $O(\log L)$ parallel steps with $O(L)$ total work [3] — full training parallelization without attention.

### 3.3 Hardware-Aware Implementation

Asymptotics are insufficient: a naive selective scan materializes the $L \times N$ expanded state in GPU HBM, making it memory-bound. Mamba's implementation [3] borrows three FlashAttention-style techniques — **kernel fusion** (discretization, scan, and gating fused into one CUDA kernel so the state lives in fast SRAM, never written back to HBM), **parallel scan in SRAM**, and **recomputation** (intermediate states discarded and recomputed in the backward pass, trading FLOPs for memory). The result: $5\times$ higher inference throughput than Transformers, with the state expansion factor (typically $N=16$ per channel) kept small enough that the scan stays compute-efficient.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 HiPPO-LegS: Optimal Polynomial Memory

HiPPO-LegS deserves a closer look because every subsequent construction inherits its design decisions. The matrix $A$ is lower-triangular: coefficient $x_n(t)$ is driven by all coefficients of degree $\le n$, with diagonal entries $-(n+1)$ setting each polynomial degree's decay rate. The $1/t$ prefactor implements *dilation*: as time advances, the polynomial basis stretches to cover $[0,t]$, so the memory never saturates — unlike LegT (translated Legendre), which keeps only a fixed sliding window and forgets everything older than $\theta$.

```python
import numpy as np

def hippo_legs_matrix(n: int):
    """Construct the HiPPO-LegS (A, B) pair, Theorem 2.1."""
    A = np.zeros((n, n))
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            if i > j:
                A[i, j] = -np.sqrt(2*i + 1) * np.sqrt(2*j + 1)
            elif i == j:
                A[i, j] = -(i + 1)
    B = np.sqrt(2*np.arange(n) + 1)
    return A, B

def legs_ode_step(x, u, t, dt, A, B):
    """One Euler step of the LegS ODE: dx/dt = (Ax + Bu)/t."""
    return x + dt * (A @ x + B * u) / t
```

The deeper significance is measure-theoretic. HiPPO is not *one* memory but a *family* indexed by measures: LegT (sliding window), LegS (uniform over history, timescale-invariant), LagT (exponential decay, fixed timescale). The choice of measure is the choice of inductive bias over *which past matters* — and this measure trilemma (timescale invariance vs. recency vs. local resolution) remains the least-explored design axis of SSMs; future architectures may learn the measure itself.

> **Theorem 4.1 (HiPPO optimality).** For any fixed measure $\omega^{(t)}$, the HiPPO update is the *unique* online linear dynamical system whose state equals the $L^2(\omega^{(t)})$-optimal polynomial projection of the history. Any other linear memory of the same order has weakly larger reconstruction error [2].

### 4.2 S4: Making the Kernel Computable

The original S4 paper [1] reports three properties that made the architecture a watershed:

- **Long Range Arena:** state-of-the-art on all six LRA tasks (sequences of length 1k–16k), including Path-X (length 16,384), which every prior model failed.
- **Sequential CIFAR-10:** 91% accuracy with no data augmentation — on par with a larger 2-D ResNet — treating the image as a 1,024-step pixel sequence.
- **Generation:** $60\times$ faster autoregressive generation than Transformers, since inference uses the $O(N)$ recurrent view rather than a KV cache growing with $L$.

Mathematically, the DPLR construction is the heart of the paper. The HiPPO-LegS matrix $A$ is first decomposed as $A = A^{(\text{normal})} - PP^*$, where $A^{(\text{normal})}$ is the *HiPPO normal* matrix — a skew-symmetric-plus-diagonal matrix that is stably diagonalizable by a unitary $V$. Conjugating, $\tilde{A} = \Lambda - \tilde{P}\tilde{P}^*$ with $\Lambda = V^* A^{(\text{normal})} V$ diagonal. The rank of the correction is at most 2. S4 then *learns* in this conjugated space: $\Lambda$ (diagonal, $N$ complex parameters), $\tilde{P}$ ($2N$ parameters), plus $B, C, \Delta$. The Cauchy-kernel trick (Section 3.1) computes $\bar{K}$ in $\tilde{O}(N+L)$.

Two subtleties deserve emphasis. First, the bilinear (Tustin) discretization maps the continuous-time stability region exactly onto the discrete-time unit disk, so the learned $\Lambda$ cannot produce exploding dynamics — a guarantee ZOH and Euler lack in principle (though Mamba's $\exp(\Delta A)$ with $A < 0$ is stable in practice). Second, S4's kernel computation is numerically delicate: Cauchy matrices are ill-conditioned, and the algorithm relies on low-rank structure to avoid explicit formation. The diagonal simplifications below were motivated as much by *implementation sanity* as by performance.

```haskell
-- The SSM kernel as a pure function: K_j = C A^j B
ssmKernel :: Int -> [Double] -> [[Double]] -> [Double] -> [Double] -> [Double]
ssmKernel len c a b =
  [ dot c (matVecPow a b j) | j <- [0..len-1] ]
  where
    dot u v        = sum (zipWith (*) u v)
    matVecPow _ v 0 = v
    matVecPow m v j = matVecPow m (matVec m v) (j-1)
    matVec m v     = [ dot row v | row <- m ]
-- S4's contribution: evaluating this in O~(N+L), not O(N^2 L),
-- via the DPLR form and Cauchy-kernel evaluation at roots of unity.
```

### 4.3 Diagonal Simplifications: DSS, S4D, and S5

The DPLR machinery is powerful but baroque. Two 2022 papers asked whether it is necessary:

- **DSS (Diagonal State Spaces)** [4] observed that restricting $A$ to be *diagonal* — $\lambda_n = -\exp(\Lambda_{\text{re},n}) + i\,\Lambda_{\text{im},n}$, $\Delta = \exp(\Delta_{\log})$ — matches S4 on LRA and Speech Commands while reducing the kernel to a closed-form expression needing no Cauchy machinery: conceptually simpler, straightforward to implement.
- **S4D** [5] supplied the theory: *almost every* SSM has an equivalent diagonal form over $\mathbb{C}$, so the restriction costs nothing in expressivity — but the *initialization* is critical. Its kernel needs "just 2 lines of code," performs comparably to S4, and averages 85% on LRA, with S4D-Inv (inverse-law spectrum) and S4D-Lin (linearly spaced) initializations approximating the HiPPO spectrum.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Gu et al., 2022, informal).** The diagonal restriction of S4's DPLR matrix recovers the same convolution kernel in the limit of infinite state dimension $N \to \infty$; at finite $N$, the initialization scheme determines how closely the diagonal model approximates the HiPPO dynamics [5].

- **S5** [6] took a different axis of simplification: replacing S4's bank of $H$ SISO SSMs with a *single* multi-input multi-output (MIMO) SSM and replacing the convolutional FFT approach with a **parallel scan** in the time domain. S5 inherits HiPPO initializations through its mathematical connection to S4, uses the diagonal approximation justified by [5], and achieves the same complexity as S4 while operating purely recurrently — foreshadowing the scan-based training of Mamba.

The architectural comparison:

| Model | $A$ parameterization | Computation | Selectivity | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S4 | DPLR (diagonal + rank-2) | FFT convolution | None | [3] |
| DSS | Diagonal (complex) | Closed-form kernel | None | [4] |
| S4D | Diagonal (complex) | 2-line kernel | None | [5] |
| S5 | Diagonal, MIMO | Parallel scan | None | [6] |
| Mamba | Diagonal real, input-independent | Parallel associative scan | $\Delta_t, B_t, C_t$ | [3] |
| Mamba-2 | Scalar-times-identity per head | SSD (matmul-based) | $\Delta_t, B_t, C_t$ (+ head structure) | [7] |

```rust
/// Mamba-style selective scan (simplified): x_t = Abar_t * x_{t-1} + Bbar_t * u_t
/// A is diagonal and input-independent; Bbar_t, C_t, and the step Delta_t are
/// input-dependent, which is what makes the scan *selective*.
fn selective_scan(u: &[f32], a: &[f32], b: &[f32], c: &[f32], delta: &[f32], n: usize) -> Vec<f32> {
    let mut x = vec![0.0f32; n];
    let mut y = Vec::with_capacity(u.len());
    for t in 0..u.len() {
        let abar: Vec<f32> = a.iter().map(|ai| (delta[t] * ai).exp()).collect();
        for i in 0..n {
            x[i] = abar[i] * x[i] + delta[t] * b[t * n + i] * u[t];
        }
        y.push((0..n).map(|i| c[t * n + i] * x[i]).sum());
    }
    y
}
```

### 4.4 The Selective Mechanism and Hardware-Aware Scan

Selectivity is best understood as *gating the memory itself*. In an LTI SSM the decay $\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)$ is fixed: every token is forgotten at the same rate. In Mamba, $\Delta_t = \text{softplus}(\text{Linear}(u_t))$ acts as an input-dependent *gate on time* — large $\Delta_t$ resets the state, small $\Delta_t$ freezes it across irrelevant tokens — while $B_t$ controls *what enters* and $C_t$ *what is read out*, implementing content-based addressing without attention.

Formally, the selective SSM computes a linear recurrence whose transition varies per timestep. The parallelization rests on associativity: define for each $t$ the pair $e_t = (\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t u_t)$; the binary operator

$$e_i \otimes e_j = (\bar{A}_j \bar{A}_i,\; \bar{A}_j (\bar{B}_i u_i) + \bar{B}_j u_j)$$

is associative, so a Blelloch parallel scan evaluates all prefixes in $O(\log L)$ depth. In TLA+ terms, the recurrence is a fold over an associative operator — parallelizable by construction:

```tla+
---- MODULE SelectiveScan ----
EXTENDS Integers, Sequences
CONSTANTS L, N
VARIABLES x, t
(* e_t = (Abar_t, Bbar_t * u_t); operator (o) is associative *)
Abar == [i \in 1..L |-> Exp(Delta[i] * A)]
Step == [t' = t + 1,
         x' = [n \in 1..N |-> Abar[t][n] * x[n] + Bbar[t][n] * u[t]]]
(* Associativity of (o) ==> parallel prefix scan computes the same
   fixed point as the sequential fold, in O(log L) depth. *)
====
```

The full Mamba *block* wraps the selective SSM: input projection (expansion $E=2$), causal depthwise convolution, the SSM, SiLU gating against a parallel branch, and output projection — echoing the Transformer's attention+MLP block while containing *neither* [3].

**Mamba-2** [7] then asked whether the scan itself can be accelerated. The State Space Duality (SSD) framework shows scalar-structured SSMs ($A_t = a_t I$ per head) are dual to *masked linear attention*: the map factorizes as $(L \circ (QK^\top))V$ with a 1-semiseparable mask $L$ derived from the $a_t$ scalars. This duality admits matrix-multiplication block decompositions (tensor cores) instead of the scan: $2$–$8\times$ faster than Mamba-1 with $8\times$ larger state [7]. The conceptual payoff: *Transformers and SSMs are not rival families but two algorithms for the same structured-matrix computation.*

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Benchmark Landmarks

1. **Long Range Arena (LRA).** S4 achieved state-of-the-art on all six LRA tasks at lengths 1k–16k, most notably solving **Path-X** (16,384 steps), on which all prior models — including efficient Transformers — failed [1]. DSS matched S4 [4]; S4D averaged 85% [5]. These results established that structured initialization, not attention, suffices for extreme long-range reasoning.
2. **Sequential CIFAR-10.** S4 reached **91%** accuracy treating the image as a 1,024-pixel sequence with no augmentation — matching a larger 2-D ResNet and demonstrating that SSMs learn spatial structure from pure order [1].
3. **Language modeling.** Mamba matched Transformers of the same size and outperformed Transformers of *twice* the size on perplexity and downstream tasks, while generating **$5\times$ faster** at inference [3]. Mamba-2 retained this parity at $2$–$8\times$ Mamba-1's training speed [7].
4. **Audio and genomics.** S4 variants achieved strong results on raw audio generation (SC09) and long movie-clip classification; Mamba showed strong performance on speech and genomics, where information-dense continuous signals suit SSM inductive biases [3, 4].

### 5.2 Theoretical Guarantees

- **HiPPO optimality (Theorem 4.1):** the memory state is the $L^2$-optimal polynomial projection of history — a memory mechanism *derived*, not invented [2].
- **Timescale robustness:** HiPPO RNNs beat LSTM/ODE baselines by 25–40% on time-warped trajectories [2].
- **Diagonal sufficiency (Theorem 4.2):** S4's DPLR kernel is recovered by a diagonal matrix as $N \to \infty$, justifying DSS/S4D/S5 [5].
- **SSD duality:** structured SSMs and linear attention are related through semiseparable decompositions — a *theorem*, not an analogy [7].
- **Signature expressivity:** rough-path analysis [8] proves selective SSM hidden states are low-dimensional projections of the input's *path signature*.

> **Theorem 5.1 (SSD, Dao & Gu 2024, informal).** Every scalar structured SSM with per-timestep scalar $a_t$ computes the same map as masked linear attention with a 1-semiseparable mask $L_{ij} = \prod_{k=j+1}^{i} a_k$; conversely, causal linear attention is a structured SSM. The two are dual views of one computation [7].

## 6. Limitations

1. **The compression bottleneck.** A selective SSM still compresses $L$ tokens into an $N$-dimensional state ($N \approx 16$–$128$ per channel in practice). For *recall-intensive* tasks — copying arbitrary facts from long contexts, associative retrieval — this fixed capacity is a hard ceiling that attention's $O(L)$ KV cache does not share. Hybrid architectures (e.g., Jamba's interleaved attention+Mamba) exist precisely because pure SSMs underperform on such tasks.
2. **Selectivity is coarse.** Mamba gates per-timestep via scalars $\Delta_t$ and vectors $B_t, C_t$; it cannot express the *pairwise* token interactions of softmax attention. The SSD duality [7] shows a spectrum — but Mamba-1 sits at the SSM end, and fine-grained relational reasoning still favors attention.
3. **Theoretical gaps.** *Why* HiPPO initializations train well in deep stacks — the interaction of timescale banks, discretization, and optimization — remains largely empirical. The measure trilemma (Section 4.1) is unexplored: all major models inherit LegS by default, not by search.
4. **Discrete data.** SSMs were born for continuous signals; on language they needed selectivity to compete, and the strongest systems remain hybrids. S4 "substantially closed the gap" to Transformers but did not close it [1].
5. **Hardware coupling.** Mamba's efficiency depends on fused CUDA kernels (selective scan in SRAM); without them the algorithm is memory-bound and unremarkable — a fragility attention, with decades of optimized kernels, does not share.
6. **Interpretability.** HiPPO states are interpretable *by construction* (polynomial coefficients), but selectivity, gating, and deep stacking render the state as opaque as any RNN hidden vector.

## 7. Conclusion

The arc from HiPPO to Mamba-2 is one of the cleanest intellectual trajectories in modern deep learning: an *optimal* online memory derived from approximation theory [2]; a structured parameterization making it computable at scale [1]; diagonal simplifications revealing the essential degrees of freedom [4, 5]; a parallel-scan reformulation [6]; a selective mechanism restoring content-awareness [3]; and finally a duality theorem unifying the whole family with attention itself [7]. Each step removed an apparent necessity — the dense matrix, the convolution, time-invariance, even the scan — while preserving the core invariant: *a fixed-size state updated by a structured linear recurrence*.

Three research directions stand out. **First**, the measure problem: learning or selecting the HiPPO measure per task, rather than inheriting LegS by default, may unlock memory profiles matched to data modalities. **Second**, the expressivity frontier: characterizing exactly which computations selective SSMs can and cannot simulate — the signature-theoretic view [8] is a start, but a complexity-theoretic account (e.g., in terms of circuit classes) is missing. **Third**, the systems frontier: as context lengths grow into the millions, the $O(1)$-per-step inference of SSMs becomes not merely convenient but necessary, and the SSD duality suggests future architectures will *mix* attention-like and SSM-like computation at the level of structured matrix primitives rather than choosing between them.

The lesson of state-space sequence models is ultimately methodological: the most durable architectures are those derived from a mathematical principle — optimal polynomial projection, structured matrix algebra — rather than assembled from heuristics. Attention won the 2020s on engineering; the 2030s may belong to whatever mathematics replaces it.
