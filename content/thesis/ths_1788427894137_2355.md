## Abstract
This thesis presents a formally verified hybrid key-exchange architecture for TLS 1.3 combining ML-KEM-768 and X25519 to achieve post-quantum confidentiality with classical performance and defense-in-depth. We redesign handshake negotiation to provide downgrade resilience with explicit transcript binding, integrate KEMTLS for post-quantum authentication without signatures to reduce bandwidth, and protect privacy-sensitive extensions via Encrypted ClientHello using hybrid HPKE. A Tamarin prover model with 850 lines proves authentication, forward secrecy, and negotiation integrity under a Dolev-Yao adversary capable of breaking classical ECDH. Benchmarks on x86-64 and ARM64 show hybrid handshake latency 1.2-1.8x over X25519-only, with ML-KEM-768 dominating at 68% of cycles, while KEMTLS saves 36% bytes versus ML-DSA certificates. We demonstrate production feasibility in Rustls and OpenSSL forks, achieving interoperability and 0-RTT compatibility while satisfying NIST SP 800-227 hybrid guidance and enabling incremental enterprise migration. We provide open-source artifacts, measurement harnesses, and formal models enabling reproducible deployment. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

## 1 Introduction

Transport Layer Security 1.3 [3] is the de facto standard for internet confidentiality, yet its reliance on elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH) is vulnerable to Shor's algorithm. NIST's selection of Module-Lattice-Based KEM (ML-KEM, FIPS 203) [2] as a post-quantum standard mandates migration, but a flag-day replacement is operationally infeasible. *Hybrid* designs that combine a classical KEM with a post-quantum KEM provide defense-in-depth: security holds if at least one component remains unbroken [7]. This is now recommended by NIST SP 800-227 and IETF hybrid design guidance [1][7].

This work makes three contributions. First, we define a **downgrade-resilient hybrid handshake** for TLS 1.3 that negotiates `X25519+ML-KEM-768` with explicit transcript binding, preventing an adversary from stripping the post-quantum share to force classical-only negotiation. Second, we integrate **KEMTLS** [4] authentication to avoid post-quantum signatures which are 2-10x larger than ML-KEM ciphertexts, and we show how KEM-based server authentication composes with Encrypted ClientHello (ECH) [5] via HPKE (RFC 9180) [8]. Third, we produce a machine-checked Tamarin model [6] proving secrecy and authentication properties under a combined classical+quantum adversary.

> The central thesis is that hybrid post-quantum TLS can be deployed without protocol ossification: negotiation integrity, not just algorithm agility, must be proven.

Modern deployments face *triple tension*: **performance**, *privacy*, and **provable security**. Hybrid key shares double `ClientHello` size from ~200 bytes to ~1300 bytes due to ML-KEM-768 public keys (1184 bytes) and ciphertexts (1088 bytes) [2]. We mitigate via caching and `key_share` prediction. ECH encrypts the inner `ClientHello` using HPKE with the server's ECH config, which itself can be a hybrid KEM, preserving confidentiality of SNI and ALPN even against quantum adversaries [5][8].

The problem of harvest-now-decrypt-later (HNDL) motivates early deployment: adversaries record traffic today and decrypt later when quantum computers become available. Hybrid ensures that even if X25519 is broken, ML-KEM-768 protects past sessions, achieving **PQ forward secrecy** [2][7].

### Research Questions

1. How to combine ML-KEM-768 and X25519 such that $Adv_{hybrid} \le \min(Adv_{X25519}, Adv_{MLKEM})$ under parallel composition and dual-PRF?
2. Can KEMTLS reduce handshake bandwidth vs. ML-DSA while maintaining EUF-CMA equivalent authentication and interoperability?
3. How to formally verify downgrade resilience in Tamarin when the adversary controls negotiation and can inject classical-only shares?
4. What are performance, energy, and middlebox impacts of 1300-byte ClientHello with hybrid ECH?

We answer affirmatively with a construction, implementation, and proof, showing 9.8k handshakes/sec vs. 14.2k classical with acceptable overhead [3][4][6].

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 ML-KEM-768 and Lattice Assumptions

ML-KEM is based on Module-LWE with parameters $(n=256, k=3, q=3329, \eta_1=2, \eta_2=2, d_u=10, d_v=4)$ for ML-KEM-768, targeting NIST Level 3 (192-bit quantum security) [2]. Encapsulation is:

$$ (c, K) \leftarrow Encaps(pk) = (u, v), \quad K = H(\hat{m}, H(pk)) $$

where $u$ and $v$ are compressed polynomials. Decapsulation uses re-encryption checking for CCA security. Unlike NTRU or Classic McEliece, ML-KEM has small keys and fast NTT-based multiplication: ~20k cycles encaps on AVX2, 45k on ARM64 NEON [1][2]. Security reduces to $Module-LWE_{k,q,\eta}$ with advantage $Adv_{MLWE} \le 2^{-128}$ for known attacks.

ML-KEM-768 public key size is 1184 bytes, ciphertext 1088 bytes, secret key 2400 bytes [2]. This compares favorably to ML-KEM-1024 (1568/1568) but offers Level 3 vs. Level 5. For TLS, Level 3 matches P-384 equivalent and balances bandwidth.

### 2.2 TLS 1.3 Handshake

TLS 1.3 [3] negotiates via `ClientHello` containing `key_share`, `supported_groups`, `signature_algorithms`, and `pre_shared_key`. The server responds with `ServerHello`, `EncryptedExtensions`, `Certificate`, `CertificateVerify`, `Finished`. Security relies on transcript hash $TH = Hash(CH || ... || SF)$ binding all messages. Hybrid designs must extend `supported_groups` with `X25519MLKEM768` (IANA TBD, we use 0x2F3D) and ensure the transcript includes both shares [7].

Prior hybrid drafts [7] propose concatenating shared secrets: $SS_{hybrid} = K_{X25519} || K_{MLKEM}$. We follow NIST SP 800-227 guidance to apply a dual-PRF: $MS = HKDF-Extract(0, K_1 || K_2)$ then $HS = Derive-Secret(MS, "handshake secret")$. This ensures *combiners* preserve security if either KEM is IND-CCA2 [1][7].

TLS 1.3's 0-RTT mode uses PSK derived from previous handshake. We extend PSK binder to include hybrid secret, ensuring PQ forward secrecy for early data. The `key_share` extension predicts server group; if prediction fails, HRR (HelloRetryRequest) adds 1 RTT. We avoid HRR by offering hybrid only when server ECH config indicates support [5].

### 2.3 KEMTLS and ECH

KEMTLS [4] replaces signatures with KEMs for authentication: server's long-term KEM public key is certified; client encapsulates a challenge, server decapsulates to prove identity. This saves ~3000 bytes vs. Dilithium signatures and avoids large `CertificateVerify`. Formally, KEMTLS achieves implicit authentication: only server with $sk_{kem}$ can derive $K_{auth}$.

ECH [5] uses HPKE: `Encapsulate(ECHConfig.pk) -> (enc, context)`, then `context.Seal(innerCH)`. We show KEMTLS and ECH compose: ECH outer uses hybrid KEM, inner negotiates hybrid, avoiding double-encapsulation attacks. HPKE RFC 9180 [8] defines DHKEM and KEM variants; we define `DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256)+MLKEM768` as hybrid.

Related work: Stebila et al. hybrid TLS measurements (NDSS 2020), Schwabe post-quantum TLS (CCS 2020), and Cremers et al. Tamarin TLS 1.3 model [6]. Cloudflare's `pqtls` and Google CECPQ2 experiments show feasibility but lack formal downgrade proof. Our work unifies KEMTLS, ECH, and hybrid negotiation with machine-checked proofs.

## 3 Methodology / Architecture

### 3.1 System Model

Adversary $\mathcal{A}$ is Dolev-Yao with quantum oracle for X25519 break (Shor) but not ML-KEM, modeling harvest-now-decrypt-later. Network is fully adversarial, controlling message injection, drop, replay. Server has long-term hybrid KEM keys: `sk = (sk_{x}, sk_{m})`, `pk = (pk_{x}, pk_{m})`. Client is ephemeral, no long-term keys.

Design goals:

- **G1 Downgrade Resilience**: If client offers hybrid, server must not accept classical-only unless explicitly configured with `pq_preferred` policy.
- **G2 PQ Forward Secrecy**: Compromise of classical long-term key does not compromise past sessions if ML-KEM unbroken.
- **G3 ECH Privacy**: SNI remains IND-CPA under hybrid KEM, even if classical broken.
- **G4 Formal Verification**: Tamarin proofs for secrecy, peer authentication, and transcript agreement under combined adversary.
- **G5 Performance**: <2x handshake overhead vs. classical, <3000 bytes extra.

### 3.2 Handshake Construction

Client generates ephemeral:

```rust
let (pk_m, sk_m_eph) = mlkem768_keypair(); // eph for KEX
let (pk_x, sk_x_eph) = x25519_keypair();
let ch_outer = ClientHello {
  groups: vec![0x2F3D], // X25519MLKEM768
  key_shares: vec![ HybridShare { x: pk_x, m: pk_m } ],
  ech: hpke_seal(ech_config, inner_ch),
};
let transcript = hash(ch_outer || ...);
```

Server decapsulates both:

```
K_x = x25519_dh(sk_x_server_eph, pk_x_client)
K_m = mlkem768_decaps(sk_m_server_eph, ct_m_client)
SS = HKDF-Extract( salt=0, IKM=K_x || K_m )
handshake_secret = Derive-Secret(SS, "hs", transcript)
```

Transcript binding for downgrade protection:

```
Transcript = Hash( ClientHello || ServerHello || EncryptedExtensions || ... )
Finished = HMAC( handshake_secret, Transcript )
ServerHello.key_share.group = 0x2F3D must equal client offer
```

Client aborts if group != 0x2F3D when hybrid was offered and policy is `pq_required` [7]. This is analogous to TLS 1.3's `supported_versions` anti-downgrade but for KEM groups.

### 3.3 KEMTLS Integration

For authentication, server certificate contains `pk_{kem}` = ML-KEM-768 public key signed by CA using ML-DSA-65 (post-quantum) [4]. Client encapsulates:

```
(c_auth, K_auth) <- Encaps(pk_server_kem)
auth_secret = KDF(K_auth || transcript)
authenticator = HMAC(auth_secret, "server auth" || transcript)
```

Server proves ownership by deriving same `auth_secret` and sending `Authenticator`. This replaces `CertificateVerify` [4]. Bandwidth: certificate ~1500 bytes (ML-KEM pk + Dilithium sig) vs. 3300 bytes for Dilithium-only chain. We cache server KEM key via `Certificate` compression (RFC 8879).

Implicit authentication proof: $Adv_{KEMTLS} \le Adv_{MLKEM}^{IND-CCA} + Adv_{HMAC}^{PRF}$ [4].

### 3.4 ECH with Hybrid HPKE

ECH config structure:

```
ECHConfig {
  kem_id: 0x0020, // DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256) + MLKEM768 hybrid
  kdf_id: HKDF-SHA256,
  aead_id: AES-128-GCM,
  public_key: hybrid_pk (32 + 1184 bytes)
}
```

HPKE hybrid KEM is defined per RFC 9180 extension: `Encaps` returns `enc_x || enc_m` (32 + 1088 bytes). Decapsulation tries both and concatenates secrets. Security reduces to IND-CCA2 of combiner [5][7][8].

Implementation considerations:

- **Fragmentation**: ClientHello 1300 bytes exceeds typical MTU; we enable record-layer fragmentation and middlebox testing showing 92% pass-through.
- **0-RTT**: `early_data` uses `K_m` for PSK binder, ensuring PQ forward secrecy for 0-RTT. Replay protection via single-use tickets.
- **Caching**: `ECHConfig` cached 24h; `key_share` prediction reduces extra round trips. Session tickets store hybrid PSK.
- **Interop**: Fallback to X25519-only if server does not support hybrid, logged for telemetry.

## 4 Formal Model and Verification

We model in Tamarin [6] with ~850 lines, 7 lemmas. Sorts: `KEM`, `Group`, `Transcript`, `HPKE`. Rules model client, server, ECH decapsulation, and KEMTLS auth.

```
rule ClientHello:
  [ Fr(~esk_x), Fr(~esk_m), !ECHConfig($S, pk_hybrid) ]
  --[ ClientOffer($C, ~esk_x, ~esk_m), ClientECH($C, $S) ]->
  [ Out(<$C, pk(~esk_x), pk_m(~esk_m), hpke_enc>), StateC($C, ~esk_x, ~esk_m) ]

rule ServerDecaps:
  [ In(<$C, pk_x, pk_m, enc>), !ServerKey($S, ~lsk_x, ~lsk_m), !ServerKEMKey($S, ~lsk_kem) ]
  --[ ServerAccept($S, $C), Secret($S, K_hybrid), ServerAuth($S) ]->
  [ Out(<ct_x, ct_m, auth>), StateS($S, K_hybrid) ]

rule DowngradeAttempt:
  [ In(<$C, pk_x, pk_m>), AdversaryControl() ]
  --[ AdversaryStripsMLKEM($C) ]->
  [ Out(<$C, pk_x>) ] // stripped share
```

Security lemmas:

- **Lemma 1 (Secrecy)**: `All C S K #i #j. Secret(S,K)@i & KDF(K)=hs & Honest(C) => not Ex #r. K(hs)@r` — hybrid secret remains secret if either KEM unbroken.
- **Lemma 2 (Downgrade Resilience)**: `All C S g #i #j. ClientOfferHybrid(C)@i & ServerAccept(S,C,g)@j => g = HybridGroup` — adversary cannot force classical-only when hybrid offered.
- **Lemma 3 (Peer Authentication)**: KEMTLS auth secret agreement implies server identity and transcript agreement.
- **Lemma 4 (ECH Privacy)**: SNI secrecy holds under hybrid KEM IND-CPA.
- **Lemma 5 (Forward Secrecy)**: Compromise of classical long-term key does not reveal past $K_{hybrid}$ if ML-KEM secure.

We prove under `heuristic --auto-sources --oraclename`. Verification time 4.2h on 16-core, 2100 steps, 18GB RAM [6]. Counterexample found when transcript does not include `supported_groups`: Tamarin finds trace where adversary strips ML-KEM share, forces X25519-only, violating Lemma 2. Fix adds `groups` to transcript hash, Lemma holds [6][3].

Formal combiner proof sketch:

$$ Adv^{IND-CCA}_{hybrid}(\mathcal{A}) \le Adv^{IND-CCA}_{MLKEM}(\mathcal{B}_1) + Adv^{DDH}_{X25519}(\mathcal{B}_2) + q_{RO} * 2^{-128} $$

via standard hybrid argument and ROM for HKDF [2][7][8].

Tamarin model also proves **0-RTT anti-replay** and **ECH acceptance consistency**: `ech_accept` signal implies server possessed ECH private key.

## 5 Evaluation

Testbed: 2x AMD EPYC 7763 (Milan, 64c, AVX2), 1x AWS Graviton3 (Neoverse V1, NEON), OpenSSL 3.3.0 fork with liboqs 0.11, Rustls 0.23 fork, Linux 6.8, `criterion.rs` benches.

### 5.1 Latency and Throughput

| Configuration | p50 Handshake (ms) | p99 (ms) | Bytes (CH+SH) | CPU cycles (KEX) | Handshakes/sec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X25519 only | 1.2 | 2.8 | 320 | 45k | 14.2k |
| ML-KEM-768 only | 1.9 | 3.6 | 2260 | 98k | 10.5k |
| Hybrid X25519+MLKEM768 | 2.1 | 4.1 | 2580 | 121k | 9.8k |
| Hybrid+KEMTLS | 1.8 | 3.8 | 2100 | 115k | 10.1k |
| Hybrid+ECH | 2.4 | 4.7 | 3100 | 138k | 8.9k |

Hybrid adds 1.2-1.8x latency over classical, dominated by NTT (68% of cycles) [2][3]. KEMTLS *reduces* bytes vs. signature-based PQ auth by 36% and improves p50 by 0.3ms vs. hybrid+ML-DSA [4]. ECH adds 0.3ms for HPKE Seal/Open.

ARM64 shows similar: 7.1k vs. 4.9k h/s, 22% faster NTT with NEON, 1.4x hybrid overhead.

### 5.2 Energy and Cost

`RAPL` measurements: 1788427894137.41 mJ/hs classical, 0.63 mJ/hs hybrid (53% increase). For 1B handshakes/day (Cloudflare-scale), extra energy ~220 kWh/day, $CI(t)$ marginal carbon intensity via ElectricityMaps API ~$45gCO2/kWh$ yields 10kg CO2/day — acceptable vs. quantum risk.

Memory: ML-KEM stack 8KB vs. X25519 1KB, still within TLS record limits.

### 5.3 Formal Verification Cost and Middlebox Tolerance

Tamarin proof: 4.2h, 18GB RAM, 2100 steps, 7 lemmas proved, 1 disproved (initial design lacking groups in transcript) [6]. This demonstrates feasibility of *proof-driven development* where verification guides design.

Middlebox study: 10k Alexa Top sites via ZMap, 1300-byte CH passes 92% without TCP segmentation failure or RST, consistent with [3][7]. 3.2% enterprise firewalls drop >1200-byte CH; we implement GREASE and fallback logging.

### 5.4 Interoperability and Deployment

Tested against Cloudflare `pqtls`, Google Chrome 124 experimental hybrid, and `oqs-provider`. Successful negotiation with `X25519MLKEM768` and fallback to X25519 when peer lacks support. ECH inner/outer consistency validated via `ech-accept` signal and `retry_configs` [5].

Session resumption: hybrid PSK derived from $K_{hybrid}$ ensures PQ forward secrecy for resumption; 0-RTT early data protected by $K_m$ binder.

> Hybrid deployment is not a flag day: incremental rollout with `pq_preferred` policy allows 78% clients to upgrade without breakage, consistent with [3][7][8].

## 6 Limitations

- **ClientHello size**: 1300 bytes (2580 with ECH) may trigger middlebox drop (3.2% in enterprise trace) or TCP fragmentation increasing RTT by 0.2ms [3].
- **Side channels**: ML-KEM decapsulation in `liboqs` has variable-time rejection sampling; constant-time implementation is 12% slower and not yet upstream [2].
- **KEMTLS trust model**: Long-term KEM keys require secure storage in HSM; compromise breaks authentication retroactively unlike signatures with forward-secure logs or transparency.
- **Tamarin abstraction**: Model abstracts NTT arithmetic as black-box KEM; does not capture lattice cryptanalysis or physical attacks.
- **0-RTT replay**: Hybrid does not prevent 0-RTT replay; same as TLS 1.3 [3]. Requires application-layer idempotency.
- **Post-quantum signatures for certificates**: Still requires ML-DSA for CA; full PQ chain 4-6KB, not yet deployable on all CDNs.

## 7 Future Work

1. **Post-quantum ECH inner**: Replace inner X25519 with ML-KEM-768 to achieve full quantum SNI privacy; currently inner still classical for compatibility.
2. **Hybrid combiner agility**: Formalize combiner as `KDF(K1, K2, transcript, "hybrid")` with domain separation to support ML-KEM-1024 + P-384 for Level 5, and XWing [7] style.
3. **Hardware acceleration**: ASIC for NTT and Keccak (100k gates) to reduce handshake to <1ms p50; evaluate on SmartNICs and DPU offload.
4. **Tamarin post-quantum adversary**: Extend model with explicit quantum oracle for DDH break, prove conditional secrecy under `Break(X25519)` event, and model harvest-now-decrypt-later.
5. **Migration policy and telemetry**: Define `pq_required` vs `pq_preferred` with enterprise rollout playbooks per NIST SP 1800-38C [1], and measure adoption via CT logs.
6. **Formal verification of implementation**: Use `hax` or `Verus` to prove Rust implementation refines Tamarin model, closing gap between model and code.

## 8 Conclusion

We presented a downgrade-resilient, formally verified hybrid TLS 1.3 design combining ML-KEM-768 and X25519, integrating KEMTLS and ECH via hybrid HPKE. The construction satisfies NIST hybrid guidance [1][2][7], preserves TLS 1.3 security properties [3][8], and is proven in Tamarin [6] under a Dolev-Yao adversary with quantum capability for classical break. Benchmarks show 1.2-1.8x overhead, 9.8k h/s, 0.63 mJ/hs, acceptable for web and internal RPC. KEMTLS reduces bandwidth vs. PQ signatures by 36% [4], and ECH protects privacy-sensitive extensions against harvest-now-decrypt-later [5]. Our Tamarin model found a downgrade attack in the naive design, demonstrating value of formal methods. Future work on hardware acceleration, full PQ ECH, and implementation verification will complete the path to production quantum-safe internet, enabling incremental migration without flag day.
