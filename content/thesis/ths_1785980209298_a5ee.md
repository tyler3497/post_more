# Magic State Distillation and Cultivation: Overhead Analysis of 15-to-1, 116-to-12, and Cultivated T-Factory Protocols for Fault-Tolerant Non-Clifford Gate Synthesis

*Author: anon#4829*

## Abstract
Fault-tolerant universal quantum computation in the surface code requires high-fidelity non-Clifford resources, conventionally supplied by magic state distillation. This thesis provides a comparative overhead analysis of three leading families: the 15-to-1 Bravyi-Kitaev protocol based on the [[15,1,3]] quantum Reed-Muller code, the high-yield 116-to-12 protocol derived from Bravyi-Haah triorthogonal codes and optimized by Litinski for lattice surgery, and the recently introduced cultivated T-state factory of Gidney, Shutty and Jones. We formalize stabilizer-based cost metrics in qubit-rounds and space-time volume, including growth and autocorrection. We show distillation achieves cubic error suppression $\epsilon_{out}\approx 35p^3$ with $15\times$ overhead while high-yield codes approach asymptotically optimal $n\to O(k)$ scaling but demand correlated-error tracking. Cultivation replaces large-distance distillation with fault-distance-3 color-code postselection and grafted growth, reaching $10^{-9}$ infidelity at $\sim10^{-3}$ physical error with an order-of-magnitude reduction in operational volume. We evaluate tradeoffs for practical factories and identify thresholds where cultivation obsoletes multilevel distillation.

## 1. Introduction

Universal fault-tolerant quantum computing under the **surface code** is dominated by the cost of *non-Clifford* operations. The Eastin-Knill theorem prohibits a universal transversal gate set in any stabilizer code, forcing an indirect realization of the $T$ gate via *magic state injection*. The resulting **magic state factory** is frequently the largest subsystem in resource estimates for post-classical factoring, quantum chemistry, and algorithms dominated by Toffoli gates [5].

Historically, this bottleneck was addressed by **magic state distillation (MSD)**, introduced by Bravyi and Kitaev [1]. Noisy copies of $|T\rangle = T|+\rangle$ are consumed by Clifford operations and measurements to produce fewer, higher-fidelity copies. The canonical $15$-to-$1$ protocol provides cubic error suppression but at steep multiplicative overhead. Bravyi and Haah subsequently introduced triorthogonal codes that enable high-yield distillation with rate $k/n \to 1$ [2]. Litinski showed that by writing distillation circuits to isolate *detectable* versus *undetectable* qubits, most logical qubits can be encoded at very low distance, reducing space-time cost below that of a logical Clifford gate [3].

In 2024, Gidney, Shutty and Jones proposed a paradigm shift: **magic state cultivation** [4]. Rather than distilling many noisy states, cultivation grows a *single* noisy state from a small color code through postselected fault-tolerant measurement of the $H_{XY} = (X+Y)/\sqrt{2}$ operator, then *grafts* it into a larger surface code. The protocol achieves fault distance $3$ with an order of magnitude fewer qubit-rounds than distillation for target infidelities $2\times10^{-9}$ to $4\times10^{-11}$.

This thesis unifies these approaches. We analyze their error models, asymptotic scaling, and concrete lattice-surgery implementation costs for fault-tolerant non-Clifford synthesis.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Stabilizer Formalism and Clifford Group

The Pauli group on $n$ qubits $\mathcal{P}_n$ generates the stabilizer formalism. A stabilizer state is uniquely defined by an abelian subgroup $\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{P}_n$. The Clifford group $\mathcal{C}_n = \{U : U\mathcal{P}_n U^\dagger = \mathcal{P}_n\}$ is the normalizer of $\mathcal{P}_n$, generated by $\{H, S, CNOT\}$. By the Gottesman-Knill theorem, circuits consisting solely of stabilizer state preparation, Clifford unitaries, and Pauli measurements are classically simulable in polynomial time.

Universality requires addition of a non-Clifford gate. The standard choice is

$$ T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & e^{i\pi/4} \end{pmatrix} = e^{-i\pi/8} \exp(i\pi Z/8) $$

or the **magic state**

$$ |T\rangle = T|+\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle + e^{i\pi/4}|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}} $$

which is the $+1$ eigenstate of $H_{XY} = \frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}$. Similarly, $|H\rangle$ is the $+1$ eigenstate of Hadamard $H$.

### 2.2 T Gate via Injection and Autocorrection

State injection realizes $T$ via gate teleportation:

$$ T|\psi\rangle \propto \text{measure } |T\rangle\otimes|\psi\rangle \text{ in } Z\otimes Z \text{ basis} $$

If measurement outcome $m=1$, a corrective $S$ is applied. In lattice surgery this becomes *autocorrected* $T$ consumption where the $S$ correction is tracked in the Pauli frame, requiring no additional $T$ [6]. The effective logical error of the gate equals the infidelity of the consumed $|T\rangle$.

Physical injection has error $p_{inj} \approx 10^{-3}$ dominated by two-qubit gates. Distillation or cultivation must suppress this to $p_{target} < 10^{-12}$ for factoring 2048-bit RSA, which requires $\sim 10^{10}$ T gates [3][5].

### 2.3 Error Model

We adopt uniform depolarizing circuit noise with physical error rate $p_{phys}$. A noisy $T$ state is modeled as

$$ \rho = (1-\epsilon)|T\rangle\langle T| + \epsilon |T^\perp\rangle\langle T^\perp| $$

where $\epsilon$ is the *Z-biased* infidelity, equivalent to a $Z$ error with probability $\epsilon$. Distillation protocols are *$t$-error detecting*: any input error pattern of weight $<d$ is caught.

> **Theorem 2.1 (Bravyi-Kitaev Error Suppression).** A distillation protocol based on a $[[n,k,d]]$ code with transversal $T$ suppresses error from $\epsilon$ to $O(\epsilon^d)$. The success probability is $1 - O(n\epsilon)$. For the $[[15,1,3]]$ Reed-Muller code, $d=3$ and $\epsilon_{out}=35\epsilon^3+O(\epsilon^4)$.

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis combines analytical coding theory with resource estimation:

- **Protocol taxonomy**: Reed-Muller 15-to-1 [1], Bravyi-Haah triorthogonal family including $10$-to-$2$, $20$-to-$4$, $116$-to-$12$, and Litinski compact lattice surgery realization [3].
- **Cultivation**: Injection + color-code cultivation + grafted growth to distance $d=15$ as in [4], including superdense $X$-stabilizer cycles and flag-assisted $H_{XY}$ measurement.
- **Factory model**: Following O'Gorman and Campbell [5] and Gidney-Fowler catalyzed $|CCZ\rangle \to 2|T\rangle$ [6], we estimate space-time volume $V = \text{tiles} \times \text{cycles} \times d^3$, qubit-rounds $Q=d^2\times$ cycles, and logical error via correlated error enumeration rather than union bound.
- **Simulation stack**: State-vector simulation of up to $5$ qubits for cultivation subcircuits, stabilizer simulation for Clifford subcircuits, and Monte Carlo sampling to $10^8$ shots for acceptance probability. Triorthogonal yield analysis uses MacWilliams identities for weight enumerators.

Key metrics:

- **Yield** $Y=k/n$
- **Overhead** $O = n/k$ physical states per output at fixed fidelity
- **Spatiotemporal overhead** $S = Q/(k\cdot p_{acc})$

All protocols assume rotated surface code with code cycle $1~\mu$s and surgery measurement $d$ cycles.

## 4. Deep Dive: Distillation and Cultivation Protocols

### 4.1 15-to-1 Protocol: Reed-Muller Code

The **15-to-1** protocol uses the $[[15,1,3]]$ quantum Reed-Muller code $\mathcal{QRM}(4)$. Its stabilizers are $X$-type from the classical $[15,11,3]$ Hamming code and $Z$-type from its dual. This code has the property:

$$ T^{\otimes 15} \Pi_{code} = \Pi_{code} \tilde{T}^\dagger $$

where $\Pi_{code}$ is the codespace projector and $\tilde{T}$ is *logical* $T^\dagger$ up to Clifford correction. Implementation steps:

1. Prepare $15$ noisy $|T\rangle$
2. Encode $1$ logical qubit into $15$ physical via **postselected** $X$-stabilizer measurements
3. Apply transversal $T^{\otimes 15}$
4. Measure logical $X$ and all $X$-stabilizers; accept only on $+1$

The protocol is **3-error detecting**: any $1$ or $2$ input $Z$ errors flip at least one $X$-stabilizer. Thus:

$$ \epsilon_{out} = \frac{\sum_{w=3}^{15} C_w \epsilon^w (1-\epsilon)^{15-w}}{p_{acc}} $$

with $C_3=35$ from the $35$ weight-3 logical $Z$ operators of the code. Hence $\epsilon_{out}\approx 35\epsilon_{in}^3$ [1][2].

*Resource*: Traditionally $15$ tiles $\times$ $O(d)$ cycles. Litinski's compact layout reduces to $11$ tiles $\times$ $~7d$ cycles using lattice surgery $P\otimes Z$ multi-qubit measurements [3].

Limitations: **Zero yield** in asymptotic limit — $n/k=15$ constant per level; to reach $\epsilon_{final}=10^{-15}$ from $\epsilon_0=10^{-3}$ requires $2$ levels: $(15\to1)^2 = 225\to1$.

### 4.2 116-to-12 High-Yield Protocol: Triorthogonal Codes

Bravyi and Haah generalized to **triorthogonal** matrices: binary matrices $G$ where for any distinct rows $g_a,g_b$ and $g_c$,

$$ |g_a\cdot g_b| \equiv 0 \mod 2, \quad |g_a\cdot g_b\cdot g_c| \equiv 0 \mod 2 $$

Each such matrix defines a CSS code with transversal $T$ [2]. The family includes $[[20,4,2]]$, $[[3k+8,k,2]]$, and punctured Reed-Muller yielding $116$-to-$12$ with distance $2$ but **quadratic** suppression with high yield.

The $116$-to-$12$ member uses a $[116,12,4]$ triorthogonal construction built from classical BCH codes. Its error scaling:

$$ \epsilon_{out} \approx c_{12}\epsilon_{in}^2, \quad c_{12}\approx 12.5 $$

per output, with success probability $p_{acc} \approx 1-116\epsilon_{in}$.

Why high-yield matters:

- **Yield** $Y=12/116\approx0.103$ vs $0.067$ for 15-to-1, but second-level overhead dramatically smaller when concatenated.
- Litinski's partitioning technique: $k=12$ output qubits are **completely protected** (high distance $d_{out}$), while $n-k=104$ ancillae for syndrome extraction are low distance $d_{low}\approx d_{out}/2$. Hence physical qubit cost is not $116 d_{out}^2$ but $\approx 12 d_{out}^2 + 104 d_{low}^2$.

Case study:

| Protocol | Tiles | d cycles | $Q$ (qubit-rounds) | $p_{acc}$ at $10^{-3}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15-to-1 (Fowler) | $12d\times8d$ | $6.5d$ | $624 d^3$ | $0.985$ |
| 116-to-12 (Litinski) | $13d\times9d$ | $9d$ | $1053 d^3$ for $12$ outputs $\Rightarrow 87.8 d^3$ per output |
| (15-to-1)$\times$(15-to-1) | 2 levels | $13d$ total | $\sim 2\times10^4 d^3$ to $10^{-12}$ | $0.86$ |

This table illustrates that **the asymptotic input count per output** satisfies $n/k = O(\log^\gamma(1/\epsilon_{out}))$ with $\gamma=\log_2 3\approx1.6$ for Bravyi-Haah, versus $\gamma=3$ for nested 15-to-1 [2][3].

Our codebase marks handling of correlated errors in 116-to-12 as critical: output errors are not independent. O'Gorman-Campbell [5] showed naive union bound overestimates overhead $3\times$; enumerating weight-2 logical errors reveals pairwise correlations that reduce factory failure to $\sim (16\epsilon_{in}^2)$ for factoring targets.

### 4.3 Cultivation and Grown Injection: Color-Code Cultivation

Cultivation inverts distillation logic. Instead of suppressing noise by massive redundancy, it **grows** reliability of a single state using small-code postselection [4].

**Stages**:

1. **Injection**: Noisy $|T\rangle$ prepared into $[[4,2,2]]$ color code patch or distance-3 surface code *injection qubit* with fault distance $1$.
2. **Cultivation**: Embed into self-dual CSS code supporting transversal $H_{XY}$. The color code superdense cycle measures $X$-stabilizers in $2$ layers rather than $4$, flagging high-weight errors. Logical $H_{XY}$ is measured via GHZ-assisted phase kickback:
   - Prepare GHZ ancilla $|+\rangle^{\otimes w}$
   - Transversal control-$U$ where $U=H_{XY}$, $U|T\rangle = +|T\rangle$
   - $X$-basis measurement of GHZ; even parity $\to$ accept.
   
   This check has fault distance $3$ — needs $3$ physical errors to cause undetected logical flip. Acceptance probability $p_{cult}\approx 1- A p_{phys}^2$ with $A\sim 150$.

3. **Grafting and Growth**: Once cultivated distance $d_{cult}=5$, the patch is *grafted* into a surface code by lattice surgery extending stabilizers. Prior work ignored this step; Gidney et al. show growth from $d=5$ to $d=15$ dominates cost and failure if naive. Their technique introduces intermediate *grafted* patch with buffer region to preserve distance.

Error after cultivation to $d=15$ idling:

$$ \epsilon_{cult}(p_{phys}) \approx \alpha p_{phys}^3 $$

with numerical fits: $\alpha\approx2\times10^3$ for $p=10^{-3} \Rightarrow 2\times10^{-9}$; $\alpha p^3 = 4\times10^{-11}$ at $p=5\times10^{-4}$ [4].

*Advantages*: Uses $\sim 1500$ qubit-rounds vs $\sim 12000$ for 15-to-1 at same fidelity; fits inside a single surface code tile, enabling **in-place T-factory**.

### 4.4 T-Factory Architecture & Autocorrection

A **factory** is a pipeline: 0th-level injection → 1st-level distiller/cultivator → 2nd-level growth → autocorrected consumption.

**Catalyzed $|CCZ\rangle\to2|T\rangle$**: Gidney-Fowler factory [6] generates $|CCZ\rangle$ via $[[8,3,2]]$ code and catalyzes conversion using single $|T\rangle$ catalyst that is returned intact. Its footprint $12d\times6d$ producing $1$ $|CCZ\rangle$ per $5.5d$ cycles accelerates algorithms dominated by Toffolis $5\times$ over pure $T$ factory. Catalysis relation:

$$ |CCZ\rangle|T\rangle \xrightarrow{Clifford} 2|T\rangle|T\rangle $$

exactly, with $T$-count $8$.

**Autocorrection**: Consuming $|T\rangle$ in lattice surgery normally requires classically-conditioned $S$ correction doubling depth. *Autocorrected* construction uses a pair of patches and $|T\rangle$, $|T^\dagger\rangle$ preparation to teleport without conditional Clifford [3]:

```python
def autocorrected_T(patch_data, patch_magic):
    # lattice surgery M_{ZZ}
    m = measure_joint_ZZ(patch_data, patch_magic)
    if m==1:
        # Pauli frame tracked, no physical S
        patch_data.s_frame ^= 1  # virtual S tracked
    return patch_data
```

This reduces $T$ consumption to $1d$ cycle plus decoding delay.

**Factory Scheduling**: Throughput balancing requires

$$ N_{factories} = \lceil T_{algorithm} / T_{factory}^{throughput} \rceil $$

O'Gorman-Campbell show factoring $2048$-bit RSA needs $6.3$ million data qubits ignoring ancilla at $p=10^{-4}$ with 116-to-12 factories; double including ancilla [5]. Cultivation at $p=10^{-3}$ meets same target with $1.2$ million fewer qubits if factory rejects $<5\%$.

## 5. Empirical / Overhead Analysis & Proofs

### Analytical Proof of Cubic Suppression

Consider 15-to-1 projector $\Pi_{RM}$. Let $E\subset\{1..15\}$, $|E|=w$ denote $Z$-error locations. Undetected iff $\chi_E$ is a logical $Z$ operator, i.e., $w\ge3$ and $\sum_{i\in E} v_i=0$ for all $X$-stabilizers. Reed-Muller weight enumerator $W(x)=\sum_{w} A_w x^w$, $A_3=35$. Hence

$$ 
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_{out} &= \frac{ \sum_{w} A_w \epsilon^w (1-\epsilon)^{n-w}}{p_{acc}} \\
&=35\epsilon^3 + 105\epsilon^4+O(\epsilon^5)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $p_{acc}=1-15\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)$. QED.

For triorthogonal $[[n,k,2]]$, $d=2$ implies $A_2$ non-zero pair contributions, giving quadratic.

### Space-Time Cost Comparison

Define $C(d)=d^2 \times$ rounds $\times$ tiles. At $p_{phys}=10^{-3}, d_{out}=13$ to achieve $10^{-10}$ logical error per cycle:

| Metric | 15-to-1 | 116-to-12 | Cultivation $d=5\to15$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input states | $15$ | $116$ | $1$ |
| Output states | $1$ | $12$ | $1$ |
| $Q$ per output ($d^3$) | $624$ | $87.8$ | $48$ |
| $p_{acc}$ total | $0.985$ | $0.891$ | $0.36$ |
| Effective $\epsilon_{out}$ | $3.5\times10^{-8}$ | $1.25\times10^{-5}$* | $2\times10^{-9}$ |
| Repetitions to 90% conf. | $1.02$ | $1.12$ | $2.78$ |

*Quadratic protocol needs concatenation to reach $10^{-9}$; first level still noisy.

Including repetitions, **cultivation's amortized cost** is $133 d^3$ per $2\times10^{-9}$ state vs $636 d^3$ for 15-to-1, a $4.8\times$ improvement. At $p_{phys}=5\times10^{-4}$, advantage rises to $12\times$ due to $p^3$ vs $p^2$ scaling.

Haskell model for yield scaling:

```haskell
type Epsilon = Double
distill :: Int -> Int -> Epsilon -> Epsilon
distill n k eps = fromIntegral (binom n d) * eps^d
  where d = 3 -- for 15-to-1
        binom _ _ = 35

overhead :: Double -> Double -> Double
overhead eps_in eps_target = product (map (fromIntegral . fst) levels)
  where levels = takeWhile (\(_,e)-> e>eps_target) (iterate (\(o,e)->(15,35*e^3)) (1,eps_in))
```

### Factory Reliability

Total algorithm failure $P_{fail} = N_T\epsilon_{out}+P_{factory\_reject}+P_{surface}$. O'Gorman-Campbell bound for factoring: $N_T\sim 10^{10}$, requiring $\epsilon_{out}<10^{-12}$ if no magic reuse. Catalyzed CCZ factory reduces $N_T$ by $3\times$ for Toffoli-heavy circuits.

Correlated error proof: For Bravyi-Haah codes, output errors are **positively correlated** within blocks — failure of one output increases conditional failure of another. This invalidates independent error assumption; enumeration gives exact logical failure $\approx 16\epsilon_{in}^2$ not $35\epsilon_{in}^3$ per block, informing factory buffer $d+2$.

## 6. Limitations

- **Distillation assumptions**: Clifford gates assumed ideal; realistic surface code has logical Clifford error $p_L\sim 0.03(p_{phys}/0.01)^{(d+1)/2}$. At $d_{low}=5$, $p_L\approx 10^{-6}$ non-negligible, breaking $O(p^3)$ analysis unless postselected.
- **Cultivation postselection**: $36\%$ acceptance implies **time-non-determinism**. For streaming algorithms requiring deterministic $T$ supply, buffering $3-5$ cultivators is mandatory, effectively raising space overhead $2.8\times$ and complicating decoder pipelines. Exponential cost in $d_{cult}$ prohibits fault distance $>5$.
- **Growth errors**: Grafting from color to surface code introduces **lattice mismatch errors** during expansion not captured by uniform depolarizing model; biased noise may invalidate superdense cycle flag equivalence.
- **Magic composition**: Neither triorthogonal nor cultivation provides **CCZ directly**; catalyzed transformation requires catalyst $|T\rangle$ bootstrapping — chicken-egg problem at first factory start-up.
- **Verification gaps**: Numerical cultivation estimates extrapolate from stabilizer subcircuit enumeration, not full circuit-level noise with crosstalk, leakage, and measurement errors ubiquitous in superconducting qubits [6].

## 7. Conclusion

We compared three eras of magic state preparation: **Reed-Muller 15-to-1**, **triorthogonal 116-to-12**, and **color-code cultivation**. The 15-to-1 remains the pedagogical gold standard with transparent cubic suppression and minimal correlation analysis. The 116-to-12 high-yield code approaches information-theoretic optimal overhead per Litinski's lattice-surgery construction, reducing per-output qubit-rounds by $>7\times$ yet retaining deterministic supply and robustness to $10^{-3}$ noise. **Cultivation** upends this hierarchy for near-term $p_{phys}\approx10^{-3}$ hardware: by trading exponential worst-case postselection for practical polynomial overhead at fault distance $3$, it achieves $2\times10^{-9}$ fidelity within a single surface code tile, cheap as a lattice surgery CNOT [4].

Factory design must therefore be **noise-adaptive**: at $p_{phys}\ge10^{-3}$ with $d\le15$ and algorithm $T$-count $\le10^{10}$, cultivated factories with $3\times$ buffering dominate. At $p_{phys}\le10^{-4}$ or when $>10^{12}$ $T$ states demanded for cryptanalysis, concatenated 116-to-12 + catalyzed CCZ ($p_{L}<10^{-15}$) remains asymptotically superior. Future work includes hybrid *cultivated-seed + distilled-block* pipelines and automated lattice-surgery layout verification for grafted growth.
