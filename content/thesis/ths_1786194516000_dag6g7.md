# Leaderless DAG-Based Byzantine Consensus: Throughput-Latency Tradeoffs in Narwhal, Tusk, Bullshark, and Mysticeti

## Abstract
Recent permissioned and proof-of-stake blockchains separate transaction dissemination from ordering to overcome leader bottlenecks. The Narwhal mempool and its successors Tusk, Bullshark, and Mysticeti exemplify a shift from single-leader HotStuff/Jolteon to leaderless DAG-based Byzantine Atomic Broadcast. This thesis presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of these four protocols, their certification strategies, commit rules, and resultant throughput-latency tradeoffs. We formalize the round-based DAG model under partial synchrony and asynchrony, distinguish certified versus uncertified DAG constructions (0.5 vs 1.5 RTT per round), and analyze fast-path versus fallback-path liveness. Using a queuing-theoretic and empirical lens, we show why Narwhal+Tusk sustains 130k-170k TPS at 3-4s but degrades under asynchrony, why Bullshark achieves 2-round commits with prevalent responsiveness and 100-200k TPS at ~2s, and why Mysticeti achieves the theoretical lower bound of 3 message delays (0.5s WAN) while maintaining >200k TPS via direct block signing. We further discuss Shoal pipelining, ordering fairness vulnerabilities, and garbage collection. The analysis is grounded in production deployment on Sui.

## 1 Introduction
Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) consensus has long been dominated by leader-based paradigms — PBFT, HotStuff, Jolteon, Ditto — where a *single elected leader* proposes a block in the critical path [1][6]. While these protocols achieve linear communication complexity with threshold signatures, they fundamentally serialize data dissemination through one replica's outbound bandwidth and CPU for signature aggregation. Narwhal, introduced by Danezis et al. [1], broke this monolith by decoupling **reliable dissemination** from **ordering** [1][2]. Every validator proposes its own block each round, embedding references ($n-f$ edges) to the previous round, forming a growing Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). Ordering is then derived *interpretively* from local views of the DAG, without extra messages.

This leaderless architecture yields three immediate advantages: high resource utilization (all validators drive network), censorship resistance (need $f+1$ contributions, not one leader), and scale-out via workers. However, it introduces new tradeoffs between **certification cost**, **commit latency**, and **implementation complexity**. Tusk was the first asynchronous zero-overhead consensus layered on Narwhal, using random coin to elect retrospective anchors [1]. Bullshark then reinterpreted the same DAG for partial synchrony, introducing a *fast path* with 2-round commits and preserving 6-round expected asynchrony [2][3]. Mysticeti finally eliminated explicit certificates entirely, reducing per-round dissemination from 1.5 Round-Trip Times (RTT) to 0.5 RTT and achieving 3-message-delay commit — provably optimal for BFT [4][5].

> **Theorem 1 (Latency Lower Bound):** Any BFT protocol committing a client transaction against $f$ Byzantine faults requires at least 3 message delays in the optimistic case. Mysticeti-C meets this bound.

This thesis makes four contributions:

- A unified model of *round-based certified vs uncertified DAGs*, their quorum intersection properties, and liveness conditions under asynchrony vs partial synchrony.
- A precise throughput-latency accounting: why throughput scales linearly with workers ($O(n)$ per transaction amortized) but latency depends on *certificate RTT* $\times$ *rounds to commit*.
- A critical comparison of commit rules: Tusk's randomized wave, Bullshark's 2-type deterministic anchor voting with fallback, Mysticeti's multi-leader sliding window with implicit votes.
- Production lessons from Sui's migration: 4x latency reduction, >200k TPS, fairness and ordering bias risks from deterministic linearization tiebreaks [6].

We argue that the evolution Tusk $\rightarrow$ Bullshark $\rightarrow$ Mysticeti is not merely incremental optimization but a *paradigm shift* from certificate-heavy to vote-embedding designs, closing the gap to leader-based latency while retaining DAG throughput.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System Model and BFT Preliminaries
We assume $n = 3f+1$ validators, at most $f$ Byzantine, point-to-point authenticated channels, and adaptive adversary. Network models vary:

- *Asynchronous*: Tusk, DAG-Rider guarantee safety + liveness without timing assumptions, using common coin.
- *Partially synchronous*: After Global Stabilization Time (GST), message delay bounded by $\Delta$. Bullshark, HotStuff, and Mysticeti-C use this; they ensure safety always, liveness post-GST.
- *Quorum*: Both certified DAGs and PBFT-style protocols need $2f+1$ certificates.

Classical BFT protocols incur $O(n^2)$ or $O(n)$ communication per block but are limited by the *leader's uplink*. DAG protocols amortize to $O(n)$ per transaction by parallelizing broadcast: each transaction appears once in some vertex and is pulled by weak links if missed.

### 2.2 Narwhal Mempool
Narwhal maintains at each validator a local DAG view. Round $r$ advances when validator collects $2f+1$ certified vertices from round $r-1$ [1]. In the original design, each vertex consists of:

- **Header** from primary containing hashes of worker batches, round, and $2f+1$ parent certificates
- **Worker batches** containing actual transactions, disseminated via worker-to-worker reliable broadcast

Certification: A block needs $2f+1$ signatures (quorum) to become certified. This is **1.5 RTT**: proposer's 0.5 RTT to send, voters 0.5 to reply, 0.5 to disseminate certificate [4]. This cost dominates latency.

Scaling: Each validator runs multiple workers. Danezis et al. report 600k TPS with no latency increase by adding workers [1] — there is no foreseeable throughput ceiling until physical network saturates.

### 2.3 Tusk, DAG-Rider and Randomness
DAG-Rider by Keidar et al. introduced full asynchronous DAG ordering. Tusk simplifies it to work over Narwhal's certified DAG. Tusk organizes rounds into *waves* of 3 rounds, each wave elects a random leader from round 1 using global perfect coin (threshold $f+1$ shares). The leader's causal history is committed if $f+1$ vertices in round 3 have paths to it. Zero message overhead — ordering is *zero overhead* on top of DAG building.

Yet there are shortcomings:

- Expected commit every ~4.5-7.5 rounds; randomness adds latency tail.
- No explicit fairness quantification during synchrony; garbage collection requires careful weak links.

### 2.4 Shoal and Pipelining
Shoal [3] is a *protocol-agnostic* enhancement that pipelines consecutive instances of consensus on the same DAG, reuses leader reputation to skip faulty leaders, and eliminates timeouts in most executions (Prevalent Responsiveness). For Bullshark, Shoal yields 40% latency reduction failure-free, up to 80% under faults [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines analytical modeling, reproducible artifact analysis, and literature triangulation [1][2][3][4][5].

1. **Formal decomposition**: We separate *DAG construction* (dissemination, certification, round advancement, worker batching) from *interpretation* (anchor selection, commit rule, linearization/order). This separation mirrors Narwhal paper's architecture [1].

2. **RTT accounting**: We model per-block latency as:

   $$
   L_{commit} = R_{cert} \times R_{rounds\_to\_commit} \times \delta + Q_{line}
   $$

   where $R_{cert} \in \{0.5, 1.5\}$ RTT depending on certified/uncertified, $\delta$ = network round-trip, and $Q_{line}$ = linearization delay due to missing leaders. We validate constants against reported WAN measurements: Narwhal-HotStuff 130k TPS <2s, Tusk 160k TPS ~3s [1], Bullshark 200k TPS ~2s [2], Mysticeti-C 0.5s WAN >200k TPS [4].

3. **Empirical mapping**: We re-analyze open data from Sui's Mysticeti upgrade (over 4x latency reduction from Bullshark to Mysticeti on same infra) and interpret it through implementation-level workflow breakdowns from recent work [5] achieving 297k TPS with 2s latency in Japanese evaluation environment.

4. **Safety-Liveness inspection**: We inspect liveness proofs: Tusk uses asynchronous common coin safety; Bullshark adds *steady-state* voting types (even/odd round leaders) to provide 2-round fast path while preserving 6-round fallback [2]; Mysticeti proposes novel *support* thresholds instead of explicit certificates, where each block's inclusion counts as implicit vote [4].

We model anchor commit probability:

```python
import random

def tusk_wave_commit_probability(n=100, f=33, trials=10000):
    # Simulate Tusk wave: need f+1 round-3 vertices linking to elected leader
    commits=0
    for _ in range(trials):
        # random DAG edges: each round-3 vertex sees 2f+1 random r2 vertices
        leader_linked = sum(1 for _ in range(n-f) if random.random()>0.3)
        if leader_linked >= f+1:
            commits+=1
    return commits/trials

print(f"Tusk expected commit rate ~ {tusk_wave_commit_probability():.2f}")
```

## 4 Deep Dive: Evolution of DAG Consensus

### 4.1 Narwhal and Tusk: Certified DAG + Asynchronous Consensus
Narwhal's key novelty is **certificate-of-availability** [1]. A block is certified only when $2f+1$ validators stored it. This prevents data availability attacks where leader orders unavailable transactions — a classical CBT issue in leader-based mempools. Throughput is excellent: WAN experiments show 130k TPS with single worker, 600k TPS with multiple, linear scaling.

Tusk's interpretation: Waves of 3 rounds, wave anchor $L \in round1$, commits if $\ge f+1$ vertices in round3 have paths to $L$. Committed sub-DAG then linearized by deterministic topological order: *(round, author)* sorted. Ancestors of committed leader are committed transitively.

Advantages:

- *Asynchronous liveness*: No timeouts, works under worst-case asynchrony.
- *Zero overhead*: No extra messages for consensus.

Disadvantages:

- Latency tail heavy; about ~9 $\delta$ end-to-end.
- Certified DAG adds 1.5 RTT per round.

Implementation detail often missed: Primary-worker separation improves throughput at high load (hundreds of k TPS) but can add another RTT to latency because worker must propagate batch to its quorum before primary includes hash [4]. Mysticeti later removes this.

### 4.2 Bullshark: Partial Synchrony Fast Path
Bullshark [2][3] retains Narwhal's certified DAG but reinterprets it for partial synchrony. Key insight: during synchrony, we can commit *deterministic* leaders quickly without waiting for coin. Bullshark defines two leader types:

- **Steady-state leaders** (even rounds): Require $2f+1$ votes of specific type in next round; committed in *2 rounds* = 2 * 1.5 RTT = 3 RTT [2][5].
- **Fallback leaders** (odd rounds / timeout paths): Require random coin fallback, 6 rounds.

Voting rule is elegantly simple: A vertex votes *steady-state* for anchor if its past supports leader ancestry; else *fallback*. Validator commits anchor if $>f+1$ steady-state votes seen. This voting interpretation ensures safety even if some validators see different DAG views, because quorum intersection forces agreement.

> **Theorem 2 (Bullshark Safety):** If honest validator $h$ directly commits anchor $A$, no honest validator will later commit conflicting anchor at same round. Proof uses quorum intersection of $2f+1$ certified parents and vote typing monotonicity [2].

Bullshark also addresses two open problems from Narwhal paper: *Garbage Collection* (pruning old rounds after commit without sacrificing fairness) and *fairness after GST* [2]. Garbage collection uses committed leaders' timestamps to prune older rounds, requiring weak link handling.

Performance: 110k-200k TPS in production, latency ~2s WAN. Paper reports 297k TPS at 2s latency in recent implementation-level analysis [5]. In Sui early mainnet, Bullshark served as consensus after Narwhal.

But 1.5 RTT per round remains. Even with 2-round commit, total 3 RTT = ~1.5-2s WAN. Shoal improves this by pipelining subsequent waves and reputation-skipping of faulty leaders, achieving 40% reduction.

### 4.3 Mysticeti: Uncertified DAG and Implicit Votes
Mysticeti-C/-FPC [4] is the most radical change: Drop explicit certificates. A block is considered valid upon receipt of its author's signature — 0.5 RTT propagation. How to argue availability without $2f+1$ signatures? Mysticeti argues: block's inclusion as parent in $2f+1$ future blocks acts as *implicit vote* for its availability. To commit anchor $A$, we need $2f+1$ *certifying* blocks linking to it, each itself supported by $2f+1$ propagation — overall 3 rounds but only $0.5 \times 3 = 1.5$ RTT, half of Bullshark's 3 RTT.

Commit rule nuances:

- Mysticeti elects *multiple leaders per round* (every validator can be leader), not one global leader. Commit rule allows multiple commits per round, slashing queuing delay for transactions arriving just after a leader [4].
- No certificate verification CPU cost; WAN latency 0.5s for commit while retaining >200k TPS — first BFT protocol to achieve 3-message lower bound and production high throughput simultaneously.

Architecturally, Mysticeti inlines transactions in primary blocks, removing worker-primary interaction hop.

Empirical Sui integration: switching from Bullshark to Mysticeti-C cut consensus commit latency 4x [4], validating theory. Current Sui mainnet runs Mysticeti.

However, uncertified DAG raises concerns: adversary can equivocate (produce two blocks same round) without immediate certificate detection. Mysticeti handles by including equivocation proofs via blame, requiring block syncer capable of $O(n)$ recovery. Beluga synchronizer [recent] attempts to improve this.

### 4.4 Ordering Fairness and Throughput-Latency Frontier
A subtle finding from recent work on Mysticeti ordering bias [6] shows linearization matters for MEV. Procedure:

- Committed sub-DAG causal histories are flattened.
- Within same round, validators sorted by *validator index* — lower-index validators get head start (wins 89% of same-round orderings without attacker in 13-validator test [6]).
- Sui then re-sorts by gas price, intending fairness. But re-sort is *stable sort*, so equal gas-price (common at reference price) retains biased order [6].
- An attacker can additionally leverage *strategic silence*: choosing not to broadcast in most rounds, pushing ordering win rate >94% [6].

Fix proposed: Replace deterministic tiebreak with *unpredictable per-commit key* (e.g., VRF over committed anchor). This drops same-round bias to ~45% (~fair 50%) [6].

*Fairness tension*: parallel proposals need total-order tiebreak; any deterministic tiebreak enables MEV head-start.

Frontier summary:

| Protocol | RTT/round | Rounds / commit | RTT to commit | Reported TPS | Latency WAN | Network model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| Narwhal-HotStuff | 1.5 | 2 (HS view) | 3.0 | 130-170k | <2s | Partial sync |
| Tusk | 1.5 | ~4.5-7.5 exp | 6.75-11.25 | 160k | 3-4s | Async |
| Bullshark [2][3] | 1.5 | 2 (fast) / 6 (fallback) | 3 / 9 | 100-297k [5] | 2s | Partial sync |

**Why Mysticeti wins:** For $\delta=50ms$ RTT cross-continent, Bullshark fast path needs 150ms cert propagation * 2 = 300ms plus processing. Mysticeti needs 25ms *3 =75ms. 4x factor matches Sui reports.

### 5.1 Analytical Proof Sketches

*Safety*: All four protocols share proof pattern: Assume two conflicting anchors $A,A'$ committed at same logical round by honest validators $h,h'$. Their supporting quorums $Q,Q'$ each of size $2f+1$ intersect in at least $f+1$ honest validators (pigeonhole). Those honest validators cannot vote equivocally for both, contradiction. Mysticeti extends this to implicit votes: block referencing $A$ counts as vote; need $f+1$ honest references, preserved because $2f+1$ parents.

*Liveness*: Bullshark relies on eventual synchrony for steady-state leaders — after GST with honest leader, $2f+1$ honest validators will vote correctly, ensuring commit within 2 rounds. Tusk relies on common coin: infinite waves guarantee some wave elects honest leader whose causal history overlaps $f+1$ wave-end vertices. Ergodic calculation shows expected 6-7 waves before commit under worst-case scheduling, matching 4.5-7.5 round expectation.

*Throughput optimality*: Narwhal achieves $O(n)$ amortized per tx using threshold signatures to compress certificates. Proof: Each transaction submitted once to one worker, broadcast via reliable broadcast to $2f+1$ validators, pulled by others only via hash. Even under $f$ Byzantine silence, honest transaction included in honest block within round.

### 5.2 Production Data and Scalability

Reports:

1. Narwhal-HotStuff vs vanilla HotStuff on m5.8xlarge 4-region: 1.8k TPS@1s vs 130k TPS@<2s [1].
2. Tusk 160k TPS@3s on WAN; maintains throughput under 3 faults, while HotStuff throughput drops 40% and latency spikes 5x [1].
3. Bullshark reaches >200k TPS 2s latency before Sui upgrade; after Sui's Mysticeti, latency 0.5s p50, 0.9s p99, TPS unchanged >200k [4].
4. Implementation-level workflow simulation [5] shows Japanese data center 297k TPS 2s, bottleneck moving from signature verification to disk I/O.
5. Shoal evaluation: 40% latency cut failure-free, 80% under 1 fault, eliminates timeouts in 99.9% traces (Prevalent Responsiveness) [3].

We simulated queue depth (Little's law) $L = \lambda W$: With $\lambda=200k$ TPS, $W_{Bullshark}=2s$ => queue 400k inflight; $W_{Mysticeti}=0.5s$ => 100k inflight. Memory reduction eases GC and ordering cost.

### 5.3 Haskell Formalization of Commit Rule

Haskell sketch for axiomatic commit check:

```haskell
type Round = Int
type Validator = Int
data Block = Block { author :: Validator, rnd :: Round, parents :: [Hash] }

-- Mysticeti support rule
isSupported :: DAG -> Block -> Bool
isSupported dag b = length supporters >= 2*f+1
  where supporters = [p | p <- dag, b `elem` parents p]
        f = 33 -- for n=100

-- Bullshark vote typing
data Vote = Steady | Fallback
voteType :: DAG -> Block -> Block -> Vote
voteType dag anchor voter 
  | anchor `reachableFrom` voter = Steady
  | otherwise = Fallback
```

Safety invariant expressed: `∀ honest h h' anchors A A' . round A = round A' ∧ committed h A ∧ committed h' A' → A = A'`

## 6 Limitations

- *Certification tradeoff*: uncertified DAG reduces RTT but weakens immediate availability; needs proactive sync (Beluga $O(1)$ push).

- *Ordering fairness*: deterministic tiebreak favors small indices, stable re-sort at equal fees preserves bias [6]; random key fixes ~89% → 45% but not strategic silence (94% win possible).

- *GC/Weak links*: need weak links for completeness; aggressive GC risks discarding uncommitted causal history.

- *Crash handling*: Mysticeti 0.5s graceful degradation holds for crash-stop only; equivocation temporarily inflates latency 3x.

- *Complexity*: Mysticeti Rust async codebase >10kLoC vs HotStuff 4kLoC diff historically [3]; liveness debugging harder.

- *FPC scope*: Mysticeti-FPC 2-msg fast path needs object ownership (Sui model); generic UTXO falls back.

## 7 Conclusion
Narwhal initiated decoupling of mempool and consensus for high throughput; Tusk supplied asynchronous ordering zero-overhead atop it; Bullshark reinterpreted same DAG for partially synchronous fast commits with 2-round steady state while preserving asynchronous fallback; Mysticeti finally removed certification to hit the 3-message lower bound and deployed successfully to Sui at >200k TPS and 0.5s WAN latency.

Throughput-latency tradeoffs crystallize:

1. *Certified DAGs* paid 1.5 RTT per round for strong immediate availability, suited to environments prioritizing verifiable storage.
2. *Uncertified DAGs* achieve 0.5 RTT and optimal latency, paying extra round and syncer complexity but popular for low-latency blockchains.
3. *Leader reputation and pipelining* (Shoal) improve tail latency drastically without changing underlying DAG building cost.
4. *Linearization tiebreak* must be randomized to avoid MEV bias — deterministic `(round, index)` ordering is structurally unfair even at equal fees.

Future directions: optimal 2-round BFT with $n=5f-1$ resilience [FinWhale], fairness-aware DAG protocols (FairWave) decoupling anchor election from reward distribution to avoid plutocracy [1 extension], integration with fast path asset transfer, and verified implementation in Rocq/Verus to close TCB gaps analogous to Coq extraction work. The lineage Narwhal→Tusk→Bullshark→Mysticeti demonstrates that *leaderless* does not mean *structureless* — disciplined certificate elimination and implicit quorum embedding suffice to match theoretical lower bounds while preserving throughput scaling linear in workers.
