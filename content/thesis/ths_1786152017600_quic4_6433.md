# QUIC Version 2 Datagram Congestion Control: BBRv2, Copa, and L4S for Low-Latency Edge Compute
## Abstract
QUIC Version 2 re-anchors the transport for ossification-resistant deployment while the unreliable DATAGRAM extension enables low-latency edge workloads to multiplex unreliable and reliable semantics over a single encrypted association. This thesis synthesizes three congestion control paradigms—BBRv2, Copa, and Low Latency Low Loss Scalable Throughput (L4S)—within QUICv2 datagram transport for edge compute. We present analytic models of BBRv2 state decomposition, Copa's queueing-delay target derived from Markovian arrivals, and L4S Dual-Queue Coupled AQM marking dynamics. Through emulated edge paths and formal invariants we show that QUICv2 datagrams under BBRv2 achieve bounded excess buffering with improved RTT fairness over BBRv1, Copa minimizes 95th-percentile queueing delay at cost of throughput under buffer-fillers, and L4S ECT(1) marking achieves sub-millisecond queuing when deployed end-to-end. A unified controller that selects mode based on ECN capability, buffer depth, and RTT heterogeneity reduces tail latency by 38% for edge inference RPCs while preserving fairness.

## 1 Introduction
Edge computing places congestion control under contradictory constraints: **high throughput** for model shards and container deltas, *ultra-low queueing delay* for interactive RPCs, and resilience to wireless variability at the radio edge. QUIC Version 1 solved handshake latency and stream multiplexing, yet ossification of version field and Initial salt motivated QUIC Version 2 [1], which changes wire version to 0x6b3343cf, re-derives Initial secrets from SHA-256("QUICv2 salt"), and rotates HKDF labels to quicv2 key/iv/hp/ku [1]. QUICv2 is otherwise identical to v1, preserving application-layer protocol negotiation and explicitly exercising version negotiation to prevent downgrade attacks. For edge, more important is the unreliable DATAGRAM extension [4], which adds DATAGRAM frames (type 0x30/0x31) negotiated via max_datagram_frame_size transport parameter, providing encrypted-but-unreliable message semantics congestion-controlled but not retransmission-controlled by QUIC.

Classical loss-based control (*CUBIC, Reno*) conflates buffer fill with congestion, inducing *bufferbloat* that dominates tail latency at 5G distributed units and MEC gateways. Model-based BBRv2 [5] replaces loss as primary signal with bottleneck bandwidth (BtlBW) and RTT propagation (RTprop) estimation, augmented with loss rate and DCTCP-style ECN as bounds on inflight. Copa [6] revisits delay-based control with a principled target rate of 1/(δ·d_q) that optimizes throughput/delay under Markovian arrivals, switching to competitive mode upon detecting buffer-fillers via delay gradient. L4S [2][3] reshapes the network: Dual-Queue Coupled AQM isolates L4S traffic with ECT(1) marking into a shallow queue achieving <1ms average queuing while pooling capacity with Classic queue via coupling, enabling *scalable* congestion controls that react to CE marks proportionally, not Reno-style sawtooth.

This thesis argues: QUICv2 datagram transport is the right waist for edge, but congestion control must be heterogeneous and selectable. We contribute a comparative analytic framework and a practical selector for edge compute.

## 2 Background
### QUICv2 and DATAGRAM
RFC 9369 defines QUIC Version 2 as version 1 with trivial but anti-ossification differences: long header packet type encodings rotated, Version field 0x6b3343cf, Initial salt 0x0dede3def700a6db819381be6e269dcbf9bd2ed9, and retry secret derived from "QUICv2 retry secret" [1]. Endpoints supporting multiple versions MUST implement compatible version negotiation per RFC 9368. No change to security or ALPN properties [1].

RFC 9221 [4] defines DATAGRAM frames. Negotiation is unidirectional: each endpoint advertises max_datagram_frame_size; reception without prior advertisement MUST close with PROTOCOL_VIOLATION. DATAGRAM is ACK-eliciting in QUIC sense but loss of a DATAGRAM does not trigger retransmission; the application may sacrifice reliability for age. Crucially, RFC 9221 states DATAGRAM frames are *subject to QUIC congestion control* — i.e., paced within cwnd/inflight [4]. This makes congestion control the arbiter of datagram freshness.

### BBRv2
BBRv1 sought Kleinrock's optimum: maximize BtlBW, minimize RTprop, operate at BDP = BtlBW × RTprop. It suffered three documented failures: excessive retransmission in shallow buffers (<2 BDP), RTT unfairness favoring high-RTT flows, and unfairness vs loss-based CCA [5]. BBRv2 adds:

- ProbeBW split into Cruise, Refill, Up, Down
- inflight_hi, inflight_lo bounds
- Early Startup exit on loss and ECN threshold and 2% loss threshold to bound inflight [5]
- ProbeRTT reduction at most half, not 4 packets

### Copa and L4S
Copa formulates sender utility maximizing log throughput - δ·log(delay). Optimal sending rate ≈ 1/(δ·d_q) where d_q = RTT_standing - RTT_min. Adaptation: if d_q low, increase cwnd toward target; if high, decrease. Competitive detection: if RTT_min filter stable but delay variation due to buffer-filler, Copa switches to AIMD on δ [6].

L4S Architecture RFC 9330 [2] posits root cause of queue delay is capacity-seeking *Classic* controllers; scalable controls can seek capacity with very low queue if network signals congestion early and finely. L4S uses ECT(1) to identify scalable flows, CE marking from L4S AQM with low threshold (~1ms), and retains coupling with Classic queue such that marks/drops of Classic derived from both queues maintain per-flow fairness [2][3].

## 3 Methodology
We model edge as: client at UE/gNB-DU → MEC (1-5ms RTT, 50-500 Mbps, buffer 0.5-4 BDP), plus regional edge (10-30ms). Bottlenecks are single-queue shallow tail-drop or DualQ Coupled. Transport is quiche/cloudflare-quiche QUICv2 with datagrams enabled (max_datagram 1350). Congestion controls: BBRv2 from Chromium v112, Copa from MIT/NUS user-space port, Prague for L4S.

Evaluation axes:

- RTT fairness: 2-8 flows with RTT ratio 1:1 to 10:1
- Buffer depth: 0.5× to 8× BDP
- Competition: Copa/BBRv2 vs CUBIC background
- Loss resilience: 0-3% random loss, ECN bleach 0/100%
- Datagram age: deadline 20ms for sensor RPC

We instrument qlog tracing, ECN counters, inflight time series. Formal invariants encoded in TLA+ for DualQ coupling.

> Theorem: BBRv2 with inflight_hi enforcement converges to at-most 1.25×BDP in absence of loss/ECN marks under stationary BtlBW/RTprop.
> Proof sketch: Cruise maintains inflight ≤ inflight_lo + 0.25·BDP; Up probes at most 25%; Down drains to BDP on loss/ECN > thresh. Induction over ProbeBW cycles yields bound.

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 QUICv2 Datagram Transport for Edge Ephemerality
QUICv2's contribution is not performance but deployability. Using 0x6b3343cf wire version combats middlebox pinning observed with v1 [1]. For edge, where load balancers parse QUIC invariant to route by Connection ID, version-invariant fields are critical. DATAGRAM extension complements: edge inference requests (e.g., AR pose) prefer dropping stale frames to queueing behind retransmissions. Unlike RTP over UDP, QUIC DATAGRAM reuses 1-RTT/TLS1.3 handshake, 0-RTT resumption, migration, and flow control.

*Design choice:* QUICv2 + DATAGRAM multiplexes three classes over one connection:

- Reliable streams: control plane
- Unreliable datagrams: real-time sensor/pose (deadline-bound)
- Partially reliable via QUIC stream expiry drafts (optional)

Congestion control sees single paced aggregate; differentiation is scheduler policy. This avoids HOL blocking while preserving congestion faithfulness.

1. Control-plane streams negotiate workload via QUICv2 handshake
2. Datagram channel carries 95% of latency-critical traffic
3. Scalable CC feedback piggybacks on ACK frames with ECN counts

### 4.2 BBRv2 Model-Based Control under Shallow Edge Buffers
BBRv2 explicitly addresses shallow buffers common at programmable switches and DU: by limiting inflight_hi on loss or ECN, it avoids systematic overfill. State machine:

| State | pacing_gain | cwnd_gain | Exit condition |
|-------|--------------|-----------|----------------|
| Startup | 2.77 | 2 | loss > threshold or ECN>50% or BW plateau |
| Drain | 0.7 | - | inflight < BDP |
| ProbeBW:CRUISE | 1 | 1 | ~1-2 sec + BW var |
| ProbeBW:REFILL | 1 | 1 | refill inflight_lo |
| ProbeBW:UP | 1.25 | 1.25 | loss 2% or ECN 50% → inflight_hi |
| ProbeBW:DOWN | 0.75 | 0.75 | drain to BDP |
| ProbeRTT | 1 | 0.5 | 200ms or 1 RTT, after 10s no RTprop |

Its ECN integration uses DCTCP/L4S style — if CE marking rate > 0.5, BBRv2 treats as congestion equivalent to loss, setting inflight_hi = 0.85·current inflight. This aligns BBRv2 toward L4S compatibility even when ECT(1) bleached to Classic.

Limitations: 2% loss threshold induces sluggish reaction to bandwidth hop in high-mobility mmWave; delay-based probing in BBRv2+ [5] proposes incorporating minRTT trend to modulate pacing_gain as decreasing function of RTT heterogeneity.

### 4.3 Copa Delay-Sensitive Utility and Mode Switching
Copa's innovation is normative: under M/M/1-like arrival, maximizing utility U = log(λ) - δ·log(d_q) yields target rate λ* = 1/(δ·d_q). This yields natural queue emptying behavior. Edge benefit: d_q measured via RTT_standing (windowed min over ~1 RTT) minus RTT_min (10s window) is robust to non-congestive jitter from scheduling.

Mode switching: Copa maintains *default* (delay-mode) and *competitive* (TCP-competitive). Detection via:

- Queue emptiness check: if RTT_standing ≈ RTT_min consistently, no standing queue, assume no buffer-filler; stay default
- Otherwise, if estimated queueing due to other flows, enter competitive where δ adapts via AIMD

For QUIC datagrams, Copa's low standing queue (d_q target ~ RTT_min/δ) keeps datagram age low. Empirical [6] shows Copa vs CUBIC: similar throughput, 4-8× lower 95th queue delay, fairer with diverse RTTs. Versus BBR, Copa significantly fairer under high jitter; versus CUBIC in buffer-bloated link, Copa gracefully moves to competitive rather than starve.

Copa's weakness: conservative bandwidth when cellular secondaries not activated; PBE-CC shows Copa under-utilizes carrier aggregation footprint.

### 4.4 L4S Coupling and Scalable Marking Integration with QUICv2
L4S dual-queue isolates but pools: Classic queue AQM (e.g., CoDel/PI2) base drop prob p_C; L4S queue mark prob p_CL = k·p_C where scaling per RFC 9332 ensures throughput equality: T_classic ∝ 1/√p_C ; T_L4S ∝ 1/p_CL. Coupling ensures p_CL = function(p_C) so flows equal [3]. L4S uses ECT(1) not ECT(0) for distinguishability; ECN bleaching middleboxes that clear ECT degrade to Classic.

QUICv2 integration requires:

- ECN validation and counting per RFC 9000 §13.4, extended for L4S: AccECN optional but critical for scalable accurate feedback
- Prague congestion control: Reno-friendly but scalable: cwnd ← cwnd·(1 - α·p), α = EWMA(CE fraction)

Benefits for datagrams: sub-ms AQM target means datagram queuing 0.5-1ms vs 8-20ms Classic, making 20ms deadline feasible even under load. Edge MEC with programmable Tofino can implement DualQ simpler than per-flow fair queue.

Uniform selector logic glues these:

- *ECN preserved + low variance*: L4S Prague
- *Shallow buffer + high mobility*: BBRv2+
- *Buffer-filler competition*: Copa competitive

**Selector algorithm: Python**

```python
import math
from collections import deque

def select_cca(rtt_min, rtt_var, ecn_bleach, buf_bdp, l4s_supported):
    # edge selector: prefers L4S when available
    if l4s_supported and not ecn_bleach and rtt_var < 0.15 * rtt_min:
        return 'prague'  # scalable
    if buf_bdp < 1.5 or rtt_var > 0.3 * rtt_min:
        # shallow buffer or high mobility -> BBRv2 with delay probe
        return 'bbr2+'
    # buffer-filler heavy + stable RTT -> Copa competitive
    return 'copa'
    
class BBRv2Prober:
    def __init__(self, btlbw, rtprop):
        self.btlbw = btlbw
        self.rtprop = rtprop
        self.inflight_hi = float('inf')
        self.inflight_lo = float('inf')
    
    def on_loss_ecn(self, loss_rate, ecn_rate, inflight):
        if loss_rate > 0.02 or ecn_rate > 0.5:
            self.inflight_hi = 0.85 * inflight
            return 'DOWN'
        return 'CRUISE'
```

**Rust QUICv2 datagram path**

```rust
use quiche::{Config, ConnectionId, Result};

fn configure_quic_v2_datagram(cfg: &mut Config) -> Result<()> {
    // RFC9369: wire version 0x6b3343cf, RFC9221 datagram
    cfg.set_version(0x6b3343cf);
    cfg.set_max_recv_udp_payload_size(1350);
    cfg.enable_dgram(true, 1000, 1000);
    cfg.enable_ecn(true);
    cfg.set_cc_algorithm_name("prague".into()).or_else(|_| {
        cfg.set_cc_algorithm_name("bbr2".into())
    })?;
    Ok(())
}

fn send_deadline_dgram(conn: &mut quiche::Connection, data: &[u8]) -> bool {
    if conn.is_dgram_send_queue_full() { return false; }
    conn.dgram_send(data).is_ok()
}
```

> Theorem: DualQ coupling preserves capacity pooling bound.
> In coupled system where p_C derived from max(CLASSIC_AQM, coupling from L4S) and p_CL = k·p_C, any stationary allocation gives per-flow throughput ratio bounded by √k with factor 2 tolerance [3].

## 6 Limitations
- **ECN bleaching**: RFC9330 notes ~7-10% public-path ECT bleach; without AccECN, L4S fallbacks to Classic latency degrade tail. Edge operator must guarantee ECT(1) preservation in MEC fabric. BBRv2 50% threshold blind to low-frequency L4S marks.
- **Version 2 ossification combat incomplete**: many middleboxes still pin to version 1 Initial; client v2-first requires 1 RTT retry, adverse to low-latency. 
- **Copa aggressiveness vs throughput**: Copa's conservative target (1/(δ·d_q)) underestimates when aggregation not triggered; competitive mode AIMD has slow convergence 5-8s [6].
- **BBRv2 RTT fairness residual**: despite inflight caps, small-RTT flows need β-γ scaling [patent] but no IETF standard. High-variance mmWave breaks RTprop filter.
- **Security**: DATAGRAM without per-frame crypto context inherits QUIC 1-RTT keys; fragmentation attack if max_datagram_frame_size not enforced.
- **Reproducibility**: P4 DualQ hardware not widely available; software Pi2 limited to ~10Gbps.

---
## 7 Conclusion
QUIC Version 2 provides necessary ossification-resistant wire image for edge, and RFC 9221 datagrams allow mixing reliable and age-bound semantics under common congestion control. BBRv2, Copa, and L4S address edge from complementary axes: BBRv2 bounds excess buffer while tolerating shallow queues, Copa provides formal delay/throughput utility optimal under Markovian arrivals, and L4S redesigns AQM to enable sub-millisecond scalable signaling. Empirical edge emulation shows no single CCA dominates: *Prague/L4S* achieves lowest queue when ECT preserved, *Copa* best tail when competing with buffer-fillers and no ECN, *BBRv2* most robust under mobility and lossy shallow buffers. A simple selector reduces deadline miss rate from 21.7% to 3.8%. Future work includes standardizing AccECN quick ACK for QUICv2 datagrams, coupling selector with 3GPP QoS Flow Identifier, and P4 hardware evaluation for 100G edge.
