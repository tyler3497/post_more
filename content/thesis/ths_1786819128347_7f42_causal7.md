# Causal Discovery Under Latent Confounding and Selection Bias: FCI/cFCI Orientation Rules, PAG Markov Equivalence, Kernel-Based Conditional Independence Tests, and Score-Based Differentiation

## Abstract
Latent confounders and selection variables break the causal sufficiency assumption that underpins the PC algorithm, necessitating ancestral graph models that represent confounding and selection without explicit latent nodes. This thesis studies the FCI family of constraint-based discovery under causal insufficiency, where the target is not a single DAG but a Markov equivalence class of Maximal Ancestral Graphs (MAGs) compactly encoded as a Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG). We unify theoretical foundations from Spirtes et al. [1][2], Zhang [3], Colombo et al. [4], and recent hybrid score-based extensions [5][6]. We formalize m-separation, discriminating paths, possible-D-SEP sets, and FCI-stable skeleton search, then dissect the full sound-and-complete orientation calculus R0-R10 that maximally orients invariant edge marks. Conservative FCI (cFCI) and dcFCI introduce stability against empirical unfaithfulness by voting and scoring PAGs [6]. On the statistical test side, we contrast parametric Fisher-Z and G^2 against non-parametric kernel tests: KCI [7], its randomized approximations RCIT/RCoT [8], and FastKCI mixture-of-experts scaling [9]. Finally we bridge to continuous optimization: NOTEARS trace-exponential DAG constraint [10], its ADMG extension with bow-free constraints [11], and deconfounded score methods exploiting low-rank plus sparse precision decompositions [12]. The goal is a practitioner-ready yet theoretically rigorous map from oracle FCI to deployable large-sample algorithms.

## 1 Introduction

***Causal discovery under latent confounding and selection bias*** is the regime where observational data alone cannot pinpoint a unique causal DAG, yet still carries invariant causal facts. Standard constraint-based learning assumes *causal sufficiency* — no unmeasured common causes of measured variables and no selection on unobserved colliders. When violated, conditional independencies no longer correspond one-to-one to d-separation in a DAG over observables [1][2].

The FCI (Fast Causal Inference) algorithm, introduced by Spirtes, Glymour and Scheines and refined through two decades, addresses this by learning a **Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG)** that represents the entire equivalence class of underlying causal structures consistent with independence facts, even allowing selection bias [1][2][3][4]. A PAG encodes what is invariant across all compatible MAGs:

- `X -> Y` : X is an ancestor of Y in every MAG, Y not ancestor of X
- `X <-> Y` : X not ancestor of Y, Y not ancestor of X, but latent confounding present
- `X o-> Y` : Y not ancestor of X, X -> Y *or* X <-> Y both possible
- `X o-o Y` : no ancestry claim invariant
- `X o- X` / undirected variants indicate selection bias context

> **Theorem 1.1 (PAG Markov Equivalence Invariant):** *A PAG represents all invariant edge marks and invariant ancestral relationships shared by all MAGs in the Markov equivalence class defined by m-separation equivalence over observed variables under latent and selection variables [3].* Any mark `*` that is not invariant is left as circle `o`.

This thesis claims four contributions:

1. Complete formalism of ancestral graphs, MAGs, PAGs, m-separation, and discriminating paths enabling orientation rules R1-R10 completeness proof due to Zhang [3].
2. Comparative analysis of FCI, FCI+, RFCI, cFCI, and ICD anytime variants trading informativeness for test complexity and stability [2][4][5].
3. Detailed treatment of kernel-based conditional independence tests KCI, RCIT/RCoT, FastKCI enabling non-parametric causal discovery at scale [7][8][9].
4. Bridging to score-based differentiable discovery under latent confounding via precision decomposition and DC-programs [11][12].

We structure remainder as background §2, methodology §3, deep dive §4.1-4.5, empirical/proofs §5, limitations §6, conclusion §7.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Ancestral Graphs and m-Separation

A **directed mixed graph** may contain directed `->` and bidirected `<->` edges. It is *ancestral* if (i) no directed cycle, (ii) no almost-directed cycle via `X <-> Y` where X is ancestor of Y, (iii) no arrowhead into undirected edge. A DAG with latent `L` and selection `S` induces over observables `O` a MAG that preserves ancestral relations and m-separation [1].

**Def 2.1 (m-separation).** In a mixed graph, a path is m-connecting given Z iff (a) every non-collider not in Z, (b) every collider is ancestor of some member of Z. Otherwise blocked. When selection variables **S** are conditioned upon by design (implicit selection), m-separation among O must hold in MAG restricted to ancestor closure of **S**.

### 2.2 Markov Equivalence of MAGs

Two MAGs are Markov equivalent iff same m-separation facts, equivalently same skeleton, v-structures, and discriminating path collider statuses [3]. The PAG is maximally informative graph with edges `*` marks : `tail -`, `arrowhead >`, `circle o`.

- Invariant tail = ancestor in all MAGs in class
- Invariant arrowhead = non-ancestor in all MAGs
- Non-invariant = circle

This generalizes CPDAG where equivalence over DAGs only.

### 2.3 Selection Bias Modeling

If selection variable `S=1` is conditioned (sample inclusion), dependencies may be induced between otherwise independent causes of selection. Ancestral models accommodate via `S` layer: MAG allows undirected edges `X - Y` representing selection-induced association where neither is ancestor but correlated given `S`. FCI explicitly allows `S` contexts without modeling `S` explicitly [2][3].

*Insight:* **selection bias breaks PC soundness** — conditional independence even given all adjacencies may not exist; need Possible-D-SEP search [1][4].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt oracle analysis plus finite-sample test analysis + combinatorial proof of rule completeness.

1. **Skeleton phase:** FCI-Stable starts complete undirected. For conditioning set size `k=0..d-2`, test `X ⊥ Y | Z` where `Z` subset of adjacencies of X or Y, exhaustive. Remove edge if independent, store sepset `Sep(X,Y)`. Order-independent by deferring deletions [4]. This yields superset of true MAG skeleton.

2. **Possible-D-SEP phase:** Standard FCI for remaining edges `X o-o Y` tests `X ⊥ Y | Z` where Z ⊆ Possible-D-Sep(X,Y) ∪ Possible-D-Sep(Y,X) — nodes reachable via circle paths that may be D-separating. If independence, remove edge. Update sepsets. RFCI skips this for speed, losing some skeleton prunes but retaining sound ancestry claims [4].

3. **Orientation phase:** Reorient all edges to `o-o`. Apply colliders via sepset rule, then R0-R10 until fixpoint [3].

4. **Statistical tests:** Fisher-Z for linear Gaussian, G^2 / chi-square for discrete, kernel tests for non-parametric. Evaluate power vs `|Z|`.

5. **Score bridge:** Continuous formulation with ADMG adjacency `W` directed + `B` bidirected covariance, trace-exp `h(W)=tr(exp(W∘W))-d` acyclicity [10], plus bow-free algebraic constraints distinguishing confounding from direct effect [11].

> **Lemma 3.1 (Possible-D-SEP Superset Property).** *In oracle limit with faithful distribution to underlying DAG with latent/selection, if X and Y are m-separated in true MAG then some Z ⊆ Possible-D-SEP(X,Y) m-separates them, so FCI skeleton search discovers removal.* *Proof sketch* via collider path characterization in Zhang 2008a Lemma 5 [3].

```python
# FCI skeleton stable implementation conceptual
from itertools import combinations
def fci_stable_skeleton(data, ci_test, alpha=0.05, max_k=3):
    nodes = list(data.keys())
    adj = {x: set(nodes)-{x} for x in nodes}
    sepset = {}
    for k in range(max_k+1):
        to_remove = []
        for x in nodes:
            for y in list(adj[x]):
                if y not in adj[x]: continue
                # test subsets of adj[x]\{y} and adj[y]\{x}
                for cond in combinations(adj[x]-{y}, k):
                    if ci_test(data, x, y, cond) > alpha:
                        to_remove.append((x,y,cond))
                        break
        for x,y,cond in to_remove:
            adj[x].discard(y); adj[y].discard(x)
            sepset[(x,y)] = cond
    return adj, sepset
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ancestral Graph Formalism and PAG Semantics Under Latent and Selection Variables

Let underlying DAG **G** over `V = O ∪ L ∪ S`, `O` observed, `L` latent, `S` selection conditioned on `S=1`. Define MAG **M** over O : `X -> Y` if X ancestor of Y in G and no other observable intermediating conditioning, `X <-> Y` if latent confounding ancestor of both but neither ancestor of other. Richardson & Spirtes show **M** preserves conditional independencies among O given S (m-separation ↔ d-separation in G) and is maximal: every missing edge corresponds to some m-separation [1][3].

**Maximality** matters: adding any edge destroys some independence equivalence. Thus MAG is minimal I-map in mixed graph class; PAG adds circles to mask unidentifiable directions.

Selection bias examples:
- Berkson's paradox: `X -> S <- Y`, conditioning on S induces `X o-o Y` even if marginally independent.
- Undirected edge `X - Y` appears in PAG when selection induces dependence where removal would otherwise exist but PAG must remain ancestral after selection conditioning.

> **Def 4.1 (Possible-D-SEP).** For nodes X≠Y in current partially oriented graph, `Pds(X,Y)` = nodes Z≠X where there exists a path X ... Z where every subpath <U,V,W> is collider or forms triangle. Sound superset of true D-Sep.

### 4.2 Orientation Ruleset: v-Structure Recovery, Discriminating Path, and Zhang's Completeness (R1-R10)

**R0 / v-structure orientation:** For triple `X o-o Z o-o Y`, X and Y non-adjacent, if `Z ∉ Sep(X,Y)` orient `X o-> Z <-o Y`. Else leave as non-collider. Correct because collider `X -> Z <- Y` makes X and Y dependent given Z, so observing dependence implies separating set excludes Z [2][3].

**R1:** If `X o-> Y` and `Z o-o X` where `Z *-> Y` (?) actually `X -> Y o-o Z` with `X` and `Z` nonadjacent, orient `Y -> Z`? Wait exact Zhang notation: `A *-> B o-o C` and `A` not adjacent to `C` ⇒ `B -> C`. Intuition: `B` being ancestor of `C` else creates new v-structure contradiction.

Zhang 2008 completes FCI's 3 rules to 10 rules [3]:

- **R0:** Collider discovery
- **R1:** As above - away from collider causing p→
- **R2:** `X -> Y o-> Z` or `X -> Y o-o Z` plus `X *-> Z` yields `X -> Z` orientation? Real rule: `X -> Y *-> Z` and `X o-o Z` ⇒ `X -> Z`
- **R3:** Double triangle: `X *-> Y <-* Z`, `X *-> W <-* Z`, `X *-> Z`, `W *-> Y`? Orient `W`? Kolmogorov-style discriminating? Formal: `X *-> Y <-* Z`, `X *-> W <-* Z` ... Paper variant: `X *-> Y <-* Z`, `X *-> W o-o Y`, `Z *-> W o-o Y` ... ⇒ orient `W -> Y`
- **R4:** Discriminating path for `Y`: path `X .. V,W,Y,Z` ending `W o-o Y o-? Z` where every node between X and Y is collider and parent of Z; if `Y ∉ Sep(X,Z)` then `W o-Y` becomes collider `W <-> Y <-? Z`, else `W -> Y -> Z`. Critical for maximality.
- **R5-R7:** Handling selection undirected edges propagating `o-` vs `-`
- **R8-R10:** Propagating tails from arrowheads: if `X -> Y` and `X o-o Z` then rules produce `Y -> Z` patterns when ancestry forces.

Completeness proof constructs canonical DAG extension: if PAG has `o`, there exist two MAGs in equivalence class differing on that mark [3] Appendix. Hence rules cannot orient further without losing some member. Zhang shows R1-R10 reach that closure — any invariant tail/arrowhead is identified.

| Rule | Antecedent pattern | Consequent | Reasoning class |
|------|-------------------|------------|-----------------|
| R0 | `A o-o C o-o B`, A⊥̸B|C? | `A o-> C <-o B` iff C∉Sep(A,B) | collider detection |
| R1 | `A *-> B o-o C`, A not adj C | `B -> C` | avoid new collider |
| R2 | `A -> B *-> C` , `A *-> C` | `A -> C` | ancestry transitive |
| R3 | two colliders to Y | `X -> Y` | discriminating-like |
| R4 | discriminating path `p` for `Y` | collider / non-collider per Sep | recursive |
| R5-R7 | `… o- Z` selective | `Z -? …` | selection unwind |
| R8-R10 | `X -> Y o-o` chains | `X ->* Z` | tail propagation |

### 4.3 Conservative FCI and Anytime FCI: Stability Under Empirical Unfaithfulness

Standard FCI assumes faithfulness and oracle tests; finite samples cause near-violations: weak dependences produce spurious independencies, flipping v-structure orientation [6]. cFCI / conservative FCI votes: for triple `X - Z - Y`, if independence `X ⊥ Y | S` holds for multiple S, record *list* of sepsets. Classify collider only if Z in *none* of them (non-collider) or *all* (collider); otherwise mark *unfaithful* (ambiguous) and leave `o-o`. Keeps soundness under weaker *adjacency-faithfulness*.

> **Theorem 4.1 (cFCI Soundness under Adjacency-Faithfulness).** *If distribution is faithful to true MAG except possibly orientation-faithfulness violations for triple-wise independencies, cFCI returns a PAG that still contains true MAG in its equivalence class, possibly less informative but not false [6][4].*

**Anytime FCI (AnFCI, dcFCI):** Instead of trusting single PAG, score candidate PAGs by *data-compatibility*: marginal likelihood under underlying semi-parametric model, or BIC-like score of implied independence constraints. dcFCI [6] defines first non-parametric PAG-compatibility score valid for mixed types: likelihood ratio of independencies entailed vs observed. Search ranks PAGs exploring edges pruned earliest via highest uncertainty. Allows top-k PAG list, visualizing structural uncertainty — promising when multiple PAGs equally plausible at n=200.

ICD iterative design [5] ties sepset size to graph distance: start small S near test nodes, expand. Every iteration yields valid MAG; can stop early (anytime) with sound but less complete result; empirically fewer tests than FCI+.

### 4.4 Kernel-Based Nonparametric Conditional Independence: KCI to FastKCI and Signature Extensions

Linear Fisher-Z `√n·z → N(0,1)` assumes Gaussianity. For nonlinear `X = f(Z)+ε`, dependence remains after linear residual. Kernel approach embeds distributions into reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS).

**KCI construction (Zhang et al. 2011) [7]:** Let `k_x, k_y, k_z` characteristic kernels (RBF). Define feature maps φ. Compute kernel matrices Kx, Ky, Kz centered `~K = H K H` with `H = I - 11ᵀ/n`. Regress out Z in RKHS: residual matrices `K_{X|Z} = K_x - K_x K_z^+ K_z` approximations via kernel ridge. Test statistic:

```
T_KCI = (1/n) Tr( K̃_{X|Z} K̃_{Y|Z} )
```

Asymptotically under H0 `X⊥Y|Z`, `T_KCI → ∑ λ_i · χ²_1` mixture, λ_i eigenvalues of product covariance operators. Null approx via Gamma match moment `k = E[T]²/Var(T)`, `θ=Var/T/E`, or Monte Carlo spectral simulation. Computational cost O(n³) kernel inversion/eigen-decomp.

**RCIT / RCoT (Strobl et al. 2019) [8]:** Approximates RKHS via Random Fourier Features (RFF) `φ_RFF(x) = sqrt(2/D)[cos(ω₁ᵀx+b₁),…,sin]`, `ω∼ N(0,σ⁻²)`. Then linear partial correlation in feature space. Complexity `O(n D²)` with `D≈20-100` vs `O(n³)`. RCoT uses extended features including product terms approximating conditional correlation operator. Empirically power retained for n≥500 while 10-100× faster; FCI with RCoT yields graph F1 similar to KCI but <20 sec for 50 nodes.

**FastKCI (2025) [9]:** Mixture-of-experts partitioning: fit Gaussian mixture `GMM(Z) = ∑ π_k N(μ_k, Σ_k)`, cluster samples `k`, run local KCI per cluster, aggregate weighted p-values via importance sampling `p_agg = ∑ w_k p_k`. Parallelizable, sublinear memory. Valid under local Gaussianity of Z within clusters.

**Signature Kernel CI for SDEs** [extension]: For stochastic processes, path signature captures ordered moments; signature kernel `k_sig( X_[0,T], Y_[0,T] ) = ⟨S(X), S(Y)⟩_tensor`. Test preserves time directionality, extends FCI to dynamical systems.

### 4.5 Differentiable and Deconfounded Scores: Bridging Constraint-Based and Score-Based with Latent Covariance

Score-based DAG discovery NOTEARS [10]: minimize least squares `||X - XW||²_F/2n + λ||W||₁` s.t. `h(W)=tr(exp(W∘W))-d=0`. `h(W)=0` iff `W` acyclic (nilpotent trace). Optimized via augmented Lagrangian `L = f(W)+α h + ρ/2 h²`. Fully differentiable, GPU-amenable.

With latent confounding, independent noise fails. True model: `X = Wᵀ X + Λ L + ε`, `L` low-rank latent factors → observed covariance `Σ^{-1}` precision has low-rank + sparse decomposition [12]. ADMG formulation Bhattacharya et al.: variables `W` directed + bidirected covariance `B` (dense symmetric). Optimization over `(W, B)` s.t. `B` positive definite, `W` acyclic, and bow-free: if `W_ij≠0` then `B_ij=0` not both directed and bidirected violating distinguishability [11]. Differentiable algebraic constraint `B∘(I+W∘W) ???` ensures ADMG realizable.

**Deconfounded Score Method (DC) [12]:** Insight: dense confounding leaves low-rank footprint in precision: `Σ^{-1}= (I-W)ᵀ Ω^{-1} (I-W)` where `Ω = ΛΛᵀ + diag`. If `Λ` shared across many O, `Σ^{-1}` = sparse (causal) + low-rank (confound). Estimate via `min_{S,L} -logdet(S-L)+tr((S-L) Σ̂) + λ₁||S||₁ + λ₂ Rank(L)` convex relax `||L||_*` nuclear norm. Disentangles spurious edges. Residual `S` yields DAG via score search thresholded. Robust to dense conf misspecification, scales to p=1000 via ADMM.

Hybrid dcFCI [6] merges both worlds: constraint phases propose skeleton, score prunes PAGs via marginal likelihood that naturally penalizes confounded models.

### 5.1 Setup

Simulation: Erdos-Renyi DAG p=20, e=2p expected, inject latent `|L|=3` each confounding pair of O (≈30% edges bidirected), selection `|S|=1` causing Berkson. Data non-linear `X = sin(WX) + 0.3 N(0,1)`. n=500,1000,2000. Tests: Fisher-Z oracle, KCI (RBF σ median heuristic), RCIT D=25, RCoT D=50. Metrics: PAG *Markov equivalence* `Structural Hamming (SHD)` counting marks (`o` vs `->`), precision/recall for ancestry (tail) and non-ancestry (arrowhead) separately, arrowhead ROC, F1 skeleton, number CI tests.

We reuse community implementations causal-learn (FCI/KCI) and pcalg (RFCI). Hardware single core Xeon 4GHz unless FastKCI parallel 8-core [9].

> **Theorem 5.1 (Soundness Preservation under Anytime Iteration).** *Each intermediate graph G_i produced by ICD after iteration i entailing conditional independencies up to conditioning size i is a supermodel of final PAG: any independence entailed by G_i holds in true MAG, and any ancestral claim in G_i also holds [5].* Proof via superset monotonic removal; orientation rules only fire when antecedent subgraph present in all completions per Zhang Lemma.

> **Theorem 5.2 (KCI Consistency).** *If kernels characteristic (RBF), regularization λ_n→0, nλ_n³→∞, then test asymptotic level α for H0 and almost surely detects H1 dependence under smoothness [7].* Sketch: HSIC extension C_~ asymptotic Wishart, Gamma approx matches first two moments.

> **Theorem 5.3 (RFCI Soundness of Causal Info).** *Even when RFCI skips Possible-D-SEP, any ancestral (tail) and non-ancestral (arrowhead) mark in its output PAG holds in underlying causal DAG with latent+selection [4].* Skeleton may be superset, but orientation rules preserve correctness.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Faithfulness fragility:** Near-violations where `X ⊥ Y | Z` weak due to cancelling paths produce RFCI/FCI instability. cFCI mitigates but leaves `o`. No complete reparametrization-free measure of practical faithfulness distribution gap [6].
- **Selection bias identifiability limits:** PAG with selection marks `X - Y` non-identifiable orientation; many queries not identifiable without structural assumptions on selection function. Local LCD/Y-structure [paper] limited to 4-variable case.
- **Scalability of kernel tests:** Even RCIT O(n D²) suffers conditioning set size exponential; kernel matrix ridge λ sensitivity. Median heuristic σ fails multivariate heavy-tail; learning σ via marginal likelihood promising but costly. FastKCI GMM clustering assumes roughly Gaussian Z clusters.
- **Mixed data:** dcFCI non-parametric PAG score required mixed continuous/discrete kernels; kernel choice among conditional mutual information estimators variance high. Copula vs KNN vs kernel equally contested [survey].
- **Differentiable ADMG optimization:** Non-convex augmented Lagrangian may converge to local minima; bow-free constraints cubic in p; initialization matters; no convergence guarantee to global DAG equivalence class. Dense conf confounding low-rank assumption fails if confounding sparse but widespread.
- **Cyclic extensions:** FCI assumes acyclic underlying causal model (possibly with latent but still DAG). Cycles with latent conf even more intricate — SCC condensation needed; Bayes approach [BayCausal] only emerging.
- **Transportability:** Causal effect estimation from PAG still requires IDA-style intervention calculus enriched with possible D-Sep sets; optimal adjustment sets for latent/selection unknown general case.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented unified thesis for causal discovery when causal sufficiency fails. Ancestral graph models trade single DAG for equivalence class encoded as PAG, rendering latent confounding and selection bias tractable without explicit latent variables [1][2][3]. FCI's two-stage skeleton search plus Zhang's complete orientation rules R0-R10 delivers maximally informative invariant features. Conservative and anytime variants (cFCI, AnFCI, ICD, dcFCI) trade completeness for robustness and computational savings, critical when faithfulness is empirically fragile [4][5][6]. Kernel-based CI tests bridge parametric linearity to non-parametric detection via RKHS embedding, with scalable RFF and MoE approximations making FCI feasible at n=10k+ [7][8][9]. Score-based differentiable perspectives reinterpret latent confounding as low-rank precision noise, enabling NOTEARS-like continuous optimization over ADMGs and deconfounded convex relaxations [11][12]. The synthesis is not antagonistic: constraint-based skeleton proposers, score-based PAG rankers, kernel-powered independence oracles constitute modern pipeline. Together they enable principled discovery of invariant ancestry in presence of latent common causes and selection, with theoretical guarantees of soundness and completeness in oracle limit and calibrated Type-I control finite-sample.

Contributions summarized: taxonomy mapping MAG–PAG semantics, compact rule-table implementing complete calculus, algorithmic sketch with stability enhancements, kernel test comparison table with complexity, differentiable bridging discussion, empirical SHD/P/R accounting with anytime and conservative tradeoffs. We close oracle-practice loop with reusable artifacts: FCI-stable reference in Python, RCIT/RCoT feature approximators, TLA+ termination spec, Rust PAG validator. Future work on time-ordered tiered background knowledge, mixed-type non-parametric PAG scoring, and high-dimensional low-rank confounding offers tangible route toward reliable causal inference from real-world observational studies where latent and selection biases are norm not exception.
