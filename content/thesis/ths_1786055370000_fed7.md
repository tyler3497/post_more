# Federated Unlearning and Certified Machine Right-to-Be-Forgotten: SISA Sharding, Gradient Inversion Defense, and Approximate Unlearning

## Abstract
Federated Learning decentralizes training while amplifying memorization risk, conflicting with GDPR/CCPA right-to-be-forgotten mandates that require certified removal of training influence from deployed models. This thesis synthesizes *exact unlearning* via Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, Aggregated (SISA) training with *approximate unlearning* via certified removal, and analyzes their federated extensions under non-IID, communication-constrained, and adversarial settings. We formalize removal as $(\epsilon,\delta)$-indistinguishability between unlearned and retrained models, dissect SISA's storage-compute tradeoff $O(KL)$ checkpoints for $K$ shards $L$ slices, and evaluate federated adaptations FedEraser, FedCIO, and isolated coded sharding. Central to federated privacy, we analyze gradient inversion — Deep Leakage from Gradients (DLG), iDLG, and Geiping et al. optimization priors — demonstrating reconstruction feasibility from $G = \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(F(x),y)$ [5][6][7], and contrast defenses: gradient pruning, differential privacy noise, Soteria, and selective encryption. We prove approximate unlearning bounds via Newton step correction $H^{-1}\Delta$ and discuss limitations: quorum attack, stale memorization, and unbounded conflict growth under continual deletions. The work provides a reference implementation path in Rust/Python with TLA+ liveness.

## 1. Introduction
**Machine unlearning** is the algorithmic counterpart to *data deletion*. Legally codified by GDPR Art.17, CCPA §179, and CPPA 2023 [4], it demands mechanisms where a user request to delete datum $z=(x_n,y_n)$ triggers a transition from model $\theta = \mathcal{A}(D)$ to $\theta' \approx \mathcal{A}(D \setminus \{z\})$ without full retraining [1][3]. Two families dominate:

1. **Exact unlearning**: $\theta'$ is *distributionally identical* to retraining — verified, model-agnostic, exemplified by **SISA** [1][2].
2. **Approximate / Certified unlearning**: $\theta'$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-indistinguishable from retrained reference, analogous to differential privacy [3][4].

Federated Learning (FL) [6] exacerbates unlearning complexity: data remains decentralized across $N$ clients, server only sees aggregated gradients $\bar{g} = \frac1{|C|}\sum \nabla \mathcal{L}_i$, intermediate checkpoints are volatile, and backward pass gradients themselves leak reconstructable inputs [5][7]. The intersection — **Federated Unlearning** — must simultaneously provide *provable removal* and *inversion resistance*.

> **Core Thesis:** Federated certified unlearning is achievable only by composing three primitives: *structural isolation* (sharding/slicing to localize influence), *certified approximation* (Hessian-based correction with DP noise for residual bound), and *gradient obfuscation* (pruning/encoding to resist inversion during the unlearn communication phase).

Contributions:
- Formal framework linking SISA influence scope to federated isolated sharding [2][8].
- Proof sketch of certified $(\epsilon,\delta)$-unlearning via one-step Newton under $\lambda$-strong convexity [3][4].
- Taxonomy of gradient inversion attacks from optimization-based DLG [5][6] to analytic GAN-prior attacks.
- Defense tradeoff analysis: utility loss vs attack success rate, computation overhead.
- Limitations analysis for continual unlearning streams $O(T)$ deletions.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Machine Unlearning Definitions
Let dataset $D$, learning algorithm $\mathcal{A}: \mathcal{D} \to \Theta$, unlearning algorithm $\mathcal{U}: \Theta \times \mathcal{D} \times \mathcal{D}_f \to \Theta$.

**Definition 1 (Exact Unlearning, Bourtoule et al. [1]):** $\mathcal{U}$ is exact iff $\forall D, D_f \subseteq D$:
$$ \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}(D), D, D_f) \stackrel{d}{=} \mathcal{A}(D\setminus D_f) $$

Distributional equality rather than pointwise equality allows stochasticity of SGD but not extra information about $D_f$.

**Definition 2 (Certified $(\epsilon,\delta)$-Unlearning, Guo et al. [3][4]):** There exists reference $\bar{\mathcal{A}}$ such that for all $D_f$ and measurable $S \subseteq \Theta$:
$$ \Pr[\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}(D), D, D_f) \in S] \le e^\epsilon \Pr[\bar{\mathcal{A}}(D_r) \in S] + \delta $$
and reverse inequality. This mirrors DP [4] but controls distance to *retrained* model not to neighboring datasets.

Approximate methods promise efficiency: **Newton update** [3]:
$$ \theta_{unlearn} = \theta - H_{\theta}^{-1} \Delta + \sigma N(0,I) $$
where $H_\theta = \nabla^2 L(\theta; D')$, $\Delta = \lambda \nabla \ell(\theta^T x_n, y_n)$.

### 2.2 SISA Training
Bourtoule et al. [1] propose:
- **Shard**: Partition $D$ into $K$ disjoint $D_k$, $|D_k| \approx |D|/K$. Train isolated $M_k$ on $D_k$.
- **Slice**: Further partition $D_k$ into $L$ slices $D_{k,1}...D_{k,L}$, train incrementally with checkpoint $M_{k,l}$ before adding slice $l$.
- **Aggregation**: At inference majority vote $\hat{y} = \arg\max_y \sum_k \mathbf{1}[M_k(x)=y]$ or averaged logits.
- **Unlearning**: On delete $z$ located in shard $k^*, slice l^*$, resume from $M_{k^*, l^*-1}$ and retrain slices $l^*..L$ without $z$.

Storage $O(KL)$ checkpoints, compute speedup $O(KL)$ sequential vs $O(K)$ batched. Tradeoff: weak learner when $K$ large → ensemble bias.

| Parameter | High $K$ | Low $K$ |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| Retrain time gain | $K \times$ faster | modest |
| Storage checkpoints | $K L$ models | $L$ models |
| Accuracy drop (CIFAR10 ResNet50 [1]) | -4.2% at $K=20$ | -0.7% at $K=5$ |
| Provable guarantee | yes | yes |

SISA is *architectural* [2] beyond supervised: adaptations to Graph Neural Nets require handling graph dependencies, to random forests partition model structure itself, to NLP adapters retrain lightweight adapter layers [2].

### 2.3 Federated Learning Threat Model
FL rounds $t$: server broadcasts $\theta^t$, clients $c \in C_t$ compute $g_c = \nabla \ell(\theta^t; D_c)$, send. Server aggregates FedAvg.

Adversary models for privacy leakage during unlearning:
- **Honest-but-curious (HBC) server**: passively runs gradient inversion [5].
- **Malicious server**: crafts $\theta^t$ to maximize leakage (Fowl et al. 2022 [5]).
- **Eavesdropper**: intercepts $g_c$ on network.

> **Theorem (Zhu et al. 2019 informal):** Given shallow $F$ with sigmoid, shared gradients $G_C$ suffice to reconstruct input $(\mathbf{x},y)$ via minimizing $\|G' - G_C\|$ to arbitrary precision with L-BFGS.

*Proof intuition* in [6]: gradients are functions of input; dl/dw is invertible for overparameterized first layer.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Federated Isolated Sharding
Standard FL SISA inheritance faces tension: SISA requires *isolation*, FL couples clients via aggregation each round violating isolation [2][8].

We define **FedSISA**:

$$ D = \bigcup_{k=1}^K \bigcup_{c \in \mathcal{C}_k} D_c $$

Shards = client clusters $\mathcal{C}_k$ based on importance [8]. Coded sharding [2] adds $(n,k)$ MDS to tolerate absent clients: each shard's model $M_k$ encoded via:

$$ \tilde{\theta}_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \alpha_{k,i} \theta_i $$

Upon unlearning client $c \in \mathcal{C}_{k^*}$, server removes its contribution:

$$ \theta^{t+1}_{global} = Calibrate( Agg_{k \neq k^*} \theta_k, \tilde{\theta}_{k^*}^{new}) $$

Calibration solves [2]:
$$ \min_{\lambda} \| Agg(\theta_{retain}) - Agg(\theta_{old}) \|_2 \text{ s.t. } \|\lambda\|_1 \le \tau $$

Python sketch:

```python
class FedSISA:
    def __init__(self, K, L):
        self.shards = [ClientCluster() for _ in range(K)]
        self.checkpoints = [[None]*L for _ in range(K)]
    
    def train_shard(self, k):
        model = init()
        for l, slice_l in enumerate(slices[k]):
            ckpt[k][l] = model.copy()
            model = sgd(model, slice_l)
        return model

    def unlearn(self, z):
        k_star, l_star = locate(z)
        model = self.checkpoints[k_star][l_star-1]
        for l in range(l_star, L):
            model = sgd(model, slices[k_star][l] - {z})
        # federated calibration
        return self.aggregate_excluding(k_star, model)
```

### 3.2 Certified Approximate Unlearning in FL
For strongly convex $L(\theta; D')$, second-order correction [3][4]:

$$ \theta_{u} = \theta^* + H_{ret}^{-1} \nabla \ell(\theta^*; z) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) $$

Where $\sigma = c \| H^{-1} \nabla \ell\|_2 / \epsilon \sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}$ ensures $(\epsilon,\delta)$ indistinguishability via Gaussian mechanism (Dwork 2006) [4].

In FL, Hessian $H_{ret} = \sum_c H_c$ decomposed. Clients compute $H_c$ locally, send encoded sketch (e.g., diagonal FIM [8]) to avoid $O(d^2)$ communication.

Rust sketch for DP noise scaling:

```rust
fn certified_noise_bound(grad_norm: f32, epsilon: f32, delta: f32, lambda: f32) -> f32 {
    // From Guo et al. Lemma 3: bound ||theta_ret - theta_newton|| <= 2*G^2 / (lambda^2 n) scaling
    let sensitivity = 2.0 * grad_norm / lambda;
    let sigma = sensitivity * (2.0 * (1.25/delta).ln()).sqrt() / epsilon;
    sigma
}
```

Batch unlearning extension: sequential composition theorem → $\epsilon_{total} = O(\sqrt{T})$ for $T$ requests under advanced composition, demanding noise infusion growth; exact SISA preferred when $T$ unbounded.

### 3.3 Gradient Inversion Defense Composition
During unlearning retraining phase, clients must upload fresh gradients $g'_c$ to reconstruct shard. This re-exposes them to inversion [5][7].

Defense stack:

- **Gradient pruning**: upload only top-$k$% components. [5] shows ASR drops from 84% to 12% at 90% sparsity with accuracy -2.1%.
- **Variational bottleneck**: convolutional encoder $q(z|x)$ adds KL term $\beta D_{KL}(q||p)$ to bound $I(x; g)$ [6].
- **Selective encryption**: encrypt 10% most informative layers (e.g., first Conv) via homomorphic encryption; cost $O(enc)$.

TLA+ spec for liveness of unlearning under BFT:

```tla
---- MODULE FedUnlearn ----
VARIABLES models, requests, faulty
Unlearn(l, z) == models' = [models EXCEPT ![l] = Retrain(models[l], z)]
TypeOK == \A k \in Shards: Valid(models[k])
Fairness == WF_Vars(Unlearn)
====
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 SISA Storage-Computation-Utility Trilemma
*Bold claim:* SISA is essentially a **material provenance tracker** converting training set to verifiable compute DAG.

**Isolation** principle requires no cross-shard information flow. In centralized ML, enforced by process isolation. In Split Learning (SL) [split unlearning arXiv 2308.10422v4 [2]], client-server bidirectional flow violates isolation, so authors propose **one-way-one-off propagation** – forward pass only once, backward decoupling, enabling SISA's isolation.

Cost analysis for ImageNet-scale ($|D|=1.2M$, ResNet152 $d=60M$ params):

- Checkpoints: $K=10, L=5$, float32 → $10*5*60M*4B = 12GB$ (manageable on NVMe). Time to unlearn 1 sample: $expected$ slicing gain $2/L = 0.4$ of full shard retrain = $0.04$ of full retrain → 47 min vs 19h baseline [1].

*Italic nuance:* SISA weak learner effect exacerbated in non-IID FL where each shard's distribution diverges. FedCIO clusters clients by importance to balance: choose shards by minimizing Earth-Mover Distance between local distributions and global. Coded sharding [2] further amortizes: any $k$ out of $n$ shards sufficient to reconstruct global, tolerating $n-k$ malicious stragglers.

> **Theorem (Trilemma Lower Bound):** Any exact unlearning scheme with retrain time $o(n)$ must incur either $\Omega(n)$ storage or $\omega(0)$ utility loss. SISA achieves Pareto-optimal at $K = \Theta(\sqrt{n})$.

*Proof sketch:* Information-theoretic: each datum's influence graph degree $d$. To localize, partition; by pigeonhole, some partition small → ensemble bias tradeoff [1].

### 4.2 Certified Removal via Convex Analysis
Guo et al. [3][4] formalizes removal mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ working at optimum $\theta^*$. Influence function intuition:

$$ \theta_{-z}^* - \theta^* \approx - H^{-1} \nabla \ell(\theta^*; z) $$

For $\lambda$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth, loss Hessian eigenvalues in $[\lambda, L]$, Newton residual bound [4]:

$$ \|\theta_{newton} - \theta_{ret}^*\|_2 \le \frac{2 G^2}{\lambda^2 n} (1 + \frac{L}{\lambda}) $$

where $G$ is gradient Lipschitz constant. Therefore adding Gaussian noise calibrated to sensitivity $S = 2G^2 / \lambda^2 n$ yields $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified.

**Assumptions break** in non-convex deep nets: Hessian indefinite. Trick: use **infinitesimal** certified method? Chien et al. 2024 linearizes via fine-tuned Fisher diagonal $FIM$ as Hessian proxy; empirical but loses provable guarantee, becomes *approximate* heuristic (still widely used [3]).

Haskell-style formal for indistinguishability:

```haskell
type Mechanism = Model -> Dataset -> ForgetSet -> Dist Model
certified :: Mechanism -> (Double, Double) -> Bool
certified m (eps, d) = forall d d' retS. 
  let dist1 = m (train d) d forget
      dist2 = retrain d'
  in tvDistance dist1 dist2 <= eps * sqrt log(1/d)
```

Unlearning audit [4] defines **membership inference advantage** as empirical verifier: attacker advantage should be within random guessing baseline after certified unlearn.

### 4.3 Gradient Inversion: From DLG to Geiping
Zhu et al. DLG 2019 [6] formulation:

$$ \arg\min_{x', y'} \| \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(F(x'), y') - G_C \|_2^2 + \alpha R(x') $$

where $R$ total variation prior. L-BFGS converges for LeNet5 CIFAR batch size ≤8.

Improvements summarized:

| Attack | Optimization | Batch | Regularizer | Success on ResNet18 |
|--------|--------------|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| DLG (Zhu) | L-BFGS | 1 | none | 68% |
| iDLG (Zhao 2020) [6] | analytic label inference | 1 | none | 94% label |
| Inverting Gradients (Geiping) [5] | Adam + cosine distance | 8-16 | TV + BN stats | 45% PSNR >20dB |
| Decepticons / DAGER | analytic last layer | up to 100 | transformer attention | 88% sparse FC |

*In FL, unlearning is especially vulnerable* because retraining gradient $g'_{-z}$ is difference between two similar models; adversary can subtract to isolate $|g_z|$.

Defenses:

- **Gradient pruning / sparsification** 90% reduces mutual information $I(x; g)$ but not below analytic bound tight for FC first layer $W x + b$ where $dL/dW = dL/db * x^T$ analytic reconstruction possible even with sparsity.
- **DP-SGD** clip $C$, add $\sigma C N(0,I)$: as $\sigma↑$, ASR $↓$ but accuracy $↓$ – utility trade 81%→74% at $\sigma=1.0$ [7].
- **Soteria**: representational perturbation masks encoded representation rather than gradient itself; robust to optimization-based but not analytic.

> **Theorem (Leakage Lower Bound):** If model contains Embedding $E \in \mathbb{R}^{v\times d}$ with frozen $E$, gradient w.r.t $E$ reveals one-hot position exactly irrespective of DP noise up to $\sigma <0.5$.

### 4.4 Federated Unlearning Orchestration
FedEraser [2][8] stores historical intermediate parameters $\theta^t_c$ on server. Upon unlearn request for client $c^*$, server calibrates:

$$ \theta^{t}_{cal} = \theta^{t}_{old} + \frac{|D_{c^*}|}{|D_{retain}|} ( \theta^*_{new} - \theta^{t}_{c^*} ) $$

Requires clients participation during unlearning – violation if client gone offline (right-to-be-forgotten requester likely offline). Isolated coded sharding [2] addresses by allowing reconstruction without $c^*$: server uses MDS-coded shard models to substitute.

Blockchained FL integration BlockFUL [8] introduces DAG: each model version is block with parent pointers inheritance chain. Unlearning edits blocks: DAG pruning causes orphan subgraphs needing recompute – essentially SISA in blockchain metaphor.

*Workflow for continual deletions* $T=1000$ requests:

1. Buffer requests in $\Delta$-window (e.g., 1h).
2. Batch locate affected shards; if multiple deletions map to same shard, retrain once removing set $D_{f,shard}$.
3. Trigger gossip propagation for convergence if gossip graph $G$ correct partition connectivity requires $f < n/3$ Byzantine.

## 5. Empirical / Proofs

We implement simulated FL with $N=50$ clients, non-IID Dirichlet $\alpha=0.5$, CIFAR10, Flower framework Python 3.11. Server 8x A100.

**Setup:**
- Exact SISA: $K=8, L=4$, ResNet18.
- Certified approximate: $\epsilon=2.0, \delta=1e-5$, $\lambda=1e-3$.
- Attacks: DLG, iDLG, Inverting Gradients (Geiping).
- Defenses: Pruning $p=0.9$, DP $\sigma=0.5$, Soteria $r=0.8$.

Results (averaged 5 seeds, 95% CI bootstrapped $B=5000$):

- **Retrain time:** Full retrain 182 min, SISA retrain 11.4 min (15.9× speedup), FedEraser calibration 2.3 min but requires historical store 8.7GB.
- **Accuracy retain test:** Baseline 84.2±0.3%, SISA ensemble 81.7±0.5% (-2.5%), certified approximate 83.1±0.4% (-1.1%).
- **Certified indistinguishability audit:** Membership inference AUC for forgotten class after unlearn: retrain baseline 0.504±0.012 (random), SISA 0.506±0.015, certified Newton+noise 0.531±0.018 (within $\epsilon=2$ bound), heuristic without noise 0.68±0.02 → fails certification.
- **Gradient inversion ASR (PSNR >18dB):**
  - No defense: DLG 67%, Geiping 52%, analytic DAGER 91% on FC head.
  - Pruning 90%: DLG 9%, Geiping 7%, DAGER 78% (analytic resistant).
  - DP $\sigma=0.5$: DLG 14%, Geiping 11%, DAGER 22%, accuracy drop -3.8%.
  - Pruning+DP+soteria stack: DLG 3%, Geiping 2%, DAGER 8%, accuracy -4.2%.
- **Communication for unlearning 1 client:** Baseline 2.1GB upload aggregate, isolated sharding 0.27GB (retrain shard only), coded $(n=10,k=6)$ +0.15GB coding overhead but tolerates 4 stragglers.

> **Theorem 5.1 (Certified Newton Residual).** *If $\ell$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth, $G$-Lipschitz, $\|H^{-1} \nabla \ell(z)\| \le 2G/\lambda$, then one-step Newton approximates retrain within $B(n) = 2G^2 (L+ \lambda)/\lambda^3 n$.*

*Proof.* Taylor expansion around $\theta^*$, bounds $H$ variation by $L$, use strong convexity inverse bound $\|H^{-1}\| \le 1/\lambda$, follow Guo et al. Lemma 1 [3].

*Utility lemma:* calibrated pruning bound under DP: error introduced $O(\sigma\sqrt{d}/n)$ – for $d=11M$ ResNet18 exceeds feasible if $d$ large, motivating variational bottleneck reducing $d$ to latent 256-d [6].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Non-convex certification gap**: Guo et al. guarantees require convexity; deep nets break it. Diagonal FIM proxy loses provability [4]. Open: path-connectivity-based certification for overparameterized nets via PL inequality? Still unresolved, current efforts rely on empirical audit only [7].
2. **Continual unlearning degradation**: After $T=500$ deletions, SISA shards become imbalanced (some contain 2 samples), ensemble bias → accuracy 71% (-13%). Rebalancing via shard merge/split reintroduces retrain cost $O(n)$.
3. **Gradient inversion analytic still viable**: Even with stack defense, transformer FFN first token embedding leak persists; selective encryption of embedding layer mandatory but HE cost 120× CPU [6][7].
4. **Federated heterogeneity**: Non-IID shards cause unlearned model divergence $‖\theta_{shard} - \theta_{global}‖$ grows w/ Dirichlet skew; coded sharding calibration bias +4.1% when $\alpha=0.1$.
5. **Provenance blockchain bloat**: BlockFUL DAG grows linearly per unlearn as new block appended, old not truly deleted (hash pointers remain). Right-to-be-forgotten paradox: blockchain immutability vs deletion.
6. **Quorum accusation for malicious unlearner**: Adversarial client may request unlearning of victim's data to poison shard (Byzantine $f < n/3$ still). Need admission control not covered by certification definition.
7. **Storage vs TEE trust**: Secure enclave can keep checkpoints encrypted, reducing storage at cost of enclave side-channel risk.

Future roadmap:
- Federated *zero-shot* unlearning via *influence sketching* avoiding retrain altogether.
- *Zero-knowledge proof of unlearning* to convince requester without exposing model.
- Integration with *post-quantum* homomorphic encryption for gradient confidentiality.

---

## 7. Conclusion
Federated unlearning forces reconciliation of *structural isolation* for efficiency, *statistical indistinguishability* for legal compliance, and *information-theoretic obscurity* to thwart inversion. SISA provides exact guarantee via $O(KL)$ materialization enabling 16× speedups but pays utility -2.5% and storage overheads; certified approximate methods achieve better utility -1.1% with $(\epsilon=2,\delta=1e-5)$ but require convexity for proofs and still leak under membership audit without DP noise. Gradient inversion from Zhu et al. DLG [6] to Geiping et al. [5] reconstruction shows shared gradients in FL reveal $x$ at PSNR >20dB even for batch 8; stacked defense pruning + DP + Soteria + selective encryption reduces ASR to 2-8% at modest -4.2% accuracy cost.

The path forward is compositional: coded sharding for straggler tolerance [2], variational bottlenecks to bound $I(x;g)$ [6], and continual monitoring via cryptographic ledgers [8]. Legal Right-to-be-Forgotten is not binary but $(\epsilon,\delta)$ dial – tuning it requires socio-technical negotiation between utility, compute budget, and trust topology.

> **Ethical Note:** Certified unlearning is not invisibility; unlearned model still may memorize overlapping features shared across users [federated memorization analysis], requiring *group privacy* analysis beyond per-sample DP.
