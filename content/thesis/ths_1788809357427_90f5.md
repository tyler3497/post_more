# Learned Video Compression with Neural Codecs: Conditional Coding, Scale-Space Flow, and the Road to Real-Time 4K

## Abstract

Neural video codecs replace the handcrafted modules of hybrid coders with end-to-end trained networks optimized directly for the rate–distortion Lagrangian. This thesis traces the field from the DVC motion-compensated residual baseline, through the scale-space flow warping of Agustsson et al. — which generalized optical flow with an uncertainty-aware scale parameter — to the conditional-coding paradigm of DCVC, which proved residue coding suboptimal and moved the condition into a learned, high-dimensional feature space. We analyze hyperprior and temporal-adaptive entropy models, the *R + λD* training machinery with uniform-noise quantization surrogates, and the engineering turn toward real-time decoding — exemplified by DCVC-RT at 125/113 fps 1080p with 21% bitrate savings over H.266/VTM, and the push for NPU/DSP 4K throughput. We contrast the transformer-based VCT, which drops warping entirely, and the voxel-flow VLVC, the first learned codec to surpass VVC on MS-SSIM. Finally we examine video coding for machines (VCM), where compression optimizes downstream task accuracy rather than pixel fidelity, and map the open problems between today's codecs and production deployment.

---

## 1. Introduction

Video occupies well over 80% of consumer network traffic, and demand for higher resolutions and frame rates keeps outpacing the efficiency gains of traditional standards [1]. From H.261 (1988) through H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, and the 2020-released H.266/VVC, every standardized codec has followed the same hybrid predictive-coding architecture: block-based motion compensation produces a predicted frame, and the residue is transform-coded, quantized, and entropy-coded [1][5]. Four decades of engineering have squeezed this paradigm close to its practical limits — VVC's gains over HEVC cost roughly an order-of-magnitude more encoder complexity.

Learned video compression proposes a different route. Rather than refining handcrafted modules, *every* component is parameterized as a neural network and trained jointly to minimize the operational rate–distortion objective:

> **Theorem (Rate–distortion training objective):** A neural codec is trained to minimize *L = R + λD*, where *R = E[−log₂ P(ŷ)]* is the expected cross-entropy of the quantized latent *ŷ* under the learned entropy model *P* (the operational bitrate under entropy coding), *D = d(x, x̂)* is the reconstruction distortion (typically MSE or MS-SSIM), and the Lagrange multiplier *λ* selects a point on the achievable rate–distortion curve [4].

Early neural codecs simply re-implemented the classical pipeline with learned parts: Lu et al.'s DVC (2019) replaced each hybrid module with a neural network while keeping the residue-coding structure intact [4]. This proved the concept but exposed a deeper theoretical limitation: as Li, Li, and Lu observed, residue coding is only a special, extremely simplified case of *conditional* coding, and a strictly suboptimal one [1]:

- **Predictive (residue) coding:** *x̂_t = f_dec(⌊f_enc(x_t − x̃_t)⌋) + x̃_t*, where the prediction *x̃_t = f_predict(x̂_{t−1})* is removed by a fixed subtraction.
- **Conditional coding:** *x̂_t = f_dec(⌊f_enc(x_t | c_t)⌋ | c_t)*, where the condition *c_t* is a learned high-dimensional context rather than a pixel-domain prediction.

Information theory is unambiguous: *H(x_t − x̃_t) ≥ H(x_t | x̃_t)* [1]. Subtraction destroys information the encoder could have used; conditioning is the general principle — and the history of learned video compression since 2020 is largely the story of that inequality turned into architectures.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the foundations: nonlinear transform coding, the hyperprior, and the motion-compensation lineage. Section 3 formalizes the methodology of a modern neural codec, from Lagrangian training to differentiable quantization. Section 4 dives deep into four pillars — DCVC's conditional coding, scale-space flow warping, entropy models, and real-time deployment. Section 5 presents empirical results and formal analysis. Section 6 catalogues limitations and open problems, including video coding for machines. Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From transform coding to learned nonlinear transforms

Classical image coding is *linear* transform coding: the DCT or wavelets decorrelate pixels, and coefficients are quantized and entropy-coded. Ballé et al. (2017) recast this as a variational autoencoder, where analysis transform *g_a*, synthesis transform *g_s*, and the entropy model are learned jointly [4]. The nonlinear *y = g_a(x)* generalizes the DCT the way a deep network generalizes a linear filter bank, and the *generalized divisive normalization* (GDN) activation — a learned local gain control — proved decisive for matching image statistics [4].

### 2.2 The hyperprior entropy model

A fully factorized prior over the latent *ŷ* leaves statistical dependencies on the table: latent coefficients of natural images are spatially correlated, so *H(ŷ)* overestimates the true achievable rate. Ballé et al. (2018) introduced the *hyperprior*: an auxiliary autoencoder *z = h_a(y)* produces side information *ẑ* that parameterizes each coefficient's distribution, typically a Gaussian *N(μ_i(ẑ), σ_i(ẑ)²)* convolved with a unit uniform matching the quantization bin [4][7]. The bitstream carries both *ẑ* and *ŷ* at total rate *−log₂ P(ẑ) − log₂ P(ŷ | ẑ)*. Extensions added autoregressive context (Minnen et al. 2018) — more accurate but serial and slow — and Gaussian mixture likelihoods [7]. The hyperprior remains the workhorse entropy model of virtually all neural video codecs.

### 2.3 The motion-compensation lineage

Learned video codecs inherit three motion ideas from the classical world and then generalize them:

1. **DVC (Lu et al., 2019):** all classical modules replaced by networks; optical flow estimated by a pretrained FlowNet, bilinear warping, residual autoencoder. Required elaborate multi-stage training [1][4].
2. **Scale-space flow (Agustsson et al., CVPR 2020):** generalizes optical flow with a per-pixel *scale* parameter; the reference frame is blurred into a Gaussian scale-space volume and warping becomes trilinear interpolation across space *and* scale, gracefully handling disocclusion and fast motion without any pretrained flow network [2][4].
3. **FVC / feature-space compensation:** motion is estimated in pixel space but compensation happens on features, anticipating DCVC's move of the whole pipeline into the feature domain [1][3].

### 2.4 Evaluation practice

The community converged on a small set of benchmarks and metrics that this thesis uses throughout:

| Dataset | Content | Resolution | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UVG** | 7 diverse sequences | 1080p/4K | Primary RD benchmark |
| **MCL-JCV** | 30 sequences | 1080p | Generalization check |
| **Vimeo-90K** | 89k clips | 448×256 | Training corpus |
| **HEVC CTC** | Classes B–E | 1080p–416p | Standards comparison |

Metrics are **PSNR** (fidelity), **MS-SSIM** (perceptual structure), and **BD-rate** — the average bitrate difference between two RD curves at equal quality, the standard yardstick for codec superiority [1][5].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Problem formulation

Lossy video compression seeks a compact description of a frame sequence *x_{1:T}* minimizing description length *R* and information loss *D* jointly. In the low-latency setting (no future frames), frames are encoded sequentially: given *x_t* and previous reconstructions *x̂_{<t}* (in practice just *x̂_{t−1}*), the encoder produces *z̄_t = f(x_t, x̂_{t−1})*, quantizes to *⌊z̄_t⌉*, and entropy-codes it under the model prior *P*; the decoder reconstructs *x̂_t = g(x̂_{t−1}, ⌊z̄_t⌉)* [4].

This sequential structure has a clean variational interpretation: the encoder is a variational posterior *q(z_t | x_t, z_{<t})*, the decoder a Gaussian likelihood, and the rate–distortion objective is the negative ELBO of a hierarchical latent-variable model [4]. Yang et al. made this explicit, showing that motion-compensated codecs like SSF are instances of a *stochastic temporal autoregressive transform* [4] — a viewpoint that prescribes concrete improvements, such as their structured prior encoding residual information *conditioned on* motion information [4].

### 3.2 Differentiable quantization

Quantization *⌊·⌉* has zero gradient almost everywhere, so training substitutes a continuous relaxation. The standard surrogate, due to Ballé et al., replaces rounding with additive uniform noise during training:

```python
# Differentiable quantization surrogate (Balle et al.): uniform noise
# in [-0.5, 0.5] stands in for rounding during training
def quantize_surrogate(y, training=True):
    if training:
        return y + torch.rand_like(y) - 0.5
    return torch.round(y)

# Rate-distortion Lagrangian used in training: L = R + lam*D
def rd_loss(x, x_hat, log_prob_sum, lam):
    distortion = F.mse_loss(x_hat, x)            # D
    rate = -log_prob_sum / math.log(2)           # R in bits
    return rate + lam * distortion
```

At inference, rounding plus arithmetic coding under *P* lands within a small gap of the cross-entropy — exactly the model mismatch *D_KL(Q ‖ P)* [3].

### 3.3 Variable-rate control

A single *λ* yields a single RD point; practical codecs need a rate ladder. Classical approaches train one model per *λ*; modern ones use *conditional* networks — e.g., scaling latents by a learned gain vector indexed by a quality parameter, or DCVC-RT's module-bank rate control that swaps quantization modules for fine-grained bitrate steps [6]. The Lagrangian multiplier becomes a runtime knob rather than a training constant.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 DCVC: the conditional-coding paradigm shift

Li, Li, and Lu's DCVC (NeurIPS 2021) asked: *what is the condition, how is it used, and how is it learned?* [1] Earlier attempts were partial — conditioning only the entropy model or encoder, or needing handcrafted skip/non-skip classification [1]. DCVC made conditioning total and learned:

1. **Definition.** The condition is *contextual features x̄_t* in the feature domain with arbitrary channel dimension, generated from the previous decoded frame *x̂_{t−1}* — not a 3-channel RGB prediction [1]. High-dimensional context carries far richer information than a pixel-domain prediction.
2. **Usage.** The context feeds the contextual encoder, the contextual decoder, *and* the entropy model, making the entropy model *temporally adaptive* — richer and more accurate than any static image prior [1].
3. **Learning.** Context is learned via *feature-domain* motion estimation and compensation: optical flow warps the *features* of *x̂_{t−1}*, and a refinement network cleans the warped features [1]. Explicit MEMC guides learning, making training far easier than latent-state alternatives.

The payoff was decisive: **26.0% bitrate saving over x265 (veryslow)** and **16.4% over the previous learned SOTA DVCPro** on 1080p test video [1]. DCVC established conditional coding as the new default architecture, and its descendants — DCVC-TCM, DCVC-HEM, DCVC-DC, DCVC-RT — form the dominant codec family today [6][7].

> **Theorem (Conditional coding dominance):** For any predictor *x̃_t*, *H(x_t − x̃_t) ≥ H(x_t | x̃_t)*, with equality only when the residue is a sufficient statistic of the conditional distribution. Residue coding is therefore never better than conditional coding, and strictly worse whenever the predictor discards information the encoder could have used [1].

### 4.2 Scale-space flow: warping under uncertainty

Bilinear warping with optical flow fails exactly where compression needs it most: disocclusions, motion blur, and fast motion, where no single reference pixel is correct [2]. Agustsson et al.'s scale-space flow (SSF) augments the flow field *F_t(x, y)* with a per-pixel *scale* field *s(x, y)*. The reference frame is convolved into a scale-space volume *V(x, y, s)* — progressively Gaussian-blurred versions — and the predicted pixel comes from *trilinear* interpolation in *(x, y, s)* [2]. Where the network is uncertain, it predicts large *s* and samples a blurred average; where motion is precise, it samples the sharp level. The whole model trains end-to-end from scratch — no pretrained FlowNet, no multi-stage schedules [2].

SSF became a building block across the field: VCT's authors cite it as the canonical flow predictor, and efficient Gaussian-pyramid implementations keep its cost manageable [3][4]. Conceptually, SSF is the motion-compensation analogue of the hyperprior — both replace a point estimate with a *distribution-aware* mechanism.

### 4.3 Entropy models: from hyperpriors to temporal adaptation

The entropy model is where bits are actually saved, and its evolution tracks the field's progress:

- **Factorized prior** (Ballé 2017): *P(ŷ) = ∏_i P(ŷ_i)* — simple, parallel, but blind to dependence.
- **Hyperprior** (Ballé 2018): side information *ẑ* adapts a Gaussian-mixture prior per coefficient; the workhorse of neural codecs [4][7].
- **Autoregressive context** (Minnen 2018): conditions on decoded neighbors — better rate, but serial decoding that kills throughput [7].
- **Temporal-conditional priors** (DCVC family): the context *x̄_t* conditions the entropy model, making it temporally adaptive without autoregression's serial bottleneck [1].
- **Transformer temporal models** (VCT): a masked block-autoregressive transformer predicts *P(y_i | y_{i−2}, y_{i−1})* — expressive, parallelizable over blocks, free of warping priors [3].
- **Structured motion–residual priors** (Yang et al.): residual latents conditioned on motion latents exploit their spatial correlation [4].

The design tension is always *expressiveness versus decodability*: every dependency the entropy model captures must be reproducible at the decoder, in order — which is why autoregressive gains come with latency costs real-time codecs must shed [3][6].

### 4.4 The real-time turn: DCVC-RT and the road to 4K NPUs

For years neural codecs were research curiosities: excellent BD-rate, hopeless speed. DCVC-RT (2024–2025, USTC/Microsoft Research Asia) attacked the problem by observing that coding speed is dominated not by MACs but by *operational* costs — memory I/O and function-call overhead [6]. Its design choices follow from that insight:

1. **Implicit temporal modeling** replaces explicit motion estimation/compensation with a lightweight feature extractor and context propagation — no flow network, no warping.
2. **Single-scale latents** at fixed 1/8 resolution replace downsampling pyramids, slashing memory traffic.
3. **Model integerization** converts inference to 16-bit integer arithmetic (scaling factors *K₁ = 512*, *K₂ = 8192*), giving bit-exact, deterministic cross-platform decoding — a prerequisite for any shipped codec.
4. **Module-bank rate control** provides fine-grained bitrate steps without retraining.

The result: **125.2 fps encode / 112.8 fps decode at 1080p** with **21% average bitrate saving over H.266/VTM** [6]. Follow-up work (DCVC-UF) pushed a chunk-based framework — encoding multiple frames into one compact latent with parallel decoders — toward ultra-fast throughput [7]. The next frontier is 4K real-time on mobile NPUs and DSPs: integer-only attention-free architectures, tiled decoding for SRAM budgets, and hardware-aware architecture search over the RD-complexity surface.

### 4.5 Beyond the mainstream: VCT and VLVC

Two contemporaries illuminate the design space's edges. **VCT** (Mentzer et al., NeurIPS 2022) asked what happens if you *delete* motion prediction and warping entirely: each frame is mapped independently to a quantized representation *y_i*, and a transformer predicts *P(y_i | y_{i−2}, y_{i−1})* for entropy coding [3]. It outperformed warping-based methods — including on synthetic panning/blur/fade sequences designed to favor handcrafted biases — proving that learned temporal modeling can subsume motion compensation [3]. Its weakness is decoder complexity: transformer entropy models are heavy.

**VLVC** (Feng et al., 2022) went the opposite direction — *more* general warping. Multiple 3D *voxel flows* describe spatial displacement plus temporal reference position, enabling one model to serve unidirectional, bidirectional, single- and multi-reference prediction modes via weighted trilinear warping, with polynomial motion-trajectory prediction cutting flow bitrate [5]. VLVC was the **first end-to-end learned codec to outperform VVC/H.266 reference software in MS-SSIM**, in both low-delay and random-access configurations [5].

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Rate–distortion standing of the field

Representative BD-rate results (negative = bitrate saving vs. anchor; PSNR unless noted) compiled from the cited works:

| Codec | Anchor | BD-rate (PSNR) | BD-rate (MS-SSIM) | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DCVC [1] | x265 veryslow | **−26.0%** | — | Conditional coding |
| DCVC [1] | DVCPro | −16.4% | — | Feature-domain context |
| DCVC-RT [6] | VTM (H.266) | **−21%** | — | Implicit temporal, INT16 |
| VLVC [5] | VTM (H.266) | ≈ 0% | **beats VVC** | Voxel-flow warping |
| SSF [2] | DVC-class baselines | −(10–20%) | — | Scale-space warping |
| VCT [3] | prior learned SOTA | SOTA at time | — | Transformer entropy |

Two facts stand out. First, the *slope* of progress: learned codecs went from "barely beats x264" to beating VTM within roughly five years [6]. Second, MS-SSIM leadership arrived before PSNR leadership — neural codecs are perceptually strong, consistent with training objectives that increasingly incorporate perceptual losses [5].

### 5.2 Formal analysis: where do the bits go?

For a conditional codec, the operational rate decomposes as

*R = E[−log₂ P(ẑ)] + E[−log₂ P(m̂ | ẑ)] + E[−log₂ P(ŷ | ẑ, x̄_t)]*

for hyperprior, motion, and content latents respectively [1][4]. Three formal observations matter:

1. **Conditioning can only help.** *H(ŷ | ẑ, x̄_t) ≤ H(ŷ | ẑ)* — any informative context reduces the content rate; in DCVC the motion-bit cost is repaid several times over [1].
2. **The cross-entropy gap is the optimization target.** With entropy coding, actual bitrate equals cross-entropy *H(Q, P) = H(Q) + D_KL(Q ‖ P)*; minimizing *R* during training is exactly minimizing the model's mismatch to the true latent distribution [3]. Every entropy-model innovation is a tighter *P*.
3. **Lagrangian geometry.** Varying *λ* traces the lower convex hull of achievable *(R, D)* points. Single-model variable-rate methods (gain modulation, module banks) approximate this hull with one parameter set; the gap versus per-*λ* models is typically 1–3% BD-rate — the price of practicality [6].

| Codec | 1080p enc / dec fps | Hardware | Notes |

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**1. The generalization gap.** Models trained on Vimeo-90K (448×256, short clips) are evaluated on UVG 1080p/4K. Resolution and content shifts — screen content, gaming, HDR — degrade RD performance in ways classical codecs, with their content-agnostic tools, do not suffer [4][5]. Domain-robust training remains unsolved.

**2. Error propagation and random access.** Low-latency codecs condition on *reconstructions*, so quantization errors compound along the prediction chain; periodic intra refresh costs bits. VCT's independent-frame design eliminates propagation by construction [3], but at a complexity price. True random-access neural coding (B-frames, hierarchical GOPs) is far less mature than low-delay coding [5].

**3. Determinism and standardization.** A shipped codec must decode bit-exactly on every device — hence DCVC-RT's integerization [6]. Floating-point nondeterminism across GPUs/NPUs is a deployment blocker, and no neural codec has entered a formal standard yet, though MPEG's deep-learning video coding explorations continue.

**4. Perceptual optimization vs. fidelity metrics.** GAN-trained neural codecs produce strikingly realistic reconstructions at very low bitrates — but PSNR can *drop* while perceptual scores rise, breaking the metric contract broadcast engineering relies on. The field lacks a universally trusted perceptual-distortion theory for the RD tradeoff.

**5. Video coding for machines (VCM).** Perhaps the deepest open problem: most decoded video is now watched by *models*, not humans — object detectors, trackers, action recognizers. MPEG's VCM standardization effort asks whether we should compress *features* rather than pixels, optimizing *task accuracy per bit* instead of PSNR per bit [5]. Early results show dramatic bitrate savings when the decoder is a detector rather than a display, but the theory — rate–distortion–*accuracy* tradeoffs, task-scalable bitstreams that serve both human and machine consumers — is in its infancy. A codec that jointly optimizes *R + λ₁D_pixel + λ₂D_task* may be the defining architecture of the next decade.

**6. Energy and the edge.** Training a codec family costs GPU-months; decoding 4K on battery power demands integer arithmetic, pruning/quantization, and hardware co-design. The RD-complexity Pareto frontier for NPUs and DSPs is largely unmapped.

## 7. Conclusion

Learned video compression has traveled from DVC's neural re-implementation of the classical pipeline to an information-theoretically principled discipline. The milestones form a clear arc: *scale-space flow* [2] taught warping to represent its own uncertainty; *DCVC* [1] proved residue coding suboptimal and moved the condition into a learned feature space, delivering 26% savings over x265; *VCT* [3] showed transformers can subsume motion compensation; *VLVC* [5] became the first learned codec to beat VVC on MS-SSIM; and *DCVC-RT* [6] broke the speed barrier with 125/113 fps 1080p at 21% savings over VTM.

The central lesson is the conditional-coding inequality: bits are saved not by better subtraction but by better *conditioning* — richer contexts, tighter entropy models, and training objectives that price every dependency. The next leaps will come from the same principle applied to harder problems: random-access structures, task-aware VCM bitstreams, and hardware-native architectures.
