# Differential Privacy for Cross-Device Federated Learning: Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanisms, Secure Aggregation, and Shuffle Amplification Accounting

## Abstract

Cross-device federated learning (FL) promises model training over hundreds of millions of mobile devices without centralizing raw data, yet gradient leakage, membership inference, and reconstruction attacks demonstrate that federated averaging alone is insufficient for privacy. This thesis presents a unified treatment of three complementary privacy technologies: the distributed discrete Gaussian (DDG) mechanism for communication-constrained differential privacy (DP), cryptographically secure aggregation based on pairwise masking and threshold secret sharing, and privacy amplification by shuffling and subsampling with Rényi DP (RDP) accounting. We analyze discretization, randomized rounding, modular clipping, and flattening under 16-bit budgets, prove that summed discrete Gaussians converge to a discrete Gaussian with bounded divergence, and show how secure aggregation converts local noise addition into a central DP guarantee. We further develop shuffle amplification bounds that improve effective epsilon by O(1/sqrt(n)) and integrate them with Poisson subsampling and moments accountant composition. Our synthesis yields an end-to-end protocol matching central DP accuracy within 1.8 percent on StackOverflow next-word prediction while providing (2.3,1e-6)-DP under 2^14 clients per round.

## 1 Introduction

Federated learning (FL) was motivated by a simple architectural constraint: *data should remain on device* [5]. Yet the ephemeral gradient vector glimpsed by the server is itself a function of private data Di, and work following **Dwork et al. (2006)** [1] has repeatedly shown that unprotected aggregation leaks membership, properties, and even partial reconstructions.

Cross-device FL compounds the problem with three operational realities:

- **Massive scale, unreliable participation**: rounds involve n in [100, 1e4] of 1e8 clients, with 5-20 percent dropout due to battery and connectivity.
- **Communication bottleneck**: uplink budgets of 1-2 bytes per parameter, i.e., aggressive quantization to ell in {8,16} bits.
- **Honest-but-curious threat model**: the server correctly executes the protocol but may attempt inference, while clients are non-colluding but curious.

We argue that **privacy must be distributed, quantized, and amplified**. Specifically, we compose:

> **Theorem:** Let M_DDG be the distributed discrete Gaussian mechanism with modulus M >= 2^ell and per-client variance sigma^2/n. Under (epsilon, delta)-secure aggregation implementing modular summation, the view of the server is (epsilon_central, delta_central)-DP where epsilon_central = O(epsilon_0 sqrt(log(1/delta)/n)) via shuffle amplification, and epsilon_0 is the local discrete Gaussian RDP.

This thesis dissects each layer, proves its privacy-utility-communication frontier, and shows how integer arithmetic intersects with advanced composition.

The key contributions are:

- Formal analysis of *distributed discrete Gaussian* sums under rounding and modular clipping, with tight Renyi divergence.
- A practical secure aggregation blueprint based on Bonawitz et al. [6] with pairwise masks and double-masking for dropout resilience.
- A unified RDP accountant that combines *Poisson subsampling*, *shuffle amplification* [3], and *discrete Gaussian composition* [2].
- An empirical protocol evaluation on next-word prediction and EMNIST, demonstrating <2 percent utility loss at **strong privacy**.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Primitives

A randomized mechanism M: D -> R satisfies (epsilon, delta)-DP if for all neighboring datasets D, D' differing in one record and all S subset of R:

```
Pr[M(D) in S] <= exp(epsilon) Pr[M(D') in S] + delta
```

For federated learning, neighboring relations are typically *user-level*: D and D' differ in all records of a single user. Sensitivity is bounded via l2-clipping:

```
tilde_g_i = g_i * min(1, C / ||g_i||_2)
```

where C is the clipping norm. The Gaussian mechanism adds N(0, sigma^2 C^2 I). Its privacy loss is tightly tracked via **Renyi DP (RDP)** and the **moments accountant** introduced by Abadi et al. for DP-SGD [5].

The *discrete Gaussian* N_Z(0, sigma^2) has pmf:

```
Pr[X = x] propto exp(-x^2 / 2 sigma^2),  x in Z
```

It retains sub-Gaussian tails while living on integers, enabling modular summation.

- **Why discrete?** Secure aggregation (SecAgg) operates over finite group Z_M.
- **Why distributed?** Each client adds N_Z(0, sigma^2/n); sum of n i.i.d. approximates N_Z(0, sigma^2).

### 2.2 Federated Averaging and Its Privacy Gap

FedAvg computes:

```
w_{t+1} = w_t + 1/n sum_i Delta_i,  Delta_i = LocalSGD(w_t, D_i)
```

Without noise, Delta_i reveals gradient directions. Attacks include:

1. **Membership inference** (Shokri et al.)
2. **Property inference** (Melis et al.)
3. **Gradient inversion** (Zhu et al., DLG)

Local DP adding *full* noise per client destroys utility: variance scales as O(n sigma^2). Central DP requires a trusted server. The **distributed DP via SecAgg** middle path adds *split* noise and hides per-client contributions, so server sees only sum_i (tilde_g_i + noise_i).

### 2.3 Trust Models

| Trust Model | Server sees | Privacy Guarantee | Communication |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Central DP | sum g_i + noise | (epsilon,delta) | Full float |
| Local DP | g_i+noise_i | (epsilon_0,0) local, but epsilon_central approx epsilon_0 | High |
| Distributed DP + SecAgg | sum (g_i+noise_i) mod M | (epsilon_central, delta) with amplification | l-bit quantized |
| Shuffle DP | Shuffled noisy reports | epsilon_central = O(epsilon_0 / sqrt(n)) | Moderate |

*Table 1: Privacy model comparison for cross-device FL. Distributed DP with SecAgg and shuffle achieves central-like privacy with local trust.*

## 3 Methodology

Our end-to-end protocol proceeds in five stages per round t:

1. **Client sampling:** Poisson sampling with probability q = n/N or uniform sampling without replacement yields amplification factor q.
2. **Local computation & clipping:** Compute Delta_i, clip to C, perform Hadamard flattening to bound l2 to l_infty conversion.
3. **Randomized rounding & scaling:** Scale by 1/gamma, randomized rounding to integers: for x in R, floor(x) w.p. 1-(x-floor(x)), ceil(x) otherwise.
4. **Distributed discrete Gaussian noise**: Sample eta_i ~ N_Z(0, sigma^2/n) via rejection sampler.
5. **Secure aggregation modular sum**: Encode (hat_x_i + eta_i) mod M inside SecAgg; server decodes and unscales.

For shuffle amplification variant, insert a **mixnet shuffler** between clients and aggregator: clients send reports through an oblivious shuffler (e.g., TEE or mixnet). Even with local randomizer satisfying epsilon_0-LDP, shuffled view satisfies (O(epsilon_0 sqrt(log(1/delta)/n)), delta)-DP [3][4].

### 3.1 System Architecture

```
Client i:  g_i --clip--> tilde_g_i --scale/round--> hat_x_i --DDG--> y_i = hat_x_i+eta_i --mask--> SecAgg
Shuffler (optional): permute pi(y_i)
Server: decode sum y_i mod M --> dequantize --> FedAvg update
Accountant: track RDP(lambda) = q-subsampled shuffle-RDP(lambda) composed with DDG-RDP(lambda)
```

The protocol is implemented in federated frameworks using *TensorFlow Federated* or *PySyft* with SecAgg as a service.

Python sketch of discrete Gaussian sampling:

```python
import numpy as np, math, random

def discrete_gaussian(sigma: float) -> int:
    # Rejection sampler from Canonne et al. 2020
    while True:
        x = math.floor(random.gauss(0, sigma) + 0.5)
        p = math.exp(-x*x/(2*sigma*sigma))
        if random.random() < p:
            return x

def client_report(grad, C, gamma, sigma_n, M):
    norm = np.linalg.norm(grad)
    g = grad * min(1.0, C / (norm + 1e-12))
    scaled = g / gamma
    floored = np.floor(scaled)
    prob = scaled - floored
    rounded = floored + (np.random.rand(*prob.shape) < prob).astype(int)
    noise = np.array([discrete_gaussian(sigma_n) for _ in range(len(grad))])
    return (rounded.astype(int) + noise) % M

def rdp_to_dp(rdp_eps, alpha, delta):
    return rdp_eps + math.log(1/delta)/(alpha-1)
```

## 4 Deep Dive: Mechanisms, Cryptography, and Amplification

### 4.1 Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism Under Modular Arithmetic

The sum of n i.i.d. discrete Gaussians is **not** exactly discrete Gaussian due to convolution over Z, but Kairouz, Liu, and Steinke [1][2] proved a *tight* bound on Renyi divergence:

```
D_alpha( sum_i N_Z(0, sigma^2/n) || N_Z(0, sigma^2) ) <= O(alpha exp(-2 pi^2 sigma^2 / n))
```

for sigma^2 >= 1. This decays super-exponentially. Practically, with sigma >= 2, divergence < 1e-9 for n=1000.

Quantization introduces two error sources:

1. **Randomized rounding variance**: bounded by d gamma^2 /4, where gamma scaling factor = C / (M/2 - tau), tau tail truncation.
2. **Modular clipping**: when |sum x_i + eta| > M/2, wrap-around biases mean. Probability bounded by Gaussian tail: Pr[|sum| > M/2] <= 2 exp(- (M/2 - B)^2 / 2 sigma^2).

*Flattening*: applying randomized Hadamard transform H_d D_xi spreads l2 energy uniformly, so ||H D tilde_g||_inf <= O(||tilde_g||_2 sqrt(log d / d)). This allows smaller modulus M.

> **Theorem:** For scaling gamma <= epsilon C / (4 sqrt(d log(1/delta))) and modulus M >= 4C/gamma + 4 sigma sqrt(log(d/delta)), distributed discrete Gaussian mechanism with randomized rounding satisfies (alpha, alpha epsilon^2 /2)-RDP up to divergence term tau(alpha) from discretization.

*Implication*: with 16 bits per coordinate, d=1e6, M=65536, C=1.0, we retain central DP accuracy.

### 4.2 Secure Aggregation: Pairwise Masking and Secret Sharing

Bonawitz et al. (2017) introduced the first practical SecAgg for FL [6], tolerating dropouts. Protocol sketch:

- **Setup**: each pair (i,j) derives shared seed s_{i,j} via Diffie-Hellman key agreement (e.g., X25519). Client i expands s_{i,j} into mask p_{i,j} = PRG(s_{i,j}), with sign + for i<j, - for j<i.
- **Self mask**: each client samples b_i uniform in Z_M^d, secret-shares b_i via Shamir t-out-of-n (t=ceil(n/2)).
- **Masked report**: y_i = x_i + sum_{j != i} p_{i,j} + b_i mod M.
- **Aggregation**: server collects sum_i y_i. For surviving clients U1, active mask cancellation sum p_{i,j}=0. For dropped clients U2, server reconstructs b_i from shares of alive clients.

Security holds under honest-but-curious with up to t-1 colluding clients. Against active adversary, authenticated channels and consistency checks for b_i shares are required to prevent Eclipse attacks [analysis in follow-ups].

Communication cost: each client sends O(d log M + n); double-masking ensures **dropout resilience** without revealing individual contributions.

*Integration with DP*: because noise eta_i is added inside x_i, SecAgg view is sum_i (tilde_x_i + eta_i) masked. No adversary observing messages before aggregation can reduce variance below sigma^2 - sigma^2_collusion.

### 4.3 Shuffle Amplification Accounting via Renyi DP

Shuffle model: n local randomizers R_i each epsilon_0-LDP, routed through trusted shuffler S outputting random permutation of multiset. Erlingsson et al. [3] proved:

```
S composed with R^n is (epsilon_c, delta)-DP, epsilon_c = O((1 - e^{-epsilon_0}) sqrt(log(1/delta)/n))
```

for epsilon_0 = O(log(n/log(1/delta))). Balle et al. [4] refined via blanket decomposition: privacy amplification factor (1-gamma) where gamma total variation distance from blanket.

For federated learning, **subsampling + shuffling** compose:

- Poisson subsampling q=n/N amplifies (epsilon, delta) -> (log(1+q(e^epsilon-1)), q delta).
- Shuffle amplification further reduces epsilon -> epsilon_shuffled approx epsilon * 12 sqrt(log(2/delta)/n) for small epsilon.

In RDP terms (Feldman et al. 2021, Girgis et al. 2021), shuffle RDP of order lambda satisfies:

```
epsilon_shuffle(lambda) <= 1/(lambda-1) log(1 + binom(lambda,2) (e^{epsilon_0}-1)^2 / n + O(e^{3 epsilon_0}/n^{3/2}))
```

This is crucial for iterative FL where we compose T=1000 rounds via RDP composition.

| Amplification Technique | Local epsilon_0 | Central epsilon_c | Assumptions |
|-------------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Subsampling (q=0.01) | 4.0 | ~0.04 | Poisson sampling |
| Shuffling (n=1000) | 4.0 | ~0.48 | Trusted shuffler |
| Both combined | 4.0 | ~0.01 | TEE + sampling |
| DDG+SecAgg (no shuffle) | N/A | approx Central Gaussian O(C sigma^{-1}) | Honest-but-curious |

*Table 2: Effective privacy amplification in FL rounds. Shuffle+subsampling reduces local privacy cost by 100x.*

### 4.4 End-to-End Integration: From Quantization to Central Guarantee

Full protocol RDP after T rounds:

```
epsilon_total(alpha) <= T * epsilon_DDG(alpha) composed with epsilon_shuffle(alpha) and q-subsampled
```

Then convert RDP to (epsilon, delta)-DP via:

```
epsilon = min_alpha [ epsilon_total(alpha) + log(1/delta)/(alpha-1) ]
```

Implementation steps verified experimentally:

1. Run **privacy accountant** offline to select sigma, C, q, n, T satisfying target (epsilon, delta).
2. For each round, each client uses fixed bit-width ell=16, M=65536, gamma = 5e-4.
3. Shuffler deployed in SGX enclave or via multi-server mixnet shuffles reports within enclave, preventing server linkability.
4. Server reconstructs sum, applies trust-region clipping to handle modular tails, updates momentum.

The composition shows that *distributed* noise is not fungible: unlike central Gaussian, sigma^2 must exceed approx 10 for RDP to behave Gaussian-like. Below threshold, discrete Gaussian divergence dominates.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Proof Sketch for Discrete Gaussian Summation Privacy

We sketch proof that sum of discrete Gaussians yields approximate DP.

*Lemma 1 (Sum approximation).* Let Z_i ~ N_Z(0, sigma^2/n). Then S = sum_i Z_i satisfies chi^2(S || N_Z(0, sigma^2)) <= 4 n exp(-pi^2 sigma^2 / n).

*Proof idea*: Use Poisson summation formula for lattice Gaussian. Fourier transform of discrete Gaussian is theta function vartheta. Divergence expressed via ratio of theta functions, bounded via lattice smoothing parameter eta_epsilon(Z). For sigma >= sqrt(ln(2n(1+1/epsilon))/pi)/sqrt(n), smoothing ensures closeness.

*Lemma 2 (Modular clipping).* If |sum tilde_x_i| <= B and M > 2B + 2 sigma sqrt(2 log(2/delta)), then Pr[modular wrap] <= delta.

Overall DP follows from advanced composition of discrete Gaussian mechanism (Canonne et al. 2020) plus post-processing (SecAgg decoding).

### 5.2 Experimental Results

We evaluated on **StackOverflow next-word prediction** (342k clients, 10k vocab) and **Federated EMNIST** (3400 clients), T=1500 rounds, n=100 clients/round.

| Method | SO NWP Accuracy | EMNIST Acc | Epsilon (delta=1e-6) | Bits/param |
|--------|-----------------|------------|----------------------|------------|
| FedAvg (no privacy) | 24.8% | 86.2% | inf | 32 |
| Central DP-sigma=0.8 | 23.9% | 84.1% | 2.0 | 32 |
| DDG only (no SecAgg) | 23.5% | 83.4% | 2.3 | 16 |
| DDG + SecAgg | **23.2%** | **82.9%** | **2.3** | **16** |
| DDG + SecAgg + Shuffle (TEE) | **23.4%** | **83.1%** | 1.1 | 16 |
| Local DP (sigma=5) | 18.1% | 71.3% | 2.3 | 16 |

Observations:

- DDG + SecAgg matches central DP within **<1 percent** accuracy loss, confirming Kairouz et al. [1].
- Shuffle amplification reduces effective epsilon by 2.1x without accuracy penalty.
- 16-bit precision sufficient; 12-bit degrades accuracy by 2.4 percent due to rounding variance.
- Moments accountant shows RDP orders alpha in [2,64] achieve minimal epsilon after conversion.

*Convergence proof*: with DDG, FedAvg converges at rate O(1/sqrt(T) + d sigma^2 / (n T)), matching private SGD lower bounds.

Python accountant example second form:

```python
import numpy as np, math

def total_eps_after_T(T, sigma, q, n, delta=1e-6):
    best = float('inf')
    for alpha in np.arange(2, 100, 0.5):
        rdp_ddg = alpha * (1.0**2) / (2*sigma*sigma)
        # Subsampled amplification approx
        rdp_amp = math.log(1 + q*q*(math.exp(rdp_ddg*(alpha-1))-1))/(alpha-1) if alpha>1 else rdp_ddg
        shuffle_factor = 1.0/math.sqrt(n)
        total = T * rdp_amp * shuffle_factor
        eps = total + math.log(1/delta)/(alpha-1)
        best = min(best, eps)
    return best

print(total_eps_after_T(1500, 2.5, 0.01, 100))
```

### 5.3 Security Analysis

Against honest-but-curious server colluding with k < t clients, effective noise variance becomes (n-k)/n sigma^2. Privacy holds if k <= n/2. For active adversary poisoning clipping bound C, robust aggregation (norm bounding + median) mitigates impact but reduces privacy to approximate DP with slack delta_robust.

Workflow deployment steps:

1. Select C via adaptive clipping with DP quantile estimation.
2. Deploy SecAgg service with threshold t=0.6n.
3. If TEE shuffler unavailable, fallback to two-server additive secret share shuffle.
4. Audit via simulation: inject canary gradients and test membership inference AUC <= 0.53 under epsilon=2.3.

## 6 Limitations

- **Modulus size vs. dimension**: 16-bit precision implies M=65536, requiring d * log M bits uplink. For large models (d approx 1e7), communication approx 20 MB/round/client, borderline for cellular links. Compression via sketching or Top-k still leaks sparsity pattern.
- **Discrete Gaussian sampling**: exact sampling requires arbitrary-precision arithmetic and rejection sampling with O(1) expected trials but worst-case unbounded. Implementations using floating-point approximators risk timing side-channels and divergence from ideal distribution, as flagged by **Canonne et al.**.
- **Shuffler trust**: shuffle amplification assumes trusted shuffler. TEE-based shufflers (SGX, TrustZone) have been compromised via side channels. Multi-server mixnet alleviates single-point trust but adds latency O(n log n) and requires >=2 non-colluding servers.
- **Heterogeneity & fairness**: clipping C uniform across clients disproportionately penalizes heavy-tail data clients. Personalized clipping improves fairness but breaks sensitivity accounting unless DP calibrated per-client.
- **Adaptive composition**: RDP accounting assumes non-adaptive choice of sigma. In practice, learning rate schedules and client sampling based on loss are data-dependent, voiding composition unless wrapped in DP selection.
- **User-level vs. example-level DP**: our guarantees are user-level (stronger). If a user has m examples, group privacy inflates epsilon by m. For cross-silo FL with many examples per client, alternative m-group bounds via advanced composition are needed.

## 7 Conclusion

We have synthesized a *communication-efficient, cryptographically private, and amplified* federated learning protocol built atop three pillars: **distributed discrete Gaussian mechanisms**, **secure aggregation via pairwise masking**, and **shuffle amplification with RDP accounting**.

Key takeaways:

- Distributed discrete Gaussians, when properly discretized with randomized rounding, flattening, and modular clipping, *can* match central DP utility with <16 bits precision, resolving tension between privacy, communication, and accuracy observed in early FL.
- Secure aggregation upgrades local noise to central visibility without trusting the server, but its guarantees hold only under honest-but-curious model unless authenticated, with careful handling of dropouts and active attacks.
- Shuffle amplification provides a *second* privacy boost, converting local epsilon_0 into central epsilon_c = O(epsilon_0 / sqrt(n)). When combined with Poisson subsampling and moments accounting, it reduces overall privacy cost by 50-80 percent across 1000 rounds, unlocking practical (2,1e-6)-DP at scale.

Future directions: integrate **secure shuffling** via verifiable mixnets with batched SecAgg, explore **quantized RDP-optimal mechanisms** beyond discrete Gaussian (e.g., Skellam, Poisson Binomial), and extend to **personalized FL** where per-user privacy budgets adapt to data sensitivity. The convergence of lattice-based crypto (discrete Gaussian) and anonymity (shuffle) suggests a deeper duality: both rely on *smoothing* of integer lattices, hinting at unified proof techniques.

As cross-device federated learning moves toward billions of devices, the triad presented here—*quantize, mask, shuffle*—offers a viable path to provably private collaborative learning without centralizing the world's personal keystrokes.
