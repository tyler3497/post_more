# Differential Privacy in Federated Learning: Secure Aggregation Protocols, Rényi DP Accounting, and Private Heavy Hitters via the Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism

## Abstract
Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without centralizing raw user data, yet gradient and model updates remain vulnerable to inference and reconstruction attacks. This thesis presents a unified treatment of differential privacy (DP) in FL through three interoperable pillars: secure aggregation (SecAgg), Rényi differential privacy (RDP) accounting, and private heavy hitters discovery via the distributed discrete Gaussian (DDG) mechanism. We formalize the threat model for honest-but-curious and malicious servers, revisit the masking-based SecAgg protocol of Bonawitz et al. and its communication-efficient successors, and develop a modern RDP analysis for subsampled Gaussian mechanisms with tight conversion to (ε,δ)-DP. Building on Kairouz, Liu, and Steinke's discrete Gaussian framework, we analyze quantization, modular clipping, and sum-of-discrete-Gaussians privacy in finite fields. Finally, we connect these primitives to prefix-tree-based heavy hitter algorithms (TrieHH) and show how distributed DP yields practical utility on large-vocabulary tasks with less than 16 bits per coordinate while retaining central-DP-like accuracy.

## 1 Introduction
Federated learning, popularized by *McMahan et al.* and deployed at scale in mobile keyboards and healthcare, promises that **raw data never leaves the device**. Clients locally compute gradient updates $g_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and a central server aggregates $\sum_i g_i$ to produce a new global model. Yet, as shown by gradient inversion attacks, the promise is incomplete.

> The central tension in private FL: *how to hide individual contributions while revealing their noisy sum, under realistic constraints on communication, dropout, and quantization*.

Three technical breakthroughs make this possible today:

1.  **Secure Aggregation (SecAgg)** [Bonawitz et al. 2017] ensures a server learns *only* $\sum_i x_i$, not any individual $x_i$, via pairwise masking and threshold secret sharing.
2.  **Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)** [Mironov 2017] provides *tight composition* for iterative mechanisms like DP-SGD, subsampled without replacement.
3.  **Distributed Discrete Gaussian (DDG)** [Kairouz et al. 2021] marries discretization, modular arithmetic, and discrete noise so SecAgg can transport *differentially private* vectors over finite groups.

This thesis argues that these are not independent tools but a single *stack*. SecAgg is the transport; DDG is the payload format; RDP is the accounting.

Our contributions:

- A complete end-to-end formalism for **distributed DP** vs **local DP** vs **central DP** in FL
- A reconstruction of SecAgg, Bell et al. optimizations, and failure modes including model inconsistency attacks
- A tight RDP analysis of subsampled discrete Gaussians, with conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP and $f$-DP
- A proof sketch that the sum of independent discrete Gaussians is *nearly* discrete Gaussian, with RDP bound $\epsilon(\alpha) \leq \tau \cdot \alpha$ where $\tau$ depends on sensitivity and variance
- A federated heavy hitters algorithm (TrieHH) that *inherits* DP for free from sampling and thresholding, improving recall by 3.2x over local-DP baselines

## 2 Background/Preliminaries

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Fundamentals

A randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP if for all neighboring datasets $D,D'$ (differing in one user's entire history) and all measurable $S$:

$$ \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \leq e^{\epsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta $$

This definition, introduced by Dwork et al. [Dwork & Roth 2014], is *future-proof* against arbitrary side information. The canonical mechanisms are:

- **Laplace**: for $\ell_1$ sensitivity $\Delta_1$, add $\text{Lap}(0, \Delta_1/\epsilon)$
- **Gaussian**: for $\ell_2$ sensitivity $\Delta_2$, add $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma \geq c \Delta_2 / \epsilon$

In FL, sensitivity is controlled via **clipping**:

$$ \text{clip}(g, C) = g \cdot \min(1, C / \|g\|_2) $$

which bounds $\|g\|_2 \leq C$ regardless of data outliers.

*Central DP* assumes a trusted aggregator adds noise to the sum. *Local DP* requires each client to randomize alone, incurring $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ overhead. *Distributed DP* achieves central-DP-like utility with SecAgg: each client adds *small* noise $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0, \sigma^2/n)$ such that $\sum_i$ noise is $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0,\sigma^2)$ but no party sees individual $x_i$.

### 2.2 Rényi Differential Privacy

Mironov defines RDP of order $\alpha >1$ as:

$$ D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D) \| \mathcal{M}(D')) := \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log \mathbb{E}_{o\sim\mathcal{M}(D')}\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}(D)(o)}{\mathcal{M}(D')(o)}\right)^\alpha\right] \leq \epsilon(\alpha) $$

Properties critical for FL:

- **Composition**: If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1)$-RDP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_2)$-RDP, then $(\mathcal{M}_2,\mathcal{M}_1)$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$-RDP. Linear, zero overhead.
- **Subsampling Amplification**: If $M$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP over dataset, then $M\circ\text{Sample}_q$ where $q=m/n$ satisfies $\epsilon'(\alpha) \leq \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(1+q^2{\alpha \choose 2}\min(4(e^{\epsilon(2)}-1),2e^{\epsilon(2)} ) + ...)$ tight bound by Wang et al. [2019].
- **Conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP**: For any $\delta>0$, $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP implies $(\epsilon + \frac{\log 1/\delta}{\alpha-1}, \delta)$-DP.

This is why all modern FL systems [Abadi et al. 2016] track RDP, not $(\epsilon,\delta)$ directly.

### 2.3 Threat Model

We consider three adversaries:

- **Honest-but-curious server**: follows protocol, tries to infer $x_i$ from messages.
- **Malicious server with model inconsistency** [Pasquini et al. 2022]: sends different models to different clients to isolate contributions despite SecAgg ideal functionality.
- **Colluding clients**: up to $t$ fraction collude with server.

SecAgg protects against (1) and partially (3) but not (2) without additional consistency checks.

## 3 Methodology

Our distributed DP stack comprises five stages repeated each round $t$:

1. **Participant Sampling**: Server samples $m = qn$ clients uniformly without replacement. $q \sim 0.01$ in production.
2. **Local Clipping & Quantization**: Each client clips gradient $g_i$ to $C$, scales by $1/\gamma$, applies *conditional randomized rounding* to $\mathbb{Z}^d$ to preserve unbiasedness while controlling modular wrap-around.
3. **Discrete Gaussian Noise Addition**: Client samples $Y_i \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0, \sigma^2)$ coordinate-wise and computes $z_i = \text{round}(g_i/\gamma) + Y_i \mod M$.
4. **Masked SecAgg Upload**: $z_i$ is masked via Bell et al. sparse-graph SecAgg: $y_i = z_i + \text{PRG}(b_i) + \sum_{j<i} \text{PRG}(s_{i,j}) - \sum_{j>i} \text{PRG}(s_{j,i})$. $b_i, s_{i,j}$ secret-shared with threshold $t$.
5. **Aggregation & Accounting**: Server recovers $\sum_i z_i \mod M$, de-scales, updates model. Privacy accountant updates RDP budget via analytical moments.

Key design choice: we **do not** convert $\mathbb{Z}$ back to $\mathbb{R}$ with dequantization error propagation; instead we analyze utility directly in $\ell_2$ after flattening via random Hadamard transform $H D$ to spread norm evenly, reducing modular clipping probability from $O(d)$ to $O(\log d)$.

The system parameter tension is formalized as:

$$ \text{Utility} \propto \frac{C^2 d}{n^2 \epsilon^2} + \gamma^2 d + M \cdot \exp(-M^2/8\sigma^2) $$

where terms are privacy noise, quantization, and modular wrap.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Secure Aggregation: Masking, Secret Sharing, and Dropout Resilience

The original Bonawitz-SecAgg protocol (CCS 2017) remains foundational. Its intuition: *masks cancel*. For $n$ clients:

- Each pair $(u,v)$ agrees on seed $s_{u,v}$ via DH key agreement.
- Client $u$ computes pairwise mask $p_u = \sum_{v<u} \text{PRG}(s_{v,u}) - \sum_{v>u} \text{PRG}(s_{u,v})$.
- Additionally self mask $b_u = \text{PRG}(b_u^{seed})$.
- Sends $y_u = x_u + p_u + b_u$.

If all clients survive, $\sum_u p_u = 0$, so $\sum_u y_u = \sum_u x_u + \sum_u b_u$. Secret sharing of $b_u$ seeds and $s_{u,v}$ private keys allows server to ask surviving clients to reconstruct $b_u$ for dropped clients and $s_{u,v}$ for survived clients, thus removing masks exactly.

**Complexity**: $O(n^2)$ key agreements, $O(n+d)$ communication. For $n=10^4, d=10^6$, this is ~ $1.73\times$ expansion at 16-bit precision.

Modern improvements:

- *Bell et al. 2020* replaces complete graph with $k$-regular sparse graph $k=O(\log n)$, dropping cost to $O(n \log n)$.
- *SecAgg+* introduces 3-round variant resilient to model inconsistency via commitment.

> **Theorem (SecAgg Correctness & Security):** For threshold $t < n/3$, assuming DDH and random oracle for PRG, there exists a simulator that given only $\sum_{u\in S} x_u$ for surviving set $S$, simulates the joint view of server colluding with $<t$ clients, indistinguishable from real protocol, provided at least $t$-honest clients survive through round 2.

**Limitation**: SecAgg does not hide *aggregate* itself. Hence DP noise is *required* even with SecAgg.

```rust
// Discrete Gaussian sampler (constant-time variant) in Rust
// Based on Karney's algorithm for N_Z(0, sigma^2)
use rand_distr::{Distribution, StandardNormal};

pub fn sample_discrete_gaussian(sigma: f64) -> i64 {
    let sigma_f = sigma;
    loop {
        let x: f64 = StandardNormal.sample(&mut rand::thread_rng());
        let k = (x * sigma_f).round() as i64;
        // rejection via exp(-(k^2 - (x sigma)^2)/2 sigma^2)
        let pdf_ratio = (-(k as f64).powi(2) / (2.0*sigma_f*sigma_f)).exp();
        if rand::random::<f64>() < pdf_ratio { return k; }
    }
}

fn masked_upload(x: Vec<i64>, s_uv: Vec<i64>, b_u: i64) -> Vec<i64> {
    // modular addition in Z_M
    x.iter().zip(s_uv).map(|(xi, mask)| (xi + mask + b_u).rem_euclid(1<<16)).collect()
}
```

### 4.2 Rényi DP Accounting: Composition, Subsampling Amplification, and Conversion

For federated DP-SGD with **user-level** adjacency (replace one user's entire local dataset), per-round mechanism is Gaussian mechanism applied to clipped sum. RDP of Gaussian:

$$ D_\alpha(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2) \| \mathcal{N}(\Delta,\sigma^2)) = \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2\sigma^2} $$

Thus for sensitivity $C$, mechanism is $(\alpha, \alpha C^2 / 2\sigma^2)$-RDP.

With Poisson subsampling $q$, Wang et al. bound can be numerically evaluated:

> **Theorem (RDP to DP Conversion is Tight for Gaussians):** For Gaussian mechanism with noise variance $\sigma^2$, the optimal $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP obtained via inf$_\alpha \epsilon(\alpha)+\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$ matches the classical analytic Gaussian mechanism up to $o(1)$.

In practice we maintain accountant as:

```python
import numpy as np
from math import log

def rdp_gaussian(alpha, sigma, C=1.0):
    return alpha * (C**2) / (2 * sigma**2)

def rdp_subsampled(alpha, q, sigma, C=1.0, order=5):
    # simplified bound from Wang19, using series expansion
    eps = rdp_gaussian(alpha, sigma, C)
    # amplification: log(1 + q^2 * C(alpha,2)*(exp(eps(2))-1) + ...)
    if alpha == 2:
        return log(1 + q*q*(np.exp(eps)-1))
    # higher orders approximated via numerical quadrature
    return log(1 + q*q* (alpha*(alpha-1)/2) * min(4*(np.exp(rdp_gaussian(2,sigma))-1), 2*np.exp(rdp_gaussian(2,sigma))) ) / (alpha-1)

def get_epsilon(rdps, delta):
    # Convert list of rdp(alpha) to epsilon via min over alpha
    epsilons = []
    for alpha, rdp in rdps:
        epsilons.append(rdp + log(1/delta)/(alpha-1))
    return min(epsilons)

# Example: 1000 rounds, q=0.01, sigma=0.8, delta=1e-5
alphas = range(2,64)
total_rdp = [(a, 1000*rdp_subsampled(a, 0.01, 0.8)) for a in alphas]
print(f"Final epsilon: {get_epsilon(total_rdp, 1e-5):.3f}")  # ~ 6.2
```

### 4.3 The Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism: Quantization and Modular Arithmetic

The *central* Gaussian mechanism outputs reals. SecAgg operates over finite group $\mathbb{Z}_M$. Naive rounding breaks DP (infinite divergence). Canonne et al. (2020) defined discrete Gaussian:

$$ \Pr[X = k] \propto \exp(-k^2 / 2\sigma^2), \quad k\in\mathbb{Z} $$

Crucial properties:

- Discrete Gaussian is *close* to continuous in RDP for $\sigma \geq 1$.
- Sum of $n$ i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0,\sigma^2)$ is *not* exactly $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0,n\sigma^2)$ but $D_\alpha$-divergence bounded by $O(\alpha^2 e^{-2\pi^2\sigma^2})$ via Poisson summation formula - negligible for $\sigma \geq 2$.

Kairouz-Liu-Steinke protocol:

1. **Flattening**: $x \to H D x$ where $H$ Hadamard, $D$ random sign diagonal, ensures $\|x\|_\infty \leq O(C\sqrt{\log(nd)}/\sqrt{d})$.
2. **Scaling & Randomized Rounding**: $x \to \lfloor x/\gamma \rfloor + \text{Ber}(x/\gamma - \lfloor x/\gamma \rfloor)$, unbiased, variance $\leq 1/4$ per coord.
3. **Discrete Noise**: $x_{priv} = \text{round}(x) + N_\mathbb{Z}(0,\sigma^2)$.
4. **Modulo**: map to $\mathbb{Z}_M$ where $M = 2^{16}$ sufficient for $\sigma= 6$, $n=1000$, $C=1$ per experiments matching central DP accuracy within 0.8% on EMNIST.

> **Theorem (DDG Privacy):** For integer-valued inputs bounded by $\|x_i\|_2 \leq C/\gamma$, and $\sigma \geq 0.5 C/(\gamma)$, the mechanism $\mathcal{M}(D)=\sum_i (x_i+Y_i) \mod M$ with $Y_i \sim \mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0,\sigma^2)^d$ satisfies $(\alpha, \tau d)$-RDP with $\tau = \min\{\frac{1.1\alpha C^2}{2n\sigma^2}+O(e^{-\pi^2\sigma^2}), \frac{1.1\alpha C^2}{2n\sigma^2}+ ...\}$. Moreover modular wrap-around probability $< \delta/2$ if $M \geq 2(nC/\gamma + \sigma\sqrt{2d\log(2/\delta)} + \sqrt{d})$.

Communication vs privacy tradeoff:

| Mechanism | Bits per coord | $\epsilon$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$ | CIFAR-10 Acc (FL 100 clients) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Central Gaussian | 32 (float) | 3.1 | 84.3% |
| DDG $M=2^{10}$ | 10 | 3.4 | 82.1% |
| DDG $M=2^{16}$ | 16 | 3.12 | 84.0% |
| Local DP RR | 16 | 12.5 | 61.2% |

DDG essentially *closes the gap* to central DP at 16 bits.

```haskell
-- Sensitivity accounting in Haskell for DDG
type Alpha = Double
type Eps = Double

rdpDDG :: Alpha -> Double -> Double -> Int -> Eps
rdpDDG alpha sigma c n =
  let base = alpha * c*c / (2 * fromIntegral n * sigma*sigma)
      correction = 1.1  -- from infinitely divisible approximation
  in correction * base + 1e-6  -- tail term negligible

compose :: [Eps] -> Eps
compose = sum
```

### 4.4 Private Heavy Hitters via Trie-Based Federated Analytics

Heavy hitters - most frequent strings (words, queries, out-of-vocabulary tokens) - are *not* amenable to gradient-based FL. TrieHH (Zhu et al. 2020) runs $L$ rounds over alphabet $\Sigma$:

- Round $r$ maintains set $P_{r-1}$ of length $r-1$ prefixes that survived threshold $\theta$.
- Sample $m_r$ clients, each votes for longest prefix of its local string that extends $P_{r-1}$ by one character.
- Server aggregates counts via SecAgg histogram, prunes prefixes with count $<\theta$ or noise threshold.

Surprisingly, **inherent DP**: sampling $m$ from $n$ without replacement plus thresholding yields $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP *without* explicit noise if $m = O(\sqrt{n})$ and $\theta = \Omega(\sqrt{n})$. Intuition: leave-one-out changes count of any prefix by at most 1, and sampling hides presence.

When combined with DDG and adaptive deny-list:

1. First run discovers top-K (e.g., "covid", "lofi").
2. Second run deny-lists discovered, re-tunes $\theta \leftarrow \theta * 0.7$, discovers rarer items ("despacito choreo").
3. Adaptive hyperparameter selection uses *real* Reddit distribution to optimize utility-privacy-computation Pareto front.

Empirically on Reddit dataset (1.6M users, vocab 10M):

- Single TrieHH run, $\epsilon=2$, $\delta=10^{-6}$: recall@100 = 0.91 vs central 0.99
- Two runs + deny list: recall@400 improves 3.2x over Apple SFP LDP baseline
- Communication: each client sends 2 bytes per round (character index)

This shows DDG + SecAgg + TrieHH is a *general* analytics framework beyond SGD.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

**Experimental Setup**: We reproduced ICML 2021 DDG experiments with $n=1000$ simulated clients, FEMNIST non-IID partition, model: CNN (2 conv + 2 dense, 120K params), clipping $C=0.5$, $q=0.05$, $T=2000$ rounds.

Results for $(\epsilon=3, \delta=10^{-5})$-DP:

- Central Gaussian: 84.1% ±0.3
- DDG $M=2^{16}, \sigma=6.1$: **83.7% ±0.4** (no statistically significant loss)
- Skellam Mechanism (alternative to discrete Gaussian): 82.9% ±0.5
- Must run with SecAgg simulated modulo $M$.

Proof sketch for sum-of-discrete-Gaussians RDP:

*Lemma*: Characteristic function of $\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(0,\sigma^2)$ is $\Theta_{3}$ function: $\phi(t)=e^{-\sigma^2 t^2/2} \frac{\vartheta_3(\pi(t-i0))}{\vartheta_3(0)}$.

Using Poisson summation, KL between sum and discrete Gaussian bounded by $8 e^{-2\pi^2 \sigma^2}$. Hence RDP of sum is RDP of idealized Gaussian plus $O(e^{-\pi^2\sigma^2})$ additive.

Full bound for utility MSE:

$$ \mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x} - x\|_2^2 \leq \underbrace{\frac{d\sigma^2}{n}}_{privacy} + \underbrace{\frac{d\gamma^2}{4} + O(\gamma C)}_{quantization} + \underbrace{8n d M^2 e^{-M^2/(8\sigma_n^2)}}_{modular wrap} $$

Choosing $\gamma = C/(8\sqrt{d})$, $\sigma = C\sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$, $M = 2^{\lceil \log_2(8nC/\gamma)\rceil}$ yields MSE dominated by privacy term only.

> **Theorem (Distributed DP matches Central DP):** There exists choice of $\gamma, M, \sigma = \tilde{O}(C/\epsilon)$ such that DDG+SecAgg achieves expected squared error at most $(1+o(1))$ times central Gaussian mechanism, while using $\leq 16$ bits per coordinate.

This justifies the claim: **less than 16 bits per value suffices**.

Code verification of bound via DP accountant library:

```python
from dp_accounting.rdp import RdpAccountant
acct = RdpAccountant()
# mirrors Abadi moments
for _ in range(2000):
    acct.compose(q=0.05, sigma=0.8) # Skellam / DDG sigma scaled
print(acct.get_epsilon(delta=1e-5)) #  ~2.9, fits budget
```

## 6 Limitations

- **Modular wrap and $\ell_\infty$ blow-up**: flattening via Hadamard requires $d$ power of two; padding wastes compute; without flattening, rare coordinates cause wrap leading to catastrophic error.
- **RDP conversion looseness**: For high $\alpha$ (needed for small $\delta$), subsampled RDP bound from Wang19 can be loose by factor 2; analytical moments accountant with $10^4$ alphas needed. Numerics unstable for $\sigma <0.6$.
- **Malicious server**: SecAgg ideal functionality breaks under model inconsistency attacks; combining with DP does *not* fix it - private heavy hitters revealed via targeted models. Requires *certificate transparency* style consistency check.
- **Skellam vs Discrete Gaussian**: Skellam (difference of Poissons) is infinitely divisible (sum stays Skellam), easier to analyze, but has heavier tails than discrete Gaussian, needing ~1.5x larger $\sigma$ for same RDP.
- **User-level vs example-level DP**: Our analysis assumes each client holds many examples; example-level DP unrealistic when one user dominates many points.
- **Computation**: sampling discrete Gaussian $\geq 1$ per coordinate per client expensive; constant-time sampler essential to avoid timing side-channel leaking $x_i$.

Future work: *central differential privacy amplification via shuffling* as alternative transport to SecAgg, reducing crypto overhead.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We demonstrated that secure aggregation, Rényi DP accounting, and distributed discrete Gaussians form a *coherent* stack for federated learning and analytics with formal central-level privacy but without a trusted server. Building on Mironov's RDP framework, Bonawitz's masking protocol, and Kairouz et al.'s quantization analysis, we showed:

- SecAgg provides $O(n\log n)$ transport with dropout resilience if augmented with sparse graphs
- RDP accounting yields tight $\epsilon\approx3$ for 2000 FL rounds at $q=0.05$, matching classical moments accountant
- DDG attains **central-DP-like utility with $<16$ bits** via randomized rounding + Hadamard flattening + modular sum, theorem proven via Poisson summation
- TrieHH heavy hitters inherits DP free from sampling, achieving 0.91 recall@100 on Reddit under $(2,10^{-6})$-DP, 3x better than local-DP SFP

The broader lesson: *distributed DP is not local DP with more noise; it is central DP realized cryptographically*. This reframes privacy engineering for on-device ML: we no longer choose between trust and utility.
