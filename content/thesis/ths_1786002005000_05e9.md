# Co-Packaged Optics and Silicon Photonic Interconnects for Disaggregated Datacenters: Microring Resonators, Optical Circuit Switching, and Wavelength-Division Multiplexing

## Abstract
Disaggregated datacenters require petascale east-west bandwidth at sub-pJ/bit efficiency that electrical I/O cannot sustain beyond 224G SerDes. Co-packaged optics (CPO) collapses the switch-ASIC-to-optics interface, placing silicon photonic engines within millimeters of the ASIC to eliminate retimers, reduce PCB trace loss, and enable wavelength-division multiplexed (WDM) scale-out. This thesis unifies three pillars — microring resonator (MRR) modulators and filters, nanosecond-ready optical circuit switching (OCS), and dense WDM with comb sources — into a practical architecture for composable rack-scale AI factories. We derive a link budget model for 32×200G 6.4T socketed CPO with external laser source (ELS), analyze MRR resonance trimming via Sb₂Se₃ phase-change cladding for full-FSR non-volatile alignment, quantify Palomar MEMS OCS at 2 dB worst-case loss enabling 40% power reduction and 50× resilience in Google Jupiter, and model distributed OCS (dOCS) for GPU scale-up with millisecond failover. Combining NVIDIA Spectrum-X photonics (409.6 Tb/s, 3.5× power savings, 10× resilience over pluggable) with WDM MRR-based transmitters achieving 100 Gbps/lane OOK, we demonstrate a path to million-GPU non-blocking fabrics with flat topologies and dynamic resource disaggregation [1][2][3][4][5][6].

> **Keywords:** co-packaged optics, silicon photonics, microring resonator, WDM, optical circuit switching, disaggregated datacenter, ELS, dOCS

## 1 Introduction

AI factories scaling to >100k GPUs expose three hard walls:

1. **Electrical I/O power wall.** A 51.2T switch with 64×800G pluggable modules burns >40% of platform power in DSPs, retimers, and 20–30 dB PCB traces [1][6]. At 224 Gbps PAM4, channel loss forces Forward Error Correction (FEC) with 5–10 ns extra latency and >5 pJ/bit.
2. **Bandwidth density wall.** Faceplate density limits 36 QSFP-DD800 per RU; copper DAC cannot leave rack. Scale-out requires 2-tier Clos but spine layer adds store-and-forward latency and 3× cost per bit [2].
3. **Static allocation wall.** Traditional hierarchical datacenters bind CPU:DRAM:GPU in fixed ratios, incurring 18–27% stranded resources under heterogeneous ML jobs [8]. Disaggregation pools memory and accelerators, but demands reconfigurable, latency-insensitive interconnects.

Co-packaged optics (CPO) re-architects the network by integrating silicon photonic modulators, photodetectors, and wavelength multiplexers directly adjacent to the switch ASIC using 2.5D interposer or 3D hybrid bond [1][3][4]. The external laser source (ELS) remains off-package for reliability and serviceability, delivering high-power continuous-wave (CW) light via polarization-maintaining fiber to the photonic chiplets.

> **Central Thesis:** *WDM-aware microring resonator transmitters + distributed MEMS/silicon-photonic OCS + CPO with ELS collectively enable disaggregated, flat, sub-2W/Tbps photonics fabrics that reduce system power by 3.5× and time-to-deployment by 1.3× while enabling million-GPU scale.*

**Contributions:**

- Formal resonance, crosstalk, and thermal tuning model for cascaded MRR WDM links with Sb₂Se₃ trimming achieving full free-spectral-range (FSR) coverage without static heater power [7].
- System-level analysis of co-packaged vs socketed CPO tradeoffs: yield, repairability, fiber attach, 3.5× efficiency and 10× resiliency claims from NVIDIA Quantum-X / Spectrum-X photonics [1][6].
- Distributed OCS (dOCS) architecture mapping GPU scale-up domains to small silicon-photonic switchlets close to compute trays, reducing failure blast radius vs centralized chassis [5][9].
- Disaggregated datacenter (DDC) composition model: FPGA-based switch and interface card (SIC) for OPS/OCS/TDM/WDM programmability, validated against Google Mission Apollo [8][9].
- Empirical power, latency, insertion loss, and BER modeling with Monte-Carlo tolerance analysis.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Why Electrical I/O Fails at 1.6TbE and Beyond

Traditional pluggable transceiver path: ASIC SerDes → package substrate bump → PCB trace 8–12 inch → connector → module retimer/DSP → E/O conversion → fiber. Each transition adds reflection and loss. At 112G PAM4, insertion loss >25 dB. DSP needs 2–3W per 800G.

CPO eliminates 80% of trace length. Electrical interface becomes ultra-short reach (USR) <10 mm with <2 dB loss at 112G, allowing removal of DSP. NVIDIA reports 3.5× energy savings because laser and modulator drivers share ASIC power delivery co-optimized for 200G SerDes [1][4]. Only conversion: E-O at modulator edge of package.

### 2.2 Silicon Photonics Platform

Silicon photonic integration leverages SOI (220 nm), SiN (400 nm) for low-loss delay lines, LNOI for high-speed Mach-Zehnder, and InP for CW lasers [2]. Manufacturing ecosystem: TSMC COUPE, GlobalFoundries Fotonix, Intel, STM, Coherent vertical stack — lasers, SiPh, VCSELs, packaging [2][3].

Key metrics 2026:

- Propagation loss: 1.2 dB/cm Si strip, 0.1 dB/cm SiN
- MRR radius 5–8 µm, footprint 20 µm², Q ~12,000, extinction >18 dB [10]
- Grating coupler loss 1.5–2.5 dB per coupler, edge coupler 0.8–1.2 dB
- 200G/lane PAM4 via microring or 400G/lane InP EAM on Si

### 2.3 Disaggregation and Optics

Three levels of disaggregation per HUBER+SUHNER taxonomy [8]:

- **Fully granular:** each resource (DRAM bank, GPU) has photonic NIC for direct optical composition — requires sub-µs switching.
- **Overlay fabric:** accelerator pools interconnected via dedicated OCS fabric overlaying packet-switched Clos — most deployable.
- **Rack-scale:** reuse conventional servers but add optical top-of-rack (ToR) with SIC enabling blade-to-blade direct light paths [9].

OCS complements electrical packet switching (EPS): EPS for bursty all-to-all small flows (<10 KB), OCS for elephant few-to-few persistent flows >10% traffic [9][11]. Skewness and Volume (SV) optimization minimizes inter-rack traffic by dynamically rewiring ToR interconnects [11].

### 2.4 Wavelength Division Multiplexing

WDM aggregates N λ onto single fiber, multiplying bandwidth per fiber. Grey-light parallel optics requires N fibers; WDM reduces fiber count N×, cable congestion, and patch-panel complexity — crucial for million-GPU fabrics [2][6]. Micro-rings offer inherent wavelength selectivity without AWG.

## 3 Methodology / System Architecture

### 3.1 CPO Link Budget Model

Consider ELS → PM fiber → CPO engine (Tx) → fiber → Rx engine.

Total power at PD:

$$ P_{RX} = P_{ELS} - IL_{GC,Tx} - IL_{MRR,mod} - IL_{MUX} - IL_{fiber} - IL_{MEMS} - IL_{GC,Rx} - PP $$

Where PP = penalty from inter-symbol interference, crosstalk, RIN.

Typical numbers for 32λ × 200G = 6.4T socketed CPO module [3][4]:

| Component | Spec |
|-----------|------|
| ELS power (per fiber) | 16× 100 mW ITU CW lines, DFB array |
| MRR modulator insertion loss | 1.5–3 dB, ER 6–9 dB at 112G PAM4 |
| MRR drop filter loss | 0.8 dB |
| SiN AWG MUX loss | 1.8 dB |
| Edge coupler to fiber | 0.9 dB |
| MEMS OCS Palomar | worst 2 dB, typical 0.8 dB [9] |
| Rx grating | 2.1 dB |
| PD responsivity | 0.9 A/W Ge PD |
| Sensitivity 200G PAM4 | –8 dBm @ BER 1e-6 pre-FEC |

Total required ELS 18 dBm to meet sensitivity with 3 dB margin.

Power per bit:

$$ E_{bit} = \frac{P_{ELS}/η_{WPE} + P_{driver} + P_{TIA} + P_{heater}}{B} $$

CPO eliminates DSP (0.8 pJ/bit saving), retimer (0.4 pJ/bit), PCB trace equalization (0.5 pJ/bit). Achievable 4 pJ/bit at 1.6T vs 14 pJ/bit pluggable [1].

### 3.2 Microring Resonator Dynamics

Resonance condition:

$$ m λ_{res} = 2π R n_{eff} $$

where m integer, R radius, n_eff effective index. FSR:

$$ FSR = λ^2 / (n_g L) $$ ≈ 18–22 nm for R=5 µm at 1310 nm / 2–3 THz.

Through port transmission (single-bus ring):

$$ T = \left| \frac{τ - α e^{jθ}}{1 - τ α e^{jθ}} \right|^2 $$

τ self-coupling, κ cross-coupling (τ²+κ²=1), α round-trip loss, θ = βL phase. Q = λ/δλ ≈ π n_g L sqrt(ατ) / (λ(1-ατ)).

*Carrier-depletion PN junction*: depletion changes n_eff by Δn ≈ –3e-4 at –2 V, shifting resonance Δλ≈ 50 pm, enabling OOK/PAM4 at 40 GHz bandwidth [6][10].

*Sub-volt MOSCAP*: high-mobility ITO conductive oxide gate reduces Vpp <0.8 V, energy 8 fJ/bit, 100 Gbps OOK demonstrated [6].

Thermal drift: silicon thermo-optic coefficient dn/dT=1.8e-4 /K → 80 pm/K shift. Causes misalignment without control.

### 3.3 Trimming Without Power

Conventional heater tuning consumes 10–20 mW per ring → 640 mW for 32 λ wasting CPO budget. Non-volatile solutions:

- **Ge ion implantation:** Δn permanent trim ±0.5 nm, one-time [7].
- **Sb₂Se₃ phase-change cladding:** low-loss (k < 1e-5 in amorphous, crystalline transition Δn ≈ 0.3). Forward-biased PN pulse crystallizes film, amorphization via ns melt-quench. Enables full FSR tuning with zero static power, retaining PN modulation [7].

Four cascaded Sb₂Se₃-integrated MRRs aligned uniformly achieving 400 Gbps combined (4×100 Gbps OOK) experimentally [7].

Implementation:

```python
# Auto-align cascaded MRRs to ITU grid using vernier & Sb2Se3 trim

    m = np.round(2*np.pi*radius*neff0/target_grid)
    return 2*np.pi*radius*neff0/m

def trim_sbsese2(delta_lambda, pulse_energy=12e-12): # pJ-level
    # delta_lambda in nm, model PCM index shift
    phase = delta_lambda*2*np.pi/1.31 # rough
    # Forward bias 2V, 10us crystallize
    return f"apply {pulse_energy*1e12:.1f} pJ pulse, phase {phase:.2f}"
```

### 3.4 Distributed OCS for Scale-Up

Centralized MEMS OCS with ~1000 ports suffers ms reconfiguration, large failure domain, rack space for chassis. Distributed OCS (dOCS) [5] spreads SiPh switch chiplets (8×8 or 32×32) inside GPU trays, directly interconnecting XPU clusters. Advantages:

- **Density:** eliminates switch chassis, frees 2–3 RU per 32 GPUs.
- **Blast radius:** failure of one dOCS module impacts 8 GPUs not 512.
- **Hot-standby:** millisecond-level failover via hot-standby GPU substitution, leveraging semiconductor optical amplifier (SOA) gating for fast ns bypass.
- **Optical domain staying:** no O-E-O, preserves behavior of tightly coupled collective (AllReduce) while changing medium.

Google Palomar architecture: 2-layer MEMS mirror arrays, collimators, camera feedback, telecom-grade reliability, scaled to thousands of systems powering Jupiter fabric [9]. Reported 40% less power, 50× less downtime vs electronic spine.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Microring vs Mach-Zehnder Tradeoff

| Metric | MRR PN | MRR MOSCAP ITO | MZM Si | InP EAM on Si |
|--------|--------|---------------|--------|--------------|
| Footprint | 150 µm² | 200 µm² | 2 mm² | 120 µm² |
| VπL / Vpp | –2 V, 40 GHz | 0.5 V, 35 GHz | 1.5 Vcm | 0.9 V, 100 GHz |
| IL | 2 dB | 1.2 dB | 3.5 dB | 2.2 dB |
| WDM native | Yes (comb filter) | Yes | No, needs AWG | No |
| Energy | 20 fJ/bit | 8 fJ/bit | 200 fJ/bit | 15 fJ/bit |
| T sensitivity | High, 80 pm/K | High | Moderate | Low |

MRR wins density and WDM nativity but needs trimming. Q trade: high Q → high ER but narrow linewidth <20 GHz limits 100G PAM4. Optimal Q ~5k–12k for 100G.

### 4.2 Polarization and Fabrication Variation

Fabrication CD ±5 nm causes resonance shift ±1–2 nm > channel spacing (0.8 nm for 100 GHz). Sb₂Se₃ solves shift without continuous power [7]. Polarization-diversity schemes: PSR + duplicate rings, or 2D grating coupler, achieving polarization-dependent loss <1 dB demonstrated for 10×10 Gbps WDM add-drop [12].

### 4.3 dOCS + SIC Programmability

FPGA-based SIC [10] replaces NIC: plugs into blade, provides programmable OPS/OCS/TDM/WDM engine. Pushes functionality from ToR to server, eliminating ToR electronics for direct intrarack blade-to-blade optical communication, enabling chip-to-chip µs latency [10].

Architecture-on-Demand (AoD) backplane uses spectrum-selective switches (SSS) with liquid crystal on silicon (LCoS) to slice spectrum.

### 4.4 Control Plane and Pacemaker

HotStuff-like pacemaker synchronizes view after GST for BFT but analogous for OCS reconfiguration controller: centralized SDN computes new matching via SV minimization MILP, pushes to OCS via NETCONF in <10 ms, data plane follows. Fast failover via precomputed backup paths.

TLA+ spec for 1+1 ELS redundancy:

```tla
---- MODULE ELSRedundancy ----
VARIABLES elsPrimary, elsBackup, active, wavelengths
TypeOk == active \in {"primary","backup"}
Failover == elsPrimary = "down" /\ active = "primary"
            /\ active' = "backup" /\ UNCHANGED <<elsPrimary, elsBackup>>
Liveness == []<>(active = "backup" => <>(elsPrimary = "down"))
====
```

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Testbed: 6.4T Socketed CPO

Coherent demonstrated three CPO paths at OFC 2026 [3]:

1. 32×200G SiPh socketed CPO + ELS module with high-power InP CW lasers — baseline for NVIDIA.
2. Multimode socketed CPO with VCSELs for <100 m reach.
3. InP modulator on Si operating 400G/lane — pathway to 3.2T per engine.

Measured TX TDECQ <3 dB at 212.5 Gbps PAM4, Rx sensitivity –7.5 dBm.

### 5.2 System-Level Gains

From NVIDIA and Google field data [1][4][9]:

- **Power:** Pluggable 800G 14 W (DSP 3W) → CPO 800G 5.5 W (no DSP, USR IO 0.5W). At 51.2T switch, saving 2.1 kW per switch. Data center with 1k switches: 2.1 MW saved → 3.5× efficiency claim justified after including ELS wall-plug (20% efficiency).
- **Resiliency:** Pluggable has 4 connector mating points, 2% annual fail; CPO eliminates faceplate connectors, ELS 1+1 redundancy reduces link failure rate 10× → 5× sustained application runtime [4].
- **Deployment:** Co-packaged simplifies fiber shuffle, pre-terminated fiber array reduces installation 1.3× faster.
- **Palomar Insertion Loss:** 2 dB worst-case → requires Tx power +2 dBm penalty, compensated by high-power ELS [9]. Downtime 50× lower due to solid-state no packet buffer SRAM SEU.

### 5.3 WDM Scaling Argument

Proof sketch: For N=32 channels at 200G with 100 GHz spacing (0.8 nm) in O-band 1270–1330 nm, total window 25.6 nm < FSR 20 nm? Need 2 FSR groups or interleaver using both C and L groups. Use dual FSR rings R1=5.0 µm, R2=5.15 µm vernier expands effective FSR to 80 nm (FSR1×FSR2/|FSR1-FSR2|). Hence 64λ reachable.

Data rate: 32×200=6.4T per engine, 8 engines per switch ASIC package =51.2T.

### 5.4 Latency

Electrical Clos 3-hop: 400 ns per hop switch + 20 ns SerDes + 60 ns FEC = ~1.4 µs p50. Flat OCS fabric: propagation 5 ns/m ×15 m=75 ns + MEMS negligible (static circuit) + 0 ns FEC (can use lighter FEC with optical path low crosstalk) = 75 ns + 2 switch bypasses = <200 ns, 7× reduction [5][8].

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Laser reliability & supply:** ELS external but still single-point massive failure; need 1+1 or N+1 with automatic polarization alignment; field-replaceable ELS module must not require fiber re-splicing. High-power 16× 200 mW DFB array thermal crosstalk remains [3].
2. **Fiber attach yield:** 2D fiber array attach to SiPh chip requires active alignment ±0.5 µm, CTE mismatch 2.6 ppm/K vs 0.6 for Si → delamination at 85°C operation.
3. **MRR control complexity:** Even with Sb₂Se₃ non-volatile trimming, dynamic thermal drift from ASIC hotspot 3–10°C gradient shifts resonance 0.24–0.8 nm (300× linewidth). Differential heater + feedback loop (5 kHz) using drop-port tap PD required → complexity. Phase-change cyclability fatigue 10⁷ cycles may degrade after years of re-tuning.
4. **OCS switching speed vs topology:** MEMS ms switching incompatible with µs-scale job reconfiguration for disaggregated memory Pool; nanosecond SiPh switches (MZI mesh, microring crossbar) target but insertion loss 5–8 dB limits cascading to <8 ports without SOA. Soliton microcomb-driven ultrafast OCS promises <93 ns for 576-byte packets but needs integrated comb source stabilization [13].
5. **Testing & serviceability:** Socketed CPO improves field-replaceability vs fully co-packaged; yet still tighter integration than pluggable transceivers; data center technician can't replace individual lane.
6. **Cost:** TSMC COUPE interposer cost >2× organic substrate; economic crossover when system power savings OPEX amortized over 3-year DC life — justification stronger for AI factories than generic cloud [2].
7. **Standardization:** IEEE 802.3dj, OIF 3.2T CEI-200G-USR define electrical USR interface; optical ELS interoperability missing; no multi-vendor ELS spec yet.

> **Open Problem 1:** Can co-integrated comb laser + MRR transmitter eliminate discrete CW lasers? Locking 32 lines with 20 MHz stability across 0–70°C.

> **Open Problem 2:** ML-learned thermal control of MRR banks using workload predictors to anticipate ASIC thermal gradient and pre-shift resonance, bounding tuning power <1 mW/ring.

> **Open Problem 3:** dOCS routing with failure prediction: predictive failure of SiPh switchlet using photocurrent drift telemetry to trigger hot-standby before catastrophic loss.

> **Open Problem 4:** Formal equivalence between disaggregated optical topology reconfiguration and satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) for composition constraints (CPU:DRAM:GPU ratio <1:8:1, latency <500ns).

## 7 Conclusion

Co-packaged optics with silicon photonic microring WDM transceivers and OCS fabrics transitions the datacenter from copper-limited hierarchical Clos to flat, disaggregated, power-efficient AI factories. Microring resonators provide compact, low-energy, inherently WDM-selective modulation when combined with non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ trimming to conquer fabrication variation without static power; dense WDM via comb sources multiplies per-fiber bandwidth 32× to 6.4T per socketed module; distributed MEMS/silicon-photonic OCS removes spine layer, delivering 40% lower power and 50× better uptime at million-GPU scale; integration around the ASIC package collapses electrical reach to deliver claimed 3.5× efficiency, 10× resiliency, 1.3× deployment velocity validated by NVIDIA Spectrum-X/Quantum-X photonics and Google Palomar field data.

Remaining challenges — fiber attach yield, thermal tracking, sub-µs switching for memory disaggregation, and ELS standardization — define a roadmap where photonics ceases to be an add-on transceiver and becomes the substrate of computation itself. As foundries scale CPO to 2028–2030 volume deployment [2], the measure shifts from raw bandwidth to **useful bandwidth per watt per dollar** after composition, resilience, and operational overhead — metrics where CPO + WDM + OCS dominates pluggable incumbent by generational margin.

> Future AI infrastructure is not built from switches connected by optics; it is built from optics interconnected by switches.
