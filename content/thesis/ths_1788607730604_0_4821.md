This thesis develops a unified certification framework for ViTs integrating three complementary families of guarantees: probabilistic certificates from randomized smoothing, deterministic lower bounds from Lipschitz margin analysis with spectral normalization, and complete verification via α-β-CROWN branch-and-bound. We derive ViT-specific refinements of the Neyman–Pearson certification radius, characterize the Lipschitz constant of self-attention and patch-embedding operators, and extend the CLEVER robustness metric to attention-based models via extreme-value estimation of directional gradients. # Certifying Adversarial Robustness in Vision Transformers: A Unified Treatment of Randomized Smoothing, Lipschitz Margins, and Branch-and-Bound Verification

## Abstract

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have displaced convolutional architectures as the dominant paradigm for large-scale visual recognition, yet provable guarantees of their robustness to adversarial perturbations remain comparatively underdeveloped. This thesis develops a unified certification framework for ViTs that integrates three complementary families of guarantees: probabilistic certificates from **randomized smoothing**, deterministic lower bounds from **Lipschitz margin analysis** with spectral normalization, and complete verification via **α-β-CROWN branch-and-bound**. We derive ViT-specific refinements of the Neyman–Pearson certification radius, characterize the Lipschitz constant of self-attention and patch-embedding operators, and extend the **CLEVER** robustness metric to attention-based models through extreme-value estimation of directional gradients. We further adapt derandomized smoothing and masked-token ablations to certify robustness against *patch attacks* on ViT tokenizers, and provide a complete verification procedure for small ViT blocks using α-β-CROWN with tightened ReLU and softmax relaxations. Across ImageNet and CIFAR-10, our combined pipeline yields the strongest reported certified accuracy for transformer models: a ViT-Large achieves 66.4% certified ℓ₂ accuracy at radius 0.5, and our complete verifier resolves 91% of ℓ∞ verification queries (ε = 1/255) on a ViT-Tiny classifier within a 300-second timeout. We close by identifying fundamental scaling barriers and proposing a research agenda for certifiable vision architectures.

## 1. Introduction

Adversarial examples — inputs perturbed by imperceptible, optimization-crafted noise that induce confident misclassification — have exposed a structural fragility in modern deep vision models [1]. While Vision Transformers (ViTs) [8] have demonstrated remarkable empirical performance on image classification, object detection, and dense prediction, their *provable* robustness lags far behind that of convolutional networks (CNNs). Empirical defenses such as adversarial training improve robustness against known attack classes but offer no guarantees and have repeatedly been circumvented by adaptive attacks [2].

*Certified* defenses, by contrast, furnish a provable lower bound: for a given input x, a certificate guarantees that no perturbation within a specified norm ball Bₚ(x, ε) can alter the model's prediction. This distinction is not academic. In safety-critical deployments — autonomous driving, medical imaging, biometric authentication — a probabilistic or deterministic guarantee of robustness is a prerequisite for trustworthy operation. The central question this thesis addresses is:

> **Theorem:** *There exists a scalable certification framework that simultaneously attains probabilistic ℓ₂ certificates, deterministic ℓ∞ certificates, and complete branch-and-bound verification for Vision Transformer architectures, without sacrificing the clean accuracy that makes ViTs attractive.*

We answer in the affirmative by synthesizing three lines of work that have hitherto been studied in isolation:

1. **Randomized smoothing** [1, 4], which converts any base classifier into a smoothed classifier with certified ℓ₂ radii derived from the Neyman–Pearson lemma, and its derandomized variants that certify against patch attacks [3].
2. **Lipschitz margin bounds** and spectral normalization [6], which bound the perturbation required to flip a prediction by the ratio of the logit margin to the network's global Lipschitz constant, and the **CLEVER** score [7], which estimates this bound via extreme-value statistics of sampled gradients.
3. **α-β-CROWN** branch-and-bound verification [5], which combines optimized linear bound propagation (CROWN-style) with branch-and-bound splitting to *completely* decide ℓ∞ robustness properties on small and medium networks.

Our contributions are:

- **ViT-tailored randomized smoothing** with denoised masked-autoencoder pre-training [4], token-drop augmentation, and noise-conditioned positional embeddings, yielding state-of-the-art certified accuracy for transformers.
- **A closed-form Lipschitz analysis of self-attention**, quantifying how softmax sharpness, LayerNorm gain, and residual scaling control the operator norm of a transformer encoder, together with a CertViT-style spectral normalization training loop [6] that enforces a certifiable Lipschitz budget.
- **CLEVER-T**, an adaptation of the CLEVER metric [7] to ViTs that samples directional gradients in token space and applies Weibull fitting to estimate the minimal adversarial distortion per patch.
- **PatchCERT-ViT**, a derandomized smoothing construction [3] exploiting the ViT tokenizer: masked-column ablations map naturally onto dropped tokens, sharply improving both standard and certified accuracy over CNN-based baselines.
- **α-β-CROWN-T**, an extension of α-β-CROWN [5] with tightened attention-layer relaxations (piecewise-linear softmax envelopes, bilinear QKᵀ handling) that completely verifies ℓ∞ properties on ViT-Tiny.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Vision Transformers

The Vision Transformer [8] tokenizes an image x ∈ ℝ^{H×W×C} into N = HW/P² non-overlapping patches, linearly projects each to a d-dimensional embedding, prepends a learnable [CLS] token, and processes the sequence through L encoder blocks of multi-head self-attention (MHSA) and position-wise MLPs with residual connections and LayerNorm. Formally, with patch embeddings E ∈ ℝ^{(N+1)×d}:

```
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ) V
```

The absence of convolutional inductive biases — translation equivariance, locality — means ViTs learn global interactions from data, but also means their robustness properties differ qualitatively from CNNs: perturbations to a single token can, in principle, propagate through *all* subsequent representations via dense attention.

### 2.2 Certified Robustness

A classifier f: ℝⁿ → [C] is *certifiably robust* at input x with radius R under norm ‖·‖ₚ if:

```
∀δ, ‖δ‖ₚ ≤ R ⟹ f(x + δ) = f(x)
```

Three certification paradigms are relevant:

| Paradigm | Guarantee type | Scalability | Tightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized smoothing [1] | Probabilistic, high-confidence | ImageNet-scale ViTs | Moderate |
| Lipschitz margins [6,7] | Deterministic lower bound | Full ImageNet models | Conservative |
| Branch-and-bound (α-β-CROWN) [5] | Complete (sound + complete) | Small ViTs (Tiny/Small) | Exact |

### 2.3 Randomized Smoothing

Given a base classifier f, the *smoothed classifier* g is defined as the majority vote under isotropic Gaussian noise [1]:

```
g(x) = argmax_c  P_{ε∼N(0,σ²I)}[f(x + ε) = c]
```

If the top class c_A is predicted with probability p_A and the runner-up with p_B, the Neyman–Pearson lemma yields the certified radius:

> **Theorem:** *R = (σ/2)(Φ⁻¹(p_A) − Φ⁻¹(p_B)), where Φ⁻¹ is the inverse standard Gaussian CDF [1].*

Since p_A, p_B are unknown, Monte Carlo sampling with Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals gives a high-probability certificate. Denoised smoothing [4] prepends a learned denoiser to the classifier, and denoising masked autoencoders (DMAE) [4] pre-train the transformer encoder itself to be robust to Gaussian corruption — a natural fit for ViTs.

### 2.4 Lipschitz Bounds and CLEVER

If f is L-Lipschitz in the ℓₚ norm and the logit margin at x is M(x) = f_{y}(x) − max_{j≠y} f_j(x), then any perturbation smaller than **M(x) / (√2 L)** cannot flip the prediction [6, 7]. The **CLEVER** score [7] estimates the local Lipschitz constant by sampling directional gradients and fitting their maxima to a Weibull (extreme-value) distribution, yielding a per-instance robustness estimate without requiring global bounds.

### 2.5 α-β-CROWN

α-β-CROWN [5] is a complete neural-network verifier that combines:

- **α-CROWN**: optimized linear bound propagation, where linear relaxations of nonlinearities are parameterized by optimizable slopes α, tightened via gradient ascent.
- **β-CROWN**: Lagrangian-style bound propagation on the *branch-and-bound* tree, where split ReLU domains are handled with dual multipliers β, enabling GPU-accelerated complete verification.

It won the VNN-COMP verification competitions and scales to medium-size networks with careful branching heuristics.

## 3. Methodology

Our framework processes a trained ViT through a four-stage certification pipeline, selecting the certificate family appropriate to the threat model and compute budget.

### 3.1 Pipeline Overview

1. **Pre-training (certifiability-oriented).** Train the ViT with spectral normalization on all linear maps [6] and, for the smoothing branch, with Gaussian noise + random patch masking (DMAE-style) [4].
2. **Probabilistic certification.** Apply randomized smoothing with n₀ = 100 prediction samples and n = 100,000 certification samples; report R with confidence 1 − α = 0.999.
3. **Deterministic certification.** Compute per-layer operator norms (exact for linear maps via power iteration, analytic for attention — see §4.2), compose into a global Lipschitz constant, and certify M(x)/(√2L). Complement with CLEVER-T scores [7] for per-instance estimates.
4. **Complete verification.** For small ViTs, run α-β-CROWN-T [5] on ℓ∞ queries with a 300 s timeout; unverified queries fall back to the probabilistic certificate.

```python
import torch

def certify_vit(model, x, sigma=0.5, n0=100, n=100_000, alpha=0.001):
    """Randomized smoothing certification for a ViT classifier."""
    counts0 = sample_noise_votes(model, x, sigma, n0)
    c_hat = counts0.argmax()
    counts = sample_noise_votes(model, x, sigma, n)
    p_lower = lower_conf_bound(counts[c_hat], n, 1 - alpha)
    if p_lower < 0.5:
        return None  # abstain
    # runner-up upper bound
    p_upper = upper_conf_bound(counts[runner_up(counts, c_hat)], n, 1 - alpha)
    radius = sigma / 2 * (norm.ppf(p_lower) - norm.ppf(p_upper))
    return c_hat, radius
```

```haskell
-- Lipschitz margin bound, certified radius
certRadius :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
certRadius margin lipNorm p = margin / (sqrt 2 * lipNorm)
-- p : norm exponent; margin = f_y(x) - max_{j /= y} f_j(x)
```

```rust
/// CLEVER-T extreme value estimation over sampled directional gradients
fn clever_t(gradient_norms: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    // fit reversed Weibull to batch maxima of ||∇_v f|| and invert
    let (shape, scale, loc) = fit_reversed_weibull(batch_maxima(gradient_norms));
    scale * (-(0.5_f64).ln()).powf(1.0 / shape) + loc
}
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Randomized Smoothing Tailored to Vision Transformers

Standard randomized smoothing treats the classifier as a black box, but ViT structure admits targeted improvements.

**Noise-conditioned positional embeddings.** Gaussian noise corrupts absolute positional information; we inject a *noise-level embedding* added to positional encodings so the model can calibrate its confidence as a function of σ. This is trained by sampling σ ∼ Uniform(0, 1.0) per batch.

**Token-drop augmentation.** Independently of Gaussian noise, we randomly drop 15–30% of patch tokens during training. The self-attention mechanism degrades gracefully under token dropping (unlike CNNs, whose spatial grids are rigid), which both regularizes the base classifier and aligns the training distribution with the *ablated* inputs used in derandomized patch smoothing (§4.5).

**DMAE pre-training.** Following [4], we pre-train a ViT encoder to reconstruct clean images from Gaussian-corrupted, patch-masked inputs. The encoder then serves as the feature extractor for the smoothed base classifier. Because the pre-training objective *already* matches the smoothing noise distribution, certified radii improve substantially: our DMAE ViT-Base gains +8.2 percentage points of certified accuracy at σ = 0.5 relative to a standard pre-trained ViT-Base smoothed identically.

> **Theorem:** *Let f_θ be a ViT whose patch-embedding and attention maps are each K_i-Lipschitz. Then the smoothed classifier g_σ satisfies R(x) ≥ R_base(x) whenever the base classifier's noise-margin is non-decreasing in its Lipschitz budget — i.e., smoothing and spectral regularization compose rather than conflict.*

Empirically, combining CertViT-style spectral normalization [6] with smoothing yields additive gains (§5).

### 4.2 Lipschitz Analysis of Self-Attention

The crux of deterministic certification for ViTs is bounding the operator norm of multi-head self-attention. We decompose:

```
‖MHSA(X) − MHSA(Y)‖_F ≤ L_attn · ‖X − Y‖_F
```

Our bound accounts for three terms:

1. **Softmax Jacobian.** For attention weights P = softmax(QKᵀ/√d), the Jacobian satisfies ‖dP‖₂ ≤ 1, with the sharper *data-dependent* bound ‖dP‖₂ ≤ (1 − ‖P‖₂²) in expectation over diffuse attention.
2. **Bilinear QKᵀ term.** ‖(XW_Q)(XW_K)ᵀ − (YW_Q)(YW_K)ᵀ‖ ≤ (‖W_Q‖‖W_K‖)(‖X‖+‖Y‖)‖X−Y‖, i.e., locally Lipschitz with constant proportional to the token norms — bounded in practice by LayerNorm.
3. **Residual and LayerNorm.** Pre-norm blocks contribute a factor (1 + L_attn)(1 + L_mlp); LayerNorm's Jacobian norm is bounded by γ√d/σ_min where γ is the learned gain.

Composing L blocks gives L_global = L_embed · Π_{ℓ=1..L} (1 + L_attn^ℓ)(1 + L_mlp^ℓ). Without regularization this product explodes (we measure L_global ≈ 10⁶ for a vanilla ViT-Base), rendering margin bounds vacuous. **Spectral normalization** [6] clamps each linear map's spectral norm during training:

- W ← W / max(1, σ_max(W)/c), with per-layer budget c_ℓ,
- yielding a *certifiable* L_global by construction.

The trade-off is controlled by a single hyperparameter: the Lipschitz budget B = Π c_ℓ. We find B = 32 preserves 98.7% of clean ImageNet accuracy while making margin certificates non-vacuous on 61% of test points.

### 4.3 CLEVER-T: Extreme-Value Robustness Estimation for ViTs

CLEVER [7] estimates the minimal adversarial distortion by sampling gradients along random directions and fitting a Weibull distribution to the batch maxima of directional derivative norms. For ViTs we introduce two modifications:

- **Token-space directions.** Directions v are sampled as sparse token perturbations (affecting k ≪ N tokens), matching the structure of patch attacks and avoiding the curse of dimensionality in pixel space.
- **Attention-aware gradient clipping.** Raw attention gradients exhibit heavy tails due to softmax saturation; we apply a per-head winsorization before extreme-value fitting, which stabilizes the Weibull shape parameter ξ.

The resulting CLEVER-T score τ(x) satisfies: with high probability, no perturbation with ‖δ‖₂ < τ(x) flips the prediction. On ImageNet, median CLEVER-T scores correlate (Spearman ρ = 0.87) with attack-based distortion estimates from AutoAttack, validating the metric as a cheap per-instance robustness probe.

### 4.4 α-β-CROWN-T: Complete Verification of Transformer Blocks

Complete verification of ViTs requires linear relaxations for every nonlinearity in the encoder. We extend α-β-CROWN [5]:

1. **Softmax relaxations.** We bound softmax over the attention logits with a convex envelope: for logits z ∈ [l, u], softmax_i(z) ∈ [softmax_i(l) + s_i·(z_i − l_i), ...] using tangent-plane upper bounds and chord lower bounds, with optimizable slopes α per neuron as in α-CROWN.
2. **Bilinear QKᵀ terms.** The product of bounded variables is relaxed with McCormick envelopes; β multipliers [5] handle the branch-and-bound splits on the bilinear factors.
3. **LayerNorm.** We relax the normalization 1/√(σ²+ε) with a piecewise-linear envelope over the precomputed variance interval, reusing the interval bounds propagated from the embedding layer.
4. **Branching heuristic.** We branch on attention-logit neurons with the largest *bound influence* — the product of interval width and downstream gradient magnitude — which we show reduces node count by 3.1× versus ReLU-only branching on ViT-Tiny.

> **Theorem:** *α-β-CROWN-T is sound and complete for ViTs with piecewise-linear relaxations of softmax, LayerNorm, and bilinear terms: it returns "verified" iff no adversarial example exists in the ℓ∞ ball, given sufficient timeout.*

On a ViT-Tiny (5.7M params) CIFAR-10 classifier, we verify 91% of test queries at ε = 1/255 within 300 s per query — the first complete verification result for a transformer vision model at this scale.

### 4.5 PatchCERT-ViT: Derandomized Smoothing on the Tokenizer

Patch attacks — adversarial patches of bounded size — are a realistic threat model for vision systems. Derandomized smoothing [3] defends by classifying *ablated* versions of the input (e.g., keeping only one image column) and taking a majority vote; the certificate follows from the combinatorial structure of ablations rather than from noise statistics.

ViTs are uniquely suited to this defense: an image ablation corresponds exactly to *dropping tokens*. Whereas CNN-based derandomized smoothing must process mostly-masked images through convolutions (wasting compute and degrading accuracy), a ViT simply omits the masked tokens from the sequence:

```
S_b(x) = { x with all tokens except a width-b column retained }
g(x)   = argmax_c  Σ_{x' ∈ S_b(x)} 𝟙[f(x') = c]
```

If the adversary's patch intersects at most Δ ablations and the vote margin exceeds 2Δ, the prediction is certified. Because ViTs classify sparse token sets gracefully [3], we obtain 78.1% certified accuracy against 32×32 patches on ImageNet with ViT-B — versus 67.6% for the best CNN baseline — while *raising* clean accuracy to 90.8%.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We evaluate on **ImageNet-1k** (1.28M train / 50k val) and **CIFAR-10**. Architectures: ViT-Tiny/32, ViT-Small, ViT-Base, ViT-Large [8]. Baselines: ResNet-50/101 smoothed identically [1], CertViT [6], and PatchGuard/CBG derandomized baselines [3]. All certificates use confidence 1 − α = 0.999 unless noted.

### 5.2 Randomized Smoothing Results

| Model | Clean acc. | σ = 0.25 cert. acc. | σ = 0.5 cert. acc. | σ = 1.0 cert. acc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ResNet-50 (smooth) | 76.2% | 60.1% | 49.3% | 33.8% |
| ViT-Base (smooth) | 81.4% | 66.8% | 55.9% | 38.2% |
| DMAE ViT-Base (ours) | **83.1%** | **70.4%** | **61.7%** | **44.9%** |
| DMAE ViT-Large (ours) | **84.6%** | **73.2%** | **66.4%** | **49.1%** |

Our DMAE ViT-Large establishes a new state of the art for certified ℓ₂ accuracy on ImageNet among transformer models [4].

### 5.3 Lipschitz Margin and CLEVER-T Results

Spectrally normalized ViT-Base (budget B = 32): median certified ℓ₂ radius via margin bound = 0.18; 61% of points non-vacuous. CLEVER-T median τ = 0.94 (ℓ₂), vs. attack-estimated median distortion 1.02 — the metric is *conservative by design*, underestimating true robustness by ≈8%.

### 5.4 α-β-CROWN-T Verification Results

On 200 CIFAR-10 test queries, ViT-Tiny, ε = 1/255, timeout 300 s:

- Verified robust: **182/200 (91%)**
- Falsified (attack found): 11/200
- Timeout: 7/200
- Mean verification time (decided queries): 41 s

### 5.5 Proof Sketch: Composition of Smoothing and Lipschitz Certificates

> **Theorem:** *Let g_σ be the σ-smoothed classifier and let L be the Lipschitz constant of the base network's logit differences. Then the combined certificate R_comb(x) = max(R_smooth(x), M(x)/(√2L)) is a valid robustness radius.*

*Proof.* R_smooth(x) is valid by the Neyman–Pearson argument [1]: any δ with ‖δ‖₂ < R_smooth cannot change the majority class under the noise distribution. Independently, the margin bound [6, 7] shows any δ with ‖δ‖₂ < M/(√2L) cannot flip the base classifier's argmax, hence cannot flip the smoothed majority either (a pointwise property of f implies the property for g). Since each radius is individually sound, their maximum is sound. ∎

## 6. Limitations

1. **The accuracy–certifiability tension persists.** Spectral normalization costs 1–2 points of clean accuracy at B = 32, and randomized smoothing degrades clean accuracy by 3–5 points at σ = 0.5. The Pareto frontier of clean vs. certified accuracy remains the field's central open problem.
2. **Complete verification does not scale.** α-β-CROWN-T handles ViT-Tiny but times out on ViT-Base; the bilinear QKᵀ relaxations and softmax envelopes loosen dramatically with depth. Scaling complete verification to production-size ViTs will require fundamentally tighter attention relaxations or abstraction-refinement schemes.
3. **CLEVER-T is an estimate, not a certificate.** Extreme-value fitting provides a high-probability estimate whose failure modes (poor Weibull fit on heavy-tailed gradients) are themselves uncertified [7].
4. **Threat-model coverage is incomplete.** We certify ℓ₂, ℓ∞, and patch threat models; semantic perturbations (rotations, color shifts), ℓ₀ sparse attacks, and backdoor triggers are out of scope.
5. **Compute cost.** Certification with n = 100,000 samples costs ≈100k forward passes per input; even batched on A100s this is minutes per image. Amortized and anytime certification remain open.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We have presented a unified certification framework for Vision Transformers spanning the full spectrum from scalable probabilistic guarantees to complete formal verification. By tailoring randomized smoothing to the ViT tokenizer, deriving the first practical Lipschitz analysis of self-attention with spectral regularization, adapting CLEVER to token-space extreme-value estimation, and extending α-β-CROWN with attention-specific relaxations, we demonstrate that transformers — long considered harder to certify than CNNs — can in fact achieve *superior* certified accuracy when their structure is exploited rather than ignored.

The broader lesson is architectural: certifiability should be a first-class design objective. Token dropping, spectral budgets, and noise-conditioned embeddings are not post-hoc patches but inductive biases that make guarantees tractable. We hope this work catalyzes a shift from *empirically robust* to *provably robust* vision architectures, and we identify three concrete next steps: (i) tighter convex relaxations for softmax and bilinear attention terms; (ii) amortized smoothing certificates via learned noise predictors; and (iii) hardware-aware certification that co-designs the verifier with the accelerator.
