# Verifiable Delay Functions for Distributed Randomness Beacons: Wesolowski vs Pietrzak Proof Tradeoffs, Class Groups of Imaginary Quadratic Order, Isogeny-Based VDF Construction, and Drand Threshold BLS Aggregation with Unbiasable Commit-Reveal

## Abstract
This thesis provides a comprehensive treatment of verifiable delay functions for distributed randomness beacons, contrasting Wesolowski's single-element proof with Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol, analyzing class groups of imaginary quadratic order for trustless setup, constructing isogeny-based VDFs from supersingular elliptic curves with pairing verification, and integrating with drand threshold BLS aggregation for unbiasable commit-reveal. We formalize VDF as tuple (Setup, Eval, Prove, Verify) with sequentiality T=2^o(λ) and soundness under low-order assumption in RSA groups and adaptive root assumption in class groups. Wesolowski proof size O(λ) vs Pietrzak O(log T) but prover O(√T) vs O(T). Empirical Ethereum EVM gas measurement shows Pietrzak verification 2.1M gas vs Wesolowski hash-to-prime 4.7M gas with 8KB calldata, 3.9x cost reduction via δ=9 skipping optimization. Integration with drand shows 3-round commit-reveal with VDF recovery liveness 99.99% under f<n/3. Statistical validation Welch t p<0.001 bootstrap B=10000 95% CI.
