# Information Geometry and the Natural Gradient: Fisher–Rao Metric, Dually Flat Manifolds, α-Connections, and Amari's Dual Affine Geometry

## Abstract

Information geometry reframes statistical inference and learning as Riemannian and affine geometry on the manifold of probability distributions. This thesis develops the dual affine geometry of statistical manifolds: the Fisher–Rao metric as the canonical Riemannian structure, unique up to monotone transformations under Markov morphisms; the one-parameter family of α-connections interpolating between the exponential (α = +1) and mixture (α = −1) connections; and Amari's dual affine framework of dually flat manifolds equipped with Legendre-dual affine coordinates and potential functions. We derive the dual flatness and Pythagorean theorems, the generalized Pythagorean geometry of canonical divergences, and the information-geometric construction of the natural gradient as the Riemannian gradient under the Fisher metric. We then analyze the practical realization of natural gradient descent through Kronecker-factored curvature approximations (K-FAC) and survey theoretical guarantees: Fisher efficiency, reparameterization invariance, plateau avoidance, and recent global convergence results for overparameterized networks. We close with limitations — computational cost, empirical versus expected Fisher pathologies, damping-induced bias — and open problems in non-parametric and quantum information geometry.

## 1 Intro

Classical statistics treats a parametric model $S = \{p(x; \xi) : \xi \in \Xi\}$ as a set of functions indexed by a parameter vector $\xi$. The foundational insight of information geometry, pioneered by Rao [7] and developed into a full differential-geometric edifice by Chentsov and Amari [4], is that $S$ is naturally a *differentiable manifold* whose geometry is dictated by the structure of statistical inference itself rather than by any arbitrary coordinate choice. Distinguishability of nearby distributions, invariance under sufficient statistics, and the dual roles of parameters-as-averages and parameters-as-generators conspire to produce a uniquely determined Riemannian metric and a one-parameter family of affine connections with a subtle duality structure.

This thesis is organized around four pillars. **First**, the Fisher–Rao metric $g_{ij}(\xi)$, the unique (up to monotone rescaling) metric invariant under sufficient statistics, which converts parameter space into a Riemannian manifold whose geodesics measure statistical distinguishability [6]. **Second**, the Amari–Chentsov $\alpha$-family of connections $\nabla^{(\alpha)}$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, interpolating between the $e$-connection ($\alpha = +1$), for which exponential families are flat, and the $m$-connection ($\alpha = -1$), for which mixture families are flat [5]. **Third**, Amari's dual affine geometry: a metric $g$ together with a pair of torsion-free dual connections yields, in the flat case, two Legendre-dual affine coordinate systems $(\theta, \eta)$ and potential functions $(\psi, \varphi)$ that make the manifold computationally tractable as a convex-analytic object [4][5]. **Fourth**, the natural gradient $\tilde{\nabla} L = F^{-1} \nabla L$, the Riemannian gradient under the Fisher metric, which Amari proved is Fisher efficient — asymptotically matching optimal batch estimation — and which corrects ordinary gradient descent's dependence on parameterization [1].

Our contribution is a self-contained synthesis: we derive the key theorems (dual flatness, generalized Pythagoras, canonical divergence geometry) with proofs, present the α-connection formalism in coordinate-free and coordinate forms, and connect the geometry to modern deep-learning practice through K-FAC approximations [2] and recent convergence theory [8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The statistical manifold and the Fisher–Rao metric

Let $S = \{p_\xi = p(\cdot; \xi) : \xi = (\xi^1, \ldots, \xi^n) \in \Xi \subset \mathbb{R}^n\}$ be a regular parametric family of probability densities (or positive measures) on a sample space $\mathcal{X}$. Assuming smoothness and mild regularity, the tangent space $T_{p_\xi} S$ is identified with score-like functions

$$A = \sum_i A^i \partial_i \ell, \qquad \ell(x; \xi) = \log p(x; \xi), \quad \partial_i = \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi^i},$$

and the **Fisher information matrix** defines the inner product [4][7]:

> **Definition (Fisher–Rao metric).**
> $$g_{ij}(\xi) = \mathbb{E}_{p_\xi}\left[\partial_i \ell \cdot \partial_j \ell\right] = \mathbb{E}_{p_\xi}\left[\frac{\partial \log p(x; \xi)}{\partial \xi^i} \frac{\partial \log p(x; \xi)}{\partial \xi^j}\right],$$
> and equivalently $g_{ij} = -\mathbb{E}_{p_\xi}[\partial_i \partial_j \ell]$ under regularity. The pair $(S, g)$ is the Fisher–Rao Riemannian manifold.

The metric is not arbitrary. Chentsov's theorem (1972) proved that the Fisher metric is, up to a positive constant, the *only* Riemannian metric on the probability simplex invariant under sufficient statistics (Markov morphisms) [4]. This uniqueness theorem is the geometric analogue of the Fisher–Neyman factorization: it says distinguishability measured by sufficient statistics selects a canonical geometry, and that geometry is $g$.

### 2.2 Statistical quantities with geometric faces

The geometry encodes statistics faithfully. For a random variable $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ with expectation functional $\bar{f}(p) = \mathbb{E}_p[f]$, one has $\|\mathrm{d}\bar{f}\|^2_g = \mathrm{Var}_p(f)$, linking variance to gradient norm; applied to submanifolds, this yields the Cramér–Rao lower bound as a geometric inequality [7]. Concretely, for an unbiased estimator, the bound $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\xi}) \succeq g^{-1}$ is the statement that no estimator moves through the manifold faster than the metric allows.

### 2.3 Divergences and the geometry they induce

A **divergence** $D: S \times S \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies $D(p:q) \ge 0$ with equality iff $p = q$, but need not be symmetric nor satisfy the triangle inequality [4]. The canonical example is the Kullback–Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(p:q) = \mathbb{E}_p[\log(p/q)]$. Every divergence induces a Riemannian metric and a pair of dual affine connections via Eguchi's construction: expand $D(p(\xi):p(\xi + d\xi))$ to second and third order; the quadratic term yields $g_{ij}$, the cubic terms yield the dual connection coefficients [4][5]. The geometry is thus not postulated — it is *derived* from the divergence that measures distinguishability.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodological stance is that the dualistic structure $(g, \nabla, \nabla^*)$ — a Riemannian metric plus a pair of torsion-free connections dual with respect to $g$ — is the primitive object of information geometry, more fundamental than the Riemannian metric alone [5].

> **Definition (Dual connections).** Two affine connections $\nabla, \nabla^*$ are *dual* with respect to $g$ if for all vector fields $X, Y, Z$,
> $$Xg(Y,Z) = g(\nabla_X Y, Z) + g(Y, \nabla^*_X Z).$$

From a dualistic structure one constructs the full $\alpha$-family. Given the totally symmetric **Amari–Chentsov tensor** $T_{ijk} = \mathbb{E}_p[\partial_i \ell \cdot \partial_j \ell \cdot \partial_k \ell]$, the Christoffel symbols of the $\alpha$-connection in the $g$-dual (covariant) form are [5]:

$$\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{ij,k} = \Gamma^{(0)}_{ij,k} + \frac{\alpha}{2} T_{ijk},$$

where $\Gamma^{(0)}$ are the Levi-Civita symbols of $g$. The $e$-connection $\nabla^{(1)}$ and $m$-connection $\nabla^{(-1)}$ are special cases; their duality with respect to $g$ is immediate from the symmetric formula.

We proceed in two moves. **Analytically**, we work with exponential families $p(x;\theta) = \exp(\theta^i F_i(x) - \psi(\theta))$, whose geometry is *dually flat*: $\theta$ is $\nabla^{(1)}$-affine, $\eta_i = \mathbb{E}_\theta[F_i]$ is $\nabla^{(-1)}$-affine, and the two are Legendre dual through the cumulant generating function $\psi(\theta)$ and its conjugate $\varphi(\eta)$ [4][5]. **Algorithmically**, we define the natural gradient as the Riemannian gradient $\tilde{\nabla}L = g^{-1}\nabla L$ and study its computable approximations, since the full Fisher matrix is quadratic in parameter count and cannot be inverted exactly for deep networks [1][2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The α-connection family and constant curvature

The family $\{\nabla^{(\alpha)}\}_{\alpha \in \mathbb{R}}$ is not merely a formal interpolation; it is the curvature spectrum of statistical geometry. A manifold with $\alpha$-flat structure for one $\alpha$ is $-\alpha$-flat — the **dual flatness theorem** — so $e$-flatness of exponential families automatically grants their $m$-flatness [5]. For finite sample spaces, the simplex is $\pm 1$-flat and carries $\alpha$-constant curvature: the sectional $\alpha$-curvature is constant, $(1-\alpha^2)/4$, a formula with no Riemannian analogue. The curvature measures how far a family departs from the exponential ideal; curved exponential families and mixture families acquire curvature precisely when their dual flatness is broken.

Beyond finite dimensions, the Amari–Chentsov $\alpha$-connections extend to infinite-dimensional non-parametric settings: recent work generalizes the $L^p$-Fisher–Rao metric together with its $\alpha$-connections to spaces of probability densities, and studies the $\alpha$-geometry of Orlicz manifolds bridging parametric and non-parametric regimes [4][6].

### 4.2 Dually flat manifolds, potentials, and the Legendre transform

A manifold is **dually flat** when it admits $\nabla^{(1)}$-affine coordinates $\theta$ and $\nabla^{(-1)}$-affine coordinates $\eta$ related by

$$\eta_i = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta^i}, \qquad \theta^i = \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial \eta_i}, \qquad \varphi(\eta) = \sup_\theta \{\theta \cdot \eta - \psi(\theta)\},$$

with strictly convex potentials $\psi, \varphi$ [4][5]. The metric is the Hessian of the potential, $g_{ij} = \partial_i \partial_j \psi(\theta)$, and its inverse is generated by the conjugate, $g^{ij} = \partial^i \partial^j \varphi(\eta)$. This is the geometric content of the Gibbs formalism: for exponential families of statistical mechanics, $\psi$ is the cumulant (log-partition) function and $\varphi$ is the negative entropy — Legendre duality of thermodynamics is dual affine geometry [5].

The payoff is computational: on a dually flat manifold, geodesics are straight lines in the affine coordinates, and the natural biorthogonal decomposition $T_pS = E_p \oplus M_p$ of tangent spaces into $e$-parallel and $m$-parallel subspaces are $g$-orthogonal — a decomposition that underlies the Cramér–Rao analysis [7].

### 4.3 The generalized Pythagorean theorem and canonical divergences

Given a dualistic structure, there is a **canonical divergence** $D: S \times S \to \mathbb{R}$ whose induced dualistic structure coincides with the given one [5]. For dually flat manifolds it takes the Bregman form

$$D(p:q) = \psi(\theta_p) + \varphi(\eta_q) - \theta_p \cdot \eta_q,$$

which specializes to KL divergence for exponential families. The **generalized Pythagorean theorem** states: if the $\nabla$-geodesic from $p$ to $r$ and the $\nabla^*$-geodesic from $r$ to $q$ meet orthogonally at $r$, then $D(p:q) = D(p:r) + D(r:q)$ [4][5].

This is the geometric engine behind the EM algorithm: the E-step and M-step are alternating $e$- and $m$-projections, and convergence follows from monotone decrease of the canonical divergence via Pythagoras [6]. It likewise governs I-projections onto exponential families and minimum-divergence estimation generally.

### 4.4 The natural gradient: definition and invariance

For a smooth loss $L: S \to \mathbb{R}$ on the model manifold, the **natural gradient** is the Riemannian gradient

$$\tilde{\nabla} L = g^{-1}(\xi)\, \nabla L, \qquad \text{update: } \xi_{t+1} = \xi_t - \eta\, g^{-1}(\xi_t)\nabla L(\xi_t),$$

where $g$ is the Fisher information matrix $F$ [1]. Whereas ordinary gradient descent follows the steepest direction in the *parameter* space — a direction that changes arbitrarily under reparameterization — the natural gradient follows the steepest direction in the space of *distributions*, measured by local KL divergence. Consequences:

- **Reparameterization invariance:** the update is intrinsically defined on $S$, so whitening or rescaling parameters cannot change the trajectory [1][8].
- **Fisher efficiency:** Amari proved online natural-gradient learning is Fisher efficient — its asymptotic error matches the optimal batch estimator, the Cramér–Rao bound [1].
- **Plateau escape:** analyses of the dynamics show natural gradient escapes the plateaus that stall backpropagation in multilayer networks [1].

The following toy implementation illustrates the geometric correction for a Gaussian model with mean $\mu$ and log-variance $\rho$:

```python
import numpy as np

def fisher_gaussian(mu, rho):
    # F for (mu, log-sigma): diagonal with entries (1/sigma^2, 1/2)
    s2 = np.exp(2 * rho)
    return np.diag([1.0 / s2, 0.5])

def natural_gradient_step(params, loss_grad, lr=0.1, damping=1e-3):
    mu, rho = params
    F = fisher_gaussian(mu, rho)
    # Damped solve: (F + lambda I)^{-1} grad  -- the practical core of NGD
    nat_grad = np.linalg.solve(F + damping * np.eye(2), loss_grad)
    return params - lr * nat_grad

# Euclidean GD slows near tiny variance (huge curvature); NGD's F^{-1}
# correction whitens the update, yielding uniform progress per KL unit.
```

### 4.5 Kronecker-factored approximations and modern practice

Exact natural gradient requires $O(n^2)$ memory and $O(n^3)$ inversion, infeasible for deep networks. K-FAC (Martens & Grosse) approximates the Fisher matrix block-per-layer as a Kronecker product of two small factors — the input activation covariance $A$ and the output pre-activation gradient covariance $S$ — so that $\hat{F}_\ell = A_\ell \otimes S_\ell$ inverts as $(A_\ell^{-1} \otimes S_\ell^{-1})$ in the cost of two small eigen-decompositions [2]. Extensions cover convolutional layers (KFC) [3] and recurrent and weight-sharing architectures. Empirically, K-FAC reaches target accuracy in far fewer iterations than tuned SGD and retains approximate reparameterization invariance [2][3]. Recent theory closes an old gap: natural gradient descent (and K-FAC) converges to global minima for overparameterized networks under NTK-type assumptions, with explicit rates [8].

| Method | Curvature used | Cost per step | Invariance |
|---|---|---|---|
| SGD | none | $O(n)$ | none |
| Diagonal NG (Adam-like) | $\mathrm{diag}(\hat{F})$ | $O(n)$ | per-coordinate |
| K-FAC | $A_\ell \otimes S_\ell$ | $O(n)$ with small constants | layer-wise affine |
| Exact NGD | full $F$ | $O(n^3)$ | full reparameterization |

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

We collect the central theorems with proof sketches.

> **Theorem 1 (Dual flatness, Amari).** If a statistical manifold is $\alpha$-flat for some $\alpha$, it is $-\alpha$-flat.
>
> *Proof sketch.* $\alpha$-flatness gives $\nabla^{(\alpha)}$-affine coordinates $\xi$ with vanishing connection symbols. Duality $Xg(Y,Z) = g(\nabla^{(\alpha)}_X Y, Z) + g(Y, \nabla^{(-\alpha)}_X Z)$ transports the affine structure across: the $g$-dual coordinate system is $\nabla^{(-\alpha)}$-affine because the metric's Hessian representation in $\alpha$-affine coordinates is constant, hence integrable. See [4, Thm 3.2] and [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Generalized Pythagoras).** On a dually flat manifold, orthogonal $\nabla / \nabla^*$-geodesics satisfy $D(p:q) = D(p:r) + D(r:q)$ for the canonical divergence.
>
> *Proof sketch.* In dual affine coordinates the Bregman divergence decomposes linearly; the orthogonality condition $g_{ij}\,\dot{\gamma}^i\dot{\sigma}^j = 0$ at $r$ kills exactly the cross term between the $\theta$-displacement and the $\eta$-displacement, leaving the additive form. See [4, Thm 3.9].

> **Theorem 3 (Amari's natural gradient efficiency).** Online natural-gradient learning with the loss as negative log-likelihood is Fisher efficient.
>
> *Proof sketch.* The parameter error covariance satisfies a Lyapunov equation whose stationary solution equals the Cramér–Rao bound; the $F^{-1}$ preconditioner aligns the stochastic noise with the metric so that the asymptotic covariance attains $g^{-1}$. The plateau-escape behavior follows from analyzing the saddle dynamics in distribution space rather than parameter space [1].

> **Theorem 4 (Global convergence, overparameterized regime).** NGD and K-FAC converge to global minima with explicit rates under standard NTK assumptions [8].
>
> *Proof sketch.* In the lazy-training regime the Fisher matrix stays close to its initialization value; NGD then behaves like a well-conditioned linear method on the function-space residuals, and the K-FAC approximation error is controlled by the Kronecker structure of the NTK.

Empirically, Grosse & Martens report KFC training convolutional networks several times faster than carefully tuned SGD and in 10–20 times fewer iterations — a direct dividend of the geometry-aware step [3].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Computational cost.** Even K-FAC is several times more expensive per iteration than SGD, requires eigen-decomposition maintenance and damping heuristics, and becomes ill-conditioned at large batch sizes, forcing strong damping that suppresses precisely the curvature information it exists to exploit.
2. **Empirical vs. expected Fisher.** Practitioners usually substitute the *empirical* Fisher (outer products of per-example gradients at the observed labels) for the expected Fisher over the model's predictive distribution. This substitution lacks theoretical justification — it does not estimate $F$ and approximates the Gauss–Newton Hessian instead — and can silently alter the geometry being followed.
3. **Damping bias.** The damped solve $(F + \lambda I)^{-1}\nabla L$ interpolates toward ordinary gradient descent as $\lambda$ grows; under strong damping NGD inherits SGD's pathologies while paying second-order cost.
4. **Non-dually-flat reality.** Deep networks are not exponential families: their manifolds are curved, non-flat, and the clean Bregman–Pythagoras calculus applies only approximately. The information-geometric guarantees (Fisher efficiency, Pythagoras) are theorems about the idealized geometry, not about finite-step damped approximations.
5. **Scope of the theory.** The finite-dimensional parametric theory is mature, but infinite-dimensional non-parametric $\alpha$-geometry (Orlicz manifolds, $L^p$-Fisher–Rao metrics) [4][6] and quantum information geometry remain active frontiers with unresolved uniqueness and integrability questions.

## 7 Conclusion

Information geometry supplies the canonical language for statistical learning: the Fisher–Rao metric as the unique invariant Riemannian structure, the $\alpha$-connections as the interpolating family between exponential and mixture geometry, and Amari's dual affine framework — dually flat manifolds, Legendre-dual potentials, canonical divergences, and the generalized Pythagorean theorem — as the computational core. The natural gradient is this geometry's algorithmic incarnation: the steepest descent in distribution space, Fisher efficient, parameterization invariant, and plateau-resistant. Modern approximations such as K-FAC make it practical at scale, and recent convergence theory validates its empirical speedups. Yet the gap between the idealized dualistic geometry and its damped, empirical, Kronecker-factored realization is where both the engineering and the open mathematics live: honest about the gap, the geometry remains the sharpest lens we have on what learning in parameter space actually means.
