# Confidential Containers and Enclave Orchestration: Gramine LibOS, Kata Containers, Enclave-CC, and SEV-SNP Attestation Binding

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, artifact-evaluable treatment of confidential containers and enclave orchestration: gramine libos, kata containers, enclave-cc, and sev-snp attestation binding, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 systems practice. We formalize threat models, cost semantics, and correctness invariants spanning confidential computing containers, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX/ACM, and industry specifications. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale workloads and hardware traces including H100 CC, 96c Genoa, NVLink4, RoCEv2 400G, NVMe ZNS, and Loihi2 neuromorphic. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance, mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient, scalable confidential computing containers production systems.
