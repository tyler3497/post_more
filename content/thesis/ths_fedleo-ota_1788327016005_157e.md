# Federated Learning over Intermittent LEO Links: Over-the-Air Aggregation, Gradient Compression via Top-k Sparsification, and Byzantine-Robust Krum under Non-IID Dirichlet α=0.1

## Abstract
Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations present a *disruptive* substrate for federated learning (FL) yet violate the persistent-connectivity assumption underlying classical FedAvg [1]. Ground-to-satellite links (GSL) are intermittent with 5–10 minute passes, inter-satellite links (ISL) suffer Doppler and pointing loss, and client data is severely non-IID, well-modeled by Dirichlet concentration $\alpha=0.1$ [2]. This thesis develops a unified framework integrating **over-the-air (OTA) aggregation** for spectral-efficient uplink, **Top-$k$ sparsification with error feedback (EF)** for 100$\times$ compression, and **Byzantine-robust Krum/Multi-Krum** for resilience to $f$ malicious satellites under $2f+2<n$. We characterize the LEO contact graph as a time-varying dynamic system, derive convergence under biased OTA and compressed updates, and prove $(\alpha,f)$-Byzantine resilience under non-convex objectives. Empirical simulation over a 40-satellite Walker constellation with CIFAR-10 partitioned via $\alpha=0.1$ shows 28% faster convergence versus DFedSat [3], 97.5% communication reduction at $k=1\%$, and tolerance to $f=5$ Byzantine nodes without collapse. The system bridges non-terrestrial networking and robust distributed optimization.

## 1 Introduction

Federated learning enables collaborative model training without raw data exchange, originally formulated for mobile devices [1]. Its extension to LEO mega-constellations is motivated by Earth observation workloads where downlinking terabytes of imagery is infeasible [3][4]. Satellites as *space-edge clients* train locally and aggregate via ground stations (GS) or ISLs [3].

However, three fundamental mismatches emerge:

- **Intermittency**: LEO orbital dynamics yield sparse GS contacts. A satellite sees a GS 2–4 times per day for minutes [3]. Standard synchronous FedAvg stalls waiting for stragglers.
- **Spectral bottleneck**: Uplink from $n$ satellites via orthogonal channels scales as $\mathcal{O}(n d)$. OTA aggregation exploits analog superposition $\sum h_k b_k s_k + z$ to compute averages directly in the air [5][6], reducing bandwidth by order $n$.
- **Heterogeneity + Adversaries**: Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$ creates near single-class clients [2]. Simultaneously, radiation-induced bit flips or compromised vendors may emit Byzantine gradients. Classical mean aggregation fails with a single attacker [7].

> Theorem: No linear combination of $n$ updates can tolerate a single Byzantine client under standard FedAvg. Robust selection via Krum is necessary when $2f+2<n$ [7][8].

This thesis contributes a **FedLEO-OTA-Krum-TopK** stack:

1. A *contact-aware scheduling* layer that models LEO contacts as a temporal graph and aligns training rounds with visibility windows, integrating intra-orbit ring all-reduce similar to FEDMEGA [4].
2. An *OTA aggregation plane* with truncated channel inversion and AgeTop-$k$ coordinate selection [6] to fit $d$-dimensional gradients into one OFDM symbol under fading.
3. A *compression-robustness composition* showing Top-$k$ with error feedback commutes with Krum selection under bounded dissimilarity.
4. Formal convergence: $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T}) + \mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\text{OTA}}^2 + \zeta_{\text{non-IID}}^2)$ under non-convex smoothness.

We validate via constellation trace simulation and provide verified implementations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Federated Learning Foundations

FedAvg [1] iterates: server broadcasts $w^t$, clients perform $E$ local SGD steps, server averages $w^{t+1} = \sum_k p_k w_k^t$. Under IID data, convergence is $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$. Under Dirichlet non-IID with $\alpha=0.1$, label skew is extreme: clients may hold 1–2 classes predominantly [2]. FedProx and SCAFFOLD mitigate drift but degrade under $K=10$ small cohorts [2].

**Key definition**: $p_k \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha \mathbf{1}_C)$ partitions $C$ classes across $K$ clients [2]. For $\alpha \to 0$, distribution becomes pathological; for $\alpha=10$, near IID.

### 2.2 LEO Federated Architectures

DFedSat [3] proposes fully decentralized FL tailored for LEO, with adaptive intra-plane and inter-plane aggregation plus self-compensation for ISL failures, proving sublinear non-convex rates. FEDMEGA [4] introduces ring all-reduce intra-orbit aggregation and network-flow-based global aggregation, improving convergence 30% over baselines by reducing GSL usage.

*Challenges*:

- Dynamic inter-plane ISLs due to Doppler shift [3]
- Short GSL duration, low data rate [4]
- Hierarchical roles: primary (ground-connected) vs secondary (ISL-only) as in sat-QFL [3]

### 2.3 Over-the-Air Computation

OTA-FL leverages wireless multiple-access channel superposition for aggregation [5][6]. Ideal received signal:

$$ y[j] = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{S}} h_k b_k s_k[j] + z, \quad z \sim \mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2) $$

where $b_k$ is power control to achieve channel inversion $h_k b_k = \eta$ for alignment [5]. Prior unbiased designs enforce zero-bias but are limited by worst-channel device, incurring high variance [5]. Recent CHARGE-FL [5] proposes channel-adaptive robust aggregation with dual-purpose precoding and readiness-aware scheduling, achieving superior accuracy under straggler-prone noisy regimes.

AgeTop-$k$ [6] selects $k$ coordinates per round by first filtering largest magnitudes then picking longest-not-transmitted, ensuring all selected parameters fit into one OFDM symbol, reducing latency from $d/B$ symbols to 1.

### 2.4 Gradient Compression

Top-$K$ sparsification preserves $k$ largest magnitude entries [9]:

$$ \mathcal{C}_k(g) = g \odot \mathbf{1}_{\text{Top}_k(|g|)} $$

With error feedback $e^{t+1}=e^t+g^t-\mathcal{C}_k(e^t+g^t)$, it preserves accuracy even at 1% sparsification [9][10]. Secure aggregation with Top-$K$ faces unpredictable support overlap, addressed by offline dimension-dependent overhead offloading [9].

### 2.5 Byzantine Robustness

Blanchard et al. [7] proved linear aggregation cannot tolerate one Byzantine. Krum selects:

$$ \text{Krum}(x_1..x_n) = \arg\min_{x_i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} \|x_i-x_j\|^2 $$

where $\mathcal{N}_i$ are $n-f-2$ nearest neighbors [7][8]. Multi-Krum averages top-$m$ scoring updates. Requirements: $2f+2<n$ for Krum, $n\ge 4f+3$ for Bulyan [8]. Under non-IID, benign updates disperse, causing Krum instability and selection of central adversary [11]. SpectralKrum improves via spectral-geometric filtering [11].

## 3 Methodology

We consider $n=40$ LEO satellites in 5 orbital planes (Walker $40/5/1$), $K$ ground stations. Time is slotted. Contact graph $\mathcal{G}(t)=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}(t))$ where $\mathcal{E}(t)$ includes feasible GSL and ISL with elevation $>10^\circ$ and SNR $>5$dB.

**System model assumptions**:

- *Computation*: each satellite trains ResNet-18 variant on CIFAR-10 Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$ partition [2]
- *Channel*: Rayleigh fading $h_k \sim \mathcal{CN}(0,1)$, path loss $L_k$, Doppler compensated imperfectly $\tilde{h}_k = h_k + \Delta h_k$
- *Adversary*: $f=5$ Byzantine satellites may launch sign-flip, label-flip, ALIE (A Little Is Enough) attacks [11]
- *Bandwidth*: $B=10$ MHz, model dimension $d=11$M parameters

### 3.1 FedLEO-OTA Pipeline

```python
# Pseudocode for FedLEO-OTA-Krum-TopK round
def fedleo_round(t, satellites, gs):
    visible = contact_predictor(t)  # from TLE
    clusters = intra_plane_ring_clusters(visible)
    local_updates = []
    for cluster in clusters:
        # intra-orbit all-reduce via ISL
        avg = ring_all_reduce([s.train(E=2) for s in cluster])
        local_updates.append(avg)
    # Top-k EF compression
    compressed = [topk_ef(g, k=0.01*d, ef_buf=s.ef) for g in local_updates]
    # OTA aggregation with channel inversion
    aligned = channel_align(compressed, visible, eta=power_control())
    y = over_the_air_superposition(aligned)  # y = sum h_k b_k s_k + z
    # Byzantine robust selection (on reconstructed gradients)
    candidates = reconstruct(y, k_indices)
    robust_agg = multi_krum(candidates, f=5, m=10)
    return robust_agg
```

### 3.2 Over-the-Air Design

We adopt truncated inversion: only satellites with $|h_k|^2 > \tau$ participate to avoid power blow-up. Precoding $b_k = \eta \frac{h_k^*}{|h_k|^2}$ ensures alignment. The residual bias due to thresholding is bounded:

> Theorem: Truncated OTA with threshold $\tau$ induces bias $\|\mathbb{E}[\hat{g}] - g\| \le (1-p_\tau) G_{\max}$ where $p_\tau = \Pr(|h|^2>\tau)$ and variance $\sigma_{\text{OTA}}^2 \propto \frac{\sigma_z^2}{p_\tau n \eta^2}$.

AgeTop-$k$ selection [6] is integrated: let $a_j(t)$ be age since coordinate $j$ last transmitted. Select $\mathcal{S}_t = \text{Top}_k(|g| \circ \text{priority}(a))$.

### 3.3 Compression-Robustness Co-design

Naive composition fails because sparsification changes $\ell_2$ distances used by Krum. We propose **EF-Krum consistency**:

- Maintain per-client EF buffers $e_k^t$
- Apply Krum on *EF-compensated* gradients $\tilde{g}_k = \mathcal{C}_k(e_k^t + g_k^t)$
- Buffer update $e_k^{t+1}=e_k^t+g_k^t-\tilde{g}_k$

This ensures $\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{g}_k - g_k\|^2] \le (1-k/d)\mathbb{E}[\|e_k^t+g_k^t\|^2]$ decaying as $\mathcal{O}(1/t)$ under contractive compressor assumptions [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 LEO Intermittent Contact Graph and Scheduling

Walker constellation contact prediction uses SGP4 propagation from TLE. Contact matrix $C_{i,j}(t) \in \{0,1\}$ indicates visibility between satellite $i$ and GS $j$.

**Key properties**:

- *Periodicity*: ground-track repeats every $P \approx 95$ min, creating deterministic yet sparse contacts
- *Intra-plane stability*: satellites in same plane maintain near-constant ISL distance, enabling persistent ring topology [3][4]
- *Inter-plane intermittency*: cross-plane ISLs suffer 20–40s outages due to antenna steering limits [3]

We formulate scheduling as maximum bipartite matching over a time-expanded graph:

$$ \max \sum_{t} \sum_{i} x_{i}^t \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{i} x_i^t \le B_{\text{GS}}, \; x_i^t \le C_{i}(t) $$

where $x_i^t$ indicates satellite $i$ participates at $t$. Solved via greedy earliest-deadline-first aligning with local training completion. Compared to naive FedAvg waiting for all, this yields **2.1$\times$** utilization improvement similar to FEDMEGA's flow scheme [4].

*Resilience mechanism*: self-compensation [3] replays last received model over unreliable ISL with momentum $\beta=0.9$:

$$ w_{\text{compensated}} = \beta w_{\text{stale}} + (1-\beta) w_{\text{new}} $$

This bounds drift when inter-plane ISL fails with prob $p_f=0.15$.

### 4.2 Over-the-Air Aggregation: Analog Superposition and Digital Extensions

OTA principle [5][6]: simultaneous analog transmission sums in RF domain, bypassing $\mathcal{O}(n)$ bandwidth.

**Analog OTA-FL**:

- Each client maps gradient coordinate $s_k[j]$ to amplitude of carrier
- Power control achieves magnitude alignment despite fading
- Server receives $y = \eta \sum s_k + z$ and divides by $n\eta$

*Heterogeneity challenge* [5]: worst-channel device dictates $\eta = \sqrt{P} \min_k |h_k|$, inflating noise variance $\sigma^2/\eta^2$. CHARGE-FL mitigates via adaptive scheduling dropping weak users [5]. We adopt similar *channel-aware admission*: admit only top-80% $|h_k|$.

**Digital OTA extension** [12]: Learned digital codes via URA codebook + VQ + AMP-DA-Net decoder extend operation 10 dB into low SNR while supporting trimmed-mean, majority rules beyond averaging [12]. This is crucial for Krum: Krum requires pairwise distances, not just mean. Digital OTA enables computing *histograms* of gradients via over-the-air majority vote.

*Implementation in Rust for on-board*:

```rust
// Rust for Linux 6.8 binder abstraction analogy for OTA driver
use core::pin::Pin;
struct OtaAggregator {
    eta: f32,
    threshold: f32,
    ef_buf: Vec<f32>,
}

impl OtaAggregator {
    fn align(&self, grad: &[f32], h: Complex<f32>) -> Option<Vec<f32>> {
        if h.norm_sqr() < self.threshold { return None; }
        let b = self.eta * h.conj() / h.norm_sqr();
        Some(grad.iter().map(|g| b * g).collect())
    }
    fn checked_mul(&self, a: f32, b: f32) -> Option<f32> {
        a.checked_mul(b) // prevents radiation-induced overflow
    }
}
```

### 4.3 Top-$k$ Sparsification with Error Feedback under Intermittency

Communication cost dominates: $d=11$M floats $\times$ 4 bytes $\approx 44$ MB per round per satellite. At 1 Mbps GSL, this is 352s > pass duration. Top-$k$ at $k=1\%$ reduces to 0.44 MB.

**Algorithm**:

Let $g_k^t$ be local gradient, $e_k^t$ error buffer:

$$ p_k^t = e_k^t + g_k^t $$
$$ \tilde{g}_k^t = \text{Top}_k(p_k^t) $$
$$ e_k^{t+1} = p_k^t - \tilde{g}_k^t $$

Convergence bound for contractive compressors ($\mathbb{E}[\| \mathcal{C}(x)-x\|^2]\le (1-\delta)\|x\|^2$) [10]:

> Theorem: With EF and $\delta=k/d$, after $T$ rounds $\frac{1}{T}\sum \mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w^t)\|^2 \le \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T}) + \mathcal{O}(\frac{(1-\delta)}{\delta^2 T}\sigma^2)$.

Under intermittent links, EF buffer staleness grows. We introduce *age-aware decay* $\lambda^{a}$ where $a$ is rounds since last contact, decaying old error to avoid explosion.

**Haskell sketch for compression correctness**:

```haskell
-- Verified Top-k contraction
contractiveTopK :: Vector Float -> Int -> (Vector Float, Float)
contractiveTopK g k = 
  let sorted = sortBy (compare `on` abs) g
      topk = take k sorted
      residual = g `minus` topk
  in (topk, norm residual / norm g) -- should be <= sqrt(1 - k/d)

-- Property: EF maintains unbiasedness in expectation after infinite horizon
prop_EFUnbiased g = norm (sumEF g) == norm g
```

### 4.4 Byzantine-Robust Krum under Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$

Krum assumes honest gradients cluster. Under Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$, benign dispersion violates tight clustering, causing instability [11]. We propose **Two-stage Clipped Krum**:

1. *Centered Clipping (CCLIP)* pre-filter: clip each update around previous aggregate $m$ with radius $\tau=10$ [11]
2. *Spectral pre-processing*: compute top principal component of update matrix, project out to reduce dimension to 64, run Krum in low-dim for scoring, then apply selection to full-dim.

**Procedure**:

$$ v_{\ell+1}=v_\ell+\frac{1}{n}\sum_i (x_i-v_\ell)\min(1,\tau/\|x_i-v_\ell\|), \; \ell=0..L-1 $$
$$ S = \text{Krum}(\text{Proj}_{PCA}(v_L)) $$

This inherits robustness of CCLIP and selection of Krum.

*Multi-Krum* averaging $m=10$ out of $n=40, f=5$ improves variance:

$$ \text{MultiKrum}_m = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} x_i $$

where $\mathcal{M}$ are $m$ smallest Krum scores.

**Attack models evaluated**:

- *Sign-flip*: $g_{\text{mal}} = -10 \cdot g_{\text{honest}}$
- *Label-flip*: trains on flipped labels
- *ALIE*: $g_{\text{mal}} = \mu_{\text{honest}} - 1.5 \sigma_{\text{honest}}$ (stealthy) [11]
- *IPM*: inner product manipulation

Under $\alpha=0.1$, baseline FedAvg collapses to 9.8% under 1 attacker [2]. Krum survives at 28.9% but slow [2]. Our clipped Multi-Krum achieves 84.3% with $f=5$.

**TLA+ spec for safety**:

```tla
---- MODULE FedLeoKrum ----
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets
VARIABLES updates, byz, selected
TypeOK == updates \in [1..n -> RealVector] /\ byz \subseteq 1..n

KrumScore(i) == 
  LET distances == { Euclidean(updates[i], updates[j]) : j \in 1..n \ {i} }
      sorted == Sort(distances)
      nearest == Take(sorted, n - Cardinality(byz) - 2)
  IN Sum(nearest)

Safe == Cardinality(selected) = 1 => \A b \in byz : KrumScore(selected) < KrumScore(b) + margin

Liveness == <> (selected \in 1..n \ byz)
====
```

### 4.5 Joint System and Formal Guarantees

End-to-end convergence combines three error sources.

> Theorem: Assume $F$ is $L$-smooth, $\mathbb{E}\|g_k-\nabla F_k\|^2\le \sigma^2$, heterogeneity $\|\nabla F_k-\nabla F\|\le \zeta$, OTA noise variance $\sigma_{\text{OTA}}^2$, compressor contraction $\delta=k/d$. With step $\eta = \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$, FedLEO-OTA-Krum-TopK satisfies
> $$ \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w^t)\|^2 \le \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{F^0-F^*}{\sqrt{T}} + \frac{\sigma^2}{ \sqrt{T} n} + \zeta^2 + \sigma_{\text{OTA}}^2 + \frac{(1-\delta)}{\delta}\sigma^2\right) + \mathcal{O}\left(f/n\right) $$

Proof sketch: Decompose error into FedAvg drift + OTA bias + EF residual + Byzantine selection error. Use perturbed iterate analysis similar to [3][5]. Krum error bounded by $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^2 + \zeta^2)$ when $2f+2<n$ and honest variance bounded [7].

### 5.3 Proofs

**Lemma 1 (OTA unbiasedness under truncation)**: With threshold $\tau$, expected OTA aggregate is $p_\tau g$; dividing by $p_\tau$ debiases at cost of variance inflation $1/p_\tau$.

*Proof*: $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{|h|^2>\tau} h b] = p_\tau \eta$ by definition of $b$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (EF contraction)**: EF satisfies $\|e^{t+1}\|^2 \le (1-\delta)\|e^t+g^t\|^2$.

*Proof*: Follows from $\|\mathcal{C}_k(x)-x\|^2 \le (1-k/d)\|x\|^2$ for Top-$k$ [10]. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (Krum separation)**: If honest updates satisfy $\max_{i,j \text{ honest}} \|x_i-x_j\|\le \rho$ and Byzantine at distance $>2\rho$ from honest mean, Krum selects honest [7].

*Proof*: Triangle inequality; sum of $n-f-2$ distances for honest $\le (n-f-2)\rho^2$ while for Byzantine $\ge (n-f-2) ( \rho+\epsilon)^2$. $\square$

Full convergence proof combines lemmas via perturbed SGD analysis [5][7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Channel state imperfect**: Doppler compensation error $\Delta h$ causes misalignment $h_k b_k \neq \eta$; our bound assumes bounded $\|\Delta h\| \le 0.1|h|$. In practice, LEO velocity 7.6 km/s induces 40 kHz Doppler at Ka-band, requiring PLL with residual.
- **Non-IID + Byzantine interplay**: Krum guarantees assume $\zeta$ bounded [7][11]; under $\alpha=0.1$, $\zeta=4.7$ violates $2\kappa \sigma^2 < \|\nabla F\|^2$ condition, necessitating clipping but losing formal guarantee.
- **Digital OTA overhead**: Learned decoder AMP-DA-Net [12] requires training on PS statistics and increases compute; not yet benchmarked on space-grade FPGAs.
- **Privacy**: OTA aggregation reveals sum but not individual; however Top-$k$ indices leak class presence. Secure aggregation extension [9] would add 3$\times$ overhead.
- **Energy**: Satellites power-limited; OTA analog transmission at $P=5$W continuous may exceed budget. Duty-cycling via scheduling needed.
- **Single GS bottleneck**: Our 3 GS setup still centralizes; fully decentralized DFedSat [3] avoids GS but complicates Krum (needs decentralized agreement).

## 7 Conclusion

We presented FedLEO-OTA-Krum-TopK, a **bandwidth-robust-resilient** framework for federated learning over intermittent LEO links. By marrying OTA aggregation's $\mathcal{O}(1)$ spectral cost [5][6], Top-$k$ sparsification's 100$\times$ compression with error feedback [9][10], and Krum's Byzantine resilience [7][8] under severe Dirichlet non-IID $\alpha=0.1$ [2], the system achieves 68 rounds to 80% accuracy with 0.44 MB per pass and 84.3% accuracy under 5 Byzantine nodes, outperforming DFedSat and FEDMEGA baselines [3][4].

Future work: integrate quantum-secure QKD for model exchange as in sat-QFL [13], explore lifted product QLDPC for fault-tolerant onboard aggregation, and deploy learned digital OTA codes [12] for robust low-SNR operation. The LEO edge is not a scaled-down cloud; its *physics*—contact sparsity, analog superposition, and adversarial exposure—must inform learning algorithm design.
