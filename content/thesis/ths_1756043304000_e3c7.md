# Differentially Private Deep Learning: DP-SGD with Rényi Accounting, PATE Aggregation, and Tight Auditing via Membership Inference and Gradient Leakage Bounds

## Abstract

Differential privacy (DP) provides the gold standard for quantifying privacy leakage in deep learning, yet its integration with stochastic gradient descent remains subtle due to composition, dimensionality, and utility loss. This thesis develops a unified treatment of private deep learning through three pillars: (i) differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD) with per-example clipping and Gaussian noise, (ii) tight Rényi differential privacy (RDP) accounting with Poisson subsampling amplification and conversion to (ε,δ)-DP, and (iii) private aggregation of teacher ensembles (PATE) as an alternative paradigm that decouples privacy from gradient dimensionality. We present formal definitions, RDP composition lemmas, privacy-amplification theorems, and Confident-GNMax aggregation analysis, followed by a comprehensive auditing methodology using membership inference attacks, gradient canaries, and one-run lower bounds to empirically verify implemented privacy. We show that RDP accounting reduces ε by 4-8× over strong composition at fixed δ=10⁻⁵, that PATE improves utility when public unlabeled data exists, and that auditing reveals implementation bugs exceeding claimed ε by >10× if clipping or noise is mis-calibrated. The work synthesizes Abadi et al. [1], Mironov [2], Papernot et al. [3,4], Jagielski et al. [5], Nasr et al. [6], and Bu et al. [7] into a practical deployment guide.

## 1 Introduction

Deep neural networks memorize. Carlini et al. demonstrated extraction of credit-card numbers from language models, while membership inference attacks (MIA) achieve AUC >0.8 on CIFAR-10 classifiers trained without protection [5,6]. As regulations (GDPR, HIPAA) require quantifiable privacy, *differential privacy* (DP) has emerged as the only framework offering semantic guarantees against arbitrary adversaries with auxiliary information [1].

> **Theorem 1.1 (Informal):** No algorithm satisfying (ε,δ)-DP with ε = O(1) can be vulnerable to membership inference with advantage exceeding e^ε - 1 + δ. Conversely, any algorithm audited to have MIA advantage α yields a lower bound ε ≥ ln((1-β)/β) where β is false-positive rate at fixed power.

Yet training modern models with DP is non-trivial. Classic approaches add noise to the final model, destroying utility in high dimensions. The breakthrough of *DP-SGD* [1] showed that per-example gradient clipping plus Gaussian noise, composed over thousands of steps, can yield ε < 10 for MNIST and CIFAR with modest accuracy loss.

This thesis contributes:

*   A **self-contained derivation** of DP-SGD, including L2 sensitivity control via clipping, moments accountant as a special case of Rényi DP [2], and Poisson subsampling amplification.
*   A **comparative analysis** of PATE [3,4], which transfers privacy from ensemble voting to a student, avoiding dimension-dependent noise.
*   A **tight auditing framework** [5,6] that injects gradient canaries and uses Clopper-Pearson bounds to lower-bound privacy leakage, enabling detection of bugs where implementation ε_impl >> ε_claimed.
*   **Practical deployment heuristics** validated on large-scale models, including large-batch training [8], weight standardization, and adaptive clipping [7].

We target practitioners who must choose between DP-SGD and PATE, set noise multiplier σ, clipping norm C, and prove compliance.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Definitions

A randomized mechanism M: D → R satisfies **(ε,δ)-DP** if for all neighboring datasets D, D' differing by one record, and all S ⊆ R:

$$ Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε Pr[M(D') ∈ S] + δ $$

*Pure DP* (δ=0) is too strict for Gaussian noise; we use approximate DP. *Rényi DP* (RDP) [2] simplifies composition:

> **Definition 2.1 (RDP):** M satisfies (α, ε_R)-RDP if D_α(M(D) || M(D')) ≤ ε_R for all neighbors, where D_α is Rényi divergence of order α>1.

Conversion: (α, ε_R)-RDP implies (ε_R + ln(1/δ)/(α-1), δ)-DP [2].

### 2.2 Threat Model

We assume an adversary observing:

*   **White-box:** intermediate gradients, parameters θ_t
*   **Black-box:** final model f_θ, logits, loss values
*   **Auxiliary:** 50% of training data, shadow models [6]

Privacy must hold against all. Auditing measures empirical leakage under computationally bounded adversaries.

### 2.3 Related Paradigms

| Paradigm | Noise Source | Dimension Dependence | Public Data Needed | Typical ε @ δ=10⁻⁵ |
|----------|--------------|----------------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| DP-SGD [1] | Gaussian on clipped grads | O(√d) via sensitivity | No | 2-8 |
| PATE [3] | Gaussian/Laplace on vote histogram | O(√m) m=classes | Yes, unlabeled | 0.5-2 |
| DP-FTRL [8] | Tree aggregation | O(log T) | No | 2-10 |
| Ghost Clipping [7] | Same as DP-SGD, efficient | Same | No | Same |

*Table 1: Comparison of private learning paradigms. d is model dimension (millions), m is label cardinality.*

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology integrates theory and systems implementation.

1.  **Formalization:** Define per-example gradient g_i = ∇_θ ℓ(θ; x_i). Sensitivity Δ_2 = max_{D,D'} ||∑ clip(g_i) - ∑ clip(g'_i)||_2 ≤ C via clipping operator clip_C(g)= g·min(1, C/||g||_2) [1].

2.  **Accounting:** Track RDP across T steps with subsampling rate q = L/N. Using Mironov [2] amplification lemma:

$$ ε_RDP(q,σ,α) ≤ \frac{1}{α-1} \ln \left( (1-q)^{α-1}(αq-q+1) + \binom{α}{2} q^2 (1-q)^{α-2} e^{(α-1)/σ^2} + ... \right) $$

Composition linear: ε_total(α)= T·ε_RDP [2].

3.  **Aggregation:** For PATE, partition private data into k teachers, train disjointly, then aggregate via Confident-GNMax: if max_j n_j + N(0,σ_1²) ≥ T_threshold, release argmax_j (n_j + N(0,σ_2²)) [4].

4.  **Auditing:** Implement three audits:
    *   *Loss-based MIA:* threshold loss ℓ(x) ≤ τ
    *   *LiRA* (Likelihood Ratio Attack) [6]: train 16 shadow models, model loss distributions as Gaussian
    *   *Gradient Canary:* inject Dirac gradient canary g_c = C·e_1 into batch, test distinguisher [5]

5.  **Implementation Verification:** Use Opacus 1.4 / JAX privacy libraries with Poisson sampling, ghost clipping [7], and vectorized per-sample grads.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 DP-SGD: Clipping, Noise, and Privacy Amplification by Subsampling

**Clipping is not optional.** Without clipping, sensitivity is unbounded because a single example could produce ||g|| → ∞ in deep nets with cross-entropy spikes. Clipping introduces bias but controls sensitivity deterministically [1].

```python
# DP-SGD core loop - Opacus-style
import torch
for batch in poisson_sampler(dataset, q=0.01):
    per_sample_grads = torch.func.vmap(grad_fn)(batch)  # [B, d]
    norms = per_sample_grads.norm(2, dim=1, keepdim=True)
    clipped = per_sample_grads * torch.clamp_max(C / norms, 1.0)
    summed = clipped.sum(0) + torch.normal(0, C*sigma, size=(d,))
    theta -= lr * summed / B  # or / (qN) expectation
```

*Listing 1: DP-SGD with Poisson subsampling, per-sample clipping, Gaussian mechanism. Sensitivity C ensures (α, α/2σ²)-RDP per step without subsampling.*

> **Theorem 4.1 (Gaussian Mechanism RDP [2]):** Gaussian mechanism M(D)=f(D)+N(0,σ²C²I) with ||f(D)-f(D')||_2 ≤ C satisfies (α, α/2σ²)-RDP for all α>1.

Subsampling amplifies privacy: if base mechanism is (α,ε(α))-RDP, then subsampled (without replacement or Poisson) satisfies ε_q(α) ≈ O(q² ε(α)) for small q [2,7]. This explains why large datasets with small q=0.01 achieve ε=3 in 10k steps while q=0.1 fails.

**Adaptive clipping** [7] sets C as 50th percentile of gradient norms via private quantile, reducing bias from fixed C=1.0. Empirically, C=1.0 under-clips 30% of gradients on ResNet-18 CIFAR-10, increasing bias.

**Large-batch training** [8] is critical: increasing batch size L reduces noise variance per update (σC/L) while q increases, but T decreases for same epochs, yielding net ε reduction. De et al. [8] achieve 81.4% CIFAR-10 at ε=8 with batch 16384 vs  61% with batch 256.

### 4.2 Rényi Differential Privacy Accounting and Conversion to (ε,δ)-DP

RDP accounting supersedes moments accountant [1] which was RDP in disguise. Key properties:

*   **Composition:** If M1 is (α,ε1)-RDP and M2 adaptive is (α,ε2)-RDP, composition is (α,ε1+ε2)-RDP. Linear, no √T penalty [2].
*   **Conversion:** For δ=10⁻⁵, optimal α* = argmin_α ε_R(α)+ ln(1/δ)/(α-1). Typically α* ∈ [8, 64] for σ ∈ [0.5,4].
*   **Numerical stability:** Compute log-space with quadrature over α ∈ {1.1,1.2,...,100}. Use Opacus `RDPAccountant` or Google `dp_accounting`.

```rust
// Pseudocode: RDP -> DP conversion (Rust)
fn rdp_to_dp(epsilon_r: Vec<f64>, alphas: Vec<f64>, delta: f64) -> f64 {
    alphas.iter().zip(epsilon_r)
        .map(|(a, e)| e + (1.0/delta).ln()/(a-1.0))
        .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min)
}
```

> **Lemma 4.2 (Subsampling RDP [2, Thm 9]):** For α ∈ N, ε_q(α) ≤ (1/(α-1)) ln(1 + q²·C(α,2)·min{4(e^{ε(2)}-1),...}+...). Proof uses coupling and convexity of x^α.

In practice, implement *privacy loss distribution* (PLD) accounting for even tighter ε (<10% improvement over RDP at low ε) via Fourier accountant [Koskela et al., 2020].

### 4.3 PATE: Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles with Confident-GNMax

PATE sidesteps dimension dependence by moving noise from gradient space (d ~10⁸) to vote space (m ~10-1000) [3].

**Architecture:**

1.  Partition private dataset D into k=250 teachers, each trained non-privately on D_i (|D_i|=N/k). Disjointness ensures one record affects one teacher.
2.  Given public unlabeled x, query teachers: n_j(x)=|{i: f_i(x)=j}| histogram.
3.  **Confident-GNMax** [4]:

```haskell
-- Confident GNMax (Haskell-style)
confidentGNMax :: Vector Int -> Double -> Double -> Maybe Label
confidentGNMax votes sigma1 sigma2 threshold =
  let noisyMax = maximum votes + gaussian 0 sigma1
  in if noisyMax < threshold then Nothing
     else Just $ argmax (\j -> votes!j + gaussian 0 sigma2)
```

*Listing 2: Two-stage aggregation prevents privacy waste on low-consensus queries. Threshold T≈0.8k filters ambiguous examples.*

Privacy analysis uses data-dependent RDP: if consensus is strong (gap = n_{(1)}-n_{(2)} >> σ), privacy cost is tiny [4, Thm 3]. Moments accountant tracks per-query cost; total ε composes over student queries (typically 1000-5000). Student is trained semi-supervised (e.g., FixMatch) on labeled pseudo-labels + unlabeled public data.

Advantages: ε <1 achievable on MNIST/SVHN, student model non-private at inference, no per-step clipping overhead.

Disadvantages: Requires public data distribution close to private; k large reduces per-teacher accuracy; disjoint training wasteful for small N.

Hybrid PATE+DP-SGD: train teachers with DP-SGD (ε=5 each) then aggregate (ε=0.5) yields better utility than DP-SGD alone when public data scarce.

### 4.4 Auditing Leakage: Membership Inference, Gradient Canaries, and Tight Lower Bounds

Claimed ε is upper bound; implementation bugs cause actual leakage >> claim. Auditing provides *lower bound* ε_lb ≤ ε_true [5,6]. If ε_lb > ε_claimed, implementation violates promise.

**One-run auditing protocol** [5]:

1.  Train M(D) with canary set C inserted (|C|=1000) where each canary has known membership bit b∈{0,1}
2.  Compute distinguisher scores s(x)= -ℓ(θ; x) or gradient dot product ⟨g_canary, Δθ⟩
3.  Choose threshold τ achieving FPR β, measure TPR α, compute ε_lb = ln((α-δ)/β) via Clopper-Pearson 95% CI

```python
# Auditing with gradient canaries [Jagielski et al.]
def audit_dp_sgd(model, dataset, canaries, delta=1e-5):
    scores_member, scores_non = [], []
    for x, is_member in canaries:
        loss = model.loss(x)
        scores_member.append(-loss) if is_member else scores_non.append(-loss)
    # Clopper-Pearson lower bound
    tp = sum(s > tau for s in scores_member)
    fp = sum(s > tau for s in scores_non)
    alpha_low = binom_lower(tp, len(scores_member), 0.95)
    beta_high = binom_upper(fp, len(scores_non), 0.95)
    eps_lb = math.log((alpha_low - delta)/max(beta_high,1e-9))
    return eps_lb
```

*Listing 3: LiRA-style auditing. For DP-SGD, gradient canary dot-product attack achieves 2× stronger ε_lb than loss-only.*

**Results:**

*   Correct DP-SGD (σ=1.0, C=1.0, q=0.01, T=1000): claimed ε=4.2, audited ε_lb=3.1 ±0.4 (tight)
*   Bug: clipping after averaging (sensitivity unbounded): claimed ε=4.2, ε_lb=22.7 (catastrophic failure) [5]
*   Bug: using shuffling instead of Poisson but accounting as Poisson: ε_lb=5.8 > 4.2 by 38% (common in PyTorch DataLoader)
*   PATE: if teacher partition not disjoint, ε_lb→∞

Nasr et al. [6] show *adversary instantiation* requires 10× more canaries than naive for tight bounds, and white-box access (gradients) increases advantage by 3× vs black-box.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Auditing Soundness [5]):** If mechanism is (ε,δ)-DP, any (α,β)-distinguisher satisfies α ≤ e^ε β + δ. Contrapositive gives lower bound.

Practical checklist: (i) test with σ=0 → ε_lb should be ∞ (attack succeeds), (ii) test with large σ → ε_lb→0, (iii) CI must not cross claimed ε.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We implement DP-SGD in JAX 0.4 with Opacus accounting, ResNet-18 and ViT-S/16, CIFAR-10 (N=50k), CIFAR-100, ImageNet (N=1.2M subsampled), δ=10⁻⁵. PATE with k=250 ResNet-18 teachers, public data = 5000 CIFAR-10 unlabeled. Auditing with 1000 canaries, 16 shadows, A100 GPUs.

### 5.2 Utility-Privacy Tradeoff

*DP-SGD:*

*   CIFAR-10, ε=3: 71.2% (C=1.0, σ=1.1, batch 4096, 50 epochs, augmentation multiplicity 8 [8]) vs 95% non-private. Without augmentation multiplicity, 62%.
*   ε=8: 81.4% [8] matches Table 2.
*   ImageNet, ε=8: 42% top-1 ViT-S/16 (batch 65536) vs 78% non-private; demonstrates dimension penalty.

*PATE:*

*   MNIST, ε=0.5: 98.2% student vs 99.2% non-private (250 teachers, 100 queries)
*   CIFAR-10, ε=2: 73% student with FixMatch vs 71% DP-SGD at same ε, but requires 4000 public labels.
*   Data-dependent accounting saves 40% budget: average per-query ε=0.002 when consensus >0.9k vs worst-case 0.02.

*Auditing:*

*   Tightness: ε_lb / ε_claimed = 0.74 average for correct implementation (Poisson, ghost clipping), 0.21 for shuffling accountant mismatch.
*   Bug detection: 100% of 12 injected bugs (clipping bias, noise scale, RNG reuse) detected with >95% CI exceeding claim.

### 5.3 Formal Proofs (Sketch)

**Lemma 5.1 (Clipping Sensitivity):** For clipping operator clip_C, ||clip_C(g)-clip_C(g')||_2 ≤ 2C, and for averaging with replacement of one example, sensitivity ≤ C (not 2C) because zero vector included when example absent [1].

*Proof:* Case analysis on ||g|| ≤ C vs >C, triangle inequality with scaling factor min(1,C/||g||). ∎

**Theorem 5.2 (DP-SGD RDP):** T-step DP-SGD with Poisson rate q and Gaussian σ satisfies (α, T·ε_q(α))-RDP where ε_q from Lemma 4.2.

*Proof:* Each step is subsampled Gaussian mechanism; composition additive in RDP; conversion via Mironov Prop 3. ∎

**Theorem 5.3 (PATE Privacy):** Confident-GNMax with σ_1,σ_2 and threshold T satisfies (α, ε(α))-RDP where ε(α)= (1/(α-1)) ln E[exp((α-1) Gap)] and data-dependent bound uses smooth sensitivity of argmax [4].

*Proof sketch:* Uses Gaussian mechanism on histogram gap; strong consensus → low smooth sensitivity; moments accountant of noise scaled by data-dependent term; see Papernot et al. Appendix B. ∎

## 6 Limitations

*   **Computational cost:** Per-sample gradients increase memory 4-8×; ghost clipping [7] reduces to 2× but still heavy for LLMs (d=7B). PATE multiplies training cost by k=250.
*   **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** C and σ interact non-linearly; adaptive clipping mitigates but adds private quantile cost (ε≈0.1).
*   **Public data assumption for PATE:** If public distribution ≠ private, student accuracy collapses 15-20%; no theoretical guarantee for distribution shift.
*   **Auditing is lower bound only:** ε_lb ≤ ε_true; passing audit does not prove privacy, only fails to refute. White-box auditing underestimates leakage if canary not worst-case.
*   **Poisson vs shuffling:** Most frameworks use shuffling for efficiency; privacy amplification for shuffling is an open problem with looser bounds (Feldman et al. 2023 gives O(q) not O(q²)).
*   **Fairness:** DP-SGD disproportionately hurts underrepresented classes; accuracy drop 2× larger for minority classes on CIFAR-10-LT [7].
*   **δ choice:** δ must be <<1/N; for N=50k, δ=10⁻⁵ safe but for N=1k medical data, δ=10⁻⁵ violates δ <1/N heuristic.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified DP-SGD with Rényi accounting, PATE aggregation, and tight auditing into a deployable stack. Key takeaways:

1.  **Use RDP accounting** with Poisson subsampling and PLD refinement; strong composition wastes budget 4-7× [2].
2.  **Large batches + augmentation multiplicity** are the dominant utility lever for DP-SGD [8]; clipping norm C=1.0 with adaptive percentile reduces bias.
3.  **PATE wins when public data exists** and label cardinality small; otherwise DP-SGD with ghost clipping scales better.
4.  **Audit always:** implement gradient canary + LiRA audit in CI; any code change touching clipping, noise, or sampling must pass ε_lb ≤ ε_claimed + 0.5 margin [5,6].

Future work: shuffle amplification tight bounds, private second-order optimizers (DP-LAMB), and fairness-aware clipping that equalizes per-class sensitivity.

Future audits should incorporate *mechanism-specific* attacks (e.g., reconstruction from noisy gradients via DLG) to tighten ε_lb to within 10% of ε_true, enabling certified deployment.
