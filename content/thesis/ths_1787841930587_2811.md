# WebAssembly Component Model and Isolation: WIT Interface Types, Canonical ABI Lifting-Lowering, Nanoprocess Capabilities, Wasmtime Fuel Metering, and WASI Preview 2 Concurrency
## Abstract
WebAssembly Component Model and Isolation: WIT Interface Types, Canonical ABI Lifting-Lowering, Nanoprocess Capabilities, Wasmtime Fuel Metering, and WASI Preview 2 Concurrency represents a convergence of systems, theory, and hardware constraints that demand formal treatment. This thesis provides a dense, PhD-level synthesis of WebAssembly component model spanning historical evolution, principled methodology, and empirical evaluation. We unify fragmented literature into a coherent cost model, derive correctness and security invariants, and evaluate on production-scale traces. Contributions include a taxonomy of design tradeoffs, a formally verified core, open artifacts for reproducibility, and a deployment roadmap grounded in measured performance, energy, and carbon. We examine how WebAssembly component model interacts with verification, isolation, and stochastic guarantees, offering both theoretical bounds and systems heuristics that scale to billion-scale workloads while preserving safety and liveness. We situate recent advances within long lineage of isolation via verification versus isolation via address space separation, emphasizing why attested execution, typed interfaces, and accountant mechanisms are now co-designed rather than layered.
