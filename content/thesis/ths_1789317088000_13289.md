# The Ising Model and Conformal Invariance: Fortuin–Kasteleyn Representations, SLE Interfaces, and the Critical 2D Lattice

## Abstract

The two-dimensional Ising model at its critical temperature is the paradigmatic exactly solvable system whose scaling limit exhibits full conformal symmetry. This thesis develops the modern rigorous route to that symmetry: the Fortuin–Kasteleyn random-cluster representation, Smirnov's discrete holomorphic fermions, and the identification of interface scaling limits with Schramm–Loewner evolution. We explain how the Edwards–Sokal coupling translates spin correlations into connectivity probabilities, how a fermionic observable with a tuned winding weight becomes discretely holomorphic at criticality, and how its convergence to a conformally covariant limit forces the interface law to be chordal SLE(3) in the spin picture and SLE(16/3) in the FK picture. The account culminates in the full scaling limit as a branching SLE and conformal loop ensemble, with numerical evidence from finite-size scaling and interface winding statistics.

## 1 Introduction

Few conjectures in statistical physics have been as influential — and as resistant to proof — as the claim that critical two-dimensional lattice models become conformally invariant in the scaling limit. The Ising model, introduced by Lenz and first solved in one dimension by Ising, became the central test case when Onsager computed its exact free energy in two dimensions, exhibiting a logarithmic singularity of the specific heat and fixing the critical temperature exactly [1]. The subsequent computation of critical exponents, the magnetization by Yang, and the correlation functions by Wu, McCoy, Tracy, and Barouch turned the 2D Ising model into the most precisely understood nontrivial statistical system in existence.

Yet exact solvability is not the same as understanding the *geometry* of criticality. Physicists since Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov have described the scaling limit through conformal field theory (CFT): the critical Ising model is the minimal model of central charge one half, with correlation functions fixed by Virasoro representation theory. But these arguments assumed conformal invariance rather than proving it, and they operated mostly on rectangular domains where only Möbius invariance — strictly weaker than full conformal covariance — is visible [3].

The mathematical breakthrough came from probability theory. Schramm's introduction of the Schramm–Loewner evolution (SLE) gave the first candidate description of random interfaces as conformally invariant random curves [5]. Smirnov then constructed *discrete holomorphic* observables for percolation and for the Ising model, proving Cardy's formula and, crucially, proving that a certain fermionic observable in the critical Ising model has a conformally invariant scaling limit [3]. Chelkak and Smirnov extended this to universality across isoradial graphs [4], and Chelkak, Hongler, and Izyurov proved conformal invariance of the spin correlations themselves [6]. Finally, Kemppainen and Smirnov identified the *full* scaling limit of the FK-Ising loop ensemble as a branching SLE and conformal loop ensemble [7].

This thesis presents that arc as a coherent whole. Our central claim is that three languages — the spin language of the Ising model, the bond language of the Fortuin–Kasteleyn representation, and the curve language of SLE — describe one object, and that discrete complex analysis is the dictionary translating between them.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Ising model and Onsager's solution

Consider a finite graph, typically a square-lattice domain, with spin variables taking values in the two-element set of plus and minus one. The Hamiltonian with ferromagnetic coupling and no external field is

$$H(\sigma) = -J \sum_{x \sim y} \sigma_x \sigma_y, \qquad J > 0,$$

the sum running over nearest-neighbor pairs. The Gibbs measure at inverse temperature $\beta$ assigns to each configuration probability proportional to $\exp(-\beta H(\sigma))$. Onsager's exact solution [1] shows that the infinite-volume free energy is singular at the critical inverse temperature determined by $\sinh(2\beta_c J) = 1$, i.e.

$$\beta_c J = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1+\sqrt{2}), \qquad T_c = \frac{2J}{k_B \ln(1+\sqrt{2})} \approx 2.269\, J/k_B.$$

There the correlation length diverges and the critical exponents take the celebrated rational values tabulated below.

| Exponent | Meaning | 2D Ising value |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | specific heat | $0$ (logarithmic) |
| $\beta$ | magnetization | $1/8$ |
| $\gamma$ | susceptibility | $7/4$ |
| $\delta$ | critical isotherm | $15$ |
| $\nu$ | correlation length | $1$ |
| $\eta$ | correlation decay | $1/4$ |

These exponents satisfy the scaling relations $\alpha + 2\beta + \gamma = 2$ and $\gamma = \nu(2-\eta)$, and the CFT prediction identifies the spin field with scaling dimension $1/16$ and the energy field with dimension $1/2$, inside the $c = 1/2$ minimal model.

### 2.2 The Fortuin–Kasteleyn representation

Fortuin and Kasteleyn discovered that the $q$-state Potts model, and in particular the Ising model at $q = 2$, admits a purely geometric reformulation [2]. On the edges of the lattice, consider bond configurations $\omega \in \{0,1\}^E$, where $\omega_e = 1$ means the edge is open. The random-cluster measure with parameters $p$ and $q$ is

$$\phi_{p,q}(\omega) = \frac{1}{Z_{p,q}}\, p^{o(\omega)} (1-p)^{c(\omega)} q^{k(\omega)},$$

where $o(\omega)$ and $c(\omega)$ count open and closed edges and $k(\omega)$ counts connected components (clusters), including isolated vertices. The definitive reference for this model is Grimmett's monograph [8].

The link to the Ising model is the Edwards–Sokal coupling: sample $\omega$ from $\phi_{p,2}$ with $p = 1 - e^{-2\beta J}$, then assign each cluster an independent fair random spin. Spin correlations then become connection probabilities:

$$\langle \sigma_x \sigma_y \rangle_{\beta} = \phi_{p,2}(x \longleftrightarrow y),$$

a dictionary of extraordinary power: analytic questions about correlations become geometric questions about random clusters. Duality for the random-cluster model gives the self-dual, hence critical, point $p_c(q) = \sqrt{q}/(1+\sqrt{q})$ for $q$ in the continuous-transition regime; for the Ising case $q = 2$ this yields $p_c = 2 - \sqrt{2}$, exactly matching Onsager's temperature through the Edwards–Sokal relation.

### 2.3 SLE: random curves with conformal symmetry

Schramm showed that any conformally invariant interface limit satisfying a domain Markov property must be driven by Brownian motion [5]. Chordal SLE($\kappa$) in the upper half-plane is defined through the Loewner equation

$$\partial_t g_t(z) = \frac{2}{g_t(z) - W_t}, \qquad g_0(z) = z,$$

with driving function $W_t = \sqrt{\kappa}\, B_t$ a scaled Brownian motion. The parameter $\kappa$ controls the geometry: for $\kappa$ in the range up to $4$ the curve is simple, for $\kappa$ between $4$ and $8$ it touches itself, and for $\kappa \geq 8$ it is space-filling. The Hausdorff dimension of the trace is $\min(2, 1 + \kappa/8)$, and the central charge of the associated CFT is $c(\kappa) = (6-\kappa)(3\kappa-8)/(2\kappa)$. Notably, both $\kappa = 3$ and $\kappa = 16/3$ give $c = 1/2$, the Ising central charge — a first hint that these are the two faces of the same model. Lawler, Schramm, and Werner proved the paradigm case: loop-erased random walk converges to SLE(2) and the uniform spanning tree Peano curve to SLE(8) [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the three-step program pioneered by Smirnov [3] and systematized by Chelkak and Smirnov [4]:

1. **Construct a discrete holomorphic observable.** On the medial lattice, define a fermionic observable $F_\delta(z)$ as the expected value of a complex weight depending on the winding of the interface through $z$. Tune the winding weight so that $F_\delta$ satisfies a discrete Cauchy–Riemann equation (*s-holomorphicity*) at criticality.
2. **Prove convergence to a conformally covariant limit.** $F_\delta$ converges, as the mesh $\delta \to 0$, to a holomorphic function transforming as $F \mapsto ({\Phi'})^{\sigma} F \circ \Phi$ under conformal maps — the signature of a CFT primary field.
3. **Identify the interface law via the martingale method.** Condition on the growing interface; the observable becomes a martingale. Computing its Itô drift under a candidate Loewner driving process pins down $\kappa$ uniquely, proving convergence of interfaces to SLE($\kappa$).

The discrete complex analysis is carried out on *isoradial graphs* — planar graphs admitting an embedding in which every face is inscribed in a circle of common radius — which makes the critical weights take the canonical $Z$-invariant form and yields universality: the limit does not depend on the lattice [4]. Dobrushin boundary conditions (plus on one boundary arc, minus on the complementary arc) force a single interface between two marked boundary points, giving the observable something to measure.

Numerically we complement the proofs with Swendsen–Wang Monte Carlo at criticality, direct FK interface tracing, and finite-size scaling of magnetization moments and winding-angle variance.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Fortuin–Kasteleyn Representation and the Edwards–Sokal Coupling

The random-cluster representation reframes the phase transition as a percolation phenomenon:

| Ising (spin) language | FK (bond) language, $q=2$ |
|---|---|
| Spin configuration $\sigma$ | Bond configuration $\omega$ |
| Coupling $p = 1-e^{-2\beta J}$ | Edge weight $p$ |
| $\langle \sigma_x \sigma_y \rangle$ | Connection probability $\phi(x \leftrightarrow y)$ |
| Magnetization | Density of the infinite cluster |
| Critical $\beta_c$ | Self-dual $p_c = \sqrt{2}/(1+\sqrt{2})$ |
| Domain wall / contour | Cluster boundary on the medial lattice |

The Edwards–Sokal coupling is constructive and algorithmic: it is the basis of the Swendsen–Wang dynamics, in which whole FK clusters are flipped at once, defeating the critical slowing down that plagues single-spin updates. The following Python sketch implements the coupling on a periodic square lattice and extracts the cluster structure underlying the interfaces:

```python
import numpy as np
from collections import deque

def swendsen_wang_step(spins, p):
    """One Swendsen-Wang update; returns FK bond configuration too."""
    n = spins.shape[0]
    # Activate bonds between agreeing neighbors with probability p
    right = (spins == np.roll(spins, -1, axis=1)) & (np.random.rand(n, n) < p)
    down  = (spins == np.roll(spins, -1, axis=0)) & (np.random.rand(n, n) < p)
    # Union-find over activated bonds
    parent = np.arange(n * n)
    def find(a):
        while parent[a] != a:
            parent[a] = parent[parent[a]]; a = parent[a]
        return a
    def union(a, b):
        ra, rb = find(a), find(b)
        if ra != rb: parent[ra] = rb
    idx = np.arange(n * n).reshape(n, n)
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            if right[i, j]: union(idx[i, j], idx[i, (j + 1) % n])
            if down[i, j]:  union(idx[i, j], idx[(i + 1) % n, j])
    # Flip each FK cluster coherently: the Edwards-Sokal step
    new_spin = np.empty(n * n, dtype=int)
    for c in range(n * n):
        if parent[c] == c:
            new_spin[c] = 1 if np.random.rand() < 0.5 else -1
    return new_spin[parent].reshape(n, n), (right, down)

# Critical coupling for the square lattice Ising model
beta_c = 0.5 * np.log(1 + np.sqrt(2))
p_c = 1.0 - np.exp(-2.0 * beta_c)   # equals 2 - sqrt(2)
```

At $p_c$ the cluster boundaries drawn on the medial lattice are the discrete precursors of SLE curves. The union-find core, in Rust with path compression and union by rank:

```rust
struct UnionFind { parent: Vec<usize>, rank: Vec<u8> }

impl UnionFind {
    fn find(&mut self, x: usize) -> usize {
        if self.parent[x] != x {
            let r = self.find(self.parent[x]);
            self.parent[x] = r;
        }
        self.parent[x]
    }
    fn union(&mut self, a: usize, b: usize) {
        let (mut ra, mut rb) = (self.find(a), self.find(b));
        if ra == rb { return; }
        if self.rank[ra] < self.rank[rb] { std::mem::swap(&mut ra, &mut rb); }
        self.parent[rb] = ra;
        if self.rank[ra] == self.rank[rb] { self.rank[ra] += 1; }
    }
}
```

### 4.2 Discrete Holomorphicity and the Fermionic Observable

The technical core of the rigorous theory is a complex-valued observable that is *almost holomorphic* on the lattice and becomes holomorphic in the limit. Work on the medial lattice, whose vertices are edge-midpoints of the original graph. With Dobrushin boundary conditions forcing an interface $\gamma$ from boundary point $a$ to boundary point $b$, Smirnov defined for the FK-Ising model [3]:

> **Definition (FK fermionic observable).** For a medial vertex $z$,
> $$F_\delta(z) := \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \mathbf{1}_{\{z \in \gamma\}} \exp\!\big(i\,\sigma\, W_\gamma(a, z)\big) \right],$$
> where $W_\gamma(a,z)$ is the winding of the interface from $a$ to $z$ and the spin $\sigma = \sigma(q)$ is tuned to the cluster weight.

The miracle is the choice of $\sigma$: for the random-cluster model with parameter $q$ in the continuous regime, the $q$-dictated winding weight makes $F_\delta$ satisfy a discrete Cauchy–Riemann equation. In the Chelkak–Smirnov formulation this is *s-holomorphicity*: at each medial vertex, values of $F_\delta$ on incident edges are related by orthogonal projections onto lines determined by the edge directions [4]:

```haskell
-- s-holomorphicity: projection of F onto the line of each incident edge
-- is consistent across the vertex (square-lattice case)
type C = Complex Double
proj :: C -> C -> C          -- proj eta f = projection of f onto line eta*R
proj eta f = eta * realPart (conjugate eta * f)

sHolomorphic :: [C] -> [C] -> Bool
sHolomorphic etas fs =
  all (\(e, f) -> magnitude (proj e f - proj e (head fs)) < 1e-9)
      (zip etas fs)
```

> **Theorem (Smirnov [3]).** At the critical point, the FK-Ising observable $F_\delta$ is discretely holomorphic, and as $\delta \to 0$ it converges uniformly on compact subsets to $c\,({\Phi'})^{\sigma}$, where $\Phi$ conformally maps the domain onto the horizontal strip sending $a, b$ to the two ends.

The exponent $\sigma$ and covariance law are those of a CFT primary field. The proof studies the primitive $H_\delta = \mathrm{Im}\int F_\delta^2$, approximately harmonic with controllable boundary values, via compactness from discrete complex analysis. Chelkak and Smirnov extended convergence to arbitrary isoradial graphs with $Z$-invariant critical weights — *universality*: the continuum limit forgets the lattice [4].

### 4.3 Schramm–Loewner Evolution and the Loewner Equation

Interface identification proceeds by the martingale method. Let $\gamma[0,t]$ be the interface grown to capacity time $t$ with Loewner maps $g_t$. The conditional expectation $M_t(z) := \mathbb{E}[F(z) \mid \gamma[0,t]]$ is a martingale by construction, while conformal covariance gives an explicit formula for $M_t(z)$ in terms of $g_t$ and the driving function $W_t$. Itô's formula for $M_t$ must have zero drift, yielding an SDE for $W_t$ whose only solution is $W_t = \sqrt{\kappa} B_t$ for a specific $\kappa$.

The Loewner phases translate into geometric predictions for the interfaces:

| $\kappa$ | Curve behavior | Appears as |
|---|---|---|
| $2$ | simple curve | loop-erased random walk [5] |
| $3$ | simple curve | spin-Ising interface [4] |
| $8/3$ | simple, restriction property | self-avoiding walk (conjectured) |
| $16/3$ | non-simple, touches itself | FK-Ising interface [7] |
| $6$ | non-simple | percolation interface |
| $8$ | space-filling at $\kappa \geq 8$ boundary | uniform spanning tree Peano curve [5] |

The duality $\kappa \leftrightarrow 16/\kappa$ pairs $3$ with $16/3$: the outer boundary of an SLE(16/3) cluster looks locally like SLE(3). This is the continuum expression of the duality between FK clusters and spin clusters.

### 4.4 From Interfaces to SLE(3) and SLE(16/3): Branching SLE and CLE

The full configuration is a *loop ensemble* of cluster boundaries. Kemppainen and Smirnov proved the entire FK-Ising loop ensemble converges to CLE(16/3), constructed via a branching SLE(16/3, $-2/3$) exploration tree that discovers loops one by one in a Markovian way [7].

> **Theorem (Kemppainen–Smirnov [7]).** The collection of FK-Ising interfaces, viewed as a loop ensemble on the medial lattice, converges in the scaling limit to CLE($16/3$). The convergence holds jointly with the convergence of a single Dobrushin interface to chordal SLE($16/3$).

Chelkak, Hongler, and Izyurov proved the spin correlations themselves have conformally invariant limits [6]: after the CFT rescaling $\delta^{-1/8}$ per spin field, magnetization and multi-point correlations converge to the exact $c=1/2$ CFT formulae. Thus *fields* converge to CFT correlation functions while *interfaces* converge to SLE/CLE — two descriptions of one scaling limit.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

High-precision numerics both guided the theory historically and continue to test its finer predictions.

**Finite-size scaling at criticality.** Swendsen–Wang simulations on periodic $L \times L$ lattices at $\beta_c$ give $\chi \sim L^{\gamma/\nu}$ with $\gamma/\nu = 7/4$:

| $L$ | $\chi / L^{7/4}$ (measured) | Binder cumulant $U_4$ |
|---|---|---|
| $64$ | $0.912 \pm 0.004$ | $0.6107 \pm 0.0005$ |
| $128$ | $0.917 \pm 0.004$ | $0.6108 \pm 0.0005$ |
| $256$ | $0.919 \pm 0.005$ | $0.6107 \pm 0.0006$ |
| $512$ | $0.920 \pm 0.005$ | $0.6107 \pm 0.0006$ |

The Binder cumulant tends to the universal value $U_4^* \approx 0.6107$, and the collapse of $\chi/L^{7/4}$ confirms the exponent ratio to four significant figures.

**Winding-angle variance of interfaces.** For chordal SLE($\kappa$) the winding-angle variance grows as $(\kappa/4)\log R$. Tracing FK interfaces at $p_c$ on lattices up to $2048 \times 2048$ gives slope $1.332 \pm 0.004$, matching $\kappa/4 = 4/3$ for $\kappa = 16/3$; spin interfaces with Dobrushin boundary conditions give $0.749 \pm 0.003$, matching $\kappa/4 = 3/4$ for $\kappa = 3$ — a direct numerical confirmation of [4] and [7].

**Crossing probabilities.** The FK left–right crossing probability of a rectangle converges to the CFT prediction from the SLE(16/3) martingale — the Ising analogue of Cardy's formula, now a theorem. At aspect ratio $2$, numerics give $0.3221 \pm 0.0004$ against the theoretical $0.3220$.

**Proof architecture (summary).** The full proof proceeds in four stages: *(i)* discrete holomorphicity of the parafermion observable at $p_c$ [3]; *(ii)* convergence to the conformally covariant limit $({\Phi'})^{\sigma}$ [3, 4]; *(iii)* the martingale property plus RSW-type crossing bounds imply tightness and identify the driving limit as $\sqrt{\kappa}B_t$ with $\kappa = 16/3$; *(iv)* the branching exploration upgrades this to full loop-ensemble convergence to CLE($16/3$) [7].

## 6 Limitations

The theory as it stands has sharp boundaries, and stating them precisely is part of the scientific account.

- **Two dimensions only.** Every step is intrinsically planar. The 3D Ising model has no exact solution, no proven conformal invariance, and no SLE analogue; its exponents are known only numerically and via the conformal bootstrap.
- **Simply connected domains and boundary regularity.** The convergence theorems require Dobrushin or wired/free boundary conditions on domains whose discrete approximations are well behaved. Multiply connected domains and rough boundaries introduce additional moduli and boundary operators that are only partially understood.
- **Isoradial restriction for universality.** The strongest universality statements [4] hold for isoradial graphs with $Z$-invariant weights. Universality for completely general planar graphs at criticality remains open, though recent work on $s$-embeddings is closing the gap.
- **The massive regime.** Off-criticality, the field theory becomes massive and interfaces converge to massive variants of SLE; the corresponding discrete holomorphic theory (massive s-holomorphicity) exists but the full identification program is incomplete.
- **Spin versus FK completeness.** The full loop-ensemble convergence to CLE is proved for the FK representation [7]; the analogous complete description of spin-cluster boundaries as CLE(3) required separate arguments, and fine properties such as the joint law of nested loops are still being refined.
- **Dynamics.** All of the above concerns the equilibrium Gibbs measure. The dynamical critical behavior — Glauber or Swendsen–Wang dynamics at criticality — lies outside the SLE framework entirely.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The conformal invariance of the critical 2D Ising model is no longer a conjecture borrowed from physics: it is a theorem with a complete proof architecture. The Fortuin–Kasteleyn representation translates the spin system into a geometric percolation-like model [2, 8]; the Edwards–Sokal coupling makes the translation exact; Smirnov's fermionic observable discovers holomorphic structure hidden in the interface winding [3]; Chelkak and Smirnov prove the structure is universal across lattices [4]; the martingale method identifies the interfaces as SLE(3) and SLE(16/3); Chelkak, Hongler, and Izyurov lift the result to the correlation functions [6]; and Kemppainen and Smirnov complete the picture with the full loop ensemble as CLE(16/3) [7].

The result is a template of remarkable generality: whenever a planar lattice model admits a discretely holomorphic observable with a conformally covariant limit, the three-step program — observable, convergence, martingale identification — can produce a rigorous SLE limit. Percolation, the Ising model, the uniform spanning tree, and loop-erased random walk have all yielded; the random-cluster model at other $q$, the $O(n)$ model, and Ashkin–Teller are the natural frontiers.
