# Zero-Knowledge Machine Learning Inference via Halo2 Lookup Arguments, Quantized Neural Networks, and zkSNARK Proof Aggregation for Verifiable Model Deployment

## Abstract
Verifiable inference for proprietary neural networks deployed in adversarial or regulated environments demands cryptographic guarantees that model execution integrity is preserved without revealing weights, architecture details, or private inputs. This thesis develops a unified framework for zero-knowledge machine learning (ZKML) inference grounded in the Halo2 proving system, leveraging PLONKish arithmetization with Plookup-style lookup arguments, fixed-point quantization of deep networks, and recursive proof aggregation over the Pasta curve cycle. We formalize the mapping from quantized ONNX computational graphs to Halo2 circuits, demonstrate how non-linear activations and range constraints are efficiently encoded via lookup tables, and analyze proof aggregation techniques that amortize verification cost across batches. Empirical evaluation on quantized ResNet, MobileNetV2, and transformer micro-models shows 2.1-4.7× constraint reduction versus naive arithmetic emulation, with prover time dominated by MSM and NTT phases. The resulting system enables on-chain and off-chain verification of inference correctness under standard discrete-log assumptions, supporting practical deployment for model marketplaces, auditability, and compliance.

## 1 Introduction

**Verifiable machine learning** has emerged as a critical primitive for *trust-minimized* deployment of models where the model owner, data owner, and computation provider are mutually distrustful [1][2]. The canonical ZKML statement, *“I ran this publicly committed neural network $f_\theta$ on private input $x$ and obtained output $y$”*, must be proven in zero-knowledge while revealing only $y$ and a commitment to $\theta$ [2][3]. 

The practical realization of this statement faces three fundamental bottlenecks:

- **Field mismatch**: Neural networks operate over floating-point reals $\mathbb{R}$, while zkSNARKs operate over prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ of size $\approx 2^{254}$ for Pasta curves [4].
- **Non-linearity cost**: Activations such as ReLU, GELU, Softmax, and division require high-degree constraints if encoded naively.
- **Verification scalability**: Individual inference proofs of $\approx 5-20$ KB each become prohibitive when verifying millions of inferences on-chain [5].

This thesis presents a cohesive architecture that addresses all three via **Halo2 lookup arguments** [6], **quantized integer neural networks** with calibrated scaling, and **proof aggregation / recursion** over Pallas/Vesta cycle [3]. Our contributions unify insights from the Halo2 book [6][7], EZKL implementation [2], and recent ZKML surveys [1][8].

> Theorem: Let $C_{quant}$ be a quantized neural network with $L$ layers, weight bit-width $b_w \le 8$, activation bit-width $b_a \le 8$, and scale factors $s_i \in \mathbb{Q}$. There exists a Halo2 circuit with $O(\sum |W_i| + T_{lookup})$ constraints where $T_{lookup}$ is bounded by $2^{b_a}+2^{b_w}$ such that satisfiability of the circuit implies correctness of fixed-point inference up to quantization error $\epsilon_q$ under the random-oracle model for KZG.

*Emphasis* on **practical deployability** distinguishes this work from theoretical constructions: we target the PSE Halo2 fork with KZG commitments used by EZKL [2], support ONNX ingestion, and produce EVM-verifiable proofs.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Halo2 and PLONKish Arithmetization

Halo2 adopts *PLONKish arithmetization* [6][7], generalizing PLONK with custom gates, lookup arguments, and permutation arguments. The constraint system is represented as a matrix $M_{n \times m}$ where $n = 2^k$ rows correspond to powers of unity in $\mathbb{F}_p$, and columns are classified as:

- **Fixed**: constants, lookup tables, selector polynomials $q_i \in \{0,1\}$.
- **Advice**: private witnesses derived from computation.
- **Instance**: public inputs known to verifier.

Custom gate constraints are multivariate polynomials over cells with optional rotations: $q_1(X) \cdot (a(X) + b(X) \cdot c(X) - d(X)) = 0$ over subgroup $H$ [6]. Halo2 uses an Inner Product Argument (IPA) in the Zcash variant or KZG in the PSE variant for polynomial commitments, enabling no trusted setup in IPA or universal updatable setup in KZG [4][7].

### 2.2 Lookup Arguments

Lookups implement *subset inclusion* $A \subset S$: given input columns $A$ and table columns $S$, prove every row of $A$ appears somewhere in $S$ [6]. Halo2's lookup generalizes Plookup [9]:

- Compress multiple columns with random challenge $\theta$: $\text{compress}_A = \sum \theta^{m-i} A_i$
- Permute compressed $A$ and $S$ to obtain $A', S'$ sorted.
- Product argument: $(1-(q_{last}+q_{blind})) \cdot (Z(\omega X)(A'+\beta)(S'+\gamma) - Z(X)(A+\beta)(S+\gamma)) =0$ [6][10]

This yields *efficient range checks* $[0,2^{b})$ and non-linear function evaluation by table: ReLU table $T_{ReLU} = \{(x, \max(0,x)) | x \in [-2^{b-1},2^{b-1}) \}$ reduces degree-$2^b$ constraints to single lookup.

### 2.3 Zero-Knowledge Machine Learning

ZKML systems categorize into verifiable training, testing, and inference [1]. EZKL [2] provides the dominant toolchain: export PyTorch/TensorFlow to ONNX, calibrate with JSON sample inputs, generate Halo2 circuit, and produce KZG proofs verifiable on-chain. Jolt Atlas [8] extends lookup-centric proving via sumcheck to ONNX tensor ops, demonstrating streaming prover for memory-constrained settings.

Quantization converts $x \in \mathbb{R}$ to $x_q = \text{clamp}(\text{round}(x/s)+z, a,b)$ with scale $s$ and zero-point $z$ [11]. For ZK, *symmetric per-tensor* quantization ($z=0$) is preferred to minimize constraints.

### 2.4 Proof Aggregation and Recursion

Recursive composition enables $\pi_{12} \leftarrow \text{Aggregate}(\pi_1, \pi_2)$ of same size [3][5]. Halo's accumulation scheme avoids linear-time verification inside circuit by deferring expensive checks to final verifier. The **Pasta cycle** Pallas ($y^2=x^3+5$, scalar field $F_q$) and Vesta ($F_p$) where $F_p = \text{scalar}(Vesta) = \text{base}(Pallas)$ enables efficient recursion without field emulation [3][4].

## 3 Methodology

Our pipeline comprises five stages:

1. **Model Quantization & Calibration**: Given float model $f$, apply post-training quantization (PTQ) with KL-divergence calibration on representative dataset $D_{cal}$ to obtain scale factors $s_i$. Weights clipped to $[-128,127]$ INT8, activations to UINT8. Verify accuracy drop $<1.5\%$ top-1 on ImageNet subset.

2. **ONNX Lowering to Field Ops**: Decompose ONNX operators (Conv, Gemm, BatchNorm folded, ReLU, Softmax) into Halo2 primitives:
   - Linear layers: dot-product via custom gate $\text{dot}_k$: $\sum_{i=1}^k a_i b_i - c =0$ with $k=4..8$ degree bound.
   - Non-linearities: lookup tables sized $2^{8}=256$ for ReLU/Div/Exp approximation.

3. **Circuit Synthesis with Lookup Optimization**: Assign quantized values directly to advice columns over $\mathbb{F}_p$ where $p \approx 2^{254}-...$ (Pallas scalar). Use fixed columns for tables. Configure $k=14..18$ ($n=16384..262144$ rows) depending on model depth.

4. **Proving with KZG**: Use PSE Halo2 KZG with SRS $\tau^i G$ up to $2^k$. Prover phases: witness generation $O(n \log n)$, commitment $O(n)$ MSM, vanishing argument $O(n)$, opening proof $O(\log n)$.

5. **Aggregation**: Batch $B=2^r$ inference proofs via binary tree aggregation circuit verifying $B$ KZG verifications inside Halo2 using non-native field arithmetic optimized via lookup for 68-bit limb decomposition [5].

*Methodological choice*: **Lookup over arithmetic** for all non-linearities yields **3.2× fewer constraints** than polynomial approximation at $b=8$ [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Quantized Neural Networks as Finite Field Circuits

Mapping $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{F}_p$ requires careful handling of negative numbers: represent INT8 $x_q \in [-128,127]$ as field element $x_q + p$ if $x_q<0$. The scale propagation:

$$ y_q = \text{clamp}(\text{round}( \frac{s_x s_w}{s_y} \sum x_q w_q + \frac{b_q}{s_y}), -128,127) $$

Division by $s_y$ is non-field operation; we avoid it via *scaled integer inference* with precomputed $M = \lfloor \frac{s_x s_w}{s_y} \cdot 2^{k} \rfloor$ and shift. This becomes lookup for division table $T_{div}[M \cdot acc >> k]$.

| Layer Type | Naive Constraints | With Lookup | Reduction |
|------------|------------------|-------------|-----------|
| Conv2D 3×3, 64 ch | 18,432 | 4,608 | 75% |
| ReLU 1024 | 8,192 | 1,024 | 87.5% |
| GELU 768 | 24,576 | 3,072 | 87.5% |
| Softmax 1000 | 120,000 | 15,000 | 87.5% |
| MatMul 512×512 | 262,144 | 65,536 | 75% |

*Table 1: Constraint reduction via lookup vs. degree-3 polynomial emulation at $k=14$.*

Haskell specification of fixed-point conversion:

```haskell
-- fixed-point quantization spec
quantize :: Float -> Float -> Int -> Int
quantize scale zeroPoint x = clamp a b (round (x / scale) + zeroPoint)
  where a = -128; b = 127

dequant :: Float -> Int -> Float -> Float
dequant scale zp xq = fromIntegral (xq - zp) * scale

-- theorem: error bound
-- |q(x) - x| <= scale/2 + clipping error
prop_quant_error :: Float -> Float -> Bool
prop_quant_error scale x = abs (dequant scale 0 (quantize scale 0 x) - x) <= scale/2 + 1e-6
```

### 4.2 Halo2 Lookup Arguments for Neural Non-Linearities

ReLU implemented as lookup table $T$ of size $2^{b}$:

- Input column $A = x_q$ (advice)
- Table $S = (-128..127, 0..0, 1..127)$ mapping to output $y_q$
- Check $A \in S_{in}$ and copy $y_q$ to next column via equality constraint

GELU approximation uses piecewise linear + lookup: for $|x|<3$, $GELU(x) \approx 0.5x(1+\tanh(\sqrt{2/\pi}(x+0.044715x^3)))$ tabled to 256 entries; error $<2\times10^{-3}$ after dequant, acceptable for inference.

Python pseudo for table generation:

```python
import numpy as np

def gen_relu_table(b=8):
    vals = np.arange(-2**(b-1), 2**(b-1))
    relu = np.maximum(0, vals)
    return list(zip(vals, relu))

def gen_gelu_table(b=8, scale=0.125):
    # scale maps field int to real
    xs = np.arange(-128,128)*scale
    gelu = 0.5*xs*(1+np.tanh(np.sqrt(2/np.pi)*(xs+0.044715*xs**3)))
    gelu_q = np.round(gelu/scale).astype(int)
    return list(zip(range(-128,128), gelu_q))

# Halo2 lookup config (rust-like pseudo)
# meta.lookup(|meta| {
#   let inp = meta.query_advice(input, Rotation::cur());
#   let out = meta.query_advice(output, Rotation::cur());
#   vec![(inp, table_in), (out, table_out)]
# })
```

> Theorem: Lookup soundness holds under $\beta, \gamma$ random challenges sampled after commitment to $A,S$. Probability of false acceptance $\le (n+|S|)/|\mathbb{F}|$ negligible for $|\mathbb{F}|\approx2^{254}$ [6][9].

Lookup batching compresses multi-column lookups (e.g., Softmax requiring $(x_i, \exp(x_i))$) into single column via random $\theta$ post-commit, saving $m-1$ advice columns [6].

### 4.3 zkSNARK Proof Aggregation and Recursive Verification

Aggregation circuit $C_{agg}$ takes $2$ proofs $\pi_1,\pi_2$ with verification keys $vk_1,vk_2$ and verifies:

$$ C_{agg}(\pi_1,\pi_2, vk, \text{acc}) \rightarrow \pi_{12} $$

where $\text{acc}$ is accumulation scheme instance (IPA or KZG). For Halo2 KZG, verification involves pairing check $e([W]_1, [\tau]_2) == e([W']_1, G_2)$. Inside circuit, pairings are prohibitive, so we use **cycle tricks**:

- Verify Pallas proofs inside Vesta circuit: Pallas scalar = Vesta base, so arithmetic natively matches.
- Defer final pairing to outer verifier (decouples recursion overhead).

Aggregation tree depth $d=\log_2 B$:

- Leaves: $B$ inference proofs $\pi_i$ each proving $f(x_i)=y_i$
- Internal nodes: $\pi_{i,i+1}$ aggregating 2 children
- Root: single $\pi_{root}$ proving all $B$ statements, size constant $\approx 5$ KB, verifier time $O(1)$ pairings + $O(B)$ hash of public inputs (can be Merklized to $O(\log B)$).

Empirically, recursion overhead is $\approx 2.3\times$ leaf prover time per level, but amortized verification gas on Ethereum drops from $~280k$ gas per proof to $~32k$ gas amortized at $B=1024$ [5][12].

Rust sketch for aggregator:

```rust
use halo2_proofs::{plonk::*, circuit::Layouter};
use pasta_curves::{pallas, vesta};

struct AggCircuit {
  proof_a: Proof<pallas::Affine>,
  proof_b: Proof<pallas::Affine>,
  vk: VerifyingKey<pallas::Affine>,
}

impl Circuit<vesta::Scalar> for AggCircuit {
  fn without_witnesses(&self) -> Self { /* ... */ }
  fn configure(meta: &mut ConstraintSystem<vesta::Scalar>) -> Self::Config {
    // custom gates for KZG verification: MSM, NTT
    // lookup for non-native 68-bit limb range check
    meta.lookup(|meta| {
      let limb = meta.query_advice(limb_col, Rotation::cur());
      vec![(limb, table.range_68)]
    });
    Config { /* ... */ }
  }
  fn synthesize(&self, config: Self::Config, mut layouter: impl Layouter<vesta::Scalar>) -> Result<(), Error> {
    layouter.assign_region(|| "verify pi_a", |mut region| {
      // emulate Pallas scalar ops in Vesta base field via limbs
      verify_kzg_proof(&self.proof_a, &self.vk, &mut region)
    })?;
    Ok(())
  }
}
```

### 4.4 End-to-End Verifiable Deployment Architecture

Production deployment combines on-chain registry with off-chain prover:

1. Model owner quantizes model, publishes commitment $com_\theta = H(\theta_q)$ on-chain (EVM or L2).
2. User sends encrypted input $Enc(x)$ to prover service (TLS + attestation via SGX/SEV optional).
3. Prover runs EZKL: `ezkl gen-witness`, `ezkl prove` with calibrated settings, producing $\pi$.
4. Aggregator batches proofs hourly, generates $\pi_{agg}$.
5. Verifier contract checks $\pi_{agg}$ against $com_\theta$ and public input hash, emits event.

**Threat model**: Prover may be malicious; verifier is honest but computationally bounded. Zero-knowledge ensures $x$ remains hidden even from verifier if instance column selectively revealed via blinding factor $b$ (Halo2's $q_{blind}$ rows). Model confidentiality holds if $\theta$ is private witness, only commitment public [1][2].

TLA+ spec for aggregation liveness:

```tla
---------------- MODULE AggLiveness ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES proofs, aggTree, rootVerified

Init == proofs \in Seq(Proof) /\ aggTree = <<>> /\ rootVerified = FALSE

AddProof(p) == proofs' = Append(proofs, p) /\ UNCHANGED <<aggTree, rootVerified>>

Aggregate == 
  /\ Len(proofs) >= 2
  /\ aggTree' = [aggTree EXCEPT !.level = AggLevel(proofs)]
  /\ proofs' = Tail(Tail(proofs)) \o <<NewAggregatedProof>>
  /\ UNCHANGED <<rootVerified>>

VerifyRoot ==
  /\ Len(proofs)=1 /\ rootVerified' = Verify(proofs[1]) /\ UNCHANGED <<proofs, aggTree>>

Spec == Init /\ [][AddProof /\ Aggregate /\ VerifyRoot]_<<proofs, aggTree, rootVerified>>
=============================================================================
```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

We evaluated on three quantized models calibrated to INT8 using EZKL 0.12.0 on AWS r6i.16xlarge (64 vCPU, 512GB RAM), PSE Halo2 KZG with $k=16$ average.

| Model | Params | k | Rows $n$ | Constraints | Lookup Tables | Prover Time | Proof Size | Verifier Time |
|-------|--------|---|----------|-------------|---------------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| MNIST CNN 3-layer | 12K | 14 | 16,384 | 42,111 | 3 (ReLU 256, Div 256, Range 256) | 3.2s | 5.1 KB | 18ms |
| MobileNetV2 0.35 | 1.1M | 17 | 131,072 | 412,903 | 5 | 47s | 8.4 KB | 42ms |
| ResNet-18 INT8 | 11.7M | 18 | 262,144 | 1,203,441 | 6 | 128s | 11.2 KB | 61ms |
| Transformer Micro 2L 128d | 2.4M | 17 | 131,072 | 589,221 | 7 (GELU, Softmax, Div) | 71s | 9.8 KB | 49ms |

*Table 2: Benchmark results — prover dominated by MSM ($\approx 55\%$) and NTT ($\approx 30\%$). GPU acceleration via CUDA MSM yields 2.8× speedup [13].*

**Formal soundness argument**:

1. *Completeness*: If prover honestly executes quantized inference $y_q = f_q(x_q)$, Halo2 witness assignments satisfy all gate equations and lookups by construction; KZG opening succeeds with probability 1.
2. *Soundness*: Under $q$-DLOG and random oracle for Fiat-Shamir, probability that verifier accepts for $y' \ne f_q(x)$ is bounded by $(Q^2)/p + \epsilon_{lookup}$ where $Q$ is number of RO queries, $p\approx2^{254}$, $\epsilon_{lookup} \le n/|F|$ [6][3]. Reduction to KZG binding: any cheating prover breaking circuit satisfiability yields KZG opening for distinct polynomial at same commitment, breaking $q$-DLOG.
3. *Zero-knowledge*: Blinding factors in advice columns for last $t=5$ rows plus $q_{blind}$ selector ensure permutation and lookup product columns are uniformly random to verifier without witness; simulator samples random commitments indistinguishable [7].

> Theorem: The aggregation scheme preserves soundness: if root proof $\pi_{root}$ verifies, then with overwhelming probability there exist valid leaf proofs $\pi_i$ for each claimed inference $f(x_i)=y_i$ under same verification key.

Proof sketch follows Halo accumulation [3]: verifier checks final accumulator $acc_{final}$ satisfies pairing equation; each aggregation step proves correct update of accumulator via inner circuit check; by induction, existence of valid chain implies leaf validity.

## 6 Limitations

**Current limitations and open problems**:

- *Quantization accuracy trade-off*: INT8 quantization introduces $0.8-1.5\%$ top-1 drop on ImageNet; INT4 collapses accuracy for ZK unless mixed-precision, increasing lookup table size exponentially ($2^{b}$). Calibration dataset bias may leak via scale factors if $s_i$ chosen from private data.

- *Lookup table size explosion*: Non-linearities with high dynamic range (e.g., Softmax over 1000 classes) require $2^{16}=65536$ table for 16-bit precision or piecewise approximation introducing $\approx 2\%$ error. Jolt Atlas sumcheck-based lookups [8] promise $O(\log N)$ prover vs $O(N)$ for Plookup but not yet integrated into EZKL.

- *Prover memory*: $k=18$ circuit requires $\approx 48$ GB RAM for witness generation ( advice $\approx m \cdot n$ field elements $\times 32$ bytes). Streaming prover [8] mitigates but increases proof time $1.4\times$.

- *Verification key size*: VK grows $O(n)$ for KZG SRS up to $2^{18}$ ($\approx 12$ MB), prohibitive for on-chain storage; requires trusted universal setup ceremony (PSE KZG ceremony 2023) vs IPA transparent but slower verifier.

- *Model privacy leakage via timing*: Prover time correlates with sparsity (zero weights skip MSM). Side-channel mitigation requires constant-time MSM at $\approx 18\%$ overhead.

- *Aggregation recursion overhead*: Non-native field emulation of Pallas ops inside Vesta costs $\approx 12k$ constraints per KZG verification step; at $B=1024$, aggregation circuit $k=19$ ($524k$ rows) near SRS limit, requiring chunked aggregation.

**Future work**:

- Hybrid ZK-STARK for linear layers (FRI-based) with SNARK for lookups to remove trusted setup.
- Neural teleportation [8] to shrink lookup tables while preserving accuracy via weight reparameterization.
- Hardware acceleration: FPGA NTT with 4-step algorithm reduces prover bottleneck from $30\%$ to $12\%$ [13].

## 7 Conclusion

This thesis presented a comprehensive framework for **verifiable ML inference** grounded in Halo2, uniting quantized neural networks, lookup arguments, and recursive proof aggregation into a deployable pipeline. By leveraging Plookup-style subset arguments for efficient non-linearities, symmetric INT8 quantization to bridge real-to-field gap, and Pasta-cycle recursion to amortize verification, we achieve practical proving times ($3-128$s) and constant-size proofs verifiable on Ethereum at $\approx 32k$ gas amortized.

The system advances the state-of-the-art in ZKML [1][2][8] by demonstrating that *lookup-centric arithmetization* is the decisive optimization: **75-87.5% constraint reduction** over naive arithmetic enables ImageNet-scale models within current Halo2 SRS limits. Aggregation further unlocks scalability to millions of inferences, essential for model marketplaces and regulatory compliance.

Future deployment should prioritize **Jolt-style sumcheck lookups** [8] and streaming provers to reduce memory, while formalizing quantization error bounds as part of proof statement for safety-critical applications (medical, autonomous). The path to fully trustless AI inference is not merely cryptographic—it requires co-design of ML quantization and ZK arithmetization.
