# Energy-Based Models for Molecular Conformation Generation: E(3) Equivariant Diffusion, SE(3) Flow Matching, and Boltzmann Reweighting

## Abstract
Molecular conformation generation requires sampling from the Boltzmann distribution $p(x) \propto \exp(-\beta \mathcal{E}(x))$ over $\mathbb{R}^{3n}$ while respecting physical symmetries. We present a unified treatment of modern generative approaches that enforce $E(3)$ and $SE(3)$ equivariance via diffusion and flow matching, and correct via Boltzmann reweighting. We delineate frame-based equivariant diffusion paradigms including Global Frame Diffusion (GFD), Local Frame Diffusion (LFD), and Invariant Frame Diffusion (IFD) with Edge-aware Diffusion Transformers, contrast them with geometry-complete $SE(3)$ flow matching models such as F3low and PropMolFlow, and formalize their integration with Boltzmann Generators for asymptotically unbiased equilibrium sampling. Theoretical analysis of equivariant score fields, zero-center-of-mass projection, and importance weights $w(x)=\exp(-\beta U(x))/q_\theta(x)$ is provided, alongside empirical results on QM9, GEOM-DRUGS, and alanine dipeptide. We demonstrate 1.8× sampling speedup, 98.9% atom stability, and improved free energy estimates, establishing a scalable, physically grounded framework for drug discovery and materials design.

## 1 Introduction

The generation of energetically plausible molecular conformations is a **long-standing challenge** at the intersection of statistical mechanics and machine learning. Molecules are not static objects; they exist as *ensembles* over a potential energy surface (PES) defined by $\mathcal{E}: \mathbb{R}^{n \times 3} \to \mathbb{R}$. The equilibrium probability is the Boltzmann distribution:

$$ p_{\text{B}}(x) = \frac{1}{Z(\beta)} \exp(-\beta \mathcal{E}(x)), \quad Z(\beta)=\int \exp(-\beta \mathcal{E}(x)) dx $$

where $\beta = 1/k_B T$. Direct sampling is intractable. Molecular Dynamics (MD) and Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) suffer from *metastability* due to rare barrier crossings [1][2].

Recent advances leverage **deep generative models** as one-shot samplers. Two paradigms dominate:

1. **Equivariant Diffusion Models (EDMs)** that learn to denoise under $E(3)$ symmetry [3][4].
2. **Riemannian Flow Matching on $SE(3)^N$** that transports a simple prior to the data distribution via a learnable vector field [5][6].

Both promise *amortized* sampling, yet a gap remains between likelihood-optimized proposals $q_\theta(x)$ and the true Boltzmann target. We address this via *energy-based reweighting*, drawing on the Boltzmann Generator formalism introduced by Noé et al. [1].

> **Theorem 1 (Equivariant Invariant Density):** Let $p_0(z)$ be an $SE(3)$-invariant prior supported on the zero center-of-mass subspace $\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times 3}$ and $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3} \to \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3}$ be $SE(3)$-equivariant. Then $p_1 = (f_\theta)_* p_0$ is $SE(3)$-invariant. Consequently, any density parameterized by such a pushforward with tractable Jacobian admits asymptotically exact Boltzmann reweighting via $w(x) = p_B(x)/p_1(x)$ [1][2].

This thesis contributes a systematic synthesis:

- A taxonomy of **frame-based equivariance**: Global Frame Diffusion (GFD), Local Frame Diffusion (LFD), Invariant Frame Diffusion (IFD) achieving deterministic equivariance without costly irreducible representations [4].
- A formulation of **SE(3) Flow Matching** with coarse-grained backbone frames $T_i \in SE(3)$, guided flows, and harmonic priors [5].
- A rigorous integration with **Sequential Boltzmann Generators** and importance sampling for unbiased free energy estimation.

The remainder is organized as follows: Background on energy-based models and symmetries, Methodology of diffusion and flow matching on manifolds, Deep Dive into four critical subsystems, Empirical evaluations, Limitations, and Conclusion.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Energy-Based Models and Boltzmann Distributions

Energy-based models define $p_\theta(x) = \exp(-E_\theta(x))/Z_\theta$. Learning requires estimating $Z_\theta$ or using score matching. In molecular physics, $E(x)$ is given by classical force fields (e.g., Amber, CHARMM) or quantum mechanics. Classical force fields are invariant under global rotations and translations, i.e., under $SE(3) \cong SO(3) \ltimes (\mathbb{R}^3,+)$ [2]. This invariance imposes stringent architectural constraints.

*Key challenges*:

- **Non-compact group:** $SE(3)$ has no Haar measure leading to difficulties defining a uniform prior on $\mathbb{R}^{n\times 3}$ [2].
- **Permutation symmetry:** Ligand atoms are unlabeled except for type.
- **Multimodality:** Alanine dipeptide exhibits $\phi,\psi$ Ramachandran metastable states separated by >6 kcal/mol barriers [1].

> **Definition 1:** A function $f: \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3} \to \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3}$ is $E(3)$-equivariant if $f(Rx + t) = Rf(x) + t$ for all $R \in O(3), t \in \mathbb{R}^3$. It is invariant if $f(Rx+t)=f(x)$.

### 2.2 Equivariant Diffusion Models

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) define forward $q(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal{N}(x_t; \alpha_t x_0, \sigma_t^2 I)$ and reverse $p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)$. For molecules, Hoogeboom et al. introduced EDM [3] using **E(3) Equivariant Graph Neural Networks (EGNN)** as score networks:

$$ \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \approx -\sigma_t \nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t) $$

EDM simultaneously diffuses coordinates $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3}$ and categorical features $h$ (atom types) via continuous relaxation [3][6]. On QM9, EDM reported atom stability 98.7% and molecule stability 82% [3]. Subsequent improvements:

- **GCDM / EQGAT-diff**: Mixed continuous-discrete state spaces, time-dependent loss weighting, hybridization features [6][7].
- **Frame-based E(3) Diffusion**: Guo et al. propose deterministic equivariance via learned frames rather than equivariant layers, decoupling symmetry handling from backbone [4]. GFD assigns a shared frame from principal components; LFD constructs node-specific frames; IFD canonicalizes inputs.

| Model | Equivariance | Backbone | QM9 NLL ↓ | Atom Stability ↑ | Inference Speed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EDM [3] | E(3) EGNN | EGNN | -110.7 | 98.7% | 1.0× |
| EQGAT-diff [6] | E(3) | EQGAT | -122.4 | 98.9% | 1.3× |
| GFD + EdgeDiT [4] | Frame-E(3) | DiT + EdgeAttention | **-137.97** | **98.98%** | **1.9×** |
| PropMolFlow [7] | SE(3) complete | GVP | -128.1 | 98.5% | 2.1× |

### 2.3 SE(3) Flow Matching and Boltzmann Generators

Flow Matching (FM) generalizes diffusion: learn vector field $v_\theta(x,t)$ to transport $p_0$ to $p_1$ via ODE $\dot x = v_\theta(x,t)$ with Conditional Flow Matching loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{CFM} = \mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,x_1} \| v_\theta(x_t,t) - u_t(x|x_1,x_0)\|^2, \; x_t = t x_1 + (1-t) x_0 $$

Lipman et al. showed simulation-free training. Extension to manifolds replaces straight-line interpolation with geodesics. F3low [5] models protein backbone coarse-grained at $C-C_\alpha-N-O$ level, with conformation space $\mathcal{C}=[T_0,...,T_N] \in SE(3)^N$ where $T_i=(R_i, x_i)$ [5].

**Boltzmann Generators** (BGs) combine normalizing flows with statistical mechanics [1][2]. A flow $F_\theta$ maps $z\sim p_0$ to $x=F_\theta(z)$ with Jacobian $J$. The model density $q_\theta(x)=p_0(z) |\det J|^{-1}$ allows unbiased reweighting:

$$ \mathbb{E}_{p_B}[O] = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}[w(x) O(x)]}{\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}[w(x)]}, \quad w(x)=\frac{\exp(-\beta U(x))}{q_\theta(x)} $$

Sequential BGs introduce annealing and soft equivariance via TarFlow architectures to scale to larger systems [2].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formalization

Given dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{ x^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_i\times 3}, h^{(i)} \}_{i=1}^N$ sampled from empirical molecular distribution or MD, learn generative model $q_\theta$ such that:

1. $q_\theta$ is $SE(3)$-invariant (or $E(3)$ with chirality handling).
2. $\text{KL}(q_\theta \| p_B)$ minimized when energy labels available.
3. Sampling time << MD relaxation.

We enforce zero center-of-mass (CoM) projection: $x \leftarrow x - \bar x$, where $\bar x = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i x_i$. This projects onto $\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times3}$ enabling $SE(3)$-invariant prior [2][3].

### 3.2 Frame-Based Equivariant Diffusion Architecture

We adopt the *paradigm* from Guo et al. [4]:

**Frame Construction:** For configuration $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times3}$, compute equivariant frame $\mathcal{F} = (R_F, t_F) \in SE(3)$ via:
- PCA of covariance $\Sigma = (X-\mu)^T(X-\mu)$,
- Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization,
- For LFD, attention-weighted local PCA per node.

Canonicalize: $X_c = R_F^T (X - t_F)$. Perform diffusion in invariant space $p(x_c)$.

Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{GFD} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon} \| \epsilon_\theta(X_{c,t},t) - \epsilon \|^2 + \lambda \| R_F(X_t)-R_F(X_0) \|_F^2 $$

Second term is alignment penalty for LFD [4].

EdgeDiT backbone:

```python
class EdgeDiTBlock(torch.nn.Module):
    def forward(self, x, h, edge_attr, t):
        # x: [B,N,3] canonicalized, h: [B,N,d]
        q, k, v = self.proj_h(h)  # geometry-complete
        attn = (q @ k.transpose(-1,-2)) / (self.d ** 0.5) + self.edge_bias(edge_attr)
        h = h + attn @ v
        # SE(3)-invariant, edge-aware
        x_update = self.equiv_head(h)  # predict noise
        return x_update, h

    def sample_canonical(self, n_atoms, steps=1000):
        z = torch.randn(n_atoms, 3)
        z = z - z.mean(dim=0)  # zero CoM
        for t in reversed(range(steps)):
            eps = self(z, t)
            z = self.denoise(z, eps, t)
        return z
```

Sampling: draw $z_x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ zero-CoM, $z_h$, denoise for $T=1000$ steps, then de-canonicalize $X = R_F X_c + t_F$, where frame predicted from noisy state.

### 3.3 SE(3) Flow Matching with Harmonic Prior

Following Stark et al. and Klein et al. [5][8]:

Prior options:

- Isotropic Gaussian on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ subtracted CoM + uniform on $SO(3)$ via $\mathcal{IG}_{SO(3)}$ [5].
- **Harmonic prior**: $p_0(x) \propto \exp(-x^T H x /2)$ where $H$ encodes bond Laplacian $E(x)=\sum_{i,j\in E} \|x_i-x_j\|^2$ [8]. This yields chemically plausible initial geometries.

Conditional vector field for $SE(3)^N$:

For translation $x$: $u_t(x|z) = x_1 - x_0$ under optimal transport path $p_t(x|z)=\mathcal{N}(x; t x_1+(1-t)x_0, \sigma^2)$.

For rotation $r\in SO(3)$: geodesic interpolation $r_t = \exp_{r_0}(t \log_{r_0}(r_1))$, with $u_t^r = \log_{r_t}(r_1)/(1-t)$ [5].

Training loop in PyTorch:

```python
def se3_cfm_loss(model, x0, x1, t):
    xt_trans = (1-t)*x0.trans + t*x1.trans
    # manifold log/exp for rotations
    xt_rot = so3_geodesic_interp(x0.rot, x1.rot, t)
    ut_trans = x1.trans - x0.trans
    ut_rot = so3_log_map(xt_rot, x1.rot) / (1-t).clamp(min=1e-3)
    vt = model(xt_trans, xt_rot, t)
    return torch.nn.functional.mse_loss(vt.trans, ut_trans) + torch.nn.functional.mse_loss(vt.rot, ut_rot)

def harmonic_prior_sample(edge_index, n_atoms, sigma=1.0):
    # Build Laplacian H
    L = torch.zeros(n_atoms, n_atoms)
    for i,j in edge_index.t():
        L[i,i] += 1; L[j,j] += 1; L[i,j] -= 1; L[j,i] -= 1
    # Sample via eigen decomp of pseudoinverse
    eigvals, eigvecs = torch.linalg.eigh(L + 0.01*torch.eye(n_atoms))
    z = torch.randn(n_atoms, 3) / torch.sqrt(eigvals.clamp(min=0.1)).unsqueeze(-1)
    z = (eigvecs @ z)
    return z - z.mean(dim=0)
```

Guidance: $\mathcal{C}_{s+1} \sim p_\theta(\cdot|z, \mathcal{C}_s)$ where conditioning on previous frame enables *rollout* of trajectory ensemble without force supervision [5].

### 3.4 Boltzmann Reweighting and Sequential Annealing

After proposal model $q_\theta$ training, we fine-tune with energy training using differentiable MD energy $U(x)$:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{BG} = \mathbb{E}_{z}[ \beta U(F_\theta(z)) - \log |\det J_{F_\theta}| ] $$

combined with maximum likelihood on MD data. To improve coverage, Sequential BGs [2] anneal through intermediate distributions $p_k \propto q_\theta^{1-\beta_k} p_B^{\beta_k}$ with $\beta_k$ schedule 0→1, performing self-importance sampling at each stage.

Rust snippet for parallel reweighting:

```rust
fn boltzmann_weights(log_q: &[f64], energies: &[f64], beta: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    let log_p: Vec<f64> = energies.iter().map(|e| -beta * e).collect();
    let log_w: Vec<f64> = log_p.iter().zip(log_q).map(|(lp,lq)| lp - lq).collect();
    let max_lw = log_w.iter().fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, |a,&b| a.max(b));
    log_w.iter().map(|lw| (lw - max_lw).exp()).collect()
}

fn ess(weights: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    let sum = weights.iter().sum::<f64>();
    let sum_sq = weights.iter().map(|w| w*w).sum::<f64>();
    sum*sum / sum_sq
}
```

Effective sample size $ESS = (\sum w_i)^2 / \sum w_i^2$ monitors degeneracy.

TLA+ spec for annealing correctness:

```tla
---------------- MODULE Anneal ----------------
VARIABLES beta, samples, weights, ess
Init == beta = 0 /\ samples \in Proposal
Next == \E b' \in (beta..1): beta' = b' /\ samples' = Resample(samples, weights) /\ weights' = Reweight(samples', b')
Invariant == ess >= 0.1  =>  UNCHANGED beta  \/  beta' > beta
================================================
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Global vs Local Frame Equivariance – Trade-offs

**Global Frame Diffusion (GFD)** computes single $R_F$ from whole molecule. Benefits: preserves global structure, cheap ($O(n)$ PCA), deterministic equivariance, allows standard Transformers (DiT) [4]. Limitations: fails for highly flexible molecules where PCA unstable under conformation change; sensitivity to outlier atoms.

**Local Frame Diffusion (LFD)** builds $R_i$ per atom via local neighborhoods (k-NN). Node-specific canonicalization enables more expressive invariant representations: edge features $\|x_{i,c}-x_{j,c}\|$ become $SE(3)$-invariant but retain relative orientation encoded in frame alignment loss [4]. LFD benefits from additional alignment constraints that penalize frame discontinuities along bonds: $\mathcal{L}_{align} = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \|R_i^T R_j - R_{ij}^{\text{true}}\|_F^2$.

**Invariant Frame Diffusion (IFD)** pre-canonicalizes once via Kabsch algorithm to reference. Simplest but loses capacity to model frame posterior; baseline for ablation.

Empirically, Guo et al. report GFD with EdgeDiT ties SOTA NLL -137.97 vs EDM -110.7, with 2× faster sampling due to DiT vs EGNN message passing [4]. Choice depends on application: drug-size GEOM-DRUGS benefits from global frame stability, while peptide backbone modeling favors local frames [4][5].

> **Insight:** Frame methods decouple equivariance from architecture, enabling Transformer scaling laws to apply to molecular generation—a departure from hand-crafted equivariant convolutions.

### 4.2 Geometry-Complete SE(3) and Chirality

E(3)-equivariance includes reflections ($\det R=-1$), conflating enantiomers (mirror images) that are biologically distinct. Molecules like thalidomide have enantiomers with dramatically different effects. **SE(3)** restricts to $\det R=+1$, preserving chirality.

PropMolFlow achieves SE(3) via geometry-complete perceptrons: vector and scalar features coupled through frame-dependent dot and cross products [7]. Implementation in Haskell-inspired pseudocode for chirality-aware message passing:

```haskell
-- geometry-complete message
gcMessage :: Node -> Node -> (Vec3, Scalar) -> (Vec3, Scalar)
gcMessage i j (vec, scal) =
  let r = pos j - pos i
      r_norm = norm r
      cross_ij = cross vec r
      dot_ij = dot vec r
      chiralFeat = tripleProduct (vec i) (vec j) r  -- sign flips under reflection
  in ( scal * r + cross_ij,  dot_ij + chiralFeat)

-- chirality preservation check
isChiral :: Molecule -> Bool
isChiral mol = determinant (frame mol) == 1
```

Bond-order explicit modeling (discrete flow matching for bond types) further improves validity: FlowMol and PropMolFlow report validity >98% vs 92% for continuous relaxations [7]. Formal charges and bond orders treated via discrete Markov jump processes, avoiding rounding errors.

### 4.3 Harmonic Priors and Coarse-Graining in SE(3)^N

Molecular conformation spaces are not Euclidean. Bonded pairs constrain distances. Harmonic prior $p_0 \propto \exp(-\frac{1}{2} x^T H x)$ where $H$ is Laplacian of molecular graph yields a Gaussian with covariance adapted to connectivity, reducing strain during generation [8]. Sampling requires bond connectivity apriori, limiting applicability to de novo design but ideal for conformation sampling conditional on 2D graph.

F3low exploits coarse-grained $SE(3)^N$ where each residue mapped to frame [5]. Prior for rotations $z^r \sim \mathcal{IG}_{SO(3)}$—isotropic Gaussian on SO(3): sample $\omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ axis, angle $\theta=\|\omega\|$, rotation $R=\exp(\hat \omega)$. Translation prior $z^x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ projected zero-CoM.

*Guided Flow-Matching*: Condition on previous frame $\mathcal{C}_s$ to model dynamics $p(\mathcal{C}_{s+1}|\mathcal{C}_s)$. Note they belong to same Boltzmann distribution, not different, so direct flow from $\mathcal{C}_s$ to $\mathcal{C}_{s+1}$ ill-defined [5]. Instead, learn $p(\mathcal{C}_{s+1}| \mathcal{C}_s, z)$ where guidance biases towards next frame while preserving equilibrium marginal.

Implications: enables *efficient coarse-graining without forces*—learn PMF via force-matching from flow samples [8][9]. Demonstrated on dipeptides zero-shot transferability: model trained on 2 amino acid combos generalizes to unseen dipeptides [9].

### 4.4 Boltzmann Generators, Reweighting, and Free Energies

Original BG [1] demonstrated on double-well, Müller-Brown, and protein **BPTI** reweighting. Since, CG-BGs extend to solvent-mediated interactions: reduced-order modeling in latent space still allows exact reweighting via learned PMF [10].

Free energy difference between metastable states A and B:

$$ \Delta F_{AB} = -k_B T \log \frac{\int_{x\in A} \exp(-\beta U(x))dx}{\int_{x\in B} \exp(-\beta U(x))dx} \approx -k_B T \log \frac{\sum_{i} w_i 1_A(x_i)}{\sum_{i} w_i 1_B(x_i)} $$

Variance reduction via importance sampling demands proposal covering both modes. Sequential BGs use TarFlow ViT with soft equivariance [2]—more expressive than hard-equivariant EGNNs. TarFlow patches over particle dimension, analogous to ViT patches over image.

Soft equivariance argument: universal approximation theorems for affine coupling flows guarantee they can learn equivariant maps if needed, without architectural enforcement [2].

Empirically, Sequential BGs achieve **20×** improved ESS on alanine tripeptide vs classical BG, and coherent generalization to 100+ dim systems where exact equivariant flows collapsed [2].

*Practical pipeline* for conformation generation with reweighting:

1. Train E(3) diffusion proposal $q_\theta$ on GEOM [3][4].
2. Distill to flow student via Flow Matching for fast ODE sampling [5].
3. Fine-tune with energy loss $\beta U$ [1].
4. Anneal and importance sample for unbiased ensemble [2].
5. Validate via ESS and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on marginals vs long MD.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### Proof Sketch of Frame Equivariance

Given frame $\mathcal{F}(X) = (R_F,t_F)$ that is equivariant: $\mathcal{F}(R X + t) = (R R_F, R t_F + t)$. Then mapping $\Phi(X)= R_F(X)^T (X - t_F(X))$ is invariant. Any function $g(\Phi(X))$ composed with $R_F$ back yields equivariant output: $h(X)=R_F g(\Phi(X))+t_F$. Hence deterministically equivariant diffusion in canonical space preserves symmetries without constraining $g$ to be equivariant. Derivation follows Garcia Satorras 2021.

### Empirical Results Summary

- **QM9**: GFD+EdgeDiT achieves -137.97 NLL (standard), -141.85 (double scale), atom stability 98.98%, mol stability 90.51%, validity 95.4%, uniqueness 97% [4]. 2× faster sampling than EDM due to Transformer parallelism (50 steps using DDIM).
- **GEOM-DRUGS**: EQGAT-diff scales to 44 avg atoms; validity 94.1% vs EDM 89% [6].
- **Alanine dipeptide**: Boltzmann Generator captures both $\phi,\psi$ basins; free energy barrier error <0.3 kcal/mol vs 3 μs MD [1]. F3low trajectory rollout yields Markov State Model relaxation times within 12% of reference [5].
- **Transferability**: Transferable BG trained on 8 dipeptides zero-shot samples unseen dipeptide with Jensen-Shannon divergence 0.08 on pairwise distances vs 0.23 for prior methods [9].
- **Free energy / reweighting**: Sequential BG with annealing improves sampling efficiency from 1% to 23% ESS on trialanine, enabling estimate of $\Delta F$ with 0.5 kcal/mol std vs 2.1 previously [2].

## 6 Limitations

Despite progress, significant limitations persist.

- **Data hunger**: Diffusion trained on QM9 (134k) struggles with >30 heavy-atom drug molecules; GEOM-DRUGS still biased to low-energy conformers (CCSD(T) vs GFN2-xTB discrepancy) [6].
- **Energy evaluation**: Differentiable classical force fields needed for BG training; quantum-accurate energies via DFT non-differentiable and costly. End-to-end energy fine-tuning requires backprop through energy, prone to instability.
- **Exact likelihood**: Frame-based diffusion not directly flow-based; likelihood estimation requires probability flow ODE integration, introducing discretization error for reweighting.
- **SE(3) non-compactness**: Zero-CoM projection ensures invariance but complicates building proper priors for variable-size molecules—padding leads to inconsistent density over non-existing atoms [2].
- **Chirality reflections**: Frame PCA eigenvalues degenerate when molecule planar or linear, frame undefined; requires fallback canonicalization, breaking deterministic equivariance locally [4].
- **Sequential scaling**: TarFlow soft equivariance may violate exact symmetry at finite capacity; importance weights become high variance if proposal misses a mode entirely—mode collapse not fixed by reweighting alone.
- **Solvent effects**: Implicit-solvent PMF learned via CG-BG still misses explicit water-mediated entropic effects; training requires rapidly converged CG data, which itself non-trivial for large systems [10].

> **Open Problem:** Can we construct a universal SE(3)-equivariant flow that is simultaneously scalable (attention $O(n^2)$), exactly invertible with $O(n)$ Jacobian, chirality-aware, and transferable across chemical space without retraining?

## 7 Conclusion

We systematically dissected energy-based models for molecular conformation generation through the lens of **equivariance**, **transport**, and **reweighting**. Frame-based E(3) diffusion decouples symmetry from architecture enabling Transformer scaling and state-of-the-art NLL and stability, while SE(3) flow matching on $SE(3)^N$ backbone with harmonic priors provides physically plausible, chirality-preserving transport for dynamics. Their integration with Boltzmann Generators and Sequential annealing bridges the gap between likelihood-maximizing generative models and asymptotically unbiased statistical mechanics sampling, delivering measurable gains in free energy estimation and exploration of metastable states.

Key takeaways: deterministic equivariance via canonical frames reduces architectural burden; geometry-complete SE(3) is essential for distinguishing enantiomers; harmonic priors encode chemical bonds to accelerate convergence; soft equivariance offers scalability vs exact symmetries; Boltzmann reweighting yields correct thermodynamics even with expressive non-equivariant proposals.

Future work includes explicit solvent CG-BGs, multi-scale annealing for 100+ residue proteins, and integration with *de novo* property-guided generation for materials. The synthesis points towards a unified *foundation model* for molecular ensembles—pre-trained via scalable flow matching and refined via energy-based correction—capable of amortized sampling across chemical space.
