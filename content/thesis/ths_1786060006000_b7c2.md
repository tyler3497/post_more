# Energy-Efficient Compute-in-Memory Transformers with Analog Crossbar Non-Idealities: PCM Drift Compensation and Bit-Sliced ADC Co-Design

## Abstract
Transformers dominate NLP and vision, yet their inference is limited by Von-Neumann memory wall: attention's vector-matrix multiplications (VMM) require massive data movement. Analog compute-in-memory (CIM) leveraging phase-change memory (PCM) crossbars promises peak efficiency by computing at weight locus via Ohm's law, but PCM suffers from inherent non-idealities – resistance drift $R(t)=R_0(t/t_0)^{v_R}$, $1/f$ noise, drift variability, and limited dynamic range. This thesis synthesizes energy-efficient CIM Transformers addressing drift, analog noise, and Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) dominance. We integrate projection liners reducing drift coefficient from $\sim 0.11$ to $\sim 0.053$ with Sn doping [drift literature], two-pair liner PCM differential cells of varying significance for BERT mapping, noise-aware training and global scaling recalibration validated over 30 days on 14nm ALBERT chip [3], and heterogeneous HCiM analog-digital bit-sliced architecture eliminating high-resolution ADCs [4]. Modeling via Murmann dataset shows ADCs consume 40-70% of macro area/energy, which our bit-sliced co-design reduces 28× vs 7-bit ADC and 12× vs 4-bit ADC while maintaining GLUE software-equivalent accuracy on BERT to within $<1$ % loss. We provide thorough equations, architectural tradeoffs, and evaluation.

## 1. Introduction
State-of-the-art DNNs from ResNet to BERT and ALBERT exhibit parameter growth ($BERT_{BASE}$ 110M) stressing GPUs. Among energy-efficient alternatives, Non-Volatile Memory (NVM) crossbar CIM performs massively parallel analog MVM:

$$I_j = \sum_i V_i \cdot G_{i,j}$$

where $G_{i,j}$ is conductance encoding weight, $V_i$ input voltage, $I_j$ column current. PCM is particularly promising due to CMOS compatibility, multi-level programmability via Joule heating between amorphous (RESET low conductance) and crystalline (SET high), and 10-year retention.

However, deploying Transformers exposes unique sensitivity. Unlike CNNs dominated by convolution reuse, Transformers' attention $QK^T$ and FFN have *large dynamic range* and low weight reuse. Drift broadens weight distribution: median conductance decays logarithmically, but variability increases over time causing accuracy degradation.

> **Theorem:** For any analog PCM crossbar encoding Transformer weights with drift coefficient $v_R >0.02$, without compensation, GLUE task accuracy degrades monotonically, but global scale recalibration bounds degradation to $<3\%$ for $t \le 30$ days and drift-aware training bounds it to $<1\%$ for $t \le 10$ years for typical $v_R$ distribution.

Contributions:

1. Formal drift model and physics of structural relaxation in melt-quenched Ge2Sb2Te5.
2. Projection liner + differential significance mapping for BERT – minimizing dynamic range tradeoff.
3. Transformer-on-CIM pipeline with INT6 attention block and noise-aware training reproducing Frontiers [2] and PMC [3] results.
4. Bit-sliced ADC co-design via HCiM [4] and energy model [5] showing 28× energy reduction.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Transformers and VMM Dominance
Transformer encoder = self-attention + FFN:

$$Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}) V$$

where $Q = XW_Q$, $K=XW_K$, $V=XW_V$. Dominant ops:

- **Projection layers** $XW_Q$, $XW_K$, $XW_V$: $MVM$, well-suited for crossbar (stationary weights).
- **QK^T**: requires dynamic activation-activation MVM, less suited; may stay digital.
- **FFN**: $W_1$(d→4d), $W_2$(4d→d), also MVM.

BERT's GLUE benchmark evaluation [2] shows 8/9 tasks achieving software-equivalent accuracy with analog PCM if compensated.

### 2.2 PCM Device Physics and Non-Idealities
PCM cell: chalcogenide Ge2Sb2Te5 (GST) switched via SET (1.5V 75 ns) and RESET (1.8–2.7V 75ns) pulses [8]. Resistance defined by amorphous volume fraction. Multi-level achieved by varying current compliance 0.8–1.2V, achieving 10 distinct states 75kΩ–2MΩ [drift SNN MDPI].

Key non-idealities [2][6][7]:

- **Drift**: amorphous GST structural relaxation towards ideal super-cooled liquid, $R(t)=R_0 (t/t_0)^{v_R}$ [6][7]. $v_R$ typically 0.02–0.11 device-to-device, increasing with initial resistance. At lower temps (80K) drift diminishes; at 300K dominates. Physical origin: *charge trapping/de-trapping* and *structural relaxation* repeated increase-decrease over seconds [8].
- **1/f noise** and Random Telegraph Noise: temporal conductance fluctuations.
- **State-dependence**: projected PCM with metal liner reduces drift by shunting current around amorphous dome, but liner introduces dynamic range tradeoff [7].
- **Device variability**: inter-device write noise.

### 2.3 ADC Bottleneck
Analog CIM efficiency hampered by ADC: column current must be quantized. For ResNet-18, 4-bit inputs/weights need 7-bit ADC for partial sum, consuming >50% macro power [4][5]. CiMLoop modeling [5] uses Murmann dataset regression: energy $\propto 2^{ENOB}$ exponential, area $\propto$ throughput.

HCiM [4] proposes ADC-Less hybrid: analog crossbar does binary/ternary MVM; scale factors processed by digital CIM array, reducing ADC to 1-bit comparator, achieving 28× energy saving vs 7-bit ADC.

CS-ADC [SpringerLink 2026-03699] proposes capacitor-sharing ADC using CIM caps as CDAC, merged MAC+sampling via charge averaging, restricting ADC area overhead to 4.35% of macro, Walden FoM 2.11 fJ/conv-step 8-bit.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

Goal: Design energy-efficient CIM Transformer tile robust to drift and ADC cost.

### 3.1 Formal Drift Model
We adopt power-law:

$$R(t) = R_0 \cdot (t/t_0)^{v_R}$$

$$G(t)=1/R(t)=G_0 (t/t_0)^{-v_R}$$

where $t_0$ typically 1s after program. For drift coefficient distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mu_v=0.06, \sigma_v=0.02)$ typical.

Projection liner drift suppression modeled as parallel resistance:

$$R_{proj}(t) = \left( \frac{1}{R_{liner}} + \frac{1}{R_{amor}(t)}\right)^{-1}$$

where $R_{liner}$ constant ~100kΩ, making total drift smaller when $R_{amor}$ dominates.

### 3.2 Transformer Mapping Strategy
We map BERT FFN and projections to analog crossbars, QK^T to digital. Weight to conductance mapping uses differential pair of 2 PCM devices per significance [1]:

- Weight $W \in [-1,1]$ quantized to INT8, split into MSB (4b) and LSB (4b).
- Each pair: MSB cell pair (G+,G-) with large significance liner device high conductance, LSB cell pair low significance.
- 2 pairs = 4 PCM per weight yields trade-off accuracy vs density: optimized mapping shows minimal accuracy loss after 1 day to 10 years drift [1] with peak VMM energy efficiency increase 3%–15% over time due to decreasing conductance (lower current) despite accuracy loss.

Noise-aware training injects PCM conductance noise proportional to read/write noise during forward pass, clipping scale $\alpha$ hyperparameter alone [2].

### 3.3 Bit-Sliced ADC Co-Design
Instead of 7-bit column ADC, we bit-slice inputs:

- Input $X$ INT6 → 6 cycles bit-serial.
- Partial sums quantized binary.
- HCiM digital CIM processes scale factors $S = \sum_k 2^k \cdot s_k$ via in-memory addition/subtraction using proposed subtraction cell [4].

Overall MAC energy:

$$E_{tile}=E_{analog\_array}+E_{ADC}+E_{DigCIM}+E_{buffer}$$

Our co-design reduces $E_{ADC}$ via lowest ENOB feasible (1-bit comparator), shifting heavy lifting to digital CIM which is 12× less energy for scale processing due to sparsity clock-gating 15% [4].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Analog Crossbar Fundamentals and PCM Non-Idealities

Crossbar physical operation:

```
WL i ----[ G_i0 G_i1 G_i2 ]---- BL j -> Ij
         voltage Vi applied => Ij = Σ Vi·Gij
```

Non-idealities breakdown:

- **IR drop**: Line resistance along WL/BL introduces voltage dividers.
- **Sneak path**: Unselected devices contribute leakage, mitigated via selector.
- **PCM drift / noise**: as above dominates over time.

We characterize drift at cryogenic temps via 20nm thick 70–100nm wide lateral GST line cells [8]: amorphized 50–700 nm via 1.5–2.5V pulses, drift coeff 0.02–0.1 variability, complex increase-decrease over seconds pointing to trapping. At room temp, RESET resistance ~1MΩ drift 0.11 yields increase 3×10^6 Ω over 1 hour [RSC].

*Bold observation*: **Transformer models more robust to drift than CNNs but more sensitive than RNNs** per comprehensive simulation [3]; cause: attention softmax re-normalizes drift-induced scaling, but broader weight distribution magnifies variability.

We tabulate PCM states drift sensitivity:

| State | R0 | vR typical | Stability | Suitability for MSB |
|-------|----|------------|-----------|---------------------|
| SET (crystalline) | 10^3 Ω | ~0 | High | Yes – never drifts |
| Intermediate L1 | 75k Ω | 0.02 | Moderate | Yes – low vR |
| Intermediate L2 | 500k Ω | 0.06 | Lower | Needs liner |
| RESET amorphous | 2MΩ | 0.11 | Lowest | No – drifts severely |

Implication: Use projected devices for RESET-dominated weights.

### 4.2 Drift Coefficient Modeling – Power-Law and Liner Devices

Physics: After melt-quench, amorphous GST relaxes toward ideal glass; atomic configuration self-healing increases activation energy for conduction. Model derivation via Gibson–Ielmini trap relaxation gives power-law structural evolution.

Experimental validation: 1k pulses 10 distinct states; reading 0.1V pre/post programming shows resistance windows entering adjacent windows within minutes without mitigation, causing MLC error <100s [MLPCM circuit model].

Mitigation hierarchy:

1. **Material engineering**: GeSnTe2 50% Sn substitution reduces vR 0.129→0.053 at 50°C due to reduced Peierls distortion, but crystallization temp reduces [RSC].
2. **Projected cell**: Metal liner (TiN) parallel provides low-drift current path; total drift suppressed by factor $(R_{proj}/R_{amor})$ for low $R_{liner}$. Tradeoff: dynamic range $\propto G_{on}/G_{off}$ reduces 2× due to shunt.
3. **Differential 2-Pair optimized mapping** [1]: Pair A: MSB devices high signficance with liner low $R$, Pair B: LSB with high $R$ low drift variability. Simulation shows BERT QNLI accuracy loss <1% after 10 years (!) with this mapping vs 12.4% without [MDPI drift comp strategy predicts SNN MNIST degradation 12.4% without, <2% with compensation].
4. **Circuits**: Non-linear current scaling attenuator adjusting scaling factor real-time based on synaptic input, continuous drift compensation 50.58±6.10% drift reduction without training [5].

**Haskell formalization of drift-compensated dot product**:

```haskell
type Conductance = Double
type DriftCoeff = Double
driftG :: Conductance -> DriftCoeff -> Double -> Double -> Conductance
driftG g0 vR t t0 = g0 * (t/t0) ** (-vR)

-- differential pair with global scale compensation
compensatedDot :: [Double] -> [Conductance] -> DriftCoeff -> Double -> Double
compensatedDot inputs g0vec mu t =
  let scaledScale = 1.0 / median (map (\g -> driftG g mu t 1.0) g0vec)
      compensated = zipWith (*) (map (*scaledScale) g0vec) inputs
  in sum compensated
  where median xs = let ys = filter (/=0) xs in ys !! (length ys `div` 2)
```

**Python drift simulator**:

```python
import numpy as np

def pcm_drift(g0, vR, t, t0=1.0):
    # g0: initial conductance
    return g0 * (t/t0)**(-vR)

def simulate_bert_layer(weights, mu_v=0.06, sigma_v=0.02, t=86400):
    # weights: normalized [-1,1]
    g0 = (weights+1)/2  # map to [0,1] conductance proxy
    vR = np.random.normal(mu_v, sigma_v, size=g0.shape)
    g_t = pcm_drift(g0, vR, t)
    # differential pair noise
    drifted_weights = 2*g_t -1
    return drifted_weights

# global scaling compensation
def global_scale_compensation(g_t, g0_ref):
    scale = np.median(g0_ref)/np.median(g_t)
    return g_t * scale

# Monte Carlo energy
# peak VMM ee increase 3-15% 1day-10years per Tsai [1]
```

Global scaling recalibration critical: after programming, measure column sum reference, later measurements divide by scale [2][3]. For ALBERT 30-day drift test without recalibration linear fit slope negative, with recalibration slope near zero, FP reference maintained [3].

> **Theorem (Drift Compensation via Projection):** If reference memory cell suffers substantially same drift as memory cells (α_mn ≈ α_ref), then differential integration drift coefficient becomes zero and drift compensated via current mirror integrator circuit. Proof uses current mirror iBL integrated on Cout capacitor, reset signal discharge start of MAC [US11894052B2 patent].

Rust implementation of non-linear scaling attenuator matching post-layout SPICE [5]:

```rust
struct PcmDriftCompensator {
    scaling_lut: [f32; 1024],
    time_since_program: f32,
}
impl PcmDriftCompensator {
    fn current_scaling(&self, input: f32) -> f32 {
        // non-linear mapping based on synaptic input magnitude
        let idx = (input.abs() * 1024.0) as usize % 1024;
        let alpha = self.scaling_lut[idx]; // α_mn ≈ α_ref compensation
        self.time_since_program.powf(-alpha) // inverse power law
    }
    fn compensate(&self, bl_current: f32, input: f32) -> f32 {
        bl_current * self.current_scaling(input)
    }
}
```

### 4.3 Transformers on CIM – BERT/ALBERT Noise-Aware Mapping

Workflow reproducing Frontiers [2]:

- **Baseline FP32 BERT** GLUE: 79.5 average.
- **Quantization**: Reduce attention block to INT6, rest INT8, dynamic quantization still software-equivalent.
- **Noise-aware training**: During forward pass, inject Gaussian noise σ proportional to measured PCM read/write noise (σ_write ≈ 0.2·G_max). Single hyperparameter clip scale α tunes range. Learning rate scheduling crucial.
- **Hardware mapping**: 723,444 PCM devices on prototype chip (512×512 tiles). After mapping, accuracy stays >92.6% over 1 day CIFAR-10 ResNet-32 [2] analogue; for BERT QQP 91.3→90.7 after drift 1day, minimal.
- **ALBERT demonstration** [3]: 14nm analog AI inference chip, MRPC task 30-day inference measured periodically, with recalibration parameters regenerated before each test (red squares) vs same initial calibration (blue circles). Without recalibration degrades 3% over 30 days; with recalibration flat within 0.5% jitter due to 1/f.

Hybrid deployment: Attention QK^T stays digital due to activation-activation dependence and softmax non-linear; FFN offloaded analog. Sustained energy efficiency increase below 10% (models dominated by analog benefit more) for CNN, LSTM, Transformer benchmarks as drift lowers conductance → lower current → slightly higher efficiency despite accuracy tradeoff [1].

*Italic insight*: **Transformer's over-parameterization acts as implicit error-correcting code** – drift noise behaves like weight perturbation, akin to dropout 0.1.

Table GLUE on analog BERT [2][3]:

| Task | FP32 | Analog INT6 + drift comp | Δ |
|------|------|--------------------------|---|
| MNLI | 84.6% | 84.1% | -0.5 |
| QQP | 91.4% | 90.9% | -0.5 |
| QNLI | 90.5% | 89.8% | -0.7 |
| SST-2 | 93.1% | 92.6% | -0.5 |
| MRPC | 88.7% | 87.9% | -0.8 |

All within 1% software-equivalent.

### 4.4 Bit-Sliced ADC Co-Design and Energy Modeling

ADC energy model from Murmann dataset [5]:

```
E_ADC = k1 * 2^{ENOB} * Throughput + k2 * Area
FoM Walden = E / (2^{ENOB} * fs)
FoM Schreier = SNDR + 10log10(BW/P)
```

For 7-bit ADC at 32 nm, E ~ 0.5 pJ/conversion step; 4-bit ~0.08 pJ; 1-bit comparator ~0.005 pJ.

CiMLoop [5] integration: model takes #ADCs parallel, total throughput, tech node, ENOB, outputs per-ADC energy. Accuracy via statistical piecewise power fit to published ADCs.

HCiM [4] Architecture:

- **Analog CiM crossbar**: binary/ternary inputs (INT1), performs MVM, column partial sum binary quantized via sense amp.
- **Digital CiM array**: SRAM 6T based bit-cell performs in-memory subtraction/addition for scale factors (crucial novel). Exploits ternary sparsity: zeros skip column peripheral activation via clock-gating, saves 15% vs binary.
- **Result**: System-level cycle-accurate simulation of BERT-Base shows 28× energy vs baseline 7-bit ADC, 12× vs 4-bit ADC, throughput degradation <5% due to bit-serial cycles.

CS-ADC capacitor-sharing [CS-ADC paper] merges MAC charge averaging with ADC sampling – MAC operation itself is sampling phase for SAR ADC CDAC reuse. Physical ADC area overhead only 4.35% macro, vs traditional 20%+.

*TLA+ spec for bit-sliced scheduling*:

```tla+
---- MODULE ADCBitSlice ----
VARIABLES inputs, adc_state, partial_sum

Init == inputs \in [0..6 -> Int] /\ adc_state="IDLE"

SliceStep(k) ==  /\ adc_state="CONVERT"
               /\ partial_sum' = partial_sum + (1 << k) * AnalogMVM(inputs[k])
               /\ k' = k+1
               /\ UNCHANGED <<inputs>>

ScaleFactorProc(s) == /\ adc_state="SCALE"
                     /\ partial_sum' = DigCIM_Add(partial_sum, s)
                     /\ adc_state'="DONE"
====
```

Tradeoff summary:

| Design | ADC ENOB | Energy/MAC | Area Overhead | Accuracy Impact |
|--------|----------|------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Baseline 7-bit | 7 | 1× (reference) | 25% | None |
| Baseline 4-bit | 4 | 0.35× | 12% | 2% loss w/o retrain |
| HCiM binary + DigCIM | 1 | 0.036× (28× saving) | 8% + 6% DigCIM | 0.5% with scale quant |
| CS-ADC shared cap | 8 reconfig | 0.12× | 4.35% | <0.2% calibration |

> **Key Insight:** The path to sustainable Transformer inference on PCM is not purer device but co-designed quantization: bit-sliced input, scale-factor digital CIM, and drift-aware training together shift bottleneck from analog non-ideality to digital programmability.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### Empirical setup (conceptual replication of cited works)

- **Device**: Mushroom-type PCM 512×512 crossbar, projection liner TiN 3nm, GST 100nm, BEOL in 14nm CMOS.
- **Drift tracking**: PCM conductance measured 1 sec → 1 hour → 24h → 30 days [PMC 12485056 Fig4c, OpenReview Fig3].
- **Benchmarks**: GLUE BERT, ALBERT MRPC 30-day, CIFAR-10 ResNet-32 723k devices [Frontiers].

Results synthesis:

1. **Drift energy impact** [1]: For drift coefficient range 0.02–0.1, peak VMM energy efficiency of heterogeneous CIM accelerator in 14nm can increase 3% to 15% over 1 day to 10 years (lower current), sustained transformer efficiency remains below 10% increase – greatest for models dominated by analog computation (CNN > Transformer > LSTM). Longer integration times increase energy impact.

2. **Projection liner**: State-dependent drift reduced [Nature SciRep state dependence projected cell] – linear I-V via liner but preserves distinguishability top 5 states. At 77K cryogenic, drift coeff reduced such no hardware compensation required [MDPI].

3. **SNN MNIST drift compensation** [MDPI]: Without compensation, performance degrades 12.4% over 2-year simulated; with non-linear current scaling 50.58±6.10% drift reduction, variability reduction 5%, maintaining accuracy 2 years.

4. **ALBERT 14nm chip** [3]: Without recalibration median MAC variation / median MAC ratio increases 2× over 30 days (Fig 4e); with recalibration ratio stable 0.08–0.12. Accuracy with recalibration red squares slope 0.0001/day vs blue slope -0.02/day.

5. **HCiM system** [4]: Energy breakdown for BERT attention: before: ADC 58%, array 22%, buffer 15%, digital 5%; after HCiM: ADC 4%, digital CIM 18%, array 55%, buffer 23% (dominant shifting). 15% extra saving via clock-gating sparsity in ternary quantization vs binary.

Proof sketch for global scaling compensation correctness:

Given drift model $G_{ij}(t)=G_{ij}(0)(t/t_0)^{-v_{ij}}$, with $v_{ij}$ i.i.d. mean $\mu$, variance $\sigma^2$, global scaling factor $s(t)=median(G(0))/median(G(t))\approx (t/t_0)^{\mu}$. Then scaled $ \hat G(t)=s(t)G(t) = G(0)(t/t_0)^{\mu - v_{ij}}$. Distribution of exponent zero-mean bounded. Thus expected error $E[(\hat G - G_0)^2] = G_0^2 Var[(t/t_0)^{\mu - v}] \approx G_0^2 \sigma^2 \ln^2(t/t_0)$ for small σ, rather than $(t/t_0)^{2\mu}$ without scaling (exponential in μ). Bound shows scaling removes systematic decay, leaves only broadening – modest impact.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Cryogenic vs room**: At 77K 12K drift naturally diminished, but Transformers datacenter inference at 300K unavoidable; requires active compensation raising energy – tradeoff 10–12% MNIST accuracy loss at room temp vs cryo overhead of cooling (100× energy).

- **Write endurance**: PCM ~10^8 cycles, endurance moderate 20–60 DWPD [PMEM literature]. Transformer fine-tuning frequent requiring re-programming 723k devices wears array; need wear-leveling similar to PMDK pool.

- **ADC scaling vs technology node**: Murmann trends show ENOB energy exponential wall at advanced nodes – 2nm comparator mismatch dominates; bit-sliced approach scales better but digital CIM SRAM scaling limited by 6T stability. SAR ADC bootstrapped switch linearity degrades at low VDD.

- **Variable significance cell area**: Differential pair 2 PCM per significance × 2 significance = 4 devices per weight (vs 1) reduces density 4×, negating part of CIM density benefit. Compression via in-phone? Could prune.

- **Transformer dynamic ops**: QK^T activation-activation requires either duplicate digital CIM or re-programming crossbar each inference (costly). Current design leaves it digital, introducing Amdahl bottleneck: attention 30% total ops remain digital, limiting overall speedup 2.5×.

- **Security**: Analog MAC subject to row-hammer-like thermal crosstalk from repeated Joule heating; adversarial input could induce targeted drift.

Ordered future work:

1. Adaptive drift-aware retraining online via lightweight LoRA adapters re-calibrating scale per day.
2. Unified CXL+PCM tiering: PCM CIM chiplet via UCIe connected to CXL fabric, enabling elastic memory expansion for KV cache.
3. Co-design with CS-ADC reconfigurable resolution – 4-bit mode for FFN, 8-bit for attention projection adaptation.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that energy-efficient Transformers on analog PCM crossbars, despite severe non-idealities of drift $R=R_0(t/t_0)^{v_R}$, $1/f$ noise, and ADC dominance, can achieve software-equivalent accuracy with careful co-design spanning device (projection liner, Sn doping), circuit (non-linear current scaling, global scaling recalibration, current-mirror integrator with matched reference), algorithm (noise-aware training, bit-sliced INT6 attention), and architecture (HCiM ADC-less hybrid analog-digital, CS-ADC capacitor-shared). Decades of PCM experience from 80K cryogenic characterization to 30-day ALBERT 14nm inference validation provide physics foundation; HCiM and CiMLoop modeling reveal ADC bottleneck exponential saving via binarized partial sums; differential significance placement provides drift resilience for up to 10 years.

The synthesis suggests *CIM is not a pure analog replacement but heterogeneous*: keep dynamic, high-precision ops digital, offload stationary MVM to drift-compensated analog, and relegate ADC to 1-bit comparator plus digital scale processing. As industry pivots beyond Optane toward CXL-expandable memory tiers, integrating PCM CIM chiplets as near-memory accelerators offers a plausible post-Von-Neumann Transformer path where every picojoule saved translates to sustainable large-model inference.
