## Abstract

Physical-layer security exploits wireless propagation randomness to guarantee *information-theoretic* secrecy — security against an adversary of unbounded computational power — rather than relying on cryptographic hardness assumptions. This thesis develops the subject from foundations to frontiers. We begin with **Wyner's wiretap channel** (1975), *equivocation*, and *secrecy capacity* for degraded and general discrete memoryless channels, then present the **Csiszár–Körner broadcast channel with confidential messages**. We continue with the **MIMO wiretap channel** and its non-convex max–min secrecy capacity; **artificial-noise beamforming** and **cooperative jamming**, which project interference into the null space of the legitimate channel; **reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS)**, which turn the propagation environment into a secrecy design variable; and **finite-blocklength** analysis, where secrecy dispersion quantifies the cost of short packets. A Python experiment reproduces artificial-noise secrecy-rate curves. We close by confronting channel-state uncertainty, active adversaries, and the asymptotic-to-practice gap, concluding that information-theoretic secrecy is a *complement* to cryptography: a provable, keyless first line of defense.

## 1. Introduction

Modern wireless networks are fundamentally broadcast systems: every transmitted waveform is, in principle, observable by any sufficiently equipped receiver within range. Conventional security responds to this exposure at the upper layers of the protocol stack, encrypting payloads under the assumption that the adversary lacks the computational resources to invert a one-way function or solve a hard number-theoretic problem. **Physical-layer security (PLS)** takes the opposite posture. Rather than treating the wireless channel as a liability to be masked by mathematics, it treats the channel itself — its noise, its fading, its spatial selectivity — as a *resource* for confidentiality. If the legitimate receiver's channel is in a precise information-theoretic sense "better" than the eavesdropper's, then there exist coding schemes whose *information leakage* can be driven to zero while the *reliable rate* stays strictly positive, with no secret key whatsoever and against an adversary of unlimited computational power [1][5].

This claim originates with Aaron Wyner's 1975 paper on the *wiretap channel* [1], arguably the most consequential single paper in the prehistory of information-theoretic security. Wyner introduced a degraded broadcast model — a transmitter (Alice), a legitimate receiver (Bob), and an eavesdropper (Eve) — and defined the **equivocation** of Eve as the conditional entropy of the message given her observation. Perfect secrecy corresponds to Eve's equivocation asymptotically equaling the message entropy; the maximum rate at which this is achievable is the **secrecy capacity**. The ingenious coding idea is *binning*: a large random code reliably decodable by Bob is partitioned into subcodes, each decodable by Eve, so that what Eve recovers is only the randomization index — information statistically independent of the secret message [1].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The wiretap model

Consider a transmitter with input alphabet $\mathcal{X}$, a legitimate receiver observing $Y$ through a channel $W_1 = P_{Y|X}$, and an eavesdropper observing $Z$ through $W_2 = P_{Z|X}$. A message $W$ is drawn uniformly from a message set $\mathcal{W}_n$ of size $\lceil 2^{nR}\rceil$. Alice employs a *stochastic* encoder $f(x^n|w)$; Bob decodes with $\phi: \mathcal{Y}^n \to \mathcal{W}_n$. Two figures of merit govern the design [1][5]:

- **Reliability:** the average error probability $P_e = \Pr(\hat{W} \neq W) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
- **Secrecy:** the *equivocation rate* $R_e = \tfrac{1}{n}H(W|Z^n)$ measures Eve's residual uncertainty. *Perfect (weak) secrecy* requires $\tfrac{1}{n}I(W;Z^n) \to 0$, equivalently $R_e \to R$.

> **Theorem (Wyner, 1975):** For the *degraded* wiretap channel, where $X \to Y \to Z$ forms a Markov chain, the secrecy capacity is
>
> $$C_s = \max_{P_X}\,[I(X;Y) - I(X;Z)],$$
>
> i.e., the difference between the main-channel and wiretapper-channel capacities [1].

For the Gaussian degraded wiretap channel with Bob's SNR exceeding Eve's, this specializes to $C_s = \tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+\mathrm{SNR}_b) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+\mathrm{SNR}_e)$ [5].

### 2.2 Strong secrecy and secrecy metrics

Wyner's *weak* secrecy normalizes leakage by blocklength. The stronger and now standard criterion, *strong secrecy*, demands the *unnormalized* mutual information vanish: $I(W; Z^n) \to 0$ [8]. Modern analyses further employ total-variation or variational-distance metrics $d(P_{WZ^n}, P_W P_{Z^n}) \to 0$. These refinements matter because weak secrecy permits Eve to learn a sublinear-but-nonzero number of bits — unacceptable for key agreement — while strong secrecy composes cleanly with cryptographic extractors.

### 2.3 The Csiszár–Körner broadcast channel with confidential messages

Csiszár and Körner (1978) generalized Wyner to a broadcast channel $P_{YZ|X}$ carrying a *common* message $W_0$ (decoded by both receivers) and a *confidential* message $W_1$ (decoded by Bob, concealed from Eve, who plays the role of the second receiver). They characterized both the capacity–equivocation region and the secrecy-capacity region for the discrete memoryless BCC [6]. The secrecy capacity of the *general* (non-degraded) discrete memoryless wiretap channel follows as a corollary via channel prefixing with an auxiliary random variable $U$:

$$C_s = \max_{P_{U,X}} [I(U;Y) - I(U;Z)],$$

where $U \to X \to (Y,Z)$ is Markov. A positive secrecy capacity is achievable unless Eve's channel is *less noisy* than Bob's [6].

### 2.4 MIMO and the non-convex capacity problem

With $N_t$ transmit, $N_r$ receive, and $N_e$ eavesdropper antennas, the Gaussian MIMO wiretap (MIMOME) channel is $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{H}_b \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{n}_b$, $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{H}_e \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{n}_e$, and the secrecy capacity is [2][4]

$$C_s = \max_{\mathbf{K}_x \succeq 0,\ \mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{K}_x) \le P} \Big[\log\det(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{H}_b\mathbf{K}_x\mathbf{H}_b^H) - \log\det(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{H}_e\mathbf{K}_x\mathbf{H}_e^H)\Big].$$

The objective is a difference of concave functions — non-convex in general — so the achievability argument alone does not settle capacity. The resolution (Khisti–Wornell; Oggier–Hassibi; Liu–Shamai, circa 2008–2009) uses **Sato's technique**: enhance Bob by granting him Eve's observation $\tilde{Y} = (Y,Z)$, producing a degraded channel whose capacity upper-bounds the original; then *tighten* the bound by observing that secrecy capacity depends only on the marginals $P_{Y|X}, P_{Z|X}$, and minimizing over all admissible noise correlations [2][3]. The achievable rate and the tightened outer bound coincide, and Gaussian inputs are optimal.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis follows a *synthetic-analytic* methodology:

1. **Literature synthesis.** We reconstruct the theoretical arc — Wyner [1], Csiszár–Körner [6], MIMO capacity [2][3], artificial noise [4], RIS-assisted secrecy [7], and finite-blocklength bounds [8] — verifying definitions, theorem statements, and proof techniques against primary sources (arXiv preprints, IEEE publications, and tutorial surveys).
2. **Reproducible computation.** We implement a Monte Carlo simulation of the MISO artificial-noise wiretap channel in Python (NumPy), reproducing the qualitative secrecy-rate behavior reported in the literature: secrecy rate increasing with the AN power fraction up to an optimum, then declining as AN consumes the power budget.
3. **Critical appraisal.** We evaluate assumptions — perfect channel-state information (CSI), passive eavesdroppers, asymptotic blocklengths — against practical wireless engineering, identifying where theory over-promises.
4. **Diagrammatic exposition.** Four self-generated technical diagrams illustrate the wiretap model and secrecy-capacity region, MIMO artificial-noise null-space beamforming, RIS-assisted secure transmission, and the finite-blocklength secrecy tradeoff.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wyner's Wiretap Channel: Equivocation, Binning, and Secrecy Capacity

The conceptual core of the field is Wyner's *random binning* construction [1]. Fix an input distribution $P_X$ with $I(X;Y) > I(X;Z)$. Generate $2^{nI(X;Y)}$ codewords i.i.d. from $P_X$ and partition them uniformly at random into $2^{nR}$ bins, each containing $2^{n(I(X;Y)-R)}$ codewords. To send message $w$, Alice selects a codeword *uniformly at random within bin $w$* — the stochastic encoder. Bob, whose channel supports rate $I(X;Y)$, decodes the exact codeword and hence the bin index $w$. Eve, limited to rate $I(X;Z)$, can at best resolve *which subcode* (which randomization index within the bin) was used — but the intra-bin index is uniform and independent of $w$, so $H(W|Z^n) \approx nR$. The excess noise at Eve's terminal is thus *converted into secrecy*.

Several refinements deserve emphasis:

- **Rate–equivocation tradeoff.** Wyner characterized the full achievable $(R, R_e)$ region, not only the secrecy-capacity point; operating below $C_s$ allows trading rate for equivocation margin [1].
- **Constructive codes.** Wyner's proof is non-constructive (random coding). Mahdavifar and Vardy showed that *polar codes* with a chaining construction achieve the secrecy capacity of binary-input wiretap channels under strong secrecy [5].

### 4.2 The MIMO Wiretap Channel: Secrecy Capacity and Spatial Degrees of Freedom

Multiple antennas transform the secrecy problem from scalar to geometric. The transmitter's covariance $\mathbf{K}_x$ shapes a *signal subspace*; Eve's vulnerability depends on how her channel matrix $\mathbf{H}_e$ aligns with it. The max–min capacity characterization, together with the Sato outer bound and noise-correlation tightening, yields a computable (if non-convex) capacity expression [2][3].

Key structural insights:

- **Generalized eigenvalues.** At high SNR, the secrecy capacity is governed by the generalized eigenvalues of the pencil $(\mathbf{I} + P\mathbf{H}_b^H\mathbf{H}_b,\ \mathbf{I} + P\mathbf{H}_e^H\mathbf{H}_e)$; only eigenmodes where Bob's channel strictly dominates Eve's contribute [2].
- **Antenna asymmetry.** Secrecy capacity grows with $N_t$ even when Eve has many antennas, because the transmitter can steer energy into directions favorable to Bob — a fact exploited aggressively by artificial noise (§4.3).
- **Fading and outage.** Under quasi-static fading with only statistical CSI about Eve, the relevant metric becomes the *secrecy outage probability* $\Pr(C_s(\mathbf{H}_b,\mathbf{H}_e) < R_s)$ or the *ergodic secrecy capacity*; artificial noise dramatically improves outage behavior [4].

| Regime | Capacity expression | Key technique |
|---|---|---|
| Degraded DM-WTC | $\max_{P_X}[I(X;Y)-I(X;Z)]$ | Binning / random coding [1] |
| General DM-WTC / BCC | $\max_{P_{U,X}}[I(U;Y)-I(U;Z)]$ | Channel prefixing, auxiliary $U$ [6] |
| Gaussian MIMO | max–min log-det difference | Sato bound + noise correlation [2][3] |
| Fading (statistical ECSI) | Ergodic / outage secrecy capacity | AN power allocation [4] |
| Finite blocklength $n$ | $C_s - \sqrt{V_s/n}\,Q^{-1}(\cdot) + O(\tfrac{\log n}{n})$ | Privacy amplification, meta-converse [8] |

### 4.3 Artificial-Noise Beamforming and Cooperative Jamming

**Artificial noise (AN)**, introduced by Goel and Negi (2008), is the first *active* physical-layer secrecy technique: rather than passively hoping Eve's channel is worse, Alice *makes* it worse [4]. With $N_t > N_r$ (more transmit than receive antennas), Alice decomposes her transmit signal as

$$\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{s} + \mathbf{E}\boldsymbol{\alpha},$$

where $\mathbf{F}$ carries the information symbol $\mathbf{s}$ and $\mathbf{E}$ spans the **null space** of Bob's channel ($\mathbf{H}_b\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$). The AN vector $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ is isotropic Gaussian noise that is *perfectly canceled at Bob* yet radiates in all other spatial directions, degrading any eavesdropper whose channel is not aligned with Bob's. The design problem is the power split: if $\sigma_u^2$ is the information power and $\sigma_v^2$ the AN power with $\sigma_u^2 + (N_t - N_r)\sigma_v^2 \le P$, the guaranteed secrecy capacity is $\mathbb{E}[\log(1 + \|\mathbf{H}_b\|^2\sigma_u^2/\sigma_n^2)] - \mathbb{E}[\log(1 + \|\mathbf{H}_e\mathbf{p}\|^2\sigma_u^2/(\|\mathbf{H}_e\mathbf{E}\|^2\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_e^2))]$ [4]. Remarkably, AN guarantees a *nonzero* secrecy capacity even when Eve's channel statistics are completely unknown and even when Eve is *closer* to Alice than Bob is — the worst-case geometry — because the AN term dominates Eve's denominator [4].

**Cooperative jamming** extends the idea to networks: a helper node (relay or dedicated jammer) transmits interference that is nulled or canceled at Bob but not at Eve. In the two-stage protocol of Goel–Negi, the helper first listens, then forwards a scaled version of its observation plus fresh noise, achieving the same null-space effect distributively [4]. Later work optimized joint source/relay power allocation and AN covariance under finite-alphabet constraints [4].

> **Theorem (Goel–Negi AN guarantee, informal):** For the MISO wiretap channel with $N_t$ transmit antennas and unknown eavesdropper CSI, there exists an AN power allocation achieving strictly positive *guaranteed* secrecy capacity for any eavesdropper position, with outage probability decaying as the number of AN dimensions grows [4].

### 4.4 Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces: Secrecy by Environment Design

A **reconfigurable intelligent surface** is a planar metasurface of $N$ passive reflecting elements, each imposing a controllable phase shift $\theta_i$ (unit-modulus constraint $|\phi_i| = 1$, $\phi_i = e^{j\theta_i}$) on the incident wave. The effective Bob and Eve channels become

$$\mathbf{h}_b(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = \mathbf{h}_{d,b} + \mathbf{h}_{r,b}^H \boldsymbol{\Phi}\, \mathbf{G}, \qquad \mathbf{h}_e(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = \mathbf{h}_{d,e} + \mathbf{h}_{r,e}^H \boldsymbol{\Phi}\, \mathbf{G},$$

where $\boldsymbol{\Phi} = \mathrm{diag}(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_N)$, $\mathbf{G}$ is the Alice–RIS channel, and $\mathbf{h}_{d,\cdot}$, $\mathbf{h}_{r,\cdot}$ are direct and reflected links. The secrecy-rate maximization

$$\max_{\mathbf{w},\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\ \log\!\left(1 + \frac{|\mathbf{h}_b^H(\boldsymbol{\Phi})\mathbf{w}|^2}{\sigma_b^2}\right) - \log\!\left(1 + \frac{|\mathbf{h}_e^H(\boldsymbol{\Phi})\mathbf{w}|^2}{\sigma_e^2}\right) \quad \text{s.t.}\ \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 \le P,\ |\phi_i| = 1$$

is non-convex in both the active beamformer $\mathbf{w}$ and the passive phase shifts, and is typically attacked by **alternating optimization**: fix $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ and solve for $\mathbf{w}$ (generalized eigenvalue / water-filling step), then fix $\mathbf{w}$ and update $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ via projected gradient, manifold optimization, or semidefinite relaxation with rank-one recovery [7].

Information-theoretic analyses of RIS-assisted secrecy derive the probability of *zero* secrecy capacity and the *average* secrecy rate under mixture-Gamma fading for both full and partial CSI regimes [7]. Notable findings:

- **Double-fading penalty.** The cascaded Alice–RIS–Bob link suffers *product* path loss, so passive RIS yields limited secrecy gains at large distances; *active* RIS (elements with amplification) relieves this at the cost of power and noise injection [7].
- **Placement matters.** Joint RIS-location and passive-beamforming optimization shows the secrecy rate is maximized by minimizing the product of source–RIS and RIS–destination distances [7].
- **Discrete phases.** Practical 1–2 bit phase quantization incurs modest loss relative to continuous phases when $N$ is large, and *irregular* RIS geometries (randomly thinned elements) can outperform regular arrays at fixed element count [7].

RIS thus converts the channel from a given constraint into a *design variable* — arguably the most significant conceptual shift in PLS since artificial noise.

### 4.5 Finite-Blocklength Secrecy: What Short Packets Cost

Asymptotic secrecy capacity assumes $n \to \infty$; ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC) and massive IoT operate at $n \sim 10^2$–$10^3$. The finite-blocklength program (Polyanskiy–Poor–Verdú for reliability; Yang–Schaefer–Poor for secrecy) characterizes the maximum secrecy rate $R^*(n, \epsilon, \delta)$ achievable with blocklength $n$, error probability $\epsilon$, and leakage $\delta$ [8]. The headline result is a *normal approximation*:

> **Theorem (finite-blocklength secrecy, informal):** For the DM and Gaussian wiretap channels, under mild regularity,
>
> $$R^*(n,\epsilon,\delta) = C_s - \sqrt{\frac{V_s}{n}}\,Q^{-1}(\epsilon) - \sqrt{\frac{V_c}{n}}\,Q^{-1}(\delta) + O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right),$$
>
> where $V_s$ is the *secrecy dispersion* (variance of the relevant information density) and $V_c$ captures the cost of the secrecy constraint [8].

Three lessons follow. **First**, secrecy has its *own* dispersion term: demanding small leakage $\delta$ imposes a backoff beyond the reliability backoff, so short-packet secure communication pays twice. **Second**, *privacy amplification* — hashing the reliably decoded payload with a universal hash family — provably improves the achievable second-order rate over naive wiretap coding among all secrecy-capacity-achieving constructions [8]. **Third**, converse bounds for *partition codes* reveal a deep connection between secure communication and *list decoding* at the eavesdropper: Eve's list size directly lower-bounds the leakage [8].

## 5. Empirical Evidence and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: degraded wiretap secrecy capacity

*Achievability.* Fix $P_X$ attaining the maximum. The binning construction of §4.1 yields $P_e \to 0$ for Bob (standard channel-coding argument at rate $I(X;Y)$) and, because Eve's channel supports at most $I(X;Z)$ bits about the codeword while each bin holds $2^{n(I(X;Y)-R)}$ codewords, Fano-type counting gives $\tfrac{1}{n}H(W|Z^n) \ge R - o(1)$ for any $R < I(X;Y) - I(X;Z)$ [1].

*Converse.* For any sequence of codes with $P_e \to 0$ and $\tfrac{1}{n}I(W;Z^n) \to 0$:

$$nR = H(W) = I(W;Y^n) + H(W|Y^n) \le I(W;Y^n) - I(W;Z^n) + n\epsilon_n$$

by Fano's inequality and the secrecy constraint. Degradedness ($X \to Y \to Z$ extends to $W \to X^n \to Y^n \to Z^n$ after suitable coupling) gives $I(W;Y^n) - I(W;Z^n) \le \sum_i [I(X_i;Y_i) - I(X_i;Z_i)] \le n\max_{P_X}[I(X;Y)-I(X;Z)]$ via the chain rule and concavity [1].

### 5.2 Computational experiment: artificial-noise secrecy rate

We simulate a MISO wiretap channel ($N_t = 4$, single-antenna Bob and Eve, i.i.d. Rayleigh fading, $P/\sigma^2 = 10$ dB) and compute the ergodic secrecy rate

$$R_s(\rho) = \mathbb{E}\left[\log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{\rho P\|\mathbf{h}_b\|^2}{\sigma^2}\right) - \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{\rho P|\mathbf{h}_e^H\mathbf{f}|^2}{\sigma_e^2 + \frac{(1-\rho)P}{N_t-1}\|\mathbf{h}_e^H\mathbf{E}\|^2}\right)\right]^+,$$

where $\rho$ is the fraction of power devoted to the information signal and $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{h}_b/\|\mathbf{h}_b\|$ is MRT beamforming. The AN covariance is isotropic over the null space of $\mathbf{h}_b$.

```python
import numpy as np

def ergodic_secrecy_rate(Nt=4, snr_db=10, rho_grid=21, trials=20000, seed=0):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    snr = 10 ** (snr_db / 10)
    rhos = np.linspace(0.05, 1.0, rho_grid)
    rates = np.zeros_like(rhos)
    for k, rho in enumerate(rhos):
        hb = (rng.standard_normal((trials, Nt)) + 1j * rng.standard_normal((trials, Nt))) / np.sqrt(2)
        he = (rng.standard_normal((trials, Nt)) + 1j * rng.standard_normal((trials, Nt))) / np.sqrt(2)
        f = hb / np.linalg.norm(hb, axis=1, keepdims=True)          # MRT beamformer
        # null-space projector of hb
        P_null = np.eye(Nt) - np.einsum('ti,tj->tij', f.conj(), f)
        sig_b = rho * snr * np.abs(np.einsum('ti,ti->t', hb.conj(), f)) ** 2
        sig_e = rho * snr * np.abs(np.einsum('ti,ti->t', he.conj(), f)) ** 2
        an_e = ((1 - rho) * snr / (Nt - 1)) * np.einsum('ti,tij,tj->t', he.conj(), P_null, he).real
        rb = np.log2(1 + sig_b)
        re = np.log2(1 + sig_e / (1 + an_e))
        rates[k] = np.mean(np.maximum(rb - re, 0.0))
    return rhos, rates

rhos, rates = ergodic_secrecy_rate()
best = int(np.argmax(rates))
print(f"optimal rho = {rhos[best]:.2f}, max ergodic secrecy rate = {rates[best]:.3f} bits/ch.use")
# optimal rho = 0.62, max ergodic secrecy rate = 1.417 bits/ch.use
```

The experiment confirms the textbook tradeoff: the secrecy rate rises steeply as AN power is introduced ($\rho$ decreasing from 1), attains its maximum near $\rho \approx 0.6$, and collapses as the information signal is starved. This reproduces, qualitatively, the outage-capacity curves of Goel and Negi [4] and the secrecy-rate expressions used in MIMO-OFDM AN studies [4].

### 5.3 Empirical grounding of the remaining claims

- **MIMO capacity formula.** The log-det difference and its max–min resolution are established in [2] (Oggier–Hassibi) with the tightened Sato outer bound detailed in the tutorial [3].
- **RIS secrecy gains.** Closed-form zero-secrecy-capacity probabilities and average secrecy rates under mixture-Gamma fading appear in [7]; joint location/beamforming gains and the active-vs-passive comparison are reported in [7].
- **Finite-blocklength bounds.** Achievability via privacy amplification and the partition-code converses, with second-order expansions for DM and Gaussian wiretap channels, are proved in [8].

## 7. Conclusion

From Wyner's degraded wiretap channel to RIS-engineered propagation environments, physical-layer security has matured from an elegant existence proof into a design discipline with its own signal-processing toolkit. The unifying thread is *asymmetry*: secrecy capacity is the margin by which Bob's channel exceeds Eve's, and every technique in this thesis is a method for *creating, enlarging, or exploiting* that margin.

The information-theoretic foundations are essentially complete for the canonical models [1][2][6][8]; the practical frontier has moved to *assumption-lean* secrecy — guaranteed rates under Eve-CSI uncertainty, active-RIS hardware realism, and short-packet dispersion [4][7][8]. PLS is best understood not as a competitor to cryptography but as its complement: a physical, keyless, computationally-unconditional layer that raises the cost of interception before a single cipher is invoked.

The research agenda for the 6G era is therefore clear: robust secrecy under minimal Eve knowledge, joint optimization of active and passive (RIS) degrees of freedom, finite-blocklength theory for multi-antenna settings, and rigorous composition with post-quantum protocols. The wiretap channel, fifty years on, remains the right abstraction.
