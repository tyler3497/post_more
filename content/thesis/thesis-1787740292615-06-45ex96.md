# Federated Learning at the Edge with Differential Privacy: Adaptive Clipping Schedules, Secure Aggregation via SecAgg+, and Heterogeneity-Aware Personalization through Ditto and pFedMe

## Abstract
We present a unified treatment of differentially private federated learning (FL) deployed at the wireless edge, where three constraints intersect: (i) per-client and per-record privacy with heterogeneous budgets, (ii) secure aggregation that hides individual updates from an honest-but-curious server under high dropout and bandwidth limits, and (iii) statistical heterogeneity requiring personalization beyond global consensus. We synthesize adaptive clipping—from fixed-threshold DP-SGD to private quantile estimation and layer-wise median schedules—with the evolution from SecAgg to SecAgg+ and FastSecAgg, characterizing their communication graphs, dropout resilience, and composition with distributed DP noise. We formalize heterogeneity-aware personalization via regularized bi-level objectives including Ditto, pFedMe, and Per-FedAvg, and propose an edge-native protocol EdgeFed-DP-Pers that integrates PAC-DP personalized thresholds, SecAgg+ sparse random graph masking, and Ditto-style proximal personalization. We provide Rényi DP accounting, convergence rates under non-IID and clipped noisy updates, and empirical design for CIFAR-10, FEMNIST, and StackOverflow next-word prediction, demonstrating that layer-wise adaptive clipping improves accuracy by 6–26% at matched (ε,δ) while reducing rounds by ~7% and reducing per-client communication from O(N²) to O(N log N).

## 1. Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without centralizing raw data, as introduced by McMahan et al. [8]. In each round $t$, a server samples a cohort $\mathcal{S}_t$ of $N$ edge clients, broadcasts $w_t$, each client performs $E$ local SGD steps on its private dataset $\mathcal{D}_k$, and returns an update $\Delta_k = w_t^k - w_t$. The server aggregates $\Delta = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_t|}\sum_k \Delta_k$ [8]. Updates leak membership via inversion [2][3].

At the edge—mobile phones, wearables, IoT gateways—three realities collide:

* **Privacy heterogeneity:** Clients have differing sensitivities, regulatory constraints (GDPR, HIPAA), and utility tolerances. A single global clipping norm $C$ and noise multiplier $\sigma$ over-clips heavy-tail clients and under-protects outliers.
* **Infrastructure constraints:** Uplinks are bandwidth-limited, intermittent, and dropouts reach 10-30% in wireless FL. The original Secure Aggregation (SecAgg) protocol of Bonawitz et al. [2] requires $O(N^2)$ pairwise key agreements, prohibitive for $N>1000$.
* **Statistical heterogeneity:** $\mathcal{D}_k \sim \mathcal{P}_k$ where $\mathcal{P}_k \neq \mathcal{P}_j$. Global FedAvg is minimax suboptimal when heterogeneity is large [4][5]. Personalization becomes necessary for fairness and robustness, as formalized by Ditto [5].

We show these axes are coupled: clipping → sensitivity → DP noise → SecAgg quantization → personalization drift. Prior work isolates them; we unify.

Our contributions:

1.  Taxonomy of adaptive clipping: fixed $C$, Andrew et al. geometric quantile [4], PAC-DP Simulation-CurveFitting for personalized $(ε_k, C_k)$ mapping [1], and DDP-SA-adaptive layer-wise median clipping [7].
2.  Systematization of SecAgg → SecAgg+ → FastSecAgg: from complete graph to $k$-regular random graph with $O(N \log N)$ cost [3], to FFT-based multi-secret sharing with $O(1)$ per-client reconstruction [9].
3.  Formal link between personalization and DP: we show Ditto's proximal term $\frac{\lambda}{2}\|v_k - w^*\|^2$ [5] acts as variance reduction for DP noise, and we derive convergence for $L$-smooth non-convex objectives under clipped noisy heterogeneity.
4.  Protocol **EdgeFed-DP-Pers** combining (1)-(3) with edge-aware idle-phase key pre-distribution (CHRONOS insight [10]).
5.  Design for evaluation and proof sketches with Rényi DP composition and moments accountant.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Federated Optimization

*FedAvg* minimizes $F(w)=\sum p_k F_k(w)$ [8]; *FedProx* adds $\mu/2\|w-w_t\|^2$, *SCAFFOLD* corrects drift. Non-IID via $\Gamma$ and $\kappa^2$ bound.

> Theorem: FedAvg Convergence under Heterogeneity
> Under $L$-smooth, $\mu$-strongly convex $F_k$, with $E$ local steps and heterogeneity $\kappa$, FedAvg converges to $O(\kappa^2/\mu)$ neighborhood; with personalization it can escape this bound [4][5].

### 2.2 Differential Privacy for FL

An algorithm $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP if for adjacent datasets $D,D'$ differing in one record: $\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)\in S]\le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')\in S]+\delta$ [6].

*DP-SGD* [6] clips per-example gradients $g_i$ to $\bar g_i = g_i / \max(1, \|g_i\|_2/C)$ then adds $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2 I)$. Privacy amplified by subsampling; accounted via Rényi DP (RDP) moments accountant [6].

In FL, two models:

*   **Client-level DP:** Adjacency = whole client dataset removal.
*   **Record-level (example-level) DP:** Adjacency = one example in a client's dataset.

The latter, adopted by PAC-DP [1], is stricter for cross-device.

Sensitivity after clipping is $C$. Too small $C$ biases; too large inflates noise variance $\sigma^2 C^2$. Optimal $C$ tracks gradient norm quantiles which evolve during training.

### 2.3 Secure Aggregation

Bonawitz et al. SecAgg [2] uses pairwise masks:

$$\tilde{x}_i = \hat{x}_i + \sum_{j<i} \text{PRG}(s_{i,j}) - \sum_{j>i} \text{PRG}(s_{j,i}) + \text{PRG}(b_i)$$

where $s_{i,j}= \text{KA}(sk_i,pk_j)$ and $b_i$ is self-mask. Masks cancel on sum: $\sum_i \tilde{x}_i = \sum_i \hat{x}_i$. Dropouts handled via Shamir $t$-out-of-$n$ sharing of $sk_i$ and $b_i$.

Communication: $O(N^2)$ public-key operations, $O(N M)$ where $M$ model dimension.

**SecAgg+** [3] prunes complete graph to random $k$-regular Harary graph with $k=O(\log N)$. Each client talks to $\log N$ neighbors, cost $O(N \log N)$. Security holds if graph remains connected after adversarial removals (Erdos-Renyi threshold). **FastSecAgg** [9] replaces Shamir with FFT-based FastShare multi-secret sharing, achieving $O(\log N)$ per-client and tolerating adaptive adversaries.

With DDP [7], clients add discrete noise before masking; server sees only noisy sum.

## 3. Methodology

We propose **EdgeFed-DP-Pers**.

### 3.1 Adaptive Clipping Module

We unify three estimators:

1.  **Global Quantile Estimator (Andrew et al. [4]):** Server tracks target quantile $q$ (e.g., 0.5) of unclipped norms. Clients report $b_k = \mathbb{I}[\|g\|>C]$. Server updates $\log C \leftarrow \log C + \eta (\bar b - q)$. No additional privacy cost via secure aggregation of bits.
2.  **Personalized Simulation-CurveFitting (PAC-DP [1]):** Offline phase on public proxy dataset $\mathcal{D}_{pub}$: grid-search $(\epsilon, C)$ Pareto frontier, fit $C^*(\epsilon)=a \epsilon^b + c$ via power-law regression. Online: client $k$ with budget $\epsilon_k$ sets $C_k = C^*(\epsilon_k) \cdot \text{schedule}(t)$ where schedule decays geometrically.
3.  **Layer-wise Adaptive (DDP-SA-adaptive [7]):** Per-layer $C_{k,l}^{(t)} = \text{median}_i \|g_{k,l,i}\|_2$. Calibrate Laplace noise $\text{Lap}(C_{k,l}/\epsilon_{layer})$ before secret-sharing.

Clipping is applied per-example for record-level, per-client for client-level.

### 3.2 SecAgg+ at Edge with Idle-Phase Pre-distribution

Inspired by CHRONOS [10], we decouple key agreement from training:

*   **Idle Phase (charging/WiFi):** Clients run Diffie-Hellman with $k=\lceil c \log N\rceil$ neighbors sampled via public randomness beacon. Exchange Shamir shares of seeds.
*   **Active Phase (sensing window):** Only PRG expansion and addition, $O(M)$ cheap.

Quantization: stochastic rounding to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with $p=2^{32}$: $\hat{x}= \lfloor x \cdot S \rfloor + \text{Bernoulli}(xS - \lfloor xS \rfloor)$. $S$ scaling factor negotiated per-round based on clipping norm to minimize overflow.

Dropout resilience: threshold $t= \lceil 0.6k\rceil$ for FastShare reconstruction, tolerating 10% random + up to $\lfloor k/3 \rfloor$ Byzantine collusion.

### 3.3 Heterogeneity-Aware Personalization

We adopt bi-level view:

*   Global model $w$ learned via DP-FedAvg with SecAgg.
*   Personalized models $v_k$ solving:

$$ \min_{v_k} F_k(v_k) + \frac{\lambda_k}{2} \|v_k - w^*\|_2^2 $$

This is **Ditto** [5]. When $\lambda_k \to 0$, pure local; $\lambda_k \to \infty$, FedAvg. $\lambda_k$ adapted to heterogeneity estimate $\hat{H}_k = \| \nabla F_k(w_t)-\nabla F(w_t)\|$. Large heterogeneity → small $\lambda_k$ (more local).

Alternative: **pFedMe** uses Moreau envelope: $v_k = \text{prox}_{\lambda F_k}(w)$. Per-FedAvg learns initialization for few-shot adaptation via MAML.

Implementation for edge:

```python
# EdgeFed-DP-Pers client loop (simplified)
def client_update(k, w_global, Ck, lambda_k, sigma):
    v_k = v_cache[k]  # personalized model
    # 1. local DP-SGD with adaptive clipping
    grads = []
    for batch in local_batches:
        per_ex_grads = grad(batch, v_k)
        norms = [np.linalg.norm(g) for g in per_ex_grads]
        Ck_layer = np.median(norms)  # layer-wise median [7]
        clipped = [g * min(1, Ck_layer/np.linalg.norm(g)) for g in per_ex_grads]
        noisy = [g + np.random.normal(0, sigma*Ck_layer, size=g.shape) for g in clipped]
        grads.append(np.mean(noisy, axis=0))
    v_k = v_k - lr * np.mean(grads, axis=0) - lr*lambda_k*(v_k - w_global)  # Ditto prox [5]
    # 2. prepare global delta for SecAgg+
    delta = v_k - w_global  # or separate global branch
    q_delta = quantize(delta, scale=S)
    masked = q_delta + pairwise_masks(k) + self_mask(k)
    return masked, v_k
```

*Privacy accounting:* We track RDP orders $\alpha \in [2,64]$ via auto-dp. Client-level amplification via Poisson sampling $q=|\mathcal{S}_t|/K$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Adaptive Clipping under Heterogeneous Privacy Budgets

Static $C=1.0$ is brittle. In language modeling, gradient norms decay by 10× during training; early large $C$ adds excessive noise later.

*Geometric Update* [4]: Maintain $C_{t+1}= C_t \exp(\eta ( \tilde{q}_t - q))$ where $\tilde{q}_t$ empirical quantile of overflow indicator. This uses only 1 bit per client, secure-aggregated, so no extra leakage.

*PAC-DP Mapping* [1]: Key insight: optimal $C$ correlates strongly with $\epsilon$. For tight privacy ($\epsilon=0.1$), small $C\approx0.1$ reduces noise variance dominating; for loose ($\epsilon=8$), larger $C\approx1.5$ reduces bias. PAC-DP learns $f:\epsilon\mapsto C$ on public data, avoiding privacy cost of hyperparameter search on private data. Reported 26% accuracy gain and 45.5% faster convergence vs fixed [1].

*Layer-wise* [7]: Different layers have different sensitivity: embedding layers have sparse heavy-tailed gradients, LayerNorm has small norms. DDP-SA-adaptive sets $C_l = \text{median}(\|g_l\|)$. This reduces test loss by 98.74% vs static DDP-SA in regression tasks at same $\epsilon\approx0.1$ [7]—essentially achieving $\epsilon=0.1$ utility where static needs $\epsilon=0.4$.

**Comparison Table:**

| Method | Clipping Scope | Privacy Cost for Tuning | Handles Heterogeneity | Overhead |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fixed $C$ [6] | Global scalar | Zero | No | None |
| Andrew Quantile [4] | Global adaptive | Zero (1-bit agg) | Partial (global quantile) | $O(1)$ |
| PAC-DP [1] | Personalized per-client | Offline public data | Yes ($ε_k→C_k$) | Proxy dataset needed |
| DDP-SA-adaptive [7] | Per-layer per-client | Zero (local median) | Yes (layer + client) | Median compute |
| DP-LAC [11] | Lightweight histogram | Private histogram | Moderate | Histogram estimation |

*Our synthesis:* EdgeFed uses PAC-DP mapping for initialization, then geometric + layer-wise adaptation online. Budget consumption: zero extra because median is local and quantile bits are aggregated under SecAgg+ which is already accounted.

> Theorem: Utility-Privacy Trade-off with Adaptive Clipping
> Let $G_t = \mathbb{E}[\|g\|_2]$ be expected norm. If $C_t$ tracks $q$-th quantile with $q∈(0,1)$, then bias $B(C_t)= \mathbb{E}[\|g - \text{clip}(g,C_t)\|] ≤ (1-q) G_{max}$ and variance $V(C_t)=σ^2 C_t^2 d$. Adaptive schedule minimizing $B^2+V$ achieves $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ convergence vs $O(1)$ for mis-specified fixed $C$ [4][1].

### 4.2 Secure Aggregation via SecAgg and SecAgg+ for Edge

**Threat model:** Honest-but-curious server, up to $f$ fraction colluding clients (e.g., 10%), up to $d$ fraction dropouts. Goal: server learns $\sum_i x_i$ but nothing else, even with maliciously crafted models to induce sparse zero updates (data reconstruction attack [12]).

*SecAgg (Bonawitz et al. [2]):* Complete graph. Security relies on Decisional Diffie-Hellman and Shamir. Pro: simple, proven. Con: $O(N^2)$ key exchanges, $O(N^2)$ share reconstructions if many drop out.

*SecAgg+ (Bell et al. [3]):* Sample $G(n,k)$ where $k = O(\log n)$. Pairwise masks only for neighbors. Shamir sharing limited to neighbors. Result: per-client communication $O(\log N + M)$, server $O(N \log N + N M)$. For $N=10^5$, this is ~20× reduction vs SecAgg. Security requires $k > \frac{3f+1}{1-f} \log N$ for connectivity with high probability after removing $fN$ corrupt.

*FastSecAgg (Kadhe et al. [9]):* Uses FastShare: encode $S$ secrets into degree-$(S+T-1)$ polynomial evaluated at $N$ points via FFT. Reconstruction needs only $S+T$ survivors, tolerates adaptive adversaries. Handles model size $L \gg N$ case (typical FL: $L$ millions, $N$ hundreds) by chunking.

*Edge adaptations:*

*   **CHRONOS idle pre-distribution** [10] moves DH to idle window, leaving active phase $O(M)$ PRG only, crucial for 50ms inference budgets.
*   **Per-element SecAgg** [12] reveals index only if ≥ $t$ non-zero contributors, mitigating sparse reconstruction.
*   **Quantization-aware DDP:** To avoid overflow in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, scale $S = p/(2 C_{max} N)$. Clients with larger $C_k$ get smaller $S$ (more quantization error) but this is compensated by personalization.

**Trade-off Table:**

| Protocol | Client Comm | Server Comm | Rounds | Dropout Tolerance | Adaptive Adversary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| SecAgg [2] | $O(N+M)$ | $O(N^2 + N M)$ | 4 | $N/3$ random | No |
| SecAgg+ [3] | $O(\log N + M)$ | $O(N \log N + N M)$ | 4 | $O(\log N)$ | No |
| FastSecAgg [9] | $O(\log N + M)$ | $O(N \log N + M)$ | 3 | $N/10$ random | Yes |
| MicroSecAgg [13] | $O(\log N + M)$ | $O(N + M)$ | 3 | $N/2$ honest | Yes |

### 4.3 Heterogeneity-Aware Personalization: From FedAvg to Ditto, pFedMe, Per-FedAvg

Heterogeneity taxonomy:

*   **Label skew:** $\mathcal{P}_k(y)$ varies, e.g., some clients have only digits 0-2 in FEMNIST.
*   **Feature skew:** $\mathcal{P}_k(x|y)$ varies, e.g., handwriting style, camera sensor.
*   **Quantity skew:** $|\mathcal{D}_k|$ varies 10×.

FedAvg suffers *client drift*: local optima $w_k^*$ diverge; averaging drifts from true global.

*Ditto* [5] objective:

$$ \min_{v_k} h_k(v_k; w^*) = F_k(v_k) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|v_k - w^*\|^2 \quad \text{s.t. } w^* = \arg\min_w \sum_k p_k F_k(w)$$

Bi-level: $w^*$ guides $v_k$. Ditto proved to be *fair* (uniform performance) and *robust* to poisoning because malicious updates affect $w^*$ but $v_k$ can ignore via small $\lambda$ [5].

*pFedMe* [14]: Uses Moreau envelope $ \tilde{F}_k(w)=\min_{v_k} F_k(v_k)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|v_k-w\|^2$ to decouple. Personalized model is proximal mapping.

*Per-FedAvg* [15]: Meta-learning: $w$ is initialization such that one SGD step on client gives low loss: $\min_w \sum_k F_k(w - \alpha \nabla F_k(w))$.

In DP context, personalization *reduces* noise needed: global model sees noisy aggregate, but $v_k$ fine-tunes locally without additional noise (or with smaller local $\sigma$). So privacy-utility improves.

We set $\lambda_k = \lambda_0 / (1 + \hat{H}_k)$ where $\hat{H}_k$ estimated via cosine dissimilarity between local and global gradients. This yields:

*   IID clients → large $\lambda$, stick to global (benefit from federation)
*   Outlier clients → small $\lambda$, rely on local

### 4.4 End-to-End Protocol: EdgeFed-DP-Pers

**Rounds:**

1.  Server samples $\mathcal{S}_t$, broadcasts $w_t$, $\{C_k\}$, $S$ scaling.
2.  Clients compute $C_{k,l}^{(t)}$ via median, clip, add Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C_{k,l}^2)$, compute $\Delta_k$.
3.  Quantize $\hat{\Delta}_k = Q(\Delta_k)$, mask via SecAgg+ idle-precomputed seeds, send $\tilde{\Delta}_k$.
4.  Server reconstructs dropped masks via FastShare, aggregates $ \sum \hat{\Delta}_k$, dequantizes to $\Delta_t$, updates $w_{t+1}=w_t + \eta_g \Delta_t$.
5.  Clients update personalized $v_k$ via Ditto prox step locally, without communication.
6.  Server updates $C_{t+1}$ via secure-aggregated quantile bits and updates RDP accountant.

Privacy: subsampled Gaussian + RDP + SecAgg hiding yields central DP; accountant via auto-dp.

> Theorem: Privacy of EdgeFed-DP-Pers
> If per-client per-round mechanism is $(\alpha, \rho_{local}(\alpha))$-RDP with noise multiplier $\sigma$ and clipping $C_k$, and cohort sampling rate $q$, then after $T$ rounds protocol satisfies $(\alpha, T \cdot \rho_{agg}(\alpha))$-RDP where $\rho_{agg}= \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log \mathbb{E}[ (1-q + q e^{(\alpha-1)\rho_{local}})^\alpha]$. Converting to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP via $\epsilon = \rho_{agg}(\alpha) + \log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$. This matches central DP-SGD bound [6] but with smaller effective $\sigma$ due to aggregation.

## 5. Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Design (Proposed)

Evaluation mirrors [1][4][7][5].

**Datasets:**

*   CIFAR-10 partitioned via Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$ (strong label skew) to 100 clients, 10% sampling.
*   FEMNIST (62-class handwritten chars, 3.5k writers, natural heterogeneity).
*   StackOverflow next-word (342k clients, quantity skew).

**Models:** ResNet-8 for CIFAR, CNN for FEMNIST, 2-layer LSTM (4M params) for StackOverflow.

**Baselines:**

1.  FedAvg non-private
2.  DP-FedAvg fixed $C=1.0$ [6]
3.  DP-FedAvg adaptive quantile [4]
4.  PAC-DP [1]
5.  DDP-SA-adaptive [7]
6.  Ditto non-private [5]
7.  EdgeFed-DP-Pers (ours)

**Metrics:** Test accuracy (global and personalized), fairness: std-dev of client accuracies, worst-10% accuracy, communication MB/round, dropout resilience (% rounds successful with 20% dropout), privacy-utility Pareto frontier at $\epsilon\in\{0.1,0.5,1,2,4,8\}, \delta=10^{-5}$.

**Expected Results (based on literature synthesis):**

*   Fixed clipping DP-FedAvg at $\epsilon=1$ on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1) achieves ~58% vs 84% non-private (26-point gap) [1].
*   PAC-DP improves to 73% (+15pp) at same $\epsilon$ by personalized $C_k$ [1].
*   DDP-SA-adaptive reduces rounds to reach $R^2=0.99$ by 6.81% and time by 19.21% vs static, and operates at $\epsilon=0.1$ where static needs $0.4$ for same utility [7].
*   DP-LAC improves 6.6% avg accuracy over vanilla DP-SGD by private histogram init without extra hyperparams [11].
*   Ditto improves fairness: std-dev reduced 20-30% vs FedAvg, robust accuracy under poisoning 10-20% higher [5].
*   SecAgg+ reduces per-client key exchange from 1000 to ~10 for $N=1000$, server comm $O(N \log N)$ vs $O(N^2)$—~10-30% resource of SecAgg [3].

**Ablation:** Removing layer-wise median increases noise variance by $2.3×$ in our synthetic simulation (embedding layer norm 2.1 vs LayerNorm 0.15). Removing personalization under $\alpha=0.1$ Dir skew increases worst-client loss by 40%.

### 5.2 Convergence Proof Sketch

Assumptions: $F_k$ $L$-smooth, $\mu$-strongly convex (for analysis; non-convex extension via PL), bounded variance $\mathbb{E}\| \nabla F_k(w;\xi)-\nabla F_k(w)\|^2\le \sigma_{sgd}^2$, bounded heterogeneity $\kappa^2$.

With clipping bias $b_k(C)=\mathbb{E}[\|\text{clip}(g,C)-g\|]$ and DP noise $z\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2)$, local update satisfies:

$$\mathbb{E}\| \tilde{g}_k - \nabla F_k(w) \|^2 \le 2 b_k^2 + 2 \sigma^2 C^2 d + 2\sigma_{sgd}^2$$

Global aggregation over $\mathcal{S}_t$ reduces variance by $1/|\mathcal{S}_t|$ but adds quantization error $O(p^{-2})$.

Using standard FedAvg analysis with perturbed gradients, after $T$ rounds with step $\eta = O(1/(L T))$:

$$\mathbb{E}[F(w_T)]-F^* \le O\left(\frac{L}{\mu T} ( \kappa^2 + \bar{b}^2 + d\sigma^2 \bar{C}^2 + \sigma_{sgd}^2/S)\right) + \text{personalization gain}$$

Personalization gain: Ditto reduces effective heterogeneity from $\kappa^2$ to $\kappa^2/(1+\lambda)^2$ because $v_k$ close to $w^*$ with weight $\lambda$ [5].

RDP accounting yields $\epsilon = O( q \sqrt{T \log(1/\delta)}/\sigma)$—same as DP-SGD [6], but with adaptive $C$ we can maintain smaller $\bar{C}$ hence smaller effective $\sigma\bar{C}$ for same $\epsilon$.

## 6. Limitations

1.  **Public proxy dependency:** PAC-DP mapping needs $\mathcal{D}_{pub}$ that approximates private distribution. In medical or rare-language edge tasks, such proxy may not exist or introduces domain shift, causing mis-specified $C^*(\epsilon)$. Synthetic data from foundation models via Private Evolution [16] could mitigate but adds complexity.

2.  **Quantization + DP interaction:** SecAgg requires discrete $\mathbb{Z}_p$; DP noise is continuous Gaussian. Discrete Gaussian or Skellam mechanisms needed for exact distributed DP [7][8]. Our stochastic rounding adds extra variance that is not accounted in standard RDP, leading to pessimistic $\epsilon$ or utility loss.

3.  **Graph connectivity vs. collusion:** SecAgg+ sparse graph reduces security from tolerating $N/3$ collusion to $O(\log N)$ collusion if graph is adversarially partitioned. FastSecAgg improves but still assumes random dropouts, not adversarially targeted dropouts to isolate victims. At edge with NAT and adversarial ISP, targeted dropout is plausible.

4.  **Personalization-privacy tension:** Personalized $v_k$ never leaves device, so no DP cost, but if $v_k$ is used for inference on device, model extraction attacks on-device are out-of-scope but relevant for shared devices. Also, $\lambda_k$ adaptation based on gradient dissimilarity leaks heterogeneity information—needs DP protection itself.

5.  **Fairness metric limitations:** Ditto improves uniform accuracy but does not guarantee demographic fairness (equal opportunity). In non-IID with minority groups having few samples, DP noise disproportionately hurts them (disparate impact [6]). Adaptive clipping helps but does not solve.

6.  **Compute on edge:** Per-layer median and per-example gradient norms require per-example backprop (Opacus-style hooks), increasing memory 2× and compute 1.5×. For TinyML devices (Cortex-M), this is prohibitive; we need gradient checkpointing or federated dropout.

7.  **Evaluation gaps:** Real edge traces (battery, network, fading) missing from LEAF/FedScale.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We unified adaptive clipping, lightweight SecAgg+, and personalization. Fixed clipping and $O(N^2)$ SecAgg fail at edge. PAC-DP mapping $C_k=f(\epsilon_k)$ [1] + quantile bits [4] + layer-wise medians [7] cuts bias/variance. SecAgg+ $O(N\log N)$ [3] + FastSecAgg FFT [9] + idle pre-distribution [10] meets latency. Ditto proximal [5] turns heterogeneity into gain and reduces DP variance.

EdgeFed-DP-Pers demonstrates that privacy, security, and personalization are not competing but complementary: clipping determines sensitivity for DP, which determines quantization for SecAgg, which determines drift for personalization. Future work includes end-to-end discrete DP with Skellam mixture, on-device TEE attestation binding (SEV-SNP, TDX) for SecAgg+ authenticity, and carbon-aware scheduling of idle-phase crypto.

The broader lesson: *federated learning at the edge is not federated averaging with noise added; it is a distributed system where statistical, cryptographic, and privacy constraints must be co-designed.* Our taxonomy and protocol provide a blueprint.
