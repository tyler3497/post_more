# Differentially Private Federated Learning with Secure Aggregation: Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism, Skellam Noise, and Private Heavy Hitters via Hadamard Randomized Response

## Abstract
Differentially private federated learning under secure aggregation demands discrete noise mechanisms compatible with finite-group arithmetic. This thesis synthesizes the Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism (DDG), the multi-dimensional Skellam mechanism, and Hadamard Randomized Response (HRR) for private heavy hitters, analyzing their integration with Bonawitz-style secure aggregation. We formalize Rényi differential privacy accounting for sums of discrete Gaussians and Skellam variables, characterize quantization, randomized rounding, flattening via Walsh-Hadamard transforms, and modular clipping effects on utility, and establish communication-privacy-accuracy trade-offs. We extend to private heavy hitters discovery in federated settings via TreeHist and Bitstogram with Hadamard encoding, achieving O(log k) bits per client. Empirical evaluations on federated benchmarks demonstrate DDG and Skellam match central Gaussian accuracy within 16 bits per value, while HRR attains order-optimal sample complexity with near-linear server runtime.

## 1 Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) enables training on decentralized data without centralizing raw examples [1]. However, model updates can leak private information via gradient inversion and membership inference [2]. Two complementary defenses have emerged: **Differential Privacy (DP)** and **Secure Aggregation (SecAgg)**. DP provides formal indistinguishability guarantees, while SecAgg ensures the server observes only the aggregate, not individual contributions, via pairwise masking and secret sharing [3][4].

The *continuous* Gaussian mechanism, workhorse of DP-SGD [5], is incompatible with SecAgg's finite-group arithmetic. Floating-point noise cannot be represented exactly modulo *m*, and naive discretization breaks privacy due to floating-point vulnerabilities [6][7]. Moreover, communication constraints in FL demand low-precision quantization—often ≤16 bits per coordinate.

Recent advances resolve this via **discrete** noise:

* The **Distributed Discrete Gaussian Mechanism (DDG)** [1][8] adds *discrete Gaussian* noise $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2)$ on quantized updates, leveraging closure under summation and Rényi DP analysis for sums of discrete Gaussians.
* The **Skellam Mechanism** [2][9] uses the difference of two independent Poisson variables $Sk(\mu_1,\mu_2)$, achieving identical privacy-utility trade-offs to the Gaussian but with sampling via standard Poisson routines—available in all ML frameworks.
* For **private heavy hitters** and distribution estimation under Local DP (LDP), **Hadamard Response (HR)** [10][11] and its extensions TreeHist/Bitstogram [12] achieve optimal sample complexity with $\log k+2$ bits communication and $\tilde{O}(n+k)$ time via Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform (FWHT).

> **Theorem 1 (Informal):** Under $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with secure aggregation over modulus $M$, there exists a DDG/Skellam mechanism achieving mean squared error $O\left(\frac{c^2 d}{n^2\varepsilon^2}\right)$ with $b = O(\log(n + \sqrt{n^3\varepsilon^2/d} + \sqrt{d}/\varepsilon))$ bits per coordinate, matching central Gaussian up to $1+o(1)$ factor.

This thesis provides a unified treatment, bridging quantization theory, lattice cryptography (discrete Gaussian sampling [13]), and communication-efficient LDP.

## 2 Background

## 2.1 Differential Privacy and Rényi DP

A mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP if for adjacent datasets $D,D'$ and all $S$:

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)\in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')\in S] + \delta$$

**Rényi DP (RDP)** [14] of order $\alpha>1$:

$$D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(D)||\mathcal{M}(D')) \le \varepsilon(\alpha)$$

RDP composes linearly and converts to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP via $ \varepsilon = \min_{\alpha} \varepsilon(\alpha) + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}$. Privacy amplification by subsampling crucially improves federated bounds [15].

## 2.2 Federated Learning and Secure Aggregation

FedAvg iterates: broadcast $w^t$, clients compute $\Delta_i^t = \text{Clip}(w_i^{t+1}-w^t)$, aggregate $\bar{\Delta} = \frac{1}{n}\sum \Delta_i$. Bonawitz et al. [3] introduced 4-round SecAgg:

1. **AdvertiseKeys** – Diffie-Hellman public keys
2. **ShareKeys** – pairwise seeds via $s_{i,j}=KA(sk_i,pk_j)$
3. **MaskedInput** – $y_i = x_i + \sum_{j<i} PRG(s_{i,j}) - \sum_{j>i} PRG(s_{j,i}) + PRG(b_i)$
4. **Unmasking** – reveal shares for dropped/surviving users.

Masks cancel: $\sum y_i = \sum x_i \mod M$. Security holds against honest-but-curious server with up to $t$ colluding clients [4]. Optimizations: Bell et al. log-degree graphs, FSSA 3-round [16].

## 2.3 Discrete Gaussian and Skellam Distributions

**Definition (Discrete Gaussian):** For $\mu\in\mathbb{Z},\sigma>0$:

$$\Pr[X=x] \propto \exp\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad x\in\mathbb{Z}$$

Sampling via rejection from discrete Laplace [7]. Key property: not closed under summation, but sum of *n* i.i.d. discrete Gaussians approximates discrete Gaussian with variance $n\sigma^2$ up to small RDP divergence, if $\sigma = \Omega(\sqrt{\log n})$ [1].

**Definition (Skellam):** $X = P_1 - P_2$, $P_i\sim \text{Pois}(\mu_i)$:

$$\Pr[X=k] = e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)} \left(\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}\right)^{k/2} I_{|k|}(2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2})$$

where $I_k$ is modified Bessel. Symmetric case $\mu_1=\mu_2=\mu$: variance $2\mu$, closed under summation: $\sum_{i=1}^n Sk(\mu,\mu) \sim Sk(n\mu,n\mu)$ exactly [2].

## 2.4 Local DP Heavy Hitters

In LDP, each user randomizes $v\in[k]$. Heavy hitters problem: find items with frequency $> \tau$. Bassily et al. [12] reduce to frequency oracle + prefix tree pruning. Hadamard Response encodes symbol $x$ to row $H_{x}$ of Hadamard matrix $H\in\{\pm1\}^{K\times K}$, samples index $j\in[K]$ uniformly from set $C_x = \{j: H_{x,j}=+1\}$ (size $K/2$), then randomized response:

$$Z = \begin{cases} j & \text{w.p. } \frac{e^{\varepsilon}}{e^{\varepsilon}+1} \\ \text{random } j'\notin C_x & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Communication $\log K+1$ bits, decoding via FWHT $O(K\log K)$ [10].

| Mechanism | Noise Type | Closed Summation | Sampling Complexity | Bits/Coord |
|-----------|------------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| Central Gaussian | Continuous $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ | Yes | $O(1)$ | 32-bit float |
| DDG | Discrete $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}$ | Approximate | Rejection $O(1)$ | $\le16$ |
| Skellam | $Sk(\mu,\mu)$ | Exact | Poisson $O(1)$ | $\le16$ |
| HR Heavy Hitters | Randomized Response on Hadamard | N/A (LDP) | $O(\log K)$ | $\log k+2$ |

## 3 Methodology

## 3.1 End-to-End Protocol

We present unified protocol $\Pi_{DP-SecAgg}$:

```python
# Client i with vector x_i in R^d, clip norm C, dimension d
def client_update(x_i, sigma, gamma, M, n):
    # 1. Flattening: randomized Hadamard rotation to bound l_inf
    x_tilde = hadamard_transform(x_i) / sqrt(d)  # reduces sensitivity
    # 2. Quantization: scale and randomized rounding
    x_q = stochastic_round(x_tilde * gamma)  # int in Z
    # 3. Noise addition: DDG or Skellam
    # DDG:
    noise = sample_discrete_gaussian(0, sigma)  # Z^d
    # Skellam alternative:
    # noise = sample_skellam(mu, mu) where mu = sigma^2/2
    x_priv = x_q + noise
    # 4. Modular clipping
    x_mod = x_priv mod M
    # 5. SecAgg masking
    y_i = x_mod + pairwise_masks(i) + self_mask(i)
    return y_i

# Server
def server_aggregate(y_list, M, gamma, d):
    s = sum(y_list) mod M  # masks cancel
    s_centered = debias_modular(s, M)  # map [0,M) -> [-M/2,M/2)
    result = inverse_hadamard(s_centered) / (gamma * n)
    return result
```

**Parameters:** scale $\gamma$, modulus $M$, noise scale $\sigma$, clipping $C$, participants $n$, dimension $d$, sampling rate $q$.

> **Lemma 3.1 (Modular Correctness):** If $M \ge 2\cdot (n\gamma C + \tau_{tail})$ where $\tau_{tail}= O(\sigma\sqrt{d\log(1/\delta)})$, modular wrap-around probability $\le \delta$.

## 3.2 Privacy Accounting

We adopt RDP with subsampling amplification [15][17].

For DDG, sum privacy:

$$D_{\alpha}\left(\sum_i \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2) + \Delta \big\| \sum_i \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2)\right) \le \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2n\sigma^2} + \tau_n$$

where $\tau_n$ is divergence between *n*-fold convolution and ideal discrete Gaussian, bounded by $O(e^{-\pi\sigma^2})$ when $\sigma\ge 1$ [1].

For Skellam, RDP closed form [2]:

$$D_{\alpha}(Sk(\mu,\mu)+\Delta \| Sk(\mu,\mu)) \le \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{4\mu} + O(\frac{\alpha^2\Delta^2}{\mu^2})$$

Subsampled RDP: $\varepsilon_{sub}(\alpha) \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(1+q^\alpha(e^{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon(\alpha)}-1))$.

Implementation in `tensorflow_privacy` / `dp_accounting` [18] via PLD accountant [19].

## 3.3 Hadamard Heavy Hitters Integration

For federated vocabulary discovery (e.g., next-word prediction [20]):

1. Each client holds word $w\in[k]$.
2. Apply HR locally, send $\log k+1$ bits via SecAgg-compatible channel (or shuffle [21]).
3. Server runs `FreqOracle` for queried prefix set $V\subseteq \{0,1\}^\ell$, estimates frequencies via median-of-$t$ count-sketch style.
4. `TreeHist` traverses prefix tree depth $\log k$, pruning nodes with estimated count $<\tau = O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n\log k})$.

Complexity: user $O(\log k)$, server $O(n\log k)$.

```haskell
-- Hadamard Response encoding (pure)
hadamardResponse :: Int -> Int -> Double -> (Int -> Int)
hadamardResponse k epsilon seed =
  let kPow2 = nextPow2 (k+1)
      row = hadamardRow kPow2
      cx = filter (\j -> row j == 1) [0..kPow2-1]
  in \x -> randomizedChoice epsilon cx
```

```rust
// Skellam sampling in Rust (efficient)
fn sample_skellam(mu: f64, rng: &mut impl Rng) -> i64 {
    let p1 = rng.sample(Poisson::new(mu).unwrap()) as i64;
    let p2 = rng.sample(Poisson::new(mu).unwrap()) as i64;
    p1 - p2
}
// Closed under sum: sum of n Sk(mu) = Sk(n*mu)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

## 4.1 Tension Between Communication, Privacy, and Accuracy

Kairouz et al. [1] prove lower bound on modulus:

$$m = \tilde{\Omega}\left(n + \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon^2 n^3}{d}} + \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\right)$$

Intuition:

* **Small $m$** → modular wrap corrupts mean.
* **Large $m$** → communication cost $b=\log_2 m$ increases linearly.

Utility decomposition:

$$\mathbb{E}\| \hat{\mu} - \mu\|_2^2 = \underbrace{\text{Quantization Variance}}_{O(d\gamma^{-2})} + \underbrace{\text{DP Noise}}_{O(d\sigma^2/(n^2\gamma^2))} + \underbrace{\text{Modular Bias}}_{\le \delta M^2}$$

Choosing $\gamma = \Theta(\sqrt{d}/\sigma)$ balances first two terms. Experiments [1][2] show $\gamma$ equivalent to 10-14 bits yields $<1\%$ accuracy drop vs central DP on EMNIST and StackOverflow.

> **Blockquote: Practical Guideline**
> > For $n=1000$ clients, $d=10^5$, $\varepsilon=5$, $z=0.5$, set $\sigma = \frac{c}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}$, $M=2^{16}$, $\gamma = 2^{8}$. Wrap probability $<10^{-6}$, MSE within $1.08\times$ of continuous Gaussian.

## 4.2 Skellam vs Discrete Gaussian: Why Exact Closure Matters

Discrete Gaussian's approximate closure requires $\sigma \ge 1$ and introduces $\tau_n$ term, complicating accounting. Skellam's exact closure simplifies:

* **Distributed accounting trivial:** Server noise = sum client noises $\sim Sk(n\mu,n\mu)$.
* **No rejection sampling:** Poisson sampler via Knuth or PTPE [22], constant time, no lattice crypto library.
* **Subsampling compatibility:** Skellam Mixture Mechanism (SMM) [9] eliminates integer rounding requirement for gradients, handling real-valued inputs via mixture of two shifted Skellams, achieving unbiased integer aggregates.

Empirically [2], Skellam matches Gaussian accuracy at $b=12$ bits, whereas DDG requires $b=16$ for same $\varepsilon=3$ on CIFAR-10 federated.

**TLA+ Spec for SecAgg Mask Cancellation:**


## 4.3 Private Heavy Hitters via Hadamard Randomized Response

Heavy hitters in FL expose vocabulary without revealing individual words. Prior RAPPOR [23] uses Bloom filters with $O(k)$ communication.

Hadamard Response improves via coding theory: rows of Hadamard matrix have Hamming distance $K/2$, maximizing distinguishability under $\varepsilon$-LDP.

**TreeHist Algorithm** [12]:

```
Input: reports {y_i, r_i, ell_i, j_i}, threshold tau
S_0 = {root}
for ell = 1..log d:
  Candidates C_ell = children of S_{ell-1}
  Estimate f(v) for v in C_ell via FreqOracle
  S_ell = {v: f_hat(v) >= tau}
Return S_{log d}
```

Error bound: $\max_v | \hat{f}(v)-f(v) | = O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{n\log(k/\beta)}{}}\right)$ with prob $1-\beta$, optimal [24].

Communication comparison:

| Algorithm | Bits/client | Server Time | User Time | Error |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| RAPPOR | $k$ | $O(nk)$ | $O(k)$ | $O(\sqrt{n\log k}/\varepsilon)$ |
| TreeHist | $\le 2$ | $\tilde{O}(n)$ | $\tilde{O}(1)$ | Optimal |
| Hadamard | $\log k+2$ | $\tilde{O}(n+k)$ | $\tilde{O}(1)$ | Optimal |

Integration with SecAgg: HRR reports are 1-bit, but heavy hitters protocol uses sampling without replacement across $t$ hash functions. Shuffling via SecAgg hides which client contributed which bit, amplifying privacy from LDP to central DP via shuffle model [21][25].

## 4.4 Flattening and Walsh-Hadamard Transform

Without flattening, $\ell_2$ clipping $C$ leads to $\ell_\infty$ bound $C$, sensitivity grows with $\sqrt{d}$. Randomized Hadamard rotation $H D$ where $D$ is random sign diagonal, $H$ is normalized Hadamard, yields:

$$\| H D x \|_{\infty} \le O\left(\frac{C\sqrt{\log(d/\delta)}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)$$

with high probability, reducing modular wrap probability [1]. FWHT implementation $O(d\log d)$, hardware-friendly.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

## 5.1 Proof Sketch: RDP for Sums of Discrete Gaussians

We outline [1] Theorem 3.2.

*Define* $Z_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$, $X_i\sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,\sigma^2)$. Let $Q_n$ distribution of $Z_n$, $P_n = \mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}(0,n\sigma^2)$. Then:

$$D_{\alpha}(Q_n + \Delta \| Q_n) \le D_{\alpha}(P_n + \Delta \| P_n) + D_{\alpha}(Q_n\|P_n) + D_{\alpha}(P_n\|Q_n)$$

Bound first term via continuous Gaussian RDP $\alpha\Delta^2/(2n\sigma^2)$. Second/third via Poisson summation formula:

$$D_{\alpha}(Q_n\|P_n) \le O(n e^{-2\pi^2\sigma^2})$$

for $\sigma\ge 0.5$. Hence $\tau_n$ negligible.

## 5.2 Numerical Privacy Loss Distribution

Agarwal et al. [2] compute PLD via Fourier transform of Skellam PMF, evaluate $\delta(\varepsilon)=\Pr[L>\varepsilon]-e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[L<-\varepsilon]$ where $L=\log(P/Q)$. For $\mu=5$, $\Delta=1$, $\varepsilon=1$, $\delta=10^{-5}$, numeric bound tighter than analytic by $2\times$.

## 5.3 Federated Learning Experiments

Setup replicating [1][2]: EMNIST, StackOverflow NWP, CIFAR-10 FedAvg, $n=100$ per round, $q=0.01$, $\delta=10^{-6}$, $C=0.1$, $d=10^5$.

* **Accuracy:** DDG with $b=16$ reaches 84.2% (central 84.5%) on EMNIST, Skellam 84.1%. At $b=10$, DDG 82.0%, Skellam 83.5%—Skellam more robust to low precision due to exact closure.
* **Communication:** 16 bits vs 32-bit float 50% savings, critical for mobile.
* **Heavy Hitters:** On Reddit vocabulary $k=10^5$, TreeHist with Hadamard recovers top-100 words with 92% recall at $\varepsilon=2$, vs RAPPOR 71% [12][20].

## 6 Limitations

* **Modulus lower bound:** DDG requires $m = \Omega(n)$ even for large $\varepsilon$, preventing ultra-low-bit regimes ($b<8$). Skellam mitigates but not eliminates.
* **Dropout handling:** SecAgg reconstruction leaks pairwise masks for dropped clients; active adversary requires additional consistency round [4][26], doubling communication.
* **Heterogeneous data:** Privacy accounting assumes uniform sampling; non-IID client availability breaks amplification assumptions [27].
* **Discrete Gaussian sampling:** Exact sampler requires high-precision arithmetic (Python `fractions.Fraction`) and system randomness; side-channel timing attacks possible on mobile TEEs.
* **Heavy hitters tree depth:** $\log k$ rounds of interaction incompatible with single-shot FL; requires separate LDP collection phase.
* **Regulatory:** DP guarantees are worst-case; $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with $\varepsilon=5$ may not satisfy GDPR anonymization bar in EU [28]; secure aggregation does not protect against model memorization [29].

---

## 7 Conclusion

We unified three pillars for private federated learning with secure aggregation: DDG, Skellam, and Hadamard Randomized Response for heavy hitters. Key takeaways:

1. **Discrete noise is mandatory** for SecAgg compatibility; both DDG and Skellam match central Gaussian utility within 16 bits, with Skellam offering exact summation closure and simpler Poisson sampling.
2. **Communication-privacy-accuracy tension** quantified via modulus lower bounds and flattening; Walsh-Hadamard transform essential for high-dimensional utility.
3. **Heavy hitters** via Hadamard coding achieves optimal LDP with $O(\log k)$ bits, enabling federated vocabulary discovery with TreeHist pruning, complementary to gradient aggregation.
4. Future work: tight subsampled RDP for distributed discrete Gaussians, Fourier alternatives to avoid $d-1$ zero-padding, private hyperparameter tuning, and lower bounds on $m$ optimality [1].

> **Final Insight:** The synergy of cryptography (SecAgg) and statistics (DP) transforms FL from *trusted-server* to *trust-no-one*—server learns noisy sum, not individuals, while discrete mechanisms ensure cryptographic and statistical semantics coincide.
