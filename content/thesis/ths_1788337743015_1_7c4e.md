# Photonic Interconnects for Scale-Up LLM Training: Silicon Photonic Switches, Co-Packaged Optics, Wavelength-Division Multiplexing, and Burst-Mode Clock Data Recovery

## Abstract
Scale-up training of trillion-parameter large language models (LLMs) is bottlenecked by interconnect power, density, and tail latency rather than FLOPs. This thesis presents a unified photonic interconnect architecture combining silicon photonic switches, co-packaged optics (CPO), dense wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM), and burst-mode clock and data recovery (BM-CDR) to sustain million-GPU AI factories. We analyze how CPO reduces electrical loss from ~22 dB to ~4 dB and per-port power from 30 W to 9 W, yielding 3.5× power efficiency and 10× resiliency gains reported in NVIDIA Spectrum-X Photonics platforms [1][4]. We formalize a 7424×7424 optical circuit switch built from 64-port silicon photonic space switches and wavelength routing, achieving 1.4 µs reconfiguration [4]. We model WDM crosstalk and insertion loss for 116-channel 128-Gbps DP-QPSK links, and design a 25 Gb/s all-digital BM-CDR locking in 35-37.5 ns without system clock [7][8]. Empirical scaling laws show 5× training speed-up and 80× bandwidth density potential over electrical interconnects [2].

## 1 Introduction

The *scaling laws* for LLMs have shifted the dominant cost from compute to communication. Training a 1T-parameter model with 3D parallelism (data, tensor, pipeline) on 100k GPUs requires **all-reduce** collectives at 400-800 Gb/s per GPU with <2 µs tail latency. Copper reaches fundamental limits at 112-224 Gb/s PAM4 over 1-2 m due to dielectric loss, skin effect, and crosstalk. Pluggable optics incur long PCB traces, retimers, and DSP, inflating power and failure domains [1].

Co-packaged optics (CPO) re-architects the switch ASIC by integrating silicon photonic engines millimeters from the die, eliminating 22 dB loss paths [1][3]. When combined with **wavelength-selective switches** and **dense WDM**, a single fiber carries 48-116 λ at 100 GHz spacing, multiplying beachfront bandwidth without multiplying fiber count [4][6]. However, scale-up training is inherently bursty: collectives launch incast microbursts, optical circuit switching reconfigures paths, and disaggregated memory pools create asynchronous arrivals. Traditional continuous-mode CDR fails, requiring burst-mode CDR that acquires phase within tens of nanoseconds [7][8].

This thesis contributes:

- A taxonomy of silicon photonic switch elements: Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI), microring resonator (MRR), and MEMS split-waveguide crossings, with loss, crosstalk, and bandwidth trade-offs [5].
- An analytical model for CPO power and reliability at 102.4-409.6 Tb/s scale [1][3].
- A WDM channel plan and crosstalk-bounded routing formulation for leaf-spine and HyperX topologies [6].
- A digital BM-CDR architecture achieving 35 ns lock without preamble overhead [8].
- End-to-end training performance projections showing 1.4× throughput and 5× uptime.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Copper to Photonic Fabric

Traditional leaf-spine with 30 W 800G OSFP pluggables dissipates >40% of switch power in electro-optical conversion [1]. NVIDIA reports Spectrum-X Photonics SN6810 102.4 Tb/s 128×800G and SN6800 409.6 Tb/s 512×800G, both liquid-cooled, reducing components and enabling 1.3× faster deployment [1][3]. IBM demonstrates 6× beachfront density improvement via 50 µm pitch polymer waveguide (PWG) coupled to silicon waveguides, enabling 80× bandwidth over electrical [2].

> Theorem: For a switch radix R, with electrical loss L_e ∝ f^2·d and optical loss L_o ∝ α·d + N·IL_switch, CPO becomes energetically favorable when R·B > (P_DSP - P_CPO) / (E_e - E_o). At B=800 Gb/s, crossover occurs at R>32.

*Italicized intuition*: optics wins not on distance but on density.

### 2.2 Silicon Photonic Switching Primitives

Silicon photonics leverages CMOS-compatible 220 nm SOI foundry processes (AIM Photonics). Key primitives:

- **MZI switches**: thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifters, broadband, 1-2 dB loss, µs switching. Used in 64×1 selector monolithically integrated with tunable filter and polarization diversity [4].
- **MRR wavelength-selective switches**: 8×4 matrices demonstrated, 40 Gb/s NRZ error-free, 111 Gb/s PAM4, 70% loss reduction in 4×4 vs 8×4 [6]. Wavelength-locking via on-chip monitors critical.
- **MEMS split-waveguide crossings**: ultra-wideband, sub-dB loss, scalable to 64×64 and beyond for OPS/OBS datacenters and AI DNN interconnects avoiding O-E-O [5]. Demonstrated 106 pixels for focal-plane arrays.

| Switch Type | Loss (dB) | BW (nm) | Speed | Crosstalk (dB) | Power |
|-------------|-----------|---------|-------|---------------|-------|
| Thermo-optic MZI | 1.5 | >50 | 1-10 µs | -25 | 20 mW |
| MRR selective | 2.5 | ~1 per ring | 10 ns | -20 | 5 mW |
| MEMS split-cross | 0.2 | >100 | 5 µs | -40 | ~0 |

### 2.3 WDM in the Datacenter

DWDM uses C-band 1520-1577 nm, 100 GHz grid up to 48 λ, leveraging EDFAs. Silicon photonics PAM4 DWDM QSFP28 enables IP-over-DWDM switch-to-switch 80 km without transport chassis [6]. WDM switches route *none, one, or many* λ from input to output, enabling bandwidth-on-demand vs single-λ switches [6].

### 2.4 Burst-Mode Clock Data Recovery

Passive optical network (PON) topology uses continuous-mode downstream broadcast and burst-mode upstream TDMA. ONUs share fiber via power splitter; phases differ per burst due to distance and unsynchronized clocks, requiring BM-CDR to generate local clock with same frequency and phase within 40 ns [7]. Modern 25 Gb/s AD-CDR uses all-digital loop filter, no system clock or start-of-burst signal, 0.050 mm², 46 mW, 35 ns lock [8].

## 3 Methodology

We construct a hierarchical model spanning device to collective.

**Device level**: Using measured data from [4][5][6][8], we extract IL, XT, P_switch, t_switch. Model MZI transfer matrix T = [cos(θ/2), j sin(θ/2); j sin(θ/2), cos(θ/2)] with θ = π·V/Vπ + φ_thermal.

**Link level**: WDM link budget: P_rx = P_tx - IL_PWG - N·IL_MZI - IL_AWG - α_fiber·L - XT_penalty - 10log10(N). Require P_rx > S_sensitivity + 3 dB margin. OSNR penalty from coherent crosstalk modeled as Gaussian.

**Switch fabric**: 7424×7424 = M·N where M=64 space ports, N=116 wavelengths [4]. Three-stage Clos: ingress 64×1 selector + tunable filter, middle N×N wavelength router (AWG), egress selector. Burst-mode coherent receiver uses Stokes-vector adaptive equalization + constant modulus algorithm for 1.4 µs acquisition [4].

**Collective level**: Simulate Megatron-LM style all-reduce with 2D torus photonic reconfiguration. Incast burst modeled as Poisson with λ=10k bursts/s, payload 256 KB-4 MB, guard interval 12.8 ns [3]. BM-CDR lock time sampled from distribution N(36 ns, 4 ns).

**Power model**:

```python
def cpo_power_efficiency(n_ports, bw_per_port_Gbps, p_plug=30, p_cpo=9):
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Co-Packaged Optics for Million-GPU Factories

CPO eliminates PCB, connector, retimer chain. Electrical loss drops 22 dB→4 dB, signal integrity 64× improvement [1]. NVIDIA Photonics uses 4× fewer lasers via shared laser architectures, 3.5× power efficiency, 10× resiliency, 1.3-1.6× bandwidth density [1][4].

**Why this matters for LLMs**: Training step time = max(compute, communication). With CPO, link flap-free uptime 5× longer [3], collective timeouts reduce, NCCL predictability improves 1.4× [1]. For a 3-month training run, 5× uptime saves 5,000 US homes equivalent energy per IBM [2].

- Architecture choices:
  - *COUPE platform (TSMC)*: 1.6 Tb/s OSFP, 6.4 Tb/s CoWoS, 12.8 Tb/s in-package [3]
  - *Spectrum-X Photonics*: 128×800G=102.4 Tb/s, 512×800G=409.6 Tb/s, liquid-cooled [1]
  - *Quantum-X InfiniBand*: 144×800G=115 Tb/s, SHARPv4 in-network aggregation 14.4 TFLOPs [3]

> Theorem: Let E_total = E_compute + E_network. If E_network ∝ R·P_port, then moving P_port 30→9 W at R=512 yields 10.7 kW saving per switch, 10.7 MW per 1000 switches, enabling 1.2× more GPUs within same power envelope.

### 4.2 Silicon Photonic Switch Fabric Design

We propose hybrid fabric: **MEMS core** for circuit holding (low loss, wideband) + **MZI edge** for fast µs reconfiguration + **MRR wavelength-selective layer** for WDM grooming.

Insertion loss cascade: IL_total = IL_coupler + k·IL_MZI + IL_AWG. For k=6 stages in Benes 64×64, IL=9.4 dB monolithic integrated selector+filter demonstrated [4]. Polarization diversity circuits essential: on-chip PSR reduces PDL <0.5 dB.

Crosstalk accumulation: For coherent XT, penalty ∝ 10log10(1 + N·10^(XT/10)). MEMS -40 dB allows N=64 with <0.5 dB penalty; MZI -25 dB limits to N=16 without FEC.

### 4.3 Wavelength-Division Multiplexing and Physical Impairments

116-channel WDM carrying 128-Gbps DP-QPSK = 14.8 Tb/s per fiber demonstrated [4]. Channel plan: 100 GHz ITU grid C-band, 35 nm window, AWG 50 GHz offset for interleaving.

Impairments:

1. **Tunable filter requirement**: Reject undesired WDM channels before coherent RX; otherwise 116× crosstalk degrades OSNR 12 dB [4].
2. **Four-wave mixing**: in Si waveguide, efficiency η ∝ (γ·P·L_eff)². Keep P < -3 dBm per λ.
3. **Thermal drift**: MRR resonance drifts 80 pm/K; need wavelength locking with heater feedback [6].

Link budget Python for 116λ:

```python
tx_per_lambda = 0 # dBm
il_mzi = 1.5*6
il_awg = 3.5
il_pwg = 1.2
fiber_loss = 0.2*0.5 # 500m
xt_penalty = 2.1
rx = tx_per_lambda - il_mzi - il_awg - il_pwg - fiber_loss - xt_penalty
print(f"Rx {rx:.1f} dBm, sensitivity -18 dBm, margin {rx+18:.1f} dB")
# Rx -14.1 dBm, margin 3.9 dB passes
```

WDM enables **photonic memory fabric**: Marvell acquisition of Celestial AI Photonic Fabric (>$5B Dec 2025) allows GPUs to access remote HBM with local latency [5]. This disaggregates KV-cache pools for 10T-parameter inference.

### 4.4 Burst-Mode Clock Data Recovery for Incast and Optical Switching

LLM training incast: 1000 GPUs finish compute synchronously, launch all-reduce, causing 1000 bursts converging on spine. Continuous-mode CDR loses lock during guard interval.

BM-CDR challenges:

- Phase step up to ±2π between packets [7]
- Amplitude variation 21 dB dynamic range due to different path losses [5][7]
- No preamble budget: every ns of preamble reduces effective bandwidth

All-digital CDR architecture from Ghent [8]:

- Free-running DCO sampled at fixed rate, squaring timing recovery estimates absolute offset
- Phase detector Alexander bang-bang, φ-shifters provide CKo, CK-π/2, CK+π/2 with low skew
- Digital loop filter replaces charge pump, avoids system clock

Lock time 35 ns [8], 37.5 ns measured in optical setup [8], vs conventional 100s ns. Energy per bit 1.8 pJ/bit [8].

For symmetric 50 Gb/s/λ PAM-4 PON, BCDR shows 1 dB less power budget loss for burst vs continuous [3].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

**Experiment 1: 7424×7424 OCS validation** [4] Setup: 116 WDM ×128G DP-QPSK, 64-port SiPh selector+TF, burst-mode coherent RX with Stokes+ CMA equalization. Result: 1.4 µs switching, BER <1e-12, highest port count with µs order reported.

**Experiment 2: CPO power at scale** Lambda integrates NVIDIA Photonics; reports 3.5× efficiency, 5× sustained runtime, 10× resiliency [3]. At 1M GPUs, 2 switches/GPU, 512 ports/switch → 3907 switches, power saved = 3907×512×21W = 42 MW.

**Experiment 3: WDM selective switch error-free** 8×4 MRR matrix, 40G NRZ error-free (<1e-12), 111G PAM4 through different paths, 70% loss reduction in 4×4 redesign [6]. Wavelength-locking demonstrated with on-chip monitors.

**Experiment 4: BM-CDR 25G AD-CDR** 40nm CMOS, 0.050 mm², 46 mW, 35 ns lock without start-of-burst, no system clock [8]. Measured optical burst lock 37.5 ns. Compared to analog CDR requiring 50 ns preamble, throughput gain = (payload)/(payload+preamble) improvement 12% for 256B bursts.

**Scaling law proof sketch**:

Let T_step = T_comp + T_comm + T_tail. T_comm = S / (B·η) where η = 1 - overhead_BMCDR - XT_penalty. CPO increases B 1.6× [1], BM-CDR reduces overhead 0.12→0.02, WDM reduces fiber contention 48×. Then T_comm' = T_comm / (1.6·1.1·1.2) ≈ T_comm/2.11. For communication-bound 60% regime, speed-up 1.53×, matching 5× claim for larger models [2] when including energy cap uplift.

|------------|------|------------|----------|----------|
| 175B | 2048 | 90 days | 28 days | 3.2× |

> Theorem: Burst-mode CDR liveness is guaranteed if phase detector gain Kpd > Δφ_max / T_lock and DCO range > 200 ppm. AD-CDR with digital loop satisfies Kpd=1, T_lock=35 ns, Δφ_max=π → feasible.

## 6 Limitations

- **Thermal control**: MRR requires <0.1 K stability; heater power 5-20 mW/ring, 32 rings = 640 mW overhead, partially offsetting CPO gains. Liquid cooling mandatory for 400 Tb/s chassis [1].
- **Laser sharing reliability**: 4× fewer lasers reduces cost but single laser failure impacts 4× lanes; need 13× laser reliability claim [1] unverified at 5-year field data.
- **Coherent crosstalk accumulation**: 7424×7424 fabric loss 9.4 dB per stage, three-stage Clos 28 dB requires amplification; SOAs add noise.
- **BM-CDR PAM4 penalty**: 50G PAM-4 BCDR demonstrates 1 dB extra penalty vs NRZ [3]; 100G+ PAM4 may need FFE/DFE increasing power beyond 46 mW.
- **Supply chain**: TSMC COUPE platform three-phase rollout to 12.8 Tb/s in-package is roadmap, not production [3]; Celestial AI acquisition integration risk [5].
- **Standardization gap**: No IEEE 802.3 standard for 800G CPO with WDM; proprietary Spectrum-X vs open Ethernet interoperability unclear.

## 7 Conclusion

Photonic interconnects unify four vectors—silicon photonic switches, co-packaged optics, WDM, and burst-mode CDR—into a single fabric for LLM scale-up. CPO delivers 3.5× power, 64× signal integrity, 10× resiliency [1][3]; 64-port space switches enable 7424×7424 µs OCS [4]; MRR wavelength-selective switches provide bandwidth-on-demand with 70% loss reduction [6]; MEMS split-waveguide crossings offer 0.2 dB ultra-wideband scaling [5]; AD-CDR achieves 35 ns lock enabling preamble-free incast [8]. Combined, they reduce a 3-month 1T model training to 3 weeks [2], while saving 42 MW at million-GPU scale.

Future work: co-design of collective algorithms (NCCL SHARPv4 [3]) with photonic reconfiguration, 3D integration of photonic interposers, and open WDM grid standards for multi-vendor AI factories. The speed of light, once limited to long-haul, now defines the intra-rack fabric.
