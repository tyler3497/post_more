# Microlocal Analysis: Pseudodifferential Operators, Wavefront Sets, and the Propagation of Singularities

## Abstract

Classical analysis locates singularities in space; microlocal analysis locates them in phase space. We survey the machinery invented by Sato, Hörmander, and their successors: pseudodifferential operators and the symbolic calculus, the wavefront set as the refined measure of a distribution's singularity, and Fourier integral operators as quantized canonical transformations. The centerpiece is the Duistermaat–Hörmander propagation of singularities theorem: for operators of real principal type, the wavefront set of a solution is invariant under the Hamiltonian flow of the principal symbol, so singularities travel along bicharacteristics. We explain Egorov's theorem, the Duistermaat–Guillemin trace formula relating the spectrum of elliptic operators to periodic bicharacteristics, and modern applications to quantum ergodicity, scattering resonances, and general relativity. We close with semiclassical limits, defect measures, and the frontier of subelliptic propagation.

## 1 Introduction

Consider the wave equation $(\partial_t^2 - \Delta)u = 0$ with rough initial data. The classical theory says singularities propagate along light rays at finite speed — but *what is a singularity*, precisely, and *which direction* does each singular component travel? A point $x$ where $u$ fails to be smooth is too coarse: a wave may be singular at $x$ only in certain *directions* of oscillation. The microlocal philosophy, born in the early 1970s with the work of Sato–Kawai–Kashiwara on the analytic wavefront set and Hörmander's $C^\infty$ version via pseudodifferential operators, is that singularities live in **phase space** $T^*X \setminus 0$, with position *and* momentum [3].

> **Thesis.** Microlocal analysis is the discovery that linear PDE is Hamiltonian mechanics in disguise: the principal symbol of a differential operator is a function on the cotangent bundle, its Hamiltonian flow governs where singularities can go, and quantization turns this classical dynamics into operator calculus. Every major result — propagation of singularities, Weyl laws, trace formulas — is a facet of this correspondence.

## 2 Background: Pseudodifferential Operators

### 2.1 Symbols and quantization

On $\mathbb{R}^n$, a **symbol** of order $m$ and type $(\rho,\delta)$ is a smooth function $a(x,\xi)$ satisfying

$$|\partial_x^\alpha \partial_\xi^\beta a(x,\xi)| \le C_{\alpha\beta} \langle\xi\rangle^{m - \rho|\beta| + \delta|\alpha|}$$

for all multi-indices. The associated **pseudodifferential operator** is

$$a(x,D)u(x) = (2\pi)^{-n} \int e^{i x\cdot\xi} a(x,\xi)\,\widehat{u}(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

The class $L^m_{1,0}$ forms an algebra: composition corresponds to the **symbolic calculus**

$$a \# b \sim \sum_{\alpha} \frac{1}{\alpha!} \partial_\xi^\alpha a \, D_x^\alpha b,$$

an asymptotic expansion in decreasing orders [3]. The leading term of the commutator is the Poisson bracket:

$$\sigma_m([A,B]) = \frac{1}{i}\{a,b\}, \qquad \{a,b\} = \sum_j (\partial_{\xi_j}a\,\partial_{x_j}b - \partial_{x_j}a\,\partial_{\xi_j}b).$$

*This is the first glimpse of the classical–quantum correspondence: commutators of operators mirror Poisson brackets of symbols.*

### 2.2 Ellipticity and parametrices

$A \in L^m$ is **elliptic** if $|a(x,\xi)| \ge c\langle\xi\rangle^m$ for large $|\xi|$. Elliptic operators admit **parametrices** $B$ with $AB - I, BA - I \in L^{-\infty}$ (smoothing), yielding the fundamental **elliptic regularity**: $\mathrm{sing\,supp}\,Au = \mathrm{sing\,supp}\,u$. Elliptic theory is "trivial" microlocally — the interesting phenomena occur at the **characteristic set** where the principal symbol vanishes.

### 2.3 The wavefront set

For $u \in \mathcal{D}'(X)$, the **wavefront set** $WF(u) \subset T^*X \setminus 0$ refines the singular support: $(x_0,\xi_0) \notin WF(u)$ iff there exist a cutoff $\phi$ with $\phi(x_0) \ne 0$ and a conic neighborhood $\Gamma$ of $\xi_0$ such that $\widehat{\phi u}(\xi)$ decays rapidly in $\Gamma$ [3]. Equivalently, $(x_0,\xi_0) \notin WF(u)$ iff some pseudodifferential operator elliptic at $(x_0,\xi_0)$ maps $u$ to a smooth function. The projection of $WF(u)$ to $X$ is exactly $\mathrm{sing\,supp}\,u$.

| Distribution $u$ | $WF(u)$ |
|---|---|
| $\delta_0$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $\{(0,\xi): \xi \ne 0\}$ |
| $H(x_1)$ (Heaviside) | $\{(0,x',\xi_1,0): \xi_1 \ne 0\}$ |
| $1/(x_1 + i0)$ | $\{(0,x',\xi_1,0): \xi_1 > 0\}$ |
| Plane wave $e^{ix\cdot\xi_0}$ | empty (smooth!) |

## 3 Methodology: Fourier Integral Operators

### 3.1 Quantized canonical transformations

A **Fourier integral operator** (FIO) generalizes pseudodifferential operators by allowing the phase to be non-linear: $Tu(x) = \int e^{i\varphi(x,y,\theta)} a(x,y,\theta) u(y)\,dy\,d\theta$. Hörmander (1971) and Duistermaat–Hörmander (1972) showed that FIOs are the operators associated to **canonical relations** — Lagrangian submanifolds of $T^*X \times T^*Y$ [2]. When the canonical relation is the graph of a symplectomorphism $\chi$, we get **Egorov's theorem**:

> **Theorem (Egorov).** If $T$ quantizes the canonical transformation $\chi$, then $T^{-1} A T$ is a pseudodifferential operator with principal symbol $a \circ \chi$.

Conjugation by FIOs moves operators along classical dynamics. This is the engine of propagation results: reduce a general operator microlocally to a model case via a canonical transformation, solve the model, and transport back.

### 3.2 Operators of real principal type

$P$ is of **real principal type** if its principal symbol $p_m$ is real and $dp_m \ne 0$ on $\{p_m = 0\} \setminus 0$. The Hamiltonian vector field

$$H_{p_m} = \sum_j \left(\frac{\partial p_m}{\partial \xi_j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} - \frac{\partial p_m}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi_j}\right)$$

generates the **bicharacteristic flow** on the characteristic set. The wave operator $\Box = \partial_t^2 - \Delta$ has symbol $\tau^2 - |\xi|^2$; its bicharacteristics project to light rays — recovering finite propagation speed, now with directional precision.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The propagation of singularities theorem

> **Theorem (Duistermaat–Hörmander, 1972).** Let $P$ be a properly supported pseudodifferential operator of real principal type, and $u \in \mathcal{D}'$ with $Pu \in C^\infty$. Then $WF(u) \setminus WF(Pu)$ is invariant under the bicharacteristic flow of $p_m$ [2].

In words: singularities of solutions can only travel along the Hamiltonian flow lines; they are neither created nor destroyed in the interior. For the wave equation, this says the wavefront set at time $t$ is the time-$t$ geodesic flow of the initial wavefront set — a precise, directional Huygens principle.

### 4.2 The Duistermaat–Guillemin trace formula

For a positive elliptic operator $P$ (e.g., $\sqrt{\Delta}$ on a compact Riemannian manifold), the **wave trace** $\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-itP}$ has singularities exactly at the periods of closed bicharacteristics — closed geodesics. Duistermaat and Guillemin (1975) computed the singularity expansion: each non-degenerate periodic orbit contributes an explicit term involving its period, Maslov index, and linearized Poincaré map [4].

> *Poisson relation:* $\mathrm{sing\,supp}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-itP} \subset \{\text{periods of closed bicharacteristics}\} \cup \{0\}$.

This is the analytic backbone of **quantum chaos**: the spectrum "hears" the classical periodic orbits. At $t = 0$ one recovers the Weyl law $N(\lambda) \sim c_n \mathrm{vol}(M)\lambda^n$; the periodic-orbit sum is the oscillatory correction — a rigorous form of Gutzwiller's trace formula.

### 4.3 Radial estimates and scattering

Modern microlocal analysis extends propagation to non-compact and singular settings. Melrose's **scattering calculus** compactifies $\mathbb{R}^n$ radially and studies operators at infinity; Vasy's program uses **radial point estimates** at the "event horizon" of phase space to prove propagation through normally hyperbolic trapped sets. Applications include:

- **Resonances**: poles of the meromorphically continued resolvent, with fractal Weyl upper bounds (Sjöstrand–Zworski).
- **Kerr black holes**: decay of waves via microlocal analysis at the trapped photon sphere (Dafermos–Rodnianski–Shlapentokh-Rothman).
- **Quantum ergodicity**: Schnirelman's theorem — almost all eigenfunctions equidistribute in phase space when the geodesic flow is ergodic — proved via Egorov and defect measures.

### 4.4 Semiclassical analysis

Introducing a small parameter $h$, the **semiclassical calculus** quantizes $a(x, h\xi)$: operators $a^w(x, hD)$ with the correspondence principle built in. The semiclassical wavefront set $WF_h(u)$ tracks $h$-oscillations, and propagation becomes the classical flow at speed $O(1)$ in rescaled time. **Quantum ergodicity**, **scarring** (Heller; Faure–Nonnenmacher entropy bounds), and tunneling estimates all live here [2].

## 5 Empirical Aspects: Seeing the Invisible

Microlocal predictions are testable. **Quantum billiards** — microwave cavities shaped as chaotic domains — exhibit eigenvalue statistics matching random-matrix predictions, and eigenfunction "scars" concentrate along unstable periodic orbits, exactly as semiclassical defect measures allow. In seismology, **travel-time tomography** inverts the propagation of singularities: earthquake wavefront sets, transported along bicharacteristics of the elastic wave operator, reveal Earth's interior. The same mathematics underlies **synthetic aperture radar** and medical ultrasound imaging, where FIOs model the measurement process and their canonical relations determine what can be reconstructed.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Linearity.** The entire edifice — symbolic calculus, Egorov, propagation — is linear. Nonlinear microlocal analysis (Bony's paradifferential calculus, Alinhac's paracomposition) exists but is far less complete; genuinely nonlinear propagation (e.g., for Einstein's equations) requires bespoke arguments.
2. **Loss of derivatives.** Propagation estimates typically lose derivatives at radial points and trapped sets; sharp regularity is delicate.
3. **Global geometry.** The theorems are microlocal — local in phase space. Global conclusions (e.g., meromorphic continuation of resolvents) need non-trapping or normally hyperbolic hypotheses that fail in general.
4. **Analytic category.** The $C^\infty$ wavefront set misses exponentially small effects; the analytic wavefront set (Sato–Kawai–Kashiwara, Bros–Iagolnitzer) is needed for tunneling and unique continuation, at the cost of much harder analysis.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Microlocal analysis fulfilled a 300-year-old promise: from Huygens' wavelets to Hamilton's optics to Hörmander's symbols, the propagation of waves is the propagation of *phase-space* singularities along classical trajectories. The wavefront set turned "singular" from a pointwise defect into a geometric object with its own dynamics; Fourier integral operators turned canonical transformations into computational tools; and the trace formula turned the spectrum into a readout of classical periodic motion. The frontier — subelliptic propagation, fractal uncertainty principles, microlocal analysis on singular spaces — continues the same program: **make the invisible geometry of PDE visible, one covector at a time** [2][3][4].
