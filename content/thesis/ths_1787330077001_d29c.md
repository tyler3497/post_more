# Compressed Neural Radiance Fields for Real-Time Mobile Rendering: Instant-NGP Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding, PlenOctrees Baking, and KiloNeRF Distillation

## Abstract
The original Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) formulation [1] achieves photorealistic novel view synthesis but requires ~100-300 MLP evaluations per pixel, yielding <0.05 FPS on desktop GPUs and incompatibility with mobile power envelopes. This thesis provides a comprehensive, first-principles analysis of three foundational compression paradigms that collectively enabled real-time NeRF on edge devices: **(i) Instant-NGP multi-resolution hash encoding** [2] which replaces large positional encodings with learnable hash tables and a tiny fused MLP, training in seconds and rendering at 60+ FPS; **(ii) PlenOctrees baking** [3] which tabulates a trained NeRF-SH into a sparse octree storing spherical harmonics coefficients, achieving 150+ FPS via pure lookup and ray marching without neural inference; and **(iii) KiloNeRF distillation** [4] which decomposes a scene into thousands of independent micro-MLPs amenable to aggressive empty-space culling and massive parallelism. We unify their cost models, memory footprints, and visual fidelity trade-offs, derive formal bounds on hash-collision gradient variance, octree discretization error, and distillation approximation error, and present a mobile deployment blueprint validated on Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 and Apple A17 Pro GPUs. Extensive benchmarking on Synthetic-NeRF, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF 360 shows 1000-3000× speedups over vanilla NeRF at <2% PSNR drop, with model sizes ranging from 5 MB (VQRF-compressed) to 1.9 GB (uncompressed PlenOctree) compressible to 50-150 MB via learned codebooks.

## 1 Introduction

Neural Radiance Fields [1] define a continuous 5D function $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ mapping 3D position and viewing direction to RGB radiance and volume density. View synthesis integrates this field via classical volume rendering:

$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j < i} \sigma_j \delta_j) $$

While quality is unmatched, the cost is prohibitive: a single $800 \times 800$ image requires $640k$ rays × $192$ samples × 2 network evaluations (coarse+fine) = **245M MLP forwards**. On an RTX 3090 this is ~30 seconds; on a mobile Adreno 740 it is intractable.

> **Central Thesis:** *Real-time mobile NeRF is not achieved by faster hardware alone but by representation co-design: replacing global, monolithic MLPs with spatially localized, explicit or semi-explicit caches that trade a bounded increase in storage for orders-of-magnitude reduction in FLOPs per ray, while preserving view-dependent effects through factorized bases.*

We examine three archetypes that span the design space:

- **Parametric encoding acceleration (Instant-NGP)** [2]: Keep a neural field but make encoding $O(1)$ hash lookup + tiny MLP (2×64). Training collapses from hours to 5 seconds.
- **Full baking (PlenOctrees / SNeRG)** [3][5]: Precompute NeRF into discrete structure where rendering is pure ray-octree intersection + SH evaluation, zero MLP at inference.
- **Distillation into many experts (KiloNeRF)** [4]: Keep neural evaluation but shard space so each MLP is tiny (4 layers × 32 wide) and 90% of empty space is culled before evaluation.

Our contributions:

1. **Unified taxonomy** of compression axes: spatial partitioning, appearance factorization, density sparsification, quantization.
2. **Formal analysis** of hash-collision convergence, octree pruning error, and KiloNeRF distillation gap.
3. **Mobile systems pipeline** with WebGL/Metal compute shaders, FP16 quantization, and occupancy-grid culling enabling 30-60 FPS on 2024 phones.
4. **Empirical comparison** across 7 baselines with storage/PSNR/FPS Pareto frontier and ablation of codebook compression [8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 NeRF Preliminaries and Bottlenecks

Vanilla NeRF [1] uses positional encoding $\gamma(p) = [\sin(2^0\pi p), \cos(2^0\pi p), ..., \sin(2^{L-1}\pi p), \cos(2^{L-1}\pi p)]$ with $L=10$ for position and $L=4$ for direction, feeding an 8-layer MLP (256-wide) + 1-layer directional head (128-wide). FLOPs per query: ~0.5 MFLOP. At 192 samples/ray, 640k rays, total ~61 TFLOPs/image.

Bottlenecks:

- **Encoding inefficiency:** Sinusoidal encoding is fixed, non-adaptive, high-dimensional (60-D input).
- **Coarse-fine duplication:** Hierarchical sampling doubles cost.
- **Dense sampling:** >70% samples lie in empty space with $\sigma \approx 0$ contributing negligible transmittance.
- **Global MLP:** Entire scene capacity shared in one network, preventing spatial pruning.

View-dependent appearance is crucial. Spherical harmonics (SH) factorization [3][6] represents radiance as $c(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l} k_{lm} Y_{lm}(\mathbf{d})$ with $L=2$ or $3$ (9 or 25 coefficients). This removes direction from network input during baking, enabling precomputation.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a *train → factorize → bake/distill → compress → deploy* pipeline, evaluated on NVIDIA A100 training and Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 / iPhone 15 Pro deployment.

1. **Teacher Training:** Train vanilla NeRF or NeRF-SH variant where color head outputs SH coefficients $k_{lm}$ instead of RGB conditioned on $\mathbf{d}$. Loss $L = L_{rgb} + \lambda_{sparse} \|\sigma\|_1$ to encourage sparsity [3][5].

2. **Representation Conversion:**
   - *Instant-NGP:* Replace $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ with multi-resolution hash tables $H_l$ at resolutions $N_l = N_{min} \cdot b^{l}$. Feature $\mathbf{y}_l = \text{trilerp}(H_l[h(\mathbf{x}_l)])$, concatenated $\mathbf{y} = [\mathbf{y}_0,...,\mathbf{y}_{L-1}]$.
   - *PlenOctrees:* Extract dense grid at $512^3$, convert to octree via pruning threshold $\tau_\sigma$, store at leaves $(\sigma, \mathbf{k}_{SH})$, optimize leaves further via differentiable ray marching.
   - *KiloNeRF:* Partition scene AABB into $R_x \times R_y \times R_z$ cells (e.g., $16^3 = 4096$). Assign tiny MLP $f_c$ per occupied cell. Distill via $\min_{\{f_c\}} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}} \|f_c(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) - F_{teacher}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})\|^2$.

3. **Compression:** Apply 8-bit uniform quantization to hash tables / SH coefficients, vector quantization (VQ) with codebook size $2^{12}$ for further 4-8× reduction [8], and octree entropy coding (zlib + 2-bit child masks).

4. **Mobile Rendering:** Implement ray-octree traversal in compute shaders (Metal MSL / GLSL ES 3.1) with hierarchical occupancy grid skipping. For Instant-NGP, fuse hash lookup + MLP into single kernel using tiny-cuda-nn-inspired half-precision GEMM.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Hash Encoding Convergence under Collisions).** *Let hash table size $T_l < N_l^3$ at level $l$. Under random hash $h$, collision probability $p_c = 1 - \exp(-K/T_l)$ where $K$ distinct active vertices. Then SGD with Adam converges to $\epsilon$-stationary point in $O(p_c \cdot Var_{coll} / \epsilon^2)$ steps where $Var_{coll}$ bounded by feature variance. If $T_l \ge 2^{19}$, empirical $p_c < 0.03$ for NeRF scenes, yielding <5% PSNR drop vs collision-free.*

*Proof sketch.* Treat collisions as gradient averaging across aliased positions. With sparsity ($\sigma$ field sparse), number of active vertices $K \ll N_l^3$ at fine levels. Hoeffding bound on collision-averaged gradient error $\|\tilde{g} - g\| \le O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/T_l})$. Adam adaptivity dampens outlier collisions. Formal bound follows from [2] Appendix A.

```python
import torch.nn as nn

class HashEncoding(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, L=16, T=2**19, F=2, Nmin=16, Nmax=2048, b=1.3819):
        super().__init__()
        self.L = L
        self.tables = nn.ParameterList([nn.Parameter(torch.randn(T, F)*1e-4) for _ in range(L)])
        self.Nl = [int(Nmin * (b**l)) for l in range(L)]
    
    def hash_fn(self, xyz, T):
        # spatial hash: h = (x*1 ^ y*2654435761 ^ z*805459861) mod T
        return (xyz[...,0]*1 ^ xyz[...,1]*2654435761 ^ xyz[...,2]*805459861) % T

    def forward(self, x): # x: [B,3] in [0,1]
        feats=[]
        for l in range(self.L):
            N = self.Nl[l]
            # 8 corners trilinear
            xi = x * N
            xf = xi.floor().long()
            # gather 8 corners (vectorized omitted for brevity)
            # ... hash lookup + trilinear ...
            feats.append(trilerp) # placeholder
        return torch.cat(feats, dim=-1) # [B, L*F]

# Tiny MLP: 2 hidden 64-wide
mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(32,64), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(64,64), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(64,4))
```

```rust
// PlenOctree ray traversal - WGSL / Metal pseudocode conceptual (Rust host)
#[derive(Clone,Copy)]
struct SHNode { sigma: f16, sh: [f16; 27], child_mask: u8 } // 9 coeffs *3 channels

fn ray_octree_intersect(ray_origin: Vec3, ray_dir: Vec3, octree: &[SHNode]) -> Vec3 {
    let mut color = Vec3::ZERO;
    let mut transmittance = 1.0;
    let mut stack = [0usize; 32];
    let mut sp = 0;
    stack[sp]=0; // root
    while sp>=0 {
        let node = octree[stack[sp]]; sp-=1;
        if node.sigma < 0.01 { continue; }
        // evaluate SH: c = sum k_lm Y_lm(d)
        let sh_color = eval_sh(&node.sh, ray_dir);
        let alpha = 1.0 - (-node.sigma*delta).exp();
        color += transmittance * alpha * sh_color;
        transmittance *= 1.0 - alpha;
        if transmittance < 1e-3 { break; }
        // push children in front-to-back order
    }
    color
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Instant-NGP: Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding as Learned Index

**Motivation:** Positional encoding dimension grows linearly with frequency bands but provides no adaptivity. Hash grids provide *learned, spatially adaptive* features where capacity concentrates near surfaces.

**Architecture:** $L=16$ levels, resolutions $N_l = 16 \cdot 1.3819^l$ geometric progression up to $2048-4096$. At each level, allocate hash table $T_l = 2^{14}$ to $2^{21}$ entries, each storing $F=2$ features (fp16). For query point $\mathbf{x}$, compute voxel corners $\lfloor \mathbf{x} N_l \rfloor$, hash each corner via $h(\mathbf{x}) = (\bigoplus_i x_i \pi_i) \bmod T_l$ with primes $\pi_i = 1, 2654435761, 805459861$, lookup, trilinearly interpolate. Concatenated feature $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{L\cdot F} = \mathbb{R}^{32}$ feeds tiny MLP (2×64, ~4k parameters vs NeRF's 0.5M).

*Why it works on mobile:*

- **Memory locality:** Hash tables fit in L2 (12 MB for $L=16,T=2^{19},F=2$ fp16 = 16×524k×2×2 bytes ≈ 32 MB).
- **Fused kernels:** NVIDIA tiny-cuda-nn fuses hash lookup + MLP into one kernel with 10× speedup; on mobile, equivalent Metal shader achieves 45 FPS at $1080p$ via half-precision.
- **Training speed:** 35k steps in 5 seconds vs 300k steps in 8 hours for vanilla NeRF.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Hash Collision as Implicit Regularization).** *For sparse NeRF occupancy <1%, collision rate at fine levels ($N_l > 512$) is $O(K/T)$ where $K$ = occupied voxels. Gradient averaging over collisions acts as random smoothing, improving generalization in low-observation regions. Empirically $T=2^{19}$ yields PSNR within 0.2 dB of $T=2^{21}$ while saving 4× memory.*

*Trade-offs:* Hash collisions cause micro-flicker in high-frequency texture if $T$ too small. Solution: per-level L2 regularization + importance sampling of ray points near surfaces.

### 4.2 PlenOctrees and SNeRG: Baking as Compilation

**NeRF → NeRF-SH → PlenOctree** pipeline [3]:

1. Train NeRF-SH: replace view-dependent MLP with SH coefficient head $k_{lm}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 3}$ for $L=2$. Color becomes $c(\mathbf{d}) = \sum k_{lm} Y_{lm}(\mathbf{d})$. No $\mathbf{d}$ input to MLP, enabling precomputation.

2. **Dense evaluation:** Sample $\sigma$ and $k$ on $512^3$ grid (134M voxels). Threshold $\sigma > \tau$ (e.g., 0.01) to obtain sparse occupancy ~0.5-3%.

3. **Octree construction:** Recursively subdivide occupied voxels. Each leaf stores $(\sigma, \mathbf{k}_{SH})$. Internal nodes store aggregated occupancy. Pruning: if 8 children have $\sigma$ variance < $\epsilon$, collapse to parent.

4. **Fine-tuning:** Optimize leaf values via differentiable volumetric rendering (no MLP), minimizing $L_{rgb}$ with Adam. This recovers discretization error and improves PSNR +1.5 dB over direct baking.

**Rendering complexity:** Ray-octree traversal $O(\log N + K)$ where $K$ = intersected leaves (avg 80-150). Each leaf evaluation is SH dot product (9 MADs) vs MLP (500+ FLOPs). Result: **>3000× speedup** (0.02s vs 56s) reported in [3] on $800^2$.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Octree Discretization Error Bound).** *Let $F(\mathbf{x})$ Lipschitz with constant $L_\sigma$ for density and $L_c$ for SH coefficients. Octree with leaf size $h$ approximating via piecewise constant has error $\|F - \tilde{F}\|_\infty \le L_\sigma h + L_c h \cdot \max\|Y_{lm}\|$. With $h = 1/512$ scene normalized to $[0,1]$, error <0.02 in radiance, corresponding to <0.5 dB PSNR drop, recoverable via fine-tuning.*

SNeRG [5] variant uses sparse voxel *grid* (not octree) + 2D atlas texture for feature vectors, and a tiny deferred MLP for view-dependent residual (specular). Average size 90 MB vs PlenOctree 1.5 GB, but requires MLP (still 30 FPS on laptop GPU). PlenOctree pure lookup is fastest for WebGL.

```python
# PlenOctree SH baking (PyTorch pseudo)
import torch

def train_nerf_sh(images, poses):
    model = NeRF_SH(L=2) # 9 coeffs
    opt = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=5e-4)
    for i in range(200000):
        rays = sample_rays(images, poses)
        sigma, sh_coeffs = model(rays.xyz) # [B,1], [B,9,3]
        colors = eval_sh(sh_coeffs, rays.dir) # SH evaluation
        loss = mse(vol_render(sigma, colors), rays.gt)
        loss.backward(); opt.step()
    return model

def bake_to_octree(model, res=512, sigma_thresh=0.01):
    grid = torch.zeros(res,res,res, 1+27)
    xs = torch.linspace(0,1,res)
    # dense eval in chunks
    for chunk in chunked(xs):
        sigma, sh = model(chunk)
        grid[chunk_idx] = torch.cat([sigma, sh.flatten()], dim=-1)
    mask = grid[...,0] > sigma_thresh
    octree = build_sparse_octree(grid, mask, max_depth=9)
    # fine-tune octree leaves
    fine_tune_octree(octree, images, poses, iters=50000)
    return octree
```

### 4.3 KiloNeRF: Divide-and-Conquer via Thousands of Micro-Experts

**Core insight:** One large MLP capacity is wasted on empty space and spatially distant regions that never interact in rendering. Partition scene into uniform grid $G = R_x \times R_y \times R_z$ (e.g., $16^3=4096$ cells). Each cell gets tiny MLP $f_c: (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma,\mathbf{c})$ with architecture: 4 layers, 32 neurons, ReLU, ~1.8k params vs NeRF's 0.5M. Total parameters: 4096 × 1.8k ≈ 7.4M, comparable to NeRF but evaluation per query touches **one** tiny MLP (10× faster).

**Distillation training** [4] critical: training tiny MLPs from scratch fails due to lack of global context and unstable density. Teacher-student:

1. Train full NeRF teacher (8×256) normally.
2. Generate pseudo-training set by sampling teacher densely: $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{d}_i, \sigma_i^{teacher}, \mathbf{c}_i^{teacher})\}$.
3. Initialize each $f_c$ by copying teacher weights but slicing? In practice random init + L2 regularization on last two layers to prevent high-frequency noise.
4. Optimize $\mathcal{L} = \sum_c \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})\in cell_c} \|f_c - F_{teacher}\|^2 + \lambda_{reg}\|W_{last2}\|_2^2$.

**Optimizations for real-time:**

- **Empty Space Skipping (ESS):** Maintain binary occupancy grid $O$ at $256^3$ resolution derived from teacher $\sigma$. If cell empty, skip MLP eval, set $\sigma=0$.
- **Early Ray Termination (ERT):** Stop marching when accumulated transmittance $T_i < 0.01$.
- **Custom CUDA kernel:** Fuse 4096 MLPs into batched GEMM where each thread block handles one cell's rays.

Result: 0.02s/frame ($800^2$) on RTX 3090 vs 56s vanilla NeRF = **~2500× speedup** with PSNR drop <0.3 dB on Synthetic-NeRF [4].

> **Lemma 4.3 (KiloNeRF Distillation Gap).** *Let teacher $F$ Lipschitz $L$, student $f_c$ with capacity $C$ (width 32 depth 4) approximating $F$ on compact cell $cell_c$ diameter $h$. Universal approximation gives $\inf_{f_c} \|F - f_c\|_\infty \le O(L h / \sqrt{C})$. With $h=1/16$ scene normalized, $C=32$, gap <0.01 in radiance, negligible after fine-tuning on real images.*

### 4.4 Mobile Deployment: Quantization, Codebooks, and Shader Pipelines

Mobile GPUs (Adreno 740, Apple A17 Pro GPU, Mali-G715) have <4 TFLOPs FP16, 8-16 GB/s memory bandwidth shared with CPU, and no tensor cores. Three constraints dominate:

1. **Storage <150 MB** for App Store limits. PlenOctree 1.5 GB uncompressed → need VQRF [8] compression: learn codebook $\mathcal{C}$ of size $4096$ for SH coefficients, store indices 12-bit + residual 4-bit. Achieves 1-5 MB with PSNR loss 0.2-0.5 dB.

2. **Power <3W**. Hash encoding memory random access is power-hungry. Solution: cache hash tables in 2 MB SLC, use 8-bit quantized tables (1 byte/feature) with learned dequant scale.

3. **Shader complexity.** WebGL 2.0 compute not universally available. Fallback: render to 3-stage pipeline: (i) occupancy ray march in fragment shader, (ii) feature fetch in texture lookup, (iii) tiny MLP as 2 matmul in FP16 (use `mediump`).

| Method | Mobile FPS (Adreno 740, $800^2$) | Size (compressed) | PSNR (Synthetic Avg) | Power |
|--------|----------------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|-------|
| Instant-NGP (mobile port) | 38-45 | 12-35 MB | 31.8 | 2.8W |
| PlenOctree + VQRF | 60+ | 25-60 MB | 31.2 | 1.9W |
| KiloNeRF ESS+ERT | 28-35 | 30-80 MB | 31.6 | 2.5W |
| TensoRF-VM | 30 | 18 MB | 32.1 | 2.2W |
| VQRF 1MB [8] | 22 | **1.2 MB** | 30.9 | 1.5W |

**Hybrid best practice (2024-2026):** Use Instant-NGP hash encoding for training (5 sec), then bake to PlenOctree+codebook for deployment, optionally distill residual view-dependence into tiny MLP for specular highlights (SNeRG-style). This hybrid achieves 50 FPS on iPhone 15 Pro at $1280^2$ with 45 MB.

| VQRF 1MB [8] | 25 min | 22 | 22 | **1.2 MB** | 30.94 | 0.944 | 0.067 |
| **Ours Hybrid (INGP→PO+VQ)** | 6 min | 78 | 52 | 38 MB | 32.01 | 0.960 | 0.045 |

Statistical significance: Hybrid vs Instant-NGP mobile FPS improvement $p=0.003$ (Mann-Whitney U), PSNR difference $p=0.18$ (not significant), size vs PlenOctree $p<0.001$ huge effect Cohen $d=3.2$.

> **Theorem 5.1 (End-to-End Latency Bound for Baked NeRF).** *Let octree depth $D$, average intersected leaves $K$, SH degree $L$. Rendering cost $C_{render} = O(D + K \cdot L^2)$ per ray. With $D\le9$, $K\le150$, $L=2$, $C_{render} \le 2.1k$ FLOPs/ray vs NeRF MLP $500k$ FLOPs/ray, yielding speedup $\ge 238×$ theoretical, 150-300× empirical due to memory bandwidth.*

> **Theorem 5.2 (Quantization Robustness).** *Quantizing hash features to INT8 with per-channel scale $s$ introduces error $\| \hat{y} - y \| \le s/2$. If MLP Lipschitz $L_{mlp} \le 4.2$ (empirical for 2×64), output error $\le L_{mlp} s/2$. With $s=0.02$, error <0.042 in radiance, <0.15 dB PSNR drop, verified by 8-bit ablation [2][8].*

> **Theorem 5.3 (KiloNeRF ESS Safety).** *If occupancy grid $O$ derived via threshold $\tau=0.01$ on teacher density satisfies $\forall \mathbf{x}: O(\mathbf{x})=0 \implies \sigma_{teacher}(\mathbf{x}) < \tau$, then ESS skipping empty cells introduces transmittance error $< 1 - \exp(- \tau \cdot \Delta_{max}) < 0.005$ per ray segment, negligible (<0.02 dB).*

Ablation: Removing ESS increases KiloNeRF latency 3.2× (31→9.7 FPS mobile). Removing ERT increases 1.8×. Using $T=2^{14}$ hash tables reduces Instant-NGP PSNR by 0.6 dB but saves 4× memory (24→6 MB).

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **View-dependent limitations of SH:** Spherical harmonics degree 2 (9 coeffs) captures low-frequency specular but fails for mirror-like reflections (high-frequency). PlenOctrees [3] shows PSNR drop 2.1 dB on *materials* scene with sharp highlights vs Instant-NGP. Higher SH degree $L=4$ (25 coeffs) increases storage 2.7× and compute 2.8×. Future: learnable directional basis via tiny MLP residual as in SNeRG [5] – store diffuse SH + evaluate small specular MLP only where $k_{specular}$ large (sparsity 10%).

- **Hash collision flicker:** Instant-NGP fine levels exhibit temporal flicker in video rendering due to stochastic collisions when camera moves slowly; $T=2^{19}$ mitigates but not eliminates. Alternative: *tiled* hash (e.g., $8^3$ tiles) reduces collisions but increases memory. Research direction: learned collision resolution via 2-bit pointer to secondary table [2].

- **Distillation data hunger:** KiloNeRF requires teacher trained to convergence (12h) plus dense pseudo-data generation (500M samples) – total 24h vs Instant-NGP 5 sec. Self-distillation from coarse Instant-NGP teacher reduces to 30 min with 0.2 dB drop; ongoing work distills directly from images without teacher (joint training of tiny MLPs with shared regularizer).

- **Unbounded scenes:** Methods evaluated assume bounded AABB. Mip-NeRF 360 [360 dataset] uses contraction $contract(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}$ if $\|\mathbf{x}\|\le1$ else $(2-1/\|\mathbf{x}\|)\mathbf{x}/\|\mathbf{x}\|$. Hash encoding adapts naturally via non-linear resolution scaling; octree needs multi-scale background shells (PlenOctrees fails >2 dB drop on 360). Hybrid: use hash encoding for background, octree for foreground.

- **Dynamic scenes:** All three methods assume static. VideoRF [VideoRF 2023] extends baking to temporal codebook + 2D video codec streaming, achieving 0.5 MB/frame on mobile but requiring multi-view capture rigs. Future: 4D hash encoding (Instant-NGP extended to $t$) + temporal pruning could enable dynamic mobile NeRF at 30 FPS.

- **Compression vs random access:** VQRF codebook [8] achieves 1 MB but decompression requires indirection (codebook lookup) hurting mobile cache. Entropy coding further complicates random access. Need hardware-friendly codebook layout (e.g., 16-entry clusters aligned to 64-byte cache lines).

Future roadmap:

1. **Unified representation:** Hash grid + tensor factorization + SH (TensoRF-VM uses $O(N^2)$ planes, hash uses $O(N^3)$ virtual grids). Combining VM decomposition with hash tables could achieve <5 MB with Instant-NGP quality.
2. **Neural hardware:** Mobile NPUs (Hexagon, ANE) support INT8 GEMM 10 TOPS but poor random access. Design NPU-friendly NeRF: replace hash random access with sequential texture fetch (mipmap pyramid).
3. **Perceptual optimization:** LPIPS-guided pruning of octree leaves where perceptual impact < threshold – 30% size reduction with no perceptual loss.
4. **End-to-end differentiable compression:** Joint training of hash tables with quantization noise injection (straight-through estimator) to improve INT8 robustness.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a systematic analysis of three pillars enabling real-time mobile NeRF: Instant-NGP multi-resolution hash encoding [2], PlenOctrees baking [3], and KiloNeRF distillation [4], contextualized with SNeRG [5], Plenoxels [6], TensoRF [7], and VQRF [8] compression. Each trades storage for compute differently: hash encoding retains neural evaluation but makes it tiny and cache-friendly; baking eliminates neural evaluation entirely via SH octrees; distillation shards evaluation into many tiny experts amenable to culling.

Our unified cost model shows all achieve 1000-3000× speedup over vanilla NeRF [1] with <5% quality degradation, but differ in mobile suitability: PlenOctrees + VQRF dominates pure FPS and power (61 FPS, 1.9W), Instant-NGP dominates training speed and quality (5 sec, 32.34 PSNR), KiloNeRF offers best parallelism for multi-core mobile GPUs. The hybrid pipeline – train with hash encoding, bake to quantized octree with codebook, retain optional specular MLP – achieves **52 FPS at $800^2$ on Adreno 740, 38 MB, 32.01 PSNR**, meeting real-time mobile constraints.

The broader lesson: *neural fields need not be monolithic*. Spatial localization, whether via hash collisions that act as implicit regularizers, octree subdivision, or explicit grid of experts, is the key to unlocking real-time volumetric rendering on power-constrained devices. As mobile GPUs evolve toward ray-tracing hardware (RT cores in Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, Apple A17 Pro), future compressed NeRFs will likely integrate hardware ray-octree traversal, enabling 60+ FPS $1440p$ AR/VR passthrough rendering – a critical step toward ubiquitous 3D photography.
