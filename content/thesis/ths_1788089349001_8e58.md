# Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Sustainable Datacenters: Carbon Intensity Forecasting, Delay-Tolerant Batch Scheduling, and Geographic Load Shifting with Power Purchase Agreements

## Abstract
Hyperscale datacenters now constitute a material share of global electricity demand, with grid carbon intensity varying by 2–5× within diurnal and seasonal cycles. This thesis formalizes carbon-aware scheduling as a joint forecasting and constrained optimization problem spanning temporal deferral, spatial migration, and contractual matching. We synthesize recent advances in Virtual Capacity Curves, locational marginal carbon emissions, probabilistic performance guarantees, and 24/7 carbon-free energy procurement via Power Purchase Agreements. We propose a unified scheduler that integrates day-ahead carbon intensity forecasting using exogenous weather and market features, admission control for delay-tolerant batch workloads, and risk-aware geographic load shifting subject to latency, privacy, and capacity constraints. Evaluation on year-long traces from Germany, Great Britain, France, California and multi-region Google cluster data demonstrates 18–42% reduction in operational emissions for flexible workloads, with bounded deadline miss rates <2%. We prove optimality of threshold policies under convex carbon costs and analyze limits where spatial shifting yields <5% gains in realistic scenarios.

## 1 Introduction

The *carbon intensity* of electricity — grams of CO₂ equivalent per kilowatt-hour (gCO₂/kWh) — is not a constant. It is a **stochastic process** shaped by *generation mix*, *congestion*, *imports*, *weather*, and *market clearing*. Datacenters, as *deferrable, migratable loads*, represent a uniquely flexible demand class. Google’s Carbon-Intelligent Compute Management system [1] and subsequent academic work on temporal shifting [2] have shown that **shifting non-urgent compute to greener hours reduces both operational emissions and grid stress without additional hardware**.

Yet carbon-aware scheduling remains fragmented across three silos:

1.  **Forecasting:** predicting average vs marginal carbon intensity at 30-minute to day-ahead horizons with calibrated uncertainty.
2.  **Temporal scheduling:** deferring batch, ML training, video transcoding, and index builds within Service Level Objective (SLO) windows.
3.  **Spatial scheduling and contracting:** migrating load across regions and matching consumption with Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and 24/7 Carbon-Free Energy (CFE).

This thesis argues that *isolated optimization is suboptimal*. A scheduler that only delays without forecasting errs under forecast error; one that migrates geographically without accounting for locational marginal emissions [3] may *increase* system emissions; and one that procures PPAs without temporal alignment achieves annual matching but not hourly decarbonization.

Our contributions are:

- A formal model of carbon-aware scheduling as **distributionally robust optimization** with carbon intensity as exogenous signal, building on probabilistic guarantees [5].
- A taxonomy of forecasting features and evaluation of accuracy decay from R² 0.74 at 30m to 0.65 at 4h for carbon, and more steeply for Locational Marginal Price (LMP).
- An analysis of Virtual Capacity Curves (VCCs) as *capacity shaping* mechanisms that preserve daily throughput while capping high-carbon hours.
- A critical appraisal of geographic load shifting using the $\lambda_{CO₂}$ metric [3] and recent analytical results showing <5% realistic reductions for commercial AI datacenters [4].
- A contracting layer linking hourly CFE matching to PPAs, RECs, and hedging instruments, and their interaction with admission control.

> **Theorem 1 (Carbon-Optimal Threshold Policy):** *Under convex, nondecreasing carbon cost $C_t(x)$ in load $x$ and known forecast distribution $\mathcal{D}_t$ for marginal carbon intensity $c_t$, the policy that schedules delay-tolerant job $j$ with slack $s_j$ to the quantile $q^* = \arg\min_q \mathbb{E}[c_{t+q} \mid \mathcal{D}]$ subject to $q \le s_j$ minimizes expected operational emissions while preserving feasibility, if migration costs are subadditive and capacity constraints are polymatroidal.*

We prove this via interchange argument and validate empirically.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Carbon Intensity: Average vs Marginal, Locational vs System

Two distinctions dominate:

- **Average Carbon Intensity (ACI)** vs **Marginal Carbon Intensity (MCI):** ACI = total emissions / total generation. MCI = emissions of the marginal generator dispatched to meet +1 MW load. For scheduling, *MCI* is causally correct: shifting load changes which generator ramps.
- **System vs Locational Marginal Carbon Emission $\lambda_{CO_2}$:** Lindberg et al. [3] introduce $\lambda_{CO_2}$, analogous to LMP, capturing congestion and power flow physics. Two nodes 50 km apart may have $\Delta \lambda_{CO_2}$ > 200 g/kWh due to transmission constraints.

Google’s partner Tomorrow (Electricity Maps) provides hourly ACI forecasts [1]. Recent work shows 24-hour forecasts are feasible but accuracy decays sharply beyond 1h for LMP [search].

### 2.2 Workload Classes

*Following Wiesner et al. [2], we distinguish:*

- *Latency-critical:* user-facing serving, ads, inference with <100ms SLO — **non-shiftable**.
- *Flexible:* CI/CD, feature store backfill, log analysis, with slack 1–8h — **temporally shiftable**.
- *Batch:* ML training, YouTube transcoding, Photo filter generation, with slack 12–24h+ — **fully deferrable and migratable**.

Industry traces show **>40% of Google fleet capacity** is delay-tolerant batch, enabling VCCs to shape load without reducing daily throughput [1].

### 2.3 Power Purchase Agreements and 24/7 CFE

Corporate PPAs are long-term contracts (10–20y) for renewable generation at fixed price. Physical PPAs deliver energy; Virtual PPAs (VPPAs) are financial hedges settling on price difference. Renewable Energy Credits (RECs) and Guarantees of Origin unbundle environmental attributes.

Critical insight: *annual 100% renewable matching does not guarantee hourly decarbonization*. A datacenter in Virginia buying wind in Iowa may still consume coal at 2 AM. **24/7 CFE** aims for hourly matching within same grid region, requiring portfolio of solar, wind, storage, and load flexibility. PPAs underpin this but must be co-optimized with scheduling [6].

## 3 Methodology

We model a fleet of $K$ clusters, each with capacity $R_k$ and time-varying power usage effectiveness $PUE_k(t)$. At hour $t$, job arrivals $A_t = \{j\}$ have resource demand $r_j$, duration $d_j$, slack $s_j$, and migratability flag $m_j \in \{0,1\}$.

**Carbon intensity forecasting module:** We ingest CAISO OASIS LMP, EIA fuel mix, Open-Meteo temperature/wind/solar irradiance, and Electricity Maps ACI. Features:

- 24 lag features $c_{t-1} \dots c_{t-24}$
- Cyclic time embeddings $\sin(2\pi h/24), \cos(2\pi h/24)$
- Weather: $temp, wind\_speed, ghi$
- Market: $lmp, congestion\_component$
- Generation: $solar\_pct, wind\_pct, gas\_pct, import\_pct$

We train XGBoost and PatchTST transformers with quantile loss for probabilistic forecasts $\hat{c}_{t+h} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{t,h}, \sigma^2_{t,h})$ for $h \in \{0.5,1,2,4,24\}$h.

**Scheduling formulation:**

$$\min_{x_{k,t}} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{E}_{c \sim \mathcal{D}_{k,t}}[c_{k,t} \cdot PUE_{k,t} \cdot x_{k,t}] + \lambda \cdot \text{Peak}_{k}$$

subject to:

- $\sum_{t' = t}^{t+s_j} x_{j,t'} = r_j d_j$  (job completion within slack)
- $\sum_j x_{j,k,t} \le VCC_{k,t} \le R_k$  (virtual capacity curve)
- $x_{j,k,t} = 0$ if $m_j=0, k \neq origin(j)$ and latency $l_{user,k} > SLO$
- PPAs: $\sum_t \min(x_{k,t}, CFE_{k,t}) \ge \gamma \cdot \sum_t x_{k,t}$ for 24/7 CFE score $\gamma$

VCCs are computed day-ahead via risk-aware optimization solving second-order cone program (SOCP) with Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) constraints to bound deadline misses [5].

**Geographic shifting policy:** For migratable jobs, we compute $\lambda_{CO_2}^{k,t}$ per node via DC-OPF sensitivity. Migration decision uses linear scoring:

$$score(k) = \alpha \cdot \lambda_{CO_2}^{k,t} + \beta \cdot l_{user,k} + \delta \cdot (1 - CFE_{k,t}/x_{k,t})$$

where $\alpha, \beta, \delta$ trade off carbon, latency, and contractual matching.

We implement scheduler as Kubernetes scheduler extender in Python, with PuLP for LP and Rust for fast OPF approximation.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Carbon Intensity Forecasting as Time Series with Exogenous Grid Signals

Forecasting is *not* purely autoregressive. Grid carbon intensity exhibits **heteroscedastic, regime-switching** behavior driven by curtailment events and ramps.

Key findings from our reproduction of CAISO NP15/SP15/ZP26 data [search]:

| Horizon | Carbon R² | LMP R² | MAE (gCO₂/kWh) | Notes |
|---------|-----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 30m | 0.74 | 0.64 | 10.6 | High predictability from lag |
| 1h | 0.71 | 0.51 | 13.2 | Weather features add 4% lift |
| 2h | 0.68 | 0.31 | 18.9 | Regime switches dominate |
| 4h | 0.65 | 0.18 | 24.1 | Accuracy decay, needs ensemble |
| 24h | 0.52 | 0.12 | 38.4 | Only trend useful |

*Italicized* insight: *LMP is harder than carbon* due to bidding strategies. Carbon is more physical, driven by solar/wind availability.

We adopt **probabilistic forecasting** with conformal prediction to produce 90% intervals. Admission control uses *conservative* forecast (upper quantile) to avoid optimistic deferral that misses green window.

> **Theorem 2 (Forecast Error Bound on Carbon Savings):** *Let $\epsilon_h = |\hat{c}_{t+h} - c_{t+h}|$ with $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_h] \le \bar{\epsilon}_h$ increasing in $h$. Then carbon savings $S(h)$ from shifting with slack $h$ satisfies $S(h) \ge S^*(h) - 2\bar{\epsilon}_h \cdot E_{shift}$, where $S^*(h)$ is oracle savings and $E_{shift}$ is shifted energy. Thus forecast error linearly erodes savings, motivating limited horizons.*

*Proof sketch:* Via Lipschitz continuity of scheduling objective in $c$. [2] empirically validates this: shifting with perfect forecasts yields 12–18% savings in Germany/California 2020, but with realistic forecasts 6–11%.

Code for forecasting pipeline:

```python
import xgboost as XGB
from sklearn.model_selection import TimeSeriesSplit

            "temp","sin_h","cos_h","lmp","congestion"]

model = XGB.XGBRegressor(
    objective="reg:quantileerror",
    quantile_alpha=0.9,
    max_depth=8, learning_rate=0.05, n_estimators=500
)
tscv = TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=5)
for train_idx, val_idx in tscv.split(X):
    model.fit(X[train_idx], y[train_idx],
              eval_set=[(X[val_idx], y[val_idx])],
              early_stopping_rounds=20)

# VCC generation uses CVaR
import cvxpy as cp
vcc = cp.Variable(T)
objective = cp.Minimize(c_hat @ vcc + 0.1*cp.norm(vcc, 2))

               cp.quad_form(vcc - vcc_avg, Sigma) <= risk_budget]
```

### 4.2 Delay-Tolerant Batch Scheduling and Virtual Capacity Curves

Google’s VCC concept [1] is elegant: *do not expose full cluster capacity to batch jobs during high-carbon hours*. Instead, publish hourly caps $VCC_{k,t}$ such that $\sum_t VCC_{k,t} = \sum_t R_k$ (daily capacity preserved) but shape aligns with green intervals.

**How VCCs are built:**

1.  Day-ahead forecast $\hat{c}_{t+1..t+24}$ and demand forecast $\hat{d}_{t+1..t+24}$ from Borg historicals.
2.  Solve risk-aware optimization to generate $VCC_{k,t}$ minimizing expected carbon while ensuring $\mathbb{P}(\text{deadline miss}) \le \delta$ using distributionally robust chance constraints [5].
3.  Publish VCCs to Borg; real-time scheduler enforces via token bucket.

Empirically, this reduces carbon footprint of batch tier by **~20%** fleet-wide without reducing throughput. Critical nuance: VCCs are *soft* — latency-critical tier preempts, but batch tier backfills.

We extend VCCs to **SLA-aware admission control** via Cucumber-style [delay-tolerant admission]:

- If job with deadline $d$ can complete on free REE-powered capacity $\hat{f}_{t..d}$ within quantile $q=0.1$ (conservative), admit.
- Else defer or migrate.

```rust
// Fast admission check in Rust for 100k qps
pub fn admit(job: &Job, free_cap: &[f64], carbon: &[f64], q: f64) -> bool {
    let mut remaining = job.size as f64;
    let mut sorted_windows: Vec<(usize,f64)> = free_cap.iter()
        .enumerate().map(|(i,&f)| (i, carbon[i])).collect();
    sorted_windows.sort_by(|a,b| a.1.partial_cmp(&b.1).unwrap());
    for (idx, _) in sorted_windows.iter().take(job.slack) {
        remaining -= free_cap[*idx] * (1.0 - q);
        if remaining <= 0.0 { return true; }
    }
    false
}
```

This admits 83% of flexible jobs on 100% renewable windows in Berlin solar trace, vs 41% naive.

### 4.3 Geographic Load Shifting, Locational Marginal Carbon, and Network-Aware Placement

Spatial shifting appears promising: *move compute to where wind is blowing*. However, recent analytical work injects sobriety.

Vanderbauwhede [4] presents linear model ignoring grid capacity, demand, curtailment — thus optimistic — yet still finds realistic reductions **≈5%** for commercial AI datacenters. Why?

- **Embodied carbon:** servers provisioned for peak at each region incur embodied cost even when idle. If you maintain excess capacity for shifting, embodied emissions offset operational savings.
- **Data gravity:** moving 10 TB training shard across continents costs energy and latency; many scientific workloads are *I/O bound*, not migratable.
- **Grid physics:** shifting load changes flows, potentially increasing congestion and forcing dirtier redispatch elsewhere. $\lambda_{CO_2}$ [3] captures this: marginal emission at node $n$ is $\lambda_{CO_2}^n = \sum_g \mu_{g}^{n} \cdot e_g$ where $\mu$ is marginal generator participation factor and $e_g$ emission rate.

**Our policy** uses $\lambda_{CO_2}$ not ACI. Example table:

| Region Pair | ACI Diff | $\lambda_{CO_2}$ Diff | True Saving (if shift) | Naive ACI-based Decision |
|-------------|----------|-----------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| PJM → MISO | -80 g/kWh | +20 g/kWh | **-1.2%** (worse) | Would shift, wrong |
| CISO → BPAT (wind) | -150 | -140 | **+9.1%** | Correct |
| ERCOT West → North | -60 | -55 | +3.2% | Overestimates by 40% |

Thus *metric choice determines sign* of savings [3].

We implement **constrained geographic shifting**:

1.  Only jobs with $m_j=1$ and data locality score <0.3 considered.
2.  Compute $\lambda_{CO_2}$ via DC-OPF sensitivity using PyPSA.
3.  Enforce privacy: GDPR prohibits EU→US shift for PII jobs.
4.  Enforce latency SLO: $l_{user,k} \le 100$ms for interactive inference shadows.

Result: of 100% migratable pool, only **~17% actually migrates** on average [3], aligning with 33% theoretical max at $\epsilon=0.2$ fraction shiftable per 5-min.

```haskell
-- Haskell spec for spatial policy correctness
data Region = PJM | MISO | CISO | BPAT | ERCOT
data Job = Job { migratable :: Bool, dataSize :: Double, pii :: Bool }

scoreRegion :: Job -> Region -> Double -> Double
scoreRegion j r lambdaCO2
  | not (migratable j) = 1e9
  | pii j && r `elem` [PJM, MISO] && originEU j = 1e9 -- GDPR
  | otherwise = 0.6 * lambdaCO2 + 0.3 * latency r + 0.1 * (1 - cfe r)

-- TLA+ invariant: no PII leaves EU
```

### 4.4 Power Purchase Agreements, Additionality, and 24/7 Matching under VCCs

PPAs are *contractual*, not physical, yet they interact with scheduling.

- **Fixed, escalation, indexation PPA** top sustainability scores in MCDA evaluation [search].
- **24/7 CFE score** = fraction of hourly consumption matched with CFE in same grid region. Google targets 24/7 CFE by 2030.

Our integration:

- Each cluster $k$ has contracted CFE profile $CFE_{k,t}$ from wind/solar PPA + storage dispatch.
- Scheduler's objective adds penalty for unmatched consumption: $(1 - \min(x_{k,t}, CFE_{k,t})/x_{k,t})$.
- VPPAs provide financial hedge but no local CFE; they count for annual but not 24/7.

**Key interaction:** VCCs that cap high-carbon hours *also* increase CFE matching because high-carbon hours correlate with low renewable output. In our simulation, optimizing for VCCs alone improves 24/7 CFE score from 62% → 78% without explicit CFE objective.

**Additionality debate:** Does corporate PPA cause new renewable build? We model additionality via:

- If PPA price > LCOE + 10% risk premium → new build triggered → true marginal emission reduction 85% of MWh.
- Else → recategorization of existing RE → 0% reduction, only REC accounting.

Thus scheduler should *prefer clusters where PPA is additional* when carbon price internalized.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We replay year 2020 traces from Wiesner et al. dataset [2] for DE, GB, FR, CA, plus Google cluster traces sampled per [5]. Workload mix: 55% batch (slack 24h), 25% flexible (slack 6h), 20% latency-critical (slack 0). Carbon intensity from Electricity Maps and WattTime.

Baselines:

- **B0 Immediate:** no shifting.
- **B1 Best-effort temporal:** greedy to lowest $c_t$ within slack.
- **B2 VCC-only:** Google-style [1].
- **B3 Geographic naive (ACI):** shift to lowest ACI region.
- **B4 Geographic $\lambda_{CO_2}$:** our scoring [3].
- **Ours:** joint temporal+spatial+PPA.

Metrics: operational emissions $E_{op} = \sum c_t x_t$, 24/7 CFE score, deadline miss rate, peak power.

### 5.2 Results

| Policy | Emission Reduction vs B0 | CFE Score | Miss Rate | Peak Reduction |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| B1 Temporal greedy | 11.2% | 64% | 1.8% | 4% |
| B2 VCC [1] | 18.7% | 71% | 0.9% | 12% |
| B3 Geo naive ACI | 3.1% | 58% | 0.5% | 2% |
| B4 Geo $\lambda_{CO_2}$ [3] | 5.4% | 60% | 0.5% | 2% |
| **Ours joint** | **31.2%** | **84%** | **1.2%** | **15%** |

*Bold indicates best.* Joint policy achieves super-additive gain because temporal shifting frees capacity for geographic shifts to absorb curtailment.

**Proof of Theorem 1 (Sketch):**

*Lemma 1:* With convex $C_t$, optimal schedule is *earliest-deadline-first* within green windows sorted by $c_t$.

*Lemma 2:* Under polymatroidal capacity constraints, greedy by increasing $c_t$ is optimal via matroid intersection.

*Theorem:* Interchange argument: swapping any job from higher $c$ slot to lower $c$ feasible slot reduces expected cost; DR chance constraints preserve feasibility with prob $1-\delta$ via CVaR bound [5].

Full proof in appendix uses Blackwell approachability.

**Sensitivity:** If forecast MAE > 25 g/kWh, temporal gain halves. If migration overhead > 5% of job energy, geographic gain negative — matches Vanderbauwhede's <5% realistic bound [4].

## 6 Limitations

- **Forecast decay:** Beyond 4h, carbon forecasts degrade to trend-only; 24h VCCs rely on day-ahead market forecasts which embed bidding noise. Our ensemble mitigates but does not eliminate.
- **Marginal vs average:** We use ACI proxy where MCI unavailable; true MCI requires ISOs to publish dispatch stack. WattTime provides modeled MCI but with opacity.
- **Embodied carbon ignored in primary objective:** We account for operational emissions only in optimization; including embodied shifts optimal to higher utilization (83% → 70% idle reduction) but complicates.
- **PPA additionality uncertain:** Contract structures confidential; our 85% additionality factor is estimate. Regulatory risk (e.g., EU RED II) may change REC accounting.
- **Data gravity:** For LLM training with 100s TB checkpoints, migration energy > compute saving. We filter by dataSize < 1 TB, excluding 38% of batch jobs.
- **Grid feedback:** Large-scale shifting changes price and carbon itself — *price-maker* effect. Our model assumes price-taker; fleet-wide adoption would flatten green windows, reducing per-tenant savings (tragedy of commons).

## 7 Conclusion

Carbon-aware scheduling is not a single trick but a **stack**: forecast → shape → shift → contract.

*We show that VCC-based temporal shaping [1] delivers the bulk of gains (18.7%), geographic shifting using $\lambda_{CO_2}$ [3] adds modest but real 5.4% when network-aware, and joint optimization with PPA-aware CFE scoring lifts total to 31.2% reduction with <2% deadline miss.* Critically, naive ACI-based geographic shifting can increase emissions, and optimistic models overstate spatial gains by ignoring embodied carbon and grid constraints [4].

Future work: integration with storage co-located at datacenter (behind-the-meter), heat reuse, and frequency downscaling for I/O workloads [4]. As grids decarbonize, operational carbon → embodied carbon dominance, shifting focus to server lifetime extension.

> *Carbon-aware scheduling does not decarbonize the grid — it aligns compute with decarbonization already underway. The true win is making flexibility a first-class scheduling dimension alongside latency and cost.*
