# Conditional Flow-Based Neural Video Compression: Scale-Space Motion Compensation, Entropy-Coded Latent Grids, and Rate–Distortion Optimization Beyond VVC

## Abstract

Learned video compression has shifted from predictive coding — encode motion, then the pixel-domain residual — to conditional coding, in which the current frame is encoded directly against a learned, high-dimensional temporal condition. This thesis unifies the conditional flow-based family exemplified by Deep Contextual Video Compression (DCVC). We formalize conditional coding as variational inference over entropy-coded latent grids and prove it dominates residual coding whenever the learned context is a sufficient statistic for temporal redundancy. We dissect its two pillars: scale-space flow warping, which augments optical flow with a per-pixel uncertainty scale so disocclusions collapse into a blurred reference volume rather than an expensive residual, and hierarchical hyperprior entropy models that sharpen the probability mass of quantized latents with spatial and temporal conditioning. Rate–distortion training is framed as Lagrangian optimization with variable-rate and hierarchical quality control. We survey benchmark evidence — 26% bitrate savings over x265, 26.6% BD-rate reduction over HEVC reference software, and bi-directional designs surpassing H.266/VVC on selected corpora — and close with honest limitations on complexity, reference drift, and standardization.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 The predictive coding orthodoxy

For three decades, hybrid video coding has meant one thing: *predict, then correct*. A block-based motion estimator computes displacement vectors against a reference frame; the displaced (motion-compensated) prediction is subtracted from the current frame; and the residual — along with the motion vectors — is transform-coded, quantized, and entropy-coded. This architecture, refined through H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, and H.266/VVC [6], achieves roughly a 50% bitrate reduction per generation at the cost of ever more elaborate hand-engineered toolboxes: quadtree-plus-multi-type-tree partitioning, affine and decoder-side motion vector refinement, multiple transform kernels, and context-adaptive binary arithmetic coding (CABAC).

The *residual* is the conceptual weak point. Subtraction is a fixed, linear, pixel-domain operation that discards precisely the information most useful for reconstruction: the rich, non-linear structure of the temporal relationship between frames. High-frequency textures that survive motion compensation poorly — foliage, water, film grain — dominate residual energy, and the codec pays bits to reconstruct them from near-scratch every frame.

### 1.2 From prediction to conditioning

The first end-to-end learned codecs, notably DVC [4], kept the predictive skeleton but replaced each module with a neural network: a learned optical-flow estimator, bilinear warping for motion compensation, and autoencoder-style networks for motion and residual compression, all jointly optimized under a single rate–distortion loss. DVC matched H.264 and approached H.265 in MS-SSIM — an important proof of concept, but still a neural *implementation* of the classical architecture rather than a rethinking of it.

DCVC [1] proposed the rethink. Instead of generating a predicted frame $\bar{x}_t$ and encoding the residual $r_t = x_t - \bar{x}_t$, the network *generates a context* $\bar{c}_t = f_{\text{ctx}}(\hat{x}_{t-1})$ — a high-dimensional feature-domain representation of temporal information — and encodes the current frame *conditioned* on it:

$$
\hat{x}_t = f_{\text{dec}}\!\left(f_{\text{enc}}(x_t \mid \bar{c}_t) \mid \bar{c}_t\right).
$$

Because the context lives in a learned feature space with hundreds of channels, different channels are free to specialize: some carry low-frequency motion structure, others carry high-frequency texture detail, others carry color priors [1]. The codec no longer decides what temporal information matters through a fixed subtraction; it *learns* the sufficient statistic for the current frame given the past.

### 1.3 Scope and contributions

This thesis:

1. **Formalizes conditional coding** as variational inference and proves its rate advantage over residual coding under mild sufficiency conditions (Section 3, Theorem 1).
2. **Analyzes scale-space flow** [3] as the motion-compensation operator of choice: a generalized warping over a Gaussian scale-space volume that internalizes uncertainty and disocclusion handling (Section 4.1).
3. **Dissects entropy-coded latent grids**, including hyperprior hierarchies, temporal conditional entropy models, and the quantization-relaxation machinery that makes end-to-end rate estimation differentiable (Section 4.2–4.3).
4. **Treats rate–distortion optimization** as Lagrangian training with variable-rate control, and surveys benchmark evidence that conditional codecs now meet or exceed VVC in specific configurations [2][5] (Sections 4.4, 5).
5. **Documents limitations** honestly: complexity, propagation of learned-reference drift, and dataset dependence (Section 6).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Rate–distortion theory and learned compression

Shannon's rate–distortion theory frames lossy compression as the problem of minimizing expected distortion $D$ subject to a rate constraint $R$, or equivalently minimizing the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = R + \lambda D$ for a trade-off parameter $\lambda$. Learned image compression reframes this as variational autoencoder training: an encoder maps $x$ to a latent $y$, quantization $\hat{y} = \lfloor y \rceil$ induces a discrete distribution, and a learned entropy model $p(\hat{y})$ provides both the differentiable rate estimate $R = \mathbb{E}[-\log_2 p(\hat{y})]$ and the actual arithmetic-coding probabilities at test time. The hyperprior extension [4] introduces a second latent $z$ capturing spatial dependencies in $y$, so that $p(\hat{y}\mid\hat{z})$ is far sharper than any factorized prior.

### 2.2 Optical flow and warping in DVC

DVC [4] established the learned hybrid pipeline: a flow network (inspired by PWC-Net-style coarse-to-fine estimation) predicts dense motion $v_t$ between $x_t$ and $\hat{x}_{t-1}$; bilinear warping produces the prediction $\bar{x}_t = \text{warp}(\hat{x}_{t-1}, v_t)$; a motion autoencoder compresses $v_t$; a residual autoencoder compresses $r_t = x_t - \bar{x}_t$. All components are trained jointly with

$$
\mathcal{L} = R_{\text{motion}} + R_{\text{residual}} + \lambda \cdot d(x_t, \hat{x}_t),
$$

where $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ is MSE or MS-SSIM. The one-to-one mapping between classical modules and networks made DVC an invaluable research bridge, and its open implementation seeded a generation of follow-ups [5].

### 2.3 The DCVC family tree

The conditional paradigm spawned a rapid lineage, now unified under open implementations [5]:

- **DCVC** (NeurIPS 2021) [1]: feature-domain context, 26.0% bitrate saving over x265 veryslow on 1080p test video.
- **DCVC-TCM** (Temporal Context Mining): multi-scale temporal contexts mined from propagated reference features, richer conditioning at multiple resolutions.
- **DCVC-HEM** (Hybrid Entropy Model): dual spatial priors plus a latent prior from the temporal context, sharpening $p(\hat{y}_t \mid \bar{c}_t)$.
- **DCVC-DC** (Diverse Contexts): hierarchical quality training and offset-diversity for long-GOP robustness.
- **DCVC-B** [2]: bi-directional extension with motion-difference context propagation and hierarchical quality structures, reporting 26.6% BD-rate reduction over HEVC reference software (random access) and surpassing VVC reference software on some datasets.

### 2.4 VVC: the classical ceiling

H.266/VVC, finalized by ITU-T in August 2020, remains the reference classical system: 128×128 CTUs, QT+MTT partitioning, affine motion, 67 intra modes, LFNST, and roughly 40–50% bitrate savings over HEVC depending on configuration [6]. Its encoder complexity (4–10× HEVC in VTM) is the price of hand-optimized RD search. Any learned codec claiming relevance must be measured against VTM under JVET common test conditions — the bar DCVC-B has begun to clear [2].

## 3 Methodology: Conditional Coding as Variational Inference

### 3.1 Problem formulation

Consider a video as a sequence $\{x_t\}_{t=1}^T$, $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$. A low-delay (P-frame) codec maintains a decoded reference $\hat{x}_{t-1}$ and must produce a bitstream for $x_t$ from which the decoder reconstructs $\hat{x}_t$. In the conditional framework [1], the encoder and decoder share a *context generator* $f_{\text{ctx}}$:

$$
\bar{c}_t = f_{\text{ctx}}(\hat{x}_{t-1}; \theta_c), \qquad \bar{c}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H' \times W' \times C},\; C \gg 3.
$$

The contextual encoder $g_a$ maps $(x_t, \bar{c}_t)$ to a latent grid $y_t$; quantization yields $\hat{y}_t$; the contextual decoder $g_s$ maps $(\hat{y}_t, \bar{c}_t)$ to $\hat{x}_t$. Crucially, both $g_a$ and $g_s$ receive $\bar{c}_t$, so the latent needs only to encode information *not already present* in the context — the conditional entropy $H(x_t \mid \bar{c}_t)$ rather than the residual entropy.

### 3.2 The rate advantage: a theorem

> **Theorem 1 (Conditional dominance).** *Let $r_t = x_t - \bar{x}_t$ be the pixel-domain residual of any fixed motion-compensated prediction $\bar{x}_t$ derived from $\hat{x}_{t-1}$, and let $\bar{c}_t$ be a context from which $\bar{x}_t$ is computable. Then there exists a conditional encoder achieving rate $R_{\text{cond}} \le R_{\text{res}} + \epsilon$ for the same distortion, where $R_{\text{res}}$ is the optimal residual-coding rate and $\epsilon$ accounts for finite network capacity. Moreover, the inequality is strict whenever $I(x_t; \bar{c}_t \mid r_t) > 0$ — i.e., whenever the context carries temporal information destroyed by subtraction.*

*Sketch.* The residual $r_t$ is a deterministic, many-to-one function of $(x_t, \bar{x}_t)$: subtraction collapses distinct $(x_t, \bar{x}_t)$ pairs with identical differences. Any information about $x_t$ recoverable from $\bar{c}_t$ but not from $r_t$ alone must be re-encoded in the residual bitstream. Conditioning on $\bar{c}_t$ at both ends removes this redundancy; the achievable rate lower bound drops from $H(r_t)$ to $H(x_t \mid \bar{c}_t) \le H(r_t)$, with strictness following from the data-processing inequality whenever the context is strictly more informative than the residual about $x_t$. ∎

In practice, DCVC's visualizations confirm the mechanism: individual context channels specialize in high-frequency content, color, and motion boundaries, directly reducing reconstruction error in texture-rich regions where residual coders struggle [1].

### 3.3 Training objective

The full P-frame training loss couples motion and content paths:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{R(\hat{m}_t) + R(\hat{z}^m_t)}_{\text{motion}} + \underbrace{R(\hat{y}_t \mid \bar{c}_t) + R(\hat{z}_t)}_{\text{content}} + \lambda \cdot d(x_t, \hat{x}_t),
$$

where $m_t$ is the transmitted motion representation (flow or scale-space flow), $z$ terms are hyperpriors, and rate terms are cross-entropies under the learned entropy models. Training proceeds in stages — motion warm-up, then joint end-to-end optimization — to avoid the degenerate solution in which the content path ignores the context [1][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Scale-space flow: warping with built-in uncertainty

Classical bilinear warping, $\bar{x}_t(p) = \hat{x}_{t-1}(p + v_t(p))$, fails catastrophically at disocclusions and fast motion: the flow is undefined where content appears from nowhere, and sub-pixel sampling aliases high frequencies. Scale-space flow [3] generalizes the displacement to a triple $g_t(p) = (v^x_t(p), v^y_t(p), \sigma_t(p))$, where $\sigma_t$ is a *scale* (blur) parameter. The reference frame is first expanded into a Gaussian scale-space volume

$$
V_{\hat{x}_{t-1}} = \big\{\, G_{\sigma_k} * \hat{x}_{t-1} \,\big\}_{k=0}^{K}, \qquad \sigma_k^2 \in \{0, \sigma_0^2, (2\sigma_0)^2, \dots\},
$$

and warping becomes trilinear sampling in $(x, y, \sigma)$-space:

$$
\bar{x}_t(p) = \text{trilerp}\!\left(V_{\hat{x}_{t-1}};\; p + v_t(p),\; \sigma_t(p)\right).
$$

Where the network is uncertain — occlusions, motion blur, sub-pixel chaos — it raises $\sigma_t(p)$, *blurring away* the unreliable high frequencies instead of aliasing them, and the content path spends bits only where the blurred prediction is insufficient. This single mechanism eliminates the pre-trained flow networks, sequential sub-network training, and adaptive rate control that burdened earlier methods [3], while an efficient Gaussian-pyramid construction keeps large-$\sigma$ kernels tractable [5]. In the DCVC lineage, scale-space flow is the natural motion representation to condition the temporal context miner on: the propagated reference feature is warped with uncertainty-awareness before context extraction, so contexts degrade gracefully rather than hallucinating.

```python
def scale_space_warp(ref, flow_xy, scale, sigma_levels):
    # ref: (B, C, H, W) reconstructed reference frame
    # flow_xy: (B, 2, H, W) displacement field
    # scale: (B, 1, H, W) per-pixel blur index in [0, K]
    volume = gaussian_scale_space(ref, sigma_levels)  # (B, C, K+1, H, W)
    grid = make_grid(flow_xy, scale)                  # (B, H, W, 3)
    return F.grid_sample(volume, grid, align_corners=False)  # trilinear
```

### 4.2 Entropy-coded latent grids and the hyperprior hierarchy

The content latent $\hat{y}_t \in \mathbb{Z}^{H/16 \times W/16 \times M}$ is the compressible object; everything else serves to make its distribution *predictable*. The hyperprior architecture [4] introduces $\hat{z}_t = \lfloor h_a(y_t) \rceil$, a coarse summary transmitted as side information, and models

$$
p(\hat{y}_t \mid \hat{z}_t, \bar{c}_t) = \prod_i \big(\mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2) * \mathcal{U}(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\big)(\hat{y}_{t,i}),
$$

where $(\mu_i, \sigma_i) = h_s(\hat{z}_t, \bar{c}_t, \text{spatial context}_i)$. The convolution with a uniform distribution exactly accounts for quantization binning, making the rate estimate $-\log_2 p(\hat{y}_t \mid \cdot)$ a tight, differentiable surrogate for the true arithmetic-coding cost. DCVC-HEM extends this with *dual spatial priors* (checkerboard-parallel autoregressive context) and a *latent temporal prior* distilled from $\bar{c}_t$, so that the entropy model sees spatial, hyperprior, and temporal evidence simultaneously [5]. Quantization itself uses the standard straight-through estimator with additive uniform noise during training — a relaxation justified by the observation that $\mathbb{E}[-\log p(\lfloor y + u \rceil)]$ upper-bounds the true discrete entropy.

### 4.3 Multi-scale temporal context mining

A single-resolution context cannot serve both coarse motion structure and fine texture detail. DCVC-TCM [5] mines contexts at multiple scales: the propagated reference feature $\hat{F}_{t-1}$ is warped by the decoded flow at resolutions $\{1, 1/2, 1/4\}$, and a context-mining network fuses these into $\{\bar{c}_t^0, \bar{c}_t^1, \bar{c}_t^2\}$, injected into the contextual encoder/decoder at matching stages. DCVC-B generalizes this to bi-directional mining, fusing forward and backward multi-scale contexts with learned gating [2]. The effect is measurable: each additional context scale shifts RD curves outward, particularly at high rates where fine-detail conditioning dominates the bit budget.

### 4.4 Rate–distortion Lagrangian optimization and variable rate

A single $\lambda$ yields a single RD point; practical codecs need a continuum. The lineage handles this three ways:

1. **Discrete $\lambda$ family.** Train $N$ models (e.g., $\lambda \in \{256, 512, 1024, 2048\}$ for MSE), interpolating between RD points — simple, but $N\times$ the parameters.
2. **Conditional modulation.** A rate-control embedding modulates intermediate features (via FiLM-style scaling), training one model over a $\lambda$ distribution; DCVC-DC uses this with hierarchical quality coefficients per temporal layer [2][5].
3. **Content-adaptive refinement.** Online per-sequence optimization (e.g., instance-adaptive finetuning of decoder-side parameters transmitted as model updates [5]) pushes individual sequences below the amortized RD curve at the cost of encoder complexity.

Hierarchical quality structures — assigning smaller $\lambda$ (higher quality) to reference frames in lower temporal layers — mirror VVC's QP cascading and are essential for long-GOP stability: the reference quality propagates forward, so under-allocating bits to layer-0 frames compounds distortion exponentially [2].

> **Theorem 2 (Lagrangian envelope).** *For the family of models $\{\theta_\lambda\}_{\lambda > 0}$ minimizing $R(\theta) + \lambda D(\theta)$, the achieved RD points lie on the convex hull of the operational RD function, and the BD-rate integral against any anchor is minimized over the choice of $\lambda$ grid by placing grid points where the anchor's RD curvature is maximal.*

This is the classical justification for $\lambda$-domain rate control, inherited unchanged by learned codecs: the only difference is that the "mode decisions" are amortized into network weights rather than searched per-block.

### 4.5 Benchmarking beyond VVC

The decisive question is empirical: does conditional flow-based coding beat the best hand-engineered system? The evidence, assembled under JVET common test conditions and standard UVG/MCL-JCV/HEVC test sets [2][5][6]:

| Codec | Anchor | BD-rate (PSNR) | Configuration |
|---|---|---|---|
| DCVC [1] | x265 veryslow | **−26.0%** | Low-delay P, 1080p |
| DCVC-TCM / HEM / DC [5] | VTM (H.266) | −10% to −18% | Low-delay P, varies by set |
| DCVC-B [2] | HM (H.265) RA | **−26.6%** | Random access, B-frames |
| DCVC-B [2] | VTM (H.266) RA | **< 0% (surpasses)** | Selected datasets, RA |

DCVC-B's surpassing of VTM on certain datasets under random access is the headline: a learned codec, trained end-to-end with a single RD Lagrangian, outperforming a standard whose encoder performs exhaustive per-block RD search [2]. The bi-directional gains come from three compounding effects: motion-difference context propagation cutting bi-directional motion bit cost, bi-directional temporal entropy models sharpening $p(\hat{y}_t \mid \bar{c}_t^f, \bar{c}_t^b)$, and hierarchical training stabilizing bit allocation across 32-frame GOPs.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

Beyond the benchmark table above, several empirical facts deserve emphasis:

1. **Ablation of conditioning.** Replacing the feature-domain context with a pixel-domain prediction (i.e., reverting to residual coding inside the same architecture) degrades BD-rate by 8–12% on UVG, confirming Theorem 1's prediction that the context carries strictly more than the residual [1].
2. **Scale-space vs. bilinear.** Under identical backbones, scale-space warping improves PSNR by 0.2–0.4 dB at matched rates on sequences with fast motion (e.g., *Jockey*, *ReadySetGo*), with the scale channel activating precisely at occlusion boundaries [3].
3. **Entropy model sharpening.** The hybrid entropy model (dual spatial + temporal latent priors) reduces the content bitrate by 5–9% relative to a hyperprior-only baseline, with the largest gains on high-entropy textures [5].
4. **Complexity reality check.** Decoder-side costs remain the Achilles' heel: representative conditional codecs require 100–300 kMACs/pixel and tens of millions of parameters [5], versus VTM's highly optimized (if slow to encode) decoder. Real-time 1080p learned decoding demands GPU or NPU acceleration — a deployment constraint, not a theoretical one.
5. **Generalization.** Models trained on Vimeo-90K transfer to UVG/MCL-JCV with modest degradation; the open training/evaluation framework of OpenDCVCs [5] has made such cross-dataset claims auditable for the first time.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Error propagation through learned references.** Unlike VVC's carefully managed reference picture lists, conditional codecs propagate *features* $\hat{F}_t$ forward indefinitely. Quantization noise in latents compounds through the recurrent reference path; DCVC-DC's hierarchical quality training mitigates but does not eliminate drift over long GOPs.
2. **Decoder complexity and memory.** Autoregressive spatial priors serialize decoding; even checkerboard-parallel variants lag behind the throughput of hardware VVC decoders by orders of magnitude on CPU.
3. **Rate estimation mismatch.** The differentiable rate $-\log p(\hat{y})$ is exact only under the learned model; arithmetic coding with mismatched probability tables (e.g., after aggressive quantization of the entropy network itself) inflates true bitrate.
4. **Dataset and metric dependence.** BD-rate leadership flips between datasets and between PSNR and MS-SSIM; "surpassing VVC" holds on selected corpora and configurations [2], not universally.
5. **Standardization gap.** VVC is a bitstream standard with conformance points; learned codecs lack normative specifications for network weights, arithmetic precision, and cross-platform determinism — prerequisites for any real deployment.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Conditional flow-based neural video compression reframes the oldest idea in video coding — exploit temporal redundancy — as a problem of *learning the right condition* rather than engineering the right prediction. Scale-space flow supplies a motion-compensation operator that internalizes uncertainty instead of aliasing it; hyperprior hierarchies and temporal conditional entropy models turn latent grids into tightly predictable, cheaply coded objects; and Lagrangian RD optimization, inherited from classical theory, trains the whole assembly end-to-end. The DCVC lineage's progression from 26% savings over x265 to parity-beyond VVC in bi-directional configurations [1][2] suggests the classical ceiling is not a law of nature but a plateau of hand-engineering. The remaining barriers — decoder complexity, reference drift, and standardization — are engineering and institutional, not information-theoretic. When they fall, the bitstream of the future will likely be written not by a committee's toolbox, but by a learned conditional distribution.
