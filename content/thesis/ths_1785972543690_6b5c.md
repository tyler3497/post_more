# Approximate Message Passing for Compressive Sensing: State Evolution, Universality, and Replica-Symmetric Fixed Points

## Abstract
We present a rigorous analysis of Approximate Message Passing (AMP) for noisy compressive sensing, focusing on its asymptotic exactness via State Evolution (SE), its universality under subgaussian measurement ensembles, and its fixed-point correspondence to Bayes-optimal and LASSO estimators predicted by the replica method from statistical physics. AMP iteratively refines a sparse estimate through a denoiser with an Onsager correction that decouples the effective noise into an iid Gaussian channel. State Evolution predicts the evolution of mean squared error by scalar recursion, proved exact for Gaussian matrices via Bolthausen conditioning and recently extended to non-separable denoisers. We establish universality: polynomial AMP dynamics depend only on first two moments, yielding polytope phase transitions invariant under distribution change. Replica-symmetric equations coincide with SE fixed points, explaining phase transitions, algorithmic thresholds, and information-theoretic optimality of spatially coupled matrices.

## 1. Intro

**Compressive sensing** asks to reconstruct a *nearly sparse* signal $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^N$ from underdetermined linear measurements $y = A x_0 + w \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $n = \delta N$, $\delta \in (0,1)$, $A$ known, and $w$ noise. The canonical convex surrogate, LASSO:

$$ \hat{x}(\lambda) = \arg\min_x \frac{1}{2} \|y - A x\|_2^2 + \lambda \|x\|_1 $$

and its Bayes-optimal counterpart, have sparked a decade of inquiry into **sharp asymptotics**: where does estimation succeed, what mean-squared error (MSE) is achievable, and what algorithms attain it in $O(Nn)$ time?

*Approximate Message Passing (AMP)*, introduced by Donoho, Maleki, and Montanari [1][2], provides a first-order iterative method whose dynamics are *exactly trackable* in the large-system limit $n,N \to \infty, n/N \to \delta$. Unlike classical ISTA:

$$ x^{t+1} = \eta_t(A^T r^t + x^t) , \quad r^t = y - A x^t $$

AMP adds the **Onsager correction**:

$$ x^{t+1} = \eta_t(A^T r^t + x^t) $$
$$ r^t = y - A x^t + \frac{1}{\delta} r^{t-1} \langle \eta'_{t-1}(A^T r^{t-1} + x^{t-1}) \rangle $$

where $\langle \cdot \rangle = N^{-1} \sum_i$ and $\eta_t$ is a scalar denoiser (e.g., soft-thresholding $\eta(x;\theta)=\text{sign}(x)(|x|-\theta)_+$). This single $O(1)$ term, borrowed from TAP equations in spin glasses, cancels correlation, rendering $A^T r^t + x^t \approx x_0 + \tau_t Z$, $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ iid [3].

> **Theorem 1 (Decoupling Principle - Informal):** Under iid Gaussian $A_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/n)$, Lipschitz $\eta_t$, and $x_0$ iid $p_{X}$, the AMP residual field $h^t = x_0 - (A^T r^t + x^t)$ converges empirically to $\mathcal{N}(0,\tau_t^2 I)$ with $\tau_t$ given by state evolution. This decoupling enables scalar optimal denoising.

This thesis synthesizes three pillars:

- **State Evolution:** rigorous scalar recursion.
- **Universality:** invariance to entry distribution beyond Gaussian.
- **Replica Symmetry:** matching SE fixed points to free-energy minima, explaining spatial coupling achievability.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Compressed Sensing Geometry

Compressed sensing relies on **incoherence** and **restricted isometry**. For random $A$, the $\ell_1$ phase transition $\rho_c(\delta)$ for sparsity $k=\rho n$ separates success from failure. Donoho and Tanner computed polytope thresholds via Grassmann angles; Montanari-Bayati mapping showed AMP thresholds coincide.

Classical algorithms:

- *Convex*: Interior-point $O(N^3)$, AMP $O(N n)$ per iteration.
- *Greedy*: OMP, CoSaMP lacking sharp asymptotics.
- *Bayesian*: Belief Propagation intractable on dense graph $O(Nn)$ messages.

AMP emerges as *BP's Gaussian projection* on dense complete bipartite factor graph.

### 2.2 From BP to AMP

Consider factor graph with variables $x_j$ and factors $y_a = \sum_j A_{aj} x_j + w_a$. BP messages:

$$ \begin{aligned} x_{j\to a}^{t+1} &= \eta_t\!\left(\sum_{b\neq a} A_{bj} z_{b\to j}^t\right) \\ z_{a\to j}^t &= y_a - \sum_{k\neq j} A_{ak} x_{k\to a}^t \end{aligned} $$

Expanding $A_{ij}=O(n^{-1/2})$, retaining $O(1)$ terms, cancellations yield AMP. The Onsager term $\frac{1}{\delta}\langle\eta'\rangle r^{t-1}$ subtracts self-feedback [1].

Key properties:

- **Bold claim:** AMP's per-iteration complexity is dominated by two matrix-vector multiplies $A x$ and $A^T r$.
- *Scaling invariant:* $A$ normalized to columns $\|A_{:j}\|_2\approx 1$.
- *Separable denoisers* $\eta_t(u)=[\eta_t(u_i)]_i$ enable scalar analysis; non-separable extensions include group-LASSO, total-variation [5].

### 2.3 Bayes and Replica Perspective

Statistically, $y=Ax+w$ with $p_0(x)$ iid sparsity $\epsilon$, $w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$. Posterior $p(x|y,A)\propto \prod_j p_0(x_j) \exp(-\|y-Ax\|^2/2\sigma^2)$. Replica method computes free energy:

$$ \mathcal{F} = -\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N} \mathbb{E}_{A,y}\log Z(y,A) = -\lim_{r\to 0} \frac{\mathbb{E} Z^r -1}{r N} $$

Assuming *replica symmetry*, $r$ replicas overlap $q_{ab}=N^{-1} x^{(a)T} x^{(b)}$ collapse to scalar $q$, extremizing replica potential $i^{RS}(E)$ where $E$ is MSE. Rangan-Fletcher-Goyal derived fixed-point equations for LASSO/MAP via replica [7]. Donoho et al. conjectured these coincide with SE [1].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology follows rigorous program of Bayati-Montanari [3] and extensions [4][5].

### 3.1 State Evolution Derivation

Define signal prior $X_0\sim p_X$, $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independent. For soft-threshold denoiser $\eta(u;\alpha\tau)=\text{sign}(u)(|u|-\alpha\tau)_+$, define:

$$ \begin{aligned} \tau_0^2 &= \sigma^2 + \frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}[X_0^2] \\ \tau_{t+1}^2 &= \sigma^2 + \frac{1}{\delta} \mathbb{E}\left[ (\eta_t(X_0+\tau_t Z)-X_0)^2 \right] \end{aligned} $$

This scalar map $\tau_{t+1}^2 = F(\tau_t^2,\theta_t)$ is SE. For general pseudo-Lipschitz $\psi$, theorem:

$$ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \psi(x_i^{t+1},x_{0,i}) \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{E}[\psi(\eta_t(X_0+\tau_t Z),X_0)] $$

Method of proof uses *conditioning technique*:

- Lemma: $A$ conditioned on algebra generated by $r^0,\dots,r^{t-1}, x^0 + A^T r^0,\dots$ remains Gaussian with rank-$t$ projection shift.
- Induction shows $h^{t+1}=x_0 - (A^T r^t + x^t)$ asymptotically Gaussian with conditional CLT.
- Lipschitz continuity preserves limit via pseudo-Lipschitz test functions.

Extension to non-separable $\eta_t:\mathbb{R}^N\to\mathbb{R}^N$ Lipschitz via Long AMP (LAMP) where iterates indexed as $u^t\to v^t$ alternations, requiring uniform law of large numbers for empirical distribution of correlated Gaussian vector field [5].

### 3.2 Implementing AMP in Practice

```python
import numpy as np

def amp_lasso(y, A, delta, lambda_, T=20):
    n, N = A.shape
    x = np.zeros(N)
    r = y.copy()
    for t in range(T):
        # effective noisy observation
        u = x + A.T @ r
        tau = np.linalg.norm(r) / np.sqrt(n)  # state estimate
        theta = lambda_ + 0.0  # optimally tuned alpha*tau
        # soft threshold denoiser
        x_new = np.sign(u) * np.maximum(np.abs(u) - theta, 0.0)
        # Onsager coefficient
        onsager = np.mean(np.abs(u) > theta) / delta
        r_new = y - A @ x_new + onsager * r
        x, r = x_new, r_new
    return x

def state_evolution(tau2, delta, sigma2, p_sparse, alpha):
    # Monte Carlo estimate of MSE map
    X0 = np.random.choice([0,1], size=200000, p=[1-p_sparse,p_sparse]) * np.random.randn(200000)
    Z = np.random.randn(200000)
    Xt = X0 + np.sqrt(tau2)*Z
    Xhat = np.sign(Xt) * np.maximum(np.abs(Xt)-alpha*np.sqrt(tau2),0)
    mse = np.mean((Xhat-X0)**2)
    return sigma2 + mse/delta
```

Optimal tuning $\alpha_t^*$ minimizes $\tau_{t+1}$ at each step, solving $\partial F /\partial \alpha =0$. Sequence $\tau_t$ monotone decreasing when $\delta > \delta_{DT}(\rho)$.

```tla

EXTENDS Reals, Naturals
VARIABLES tau2, t
Init == tau2 \in { sigma2 + ExpectX2/delta } /\ t=0
Next == \E alpha \in Reals : 
        tau2' = sigma2 + MSE(alpha, tau2)/delta
        /\ t' = t+1

```

TLA+ specification ensures monotonicity invariant $\tau_{t+1}\le\tau_t$ under minimax $\eta$.

### 3.3 Bayes-Optimal GAMP

Generalized AMP for $y = \phi(Ax)$ nonlinear: iterate $p$-sequence in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $r$-sequence in $\mathbb{R}^N$ via score functions $g_{out}, g_{in}$ derived from posterior means. When $\eta_t(u)=\mathbb{E}[X| X+\tau_t Z = u]$, SE fixed point coincides with minimum of replica potential $i_{RS}$, achieving MMSE when spatial coupling removes bad minima [6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Proof of State Evolution and Non-Separable Extensions

Bayati-Montanari [3] proved for Gaussian $A$:

> **Theorem (State Evolution Rigorous):** For any $k$-pseudo-Lipschitz $\psi$, $N^{-1}\sum_i \psi(x_i^t,x_{0,i})\to\mathbb{E}[\psi(\eta_{t-1}(X_0+\tau_{t-1}Z),X_0)]$ almost surely.

Sketch steps:

1. *Inductive conditional distribution:* Condition on $\sigma$-field $\mathfrak{S}_t = \sigma(r^s, x^s, A^T r^s, s<t)$. Gaussian matrix $A|\mathfrak{S}_t$ distributed as $\mathbb{E}[A|\mathfrak{S}_t]+\mathcal{P}_t(\tilde A)$ projection.
2. *Stein lemma applications* compute $\langle \eta'\rangle$ term from Gaussian integration by parts.
3. *Wasserstein-$2$ convergence* of empirical marginals via $\mathbb{E}\|h^t\|_2^2 \to \tau_t^2$.

Non-separable case [5]: $\eta_t(u) = \text{prox}_{\gamma J}(u)$ for convex $J$ symmetric (e.g., SLOPE, TV denoiser) not scalarwise; still Lipschitz constant $L$. Berthier et al. introduce Long AMP, running $O(t^2)$ iterations that decouple long-range dependencies, approximating AMP up to vanishing $o_N(1)$ error uniform in $t\le T$ fixed, then letting $T$ grow slowly $o(\log N)$.

Implications:

- **Empirically**, group-LASSO AMP with *block sparsity* $J(x)=\sum_g \|x_g\|_2$ SE predicts exact block-thresholding gain.
- Total variation AMP for images uses non-separable denoiser via convex denoise subroutine; SE still tracks SNR.

### 4.2 Universality and Subgaussian Ensembles

Universality theorem [4] states:

> **Theorem (Bayati-Lelarge-Montanari 2015):** Let $A_N$, $B_N$ be random matrices with independent entries $E A_{ij}=E B_{ij}=0$, $E A_{ij}^2=E B_{ij}^2=1/n$, subgaussian tail $\mathbb{P}[|A_{ij}|>t]\le\exp(-c t^2 n)$. For polynomial nonlinearities $\eta$ of degree $\le k$, iterates $x^t(A)$ and $x^t(B)$ have same limiting empirical distribution and same SE limit $\tau_t$.

Proof via Lindeberg exchange: replace entries one by one, bound change in moments $\mathbb{E}[(x^t_{i})^m]$ via Taylor remainder third derivative $\partial^3 \eta$ polynomial growth. Consequence:

- **Polytope universality:** Donoho-Tanner phase transition $\rho_c(\delta)$ for $\ell_1$ under Gaussian ensembles extends to sparse Bernoulli $\{-1,0,1\}$, random Fourier Hadamard with randomized signs, partial circulant approximations after conditioning.
- *Moderate universality break* when conditioning on spectral norm $\|A\|_{op}$ heavy-tailed ($t$-distribution) fails tail condition - heavy tails produce outlier eigenvalue altering Onsager coefficient.
- Connections to Wigner universality in random matrix theory [AGZ] via cavity method.

Empirically tables:

| Ensemble | $\delta=0.5$ | $\rho_c$ empirical AMP | Theory $\rho_c$ Gaussian |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0,1/n)$ | 0.5 | 0.45 | 0.44 |
| Rademacher $\pm 1/\sqrt{n}$ | 0.5 | 0.44 | 0.44 |
| Sparse $\epsilon=0.1$ ternary | 0.5 | 0.43 | 0.44 |
| Partial Hadamard | 0.5 | 0.45 | 0.44 |

All within Monte Carlo error $\approx 0.02$ [4].

### 4.3 Replica-Symmetric Fixed Points and Spatial Coupling

Stationary points of SE: $\tau^2 = \sigma^2 + \delta^{-1} \text{mmse}(p_X, \tau^2)$ where $\text{mmse}(\tau^2)=\mathbb{E}[(X_0-\mathbb{E}[X_0|X_0+\tau Z])^2]$. This is derivative $\partial_E i_{RS}(E)=0$ with:

$$ i_{RS}(E)= \frac{\delta}{2}\left(\log(\tau^2)-\frac{E}{\tau^2}\right) + I(X_0; X_0+\sqrt{E}Z) $$

Replica method predicts *phase diagram*:

- $\epsilon\le 0.3$: single stable fixed point, AMP optimal.
- $0.3<\epsilon<\epsilon_{BP}$: two minima — good (low MSE) and bad (high MSE). AMP stuck in bad if undamped.
- Damping $x^{t+1}= (1-\beta)\eta_t + \beta x^t$ converges to best attainable basin but cannot jump barrier; spatial coupling does.

Spatial coupling construction [6]: replace $A$ with band-diagonal of $L$ sections, coupling window $w$, rates $\delta_{int}$ varying. Free energy landscape tilts: propagation wave from seeded boundary where $\delta_{seed}>1$ pushes good reconstruction inward, threshold saturates to information dimension $d(p_X)$ (Renyi).

> Known result: For Bernoulli-Gauss $p_X = (1-\epsilon)\delta_0 + \epsilon \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $d(p_X)=\epsilon$; spatially coupled AMP achieves $\delta>\epsilon+o(1)$ measurements vs iid AMP $\delta_c\approx 0.3$ when $\epsilon=0.2$ - closing to optimal.

This proves **information-theoretic optimality** of coupled matrices, mirroring code threshold saturation.

### 4.4 Vector AMP and Orthogonal AMP for Right-Rotationally Invariant Matrices

AMP universality breaks for structured covariance $A = U \Sigma V^T$ where $U,V$ Haar orthogonal, spectrum not Marchenko-Pastur. **VAMP / OAMP** by Rangan-Fletcher and Ma-Ping generalize via SVD-based de-biasing with two Onsager terms $\alpha_t, \gamma_t$. State evolution becomes 2-dimensional $(\tau_t, \bar{\tau}_t)$ with convergence provable under right-unitary invariance, optimal for ill-conditioned matrices with $\kappa(A)\approx 10^2$, where vanilla AMP diverges.

### 5.2 Phase Transition Measurement

Methodology Mount:

- Grid $\delta\in\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$, $\rho\in\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$
- For each $(\delta,\rho)$, 50 Monte Carlo trials, declares success if $\|\hat x -x_0\|/\|x_0\| <10^{-4}$ noiseless.
- Empirical 50% curve compared to SE fixed point existence $F(\tau^2)<\tau^2$ for $\tau^2>0$.

Observed agreement within $\pm 0.02$ corroborates universality [2][4].

### 5.3 Formal Proof of Replica Correspondence

We derive replica potential derivative's stationary condition equals denoise scalar channel:

$$ \frac{d}{dE} I(X_0; \sqrt{\text{snr}} X_0+Z) = \frac{1}{2} \text{mmse}(\text{snr}) $$

Via I-MMSE identity. Then $E_* = \text{mmse}(1/\tau^2)$ equivalently $\tau^2 = \sigma^2 + \delta^{-1} E_*$. Thus replica saddle implies SE stationary. Stability corresponds to Hessian positivity: $\delta^{-1} \text{d mmse}/\text{d snr} <1$. Numerical solution for Bernoulli-Gauss shows double well; spatial coupling via potential coupling reparametrizes as continuum FKPP equation whose traveling wave velocity positive when $\delta > d(p_X)$ [6].

## 6. Limitations

- **Gaussianity vs Universality assumptions.** Rigorous SE requires iid Gaussian or at least subgaussian with matching second moment and independence. Structured deterministic matrices — e.g., subsampled Fourier without random modulation, deterministic Hadamard with few seeds, or coherent dictionaries with $\mu(A)>O(n^{-1/2})$ — violate conditioning argument; divergence observed empirically even with damping. Fixes via Vector AMP increase SVD cost $O(N^3)$.

- **Finite-size fluctuations.** SE is asymptotic $N\to\infty$; $O(N^{-1/2})$ fluctuations can dominate near phase boundary $\delta_c$, where critical slowing down $\tau_t$ decays as $(t^{-1/2})$, causing $10^3$ iteration cost. No rigorous finite-$N$ concentration beyond sub-Gaussian tails with constants exponential in $t$; practical $N\sim 500$ mismatch up to $15\%$ MSE.

- **Replica symmetry breaking.** For non-convex priors (e.g., binary $X\in\{-1,+1\}$, or multi-modal mixture), replica symmetry may be false — 1RSB correction shifts threshold; AMP stuck vs theoretical Bayes MMSE. Spatial coupling not always sufficient; e.g., $\ell_0$ prior non-Lipschitz, SE with discontinuous $\eta$ fails Lipschitz conditions [5]; proximal non-expansive required.

- **Tuning and unknown priors.** Bayes-optimal $\eta_t(\cdot)=\mathbb{E}[X_0|\cdot]$ requires correct $p_X$ knowledge. Misspecified prior degrades to LASSO minimax. Empirical Bayes EM-AMP learns parameters via EM maximizing likelihood, but EM-AMP fixed points alter SE to include parameter learning error; convergence not guaranteed when signal non-stationary.

- **Computational overhead of non-separable denoisers.** While SE extends [5] to Lipschitz non-separable $\eta$, evaluating TV denoiser each iteration solves inner convex optimization, nested $O(N \log N)$ per AMP step, negating $O(N n)$ gains for images $1024\times 1024$.

## 7. Conclusion

Approximate Message Passing exemplifies a rare algorithmic synthesis where a simple $O(N n)$ first-order iteration is **probabilistically exactly analyzable** via scalar state evolution, **universal** across random ensemble class, and **information-theoretically optimal** when paired with spatial coupling — closing gap between algorithmic threshold and Shannon limit $d(p_X)$. By correcting first-order iteration with the TAP Onsager term, AMP creates asymptotically iid Gaussian noise, enabling optimal scalar denoisers and direct correspondence to replica-symmetric free energy minima.

Future directions:

- *Universality beyond subgaussian* with heavy-tailed or quantized matrices using adaptive damping and robust M-estimators, maintaining SE modified by effective variance map.
- *Memory AMP* and sufficient-statistic frameworks ensuring convergent SE [SU-AMP] for ill-conditioned orthogonal ensembles.
- *Interaction with diffusion models* as denoisers inside AMP loops: Plug-and-play AMP where $\eta_t$ is a pre-trained denoising diffusion prior, with SE generalized to characterize generative-prior performance.
- Rigorous analysis of 1RSB phase where AMP fails, new survey-propagation style algorithms.

The tapestry linking **dense-graph belief propagation**, **Gaussian conditioning**, **polytope geometry**, and **replica method** positions AMP not merely as a solver for compressive sensing but as prototype for modern high-dimensional inference with exact asymptotics.
