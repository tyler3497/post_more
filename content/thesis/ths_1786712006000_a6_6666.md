# Hierarchical Locomotion Control for Quadruped Robots: Convex Model Predictive Control with Raibert Heuristic Foot Placement, Whole-Body Impulse Torque Optimization via QP Cascades, Residual RL Policy Distillation with Domain Randomization, and Sim2Real Verification via System Identification on Unitree Go2 and ANYmal-C

## Abstract
Quadruped locomotion in unstructured terrain requires *real-time* planning of centroidal momentum, *contact-consistent* torque allocation, and *adaptive* robustness to unmodeled dynamics. Pure model-based controllers achieve agility [1][2][3] but degrade under contact uncertainty; pure RL achieves resilience [4][5] but lacks safety guarantees and high-frequency explainability. This thesis proposes a **hierarchical locomotion stack**: **convex MPC** for 12-state centroidal dynamics at 200 Hz, **Raibert heuristic** foot placement regulation, **Whole-Body Control (WBC) QP cascade** for impulse torque optimization at 500 Hz, and **residual RL policy distillation** with domain randomization [6][7] for sim2real on **Unitree Go2** and **ANYmal-C**. We formalize recursive feasibility, foot placement capture, and residual stability, derive algorithmic integration, and evaluate on slopes 35°, steps 22 cm, slip µ=0.15, payload 10 kg, with system-identified actuator delays 8-18 ms. Contributions include provable QP stability, open reference implementation Rust/Python, TLA+ contact sequencing spec, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient quadruped autonomy.
