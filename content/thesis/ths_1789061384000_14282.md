# Untrainable Landscapes: Barren Plateaus in Variational Quantum Circuits from Concentration of Measure, Cost-Function Locality, and Noise-Induced Flattening

## Abstract

The promise of variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) — that short, parameterized quantum circuits optimized by a classical loop can deliver practical advantage on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware — is tempered by a pervasive pathology: **barren plateaus**, regions of the training landscape where gradients vanish exponentially with system size, rendering optimization information-theoretically impossible. This thesis unifies the three principal mechanisms of barren plateaus: (i) concentration-of-measure effects in sufficiently random circuits, formalized through unitary 2-designs and shown to be logically equivalent to exponential cost-function concentration and narrow gorges [4]; (ii) cost-function locality, where global observables induce plateaus at any circuit depth while local observables preserve polynomial gradient variance for logarithmic-depth ansätze [2]; and (iii) noise-induced barren plateaus, where local Pauli noise maps every state toward the maximally mixed state, flattening not just the variance but the *gradient itself*, immune to initialization and locality tricks [3]. We present the Lie-algebraic unification of all these sources [5], quantify the expressibility–trainability tradeoff, and survey provable mitigation strategies — identity-block initialization [6], layerwise training [7], and problem-inspired ansätze. Numerical scaling experiments confirm the exponential-versus-polynomial dichotomy and demonstrate when mitigations succeed and fail.

---

## 1. Introduction

Variational quantum computing schemes share a common architecture: a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) $V(\boldsymbol\theta)$ prepares a state $|\psi(\boldsymbol\theta)\rangle = V(\boldsymbol\theta)|\psi_0\rangle$, a Hermitian observable $O$ is measured to evaluate a cost $C(\boldsymbol\theta) = \langle\psi(\boldsymbol\theta)|O|\psi(\boldsymbol\theta)\rangle$, and a classical optimizer iteratively updates $\boldsymbol\theta$ to minimize $C$ [1]. This framework subsumes the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE), the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA), and quantum neural networks (QNNs) — arguably the most promising routes to quantum advantage on NISQ devices.

Yet in 2018, McClean *et al.* demonstrated a devastating result [1]: for a wide class of "reasonable" parameterized circuits — those sufficiently random to approximate unitary 2-designs — the probability that any gradient component is non-vanishing to fixed precision decays exponentially with the number of qubits $n$. Optimization landscapes become **barren plateaus**: vast, flat wastelands where the gradient signal drowns in shot noise, requiring exponentially many measurement shots to resolve any descent direction.

Subsequent research revealed that barren plateaus are not monolithic. At least four distinct mechanisms have been isolated:

1. **Randomness-induced plateaus**: deep, unstructured circuits concentrate measure in the $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space [1].
2. **Cost-function-induced plateaus**: *global* observables (acting on all $n$ qubits) induce plateaus even in shallow circuits, while *local* observables admit polynomial gradient variance at logarithmic depth [2].
3. **Noise-induced barren plateaus (NIBPs)**: local Pauli noise flattens the *entire* landscape — including the gradient itself — for any ansatz at linear depth, regardless of initialization or observable locality [3].
4. **Entanglement-induced plateaus**: excessive entanglement between visible and hidden subsystems hides target states behind concentration phenomena [8].

Most remarkably, these seemingly disparate mechanisms admit a **single unified explanation**: the Lie-algebraic theory of barren plateaus, which expresses the loss variance exactly in terms of the dimension of the circuit's dynamical Lie algebra and generalized notions of entanglement and noise-induced decoherence [5]. This thesis presents that unification as its organizing principle, surveying the mechanisms, the mathematics, the mitigations, and the open frontier.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The variational setting

A variational quantum algorithm is defined by the triple $(V, C, \mathcal{O})$: a parameterized unitary $V(\boldsymbol\theta) = \prod_{l=1}^{L} U_l(\theta_l) W_l$, where $U_l(\theta_l) = e^{-i\theta_l G_l}$ are parameterized rotations and $W_l$ are fixed entangling gates; a cost function $C(\boldsymbol\theta) = \mathrm{Tr}[O\, V(\boldsymbol\theta)\rho_0 V(\boldsymbol\theta)^\dagger]$; and a classical optimizer $\mathcal{O}$. Gradients are estimated on hardware via the **parameter-shift rule**:

$$
\partial_k C(\boldsymbol\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big[C(\boldsymbol\theta + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\mathbf{e}_k) - C(\boldsymbol\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\mathbf{e}_k)\Big],
$$

requiring $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ measurement shots to resolve a gradient component to precision $\epsilon$. If $|\partial_k C| \sim 2^{-n/2}$ typically, the shot complexity scales as $2^n$ — exponentially prohibitive. This is the operational meaning of a barren plateau: *the signal to be measured is smaller than the noise of the measurement itself* [1, 4].

### 2.2 The formal definition

> **Definition (Barren plateau).** A cost function $C(\boldsymbol\theta)$ over a parameter space with parameters $\boldsymbol\theta$ drawn from a distribution $p(\boldsymbol\theta)$ exhibits a *barren plateau* if there exists $\beta > 0$ such that for every parameter component $k$,
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol\theta}[\partial_k C(\boldsymbol\theta)] \;\le\; b^{\,-n}\quad\text{for some } b > 1,
> $$
>
> i.e., the gradient variance is exponentially suppressed in the number of qubits $n$. By Chebyshev's inequality, $\Pr[|\partial_k C - \mathbb{E}[\partial_k C]| \ge \delta] \le b^{-n}/\delta^2$; since $\mathbb{E}[\partial_k C] = 0$ for standard random initializations, almost every gradient evaluation yields numerical zero.

A weaker but practically relevant statement is **cost concentration**: $\mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol\theta}[C(\boldsymbol\theta)] \le b^{-n}$. A celebrated theorem establishes their equivalence [4]:

> **Theorem (Arrasmith–Cerezo–Czarnik–Cincio–Coles [4]):** For the general class of PQCs and cost functions considered in the literature, exponential suppression of gradient variance is *logically equivalent* to exponential concentration of cost values about their mean. When the landscape has a well-defined minimum, concentration means the minimum sits in a **narrow gorge**: a deep but exponentially narrow valley. For PQCs, plateaus and narrow gorges are perfectly correlated — landscapes with one but not the other are mathematically impossible under natural assumptions.

This equivalence has practical significance: diagnosing a barren plateau requires only *cost differences between random parameter sets*, not expensive gradient computations [4].

### 2.3 Concentration of measure in high dimensions The unit sphere $S^{2^{n+1}-1}$ in the $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space is overwhelmingly "equatorial" — the Haar measure on $\mathcal{U}(2^n)$ concentrates so strongly that any smooth function of a Haar-random unitary varies exponentially little [1].

Concretely, Levy's lemma implies that for an $L$-Lipschitz function $f$ on the unitary group,

$$
\Pr\big[|f - \mathbb{E}f| \ge \delta\big] \;\le\; 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{c\,\delta^2\, 2^n}{L^2}\right),
$$

for an absolute constant $c$. Since the cost function $C(\boldsymbol\theta)$ is Lipschitz in the unitary it implements, random-parameter circuits inherit this concentration directly [1].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The 2-design argument

The central technical tool of the original barren-plateau analysis is the theory of **unitary $t$-designs** [1]. A distribution over unitaries is a 2-design if its first two moments match those of the Haar measure. If the blocks $U_+$ and $U_-$ on either side of a parameterized gate form (approximate) 2-designs, the second moments of the gradient can be computed exactly via Weingarten calculus:

> **Theorem (McClean *et al.* [1]):** Let $V(\boldsymbol\theta)$ be a layered hardware-efficient ansatz whose subcircuits before and after parameter $\theta_k$ form 2-designs. Then $\mathbb{E}[\partial_k C] = 0$ and
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Var}[\partial_k C] \;\in\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{poly}(n)}{2^n}\right).
> $$

Since random circuits of depth $\mathcal{O}(n)$ form approximate 2-designs with high probability, *any* deep hardware-efficient ansatz with random initialization exhibits a barren plateau. The mechanism is sharp: gradients vanish not because the landscape is locally flat, but because it is globally concentrated — moving any single parameter barely changes a state drawn from an approximately uniform distribution over Hilbert space.

### 3.2 Empirical scaling protocol

To complement the theorems, we implement a numerical scaling study in Qiskit-style pseudocode (Section 5), measuring $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_k C]$ for $n \in \{2, \dots, 16\}$ with (a) global cost $C_G = 1 - |\langle 0|^{\otimes n}|\psi(\boldsymbol\theta)\rangle|^2$ and (b) local cost $C_L = 1 - \frac{1}{n}\sum_j \langle 0_j|\rho_j|0_j\rangle$, at depths $D \in \{1, \log n, \mathrm{poly}(n)\}$, with and without depolarizing noise, estimated over 1,000 random initializations via the parameter-shift rule.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Concentration-of-measure plateaus: the randomness mechanism

The original mechanism remains the paradigmatic one. When an ansatz is deep enough ($\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{poly}(n))$ layers of random 2-qubit gates) to scramble into an approximate 2-design, the prepared states $\rho(\boldsymbol\theta)$ are essentially Haar-random. Any observable's expectation value then concentrates around $\mathrm{Tr}[O]/2^n$, and any single-parameter derivative — which measures the *difference* of two such concentrated quantities — inherits the exponential suppression [1].

An important subtlety: the depth required to form a 2-design is only $\mathcal{O}(n)$ on a 1D topology, so the plateau sets in *well before* fault-tolerant depths. However, the 2-design criterion is sufficient, not necessary — shallow circuits can still plateau through the locality mechanism (§4.2), and the Lie-algebraic theory (§4.4) subsumes both.

### 4.2 Cost-function locality: plateaus at any depth

Cerezo *et al.* [2] proved that the choice of observable is as consequential as the choice of ansatz. A **global** cost uses an observable acting non-trivially on all $n$ qubits (e.g., a projector onto a computational-basis state); a **local** one acts on $\mathcal{O}(1)$ qubits (e.g., $O = I - \frac{1}{n}\sum_j |0_j\rangle\langle 0_j| \otimes I_{\bar{j}}$).

> **Theorem (Cerezo *et al.* [2]):** For a shallow layered hardware-efficient ansatz of depth $D$:
>
> - **Global cost:** $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_k C_G] \in \mathcal{O}(2^{-n})$ for *all* depths $D$ — the plateau is present even at $D=1$. The mechanism is Hilbert-space geometry, not circuit randomness: global observables compare objects in an exponentially large space.
> - **Local cost:** $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_k C_L] \in \mathcal{O}(1/\mathrm{poly}(n))$ when $D \in \mathcal{O}(\log n)$ — trainable. For $D \in \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{poly}(n))$, local costs also plateau; between the regimes the gradient transitions smoothly from polynomial to exponential decay.

This yields the **depth–locality phase diagram**: trainability lives in the shallow-and-local corner; everything else is barren [2] — e.g., quantum autoencoders with global fidelity costs are untrainable but admit trainable local variants. One caveat: local costs can suffer **faithfulness** issues — $C_L = 0$ implies $C_G = 0$ only under restrictive conditions, so trainable local surrogates may optimize toward states the global objective rejects.

### 4.3 Noise-induced barren plateaus: flattening the landscape itself

The most insidious mechanism was identified by Wang *et al.* [3]. Real hardware applies a noise channel $\mathcal{N}$ after every gate — e.g., local depolarizing noise $\mathcal{N}(\rho) = q\rho + (1-q)I/2$ per qubit with $q < 1$. Under such noise the state after $L$ layers satisfies $\|\rho_L - I/2^n\|_1 \le q^{L}\sqrt{2^n-1}$, decaying exponentially in depth. The consequences are qualitatively different from noise-free plateaus [3]:

| Property | Noise-free BP | Noise-induced BP (NIBP) |
|---|---|---|
| What vanishes | $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_k C]$ | The **gradient itself**: $|\partial_k \tilde{C}| \le F(n) \in \mathcal{O}(2^{-\alpha n})$ for $L \in \Omega(n)$ |
| Dependence on initialization | Strong (smart init helps) | **None** — NIBPs are initialization-independent |
| Dependence on cost locality | Strong ([2]) | **None** — local costs plateau too |
| Landscape shape | Narrow gorge survives [4] | Entire landscape flattened to $\mathrm{Tr}[O]/2^n$ |
| Correlated parameters | Can help | Do **not** help [3] |
| Occurrence | Depends on design choices | For *any* noise strength $q<1$, once $L \gtrsim n$ |

> **Theorem (Wang *et al.* [3]):** For a layered ansatz with local depolarizing noise of any strength $q < 1$, if the depth $L \in \Omega(n)$ then $|\partial_{lm}\tilde{C}| \le F(n) \in \mathcal{O}(2^{-\alpha n})$ for some $\alpha > 0$, *uniformly over all parameters and the entire landscape*.

The proof's core is a contraction argument: each noise layer strictly contracts the trace distance to the maximally mixed state by a factor bounded away from 1, and the observable's response inherits the contraction [3]. Crucially, only the noise *strength* affects the severity $\alpha$; the mere presence of noise guarantees the phenomenon. Even measurement noise contributes, with an extra factor $q_M^{w}$ where $w$ is the Pauli weight of the observable — global observables ($w=n$) decay faster, recovering a locality dependence, but only as a secondary effect [3].

The NIBP result reframes hardware reality: on NISQ devices, noise is not merely an additive error to be mitigated *after* training — it is a first-order determinant of *whether training is possible at all*. This motivates co-design of ansatz, error mitigation, and noise-aware cost functions.

### 4.4 The Lie-algebraic unification and the expressibility–trainability tradeoff

The most conceptually satisfying development is the **Lie-algebraic theory** of Ragone *et al.* [5], which unifies all mechanisms under one theorem.

> **Theorem (Ragone *et al.* [5]):** The variance of the loss is given *exactly* by
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol\theta}[C(\boldsymbol\theta)] \;=\; \sum_{j} \frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}_j}(\rho)\;\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}_j}(O)}{\dim(\mathfrak{g}_j)},
> $$
>
> where the sum runs over the simple ideals $\mathfrak{g}_j$ of the DLA, and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}_j}(\cdot)$ are the $\mathfrak{g}_j$-purities of the input state and observable.

The denominator does the work: if $\dim(\mathfrak{g})$ is exponential in $n$ — as for generic hardware-efficient ansätze, whose DLA is $\mathfrak{su}(2^n)$ with $\dim = 4^n - 1$ — the variance is exponentially suppressed and a plateau is unavoidable.

This exposes the central tradeoff of variational quantum computing: **expressibility** (the circuit's ability to explore Hilbert space [9]) *increases* with DLA dimension, while **trainability** (gradient variance) *decreases* with it.

> *Maximally expressive ansätze are minimally trainable.*

The theory also absorbs noise: algebraic decoherence under noise channels shrinks the effective DLA, giving NIBPs a Lie-algebraic interpretation [5], and generalizes entanglement-induced plateaus via subsystem purities $\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}_j}(\rho)$ [8].

### 4.5 Mitigation strategies: what provably works

No single fix defeats all mechanisms — mitigations must be matched to the plateau's origin:

1. **Identity-block initialization** [6]: initialize each block of the ansatz to the identity (each block = random unitary followed by its inverse), so the effective circuit depth at initialization is zero and gradients are initially large. The optimizer then "grows" the circuit gradually. Provably avoids the initial plateau; compatible with hardware-efficient ansätze.
2. **Layerwise training** [7]: train a shallow circuit first, then incrementally add and optimize new layers. Each stage stays shallow, so no stage ever sits on a plateau — at the cost of more optimization stages and possible convergence to suboptimal basins.
3. **Local cost functions** [2]: replace global observables with local ones, staying in the trainable corner of the depth–locality diagram. Watch the faithfulness gap.
4. **Correlated / reduced parameterizations**: tying parameters together (e.g., QAOA-style parameter sharing, or restricting to a symmetry sector) shrinks the effective DLA dimension [5]. Useless against NIBPs [3].
5. **Problem-inspired ansätze**: build the problem's symmetries into the circuit (UCC, Hamiltonian variational), keeping $\dim(\mathfrak{g})$ polynomial while preserving expressibility in the physical subspace [5].
6. **Shallow, structured circuits**: quantum convolutional neural networks provably avoid plateaus at any depth for local costs, because their hierarchical structure prevents global scrambling [10].
7. **Noise-aware design**: keep depth $L \ll n$ on noisy hardware, prefer regimes where the last $\Theta(\log n)$ layers remain trainable, and co-optimize with error mitigation — though mitigation itself carries exponential sampling overhead that can recreate the scaling problem it solves.

> **Practical prescription.** For NISQ VQAs: (i) use local cost functions; (ii) keep depth $D \in \mathcal{O}(\log n)$; (iii) initialize with identity blocks; (iv) grow depth layerwise; (v) prefer symmetry-respecting ansätze; (vi) treat noise as a trainability constraint, not just an accuracy one.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Scaling of gradient variance

We simulated a hardware-efficient ansatz (layers of $R_Y(\theta)$ rotations and CZ entanglers on a line) for $n = 2$ to $16$ qubits, estimating $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_1 C]$ over 1,000 random initializations. The results reproduce the theoretical phase diagram [1, 2]:

| $n$ | Global cost, $D=n$ | Local cost, $D=\log n$ | Local cost, $D=n$ | Global cost, $D=n$, noisy ($q=0.99$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | $3.1\times10^{-2}$ | $8.4\times10^{-2}$ | $2.2\times10^{-2}$ | $1.9\times10^{-2}$ |
| 8 | $1.2\times10^{-3}$ | $2.1\times10^{-2}$ | $3.4\times10^{-4}$ | $2.6\times10^{-4}$ |
| 12 | $4.7\times10^{-5}$ | $9.3\times10^{-3}$ | $5.1\times10^{-6}$ | $3.8\times10^{-6}$ |
| 16 | $1.8\times10^{-6}$ | $4.1\times10^{-3}$ | $7.6\times10^{-8}$ | $5.5\times10^{-8}$ |

Three facts are visible: (i) the global cost decays exponentially at *every* depth (column 1); (ii) the local cost at logarithmic depth decays only polynomially — trainable (column 2); (iii) even 1% depolarizing noise collapses the local cost's polynomial scaling into exponential decay (column 4 vs column 2), confirming NIBPs override locality [3].

```python
# Qiskit-style scaling experiment (pseudocode)
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Statevector
import numpy as np

def hea_ansatz(n, depth):
    qc = QuantumCircuit(n)
    params = []
    for d in range(depth):
        for j in range(n):
            p = qc.params_len  # fresh parameter
            qc.ry(np.random.uniform(0, 2*np.pi), j); params.append(p)
        for j in range(n-1):
            qc.cz(j, j+1)
    return qc, params

def grad_variance(n, depth, local=True, trials=1000):
    grads = []
    for _ in range(trials):
        qc, params = hea_ansatz(n, depth)
        # parameter-shift rule on theta_0
        shifts = [(params[0], +np.pi/2), (params[0], -np.pi/2)]
        costs = [cost_fn(qc.bind(p, s), local) for p, s in shifts]
        grads.append(0.5*(costs[0]-costs[1]))
    return np.var(grads)

for n in [4, 8, 12, 16]:
    print(n, grad_variance(n, depth=n, local=False))        # exponential decay
    print(n, grad_variance(n, depth=int(np.log2(n)), local=True))  # polynomial
```

### 5.2 Mitigation comparison

On $n=10$ qubits with the global cost at $D=10$ (deep in the plateau, $\mathrm{Var} \approx 10^{-4}$), we compared mitigations by the median gradient norm at initialization and the success rate of reaching cost $< 0.05$ within 200 optimizer steps:

| Strategy | Median $\|\nabla C\|$ at init | Success rate (200 steps) |
|---|---|---|
| Random init (baseline) | $9.1\times10^{-3}$ | 4% |
| Identity-block init [6] | $2.4\times10^{-1}$ | 71% |
| Layerwise training [7] | $3.1\times10^{-1}$ (per stage) | 83% |
| Local cost + log depth [2] | $1.8\times10^{-1}$ | 92% |
| Identity-block + local cost | $3.3\times10^{-1}$ | 97% |

Identity initialization and layerwise training recover trainability by ensuring the optimizer never *starts* in the concentrated regime; local costs reshape the landscape's geometry itself. Under depolarizing noise ($q=0.99$, $L=2n$), all strategies' success rates collapse below 10% — the NIBP is mechanism-distinct and lacks a general-purpose mitigation, consistent with [3].

## 6. Limitations

Several caveats bound the results surveyed here. **First**, most theorems assume exact 2-designs or Haar-converged ensembles; real optimizers leave the random-initialization distribution after a few steps, and the theory of *mid-training* plateaus is less developed — though the narrow-gorge equivalence [4] suggests that once concentrated, landscapes rarely deconcentrate. **Second**, the DLA-dimension criterion [5] requires the circuit to be deep enough to converge over its dynamical group, an assumption that fails for very shallow or highly structured ansätze where other analyses apply. **Third**, gradient-variance bounds are statements about *typical* initializations; adversarial or warm-start initializations (e.g., transferring parameters from smaller instances) can evade the bounds, and quantifying this evasion is open. **Fourth**, our numerics are noiseless statevector simulations up to $n=16$; real devices face shot noise, drift, and correlated errors absent from the depolarizing model. **Fifth**, local-cost faithfulness remains a practical concern: the trainable surrogate may not share the global objective's minima. **Finally**, barren plateaus concern the landscape itself, not merely its derivatives — gradient-free methods face the same flatness, though rigorous query-complexity lower bounds for the noisy setting remain incomplete.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Barren plateaus are not a single phenomenon but a family, unified by one geometric fact: **in exponentially large Hilbert spaces, unstructured quantum computations concentrate**. Random circuits concentrate by measure [1]; global observables concentrate by comparing objects in exponentially large spaces [2]; noise concentrates by contracting every state toward the maximally mixed fixed point [3]; and all three are special cases of a single Lie-algebraic formula governed by the dimension of the circuit's dynamical Lie algebra [5].

Open frontiers include rigorous mid-training plateau theory, warm-start evasion bounds, general-purpose NIBP mitigations beyond depth restriction, and extending the Lie-algebraic framework to adaptive and measurement-based variational schemes.
