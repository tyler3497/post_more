# Selective State Space Models Beyond Attention: HiPPO Polynomial Memory, Input-Dependent Gating in Mamba, Associative Parallel Scans, and the Structured State Space Duality of Mamba-2

## Abstract

Structured state space models (SSMs) have emerged as the most credible linear-time alternative to the Transformer's quadratic attention, but only after resolving a fundamental deficiency: classical SSMs are linear time-invariant (LTI) systems and therefore incapable of *content-based* reasoning over discrete token streams. This thesis traces the full intellectual arc from HiPPO's theory of online polynomial memory compression, through S4's structured parameterization that made LTI-SSM training practical, to Mamba's selective state spaces — where the input, output, and discretization parameters become *functions of the input itself* — and culminates in Mamba-2's structured state space duality (SSD) framework, which proves that attention and selective SSMs are two faces of the same semi-separable matrix computation. We derive the discretization identities, the associative parallel scan that makes selective training tractable, and the IO-aware kernel design (in the spirit of FlashAttention) that delivers up to 5× inference throughput with linear scaling to million-token contexts. We survey the empirical evidence — Long Range Arena, selective copying, associative recall, and large-scale language modeling — and close with the framework's limitations and open research directions in hardware-software co-design for long-context sequence modeling [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1. Introduction

The Transformer architecture dominates modern sequence modeling, yet its core operator — softmax attention — costs *O(L²)* time and memory in sequence length *L*, and its autoregressive inference requires a key-value cache of size *O(L)*. For a decade, recurrent and convolutional alternatives promised linear scaling but consistently underperformed on information-dense modalities such as language [1]. The structured state space model (SSM) lineage — HiPPO, S4, and their descendants — broke this stalemate by combining three ideas: a principled theory of *continuous-time memory* as online function approximation, a structured parameterization of the state matrix that admits near-linear-time training, and ultimately a *selection mechanism* that lets the model decide, per input token, what to remember and what to forget.

The central technical claim of the Mamba paper [1] is deceptively simple: the reason LTI SSMs fail at content-based reasoning is that their parameters *(A, B, C, Δ)* are fixed for all inputs. By making the projection matrices *B* and *C* and the step size *Δ* functions of the input *x*, the SSM becomes a data-controlled dynamical system that selectively propagates or discards information — recovering the content-awareness of attention while retaining linear-time inference with *constant* state size. The price of this generality is the loss of the convolutional training mode used by S4; the remedy is a hardware-aware parallel scan algorithm that keeps the computation memory-bound-optimal on modern accelerators.

Mamba-2 [2] then completes the circle: the *state space duality* (SSD) framework shows that selective SSMs and (linearized) attention are instances of one unified computation — multiplication by a structured *semi-separable* matrix — yielding an architecture with multi-head structure, 2–8× faster kernels than Mamba, and a state size up to 8× larger. This thesis develops the mathematics, systems, and empirics of this trajectory in full.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Continuous-time state space models

A state space model maps a scalar input signal *u(t)* to an output *y(t)* through a latent state *x(t) ∈ ℝ^N*:

> **Definition (Continuous-time SSM):**
> *x′(t) = A x(t) + B u(t)*, *y(t) = C x(t)*, with *A ∈ ℝ^{N×N}*, *B ∈ ℝ^{N×1}*, *C ∈ ℝ^{1×N}*.

Deep sequence models discretize this system with a step size *Δ > 0*. Under the zero-order-hold (ZOH) assumption that *u(t)* is constant over each interval, the discrete recurrence is

*x_k = Ā x_{k−1} + B̄ u_k*, *y_k = C x_k*,

with *Ā = exp(ΔA)* and *B̄ = (ΔA)^{−1}(exp(ΔA) − I)·ΔB*. A crucial algebraic fact is that this discrete system admits **three equivalent views** [4][6]: (i) the recurrent view above, with *O(1)* per-step cost and constant memory at inference; (ii) the convolutional view *y = u ∗ K̄* with kernel *K̄ = (CB̄, CĀB̄, …, CĀ^{L−1}B̄)*, enabling *O(L log L)* FFT-based parallel training; and (iii) the continuous-time view of the underlying ODE.

### 2.2 HiPPO: memory as online function approximation

Before S4, Gu et al. [3] introduced **HiPPO** (High-order Polynomial Projection Operators), a framework for compressing a continuous signal *f(t)* into a fixed-size memory by projecting its history onto an orthogonal polynomial basis with respect to a measure *μ(t)* that weights the past:

> **Theorem (HiPPO):** For a given measure *μ* on the history, the coefficients *c(t)* minimizing the online function approximation error satisfy a linear ODE *dc/dt = A c + B f(t)* whose matrices *A, B* are determined in closed form by *μ* and the basis.

The flagship instantiation, **HiPPO-LegS** (scaled Legendre), uses the scaled measure *μ^{(t)} = (1/t)·1_{[0,t]}*, which assigns uniform weight to all history and is *timescale-robust* — no prior on sequence length is required [3]. Its state matrix has the closed form

- *A_{nk} = −(2n+1)^{1/2}(2k+1)^{1/2}* for *n > k*,
- *A_{nn} = −(n+1)*,
- *A_{nk} = +(2n+1)^{1/2}(2k+1)^{1/2}* for *n < k*,

a structure whose eigenvalues have negative real parts (stability) and whose dynamics optimally track the Legendre coefficients of the full input history [3]. HiPPO-LegS set a new state of the art on permuted MNIST (98.3%) and, crucially, supplied S4 and Mamba with their **initialization**: the *A* matrix is initialized to the HiPPO-LegS matrix (or a normal-plus-low-rank approximation of it) so that the model starts training with provably optimal memory dynamics rather than random recurrency.

### 2.3 S4: making LTI SSMs trainable

The Linear State Space Layer (LSSL) [6] and its successor **S4** [4] showed how to train deep SSMs efficiently. The innovation is parameterization: writing the HiPPO matrix as *diagonal plus low-rank* (DPLR),

*A = Λ − P Q^\* ∈ ℂ^{N×N}*,

which permits computing the convolution kernel *K̄* in *Õ(N + L)* operations via the Cauchy kernel identity, rather than the naive *O(N²L)*. Concretely, the generating function *K̂(z) = C̃ (I − zĀ)^{−1} B̃* is evaluated at roots of unity using a Cauchy matrix-vector product, then inverted by FFT. S4 achieved ~86% average on the **Long Range Arena** benchmark, dramatically outperforming prior efficient-Transformer variants, and S4D [5] later showed that a purely *diagonal* complex *A* recovers most of S4's performance with a far simpler implementation.

The remaining weakness was theoretical and empirical: LTI systems cannot perform *content-based* reasoning. An LTI SSM treats every token identically; it cannot decide to ignore an uninformative token or amplify a salient one. The Mamba authors demonstrated this with the **selective copying** task — a sequence containing a sparse subset of tokens to be memorized amid noise — on which LTI models fail regardless of scale [1].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The selection mechanism (S6)

Mamba's core methodological move is to drop time-invariance. Given input *x_t ∈ ℝ^D*, the SSM parameters become input-dependent projections [1]:

```python
# Selective SSM (S6) -- per-timestep parameterization (Gu & Dao, 2023)
B_t = s_B(x_t)                   # R^D -> R^N   (input projection, selective)
C_t = s_C(x_t)                   # R^D -> R^N   (output projection, selective)
Delta_t = softplus(s_Delta(x_t)) # R^D -> R^D   (step size, selective)
# A stays input-independent (diagonal, initialized from HiPPO/S4D)
A_bar_t = exp(Delta_t * A)       # ZOH discretization of state matrix
B_bar_t = Delta_t * B_t          # simplified Euler form used in the official kernel
h_t = A_bar_t * h_{t-1} + B_bar_t * x_t
y_t = C_t @ h_t
```

Three consequences follow. First, *Δ_t* acts as a **learned gate**: large *Δ_t* resets the state to focus on the current input, while small *Δ_t* preserves the state and ignores the input — formally generalizing the gating of GRUs [1][3]. Second, input-dependent *B_t* and *C_t* let the model decide *what to write* into the state and *what to read* from it, mirroring the query/key/value routing of attention. Third, the system is no longer LTI, so the convolutional training mode of S4 is unavailable: training must proceed in recurrent mode.

### 3.2 The Mamba block

Mamba embeds the selective SSM in a simplified block: the input is expanded by a factor *E* (typically 2), passed through a short causal depthwise convolution and SiLU activation, projected to *(Δ, B, C)*, run through the selective scan, and then multiplied by a SiLU-gated branch before a final linear projection [1]. Notably, the architecture contains **neither attention nor MLP blocks** — it is a homogeneous stack of selective SSM blocks with residual connections and normalization.

### 3.3 The SSD framework (Mamba-2)

Mamba-2 [2] reframes selective SSMs as **structured matrix multiplication**. The sequence map *y = SSM(A, B, C)(x)* can be written as *y = M x*, where *M* is a *semi-separable* (sequentially semiseparable) matrix of rank bounded by the state size *N*:

*M_{ji} = C_j^\top A_{j:i} B_i* for *j ≥ i*,

with *A_{j:i} = A_j ··· A_{i+1}* the transition product. Dao and Gu prove a duality: this is precisely **1-semi-separable masked attention** — linear attention whose scalar mask is generated by the SSM's *Δ* and *A* parameters [2]. Under this duality, the quadratic "attention form" and the linear "recurrent form" are related by the *chunkwise* decomposition: split the sequence into chunks, compute intra-chunk contributions with the quadratic form (tensor-core friendly) and inter-chunk contributions with the linear recurrence, exactly as in FlashAttention's tiling [2]. Mamba-2 exploits this to add **multi-head structure** (heads analogous to attention heads), increase the state size (e.g., *N = 64 → 128/256*), and simplify the kernel — yielding 2–8× speedups over Mamba at equal state size [2].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why selectivity is necessary: the selective copying task

The selective copying benchmark constructs sequences of length *L* (e.g., 4096) in which only *K ≪ L* tokens (e.g., 16) are payload to be reproduced, with positions randomized and the rest filled with noise tokens [1]. LTI SSMs — S4, H3, Hyena — achieve near-zero accuracy even when the sequence is shortened, because a time-invariant filter cannot distinguish payload from noise: it must allocate state capacity to *all* tokens equally. Mamba with selectivity achieves ~100% accuracy and extrapolates to 2× training length. The lesson is crisp: **content-awareness requires input-dependent dynamics**, and the selective SSM is the minimal such generalization of the LTI model [1].

### 4.2 Associative parallel scans: training without convolution

The selective recurrence *h_t = Ā_t h_{t−1} + B̄_t x_t* is a first-order linear recurrence with time-varying coefficients. Such recurrences admit a **parallel associative scan**: define the binary operator on pairs *(a, b)* as *(a₁, b₁) ⊗ (a₂, b₂) = (a₂a₁, a₂b₁ + b₂)*, which is associative, so a Blelloch-style work-efficient scan computes all prefixes in *O(L)* work and *O(log L)* depth [1]. This replaces the FFT convolution path entirely.

```python
# Associative scan for the selective SSM recurrence (conceptual)
# elements e_t = (A_bar_t, B_bar_t * x_t); combine with the ⊗ operator
def combine(e_left, e_right):
    a1, b1 = e_left
    a2, b2 = e_right
    return (a2 * a1, a2 * b1 + b2)   # associative: scan yields all h_t
h_all = associative_scan(combine, elements)  # O(L) work, O(log L) depth
```

The scan is *work-efficient* and maps naturally to GPU thread blocks, but its practical speed hinges on memory traffic — the subject of the next section.

### 4.3 Hardware-aware kernel design: the IO-aware selective scan

Modern GPU computation is **memory-bound**, not compute-bound: HBM bandwidth is the bottleneck, while SRAM is an order of magnitude faster but tiny (tens of MB per SM). The Mamba kernel, in the spirit of FlashAttention, is designed around this reality [1]:

1. **No materialization of the expanded state.** The recurrent state has shape *(B, L, D, N)* — for typical *D = 2048, N = 16*, this is ~16× the input size. Instead of writing it to HBM, the kernel loads *(Δ, A, B, C)* into SRAM, performs the discretization and scan *on-chip*, and writes only the *(B, L, D)*-shaped output back.
2. **Kernel fusion.** Discretization (*Ā = exp(ΔA)*), the scan, and multiplication by *C* are fused into a single kernel, eliminating round-trips to HBM between stages.
3. **Recomputation in the backward pass.** Intermediate states needed for gradients are recomputed on-chip from stored inputs rather than stashed in HBM, trading cheap FLOPs for expensive bandwidth.

The result is a scan whose HBM traffic is *O(BLD)* — the same order as the input itself — despite the recurrence's nominally *O(BLDN)* footprint. This systems decision is as important as the mathematics: without it, selective SSMs would be a theoretical curiosity [1].

### 4.4 State space duality: attention as a special case

The SSD framework's deepest result is the identification of selective SSMs with a structured attention [2]. Writing the recurrence's unrolling as a matrix gives the **semi-separable form** above; constraining the state transition to a *scalar* per step (*A_t = a_t I*) reduces the model to linear attention with a data-controlled scalar mask — the "1-SS" case — while the general matrix *A_t* yields multi-dimensional state dynamics with no attention equivalent. The duality has three practical payoffs:

| Property | Mamba (S6) | Mamba-2 (SSD) |
|---|---|---|
| Parameterization | single-head, per-channel *Δ* | multi-head, per-head *Δ, A, B, C* |
| State size *N* | 16 (typical) | 64–256 (8× larger) |
| Kernel strategy | parallel associative scan | chunkwise: quadratic intra-chunk + linear inter-chunk |
| Attention synergy | none | blocks interchangeable with MHA; tensor-parallel friendly |
| Reported speedup | baseline | 2–8× vs Mamba at equal *N* [2] |

Because SSD's matrix form exposes the same tiling structure as FlashAttention, Mamba-2 inherits attention's systems maturity — tensor parallelism, head sharding, and mixed SSD/attention hybrid models — while keeping linear-time generation with a *constant-size* state [2].

### 4.5 Long-context behavior and the discretization gate

The step size *Δ_t* also governs **context utilization**. Since *Ā_t = exp(Δ_t A)* with *A* negative (HiPPO-initialized), small *Δ_t* yields *Ā_t ≈ I* (perfect memory retention), while large *Δ_t* yields rapid forgetting. Empirically, trained Mamba models learn a bimodal *Δ* distribution: most tokens receive tiny *Δ* (the state coasts through filler), while salient tokens trigger large *Δ* (state reset and write) [1]. This is the mechanism behind Mamba's demonstrated perplexity improvements on real data at **million-token** sequence lengths — the model has learned, end-to-end, an input-adaptive memory hierarchy with no explicit cache eviction policy.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Synthetic reasoning tasks

- **Selective copying** (length 4096, 16 payload tokens): Mamba achieves ~100% accuracy with 2× length extrapolation; all LTI baselines (S4, H3, Hyena, RWKV) fail [1].
- **Induction heads**: Mamba solves the synthetic induction-head task used to probe in-context copying, on which prior SSMs struggled [1].
- **Associative recall (MQAR)**: selective SSMs match attention-based models on multi-query associative recall, the canonical test of content-based lookup [1].

### 5.2 Long Range Arena and genomics

S4's LRA average of ~86% established SSMs as the architecture to beat on 1K–16K-length classification [4]. Mamba extends the advantage to *discrete* modalities: on language modeling, Mamba-3B **outperforms Transformers of the same size and matches Transformers twice its size** in both pretraining perplexity and downstream evaluations [1]. In genomics, Mamba-based models handle DNA sequences at single-nucleotide resolution over very long contexts, where attention's quadratic cost is prohibitive [1].

### 5.3 Throughput and scaling

Mamba reports **5× higher generation throughput** than comparable Transformers, with prefill scaling linearly in *L* and near-constant decode latency — the recurrent state is fixed-size, so per-token cost does not grow with context [1]. Mamba-2's SSD kernels add a further 2–8× over Mamba at the same state size while supporting 8× larger states, and hybrid SSD+attention models remain competitive with pure Transformers on language modeling [2]. The production `mamba-ssm` package exposes both the selective scan interface and the SSD layer for CUDA-accelerated deployment [7].

### 5.4 Theoretical guarantees

Three proof-level results anchor the framework: (i) HiPPO's optimality — the LegS matrices are the *exact* solution of the online function approximation problem for the scaled measure, with bounded gradients and timescale robustness [3]; (ii) S4's complexity — the DPLR parameterization computes the SSM convolution kernel in *Õ(N + L)* via the Cauchy-kernel trick [4]; (iii) SSD duality — the selective SSM equals multiplication by a semi-separable matrix, i.e., masked linear attention with a data-dependent mask, with the recurrent, convolutional, and chunkwise algorithms as three exact realizations of the same operator [2].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Expressivity vs. attention.** The 1-SS case is provably a form of *linear* attention with scalar masks; it cannot express the full pairwise *softmax(QKᵀ)* interactions that let Transformers implement arbitrary associative lookups in a single layer. Tasks requiring fine-grained token-to-token routing may still favor attention, which is why hybrid SSD+attention models exist [2].
2. **State capacity bottleneck.** Even Mamba-2's *N = 256* state is a fixed-size summary; information-theoretically, exact recall of *K* arbitrary tokens requires *Ω(K)* state. Selectivity mitigates but does not eliminate this bound — it is a compression scheme, not a cache.
3. **Training systems complexity.** The hardware-aware kernels are bespoke CUDA; porting to new accelerators (TPUs, custom ASICs) requires re-engineering the SRAM tiling, unlike the comparatively portable attention implementations. Mamba-2's simpler chunkwise algorithm partially addresses this [2].
4. **Ecosystem immaturity.** Fine-tuning recipes, quantization, speculative decoding, and serving infrastructure for SSMs lag Transformers by years. Hybrid architectures ease adoption but complicate the stack.
5. **Evaluation at extreme lengths.** "Million-token" results remain concentrated in perplexity and synthetic retrieval; rigorous long-context *reasoning* benchmarks (multi-hop QA, agentic rollouts) for SSMs are still catching up to the Transformer literature.

## 7. Conclusion

The trajectory from HiPPO to Mamba-2 is a case study in co-designing theory, algorithms, and hardware. HiPPO [3] supplied the *principle* — memory as optimal online polynomial approximation — and the *initialization* that makes deep SSMs trainable. S4 [4] and S4D [5] supplied the *structured parameterization* that made LTI training efficient. Mamba [1] identified the missing ingredient — *input-dependent selection* — and paired it with an IO-aware parallel scan that made non-LTI training practical, achieving Transformer-class language modeling with linear scaling and 5× inference throughput. Mamba-2 [2] then unified the picture: structured state space duality reveals selective SSMs and attention as two algorithms for the same semi-separable matrix computation, delivering multi-head structure, 8× larger states, and 2–8× faster kernels.

The broader lesson is architectural: *content-awareness* and *linear complexity* are not fundamentally opposed — they are reconciled by making dynamics data-dependent while keeping the state update associative and memory-hierarchy aware. As context lengths push toward tens of millions of tokens, the SSD framework's blend of recurrent efficiency and attention-compatible systems design positions selective state spaces as a durable foundation for the next generation of sequence models.
