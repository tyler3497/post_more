# Federated Learning: Secure Aggregation, Differentially Private Training, and the Communication Bottleneck

## Abstract

Federated learning (FL) trains statistical models across decentralized devices holding local data, without raw data ever leaving the device. This paper gives a rigorous, doctoral-level treatment of the three central tensions that define the field. First, *communication*: we formalize the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm, derive its convergence behavior under local-update regimes, and survey compression operators — quantization (QSGD), sign-based methods (signSGD), and error feedback — with quantitative bandwidth analyses. Second, *secrecy against the aggregator*: we present the Bonawitz et al. secure aggregation protocol in full, showing how pairwise Diffie–Hellman masking with Shamir-secret-shared recovery permits an honest-but-curious server to learn exactly the sum of client updates and nothing more, robust to dropouts. Third, *differential privacy*: we develop DP-FedAvg, adaptive gradient clipping, and the moments-accountant composition framework, and prove how distributed differential privacy (DP-SGD composed with secure aggregation) yields a strictly stronger guarantee than either technique alone. We further analyze the statistical heterogeneity problem (non-IID data), characterize the *client-drift* phenomenon with tight lower bounds, and show how control-variate correction (SCAFFOLD) eliminates it. We conclude with an empirical synthesis across CIFAR and language-modeling benchmarks and a frank accounting of open limitations.

## 1. Introduction

Machine learning has historically assumed the existence of a *data center*: raw observations are logged to a central store, and training is performed on pooled data [1]. This paradigm collapses under the weight of modern edge deployments. Consider a fleet of one billion mobile phones, each holding thousands of text messages, photographs, and keystroke traces. Logging this corpus centrally is infeasible on three independent axes:

1. **Privacy.** User-generated data is often sensitive; exfiltrating it violates reasonable expectations of confidentiality and, increasingly, regulation.
2. **Communication.** Uplink bandwidth on mobile networks is measured in kilobits per second for much of the world; a 100 MB model synchronized thousands of times per device is a non-starter.
3. **Storage.** Retaining exabytes of training data centrally for reproducibility is operationally and economically prohibitive.

*Federated learning*, introduced by McMahan et al. [1], inverts the architecture: instead of bringing the data to the model, bring the model to the data. A coordinator distributes the model to a subset of clients; each computes an update from its local data; the coordinator aggregates and iterates. Raw data never traverses the network. This deceptively simple protocol — **Federated Averaging (FedAvg)** — conceals profound mathematical structure, and three decades of optimization, cryptography, and privacy theory collide in its analysis.

This thesis develops three pillars: **§3** — FedAvg, compression operators, and the communication–computation frontier; **§4** — the Bonawitz et al. secure aggregation protocol in full; **§5** — DP-FedAvg, the moments accountant, and distributed DP; **§6** — heterogeneity, the client-drift lower bound, and the SCAFFOLD correction.

Our thesis claim is the following:

> **Thesis claim.** *Federated learning is the only known architecture that simultaneously respects device data locality, operates within mobile communication budgets, and admits a rigorous, end-to-end privacy analysis. Achieving all three properties requires the joint composition of local-update optimization, cryptographic secure aggregation, and differential privacy — no two of the three suffice.*

The remainder of the paper formalizes the setting (§2), develops the three methodological pillars (§3–§5 plus heterogeneity corrections), takes a deep dive into the interplay of compression, secrecy, and noise (§4's subsections), presents convergence proofs and empirical evidence (§5), discusses limitations (§6), and concludes (§7).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The federated optimization problem

Let $K$ be the number of clients. Client $k$ holds a private dataset $D_k$ of $n_k$ points, with $n = \sum_k n_k$. The global objective is the *weighted empirical risk*:

$$F(w) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n}\, F_k(w), \qquad F_k(w) = \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{d \in D_k} f(w; d).$$

This is standard empirical risk minimization (ERM), except that $F_k$ is accessible *only through* client $k$, and the server may never observe $D_k$ directly. We distinguish **cross-device FL** ($K$ enormous, unreliable clients, tiny participation fractions, strongly non-IID data) from **cross-silo FL** ($K$ small, reliable organizations). This thesis targets cross-device FL, the harder and more consequential regime.

### 2.2 Standard distributed SGD and its cost

Parallel synchronous SGD samples a cohort $S_t$ of clients, each computes a stochastic gradient $g_k = \nabla f(w_t; B_k)$, and the server forms

$$w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta \sum_{k \in S_t} \frac{n_k}{n_S} g_k.$$

Communication per round: each client downloads $d$ parameters (32-bit floats) and uploads $d$ gradient coordinates — $64\, d\, |S_t|$ bits per round. We quantify this in the bandwidth model of §4.1.

### 2.3 Privacy threat model

We assume an *honest-but-curious* server: it follows the protocol but inspects all received messages, potentially inverting gradients to recover training data. Gradient inversion attacks demonstrate that a single gradient of a neural network can reveal its input batch with high fidelity. The server may also collude with a bounded fraction of clients. Goals:

- **Input privacy (cryptographic):** the server learns only the aggregate $\sum_k x_k$, never individual $x_k$ — delivered by secure aggregation [4].
- **Output privacy (statistical):** the final model reveals bounded information about any single client's participation — delivered by differential privacy [5].

### 2.4 Differential privacy: definitions

A randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP if for all adjacent datasets $D, D'$ differing in one record and all measurable $S$:

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \le e^\varepsilon \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta.$$

The *Gaussian mechanism* adds noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ to a query with $\ell_2$-sensitivity $\Delta_2$; the privacy loss random variable's moments can be bounded tightly via the *moments accountant* [5], which yields order-of-magnitude improvements over naive composition for iterative training.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The FedAvg algorithm

The methodology of this thesis is constructive: we build the federated stack from the ground up.

```
Round t: server broadcasts w_t to cohort S_t (|S_t| = m);
each client k in parallel runs E local SGD epochs from w_t,
sends delta_k = y_{k,E} - w_t;
server forms w_{t+1} = w_t + sum_k (n_k / n_S) delta_k.
```

The integer $E$ (local epochs, or equivalently local steps $L$) is the *communication lever*: for $E = 1$ this is parallel SGD; for $E \gg 1$ each round amortizes one download/upload pair over many gradient evaluations [1].

### 3.2 Secure aggregation protocol (Bonawitz et al.)

The server must compute $\sum_k x_k$ where $x_k$ is client $k$'s (quantized) update, learning nothing else. The four-round protocol [4]:

1. **Advertise keys.** Each client generates Diffie–Hellman key pairs $(s_k^{PK}, s_k^{SK})$ and broadcasts public keys via the server.
2. **Share keys.** Each pair $(u, v)$ derives a shared seed $s_{u,v}$. Each client generates a private mask seed $b_u$ and secret-shares both $b_u$ and all $s_{u,v}$ with the other clients via $(t, n)$ Shamir sharing.
3. **Masked input collection.** Client $u$ sends the masked vector

   $$y_u = x_u + \mathrm{PRG}(b_u) + \sum_{v < u} \mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v}) - \sum_{v > u} \mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v}) \pmod p.$$

   Pairwise masks cancel when summed over all clients: $\sum_u y_u = \sum_u x_u + \sum_u \mathrm{PRG}(b_u)$.
4. **Unmasking.** For surviving clients the server reconstructs $b_u$ from $t$ shares and subtracts $\mathrm{PRG}(b_u)$; for dropped clients it reconstructs the pairwise seeds $s_{u,v}$ and adds/subtracts the corresponding masks. The result is exactly $\sum_{u \in U'} x_u$.

Correctness tolerates up to $\frac{1}{3}$ of users failing to complete the protocol [4]; for 16-bit inputs the communication expansion is only $1.73\times$ for $2^{10}$ users and $2^{20}$-dimensional vectors.

### 3.3 Differentially private federated training

DP-FedAvg modifies the round as follows:

1. **Per-example clipping** (or per-update clipping for efficiency): each client's update is clipped to $\ell_2$ norm $C$, bounding sensitivity at $C$.
2. **Noise injection:** the server (or, with distributed DP, the clients in aggregate) adds Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 C^2)$ to the sum.
3. **Accounting:** the moments accountant [5] tracks the cumulative privacy loss $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ over $T$ rounds with sampling amplification from the client subsampling rate $q = m/K$.

Adaptive clipping (tuning $C$ via a differentially private quantile of update norms) removes the need to hand-pick $C$ [5-analogous practice in DP-FedAvg deployments].

### 3.4 Compression operators

A compressor $\mathcal{Q}: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is applied to each upload. Canonical families:

- **QSGD [2]:** stochastic quantization; unbiased, variance bounded by $\|g\|^2$; trades bits per coordinate against convergence rate; ResNet-152 on ImageNet trained $1.8\times$ faster on 16 GPUs.
- **signSGD [3]:** 1 bit per coordinate (the sign), majority-vote aggregation; total cost $2Md$ bits per round vs. $64Md$ for full-precision SGD ($M$ workers, $d$ dimensions) — a **32×** reduction.
- **Error feedback (EF):** compresses the residual $g + e$ and carries the compression error $e_{t+1} = g + e_t - \mathcal{Q}(g + e_t)$ forward; biased compressors such as top-$k$ converge with EF.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The communication–computation frontier of local updates

The central quantitative question of FedAvg: *how large can $E$ be before local updates diverge from the global optimum?* For IID data and smooth objectives, Stich's analysis shows local SGD converges with rate

$$\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(\bar{w}_T)\|^2 = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{KET}}\right)$$

in the nonconvex case, meaning rounds $T$ can be cut proportionally to $KE$ with no loss [1, 6]. The product $KET$ is the *total gradient budget*; FedAvg spends the budget locally rather than in synchronized rounds.

A concrete bandwidth model clarifies the stakes. Let $d = 10^8$ parameters, 32-bit floats, $m = 10^3$ clients per round, $T = 2000$ rounds:

| Scheme | Bits per round (up+down) | Rounds | Total traffic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sync SGD ($E=1$) | $64 \cdot 10^8 \cdot 10^3 = 6.4 \times 10^{12}$ | 2000 | $1.28 \times 10^{16}$ |
| FedAvg ($E=20$) | $64 \cdot 10^8 \cdot 10^3$ | 100 | $6.4 \times 10^{14}$ |
| FedAvg + signSGD | $2 \cdot 10^8 \cdot 10^3 = 2 \times 10^{11}$ | 150 | $3.0 \times 10^{13}$ |
| FedAvg + SecAgg (16-bit) | $\approx 1.73 \times 32 \cdot 10^8 \cdot 10^3$ | 100 | $5.5 \times 10^{14}$ |

The table shows the compounding structure: *fewer rounds* (local updates) multiplies with *cheaper rounds* (compression), while secure aggregation adds a modest, bounded cryptographic tax ($\approx 1.73\times$ for 16-bit quantized inputs [4]).

> **Theorem 1 (Informal convergence of local SGD).** *Under $L$-smoothness, bounded variance $\sigma^2$, and $\mu$-strong convexity, FedAvg with local steps $E$ and learning rate $\eta \le 1/(8LE)$ achieves $\mathbb{E}[F(\bar w_T)] - F^\star = \mathcal{O}(1/KET) + \mathcal{O}(\eta E \sigma^2)$ on IID data — matching parallel SGD up to the residual heterogeneity term, which vanishes when client objectives coincide [6].*

### 4.2 Secure aggregation, in full detail

We now prove the two properties that make Bonawitz et al. [4] the practical foundation of private FL.

**Mask cancellation.** Summing the masked vectors over all participating clients $U$:

$$\sum_{u \in U} y_u = \sum_u x_u + \sum_u \mathrm{PRG}(b_u) + \sum_{u}\sum_{v<u}\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v}) - \sum_{u}\sum_{v>u}\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v}).$$

Each unordered pair $\{u,v\}$ contributes $\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v})$ once positively and once negatively, so all pairwise masks cancel exactly, leaving $\sum_u x_u + \sum_u \mathrm{PRG}(b_u)$, from which the server removes the reconstructible self-masks. ∎

**Dropout recovery.** If client $u$ drops before sending $y_u$, its pairwise masks survive inside other clients' masked vectors; because $s_{u,v}$ was $(t,n)$-secret-shared, any $t$ survivors reconstruct $s_{u,v}$ and the server adjusts accordingly. Crucially, $b_u$ is *not* reconstructed for dropouts, since their $y_u$ was never received — this asymmetry is the protocol's key design insight [4].

> **Security guarantee.** *In the honest-but-curious model with fewer than $t$ colluding clients, the server's view is simulatable given only $\sum_{u \in U'} x_u$; i.e., it learns nothing about any individual $x_u$ beyond what the sum reveals [4].*

### 4.3 Differential privacy accounting: the moments accountant

Training runs $T$ rounds; naive composition would give $\varepsilon = \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}\cdot \varepsilon_0)$ only under strong composition, or $\mathcal{O}(T)$ naively. The moments accountant [5] instead tracks, for the privacy loss random variable $c(o) = \log \frac{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)=o]}{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')=o]}$,

$$\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda) = \max_{D,D'} \log \mathbb{E}_{o \sim \mathcal{M}(D)}\left[e^{\lambda c(o)}\right],$$

the *log moment generating function*. Composition becomes addition: $\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{1:T}}(\lambda) = \sum_t \alpha_{\mathcal{M}_t}(\lambda)$, and conversion to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP is via the tail bound $\delta = \min_\lambda \exp(\alpha(\lambda) - \lambda \varepsilon)$. Combined with *privacy amplification by subsampling* (each round touches each client with probability $q = m/K \ll 1$), the moments accountant yields the tightest known bounds for DP-SGD [5].

**Distributed DP.** When secure aggregation is in place, clients add discrete Gaussian noise shares whose sum equals the desired $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$; the server never sees noiseless individual updates. The composition is *defense in depth*: cryptographic secrecy defeats the curious server; DP defeats membership-inference and reconstruction attacks against the released model.

### 4.4 Client drift and the SCAFFOLD correction

The pathology. On heterogeneous data, client $k$'s local optimum $\arg\min F_k$ differs from the global $\arg\min F$. With many local steps, each client drifts toward its own optimum; the average of drifted models can diverge from the global optimum *even with full-batch gradients and exact averaging*. Karimireddy et al. [6] prove this is fundamental: FedAvg's convergence rate must contain a term scaling with the heterogeneity

$$\zeta^2 = \max_k \|\nabla F_k(w^\star) - \nabla F(w^\star)\|^2,$$

and they exhibit lower bounds showing the dependence is tight.

**SCAFFOLD** [6] eliminates drift with control variates. Each client maintains $c_k$ estimating its local gradient direction; the server maintains $c = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k c_k$. The local update becomes

$$y \leftarrow y - \eta_\ell \big(g_k(y) - c_k + c\big).$$

The correction $(c - c_k)$ estimates the *drift*: when client gradients are similar ($c_k \approx c$), the method reduces to FedAvg and exploits local steps fully; when they differ, the variate recenters each client's trajectory on the global direction. Karimireddy et al. prove SCAFFOLD requires strictly fewer communication rounds than FedAvg under heterogeneity and is unaffected by client sampling [6] — the first variance-reduction method for federated optimization.

### 4.5 The trilemma: utility, privacy, and communication

We can now state the governing trade-off quantitatively. Per communication round, with model dimension $d$, cohort size $m$, clipping norm $C$, noise multiplier $z = \sigma/C$, quantization bits $b$ per coordinate, and $T$ total rounds:

$$\underbrace{\varepsilon(T, q, z)}_{\text{privacy cost}} \;\; \text{vs.} \;\; \underbrace{b \cdot d \cdot m \cdot T}_{\text{communication cost}} \;\; \text{vs.} \;\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{mT}} + \frac{\sqrt{d}\,z\,C}{\sqrt{mT}} + \zeta\text{-terms}}_{\text{optimization error}}.$$

Three observations of thesis-level importance:

1. **Noise hurts relatively less at scale.** The DP error term shrinks with $\sqrt{m}$ (more clients per round average the noise away) — *federated scale is privacy's friend*.
2. **Compression and DP noise interact.** Stochastic quantization adds variance comparable to a small increase in the DP noise multiplier; error feedback largely neutralizes this interaction.
3. **Secure aggregation is the linchpin.** Without it, per-client noise must protect each update individually ($\sigma \propto C\sqrt{m}$); with it, noise need only protect the *sum* ($\sigma \propto C$). Secure aggregation thus buys a $\sqrt{m}$ reduction in required noise — the central quantitative argument for composing cryptography with differential privacy.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: why local steps help (and when they do not)

We sketch the core argument behind the communication savings [1, 6]. Assume each $F_k$ is $L$-smooth and the stochastic gradients have variance $\sigma^2$. Define the *client drift* after $E$ local steps as $\Delta_k = \|y_{k,E} - w_t + \eta E \nabla F(w_t)\|$.

**Lemma.** *$\mathbb{E}[\Delta_k^2] \le \mathcal{O}(\eta^2 E^2 (\zeta^2 + \sigma^2))$, where $\zeta$ measures data heterogeneity.*

*Proof sketch.* Each local step contracts toward the client's own gradient $\nabla F_k$ rather than $\nabla F$. Telescoping the $E$ steps, the deviation decomposes into a heterogeneity term (bounded by $\zeta$) and a stochastic term (bounded by $\sigma$); squaring and summing gives the claim. ∎

Choosing $\eta = \mathcal{O}(1/(LE))$ keeps drift bounded, and the standard SGD analysis on the averaged pseudo-gradient yields the $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{KET})$ rate [6]. The *tightness* of the $\zeta$-dependence is established by an explicit quadratic lower-bound construction in [6].

### 5.2 Empirical evidence

**FedAvg communication savings.** McMahan et al. [1] train a CNN on MNIST (non-IID, pathological label skew) and an LSTM on Shakespeare text: FedAvg with $E = 20$–$50$ local epochs reaches target accuracy in **34–90× fewer rounds** than FedSGD ($E=1$) — the canonical 10–100× figure.

**Compression.** Alistarh et al. [2] (QSGD) train ResNet-152 on ImageNet with 2–4 bit quantization: full accuracy preserved with $1.8\times$ end-to-end speedup. Bernstein et al. [3] (signSGD, majority vote): matches full-precision SGD accuracy on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet transmitting 1 bit per coordinate.

**Privacy–utility.** DP-FedAvg [7] on Federated EMNIST: with $\varepsilon \approx 8$, $\delta = 10^{-5}$, and $m = 100$ clients/round, accuracy drops only $\approx 2$–$3$ points versus non-private FedAvg; the moments accountant [5] makes the $\varepsilon$ bookkeeping feasible at $T = 1500$ rounds, where naive composition would report $\varepsilon$ in the hundreds.

**Heterogeneity.** Karimireddy et al. [6] on EMNIST with sorted (pathological non-IID) partitions: FedAvg stalls at $\approx 60\%$ accuracy while SCAFFOLD reaches $\approx 85\%$, and SCAFFOLD's round complexity is provably independent of the heterogeneity constant $\zeta$.

### 5.3 The composition theorem (informal)

> **Theorem 2 (Informal).** *Consider $T$ rounds of FedAvg with per-round cohort sampling probability $q$, per-update clipping norm $C$, client-added distributed Gaussian noise of total variance $\sigma^2 C^2$, and aggregation via the Bonawitz et al. protocol [4]. Then: (i) the server learns nothing beyond the noisy aggregate in the honest-but-curious model; (ii) the released model sequence is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP at the client level with $\varepsilon$ computed by the moments accountant [5]; (iii) per-round communication is $\mathcal{O}(b\, d\, m)$ for a $b$-bit compressor [2, 3]; (iv) with SCAFFOLD correction the optimization error is independent of heterogeneity $\zeta$ [6].*

This is the formal content of our thesis claim: each cited result supplies one clause, and their composition is the system.

## 6. Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires enumerating what the above does *not* achieve.

1. **Malicious clients.** Secure aggregation protects against an honest-but-curious *server*, not Byzantine clients poisoning the sum. Robust aggregation (median, Krum) is nonlinear and breaks mask cancellation — a fundamental tension. Byzantine-robust *and* private federated aggregation remains largely open.

2. **The last-mile privacy gap.** Secure aggregation hides individual updates from the server, but the *noisy aggregate itself* is visible, and DP's guarantee is statistical, not cryptographic. Membership inference against the final model is bounded by $(\varepsilon,\delta)$, not eliminated.

3. **Client-level vs. example-level DP.** Our analysis is at *client granularity*. Example-level DP is stronger but requires per-example clipping on device — computationally heavy and demanding distributed noise at finer granularity.

4. **Non-IID theory beyond quadratics.** SCAFFOLD's heterogeneity-independence is proven for convex objectives; nonconvex rates retain residual dependence on heterogeneity measures. Real deployments (next-word prediction across dialects) are nonconvex and severely non-IID.

5. **System constraints.** The four-round SecAgg protocol [4] multiplies round-trip latency; with $E$ local epochs of computation on weak devices, stragglers dominate wall-clock time. FedProx-style proximal terms and asynchronous protocols mitigate but do not eliminate this.

6. **Quantization–DP tension.** Clipping bounds sensitivity, but aggressive quantization coarsens the very norm estimates clipping relies on; adaptive clipping under secure aggregation requires a separate DP quantile-estimation subprotocol, adding rounds.

7. **Fairness.** Optimizing the weighted average $F(w)$ can sacrifice minority clients with atypical data. Agnostic federated learning (minimax over client mixtures) addresses this at the cost of harder optimization.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Federated learning reframes machine learning as a *distributed systems* problem with cryptographic and statistical constraints, not merely an optimization problem. Local-update optimization (FedAvg [1], corrected by SCAFFOLD [6]) solves the communication bottleneck; secure aggregation [4] supplies cryptographic input privacy; differential privacy with tight accounting [5] supplies statistical output privacy. Quantitatively, secure aggregation is the enabler: it converts per-client noise into per-sum noise, buying a $\sqrt{m}$ utility improvement that makes client-level DP practical at scale. The open frontiers — Byzantine robustness compatible with mask cancellation, nonconvex heterogeneity theory, and fairness — are precisely the problems a doctoral research program in this area should attack next.
