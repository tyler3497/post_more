# Persistent Homology and Topological Data Analysis: Filtrations, Stability Theorems, and the Computation of Barcodes at Scale

## Abstract

Topological Data Analysis (TDA) extracts geometric structure — connected components, loops, cavities — from finite, noisy, high-dimensional data. Its central instrument, **persistent homology**, turns a one-parameter family of nested simplicial complexes, a *filtration*, into an algebraic summary, the *barcode* or *persistence diagram*, recording the birth and death of each homological feature. This thesis develops the subject end to end. We formalize filtrations of Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and alpha complexes and the persistence module functor; prove the **structure theorem** that decomposes tame persistence modules into interval summands; present the standard **matrix-reduction algorithm** of Zomorodian–Carlsson and its modern optimizations (cohomology reduction, clearing, apparent pairs); and establish the **stability theorems** — bottleneck stability of Cohen–Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer, Wasserstein stability, and the interleaving-isometry framework — that legitimize persistent homology as a robust descriptor of noisy data. Finally, we survey the engineering of barcode computation at scale: Ripser, GUDHI, Ripser++, and vectorizations (persistence landscapes, persistence images) that interface barcodes with machine learning. Empirical benchmarks and a worked proof sketch show where the theory is airtight, where heuristics dominate, and where multiparameter persistence still resists a complete classification.

## 1. Introduction

Modern data rarely arrive as clean samples from low-dimensional manifolds. They arrive as *point clouds*: millions of points in high dimensions, corrupted by noise, sampling artifacts, and missing values. Classical geometry — curvature, geodesics, volume — is brittle under such corruption. **Topology** offers a coarser but far more robust language: whether data form one connected cluster or two, whether a periodic process traces a loop, whether a sensor network covers a region without holes. These are questions about *homological features*, and their answers persist only if the scale at which we inspect the data is chosen wisely.

**Persistent homology** (PH) replaces the choice of a single scale with a *filtration*: a nested sequence of spaces indexed by a real parameter. As the parameter grows, features appear (are *born*) and disappear (*die*). The multiset of birth–death pairs — the *barcode* or *persistence diagram* — is the signature of the data across all scales simultaneously [1]. Features that live long are treated as signal; short-lived ones as topological noise. This single heuristic, once armed with theorems, became a field.

Three pillars support the subject, and this thesis treats each in depth:

1. **Filtrations and the algebra of persistence.** A filtration of simplicial complexes induces, through the homology functor with field coefficients, a *persistence module* — a diagram of vector spaces and linear maps indexed by the real line. The **structure theorem** [1] states that a tame such module decomposes uniquely into *interval modules*, each contributing one bar. This is the precise sense in which "the barcode" is well-defined.
2. **Stability.** The deep result of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer [2][3] asserts that the bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams is bounded by the sup-norm perturbation of the underlying filter functions. Later work extended this to the interleaving distance on modules with an isometry theorem. Stability is what elevates PH from a curiosity to a measurement instrument.
3. **Computation at scale.** The classical algorithm reduces the filtration boundary matrix in cubic time [1]. Two decades of engineering — reducing in *cohomology*, the *clearing* optimization, *apparent pairs*, implicit coboundary enumeration — culminated in Ripser [4], which computes Vietoris–Rips barcodes of hundreds of thousands of simplices in seconds, and GPU successors [5]. Benchmarks comparing Gudhi, DIPHA, Dionysus, and Ripser confirm a clear hierarchy [6].

Beyond single-parameter filtrations lies the frontier: multiparameter persistence, where the structure theorem provably fails and no complete discrete invariant exists. We close by mapping these limits honestly.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Simplicial complexes and homology

A finite **simplicial complex** $K$ on a vertex set $V$ is a collection of subsets closed under taking subsets: if $\sigma \in K$ and $\tau \subseteq \sigma$ then $\tau \in K$. A subset of size $p+1$ is a *$p$-simplex*. Fixing a field $\mathbb{F}$ (typically $\mathbb{Z}/2$ or $\mathbb{Q}$), the **boundary operator** $\partial_p: C_p(K) \to C_{p-1}(K)$ sends a $p$-simplex to the alternating sum of its $(p-1)$-faces. The defining identity $\partial_{p-1} \circ \partial_p = 0$ yields a chain complex, and the **$p$-th homology group**

$$H_p(K) = \ker(\partial_p) \,/\, \mathrm{im}(\partial_{p+1})$$

is a vector space whose dimension, the **Betti number** $\beta_p$, counts $p$-dimensional holes: $\beta_0$ counts connected components, $\beta_1$ counts independent loops, $\beta_2$ counts enclosed voids. An inclusion of complexes $L \hookrightarrow K$ induces a linear map $H_p(L) \to H_p(K)$ — homology is functorial, and this functoriality is the engine of persistence.

> **Theorem (Structure theorem for persistence modules, Zomorodian–Carlsson).** A tame persistence module $\{V_t, v_s^t\}$ over a field decomposes uniquely, up to ordering, as a direct sum of interval modules $I[b,d)$. The multiset of intervals is the *barcode*; the multiset of endpoints $\{(b,d)\}$ with the diagonal adjoined is the *persistence diagram* [1][7].

"Tameness" is a finiteness condition: finitely many critical values and finite-dimensional vector spaces. Without it, pathological modules need not decompose — a first warning that the theory's clean statements rest on discipline about inputs.

### 2.2 Filtrations from data

Given a finite metric point cloud $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$, a **filtration** is a nested family $\{K_r\}_{r \ge 0}$ with $K_r \subseteq K_s$ for $r \le s$. Three constructions dominate practice:

| Complex | Definition of $\sigma \in K_r$ | Size | Key property |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vietoris–Rips** $\mathrm{VR}_r(X)$ | $\mathrm{diam}(\sigma) \le r$ | $O(n^{d+1})$ in dim $d$; exponential in worst case | Flag complex: determined by edges; easy from distance matrix |
| **Čech** $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r(X)$ | $\bigcap_{x \in \sigma} B_r(x) \neq \varnothing$ | Like VR but stricter membership | Nerve lemma: homotopy equivalent to the union of balls |
| **Alpha** $A_r(X)$ | $\bigcap_{x \in \sigma} (B_r(x) \cap V_x) \neq \varnothing$, $V_x$ Voronoi cell | $O(n^{\lceil d/2 \rceil})$ via Delaunay | Same PH as Čech, vastly smaller; requires Delaunay triangulation [8] |

The inclusions $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r \subseteq \mathrm{VR}_r \subseteq \check{\mathrm{C}}_{\sqrt{2}\,r}$ (in Euclidean space) interleave the complexes, so Vietoris–Rips persistence approximates Čech persistence up to a known multiplicative factor — the standard justification for computing with the simpler flag complex [7]. The alpha complex, built on the Delaunay triangulation, carries *identical* persistent homology to the Čech complex while using far fewer simplices [8].

### 2.3 From filtrations to modules

Applying $H_p(-; \mathbb{F})$ to a filtration yields a **persistence module**: vector spaces $V_r = H_p(K_r)$ and linear maps $v_r^s: V_r \to V_s$ induced by inclusions, satisfying $v_s^t \circ v_r^s = v_r^t$. A class is *born* at $b$ if it appears in $V_b$ but in no $V_r$ for $r < b$; it *dies* at $d$ if $v_b^d$ kills it but no earlier map does. The pair $(b,d)$ is a point of the persistence diagram; the interval $[b,d)$ a bar. Features with $d = \infty$ are **essential** — they survive to the terminal complex.

## 3. Methodology: The Standard Algorithm and Its Descendants

### 3.1 Boundary matrix reduction

Order all simplices $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_m$ compatibly with the filtration (faces before cofaces; ties broken by dimension, then arbitrarily). The **filtration boundary matrix** $D$ has $D_{ij} = 1$ iff $\sigma_i$ is a codimension-one face of $\sigma_j$. The classical algorithm of Zomorodian and Carlsson, building on Edelsbrunner–Letscher–Zomorodian [1], reduces $D$ by left-to-right column additions over $\mathbb{F}$:

```python
def reduce_boundary(D):
    """Standard persistence reduction. D: boundary matrix, columns ordered
    by filtration. Returns list of (birth, death) index pairs."""
    n = D.shape[1]
    low = {}            # row -> column owning that pivot
    pairs = []
    R = D.copy()
    for j in range(n):
        # eliminate using previously reduced columns
        while True:
            lj = lowest_one(R[:, j])   # max row index with nonzero entry
            if lj is None or lj not in low:
                break
            R[:, j] = R[:, j] + R[:, low[lj]]   # column addition over F
        if lowest_one(R[:, j]) is not None:
            low[lowest_one(R[:, j])] = j
            pairs.append((lowest_one(R[:, j]), j))  # birth, death
    return pairs, R
```

If column $j$ reduces to zero, $\sigma_j$ is **positive**: it creates a homology class born at its filtration value. If column $j$ has pivot row $i = \mathrm{low}(j)$, then $\sigma_j$ is **negative**: it kills the class created by $\sigma_i$, producing the pair $(\sigma_i, \sigma_j)$ and the interval $[f(\sigma_i), f(\sigma_j))$ where $f$ is the filtration function. Unpaired positive simplices yield essential classes $(b, \infty)$. The algorithm is correct because the reduced matrix exhibits a basis of cycles and boundaries adapted to the filtration; a full proof appears in the standard references [1][6].

The worst-case complexity is $\Theta(m^3)$ where $m$ is the number of simplices, but observed behavior is far better — typically near-linear in $m$ for geometric filtrations. The gap between worst case and practice is itself a research topic.

### 3.2 The modern optimization stack

Five ideas transformed the cubic algorithm into software that handles millions of simplices:

1. **Cohomology instead of homology.** Reducing the *coboundary* matrix from right to left (de Silva–Morozov–Vejdemo-Johansson) is empirically far faster: the coboundary matrix is sparser in the relevant regime, and the anti-transpose trick localizes work [4].
2. **Clearing.** A simplex that kills a class cannot itself create one later — its column in the *next* dimension's matrix can be skipped entirely. Chen–Kerber's clearing optimization removes these columns before reduction begins, often eliminating the majority of the work [6].
3. **Apparent pairs.** Bauer's key insight for Ripser: with a suitable simplex order (the *lexicographic* or *reverse colexicographic* order), most pairs are **apparent** — detectable from the filtration order alone without any column operations. Ripser++ reports that up to 99.9% of columns in a cleared coboundary matrix are apparent [4][5].
4. **Implicit matrices.** Never storing the full (co)boundary matrix: Ripser enumerates coboundaries on the fly from the distance matrix, trading recomputation for the dominant memory cost [4].
5. **Duality and image persistence.** Recent work computes barcodes of *images* of inclusion-induced maps, with the clearing optimization adapted to relative cohomology — implemented in the Ripser framework [9].

The benchmarking study of Otter et al. [6] remains the reference comparison: Ripser dominates Vietoris–Rips computation; Gudhi and DIPHA follow; for cubical complexes Gudhi leads on memory while DIPHA leads on wall time; dual (cohomology) implementations uniformly beat standard ones.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The anatomy of a filtration: what barcodes actually measure

A barcode is not a property of the data alone; it is a property of the *pair* (data, filtration function). Change the filtration — e.g., from Vietoris–Rips to a density-sublevel filtration — and the barcode changes, sometimes dramatically. This is a feature, not a bug: choosing the filtration is choosing the *lens*. Sublevel filtrations of a function $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ detect the topology of superlevel/sublevel sets (Morse-theoretic persistence); Vietoris–Rips filtrations detect the multiscale clustering and looping of point clouds; *witness* and *lazy-witness* complexes trade accuracy for scalability on massive data.

### 4.2 The structure theorem, precisely

Let $\mathbf{Vec}$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{F}$. A **persistence module** is a functor $V: (\mathbb{R}, \le) \to \mathbf{Vec}$. The **interval module** $I_{[b,d)}$ assigns $\mathbb{F}$ to $t \in [b,d)$, $0$ elsewhere, with identity maps inside the interval. Tameness — finitely many points where $V$ changes, each $V_t$ finite-dimensional — guarantees:

> **Theorem (Decomposition).** Every tame persistence module is isomorphic to $\bigoplus_k I_{[b_k, d_k)}$, and the multiset $\{[b_k,d_k)\}$ is unique up to permutation [1][7].

The proof adapts the structure theorem for modules over a PID: a $\mathbb{Z}$-indexed persistence module is a graded module over $\mathbb{F}[t]$. Over $\mathbb{Z}$ coefficients (not a field), torsion appears and no barcode exists — one reason practitioners work over $\mathbb{F}_2$ or $\mathbb{Q}$.

*Multiparameter* persistence — modules over $(\mathbb{R}^n, \le)$ — admits **no** such decomposition: $\mathbb{F}[t_1, \dots, t_n]$ is not a PID for $n \ge 2$, and indecomposables are wild. This representation-theoretic obstruction is the central theoretical wall of the field; current research seeks computable incomplete invariants (rank invariants, fibered barcodes, multicover bifiltrations) rather than a full classification.

### 4.3 Stability theorems

Stability is the theorem that makes TDA a data science. Let $f, g: X \to \mathbb{R}$ be tame functions with persistence diagrams $\mathrm{Dgm}(f), \mathrm{Dgm}(g)$. The **bottleneck distance** is

$$d_B(D_1, D_2) = \inf_{\gamma} \sup_{p \in D_1} \|p - \gamma(p)\|_\infty,$$

the infimum over all bijections $\gamma: D_1 \to D_2$ (diagonal included) of the worst matched-pair displacement.

> **Theorem (Bottleneck stability; Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, Harer 2007).** For tame functions $f, g$ on a triangulable space, $d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(f), \mathrm{Dgm}(g)) \le \|f - g\|_\infty$ [2][3].

The proof proceeds through *interleavings*: an $\varepsilon$-perturbation of $f$ yields $\varepsilon$-interleaved persistence modules, and interleaved modules have bottleneck-close diagrams. The algebraic reformulation — the **interleaving distance** $d_I$ on persistence modules and the **isometry theorem** $d_I(V, W) = d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(V), \mathrm{Dgm}(W))$ — is now the standard language, with universality results characterizing the bottleneck distance as the largest stable metric on (extended) diagrams [10].

For point clouds, stability takes a geometric form: if $X, Y \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ are close in Hausdorff distance $d_H$, the distance functions $d_X, d_Y$ satisfy $\|d_X - d_Y\|_\infty = d_H(X, Y)$, so

$$d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(\mathrm{VR}(X)), \mathrm{Dgm}(\mathrm{VR}(Y))) \le 2\, d_H(X, Y)$$

(up to the standard factor conventions on the Rips parameter). **Wasserstein stability** ($W_p$ for finite $p$) holds under additional hypotheses on the space and function — Lipschitz functions on "reasonable" spaces — proved by Cohen-Steiner et al. [2]. These quantitative controls are what allow statistical inference: Fasy et al. build confidence sets for persistence diagrams from the stability theorem plus sampling theory [11].

### 4.4 Barcodes at scale: software and vectorization

**Software.** The ecosystem has stratified by complex type. Ripser [4] is the reference for Vietoris–Rips, combining implicit coboundaries, apparent pairs, and clearing; Ripser++ [5] ports filtration construction and apparent-pair detection to GPUs for up to ~30× speedups; GUDHI [8] covers alpha, witness, cubical, and periodic complexes with a Python interface; giotto-tda and ripser.py wrap these engines for scikit-learn pipelines. The roadmap benchmarks [6] give the practical rule of thumb: small complexes (≤ 10⁷ simplices) are routine; large ones (10⁸–10⁹) demand the dual algorithm and careful memory engineering.

**Vectorization.** Barcodes are multisets — awkward inputs for classifiers. Two stable vectorizations dominate:

- **Persistence landscapes** (Bubenik): the $k$-th landscape $\lambda_k(t)$ is the $k$-th largest value of the tent functions of the bars; the map diagram → landscape is 1-Lipschitz into $L^p$, enabling means, confidence bands, and hypothesis tests in a Banach space.
- **Persistence images** (Adams et al.): transform $(b, d) \mapsto (b, d-b)$, place weighted Gaussians at each point, and discretize the resulting surface on a pixel grid. The construction is stable in 1-Wasserstein distance and feeds directly into CNNs and SVMs, with demonstrated gains on dynamical-system parameter inference [12].

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Worked miniature: reducing a triangle

Consider three points forming a triangle with edge lengths $1, 1, \sqrt{2}$ and the Vietoris–Rips filtration. Vertices enter at $r = 0$ (three $H_0$ classes born). Edges enter at $r = 1, 1, \sqrt{2}$: the first two edges merge components (two $H_0$ deaths at $r=1$); the third edge creates an $H_1$ class (a loop, born at $\sqrt{2}$). The triangle (2-simplex) enters at $r = \sqrt{2}$ (diameter $\sqrt{2}$) and immediately kills the loop. The barcode: $H_0$: $[0,1), [0,1), [0,\infty)$; $H_1$: $[\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2})$ — a zero-length bar, invisible in the diagram. Perturb one vertex slightly: bottleneck stability guarantees the diagram moves by at most the perturbation size, so the $H_0$ bars $[0,1)$ shift negligibly. This is the stability theorem made concrete.

### 5.2 Benchmark sketch

On the standard test suite of Otter et al. [6] — e.g., the *dragon* point cloud ($n = 1000$, VR complex to dimension 2) — the measured hierarchy is stark: the standard (homology) reduction in Dionysus takes minutes and gigabytes; the dual implementation takes seconds; Ripser's apparent-pair shortcut reduces the column-operation count by orders of magnitude, with up to 99.9% of columns resolved without arithmetic [4][5]. For alpha complexes in $\mathbb{R}^3$, Gudhi and Dionysus perform comparably, and both finish complexes with tens of millions of simplices within workstation memory [6][8]. Cubical filtrations on voxel data (e.g., 3D medical images) are handled by Gudhi's and DIPHA's dual algorithms at a 3–4× memory advantage for Gudhi [6].

### 5.3 Proof sketch: bottleneck stability

We outline the Cohen-Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer argument [2][3]. Let $\|f - g\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$. Then sublevel sets interleave: $f^{-1}(-\infty, t] \subseteq g^{-1}(-\infty, t+\varepsilon] \subseteq f^{-1}(-\infty, t+2\varepsilon]$. Applying homology gives an $\varepsilon$-interleaving of persistence modules $V = H_*(f)$, $W = H_*(g)$: maps $\phi_t: V_t \to W_{t+\varepsilon}$, $\psi_t: W_t \to V_{t+\varepsilon}$ whose compositions are the $2\varepsilon$-shift maps. The **induced matching theorem** (a combinatorial consequence of the structure theorem) converts an $\varepsilon$-interleaving into a matching between barcodes where matched bars have endpoints within $\varepsilon$ and unmatched bars have length at most $2\varepsilon$. Extending the matching to a bijection via the diagonal yields $d_B \le \varepsilon$. The Wasserstein variant requires bounding the *number* of long bars — possible only under geometric hypotheses, which is why $W_p$-stability is conditional [2].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Filtration dependence.** Persistent homology measures the filtration, not the data. A poor filtration choice (wrong scale convention, unnormalized density) produces barcodes that are provably stable yet scientifically vacuous. The theory offers almost no guidance on *which* filtration to use.
2. **The field-coefficient restriction.** Barcodes require field coefficients. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, torsion classes (e.g., in projective spaces, lens spaces) are invisible to the standard pipeline, and integral persistent homology remains computationally immature.
3. **Multiparameter wildness.** For two or more parameters there is no barcode; indecomposable modules are unclassifiable. Multiparameter TDA therefore works with *incomplete* invariants, and stability theory there is still being built.
4. **Metric fragility of vectorizations.** Landscapes and images are stable, but their hyperparameters (resolution, weighting, bandwidth) are noncanonical and can dominate downstream ML performance; stability constants degrade with poor choices [12].
5. **Computational walls.** Vietoris–Rips complexes grow exponentially in the worst case; dimension $\ge 3$ persistence on dense clouds remains infeasible without sparsification (witness complexes, graph-induced complexes), each introducing its own approximation error that stability theorems only partially cover.
6. **Interpretability.** A long bar certifies a *homological* feature, not a *geometric* one — generators are not canonical, so "the loop" a bar refers to has no unique representative cycle. Applications that need explicit cycles must solve the harder *optimal representative* problem.

## 7. Conclusion

Persistent homology succeeded because it paired a computable invariant with theorems that justify computing it. The structure theorem says the barcode exists; the reduction algorithm says it can be found; the stability theorems say it means something; and the modern software stack — Ripser's apparent pairs, GUDHI's alpha complexes, GPU acceleration — says it can be found at scale [4][5][6][8]. The interleaving framework and isometry theorem recast stability as pure algebra, and vectorizations carry barcodes into statistical and machine-learning workflows with Lipschitz guarantees intact [12].

The open frontier is multiparameter persistence, where the algebra refuses to decompose and the algorithms must therefore be reimagined around incomplete invariants. If the first two decades of TDA were about proving that one-parameter persistence is well-behaved, the next will be about extracting reliable signal from filtrations the structure theorem cannot tame. The lesson of the stability theorems will remain the standard: an invariant is only as useful as its modulus of continuity.
