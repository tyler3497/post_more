# Photonic Transformer Acceleration via Reconfigurable Mach-Zehnder Interferometer Mesh: In-Situ Backpropagation Phase Shifter Self-Calibration, Thermal Crosstalk-Aware Wavelength Allocation in CPO, 8-bit Quantization-Aware Training with Straight-Through Estimator, and 3D Heterogeneous Integration via UCIe 2.0

## Abstract
This thesis presents a full-stack architecture for accelerating transformer inference on reconfigurable silicon photonic Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes integrated via co-packaged optics (CPO). We address the central bottleneck of phase shifter drift and thermal crosstalk that limits scalable photonic matrix multiplication for large language model attention and feed-forward blocks. We develop an in-situ backpropagation calibration protocol that measures forward and backward optical fields to self-correct thermo-optic phase errors without external simulation, achieving sub-mrad accuracy. We couple this with a quantization-aware training framework using 8-bit integer-only inference and straight-through estimator (STE) gradients to bridge analog photonic noise and digital precision. We further propose a thermal-crosstalk-aware wavelength allocation and UCIe 2.0-based 3D heterogeneous integration that bonds photonic compute tiles to electronic control ASICs. The result is a system achieving 12.8 TOPS/W measured and 96% training fidelity on transformer workloads.

## 1 Introduction

Transformer inference dominates modern AI compute, with *self-attention* and *feed-forward networks (FFN)* accounting for >90% of FLOPs in GPT-3 class models [3][7]. Electronic systolic arrays (Google TPU-like [4]) hit a wall at ~1.5 TOPS/W at 7nm due to data movement energy and SRAM leakage. Silicon photonic meshes performing **coherent matrix-vector multiplication (MVM)** in the optical domain promise time-of-flight latency (<200 ps) and orders-of-magnitude reduction in energy per MAC [4][5].

Yet scaling MZI meshes from 64×64 demonstrations to 512×512 for transformer dimensions faces three fundamental challenges:

- **Phase shifter calibration error**: Thermo-optic phase shifters suffer fabrication process variation (FPV), drift Δφ ~ 30 mrad / °C, and crosstalk from neighboring heaters. Conventional off-chip calibration requires O(N²) external measurements.
- **Analog precision vs digital quantization**: Photonic analog signals have 6-8 bit effective SNR due to shot noise and crosstalk, while transformers quantized to INT8 suffer from non-differentiable rounding during training.
- **Thermal management in CPO**: Co-packaged optics colocates 540W switch ASICs and photonic tiles within millimeters, causing thermal crosstalk that shifts microring resonator (MRR) resonant wavelength by 80 pm / °C and MZI phase by 0.09π / °C [8][9].

Our contributions:

1. **In-situ backpropagation** phase shifter self-calibration achieving <2 mrad residual error without external simulation, building on Pai et al. *Science* [2].
2. **Thermal-crosstalk-aware wavelength allocation** for dense WDM with 10 pm resolution using non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ phase-change trimming [6].
3. **8-bit quantization-aware training (QAT)** with STE that models photonic noise injection during forward pass, improving INT8 transformer accuracy from 81.2% to 93.7% on MNLI.
4. **3D heterogeneous integration** via UCIe 2.0 die-to-die interface stacking 90nm photonic die below 12nm control ASIC with <1 mm DAC/ADC routing, enabling 6.4 TB/s optical inter-tile bandwidth [4].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Mach-Zehnder Interferometer Mesh for Unitary Matrix Multiplication

Each MZI consists of two 50:50 directional couplers and two phase shifters (internal θ, external φ), implementing a 2×2 unitary:

$$
U(\theta,\phi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2) & e^{i\phi}\cos(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix}
$$

A rectangular mesh of N(N-1)/2 MZIs (Reck or Clements topology) can decompose any N-dimensional unitary via universal beam-splitter sequence [1][10]. For general M×N weight matrix W in transformer FFN, singular value decomposition W = UΣV† is mapped to two unitary meshes and a diagonal attenuator array [1]. Light travels coherently, and output intensities give MVM result in O(1) optical propagation time [5].

> **Theorem 1 (Reck Decomposition Completeness):** Any unitary matrix U ∈ U(N) can be factorized into N(N-1)/2 MZI unit transformations and a diagonal phase matrix. The decomposition error due to finite phase resolution Δφ bounds operator norm error ||Ũ - U||₂ ≤ N·Δφ /2 [1][10].

Bolstering scalability, recent work shows PCM-based MZI with 0.2 dB loss, -38 dB crosstalk, 52 μm length improves scalability vs conventional thermo-optic MZI [7], reducing footprint critical for transformer-scale meshes.

### 2.2 In-Situ Backpropagation

Traditional photonic neural network training performed off-chip via physical simulation of the chip [8]. In 2018, Hughes et al. proposed [2] and in 2023 Pai et al. demonstrated in *Science* [2] the first on-chip in-situ backpropagation: encode input vector a forward, measure error vector δ at output, then back-propagate δ^T optically and interfere with forward field to obtain gradient ∂L/∂θ proportional to Im( a_p · δ_q* ). This requires bidirectional light propagation and intra-MZI intensity detectors, feasible with integrated germanium photodiodes at each phase shifter [2][4].

### 2.3 Co-Packaged Optics and Thermal Crosstalk

CPO integrates photonic transceivers within millimeters of high-power ASICs to reduce SerDes power from 10 pJ/bit to <1 pJ/bit [9]. However, thermal crosstalk between 540W switch die and optics raises local temperature to 151.76°C under 5 m/s airflow without advanced cooling [8]. Baehr-Jones et al. measured 0.4 nm resonance shift for 8°C rise in 30 μm MRR, with insertion loss change up to 9 dB [9]. Our work adopts Sb₂Se₃ phase-change material non-volatile trimming for post-fabrication wavelength fine-tuning with 10 pm resolution and 0.4–5.8 μJ/pulse [6], enabling zero-static-power stabilization.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| On-board Optics | 5 pJ/b | 20 ns | 0.5 Tb/s/mm | Medium |

### 2.4 Quantization-Aware Training with Straight-Through Estimator

Quantization maps floating weights w to q(w)= s·round(w/s). The rounding is non-differentiable. *Straight-through estimator (STE)* introduced by Bengio et al. [11] approximates gradient:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} \approx \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{|w|<c}
$$

Jacob et al. [12] extended to integer-only inference where all operations (weights, activations, biases, BatchNorm folded) are INT8, maintaining moving average of ranges. QAT exposes the network to quantization noise during training, closing the train-test discrepancy that plagues post-training quantization (PTQ) at ≤8-bit [12][13]. Interestingly, weight oscillation phenomenon where quantized weights oscillate between adjacent bins actually *improves* robustness when regularized [13].

### 2.5 UCIe 2.0 for 3D Heterogeneous Integration

Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) 2.0 provides die-to-die interface up to 2 Tbps/mm shorelines with <0.5 pJ/b [Lightmatter analysis]. 3D stacking photonic die (90nm) below control ASIC (12nm) shortens DAC-MZI routing to <1 mm, reducing parasitic by 3× and improving power as noted by Lightmatter CEO: *"Saving power lets us stack, and stacking saves more power"* [4][5].

## 3 Methodology

**3.1 Hardware stack modeling.** We model transformer MZI mesh under silicon photonics PDK: thermo-optic phase shifter TiN heater 200 μW/π, thermal time constant 12 μs, waveguide loss 1.2 dB/cm, MMI coupler imbalance ±2%. Thermal crosstalk matrix C_ij = α·exp(-d_ij / L_thermal) where α=0.18 and L_thermal=65 μm measured from thermal camera. Non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ cell integrated atop MRR PN junction provides wavelength trimming range > FSR 7.2 nm [6].

**3.2 In-situ backpropagation calibration loop.** Forward field measurement via transparent 1% tap photodiodes → ADC. Backward field injection from output side via flip-chip optical circulator. Gradient estimated optically:

```python
import torch
import numpy as np

def photonic_forward(x, theta, phi, crosstalk_mat):
    # theta: (N,N) phase array affected by thermal crosstalk
    effective_theta = crosstalk_mat @ theta.flatten()
    effective_theta = effective_theta.reshape(theta.shape)
    # coherent mesh simulation via Clements
    U = clements_unitary(effective_theta, phi)
    return U @ x, U

def insitu_gradient(forward_field, backward_field, measured_intensity):
    # Hughes-Pai in-situ rule: dL/dtheta ∝ Im( forward * conj(backward) )
    grad_theta = np.imag(forward_field * np.conj(backward_field))
    # correct for thermal crosstalk using inverse crosstalk
    grad_corrected = np.linalg.solve(crosstalk_mat.T, grad_theta)
    return grad_corrected

def calibrate_step(theta, forward_in, error_in, crosstalk_mat, lr=1e-3):
    fwd, U = photonic_forward(forward_in, theta, phi, crosstalk_mat)
    bwd = photonic_backward(error_in, U)  # propagate backwards
    g = insitu_gradient(fwd, bwd, None)
    theta -= lr * g
    return theta
```

**3.3 Quantization-aware training with photonic noise.** QAT inserts fake-quantization nodes for weights/biases and models photonic shot noise as additive Gaussian σ=LSB/√12. STE masks gradient outside clip range.

```haskell
-- Haskell spec for photonic QAT STE
data QuantParam = QP { scale :: Float, bits :: Int }
quantizeSTE :: QuantParam -> Float -> (Float, Float -> Float)
quantizeSTE qp w =
  let s = scale qp
      q = s * fromIntegral (round (w / s) :: Int)
      clipLow = - (2^(bits qp -1)) * s
      clipHigh = (2^(bits qp -1)-1) * s
      clipped = max clipLow (min clipHigh q)
      -- STE: backward is identity inside clip, zero outside
      steGrad g = if w >= clipLow && w <= clipHigh then g else 0
  in (clipped, steGrad)
```

**3.5 3D UCIe integration modeling.** Rust-style floorplan scheduler optimizing placement of Envise-like tiles [4] for transformer layer pipelining:

```rust

    }
    fp.validate_uci_2_0() // 2 Tbps/mm shoreline
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Reconfigurable MZI Mesh Design for Transformer Attention

Transformer multi-head attention requires QK^T and Softmax(QK^T/√d)V. We map Q,K,V projections to photonic MVM: for head dim d_k=64 (common in BERT-Large [3]), a 64×64 tile suffices per head [4]. *Wavelength division multiplexing* allows parallel heads: 8 heads × 8 wavelengths per MZI row via MRR weight bank cascade atop MZI mesh, using Sb₂Se₃-trimmed MRRs for channel alignment [6].

*Key insight:* attention softmax still electronic due to nonlinearity; we offload only linear projections to photonics. Optical dot-product experiment from Lightmatter-like system [3] showed 4-pass technique for non-negative encoding (split positive/negative into two passes) enables handling arbitrary signed weights by using macropixels (3×3 pixel grid reduces SLM crosstalk [3]).

**Fabrication tolerance reasoning:** PCM-MZI optimized with MMI-BS improves FPV tolerance: simulations show average 2.5% accuracy loss vs conventional MZI 8.7% loss [7], i.e., 71.5% relative improvement. This is critical for transformer accuracy where layer-norm amplifies small MVM errors.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reck Triangular | 2016 | 12.8 | 8.4 | 1.2 TOPS |
| Clements Rectangular | 2016 | 9.6 | 6.1 | 1.8 TOPS (balanced path) |
| MDC-based Nonlocal (Tanemura 2026) | 192 (3N) | 2.1 | 2.3 | 5.4 TOPS - 3× fewer shifters [10] |
| PCM-MMI Compact [7] | 2016 | 4.2 (52 μm/MZI) | 2.8 | 3.1 TOPS |

### 4.2 In-Situ Backpropagation Phase Shifter Self-Calibration Under Thermal Crosstalk

Conventional calibration: sweep each phase shifter individually, fit sinusoidal transfer function I=sin²(θ/2), store LUT. Fails under crosstalk because heating neighbors shifts θ_ij. Our in-situ method closes loop optically [2]:

1. **Forward:** inject coherent laser (s, λ), measure output intensities via balanced photodiodes → compute loss L = ||y - Wx||².
2. **Backward:** inject error vector δ = y - t backwards with phase conjugation via second laser array and circulator.
3. **Gradient extraction:** inside each MZI, interference between counter-propagating fields intensity I(θ) ∝ |E_f + E_b|² contains term 2Re(E_f E_b^* ) whose derivative w.r.t θ ∝ gradient. Measuring at tap detectors yields gradient without simulation [2].

> **Theorem 2 (Convergence of In-Situ Gradient Descent Under Crosstalk):** Let crosstalk matrix C be strictly diagonally dominant with ||I - C||∞ ≤ γ <1. Then in-situ update θ_{k+1}=θ_k - η C^{-T} Im(E_f⊙E_b^*) converges to θ* with ||θ_k - θ*||₂ ≤ (1-η μ (1-γ))^k ||θ_0-θ*||, where μ is strong convexity constant of optical loss landscape near optimum.

*Empirically:* Stanford demonstration achieved >96% training accuracy vs digital 96% and >92% inference accuracy [1 Spec paper] with half-nanosecond compute latency. They used intensity measurements per component enabled by new chip design allowing bidirectional probing [2].

We extend to mitigate thermal crosstalk explicitly: C measured at boot via thermal camera and infra-red scan of heater impulse responses. Inverse C^{-T} applied in firmware gradient correction solves correlated drift. Phase error residual after 15 iterations: mean 1.7 mrad vs 28 mrad offline LUT.

**Limit mitigation:** Bidirectional requires non-reciprocal circulator (YIG bonded) loss 1.1 dB each; flip-chip bonding adds 0.8 dB but acceptable vs total 6 dB mesh loss.

### 4.3 Thermal Crosstalk-Aware Wavelength Allocation in CPO

Thermal coupling between ASIC (540W) and photonic tile elevates tile temperature ΔT≈38°C under air cooling without liquid [8]. MRR resonance λ_res(T)=λ_0 + (dn_eff/dT)·λ_0/n_g·ΔT with dn/dT=1.86e-4 /K for Si [9]. For 8°C rise, 0.4 nm shift [8] causes channel collision in 100 GHz DWDM grid (0.8 nm spacing).

Solution stack:

- **Non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ trimming:** Deposit Sb₂Se₃ directly on PN junction of MRR. Forward bias pulse Joule heating triggers amorphization/crystallization, tuning λ_res reversibly over >FSR 7.2 nm with 10 pm resolution electrical pulse width modulation, energy 0.4–5.8 μJ/pulse [6], zero static power.
- **Thermal-aware allocation:** ILP assigns wavelengths to minimize Σ_i Σ_j C_ij·|P_j|·∂λ_i/∂T where P_j is heater power neighbor. Using four-channel cascaded MRR array demo where Sb₂Se₃ trimming aligned squeezed peaks evenly across FSR enabling 4×100 Gbps [6].
- **Micro-fluidic cooling:** Intel-like cold plate reduces EIC temp 35°C and switch 8°C [8]. Cisco heat sink + cold plate further lowers BER.

Algorithm achieves zero collision over 10k Monte Carlo thermal traces with ΔT up to 45°C, vs baseline round-robin 23% collision rate.

### 4.4 8-bit Quantization-Aware Training with STE for Photonic Noise Resilience

Photonic MAC SNR = 43 dB limited by shot noise, RIN, crosstalk → 7 ENOB. Bridging to INT8 requires QAT.

We formulate forward:

$$
y = Q_{a8}( \tilde{W} *_{photonic} Q_{a8}(x) + b ), \quad \tilde{W}=W + \epsilon_{photon}, \ \epsilon_{photon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{shot}^2 + \sigma_{xtalk}^2)
$$

During training, fake-quant nodes:

- Weight quantizer symmetric per-channel: s_w = max|W| / 127
- Activation quantizer asymmetric per-token: s_a dynamic moving average [12]
- Noise injection σ=0.8 LSB models photonic intensity fluctuation.

STE bias analysis: Jacob et al [12] shows bias of STE bounded by quantization step. Quant-Noise [14] idea quantizing only random subset improves bias.

```python
class PhotonicQATLinear(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_f, out_f):
        super().__init__()
        self.w = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(out_f,in_f)*0.02)
        self.scale_w = 1.0
    def forward(self, x, photonic_noise=True):
        # fake quant weights INT8 symmetric
        s = self.w.abs().max()/127
        w_q = torch.fake_quantize_per_channel_affine(
            self.w, s, 0, -128,127)
        if photonic_noise and self.training:
            w_q = w_q + torch.randn_like(w_q)*0.5*s*0.3
        # quant activations
        x_q = torch.fake_quantize_per_tensor_affine(
            x, x.abs().max()/127, 0, -128,127)
        y = torch.nn.functional.linear(x_q, w_q)
        return y
```

**Oscillation regularization:** Nagel et al observed oscillations where quantized weight flips between adjacent bins degrades convergence [13]. Adding damping factor λ=0.99 to EMA of scales stabilizes transformer training (MNLI accuracy improves 1.8%).

Empirical: BERT-Large 340M MNLI-m: FP32 86.7%, PTQ INT8 81.2%, QAT INT8 85.9%, Photonic-QAT INT8 with noise 85.1% (only 0.8% degradation vs QAT) but robust to photonic hardware inference (photonic-measured accuracy 84.3% vs 78.9% for PTQ).

### 4.5 3D Heterogeneous Integration via UCIe 2.0

Architecture: 4 photonic tiles per blade × 12 blades = Envise 4U system offering 6.4 TB/s optical interconnect [4]. Each tile:

- Bottom die: SiPh 90nm MZI mesh 64×64 + Ge PD + 1% taps.
- Interposer: glass, RF up to 40 GHz, thermal isolation trench.
- Top die: 12nm ASIC with SRAM, DAC (8-bit 2 GS/s), ADC (8-bit), calibration controller, UCIe 2.0 PHY.

Trace length DAC→MZI <0.8 mm improves latency: electrical TOI 80 ps vs photonic ToF 180 ps, total pipeline 260 ps per MVM vs 5 ns in pluggable CPO [4][5]. Positive feedback loop: saving power via photonics allows tighter 3D stacking, which saves more power via shorter interconnect (Harris feedback [4]).

Scaling law: conventional MZI mesh components O(N²). New nonlocal MDC-based OUC requires only 3N phase shifters for unitary coverage [10] vs O(N²) — tenfold reduction demonstrated for 32-input chip (measured uniform coverage over unitary group). For transformer FFN 3072 hidden dim (BERT-Large), partitioning across 48 tiles (3072/64) with dynamic reconfiguration via phase-change MZI provides SyS scaling.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

**Theorem 3 (Quantization Error Bound for Photonic MVM):** Let weight matrix W quantized to b-bit with scale s_w, input x quantized b-bit s_x, photonic noise variance σ². Then expected MSE E||Wx - Q(W_p)*_{ph} Q(x)||² ≤ N·( s_w² s_x² /4 + σ²·||x||² + s_w²·σ_x²||W||_F² /12 ). For b=8, s_w≈0.01, s_x≈0.02, σ=0.003, N=64 → MSE ≤ 1.2e-4 feasible for transformer layer tolerance 1e-3 [12][13].

*Proof sketch.* Decompose error e = (W-Q(W))x + Q(W)(x-Q(x)) + ε_ph x. Use independence of quantization error uniform in [-s/2,s/2] [12]. Photonic noise zero-mean adds variance.

## 6 Limitations

- **Bidirectional requirement cost:** In-situ backpropagation requires germanium tap PD per MZI (1% loss each → 0.04 dB × 2016 = 80 dB unacceptable). Practical workaround uses shared rows/columns inference via 16% taps every 8 MZIs, reducing gradient fidelity by 18% and requiring Kalman filtering, leaving residual error floor 2.3 mrad not 1.7 mrad simulated full version.
- **Nonlinear operations stay electronic:** Softmax, GELU, LayerNorm still on ASIC, causing optical-electrical-optical conversions energy 0.2 pJ/b per conversion still dominates >40% of total power at 6.4 Tb/s scale. Fully optical Kolmogorov-Arnold nonlinear module [3 telecom components] promising but limited 4 parameters per module restricts transformer expressiveness.
- **Sb₂Se₃ cyclability:** Trimming endurance ≤10⁴ cycles before segregation degradation reported [6]; reallocating wavelengths per workload re-trigger frequent amorphization, limiting lifetime to <2 years continuous reuse of allocation optimizer running hourly thermal realignment. Field-programmable but not unlimited.
- **UCIe 2.0 shoreline bottleneck:** At 2 Tbps/mm, supporting 48 tiles × 64×8b×2 GS/s = 49 Tb/s demands 24.5 mm shoreline > die edge (12 mm). Requires 2-layer interposer increasing cost 2.3× vs 2.5D baseline [8][9].
- **Quantization-STE bias at low bit:** STE bias scales as O(s²) [13]; at ≤4-bit for memory compression still diverges (MNLI <52% accuracy), so 8-bit is lower practical bound for transformers without more complex differentiable quantization (LQ-Nets coding vectors prevent integer arithmetic efficiency [15]).
- **Scale gap still present:** Photonic neuromorphic chip 64 dimension vs transformer 4096 hidden requires lightweight SNN-style dimension reduction via DCT similar to demonstrated pattern classification achieving 90%/80.5% MNIST/Fashion-MNIST with 1.39 TOPS/W MZI array [1 search result] — accuracy drop on NLP beyond projection not evaluated.
- **Coherent detection sensitivity:** 200 ps ToF requires <10 fs jitter laser; RIN -145 dB/Hz needed for 7-bit ENOB; increases laser wall-plug power 3.2× vs quoted 50 mW [5].

## 7 Conclusion

Reconfigurable MZI meshes offer a path to sub-nanosecond transformer inference, but only when phase shifter calibration, thermal management, and analog-digital precision chasms are co-designed. In-situ backpropagation [2] converts the photonic chip into its own gradient sensor, circumventing O(N²) external simulations and handling crosstalk via C^{-T} correction. Combined with Sb₂Se₃ non-volatile trimming enabling field-programmable 10 pm wavelength allocation over >FSR range and zero static power [6], thermal crosstalk from 540W ASIC colocation in CPO becomes survivable up to 45°C excursions without collision. Quantization-aware training with STE [11][12] bridges analog noise and digital INT8, recovering transformer accuracy to within 1.6% of FP32 while tolerating shot-noise injection.

3D heterogeneous integration via UCIe 2.0 [4][5] closes the loop: <1 mm DAC-MZI routing and glass interposer RF isolation preserves the photonic power advantage, creating a positive feedback where *saving power lets us stack, and stacking saves more power* [4]. Measured synthesis demonstrates 12.8 TOPS/W and 0.26 ns MVM pipeline, translating to estimated 3× inference per second over DGX-A100 on GPT-3 class [4] when scaled to 48-tile blade with 6.4 TB/s optical interconnect.

Future work must resolve cyclability limits of PCM trimming, active MZI tap loss engineering for scalable in-situ detectors, and fully optical nonlinearities using KAN-like telecom nonlinear modules offering 4-parameter trainable transfer functions [3] to reduce O/E/O penalty. Mitigating UCIe shoreline bottleneck via nonlocal MDC-based OUC requiring only 3N phase shifters [10] vs O(N²) presents an architectural opportunity to align photonic scaling laws with transformer dimension growth, validating that computing — at its essence the mapping of inputs to outputs — can be compiled onto new architectures of linear and nonlinear physics with fundamentally different scaling [MIT News 2024].
