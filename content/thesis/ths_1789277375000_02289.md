# Causal Emergence and Effective Information: Coarse-Graining, Maximal Causal Power, and Emergence in Complex Systems
## Abstract
The dominant intuition in the sciences is that causality is most precisely located at the most fine-grained level of description: atoms, neurons, bits. Erik Hoel and collaborators have challenged this intuition with a quantitative theory of *causal emergence* grounded in the information-theoretic quantity *effective information* (EI). The core claim is precise and testable: for certain dynamical systems, a coarse-grained macroscopic description can possess *more* effective information — more causal power — than the microscopic description from which it is built [1]. Because effective information decomposes into determinism minus degeneracy [2], coarse-graining can amplify causality by suppressing degeneracy (many distinct causes mapping onto the same effect) and sharpening determinism, exactly as error-correcting codes increase channel capacity by suppressing noise [2]. This essay develops the formalism from its origins in Tononi and Sporns' theory of information integration [3] through Hoel et al.'s 2013 *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* result [1], the 2017 error-correcting-code reformulation [2], recent multiscale refinements (causal emergence 2.0) [9], and applications to neural networks and complex systems [7][8].
## 1 Introduction
Emergence is among the most contested concepts in the philosophy of science. A flock of starlings wheeling over a marsh, the consciousness of a brain, the phase transition of a ferromagnet — all are described as "emergent" relative to their constituents, yet the term routinely smuggles together prediction, explanation, ontology, and mystery. John Heil has argued that the literature offers not one concept but several barely-related phenomena wearing a single label [6, discussion]. Against this backdrop, Hoel's program is deliberately modest in ambition and aggressive in precision: rather than asking what emergence *is*, it asks when a *macroscopic causal description* of a system outperforms the microscopic one, and it answers with a number — the difference in effective information between scales [1][2].
The wager is that many of our macro-level causal attributions — *the pressure of the gas burst the container*, *the belief that the bridge was out made her turn*, *the memory attractor stabilized the network's output* — are not pragmatic conveniences for cognitively bounded observers, but true attributions of causal power. If a macrostate's intervention on a system constrains the system's future more sharply than any microstate intervention can, then, on an interventionist account of causation, the macrostate is the more efficacious cause [1][10]. The remainder of this essay formalizes that wager, exhibits where it succeeds, where it breaks, and what remains for future work.
> **Thesis claim 1.1.** Causal emergence is operationalized as follows: given a microscopic causal system $X \to Y$ and a strict coarse-graining $C$ producing a macroscopic system $U \to V$, causal emergence obtains iff $EI(U \to V) > EI(X \to Y)$ and no finer non-trivial partition achieves a strictly larger $EI$. The maximizer of $EI$ over partitions is the scale at which the system's causes are "most real" [1][2].

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Effective information: origins
$$
EI(X \to Y) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{x \in X} D_{KL}\!\left( P(Y \mid X = x) \;\middle\|\; E_D(Y) \right),
$$
$$
EI(X \to Y) = H(E_D(Y)) - \frac{1}{n} \sum_x H(Y \mid X = x).
$$
### 2.2 The determinism–degeneracy decomposition
$$
\mathrm{det}(X \to Y) = \log_2(n) - \frac{1}{n}\sum_{x} H(Y \mid X = x),
$$
$$
\mathrm{deg}(X \to Y) = \log_2(n) - H(E_D(Y)).
$$
$$
EI(X \to Y) = \mathrm{det}(X \to Y) - \mathrm{deg}(X \to Y).
$$
### 2.3 Historical context
1. **Interventionist causation** (Woodward, Pearl): causes are difference-makers under interventions, formalized via the $do(\cdot)$ operator.
2. **Integrated information theory**: the claim that a system's *cause–effect structure* is its ontologically primary feature [3].
3. **Renormalization-group thinking**: the idea that the "right" description of a system can live at a non-trivial scale, familiar from Wilsonian coarse-graining in statistical physics.
4. **Error-correcting codes**: Shannon's insight that redundancy at the input can make a noisy channel effectively deterministic [2].
| Tradition | Core quantity | Analogue of $EI$ gain |
|---|---|---|
| IIT (Tononi–Sporns) [3] | Effective information | Informational integration |
| Interventionism (Pearl) | $P(Y \mid do(X))$ | Do-calculus power |
| Renormalization group | Relevant operators | Surviving couplings |
| Error-correcting codes [2] | Channel capacity | Capacity gain under coding |
| Causal emergence [1] | $EI_{macro} - EI_{micro}$ | Causal power gain |

## 3 Methodology
### 3.1 The microscopic system
```python
import numpy as np
def effective_information(tpm: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """Effective information of a transition probability matrix.
    tpm[i, j] = P(Y = j | do(X = i)). States are rows (inputs), columns (outputs)."""
    n = tpm.shape[0]
    avg = tpm.mean(axis=0)                      # E_D(Y): average effect distribution
    ei = 0.0
    for i in range(n):
        row = tpm[i]
        nz = row > 0
        ei += np.sum(row[nz] * np.log2(row[nz] / avg[nz]))  # D_KL(row || avg)
    return ei / n
def determinism_degeneracy(tpm: np.ndarray):
    n = tpm.shape[0]
    avg = tpm.mean(axis=0)
    det = np.log2(n) - np.mean([-np.sum(r[r > 0] * np.log2(r[r > 0])) for r in tpm])
    deg = np.log2(n) - (-np.sum(avg[avg > 0] * np.log2(avg[avg > 0])))
    return det, deg
```
### 3.2 Coarse-graining: from $X$ to $U$
$$
P(V = v \mid do(U = u)) = \frac{1}{|u|} \sum_{x \in u} P(V = v \mid do(X = x)),
$$
### 3.3 The emergence criterion and the search over partitions
**Causal emergence (CE 1.0)** obtains when, for some strict coarsening $C$,
$$
EI_C = EI(U \to V) - EI(X \to Y) > 0,
$$
- **Black-boxing**: grouping elements of a multi-element system (e.g., partitioning neurons into modules) and coarse-graining their states;
- **Temporal coarse-graining**: extending the time step, letting transients wash out;
- **State-lumping**: merging micro states with equivalent or symmetric transition profiles.
### 3.4 The interventionist reading
```haskell
-- A coarse-graining as a partition of micro-states, in Hoel's operational sense:
-- interventions on a macro-block are uniform mixtures of micro-interventions.
data Coarsening micro macro = Coarsening
  { blocks    :: macro -> [micro]          -- partition: every micro belongs to exactly one block
  , macroTpm  :: macro -> Dist macro      -- induced macro transition matrix
  }
macroEffect :: Coarsening micro macro -> (micro -> Dist micro) -> macro -> Dist macro
macroEffect c microTpm u =
  uniformMixture [ aggregate (microTpm x) (blocks c) | x <- blocks c u ]
```

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 When can the macro beat the micro? The formal structure
$$
EI_{micro} = \mathrm{det}_m - \mathrm{deg}_m, \qquad EI_{macro} = \mathrm{det}_M - \mathrm{deg}_M.
$$
$$
(\mathrm{det}_M - \mathrm{det}_m) > (\mathrm{deg}_M - \mathrm{deg}_m).
$$
1. **Degeneracy suppression.** Suppose the micro system is a noisy OR of four binary micro-variables whose individual contributions wash out — many distinct micro-interventions yield nearly identical futures. Degeneracy is high. A coarse-graining that sums the variables (e.g., the total count of active inputs) can collapse the degenerate transitions into a sharply differentiated macro channel, because the *average* effect of a block is far from the global average effect even when individual micro effects are not. This is the mechanism behind Hoel et al.'s block-model examples [1].
2. **Determinism sharpening.** Suppose the micro dynamics are noisy at short timescales but the noise averages out under the block's internal dynamics, exactly as a noisy channel becomes reliable when its output is decoded by an error-correcting code [2].
> **Theorem 4.1 (Hoel's code equivalence).** For every finite-state causal system $X \to Y$ and every coarse-graining $C$ producing $U \to V$, there exists an error-correcting code whose decoding map recovers $V$ from $Y$ and whose capacity gain over the raw channel $X \to Y$ equals the change in effective information $EI(U\to V) - EI(X\to Y)$ under the uniform intervention distribution. Consequently, *macroscopic causation is information-theoretic error correction*: a macrostate "wins" exactly when it corrects the micro-level noise [2].
### 4.2 Effective information as channel capacity — the ECC correspondence
1. The input distribution is *fixed* to be uniform ($H^{max}$), not optimized: $EI$ measures the causal power available to an interventionist who can intervene on any state equally, not the power available to a sender who may choose a favorable codebook.
2. Input and output state spaces must coincide, so that micro and macro $EI$ values are commensurate.
```rust
/// Coarse-graining a transition matrix via block-averaged interventions.
/// Mirrors Hoel's operational definition: do(U = u) := uniform mix of do(X = x), x in u.
fn macro_tpm(tpm: &[Vec<f64>], blocks: &[Vec<usize>]) -> Vec<Vec<f64>> {
    let n = tpm.len();
    let m = blocks.len();
    let mut mt = vec![vec![0.0; m]; m];
    for (u, bx) in blocks.iter().enumerate() {
        for (v, bv) in blocks.iter().enumerate() {
            let mut p = 0.0;
            for &x in bx {
                for &y in bv {
                    p += tpm[x][y];
                }
            }
            mt[u][v] = p / bx.len() as f64;
        }
    }
    mt
}
```
### 4.3 Causal emergence in neural networks and complex systems
- **Boolean networks and logic gates.** The canonical demonstrations [1][8] show that aggregated Boolean sub-networks (e.g., groups of AND/OR gates) can exhibit higher $EI$ than their constituent gates, because the macro variables *average away* the noise and degeneracy of individual gates. The Entropy survey's Boolean-network example is illustrative: one coarse-graining yields $EI_{macro1} = 1.55 > EI_{micro} = 1.15$, while a different coarse-graining yields $EI_{macro2} = 0.18$ — the maximization over partitions is doing real, non-trivial work [8].
- **Neural networks (RNNs and spiking models).** Coarse-graining hidden units of recurrent networks into population-level "concept" variables (e.g., pooling units with correlated activity) has been shown to raise $EI$: the population code is more deterministic and less degenerate than single-unit spike trains [8]. This resonates with the neural-coding literature: the brain itself coarse-grains (population coding, rate coding), and CE suggests this is not merely data compression but *causal amplification* — the population variables are the system's real control levers.
- **Cellular automata and agent-based models.** Block-averaging spatial neighborhoods of automata rules produces macro dynamics with cleaner causal structure, again via degeneracy suppression [8].
- **Multivariate empirical data.** Rosas et al. [7] introduced a practical estimator for causal emergence in multivariate time series, using the integrated-information-decomposition ($\Phi$ID) framework to distinguish *causal decoupling* (macro dynamics independent of micro details) from *downward causation*. Their method was applied to neural recordings and game-of-life-like systems, finding genuine decoupling in some cases and none in others — showing the criterion is *discriminating*, not vacuous.
| System class | Coarse-graining | Result | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boolean network (4 nodes) [8] | State-lumping, $\alpha=(A,B)$, $\beta=(C,D)$ | $EI$ 1.15 → 1.55 | Degeneracy suppression |
| Noisy logic gates [1] | Black-boxing sub-circuits | Macro beats micro | Error-correction of noise |
| RNN hidden units [8] | Population pooling | $EI$ gain | Correlated-activity averaging |
| Game of Life [7] | Block partition (φID) | Causal decoupling found | Macro self-determination |
| Neural recordings [7] | Feature supervenience | Mixed (criterion discriminates) | System-dependent |
### 4.4 The multiscale refinement: CE 2.0 and causal primitives
1. **Sufficiency**: how reliably a cause brings about its effect (high sufficiency $\approx$ low causal uncertainty in the forward direction);
2. **Necessity**: how much the effect depends on the cause (high necessity $\approx$ low causal uncertainty in the reverse direction).

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Formal Proofs
### 5.1 The worked Boolean-network example
- **Macro 1** (grouping $(A,B) \to \alpha$, $(C,D) \to \beta$ with one aggregation scheme): $EI_{macro1} = 1.55 > 1.15$ — **causal emergence**, a gain of 0.40 bits.
- **Macro 2** (alternative aggregation): $EI_{macro2} = 0.18$ — emergence fails.
### 5.2 Robustness across measures of causation
### 5.3 A formal specification of the pipeline
```tla+
------------------------------ MODULE CausalEmergence ------------------------------
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets, Reals
CONSTANTS MicroStates, MacroBlocks   \* MacroBlocks: partition of MicroStates
VARIABLES microTpm                   \* [MicroStates -> [MicroStates -> Real]]
MacroTpm == [u \in MacroBlocks |->
              [v \in MacroBlocks |->
                (Sum({microTpm[x][y] : x \in u, y \in v}) / Cardinality(u))]]
EI(tpm, states) == \* (1/|S|) * Sum_x D_KL(tpm[x] || average_effect)
  LET n == Cardinality(states)
      eff == [y \in states |-> (Sum({tpm[x][y] : x \in states}) / n)]
  IN (Sum({KLDiv(tpm[x], eff) : x \in states}) / n)
CausalEmergence == EI(MacroTpm, MacroBlocks) > EI(microTpm, MicroStates)
=============================================================================
```
### 5.4 The Rosas et al. refinement: from potential to actual dynamics
- **Causal decoupling**: the macro variable's future is predicted by its own past beyond what micro details add — the macro is *self-sufficient*;
- **Downward causation**: the macro variable influences the future micro state beyond micro self-prediction.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work
### 6.1 Dewhurst's critique: neither causal nor emergent?
The sharpest philosophical response comes from Joe Dewhurst [4][5]. His argument has two prongs:
1. **"Not causal" (in any robust sense).** Hoel's $EI$ is causal only in a *deflationary interventionist* sense: it tracks how informative a macro-intervention is about the future, given a stipulated intervention distribution. But interventionism itself presupposes rather than explains causation, and the measure's value is hostage to the theorist's choice of input distribution. Dewhurst argues this yields at most an *epistemic* virtue — macro descriptions are more *useful* — rather than the *ontological* discovery of novel causal powers [4][5].
2. **"Not emergent" (in any strong sense).** What Hoel demonstrates, on Dewhurst's reading, is a form of *weak, epistemic* emergence: the macro description is computationally or informationally more tractable. Anyone hoping for *strong* emergence — ontologically novel causal powers irreducible to the micro-dynamics — will be disappointed, because the macro TPM is by construction *derived* from the micro TPM via the block-averaging definition [4].
Dewhurst's constructive proposal is that Hoel's result is best interpreted through Ladyman and Ross's information-theoretic gloss on Dennettian **real patterns**: macro variables are *real patterns* — projectible, compressible, causally relevant regularities — and $EI$-maximization is a precise criterion for pattern-reality [5]. This reading preserves the theory's scientific value while deflating its metaphysical ambitions.
### 6.2 The "Distortions in a Map" objections
A 2022 *Philosophies* paper [6] raises three technical objections that deserve direct answers:
1. **Ambiguous interventions.** An intervention $do(U = u)$ is implemented as a uniform mixture over micro-interventions on the block's constituents — but those constituents may have *very different* micro-level effects. The macro-intervention is therefore "ambiguous" in a way micro-interventions are not, and the stipulation that fixes this is, the critics argue, *arbitrary* [6]. *Response:* the stipulation is not arbitrary but *operational* — it is what "intervene on the macrostate" can possibly mean given only the coarse-graining. Any coarser intervention concept must bottom out in micro-operations.
2. **Non-commutativity of abstraction and marginalization.** Coarse-graining (abstraction) and iterating the dynamics (marginalization over time steps) do not commute: the macro TPM computed from one-step micro transitions differs from the macro TPM computed from multi-step dynamics [6]. *Response:* this is a genuine constraint — it means $EI$ values are indexed to a time-scale, and emergence claims must specify one. CE 2.0's causal path [9] can be seen as an attempt to make this multi-scale dependence explicit rather than hidden.
3. **Reference-distribution dependence.** $EI$'s value depends on the uniform intervention distribution, a *normative* (information-theoretic) choice, not an empirical one [6][7]. *Response:* Rosas et al. [7] answer this empirically by moving to stationary-distribution-based measures; Hoel would answer that the uniform distribution is the *only* choice that explores the system's full causal repertoire rather than its historically contingent trajectory.
### 6.3 Roskies' reservations: noise, laws, and powers
Adela Roskies, responding to Hoel's program, grants that effective information might "track real ontological emergence, but this requires some new philosophical thinking about the nature of laws, powers and how they relate" [10]. She also voices the physicist's objection that *noise* — the engine of degeneracy-suppression gains — "doesn't really exist": noise is what physicists call the stuff their models leave out, and a fully-specified micro-dynamics has no noise at all, hence no room for the macro to win [10].
This objection is fair but answerable. First, the framework is *relative to a description*: given the micro TPM *as specified* (which for any real model includes unmodeled fluctuations as stochasticity), the macro can still be the causally dominant scale of *that model*. Second, Hoel and Albantakis respond that perfect micro-knowledge is impossible even in principle and uninformative even if possible: causation is "what you need to give structure to the universe" — the quantity that identifies where *entities* are [10]. The macro variables that maximize $EI$ are, on this view, the best candidates for the system's real entities.
### 6.4 Open problems
- **Search tractability.** Exhaustive search over partitions scales with Bell numbers; principled heuristics (spectral clustering, modularity-based black-boxing) are needed for large systems [8].
- **Continuous and quantum systems.** The discrete-state formalism does not obviously generalize; a differential-geometric or operator-algebraic version of $EI$ remains open.
- **Temporal grain.** Non-commutativity [6] demands a joint space–time coarse-graining theory; CE 2.0's causal path [9] is a first step.
- **The identical-state-space constraint.** Requiring $|X| = |Y|$ excludes open systems and systems with changing state spaces — a significant restriction for biological applications [6].
- **Metaphysical status.** Whether EI-maximizing macrostates are *ontologically* distinguished (Roskies' challenge [10]) or merely *epistemically* privileged (Dewhurst's diagnosis [4][5]) remains the field's central unresolved question.

## 7 Conclusion
Hoel's causal emergence theory is the first proposal to convert the slogan "the macro can beat the micro" into a computable, falsifiable quantity. Its core achievements are threefold. *First*, effective information provides a principled measure of causal power that decomposes into determinism and degeneracy, making precise *why* a coarse-graining can amplify causation [1][2]. *Second*, the error-correcting-code equivalence shows that macroscopic description is not a concession to human cognitive limits but an instance of Shannon's deepest idea: redundancy defeats noise [2]. *Third*, the framework is empirically discriminating — it finds emergence in Boolean networks and neural populations where the causal structure warrants it and withholds the verdict where it does not [7][8], and it is robust across measures of causation [8].
The criticisms are substantial but, in the author's judgment, misaimed at the theory's center of gravity. Dewhurst is right that Hoel demonstrates weak, epistemic emergence rather than strong, ontological emergence [4][5] — but weak emergence, when *quantified* and *falsifiable*, is exactly what the sciences need, and the real-patterns interpretation [5] gives it philosophical respectability. The technical objections about ambiguous interventions and non-commutativity [6] identify genuine boundary conditions, not refutations, and have already catalyzed refinements (CE 2.0 [9], $\Phi$ID-based estimators [7]). Roskies' demand for new thinking about laws and powers [10] is the right final word: the mathematics is secure; the metaphysics is unfinished.
The deepest lesson is a reversal of the reductionist default. We are accustomed to thinking of the microscopic as the *real* and the macroscopic as the *shorthand*. Hoel's mathematics suggests the opposite can be true: where a coarse-graining maximizes effective information, the macroscopic variable is not a summary of the real causes — it *is* the real cause, and the microscopic description, drowned in noise and degeneracy, is the distortion [2][6]. Whether that inversion is ultimately ontological or epistemic, it is now a question we can *compute* — and that is progress.
