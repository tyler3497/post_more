# Axiomatic Weak Memory Models: C11 RC11, TSO Store Buffers, and Verified Compilation to ARMv8 and POWER

## Abstract

Modern multiprocessors and optimizing compilers conspire to violate sequential consistency. The 2011 C and C++ standards responded with the axiomatic C11 memory model, defined over execution graphs linked by *sequenced-before*, *reads-from*, and *modification-order* relations—yet C11 proved broken in three ways: its SC-atomics semantics was simultaneously too strong for standard source transformations and too weak for sound POWER compilation, and its relaxed fragment admitted *out-of-thin-air* reads. This thesis develops the full arc of the repair. We present RC11, which reworks the coherence and SC axioms into `irreflexive(hb;eco?)` and `acyclic(psc)` and restores sequential consistency for race-free programs [1]. We give the operational x86-TSO model with per-thread store buffers [4], survey verified compilation schemes to x86, ARMv8, and POWER under the multicopy-atomicity revision [2], and examine the promising semantics, whose *promise* mechanism admits hardware reorderings while provably excluding out-of-thin-air behaviors [3], alongside model checking with Herd7 and Dartagnan [5].

## 1 Introduction

*Sequential consistency* (SC)—Lamport's 1979 proposal that every execution appear as an interleaving of thread program orders—remains the gold standard of programmer intuition, but neither hardware nor compilers respect it: processors reorder independent accesses to hide latency, and compilers routinely perform *roach-motel* motion and expression linearization. The result is a family of *weak memory models* permitting executions no interleaving can explain.

Two formal styles dominate the field. *Operational* models describe abstract machines—store buffers, speculative pipelines—whose transitions generate the architecturally allowed behaviors. *Axiomatic* models define *candidate executions* as event graphs accepted or rejected by relational predicates such as acyclicity. The C11 model of Batty et al. [6] chose the axiomatic style: it is specified entirely through *sequenced-before* (`sb`), *reads-from* (`rf`), *modification order* (`mo`), and the derived *happens-before* (`hb`).

This choice bought hardware independence but at a steep price. Within six years of publication, three deep flaws in the C11 model had been exposed:

1. **SC semantics too strong.** The C11 SC axioms forbade executions that simple, standard program transformations—linearizing expressions, reordering independent accesses across SC operations—can produce, meaning the model could not serve as a semantics for compiler intermediate languages [1].
2. **SC semantics too weak.** Contrary to published claims, the two standard compilation schemes of C11 to POWER (leading-sync and trailing-sync) were both *unsound*: the IRIW-with-SC-acquires litmus test exposed a concrete counterexample [1].
3. **Out-of-thin-air reads.** The relaxed fragment permitted *causality cycles* in which a thread reads a value that no write in the program ever produced—a *thin-air* read—destroying every hope of invariant-based or type-safety reasoning [3].

This thesis traces the research program that repaired, replaced, and verified these models. Our contributions, pitched as a doctoral survey, are: a complete presentation of **RC11** in closed relational form with its key metatheory; an exposition of **x86-TSO** as a store-buffer machine and the transformation-based characterization separating TSO from POWER/ARM; a catalog of **verified compilation schemes** to x86, ARMv8, and POWER; an account of the **promising semantics** and its *promise*/certification mechanism; and a survey of the **model-checking** infrastructure (Herd7, Dartagnan, CppMem) validating models against silicon.

Section 2 reviews the models and related work; §3 describes our methodology; §4 is the deep dive; §5 reports empirical and theoretical results; §6 discusses limitations; §7 concludes.

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The C11 axiomatic model

Batty et al. gave the first complete formalization of the C++11 concurrency semantics [6]. A *candidate execution* comprises events `E` (reads `R`, writes `W`, RMWs, fences `F`), *sequenced-before* `sb` (program order, plus `asw` thread-creation edges), *reads-from* `rf ⊆ W × R`, and per-location total *modification order* `mo ⊆ W × W`, deriving: **synchronizes-with** `sw` (release/acquire pairs via release sequences), **happens-before** `hb = (sb ∪ sw)⁺`, **from-read** `fr = (rf⁻¹; mo)`, and **extended coherence** `eco = (rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)⁺`.

Consistency was imposed by coherence conditions per location, *happens-before* consistency of `rf`, RMW atomicity, and clauses governing SC atomics via a total order `S`. *Data races*—conflicting accesses unordered by `hb`—on non-atomics yield undefined behavior (*catch fire*), while races on atomics are permitted but axiomatically constrained.

### 2.2 The out-of-thin-air problem

The most philosophically damaging flaw of C11 was its tolerance of *out-of-thin-air* (OOTA) executions. Consider the load-buffering program with a data dependency:

```
a := x_rlx; // 1        b := y_rlx; // 1
y_rlx := a;             x_rlx := b;
```

C11 admitted the outcome `a = b = 1` even though no write of `1` exists in the source: each read was justified *speculatively* by the other. Such *causality cycles* break type safety and invalidate compiler reasoning. Proposed fixes faced a dilemma: forbidding cycles syntactically (`acyclic(sb ∪ rf)`) is *too strong*—it bans load-buffering that POWER and ARM exhibit—while permitting dependency-respecting cycles is *too weak*. This tension drove a decade of research [3].

### 2.3 Operational models: x86-TSO and beyond

Owens, Sarkar, and Sewell gave the definitive formal model of Intel/AMD multiprocessors, **x86-TSO** [4]. TSO is *total store order*: each hardware thread has a FIFO *store buffer*; writes enter the local buffer and drain to shared memory asynchronously; reads first consult the local buffer (*store forwarding*) and otherwise hit shared memory. This single mechanism explains exactly the observable relaxations of x86: store→load reordering (the `SB` litmus test) is allowed, while load→load, load→store, and store→store reorderings are not. Lahav et al. proved TSO behaviors are *exactly* those obtainable from SC by two local program transformations, whereas non-multicopy-atomic models (C11, POWER, ARM) admit no such characterization [1].

POWER and pre-2018 ARM were far wilder: non-multicopy-atomic, with cumulativity subtleties in their barriers (`sync`, `lwsync`, `DMB`) and observable behaviors such as IRIW. The turning point came when ARM revised its architecture to guarantee **multicopy atomicity** (MCA)—a write, once visible to one observer, is visible to all—and Pulte et al. produced matching axiomatic and operational models for ARMv8, dramatically simplifying the landscape [2].

### 2.4 Compilation and verification tools

Compiling C11 to hardware requires *mapping schemes* [7]: on x86, release/acquire map to plain `MOV`, SC stores need `XCHG` or `MFENCE`; on POWER, leading/trailing-sync schemes bracket accesses with `sync`/`lwsync`; on ARMv8, `LDAR`/`STLR` or `DMB`-bracketed accesses. Proving every hardware behavior of compiled code is allowed by the source model is the *verified compilation* problem.

Three complementary tool families emerged: **CppMem** [6] enumerates C11 candidate executions; **Herd7** [5], a parametric *axiomatic simulator*, evaluates CAT-language models on litmus tests and compares against hardware observations from **Litmus7**; **Dartagnan** extends this to *bounded model checking* of concurrent C under weak memory via SMT. These tools turned memory-model research into an experimental science.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the established discipline of the weak-memory community, combining axiomatic specification, litmus-test-driven validation, mechanized proof, and hardware testing.

**Axiomatic specification.** We work with candidate executions `G = (E, lab, sb, asw, rf, mo, sc)` where `lab` assigns each event an action (location, value, access mode). Derived relations are defined once and reused across models:

| Relation | Definition | Intuition |
|---|---|---|
| `hb` | `(sb ∪ asw ∪ sw)⁺` | happens-before |
| `eco` | `(rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)⁺` | extended coherence order |
| `fr` | `rf⁻¹; mo` | read overwritten by later write |
| `psc` | see §4.1 | SC-before order |

Model consistency is a conjunction of *global* relational predicates—acyclicity and irreflexivity—which Herd7 can evaluate directly on the execution graph.

**Litmus tests.** We use the standard corpus of litmus tests as *differential probes* between models and hardware:

1. `MP` (message passing): tests release/acquire synchronization; forbidden outcome `r1=1, r2=0`.
2. `SB` (store buffering): two threads store then load; outcome `r1=r2=0` allowed on TSO/POWER/ARM, forbidden under SC.
3. `LB` (load buffering): outcome `r1=r2=1` allowed on POWER/ARM, forbidden on TSO.
4. `IRIW` (independent reads of independent writes): the classic non-MCA discriminator; forbidden on ARMv8 after the MCA revision.
5. `WRC`, `ISA2`, `2+2W`: probe cumulativity and coherence subtleties.

**Proof techniques.** Compilation correctness is proved by *execution-graph simulation*: from a consistent target execution, construct a consistent source execution with the same observable behavior. Transformation validity uses *monotonicity* (weakening modes preserves consistency); the promising semantics is verified by *certification* over thread-local futures.

**Tool validation.** We cross-check every claimed allowed/forbidden outcome against Herd7 simulations of the CAT models and against published hardware-observation campaigns [2][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The RC11 Axiomatic Model and the Out-of-Thin-Air Repair

RC11 [1] keeps C11's event structure but replaces its defective axiom list with four crisp conditions. With `sw` release/acquire synchronizes-with, `hb = (sb ∪ asw ∪ sw)⁺`, `eco = (rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)⁺`, define *SC-before*:

> **Definition (psc).** `psc = ([F^sc]; hb; [F^sc]) ∪ ([F^sc]; hb; eco; hb; [F^sc]) ∪ ...` — formally, `psc` relates SC events (and SC fences) ordered by happens-before possibly interleaved with coherence edges, capturing the constraints C11 placed on its total SC order `S` but without requiring `S` to exist as a global total order.

The four RC11 axioms are then:

> **Axiom (NO-THIN-AIR).** `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)`. No causality cycles: reads-from edges may not close a cycle with program order.
>
> **Axiom (COHERENCE).** `irreflexive(hb; eco?)`. A happens-before path followed by *at most one* coherence step may not form a cycle.
>
> **Axiom (ATOMICITY).** No `fr; mo`-adjacent pair straddles an RMW: read-modify-writes are indivisible with respect to modification order.
>
> **Axiom (SC).** `acyclic(psc)`. The SC-before relation is acyclic.

Three aspects deserve emphasis. First, **COHERENCE** is strictly weaker than the naive `acyclic(hb ∪ eco)`: allowing at most one `eco` step after an `hb` path permits the `SB` pattern (two `fr` segments between `hb` segments) while still forbidding message-passing's single-`fr` cycle—*reconstructing* the intended release/acquire semantics.

Second, **NO-THIN-AIR** is the *conservative* OOTA fix: it forbids `sb ∪ rf` cycles outright, ruling out thin-air reads at the cost of some hardware-realizable load-buffering executions—a deliberate trade, since compilers do not preserve syntactic dependencies [3].

Third, the **SC** axiom repairs both C11 SC defects: constraining only `psc` admits executions that expression linearization produces (fixing "too strong"), while the fence-inclusive formulation restores trailing-sync POWER compilation soundness (fixing "too weak") [1]. RC11 enjoys **DRF-SC**, validates *roach-motel* reorderings, sequentialization, and deordering of independent accesses, and its POWER compilation proofs are mechanized in Coq.

```haskell
-- RC11 consistency, executable sketch in the Herd7 CAT fragment style
let hb  = (sb | asw | sw)+
let eco = (rf | mo | fr)+
acyclic (sb | rf)            as NO_THIN_AIR
irreflexive (hb ; eco?)      as COHERENCE
acyclic psc                  as SC
empty (fr & (mo^-1 ; mo))    as ATOMICITY  -- no RMW straddling
```

### 4.2 x86-TSO: Store Buffers and the Operational Story

x86-TSO [4] is the paradigmatic *operational* model, illuminating what axiomatic models must encode. Its state comprises shared memory `m : Loc → Val`, per-thread FIFO store buffers `b_t`, and a global lock flag for `LOCK`'d instructions.

Transitions are few: **enqueue** a write to the buffer, **dequeue** the oldest write to memory, **read** (own buffer first—*store forwarding*—then memory), and **fence** (`MFENCE` drains the buffer). The model's entire weakness flows from one fact: a read may be satisfied from the buffer *before* an earlier-buffered write to a different location drains.

> **Theorem 1 (TSO characterization).** An execution is admitted by x86-TSO iff it is admitted by SC after applying (i) elimination of redundant read-after-write pairs and (ii) reordering of independent write/read pairs. In particular, TSO = SC + store→load reordering, nothing more [1].

The litmus consequences are sharp and empirically confirmed:

| Test | Outcome | TSO | SC |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SB` | `r1 = r2 = 0` | ✅ allowed | ❌ forbidden |
| `LB` | `r1 = r2 = 1` | ❌ forbidden | ❌ forbidden |
| `MP` | `r1 = 1, r2 = 0` | ❌ forbidden | ❌ forbidden |
| `IRIW` | four reads disagree | ❌ forbidden | ❌ forbidden |

TSO is thus *multicopy-atomic* and *causally strong*: once a write drains to memory, all threads observe it. The axiomatic rendering of TSO is correspondingly simple—essentially RC11's axioms plus `acyclic(sb)`-style store-order constraints—and Herd7's TSO CAT model agrees with the operational model on the full litmus corpus [5].

The deeper lesson [1] is a *separation result*: TSO is SC under two local transformations, but **no** such characterization exists for C11, POWER, or ARM—non-multicopy-atomic models are operationally irreducible to reordering stories, which is why the axiomatic style remains indispensable.

### 4.3 Verified Compilation: Fences and Mappings to ARMv8 and POWER

Verified compilation asks: for a mapping `⟦·⟧` from source operations to target instruction sequences, is every target behavior of `⟦P⟧` admitted by the source semantics of `P`? The industrial mappings [7] are:

| C11 operation | x86-TSO | ARMv8 (MCA) | POWER |
|---|---|---|---|
| relaxed load | `MOV` | `LDR` | `LD` |
| relaxed store | `MOV` | `STR` | `ST` |
| acquire load | `MOV` | `LDAR` *or* `LDR; DMB LD` | `LD; CMP; BC; ISYNC` |
| release store | `MOV` | `STLR` *or* `DMB ST; STR` | `LWSYNC; ST` |
| SC load | `MOV` | `LDAR` | trailing/leading `SYNC` |
| SC store | `XCHG` *or* `MOV; MFENCE` | `STLR` | trailing/leading `SYNC` |
| SC fence | `MFENCE` | `DMB SY` | `SYNC` |

Three verification milestones structure this area. **First**, the x86 mappings were proved sound early: TSO's strength means plain `MOV`s suffice except for SC stores [6]. **Second**, POWER was the hard case: the original C11 soundness claims for leading-sync and trailing-sync were *wrong*—refuted by IRIW-with-SC-acquires—and RC11's repaired SC axiom is what made corrected schemes provable [1], including a *fake control dependency* after every relaxed read whose correctness RC11 first established.

**Third**, ARMv8's MCA revision [2] transformed verification: before MCA, proofs had to handle non-multicopy-atomicity and elaborate `DMB` cumulativity; after MCA, ARMv8 is *simpler than POWER*, with `LDAR`/`STLR` providing acquire/release in hardware and the mapping proof reducing to an axiom-refinement check Herd7 can partially automate [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Compilation soundness, RC11 → POWER).** The trailing-sync mapping is sound: every POWER behavior of compiled RC11 code is an RC11-consistent behavior of the source. The proof is mechanized in Coq [1].

Barrier *cumulativity*—a barrier orders not only its thread's accesses but also accesses it has *observed*—remains the subtlest ingredient: POWER's `lwsync` is cumulative as release/acquire requires, `sync` adds SC-restoring strength, and ARMv8's `DMB` is analogous [2]. Axiomatically, cumulativity is the interaction of `hb` with `eco`—exactly what RC11's `irreflexive(hb;eco?)` was designed to absorb.

### 4.4 The Promising Semantics: Operational Hope for OOTA

RC11's NO-THIN-AIR axiom solves OOTA by *fiat*—forbidding `sb ∪ rf` cycles outright, including ones hardware exhibits. The **promising semantics** of Kang et al. [3] takes the opposite, operational route: it *explains* which cycles are real and which are thin air, via a machine with two novel ingredients.

The machine pairs thread-local state with a *memory* of timestamped messages `⟨x : v @ (f, t]⟩` and per-thread *views* of each location's timeline. Execution is SC-style interleaving, except a thread may take a **promise** step: it inserts a future write into memory, letting others read it *before* it executes. The guard is **certification**: a promise is legal only if the thread can *fulfill* it executing alone from its current state—the promised write must not rely on the promise's own effects. Thin-air cycles fail certification.

> **Theorem 3 (No OOTA, DRF, compilation).** The promising semantics (i) admits no out-of-thin-air behaviors—every read value is produced by a certifiable execution; (ii) satisfies DRF-RA/SC guarantees; (iii) validates standard compiler optimizations (reordering, elimination, introduction of independent accesses); and (iv) soundly compiles to x86-TSO and POWER. Most results are mechanized in Coq [3].

The promising semantics also gives racy programs defined (if weak) semantics instead of C11's *catch-fire* undefined behavior—a prerequisite for type-safe languages like Java. Its cost is complexity: certification quantifies over thread-local futures, so axiomatic models and Herd7 remain the practical vehicle for litmus reasoning while the promising semantics supplies the *foundational* justification [3][5].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Results

**Litmus-test agreement.** We validated the axiomatic models against the standard corpus using Herd7's CAT implementations. The table below summarizes representative outcomes (✅ = allowed, ❌ = forbidden), cross-checked with published hardware campaigns [2][5]:

| Test | RC11 | C11 (orig.) | x86-TSO (hw) | ARMv8 (hw) | POWER (hw) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SB` (`r1=r2=0`) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ observed | ✅ observed | ✅ observed |
| `LB` (`r1=r2=1`) | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ observed | ✅ observed |
| `MP+relacq` | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| `IRIW` | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ (post-MCA) | ✅ observed |
| `WRC` | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ observed |
| `LB+ctrl` (thin-air shape) | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ (hw dep.) | ❌ (hw dep.) |

Two rows carry the central empirical messages: the `LB` row shows RC11 *deliberately* forbidding a hardware-observable behavior—the price of its OOTA fix—while the `IRIW` row shows ARMv8's MCA revision *removing* a hardware behavior, enabling [2]'s simplified models.

**Mechanization scale.** The Coq developments for RC11/POWER compilation [1] and the promising semantics [3] each span tens of thousands of lines—the latter actively maintained for modern Rocq toolchains—showing weak-memory metatheory has crossed into machine-checked mathematics.

**Bounded model checking.** Dartagnan encodes axiomatic models as SMT constraints and has verified lock-free algorithms (Peterson's, Treiber stacks) under RC11-like models, finding bugs testing missed—evidence the axiomatic style scales beyond litmus tests [5].

**Compilation proof coverage.** Soundness is established for RC11→x86-TSO, RC11→POWER (trailing-sync and repaired leading-sync) [1], promising-semantics→x86-TSO/POWER [3], and C11/RC11→ARMv8 under MCA models [2]—composing to end-to-end theorems: race-free RC11 programs compiled to ARMv8 exhibit only SC behaviors.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**The OOTA problem is not closed.** RC11's `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)` is *sufficient* but not *necessary*—it forbids hardware-realizable executions—while the promising semantics buys precision through the heavy certification mechanism. A *simple, complete* characterization of physical causality cycles remains elusive; "Promising 2.0", *weakestmo*, and event-structure models continue the search.

**Consume and dependencies.** C++'s `memory_order_consume` tried to expose hardware dependency-ordering cheaply, but compilers could not implement its definition and it is effectively deprecated—syntactic dependencies are not preserved by optimization [1].

**Mixed-size accesses.** Real programs access overlapping byte ranges at different widths; the models here assume single-size accesses. Mixed-size extensions [2] reveal behaviors (a read observing *half* a write) that strain the `rf`/`mo` vocabulary and remain only partially integrated with RC11 coherence.

**Language evolution.** C++20 strengthened SC atomics further, and the model keeps evolving. Each revision reopens compilation-soundness proofs for re-mechanization—an argument for architectures like RC11's that localize SC reasoning in the single `psc` axiom.

**Verification scale.** Dartagnan-style BMC handles bounded programs, but *unbounded* verification of lock-free structures under weak memory remains heroic; bridging axiomatic models with concurrent separation logics (GPS, Iris-based logics atop the promising semantics) is active research.

---

## 7 Conclusion

A decade of research transformed the C11 memory model from a flawed standard into a verified stack. **RC11** [1] repaired sequential consistency with four axioms—`acyclic(sb ∪ rf)`, `irreflexive(hb;eco?)`, atomicity, `acyclic(psc)`—restoring DRF-SC and sound POWER compilation. **x86-TSO** [4] showed some hardware *is* just SC plus store buffers, while [1]'s separation result proved genuinely weak models resist reordering-story reductions. The **ARMv8 MCA revision** [2] simplified architecture and models alike. The **promising semantics** [3] gave the first operational account excluding out-of-thin-air executions while validating optimizations. **Herd7/Dartagnan** [5] made axiomatic models executable and hardware-testable.

The through-line is methodological: *axiomatic models for specification and tooling, operational models for foundational justification, mechanized proof for trust*. The out-of-thin-air problem shows the gap between these styles is not yet closed—but the machinery built to narrow it is among the most rigorous work in programming-language semantics.
