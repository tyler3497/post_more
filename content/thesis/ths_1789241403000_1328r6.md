# Thermoelectricity from Boltzmann Transport Theory: Phonon Engineering, the Slack and Glassman Bounds, and the Push Beyond ZT = 3

## Abstract

Thermoelectric energy conversion is governed by the dimensionless figure of merit *ZT* = *S*²σ*T*/κ, whose optimization requires the simultaneous and contradictory tuning of electronic and vibrational transport. This thesis develops the field from the semiclassical Boltzmann transport equation in the relaxation-time approximation, through Slack's phonon-glass electron-crystal design criterion and the associated bounds on transport coefficients, to the amorphous glass-limit floor on lattice thermal conductivity. We derive the coupled transport integrals, the quality factor *B*, and the Mahan–Sofo upper bound on *ZT*, and show how modern phonon engineering — spanning Umklapp, point-defect, boundary, and resonant-rattler scattering across nanometre to micrometre scales — has driven laboratory records from the *ZT* ≈ 1 plateau of Bi₂Te₃ to *ZT* = 2.6 in SnSe single crystals and *ZT* ≈ 3.1 in purified polycrystalline SnSe [3,4]. The analysis closes by delineating the fundamental limits, metrological controversies, and device-level constraints that bound the push beyond *ZT* = 3.

## 1 Introduction

In 1821 Thomas Johann Seebeck observed that a circuit of two dissimilar conductors develops an electromotive force when its junctions sit at different temperatures; thirteen years later Peltier demonstrated the converse, that a current driven through such a junction pumps heat [5]. Lord Kelvin's relations of 1851–1854 unified the two effects as manifestations of a single reversible coupling between charge and entropy currents — *solid-state heat engines with no moving parts*.

The modern language of the field is the dimensionless figure of merit

$$ZT = \frac{S^2 \sigma T}{\kappa_e + \kappa_L}$$

where *S* is the Seebeck coefficient, σ the electrical conductivity, *T* the absolute temperature, and κ_e and κ_L the electronic and lattice contributions to the thermal conductivity. Device efficiency at hot- and cold-side temperatures *T_h*, *T_c* follows from

$$\eta = \frac{\Delta T}{T_h}\,\frac{\sqrt{1+ZT}-1}{\sqrt{1+ZT}+T_c/T_h} \quad [6]$$

so that *ZT* ≈ 1 yields efficiencies near 10%, while *ZT* ≈ 3 approaches 25–30% — the threshold at which waste-heat recovery becomes broadly competitive with mechanical engines [5].

Three tensions structure everything that follows:

1. **Interdependence.** *S*, σ, and κ_e are not independent knobs: they are different moments of the *same* non-equilibrium carrier distribution, coupled through the carrier concentration and the band structure.
2. **Length-scale separation.** Electrons scatter on nanometre scales while the phonons that carry heat span nanometres to microns — a mismatch that makes *selective* phonon engineering possible [6].
3. **Bounds.** There are hard floors (the glass limit on κ_L [2]) and soft ceilings (the Mahan–Sofo bound on the power factor [7]) that together delimit what chemistry can achieve.

This thesis argues that the leap from *ZT* ≈ 1 to *ZT* > 3 is not a story of a single breakthrough but of the *systematic* dismantling of these tensions: Boltzmann transport theory tells us exactly which knobs exist, the Slack and glass-limit bounds tell us how far each knob can turn, and phonon engineering — hierarchical, multi-scale, and increasingly first-principles-guided — has turned them nearly to their stops.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Boltzmann transport formalism

The workhorse of thermoelectric theory is the semiclassical Boltzmann transport equation (BTE) for the carrier distribution *f*(**r**, **k**, *t*). In steady state, under weak electric field **E** and temperature gradient ∇*T*, the linearized BTE in the relaxation-time approximation (RTA) gives the charge and heat current densities as

$$\mathbf{J} = L_{11}\mathbf{E} + L_{12}(-\nabla T/T), \qquad \mathbf{J}_Q = L_{21}\mathbf{E} + L_{22}(-\nabla T/T)$$

with Onsager coefficients expressed through the transport distribution function Σ(*E*):

$$L_{ij} \propto K_{i+j-2}, \qquad K_n = \int \Sigma(E)\,(E-\mu)^n \left(-\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial E}\right) dE$$

where *f_0* is the Fermi–Dirac distribution and the transport distribution

$$\Sigma(E) = \sum_{\mathbf{k}} v(\mathbf{k})^2 \,\tau(\mathbf{k})\,\delta(E - E(\mathbf{k}))$$

encodes the band structure through the group velocity *v* and the momentum relaxation time τ. All three transport coefficients derive from moments of Σ(*E*):

| Coefficient | Expression | Physical content |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical conductivity | σ = *e*²*K_0* | Zeroth moment; total mobile charge |
| Seebeck coefficient | *S* = −(*K_1*)/(*eTK_0*) | First moment; *asymmetry* of conduction about μ |
| Electronic thermal conductivity | κ_e = (*K_2* − *K_1*²/*K_0*)/*T* | Second moment; variance of carried energy |

> **Theorem:** *For any band structure in the RTA, the Seebeck coefficient is proportional to the logarithmic energy derivative of the transport distribution at the Fermi level, S ∝ d ln Σ(E)/dE|_{E=μ} (the Mott formula). Large thermopower therefore demands sharp structure in Σ(E) — heavy bands, resonant states, or low dimensionality — which is invariably in tension with high mobility.*

Two classical relations follow. The **Wiedemann–Franz law**, κ_e = *L*σ*T*, holds with the Sommerfeld value only for degenerate, elastic scattering; in non-degenerate semiconductors the Lorenz number *L* is reduced and must be computed from the scattering mechanism [5]. The **Pisarenko relation** ties *S* to carrier concentration *n* for a single parabolic band, *S* ∝ *m*\* *T n*^(−2/3): raising *n* boosts σ but collapses *S*.

### 2.2 Slack's phonon-glass electron-crystal criterion

In the mid-1990s G. A. Slack articulated the design principle that has organized the field ever since: an ideal thermoelectric should scatter phonons as a *glass* while conducting electrons as a *crystal* — the **phonon-glass electron-crystal (PGEC)** concept [5]. The physical realizations are cage compounds — skutterudites such as CoSb₃ and clathrates — in which a covalent framework provides high carrier mobility while loosely bound "rattler" guest atoms execute large-amplitude, low-frequency vibrations that resonantly scatter acoustic phonons [5].

Slack and Tsoukala formalized the electronic side with rigorous bounds on the Seebeck coefficient and electrical conductivity attainable in real solids [1], showing that the power factor is fundamentally limited by the strength of electron–phonon coupling (the deformation potential Ξ). Chasmar and Stratton's earlier analysis is recovered in the modern **quality factor**

$$B = \frac{2k_B^2 \hbar}{3\pi}\,\frac{N_v C_l}{m_I^* \,\Xi^2 \,\kappa_L}\,T$$

where *N_v* is the valley degeneracy, *C_l* the elastic modulus, and *m_I*\* the inertial effective mass [6]. The maximum achievable *Z\={T}* is a universal, monotonically increasing function of *B*: at fixed chemistry, the only routes to higher *ZT* are raising *B* (band engineering) or lowering κ_L (phonon engineering).

### 2.3 The glass limit on lattice thermal conductivity

The lattice thermal conductivity in the phonon-gas picture is κ_L = (1/3)*C v* ℓ, with heat capacity *C*, sound velocity *v*, and phonon mean free path ℓ. Cahill, Watson, and Pohl asked how far ℓ can be pushed down and derived the celebrated **minimum thermal conductivity** by setting ℓ to half the phonon wavelength — the Ioffe–Regel condition at which the phonon quasiparticle itself dissolves [2]:

$$\kappa_{\min} = \left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{1/3} k_B n^{2/3} \sum_i v_i \left(\frac{T}{\Theta_i}\right)^2 \int_0^{\Theta_i/T} \frac{x^3 e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}\,dx$$

This "glass limit," typically 0.2–0.5 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ for heavy chalcogenides, is the floor beneath the PGEC program: one may approach it, but within the phonon-gas picture one cannot pass through it [2,8]. Recent unified treatments show that in strongly disordered or complex crystals, wave-like *diffuson* and coherence contributions provide additional channels that refine — but do not abolish — this bound, with κ_L^min bounded between roughly 0.1 and 2.6 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ across inorganic compounds [8].

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is theoretical and synthetic; its "methodology" is the combined computational–experimental toolkit by which the bounds above are evaluated and approached.

**First-principles phonon transport.** DFT yields harmonic force constants via perturbation theory and third-order constants from finite displacements. The phonon BTE is solved iteratively (ShengBTE, almaBTE, phono3py) including three-phonon, isotope, and boundary scattering. Mode Grüneisen parameters diagnose anharmonicity: SnSe's giant γ (up to ~4) quantitatively explains its ultralow κ_L [3].

**Electronic transport.** Boltzmann transport for electrons (BoltzTraP and successors) computes Σ(*E*) from DFT band structures under the constant- or energy-dependent-RTA, giving *S*, σ, and κ_e as functions of μ and *T*. Deformation-potential and electron–phonon Wannier-interpolated scattering rates refine τ(**k**) beyond the RTA.

**Experimental metrology.** The thermal conductivity is measured by laser-flash diffusivity (κ = α·ρ·*C_p*), the Seebeck coefficient and resistivity by steady-state or commercial ZEM systems, and carrier concentration by Hall effect. High-temperature heat capacity requires careful *C_p* determination, since errors propagate directly into κ and hence into reported *ZT* — a point of live controversy discussed in §6.

**Computational screening.** High-throughput workflows now evaluate κ_L^min, band degeneracy, and deformation potentials across tens of thousands of compounds, with machine-learned models trained on the unified minimum-conductivity formalism [8] flagging ultralow-κ candidates before any synthesis.

```python
# Illustrative: estimating the Cahill-Watson-Pohl minimum thermal
# conductivity for a heavy chalcogenide (high-T limit form)
import numpy as np

def kappa_min_highT(n_atoms_per_m3, v_long, v_trans):
    """n: atomic number density [m^-3]; v: sound velocities [m/s]."""
    kB = 1.380649e-23
    pref = (np.pi / 6.0) ** (1.0/3.0) * kB * n_atoms_per_m3 ** (2.0/3.0)
    # sum over 1 longitudinal + 2 transverse branches
    return 0.5 * pref * (v_long + 2.0 * v_trans)   # W / (m K)

# SnSe-like parameters: n ~ 3.6e28 m^-3, v_l ~ 3400, v_t ~ 1900 m/s
print(f"kappa_min ~ {kappa_min_highT(3.6e28, 3400, 1900):.2f} W/m/K")
# -> kappa_min ~ 0.42 W/m/K, the floor SnSe's 0.23 W/m/K strains against
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Slack bounds: phonon glass, electron crystal, and the quality factor

Slack's contribution was twofold: a *design rule* (PGEC) and *quantitative bounds* on what electronic transport can deliver [1,5]. The PGEC rule exploits the different length scales of electron and phonon scattering: electrons in a good crystal have mean free paths of a few nanometres, while heat-carrying acoustic phonons span 1 nm–1 µm. Structural features on the 1–100 nm scale therefore scatter phonons preferentially — the central asymmetry of the field.

The quality factor *B* makes the prescription quantitative [6]. Consider its ingredients:

- **Valley degeneracy *N_v*** enters linearly: converging multiple carrier pockets (band convergence) multiplies the density-of-states effective mass without increasing the inertial mass that degrades mobility.
- **Deformation potential Ξ** enters as Ξ⁻²: weak electron–phonon coupling is doubly rewarded, since it raises mobility *and* the power factor.
- **κ_L** enters inversely: every phonon-engineering advance directly inflates *B*.

The Slack and Tsoukala bounds [1] show that for a given Ξ there is a maximum attainable power factor; pushing beyond it requires changing the band structure itself — the subject of §4.4. The filled skutterudites (e.g., Yb-filled CoSb₃, *ZT* ≈ 1.3–1.5) remain the canonical PGEC validation: the covalent framework preserves mobility above 100 cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹ while Yb rattlers collapse κ_L toward the glass limit [5].

### 4.2 The glass limit and minimum lattice thermal conductivity

The Cahill–Watson–Pohl minimum [2] is derived by a beautifully simple argument: in the Debye model, replace the phonon mean free path by half a wavelength, ℓ(ω) = π*v*/ω, and integrate over the spectrum. The result depends only on the atomic density and sound velocities — no adjustable scattering parameters. For PbTe the bound sits near 0.36 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹; for SnSe near 0.4 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Two modern refinements matter. First, the **diffuson picture**: in complex unit cells with dozens of atoms, most vibrational modes are not propagating phonons at all but non-propagating diffusons, and the Allen–Feldman theory for glasses becomes the more appropriate starting point [8]. Second, the **unified first-principles model** of Chen and colleagues demonstrates that κ_L^min is approximately insensitive to disorder for a given parent compound — disorder merely shifts weight between phonon-gas and diffuson channels — and is bounded universally between ~0.1 and ~2.6 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ [8]. The practical moral: the glass limit is a genuine floor, and the remaining gains must come from the numerator of *ZT* (power factor, operating temperature) rather than the denominator.

Notably, SnSe's reported κ_L ≈ 0.23 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 973 K [3] *undercuts* the simple CWP estimate — a tension resolved by its extreme anharmonicity and by diffuson contributions beyond the phonon-gas picture [8].

### 4.3 Phonon engineering across length scales

If the bounds define the playing field, phonon engineering is the game.

| Mechanism | Targets | Length scale | Realization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Umklapp (phonon–phonon) | Short-λ phonons | ~1–10 nm | Intrinsic anharmonicity; lone-pair compounds |
| Point-defect / alloy scattering | Short–mid λ | atomic–5 nm | Mass & strain fluctuations (SiGe, PbTe–Se) |
| Resonant rattling | Mid λ, select ω | cage scale | Filled skutterudites, clathrates [5] |
| Nanoprecipitates / endotaxy | Mid λ | 5–50 nm | SrTe in PbTe; all-scale hierarchy [6] |
| Grain boundaries | Long λ | 50 nm–1 µm | Nanostructured bulk; ball-milled compacts |

The landmark demonstration is Biswas *et al.*'s **all-scale hierarchical architecturing** in PbTe–SrTe: atomic-scale alloying, endotaxial SrTe nanocrystals, and mesoscale grain boundaries scatter the *entire* phonon spectrum while the endotaxial alignment preserves carrier mobility, reaching *ZT* ≈ 2.2 at 915 K [6].

> **Theorem:** *Selective phonon scattering is possible if and only if the electron and phonon mean-free-path spectra are separable. In PbTe-class materials, ℓ_e ~ 1–10 nm while ℓ_ph spans 1 nm–1 µm; nanostructures in the 5–100 nm window therefore reduce κ_L by factors of 2–4 with <20% mobility penalty [6].*

### 4.4 Electronic-structure routes: resonant levels, band convergence, and low dimensionality

While κ_L approaches its floor, the power factor *S*²σ has been raised by reshaping Σ(*E*) near the Fermi level — precisely the Mott-formula imperative of §2.1 [5]:

1. **Resonant impurity levels.** Thallium doping in PbTe introduces a resonant state that distorts the density of states at *E_F*, doubling the effective mass contribution to *S* without a commensurate mobility loss (*ZT* ≈ 1.5 at 773 K) [5].
2. **Band convergence.** In PbTe₁₋ₓSeₓ alloys, the light-hole *L* band and heavy-hole Σ band converge with temperature; the effective valley degeneracy rises from 4 toward 12, boosting *S*²σ by ~50% [5].
3. **Low dimensionality.** Quantum wells and superlattices sharpen the density of states into steps, increasing the asymmetry factor *K_1*/*K_0*; Bi₂Te₃/Sb₂Te₃ superlattices reported *ZT* ≈ 2.4 at 300 K, the thin-film record [5].
4. **Modulation doping and energy filtering.** Spatially separating dopants from the conduction channel, or inserting potential barriers that filter cold carriers, raises mobility and the mean energy per carrier simultaneously [5].

Each route is a direct assault on the Slack–Tsoukala bounds [1]: rather than accepting the power-factor ceiling for a given Ξ, these strategies change the band structure — and therefore the ceiling itself.

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 The Mahan–Sofo upper bound

The deepest formal result in the field is due to Mahan and Sofo: within Boltzmann theory, the *best* thermoelectric is obtained when the transport distribution is a **delta function**, Σ(*E*) ∝ δ(*E* − *E_0*), i.e., when conduction occurs through an infinitely narrow energy channel [7]. In that limit the electronic thermal conductivity vanishes (zero variance of carried energy) while *S* remains finite, and *ZT* is bounded only by κ_L. Quantitatively, for a delta-function channel of width → 0 at optimal position, *ZT* can in principle diverge as κ_L → 0 — there is *no finite universal upper bound* on *ZT* from electronic considerations alone [7].

> **Theorem (Mahan–Sofo):** *The thermoelectric figure of merit is maximized by a transport distribution of vanishing energy width. Consequently, ZT has no finite upper bound derivable from electronic transport theory; the practical ceiling is set by the minimum achievable lattice thermal conductivity (the glass limit [2,8]) and by the finite widths imposed by thermal broadening (~k_B T) and disorder.*

This reframes the entire field: the *ZT* > 3 quest is, formally, a quest to make Σ(*E*) narrow (band engineering) while making κ_L small (phonon engineering), with the glass limit as the final arbiter.

### 5.2 The empirical record

| Material | Peak *ZT* | *T* (K) | Strategy | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bi₂Te₃ (bulk) | ~1.0 | 300 | Narrow gap, heavy bands | [5] |
| PbTe–SrTe (hierarchical) | ~2.2 | 915 | All-scale phonon scattering + band engineering | [6] |
| SnSe (single crystal, *b*-axis) | 2.6 ± 0.3 | 923 | Intrinsic anharmonicity, ultralow κ_L | [3] |
| SnSe (Br-doped, *a*-axis) | ~2.8 | 773 | n-type doping, anharmonicity | [4] |
| SnSe (purified polycrystal) | ~3.1 | 783 | Oxide removal + grain-boundary scattering | [4] |

The SnSe story is the empirical climax [3,4]. Zhao *et al.* reported κ_L ≈ 0.23 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 973 K in single crystals — attributed to the giant anharmonicity of the layered, lone-pair-distorted structure (Grüneisen parameters exceeding 4) — yielding *ZT* = 2.6 ± 0.3 at 923 K along the *b*-axis [3]. Subsequent work showed that polycrystalline SnSe surpasses the single crystal once surface tin oxides are removed: purified polycrystals reach *ZT* ≈ 3.1 at 783 K with κ_L ≈ 0.07 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, as grain boundaries add a further scattering channel atop the intrinsic anharmonicity [4].

Formally, these results are consistent with the Mahan–Sofo picture: SnSe combines a *multi-valley* valence band structure (high *N_v*, hence large *B*) with a κ_L pressed against — and arguably beneath, via diffuson channels — the naive glass limit [3,8]. The *ZT* ≈ 3 barrier has thus been breached in the laboratory, albeit at high temperature and under exacting synthesis conditions.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Bipolar conduction.** At high *T*, thermally excited minority carriers contribute oppositely to *S* while both carrier types conduct heat, collapsing the power factor and adding a bipolar term κ_b. Wide-gap engineering and heavy doping suppress it, but the operating window is bounded by *E_g* ≳ 6–10 *k_B T*.
2. **The RTA and beyond.** Much of the theoretical edifice — including the quality factor and the Mahan–Sofo bound — rests on the relaxation-time approximation. Full solutions of the coupled electron–phonon BTE and *ab initio* electron–phonon matrix elements are revising quantitative predictions, particularly for κ_e in narrow-gap systems.
3. **Metrological controversy.** The SnSe record has faced reproducibility challenges: reported κ values depend sensitively on sample density, *C_p* determination, and oxide content, with some groups measuring substantially higher κ_L in polycrystals [4]. The field now recognizes that *ZT* claims require full disclosure of measurement protocols.
4. **Device vs. material *ZT*.** Contact resistances, thermal-interface losses, and the need for compatible *n*- and *p*-type legs over the same Δ*T* typically halve realized efficiency; segmented architectures recover part of the loss at added complexity.
5. **Stability, toxicity, and cost.** The champion materials contain Pb, Te, or both; SnSe's advantage is precisely its earth-abundant, less toxic composition [3]. High-temperature sublimation, oxidation, and mechanical fragility remain deployment barriers.
6. **The denominator is exhausted.** With κ_L within a factor of two of the glass limit in the best materials [2,8], future gains must come predominantly from the power factor and from raising the operating temperature — both of which re-encounter the bipolar and stability limits.

---

## 7 Conclusion

From the Boltzmann transport equation, three inescapable conclusions flow. First, the Seebeck coefficient, conductivity, and electronic thermal conductivity are moments of a single transport distribution, so their joint optimization is a problem of *shaping* Σ(*E*) — the formal content of the Mott formula and of the Mahan–Sofo delta-function optimum [7]. Second, Slack's bounds and the quality factor *B* quantify exactly how far band engineering can push the power factor for a given electron–phonon coupling [1,5]. Third, the glass limit sets the floor beneath the lattice thermal conductivity, a floor that disorder, anharmonicity, and diffuson channels can approach but — within our present understanding — not breach [2,8].

The empirical record vindicates this framework: hierarchical phonon engineering [6] and the intrinsic anharmonicity of SnSe [3,4] have carried laboratory *ZT* from the decades-long plateau near 1 to values exceeding 3. The "phonon glass, electron crystal" is no longer an aspiration but an achieved materials-design paradigm, executed across length scales from the atomic to the mesoscopic.

The path beyond *ZT* = 3 is therefore clear in principle and arduous in practice: narrower transport distributions via resonant and topological band features, κ_L pinned at the glass limit via multi-scale scattering, and device architectures that preserve material gains through interfaces and thermal cycling. The bounds do not forbid *ZT* = 4 or 5; they merely dictate the price — paid in electronic-structure precision and phonon-spectrum control — that must be met to get there.
