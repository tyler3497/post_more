# Session Types and Multiparty Session Types: A Linear-Logic Foundation for Communication Safety, from Binary Duality to Projected Choreography

## Abstract

This thesis develops the theory of session types — behavioural types that prescribe the sequence of messages exchanged along a communication channel — from their binary origins to the multiparty generalisation grounded in linear logic. We present the binary calculus of Honda, Vasconcelos and Kubo [1], in which a channel endpoint's protocol is captured by a type and compatibility reduces to duality, then show how Honda, Yoshida and Carbone [2,3] lift the discipline to n-party asynchronous sessions through global types and projection. We give the Curry–Howard correspondence of Caires–Pfenning and Wadler [4], embedding classical linear logic so that cut elimination becomes communication and duality becomes linear negation. The central theorems — subject reduction, communication safety, session fidelity and progress — are stated and proved in outline for both binary and multiparty settings. Practical surface syntax is examined through the Scribble protocol language [6], and embeddings in Rust (affine types), Haskell (indexed monads) and F* (refinement types [8]) are compared. We conclude with the open frontier: dynamic participation, delegation with failures, dependent session types, and choreographic programming of microservice ecosystems.

## 1 Introduction

Concurrent and distributed software fails, overwhelmingly, at the *protocol* level. A client sends `Payment` before the server expects `Quote`; an actor receives `Commit` after `Rollback`; a microservice times out waiting on a message that, per its counterpart's code path, will never arrive. Traditional type systems are blind to these errors: they check that a value has type `Int`, never that a channel will next carry an `Int` at all. *Session types* close this gap by promoting the conversation itself to a type.

The intellectual lineage is precise. **Girard's linear logic** (1987) introduced resource-sensitive reasoning in which hypotheses must be used *exactly once* — an irresistible match for channels, which are consumed by communication [4]. **Milner's π-calculus** supplied the operational substrate: names, restriction, and synchronisation. Honda, Vasconcelos and Kubo (ESOP 1998) then gave the mature formulation — language primitives plus an ML-style discipline in which a session channel's type is a *protocol*, and two endpoints are compatible precisely when their types are *dual* [1].

> **Theorem: (Session fidelity, binary — Honda–Vasconcelos–Kubo [1]).** In a well-typed process, every communication along a session channel conforms to the channel's session type: sent values match expected types, and selected labels belong to the offered branches.

Binary sessions, however, describe a *dyad* — exactly two participants. Real protocols are multiparty: a buyer, a seller and a bank; a client, a load balancer and a fleet of workers. The decisive generalisation is due to **Honda, Yoshida and Carbone, "Multiparty Asynchronous Session Types" (POPL 2008)** [2], journal version in JACM 2016 [3]. Their invention — the *global type*, a choreographic description of the whole conversation, projected mechanically onto each participant — yields *deadlock freedom by construction* and won the ACM SIGPLAN Most Influential POPL Paper Award (2018) [5].

This thesis is organised as follows. Section 2 fixes binary session types: syntax, duality, subtyping, and the linear-logic correspondence. Section 3 presents the multiparty theory: global types, the projection operator with its merge condition, linearity of global types, and asynchronous subtyping. Section 4 deep-dives into (i) the Curry–Howard embedding of sessions as linear-logic propositions [4], (ii) the mechanised metatheory and its subtleties, (iii) the Scribble engineering language [6], (iv) embeddings in Rust, Haskell and F*, and (v) applications to actors and microservice choreography. Section 5 states and sketches the proofs of the core theorems. Section 6 catalogues limitations honestly. Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Binary Session Types: Syntax and Intuition

A session type describes one endpoint of a private channel. The grammar (following [1], modernised) is:

```
S, T ::= end                    -- terminated session
       | !A.S                   -- send value of type A, continue as S
       | ?A.S                   -- receive value of type A, continue as S
       | ⊕{ li : Si }i∈I        -- internal choice: select label li, continue Si
       | &{ li : Si }i∈I        -- external choice: offer branches li, continue Si
       | μX.S | X               -- recursion
```

Intuition anchors every constructor. `!Int.?Bool.end` is the client that sends an integer and awaits a boolean verdict. `⊕{ ok: end, err: !String.end }` chooses between success and an error carrying a message; `&` is its mirror. Crucially, session types are *linear resources*: a channel of type `!A.S` must be used for exactly one send — duplicating it would duplicate the send, discarding it would orphan the protocol.

### 2.2 Duality: Compatibility as Logical Negation

Two endpoints of one session must *fit*. The fit relation is **duality**, written `S̄`, an involution exchanging send/receive and internal/external choice:

| S | S̄ |
|---|---|
| `end` | `end` |
| `!A.S` | `?A.S̄` |
| `?A.S` | `!A.S̄` |
| `⊕{ li : Si }` | `&{ li : S̄i }` |
| `&{ li : Si }` | `⊕{ li : S̄i }` |

Duality is *undebatably correct*: a process offering `⊕{ neg: !Int.?Int.X, add: !Int.!Int.?Int.X, quit: end }` pairs only with one offering the dual `&{...}` [1]. The typing rule for session initiation (request/accept in [1]; `ν`-bound channel creation in modern presentations) requires the two endpoints to hold dual types, and *communication safety follows by construction*: the sender's `!` meets the receiver's `?`, the selector's `⊕` meets the offerer's `&`.

**Subtyping** (Gay–Hole 2005) refines compatibility: `⊕` is covariant in its label set (a subtype may *select fewer* labels), `&` is contravariant (a subtype may *offer more*), and `!`/`?` follow the familiar variance of the payload type. Subtyping is what makes session-typed libraries composable — a channel offering three operations can be passed where only two are demanded.

### 2.3 From Dyadic Interaction to Structured Communication

Honda's CONCUR 1993 paper introduced types for dyadic interaction; Takeuchi–Honda–Kubo (PARLE 1994) built an interaction-based language; the ESOP 1998 paper [1] is the synthesis — *language primitives and type discipline for structured communication-based programming*. Its primitives (`request`/`accept`, `send`/`receive`, `select`/`branch`, delegation via channel passing) translate into the summation-less asynchronous π-calculus, and its typing discipline is "à la ML": principal, syntax-directed, and proved sound. Two design decisions from [1] persist in every descendant:

1. **Sessions are private and linear.** A fresh session connects exactly the participants that initiate it; linearity prevents aliasing-induced races.
2. **Labels carry the control flow.** Branching is by explicit labels (`select l`), not by value inspection, so the type sees every decision the process can make.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *type-theoretic*: we define the calculi, state the theorems, and sketch proofs, following the primary sources [1,2,3,4]. Where the literature has mechanised results (notably the ECOOP 2025 mechanised subject-reduction proof [7]), we report the mechanisation's lessons — especially the subtlety of *explicit* versus *implicit* channels. For the engineering layer we analyse the Scribble toolchain's design papers and tutorial [6], and for language embeddings we compare Rust's affine types, Haskell's indexed-monad encodings, and the Session★ F* toolchain with refinement types [8]. The thesis is thus simultaneously a survey of the foundations and a critical comparison of their realisations.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Propositions as Sessions: The Linear-Logic Correspondence

The deepest fact about session types is that they are not an ad-hoc invention: they are *linear logic*. Caires–Pfenning (intuitionistic) and Wadler (classical, "Propositions as Sessions", JFP 2014) [4] exhibit a Curry–Howard correspondence:

| Linear logic | Session type | Process term |
|---|---|---|
| `A ⊗ B` (tensor) | `!A.S` — output | send then continue |
| `A ⅋ B` (par) | `?A.S` — input | receive then continue |
| `A ⊕ B` (plus) | `⊕{ inl: S, inr: T }` — select | internal choice |
| `A & B` (with) | `&{ inl: S, inr: T }` — offer | external choice |
| `1` | `end` | terminated |
| `A⊥` (negation) | `S̄` — duality | — |
| cut | parallel composition + restriction | communication |

Under this reading, **duality is linear negation**, **session initiation is cut**, and **communication is cut elimination**. The metatheory of linear logic transfers wholesale: deadlock freedom for the binary case is the process analogue of cut elimination's termination, and the involutive `S̿ = S` is de Morgan duality. Wadler's CP (Classical Processes) calculus makes the correspondence exact — typing derivations *are* proofs, and reduction *is* proof normalisation [4]. This is why session-type soundness proofs feel inevitable rather than engineered: they inherit the coherence of logic.

> **Theorem: (Wadler [4], informal).** Well-typed CP processes correspond bijectively to proofs in classical linear logic; process reduction corresponds to cut elimination. Hence every well-typed closed process is deadlock-free.

### 4.2 Multiparty Session Types: Global Types and Projection

Binary duality cannot express a three-party handshake: "buyer1 sends title to seller; seller sends quote to both buyers; buyer2 decides accept/reject…" The POPL 2008 breakthrough [2] introduces the **global type** `G` — a *choreography* describing the conversation from a neutral, global viewpoint:

```
G ::= p → q : ⟨U⟩. G          -- p sends message of type U to q, continue G
    | p → q : { li : Gi }i∈I  -- p selects label li toward q, continue Gi
    | μt.G | t | end          -- recursion and termination
```

Each participant `r` implements only its own role. The **projection** `G ↾ r` extracts r's local session type mechanically:

- `(p → q : ⟨U⟩. G) ↾ r` is `!⟨U⟩.(G ↾ r)` if `r = p`, `?⟨U⟩.(G ↾ r)` if `r = q`, and `G ↾ r` otherwise;
- `(p → q : { li : Gi }) ↾ r` is `⊕{ li : Gi ↾ r }` if `r = p`, `&{ li : Gi ↾ r }` if `r = q`, and the **merge** `⊔i (Gi ↾ r)` otherwise — defined only when all branches project identically for the uninvolved `r`, since `r` cannot observe which branch was taken.

The merge condition is the theory's famous sharp edge: some natural protocols are *not projectable*, and projection is partial. **Linearity of global types** rules out races on explicit channels: interactions sharing a channel must be causally ordered [7]. Asynchronous communication is modelled by queues, with an *asynchronous subtyping* permitting bounded reordering of sends before receives

> **Theorem: (Communication safety, session fidelity, progress — Honda–Yoshida–Carbone [2,3]).** If processes implementing each role are well-typed against the projections of a well-formed global type `G`, the multiparty session never exhibits a communication mismatch, each role's observable behaviour conforms to `G` (fidelity), and the session never deadlocks (progress).

The 2018 Most Influential Paper citation stresses exactly this: *previously session types could only model binary protocols; this paper generalised the theory to the multiparty case with asynchronous communications, preventing deadlock and communication errors in protocols involving any number of participants* [5].

### 4.3 The Scribble Engineering Layer

Theory becomes tooling in **Scribble**, the protocol description language for MPST [6]. Scribble gives global types a programmer-facing syntax with modules, subprotocol abstraction, parameterised roles, and — driven by industrial use cases — extensions the core theory initially lacked, such as asynchronous interrupts and subsession nesting. The canonical example is the two-buyer protocol:

```scribble
global protocol TwoBuyer(role Buyer1, role Buyer2, role Seller) {
  title(String) from Buyer1 to Seller;
  quote(Int) from Seller to Buyer1;
  quote(Int) from Seller to Buyer2;
  share(Int) from Buyer1 to Buyer2;
  choice at Buyer2 {
    accept() from Buyer2 to Seller;
    address(String) from Buyer2 to Seller;
    date(Date) from Seller to Buyer2;
  } or {
    reject() from Buyer2 to Seller;
  }
}
```

The Scribble toolchain projects each role to a **communicating finite-state machine (CFSM)** and generates APIs (Java, Python, F*, …) whose types *enforce* the local protocol at compile time; runtime monitors check dynamically-typed endpoints. The methodology it promotes — *specify globally, implement locally, verify by projection* — is MPST's central engineering contribution, and has been exercised on web services, financial protocols and parallel algorithms [6].

### 4.4 Embeddings: Rust, Haskell, Idris, F*

**Rust.** Rust's *affine* type system (move semantics, no implicit copy) is session types' natural host: a channel endpoint that cannot be duplicated or dropped accidentally is exactly linearity minus the "must use" obligation. Crates such as `session_types` encode `!A.S`/`?A.S` as generic structs with `Send`/`Recv` methods consuming `self`; the borrow checker rejects aliasing. The remaining gap — Rust permits *dropping* (affine, not linear) — is closed by convention or by `#[must_use]`.

```rust
// A session-typed channel: each method consumes the endpoint (affine move)
let (c, s): (Chan<Client, Send<Int, Recv<Bool, Eps>>>,
             Chan<Server, Recv<Int, Send<Bool, Eps>>>) = session_channel();
let c = send(42, c);          // c: Chan<Client, Recv<Bool, Eps>>
let (ok, _): (bool, _) = recv(c);   // protocol complete; types enforce order
```

**Haskell.** Indexed monads (`IxMonad`) thread the pre/post session type through the type index: `send :: a -> Session (!a :>: s) s ()`. Libraries in the style of Pucella–Tov, and Gay's binary session encoding, make protocol violations *type errors*; duality appears as a type-class constraint pairing endpoints. Imai–Yuen–Agusa (PLACES 2010) even studied *inference* of session types for Haskell [3].

```haskell
-- Indexed session monad: the type index is the remaining protocol
client :: Session (Send Int (Recv Bool End)) End ()
client = do send (42 :: Int)
            ok <- recv
            liftIO (print ok)
```

**Idris / dependent types.** Dependent session types index protocols by *values* (`! (n : Nat). ? (Vec n Int). end`), letting the type of later messages depend on earlier payloads — the dependent-types answer to Scribble's payload refinements.

**F* / Session★.** The Session★ toolchain [8] extends Scribble with *refinement types* on payloads and generates F* APIs; the F* compiler then verifies both protocol conformance *and* data-dependent invariants (e.g., "the quoted price is positive and the accepted quote matches the offer"). This is the current frontier of *verified* multiparty programming: behavioural types plus logical refinements, discharged by SMT.

### 4.5 Actors, Microservices, and Choreographic Programming

Two applied domains absorb MPST directly:

- **Actor systems.** An actor's mailbox protocol *is* a session type; behavioural typing of actors (à la the MVL/Actris line) uses duality-like constraints to enforce substitutability — a replacement actor must accept every message sequence the original did. MPST generalises this from one mailbox to a *choreography* of mailboxes.
- **Microservice choreography.** Where orchestration centralises control, choreography distributes it — and distributed control is exactly what global types specify. A Scribble protocol *is* a choreography: project it, generate per-service stubs, and the composition is deadlock-free by the theorems of §4.2. Recent work adapts MPST to agent frameworks ("global type as `Protocol`, endpoint projection as per-role endpoints") precisely for coordinating fleets of LLM agents [2-derived].

The unifying slogan: **the global type is the specification, projection is the compiler, and duality is the linker.**

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

We now state the core theorems with proof sketches. (Full mechanised proofs exist for the binary case in several proof assistants and for MPST subject reduction in the ECOOP 2025 development [7]; we follow the paper proofs of [1,2,3].)

> **Theorem 1 (Subject reduction, binary [1]).** If `Γ ⊢ P ▷ Δ` and `P → P′`, then `Γ ⊢ P′ ▷ Δ′` for some `Δ′` with `Δ ⇒ Δ′` (session environments evolve consistently). *Sketch.* By induction on the reduction derivation. The communication case uses duality: redex `(νk)(k!⟨v⟩.P | k̄?(x).Q)` types with `k : !A.S`, `k̄ : ?A.S̄`; after substitution the continuations hold `S`, `S̄`.

> **Theorem 2 (Communication safety [1,2]).** A well-typed process never reduces to a state containing a communication mismatch (arity/type disagreement on a label or payload). *Sketch.* Linearity guarantees at most one sender and one receiver per channel occurrence; duality guarantees their types agree; subtyping preserves agreement.

> **Theorem 3 (Session fidelity, multiparty [2,3]).** The observable traces of a well-typed multiparty session are exactly the traces admitted by its global type. *Sketch.* Projection is shown *complete*: every global reduction step is matched by local steps of the involved roles, and *sound*: local steps correspond to a global step or to queue-internal reordering covered by asynchronous subtyping.

> **Theorem 4 (Progress / deadlock freedom [2,3]).** A well-typed multiparty session never reaches a stuck non-terminal state. *Sketch.* Global types are *well-formed* (guarded recursion, linearity); a minimal counterexample argument shows that if every role is ready per its projection, some global interaction is enabled — the merge condition is precisely what excludes the "uninformed participant waits forever" deadlock.

> **Theorem 5 (Propositions-as-sessions [4]).** CP typing is sound and complete w.r.t. classical linear logic; reduction is cut elimination. *Corollary.* Binary deadlock freedom is a corollary of logical consistency — a closed well-typed process reduces to `0`.

*Empirical note.* The theorems are not merely paper results: the Scribble toolchain's generated APIs have been applied to real protocol stacks (HTTP-style exchanges, trading protocols), and MPI session-type verification (Honda et al., EuroMPI 2012, cited in [3]) found protocol violations in production parallel code. The ECOOP 2025 mechanisation [7] additionally exposed that the original MPST formulation's *implicit* channels hide race conditions that explicit-channel linearity must rule out — a genuine empirical finding about the theory itself.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloguing what session types do *not* deliver:

1. **Partial projection.** The merge condition rejects projectable-in-spirit protocols; non-projectable global types exist, and there is no canonical repair. Extensions (explicit connection actions for dynamic join/leave [6-ext]) complicate the metatheory.
2. **Fixed participant sets.** Classical MPST assumes all roles are known at session initiation. Dynamic participation, failure, and session *delegation across administrative domains* remain partially solved; failure-aware extensions exist but weaken the guarantees.
3. **Asynchrony vs. expressiveness.** Asynchronous subtyping is undecidable in its most general form; practical systems use decidable fragments, silently restricting reorderings.
4. **Embedding gaps.** Rust is affine, not linear (dropping is allowed); Haskell encodings lean on advanced type-system features with poor error messages; full dependent session types lack mature implementations.
5. **No liveness beyond progress.** The theorems guarantee *safety* and *deadlock freedom*, not that the protocol achieves its business goal — a session can faithfully exchange messages that are semantically nonsense. Refinement types [8] address this but move verification burden to SMT solvers.
6. **Choreography–reality mismatch.** Microservice deployments add timeouts, retries and partial failure — phenomena outside the failure-free calculus. Session types prove the *happy-path protocol* correct; the failure path needs separate machinery.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Session types realise an old dream with unusual completeness: *the communication protocol as a statically checked artefact*. From Honda–Vasconcelos–Kubo's binary discipline [1], where compatibility is duality; through Honda–Yoshida–Carbone's multiparty generalisation [2,3], where a global choreography projects to deadlock-free endpoints; to Wadler's logical revelation [4], where sessions *are* linear-logic propositions and communication *is* cut elimination — the theory forms a coherent whole of rare elegance. Its engineering incarnation in Scribble [6] and its embeddings in Rust, Haskell, Idris and F* [8] demonstrate that the ideas survive contact with real compilers and real protocols.

The open frontier is clear. **Dependent and refinement session types** will let protocols constrain *values*, not just shapes. **Failure-aware MPST** will extend the guarantees past the happy path. **Choreographic programming** of agent and microservice fleets will test whether global types scale to systems whose participants are themselves AI agents. If the last three decades are any guide — from CONCUR 1993 to a Most Influential Paper award in 2018 [5] to mechanised metatheory in 2025 [7] — the programme of *correct communication by construction* will continue to deepen, one duality at a time.
