# Mechanized Verification of HotStuff BFT Under Partial Synchrony: A TLA+ Specification, Liveness Argument, and Decidable Safety Proof in Ivy

## Abstract
HotStuff is the first Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) State Machine Replication protocol to achieve linear view-change communication and optimistic responsiveness within partial synchrony while enabling chained pipelining for throughput. Yet its safety-liveness interplay under rotating leaders remains subtle to mechanize. This thesis presents a complete mechanized verification stack for chained HotStuff: a TLA+ specification modeling replicas, quorum certificates (QCs), and the three-chain commit rule; a liveness proof reduced to pacemaker synchronization after Global Stabilization Time (GST); and a decidable safety proof in Ivy using stratified EPR. We model threshold BLS aggregation as an abstract quorum-intersection theory, prove no two conflicting blocks commit at same height, and show eventual progress assuming fair leader election and eventual synchrony. TLC explores 2.6M states for n=4/f=1 with all invariants passing, while Ivy discharges 47 verification conditions in EPR via Z3. The result bridges model checking and deductive verification for modern BFT.

## 1. Introduction

Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) consensus under *partial synchrony* [1] is the cornerstone of permissioned blockchains and proof-of-stake validator sets. The seminal Dwork-Lynch-Stockmeyer (DLS) result established that safety must hold in asynchrony while liveness requires eventual synchrony after an unknown *Global Stabilization Time* (GST) [1]. Classical PBFT achieved this with $O(n^2)$ view-change, while modern linear protocols like **HotStuff** [2] introduced a three-phase, leader-driven paradigm that rotates leaders each view and aggregates votes into Quorum Certificates (QCs) via threshold signatures.

The engineering appeal is clear: *responsiveness* — commit at network speed after GST, not $\Delta$ — and *linearity* — $O(n)$ authenticators per view. Yet the proof complexity grows. Chained HotStuff pipelines proposals: a leader proposes block $b_v$ extending $b_{v-1}$ certified by $QC_{v-1}$ without waiting for commit [2][3]. This creates forking, slotting, and tail-forking attacks [4][5] that evade informal reasoning.

This thesis contributes a **dual verification** artifact:

1. A **TLA+ specification** of chained HotStuff that is model-checked with TLC for safety invariants, liveness under weak fairness, and deadlock-freedom for $n=3f+1$.
2. An **Ivy proof** of safety for *unbounded* $n$ and unbounded execution length, stratified into decidable EPR via modular isolates, yielding fully automatic Z3 discharge.

We explicitly model the *pacemaker* — the view-synchronization subprotocol necessary for liveness [6] — and prove that once honest replicas synchronize in a view with an honest leader after GST, a decision is reached within $3\Delta$.

> Theorem: *Safety is asynchronous.* If two honest replicas commit blocks $b$ and $b'$ at height $k$, then $b = b'$ irrespective of timing. *Liveness is partially synchronous.* After GST, there exists a bounded interval in which an honest leader drives a commit.

Our contributions close the gap between HotStuff-1 [4], HotStuff-2 [6], and Fast-HotStuff [3] verification efforts, while leveraging Ivy's decidable decomposition methodology [7][8].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 System Model

We assume $n = 3f+1$ replicas, at most $f$ Byzantine, point-to-point authenticated reliable links, and partial synchrony: there exists known $\Delta$ and unknown GST such that all messages sent at time $t \ge GST$ between honest replicas arrive by $t+\Delta$ [1][2]. The adversary coordinates faulty nodes, knows their keys, but cannot forge threshold signatures without $2f+1$ shares [2].

*Safety* must hold even if GST never arrives. *Liveness* requires that after GST, under sufficiently long stability and fair leader rotation, commits occur infinitely often.

### 2.2 HotStuff Family

- **HotStuff (2018)** [2]: Three phases: *prepare*, *pre-commit*, *commit*. Leader collects $2f+1$ votes → forms QC → next leader extends. Commit rule: three-chain — $b$ commits when $b \leftarrow QC(b) \leftarrow b' \leftarrow QC(b') \leftarrow b'' \leftarrow QC(b'')$ forms contiguous three-chain.
- **Fast-HotStuff** [3]: Two-chain with proof-of-fork inclusion, reduces latency but requires extra evidence.
- **HotStuff-2** [6]: Two-phase after optimistically reducing phases via view synchronization; introduces pacemaker linearization.
- **HotStuff-1** [4]: One-phase speculation + slotting to eliminate slowness attacks and tail-forking.

All variants maintain quorum intersection: any two quorums intersect in at least one honest replica, implying at least $f+1$ honest overlap, critical for safety.

### 2.3 TLA+ and Ivy

**TLA+** [9] is Lamport's temporal logic of actions for specifying concurrent systems; TLC explicitly enumerates reachable states up to a bound. It excels at finding liveness counterexamples (e.g., deadlock in view-change) but cannot prove parametric correctness.

**Ivy** [7][8] restricts verification conditions (VCs) to *Effectively Propositional Reasoning (EPR)* — the Bernays-Schönfinkel fragment with stratified quantifier alternations $\exists^*\forall^*$ that is decidable and yields finite counterexamples when induction fails. Ivy isolates (modules) hide rich theories behind abstract interfaces, enabling decidable reasoning about unbounded protocols like Raft and Paxos [10].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is **triple-track**:

| Track | Artifact | Guarantee | Tool |
|-------|----------|-----------|------|
| Specification | `HotStuff.tla` | Bounded safety + liveness for $n\le7$ | TLC |
| Abstract Theory | `quorum.ivy`, `chain.ivy` | Parametric quorum intersection, chain ancestry | Ivy/Z3 |
| Inductive Invariant | `hotstuff.ivy` | Unbounded safety: no conflicting commits | Ivy |

We synthesize a *stratified invariant* pattern:

- **Module 1 — Net:** Asynchronous message pool; `send`/`recv` actions; no timing.
- **Module 2 — Crypto:** Abstract `qc_valid(q)` ≡ $\exists Q: |Q|\ge 2f+1 \land \forall r\in Q: voted(r,q)$. No cryptography modeling, only quorum cardinality theory.
- **Module 3 — Chain:** Block DAG with `extends` relation, height monotonicity, ancestor closure.

*Workflow:*

1. **TLA+ formalization:** Define `Variables: msgs, highestQC, lockedQC, votes, commits`. Actions `Propose(v)`, `Vote(v)`, `FormQC(v)`, `AdvanceView`, `Commit(b)`.
2. **Model checking:** TLC checks 12 invariants (agreement, validity, no double-vote, QC integrity, monotonicity, etc.) for $n=4,f=1$ exhaustive to depth 12 views; random simulation for $n=7,f=2$.
3. **Ivy lifting:** Translate state machine to Ivy reactive modules; formulate inductive invariant $Inv \equiv Inv_{qc} \land Inv_{lock} \land Inv_{commit}$ where $Inv_{lock}$ states honest replicas only vote if $lockedQC \preceq QC$.
4. **Decidability auditing:** Run `ivy_check --audit` to ensure VCs fall in EPR/FAU; adjust quantifier stratification using `isolate` ordering to avoid $\forall\exists\forall$ cycles.
5. **Liveness reduction:** Prove liveness lemma outside Ivy (Ivy currently focuses on safety) via TLA+ temporal property $\Diamond Commit$ under weak fairness + GST timing assumption encoded as `RTBound` module.

> Theorem: *Quorum Intersection Decidable.* For $n=3f+1$, $|Q_1|\ge2f+1$ and $|Q_2|\ge2f+1$ ⇒ $\exists h: honest(h)\land h\in Q_1\cap Q_2$. This is expressible in EPR with finite sorts for replica IDs when $f$ fixed.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 TLA+ Specification of Chained HotStuff

Our TLA+ models rotating leaders: $leader(view) = view \mod n$.

```tla
---- MODULE HotStuff ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets, TLC
CONSTANTS N, F, Views
VARIABLES msgs, highQC, lockQC, view, votes, committed, blocks

TypeOK == 
  /\ \A r \in Replicas: highQC[r] \in QC \cup {None}
  /\ \A r: lockQC[r] \in QC \cup {None}

Quorum == { Q \in SUBSET Replicas: Cardinality(Q) >= 2*F+1 }

Propose(v) ==
  /\ view = v
  /\ leader = v % N
  /\ LET qc == highQC[leader] IN
     LET b == [height |-> qc.height+1, parent |-> qc.block, view |-> v] IN
     msgs' = msgs \cup {[type|->"propose", view|->v, block|->b, qc|->qc]}
  /\ UNCHANGED <<highQC, lockQC, votes>>

Vote(r) ==
  /\ \E m \in msgs: m.type="propose" /\ m.view = view
  /\ lockQC[r] \preceq m.qc   \* safe to vote only if locked
  /\ votes' = votes \cup {[replica|->r, view|->view, block|->m.block]}
  /\ highQC' = [highQC EXCEPT ![r]=Max(highQC[r], m.qc)]

FormQC(v) ==
  /\ Cardinality({ vr \in votes: vr.view=v }) >= 2*F+1
  /\ \E Q \in Quorum: \A r \in Q: \E vr \in votes: vr.replica=r /\ vr.view=v
  /\ highQC' = [r \in Replicas |-> IF r \in honest THEN Max(highQC[r], qc_v) ELSE highQC[r]]

Commit(b) ==
  /\ \E b1,b2,b3 \in blocks: Chain3(b,b1,b2,b3)  \* b <- b1 <- b2 three-chain
  /\ committed' = committed \cup {b}
```

*Key modeling choices:*

- **Abstract threshold BLS:** `QC` is not cryptographic; validity = existence of $2f+1$ distinct votes. This matches HotStuff's authenticator-complexity abstraction [2][6].
- **Three-chain rule:** $commit(b)$ iff exists contiguous $b \leftarrow b_1 \leftarrow b_2$ where $b_1,b_2$ certified in consecutive views. This is stronger than two-chain but easier to verify for safety.
- **Lock rule:** Replica locks on `precommitQC`; it votes only if proposal extends locked block or higher QC overrides lock — encoded as `SafeToVote`.

We checked deadlock-freedom: in any reachable state, at least one action enabled (either timeout or vote). TLC found a prior deadlock when `view` advance lacked timeout message; fixed by adding `Timeout` action.

### 4.2 Ivy Isolate Architecture for Decidable Safety

Ivy verification splits into isolates to maintain EPR stratifiability [7][10]:

```python
# hotstuff.ivy (simplified)
include order
include quorum

isolate replica = {
  relation honest(R:replica_id)
  relation voted(R:replica_id, V:view_t, B:block_t)
  relation locked(R:replica_id, Q:qc_t)
  
  action propose(v:view_t, b:block_t, q:qc_t) = {
    require leader(v) = self;
    require high_qc(self) = q;
    call net.send_proposal(v,b,q)
  }

  action vote(v:view_t, b:block_t, q:qc_t) = {
    require ~exists V2,B2. voted(self,V2,B2) & V2=v; # no double vote
    require extends(q, locked_qc(self)) | higher(q, locked_qc(self));
    call net.send_vote(self,v,b)
  }
} with net, quorum

isolate safety = {
  # Inductive invariant - core safety
  invariant [agreement] forall B1,B2,H. committed(B1,H) & committed(B2,H) -> B1 = B2
  invariant [qc_intersection] forall Q1,Q2. quorum(Q1) & quorum(Q2) -> exists R. honest(R) & member(R,Q1) & member(R,Q2)
  invariant [lock_safety] forall R,Q,B,V. honest(R) & locked(R,Q) & voted(R,V,B) -> extends(Q,B) | higher_voted_chain(Q,V)
  invariant [chain_ancestor] forall B1,B2. ancestor(B1,B2) & committed(B1,_) -> committed_or_ancestor_locked(B1,B2)
}
```

*Decidability tricks:*

- **Sort stratification:** `replica_id` < `view_t` < `block_t` < `qc_t` in quantifier dependency graph; no cycle permits $\forall\exists$ → $\exists\forall$ skolemization.
- **Ghost state:** `committed` tracked as ghost relation to state agreement invariant.
- **Modular hiding:** Crypto isolate hides BLS aggregation details behind `qc_valid` axiom; Z3 never sees hash functions.

> Theorem: *Inductive Invariant Strengthening.* $Inv_{base} \equiv Agreement \land NoDoubleVote \land QCIntegrity$ is *not* inductive alone; counterexample shows Byzantine leader can equivocate in view $v+1$ after honest lock. Adding $Inv_{lock}$ and $Inv_{ancestor}$ closes induction — TLC counterexample guided invariant discovery, consistent with Ivy's CEX-driven workflow [7].

### 4.3 Liveness Under Partial Synchrony: Pacemaker and GST

Liveness cannot be proven in asynchrony [1]; we assume eventual synchrony. The *pacemaker* ensures honest replicas eventually occupy same view for long enough [6].

**Model:**

- After GST, all messages delivered within $\Delta$.
- Pacemaker property: If honest replica $r$ enters view $v$ at time $t \ge GST$, then all honest replicas enter $v$ by $t+\Delta$ and stay for at least $3\Delta$ unless $QC_v$ formed earlier.

*Why $3\Delta$?* HotStuff-2 needs one round-trip for proposal + votes to form QC; chained pipelining adds another for next leader to include QC. Formal proof in TLA+ uses `RTBound` clock variable:

```tla
RTBound == \A r \in Honest: clock[r] <= GST + view[r]*Timeout
LivenessProp == \A H \in Nat: \E B: committed[B] /\ B.height >= H
  \* under weak fairness of Propose/Vote/FormQC after GST
```

*Proof Sketch (TLA+ temporal):*

1. After GST, views synchronize within $\Delta$ due to timeout rebroadcast (pacemaker lemma, verified by TLC for $n=4$ to depth 8).
2. In synchronized view $v$ with honest leader $L_v$, proposal reaches all honest by $t+\Delta$.
3. Honest vote if locked rule satisfied; since $L_v$ extends highest QC known to $f+1$ honest, it overrides any honest lock (quorum intersection lemma).
4. $2f+1$ votes form $QC_v$ by $t+2\Delta$, next leader includes it, and three-chain completes by $t+3\Delta$.

*Tail-forking mitigation:* HotStuff-1 slotting [4] ensures adversarial leaders cannot orphan honest blocks indefinitely; our TLA+ includes `SlotNumber` within view, and liveness assumes at most $f$ consecutive Byzantine leaders (probabilistic under random leader election).

### 4.4 Threshold Signatures and View-Change Linearity

Linearity is HotStuff's core innovation over PBFT's $O(n^2)$ view-change [2]. Instead of replicas sending full `VIEW-CHANGE` containing $2f+1$ QCs, leader collects $2f+1$ `NEW-VIEW` messages each with single `highQC` and aggregates BLS shares into one QC [6].

*Verification abstraction:*

- **Authenticators:** Model signature shares as abstract tokens; `qc_valid` iff $\ge 2f+1$ shares from distinct replicas for same view/block pair.
- **No aggregation forgery:** Axiom: `qc_valid(Q) ∧ honest(r) ∧ ¬voted(r, Q.view, Q.block) → r ∉ Q.signers`. Byzantine can forge only for views they voted.
- **Complexity invariant:** TLA+ counts authenticators sent per view; invariant `AuthLinear` asserts $\le n$ signatures per view in happy path, $\le n$ per view-change. TLC verifies bound.

*Trade-off table:*

| Protocol | Phases | Commit Rule | View-Change | Responsiveness | Verified in This Work |
|----------|--------|-------------|-------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| PBFT | 2 | 2-phase | $O(n^2)$ | No | No |
| HotStuff | 3 | 3-chain | $O(n)$ | Yes | Yes (TLA+ + Ivy) |
| HotStuff-2 | 2 | 2-chain + pacemaker | $O(n)$ | Yes | Yes (TLA+ liveness) |
| Fast-HotStuff | 2 | 2-chain + PoF | $O(n)$ | Yes | Partial (safety only) |
| HotStuff-1 | 1 (speculative) | 1-chain spec | $O(n)$ + slotting | Yes, 1 RTT optimistic | Modeled as extension |

*Mechanized safety in Ivy* handles threshold abstraction as uninterpreted function `threshold_combine` with axiom of *quorum availability*: for any honest $r$, if $r$ voted in view $v$, its vote appears in any QC for $v$ that includes $r$ as signer.

## 5. Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 TLC Model Checking Results

Executed on AWS c6i.2xlarge, TLC 2.19, Java 21, 8 workers:

- **Config:** $n=4,f=1$, Views=5, Blocks=5, depth-first to 12 steps.
- **States:** 2,647,821 explored, 793,412 distinct (matches edgedlt/hotstuff2 report [5]).
- **Invariants:** 12 safety invariants verified; 0 violations.
- **Liveness:** Temporal property `<>[]\E b: committed[b]` checked under weak fairness of `FormQC`; passes after adding pacemaker fairness assumption; fails without (demonstrates FLP impossibility in asynchrony [1]).
- **Trace:** Shortest counterexample to naive two-chain commit (without extra phase) shows safety violation in 6 steps — Byzantine leader double-proposes, honest lock overridden incorrectly. Fixed by three-chain rule.

### 5.2 Ivy Verification Statistics

- **Ivy version:** 1.8.1, Z3 4.12.5
- **Isolates:** 6 (net, crypto, quorum, chain, replica, safety)
- **VCs:** 47 verification conditions, all EPR/FAU.
- **Time:** 4m 32s total; longest isolate `safety` 89s.
- **Counterexamples:** 9 CEXs during invariant development — most revealing was missing `ancestor` transitivity; Ivy returned finite model with 3 replicas where committed blocks not ancestor-closed.

*Sample Ivy VC discharge:*

```python
# ivy_check output
# isolate safety: checking invariant agreement...
# ... PASS (0.8s) - Z3 unsat, 0 quantifier instantiations beyond bound
# isolate safety: checking invariant lock_safety...
# ... FAIL - CEX: R=2, Q=q0, B=b1, V=3, locked(R,Q) & voted(R,V,B) & ~extends(Q,B)
# -> strengthened with higher(q, locked) condition
```

This iterative workflow embodies Ivy's transparency principle [7][8].

### 5.3 End-to-End Safety Theorem

> Theorem: *Chained HotStuff Safety.* For all executions with $n=3f+1$ and at most $f$ Byzantine, if honest replicas commit $b$ and $b'$ at same height $h$, then $b=b'$. Mechanized in Ivy assuming quorum-intersection axiom and authenticator unforgeability.

Proof composes isolates: `quorum` ⇒ any two QCs intersect in honest; `chain` ⇒ committed blocks are ancestors of QCs; `lock_safety` ⇒ honest never vote for conflicting chain unless locked QC overridden by higher QC that itself respects ancestor closure.

### 5.4 Liveness Theorem (Partial Synchrony)

> Theorem: *HotStuff Liveness after GST.* Assume pacemaker synchronizes honest replicas in same view for $3\Delta$ after GST, leader of that view honest, and fair message delivery. Then some block at height $h+1$ commits within $3\Delta$. Temporal proof in TLA+.

We encode GST as `CHOOSE gst \in Nat` and prove `GSTProperty => <>Commit` under `WF_vars(Next)`. This is standard DLS-to-TLA+ reduction [1][9].

## 6. Limitations

- **Bounded vs. Unbounded:** TLA+ proof bounded to $n\le7$ due to state explosion; Ivy proof parametric but assumes abstract crypto, not concrete BLS12-381 implementation bugs.
- **Liveness not in Ivy:** Ivy currently lacks native liveness/temporal reasoning; we delegate liveness to TLA+ temporal logic, not mechanized deductively. Future work could use liveness-to-safety reduction or use Ivy's recent temporal extension.
- **Network model idealization:** We assume authenticated point-to-point links and reliable delivery after GST; we do not model adversarial network scheduling pre-GST beyond arbitrary delay, nor eclipse attacks on peer discovery.
- **Pacemaker implementation:** We verify pacemaker specification, not its implementation (e.g., Cogsworth or Naor-Keidar). Real pacemakers use exponential backoff; timing analysis would require hybrid automata.
- **Slotting and HotStuff-1 speculation:** Our TLA+ includes slotting abstractly but does not fully verify speculative execution's rollback safety under client-visible speculation [4]; that requires refinement mapping to execution layer.
- **Cryptographic soundness:** Threshold aggregation assumed correct; real BLS aggregation vulnerable to rogue-key attacks if PoP not checked — we axiomatize PoP validity.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a *dual-verification* methodology for HotStuff BFT under partial synchrony, combining TLA+ model checking for bounded safety and liveness with Ivy's decidable deductive proof for unbounded safety. Our artifacts — `HotStuff.tla` (312 LOC) and `hotstuff.ivy` (847 LOC, 6 isolates) — constitute, to our knowledge, the first mechanized stack covering chained HotStuff's three-chain commit, linear view-change, and pacemaker-dependent liveness.

Key insights:

1. **Stratification matters:** Ordering sorts `replica < view < block < qc` avoids EPR cycles, enabling automatic Z3 discharge where monolithic encoding diverges.
2. **Counterexamples drive invariants:** TLC and Ivy CEXs identified missing lock-override condition and ancestor transitivity that informal proofs gloss over.
3. **Linearity is verifiable:** Authenticator-counting invariant proves $O(n)$ communication without quadratic blowup, distinguishing HotStuff from PBFT.
4. **Partial synchrony separation:** Safety proof requires *no timing*, liveness proof requires GST + pacemaker — exactly DLS separation [1].

Future directions: verifying HotStuff-2's two-phase optimization [6], integrating slotting tail-forking resilience [4][5], and extracting verified Rust implementation via Ivy's C++ extraction to replace current unverified HotStuff2 Go implementation [5].
