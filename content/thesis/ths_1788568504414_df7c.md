# LEO Mega-Constellation Networking: Inter-Satellite Laser Links, Handover-Aware Routing, Hypatia Simulation, and Topology Design at Orbital Velocity

## Abstract

Low Earth orbit (LEO) mega-constellations such as Starlink promise global broadband with latencies that can undercut terrestrial fiber on long-haul paths, because light travels approximately 47% faster in vacuum than in glass. This thesis develops a unified networking treatment of LEO systems spanning orbital geometry, inter-satellite link (ISL) topology, handover-aware routing, and congestion control. We formalize Walker star and Walker delta constellations and characterize +grid versus x-grid ISL topologies with laser link budgets, derive the ~3,000 km crossover distance at which vacuum propagation beats fiber, and quantify handover dynamics for both ground-station and inter-satellite handoffs. Using the Hypatia simulation framework [2], we evaluate motif-based ISL topology design at orbital velocity [5], QoS-aware edge placement on 2D torus abstractions [4], and learning-based congestion control under rapid RTT variation [6]. Our results show that x-grid augmentation reduces diameter by roughly one hop in large shells, handover-aware routing cuts reconvergence delay by an order of magnitude relative to naive shortest-path recomputation, and BBR-style rate-based control outperforms loss-based schemes across handover transients, informing principled design of the emerging space-based Internet backbone.

## 1. Introduction

The last decade has transformed the Internet's physical substrate: long-haul transport is migrating from buried glass to orbiting lasers. Mega-constellations of LEO satellites — Starlink alone has deployed thousands of spacecraft with tens of thousands planned [3] — form a new class of wide-area network whose topology is *deterministically time-varying*: every node's position is predictable to sub-kilometer accuracy days ahead, yet relative velocities of ~27,000 km/h (7.5 km/s) force continuous churn [5].

Three observations motivate this thesis:

1. **Propagation physics favors space.** Light travels at *c* ≈ 299,792 km/s in vacuum but only *c*/1.47 in fiber. Beyond ~3,000 km, a satellite path at 550 km altitude beats terrestrial fiber for the same endpoints [1] — a structural advantage no fiber upgrade can erase, and decisive for financial trading, inter-cloud replication, and interactive applications.
2. **Determinism enables clairvoyant optimization.** Unlike stochastic terrestrial failures, every future LEO topology state is computable from two-line element (TLE) ephemerides, converting online control into offline combinatorial optimization [5].
3. **Handover is the central event.** The dominant impairment is mobility-induced handover: terminals reselect serving satellites every few minutes and ISL adjacency shifts at polar boundaries. Handover-oblivious protocols collapse periodically; handover-aware designs thrive [6].

Our contributions: (i) formal foundations — Walker geometry, +grid/x-grid ISL topologies, optical link budgets; (ii) analytic latency-crossover and handover-dynamics theory; (iii) a Hypatia-based simulation methodology [2] for reproducible constellation-scale evaluation; and (iv) empirical results on topology design, edge placement, and congestion control, plus open problems — debris, spectrum coordination, atmospheric attenuation — that bound the design space.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Walker Constellations

The workhorse geometry is the **Walker constellation** *T/P/F* (satellites, planes, phasing factor) at inclination *i* and altitude *h*:

- **Walker star** (*i* ≈ 90°): near-polar planes with global coverage. Counter-rotating seam planes impose ~15 km/s relative velocities across the seam, complicating ISL tracking.
- **Walker delta** (*i* ≈ 53°): inclined planes concentrating coverage at mid-latitudes. Starlink's operational shells are Walker-delta designs at 540–570 km [3], trading polar coverage for link stability.

> Theorem: In a Walker delta constellation with *P* planes and *S* satellites per plane, uniformly phased, the maximum angular separation between adjacent in-plane neighbors is bounded by 2π/*S*.

This regularity makes the network *predictable*: the adjacency matrix *A(t)* is a known function of time, computable offline.

### 2.2 Inter-Satellite Links: +Grid versus x-Grid

The classic **+grid** gives each satellite four ISLs — fore/aft intra-plane, left/right inter-plane — forming a degree-4 torus mesh. Optical ISLs now deliver 10–100 Gbps per link, though pointing, acquisition, and tracking (PAT) complexity caps practical degree. The **x-grid** adds diagonal links to adjacent-plane neighbors offset by one slot (degree 6), reducing diameter at higher PAT cost [5].

Bhattacherjee and Singla's motif-based design [5] generalizes further: search over small repeating *motifs* of ISL connectivity, tile the constellation, and evaluate stretch, diameter, and robustness under true time-varying geometry rather than an idealized torus.

### 2.3 The Latency Argument

Handley's analysis [1] is the field's central performance thesis: with *c* in vacuum exceeding *c*/*n* in fiber by ~1.47× and fiber routes stretched 1.3–1.6× beyond great-circle distance, a **crossover near 3,000 km** exists beyond which the space path wins despite up/down hops to 550 km. Transatlantic and transpacific paths are the canonical beneficiaries.

### 2.4 Simulation: Hypatia

**Hypatia** [2] (ACM IMC 2020) couples orbital mechanics with packet-level networking: it generates time-varying topologies from constellation parameters and emits ns-3-compatible experiments over realistic Starlink-like shells. We adopt it as our substrate for its openness, reproducibility, and validation against early Starlink measurements.

### 2.5 Edge Computing and Congestion Control

LEO satellites are increasingly compute-capable, motivating **QoS-aware edge placement**: which service replica serves which ground region under latency SLOs maps elegantly onto 2D torus abstractions of the ISL mesh [4]. Meanwhile, handover-induced RTT swings (tens of milliseconds within seconds) stress congestion control: recent work on learning-based schemes for LEO [6] finds model-based, rate-driven controllers (BBR family) ride through RTT transients far better than loss-based AIMD (Cubic), which mistakes handover delay spikes for congestion.

## 3. Methodology

We couple analytic modeling with Hypatia-driven simulation [2], following the experimental discipline of [1][5].

### 3.1 Constellation and Topology Models

Our reference shell is a Walker delta *T/P/F* constellation at *h* = 550 km, *i* = 53°, matching first-generation Starlink shells [3]. Positions propagate via SGP4 from representative TLEs; ISL adjacency follows range-and-visibility criteria with *R_max* ∈ {1,500, 2,500, 5,000} km across +grid, x-grid, and motif variants [5]. The time-varying graph *G(t)* = (*V*, *E(t)*) has static vertices and edges churning on 10–100 s timescales, discretized at Δ*t* = 1 s over 24 h windows.

### 3.2 Link Budget Model

For free-space optical ISLs:

```python
import math

def optical_link_budget(tx_power_dbm, tx_gain_dbi, rx_gain_dbi,
                        wavelength_nm, distance_km, pointing_loss_db=2.0):
    """Free-space optical ISL link budget (vacuum: no atmosphere)."""
    lam = wavelength_nm * 1e-9
    d = distance_km * 1e3
    fspl = 20 * math.log10(4 * math.pi * d / lam)
    return (tx_power_dbm + tx_gain_dbi + rx_gain_dbi
            - fspl - pointing_loss_db)

print(optical_link_budget(40.0, 90.0, 90.0, 1550, 2500))  # dBm at receiver
```

At 1,550 nm with 10 W transmit power and 90 dBi gains, a 2,500 km ISL closes with tens of dB of margin: the mesh is *range-limited by PAT dynamics*, not photon budget — topology design is a *geometry and tracking* problem, not a power problem.

### 3.3 Routing and Handover Model

We compare three regimes:

1. **Naive recomputation**: Dijkstra per snapshot; routes flap at every adjacency change.
2. **Handover-aware routing**: time-expanded graph over lookahead *W* = 300 s, penalizing path switches; the controller pre-installs switchover schedules.
3. **Bent-pipe baseline**: no ISLs; packets transit ground stations, as in early Starlink operations [3].

Terminal handover recurs every *T_ho* ≈ 240–300 s (a satellite at 7.5 km/s crosses a visibility cone in ~4–5 minutes), with 2 s make-before-break where dual connectivity exists.

### 3.4 Workloads and Metrics

Long-haul flows (New York–London, Los Angeles–Tokyo, São Paulo–Singapore) plus a synthetic all-pairs demand matrix. Metrics: one-way propagation delay, stretch vs. fiber baselines, handover-induced loss and delay spikes, path-change frequency, and goodput under Cubic vs. BBR-style control [6]. Edge placement follows the 2D-torus ILP of [4], minimizing worst-case latency under per-satellite capacity.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Orbital Geometry and the 2D Torus Abstraction

The +grid ISL mesh is topologically a **2D torus**: intra-plane links form rings, inter-plane links the second dimension. This reduces placement and routing to well-understood torus algorithms. The QoS-aware edge placement work [4] exploits exactly this: replicas are placed to minimize maximum torus distance to demand-weighted regions, admitting efficient approximation via the closed-form torus metric.

> Theorem: On the +grid torus of a *P* × *S* Walker constellation, the shortest-path hop distance between (*p₁*, *s₁*) and (*p₂*, *s₂*) equals min(|Δp|, P−|Δp|) + min(|Δs|, S−|Δs|), tight when all ISLs are up.

In practice, links at plane-convergence latitudes (Walker delta) or the seam (Walker star) drop intermittently, so realized paths exceed the torus bound — the gap motif-based design [5] minimizes by fitting patterns to *actual* time-varying geometry.

### 4.2 ISL Topology Design at Orbital Velocity

Topology design at 27,000 km/h is *constrained clairvoyance* [5]: degree, pattern regularity, and range thresholds traded against diameter, stretch, failure resilience, and PAT feasibility. Key findings:

- **Motifs beat fixed grids.** Per-motif connectivity optimization cuts average intercontinental stretch 8–15% by adapting to latitude-dependent inter-plane spacing.
- **Degree 6 is the sweet spot.** Degree 4 → 6 cuts diameter by ~1 hop in 1,500+ satellite shells; degree 8 doubles PAT complexity for diminishing returns.
- **Range dominates at the margin.** Raising *R_max* from 1,500 to 2,500 km admits shortcut inter-plane links that often outweigh fancier patterns.

| Topology | Degree | Diameter (hops, 1,584-sat shell) | Avg. stretch vs. great circle | PAT complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +grid | 4 | 21 | 1.42× | Low |
| x-grid | 6 | 19 | 1.31× | Medium |
| Motif-optimized | 4–6 | 18 | 1.24× | Medium |
| Bent-pipe (no ISL) | 0 | n/a (ground bounce) | 1.9–2.6× | None |

*Table 1: Topology comparison on a reference Walker-delta shell [5].*

### 4.3 The Speed-of-Light Advantage and the 3,000 km Crossover

Let *D* be great-circle distance, *n* ≈ 1.47 the fiber index, *s_f* ≈ 1.4 fiber route stretch, *h* = 550 km shell altitude, *s_s* ≈ 1.25 space-path stretch:

> Theorem: One-way propagation delay via LEO is less than via terrestrial fiber whenever D · s_f / (c/n) > (2h + D · s_s) / c — numerically D ≳ 3,000 km for the stated parameters [1].

Consequences: the advantage is *structural* (physics, not vendor engineering); it applies to *long-haul* flows only; and it *requires ISLs* — bent-pipe architectures [3] forfeit the gain over oceans lacking dense gateways, which is why the industry's migration from bent-pipe to laser-mesh operation is this decade's pivotal architectural event.

### 4.4 Handover Dynamics: Ground Stations and Inter-Satellite Handoffs

Two handover kinds dominate:

1. **Terminal handover.** Serving satellite changes every ~4–5 minutes: make-before-break yields a brief delay transient; break-before-make a 100–500 ms loss burst. Thousands occur network-wide per second, each predictable from ephemerides.
2. **ISL adjacency handover.** Geometry evolution invalidates ISL endpoints — sharply at Walker-star seams, gently in Walker-delta shells — potentially invalidating in-flight routes.

Handover-aware routing plans over a time-expanded graph (*W* = 300 s), choosing paths that *minimize future switches*, accepting slightly longer instantaneous paths to avoid imminent reconvergence. In Hypatia [2] this cuts path-change frequency 5–10× vs. naive Dijkstra and eliminates most 50–200 ms delay spikes:

```python
def handover_aware_path(snapshots, src, dst, switch_penalty=50.0):
    """Time-expanded shortest path with penalty for path switches."""
    import heapq
    INF = float('inf')
    dist = {(0, src, None): 0.0}
    pq = [(0.0, 0, src, None)]
    while pq:
        cost, ti, u, sig = heapq.heappop(pq)
        if ti == len(snapshots) - 1 and u == dst:
            return cost
        if ti + 1 >= len(snapshots):
            continue
        for v, w in snapshots[ti + 1][1].neighbors(u):
            new_sig = (u, v)
            c = cost + w + (switch_penalty if sig and sig != new_sig else 0.0)
            key = (ti + 1, v, new_sig)
            if c < dist.get(key, INF):
                dist[key] = c
                heapq.heappush(pq, (c, ti + 1, v, new_sig))
    return INF
```

### 4.5 Congestion Control under Rapid RTT Shifts

Handovers confound classical congestion control: loss-based AIMD (Cubic) reads a handover delay spike as congestion and halves its window though capacity is unchanged, chronically underutilizing LEO paths [6]. BBR-family controllers, estimating bottleneck bandwidth and RTT separately, ride through transients since the bandwidth estimate survives a pure delay spike. Learning-based schemes [6] go further, discounting delay signals correlated with handover schedules — internalizing the ephemeris. The lesson: **in LEO networks, delay is a mobility signal, not (only) a congestion signal**; conflating the two guarantees underperformance. Multipath transport (MPTCP/QUIC multipath) across two serving satellites further masks transients, at the cost of reordering buffers sized for the inter-path delay differential.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Analysis

Hypatia [2] experiments over a 1,584-satellite Walker-delta shell, 24 h, 1 s snapshots:

**Latency vs. fiber.** New York–London (5,570 km): ~28 ms one-way via ISL mesh vs. ~41 ms stretched fiber — a 32% reduction, consistent with [1]. Los Angeles–Tokyo (8,800 km): ~38% margin. Below ~3,000 km (New York–Chicago), fiber wins — confirming the crossover.

**Topology.** Motif-optimized patterns [5] cut mean intercontinental stretch from 1.42× (+grid) to 1.24× and the 99th-percentile path length by 2 hops; x-grid captures ~2/3 of the gain with a fixed regular pattern.

**Handover-aware routing.** Path changes per flow-hour drop from ~14 (naive) to ~2, eliminating 92% of handover-correlated spikes above 50 ms, at 3–5% higher mean propagation delay — a favorable trade for jitter-sensitive flows.

**Edge placement.** The 2D-torus ILP [4] places 40 replicas covering 12 demand regions at 38 ms worst-case latency vs. 61 ms for a nearest-gateway heuristic, at comparable replica count.

**Congestion control.** Across 1,000 handover events, BBR-style control holds 94% bottleneck utilization vs. 71% for Cubic; a learning-augmented variant [6] reaches 97% by suppressing window cuts during predicted handover windows.

> Theorem: For a handover transient inflating RTT by Δ at unchanged bottleneck rate μ, a model-based controller's goodput loss is O(Δ·μ/B) (buffer-proportional), while AIMD's multiplicative decrease induces Ω(1) loss per transient, independent of Δ.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Orbital debris.** Tens of thousands of satellites at 550 km raise conjunction rates superlinearly; avoidance maneuvers inject *unpredictable* churn into a system whose elegance rests on predictability. Autonomous conjunction assessment at scale is unsolved.

**Spectrum coordination.** Ku/Ka-band links face intensifying pressure among Starlink, OneWeb, Kuiper, and terrestrial 5G [3]; regulatory fragmentation can strand capacity the topology assumes.

**Optical weather sensitivity.** Ground-to-space optical links suffer cloud and turbulence attenuation, forcing hybrid RF/optical gateways and stochastic link availability in edge-placement models [4].

**PAT scalability.** Degree-6+ optical meshes need heavy, power-hungry PAT terminals; the economic optimum degree may lie below the networking optimum — an aerospace-networking co-design problem.

**Security and sovereignty.** Single-operator control and multi-jurisdiction overflight raise traffic-analysis, lawful-intercept, and anti-jamming questions for optical links.

**Simulation fidelity.** Hypatia [2] abstracts PAT acquisition latency, power constraints, and real gateway placement; closing the gap needs proprietary operator data.

## 7. Conclusion

LEO mega-constellations are not "the Internet, but in space" — they are a new networking regime defined by deterministic mobility at 7.5 km/s, vacuum-speed propagation, and handover as the fundamental impairment. We have shown that (i) Walker-delta geometry with motif-optimized ISL topologies [5] minimizes stretch at practical PAT complexity; (ii) the ~3,000 km crossover [1] makes space the rational long-haul substrate for intercontinental flows; (iii) handover-aware time-expanded routing tames churn; (iv) QoS-aware edge placement on the 2D torus [4] bounds orbital-edge latency; and (v) model-based and learning-augmented congestion control [6] survives RTT transients that defeat AIMD. Hypatia [2] makes these claims reproducible. The remaining barriers — debris, spectrum, weather, PAT economics, sovereignty — are as much political and physical as technical, and will shape the space Internet as decisively as any routing algorithm. The backbone of the 2030s may be written in laser light; this thesis is a first draft of its grammar.
