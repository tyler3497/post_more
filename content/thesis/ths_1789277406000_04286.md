# Forcing and the Independence of the Continuum Hypothesis: Cohen's Method, Inner Models, and the Multiverse of Set Theory

## Abstract

This thesis provides a rigorous exposition of Paul Cohen's forcing technique and its inaugural triumph: the proof that the Continuum Hypothesis (CH) is independent of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the Axiom of Choice (ZFC). We reconstruct both horns of the independence result — Gödel's 1940 constructible universe *L*, establishing Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + CH), and Cohen's 1963–1964 forcing construction, establishing Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + ¬CH) [1][2][3]. The exposition develops the Boolean-valued and countable-transitive-model presentations of forcing, the forcing theorem relating generic filters to truth in generic extensions *M[G]*, and the specific application of Cohen forcing Fn(κ, 2) to adjoin κ-many Cohen reals, thereby pushing 2^{ℵ₀} ≥ κ and refuting CH while preserving all cardinals. We then examine inner models as a complementary technology for consistency results, survey the continuum function and Easton's theorem on its behavior at regular cardinals, and conclude with the modern philosophical upshot: the set-theoretic multiverse articulated by Hamkins [4][5], in which forcing is reconceived not as a mere consistency device but as revealing a genuine plurality of set-theoretic universes.

## 1 Introduction

Few problems have resisted mathematical reason as stubbornly as Cantor's Continuum Hypothesis. Posed in 1878 and placed first among Hilbert's twenty-three problems in 1900, CH asserts the clean, almost musical claim that

> **Continuum Hypothesis (CH):** Every uncountable set of real numbers is in bijection with the entire real line ℝ. Equivalently, 2^{ℵ₀} = ℵ₁.

Cantor proved ℵ₀ < 2^{ℵ₀} but could never settle whether anything lies between. Gödel spent a quarter century on the problem without success [6], and it was left to Paul J. Cohen, in a pair of 1963–1964 papers [1][2], to deliver what Gödel himself called "the most important progress in set theory since its axiomatization" [6]: the discovery of **forcing**, a method for constructing new models of ZFC from old ones, by which CH could be *falsified* in a model while ZFC held. Since Gödel's inner model *L* already showed CH could never be refuted from ZFC [3], the two halves together establish full independence:

| Result | Direction | Witness | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative consistency of CH | Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + CH) | Constructible universe *L* | 1940 [3] |
| Relative consistency of ¬CH | Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + ¬CH) | Cohen forcing extension | 1963–64 [1][2] |

This thesis develops the machinery of forcing from first principles, proves the independence theorem in its modern form, and explores the aftermath: inner models as the dual technology, the theory of cardinal invariants and the continuum function, and the multiverse conception of set theory.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Cumulative Hierarchy and Models of ZFC

Working within the informal ZFC metatheory, the set-theoretic universe *V* is stratified by the *cumulative hierarchy*:

```haskell
-- Conceptual sketch: V_{alpha+1} = P(V_alpha), V_lambda = union over beta < lambda
cumulative :: Ordinal -> Set
cumulative 0          = Empty
cumulative (Succ a)   = PowerSet (cumulative a)
cumulative (Limit l)  = Union [cumulative b | b <- ordinalsBelow l]
```

Forcing builds *new* models by *approximating* a new set from outside *M* via a partial order of conditions, then adjoining an ideal object — a **generic filter** — that the ground model *M* itself cannot see.

### 2.2 Gödel's Constructible Universe

Gödel's response to CH, published in his 1940 monograph [3], was to *thin out* the universe: replace the reckless power set operation P(*X*) with the definable power set Def(*X*), the collection of subsets of *X* first-order definable over (*X*, ∈). The resulting hierarchy *L₀ = ∅, L_{α+1} = Def(L_α), L_λ = ∪_{β<λ} L_β* yields the inner model *L* = ∪_α *L_α*, the **constructible universe**.

> **Theorem (Gödel, 1940):** *L* is an inner model of ZF; moreover *L* ⊨ "every set is constructible" (V = L), AC, and the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis GCH. Hence Con(ZF) → Con(ZFC + CH) [3].

The definable power set operation is absolute for transitive models, and the fine structure of *L* yields a canonical global well-ordering of *L* — a Δ₁-definable well-order — from which AC follows inside *L*, and a counting argument shows |P^{L}(ℵ_α)| = ℵ_{α+1}, i.e. GCH. Philosophically, *L* is the "minimal" universe: it contains exactly the sets forced to exist by definability.

### 2.3 The Problem of ¬CH

Gödel's construction can only ever produce *narrower* models satisfying CH. To get ¬CH, one must go in the opposite direction: *widen* a model by adding new reals. The naive approach — adjoining an arbitrary new subset of ω — risks collapsing cardinals or violating other axioms. Cohen's genius was to make the adjunction *controlled* via finite approximations, ensuring the extension satisfies ZFC by a verification argument carried out with **forcing names** and the **forcing relation**.

## 3 Methodology

Our method is the *countable transitive model* (ctm) approach to forcing, standard in modern presentations (Jech [8]; the Boolean-valued approach of Scott–Solovay is equivalent). Assume Con(ZFC); by the reflection and Löwenheim–Skolem theorems plus Mostowski collapse, there exists a countable transitive model *M* ⊨ ZFC. Inside *M*, we fix a partial order (ℙ, ≤) — the **forcing notion** — with maximum element 1_ℙ.

```python
# Schematic: Cohen forcing for adding kappa many reals
# Conditions are finite partial functions from kappa x omega into 2
def cohen_conditions(kappa):
    conds = []
    for p in finite_partial_functions(kappa * omega, {0, 1}):
        conds.append(p)
    return conds  # ordered by reverse inclusion: p <= q iff p extends q
```

A filter *G* ⊆ ℙ is *M*-generic if it meets every dense subset *D* ⊆ ℙ with *D* ∈ *M*. The Rasiowa–Sikorski lemma guarantees such filters exist outside *M*: since *M* is countable, enumerate its dense sets {*D_n*} and diagonalize. The **generic extension** is then

*M[G]* = { τ^G : τ ∈ *M*^ℙ },

where *M*^ℙ is the class of ℙ-names in *M* and τ^G is the interpretation of the name τ by *G*. The entire construction is governed by the **Forcing Theorem**:

> **Theorem (Forcing Theorem):** For every formula φ and ℙ-names τ₁, …, τ_n ∈ *M*^ℙ:
> 1. (Definability) The relation *p* ⊩ φ(τ₁, …, τ_n) ("*p* forces φ") is definable in *M*.
> 2. (Truth) *M[G]* ⊨ φ(τ₁^G, …, τ_n^G) ⟺ (∃*p* ∈ *G*) *p* ⊩ φ(τ₁, …, τ_n).

This is the load-bearing pillar: it lets us prove facts about the *inaccessible* model *M[G]* by combinatorial arguments about the poset ℙ carried out *inside* *M*. Cohen's original 1963 papers [1] developed the method in a syntactic form (with "complete sequences" playing the role of generic filters), later streamlined by Solovay, Scott, and others [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cohen Forcing and the Addition of Reals

The forcing notion **Cohen forcing** for adding κ-many new reals is

ℙ = Fn(κ × ω, 2) = { *p* : *p* is a finite partial function κ × ω → 2 },

ordered by reverse inclusion: *p* ≤ *q* iff *p* ⊇ *q*. The generic filter *G* determines, for each α < κ, a real *r_α* = { *n* : (∃*p* ∈ *G*) *p*(α, *n*) = 1 }. The density argument shows each *r_α* is total and distinct from every real of *M*:

- For each α, *n*: *D_{α,n}* = { *p* : (α, *n*) ∈ dom(*p*) } is dense, so *r_α* is a genuine function ω → 2.
- For each *x* ∈ P(ω) ∩ *M*: *D_x* = { *p* : (∃α, *n*) *p*(α, *n*) ≠ *x*(*n*) } is dense, so each new real differs from every old real.

Thus *M[G]* contains at least κ-many *new* reals, and 2^{ℵ₀} ≥ κ in *M[G]*. Choosing κ = (ℵ₂)^M (or indeed any cardinal with uncountable cofinality), we obtain *M[G]* ⊨ ¬CH [1][2].

### 4.2 Preserving Cardinals: The Chain Condition

The delicate part is showing *M[G]* does not collapse cardinals — otherwise "2^{ℵ₀} ≥ κ" would be vacuous. Cohen forcing satisfies the **countable chain condition** (ccc): every antichain is countable. The proof is a Δ-system argument: any uncountable family of finite partial functions contains an uncountable subfamily with pairwise compatible domains on a fixed root.

> **Lemma:** If ℙ is ccc, then forcing with ℙ preserves all cardinals and cofinalities.

*Proof sketch.* Suppose *p* ⊩ "ḟ : κ̌ → λ̌ is cofinal" with κ < λ regular in *M*. For each α < κ, the set of possible values of ḟ(α) forced by conditions below *p* forms a maximal antichain, hence countable; the union over α < κ of these possibilities has size ≤ κ · ℵ₀ < λ (using AC in *M*), contradicting cofinality in λ. ∎

Consequently, if κ = ℵ₂^M, then ℵ₁ and ℵ₂ are preserved, and *M[G]* genuinely satisfies 2^{ℵ₀} ≥ ℵ₂ > ℵ₁ = (ℵ₁)^{M[G]}, i.e. ¬CH. This completes Cohen's half of the independence theorem [2].

### 4.3 The Forcing Relation in Detail

The definition of ⊩ proceeds by recursion on formulas, but the atomic cases already reveal the mechanism. For names σ, τ:

- *p* ⊩ σ ∈ τ ⟺ the set { *q* ≤ *p* : (∃(π, *r*) ∈ τ) *q* ≤ *r* ∧ *q* ⊩ σ = π } is dense below *p*;
- *p* ⊩ σ = τ ⟺ *p* ⊩ σ ⊆ τ and *p* ⊩ τ ⊆ σ, with ⊆ defined via the ∈-clause.

```tla+
---- MODULE ForcingRelation ----
\* TLA+ sketch of the forcing clauses for atomic formulas
EXTENDS Naturals
ForceIn(p, sigma, tau) ==
    \E D \in DenseBelow(p) :
        \A q \in D : \E pi, r : <<pi, r>> \in tau /\ q <= r /\ ForceEq(q, sigma, pi)
====
```

The crucial metatheorem is the **forcing theorem** stated in §3: truth in *M[G]* is exactly forced truth by some condition in *G*. This converts the semantic question "what holds in the extension?" into the combinatorial question "what do conditions force?", which can be answered inside the ground model.

### 4.4 Inner Models vs. Outer Models: A Duality

Forcing and inner-model theory are dual technologies for relative consistency:

- **Inner models** (*L*, *L*[U], core models) *shrink* the universe, proving statements *consistent* by exhibiting minimal universes where they hold — e.g. V = L → CH [3].
- **Forcing extensions** *M[G]* *expand* the universe, proving statements consistent by exhibiting richer universes — e.g. adding ℵ₂ Cohen reals → ¬CH [1][2].

This duality drives the *covering lemma* program (Jensen, Dodd–Jensen, Mitchell–Steel), which shows that if 0^♯ does not exist then *L* closely approximates *V*; conversely, forcing axioms like Martin's Axiom and PFA *maximize* the universe in the direction opposite to V = L [4][7].

### 4.5 The Continuum Function and Easton's Theorem

Cohen's result leaves open: what values *can* 2^{ℵ₀} take? König's theorem gives the only ZFC-provable restriction: cf(2^{ℵ₀}) > ℵ₀, i.e. the continuum cannot have countable cofinality. Easton's theorem (1970) shows this is essentially the *only* restriction for regular cardinals:

> **Theorem (Easton):** Let *F* be a class function on regular cardinals with κ < λ → *F*(κ) ≤ *F*(λ) and cf(*F*(κ)) > κ. Then Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + "2^κ = *F*(κ) for all regular κ").

Thus ZFC is compatible with 2^{ℵ₀} = ℵ₁, ℵ₂, ℵ₁₇, ℵ_{ω+1}, or essentially any prescribed pattern subject to monotonicity and cofinality constraints [7][8]: the continuum is *radically* underdetermined by ZFC.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We now assemble the independence theorem in its canonical modern statement.

> **Theorem (Gödel–Cohen Independence of CH):** If ZFC is consistent, then so are ZFC + CH and ZFC + ¬CH. Hence CH is *independent* of ZFC: neither CH nor ¬CH is provable from ZFC (assuming consistency).

*Proof.* (→ CH) Gödel's *L* satisfies ZFC + GCH, hence ZFC + CH [3]. Formally, inside any model *M* ⊨ ZF, the class *L^M* is a transitive (class) model of ZFC + V = L + GCH, so Con(ZF) → Con(ZFC + CH).

(→ ¬CH) Let *M* be a countable transitive model of ZFC (exists by Con(ZFC) + reflection + collapse). Let κ = (ℵ₂)^M and ℙ = Fn(κ × ω, 2) ∈ *M*. Let *G* be *M*-generic for ℙ (exists by Rasiowa–Sikorski, since *M* is countable). Then:

1. *M[G]* ⊨ ZFC, by the forcing theorem and verification of each axiom via names [1][2].
2. ℙ is ccc (Δ-system lemma), so cardinals are preserved: (ℵ₁)^{M[G]} = ℵ₁^M, (ℵ₂)^{M[G]} = ℵ₂^M.
3. The κ-many Cohen reals {*r_α* : α < κ} are pairwise distinct and new, so *M[G]* ⊨ |P(ω)| ≥ κ = ℵ₂ > ℵ₁.
4. Hence *M[G]* ⊨ ZFC + 2^{ℵ₀} ≥ ℵ₂, i.e. *M[G]* ⊨ ¬CH.

Therefore Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + ¬CH). ∎

**Corollary.** The same template — choose a poset, prove a preservation lemma, count the new objects — yields independence results for thousands of statements: Souslin's hypothesis, the Borel conjecture, and the cardinal invariants of the continuum, whose interrelations form Cichoń's diagram [8].

```rust
// The forcing proof template, schematically: a poset, a preservation
// lemma, and a counting argument yield a model of ZFC + ¬CH.
fn independence<M: Ctm, P: Ccc>(m: &M, p: &P, g: Generic<P>) -> Extension {
    let ext = m.adjoin(g);            // M[G]
    debug_assert!(ext.models_zfc());  // forcing theorem
    debug_assert!(p.preserves_cardinals());
    debug_assert!(ext.models_not_ch()); // count the new Cohen reals
    ext
}
```

## 6 Limitations

1. **Relative consistency only.** Forcing proves Con(ZFC) → Con(ZFC + φ), never Con(ZFC) outright — by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, no such proof is possible within ZFC. The entire edifice rests on the unprovable assumption of ZFC's consistency.
2. **The countable transitive model fiction.** The ctm approach assumes the *existence* of a countable transitive model of ZFC, which is strictly stronger than Con(ZFC) (it implies Con(ZFC + Con(ZFC))). The Boolean-valued model approach [7][8] avoids this by working with *V*^𝔹 directly, at the cost of heavier algebra.
3. **Forcing cannot prove CH.** A deep limitation: no "reasonable" forcing extension of a model of ¬CH satisfies CH in a nontrivial way — forcing can *add* reals but cannot *remove* them, so it is essentially a method for *falsifying* CH-like statements, while inner models handle the positive direction. (More precisely, forcing extensions always contain the ground model's reals.)
4. **Singular cardinals and pcf theory.** Easton's theorem controls 2^κ only for *regular* κ. The behavior at singular cardinals is constrained by Shelah's pcf theory (e.g. if 2^{ℵ_n} < ℵ_ω for all *n*, then 2^{ℵ_ω} < ℵ_{ω₄}), showing ZFC *does* prove nontrivial theorems about the continuum function at singulars.
5. **Philosophical incompleteness.** Independence tells us ZFC does not decide CH, but not whether CH has a *determinate truth value*. The multiverse view [4][5] embraces the plurality; the universe view (Woodin, Steel) seeks new axioms — large cardinals, forcing axioms, Ultimate *L* — to settle it. This debate is philosophical, not mathematical, and forcing alone cannot resolve it [6].

## 7 Conclusion

Cohen's forcing transformed set theory from a discipline seeking *the* universe of sets into one exploring a *space* of universes, connected by the precise, controllable operation of generic extension [1][2]. Together with Gödel's inner model *L* [3], it established the independence of the Continuum Hypothesis — the first great independence result and still the paradigmatic one. The method's power lies in its modularity: posets are combinatorial objects, preservation is proved by chain conditions and properness, and truth in the extension is reduced by the forcing theorem to forcing relations computable in the ground model. From this single idea flowed the independence of Souslin's hypothesis, the axiom of choice fragments, cardinal invariants, and the entire modern theory of the continuum. Whether one adopts Hamkins's multiverse pluralism [4][5] — in which every consistent sharpening of the set concept is realized in some universe — or holds out for new axioms that restore uniqueness, forcing remains the indispensable instrument: the telescope through which we survey the plurality of mathematical worlds that ZFC permits.
