# Deductive Program Synthesis: Hoare-Logic-Driven Derivation, Angelic Nondeterminism and Refinement, and the Spectrum from Correctness by Construction to Counterexample-Guided Induction

## Abstract

Program synthesis seeks to derive executable programs mechanically from declarative specifications. This thesis reconstructs the *deductive* lineage of the field, in which synthesis is proof: the program is extracted from a constructive derivation that its specification is realizable. We formalize the Manna–Waldinger deductive framework and Hoare-logic-driven synthesis, where inference rules over pre/postcondition triples build the program and its correctness certificate in a single derivation [1]. We then develop two principled extensions that preserve the deductive guarantee while attacking its intractability: **angelic nondeterminism**, under which unknown recursive calls are executed optimistically against the specification and the resulting assumptions discharged by re-synthesis [4], and **stepwise refinement**, where abstract specifications descend through a lattice of correctness-preserving transformations to executable code. We formalize *synthesis modulo theories* as the delegation of deductive obligation to SMT decision procedures [2], and contrast correctness by construction with the dominant modern alternative, counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) [6,8]. Our thesis is that the deductive/inductive divide is a spectrum rather than a dichotomy: SMT-backed deductive techniques recover the proof-theoretic guarantees of classical synthesis while approaching the empirical scalability of inductive search.

## 1 Intro

The dream of automatic programming is as old as the stored-program computer: specify *what* is required, and let a machine discover *how* to compute it. The earliest serious attempt cast synthesis as theorem proving. Given a specification

$$\exists f.\, \forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(f, \bar{x}),$$

where $\varphi$ relates the unknown function $f$ to its inputs, Green and later Manna and Waldinger showed how to *prove* this existential statement constructively: the witness term assembled from the unifying substitutions of the proof is, itself, a program satisfying the specification [1]. The program never needs independent verification, because the proof that it exists is a proof that it works. This is the founding creed of **deductive synthesis**: correctness by construction, derived in one artifact.

Hoare logic provided the natural proof system for this program. A Hoare triple $\{P\}\, S\, \{Q\}$ states that if precondition $P$ holds before executing $S$, then postcondition $Q$ holds after [7]. If we *run the inference rules backwards* — starting from the desired triple $\{P\}\, ?\, \{Q\}$ with a program hole — each rule application decomposes the hole into smaller holes while accumulating the conditions each piece must satisfy. Deductive synthesis is therefore Hoare logic inverted: verification goes from program to proof; synthesis goes from proof to program.

The creed was elegant but intractable. Proof search in first-order logic explodes; the invariants and intermediate assertions that Hoare's while-rule demands must be invented; and real specifications require arithmetic, arrays, and bit-vectors that pure proof systems handle poorly. The field's pragmatic answer was the **inductive** turn: instead of proving $\forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi$, search a syntactic space of candidate programs and *check* each candidate against the specification on concrete counterexamples, generalizing to full correctness only through an oracle [6,8]. Counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) became the dominant paradigm, and the dream of proof-driven synthesis receded into folklore.

This thesis argues that the recession was premature. Three modern developments re-arm the deductive approach: **angelic semantics**, which lets bottom-up synthesis execute unknown recursive calls optimistically [4]; **refinement calculi**, which structure the descent from specification to code as a verified algebra of transformation laws; and **synthesis modulo theories**, which outsources the hardest deductive steps to SMT solvers [2]. Together they form a continuous spectrum from pure deduction to pure induction — and modern tools increasingly occupy its middle.

## 2 Background

We fix a simple imperative language of guarded commands in the Dijkstra style, with assignments, sequential composition, conditionals, and loops, plus a minimalist functional core of recursion and arithmetic for the Manna–Waldinger derivations [1].

### Hoare triples and weakest preconditions

A triple $\{P\}\, S\, \{Q\}$ is *partially correct* when every terminating execution of $S$ started in a state satisfying $P$ ends in a state satisfying $Q$. Dijkstra's predicate transformer $wp(S, Q)$ gives the weakest precondition guaranteeing $Q$ after $S$, with the characteristic laws

$$wp(x := e, Q) = Q[e/x], \qquad wp(S_1; S_2, Q) = wp(S_1, wp(S_2, Q)).$$

Weakest preconditions make synthesis *calculational*: the program hole between $P$ and $Q$ can be filled by any $S$ with $P \Rightarrow wp(S, Q)$. Hoare's rules for assignment, consequence, composition, and iteration become the generative grammar of the synthesizer [7].

### Angelic versus demonic nondeterminism

Nondeterministic choice $S_1 \sqcap S_2$ can be interpreted *demonically* — the choice is resolved adversarially, so correctness must hold for *all* resolutions — or *angelically* — the choice is resolved cooperatively, so correctness need hold for *some* resolution. Demonic choice underlies refinement: replacing a specification by a program must preserve correctness under every interpretation. Angelic choice, by contrast, is the logician's existential quantifier made operational: it asks whether *there exists* a resolution making the specification true [4].

### Refinement and the specification statement

Back's refinement calculus treats programs as predicate transformers ordered by refinement: $S \sqsubseteq T$ when every behavior of $T$ is a behavior of $S$, i.e., $wp(S, Q) \Rightarrow wp(T, Q)$ for all $Q$. The *specification statement* $x :(P, Q)$ denotes any program that, started in $P$, establishes $Q$ over the frame $x$. Synthesis is then the derivation of a chain

$$x :(P, Q) \sqsubseteq S_1 \sqsubseteq S_2 \sqsubseteq \cdots \sqsubseteq S_n,$$

where each $\sqsubseteq$ step is justified by a refinement law, and $S_n$ is executable. Every law application preserves the contract, so the final program is correct *by construction* — no post hoc verification required.

### The synthesis problem, modernized

The contemporary formalization is syntax-guided synthesis (SyGuS): find a term $f$ in a grammar $G$ such that $\forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(f, \bar{x})$ holds in a background theory $T$ [3]. Semantics-guided synthesis (SemGuS) generalizes the framework by letting users define the *meaning* of grammar constructs through inference rules, bridging the gap between fixed-theory SyGuS and imperative synthesis with loops [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our method in this thesis is comparative and formal: we reconstruct each paradigm as a precise inference system, state its soundness and completeness properties, and evaluate the tradeoffs empirically.

**Deductive core.** We present the Manna–Waldinger sequent calculus: assertions and goals in non-clausal form, structural induction for recursion, and program extraction from unifying substitutions [1]. A derivation of $\exists f.\, \forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(f, \bar{x})$ yields a term $t$ with $\forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(t, \bar{x})$; the rules are engineered so that the witness is a program in the target language rather than an arbitrary logical term.

**Hoare-driven derivation.** We exhibit the inversion of Hoare's rules as synthesis rules. The consequence rule becomes *specification strengthening/weakening*; the while-rule becomes *invariant invention followed by body synthesis*; the assignment axiom becomes *backward substitution search*. We work a complete example (maximum of two numbers) in the methodology and generalize.

**Angelic extension.** We define angelic semantics $\llbracket P \rrbracket^{\varphi}$ for recursive procedures, where a recursive call $f(v)$ may return any value $v'$ consistent with the specification $\varphi$ [4]. Angelic satisfaction $P \models_v \varphi$ holds when some angelic execution validates the spec. The *Burst* algorithm synthesizes under angelic semantics, extracts the assumption witness $\omega$ that the angelic execution relied upon, and re-attempts synthesis on the strengthened specification $\chi \wedge \omega$, backtracking on $\chi \wedge \neg\omega$ when the assumption proves inconsistent.

**Refinement chains.** We give the refinement laws — skip introduction, sequential decomposition, conditional introduction, loop introduction with variant — and show how each corresponds to a Hoare-rule inversion, unifying the two views.

**Synthesis modulo theories.** We formalize CEGIS($T$) [2]: the learner proposes candidates, the verifier refutes them with counterexamples, and a *theory solver* generalizes the refutation via quantifier elimination (e.g., Fourier–Motzkin over linear arithmetic) to prune entire families of candidates, including the synthesis of *arbitrary constants* that pure enumerative CEGIS cannot reach.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hoare-Logic-Driven Synthesis and Proof-Theoretic Program Extraction

The Manna–Waldinger framework begins from a first-order specification and constructs a proof in a non-clausal deductive system: resolution without clausal normal form, splitting of conjunctive assertions and disjunctive goals, equality handling via generalized E-resolution and paramodulation, and structural induction to introduce recursion [1]. The proof maintains an *output expression* — initially the variable $f(\bar{x})$ — and each inference step that instantiates variables refines the output expression. When the proof closes, the output expression is the program.

> **Theorem:** (Manna–Waldinger extraction) If the deductive system derives the goal $\exists f.\, \forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(f, \bar{x})$ with output expression $t$, then $\forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(t, \bar{x})$ is valid and $t$ is a program in the target language. *Proof sketch.* Each rule preserves the invariant that the current goal plus output expression denotes the set of admissible completions; resolution steps correspond to program case splits, and induction steps to recursive calls. The invariant is established by induction over the derivation [1].

Consider synthesizing $\mathit{max}(x, y)$ with specification $\mathit{max}(x,y) \geq x \wedge \mathit{max}(x,y) \geq y \wedge (\mathit{max}(x,y) = x \vee \mathit{max}(x,y) = y)$. Backward application of the conditional rule splits the goal on $x \geq y$; in each branch the specification simplifies to a ground choice, and the output expression becomes

```haskell
max :: Int -> Int -> Int
max x y = if x >= y then x else y
```

The conditional *is* the resolution step; the proof's case analysis *is* the program's control flow. This identity between proof structure and program structure is the aesthetic and technical core of deductive synthesis: control constructs are the computational content of logical rules. Inverting the while-rule,

$$\frac{\{I \wedge b\}\, S\, \{I\}}{\{I\}\, \texttt{while } b \texttt{ do } S\, \{I \wedge \neg b\}},$$

synthesis must *invent* the invariant $I$ before it can synthesize the body $S$. This is the paradigm's notorious bottleneck: the creative step of verification becomes the creative step of synthesis. Modern deductive tools therefore pair the inverted rules with invariant *inference* engines — abstract interpretation, interpolation, or SyGuS-based invariant synthesis [3] — recovering automation without abandoning the proof-theoretic guarantee.

### 4.2 Angelic Nondeterminism: Optimistic Execution Under Specification

Bottom-up synthesis evaluates candidate subexpressions to prune the search, but recursive programs cannot be executed before they are fully constructed.

> **Definition:** (Angelic semantics) Given a recursive procedure $P$ and specification $\varphi$, the angelic semantics $\llbracket P \rrbracket^{\varphi}$ interprets recursive calls angelically: $\llbracket f(v) \rrbracket^{\varphi}$ is the set of all $v'$ such that $f(v) = v'$ is consistent with $\varphi$. *Angelic satisfaction* $P \models_v \varphi$ holds iff $\exists v'.\, v' \in \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\varphi}(v) \wedge \mathrm{SAT}(f(v) = v' \wedge \varphi)$ [4].

Angelic execution is an *overapproximation*: if no angelic execution satisfies the spec, no real execution can.

> **Theorem:** (Soundness of angelic synthesis) If `SYNTHESIZE`($\chi$) returns a program $P$, then $P \models \chi$ under the true semantics. *Proof.* Every returned program passes the line-8 true-semantics check; angelic assumptions are only used to guide search, never to justify the final artifact [4].

> **Theorem:** (Relative completeness) If `SYNTHESIZE`($\chi$) returns failure, then no program satisfies $\chi$, provided the angelic synthesizer is complete and assumption extraction is faithful [4].

Philosophically, angelic nondeterminism is deduction's answer to induction's guessing: where CEGIS *guesses* candidates and *tests* them, angelic synthesis *assumes* the best about unknown parts and *discharges* the assumptions.

### 4.3 Refinement, the Refinement Calculus, and Correctness by Construction

Where Hoare-logic-driven synthesis inverts verification rules, the refinement calculus inverts *development*: it starts from the most abstract artifact — the specification statement $x :(P, Q)$ — and applies laws until only executable constructs remain.

- **Skip introduction:** $x :(P, P) \sqsubseteq \mathbf{skip}$.
- **Assignment introduction:** $x :(P, Q) \sqsubseteq x := e$ when $P \Rightarrow Q[e/x]$.
- **Sequential decomposition:** $x :(P, Q) \sqsubseteq x :(P, M)\,;\, x :(M, Q)$ for any midcondition $M$.
- **Conditional introduction:** $x :(P, Q) \sqsubseteq \mathbf{if}\ b\ \mathbf{then}\ x :(P \wedge b, Q)\ \mathbf{else}\ x :(P \wedge \neg b, Q)$.
- **Loop introduction:** with invariant $I$ and variant $V$ decreasing over a well-founded order, $x :(P, Q) \sqsubseteq \mathbf{while}\ b\ \mathbf{do}\ x :(I \wedge b \wedge V = v_0, I \wedge V < v_0)$.

Each law is justified once, by the predicate-transformer semantics, and then applied any number of times.

```tla
---- MODULE MaxRefinement ----
EXTENDS Integers
VARIABLES x, y, m
MaxSpec == m \in {a \in Int : a >= x /\ a >= y /\ (a = x \/ a = y)}
Refine  == (* x:[TRUE, MaxSpec] *)
           IF x >= y THEN m := x    (* conditional introduction *)
                    ELSE m := y
====
```

The TLA+ sketch above mirrors the refinement derivation: the abstract postcondition is decomposed by conditional introduction, and each branch is discharged by assignment introduction via backward substitution.

### 4.4 Synthesis Modulo Theories

Pure deductive synthesis founders on arithmetic: proving facts about integers, arrays, and bit-vectors inside a general proof system is prohibitively expensive.

1. **Learn.** From the current specification $\sigma$ (initially the user's spec plus accumulated refinements), synthesize a candidate $f^*$ — possibly containing symbolic constants.
2. **Verify.** Ask the SMT solver whether $\exists \bar{x}.\, \neg\sigma(f^*, \bar{x})$ is satisfiable. If unsatisfiable, $f^*$ is correct and the loop terminates.
3. **Generalize.** If a counterexample exists, replace concrete constants in $f^*$ by symbolic variables and use *quantifier elimination* (e.g., Fourier–Motzkin over linear arithmetic) to characterize *all* valuations admitting a counterexample, pruning the entire family at once.

The decisive advantage over plain CEGIS is the synthesis of *arbitrary constants*: where enumerative inductive synthesis can only assemble constants present in the problem description, CEGIS($T$) derives them from the theory, solving benchmarks (e.g., thresholds like 334455) on which tools like CVC4 fail [2].

Dually, SMT-based deductive synthesis (as in modern SyGuS solvers) encodes the *entire* synthesis problem $\exists f.\, \forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi(f, \bar{x})$ as a quantified SMT formula over the theory $T$, discharging the universal quantifier by counterexample-driven instantiation inside the solver itself [3].

### 4.5 Correctness by Construction versus Counterexample-Guided Induction

We can now state the comparison crisply.

| Dimension | Correctness by construction (deductive) | Counterexample-guided induction (CEGIS) |
|---|---|---|
| Search driver | Proof rules / refinement laws | Candidate enumeration or constraint solving over examples |
| Correctness argument | The derivation *is* the proof | A final oracle check $\forall \bar{x}.\, \varphi$ |
| Intermediate artifacts | Specifications with precise semantics | Programs correct on finitely many inputs |
| Creative burden | Invariants, midconditions, induction schemas | Grammar design, example selection |
| Failure mode | Proof search explosion | Overfitting to counterexamples; constant invention |
| Theory reasoning | Delegated to SMT (synthesis modulo theories) [2] | Delegated to SMT (verifier) [8] |

CEGIS, formalized as the iterative loop of *synthesis phase* (candidate correct on known inputs) and *verification phase* (oracle refutation yielding a new input), is complete in the limit for finite program spaces but offers no correctness insight for the candidates it rejects [8].

The SemGuS framework [5] points beyond the dichotomy: by expressing program semantics as inference rules, it lets a single engine mix deductive reasoning about *meaning* with inductive search over *syntax*.

## 5 Theoretical Analysis and Empirical Results

### Soundness and completeness landscape

> **Theorem:** (Extraction soundness) Any program extracted from a closed Manna–Waldinger derivation satisfies its specification in all models of the background theory. *Proof.* By induction on the derivation, maintaining that the output expression denotes exactly the set of terms consistent with the proven assertions [1].

> **Theorem:** (CEGIS convergence) For a finite candidate space ordered by the learner, the CEGIS loop terminates with a correct program iff one exists in the space. *Proof.* Each iteration eliminates at least the refuted candidate (in fact, all candidates failing the new counterexample); finiteness bounds the iterations [8].

The asymmetry is instructive: deductive completeness is relative to the *proof system's* strength (first-order logic is complete, but invariant invention is not mechanized), while inductive completeness is relative to the *grammar's* expressiveness. Neither paradigm escapes the fundamental undecidability of synthesis over rich theories; they merely choose different incomplete-but-useful fragments.

### Complexity comparison

| Technique | Dominant cost | Decidable fragment | Constants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manna–Waldinger deduction | Proof search (unbounded) | FOL (semi-decidable) | Derived by unification |
| Hoare-rule inversion | Invariant invention (undecidable in general) | Relative completeness (Cook) | Via consequence rule |
| Angelic synthesis (Burst) | Version-space learning over angelic executions | Recursive programs, ground specs | From version space |
| Refinement calculus | Law selection; midcondition invention | Predicate transformers over wp | Via assignment law |
| CEGIS($T$) | SMT calls per iteration; QE cost | Theory $T$ (e.g., LIA via Fourier–Motzkin) | Arbitrary, via QE [2] |
| Enumerative SyGuS | Grammar enumeration, exponential in size | Grammar-bounded | Description-bound [3] |

### Empirical evidence

- **Angelic synthesis.** The Burst prototype synthesizes solutions to 94% of its benchmark suite, outperforming prior bottom-up techniques; the angelic overapproximation prunes the search without sacrificing the soundness guarantee [4].
- **Synthesis modulo theories.** CEGIS($T$) solves benchmarks requiring arbitrary integer constants (e.g., thresholds unguessable by enumeration) on which CVC4 fails, at the cost of slower per-iteration performance due to quantifier elimination [2].
- **Inductive dominance.** Enumerative techniques remain the dominant SyGuS strategy; SyGuS-Comp results show that search heuristics, not deductive strength, decide most winners — though every competitive solver embeds SMT-based deductive pruning [3].
- **Semantics-guided synthesis.** SemGuS demonstrates that expressing semantics as inference rules recovers imperative synthesis (loops, mutable state) outside any fixed SMT theory, at the cost of a larger, less automated search space [5].

The pattern across all four data points: *deduction buys guarantees and generality; induction buys speed; theory solvers convert between the two currencies.*

## 6 Limitations

**Intractability of proof search.** Deductive synthesis inherits the worst case of automated theorem proving. Non-clausal resolution, E-resolution, and paramodulation control the explosion only partially; realistic specifications defeat unaided proof search, which is why the original Manna–Waldinger vision never scaled to industrial code [1].

**The invariant bottleneck.** Both Hoare-rule inversion and loop introduction in the refinement calculus require inventing invariants, variants, and midconditions — creative acts that resist automation. Deductive synthesis does not eliminate the hard part of verification; it relocates it to the front of the pipeline.

**Angelic assumptions can mislead.** Angelic synthesis is complete only relative to faithful assumption extraction; a poor witness $\omega$ sends the search down barren strengthenings, and the backtracking over $\chi \wedge \neg\omega$ can degenerate into enumeration in adversarial cases [4].

**Theory boundaries.** Synthesis modulo theories is confined to decidable theories. Nonlinear arithmetic, heap reasoning, and higher-order specifications fall outside the SMT fragment, and quantifier elimination (Fourier–Motzkin is doubly exponential in the worst case) bounds the practical size of the generalized refutations [2].

**Grammar dependence of the inductive side.** CEGIS and SyGuS solvers are only as good as their grammars: too restrictive, and no solution exists in the space; too permissive, and enumeration drowns. Grammar design remains a dark art, and overfitting to counterexamples produces programs that are correct but inscrutable [3,6].

**Evaluation gaps.** Benchmark suites (SyGuS-Comp, Burst's suite) measure solvability on curated problems, not maintainability, readability, or robustness of the synthesized code — precisely the qualities deductive derivations were designed to provide.

## 7 Conclusion

Deductive program synthesis began with a radical proposition: that writing a program and proving a theorem are the same activity, and that a machine performing the latter thereby performs the former [1]. Hoare logic gave the proposition its imperative form [7]; the refinement calculus gave it an algebra; angelic semantics gave it a way to reason optimistically about the unknown [4]; and SMT solvers gave it the arithmetic muscle it always lacked [2].

The inductive counter-revolution — sketching, CEGIS, SyGuS — won the empirical battles by replacing proof search with guess-and-check [6,8]. But the war is ending in synthesis rather than victory: every scalable modern technique is a hybrid. CEGIS($T$) embeds a deductive theory solver inside an inductive loop [2]; angelic synthesis embeds inductive version-space learning inside a deductive assumption-discharge discipline [4]; SemGuS expresses inductive search spaces in deductive semantic rules [5].

The research frontier is the principled placement of that obligation: enough deduction to guarantee correctness and explain it, enough induction to find the program before the heat death of the universe. Fifty years after Hoare's axiomatic basis, the deductive dream is not dead — it has simply learned to delegate.
