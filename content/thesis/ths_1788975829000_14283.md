# PLONK, KZG Commitments, and FRI: The Arithmetization Stack of Modern SNARKs

## Abstract

Modern succinct arguments of knowledge are assembled from interoperable modules: an *arithmetization* converting computation into polynomial relations, a *permutation argument* enforcing wire equality, a *polynomial commitment scheme* binding the prover to those polynomials, and a Fiat–Shamir transform compressing the interaction. This thesis gives a rigorous, self-contained account of the three pillars of the most widely deployed SNARK stack: the PLONK constraint system and its grand-product permutation argument, the Kate–Zaverucha–Goldberg (KZG) polynomial commitment scheme on pairing-friendly curves, and the Fast Reed–Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity (FRI) that replaces trusted setup with hash-based transparency. We present the full five-round PLONK protocol with quotient splitting and batch opening, sketch the essential soundness lemmas, derive the KZG pairing equations and their reduction to $q$-SDH, and analyze FRI's folding operator and $O(\lambda \log n)$ query complexity. We close with quantitative prover/verifier comparisons, concrete deployments, and the limitations — SRS toxic waste, field–pairing tradeoffs, FRI proof-size overhead — and open problems defining the polynomial-IOP frontier.

## 1. Introduction

Since the introduction of practical zk-SNARKs, proof-system engineering has converged on a uniform skeleton: computation is arithmetized into a *polynomial interactive oracle proof* (poly-IOP), the polynomial oracles are instantiated with a *polynomial commitment scheme*, and the interaction is made non-interactive by the Fiat–Shamir heuristic. PLONK [1], introduced by Gabizon, Williamson, and Ciobotaru in 2019, is the canonical exemplar of this modular design and the intellectual ancestor of Halo2, dusk-network's Dusk-Plonk, and several production zkEVMs.

This thesis studies the three load-bearing components of this stack:

1. **PLONK's arithmetization and permutation argument.** Unlike rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS), PLONK encodes a circuit with $n$ gates as *evaluations on a multiplicative subgroup* $H \subset \mathbb{F}$ of order $n$, with selector polynomials defining each gate's type. Wire equality ("copy constraints") is enforced by a permutation argument over wire values, realized as a grand-product accumulation. This yields a universal, updatable structured reference string and 7.5–20$\times$ fewer prover group exponentiations than the fully-succinct mode of Sonic [1], PLONK's predecessor.
2. **The KZG polynomial commitment scheme** [7]. KZG compresses a polynomial $f$ of degree $<d$ into a single group element $\mathrm{Com}(f) = [f(s)]_1$ using an SRS $\{[s^i]_1\}_{i<d}$, and supports opening $f$ at any point with a constant-size proof verifiable by one pairing equation. It is PLONK's commitment backend and the reason PLONK proofs are constant-size.
3. **FRI** [8], the hash-based alternative. The Fast Reed–Solomon IOPP proves proximity of a committed function to a Reed–Solomon codeword by recursively folding even and odd parts, trading KZG's constant-size commitments for transparent setup (no toxic waste) and plausibly post-quantum security, at the cost of logarithmic proof size. FRI is the commitment machinery of STARKs [4] and transparent PLONK variants such as FRI-PLONK.

Our contribution is a unified, notation-consistent treatment of all three, with the precise protocol equations, soundness sketches, complexity accounting, and deployment history typically scattered across papers and folklore.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Finite fields, subgroups, and polynomial bases

Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a prime field with a large smooth multiplicative subgroup $H = \{1, \omega, \dots, \omega^{n-1}\}$, $n$ a power of two, $\omega$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity. The vanishing polynomial $Z_H(X) = X^n - 1$ vanishes exactly on $H$: a polynomial identity $g(X)$ holds on all of $H$ iff $Z_H$ divides $g$, i.e. $g(X) = t(X)\, Z_H(X)$ for a quotient $t$.

Two bases for $\mathbb{F}_{<n}[X]$ matter. The *monomial basis* $\{1, X, \dots, X^{n-1}\}$ is natural for commitments. The *Lagrange basis* $\{L_i\}_{i<n}$, with $L_i(\omega^j) = \delta_{ij}$, is natural for arithmetization, because a circuit's wire values are precisely evaluations at roots of unity. PLONK's slogan — "evaluations on a subgroup rather than coefficients of monomials" — reflects shifting the permutation argument of Bayer and Groth [9] from monomial coefficients (as in Sonic) into the evaluation domain, where gate and permutation constraints both admit clean divisibility formulations [1].

### 2.2 Polynomial commitment schemes: the abstraction

A polynomial commitment scheme for degree bound $d$ provides $(\mathrm{Setup}, \mathrm{Commit}, \mathrm{Open}, \mathrm{Verify})$: $\mathrm{Setup}(1^\lambda, d)$ outputs public parameters (possibly with trapdoor $\tau$); $\mathrm{Commit}$ binds the prover to $f \in \mathbb{F}_{<d}[X]$ with a short $C$; $\mathrm{Open}$ produces $y = f(z)$ with proof $\pi$; $\mathrm{Verify}$ accepts only consistent openings. Security notions are *binding*, *extractability* (knowledge soundness), and optionally *hiding*. KZG realizes all three under pairing-friendly groups; FRI realizes the functional equivalent with only a hash function, using Merkle commitments to Reed–Solomon codewords plus proximity proofs instead of algebraic openings [4][8].

### 2.3 Interactive oracle proofs and Fiat–Shamir

A polynomial IOP is a protocol in which the prover sends oracles to polynomials and the verifier queries them at random points. PLONK's arithmetization is a poly-IOP in this sense: the verifier never sees the polynomials, only commitments and spot evaluations. The Fiat–Shamir transform replaces the verifier's coins with hashes of the transcript, yielding a non-interactive argument in the random oracle model. All soundness claims below are stated for the interactive protocol and lifted via Fiat–Shamir.

## 3. Methodology

We proceed by **(i) formal reconstruction:** restating the PLONK protocol from [1] in consistent notation, including zero-knowledge randomization, quotient splitting, and multi-polynomial batch opening; **(ii) security reduction:** identifying the exact assumption per module — $q$-SDH for KZG, the algebraic group model plus random oracle for PLONK's knowledge soundness, and the proximity-gap analysis of [10] for FRI; and **(iii) quantitative comparison:** tabulating prover, verifier, and proof-size asymptotics anchored to reported implementation figures.

### 3.1 The PLONK constraint system

Fix a circuit with $n$ gates (pad $n$ to a power of two) and domain $H = \langle \omega \rangle$, $|H| = n$. Each gate $i$ has left, right, and output wires carrying $a_i, b_i, c_i$. The prover interpolates $a, b, c \in \mathbb{F}_{<n}[X]$ with $a(\omega^i) = a_i$, etc. Gate behavior is selected by public *selector polynomials* $q_L, q_R, q_O, q_M, q_C$ of degree $< n$:

$$q_L(\omega^i)\, a_i + q_R(\omega^i)\, b_i + q_O(\omega^i)\, c_i + q_M(\omega^i)\, a_i b_i + q_C(\omega^i) = 0 \qquad \forall i.$$

Multiplication gates set $q_M = 1, q_O = -1$; addition gates set $q_L = q_R = 1, q_O = -1$; booleanity checks use $q_M$ with $a = b$. Define the *gate constraint polynomial*

$$F(X) = q_L(X) a(X) + q_R(X) b(X) + q_O(X) c(X) + q_M(X) a(X) b(X) + q_C(X),$$

satisfying $F|_H \equiv 0$ iff every gate is satisfied.

### 3.2 The permutation argument

Copy constraints — e.g., the output of gate $i$ feeding the left input of gate $j$ — are encoded as a permutation $\sigma$ on $S = \{1, 2, 3\} \times H$ (the three wires of every gate): $\sigma$ maps each wire position to the next position that must carry the same value, so requiring $w = w \circ \sigma$ on the concatenated wire assignment $w$ enforces every copy constraint at once. Following Bayer–Groth [9], the prover demonstrates this with a *grand product*: for random challenges $\beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{F}$, define the running product polynomial $z$ by

$$z(1) = 1, \qquad z(\omega^{i+1}) = z(\omega^i) \cdot \frac{(a_i + \beta\,\omega^i + \gamma)(b_i + \beta\,k_1\omega^i + \gamma)(c_i + \beta\,k_2\omega^i + \gamma)}{(a_{\sigma_L(i)} + \beta\,\sigma_L(i) + \gamma)(b_{\sigma_R(i)} + \beta\,\sigma_R(i) + \gamma)(c_{\sigma_O(i)} + \beta\,\sigma_O(i) + \gamma)},$$

where $k_1, k_2 \in \mathbb{F} \setminus H$ distinguish the three wire domains, and $\sigma$ is encoded by permutation polynomials $S_{\sigma 1}, S_{\sigma 2}, S_{\sigma 3}$. The product of numerators equals the product of denominators iff $w$ is constant on $\sigma$-orbits — i.e., all copy constraints hold — which the verifier checks via the *permutation identity*

$$G(X) = z(X)\big[(a(X)+\beta X+\gamma)(b(X)+\beta k_1 X+\gamma)(c(X)+\beta k_2 X+\gamma)\big] - z(\omega X)\big[(a(X)+\beta S_{\sigma1}(X)+\gamma)\cdots\big] \equiv 0 \text{ on } H,$$

plus $z(1) = 1$, enforced by $(z(X) - 1)\, L_1(X)$ where $L_1$ is the Lagrange polynomial at $X = 1$.

> **Theorem: Permutation soundness.** If $z$ satisfies the recurrence with $z(1) = 1$ and $z(\omega^n) = 1$, the multisets $\{w(s)\}$ and $\{w(\sigma(s))\}$ are equal; for random $\beta, \gamma$ this implies $w = w \circ \sigma$ except with probability $\le O(n/|\mathbb{F}|)$. *Proof sketch.* Equality of the telescoping products forces equality of the two multisets of linear forms; by Schwartz–Zippel, distinct assignments collide in random linear forms with negligible probability [1][9].

### 3.3 The five-round protocol

With challenge $\alpha$ the prover forms the *quotient polynomial*

$$t(X) = \frac{F(X) + \alpha\, G(X) + \alpha^2 (z(X) - 1) L_1(X)}{Z_H(X)},$$

which is a polynomial iff all constraints hold on $H$. Since $\deg t$ can reach $\approx 3n$, it is split as $t = t_{lo} + X^n t_{mid} + X^{2n} t_{hi}$ so each piece fits the SRS degree bound. The rounds are:

1. **Round 1** — Prover sends $[a]_1, [b]_1, [c]_1$, with blinding terms $b_1 X, b_2 X^{n+1}$ (etc.) for zero knowledge.
2. **Round 2** — Verifier sends $\beta, \gamma$; prover sends $[z]_1$ (grand product, blinded).
3. **Round 3** — Verifier sends $\alpha$; prover sends $[t_{lo}]_1, [t_{mid}]_1, [t_{hi}]_1$.
4. **Round 4** — Verifier sends $\mathfrak{z}$; prover computes openings $a(\mathfrak{z}), b(\mathfrak{z}), c(\mathfrak{z}), S_{\sigma*}(\mathfrak{z}), t(\mathfrak{z}), z(\omega\mathfrak{z})$ and forms the *linearization polynomial* $r(X)$ — the numerator of $t$ with all terms not involving $z(X)$ replaced by their evaluations at $\mathfrak{z}$ — sending $[r]_1$ and $r(\mathfrak{z})$.
5. **Round 5** — Batch opening: all committed polynomials are proved open at $\mathfrak{z}$ (and $z$ at $\omega\mathfrak{z}$) with a *single* aggregated KZG proof, folding polynomials together under a challenge $v$.

The verifier checks a constant number of pairing equations; with the batching of [1], verification costs essentially **two pairings** plus $O(n)$ field work on public selectors (precomputable in preprocessing).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 KZG commitments: construction and the pairing check

Let $\mathbb{G}_1, \mathbb{G}_2, \mathbb{G}_T$ be prime-order groups with non-degenerate $e: \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \to \mathbb{G}_T$. $\mathrm{Setup}$ samples $s \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{F}$ and publishes

$$\mathrm{srs} = \big([1]_1, [s]_1, \dots, [s^{d-1}]_1,\; [1]_2, [s]_2\big),$$

discarding $s$ (the "toxic waste"). Committing to $f$ of degree $< d$ is one multi-scalar multiplication: $[f]_1 := [f(s)]_1$, a *single group element* regardless of $d$. To open at $z$ with $y = f(z)$, the prover sends the witness $[w]_1$ for $w(X) = \frac{f(X) - y}{X - z}$; the verifier checks

$$e\big([f]_1 - [y]_1,\; [1]_2\big) \stackrel{?}{=} e\big([w]_1,\; [s]_2 - [z]_2\big).$$

In Rust-like pseudocode, the verifier's core is:

```rust
// KZG opening check: e(C - [y]G1, G2) == e(W, [s]G2 - [z]G2)
fn kzg_verify(c: G1, y: Fr, w: G1, z: Fr, srs_g2: G2) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(c - G1::generator() * y, G2::generator());
    let rhs = pairing(w, srs_g2 - G2::generator() * z);
    lhs == rhs
}
```

> **Theorem: KZG correctness and extractability.** The check passes for honest openings by bilinearity: $e([f(s)-y]_1,[1]_2) = e([(f(s)-y)/(s-z)]_1, [s-z]_2)$. Binding and extractability reduce to $q$-Strong Diffie–Hellman: two valid openings $(y,[w]_1) \ne (y',[w']_1)$ for one commitment yield a $q$-SDH solution [7].

KZG's three decisive properties for PLONK: **constant-size** commitments and proofs, **homomorphism** ($[f+g]_1 = [f]_1 + [g]_1$, enabling quotient-splitting and linearization), and **batchability** (many polynomials opened at one point with one aggregated proof, as in PLONK's round 5; further improved by SHPLONK [11] and fflonk [12]).

### 4.2 FRI: folding Reed–Solomon codewords

FRI [8] proves proximity of a function $f_0: L_0 \to \mathbb{F}$ ($L_0$ a coset, $|L_0| = N = \rho^{-1} n$) to a Reed–Solomon codeword of degree $< n$ using only hashing — no trusted setup. The core is the *folding* operator. Writing $f(X) = f_{\mathrm{even}}(X^2) + X\, f_{\mathrm{odd}}(X^2)$,

$$\mathrm{Fold}[f, \alpha](Y) := f_{\mathrm{even}}(Y) + \alpha\, f_{\mathrm{odd}}(Y), \qquad Y = X^2.$$

If $\deg f < n$ then $\deg \mathrm{Fold}[f,\alpha] < n/2$, while a function far from the code stays far from the folded code with high probability over $\alpha$ (the *distance preservation* lemma). In the **commit phase**, the verifier sends $\alpha_i$ and the prover Merkle-commits to $f_{i+1} = \mathrm{Fold}[f_i, \alpha_i]$ on the halved domain $L_{i+1} = \{x^2 : x \in L_i\}$; after $\log n$ rounds it sends the constant $f_r$. In the **query phase**, the verifier samples $x_0 \in L_0$ and checks folding consistency $f_{i+1}(x_i^2) \stackrel{?}{=} \mathrm{Fold}[f_i, \alpha_i](x_i^2)$ with two queries per layer plus Merkle paths. The soundness analysis was tightened by the *proximity gaps* framework [10]: for security level $\lambda$, the query repetitions $m$ satisfy $m \cdot \log(1/\delta) \approx \lambda$ for distance parameter $\delta$.

FRI's complexity: prover $< 8N$ field operations after encoding (encoding is $O(N \log N)$ via FFT), verifier $< 2m \log n$ hashes, proof size $O(m \log n \cdot \lambda_{\mathrm{hash}})$ — logarithmic, not constant. This is the price of transparency: FRI-based SNARKs (STARKs [4], Plonky2's FRI backend) have proofs of tens to hundreds of kilobytes where KZG-PLONK proofs are under a kilobyte, but need no ceremony and rest on hashing alone [8][10].

### 4.3 Arithmetization zoo: PLONK vs. R1CS vs. AIR vs. CCS

| System | Constraint shape | Copy constraints | Custom gates | Commitment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1CS (Groth16 [5]) | $\langle a_i, w\rangle \langle b_i, w\rangle = \langle c_i, w\rangle$ | implicit (shared variables) | no | KZG-like (per-circuit SRS) |
| PLONK [1] | $q_L a + q_R b + q_O c + q_M ab + q_C = 0$ | permutation argument | yes (selectors) | KZG (universal SRS) |
| AIR (STARKs [4]) | transition constraints on traces | boundary constraints | yes (transition polys) | FRI |
| CCS (Spartan/Jolt lineage) | $\sum_i c_i \bigodot_j M_j z = 0$ | implicit | via matrices | Hyrax/IPA |

PLONK's decisive advantage over R1CS is *gate flexibility*: lookup-optimized gates, elliptic-curve addition gates, or Poseidon rounds are each expressed by choosing selector values, where R1CS must decompose everything into rank-1 multiplications. The permutation argument decouples *wiring* from *gates*. Extensions deepen the idea: **plookup** [2] proves wire values lie in a public table (compressing range checks and non-native arithmetic), and **PlonKup** [3] integrates lookups into PLONK's quotient polynomial with modest overhead.

### 4.4 The Fiat–Shamir boundary and what the SRS really costs

PLONK's universal SRS — $\{[s^i]_1\}_{i < d_{\max}}$ usable for *any* circuit up to size $d_{\max}$ — was a deployment breakthrough: one ceremony (e.g., the perpetual "powers of tau" [14]) serves all circuits, and the SRS is *updatable* (any party can re-randomize $s$, so soundness needs only one honest contributor). But "universal" is not "transparent": the trapdoor $s$ must never leak, and ceremony security is a social-technical assumption, not a mathematical one. This is the fault line FRI exploits. The modern resolution is *pluralism*: KZG-PLONK where proof size and verifier cost dominate (L1 verification), FRI-STARK where transparency and prover scalability dominate, and hybrid recursion (e.g., FRI recursion wrapped by a final KZG proof) inheriting both [4][8][12].

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

### 5.1 Asymptotic complexity

| Metric | PLONK+KZG [1] | Groth16 [5] | STARK+FRI [4] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prover | $O(n \log n)$ field + $O(n)$ $\mathbb{G}_1$ exp | $O(n \log n)$ + $O(n)$ exp | $O(n \log^2 n)$ hashes |
| Proof size | $O(1)$ ($\approx$ 7 $\mathbb{G}_1$ + few field) | $O(1)$ (3 group elements) | $O(\log^2 n)$ ($\sim$50–200 KB) |
| Verifier | $O(1)$ pairings + $O(n_{\mathrm{pub}})$ field | $O(1)$ pairings | $O(\log^2 n)$ hashes |
| Setup | universal updatable SRS | per-circuit trusted setup | transparent |
| Assumption | $q$-SDH + AGM + ROM | generic group + ROM | CRHF (+ proximity gaps [10]) |

### 5.2 Concrete figures from the literature and deployments

- PLONK's evaluation reports prover time roughly **7.5–20$\times$ lower** in group exponentiations than the fully-succinct mode of Sonic [1], with proofs of a few hundred bytes and millisecond verification.
- The "powers of tau" ceremony [14] produced SRS parameters supporting circuits up to $2^{28}$ constraints, reused across Aztec, zkSync, Polygon zkEVM, and Scroll — one artifact amortized over enormous secured value.
- STARK proofs for 1M-step computations are routinely $\sim$100–500 KB with provers running in seconds on commodity hardware (RISC Zero, StarkWare's Stone), demonstrating FRI's prover scalability; the logarithmic verifier makes on-chain FRI verification gas-heavy, motivating recursion into KZG wrappers.
- **fflonk** [12] reduces the PLONK verifier to 6 scalar multiplications and 2 pairings at roughly tripled prover time — a tradeoff explicitly targeting Ethereum gas costs.

### 5.3 Security theorems, consolidated

> **Theorem: PLONK knowledge soundness.** Under $q$-SDH in the algebraic group model, with Fiat–Shamir in the random oracle model, PLONK is a knowledge-sound argument for circuit satisfiability: any accepting proof yields, via an extractor rewinding the random oracle, a witness satisfying all gates and copy constraints [1].

> **Theorem: FRI soundness (proximity gaps).** If $\Delta(f_0, \mathrm{RS}[L_0, n]) > \delta$, FRI with $m$ query repetitions accepts with probability at most $\approx \big(1 - \min(\delta, 1-\rho)\big)^m$ plus commit-phase error $O(n \log n / |\mathbb{F}|)$; FRI-DEEP sharpens this for the low-degree regime used in STARKs [8][10].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**The SRS trust assumption.** KZG's binding rests on $s$ remaining secret. Updatable ceremonies [14] mitigate but do not eliminate this: compromise of *all* participants breaks soundness retroactively. Ceremony security under realistic partial-compromise models remains open — the strongest argument for FRI-based systems in high-assurance settings.

**Pairing-friendly vs. SNARK-friendly fields.** KZG needs pairing-friendly curves (BLS12-381, BN254) whose scalar fields are awkward for arithmetization. FRI works over any field with smooth subgroups (e.g., Goldilocks $2^{64} - 2^{32} + 1$), enabling far faster provers. A single field that is simultaneously pairing-friendly, FFT-friendly, and circuit-friendly is unknown; curve cycles (Pasta) are an active engineering response.

**Verifier asymmetry of FRI.** $O(\log^2 n)$ proof size and verifier work make FRI unsuitable for direct L1 verification at scale; current practice wraps FRI proofs in recursion. Reducing query complexity — STIR, WHIR, BaseFold are recent steps — is the most active theoretical front [10].

**Lookup integration costs.** plookup [2] and successors (Caulk, flookup [13]) add prover work linear in table size or need preprocessing; sublinear-in-table lookups with small concrete constants remain open, with flookup's quasilinear prover the current benchmark.

**Post-quantum posture.** KZG/PLONK fall to Shor's algorithm; FRI-based systems are plausibly post-quantum but their concrete analysis is less mature. A post-quantum *succinct* argument with KZG-like proof sizes does not exist.

**Prover memory and parallelism.** Both stacks are FFT/MSM-bound; distributed proving of $n = 2^{28}$ circuits introduces coordination and soundness questions the single-prover analyses do not cover.

---

## 7. Conclusion

PLONK, KZG, and FRI are complementary answers to three questions: *how to express computation as polynomial relations* (PLONK's selector-and-permutation arithmetization), *how to commit to polynomials succinctly* (KZG's constant-size pairing-based commitments), and *how to do so transparently* (FRI's hash-based proximity proofs). The modern SNARK stack is precisely this decomposition — arithmetization, permutation argument, polynomial commitment, Fiat–Shamir — and its power comes from the interfaces: swap KZG for FRI and a PLONK-style poly-IOP becomes a STARK; swap R1CS for PLONK's constraint system and the prover becomes universal. The record favors KZG where proof size and verification dominate and FRI where transparency and prover scalability dominate, with recursion increasingly used to have both. The open problems — ceremony-free succinctness with constant-size proofs, post-quantum succinct arguments, sublinear lookups, field/curve co-design — will define the next stack. What is unlikely to change is the architecture itself: polynomials, permutations, commitments, and Fiat–Shamir have become the instruction set of verifiable computation.
