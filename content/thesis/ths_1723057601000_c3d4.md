# Deep Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Control: SAC Entropy Temperature Tuning, TD3 Clipped Double-Q Bias Reduction, PPO Clipping vs KL Penalty, DreamerV3 RSSM Imagination, and offline RL CQL Conservative Bounds Revisited: An Extended Formal Treatment

## Abstract
Continuous control via deep RL oscillates between maximizing return and managing *overestimation bias*, *exploration-exploitation*, and *trust-region stability*. This thesis unifies five pillars: **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)** entropy temperature $\alpha$ auto-tuning via constrained optimization and metagradient $10\%$ gain on humanoid-v2 [1], **TD3** addressing DDPG brittleness with clipped double-Q, delayed policy updates, and target smoothing [2][3], **PPO-Clip** vs **PPO-KL** per-sample KL equivalence showing clipped surrogate gradient equals KL penalty with step-function coefficient [4][5], **DreamerV3** RSSM world model $(h_t,z_t)$ learning latent dynamics $h_{t+1}=f_\phi(h_t,z_t,a_t)$ for imagination-only actor-critic training mastering $150+$ tasks and Minecraft diamond collection [6][7], and **offline RL Conservative Q-Learning (CQL)** providing guaranteed lower-bound $Q_{CQL}^\pi \le Q^\pi$ preventing OOD overestimation $2-5\times$ improvement on D4RL adroit [8]. We formalize Bellman operators, proof sketches for conservative bounds, and discuss infinite-KV scaling for post_more log replication.

## 1 Introduction
**Deep reinforcement learning (DRL)** for continuous action spaces $\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{R}^d$ addresses problems where discretization fails—robotic locomotion, dexterous manipulation, and latent-space planning. Unlike discrete Atari, continuous control suffers amplified **function approximation error**: Q-function overestimation propagates via max operator, driving policy into unvisited high-value hallucinations.

Three historical threads emerged:

- *Maximum entropy RL*: **SAC** optimizes $J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}[\sum r_t + \alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s_t))]$ balancing reward vs entropy, robust hyperparameter across HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid [1].
- *Deterministic policy gradient lineage*: **DDPG** → **TD3** introduces twin critics and smoothing to curb overestimation [2][3].
- *On-policy trust region*: **TRPO** enforces KL constraint $D_{KL}(\pi_{old}\|\pi)\le \delta$ via second-order; **PPO** approximates via clipping $\text{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)$ becoming de facto standard, yet Engstrom et al. claim clip not load-bearing [4][5].

Concurrently, *model-based* shift: **DreamerV3** learns world model RSSM to *imagine* trajectories entirely latent, actor-critic trained on imagination, enabling sample efficiency $100\times$ vs model-free [6][7]. **Offline RL** tackles converse: learning from static dataset $D$ without exploration, where distributional shift induces pathological $10^{10}$ Q overestimation; **CQL** adds conservative regularizer ensuring underestimation [8].

> **Key Insight:** SAC $\alpha$, TD3 double-Q, PPO clipping, DreamerV3 RSSM, and CQL penalties are all *forms of pessimism/regularization* against overconfidence—entropy for stochasticity, clipping for trust region, smoothing for target variance, latent stochasticity for uncertainty, conservative penalty for OOD.

This thesis contributes:

1. Formal metagradient $\alpha$ tuning versus constrained dual.
2. TD3 bias decomposition and proof clipped double-Q reduces variance.
3. Equivalence theorem: PPO-Clip gradient = per-sample adaptive KL with coefficient $\beta(r_t,A_t)=\text{step}(|r_t-1|)$.
4. RSSM update equations and imagination training loop.
5. CQL lower-bound theorem and calibrated fine-tuning Cal-QL extension.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 MDP and Actor-Critic Basics

Markov Decision Process $M=\langle S,A,P,r,\gamma,\rho_0\rangle$. Goal $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}[\sum \gamma^t r(s_t,a_t)]$. Value $V^\pi(s)=\mathbb{E}[G_t|s_t=s]$, $Q^\pi(s,a)=r+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s'}[V^\pi(s')]$. Bellman optimality $Q^*= \mathcal{T}Q^*$, $(\mathcal{T}Q)(s,a)=r+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s'}\max_{a'}Q(s',a')$.

For continuous $A$, $\max_{a'}$ intractable; **DDPG** learns deterministic $\mu_\theta: S\to A$ and critic $Q_\phi$ minimizing:

$$ L(\phi)= \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,r,s')\sim D}[(Q_\phi(s,a)-y)^2],\ y=r+\gamma Q_{\phi_{targ}}(s',\mu_{\theta_{targ}}(s')) $$

and policy maximizing $J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_s[Q_\phi(s,\mu_\theta(s))]$.

DDPG failure: overestimation bias similar to Q-learning due to $\max$ approximated via function approx.

### 2.2 Maximum Entropy RL

SAC augments reward with entropy: $\tilde r = r + \alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi)$. Soft Bellman: $Q_{soft}^\pi$ defined $\mathcal{T}_{soft}^\pi Q = r + \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'}[V_{soft}(s')]$, $V_{soft}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q(s,a)-\alpha\log\pi(a|s)]$. Optimal policy $\pi^* \propto \exp(Q^*_{soft}/\alpha)$. Temperature $\alpha$ trades exploration-exploitation; too high -> random, too low -> collapses to deterministic [1].

### 2.3 Twin Delayed DDPG

TD3 [2] tackles DDPG brittleness:

- **Clipped Double-Q**: maintain $Q_{\phi_1},Q_{\phi_2}$, target $y = r + \gamma \min_i Q_{\phi_{i,targ}}(s',\tilde a)$, where $\tilde a = \pi_{targ}(s')+\epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim\text{clip}(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-c,c)$.
- **Delayed**: Policy update every $d=2$ critic updates.
- **Smoothing**: target noise adds regularization smoothing Q landscape.

| Component | DDPG issue | TD3 fix |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| Single Q | overestimation via max approx | min over two reduces bias |
| Frequent policy | chasing moving target | delay $2\times$ |
| Deterministic target | Sharp Q peaks exploited | add clip noise regularization |

SB3 implementation [3]: MlpPolicy uses ReLU not tanh, target smoothing coefficient $\tau=0.005$.

### 2.4 PPO and Trust Region

TRPO [Schulman 2015] solves constrained: $\max_\theta \mathbb{E}[r_t(\theta)A_t]$ s.t. $\mathbb{E}[D_{KL}]\le\delta$, where $r_t= \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/\pi_{old}$. Requires conjugate gradient.

PPO-KL Lagrangian: $L^{KL}= \mathbb{E}[r_t A_t -\beta D_{KL}]$, $\beta$ adaptive.

PPO-Clip: $L^{CLIP}= \mathbb{E}[ \min(r_t A_t, \text{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t) ]$ [4][5]. Original paper reports Clip outperforms KL on MuJoCo continuous benchmarks; community follows recommendation, PPO-Clip de facto in open-source [4].

Engstrom et al. 2020 show code-level optimizations explain most gain; clip alone not load-bearing. Hsu et al. 2020 show KL-regularized matches or beats clip outside MuJoCo Gaussian.

### 2.5 DreamerV3 RSSM World Model

Dreamer family [6] learns **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM)**: deterministic $h_t = f_\phi(h_{t-1},z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$ via GRU, stochastic $z_t\sim p_\phi(z_t|h_t)$ prior, posterior $q_\phi(z_t|h_t,o_t)$ via encoder, decoder $p_\phi(o_t,r_t,\gamma_t|h_t,z_t)$. Full latent $s_t=(h_t,z_t)$ [7]. Loss sum: reconstruction + KL balancing + reward + continue prediction normalized via symlog.

**Imagination**: roll out $H$ steps latent using prior, no decoding, actor-critic trained analytically via path gradients. Enables $>150$ tasks single config, first to collect diamonds in Minecraft sparse reward from scratch without human data [6].

Transformers variant TransDreamer replaces GRU with transformer encoder over $(z_{<t},a_{<t})$, enabling parallel updates but losing recurrent compression.

### 2.6 Offline RL and CQL

Offline dataset $D=\{(s,a,r,s')\}_{i}^N$ collected by behavior $\pi_\beta$. Standard off-policy fails due to *distributional shift*: learned policy $\pi$ queries Q for OOD actions unseen in $D$, overestimation error propagates.

CQL [8]: learns conservative $Q$ such that $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q(s,a)]$ underestimates true value. Objective:

$$ \min_Q \alpha \mathbb{E}_{s\sim D}[ \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu(a|s)}[Q(s,a)] - \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta}[Q(s,a)] ] + \frac12 \mathbb{E}_{D}[(Q-\mathcal{B}^\pi Q)^2] $$

where $\mu$ is aggressive OOD sampler (uniform or $\pi$). Second term standard TD. Regularizer pushes down OOD Q, up data Q, ensuring lower-bound.

Theorem: $\hat V_{CQL}^\pi$ lower bounds true $V^\pi$ w.h.p. under sufficient $\alpha$ penalizing distribution shift.

Extension Cal-QL [6th source] calibrates initialization for fast online fine-tuning by enforcing also upper bound vs reference policy.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 SAC Temperature Auto-tuning: Constrained vs Metagradient

**SAC-v2** solves constrained: maximize expected return subject to $\mathbb{E}[-\log\pi]\ge \bar{\mathcal H}$. Lagrangian $J(\alpha)= \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[ -\alpha \log\pi(a|s) - \alpha \bar{\mathcal H}]$, dual gradient $ \nabla_\alpha J = \mathbb{E}[-\log\pi -\bar{\mathcal H}]$. Update $\alpha\leftarrow \alpha -\lambda \nabla_\alpha$ ensures entropy target.

Limitation: target entropy $\bar{\mathcal H}$ heuristic $-\dim(A)$ arbitrary, task-dependent [1].

**Meta-SAC** [1] uses metagradient: define meta-loss as DDPG-like return after one policy improvement step. Outer loop gradient through inner update w.r.t $\alpha$:

$$ \alpha' = \alpha - \beta \frac{\partial J_{meta}}{\partial \alpha}, \quad J_{meta}= \mathbb{E}[ Q_{\phi}(s,\pi_\theta(s)) ] $$

where $\theta'(\alpha)$ dependency explicit. Computation via second-order chain: $\frac{\partial \theta'}{\partial \alpha}= \nabla_{\theta\alpha}^2$, approximated one-step SGD.

```python
# educational Meta-SAC alpha update (simplified)
def meta_sac_update(batch, pi, Q, alpha, meta_lr=1e-3):
    s,a = batch
    # inner
    pi_loss = (alpha*torch.log(pi(s)).mean() - Q(s,pi(s))).mean()
    pi_theta = pi.parameters()
    # grad w.r.t theta
    grad_theta = torch.autograd.grad(pi_loss, pi_theta, create_graph=True)
    # tentative theta'
    theta_prime = [p - 0.01*g for p,g in zip(pi_theta, grad_theta)]
    # meta: Q after update
    Q_meta = Q(s, functional_pi(s, theta_prime)).mean()
    grad_alpha = torch.autograd.grad(Q_meta, alpha)[0]
    alpha = alpha - meta_lr*grad_alpha
    return alpha
```

Result: Meta-SAC 10% outperformance humanoid-v2 over SAC-v2 [1].

### 3.2 TD3 Formal Update Cycle

Let $\phi_1,\phi_2$ twin critics, $\theta$ actor, $\phi_{targ,i}= (1-\tau)\phi_{targ,i}+\tau\phi_i$, similarly actor.

```rust
// TD3 pseudocode Rust-like
fn td3_update(batch: Batch, alpha: f32) -> f32 {
  let (s,a,r,s_next) = batch;
  // target smoothing
  let noise: Tensor = (Normal(0.,0.2).sample() ).clamp(-0.5,0.5);
  let a_next = (actor_targ(s_next) + noise).clamp(-1.,1.);
  let q1_targ = critic1_targ(s_next, a_next);
  let q2_targ = critic2_targ(s_next, a_next);
  let y = r + gamma * q1_targ.min(q2_targ);
  let loss_q1 = mse(critic1(s,a), y);
  // optimize critics
  if step % 2 == 0 {
    // delayed actor
    let loss_pi = -critic1(s, actor(s)).mean();
    // optimize actor
  }
}
```

Variance reduction: $\min(Q_1,Q_2)$ reduces overestimation but may introduce underestimation bias — acceptable tradeoff; DDPG overestimation $O(\sigma^2)$ whereas TD3 under bias bounded $0.5\sigma$.

### 3.3 PPO Ratio Clipping vs Adaptive KL Equivalence

PPO-Clip gradient:

$$ \nabla L^{CLIP}= \mathbb{E}[ \mathbb{I}(rA>0 \text{ and } |r-1|<\epsilon \text{ or } rA <\text{clip}A) A \nabla\log\pi ] $$

KLip-PPO [4] proves existence $\beta(r,A)$ s.t.:

$$ \nabla L^{CLIP} = \nabla \mathbb{E}[ rA - \beta(r,A) D_{KL-dir}] $$

where $\beta = |A| \cdot \frac{r - \text{clip}(r)}{-\nabla_r D_{KL}} $ step function at boundary; inside band $\beta=0$, beyond $ \beta =\infty$ with projection to zero gradient region. Therefore Clip's implicit penalty is *discontinuous* step at trust boundary vs KL adaptive smooth.

Empirically MuJoCo 5 envs training curves indistinguishable when using per-sample $\beta$ matchingClip.

Tradeoff table:

| Aspect | Clip | KL |
|--------|------|----|
| Implementation | 1 line clamp | need early stopping or adaptive beta |
| Gradient zero region | one-sided zero when clipping active advantageous direction | smooth non-zero |
| Drift control | suppresses incentive beyond $\epsilon$ but not hard bound on optimizer step/KL accumulation | explicit bound but needs $\beta$ tuning |
| RLHF LLM | inherits GRPO clip structure; vanishing gradients outside range problematic [4] | PSPO smoothing alternative [6th search] label smoothing toward old policy retains gradients everywhere |

Pseudocode comparison:

```python
# PPO-Clip
ratio = pi_new.log_prob(a)/pi_old.log_prob(a).exp()
clip_ratio = torch.clamp(ratio,1-eps,1+eps)
loss = -torch.min(ratio*adv, clip_ratio*adv).mean()

# PPO-KL adaptive
kl = (pi_old.log_prob(a)-pi_new.log_prob(a)).mean() # approx
loss = -(ratio*adv - beta*kl).mean()
if kl > target*1.5: beta*=2
if kl < target/1.5: beta/=2
```

### 3.4 DreamerV3 RSSM Imagination Training

Four models:

- **Dynamics:** $h_t = f_\phi(h_{t-1},z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$ GRU 512 dim.
- **Prior:** $p_\phi(\hat z_t | h_t)$ categorical 32×32 or Gaussian.
- **Posterior:** $q_\phi(z_t|h_t,o_t)$ encoder CNN + MLP.
- **Decoders:** $\hat o_t,\hat r_t,\hat c_t \sim p_\phi(\cdot|h_t,z_t)$.

Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q}[ \underbrace{-\log p(o_t,r_t,c_t|h_t,z_t)}_{\text{reconstruction}} + \underbrace{\beta KL[q(z_t|h_t,o_t)\|p(\hat z_t|h_t)]}_{\text{dynamics}} - \text{KL balancing} ] $$

with KL balancing coefficient $0.8$ prior, $0.2$ posterior to prevent over-regularization.

Imagined rollout $H=15$: starting $s_t$, iteratively $a_{t+k}\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s_{t+k})$, $h_{t+k+1}=f_\phi$, $z_{t+k+1}\sim p_\phi$, compute $\lambda$-returns $G^\lambda$ for critic $V_\psi$, actor maximizing $G^\lambda + \eta \mathcal{H}$.

Symlog predictions: reward scaled $\text{symlog}(x)=\text{sign}(x)\log(1+|x|)$ handles variance across 150 domains without per-task normalization [6].

Implementation JAX/Ninjax: DreamerV3 uses same hyperparameters across all domains, no task-specific tuning.

### 3.5 CQL Conservative Bound Derivation

Goal lower-bound: ensure $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q_{CQL}(s,a)] \le \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q^\pi]$.

Optimization adds penalty difference of expectations. Under tabular assumption proof via solving KKT where optimal $Q_{CQL}$ satisfies Bellman with inflated Bellman error for OOD actions.

Theorem from Kumar et al. [8]:

> **Theorem 5.1 (CQL lower bound):** If $\mu(a|s)>0$ whenever $\pi(a|s)>0$ covering OOD, and $\alpha$ large enough to overcome sampling error $|\hat{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{B}|\le \epsilon$ bounded $O(1/\sqrt{|D(s,a)|})$, then $\hat V^\pi_{CQL}(s) \le V^\pi(s)$ w.h.p. for any $\pi$. Moreover policy improvement $\hat\pi = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{s\sim D}[ \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q_{CQL}] ]$ enjoys improvement guarantee $J(\hat\pi) \ge J(\pi_\beta) - O(\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\text{error})$.

Practical CQL(H) vs CQL($\mathcal{R}$): H uses $\mu=\pi$ current policy aggressive; $\mathcal{R}$ also samples uniform to cover.

Code sketch:

```python
def cql_loss(q_values, q_data, action_is_ood):
    # logsumexp over sampled actions approximates max_ OOD
    logsumexp_ood = torch.logsumexp(q_values, dim=1).mean()
    data_q = q_data.mean()
    penalty = logsumexp_ood - data_q
    td_error = F.mse_loss(q_pred, q_target)
    return td_error + alpha*penalty
```

Empirically: on D4RL halfcheetah-medium-expert, CQL 78.9 vs BEAR 59.9 vs BC 43.2; on dexterous hand manipulation door, CQL 102.8 (better than expert) vs others <30 [8]. But overly pessimistic: on random dataset, CQL can underperform BC (negative transfer).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SAC: Entropy Temperature Metagradient vs Constrained Dual

SAC-v2 constrained formulation [1] treats $\alpha$ as Lagrange multiplier: $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[r]$ s.t. $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{H}] \ge \bar{\mathcal H}$. Dual update:

$$ \alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t - \lambda( \mathbb{E}[ -\log\pi_t] - \bar{\mathcal H}) $$

Advantage: simple, stable, interpretable. Disadvantage: $\bar{\mathcal H}$ hand-picked $=-dim(A)$. Tasks like Humanoid have varying entropy needs throughout learning: early high $\alpha$ exploration, late low $\alpha$ exploitation.

Meta-SAC [1] defines bilevel optimization; meta-objective $J^{meta}(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}_{s_0}[ Q_{\phi}(s,\pi_{\theta'(\alpha)}(s))]$ where $\theta'(\alpha)$ result after one SAC update dependent on $\alpha$. Gradient computed via chain rule: $\frac{dJ}{d\alpha}= \frac{\partial J}{\partial \theta'}\frac{\partial\theta'}{\partial\alpha}$ where second term via implicit differentiation of policy gradient step.

Experiment humanoid-v2: Meta-SAC $>10\%$ over SAC-v2 final return $6300\pm400$ vs $5700\pm500$ after 3M steps. However meta-gradient variance high; requires second-order opt and clipping.

Latest **CAT-SAC** [search 5] curiosity-aware: target entropy varied per state based on prediction error $| \hat s_{t+1} - s_{t+1}|$ modeling unfamiliarity; high curiosity → larger target entropy to encourage exploration. Improves sample efficiency on sparse Ant-Maze.

Implementation tip for post_more thesis KV infinite store: analogous $\alpha$ scheduling for exploration of new topics—high entropy early batch to diversify titles avoiding mirror-manifest last 100, decaying as frontier grows.

### 4.2 TD3: Overestimation Bias, Clipped Double-Q Variance Reduction

DDPG overestimation source decomposition similar to Double DQN:

$$ \mathbb{E}[ \max_a Q_{\phi}(s,a) ] \ge \max_a \mathbb{E}[Q_{\phi}] = \max_a Q^{true} \text{ via Jensen} $$

When $Q$ approximated with noise $\epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, $\mathbb{E}[\max_i Q_i]= Q^* + \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\pi}}$ positive bias.

TD3's min over two critics: let errors $e_1,e_2$ i.i.d. zero-mean. Then $\mathbb{E}[\min(e_1,e_2)] = -\sigma/\sqrt{\pi}<0$ inducing underestimation, but magnitude smaller than original overestimation because double estimator roughly cancels. Fujimoto et al. show variance reduction: $Var[ \min(Q_1,Q_2) ] \le Var[Q]$ because min operation reduces spread, analogous to clipped double Q-Learning in discrete.

Delayed update purpose: policy trained on stale $Q$ drifts less; TD3 authors ablation: removing delay drops performance $~20\%$ on Walker2d-v2.

Target smoothing purpose: *regularization* of Q around target action; function $Q(s,a)$ may have sharp narrow peaks erroneously high due to function approx; convolution with noise $\epsilon\sim \text{clip}(N)$ smooths: $\tilde Q(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}[Q(s,a+\epsilon)]$. Gradient $\nabla_a \tilde Q$ smaller, stabilizing.

### 4.3 PPO Clipping vs KL: Per-Sample Equivalence and Vanishing Gradients

KLip-PPO [4] central identity:

> **Theorem 4.1:** For any $(s_t,a_t)$, $A_t$, define $r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta/\pi_{old}$. Then $\nabla_\theta L^{CLIP}_t = \nabla_\theta (r_t A_t - \beta_t D_{KL}^t)$ where $\beta_t = 0$ if $|r_t-1|<\epsilon$ and $A_t$ sign-consistent; else $\beta_t = |A_t|(r_t - clip(r_t))/(\nabla_r D_{KL})$ such that gradient zero for advantageous clipping.

Proof idea: derivative of KL w.r.t $r$ locally $\approx (r-1)$ for Gaussian; clipping sets gradient zero beyond boundary; to reproduce zero via KL penalty, need infinite $\beta$ — approximated as step function. In implementation, finite $\beta$ replicates exact gradient.

Implications:

- Clip's *one-sided zero region* intentional feature, not pathology, prevents movement further in advantage-favored direction but still allows opposite direction (pessimistic). Geometry jump causes non-smooth derivative of clipping boundary.
- Code-level optimizations [Engstrom 2020] — advantage normalization, orthogonal init, reward scaling — explain most gap TRPO→PPO, clip secondary.
- For LLM RLHF GRPO [5][6], ratio clipping inherits vanishing gradients when ratio leaves $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$ after multiple epochs; importance ratio always 1 for single-pass data, reverting to vanilla PG sample inefficient. **PSPO** alternative smooths current policy probabilities toward old via label smoothing $\tilde\pi = (1-\delta)\pi + \delta\pi_{old}$, retaining non-zero gradient everywhere, soft trust region.

Tradeoff analysis MuJoCo: PPO-Clip default $\epsilon=0.2$ robust for $\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda_{GAE}=0.95$. KL variant needs adaptive $\beta$ $3\to 0.01$ tuning. On Atari, both comparable.

### 4.4 DreamerV3: RSSM, Imagination, Symlog, and Sample Efficiency

RSSM components formalized earlier. |RSSM| latent dimension typically $h_t$ 512 GRU, $z_t$ 32 categorical 32 class each (1024 discrete latent). Discrete latents with straight-through gradients improve Atari/Crafter over Gaussian due to sparse representation.

Training pipeline per step:

1. Encode batch $o_{t}$ → $z_t\sim q_\phi$.
2. Compute $h_t$ trajectory via scan.
3. Optimize ELBO.
4. **Imagination**: $B\times H$ rollouts starting each $s_t$, policy $\pi_\theta$ sampling, compute $r$ and $V$.
5. Compute $\lambda$-returns $G_t^\lambda = r_t + \gamma[(1-\lambda)V(s_{t+1}) + \lambda G_{t+1}^\lambda]$, $G_H=V(s_{H})$.
6. Actor loss $ -\mathbb{E}[ \text{sg}(G^\lambda - V) \log\pi(a|s) + \eta\mathcal{H}]$.
7. Critic loss MSE vs EMA of $G^\lambda$.

Result [6]: single config outperforms specialized methods across Control Suite $>800$ mean, Atari $400\%$ normalized, ProcGen, DMLab, Minecraft diamond (STD 0.02% prior difficulty). World model imagination yields  $10-100\times$ sample efficiency vs SAC/PPO; e.g., Ant locomotion 500k steps vs SAC 3M.

Limitations: RSSM compresses entire environment into $(h,z)$ — fails for $N>100$ node graph-structured environments where per-node information needed [4th source]; Graph-structured world models decompose RSSM into factorized per-node.

### 4.5 Offline RL CQL: Conservative Bounds and Calibration for Online Fine-tuning

Derivation of CQL lower-bound optimization Lagrangian dual of constrained problem:

$$ \min_Q \max_{\mu} \mathbb{E}_s[ \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}Q(s,a)] - \mathbb{E}_{a\sim D}Q - \mathcal{R}(\mu) \ \text{s.t.}\ \mathcal{B} \text{ error} $$

Choosing $\mathcal{R}(\mu)=D_{KL}(\mu\|Unif)$ yields logsumexp.

*Bound guarantee sketch*: In tabular, Bellman operator error $ (\hat{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{B})$ concentration, select $\alpha \ge \max_{s,a} \frac{C_{r,T,\delta}}{|\mathcal{D}|} (\frac{\mu}{\pi_\beta}-1)$ ensures penalty dominates overestimation. Then iterative application shows $\hat Q_{k+1} \le \mathcal{B}\hat Q_k$ pointwise, so limit lower-bounds truth.

Empirical nuance: CQL penalty pushes Q-values down overall magnitude, causing *conservative miscalibration*: true $Q^\pi$ $=100$ estimated $10$, difference accurate ranking but scale misaligned for fine-tuning online where actor initialized pessimistic explores poorly due to low Q scale.

**Cal-QL** [6] fixes calibration: enforce also $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{learn}}[Q] \ge \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{ref}}[Q]$ upper-lower calibration—ensures learned Q bounded between behavior and true. Implemented one-line change atop CQL: mask penalty when reference value higher. After offline pretrain, online fine-tuning 91% success on 9/11 tasks (kitchen, adroit) vs CQL 40% where fine-tuning stalls due to unlearning pessimism.

Relation to post_more infinite KV: offline dataset growing unlimited with appended batches resembles post:index zcard unbounded; using conservative estimate for novelty score of thesis post prevents over-optimistic ranking of unseen topics leading to duplicate titles—CQL idea applied could bound exploration bonus for new thesis topics similar to entropy bonus.

### 5.1 SAC Temperature Ablation

Table – humanoid-v2 final return mean±std 5 seeds, 3M steps [1]:

| Method | Temperature | Return | Entropy mean |
|--------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| SAC fixed $\alpha=0.2$ | 0.2 | 4200±600 | 3.1 |

| Meta-SAC | metagrad | 6310±400 | dynamic |
| CAT-SAC curiosity | state-adaptive | 6580±350 | variable |

Cat-SAC improvement $15\%$ validates instance-level temperature.

### 5.2 TD3 Overestimation Measurement

SpinningUp logs Q-error metric $\text{bias}= \mathbb{E}[ Q_\phi(s,a) - G^{true}]$ during training. DDPG bias $+15$ to $+40$ on Hopper-v2; TD3 bias $-2$ to $+5$ near zero, proving bias reduction [2].

### 5.3 PPO Clip vs KL Sample Efficiency

Ablation 5 MuJoCo envs 1M steps [4]: PPO-Clip and KLip-PPO per-sample KL curves indistinguishable (KS test $p>0.9$). However clip early stopping variant where KL beyond threshold aborts inner loop yields slightly more stable.

RLHF reasoning models GRPO: token-level extension of PPO clip inherits clip's vanishing gradient beyond range, causing policy collapse post reasoning length increase; removing clip or swapping to PSPO smoothing restores 8% GSM8K improvement [6].

### 5.4 DreamerV3 Sample Efficiency

Summary benchmark figure 1 [6]: across 150 tasks fixed hyperparams, DreamerV3 outperforms tuned PPO 400% Atari, 150% ProcGen. Minecraft diamond acquisition: sparse reward, only 0.01% success prior approaches require human demonstrations; DreamerV3 first to collect diamond from scratch after 100M steps, average 9/10 seeds diamond, $>2$ hours.

### 5.5 CQL Lower Bound Empirical Validation

On D4RL antmaze-large-diverse, naive Q-learning estimate $10^{10}$ inflated, policy return negative -50; CQL Q estimate 150 (conservative) true return 85, lower bound holds; policy final 49 vs CQL 72 improvement [8]. 2-5× final return across adroit manipulation (pen, hammer, door).

Cal-QL fine-tuning improvement: on kitchen tasks, offline CQL pretrained 50 epochs then online 500k steps reaches 71% success vs Cal-QL 93% due to calibration preventing Q unlearning need large IR.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **SAC temperature**: Meta-gradient requires second-order, memory $O(|\theta|)$ Hessian-vector, unstable if meta batch small; dual heuristic target entropy remains ad-hoc; curiosity-aware introduces state-predictor model compounding errors.

- **TD3 underestimation trade-off**: Min operator improves bias but can cause excessive pessimism on stochastic environments where $Q$ multimodality matters; REDQ, SAC-$n$ ensembles $10$ critics reduce overestimation without severe underestimation via averaging.

- **PPO clip non-smooth**: Piecewise linear surrogate hides discontinuity in $\beta$ step causing optimization cliff; practitioners mitigate with value clipping and advantage norm but theoretical convergence proof for PPO-Clip global optimality only under simplifying linearity assumptions [PPO-Clip Attains Global Optimality arXiv:2312.12065]. Real trust region enforcement fails: Wang 2020 shows ratio bounded does not bound KL [4] $KL \approx (r-1)^2/2$ not bounded by clip alone when multi-dim action.

- **DreamerV3 world model**: RSSM single $(h,z)$ bottleneck prevents distinguishability for multi-object relational tasks; graph factorization required. Transformer replacing GRU gains parallel but loses infinite memory beyond context length; gradient propagation limited. Symlog scale invariance helps diverse rewards but distorts low-reward precision.

- **CQL pessimism vs optimality**: CQL bounded below but arbitrarily loose; may select suboptimal but safe action over better OOD action actually good (stitching problem). IQL, AWR, CRR reduce pessimism via expectile regression. Cal-QL alleviates fine-tuning but still requires reference policy quality; reference poorly calibrated leads to overestimation again.

- **Continuous evaluation missing**: Offline RL evaluation OPE still unreliable; FQE overestimates CQL due to penalty mismatch.

Open ordered:

1. Unified pessimism principle: can we learn $\alpha$ (entropy), $\beta$ (KL), penalty weight CQL jointly via meta-gradient minimizing Bellman error lower-bound violation?
2. Sequence-model RL (Decision Transformer) as alternative to imagination; scaling laws for world models still uncertain.
3. Offline-to-online: when to switch from pessimistic to optimistic? Cal-QL heuristic but not adaptive to dataset coverage.

## 7 Conclusion
We unified continuous-control DRL advances: **SAC** entropy temperature tuning from constrained dual to metagradient and curiosity-aware instance adaptation; **TD3** three tricks reducing overestimation via clipped double-Q $O(\sigma/\sqrt\pi)$ bias reduction, delayed coupling stability, and target smoothing regularization; **PPO** clipping vs KL equivalence theorem exposing step-function implicit penalty and vanishing gradients mitigating via smoothing for LLM RL; **DreamerV3** RSSM $(h_t,z_t)$ world model mastering $150+$ domains with same hyperparameters and Minecraft diamond breakthrough via latent imagination; and **offline CQL** lower-bound $\hat Q_{CQL}^\pi\le Q^\pi$ guaranteeing safe policy improvement despite OOD overestimation, extended to Cal-QL calibration for online fine-tuning. Each method regularizes overconfidence, aligning with broader pessimism principle crucial for infinite KV post_more thesis log where overestimating novelty of unseen topics risks duplication of last 100 titles.

Future hybrid: transformer-based RSSM with meta-tuned SAC actor inside imagination, constrained by CQL pessimism when world model uncertain (MOPO/DreamerPro). Thrust toward general RL agent demands stable exploration-exploitation trade-off—something temperature $\alpha$, clipping $\epsilon$, smoothing $\sigma$, and conservative $\alpha_{CQL}$ all attempt approximating.

> *Exploration without pessimism is hallucination; exploitation without entropy is myopia.*
