# Robust Mechanism Design: Wilson's Doctrine, Ex Post Implementation, and the Maxmin Frontier

## Abstract

Classical mechanism design derives elegant, optimal institutions under exacting common-knowledge assumptions: the designer knows the prior over types, the agents know that the designer knows, and so on up an infinite hierarchy of beliefs. Robert Wilson's doctrine insists that useful theory must survive the repeated weakening of that common-knowledge base [1]. This thesis unifies the two great responses to Wilson's challenge. First, the *belief-robust* program of Bergemann and Morris, which moves analysis to the universal type space: in separable environments, interim implementation on *every* type space collapses to the belief-free notion of ex post implementation [2]. Second, the *maxmin* program of Carroll, which abandons probabilities over the unknown and evaluates mechanisms by worst-case guarantees — selecting linear contracts and separate selling as robustly optimal [5][6]. We reconstruct the core equivalence theorems with proofs, extend the analysis to robust virtual implementation [3][4], quantify the price of robustness with computational experiments, and map the frontier where robustness still fails — non-separable environments, interdependent values, and resale. The robustness premium is real, bounded, and usually worth paying.

##1 Intro

The classical program of mechanism design — from the revelation principle through Myerson's optimal auction — is a triumph of applied mathematics. Myerson (1981) characterized the revenue-maximizing auction under independent private values and a known distribution: allocate to the highest non-negative virtual valuation and charge incentive-compatible payments. The construction is exact, beautiful, and almost never used. As Roughgarden observes, the optimal auction "requires only modest distributional knowledge (the monopoly price)" in the symmetric case, but in general it demands precise knowledge of an exponential-sized joint distribution and can award the item to a low bidder over a high one — too inscrutable for real-world deployment [7]. This is the bite of Wilson's doctrine.

> **Wilson (1987):** "Game theory has a great advantage in explicitly analyzing the consequences of trading rules that presumably are really common knowledge; it is deficient to the extent it assumes other features to be common knowledge, such as one player's probability assessment about another's preferences or information. I foresee the progress of game theory as depending on successive reductions in the base of common knowledge required to conduct useful analyses of practical problems. Only by repeated weakening of common knowledge assumptions will the theory approximate reality." [1]

The literature's response to this critique has taken two distinct architectural forms, and this thesis is organized around their unification:

1. **Belief robustness (Bergemann–Morris).** Keep the Bayesian formalism but commit to no particular type space: a mechanism is *robust* if it implements the social choice function in interim equilibrium on *all* type spaces. In separable environments this demanding requirement collapses to the belief-free notion of *ex post implementation* [2].
2. **Maxmin robustness (Carroll).** Abandon probabilities over the unknown: the designer knows only a coarse description — expected outputs, marginal distributions — and evaluates each mechanism by worst-case performance over all consistent full environments. This conservative criterion selects strikingly simple institutions: linear contracts and separate selling [5][6].

These programs are complements: Bergemann–Morris robustness guards against *belief* misspecification inside a Bayesian model; Carroll robustness guards against *environment* misspecification outside any probabilistic model. Together they define the **maxmin frontier**: mechanisms whose guarantees survive both relaxations, and the boundary beyond which no Wilson-compatible guarantee exists.

**Roadmap.** Section 2 sets the baseline; Section 3 describes methods; Section 4 develops the four deep results; Section 5 gives proofs and simulations; Sections 6–7 state limitations and conclude.

##2 Background

### 2.1 The Bayesian baseline

A mechanism design problem is specified by a set of agents *N = {1, …, n}*, an outcome space *X*, and for each agent *i* a type space *Θ_i* of payoff-relevant private information, with *Θ = ×_i Θ_i*. Agent *i* of type *θ_i* has utility *u_i(x, θ)* over outcomes, quasi-linear in the leading applications: *u_i = v_i(a, θ) − t_i*, where *a* is an allocation and *t_i* a transfer. A *social choice function* *f : Θ → X* maps type profiles to outcomes; a mechanism *(M, g)* has message spaces *M_i* and outcome function *g : M → X*. The *revelation principle* reduces implementability to truthful direct mechanisms (*M_i = Θ_i*, *g = f*) satisfying incentive constraints.

In the interim (Bayesian) formulation, each type *θ_i* holds a belief *μ_i(· | θ_i)* over *θ_{−i}*, and truth-telling must maximize *interim* expected utility:

> **Bayesian incentive compatibility (BIC):** for all *i, θ_i, θ_i'*,
> *E_{θ_{−i} ~ μ_i(·|θ_i)}[u_i(f(θ_i, θ_{−i}), θ)] ≥ E_{θ_{−i} ~ μ_i(·|θ_i)}[u_i(f(θ_i', θ_{−i}), θ)]*.

Everything downstream — optimal auctions, efficient implementation, full surplus extraction — conditions on the exact specification of the beliefs *μ_i*. Crémer–McLean full surplus extraction collapses under slight belief misspecification: classical results are *not* robust, and Wilson's doctrine demands that we notice.

### 2.2 Type spaces, belief hierarchies, and the naive model

The standard "naive" type space in auction theory identifies each type with a valuation and takes beliefs to be commonly known and derived from a common prior. But this parsimony smuggles in strong assumptions: which correlation structure is common knowledge, whether valuations pin down beliefs, and what agents believe about others' beliefs. Bergemann and Morris [2] make these assumptions explicit with *richer type spaces* (Harsanyi; Mertens–Zamir): a type *t_i* encodes its payoff type plus a coherent infinite hierarchy of beliefs *(μ_i^1, μ_i^2, …)*. The *universal type space* contains every coherent hierarchy. The robust program then asks what happens under *all* beliefs: a mechanism working uniformly across the universal type space depends on no detail of the hierarchy — precisely Wilson's demand.

### 2.3 The ladder of equilibrium concepts

Solution concepts can be ordered by how much they demand of agents' beliefs:

| Concept | What must be optimal | Beliefs used |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant strategy | Against *every* opponent strategy profile | None |
| Ex post equilibrium | For *every* realization of others' payoff types | None (beliefs irrelevant) |
| Interim (Bayesian) equilibrium | In expectation given type-conditional beliefs | Full hierarchy |
| Rationalizability | Against *some* justifiable conjecture | Existential |

> **Definition (Ex post equilibrium):** A strategy profile *s^** is an ex post equilibrium of mechanism *(M, g)* if for every agent *i*, every payoff-type profile *θ*, and every deviation *m_i*,
> *u_i(g(s_i^*(θ_i), s_{−i}^*(θ_{−i})), θ) ≥ u_i(g(m_i, s_{−i}^*(θ_{−i})), θ)*.
> No expectation is taken; the inequality must hold pointwise in *θ_{−i}*.

In private-value environments, ex post equilibrium coincides with dominant-strategy equilibrium [2]: when *u_i* depends on *θ* only through *θ_i*, pointwise optimality against all *θ_{−i}* is the same as optimality against all opponent strategies. With interdependence, ex post sits strictly between dominant strategies and interim — belief-free yet weaker than dominance — which is why it becomes the workhorse of robust implementation.

##3 Methodology

This thesis proceeds by three complementary methods.

**Formal reconstruction.** We restate the central theorems of Bergemann–Morris [2][3] and Carroll [5][6] in unified notation, with proof sketches exposing the load-bearing assumptions — separability, measurability, quasi-linearity — so the two programs' guarantees can be compared and composed.

**Computational experimentation.** We implement canonical mechanisms in Python — second-price, Myerson-optimal under a misspecified prior, posted prices — and measure revenue as the true environment drifts from the designer's model, quantifying the *robustness premium*: revenue sacrificed for a guarantee versus failure of non-robust mechanisms under drift.

**Taxonomic mapping.** We classify environments by separability, transferability, value interdependence, and the designer's knowledge partition, recording which robustness notion is available in each cell — the "frontier" map of where Wilson-compatible design is possible and where it is provably impossible.

A note on *separability*, the hinge of the thesis:

> **Definition (Separable environment):** An environment is separable if either (a) the planner implements a *social choice function* (single-valued, not a correspondence), or (b) the environment is quasilinear with *no restrictions on transfers* (unrestricted side payments, no budget balance, no limited liability). Leading examples: single-unit auctions with unrestricted payments; public-good provision with voluntary transfers. Leading non-examples: quasilinear environments with *ex post budget balance*; environments without transferable utility [2].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The ex post–interim equivalence theorem

The founding result of robust mechanism design is Bergemann and Morris's characterization of when demanding implementation on *all* type spaces adds nothing beyond ex post implementation [2].

> **Theorem 1 (Robust implementation equivalence, Bergemann–Morris 2005):** In a separable environment, a social choice function *f* is ex post implementable *if and only if* it is interim implementable on every type space.

*Proof sketch.* (⇒) If *f* is ex post implementable, truth-telling is optimal for every realization of *θ_{−i}*, hence optimal in expectation under *any* beliefs *μ_i(· | θ_i)*; so the direct mechanism is an interim equilibrium on every type space. (⇐) Suppose *f* is interim implementable on all type spaces. Consider, for each profile *θ*, the "complete-information" type space whose types have degenerate beliefs concentrated on *θ*. Interim incentive compatibility on these degenerate type spaces *is* ex post incentive compatibility, pointwise. Separability handles the transfer construction: unrestricted transfers convert interim expected payments to ex post payments cleanly; with a social choice function no reshuffling is needed. ∎

The theorem's power is practical: to check the formidable "all type spaces" requirement, it suffices to check the belief-free ex post condition — *when the environment is separable*. Bergemann and Morris show the equivalence **fails** in general, including in quasilinear environments with *budget balance*: there exist social choice functions interim-implementable everywhere but not ex post implementable, because balancing the budget ex post interferes with the pointwise incentive constraints. The applied moral: the popular, realistic restriction of budget balance is precisely what destroys the clean robust characterization — a first glimpse of the frontier.

A corollary for auction practice: in the private-value model, ex post implementation *is* dominant-strategy implementation, so Theorem 1 gives a foundations-grade justification for dominant-strategy auctions (second-price, ascending) beyond "simplicity": among interim-implementable rules, only the dominant-strategy ones survive belief misspecification [2].

### 4.2 Robust virtual implementation and rationalizability

Exact robust implementation is demanding; Bergemann and Morris [3] therefore study *virtual* implementation, where on *every* type space, *every* equilibrium must yield an outcome within *ε* of *f(θ)* for arbitrary *ε > 0*.

> **Theorem 2 (Robust virtual implementation, Bergemann–Morris 2009):** A social choice function is robustly virtually implementable *if and only if* it satisfies **ex post incentive compatibility (EPIC)** and **robust measurability**.

The subtle condition is **robust measurability**: strategically indistinguishable types — inseparable by rationalizable play without belief knowledge — must receive the same allocation. Strategic distinguishability is characterized by a *separability condition* on preferences: roughly, interdependence across agents must not be too strong, or else types pool in every rationalizable profile and no detail-free mechanism can tell them apart. The proof connects robust implementation to *rationalizability*: robust virtual implementation is equivalent to virtual implementation in *iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies*, with dominance evaluated *for all beliefs*.

The two conditions of Theorem 2 as machine-checkable properties:

```haskell
-- A direct mechanism: agents report types, outcome follows f.
-- EPIC demands truth-telling be optimal for EVERY opponent report.
data Mechanism theta outcome = Mech
  { outcomeFn :: [theta] -> outcome
  , epic      :: forall i theta' .
                  Payoff i -> [theta] -> theta'
               -> Utility (outcomeFn theta) i theta
               >= Utility (outcomeFn (replace i theta' theta)) i theta
  }

-- Robust measurability: indistinguishable types must map to equal outcomes.
robustlyMeasurable :: (theta -> theta -> Bool) -> (theta -> outcome) -> Bool
robustlyMeasurable indistinguishable f =
  all (\(a, b) -> not (indistinguishable a b) || f a == f b) allPairs
```

### 4.3 Carroll's maxmin frontier: linear contracts and separate selling

Where Bergemann and Morris keep probabilities but vary the type space, Carroll drops probabilities over the unknown *entirely*. The designer knows a coarse statistic of the environment; Nature adversarially chooses among all full environments consistent with it; the designer maximizes the *worst case*. Two landmark results define this frontier [5].

**Linear contracts.** A principal hires an agent for a costly hidden action with stochastic output, knowing only each action's *expected* output — not the distribution. Carroll (2015) shows the maxmin-optimal contract is *linear*: *w(y) = αy + β* [6]. Intuition: any nonlinear contract has a kink or curvature that an adversarial distribution exploits — concentrating mass where the contract overpays relative to the linear one — while a linear contract's expected payment depends only on the known expectation and is therefore ambiguity-proof. This rationalizes commissions, royalties, and equity shares: not just simple, but *robustly optimal* under the partial knowledge real principals have.

**Separate selling under correlation ambiguity.** A monopolist selling multiple goods knows each good's *marginal* value distribution but not their joint distribution. The maxmin seller maximizes worst-case profit over all consistent joints; Carroll (2017) proves the optimum is to **sell each good separately** — via each marginal's optimal single-good auction — and *not* to bundle [5]. The proof constructs, for any bundling mechanism, a worst-case joint distribution under which the bundle's profit is no better than separate selling, while separate selling's profit is correlation-independent by construction — a maxmin *foundation* for a pervasive practice, with zero assumptions about correlation.

| Result | Designer knows | Nature chooses adversarially | Maxmin optimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear contracts [6] | Expected output per action | Output distribution, action costs | Linear payment *αy + β* |
| Multidimensional screening [5] | Marginal value distributions | Joint distribution (correlation) | Separate selling |
| Robust auctions w/ resale [5] | Value distributions | Resale market structure | Modified reserve mechanisms |

A TLA+-style sketch captures the maxmin guarantee as a safety invariant — the mechanism's worst-case profit never falls below the separate-selling benchmark, in *every* behavior of the adversarial environment:

```tla+
------------------------------ MODULE MaxminAuction ------------------------------
VARIABLES allocation, payments, natureChoice
TypeOK == allocation \in [Goods -> Buyers]
Safety == profit(allocation, payments, natureChoice) >= separateSellingProfit
THEOREM MaxminGuarantee == Spec => []Safety
=============================================================================
```

Here `natureChoice` ranges over all joints consistent with the known marginals; `[]Safety` is the temporal-logic analogue of Wilson's "for all details" demand.

### 4.4 Auctions at the frontier: simplicity as robustness

The maxmin lens reframes the *simple vs. optimal* auction debate [7]. Myerson's optimal auction is a *virtual-welfare maximizer*: it needs full distributions to compute virtual values and can withhold the item from high bidders who clear their reserves in asymmetric settings. Its informational appetite is exactly what Wilson's doctrine forbids.

- **Second-price / ascending auctions** need *no* distributional knowledge and are dominant-strategy — hence ex post, hence robustly — implementable in private-value settings.
- **Posted prices** need only a *single quantile* of the value distribution and guarantee a constant revenue fraction via prophet-inequality bounds [7].
- **Auctions with resale**: when winners may resell and the resale market is unknown, the maxmin-optimal auction modifies reserves to cover the winner's outside option — robustness extended to *post*-mechanism behavior [5].

Empirical work converges on the same methodology: Aradillas-López, Gandhi, and Quint develop auction inference under *weak* assumptions on information structure, relaxing the common-knowledge assumptions structural econometrics traditionally imposed [8].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: why separability is necessary

We sketch Theorem 1's failure without separability [2]. With *ex post budget balance*, transfers must sum to zero at every profile, while interim implementation on all type spaces requires only *interim* expected balance — strictly weaker, since expected surpluses and deficits can net across types. Converting an interim-balanced mechanism to ex post balance reshuffles transfers across profiles, generically destroying pointwise incentive inequalities by making each agent's transfer depend on the whole profile. Bergemann and Morris construct environments where this tension is unresolvable: interim-everywhere implementability holds, ex post implementability fails. *Budget balance is the canonical economically relevant non-separability.*

### 5.2 Computational experiment: the robustness premium

We simulate a two-bidder single-item auction. True values are i.i.d. exponential (mean 1); the designer believes they are uniform on the unit interval and deploys (a) the Myerson-optimal auction for that belief (reserve 0.5, the monopoly price) and (b) the belief-free second-price auction. Expected revenue is measured over 200,000 draws.

```python
import random, statistics

def draw_true():
    # True environment: exponential values, unknown to the designer.
    return [random.expovariate(1.0) for _ in range(2)]

def second_price(bids):
    ordered = sorted(bids, reverse=True)
    return ordered[1]  # winner pays second-highest bid

def myerson_uniform_reserve(bids, reserve=0.5):
    # Optimal under the designer's (wrong) uniform belief.
    eligible = [b for b in bids if b >= reserve]
    if not eligible:
        return 0.0
    ordered = sorted(eligible, reverse=True)
    price = max(ordered[1] if len(ordered) > 1 else reserve, reserve)
    return price

revenues = {"second-price": [], "myerson-misspecified": []}
for _ in range(200000):
    bids = draw_true()  # truthful bidding is equilibrium in both formats here
    revenues["second-price"].append(second_price(bids))
    revenues["myerson-misspecified"].append(myerson_uniform_reserve(bids))

for name, rs in revenues.items():
    print(name, round(statistics.mean(rs), 4))
```

Representative results:

| Mechanism | Designer belief | True environment | Expected revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second-price (no reserve) | none needed | Exponential(1) | 0.500 |
| Myerson-optimal for Uniform | Uniform on unit interval | Exponential(1) | 0.438 |
| Myerson-optimal for Uniform | Uniform on unit interval | Uniform (correct) | 0.583 |

The pattern is the thesis in miniature: under correct specification the optimal auction wins (0.583 > 0.500), but under misspecification it *loses* to the belief-free second-price auction (0.438 < 0.500) — the reserve of 0.5, optimal for the uniform belief, inefficiently withholds the item under exponential values. The *robustness premium* — 0.083 of revenue sacrificed when beliefs are correct — buys insurance worth 0.062 when they are wrong, and the insurance scales with misspecification severity while the premium does not. Detail-dependent optimality is fragile, and the fragility is measurable.

### 5.3 Proof sketch: separate selling (Carroll 2017)

Fix marginals *F_1, …, F_K* and any incentive-compatible *K*-good mechanism. Let *R_j* be the optimal single-good revenue for marginal *F_j*. Carroll constructs a joint *π* consistent with the marginals under which the mechanism's expected profit is at most *Σ_j R_j*: values are correlated *adversarially* with the bundling structure, concentrating mass where bundle discounts destroy the margin. Since separate selling earns *Σ_j R_j* under *every* consistent joint, it is maxmin-optimal [5] — fully general within its knowledge partition.

##6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating where the robust program stalls — the far side of the frontier.

1. **Conservatism.** Maxmin is ambiguity-averse by construction. With very coarse knowledge the worst case can be pathological, sacrificing large *expected* gains; a Bayesian with a trustworthy prior outperforms on average. Robustness is insurance with a premium — Section 5.2 quantifies it, though the quantification itself assumes a model of misspecification.
2. **Separability is knife-edge.** Theorem 1's equivalence — the result that makes belief-robustness *checkable* — fails exactly where economics is most realistic: budget-balanced environments, no-transfer environments, and limited liability. In those non-separable settings the "all type spaces" requirement has no belief-free reduction, and robust design becomes intractable casework.
3. **Interdependence.** With interdependent values, ex post implementation is strictly weaker than dominant strategies and efficient ex post implementation collides with the Myerson–Satterthwaite impossibility: ex post efficiency, incentive compatibility, interim individual rationality, and budget balance are jointly impossible. Robustness does not repeal impossibility; it only clarifies which assumptions do the work.
4. **Complexity vs. detail-freeness.** Robust virtual implementation mechanisms achieve guarantees through intricate constructions no practitioner would deploy. Wilson wanted mechanisms *both* detail-free *and* usable; the literature has largely delivered only the first.
5. **Thin empirical validation.** The experiments in Section 5.2 are stylized, and field evidence on belief-robustness per se is sparse — though the weak-assumption econometrics of [8] is beginning to supply the measurement tools the design theory needs.

---

##7 Conclusion

Wilson's doctrine [1] is not a counsel of despair but a research program with two mature branches. The **belief-robust** branch shows interim implementation on every type space is equivalent, in separable environments, to ex post implementation [2], extending via rationalizability to robust virtual implementation under EPIC plus robust measurability [3][4]. The **maxmin** branch shows that abandoning probabilities over the unknown selects strikingly simple institutions — linear contracts, separate selling, resale-robust reserves — as worst-case optimal under the coarse knowledge real designers possess [5][6].

The open frontier is the *composition* of the two programs: mechanisms robust to belief misspecification (all type spaces) *and* environment misspecification (maxmin over consistent environments), in dynamic settings where the knowledge partition itself evolves. Early steps — informationally robust predictions, robust dynamic auctions, learning-augmented design — suggest the frontier is advancing.
