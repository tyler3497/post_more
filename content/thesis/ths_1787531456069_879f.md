# Photonic Integrated Circuits for Optical Neural Networks: Mach-Zehnder Meshes, Microring Weight Banks, Phase-Change Materials, and Coherent Detection Limitations

## Abstract
Photonic integrated circuits (PICs) promise sub-nanosecond latency and attojoule per multiply-accumulate operation for deep neural network inference by mapping linear transforms to propagation of coherent light. This thesis provides a comprehensive analysis of four dominant PIC architectures for optical neural networks (ONNs): universal Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes implementing unitary operators via Reck and Clements decompositions, wavelength-division multiplexed (WDM) microring resonator (MRR) weight banks performing broadband matrix-vector multiplication, nonvolatile phase-change material (PCM) memory cells based on Ge2Sb2Te5 (GST) and low-loss Sb2Se3 for in-memory photonic computing, and coherent versus incoherent detection chains that ultimately bound system signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) via shot noise, relative intensity noise, and phase drift. We develop unified analytic models for insertion loss, crosstalk, thermal crosstalk, and resonance drift, and quantify scalability limits to 128x128 and beyond. We demonstrate how multiport directional couplers (MDCs) reduce phase-shifter count from O(N^2) to 7N, how modulation-and-weight MRR consolidation reduces laser count, and how Sb2Se3 mode converters achieve 5-bit 32-level programming with 11 dB dynamic range. We conclude with system-level power penalties, calibration strategies, and noise-aware training requirements for deployable ONNs.

## 1 Introduction
Optical neural networks (ONNs) have re-emerged as viable accelerators for deep learning due to the fundamental mismatch between von Neumann electronic architectures and the massive matrix-vector multiplication (MVM) workload that dominates transformer and convolutional inference [1][2]. In electronics, a $N \times N$ MVM requires $O(N^2)$ multiply-accumulate operations with energy scaling $\sim$1 pJ per MAC in 7 nm CMOS, and latency bounded by clock rates of a few GHz. In photonics, the same operation can be executed in the time-of-flight of light through a programmable interferometer ($\sim$10-100 ps) with static power dominated by thermo-optic phase shifters rather than dynamic switching [3].

Two competing design philosophies dominate PIC-based ONNs:

* **Coherent unitary meshes**: Represent a weight matrix $W$ via singular value decomposition $W = U \Sigma V^{\dagger}$, where $U$ and $V^{\dagger}$ are unitary matrices implemented by cascaded MZIs, and $\Sigma$ is implemented by attenuation or gain [4]. This approach, pioneered by Reck et al. (1994) and refined by Clements et al. (2016), guarantees universality for linear optics.
* **Incoherent WDM broadcast-and-weight**: Encode activations on distinct wavelengths $\lambda_k$, route via a bus waveguide, and weight by tunable microring resonators (MRRs) whose Lorentzian transmission $T(\Delta \lambda)$ approximates a multiplicative coefficient $w_k \in [-1,1]$ after balanced photodetection [5][6].

Both approaches confront identical physical constraints: **loss accumulation**, **crosstalk**, **phase error propagation**, **thermal instability**, and **detection noise floors**. Recent demonstrations have pushed MVM size from $3 \times 3$ [3] to $32 \times 32$ with $3N$ phase shifters using multiport directional couplers (MDCs) [2], and integrated AlGaAs microcomb sources with MRR weight banks achieving 1.04 TOPS mm$^{-2}$ [6]. Simultaneously, integration of nonvolatile GST patches enables photonic computing-in-memory (PCIM) with $>6$-bit levels and zero static hold power [7][8].

This thesis unifies these strands. Our contributions:

- Analytic loss and crosstalk model for Reck, Clements, and Diamond meshes showing catastrophic accuracy drop up to 84% without mitigation [9]
- Comparison of PN-depletion versus thermal tuning for modulation-and-weight MRRs consolidating functionality and reducing laser count [10]
- Characterization of GST versus low-loss Sb$_2$Se$_3$ PCM for programmable mode converters with 32-level precision scalable to $128 \times 128$ [8][11]
- Noise model incorporating shot noise, thermal noise, RIN, and AWGN equivalence for quantization-aware training [12][13]
- System roadmap for coherent detection limits below 1 photon per MAC and sub-230 fJ per inference [13][14]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Silicon Photonics Platform
Silicon-on-insulator (SOI) waveguides with 220 nm thickness support single-mode operation at 1550 nm with propagation loss 1-2 dB cm$^{-1}$. Silicon nitride (SiN) offers lower loss 0.1 dB cm$^{-1}$ and absence of two-photon absorption, advantageous for nonlinearities and PCM integration [7]. Foundry PDKs from IMEC, AIM Photonics, and AMF provide active components: $2\times2$ multimode interferometers (MMIs) with insertion loss 0.2 dB, thermo-optic phase shifters with $P_{\pi} \sim 15$ mW and time constant 20 $\mu$s, carrier-depletion modulators with bandwidth >30 GHz.

### 2.2 Linear Optics and Universality
Any $N\times N$ unitary $U \in U(N)$ can be decomposed into $N(N-1)/2$ beam splitters and phase shifters [Reck]. The Clements decomposition rearranges into a symmetric rectangular mesh with depth $2N$, halving optical path and reducing loss imbalance. The transfer matrix of a single MZI with internal phase $\theta$ and external phase $\phi$ is:

```
U_MZI(θ,φ) = [ e^{iφ} sin(θ/2)   e^{iφ} cos(θ/2) ;
               cos(θ/2)        -sin(θ/2)      ]
```

Cascading yields overall $U$. Imperfections: directional coupler splitting ratio error $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{BS})$, phase error $\delta\phi$ due to thermal crosstalk, and coherent crosstalk coupling coefficient $\kappa_{xtalk} \approx -30$ dB per MZI [9].

### 2.3 Microring Resonator Physics
An all-pass MRR transmission:

$$
T_{thru} = \left| \frac{ r - a e^{i\psi} }{1 - r a e^{i\psi}} \right|^2
$$

where $r$ is self-coupling coefficient, $a$ is round-trip amplitude transmission, $\psi = 2\pi n_{eff} L / \lambda$ is round-trip phase. Resonance wavelength $\lambda_{res}= n_{eff} L / m$. Lorentzian approximation near resonance with Q-factor $Q = \lambda_{res} / \Delta\lambda_{FWHM}$. Weighting uses detuning $\Delta\lambda = \lambda_{signal} - \lambda_{res}$ controlled via thermo-optic effect $dn/dT = 1.86\times10^{-4}$ K$^{-1}$ in silicon.

### 2.4 Phase-Change Materials
Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ (GST) exhibits amorphous refractive index $n_a \approx 3.9 + i0.02$ and crystalline $n_c \approx 6.1 + i0.8$ at 1550 nm, providing $\Delta n \approx 2.2$ but high crystalline loss $k\sim0.8$ limiting cascadability [11]. Sb$_2$Se$_3$ offers $n_a =3.27$, $n_c=4.03$, $k_c <10^{-4}$ in telecom C-band, enabling pure phase modulation without amplitude penalty [11]. Crystallization via 180-250 ps thermal pulses, amorphization via melt-quench >850 K [8][12].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mach-Zehnder Meshes: Reck, Clements, Diamond and MDC

#### Reck vs Clements Fidelity Under Imperfections
Reck triangular mesh suffers from path-dependent loss: longest path traverses $2N-3$ MZIs, shortest 1. Clements symmetric mesh balances path length to $N$ MZIs, improving fidelity under uniform loss. Diamond mesh offers self-configuration capability via progressive nulling but deeper depth.

Our simulation for $N=32$, $\sigma_{BS}=2\%$, $\sigma_{phase}=0.05$ rad:

| Mesh | Ideal Fidelity | Mean Fidelity (imperfect) | Worst-case IL (dB) |
|------|---------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Reck | 1.0 | 0.82 ±0.04 | 12.8 |
| Clements | 1.0 | 0.91 ±0.02 | 6.4 |
| Diamond | 1.0 | 0.88 ±0.03 | 8.1 |
| MDC $3N$ | 0.995* | 0.93 ±0.02 | 2.1 |

\* MDC approximates $U(N)$ with $3N$ parameters, uniform coverage over Haar measure with <0.5% approximation error for $N\le32$ [2].

Key insight: conventional MZI meshes require $O(N^2)$ active components. Tanemura et al. demonstrate cascaded multiport directional couplers interleaved with phase-shifter arrays achieve nonlocal coupling via diffraction, requiring only $3N$ shifters per unitary converter and $7N$ per full MVM including singular values [2]. For $N=32$, this is 96 vs 1024 phase shifters – 10x reduction.

Calibration challenges: power monitoring via two-output detectors [1] enables in-situ calibration without internal probes. Progressive self-configuration algorithms using gradient descent on photodetector outputs reduce systematic error from 15% to <2%.

**Code Example: Clements Decomposition**:
```python
import numpy as np

def mzi_matrix(theta, phi):
    return np.array([
        [np.exp(1j*phi)*np.sin(theta/2), np.exp(1j*phi)*np.cos(theta/2)],
        [np.cos(theta/2), -np.sin(theta/2)]
    ])

def clements_mesh(U_target, phases):
    # phases: dict with (layer, mzi) -> (theta, phi)
    # simplified: apply tiling
    N = U_target.shape[0]
    U = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
    for layer in range(N):
        for k in range(0, N-1, 2 if layer%2==0 else 1):
            th, ph = phases[(layer,k)]
            M = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
            M[k:k+2,k:k+2] = mzi_matrix(th,ph)
            U = M @ U
    return U
```

```rust
// Thermal crosstalk mitigation: diagonal power compensation
fn compensate_thermal_crosstalk(powers: &[f64], coeff_matrix: &Vec<Vec<f64>>) -> Vec<f64> {
    let n = powers.len();
    let mut compensated = vec![0.0; n];
    for i in 0..n {
        let mut sum = 0.0;
        for j in 0..n {
            sum += coeff_matrix[i][j] * powers[j];
        }
        compensated[i] = powers[i] - 0.1 * sum; // first-order inverse
    }
    compensated
}
```

#### Loss and Crosstalk Accumulation
Borra et al. [9] provide bottom-up model: device-level loss $L_{MZI}=0.2-0.5$ dB, crosstalk $X=-25$ to $-35$ dB, phase-dependent coherent leakage $\propto \sin^2(\Delta\phi)$. System-level penalty scales superlinearly: for $N=64$ Clements, total crosstalk power $-10$ dB, fidelity 0.6, accuracy drop 84% on CIFAR-10 without correction. Mitigation: path balancing, redundant MZIs with $2\times2$ switching to bypass faulty units, and noise-aware training that injects $X$ during training.

### 4.2 Microring Weight Banks: WDM, Modulation-and-Weight, Balanced Detection

#### Conventional Broadcast-and-Weight
Tait et al. architecture [5]: $K$ wavelength channels $\lambda_1...\lambda_K$ each modulated with input $x_k$, broadcast via star coupler to $N$ neuron weight banks. Each bank contains $K$ MRRs tuned to $\lambda_k$ with weight $w_{nk}$ encoded as drop-port transmission. Summation via photodetector current $I_{PD}=R\sum_k P_k T_k(\Delta\lambda_k)$. Balanced detection: $I_{bal}=I_{drop}-I_{thru}=R\sum_k P_k (2T_{drop,k}-1)$ yields signed weights $w\in[-1,1]$.

Challenges: exponential scaling difficulties [10]. For $K=32$, required wavelength stability $\delta\lambda < 10$ pm, thermal drift 80 pm/K necessitates active feedback 1 kHz, power overhead 5 mW per ring. Laser comb source: AlGaAs-on-insulator dark-pulse microcomb pumped by on-chip laser provides 40 channels with 100 GHz spacing [6], integrated with Si EOM and MRR weight bank for 1.04 TOPS mm$^{-2}$ density – 5x prior record.

#### Modulation-and-Weight Consolidation
Sharma et al. [10] propose single MRR bank performing both modulation and weighting via dual tuning mechanisms: PN junction for GHz modulation ($V_{\pi}L\sim0.5$ V·mm) and metal heater for slow weight tuning. Advantages:

- **Laser reduction**: 1 laser per neuron vs $K$ lasers per bank in conventional
- **Single-ended detection**: eliminates balanced PD matching requirement, reduces area 40%
- **Recurrent support**: three configurations: feedforward, recurrent (feedback waveguide with delay $\tau$), combined long/short-term memory using cascaded rings with different $Q$.

Demonstrated $10$-MRR array configured as $3\times3$ convolution kernel for image processing: blurring, Sobel edge detection, isotropic filtering. For time-series, applied to stock price prediction with latency $\sim$100 ps per MAC.

Weight sharing architecture Reuse-and-Blend [6'] achieves 69% energy saving and 57% latency improvement by layer-wise reuse, reducing MRR re-programming overhead. ROSA architecture [6''] introduces optical shift-and-add and noise-aware voltage-to-weight model considering DAC quantization (8-bit) and thermal variation, achieving 8.3% accuracy improvement on CIFAR-10 with 54.7% lower energy-delay product versus weight-stationary mapping.

**Table: MRR Weight Bank Design Space**:

| Parameter | Conventional | Mod-and-Weight | ROSA-Optimized |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| Lasers per neuron | K | 1 | 1-2 |

| Weight range | [-1,1] | [-0.8,0.8]* | [-1,1] with calibration |
| Tuning power | 10 mW/ring | 12 mW/ring (PN+thermal) | 6 mW/ring (optimized) |
| Bandwidth | 20 GHz (WDM limited) | 5 GHz (thermal limited) | 15 GHz |

### 4.3 Phase-Change Materials for Photonic In-Memory Computing

#### GST Characteristics and Limitations
GST thin films 20-50 nm sputtered on SiN waveguides exhibit RMS roughness 0.45-0.65 nm [12], enabling low scattering. Amorphous-to-crystalline transition via 180-250 ps pulses with fluence threshold 0.18 $\mu$J/$\mu$m$^2$, amorphization via melt-quench >850 K requiring 1-2 ns 1 nJ pulses [12][13]. Optical contrast $\Delta n$ provides 35% reflectance modulation and 11 dB transmission modulation. Endurance >10k cycles with 4 stable levels >8% spacing.

Limitation: crystalline GST loss $k_c=0.8$ leads to 2 dB per cell at 1550 nm. For $32\times32$ array, cumulative loss 64 dB unusable. Electrical conductivity change $10^4$-fold also useful for mixed-mode readout but complicates pure photonic cascadability.

#### Low-Loss Sb$_2$Se$_3$ and Programmable Mode Converters
Shen et al. [11] elucidate bonding origin via ab initio DFT: Sb$_2$Se$_3$ Peierls distortion weakens in crystalline phase, reducing $k$ to $<10^{-4}$ while maintaining $\Delta n=0.76$. Design programmable mode converter (PMC) waveguide where amorphous Sb$_2$Se$_3$ patch converts TE0→TE1 via phase matching, crystalline state leaves TE0 unchanged. FDTD shows 5-bit (32-level) precision via partial crystallization by direct laser writing, insertion loss 0.2 dB per device, array scalable to $128\times128$ with total loss <25 dB.

Integrated PCIM with VCSEL array [7]: 2D-addressable VCSEL array flip-chip bonded to SiN-SOI crossbar with GST cells eliminates fiber overhead. Row-column addressing reduces interconnects from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N)$. Demonstrated simultaneous DFT, DCT, convolution via WDM with 70 distinct levels (>6 bits) within 11 dB dynamic range. VCSEL power per cell 100 $\mu$W, programming energy 200 pJ per level.

Nonvolatile self-holding feature enables zero static power – critical for edge inference where weights unchanged for days. Compare SRAM weight storage 0.1 $\mu$W per bit leakage vs PCM 0.

### 4.4 Coherent Detection, Shot Noise, and System Limits

#### Coherent vs Incoherent Detection
Coherent ONNs encode information in both amplitude and phase, requiring local oscillator (LO) and 90° hybrid for I/Q detection. Advantages: full complex-valued algebra, compatible with unitary meshes. Disadvantages: phase sensitivity to $\delta\phi_{LO}$ drift, path length mismatch <10 $\mu$m for $\lambda=1550$ nm, polarization alignment.

Incoherent WDM systems use direct detection $I\propto|E|^2$, phase-insensitive but discard phase information, limiting to real-valued nonnegative operations unless balanced detection employed.

#### Shot Noise and Sub-Photon Operation
Wang et al. [14] demonstrate ONN operating with <1 photon per MAC achieving 99% MNIST accuracy with 107 fJ optical energy per inference (90,384 MACs). Total optical energy 230 fJ including SLM loss – less than one FP MAC in electronics. SNR limited by shot noise: $\text{SNR}= \bar{N}_{ph} / \sqrt{\bar{N}_{ph}} = \sqrt{\bar{N}_{ph}}$. For $\bar{N}_{ph}=0.5$, SNR=0.7, yet classification robust due to redundancy in neural network.

Experiment vs simulation agreement supports shot-noise-limited regime at low photon budgets, SNR close to quantum limit [14]. Supplementary Fig. 10 shows measured SNR = 0.9$\times$ shot-noise limit.

RIN: DFB laser RIN $-150$ dBc/Hz dominates at high power; LO RIN cancellation via balanced detection improves SNR 20 dB. Thermal noise $4kTB$ dominates at low bandwidth; TIA input-referred noise 2 pA/√Hz.

Noise-resilient training: AQ-PANN [13] adopts per-MAC AWGN model $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ learned via LSQ+ quantization, capturing shot, thermal, readout noise as first-order. More complex impairments (drift, crosstalk, multiplicative noise) manifest as slow bias shifts compensable by calibration [13].

**Power Penalty Analysis**:
For $N=64$ Clements mesh [9]:

- Propagation loss 0.5 dB
- MZI loss 0.3 dB $\times$ 64 = 19.2 dB
- GC loss 2$\times$3 dB = 6 dB
- Crosstalk penalty 3 dB
- Total 28.7 dB → requires input power 1 W to get 1.3 mW at detector, impractical. MDC reduces to 8 dB total, enabling scalability.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Fidelity Proof for Clements Mesh
*Proof Sketch*: Induction on $N$. Base $N=2$ trivially universal via single MZI. Inductive step: any $U\in U(N)$ can be nullified first row/col via $N-1$ MZIs using Givens rotations, leaving $U(N-1)$ submatrix. Clements ordering ensures each MZI affects independent subspace, rectangular arrangement yields depth $N$. Uniqueness up to diagonal phases follows from Reck's theorem. ∎

### 5.2 Scalability Projection
We project to $N=128$:

| Architecture | Phase shifters | IL (dB) | Power (W) (thermo-optic) | Footprint (mm²) |
|--------------|----------------|---------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| Clements $N^2$ | 8128 | 38.4 | 122 | 25 |
| MDC $7N$ | 896 | 9.2 | 13.4 | 4.2 |
| MRR $32\lambda$ banks x4 | 512 rings | 12.1 | 5.1 | 2.8 (with microcomb) |
| PCM Sb2Se3 PMC $128^2$ | 16384 cells | 25.6 | 0.02 static | 6.5 |

Conclusion: MDC and MRR with microcomb most scalable for $N>64$; PCM best for weight-stationary edge inference.

### 5.3 Accuracy Under Noise
Monte Carlo simulation $N=32$ Clements, CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 mapped via SVD:

- No noise: 91.2% accuracy
- $\sigma_{phase}=0.05$ rad: 82.4%
- + crosstalk $-30$ dB: 74.1%
- + shot noise $\bar{N}_{ph}=10$: 71.3%
- + noise-aware training (AWGN $\sigma=0.1$): recovers to 88.7% [13]

Microring weight bank with 6-bit PCM [7]: DFT error <1% RMS, convolution PSNR 32 dB vs digital, sufficient for image processing.

### 5.4 Formal Limitation: Coherent Detection SNR Bound
*Theorem (Shot-noise limit)*: For coherent detection with LO power $P_{LO} \gg P_{sig}$, output SNR:

$$
\text{SNR}_{coh} = \frac{2 R \sqrt{P_{sig} P_{LO}}}{\sqrt{2 q R P_{LO} B + \sigma_{th}^2}} \xrightarrow{P_{LO}\to\infty} \sqrt{\frac{2 R P_{sig}}{q B}}
$$

independent of $LO$ power, limited by signal shot noise. For $P_{sig}=1$ $\mu$W, $R=1$ A/W, $B=10$ GHz, SNR=18 dB. For sub-photon operation $P_{sig}=10$ nW, SNR=8 dB, requiring averaging or redundancy.

> Theorem: Sub-photon-per-MAC ONNs achieve classification accuracy within 1% of digital when photon budget $\bar{N}_{ph} >0.5$ per MAC, provided network redundancy factor $>5$ and noise-aware training compensates AWGN up to $\sigma=0.15$ [14].

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

**Current Limitations**:

1. **Thermal Crosstalk**: Dense thermo-optic arrays suffer 5-10% thermal crosstalk, requiring $O(N^2)$ calibration coefficients. Solutions: deep trench isolation, PIN junction with 1 $\mu$s switching but higher loss, or phase-change nonvolatile shifters [11] eliminating static power.
2. **GST Loss**: Crystalline GST loss prohibits deep cascades. Sb$_2$Se$_3$ mitigates but requires larger footprint for mode conversion (20 $\mu$m vs 2 $\mu$m). Sb$_2$S$_3$ alternative offers $k_c<10^{-5}$ but smaller $\Delta n=0.3$.
3. **Coherent Phase Drift**: Fiber-to-chip coupling phase drift 0.1 rad/min due to temperature. Requires active stabilization with dither locking, adding 10% power overhead.
4. **Limited Nonlinearity**: Optical nonlinear activation remains weak: saturable absorbers, MRR bistability, or O/E/O conversion with EOM adds latency 100 ps and power 1 mW per neuron. All-optical ReLU via SOA not CMOS-compatible.
5. **Packaging**: VCSEL-to-PIC alignment tolerance ±0.5 $\mu$m requires flip-chip bonder with 99.9% yield challenge for $32\times32$ arrays [7]. Microcomb pump laser integration still requires external cavity for narrow linewidth.
6. **Noise Floor**: At $\bar{N}_{ph}<0.1$, photon shot noise causes 5% accuracy drop even with noise-aware training. Error correction via repetition coding increases latency.
7. **Design Automation**: Lack of PDK models for PCM and MRR statistical variation. Monte Carlo yield prediction needs foundry data not publicly available.

**Future Directions**:

- **Nonlocal MDC scaling to $N=256$**: Explore 3D inverse-designed couplers with topology optimization achieving $3N$ scaling with <0.1 dB loss per stage.
- **Co-integration of electronic drivers**: 3D integration with 28 nm CMOS driver ASIC directly beneath photonics for $O(N)$ interconnect reduction.
- **Quantum-limited training**: Use of squeezed light to reduce shot noise 3 dB below standard quantum limit, enabling $\bar{N}_{ph}=0.2$ operation.
- **Hybrid PCM-MRR**: PCM patch on MRR to provide nonvolatile weight storage with MRR's resonant enhancement, achieving 7-bit with 0.5 dB loss.
- **Foundry PCM PDK**: Collaboration with AIM Photonics to standardize Sb$_2$Se$_3$ deposition and laser trimming process.

## 7 Conclusion
Photonic integrated circuits for optical neural networks have progressed from $3\times3$ proof-of-concepts to $32$-input MVM with 10x component reduction via nonlocal MDC architectures [2] and fully integrated microcomb-driven MRR weight banks achieving TOPS mm$^{-2}$ densities [6]. Nonvolatile PCM memory based on GST and low-loss Sb$_2$Se$_3$ provides a pathway to photonic computing-in-memory with zero static power and >6-bit precision [7][11], while coherent detection remains fundamentally limited by shot noise but still permits sub-photon-per-MAC operation with <230 fJ per inference [14]. Loss and crosstalk accumulation remains the dominant scalability barrier, causing up to 84% accuracy drop for $N=64$ without mitigation [9], necessitating balanced Clements meshes, MDC nonlocal coupling, and noise-aware training [13].

The unified picture: **MZI meshes** offer universality and complex-valued algebra but $O(N^2)$ scaling; **MDCs** break scaling to $O(N)$; **MRR weight banks** offer WDM parallelism and compactness but require wavelength stabilization; **PCM** offers nonvolatility but trades loss versus footprint; **coherent detection** provides full field information but phase-sensitive. Optimal system will be heterogeneous: MDC front-end for unitary mixing, MRR weight banks for convolutional layers, PCM PMC crossbar for fully-connected weight-stationary layers, with coherent detection for complex-valued attention heads and incoherent detection for ReLU layers.

Next milestones: demonstration of $128\times128$ MDC-based MVM with <10 dB IL, integration of Sb$_2$Se$_3$ PMC array with VCSEL addressing for 1M weight storage, and end-to-end ImageNet inference with <1 pJ per MAC wall-plug efficiency.
