# Edge-Cloud Collaborative DNN Inference: Neurosurgeon Partitioning, Early-Exit Branches, Model Cascades, and SLO-Aware Offloading under Variable Bandwidth and Heterogeneous Compute

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of edge-cloud collaborative dnn inference: neurosurgeon partitioning, early-exit branches, model cascades, and slo-aware offloading under variable bandwidth and heterogeneous compute, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 systems practice. We formalize cost models, invariants, and coherence protocols spanning edge-cloud collaborative inference, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, and ACM DL. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and heterogeneous hardware traces including H100 CC, 96c Genoa, NVLink4 600GBps, RoCEv2 400G CX7, Micron7450 1.6TB NVMe ZNS, Morello prototype, Loihi2 Oheo Gulch Lava0.8, USRP X310 6GHz IQ streams. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds, deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage bounds, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance, mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient, scalable edge-cloud collaborative inference production systems.
