# Verified Register Allocation via Chaitin-Briggs Graph Coloring: Interference Graphs, SSA Deconstruction, and Coalescing Heuristics in Modern Middle-Ends

## Abstract
Register allocation remains the fulcrum between machine-independent optimization and target-specific resource constraints, where an unbounded set of temporaries must be mapped onto $k$ physical registers. This thesis dissects the Chaitin-Briggs family of graph-coloring allocators, its integration with static single assignment form, and its modern verified instantiation in CompCert. We formalize interference as intersection of live ranges over a dominance-tree-induced chordal structure, characterize SSA deconstruction as isomorphism-preserving parallel-copy insertion, and analyze iterated, conservative, and optimistic coalescing through affinity graphs. A mechanized validator model is presented that decouples untrusted heuristic coloring from a verified Coq checker, preserving forward simulation. Evaluation across SPEC-like and MiBench workloads shows chordal-aware coloring reduces spill code by 18\% and move density by 23\% over baseline Chaitin, while verification overhead remains $O(|V|+|E|)$.

## 1. Introduction

The register allocation problem is *deceptively* simple to state and notoriously hard to solve optimally. Given a program in an intermediate representation (IR) with an infinite supply of virtual registers — temporaries, pseudo-registers, versioned SSA names — assign each live value to one of $K$ physical registers such that no two simultaneously live values share a register, minimizing spills to memory and moves induced by calling conventions, precolored constraints, and coalescing failures.

Graph coloring provides the canonical abstraction [1][2]. Since Chaitin's seminal 1981-1982 formulation [3], the field has oscillated between two poles: **correctness** and **quality**. On one hand, Chaitin proved that for *any* undirected graph $G$ there exists a program whose interference graph is $G$, establishing NP-completeness of optimal $k$-coloring for $K \ge 3$. On the other hand, Briggs, Cooper, and Torczon [2] observed that *optimistic* coloring — delaying spill decisions until select phase — dramatically improves colorability without sacrificing soundness.

> The central insight of verified compilation is not to verify the heuristic, but to verify the result.

Modern middle-ends complicate this narrative in three ways:

- **SSA form** dominates contemporary IRs (LLVM, Cranelift, GCC GIMPLE, CompCert RTL). Hack et al. [4] and independently Bouchez et al. proved that **strict SSA** interference graphs are *chordal*, thus $k$-colorable in $O(|V|+|E|)$ via simplicial elimination ordering.
- **Deconstruction** out of SSA reintroduces copies and critical-edge splitting, potentially destroying chordality. Pereira and Palsberg [5] show classical SSA elimination is NP-complete to minimize copies.
- **Verified toolchains** such as CompCert [6] cannot trust a 10k-line OCaml graph-coloring heuristic. Instead, Leroy's methodology [7] employs *translation validation*: an untrusted oracle proposes a mapping $\phi: \text{Var} \to \text{Reg} \cup \text{StackSlot}$ and a verified validator checks $\forall (x,y) \in E_{intf}, \phi(x) \neq \phi(y)$.

This thesis unifies these threads.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

We work over a control-flow graph CFG $= \langle B, E, entry \rangle$ where each block $b \in B$ is a sequence of instructions in *RTL-like* 3-address form.

### 2.1 Liveness and Interference

Classical dataflow defines $\text{LiveIn}$ and $\text{LiveOut}$ via backward fixpoint:

$$
\text{LiveIn}(n) = \text{Use}(n) \cup (\text{LiveOut}(n) \setminus \text{Def}(n))
$$
$$
\text{LiveOut}(n) = \bigcup_{s \in succ(n)} \text{LiveIn}(s)
$$

With $n$ up to $10^5$, bitset-accelerated iteration achieves $O(N \cdot |\text{Var}| / 64)$.

> **Definition:** *Live Range.* The live range of $v$ is the set $\mathcal{L}(v) = \{ p \mid v \in \text{LiveIn}(p) \cup \text{LiveOut}(p) \}$ represented as a collection of intervals over a linearized order or as a subtree of the dominance tree in SSA.

> **Definition:** *Interference.* Variables $x,y$ interfere iff $\mathcal{L}(x) \cap \mathcal{L}(y) \neq \emptyset$ and not $x,y$ are connected solely by a move $x:=y$ that can be coalesced. Formally, interference graph $G_{I} = (V,E_{I})$ where $(x,y)\in E_{I}$ if they are simultaneously live at a program point and have distinct values.

Note subtlety from CMU lecture notes [8]: simultaneous liveness is *necessary but not sufficient*; e.g., `a = b` with no intervening redefinition does not create interference if copy propagation proves same value.

### 2.2 SSA and Chordality

Static Single Assignment requires each variable defined once and $\phi$-functions at join points merge versions.

> **Definition:** *Chordal Graph.* An undirected graph is chordal if every cycle $C$ with $|C|\ge 4$ has a chord — an edge connecting two non-consecutive vertices of $C$. Equivalent to possessing a perfect elimination ordering.

> **Definition:** *Simplicial Vertex.* A vertex $v$ where $N(v)$ induces a clique.

**Dirac's theorem**: $G$ chordal iff it admits a simplicial elimination ordering (SEO). Maximal Cardinality Search (MCS) computes SEO in $O(|V|+|E|)$.

Importance: In strict SSA, live ranges correspond to *subtrees* of dominance tree. Subtree intersection graphs are chordal (Gavril 1974). Hence interference of SSA programs is chordal [4][5].

### 2.3 Coalescing Landscape

Move instructions `d = s` suggest assigning same color to $d,s$ to eliminate the move.

- **Aggressive (Chaitin 1981):** merge any non-interfering move-related nodes. May produce uncolorable merged node — *reckless*.
- **Conservative (Briggs 1994):** merge $a,b$ only if $|N(a)\cup N(b)| < K$ or merged node's degree < K after accounting for shared neighbors. Guarantees colorability preservation.
- **Iterated (George-Appel 1996):** Interleaves simplification with coalescing: simplify $deg < K$ nodes, then conservative coalesce, then freeze moves, then potential spill. Repeats until fixed point.
- **Optimistic (Park-Moon):** coalesce recklessly, but during select, if coalesced node uncolorable, split back, and assign distinct colors but keep move.

> This taxonomy is not merely historical; LLVM's `Greedy` and GCC's `IRA` still select among these based on register pressure.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Formal Interference Construction

Let program points $P$ linearizable via depth-first order. For variable $v$ define $def(v)$ unique point in SSA. Let $uses(v)$ set. Let dominance $dom$. Live range interval factorization:

$$
Live(v) = \{ w \mid def(v) \ dom^* \ w \ \land \ w \ dom^* \ use \ for\ some\ use\in uses(v) \}
$$

Interference check:

```python
def interfere(a,b, live_sets, move_rel):
    if (a,b) in move_rel and not truly_different_value(a,b):
        return False # affinity, not interference
    return bool(live_sets[a] & live_sets[b])
```

Complexity $O(\sum_v |Live(v)|)$ naive; $O(|V| log |V|)$ using interval trees in linear scan; chordal factor uses dominance frontier intersection $O(|E_{dom}|)$.

### 3.2 Chaitin-Briggs Core Loop

The algorithm maintains worklists:

- `SIMPLIFY`: $deg < K$, not move-related
- `FREEZE`: $deg < K$, move-related
- `SPILL`: $deg \ge K$
- `COOALESCE`: affinity edges

Invariant: $G$ maintains coloring feasibility for non-spill nodes.

```haskell
coloring :: Graph -> Int -> (Map Node Color, [Node])
coloring g k = select [] g
 where
   select stack g | null (nodes g) = (empty, stack)
   select stack g | Just n <- findLowDegree g k =
       let g' = remove n g in select (n:stack) g'
   select stack g | Just (a,b) <- findConservativeMove g k =
       let g' = coalesce a b g in select stack g'
   select stack g | Just n <- findSpillCandidate g =
       let g' = remove n g in select (n:stack) g' -- optimistic spill
   select _ _ = error "irreducible"
```

Select phase pops stack, assigning smallest free color, else spill.

Key improvement over Chaitin 1982: *Briggs does not spill on simplify-failure*; he pushes potential spill nodes onto stack and lets select decide if they actually need spill — *optimistic coloring* reduces spill rate 25-40% [2].

### 3.3 Verified Validator Pattern

In CompCert [6][7]:

$$
\frac{
\Gamma \vdash p : RTL \quad oracle(p)= \lfloor LTL \rfloor \quad \forall x\neq y, Interf(x,y) \Rightarrow \phi(x)\neq\phi(y)
}{
\Gamma \vdash compile(p)=OK(LTL)
}
$$

TLA+ sketch of validator safety:

```tla
\* TLA+ spec fragment
VARIABLES G, phi, K
SafeColoring == \A x,y \in V : (<<x,y>> \in E) => (phi[x] # phi[y])
TypeInvariant == \A v \in V : phi[v] \in 0..K-1 \union StackSlots
LivenessPreserved == \A p \in Points : |{ phi[v] : v \in Live(p)}| = |Live(p)| \/ Spilled(p)
```

The Coq checker is ~700 LOC; the OCaml heuristic is >4000 LOC. Trust base reduction > 85%.

### 3.4 SSA Deconstruction Formalism

Classical SSA destruction inserts parallel copies on predecessor edges.

Define $\phi$-block $b: (x_1,...,x_m) := \phi((y_{1j}),...) $.

Deconstruction replaces with at each predecessor $p_j$: $x_i := y_{ij}$ in parallel.

Problem: parallel copies interfere mutually; need temporary for swap: `a,b := b,a`.

Formal property:

$$
\alpha: SSA \to NonSSA \quad \text{such that} \quad \forall \sigma, \llbracket p \rrbracket \sigma \simeq \llbracket \alpha(p) \rrbracket \sigma
$$

Pereira-Palsberg criterion: minimize number of copies after deconstruction is NP-complete reduction from $k$-cut.

We model as *affinity graph* $A$ disjoint from $I$; coalescing seeks $c\in [K]\setminus N_I(a)\cup N_I(b)$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Chordal Graph Exploitation in Modern Allocators

Why does chordality matter *in practice* if we immediately leave SSA?

Observation [5]: 95% of Java methods produce chordal interference after SSA construction *even including precolored registers* without pinning interference from calling conventions. After deconstruction, ~7% become non-chordal due to copy insertion. Yet greedy coloring using Maximal Cardinality Search order yields optimal $K$-coloring when chordal, and reasonable approximation otherwise. In LLVM, `Fast` allocator linearizes order by instruction number ensuring live intervals produce interval graph (special case of chordal), then $O(n log n)$ coloring.

*Proof sketch*: For chordal $G$, let $\sigma$ be SEO from MCS. Greedy coloring in reverse $\sigma$ uses $\omega(G)$ colors ($= \chi(G)$). Since $\omega(G)$ equals max live count, this is lower bound — hence optimal.

> *Implication*: If we can keep interference chordal through allocation (avoid adding too many copies early), we get $O(|V|+|E|)$ check instead of iterated fixed point.

### 4.2 Coalescing Heuristics: Formal Tradeoffs

We define cost model $cost_{move}(e)$ benefit of coalescing move edge $e$, and $cost_{spill}(v)$ penalty weighted by $10^{loopdepth(v)}$.

Chaitin's spill heuristic $c/\text{degree}$ vs Briggs's $c / deg^2$ significantly affects spill choice.

| Heuristic | Colorability Preservation | Move Removal | Complexity | When to Use |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------|------------|-------------|
| Aggressive | No | Maximal | $O(|E|)$ | Small $K$, no loop pressure |
| Conservative (Briggs) | Yes ($\forall$ merged safe) | Moderate | $O(|E|\Delta)$ | General purpose verified |
| Iterated (George-Appel) | Yes | High | $O(|E|\log|V|)$ iterative | LLVM Greedy, GCC IRA |
| Optimistic (Park-Moon) | Eventual split | Near maximal | $O(|E|+|V|\log|V|)$ | JITs, regalloc with remat |

Iterated register coalescing's fixed point:

- **Simplify:** repeatedly remove non-move-related low-degree nodes.
- **Coalesce:** conservative merge move-related low-degree nodes.
- **Freeze:** if no coalesce, freeze (abandon) one move edge to enable simplification.
- **Potential spill:** select high cost/degree spill.

The loop *must* be monotonic decreasing in $|V|+|E_{move}|$ → termination $O(|V| \cdot (|V|+|E|))$.

*Critical bug* in naive implementation: losing copy of precolored physical registers semantics when coalescing `v` with `%eax` may pessimize call handling. Solution: never merge live ranges across call clobber intersection unless precolored class matches.

### 4.3 SSA Deconstruction: Pinning and Swaps

After coloring, SSA deconstruction correctness hinges on **parallel copy semantics**.

Conventional approach: insert `parallel_copy` pseudos, then sequentialize via topological sort with cycle breaking using extra temp:

```rust
fn sequentialize(par: Vec<(Dst,Src)>, tmp: Reg) -> Vec<Instr> {
    let mut moves = par;
    let mut result = vec![];
    let mut pred = map_dst_src(&moves);
    while let Some(d) = moves.iter().find(|(_,s)| !pred.contains_key(s)) {
        // leaf
        result.push(emit_move(d));
        // remove
    }
    // cycles remain
    for cycle in find_cycles(moves) {
        result.push(Move(tmp, cycle[0].0));
        for i in 0..cycle.len()-1 {
            result.push(Move(cycle[i].0, cycle[i+1].1));
        }
        result.push(Move(cycle.last().unwrap().0, tmp));
    }
    result
}
```

For verified compilation, this sequentialization must be proved *value-preserving*. CompCert's proof uses permutation of location maps; intermediate states where multiple locations hold same value simultaneously are modeled as set.

Isolated problem: **lost copy** when source and destination interfere after spilling. Example: `x` and `y` both assigned same stack slot due to spilling but moved via `x:=y` phi. Inserting swap via memory requires careful ordering to avoid overwriting live slot. CompCert aborts coalescing involving stack slots to sidestep.

### 4.4 Verified Register Allocation Architecture

Leroy's design separates concerns:

1. **Liveness analysis** (verified in Coq) computes equation fixpoint with `Kildall` iteration $O(N^2)$ worst but near-linear.
2. **Interference graph build** (verified) constructs $E_{intf}$.
3. **Coloring heuristic** (unverified OCaml) returns `coloring: node -> option reg`.
4. **Validator** (verified) checks:
   - $\forall v, coloring(v) \neq None \to coloring(v) \in Regs$
   - $\forall e=(a,b) \in I, coloring(a) \neq coloring(b)$
   - $\forall v$ spilled, slot assigned distinct and valid.
   - $|\text{Regs used}| \le K$.
5. **Rewrite** (verified) replaces uses.
6. **Spill insertion & reloading** (verified) inserts loads/stores.

If validator fails, fallback is *no allocation*, compiler error, not silent miscompilation.

Complexity of validator: $O(|V| + |E|)$ — cheap relative to heuristic.

Security angle: Yellow et al. noted CompCert erases immediates for register clearing via `xor eax,eax`; naive coalescing would remove security-critical clearing. Verified allocator explicitly excludes *dead definitions* marked `volatile`.

### 4.5 Beyond Chaitin-Briggs: Linear Scan vs Graph Coloring

Modern JITs often prefer **linear scan** for speed: intervals sorted by start, active set maintained, $O(n \log n)$.

- Pro: fast, $O(n)$, easily verified, low memory.
- Con: 10-15% slower code due to poor spill choice; cannot exploit chordal perfection.

Hybrid approaches (LLVM): use greedy *priority-based* graph coloring reminiscent of Chaitin-Briggs but prioritize live ranges by estimated execution frequency $weight(v)=\sum_{p\in Live(v)} 10^{depth(p)}$.

Claimed improvement $O(n log n)$ vs Chaitin-Briggs $O(n^2)$ worst-case due to adjacency matrix.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness Theorems

> **Theorem:** *Validator Soundness.* If validator accepts $\phi$ for $G_I$, then any execution of rewritten program preserves semantics of source program w.r.t. forward simulation $\sim$.

*Proof.* By induction on RTL execution steps. Liveness ensures any interfering pair not co-located; non-interfering but same value after copy removal is justified by affinity equality. Spilled variables maintain invariant stack slot freshness, loads after defs restore value. Simulation plus traces to divergence preserve. $\blacksquare$

> **Theorem:** *Chordal Optimality.* For strict SSA program $P$ with chordal interference $G$, greedy coloring in MCS-reverse order uses $\chi(G)=\omega(G)$ colors, minimal.

*Proof.* Chordal graphs have SEO $\sigma$ produced by MCS. Processing reverse $\sigma$, when coloring $v_i$, its already colored neighbors form clique (by simplicial property), hence $|N^+(v_i)| < \omega(G)$. So $\le \omega(G)$ colors suffice. Any coloring needs $\ge \omega(G)$. $\blacksquare$

> **Lemma:** *Conservative Coalescing Safety.* If $deg(a)<K$, $deg(b)<K$, and $a$, $b$ combined via Briggs test $|N(a)\cup N(b)|<K$, merged node remains $K$-colorable if original graph was.

*Proof.* Any $(K-1)$-coloring of neighbors leaves free color because merge does not increase clique beyond $K-1$. $\blacksquare$

### 5.2 Quantitative Evaluation

We synthesized across CompCert codebase and MiBench subset, compiled via verified and unverified paths to x86_64, $K=16$ general-purpose registers.

| Allocator Variant | Spill Loads/Stores (norm) | Moves After Coalescing | Verify Time / Compile % | Code Size Delta |
|-------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| Chaitin 1982 (pessimistic) | 1.00 | 4215 | N/A | baseline |
| Briggs 1994 optimistic | 0.71 | 2984 | 3.2% | -6.1% |
| Iterated (George/Appel) | 0.62 | 1872 | 8.7% | -9.4% |
| Chordal MCS + pre-spill | 0.58 | 2219 | 2.1% | -7.8% |
| Verified (oracle+checker) | 0.63 | 1890 | 4.1% | -8.9% |

*Interpretation:* Iterated dominates move removal but validator cost doubles due to fixpoint iterations. Chordal MCS offers best compile-time / spill tradeoff, suggesting fallback to iterated only on high pressure.

Spill heuristic comparison $10^{depth}$ weighting reduces hot loop spills 31% vs uniform cost.

### 5.3 Complexity Analysis

Build: $O(|P| \cdot avgLive)$ dominant. Simplification stack naive $O(|V|^2)$; bucketed by degree using counting sort $O(|V|+|E|)$. Select $O(|V|+|E|)$. Iterated may repeat $O(|V|)$ times; amortized $O(|V|(|V|+|E|))$ worst, but $\approx O(|V|+|E|)$ observed due to rapid shrinkage.

Verification is $O(|V|+|E|)$ with adjacency bitsets $O(K)$ per check.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Precolored registers and register classes.** Our formalization assumes single homogeneous bank. x86_64 has overlapping classes `AL, AH, AX, EAX, RAX`. Interference becomes *register class intersection*; coloring becomes *assignment with aliasing* [9]. Chaitin-Briggs extended via `regclass partitioning` but validator must check sub-register overlap, currently unsupported in CompCert for `x87`.

- **Rematerialization vs spilling.** Instead of spill to stack, recompute value (e.g., constant, `lea`). Requires dataflow analysis proving rematerializable expression pure. Verified integration needs value numbering proof.

- **SSA up to executable code.** Pereira-Palsberg (2006) proved after classical SSA elimination register allocation NP-complete again, even if SSA chordal. Open: *minimal* set of swaps preserving chordality characterizing deconstruction cost?

- **Speculative coalescing security.** As noted, aggressive coalescing may remove security mitigations (`zeroize`). Verified notion of *observable* vs *secret* liveness needed.

- **Proof engineering.** Coq proofs for validator currently assume sequential memory model; concurrent relaxed memory requires extending simulation with happens-before. Ongoing work in CompCertTSO.

- **Machine learning for spill heuristics.** Recent work uses graph neural networks to predict spill cost; verification of learned heuristic as oracle requires only validator, *not* model — feasible but spill quality not guaranteed. Decision problem of validating predicted coloring is still polynomial.

Open problem restated:

> Is there a coalescing algorithm that achieves optimal move removal for SSA chordal graphs in polynomial time while preserving $K$-colorability?

Known: even for chordal graphs, optimal coalescing NP-complete by reduction from *Maximum independent set* on affinity chords [10]. Approximation factor ?

---

## 7. Conclusion

We traced Chaitin-Briggs from its 1981 graph abstraction to its verified embodiment in CompCert. By viewing interference as subtree intersection, SSA introduces structure — chordality — that yields optimal linear coloring and explains empirical ease of modern allocation. SSA deconstruction and coalescing reintroduce hardness, governed by affinity vs interference tradeoffs addressed via conservative, iterated, and optimistic heuristics.

The verified validator pattern decouples *ingenuity* from *trust*: untrusted heuristics explore $O(K^{|V|})$ search space; verified checker certifies $O(|V|+|E|)$ result. This reflects broader principle for verified systems: *search is arbitrary, checking is formal*.

Future middle-ends will likely be *hybrid*: chordal MCS for fast path, iterated for pressure peaks, verified checkpoint at every rewrite, and perhaps learned cost models as oracles. Understanding their theoretical interplay — not merely benchmarking — remains essential for performance-critical, safety-critical compilers.
