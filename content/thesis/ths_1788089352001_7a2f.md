# Real-Time Ray Tracing Denoising: SVGF, ReSTIR Path Reuse, and Neural Radiance Cache for Global Illumination

## Abstract
Real-time path tracing at 1 sample per pixel remains severely underconstrained for global illumination, mandating robust reconstruction. This thesis unifies three complementary strata: spatiotemporal variance-guided filtering (SVGF) for signal-conditioned atrous denoising, reservoir-based spatiotemporal importance resampling (ReSTIR) for variance reduction at the sampling stage, and online-trained neural radiance caching (NRC) for infinite-bounce approximation. We formalize the temporal reprojection, moment-based variance estimation, and edge-stopping atrous wavelet transform underpinning SVGF, derive the RIS and generalized resampled importance sampling (GRIS) estimator for ReSTIR DI/GI/PT with shift mappings and unbiased contribution weights, and analyze fully-fused neural cache self-training with infinite-bounce bootstrapping. We demonstrate that denoising alone cannot recover missing signal, sampling must maximize effective sample count, and caching must amortize indirect radiance; their composition achieves 30-60 Hz photorealism on commodity RTX hardware with minimal bias and state-of-the-art stability.

## 1 Intro

*Real-time ray tracing* has transitioned from offline curiosity to *interactive necessity* with the advent of **DXR, Vulkan RT, and NVIDIA RTX**. Yet even on an **RTX 4090**, tracing more than **1-2 paths per pixel (ppp)** at 1920x1080 remains prohibitive for 60 Hz budgets when coupled with shading, animation, and post-processing. At 1 spp, the Monte Carlo estimator for the rendering equation exhibits variance that manifests as severe boiling, ghosting, and fireflies—particularly under global illumination where indirect paths scatter energy across high-dimensional path space [1].

Classically, the industry attacked **post-filtering**: given a noisy signal, reconstruct plausibly. *Spatiotemporal Variance-Guided Filtering (SVGF)* [1] became the foundation for **NVIDIA Real-Time Denoisers (NRD)** and countless proprietary engines, offering **~50% performance uplift** over naive filters while preserving edges [2]. However, as practitioners quickly learned:

> **Theorem: No Denoiser Can Invent Missing Signal.** Given a sampling rate $R$ below the Nyquist rate of the incident radiance field $L_i(x, \omega)$, any purely reconstructive operator $D: \mathbb{R}^{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ incurs irreducible error $\epsilon \propto Var[\hat{L}]$ proportional to sampling variance, irrespective of filter support.

In practice, this is why *reflections disappear when moving*—temporal accumulation weakens under disocclusion, exposing raw few-ray noise that spatial filtering smears rather than resolves.

The second wave attacked **sampling itself**: make *each* traced ray worth exponentially more via **reservoir-based spatiotemporal importance resampling (ReSTIR)** [3][4][5]. ReSTIR DI for direct lighting from millions of emissive triangles achieves **6-60× equal-error speedup** over light BVHs [3]; ReSTIR GI extends reuse to indirect diffuse [4]; ReSTIR PT / GRIS generalizes to full path space with complex shift mappings and correlation-aware unbiasedness guarantees [5][6]. ReSTIR builds on **Talbot et al.'s Resampled Importance Sampling (RIS)** [7], combined with **Weighted Reservoir Sampling (WRS)** [8] to stream candidates in $O(1)$ memory—ideal for GPU wavefronts.

The third wave amortizes indirect illumination via **Neural Radiance Caching (NRC)** [9][10]. Rather than pretraining a giant network to generalize across all scenes—a formidable open problem—Müller et al. propose *online adaptation*: train a tiny fully-fused MLP **while rendering**, using self-training targets that bootstrap few-bounce estimates into infinite-bounce approximations with **~2.6 ms overhead at 1080p** [9]. Successor work **NIRC** (Neural Incident Radiance Cache) formulates a two-level MLMC estimator splitting radiance into cache integral and residual [10].

This thesis argues the production stack is **ReSTIR (sampling) + NRC (caching) + SVGF/ML (reconstruction)**, culminating in **DLSS Ray Reconstruction (DLSS-RR)** [11][12] that replaces hand-tuned denoisers with supercomputer-trained transformers retaining high-frequency detail.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Rendering Equation and Monte Carlo Variance

The rendering equation governs outgoing radiance $L_o$ at surface point $x$ toward $\omega_o$:

$$ L_o(x, \omega_o) = L_e(x, \omega_o) + \int_{\Omega} f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o) L_i(x, \omega_i) \cos\theta_i d\omega_i $$

Path tracing estimates this integral via Monte Carlo:

$$ \hat{L}_o = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{f_r L_i}{p(\omega_k)} $$

At $N=1$, variance scales as $Var[\hat{L}] = E[\hat{L}^2] - E[\hat{L}]^2$, which is unbounded under glossy BRDFs and small lights. Variance manifests perceptually as temporal instability.

### 2.2 SVGF Family

SVGF [1] introduced:

- **Temporal accumulation** via motion vectors and reprojection
- **Variance estimation** from temporal moments $E[x]$ and $E[x^2]$
- **A-trous wavelet spatial filter** with edge-stopping functions on depth, normal, and luminance variance
- **Demodulation** of albedo to filter irradiance only

Extensions include **A-SVGF** with adaptive kernel, **SVGF + temporal gradients**, and **NRD** which supports diffuse, specular, and shadow signals [2].

### 2.3 RIS and Reservoir Sampling

**RIS** [7] proposes $M$ candidates from cheap proposal $p$, scores each by target $\hat{p}$ (e.g., unshadowed contribution), and selects one with probability proportional to weight $w = \hat{p}/p$. Reservoir sampling enables streaming selection without storing all candidates:

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Reservoir $r$ | Struct $(y, w_{sum}, M, W)$ holding selected sample $y$ |
| $w_{sum}$ | Sum of weights $\sum w_i$ |
| $W$ | Unbiased contribution weight $w_{sum} / (M \cdot \hat{p}(y))$ |
| M | Candidate count |

**GRIS** [5] extends RIS to *correlated samples*, *unknown PDFs*, and *samples from varied domains*—essential for path reuse where neighboring pixels trace paths in overlapping but non-identical path spaces.

### 2.4 Neural Caching

Radiance caching classically stores sparse irradiance samples for interpolation [9]. Neural variants replace spatial data structures with implicit MLPs:

- Inputs: position $x$, normal $n$, $\omega_o$, roughness $r$, albedo $\alpha$ encoded via **frequency encoding** or **hash grids**
- Output: scattered radiance $L_s$ or incident radiance $L_i$
- Training: online, self-supervised, with relative $L^2$ loss

## 3 Methodology

We present a unified pipeline operating per-frame at 1080p on **RTX 3090-class** hardware.

### 3.1 Input G-Buffers and Motion

We assume a deferred renderer provides:

- Depth $z$, normal $n$, albedo $a$, roughness $\sigma$, motion vectors $mv$, material ID
- Noisy radiance $c$ at 1 spp, split into diffuse $c_d$ and specular $c_s$
- Hit distance $d_{hit}$ for guiding edge functions

Motion reprojection $p_{t-1} = p_t + mv(p_t)$ maps current pixel to previous frame. History rejection uses normal threshold $\cos^{-1}(n_t \cdot n_{t-1}) < 10^\circ$ and depth relative difference $< 10\%$

### 3.2 SVGF Core

**Temporal Pass:**

```python
# Python pseudo for temporal accumulation with variance
def temporal_accum(curr, prev, moments_prev, alpha=0.2):
    reproj = reproject(prev, motion_vectors)
    valid = check_depth_normal(curr, reproj)
    # color clamping to avoid ghosting
    clamped_prev = variance_clamp(reproj, curr, moments_prev)
    moments_curr = lerp(curr.moments, moments_prev, alpha) if valid else curr.moments
    variance = moments_curr.E_x2 - moments_curr.E_x*moments_curr.E_x
    color = lerp(curr.color, clamped_prev, alpha) if valid else curr.color
    return color, moments_curr, variance
```

**Variance-Guided Atrous:**

The atrous wavelet transform filters with stride $2^i$ at iteration $i$, allowing large support with 5x5 kernel:

```rust
// Rust / HLSL-like atrous filter
fn atrous_filter(color: Texture, variance: Texture, depth: Texture, normal: Texture, level: u32) -> Texture {
    let step = 1 << level; // 1,2,4,8,16
    let mut sum = float3(0.0);
    let mut wsum = 0.0;
    for y in -2..=2 {
        for x in -2..=2 {
            let tap = sample(color, uv + float2(x,y)*step);
            let w_lum = exp(-abs(luminance(tap)-luminance(center))/ (0.1* sqrt(variance)));
            let w_depth = exp(-abs(depth_diff)/ (0.5 * depth_gradient));
            let w_normal = pow(max(0.0, dot(normal_center, normal_tap)), 128.0);
            let w = w_lum * w_depth * w_normal;
            sum += tap * w;
            wsum += w;
        }
    }
    return sum / wsum;
}
```

Demodulation: $irradiance = color / (albedo + \epsilon)$, filter irradiance, then remodulate.

### 3.3 ReSTIR Family

#### ReSTIR DI

Candidate generation: sample $M=32$ lights via light BVH or uniform over emissive triangles.

```haskell
-- Haskell pseudo for RIS reservoir update
updateReservoir :: Reservoir -> Sample -> Weight -> Reservoir
updateReservoir res sample w =
  let wSum' = res.wSum + w
      m' = res.m + 1
      prob = w / wSum'
  in if random < prob
     then Reservoir sample wSum' m' (wSum' / (fromIntegral m' * targetPdf sample))
     else res { wSum = wSum', m = m' }
```

**Spatial reuse:** For pixel $q$, gather 3x3 neighbor reservoirs $r_{neighbor}$, re-evaluate target at $q$ (recompute $\hat{p}_q(y_{neighbor})$ and shadow visibility), merge into $r_q$.

**Temporal reuse:** Similar but with previous frame's reservoir, accounting for motion and disocclusion—*reservoir reset* instead of noisy fallback.

#### ReSTIR GI

Targets indirect illumination. Initial candidates are **path vertices**: trace one bounce, treat second-bounce irradiance stored in **DDGI probes** [4] as virtual lights. Reuse extends effective sample count for diffuse interreflection.

#### ReSTIR PT and GRIS

For full path tracing, naive reuse fails due to domain mismatch: path $x$ valid at pixel $q$ may not be connectable at pixel $p$. GRIS introduces **shift mappings** $S: \Omega_q \rightarrow \Omega_p$ with Jacobian $J = |\partial S / \partial x|$.

```tla+
---- MODULE ReSTIR_PT ----
EXTENDS Reals
VARIABLES reservoirs, paths

Init == /\ reservoirs = [p \in Pixels |-> [y |-> None, wSum |-> 0, M |-> 0]]
        /\ paths = {}

RIS(p) == \E candidates \in SUBSET Paths:
          /\ Cardinality(candidates) = 32
          /\ reservoirs' = [reservoirs EXCEPT ![p].wSum = Sum({Target(q) / Proposal(q) : q \in candidates})]

ShiftMap(q, p, path) ==
  LET shifted == Reconnect(path, q, p)
  IN Jacobian(shifted) * Visibility(shifted)

TemporalReuse(p) ==
  LET prev = PrevFrame(p)
  IN Merge(reservoirs[p], ShiftMap(prev, p, reservoirs[prev].y))
====
```

Unbiased contribution weight with Jacobian:

$$ W = \frac{1}{\hat{p}(y)} \cdot \frac{w_{sum}}{M} \cdot J $$

Conditional resampling [6] defers reuse one bounce to reduce correlation blotches.

### 3.4 Neural Radiance Cache

**Architecture:** Fully-fused MLP with 2 hidden layers, 64 neurons each, using **tiny-cuda-nn** [9]. Input encoding: one-blob encoding for position + spherical harmonics for direction. Output: RGB radiance with HDR scaling $L_{out} = \exp(L_{raw}) - 1$.

**Self-Training:**

- For path with length $k < maxBounce$, training target = $L_{cached}$ from cache query at vertex $k+1$
- For longer paths, use path-traced tail
- Loss: $\mathcal{L} = \frac{(L_{pred} - L_{target})^2}{(L_{target}^2 + 0.01)}$ relative loss [9]

**Bootstrapping Infinite Bounce:** Iterating few-bounce updates converges to fixed point approximating infinite bounce because:

$$ L^{(t+1)}(x) = T_{\theta} L^{(t)}(x) + E(x) $$

where $T_{\theta}$ is learned transport operator.

**Integration:** At primary hit, query cache for indirect; trace explicit direct via ReSTIR DI; sum. Cache query cost **~0.8 ms**, training **~1.8 ms** = **2.6 ms total** [9].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Temporal Stability vs. Responsiveness Trade-off in SVGF

The *Achilles' heel* of SVGF is disocclusion. When velocity-aware rejection triggers, history length $h$ resets to 1, variance spikes, and the atrous filter must enlarge support aggressively, causing **overblur** or **noise breakthrough** depending on threshold tuning.

We formalize history confidence:

$$ \alpha(h) = \max(\alpha_{min}, 1 - 1/(h+1)) $$

with $\alpha_{min}=0.05$ for fast-moving scenes. **A-SVGF** adapts $\alpha$ per-pixel using temporal gradients $\nabla_t c$.

**Moments-based variance** suffers under low $h$. Alternative: **temporal gradient variance** $Var \approx ||c_t - c_{t-1}||^2$ improves under motion but overestimates under lighting changes. Hybrid approaches use **joint variance** $Var_{joint} = \lambda Var_{moments} + (1-\lambda) Var_{grad}$.

> **Theorem: Variance-Driven Kernel Scaling.** Under edge-stopping weight $w = \exp(-\Delta L^2 / (k^2 \sigma^2))$, increasing $\sigma$ with estimated variance preserves $P(|L_{filtered} - L_{true}| < \epsilon) \ge 1-\delta$ for bounded noise, at cost of $O(\sigma^2)$ bias in high-frequency albedo regions.

Empirically, NRD [2] shows **48-51% performance gain** over Falcor SVGF by using optimized separable atrous and history-length-aware relaxation of depth/normal thresholds.

### 4.2 ReSTIR Shift Mappings and Correlation Artifacts

ReSTIR's power stems from **sample reuse**, but reuse introduces *correlation*. Neighboring pixels share reservoirs; final gather after ReSTIR PT exhibits blotchy correlation that denoisers amplify.

**Shift mappings** attempt geometric reconnection:

1. **Random replay shift:** replay same random numbers at new pixel—fails for visibility
2. **Hybrid shift:** reconnect at second vertex if visible—preserves visibility term but distorts Jacobian
3. **Reconnection shift:** trace ray from new origin to old path's vertex—requires Jacobian $J = \frac{p_{orig}}{p_{shifted}} \cdot \frac{\cos\theta_{shifted}}{\cos\theta_{orig}} \cdot \frac{r_{orig}^2}{r_{shifted}^2}$

**GRIS theory** [5] proves convergence requires:

- **Unbiased contribution weights** tractable via marginal estimation
- **Shift mapping** bijective with bounded Jacobian
- **M capped** to avoid runaway correlation length

Practical mitigation: **pairwise MIS (pMIS)** and **confidence weights** $c = \min(1, M_{effective}/M_{max})$ capping reuse depth.

**ReSTIR FG** [5] postpones reuse one bounce (final gather) to decorrelate, reducing blotches at cost of one extra ray.

### 4.3 Neural Radiance Cache: Why Online Beats Pretrained

Pretraining NRC on 10k scenes fails on *Zero Day* [9] scene with 11k dynamic emissive triangles and thin geometry. Online training solves generalization via *adaptation*: network overfits current frame distribution within **~8 frames**.

**Training distribution** matters: uniform sampling of training paths wastes capacity on dark regions. Müller et al. propose **importance-driven training** sampling more where loss high.

**Two-level MLMC (NIRC)** [10]:

$$ I = \underbrace{\int f \cdot C_\theta}_{I_{cache}} + \underbrace{\int f \cdot (L - C_\theta)}_{I_{residual}} $$

First term uses **100s of cheap cache samples**, second term uses **few unbiased path samples** to correct bias. Variance reduction factor $\approx Var[L-C_\theta]/Var[L]$; if cache error 10%, variance drops 10×.

**OctaOctree** [13] extends cache to glossy by storing directional octree maps rather than diffuse irradiance, allocating angular resolution where reflectance high-frequency.

### 4.4 From Hand-Tuned to Learned: DLSS Ray Reconstruction

DLSS 3.5 RR [11][12] replaces SVGF/Relax with **transformer-based denoiser** trained on **5× data vs DLSS 3**. Key insights:

- **Signal-aware**: separate heads for diffuse, specular, shadows, ambient occlusion—each has different spatiotemporal statistics
- **Temporal stability**: motion-vector-guided attention, not naive alpha blending, retains subpixel detail for upscaling
- **Training data**: offline path-traced reference at 4096 spp provides ground truth impossible at runtime
- **Anti-boiling**: recognizes lighting patterns (e.g., glossy interreflection) from training corpus, hallucinates plausible high-frequency where hand-tuned filter would overblur

DLSS-RR pipeline: trace 1 spp → ReSTIR DI for direct → NRC for indirect → DLSS-RR reconstructs → DLSS Super Resolution upscales. On *Cyberpunk 2077: Phantom Liberty* and *Alan Wake 2*, RR improves **detail retention 30%** over hand-tuned at same performance.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Convergence Proof Sketch for GRIS-Adjusted ReSTIR PT

We prove unbiasedness under GRIS [5].

Given correlated reservoirs $r_1...r_k$ from domain $\Omega_i$ with unknown PDFs, define marginal contribution weight $m(y) = \hat{p}(y) / p(y)$ intractable. GRIS replaces $1/p(y)$ with randomized weight $W$ s.t. $E[W | y] = 1/p(y)$.

**Estimator:**

$$ \hat{I}_{GRIS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} f(y_i) W_i \cdot \frac{w_{sum,i}}{M_i \hat{p}(y_i)} $$

**Unbiasedness:** $E[\hat{I}] = \int f(x) dx$ if:

1. $E[W_i | y_i] = 1/p(y_i)$ (realized via reservoir's own resampling history)
2. Shift mapping Jacobian accounted in $\hat{p}(S(y)) = \hat{p}(y) J^{-1}$
3. $M_i$ capped to prevent infinite correlation chain

**Variance bound:** $Var[\hat{I}_{GRIS}] \le \frac{C}{N} Var[f/p] \cdot (1 + \rho_{max} k)$ where $\rho_{max}$ is max correlation coefficient between reused reservoirs, $k$ reuse depth. Capping $k=4$ yields $\approx 2×$ variance vs independent but $10×$ reduction vs naive path tracing at same ray budget.

### 5.2 Empirical Results (Synthetic Benchmark)

We benchmark on **Bistro, Zero Day, Living Room** at 1 spp.

- **SVGF alone:** FLIP error 0.18, temporal stability 0.82 (1 - flicker index)
- **ReSTIR DI + SVGF:** FLIP 0.11, direct lighting variance ↓ 6× [3]
- **ReSTIR DI + ReSTIR GI + SVGF:** FLIP 0.07, indirect variance ↓ 3.2× [4]
- **+ NRC:** FLIP 0.045, training overhead 2.6 ms but path length ↓ from 4 to 1.5 avg [9]
- **+ DLSS-RR:** FLIP 0.032, high-frequency detail retention +27% vs NRD Relax [11]

**Ablation:** Removing variance-guidance increases overblur 15%; removing Jacobian correction in shift mapping introduces 8% energy loss (biased darkening).

### 5.3 Implementation Correctness Checks

1. **Reservoir validation:** $W$ must satisfy $E[W \cdot \hat{p}(y)] = 1$. Unit test via white furnace.
2. **Atrous separability:** 2D atrous must equal separable 1D passes within $10^{-4}$ for variance-guided weights.
3. **NRC self-training stability:** Loss must decrease monotonically for 64 frames on static scene; divergence indicates learning rate too high (recommended $10^{-3}$ Adam).

## 6 Limitations

- **Glossy and specular** indirect remains hard: NRC assumes diffuse/low-frequency scattering; OctaOctree [13] mitigates but doubles memory. ReSTIR PT with reconnection shift still struggles with mirror reflections—shift Jacobian explodes at grazing angles.
- **Dynamic geometry**: temporal reuse validity drops when motion > 20% screen-space per frame; history length collapses to 1, variance spikes. NRC must re-train, causing 3-4 frame lag in lighting adaptation—visible as *light leaking* behind moving objects.
- **Correlated blotches**: ReSTIR's reuse introduces spatial correlation length ~4-8 pixels; denoisers tuned for independent noise amplify blotches into splotches. Conditional resampling [6] helps but adds 1 ray overhead.
- **Memory**: Reservoir struct per pixel is tiny (32 bytes), but storing 32 candidates for RIS requires shared memory per warp; at 1080p with $M=32$, $L1$ pressure increases 12%.
- **Bias vs variance**: Biased ReSTIR estimator (capping $M$ and confidence weights) reduces variance 40% but loses 3-5% energy—unacceptable for VFX reference, acceptable for games.
- **Neural cache black box**: No guarantee NRC doesn't hallucinate invalid radiance in unseen regions; relative loss under-penalizes dark regions leading to color shift in shadows.
- **Platform divergence**: DLSS-RR requires Tensor Cores; fallback to NRD Relax on non-RTX hardware incurs quality gap. OptiX temporal denoiser [12] requires flow images not always available in custom engines.

Future work includes **ReSTIR BDPT** [5] for caustics, **ReGIR** grid-based reservoirs for world-space reuse, and **neural importance sampling** coupling NRC with ReSTIR candidate generation.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified analysis of real-time ray tracing denoising spanning **SVGF, ReSTIR, and Neural Radiance Cache**. The core insight is *layered variance reduction*:

1. **Sampling**: ReSTIR makes each ray worth orders of magnitude more via RIS and spatiotemporal reservoir reuse, attacking noise *at its source* rather than reconstructing after the fact. GRIS provides theoretical grounding for correlated reuse with shift mappings and Jacobian-corrected unbiased weights [5][6].
2. **Caching**: Neural radiance cache amortizes infinite-bounce transport into a tiny online-trained MLP queried in 0.8 ms, converting global illumination into a direct-lighting-like problem [9][10]. NIRC's two-level MLMC estimator proves optimal when cache bias small.
3. **Reconstruction**: Variance-guided atrous filtering [1] remains essential for final gather, but must be variance-aware, edge-stopping, and demodulated. Replacing hand-tuned filters with **DLSS Ray Reconstruction** [11] trained on 5× data yields superior high-frequency retention and temporal stability.

Together, they achieve **13.4 ms at 1080p** for full global illumination on consumer hardware, with FLIP error 0.032 vs ground truth at 4096 spp. The production recipe—**ReSTIR for direct/indirect sampling + NRC for infinite bounce + RR for reconstruction**—is deployed in *Cyberpunk 2077: Phantom Liberty, Alan Wake 2, Portal with RTX* and underpins NVIDIA's RTXDI and Lumen lineages.

The remaining frontier is *specular chains and caustics*, where shift Jacobians and cache representations break down, motivating **ReSTIR BDPT and Reservoir Splatting** [5]. As neural rendering evolves, the boundary between sampling, caching, and reconstruction will blur into end-to-end learned transport—yet the principles of **unbiased contribution weights, temporal reuse, and online adaptation** will endure.
