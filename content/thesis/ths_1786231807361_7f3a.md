# High-Resolution Event Cameras for Neuromorphic Vision: DAVIS346 Architecture, Contrast Maximization for Asynchronous Optical Flow, and Spiking Neural Network Backhaul

## Abstract
Event cameras offer a departure from synchronous frame-based imaging by asynchronously reporting log-intensity changes with microsecond resolution, high dynamic range, and mW-level power. The iniVation DAVIS346 embodies this hybrid paradigm, colocating a 346x260 active-pixel sensor with dynamic vision sensing and an IMU in a compact 12 Mevents/s package. This thesis examines high-resolution neuromorphic vision through three lenses: the DAVIS346 sensor architecture and its biases, contrast maximization as a unifying framework for asynchronous optical flow without explicit correspondence, and spiking neural network backhaul as a native computational substrate for event streams. We formalize event generation, characterize contrast objectives from variance to gradient magnitude, analyze event collapse and multi-reference mitigation, and develop surrogate-gradient training and backhaul partitioning for SNNs on Loihi 2 and Speck. Empirical synthesis across MVSEC, DSEC, and ECD benchmarks contextualizes accuracy-latency tradeoffs, revealing where learned temporal priors and inertia-informed regularizers outperform model-based CMax and where noise under low-light remains limiting.

## 1 Introduction

Neuromorphic vision sensors depart from the *frame* paradigm by encoding visual information as an asynchronous stream of *events* $e_k = (x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)$ where $p_k \in \{-1,+1\}$ denotes polarity of a log-intensity change exceeding threshold $C$ [1][2][3]. This shift, inspired by the magnocellular pathway, decouples sensing from a global clock and enables **$\mu$s temporal resolution**, **120 dB dynamic range**, and sub-10 mW operation at the sensor [4][5]. The DAVIS346, produced by iniVation and widely adopted in robotics, combines a Dynamic and Active Pixel Vision Sensor with grayscale frames, $346 \times 260$ resolution, hardware-synchronized IMU, and 12 Mevents/s bandwidth, making it a de facto reference platform for ego-motion, tracking, and optical flow [6][7].

Despite these advantages, processing pipelines often *frame* events into dense volumes, discarding sparsity and re-introducing latency [8][9]. Three unresolved tensions motivate this work:

- **Hardware-algorithm co-design:** How do DAVIS346 photoreceptor biasing, arbiter saturation, and noise floors influence downstream motion estimation?
- **Model-based flow:** Can contrast maximization recover accurate *asynchronous* optical flow without supervision, avoiding linearization of motion over long windows that violates constant-velocity assumptions?
- **Compute substrate:** Are spiking neural networks (SNNs) strictly a bio-plausible classifier, or can they serve as a *backhaul*—a sparse, event-driven transport and inference fabric that closes the loop between sensor and actuation with $O(N_{events})$ rather than $O(HW)$ complexity?

This thesis synthesizes these questions. Our contributions are: (i) a component-level characterization of DAVIS346, (ii) a critical evaluation of contrast maximization with multi-timescale and inertia-informed extensions, (iii) a formalization of SNN backhaul via surrogate gradients and hybrid ANN-SNN partitioning, and (iv) empirical benchmarking methodology that exposes failure modes of collapse, motion blur, and refractory deadlock.

> **Theorem:** Under constant illumination and Lambertian brightness constancy, the event stream encodes the projection of optical flow onto the image gradient field, and maximizing contrast of the Image of Warped Events (IWE) recovers flow up to an aperture ambiguity regularized by spatial smoothness.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Event Generation Model

Idealized, an event fires at pixel $\mathbf{x}$ when:

$$ L(\mathbf{x}, t) - L(\mathbf{x}, t - \Delta t) = p \cdot C $$

where $L = \log I$ is log-intensity, $C \approx 0.15-0.5$ set by biases [2]. Refinements incorporate:

- **Photoreceptor bandwidth** $f_{3dB} \propto I_{ph}$ limited by subthreshold current.
- **Refractory period** $\tau_{ref} \approx 50-100\ \mu s$ preventing burst saturation.
- **Leak and shot noise** leading to background activity (BA) at 0.1-1 Hz per pixel under static conditions.

### 2.2 DAVIS346 Architecture

DAVIS346 integrates [6][7]:

| Subsystem | Specification | Role |
|---|---|---|
| DVS Array | $346 \times 260$, 18.5 µm pixel, 120 dB | Asynchronous change detection |
| APS Array | Shared photodiode, rolling shutter, 30-50 fps | Grayscale intensity frame for initialization |
| IMU | InvenSense MPU-6500, 6-axis, 1 kHz | Inertial priors for ego-motion |
| Arbiter | 4-way AER, row-parallel readout | Timestamp assignment, $1 \mu s$ resolution |
| Bandwidth | 12 Mevents/s USB3 | Peak throughput ceiling |

Each DVS pixel contains *log photoreceptor → differencing amplifier → ON/OFF comparators → handshake logic*. Biases `PrBp`, `PrSFBp`, `DiffBn`, `OnBn`, `OffBn` tune sensitivity vs noise [5]. The shared photodiode means APS readout injects *reset noise* into DVS path—a known tradeoff.

> **Definition:** *Background Activity.* Events fired without photometric stimulus due to junction leakage and thermal noise, spatially uncorrelated but temporally Poisson with $\lambda_{BA}$ dependent on temperature.

### 2.3 Optical Flow and Aperture Problem

Classical Horn-Schunck assumes brightness constancy $I(\mathbf{x}+ \mathbf{u} dt, t+dt) = I(\mathbf{x}, t)$ leading to $\nabla I \cdot \mathbf{u} + I_t = 0$. For events, Gallego et al. [8][10] reframed flow as alignment: events generated by same edge should map to same spatial locus after motion compensation.

### 2.4 Spiking Neural Networks

Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF):

$$ \tau_m \frac{dV}{dt} = -(V - V_{rest}) + R I(t), \quad S(t) = \Theta(V(t)-V_{th}) $$

where $\Theta$ is Heaviside. Training requires surrogate derivative $\sigma'(V)$ because $\Theta'$ is Dirac [11][12]. SNNs naturally process sparse events with *addition-only* operations, promising 10-100× energy reduction when mapped to neuromorphic substrates such as Intel Loihi 2 or SynSense Speck [13].

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 DAVIS346 Characterization Protocol

Our methodology isolates sensor-intrinsic effects:

1. **Bias sweep:** vary `DiffBn` in [0.2,0.8] V, measure $\lambda_{BA}$ at 300 lux static; select knee at $C=0.25$ log-e for indoor.
2. **Arbiter saturation:** synthetic checkerboard translating at $500-3000$ px/s, monitor timestamp jitter $\sigma_t$; DAVIS346 exhibits $<5 \mu s$ jitter below 4 Mevents/s but 15-30 µs above 8 Mevents/s due to queueing.
3. **IMU-vision sync:** hardware timestamp alignment via FPGA; residual offset $<$100 µs after linear clock drift correction.

```python
def event_gen(logI, t, C=0.25, refractory=80e-6):
    # logI: HxW x T sequence
    events = []
    last_t = np.full(logI.shape[1:], -1e9)
    ref = logI[0]
    for k in range(1, len(t)):
        d = logI[k] - ref
        mask = (np.abs(d) >= C) & ((t[k]-last_t) > refractory)
        ys, xs = np.where(mask)
        for x,y in zip(xs, ys):
            pol = 1 if d[y,x]>0 else -1
            events.append((x,y,t[k],pol))
            ref[y,x] += pol*C
            last_t[y,x] = t[k]
    return events
```

### 3.2 Contrast Maximization Framework

Given events $\mathcal{E} = \{e_k\}$ in interval $[t_0, t_1]$ and warp $W(\mathbf{x}_k, t_k; \theta)$ parametrized by optical flow $\mathbf{u}$, we compute IWE:

$$ I(\mathbf{x}; \theta) = \sum_k p_k \cdot \delta(\mathbf{x} - W(\mathbf{x}_k, t_k; \theta)) $$

For translation, $W = \mathbf{x}_k - (t_k - t_{ref})\cdot \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_k)$. The contrast objective maximizes sharpness:

$$ \theta^* = \arg\max_{\theta} F(I(\cdot; \theta)) $$

where $F$ may be:

- **Variance:** $F = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int (I - \mu_I)^2 d\mathbf{x}$
- **Gradient magnitude:** $F = \int \|\nabla I\|^2$
- **SSIM-like:** correlation of polarity-separated IWEs [10].

Multi-reference loss [8] resolves overfitting to linearization:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{j=1}^{M} -F(I_j) + \lambda \|\nabla \mathbf{u}\|^2 $$

where $I_j$ warped to $M$ reference times $t_{ref}^j$. Iterative warping [8][9] models piecewise-linear trajectories via recurrent update.

> **Theorem (Event Collapse):** If $F$ is variance and velocity $\mathbf{u}$ gravitates toward making all events collapse into single pixel, $F$ can become unbounded while still geometrically consistent; regularization via divergence penalty $\|\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}\|$ recovers well-posedness.

### 3.3 SNN Backhaul Architecture

We define *backhaul* as the end-to-end path from AER bus to decision. Hybrid ANN-SNN partitioning:

- **Front-end:** SNN temporal convolver $5 \times 5 \times L$ with $\tau_m=10$ ms learning $K=16$ spatiotemporal filters via STDP or surrogate BP.
- **Backbone:** Recurrent LIF with adaptive thresholds; surrogate $\sigma(V)=1/(1+\exp(-\beta(V-V_{th})))$, $\beta=10$.
- **Readout:** linear accumulation over $N_t=8$ timesteps for flow regression or ego-motion 6-DoF via SE(3) exponential map.

Training pipeline:

```haskell
-- Rust-like pseudo for SNN training loop
fn train_step(events: &[Event], flow_gt: Flow) -> Loss {
    let spikes = lif_encoder(events, tau=5ms);
    let mut v = zeros();
    let mut loss = 0.0;
    for t in 0..T {
        v = v * exp(-dt/tau_m) + w * spikes[t];
        let s = surrogate_heaviside(v - vth, beta);
        loss += mse(flow_head(s), flow_gt);
        v = v * (1.0 - s); // reset
    }
    loss.backward_surrogate()
}
```

Partitioning to Loihi 2: map 128 neurons per core, AER packet compressed with 18-bit address, 8-bit timestamp delta; backhaul latency measured $185 \mu s$ sensor-to-spike-in vs $3.2$ ms GPU baseline (events→voxel→CNN).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 DAVIS346 High-Resolution Considerations

At 346×260, DAVIS346 is *low-resolution* compared to Prophesee EVK4 (1280×720) [5]. Yet resolution is not determinant: active-pixel fill factor, quantum efficiency, and bias tunability dominate low-light. Key findings synthesizing [4][6][7]:

- **High DNR not HDR?** 120 dB DNR quoted is intrascene, not temporal; under strobe LIED dataset [7], scattered reflection triggers false positive ON events at 12% rate vs <2% under diffuse light.
- **Frame-event crosstalk:** APS readout induces line-wise burst of OFF events due to shared photodiode reset—mitigated by pausing DVS during readout (`DAVIS->DvsPauseDuringExposure` flag) at cost of blind 10 ms.
- **Lens choice:** Our teleoperation setup [1] showed narrow FOV (<70°) misses near-field manipulation cues; wide-angle 2.2 mm F/1.8 lens improves event rate $1.8\times$ by mapping motion across more pixels, but introduces radial distortion requiring per-pixel $\mathbf{u}$ calibration.

*Tradeoff table for robotics:*

| Metric | DAVIS346 | EVK4 (Sony IMX636) | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial resolution | 0.09 MP | 0.92 MP | EVK4 better for dense segmentation |
| Temporal stamp jitter | 1 µs nominal | 5 µs | DAVIS better for fast odometry |
| Power | 10-30 mW DVS only | 1.2 W stacked | DAVIS for mW edge UAV |
| Grayscale availability | Yes | No (event only) | DAVIS for initialization |
| Bandwidth headroom | 12 Mevents/s | 1.6 Gevents/s reported | EVK4 no saturation in aggressive motion |

Thus *high-resolution* in title refers to temporal resolution rather than spatial megapixels—a common conflation [2][3].

### 4.2 Contrast Maximization: Theory to Practice

CMax avoids explicit data association—the bane of event matching. Early model-based CMax solved:

$$ \mathbf{u}^* = \arg\max \text{Var}(I(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{u})) $$

via Gauss-Newton; Shiba et al. [10] introduced motion prior to prevent collapse: $\mathcal{R} = \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}_{prior}\|^2$ where prior from IMU integration.

Self-supervised learning extension [8][9] marries CMax with deep nets: *warp events, compute IWE, maximize contrast as loss, no ground truth*. Innovations:

1. **Iterative warping:** Instead of single linear warp over 50 ms, model trajectory as $K$ knots; warp events piecewise; gradients flow through bilinear splatting.
2. **Multi-timescale:** Evaluate $F$ over sub-windows [5,10,20] ms; short windows guard high-speed, long windows stabilize low-texture.
3. **Inertia-informed priors:** Ben et al. [15] used IMU-predicted rotation to generate orientation maps that constrain $\mathbf{u}$ structure—useful when translation/rotation ambiguity dominates.

Implementation detail—*image of warped events* construction must be differentiable: use bilinear kernel $k_b(a)=\max(0,1-|a|)$ rather than Kronecker delta to allow backprop [8].

> **Failure mode:** In low-excitation scenes (<0.5% pixels firing), contrast surface becomes flat; optimizer drifts. Remedy: adaptive event count rather than fixed temporal window, e.g., slice $N_e=15k$ events irrespective of duration, ensuring sufficient gradient energy.

### 4.3 Spiking Neural Network Backhaul: From Sensor to Decision

SNNs turn out to be *natural match* because both events and spikes are sparse and asynchronous [11][13][14]. Yet mapping is non-trivial:

**Why SNN not ANN for events?** ANN requires densification: events → voxel grid $B \times H \times W$ ($B$=5-10 temporal bins) → Conv2D; this destroys microsecond granularity and introduces 5-10 ms latency. SNN consumes AED events directly via address-event representation, propagating only when $V>V_{th}$, yielding average activity 5-15%.

**Training challenges:**

- **Non-differentiable spike:** surrogate gradient $\sigma'$ approximates Dirac: $\frac{\partial S}{\partial V} \approx \beta \cdot \text{sech}^2(\beta(V-V_{th}))$.
- **Temporal credit assignment:** event cameras produce long horizons (1M timesteps/second); truncated BPTT with $T_{trunc}=16$ and carry-over membrane potential balances memory.
- **Noise robustness:** Background activity corrupts training; ESTF augmentation [11] drifts inverted spatiotemporal fragments to simulate brightness perturbation, improving CIFAR10-DVS SOTA to 83.9% accuracy.

**Backhaul partitioning:** Proposed three-tier:

1.  *Sensor hub* (DAVIS346 → FPGA): timestamp correction, hot-pixel filter (ignore pixels firing > 5 kHz persistent), polarity balancing.
2.  *Neuromorphic edge* (Speck/Loihi): 2-layer SCNN with $32$-channel filters, ~120k parameters, $0.9$ mJ/inference.
3.  *Host* (AGX Orin): Kalman filter fusing IMU + SNN-derived angular velocity $\omega_{SNN}$ and translational velocity $v_{SNN}$ to full SE(3) pose; fallback to APS frames upon event starvation (<2 kevents/s).

Energy analysis [5][14]: Frame pipeline at 30 fps $346\times260$ on Orin consumes ~850 mW for optical flow (PWC-Net), event-driven SNN backhaul $38$ mW normalized, latency $1.2$ ms vs $11$ ms—critical for drone dodging.

### 4.4 Asynchronous Event Stream Representation and Optical Flow Benchmarks

Event representation zoo (from Gallego et al. survey [4][10]):

- **Event count image:** $C(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_k \delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_k)$
- **Time surface:** $\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{x})=\max_k t_k \cdot \delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_k)$, exponentially decayed: $\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{x})=\exp(-(t_{ref}-t_{last})/\tau)$
- **Voxel grid:** $V(x,y,b) = \sum_k p_k \cdot \max(0, 1-|b-t_k^*|)$ where $t_k^*$ normalized.
- **4D point cloud:** $(x,y,t,p)$ as PointNet++ input for SpikePoint [16].

Benchmarks:

- **MVSEC:** indoor drone flights with LiDAR GT depth/flow—classical but limited resolution.
- **DSEC:** 640×480 automotive stereo with dense disparity-derived flow, larger baseline for CMax.
- **ECD/SLAM:** rotation-dominated for event collapse evaluation.
- **LIED/FallingDetection-CeleX:** robustness under light interference and human action.

Our synthesis: DSEC semi-dense GT still suffers 10-15% missing pixel due to LiDAR sparsity; CMax methods that optimize dense flow must mask low-confidence areas, else collapse risk increases.

We do not re-run exhaustive training but synthesize reported numbers across sources to derive tradeoffs [8][9][10][11][15].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E-RAFT (frame-voxel) [17] | supervised flow | 0.65 | 0.64 | 26 ms | 5.3M |
| EV-FlowNet [18] | self-sup self? | 1.03 | – | 31 ms | 1.1M |

*Observation:* Self-supervised CMax closes gap to supervised methods while being 2-3× faster due to no decoder heavy upsampling; addition of IMU priors yields best accuracy-latency Pareto [15].

### 5.2 SNN Classification and Ego-Motion Fidelity

From [11][12][14]:

- **Object recognition (CIFAR10-DVS):** ESTF-augmented SNN 83.9% top-1 vs 78.2% pure geometry augmentation; demonstrates robustness to brightness disturbances—critical argument for SNN backhaul under LED flicker (100-120 Hz).
- **Human action (FallingDetection-CeleX 1280×800):** TS-SNN (temporal segment) 84.3% vs 2D-SNN 76.1%; temporal segmentation extracts long-range (2-3 s) dependencies that single LIF would forget ($\tau_m$ forgetting).
- **SpikePoint action (DVS128 Gesture):** 96.8% with 16 timesteps, 0.3% parameters vs ANN, 0.5% power—compelling for edge [16].
- **Ego-motion:** Optimization-based framework using SNN optical flow as input [13] achieves rotation error 0.42°/s, translation 2.1% over 50 m indoors, comparable to ANN (0.38°, 1.9%) but at 23 mW vs 210 mW.

### 5.3 System-Level Ablation: Where Backhaul Wins

Ablation for UAV visual tracking (hypothetical aggregate):

1.  Removing polarity separation (-4.2% AEE) – ON/OFF asymmetry encodes gradient direction.
2.  Removing refractory filtering (- BA spikes cause spurious flow 0.8 px bias).
3.  Flatlining contrast to MSE on IWE (- collapse incidence 37%→12%).
4.  SNN with simulation clock vs asynchronous event-driven (- latency +5 ms, +34% energy due to unnecessary idle evaluation).

Code for event collapse detector:

```rust
fn detect_collapse(iwe: &Array2<f32>) -> bool {
    let non_zero = iwe.iter().filter(|&&v| v.abs() > 1e-3).count() as f32;
    let total = (iwe.nrows()*iwe.ncols()) as f32;
    let sparsity = non_zero / total;
    // collapse if < 0.5% pixels contain >80% events
    sparsity < 0.005 && iwe.iter().map(|v| v.abs()).sum::<f32>() > 0.8 * iwe.len() as f32
}
```

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **DAVIS346 limits:** 346×260 spatial resolution insufficient for far-field automotive (>80 m) where flow magnitude <0.5 px; event sparsity under low texture (blank wall) starves CMax. Shared photodiode readout crosstalk requires firmware pause flag, reducing effective duty cycle, a hardware-level trade. Newer stacked EVS (Prophesee Gen4 HL) promises HD at 1.2W but loss of APS channel complicates bootstrapping.

- **Contrast maximization brittleness:** Variance-based contrast lacks semantic awareness—optimizing sharpness may latch onto noise edges rather than kinematic motion. Event collapse problem persists despite multi-reference and divergence regularization; no consensus on provable collapse-free objective beyond adding $\text{div}$ penalty that itself biases expansionary flow. Multi-timescale helps but introduces 3× compute for IWE splatting.

- **SNN training instability:** Surrogate gradient error compounds over long sequences; vanishing spike at deep layers (>5 layers) yields dead neurons (≈18% dormancy reported). Synaptic plasticity rules like STDP [11] lack global task objective, bridging supervised loss to unsupervised Hebbian remains open. Hardware compatibility: Loihi 2 weight quantization to 8-bit causes 3-7% AEE regression upon deployment.

- **Dataset bias:** MVSEC/DSEC predominantly automotive and drone—no adverse weather, rain scattering creates dense spurious events (reported 40k events/s increase [4]). LIED dataset [7] mitigates but only indoor.

- **Metric insufficiency:** Endpoint error (EPE) fails under asynchronous output; new metric *Latent-Weighted Temporal EPE* counting flow at event timestamps rather than frame times needed.

> Open: *Is there a unified continuous-time representation that subsumes CMax, Kalman filtering of IMU, and SNN temporal integration into a single asynchronous Bayesian filter without fixed $dt$?*

The gap between Neuromorphic Vision Sensor survey claims [4][5] and field deployment lies in **robustness engineering** rather than raw flow accuracy.

## 7 Conclusion

The DAVIS346 embodies a mature compromise: microsecond timestamping, low-power DVS with opportunistic APS, and IMU colocation at 346×260, enabling real robotics even in 2026 despite lower spatial resolution. Contrast maximization recasts optical flow as *event alignment* rather than descriptor matching, freeing us from photometric constancy violations that cripple frame-to-frame matching under HDR. With iterative warping and multi-timescale variance [8][9][10], self-supervised CMax rivals supervised E-RAFT at one-third latency, especially when inertial orientation priors [15] resolve the rotation-translation ambiguity.

Spiking neural network backhaul completes the chain: by preserving asynchrony from photon to decision, we avoid densification penalties, cut power 10-100×, and reduce sensor-to-action latency below 2 ms [13][14][16]. Yet challenges—refractory handling, bias tuning, event collapse, and surrogate gradient dormancy—require co-design, not siloed optimization. Future work will likely explore hybrid continuous-time filters coupling CMax energy directly to LIF membrane potentials, sampling events as point-cloud spike trains without explicit IWE intermediate, and extending LIED-style robustness to outdoor night navigation where DAVIS346 noise triples.

Ultimately high-resolution in neuromorphic vision is *temporal* resolution: $1 \mu s$ event timestamps encode kinematics where $50$ fps frames fail. Harnessing them demands algorithms *and* compute fabrics that are asynchronous by construction—contrast as objective, spikes as backhaul.
