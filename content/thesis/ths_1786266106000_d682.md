# Silicon Photonic Interconnects for Distributed Training: Co-Packaged Optics, Microring Resonator Switching, AWGR Routing, and DWDM Crosstalk Mitigation **ID:** `ths_1786266106000_d682` — **Author:** anon#d682 — **Type:** PhD Thesis — **Timestamp:** 1786266106000 ## Abstract The scaling of large-language-model training to 10k–128k GPUs has exposed electrical interconnect as the dominant energy and tail-latency bottleneck. A 400k-GPU fabric with pluggable OSFP 800G optics dissipates ~72 MW in optical + DSP power, limited by 22 dB channel loss traversing front-panel retimers. This thesis presents a vertically integrated silicon-photonic interconnect architecture for distributed training that couples (i) co-packaged optics on 2.5D SiON/Si interposers, (ii) micro-ring resonator switching fabrics with sub-microsecond reconfiguration, (iii) passive AWGR wavelength routing for contention-free all-to-all, and (iv) dense-WDM crosstalk mitigation via MRR pre-filtering and inverse-designed crossings. We build on NVIDIA Quantum-X/Spectrum-X photonics [4][6], Flex-SiPAC flexible bandwidth steering [1], heterogeneous CPO integration pathways [2][3], MRR router design [7][8], and low-crosstalk AWG routers [9][10]. Contributions include a formal model of cyclic AWGR routing as Latin square, non-blocking condition for MRR Clos under thermal crosstalk, and a validated BER-penalty model showing -26.8 dB side-lobe after MRR integration vs -16.3 dB conventional [10]. System-level simulations on 4-GPU and large-scale collective simulations demonstrate 26–29% communication time reduction and 23% job completion time improvement over leaf-spine baselines [1]. --- ## 1. Introduction > **Theorem (Photonic Scaling Wall):** *For GPU count G ≥ 10⁴, electrical channel loss L_e ≈ 22 dB at 224 Gb/s/lane requires DSP per port → power scales as P ∝ G·log G, exceeding power budget before bandwidth ceiling, necessitating CPO.* Distributed training communication is dominated by collectives: AllReduce for data-parallel gradient synchronization, AllToAll for MoE expert routing, and ReduceScatter for ZeRO sharding. Prior electrical fat-trees achieve full bisection but at cost of 655k discrete transceivers in a 128k-GPU DC — each a failure point [5][6]. CPO eliminates faceplate traversal, reducing channel loss 22 dB → 4 dB [4] and removing DSP retimers, cutting latency <100 ns port-to-port with SHARP v4 in-network aggregation 9× improved [7]. We answer: - **Q1**: How to co-package silicon photonics engines with switch ASICs at 51.2–115.2 Tb/s while preserving signal integrity and serviceability? - **Q2**: Can microring resonators replace power-hungry Mach-Zehnder switches for nanosecond reconfigurable bandwidth steering? - **Q3**: Does AWGR passive routing provide deterministic all-to-all without central arbiter, and at what crosstalk cost for 0.8 nm DWDM? - **Q4**: What mitigation restores BER <1e-12 under dense 8λ/16λ per fiber CPO? **Contributions:** 1. Background ### 2.1 Co-Packaged Optics for AI Factories CPO integrates photonic integrated circuits (PICs) directly within or close to electronic IC (EIC) packages, reducing electrical interconnect to sub-mm via copper pillar + TMV vs TSV [2]. NVIDIA's path commercialized with TSMC: Quantum-X800 supports 144 ports 800 Gb/s = 115.2 Tb/s non-blocking in 4U, 18 removable external laser source modules (1 per 8 ports), 324 optical connections, 48V DC busbar, liquid dual-loop [6]. Spectrum-X variants: SN6810 128×800G (102.4 Tb/s), SN6800 512×800G (409.6 Tb/s) [6]. Key metrics vs pluggable: | Metric | Pluggable OSFP | CPO (Quantum-X) | Δ | |--------|---------------|-----------------|---| | Laser count / port | 1 EML per module | Shared CW 2W vs 10W EML | 4× fewer [8] | | Optical interconnect power @400k GPU | 72 MW | 21.6 MW | 3.5× ↓ [6] | | Signal integrity loss | 22 dB | 4 dB | 63× ↑ [6] | | Resiliency / failure points | 655k transceivers @128k GPU | 5× sustained runtime | 10× [6] | | Deployment time | Baseline | 1.3× faster | [6] | TSMC Si photonics + SoIC 3D stacking enables million-GPU AI factories [6]. Heterogeneous integration survey [2] enumerates FOWLP, TSV, TGV, femtosecond laser direct-write, ion-exchange glass waveguides. Micro-ring modulators offer compact 5 µm radius, 30 dB out-of-band rejection, finesse ~285 [3] but suffer thermal crosstalk. ### 2.2 Microring Resonator Switching MRR exploits resonant enhancement condition: $m\lambda_{res}=2\pi R n_{eff}$. Thermo-optic (TO) or electro-optic (EO) tuning shifts $n_{eff}$ → $\Delta\lambda$ ~ 0.1 nm/mW. Yu et al. 2016 design [7] proposes 16 MRRs, 14 crossings, 4 90° bends for 5×5 non-blocking router. All rings operate at same wavelength → scalable to any network size [7]. Compared to prior 5×5 switching router, footprint, loss, power lower. Degradation index introduced [13] characterizes asymmetric loss of MRR vs straight waveguide. Work [14] proposes high-speed non-volatile memresonator: III-V/Si MRR with metal-oxide memristor tuning phase → retention 12h, endurance 1000 cycles, SET 300 ps 0.15 pJ, RESET 900 ps 0.36 pJ [14]. Enables weight storage in photonic neural nets without external memory fetch — relevant to in-network SHARP aggregation. Analysis from transponder aggregator [15]: residual dropped/added crosstalk evaluated via transfer matrix method. Bandwidth, insertion loss, side-lobe suppression, adjacent rejection, group dispersion critical at 100 Gb/s DP-QPSK. ### 2.3 AWGR and DWDM AWGR implements wavelength routing function $R(i,j) = (i+j) \mod N$ — cyclic property. Single passive device provides $N^2$ virtual connections via $N$ wavelengths. Si nanowire AWG routers demonstrated [9] with 130 nm CMOS. Comprehensive optimization vs single-technique optimization shows comprehensive wins. Functionality includes cyclic rotation verified, eye diagrams under multi-wavelength high-speed launch show low power penalty due to low crosstalk [9]. DWDM targeting 0.8 nm (≈100 GHz) channel spacing at 1550 nm supports 8λ/16λ per fiber NVIDIA SCALE [5]. Dense spacing increases coherent crosstalk: $XT = 10\log_{10}(P_{adj}/P_{sig})$. Conventional AWG side-lobe -16.3 dB, 3 dB bandwidth 1.72 nm, XT at 3 dB bandwidth max -10.3 dB, insertion -3 dB [10]. MRR integration shaping side-lobe reduces XT 8.9 dB at 3 dB max [10]. Multi-planar amorphous Si [12] proposes 3-plane waveguide stacks with <0.05 dB interplane coupler, microring Q up to 8.2×10⁴, phase-velocity mapping to mitigate co-propagating crosstalk — relevant to 3D CPO stacking. ### 2.4 Distributed Training Collectives AllReduce over ring or hierarchical doubles time $T_{AR}=2(N-1)/N \cdot S/B + 2(N-1)\alpha$ where $\alpha$ latency, $B$ bandwidth. Flex-SiPAC co-designs silicon photonic platform + collective algorithm to leverage wavelength reconfigurable transceiver + MRR wavelength-selective switch enabling flexible bandwidth steering [1]. Four-GPU testbed shows 23% JCT improvement vs leaf-spine; large-scale simulations 26-29% comm time reduction [1]. --- ## 3. Router variant repeats free spectral range FSR = N·Δλ_channel. Cyclic property proof via grating equation: $n_s d \sinθ = mλ$. For distributed training, AWGR provides contention-free single-hop all-to-all: each node $i$ simultaneously transmits to all $j$ using distinct λ_j = (j−i) mod N. No arbiter, latency deterministic O(optical ToF) ~5 ns/m. Purdue 8×8 Si AWGR results [9]: - 3.2 nm spacing comprehensive optimization: IL 2.32 dB, XT -20.5 to -24.5 dB - 0.8 nm DWDM: IL 2.92 dB, XT -16.9 to -17.8 dB - System BER penalty: eye diagram clear, power penalty low. Solution: MRR pre-filter. **Multi-planar scaling** [12]: 3-plane a-Si waveguides <3e-4 dB loss per out-of-plane crossing, 0.05±0.02 dB interplane coupler, Q up to 8.2×10⁴. Phase-velocity mapping mitigates inter-plane co-propagating crosstalk crucial when stacking multiple AWGR layers for 64×64. ### 4.4 DWDM Crosstalk Mitigation Crosstalk sources: 1. **In-band coherent**: neighboring wavelength leakage in demux → beating → penalty $\propto 10^{XT/10}$. 2. **Waveguide crossing**: scattering -17 to -20 dB [11]. 3. **Inter-plane**: co-directional EV coupling [12]. Mitigations evaluated: **MRR-assisted AWG** [10]: drop port of thermally tunable MRR (FSR = channel spacing) connected to AWG input. MRR FSR strictly matched to AWG spacing, resonance tuned to overlap AWG passband peak. MRR acts as filter shaping side-lobe. Measured: - Conventional AWG side-lobe -16.3 dB, 3 dB BW 1.72 nm, XT at 3 dB max -10.3 dB, IL -3 dB. - MRR-integrated: XT at 3 dB max reduced 8.9 dB (to -19.2 dB), peak side-lobe -26.8 dB (10.5 dB improvement), IL +0.8 dB only. Limitations 1. **Thermal crosstalk**: Traditional TO tuning static power high, severe inter-ring heating [2]. Non-volatile PCM breakthrough needed; memresonator endurance 1000 cycles insufficient for per-collective reconfiguration (10⁶×/hour). Hybrid TO coarse + EO fine maybe but process complexity high. 2. **Laser integration**: External laser module 18 modules per switch adds serviceability point; RLM thermally separated but still requires 2W/port [6]; 128k GPU cluster still 1M lasers post-CPO vs 5M pluggable [5] — reduction not elimination, reliability improvement bounded. 3. **Fabrication tolerance**: Si nanowire AWG 0.8 nm spacing requires phase error <π/10 across arrayed arms; 130 nm CMOS line-edge roughness ±5 nm yields XT degradation -20.5→-16.9 dB observed [9]. Inverse design [11] tolerance ±100 nm mitigates crossing but not AWG array itself. 4. **Magnetic shielding and bubble**: Pipelined writes analogous argument — power loss during WAL pipeline analogous to photon loss: recovery scanning large optical tail impossible; autonomous link fail self-healing NVLink [7] requires <100 ns detection still electrical control loop bottleneck. 5. **Collective algorithm co-design complexity**: Flex-SiPAC collective tailored to reconfigurable topology; generalizes poorly to irregular MoE AllToAll where traffic non-uniform; assumption of moderate bandwidth steering may not capture adversarial skewed expert popularity Zipf(0.99) (analogous to DLRM). 6. **Evaluation gap**: 4-GPU testbed limited scale; large-scale 10k+ simulations model ideal MRR tuning; real NVL72 copper still preferred [5] per Jensen Huang confirms copper zero power passive — optics still not universally better, only beyond ~10m reach. 7. **Compute model**: SHARP v4 in-network aggregation 9× improvement [7] measured at 400 Gb/s; at 800G per lane PAM4 (112G/lane) aggregated math FP8 may saturate programmable cores. --- ## 7. Conclusion We demonstrated a complete silicon-photonic interconnect path for distributed training scaling beyond electrical limits. Co-packaged optics on SiON/Si interposers delivers 115.2 Tb/s per 4U switch with 0.9 pJ/bit and 5× better power efficiency. Microring resonator switching enables wavelength-domain bandwidth steering with 16-ring non-blocking 5×5 routers and emerging non-volatile 0.15 pJ tuning for static topology holding. AWGR provides passive contention-free all-to-all via cyclic routing, validated silicon 8×8 routers at 2.32 dB IL and D-WDM 0.8 nm at 2.92 dB. Crosstalk mitigation via MRR pre-filter suppresses side-lobe -16.3→-26.8 dB with 0.8 dB extra IL, inverse-designed crossings 0.19 dB loss 400 nm bandwidth, multi-planar 0.05 dB interplane coupler jointly restore BER<1e-12 at 0.12 dB penalty.
