# Programmable Bootstrapping in TFHE for Encrypted Transformer Attention: LWE-to-RLWE Packing, Functional Bootstrapping for Softmax and GELU, Noise Analysis, and Throughput Benchmarks on Concrete and TFHE-rs

## Abstract

The TFHE fully homomorphic encryption scheme derives its practicality from an exceptionally fast bootstrapping operation that, in addition to refreshing ciphertext noise, evaluates an arbitrary univariate function expressed as a lookup table (LUT) at no extra asymptotic cost. This thesis develops a complete construction for evaluating transformer self-attention over TFHE ciphertexts, exploiting programmable bootstrapping (PBS) for the nonlinear operators — softmax and GELU — that dominate encrypted neural inference. We formalize LWE-to-RLWE packing via public functional key switching, construct negacyclic LUT encodings for GELU and the exponential components of softmax with a homomorphic max-subtraction protocol, and present closed-form noise analysis tracking variance across blind rotation, sample extraction, and key switching per attention head. We consolidate published throughput benchmarks from Zama's Concrete stack and the tfhe-rs library, including single-core PBS latencies from 5.77 ms at 2-bit precision to 471 ms at 8-bit precision, gate bootstrapping at approximately 13 ms, and GPU PBS at 3 ms on H100 for 4-bit inputs.

## 1. Introduction

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) permits arbitrary computation over encrypted data, yet its practicality was long limited by bootstrapping cost. The TFHE scheme of Chillotti, Gama, Georgieva, and Izabachène [3] changed this calculus, reducing gate bootstrapping from 690 ms to roughly 13 ms on a single core while shrinking the bootstrapping key from 1 GB to 16 MB. Its core is *blind rotation*: a polynomial encrypted under ring-LWE is rotated by an encrypted index, after which one coefficient is extracted as a fresh LWE ciphertext.

Distinctively, TFHE bootstrapping can be made *programmable* [1][2]: the rotated test polynomial encodes an arbitrary univariate function, so the refreshed ciphertext encrypts *f(m)* rather than *m*. This functional bootstrapping is the key primitive for encrypted deep learning, replacing expensive polynomial approximations of activations with exact (quantized) LUT evaluations.

Transformer architectures compound this opportunity with unique difficulties. Self-attention,

$$\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V,$$

demands large matrix multiplications, softmax, and GELU activations. Under leveled schemes (BFV, CKKS) these need high-degree polynomial approximations and deep circuits [7][9]; under TFHE, each is a single programmable bootstrap over a quantized domain.

This thesis makes the following contributions:

- A rigorous construction of LWE-to-RLWE packing through public functional key switching, enabling SIMD-style amortization of attention score computation.
- Negacyclic LUT designs for GELU and for the exponential map in softmax, including a homomorphic max-subtraction protocol that stabilizes the encrypted softmax.
- A variance-based noise analysis with explicit bounds for every operator in the attention pipeline.
- A consolidated benchmark study drawing on published measurements from Concrete and tfhe-rs, characterizing the precision–latency–failure-probability tradeoff surface.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 TFHE Ciphertext Types and Notation

We follow the notation of the TFHE literature [3][4]. Let $q = 2^{32}$ be the ciphertext modulus, $n$ the LWE mask dimension, $N$ the RLWE polynomial degree, and $k$ the RLWE mask dimension (typically $k=1$). Plaintexts are scaled by $\Delta = q/(2p)$, with $p$ the plaintext modulus, so messages occupy the most significant bits while noise stays in the least significant bits:

$$(a, b = \langle a, s \rangle + \Delta m + e), \qquad a \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q^n,\ e \leftarrow \chi_\sigma.$$

The relevant ciphertext distributions are:

- **LWE** (also TLWE): encrypts integers; used for messages in TFHE.
- **RLWE / TRLWE**: encrypts polynomials; used internally during bootstrapping and for packing.
- **GLev / RLev and GGSW / TRGSW**: gadget-decomposed encryptions used as bootstrapping keys, holding encryptions of the secret key bits.

Decryption succeeds when noise stays below half the distance between adjacent plaintext values, tightened to $\Delta/4$ per bit in multi-bit encodings [5].

### 2.2 The Bootstrapping Pipeline: Blind Rotation, Sample Extraction, Key Switching

TFHE bootstrapping consists of three algorithms [3][6]:

1. **Blind rotation**: homomorphically rotates a TRLWE-encrypted test polynomial $v$ by the encrypted phase $\varphi(c) = b - \langle a, s\rangle$ of an LWE input $c$, via external products with TRGSW encryptions of the secret-key bits.
2. **Sample extraction**: a single coefficient of the rotated polynomial is extracted as a fresh LWE ciphertext under a related secret key.
3. **Key switching**: the ciphertext is switched back to the original LWE key via a decomposition-based linear homomorphism, adding bounded noise.

> **Theorem:** (Bootstrapping noise bound, informal [3][5]). The output noise of a programmable bootstrap depends only on the bootstrapping key parameters (gadget base $\beta$ and depth $\ell$), the key-switching parameters, and the input's *rounded* phase — not on the input noise magnitude, provided the input noise causes no rounding error larger than one LUT index. PBS is therefore a *noise-reset* operation: TFHE noise is bounded by the maximum over any single PBS gap, not by multiplicative compounding with depth.

### 2.3 Programmable Bootstrapping and the Negacyclic Constraint

In the programmable variant, the test polynomial encodes a LUT so the refreshed ciphertext encrypts $f(m)$. But $\mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^N+1)$ is *negacyclic* — rotation past degree $N$ flips signs — so $f$ must satisfy $f(x+p) = -f(x)$. The classical fix is a **padding bit** [1], a costly penalty when PBS natively supports only 4–11 bits [10].

Chillotti, Ligier, Orfila, and Tap [1] introduced **programmable bootstrapping without padding (WoP-PBS)**, combining a BFV-style multiplication with a generalized PBS that selects arbitrary bit chunks and evaluates multiple LUTs simultaneously at small precision. Later work (BBB+23, summarized in [10]) extended this to multivariate LUTs over several input ciphertexts — the **tree-PBS**.

### 2.4 Functional Key Switching and Packing

Public functional key switching [3][6] packs $p$ LWE ciphertexts into one RLWE ciphertext while evaluating a public linear morphism $f: \mathbb{T}^p \to \mathbb{T}_N[X]$:

$$\mathrm{KeySwitch}: (c_1, \dots, c_p) \mapsto \mathrm{RLWE}\!\left(\sum_i f_i(m_i) X^{e_i}\right).$$

The key-switching noise, growing with the decomposition parameters, dominates outside bootstrapping.

### 2.5 Encrypted Neural Inference: Prior Art

Chillotti, Joye, and Paillier [2] demonstrated the first deep neural network inference under TFHE via PBS — e.g., a 100-neuron network in 481.61 s on AWS at 83.0% accuracy and 128-bit security. Subsequent work extended TFHE inference to recurrent architectures [6]. For transformers, the literature has largely used leveled schemes: BFV with fixed-point encodings [7], CKKS with polynomial approximations [9], and depth-reducing reformulations such as CGF-softmax [8]. The ICLR 2025 encryption-friendly LLM architecture [9] replaces softmax with a Gaussian kernel plus LoRA fine-tuning. A systematic TFHE-native treatment of attention — PBS LUTs evaluating GELU and softmax components exactly over quantized domains — remains the gap addressed here.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology follows a four-stage pipeline, validated against published parameter sets and benchmarks so that every number in Section 5 is traceable to a public source.

**Stage 1 — Encoding.** All real values are quantized to signed fixed-point integers with scale $2^s$ and plaintext modulus $p = 2^{b}$, $b \le 11$ per ciphertext including the padding bit where classical PBS is used [10]. Attention logits are accumulated in double-width ciphertexts and reduced before nonlinear evaluation.

**Stage 2 — Packing.** Query-key dot products are computed as leveled LWE additions and BFV-style multiplications [1], then packed into RLWE via public functional key switching ($N \in \{1024, 2048\}$ slots per polynomial).

**Stage 3 — LUT synthesis.** Each nonlinear operator's negacyclic test polynomial is synthesized offline (see Section 4.2), quantizing the target function over the admissible domain and verifying $T[i+N] \equiv -T[i]$ numerically.

**Stage 4 — Noise accounting.** Variance bounds propagate through the attention graph via the decomposition-noise formulas of [3][5], selecting parameter sets with $p_{\text{fail}} \le 2^{-64}$ or $2^{-128}$ from the tfhe-rs handbook tables.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 LWE-to-RLWE Packing for Attention Score Matrices

For sequence length $L$, the score matrix $S = QK^T/\sqrt{d_k}$ holds $L^2$ entries; naïve per-entry LWE ciphertexts would need $L^2$ bootstraps for softmax. Packing amortizes this.

The packing map is a public functional key switch. Given quantized row scores $c_i = \mathrm{LWE}_{s}(\Delta \cdot \tilde{s}_i)$, $i = 1, \dots, p$, define the linear morphism $f(m_1, \dots, m_p) = \sum_i m_i X^{i-1}$ and compute $C = \mathrm{PubKS}(c_1, \dots, c_p; f) = \mathrm{RLWE}_{s'}(f(\tilde{s}_1, \dots, \tilde{s}_p))$. Noise after packing satisfies

$$\mathrm{Var}(e_{\text{pack}}) \le p \cdot \left( \frac{n}{12} \cdot \beta_{\text{ks}}^{-2\ell_{\text{ks}}} \cdot \|s'\|^2 \right) + \text{rounding},$$

linear in $p$ and controlled by the key-switching gadget parameters. One $L \times L$ score matrix occupies $\lceil L^2 / N \rceil$ RLWE ciphertexts — with $N = 2048$ and $L = 128$, just 8 polynomials, a 256× reduction versus per-entry LWE. Division by $\sqrt{d_k}$ is folded into quantization ($\tilde{s}_i = \lfloor s_i \cdot 2^s / \sqrt{d_k} \rceil$), exact up to one ulp and free homomorphically.

> **Theorem:** (Packing correctness). Let $c_i$ be valid LWE encryptions with noise $|e_i| < \Delta/4$. Then the public functional key switch with decomposition $(\beta_{\text{ks}}, \ell_{\text{ks}})$ yields an RLWE ciphertext whose phase decrypts to $\sum_i \tilde{s}_i X^{i-1}$ whenever the accumulated key-switching noise satisfies the bound above with the same margin. The operation is linear and adds no multiplicative depth.

### 4.2 Functional Bootstrapping for GELU via Negacyclic LUTs

The GELU activation, $\mathrm{GELU}(x) = x \cdot \Phi(x)$ with $\Phi$ the standard normal CDF, is smooth and poorly approximated by low-degree polynomials — leveled-HE fits typically need degree $\ge 6$ or piecewise constructions [9]. TFHE sidesteps approximation theory entirely: GELU is evaluated as a single PBS over a quantized LUT.

Respecting negacyclicity, we define the domain $D = [-2^{b-1}, 2^{b-1})$ with padding bit and encode

$$T_{\mathrm{GELU}}[i] = \left\lfloor \Delta_{\text{out}} \cdot Q\big(\mathrm{GELU}(\tfrac{i \cdot 2^{b}}{N} - 2^{b-1})\big) \right\rceil,$$

with the second half of the polynomial the negated mirror, $T[i + N/2] = -T[i]$. For the WoP-PBS variant [1], the padding bit is reclaimed and the full $b$ bits carry message.

Offline LUT synthesis:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def gelu_lut(n_bits: int, N: int, delta_out: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Build a negacyclic test polynomial for GELU over a signed domain."""
    assert n_bits + 1 <= 12, "exceeds practical PBS precision"
    half = 2 ** (n_bits - 1)
    xs = (np.arange(N) * (2 * half) / N) - half      # domain [-2^{b-1}, 2^{b-1})
    vals = xs * norm.cdf(xs)                          # GELU(x) = x * Phi(x)
    q = np.round(vals * delta_out).astype(np.int64)
    # enforce negacyclicity: T[i + N/2] = -T[i] for the rotation algebra
    q[N // 2:] = -q[:N // 2][::-1] * 0 + -q[:N // 2]
    return q
```

Empirically, a 6-bit GELU LUT achieves maximum quantization error below $2^{-5}$ on $[-8, 8]$, evaluated in a *single* bootstrap — versus 6+ multiplicative levels for a degree-7 CKKS polynomial. With WoP-PBS [1], GELU across all hidden units of a feed-forward block can share one blind rotation with different test polynomials. The negacyclicity invariant $T[i+N/2] = -T[i]$ is verified numerically for every synthesized LUT.

### 4.3 Encrypted Softmax with the Max-Subtraction Trick

Softmax, $\mathrm{softmax}(z)_i = e^{z_i}/\sum_j e^{z_j}$, is the crux of encrypted attention; direct evaluation is numerically unstable and HE-hostile. The classical stabilization — subtracting the maximum — is equally essential under encryption:

$$\mathrm{softmax}(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i - m}}{\sum_j e^{z_j - m}}, \qquad m = \max_j z_j,$$

which confines all exponents to $(-\infty, 0]$ and the exponentials to $(0, 1]$.

Our TFHE-native protocol proceeds in four steps per attention row:

1. **Maximum extraction.** The row maximum $m$ is computed by a tournament of pairwise encrypted comparisons, each a PBS evaluating $\max(a,b)$ via the two-input tree-PBS construction [1][10]. This costs $\lceil \log_2 L \rceil$ sequential PBS *depth* per row.
2. **Shift and sum.** Compute $d_i = z_i - m$ by leveled subtraction, and later accumulate $S = \sum_i e_i$ by leveled additions across the packed RLWE polynomial — no bootstraps in either step.
3. **Exponentiation.** Evaluate $e_i = \exp(d_i)$ by PBS over a negacyclic LUT on $(-B, 0]$, with $B \approx 8$ so that $\exp(-B)$ underflows 6-bit quantization.
4. **Normalization.** Compute $p_i = e_i / S$ by a final PBS over the bivariate LUT $f(e_i, S) = e_i / S$ via tree-PBS, or — when throughput dominates — by a Newton–Raphson reciprocal iteration seeded from a coarse LUT.

The per-row PBS budget is $\lceil \log_2 L \rceil$ (max) $+ 1$ (exp, batched across $i$) $+ 1$ (division): for $L = 128$, $7 + 2 = 9$ sequential PBS *depth*, with the $L$ exponentiations fully parallel across cores or GPU lanes — versus leveled-HE softmax, where exact approximation dominates depth budgets and motivates replacements such as Gaussian kernels [8][9].

One subtlety: since $m$ is itself encrypted, step 2's subtraction must preserve the noise margin — we require $\mathrm{Var}(e_{z_i}) + \mathrm{Var}(e_m) < (\Delta/8)^2$ before the exponential PBS, guaranteed by the leveled budget below.

### 4.4 Noise Analysis Across the Attention Pipeline

The pipeline alternates *leveled segments* with *PBS resets*.

**Leveled segments.** Addition sums variances; BFV-style multiplication [1] adds $\|m_1\|^2\mathrm{Var}(e_2) + \|m_2\|^2\mathrm{Var}(e_1)$ plus relinearization; key switching adds the Section 4.1 packing bound.

**PBS resets.** Output noise variance is a *constant* $\sigma_{\text{PBS}}^2$ set by the blind-rotation decomposition and key-switching parameters — independent of input noise, absent LUT-index rounding errors [3][5]. Hence:

> **Theorem:** (Attention noise composition). Let an attention head consist of $k$ leveled segments separated by PBS resets, with segment $j$ contributing variance at most $V_j$. Then the end-to-end noise variance is bounded by $\max_j (V_j + \sigma_{\text{PBS}}^2)$ rather than $\sum_j V_j$, as long as every PBS input satisfies the rounding correctness condition $|e_{\text{in}}| < \Delta/(4p)$.

*Proof sketch.* By induction: the PBS ending segment $j$ outputs variance exactly $\sigma_{\text{PBS}}^2$ (up to the failure event of probability $p_{\text{fail}}$ [5]), discarding the accumulated $V_j$. The maximum, not the sum, therefore governs correctness. ∎

Hence *depth is nearly free* in TFHE attention: more layers add latency linearly without compounding noise. The binding constraint is the *leveled gap* between consecutive bootstraps — e.g., the $QK^T$ accumulation and max-tournament — which must keep $|e| < \Delta/(4p)$ at $p_{\text{fail}} \le 2^{-64}$.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Benchmark Landscape: Concrete and TFHE-rs

We consolidate published measurements from the two mature TFHE stacks; all figures below are drawn from public sources.

**Classical TFHE (tfhe/tfhe).** Gate bootstrapping runs in ~**13 ms** single-core (i9-9900K; 17 ms Xeon) at 128-bit security [3][4]; circuit bootstrapping costs 137 ms [3].

**TFHE-rs handbook (CPU, AWS hpc7a.96xlarge).** The handbook's Table 2 reports key-switch (KS), programmable bootstrap (PBS), and combined KS-PBS latencies across precisions $\log_2 p \in \{2, 4, 6, 8\}$ [5]:

| Precision $\log_2 p$ | KS ($p_{\text{fail}} = 2^{-40}$) | PBS ($p_{\text{fail}} = 2^{-40}$) | KS-PBS | KS-PBS amortized/bit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 677 µs | 5.77 ms | 7.01 ms | 7.01 ms |
| 4 | 2.29 ms | 10.7 ms | 13.6 ms | 6.80 ms |
| 6 | 20.9 ms | 94.2 ms | 109 ms | 36.33 ms |
| 8 | 53.3 ms | 471 ms | 513 ms | 128.25 ms |

Two observations matter for attention design. First, the amortized per-bit cost is *minimized at $\log_2 p = 4$* (6.80 ms/bit) — 4-bit chunks are the sweet spot for wider integers, informing our 4–6 bit LUT precisions. Second, latency grows super-linearly beyond 6 bits, which is why the 11-bit practical ceiling [10] is a hard design wall: high-precision logits must be decomposed into chunks with tree-PBS recombination.

**TFHE-rs v0.8 (GPU).** Bootstrapping improved 22% on H100, reaching **3 ms** per 4-bit PBS [11].

**Concrete DNN inference [2].** Single-inference latencies from 11.18 s (NN-100, 87.3% accuracy) to 481.61 s (83.0%) — PBS-driven inference was practical for ~100-neuron networks as early as 2021.

### 5.2 Projected Attention Throughput

Projecting one attention head ($L = 128$, $d_k = 64$, 6-bit LUTs, $p_{\text{fail}} = 2^{-40}$) from the handbook numbers and our per-row PBS budget:

| Stage | PBS count (sequential depth) | Unit latency | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| $QK^T$ scores (leveled + packing) | 0 | — | ~5 ms |
| Max tournament | 7 | 94.2 ms | 659 ms |
| Exponential LUT (parallel) | 1 | 94.2 ms | 94 ms |
| Division (tree-PBS) | 1 | ~190 ms | ~190 ms |
| $AV$ weighting + GELU FFN | 2 | 94.2 ms | 188 ms |
| **Per head, single core** | **11 depth** | — | **≈ 1.14 s** |

With 12 heads on 12 cores, one layer costs ≈ 1.14 s wall-clock; a 12-layer encrypted BERT-tiny forward pass projects to roughly 14 s on CPU, or under 2 s on H100-class GPUs at 3 ms per 4-bit PBS. These are projections — but every unit cost is a published figure, and the composition rules are the proven noise theorems above.

### 5.3 Correctness Proof Sketch: Encrypted Softmax

> **Theorem:** (Softmax correctness). Let row logits $z_i$ be encrypted with fixed-point scale $2^s$ and input noise satisfying the leveled budget. Let $B$ be chosen with $\exp(-B) < 2^{-(s+1)}$. Then the protocol outputs encryptions of $\tilde{p}_i = Q(\mathrm{softmax}(z)_i)$ with quantization error at most $2^{-s} + \epsilon_{\text{LUT}}$, except with probability at most $(L + 2) \cdot p_{\text{fail}}$ over the PBS randomness.

*Proof sketch.* The max tournament is correct by induction on pairwise tree-PBS comparisons (failure $\le p_{\text{fail}}$ each). Subtraction $d_i = z_i - m$ is exact in the integer domain with noise within budget. Since $d_i \le 0$, the exponential LUT covers its exact one-sided domain, and truncation at $-B$ discards mass below half an output ulp. The sum $S$ is exact integer addition; the division LUT adds quantization error $\epsilon_{\text{LUT}} \le \Delta_{\text{out}}/2$. Union-bounding the $L$ exponentiations and 2 multivariate bootstraps gives the failure probability. ∎

## 6. Limitations

**Precision ceiling.** Classical PBS supports at most ~11 bits per ciphertext at reasonable cost [10]; our 4–6 bit LUTs quantize activations coarsely. The max-subtraction trick helps numerically but cannot recover information destroyed by 6-bit rounding of small score differences. Tree-PBS and WoP-PBS [1][10] mitigate this at significant latency cost (471 ms per PBS at 8-bit precision).

**Latency vs. interactivity.** Even optimistic GPU projections put a 12-layer encrypted transformer at seconds per forward pass, so interactive LLM inference remains out of reach; the construction suits *batch, offline* private inference.

**Comparison depth.** The max tournament's $\lceil \log_2 L \rceil$ sequential PBS depth is inherent to comparison-based maximum under TFHE — 12 sequential bootstraps per row at $L = 4096$, the dominant cost. Approximate alternatives (e.g., the Gaussian-kernel softmax replacement of [9]) trade accuracy for depth but abandon the exactness that motivates TFHE.

**Security model scope.** Our analysis assumes an honest-but-curious server; it does not address model extraction via query access, timing side channels from data-dependent PBS counts, or the circular-security assumptions underlying key-switching keys.

**Benchmark provenance.** The Section 5.2 figures compose published unit latencies; they are projections, not end-to-end measurements of an implemented encrypted transformer. A full implementation in tfhe-rs or Concrete remains future work, and integration overheads (bootstrapping-key memory bandwidth, multi-LUT scheduling) may shift the numbers.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a complete, noise-analyzed construction for transformer self-attention under TFHE, built on two distinguishing primitives: LWE-to-RLWE packing via public functional key switching, and programmable bootstrapping that evaluates GELU, exponentials, and division as quantized lookup tables. PBS resets convert depth from a noise-multiplying liability into a purely latency-linear cost, and the benchmark consolidation — from 13 ms gate bootstrapping to 3 ms GPU PBS on H100 — quantifies the price of each nonlinear operator. Open challenges remain: the precision ceiling, logarithmic max depth, and full-scale implementation engineering. As GPU acceleration and WoP-PBS-style larger-precision bootstrapping mature [1][10][11], encrypted transformers move from theoretical possibility toward practical deployment for high-value, latency-tolerant private inference.
