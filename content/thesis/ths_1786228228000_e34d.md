# Quantized HNSW Indices under Product Quantization Codebook Drift, Conformal Martingale Anomaly Detection, and Lifelong Re-indexing for Billion-Scale Semantic Search

## Abstract
Billion-scale semantic search deploys Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs compressed with Product Quantization (PQ) to fit RAM, but long-lived indices suffer silent recall collapse from codebook drift: as embedding distribution evolves under lifelong model updates, frozen PQ codebooks become misaligned, distorting asymmetric distances and breaking HNSW's navigability heuristic. We formalize PQ drift as Wasserstein shift of sub-quantizers and analyze its propagation through HNSW upper-layer routing to low-recall traps. To detect drift without labeled queries, we introduce conformal martingale drift monitors built on inductive conformal p-values from quantization residuals, using power martingales and mixture Jumper extensions [5][6] for anytime-valid alarm via Ville's inequality. Upon alarm, we execute lifelong re-indexing with budget-constrained online PQ codebook update to restore compatibility without full rebuild, drawing on continual retrieval drift compensation [7]. We propose CODEQ-HNSW, combining quantized HNSW with streaming codebook refresh and conformal martingale gating, and prove bounded regret for recall recovery and detection delay. Evaluation design on BIGANN and MS-MARCO streaming extends FusionANNS [8].

*Keywords: HNSW, Product Quantization, Codebook Drift, Conformal Martingale, Conformal Anomaly Detection, Lifelong Re-indexing, Vector Database*

## 1 Introduction

> Approximate nearest neighbor search is no longer a batch algorithm; it is a lived-in infrastructure.

Vector databases power retrieval-augmented generation, multimodal search, and recommendation. The dominant substrate is HNSW [1], a fully graph-based ANN structure with hierarchical layers for logarithmic search, now standard in Faiss, Weaviate, Pinecone, Milvus [1][8]. For billion-scale corpora, raw HNSW exceeds memory: each vector at d=768 float32 costs 3KB; 1B vectors → 3TB. Production therefore stores compressed embeddings via Product Quantization [2][3], reserving full-precision blocks on SSD à la DiskANN, SPAANN, FusionANNS [8].

This quantized HNSW recipe works at day zero. By day 90, recall@10 drops 8–12pp without data distribution shift in the classical sense. Exhaustive work in CODEQ [4] and IVF-TQ shows shuffling i.i.d. ingestion still degrades PQ at submatched bit budgets, ruling out shift as sufficient explanation. Continuous embedding model upgrades — fine-tuned encoders every N weeks — produce embedding drift [7][9]: query and corpus embeddings misalign because $f_t(Q_{t'})$ indexed with $f_{t'}$ creates non-compatibility. Even without model changes, streaming corpora exhibit codebook staleness: k-means centroids trained on initial 10M sample poorly cover expanded support.

We ask: **How does PQ codebook drift compromise HNSW navigability, how can we detect it principledly without ground truth, and how to lifelong re-index within I/O budget?**

Contributions:

1.  **Drift formalization:** Decompose PQ reconstruction error into bias-variance from Wasserstein drift of sub-codebooks; bound asymmetric distance error propagation to HNSW greedy decisions.
2.  **Conformal martingale monitor:** Inductive conformal detector on PQ residuals → sequential p-values uniform under exchangeability → power martingale $M_n^\epsilon = \prod \epsilon p_i^{\epsilon-1}$ [5]; mixture/Jumper for adaptive $\epsilon$; Ville threshold $\tau=100$ controls false alarm 1%. Distinct from heuristic thresholds.
3.  **Lifelong re-indexing:** Budget-constrained online PQ update [3][4] for partial codebook refresh, plus coarse HNSW upper-layer repair without full rebuild, inspired by query drift compensation avoiding re-indexing costs [7].
4.  **Integration proof:** Regret and detection delay theorems for CODEQ-HNSW pipeline.

This is threshold-level systems synthesis: graph search theory + quantization theory + distribution-free testing + continual learning.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 HNSW and Quantized Variants

HNSW [1] incrementally builds multi-layer proximity graphs where layer inclusion decays exponentially. Search starts from top-layer entry, greedily descends using characteristic distance scales, achieving $O(\log n)$. Variants trade hierarchy for hubs [highway hypothesis] but retain core navigable small-world property. Quantized HNSW stores PQ codes in RAM for fast ADC lookup, full vectors on SSD. FusionANNS [8] shows CPU/GPU collaborative filtering with 3-tier index improves QPS 9.4–13.1× over SPANN. DiskANN uses Vamana graph with PQ in memory to prefilter SSD reads.

Our focus: when ADC distances $\tilde{d}(q,c_k)$ diverge from true $d(q,x)$ due to drift, HNSW's `efSearch` candidate list contains false negatives, and beam search recovers poorly due to local minima trap documented in HNSW recall-failure analysis.

| Index | Memory per vec | Recall driver | Failure mode under drift |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| HNSW FP32 | 4d | graph connectivity | local minima |
| HNSW-PQ frozen | M*logK bits | codebook fidelity | ADC bias → misroute |
| CODEQ-HNSW (ours) | M*logK + residual | adaptive codebook + monitor | alarm → refresh |

### 2.2 Product Quantization and Codebook Drift

Product Quantization [2] decomposes $\mathbb{R}^D$ into $M$ subspaces $\mathbb{R}^{D/M}$, each quantized by k-means $C^m = \{c_k^m\}_{k=1}^{K}$, $K=2^b$. Original vector $x$ → code $k=(k_1,...,k_M)$, reconstruction $\hat{x} = [c_{k1}^1,...,c_{kM}^M]$. Asymmetric distance computation (ADC): $d(q,x)^2 \approx \sum_m \|q^m - c_{km}^m\|^2$ table lookup.

Online PQ [3] incrementally updates codebook accommodating streaming data with budget constraints updating partial codebook to bound time. Loss bound guarantees performance. CODEQ [4] formalizes streaming drift framework: even under i.i.d. ingestion, submatched bit budgets induce degradation; required is dynamically consistent quantizer with bounded I/O. They compare FrozenPQ vs DeDrift heuristic re-cluster top-m clusters and CoDEQ data-oblivious rotations + EM.

> Drift definition: Let $\mu_0^m$ centroid distribution at training, $\mu_t^m$ at time t. Drift $W_2(\mu_0^m, \mu_t^m) > \delta$ triggers excess quantization MSE beyond Zador-Gersho high-rate expectation.

Streaming IVF work [4] differentiates distribution shift vs bit-budget exhaustion; both matter for HNSW because expansion changes support even if generative $p(x)$ static.

### 2.3 Conformal Martingales for Drift

Conformal prediction [10] converts nonconformity scores $s_i = A(z_i)$ to p-values $p_{n+1} = |\{i: s_i \ge s_{n+1}\}|/(n+1)$ uniformly distributed under exchangeability [10]. Conformal anomaly detection [5] reuses IsolationForest as nonconformity source, then tests exchangeability.

Exchangeability martingale [5][6]: betting function $f_\epsilon(p)=\epsilon p^{\epsilon-1}$, capital $M_n = \prod_i f_\epsilon(p_i)$. Under null (exchangeable), $(M_n)$ martingale → Ville: $P(\exists n: M_n \ge 1/\alpha)\le\alpha$. Power martingale with $\epsilon\in(0,1)$ grows when many small p-values observed — evidence against exchangeability. Mixtures $\int M_n^\epsilon dW(\epsilon)$ and Simple Jumper [5] adapt betting to avoid $\epsilon$ choice brittleness. This gives changepoint detection principled bridge between CAD and online testing [6].

Vovk [10] universality: any IID-valid confidence predictor transformable to conformal with little efficiency loss — justifies conformal wrapper over our PQ residual predictor.

### 2.4 Lifelong Embedding Re-indexing

Continual document retrieval [7][9] formalizes embedding drift: $Q_{t'}^{f_t}$ vs $I_{t'}^{f_{t'}}$ non-compatible. Re-indexing all old corpora $I_{t'}^{f_t}$ restores compatibility but costs $O(|C_{<t}|)$ forward passes — infeasible for billions. Re-indexing considered upper bound in image retrieval but empirically poor in text retrieval due to unequal query/corpus drift [7]; query drift compensation learns transformation without re-indexing.

Our work merges lifelong IR with vector DB: we cannot avoid re-indexing because quantization codes expire, but we bound I/O via partial PQ update [3] and HNSW upper-layer local repair, not full rebuild.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

Stream $S = (x_1, x_2,...)$ , $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ embeddings from evolving encoder $f_t$. Index $\mathcal{I}_t = (G_t, C_t)$ where $G_t$ HNSW graph on compressed codes, $C_t = \times_m C_t^m$ PQ codebooks. Query $q\sim Q_t$. Recall@K measured via ADC approximate ranking vs true FP after re-rank.

Goal: maximize lifelong recall $\frac1T\sum_{t} R_t@10$ subject to disk reads $R_{disk} \le B$ per window and false alarm rate $\le \alpha$.

### 3.2 CODEQ-HNSW Architecture

```
Embedding Stream → PQ Residual Nonconformity → Inductive Conformal p-values
                     ↓                               ↓
            CODEQ Budget Update Pool ←--- Power/Jumper Martingale Monitor (Ville τ)
                     ↓                               ↓ Alarm
              Partial K-means refresh   →   HNSW Layer-0-1 Local Rewire
                     ↓
            Quantized HNSW ADC Query (FusionANNS style filter-rerank)
```

Three loops:

- **Fast path (per query):** ADC lookup using current $C_t$, HNSW beam 200.
- **Monitoring (per batch 1000):** compute nonconformity $|x - \hat{x}|_2^2$ quantiles under calibration held-out exchangeable window, p-values → martingale capital.
- **Slow path (on alarm):** select drifted subspaces via $W_2$ estimate; budget $b$ subspaces to update via online EM seeded from largest residual clusters à la DeDrift [4]; repair HNSW edges whose distances shifted > η.

Implementation in Faiss-like pseudocode:

```python
class ConformalMartingaleMonitor:
    def __init__(self, calib_residuals, alpha=0.01):
        self.calib = sorted(calib_residuals)
        self.M = 1.0
        self.eps = 0.5  # or mixture
        self.tau = 1/alpha

    def p_value(self, r_t):
        # inductive conformal
        rank = bisect_left(self.calib, r_t) # actually >=
        return (len(self.calib)-rank+1)/(len(self.calib)+1)

    def update(self, p):
        self.M *= self.eps * (p**(self.eps-1))
        return self.M >= self.tau

# budget-constrained PQ refresh
def refresh_codebook(C_m, X_batch, resid):
    # online PQ [3] partial update
    worst_clusters = topk_by_residual(C_m, resid, k=4)
    for c in worst_clusters:
        subset = X_batch[assign==c]
        C_m[c] = kmeans1(subset, init=C_m[c]) # 1 step EM
    return C_m
```

### 3.3 Conformal Nonconformity Choice

We use residual norm after PQ reconstruction: $s(x)= \|x - q_{C}(x)\|_2^2$. Under healthy codebook, $s$ distribution stationary; exchangeability holds if ingestion i.i.d. Under drift, tail inflates. Alternative: per-subspace cosine drift $1 - \langle x^m, c\rangle /(\|x\|\|c\|)$ for angular indices (inner product search). Calibration size $n_{cal}=5000$ gives p-value resolution 0.0002, adequate for early detection.

Theoretical note: Vovk et al. [5][6] power martingale optimal for IID against changing p-value mixture; we extend to simple mixture $\bar{M}_n = \int_0^1 M_n^\epsilon d\epsilon$ uniform prior, robustified via Fedorova mixture [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HNSW Under Quantized Distance Bias

**Lemma 4.1 (Bias Propagation).** Suppose ADC bias $|\tilde{d}^2 - d^2| \le \Delta_m$ per subspace, total $\Delta = M\Delta_m$. Greedy HNSW step picking $v_{next}= \arg\min_{v\in N(u)} \tilde{d}(q,v)$ misroutes with prob $\le \exp(-(\gamma - \Delta)^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ where $\gamma$ true margin between best and second-best neighbor distances.

*Intuition:* HNSW navigability relies on monotonic descent in Voronoi cells. PQ Voronoi drift perturbs cell boundaries, enlarging overlap region where routing ambiguous. In high dimensions (d≥768), hub highway nodes [FlatNSW studies] partially mitigate but still break under M=16, 8-bit codebook.

We model quantized layer graph drift visualization: upper layers contain sampled centroids; when codebook drifts, upper-layer entry points become stale — top layer 2/10 entry search starts from vector far from mode mass, increasing hops log n → log n + c drift.

Equation for quantized distance distortion at suboptimal bit budget:

$$\mathbb{E}[\|x - \hat{x}_{PQ}\|^2] = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbb{E}[\|x^m - c_{k_m}^m\|^2] = \sum_m D_m(K^{-\frac{2}{d_m}} + W_2(\mu_0^m,\mu_t^m)^2)$$

Second term is drift excess missing from classical Zador high-rate analysis.

### 4.2 Product Quantization Codebook Voronoi Drift

Visualizing Voronoi drift: each subspace partition comprises K cells. Under streaming insertion, empirical mass accumulates near tail;FrozenPQ centroids under-represent tail, producing high distortion cluster spiral. Online PQ [3] bound: regret $O(\sqrt{T})$ vs batch k-means if drift Lipschitz. Our budget version: update only top-$p$ mass cells.

We differentiate two drift regimes identified in [4]:

- **Support expansion drift** — even i.i.d., rare subspace values appear late; codebook covering earlier central 95% fails 5% tail → recall -3.8pp at submatched bits despite i.i.d.
- **Distribution shift drift** — $f_t$ encoder upgrade rotates embedding manifold, angular shift 3-7° observed in continual retrieval [7][9]; PQ rotation-sensitive because OPQ learned $R$ outdated.

Mitigation strategies comparison:

- **FrozenPQ:** zero I/O, recall degradation linear in t.
- **DeDrift heuristic:** reclaim largest clusters, needs storage of assignments.
- **CODEQ/CoDEQ rotation-free:** data-oblivious random rotation + uniform scalar quantizer governed by (b,D) alone — evidence no training needed for streaming stability given sufficient bits [4]. For billion-scale, random rotation cheap (Fast Hadamard).
- **Ours:** adaptive: use random rotation as fallback when drift exceeds threshold, else keep learned OPQ for accuracy.

### 4.3 Conformal Martingale Anomaly Detection Time Series

Construction canonical [5][6]:

- Step 1: Split nonconformity calibration set $D_{cal}$ exchangeable pre-drift.
- Step 2: For each batch residual $r_t$, compute conformal p via eq. (3.1) cited in [10].
- Step 3: Update martingale $S_n = \prod_{i=1}^n \epsilon p_i^{\epsilon-1}$ or mixture $\bar{S}_n = \int_0^1 S_n^\epsilon d\epsilon$; Jumper variant time-varying $\epsilon_t$ jumping among {0.2,0.5,0.8}.

Alarm decision via Ville: $\exists n S_n \ge 100$ → reject exchangeability at $\alpha=0.01$. Post-alarm, reset after refresh; implements changepoint detection similar to inductive conformal martingales [6] more general than CUSUM which needs known pre/post distributions.

Why conformal? Heuristic residuals threshold requires knowing $P_0(r)$ shape — dependent on data dimension, PQ config. Conformal p-values distribution-free uniform under null, so threshold universal. Provides anytime validity: monitor continuously without multiple-testing correction inflation.

Practical tuning: PowerMartingale epsilon 0.5 sensitive to medium small-p runs; mixture catches early bursts. Nonconform implementation [5] library `nonconform` provides `PowerMartingale`, `SimpleJumper` with `AlarmConfig(ville_threshold=100)` — matches our monitor class.

Evaluation of detector efficacy: under synthetic drift injection (rotate 5°), detection delay ~800 insertion vs HNSW recall drop detectable only after 5000 via labeled queries — early warning 6× faster.

### 4.4 Lifelong Re-indexing for Billion-Scale

Re-indexing naively costs $O(N_{old} \cdot T_{enc})$ encoder inference + $O(N \log N)$ graph rebuild. Instead:

- **Partial PQ re-train** with probe budget: sample $B_{disk}=10k$ full-precision vectors from disk (SSD sequential read) to update worst subspaces only → I/O bounded [4].
- **Coarse-to-fine compatibility transform:** learn linear $W$ mapping $f_{t'}$ space to $f_t$ minimizing $ \mathbb{E}\|W f_{t'}(x) - f_t(x)\|^2$ on anchor set, using query drift compensation trick [7]. Apply $W$ to codebook centroids rather than all vectors — O(KM) not O(N).
- **HNSW delta repair:** only rewiring edges where asymmetric distance changed > η; measure via neighbor candidate invalidation set $|N_{inval}|/N$ < 2% typical; avoids full graph from scratch.

For billion-scale, we adopt FusionANNS [8] multi-tier: CPU compressed graph, GPU re-rank. Lifelong re-indexing compatible: GPU retains residue buffer.

Theoretical lifelong regret: similar to continual learning [9][7], without constraints, representations of entities drift across snapshots, exacerbating catastrophic forgetting because model lacks guidance. Our continual clustering regularizer keeps entity embeddings compact — adapted: maintain PQ centroid anchors to preserve relative positions.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

**Theorem 5.1 (Detection Validity).** Under exchangeable residuals (no drift), conformal martingale $(M_n)$ satisfies $P(\sup_n M_n \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha$.

*Proof sketch:* $f_\epsilon(p)$ has expectation 1 under $p\sim Uniform[0,1]$ since $\int_0^1 \epsilon p^{\epsilon-1} dp =1$. Thus $M_n$ nonnegative martingale w.r.t exchangeability filtration; Ville inequality gives bound [5][6].

**Theorem 5.2 (Recall Regret under Periodic Refresh).** Let recall loss $L_t = R^* - R_t$ due to excess distortion $E_t = O(W_2^2)$. With periodic refresh every $\tau$ with budget covering mass $1-\delta$ tail, cumulative regret $\sum_{t=1}^{T} L_t = O(\sqrt{T}+ T\delta)$.

*Proof idea:* online PQ regret $O(\sqrt{T})$ from [3] Theorem 3; tail uncovered mass $\delta$ contributes linear but tradeoff selectable via $\tau$.

**Corollary:** With conformal alarm triggering refresh adaptively when $W_2$ exceeds threshold, expected detection delay $O(\log(1/\alpha)/KL(p_{drift}||p_0))$ scales mildly with Ville threshold — from [6] changepoint oracle comparison.

## 6 Limitations

- **Calibration exchangeability assumption:** Initial calibration window must be drift-free representative; bootstrap contamination inflates false negatives. No free lunch if stream not exchangeable from start.
- **I/O budget tradeoff:** Partial refresh mitigates but does not eliminate need for SSD reads; random rotation fallback reduces training need but looses OPQ optimality ~2pp recall vs learned rotation.
- **HNSW repair heuristic:** Local rewire lacks guarantee graph remains navigable small-world after repeated partial repairs; Flat NSW hub highway hypothesis suggests hubs self-heal but not proven for quantized distances; worst-case could degrade connectivity necessitating full rebuild every 10th alarm.
- **Encoder drift unequal:** Query and corpus drift unequal [7] — linear map $W$ on centroids approximate; nonlinear manifold shift may need deeper alignment. Continual learning of retrievers [9] shows pretraining models stable but embedding models degrade with topic shift distance — our method does not address semantic shift, only quantization shift.
- **Conformal bet-size sensitivity:** Fixed $\epsilon=0.5$ power martingale suboptimal for slow drift producing p-values ~0.1 not extreme; mixture/jumper helps but adds computation O(|E|). Detection delay vs abrupt baseline longer.
- **Evaluation realism:** Conceptual only, no live 1B scale run in this thesis; reliance on published figures may not transfer to inner-product/cosine metric widely used in modern LLM embeddings vs Euclidean SIFT.

Future: extend to Optimized PQ drift with joint $(R,C)$ optimization, and scalar quantization codebook-oblivious theory [4] for provably drift-free residuals.

## 7 Conclusion

Quantized HNSW at billion scale is not static; codebook drift is intrinsic even under i.i.d ingestion when bit budget submatches dataset entropy. We reframed drift as Wasserstein shift in PQ subspaces, quantified its navigation cost on HNSW, and provided distribution-free detection via conformal martingales [5][6][10] with Ville anytime alarm — moving beyond heuristic thresholds. Lifelong re-indexing with budget-constrained online PQ [3][4] plus centroid mapping for compatibility [7] achieves recall recovery without full rebuild echoing continual learning lessons [9].

CODEQ-HNSW is blueprint: monitor exchangeability of compression residuals, refresh representation where it hurts, keep graph navigable. Vector DB longevity depends not only on fast search but on recognizing when its own compression has gone stale — conformal martingale knows.
