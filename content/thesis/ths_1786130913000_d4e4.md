# Neural Scene Representations in the Wild: Mip-NeRF Anti-Aliasing, Block-NeRF Scaling, and 3D Gaussian Splatting Spectral Analysis

## Abstract
Neural radiance fields have transitioned from bounded object-centric reconstructions to city-scale, unbounded, and real-time rendering. This thesis unifies three pivotal advances: Mip-NeRF's cone tracing and integrated positional encoding for anti-aliasing, Mip-NeRF 360's non-linear contraction, proposal sampling and distortion regularizer for unbounded scenes, and Block-NeRF's compositional scaling with appearance and pose conditioning. We then contrast these implicit ray-marching representations with explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its spectral aliasing behavior. We derive covariance projection under EWA splatting, analyze frequency-domain leakage when Nyquist limits are violated, and evaluate Zip-NeRF's multisampling as a bridge between grid-based acceleration and cone-aware filtering. We provide rigorous Fourier derivations, block tiling analysis via partition of unity, and hybrid practical guidelines, demonstrating 8–77% error reduction, 24× training speedup, and 150+ fps real-time rendering with principled anti-aliasing. At 2,600 words we synthesize theory, empirical PSNR/latency tables, and implementation recipes for robust in-the-wild capture.

---

## 1. Introduction

The transition from *photo collections* to *photorealistic world models* represents one of the most consequential shifts in computer vision. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [Mildenhall 2020] demonstrated that a *multilayer perceptron* mapping $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ can be optimized via differentiable volume rendering to reproduce view-dependent radiance with unprecedented fidelity. Yet the original formulation assumes **infinitesimal rays**, **bounded scenes inside a unit sphere**, and **single-GPU memory** — violently violated *in the wild*.

Three failure modes dominate real capture:

1. **Scale aliasing** — A pixel observing a brick wall at 2m vs 200m subtends a footprint differing by $100\times$. Ray sampling with fixed positional encoding (PE) cannot represent prefiltered radiance [1].
2. **Unboundedness** — Outdoor scenes contain content at $z\to\infty$ and $180^\circ$–$360^\circ$ coverage. Euclidean parameterization wastes capacity on empty space and fails to regularize floaters [2].
3. **City-scale** — 2.8 million images of San Francisco cannot fit in one MLP. Per-pixel training time scales linearly with network size; inference must decouple from scene extent [4].

Simultaneously, **3D Gaussian Splatting** [5] proposes an explicit alternative: millions of anisotropic Gaussians rasterized via tile-based $\alpha$-blending, achieving $>150$ fps at 1080p while preserving volumetric optimization benefits. Its rapid adoption forces a critical question: *is implicit cone tracing still necessary, or does explicit covariance filtering subsume anti-aliasing?*

This thesis provides a PhD-level synthesis:

- Formal derivation of **Mip-NeRF** integrated positional encoding (IPE) as expectation of sinusoids over conical frustums, Gaussian approximation, and 60% error reduction on multiscale Blender [1].
- Extension to **Mip-NeRF 360** parameterization $\text{contract}(\mathbf{x})$, online distillation via proposal MLP, and distortion loss $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ [2][6].
- Scalability via **Block-NeRF** spatial tiling, appearance embeddings $\mathbf{a}_i$, learned pose refinement $\delta R, \delta t$, and exposure conditioning with inverse distance blending [4].
- **Spectral analysis of 3DGS**: EWA filter $\mathcal{G}_{2D}'$, Nyquist sampling limit $f_s > 2 f_{max}$, dilation/shrinkage pathology, Mip-Splatting, and Zip-NeRF's 8-tap multisampling unification [3].
- Implementation benchmarks, failure tables, and hybrid recipes.

> **Theorem:** *Frustum Prefiltering Preservation.* Let conical frustum $F(\mu,\Sigma)$ with mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma = \text{diag}(\sigma_t^2, \sigma_r^2, \sigma_r^2)$ in local frame. Define IPE $ \gamma_{IPE}(\mu,\Sigma)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)}[\gamma_{PE}(\mathbf{x})]$. Then for frequency $k$, amplitude scaling $\exp(-0.5 \, \text{diag}(\Sigma) k^2)$ zeroes out features when period $< 2\pi\sigma$. Hence MLP input vanishes for wavelengths smaller than footprint, enforcing explicit Nyquist low-pass. [1]

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Volume Rendering and NeRF

For ray $\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}$, color:

$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t),\mathbf{d}) dt,\quad T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds) $$

Discretized with piecewise constant $K$ samples:

$$ \hat{C} = \sum_{i=1}^K w_i \mathbf{c}_i,\quad w_i=T_i(1-\exp(-\sigma_i\delta_i)) $$

NeRF uses hierarchical sampling: coarse $64$ uniform + fine $128$ importance. Capacity $8\times256$ MLP ~5 MB.

*Limitation:* PE $\gamma(x)=[\sin(2^k x), \cos(2^k x)]_{k=0}^{L-1}$ with $L=10$ has fixed bandwidth. A high-frequency brick texture visible at near scale becomes aliased at far scale because same PE injected; network learns *average* causing both jaggies and blur [1].

### 2.2 Unbounded Scene Contraction

Mildenhall's NDC for front-facing scenes maps $z\in[0,\infty)$ to $[0,1]$. Fails for $360^\circ$. Mip-NeRF 360 [2][6] proposes:

$$ \text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} \mathbf{x} & \|\mathbf{x}\|\le 1 \\ \left(2 - \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} \right) \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \|\mathbf{x}\|>1 \end{cases} $$

This maps $\mathbb{R}^3\to [-2,2]^3$ with smooth radial compression, preserving uniform distribution inside unit sphere (foreground) while allocating diminishing capacity to infinity. Invertible, $C^1$.

### 2.3 3D Gaussian Splatting

Each Gaussian $k$ defined by $\mu_k\in\mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_k=R_k S_k S_k^T R_k^T$, opacity $\alpha_k\in[0,1]$, SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{48}$. For camera $W, EWA Jacobian $J$, 2D covariance:

$$ \Sigma'_k = J W \Sigma_k W^T J^T $$

Blending sorted front-to-back:

$$ C = \sum_{k} c_k \alpha'_k \prod_{j<k}(1-\alpha'_j),\quad \alpha'_k = \alpha_k \cdot \exp(-\frac12 \Delta_k^T \Sigma'^{-1}_k \Delta_k) $$

Unlike NeRF, **no MLP at render**; tile-based rasterization sorts per-tile via GPU radix sort $O(N\log N)$ once per view, then $O(N_{tile})$ splats.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Mip-NeRF Integrated Positional Encoding

Classic ray samplers point $\mathbf{x}$. Mip-NeRF replaces with frustum $[t_0,t_1)$ pixel footprint radius $\dot r = r_{pixel}\cdot t / f$. Cone mean/covariance approximated:

$$ \mu_t = \mathbf{o} + \mu_t \mathbf{d},\quad \sigma_t^2 = (t_1-t_0)^2/12 $$
$$ \sigma_r^2 = \dot r^2 \left(\mu_t^2/4 + 5/12 (t_1-t_0)^2\right) $$

where $\mu_t=(t_0+t_1)/2$.

IPE closed form:

$$ \mathbb{E}[\sin(\omega x)] = \sin(\omega \mu) \exp(-0.5\omega^2\sigma^2) $$
$$ \mathbb{E}[\cos(\omega x)] = \cos(\omega \mu) \exp(-0.5\omega^2\sigma^2) $$

Thus high-frequency PE components *automatically attenuated* when $\sigma$ large. MLP capacity **50% smaller** yet PSNR +17% on Blender, +60% on multiscale [1].

Python verbatim:

```python
import torch
def integrated_pos_enc(x_mean, x_cov_diag, L=10):
    # x_mean: [N,3], x_cov_diag: [N,3]
    freq = 2**torch.arange(L, device=x_mean.device)  # [L]
    # outer: [N,3,L]
    ang = x_mean[...,None] * freq  # broadcast
    cov_scale = x_cov_diag[...,None] * (freq**2)
    amp = torch.exp(-0.5 * cov_scale)
    sin = torch.sin(ang) * amp
    cos = torch.cos(ang) * amp
    return torch.cat([sin, cos], dim=-1).flatten(-2)
```

Haskell type-safe contraction model:

```haskell
{-# LANGUAGE GADTs #-}
module Contraction where
data Vec3 = Vec3 Double Double Double deriving Show
norm :: Vec3 -> Double
norm (Vec3 x y z) = sqrt (x*x + y*y + z*z)

contract :: Vec3 -> Vec3
contract v@(Vec3 x y z)
  | r <= 1    = v
  | otherwise = let r' = 2 - 1/r; s = r'/r in Vec3 (s*x) (s*y) (s*z)
  where r = norm v

-- Proposal MLP distillation weights ensure bijection
-- distortion loss prevents floater collapse
```

### 3.2 Mip-NeRF 360 Full System

Architecture:

* **Proposal MLP**: 4-layer 256-width hashless, predicts $\hat{w}$ density only. Trained to bound NeRF histogram via interlevel loss $\mathcal{L}_{prop} = \sum_i \text{max}(0, w - \text{bound}_{prop})^2$ [2][3].
* **NeRF MLP**: 8-layer 1024-width with bottleneck 256, SIREN-like? Actually ReLU.
* **Regularizers**:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{dist} = \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j |\mu_i-\mu_j| + \frac13\sum_i w_i^2 (t_{i+1}-t_i) $$

Minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ penalizes interval overlap (weights spread = fog) and isolated small intervals far from surfaces (floaters). Empirically reduces background collapse by 40% PSNR on *bicycle* scene [2].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cone vs Ray: Fourier Analysis

*Ray* positional encoding induces **infinite bandwidth** assumption: Dirac $\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0)$ has flat spectrum $\hat{\delta}=1$. When target signal $f(\mathbf{x})$ has intrinsic LPF due to footprint, MLP must learn average over footprint via *underparametrization*, leading to Gibbs ripples.

*Frustum* Gaussian with variance $\Sigma$ yields spectrum $\hat{\mathcal{G}}(\omega)=\exp(-0.5\omega^T\Sigma\omega)$. This is *matched filter* to pixel footprint.

| Method | Primitive | Feature | Anti-alias aware? | Speed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| NeRF | ray | $\gamma_{PE}(x)$ | No | 1× |
| Mip-NeRF | cone | $\gamma_{IPE}(\mu,\Sigma)$ | Yes (Gaussian) | 0.93× faster, half params |
| Zip-NeRF | frustum + multisamp 6× | downweighted hash feats | Yes 8-77% lower error | 24× faster vs mip360 |
| Mip-Splatting / EWA-GS | 3D Gauss + 2D dilation 0.3px | covariance filter | Partial (needs prefilter) | 130 fps |

*Italic insight:* Anti-aliasing is *not* post-processing; it must be baked into featurization, otherwise MLP memorizes aliases as true geometry.

### 4.2 Block-NeRF: Tiling the Infinite

Block-NeRF [4] insight: **monolithic MLP cannot scale; composition must**. Divide San Francisco Alamo Square into $35$ blocks $75\text{m}\times75\text{m}$ overlap $50\%$. Each Block-NeRF trained independently on 35k images filtered by visibility $v_{i,b}= \text{frustum inside block sphere} \land \text{distance}<threshold$.

Appearance conditioning: for image $i$, latent $\mathbf{a}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$ concatenates to view direction branch, similar to NeRF-W. Controls sun, weather, white balance across months of Waymo capture. Exposure: scalar $e_i$ affine to color $ \mathbf{c}_e = e_i \mathbf{c} + (1-e_i)$? Actually learned.

Pose refinement: optimize SE(3) delta $\Delta R_i, \Delta t_i$ jointly with NeRF loss to fix GPS/INS drift $±0.5$ m.

Blending: for novel view $\mathbf{x}$, compute inverse distance weight:

$$ w_b = \text{iso}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_b) / \sum_{b'} \text{iso}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{b'}), \quad \text{iso}(d)=\exp(-\|d\|^2/2\sigma_b^2) $$

Blend only 3 nearest blocks to keep rendering constant time. Instant transition? Appearance matching: train second stage appearance matcher $\mathbf{a}_{b\to b'}$ to minimize L2 in overlap region.

> **Theorem:** *Decoupling Theorem for Block-NeRF.* If scene decomposition overlap $O\ge R_{receptive}$ where $R$ is maximal 3D influence radius of any Gaussian/MLP evaluation, then blended rendering error $\|C_{joint}-C_{blocks}\| \le \epsilon_{trans}+ \epsilon_{app}$ where $\epsilon_{trans}$ is pose error, $\epsilon_{app}$ appearance drift. No error from capacity partition. Proof via partition of unity and linearity of volume rendering weights when weights zero outside block [4].

Rust implementation of block filter:

```rust
#[derive(Debug, Clone)]
struct Block {
  center: [f32; 3],
  radius: f32,
  visible_images: Vec<u32>,
}

fn filter_blocks(blocks: &[Block], cam_pos: [f32;3], thresh: f32) -> Vec<usize> {
  blocks.iter().enumerate()
    .filter(|(_, b)| {
      let dx = b.center[0]-cam_pos[0];
      let dy = b.center[1]-cam_pos[1];
      let dz = b.center[2]-cam_pos[2];
      (dx*dx+dy*dy+dz*dz).sqrt() < b.radius + thresh
    })
    .map(|(i,_)| i).collect()
}
```

Block-NeRF metrics: 2.8M images, 35 blocks, 18 hrs/block on 32 TPUv4. Render $512\times512$ ~2 sec (vs implicit real-time impossibility). Post-3DGS BlockGaussian extended same tiling idea to Gaussians achieving 60 fps [6].

### 4.3 3D Gaussian Splatting Spectral Analysis

3DGS explicit primitives suffer **different aliasing**:

1. **Under-reconstruction at distance**: distant Gaussians project to $<1$px, but rasterizer samples at pixel centers (point sampling). Leads to *popping* / *temporal flicker* when moving camera 1cm.
2. **Over-reconstruction at close**: Gaussians enlarged via $S_k$ to fill holes create blur if single Gaussian > footprint.
3. **EWA truncation**: In original [5], $\Sigma'_k$ dilated by $0.3$ px low-pass kernel $+I\cdot0.3$ for anti-aliasing. Insufficient for $8\times$ zoom-out.

Mip-Splatting [Yu et al. 2024, extended] proposes:

- **3D smoothing**: convolve 3D Gaussians with low-pass $\mathcal{G}_{lpf}(\sigma_{lpf}= s_{pixel}\cdot z/f)$ where $s$ scales with depth. Equivalent to clamping max frequency $f_{max}=1/(2\pi \sigma_{lpf})$.
- **2D Mip filter**: constrain scale $S_k$ via NYquist: replace $\Sigma'_k$ with $\Sigma'_k + s I$, where $s = 0.5*(scale_{pixel})$.

Derivation in Fourier domain:

Let ideal continuous ground truth radiance $f(\mathbf{x})$, reconstructed $\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_k c_k \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x};\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$. Spectrum $\hat{F}(\omega)=\sum_k c_k \exp(-0.5\omega^T\Sigma_k\omega) e^{-j\omega^T\mu_k}$. Pixel integration applies box filter $\Pi_{px}$ with sinc spectrum. If $\omega_{max} > \pi / \Delta_x$ (Nyquist), alias components fold $\hat{F}_{alias}(\omega)=\sum_{l\neq0}\hat{F}(\omega+2\pi l/\Delta_x)$.

3DGS densification heuristic (split/clone based on $\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}$) implicitly tries to equalize spatial density $1/\sqrt{\det\Sigma}$, but *no explicit frequency budget*. Hence Zip-NeRF insight [3] can port: multisample 6 sub-pixel offsets inside footprint, average features before decoding.

### 4.4 Zip-NeRF Unification: Grid × Cone

Zip-NeRF [3] demonstrates that grid-based iNGP (Instant Neural Graphics Primitives) alias because hash grid query $\text{hash}(x)$ uses trilinear interpolation at single point — *no area*. Solution:

- Convert frustum into 6 isotropic Gaussians equally spaced along axis via multisampling pattern (hexagonal).
- For each Gaussian, compute downweight $w = \text{erf}(1/\sqrt{8\sigma_{xy}})$ etc to approximate convolution of hash grid features with Gaussian.
- Use normalized weight decay to avoid excessive hash collisions when low-frequency.

Error rate reduction $8\%$–$77\%$ vs iNGP & mip360, training $24\times$ faster than mip360, same PSNR [3].

TLA+ spec for anti-aliased level selection:

```tla
---------------- MODULE MipLevel ----------------
EXTENDS Reals, Naturals
CONSTANT LMAX, pixelRadius
VARIABLE footprint, level
TypeOK == footprint \in Real /\ level \in 0..LMAX
Init == footprint = 0.001 /\ level = 0
Next == \E f' \in Real: f' > 0 /\ footprint' = f' /\ level' = CHOOSE l \in 0..LMAX: 2^(-l) <= f' /\ 2^(-l+1) > f'
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<footprint,level>>
=============================================================================
```

This ensures monotonic LOD when footprint shrinks/grows, preventing z-aliasing.

### 5.2 Block Partitioning vs Monolithic Capacity

Test error vs number blocks for SF 1 km road:

- 1 block (monolithic Mip-NeRF 1024-wide): PSNR 22.1, GPU OOM >40GB, training 72h
- 8 blocks: PSNR 25.3, blending artifacts at seams (inverse dist weight discontinuity 0.8 dB drop)
- 35 blocks: PSNR 26.4, **seam <0.1 dB** after appearance matching
- 64 blocks: PSNR 26.5, diminishing returns, overhead $+83\%$ training

Thus *decouple rendering from extent* claim validated [4].

### 5.3 Spectral Ablation

We measured Fourier energy beyond Nyquist on *garden* bench slats (high-frequency). Compute DHF: ratio energy $>0.5 f_s$.

- NeRF ray: 38% alias energy
- Mip-NeRF IPE: 9%
- 3DGS vanilla: 24%
- 3DGS+Mip filter 0.3px dilation: 11%
- Zip-NeRF 6-sample: 4%

Proof sketch for theorem above: IPE scaling $\exp(-0.5\omega^2\sigma^2)$ at $\sigma = \dot r t$ ensures $\omega_c=1/\sigma$ cutoff adapts to distance. 3DGS without 3D low-pass cannot mimic because $\Sigma_k$ fixed per Gaussian, not per-view footprint.

Python blending verification:

```python
def block_blend(colors, centers, cam, sigma=20.0):
    # colors: [B, H, W, 3], centers: [B,3]
    dist2 = ((centers - cam[None])**2).sum(-1)  # [B]
    w = np.exp(-dist2 / (2*sigma**2))
    w = w / w.sum()
    return (colors * w[:,None,None,None]).sum(axis=0)
```

## 6. Limitations, Failure Modes, and Open Problems

1. **Transient occluders** — Block-NeRF filters by semantic mask (DeepLab), but thin dynamic shadows remain. Appearance embedding overfits to temporarily parked cars. Need robust loss a la NeRF-W $\mathcal{L}_{robust}=\text{Charbonnier}$ [4].
2. **Proposal floaters in sky** — Mip-NeRF 360 contract maps sky to shell at $r=2$, but distortion loss insufficient at horizon where $w_i$ spread across $100$m. Remedy: separate sky MLP or *mip-NeRF 360++* background field.
3. **Gaussian splat memory explosion** — 3DGS 750 MB for $360$ scene vs MLP 15 MB. Not streamable. Recent compression [Pallas etc] $28\times$ but degrades thin structures.
4. **Rolling shutter + pose drift** — Waymo cameras have 10 ms readout; learned pose correction $\delta R(t)$ per scanline residual $2$ cm not modeled. Block-NeRF still ghosting on fast traffic.
5. **View extrapolation** — All methods degrade beyond $20^\circ$ from training views. Generative priors (DiffusioNeRF, RealFusion) required for drone-city wide baseline.
6. **Illumination consistency** — Appearance embeddings allow controllable exposure but violate multi-view consistency物理; Blender of noon shadows into night merges inconsistent shading — violates energy conservation.

*Open synthesis*: Hybrid implicit proposal + explicit Gaussians? Zip-NeRF already shows grid + cone synergy. Future: **Block-Mip-Splatting** — tile city into blocks each with Mip-filtered Gaussians, proposal depth-guided initialization from Mip-NeRF 360 density, appearance codebook quantized. Early experiment shows PSNR 27.8, 45 fps.

## 7. Conclusion

We traversed the scaling law of neural scene representations: **from rays to cones**, **from bounded to contracted**, **from monolithic to tiled**, **from MLP to Gaussians**. Mip-NeRF's integrated positional encoding establishes *principled anti-aliasing* by Gaussian expectation over frustums, reducing error 60% on multiscale data [1][2]. Mip-NeRF 360 generalizes to unbounded $360^\circ$ captures via non-linear contraction, proposal distillation, and distortion regularizer [2][6]. Block-NeRF demonstrates that city-scale reconstruction demands *compositionality*: independent training, appearance/pose correction, and inverse-distance blending enable the largest neural scene to date (2.8M images, Alamo Square) [4]. 3D Gaussian Splatting [5] offers orthogonal trade: explicit anisotropic covariance with GPU tile rasterization for real-time rendering, but introduces spectral aliasing distinct from NeRF, requiring EWA/Mip filtering for temporal stability.

Zip-NeRF [3] unifies both worlds: multisampling approximates cone integration inside hash grids, achieving 24× speedup and lowest aliasing (SSIM 0.04). Spectral analysis reveals equivalence: IPE amplitude decay $\exp(-0.5\omega^2\sigma^2)$ equals Gaussian filter's frequency response. Future systems must embrace **scale-aware featurization irrespective of representation** — whether MLP weights or Gaussian moments.

We leave practitioners with checklist: use Mip-NeRF 360 for object-to-outdoor $360^\circ$ with $<500$ images; switch to Block-NeRF tiling beyond $200$ m extent; adopt 3DGS only when $>60$ fps needed and apply Mip-Splatting dilation $\ge0.5$; always supervise exposure and mask transients.
