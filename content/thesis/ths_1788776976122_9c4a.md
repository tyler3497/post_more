# Simultaneous Localization and Mapping: EKF-SLAM Consistency, Pose-Graph Optimization, and ORB-SLAM3's Atlas Architecture

## Abstract

This thesis examines three foundational pillars of modern simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM): the consistency of extended Kalman filter (EKF) formulations, the nonlinear least-squares machinery of pose-graph optimization, and the multi-map architecture of ORB-SLAM3. Classical EKF-SLAM becomes inconsistent in large environments because linearization about inaccurate state estimates spuriously raises the rank of the observability matrix, artificially contracting covariance and rejecting valid measurements [1][2]. Pose-graph optimization escapes this pathology by re-linearizing the full trajectory, solving a sparse factor-graph least-squares problem with on-manifold Gauss-Newton methods implemented in frameworks such as g2o [4]. ORB-SLAM3 synthesizes both traditions into a real-time system built on bundle adjustment and pose-graph optimization, augmented by the Atlas multi-map system that survives tracking loss by spawning new submaps and merging them through place recognition [3]. We derive the key equations, survey results on KITTI, EuRoC, and TUM-VI, and identify open limitations.

## 1. Introduction

Simultaneous localization and mapping asks a robot to build a map of an unknown environment while concurrently estimating its own pose inside that map. The *chicken-and-egg* coupling between these two estimation problems — accurate mapping requires accurate pose, and accurate pose requires an accurate map — has made SLAM one of the most enduring research programs in robotics [8].

The historical arc of SLAM estimation divides into three eras. The **filtering era** converged on the extended Kalman filter, whose state concatenates robot pose with landmark positions; field experience revealed that in large environments the filter becomes *inconsistent*, growing overconfident in wrong estimates [1]. The **graph era** recast SLAM as MAP estimation over the entire trajectory, solved by nonlinear least squares over a sparse factor graph — embodied in g2o and GTSAM [4][5]. The **system era** built real-time visual SLAM culminating in ORB-SLAM3, fusing keyframe-based bundle adjustment, pose-graph optimization, loop closure, and a multi-map Atlas architecture recovering from tracking loss [3].

This thesis connects these three threads. We analyze *why* EKF-SLAM fails its consistency tests, *how* pose-graph optimization avoids the failure mode, and *what* ORB-SLAM3 adds: a system reusing all previous information across tracking, mapping, and loop-closing threads, surviving long periods of poor visual information via an atlas of disjoint maps merged through place recognition [3].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The EKF-SLAM Formulation

In EKF-SLAM the state vector concatenates the robot pose with *n* landmark positions. The filter maintains a Gaussian belief $\mathcal{N}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k, \mathbf{P}_k)$, where the covariance encodes marginal uncertainties and the robot–landmark cross-correlations — the mathematical soul of SLAM, enabling re-observed landmarks to correct drifted poses.

Prediction applies the nonlinear motion model $\mathbf{x}_k = f(\mathbf{x}_{k-1}, \mathbf{u}_k) + \mathbf{w}_k$ and propagates covariance through the Jacobian $\mathbf{F}_k = \partial f / \partial \mathbf{x}$. Update incorporates landmark observations via the Kalman gain from the measurement Jacobian $\mathbf{H}_k = \partial h / \partial \mathbf{x}$ [1][7].

### 2.2 Consistency and the NEES Test

The standard quantitative test is the normalized estimation error squared (NEES), which for a consistent *d*-dimensional estimator follows a $\chi^2_d$ distribution; persistent violation of the $\chi^2$ bounds means the filter claims more accuracy than it has — it is *optimistic*, the canonical symptom of EKF-SLAM failure [1][2].

### 2.3 From Filtering to Graph Optimization

Graph-based SLAM estimates the *entire trajectory* $\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_0, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N\}$ and map $\mathbf{M}$ given all measurements $\mathbf{Z}$ — the MAP problem $\arg\max p(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{M} \mid \mathbf{Z})$ factored into motion and observation terms.

Under Gaussian noise this becomes a nonlinear least-squares problem over a *factor graph*, where each measurement contributes a squared residual weighted by its information matrix [4][5]. Crucially, the graph formulation **re-linearizes** at every iteration, whereas the EKF linearizes each measurement once and marginalizes the past forever.

### 2.4 Visual SLAM Building Blocks

Modern visual SLAM rests on several components that appear throughout this thesis:

- **ORB features**: Oriented FAST keypoints with Rotated BRIEF descriptors — viewpoint/illumination invariant at low cost [3].
- **Keyframes**: a sparse subset of frames that carry the map; all estimation operates on keyframes, not raw video.
- **Bundle adjustment (BA)**: joint nonlinear refinement of keyframe poses and 3D landmarks minimizing reprojection error.
- **Loop closure**: recognizing previously visited places (typically via bag-of-words place recognition, DBoW2) and adding drift-correcting constraints.
- **IMU preintegration**: tightly coupling inertial measurements between keyframes without re-propagating at every iteration [3].

Our analysis proceeds on three levels. First, the theoretical argument for EKF-SLAM inconsistency: linearization-point dependence of the observability matrix, the rank test of Huang et al. [2], and the consistent remedies (FEJ-EKF, OC-EKF, robocentric mapping). ## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The EKF-SLAM Consistency Collapse

> **Theorem:** *In ideal (linear) SLAM the observability matrix has a nullspace of dimension 3 (planar) corresponding to the unobservable global position and orientation. The standard EKF linearizes Jacobians at the latest state estimates, which depend on the measurements; this data-dependent linearization can spuriously reduce the dimension of the nullspace, rendering globally unobservable directions apparently observable.*

This is the core result of Huang, Mourikis, and Roumeliotis [2]. In the true nonlinear system, the absolute pose of the map is unobservable — shifting the entire map and trajectory rigidly leaves all relative measurements unchanged, and the linearized system *should* inherit this nullspace. But because the Jacobians are evaluated at the current estimate rather than the true state, the linearized system acquires **spurious information** along unobservable directions. Covariance then shrinks where it should not, the NEES test fails, and the estimator becomes optimistic.

Castellanos, Neira, and Tardós demonstrated the practical consequence in 2004: in large environments the map "quickly becomes inconsistent due to linearization errors" [1]. Two families of remedies emerged:

1. **FEJ-EKF (First-Estimates Jacobian EKF)**: evaluate all Jacobians at the *first* estimate of each state, freezing linearization points so the observability properties of the linearized system match the nonlinear system [2].
2. **OC-EKF (Observability-Constrained EKF)**: at each step, choose the linearization point that minimizes expected linearization error *subject to* the constraint that the observability matrix retains the correct nullspace [2].

Extensive simulations in [2] show FEJ-EKF and OC-EKF achieving nearly identical NEES and RMS performance, both dramatically better than the standard EKF — despite using *less accurate* linearization points. The design rule: **correct observability properties matter more than linearization accuracy**. A related remedy, *robocentric mapping*, reparameterizes landmarks relative to the robot to reduce linearization errors directly [1].

The following table summarizes the consistency behavior:

| Estimator | Linearization point | Nullspace preserved? | NEES behavior (planar SLAM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard EKF | Latest estimate | No — spurious observability gain | Diverges above $\chi^2$ bounds [1][2] |
| Robocentric EKF | Robot-relative frame | Partially | Improved, still degrades at scale [1] |
| FEJ-EKF | First estimate | Yes | Consistent, bounded NEES [2] |
| OC-EKF | Constrained optimum | Yes | Consistent, matches FEJ [2] |

```python
# NEES consistency check (conceptual): fraction of samples outside the chi^2 bound
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import chi2

def nees_frac(errors, covs, d=3):
    vals = [e @ np.linalg.inv(P) @ e for e, P in zip(errors, covs)]
    return np.mean(np.array(vals) > chi2.ppf(0.95, d))

# A consistent filter stays near 5%; the standard EKF in [1] drifts far above.
```

### 4.2 Pose-Graph Optimization: Re-linearize Instead of Marginalize

Pose-graph optimization sidesteps the consistency trap structurally. Rather than maintaining a single Gaussian over robot-plus-landmarks and marginalizing the past, it optimizes the full trajectory against all measurements simultaneously [4].

The objective for a pose graph with odometry edges $\mathcal{E}_o$ and loop-closure edges $\mathcal{E}_l$ is:

$$\mathbf{X}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{X}} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} \left\| \mathbf{e}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \right\|^2_{\mathbf{\Omega}_{ij}}$$

where the residual $\mathbf{e}_{ij} = \mathbf{z}_{ij} \ominus h(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$ measures the discrepancy between the measured relative pose $\mathbf{z}_{ij}$ and the predicted one, with $\ominus$ the on-manifold difference operator on SE(3) (or SE(2), Sim(3) for scale-aware monocular constraints). Each iteration linearizes around the current iterate:

$$\mathbf{e}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}_i \boxplus \Delta\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j \boxplus \Delta\mathbf{x}_j) \approx \mathbf{e}_{ij} + \mathbf{J}_{ij} \Delta\mathbf{x}$$

and solves the normal equations $\mathbf{H}\Delta\mathbf{x} = -\mathbf{b}$ with $\mathbf{H} = \sum \mathbf{J}_{ij}^T \mathbf{\Omega}_{ij} \mathbf{J}_{ij}$, iterating with Gauss-Newton or Levenberg-Marquardt. Because **every measurement is re-linearized at every iteration**, the linearization point converges toward the true state rather than being frozen at a noisy early estimate — precisely the EKF's failure mode [4].

Three properties make this real-time:

- **Sparsity**: $\mathbf{H}$ inherits the graph's adjacency structure; sparse Cholesky factorization solves the normal equations in near-linear time for typical graphs [4].
- **Robust kernels**: Huber or Cauchy M-estimators down-weight false loop closures — a critical defense, since one wrong data association can destroy an EKF but is merely down-weighted in the graph [7].
- **Good initialization**: odometry-chained poses seed the optimizer inside the basin of convergence.

The g2o framework [4] generalizes this beyond poses: vertices and edges are templated C++ types, so the same solver handles pose graphs, full SLAM graphs, and bundle adjustment with performance comparable to problem-specific state-of-the-art implementations. GTSAM exposes the same mathematics through an explicit factor-graph API — priors, `BetweenFactor`s, and noise models composed into a `NonlinearFactorGraph` — and adds incremental solvers (iSAM2) that update the Bayes tree rather than re-solving from scratch [5].

*Conceptual contrast:* the EKF is a filter — it commits to each linearization irreversibly. Pose-graph optimization is a smoother — it revisits every decision as evidence accumulates. The empirical consequence is visible in the classic before/after loop-closure figure: an odometry-only trajectory drifts into a tangled mess, and one global optimization snaps it into the true topology.

### 4.3 ORB-SLAM3: Threads, Tightly-Coupled Inertial Fusion, and MAP Estimation

ORB-SLAM3 is, in the authors' words, "the first system able to perform visual, visual-inertial and multi-map SLAM with monocular, stereo and RGB-D cameras, using pin-hole and fisheye lens models" [3]. Its architecture descends from ORB-SLAM's three parallel threads, but every stage now exploits the Atlas and the inertial sensor:

1. **Tracking thread**: localizes each frame against the local map via ORB matching and motion-model prediction, decides keyframe insertion, and — on tracking failure — triggers Atlas map creation rather than dying.
2. **Local mapping thread**: processes new keyframes, triangulates map points, culls redundant keyframes/points, and runs **local bundle adjustment** over the covisible window — including inertial residuals when an IMU is present.
3. **Loop closing thread**: detects loop candidates for every new keyframe with the improved place-recognition pipeline, validates them geometrically, and on success runs pose-graph optimization followed by full BA in a background thread.

The first main novelty of [3] is the **tightly-coupled visual-inertial system fully based on MAP estimation, even during IMU initialization**. Earlier systems initialized the IMU with loosely-coupled algebraic steps; ORB-SLAM3 instead solves a MAP problem over keyframe poses, velocities, IMU biases, and gravity direction, using IMU preintegration on the manifold. Initialization completes in roughly 15 seconds of motion, yielding accuracy "2 to 5 times" better than previous approaches (up to 10× in the revised manuscript): 3.6 cm average error on EuRoC stereo-inertial and 9 mm in fast handheld TUM-VI sequences [3].

Place recognition gates everything else: ORB-SLAM3 replaces the classic DBoW2 query with a pipeline that boosts recall — critical for the Atlas, since map merging depends on recognizing places across *disjoint* maps built minutes or sessions apart. Each candidate undergoes geometric verification before any merging or loop correction is accepted [3].

### 4.4 The Atlas: Multi-Map SLAM as a Robustness Architecture

The second main novelty of ORB-SLAM3 is the **Atlas**, a multi-map system that reconceives tracking loss from a fatal error into a routine event [3].

> **Atlas principle:** *when tracking is lost, do not stop — start a new map. When place recognition fires across maps, merge them seamlessly.*

Concretely, the Atlas maintains a set of disjoint maps, exactly one of which is *active*. If tracking is lost (occlusion, motion blur, featureless corridor), the system:

1. Freezes the current map as non-active and spawns a fresh active map, re-initializing tracking within seconds;
2. Continues real-time operation in the new map;
3. Runs place recognition of every new keyframe against *all* maps in the Atlas;
4. On a verified cross-map match, computes the aligning transformation (SE(3) for stereo/RGB-D/inertial, **Sim(3)** for monocular to absorb scale drift) and **merges** the maps: keyframes and points are transformed into the larger map's reference, duplicates fused, and a global BA harmonizes the joint map.

This yields a property no prior visual SLAM system had: **ORB-SLAM3 reuses all previous information in all algorithm stages**, including keyframes that are covisible but "widely separated in time or come from a previous mapping session" [3]. A robot that loses tracking, wanders, and re-enters a mapped room does not merely relocalize — its intervening trajectory is retroactively anchored into a single consistent map, and high-parallax observations from the old session sharpen the new map's geometry through joint bundle adjustment.

The Atlas also handles *multi-session* SLAM naturally: maps persist across runs, so a second traversal merges with the first session's map. Keyframe culling and merging control duplication, though the fundamental lifelong-mapping question of what to forget remains open.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

**Consistency (theory → experiment).** Castellanos et al. [1] show NEES divergence of standard EKF-SLAM growing with environment size, while local-map joining with local uncertainty representations stays consistent. Huang et al. [2] quantify the fix: the standard EKF's average NEES climbs far above the $\chi^2$ 95% bound, whereas FEJ-EKF and OC-EKF remain inside it with nearly identical RMS errors — proving that *observability correctness, not linearization accuracy*, governs consistency.

**Pose-graph optimization.** Kümmerle et al. [4] evaluate g2o across 2D/3D pose graphs and bundle adjustment, matching specialized solvers while remaining general-purpose. The GTSAM tutorial [5] shows how prior and between-factors compose into a jointly optimized estimate whose covariance correctly reflects loop-closure information gain.

**ORB-SLAM3 system benchmarks** [3] (stereo-inertial unless noted):

| Dataset | Metric | ORB-SLAM3 | Prior art (VINS-Mono / ORB-SLAM-VI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EuRoC MAV (11 seq.) | mean RMSE ATE | **0.036 m** | ~0.08–0.12 m |
| TUM-VI room (fast motion) | RMSE ATE | **0.009 m** | ~0.02–0.05 m |
| KITTI odometry | translational drift | competitive SOTA | — |

Across *all* sensor configurations tested — monocular, stereo, RGB-D, with and without IMU — the authors report robustness equal to the best literature systems and substantially better accuracy, attributing the gain to MAP inertial initialization, reuse of covisible keyframes across time and maps, and the Atlas's conversion of tracking failures into mergeable submaps [3].

**Proof sketch (observability rank).** For planar EKF-SLAM, the ideal linearized observability matrix has a 3-dimensional nullspace spanning global translation and rotation [2]. Evaluating Jacobians at the evolving estimate injects estimate-dependent terms into the measurement rows, generically raising the rank by one and collapsing a nullspace dimension. The FEJ-EKF restores the rank by construction, since Jacobians frozen at first estimates satisfy the same algebraic identities as true-state Jacobians. ∎

## 6. Limitations

1. **Dynamic environments.** The static-world assumption underlies EKF consistency analysis, factor-graph residuals, and ORB-SLAM3's triangulation alike. Moving objects inject outliers; robust kernels and RANSAC mitigate but do not solve the problem [3][8].
2. **Degenerate motion.** Monocular visual-inertial initialization requires sufficient excitation; pure rotation or constant-velocity motion leaves scale and biases weakly observable, and the MAP initialization of [3] degrades gracefully but cannot conjure information that is not there.
3. **Long-term map maintenance.** The Atlas grows without bound across sessions; keyframe culling and map merging control duplication but not the fundamental lifelong-mapping problem of deciding what to forget. Appearance change (day/night, seasons) erodes place-recognition recall, the linchpin of merging.
4. **Computational scaling.** Full BA is cubic in the local window in the worst case; ORB-SLAM3 contains it via the covisibility window and a background full-BA thread, but city-scale single-session maps still strain real-time budgets — the regime where pose-graph-only back-ends (no landmarks) remain attractive [4].
5. **Consistency of the smoother.** Re-linearization cures the EKF's frozen-linearization pathology but introduces its own subtleties: wrong loop closures accepted before robust convergence, and poor initializations in highly nonlinear problems, can still yield overconfident estimates. The guarantees of [2] apply to filters, not to the iterated smoothers that dominate practice — a gap only partially closed.

## 7. Conclusion

Three ideas, spanning four decades, explain why modern SLAM works. **First**, the EKF taught us that SLAM is fundamentally about correlations — and that linearizing about noisy estimates silently manufactures information, formalized in the observability analysis of [2] and the consistency experiments of [1]. **Second**, pose-graph optimization taught us to stop marginalizing: keep the trajectory, re-linearize everything, and let sparsity and robust estimation do the heavy lifting [4][5]. **Third**, ORB-SLAM3 taught us that robustness is an architecture — the Atlas turns tracking loss into a mergeable submap, and MAP-based inertial fusion makes the IMU a first-class citizen of the estimation problem [3].

The through-line is *respect for nonlinearity*: never freeze a linearization you may regret, never discard information you may need, and never throw away a map you may revisit. Open frontiers — dynamic scenes, lifelong operation, and consistency guarantees for iterated smoothers — will likely be settled by the same discipline: analyze what is actually observable, optimize what you can re-linearize, and architect systems that degrade into smaller, mergeable pieces rather than failing all at once.
