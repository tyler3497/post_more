# Free Models, Effect Theories, and Delimited Control: A Categorical Account of Algebraic Effects and Handlers

## Abstract

Algebraic effects, introduced by Plotkin and Power and given a programming discipline by Plotkin and Pretnar's handlers, provide a categorical treatment of computational effects in which operations, rather than monads, are primary. This thesis develops the full categorical account: an effect is presented by an **equational theory** of operations; its denotation is the **free model** of that theory, inducing the computational monad; a handler is a **homomorphism** out of the free model, granted by its universal property; and delimited continuations emerge as the special case of handling with respect to the empty algebra. We prove the fundamental correspondence between algebraic operations and generic effects, show how handlers internalize **delimited control** (subsuming shift/reset and Filinski's representation of layered monads), and analyze modularity through the sum and tensor of effect theories, with operation transformers recovering Moggi's monad transformers. Empirical evidence from capability-passing compilation, the Eff language, and Haskell's freer monads is surveyed, and the limitations of the algebraicity condition — notably the exclusion of continuations and certain resource effects — are precisely delineated.

## 1. Introduction

Since Moggi's pioneering work, the semantics of computational effects has been dominated by the **monad**: a notion of computation is a strong monad *T* on a cartesian closed category *C*, and computations returning values in *X* are elements of *T X* [1]. Monads unify exceptions, state, nondeterminism, and I/O, but suffer two persistent weaknesses. First, **monads do not compose**: there is no canonical composite *T₁T₂*, and the monad transformer tower is an ad hoc engineering artifact rather than a mathematical construction. Second, the monad is an *interpretation* of effects, not a *presentation*: the programmer's vocabulary of operations — `get`, `put`, `raise`, `choose` — is buried inside the structure, unavailable for manipulation.

The algebraic account, initiated by Plotkin and Power [1], reverses this priority: **operations are primary, monads derived**. An effect is specified by a *signature* of operation symbols together with *equations* — a presentation of an equational theory *E*. The computational monad is recovered as the **free-model monad** *T X = U(F_E X)*, where *F_E X* is the free *E*-algebra on *X*. Moggi's effects become instances: the state monad is the free model of lookup and update; the exceptions monad is the free model of the empty theory extended with raising [2].

The decisive programming contribution is Plotkin and Pretnar's **handlers** [2], generalizing exception handlers to arbitrary algebraic effects. A handler supplies, for each operation, a clause interpreting it with access to the **continuation** — the delimited remainder of the computation. Denotationally, handling is precisely the homomorphism induced by the universal property of the free model.

This thesis argues that algebraic effects and handlers are the *correct* categorical setting for effectful language design. We develop the theory from first principles — signatures, free models, generic effects, handlers-as-homomorphisms — show that **delimited control** (shift/reset) is an instance of the handler discipline, and give the modular account of effect combination via sums and tensors of theories [3], connecting to monad transformers and modern implementations [4][5].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From monads to operations

Moggi's computational λ-calculus distinguishes **values** of type *A* from **computations** of type *F A*, interpreted in *T⟦A⟧*. Unit η and Kleisli extension interpret `return` and sequencing. The semantics is adequate and compositional, but the operations that *produce* effects have no syntactic status: `get` is some Kleisli arrow, not an algebraic datum.

Plotkin and Power [1] identified the missing structure. Given a strong monad *T*, an **algebraic operation** of arity *(v, w)* is an *ObC*-indexed family

> *α_X : (T X)^v → (T X)^w*, natural in *X* and compatible with Kleisli extension,

i.e. commuting with sequencing on the left: *α_Y ∘ (f*)^v = f* ∘ α_X* for all *f : X → T Y*. This algebraicity condition is the categorical essence of "effect operation": it permits the operation to move past `let`-sequencing. All of Moggi's leading examples — exceptions, state, nondeterminism, I/O — are algebraic; the notable exception is **continuations**, which are provably non-algebraic.

### 2.2 Lawvere theories

An **effect theory** is formulated as a **Lawvere theory**: a small category *L* with finite products and a distinguished object *n* for each natural number, with *n* the *n*-fold product of *1*. Maps *n → 1* are the *n*-ary operations. Models are product-preserving functors *L → Set*; the forgetful functor *U : Mod(L) → Set* has a left adjoint *F_L*, and the induced monad *T_L = U ∘ F_L* is **finitary** — conversely, every finitary monad on *Set* arises from a Lawvere theory [1]. For recursion and infinitary effects (I/O as interaction streams), one passes to **countable** Lawvere theories enriched in *Cpo* [3].

### 2.3 Handlers and delimited continuations

Plotkin and Pretnar [2] introduced the handling construct `with h handle M`. The handler *h* provides a **value clause** `return x ↦ N` and, for each operation `op`, an **operation clause** `op(p; k) ↦ N`, where *k* is bound to the **continuation** — the rest of the computation, delimited by the handler. When *M* performs `op(p)`, control transfers to the clause with the delimited continuation reified as *k*, which may be invoked zero times (exceptions), once (state), or many times (nondeterminism).

Independently, Felleisen's **shift/reset** captured *delimited* continuations, and Filinski showed any monad is representable via shift/reset — delimited control is control-universal. As §4.4 shows, this is no coincidence: **handlers are delimited control, typed and algebraized**.

| Effect | Signature operations | Equations | Induced monad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exceptions (set *E*) | `raise_e : 1 ⇢ 0` for *e ∈ E* | none | *X ↦ X + E* |
| State (store *S*) | `get : 1 ⇢ S`, `put_s : S ⇢ 1` | 7 Plotkin–Power laws | *X ↦ (X × S)^S* |
| Nondeterminism | `choose : 1 ⇢ 2` | semilattice laws | finite powerset *𝒫_fin* |
| Interactive I/O | `read : 1 ⇢ I`, `write_o : O ⇢ 1` | none | resumption/IO monad |

## 3. Methodology

Our method is the **categorical reconstruction** of effectful programming from the Plotkin–Power–Pretnar corpus [1][2][3], re-deriving the central correspondences (operations ↔ generic effects, handlers ↔ homomorphisms) and testing each against three axes:

1. **Semantic fidelity.** Every construction is validated against its free-model denotation; adequacy (Plotkin & Power, FoSSaCS 2001) is the criterion that operational and denotational accounts agree.
2. **Expressive completeness.** The handler calculus must subsume delimited control (§4.4) and theory combination must recover monad transformers (§4.5) — no expressive power of the monadic tradition is lost.
3. **Empirical grounding.** We survey Eff [4], Koka's row-typed handlers, Multicore OCaml, Haskell's freer monads [8], and capability-passing compilation [6], extracting quantitative evidence on handler overhead and its elimination (§5).

We assume graduate-level category theory (adjunctions, Yoneda, Lawvere theories) and programming-language semantics.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Algebraic signatures and the free-model adjunction

Fix a countable signature *Σ* of operation symbols, each with parameter type *P* and arity type *A* (`op : P ⇢ A`: given a parameter of type *P*, perform the operation and receive a result of type *A*). An **equational theory** *E = (Σ, Eq)* adds equations; a **model** is a set with interpretations of the operations satisfying them.

> **Theorem (Free models).** The forgetful functor *U : Mod(E) → Set* has a left adjoint *F_E*, and for every *f : X → U M* there is a unique homomorphism *f̄ : F_E X → M* extending *f*. The induced monad *T_E = U F_E* is the computational monad of the effect.

This universal property is the engine of the theory — and of handlers: a handler determines a model *M* of *E* plus a value map, and handling applies the induced homomorphism to the computation's denotation [2].

The equations *determine* the monad: the seven **Plotkin–Power laws for global state** (e.g. *get; λs. put s = return ()*) present the state monad as a free model. Change the equations and the monad changes: dropping idempotence from nondeterminism's semilattice laws yields multisets rather than sets. The theory is the *specification*; the monad is a *theorem*.

### 4.2 Generic effects and the Yoneda correspondence

Plotkin and Power's deepest insight [1] is the equivalence between **algebraic operations** and **generic effects**. A **generic effect** of arity *(v, w)* is a morphism *g : v → T w* — an element of the monad at the parameter object. The generic effect for reading is `read : 1 → T I`; for writing, `write : O → T 1`.

> **Theorem (Plotkin–Power).** To give an algebraic operation *α_X : (T X)^v → (T X)^w* is equivalent to giving a generic effect *g : v → T w*, the correspondence mediated by the Yoneda embedding between Lawvere-theory maps *w → v* and generic effects.

In nondeterminism, the *operation* `or : T X × T X → T X` (a map *2 → 1* in the theory) is the natural syntactic presentation; in infinitary I/O, the *generic effect* `read : 1 → T I` is what appears in programs. The theorem unifies them: the Yoneda embedding mediates between the algebraic (theory-internal) and computational (monad-internal) views. This duality is what lets handlers be *both* algebraic (homomorphisms) and operational (continuation-passing interpreters).

### 4.3 Handlers as algebra homomorphisms

The slogan **"handlers are homomorphisms"** is made precise by the typing rule

```
Γ ⊢ M : F A        Γ ⊢ H : C handler
──────────────────────────────────
Γ ⊢ (with H handle M) : C
```

Denotationally, *⟦M⟧ ∈ T_E⟦A⟧* and the handler determines a model *M_H* with carrier *⟦C⟧* plus a value map from the return clause; the handling construct denotes the **unique homomorphism** *F_E⟦A⟧ → M_H* applied to *⟦M⟧*. Operational behavior follows uniformly:

- **Exceptions**: the `raise` clause ignores the continuation — no further homomorphism application occurs.
- **State**: the carrier is *S → (X × S)*; `get`/`put` clauses thread state through the continuation, invoked exactly once.
- **Nondeterminism**: the carrier is a powerset algebra; the `choose` clause invokes *k* **twice** and unions results — the homomorphism property guarantees the semilattice equations by construction.

A **handler correctness theorem** [2][7] states the small-step operational semantics — substituting the captured continuation into operation clauses — is **adequate** for this denotation. Handling cannot violate the theory: every equation of *E* holds of all handled computations.

```haskell
-- The handler-as-homomorphism, in Haskell's freer-monad encoding [8]
data Eff :: [(* -> *)] -> * -> * where
  Val :: a -> Eff es a
  Op  :: Effect f => f a -> (a -> Eff es b) -> Eff (f ': es) b

-- Handling = fold over the free structure = the unique homomorphism
handle :: (a -> r) -> (forall x. f x -> (x -> r) -> r) -> Eff (f ': es) a -> r
handle ret _   (Val x)   = ret x
handle ret alg (Op op k) = alg op (handle ret alg . k)
```

This is the universal property made executable: `handle` is exactly the unique homomorphism from the initial algebra. Kiselyov and Ishii's *freer monads* [8] show this encoding supports **extensible** effects — open unions of signatures — with competitive performance.

### 4.4 Delimited control as the handler calculus

An operation clause receives *exactly* a delimited continuation: the evaluation context of the operation call, delimited by the nearest enclosing handler. Hence `shift` is definable as an operation whose clause manipulates the captured context, while the return clause plays the role of `reset`'s boundary. Conversely, Multicore OCaml compiles handlers to one-shot delimited continuations (fibers), and Xie *et al.*'s "Effect Handlers, Evidently" (ICFP 2020) gives handlers a direct operational semantics via **evidence-passing** — a typed, efficient realization of delimited control.

The categorical moral: **algebraicity is the condition under which delimited control is well-behaved**. Undelimited `call/cc` does not commute with sequencing, hence is non-algebraic — which is exactly why handlers capture only *delimited* control. The boundary of the algebraic is the boundary of the modular: not a limitation of the theory, but its central discovery.

> **Theorem (Filinski, representation).** Every monad on *Set* is representable in the continuation monad with shift/reset. In handler terms: the free-model monad of any effect theory is representable by handling with respect to a single delimited-control operation.

### 4.5 Modularity: sums, tensors, and operation transformers

The payoff of the theory-first stance is **modularity for free**. Hyland, Plotkin, and Power [3] combine effect theories by bifunctors on the category of theories:

- The **sum** *E₁ + E₂* is the disjoint union of operations and equations — yielding Moggi's **exceptions** and **resumption** monad transformers.
- The **tensor** *E₁ ⊗ E₂* adds **commutativity equations** between the two families — yielding the **state** transformer.
- The **distributive tensor** adds distributivity laws, covering nondeterminism combinations.

| Combination | Theory operation | Transformer recovered |
|---|---|---|
| Sum *E₁ + E₂* | disjoint union, no new equations | exceptions, resumption |
| Tensor *E₁ ⊗ E₂* | commutativity equations *op₁/op₂* | state (side-effects) |
| Distributive tensor | distributivity laws | nondeterminism combinations |

Each combination comes with **operation transformers**: a systematic way to redefine operations of the combined theory, recovering Moggi's monad transformers as theorems [3]. Contrast Haskell, where each transformer is hand-written and the *n²* lifting problem arises. In the algebraic setting, **handlers compose by layering** — `with h₁ handle (with h₂ handle M)` — and the tensor's commutativity equations say exactly when reordering is sound. Row-based systems make this practical: **Koka** tracks effects as rows *⟨exn, state | μ⟩*; **Effekt** [6] uses *capability-passing style* so handlers are lexically scoped capabilities with zero dynamic lookup.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketches

**Proposition 1 (Handler adequacy [2]).** *The operational semantics of handling is adequate for the free-model denotation.*

*Sketch.* By logical relations over the free model: induction on big-step derivations shows the homomorphism induced by the handler maps *⟦M⟧* to the denotation of the reduct. The key lemma is that the operational rule for `op` — substituting the *delimited continuation* (an evaluation context *E*) into the operation clause — matches the homomorphism's action on the generator `op(p; −)`, since evaluation contexts denote Kleisli arrows and algebraicity makes operations commute with them. ∎

**Proposition 2 (Correctness of theory combination [3]).** *The monad of the sum (resp. tensor) of theories is the coproduct (resp. commuting tensor) of the corresponding monads, with operation transformers uniquely determined.*

*Sketch.* Sums are coproducts in the category of Lawvere theories; the free-model functor, a left adjoint, preserves them. For the tensor, the imposed commutativity equations are exactly those making the two monad structures commute — the tensor product of monads in enriched category theory. ∎

### 5.2 Empirical evidence

The theory predicts handlers should be *compilable to efficient code* — the homomorphism is a fold, and folds fuse. Three lines of evidence confirm this:

1. **Capability-passing compilation** [6]. Translating the handler calculus λ^Cap to simply-typed λ-calculus in *iterated* CPS, Schuster *et al.* report order-of-magnitude speedups over naive implementations, and a staged subset (λ^λ_Cap) for which the translation *provably eliminates all handler-related abstractions* — abstraction without regret via a type-directed staging translation.
2. **The Eff language** [4]. Eff's resource-aware operational semantics yields handler-based backtracking and state competitive with native code; its `resource` construct shows the algebraic discipline scales to real effectful programming (I/O, randomness, concurrency).
3. **Handlers in action** [5]. A large case study in Haskell and SML — concurrent actors, transactional state — demonstrates composable, modular effectful programs, with the equational theory enabling *proof*: programs are verified against the theory's equations independently of any handler.

Together these validate the categorical account empirically: the free-model/homomorphism structure is not only the right semantics but a *compilation-friendly* one, because universal properties are folds — and folds are what optimizers understand.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Non-algebraic effects are excluded.** Undelimited continuations (`call/cc`) do not commute with sequencing and are provably non-algebraic; the theory covers only the delimited fragment. Effects whose operations inspect the entire future (e.g. certain backtracking/resource protocols) strain the algebraicity condition, and the general characterization of "which control effects are algebraic" remains open.
2. **Equations are global and static.** The theory *E* is fixed before handling; handlers interpret operations but cannot change the laws. Effects with *dynamic* protocols have no direct account, though work on scoped and latent effects begins to address this.
3. **The tensor is not always the desired combination.** Commutativity equations are strong: state tensored with I/O forces every `get`/`put` to commute with every `read`/`write`, which buffering breaks. The theory tells you *where* to look (the category of theories) but not *which* combination your application needs.
4. **Multi-shot continuations are costly.** Invoking *k* many times (nondeterminism, probabilistic programming) requires copying the delimited context; one-shot restrictions (Multicore OCaml) recover efficiency at the cost of expressiveness.
5. **Dependent and quantitative extensions are nascent.** The classical account is simply-typed and qualitative; integrating dependent types and quantitative reasoning (probabilities, costs) with free-model machinery is active research.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The arc of this thesis: **operations, not monads, are primary**; an effect is an equational theory, its monad the free model, and a handler the homomorphism the universal property provides. From this single adjunction flow the programming constructs — generic effects via Yoneda, handling via homomorphism induction, delimited control as the algebraic fragment of continuations, and modular combination via sums and tensors of theories.

The practical significance is that categorical structure *is* compilation structure: homomorphisms are folds, folds fuse, and staged compilation can erase the handler abstraction entirely [6]. The surveyed systems — Eff [4], Koka, Effekt, Multicore OCaml, Haskell's freer monads [8] — show the theory scales from blackboard to benchmark.

The deepest lesson is the identification of **algebraicity as the boundary of modular control**. What commutes with sequencing can be handled; what cannot resists modular treatment. In delineating this boundary precisely, the theory of algebraic effects does for control what monads did for effects: it provides the mathematics in which the next generation of effect systems will be written.
