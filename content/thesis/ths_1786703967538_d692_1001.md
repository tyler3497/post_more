# Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Hyperscale Data Centers: Power Capping via RAPL and Liquid Cooling Telemetry, Workload Shifting with Carbon Intensity Forecasting, Scope 2 and Scope 3 Emission Accounting with PUE-WUE-CUE, and Market-Based Demand Response for Grid Interaction

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, PhD-level investigation of ***carbon-aware scheduling data centers RAPL power capping*** within the broader context of ***sustainable computing***, synthesizing classical foundations with 2024-2026 systems practice and contemporary experimental evaluation. We formalize cost models, safety invariants, and coherence protocols spanning sustainable computing, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, and ACM DL. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and neuromorphic/quantum/CSD/chiplet hardware traces. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, build time with statistical confidence intervals and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage bounds, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified efficient scalable carbon-aware scheduling data centers RAPL power capping production systems.
