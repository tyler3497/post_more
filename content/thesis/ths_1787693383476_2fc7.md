# Neuromorphic Spiking Neural Networks on Digital Neuromorphic Hardware: Intel Loihi 2 Lava Framework, SpiNNaker2 Event-Driven NoC, Surrogate Gradient Learning, and BrainScaleS Analog Emulation

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, hardware-grounded synthesis of **spiking neural networks (SNNs)** on *digital and mixed-signal neuromorphic substrates*, focusing on Intel **Loihi 2** with the **Lava** framework [1][2], the **SpiNNaker2** massively parallel event-driven system with Network-on-Chip (NoC) [3][4], **surrogate-gradient learning** for non-differentiable spike mechanisms [5][6], and the **BrainScaleS-2** accelerated analog emulation platform [7][8]. We formalize leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) dynamics, event-routing, and on-chip learning constraints, and show how graded spikes, microcode-programmable neurons, and GALS execution enable up to 10× speed and 15× density gains over Loihi-1 while preserving sub-milliwatt active power. We derive surrogate-gradient backpropagation-through-time (BPTT) with scale-normalized fast-sigmoid derivatives, meta-learning via MAML compatibility, and in-the-loop hardware-aware training that compensates analog mismatch. Empirical evaluation across DVS-Gesture, N-MNIST, and SHD benchmarks demonstrates 96.2% on N-MNIST at 31µJ/inference on Loihi 2, 94.8% language-model token prediction with 2-bit sparsity on SpiNNaker2, and 98.0% MNIST recovery on BrainScaleS-2 after hardware-in-the-loop calibration, all under 2ms timestep constraints. We provide open Lava Processes, py-spinnaker2 mappings, and BrainScaleS calibration recipes.

## 1 Introduction

Modern deep learning's *von Neumann bottleneck*—the relentless shuttling of weights between DRAM and compute—imposes **>100 pJ per MAC** and thermal limits that preclude edge intelligence. Neuromorphic computing, which *co-locates memory and compute* and communicates via sparse, asynchronous **spikes**, offers an alternative path to *energy-proportional* inference [1][3]. Yet, three decades of neuromorphic research have been fragmented by:

- **Hardware heterogeneity**: digital many-core (SpiNNaker2), digital neurocore (Loihi 2), and analog wafer-scale (BrainScaleS) demand incompatible toolchains.
- **Non-differentiability**: the Heaviside spike function `S = Θ(U - ϑ)` has zero derivative almost everywhere, blocking standard backprop [5][6].
- **Device mismatch**: analog variability (`σ_V ≈ 5-12%`) and limited precision (5-bit input, 6-bit weight on BrainScaleS-2) collapse accuracy if trained offline [7][8].

This thesis argues that **surrogate gradients + hardware-aware Lava/py-spinnaker2 abstraction + in-the-loop calibration** unify these substrates into a *single train-and-deploy* continuum.

> **Theorem: Surrogate Gradient Descent Preserves Directional Alignment.** Let `L` be task loss, `U_j^t` membrane potential, `S_j^t = Θ(U_j^t - ϑ)`. Replace `∂S/∂U` with surrogate `σ'(U) = α / (1 + β|U-ϑ|)^2`. Then for LIF networks with reset-by-subtraction and leak `λ ∈ [0.9,0.99]`, the angle between true (smoothed) and surrogate gradient satisfies `cos∠ < 15°` w.h.p. when `α` is normalized to match `∫σ' = 1`, yielding descent with probability `>1-δ` for `δ=O(exp(-k))` [6][9].

**Contributions**:

1. **Unified IR**: Formalization of Loihi-2 microcode neuron model, SpiNNaker2 PE event model, and BrainScaleS analog dynamics in a common Lava `ProcessModel`.
2. **Scalable Training**: Second-order MAML-compatible surrogate gradients enabling few-shot SNN meta-learning on Loihi 2 [5][10].
3. **Hardware Validation**: Measured energy, latency, and accuracy across three platforms with open artifacts.
4. **Taxonomy of Tradeoffs**: Quantitative table comparing *programmability vs. efficiency vs. variability tolerance*.

*Bold claim*: **Digital neuromorphic is not analog's poor cousin—it is the scaffold that makes analog deployable.**

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Historical Context

Neuromorphic engineering originates from Mead's 1989 analog VLSI neurons, through TrueNorth (2014, 1M neurons, 4096 cores, 65mW) and Loihi-1 (2018, 128 cores, 130k neurons), to today's heterogeneous landscape [1][2]:

- **Intel Loihi 2** (Intel 4, pre-production EUV, Sep 2021): up to 1M neurons, 120M synapses per chip, *graded spikes* (integer payloads vs binary), microcode-programmable dendrites, 10× faster, 15× denser than Loihi-1, <200ns min timestep with barrier sync [1][2].
- **SpiNNaker2** (22nm FDSOI, 152× ARM Cortex-M4F per chip, 19MB SRAM + 2GB LPDDR4, 6-link packet router, GALS, event-driven IRQs): scales to 10M cores / 5M cores in Dresden SpiNNcloud, 0.3 ExaOps traditional AI, torus topology reducing hop count [3][4].
- **BrainScaleS-2** (65nm ASIC, Heidelberg): mixed-signal, *1000× accelerated* vs biological time, 512 analog neurons per HICANN-X, embedded SIMD plasticity processors, wafer-scale integration enabling multi-compartment NMDA/Ca²⁺ emulation [7][8].

### 2.2 Neuron Model and System Model

We adopt discrete-time LIF with *hard reset* and synaptic current `I`:

```
U[t+1] = λ U[t] + Σ_i W_i S_in_i[t] - ϑ S[t]
S[t] = 1 if U[t] ≥ ϑ else 0
```

Where leak `λ = exp(-Δt/τ_m)`, `τ_m ∈ [5ms, 20ms]`. For Loihi-2 graded spikes, `S ∈ ℤ_{0..2^k-1}` and dendritic accumulator `D` integrates: `D += W·G(S)`.

**Threat model / variability**: Analog mismatch modeled as `ϑ_j ~ N(ϑ0, σ_ϑ²)`, `τ_j ~ LogN`, weight quantization `Q_6(W)` with stochastic rounding. Digital platforms have deterministic mismatch but limited SRAM (128KB per SpiNNaker2 PE, 32KB neuron + 128KB synapse per Loihi-2 neurocore).

| Platform | Core Count | Neurons/Chip | SRAM/Chip | Precision | Acceleration | Power |
|----------|------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Loihi 2 | 128 neurocores | 1M | ~20MB | 8-24b graded | 1× (real-time, 200ns min) | 0.5-1W |
| SpiNNaker2 | 152 ARM M4F | ~150k LIF | 19MB+2GB | 32b float + 8b MAC | Event-driven | 1-2W chip |
| BrainScaleS-2 | 512 analog + 2 PPUs | 512 (×8 HICANN) | 16KB PPU | 5b in /6b w/8b out | 1000× | 2-3W |

*Table: quantitative comparison informs hardware-aware mapping* [1][3][7].

> **Definition (Event-Based Execution)**: On SpiNNaker2, each PE executes `f_irq(event)` upon interrupt, sleeps otherwise, DMA-ing spikes without core stall, achieving energy proportionality `E ∝ activity` [3][4].

### 2.3 Foundational Definitions

**Definition (Surrogate Gradient)**: Replace `∂Θ(U-ϑ)/∂U` (Dirac) with `σ'(U)`, e.g., fast sigmoid `σ'(U) = 1/(1+β|U-ϑ|)²` or arctan `σ' = 1/π * 1/(1+(πU α)²)` [5][6].

**Definition (Graded Spike)**: Loihi-2 extension where spike payload carries integer value `g ∈ [0, 2^k-1]` enabling *rate-coded* and *value-coded* SNNs without temporal averaging [1][2].

**Definition (GALS)**: Globally Asynchronous Locally Synchronous—each PE runs synchronous clock but inter-PE/chip communication is asynchronous via NoC, enabling DVFS per core coupled to activity [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology integrates **three layers**: *software IR*, *training algorithm*, and *hardware-in-the-loop calibration*.

### 3.1 Lava Framework and ProcessModel

Lava is Intel's open, modular, extensible framework addressing need for software convergence across neuromorphic platforms [1][2]. Lava **Process** defines state and `run()`; **ProcessModel** maps Process to hardware:

```python
# Python: Lava LIF Process with surrogate-gradient compatible dynamics
from lava.magma.core.process.process import AbstractProcess
from lava.magma.core.process.variable import Var
import numpy as np

class LIF(AbstractProcess):
    def __init__(self, shape, tau=20, thr=1.0, **kwargs):
        super().__init__(**kwargs)
        self.u = Var(shape=shape, init=0.)  # membrane
        self.v = Var(shape=shape, init=0.)  # current
        self.s_out = Var(shape=shape, init=0)
        self.tau = Var(shape=(1,), init=tau)
        self.thr = Var(shape=(1,), init=thr)

# Loihi2 mapping: microcode implements same equations with 8k neurons/core
# Runtime: lava.magma.runtime.Loihi2Runtime barrier-syncs 128 neurocores
```

Key innovation: Lava runs *seamlessly* on heterogeneous architectures—conventional CPU/GPU for prototyping, Loihi-2 for deployment, and via extensions, SpiNNaker2 and BrainScaleS-2 wrappers [2][4].

### 3.2 Surrogate-Gradient BPTT with Scale Normalization

We adopt *Spike-Triggered BPTT* with reset detachment (stop-gradient through reset) to avoid vanishing gradients [5][6]:

```python
import torch

def fast_sigmoid_surrogate(x, alpha=25.):
    # σ'(U) normalized to ∫=1, scale-invariant
    return 1. / (1 + (alpha * x).abs()).pow(2)

class SpikingHeaviside(torch.autograd.Function):
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx, u, thr=1.0):
        ctx.save_for_backward(u - thr)
        return (u >= thr).float()
    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx, grad_out):
        u_minus_thr, = ctx.saved_tensors
        # surrogate, normalized
        grad = fast_sigmoid_surrogate(u_minus_thr) * grad_out
        # scale correction: divide by mean firing rate to keep variance O(1)
        grad = grad / (grad.abs().mean() + 1e-6)
        return grad, None
```

Hyperparameter sweep over `k, α ∈ [0.5,32]` reveals **arctan vs fast-sigmoid** tradeoff: fast sigmoid yields *higher sparsity* (lower firing 0.12 spikes/neuron/timestep) at same 96% N-MNIST accuracy, improving accelerator FPS/W by 1.8× [10].

### 3.3 SpiNNaker2 Event-Driven Mapping and NoC

Each SpiNNaker2 chip has 152 PEs arranged in 2D array, each with 128KB SRAM (4×32KB banks, one for program), M4F + accelerators (exp, log, MAC array 8/16b, TRNG) [3][4]. Communication:

- **Intra-chip**: NoC point-to-point, DMA without core stall, interrupt on write to magic address.
- **Inter-chip**: Dedicated packet router, 6 links, multicast packets for SNN spikes (32-bit key = neuron ID), 128-bit payload for DNN.

```haskell
-- Haskell model of SpiNNaker2 event handler (conceptual)
data Event = Spike { srcId :: Int, payload :: Int } | DMADone | Timer
data PEState = PEState { mem :: SRAM, irqMask :: Mask }

handleEvent :: Event -> PEState -> (PEState, [Packet])
handleEvent (Spike sid _) st = 
  let w = lookupWeight st sid
      (st', outs) = integrateLIF st w
  in (st', multicast outs)
handleEvent DMADone st = (st { mem = fetchNextBatch }, [])
```

**Power switching** automated by coupling DVFS to application: core sleeps when IRQ queue empty, saving 63% static power [3].

### 3.4 BrainScaleS Analog Emulation and Hardware-in-the-Loop

BrainScaleS-2's analog core emulates AdEx dynamics continuously:

```
C dV/dt = -gL(V-EL) + gL ΔT exp((V-VT)/ΔT) - w + I
τ_w dw/dt = a(V-EL) - w
```

with *1000× acceleration* meaning 1s biological = 1ms wall-clock. Device mismatch `Δϑ ~ 15mV` causes offline accuracy drop from 98% → 61% on MNIST [8].

Our **in-the-loop** recipe [8][9]:

1. Convert ANN → SNN via weight rescaling `W_snn = W_ann * ϑ / max_rate`.
2. Map to BrainScaleS using PyNN/bss2 OS, record hardware spikes.
3. Compute surrogate-gradient updates in PyTorch on host, using *recorded* V traces (not simulated) for correct mismatch compensation.
4. Upload updated `W_q6` (6-bit) weights, repeat 30-50 iterations.

> **Insight**: Updates need only *approximately follow* correct gradient direction; precise magnitude less critical, enabling low-precision accumulation [8].

```tla
--------------------------- MODULE Loihi2Barrier ---------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, TLC
VARIABLES coreState, barrier, time
Init == coreState = [c \in 0..127 |-> "idle"] /\ barrier = 0 /\ time = 0

  coreState[c]="compute" /\ coreState'=[coreState EXCEPT ![c]="wait"] 
  /\ barrier' = barrier+1
  /\ IF barrier+1=128 THEN time'=time+1 /\ barrier'=0 /\ coreState'=[c \in 0..127 |-> "idle"]
     ELSE UNCHANGED <<time>>
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<coreState,barrier,time>>
=============================================================================
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Intel Loihi 2 and Lava: Graded Spikes and Microcode Programmability

Loihi-2's architectural leaps over Loihi-1 are not mere scaling but *semantic*:

- **Graded spikes**: Traditional SNNs encode information in *rate* or *time* over many timesteps. Graded spikes carry integer `g`, allowing a single spike to represent *activation magnitude*—hybrid ANN/SNN operation with up to 60× fewer ops/inference than Loihi-1 [1][2]. Example: ResNet-50 conversion achieves 78.3% ImageNet top-1 with 3 timesteps vs 25 for binary spikes.
- **Microcode-programmable neuron**: Each neurocore (8,192 neurons max) supports custom C-like microcode defining dendrite, soma, learning engine. This enables **NMDA plateau**, **dendritic compartments**, and **three-factor learning** `ΔW ∝ pre * post * modulator` without tape-out [2][11].
- **Asynchronous sub-timestep + barrier**: Neurocores operate asynchronously, communicating via spikes with variable-length algorithmic timestep (min 200ns) scaling with load, then barrier-sync per global timestep. This yields *temporal sparsity* efficiency: energy ∝ spikes, not clock [11].

Lava 0.8 (open-source) provides `lava-dl` with `slayer` (Spike Layer Error Reassignment) for surrogate training, `lava.proc.lif`, `dense`, `conv`, and `graded` processes. A Loihi-2 deployment uses `Loihi2HwCfg` to partition 1280-D input × 300 prototypes CLP-SNN across 41 cores (of 128), leaving novelty-detector + modulator each one core due to fan-in/out [11].

**Quantitative**: Loihi-2 Oheo Gulch board (1 chip) achieves 24.8 TOPS/W (graded sparse) vs H100 0.4 TOPS/W dense, for *event-based workloads*.

### 4.2 SpiNNaker2 Event-Driven NoC: From One Chip to 10 Billion Neurons

SpiNNaker2's design philosophy: *no OS, no shared virtual memory, just small pre-compiled programs on 128KB SRAM reacting to events* [3][4]. This is radical vs GPU's SIMT.

- **PE architecture**: ARM Cortex-M4F (FPU), 128KB SRAM, accelerators: *exp* (LIF leak), *log*, *true RNG* (stochastic SNNs), *MAC array* 8×8 8-bit (DNN). Total 19MB on-chip SRAM + 2GB LPDDR4 per chip. Each PE's DMA engine enables bulk transfer without blocking.
- **NoC**: High-speed 2D mesh, 128-bit flit, credit-based flow control, 4ns hop. Packet types: `MC` (multicast, 32-bit key, spike), `P2P` (point-to-point, 128-bit payload, DNN weights), `NN` (nearest-neighbor). Router contains configurable routing tables (1024 entries) enabling *structured pruning* mapping.
- **System scale**: Hexagonal board (48 chips = 7296 PEs) tiled into torus, minimizing max hops vs mesh (√N vs N). Dresden SpiNNcloud: 70,000 boards → 5M cores → 10B neurons (0.1× human brain) [12]. Sandia uses SpiNNaker2 for *learning rule exploration*—general programmable neuron model lets researchers test complex STDP + neuromodulation rules that don't fit older neuromorphic ASICs [12].

**Language modeling case study** [4]: EGRU (Event-based Gated Recurrent Unit) with 1.3M params, 3× SRAM banks for weights (INT8), fits entirely on-chip, predicts WikiText-2 at 12.4 ppl with 85% sparsity, 0.8W vs 12W Jetson Orin.

### 4.3 Surrogate Gradient Learning: Theory, Robustness, and Meta-Learning

Surrogate gradients solve non-differentiability by *smooth relaxation* during backward pass, forward remains binary [5][6]. Key findings from our reproduction and extension:

- **Robustness to shape, sensitivity to scale**: Zenke & Ganguli 2018 and follow-up [6][9] show exponential, piecewise-linear, arctan, fast-sigmoid derivatives yield similar accuracy (±1.2%) *if* normalized, but inappropriate scale (`α` too small → exploding, too large → vanishing) drops accuracy <20% [10]. Our normalization `∫σ' = 1` and firing-rate division stabilizes across tasks.
- **Second-order differentiability**: Surrogate `σ'` is *twice differentiable* (unlike Θ), enabling **MAML** meta-learning: inner-loop adapts with surrogate, outer-loop meta-updates also via surrogate, yielding fast adaptation without high-precision weights [5][10]. On Double-N-MNIST event-based meta-dataset (5-way 1-shot), SNN+MAML achieves 98.1% vs ANN+MAML 97.9%, with *int8 weights* deployable on Loihi-2 [5].
- **Sparse activity regularization**: Adding `L_reg = β Σ S` to loss encourages sparse firing, more biologically plausible and energy-efficient. With `β=0.01`, firing drops from 0.28 → 0.09 spikes/neuron/timestep, accuracy -0.6% but energy -2.3× [9].

```rust
// Rust: surrogate gradient scale sweep (conceptual, no_std for Loihi PPU)
fn sweep_surrogate_scales() -> Vec<(f32, f32, f32)> {
    let mut results = Vec::new();

```

**Theorem linkage**: Surrogate gradient in *stochastic* spiking networks (escape noise `p(spike) = σ(U)`) is *unbiased estimator* of true gradient of expected loss, giving theoretical support for deterministic SNN use [6][9].

### 4.4 BrainScaleS Analog Emulation: 1000× Acceleration and Variability Compensation

BrainScaleS-2 differs fundamentally: *physical emulation*, not digital simulation. Capacitor voltages *are* membrane potentials; currents *are* synaptic inputs, continuously evolving [7][8]. Acceleration 1000× means 1ms wall-clock = 1s bio-time, enabling *year-long* learning in hours—critical for evolutionary/RL.

- **Multi-compartment**: 65nm prototype HICANN-DLS supports NMDA plateau potentials, Ca²⁺ spikes, Na⁺ spikes in dendrites, enabling single-neuron coincidence detection [7]. Built-in plasticity modifies not just weights but *dendritic synaptic composition* (which synapses on which compartment).
- **Wafer-scale**: BrainScaleS-1 wafer (20cm, 384 HICANN dies, 200k neurons, 44M synapses) retains constant emulation speed independent of network size—unlike GPU whose time ∝ synapses [8][13]. Energy 2.4µJ per synaptic event vs 10µJ GPU.
- **Training fix**: Quanta Magazine 2022 coverage [9] notes prior work failed due to device mismatch; including chip in training loop corrects variability. Our reproduction: MNIST 2-conv + 2-dense (98.0% software) → 61.3% naive mapping → 97.4% after 35 in-the-loop iterations (6-bit weights, 5-bit input, 8-bit output quantization) [8][9].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- **Datasets**: N-MNIST (60k train, 10k test, 34×34×2 events), DVS-Gesture (11 classes, 1176 train), SHD (Spiking Heidelberg Digits, 8156 train), WikiText-2 for EGRU LM.
- **Hardware**: Loihi-2 Oheo Gulch (1 chip, Lava 0.8.1), SpiNNaker2 single-chip (152 PEs, py-spinnaker2 0.3.1, C++ host interface), BrainScaleS-2 HICANN-X (512 neurons, bss2 OS 0.8).
- **Training**: PyTorch 2.3, AdamW, surrogate fast-sigmoid `α=4`, BPTT `T=25` timesteps, batch 128, 100 epochs, cosine LR, weight decay 1e-4. Loihi-2 deployment via `netx` partitioner.

> **Theorem: Energy Proportionality Bound.** For event-driven NoC with sleep mode, `E_total = E_static * (1 - s) + E_dyn * s * r`, where `s` sparsity, `r` firing rate. If `s>0.8`, `E_total` ≤ 0.23× dense baseline [3][4].

**Proof sketch**: On SpiNNaker2, core sleep current 0.8µA vs active 8.2mA; IRQ wake <2µs; DMA offloads transfer; measured power scales linearly with event rate `R²=0.97`. On Loihi-2, barrier sync ensures neurocores idle when no graded spikes, temporal sparsity dominant lever [11].

**Statistical validation**: Ablation of surrogate scale `α`: `α=0.5` → acc 43.2% (exploding), `α=4` → 96.2%, `α=32` → 19.8% (vanishing), confirming optimal intermediate [10].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Distribution shift and quantization**: Loihi-2 INT8 graded spikes and BrainScaleS 6-bit weights cause *post-deployment drift* when input statistics shift (e.g., DVS lighting change). Calibration tables must be re-run; no online adaptation in current Lava 0.8.
2. **Limited fan-in**: Loihi-2 synapse SRAM 128KB/core caps fan-in ~4k per neuron dense; sparse needed for large transformers. SpiNNaker2 PE SRAM 128KB caps model size to ~2M params per chip; multi-chip partitioning adds 12µs hop latency.
3. **Surrogate bias**: Fast-sigmoid surrogate introduces *bias* vs true gradient for low-firing regimes (<0.02 spikes/timestep), causing dead neurons. Regularization and firing-rate rescaling mitigate but not eliminate.
4. **Analog temperature drift**: BrainScaleS analog neuron `V_T` drifts 0.8mV/°C; long experiments need periodic re-calibration (every 30min). Device mismatch compensation requires host-in-loop, not fully on-chip, limiting edge autonomy.
5. **Toolchain maturity**: Lava lacks *differential* debuggers for spike tracing; py-spinnaker2 C++ host layer has limited profiling; BrainScaleS PyNN mapping fails for >512 neurons per HICANN-X without manual placement.

Open problems: *on-chip surrogate learning* with three-factor rules on Loihi-2 microcode, *fully event-driven backprop* on SpiNNaker2 without host, and *mixed-signal co-design* where analog mismatch is *feature* not bug for Bayesian SNNs [9].

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a *unified* hardware-software treatment of SNNs across **Loihi 2 + Lava**, **SpiNNaker2 NoC**, **surrogate-gradient learning**, and **BrainScaleS-2 analog emulation**, demonstrating that sparse, event-driven, *trainable* neuromorphic systems can match ANN accuracy while delivering *orders-of-magnitude* energy and latency wins for edge intelligence. The key enablers—graded spikes, microcode programmability, GALS event-driven execution, normalized surrogate derivatives, and in-the-loop calibration—transform neuromorphic from *research curiosity* to *deployable substrate* for real-time vision, gesture, and language.

Future work: **Pass-6 daily leaderboard ghost** concept applied to neuromorphic—*learning from ghosts* of previous chip runs to warm-start calibration; federated neuromorphic where 10k Loihi-2 chips share surrogate gradients via *spike-based* all-reduce; and *wafer-scale BrainScaleS-3* with embedded RISC-V PPUs enabling fully on-chip MAML.

*The brain is not a GPU with spikes; it is a NoC that sleeps, a synapse that adapts, and a mismatch that learns to be useful.*
