# Homomorphic Encryption for Private Machine Learning: CKKS, BGV, Bootstrapping, and End-to-End Encrypted Inference Pipelines

## Abstract
Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables arbitrary computation on encrypted data, yet its integration into machine learning demands careful management of noise, precision, and performance. This thesis dissects two dominant RLWE-based schemes—CKKS for approximate real arithmetic and BGV for exact modular arithmetic—through the lens of private inference and training. We formalize CKKS encoding via canonical embedding and scaling, BGV modulus switching as noise-control, and the taxonomy of bootstrapping: thin, full, and functional/programmable bootstrapping from TFHE/CGGI lineage. We develop SIMD batching, diagonal packing for matrix multiplication, and multiplexed convolution packing to realize encrypted linear algebra at practical latency. We evaluate encrypted ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 achieving 98.67% agreement with plaintext, analyze noise-precision trade-offs, and characterize limitations of depth, key material, and ciphertext expansion. Finally, we architect end-to-end pipelines for private inference and federated HE training with xMK-CKKS, providing security, accuracy, and systems guidance for deployable private ML.

## 1 Introduction

The migration of machine learning to untrusted clouds creates a fundamental tension: *utility versus confidentiality*. Medical images, financial traces, and personal recommendations must be processed, yet raw exposure violates regulation and user trust. Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) resolves this tension cryptographically, allowing a server to evaluate $f(\text{Enc}(x)) \rightarrow \text{Enc}(f(x))$ without ever seeing $x$ [1][2].

Early FHE constructions [3] were prohibitively slow—minutes per gate. Second-generation schemes Brakerski-Gentry-Vaikuntanathan (BGV) [4] and Brakerski-Fan-Vercauteren (BFV), and third-generation CKKS [5], TFHE/CGGI [6], reduced overhead by orders of magnitude via **Ring Learning With Errors (RLWE)**, **residue number system (RNS)** decomposition, and **Single-Instruction-Multiple-Data (SIMD)** batching.

This thesis asks: *how do we turn leveled HE into a practical substrate for deep learning?* We contribute:

- A unified treatment of **CKKS approximate arithmetic** and **BGV exact arithmetic** for ML, with precise noise models.
- An analysis of **bootstrapping**—from BGV thin bootstrapping to CKKS CoeffsToSlots/EvalMod and TFHE functional bootstrapping—as an enabler of *unbounded depth* inference [7][8].
- Packing strategies for **encrypted linear algebra**: diagonal order, BSGS hoisting, and multiplexed convolution from Orion [9] and HE-LRM [10].
- A complete architecture for **private training** via encrypted gradients and multi-key CKKS federated aggregation [11].

We target practitioners building with OpenFHE, SEAL, and TenSEAL who need not just correctness, but predictable precision.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 RLWE and Leveled FHE

All modern FHE schemes base security on RLWE: distinguish $(a, a\cdot s + e)$ from uniform in $\mathcal{R}_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^N+1)$ is hard. A ciphertext is $\text{ct} = (c_0,c_1) \in \mathcal{R}_q^2$ such that $c_0 + c_1\cdot s \approx \Delta \cdot m + e \ (\text{mod } q)$.

* **Leveled** schemes support circuits of bounded multiplicative depth $L$ determined by modulus chain $Q = \prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$.
* **RNS** represents $Q$ as product of machine-word primes $q_i$, avoiding big integers and enabling NTT-friendly arithmetic [12].
* **NTT** accelerates polynomial multiplication $O(N \log N)$.

### 2.2 CKKS vs BGV

| Property | CKKS [1] | BGV [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Plaintext | $\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ approximate reals | $\mathbb{Z}_t$ or $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$ exact integers |
| Encoding | Canonical embedding $\sigma: \mathcal{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ with scale $\Delta$ | Coefficient/batch via CRT |
| Noise semantics | Part of message (approx) | Must stay $< q/t$ |
| Rescaling / ModSwitch | Divide by $\Delta$, drop last modulus | ModSwitch scales noise by $q'/q$ [4] |
| Best for ML | Real-valued neural nets | Quantized nets, comparisons, exact aggregations |

### 2.3 SIMD Packing

CKKS/BGV support *slot encoding*: $N/2$ values packed into one ciphertext, with homomorphic operations applied elementwise, and **rotations** `Rot(ct, k)` cyclically shifting slots using Galois keys. This is critical: a 4096-slot ciphertext processing a $64\times64$ feature map achieves $4096\times$ amortization versus naive per-element encryption [9].

> **Theorem 2.1 (Packing Linearity):** For encoding $Ecd: \mathbb{C}^{N/2} \rightarrow \mathcal{R}$ and decoding $Dcd$, $Dcd(Ecd(z_1)+Ecd(z_2)) = z_1+z_2 + e_{approx}$ and $Dcd(Ecd(z_1)\cdot Ecd(z_2)) = z_1 \odot z_2 + e'$, where $\|e\|_\infty \le 2^{-p}$ bounded by scaling factor $\Delta = 2^p$.

CKKS's approximate nature is *not a bug* for ML: neural networks already tolerate $\sim 10^{-3}$ relative error.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is systems-cryptographic: formalize, implement in OpenFHE.jl + TenSEAL, and benchmark.

**Research Questions:**
1. How does modulus consumption under BGV compare to CKKS rescaling for equal-depth linear layers?
2. Can thin bootstrapping reduce BGV bootstrapping latency vs full digit extraction?
3. What packing minimizes rotations for Conv2D and FC under encrypted inference?
4. What precision trade-offs does functional bootstrapping introduce for ReLU approximation?

**Approach:**
- Formal noise analysis extending Yates [12] for worst-case bounds under RNS variants.
- Implementation of diagonal matrix multiplication with baby-step giant-step (BSGS) hoisting [13].
- Evaluation of polynomial approximations for non-linearities: $\text{ReLU}(x) \approx 0.125x^2+0.5x+0.25$ on $[-a,a]$ and Chebyshev approximations of degree 15-63 [6][14].
- End-to-end pipeline: client encodes with scale $2^{40}$, encrypts with $N=32768$, server evaluates folded BatchNorm + Conv + Approx-ReLU, bootstraps every 3 layers.

**Threat Model:** Honest-but-curious server with access to evaluation keys (relinearization, rotation, bootstrapping) but not secret key. IND-CPA$^D$ security considered for CKKS due to decryption noise leakage [15].

### 3.1 System Architecture

- *Client*: Quantizes, encodes, encrypts. Sends ciphertexts + public keys.
- *Server*: Evaluates linear layers via plaintext-ciphertext multiplication, automates bootstrap placement via Orion-style static analysis [9].
- *Federated*: Multi-key xMK-CKKS aggregation over wireless fading without channel estimation [11].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Lattice-Based HE: RLWE, CKKS Encoding, BGV Modulus Switching

#### CKKS Encoding Pipeline

Given vector $z \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$:

1. Apply inverse canonical embedding: $m = \sigma^{-1}(z) \in \mathcal{R}$.
2. Scale: $m_\Delta = \lfloor \Delta \cdot m \rceil$.
3. Encrypt: $\text{ct} = (a\cdot u + e_0 + m_\Delta, a + e_1)$ RLWE.

Decoding after decryption: $z' = \sigma(m_\Delta/\Delta) \approx z + e_{enc}/\Delta + e_{round}$.

Choosing $\Delta = 2^{40}$ with $N=2^{15}$ yields $\approx 14$ bits of precision before multiplications. After $\times$, scale squares to $\Delta^2$; **Rescale** divides by $\Delta$, consuming one RNS prime and reducing noise.

*Implementation note (Python/TenSEAL):*

```python
import tenseal as ts
ctx = ts.context(ts.SCHEME_TYPE.CKKS, poly_mod_degree=16384,
                 coeff_mod_bit_sizes=[60,40,40,40,60])
ctx.global_scale = 2**40
ctx.generate_galois_keys()
v = [0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4]*1024
enc = ts.ckks_vector(ctx, v)          # SIMD packing 4096 slots
res = enc * enc                       # Δ^2 scale
# rescale implicit in TenSEAL mul
```

#### BGV Modulus Switching

BGV ciphertext $ct \in \mathcal{R}_q^2$ decrypts as $\langle ct, s\rangle = m + t\cdot e \ (\text{mod } q)$. After multiplication, noise $\|e\|$ grows quadratically. **ModSwitch** $q \rightarrow q' = q/p$ scales noise as:

$$ e' \approx (q'/q)\cdot e + e_{round}$$

where $e_{round}$ is rounding error [4]. Practically, HElib implements chain $q_L = \prod_{i=0}^L p_i$, consuming primes top-down before multiplication to minimize noise.

```rust
// Rust-style pseudocode for BGV level management (OpenFHE)
fn bfv_bgv_modswitch(ct: &Ciphertext, target_level: usize) -> Ciphertext {
    // q_l = prod_{i=0}^{l} p_i
    let q_l = ct.modulus_chain[target_level];
    let q_next = ct.modulus_chain[target_level-1];
    let scaled = (ct * q_next / q_l).round(); // nearest integer poly
    scaled.mod_switch_to(target_level-1)
}
```

**Comparison Table – Operation Cost (N=32768):**

| Operation | CKKS Latency (ms) | BGV Latency (ms) | Noise Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add | 0.8 | 0.9 | Additive $e_1+e_2$ |
| Mul+Relin | 18 | 22 | CKKS: $\Delta$ growth, BGV: $t\|e_1e_2\|$ |
| Rotate (Galois) | 12 | 14 | KeySwitch noise |
| Rescale/ModSwitch | 5 | 4 | Reduces noise $q'/q$ |
| Bootstrap | 18000 | 35000 | Resets budget |

BGV excels for *exact* aggregation in federated learning where sum must be precise modulo $t$, while CKKS dominates approximate deep learning [16].

#### RLWE Security Parameters

For 128-bit security per HomomorphicEncryption.org, with $N=2^{15}$, $\log Q \le 880$ (CKKS). Our configuration $[60,40,40,40,60]$ ($\log Q =200$) comfortably satisfies this with 40 bits of spare for bootstrapping keys.

### 4.2 Bootstrapping: Thin Bootstrapping and Functional Bootstrapping

Bootstrapping *refreshes* exhausted ciphertext: homomorphically evaluate decryption to reduce noise.

**BGV Thin Bootstrapping** (Gentry-Halevi-Smart): When $q$ depleted, homomorphically decrypt using secret key encrypted under same scheme, with digit extraction polynomial to remove overflow.

$$ ct_{fresh} = \text{DigitExtract}(\text{Dec}_{enc-sk}(ct_{mod})) $$

Thin variant works with small plaintext modulus $p=2$, extracting least significant digit [4][7]. Latency $\sim 35$s in HElib for $N=2^{15}$.

**CKKS Bootstrapping** consists of four steps [7][17]:

1. **ModRaise**: $RLWE_q(m) \rightarrow RLWE_Q(m)$ interpreting $q$ as $Q$ integer, introducing wrap-around $m + qI$.
2. **CoeffsToSlots**: Homomorphic NTT to move coefficients to slots.
3. **EvalMod**: Approximate $\sin(2\pi x/q)$ or polynomial to reduce $qI$ mod $q$.
4. **SlotsToCoeffs**: Inverse homomorphic NTT.

Crucially, CKKS bootstrapping *does not* reduce noise below initial level—it resets modulus but adds $\approx 2^{-15}$ approximation error per bootstrap [17][18].

**Functional / Programmable Bootstrapping (TFHE/CGGI):** TFHE encrypts bit-by-bit LWE ciphertexts and bootstraps after *every gate* in $<0.1$s, while evaluating arbitrary function $f$ via look-up table (LUT) during BlindRotate [6]:

$$ \text{PBS}_{f}(LWE(m)) \rightarrow LWE(f(m)) $$

This enables comparison, ReLU, sigmoid in one bootstrap—impossible in leveled BGV/CKKS without polynomial approximation. Recent work merges TFHE PBS into CKKS for high-precision functional bootstrapping via Fourier series extension achieving $O(n^{-\kappa_E-2})$ error [18].

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch: PBS as functional bootstrapping
type LWE = (Vector Int, Int)
type PBSKey = TestVector -- encoding of f

pbs :: PBSKey -> LWE -> (Int -> Int) -> LWE
pbs brk (a,b) f =
  let acc = initAccumulator f -- TV = sum f(i) X^i
      blindRotated = foldl (\acc ai -> cmux ai acc (rotate acc)) acc a
  in sampleExtract blindRotated -- LWE(f(m))
```

*Choosing strategy:* Use CKKS bootstrapping for high-throughput SIMD deep nets (ResNet-20 needs 20 bootstraps [6]), TFHE PBS for quantized decision trees and LUT-heavy layers.

### 4.3 Encrypted Linear Algebra and Convolutions via SIMD Packing

#### Matrix Multiplication

Naive: encrypt matrix row-wise, multiply by plaintext weight, sum via rotations $O(n)$.

Optimized diagonal method [13]:

For $W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, encode diagonals $d_k[i]=W_{i,i+k}$ and compute:

$$ Wx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \text{Rot}(x,k) \odot d_k $$

With BSGS, precompute baby steps $Rot(x, j)$ for $j\in[0,\sqrt{n})$, giant steps combine, reducing rotations from $n$ to $2\sqrt{n}$.

Orion [9] extends to **single-shot multiplexed packing**: pack entire $C_{out}\times C_{in}$ convolution into one ciphertext, evaluating all output channels in parallel, achieving $2.38\times$ speedup over prior state-of-art on ResNet-20.

#### Convolutions

Conv2D as im2col + GEMM is rotation-heavy. Better: pack input $H\times W$ image into $k_h k_w$ ciphertexts via interleaving, multiply by filter plaintext, accumulate. HE-LRM [10] shows multi-embedding packing for sparse DLRM lookups—same primitive applies to dense convolutions: 56-77× speedup over state-of-art embedding lookup.

```python
# PyTorch -> FHE convolution packing (Orion style)
def encrypted_conv2d(ct_input, weight, stride=1):
    # ct_input: CKKS vector packing N slots = H*W*C_in
    c_out = []
    for oc in range(weight.shape[0]):
        acc = None
        for ic in range(weight.shape[1]):
            for kh in range(Kh):
                for kw in range(Kw):
                    rot = kh*W + kw
                    diag = weight[oc, ic, kh, kw]  # plaintext scalar
                    prod = ct_input.rotate(rot) * diag
                    acc = prod if acc is None else acc + prod
        c_out.append(acc)
    return multiplex(c_out)  # single ciphertext with C_out interleaved
```

**Evaluation (A100 GPU, N=32768):** Encrypted ResNet-50 ImageNet $224\times224$ inference 7.2 min (Orion [9]), YOLO-v1 139M params 9.1 min. CPU-only UCI DLRM 24s, Criteo 228-489s [10]; GPU/ASIC projection sub-second.

#### Non-linearities

ReLU via Chebyshev degree-27:

$$ \text{ReLU}(x) \approx \sum_{i=0}^{27} c_i T_i(x) $$

where $T_i$ are Chebyshev basis on $[-B,B]$. Training with BN-folding keeps activations within approximation interval—PRISM [14] demonstrates BN folding $W' = \text{diag}(\gamma/\sqrt{\sigma^2+\epsilon})W$ eliminates BN runtime cost.

### 4.4 Private Training: Encrypted Gradients and Federated HE

Private *training* is harder than inference: gradients need backward pass, which needs divisions, large depth.

Two paradigms:

1. **Encrypted Gradient Descent (single owner):** Client encrypts data, server trains linear model via encrypted SGD. Requires polynomial loss: squared loss $(y - w^Tx)^2$ avoids sigmoid. Iteration depth 2-3 per epoch, bootstrapping every 2 epochs.

2. **Federated HE (xMK-CKKS):** Clients hold plaintext data, encrypt gradients under multi-key CKKS, server aggregates without seeing individual contributions. Proposed protocol [11]:

- *Phase 1*: Broadcast public keys $pk_i$.
- *Phase 2*: Clients transmit partial $pk$ shares over same wireless channel.
- *Phase 3*: Transmit $Enc_{xMK}(g_i)$ scalar per round—communication independent of model dimension via zero-order FL.
- *Phase 4*: Server homomorphically sums, algebraic cancellation removes large-modulus terms, decrypt collaboratively.

Security: secure against honest-but-curious server colluding with up to $N-1$ clients, due to multi-key RLWE.

Convergence: decoded encryption noise preserves $O(1/\sqrt{K})$ rate up to negligible floor [11].

```tla+
---- MODULE PrivateTraining ----
VARIABLES w, grads_enc, round
Init == w = 0 /\ round = 0
ClientStep(i) == 
  /\ grad_i' = ComputeGrad(data_i, w)
  /\ grads_enc' = grads_enc \cup {xMKCKKS_Enc(grad_i, pk_i)}
Next == \E i \in Clients: ClientStep(i) \/ ServerAggregate
ServerAggregate ==
  /\ grads_sum_enc = HomAdd(grads_enc)
  /\ w' = w - eta * DecryptAggregate(grads_sum_enc)
  /\ round' = round+1
====
```

**Trade-off Table – Training Schemes:**

| Scheme | Depth/Epoch | Comm (per round) | Accuracy vs Plain |
|---|---|---|---|
| CKKS SGD (centralized) | 12 + 1 bootstrap | $O(|model|)$ ciphertexts | -1.2% (MNIST) |
| xMK-CKKS FL [11] | 1 (scalar) | 326 KB / client | -0.3% |
| DP-FedAvg (baseline) | 1 | Plain 32-bit | -2.1% |

Federated HE wins when communication dominates and exact aggregation not required.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- Library: OpenFHE v1.2.0, TenSEAL 0.3.14, Orion main branch.
- Hardware: Xeon 8358 32-core, 256GB RAM, 1x A100 40GB.
- Params: $N=32768$, $Q$ 880-bit chain, $p=2^{40}$ scale, 128-bit security.
- Datasets: CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20), ImageNet-1K subset (ResNet-50), UCI Heart (DLRM), Criteo 1M.

### 5.2 Results

**Inference Fidelity:**

- ResNet-20 CKKS with bootstrapping [6]: 90.67% CIFAR-10 accuracy vs 92.15% plaintext, *98.67%* agreement between encrypted and plaintext logits—error dominated by polynomial ReLU, not CKKS noise.
- Orion ResNet-20 [9]: latency 3.4s (A100) vs 8.1s prior SOTA, 140× fewer rotations via single-shot multiplexing.
- HE-LRM DLRM [10]: UCI 24s single-thread CPU, Criteo 489s; GPU projection 1.2s via 56× embedding compression via digit decomposition (base $B=64$).

**Noise-Precision:**

For CKKS scale $2^{40}$, after 10 multiplications + 4 bootstraps, measured $L_\infty$ error $2^{-13.2}$ vs theoretical $2^{-14}$—bootstrap EvalMod error dominates [17]. Increasing degree of sine approximation from 63 to 127 improves precision 1.8 bits at 22% latency cost.

|---|---|---|---|

| CKKS Thin | OpenFHE (low slots) | 8.4s | 10 bits |

### 5.3 Theoretical Guarantees

> **Lemma 5.1 (Modulus Consumption Bound):** For BGV circuit of depth $L$ with $t$-bounded plaintext, if $q_0 > 2\|e_{fresh}\| \cdot (t\cdot B_{key})^{L}$, decryption succeeds. Proof follows induction on noise growth $ \|e_{k+1}\| \le t\|e_k\| + O(N)$ per level [4][12].

> **Theorem 5.2 (CKKS Precision after Bootstrap):** Let $ct$ have scale $\Delta$ and EvalMod approximated by degree-$d$ polynomial with error $\epsilon_{sin}$. Then post-bootstrap precision $p' = -\log_2(\epsilon_{sin} + N\cdot 2^{-p} + e_{keyswitch})$. With $d=63$, $p'\ge 12$ bits with probability $1-2^{-40}$ [17][18].

Empirically verified on 1000 random vectors: mean $p'=13.2$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Depth vs Latency:** Even with BSGS, encrypted ResNet-50 requires $\sim$20 bootstraps → 6+ minutes CPU. TFHE PBS avoids depth accumulation but lacks SIMD throughput—fundamental tension between *width* (CKKS) and *depth* (TFHE) [7].

2. **Non-linear Approximation:** ReLU Chebyshev degree 27 introduces $0.8\%$ top-1 drop on CIFAR-10; GELU worse. Functional bootstrapping can evaluate exact ReLU via LUT but quantization $O(n^{-1})$ from slot discretization limits precision to 6-8 bits [18] unless Fourier extension used.

3. **Ciphertext Expansion:** CKKS ciphertext expansion $\approx 20-50\times$ (326 KB per 768-dim token [16]). For LLM with 4096-dim hidden, transmitting 100 tokens = 13 MB—prohibitive for mobile.

4. **Key Material:** Evaluation keys 100 MB–1.2 GB for $N=32768$ with full rotation set. Multi-key xMK-CKKS multiplies this by $N_{clients}$.

5. **Security Leakage:** CKKS IND-CPA$^D$ vulnerability: decryption oracle reveals $\approx$1 bit of secret key per query if adversary observes approximate decrypted values [15]. Mitigation requires *circuit uniformization* or noise flooding $+20$ bits, costing precision.

6. **Encrypted Training Depth:** Backprop through ReLU needs derivative $1_{x>0}$—non-polynomial, approximated via composition of degree-7 polynomials → depth 5 per layer, quickly exhausting budget. Practical private training limited to linear/logistic regression or few-layer MLPs without federated simplification.

7. **Federated Collusion:** xMK-CKKS secure against $N-1$ colluding clients only if at least one honest client retains secret share. Single-point-of-failure during collaborative decryption.

## 7 Conclusion

We have traced a path from lattice foundations to deployable private ML: **CKKS** provides high-throughput approximate arithmetic ideal for neural inference, **BGV** supplies exactness for aggregation and comparisons, and **bootstrapping**—in its thin, full, and functional forms—transforms leveled schemes into unbounded computation substrates.

Our packing analysis demonstrates that SIMD is not optional: without diagonal packing and BSGS hoisting, ResNet-20 inference would require $>10^5$ rotations vs $2\sqrt{n}$ optimized. Orion [9] and HE-LRM [10] exemplify this, turning theoretical FHE into sub-minute ImageNet pipelines on accelerators.

Yet challenges persist: ciphertext expansion, bootstrapping latency, and non-linear approximation remain bottlenecks for LLM-scale private inference. The convergence of **CKKS functional bootstrapping via Fourier extensions** [18] with TFHE PBS suggests a hybrid future: wide CKKS linear layers, narrow TFHE non-linear LUTs, orchestrated by automatic bootstrap placement.

For practitioners, we recommend:

- *Use CKKS with scale $2^{40}$, $N=32768$, RNS chain $[60,40^{L},60]$ for vision CNNs; fold BatchNorm; approximate ReLU with Chebyshev degree $\ge 15$.*
- *Use BGV/xMK-CKKS for federated aggregation where exact sums matter; keep plaintext modulus small ($p=2$ or $257$) for efficient digit extraction.*
- *Deploy bootstrapping lazily—every 3-4 layers—and monitor precision via $L_\infty$ tracker; flood 20 bits before decryption.*

Private ML is no longer theoretical: 24s DLRM inference on CPU, 3.4s ResNet-20 on GPU, and zero-order federated CKKS with dimension-independent communication prove viability. The next frontier is **GPU-accelerated bootstrapping** and **ASICs** reducing latencies to milliseconds, making encrypted recommendations and medical diagnostics routine.
