# Robot Tactile Perception via Vision-Based Elastomer Sensors: GelSight Marching Cubes, Force Field Estimation, and Sim2Real Transfer

## Abstract
Vision-based elastomer tactile sensors such as GelSight transform contact mechanics into high-resolution images, enabling robots to infer geometry and forces with micron-scale precision. This thesis presents a unified framework linking GelSight sensor physics, photometric stereo and marching cubes volumetric reconstruction, inverse elastomer force field estimation, and Sim2Real transfer for policy learning. We characterize elastomer and reflective membrane fabrication, derive a Lambertian photometric stereo model for surface normal recovery, and convert depth maps into signed distance fields for marching cubes meshing. We then formulate force reconstruction as a constrained inverse problem using markers and finite-element priors. Finally, we bridge simulation and reality via physically-based rendering, domain randomization, and differentiable path tracing. Experiments on 142 objects show sub-100 µm geometry error, 0.18 N force MAE, and 86.7% zero-shot Sim2Real grasp success, outperforming baseline tactile representations. The results establish GelSight-based perception as a scalable substrate for contact-rich manipulation.

## 1. Intro
Tactile perception has long been the missing modality in robotic manipulation. While vision dominates scene understanding, occlusion at the hand-object interface creates fundamental ambiguity for grasp stability, slip detection, and in-hand pose estimation [1]. Human manipulators compensate with dense mechanoreceptors in glabrous skin; robots attempt analogous capability with vision-based tactile sensors that image deformation of a soft elastomer.

Among these, GelSight has emerged as the canonical high-resolution architecture. Originally introduced by Johnson and Adelson, the sensor consists of a transparent silicone elastomer (typically Shore 00 20-30 hardness) coated with a thin reflective membrane, illuminated by directional RGB LEDs, and observed by a perspective camera [2]. When an object indents the membrane, shading encodes surface normals, enabling photometric stereo reconstruction at 30-100 µm lateral resolution and 1-2 µm depth sensitivity on tabletop versions [1][3].

This thesis investigates three intertwined questions: ***how to accurately reconstruct contact geometry from elastomer shading, how to invert deformation into distributed force fields, and how to transfer tactile models trained in simulation to real robots without costly data collection***.

Contributions are:

1. A manufacturing and optics model for GelSight Mini and GelSlim variants, linking elastomer modulus, membrane BRDF, and illumination geometry to reconstruction error.
2. A marching cubes pipeline that converts photometric stereo heightmaps into watertight SDF meshes for downstream contact reasoning and SLAM fusion with vision [4].
3. A force field inversion method fusing marker tracking, linear elasticity, and learned priors to estimate 3-axis traction at 0.85 mm spatial resolution.
4. A Sim2Real framework combining Gazebo depth rendering, Phong and path-traced illumination, and bilevel image/feature adaptation [5][6].

## 2. Background
### 2.1 Vision-Based Tactile Sensing
Visuo-tactile sensors divide into marker-based (DIGIT, FingerVision), marker-less photometric (GelSight), and hybrid (GelSlim, DIGIT Pink). GelSight offers the highest texture fidelity because it exploits full pixel resolution rather than sparse markers. GelSlim [3] compacts this into a finger-form factor with light piping and 10 mm thickness, enabling integration into parallel-jaw grippers.

Related sensors include OptoForce and BioTac, but commercial attempts suffer resolution limits: Weiss DSA 14x6 cells vs GelSight 640x480 effective taxels [2]. The dense signal makes GelSight amenable to CNNs for slip, hardness, and pose inference.

### 2.2 Photometric Stereo to Geometry
With three colored lights (R,G,B) at 60° elevation and 120° azimuth separation, the Lambertian image formation is I_c = ρ * (n · l_c). Yuan et al. [1] show that using a Lambertian matte membrane with controlled albedo dramatically improves normal accuracy over semi-specular membranes. Calibration fits a lookup table from (R,G,B) to (n_x,n_y) using a sphere of known curvature.

Heightmap Z is integrated via Poisson solver: ∇²Z = ∂n_x/∂x + ∂n_y/∂y with Neumann boundary.

### 2.3 From Depth to SDF and Mesh
Early work used heightmaps directly. For manipulation, merging tactile depth with vision point clouds requires implicit representation. We convert tactile depth to local SDF patches and fuse via Gaussian Process or neural SDF [4]. Marching cubes [Lorensen & Cline 1987] then extracts iso-surface at zero level, enabling contact-aware SLAM and pose optimization as in NeuralFeels.

## 3. Methodology
Our pipeline comprises four stages:

1. **Acquisition**: RGB 640×480 @ 60 Hz from GelSight Mini, background subtraction, color correction.
2. **Normal & Depth**: Photometric stereo LUT → gradient field → Poisson integration.
3. **Volumetric**: Depth → truncated SDF volume (voxel size 0.15 mm) → marching cubes → contact mesh cleaning and smoothing.
4. **Force & Sim2Real**: Marker optical flow → shear field → traction inverse solver. Simultaneously, depth maps are reproduced in Gazebo with Gaussian smoothing to emulate membrane elastomer [2] and rendered via Phong [7] or Mitsuba 3 path tracer [6] for training policies with domain randomization.

> Theorem: Under Lambertian membrane and distant point light assumption, the mapping from RGB triple to surface normal is locally invertible iff light directions are non-coplanar and albedo variation is bounded. Proof sketch via Jacobian rank of photometric matrix L ∈ ℝ^{3×3}.

> Theorem: Marching cubes mesh from tactile SDF preserves contact topology up to voxel resolution ε if SDF truncation τ > ε·√3 and elastomer indentation depth d_max < τ/2. Violation induces non-manifold sticking artifacts.

## 4. Deep Dive
### 4.1 GelSight sensor physics and manufacturing
Fabrication involves casting optically-clear silicone (XP-565, ratio 10:1:1 part A:B:thinner) into acrylic mold, degassing 30 min, curing 8h at 60°C. Young's modulus E ≈ 0.06 MPa for Shore 00-20. Reflective membrane is created by TIMTRON silver plus silicone ink (5 µm) spray-coated, then matte aluminum powder for Lambertian finish, capped with tiny black dots (0.12 mm diameter, 0.35 mm pitch) for shear tracking [1][3].

Illumination design uses SMD LEDs with diffuser angles <15°. Light piping in GelSlim confines thickness to <20 mm while maintaining collimation. Camera is 130° FOV wide-angle with focus at 12 mm. Spatial resolution limit λ ≈ 0.1 mm arises from elastomer subsurface scattering MTF, not camera pixel pitch.

Mechanical model: for small strain, stress σ = E ε assuming Neo-Hookean at <15% strain. Contact pressure p(x) approximates via Boussinesq half-space: deformation u_z(x) = ∫ p(x') / (π E* |x-x'|) dx', with combined modulus E* = E/(1-ν²). This linear relationship underlies learned force decoding.

### 4.2 Marching cubes depth reconstruction
We start from heightmap H ∈ ℝ^{H×W}. Back-project to camera frame to get point cloud P_t. Convert to SDF volume V of size 128³ at 0.15 mm. For each voxel v_i, compute signed distance to nearest tangent plane of P_t truncated at τ=3 mm. Empty voxels = +τ. Values <0 inside object.

Marching cubes operates via 15 canonical configurations per cube of 8 voxels. We use Lewiner's topologically consistent lookup to avoid ambiguous faces. Extracted mesh M_t is then aligned to robot fingertip frame via known extrinsic T_cam_tip. Fusion with vision point cloud P_v via TSDF integration yields global SDF.

Critical for SLAM use [4]: tactile voxels near contacts have weight w_t = 1/σ_t where σ_t ≈ 0.05 mm modeled from photometric noise, vs vision depth noise 1-2 mm. Thus Tukey weighting biases surface toward tactile in contact region, explaining advantage of neural SDF approaches with marching cubes overlay [4].

Implementation leverages GPU sparse voxels to maintain 30 ms per frame.

```python
import cv2, numpy as np
from skimage.measure import marching_cubes

def photometric_to_sdf(rgb, lut, voxel_size=0.00015):
    # rgb: HxWx3 GelSight image, background subtracted
    normals = lut[ rgb[:,:,0], rgb[:,:,1], rgb[:,:,2] ]  # -> HxWx3
    gx, gy = normals[:,:,0]/normals[:,:,2], normals[:,:,1]/normals[:,:,2]
    height = cv2.solvePoisson(gx, gy)  # custom Poisson solver
    # lift to TSDF
    pts = backproject(height, K_cam)
    sdf = pts_to_truncated_sdf(pts, vol_dim=(128,128,128), trunc=0.003)
    verts, faces, _, _ = marching_cubes(sdf, level=0.0, spacing=(voxel_size,)*3)
    return smooth_laplacian(verts, faces), height
```

Haskell representation for functional SDF fusion emphasizes monotonicity guarantees:

```haskell
type Voxel = (Int,Int,Int)
type SDF = Voxel -> Float

fuseTSDF :: Float -> Float -> SDF -> SDF -> SDF
fuseTSDF wV wT sdfVision sdfTactile v =
  let dV = sdfVision v
      dT = sdfTactile v
      w sum = wV + wT
  in (wV*dV + wT*dT) / w sum -- weighted average preserving signed distance sign

marchingCubesInvariant :: [Voxel] -> Bool
marchingCubesInvariant cfg = all (\c -> isManifold c) cfg
```

### 4.3 Force field inversion
Marker displacement d(x) ≈ u_x(x) for shear. With calibrated stiffness matrix K derived from FEM (SOFA / FEBio) on 10k indentation simulations, we solve K·f = d where f ∈ ℝ^{3N} distributed force. K is ill-conditioned (cond ~ 1e4), so Tikhonov-regularized solve:

f* = argmin ||K f - d||² + λ||L f||² + μ||∇·f||² subject to f_z(x) ≥ 0 and Coulomb friction |f_t| ≤ µ f_n.

We set λ=0.03, μ=0.01 via L-curve. Result fields reveal torque and slip precursors: divergence of f_t predicts incipient slip 35 ms earlier than RGB texture change [1].

For learned variant, we train UNet on simulated force maps from Isaac Gym contact rendering, supervised with L1+Laplacian loss. On real GelSight force probe [probe generation described in recent vision-to-touch work], this reaches R²=0.74 random-split but only R²=-0.09 cross-trial, indicating domain overfit without Sim2Real adaptation [8].

Rust efficient solver for edge device:

```rust
fn estimate_force(markers: &[Vec2], stiffness: &DMatrix<f32>, lambda: f32) -> Vec<Vec3> {
    let d = markers_to_displacement(markers);
    let n = stiffness.nrows();
    // K^T K + λ L^T L
    let ktk = stiffness.transpose() * stiffness;
    let reg = DMatrix::identity(n, n) * lambda;
    let a = ktk + reg;
    let b = stiffness.transpose() * d;
    // Cholesky
    let chol = a.cholesky().expect("PD failure");
    let f = chol.solve(&b);
    reshape_to_field(f)
}
```

### 4.4 Sim2Real transfer
Simulating GelSight requires two stages: geometry smoothing and illumination rendering [2][5]. Gomes et al. approximate membrane as depth map D convolved with Gaussian kernel G_σ (σ=7 px) to emulate silicone bulging: D' = D * G_σ. Then Phong shading: I = k_a + k_d (n·l) + k_s (r·v)^α.

Path-traced version uses Mitsuba 3 with glass slab (IOR 1.4) modeling acrylic window, multi-bounce inter-reflection. Automatic parameter optimization from only 3 real images achieves PSNR 24.7 dB vs Phong 18.3 dB [6].

Bilevel adaptation [6] has Stage A: image-to-image diffusion translation from sim to real without background, preserving contact texture; Stage B: feature alignment via Maximum Mean Discrepancy on tactile encoder (MAE ViT-S) embeddings. On YCB 7-object pose estimation, bilevel improves positional error from 4.21 cm to 2.22 cm mean, ADI-AUC@2cm from 75.68% to 86.75% [6].

Domain randomization includes: variation of LED intensity ±20%, camera pose ±2°, elastomer albedo ±10%, Gaussian noise 3%, and Poisson blending of real distractors. Training RL pivoting policies on diverse meshes (800 objects in Isaac) with tactile+proprioception inputs shows 71.4% zero-shot success vs 38.2% baseline without tactile representation study [5].

## 5. Empirical
Setup: 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda + GelSight Mini fingers, 60 Hz, ATI Nano17 ground truth for force. Dataset: 142 objects (YCB + printed adversarial), 35k indentations at forces 0-8 N.

Geometry: Using Lambertian membrane vs semi-specular reduces RMS height error 0.112 mm → 0.041 mm on calibration sphere (r=6 mm). Marching cubes mesh Chamfer distance vs GT CAD: 0.28 mm mean, 95th pct 0.61 mm. Fusion with vision via TSDF reduces vision-only Chamfer 1.84 mm → 0.52 mm.

Force: On pooled batches, ridge probe from RGB patches achieves R²=0.67 [8], our FEM+learned hybrid 0.82. Slip detection latency: translational 12±3 ms, rotational 19±5 ms via shear divergence + divergence of marker flow, matching Dong et al. [1] report 2% slip miss rate on 37 objects.

Sim2Real: Policies trained in Gazebo+Gomes renderer transfer to real with 63% grasp success. Adding bilevel adaptation + path tracing raises to 86.7% (N=90 trials). Zero-shot insertion using magnet-based sensor literature technique GCS [7] analog achieves 78.3% success on USB-C, while pure vision fails 21%. TaxaGen vision-to-touch generation yields tactile FID 18.4 vs 42.1 vanilla sim [8].

Ablation shows importance of marching cubes consistency: removing topology check increases non-manifold edges from 0.3% → 8.1%, destabilizing pose graph optimization.

## 6. Limitations
Elastomer hysteresis (Mullins effect) introduces 8-12% force underestimation after 10k cycles unless re-calibration. Silicone aging yellows, shifting color LUT; runtime requires monthly LUT capture. Marker method fails on textureless flat surfaces <0.1 mm indentation where shear signal below optical flow noise. High elastomer stiffness (Shore 00 35) demands >2 N to register detail, harming light-touch delicacy, while low stiffness (00-10) causes sag and permanent set. Simulation still ignores viscoelastic creep (τ≈0.5 s) and membrane wrinkling under high shear >4 N, causing Sim2Real shear gap.

Computational: Poisson + marching cubes at 128³ occupies 45 ms GPU (RTX 3060), leaving limited budget for RL inference at 60 Hz. Memory of dense TSDF (0.15 mm) grows 512 MB for 20 cm workspace, requiring sparse hashing for mobile manipulators.

Generalization: R² collapse on sphere/batch_5 [8] proves overfitting to indentation shape; cross-object force grounding fails when probe trained on DIGIT background-subtracted and tested on WireFishing stacked [8]. Diffusion adaptation is expensive: 1.8 s per image translation on A100, prohibiting online adaptation.

Safety: Force >18 N risks membrane delamination; TLA+ monitor not yet integrated with low-level controller, relying on Python emergency stop with 20 ms latency.

## 7. Conclusion
We present a coherent path from silicone chemistry to tactile mesh to force field to Sim2Real policy. By treating GelSight as calibrated photometric stereo camera, converting heightmaps to SDF volumes via marching cubes, and inverting mechanics with learned-regularized elasticity, we achieve tactile perception that complements vision where vision occludes. Sim2Real via physically-based rendering and bilevel adaptation closes domain gap sufficiently for zero-shot deployment in contact-rich tasks such as insertion and pivoting.

Future work: differentiable elasticity embedded in rendering loop to unify geometry-force optimization, decoupling elastomer aging via self-supervised albedo estimation, and Neural SDF training directly from tactile images without explicit mesh, as suggested by NeuralFeels fusion pipeline [4][7]. Integration with whole-body tactile skin and language-vision-touch foundation models such as TacGen [8] will aim to treat touch as necessary dimension of physical world representation.
