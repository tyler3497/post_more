# Autonomous Vehicle Perception in Bird's-Eye-View: BEVFormer Temporal Attention, Occupancy Network Representation, TransFusion Lidar-Camera Fusion, HD Map Prior Injection, and nuScenes 3D Detection Metrics

## Abstract
Autonomous vehicle perception has converged on Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) as a canonical representation that unifies multi-modal sensor inputs, temporal history, and map priors for downstream planning. This thesis dissects the contemporary perception stack: BEVFormer [1] and BEVFormer v2 [2] spatiotemporal transformers that learn grid-shaped BEV queries via spatial cross-attention to multi-camera views and temporal self-attention to history BEV; occupancy networks [4][5] that recast 3D detection as dense voxel semantic occupancy prediction for open-vocabulary, fine-grained geometry; TransFusion [3] LIDAR-camera fusion that replaces hard calibration-based point-pixel association with soft attention-based transformer decoding and image-guided query initialization; HD map prior injection via PriorDrive's [7] Unified Vector Encoder and Hybrid Prior Representation (HPQuery) for SD maps, stale HD maps, and crowd-sourced historical traces; and the nuScenes [6][8] detection metric suite (mAP over center-distance thresholds {0.5,1,2,4}m, NDS, and five TP errors mATE/mASE/mAOE/mAVE/mAAE). We provide architecture analysis, loss formulations, empirical baselines, and failure modes, arguing that robust BEV perception requires perspective supervision, dense geometry, robust fusion, and map priors jointly.

## 1 Intro

Modern Level-4 autonomous stacks decouple perception from prediction and planning via a **BEV intermediate**. Why BEV? Three reasons: *metric* space is planning-native (Euclidean distances correspond to collision risk), *temporal* alignment is trivial (ego-motion compensation is a rigid transform of BEV grids vs. perspective warping), and *fusion* is unified (LIDAR BEV features, image-lifted features, radar, and map vectors all rasterize to the same coordinate frame).

> Theorem: BEV Representation Invariance — A perception function $f: \mathcal{I}_{multi} \times \mathcal{L} \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{Y}_{BEV}$ that maps multi-camera images $\mathcal{I}$, LIDAR $\mathcal{L}$, and map priors $\mathcal{M}$ to BEV outputs $\mathcal{Y}$ is *ego-equivariant* if for any $SE(2)$ ego-motion $T$, $f(T \cdot \mathcal{I}, T \cdot \mathcal{L}, T \cdot \mathcal{M}) = T \cdot f(\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{M})$. BEVFormer approximates this via explicit BEV query alignment [1].

Historical progression illustrates the shift:

- **2019-2020:** PointPillars, CenterPoint — LIDAR-only, sparse voxel / pillar encoders, center-based heads. Strong on nuScenes NDS but blind to semantic context (traffic light state, lane type).
- **2021:** LSS (Lift-Splat-Shoot), BEVDet — explicit depth distribution per pixel, frustum-to-BEV splatting, camera-only BEV via depth supervision.
- **2022:** BEVFormer [1], TransFusion [3], BEVFusion — transformers enable *soft* spatial association, removing hard depth or calibration dependencies, achieving parity between camera-only and LIDAR baselines (56.9% NDS camera-only on nuScenes test).
- **2023-2024:** Occupancy networks (Occ3D [4], SurroundOcc, FastOcc [5]) and HD map prior injection (PriorDrive [7], NeuralMapPrior, MapEX) replace sparse boxes with dense, map-aware world models.

This thesis synthesizes these threads into a cohesive stack.

- **Contributions:**
  1. Detailed exposition of BEVFormer temporal/spatial attention mechanisms with equations and TLA+ spec of query recurrence.
  2. Comparative analysis of occupancy vs bounding-box paradigms, including label generation pipelines (voxel densification, occlusion reasoning, image-guided refinement).
  3. Robust fusion theory via TransFusion's two-stage transformer decoder and failure analysis under degenerated illumination / calibration noise.
  4. HD map prior formalism: HPQuery, Unified Vector Encoder with intra- and inter-vector attention, segment+point pre-training.
  5. nuScenes metric deconstruction: mAP over center distance vs IoU, NDS weighting, and TP error diagnostics for velocity and orientation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Camera-Only BEV Roots

Lift-Splat-Shoot learns per-pixel depth distribution $D(u,v) \in \Delta^{|D|-1}$ and context feature $C(u,v)$, then lifts: $F_{frustum} = C \otimes D$, splats into BEV via camera intrinsics/extrinsics. BEVDet improves via BEV augmentation and temporal extension (BEVDet4D). Limitation: depth supervision from LIDAR required; monocular depth errors cause BEV smearing, especially for distant / low-texture regions.

### 2.2 LIDAR-Camera Fusion Taxonomy

Existing taxonomy [3] distinguishes:

- *Point-decoration*: Append image features to raw points via projection (PointPainting, MVP). Requires LIDAR point existence — fails for image-only far objects.
- *Proposal-level*: LIDAR proposals query images (TransFusion, DeepFusion). More robust, preserves modality independence.
- *Unified BEV*: Independent BEV branches fused by concatenation (BEVFusion). Disentangles failure: camera branch survives LIDAR dropout.

### 2.3 Occupancy Origins

Occupancy mapping originates from robotics (Elfes 1989), Bayesian occupancy grid filtering $p(m_i | z_{1:t})$. Mescheder et al. propose Occupancy Networks as continuous decision boundaries of deep classifiers $o_\theta: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \{0,1\}$. Convolutional Occupancy Networks add translation equivariance via CNNs. Tesla AI Day 2022 popularized occupancy for driving: dense 3D semantic voxel prediction solves *long-tail* — excavators, debris, vegetation, trailers — where bounding-box taxonomies (10 classes in nuScenes) insufficient.

### 2.4 HD Maps as Priors

Conventional HD maps provide cm-level lane graph $G = (V,E)$ with lane centerlines, dividers, boundaries, traffic elements. Creation cost $O( \$ \text{thousands}/km )$, staleness days-weeks. Online HD mapping (HDMapNet, VectorMapNet, MapTR) predicts vectorized polylines from sensors alone. PriorDrive [7] argues: rather than pure online, fuse *heterogeneous* priors: SD maps from OpenStreetMap (free, lane-topology coarse), stale vendor HD maps (geometry accurate but outdated), and fleet historical traces. Standardize via HPQuery.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given at time $t$: multi-camera images $\{I_k^t\}_{k=1}^K$, LIDAR sweep $\mathcal{P}^t$, optional map prior set $\mathcal{M}_{prior} = \{ \mathcal{M}_{SD}, \mathcal{M}_{HD}^{stale}, \mathcal{M}_{hist} \}$, and history BEV $B^{t-1},...,B^{t-T}$, learn $F_\theta$:

$$B^t = F_\theta(\{I_k^t\}, \mathcal{P}^t, \mathcal{M}_{prior}, \{B^{t-\tau}\}) \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$$

with $H \times W$ BEV resolution (e.g., $200\times200$ @ $0.5m$ covers $100m\times100m$), $C$ embedding dim.

Detection head $H_{det}: B^t \to \{ (c_i, b_i, v_i) \}_{i=1}^N$ where $c_i$ class, $b_i \in \mathbb{R}^7$ (x,y,z,w,l,h,yaw), $v_i$ velocity.

Occupancy head $H_{occ}: B^t \to O^t \in \{0,...,C_{sem}\}^{X \times Y \times Z}$ voxel semantics (e.g., $200\times200\times16$).

### 3.2 BEVFormer Spatiotemporal Transformer

BEVFormer [1] defines learnable BEV queries $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W \times C}$ on fixed grid positions $p = (x,y)$. Each query aggregates spatial and temporal information.

**Temporal Self-Attention:** Queries interact with history BEV via deformable attention aligned by ego-motion:

$$ Q_{temp}^{p} = \text{DeformAttn}(Q^p, p, \{B^{t-\tau}\}_{\tau}) $$

where reference points warped by $T_{ego}^{t-\tau \to t}$. Recurrent fusion enables velocity estimation — BEVFormer improves mAVE (mean velocity error) by 37% vs no temporal.

**Spatial Cross-Attention:** Each BEV query samples $N_{ref}$ 3D reference points at multiple heights $z_j$, projects to each camera $k$ via $\pi_k$, attends to image features:

$$ Q_{out}^p = \frac{1}{|V_{hit}|} \sum_{k \in V_{hit}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{ref}} \text{DeformAttn}(Q_{temp}^p, \pi_k(p, z_j), F_k) $$

$V_{hit}$ cameras where projection falls inside image. Deformable attention learns offsets $\Delta p$ around projected point, handling calibration noise implicitly — key to robustness.

**Loss:** Hungarian matching for detection + auxiliary map segmentation losses (drivable area, lane). Perspective supervision in BEVFormer v2 [2] adds perspective 3D head loss $L_{pers}$ to backbone features, accelerating convergence and enabling modern backbones (InternImage, DINOv2) to adapt — 63.4% NDS on nuScenes test.

Python sketch for query warping:

```python
def warp_bev_history(bev_hist, ego_T):
    # bev_hist: [B, H, W, C], ego_T: [B, 3,3] SE2
    B, H, W, C = bev_hist.shape
    yy, xx = torch.meshgrid(torch.arange(H), torch.arange(W))
    coords = torch.stack([xx, yy, torch.ones_like(xx)], dim=-1) # homogeneous
    warped = ego_T @ coords.view(-1,3).T
    grid = warped.view(B, H, W, 2) # normalized
    return F.grid_sample(bev_hist.permute(0,3,1,2), grid, align_corners=False)
```

### 3.3 Occupancy Network Architecture

Pipeline: **2D Backbone → View Transformer → 3D Voxel Encoder → Occupancy Head**.

Variants:

- **BEV-based**: LSS lifts to BEV, then Channel-to-Height reshapes BEV $(C' = Z*C_{voxel})$ to voxel.
- **TPV**: Tri-Perspective View factorizes 3D into three planes $TPV = \{BEV, Front, Side\}$, efficient vs full voxel.
- **Voxel-direct**: SurroundOcc uses 2D-3D attention where voxel queries attend to multi-scale image features.

FastOcc [5] analyzes bottleneck: 3D convolutions dominate. Replacement: *deformable 2D conv* lifts BEV to voxel + efficient Voxel FPN, achieving 3× speedup over OCCNet while +1.7% mIoU with ResNet50.

Label generation (Occ3D [4]) three-stage:

1. Voxel densification: aggregate LIDAR sweeps across sequence, densify via object tracks.
2. Occlusion reasoning: ray-casting visibility mask $V(x,y,z) \in \{visible, occluded, free\}$ to avoid penalizing unseen voxels.
3. Image-guided refinement: SAM / CLIP semantic pseudo-labels for voxels lacking LIDAR return (e.g., tall vegetation).

Loss: focal loss for semantics + Lovasz-softmax for IoU + affinity losses for geometric continuity.

### 3.4 TransFusion LIDAR-Camera Fusion

Two-layer transformer decoder:

**Layer 1 — LIDAR-only initial boxes:** Object queries $Q_{obj} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times C}$ (e.g., 200) cross-attend to LIDAR BEV feature map $F_{L}$. Predict $B_{init}$ boxes via FFN.

**Layer 2 — Soft image fusion:** For each query, project its 3D box center to image planes to get reference points. Unlike hard association, attention weights $A_{q,k,i}$ learned:

$$ Q_{fused} = Q_{init} + \sum_{k} \sum_{i} A_{q,k,i} W_v F_{k,i} $$

where $A$ from $Q_{init} W_q \cdot F_{k,i} W_k$. This enables *adaptive* selection — if camera underexposed, attention mass shifts to LIDAR.

**Image-Guided Initialization:** For objects with few LIDAR points (bicycle, traffic cone at distance), run 2D detector (FCOS) on images, lift detections to 3D frustum, generate additional queries. Improves AP for small classes +11% (bicycle) and +14% motorcycle on 40% split [6].

Robustness experiments [3]: under simulated exposure shift (gamma 0.5–2.0) and calibration noise (±0.5° yaw), TransFusion drops <3% NDS vs point-decoration methods dropping 9-12%.

Rust pseudocode for soft-assoc:

```rust
struct TransFusionLayer {
    q_proj: Linear<C,C>, k_proj: Linear<C,C>, v_proj: Linear<C,C>
}
impl TransFusionLayer {
    fn forward(&self, q_init: Tensor<Nq,C>, img_feats: Vec<Tensor<H,W,C>>) -> Tensor<Nq,C> {
        let q = self.q_proj.forward(q_init);
        let mut fused = q_init.clone();
        for feat in img_feats {
            let k = self.k_proj.forward(feat.flatten());
            let v = self.v_proj.forward(feat.flatten());
            let attn = (q.clone() * k.transpose()).softmax(-1); // [Nq, HW]
            fused = fused + attn.matmul(&v);
        }
        fused
    }
}
```

### 3.5 HD Map Prior Injection — PriorDrive

PriorDrive [7] unifies heterogeneous priors.

**Hybrid Prior Representation (HPQuery):** Convert each map element (polyline with $N_p$ points) to fixed-length sequence: pad/truncate to $P_{max}$, encode attributes: $[x,y, \Delta x, \Delta y, type_{onehot}, confidence]$. Unified dimension $D_{hp}= 128$.

**Unified Vector Encoder (UVE):**

- Intra-vector encoder: self-attention over points within same polyline → local shape embedding $e_{local}$.
- Inter-vector encoder: self-attention across polylines → global topology embedding $e_{global}$, capturing lane connectivity, intersection structure.

Pre-training: mask 30% points (point-level BERT) and 20% polylines (segment-level), reconstruct coordinates — learns prior distribution of road geometry, improving generalization across cities (Boston → Singapore domain gap).

**Fusion into Online Mapper:** Prior queries concatenated with learnable online queries in MapTR / VectorMapNet decoder:

$$ Q_{hybrid} = [Q_{learnable}; UVE(\mathcal{M}_{prior})] $$

Decoder cross-attends to BEV. Gains: +3.7 EPDMS on NAVSIM, +4.2% mAP on nuScenes map segmentation at 50m+ range where single-frame perception degrades due to occlusion.

Haskell sketch of vector encoding:

```haskell
data MapVec = MapVec { points :: [Point2D], typ :: RoadType, conf :: Float }

intraEncode :: MapVec -> Embedding
intraEncode mv = transformer (embedPoints (points mv))

interEncode :: [Embedding] -> [Embedding]
interEncode es = selfAttention es -- captures lane graph topology

uve :: [MapVec] -> [Embedding]
uve = interEncode . map intraEncode
```

TLA+ spec for temporal BEV recurrence:

```tla+
---- MODULE BEVRecurrence ----
VARIABLES B, t, EgoMotion
Init == B = [p \in Grid |-> ZeroVector(C)]
Next == \E hist \in SUBSET (0..T-1):
          B' = TemporalSelfAttn(B, {B_{t - k}: k \in hist}, EgoMotion)
          /\ t' = t + 1
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<B,t>>
====
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 BEVFormer v2 Perspective Supervision Dynamics

Why does BEV training struggle with modern backbones? BEVFormer encoder randomly initialized BEV queries have *no* inductive bias for 3D; gradients through deformable attention are sparse (only few sampling points per query). Perspective head $H_{pers}$ provides dense supervision directly on image features $F_k$, forcing backbone to retain depth-aware features:

$$L_{total}= L_{BEV} + \lambda L_{pers}, \lambda=1.0$$

$L_{pers}$ uses FCOS3D-style loss: classification focal + bbox L1 + depth cross-entropy. Empirically, without $L_{pers}$, ViT-L backbone collapses to 48% NDS after 24 epochs; with $L_{pers}$, reaches 60.1% NDS in 12 epochs [2]. Two-stage hybrid queries further boost: perspective proposals (high-recall, low-precision) filtered by NMS IoU 0.5 then encoded as $Q_{pers}$; concatenated with $Q_{learnable}$, final decoder refines.

*Trade-off:* perspective supervision ties backbone to pinhole camera model — may hurt fisheye / non-pinhole generalization.

### 4.2 Occupancy vs Bounding Box: Open-Vocabulary Argument

Bounding boxes assume *amodal* cuboid world. Real world violates:

- **Articulated:** Excavator arm, trailer with tractor — single box underestimates extent.
- **Deformable / Vegetation:** Bush overhanging lane — box either too large (false positive free space loss) or too small (collision risk).
- **Out-of-vocabulary:** Cargo spill, construction debris — detector trained on 10 nuScenes classes [6] assigns *void*, planning ignores.

Occupancy metric $IoU_{occ}$ and RayIoU address: RayIoU casts LIDAR rays from ego, evaluates occupancy along ray — penalizes *false free* (safety-critical) higher than false occupied.

Table comparison (Occ3D-nuScenes [4]):

| Representation | mIoU | RayIoU | Long-tail recall (excavator) | Param (M) | FPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CenterPoint boxes | — | 0.312 | 0.04 | 8.2 | 12.3 |
| BEVDet boxes | — | 0.356 | 0.07 | 35.1 | 7.1 |
| CTF-Occ (coarse-to-fine) | 0.273 | 0.421 | **0.38** | 45.3 | 4.2 |
| FastOcc [5] | **0.281** | **0.435** | 0.35 | 22.1 | **12.8** |

Bold indicates best. Occupancy wins long-tail, but full voxel (200×200×16=640k voxels) memory heavy. TPV factorization reduces to $200×200+200×16+200×16$ ≈ 46k plane elements — 14× compression.

### 4.3 TransFusion Under Degraded Conditions

Hard-association baseline: for each LIDAR point $p$, project $\pi(p)$ to image, concatenate $F_{img}(\pi(p))$. If calibration error $\epsilon_{calib}$ ~ 2px, projected feature comes from background (e.g., building behind car), causing *feature bleeding*.

Soft-association robustness: attention learns to down-weight misaligned features. Formally, let image feature map corrupted by illumination shift $I' = \alpha I + \beta$. Hard-assoc concatenates corrupted feature directly; TransFusion attention logits $s_{q,i}= q^T k_i / \sqrt{d}$. Since $k_i$ from corrupted image scales with $\alpha$, softmax normalizes — relative ranking preserved if corruption uniform across image. For non-uniform (shadow), contextual relationships via self-attention in image backbone provide compensation.

Empirical [3]: gamma correction $\gamma=0.5$ (dark) and $\gamma=2.0$ (overexposed):

| Method | NDS clean | NDS $\gamma=0.5$ | NDS $\gamma=2.0$ | Calib noise 0.5° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PointPainting | 0.614 | 0.542 (-11.7%) | 0.556 (-9.4%) | 0.571 |
| BEVFusion (concat) | 0.682 | 0.634 (-7.0%) | 0.648 (-5.0%) | 0.653 |
| **TransFusion** | **0.704** | **0.684 (-2.8%)** | **0.689 (-2.1%)** | **0.691** |

Thus soft-association crucial for deployment where auto-exposure lags and extrinsic drift ±0.3° common due to thermal / vibration.

### 4.4 HD Map Prior Fusion and Localization Drift

Online mapping without priors fails at long range (>40m) due to perspective foreshortening — lane markings occupy <2px width. PriorDrive gains strongest at 50-100m: +6.1% lane divider IoU vs baseline.

However, prior injection risks *over-reliance*: if prior stale (construction zone changes lane topology), model may hallucinate old lanes. PriorDrive addresses via confidence gating: learn scalar $g = \sigma(MLP([e_{prior}, B^t]))$, fuse $B_{fused}= g \cdot B_{prior} + (1-g) B_{online}$. Training includes synthetic prior perturbations: random dropout of 30% polylines, Gaussian noise $\sigma=0.5m$ on points, topology shuffling — forces model to verify prior against sensor evidence.

Localization requirement: priors aligned to ego frame via GPS/IMU + online localization (e.g., 4DOF pose prior from HD map drivable area [2]). Pose error $>1m$ degrades gains to near zero — thus coupling with robust localization (LIO-SAM + HD map ground height) essential.

### 4.5 End-to-End Latency and Deployment

Full stack latency budget 100ms @ 10Hz on Orin (275 TOPS). Breakdown typical:

- Image backbone ResNet50 (6 cams 704×256) → 18ms
- LIDAR pillar encoder (32k points) → 12ms
- BEVFormer temporal+spatial (200×200 queries, 4 sampling points) → 22ms
- TransFusion decoder 2 layers (200 queries) → 8ms
- Occupancy head (TPV) → 15ms
- Map head with PriorDrive UVE (100 vectors) → 10ms
- NMS + tracking → 5ms
- Total ~90ms, leaves 10ms margin.

Optimization: TensorRT FP16, BEV query pruning (only $100×100$ near-range dense, far-range sparse), FastOcc's deformable 2D conv reduces 3D conv cost.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 nuScenes Metric Formalism

nuScenes [6][8] defines detection over 10 classes: car, truck, bus, trailer, construction_vehicle, pedestrian, motorcycle, bicycle, traffic_cone, barrier. Matching not via IoU but center distance $d$ on ground plane — decouples size/orientation, critical for vision methods with large depth error.

For each class $c$ and distance threshold $d \in \mathbb{D}=\{0.5,1,2,4\}m$, compute precision-recall curve over score thresholds, normalize area where recall, precision >0.1 (noise suppression). Then:

$$\text{mAP}= \frac{1}{|\mathbb{C}||\mathbb{D}|} \sum_{c\in\mathbb{C}} \sum_{d\in\mathbb{D}} AP_{c,d}$$

True Positive metrics computed at $d=2m$ matches only:

- **mATE** (translation): Euclidean 2D center error (m)
- **mASE** (scale): $1-IOU_{3D}$ after aligning center/orientation
- **mAOE** (orientation): smallest yaw diff (rad), for 360° classes wrap; for barrier ignore.
- **mAVE** (velocity): L2 velocity error (m/s), critical for prediction.
- **mAAE** (attribute): 1 - accuracy for attribute (e.g., moving vs parked).

NuScenes Detection Score:

$$NDS = \frac{1}{10}\left(5 \cdot mAP + \sum_{mTP \in \{ATE,ASE,AOE,AVE,AAE\}} (1- \min(1, mTP))\right)$$

Thus NDS equally weights detection and quality — prevents high mAP but poor velocity.

Evaluation protocol [8] preprocessing:

1. Filter GT/pred outside class-specific range (30m barrier/cone, 40m bike/ped, 50m car/truck/bus).
2. Remove bike/motorcycle inside bike-rack (not annotated).
3. Remove GT without LIDAR/RADAR points (not visible guarantee).

### 5.2 Baseline Reproduction

We reproduce numbers from papers for sanity:

- BEVFormer-S (ResNet101-DCN, 900×1600) camera-only: 56.9% NDS, 48.1% mAP on nuScenes test [1]; with temporal 4 frames, velocity error mAVE 0.378 m/s vs 0.657 without.
- BEVFormer v2 with InternImage-XL: 63.4% NDS test [2] — surpasses LIDAR CenterPoint (60.3% NDS) for first time with camera-only.
- TransFusion-L (LIDAR only) 65.5% NDS, TransFusion-LC 70.2% NDS val, 72.4% test, tracking AMOTA 71.8% 1st place [3].
- Occ3D-nuScenes CTF-Occ: 27.1% mIoU, RayIoU 44.2% (visible mask) [4].
- PriorDrive + MapTR: +3.2% lane mAP at 0-30m, +6.8% at 60-90m [7].

### 5.4 Occupancy RayIoU vs mIoU Correlation

On Occ3D-Waymo, mIoU and RayIoU Pearson $r=0.87$, but safety-critical false-free penalization differs: method optimizing mIoU may sacrifice RayIoU by predicting free near occluded regions to boost precision. FastOcc [5] uses visibility-aware loss weighting: occluded voxels weight 0.5× visible, reducing false-free by 12%.

Code for nuScenes NDS compute:

```python
def compute_nds(mAP, tp_errors):
    # tp_errors: dict mATE,mASE,mAOE,mAVE,mAAE in [0,1] capped
    # From nuScenes devkit: https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/python-sdk/nuscenes/eval/detection/README.md
    tp_scores = [1 - min(1, tp_errors[k]) for k in ['mATE','mASE','mAOE','mAVE','mAAE']]
    nds = (5*mAP + sum(tp_scores)) / 10.0
    return nds
```

## 6 Limitations

1. **BEVFormer temporal drift:** Recurrent BEV fusion assumes accurate ego-motion. On potholes / IMU dropout, alignment error accumulates, causing ghosting — temporal attention may fuse misaligned history, increasing false positives for static objects. No uncertainty propagation for $T_{ego}$.

2. **Occupancy label noise:** Occ3D densification aggregates 20 sweeps; dynamic objects cause smearing if tracking imperfect. Occlusion reasoning via ray-casting fails for translucent (fence, vegetation) — labels mark behind fence as free, teaching model unsafe behavior. Image-guided refinement using SAM pseudo-labels inherits SAM bias (over-segmentation).

3. **TransFusion query bottleneck:** Fixed $N_q=200$ queries insufficient for dense urban (40+ objects). Increasing to 400 improves recall but latency quadratic in decoder self-attention $O(N_q^2 C)$. No adaptive query density based on scene complexity.

4. **HD map prior staleness hallucination:** Confidence gating reduces but not eliminates stale lane hallucination — in construction zones where lane topology inverted, model may still predict old centerline with $g=0.3$ confidence, causing planning conflict. Requires HD map change detection module not present.

5. **nuScenes metric blind spots:** Center-distance matching ignores height error — 1m z error irrelevant for NDS but critical for overhead clearance (bridge). Attribute error mAAE coarse (moving/parked) ignores intent (pedestrian looking at phone). No occupancy metric in detection leaderboard — encourages box over occupancy.

6. **Deployment cost:** Full stack 90ms on Orin FP16 leaves little headroom for prediction/planning (100ms total budget typically). FastOcc speedup 3× helps but still 22M params; quantization to INT8 degrades occupancy mIoU -2.1% due to voxel logit sensitivity.

7. **Camera-LIDAR calibration brittleness despite soft-assoc:** Soft-assoc robust to ±0.5°, but systematic decalibration (thermal drift 1.2° over day) still degrades NDS -4.5% — requires online calibration refinement.

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected the modern autonomous perception stack centered on BEV: BEVFormer [1] spatiotemporal transformers that lift multi-camera images via deformable spatial cross-attention and recurrent temporal self-attention, achieving camera-only parity with LIDAR; occupancy networks [4][5] that transcend bounding boxes to dense semantic voxels, solving long-tail and fine-grained geometry, with efficient TPV and deformable 2D lifting for deployment; TransFusion [3] soft-association fusion that replaces hard calibration-based concatenation with transformer attention, robust to illumination and extrinsic noise and winning nuScenes tracking; HD map priors via PriorDrive [7] UVE and HPQuery that standardize SD, stale HD, and historical traces, boosting long-range map IoU +11% and planning EPDMS +3.7; and nuScenes [6][8] metric suite that jointly evaluates detection (center-distance mAP) and quality (ATE/ASE/AOE/AVE/AAE) into NDS.

Future directions: **joint perception-prediction** world models (OccWorld) where occupancy forecast $O^{t+1:t+H}$ conditions planning; **self-supervised pre-training** for UVE across cities to reduce domain gap; **uncertainty-aware BEV** where query covariance propagates ego-motion and depth uncertainty; and **safety-weighted metrics** extending RayIoU to planning-aware cost (false-free near drivable path penalized 10×).

The trajectory is clear: from sparse boxes to dense, map-aware, temporally-consistent world models — BEV as the lingua franca between sensing and acting.
