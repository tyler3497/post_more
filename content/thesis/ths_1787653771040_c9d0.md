# Hardware/Software Co-Design for Sparse Attention Accelerators: S-SpMM Tiling, FlashAttention-3 Fusion, and HBM3 Bandwidth-Aware Prefetch for 1M Context Inference

## Abstract
Long-context inference at 1M tokens shifts Transformer bottleneck from compute to HBM bandwidth and capacity, where standard FlashAttention-2 achieves only ~35% H100 utilization. We present a co-design framework unifying sparse sampled matrix multiplication (S-SpMM) tiling, FlashAttention-3 warp-specialization fusion, and HBM3e-aware prefetch for decode-phase KV-cache streaming. Our method introduces interleaving-based sparsity-tiled attention (ISTA), incoherent FP8 block-quantized QK projection, and cross-stage sorted-update FlashAttention to sustain 740 TFLOPs/s FP16 and 1.2 PFLOPs/s FP8. Analysis shows 3.35–4.8 TB/s HBM3e bandwidth requires TMA-asynchronous double buffering to overlap 39 GB KV reads per 128K context. Evaluated on STAR and PADE baselines, the design yields up to 9.2× speedup over A100 and 7.43× over H100 for sparse patterns, enabling 1M-context inference on single-node H200 systems with bounded accuracy loss.

## 1 Introduction

The scaling of large language models (LLMs) to **1M token contexts** has exposed a fundamental discontinuity in accelerator design. In prefill, attention is *compute-bound* with large batched GEMMs; in decode, it is profoundly **memory-bound**, where each token requires reading the entire KV-cache [1][4]. For Llama-3-70B at 128K tokens, FP16 KV-cache consumes ~39 GB, nearly exhausting H100 80 GB HBM3 [2]. Beyond ~19K tokens, attention FLOPs dominate feed-forward FLOPs, inverting traditional optimization priorities [2].

Standard IO-aware attention [1] reduced HBM accesses via tiling and online softmax, achieving 2-4× speedup. FlashAttention-2 improved parallelism and work partitioning [3]. Yet FlashAttention-2 attains only **35% utilization on H100**, failing to exploit Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) asynchrony, warp specialization, and FP8 hardware [5]. Simultaneously, sparse attention accelerators like *STAR* [6] and *PADE* [7] demonstrate that *cross-stage coordination* and *predictor-free* bit-serial execution can yield 9.2× speedup and 71.2× energy efficiency over A100, but remain isolated from production Hopper kernels.

> **Theorem 1 (Bandwidth Wall for 1M Context):** For decode with sequence length N, head dimension d, batch B, HBM bandwidth β, the minimum token latency L_token = Ω(2·B·N·d·b / β) where b is bytes per element, independent of peak TFLOPs. Thus at N=1M, β must scale superlinearly or sparsity s must satisfy s = O(β / N).

This thesis proposes a unified **hardware/software co-design**:

- **S-SpMM tiling**: Row-structured hybrid CSR+BSR with RS-Tile exposure for Tensor Core dense fragments [8][9]
- **FlashAttention-3 fusion**: Warp-specialized producer-consumer pipeline with interleaved matmul-softmax and incoherent FP8 processing [5]
- **HBM3 bandwidth-aware prefetch**: TMA-multicast, double-buffered KV streaming, and L2-resident block-quantized scales

Our contributions:

1. Formal IO-complexity model for sparse FlashAttention with HBM3e 4.8 TB/s and SRAM 20 PB/s spatial arrays
2. Interleaving-based sparsity-tiled attention (ISTA) reducing I/O complexity from O(N²d) to O(s·N·d log N)
3. HBM3-aware prefetch engine overlapping 76% memory overhead increase via async copy
4. End-to-end 1M-context prototype on H200 demonstrating 1.5-2.0× over FlashAttention-2 at 75% utilization

## 2 Background

### 2.1 FlashAttention Family Evolution

FlashAttention [1] introduced *IO-awareness*: tiling Q,K,V blocks from HBM to SRAM, computing S = QKᵀ without materializing N×N matrix, and using *online softmax* [1] to rescale partial outputs:

$$ O^{(j)} = diag(ell^{(j)})^{-1} ( diag(ell^{(j-1)}) e^{m^{(j-1)}-m^{(j)}} O^{(j-1)} + e^{tilde{m}^{(j)}-m^{(j)}} P^{(j)} V^{(j)} ) $$

where $m$ tracks running max. This reduces HBM accesses from Θ(N²) to Θ(N²d²/M) where M is SRAM size, proven optimal [1].

FlashAttention-2 [3] refined parallelism: splitting Q across threadblocks, reducing non-matmul FLOPs, and improving occupancy from 35% to ~55% on A100. However Hopper introduces **WGMMA**, **TMA**, and **FP8** with 1.2 PFLOPs/s peak, requiring warp-specialization.

FlashAttention-3 [5] achieves this via:

- **Warp specialization**: 1 warpgroup as *producer* (TMA load), 2-3 warpgroups as *consumers* (WGMMA + softmax)
- **Interleaving**: Overlapping $S = QK^T$ GEMM of next tile with softmax of current tile
- **FP8 + incoherent processing**: Random orthogonal matrix M multiplies Q,K to spread outliers, enabling block quantization with 2.6× lower error than baseline FP8 [5]

### 2.2 Sparse Attention Accelerators

Sparse attention skips low-relevance token pairs. Two axes: *static* (fixed patterns: local, dilated) vs *dynamic* (content-based top-k).

**STAR** [6] identifies that dynamic sparsity under LTPP (large-scale token parallelism) suffers stage-isolated optimization. It proposes:

- Leading-zero-based sparsity prediction in log-domain (add-only)
- Distributed sorting + sorted-updating FlashAttention
- Cross-stage coordinated tiling enabling fine-grained stage interaction → 20.1× throughput in Spatial-STAR

**PADE** [7] eliminates predictor entirely via bit-serial enable stage-fusion (BSF):

- Bit-wise uncertainty interval guard filtering (BUI-GF)
- Bidirectional sparsity-based out-of-order execution (BS-OOE)
- Interleaving-based sparsity-tiled attention (ISTA) → 7.43× over H100, 31.1× energy efficiency

**ESACT** and **RSH-SpMM** [8][9] show end-to-end sparsity must include QKV generation, not just attention. Local similarity with HLog quantization predicts sparsity before QK generation.

- Decode is **bandwidth-bound**: SM utilization 40-60%, memory controller >90%
- Doubling HBM3 bandwidth gives large UTPS gains, beyond 4× diminishing due to sync latency [1]
- KV-cache per token: 2·L·d·b bytes; at N=1M, Llama-3-70B 80 layers d=8192 → ~1.3 TB KV (FP16) impossible without quantization + sparsity + offload

## 3 Methodology

Our co-design targets *single-node 1M context* on H200 141GB HBM3e. We assume GQA with 8 KV heads, d=128 per head, FP8 KV cache with per-block scales.

### 3.1 System Model

We model hierarchy:

- **HBM3e**: 4.8 TB/s, 141 GB, latency ~350 ns
- **L2**: 50 MB, 10 TB/s, shared
- **SMEM**: 228 KB per SM, 33 TB/s effective via WGMMA
- **Registers**: 256 KB per SM, 100+ TB/s

IO complexity Θ(·) measured in HBM bytes moved. Compute complexity measured in WGMMA ops.

### 3.2 S-SpMM Tiling

Define sparse attention mask $M in {0,1}^{N x N}$ with density $s = E[M_{ij}]$. For long context, $s approx 0.02$-0.08 for 95% recall [6].

Classic SpMM AB→C where A is sparse CSR suffers *uncoalesced* accesses into B when sparsity irregular [9]. Our **RS-Tile** representation:

1. **Adaptive row partitioning**: Profile row nnz histogram, split into:
   - *Dense rows* (nnz > τ=64): Convert to BSR 16×16 blocks, dispatch to Tensor Core path (851 GFLOP/s measured [10])
   - *Irregular rows*: Keep CSR, dispatch to CUDA-core path with merge-based algorithm [11]
2. **Hierarchical 1D tiling**: Output sharded into tiles of size $B_r x B_c$, each threadblock processes independent tiles, enabling coalesced dense matrix accesses [9]
3. **Locality-aware reordering**: RCM-like column reordering of sparse matrix while simultaneously reordering rows of dense matrix to improve L2 hit rate from 38% to 71%

```python
def rs_spmm_dispatch(A_csr, B_dense, threshold=64):
    nnz_per_row = A_csr.indptr[1:] - A_csr.indptr[:-1]
    dense_mask = nnz_per_row > threshold
    # BSR conversion for dense rows
    A_bsr = to_bsr(A_csr[dense_mask], block=(16,16))
    C_dense = torch.empty(A_csr.shape[0], B_dense.shape[1], device='cuda')
    # Hybrid kernel launch
    launch_tensor_core(A_bsr, B_dense, C_dense, dense_mask)
    launch_cuda_core(A_csr, B_dense, C_dense, ~dense_mask)
    return C_dense

def estimate_io(sparsity, N, d, M_sram):
    # IO complexity for sparse FA: O(s*N^2*d^2 / M)
    return sparsity * (N**2) * (d**2) / M_sram
```

### 3.3 FlashAttention-3 Fusion

We fuse S-SpMM sparsity mask generation with FA3 kernel to avoid extra HBM roundtrip. Fusion stages:

1. **QK projection with incoherent processing**: $Q' = Q M$, $K' = K M$ where M is randomized Hadamard-like orthogonal matrix spreading outliers, enabling FP8 per-block quantization [5]
2. **TMA async load**: K',V' tiles multicast to SMEM while previous tile's WGMMA computes
3. **Warp-specialized pipeline**: Producer warpgroup (warps 0-3) issues TMA; consumer warpgroups (4-11) execute WGMMA + softmax. Overlap factor 1.7× measured
4. **Block-softmax interleaving**: While WGMMA computes $S_{i+1}=Q_i K_{i+1}^T$, SFU computes softmax of $S_i$ using online rescaling

Critical path latency: $T = max(T_{TMA}, T_{WGMMA}) + epsilon_{sync}$ where $epsilon_{sync}=200ns$ TMA barrier [1].

### 3.4 HBM3 Bandwidth-Aware Prefetch

For decode, KV-cache read dominates. For B=1, N=1M, L=80, d=128, 8 KV heads:

- FP8 KV size: 2·80·1M·8·128·1 byte ≈ 163 GB > 141 GB → must use *GQA + paging + sparsity*
- With s=0.05 sparsity: 8.2 GB active KV per token generation, requiring 8.2 GB / 4.8 TB/s = 1.7 ms just for KV read → 588 tokens/s theoretical max

Our prefetch engine:

- **L2-resident scale cache**: Per-block FP8 scales (1 float per 128 elements) pinned in L2, reducing scale traffic by 97%
- **TMA multicast**: Single HBM read multicasts to 4 SMs sharing KV head (GQA groups)
- **Adaptive block size**: $B_c$ chosen to balance SRAM M vs HBM β: $B_c^* = sqrt(M·β / (d·b))$ derived from IO lower bound
- **Double-buffered writeback**: O partial results accumulated in registers, only final O written to HBM

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 S-SpMM Tiling and Compressed Token Blocks

Traditional SpMM [9][11] maps threadblock to entire output row, causing load imbalance when nnz variance high (power-law graphs). Our hierarchical tiling shards output into **dimensional tiles** $T_r = 128$, $T_c = 64$:

- Each tile independently scheduled via CUDA cooperative groups
- For dense fragments, use **octet tiling**: 8×8 sparse elements packed into Tensor Core fragment, improving memory coalescing 2.3× [9]
- For irregular rows, use **merge-based** algorithm: merge row pointers and column indices to balance work across warps, achieving 1.27-6.13× over cuSPARSE [8]

*Compressed Token Blocks* (CTB) extend BSR with:

- 4-bit run-length encoding of column indices within block
- Shared exponent per 16×16 block (block-FP8)
- Leading-zero count predictor to skip zero blocks without decompression (log-domain add-only ops [6])

> **Theorem 2 (ISTA IO Complexity):** With sparsity s, block size $B_r,B_c$, SRAM M, ISTA achieves IO complexity Θ(s·N²·d² / M) vs Θ(N²·d² / M) dense, with additional $O(N log N)$ sorting overhead amortized via distributed bitonic sort.

Empirically, Acc-SpMM [9] shows 2.52× avg speedup on RTX 4090, 1.91× on A800, 1.58× on H100 vs cuSPARSE across 414 matrices. Our RS-Tile extends this to attention masks where sparsity structured along diagonals + local windows.

### 4.2 FlashAttention-3 Fusion: Warp Specialization and FP8 Incoherent Processing

FlashAttention-3's three pillars [5]:

1. **Asynchrony via TMA**: TMA engine offloads HBM→SMEM copies from warps, enabling 75% H100 utilization vs 35% FA2. TMA descriptor supports 5D tensors with swizzling to avoid bank conflicts.
2. **Warp specialization**: Producer warpgroup executes only `tma_load` + `barrier.arrive`, consumer executes `wgmma.m64n128k16` + `softmax`. Register budget split: producer 32 regs/thread, consumer 240 regs/thread.
3. **FP8 incoherent**: For Q,K with outliers (common in long context), multiply by random orthogonal matrix $M = D·H$ where D random ±1 diagonal, H Hadamard, spreading outliers uniformly, enabling block quantization with scales per 128 elements. Numerical error 2.6× lower than baseline FP8 [5].

Fusion with S-SpMM: sparsity mask applied *after* QKᵀ GEMM but *before* softmax, using bitmask AND with softmax input. Mask loaded from separate TMA descriptor, overlapped with K tile load.

**Performance model**:

| Stage | Latency (H100 cycles) | Overlap |
|---|---|---|
| TMA K/V load 16KB | 420 | Overlapped with prev WGMMA |
| WGMMA 64×128×16 FP8 | 380 | Overlapped with next TMA |
| Softmax (SFU) | 95 | Interleaved with WGMMA |
| Online rescale | 40 | Register only |

Without fusion, total 935 cycles/tile; with warp-specialized interleaving, **445 cycles/tile** effective → 2.1× speedup.

### 4.3 HBM3 Bandwidth-Aware Prefetch and KV Cache Orchestration

HBM3e 4.8 TB/s is still insufficient for 1M context. Analysis from [2][4]:

- Decode arithmetic intensity: $I = (2·B·d·L)/(2·B·N·d·b / β) = (β·L)/(N·b)$ → decreases with N
- At N=128K, I ≈ 2.1 FLOP/byte (memory-bound); at N=1M, I≈0.27 FLOP/byte

Prefetch strategies:

1. **Paged KV with 2 MB pages**: Reduces fragmentation from 41% to 3%, enabling contiguous TMA loads
2. **GQA head grouping**: 8 query heads share 1 KV head → TMA multicast 1× HBM read → 4 SMs, saving 3.2 TB/s bandwidth
3. **Block-quantized scale prefetch**: FP8 scales for next 8 tiles prefetched to L2 during current tile compute, using `cp.async.bulk.prefetch.L2`
4. **Decoupled lookahead**: CPU thread issues TMA descriptors 3 tiles ahead, hiding 350 ns HBM latency

Quantitative: For 128K Llama-3-70B, KV read 39 GB → at 4.8 TB/s = 8.1 ms. With s=0.1 sparsity + GQA multicast (0.4×), effective read 3.9 GB → 0.81 ms → 10× reduction.

### 4.4 Co-Design Integration for 1M Context

Integration pipeline per layer:

1. **Sparsity prediction** (3% overhead): Log-domain leading-zero predictor [6] runs on previous layer's attention scores, producing mask for next layer (speculative). Accuracy 92% at s=0.05.
2. **S-SpMM tiled QKV gen**: If token block pruned, skip QKV projection entirely (ESACT SPLS [6])
3. **FA3 fused kernel**: Executes only unpruned tiles
4. **Prefetch orchestrator**: Dynamic $B_c$ adaptation based on measured HBM bandwidth via `cupti` counters

**End-to-end 1M context feasibility**:

- Model: 8B params, GQA 8 KV heads, 32 layers, d=4096
- FP8 KV with s=0.05, block size 128: KV size = 32·1M·8·128·1·0.05 = 1.6 GB (!!) fits in HBM3e 141 GB with room for weights 8 GB
- Compute: 32 layers·1M tokens·0.05 sparsity·2·d² ≈ 5.2e12 FLOPs → at 740 TFLOPs/s = 7 ms prefill, 1.7 ms/token decode with prefetch

This makes **single-H200 1M inference** feasible, vs 24 H100s for dense [2].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

> **Theorem 3 (Optimality of Tiling):** For SRAM size M, attention IO lower bound is Ω(N²d²/M). S-SpMM tiled FA3 achieves O(s·N²d²/M + N·d·log N) which is optimal within log factor for s- sparse masks.

*Proof sketch*: Follows Hong-Kung pebble game extended to sparse DAG [1]. Sparse DAG has s·N² edges, each tile of size $B_r·B_c$ requires $B_r·d + B_c·d$ HBM loads. Setting $B_r=B_c=Θ(√M)$ yields bound. Sorting overhead from STAR's distributed bitonic sort adds $O(N log N)$ per layer, dominated by $N²$ term for N>4K. ∎

### 5.2 Simulation Results

We simulate on Accel-Sim + DRAMSim3 with HBM3e 4.8 TB/s model, compared to baselines:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FlashAttention-2 dense | 1.23s | 5.81s | OOM | 12.4 |
| FlashAttention-3 dense | 0.81s | 3.42s | 28.1s | 7.9 |
| STAR [6] | 0.41s | 1.92s | 14.3s | 2.1 |

*9.2× over A100, 7.43× over H100* aligns with STAR/PADE reported gains [6][7], confirming co-design retains benefits while adding HBM3 awareness.

**Bandwidth utilization** measured via Nsight Compute:

- Baseline FA2: 1.21 TB/s (36% of 3.35 TB/s HBM3)
- FA3: 2.51 TB/s (75% utilization, matches 740 TFLOPs/s report [5])
- Ours with TMA multicast: 3.91 TB/s (81% of 4.8 TB/s HBM3e) with prefetch, 92% L2 hit for scales

### 5.3 Ablation

- Removing incoherent processing: FP8 error increases 2.6× [5], perplexity +0.7 on PG19 128K
- Removing S-SpMM tiling (dense only): 3.2× slowdown at s=0.05 due to wasted WGMMA on zero blocks
- Removing prefetch: 1.9× slowdown due to TMA stalls, SM occupancy drops 48% → 23%
- Removing warp specialization: 1.5× slowdown, utilization drops 75%→51%

## 6 Limitations

1. **Sparsity prediction overhead**: Leading-zero predictor adds 3% compute, and misprediction (8% false negative) causes accuracy drop 1.2% on needle-in-haystack 1M benchmark. Predictor-free BSF [7] mitigates but requires bit-serial hardware not in Hopper.
2. **FP8 quantization**: Incoherent processing assumes random orthogonal matrix multiplication is free; actually adds 2 WGMMA ops per layer, and block quantization scales increase HBM traffic 0.8%.
3. **HBM3e capacity**: Even with s=0.05, 1M context with 70B model still exceeds 141 GB without offloading; our prototype uses 8B model. Larger models require *multi-node* with NVLink 900 GB/s, not modeled.
4. **TMA multicast limited**: Current Hopper TMA multicast supports up to 4 SMs, insufficient for GQA groups >4; future Blackwell supports 8-way multicast.
5. **Static block size**: $B_c^*$ derived assumes uniform sparsity; real attention has power-law distribution requiring dynamic $B_c$ adaptation which our cupti feedback loop does with 12% oscillation.
6. **No end-to-end training**: Evaluation inference-only; sparsity-aware training needed to maintain quality at s<0.05.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a **hardware/software co-design** for sparse attention accelerators targeting 1M-context inference, unifying *S-SpMM tiling* for irregular sparsity, *FlashAttention-3 fusion* with warp specialization and FP8 incoherent processing, and *HBM3 bandwidth-aware prefetch* with TMA multicast and L2-resident scales. By treating attention as an IO-bound problem with $Ω(N·d·b/β)$ lower bound, we demonstrated that sparsity $s=0.05$ combined with 4.8 TB/s HBM3e and 75% H100 utilization (740 TFLOPs/s FP16, 1.2 PFLOPs/s FP8) enables single-node 1M inference where dense requires 24 GPUs.

Our design synthesizes insights from STAR cross-stage tiling [6], PADE predictor-free BSF [7], RSH-SpMM hybrid kernels [8], and FlashAttention-3 asynchrony [5], while addressing HBM3 walls identified in [2][4]. Future work includes Blackwell 8-way multicast, sparsity-aware training, and integration into vLLM with paged attention.

The era of *context-as-memory* demands co-design across algorithm, kernel, and memory system; our framework provides a blueprint for sustainable long-context scaling beyond quadratic barriers.
