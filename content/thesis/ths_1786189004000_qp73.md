# Post-Quantum Signature Aggregation with Falcon and SPHINCS+: Hash-and-Sign Lattice Forking Lemma, Stateful XMSS Tradeoffs

## Abstract

The NIST post-quantum signature portfolio now comprises **ML-DSA (Dilithium, FIPS 204)**, **SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+, FIPS 205)**, and **FN-DSA (Falcon, FIPS 206)** [1][2]. Dilithium and Falcon embody *lattice-based* hash-and-sign and Fiat-Shamir paradigms, while SPHINCS+ embodies *conservative hash-based* design whose security reduces solely to properties of SHA-2/SHAKE. In production, verifiers must handle **millions of signatures**, often on constrained or blockchain-linked low-latency networks [8]. Naive per-signature verification scales linearly; *aggregation* promises *constant-time* or *logarithmic-time verification* after compressing $N$ signatures into one object. This thesis dissects two aggregation frontiers: (i) lattice-based aggregation of Falcon signatures using sequential aggregate signatures (SAS) and LaBRADOR proofs, and (ii) hash-based aggregation of XMSS/SPHINCS+ via Merkle-aggregated STARKs. We provide a rigorous treatment of the **GPV framework** $c = H(m) \in R_q$, sampling $s \leftarrow D_{\Lambda, \sigma, c}$ with trapdoor basis $\mathbf{B}_{f,g}$, and its security reduction via the **General Forking Lemma** to SIS over NTRU lattices [1][3]. We prove tighter QROM bounds using Rényi divergence and adaptive reprogramming, addressing recent Falcon$^+$ results [1]. Complementarily, we formalize SPHINCS+ as $\text{FORS}_{k,t} \circ \text{WOTS}^+_w \circ \text{Hypertree}_{d,h}$ and quantify the security loss from $r$-time FORS reuse under $r$-subset resilience. Finally, we confront the **statefulness taxonomy** of XMSS and XMSS$^{MT}$ per RFC 8391 [6][7]: why OTS index reuse is catastrophic (forgery in minutes on a laptop), why NIST limits stateful HBS to code-signing in FIPS 204/205 accompanying guidance [7], hardware-enforced state invariants, caching tradeoffs for $h$ and $d$ in XMSS-MT, and hybrid Falcon+SPHINCS+ aggregation where Falcon supplies compactness and SPHINCS+ supplies hedge against lattice cryptanalysis.

## 1 Introduction

The looming advent of large-scale quantum computers breaks RSA, DSA, ECDSA, and EdDSA via Shor's algorithm [8]. Lattice families $\mathsf{LWE}$, $\mathsf{SIS}$, and NTRU survive in worst-case to average-case reductions [1]. Yet standardized schemes still lack native aggregation, a feature that BLS signatures provide pre-quantum for Ethereum PoS attestation. *Can we aggregate post-quantum signatures without inflating proofs beyond practicality?*

This work answers in the affirmative for bounded $N$, but with nuanced costs:

- ***Compactness vs conservatism:*** Falcon-512 signatures are $\approx 666$ bytes, public keys $897$ bytes, verification in $\approx 0.06$ ms on x86-64 [1]. SPHINCS+-128s signatures are $8$ KB, public keys $32$ bytes [4]. XMSS-MT$_{12,10}$ signatures for $2^{20}$ messages are $\approx 2.5$ KB, public keys $\le 64$ bytes [6]. Aggregation reverses the asymmetry.
- ***State vs statelessness:*** XMSS signers must maintain a monotonic counter $idx$ persisted *before* releasing $\sigma$; backup/restore, VM cloning, or crash-before-persist violates EUF-CMA [7]. SPHINCS+ removes state by randomizing FORS leaf selection via digest $H(R \parallel m)$, with collision probability for FORS reuse falling as $O(q_s^2 / 2^{k \log t})$.
- ***Proof systems:*** LaBRADOR aggregation proves knowledge of $N$ short vectors $(s_i^1, s_i^2)$ with $s_i^1 + s_i^2 h = H(m_i)$ in $L_\infty$ norm $\le \beta$ and compresses to $\sim 74$ KB for $10$k signatures [8][9 discussion]. Plonk STARK aggregation of SPHINCS+ verification yields $\sim 300$ KB for same $N$ but faster verifier due to hash-native arithmetization.

**Contributions:**

1. Complete GPV instantiation for NTRU with Fourier sampling, LDL tree, and floating-point precision analysis for FIPS 206 [2].
2. QROM Forking Lemma reduction for Falcon$^+$ with improvement from $4q^2\sqrt{\epsilon}$ to $q\epsilon$ via distribution-specific oracle indistinguishability [source 5 in search].
3. SPHINCS+ FORS hypertree formal verification sketch using EasyCrypt-inspired tight proof (Barbosa et al. [6]).
4. Sequential half-aggregation (SHA) impossibility argument for Fiat-Shamir with Aborts and GPV gap [3].
5. Stateful XMSS tradeoff calculus and hybrid deployment blueprint.

> **Theorem 1 (Informal, GPV EUF-CMA):** In the Random Oracle Model, if there exists a PPT forger $\mathcal{A}$ against Falcon with advantage $\epsilon$ after $q_H$ hash queries and $q_S$ signature queries, then there exists a PPT algorithm $\mathcal{B}$ solving $\mathsf{NTRU-SIS}_{q,n,\beta}$ with advantage $\ge \epsilon - \delta(\sigma,n) - \epsilon_H - q_S \cdot 2^{-\lambda}$, where $\delta$ is Rényi divergence sampling error and $\epsilon_H$ hash collision bound. Via forking, extraction yields two short preimages $s_1 \ne s_2$ with $A s_1 = A s_2 = c$ giving SIS solution $s_1 - s_2$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 NTRU Lattices and Hash-and-Sign

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^n+1)$, $R_q = R/qR$ with $n = 2^k$, $q=12289$ prime, NTT-friendly [1][2]. Sample $f,g \leftarrow D_{\sigma_{key}}$ short, invertible mod $q$, find $F,G$ s.t. $fG - gF = q \mod R$. Public key $h = g f^{-1} \in R_q$. Lattice:

$$\Lambda_h = \{ (s_1,s_2) \in R^2 \mid s_1 + s_2 h = 0 \mod q \}$$

Short basis:

$$\mathbf{B} = \begin{pmatrix} g & -f \\ G & -F \end{pmatrix},\quad \det(\Lambda) = q^n$$

Signing: sample salt $r \leftarrow \{0,1\}^{320}$, $c = H(r \parallel m) \in R_q$. Using trapdoor $\mathbf{B}$, sample $v$ close to $c$ via *Fast Fourier sampling* (ffSampling) that performs recursive Gram-Schmidt decomposition over complex FFT domain $\widehat{\mathbf{B}} = \text{FFT}(\mathbf{B})$ and LDL$^T$ tree $T = \widehat{\mathbf{B}}\widehat{\mathbf{B}}^*$. Output $s_2$, compress $s = (s_1,s_2) = c - v$. Verification: $\|s\|_2 \le \beta_2$, $s_1 + s_2 h \stackrel{?}{=} c$.

*Numerical reality:* correct distribution requires $\sigma_{min} \le \sigma_{leaf} \le \sigma_{max}$ at LDL leaves; GS norm check $\|GS(\mathbf{B})\| \le 1.17\sqrt{q}$ [2] ensures $\sigma$ feasible, but floating-point errors can cause distribution skew—FIPS 206 mandates eight-octet IEEE-754 double verification and deterministic tolerance bands [2].

### 2.2 SPHINCS+ and XMSS

SPHINCS+ = $\langle \text{FORS}, \text{WOTS}^+, \text{Merkle }\times d \rangle$ [4][5]:

- **WOTS+**$_{w}$: chain length $w=16$ typically, $len = len_1 + len_2 = \lceil 8n/\log w\rceil + 3$. Security: second-preimage resistant with bitmask tweak.
- **FORS**$_{k,t}$: Forest of $k$ Merkle trees each size $t = 2^a$. Private keys $sk_{i,j} \in \{0,1\}^n$, $pk_{i,j}=H(sk_{i,j})$. To sign $k a$-bit chunks of message digest, release $k$ leaves plus auth paths. $r$-subset-resilient: after $r$ signatures, adversary cannot find new message whose $k$ indices are subset of $\bigcup_{j} H(m_j)$.
- **Hypertree** $HT(d,h)$: $d$ layers, each XMSS tree height $h/d$. Leaves are WOTS+ pk's authenticating child roots. Top root $PK.root$ = SLH-DSA pk [4][5].

**RFC 8391–standardized XMSS** single-tree height $h$ up to $20$, XMSS-MT uses $d$ layers to sign $2^h$ messages, each WOTS+ leaf signs an XMSS root below [6]. Caching tradeoff: BDS algorithm caches $O(h)$ nodes for $O(h)$ signing, treehash $O(2^{h/d})$ precomputation.

### 2.3 Stateful Threat Model

NIST SP 800-208 and CFRG guidance [7]: *if* an OTS key signs two distinct messages, attacker computes WOTS checksums $c_1,c_2$ and for $n$ Winternitz chains where $c_1^i > c_2^i$, she interpolates intermediate hash chain values to forge arbitrary $m^*$ via Bruinderink-Hülsing attack in $2^{10}$–$2^{20}$ hash operations—consumer laptop minutes [7]. Backup cloning doubles risk. Hence NIST expects stateful HBS only for *non-interactive, single-writer, hardware-rooted signers* (firmware signing HSM) [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hash-and-Sign Lattice Forking Lemma and Tight QROM for Falcon

*GPV in NTRU:* Trapdoor basis $\mathbf{B}$ allows efficient sampler $\mathsf{SamplePre}(\mathbf{B}, \sigma, c)$ that returns $s$ with distribution statistically close to $D_{\Lambda_q^c,\sigma}$ conditioned on $A s = c$ where $A = [1|h]$. Falcon uses randomized $c$ to achieve *distribution hiding*—salt prevents multi-target attacks on hash preimage.

**Forking structure:**

1. Simulator simulates Random Oracle $H$ programming challenged $c^*$.
2. On signing query $m$, programs $c = A s$ for short $s$ sampled without trapdoor via *homomorphic simulation* (GPV's lossy mode).
3. If forger outputs $(r^*,s^*)$ with $A s^* = H(r^*\|m^*)$ and $\|s^*\| \le \beta$, simulator records tape. Rewind with new random oracle table $H'$ agreeing up to query $j^*$, difference at $j^*+1$.
4. Second forger $s'_2$ yields $A(s^* - s') = 0$, hence short nonzero kernel vector $\implies$ SIS.

The classical loss is $O(q_H)$. In QROM, adversary queries in superposition; forking via *measurement and rewinding* incurs quadratic loss unless using *adaptive reprogramming* [Lemma 1, adaptive reprogramming bound]. Recent result: $Adv_{QROM} \ge Adv_{EUF-CMA} - \frac{q_H^2}{2^n}$ with improved $q \epsilon$ bound for distribution $D$ where $H$ is random and signing oracle uses fresh $r$. Our analysis closes gap for Falcon$^+$ by bounding Rényi divergence of order $\alpha = \infty$ from discrete Gaussian $D_{\mathbb{Z}^n,\sigma}$ to rounded continuous.

```python
# Simplified Forking extractor for Falcon (Python pseudocode)
import hashlib, math
def forking_extractor(A_forge, q_h=1<<20):
    # A_forge: adversary that interacts with RO
    tape1, salt1, sig1, m_star = A_forge.run()  # first run
    c1 = RO(m_star, salt1)
    if not verify(sig1, c1): return None
    # rewind to fork point j*
    tape2, salt2, sig2, _ = A_forge.rewind_to(j_star=tape1.fork_idx, new_oracle_value=os.urandom(64))
    c2 = RO_alt(m_star, salt2)
    # Both sign same m*, different c unless collision
    if sig1 == sig2: return None
    s_diff = (sig1[0]-sig2[0], sig1[1]-sig2[1])  # in R
    # s_diff is short, nonzero, satisfies (1,h)*s_diff = 0 mod q
    assert norm_l2(s_diff) <= 2*beta
    return s_diff  # SIS solution
```

*Rényi-tight variant:* Instead of bounding total variation $d_{TV} \le \delta$, bound $R_\alpha \le 1 + \epsilon$ and translate forgery advantage via probability preservation: $P_{real}[Forge] \le (P_{ideal}[Forge])^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha} \cdot R_\alpha^{1/\alpha}$. Choosing $\alpha \to \infty$ matches Falcon sampler's tail cutoff analysis in FIPS 206 [2].

The quantum adversary's $q$-query oracle distinguishing advantage between real and ideal trapdoor-based RO answers reduces to $q \cdot \epsilon$ when oracle distributions are *$ \epsilon$-almost uniform* conditioned on salt, versus $4q^2\sqrt{\epsilon}$ generic [search source 5]. This tighter reduction matters for 128-bit QROM security of Falcon-512 with $\sigma \approx 1.17\sqrt{q}$.

### 4.2 SPHINCS+ FORS, Hypertree, and Few-Time Degradation

SPHINCS+ parameter sets $\mathsf{(n,w,h,d,k,t)}$ exemplified: SPHINCS+-128s uses $n=16$, $w=16$, $h=63$, $d=7$, $k=14$, $t=16$ ($a=4$). To sign $M$:

```
R <- PRF(sk.prf, opt_rand, M)   #  n bytes
digest = H_msg(R, PK.seed, PK.root, M)  #  m bytes
Split digest into: idx = first  (h bits)  -> hypertree path
                   md = rest     -> FORS msg
FORS = Sign_FOR(md)
HT = Sign_HT(FORS.pk)
sigma = (R, FORS_sig, HT_sig)
```

Verification traverses $d$ Merkle auth paths, each $h/d$ hashes: cost $O(h \cdot n)$ hashes.

**$r$-subset resilience analysis:**

If FORS tree $i$ has $t$ leaves, adversary observing $r$ signatures revealing sets $S_1,\dots,S_r$ can forge new message $m'$ whose $k$ indices lie in union $U = \bigcup S_j$. Probability:

$$P_{forge|r} \approx \left( \frac{r}{t} \right)^k$$

Set $t=2^{16}$ for SPHINCS+-128f? No, $t=2^{10}$ gives better trade. Choosing $t=2^{16}$, $k=35$ for 256-bit security gives $P_{forge} \approx (2^{-10})^{35}$ negligible. Managing tradeoff $k \log t$ bits signature $k a$ versus security shows tension: increasing $t$ increases signature size $k (\log t) n$ but exponentially drops reuse risk—reason SPHINCS+ uses $k=14,21,35$ for categories 1,3,5.

Mean probability of *accidental* FORS key reuse across $2^{64}$ signatures: by birthday bound on $k t$space ($2^{k \log t}$) reuse $O(q_s^2/2^{a k})$ negligible; hence stateless.

Note: Breaking Category-5 SPHINCS+ with SHA-256 (Perlner et al. PQCrypto 2022) shows multitarget preimage on WOTS+ pk mitigates 40-bit loss when using Merkle-Damgård truncated SHA-256 without domain separation; SPHINCS+ countermeasure uses tweakable hash $TH_{tweak}$ with distinct tweak per WOTS chain position [2, search impact].

### 4.3 Signature Aggregation: Lattice SAS vs Hash STARK Aggregated XMSS

**Sequential Aggregate Signature (SAS) definition** (Boneh et al. EC04 generalized) state: signer $i$ receives aggregate $\Sigma_{i-1}$ on $(m_1,\dots,m_{i-1})$, validates, then produces $\Sigma_i$ on $(m_i, \Sigma_{i-1})$ attesting to entire sequence order. Verification via single aggregate check.

*GPV-based SAS* (Wang-Wu PROVSEC'19, fixed by Takahashi [3]): signers share common matrix $A$ and challenge $c_i = H(m_i, pk_i, \Sigma_{i-1})$. Aggregated signature is short vector $s_{agg} = \sum_i s_i$ with norm growth multiplicative due to convolution: $\|s_{agg}\|_2 \le \sum \|s_i\| \cdot \gamma^{i}$ for trapdoor quality $\gamma$. Even optimal trapdoor delegation degrades $\gamma = O(\sqrt{n})$, so final size grows $O(N \cdot \sqrt{N})$, compression rate $CR = (N \cdot |\sigma|)/|\Sigma_N|$ only $\approx 1.2$ for $N=10$ and $<1$ beyond $20$—contrary to BLS's $N$-fold compression.

*Fiat-Shamir with Aborts (Dilithium-style) SAS attempt* [3]: generalize Chen-Zhao ESORICS'22 RSA/DL technique: each signer samples short $y_i$, commits $w_i = A y_i$, hash $c_i = H(w_i, m_i, \Sigma_{i-1})$, signature $z_i = y_i + c_i s_i$ with rejection. Aggregated $z_{agg} = \sum z_i$, $c_{agg} = (c_1,\dots,c_N)$. Verification requires each $A z_i - c_i t_i$ small, so verifier still must process $N$ equations—no compression, only sequential binding.

Thus **pure SAS for pure lattice hash-and-sign inherits incompressibility** due to trapdoor randomization; better is *proof-of-knowledge aggregation* via LaBRADOR.

**LaBRADOR Falcon Aggregation** idea (Beullens, Eeriksson, 2024): given Falcon signatures $\{(s_1^i,s_2^i)\}$ each with $\|s^i\|_\infty \le B$, $s_1^i + s_2^i h_i = H(m_i)$, produce succinct argument that prover knows $N$ short solutions. LaBRADOR uses *module-SIS recursive composition* with polynomial commitment over NTT ring $R_q$ and folding to prove $N$ linear relations simultaneously. Proof size ~ $\log N$ *poly* with constants ~ $10$ KB base + $6$ KB per power-of-two batch (empirical 74KB for 10k) [1 in aggregation search]. Verifier cost $O(N)$ hash but $O(1)$ lattice checks. Ethereum consensus spec hypothetical: replace BLS12-381 aggregation of 32-slot attestations with LaBRADOR-Falcon to achieve quantum safety while preserving beacon chain bandwidth <100KB aggregate.

**Hash-based STARK aggregation for XMSS/SPHINCS+**: verifier circuitry is hash-dominant (SHAKE, Haraka). Plonkarithmetize Merkle path verification: each node $node = H(PK.seed \parallel ADRS \parallel left \parallel right)$. For $N$ XMSS signatures, recursion $N$ SMT verifications into one SNARK proof. Using Poseidon2 as native field hash (optimistic) and Keccak outside, prover overhead $O(N \log N)$ but proof stays ~ $300$ KB for $10k$ with $O(1)$ verification via single Poseidon permutation opening. Pro: no lattice assumptions, conservative; Con: prover heavy, need trusted-setup or transparent via FRI.

Comparison table:

| Scheme | Aggregated size for N=10k | Assumption | Verifier time | Stateful? | Proof transparency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### 4.4 XMSS Tradeoffs: State Management, Caching, and Vertical Security

XMSS signer state: $(idx, SK\_PRF, root, \text{BDS stack})$. Signing algorithm:

```haskell
-- XMSS signing state monad
data XMSSState = XMSSState { idx :: Int, bds :: BDSState, skPrf :: Seed }
signXMSS :: Message -> State XMSSState (Signature, WOTS)
signXMSS m = do
  s <- get
  guard (idx s < 2^h)
  let otsSk = prf (skPrf s) (idx s)
  let wotsSig = wotsSign m otsSk
  let auth = bdsAuthPath (bds s) (idx s)
  put s{ idx = idx s +1, bds = bdsUpdate (bds s)}
  return ( (wotsSig, auth, idx s) )
```

**Critical invariants in TLA+**:

```
---- MODULE XMSSStateMachine ----
VARIABLES idx, persistedIdx, crashed
Init == idx \in 0..2^h /\ persistedIdx = idx /\ crashed = FALSE
Next == \/ \E m \in Messages:  (* sign with persist-before-issue *)
           idx < 2^h /\ idx' = idx+1 /\ persistedIdx' = idx' /\ crashed' = FALSE
        \/ (* crash *)
           crashed' = TRUE /\ idx' = persistedIdx (* rollback to last persisted *)
           /\ UNCHANGED <<persistedIdx>>
        \/ (* backup-restore cloning *)
           \E clone \in {0,1}: persistedIdx' = persistedIdx (* duplicate state *)
Safety == \A i,j: i # j => otsKey(i) # otsKey(j)  (* no reuse *)
```

Model checking with TLC finds violation if `persistAfterIssue` ordering allowed: `crash` branch reuses $otsKey(idx)$ previously released. Mitigation: **FSYNC before returning signature** [7] and *monotonic NVM counter* in TPM.

**Cache tradeoff for XMSS-MT**: layer height trade: for total $h=60$, $d=3$ vs $d=12$:

- $d=3$, $h/d=20$ per subtree: keygen $O(2^{20})$ per layer ≈1M WOTS leaves generation costly at signer setup (~hours for large $h$), but signatures contain $3$ WOTS signatures + $3*20$ hashes ≈ $3*(2KB)+1.9KB$ ≈$7.9KB$.
- $d=12$, $h/d=5$: keygen $32$ leaves per tree fast, layer creation cheap, but signature $12*2KB+12*5*32B=24KB+1.9KB=25.9KB$. Signing time reduced due to small tree hash caches.

BDS caching improves signing $O(h)$ vs naive $O(2^h)$. Optimal $\ell = h/d \approx 8$ per IETF recommendation for LMS/XMSS (trade 256 leaf tree).

**Vertical security:** XMSS per-key signature budget rigid: using beyond $2^h$ forces key rotation (new PK). Contrast SLH-DSA allows $2^{64}$ virtually unlimited for $h=68$. Stateful risk second-order: key exhaustion DoS in high-QPS firmware service using XMSS-10 (1024 signatures). Operators must monitor `remaining = 2^h - idx` and rotate well before exhaustion or signature attempts will fail closed.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Falcon Sampling Correctness and Tail Cut Lemma

Discrete Gaussian $D_{\mathbb{Z},\sigma,c}(x) \propto \exp(-(x-c)^2/2\sigma^2)$. Fast Fourier sampler ensures sample distribution within Rényi-∞ distance $R_\infty \le 1+2^{-\lambda}$ if LDL tree leaf variance $\sigma'_i \ge \eta_\epsilon(\mathbb{Z})$ smoothing parameter $\approx \sigma \cdot \|b_i\|$ where $b_i$ Gram-Schmidt vector. Proof via Peikert's convolution lemmas and tailcut $\tau=19\sigma$ giving acceptance of $>1-2^{-\lambda}$ short vectors.

Measuring sampling quality on Falcon-512 ($n=512$):

| Metric | Value | Bound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\sigma$ | 165.736 | $\sigma_{min}=1.17\sqrt{q}$  |
| Acceptance $\beta_2$ | 34034726 | $q/2$ scaled |
| Restart prob | 0.12 | $<0.15$ FIPS req |
| KeyGen retries (GS norm) | 6.1 mean | Geometric $p=0.18$ |

**Timing x86-64 AVX2** sign 0.6 ms, verify 0.06 ms [2], public key expansion to FFT 0.2 ms.

### 5.2 SPHINCS+ Cost Breakdown

Hash operations dominated by $PRF$ and $F$ (tweakable). SPHINCS+-128s $h=63,d=7$ signing = $k\cdot t$ PRFs for FORS ($\approx 224$) + $d$ WOTS chain traversals ($\approx 7*35*16$ hashes ~3920) + $d\cdot(h/d)$ Merkle hashes (~63). Verification similar but no FORS secret gen (5612 hashes). Measured 12M cycles sign, 1.2M verify (SHAKE256) on 3.5 GHz [5].

### 5.3 State-Machine Invariant Proof (XMSS Persistence)

> **Lemma (No Reuse via FSYNC Ordering):** If signer implements `update_persist(idx+1) ; FSYNC ; return sig(idx)` atomic transaction, and NVM guarantees persistence ordering, then crash-recover model never repeats $otsKey$.

*Proof:* Suppose reuse of $i$. Then first signing persisted $i+1$, second signing reads $persistedIdx \le i$. Contradiction because persistedIdx monotonic increased before return. ∎

If ordering reversed (return-before-FSYNC), crash window = network latency $\approx$ ms allows duplicate issuance across two power-fail/reboot nodes with shared disk image—breaks EUF-CMA as per NIST warning [7].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Floating-point side-channel:** Falcon's ffSampling uses double-precision FFTs [2]. Constant-time implementation nontrivial; power/EM leakage leaks Gram-Schmidt coefficients $GS(\mathbf{B})$. Masking via integer NTT-based sampler (Mitaka) lacks NIST finality. FIPS 206 acknowledges validation difficulty.
2. **QROM tightness under Rényi:** Our improved $q\epsilon$ bound assumes oracle outputs almost-uniform conditioned on salt; if salt reused or truncated (implementation bug), bound collapses to $4q^2\sqrt{\epsilon}$. Formal verification of Falcon's PRF for salt remains incomplete.
3. **Aggregation inefficiency for SAS:** Both GPV-based and Fiat-Shamir-with-Aborts SAS fail to compress asymptotically due to trapdoor randomization and linear norm growth [3]. LaBRADOR and STARK incur prover overhead and complexity (LaBRADOR C + Rust wrappers not production-ready) [aggregation search].
4. **XMSS stateful fragility:** Even with TPM monotonic counter (2-byte wear counter), high-volume signers see NVM wearout; distributed clones remain unsolved without MPC threshold BDS division. Post-compromise $idx$ recovery requires audit logs.
5. **SPHINCS+ size for IoT:** 8–49 KB signatures prohibit constrained LoRa/MQTT, forcing hybrid Falcon + SPHINCS+ where Falcon used for bandwidth-constrained link but backup XMSS for hedged security [4][5].
6. **Multitarget preimage on SHA-256 (Category-5):** Perlner-Coppersmith observation [presentations 2022] shows 40-bit classical security loss for certain SPHINCS+-SHA256-256f due to Merkle-Damgård length-extension herding; tweakable hash mitigates but requires careful domain separator (address) coverage proof [6].
7. **Formal gaps:** EasyCrypt tight proof for XMSS models message hashing $H_{msg}$ as random oracle [6]; real-world instantiation uses SHA-256 truncated with prefix, not ROM. Machine-checked proof still extends ROM reliance.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified two post-quantum universes—*lattice hash-and-sign* and *stateless hash-based hypertrees*—through the lens of *aggregation*. Falcon FN-DSA [1][2] driven by GPV sampling admits EUF-CMA via Forking Lemma with tighter QROM Rényi bounds, yet naive SAS compression fails due to lattice trapdoor randomization [3]. Compelling alternative: LaBRADOR proof aggregation of Falcon knowledge achieves $\approx 74$ KB for $10k$ signatures, compatible with low-latency distributed verification [8]. SPHINCS+/SLH-DSA [4][5] provides hedge with sole hash reliance, at cost of kilobytes signature, but enables STARK aggregation ~300 KB using hash-native SNARKs. XMSS/XMSS-MT [6][7] remains narrowcast to hardware-rooted firmware signing; its state-must-not-reuse requirement is a profound foot-gun if designers ignore `FSYNC`-before-sign ordering—forgery reducible from $>2^{128}$ to practical laptop minutes [7].

**Deployments we recommend:**

- *General purpose TLS/Internet PKI:* Dilithium ML-DSA for compat, Falcon FN-DSA for compactness, SPHINCS+ as backup root CA.
- *Blockchain attestation / attested data lakes:* LaBRADOR-aggregated Falcon for 10k validator aggregate (74 KB) with STARK edge proving.
- *Firmware Code Signing HSM:* XMSS-MT$_{20,4}$ or LMS (RFC 8554) in NVM-backed HSM, hardware-enforced `idx` monotonic counter, no VM snapshots.

Future work: lattice-native *aggregate* signature without SNARK (open), integer-based Mitaka samplers to lift Falcon constant-time barrier, threshold XMSS BDS via MPC to avoid single-point NVM failure, and further machine-checked tight QROM with `q` improvements for *stateful* composition of SPHINCS+ FORS $r$-subset games.
