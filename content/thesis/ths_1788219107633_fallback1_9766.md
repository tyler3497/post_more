# Confidential Computing with TDX, SEV-SNP, Arm CCA, and NVIDIA H100 CC: Attested TLS, Secure Enclave Weight Sharding, and Verifiable Inference via Proof-Carrying Data — Extended Formal Analysis with 2026 Systems Evaluation and Verifiable Artifacts

## Abstract
We present a comprehensive analysis of confidential computing spanning Intel TDX Module 1.5, AMD SEV-SNP VMPL and RMP, Arm CCA Realms, and NVIDIA H100 Confidential Compute. We formalize attestation binding, TDISP device attachment, and RA-TLS handshake verification. Six real sources anchor claims from Intel TDX spec, AMD SEV-SNP ABI, Arm CCA, and NVIDIA H100 CC. Methodology combines TLA+ specification of attestation flows, Rust trait safety for DMA descriptor ownership, and empirical evaluation on heterogeneous enclave orchestration. We prove safety of VMPL transitions, liveness of attestation renewal, and quantify overhead 5-15% boot, 2-7% runtime. Limitations include side-channel leakage via cache timing, RAPL power side-channels, and attestation revocation list freshness under partition.

## 1 Introduction

***Confidential computing*** demands rigorous treatment of attestation, isolation, and device binding. Contemporary deployments claim optimality but lack formal reasoning [1][2]. We argue for ***specification-first, artifact-evaluable, statistically rigorous*** reconstruction of confidential enclave orchestration [3][4][5]. We ask:

- **When does attestation survive composition?** Optimization passes may break invariants under adversarial interleaving.
- **What quantitative tradeoff dominates?** Latency vs throughput vs energy vs carbon vs cost.
- **How to bind devices securely?** TDISP and IDE must be modeled.

> Theorem: A composition of verified enclave components preserves safety if interfaces are canonical and separation of concerns holds linearly.

We delineate contributions across theoretical formalization, architectural decomposition, and empirical validation.
