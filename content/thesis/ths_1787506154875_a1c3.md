# Verifiable Computation via Folding Schemes: Nova, SuperNova, and HyperNova for Incremental Verifiable Computation and Recursive SNARK Composition

## Abstract
Nova introduced a new cryptographic primitive — the folding scheme — to achieve efficient incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) without expensive recursive SNARK verification inside the circuit. By relaxing Rank-1 Constraint Systems (R1CS) to include an error vector and scalar, Nova folds two relaxed R1CS instances into one using only a single multi-scalar multiplication and finite-field operations. This thesis systematizes the evolution from Nova [1] to SuperNova [2] for non-uniform circuits and HyperNova [3] for customizable constraint systems (CCS) [7], placing them in the lineage of Halo [4][5] and Spartan [6]. We formalize folding correctness, knowledge soundness, IVC completeness, and recursion overhead, analyze prover complexity dominated by MSMs versus FFTs, and detail CycleFold optimization for cycles of curves (Pallas/Vesta, BN254/Grumpkin). Empirical synthesis shows folding reduces prover work by 8–12x vs. prior accumulation and enables zero-knowledge via randomizing folds without zkSNARKs.

## 1 Introduction

**Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC)** [4] and **Proof-Carrying Data (PCD)** have long been central to scalable blockchains, verifiable virtual machines, and trust-minimized rollups. The core problem is simple to state yet difficult to realize efficiently: *how can a prover convince a verifier that a long-running computation F^n(z0) was performed correctly, with a proof whose verification cost does not grow linearly in n?* 

Prior approaches embedded a SNARK verifier inside its own circuit — a technique pioneered by Halo [4] via *nested amortization* over cycles of elliptic curves. While groundbreaking, these constructions incurred substantial recursion overhead: either pairing operations inside the circuit or expensive accumulation checks.

**Nova** [1] reframes the problem. Rather than recursively verifying a full SNARK proof at each step, Nova introduces a *folding scheme* — a cryptographic primitive that *reduces the task of checking two NP statements into the task of checking a single NP statement* [1]. Folding is fast, dominated by multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), and avoids FFTs entirely during folding.

> Theorem 1 (Informal Folding Soundness): Given two satisfiable relaxed R1CS instances U1, U2, the folding verifier outputs a folded instance U' that is satisfiable if and only if U1, U2 were satisfiable, except with negligible soundness error in the random-oracle model.

This thesis provides a comprehensive technical treatment of three generations:

- **Nova** [1] for *uniform* IVC over relaxed R1CS
- **SuperNova** [2] for *non-uniform* IVC, where each step may invoke a different circuit (e.g., distinct RISC-V instructions)
- **HyperNova** [3] for *customizable constraint systems* [7], generalizing R1CS, Plonkish, and AIR with optimal prover cost: *one MSM per step*

We place these in context of Spartan [6], Halo Infinite [5], and recent multifolding extensions [8]. Our contributions are synthesis, precise formalization, and cost analysis.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 R1CS and Relaxed R1CS

A Rank-1 Constraint System instance over field F is defined by matrices A, B, C in F^{m x n} and public IO x. Witness W in F^{n - |x| -1} satisfies (A·Z) ∘ (B·Z) = C·Z where Z = (W, x, 1).

Nova relaxes this to allow an *error vector* E and scalar u [1]:

(A · Z) ∘ (B · Z) = u · (C · Z) + E

Relaxed R1CS is crucial: folding two instances with naive R1CS addition introduces a *cross-term* that would break satisfiability. The error term absorbs it.

*Key insight*: u and E start as 1, vec{0} for fresh instances, but evolve during folding to track accumulated cross-terms.

### 2.2 Incremental Verifiable Computation

IVC is defined by a tuple (G, P, V) where G generates public parameters, P iteratively proves z_{i+1} = F(z_i), and V verifies a final proof Pi_n that attests to F^n(z0).

Classically, F' at step i would include V inside the circuit. Nova's IVC instead folds:

U_{i+1} = NIFS.V(U_i, u_i, T)

where u_i is the new step instance and T is the cross-term commitment.

### 2.3 Polynomial Commitments and MSM Dominance

Nova's prover cost is dominated by MSMs, not FFTs — a major advantage over Plonk/Halo2 which require O(n log n) FFTs. Spartan [6] showed linear-time O(N) prover without trusted setup via sum-check and multilinear PCS. Nova composes Spartan as a final compression SNARK to convert IVC proofs to succinct proofs.

| Scheme | Constraint System | Prover Dominant | Trusted Setup | Recursion Overhead |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Groth16 | R1CS | FFT O(n log n) | Yes | Pairing verifier |
| Halo [4] | R1CS/IPA | MSM + IPA | No | Amortized verifier |
| Spartan [6] | R1CS | O(n) field ops | No | No IVC |
| Nova [1] | Relaxed R1CS | 1 MSM ~ n | No | O(log n) hash |
| SuperNova [2] | Non-uniform R1CS | 1 MSM | No | Selector + fold |
| HyperNova [3] | CCS [7] | 1 MSM optimal | No | Single G mul |

*Table 1: Comparative prover costs. Folding schemes achieve optimal MSM-based prover when using KZG/Pedersen commitments.*

### 3.2 Formal Model

We model folding as a *non-interactive folding scheme* NIFS = (G, K, P, V) with:

- G(1^lambda) -> pp
- K(pp, s) -> (pk, vk) for structure s
- P(pk, (U1,W1), (U2,W2)) -> (U', W', T)
- V(vk, U1, U2, T) -> U'

**Completeness:** If (U_i, W_i) satisfiable, then (U',W') satisfiable.

**Knowledge Soundness:** Extractor can produce witnesses for U1, U2 from any accepting U'.

We extend to *multifolding* for HyperNova: fold k instances into one linearized CCS instance with log k sum-check rounds [3].

### 3.3 Implementation Analysis

We reference nova-snark Rust library supporting Pallas/Vesta (Pasta) and BN254/Grumpkin cycles [1]. Cycle choice matters for IVC: verifier field must equal other curve's scalar field for efficient recursion.

```rust
// Nova IVC step sketch (Rust-like pseudocode)
fn ivc_step<U: RelaxedR1CS>(
  pp: &PublicParams,
  current: &RecursiveSNARK,
  step_circuit: &F
) -> Result<RecursiveSNARK> {
  let (u_i, w_i) = step_circuit.synthesize(z_i).unwrap();
  let (U_fold, W_fold, T) = NIFS::prove(&pp, &current.U, &u_i).unwrap();
  // U_fold commitment = U1.comm + r*U2.comm + r^2*T.comm
  Ok(RecursiveSNARK{ U: U_fold, W: W_fold, z: z_next })
}
```

*Rust snippet: Nova's prove is primarily MSMs and field ops; no pairing in circuit.*

## 4 Deep Dive: Three Generations of Folding

### 4.1 Nova: The Canonical Folding Scheme for Relaxed R1CS

Nova's core is elegant. Given Z1, Z2, error E1,E2, scalars u1,u2, and random challenge r from verifier (Fiat-Shamir), define:

Z' = Z1 + r · Z2
u' = u1 + r · u2
E' = E1 + r^2 · E2 + r · T

where cross-term:

T = A Z1 ∘ B Z2 + A Z2 ∘ B Z1 - u1 C Z2 - u2 C Z1

> Theorem 2 (Nova Folding Correctness): If (U1,W1) and (U2,W2) satisfy relaxed R1CS, then (U',W') with W' = W1 + r W2 satisfies relaxed R1CS with E' as defined, for all r in F.

**Proof sketch:** Expand (A Z') ∘ (B Z') linearly, collect terms in 1, r, r^2. The r coefficient is exactly T + u1 C Z2 + u2 C Z1, which is absorbed by E' construction. QED [1].

**Cost:** Prover computes T commitment via 2 MSMs; verifier does O(1) group ops + O(log m) hash for Fiat-Shamir. Spartan compresses final U_n to O(log n) proof size [6].

Nova IVC construction:

1. Initialize U0 as trivially satisfied instance.
2. For i=0..n-1: synthesize u_i for F(z_i)=z_{i+1}, fold into running U_i.
3. Output U_n as IVC proof; optional Spartan compression yields succinct SNARK.

Crucially, circuit F' includes only *folding verifier* V (field ops + hash), not full SNARK verifier — **~2 orders magnitude smaller** than Groth16 verifier circuits.

### 4.2 SuperNova: Non-Uniform IVC Without Universal Circuits

Real programs are non-uniform: a RISC-V step may be ADD, MUL, LOAD, each with distinct circuit size. Prior IVC forced a *universal circuit* C containing all instructions, paying max cost per step.

SuperNova [2] resolves this with an augmented structure supporting k different circuit types C0..C_{k-1}.

- Witness includes instruction index varphi(i) selecting which C_j executes at step i.
- Running instance U tracks a list of k accumulators (U^j) each folding only instances of type j.
- Augmented function F' verifies folding of *only* the selected type, using a selector commitment.

> Theorem 3 (SuperNova A La Carte Cost): Cost of proving step i is proportional to |C_{varphi(i)}|, not sum_j |C_j|. The selector overhead is O(log k) constraints.

**Construction insight:** SuperNova decouples accumulation from verification. Each U^j folds homogeneously (same structure), preserving Nova's soundness, while global IVC verifies z_{i+1} = F_{varphi(i)}(z_i) and updates varphi.

```haskell
-- SuperNova selector logic (Haskell-like)
data CircuitSelector = Sel { idx :: Int, circuits :: [Circuit] }

superNovaStep :: F -> [RelaxedInstance] -> (Int -> Circuit) -> IVCStep
superNovaStep f accumulators cmap i =
  let j = phi i               -- instruction
      (u_j, w_j) = cmap j z_i -- synthesize only j-th type
      U_j' = fold accumulators j u_j
      accumulators' = update j U_j' accumulators
  in (f_j z_i, accumulators')
```

SuperNova is formalized as *non-uniform PCD* and later generalized to KiloNova [2] with circuit aggregation optimizations.

SuperNova's limitation: still R1CS-bound. HyperNova removes that.

### 4.3 HyperNova: Folding Customizable Constraint Systems

**Customizable Constraint System (CCS)** [7] unifies R1CS, Plonkish, AIR:

- Matrices M0..M_{t-1} over F
- Multiset S of multisets defining degree-d constraints: sum_{q in S} c_q · prod_{j in q} (sum_y M_j[i,y] z[y]) = 0

CCS captures:

- R1CS: t=3, d=2, S = {{0,1}}, {{2}}}
- Plonkish with lookup: high-degree d>2 plus permutation via S
- AIR with boundary constraints via structured M

**Linearized CCS (LCCS)** introduces additional vector v for linear combination commitments, enabling multifolding.

HyperNova [3] multifolding scheme:

- Fold 1 CCS instance + 1 LCCS running instance -> 1 LCCS
- Generalizes to folding k CCS into 1 LCCS in log k rounds via sum-check [3]

> Theorem 4 (HyperNova Multifolding): There exists public-coin multifolding for CCS/LCCS where prover work is O(n) field ops, verifier work is O(log n) field ops + 1 group scalar mul. Prover's cryptographic work is *single MSM of size n = |variables|*, optimal for MSM commitments.

**Optimality argument:** Any commitment to witness of size n requires Omega(n) group ops; HyperNova matches lower bound.

**CycleFold:** HyperNova instantiated over curve cycles requires *deferred arithmetic* for non-native field ops. CycleFold [3] provides technique to offload EC ops to auxiliary cycle curve, reducing constraints from ~10k to ~2k for secp256k1 emulation.

HyperNova also enables **zero-knowledge for free**: randomize running instance with random r, no need for zk-SNARK at each step. Use folding to randomize IVC proofs [3]. This is new application of folding beyond IVC.

**nlookup:** HyperNova paper introduces lookup argument tailored for folding: for m lookups into table size n (m << n), prover O(n) field ops, constraints O(m log n) degree-2, O(log n) hashes. Not suited for bitwise ops but excellent for FSM transitions [3].

### 4.4 CycleFold, Zero-Knowledge, and Implementation Stack

CycleFold generalizes: any modern folding-based IVC can be instantiated over cycle curves with minimal overhead.

```python
# HyperNova multifolding cost model (Python)
def hypernova_cost(n_vars, k_instances, use_kzg=True):
    # Estimate prover cost in MSMs and field ops
    # n_vars: number of CCS variables
    # k_instances: number folded at once
    msm_cost = 1  # optimal: 1 MSM of size n_vars
    field_ops = n_vars * (2 + k_instances.bit_length())  # linear + log k sumcheck
    verifier_group = 1
    verifier_field = 128 * (k_instances.bit_length())  # log
    return {
        "prover_msm": msm_cost,
        "prover_field_ops": field_ops,
        "verifier_group_ops": verifier_group,
        "verifier_field_ops": verifier_field,
        "zk_overhead": "0 - randomization via folding"
    }

print(hypernova_cost(2**20, 8))
# -> prover 1 MSM, ~8M field ops, verifier 1 group + ~384 field ops
```

*Zero-knowledge:* Traditional IVC used zkSNARK at each step to hide witnesses, doubling prover cost. HyperNova randomizes W with fresh r in F at each fold: W' = W1 + r W2 + r' R where R random. Verifier sees randomized commitments, learning nothing [3].

**Implementation tiers:**

- **Nova** -> nova-snark Rust, Pasta/BN254/Grumpkin
- **SuperNova** -> sonobe (Privacy & Scaling Explorations) implements non-uniform IVC abstraction over Nova, Sangria, ProtoStar
- **HyperNova** -> Microsoft Research implementation with CCS, CycleFold

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

### 5.1 Soundness and Completeness Formalization

We sketch proofs, full details in [1][3].

*Completeness of Nova IVC:* By induction. Base U0 trivially satisfiable (choose W=0, E=0, u=1). Inductive step: if U_i satisfiable and u_i satisfiable (by F correctness), folding yields U_{i+1} satisfiable via Theorem 2. Hence final U_n satisfiable.

*Knowledge Soundness:* From multifolding forking lemma. For 2-instance folding, 3 transcripts with distinct r yield linear system solvable for W1,W2; extractor rewinds prover. HyperNova requires (d+1)-special soundness for degree-d CCS [3].

> Theorem 5 (IVC Knowledge Soundness): Assuming discrete-log hardness in random-oracle model and binding of commitment, Nova IVC is knowledge-sound: any PPT prover producing accepting IVC proof for F^n(z0)=z_n implies extractor obtains intermediate z_i witnesses.

### 5.2 Prover Complexity Benchmarks (Analytic)

Synthetic benchmarks from literature [1][3][6]:

- **Nova** 2^20 constraints, Pasta curves, Apple M1: prover ~1.2s per step (MSM 2^20 ~ 0.9s), verifier ~5ms, proof 9KB (compressed via Spartan)
- **SuperNova** 8 instruction types, avg size 2^18: step cost avg 0.31s vs universal circuit 2.1s (6.7x win) [2]
- **HyperNova** Plonkish circuit with degree-4 gates, 2^20 vars: prover 1 MSM 0.85s, field ops 0.12s, vs Plonk prover 3.4s (4x win), no FFTs
- **Halo 2** (pre-folding): recursion overhead ~2^17 constraints per verifier, vs Nova folding verifier ~12k constraints (14x smaller)

*Note:* Numbers are approximate cross-paper syntheses; exact hardware varies. Trend is robust: folding reduces recursion overhead by order magnitude.

### 5.3 Recursive Composition Limits

Recursion depth n impacts proof size *only* via final compression, not per-step. IVC proof U_n grows O(1) (commitment + W). Spartan compression to SNARK yields O(log n) proof size.

**Practical limit:** Witness W_n grows linearly in n if retained for extraction; streaming prover discards intermediate W_i after folding, keeping only W_{folded} of size O(n_{vars}), not O(n · n_{vars}).

For **PCD** from multifolding [8], folding k leaves simultaneously requires log k sum-check rounds, verifier O(log k) field ops, prover O(k n) but with k-way parallel MSMs.

```tla
---------------- MODULE NovaIVC ----------------
VARIABLES U, z, i

Init == U = TrivialInstance /\ z = z0 /\ i = 0

Next == \E u, w \in Circuit(F, z):
          /\ u.satisfiable /\ w witnesses u
          /\ U' = NIFS_Fold(U, u)
          /\ z' = F(z)
          /\ i' = i + 1

Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<U,z,i>> /\ WF_<<U,z,i>>(Next)

THEOREM Soundness == Spec => [] (i = n => VerifierAccepts(U))
-------------------------------------------------------------
```

*TLA+ spec: IVC liveness and soundness invariant.*

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**1. R1CS Expressiveness:** Nova/SuperNova limited to degree-2 constraints. HyperNova solves via CCS but requires new tooling; Plonkish custom gates not yet fully folded in production nova-snark.

**2. Lookup Incompatibility:** Origami [2] and nlookup [3] provide folding-friendly lookups, but bitwise operations (e.g., Keccak chi) still expensive: O(m log n) constraints where m lookups. Jolt [6] uses Lasso + sum-check, but integration with folding remains active research.

**3. Curve Cycle Security:** Cycles like Pallas/Vesta have ~128-bit security but are newer than BLS12-381. Revisiting Nova on cycle of curves [1] proves security under discrete-log, but no pairing-friendly cycle achieves 128-bit with efficient verifier; BN254/Grumpkin is ~100-bit.

**4. Zero-Knowledge Composability:** HyperNova's free ZK relies on randomizing fold; if prover reuses randomness across folds, witness leaks. Requires careful Fiat-Shamir domain separation and fresh r sampling.

**5. Non-Uniform Witness Size Blowup:** SuperNova's k accumulators grow O(k · n_{vars}) storage. KiloNova [2] mitigates via circuit aggregation but adds O(log k) verifier overhead.

**6. Post-Quantum:** All folding schemes rely on discrete-log / Pedersen commitments. Lattice-based folding is open; no known post-quantum folding with MSM dominance.

*Open directions:*

- NeutronNova [3] generalizes to zero-check relations (any RoK to zero-check), enabling folding of Lasso lookups and grand products directly.
- ProtoStar [3] generic accumulation for (2k-1)-move special-sound protocols, verifier k+2 EC muls.
- Proof-carrying data from multifolding [8] enabling DAG recursion, not just linear chain.

## 7 Conclusion

Folding schemes represent a paradigm shift in verifiable computation: replacing recursive SNARK verification with lightweight folding of relaxed instances. **Nova** [1] demonstrated that a single MSM per step suffices for uniform IVC, **SuperNova** [2] extended this to non-uniform programs achieving *a la carte* cost, and **HyperNova** [3] lifted the construction to CCS [7] with optimal prover cost and free zero-knowledge.

The lineage from Halo's nested amortization [4][5] to Nova's folding reduces recursion overhead from thousands of pairing checks to a few thousand field constraints, enabling practical zkVMs (e.g., RISC-V via Jolt-inspired lookups), scalable rollups, and auditable distributed systems without trusted setup.

Future work lies in post-quantum folding, folding-friendly lookup synthesis, and production hardening of CycleFold over Pasta and Grumpkin cycles. As folding matures, we anticipate IVC becoming the default abstraction for verifiable stateful machines, much as R1CS became the default for static circuits.

The folding verifier's simplicity — *one random linear combination* — belies its power: compressing unbounded computation into a single satisfiable instance.
