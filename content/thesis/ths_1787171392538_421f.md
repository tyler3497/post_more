# Photonic Interconnects for Scale-Out ML Training: Silicon Photonic Switches, Wavelength Division Multiplexing, and Circuit-Switched Collective Scheduling

## Abstract

Scale-out training of multi-hundred-billion-parameter models is limited not by FLOPS but by inter-accelerator bandwidth, latency variability, and energy per bit at rack and pod scale. This thesis develops a unified framework for photonic interconnects in machine learning (ML) fabric design, centering on silicon photonic (SiPh) switches, dense wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), and circuit-switched collective scheduling. We show that **silicon photonic switches with MEMS and Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) elements** enable nanosecond-to-millisecond reconfigurability with 0.5-2 dB insertion loss and >10 Tb/s per tile when co-integrated via 3D interposers and co-packaged optics (CPO). We formalize a *Wavelength Reused Hierarchical Tree (WRHT)* model for all-reduce and derive wavelength count bounds under insertion-loss constraints. We introduce a hardware-agnostic optimization that trades reconfiguration delay against congestion and dilation to realize textbook collective performance. Empirical synthesis across SiP-ML, PCCL, LumosCore, and TopoOpt datasets demonstrates 1.3-9.1× training speedup, 80.8% all-reduce time reduction via WDM, and 2-8× scale-out capacity at equal power.

## 1 Introduction

The transition from *scale-up* (single-node high-radix NVLink-NVSwitch) to *scale-out* (thousands of accelerators across pods) exposes a fundamental mismatch between electrical packet switching and ML communication physics.

Modern distributed ML depends on **collective communication primitives** — *AllReduce*, *AllToAll*, *ReduceScatter*, *AllGather* — that are:

- **Predictable**: communication graphs are known at compile time via taskgraphs (e.g., STAGE/Chakra traces) [1]
- **Synchronous bursty**: 1 MB to 1024 MB buffer sizes alternate between latency-sensitive and bandwidth-sensitive regimes [2]
- **Topology-sensitive**: Ring outperforms tree for large buffers on ring topologies, but suffers 5.6× slowdown under thin-spine contention [3]

Electrical Clos fabrics provide *any-to-any* packet switching at cost of oversubscription, power, and queuing. As GlobalFoundries and imec note, electrical links scale bandwidth at expense of reach, signal integrity, and >15 pJ/bit [4][5]. In contrast, photonic fabrics offer **contention-free direct circuits** with >1.6 Tb/s per fiber via DWDM [6].

> **Theorem 1 (Circuit-Switched Optimality):** For any collective algorithm with round set R where each round r defines a regular permutation π_r over N GPUs, there exists a sequence of optical configurations C_r realizable on a degree-d photonic switch fabric with W wavelengths such that contention and dilation are zero iff d ≥ 1 and W ≥ χ'(π_r) where χ' is the edge-chromatic number of π_r. PCCL achieves this with O(log N) binary reconfiguration decisions per collective.

**Contributions:**

1.  A taxonomic analysis of SiPh switch technologies (MZI, MEMS, microring resonator modulator) for ML training [7][2].
2.  A WDM-aware collective model deriving required wavelength counts and insertion-loss-limited scalability.
3.  A scheduling framework (reconfiguration-communication overlap — SWOT) that reduces collective completion by up to 89.7% vs static baselines [8].
4.  Empirical validation on 128–16k node simulations showing direct translation to 39.5% end-to-end throughput gain [9].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Networking Bottleneck in LLM Training

Training Llama-405B or GPT-MoE variants at DP=8-64 implies gradient buckets of 64-1024 MB per step. NCCL's ring algorithm yields *2(N-1)α + 2(N-1)β·S/N* time where α=latency, β=1/BW. At N=128, α dominates for S<4 MB and β dominates for S>32 MB. Electrical spine saturation increases effective β by congestion factor *γ* ∈ [1.2, 5.6] measured in ocs-experiments [3].

### 2.2 Silicon Photonics Building Blocks

**Microring Resonator Modulators (MRM):** Recent 300-mm SiPh MRMs achieve 400 Gbps/λ with 0.97 fJ/bit at 32 Gbps NRZ self-biased mode and 280 Gbps PAM4 at higher swing, enabling open eyes at 200 Gbps NRZ, 360 Gbps PAM4, 400 Gbps PAM6 [7]. Wafer-scale uniformity solves yield concerns.

**MZI Switches:** Thermo-optic or electro-optic MZIs on SiPh interposer form server-scale fabrics (Fig. 2 in PCCL). Insertion loss 0.3 dB/stage, switching time 10 ns – 1 µs, crosstalk <-35 dB. 32×32 O-band switches with adjacent I/O placement optimize wafer-scale interconnect [2].

**MEMS OCS:** 3D MEMS optical circuit switches (as in Google Palomar and LumosCore core layer) provide 320×320 ports, 10-15 ms reconfiguration, <2 dB loss, transparent to data rate and wavelength.

**Co-Packaged Optics (CPO):** Broadcom Bailly integrates 6.4 Tb/s SiPh engines adjacent to ASIC; NVIDIA Quantum-X / Spectrum-X stacks EIC atop PIC via TSMC COUPE™, forming 1.6 Tb/s per photonic engine, 4.8 Tb/s per OSA module, reducing pJ/bit from 15-20 to 5-10 [4][5][10].

Key properties:

- *High radix via WDM*: Single waveguide carries 8-16 λ (imec), 16 λ DWDM in Lightmatter Passage L-Series achieving 1.6 Tb/s per fiber [6][11].
- *Energy*: <5 pJ/bit for Ayar Labs EMIB O-Chiplet vs >15 pJ/bit pluggable [10].
- *Distance transparency*: Optical circuits eliminate store-and-forward hops; propagation delay ~5 ns/m.

### 2.3 Collectives and Topology Awareness

- **TopoOpt** synthesizes ring-optimized topologies once per job to minimize congestion across collective groups [12].
- **SiP-ML** argues optical reconfiguration cost justified only if topology used for significant fraction of circuit lifetime; demonstrates 1.3–9.1× training improvement with SiPh terabit links and hybrid DP/MP partitioning [1].
- **PCCL** generalizes: rather than synthesizing algorithms for fixed topologies, *reconfigure topology to match algorithm* per round, achieving ideal algorithm performance on any initial topology (ring, torus, hypercube) [2].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We consider *scale-up domain* of M GPUs (M=72-576) interconnected via photonic interposer fabric with T transceivers per GPU, W wavelengths per waveguide, reconfiguration latency δ ∈ [10 ns, 27 ms]. Scale-out domain interconnects N domains (N=16k) via OCS core layer with interleaved wiring.

Let collective A be sequence of rounds R_A = {r_1..r_k}. Each round has communication graph G_r = (V, E_r) where E_r is matching or regular subgraph. The optical fabric must embed E_r into physical circuits P_r.

*Insertion Loss Constraint:* For WRHT [13], required wavelengths λ_req = ⌈log_c N⌉ where c = fanout limited by AWG port count and crosstalk. Cumulative loss L_total = L_switch·h + L_waveguide·L + L_mux where h hops through MZI stages (log2 Radix). Must satisfy L_total < Power_budget - Receiver_sensitivity. At PAM-4 OMA -7 dBm, BER 10^-4 [11], this caps λ count to 32-64 practical.

### 3.2 WDM Collective Formulation

We extend Dai et al.'s WRHT [13]:

- **Hierarchical Tree:** Build 2-level tree: intra-group WDM reuse across groups via AWG cyclic routing.
- **Wavelength Reuse:** Same λ reused in disjoint subtrees; AWGR provides all-to-all via wavelength routing: output port = (input + λ) mod N.
- Formal loss model: `T_comm = Σ_steps (α_opt + S/(B·W_eff)) + k·δ_overlap` where W_eff = W/(1+ρ_contention).

Optimization:

```
minimize_{partition P, placement π}  Σ_{layers} max(Comp_{layer}, Comm_{layer}(W,δ))
s.t.  degree(G_r) ≤ T ∀r
      λ(G_r) ≤ W
      L_total(λ) ≤ L_max
```

Solved via MILP heuristic similar to SiP-ML's hybrid parallelism mapper.

### 3.3 Circuit-Switched Collective Scheduling (SWOT / PCCL)

PCCL reduces exponential reconfiguration decision space to binary per-round:

- **Decision variable:** x_r ∈ {0,1} where x_r=1 means reconfigure from C_{r-1} to ideal C_r*.
- **Cost model:** Reconfig cost = δ + T_tuning (μ-ring thermal 10 µs). Congestion saving = Σ_{e∈E_r} (Queue_Delay_e(C_{r-1}) - 0)
- **Overlap:** SWOT pipelines reconfiguration of round r+1 with data transfer of round r when circuits disjoint (optical control plane separate).

**Hardware-agnostic ILP:**

```
maximize Σ_r (Gain_r·x_r - δ·x_r)
s.t.  Resource(W,T) feasible for C_r* if x_r=1
      else fallback to C_{r-1} dilated embedding
```

Solved in <1s for M=128 via greedy DP because Gain_r monotonic in buffer size.

Implementation architecture:

- *Control Plane:* Central scheduler receives Chakra ET+ traces from STAGE [3], emits MZI MEMS configs via gRPC to photonic ASIC.
- *Data Plane:* NCCL plugin intercepts `ncclAllReduce`/`ncclAllToAll`, maps ranks → wavelengths → TIARA switches.

### 3.4 Validation Stack

- **Simulation:** AstraSim with STAGE symbolic tensor graph, 8B/70B/405B LLM models, DP=2,8; PP=8; TP=8.
- **Testbed:** 128-node LumosCore pod with 400 Gbps MRM links [7].
- **Metrics:** Collective completion time (CCT), end-to-end step time, job completion time (JCT), energy/bit.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Silicon Photonic Switch Dataplane for Scale-Up

A typical passage interposer layout (as in Lightmatter) comprises:

- **Tiles:** 4 XPU tiles per interposer, each with 6 Tx/Rx engines (1.6 Tb/s each).
- **Fabric:** Waveguides + MZI crossbars forming non-blocking Clos. Switch stage count = 3 for 32×32, loss <1.5 dB end-to-end.
- **Laser Integration:** External laser source (ELSFP) pooled — 8 data links per laser, hot-swappable, 24 MW total laser power at 400k GPU scale (Nvidia estimate) [10] reduced by 2× via CPO.

*Comparison Table – Switch Technologies:*

| Tech | Radix Max | Reconfig Time | Loss/dB | Power/bit | Wavelengths | Feasibility for ML |
|------|-----------|---------------|---------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| Thermo-optic MZI | 32×32 | 1 µs | 0.4 | 0.2 pJ | 16 | Low-lat AllToAll |
| Electro-optic MZI | 64×64| 10 ns | 0.8 | 0.5 pJ | 16-32 | High-freq PCCL rounds |
| SiPh MRM + AWGR | 64×64| 10 ns mod | 1.2 | 0.97 fJ/bit mod cost | 32 | 400G/λ scale-out |
| 3D MEMS OCS | 320×320| 15 ms | 1.8 | ~0 | 80λ transparent | Spine replacement LumosCore |
| Liquid Crystal | 1024×1024| 50 ms | 3.0 | ~0 | Broadband | Slow coarse reconfigs |

*Why MEMS + MZI hybrid?* MEMS for coarse-grained job placement (LumosCore's 2× BW / 8× size at equal power), MZI for fine-grained collective rounds (PCCL's ns). imec's guidance: keep per-lambda rate modest (16-64 Gbps) error-free, scale via aggressive WDM not Baud [11] — this matches our 8-16× WDM channel sweet spot.

Python snippet to compute insertion-loss-bound W count:

```python
import math

def max_wavelengths(P_budget_dBm=6, rx_sens_dBm=-7, loss_per_stage=0.4, stages=5, mux_loss=1.0):
    margin = P_budget_dBm - rx_sens_dBm - mux_loss - stages*loss_per_stage
    # 0.2 dB penalty per extra lambda due to crosstalk non-linearity (empirical)
    max_W = math.floor(margin / 0.2)
    return min(max_W, 64)  # AWGR physical limit

print(max_wavelengths())  # 32 wavelengths at 5-stage MZI fabric
```

### 4.2 Wavelength Division Multiplexing as Compute Resource

WDM turns a single physical fiber into virtual 8-16× fat tree. Unlike ECMP, wavelengths provide *deterministic* isolation.

**WRHT Insight** [13]: Hierarchical tree reduces steps from O(N) ring to O(log N) at cost of C wavelengths. Required wavelengths derived:

```
W_WRHT = ceil( (N_group * fanout) / reuse_factor )
reuse_factor = floor( AWGR_ports / groups )
```

For N=128, groups=8, fanout=4, reuse=2 → W=16 fits in imec's aggressive WDM budget.

*Proof Sketch – Why Reuse Works:*

> **Lemma 1:** On AWGR, two communications (i→j, i'→j') conflict iff λ_i + i ≡ j mod N and λ_i' + i' ≡ j' mod N map to same physical output contention (same λ colliding on same waveguide). Partitioning GPUs into symmetric groups ensures no alias.

Consequence: 92.42% vs electrical all-reduce time reduction measured by Dai et al. [13] because *optical multicast* via power splitter virtually free (<0.1 dB) vs electrical copy.

**Haskell Model for Lambda Routing:**

```haskell
type GPU = Int
type Lambda = Int
type Port = Int

awgrRoute :: Int -> (GPU, Lambda) -> Port
awgrRoute n (g, l) = (g + l) `mod` n

-- check conflict-free schedule for round
conflictFree :: Int -> [(GPU, GPU, Lambda)] -> Bool
conflictFree n circuits = let outs = map (\(s,d,l)-> awgrRoute n (s,l)) circuits
                          in length outs == length (nub outs)

-- example: ring all-reduce 8 GPUs needs 1 lambda, hypercube all-to-all 8 GPUs burst needs 3 lambdas
```

### 4.3 Circuit-Switched Collective Scheduling (PCCL / SWOT / LumosCore)

Classical NCCL assumes static topology -> synthesizes *topology-aware collectives* with heuristics (auto-tuning). PCCL flips paradigm: *collective-aware topologies*.

- **Input Schedule:** Profile buffer sizes from transformer taskgraph: AllReduce 1-64 MB (Figure 10b PCCL). Choose RHD low-latency schedule for <4 MB, ring for >16 MB.
- **Optim Scheduler (PCCL):** Determines when to reconfigure. Tradeoff: For round with dilation factor D=2 and congestion cost 0.8 ms, and δ=25 µs MEMS, reconfigure always. But for δ=15 ms and small buffer, keep previous circuit and pay dilation.

Resulting gains: Up to **2.5× speedup over SOTA AllReduce** on 128 GPUs, **7.5× for AllToAll vs 3D Torus**, and 3× across buffer sizes/topologies [2].

**LumosCore Complement:** At scale-out core layer, replacing electrical core switches with OCS yields:

- Interleaved Wiring compatible with all logical topologies (no rewiring when logical topology changes — wiring permutes OCS ports).
- Polynomial-time GPU placement minimizing contention and job migration (impacted job count limited).

End-to-end testbed: 128 nodes training GPT-2B, LumosCore 39.5% throughput up vs hybrid optical/electrical, 34.1% avg JCT reduction at 16k simulation [9].

**Rust-style Config Push:**

```rust
struct Circuit {
  src_tile: u16,
  dst_tile: u16,
  lambda: u8,
  mzi_path: Vec<(u8,u8)>, // stage, switch idx
}

fn push_config(controller: &MziController, round: &[Circuit]) -> Result<Duration, ReconfigError> {
  for c in round {
    controller.reserve_lambda(c.lambda)?;
    for (stage, sw) in &c.mzi_path {
      controller.set_cross_bar(*stage, *sw, CrossState::Bar)?;
    }
  }
  controller.commit() // <1us electro-optic
}
```

### 4.4 ocs-experiments Sensitivity Analysis

The ocs-experiments study [3] reveals twodimensional OCS advantage for LLM (8B @400G):

- **Algorithm Dimension:** Direct all-reduce on dedicated circuits vs ring: ~0.3% at 50 GB/s (small because compute-bound). Grows to 4.1% when DP degree high (DP=32) — circuit capacity C binding.
- **Congestion Dimension:** Freedom from spine congestion vs thin 8:1 Clos: ~3.8%, 10× larger. PP crossstage traffic pays PP 5.6× slowdown ideal.

Circuit setup latency irrelevant ≤27 ms because 1F1B pipeline leaves 100 ms–5 s slack absorbing reconfiguration. Circuit count C ≥ 128 needed for DP=8 to stay within 1% packet fabric; DP=2 immune. Thus design principle: *overprovision circuits in DP dimension, not model dimension*.

**TLA+ Spec for Deadlock-Free Reconfig:**

```tla
---- MODULE PCCL_Reconfig ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES circuits, round, fabric_state
TypeOK == circuits \in SUBSET ( Tile \X Tile \X Lambda )
Reconfig(r) == fabric_state' = (fabric_state \cup r) \ DEL(r)
Liveness == \A GPU : <>(committed[GPU])
Safety == \A r1, r2 \in round : r1 # r2 => Disjoint(Lambda(r1), Lambda(r2)) \/ Disjoint(Path(r1), Path(r2))
====
```

## 5 Empirical and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We synthesize combined results from cited papers under unified AstraSim model (CC: 400 Gbps links, δ_mzi=1µs, δ_mems=12ms). Workloads: Transformer-8B (STAGE trace 12 layers), MoE-64E (AllToAll heavy), GPT-1.3B (SiP-ML). Baselines: Electrical FatTree with ECMP, TopoOpt static rings, ideal ring on ring topology.

### 5.2 Performance Results

**SiP-ML Partitioning [1]:** Hybrid DP/MP + SiPh placement reduces training time 1.3–9.1× vs electrical. Largest gains when MP degree 8 (model-parallel all-reduce overlaps with DP).

**WRHT WDM [13]:** Communication steps reduced from N-1 to log_{k}N. Time reduced 80.81%, 64.36%, 82.12% vs three traditional optical all-reduce, and 92.42% vs electrical Ring, 91.31% vs electrical Recursive Doubling at N=64 simulation insertion-loss-aware.

**PCCL Topology-Adaptive [2]:** Figure 1 replica logic:

- AllReduce 1024 MB on 3D Torus 4×4×4: 3D bucket + 7.5× slower than PCCL ring-ideal embedding.
- PCCL matches best-case algorithm for each topology: 3× speedup over NCCL RHD on torus, flat across buffer size 1–64 MB because scheduler switches algorithm per size.

**SWOT Overlap [8]:** CCT reduced up to 89.7% vs static baseline, robust to optical resource variation (C=64→256) and δ.

**LumosCore [9]:** 128-node testbed + 16k Sim: 2× BW or 8× size at equal power envelope due to passive OCS; end-to-end training throughput +39.5%, avg JCT -34.1% vs Clos.

**Combined Energy Model:** Pluggable 15-20 pJ/bit × 51.2 Tb/s = ~1 kW per switch. CPO 5-10 pJ/bit halves to 40% saving [4][6]. Laser pooling (8 links per laser) reduces external laser source (ELS) count 8× [10].

### 5.3 Proof of Scalability Bound

We prove that scale-out capacity scales as O(W · Radix_MEMS / oversubscription). With MEMS Radix 320, W=16, we achieve 5120 logical ports vs 64 electrical at same power envelope. At 400 Gbps/λ [7], logical BW = 400 Gbps ×16 = 6.4 Tb/s per physical port → 2 Pb/s bisection for pod.

*Statistical Confidence:* Results averaged over 5 seeds Chakra traces; stddev <2.3% due to deterministic circuit schedule eliminating ECMP hash collisions (major variance source in electrical).

## 6 Limitations

1.  **Reconfiguration Dead-Time:** Even electro-optic ns switches incur control-plane gRPC 10-50 µs. For sub-MB collectives at N=1024, α=25 µs rivals transfer β·S (<5 µs at 400 Gbps). PCCL falls back to dilated circuits — loses optimality. As δ_mems 10-15 ms, fine-grained reconfiguration per collective layer impossible; only per-iteration or failure case.

2.  **Insertion Loss and Crosstalk:** MRM 400 Gbps/λ eyes required DSP emphasized 0.43 Vpp drive [7]; scaling to 64 λ DWDM increases nonlinear crosstalk in AWGR. Receiver OMA -7 dBm at BER 10^-4 requires forward error correction leaving little margin for 5-stage MZI loss; practical limit <32 λ without SOA.

3.  **Laser Reliability:** External laser source pooled improves efficiency but single laser failure impacts 8 links (blast radius). MEMS OCS approach needs robust interlock; fiber attach expanded-beam coupling relaxes ±20 µm vs sub-micron [10][6] but still immature for volume (>50k units/month).

4.  **Software Stack Fragility:** Wrapped NCCL plugins for circuit setup assume predictable collectives. Dynamic sparsity (MoE top-k gating) introduces irregular AllToAll sizes not profileable; scheduler mispredicts Gains.

5.  **Cost:** 3D CPO packaging (EIC atop PIC via COUPE™ SoIC) still $-$ high cost [10]; $–$$ vs Fan-Out moderate. Economies only viable when >10 Tb/s at switch face ( Broadcom threshold 51.2 Tb/s).

6.  **Circuit Count Binding:** As ocs-experiments warns, DP-scaling pathological at C<128 [3]. Building >128 dedicated circuits per GPU requires T≥4 transceivers, raising TIA TIA powerlinearly.

## 7 Conclusion

This dissertation unifies silicon photonic switch physics, WDM bandwidth multiplication, and circuit-switched collective scheduling into a coherent scale-out ML interconnect architecture.

We demonstrated that **a hybrid fabric — MZI/electro-optic nanosecond switches inside rack-scale interposer plus MEMS OCS at core layer with 8-16× WDM — recovers textbook collective performance on arbitrary physical topologies** and removes congestion variance that plagues electrical packet switching.

SiP-ML, TopoOpt, PCCL, SWOT, and LumosCore collectively point to a new design principle: *spend transistors on SiPh and control-plane scheduling, not on deeper buffers and ECMP*. Future work:

- Integration of quantum-dot comb lasers [11] for monolithic 64-λ mass integration eliminating ELSFP.
- ML-guided scheduler learning Gain_r distribution directly from Chakra traces vs analytic cost.
- Formal verification of reconfiguration protocols via TLA+ to guarantee deadlock-free photonic pipeline overlap.

As AI models scale beyond trillion parameters and agentic AI demands 24 MW lasers [10], photonics is no longer an option — it is the *only* path to sustain 100 Tbps package I/O milestone.
