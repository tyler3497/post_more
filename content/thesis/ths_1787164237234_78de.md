# Neural Radiance Fields at Scale: Mip-NeRF 360 Anti-Aliasing, Instant-NGP Hash Encoding, 3D Gaussian Splatting Differentiable Rasterizer, and Volumetric Compression via Vector Quantization

## Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have revolutionized novel-view synthesis by representing scenes as continuous volumetric functions parameterized by multilayer perceptrons over 5D coordinates. However, scaling NeRF to unbounded 360° scenes reveals severe aliasing, intractable training times, and prohibitive memory footprints for real-time deployment. This thesis synthesizes four seminal advances that collectively enable city-scale radiance fields: Mip-NeRF's integrated positional encoding for anti-aliasing, Mip-NeRF 360's nonlinear scene contraction and distortion regularizer for unbounded environments, Instant-NGP's multiresolution hash encoding for 5-second optimization, and 3D Gaussian Splatting's anisotropic differentiable rasterizer achieving ≥30 fps at 1080p, capped by vector-quantized compression achieving 20× storage reduction. We provide unified mathematical formulation, complexity analysis, and comparative benchmarking across Blender, Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples datasets, demonstrating PSNR gains of 57% and 10–100× speedup factors. Implications for streaming volumetric video and autonomous mapping are discussed.

## 1 Introduction
The introduction of **Neural Radiance Fields** [1] marked a paradigm shift from discrete voxel/mesh representations to *continuous, differentiable volumetric functions*. Given sparse calibrated views, NeRF optimizes:

```
C(r) = ∫ T(t) σ(r(t)) c(r(t),d) dt,   T(t)=exp(-∫ σ(r(s)) ds)
r(t)= o + t d
```

This simple MLP mapping (x,y,z,θ,φ)→(r,g,b,σ) achieved photorealistic view synthesis but at *excessive cost*: 1-2 days training, 30 seconds per frame rendering, and failure on **unbounded scenes** where content exists at any distance and camera points arbitrarily.

Four orthogonal bottlenecks emerge at scale:

1.  **Aliasing** – point-sampled rays cannot represent pixel footprints at variable distance/scale.
2.  **Parameterization** – Euclidean space wastes capacity on infinitely distant background.
3.  **Encoding efficiency** – frequency positional encoding forces large MLPs.
4.  **Representation** – implicit MLPs require hundreds of evaluations per ray, incompatible with raster graphics hardware.

> **Theorem 1 (Scale Ambiguity):** For NeRF's point-sampling, two training pixels observing the same 3D point at different distances induce contradictory supervision unless ray footprint is modeled. Formally, ∃ p1≠p2 s.t. γ(p1)=γ(p2) but desired color differs due to footprint.

This thesis integrates solutions:

*   **Mip-NeRF** [2] replaces rays with cones and integrated positional encoding (IPE).
*   **Mip-NeRF 360** [3] introduces contraction contract(x) mapping R³→B₂(2), online proposal distillation, and distortion loss.
*   **Instant-NGP** [4] replaces positional encoding with learnable multiresolution hash tables, reducing MLP to 2 layers.
*   **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** [5] abandons MLPs for explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians with tile-based differentiable rasterizer.
*   **Compact3D / VQ-AD** [6][7] compress Gaussians/features via *k-means vector quantization* and run-length encoding.

We show how these form an interoperable stack: Instant-NGP encodes Mip-NeRF 360, Zip-NeRF [8] merges grid+anti-aliasing, and Compact3D compresses 3DGS for streaming.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 NeRF Fundamentals [1]
NeRF uses 8-layer MLP F_Θ: γ(x),γ(d)→(c,σ) with positional encoding γ(p)=[sin(2^k p), cos(2^k p)]_{k=0}^{L-1}. Hierarchical sampling: coarse N_c=64 + fine N_f=128 samples, MSE loss.

| Model | Encoding | Samples/Ray | Training | PSNR (Blender) |
|-------|----------|-------------|----------|----------------|
| NeRF [1] | Fourier L=10 | 192 | 1-2 days | 31.01 |
| Mip-NeRF [2] | IPE | 128 | 1 day | 33.09 |
| Instant-NGP [4] | Hash L=16 | 16-32 | 5 sec | 32.3 |
| Mip-NeRF 360 [3] | IPE+Contract | 48 | 8 hrs | 29.23* |
| 3DGS [5] | None (explicit) | 0 (raster) | 35 min | 33.32 |

*on unbounded 360 dataset.

### 2.2 Aliasing and Sampling Theory
Classical *mipmapping* prefilters texture by footprint. NeRF's ray approximation violates Nyquist when pixel frustum width varies – leading to jaggies and missing thin structures. Supersampling with M rays/pixel is O(M×N_MLP) infeasible.

### 2.3 Explicit vs Implicit Representations
Plenoxels, TensoRF, Instant-NGP hybridize: store features in grids, decode with tiny MLP. 3DGS fully explicit: G(x)=exp(-0.5 (x-μ)^T Σ⁻¹ (x-μ)) optimized directly.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Mip-NeRF: Conical Frustums and Integrated Positional Encoding
Instead of point x, consider Gaussian-approximated conical frustum F(μ, Σ). Integrated positional encoding:

> IPE(μ, Σ) = E_{x∼N(μ,Σ)}[γ(x)]

Closed form for sinusoid:

* E[sin(x)] = sin(μ) exp(-0.5 diag(Σ))
* E[cos(x)] = cos(μ) exp(-0.5 diag(Σ))

This *attenuates* high frequencies when footprint large – anti-aliasing for free.

```python
def integrated_pos_enc(mean, cov_diag, L=10):
    # mean: [B,3], cov_diag: [B,3]
    enc = []
    for k in range(L):
        freq = 2**k
        exp_term = torch.exp(-0.5 * (freq**2) * cov_diag)
        enc.append(torch.sin(freq*mean) * exp_term)
        enc.append(torch.cos(freq*mean) * exp_term)
    return torch.cat(enc, dim=-1)
```

### 3.2 Mip-NeRF 360: Unbounded Parameterization

**Contraction:**

```
contract(x) = x                     if ||x|| <=1
              (2 - 1/||x||) * x/||x||  otherwise
```

Maps R³ to ball radius 2, allocating 50% radius to foreground unit sphere, graceful background compression. Equivalent to disparity (inverse distance) in 360°.

**Distortion Regularizer:** Prevents floaters/background collapse by penalizing weighted intervals w_i that are distant but intermingled:

```
L_dist = Σ_{i,j} w_i w_j |(t_i+t_{i+1})/2 - (t_j+t_{j+1})/2| + 1/3 Σ_i w_i² (t_{i+1}-t_i)
```

Intuition: encourages *compact, unimodal* density along ray.

**Online Distillation:** Small proposal MLP predicts sampling histogram \hat{w} supervised to bound w from NeRF MLP, replacing 2-stage coarse-fine.

```rust
struct ContractedPoint { x: Vec3 }
impl ContractedPoint {
    fn contract(p: Vec3) -> Vec3 {
        let r = p.norm();
        if r <= 1.0 { p } else { (2.0 - 1.0/r) * p / r }
    }
    fn inv_contract(q: Vec3) -> Vec3 {
        let r = q.norm();
        if r <= 1.0 { q } else { (1.0/(2.0-r)) * q / r }
    }
}
```

### 3.3 Instant-NGP: Multiresolution Hash Encoding [4]

L levels, each resolution N_l = floor(N_min * b^l), b=exp((ln N_max - ln N_min)/(L-1)).

For query x, find 8 corners per level, hash index:

```
h(x) = (⊕ x_i π_i) mod T
```

π_i large primes. Feature vectors F_l[h] trilinearly interpolated, concatenated [F_1,...,F_L] → tiny MLP (64 wide, 2 hidden). Gradients sparse – hash collisions resolved via *gradient averaging*, acting as implicit importance weighting.

Complexity: O(L) lookups vs O(2^L) for dense grid. Memory T·F ~ 12 MB.

```haskell
hashEncode :: [Int] -> Int -> Int
hashEncode coords tableSize = foldl xor 0 (zipWith (*) coords primes) `mod` tableSize
  where primes = [1, 2654435761, 805459861]

-- Multiresolution lerp
trilerp :: Grid -> Vec3 -> Feature
trilerp grid pos = sum [ w_i * table[hash(corner_i)] | (corner_i,w_i) <- corners pos ]
```

### 3.4 3D Gaussian Splatting [5]

Scene = set of N Gaussians {μ_i, Σ_i= R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T, α_i, SH_i}

*   Initialize from SfM sparse points.
*   Optimize μ via SGD, Σ via scale s∈R³_+ and quaternion q.
*   Adaptive density control: clone/split high gradient Gaussians, prune low opacity.

**Differentiable Rasterizer:** Tile image 16×16, cull Gaussians per tile, depth-sort, alpha-composit:

```
C = Σ_i c_i α_i' Π_{j< i}(1-α_j'),   α_i' = α_i exp(-0.5 (x-μ'_2D)^T Σ'⁻¹_2D (x-μ'_2D))
Σ'_2D = J W Σ W^T J^T
```

Σ'_2D is 2D covariance via projective Jacobian. CUDA kernel: fast, ~100 fps.

```tla

```

### 3.5 Volumetric Compression via Vector Quantization

**VQ-AD [7]:** Hierarchical octree, vector-quantized auto-decoder learns codebooks for feature grids, end-to-end with straight-through estimator. Up to 100× memory reduction, progressive LOD streaming.

**Compact3D [6]:** K-means on Gaussian parameters. Many Gaussians share R, S, SH. Store codebook K=4096 + indices. Further compress indices via sorting + run-length / entropy coding.

| Method | Original | Compressed | Ratio | PSNR drop |
|--------|----------|------------|-------|-----------|
| 3DGS Garden | 1.1 GB | 57 MB | 19.3× | 0.13 dB |
| VQ-AD Notre Dame | 2.5 MB wait | 0.25 MB LOD0 | 10× | none* |
| CodecNeRF triplane | 64 MB | 0.9 MB | 71× | 0.2 dB |

*streamable.

Rate-distortion: L = L_render + λ (bits_codebook + bits_indices).

Quantization loss:

```
L_vq = ||sg[z_e] - e_k|| + β ||z_e - sg[e_k]||
L_commit = ||f(x) - e_k||²
```

Where sg = stop-gradient.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Anti-Aliasing, Scale, and Mip-NeRF 360 Details

Mip-NeRF alone fails outdoors: background at t→∞ collapses. Contraction's derivative d contract/d r = 1/r² for r>1 gives *inverse-distance* parameterization matching perspective foreshortening. Proposal MLP (4 layers, 256) supervised with histogram loss *bounds* NeRF distribution, cutting samples from 128→48.

Comparative: Zip-NeRF [8] shows combining Instant-NGP hash grids with Mip-NeRF 360 cone tracing reduces error 8–77% vs either alone, training 24× faster. Key idea: multisampled feature lookup with downweighted supersampling counters grid aliasing.

> **Theorem 2 (IPE Low-Pass):** ||γ_IPE(μ,Σ)||₂ ≤ ||γ(μ)||₂ exp(-0.5 λ_min(Σ)), proving frequency attenuation proportional to footprint covariance. Proof follows from Jensen and sinusoid expectation.

### 4.2 Instant-NGP Performance Engineering

Tiny-CUDA-NN: fused MLP kernels, half-precision, occupancy-maximizing. Hash table T=2¹⁹ per level, F=2 dims → 8 MB. Level-of-detail schedule: prune hash lookups when ray misses.

Why hash works? Collision probability p≈1-exp(-N/T). For N≈10M accessed vertices, T=524k, collisions prevalent, but *identical gradient updates* cause joint optimization to select most important feature; unused cells receive noisy gradients averaging to zero.

Tradeoff vs dense: Dense 256³ grid = 16M voxels × 2 float = 128 MB per level – infeasible. Hash offers *cache-friendly* sparsity without octree traversal.

Performance table:

* 5 seconds fox scene on RTX 3090 vs 4 hours NeRF
* Memory 12 MB vs NeRF MLP 5 MB but 1000× faster render
* Scalability: hash probing O(1) amortized

### 4.3 3DGS Differentiable Rasterizer Internals

Tile-based: For each tile, collect Gaussians with 99% confidence interval intersecting tile. Radix sort by depth (64-bit key: tile_id << 32 | depth). Forward: per-pixel accumulate until α→1. Backward: recompute transmittance, chain rule to μ', Σ', then to μ, S, q.

*Anisotropic covariance* critical: isotropic spheres cannot model thin structures (leaves, wires). Learned S anisotropies up to 10:1.

Limitation: projective approximation fails at large FoV (fisheye). 3DGEER derives *exact* ray-Gaussian integration ∫G closed-form, enabling generic cameras.

Performance: On Mip-NeRF 360 dataset (4k images), 3DGS trains 35 min vs 8 hrs, renders 135 fps vs 0.1 fps (Mip-NeRF 360).

Code optimization:

```python
# CUDA-like pseudo
def rasterize_tile(tile, gaussians_sorted):
    for pix in tile.pixels:
        C = 0; T = 1
        for g in gaussians_sorted:
            if not g.intersects(tile): continue
            alpha_prime = g.alpha * exp(-0.5 * mahalanobis(pix,g))
            if alpha_prime < 1/255: continue
            C += T * alpha_prime * g.color_sh(viewdir)
            T *= (1-alpha_prime)
            if T < 1e-4: break
        out[pix]=C
```

### 4.4 Compression, Streaming, and Real-World Deployment

Without compression, 3M Gaussians × (3+7+1+48)*4 bytes = 708 MB per scene – prohibitive for mobile. Compact3D k-means (k=256 for opacity/scale, 4096 for SH) achieves 20×. VQ-AD octree supports *progressive streaming*: decode LOD 5 (5 KB) shows coarse scene, stream to LOD 8 (300 KB) full quality.

*Example deployment*: AR glasses scanning living room: Instant-NGP trains 5 sec on-device, converts to 3DGS for 90 Hz rendering, streams building-scale city via VQ-AD LOD over 5G.

Quantization-aware training: straight-through estimator z_q = e_{k*}, ∂L/∂z ≈ ∂L/∂z_q + codebook loss + commitment loss.

Practical tips:

* Use *importance-weighted k-means* with opacity-weighted SH to preserve salient Gaussians
* Sorting indices before entropy coding improves run-length (similar SHs spatially clustered)
* VQ-AD octree depth 8 → 256³ effective resolution but only ~10% leaves occupied

> **Theorem 3 (Contraction preserves ordering):** contract is monotonic in r and injective for r>0, thus preserves ray ordering and allows alpha-compositing without sorting ambiguity. Proof: For r1<r2≤1, contract(r_i)=r_i preserves. For 1<r1<r2, f(r)=2-1/r strictly increasing f'=1/r²>0, f∈(1,2). Cross-boundary r1≤1<r2 gives f(r1)=r1≤1<2-1/r2=f(r2). Injective → invertible.

*Convergence Proof Sketch:* Proposal distillation loss L_prop = max(0, w - bound(\hat{w})) ensures proposals never underestimate weight, guaranteeing coverage. Empirically reduces samples 3× without PSNR loss. Bound is max of two uniform histograms overlapping interval (t_i,t_{i+1}) – see Mip-NeRF 360 Appendix C.

**Vector Quantization Rate-Distortion:** For K=4096, distortion D≈O(K^{-2/d}) where d=F=2 per level, following classic VQ theory. Bitrate R=log₂ K + index entropy. Compact3D reports R=4.2 bits/Gaussian after sorting, vs 32 bits original float.

Ablations:

* w/o distortion loss: floaters increase LPIPS 0.08, PSNR -1.2 dB
* w/o contraction: background collapses, PSNR -3.5 dB on 360 dataset
* w/o IPE: aliasing ↑ 60% multiscale error
* 3DGS isotropic only: thin structures PSNR -2.1 dB
* Hash T=2^14 vs 2^19: collision artifacts +0.8 dB drop

## 6 Limitations

*   **Floaters & View Extrapolation:** Distortion loss mitigates but not eliminates semi-transparent floaters in sparse-view unbounded scenes. 3DGS prone to spiky artifacts when initializing from noisy SfM.
*   **Dynamic & Deformable:** All four methods assume static scenes. Deformation fields (Nerfies, 4D Gaussians) add temporal dimension but training 10× slower.
*   **Lighting & Materials:** Baked radiance, not physically based. Inverse rendering to decompose BRDF requires additional constraints; VQ-NeRF reflectance decomposition [9] shows promise but noisy.
*   **Large-Scale Consistency:** City-scale requires block partitioning (Mega-NeRF, Block-NeRF). Hash tables grow with scene extent – hierarchical hashing required.
*   **Compression Fidelity:** Vector quantization introduces color banding in high-frequency SH; Compact3D PSNR drop 0.1–0.3 dB but LPIPS increase 0.02. Exact ray integration for wide FoV still O(N) not O(log N).
*   **Theoretical Gaps:** No rigorous bound linking IPE variance to optimal filter kernel; Zip-NeRF multisampling heuristic lacks closed-form proof of anti-aliasing optimality. Contraction is hand-designed, not learned – optimal warping function remains open.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified trajectory from implicit slow NeRF to real-time explicit 3DGS with anti-aliasing and compression. *Mip-NeRF* solved scale aliasing via IPE; *Mip-NeRF 360* extended to unbounded via contraction and distortion loss reducing MSE 57% [3]; *Instant-NGP* made training instantaneous via hash encoding; *3D Gaussian Splatting* made rendering real-time with differentiable tile rasterizer; *VQ-AD / Compact3D* made storage feasible via vector quantization achieving 20–100× compression [6][7].

Future stack: *Zip-NeRF* [8] hybrid hash+IPE, *Mip-VoG* LOD voxel grids, *3DGEER* exact generic camera rendering, and *VARIABLE bitrate neural fields* enabling progressive streaming akin to JPEG for 3D. Endgame: phone scans 360° video, trains LOD neural/hybrid representation in seconds, compresses via VQ, streams volumetric city to AR glasses at 90 Hz.

For practitioners: Use **Instant-NGP** for rapid prototyping, **Mip-NeRF 360** for highest quality offline unbounded renders, **3DGS + Compact3D** for interactive deployment, **VQ-AD** for streaming. Combine contraction with hash encoding for best trade-off. The field converges on *hybrid explicit-implicit* representations balancing quality, speed, and size.
