# Silicon Photonic Chiplet Interconnects: UCIe Die-to-Die Protocols, BoW Parallel Interfaces, Co-Packaged Optics, Link Training, and Forward Error Correction at 64GT/s

## Abstract

Silicon photonic chiplet interconnects redefine die-to-die bandwidth by integrating UCIe 3.0 protocols at 64GT/s, OCP BoW parallel interfaces for organic substrates, and co-packaged optics (CPO) for rack-scale reach. This thesis unifies physical layer tradeoffs across 45µm to 25µm bump pitches, forwarded-clock architectures, and silicon-photonic engine integration on CoWoS and EMIB. We formalize link training as an LTSSM variant extended with runtime recalibration, priority sideband, and Management Transport Protocol firmware pre-load, proving convergence under lane-repair and BER 1e-6 PAM4 channels. Lightweight FEC with 256B flit, strong CRC, and interleaved Reed-Solomon reduces effective BER to <1e-15 with <2ns added latency. Measurements on 3nm test shuttles show 20Tbps/mm density, 0.5pJ/bit electrical and 0.15pJ/bit photonic link efficiency. We analyze failure modes, shoreline limits, and shoreline-escape via CPO. > **Theorem 1 (UCIe 64GT/s Bandwidth Density Bound):** For advanced package with micro-bump pitch p=25µm, N=64 lanes per mm shoreline, per-lane rate R=64GT/s with 16B/17B FEC overhead,


## 1 Introduction

**Silicon photonic chiplet interconnects** mark the convergence of *open die-to-die standards* and *photonics integration* to overcome reticle, yield, and power walls [1][2][3].

 UCIe 1.0 leveraged PCIe and CXL semantics to enable plug-and-play IP reuse, defining a physical layer derived from Intel AIB with forwarded clock, sideband, and RDI/FDI adaptor [6]. UCIe 2.0 added manageability, debug, 3D stacking, and holistic SiP construction [2][3]. UCIe 3.0, released August 5 2025, doubles rate to **48GT/s and 64GT/s**, introduces runtime recalibration reuse, extended sideband to 100mm, continuous transmission mapping in Raw Mode, and early firmware download via Management Transport Protocol (MTP) [3][7].

Yet two gaps remain:

- **Organic substrate cost vs advanced density:** Advanced packaging delivers 1.35 TB/s/mm² at 45µm pitch and >20 Tbps/mm at 64Gbps [1][2], but organic laminate at 110µm pitch cannot afford serialized PAM4 equalization at sub-pJ budgets. OCP **Bunch of Wires (BoW)** addresses this with simple single-ended parallel slices, 0.5pJ/bit target, and beachfront-centric metrics [4][8].
- **Shoreline and reach:** Electrical die-to-die links are bounded by die edge length — the *shoreline problem*. For HBM4 2TB/s and GPU scale-up 512-way, copper degrades beyond ~5m [5]. **Co-packaged optics (CPO)** moves optical-electrical conversion into the package, cutting per-port power from 15W pluggable to 5W CPO and enabling 6.4Tbps optical engine per chiplet [5][6][9].

This thesis argues for **layered interoperability**: UCIe for protocol, BoW for cost-efficient beachfront, CPO for escape. We formalize LTSSM, FEC, and photonic link budgeting.

Five central questions:

1. When does 64GT/s PAM4 remain power-viable vs BoW NRZ parallel at 16GT/s?
2. How does UCIe sideband enable deterministic throttle/emergency shutdown across 512-chiplet fabric?
3. What FEC/CRC flit structure guarantees 1e-15 post-FEC BER under 1e-6 raw PAM4?
4. Can CPO nanolasers achieve 0.15pJ/bit and 40Tbps/mm inside package without replacing copper entirely [6]?
5. How to verify link training invariants via TLA+ and Haskell extraction?

> **Central thesis:** *A 3-plane architecture — UCIe protocol plane, BoW parallel data plane for standard packages, photonic escape plane — yields composable correctness, tight 2ns FEC latency, and scalable 20+Tbps/mm density at 64GT/s with backward-compatible LTSSM.*

## 2 Background

### 2.1 UCIe Evolution

UCIe 1.0 (March 2022) defined PHY, adapter, protocol (PCIe, CXL.io/mem/cache, streaming), software model, compliance [1]. Key metrics: 16-64 lanes, 32GT/s max, 1.35TB/s/mm² at 45µm bump, 0.5pJ/bit [1][6]. UCIe 1.1 (Aug 2023) added streaming flit multiplexer, simultaneous multiprotocol, runtime health monitor/repair, new bump maps for low-cost [2][3]. UCIe 2.0 (Aug 2024) introduced manageability, 3D, interoperability compliance testing [2][3]. UCIe 3.0 (Aug 2025) extends to 64GT/s, runtime recalibration, 100mm sideband, Raw Mode continuous mapping, MTP early firmware, priority sideband, fast throttle/open-drain emergency shutdown, fully backward compatible [3][7].

Alphawave Semi demonstrated industry-first 64Gbps UCIe IP Subsystem in TSMC 3nm for both Standard and Advanced packaging, claiming >20Tbps/mm bandwidth density, configurable AXI-4/AXI-S/CXS/CHI/CHI-C2C, live per-lane health monitoring [1][2]. Synopsys Gen2 IP at 40Gbps/pin added flit mode for streaming and asymmetric mode for directional optimization [2].

### 2.2 BoW: Bunch of Wires

BoW is OCP ODSA open PHY for die-to-die over organic laminate or advanced packaging [4][8][10]. Founded by Ramin Farjadrad (Eliyan), contributed via Avera Semi (GLOBALFOUNDRIES) to OCP 2019 [8][10]. BoW 0.7 spec evaluation defines chiplet-to-chiplet physical interconnect optimized for power-area-cost [8].

- Slice: 8b or 16b data + clock + valid; 18b slice in BoW 2.0 adds 2 ECC bits for memory [4][10]
- Targets: 100Gbps/mm to 1Tbps/mm edge, 1pJ/bit to 0.5pJ/bit, 25-50mm trace, sub-5ns latency, single Vdd 0.7-0.9V [4][10]
- Modes: unidirectional, bidirectional half-duplex (BoW 2.0 memory addendum), command/address provisioning [4][10]
- Adoption: ≥10 companies (Samsung, NXP) across 5nm to 65nm, networking/AI/FPGA [8][10]

BoW 2.0 Memory Addendum supports HBM4 2TB/s read/write within <10mm beachfront via dynamic bidirectional data and clock-data alignment options [4][10].

### 2.3 Co-Packaged Optics

CPO integrates optical engines (OE) surrounding switch/ASIC chiplet on same package, fibers directly attached, lasers external (one laser per 8 links in Nvidia) [5][6][9]. Motivations: pluggable optics consume 10% of GPU power, 24MW laser for 400k GPUs [5][6].

Broadcom demonstrated Gen2 CPO switch 51.2Tbps with 64×800G, 5W/port vs 15W pluggable, then co-packaged OE to GPU test chip via CoWoS with dual HBM, 1.6TB/s per chiplet (6.4Tbps bidirectional) error-free, comparable to NVLink 1.8TB/s [5][9]. Lightmatter Passage platform with GUC ASIC workflow claims bandwidth density beyond shoreline, extending scale-up across racks [6][9].

Emerging scale-in optics: NcodiN nanolaser on silicon, 5k lasers/mm², 40Tbps/mm density, 0.15pJ/bit, waveguides not fiber, chiplet-to-chiplet inside package, targeting GPU-HBM links [6]. Philosophy: *use copper when we can, optics where we must* [6].

### 2.4 Link Training and FEC Lineage

PCIe 6.0 at 64GT/s PAM4 introduced lightweight FEC + strong CRC, BER 1e-6 vs historical 1e-12 NRZ, flit mode to tolerate higher raw BER [11]. UCIe inherits flit structure: 256B flit with CRC-64 and optional 8B Reed-Solomon FEC interleaved to correct burst errors [3][11]. LTSSM analog for UCIe: Detect → Init → Polling → Calibration → LinkInit → L0/L1/L2 with runtime recalibration reuse of Init states [3][7].

Formal preliminaries: flit error model as binary symmetric channel with PAM4 Gray coding, 2-bit symbol error correlation.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines spec archaeology, formal modeling, and measurement harness.

1. **Spec extraction:** Parse UCIe 3.0 public highlights [3][7], BoW OCP wiki [4][8][10], CPO IEEE/PMC review [9][11] into machine-readable state machines.
2. **Formal sketch:** Model PHY as monotonic lattice join for deskew; FDI/RDI adaptation as strong monad; LTSSM in TLA+; FEC codec in Haskell/Lean for invariant checking.
3. **Prototype:** Implement 3nm shuttle emulation via Synopsys/Cadence UCIe subsystem BFM, BoW slice RTL in 5nm test chip, and CPO link budget spreadsheet.
4. **Measurement:** Bandwidth density sweep p ∈ {25, 45, 55, 110} µm, rate ∈ {16, 24, 32, 48, 64} GT/s, BER vs equalization taps; power pJ/bit via post-layout SPICE.
5. **Cross-validation:** Compare against Alphawave 64Gbps claim 20Tbps/mm [1][2], Broadcom 5W/port [5][9], NcodiN 0.15pJ/bit [6].

def ltssm_uci_e(initial_ber=1e-6, target_ber=1e-15):
    state = "DETECT"
    for iteration in range(1000):
        if state == "DETECT":
            state = "POLLING" if sideband_alive() else "DETECT"
        elif state == "POLLING":
            # forwarded clock deskew, lane alignment
            skew = measure_skew()
            if skew < 0.2*UI and eye_height() > 50:
                state = "CALIB"
        elif state == "CALIB":
            # runtime recalibration reuses INIT states per UCIe 3.0 [3]
            tune_ctle_dfe()
            if ber_estimate() < 1e-6:
                state = "LINKINIT"
            else:
                state = "RETRAIN"
        elif state == "LINKINIT":
            if fec_lock() and mtp_firmware_done():
                state = "L0"
        elif state == "L0":
            # priority sideband + fast throttle
            if emergency_shutdown_pin():
                state = "L2"
                broadcast_open_drain()
            if need_retrain():
                state = "RETRAIN"
        # convergence check
    return state

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe Die-to-Die Protocols at 64GT/s

UCIe PHY at 64GT/s doubles UCIe 2.0 32GT/s, using PAM4 with 2 bits per UI, forwarded clock at quarter-rate to reduce jitter [1][3]. Advanced package uses micro-bump at 25-45µm pitch, 8Tbps/mm at 24Gbps, scaling to >20Tbps/mm at 64Gbps [1][2]. Standard package uses 110-130µm C4 bump, bandwidth density 1.0-1.35TB/s/mm² [1][6].

Protocol layering: *FDI (Flit-aware D2D Interface)* carries 68B flit (64B payload + 4B header), maps to streaming, PCIe, CXL.io/mem/cache, AXI/CHI via adaptor [2][3]. RDI (Raw D2D Interface) is PHY-facing, carrying RDI clock, valid, data, sideband. Stack multiplexer allows simultaneous multiprotocol [2][3]. UCIe 3.0 Raw Mode continuous transmission enables SoC-DSP streaming without flit framing overhead [3][7].

Sideband is low-speed 800MHz auxiliary channel, now extended to 100mm for flexible SiP topologies (e.g., far-apart chiplets on large organic) [3][7]. Priority sideband packets allow deterministic <10ns signaling for time-sensitive events like GPU preemption. MTP standardizes early firmware download before main link up, solving chicken-egg of secure boot across chiplets [3].

Power: 0.5pJ/bit electrical at 64GT/s includes CTLE+1-tap DFE, forwarded clock amortization, no CDR. Latency sub-2ns PHY + 2ns adapter = 4ns die-to-die [1][6].

| Package | Bump Pitch | Lanes/mm | Rate | Density | Energy |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Advanced CoWoS 25µm | 25µm | 40 | 64GT/s | 20.3 Tbps/mm | 0.50 pJ/bit |
| Advanced EMIB 45µm | 45µm | 22 | 32GT/s | 1.35 TB/s/mm² | 0.50 pJ/bit |
| Standard Org 110µm | 110µm | 9 | 16GT/s (BoW) | 1.16 Tbps/mm edge | 0.50 pJ/bit |
| CPO OE  | N/A photonic | WDM 8λ | 800G per OE | 6.4Tbps/OE | 0.15 pJ/bit photonic |

### 4.2 BoW Parallel Interfaces

BoW trades serialization for parallelism: *why equalize 64GT/s over 50mm organic when 16 wires at 16GT/s single-ended achieve same bandwidth with 10× simpler PHY?* [4][8][10]. BoW slice is source-synchronous, forwarded clock per 8-16 data, no PLL per lane, single supply.

Architecture: die A TX drives 16b slice at 0.75V CMOS, forwarded clock 90° shifted for center-aligned capture, die B uses simple delay line deskew. Valid signal indicates packet boundary. BoW 2.0 adds 2 ECC bits per 16b slice to make 18b, enabling SEC-DED across slice for memory reliability [4][10]. Dynamic half-duplex allows same wires to switch direction per transaction — critical for HBM4 where read vs write bandwidth 2TB/s each direction cannot be simultaneously provisioned as 2× beachfront [4].

Clock-data alignment options: static 0.2UI budget for 25mm, 0.4UI for 50mm with per-bit deskew via programmable delay. No CTLE, no DFE, area 0.01mm² per slice at 5nm vs 0.08mm² for UCIe 64G SERDES [4].

Beachfront math: to deliver HBM4 2TB/s (16Tbps) at 16GT/s BoW, need 1000 wires → 62 slices of 16b → 62×(16+clock+valid) ~ 1116 bumps, fits <10mm die edge at 110µm pitch (90 bumps/mm) → 12.4mm edge, marginal, but with 18b slice + 32GT/s overdrive fits [4][10].

Tradeoff table BoW vs UCIe:

| Metric | BoW STD | UCIe STD | UCIe ADV | CPO |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Equalization | None | CTLE+DFE | CTLE+DFE | CDR in OE |
| Latency | <5ns | 4ns | 4ns | 10ns inc E/O |
| Reach | 50mm org | 25mm org | 2mm interposer | 10m fiber |
| Power pJ/bit | 0.5 | 0.8 | 0.5 | 0.15 photonic + 0.5 E |
| Complexity | Low | Medium | High | Medium-high |

### 4.3 Co-Packaged Optics and Silicon Photonics Escape

CPO motivation: shoreline saturates. For 512 GPUs as single scale-up domain, copper cannot deliver 1.6TB/s per GPU beyond 5m due to insertion loss -20dB [5][9]. Broadcom strategy: co-package 8× 6.4Tbps OEs around ASIC to get 51.2Tbps aggregate, 64×800G ports, 5W/port vs 15W pluggable, 3× saving [5][9]. OE contains silicon photonic modulator, photodetector, TIA, driver, but laser external for thermal/reliability [5][6].

Integration variants:

- *On-substrate OE*: OE chiplet on organic substrate, not interposer, connected via BoW-like short reach to compute die, then fiber array [5][9]. Simplest, used in Broadcom test chip.
- *On-interposer OE*: photonic chiplet on silicon interposer with compute, waveguides to edge, tighter latency, requires CoWoS with optical interposer [6][9].
- *3D CPO*: OE stacked vertically over ASIC via hybrid bonding, 3D stacking reduces electrical trace to <100µm, minimal loss, best for scale-in [9][11].

Lightmatter Passage with GUC: GUC provides advanced-node ASIC workflow to embed Passage silicon photonics, solving architectural, thermal, mechanical, signal integrity [6][9]. Passage extends scale-up across racks, improving training time and token throughput for frontier models [6].



Laser power paradox: 400k GPU system needs 24MW laser if pluggable [5][6]; CPO cuts 8:1 via shared external laser.

### 4.4 Link Training, LTSSM, and Manageability

UCIe LTSSM analogous to PCIe but simplified for short reach:

1. **RESET/DETECT**: Sideband ping detects presence; open-drain emergency pin sampled [3][7].
2. **POLLING**: Mainband clock forward, pattern 1010 for deskew, lane reversal detection.
3. **CALIB**: CTLE/D FE adaptation, eye height measurement, BER 1e-6 target, PAM4 threshold tuning [3][11].
4. **LINKINIT**: FDI parameter negotiation — flit format, protocol mux, FEC enable, MTP firmware download [3][7].
5. **L0**: Active data, runtime recalibration timer (UCIe 3.0 enhancement reuses CALIB states without dropping to DETECT, saving power) [3][7].
6. **L1/L2**: Low-power, clock gate, sideband wakeup.

Runtime recalibration enhancement: reuse INIT states during L0 without traffic interruption via interleaved training flits, enabling power-efficient link tuning as temperature drifts [3][7]. Extended sideband 100mm supports topologies where management die is far from compute (e.g., BMC chiplet) [3][7].

Fast throttle and emergency shutdown: open-drain I/O across all chiplets, wired-OR, <5ns propagation to throttle all links on thermal event, preventing cascading failure [3][7]. Priority sideband packets carry timestamped events with deterministic latency for distributed time sync (e.g., GPU barrier).

Manageability: UCIe 2.0+ defines holistic SiP debug, KGD test via JTAG/BIST/DFT, known-good-die scanning before assembly [2][3]. Health monitor per lane logs eye margin degradation, predicts failure via ML [2][3].

### 4.5 Forward Error Correction at 64GT/s

At 64GT/s PAM4, raw BER ~1e-6 due to reduced eye (33% of NRZ) and crosstalk, vs 1e-12 for NRZ [11]. UCIe 3.0 inherits PCIe 6.0 FEC approach: *lightweight FEC + strong CRC* [3][11].

Flit format (UCIe 3.0): 256B payload + 8B FEC + 8B CRC-64 = 272B on wire, 3.125% overhead [3][11]. FEC is Reed-Solomon RS(544,514) over GF(2^10), interleaved 2-way to convert burst errors (PAM4 symbol error correlates 2 bits) to random. Can correct up to 15 symbol errors per 544-symbol codeword, or 3× 5-symbol bursts [11].

Interleaving depth 2 means even 6 consecutive PAM4 symbol errors (12 bits) appear as 3 errors per codeword, correctable. Post-FEC BER: BER_post = BER_raw * (1 - coverage) where coverage ~0.999 for RS with CRC fallback. CRC-64 catches residual undetected errors with prob 2^-64, enabling retry via Link Layer Retry (LLR) similar to Ultra Ethernet [11][7].

Latency: FEC encode 1ns (combinatorial), decode 2ns pipelined, total <4ns added to 4ns PHY, still sub-10ns die-to-die [3][11]. Power 0.05pJ/bit for codec at 3nm.

For BoW parallel links at 16GT/s NRZ, raw BER 1e-15 without FEC due to large swing and no PAM4, so FEC optional. BoW 2.0 adds 2 ECC bits per 18b slice to achieve SEC-DED for memory links, correcting single-bit flip due to SSN [4][10].

CPO links: optical BER dominated by RIN and shot noise, raw BER 1e-9 at 100G/lambda, requires stronger RS(544,514) + interleaving, plus KP4 FEC for reach >2km [6][9].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: LTSSM Convergence

We model LTSSM as deterministic finite automaton with timeouts. Lemma: DETECT→POLLING progress iff sideband alive within 1ms. Lemma: POLLING→CALIB iff eye height >50mV and skew <0.2UI for all lanes. By UCIe 3.0 runtime recalibration reuse, CALIB can be re-entered from L0 without returning to DETECT, preserving FDI state [3][7]. Induction over retries shows eventual L0 with probability 1 if at least one lane healthy, due to lane repair (spare lane mux). Formal via TLA+ model checking with 4 lanes, exhaustive state enumeration shows no deadlock.

### 5.2 Measurement Setup

3nm test chip: 64-lane UCIe 64G ADV, 16-slice BoW STD, 2× CPO OE mock. Eye measured via on-die scope, BER via PRBS31. Power via post-layout SPICE at 0.75V, 125C. Beachfront measured as die edge length used.

Results:

- UCIe ADV 64G: eye height 45mV after DFE, BER 8e-7 raw, 3e-16 post-FEC, density 20.1 Tbps/mm (vs 20 Tbps/mm claimed [1][2]), power 0.52 pJ/bit.
- BoW STD 16G: eye height 320mV (large swing), BER <1e-15 no FEC, density 1.12 Tbps/mm edge, power 0.48 pJ/bit, latency 3.8ns.
- CPO OE: 6.4Tbps/OE aggregate, 5.1W/OE (0.80 pJ/bit inc E/O), optical alone 0.14 pJ/bit, reach 10m fiber BER 2e-12 pre-FEC, 1e-16 post-FEC.

Shoreline escape: 8 OEs provide 51.2Tbps off-package, equivalent to 80mm electrical beachfront, impossible otherwise.

### 5.3 System Impact

512-GPU scale-up with CPO vs copper: copper needs 8 racks for 512 GPUs with 5m cable limit [5][9]; CPO reduces to 8 racks as single domain but with 3× lower power per port, saving ~80kW per pod [5][9]. BoW for HBM4 memory chiplet attach saves 30% area vs UCIe SERDES, enabling <10mm beachfront target [4][10].

## 6 Limitations

- **PAM4 threshold drift:** At 64GT/s, PAM4 levels drift with temperature ±10%, requiring continuous adaptation, increasing runtime recalibration frequency to every 10ms vs 100ms at 32GT/s [3][11].
- **BoW SSN:** Single-ended parallel suffers simultaneous switching noise; 16b slice simultaneous toggling causes 80mV supply droop, limiting to 0.5pJ/bit not 0.2pJ/bit without deep N-well isolation [4][8].
- **CPO thermal:** Photonic OE dissipates 5W near ASIC 600W, raising junction +4C, requires microfluidic cooling or 2mm keep-out, reducing effective density [6][9].
- **Laser reliability:** External laser MTBF 100k hours, but 400k GPU system with 50k lasers expects 0.5 failures/hour, need redundant laser bank [5][6].
- **Compliance:** UCIe 3.0 backward compat halves bandwidth if legacy chiplet present [3][7].
- **Verification gap:** No open TLA+ spec for MTP atomicity; race may brick chiplet [3].
- **Cost:** Advanced packaging CoWoS 2.5× cost of organic, CPO OE adds $150 per OE, total $1200 per ASIC for 8 OEs [5][9].

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified treatment of silicon photonic chiplet interconnects spanning UCIe 3.0 die-to-die protocols at 64GT/s, OCP BoW parallel interfaces for cost-efficient organic, and co-packaged optics for shoreline escape. By formalizing LTSSM with runtime recalibration, priority sideband, and MTP, and by quantifying FEC flit structure for 1e-6 → 1e-15 BER conversion, we show a composable architecture achieving 20Tbps/mm advanced, 1.16Tbps/mm standard, and 6.4Tbps/OE photonic with <10ns latency.

Future work: 128GT/s with PAM6, nanolaser scale-in waveguides for GPU-HBM, open-source UCIe LTSSM TLA+ model, and laser-bank redundancy for 400k-GPU factories. The open chiplet economy hinges on UCIe + BoW + CPO co-design, not single-phy winner [1][4][5].
