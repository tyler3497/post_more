# AlphaFold2 and the Protein Folding Problem: Evoformer Attention, Invariant Point Attention, and End-to-End Differentiable Structure Prediction

## Abstract

The prediction of a protein's three-dimensional structure from its amino-acid sequence — the *protein folding problem* — resisted five decades of computational effort before DeepMind's AlphaFold2 achieved near-experimental accuracy at the CASP14 blind assessment, with a median backbone deviation of 0.96 Å and more than two-thirds of targets predicted to experimental-grade accuracy [1][7]. This thesis dissects the architecture responsible for that step change: the **Evoformer**, a 48-block attention trunk that jointly refines a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) representation and a two-dimensional pair representation through row- and column-wise gated self-attention, outer-product mean projections, and triangle consistency operations; and the **structure module**, which folds an unstructured *residue gas* into coordinates using **Invariant Point Attention (IPA)**, an $\mathrm{SE}(3)$-equivariant operator that renders attention invariant to global rotation and translation. We formalize the end-to-end training objective — dominated by the Frame Aligned Point Error ($\text{FAPE}$) loss — describe the recycling loop that iteratively refines predictions, and review empirical evidence from CASP14, the human proteome [2], and AlphaFold-Multimer [4]. We close with a candid treatment of limitations: MSA dependence, intrinsic disorder, conformational dynamics, and compute cost.

## 1 Introduction

In 1973, Christian Anfinsen articulated the thermodynamic hypothesis: a protein's native conformation is determined by its amino-acid sequence alone, corresponding to the global free-energy minimum of the chain [5]. Yet the same decade, Cyrus Levinthal observed a paradox: a modest 100-residue chain possesses on the order of $3^{100}$ conformations, far too many to enumerate by random search within the age of the universe — implying that folding follows directed pathways rather than blind sampling. Between these two poles, the *protein folding problem* became the central challenge of computational biology: given a sequence of amino acids, predict the three-dimensional coordinates of every atom.

Progress was historically incremental. The biennial Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction (CASP), running since 1994, recorded long plateaus punctuated by short bursts [7]. Classical approaches — fragment assembly, homology modelling, and physics-based molecular dynamics — each captured part of the truth but none generalized. Statistical potentials learned from the Protein Data Bank improved slowly; co-evolutionary contact prediction (direct coupling analysis) added genuine signal but remained coarse. At CASP13 in 2018, DeepMind's first-generation AlphaFold achieved a median accuracy of 6.6 Å on the hardest targets [5] — technically impressive yet "arguably only an incremental improvement over competing algorithms" in both design and performance [4].

CASP14, held in 2020, was different in kind. AlphaFold2 advanced the state of the art to atomic accuracy in a single step: median polypeptide backbone accuracy of **0.96 Å**, with almost two-thirds of targets predicted to accuracy competitive with the best experimental results [1][7]. The fraction of correctly predicted structure crossed 90%, a leap the community had not anticipated for another decade [2]. The accompanying *Nature* paper — some sixty pages with thirty-two distinct algorithmic components — documented a wholesale architectural re-engineering [1]. This thesis is a systematic study of that architecture: what the Evoformer computes, why triangle operations enforce geometric consistency, how Invariant Point Attention achieves differentiable equivariant folding, and where the system still fails.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Co-evolution and the statistical coupling signal

Proteins evolve under structural constraint: residues in spatial contact tend to mutate in a correlated fashion across homologs. Given a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) of related sequences, correlated mutation columns carry information about which residues are proximal in three dimensions. Direct coupling analysis (DCA) and its successors (e.g., CCMpred) formalized this into pairwise contact scores, and contact-assisted predictors such as trRosetta and the original AlphaFold consumed these scores as features for downstream optimization [5]. The limitation was representational: contact maps are two-dimensional marginals of a three-dimensional object, and thresholding them discards the joint structure of the distance distribution.

### 2.2 AlphaFold1: learned potentials over distances

The first AlphaFold treated structure prediction as the construction of a learned potential: a deep residual network predicted, for every residue pair, a full *distogram* — a probability distribution over 64 distance bins — and predicted backbone torsion angles, which were then optimized into coordinates by gradient descent over the implied energy landscape [5]. This achieved the CASP13 breakthrough but retained an architectural seam: the network predicted *descriptors* of structure, while a separate optimization procedure assembled them. Errors in the distogram propagated into the optimizer, and the system could not be trained end-to-end on final coordinates.

### 2.3 The 2021 convergence: RoseTTAFold and AlphaFold-Multimer

AlphaFold2 did not emerge in isolation. In the same year, Baek *et al.* introduced RoseTTAFold, a "three-track" network processing sequence, distance, and coordinate information simultaneously, published in *Science* [3]. RoseTTAFold demonstrated that the two-track (MSA plus pair) idea had independent merit, though its accuracy trailed AlphaFold2. Meanwhile Evans *et al.* extended the architecture to protein complexes in AlphaFold-Multimer, showing that paired MSAs across interacting chains generalize the Evoformer to quaternary structure [4]. The community response was rapid: ColabFold packaged accelerated sequence search with the released parameters, making proteome-scale prediction feasible on commodity hardware [6].

> **Theorem (informal):** *End-to-end differentiability.* By replacing the distogram-plus-optimizer pipeline with a structure module that directly regresses 3D coordinates under a frame-aligned loss, AlphaFold2 makes the full mapping from sequence and MSA to atomic coordinates differentiable, permitting gradient signal from final geometric error to flow back into the evolutionary representations of the trunk [1].

## 3 Methodology

We now describe the AlphaFold2 inference pipeline as published [1], in the order data flows through it.

**Inputs.** For a target sequence of length $N_{\text{res}}$, the system builds (i) an MSA by searching sequence databases (HHblits against BFD and MGnify, JackHMMER against UniRef90), subsampled to $N_{\text{seq}}$ sequences (typically a few hundred after clustering), and (ii) a set of structural templates from the PDB, encoded as distogram-like features and backbone frames [1][6].

**Embedding.** The MSA is one-hot encoded and projected to the *MSA representation* $m_{si} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_m}$ ($s$ indexes sequences, $i$ residues; $c_m = 256$). The pair representation $z_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_z}$ ($c_z = 128$) is initialized from the target sequence's outer products and template features. These two tensors are the entire state of the trunk.

**The Evoformer trunk.** Forty-eight identical blocks alternately update the two representations. Each block applies, in order:

1. *MSA row-wise gated self-attention* — attention across residues within each sequence, biased by the pair representation;
2. *MSA column-wise gated self-attention* — attention across sequences at each residue position, the operation that pools evolutionary information;
3. an *MSA transition* (two-layer MLP with gating);
4. the *outer-product mean*, which projects MSA information into the pair representation;
5. a *pair stack*: triangle multiplicative updates followed by triangle self-attention (row- then column-wise), then a pair transition [1].

**The structure module.** After the trunk, a single-sequence representation $s_i$ is derived from the first MSA row, and eight structure-module blocks refine a set of per-residue rigid frames (backbone frames). Each block applies Invariant Point Attention to update $s_i$, then predicts frame updates that move each residue's local coordinate frame in 3D space. Final atom positions (backbone plus side chains via torsion prediction) are read out differentiably [1].

**Recycling.** The predicted structure, the pair representation, and the MSA representation are fed back as inputs for up to three additional passes (with stopped gradients through the recycle), each iteration refining the previous prediction [1][6].

**Losses.** Training combines the Frame Aligned Point Error ($\text{FAPE}$) on all atoms with auxiliary heads: the distogram, masked-MSA reconstruction (a BERT-like objective), predicted LDDT ($\text{pLDDT}$), predicted aligned error ($\text{PAE}$), and experimentally resolved loss terms [1].

**Relaxation and ranking.** Five independently parameterized models are run; each output is relaxed with AMBER to resolve steric clashes, and structures are ranked by mean $\text{pLDDT}$, the per-residue confidence estimate [1][6].

| Stage | Input representations | Output | Key operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Embedding | Sequence, MSA, templates | $m_{si}$, $z_{ij}$ | Linear projections, outer products |
| Evoformer (×48) | $m_{si}$, $z_{ij}$ | Refined $m_{si}$, $z_{ij}$ | Row/col attention, triangle updates |
| Structure module (×8) | $s_i$, $z_{ij}$ | Backbone frames, atom coords | Invariant Point Attention |
| Recycling (×3) | Prior coords, $m$, $z$ | Refined prediction | Stop-gradient feedback |
| Heads | Final state | $\text{pLDDT}$, PAE, distogram | Confidence estimation |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Evoformer as a learned belief-propagation engine

The Evoformer's design is best understood as a neural analogue of belief propagation on a graphical model whose nodes are residues and whose edges carry pairwise geometric beliefs. The MSA representation $m_{si}$ stores *what evolution says* about each position in each homolog; the pair representation $z_{ij}$ stores *what the network currently believes* about the spatial relationship of residues $i$ and $j$. Crucially, attention in the MSA stack is **gated by the pair representation**: the attention logits for MSA row-wise attention receive an additive bias computed from $z_{ij}$, so that evolving geometric hypotheses re-weight which sequence positions attend to each other. Conversely, the outer-product mean injects MSA information into $z_{ij}$ via

```python
# Outer-product mean: MSA (s, i, c_m) -> pair update (i, j, c_z)
m = layer_norm(msa)                       # (n_seq, n_res, c_m)
a, b = linear_a(m), linear_b(m)           # two projections
outer = einsum('sic,sjd->ijcd', a, b)     # per-sequence outer products
pair_update = linear_out(outer.mean(dim=0))  # mean over sequences
z = z + pair_update
```

Averaging the outer product over sequences is the differentiable descendant of covariance estimation in DCA: it measures which position pairs co-vary *in the learned feature space* rather than in raw amino-acid identity. Column-wise attention, meanwhile, lets each position gather evidence across the whole alignment — effectively performing a learned, position-specific weighting of homologs that generalizes the sequence-weighting heuristics of classical MSA processing [1].

### 4.2 Triangle operations: enforcing geometric consistency

Pairwise distance beliefs must satisfy the triangle inequality: if $i$ is near $k$ and $k$ is near $j$, then $i$ cannot be far from $j$. The Evoformer enforces this through two operations on $z_{ij}$ [1]:

- **Triangle multiplicative update.** For each ordered pair $(i,j)$, the update aggregates over a third index $k$: $z_{ij} \mathrel{+}= \sum_k g(z_{ik}) \odot h(z_{kj})$, where $g, h$ are learned projections and $\odot$ is elementwise multiplication with gating. This is a differentiable, learned generalization of the triangle inequality applied in representation space — information about the $i$–$k$ and $k$–$j$ relationships directly revises the $i$–$j$ belief.
- **Triangle self-attention.** Standard self-attention is applied with $i$ (or $j$) as the batch dimension and the third index as the sequence dimension, with attention logits biased by the pair representation itself. Starting-node and ending-node variants propagate constraints in both directions around every triangle.

These operations are the architectural reason AlphaFold2's pair representation behaves like a consistent distance geometry rather than a bag of independent contact scores. Ablations in the *Nature* paper show that removing triangle updates degrades accuracy substantially, confirming that geometric consistency is *learned in the representation*, not merely imposed at the end [1].

> **Theorem:** *Triangle consistency.* Under the triangle multiplicative update, the pair representation $z_{ij}$ receives gradient signal from every length-2 path $i \to k \to j$, so that after sufficient blocks the representation approximates a transitively consistent pairwise belief state; residual inconsistency is penalized downstream by the $\text{FAPE}$ loss on realized coordinates [1].

### 4.3 Invariant Point Attention: equivariance without coordinates

The structure module must reason about 3D geometry while being insensitive to the arbitrary global reference frame of the prediction. Invariant Point Attention achieves this elegantly [1]. Each residue $i$ carries a local rigid frame $T_i = (R_i, t_i) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ derived from its backbone atoms (N, Cα, C). The network predicts, for each residue, a set of *query points*, *key points*, and *values* in 3D — but attention weights are computed **after transforming all points into each residue's local frame**:

```python
# Invariant Point Attention (schematic)
T = backbone_frames(s)                    # per-residue SE(3) frames
q_pts, k_pts, v_pts = linear_points(s)    # learned 3D points per residue
# express points in local frames: invariant to global rotation/translation
q_local = T_i.invert().apply(q_pts_i)     # (n_res, n_q, 3)
k_local = T_j.invert().apply(k_pts_j)
attn = softmax(q_local @ k_local.T / sqrt(d) + pair_bias(z_ij))
out_pts = sum_j attn_ij * T_i.apply(v_pts_j)  # back to global
s = s + linear(concat(out_pts, attn_features))
```

Because the squared distances between points expressed in local frames are unchanged by any global rigid transformation, the attention weights are **invariant** to global rotation and translation, while the aggregated outputs transform **equivariantly** — exactly the symmetry group of the physics. IPA therefore lets the network perform attention *in 3D space* without ever committing to an absolute coordinate system, and without the discretization or voxelization that plagued earlier 3D-aware networks [1][3].

### 4.4 From residue gas to atomic coordinates

The structure module initializes all residue frames at the origin — the *residue gas*, a maximally uninformative configuration — and each of the eight blocks predicts incremental frame updates $(\Delta R_i, \Delta t_i)$ via IPA-refined single representations. Backbone atom positions follow deterministically from frames and idealized geometry; side-chain atoms are placed by predicting $\chi$ torsion angles autoregressively and applying them through the same differentiable frame machinery [1]. Because every step is differentiable, the $\text{FAPE}$ loss — which aligns predicted and true structures in each residue's local frame and penalizes clamped coordinate deviations — provides per-residue gradient signal that is itself invariant to global superposition. The auxiliary $\text{pLDDT}$ head, trained to predict the true LDDT-Cα score, is what makes the system *self-aware*: at inference, mean $\text{pLDDT}$ ranks the five model outputs and flags disordered or unreliable regions, a property exploited at proteome scale [2][6].

## 5 Empirical Results / Theoretical Guarantees

**CASP14.** The blind assessment remains the gold standard [7]. AlphaFold2's median backbone accuracy of 0.96 Å and >90% correctly predicted structure fraction represented a discontinuity: the second-best group's performance resembled the historical trend line, while AlphaFold2 sat far above it. Nearly two-thirds of targets reached accuracy "competitive with the best experimental results" (~1 Å backbone deviation) [1].

**Ablation evidence.** The *Nature* paper's ablations isolate the contributions of the novel components: removing the pair representation bias in MSA attention, removing triangle updates, or replacing IPA with standard attention each degrade GDT scores markedly, with the full model recovering the CASP14-winning performance only when all components are present [1].

**Proteome scale.** Tunyasuvunakool *et al.* applied the system to the human proteome: 98.5% of human proteins received predictions, with 58% of residues predicted confidently and 36% at very high confidence, against a background where only 17% of residues had experimental coverage [2]. Confidence-calibrated interpretation (high $\text{pLDDT}$ domains vs. low-confidence disordered linkers) turned the raw predictions into a usable biological resource.

**Complexes and accessibility.** AlphaFold-Multimer extended the Evoformer to multimeric inputs via paired MSAs, achieving accurate complex prediction where paired evolutionary signal exists [4]. ColabFold's reimplementation — MMseqs2-based MSA search with early-stopping on $\text{pLDDT} \geq 85$ — reproduced AlphaFold2 accuracy (mean $\text{pLDDT}$ 89.75 vs. 88.78 on a 50-protein subset) while making a 1,762-protein proteome predictable in 48 hours on a single GPU [6].

| System | Year | Key innovation | Reported accuracy signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| AlphaFold1 | 2020 | Learned distogram potentials + optimization [5] | 6.6 Å median, hardest CASP13 targets |
| RoseTTAFold | 2021 | Three-track sequence/distance/coordinate network [3] | Near-AF2, below on CASP14-style targets |
| **AlphaFold2** | 2021 | Evoformer + IPA + $\text{FAPE}$ end-to-end [1] | 0.96 Å median backbone, CASP14 |
| AlphaFold-Multimer | 2021 | Paired MSAs for complexes [4] | High accuracy where co-evolution signal exists |
| ColabFold | 2022 | Fast MSA + early stopping [6] | Parity with AF2 at fraction of compute |

> **Theorem:** *Confidence calibration.* The $\text{pLDDT}$ head is trained by regression against true per-residue LDDT-Cα; empirically, predicted confidence correlates strongly with actual accuracy, so that mean $\text{pLDDT}$ is a reliable model-selection and disorder-detection statistic without access to ground truth [1][2].

## 6 Limitations

AlphaFold2's limitations are as instructive as its successes, and several are architectural rather than incidental.

1. **MSA dependence.** The Evoformer is an engine for distilling co-evolutionary signal; for orphan proteins, designed sequences, or viral proteins with shallow alignments, accuracy degrades sharply. The network has no physics engine to fall back on — it is a *statistical* folder, not a *physical* one [1][6].
2. **Single static conformation.** Training on PDB structures biases predictions toward the crystallized state. Intrinsically disordered regions are flagged by low $\text{pLDDT}$ rather than modelled as ensembles [2]; allosteric transitions, folding pathways, and kinetics are outside the model's scope entirely.
3. **Side chains and chemistry.** While backbone accuracy is atomic, side-chain placement and ligand, ion, and post-translational-modification handling were not part of the original objective; steric plausibility relies on post-hoc AMBER relaxation rather than learned chemistry [1].
4. **Data and compute cost.** Training required the PDB's ~170,000 structures and massive compute; inference with recycling and five models remains expensive relative to ColabFold's optimized pipeline [6]. Template and MSA search dominate wall-clock time.
5. **Evaluation bias.** CASP targets are single chains amenable to experimental structure determination — precisely the regime where PDB training data is densest. Performance on membrane proteins, large complexes (pre-Multimer), and novel folds is less well characterized [4][7].

These limitations delineate the boundary between *structure prediction* and *the protein folding problem* in its full physical sense: AlphaFold2 solved the mapping from sequence to native-state coordinates for well-represented protein families, while dynamics, disorder, and design remain open [1][2].

## 7 Conclusion

AlphaFold2's achievement rests on three interlocking ideas. First, the **Evoformer** treats evolutionary and geometric information as two coupled representations refined by 48 blocks of row/column attention, outer-product mean projections, and triangle consistency operations — a learned, differentiable belief-propagation over residue pairs [1]. Second, **Invariant Point Attention** brings 3D reasoning into the network with the correct symmetries: attention computed in per-residue local frames is invariant to global rigid motion while updates remain equivariant, enabling direct coordinate regression without discretization [1]. Third, **end-to-end differentiability** under the $\text{FAPE}$ loss, closed by the recycling loop and calibrated by $\text{pLDDT}$, turns the entire pipeline into a single trainable function from sequence to structure [1][2].

The empirical record — 0.96 Å median backbone accuracy at CASP14 [7], 98.5% coverage of the human proteome [2], extension to complexes [4], and community reimplementation at commodity scale [6] — marks the transition of structure prediction from a research competition to infrastructure. Yet the system's dependence on evolutionary signal, its silence on dynamics and disorder, and its distance from physical simulation define the next frontier. The protein folding problem, in Anfinsen's thermodynamic sense, is now a solved *prediction* problem for much of the known protein universe; the *mechanistic* problem — how and why chains fold, move, and malfunction — remains gloriously open.
