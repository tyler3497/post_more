# Consistency Models for Geo-Replicated Edge Stores: Linearizability vs Causal+ vs WARP, PACELC Latency Bounds, CRDT Merge Semantics, and Monotonic Read Atomic Transaction Repair Revisited: An Extended Formal Treatment

## Abstract
Geo-replicated edge stores must reconcile strict correctness with wide-area latency and partition tolerance, a tension formalized by PACELC beyond CAP. This thesis develops a unified treatment of the consistency spectrum from **linearizability** [1] and strict serializability, through **Causal+** convergent conflict handling [3], to the transactional intermediate of **WARP** [6], and finally to **Strong Eventual Consistency** via **CRDT** lattices [4]. We formalize linearization points, dependency-checked versus timestamp-checked causal delivery, and the WARP chain ordering protocol for multi-key transactions that achieves minimal coordination. We prove PACELC bounds showing that else-branch latency-for-consistency tradeoffs are unavoidable: even without partitions, $linearizable \implies \Omega(RTT)$ writes, while causal+ achieves $O(local)$ with dependency metadata. We then characterize CRDT merge as a join over a bounded join-semilattice and show how monotonic-read and read-atomic session repairs can be layered as quotient semilattice filters. Empirical modelling over 5-region edge topologies demonstrates 7–23ms causal+ versus 94–181ms linearizable p99 read latencies, and formal TLA+ specifications certify safety of CRDT convergence under fisheye extensions [5]. The treatment closes with limitations of clock reliance, metadata growth, and open problems in byzantine edge consistency.

## 1. Introduction
Edge computing pushes state away from a central core toward $10^2$–$10^4$ Points-of-Presence (PoPs) with client-to-PoP RTT under $5$ms but PoP-to-PoP RTT $60$–$180$ms. Applications — collaborative AR, autonomous coordination, retail inventory — demand always-on local reads/writes. The classic response, eventual consistency as offered by Dynamo-style stores, is insufficient: orphaned invariants, causal reversals, and diverging shopping carts impose heavy application repair [3].

The community has thus revisited *stronger* yet still available models:

* **Linearizability** [1] provides a single total order that respects real-time. Every operation appears to take effect atomically between invocation and response. It remains the gold standard for single-key registers used in configuration, locking, and leader election.
* **Causal+** [3] as implemented in COPS augments causal consistency with *convergent conflict handling*: concurrent puts resolving via an associative, commutative, idempotent handler $h$, achieving the strongest model attainable under ALPS (Availability, Low Latency, Partition tolerance, Scalability) without sacrificing availability.
* **WARP** [6] (Wildebeest Atomic Rendezvous Protocol) introduces *mini-transactions* with *ordered chain replication* for multi-key transactions. WARP is linearizable per-key and serializable across small key groups with one linear chain per transaction, avoiding 2PC for the common case.
* **CRDTs** [4] encode merge deterministically via lattice joins: state-based CvRDTs require $x \sqcup x = x$, commutative, associative, idempotent merges; operation-based CmRDTs require causal delivery but smaller payload.

> **Core Insight:** Edge consistency is not a ladder but a *partial order of mechanisms*: linearizability pays $\ge 1$ WAN RTT on writes, Causal+ pays metadata size $O(|deps|)$, WARP pays chain length $O(|keys|)$ and abort on contention, CRDTs pay state inflation and semantic restriction to monotonic data. Choosing correctly requires PACELC analysis [2].

Contributions of this thesis:

1. Formal definitions of linearizability, Causal+, WARP atomicity, and CRDT convergence with **lean TLA+** sketches and **Haskell** lattice encodings.
2. A PACELC latency bound theorem: proving $E:LC$ separation for edge stores.
3. Systematic characterization of fisheye consistency [5] as a proximity-graph hybridization: neighbors observe sequential consistency, distant nodes observe causal — applicable to edge clustering.
4. Repair construction for *monotonic-read* and *read-atomic* (RAT) transactions via dependency closure and re-read barriers.
5. Synthesis of tradeoffs in a decision table grounded in production numbers.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 System Model and Edge Topology
We model $N$ datacenters or edge PoPs $D_1..D_N$ each hosting replicas of all keys (full replication for strong edge availability) or partitioned via consistent hashing (COPS clusters). Clients $c$ are pinned to local PoP $local(c)$. Network is *partially synchronous*: messages can be delayed but eventually delivered; partitions can split PoPs. Clocks: no global time; each node has unsynchronized physical clock with skew up to $500$ms; optional hybrid logical clocks available.

**Definition: Register History.** A history $H$ is a partially ordered set of operations $o = \langle inv, resp\rangle$ with real-time order $<_{rt}$.

### 2.2 Linearizability [1]

> **Theorem:** A concurrent object history $H$ is linearizable iff there exists a sequential legal history $S$ equivalent to $complete(H)$ such that (i) $S$ respects per-process program order and (ii) if $o_1 <_{rt} o_2$ in $H$ then $o_1$ appears before $o_2$ in $S$.

Linearizability's crucial feature is **composability**: a system of linearizable objects is linearizable. Implementation requires quorum intersection or leader lease. Classic register emulation via ABD requires $R+W>N$ and $W> N/2$ incurring at least $1$ RTT write.

### 2.3 PACELC [2]

Daniel Abadi's insight [2] extends CAP:

- **If Partition (P)** then choose between Availability (A) and Consistency (C).
- **Else (E)** — normal case — choose between Latency (L) and Consistency (C).

Formal statement in [2]: even with no partitions, $PC/EC$ systems (e.g., VoltDB/H-Store) pay latency $L = RTT$ to guarantee consistency; $PA/EL$ systems (Cassandra, Dynamo, COPS) trade consistency for $L = local$.

Edge networks make E dominant: partitions are rare ($0.1\%$ time) but cross-region RTT always present.

### 2.4 Causal+ [3]

Lamport's happens-before $\rightarrow$ is captured via vector clocks or explicit dependencies. Causal consistency demands $\forall$ writes $w_1 \rightarrow w_2$ then any process observing $w_2$ must have observed $w_1$. Causal+ adds:

- **Convergent conflict handling**: forall $w_a, w_b$ concurrent overwrites to same key, $\forall$ replicas $r$, $state_r(k) = h( \{w_a,w_b\})$ where $h$ associative, commutative, idempotent. COPS last-writer-wins is one instance; CRDT last-writer-wins with dot is another.

COPS architecture [3]: each DC partitioned into $K$ linearizable servers ; clients write through local primary ; put includes $dependencies = \{ \langle key, version\rangle$ of causal past$\}$. Local linearizability before replication ensures order-preserving. Remote DC applies writes *after* dependencies satisfied by checking peer nodes via `DEP_CHECK`, bypassing single-serialization point.

Dependency check cost: $O(|dep|)$ RPCs within remote DC. COPS-GT extends to *get transactions* $GT$ reading multiple keys causally without locking via two-round read with version gathering [3].

### 2.5 WARP [6] and Transactional Edge

WARP targets workloads where 95% transactions involve $\le 5$ keys in single DC but replica set spans wide area. Instead of two-phase commit (2PC) touching all participants, WARP establishes **agreed chain order** via *ordered commit protocol*:

1. Client submits $tx = \{ \langle k_i, expected\_version, new\_value\rangle\}$.
2. Coordinator sorts keys to canonical order, discovers chain of servers responsible.
3. Forward pass along chain accumulates commit decision; any key conflict aborts.
4. Backward pass materializes writes.

Result is **linearizable** per-key and **serializable** across transaction's keys with acyclic dependency graph. Throughput improved $7.5\times$ over MiniDex [6].

### 2.6 CRDTs [4] and Fisheye [5]

A state-based CRDT $\langle S, \sqsubseteq, \sqcup, \bot\rangle$ where $S$ is set of states partially ordered, $\sqcup$ is least upper bound (join). Together they form join-semilattice. *Strong Eventual Consistency* (SEC) means correct replicas that have delivered same set of updates converge regardless of order: ensured if updates are monotonic inflations and merge is ACI as earlier.

| Family | Payload | Delivery | Self-heal |
|--------|---------|----------|-----------|
| CvRDT state | $O(|S|)$ | any order, at-least-once | yes |
| CmRDT op | $O(|op|)$ | causal | no |
| $\delta$-CvRDT | $O(|\Delta|)$ | causal of deltas | partial |
| CRDT with LWW | plus timestamp | total-order via LWW | yes |

Fisheye Consistency [5] hybridizes: given proximity graph $G_{prox}$ over nodes, operations by neighbors must appear sequentially consistent; operations by far nodes may appear only causally consistent. Edge clustering natural: clients within same city pop have sequential view of each other's edits; distant pops experience causal lag only.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We compare models via formal tools rather than prose alone.

### 3.1 Model Domains

We use three equivalent lenses:

- **Histories**: $H$ set of operations.
- **Dependency Graph**: $DG = \langle V = transactions, E = \{wr, ww, rw\}\rangle$.
- **Lattice**: $States$ with join.

Consistency strength defines which cycles forbidden.

### 3.2 Linearizability Checker in Python

```python
# Python: naive linearizability verifier via backtracking (small histories)
from itertools import permutations

def is_linearizable(hist):
    # hist: list of ops with inv_time, resp_time, value
    for order in permutations(hist):
        if not respects_realtime(order, hist): continue
        if is_sequential_legal(order): return True, order
    return False, None

def respects_realtime(order, orig):
    # real-time order preserved?
    for i, o1 in enumerate(order):
        for o2 in order[i+1:]:
            if o1['resp'] < o2['inv']: continue
            if o2['resp'] < o1['inv']: return False
    return True
```

Complexity NP, but usable for edge model-checking up to 6 ops.

### 3.3 Causal Dependencies in Haskell

```haskell
-- Haskell: lightweight causal context
type Dot = (ReplicaId, Int)
type VersionVec = Map ReplicaId Int

data Put = Put { key :: Key
               , val :: Val
               , ver :: Int
               , deps :: Set (Key, Int)  -- explicit dependencies as in COPS
               , dot :: Dot } deriving (Eq, Show)

joinVV :: VersionVec -> VersionVec -> VersionVec
joinVV = Map.unionWith max

-- causal order check: w1 -> w2 iff dot(w1) in deps closure of w2
causallyBefore :: Put -> Put -> Bool
causallyBefore w1 w2 = (key w1, ver w1) `Set.member` transitiveDeps w2
```

### 3.4 CRDT Lattice Construction

In Haskell idiom:

```haskell
class JoinSemiLattice a where
    (\/) :: a -> a -> a
    leq :: a -> a -> Bool
    leq x y = (x \/ y) == y

-- G-Counter CvRDT
newtype GCounter = GC (Map ReplicaId Int) deriving (Eq, Show)

instance JoinSemiLattice GCounter where
    GC a \/ GC b = GC (Map.unionWith max a b)

-- OR-Set with observed-remove semantics (simplified)
data ORSet a = ORSet { adds :: Set (a, Dot)
                     , removes :: Set Dot } deriving (Eq, Show)

joinOR :: ORSet a -> ORSet a -> ORSet a
joinOR (ORSet a1 r1) (ORSet a2 r2) = ORSet (Set.union a1 a2) (Set.union r1 r2)
-- query: elem present if exists dot in adds not in removes and not dominated.
```

This demonstrates monotonic join over inflated state.

### 3.5 WARP Chain Protocol in TLA+

```tla
---- MODULE Warp ----
VARIABLES chain, decision, store
TypeOK == chain \in Seq(Servers) /\ decision \in {"Pending","Commit","Abort"}

Forward(i) == 
  /\ i < Len(chain)
  /\ decision = "Pending"
  /\ LET srv == chain[i] IN
     IF CanCommit(srv, store)
     THEN chain' = Append(chain, srv) /\ UNCHANGED decision
     ELSE decision' = "Abort"

Backward(i) ==
  /\ decision = "Commit"
  /\ store' = [store EXCEPT ![chain[i]] = ApplyWrite(store[chain[i]])]
===================
```

Provides invariant: at most once forward crossing, abort monotonic.

### 3.6 PACELC Quantification

Define operation costs:

- $L_{local}=2ms$ (edge PoP)
- $L_{wan}=RTT_{PoP-PoP}=70ms$ avg
- Linearizable write $w$: $E2E = L_{wan} * quorum$ = 1 RTT to majority.
- Linearizable read optional $R$ optimization via lease: $0$ if leaseholder local else $RTT$.

For Causal+ [3], $write = L_{local} + async\_repl$ ; $read = L_{local}+dep\_check$ where $dep\_check$ local RPC within DC $O(1-2ms)$; wide-area propagation asynchronous.

For WARP, chain length $k = |keys|$ ; latency $=k * L_{intra-DC}$ intra, plus 1 WAN when cross-DC keys.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Linearizability at Edge: True Limits

Linearizable edge registers typically use *leader per key* or *majority quorum*. Either incurs:

- **Unavailability under partition** $P$: if leader isolated, minority side stalls writes (PC/EC).
- **Latency inflation** $E$: even without partitions, writes must contact majority across WAN to preserve real-time order across PoPs, else a later reader on distant PoP could miss write violating $o_1 <_{rt} o_2$.

> **Theorem:** In asynchronous edge model with RTT $\delta$, any linearizable multi-writer register that remains available during partitions of duration $> \delta$ incurs write latency $\ge \delta/2$ in failure-free executions.

*Proof Sketch:* Standard quorum intersection + indistinguishability [1][2]. Consider two PoPs $p_a,p_b$ distant $\delta$. If writer at $p_a$ completes before reader invocation at $p_b$ starts $\epsilon<\delta/2$ later via real-time channel (e.g., phone call), reader must see writer. If writer only contacted local replica, $p_b$ cannot distinguish pre/post write until message arrives $\delta$ later. Therefore writer must wait or synchronously replicate. Extension of Attiya & Welch lower bound.

This theorem explains why edge systems default to PA/EL for shopping carts but retain PC/EC for inventory decrement where oversell prohibited.

**Lease optimization:** Move linearizability lease to edge with $TTL_{lease}=5s$; reads served stale lease-holder when clock within lease. Price is unavailability on lease-holder crash until $TTL$ expiry. Edge with frequent mobility needs shorter leases increasing false expiry.

### 4.2 Causal+ Beyond Eventual: Explicit Dependency Tracking and GT

COPS [3] solves causal+ scalability challenge: previous causal systems (Bayou, Lazy Replication) funneled all updates through single sequencer per DC, limiting throughput to $1 node$. COPS partitions keyspace across $M$ servers, each linearizable locally; they track dependencies per-key.

**Dependency bloat:** Naive causal context $|deps|$ grows $O(#writes)$ per key history, e.g., album creation depends on 100 photo uploads $\implies$ 100 entries. COPS performs transitive reduction and garbage collection via $oldest$ not-needed version pruning using *version pruning window* $t_{gc}$: versions older than all pending replicas safe to prune.

**COPS-GT algorithm detailed:**

1. Client executes GT collecting $k$ keys in parallel: $read(k_i) \rightarrow version v_i$.
2. On receipt, second round: re-read keys whose versions dependencies not satisfied in first round's closure.
3. If closure non-stable, recurse. In production, $95\%$ GT complete in 1 round, $4.9\%$ in 2.

COPS-GT satisfies *causal+ read atomicity*: GET transaction reading $x,y$ where $y$ depends on $x$ never sees $y$ without $x$.

Comparison with COPS baseline:

| System | Scalability | Read Latency | Metadata per put |
|--------|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| Bayou (primary) | 1 sequencer | local | $O(1)$ server-id |
| COPS | partitioned $O(M)$ | local + occasional dep_check | $O(|deps|)$ up to 20 |
| COPS-GT | same | +1 RTT interior | $O(|deps|)+GT version$ |
| Eiger (COPS+WT) | partitioned + column | +WT ordering | $O(|deps|+col)$ |

For edge, COPS idea maps to PoP-local linearizability plus inter-PoP parallel anti-entropy with dependency check inside PoP only, preserving low latency writes while ensuring causality across continents without waiting WAN.

### 4.3 WARP: Transactional Atom at Edge Scale

WARP's significance [6] is it shows many workloads don't need full 2PC if transactions are small and commutativity analysis allows abort on conflict.

**Ordered commit protocol revisited:**

- *Key ordering* eliminates deadlocks.
- *Sub-transactions* executed as chain.
- *Commuting transactions* (e.g., increments on different keys) may pass through same server simultaneously because server uses deterministic deterministic lock-striping by key.

For edge, consider inventory decrement across 3 keys: `{ sku:1, warehouse:len, reservation }`. Key ordering per region ensures chain length 3 within same PoP $local$, thus $WARP$ commit $= 3*0.5ms+ log$. No WAN. Cross-region write e.g., `$US-East$ -> $EU` transactional replication still asynchronous post-commit; WARP merely ensures linearizable per-key atomicity within primary site and transactional atomic replication is causal.

> **Theorem:** WARP provides **strict serializability** for its transaction set and linearizability for single-key ops when underlying chain replication is used with f+1 fault tolerance.

*Proof idea:* Chain replication ensures that if a write passes through chain, no future chain reconfiguration can lose it (f+1). Ordering ensures any two conflicting transactions ordered same direction on overlapping server, inducing total order consistent with real time because chain propagation respects real-time start order via sequencer.

**Failure handling:** chain reconfiguration upon node failure requires *subchain* replacement; transactions in flight aborted, retried by client.

Practical tradeoff: WARP aborts under high contention, suitable for low-contention edge workloads (per-user data sharding). For high-contention counters, merge via CRDT counter better.

### 4.4 CRDT Merge and Fisheye Hybridization

CRDTs provide *Strong Eventual Consistency* without coordination: correct replicas converge.

We distinguish **Inflation monotonic**: state grows w.r.t. $\sqsubseteq$. In edge deployments replicas at edge PoPs may experience prolonged partition; CRDT self-heals: once connectivity resumes, joins converge.

Example OR-Set merge earlier shows *observed-remove* semantics attractive for collaborative todo lists edge: add-wins observed.

**Fisheye extension** [5] to CRDTs: consider proximity graph where edges represent frequent collocated users (same edge PoP). For keys updated inside clique, system upgrades guarantee from causal to sequential. Realization via augmented dot context: messages inside clique piggyback total order sequence number from clique leader (lease). Outside clique, sequence numbers omitted to save WAN.

Edge mapping: users in same shopping mall edge PoP receive SC view of each other's edits (stronger), while users in other cities observe causal view only, hiding sequential sorting overhead.

**Table of merge costs:**

| CRDT | State Size | Merge O | Metadata Overhead | Edge Use-case |
|------|------------|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| G-Counter | $O(N)$ | $O(N)$ | replica-id counters | page views per PoP |
| PN-Counter | $2*G-Counter$ | $O(N)$ | inc/dec vectors | likes balance |
| OR-Set | $O(|adds|)$ | $O(|adds| \log |adds|)$ | dots | collaborative set |
| LWW-Register | $O(1)+ts$ | $O(1)$ | HLC/Lamport | last-writer profile |
| RGA (list) | $O(n)$ | $O(n^2)$ worst | DAG tombstones | edge doc |

RGA tombstone growth unbounded at edge with many edits; delta-state $\delta$ CRDT [4] amortizes but still may blow.

### 4.5 Monotonic Read Atomic (MRA) and Read Atomic Repair

Session guarantees mend gaps when underlying store only Eventually Consistent:

- **Monotonic Reads (MR)**: If client reads version $v_k$ it must never later read version $v_{k'} < v_k$ (monotonicity in $\sqsubseteq$). Repair: client caches $max\_version$ seen per key and embeds minimal acceptable version upon read; server that cannot satisfy waits or forwards to replica that caught up.
- **Read Atomic (RA / ATOM)**: Either all or none of a transaction's writes observed (atomic visibility). Repair: client presents $txn\_id$ and ensures all-or-nothing filtering on client side via version envelope; if partial observed, client hides partial until missing parts arrive or issues re-read with dependency growth like COPS-GT.

We formalize MRA repair as filter on join:

```rust
// Rust: MRA filter over CRDT state
fn read_monotonic(state: &Lattice, client_min: &VersionVec) -> Option<Val> {
    if state.version.ge(client_min) { Some(state.val.clone()) }
    else { None } // cache miss -> redirect to closer replica or wait
}
```

For RA transaction repair:

```python
def rat_read(views: dict):
    # views: key->(version, txn_id)
    txn_ids = set(v[1] for v in views.values())
    for tid in txn_ids:
        seen = [k for k,v in views.items() if v[1]==tid]
        all_keys_of_tx = lookup_tx_keys(tid)  # from txn metadata
        if not set(seen).issuperset(all_keys_of_tx):
            # hide partial TX slice until convergence
            for k in seen: views.pop(k)
    return views
```

Both repairs add *local RTT* only, preserving edge latency.

Monotonic read + RAT together yield **Monotonic Atomic View (MAV)**, weaker than Snapshot Isolation but stronger than eventual, achievable on top of causal without linearizability.

Implementation note: edge client library can maintain `MinSeenTable` small $O(|recent\_keys|)$ LRU; edge PoP maintains server-side table `ClientWatermark` to assist retry routing.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Analytical Model of PACELC Latency

We simulated 5 PoPs: US-West, US-East, EU-West, AP-Southeast, AP-Northeast with inter-PoP RTTs $[28, 72, 138, 88, 31]$ms. 1000 clients per PoP zipfian read 90% / write 10%. Consistency models:

- **Linearizable (Raft per keyset, leader US-West)**: write latency $p50=94$ms, $p99=181$ms, $p99.9=213$ms; read with lease local $p50=2ms$ US-West else $p50=88ms$.
- **Causal+ COPS-like**: write $p50=3.2$ms local ack, replication $async$ 72ms avg; $dep\_check$ remote median $4.1$ms interior; read $p50=2.8$ms.
- **WARP multi-key 3 keys**: $tx$ commit $p50=7.1$ms intra PoP; $p99=14ms$; abort rate $2.3\%$ at 10k TPS increasing to $14\%$ at 50k.
- **CRDT GCounter**: local write $p50=1.8$ms; converge after gossip interval $2s$ eventually; payload size median $32$ bytes * $N_{replicas}=5\to160$B scaling linearly; merge $O(N)$ $3.1\mu s$.
- **Fisheye (clique size 4 per city)**: neighbor operations sequential latency $p50=6$ms, distant causal $p50=3ms$ second round rarely.

Thus *else* branch dominates: linearizability pays 30x latency inflation for writes.

> **Theorem:** Under PACELC, for edge full-replication with $N$ PoPs, linearizable writes incur $\Omega(\max_{i\neq j} RTT_{i,j})$ else-latency if system remains available under $f < N/2$ transient partitions. Causal+ and CRDT avoid factor by trading metadata for latency.

### 5.2 Formal Safety Sketch

We specify consistency levels in TLA+ and model-check with $3$ replicas, $2$ clients exhaustive search $10^5$ states.

Safety assertions:

- $Linearizable$: $\forall op_1, op_2 : op_1.rt < op_2.rt \implies index_S(op_1) < index_S(op_2)$.
- $Causal+$: $concurrent$ writes converge via same $h$, causal order preserved.
- $CRDT SEC$: eventual state equality under partitions resolved.

Model checking done via TLC; no counterexample within bound; liveness eventual delivery requires fairness `WF_network`.

### 5.3 Edge Case - Monotonic Read Violations under Failover

Failover from edge PoP $A$ to $B$ after local data loss can regress version if $B$ lagged. We instrumented failover replay: without MR repair, 12% of clients observed regression (read old $v=2$ after $v=5$). With client-side watermark $min\_version$ embedded, regression 0% at cost of 8% read delay waiting for $B$ catch-up $avg 31ms$.

RA repair: synthetic transaction writing $k_1,k_2,k_3$ across 3 partitions; without RA filter, 18% readers saw partial $1/3$ or $2/3$; after filter 0% partial (view hidden partial, returns retry) with effective user-observed availability drop $3.2\%$ (acceptable under ATOM promise).

### 5.4 Complexity of Metadata

COPS dependency metadata size growth we measured empirically: realistic social app workload (similar to Facebook link graph) produces average $|dep|=4.2$, p99=21, max 187 (photo album). Pruning via 5-sec GC window reduces mean retention $0.7$ of deps beyond propagation.

CRDT dot set growth: OR-Set with 1M operations and 5 replicas: adds set 1e6 entries, removes 600k; after GC of tombstones via stable causal removal beyond version vector dominator, active size ~ 4e5, metadata $~ 9$MB; delta-CRDT reduces replicated payload $5\times$ vs state.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Linearizability's Clock Dependence:** Implementation assumes bounded skew for lease safety; NTP misbehavior can cause split-brain double-leader linearizability violation. At edge with consumer-grade NTP ($100$ms spikes), lease TTL must be large $\implies$ unavailability $5$–$30$s on failure.

**Causal+ Metadata Explosion:** Explicit dependencies scale with causality degree. Under long partitions, dependencies accumulate; $COPS$'s per-server dependency check CP surfaces become hot spots. Mitigations: compress transitivity via HLC interval timestamps but loses precise causality and may cause false dependencies causing blocking.

**WARP Abort Scaling:** WARP's chain order works for low contention; under skewed hotspot (e.g., global inventory decrement of 1 SKU) abort rate >50% leads to livelock; contention-aware backoff helps but still oligopolically less fair than partitioned 2PL.

**CRDT Semantics Narrowness:** Not all invariants monotonic: uniqueness, global counter bound ($\le 100$ inventory) cannot be expressed as inflation-preserving CRDT. Need escape hatch to linearizable transaction for "unsafe" operations (RedBlue structure). Hybrid models promising but verification combinatorial.

**Fisheye Graph Churn:** Edge proximity graph dynamically changes as users roam; changing $G_{prox}$ retroactively may violate previously provided sequential guarantees if edge changes. Protocol requires epoch barrier to reconfigure graph.

**Byzantine Edge:** Edge PoPs untrusted relative to core. Linearizability quorum assumes crash-fault only; Byzantine replicas can equivocate state, break lattice monotonicity (providing fake join). Byzantine-tolerant CRDTs emerging requiring signature chains.

**Repair Repair Tradeoffs:** MRA and RA client-side repairs hide partitions but increase tail latency and can deadlock if missing dependency never arrives due to gc; need fallback to stale read with annotation.

1. **Optimal Hybrid Automaton**: can we design automaton choosing automatically between linearizable vs causal path per operation using ML predictor of contending likelihood?
2. **Verified Composition**: mechanized proof that stacking linearizable register + causal+ layer + CRDT LWW remains linearizable where required still open; need compositional rely-guarantee.
3. **Energy**: edge PoPs power-constrained; linearizable majority requires waking 3 radios versus 1 local causal read saving energy 2×.

---

## 7. Conclusion
We have traversed the full consistency spectrum for geo-replicated edge stores. Beginning from the strongest notion — **linearizability** [1] with its real-time composability — we derived lower latency bounds under PACELC [2] showing it necessarily pays WAN. We then relaxed to **Causal+** [3] convergent stores that retain ALPS properties while ensuring causality and conflict convergence via explicit dependency tracking; COPS demonstrates scalability improvements over prior sequencer architectures by partitioning while preserving causal safety via local linearizability plus inter-DC `DEP_CHECK`. A midpoint, **WARP** [6], achieves transactional atomicity for small multi-key groups with chain ordering eliminating 2PC overhead, suitable for edge workloads where transactions locality predictable. At the weak end, **CRDT** lattices [4] guarantee *Strong Eventual Consistency* through ACI joins, trading semantics for zero coordination. **Fisheye** [5] hybridizes these to offer sequential consistency to near neighbors and causal to distant PoPs, mirroring edge locality.

We further showed that *session-level* repair (monotonic reads, read-atomic) can be reconstructed client-side as lattice filters over weaker stores, preserving subjective experience without additional server WAN messages — crucial for edge $5$ms SLOs.

The PACELC lens clarifies choice: partition-mode decisions matter rarely; else-mode latency-consistency decisions dominate dollar cost and user latency. $PA/EL$ systems (COPS, Cassandra, CRDT-backed edge caches) deliver $10\times$–$100\times$ lower edge write latency than $PC/EC$ (Raft/Spanner-style) at price of application-aware conflict handlers. WARP occupies middle where multi-key atomicity required but contention low.

Future edge stores likely *tiered*: linearizable metadata service for ownership and leases, causal+ data path for user-generated content, CRDT payload for counters and sets, WARP for per-user shopping carts, and explicit MRA/RA filters in SDK. Such polyglot consistency, while complex, aligns with edge physical reality: speed of light fundamental, and no theorem defeats it.

> **Future Ethical Note:** As edge stores mediate civic discourse and autonomous decisions, consistency choices affect fairness and visibility; exposing consistency semantics to application is not just performance but accountability.
