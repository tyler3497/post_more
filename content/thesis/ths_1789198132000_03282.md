# The Langlands Program: Functoriality, the Geometric Correspondence, and the Fargues–Scholze Geometrization

## Abstract

The Langlands program proposes a deep correspondence between automorphic representations of reductive groups and Galois representations, unifying number theory, harmonic analysis, and algebraic geometry. This article develops the program from its classical origins — automorphic forms, L-functions, and the local–global principle — through its three great axes: local functoriality and endoscopic classification, the global functorial transfer mediated by the Arthur–Selberg trace formula, and the geometric Langlands correspondence of Beilinson–Drinfeld, which recasts Hecke eigensheaves on Bun_G as the spectral side of a categorical equivalence. We then examine the most transformative recent development: the Fargues–Scholze geometrization of the *local* Langlands correspondence, which constructs sheaves on the moduli stack Bun_G of the Fargues–Fontaine curve, proves a geometric Satake equivalence in mixed characteristic, and defines a spectral action of the category of perfect complexes on the stack of L-parameters, yielding a canonical candidate L-parameter for every smooth irreducible representation. We assess what is proved, what remains conjectural — including the categorical conjecture and its relation to endoscopy — and where the program is heading.

## 1 Intro

Few conjectural edifices in mathematics match the Langlands program for scope and persistence. Conceived in Robert Langlands's 1967 letter to André Weil, the program predicts that the analytic world of *automorphic forms* — functions on locally symmetric spaces with prescribed transformation laws — encodes the same information as the arithmetic world of *Galois representations* [1]. The bridge between them is functoriality: a homomorphism of L-groups ^L H → ^L G should induce a transfer of automorphic representations from H to G, subsuming essentially every reciprocity law and lifting problem in the theory of automorphic forms.

The program has since ramified into several mutually reinforcing branches. The *classical* axis concerns number fields and p-adic fields: local Langlands parametrizations of smooth representations of G(F_v), global functoriality for automorphic representations, and the analytic theory of L-functions — their Euler products, functional equations, and special values. The *geometric* axis, initiated by Drinfeld and developed by Beilinson–Drinfeld, replaces number fields by function fields of curves and Galois representations by local systems, turning the correspondence into an equivalence of *categories* — coherent sheaves on the stack of local systems versus D-modules on the moduli stack of G-bundles [5][6]. Most recently, the *geometrization* axis of Fargues and Scholze has imported geometric methods directly into the p-adic local theory, constructing a candidate local Langlands correspondence for *every* reductive group via sheaves on the Fargues–Fontaine curve [2].

This article is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews automorphic forms, L-functions, the dual group, and local versus global functoriality. Section 3 describes the methodological toolkit: the trace formula, geometric Satake, excursion operators, and categorical methods. Section 4 develops four deep dives — classical functoriality and endoscopy, the geometric Langlands correspondence, the Fargues–Scholze geometrization, and recent categorical and motivic advances. Section 5 discusses the empirical and proof-theoretic record: which theorems are now unconditional and which conjectures remain. Section 6 examines limitations, and Section 7 concludes with directions for future work.

## 2 Background

### Automorphic forms and L-functions

An automorphic representation π of a reductive group G over a number field F is an irreducible constituent of the space of functions on G(F)\G(A_F) satisfying growth and K-finiteness conditions. For G = GL_2 over Q, these recover classical modular forms; for GL_n, they are the natural habitat of the *standard L-function* L(s, π), defined by an Euler product over all places v,

> Definition: L(s, π) = ∏_v L(s, π_v),

where each local factor is determined by the local Langlands parameter of the component π_v [1]. The conjectural analytic properties — meromorphic continuation, functional equation, boundedness in vertical strips — generalize the classical theory of Dirichlet L-functions and are among the deepest open problems in the subject.

### The dual group and L-parameters

The organizing principle is the *Langlands dual group* Ĝ, the complex reductive group whose root datum is dual to that of G. The L-group is ^L G = Gal(Ḟ/F) ⋉ Ĝ. A *local L-parameter* is a homomorphism φ: W_F × SL_2(C) → ^L G (modulo conjugacy), where W_F is the Weil group; the SL_2(C) factor encodes monodromy (the Weil–Deligne representation). The *local Langlands conjecture* asserts a finite-to-one surjection

π ↦ φ_π

from irreducible smooth representations of G(F) to L-parameters, with fibers called *L-packets* [1]. The size and internal structure of L-packets is governed by the component group of the centralizer of φ — the subject of endoscopy.

### Local versus global functoriality

*Local functoriality* is the statement that an L-homomorphism ^L H → ^L G induces a transfer of L-packets, and hence of representations, from H(F_v) to G(F_v). *Global functoriality* asserts the analogous transfer of automorphic representations. Global functoriality implies spectacular consequences: the analytic continuation of L-functions, cases of the Ramanujan conjecture, and the Sato–Tate distribution of Frobenius eigenvalues. The principal tool for proving global functoriality has been the *Arthur–Selberg trace formula*, which compares the spectral expansion (automorphic representations) with the geometric expansion (orbital integrals) of a test function [7].

### Endoscopy

When G is not quasi-split, or when L-packets contain more than one element, the trace formula must be *stabilized*: orbital integrals are grouped into stable conjugacy classes, and the difference is expressed through *endoscopic groups* H — smaller quasi-split groups attached to elements of the dual group. The *fundamental lemma*, proved by Ngô, identifies the relevant orbital integrals, and Arthur's *endoscopic classification* for classical groups describes the discrete automorphic spectrum in terms of parameters factoring through endoscopic groups [7]. Endoscopy is thus the fine structure theory of functoriality: it explains multiplicities in the automorphic spectrum that naive functoriality cannot see.

## 3 Methodology

The modern Langlands toolkit blends analytic, algebraic, and categorical techniques. We highlight four pillars.

1. **The Arthur–Selberg trace formula and stabilization.** The trace formula equates a spectral sum over automorphic representations with a geometric sum over conjugacy classes. Stabilization rewrites the geometric side via endoscopic transfer, enabling comparison of trace formulas on different groups — the engine behind Arthur's classification and many cases of functoriality [7].

2. **Geometric Satake.** The classical Satake isomorphism identifies the spherical Hecke algebra of G(F_v) with the representation ring of Ĝ. Its geometrization — the *geometric Satake equivalence* — identifies perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with representations of Ĝ [3]. This is the bridge that lets representation-theoretic data (Hecke operators) be read off from geometry, and it appears in every modern advance, from Lafforgue's work to Fargues–Scholze [2][4].

3. **Excursion operators.** Introduced by V. Lafforgue in the function-field setting, excursion operators act on the cohomology of moduli spaces of *shtukas* and produce the *excursion algebra*, whose characters are exactly global L-parameters [4]. Fargues and Scholze transplanted this mechanism to the p-adic world: excursion operators on sheaves over Bun_G yield semisimple L-parameters for smooth representations of p-adic groups [2].

4. **Categorical and motivic methods.** The geometric Langlands program is formulated as an equivalence of ∞-categories; proving it requires derived algebraic geometry, higher category theory, and the theory of D-modules and factorization algebras [6]. Scholze's recent motivic extension replaces ℓ-adic sheaves by rigid-analytic motives, proving independence of ℓ of the Fargues–Scholze parameters [3].

A computational illustration: the Satake parameter of an unramified representation of GL_n(Q_p) can be extracted from Hecke eigenvalues. The following Python sketch mirrors the logic (for exposition only):

```python
def satake_parameter(hecke_eigenvalues, n, p):
    """Recover the conjugacy class in GL_n(C) from Hecke eigenvalues.
    hecke_eigenvalues[k] = eigenvalue of the k-th Hecke operator T_{p,k}.
    """
    import numpy as np
    # Elementary symmetric functions of the Satake parameters t_1..t_n
    e = [1.0] + [float(v) for v in hecke_eigenvalues]
    coeffs = [e[n - k] * ((-p) ** (-k * (n - k) / 2)) for k in range(n + 1)]
    roots = np.roots(coeffs[::-1])
    return sorted(roots, key=abs, reverse=True)  # the semisimple class diag(t_i)
```

The point is structural: Hecke eigenvalues *are* the coordinates of a conjugacy class in the dual group — the numerical shadow of the correspondence.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Classical functoriality, L-functions, and endoscopy

The classical program rests on two pillars: the *local* parametrization and *global* functorial transfer. For GL_n, both are theorems: Harris–Taylor and Henniart proved local Langlands for GL_n, and the global correspondence for GL_n over function fields is due to Drinfeld (n = 2) and L. Lafforgue (general n). For general reductive groups, the local correspondence is known in many cases — notably via Arthur's endoscopic classification for quasi-split classical groups — but remains open in full generality.

Endoscopy refines the picture decisively. The *stable* trace formula isolates the contribution of tempered L-packets, and the endoscopic character identities express individual characters within a packet in terms of stable characters on endoscopic groups [7]. Arthur's multiplicity formula then gives an explicit description of the discrete spectrum:

> Theorem: (Arthur, endoscopic classification) For quasi-split classical groups, the discrete automorphic spectrum is parametrized by global Arthur parameters — formal sums of cuspidal automorphic representations of GL_n twisted by representations of SL_2(C) — with explicit multiplicity given by characters of the global component group.

The SL_2(C) factor here is the *Arthur SL_2*, governing non-tempered (e.g., residual) spectrum. Recent work extends these ideas beyond quasi-split groups: Fargues's theory of rigid inner forms and the extended Fargues–Scholze spectral action aim at a categorical local Langlands statement valid for arbitrary connected reductive groups, including non-trivial inner forms of SL_n [2].

### 4.2 The geometric Langlands correspondence of Beilinson–Drinfeld

The geometric program begins with Drinfeld's insight: over a function field F = F_q(X) of a curve X, unramified automorphic forms become functions on Bun_G(F_q), and Galois representations become ℓ-adic local systems on X. Beilinson and Drinfeld *categorified* this: they conjectured an equivalence between the derived category of D-modules on Bun_G and the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on LocSys_Ĝ, the stack of Ĝ-local systems on X [5][6].

The construction of the automorphic-to-Galois direction rests on the *quantization of the Hitchin system*. Hitchin defined an integrable system h: T^*Bun_G → H_G, where H_G is an affine space (the Hitchin base). Beilinson and Drinfeld proved that the algebra of global differential operators on Bun_G (suitably twisted) is canonically isomorphic to functions on the space of *opers* — a Lagrangian section of the Hitchin fibration — thereby assigning to each oper a Hecke eigensheaf, i.e., a D-module on Bun_G with prescribed Hecke eigenvalues [5]. Bezrukavnikov and Travkin later gave a short proof for GL_n by reduction to positive characteristic, deducing the result from generic Langlands duality [5].

The *categorical* conjecture, made precise by Arinkin–Gaitsgory, asserts an equivalence of ∞-categories

> Conjecture: L_G: QCoh(LocSys_Ĝ) ≃ D-mod(Bun_G),

compatible with Hecke actions on both sides [6]. Gaitsgory outlined a proof strategy for GL_2, and the subject has now reached a historic milestone: the *unramified* geometric Langlands conjecture has been proved by Gaitsgory–Raskin with Arinkin, Beraldo, Campbell, Chen, Færgeman, Lin, and Rozenblyum, in a series of papers and a two-volume monograph building on Lurie's higher algebra and two decades of preparatory work [6]. The ramified case — allowing singularities of local systems at marked points — remains largely open and is the current frontier.

### 4.3 The Fargues–Scholze geometrization of local Langlands

The most radical recent advance transplants geometric Langlands ideas into the *p-adic* local setting. Fargues and Scholze work with the *Fargues–Fontaine curve* X — a one-dimensional "curve" attached to a p-adic field whose geometry mirrors that of a smooth projective curve over a finite field — and the moduli stack Bun_G of G-bundles on it [2]. Their achievements include:

- **A category of ℓ-adic sheaves** D_lis(Bun_G, Q̄_ℓ) with a well-behaved six-functor formalism.
- **Geometric Satake over the Fargues–Fontaine curve**, identifying Hecke operators with representations of the dual group.
- **The stack of L-parameters** Z^1(W_F, Ĝ)/Ĝ and its ring of functions — the *spectral Bernstein center*.
- **The spectral action**: a monoidal action of Perf(Z^1(W_F, Ĝ)/Ĝ) on D_lis(Bun_G), categorifying the classical Bernstein center action [2].
- **Excursion operators** producing, for every Schur-irreducible object — in particular every smooth irreducible representation π of G(F) — a semisimple L-parameter φ_π, compatibly with the classical correspondence for GL_n [2].

In particular, Fargues–Scholze construct a *candidate* local Langlands correspondence

LLC^FS_G: Π(G) → Φ^ss(G), π ↦ φ_π

for *every* connected reductive group G over a p-adic field, with the expected compatibilities (products, twists, central characters). They also prove deep finiteness theorems for the cohomology of local Shimura varieties and moduli of local shtukas as applications [2].

### 4.4 Categorical conjectures, motivic refinements, and recent progress

The Fargues–Scholze construction is expected to be the shadow of a *categorical local Langlands conjecture*: an equivalence between D_lis(Bun_G) and a suitable category of sheaves on the stack of L-parameters, intertwining the spectral action. Formulating the precise statement — including the correct "spectral" category with nilpotent singular support conditions — is active research; recent work of Dillery–Eteve extends the spectral action to the stack Bun_G^e of torsors over the Kaletha gerbe, yielding the first categorical statements for groups that are not extended pure inner forms of a quasi-split group, such as non-trivial inner forms of SL_n [2].

A second major advance is *motivic*: Scholze has extended the geometrization from ℓ-adic to motivic sheaves using the formalism of rigid-analytic motives, proving that the Fargues–Scholze L-parameters are *independent of ℓ* — a property long expected of any true Langlands parametrization but previously inaccessible [3]. A forthcoming survey of the geometric correspondence emphasizes the unifying role of *factorization algebras* — the "hidden commutativity" underlying both the classical Hecke algebra and its geometric upgrades — as the beating heart of the subject [6].

Finally, the interaction with endoscopy persists at the categorical level: Arthur packets are expected to arise from natural operations on the spectral side (e.g., pushing forward skyscraper sheaves along singular loci of the parameter stack), and conjectural constructions of Arthur packets within the Fargues–Scholze framework are now being proposed [2].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

What is the *proof-theoretic* status of the program? It is useful to sort claims into three tiers.

| Tier | Claim | Status |
|------|-------|--------|
| Proved | Local Langlands for GL_n (Harris–Taylor, Henniart) | Theorem |
| Proved | Global Langlands for GL_n over function fields (Drinfeld, L. Lafforgue) | Theorem |
| Proved | Automorphic-to-Galois direction for general G over function fields (V. Lafforgue, via shtukas and excursion operators) [4] | Theorem |
| Proved | Fundamental lemma (Ngô); endoscopic classification for quasi-split classical groups (Arthur) [7] | Theorem |
| Proved | Unramified categorical geometric Langlands (Gaitsgory–Raskin et al.) [6] | Theorem |
| Proved | Fargues–Scholze: geometric Satake over the Fargues–Fontaine curve, spectral action, excursion parameters for all G [2] | Theorem |
| Proved | Independence of ℓ of Fargues–Scholze parameters (Scholze, motivic) [3] | Theorem |
| Conjectural | Full categorical local Langlands equivalence (precise spectral category) | Open |
| Conjectural | Ramified geometric Langlands in full generality | Open |
| Conjectural | Global functoriality for general G over number fields | Open |

The "empirical" side of the program is largely *numerical verification* of its predictions: Sato–Tate distributions of Frobenius traces for modular forms and higher-rank groups have been confirmed computationally to high precision; special values of L-functions match predictions of the Bloch–Kato and Beilinson conjectures in extensive tables; and the Fargues–Scholze parameters have been checked to agree with all previously known cases (Harris–Taylor for GL_n, Arthur's classification, the local Langlands correspondence for classical groups). Each new theorem in the area is typically accompanied by compatibility checks against this accumulated numerical evidence.

A useful formal perspective comes from type theory. The functoriality principle can be encoded as a dependent-function principle — a transfer along L-homomorphisms:

```haskell
-- Functoriality as a type-theoretic principle (schematic)
type LGroup g = WeilGroup -> DualGroup g   -- L-parameters as morphisms

functorialTransfer :: (LHomomorphism h g) -> AutomorphicRep h -> AutomorphicRep g
functorialTransfer f pi = transferAlong f (parameterOf pi)
-- The content: parameterOf (functorialTransfer f pi) == f . parameterOf pi
```

The equation in the comment *is* the functoriality conjecture: transfer of representations corresponds to composition of parameters.

## 6 Limitations

The program's limitations are as instructive as its triumphs.

1. **Global functoriality over number fields remains wide open.** Beyond GL_n and classical groups (via Arthur), functoriality for exceptional groups, and even many cases of tensor-product functoriality, are unknown. The trace formula approach faces the formidable analytic difficulty of the *beyond-endoscopy* program proposed by Langlands: isolating functorial transfers requires a "beyond endoscopic" decomposition of the trace formula that exists only in nascent form.

2. **The Galois-to-automorphic direction.** Lafforgue's theorem proves automorphic → Galois over function fields; the converse — constructing automorphic forms from Galois representations (the function-field analogue of modularity lifting) — remains open in general. Similarly, the Fargues–Scholze construction produces parameters from representations, not representations from parameters.

3. **The categorical conjectures are not yet precisely stated in full generality.** The expected equivalence on the spectral side requires a delicate choice of category (ind-coherent sheaves with nilpotent singular support, in the global geometric case); for the p-adic categorical conjecture, even the correct formulation for non-quasi-split groups is recent work in progress [2].

4. **Effectivity and computability.** The Fargues–Scholze parameters are defined via excursion operators on enormous categories; extracting an explicit parameter for a given supercuspidal representation remains impractical. The program gives structural understanding, not algorithms.

5. **The analytic theory of L-functions.** Meromorphic continuation and functional equation are known only for L-functions attached to representations known to be functorial transfers from GL_n. The general conjectures (e.g., for general Spin groups) remain out of reach, limiting the program's analytic payoff.

6. **Dependencies on heavy foundations.** The recent breakthroughs lean on Lurie's higher topos theory, condensed mathematics, and perfectoid geometry — foundations whose very statements require substantial machinery to parse, raising the barrier to entry and to independent verification.

## 7 Conclusion

The Langlands program has evolved from a letter about L-functions into a sprawling, multi-front research program whose recent progress exceeds what seemed plausible a decade ago. Three developments stand out: the *proof of unramified geometric Langlands*, completing a thirty-year categorical vision [6]; the *Fargues–Scholze geometrization*, which gives the first uniform construction of local Langlands parameters for all reductive p-adic groups [2]; and the *motivic refinement*, proving the long-expected independence of ℓ [3]. Together with Lafforgue's function-field parametrization [4] and Arthur's endoscopic classification [7], they amount to the most productive period in the program's history.

The central challenges ahead are clear: formulate and prove the categorical local Langlands equivalence, extend geometric Langlands to the ramified setting, develop beyond endoscopy into a working tool for global functoriality over number fields, and close the Galois-to-automorphic gap. Each will likely require new ideas of the same magnitude as excursion operators and the Fargues–Fontaine curve. The program's history suggests a pattern: every time the correspondence is geometrized — Drinfeld's shtukas, Beilinson–Drinfeld's D-modules, Fargues–Scholze's diamonds — new theorems follow. The next geometrization may be the one that finally captures functoriality itself.
