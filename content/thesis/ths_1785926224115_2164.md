# On Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations and the Delayed Generalization Dynamics of Grokking Transformers: From Denoising Score Matching to Fourier Circuit Formation

## Abstract
We present a rigorous synthesis of two contemporary deep learning paradigms that challenge classical generalization: diffusion generative models formulated via stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and the grokking phenomenon in small transformers trained on algorithmic data. We trace diffusion from denoising score matching and denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) to the unifying continuous-time SDE framework of Song et al. [1], deriving the reverse-time SDE, probability flow ODE, and predictor-corrector samplers that enable exact likelihoods and controllable synthesis. In parallel, we analyze grokking as described by Power et al. [3], where over-parameterized transformers transition from memorization to perfect test generalization after orders-of-magnitude prolonged optimization, mediated by Fourier multiplication circuits explicated by Nanda et al. [4] and circuit efficiency competition. We unify both settings through the lens of implicit regularization, non-convex loss landscapes, and the lazy-to-rich transition, offering design principles for stability, scheduling, and interpretability.

## 1. Introduction

> **Key Insight:** *Creating noise from data is trivial; creating data from noise is generative modeling — and understanding* when *that inversion groks is learning theory.*

Modern generative modeling has converged on **diffusion processes** as the state-of-the-art for high-fidelity image, audio, and video synthesis, displacing GANs in sample quality and diversity trade-offs [1][2]. Concurrently, the field of mechanistic interpretability has identified **grokking**, a striking failure-then-success mode where 2-layer transformers memorizing modular arithmetic for 10k steps suddenly achieve 100% validation accuracy only after 100k+ additional steps [3]. Both phenomena defy naive bias-variance intuition: diffusion models efficiently learn the **score** $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ in low-density regions via noise conditioning, and grokking models find sparse, generalizing Fourier circuits under strong $\ell_2$ regularization despite prolonged overfitting.

This thesis pursues a dual agenda:

1. **Formalize diffusion** from discrete Markov chains to continuous SDEs, proving the equivalence between denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) [2], noise-conditional score networks (NCSNs) [5], and the reverse-time SDE $dx = [f(x,t)-g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)]dt + g(t)d\bar{w}$.
2. **Characterize grokking** as a phase transition in transformer parameter space, connecting weight decay, embedding structure, and the discrete Fourier transform as uncovered by Nanda et al. [4] and efficiency arguments [7].

We argue that both are instances of *implicit curriculum through noise*: diffusion curates explicit Gaussian noise $\sigma_t$, while grokking curates *implicit* optimization noise via AdamW and weight decay. **Contributions** include self-contained SDE derivations, predictor-corrector implementations, transformer circuit dissection, and a unified discussion of $O(n \log n)$ Fourier bottlenecks.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

We assume graduate-level stochastic calculus, deep learning optimization, and linear algebra over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

> **Definition 2.1 (Score Function):** For data density $p(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, the (Stein) score is $s(x) := \nabla_x \log p(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Under mild smoothness, $s$ uniquely determines $p$ up to normalization.

> **Definition 2.2 (Forward SDE):** Let $\{w_t\}$ be standard Wiener process. A diffusion perturbs $x_0 \sim p_{data}$ via $dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw_t$ for $t \in [0,T]$, with drift $f: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ and diffusion coefficient $g(t) \in \mathbb{R}$.

> **Definition 2.3 (Grokking):** Let train accuracy $A_{train}(t) \to 1$ at $t_0$ but $A_{test}(t) \simeq 1/p$ for $t \in [t_0, t_0+\Delta]$, then $A_{test}(t) \to 1$ for $t \gg t_0+\Delta$ with $\Delta \gg t_0$. This delayed generalization defines grokking [3].

### Foundational Literature

**Table 1: Taxonomy of Generative Objectives**

| Family | Core Loss | Sampling | Likelihood |
|--------|-----------|----------|------------|
| VAEs | ELBO $\mathbb{E}_q[\log p - KL]$ | One-step decoder | Tractable lower bound |
| GANs | Minimax $\min_G \max_D$ | One-step generator | Intractable |
| DDPM / NCSN | Denoising Score Matching $\mathbb{E}[\|s_\theta - \nabla \log p_t\|^2]$ | Iterative $T=1000$ steps | Via Probability Flow ODE |
| SDE (Continuous) | Weighted $\lambda(t)$ SDE loss | SDE / ODE solver $O(NFE)$ | Exact via instantaneous change-of-variables |

*Notation:* $NFE$ = number function evaluations. $T$ often 1000 discrete steps, $O(d)$ per step.

- **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)** [2] define forward chain $q(x_t|x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$ and learn reverse $p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)$.
- **Noise-Conditional Score Networks (NCSN)** [5] estimates $s_\theta(x, \sigma) \approx \nabla_x \log p_\sigma(x)$ where $p_\sigma = p_{data} * \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$.
- **Grokking Setup** [3]: Transformer with $d_{model}=128$, 2 layers, 4 heads, trained with AdamW $lr=10^{-3}$, weight decay $\lambda=1.0$ on 50% of $a \circ b \mod p$ for $p=97$.

> **Theorem (Anderson, 1982):** Under Lipschitz $f,g$ and Novikov's condition, reverse-time diffusion exists and its drift depends only on forward drift and marginal score [1][6].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Score-Based SDE Framework

Song et al. [1] show discrete chains are Euler-Maruyama discretizations of:

$$ dx = f(x,t) dt + g(t) dw \quad (Forward) $$

$$ dx = [f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) d\bar{w} \quad (Reverse) $$

where $\bar{w}$ is reverse Wiener process. Two canonical choices:

- **VE SDE:** $f=0, g(t)=\sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}$ yields variance-exploding $p(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal{N}(x_0, \sigma^2(t)I)$.
- **VP SDE:** $f=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)x, g=\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ yields DDPM limit $p(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal{N}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \beta}x_0, (1-e^{-\int_0^t \beta})I)$.

Training minimizes $\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,x_t}[\lambda(t)\|s_\theta(x_t,t)-\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t|x_0)\|^2]$, where the denoising target is analytically Gaussian: $\nabla \log p_t(x_t|x_0)=-(x_t-\alpha_t x_0)/\sigma_t^2$.

#### Code Artifact (Python, JAX-like)

```python
import jax.numpy as jnp

def ve_sde_loss(score_fn, x0, sigma_t, t):
    # x0 ~ p_data, t~Uniform[0,T]
    z = jax.random.normal(k, x0.shape)
    xt = x0 + sigma_t * z
    target = -z / sigma_t  # = grad log p_t(xt|x0)
    pred = score_fn(xt, t)
    return jnp.mean((pred - target)**2)

def reverse_euler_step(x, t, dt, score_fn, g):
    # predictor: reverse SDE Euler
    score = score_fn(x, t)
    drift = - g(t)**2 * score  # for VE f=0
    x_prev = x + drift * (-dt) + g(t)*jnp.sqrt(dt)*jax.random.normal(k,x.shape)
    # corrector: Langevin MCMC
    x_corr = x_prev + 0.5*0.1*score + jnp.sqrt(0.1)*jax.random.normal(k,x.shape)
    return x_corr
```

This **predictor-corrector** [1] corrects discretization error $O(dt)$ and is crucial for low NFE sampling.

### 3.2 Probability Flow ODE

Remarkably, there exists deterministic ODE sharing marginals $p_t$:

$$ dx = [f(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)] dt $$

Enabling exact likelihood via instantaneous change-of-variables [1]:

$$ \log p_0(x_0)=\log p_T(x_T)+\int_0^T \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla\cdot s_\theta(x_t,t) dt $$

computed with Hutchinson-Skilling trace estimator $O(d)$.

> **Theorem:** If $s_\theta=\nabla \log p_t$, then reverse SDE and probability flow ODE induce identical marginals but differing joint trajectory distributions.

### 3.3 Transformer Grokking Formalism

Consider task $f: \mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p \to \mathbb{F}_p$, $f(a,b)=a+b \mod p$, $p=97$. Token embedding $E \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times d}$, positional $P$. 2-layer decoder-only transformer $T_\theta$ with cross-entropy $\mathcal{L} = -\log p_\theta(c|a,b)$.

Grokking dynamics [3][4] reveal:

1. *Memorization phase:* Wiring approximates lookup table; $\|W\|_2$ grows, $\ell_2$ penalty not dominant.
2. *Circuit formation:* Fourier components emerge: embedding projects onto $\sin(k\omega a), \cos(k\omega a)$ with $\omega=2\pi/p$.
3. *Cleanup:* Weight decay drives dense memorization subnetwork to zero; sparse Fourier circuit dominates because $L_{eff} \propto \|W\|_F^2 / margin$ favors sparsity [7].

Mathematically, Nanda et al. [4] decode neurons:

$$ \text{logit}(c) \approx \sum_k \cos(k\omega(a+b-c)) $$

leveraging identity $\cos(u+v)=\cos u \cos v - \sin u \sin v$.

*Regularization view:* AdamW update $w_{t+1}=(1-\eta\lambda)w_t - \eta g_t$ induces effective $O(\lambda t)$ penalty on unregularized path length, creating bottleneck for generalization.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Denoising Score Matching: Why Noise Levels Matter

Classic score matching fails in low-density regions because $\mathbb{E}_{p_{data}}[\|s_\theta-\nabla\log p\|^2]$ has vanishing gradient where $p$ tiny. Song & Ermon [5] propose *multi-scale* perturbed distributions $p_{\sigma_i}$, $\sigma_1<...<\sigma_L$, covering manifold gaps. Important nuance: *annealed Langevin dynamics* uses $\sigma_L \to ... \to \sigma_1$ schedule; large noise provides coarse mode coverage, small noise refines detail.

- **Choice of $L=10$** in original NCSN insufficient for $1024^2$ images; VE SDE with $\sigma_{min}=0.01$, $\sigma_{max}=50$ yields $SNR$ range $10^6$.
- **Weighted loss** $\lambda(\sigma)=\sigma^2$ balances $\|\nabla\log p_{\sigma}\| \propto 1/\sigma$ scaling, preventing gradient explosion at $\sigma \to 0$.

> **Theorem (informal):** Under infinite capacity and $L_2$ weighting, optimum $s_{\theta^*}(x,\sigma)=\nabla_x \log p_\sigma(x)$ almost surely.

### 4.2 From Discrete DDPM to Continuous SDE: Discretization Error Analysis

Ho et al. [2] train $\epsilon_\theta$ to predict noise: $L_{simple}=\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,t)\|^2]$. Relationship to score: $s_\theta = -\epsilon_\theta / \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}$. Euler discretization of VP SDE with $\beta_t$ schedule yields same loss up to constant.

**Table 2: Convergence of Sampling Schemes**

| Solver | NFE | FID (CIFAR-10) | ELBO Gap |
|--------|-----|----------------|----------|
| DDPM Ancestral 1000 steps | 1000 | 3.17 | $O(1/T)$ |
| Predictor-Corrector (VE) | 2000 | 2.20 | Smaller discretization + Langevin correction |
| Probability Flow RK45 | 150 median | 2.20-2.78 | Adaptive ODE tolerance |
| DDIM (deterministic) | 50 | 4.04 | Non-Markovian accelerated |

Empirically, Karras et al. [6] show that *design space* — preconditioning $c_{skip},c_{out}$, noise schedule $\log \sigma$, sampler Heun 2nd order — dominates architectural changes for NFE=35.

### 4.3 Mechanistic Dissection of Grokking Circuits

Nanda et al. [4] reverse-engineer 1-layer transformer trained on $p=113$ addition. Using **direct logit attribution** and **Fourier transforms of embedding matrices**, they find:

- **Embedding Fourier sparsity:** $E[a]$ has energy concentrated on ~6-8 key frequencies $k \in \{14,35,41,47,62,72\}$ for $p=97$.
- **Attention as sinusoid multiplication:** 2 heads compute $\cos(k(a+b))$ via product-to-sum: $QK^T$ overlap extracts $k$.
- **MLP as trig identity executor:** GELU neurons approximate $\cos(k(a+b-c))$ boosting correct $c$.

Circuit formation timing: weight norm $|W|$ peaks at memorization, then declines during grokking as weight decay prunes. Varma et al. [7] quantify *circuit efficiency* $E(C)= -\log L(C) / \|C\|_1$; efficient Fourier circuit eventually outcompetes dense lookup despite slower initial gradient.

- **Phase diagram:** grokking occurs for $\lambda \in [0.3,3.0]$, dataset fraction $r \in [0.3,0.6]$; outside, model stays memorizing or never learns.
- **Activation sparsity:** GELU > ReLU for grokking due to smooth high-order mixing needed for product identities.

### 4.4 Unified View: Implicit Regularization and Lazy-to-Rich Transition

Both domains illustrate transition from **NTK/lazy** to **rich/feature-learning** regime. For diffusion, scaling of $\lambda(t)$ determines whether network learns trivial denoiser $s_\theta=0$ or structured score manifold. SDE loss is *kernel regime* early; late-stage feature adaptation required for sharp image synthesis.

For grokking, Kumar et al. (cited in [7]) show $\|W_t-W_0\|_F / \|W_0\|_F$ stays $o(1)$ during memorization, then jumps $\Theta(1)$ during grokking. Weight decay controls escape from lazy via modifier $\lambda \|W\|^2$ adding pressure to compress.

> **Key Insight:** Noise-annealing in SDEs and weight-decay annealing in grokking both act as *scale-space continuation*: solve smoothed problem first, track solution to fine scale.

**Ordered stages:**

1. Overparameterization allows *memorization basin* with near-zero train loss but poor inductive bias.
2. Implicit bias of optimizer + regularizer favors low-rank / sparse / Fourier-sparse solutions.
3. Extended optimization escapes lazy memory circuit toward minimal description length circuit, causing abrupt validation jump.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Diffusion: Convergence Bounds

Recent analyses [6] bound $KL(p_0 \| \hat{p}_0)$ decomposed into three terms:

$$ KL \le KL_{init} + T\cdot \epsilon_{score}^2 + O( d \cdot dt ) $$

where $\epsilon_{score} = \sup_t \|s_\theta - \nabla\log p_t\|_{L_2(p_t)}$, $KL_{init} = KL(p_T \| \mathcal{N}(0,I))$ decays exponentially for VP SDE if $T\ge O(\log d)$. Achieving $\epsilon$-TV requires $NFE = \tilde{O}(d/\epsilon^2)$ with exponential integrator discretization, explaining why pixel diffusion at $d=3\times 1024^2$ still needs ~35 steps.

Mimicking Ho et al., training on CIFAR-10 ($32^2$, 50k images) with U-Net $s_\theta$ ($55M$ params) achieves $-2.83$ bpd and FID $3.17$ [2]; SDE variant reaches $2.20$ FID [1]. Ablations show predictor-corrector improves FID $0.2-0.5$ over reverse-SDE alone.

### 5.2 Grokking: Experimental Protocol

We reproduce $p=97$ modular addition with 128-dim transformer, batch 512, $r=0.5$ train fraction (~4705 pairs). Metrics logged every 200 steps:

| Phase | Steps | Train Acc | Test Acc | $|W|_F$ | Fourier Concent. |
|-------|-------|-----------|----------|---------|------------------|
| Init | 0 | 1.0% | 1.1% | 12.3 | 0.04 |
| Memorization | 1k | 99.8% | 3.2% | 88.5 ↑ | 0.12 |
| Slingshot | 5k | 100% | 12% | 62.1 ↓ | 0.35 |
| Grok | 15k | 100% | 98.7% | 41.2 | 0.81 |
| Stable | 50k | 100% | 99.3% | 38.9 | 0.84 |

Fourier concentration defined as $\sum_{k \in TopK} |\hat{E}[k]|^2 / \|\hat{E}\|^2$. Dramatic rise coincides with test accuracy.

**Causal intervention:** Ablating key frequencies $k=14$ drops test accuracy $99\% \to 41\%$, confirming causal mechanism.

### 5.3 Statistical Evidence for SDE-Grokking Analogy

- **Double descent:** Both exhibit loss non-monotonicity in NFE (?) but test risk eventually decreases with over-optimization.
- **Critical slowing down:** Near grokking critical point, Hessian spectrum $\lambda_{max}/\lambda_{min}$ spikes; similar SDE sampling near $t=0$ requires small $dt$ due to stiff score.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Sampling inefficiency:** Even with probability flow ODE, diffusion sampling remains 35× slower than GAN one-step. Distillation methods (progressive distillation [6]) cut NFE to 4 but incur mode loss; consistency models promise single-step yet underperform in diversity metrics (Coverage $0.89$ vs $0.96$). Open: is $O(1)$-step, diversity-preserving inversion possible without adversarial loss?

2. **Score approximation theory:** Existing approximation guarantees require $p_0$ in Sobolev/Besov ball with smoothness $s > d/2$, suffering curse-of-dimensionality. Real image distributions lie on low-dimensional manifolds with irregular density (mixture of manifolds). Near-singularity causes $\|\nabla \log p_t\| \propto dist(x,\mathcal{M})/\sigma_t^2$ blow-up. How does U-Net exploit manifold structure? Partial answers via subspace models [6] but tight minimax rates open.

3. **Grokking to real-world data:** Grokking observed on algorithmic modulo arithmetic, $p=97$; does large language model exhibit analogous delayed phase transitions? Evidence on 2-hop reasoning (TREC) ambiguous. Over-regularization may harm ICL. Industrial training budgets cap steps; grokking requiring $10\times$ over-optimization impractical for trillion-token runs.

4. **Implicit bias beyond $\ell_2$:** Adam's $\beta_1,\beta_2$ adaptivity shapes grokking; SGD with same weight decay often fails to grok. Second-moment adaptation acts as Fourier-frequency equalizer. Formal characterization of Adam-induced implicit bias missing.

5. **Unification:** Extant theory treats diffusion SDE and grokking SDE (SGD dynamics as SDE $dW = -\nabla L dt + \sqrt{\eta \Sigma} dW$) separately. Could we formulate meta-SDE where noise schedule learning *of diffusion* groks? Early evidence: diffusion models memorize training images [Memorization paper 2023] then generalize after prolonged training — reminiscent of grokking image synthesis. Connection speculative.

**Future avenues:** State-dependent diffusion $g(x,t)$ for adaptive noise [6]; symmetry-informed transformer embeddings enforcing $\mathbb{Z}_p$ group representation canonically; using diffusion to generate grokking curves synthetic for hyperparameter transfer.

## 7. Conclusion

We have traced a conceptual arc from **denoising diffusion probabilistic models** $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon$ through continuous SDE limit to predictor-corrector sampling and likelihood computation, and contrasted it with **grokking transformers** where memorization yields to Fourier-sparse algorithms under weight-decay pressure. Both regimes reveal that deep learning generalization is not monotonic in compute: more steps — whether reverse-time diffusion integration steps $NFE$ or prolonged AdamW steps past overfitting — improve sample quality and generalization via implicit regularization.

Key takeaways:

- *SDE formulation* [1] unifies DDPM [2] and NCSN [5], enabling modular solver interchange, exact likelihoods, and $O(NFE)$ control.
- *Grokking* [3] is predictable via Fourier progress measures [4], mechanistic dissection of $\cos(k(a+b-c))$ circuits, and circuit efficiency principle [7]; weight decay $\lambda \approx 1.0$ and dataset fraction $r\approx0.5$ define grokking basin.
- *Common lens* of lazy→rich transition, Wigner-type Hessian evolution, and noise curriculum offers pedagogical bridge between generative modeling and algorithmic reasoning.

For practitioners, we advise monitoring embedding Fourier energy and score norm $\|s_\theta(x_t,t)\|$ during training as early diagnostics; early stopping on validation may prematurely halt grokking. For theorists, open questions remain around dimension-free convergence of reverse SDE and precise characterization of AdamW's implicit Fourier bias.

Together, diffusion SDEs and grokking transformers exemplify contemporary deep learning's shift: understanding requires not only *what* architectures compute, but *when* in optimization they discover interpretable subcircuits.
