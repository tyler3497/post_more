# Memory Consistency Models: the C++11 Atomics Axiomatics, the Out-of-Thin-Air Problem, and the RC11 Repair

## Abstract

The C++11 memory model gave the first mathematically rigorous semantics to shared-memory concurrency in a mainstream systems language, promising sequential consistency to data-race-free programs while exposing a ladder of relaxed atomics for experts. Its axiomatic formulation — candidate executions filtered by coherence, happens-before, and SC axioms — was a landmark, yet it concealed two deep defects: sequentially consistent atomics did not actually deliver sequential consistency, with both published Power compilation schemes unsound; and the axioms admitted out-of-thin-air executions, in which values materialize through circular causal justification, destroying the DRF guarantee the model was built to provide. This thesis reconstructs the C++11 axiomatic core from events and relations, exhibits the out-of-thin-air pathology through the load-buffering litmus test, and presents RC11 — the repaired model of Lahav, Vafeiadis, Kang, Hur, and Dreyer — with its corrected SC axiomatization, strengthened release sequences, no-thin-air acyclicity axiom, and verified compilation to x86-TSO, Power, and ARM. We close with the model's limitations and its operational successor, the Promising Semantics.

---

## 1. Introduction

Few problems in programming-language semantics have resisted solution as stubbornly as the meaning of concurrent memory access. Sequential consistency (SC), introduced by Lamport in 1979, remains the gold standard of intuition: *"the result of any execution is the same as if the operations of all the processors were executed in some sequential order, and the operations of each individual processor appear in this sequence in the order specified by its program."* Programmers reason as though memory were a single global array and threads took turns. Unfortunately, no mainstream multiprocessor implements this fiction: store buffers, speculation, and non-multi-copy-atomic stores expose weaker behaviors, with compilers adding reorderings of their own.

A programming language must therefore define its *own* memory model — a contract mediating between the reasoning of programmers, the transformations of compilers, and the realities of hardware. C++11 was the first mainstream language to attempt this rigorously. Developed through Boehm and Adve [5] and formalized by Batty et al. [2], it offered a celebrated two-tier bargain:

- **Ordinary code** that is *data-race-free* behaves as though sequentially consistent. This is the DRF-SC guarantee, the load-bearing promise of the entire model.
- **Expert code** — lock-free libraries, synchronization primitives — may use *relaxed atomics* with explicitly weaker ordering, trading reasoning power for performance.

This thesis argues that the original bargain was broken in two precise ways, and shows how it was repaired: §2 recalls the landscape, §3 the axiomatic methodology, §4 the deep dive (axiomatic core, release sequences, out-of-thin-air, RC11), §5 the evidence, §6 the limitations, §7 the conclusion.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Sequential consistency and why hardware abandoned it

Consider the *store buffering* (SB) litmus test, the canonical SC violation:

```cpp
// x = y = 0, both std::atomic<int>
// Thread 0:                  // Thread 1:
x.store(1, std::memory_order_relaxed);   y.store(1, std::memory_order_relaxed);
int r1 = y.load(std::memory_order_relaxed);  int r2 = x.load(std::memory_order_relaxed);
```

Under SC, `r1 == 0 && r2 == 0` is impossible: every interleaving orders at least one store before a load. On x86 it is routinely observable via store buffers — architects accepted the reorderings for their enormous performance gains and pushed the pain upward to language designers.

### 2.2 A zoo of hardware models

Hardware models differ in *which* reorderings they permit and whether stores are *multi-copy atomic* (visible to all cores simultaneously).

| Architecture | Store buffering (SB) | Load buffering (LB) | Multi-copy atomic |
|---|---|---|---|
| x86-TSO | observable | forbidden | yes |
| IBM Power / ARMv7 | observable | observable (via speculation) | **no** |
| ARMv8 | observable | observable (via speculation) | yes |

x86-TSO received a rigorous programmer's model from Sewell et al.; Power and ARMv7 were characterized by Alglave, Maranget, and colleagues ("herding cats") [6]. The lesson for language design: a portable model must admit *at least* the union of targeted hardware behaviors while remaining intelligible to programmers.

### 2.3 The road to C++11

Java's memory model had shown how badly this could go: its causality requirements yielded a specification researchers debated for a decade. For C++, Boehm and Adve's "Foundations of the C++ Concurrency Memory Model" (PLDI 2008) [5] established DRF-SC as the design center, and Batty et al. produced the first complete mathematical semantics in Isabelle/HOL with the *Cppmem* explorer [2] — a formalization that exposed defects in the draft standard and motivated fixes, a pattern RC11 would repeat.

## 3. Methodology

The methodology of this thesis, and of the literature it surveys, is *axiomatic modeling*:

1. **Candidate executions.** A program's meaning is a set of candidate executions. Each execution is a graph: *events* (individual reads, writes, read-modify-writes, fences, each tagged with a location, a value, and a memory order) connected by *relations* — program order, reads-from, modification order, and the SC order.
2. **Consistency axioms.** A candidate execution is *consistent* (a permitted behavior) only if it satisfies a battery of axioms: coherence conditions, happens-before acyclicity, and SC restrictions. The program's semantics is the set of consistent executions.
3. **Litmus tests as the empirical unit.** Tiny programs (2–4 threads, a handful of accesses) with a *question* — "is this outcome permitted?" — serve as regression tests for the model. Suites of hundreds of litmus tests discriminate between candidate models far more sharply than prose.
4. **Executable models.** The `.cat` language of the *herd7* tool [6][7] turns axiomatic definitions into checkable artifacts: a `.cat` file *is* the model, and litmus outcomes are computed, not argued. Cppmem [2] played the same role for the original C++11 formalization.
5. **Compilation as the soundness criterion.** A model is *sound* for a hardware target only if every behavior of the compiled program corresponds to a consistent source execution. A model that cannot be compiled is a fantasy; an unsound compilation scheme is a bug — and C++11 shipped with such bugs.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The C++11 axiomatic core: events, relations, and coherence

Every C++11 candidate execution is built from *events*. A read event `R(x, v, o)` observes value `v` at location `x` with memory order `o`; a write `W(x, v, o)` produces it; read-modify-write events (compare-exchange, fetch-add) are both; fences `F(o)` order without accessing. The relations over events are the model's real content:

| Relation | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sequenced-before | `sb` | program order within a thread |
| additional sync-with | `asw` | thread creation/join, mutex edges |
| reads-from | `rf` | write → read that observes it |
| modification order | `mo` | per-location total order of writes |
| from-reads | `fr = rf⁻¹; mo` | read → write it did *not* observe (a later overwrite) |
| synchronizes-with | `sw` | release→acquire synchronization (§4.2) |
| happens-before | `hb = (sb ∪ sw)⁺` | the causal backbone |
| SC order | `sc` | total order over SC operations |

The first battery of axioms is **coherence**: four conditions (CoRR, CoWR, CoRW, CoWW) jointly requiring *per-location sequential consistency* — for each location, `hb ∪ rf ∪ mo ∪ fr` must be acyclic. Coherence is undisputed; the trouble lay in the *global* axioms — happens-before consistency and, especially, the SC axioms.

The C++11 **SC axioms** demanded a strict total order `sc` over all sequentially-consistent operations such that:

- `sc` is consistent with `hb` and with `mo` (restricted to SC events);
- **SC reads are restricted**: an SC read must observe either the most recent SC write to its location in `sc` order, or a non-SC write that is not hidden behind such an SC write — formally, a non-SC write `w` with no SC write `w'` satisfying `w --hb--> w'` where `w'` precedes the read in `sc`.

> **Theorem (DRF-SC, as intended by C++11).** *A data-race-free program — one in which every pair of conflicting accesses is ordered by happens-before — exhibits only sequentially consistent behaviors.*

This theorem was the model's *raison d'être*. As §4.4 shows, the proof had a hole: the SC axioms were too weak to actually deliver it.

### 4.2 Synchronizes-with, release sequences, and happens-before

Between full SC and chaos lies the workhorse of systems programming: *release/acquire* synchronization. The message-passing idiom is the canonical example:

```cpp
int data = 0;                       // non-atomic
std::atomic<bool> flag{false};
// Thread 0:
data = 42;
flag.store(true, std::memory_order_release);
// Thread 1:
if (flag.load(std::memory_order_acquire)) {
    assert(data == 42);             // must hold
}
```

The acquire load *synchronizes with* the release store when it reads from a write in the store's **release sequence**, creating the happens-before edge that carries `data = 42` across threads. C++11 defined the sequence, with load-bearing punctuation, as:

> *A release sequence headed by a release operation A on atomic M is a maximal contiguous sub-sequence of the modification order of M, where the first operation is A, and every subsequent operation is performed by the same thread that performed A, or is an atomic read-modify-write operation.*

Note the restriction: a plain relaxed store by a *different* thread **breaks** the sequence — a genuine controversy, since C++20 (P0982R1) weakened release sequences in the opposite direction [8] while RC11 strengthened them (§4.4). A *data race* — conflicting accesses, at least one a write and one non-atomic, unordered by `hb` — is undefined behavior, which is what lets compilers optimize non-atomics aggressively.

### 4.3 The out-of-thin-air problem

Now the pathology. Consider *load buffering* (LB):

```cpp
// x = y = 0, both std::atomic<int>
// Thread 0:                              // Thread 1:
int r1 = x.load(std::memory_order_relaxed);  int r2 = y.load(std::memory_order_relaxed);
y.store(r1, std::memory_order_relaxed);       x.store(r2, std::memory_order_relaxed);
```

**Question: can `r1 == r2 == 42`?** The value 42 appears nowhere in the program, yet the C++11 axioms permit it: choose `rf` edges `W(y,42)` → `R(y)` and `W(x,42)` → `R(x)`. Every write writes what its thread read; every read reads what was written. Coherence holds, happens-before is acyclic, the SC axioms are vacuous — the execution is *consistent*, with values materialized **out of thin air**, each justified circularly by the other.

The consequences are severe:

- **The DRF guarantee is undermined.** Out-of-thin-air values let a compiler's speculation "guess" a value, use it, and later justify the guess — breaking the causal reasoning DRF-SC was meant to protect.
- **Compositional reasoning collapses.** A thread's behavior can depend on values no thread ever legitimately produced.
- **Security boundaries leak.** A speculative guess could, in principle, materialize a secret value into an observable channel.

The standard's response was a non-normative *note*: implementations "should ensure that no 'out of thin air' values are computed that circularly depend on their own computation" — defined by examples only [4]. Boehm's N3786 proposed a normative prohibition for C++14 [4], and Boehm and Demsky's "Outlawing ghosts" explored enforcement — but the committee never adopted a formal fix.

Why so hard? Axiomatic models quantify over *complete* executions, where "circular justification" has no natural formalization — causality is inherently *operational*. Dependency tracking (forbid LB only absent a syntactic read→write chain) fails because compilers don't preserve syntactic dependencies: the "fake dependency" `y := a + 1 - a` optimizes to `y := 1`, erasing the evidence [3]. Any syntax-inspecting model is at the optimizer's mercy.

### 4.4 RC11: the repair

In "Repairing Sequential Consistency in C/C++11" (PLDI 2017), Lahav, Vafeiadis, Kang, Hur, and Dreyer demonstrated that the problems ran far deeper than thin air [1]. Their abstract is blunt: *the semantics of SC atomic accesses in C/C++11, as well as in all proposed strengthenings of the semantics, is flawed, in that both suggested compilation schemes to the Power architecture are unsound.* Three repairs followed.

**Repair 1: SC atomics that actually guarantee SC.** The C++11 SC axioms were too weak — executions satisfied every stated axiom while exhibiting non-SC behavior for SC-only programs. RC11 replaces them with acyclicity of a derived relation, `psc` (*preserved SC order*), closing SC events under happens-before, coherence chains `eco = (rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)⁺`, and fence orderings, with tightened SC-read restrictions. SC *fences*, whose C++11 semantics were likewise too weak, are repaired via `psc_f`; the unsound Power mappings were corrected (proper `lwsync`/`sync` placement) and proved sound.

**Repair 2: strengthened release sequences.** RC11 drops C++11's same-thread/RMW restriction: a release sequence is a maximal *contiguous* run of modification order from the release write, regardless of thread. In the `.cat` model this is one subtracted term — C++11 subtracts `(coe; coe)` from the sequence relation; RC11 does not [6] — and it was load-bearing in the repaired Power proof.

**Repair 3: no thin air, by fiat.** RC11 adds one axiom of disarming simplicity:

> **Axiom (No-Thin-Air).** *The relation `sb ∪ rf` is acyclic.*

Return to the LB execution with `r1 = r2 = 42`. Its edges contain the cycle `R(x) --sb--> W(y) --rf--> R(y) --sb--> W(x) --rf--> R(x)` — program order within each thread closed by the reads-from edges. The axiom kills it outright. No dependency tracking, no operational machinery: a single global acyclicity condition.

> **Theorem (RC11 DRF-SC, Lahav et al. [1]).** *Every data-race-free RC11 program exhibits only sequentially consistent behaviors, with no out-of-thin-air values — the original guarantee, finally proved.*

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Litmus evidence

The executable `.cat` models [6] decide each litmus test mechanically. The table below summarizes the discriminating outcomes ("forbidden" = no consistent execution exhibits it):

| Litmus test | Outcome | C++11 | RC11 | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SB (store buffering) | `r1 = r2 = 0` | allowed | allowed | observable (x86, Power, ARM) |
| MP (message passing) | `data != 42` read | forbidden | forbidden | forbidden |
| LB (load buffering) | `r1 = r2 = 42` | **allowed (OOTA)** | **forbidden** | not observable |
| LB+data-dep | `r1 = r2 = 1` via dependency | allowed | forbidden | observable via speculation |
| IRIW | non-SC outcome | forbidden (SC) | forbidden (SC) | observable (Power/ARMv7) |
| WRC | causal violation | forbidden | forbidden | forbidden (with cumulativity) |

Two rows deserve comment. LB is the §4.3 out-of-thin-air execution: permitted by C++11, forbidden by RC11, and never observed on hardware — the empirical smoking gun that C++11 was *too weak*. LB+data-dep is RC11's deliberate over-approximation (see §6): real hardware can produce it via speculation, but blunt `sb ∪ rf` acyclicity forbids it anyway.

### 5.2 Verified compilation

A model earns its keep only if it compiles. RC11's paper proves soundness of mappings from the full model — relaxed, release/acquire, SC atomics, and fences — to each major target:

| C++11 primitive | x86-TSO | IBM Power (repaired) | ARMv8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC store | `xchg` (or `mov; mfence`) | `sync; st` | `stlr` |
| SC load | `mov` | `ld; cmp; bc; isync` + `sync` placement | `ldar` |
| release store | `mov` | `lwsync; st` | `stlr` |
| acquire load | `mov` | `ld; cmp; bc; isync` | `ldar` |
| SC fence | `mfence` | `sync` | `dmb ish` |

> **Theorem (Compilation soundness [1]).** *For each target (x86-TSO, Power, ARMv7, ARMv8), every behavior of a program compiled by the RC11 scheme is a behavior permitted by the RC11 source semantics.*

The Power column is where the bodies are buried: *both* previously published schemes were proved unsound against the repaired SC semantics, and only the paper's corrected `lwsync`/`sync` placement validates.

### 5.3 Mechanization and model checking

The RC11 development was carried out with machine-checked rigor, and it enabled a new generation of tooling:

1. **Executable `.cat` models** of RC11 and C++11 in herd7 [6][7] decide any litmus test in milliseconds; the models differ by a handful of `.cat` lines — the `(coe; coe)` subtraction and `acyclic (sb | rf)`.
2. **RCMC**, a stateless model checker for RC11 (Kokologiannakis et al., POPL 2018), explores real concurrent C/C++ programs under the repaired semantics [3].
3. **Dynamic analyses** like C11Tester adopt RC11-style strengthenings (e.g., `hb ∪ sc ∪ rf` acyclic), since supporting genuine thin-air executions in a race detector is prohibitively expensive [8].

## 6. Limitations

RC11 is a repair, not a final answer. Its limitations are instructive:

- **Axiomatic, not operational.** RC11 says which whole executions are legal but not *how* executions unfold; "never produce an `sb ∪ rf` cycle" is no local transformation rule. This gap motivated the *Promising Semantics* (Kang et al., POPL 2017): threads may *promise* future writes but must *certify* them — banning thin air with an executable, compiler-oriented intuition.
- **The no-thin-air axiom is blunt.** Because `sb` is program order *regardless of dependencies*, `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)` forbids LB+data-dep executions that real hardware genuinely exhibits through speculation (§5.1). RC11 trades hardware fidelity for a simple, checkable, global condition — a defensible trade for a language specification, but a real over-approximation.
- **Whole-execution conditions are expensive dynamically.** Race detectors and model checkers must effectively search for cycles over the execution graph; tools like C11Tester respond with even stronger (cheaper-to-check) acyclicity conditions [8].
- **`consume` remains dead.** No production compiler implements `memory_order_consume` as specified (all strengthen it to acquire); RC11 doesn't repair it, and the standard has effectively deprecated it.
- **Partial adoption.** C++20 adopted RC11's *SC repair* and independently *weakened* release sequences (P0982R1) [8] — but the out-of-thin-air prohibition remains a non-normative "should" note in the standard. A decade after N3786 [4], the language still has no normative causality axiom.
- **Scope.** Mixed-size atomics, the interaction of the model with link-time optimization, and the LLVM-level memory model are all outside RC11's remit.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The C++11 memory model was a heroic first draft: the first rigorous concurrency semantics for a mainstream systems language, built on the elegant axiomatic machinery of Batty et al. [2]. But elegance concealed two defects — SC atomics that did not deliver sequential consistency (with unsound Power compilation to prove it), and axioms permissive enough to conjure values out of thin air. RC11 [1] repaired both with a corrected `psc` axiomatization of SC, strengthened release sequences, and a one-line no-thin-air axiom, `acyclic(sb ∪ rf)` — restoring the DRF-SC guarantee with a proof and verifying compilation to every major architecture.

The deeper lesson transcends any single model. A language memory model serves three masters — the programmer's reasoning, the compiler's local transformation rules, the hardware's weak reality — and they pull in different directions. RC11 optimized for *specification clarity* (few global axioms, mechanically checkable); the Promising Semantics for *operational implementability* (promises and certification). That tension remains the central open problem — and until the standard gains a normative causality axiom, every C++ compiler ships with a "should" where a theorem ought to be.
