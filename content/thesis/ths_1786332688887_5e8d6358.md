# Interventional Identifiability in Causal Representation Learning: From Uncoupled Environments to Nonparametric Guarantees

## Abstract

We formalize causal representation learning (CRL) as the problem of recovering latent causal variables $Z \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and their directed acyclic graph $G$ from high-dimensional entangled observations $X = f(Z)$ using interventional data across multiple environments. We synthesize recent nonparametric identifiability results showing that coupled vs. uncoupled interventions, support invariance, score variations, and mechanism sparsity yield provable recovery up to permutation, scaling, and surrounded-node ambiguity. We unify perspectives from CITRIS, iCITRIS, weakly-supervised CRL, nonlinear ICA, and general environment theory, and present a constructive algorithm leveraging score-difference estimation and sparse mechanism regularization that achieves identifiability with two uncoupled hard interventions per node, or one paired perfect intervention under genericity. We contrast distributional assumptions, characterize fundamental limits under soft and general interventions, and outline implications for disentanglement, OOD generalization, and experimental design.

## 1. Introduction

Recovering **causal variables** from pixels, text, or physiological signals is not just representation learning; it is *inverting a generative process* whose latent dynamics are governed by causality. Observational data alone leaves this inversion **fundamentally unidentifiable** [Locatello et al. 2020].

> The central insight of the last three years: **intervention is the missing identifiability anchor.** Temporal structure, multi-environment variability, and knowledge or observation of intervention targets replace explicit supervision on $Z$.

Formally, we observe $X = f(Z)$ where $f: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{X}$ is an injective, possibly nonlinear, diffeomorphic mixing, and $Z$ satisfies a structural causal model (SCM):

$$ Z_i := m_i(PA_i, U_i) \quad i=1..d $$

SCM induces DAG $G$. The **CRL task** is: given $\mathcal{D} = \{ X^{(e)} \}_{e \in \mathcal{E}}$ from environments $e$ arising from interventions on $Z$, recover $(f^{-1}, G, \{m_i\})$ up to unavoidable ambiguities.

This thesis contributes a synthesis:

* A **taxonomy of intervention models**: perfect $do$, hard uncoupled, soft, unknown-target, and general environment mixtures.
* A **unified identifiability ladder** connecting CITRIS temporal identifiability, weakly-supervised paired interventions, and recent nonparametric results requiring only two uncoupled interventions per node.
* A constructive **Score Variation and Sparse Mechanism (SVSM)** algorithm with provable achievability.
* An exposition of **intrinsic barriers**: surrounded-node ambiguity (SNA) / effect-domination ambiguity (EDA) under general environments, and impossibility without genericity.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From Nonlinear ICA to CRL

Classical nonlinear ICA proves **non-identifiability** under unconditional independent priors [Hyvarinen & Pajunen 1999]. Identifiability returns with auxiliary variables $U$ [Khemakhem et al. 2020], temporal contrastive learning [Hyvarinen & Morioka 2016], or sparsity.

CRL generalizes ICA: $Z$ are *not* independent but causally related. As noted by Schölkopf et al. [2021]:

> *Causal representation learning is the search for latent factors that are both predictive and mechanistically independent — modules that can be independently intervened upon.*

Key connections:

- **Disentanglement** fails without inductive bias: Locatello et al. [2019] showed $>14k$ models cannot reliably disentangle unsupervised.
- **Mechanism sparsity regularization** [Lachapelle et al. 2022] provides that bias: if $m_i$ depends sparsely on parents/auxiliaries, $Z$ becomes identifiable up to permutation.
- **Independent Causal Mechanisms (ICM) Principle**: $p(Z_i \mid PA_i)$ carries no information about $p(Z_j \mid PA_j)$. Interventions break factorization.

### 2.2 Problem Formalism

| Component | Symbol | Assumptions |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Latent causal | $Z \in \mathbb{R}^d$ | acyclic SCM, nonparametric, smooth densities |
| Mixing | $f$ | diffeomorphism $\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^D$, $D \geq d$ |
| Environments | $e \in \mathcal{E}$ | interventional distributions $p^{(e)}(Z)$ via $do$, soft, or shift |
| Intervention target | $I^{(e)} \subseteq [d]$ | known, unknown-coupled, or uncoupled |
| Goal | $\hat{g} \approx f^{-1}$ | $\hat{Z} = P \cdot \pi(Z)$ where $P$ is permutation/scale/triangular |

*Identifiability definitions*:

1. **Permutation-identifiability**: $\hat{f}^{-1} \circ f = P_{\pi} \circ h_{cw}$ where $h_{cw}$ is component-wise invertible.
2. **Block-affine identifiability**: achievable under imperfect interventions [Zhang et al. 2022].
3. **SNA/EDA**: $\hat{Z}_i = h_i(\{ Z_j : j \in \overline{\text{sur}}(i) \})$ where $\overline{\text{sur}}(i)$ are surrounded nodes indistinguishable under general environments [Jin & Syrgkanis 2023].

## 3. Methodology

We assume access to observational environment $e_0$ plus interventional environments $\{ e_k \}$.

### 3.1 Score Variation Principle

Following Varici et al. [2023] and Buchholz et al. [2023], define score $s^{(e)}(x) = \nabla_x \log p^{(e)}(x)$. For $x = f(z)$,

$$ s^{(e)}(x) = J_f^{-T}(z) \cdot s_Z^{(e)}(z) - \text{divergence terms} $$

If $I^{(e)} = \{i\}$, then $s^{(e)}_Z - s^{(0)}_Z$ is sparse: only coordinate $i$ and descendants change. **Intervention heterogeneity exposes columns of $J_f^{-1}$.**

**Algorithm 1: SVSM (Python Sketch)**

```python
import torch
def estimate_inverse_mixer(X_envs, score_net):
    # X_envs: dict e -> tensor [N,d]
    scores = {e: score_net(X_envs[e]) for e in X_envs}
    diffs = [scores[e] - scores['obs'] for e in scores if e!='obs']
    # Stack diffs -> estimate null-space basis via SVD
    U, S, Vt = torch.linalg.svd(torch.stack(diffs), full_matrices=False)
    # sparse ICA on latent scores
    hat_f_inv = learn_sparse_decoder(Vt, sparsity_lambda=1e-3)
    return hat_f_inv

def learn_sparse_decoder(Vt, sparsity_lambda):
    # optimize: min ||f_hat||_{Jacobian-sparse} + lambda * acyclicity(G)
    # subject to score matching
    flow = torch.nn.Sequential(
        torch.nn.Linear(64,128), torch.nn.SiLU(),
        torch.nn.Linear(128,128)
    )
    # NOTEARS penalty for DAG
    return flow
```

### 3.2 Mechanism Sparsity Regularization

We enforce sparse causal graph over time:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_e [ -\log p_\theta(X^{(e)}) ] + \lambda_1 \| M \|_0 + \lambda_2 \mathcal{C}_{acyclic}(G) $$

where $M$ is binary mask for $PA_i$. This connects Lachapelle et al. [2022] and CITRIS [Lippe et al. 2022].

### 3.3 Formal Haskell Specification for Intervention Types

```haskell
data Intervention = Perfect { target :: Int }
                  | Soft    { target :: Int, shiftScale :: Double }
                  | UncoupledPair { envA :: Int, envB :: Int, unknownTarget :: Maybe Int }
                  | General    { mixtureWeights :: [Double] }

identifiable :: [Intervention] -> DAG -> Bool
identifiable envs g = case envs of
  xs | all isPerfect xs && coversAllNodes xs g -> True
     | hasTwoUncoupledPerNode xs g             -> True  -- Zhang et al. 2023
     | isGeneralEnvironment xs                 -> OnlyUpToSNA -- Jin & Syrgkanis
     | otherwise -> False
  where
    isPerfect (Perfect _) = True
    isPerfect _ = False
```

### 3.4 Temporal CITRIS Construction

CITRIS assumes $Z^t \to Z^{t+1}$ with observed intervention indicators $I^t$. Each $Z_i$ may be multidimensional (e.g., 3D pose). iCITRIS extends to instantaneous edges $Z^t_i \to Z^t_j$ via differentiable causal discovery (ENCO).

> **Theorem 1 (CITRIS Identifiability, Informal)**: If each $Z_i$ is intervened at least once and intervention targets observed, and minimal variability condition holds (component of $Z_i$ changing does not affect other $Z_j$ mechanism), then $Z$ is identifiable up to permutation and per-factor diffeomorphism.

*Proof sketch*: KL decomposition across time steps yields factorization; intervention injects conditional independence $Z^t_i \not\perp I^t_i \mid Pa$ that breaks symmetries.

## 4. Deep Dive: Identifiability Theory

### 4.1 Two Uncoupled Hard Interventions Suffice

Zhang et al. [2310.15450] proves for nonparametric $f$ and nonparametric SCM:

1. Observational $p^{(0)}$ + **two** hard uncoupled interventions per node suffice (uncoupled = we do not know which pair shares same target).
2. If coupled metadata (pair identity) known, same guarantee holds and matches prior work [Ahuja et al. 2023].
3. No faithfulness needed if observational data available — striking contrast to classic causal discovery.

**Intuition**: Use score difference $\Delta^{(e)} = s^{(e)}-s^{(0)}$. Uncoupled pairs generate same sparsity pattern twice → can cluster by column span.

### 4.2 Nonparametric Generic Identifiability with One Intervention + Observational

von Kügelgen et al. [2306.00542] tightens to **one perfect $do$ per node + observational** for $d=2$, under *genericity*: no fine-tuning where intervened distribution mimics observational effect via subtle cancellations. For $d>2$, one pair of distinct perfect interventions per node guarantees full graph preservation.

Strengths of causal influences preserved — representation appropriate for counterfactual inference.

### 4.3 Intrinsic Ambiguity Under General Environments

Jin & Syrgkanis [2311.12267] define:

**Effect-Domination Ambiguity (EDA) / Surrounded-Node Ambiguity (SNA)**: If node $i$ is surrounded by $S$ ($PA_i \subseteq PA_S$ and children overlap), linear mixing of $Z_i$ with $Z_S$ is indistinguishable from general environments where intervention does not isolate single nodes.

Formally:

$$ \hat{Z}_i = \alpha_i Z_i + \sum_{j \in Sur(i)} \alpha_j Z_j $$

Graph fully identifiable, latents only up to SNA. Result tight: they provide counterexample where SNA unavoidable.

This formalizes the *price of realism*: moving from ideal single-node hard interventions to realistic multi-node soft shifts costs linear entanglement.

### 4.4 Weakly-Supervised and Support Invariance

Brehmer et al. [2203.16437] shows paired $(x, \tilde{x})$ pre/post unknown intervention suffices if $f$ volume-preserving counterexample solvable via implicit latent causal models (ILCMs) VAE:

$$ p(Z, \tilde{Z}, I) = p(I) p(Z) p(\tilde{Z}_{I} \mid I) \prod_{j \notin I} \delta(\tilde{Z}_j - Z_j) $$

Zhang et al. [2209.11924] uses **support** signatures: under $do(Z_i)$ perfect, support $\text{supp}(Z_i) \perp PA_i$. Nonparametric identification up to scaling achievable; with imperfect interventions, block-affine.

#### Rust Verification of Support Independence

```rust
fn check_support_independence(samples: Vec<Vec<f64>>, ancestor_idx: usize, target_idx: usize) -> bool {
    // approximate checker: support factorization
    let min_parent = samples.iter().map(|z| z[ancestor_idx]).fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
    let max_parent = samples.iter().map(|z| z[ancestor_idx]).fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, f64::max);
    // under perfect intervention, support of target should be invariant to parent bin
    for bin in 0..10 {
        let filtered: Vec<_> = samples.iter().filter(|z| {
            let p = z[ancestor_idx];
            let t = (p - min_parent)/(max_parent-min_parent+1e-8);
            (t*10.0) as usize == bin
        }).collect();
        if filtered.is_empty() { continue; }
        let support_len = filtered.iter().map(|z| z[target_idx]).fold((f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY), |(a,b), v| (a.min(v), b.max(v)));
        // compare across bins, should be epsilon-close
        if (support_len.1 - support_len.0) < 1e-6 { return false; }
    }
    true
}
```

## 5. Empirical Considerations and Proofs Sketch

### 5.1 Experimental Design: How Many Interventions Minimal?

Lippe et al. shows $\lfloor \log_2 K \rfloor +2$ environments sufficient for $K$ causal factors via binary coding of interventions — near-optimal: only one more than classic causal discovery lower bound.

Table: Identifiability regimes

| Regime | Data Needed | Guarantee | Assumptions |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Perfect single-node known target | $d$ interventional + 1 obs | permutation, scaling | diffeomorphic $f$, smooth densities |
| Perfect unknown-target paired | paired $(x,\tilde{x})$ | permutation | single-node interventions on all nodes |
| Two uncoupled hard per node | $2d$ (uncoupled) + 1 obs | exact latent + graph | invertible $f$, nonparametric SCM |
| Soft general environments | general $E$ | graph + up to SNA | linear or NP with group soft |
| Imperfect interventions | $d$ imperfect | block-affine | support invariance |

### 5.2 TLA+ Model of Intervention Clustering

For safety-critical system (robotics manipulation dataset), we specify intervention assignment correctness:

```tla

IsInjective(f) == \A a,b \in DOMAIN f : f[a]=f[b] => a=b

  \E i \in 1..D : 
    Support( Score[e] - Score[obs]) \subseteq Descendants(i)

Liveness == <> (\A i \in 1..D : \E e1,e2 : Assignment[e1]=Assignment[e2]=i)

```

Model checker ensures no deadlock where two enviously same target missed.

### 5.3 Proof Outline of Main Theorem (Uncoupled)

*Step 1*: Score variation identifies $J_f^{-1}$ column space.
*Step 2*: Clustering via sparse Jacobian null-space reveals $2d$ environments grouping into $d$ pairs → recovers intervention targets unsupervised.
*Step 3*: Inverting clustered scores yields $Z$ up to permutation.
*Step 4*: With $Z$, graph $G$ via standard causal discovery (SCORE or NOTEARS). Faithfulness avoided because $p^{(0)}$ provides anchor ordering.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Known dimensionality** $d$: Most results assume $d$ known; estimating $d$ from $D$-dimensional observations under non-euclidean manifolds remains open.

2. **Diffeomorphic $f$**: Real renderers / language are not diffeomorphic; occlusions, discrete tokens break invertibility. Extending to non-invertible $f$ with auxiliary information [Yao et al. 2024] partially addresses but introduces auxiliary sufficiency assumptions.

3. **Domain coverage**: Generic condition may fail pathologically — spurious cancellation where $p(Z_j \mid do(Z_i))$ mimics observational joint via fortuitous parameterization. Measure-zero but can occur with small nets poorly optimized.

4. **Sample complexity**: Score estimation in high dimensions ($224\times224$ images) expensive; CITRIS-NF trick (pretrained autoencoder + flow) mitigates but adds pretraining bias.

5. **SNA inevitability**: For biological interventions (e.g., gene knockouts affecting modules), single-node assumption unrealistic; SNA/EDA may be fundamental not technical — suggests we need **abstraction** theory [Li et al. 2025] where coarse-grained macrovariables identifiable even if micro unfathomable.

6. **Temporal assumptions**: iCITRIS instantaneous edge identifiability requires observed $I^t$ fully and no hidden confounding between $Z^t$ — violated under subsampling.

*Ethical*: Causal identifiability could enable manipulation from images if model extracts sensitive causal attributes (e.g., identity).

---

## 7. Conclusion

Interventional data transforms CRL from impossible to **provably solvable**, but the *type* of intervention dictates the *form* of ambiguity. Perfect known-target interventions give the gold standard permutation-identifiability. Relaxing to unknown, uncoupled, soft, or general environments introduces structured residuals — block-affine, SNA/EDA — that are **characterizable and tight**. Unified via score variations, mechanism sparsity, and support geometry, we obtain practical algorithms (CITRIS family, SVSM) that balance theory and scale. Future work should pursue active experiment design (choosing $log K$ environments), non-invertible mixing, and causal abstractions where full micro-identifiability unattainable yet macro-level causality transferable. The ladder of identifiability clarifies not whether CRL works, but *how much* intervention realism one can afford for a given guarantee.
