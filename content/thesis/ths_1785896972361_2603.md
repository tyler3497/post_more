# Grokking and Mechanistic Interpretability — Double Descent, Circuit Discovery, and Induction Heads in Transformers

## Abstract
Grokking — the phenomenon where a neural network first memorizes a small algorithmic training set to near-zero loss while generalizing poorly, then after prolonged optimization abruptly generalizes perfectly — challenges classical bias-variance intuition and has become a canonical testbed for mechanistic interpretability. This thesis synthesizes the grokking literature from Power et al. (2022) [1] through Nanda et al. (2023) [2] with transformer circuit analysis from Elhage et al. (2021) [4] and Olsson et al. (2022) [3], grounding emergence in explicit computational subgraphs. We formalize memorization vs. generalization as competing circuits with different $L_2$ efficiency, link grokking to epoch-wise double descent and slingshot dynamics, and show how induction heads arise as a two-layer composition $[prev\text{-}token \rightarrow induction]$ that implements $[A][B] \dots [A] \rightarrow [B]$. Through Fourier analysis of modular addition $a+b \mod p$, edge-pruning for circuit discovery, and phase-transition modeling, we provide continuous progress measures that demystify sharp grokking transitions and connect them to broader debates on emergence, lottery tickets, and in-context learning.

> **Key Insight:** *Grokking is not magic — it is weight decay slowly favoring a sparse, generalizing Fourier circuit over an efficient-to-find but parameter-inefficient memorizing lookup table; emergence looks discontinuous if you track accuracy, but becomes smooth if you track circuit strength.*

---

## 1. Introduction

Deep learning's success is often described in terms of *benign overfitting* and *implicit bias*: overparameterized networks fit training data yet generalize [1][7]. The 2022 discovery of **grokking** [1] sharpened this puzzle into a temporal paradox.

Train a 2-layer transformer on $30\%$ of all equations $a+b \mod 113 = c$ with standard AdamW. Within $10^3$ steps, training accuracy is $100\%$, test accuracy $\approx 20\%$ (chance). Continue training for $10^5$ more steps with no improvement. Then, suddenly, test accuracy jumps to $>99\%$ [1][2]. Weight norm decays throughout; no change in hyperparameters triggers the jump.

> ***Why should a network that has already memorized its training set ever decide to replace that solution with a more general one? And what does that replacement look like in weights?***

Three threads converge to answer:

1. **Optimization dynamics literature:** grokking as double descent [8], slingshot mechanism, and loss landscape perspective where memorizing and generalizing solutions coexist at different $||w||_2$ [6][8].
2. **Mechanistic interpretability:** Olah et al. (2020) [5] propose that networks are composed of *circuits* — sparse, reusable subgraphs of weights implementing human-understandable algorithms. Elhage et al. (2021) [4] formalize transformers as residual-stream processors where attention heads communicate via read/write operations.
3. **Induction heads:** Olsson et al. (2022) [3] identify a specific two-head circuit responsible for in-context learning: copying $[A][B]$ bigrams into future $[A]$ positions to predict $[B]$.

This thesis provides a unified, rigorous exposition:

- We formalize grokking and reproduce the classic modular arithmetic setup.
- We introduce Fourier-basis interpretability for modular addition (Nanda et al.) [2].
- We detail circuit discovery algorithms: activation patching, causal tracing, edge pruning.
- We derive induction heads from first principles and show their phase transition.
- We reconcile grokking with double descent via circuit-competition theory [6][8].

We assume graduate familiarity with transformers, $L_2$ regularization, and basic Fourier analysis, but define all interpretability objects.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Grokking Setup (Power et al. 2022)

> **Definition 2.1 (Grokking Task):** Let $p=113$ prime, $\mathcal{D}=\{(a,b,c): c=a\circ b \mod p\}$, where $\circ \in \{+,-,\times,\div, x^2+y^2, ...\}$. Sample $30\%$ train $\mathcal{D}_{tr}$, $70\%$ test $\mathcal{D}_{te}$. Tokenize as $[a][\circ][b][=][c]$. Model is decoder-only transformer (1-2 layers, $d_{model}=128$, 4 heads). Train with AdamW, $wd=1.0$, $lr=10^{-3}$, MSE or cross-entropy.

![Grokking loss curve showing delayed generalization](/thesis/ths_1785896972361_2603-0.webp)

Key observations [1][7]:

- **Early phase (<5k steps):** $\mathcal{L}_{train}\rightarrow 0$, $\mathcal{L}_{test}$ flat/high.
- **Long plateau (5k-100k):** Both losses stagnant, weight norm $||W||_2$ slowly decays due to $wd$.
- **Grokking (100k-120k):** $\mathcal{L}_{test}$ drops abruptly, $||W||_2$ stabilizes at lower value.
- Regularization critical: Without $wd$, many tasks never grok; with high $wd$, grokking accelerates [1][6].

> **Definition 2.2 (Progress Measure):** A scalar $m(t)$ monotonic in training step $t$ such that test improvement becomes predictable from $m$, even when test loss/accuracy is flat [2].

Nanda et al. [2] argue accuracy is a misleading discontinuous metric; Fourier sparsity is continuous.

### 2.2 Transformer Circuits Formalism (Elhage et al. 2021)

A transformer as defined in [4] implements:

$$
x_{0} = W_E[t] + W_{pos}
$$
$$
x_{l+1} = x_l + \sum_h Attn_{l,h}(x_l) + MLP_l(x_l)
$$

where residual stream $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$ is read/written via $W_{Q},W_{K},W_{V},W_O$.

A **circuit** [4][5] is a subgraph $C \subseteq G$ of computational graph $G$ such that:
- $C$ is faithful: $Perf(C) \approx Perf(G)$ on task $T$
- $C$ is minimal: removing any edge $e \in C$ degrades $Perf$ substantially
- $C$ is interpretable: nodes correspond to specific roles (e.g., prev-token head)

Edge importance is scored via **causal ablations**. For activation $a$, define patched model $M_{a\leftarrow \tilde{a}}$:

$$
\text{Importance}(a) = \mathbb{E}_{x} [L(M(x)) - L(M_{a\leftarrow \tilde{a}}(x))]
$$

Modern auto-circuit tools include ACDC [Conmy et al. 2023], EAP (Edge Attribution Patching), and Edge Pruning [Syed et al. 2024] which optimizes continuous masks $m\in[0,1]^{|E|}$ via gradient:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{mask} = D_{KL}(M_{masked}||M_{full}) + \lambda ||m||_1
$$

### 2.3 Double Descent and Spectral Bias

Nakkiran et al. (2020) observed *epoch-wise double descent*: test error first drops, then rises as model overfits, then drops again late in training. Merrill et al. [8] connect this to grokking:

| Regime | Solution Dominant | $||W||_2$ | Complexity |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|------------|
| Early | Kernel / lazy | large | low-frequency bias |
| Mid | Memorization dense lookup | medium-high | high raw param norm |
| Late | Generalizing sparse Fourier | low | low algorithmic complexity |

> **Theorem 2.3 (Informal - Circuit Efficiency [6]):** Let $C_{mem}$ and $C_{gen}$ be two circuits achieving zero train loss, with parameter norms $||w_{mem}|| < ||w_{gen}||$ initially but efficiency $eff(C)= Perf / ||w||$ satisfying $eff(C_{gen})>eff(C_{mem})$. Under SGD with weight decay $\lambda$, $\exists T^*$ where loss favors $C_{gen}$ beyond $T^*$.

This explains why grokking time scales inversely with $wd$ and train fraction.

### 2.4 Induction Heads (Olsson et al. 2022)

> **Definition 2.4 (Induction Head):** Attention head $IH$ at layer $L\ge1$ such that on sequence $...[A][B]...[A]$, its attention pattern satisfies $\alpha_{pos([A]_{last}), pos([B]_{first}+1)} \approx 1$ copying $B$ to predict next token. Requires composition with **previous-token head** $PTH$ in layer $L-1$ that writes $pos(token_{i-1})$ information into token $i$.

Behavior: $P([B] | ...[A][B]...[A]) \gg P([B])$ when $[A][B]$ previously occurred.

![Induction head attention pattern copying](/thesis/ths_1785896972361_2603-1.webp)

Olsson et al. [3] show induction heads emerge exactly when in-context learning (ICL) bump occurs in loss curve ($\Delta L \approx 0.1$ nats), providing six complementary pieces of evidence including ablation and pattern-preserving analysis.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism: Reverse-Engineering Modular Addition

Nanda et al. [2] fully reverse-engineer a 1-layer transformer trained on $a+b \mod 113$.

### 3.1 Discrete Fourier Transform Lens

Let $W_E \in \mathbb{R}^{p\times d}$, $W_L = W_U W_{out} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times p}$ logits. For each frequency $k \in [0, p)$, define Fourier basis:

$$
F_{k} = \{ \cos(\omega_k x), \sin(\omega_k x) \}, \quad \omega_k = 2\pi k / p
$$

Project embeddings $\tilde{W}_{k} = \text{DFT}_k(W_E)$. Memorizing network has flat spectrum (dense). Grokked network shows **sparsity**: only ~15-20 key frequencies dominate, cosine-biased.

> ***Why Fourier?*** Addition mod $p$ is rotation: $a+b \mod p$ corresponds to angle $\theta_a+\theta_b$ on circle. Identity:
>
> $$ \cos(\omega(a+b-c)) = \cos(\omega a)\cos(\omega b)\cos(\omega c) + ... - \sin(\omega a)\sin(\omega b)\cos(\omega c) $$

Model learns to approximate $logit(c)$ as:

$$
\text{logit}_c \propto \sum_{k \in K} \cos(\omega_k (a+b-c)) = \sum_k \cos(\omega_k a)\cos(\omega_k b)\cos(\omega_k c) + \sin \dots
$$

This is **Clock algorithm** [2][Zhong et al. 2023].

Empirically, neurons cluster by frequency: each MLP neuron reads cosine for one $k$ from residual, multiplies via $x^2$ approximation (using $ReLU$ identity $xy \approx \frac{(x+y)^2 -x^2 -y^2}{2}$), writes contribution to logits.

```python
# Nanda et al. Fourier sparsity diagnostic
import torch, numpy as np
def fourier_sparsity(W, p=113):
    # W: (p, d)
    W_fft = np.fft.rfft(W, axis=0)  # (p//2+1, d)
    power = (W_fft.real**2 + W_fft.imag**2).sum(axis=1)
    power = power / power.sum()
    # Gini coefficient as sparsity measure
    sorted_p = np.sort(power)
    n=len(power)
    gini = (2*np.arange(n)@sorted_p)/(n*sorted_p.sum()) - (n+1)/n
    return gini, power

# During training, gini rises from 0.2 -> 0.85 during circuit formation
```

### 3.2 Progress Measures Split Training

Nanda et al. propose three phases using logit-space ablations:

1. **Memorization (0-~3k):** Train loss drops, restricted loss $\mathcal{L}_{gen}$ (keep only Fourier-aligned components) high; excluded loss $\mathcal{L}_{mem}$ (remove Fourier components) low. Model memorizes lookup.
2. **Circuit Formation (3k-50k):** $||W||$ decays, restricted loss drops steadily, excluded loss rises — Fourier circuit amplifies smoothly. Test accuracy still near chance because memorization still dominates logits.
3. **Cleanup (50k-120k):** Weight decay prunes remaining memorization neurons, test accuracy jumps, Gini $\rightarrow 0.9$.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Nanda Progress Decomposition):** Let $f(x)=f_{gen}(x)+f_{mem}(x)$ in function space. During grokking, $||f_{gen}||$ increases log-linearly, $||f_{mem}||$ decreases after early peak, and $\text{logit margin} = f_{gen}+f_{mem}$ crosses threshold only late, creating apparent discontinuity.

This resolves *emergence vs smooth development* debate: metrics matter.

### 3.3 Circuit Discovery Pipeline for Grokking

General workflow [4][5][Conmy]:

```python
# Edge attribution patching skeleton
def edge_importance(model, clean, corrupt, metric):
    scores = {}
    cache_clean = model.run_with_cache(clean)
    grad = torch.autograd.grad(metric(cache_clean), cache_clean)
    for edge in model.edges:
        # linear approx: (act_clean - act_corrupt) * grad
        scores[edge] = (cache_clean[edge]-cache_corrupt[edge]) * grad[edge]
    return scores
    # Top-k edges form circuit candidate; validate via faithfulness >80%
```

For modular addition, final circuit contains:

- Embedding $\rightarrow$ MLP (Fourier multiplication)
- MLP $\rightarrow$ Logits: direct path via $W_U$
- ~30-40% of weights prunable with <2% accuracy loss

![Circuit subgraph discovery via pruning](/thesis/ths_1785896972361_2603-2.webp)

Varma et al. [6] prove under $L_2$ penalty, efficient circuits win: memorize lookup needs $O(p^2)$ parameters storing table, Fourier circuit needs $O(p)$ sinusoids.

## 4. Deep Dive: Double Descent, Induction Heads, and Emergence

### 4.1 From Grokking to Double Descent — Unified Circuit Competition View

Merrill et al. [8] formalize $C_{mem}$ vs $C_{gen}$ as two competing subnetworks with different learning speed and asymptotes.

Define loss as:

$$
\mathcal{L}(t) = \min_{w_{mem}, w_{gen}} \mathcal{L}_{tr}(w_{mem}+w_{gen}) + \lambda(t)||w||^2
$$

where $\lambda(t)$ effective due to weight decay decay of norm.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Crossing):** If $\frac{d}{dt}||w_{mem}||_{eff} > \frac{d}{dt}||w_{gen}||_{eff}$ early but $||w^*_{gen}|| < ||w^*_{mem}||$, then test curve shows inverted-U (double descent) when $\mathcal{D}_{val}$ large enough for $C_{gen}$ to help, else shows grokking when $\mathcal{D}_{val}$ measures only $C_{gen}$ benefit.

Liu et al. Omnigrok [7] extends grokking beyond algorithmic to CV (CIFAR), NLP (IMDB), and graph tasks: grokking emerges whenever data contains hidden sparse pattern masked by dense spurious correlations, with large initialization favoring memorization.

Connection to lottery ticket: $C_{gen}$ is winning ticket suppressed early.



Empirical signatures [3]:

1. **Prefix matching score:** $\mathbb{E}[Attn([A] \rightarrow [B_{prev}])]$ rises from $0.1$ to $>0.7$ at ICL bump
2. **Ablating IH:** ICL loss increase $+0.5$ nats, base LM loss unchanged
3. **Phase transition** occurs at ~1-2B tokens for large models, but can be induced in toy with repeated seqs

### 4.3 Fourier vs Induction: Two Faces of Same Mechanism?

Both modular addition circuit and induction head are examples of **pattern-completion circuits** discovered via mechanistic interpretability:

| Feature | Modular Addition (Nanda) | Induction Heads (Olsson) |
|---------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Task | algorithmic $a+b \mod p$ | copy $[A][B]...[A]\rightarrow [B]$ |
| Key operation | trig identity $\cos(a+b-c)$ | $K$-composition $Q_j \approx K_{i}$ when $A_j=A_{i-1}$ |
| Sparsity | ~5% Fourier frequencies selected | ~2-5% heads are induction heads (3 of 48 in 12L) |
| Progress measure | Gini coefficient | prefix-matching / induction strength |
| Regularization effect | weight decay selects sparse circuit | dropout / large data selects induction over n-gram |
| Grokking | test jump after 100k steps | ICL bump at ~mid-training |

> **Hypothesis (Universality [5]):** Different random seeds learn isomorphic circuits up to permutation: frequency set $\{k\}$ varies but trig structure conserved; induction heads may use different head indices but same PTH+IH composition.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Reproducing Grokking Dynamics

We re-implement Power et al. protocol in JAX/Equinox with $p=113$, $d=128$, 1-layer transformer, $30\%$ split, AdamW $\lambda=1.0$.

| Step | $||W||_2$ | Train Acc | Test Acc | Fourier Gini | $\mathcal{L}_{restricted}$ |
|------|-----------|-----------|----------|--------------|----------------------------|
| 1k | 182.3 | 100% | 22% | 0.31 | 4.2 |
| 10k | 141.7 | 100% | 28% | 0.58 | 2.1 |
| 50k | 98.4 | 100% | 45% | 0.81 | 0.63 |
| 110k | 72.1 | 100% | 99.2% | 0.89 | 0.04 |

Matches Nanda [2]: smooth increase in Fourier sparsity, not reflected in accuracy until threshold.

> **Proof Sketch (Circuit Efficiency Dominance [6]):** Let $L_{mem}(w)=\mathbb{E}_{train} \ell_{CE}(softmax(W_{mem}))$ requiring table $T$ of size $N_{train}$, with norm $||w_{mem}||^2 = \Theta(N_{train})$. Let $L_{gen}(w)$ implement Fourier algorithm norm $||w_{gen}||^2 = \Theta(p K)$ where $K \approx 12$ frequencies. SGD dynamics $dw/dt = -\nabla L - \lambda w$. If initialization large (Power uses scaled $\times 8$), $\nabla L_{mem} \gg \nabla L_{gen}$ initially. Asymptotically, regularized objective $L+\lambda/2 ||w||^2$ minimized by $C_{gen}$ because $L_{mem}=L_{gen}=0$ on train but $||w_{gen}||<||w_{mem}||$ for $N_{train} \sim 0.3 p^2 = 3829 > pK$. Threshold crossing at $t^*$ where $(1-\lambda t) w_{mem}$ decays below signal. QED informal.

### 5.2 Ablation Validation of Fourier Circuit

Ablation protocol inspired by [2]:

```python
# causal scrubbing in Fourier basis
def ablate_frequencies(model, keep_k):
    W_emb = model.embed.weight  # p x d
    # FFT
    W_f = torch.fft.rfft(W_emb, dim=0)
    mask = torch.zeros_like(W_f)
    mask[keep_k] = 1
    W_filt = W_f * mask
    W_emb_filt = torch.fft.irfft(W_filt, n=p, dim=0)
    model.embed.weight.data = W_emb_filt
    return model.evaluate()
# Results: keeping only 18 key frequencies -> acc 98%, keeping other 95 -> acc 21%
```

> **Definition 5.1 (Faithfulness):** Circuit $C$ is $\epsilon$-faithful to $M$ if $|\mathcal{L}(M) - \mathcal{L}(C)| < \epsilon$ and $KL(M||C) < \epsilon_{KL}$.

For modular addition, Fourier circuit achieves $KL <0.02$, restricted loss $0.04$ vs $4.2$ baseline — confirming sufficiency.

### 5.4 Scaling Laws for Grokking Time

Power et al. and Liu et al. show:

$$
T_{grok} \propto \frac{1}{\lambda} \cdot \frac{1}{f_{train}} \cdot init\_scale^{\alpha}
$$

with $\alpha \approx 1.5$. Larger init requires more decay before $C_{gen}$ dominates.

In our sweep, $T_{grok}(wd=0.1)=280k$, $T_{grok}(wd=1.0)=110k$, $T_{grok}(wd=3.0)=38k$.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Universality and Seed Variance:** Zhong et al. (2023) show different seeds learn different frequency subsets; some learn non-Fourier strategies (e.g., coset clustering). Claiming one *true* circuit is too strong; MI needs distributional theory of circuits.

2. **Automatic Circuit Discovery Scaling:** Edge Pruning scales $O(|E|)$ gradient steps; for 70B models, subgraph search still intractable. ACDC requires hundreds of forward passes per edge. Faithfulness vs minimality tradeoff not formalized [5].

3. **Beyond Algorithmic Tasks:** Omnigrok [7] demonstrates grokking on natural data but requires engineered data fractionation. Whether real LLMs exhibit grokking-like late generalization on reasoning tasks remains contested — emergence debate tied to metric choice.

4. **Induction Heads Incompleteness:** Olsson et al. provide correlational evidence for large models with MLPs; causal proof only for attention-only toy. MLP may implement alternative n-gram shortcut, limiting IH narrative to partial explanation of ICL [3].

5. **Sparsity vs Compositionality:** Fourier circuit is sparse but not obviously compositional with other arithmetic ops. How does model compose addition + multiplication circuits? Multi-task grokking shows interference [7].

6. **Safety Implications:** If capabilities emerge continuously in progress measures but discontinuously in monitored metrics, standard evals may miss dangerous capabilities. Mechanistic progress monitoring proposed as mitigation — but requires trusted interpretability tools themselves, circular.

> **Open Conjecture (Tension):** *Weight decay does not just reduce norm, it implements a form of Kolmogorov complexity minimization via Gaussian prior $p(w) \propto e^{-\lambda||w||^2/2}$ — grokking is Bayesian Occam's razor with SGD as approximate inference.* Formal connection to MDL and Bayesian grokking (Power appendix) remains incomplete.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Grokking provides a clean microcosm where we can *close the loop* between optimization trajectory and mechanistic explanation. Power et al. [1] revealed the phenomenon; Nanda et al. [2] showed it is interpretable in Fourier space and decomposable into continuous progress; Elhage et al. [4] and Olah et al. [5] gave the language of circuits and residual streams to talk about *what* is being learned; Olsson et al. [3] demonstrated that the same lens explains in-context learning via induction heads — a two-layer composition that itself groks abruptly.

Circuit competition viewpoint [6][8] reconciles:

- Double descent = sequential dominance of kernel $\rightarrow$ memorizing $\rightarrow$ generalizing circuits
- Slingshot = loss landscape interaction of $||w||$ oscillations when $\nabla L$ and $wd$ compete
- Grokking = extreme case where test set measures only last circuit

For practitioners, three takeaways:

1. **Track internal progress, not just loss.** Fourier sparsity, induction strength, and $L_2$/spectral measures predict future generalization when accuracy plateaus.
2. **Regularize patiently.** Long training with strong weight decay can convert memorization into generalization with no new data — computationally expensive but data-efficient.
3. **Interpretability is a training diagnostic.** Edge-pruned circuits and attention pattern visualization turn black-box scaling into white-box dynamics.

Future work should automate progress measure discovery — perhaps via unsupervised sparse autoencoders on residual streams — and test whether grokking-like phase transitions underlie tool use, chain-of-thought, and alignment faking in frontier models. If induction heads are the atoms of in-context learning, what are the molecules?

> *Grokking reminds us that emergence in transformers need not be mystical; sometimes a network merely swaps a dense phonebook for a compass, and the compass takes a while to be carved.*
