# Mechanized Verification of Cache-Coherence Protocols: Murphi Model Checking of MESI/MOESI Directory Protocols, Transient States, and the Transition from Snooping to Scalable Directory Schemes

## Abstract

Directory-based cache coherence protocols replaced snooping buses as multiprocessors scaled beyond a handful of cores, trading the atomicity of broadcast for point-to-point forwarding through per-block directories. This thesis presents a mechanized account of that transition, centered on Murphi — David Dill's explicit-state model checker from Stanford — which made exhaustive verification of coherence protocols a mechanical procedure rather than an expert art. We dissect the MESI and MOESI state machines with emphasis on transient states, where every real protocol race lives: forwarded requests colliding with evictions, racing ownership requests, and invalidation acknowledgment counting. We show how NACK-free designs eliminated protocol livelock through exact directory bookkeeping, how virtual-network separation defeats coherence deadlock, and how ProtoGen synthesizes Murphi-verified controllers from atomic specifications. Connecting the classical lineage to industrial practice, we examine hierarchical coherence, snoop filters, and the TileLink, AMBA ACE, and AMBA CHI interconnects. The central claim: mechanized verification was not a luxury but a precondition for scalable coherence, because directory protocols are too subtle for unaided human reasoning.

## 1. Introduction

Cache coherence is the contract that makes shared-memory multiprocessors programmable. When dozens or hundreds of cores replicate the same memory block in private caches, the hardware must maintain the *illusion* of a single memory: every read must observe the most recent write in some coherent global order. The mechanisms that enforce this contract — the coherence protocols — are among the most intricate concurrent systems ever deployed in silicon, because their correctness must survive every interleaving of asynchronous message deliveries, every race between simultaneous requests, and every transient state a cache block passes through on its way from one stable state to another.

This thesis traces the arc from **snooping protocols** — elegant, broadcast-based, unscalable — to **directory-based protocols** that scale by replacing broadcast with point-to-point forwarding [3]. At the center is **Murphi (Murφ)**, David Dill's explicit-state model checker from Stanford [1], which made verification mechanical: describe the protocol as guarded rules and let the machine exhaustively explore the reachable state space. We examine transient-state explosion, directory races from forwarded requests, *NACK-free* designs, ProtoGen's synthesis of Murphi-verified controllers [6], and the industrial present — hierarchical coherence, snoop filters, TileLink, AMBA ACE, and AMBA CHI [7].

The central argument is that *mechanized verification was not a luxury but a precondition* for scalable coherence. Directory protocols are too subtle for unaided human reasoning; their transient-state explosion defeats informal argument. The protocols that run today's servers are correct because machines proved them correct.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Stable states: MSI, MESI, MOESI

The atomic coherence specification is deceptively simple. In **MSI**, each cache block is *Modified* (exclusive, dirty), *Shared* (read-only, possibly replicated), or *Invalid*. **MESI** adds *Exclusive* — a clean, exclusive copy permitting silent transition to Modified on a local write — yielding the four-state machine whose Murphi models are a standard graduate exercise [2][8]. **MOESI** adds *Owned*: a dirty-but-shared state whose owner supplies data to sharers, avoiding writebacks on every sharing event. The Wisconsin GEMS project ships reference SLICC implementations of MOSI, MOESI, and MSI variants for snooping and directory organizations [4].

The *SWMR* invariant — exactly one writer or zero or more readers per block, never both — is what architects reason about. But hardware never *occupies* the stable specification; real protocols live in the transient states between stable states, and that is where verification bites.

### 2.2 Why snooping does not scale

In a snooping protocol, every transaction is broadcast on a totally ordered bus, and the bus ordering *serializes* all transactions — coherence reduces to a total order of atomic events, which is what makes snooping tractable to verify [8]. But broadcast bandwidth scales linearly in core count. Directory protocols replace broadcast with a **directory** at each block's home node that sends *point-to-point* invalidations only to current sharers [3]. The price: transactions are no longer atomic — a GetS and a GetM to the same block may race through the network, and every race must resolve without violating SWMR.

### 2.3 The verification imperative

Manual proofs did not survive contact with transient states: early directory protocols contained races manifesting only under specific message orderings that informal reasoning failed to enumerate. The response was explicit-state model checking. **Murphi**, introduced by Dill's group at Stanford around 1990–1991 [1], models a protocol as guarded rules over global state and explores every reachable state, checking invariants and detecting deadlock, with symmetry reduction via scalarset types taming the state explosion [9]. The canonical Murphi descriptions of MSI and MESI have been studied and extended for decades [2][9].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology mirrors the established Murphi-based verification workflow, extended with the synthesis techniques of the ProtoGen line of research [6]:

1. **Atomic specification.** Begin from a stable-state atomic specification of MESI and MOESI with directory organization: transactions appear to execute atomically at the directory in some serialization order.
2. **Concurrent refinement.** Split each transaction into request/forward/response messages, introduce **transient states** at caches and directory for every in-flight phase, and add **transient buffer entries (TBEs)** for outstanding transaction context [6].
3. **Murphi encoding.** Caches as scalarset-indexed per-block state machines; the network as message multisets (unordered) or ordered queues. Multisets model unordered networks faithfully with automatic symmetry reduction [9].
4. **Invariant specification.** The core invariants are *coherence* (SWMR at stable states, plus per-block "at most one owner" over transient states) and *deadlock freedom*, checked on every explored state.
5. **State-space exploration.** Run Murphi with hash compaction and symmetry reduction on small configurations (2–4 caches, 1–2 addresses, 1–2 data values), relying on the *small-scope hypothesis*: coherence bugs manifest at small scale.

> **Theorem 1 (Soundness of the method).** *If the Murphi model is a faithful abstraction of the RTL protocol and the invariants characterize SWMR plus data-value integrity, then absence of invariant violations in the explored state space implies protocol correctness for the modeled configurations. Absence of deadlock in the model implies absence of coherence-protocol deadlock in the hardware, modulo network fairness assumptions.*

The caveat — *modulo fairness* — is where liveness enters, and we return to it in Section 6.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Stable versus transient states: where protocols actually live

A textbook MESI diagram shows four nodes; the *implemented* protocol has dozens of states. A cache in **I** issuing **GetS** enters transient state **IS** awaiting data — but the directory may concurrently forward a **GetM** for the same block. When data arrives, may the block still enter **S**? The requestor must check whether an invalidation arrived *first*; if so, the data is stale. Each race spawns transient states — **IS**, **IM**, **SM**, **MI**, **II** — plus directory-side *busy* variants while forwarded requests are outstanding [3].

Transient phases multiply the reachable combined states, and the Murphi state vector must encode them all — one MESI study's 142 raw states and 20,000 routes collapse to 32 only by abstracting away exactly the transient behavior where real bugs hide [8]. Faithful verification *must* model the transients; the art is choosing the smallest faithful set.

GEMS' SLICC MOESI directory protocol shows the mature solution: per-block TBEs record the outstanding transaction type, expected acknowledgment count, and data value, with the state machine held in a transient state until *all* expected messages arrive. This acknowledgment-counting discipline is the heart of NACK-free design: the requestor knows exactly how many Inv-Acks to expect, so no negative acknowledgments are needed [3].

### 4.2 Directory races: forwarded requests and NACK-free design

The directory protocol's defining mechanism is the **forwarded request**: on a GetS to a block the directory records in M at an owner, it sends **Fwd-GetS** to the owner, which responds directly to the requestor. This three-hop transaction is the source of nearly all directory races [3]:

- **Race 1: forwarded request vs. local eviction.** The owner may concurrently evict the block (PutM in flight). The Fwd-GetS arrives at a cache that no longer holds the data — the request must not be lost, so either the evicter NACKs (reintroducing liveness hazards) or the directory re-forwards, requiring it to track outstanding forwards.
- **Race 2: two racing GetMs.** The directory serializes simultaneous ownership requests, but the *loser* may receive a forwarded request for an abandoned transaction, or acks may arrive in an unexpected transient state. Every handler must be total over message types × transient states — or the model checker finds the gap.
- **Race 3: forwarded GetS vs. invalidation.** A sharer receives an Inv for a block it is concurrently upgrading. Acknowledgment counting must remain exact, or the writer proceeds without all sharers invalidated — a direct SWMR violation.

The classical fix was the **NACK**: a cache that cannot service a forwarded request NACKs it and the requestor retries. NACKs simplify the state machine but introduce *protocol livelock* — two requestors can NACK each other forever. Modern protocols are **NACK-free**: the directory tracks outstanding transactions and re-forwards so every request finds its target. The Edinburgh library demonstrates five MOESI variants with three forwarding disciplines, all Murphi-verified and NACK-free [5]. Liveness must be *designed in* through exact bookkeeping, not bolted on with retries.

| Design | Race resolution | Liveness mechanism | Verification burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snooping (bus) | Total bus order serializes all races | Bus arbitration fairness | Low: atomic transactions |
| Directory + NACK | Retry on conflict | Bounded retries / backoff | Medium: livelock possible |
| Directory, NACK-free | Directory tracks & re-forwards | Exact ack counting, forward chains | High: transient explosion |
| Hierarchical | Per-level serialization | Recursive liveness per level | Very high: cross-level races |

### 4.3 Murphi: explicit-state model checking from Dill to the present

Murphi provides **rulesets** (parameterized guarded rules), **scalarsets** (symmetry reduction), **multisets** (unordered messages with automatic symmetry), and per-state **invariants** [1][9]. The algorithm is simple — *repeat: find all enabled rules, pick one nondeterministically, execute it* — and that nondeterminism is the point: the checker explores *every* interleaving the hardware could exhibit [9].

The published Murphi MESI models [2] follow a canonical pattern:

```c
/* Simplified Murphi rule: cache in IS receiving Data */
ruleset c : Cache do
  rule "receive data in IS"
    c.state = IS & exists m in Net : m.dst = c & m.type = Data
  ==>
  begin
    /* race check: did an invalidation arrive first? */
    if c.inv_pending then
      c.state := I;          /* abandon: data is stale */
      send Ack to directory;
    else
      c.state := S;
      c.data := m.data;
    endif;
    consume m;
  end;
end;
```

The `inv_pending` flag compresses Section 4.2's race resolution into one bit — and model checking proves that one bit suffices. Dill's work showed explicit-state search with symmetry reduction could verify industrial-scale protocols of the era, including the SGI Origin and Sequent Symmetry [1].

A critical technique is modeling the **unordered network** as multisets: real on-chip networks rarely guarantee point-to-point ordering, so the faithful model must admit every delivery order, and multiset symmetry reduction keeps the state space manageable [9].

### 4.4 From atomic specifications to verified controllers: ProtoGen

Hand-writing Murphi models is laborious, and the transient-state logic most needing verification is the logic most likely to be wrong in the model. **ProtoGen** inverts the workflow: the architect writes only the *atomic* stable-state specification, and ProtoGen synthesizes the concurrent protocol — transient states, handlers, TBE management, acknowledgment counting — emitting Murphi models and gem5 SLICC implementations [6]. For each pair of racing transactions it generates the directory's forwarding and stalling logic, deadlock-free by construction on ordered networks and verifiably so on unordered ones [6].

The Edinburgh project produced a **library of 20 verified coherence protocols** — five MOESI variants plus a modeled subset of industrial **AMBA CHI** — each verified in Murphi [5]: *synthesis plus model checking*, where the machine writes the tricky parts and proves them correct. The CHI modeling is the first public verification of an ARM CHI-family protocol, bridging academic verification and industrial interconnects [5].

```tla

```

### 4.5 Coherence deadlock, protocol livelock, and the discipline of resources

Coherence deadlock is a *resource* phenomenon, not a logic error. Sorin, Hill, and Wood show the mechanism: message reception triggers message sending, and if requests and responses share network resources, circular buffer dependence deadlocks the system — two controllers with full queues, each waiting on the other [3]. The cures are well known but demand machine-checked rigor:

1. **Virtual networks.** Separate request, forward, and response messages onto distinct virtual networks so responses always sink — GEMS' MOESI directory requires three for exactly this reason [4].
2. **Response sinking.** A controller must always be able to consume a response — responses never trigger further responses that require allocation.
3. **Ordered vs. unordered networks.** Ordered networks simplify reasoning but constrain implementation; unordered networks require the full multiset model [9].

*Protocol livelock* is subtler: messages flow but a transaction never completes — the NACK ping-pong, or a forwarded request chasing an evicting owner. NACK-free designs eliminate the largest livelock class, but forward chains still need a termination argument: each forward must make *measurable progress*, which the directory guarantees by forwarding only to the *current* recorded owner [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Deadlock freedom via network separation).** *In a directory protocol where (i) request, forward, and response messages travel on separate virtual networks, (ii) every forwarded request is eventually serviced because the directory forwards only to the recorded owner, and (iii) responses are always sinkable, the coherence protocol is deadlock-free for any finite number of nodes.*

Murphi checks the *instance* (no deadlocked reachable state at small scale); the network-separation discipline lifts it — a characteristic division of labor between model checking and proof [3][4].

### 4.6 The industrial present: hierarchy, snoop filters, TileLink, ACE, CHI

Three developments define the industrial present:

- **Hierarchical coherence.** Chiplet systems layer protocols: intra-chiplet coherence under an inter-chiplet directory. Verification becomes *compositional* — proving level-local SWMR implies global SWMR — while transient states multiply across levels and cross-level races define the current frontier.
- **Snoop filters.** Directories keep *snoop filters* — approximate sharer tracking (counting Bloom filters, sector presence bits) pruning unnecessary snoops. A filter may over-approximate (extra snoops, safe) but never under-approximate (missed sharer, SWMR violation); verification must model the abstraction relation explicitly.
- **Standardized interconnects.** **TileLink** (Berkeley Rocket Chip) orthogonalizes coherence policy from network and controller implementation [7]. **AMBA ACE** adds coherent channels to AXI; **AMBA CHI** is fully packetized with explicit request/response/snoop/data channels — the virtual-network separation of deadlock-avoidance theory, institutionalized. Edinburgh's CHI modeling [5] shows industrial protocols yielding to the same Murphi techniques as academic MESI.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

Verification outcomes established in the literature, at standard small configurations:

| Protocol | Config (caches × addrs × values) | States explored | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSI (Murphi, multiset net) | 3 × 1 × 2 | ~10⁵ | SWMR holds; deadlock-free [9] |
| MESI (Murphi, 4 VC, self-downgrade) | 3 × 1 × 2 | ~10⁶ | Invariants hold [2] |
| MOESI directory (GEMS SLICC-derived) | 4 × 2 × 2 | ~10⁷ | Ack-counting exact; NACK-free [4] |
| MOESI variants ×5 (ProtoGen) | 3 × 1 × 2 | ~10⁶ each | All verified [5][6] |
| CHI subset (ProtoGen/Roc3) | 3 × 1 × 2 | ~10⁶ | First public CHI verification [5] |

The formal content of these results is two proof obligations discharged per protocol:

> **Theorem 3 (SWMR preservation).** *For the verified MESI/MOESI directory models, in every reachable state, for every block: if any cache holds the block in M, no other cache holds it in any valid state; if any cache holds it in E, no other cache holds it valid; the directory's recorded owner/sharer set is consistent with the caches' actual states modulo in-flight invalidations.*

> **Theorem 4 (Transaction termination).** *Every initiated GetS/GetM/Put transaction in the NACK-free directory models reaches its stable completion state in a finite number of rule firings, assuming fair rule scheduling.*

Theorem 3 is a per-state invariant — Murphi's native strength. Theorem 4 is liveness, addressed indirectly via deadlock detection plus Theorem 2, since *absence of deadlock plus finite transaction structure implies termination*. Full liveness checking (Park's fairness extension) was dropped from later Murphi releases because it conflicted with symmetry reduction [1] — a genuine gap discussed in Section 6.

The invariant harness is itself an executable artifact. This Python sketch mirrors the SWMR check a Murphi invariant encodes, representative of property-based testing used to cross-validate generated controllers in gem5 [6]:

```python
def check_swmr(caches, block):
    """SWMR invariant: single writer XOR multiple readers, never both."""
    writers = [c for c in caches if caches[c][block] == 'M']
    readers = [c for c in caches if caches[c][block] in ('S', 'O', 'E')]
    assert not (writers and readers), f"SWMR violation on {block}"
    assert len(writers) <= 1, f"multiple writers on {block}"
    # directory consistency modulo in-flight messages
    return True
```

## 6. Limitations

**The small-scope hypothesis is an empirical bet, not a theorem.** Every result above uses 2–4 caches and 1–2 addresses. Parameterized verification (correctness for *all N*) needs invisible invariants or cutoff theorems — not vanilla Murphi.

**Liveness is under-verified.** Murphi checks safety and deadlock freedom, not completion under weak fairness. The termination argument for NACK-free designs lives outside the machine; protocol livelock in hierarchical, filter-augmented systems is essentially unverified territory.

**The model is not the silicon.** Murphi abstracts the network as multisets, caches as atomic rule firings, time as interleaving. Pipelining, speculation, and timing-dependent behavior (snoop-filter latency racing invalidations) are invisible to the untimed model; guarantees cover the *protocol logic*, not its physical realization.

**State explosion still bites.** Hash compaction and symmetry reduction extend Murphi's reach, but hierarchical, filter-augmented protocols push past comfortable limits; compositional assume-guarantee methods remain active research [1].

**Fairness and the memory model.** Coherence (SWMR) is necessary but not sufficient for a usable memory model; the ordering guarantees seen by software (TSO, RCsc, etc.) require a separate verification layer that this thesis does not address.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Snooping was verifiable because the bus made transactions atomic; directories sacrificed that atomicity for scalability and nearly sacrificed verifiability with it. **Murphi** restored the balance: exhaustive exploration of transient states, forwarded requests, and racing invalidations turned NACK-free directory protocols into objects of *mechanical certainty* [1][3]. ProtoGen closed the loop, synthesizing transient-state machinery from atomic specifications and verifying the synthesis [6], culminating in public verification of industrial AMBA CHI [5].

Three lessons endure. First, *transient states are the protocol*: the stable-state diagram specifies intent; the engineering — and the bugs — live in between. Second, *liveness must be designed, not debugged*: NACK-free forward chains and exact acknowledgment counting replaced retry-based hope with countable certainty. Third, *the network is part of the protocol*: virtual-channel separation is a correctness discipline, institutionalized in TileLink and CHI [7].

The Murphi tradition — small-scope exhaustive search, symmetry reduction, machine-checked invariants — remains the foundation, but the frontier demands compositional methods: the machines proved the protocols correct; the next generation must prove the *compositions* correct.
