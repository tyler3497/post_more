# The Polyhedral Compilation Model: Affine Dependence Analysis, Farkas-Lemma Scheduling, and Auto-Tuned Tiling for GPU Offload

## Abstract

The polyhedral compilation model recasts *static control parts* of imperative loop programs as computations over integer polyhedra, dependence maps, and affine schedules, reducing optimization to geometry on lattice sets. This thesis treats three pillars: (1) **affine dependence analysis** in Presburger arithmetic, where statement instances are integer points in iteration domains and dependences become polyhedral relations computed exactly by array dataflow analysis; (2) **affine scheduling** via the Farkas lemma, which expresses every semantics-preserving schedule as a positive affine combination of dependence-polyhedron faces, yielding a convex, ILP-programmable transformation space exploited by Feautrier's greedy scheduler and Pluto's locality-optimizing scheduler; (3) **parametric tiling and GPU offload**, where legal tiling hyperplanes are auto-tuned and mapped onto the CUDA grid/block hierarchy by generators such as PPCG. We prove tiling legality, derive Pluto's cost model, sketch the Farkas encoding's correctness, and review Polybench results. The model underpins production compilers (GCC Graphite, LLVM Polly, MLIR's affine dialect), showing exact dependence analysis can coexist with heuristic search over a convex space.

## 1 Introduction

Optimizing compilers routinely rewrite loops — fusing, tiling, skewing, permuting — to expose parallelism and locality, but most do so through fragile pattern matching over loop nests. The polyhedral model takes a fundamentally different stance: any *static control part* (SCoP) of a program, i.e., a loop nest whose bounds, conditions, and array subscripts are affine functions of enclosing iterators and symbolic parameters, is lifted into a precise mathematical representation in which legality of every transformation is decidable [1][2].

In this representation:

- **Iteration domains.** Each statement \(S\) executes over a set of integer points \(\mathcal{D}_S = \{\,\vec{\imath} \mid A_S \vec{\imath} + B_S \vec{p} + \vec{c}_S \ge 0\,\}\), a (possibly parametric) integer polyhedron in the iteration space \(\mathbb{Z}^{d_S}\), with \(\vec{p}\) a vector of program parameters such as array sizes.
- **Dependences.** Read-after-write, write-after-write, and write-after-read dependences become *relations* \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S} \subseteq \mathcal{D}_R \times \mathcal{D}_S\), themselves polyhedral, mapping source instances to sink instances. They are computed exactly by array dataflow analysis using parametric integer programming [1].
- **Schedules.** A schedule \(\Theta_S : \mathcal{D}_S \to \mathbb{Z}^m\) assigns each dynamic instance a multidimensional timestamp. It is legal iff it is injective on instances of the same statement and strictly increasing along every dependence: \(\Theta_R(\vec{x}_R) \prec \Theta_S(\vec{x}_S)\) for all \((\vec{x}_R, \vec{x}_S) \in \mathcal{D}_{R,S}\).

> **Theorem (Schedule legality):** A multidimensional affine schedule \(\Theta\) preserves the sequential semantics of a SCoP iff for every dependence polyhedron \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S}\) and every \((\vec{x}_R,\vec{x}_S) \in \mathcal{D}_{R,S}\), the lexicographic inequality \(\Theta_S(\vec{x}_S) - \Theta_R(\vec{x}_R) \succ 0\) holds. Exact integer verification of this condition is decidable because all objects are Presburger-definable [2][3].

Because legality is decidable and the space of affine schedules is convex, the search for good schedules becomes an optimization problem over a polyhedron of schedules rather than a syntactic rewriting game. This thesis traces the full pipeline: dependence extraction, Farkas-lemma scheduling (Feautrier and Pluto), tiling legality, and GPU code generation in the style of PPCG [4], closing with empirical results and open problems.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Polyhedral representation of programs

A statement instance is identified by its *iteration vector* \(\vec{\imath} = (i_1, \dots, i_d)\). The set of all instances of statement \(S\) is the **iteration domain** \(\mathcal{D}_S\), a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-polyhedron (integer points of a rational polyhedron) [3]. Access relations \(\mathcal{A}_S^{\text{read}}, \mathcal{A}_S^{\text{write}}\) map iteration vectors to array cells via affine functions, e.g., \(\mathcal{A}(i,j) = (i, j)\) for `A[i][j]` or \(\mathcal{A}(i,j) = (i)\) for `B[i]`. Exact dependence analysis computes, for every pair \((R, S)\) of statements, the *dependence polyhedron*

\[
\mathcal{D}_{R,S} = \{\,(\vec{x}_R, \vec{x}_S) \in \mathcal{D}_R \times \mathcal{D}_S \mid \mathcal{A}_R^{\text{write}}(\vec{x}_R) = \mathcal{A}_S^{\text{read}}(\vec{x}_S)\,\},
\]

restricted to the *last write* by the classical array dataflow (Feautrier) construction, which uses parametric integer linear programming to identify, for each read, the unique source instance that produced the value [1]. The emptiness and projection operations on these objects are exact because they live inside Presburger arithmetic, decidable via Fourier–Motzkin elimination or the Omega test.

### 2.2 The affine form of Farkas' lemma

The Farkas lemma is the bridge between geometric legality constraints and linear programming. Its affine form states:

> **Theorem (Affine form of Farkas' lemma):** Let \(\mathcal{D} = \{\,\vec{x} \mid A\vec{x} + \vec{b} \ge 0\,\}\) be a nonempty polyhedron. An affine function \(f(\vec{x})\) is nonnegative on all of \(\mathcal{D}\) iff it is a positive affine combination of the faces of \(\mathcal{D}\): \(f(\vec{x}) \equiv \lambda_0 + \vec{\lambda}^T (A\vec{x} + \vec{b})\) with \(\lambda_0, \vec{\lambda} \ge 0\) [3].

The coefficients \(\lambda_0, \vec{\lambda}\) are the *Farkas multipliers*. Because schedule-legality constraints have the form "affine function \(\ge 0\) over a dependence polyhedron," Farkas' lemma converts every semantic constraint into linear constraints over schedule coefficients plus multipliers. Any feasible solution is a legal schedule; the *space of all legal affine schedules is convex* — the central enabling property of the model.

### 2.3 Toolchain ecosystem

The polyhedral pipeline is implemented by several interoperating components:

| Tool | Role | Key technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Clan / PET** | SCoP extraction from C | Polyhedral AST, OpenScop format |
| **isl** (integer set library) | Exact polyhedral arithmetic | Parametric ILP, lexmin, codegen [5] |
| **PIP / Piplib** | Parametric integer programming | Lexicographic minimization |
| **CLooG** | Polyhedral code generation | Scanning of transformed polyhedra |
| **Pluto** | Auto-parallelizer / locality optimizer | Farkas-based ILP scheduling + tiling [6][7] |
| **PPCG** | Polyhedral GPU code generator | isl scheduling, CUDA/OpenCL emission [4] |
| **Polly** (LLVM), **Graphite** (GCC) | Production loop optimizers | Polyhedral analysis in mainstream compilers |
| **MLIR affine dialect** | Modern IR with polyhedral ops | Progressive lowering to GPU dialects |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 From source to dependence polyhedra

The compilation flow proceeds in four stages:

1. **Extraction.** Identify maximal SCoPs: regions where all control flow and memory accesses are affine in iterators and parameters. Non-affine constructs (pointer chasing, `while` loops with data-dependent bounds, indirect subscripts `A[B[i]]`) terminate SCoP boundaries.
2. **Domain and access modeling.** Build \(\mathcal{D}_S\) and the access relations \(\mathcal{A}_S\) as isl sets/maps.
3. **Dependence analysis.** Compute \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S}\) for RAW/WAW/WAR pairs, optionally value-based (array dataflow) to eliminate false dependences arising from memory reuse, via isl's exact dependence computation.
4. **Scheduling.** Solve an optimization problem over the convex space of legal affine schedules encoded with Farkas multipliers.

### 3.2 The Farkas-encoded scheduling ILP

Let a one-dimensional affine schedule for statement \(S\) be \(\theta_S(\vec{\imath}) = \vec{c}_S \cdot \vec{\imath} + \vec{d}_S \cdot \vec{p} + c_{S,0}\). Legality requires \(\theta_S(\vec{x}_S) - \theta_R(\vec{x}_R) - 1 \ge 0\) on each dependence polyhedron \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S}\). Applying Farkas' lemma to each such constraint yields linear equalities between the schedule coefficients \(\vec{c}_S, \vec{d}_S, c_{S,0}\) and fresh multiplier variables \(\lambda_0, \vec{\lambda} \ge 0\), by equating coefficients of iterators and parameters on both sides of the identity

\[
\theta_S(\vec{x}_S) - \theta_R(\vec{x}_R) - 1 \;\equiv\; \lambda_0 + \vec{\lambda}^T (A\vec{x} + \vec{b}), \qquad (\vec{x}_R, \vec{x}_S) \in \mathcal{D}_{R,S}.
\]

Stacking all constraints produces a single integer linear program. Two classical objective choices then select *which* legal schedule to emit:

- **Feautrier's greedy scheduler** [1] minimizes schedule latency dimension by dimension: it finds, for each schedule row, the lexicographically minimal valid affine function, satisfying as many dependences as early (outermost) as possible, yielding maximal outer parallelism at the cost of locality.
- **Pluto's scheduler** [6][7] instead *bounds the dependence distance* \(\delta_e(\vec{t}) = \phi_S(\vec{x}_S) - \phi_R(\vec{x}_R)\) on each dependence edge \(e\) by a parametric function \(v(\vec{p}) = \vec{u}\cdot\vec{p} + w\), applies Farkas to \(v(\vec{p}) - \delta_e(\vec{t}) \ge 0\) over \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S}\), and lexicographically minimizes \((\vec{u}, w, \dots, \vec{c}_S)\). Because \(\delta_e\) equals the reuse distance for uniform dependences, minimizing it brings iterations that reuse data close together in the transformed time — optimizing *temporal locality* while simultaneously nulling distances to expose *parallel* loops. The optimization proceeds row by row, enforcing linear independence of rows via null-space constraints so the schedule remains injective.

The row-by-row construction is the practical compromise: a single monolithic ILP over all schedule rows is intractable, while the greedy/recursive formulation keeps each ILP small and still discovers permutable loop bands.

### 3.3 Tiling legality and skewing

A set of one-dimensional affine schedules \(\phi_1, \dots, \phi_k\) is a **legal tiling** iff tiles can be executed atomically with a valid inter-tile order — equivalently, iff no dependence crosses tile boundaries in both directions. The Pluto framework proves legality by requiring each tiling hyperplane to satisfy the same bounding condition \(\phi_S(\vec{x}_S) - \phi_R(\vec{x}_R) \le v(\vec{p})\) used in scheduling, generalized to imperfectly nested statements with different dimensionalities [6]. When a dependence has a negative component along a candidate tiling dimension, **loop skewing** — e.g., \((i, j) \mapsto (i, j + i)\) for the stencil \(A[i][j] = A[i-1][j] + A[i][j-1]\) — is applied first; skewing is itself an affine schedule, so it composes seamlessly. The result is rectangular or diamond tiling: PPCG additionally supports hexagonal/hybrid tiling for time-tiled stencils [4].

### 3.4 GPU offload code generation

PPCG [4] maps the scheduled, tiled SCoP onto the GPU hierarchy as follows:

1. **Kernel extraction.** Bands of parallel loops in the schedule become kernels; sequential bands become host-side loops or intra-kernel sequential dimensions.
2. **Mapping.** isl's AST generation is steered so that outermost parallel tiled dimensions map to CUDA *grid* dimensions, inner parallel dimensions to *block* (thread) dimensions, with tile sizes supplied by the auto-tuner or user annotations.
3. **Memory management.** Array regions accessed by a kernel are computed exactly as polyhedral *footprints*; PPCG emits host↔device copies restricted to those regions, and can stage data through shared memory using the schedule's reuse structure.
4. **Code emission.** The transformed union of domains is scanned by isl's code generator into CUDA/OpenCL with `__global__` kernels, `blockIdx`/`threadIdx` index expressions derived symbolically from the schedule.

Because every step manipulates exact sets, the generated GPU code is correct by construction — a decisive advantage over syntactic loop parallelizers.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The geometry of iteration domains and schedules

Consider the classic matrix-multiplication nest:

```c
for (i = 0; i < N; i++)
  for (j = 0; j < N; j++)
    for (k = 0; k < N; k++)
      C[i][j] += A[i][k] * B[k][j];   // S
```

Here \(\mathcal{D}_S = \{\,(i,j,k) \mid 0 \le i,j,k < N\,\}\) is a parametric cube in \(\mathbb{Z}^3\). The reduction on `C[i][j]` yields dependences \(\mathcal{D}_{S,S} = \{\,((i,j,k),(i,j,k')) \mid k < k'\,\}\), a polyhedron defined by the equality of the \(i,j\) coordinates and the strict inequality on \(k\). A schedule \(\Theta(i,j,k) = (i, j, k)\) trivially satisfies legality; more interestingly, the Pluto scheduler discovers \(\Theta(i,j,k) = (i, k, j)\)-style permutations and tilings that keep the \(k\)-reuse of `A[i][k]` and the \(j\)-streaming of `B[k][j]` in cache. The schedule space for this nest is a convex polyhedron in \(\mathbb{R}^{12}\) (coefficients of the 3-D affine schedule plus multipliers); the ILP picks an extreme point minimizing the reuse-distance bound.

### 4.2 Why Farkas multipliers make scheduling convex

The crucial insight is a change of perspective: instead of *searching over transformations* (a discrete, non-convex space of loop permutations, reversals, skewings), we search over *schedule coefficient vectors* \(\vec{c}_S\), and legality is exactly the constraint set produced by Farkas elimination. Key consequences:

- **Completeness of the search space.** Every sequence of unimodular transformations, plus skewing, reversal, shifting, fusion, and distribution, is representable as a multidimensional affine schedule; the Farkas encoding therefore never excludes a legal affine transformation.
- **Convexity enables ILP.** The feasible set is a rational polyhedron; optimizing any linear objective (latency, reuse distance, stride properties) over it is an integer linear program, solvable by off-the-shelf solvers (Pluto uses a customized PIP-based solver; isl exposes its ILP via its scheduler).
- **Parametricity.** Because program parameters \(\vec{p}\) (e.g., \(N\)) appear symbolically, one compiled schedule is valid for all parameter values — no recompilation per problem size.

> **Theorem (Pluto tiling legality):** Let \(\phi_1, \dots, \phi_k\) be one-dimensional affine schedules each satisfying the parametric distance bound \( \phi_S(\vec{x}_S) - \phi_R(\vec{x}_R) \le \vec{u}\cdot\vec{p} + w\) on every dependence edge. Then the tiling they define is legal: there exists a total order of tiles consistent with all dependences, and each tile is atomically executable [6].

### 4.3 The Pluto cost model and auto-tuning

Pluto's objective — lexicographic minimization of \((u_1, \dots, u_n, w, c_{S,1}, \dots)\) — has a direct physical reading: \(u\) and \(w\) bound the *dependence distance* in units of the parameters, so minimizing them minimizes reuse distance (temporal locality); once distances are minimized, minimizing the schedule coefficients biases toward simple permutations (permutability); null distances mark parallel loops. Tiling is then applied to the permutable band with tile sizes chosen by a lightweight auto-tuner: Pluto ships a heuristic tuner that explores tile sizes and fusion choices guided by cache-size models, while PPCG accepts user-specified tile/grid/block sizes per kernel [4]. Recent work extends the cost vocabulary with stride-0/1 constraints for SIMD vectorization and "spatial proximity" relations to model cache lines and false sharing [8].

### 4.4 From schedules to GPU kernels: PPCG internals

PPCG's GPU backend performs *schedule-tree-based* code generation on top of isl. The schedule tree marks each band node with parallelism properties (from dependence analysis: a band is parallel if all dependences have zero distance along it). Mapping decisions:

- Bands marked parallel and chosen for the device become kernel dimensions; PPCG inserts `__syncthreads`-free SPMD code since threads within a block execute independent iterations by construction.
- Sequential bands inside a kernel become per-thread loops; sequential bands outside become host loops launching kernels repeatedly.
- Reductions are detected as self-dependences with associative structure and mapped to tree reductions or atomics.

The exact footprint computation — \(\text{read}(\text{kernel})\), \(\text{write}(\text{kernel})\) as unions of accessed array regions — minimizes PCIe traffic to precisely the data each kernel touches, a precision unattainable with interval-based analyses.

### 4.5 Correctness argument for the affine Farkas encoding

We sketch why the ILP solutions are exactly the legal schedules. *Soundness:* any feasible assignment of coefficients and nonnegative multipliers satisfies, by Farkas' identity, \(\theta_S(\vec{x}_S) - \theta_R(\vec{x}_R) \ge 1\) on each \(\mathcal{D}_{R,S}\), hence strict lexicographic increase — semantics preserved. *Completeness:* if an affine schedule is legal, then \(\theta_S(\vec{x}_S) - \theta_R(\vec{x}_R) - 1 \ge 0\) holds on each nonempty dependence polyhedron, and the *if* direction of Farkas' lemma guarantees multipliers exist; thus the ILP is feasible. The subtlety is emptiness: Farkas' lemma requires a nonempty \(\mathcal{D}\), which holds because dependence polyhedra are constructed only for realizable dependences. Integrality is handled by restricting coefficients to integers (unimodularity of the relevant matrices keeps the relaxation tight in the cases that matter, and PIP solves the integer program directly where needed).

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Performance evidence

The Pluto system was evaluated on the Polybench benchmark suite (linear algebra, stencil, and data-mining kernels) against production compilers [6][7]:

| Benchmark class | Pluto vs. Intel ICC -O3 -parallel | Pluto vs. GCC -O3 |
|---|---|---|
| Dense linear algebra (gemm, syrk, 2mm/3mm) | 1.5–4× speedup | 2–7× speedup |
| Stencils (jacobi-2d, seidel-2d) | 2–5× speedup | 3–10× speedup |
| Data mining (covariance, correlation) | 1.2–2× speedup | 1.5–3× speedup |

The Pluto+ extension [7] proves the recursive scheduler sound and complete for all affine loop nests (closing a gap in the original formulation regarding loop reversals and negative skew factors) and reports a mean 1.33× speedup over Pluto on lattice-Boltzmann simulations with periodic boundaries, with only ~15% increase in source-to-source optimization time on Polybench. For GPU targets, PPCG-generated CUDA from plain sequential C routinely matches hand-tuned libraries on regular kernels, and its exact footprint analysis eliminates redundant transfers that naive `cudaMemcpy`-whole-array strategies incur [4].

### 5.2 Scalability limits of the ILP

A caveat documented in the literature: Pluto's scheduling time grows sharply with loop-nest depth and statement count — deep sequences (e.g., convolution followed by ReLU in deep-learning primitives) have been observed to take hours without producing output [9]. This motivates the single-ILP "performance vocabulary" formulations [8] and isl's more scalable scheduler, trading the row-by-row greedy structure for a unified convex program with richer, vectorization-aware objectives.

### 5.3 Formal results summarized

1. **Decidability of legality** via Presburger arithmetic and Farkas elimination [1][3].
2. **Convexity of the legal schedule space**, enabling ILP-based search [6].
3. **Tiling legality theorem** generalizing Irigoin–Triolet to imperfectly nested, multi-statement programs [6].
4. **Soundness and completeness of Pluto+** for all affine loop nests [7].

## 6 Limitations, Open Problems, and Future Work

- **SCoP restriction.** Real programs are rife with non-affine constructs: indirect accesses, data-dependent `while` loops, pointer arithmetic, and recursion all fall outside the model. Extending exactness to *over-approximated* domains (may-dependences) sacrifices the precision that makes the model attractive; hybrid exact/approximate analyses remain an open engineering challenge.
- **ILP scalability.** As noted, scheduling deep nests can take hours [9]. Decomposition frameworks (Kong et al.) and learned cost models are active mitigations, but a principled complexity characterization of the scheduling ILP is missing.
- **Profitability modeling.** Legality is exact; profitability is not. The Pluto cost model is a heuristic proxy for cache behavior on multicores, and GPU cost models (occupancy, coalescing, shared-memory pressure) are even cruder. Auto-tuning tile sizes empirically is the pragmatic answer, but it explodes compile time — *model-driven* tuning that predicts performance from the schedule tree is a major open problem.
- **Beyond affine schedules.** Piecewise-affine and parametric-tiled schedules (e.g., diamond tiling with parametric tile sizes) strain the classical encoding; isl's schedule trees partially address this, but code generation for deeply parametric schedules remains fragile.
- **Integration with ML compilers.** Tensor compilers (TVM, XLA, Triton) solve a narrower problem (dense tensor algebra) with schedule primitives rather than automatic dependence analysis. A convergence — polyhedral legality proofs for tensor-compiler schedules — is an attractive research direction, as is lifting MLIR's affine dialect to carry Farkas-derived legality certificates through progressive lowering to GPU dialects.

## 7 Conclusion

The polyhedral model stands as the most mathematically complete framework for automatic loop optimization: programs become integer polyhedra, dependences become polyhedral relations, and the entire space of legal affine transformations becomes a convex polyhedron navigable by integer linear programming, courtesy of Farkas' lemma. Feautrier's latency-minimizing scheduler and Pluto's locality-optimizing scheduler are two objective choices over the same convex space; tiling legality and GPU mapping in PPCG follow from the same geometric facts. Empirically, the model delivers multi-fold speedups on Polybench and correct-by-construction GPU kernels from sequential C. Its limits — the SCoP boundary, ILP scalability, and heuristic profitability — define the current research frontier, now playing out inside MLIR, LLVM Polly, and the next generation of tensor compilers. The central lesson endures: when program semantics are expressed geometrically, optimization becomes optimization in the literal, mathematical sense.
