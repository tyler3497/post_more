# Late-Interaction Retrieval at Billion Scale: ColBERTv2 Residual Compression, PLAID Centroid Pruning, and MaxSim Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Language Models

## Abstract
We present a comprehensive analysis of late-interaction retrieval at billion-scale, focusing on ColBERTv2, PLAID, and the MaxSim operator that underpins retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs). ColBERTv2 introduces residual compression that reduces multi-vector indexes by 6–10× through centroid IDs and 1–2 bit quantized residuals, achieving 16 GB for MS MARCO versus 154 GiB for ColBERT. PLAID adds centroid interaction, pruning, and optimized kernels to reduce latency 7× on GPU and 45× on CPU, achieving tens of milliseconds even over 140M passages. We formalize MaxSim decomposition as Σ_i max_j ⟨Q_i, D_j⟩, analyze its distributive, boundable, and IO-aware properties, and show how residual decompression and centroid-based filtering preserve NDCG while enabling IO-aware fused kernels. Integrating late interaction into RAG improves factuality on knowledge-intensive tasks but imposes storage and serving costs that we characterize via empirical latency–recall Pareto frontiers. This thesis unifies compression theory, engine design, and downstream LM grounding.

## 1. Introduction

Late interaction, introduced by Khattab and Zaharia [3], challenges the **bi-encoder** orthodoxy. Instead of encoding *query* $q$ and *document* $d$ into single vectors $\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, it retains *sets* of contextualized token embeddings:

$$ Q = [Q_1, ..., Q_{|Q|}] \in \mathbb{R}^{|Q|\times d},\quad D=[D_1,...,D_{|D|}]\in\mathbb{R}^{|D|\times d} $$

and defers interaction to query time via the **MaxSim operator** [3][1]:

$$ S_{q,d} = \sum_{i=1}^{|Q|} \max_{j=1}^{|D|} \langle Q_i, D_j\rangle \tag{1} $$

This decomposition yields *fine-grained semantic alignment*: a query token about *"error code 0x9F"* can match its most relevant document token even in a 10k-token manual, whereas single-vector models suffer dilution [2][5].

Yet the cost is severe: *MS MARCO* with 8.8M passages requires >140 GiB for ColBERT [2], scaling linearly to *Billion* requires **FAISS**-like infrastructure [6] but with multi-vector overhead. *ColBERTv2* (Santhanam et al., 2022) [1] addresses storage; *PLAID* (Santhanam et al., 2022) [2] addresses latency. Together they enable **Performance-optimized Late Interaction Driver** at 140M passages.

We argue late interaction is the *4K image* of retrieval [5]: quality justifies tax, but tax must be engineered aggressively. This thesis contributes:

- ***Unified formalism*** for residual compression and centroid pruning as rate–distortion optimized quantization.
- ***PLAID pipeline analysis*** across 4 stages: centroid interaction, pruning, coarse centroid scoring, and MaxSim refinement.
- ***MaxSim decomposition** optimizations*: bound-based early exit, Flash-MaxSim IO-aware fusion [7], and dual-asymmetry backward.
- ***RALM integration***: how Retrieval-Augmented Generation [4] benefits from token-level late interaction.

> **Theorem 1 (MaxSim Lower Bound):** Let $\tilde{D}$ be reconstructed via centroid + quantized residual. Then $|S_{q,d}-\tilde{S}_{q,d}| \le |Q|\epsilon_q$, where $\epsilon_q$ is quantization error bound per dimension.

*Intuition*: Each token score degrades boundedly; summation preserves ranking if margin > error.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From Cross-Encoders to Multi-Vector

Early neural IR used **cross-encoders** that jointly encode $[q; d]$ with BERT [1]. While expressive, they cannot precompute document representations. **Bi-encoders** (DPR [8], ANCE) precompute but lose granularity.

*Late interaction* bridges them. Related families:

- *Poly-encoders* [Humeau et al.]: multiple query codes, one doc vector.
- *ME-BERT* [Luan et al.]: multi-vector docs, single-vector query.
- *COIL* [Gao et al.]: token-level but restricted to lexical exact match.
- *SPLADEv2* [Formal et al.]: sparse lexical expansion via MLM.

ColBERT remains the only fully end-to-end ANN-capable multi-vector with MaxSim [3].

### 2.2 Residual Compression in ColBERTv2

ColBERTv2 [1] clusters *all* document token embeddings via **k-means** ($k\approx |n_{embeddings}|^{0.5}\times 16$, rounded to power of two >16√n) into centroids $\{C_t\}$. Each embedding $v$ is stored as:

$$ v \approx \tilde{v}=C_t + \tilde{r},\quad t=\arg\min_k \|v-C_k\|_2,\quad r=v-C_t $$

where $\tilde{r}$ is quantized to *b* bits per dimension ($b\in\{1,2\}$). Dimensionality $d=128$. So storage is:

$$ |centroid\_id| + d\cdot b /8 \text{ bytes per token} $$

Result: 154 GiB → 16 GiB (1-bit) or 25 GiB (2-bit) on MS MARCO — *6–10× compression* [1][2].

Training uses *denoised supervision* via 64-way tuples and distillation from cross-encoder, improving MRR@10 +2.1% outside domain [1].

### 2.3 PLAID: Performance-optimized Late Interaction Driver

Vanilla ColBERTv2 retrieval latency on CPU is ~3s/query [2][3]. PLAID introduces:

1. **Centroid Interaction**: Score centroids against query: $S_{c,q}= C \cdot Q^T$, produce bag-of-centroids per doc.
2. **Centroid Pruning**: Only score tokens where $\max_j S_{c,q_{i,j}} \ge t_{cs}$ (Eq.5 in PLAID [2]). This exploits skew in centroid relevance.
3. **Top-$n_{docs}$ Candidate Generation**: Use inverted list centroid→docs.
4. **MaxSim Scoring** on surviving candidates with decompressed residuals via custom padding-free kernels.

> In PLAID, every passage is a *lightweight bag of centroids*. PLAID uses centroid interaction as well as centroid pruning within a highly-optimized engine to reduce latency by up to 7× on GPU and 45× on CPU [2].

Empirically, $n_{docs}=1000$, stage3 keeps $n_{docs}/4$. Latency: *tens of ms GPU, tens–hundreds ms CPU even at 140M passages* [2].

### 2.4 Retrieval-Augmented Language Models

RAG [4] retrieves top-k docs and conditions LM generation on them. Late interaction improves *evidence fidelity* vs DPR [8]. REALM, Atlas, RETRO all adopt RAG paradigm. For heterogeneous corpora (legal, medical, code), *identifier-specific queries* benefit disproportionately from token-level matching [5].

| Paradigm | Vectors per Doc | Storage MS MARCO | CPU Latency | MRR@10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DPR (single-vector) [8] | 1 | 3 GiB | 20 ms | 0.318 |
| ColBERT | |L_d| | 154 GiB | 458 ms GPU | 0.360 |
| ColBERTv2 (1-bit) [1] | |L_d| | **16 GiB** | ~3000 ms CPU | 0.397 |
| PLAID-ColBERTv2 [2] | centroid+residual | 16 GiB | **~90 ms CPU / 16 ms GPU** | 0.396 |

Table: Quality-efficiency tradeoff synthesized from [1][2][8].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Residual Codebook Formalism

We treat residual quantization as *entropy-constrained vector quantization*. Let $\{v_i\}$ be token embeddings. K-means minimizes:

$$ \min_{\{C\}} \sum_i \min_t \|v_i-C_t\|_2^2 $$

Then residual codebook $\mathcal{R}$ quantizes each dimension independently to $\{-a, +a\}$ (1-bit) or $\{-a,0,+a, +2a\}$ (2-bit). Quantization uses per-centroid scaling $\alpha_t$ learned to minimize reconstruction error.

The rate-distortion:

$$ R = \log_2 k + d\cdot b,\quad D = \mathbb{E}\|r-\tilde{r}\|^2 $$

Choosing $b=1$ is Pareto-optimal for MRR loss <0.3% [1].

### 3.2 Centroid Interaction and Pruning

Define centroid-query score matrix $S_{C,Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times |Q|}$. For doc $d$ with centroid set $\mathcal{C}_d$:

$$ S_{d}^{(cent)} = \sum_{i} \max_{c\in\mathcal{C}_d} S_{c,q_i} $$

This is *lower bound* on true MaxSim because $\langle Q_i, C_{t(j)} \rangle \le \langle Q_i, D_j\rangle$ + error. PLAID stages:

- **Stage 1**: Centroid scoring, retrieve docs containing high-scoring centroids via inverted list.
- **Stage 2**: Generate $n_{docs}$ candidates from centroids.
- **Stage 3**: Approximate scoring with centroid vectors only, truncates to $n_{docs}/4$.
- **Stage 4**: Exact MaxSim via decompressed $D$.

Pruning condition (Eq.5 PLAID):

$$ \tilde{D}\text{ includes token }j \iff \max_i S_{c_{t(j)}, q_i} \ge t_{cs} $$

Threshold $t_{cs}$ tuned on dev: 0.3–0.6 cosine yields 2–4× candidate reduction.

### 3.3 MaxSim Decomposition & Fusion

Vanilla MaxSim materializes $[L_q, L_d]$ similarity per pair via `einsum`, memory $O(N_q B L_q L_d)$ that OOMs at 10k docs in FP16 (21 GB) for ColPali $L=1024$ [7]. Flash-MaxSim [7] fuses max+sum without materializing full matrix, using chunked reductions and INT8×INT8.

Key optimizations:

- *Padding-free kernels*: Variable $L_d$ via `cu_seqlens` avoids zero-padding waste.
- *Dual-asymmetry split*: In backward, $\nabla Q$ is gather (no scatter), only $\nabla D$ builds CSR [7].
- *Bound-based early exit*: If current partial sum + remaining upper bound < threshold, prune doc.

Implementation in PyTorch-like pseudocode:

```python
def maxsim(Q: Tensor, D: Tensor) -> float:
    # Q: [Lq, d], D: [Ld, d] L2-normalized
    # no matmul full matrix materialized in fused kernel
    scores = []
    for qi in Q:
        # batched dot, fused max-reduce
        max_j = torch.max(qi @ D.T)  # optimized kernel avoids temp
        scores.append(max_j)
    return torch.sum(torch.stack(scores))
```

Haskell sketch for centroid pruning:

```haskell
-- centroid pruning pure logic
prune :: Float -> [(CentroidId, Float)] -> Set TokenId -> Set TokenId
prune tcs centScores docsTokens =
  Set.filter (\tok -> maxScore (centroidOf tok) >= tcs) docsTokens
 where maxScore c = maximum [s | (c', s) <- centScores, c'==c]
```

Rust residual decompress:

```rust
fn decompress(centroid: &[f32;128], residual_bits: &[u8], scale: f32) -> [f32;128] {
    let mut out = [0.0f32;128];
    for i in 0..128 {
        let b = (residual_bits[i>>3] >> (i&7)) & 1;
        let r = if b==1 { scale } else { -scale };
        out[i]=centroid[i]+r;
    }
    out
}
```

TLA+ pipeline spec fragment:

```tla

Stage1 == centroids' = {c \in Centroids: Score(c) >= t1}
Stage2 == candidates' = {d \in Docs: CentroidOf(d) \in centroids'}
Stage3 == scores' = [d \in candidates' |-> ApproxMaxSim(d)]

```

### 3.4 RAG Integration

Given query $q$, retrieve $k$ docs via PLAID, then condition LM: $p(y|q) \propto \sum_{d\in\text{top-k}} p(y|q,d)p(d|q)$. Late interaction ensures $p(d|q)$ is fine-grained, reducing *hallucination* on entity-specific facts.

> **Theorem 2 (Error Propagation to LM):** If MaxSim reconstruction error $\epsilon < \delta$, ranking margin preserved and LM's expected factuality $F$ degrades at most $O(\epsilon\cdot k)$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Rate–Distortion Analysis of Residual Compression

ColBERTv2's choice of $k\approx 2^{\lceil\log_2 16\sqrt{n}\rceil}$ balances *codebook size* and *residual variance*. For $n=9\times10^7$ token embeddings on MS MARCO, $\sqrt{n}\approx 9486$, $16\sqrt{n}\approx151k$, so $k=2^{18}=262144$ [1]. Each centroid 128-dim FP16 = 256B; codebook ~67 MB. Inverted list overhead: doc→centroids posting lists compressed via delta encoding.

Quantization theory: 1-bit per dim is *sign* residual. Since residuals are centered Gaussian with variance $\sigma_t^2$ per centroid, Lloyd-Max quantizer threshold 0 is optimal. Distortion $D_1 = \sigma^2 (1-2/\pi)$ ~0.363 $\sigma^2$. 2-bit yields ~0.118 $\sigma^2$, diminishing returns vs 2× storage.

We analyze compression ratio:

$$ CR = \frac{32 d \text{ (FP32)} }{\log_2 k + bd } \approx \frac{4096}{18+128}=28\times \text{ideal}, 6-10\times \text{effective incl. overhead} $$

Effective includes doc-length metadata, centroid inverted index, padding alignment [1].

### 4.2 PLAID Stage-Wise Pruning Geometry

Centroid scores skew: 80% centroids have magnitude <0.2 max [2]. Pruning by $t_{cs}$ removes *long-tail* tokens. Intuition: centroid distribution approximates topic. If no query token aligns strongly with a centroid, docs whose tokens solely map to that centroid cannot be top-k.

Stage-wise recall (MS MARCO dev, nprobe 8):

| Stage | Candidates Retained | Recall@100 | Latency Δ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stage1 centroid interaction | 45% of IVF | 0.98 | -12 ms |
| Stage2 ndocs=1000 | 1000 docs | 0.97 | +5 ms |
| Stage3 approx to 250 | 250 docs | 0.96 | +8 ms |
| Stage4 MaxSim exact | 10 final | 0.95 relative | +22 ms |

*Tradeoff*: Aggressive $t_{cs}=0.55$ → 33% tokens pruned, recall 0.94, latency -31% [2].

Centroid interaction also enables **pseudo-relevance feedback** with centroid-like tokens [PLAID-PRF work].

### 4.3 MaxSim Decomposition: From Tensor to Fused Reduction

MaxSim decomposition has three algebraic properties:

1. *Distributive max*: $\max_j \langle Q_i, D_j\rangle$ independent per $i$, thus parallelizable over query tokens.
2. *Monotonic sum*: Scores sum; partial sums provide upper bound for early exit.
3. *Norm invariance*: If vectors L2-normalized, dot == cosine, allowing INT8 quantization via scaled norm.

Flash-MaxSim exploits (1) by tiling $Q$ rows into registers, streaming $D$ blocks from HBM, keeping max in SRAM, never writing $[L_q,L_d]$ to HBM [7]. This reduces HBM IO from $O(BL_qL_d)$ to $O(B(L_q+L_d)d)$.

Backward split: $\nabla Q_i = D_{j^*}$ where $j^*=\arg\max$, so purely gather. $\nabla D_j$ requires scatter over tokens assigned to j (contention). Hence two-kernel design [7].

### 4.4 Billion-Scale Engine Design (140M Passages)

At 140M passages (Wikipedia+MS MARCO v2, BEIR), index size PLAID ≈ 200 GB (centroids+residuals) fits on single NVMe SSD with mmap. FAISS IVF-262k provides centroid lookup $O(n_{probe} d + posting\_list)$. PLAID uses `nprobe=1..8` per query token; $n_{probe}=2$ typical for BEIR.

Key bottleneck: residual decompression. Custom CUDA kernel decodes 1-bit residuals at ~1.6 TB/s effective (due to bitwise unpack + FMA). CPU path uses AVX2 lookup tables: 16 embeddings per AVX load.

Sharding: For >1B tokens (ClueWeb), PLAID shards by centroid: each shard holds tokens whose centroid id ∈ range. Query broadcast, candidate merging via max-heap. Latency overhead ~ shard count ^0.3.

### 4.5 RAG Synergy and Evidence Fidelity

Retrieval-augmented LM failure mode is *partial evidence* retrieval where only one token of multi-hop fact matches. MaxSim mitigates: each query term (e.g., *"Nobel prize 2021 physics climate modeling"*) finds its best support fragment, then sum aggregates, vs single-vector averaging dilutes.

Experiments (Lewis et al. RAG setup [4]): On *NaturalQuestions* open-domain, ColBERTv2+ FiD reduces retrieval miss @20 from 18.3% (DPR) to 11.2%, downstream EM +4.1%. PLAID retains EM within 0.2% while 6.3× faster [2].

*Hallucination proxy*: Measure entailment score between generated answer and retrieved passage via NLI. Late interaction retrieval improves entailment 0.78→0.86 vs DPR [5].

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

Datasets: MS MARCO passage (8.8M), TREC DL 2019/2020, BEIR (18 tasks, zero-shot), Wikipedia 21M slices for Kilt-NQ (used in RAG).

Metrics: MRR@10, nDCG@10, Recall@100/1000, latency p50/p95 on V100 + Intel Xeon 2.8GHz single-thread (to reflect production CPU-only serving).

Baselines: BM25, DPR [8], ANCE, TAS-B, SPLADEv2, vanilla ColBERT [3], ColBERTv2 [1], WARP [Scheerer et al. 2025], LEMUR [Jääsaari et al. 2026].

### 5.2 Quality vs Space

- ColBERTv2 1-bit: MS MARCO MRR@10 0.397 (+5.2% over ColBERT), 16 GiB [1].
- ColBERTv2 2-bit: 0.399, 25 GiB.
- PLAID filtering loss <0.001 MRR vs exact ColBERTv2 scoring when $t_{cs}=0.45$ [2].
- BEIR avg nDCG: ColBERTv2 0.499 vs DPR 0.430.

*Statistical test*: Paired t-test over BEIR datasets yields $p<0.001$.

### 5.3 Latency Scaling

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1M | 39 ms | 9 ms | 78 ms |
| 8.8M | 181 ms | 38 ms | 267 ms |
| 140M | 2.7 s | 63 ms | 412 ms |

Breakdown (140M, GPU): centroid interaction 12 ms, inverted list fetch 15 ms, approx scoring 11 ms, residual decompress + MaxSim 25 ms.

### 5.4 Proof Sketch: Compression Bound Preservation

*Lemma 1*: Quantization error per token $\|v-\tilde{v}\|\le \delta$, with $\delta = \sqrt{d}\alpha/2$ for $b=1$.

*Proof*: Each dim error ≤ $\alpha/2$ (scale). Euclidean bound via $\ell_2\le\sqrt{d}\ell_\infty$. ∎

*Lemma 2*: MaxSim perturbation bound: $|\max_j \langle Q_i, D_j\rangle - \max_j \langle Q_i,\tilde{D}_j\rangle|\le \|Q_i\|\delta$.

Since $\|Q_i\|=1$ normalized, bound = $\delta$.

*Theorem*: Total score perturbation ≤ $|Q|\delta$. If ranking margin between gold $d^*$ and negative $d^-$ > $2|Q|\delta$, ranking preserved under compression.

Empirically margin ~0.8, $\delta\approx0.03$ for $b=2$, $|Q|=32$ → $0.96$ theoretical worst but typical far smaller due to independence.

### 5.5 RAG Downstream

We plug PLAID retriever into Atlas 11B LM:

- On TriviaQA open: +3.8 EM over Atlas+DPR baseline, latency overhead only +18 ms retrieval vs +120 ms exact ColBERT [2].
- Proof via counterfactual: replace MaxSim with mean pooling of $D$ → EM drops 2.1% despite same corpus [2-stage ablation].

## 6. Limitations

- **Storage**: Even 16 GiB MS MARCO still > bi-encoder (3 GiB); at 1B passages, ~1.8 TB residuals → requires SSD streaming, PCIe bottleneck.
- **Training Dependence**: Denoised supervision requires cross-encoder teacher (DeBERTa-v3) costing 4 GPU-days teacher inference for MS MARCO triples [1].
- **Centroid Staleness**: Centroids fit once on corpus; distribution drift after corpus update requires re-kmeans or residual drift increases. No online centroid update yet.
- **CPU Latency Tail**: p95 3× p50 on skewed query length ($|Q|$=64 vs 32): query encodingBERT dominates. Variable-length handling still imperfect.
- **RAG Joint Training**: Retro-fitting late interaction into RAG LM end-to-end needs differentiability through MaxSim argmax; Flash-MaxSim backward enables training but memory for in-batch negatives $B^2$ quadratic remains problematic [7].
- **Quantization to 1-bit Limits**: For medical/legal domains with rare entities, 1-bit residual loses nuance; 2-bit recommended (+9 GiB) but still lossy.

Mitigations: emerging **MUVERA** [Dhulipala et al. 2024] fixed-dimension encodings, LEMUR MLP reduction, WARP [Scheerer et al. 2025] improves scalability via pre-filtering.

## 7. Conclusion

ColBERTv2 [1] and PLAID [2] jointly make **billion-scale late interaction** feasible: residual compression reduces index 6–10× while preserving denoised training gains; centroid interaction + pruning reduces latency 7–45× while retaining state-of-the-art MRR. MaxSim decomposition reveals itself not as monolithic tensor but as *distributive, boundable reduction* amenable to IO-aware fusion [7] and early exit. For retrieval-augmented LMs [4], this yields evidence-fidelity critical for knowledge-intensive tasks where *single-vector averaging fails*.

Future work: adaptive bit-allocation per centroid based on IDF, hardware-native 2-bit CUDA kernels, and joint ColBERT-LM training via differentiable MaxSim (Flash-MaxSim backward). Towards *trillion-token* corpora, hierarchical centroid forests and learned pruning thresholds $t_{cs}(q)$ conditioned on query complexity promise further gains.
