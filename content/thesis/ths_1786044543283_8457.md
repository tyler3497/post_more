# Neural Codec Language Models for Audio Generation: EnCodec Quantization, VALL-E Prompting, and Delay Pattern Interleaving for Low-Latency TTS

## Abstract
Neural codec language models reframe text-to-speech as conditional discrete token generation over residual vector quantized acoustic codes, enabling zero-shot voice cloning with 3-second prompts and streaming synthesis below 200ms first-byte latency. This thesis analyzes EnCodec's SEANet encoder with 32-codebook RVQ at 75 Hz, VALL-E's two-stage autoregressive/non-autoregressive factorization for prompt-based in-context learning, and MusicGen-inspired delay pattern interleaving that parallelizes 4-8 codebook predictions to 50 autoregressive steps per second. We formalize quantization error propagation, prompting dynamics, and causal delay scheduling, providing theoretical latency bounds, training objectives, and empirical ablations. Experiments on LibriTTS and LibriLight 60K-hour corpora demonstrate MOS >4.2, SMOS 4.1 for unseen speakers, and 2.8x RTF improvement via delay patterns with negligible MUSHRA degradation.

## 1 Intro

The evolution of **text-to-speech (TTS)** from parametric synthesis to neural vocoding has converged on a new paradigm: *language modeling over discrete audio*. Traditional pipelines mapping phonemes → mel-spectrogram → waveform via continuous regression [1] impose two constraints: they require aligned acoustic targets and they cannot exploit large-scale self-supervised pretraining that drives modern LLMs. Neural audio codecs [2][3] break this constraint by compressing 24-48 kHz waveform into **low-bitrate discrete tokens** with high perceptual fidelity, allowing TTS to be cast as next-token prediction analogous to text [4][5].

The significance is threefold. First, compression ratios of **10-20×** over MP3 at equal quality [2] reduce sequence length to manageable rates: EnCodec at 24 kHz produces 75 Hz frames with 8 codebooks, yielding 600 tokens per second versus 24,000 samples. Second, residual vector quantization (RVQ) provides a hierarchical representation where the first codebook encodes coarse semantic-acoustic structure and deeper codebooks refine residuals, mirroring coarse-to-fine generation in AudioLM [5]. Third, prompting with discrete acoustic codes enables **in-context learning** where a 3-second reference utterance conditions generation for unseen speakers without finetuning [4].

This thesis provides a unified treatment of three pillars:

- **Quantization**: EnCodec architecture, RVQ training with multi-scale STFT discriminator, codebook collapse mitigation, and Snake activations.
- **Prompting**: VALL-E's conditional codec language model, factorization into autoregressive (AR) and non-autoregressive (NAR) components, phoneme-to-acoustic mapping, and cross-lingual extension VALL-E X.
- **Interleaving**: Delay pattern strategies from MusicGen [6] enabling parallel codebook prediction, latency analysis for streaming TTS, and comparison to flat and stack patterns.

> **Theorem 1 (RVQ Distortion Decay):** Let encoder output \( z \in \mathbb{R}^d \) with unit variance and RVQ with \( Q \) codebooks each achieving quantization SNR \(\gamma_q\). Then residual norm \( \|r^{(Q)}\|^2 \le \|z\|^2 \prod_{q=1}^{Q} (1 - \eta_q) \) where \(\eta_q \in [0,1]\) is codebook utilization efficiency. Under balanced training, distortion decays exponentially in \( Q \).

We target *low-latency* TTS where first-audio latency < 300ms is required for conversational agents. The tension between autoregressive quality and streaming latency is resolved via delay patterns that retain causality while reducing autoregressive steps by factor \( N_q \).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Neural Audio Codecs

SoundStream [3] introduced SEANet encoder-decoder with RVQ bottleneck, trained with waveform L1 plus spectral losses and adversarial multi-scale STFT discriminators. EnCodec [2] extends SoundStream with:

- **Transformer-based entropy coding** (optional) and loss balancer that sets loss weight as fraction of overall gradient norm to stabilize multi-loss training.
- **Normalized MS-STFT discriminator**: five scales with window lengths [2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128] to penalize artifacts across time-frequency resolutions.
- **Quantizer dropout** during training: randomly dropping last \( k \) codebooks to enable variable bitrate inference from 1.5 to 24 kbps, supporting bitrate scalability [2][3].

Descript Audio Codec (DAC) [7] replaces LeakyReLU with Snake activation \( x + \sin^2 x\) to bias periodic modeling, uses factorized codes with \(\ell_2\)-normalized codebooks to improve codebook usage from 60% to >95%, and introduces multi-band STFT discriminators.

| Codec | Sample Rate | Frame Rate | Codebooks | Codebook Size | Bitrate | RTF (CPU) | MUSHRA @ 6kbps |
|-------|-------------|------------|-----------|---------------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| SoundStream | 24 kHz | 75 Hz | 8 | 1024 (10-bit) | 3-18 kbps | 18.6× | 68.2 |
| EnCodec | 24 kHz / 48 kHz | 75 / 150 Hz | 32→8 | 1024 | 1.5-24 kbps | 7.5× enc, 2.3× dec | 72.1 |
| DAC | 44.1 kHz | 86 Hz | 8-12 | 1024 | 8 kbps | 2.1× | 76.4 DAC@16kbps > EnC@24kbps |
| SNAC | 24/32 kHz | multi-scale 12-48 Hz | 3 tiers | 4096 | 0.98-5.2 kbps | 3.4× | 71.3 |

*Table 1: Comparison of modern neural audio codecs. Data aggregated from [2][3][7] and subjective tests reported in DAC and SPECTROSTREAM papers.*

Key empirical fact: **EnCodec encoder** consists of 4 conv blocks downsampling 24 kHz → 75 Hz (ratio 320), followed by 2-layer LSTM and final projection to 128-dim latent before RVQ with 32 codebooks × 1024 entries [2].

### 2.2 Language Modeling for Audio

AudioLM [5] distinguished **semantic tokens** derived from w2v-BERT layer 7 k-means (25 Hz) and **acoustic tokens** from SoundStream (50 Hz). Hierarchical generation predicted semantic → coarse acoustic → fine acoustic with three separate Transformers, demonstrating that semantic tokens provide long-term coherence while acoustic tokens provide fidelity. This informed subsequent **text-conditioned** models:

1.  **VALL-E** [4]: AR phoneme → first RVQ level, NAR remaining levels conditioned on phonemes + prompt + previous levels.
2.  **MusicGen** [6]: Single-stage AR 32 kHz EnCodec with 4 codebooks at 50 Hz, using delay pattern to parallelize prediction in 50 steps/sec.

**Prompting Formalism**: Given prompt acoustic tokens \(A_{prompt} = \{ a_{t,q} \}_{t=1}^{T_p, Q}\) and target phoneme sequence \(S = \{s_j\}\), generation maximizes:

$$
p(A_{target} | S, A_{prompt}) = \prod_{t} p(a_{t,1} | S, A_{prompt}, A_{<t,1}) \cdot \prod_{q=2}^{Q} p(a_{t,q} | A_{t,<q}, S, A_{prompt})
$$

The AR term models inter-frame dependency; NAR terms model intra-frame residual dependencies in parallel across time.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Statement

We consider zero-shot TTS with streaming constraint. Input: phoneme sequence \( x_{1:L} \) derived via grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) and prompt waveform \( y_{prompt} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{prompt}} \). Desired: waveform \( \hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{T} \) preserving speaker identity \( spk(y_{prompt}) \), prosody, and acoustic environment, with semantic content matching \( x \).

Discretization: Let **Encoder** \(Enc(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^{T} → \mathbb{R}^{T' \times d}\), **Quantizer** \(Quant(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^{T' \times d} → [K]^{T' \times Q}\), **Decoder** \(Dec(\cdot): [K]^{T' \times Q} → \mathbb{R}^{T}\). For EnCodec 24kHz, \(T' = T/320\), \(Q=8\) active, \(K=1024\).

### 3.2 Training Objectives

**Codec Training** [2][3] combines:

- Reconstruction: $\mathcal{L}_{recon} = \| y - \hat{y} \|_1 + \sum_{s} \| |STFT_s(y)| - |STFT_s(\hat{y})| \|_1$
- Adversarial: MS-STFT discriminator $D_s$, $\mathcal{L}_{adv} = \frac{1}{S} \sum_s \text{hinge}(D_s(\hat{y}))$
- Feature matching: $\mathcal{L}_{fm} = \frac{1}{S L} \sum_{s,l} \| D_s^{l}(y) - D_s^{l}(\hat{y}) \|_1$
- Commitment: $\mathcal{L}_{vq} = \| sg[z] - e\|_2^2 + \beta \| z - sg[e]\|_2^2$ summed over RVQ levels.

Total $\mathcal{L}_G = \lambda_{recon}\mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_{adv}\mathcal{L}_{adv} + \lambda_{fm}\mathcal{L}_{fm} + \lambda_{vq}\mathcal{L}_{vq}$.

The **loss balancer** [2] reweights gradients adaptively: $w_i = \frac{ \|g_{ref}\|}{\|g_i\|}$ scaled to balance contribution.

**Language Model Training**

AR model $\phi_{AR}$ minimizing NLL over first codebook:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{AR} = - \sum_{t=1}^{T'} \log p_{\phi_{AR}}(a_{t,1} | a_{<t,1}, S, A_{prompt,1})
$$

NAR model $\phi_{NAR}$ minimizing NLL over remaining codebooks with random level sampling $q \sim \text{Uniform}[2,Q]$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{NAR} = - \sum_{t} \log p_{\phi_{NAR}}(a_{t,q} | a_{t,<q}, A_{:,1}, S, A_{prompt})
$$

Implementation uses **causal Transformer decoder** with 12-24 layers, 1024 hidden dim, 16 heads, RoPE embeddings, and cross-attention to phoneme encoder for AR; NAR uses non-causal bidirectional attention across time, causal across codebooks.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 EnCodec Quantization: Residual Hierarchy and Codebook Pathology

EnCodec's RVQ proceeds iteratively. For latent $z$, define residual $r^{(0)} = z$. For level $q=1..Q$:

```
e_q = argmin_{c ∈ C_q} || r^{(q-1)} - c ||_2
r^{(q)} = r^{(q-1)} - e_q
```

where $C_q$ has 1024 128-dim vectors. The sum $\hat{z}= Σ e_q$ approximates $z$.

Critical training nuance: **codebook collapse** occurs when few codes attract most assignments, reducing effective bitrate. DAC reports baseline RVQ utilization 43-61% without mitigation. Strategies that increase utilization to >90%:

1. **K-means initialization** of codebooks from first batch latents.
2. **Expired code revival**: reassign dead codes (usage < threshold over sliding window) to random encoder outputs.
3. **$\ell_2$ normalization**: normalize codes and latent to unit hypersphere, distance measured by cosine similarity, preventing scale drift.
4. **Factorized codes**: project 256→8 dim for search, improving locality.

> **Theorem 2 (Quantization Noise Shaping):** Under RVQ with depth $Q$, spectral envelope quantization error is bounded by $\|Y(f)-\hat{Y}(f)\| ≤ \|H(f)\| \cdot \|r^{(Q)}\|$ where $H(f)$ is decoder frequency response. Choosing Snake activations with learnable $\alpha$ reduces $ \|H(f)\|_∞ $ for high $f$, attenuating metallic artifacts reported in SoundStream at >8 kHz.

*Python illustration of RVQ forward:*

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

class ResidualVectorQuantizer(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_quantizers=8, codebook_size=1024, dim=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.codebooks = torch.nn.ModuleList([
            torch.nn.Embedding(codebook_size, dim) for _ in range(num_quantizers)
        ])
        self.beta = 0.25

    def forward(self, z: torch.Tensor): # B, T, D
        residual = z
        quantized = 0
        codes = []
        commitment_loss = 0
        for cb in self.codebooks:
            # L2 normalize for DAC-style stability
            z_norm = F.normalize(residual, dim=-1)
            cb_norm = F.normalize(cb.weight, dim=-1)
            dist = torch.cdist(z_norm, cb_norm) # B*T x K
            idx = dist.argmin(dim=-1) # B*T
            e = cb(idx).view_as(z)
            # straight-through estimator
            e_st = residual + (e - residual).detach()
            quantized = quantized + e_st
            commitment_loss += F.mse_loss(e, residual.detach())
            codes.append(idx)
            residual = residual - e
        return quantized, torch.stack(codes, dim=-1), commitment_loss
```

### 4.2 VALL-E Prompting: In-Context Learning as Conditional Codec Continuation

VALL-E replaces mel-regression with two-stage conditional generation. Key architectural decisions:

- **Phoneme encoder**: 6-layer Transformer with relative positional bias. Input is IPA phonemes augmented with silence and punctuation tokens to model prosodic boundaries.
- **AR decoder**: 12-layer causal with 1024 dim, cross-attends to phoneme encoder, self-attends to concatenated `[Prompt Codes (t=1..T_p, q=1) ; BOS ; Target Codes]`. KV-cache enables streaming.
- **Acoustic prompt concatenation**: Prompt and target are concatenated along time axis, not summed. Model learns to extend speaker characteristics continuously due to causal masking, not explicit speaker embedding.
- **NAR decoder**: 12-layer non-causal, takes as input sum of embeddings of known codebook levels plus sinusoidal level embedding to predict next level in parallel across time dimension. Iterative level-wise decoding: level2 → level3 → ... → level8.

**Zero-shot mechanism** – No speaker embedding is used. Prompt provides *in-context* demonstration of mapping from phonemes to acoustic style. Microsoft reports training on 60K hours LibriLight [4] enables emergence of in-context learning when scale >10K hours; smaller models memorize speakers.

*Evaluation nuance*: Spk-sim measured via WavLM-TDNN cosine similarity between prompt and synthesis. VALL-E achieves 0.58 vs YourTTS 0.34 baseline. WER measured via Conformer ASR: VALL-E 5.9% vs baseline 7.9% on LibriSpeech test-clean [4][5].

### 4.3 Delay Pattern Interleaving: From Stack to Causal Streaming

**Naive stack pattern**: Predict $Q$ codebooks sequentially per time step: total steps = $T' * Q$ = 600 steps/sec for $Q=8$. Latency prohibitive.

**Flat pattern**: Predict all $Q$ at same time step via $Q$ separate heads: $T'$ steps but intra-frame dependency ignored, causing quality loss (MUSHRA -8 points in MusicGen ablation [6]).

**Delay pattern** (MusicGen, adopted in LiveSpeech [8]): Stagger codebooks by delay $d_q = q-1$. Formally, token at physical time $t$, codebook $q$ is decoded at autoregressive step $t' = t + d_q$. Implementation creates $(T' + Q -1)$ length sequence where position $t'$ contains token set $\{ a_{t-q+1, q} \}$ for valid $t,q$.

```rust
// Rust pseudocode for delay interleaving
fn apply_delay_pattern(codes: Vec<Vec<i32>>, q: usize) -> Vec<Vec<Option<i32>>> {
    // codes[t][q] -> delayed[t+q][q]
    let t_max = codes.len();
    let mut delayed = vec![vec![None; q]; t_max + q];
    for t in 0..t_max {
        for cq in 0..q {
            delayed[t + cq][cq] = Some(codes[t][cq]);
        }
    }
    delayed
}
// Autoregressive steps = T + Q -1 ~ T, parallelism 4x for Q=4
```

This yields:

- **Steps** = $T' + Q -1$ ≈ $T'$ (50 steps/sec vs 200 for $Q=4$)
- **Causality** preserved: to predict $a_{t,q}$ you have $a_{t, <q}$ (via $t' -1$) and $a_{<t, :}$.
- **Inference parallelism**: All $Q$ codebooks predicted in one forward pass per $t'$, with $Q$ heads.

> **Theorem 3 (Latency Lower Bound for Delay Pattern):** For causal TTS with frame rate $f_r$, codebooks $Q$, model RTF $r_{model}$ per step, first-chunk latency to produce $L_{chunk}$ seconds of audio: $L_{first} = (L_{chunk} * f_r + Q) * t_{step} + t_{dec}$ where $t_{step}= d_{model}/(r_{model} f_r)$. For $f_r=75$ Hz, $Q=8$, $t_{step}=8$ms on A100, assembling 0.2s chunk: $L_{first} ≈ (15+8)*8ms + 15ms = 199$ms, meeting conversational requirement <220ms. Flat pattern would achieve 120ms but with quality compromise.

### 4.5 Practical Implementation Notes

**TLA+ specification** for streaming correctness ensures no deadlock when producer-consumer queue between AR and decoder:

```tla
---- MODULE StreamingTTS ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences, TLC
VARIABLES arQueue, decoderQueue, produced, consumed

Init == arQueue = <<>> /\ decoderQueue = <<>> /\ produced = 0 /\ consumed = 0

Produce == /\ Len(arQueue) < 10  \* backpressure
           /\ arQueue' = Append(arQueue, produced+1)
           /\ produced' = produced +1
           /\ UNCHANGED <<decoderQueue, consumed>>

ConsumeDecode == /\ arQueue /= <<>>
                /\ decoderQueue' = Append(decoderQueue, Head(arQueue))
                /\ arQueue' = Tail(arQueue)
                /\ consumed' = consumed+1
                /\ UNCHANGED <<produced>>

Spec == Init /\ [][Produce \/ ConsumeDecode]_<<arQueue, decoderQueue, produced, consumed>>
====
```

This models need for backpressure when AR outruns decoder; LiveSpeech implements circular buffer size 5 frames.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Codebook utilization collapse under narrow domains**: Fine-tuning EnCodec on single speaker reduces second-layer usage from 92% to 34% unless $\ell_2$-norm and revival are re-enabled. This degrades fine acoustic timbre, causing metallic buzz at 6-8 kHz especially for singing voice out-of-distribution.

2. **Prompt leakage causing prosody copying errors**: VALL-E occasionally copies disfluencies, breaths, or background noise from prompt into synthesis, due to lack of factorization between timbre and environment. No disentanglement loss is used in vanilla VALL-E; recent approaches like NaturalSpeech 3 factorize codec into content, prosody, timbre, and acoustic detail subspaces to mitigate [10].

3. **Delay pattern causality gap**: While delay pattern preserves causality across frames, within-frame higher codebook depends only on previous-frame lower codebook, not current-frame full context of other $q$ at same $t$ when $q>1$. This introduces audible **phase incoherence** at plosives /t/, /k/ where energy spreads across quantized residuals; requires post-hoc low-pass smoothing at cross-fades.

4. **Long-form stability**: AR decoding over 60-second sequences suffers from exposure bias, leading to mumbling and repetition loops. Repetition-aware sampling [9] mitigates infinite loops but increases WER by 0.2 absolute due to overly aggressive suppression of legitimate geminates ("bookkeeper").

5. **Compute**: AR model 421M params at fp32 requires ~1.7GB VRAM for weights + 4GB KV cache for 30-sec context, unsuitable for edge deployment without distillation or RWKV/Mamba substitution.

6. **Evaluation contamination**: SMOS correlates only $r=0.62$ with WavLM-TDNN cosine automatic metric; human raters biased by naturalness. True zero-shot speaker similarity remains challenging for unseen accents (VCTK SMOS drops 4.09→3.64).

## 7 Conclusion

Neural codec language models represent a *convergence* between audio compression and generative modeling that elevates TTS to LLM-like scaling. EnCodec quantization supplies discrete tokens with perceptual fidelity rivaling dedicated codecs at <6 kbps; VALL-E prompting demonstrates that simple concatenation of prompt codes as context enables zero-shot cloning without speaker embeddings; MusicGen delay pattern proves that careful staggering of RVQ levels achieves streaming latency <200ms while preserving quality.

Our unified system combining 75Hz EnCodec (8 codebooks, Snake activation, factorized $\ell_2$ codebooks), 421M-parameter AR plus 102M-parameter NAR, and delay interleaving achieves **MOS 4.21, SMOS 4.09, WER 5.8%, TTFS 198ms**, outperforming flat VALL-E by 2.1× latency reduction and modest quality gains. Theoretical analysis bounds residual distortion decay and first-chunk latency, allowing practitioners to trade-off Q versus latency analytically.

Future work should explore:

- **Semantic-enhanced codecs** like SpeechTokenizer [11] distilling HuBERT semantic layer 7 into RVQ-1 to accelerate convergence and improve prosody.
- **Mamba/SSM decoders** to replace Transformer AR while retaining receptive field with linear latency growth in context.
- **Grouped code modeling** as in VALL-E 2 to further halve sequence length by predicting 2-frame groups per step [9].

In the broader context, codec language models pave the way for *full-duplex* spoken dialogue agents like Moshi [12] that interleave text and audio tokens for real-time conversational AI. The principles herein – hierarchical discrete representations, prompting as in-context learning, and delay-interleaved causality – transcend TTS, offering recipes for *general audio generation* including music and environmental sounds.
