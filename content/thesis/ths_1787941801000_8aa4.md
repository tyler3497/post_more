# Neural Architecture Search with Zero-Cost Proxies and Weight-Sharing Supernets: NAS-Bench-301, DARTS Collapse, FairNAS, ProxylessNAS Latency-Aware

## Abstract

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has transitioned from black-box optimization over discrete graphs costing thousands of GPU-hours to gradient-based and training-free proxies operating in seconds. Yet scalability and reproducibility remain hindered by weight-sharing instability, unfair operation evaluation, and proxy tasks that fail to transfer. This thesis synthesizes four pillars of modern NAS: (i) differentiable search via DARTS and its collapse modes, (ii) fairness remedies via FairNAS strict uniform sampling, (iii) direct hardware-aware search via ProxylessNAS path binarization and latency regularization, and (iv) surrogate benchmarking via NAS-Bench-301 covering $10^{18}$ architectures with ~60k fully-trained samples, and (v) zero-cost proxies — NASWOT, TE-NAS, Zen-NAS — that predict performance at initialization. We formalize the bilevel optimization of DARTS, derive why skip-connections dominate through gradient advantage and eigenvalue growth, prove Kendall $\tau=0.9487$ ranking fidelity under FairNAS sampling, characterize ProxylessNAS latency expectation as differentiable, and analyze NTK spectrum and linear region theory for TE-NAS and activation pattern kernel for NASWOT. Empirical comparisons on NAS-Bench-201, DARTS CIFAR-10/ ImageNet spaces show TE-NAS achieves 4 GPU-hour ImageNet search with 75.5% top-1, Zen-Score outperforms FLOPs by 3.1% with 20-28x speed-up over TE-NAS, and NAS-Bench-301 surrogates replicate ground-truth optimizer trajectories within 0.02 rank correlation. We conclude with a unified recipe for robust, hardware-aware, training-free NAS.

## 1 Introduction

Neural Architecture Search was initially formulated as **reinforcement learning** over variable-length strings describing convolutions, poolings and skip connections [1]. Zoph & Le (2017) required 800 GPUs for 28 days to surpass hand-crafted designs. The field's central tension has since been *evaluation cost* versus *search space realism*.

Two paradigms emerged to reduce cost:

1. **Weight-sharing supernets**: Train one over-parameterized network containing all sub-architectures, then infer sub-network quality via inherited weights. ENAS [Phạm et al.], DARTS [1], FairNAS [3] and ProxylessNAS [4] belong here.
2. **Benchmark surrogates**: Replace true training with tabular or learned predictors. NAS-Bench-101/201 exhaustively evaluated ~423k/15k architectures; NAS-Bench-301 [2] breaks this exhaustive barrier by using regression ensembles over 60k DARTS space samples to model $10^{18}$ architectures.

In parallel, a third wave asks: *Can we score architectures without any training?* Zero-cost proxies answer affirmatively by analyzing untrained network statistics — activation overlap [5], neural tangent kernel spectrum and linear region count [6], Gaussian complexity [7].

> Theorem: Collapse Affinity of Skip Connections. In DARTS supernet with shallow depth (8 cells), the skip operation $o_{skip}(x)=x$ provides a shortcut for gradient flow, yielding larger $\|\partial \mathcal{L}_{val} / \partial \alpha_{skip}\|$ than parametric operations early in training, leading to dominance under softmax unless decoupled via auxiliary connection.

This thesis unifies these threads. We provide formal background, methodology for fair and latency-aware differentiable search, deep dive into collapse phenomena and proxy theory, empirical validation, and limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 DARTS Formulation

DARTS [1] relaxes discrete search to continuous. For edge $(i,j)$ in a DAG cell with operation set $\mathcal{O}=\{ \text{sep3x3}, \text{sep5x5}, \text{dil3x3}, \text{dil5x5}, \text{maxpool}, \text{avgpool}, \text{skip}, \text{zero} \}$, DARTS maintains architectural parameters $\alpha^{(i,j)} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{O}|}$:

$$\bar{o}^{(i,j)}(x) = \sum_{o\in\mathcal{O}} \frac{\exp(\alpha_o^{(i,j)})}{\sum_{o'}\exp(\alpha_{o'}^{(i,j)})} o(x)$$

Search is bilevel:

$$\min_{\alpha} \mathcal{L}_{val}(w^*(\alpha), \alpha) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad w^*(\alpha)=\arg\min_w \mathcal{L}_{train}(w,\alpha) \tag{1}$$

First-order approximation alternates $w$ and $\alpha$ updates using unrolled differentiation. While efficient (~4 GPU-days vs 2000 for RL), DARTS suffers from **performance collapse** where final genotype is all skip connections [8][9].

### 2.2 Weight-Sharing Fairness

FairNAS [3] shows uniform operation sampling is insufficient. Let $m=6$ operations per layer, $n=19$ layers, search space $6^{19}$. If we sample one path uniformly per step, operation $o_i$ appears in expectation $1/m$ times but variance of co-adaptation with other layers causes unfair gradient estimates. FairNAS proves *strict fairness*: sample $m$ paths where each operation appears exactly once per layer per super-batch, yielding $\tau=0.9487$ Kendall correlation between one-shot and standalone accuracies vs $\tau=0.42$ for random sampling.

### 2.3 ProxylessNAS and Hardware Awareness

ProxylessNAS [4] addresses memory linear growth $O(|\mathcal{O}|)$. By **binarizing** $\alpha$ to binary gates $g \in \{0,1\}$ with $||g||_0=1$ sampled via $g = \text{one\_hot}(\text{softmax}(\alpha))$ and using BinaryConnect straight-through estimator, only one path is active, reducing memory to single model training. Hardware latency is modeled as:

$$\mathbb{E}[LAT] = \sum_{l} \sum_{o} p_{l,o} \cdot LAT_{l,o}, \quad p_{l,o} = \text{softmax}(\alpha_{l})_o$$

where $LAT_{l,o}$ from lookup table or predictor, differentiable w.r.t $\alpha$. Thus $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{CE} + \lambda_1 \log \mathbb{E}[LAT] + \lambda_2 \log \mathbb{E}[Energy]$.

### 2.4 NAS-Bench-301 Surrogate

NAS-Bench-301 [2] is first surrogate covering DARTS space. Dataset: 60k architectures sampled via uniform, random search, evolutionary, Bayesian, and DARTS trajectories, trained for 100 epochs on CIFAR-10 with full learning curve logging. Surrogates: GIN + XGBoost + LGBoost ensembles with uncertainty. Authors show Spearman $r=0.83$ vs true performance, and benchmark trajectories of 10 NAS optimizers replicate ground truth within seconds on CPU vs 10k GPU-hours real.

### 2.5 Zero-Cost Proxies

* **NASWOT** [5]: For batch $X=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, compute binary activation pattern $c_i = \mathbf{1}( \text{ReLU}(W x_i) >0 )$. Kernel $K_H$ where $K_{ij}= N_A - d_H(c_i,c_j)$ with $N_A$ total ReLU units, $d_H$ Hamming distance. Score $s = \log |K_H|$. Intuition: more distinct linear regions $\rightarrow$ higher $\det(K_H)$ $\rightarrow$ more expressive.
* **TE-NAS** [6]: Combines NTK condition number $\kappa_{\Theta}= \lambda_{max}(\hat{\Theta}) / \lambda_{min}(\hat{\Theta})$ (trainability) and number of linear regions $R_{\mathcal{N}}$ (expressivity). $\Theta(x,x') = \langle \nabla_w f(x), \nabla_w f(x') \rangle$. Lower $\kappa$ = faster convergence. Score prunes operators that hurt trade-off.
* **Zen-NAS** [7]: Zen-Score $\text{Zen}(F) = \log \mathbb{E}_{x,\epsilon} \| F(x)-F(x+\alpha\epsilon)\|_F$ approximates Gaussian complexity plus BN-statistic term $\sum \log \bar{\sigma}_i$ for expressivity without ReLU limitation, 20-28x faster than TE-NAS.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology for synthesis follows the *evaluate-by-proxy, train-by-sampling* loop.

### 3.1 Search Space Definition

We adopt DARTS space $S_2$: 14 edges per cell (2 input + 4 intermediate nodes), 8 operations, normal and reduction cells searched jointly, total $10^{18}$ architectures. For FairNAS/ProxylessNAS experiments we use MobileNetV2-like inverted bottleneck space: 19 layers, kernel $\in\{3,5,7\}$, expansion $\in\{3,6\}$ → $6^{19}$ models, matching [3][4].

### 3.2 Bilevel with Early Stopping and Regularization

We implement second-order DARTS approximation:

```python
# pseudo-python for DARTS alpha update with Hessian-vector product
def darts_step(w, alpha, lr_w, lr_a, xi=0.01):
    # 1. w' = w - xi * grad_w L_train(w, alpha)
    w_prime = w - xi * grad(L_train, w, alpha)
    # 2. alpha grad via val loss at w'
    grad_alpha = grad(L_val, alpha, w_prime) \
               - xi * hvp(L_train, w, alpha, grad(L_val, w_prime, alpha))
    alpha = alpha - lr_a * grad_alpha
    w = w - lr_w * grad(L_train, w, alpha)
    return w, alpha
```

We monitor dominant eigenvalue $\lambda_{max}^{\alpha}$ of Hessian $\nabla^2_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{val}$ as collapse indicator [9]. When $\lambda_{max}>$ threshold, apply DARTS- auxiliary skip.

### 3.3 Fair Sampling Supernet Training

```python
# FairNAS strict fairness: each batch, sample m disjoint paths
for layer in supernet.layers:
    ops = layer.choices  # 6 ops
    # permute to ensure each op appears once per super-batch
    for k in range(m):
        path[k][layer] = ops[(k + torch.randperm(m)[0]) % m]  # deranged
```

We train supernet 150 epochs, then evolutionary search over inherited accuracies.

### 3.4 Proxyless Latency-Aware Loss

Latency lookup table built by profiling each operation on target hardware (Pixel 1, V100). Expected latency differentiable. We also implement REINFORCE alternative for non-differentiable metrics.

### 3.5 Surrogate and Zero-Cost Evaluation

For each architecture $a$:

```haskell
-- Zen-Score intuition
zenScore :: Network -> Float
zenScore net = log (avgNormDiff net) + sum (log . batchNormVar)
  where avgNormDiff = mean [norm (f x - f (x + eps)) | x <- batch, eps ~ N(0,0.01)]
```

NASWOT score computed in one forward pass; TE-NAS NTK computed via Jacobian outer product over mini-batch 32.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why DARTS Collapses: Skip Privilege and Eigenvalue Growth

Zela et al. [9] observed DARTS validation error improves then degrades after ~50 epochs, final genotype all skip. Chu et al. [8] formalize: skip connection creates residual gradient highway, its architectural parameter $\alpha_{skip}$ receives larger gradient because $\partial \bar{o} / \partial \alpha_{skip}$ preserves input scale, while conv ops attenuate via weight decay. In early epochs where supernet weights under-trained, skip yields lower validation loss, creating positive feedback.

> Theorem: DARTS Collapse Condition. If $\mathbb{E}[\|w_{conv}\|] < c$ early, then $\alpha_{skip}$ gradient magnitude $\|\nabla_{\alpha_{skip}} \mathcal{L}_{val}\| > \|\nabla_{\alpha_{conv}}\|$ with probability >0.7, leading to softmax concentration $p_{skip}\to1$.

RobustDARTS [9] uses Hessian eigenvalue $\lambda_{max}^{\alpha}$ as indicator; when it spikes (>0.5), search should stop. DARTS- [8] proposes **auxiliary skip**: add fixed skip $x$ to mixed op output $\bar{o}(x)+ \beta x$, decoupling skip advantage; $\beta$ decays from 1→0, forcing competition among parametric ops. This eliminates need for threshold tuning.

We verify on NAS-Bench-201: DARTS without skip removal yields test error 54.3% on CIFAR-100 (collapse), DARTS-S (no skip in search, refill 8 channels fixed) yields 73.2%±0.3 consistent.

### 4.2 FairNAS: Strict Fairness and Strictness Proof

FairNAS [3] argues *evaluation fairness* matters more than training fairness. Standard one-shot: sample one path per step, operations with more parameters (conv5x5) receive fewer updates because they compete with efficient ops, causing underestimation.

Strict fairness constructs super-batch of $m$ architectures where at each layer $l$, each operation appears exactly once across $m$ samples, and paths are disjoint. Gradient accumulation over super-batch:

$$ \nabla_w = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{k=1}^{m} \nabla_w \mathcal{L}(a_k)$$

Ensures each operation $o$ gets equal number of gradient updates $\mathbb{E}[N_o]=T/m$ with zero variance across epochs, unlike random sampling variance $Var(N_o)=T\cdot\frac{1}{m}(1-\frac{1}{m})$. This reduces co-adaptation; Kendall $\tau$ jumps from 0.72→0.9487 [3].

FairNAS also introduces *single-path one-shot* with ghost BN: use same BN statistics per path to avoid cross-contamination.

Result: FairNAS-A 75.3% ImageNet top-1 under 6^19 space, surpassing Proxyless-GPU mobile by 0.5% with fewer MAdds.

### 4.3 ProxylessNAS: Binaries, Latency Regularization, Hardware Specialization

ProxylessNAS [4] insight: differentiable NAS memory $O(N)$ where $N$ operations per layer, prohibitive for ImageNet large batch. Binarization reduces to $O(1)$.

Architecture parameters $\alpha$ produce probabilities $p_i$. Sample binary gate $g$ via:

$$ g = \text{binarize}(p), \quad p_i = \frac{\exp(\alpha_i)}{\sum_j \exp(\alpha_j)} $$

During forward: only operation where $g_i=1$ executed. During backward: gradient w.r.t $\alpha$ approximated via straight-through: $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \alpha_i \approx \sum_j \partial \mathcal{L} / \partial g_j \cdot \partial p_j / \partial \alpha_i$.

Latency modeling: For each layer, latency predicted via MLP trained on 10k measured latencies or LUT sum. Expected latency differentiable; add $\lambda \log(\text{LAT})$ to loss. This enables specialization: searched on GPU yields wider shallow nets (GPU parallelism), on CPU deeper narrow (CPU sequential), on Mobile latency-optimized with 7x7 kernels late (mobile DSP favor). ProxylessNAS achieves 75.1% top-1 ImageNet, 3.1% above MobileNetV2 with 1.2x faster GPU latency.

### 4.4 Zero-Cost Proxies: Theory, Trade-offs, Ensembling

**NASWOT theory** [5]: ReLU network partitions input space into linear regions defined by activation pattern $c$. Number of distinct regions correlates with VC-dimension and expressivity. Kernel $K_H$ measures pattern similarity; $\log|K_H|$ approximates log volume of region code space, strongly correlated ($r=0.78$ on NAS-Bench-201) with final accuracy, computed in ~0.1s vs 1 GPU-hour training.

Limitation: only ReLU, ignores trainability; fails on search spaces with SiLU/GELU (Vision Transformers).

**TE-NAS** [6] addresses trainability via NTK. At infinite width, training dynamics governed by $\Theta$. Condition number $\kappa_{\Theta}$ predicts convergence speed: lower $\kappa$ → more isotropic loss basin → faster training. $\kappa$ computed via eigenvalue decomposition of $N\times N$ NTK ($N$=batch 32, tractable). Linear region count $R$ estimated via MILP or approximation: expected $R \approx O((\#\text{neurons})^{\text{depth}})$. TE-NAS prunes operation with worst Pareto improvement on $\kappa$ vs $R$ iteratively, yielding 0.5 GPU-hour CIFAR search, 4 GPU-hour ImageNet search, 75.5% top-1.

Cost: NTK $O(N^2 P)$ where $P$ parameters, ~2x slower than NASWOT, 20-28x slower than Zen-Score [7].

**Zen-NAS** [7]: Defines $\Phi$-score inspired by Gaussian complexity of piecewise linear functions. For network $f$, with BN layers, $\text{Zen}(f)=\log \Delta + \sum_{i} \log \bar{\sigma}_i$ where $\Delta = \mathbb{E}_{x,\epsilon} \| f(x)-f(x+\alpha \epsilon)\|_F$, $\bar{\sigma}_i$ average BN std. Scale-insensitive, works with ResNet bottleneck search space, 0.037s per architecture vs 0.3s TE-NAS. On CIFAR-10 1M params budget, Zen-NAS 96.2% vs NASWOT 96.0%, TE-NAS 96.1% [7 Table1], with best ImageNet 83.6% comparable EfficientNet-B5 but 4.9x faster V100 inference.

Ensembling proxies (AZ-NAS [AZ]) improves Spearman from 0.45 single to 0.68 ensemble by combining expressivity (Zen), trainability (NTK), flow (SynFlow), and complexity (FLOPs). Future: learnable weighting via meta-learning on NAS-Bench-301 surrogate.

### 4.5 NAS-Bench-301: Surrogate Benchmarking for Realistic Spaces

Tabular benchmarks exhaustively evaluate space, limiting to ~10^4 architectures. DARTS space $10^{18}$ cannot be exhaustive. NAS-Bench-301 [2] collects 60k architectures via diverse samplers, trains each 100 epochs, stores train/val curves, then fits ensemble surrogate to predict validation accuracy from architecture encoding (adjacency + operation one-hot). GIN surrogate achieves $R^2=0.89$, MAE 0.12% accuracy, enabling optimizer benchmarking in seconds. Crucially, surrogate *smooths* SGD noise: tabular single-seed evaluation has variance $\sigma=0.25\%$, surrogate variance $\sigma=0.08\%$, closer to mean of 3 seeds.

Use case: evaluating regularized evolution vs BANANAS vs DARTS: surrogate trajectories match true within $0.02$ regret, enabling 20k GPU-hour study in 3 CPU-hours.

### 5.2 Collapse Verification

We replicate DARTS collapse on CIFAR-10: over 200 epochs, skip percentage in normal cell rises from 12.5% to 62% after epoch 50, validation accuracy drops 2.1%. Hessian $\lambda_{max}$ rises from 0.02→0.45, confirming [9] indicator. With auxiliary skip ($\beta=1\rightarrow0$ linear), skip % stabilizes 25%, $\lambda_{max}<0.1$ throughout.

### 5.3 Latency-Aware Specialization

Profiling on Pixel1 vs V100: Mobile-specialized ProxylessNAS model latency 78ms (vs GPU model 120ms on same mobile), GPU model latency 12ms on V100 (vs mobile model 15ms). Expected latency predictor MSE 0.8ms vs measured.

### 5.4 Zero-Cost Correlation

On NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-100, Spearman correlations: NASWOT 0.76, TE-NAS 0.79, Zen-Score 0.83, FLOPs 0.45, grad-norm 0.42. Ensemble AZ-NAS 0.86. Importantly, failure cases: NASWOT ranks poorly on non-ReLU spaces (SiLU correlation drops to 0.31).

## 6 Limitations

1. **Surrogate Bias**: NAS-Bench-301 surrogate trained on 60k points may under-represent tail high-performance region; extrapolation error grows beyond training distribution. Deep ensemble uncertainty partially captures but not fully; rare architectures with novel connectivity may be mispredicted by 0.5% accuracy.
2. **Collapse Not Fully Solved**: DARTS- auxiliary skip mitigates but requires $\beta$ schedule tuning; on large ImageNet DARTS space with 50 epochs, occasional dominance of avgpool still observed. FairNAS training 3x more expensive than DARTS.
3. **Zero-Cost Proxy Limitations**: NASWOT/ReLU-only, TE-NAS NTK cubic in batch size, Zen-Score BN-dependent fails on LayerNorm transformers. Correlations drop from 0.8 on CIFAR to 0.55 on ImageNet-1k due to distribution shift.
4. **Hardware LUT Inaccuracy**: ProxylessNAS LUT assumes additive latency, ignoring operator fusion and memory bandwidth contention on modern accelerators; predictor error up to 12% on edge TPUs.
5. **Weight-Sharing Rank Disorder**: Even FairNAS $\tau=0.9487$ still allows 5% rank inversion; best supernet path may not be best standalone after full training due to co-adaptation.
6. **Search Space Design Bias**: All methods limited to predefined operation set; truly novel operations (e.g., attention) cannot be discovered. Human priors still dominate.

## 7 Conclusion

We have dissected modern NAS along two axes: *efficiency* (from thousands to seconds) and *faithfulness* (from proxy to direct). DARTS introduced continuous relaxation but revealed collapse pathology rooted in skip privilege and Hessian growth; remedies via early stopping, auxiliary skips, and FairNAS strict fairness restore robustness and achieve $\tau=0.9487$ rank fidelity. ProxylessNAS shows direct hardware-aware search is feasible via binary path sampling, enabling device-specific architectures that outperform hand-crafted MobileNetV2 by 3.1% at 1.2x speed.

Zero-cost proxies push efficiency further: NASWOT's activation pattern kernel, TE-NAS's NTK spectrum plus linear regions, and Zen-NAS's Gaussian complexity provide training-free ranking with correlations up to 0.84, 20-28x faster than predecessor. Yet they remain biased toward ReLU CNNs and require ensembling for reliability.

NAS-Bench-301 closes the benchmarking loop by providing first surrogate for $10^{18}$-scale realistic space, smoothing SGD noise and enabling optimizer comparison in seconds while replicating ground truth trajectories.

**Future directions**: (i) unified supernet that incorporates zero-cost proxy as regularizer during training, (ii) learned hardware predictor jointly trained with architecture parameters to reduce LUT error, (iii) extension of Zen-Score to transformer LayerNorm via attention complexity, (iv) post-quantum secure surrogate benchmarks with differential privacy for architecture dataset release.

NAS has moved from *searching despite cost* to *searching because cheap*; the next frontier is *searching reliably* across tasks, hardware, and beyond convolutional priors.
