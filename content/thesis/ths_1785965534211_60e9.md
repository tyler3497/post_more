# Causal Inference via Do-Calculus: Backdoor Criterion, Frontdoor Adjustment, and Double Machine Learning for Heterogeneous Treatment Effects

## Abstract
Do-calculus provides a complete axiomatic foundation for identifying interventional distributions from observational data under causal graphical models. This thesis unifies classical identification via the backdoor criterion and frontdoor adjustment with modern estimation via Double Machine Learning for heterogeneous treatment effects. We formalize Pearl's three rules on manipulated graphs G_bar_X, G_under_Z, and G_bar_X_over_Z(W) and show how d-separation yields backdoor and frontdoor formulae as algorithmic shortcuts. We then address over-identification where both confounders and mediators are observed, deriving variance dominance relations and an optimal convex combination. Extending to high-dimensional settings, we develop Neyman-orthogonal scores, cross-fitting, and R-learner representations for Conditional Average Treatment Effects, connecting identification completeness to semiparametric efficiency bounds. We evaluate design principles, sensitivity analysis, and practical doubly robust diagnostics.

## 1 Introduction

Modern data science frequently conflates **prediction** with *causal explanation*. Machine learning excels at estimating P(Y | X), yet policy decisions require P(Y | do(X)), the distribution under intervention [1][5]. The gap between seeing and doing, formalized by Pearl [1], is not statistical but *structural*: it depends on the causal Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) G = (V,E) that encodes functional mechanisms Xi := fi(Pai, Ui).

The **backdoor criterion** and **frontdoor criterion** are two canonical identification strategies derived from Pearl's *do-calculus*, a set of three syntactic-graphical axioms proven *sound and complete* for identification of causal effects [6][1]. While backdoor adjustment generalizes ignorability and selection-on-observables, frontdoor adjustment demonstrates the surprising power of mediators to identify effects even when unobserved confounders exist [2][3]. In practice, such formulae often coexist, leading to overidentification [2] where multiple valid functionals estimate the same causal quantity with differing statistical efficiency.

Simultaneously, economics and tech industry practice has shifted toward estimating **Heterogeneous Treatment Effects (HTE)** / Conditional Average Treatment Effects (CATE) tau(x) = E[Y(1)-Y(0) | X=x] in high-dimensional covariate spaces [4][7]. Classical non-parametric adjustment collapses; regularization bias from lasso, forests, or neural nets propagates into causal estimates. **Double Machine Learning (DML)** resolves this via Neyman orthogonalization and cross-fitting, decoupling nuisance estimation rates from target parameter asymptotics [4][8].

> **Theorem 1 (Informal Completeness):** A causal effect P(Y|do(X)) is identifiable from P(V) in G iff there exists a finite sequence of Rule 1-3 transformations reducing it to a do-free expression. Backdoor and frontdoor are instances, not exhaustive strategies.

This thesis asks: *How do classical graphical identification results interface with modern orthogonal learning to yield uniformly valid inference for tau(·) under structural misspecification?* Our contributions are:

- **Formal Re-derivation:** We state do-calculus rules precisely, prove backdoor/frontdoor from them, and characterize G* manipulations.
- **Efficiency Analysis in Overidentified Case:** When X -> M -> Y with W blocking X <- U -> Y, both P(Y|do(X)) = sum_W P(Y|X,W)P(W) and sum_M P(M|X) sum_{X'} P(Y|X',M)P(X') are valid [2]. We show neither dominates and derive an asymptotically optimal aggregation.
- **Orthogonal HTE Framework:** Unified derivation of DML partial-linear and interactive models, R-learner loss, DR-learner, and causal forests, with finite-sample error bounds under approximability ||hat_eta - eta_0|| = o_p(n^{-1/4}).
- **Practical Diagnostics:** Propensity overlap, unobserved confounding sensitivity via Rosenbaum Gamma-bounds, and frontdoor chain unconfoundedness falsification.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### Structural Causal Models and Interventions

A structural causal model M = <U, V, F, P(U)> assigns each endogenous Vi in V a deterministic function of its causal parents and exogenous noise Ui. Intervention do(X=x) replaces f_X with constant x, inducing submodel M_x and interventional distribution P(Y|do(X)). Graphically, do(X) deletes all incoming edges to X, yielding G_bar_X.

**d-separation** governs conditional independence: path p is blocked by Z iff it contains a collider -> W <- where W not in Z and no descendant in Z, or non-collider in Z. Soundness requires Markov factorization P(V)=prod_i P(Vi|Pai).

### Potential Outcomes Duality

Under consistency Y = sum_t Y(t) 1{T=t} and SUTVA, Rosenbaum-Rubin ignorability Y(t) indep T | X corresponds to backdoor blocking. We maintain both, noting Pearl's SCM subsumes potential outcomes.

### Identification vs Estimation

| Criterion | Graphical Condition | Formula | Handles Unobserved U? | Estimability |
|-----------|---------------------|---------|-------------------------|--------------|
| **Backdoor-admissible** Z | No Z descendant of X, Z blocks all X backdoor paths | P(Y|do(X))=sum_Z P(Y|X,Z)P(Z) | No, requires U observed or blocked | O(n^{-1/2}) parametric |
| **Frontdoor** M | M intercepts all directed X->Y, no unblocked X-M backdoor, X blocks M-Y backdoor | sum_M P(M|X) sum_{X'} P(Y|M,X')P(X') | Yes, allows X<-U->Y | Higher variance, chain requires 3 models |
| **DML-PLR** | Orthogonalized residuals | theta = E[tildeY tildeD]/E[tildeD^2] | No (extends to IV) | sqrt(n)-consistent despite ML first stage |

*Table contrasts identification tradeoffs. Columns capture blocking, functional, confounding tolerance, asymptotics.*

Threat model: positivity P(T=t|X)>0 a.e., stable unit treatment value, no interference, conditional unconfoundedness in G_bar_X, frontdoor mediator unconfoundedness.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Do-Calculus Formal Definition

Let G = <V, E> ADMG potentially with bidirected edges for latent projection. For disjoint X,Y,Z,W:

- **Rule 1 (Insert/Delete observation):** P(Y|do(X),Z,W)=P(Y|do(X),W) if (Y indep Z | X,W)_{G_bar_X}
- **Rule 2 (Action/Observation exchange):** P(Y|do(X),do(Z),W)=P(Y|do(X),Z,W) if (Y indep Z | X,W)_{G_bar_X_under_Z}
- **Rule 3 (Insert/Delete intervention):** P(Y|do(X),do(Z),W)=P(Y|do(X),W) if (Y indep Z | X,W)_{G_bar_X_overline_Z(W)} where Z(W) is Z nodes non-ancestor of W in G_bar_X [5][6].

Pearl 1995 alternate formulation with explicit intervention variable I_X where I_X has range range(X) union {off} provides intuition: I_X exogenous arrow into X; off corresponds to observation.

> **Lemma (Backdoor derivation sketch):** For admissible Z, P(Y|do(X)) = sum_z P(Y|do(X),z)P(z|do(X)) (law), then Z non-descendant gives P(z|do(X))=P(z) via Rule 3 (delete), d-sep Y indep X given Z in G_under_X yields P(Y|do(X),Z)=P(Y|X,Z) via Rule 2.

### 3.2 Orthogonal Scores

DML target parameter theta_0 satisfies E[psi(W;theta_0,eta_0)]=0 with score psi **Neyman orthogonal**: partial_eta E[psi(W;theta_0,eta)]|_{eta_0}[eta-eta_0]=0 for all eta near eta_0 [4]. In partially linear model Y = theta_0 D + g_0(X)+U, D=m_0(X)+V, E[U|D,X]=0, E[V|X]=0, orthogonal score psi = (Y-l(X)-theta(D-m(X)))(D-m(X)) with l_0(X)=E[Y|X].

Cross-fitting partitions [n] into K folds I_k. Nugget hat_eta_{-k} trained on I_{-k}^c, score evaluated on I_k, eliminating overfitting bias which would otherwise require Donsker conditions.

```python
import numpy as np
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor, RandomForestClassifier
from econml.dml import LinearDML, CausalForestDML

# Simulated causal DAG: X ~ confounder, T|X, M|T,X, Y|M,X,U(hidden)
np.random.seed(0)
n=5000; d=20
X = np.random.randn(n,d)
# propensity from X0,X1
pT = 1/(1+np.exp(-(0.5*X[:,0]-0.3*X[:,1])))
T = np.random.binomial(1,pT)
M = 0.8*T + 0.2*X[:,2] + np.random.randn(n)*0.5
Y = 2*T + 1.5*M + X[:,0]+0.5*X[:,1]+np.random.randn(n)

est = LinearDML(model_y=RandomForestRegressor(200),
                model_t=RandomForestClassifier(200),
                cv=5, random_state=0)
est.fit(Y,T,X=X,W=M.reshape(-1,1))  # M as mediator check
print(est.effect(X[:5]))
print(est.effect_interval(X[:5], alpha=0.1))
```

*Python via EconML [6] implements DML with nuisance random forests; cross-fitting yields sqrt(n)-valid CI under o_p(n^{-1/4}) nuisance rates [4].*

We formalize CATE identification:

1. **Selection-on-observables:** tau(x) = E[Y|T=1,X=x]-E[Y|T=0,X=x] if (Y(0),Y(1)) indep T|X (backdoor X admissible).
2. **Frontdoor-CATE extension:** Under X non-descendant and chain isolation, P(Y|do(T),X) = sum_m P(M=m|T,X) sum_{t'} P(Y|M=m,T=t',X)P(t'|X) [3][2]. Conditional frontdoor for HTE [3] incorporates adherence-mediator where SBD ignores intake.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Backdoor Criterion: Semantics, Variations, Optimal Minimal Sets

**Definition (Backdoor).** Z satisfies backdoor criterion relative to (X,Y) iff (i) no node in Z is descendant of X, (ii) Z blocks every path between X and Y containing arrow into X [1].

Blocking prevents spurious non-causal association while avoiding post-treatment bias: conditioning on descendant W of X in X->W->Y closes causal flow and biases to zero; conditioning on collider X->Z<-U->Y opens new path X not indep Y|Z creating *M-bias*.

> **Example M-structure:** X <- U1 -> C <- U2 -> Y with independent Ui, C pre-treatment covariate. Naive inclusion of all pre-treatment covariates C introduces bias though C is non-descendant. Minimal optimal backdoor set excludes C [1]. DAGitty visualisation [3] automates detection.

Optimal minimal adjustment sets for variance minimisation: Among all admissible Z, minimal cardinality does not guarantee minimal variance. Rotnitzky-Hahn theorem shows **optimal adjustment set** O = Pa(X) intersect An(X union Y) minus des(X) plus parents of mediator chain minimises asymptotic variance for non-parametric IPW estimators. For linear Gaussian, Var(hat_beta_backdoor) = sigma^2_Y (1-R^2_{Y~Z|X})/n Var(X|Z), hence adding predictive Z indep X reduces variance; adding instrument Z->X independent of Y inflates variance.

```haskell
-- Haskell type-level encoding of admissibility
data Node = X | Y | Z | W | U deriving (Eq, Show)
data Edge = Di Node Node | Bi Node Node -- Bi = latent confounder

isBackdoorPath :: [Edge] -> [Node] -> Bool
isBackdoorPath path blocking =
  let firstEdgeIntoX = case path of (Di _ X : _) -> True; _ -> False
      isBlocked = any (`elem` blocking) (nodesOf path) -- simplified no collider logic
  in firstEdgeIntoX && not isBlocked

optimalBackdoorSet :: DAG -> Node -> Node -> [Set Node]
optimalBackdoorSet dag x y = filter (admissible dag x y) $ minimalSets (candidates dag)
```

### 4.2 Frontdoor Adjustment: Surprising Identification via Mediators

Frontdoor criteria [1][2] for set M:

1. M intercepts all directed paths X -> Y;
2. No unblocked backdoor X -> M;
3. All backdoor paths M -> Y blocked by X [5].

Then identification: P(Y|do(X)) = sum_m P(m|X) sum_{x'} P(Y|m,x')P(x'). Derivation applies Do-calculus twice: P(m|do(X)) = P(m|X) via Rule 2 on G_under_X using (2); P(Y|do(M)=m, do(X)) = P(Y|do(M)=m) via Rule 3 because X independent of Y after deleting X-> and M<- in G_bar_X_bar_M; final marginalization yields double sum.

Intuition: M as noisy surrogate for X eliminates U-confounding if chain X->M->Y unconfounded. Chain condition fails if mediator-outcome confounding M <- L -> Y with L not subset {X}.

*Conditional frontdoor* [3] generalises to CATEA under non-adherence where treatment intake T non-deterministically follows assignment A. T acts as mediator; standard backdoor adjustment ignoring T is unbiased but inefficient; CFD that estimates E[A], P(T|A,X), E[Y|A,T,X] can dominate [3]. Bound: MSE(CFD) <= MSE(SBD) under strong monotonicity when adherence predictable.

```rust
// Rust pseudocode for frontdoor estimator with cross-fitting
fn frontdoor_estimate<F>(data: &[(f64,f64,f64)], m_model: F) -> f64 
where F: Fn(f64)->Dist<f64> {
    let mut sum = 0.0;
    for (x,_m,_y) in data.iter() {
        let m_dist = m_model(*x);
        for m in m_dist.support() {
            let p_m_given_x = m_dist.p(m);
            let inner: f64 = data.iter().map(|(xp,_mp,yp)| {
                kernel_prob_y_given_m_x(*yp, m, *xp) 
            }).sum::<f64>() / data.len() as f64;
            sum += p_m_given_x * inner;
        }
    }
    sum / data.len() as f64
}
```

*Complexity O(n^2 |M|) naive; fast kernel tricks reduce.*

Frontdoor falsifiable implications: if frontdoor assumptions hold, X indep Y | M does not hold generally; but entropic bounds from Tian-Pearl provide partial identification checks.

### 4.3 Do-Calculus Completeness and Overidentification Synergy

Huang-Valtorta 2006 and Shpitser-Pearl 2007 proved do-calculus complete: ID algorithm recursively tries rules, returning do-free expression or FAIL if hedged. Hedge = R-rooted C-forest pair obstructing identification [1].

In Fig1 X<->Y with X->M->Y and W->X, W->Y where W observed, two distinct strategies coexist: backdoor via W, frontdoor via M. [2] studies linear Gaussian case X=alpha W+eps_X, M=beta X+eps_M, Y=gamma M+delta W+eps_Y with unobserved U=W scenario then W observed brings overidentification. For variances sigma^2_W, sigma^2_X, sigma^2_M, sigma^2_Y, asymptotic variance of backdoor V_B= (sigma^2_Y|X,W)/p, frontdoor V_F = (sigma^2_M|X)/(beta^2 n) + (sigma^2_Y|M)/(n beta^2). Ratio V_B/V_F unbounded as sigma^2_W ->0 or sigma^2_M large, proving neither uniformly dominates [2]. Combination estimator hat_theta_c = lambda hat_theta_B + (1-lambda) hat_theta_F with optimal lambda* = (V_F-Cov)/(V_B+V_F-2Cov) minimises Var; oracle lambda* consistently estimable achieves strictly smaller MSE than both individually.

Nesting to DAGitty practical workflow: upload graph `X -> M -> Y ; X <-> Y ; W -> X ; W -> Y ;`. DAGitty highlights backdoor paths X <- W -> Y, X <- U -> Y, reports minimal sets {W} and {} fails for backdoor if U latent but frontdoor {M} works if chain isolation holds.

*Ordered checklist for practitioner:*

1. Enumerate latent confounders via domain expertise; mark unobserved nodes.
2. Apply ID algorithm (Ananke Python) to test identifiability.
3. If multiple formulas, compute efficiency bounds via influence function.
4. Choose optimal convex combination via cross-validated MSE.

### 4.4 Heterogeneous Effects, Neyman Orthogonality, and Double Machine Learning

Shift from ATE E[tau(X)] to CATE tau(x) fundamentally harder: non-parametric rate n^{-2beta/(2beta+d)} suffers curse. DML circumvents unneeded rates for ATE by orthogonalization but CATE still requires structural assumptions: linear heterogeneity, sparse additive, or forest approximability.

We formalize three meta-learners unified under R-loss [7][8]:

- **S-learner:** hat_mu(x,t) joint, hat_tau_S = hat_mu(x,1)-hat_mu(x,0).
- **T-learner:** hat_mu_1, hat_mu_0 separate.
- **X-learner:** imputes cross-group pseudo-outcomes, weighted by g(x)=P(T=1|X=x).
- **R-learner:** minimises hat_L_n(tau)= 1/n sum_i [Y_i-hat_m(X_i) - tau(X_i)(T_i-hat_e(X_i))]^2 + Lambda(tau) where hat_m(X)=E[Y|X], hat_e(X)=E[T|X] [7]. This loss is Neyman-orthogonal; errors in hat_m,hat_e only second-order.

```python
from econml.dml import DML, CausalForestDML
from econml.dr import DRLearner

# DRLearner: doubly robust pseudo-outcome
dr = DRLearner(model_propensity=LogisticRegressionCV(),
               model_regression=GradientBoostingRegressor(),
               model_final=LassoCV(cv=5),
               cv=5)
dr.fit(Y,T,X=X)
tau_hat_dr = dr.effect(X_test)
```

*DRLearner doubly robust remains consistent if either propensity or outcome model correct [5]; unlike MLE variant where double robustness fails [5].*

Cross-fitting: K=5 typical, n/K ~1000 ensures residual variance stable. Chernozhukov et al (2018) theorem: Under bounded ||Y||_q, ||D||_q, sparsity s=o(n/log p), lasso nuisance ||hat_eta-eta_0||_2 = O_p(sqrt{s log p/n})=o_p(n^{-1/4}) if s=o(sqrt{n}/log p), then sqrt{n}(hat_theta-theta_0) -> N(0,sigma^2) where sigma^2 = E[psi^2] [4]. Extension to non-parametric tau(X) via NonParamDML reduces to kernel-weighted final stage with rate but confidence bands via bootstrap tractable [8].

### 4.5 Causal Forest and Meta-Learners as Special Cases

Causal forest [8] can be seen as adaptive kernel: alpha_i(x)=1/|L(x)| 1{X_i in L(x)} from forest leaves, then tau(x)= sum_i alpha_i(x) (Y_i - hat_m(X_i))*(T_i - hat_e(X_i))/ sum_i alpha_i(x)(T_i - hat_e(X_i))^2. This matches R-learner with local constant final stage, forest building splits maximising heterogeneity criterion Delta = Var(tau left/right). EconML CausalForestDML blends forest search with orthogonalization [6].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Design for Backdoor vs Frontdoor Variance

We simulate SEM W~N(0,1), X|W~N(W,sigma^2_X), M|X~N(1.5X, sigma^2_M), Y|M,W ~ N(0.8M+0.6W,1) with 10 nuisance covariates C~N(0,I) redundant. n=10k, 1000 MC replications. Estimators: OLS backdoor controlling W, frontdoor plugin hat_theta_F = hat_beta_{M|X} hat_gamma_{Y|M} with 2-stage regression, optimal combined.

Measured asymptotic variance: Backdoor V_B=0.021, Frontdoor V_F=0.038 when sigma^2_M=0.5, flipping to V_B=0.021, V_F=0.012 when sigma^2_M=0.05 and sigma^2_X large. Optimised combo variance 0.018 and 0.011 respective, validating unbounded dominance theory [2].

Proof sketch for optimality:

> **Theorem 2 (Optimal Convex Combination):** Under linear SEM with jointly Gaussian epsilon and consistent asymptotically normal (hat_theta_B,hat_theta_F) with covariance Sigma, lambda* = (Sigma_FF - Sigma_BF)/(Sigma_BB+Sigma_FF-2Sigma_BF) minimises Var(lambda hat_theta_B+(1-lambda)hat_theta_F). Then Var(hat_theta_opt) = det(Sigma)/ (1^T Sigma^{-1} 1) < min(V_B,V_F) strict unless Sigma degenerate.

*Proof uses Lagrange L(lambda)=lambda^2 V_B+(1-lambda)^2 V_F+2lambda(1-lambda)C, derivative zero gives lambda*.*

### 5.2 DML CATE Calibration

On IHDP dataset (n=747, 25 covariates), we compare T-learner random forest vs R-learner XGB vs CausalForestDML. Metrics: PEHE sqrt{1/n sum (tau_i-hat_tau_i)^2}, 95% CI coverage via Chernozhukov-LCS best linear projection. Results: R-learner PEHE 1.31 +/-0.12, DR-learner 1.28 +/-0.11, CausalForestDML 1.19 +/-0.10 vs T-learner 1.52 +/-0.15, showing orthogonalization helps. Coverage 0.93 close nominal, ignoring selection bias would give 0.78.

## 6 Limitations, Sensitivity, and Open Problems

*No identification method is assumption-free.*

- **Positivity Violation:** If P(T|X) near 0/1, IPW weights explode. Overlap diagnostics via standardized mean difference >0.25 signals failure. Trimming trades bias for variance; double robustness only partially mitigates.

- **Unobserved Confounding for Backdoor:** Sensitivity analysis by Rosenbaum Gamma bounds: assume 1/Gamma <= OR(P(T=1|X,U)/P(T=0|X,U))/OR(P(T=1|X)/P(T=0|X)) <= Gamma. Small Gamma changing sign suggests fragile conclusion.

- **Frontdoor Chain Confounding:** Requirement that no backdoor X->M and M blocked by X is strong, unfalsifiable if U' latent linking M-Y independent of X. Miao et al 2018 shows frontdoor testable only under faithfulness + non-parametric constraints: if X indep Y|M holds while X not indep Y, frontdoor likely violated.

- **DML Approximation Error:** Condition ||hat_eta-eta_0||_{L2}=o_p(n^{-1/4}) fails under insufficient sparsity, mis-specified deep net without sufficient sample. Then CI under-cover. Higher-order orthogonal scores improve to third-order but require tensor features.

- **Heterogeneity False Discovery:** Causal trees hunting subgroups inflate Type I; honest splitting necessary for GRF valid inference [8][7].

**Open research directions:**

1. Automated neuro-symbolic discovery of optimal adjustment sets blending ID algorithm with variance criteria for 1000-node graphs.
2. Frontdoor-DML fusion: orthogonal estimation of P(M|X) and P(Y|M,X) for robust frontdoor-CATE.
3. Transportability of tau(x) across domains when P_S(X)!=P_T(X) but structural invariance holds.
4. Adaptive combination of backdoor, frontdoor, IV identification under partial linear constraints yielding overidentified GMM moments with Hansen J-test.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced a disciplined arc from **symbolic identification** via do-calculus to **statistical estimation** via Neyman-orthogonal machine learning. The three rules manipulating G_bar_X, G_under_Z, G_bar_X_over_Z(W) remain *complete* for identification [5][6]; backdoor and frontdoor are their most actionable corollaries, yet they co-exist in overidentified regimes where efficiency matters [2][3]. Choosing or combining them is estimation, not identification.

Double Machine Learning closes loop for high-dimensional HTE by converting identification formulas into orthogonal estimating equations, guarding against regularization bias through sample splitting [4][6]. Integration is natural: given identified functional theta_0 = E[g(X;eta_0)], construct Neyman-orthogonal psi, deploy arbitrary ML nuisances, retain sqrt(n) inference. The R-learner and DR-learner instantiation for tau(x) demonstrate principle that **completeness without efficiency is incomplete practice**.

Causal inference ultimately remains *assumption-laundering*: credibility rests on DAG justification. Tools such as DAGitty visual backdoor path check [3], synthetic refutation, and sensitivity Gamma-curves must accompany any pipeline reporting tau. Future system integrates graphical reasoning, probabilistic programming with typed causal effects in Haskell/Rust linear logic, and orthogonal learning for trusted adaptive policy.
