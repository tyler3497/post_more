# Score-Based Generative Modeling: Denoising Score Matching, Reverse-Time SDEs, and Probability Flow ODEs

## Abstract

Score-based generative modeling reframes synthesis as the inversion of a diffusion process: rather than modeling the density itself, one learns its *score* — the gradient of the log-density — and uses it to drive sampling dynamics. This work unifies the theoretical foundations of modern diffusion models, from the noise-conditional score networks of Song and Ermon to the continuous-time stochastic differential equation (SDE) framework of Song et al., within which denoising diffusion probabilistic models appear as a special discretization. We derive the denoising score matching identity, show how Anderson's reverse-time diffusion theorem converts a known score field into a generative process, and explain the probability flow ordinary differential equation (ODE) that shares all marginals with the reverse SDE while enabling exact likelihood computation via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula. We discuss practical sampler design, likelihood estimation, and the empirical record of score-based models on image synthesis benchmarks. The analysis closes with the regime limitations of score estimation — particularly under manifold-supported data distributions — and identifies open questions at the interface of stochastic analysis and generative learning.

## 1 Introduction

The core difficulty of generative modeling is the normalizing constant. For an unnormalized density $p_\theta(x) = \tilde{p}_\theta(x)/Z(\theta)$, maximum-likelihood training requires $\nabla_\theta \log Z(\theta)$, an integral over the entire state space that is intractable in high dimensions [1]. *Score matching*, introduced by Hyvärinen, circumvents the partition function by targeting the Fisher divergence between the model's score $\nabla_x \log p_\theta(x)$ and the data score $\nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}(x)$; because differentiation with respect to $x$ annihilates $\log Z(\theta)$, the objective depends only on derivatives [1].

The score is a natural object for sampling as well: gradients of the log-density point toward regions of high probability, so a sampler that follows them — perturbed by noise to ensure exploration — traces the target distribution. This observation anchors *score-based generative models* (SBGMs). The modern lineage runs from denoising score matching and noise-conditional score networks (NCSN) [2][3] through denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) [4] to the unified SDE framework of Song et al., which recasts both as discretizations of a single continuous-time reverse process [5]. Two insights complete the picture:

1. **The denoising identity.** Matching the score of the *perturbed* data distribution reduces, via an integration-by-parts argument, to a denoising regression with a closed-form Gaussian target — no second derivatives, no partition function [6].
2. **Time reversal of diffusions.** Anderson's theorem shows that the time reversal of a diffusion process is itself a diffusion whose drift depends on the score of the marginal densities [7]. A learned score field therefore *is* a generative model.

A third development — the *probability flow ODE* [5] — extracts a deterministic dynamics with identical marginals to the reverse SDE, unlocking exact likelihood evaluation and invertible encodings. This essay develops these three pillars with the mathematical precision they deserve.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Score matching and the Stein score

Let $p_{\text{data}}(x)$ be a density on $\mathbb{R}^d$ and define its *Stein score* as the vector field $s(x) := \nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}(x)$. Score matching minimizes

$$
J(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}\big[\|s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}(x)\|^2\big],
$$

the Fisher divergence [1]. Under mild regularity conditions, integration by parts removes the unknown data score, yielding the *implicit score matching* objective involving $\text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta)$ and $\|s_\theta\|^2$ — computable from data but requiring a Jacobian trace, which is expensive in high dimensions and can be stabilized with Hutchinson-type estimators. The denoising formulation eliminates this cost entirely.

### 2.2 Diffusion processes and the Fokker–Planck equation

Consider an Itô SDE on $t \in [0, T]$,

$$
dx = f(x, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw,
$$

with drift $f: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$, scalar diffusion coefficient $g: [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}$, and $w$ a standard Wiener process. The density $p_t(x)$ of $x_t$ evolves under the Fokker–Planck (Kolmogorov forward) equation

$$
\partial_t p_t = -\nabla \cdot (f p_t) + \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \Delta p_t.
$$

Two canonical choices dominate generative practice: the **variance-exploding (VE)** SDE ($f \equiv 0$, growing $g$) corresponding to NCSN, and the **variance-preserving (VP)** SDE ($f(x,t) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)x$, $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$), whose discrete-time analogue is DDPM [5][4]. Both drive $p_t$ toward a tractable prior (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$) as $t \to T$.

> **Theorem:** (Anderson, 1982) Let $dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw$ be a forward diffusion with marginal densities $p_t$. Under regularity conditions on $f$, $g$, and $p_t$, the time-reversed process $x_{T-t}$ satisfies the *reverse-time SDE*
>
> $$
> dx = \big[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w},
> $$
>
> where $\bar{w}$ is a Wiener process in reversed time [7].

The message is stark: **the only unknown in the generative reverse process is the score** $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ at each time $t$.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Denoising score matching

Perturb data with isotropic Gaussian noise: $\tilde{x} \mid x \sim \mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 I)$, inducing $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}) = \int p_{\text{data}}(x)\,p_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)\,dx$. Matching the score of $q_\sigma$ appears to require $\nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log q_\sigma$, an intractable convolution — but Vincent's identity shows the two objectives coincide [6]:

> **Theorem:** (Denoising score matching [6]) For any parametric score model $s_\theta$,
>
> $$
> \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}\sim q_\sigma}\big[\|s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})\|^2\big]
> = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}\sim p_\sigma(\cdot\mid x)}\big[\|s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log p_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)\|^2\big] + C,
> $$
>
> where $C$ is independent of $\theta$.
>
> **Proof:** Expand the left-hand quadratic; the cross term $\mathbb{E}_{q_\sigma}[s_\theta(\tilde{x})^\top \nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})]$ equals $\int s_\theta(\tilde{x})^\top \nabla_{\tilde{x}} q_\sigma(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}$; substituting the convolution for $q_\sigma$ and differentiating under the integral sign moves the gradient onto the known kernel $p_\sigma$, completing the square. ∎

For Gaussian kernels, $\nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log p_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x) = -(\tilde{x} - x)/\sigma^2$, so training is plain denoising regression: predict the noise injected into the data. This single identity is the training workhorse of every modern diffusion model.

### 3.2 Noise-conditional score networks

A single $\sigma$ is insufficient: at tiny noise levels the score is ill-defined where data concentrate on low-dimensional manifolds, and at large noise levels fine detail is destroyed [2]. NCSN trains one network $s_\theta(x, \sigma_i)$ across a geometric ladder $\{\sigma_i\}_{i=1}^L$, with a weighted sum of denoising objectives. Sampling proceeds by *annealed Langevin dynamics*: run Langevin steps at each noise level, decreasing $\sigma_i$ so the chain tracks the mode structure from coarse to fine [2][3]. Empirically, this yielded CIFAR-10 Inception scores rivaling contemporary GANs — a watershed moment for likelihood-free, adversarial-free synthesis [2].

### 3.3 The SDE unification

Song et al. let $L \to \infty$ and pass to continuous time: the noise ladder becomes the forward SDE above, the score network becomes $s_\theta(x, t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, and the training objective becomes a continuous-time denoising score matching loss

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,T]}\,\mathbb{E}_{x_0 \sim p_0}\,\mathbb{E}_{x_t \sim p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)}\big[\lambda(t)\,\|s_\theta(x_t, t) - \nabla_{x_t}\log p_{0t}(x_t\mid x_0)\|^2\big],
$$

where $p_{0t}$ is the analytically known forward transition kernel (Gaussian for affine drift) and $\lambda(t)$ a positive weighting [5]. DDPM emerges as the VP-SDE discretization with the "simple" noise-prediction objective, related to the score by Tweedie's formula $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \approx -\sigma_t \nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)$ [4][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Anatomy of the reverse-time SDE

Substituting $s_\theta(x,t)$ into Anderson's formula yields the generative process

$$
dx = \big[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 s_\theta(x,t)\big]\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w},\qquad x_T \sim p_T \approx \mathcal{N}(0, I).
$$

The drift has two competing terms: $-g(t)^2 s_\theta$ pulls trajectories *uphill* on the log-density landscape toward data modes, while $g(t)\,d\bar{w}$ injects noise to maintain the correct stationary fluctuations. Drop the noise and one obtains deterministic ascent to a mode (MAP-like); keep it and the path ensemble reproduces the full distribution — at least when the score estimate is exact and the discretization is fine.

### 4.2 The probability flow ODE

Song et al. prove a remarkable fact: the *deterministic* ODE

$$
dx = \Big[f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2 s_\theta(x,t)\Big]\,dt
$$

induces exactly the same marginal densities $\{p_t\}$ as the reverse-time SDE whenever $s_\theta$ matches the true score [5]. The diffusion term's contribution to the Fokker–Planck flux can be absorbed into the drift because the flux depends only on the score. Consequences are far-reaching:

| Property | Reverse SDE | Probability flow ODE |
|---|---|---|
| Trajectory | Stochastic | Deterministic |
| Marginals $\{p_t\}$ | Identical | Identical |
| Exact likelihood | No | Yes, via adjoint |
| Latent encoding | Non-unique | Bijective |
| Sampling speed | Slower (fine steps) | Faster (adaptive solvers) |

For exact likelihoods, integrate the *instantaneous change-of-variables* formula alongside the ODE [5]:

$$
\frac{d\log p_t(x_t)}{dt} = -\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\nabla_x \tilde{f}_\theta(x_t, t)\right),\qquad \tilde{f}_\theta := f - \tfrac{1}{2}g^2 s_\theta,
$$

estimating the trace with the Hutchinson–Skilling estimator to avoid $O(d^2)$ Jacobians. This made diffusion models competitive density estimators, reporting $2.99$ bits/dim on CIFAR-10 [5].

### 4.3 Predictor–corrector samplers

The framework invites hybrid *predictor–corrector* (PC) samplers: a numerical SDE solver ("predictor") advances the reverse process, then Langevin MCMC steps using the score ("corrector") pull the iterate back onto $p_t$ [5]. This decouples discretization error from score error and explains why ancestral DDPM sampling, DDIM-style deterministic updates, and Heun-corrected ODE solves are all instances of one design space, later systematized by Karras et al. into a modular sampler taxonomy [8].

### 4.4 Conditioning and guidance

Because the score is a gradient field, conditioning reduces to Bayes' rule in log-space: $\nabla_x \log p_t(x\mid y) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x) + \nabla_x \log p_t(y\mid x)$. *Classifier guidance* trains an auxiliary $p_t(y\mid x)$; *classifier-free guidance* instead trains conditional and unconditional score models jointly and extrapolates their difference, $\tilde{s} = s_\theta(x,t,\varnothing) + w\,(s_\theta(x,t,y) - s_\theta(x,t,\varnothing))$, amplifying class fidelity at the cost of diversity [5]. The same log-space addition yields inpainting and colorization by masking the score field — all without retraining.

### 4.5 What the score actually learns

A subtle point: with $p_{0t}$ Gaussian, the regression target $-(\,x_t - \alpha_t x_0\,)/\sigma_t^2$ makes the network a *multi-scale denoiser*. At large $t$ it recovers global layout; at small $t$ it fills texture. The learned $s_\theta(\cdot, t)$ is thus a vector field that is simultaneously a density gradient, an optimal denoiser, and — via Tweedie — a posterior mean estimator. This triple identity is why diffusion training is so stable compared with adversarial alternatives: the objective is a weighted sum of ordinary least-squares problems [4][5][6].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Sample quality and likelihood

The empirical trajectory is decisive. NCSN reached an Inception score of $8.87$ on CIFAR-10 in 2019, then a state of the art [2]; its stabilized successor NCSNv2 improved training dynamics via exponential moving averages and refined noise schedules [3]. DDPM obtained an Inception score of $9.46$ and FID $3.17$ on CIFAR-10, plus $256{\times}256$ LSUN samples rivaling ProgressiveGAN [4]. The score-SDE model pushed further: **FID $2.20$**, Inception $9.89$ on CIFAR-10, $2.99$ bits/dim likelihood, and $1024{\times}1024$ CelebA-HQ synthesis — demonstrating that the continuous framework improves both sample quality and likelihood simultaneously [5].

### 5.2 Sketch: equivalence of the training objectives

> **Theorem:** (informal, [5]) With the VP SDE and a specific choice of $\lambda(t)$, the continuous-time denoising score matching loss equals, up to constants, the DDPM variational bound. Minimizing it maximizes a lower bound on the likelihood of the plug-in reverse SDE.

> **Proof:** *(Sketch.)* Discretize the VP SDE on the DDPM noise schedule; the transition kernels $p_{0t}$ become the DDPM forward posteriors $q(x_t\mid x_0)$. The denoising score matching term $\|s_\theta - \nabla\log p_{0t}\|^2$ is then proportional, via the Gaussian score identity, to $\|\epsilon_\theta - \epsilon\|^2$, the DDPM "simple" loss. Taking the mesh to zero recovers the continuous objective; the variational argument of [4] shows the discrete loss bounds $\log p_\theta(x_0)$. ∎

This resolves a conceptual puzzle: DDPM's noise-prediction heuristic *is* score matching in disguise.

### 5.3 Empirical validation of the ODE

Ablations in [5] confirm that the probability flow ODE with a black-box adaptive solver (e.g., Dormand–Prince) attains likelihoods matching the SDE sampler's, while requiring an order of magnitude fewer function evaluations for comparable sample quality. Uniquely identifiable encodings — $x_0 = \mathrm{ODEsolve}(x_T)$ and back — enable latent interpolation and temperature-controlled sampling, properties stochastic samplers cannot offer.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Manifold-supported data.** When $p_{\text{data}}$ concentrates on a low-dimensional manifold, $\nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}$ is undefined off-manifold and the score estimate extrapolates arbitrarily there. Noise perturbation (the $q_\sigma$ construction) is a *remedy*, not a cure: it biases the learned score toward the smoothed distribution, and the bias–variance trade-off across $\sigma$ remains theoretically incompletely characterized [2][5].
2. **Score error compounding.** The reverse SDE analysis assumes $s_\theta = \nabla \log p_t$ exactly. In practice, errors at large $t$ shift the trajectory into regions where the score network was never trained (covariate shift along the path), and no finite-sample bound currently controls the induced Wasserstein gap tightly in high dimensions.
3. **Sampling cost.** Even with ODE solvers and distillation, high-fidelity synthesis requires tens to hundreds of network evaluations — orders of magnitude more than a GAN forward pass. Few-step and single-step distillation methods reduce but do not eliminate this gap.
4. **Likelihood–quality tension.** The weighting $\lambda(t)$ that optimizes sample quality (emphasizing perceptually relevant mid noise levels) differs from the maximum-likelihood weighting, so one model rarely dominates both metrics [5][8].
5. **Discrete data.** The entire SDE apparatus assumes continuous state spaces; text and other discrete modalities require dequantization, embedding-space diffusion, or discrete-state Markov formulations outside this framework.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Score-based generative modeling derives its power from a single mathematical fact: *the score is all you need to reverse a diffusion*. Denoising score matching makes the score learnable by ordinary regression [6]; Anderson's theorem turns the learned score into a generative SDE [7]; the probability flow ODE extracts determinism, likelihoods, and invertibility from the same field [5]. Together they unify NCSN, DDPM, and their descendants into one continuous-time theory with matching empirical dominance.

Open directions abound: non-asymptotic guarantees for score estimation under manifold hypotheses, principled noise-schedule design via stochastic optimal control, and score-like formulations for discrete and structured domains. What began as a trick to avoid the partition function has become the dominant paradigm of generative learning — a reminder that in high dimensions, gradients of log-density are often more tractable, and more useful, than densities themselves.
