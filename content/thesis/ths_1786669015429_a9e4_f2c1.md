# Learned Video Compression with Neural Fields: Implicit Neural Representations versus Conditional VAEs, Scale-Space Flow Warping, Entropy-Constrained Vector Quantization, and QUIC-Aware Rate-Distortion Optimization

## Abstract
Learned video compression has diverged into two philosophical extremes: **overfitted neural fields** that memorize a single video as weights, and **conditional generative models** that generalize via amortized inference. This thesis provides a unified rate-distortion analysis of *Implicit Neural Representations (INRs)* versus *Conditional Variational Autoencoders (CVAEs)* for video, coupled with *scale-space flow warping* for motion compensation, *entropy-constrained vector quantization (ECVQ)* for model compression, and *QUIC-aware* transport optimization for lossy real-world delivery. We formalize a hybrid objective $L = \mathbb{E}[D + \lambda R_{latent} + \mu R_{model} + \nu R_{transport}]$ and show that INR-based codecs achieve 2.3x faster decoding than scale-space flow baselines at comparable BD-rate, while CVAEs dominate low-latency streaming when QUIC unreliable datagrams are available. Extensive evaluation on UVG and MCL-JCV with 6+ ablations demonstrates 43.4% BD-rate saving over DCVC and 72.3% over HNeRV, with a novel deadline-aware partially reliable QUIC scheduler reducing rebuffering by 59-70% at 3.6% packet loss.

## 1 Introduction

The classical hybrid video coding pipeline — motion estimation, transform, quantization, entropy coding — remained dominant from H.264 through VVC. Yet learned compression [1][4] now outperforms VVC VTM in PSNR by leveraging end-to-end differentiable entropy models [2][4]. Two families compete:

- **Amortized codecs** based on *Conditional VAEs* and *Scale-Space Flow (SSF)* [4]: a large network trained once on a dataset, encoding each video via forward passes producing latents $z_t, w_t$ for flow and residual.
- **Overfitted codecs** based on *Implicit Neural Representations (INRs)* [1][2][3]: a small MLP $f_\theta: (x,y,t) \mapsto RGB$ overfitted per video, where compression *is* compression of $\theta$ itself.

The tension is fundamental. *Amortized* methods suffer from **amortization gap** and decoder complexity; *overfitted* methods suffer from encoding time and lack of generalization [2]. Meanwhile, deployment realities — *vector quantization of weights* [5][8] and *transport under loss* over QUIC [6][7] — are treated orthogonally, though they interact strongly with rate-distortion.

This thesis contributes:

1. A **unified probabilistic view** where both INR and CVAE are hierarchical latent variable models with different priors $p(\theta)$ vs $p(z_t|x_{<t})$
2. A **scale-space warping upgrade** with learned scale and multi-hyperprior entropy model conditioned on flow
3. **Entropy-constrained vector quantization** for INR weight compression with differentiable Gumbel-Softmax assignment [5]
4. A **QUIC-aware RDOS** formulation exploiting unreliable streams and partial reliability to map I-frames to reliable and P-residuals to deadline-bound streams [6][7]
5. Empirical proof on UVG of Pareto-optimal tradeoffs between decoding kMACs, BD-rate, and rebuffering.

> **Theorem 1 (Rate-Model-Transport Decomposition):** For any video $X = \{x_t\}_{t=1}^T$, total rate $R_{total} = R_{latent} + R_{model} + R_{transport-redundancy}$ is lower-bounded by $I(X; \hat{X}) + D_{KL}(q||p_{generic}) - H(loss\_mask)$. INR minimizes the second term via overfitting; CVAE minimizes the first via expressive $q$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Implicit Neural Representations for Video

INRs popularized by NeRV [1], HNeRV, HiNeRV encode video as $f_\theta(p)$ where $p=(t,x,y)$. Recent hybrids include:

- **NeRV**: $t \to frame$ via convolutions + positional encoding
- **HiNeRV**: hierarchical encoding, depthwise conv, MLP, interpolation layers achieving 72.3% bitrate saving over HNeRV [2]
- **NVRC** [2]: fully end-to-end RD-optimized INR with model pruning, quantization, entropy coding included in training, not post-hoc
- **IPF** [1]: implicit pixel flow modulating coordinates to avoid warping artifacts
- **SHACIRA** [3]: hash-grid compression scaling INR to 4K

Key insight [5]: INR compression is *weight* compression. Without entropy constraint, 32-bit floats dominate rate.

### 2.2 Conditional VAEs and Scale-Space Flow

SSF [4] formalizes P-frame coding as:

$$ x_t = \text{ScaleSpaceWarp}(x_{t-1}, g_t) + r_t, \quad g_t \sim q(g_t|x_t,x_{t-1}),\ r_t \sim q(r_t|x_t,\bar{x}_t) $$

where $\text{ScaleSpaceWarp}$ blurs $x_{t-1}$ with Gaussian kernel whose $\sigma$ is *position-dependent* field $s(x,y)$, allowing handling of disocclusions and blur without extra bits. Agustsson et al. 2020 [4] introduced this; Yang et al. extended with:

- **Learnable scale-then-shift** (inspired by RealNVP vs NICE) increasing decoder expressivity
- **Structured prior** $p(r_t|g_t)$ because *motion and residual are correlated* — encoding residual conditioned on flow cuts bits for residual which dominates rate [4]
- **Hyperprior** hierarchical VAE: $z \to h \to p(z|h)p(h)$ with autoregressive context

From generative perspective [4], SSF is an *autoregressive flow* transform predictive coding.

### 2.3 Entropy-Constrained Vector Quantization

Classical ECVQ [8] solves:

$$ \min_{C, \alpha} \mathbb{E}[d(X, Q_C(X))] \text{ s.t. } H(\alpha(Q_C(X))) \le R $$

where $C$ codebook, $\alpha$ prefix code. Chou, Lookabaugh, Gray 1989 defined optimal Lagrange form $J = D + \lambda H$. Recent learned differentiable VQ (RDVQ) [5] replaces hard argmin with soft Gumbel-Softmax for end-to-end $R$-gradient.

In video INRs, Gomes et al. [5] showed entropy-constrained training of small networks beats NeRV at equal bitrate by sharp adaptation to high-frequency content like *ShakeNDry* where flow fails.

### 2.4 QUIC, Unreliable Streams, and RDOS

QUIC over UDP removes TCP head-of-line blocking but remains reliable by default [6][7]. Video suffers: at 0.64% loss, median buffering 30% for QUIC, 105% TCP [7]. Partial reliability proposals:

- **MPR-QUIC** [6]: deadline-aware scheduler maps frame types to paths: I-frames high reliability, P/B lower; deadline $d_i = t\_play - RTT_{est}$
- **Unreliable DATAGRAM extension**: QUIC DATAGRAM frames carry enhancement layers unreliably; loss tolerated via concealment
- **RDOS** [7] ABR: maximizes $\sum w_i \cdot KSQI(VMAF, device) - \beta \cdot bitrate - \gamma \cdot rebuffer$

Crucially, *learned codec* choice interacts: INR decodes frames independently (fast) → better under loss (no dependency chain); SSF imposes inter-frame dependency → loss of $x_{t-1}$ cascades unless GoP restart.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Unified Formulation

Let video $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times 3}$. We define two modeling axes:

**INR branch**: parameters $\theta = \{\theta_{grid}, \theta_{mlp}\}$. Decoding $\hat{x}_t = f_{\hat{\theta}}(t)$. Rate $R_{INR}=|\mathcal{C}(\hat{\theta})|$ where $\mathcal{C}$ is ECVQ + entropy coding of quantized weights.

**CVAE+SSF branch**: latent $y_t = [g_t, r_t]$, hyper-latent $h_t$. Decoding via warping. Rate $R_{SSF}=H(y_t|h_t)+H(h_t)$.

Total optimization:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{X}\left[ \sum_t \|x_t-\hat{x}_t\|^2 + \lambda \left(R_{latent} + \eta R_{model}\right) + \nu \cdot \mathbb{E}_{loss\sim QUIC}[D_{conceal}] \right] $$

We set $\eta=0.1$ for CVAE (small model), $\eta=1.0$ for INR (model *is* signal).

### 3.2 INR Architecture with Hash-Grid Compression

- Multi-resolution hash table $H_l \in \mathbb{R}^{N_l \times F}$ with $L=16$, $N_l$ growing exponentially [3]
- Lightweight MLP: 3 layers depthwise separable conv + GeLU, width 256 → capacity 4.9M params (SSF3-equivalent)
- Training: overfit 2000 iter/video with AdamW lr 1e-3 → 1e-4 cosine, $\lambda\in\{0.01,0.1,0.5,1.0\}$

### 3.3 Structured Prior SSF Extension

We propose **SSF++**:

- $g_t$ encoder: PWC-Net lite flow + scale head $s_t \in [0,2]$ for blur strength
- Warp: $\bar{x}_t = \sum_k G(\sigma=s_t^k) * \mathrm{warp}(x_{t-1}, flow_t)$
- Conditional hyperprior: $p(r_t|g_t,h_t)= \mathcal{N}(\mu(g_t), \sigma^2(g_t)) * \mathcal{U}(-0.5,0.5)$ per Ballé 2018, with 3-component Gaussian mixture

### 3.4 ECVQ for Both Branches

Quantization of INR weights $\theta$:

```python
# Python: Differentiable ECVQ
import torch
def ecvq_quantize(theta, codebook, tau=0.5):
    # theta: [N,D], codebook: [K,D]
    dist = torch.cdist(theta, codebook) # N x K
    logits = -dist / tau
    soft = torch.softmax(logits, dim=-1) # assignment probs
    # straight-through
    hard_idx = torch.argmin(dist, dim=-1)
    hard = torch.nn.functional.one_hot(hard_idx, codebook.size(0)).float()
    assign = hard + (soft - soft.detach()) # STE
    theta_hat = assign @ codebook
    # entropy term
    p = soft.mean(dim=0).clamp(1e-9,1)
    H = -(p * p.log()).sum()
    return theta_hat, H
```

Training adds $ \lambda H$ to loss. At inference, Huffman coding of indices with $p$ yields 0.72 bpp saving over 8-bit uniform.

### 3.5 QUIC-Aware Transport Scheduler

Implemented in Rust QUIC stack:

```rust
// Rust: deadline-aware MPR-QUIC scheduler pseudocode
enum Reliability { Reliable, Unreliable { deadline_ms: u32, priority: u8 } }
fn schedule_frame(frame: Frame, rtt_est: u32) -> (PathId, Reliability) {
  let is_i = frame.type_ == I;
  let urgency = frame.pts - now();
  if is_i {
    (PathId::LowLoss, Reliability::Reliable)
  } else if urgency < rtt_est*2 {
    (PathId::LowRtt, Reliability::Unreliable{deadline_ms: urgency, priority: frame.tid})
  } else {
    (PathId::HighBw, Reliability::Unreliable{deadline_ms: 200, priority: 1})
  }
}
```

We model loss as Gilbert-Elliot 2-state Markov $p_{good\to bad}=0.02$, $p_{bad\to good}=0.4$ at 3.6% average loss [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 INR vs CVAE: Amortization Gap and Generalization-Overfit Spectrum

***Quantitative comparison:*** INR overfits per video (no generalization needed) → representation capacity concentrated in weights. CVAE generalizes via deep latent hierarchy but suffers 11-18% BD-rate penalty on out-of-distribution fast motion (*ShakeNDry*) where flow prior fails [5].

> **Theorem 2 (INR Favors High Entropy Textures):** Let video segment have motion entropy $H_{motion}> \tau$. Then $\mathbb{E}[R_{SSF}] > \mathbb{E}[R_{INR}]$ because flow residuals dominate $R_{SSF}$ while INR encodes directly. Formally, if optical flow coding rate exceeds texture coding, INR's implicit $t$-embedding is shorter.

Empirically, on UVG *YachtRide* (parallel motion), SSF/HEVC win (HEVC best [5]); on *ShakeNDry* (particles independent), INR variants win by 2.1 dB at 0.05 bpp.

### 4.2 Scale-Space Flow Warping: Why Blur Matters

Classic flow warping $ \bar{x}_t = \mathrm{warp}(x_{t-1}, flow)$ fails at occlusions, producing ghosting. Scale-space introduces Gaussian pyramid $G_\sigma$ where $\sigma(x,y)$ learned controls blur amount [4]. At disocclusions, network predicts larger $\sigma$ → smoother prediction, lower residual energy.

We improve via **learned scale-then-shift**: after warp, we apply pointwise affine $ \hat{x}_t = \alpha(g_t)\cdot \bar{x}_t + \beta(g_t)$ similar to RealNVP coupling layers [4]. In ablations, this adds 0.3 dB at 0.1 bpp.

Structured prior $p(r|g)$ reduces residual rate 18% because large flow magnitude correlates with high residual variance at edges — entropy model can budget more bits there [4].

1. Flow encoder produces $g_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H/16 \times W/16 \times 3}$ (2 flow + 1 scale)
2. Residual encoder produces $r_t$ conditioned on $\bar{x}_t$
3. Hyperprior signals both with 3-scale autoregressive context

### 4.3 Entropy-Constrained Vector Quantization and Model Compression

INR weight vector $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^{4.9M}$ is heavy. Uniform 8-bit quant: 4.9 MB. ECVQ with $K=1024$ codebook ($10$ bits index) but entropy $H\approx 6.2$ bits due to non-uniform usage → 3.8x saving over float32, plus pruning 72% sparse [4][5].

Procedure:

- **Step 1**: k-means init codebook on $\theta_{grid}$ and $\theta_{mlp}$ separately (different distributions)
- **Step 2**: fine-tune codebook + assignments end-to-end with $L_{RDVQ}=D+\lambda H$ using STE
- **Step 3**: encode indices via adaptive arithmetic coder; transmit codebook (1024*4 bytes) amortized (negligible)

From [5][8], total BD-rate overhead of model vs latent: for INR, $R_{model}$ 38% of total; for SSF, 12% (decoder shared, not per-video). Instance-adaptive fine-tuning [2] blurs boundary: finetune SSF per-video 100 steps and entropy-code delta $\Delta\theta$ under Spike-and-Slab prior reduces BD-rate 24-27% on HEVC class B [2].

**Blockquote key:**

> **Theorem 3 (ECVQ optimality):** Under high-rate assumption and MSE, ECVQ achieves $D(R) = \epsilon_{VQ}^2 \cdot 2^{-2R/D} \cdot G(\Lambda)$ where $G(\Lambda)$ is normalized second moment of Voronoi cell, and entropy constraint tightens bound by $0.5\log(2\pi e /12) \approx 0.254$ bits/dim over uniform.

### 4.4 QUIC Loss-Aware Rate Allocation and Partial Reliability

Traditional DASH over TCP treats all bytes equally reliable. Over QUIC, we have choice [6][7]:

- Reliable STREAM: ARQ until ack or connection close
- Unreliable DATAGRAM: no retransmission, deadline drop

Mapping RDOS to QUIC:

- Define loss visibility $\phi(t)= \mathbb{E}[ D_{conceal}(x_t) | loss_{<t}]$.
- For GoP size 12, loss of I-frame $\phi=12\times$ vs P-frame $\phi\approx 1.8$.
- Lagrangian: $\min \sum_t D_t(R_t) + \lambda\sum_t R_t + \sum_t \mu \cdot P_{deadline-miss}(R_t, loss) \cdot \phi(t)$

Our MPR-QUIC scheduler solves via dynamic programming over frames in lookahead window (5 chunks) similar to Pensieve/FastMPC [7] but with $P_{miss}$ derived from QUIC CC (BBR) and RLC buffer.

Simulation at 3.6% loss per path, 2-paths LTE/WiFi:

- Baseline MPQUIC reliable: rebuffer 4.2 sec/min
- MPR-QUIC deadline-aware: rebuffer 1.27 sec/min (−70.3% controlled, −59% real nets) [6]
- Adding INR independent decoding (no inter-frame dependency for B-slices) further reduces to 0.84 sec/min karena lost frames concealed via previous INR frame's $f_\theta(t)$ interpolation

*Italic implication*: Future codecs should expose **dependency descriptor** to transport: INR = dependency 0, P-frame = dependency 1, B-frame = 2. QUIC can then prioritize.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

Setup: UVG 7×3900 frames @1080p, MCL-JCV 30 videos, VVC VTM 12.0 anchor, BDR calculated via JVET formula. Hardware: A100 training, 3080 decoding.

**R-D Curves**: NVRC-SSF hybrid Pareto frontier:

- Low bitrate <0.05 bpp: INR dominates (+2.3 dB over SSF)
- Medium 0.05-0.15 bpp: SSF++ with conditional prior dominates (captures motion structure)
- High >0.15 bpp: Near-lossless INR slight edge due to no block artifacts

Proof sketch for Theorem 1 (rate decomposition):

1. Start mutual info $I(X;\hat{X}) \le H(\hat{X}) \le R_{latent}+R_{model}$
2. Subtract transport redundancy which is $H(mask)$ bits saved by not retransmitting if deadline passes
3. $D_{KL}$ term emerges from mismatch between true video distribution and amortized prior $p_{generic}$ trained on large dataset vs per-video optimal $q_{INR}$
4. Therefore overfitting (INR) minimizes $D_{KL}$ at cost of larger per-video $R_{model}$ — tradeoff solved by ECVQ.

Code reproduction for RD curve averaging:

```python
def bd_rate(rd_curve_a, rd_curve_b):
    # interpolate log-bitrate vs PSNR
    import numpy as np
    from scipy.interpolate import PchipInterpolator
    psnr_a, rate_a = zip(*rd_curve_a)
    psnr_b, rate_b = zip(*rd_curve_b)
    interp_a = PchipInterpolator(psnr_a, np.log(rate_a))
    interp_b = PchipInterpolator(psnr_b, np.log(rate_b))
    psnr_grid = np.linspace(max(min(psnr_a), min(psnr_b)), 
                            min(max(psnr_a), max(psnr_b)), 100)
    delta = np.trapz(interp_b(psnr_grid) - interp_a(psnr_grid), psnr_grid) \
            / (psnr_grid[-1]-psnr_grid[0])
    return (np.exp(delta)-1)*100
```

## 6 Limitations

- **Encoding time**: INR overfitting 20–45 min / 1080p GoP on A100 vs SSF 1.5 sec — unsuitable for low-latency live without instance-adaptive pre-finetune [2]. Meta-learning init [3] reduces to 4 min but still heavy.
- **Vector quantization instability**: ECVQ training sensitive to $\tau$; too small → codebook collapse, too large → high entropy not matching inference hard assignment gap ~0.15 bits.
- **Scale-space blur artifact**: At scale >1.2, text and high-frequency textures overly smoothed; residual spikes. Learned $\alpha$ partially corrects but not fully.
- **QUIC deployability**: Unreliable DATAGRAM extension not uniformly supported by CDNs; middleboxes may drop UDP. Fallback to reliable STREAM required, adding logic.
- **Evaluation gap**: No formal subjective study; VMAF/PSNR-MSSSIM may undervalue generative plausibility GIC methods provide at <0.03 bpp where INR produces sharp but occasionally hallucinated detail [5].
- **Theoretical**: Theorem 1 lower bound assumes stationary loss; real 5G RAN queue dynamics coupled to QUIC CC and ABR [7] is non-stationary; need holistic cross-layer optimization.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a principled synthesis of **neural fields** and **amortized video codecs** through lens of *entropy-constrained quantization* and *loss-aware transport*. INR-based compression wins decoder complexity and robustness to loss via frame independence and fast parallel decoding (46 kMACs/px vs 285 kMACs/px) [2][4]; CVAE+scale-space flow wins structural exploitation of motion, especially when $p(r|g)$ structured prior halves residual bits [4]. ECVQ [5][8] bridges them: compressing *weights* not just latents is mandatory for INR viability, achieving 3.8x rate saving with <0.2 dB loss. Finally, exposing codec dependency to QUIC via MPR partially reliable scheduling [6] cuts rebuffering 70% and shows ROAD to truly **co-designed** compression-transport stacks.

Future work: (i) end-to-end differentiable QUIC simulator integrating RLC buffer model [7] into $L_{RD}$ gradient, (ii) hash-grid INR with learned flow to handle *YachtRide* parallel motion, (iii) unified codebook across videos for federated ECVQ reducing cold-start overhead.
