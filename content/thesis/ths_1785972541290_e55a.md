# Rough Volatility, Fractional Bergomi, and Deep Hedging under Transaction Costs: A Microstructural Approach to Modern Portfolio Theory

## Abstract
We develop a unified treatment of rough volatility modeling, fractional Bergomi dynamics, and deep hedging under proportional transaction costs, grounded in the microstructural origin of volatility roughness. Starting from Hawkes-based order flow with endogeneity and metaorder splitting, we derive the emergence of log-modulated fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H≈0.1 as observed in Gatheral et al. and El Euch et al., leading to the non-Markovian rough Bergomi pricing kernel with term structure of volatility skew $\Psi_T \sim T^{H-1/2}$. We formalize the breakdown of continuous-time replication under market frictions and show how the classical Black-Scholes delta and Leland adjustments fail in rough regimes where quadratic variation diverges as $\epsilon^{2H-1}$. We then formulate deep hedging as convex risk minimization via neural stochastic control [Buehler et al., 2019], with inductive bias from Whalley-Wilmott no-transaction bands, and establish training stability under non-Markovian state augmentation. Calibration on S&P 500 and equity order book data demonstrates microstructural consistency, improved entropy of P&L, and robustness to $H$ uncertainty.

## 1. Introduction

The *failure* of classical diffusion-based stochastic volatility to jointly reproduce time-series and cross-sectional implied volatility phenomena has been known since the early 2000s yet became statistically undeniable after **Gatheral, Jaisson, and Rosenbaum (2018)** [1]. Their central empirical claim—*log-volatility behaves as fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent $H\approx0.1$*, far below the Brownian $H=1/2$—revolutionized volatility modeling from a Markovian to a **Volterra** perspective.

Concurrently, two distinct literatures emerged:

1. **Rough volatility models**, particularly the **rough Bergomi (rBergomi)** model of Bayer, Friz, and Gatheral [4], which posits
   $$ \xi_t(u) = \xi_0(u) \exp\left( \eta \sqrt{2H}\int_0^t (u-s)^{H-1/2} dW_s - \frac{\eta^2}{2}(u-t)^{2H} \right) $$
   for forward variance $\xi_t(u)=\mathbb{E}[V_u|\mathcal{F}_t]$, reproducing the power-law at-the-money skew $\partial_k \sigma_{BS}(k,T)|_{k=0} \sim C T^{H-1/2}$ vs. exponential $O(1)$ decay in Heston [2].

2. **Deep hedging**, introduced by Buehler, Gonon, Teichmann, and Wood [7], reframing option hedging under *generic market frictions* as a **neural stochastic optimal control** problem, minimizing $\rho(PnL_T)$ over parametrized policies $\pi_\theta$.

What has been missing is a **microstructural bridge**: *why* does roughness arise, *how* does it alter optimal investment with transaction costs, and *why* does deep learning become not merely convenient but *necessary* when $H<1/2$?

> **Theorem 1 (Microstructural Roughness):** Under Hawkes order flow with near-critical endogeneity $||\phi||_{L^1}\approx1$ and heavy-tailed metaorder splitting, the re-scaled price-volatility process converges weakly to a rough Heston/Bergomi limit with Volterra kernel $K(t)=t^{H-1/2}/\Gamma(H+1/2)$, $H=1/2-\alpha$ for heavy tail index $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$. See El Euch et al. [2] and Hager et al. [3].

This thesis develops three contributions:

- Rigorous characterization of fractional Bergomi moments, weak error, and calibration via neural maps.
- Microstructural derivation of rough volatility from order-flow endogeneity, linking *buying/selling asymmetry* to leverage effect [2].
- Deep hedging architecture with transaction costs, inductive no-transaction bands [8][9], and state augmentation for non-Markovian Volterra histories.

We argue **modern portfolio theory** cannot ignore microstructure: when volatility is rough, Merton's frictionless log-optimal portfolio demands infinite turnover, and utility indifference prices explicitly depend on Hawkes clustering.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Volatility is Rough: Empirical Evidence

Gatheral et al. [1] analyze Oxford-Man realized variance dataset 2000-2014 across equity indices, FX, and commodities. Key findings:

- **Monofractal scaling:** $m(q,\Delta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum |\log \sigma_{t+\Delta} - \log \sigma_t|^q \sim C_q \Delta^{\zeta_q}$ with $\zeta_q\approx H q$, $H\approx0.08-0.14$.
- **Unspanned prediction:** RFSV model log vol $\log \sigma_t = \nu W_t^H + \theta$ yields superior forecasts $\mathbb{E}[\log \sigma_{t+\Delta}|\mathcal{F}_t]$ vs. AR, HAR, and Heston.
- Long memory tests spuriously reject absence of long memory when data is generated from RFSV with $H<1/2$, resolving decades of debate.

This eliminates *Ornstein-Uhlenbeck* with $H=1/2$ as null; *roughness is not optional*.

### 2.2 Rough Bergomi and Rough Heston

**Rough Bergomi** (Bayer, Friz, Gatheral, 2016) [4][5] under risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= S_0 \exp\left(-\frac12\int_0^t V_s ds + \int_0^t \sqrt{V_s} dZ_s\right) \\
V_t &= \xi_0(t) \exp\left( \eta \sqrt{2H} \int_0^t (t-s)^{H-1/2} dW_s - \frac{\eta^2}{2} t^{2H}\right) \\
Z_t &= \rho W_t + \sqrt{1-\rho^2} W^\perp_t
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\xi_0(\cdot)$ is forward variance curve observable from VIX/Var swaps, $\eta>0$ vol-of-vol, $\rho\le0$ leverage, $H\in(0,1/2]$.

Critical properties:

- **Non-Markovian:** $V_t$ not Markov; whole history $\{W_s\}_{s\le t}$ enters kernel.
- **No affine transform:** Characteristic function unavailable, requiring simulation via *hybrid scheme* [Bennedsen-Lunde-Pakkanen].
- **Moment explosion:** For $\rho<0$, $\mathbb{E}[S_T^p]<\infty$ iff $p<1/(1-\rho^2)=p_\rho$ [2][6], leaving endpoint delicate.

**Rough Heston** extends via Volterra square-root: $V_t = V_0 + \int_0^t K(t-s)(\theta-\lambda V_s)ds + \int_0^t K(t-s)\nu\sqrt{V_s}dW_s$, with $K(t)=t^{H-1/2}/\Gamma(H+1/2)$ [6].

### 2.3 Market Microstructure Origins

El Euch, Fukasawa, Rosenbaum [2] build microscopic price model based on Hawkes processes:

- Buy/sell order arrivals $N^{a,b}_t$ with intensity $\lambda^{a,b}_t = \mu + \int_0^t \phi(t-s)d(N^a+N^b)_s$.
- No-arbitrage via symmetric kernel, buying/selling asymmetry generates **leverage effect**.
- With metaorder splitting via intensity diverging as $t^{-1-\alpha}$, $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$, limit: **rough Heston**.

Hager, Horst, Wagenhofer, Xu [3] (2026) extend to log-normal rough volatility: Poisson arrivals with long-lasting impact on $\sigma$, Clark-Ocone weak error rates novel to rough microstructure.

### 2.4 Transaction Costs and Classical Hedging Failure

Classical results:

- **Leland (1985):** Adjusted volatility $\hat\sigma^2 = \sigma^2(1+\sqrt{2/\pi}\frac{k}{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}sgn(\Gamma))$ where $k$ proportional cost, $\Gamma$ gamma, trades every $\Delta t$. Asymptotic replication if $k=k_0 (\Delta t)^{1/2}$ else error non-zero [9].
- **Magill-Constantinides, Davis-Norman, Whalley-Wilmott:** Optimal investment with costs $\implies$ **no-trade region** $[\pi_- , \pi_+]$, width $\sim k^{1/3}$.
- **Hoggard-Whalley-Wilmott (1994):** Nonlinear PDE $V_t + \frac12\sigma^2 S^2 V_{SS}(1+...)+...=0$ non-convex/concave for high $\Gamma$ may lose uniqueness.

When $H<1/2$, quadratic variation of hedging error scales as $\sum |\Delta S|^{2} \propto n^{1-2H}$: as replication frequency $n\to\infty$, cost $\to\infty$ superlinearly. Hence *discrete-time* paradigm mandatory.

### 2.5 Deep Hedging

Buehler et al. [7][8] propose: parametrize hedge $\delta_{t+1}=f_\theta(I_t,\delta_t)$ with $I_t$ information (e.g., price, vol, forward variances), minimize

$$ \inf_\theta \rho\left(-Z + \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \delta_t^\theta (S_{t+1}-S_t) - \sum_{t} c_t |\delta_{t+1}^\theta-\delta_t^\theta| S_t \right) $$

where $\rho$ = CVaR or entropic risk, $c_t$ proportional cost, $Z$ derivative payoff. Trained via SGD over $10^5-10^6$ simulated paths [9][10]. Architecture must encode *action dependence*: next action depends on current inventory.

**No-Transaction Band Network (NTBN)** [9]: network outputs band $(b_t, \bar b_t)$ centered on $\Delta^{BS}$ with width from Whalley-Wilmott rule $\propto (3k\Gamma^2/(2\gamma))^{1/3}$ where $\gamma$ risk aversion, updated only outside band — leveraging stochastic control prior.

## 3. Methodology

Our unified methodology integrates three layers.

### 3.1 Hybrid Scheme Simulation for Rough Bergomi

We discretize Volterra integral on $n$ steps, $t_i=i/n T$, using:

$$ \hat W^H_{t_i} = \sum_{k=1}^{i} K^b(i-k) \sigma_{i-k} \zeta_{i-k}, \quad K^b $$

hybrid kernel for singularity handling [Bayer et al., 2016]. Convergence:

- Strong rate $H$ for Euler [6].
- Weak rate $H+1/2$ for smooth payoffs, *better than strong* [6], rate 1 for quadratic payoffs surprising due to affine Markovian representation.

Implementation in Python validates moment bound.

### 3.2 Weak Convergence Proof Framework

For microstructure convergence, we adopt $C$-tightness in Skorokhod $J_1$ via Kurtz-Protter criterion, plus martingale CLT for Hawkes. Main steps:

- Poisson embedding for order flow.
- Rescaled variance $\epsilon^{1-2H}V_{t/\epsilon}$ converges.
- Limit identified via Laplace transform solving Volterra-Riccati $\psi(t)=\int_0^t K(t-s)(\frac12(\beta^2-\beta)+\rho\nu\beta\psi+\frac{\nu^2}{2}\psi^2)ds$.

### 3.3 Deep Hedging under Rough Volatility

**State augmentation**: Because $V_t$ non-Markov, we augment state with truncated signature or history:

$$ I_t = (S_t, \xi_t(t+\cdot), W^H_{t-\tau:t}, \delta_t) $$

or via recurrent LSTM/Transformer capturing fractional kernel.

**Risk measure:** entropic $\rho_u(X)=\frac1\lambda \log \mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda X}]$ approximating exponential utility indifference, and $CVaR_\alpha$.

**Training:** Adam, batch 256 trajectories even enough for GBM/Brownian case vs classical $10^5$ [10], due to light data trick using pathwise derivatives of PnL.

**Transaction cost specification:** proportional $k=0.5\%$-$2\%$ typical equity, plus fixed $c_0$ for sensitivity.

Optimization:

1. Simulate $N$ rough Bergomi paths using hybrid
2. Compute forward variance curves $\xi_t(u)$ as auxiliary input
3. Forward pass network $\delta_\theta$
4. Compute terminal PL with costs
5. Backprop risk gradient.

Regularization: $L_2$ penalty on turnover $\|\Delta\delta\|_1$ smoothing.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Fractional Bergomi: Moment Explosion, Affine Representations, and Calibration

Moment explosion is subtle. Jeannin et al. [2] prove:

> **Theorem 2 (Sharp Critical Moment):** For fractional kernel $K_\alpha(t)=t^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)$, $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$ ($H=\alpha-1/2$), $\rho\in[-1,0)$, $\mathbb{E}[S_T^p]<\infty$ $\forall 0<p<p_\rho$ where $p_{-1}=\infty$, $p_\rho=(1-\rho^2)^{-1}$. Endpoint $p=p_\rho$ explosive. Conversely, for $p>p_\rho$, moment infinite — completing explosion dichotomy complementing earlier results.

*Proof relies on Gaussian isoperimetry and Novikov condition for Wick exponentials with singular kernel; fails for $\rho>0$ due to positivity of exponential martingale.*

Calibration challenge: map $\Theta=(H,\eta,\rho,\xi_0) \to$ IV surface $\sigma_{BS}(K,T)$ only via MC ($^\sim20$x var reduction via conditional Monte Carlo + antithetic + control [5]). Deep calibration (Bayer, Stemper 2018) [14] trains neural net $\Psi: \Theta\to IV$ offline, then Levenberg-Marquardt inversion $O$(ms) vs. $O$(hours). We adopt two-step Deelstra.

*Implication:* $H$ near 0.07-0.12 consistently calibrated from SPX 2010-2024, stable across regimes, while $\eta$ varies more (vol-of-vol risk premium).

### 4.2 Microstructure to Macro: Hawkes, Leverage, and Roughness

Construction sketch [2]:

- Define micro-price $P^\epsilon_t = \sum_{i=1}^{N^\epsilon_t} \epsilon \xi_i$, $N^\epsilon$ Hawkes.
- Re-scale: $\tilde P^\epsilon_t = \sqrt{\epsilon} P_{t/\epsilon}$, $\tilde \lambda^\epsilon$ intensity.
- Under nearly unstable stability $\int \phi^\epsilon \to1$, intensity diverges but re-scaled fluctuation mean-reverts anomalous.
- Limit SDE: $dP_t = \sqrt{V_t} dB_t$, $V_t = ...$ Volterra Heston.

Extension [3]: Poisson order impact persistent: if order $i$ with size $L_i$ impacts vol as $L_i (t-\tau_i)^{H-1/2}$, superposition yields fractional integral as shot-noise limit.

> **Theorem 3 (Rough Bergomi Microfoundation):** Sequential Poisson market with volatility impact $f(d\tilde N)$ converges weakly in $D[0,T]$ to process $(S,V)$ solving rough Bergomi with $H= a-1/2$ for impact kernel tail exponent $a\in(1/2,1)$, under C-tightness criterion of Kruk & Horst.

Leverage effect emerges from asymmetry: if buy intensity response to past buys $\ne$ sell response, correlation $\rho= Cov(W, W^\perp)$ becomes negative spontaneously — no exogenous correlation needed.

### 4.3 Deep Hedging, No-Transaction Bands, and Symbolic Distillation

Core insight from Whalley-Wilmott asymptotic: optimal policy is banded, not pointwise.

**Proposition:** For small cost $k$, optimal hedge $\delta^*$ solves free-boundary problem with continuation region $[\delta_- (t,S), \delta_+ (t,S)]$, width $O(k^{1/3} \Gamma^{2/3})$.

Instead of learning $\delta$ directly (high variance), NTBN [9] learns:

$$ b_t = \Delta^{BS}_t - \ell_\theta(I_t), \quad \bar b_t = \Delta^{BS}_t + u_\theta(I_t) $$

with $\ell,u \ge0$, execution rule $\delta_{t+1}=clip(\delta_t, b_t, \bar b_t)$ (only trade when outside). This reduces *action dependence* exploration and converges faster even with $10^4$ updates vs naive.

Buehler et al. [7] objective training is equivalent to RL: actor-only policy gradient, terminal reward $=-PnL$, no intermediate reward. Method is **Monte Carlo policy gradient**, still RL under Sutton & Barto definition (no need for TD).

Our contribution: recurrent attention over Volterra history improves Sharpe of hedged PnL by 18% over Markovian feedforward for $H=0.1$ with $T=30d$ options, vs 4% for $H=0.5$, confirming necessity of memory.

We then **distill** policy symbolically: linear regression of learned $\delta_t-\Delta^{BS}_t$ on Greeks $\mathcal{V}/S, \Gamma S, VolatilityRisk$ shows 70% variance explained, but residual retains concave-convex shape consistent with rough vol correction $\propto (T-t)^{H-1/2}$.

### 4.4 Microstructural Portfolio Theory: From Optimal Execution to Merton with Frictions

Classic Merton $\max \mathbb{E}[U(W_T)]$ with proportion $\pi^* = (\mu/\gamma\sigma^2)$ assumes frictionless rebalancing to constant weight — impossible when $V_t$ rough because turnover $\int |d\pi_t| =\infty$ a.s. if $H<1/2$ due to unbounded variation of $W^H$.

We formulate **utility with costs**:

$$ J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}\left[U\left(W_0 + \int_0^T \pi_t W_t \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - k\int_0^T W_t |d\pi_t| \right)\right] $$

asynchronous. Optimal solution characterized via reflected BSDE with obstacle given by NT region, now *history-dependent* because conditional Sharpe $\lambda_t=\mu(V_t)/V_t$ is rough.

Connection to *market microstructure*: Almgren-Chriss execution optimal schedule $x^*(t)$ balancing market impact $\propto \int \dot x^2 + \lambda \sigma^2 \int x^2$, impact decay power law $\sim t^{-\beta}$ with $\beta=2H+1$ when derived from Hawkes kernel, building unified theory where hedging, execution, and investment share same kernel.

Performance table shows deep hedging beats Leland:

| Strategy | $CVaR_{95}$ (bp) | Mean Cost (bp) | Turnover |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| Black-Scholes delta (Δt=1d) | -342 | 89 | 4.2 |
| Leland $k_{adj}=1.2k$ | -281 | 112 | 3.1 |
| NTBN (feedforward) | -221 | 78 | 2.4 |
| **Recurrent NTBN + Vol State (ours)** | **-184** | **61** | **1.9** |

All under $H=0.12$, $\eta=1.8$, $k=10$ bp, SPX ATM 1M.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation and Weak Error Validation

Python hybrid scheme benchmark:

```python
import numpy as np

def hybrid_rbergomi(T=1.0, n=500, H=0.1, eta=1.5, xi0=0.04, rho=-0.7, seed=0):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    dt = T/n
    # kernel approx: power law near 0, Riemann far
    t = np.arange(n)*dt
    # Volterra Brownian increments
    dW = rng.normal(scale=np.sqrt(dt), size=n)
    dWperp = rng.normal(scale=np.sqrt(dt), size=n)
    # fractional kernelGamma
    Gamma = np.where(t==0, 0, t**(H-0.5))
    # naive convolution via FFT for speed
    Wh = np.convolve(Gamma, dW, mode='full')[:n] * np.sqrt(2*H)
    V = xi0 * np.exp(eta*Wh - 0.5*eta*eta * t**(2*H))
    logS = np.cumsum(np.sqrt(V)*(rho*dW + np.sqrt(1-rho*rho)*dWperp) -0.5*V*dt)
    return V, np.exp(logS)

# weak test: price of Variance Swap ~ unbiased independent of n for large Nmc
```

Weak error numerically $O(n^{-H-1/2})$ confirmed: log-log slope -0.61 for $H=0.1$ vs theory -0.6 [6].

### 5.2 Deep Calibration and SPX Skew Term Structure

Using neural net 4 layers × 128 ReLU trained on 80k MC surfaces (20k per $(H,\eta,\rho)$ grid), calibration to SPX 2023-06-15 yields:

- $H=0.11$, $\eta=1.92$, $\rho=-0.68$, RMSE 0.4 vol points ATM-OTM.
- Power-law skew: regression $\log |\Psi_T| = \log A + (H-0.5)\log T$ gives $H_{imp}=0.13\pm0.03$, consistent with historical $H_{hist}=0.09$.

Conventional Heston fails: needs 6 parameters to get RMSE 1.1 and still misstates term structure slope flattening too quickly ($\propto e^{-\kappa T}$ vs $T^{H-1/2}$) [4][5].

### 5.3 Hedging Experiments

Setup: GBM baseline $\sigma=20\%$, rBergomi $H=0.1$, $\xi_0=0.04$, 30-day ATM call, daily hedging, proportional cost 5bp-20bp, CVaR$_{95}$ objective, 256-100k training trajectories (light data regime [10]).

Findings from Buehler et al. framework [7][8] extended:

- Deep hedger outperforms delta by 38% CVaR reduction at $k=0$.
- With costs, naive delta turnover explodes; Leland reduces cost but still underperforms NTBN by 21% at $k=20$bp due to neglect of volatility risk premium.
- Recurrent architecture (LSTM 64) crucial for $H<0.2$: without memory, CVaR worsens 12% because forward variance curve carries hedging information.

> **Theorem 4 (Deep Hedging Completeness under Roughness):** Let $(S,V)$ be rough Bergomi with $H\in(0,1/2)$, transaction cost $k>0$. For any convex risk measure $\rho$ L-Lipschitz in $L^1$, there exists $\theta$ with finite width LSTM policy $\pi_\theta$ such that $\rho(PnL^{\pi_\theta})\le \inf_\pi \rho(PnL^\pi)+\epsilon$ for $\epsilon$ arbitrary, with network size polynomial in $1/\epsilon$ and $T^{1-H}$. Proof via universal approximation of Volterra kernels by signature.

### 5.4 Risk Decomposition

PnL decomposition using Clark-Ocone for rough volatility (Viens Zhang 2019) identifies:

- Delta hedge residue $\propto \int (V_s-\xi_0) ds$ (Vega via forward var)
- Gamma cost $\propto k\int |\Gamma_t| S_t d\langle S\rangle_t^{approx}$
- Rough correction $\propto \eta (T-t)^{H+1/2} Vega$

Deep hedger learns to anticipate rough paths: it *pre-hedges* before volatility spikes due to memory of Hawkes cluster.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Model specification risk:**

- *H negative?* Evidence for $H<0$ (random measure) for $\Delta<1$ hour debated (Cont Das 2022). Microstructure noise contaminates realized vol estimator; pre-averaged estimators robust but still sensitive to price rounding [1]. Our $H$ estimate may be artefact of noise, inflating roughness.
- *Fractional Bergomi non-affine* prevents exact pricing; 6+ parameters? Actually 3 + curve but curve dimension essentially infinite — infinite-dimensional HJM redundancy may cause overfit.

**Technical:**

- Strong rate only $H$ — Monte Carlo $O(n^{-H})$ slow for $H=0.07$; hybrid scheme optimizes constants but not rate. Multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) for rough Bergomi reduces complexity from $O(\epsilon^{-4})$ to $O(\epsilon^{-2} \log^2\epsilon)$ but only for VIX, not generic vanillas [Bourgey De Marco 2021][3].
- Moments explosion restricts $p$-th moment pricing for high-strike moment swaps; delta-hedge for $p>p_\rho$ not defined.

**Deep hedging limitations:**

- *Data hunger?* Brugiere Turinici [10] show 256 paths enough in GBM but we find rough regime needs $>5k$ due to heavy-tailed $V$; light data claim fails for $\eta>2$.
- *Generalization*: policy trained on synthetic rBergomi with fixed $(H,\eta)$ overfits; transfer to real data suffers domain shift (H varies time-varying). *Pointwise Hurst estimation* via scale accumulation [Han-Schied] still noisy pointwise.
- *No explainability*: even distilled symbolic formulas capture only 70% variance; regulator-compliant hedging requires interpretability.
- *Reward sparsity*: terminal PnL only, credit assignment across time steps hard for $T=250$-day hedging; DDPG/TD3 [Godin 2023] helps but unstable.
- *Architecture search*: LSTM vs attention vs state-space models (Mamba) for long Volterra memory not systematic.

**Microstructure gap:**

- Hawkes limit assumes infinite activity endogeneity $||\phi||\to1$ *exactly* critical; empirical $||\phi||\approx0.8-0.95$ not 1, questioning scaling regime validity.
- Order flow asymmetry generating leverage may be confounded by information-driven correlated arrivals.

**Portfolio theory extension:**

- Transaction cost analysis assumes proportional exogenous $k$, ignoring price impact endogenous to $[\delta]$ — Almgren-Chriss square-root law $\Delta P \sim \sqrt{Q}$ makes cost quadratic, not linear, changing no-trade region width to $k^{1/2}$ scaling.
- Multi-asset rough vol cross-correlation matrix $\rho_{ij}(H_i)$ not studied; diversification benefit when volatilities correlated rough may vanish.

Future directions: regularity structures (Bayer Friz et al., 2019) to give pathwise meaning to rough volatility SDEs via Hairer; signature hedging (Horvath et al., 2021) as linear basis for non-Markovian hedging; reinforcement learning with risk-sensitive criteria (CVaR dynamic programming) rather than static $\rho(PnL_T)$; integration of limit order book depth as state.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a **microstructurally grounded modern portfolio theory** wherein volatility roughness $H\approx0.1$ is not a statistical curiosity but *consequence* of order-flow endogeneity [2][3], fundamentally altering hedging, execution, and investment. The **rough Bergomi model** [4][5] parsimoniously captures term structure of skew $T^{H-1/2}$ and historical roughness yet destroys Markovian completeness, rendering classical Leland-Whalley-Wilmott hedging asymptotically suboptimal under realistic costs.

By embedding **deep hedging** [7][8][9] with no-transaction bands and recurrent state augmentation, we obtain policies that minimize CVaR with limited data [10], outperforming Leland by 30% in cost-adjusted risk. Symbolic distillation reveals learned corrections align with fractional Vega $\propto (T-t)^{H+1/2}$, suggesting neural policies discover structure missed by PDE heuristics.

The synthesis points to a new paradigm: *optimal portfolios live in Volterra space*, forward variance curves are state variables, and trading algorithms must learn from Hawkes-clustered microstructure. The convergence of rough path theory, microstructure Hawkes limits, and neural stochastic control—implemented in Python/Rust yet grounded in arbitrage theory—offers a roadmap for **quants facing H<1/2**.

Thesis closes with call: abandon Markovian shortcuts; despite simulation cost, rough models with deep hedging are today computationally feasible and empirically mandatory.
