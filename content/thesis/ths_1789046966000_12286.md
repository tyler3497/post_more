# Abstract Interpretation of Program Semantics: Galois Connections, Widening Operators, and Soundness Proofs

## Abstract

Static program analysis by *abstract interpretation* — introduced by Patrick and Radhia Cousot in 1977 [1] and systematized in the 1992 framework papers [2,3] — computes sound over-approximations of program behavior through a disciplined correspondence between concrete semantics and abstract domains. This thesis develops the theory's three pillars: (i) *Galois connections* (and their widening-based generalizations) as the mechanism mapping concrete properties to their best abstract representations; (ii) *widening* and *narrowing* operators as the principled device forcing termination of abstract fixpoint iteration on non-Noetherian domains while preserving soundness; and (iii) the *soundness theorems* — the fixpoint transfer lemmas and abstract induction principles — guaranteeing every computed post-fixpoint over-approximates the concrete collecting semantics. We develop each pillar from first principles, prove the central results (the Galois-connection soundness theorem, the widening convergence theorem, the post-fixpoint soundness corollary), illustrate them on the interval and sign domains, and survey mechanized and industrial validation including Astrée and machine-checked abstract interpreters [8]. We close by assessing inherent precision limits, the undecidability of completeness, and open directions in quantitative abstraction.

## 1 Introduction

The fundamental tension of program analysis is this: the *concrete semantics* of a program — the set of all its behaviors, typically formalized as the least fixpoint of a monotone transformer on the complete lattice of properties — is almost never computable. Rice's theorem tells us that all non-trivial semantic properties of programs are undecidable; Tarski's fixpoint theorem tells us the answer exists but not how to reach it in finite time. Abstract interpretation [1,2,3] resolves this tension not by surrendering to undecidability but by *approximating* the semantics within a deliberately chosen abstract domain, and by doing so *soundly*: every conclusion the analyzer draws about the abstract domain must remain a valid (if over-approximate) conclusion about the concrete one.

> **Theorem (informal statement of the program):** Every computable analysis of undecidable program properties is an approximation. The contribution of abstract interpretation is to make approximation a *calculational discipline* — specifying which approximations are sound, which are complete, how to compose them, and how to force them to terminate.

The present work is organized around the three mathematical pillars that make this discipline rigorous. First, **Galois connections** provide the correspondence machinery between concrete and abstract: an abstraction function *α* and a concretization function *γ* satisfying *α(c) ⊑ a ⟺ c ⊆ γ(a)*, which canonically induces *best correct approximations* of all concrete operators. Second, **widening** (and its dual, narrowing) operators solve the termination problem: on infinite-height lattices such as intervals, naive Kleene iteration may never stabilize, and widening accelerates the iteration so that it always converges in finitely many steps to a sound post-fixpoint. Third, the **soundness proofs** — the fixpoint abstraction theorems of Cousot and Cousot — connect the pillars: they prove that abstract iteration with sound operators and widening yields a result that over-approximates the concrete least fixpoint.

We take care throughout to distinguish three kinds of guarantees: *soundness* (no false negatives), *termination* (the analyzer always halts), and *precision* (the over-approximation is as tight as possible). The first two are theorems; the third is an engineering discipline.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Concrete Semantics and Collecting Properties

We work with the *collecting semantics* of programs: the strongest property expressible about a program, namely the set of all its reachable states (or traces). Let *Σ* be the set of concrete states and *℘(Σ)* the powerset lattice ordered by inclusion *⊆*, with join *∪* and meet *∩*. The semantics of a program is modeled as the *least fixpoint* *lfp F* of a monotone endofunction *F : ℘(Σ) → ℘(Σ)* that encodes one step of execution. Tarski's theorem guarantees *lfp F* exists; Kleene's iteration *⊥ ⊆ F(⊥) ⊆ F²(⊥) ⊆ ⋯* converges to it when *F* is continuous.

An analysis is useful only if it is *sound*: every abstract answer must over-approximate the concrete answer. It is *complete* if every concrete answer is exactly representable — a property that is both rare and, by undecidability results, generally unachievable in full generality.

### 2.2 Lattices, Order Theory, and Fixpoints

The ambient mathematical setting is order theory. A **complete lattice** *⟨L, ⊑, ⊥, ⊤, ⊔, ⊓⟩* admits arbitrary meets and joins. Abstraction maps between lattices must be monotone; the strongest results require *α* to preserve joins (be *additive*) and *γ* to preserve meets (be *co-additive*). The following table summarizes the core vocabulary used throughout this thesis.

| Concept | Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete lattice | *⟨C, ⊆⟩* | Typically *℘(Σ)*, the collecting domain |
| Abstract lattice | *⟨A, ⊑⟩* | e.g. intervals, signs, octagons |
| Abstraction | *α : C → A* | Maps concrete sets to their best description |
| Concretization | *γ : A → C* | Maps descriptions to the sets they denote |
| Least fixpoint | *lfp F* | The concrete semantics of the program |
| Widening | *∇ : A × A → A* | Accelerates convergence, guarantees termination |
| Narrowing | *∆ : A × A → A* | Recovers precision after widening |

### 2.3 The Historical Arc: 1977 to the Present

Abstract interpretation was introduced by Cousot and Cousot at POPL 1977 [1] as "a unified lattice model for static analysis of programs by construction or approximation of fixpoints." The 1992 framework papers [2,3] recast the theory in full generality, comparing Galois-connection-based abstraction with the widening/narrowing approach and demonstrating the lattice of abstract interpretations. Later milestones include the dual/widening calculus of POPL 2014 [4], the application to Hoare, incorrectness, and transformational logics [5], and the industrial apotheosis of the theory in the **Astrée** analyzer, which proved the absence of runtime errors in Airbus flight-control software — the first large-scale demonstration that sound abstract interpretation scales to safety-critical embedded C. A personal historical perspective by Patrick Cousot [6] recounts the research program from its origins.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *calculational*: following the Cousot school, we do not merely *check* soundness after the fact; we *derive* analyzers by calculation from the concrete semantics, so that soundness holds *by construction* [4,5].

**Step 1 — Fix the concrete semantics.** Specify the program language with a structural operational semantics, lift it to a collecting semantics as a monotone fixpoint equation *X = F(X)* over *℘(Σ)*.

**Step 2 — Choose the abstraction.** Select an abstract domain *⟨A, ⊑⟩* and establish the correspondence with the concrete domain, ideally a Galois connection *⟨℘(Σ), ⊆⟩ ⇆ ⟨A, ⊑⟩*. Where no best abstraction exists, use a sound concretization-only or widening-based framework [2].

**Step 3 — Derive abstract transformers.** For each concrete operator *f*, compute its *best correct approximation* *f^α = α ∘ f ∘ γ*. If the exact calculation is infeasible, replace it with any *sound approximation* *f♯ ⊒ f^α* — soundness is preserved by the abstraction theorems, at the cost of precision.

**Step 4 — Iterate with widening.** Solve the abstract equation *X♯ = F♯(X♯)* by iterating from *⊥* with widening at loop heads, guaranteeing convergence in finitely many steps to a *post-fixpoint* *X♯ ⊒ F♯(X♯)*, then improve it with narrowing.

**Step 5 — Verify the meta-theory.** Prove (or machine-check) the soundness theorems; validate empirically on benchmark suites.

This methodology is not hypothetical. It is implemented in production analyzers and has been mechanized in proof assistants: Monniaux [7] formalized the minimal correct axiomatization of widening in higher-order logic, and Jourdan et al. [8] verified a functional abstract interpreter in Coq/F* with soundness encoded in refinement types.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Galois Connections and Best Correct Approximations

> **Definition (Galois connection):** Let *⟨C, ≤_C⟩* and *⟨A, ≤_A⟩* be posets. A pair of monotone maps *α : C → A* and *γ : A → C* forms a *Galois connection*, written *⟨C, ≤_C⟩ ⇄ ⟨A, ≤_A⟩*, iff for all *c ∈ C* and *a ∈ A*:
>
> *α(c) ≤_A a ⟺ c ≤_C γ(a)*.
>
> If additionally *α ∘ γ = id_A*, the connection is a *Galois insertion* (surjection): every abstract element represents a *distinct* concrete property.

The Galois connection is the *specification* of the abstraction. It induces a canonical notion of *best* abstract operator:

> **Definition (best correct approximation):** For *f : C → C* monotone and a Galois connection *⟨α, γ⟩*, the *best correct approximation* is *f^α ≜ α ∘ f ∘ γ : A → A*. An abstract operator *f♯* is *sound* iff *f^α ⊑ f♯*, equivalently *f(γ(a)) ≤_C γ(f♯(a))* for all *a*.

Any sound operator suffices for soundness; the best one gives maximal precision. The existence of *f^α* is guaranteed precisely by the Galois connection — this is why the framework [2] treats Galois connections as the "perfect situation" where every concrete property has a unique best abstract approximation.

**Example — the sign domain.** Consider *C = ℘(ℤ)* and *A = {⊥, −, 0, +, ⊤}* ordered by precision. The abstraction *α(S)* maps a set of integers to its sign class; *γ* maps each class back to the set of integers it denotes (e.g. *γ(+) = {n > 0}*). The abstract addition *+♯* is computed by cases:

```ocaml
(* abstract sign domain, best correct approximation of (+) on ℘(ℤ) *)
type sign = Bot | Neg | Zero | Pos | Top
let ( ++ ) : sign -> sign -> sign = fun x y ->
  match x, y with
  | Bot, _ | _, Bot -> Bot
  | Zero, s | s, Zero -> s
  | Pos, Pos -> Pos
  | Neg, Neg -> Neg
  | Pos, Neg | Neg, Pos -> Top      (* could be zero or either sign *)
  | _, _ -> Top
```

This operator is *exact* (equal to the best correct approximation) except where information is genuinely lost — precisely the cases where the abstraction itself cannot express the answer.

### 4.2 Widening Operators and the Convergence Theorem

The interval domain *℘(ℤ)* → intervals *[l, u]* has *infinite ascending chains* (e.g. *[0,0] ⊑ [0,1] ⊑ [0,2] ⊑ ⋯*), so Kleene iteration may diverge. **Widening** forces convergence [1,2]:

> **Definition (widening):** A binary operator *∇ : A × A → A* is a *widening* iff
> 1. *x ⊑ x ∇ y* and *y ⊑ x ∇ y* (it over-approximates the join), and
> 2. for every ascending chain *x₀ ⊑ x₁ ⊑ ⋯*, the widened sequence *y₀ = x₀*, *y_{n+1} = y_n ∇ x_{n+1}* stabilizes in finitely many steps.

> **Theorem (widening convergence [1]):** Let *F♯ : A → A* be monotone and sound, *∇* a widening. Then the sequence *X₀ = ⊥*, *X_{n+1} = X_n ∇ F♯(X_n)* is ascending, stabilizes after finitely many steps at some *X_N*, and *X_N ⊒ F♯(X_N)* — i.e. the limit is a *post-fixpoint* — regardless of the domain's chain height.

The standard interval widening extrapolates unstable bounds to infinity:

```haskell
-- standard widening on intervals: unstable bounds jump to ±∞
widen :: Interval -> Interval -> Interval
widen (l1, u1) (l2, u2) =
  ( if l2 < l1 then NegInf else l1
  , if u2 > u1 then PosInf else u1 )
```

Monniaux [7] showed that the usual axiom set for widening is *over-specified*: many standard axioms (e.g. that widened sequences be ascending) are unnecessary for the correctness proof. Keeping only the useful axioms, widening is equivalent to a *lazily constructed well-founded tree* — a characterization that transfers cleanly to higher-order-logic formalization.

Gange, Navas, Schachte, Søndergaard, and Stuckey [9] *dissected* widening further, separating the termination mechanism from the information content: widening may operate on values outside the abstract domain proper, provided the correspondence is made precise. This generalization yields compositional widenings for product domains, non-intrusive *delayed widening* variants, and robustness against syntactic/semantic category mistakes by analysis implementors.

### 4.3 Narrowing: Recovering Precision After Widening

Widening buys termination at the price of precision: jumping to *⊤* (or *[0, +∞]*) discards information the iteration had legitimately earned. **Narrowing** recovers it [1,2]. Given the widened post-fixpoint *X^∇*, the descending sequence *Y₀ = X^∇*, *Y_{n+1} = Y_n ∆ F♯(Y_n)* is applied for a *finite* (often small, e.g. 1–3) number of steps, where *∆* satisfies *y ⊑ x ⇒ y ⊑ x ∆ y ⊑ x*. Because the sequence is descending and bounded below by the true least fixpoint's abstraction, any finite prefix remains sound. In practice, even a single narrowing step frequently restores interval bounds lost to widening — e.g. refining *[0, +∞]* back to *[0, 100]* when the loop guard *x ≤ 100* is re-applied.

### 4.4 The Soundness Theorems: Fixpoint Transfer and Abstract Induction

The capstone results are the *fixpoint abstraction theorems* [1,2]. We state the two principal forms.

> **Theorem (fixpoint soundness / transfer lemma):** Let *⟨α, γ⟩* be a Galois connection between complete lattices *C* and *A*, *F : C → C* and *F♯ : A → A* monotone, with *F♯* sound for *F* (i.e. *F ∘ γ ⊑ γ ∘ F♯* pointwise). Then
>
> *α(lfp F) ⊑ lfp F♯*.
>
> In words: the abstraction of the concrete least fixpoint is below the abstract least fixpoint — the abstract fixpoint *over-approximates* the truth.

> **Corollary (post-fixpoint soundness):** Any abstract post-fixpoint *X♯ ⊒ F♯(X♯)* satisfies *α(lfp F) ⊑ X♯*, equivalently *lfp F ⊆ γ(X♯)*. Hence the *widened* limit of §4.2 — which is a post-fixpoint — is a *sound* over-approximation of the concrete semantics, and so is every finite narrowing refinement of it.

The modern formulation goes further: Cousot's 2014 Galois-connection calculus [4] and the 2024 transformational-logics paper [5] show that *proof rules themselves* can be derived by calculus from these theorems, making entire program logics sound by construction. Recent work applies the same skeleton to parallel and polynomial analyses, proving soundness of forward passes, widening iterations, and narrowing refinements in a single unified theorem [10].

The *abstract induction* principle [4] is the proof-theoretic dual: to prove that an abstract property *P♯* holds of the concrete fixpoint, it suffices to exhibit an abstract *inductive invariant* — a post-fixpoint *X♯ ⊒ F♯(X♯)* with *X♯ ⊑ P♯* — discharging verification into a finite abstract check.

## 5 Empirical Evidence / Proofs

### 5.1 A Worked Example: Interval Analysis of a Loop

Consider the program:

```ocaml
x := 0;
while x < 100 do x := x + 1 done
```

With the interval domain, naive iteration yields *[0,0], [0,1], [0,2], …* — one hundred steps to converge. With widening at the loop head: *[0,0] ∇ [0,1] = [0,+∞]* (the upper bound, being unstable, jumps to infinity), stabilizing in 2 steps. The guard *x < 100* on exit then yields *[0,99]*, and one narrowing step with the body transformer refines the invariant to the precise *[0,100]*. Total: 3 abstract steps instead of 100 — the canonical demonstration that widening trades a *bounded* precision loss for *unbounded* speedup, with narrowing recovering the loss when the domain permits.

### 5.2 Industrial Evidence: Astrée and Beyond

The Astrée analyzer — a direct descendant of the Cousot framework — proved the absence of runtime errors in the *entire* Airbus A340/A380 primary flight-control software (hundreds of thousands of lines of synchronous C), achieving *zero false alarms* on the production code after domain tuning. This remains the strongest empirical evidence that the theory scales: soundness is not merely a theorem about toy languages but a property of an industrial toolchain. Subsequent analyzers (e.g. for Java, for device drivers, for neural-network robustness) reuse the same three pillars.

### 5.3 Mechanized Evidence

Monniaux's minimalistic axiomatization [7] and the verified functional abstract interpreter of Jourdan et al. [8] — where abstract expression evaluation carries a refinement type stating *∀ m₀. m₀ ∈ γ(m₀♯) ⇒ osem(e)(m₀) ⊆ γ(asem(e)(m₀♯))* — show the soundness theorems surviving machine-checked formalization. When the proof assistant accepts the transfer lemma, soundness is no longer a claim in a paper but a *checked fact*.

| Evidence class | Artifact | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| Theory | Fixpoint transfer theorems [1,2] | Soundness of all abstract post-fixpoints |
| Widening | Minimal axioms [7]; dissected widening [9] | Termination with minimal assumptions |
| Industry | Astrée on Airbus flight control | Zero-alarm sound analysis at 10⁵+ LOC |
| Mechanization | Coq/F* abstract interpreter [8] | Machine-checked soundness theorem |
| Modern extensions | Parallel polynomial analysis [10] | Soundness of widening+narrowing pipelines |

## 6 Limitations

Honesty about limits is part of the theory itself. The principal limitations are:

1. **Incompleteness is the norm.** Best correct approximations exist mathematically but are rarely computable; real analyzers use *sound but incomplete* operators, and false alarms are the price of decidability. There is no general method to eliminate them without sacrificing termination.
2. **Widening is non-monotone and heuristic.** Any terminating widening is *not* monotone in its first argument [4]; precision depends on heuristics (thresholds, delayed widening, widening points) with no universal optimum — indeed, no *best* widening exists [4].
3. **Domain design is the hard part.** The framework tells you *how* to build a sound analysis from a domain, not *which* domain captures the property you need. Relational domains (octagons, polyhedra) gain precision at exponential cost; the cost/precision trade-off is irreducibly empirical.
4. **Non-termination of the concrete semantics.** Abstract interpretation computes *safety* (over-approximations of reachable states); liveness and termination require separate, dual analyses (e.g. ranking-function synthesis), though the same fixpoint machinery applies dually.
5. **Quantitative and probabilistic programs.** Classical abstract interpretation is *qualitative* (sets of states). Extending Galois connections and widening to distributions, expectations, and differential privacy is active research with no settled canonical framework yet.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Abstract interpretation endures — nearly five decades after POPL 1977 — because its three pillars answer the three questions every static analysis must face. *What does it mean to approximate?* A Galois connection (or its modern generalizations), which canonically determines best correct approximations. *How do we guarantee termination?* Widening, whose minimal axiomatization [7] and dissected generalization [9] show that convergence needs far less than the classical axioms assumed. *Why should we trust the answer?* The fixpoint transfer theorems and their post-fixpoint corollary, which reduce soundness to the check *X♯ ⊒ F♯(X♯)* — a single inequality in the abstract world that implies correctness in the concrete one.

The calculational methodology — deriving analyzers from semantics so that soundness holds by construction [4,5] — remains the deepest lesson: verification need not be an afterthought bolted onto an analysis, but can be the *process by which the analysis is designed*. As program analysis confronts probabilistic programs, machine-learning models, and quantitative properties, the pillars will need generalization; the discipline of making approximation mathematically explicit will not.
