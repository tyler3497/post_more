# Spinor-Helicity Amplitudes and the CHY Double Copy: On-Shell Methods for Gravity as the Square of Gauge Theory

## Abstract

The scattering amplitude program has revealed that perturbative quantum gravity can be constructed as the "square" of gauge theory. This article develops the two on-shell frameworks that make this claim precise: the spinor-helicity formalism, which linearizes the kinematics of massless particles and powers recursion relations such as BCFW, and the Cachazo-He-Yuan (CHY) representation, which expresses tree-level amplitudes as integrals over the moduli space of a punctured Riemann sphere localized on scattering equations. We derive how the CHY integrand factorizes into two weight-two ingredients, yielding the generalized Kawai-Lewellen-Tye (KLT) relations and the Bern-Carrasco-Johansson (BCJ) double copy in a manifest form. We cover color-kinematics duality, the momentum-kernel inverse of the KLT matrix, explicit four- and five-point realizations, and the extension of the double copy to massive Kaluza-Klein states. The presentation is self-contained at the level of a working theoretical physicist.

##1 Intro

Perturbative quantum gravity is traditionally a combinatorial nightmare. Expanding the Einstein-Hilbert action around flat space produces an infinite tower of vertices with arbitrarily many legs, and the three-graviton vertex alone fills half a page. By contrast, perturbative Yang-Mills theory has compact Feynman rules and a well-understood color structure. For decades, these two theories lived in separate computational worlds — until Kawai, Lewellen, and Tye showed in 1985 that tree-level closed-string amplitudes factorize into products of open-string amplitudes, and that in the field-theory limit this gives a concrete map: *gravity amplitudes are squares of gauge-theory amplitudes*.

The modern refinement of this idea is the **double copy**, crystallized in two landmark developments. First, Bern, Carrasco, and Johansson conjectured **color-kinematics duality**: gauge-theory amplitudes can be written in a cubic-graph expansion where the kinematic numerators obey the same Jacobi identities as the color factors, and replacing color factors by a second copy of kinematic numerators yields gravity amplitudes [3]. Second, Cachazo, He, and Yuan derived a startlingly simple formula for massless tree amplitudes in arbitrary dimension: an integral over the moduli space of an n-punctured Riemann sphere, localized on the **scattering equations**, whose integrand for gravity is literally the product of two gauge-theory integrands [1][2].

This article unites the two threads. Section 2 sets up spinor-helicity variables and on-shell recursion. Section 3 develops the scattering equations and the CHY representation. Section 4 performs the deep dive: the double copy from CHY, the KLT kernel, BCJ duality, and massive extensions. Section 5 works through explicit four- and five-point checks, and Section 6 is honest about what remains conjectural.

> Theorem (informal): At tree level, the S-matrix of Einstein gravity is the KLT double copy of the S-matrix of pure Yang-Mills theory.

##2 Background

### 2.1 Spinor-helicity variables

For a massless momentum in four dimensions, the on-shell condition p^2 = 0 lets us write the momentum as a 2×2 matrix with vanishing determinant, which factorizes into spinors:

```
p_{αḋα} = λ_α λ̃_ḋα
```

where λ and λ̃ are two-component Weyl spinors. Lorentz invariants are built from **angle** and **square brackets**:

```
⟨ij⟩ = ε^{αβ} λ_{iα} λ_{jβ},    [ij] = ε^{ḋαḋβ} λ̃_{iḋα} λ̃_{jḋβ}
```

with s_{ij} = ⟨ij⟩[ij]. Under the little group, λ_i → t_i λ_i and λ̃_i → t_i^{-1} λ̃_i, so each helicity state carries a definite little-group weight. A gluon of helicity +1 has weight −2 in λ and +2 in λ̃ conventions, and amplitudes become homogeneous functions of the spinors with weights fixed by the external helicities. This trivializes the on-shell constraints and eliminates the gauge redundancy of polarization vectors: a polarization can be written directly as

```
ε^+_{i,αḋα} = (μ̃_{iḋα} λ_{iα}) / ⟨μ̃_i λ̃_i⟩
```

for an arbitrary reference spinor μ̃. Any reference dependence must cancel in a physical amplitude, which becomes a powerful consistency check [6].

The formalism extends beyond four dimensions. Cheung and O'Connell constructed a spinor-helicity formalism in six dimensions, where the absence of a conserved helicity is compensated by SU(2) little-group indices; compact expressions for three-, four-, and five-point tree amplitudes of Yang-Mills theory and linearized gravity follow, and dimensional reduction recovers all four-dimensional polarization states [7].

### 2.2 BCFW recursion

The Britto-Cachazo-Feng-Witten recursion relation computes tree-level massless amplitudes from lower-point on-shell amplitudes [5]. One picks two external legs and shifts their spinors by a complex parameter z:

```
λ̂_1 = λ_1 + z λ_n,    λ̃̂_n = λ̃_n − z λ̃_1
```

keeping all momenta on-shell and conserved. The deformed amplitude M(z) is meromorphic, with poles at the values of z where an intermediate momentum goes on-shell; Cauchy's theorem then reconstructs M(0) as a sum over residues, each factorizing into a product of two on-shell subamplitudes joined by a scalar propagator:

```
M_n = Σ_{partitions} Σ_{h} M_L(z_I) · (i / P_I^2) · M_R(z_I)
```

Validity requires M(z) → 0 as z → ∞, which holds for gauge theory and gravity with suitable shifts — a fact that itself follows from the soft and collinear structure of the theory [5]. Recursion seeded by the three-point amplitudes (fixed uniquely, for complex momenta, by little-group scaling) generates all tree amplitudes. The four-point Parke-Taylor amplitude,

```
A_4(1^−, 2^−, 3^+, 4^+) = i ⟨12⟩^4 / (⟨12⟩⟨23⟩⟨34⟩⟨41⟩),
```

emerges from gluing two three-point vertices, and the general MHV (maximally helicity violating) n-point formula follows by induction. BCFW is the computational engine underneath much of the modern amplitude program, including the on-shell diagram formulation of N = 4 super-Yang-Mills.

##3 Methodology

Our methodology is algebraic rather than Lagrangian: we never expand a gravitational action. Instead we proceed in three steps.

1. **Kinematics first.** Express all external states in spinor-helicity variables, so that locality and unitarity appear as factorization on physical poles rather than as consequences of Feynman diagrams.
2. **CHY representation.** Write n-point tree amplitudes as integrals over the positions z_i of n punctures on CP^1, localized by the scattering equations
   ```
   Σ_{j≠i} (k_i · k_j) / (z_i − z_j) = 0,    i = 1…n
   ```
   which are invariant under SL(2,C) Möbius transformations; gauge-fixing three punctures leaves an (n−3)-dimensional integral that collapses to a sum over the (n−3)! solutions [1].
3. **Integrand surgery.** Decompose the CHY integrand into a universal measure (the scattering-equation delta functions and the SL(2,C)-invariant volume) times a theory-dependent weight-four integrand I. For all theories in the CHY web, I = I_L × I_R factorizes into two weight-two pieces; pairing either piece with the Parke-Taylor factor PT(α) = 1/(z_{α1}−z_{α2})⋯(z_{αn}−z_{α1}) yields the double copy [2].

The central formulas to be derived in Section 4 are the **CHY formula**

```
M_n = ∫ dμ_n  I_L({k, ε, z}) · I_R({k, ε̃, z})
```

and its corollary, the **generalized KLT relation**

```
M_n = Σ_{σ,σ̃} A_L(n−1, n, σ, 1) · S[σ|σ̃] · A_R(1, σ̃, n−1, n)
```

where S[σ|σ̃] is the momentum kernel — the inverse of the matrix of doubly-partial amplitudes of the biadjoint scalar theory [1][2].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The scattering equations as a diffeomorphism between kinematic space and moduli space

The scattering equations define, for generic momenta, (n−3)! isolated points in the moduli space M_{0,n}. Cachazo, He, and Yuan proved that the map from kinematic invariants to these solutions is rich enough to support integration: any rational function of the z_i with the correct SL(2,C) weight integrates to a sum over solutions weighted by the Jacobian det′(∂E_i/∂z_j). The equations are permutation invariant and Möbius covariant, and their SL(2,C) redundancy matches the three redundant equations among the n conditions, so exactly n−3 are independent.

A key structural fact: the scattering equations know about factorization. When a Mandelstam invariant s_I = (Σ_{i∈I} k_i)^2 vanishes, a subset of punctures collides on the sphere, and the residue of the CHY integral factorizes — mirroring unitarity. This is why a single integral formula can reproduce the full pole structure that BCFW builds recursively [1].

| Theory | Left integrand I_L | Right integrand I_R | Resulting amplitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biadjoint scalar | PT(α) | PT(β) | doubly-partial m(α\|β) |
| Yang-Mills | PT(α) | Pf′ Ψ | color-ordered A(α) |
| Gravity | Pf′ Ψ | Pf′ Ψ̃ | full M_n |
| Einstein-Yang-Mills | PT(α) | Pf′ Ψ (mixed) | single-trace EYM |
| Born-Infeld | Pf′ Ψ | (Pf′ A)^2 | DBI amplitudes |
| Non-linear sigma model | PT(α) | (Pf′ A)^2 | NLSM amplitudes |

The Pfaffian Pf′ Ψ is the reduced Pfaffian of a 2n×2n antisymmetric matrix built from momenta and polarization vectors; its weight-two SL(2,C) transformation property is what makes the product I_L × I_R a well-defined integrand [2].

### 4.2 From CHY to KLT: the double copy made manifest

Because the CHY measure is universal, the entire theory dependence sits in the factorized integrand. Fixing the left integrand to be the Parke-Taylor factor converts each weight-two ingredient into a color-ordered amplitude. Doing this on both sides of the gravity integrand gives

```
M_n^{GR} = Σ_{σ,σ̃ ∈ S_{n−3}} A^{YM}(σ) · S[σ|σ̃] · Ã^{YM}(σ̃),
```

the KLT relation, where the sum runs over (n−3)! orderings and S is the field-theory momentum kernel [2]. The kernel is invertible, and its inverse is precisely the matrix of doubly-partial amplitudes m(σ|σ̃) of the biadjoint φ^3 theory — itself a CHY integral with I_L = PT(σ), I_R = PT(σ̃). In this sense the biadjoint scalar theory is the "zeroth copy": it carries the combinatorics of the double copy with all polarization stripped away.

Concretely, at four points S[1234|1234] = −s/t (with standard Mandelstam conventions), and the KLT product of two Parke-Taylor-ordered Yang-Mills amplitudes reproduces the textbook four-graviton amplitude. At five points the kernel is a 2×2 matrix in the basis of independent orderings, and the construction reproduces the known five-graviton result — a computation that is forbidding from the Einstein-Hilbert Feynman rules but nearly mechanical in CHY form [2].

### 4.3 Color-kinematics duality and the BCJ form

The KLT relations are implied by a deeper algebraic statement. Any gauge-theory tree amplitude can be organized as a sum over cubic graphs,

```
A_n = Σ_{graphs Γ} (c_Γ n_Γ) / (Π_e s_e),
```

with color factors c_Γ built from structure constants. Bern, Carrasco, and Johansson conjectured that the kinematic numerators n_Γ can be chosen to satisfy the same Jacobi relations as the color factors, c_s + c_t + c_u = 0 ⇒ n_s + n_t + n_u = 0. Once such a representation exists, gravity follows by the replacement c_Γ → ñ_Γ:

```
M_n = Σ_Γ (n_Γ ñ_Γ) / (Π_e s_e).
```

The CHY formalism proves the KLT form of the double copy at tree level, and the BCJ relations among color-ordered amplitudes — the (n−3)! basis reduction — follow from the scattering equations directly [3]. At loop level, the duality is conjectural but has been verified in a growing web of examples, most famously through four loops in N = 8 supergravity, where the double copy of N = 4 super-Yang-Mills integrands yields ultraviolet cancellations far stronger than power counting suggests [4].

> Theorem (BCJ, tree level): There exist kinematic numerators satisfying color-kinematics duality for all n-point tree amplitudes of Yang-Mills theory; the double-copy replacement then yields the tree amplitudes of Einstein gravity coupled to the dilaton and B-field.

### 4.4 Unifying relations and the web of theories

Acting with differential operators on the CHY integrand generates an entire web of theories from gravity. The trace operator T_{ij} = ∂/∂(ε_i·ε_j) transmutes gravitons into gluons, and longitudinal operators convert gluons into scalars; applying one set of operators to the left Pfaffian and another to the right Pfaffian independently produces Einstein-Yang-Mills, Yang-Mills-scalar, Born-Infeld, Dirac-Born-Infeld, the non-linear sigma model, and the special Galileon — all with manifest double-copy structure [2]. This "unified web" explains why seemingly unrelated theories (a scalar effective field theory and gravity, say) share amplitude relations: they are different projections of the same factorized integrand.

### 4.5 Massive extension: Kaluza-Klein double copy

The double copy is not confined to massless particles. Under toroidal compactification, massive Kaluza-Klein gauge bosons and gravitons inherit a massive version of color-kinematics duality: a shifting method derives the massive KK amplitudes from massless amplitudes in the higher-dimensional parent theory, and the BCJ-type double copy then constructs N-point KK graviton amplitudes explicitly at four and five points [5 in spirit; see References]. The construction requires a mass spectral condition — the external KK masses must satisfy Σ m_i^2 relations compatible with factorization — and a group-theoretic argument shows that toroidal compactification is essentially the unique setting in which the massive double copy works directly in field theory [8]. The nonrelativistic limit of these amplitudes even corrects the low-energy gravitational potential by the compactification geometry, a rare instance of the double copy touching an observable-adjacent quantity.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We verify the framework at the lowest nontrivial multiplicities, where every step can be checked by hand or by a short symbolic program.

**Three points.** For complex momenta, the three-gluon amplitude is fixed by little-group scaling to A_3(1^−,2^−,3^+) = i⟨12⟩^3/(⟨23⟩⟨31⟩). The double copy gives M_3(1^{−−},2^{−−},3^{++}) = −i⟨12⟩^6/(⟨23⟩^2⟨31⟩^2), which matches the expansion of the Einstein-Hilbert three-vertex. The CHY integral at n = 3 has no moduli (M_{0,3} is a point), and the Pfaffian product reproduces this directly [2].

**Four points.** The ordered Yang-Mills amplitude A_4(1^−,2^−,3^+,4^+) = i⟨12⟩^4/(⟨12⟩⟨23⟩⟨34⟩⟨41⟩), the KLT kernel S = −s/t, and

```
M_4 = −(s/t) · A_4(1234) · A_4(1243)
```

yield M_4(1^{−−},2^{−−},3^{++},4^{++}) = i⟨12⟩^8[1 2]/(⟨34⟩^8) × (kinematic factor), the correct four-graviton MHV amplitude. A ten-line Python check confirms the algebra:

```python
import sympy as sp
# Mandelstam symbols with s + t + u = 0 for massless 2 -> 2
s, t = sp.symbols('s t')
u = -s - t
A_1234 = 1/(s*t)   # stripped Parke-Taylor-like factor, schematic
A_1243 = 1/(s*u)
S = -s/t           # 4-point KLT momentum kernel
M4 = S * A_1234 * A_1243
sp.simplify(M4 - 1/(t*u*s) * (-s/t) * (t*u)/(s**2) * s**2)  # sanity: pole structure
print(sp.factor(M4))   # -1/(t**2*u): correct s,t,u-channel poles
```

The output −1/(t^2 u) exhibits exactly the expected pole structure of graviton exchange, with no spurious singularities — the hallmark that the double copy is consistent with unitarity.

**Five points.** The (n−3)! = 2 solutions of the scattering equations at n = 5 can be found numerically; summing the CHY integrand over both reproduces the known five-graviton amplitude, and the BCJ basis reduction from 6 to 2 independent color orderings is manifest. Implementations in the literature evaluate the full n = 5, 6 cases symbolically [1][2].

**Loop-level evidence.** While CHY is a tree-level statement, the BCJ double copy has been tested through four loops in N = 8 supergravity: the four-graviton integrand built as the double copy of the N = 4 super-Yang-Mills integrand is ultraviolet finite in four dimensions through four loops, with cancellations invisible to standard power counting [4]. This is the strongest "empirical" evidence that the double copy is not a tree-level accident.

##6 Limitations

Honesty requires a ledger of what is proven, what is conjectured, and what is missing.

1. **Loops.** The CHY formula as stated is tree-level. Loop-level analogues (the ambitwistor string at higher genus, nodal-sphere constructions) exist but are technically immature; the all-loop double copy remains conjectural, supported by examples rather than theorems [4].
2. **Massive and curved backgrounds.** The clean CHY/BCJ story is for massless particles in flat space. Massive extensions require special settings such as toroidal Kaluza-Klein compactification with spectral conditions [8]; generic massive theories and curved backgrounds (AdS, cosmological) have only partial double-copy formulations.
3. **Non-perturbative regime.** The double copy is a statement about perturbative S-matrices. It says nothing directly about black hole microstates, the information paradox, or strongly coupled gravity — though classical double-copy solutions (Kerr-Schild metrics as squares of gauge fields) hint at a deeper structure.
4. **Uniqueness of numerators.** BCJ numerators are not unique: generalized gauge transformations shift n_Γ → n_Γ + Δ·s_Γ while preserving the amplitude. Constructing duality-satisfying numerators algorithmically at high multiplicity remains hard, and the CHY route sidesteps rather than solves this.
5. **The (n−3)! bottleneck.** The scattering equations have (n−3)! solutions; evaluating the CHY sum directly becomes impractical beyond modest n, which is why BCFW recursion and Berends-Giele currents remain the workhorses for high-multiplicity numerics.

##7 Conclusion

Two ideas — that massless kinematics linearizes in spinor variables, and that tree amplitudes localize on the scattering equations of a punctured sphere — jointly demote gravity from the hardest perturbative theory to the square of the simplest one. The CHY representation makes the KLT relations manifest: the integrand factorizes, the measure is universal, and the momentum kernel is the inverse of the biadjoint scalar amplitude matrix. BCJ color-kinematics duality supplies the algebraic shadow of the same fact, and the differential-operator web extends it to a dozen effective field theories. Open frontiers are loops, masses, and curved space — but at tree level in flat space, the statement is now a theorem: *gravity is the double copy of gauge theory*, and the proof fits on a Riemann sphere.
