# Interactive Theorem Proving in Lean 4: Dependent Type Theory, Tactic Metaprogramming, Mathlib Formalization, and Verified Compilation Pipelines

## Abstract

Lean 4 unifies an interactive theorem prover, a dependently typed functional programming language, and an industrial-strength compiler toolchain inside a single system. This thesis examines the architecture end to end: the kernel's implementation of the Calculus of Inductive Constructions extended with quotient types and proof irrelevance; the elaborator's synthesis of implicit arguments through metavariable assignment and higher-order unification; the monadic tactic-metaprogramming framework that exposes the elaborator's internals to verified user code; the Mathlib library as an existence proof that machine-checked mathematics can be engineered at the scale of millions of lines; and the emerging program to verify Lean's own trusted core, exemplified by the self-hosted Lean4Lean external typechecker. Drawing on the system-description literature and recent metatheory results, we argue that Lean 4's distinguishing contribution is not any single logical innovation, but the co-design of logic, elaboration, automation, and compilation into one coherent artifact — making formalized mathematics and verified software two faces of the same discipline.

---

## 1 Introduction

Interactive theorem proving has undergone a quiet phase transition. Systems once confined to hardware verification now absorb entire mathematics curricula, and proof assistants once dismissed as research toys ship compilers, package managers, and language servers. Lean 4 sits at the center of this transition: reimplemented from scratch — in Lean itself — as both a theorem prover and a general-purpose functional language with efficient code generation [1][2].

Lean 4's central thesis is that *proof automation should be written in the same language as the proofs themselves*, compiled by the same compiler, and executed at native speed [2]. The boundary between "tactic" and "program", and between "proof checker" and "compiler", is therefore deliberately thin. Understanding Lean 4 requires understanding several coupled subsystems at once: a dependently typed kernel, a sophisticated elaborator, a metaprogramming framework, a million-line mathematical library, and a compilation pipeline that turns verified programs into machine code.

This thesis contributes: (i) a precise account of Lean 4's logical foundations — CIC with universe polymorphism, definitional versus propositional equality, inductive families, and quotient types (§4.1); (ii) an analysis of elaboration via metavariables, higher-order unification, and tabled typeclass resolution (§4.2); (iii) a treatment of the monadic tactic framework, hygienic macros, and reflection (§4.3); (iv) Mathlib as engineering evidence for scalability (§4.4); and (v) a survey of the verification frontier, centered on the Lean4Lean external typechecker [5] (§4.4–§5). Section 2 reviews background, §3 describes methodology, §4 contains the deep dive, §5 collects empirical results, §6 discusses limitations, and §7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The LCF tradition and the de Bruijn criterion

Modern proof assistants descend architecturally from Milner's LCF: a *small trusted kernel* whose correctness implies the correctness of every accepted proof, because all proof-producing code funnels through it. This is the *de Bruijn criterion*. Lean follows it strictly — every declaration elaborated by the large, unverified frontend is re-checked by the kernel before admission to the environment [1][5]. The kernel's smallness is what makes projects like Lean4Lean feasible: reimplementing and *verifying* a typechecker is tractable precisely because the trusted core is thousands of lines, not hundreds of thousands [5].

### 2.2 Dependent type theory and the Calculus of Inductive Constructions

Under Curry–Howard, propositions are types and proofs are programs. In dependent type theory, types may depend on terms: the proposition "for all n, P n" is the dependent function type `∀ n : Nat, P n`, and its proof is a function mapping each `n` to a proof of `P n`. The Calculus of Inductive Constructions — underlying Coq and Lean — extends the Calculus of Constructions with primitive inductive types and a predicative universe hierarchy [1][3]. Lean's variant makes distinctive choices:

- **An impredicative, proof-irrelevant `Prop`.** Quantifying over all propositions yields a proposition (`∀ P : Prop, P → P : Prop`), and any two proofs of the same proposition are definitionally equal — licensing aggressive proof erasure at compile time.
- **A predicative tower above `Prop`.** `Type u` classifies data; quantifying over `Type u` lands in a strictly larger universe, excluding Girard's paradox.
- **Universe polymorphism.** Definitions parameterized by universe levels work uniformly at every universe.
- **Kernel quotient types.** `Quot.mk` and `Quot.lift` make setoid-style reasoning primitive rather than propositional.

Classical reasoning is available but quarantined: propositional extensionality (`propext`), function extensionality (`funext`), and Hilbertian choice are consistent with the theory and explicitly tracked — `#print axioms` flags any theorem depending on them [3].

### 2.3 Definitional versus propositional equality

This distinction is the fulcrum of the system. *Definitional* (judgmental) equality `t ≡ t'` is the kernel's decidable conversion relation: δ-unfolding of definitions, β-reduction, η-expansion, and ι-reduction of recursors on constructors. *Propositional* equality is the inductive family `Eq : α → α → Prop`, whose proofs are constructed explicitly and transported with `Eq.subst`. Definitional equality is checked silently; propositional equality must be *proved*. Much of the elaborator exists to bridge this gap — turning equalities mathematicians take for granted into explicit proof terms the kernel accepts.

### 2.4 Metaprogramming lineage

Tactic metaprogramming predates Lean — Coq's Ltac, Isabelle's Eisbach, Lean 3's tactic framework [4]. Lean 4's innovation is making metaprograms *first-class Lean programs*: typechecked, compiled to C, executed natively, with direct API access to elaborator internals. De Moura calls this *white-box automation* — automation that manipulates the prover's internals rather than speaking through a narrow tactic language [1][2].

## 3 Methodology

We decompose Lean 4 into five strata — kernel, elaborator, tactic framework, library, compiler — and triangulate between the system descriptions [1][2], the metaprogramming paper [4], the tutorial text [3], the Lean4Lean report [5], and the Mathlib engineering literature [6][8]. Each stratum is evaluated against four criteria:

| Criterion | Question asked |
|---|---|
| **Soundness** | Does the stratum preserve kernel guarantees? Where is the trusted base? |
| **Completeness** | Which valid inputs fail, and why? |
| **Performance** | What are the asymptotic and empirical costs? |
| **Scale evidence** | Has the stratum been exercised at industrial scale? |

This is a systems thesis: stated theorems are either standard CIC metatheory or results reported by the surveyed verification projects, cited accordingly.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Kernel: A Small Checker for a Rich Theory

The kernel manipulates a minimal expression language — constants, free/bound variables, metavariables, applications, lambdas, dependent products (`Expr.forallE`), let-bindings, literals, projections — and implements two judgments: typing (`Γ ⊢ t : T`) and definitional equality (`t ≡ t'`).

**Universes and formation.** `Sort u` with `Prop := Sort 0`, `Type u := Sort (u+1)`:

> **Theorem:** (Π-formation). If `Γ ⊢ A : Sort u` and `Γ, x : A ⊢ B : Sort v`, then `Γ ⊢ (∀ x : A, B) : Sort (imax u v)`, where `imax u v = 0` when `v = 0` (impredicativity of `Prop`), else `max u v`.

Introduction (`fun x => b`) and elimination (application) are standard, with β-reduction computed definitionally. One mechanism uniformly handles universal quantification, implication (`A → B` as the non-dependent case), and dependent data.

**Inductive types and termination.** Inductive families declare constructors and eliminate via recursors, with ι-reduction computing recursors on constructors. Termination checking lives *outside* the kernel: the frontend compiles structural and well-founded recursion into recursors (`brecOn`) the kernel accepts, so the kernel needs no termination oracle [2][5].

**Conversion, operationally.** `t ≡ t'` is decided by reducing both sides to weak head normal form and comparing structurally, with lazy, cached δ-unfolding. This is where performance lives or dies: unfolding a large Mathlib definition during conversion can dominate elaboration, which is why Mathlib engineering invests in `@[simp]`, reducibility annotations, and discrimination-tree indexing [8].

**Quotients, irrelevance, axioms.** Proof irrelevance is definitional: `p q : P` implies `p ≡ q`. Quotient types are primitive, with the kernel enforcing respect for the relation. The three admitted axioms — `propext`, `funext`, `Classical.choice` — are consistent extensions whose use is tracked per-declaration [3].

### 4.2 The Elaborator: Metavariables, Unification, and Typeclass Resolution

The elaborator maps *preterms* — surface syntax with omitted implicits and unknown types — to fully explicit kernel terms via three mechanisms.

**Metavariables.** Each unknown becomes `?m : T` in the metavariable context (MCtx), recording its type, local context, and eventual assignment. Elaboration is constraint generation plus solving: `fun x => x + 1` against an expected type spawns metavariables for domain and codomain that unification fills in.

**Unification.** Lean combines first-order unification with Huet-style *pattern* unification — the decidable Miller fragment, where `?m x y` for distinct bound `x y` solves by imitation or projection. Non-pattern cases (`?m (f x)`, flex–flex pairs) are *postponed*: elaboration continues, hoping later constraints produce a pattern. Postponement buys remarkable flexibility at the cost of occasional incompleteness (§6).

**Typeclass resolution.** Instance-implicit arguments (`[CommRing R]`) are discharged by logic-programming search over the instance database. Lean 4 replaced Lean 3's depth-first search with *tabled* resolution, memoizing subgoals to kill the exponential blowups that plagued large hierarchies [2]. The payoff is concrete: Mathlib's algebraic hierarchy — hundreds of interlocking classes from `Semigroup` to `NormedAlgebra` — elaborates routinely where Lean 3 demanded manual guidance.

| Phase | Input → Output | Key structure |
|---|---|---|
| Parsing | Text → `Syntax` | Hygienic syntax trees |
| Macro expansion | `Syntax` → preterm | `macro_rules` |
| Elaboration | Preterm → `Expr` + metavariables | MCtx, postponed constraints |
| Unification | Constraints → assignments | Pattern unifier |
| Kernel check | `Expr` → acceptance | `isDefEq`, whnf cache |

```lean
-- Elaboration at work: implicits, typeclasses, and coercions are all inferred.
example : ∀ n : Nat, n + 0 = n := by
  intro n
  induction n with
  | zero => rfl          -- definitional: 0 + 0 ≡ 0 by ι-reduction
  | succ k ih =>         -- ih : k + 0 = k
    rw [Nat.succ_add, ih]
```

Before the first tactic runs, implicit arguments were inferred, `0` was coerced via `OfNat`, and the induction motive was synthesized by higher-order unification.

### 4.3 Tactic Metaprogramming: Monads, Macros, and Reflection

Tactics are ordinary Lean programs with privileged access to prover internals, organized as a monad stack [2][4]:

| Monad | State exposed | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `CoreM` | Environment, options, diagnostics | Inspecting declarations |
| `MetaM` | Metavariable context, caches | Unification, `isDefEq`, whnf |
| `TermElabM` | Elaboration context, pending constraints | `elabTerm`, coercion insertion |
| `TacticM` | Goal list, tactic context | Proof scripts, goal manipulation |
| `CommandElabM` | Command state, scopes | New commands, `#eval` |

Concretely, `TacticM α` is `ReaderT Context (StateRefT State TermElabM) α`: the state holds open goals — each a metavariable whose type is the remaining proposition — and the context marks the main goal. Tactics transform this state: `intro` assigns a lambda to the main goal's metavariable; `apply` unifies the goal against a lemma's conclusion, spawning subgoals for premises; backtracking combinators snapshot and restore the metavariable context, giving proof search transactional semantics.

**Hygienic macros.** `syntax` extends the parser; `macro_rules` define expansions that are hygienic by construction — macro-introduced identifiers cannot capture user variables [2]. Tactics are typically two layers: a `syntax`/`macro` frontend and an `elab` implementation in `TacticM`:

```lean
-- A tiny reflective tactic closing any goal of the form `a = a`.
syntax "myrfl" : tactic
elab "myrfl" : tactic => do
  let goal ← getMainGoal
  goal.withContext do
    let target ← goal.getType
    unless target.isAppOfArity ``Eq 3 do
      throwError "myrfl: goal is not an equality"
    let #[_, lhs, rhs] := target.getAppArgs | throwError "impossible"
    unless ← isDefEq lhs rhs do
      throwError "myrfl: sides not definitionally equal"
    goal.assign (← mkAppM ``Eq.refl #[lhs])
```

This is *white-box automation* [1]: the tactic inspects the goal's `Expr`, invokes the kernel's own `isDefEq`, and builds the proof term directly. Because tactics compile to C, automation like `simp` (conditional rewriting over discrimination trees) and `aesop` (best-first search) runs at native speed — a decisive advantage over interpreted tactic languages and a large part of why Mathlib-scale automation is practical [8].

**Reflection and quotation.** The `Qq` library provides typed quotation, letting metaprograms match on and construct terms with static shape guarantees. Delaboration (`DelabM`) inverts elaboration, pretty-printing kernel terms with implicits re-inserted — essential for readable proof states and diagnostics.

### 4.4 Mathlib at Scale and the Verified Compilation Horizon

**Mathlib as scale evidence.** Mathlib is the community mathematical library for Lean, developed openly at [7] and described in [6]: on the order of one and a half million lines of Lean, well over one hundred thousand theorems and definitions, in a layered, acyclic import DAG:

- **Foundations**: `Mathlib.Logic`, `Mathlib.Data` (quotients, finsets)
- **Algebra**: the typeclass hierarchy — monoids through fields, modules, algebras
- **Order/topology**: lattices, filters, topological and uniform spaces
- **Analysis**: metric spaces, measure theory, calculus, manifolds
- **Bridges**: category theory, algebraic geometry, number theory, combinatorics

CI enforces acyclicity on every pull request, and `lake exe graph` visualizes the DAG [7][8].

**Engineering a million-line formalization.** The "Growing Mathlib" study [8] documents the maintenance practices behind this corpus: bors merge queues, per-PR full builds, and continuous investment in automation ergonomics. A representative finding: after the Lean 4 port, discrimination-tree indexing made the default `simp` set cheap to query, so contributors *dropped* thousands of brittle `simp only [...]` annotations — reducing maintenance burden when lemmas changed [8]. Likewise, general-purpose `aesop` search is increasingly wrapped by narrow, fast tactics like `fun_prop` wherever proof shape is predetermined, trading completeness for predictable performance. The general lesson: at scale, the economics of automation favor *fast, predictable, composable* tools over maximally powerful ones.

**Compilation: functional but in-place.** Lean 4's compiler embodies the *functional but in-place* paradigm [2]: pure programs compile to code that reuses memory in place when reference counts prove it safe, recovering imperative performance without sacrificing equational reasoning. The pipeline runs Lean source → Lean IR → C → machine code, and because the compiler is written in Lean itself, the system that checks proofs is the system that compiles tactics.

**Toward verified compilation.** The trust story has a gap: the kernel is small, but the elaborator and backend are large and unverified. Two directions address this. First, *kernel verification*: Carneiro's Lean4Lean [5] implements a complete external Lean 4 typechecker — written in Lean — competitive with the C++ reference (20–50% slower) and used to verify all of Mathlib. Because the checker is written in a verifiable language, Carneiro formalizes Lean's type theory abstractly, proves metatheorems about it, and composes these into partial kernel-correctness proofs — work that already caught and fixed one genuine soundness bug [5]. Second, *verified compilation* à la CompCert/CakeML: proving IR transformations preserve semantics. Lean's self-hosting makes a striking endgame conceivable — a compiler verified in Lean, checked by a kernel verified in Lean — though elaborator and backend remain unverified today (§6).

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

| Result | Source | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| External checker performance | [5] | Complete Lean 4 typechecker in Lean; 20–50% slower than C++; verifies all of Mathlib |
| Soundness bug discovery | [5] | Verification exposed one genuine kernel soundness bug, since fixed |
| Partial kernel correctness | [5] | Abstract type-theory formalization + metatheorems composed into correctness proofs for kernel parts |
| Corpus scale | [6][7] | ~1.5M lines; >100k theorems/definitions; full CI per PR |
| Simp-set maintenance | [8] | Discrimination-tree indexing made unrestricted `simp` cheap; mass migration away from brittle `simp only` |
| Tactic specialization | [8] | Narrow tactics (`fun_prop`) replace general search (`aesop`) where shape is predetermined, with large speedups |
| Tabled typeclass resolution | [2] | Memoized search eliminates Lean 3's exponential blowups |

On the formal side, the metatheorems established for Lean's theory include the standard CIC suite:

> **Theorem:** (Subject reduction, as formalized in Lean4Lean [5]). If `Γ ⊢ t : T` and `t` reduces to `t'` under the kernel's reduction relation, then `Γ ⊢ t' : T`.

Two caveats. First, verification is *partial*: it covers the kernel's type theory, not elaborator unification or code generation. Second, an ineliminable bootstrapping regress remains — the verified Lean checker is compiled by the unverified toolchain — mitigable via diverse double-compilation but not eliminable. The honest claim: *the kernel's implementation now enjoys machine-checked evidence of correspondence to its specification* — more than any previous Lean release could claim, less than a fully verified stack.

## 6 Limitations

**Elaborator incompleteness.** Every declaration is kernel-checked, so elaborator bugs surface as *failures* rather than unsoundness. But incompleteness is a daily tax: postponed constraints can stagnate, typeclass search can time out, and errors ("stuck at solving universe constraints") expose internals rather than mathematics.

**Axioms.** `propext`/`funext`/`choice` are consistent and tracked, yet foreclose fully computational readings; axiom-free developments must police transitive dependencies via `#print axioms`.

**Performance cliffs.** Definitional unfolding dominates costs — one `rfl` unfolding a large definition can take seconds — and typeclass inference still goes superlinear in adversarial cases. Mathlib's response (reducibility control, discrimination trees, tactic specialization [8]) works but is manual.

**No universe cumulativity.** Non-cumulative universes simplify kernel and metatheory [5] but force occasional boilerplate universe management on library authors.

**The verification gap.** Lean4Lean covers the kernel, not the elaborator or backend. A verified elaborator and a CakeML-style verified backend for Lean's IR remain the open frontier.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Lean 4 is a single co-design decision pursued relentlessly: *one language for proving, programming, automating, and compiling*. The kernel implements a CIC variant tuned for working mathematics — impredicative proof-irrelevant `Prop`, quotient types, tracked classical axioms [1][3]. The elaborator turns human-scale notation into kernel terms via metavariables, pattern unification, and tabled typeclass search [2]. The metaprogramming framework compiles tactics to native code with white-box elaborator access [4]. Mathlib proves the stack scales to millions of lines under continuous integration [6][8]. And Lean4Lean shows the kernel itself can be machine-checked, with verification already paying for itself in discovered bugs [5].

What remains is pushing verification outward — through the elaborator into the compiler backend — until the pipeline from mathematical statement to machine code carries a machine-checked certificate. Lean 4's self-hosting makes this uniquely well-posed: verifier, verified, and language of verification are one and the same. Completing that program would make Lean 4 not merely a very good proof assistant, but the system that made verified software engineering routine.
