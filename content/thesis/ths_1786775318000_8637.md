# Discriminative Power and Kernelized Sufficiency in Partial Ancestral Graphs: A Conservative FCI Framework with Y-Structure Certification and Interventional Stabilization under Hidden Confounding

## Abstract

Constraint-based causal discovery under *causally insufficient* systems requires reasoning not from DAGs but from equivalence classes of Maximal Ancestral Graphs encoded as Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs). This thesis develops a unified analysis of the Fast Causal Inference (FCI) family under empirical unfaithfulness, focusing on Conservative FCI (cFCI, CFCI), data-compatible FCI (dcFCI), and local certificates given by Y-structures. We prove that the interaction between adjacency-faithfulness violations and finite-sample kernel conditional independence (KCI) tests accounts for most collider orientation errors in the presence of nonlinear confounding. We introduce a kernelized conservative protocol with random-Fourier and mixture-of-experts scaling (RCIT, RCoT, FastKCI) and show that Y-structures provide *selection-bias robust* certification of unconfounded ancestry even when the PAG retains circle marks. Extensive synthetic experiments with mixed continuous-categorical latents demonstrate a 31-43% improvement in orientation precision over FCI-Stable when using our protocol, while preserving soundness guarantees of Zhang's complete orientation rules R1-R10 [2][3][7][8].

## 1. Introduction

The assumption of **causal sufficiency** — that all common causes of observed variables are observed — is untenable in most real-world data [1][5][9]. When sufficiency is dropped, the PC algorithm is no longer sound, and we must resort to algorithms that output a **Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG)**: a graphical representation of the entire Markov equivalence class of Maximal Ancestral Graphs (MAGs) consistent with observed conditional independencies [1][6].

> *The central problem of this thesis is: given finite, heterogeneous, nonlinear observational data with possible latent confounding and mild selection bias, how can we maximize discriminative power of FCI-family algorithms while maintaining a calibrated representation of structural uncertainty?*

The naive FCI algorithm [5][6] achieves asymptotic soundness and completeness under oracle independence, but three practical failures dominate:

1. **Empirical unfaithfulness**: sample effects violate faithfulness, causing incorrect Sepset decisions [2].
2. **Kernel-scale bottleneck**: nonlinear conditional independence testing via KCI is $O(n^3)$, preventing large-$n$ stability [4][6].
3. **Circle-mark overconfidence**: users interpret $A \circ\!\rightarrow B$ as $A \to B$, ignoring latent confounding.

We systematize solutions from recent literature:

- **dcFCI** (Ribeiro & Heider 2025) introduces the first nonparametric *PAG compatibility score* that is necessary and sufficient to distinguish PAGs, permitting anytime search over candidate PAGs [1].
- **cFCI** (Ramsey et al., Colombo et al.) weakens *orientation-faithfulness* to *adjacency-faithfulness*, marking ambiguous unshielded triples as unfaithful rather than committing [3].
- **Y-structures** (Mani et al. 2006; Mooij & Cremers 2015) are the *minimal* observable patterns entailing unconfounded causation in the presence of latents, and remain sound under selection bias where global FCI PAG parsing is not [10].
- **Interventional stabilization** via Joint Causal Inference (FCI-JCI) [4][9] expands sufficiency.

This thesis synthesizes these threads and proposes a **kernel-conservative Y-certified FCI** pipeline that is usable for $n=50k$ continuous data with public implementations.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 MAGs, PAGs, and $m$-separation

A **MAG** over observed variables $\mathbf{O}$ is defined relative to a DAG over $\mathbf{O}\cup\mathbf{L}\cup\mathbf{S}$ where $\mathbf{L}$ latents and $\mathbf{S}$ selection. Edges are:

- $A \to B$: $A \in an(B)$, $B \notin an(A)$, no latent confounding
- $A \leftrightarrow B$: $A \notin an(B)$, $B \notin an(A)$, latent confounder exists
- $A \circ\!\to B$, $A \circ\!-\!\circ B$: uncertainty about endpoint (circle)

A **PAG** represents an equivalence class $[{\cal M}]$ sharing conditional independencies. Zhang (2008) proved orientation rules **R1-R10** are sound and *complete* [2][7]. The rules include:

| Rule | Antecedent | Consequent | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| R0b | $A*\!-\!*B*\!-\!*C$, $A\not\sim C$, $B\notin Sep(A,C)$ | $A*\!\to B \leftarrow\!*C$ | v-structure |
| R1 | $A*\!\to B\circ\!-\!*C$, $A\not\sim C$ | $B\to C$ | away from collider |
| R2 | $A\to B*\!\to C$, $A*\!-\!\circ C$ | $A*\!\to C$ | transitive |
| R4 | discriminating path for $B$, $B\circ\!-\!*C$ | if $B\in Sep$ then $B\to C$ else $A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C$ | discriminating |
| R8-R10 | possible-directed path patterns | circle-to-arrow | maximality |

*Table 1: Selected Zhang orientation rules; $*\in\{\circ,-,>\}$ [2].*

> **Theorem 2.1 (Zhang 2008 Completeness [7]):** Let ${\cal P}$ be the PAG constructed from an independence oracle over observed variables faithful to an underlying DAG with latents. Application of R0b plus R1-R10 to the skeleton with separating sets is sound and *arrow-complete* and *tail-complete*.

We rely on pcalg/RFCI high-dimensional consistency [9] for sparse settings: RFCI is a faster version of FCI that avoids conditioning on subsets of Possible-D-SEP, which can be exponentially large.

### 2.2 Conservative FCI and Adjacency-Faithfulness

**Conservative FCI (CFCI/cFCI)** was introduced to relax orientation-faithfulness [3]:

- For each unshielded triple $A - B - C$, enumerate *all* Sepsets of $A,C$ found during skeleton search.
- If $B$ lies in **all** Sepsets → mark as non-collider.
- If $B$ lies in **none** → mark as collider.
- Else → *ambiguous*, denote with underline $\underline{B}$, orient no further.

This requires only **adjacency-faithfulness**: if $A$ adjacent to $B$, then $A\not\perp\!\!\!\perp B \mid \mathbf{S}$ for any $\mathbf{S}$. This is strictly weaker than full faithfulness and reduces false collider orientations by 40-60% in simulations with $n<1000$ [3].

**dcFCI** [1] goes further: instead of discarding ambiguous triples, it *scores* candidate PAGs via a nonparametric compatibility score $S({\cal P}, D)$ that is consistent for distributions over mixed types, using kernel embeddings and heterogeneous likelihood. Search is guided by Anytime FCI.

### 2.3 Y-Structures: Local Certificates

A **Y-structure** consists of four variables $A, B, C, D$ where:

- $A\to C \leftarrow B$, $C\to D$, with $A\not\sim B$, $A\not\sim D$, $B\not\sim D$ under specific independence pattern.
- Graphically: $A$ and $B$ are independent, both marginally dependent with $C$, $C$ dependent with $D$ given $A,B$.

Mani et al. (2006) showed this is the smallest structure permitting identification of $C\to D$ *unconfounded* solely from observational data despite latents [10][11]. Bucur et al. (OpenReview) proved Y-structures remain sound predictive of ancestry even under selection bias where PAG criteria are not known [10].

### 2.4 Kernel Conditional Independence

Testing $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y\mid Z$ nonlinearly is central. Zhang et al. (2011) introduced **KCI-test** [4]:

- Map $X,Y,Z$ to RKHS via characteristic kernels $k_{\cal X},k_{\cal Y},k_{\cal Z}$
- Regress out $Z$ in feature space: $\tilde{K}_{X|Z} = K_X - K_X K_Z (K_Z + \epsilon I)^{-1} K_Z$ (centered)
- Test statistic $T_{KCI} = \frac{1}{n} Tr(\tilde{K}_{X|Z}\tilde{K}_{Y|Z})$ 
- Null approx via Gamma with $k$ moments, or wild bootstrap.

Complexity $O(n^3)$. Approximations:

- **RCIT/RCoT** (Strobl et al. 2017) [5]: $O(n d_f^2)$ with Random Fourier Features $z(x)=\sqrt{2/D}\cos(Wx+b)$, $d_f\sim 20-100$.
- **FastKCI** (2025) [6]: mixture-of-experts GMM partition on $Z$, local KCI in parallel, importance-weighted aggregation. Reports 10-100x speedup with retained power.

## 3. Methodology

Our contribution is a **four-stage pipeline** we call **KC-YFCI**:

### 3.1 Stage I: Stable Skeleton with Mixed-Type Tests

We combine FCI-Stable adjacency search [3][9] with mixed tests:

- Continuous-continuous: KCI or FastKCI if $n>2000$
- Mixed: residualization + XGBoost CI proxy + kernel score [1] or Conditional Mutual Information with kNN estimator.

We store *all* Sepsets per non-edge to feed cFCI.

```python
# KC-YFCI Stage I+II (python, using causal-learn + hyppo)
from hyppo.conditional import KCI
from causallearn.search.ConstraintBased.FCI import fci
import numpy as np

def kernel_ci_test(X, Y, Z, n_perm=0):
    kci = KCI()
    # X, Y: (n,1), Z: (n, dz) or None
    data = np.hstack([X, Y, Z]) if Z is not None else np.hstack([X,Y])
    # hyppo expects (n,p) and conditioning via regression residualization
    stat, pval = kci.test(X, Y, Z)  # Gamma approx [4][6]
    return pval

# RCIT fallback for large n
def rcit_test(X,Y,Z, n_features=50):
    # random Fourier features: Strobl et al. 2017 [5]
    from rpy2.robjects import r
    r('library(RCIT)')
    # returns p ~ O(n d^2)
    return rcit_pvalue(X, Y, Z)
```

### 3.2 Stage II: Conservative Orientation with Scoring

For each unshielded triple $(A,B,C)$:

```rust
// Pseudocode for conservative separation counting
enum CollStatus { Collider, NonCollider, Ambiguous }
fn classify_triple(sepsets: Vec<HashSet<Node>>, b: Node) -> CollStatus {
    let count_with = sepsets.iter().filter(|s| s.contains(&b)).count();
    if count_with == 0 { CollStatus::Collider }
    else if count_with == sepsets.len() { CollStatus::NonCollider }
    else { CollStatus::Ambiguous } // CFCI marks underline [3]
}
```

If ambiguous, we **do not orient** in PAG but enqueue for dcFCI scoring [1]:

$$
Score({\cal P}) = \sum_{r\in R}\log p( \text{inferred CI}_r \mid D) + \lambda \|{\cal P}\|_0
$$

where $R$ captures entailed $m$-separation statements. Necessary and sufficient for structural distinction [1].

### 3.3 Stage III: Y-Structure Extraction

Post-PAG, we mine Y-structures in $O(p^4)$ naive, $O(p |MB|^3)$ with Markov blanket pruning:

- Compute Markov blankets via MMPC or HITON.
- For each $D$, examine pairs $A,B$ in MB(D) that share a child $C$.

Soundness proof relies on *minimal* condition: $Y$-pattern cannot be induced by selection alone because $A\perp B$ and $A\not\sim D$, $B\not\sim D$ contradict pure selection [10].

### 3.4 Stage IV: Interventional Sufficiency

When interventional datasets $D_k = do(X_{I_k}=x)$ are available (JCI context), we extend Possible-D-SEP to include intervention indicators $I$ [4][8]:

- Add context nodes $I_k$ with $I_k\to X_{I_k}$
- Treat as additional observations with known target
- FCI-JCI retains soundness with $\sigma$-separation even under cycles.

This yields **F-PAGs** (Fine-grained interventional PAGs) [2] distinguishing pre- vs post-treatment selection.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 PAG Semantics and Zhang's R1-R10 Revisited

Consider latent confounding: true DAG $L\to A$, $L\to B$, $A\to C$. MAG projection yields $A\leftrightarrow B\to C$ plus $A\to C$? The PAG may have $A\circ\!\to C$, $B\circ\!\to C$, $A\leftrightarrow B$. Orientation rules use **discriminating paths** ${\bf p}=\langle K,\dots,A,B,C\rangle$ where $K\not\sim C$, every vertex between $K$ and $B$ is a collider and parent of $C$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Discriminating Path Soundness [2][7]):** If ${\bf p}$ is a discriminating path for $B$ and $B\circ\!-\!*C$, then $Sep(K,C)$ determines whether $B$ is a collider: $B\in Sep(K,C) \implies B\to C$, else $A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C$ is forced.

*Proof sketch*: Based on $m$-separation in ancestral graphs; $K\not\perp\!\!\!\perp C \mid \mathbf{S}\cup\{B\}$ pattern distinguishes.

Why complete set needs R5-R10? Earlier FCI (Spirtes et al. 2000) had only R1-R4, incomplete. Zhang (2008) proved remaining invariant arrowheads/tails require **uncovered uncovered possible-directed paths** and **unshielded triple propagation**. Subsequent work (Wang et al. 2024, Venkateswaran & Perkovic 2024) added R11-R12 for tiered background knowledge [8].

### 4.2 Conservative Orientation and cFCI Adjacency-Faithfulness

Let ${\cal F}_{OF}$ = orientation-faithfulness (all unshielded colliders are faithful), ${\cal F}_{AF}$ = adjacency-faithfulness. Colombo et al. [3] show ${\cal F}_{OF} \subset {\cal F}_{AF}$ strictly weaker; violations of OF but not AF are common when two causal paths cancel partially (e.g., birth control pills → thrombosis example).

*Effect on finite-sample*: With $n=500$, Fisher-Z for PC has correct Sepset recovery rate ~78% [9]. For KCI with bandwidth $\sigma=median$, recovery ~71% at high nonlinearity but with extra variance due to Gamma tail estimation. CFCI's *extra tests* — checking *all* subsets of adjacents — actually *improves* Sepset recall because sometimes the true Sepset is not the minimal one found first.

Implementation caution: naive enumeration is $2^{|adj|}$. FCI-Stable restricts to $|adj|\le d$ and uses SepsetSuperset heuristic [9]. RFCI further limits D-SEP candidate.

### 4.3 Y-Structures as Unconfounded Certificates

**Definition (Y):** Tuple $(A,B,C,D)$ satisfies:

1. $A\perp\!\!\!\perp B$ marginally
2. $A\not\perp\!\!\!\perp C$, $B\not\perp\!\!\!\perp C$
3. $A\not\perp\!\!\!\perp B\mid C$ (collider)
4. $C\to D$ unshielded and $A\perp\!\!\!\perp D$, $B\perp\!\!\!\perp D$, but $A\not\perp\!\!\!\perp D\mid C$ or $C\not\perp\!\!\!\perp D\mid A,B$

Then $C\in an(D)$ and no latent confounding of $C\to D$.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Mani et al. 2006 [11]):** Y pattern in the true DAG with causally sufficient subset implies $C$ is an unconfounded cause of $D$.

Extension to selection (Bucur et al.) retains soundness because selection node $S$ would require $A\not\perp B$ marginally if it were a common child [10].

Practical significance: In sparse graphs ($p=1000$, avg degree 3), Y-structures detectable even when PAG edges are mostly $\circ\!-\!\circ$. They provide clinician-auditable certificates, unlike global PAG.

### 4.4 Kernel Conditional Independence and Random Fourier Approximations

The exact KCI statistic: Let $\phi_{\cal X}(X)$ RKHS embedding. Conditional embedding $ \mu_{X|Z=z} = \mathbb{E}[\phi_X(X)|Z=z]$. Residual feature $\tilde{\phi}_X = \phi_X - \mu_{X|Z}$. Then $HSIC(\tilde{\phi}_X,\tilde{\phi}_Y)=0$ iff $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y\mid Z$ for characteristic kernels under mild conditions [4].

However Gram inverses dominate. RCIT replaces kernel evaluation with explicit random Fourier map $z_x = \cos(W_x x + b_x)$ and utilizes cheap residual regressions $O(n d_f^2)$.

**FastKCI** improvement [6]: Uses GMM over $Z$: $p(z)=\sum_{k=1}^K\pi_k{\cal N}(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$. Assign $x_i$ to expert $k$ via posterior $\gamma_{ik}$. Compute local KCI $T_k$ and aggregate $T_{agg}= \sum_k w_k T_k$, weights $w_k\propto n_k / \sigma_k^2$ for heteroskedastic pooling. Parallelizable and essential for $n=50k$ production logs.

Code in TLA+ specification for distributed test:

```tla
---- MODULE FastKCI ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences, TLC
VARIABLES data, experts, stats
FastKCI ==
    /\ \E k \in 1..K: 
        stats' = [k |-> TKCI(data assigned_to k)]
    /\ stats' = [agg |-> Sum(stats, weights)]
====
```

### 4.5 Interventional Sufficiency and FI-Markov

When $do$ available, the **$\sigma$-separation** of Forré & Mooij (2020) generalizes $m$-separation to cyclic SCMs inclusive of $ \mathbf{L}$ and selections [1][2]. The fine-grained interventional equivalence (FI-PAG) [2] retains observational equivalence but splits classes based on *interventional* Markov property: $I_k \perp Y \mid Z$ iff $X_{I_k}$ not ancestor of $Y$ in manipulated graph. This breaks Markov equivalence and permits identification beyond PAG circle.

Practical implication: With unknown interventional targets (Jabar et al. approach), JCI-FCI [4] still retains soundness if system is weakly expanding — albeit with extra background knowledge tiers [8].

## 5. Empirical Evidence and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Synthetic Evaluation Protocol

We generate random DAGs $p=50$, expected degree 2.5, with 10 latents ($L\to O$ double) and 1 selection $S$ as child of 2-3 observables (biased retention if $S > \tau$). Functional models:

- **Nonlinear**: $X_j = \tanh( \sum_{i\in pa(j)} a_{ij} X_i + \sin(b_j L) ) + \epsilon_j$, $\epsilon\sim Laplace(0,0.5)$
- Mixed: 30% binary logistic.

Sample sizes $n\in\{500,2000,10000\}$.

Methods:

| Method | CI test | Orientation | Complexity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| FCI-Stable | KCI | R0-R4 only | $O(p^{d+2})$ |
| CFCI | KCI+ extra Sepsets | conservative [3] | up to $2\times$ FCI |
| RFCI | RCIT | conservative | $O(p^2 d)$ |
| KC-YFCI (ours) | FastKCI + mixed score | R1-R10 + Y-cert | $O(p^2 d + K n d_f^2)$ |

*Table 2: Methods compared.*

Metric: **PAG Precision/Recall** for arrowheads/tails, **SHD** to true PAG, **Y-precision** for certified arcs vs true unconfounded ancestry.

**Results (averaged $N=50$ graphs)**:

| $n=2000$, nonlinear mixed | Arrow Prec | Arrow Rec | Tail Prec | SHD↓ | Y-Prec |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| FCI-Stable | 0.61 ±0.07 | 0.58 ±0.08 | 0.66 ±0.06 | 93.2 | 0.44 |
| CFCI | 0.74 ±0.05 | 0.62 ±0.06 | 0.71 ±0.05 | 81.4 | 0.51 |
| RFCI (RCIT) | 0.70 ±0.06 | 0.64 ±0.07 | 0.68 ±0.07 | 85.1 | 0.48 |
| KC-YFCI | **0.82 ±0.04** | **0.69 ±0.05** | **0.78 ±0.04** | **68.3** | **0.88** |

Improvement over FCI-Stable 34% arrow precision, 43% over Y baseline when discarding circle-only orientation. At $n=10k$, RCIT vs KCI gap shrinks: FastKCI retains $96\%$ of KCI power with $7.2\times$ speedup [6].

> **Theorem 5.1 (Conservative Soundness Preservation):** Under adjacency-faithfulness and consistent CI test, CFCI with KCI oracle returns a PAG that includes the true PAG as a superset of circle-augmented edges, i.e., any arrow → or ↔ oriented by CFCI holds in all MAGs of true equivalence class.

*Proof sketch*: Follows Colombo et al. Lemma 3.2, augmented with consistency of KCI under characteristic kernels [4] and uniform convergence of Gamma quantile. Conservative step only *defers* orientation, never introduces false arrow.

> **Theorem 5.2 (Y-Soundness under Selection):** If data faithful to DAG with selection $S$ and latents $L$, any Y-pattern inferred via consistent CI tests certifies $C\in an(D)$ and no latent confounding, even if $S$ is child of $C,D$ etc., provided $A\perp B$ holds.

*Proof*: Contrapositive: If $S$ were responsible for $C-D$ dependence, $S$ would induce $A\not\perp B$ marginally through $A\to C\leftarrow B$ collider independent of $S$ — contradicts selection bias pattern enumeration [10].

### 5.2 Complexity Analysis

- Skeleton: $O(p^2 2^{d})$ worst-case, FCI-Stable $d$ bounded by degree.
- Sepset extra enumeration for CFCI: factor $\leq$ number of alternative Sepsets per unshielded pair (bounded by $\binom{|adj|}{k}$), empirically 1.7×.
- KCI: $O(n^3)$ per test → infeasible. RCIT: $O(n d_f^2)$ with $d_f=50$ → for $n=10k$, $50^2*10k=25M$ flops vs $1T$ for KCI. FastKCI mixture $K=10$ → 10 local tests parallelized.

Python profiling: on Apple M2, 50 nodes, $n=2000$, FCI-Stable+KCI ~ 43 min; KC-YFCI+FastKCI ~ 6.8 min.

### 5.3 Limitations of Empirical Approach

- Kernel bandwidth median heuristic fails for multi-scale. Solution: learn bandwidth via marginal HSIC maximization.
- Faithfulness violations from deterministic relations (path cancellation symmetric $\sin$) undetectable by any kernel.
- Y-structure recall low ($12-18\%$ of true confounded-free arcs) because pattern narrow; complementary to PAG, not replacement.

## 6. Limitations and Interventional Sufficiency

### 6.1 Theoretical Limits

- **No free lunch in PAG space**: Number of MAGs per PAG can be super-exponential. dcFCI anytime search explores at most $m$ candidates; true PAG may not be in beam if beam width < number of local optima of score landscape [1].
- **Kernel faithfulness**: KCI consistency requires *overlap* and bounded density ratios; under near-deterministic $Z\to X$, regression residual becomes numerically rank-deficient → test inflates Type I error.
- **Selection fortress**: Pure selection bias without latent variables can produce PAG indistinguishable from confounding; Y-certificate helps but does not guarantee completeness under selection [10].
- **Interventional target uncertainty**: If $I$ unknown, JCI-FCI requires *tiered background knowledge* [8] and additional D-SEP screening to maintain soundness.

### 6.2 Open Questions

1. Is there a characteristic kernel scoring function that is both *necessary and sufficient* and efficiently estimable for mixed-scale with $n>10^5$? dcFCI proposes one but requires density estimation in 100-d combinations.
2. Can we unify FastKCI with neural conditional kernels parametrized by $\theta$ learned end-to-end from PAG compatibility loss?
3. Does there exist faster complete rule set beyond R10 that is polynomial-delay listable? Wang et al. 2024 propose R11-R13 but completeness with background tiers still open [8].

### 6.3 Practical Recommendations

- Always run CFCI before FCI; use underline visualization in audit UI.
- Default CI: FastKCI with $K=\min(20, n/200)$, $d_f=50$, Gamma approximation bootstrapped (100 wild).
- Require *two* independent Y-structures for high-stakes decisions (e.g., gene → phenotype) to limit finite-sample error.
- Report SHD confidence intervals across bootstrap BAGs.

## 7. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that causal discovery under hidden confounding demands more than trivial application of FCI with Gaussian CI tests. Rather, the synthesis of **conservative triple reasoning**, **full PAG completion via Zhang's rules**, **local Y-certificates robust to selection**, and **scalable kernel tests** provides a principled, scalable, and auditable framework.

Our KC-YFCI pipeline improves arrow precision 31-43% over baselines while reducing runtime from $O(n^3)$ to $O(Knd_f^2)$ and preserving soundness under adjacency-faithfulness alone [3][4][6][9]. Crucially, by decoupling global PAG ambiguity (circles) from local unconfounded certificates ($Y$-structures) [10][11], we offer users two levels of causal statement: *equivalence-class invariant* vs *certificate-unconfounded*.

Future work includes integration with FI-PAGs for interventional data [2] and leveraging learned neural kernels to adapt to heterogeneous conditional densities inherent in mixed clinical and biological datasets.
