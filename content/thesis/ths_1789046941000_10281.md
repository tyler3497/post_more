# Isogeny-Based Cryptography after the SIDH Break: SQIsign, CSIDH Group Actions, and the Search for Post-Quantum Signatures from Elliptic-Curve Isogenies

## Abstract

The July 2022 key-recovery attack on SIDH by Castryck and Decru — generalized within weeks by Maino–Martindale and Robert into a provably polynomial-time algorithm — destroyed one of the most compact post-quantum key-exchange proposals and threw isogeny-based cryptography into its deepest crisis [2][3][7]. Yet the attack's mathematics, which reduces the SIDH key-recovery problem to evaluating *higher-dimensional* isogenies between abelian surfaces via a 1997 theorem of Kani, simultaneously handed isogeny cryptographers their most powerful new constructive tool [2][7]. This thesis develops an account of the post-SIDH landscape. We formalize the auxiliary-point weakness that enabled the attack, survey the Deuring correspondence and endomorphism-ring hardness assumptions that survive it, analyze the SQIsign signature scheme — the sole isogeny candidate in NIST's signature process — including its KLPT-based signing pipeline, 2D acceleration techniques, and compactness (64–128 byte keys, 177–335 byte signatures) [5][9], and evaluate the commutative class-group action of CSIDH, whose security is orthogonal to the SIDH auxiliary-input structure [11][13]. We close with a taxonomy of the new generation of higher-dimensional constructions (SQIsignHD, FESTA, QFESTA) that convert the attacker's weapon into the signer's instrument.

## 1 Introduction

Isogeny-based cryptography was the minimalist hope of post-quantum cryptography: public keys in hundreds of bytes, deep arithmetic geometry behind concrete protocols, and hardness — *finding an isogeny between two supersingular elliptic curves* — resisting classical and quantum attack alike [1]. SIDH [1] and its KEM SIKE reached round 4 of the NIST PQC process and, in 2022, were widely expected to survive.

That expectation died in about an hour of single-core computation. On July 30, 2022, Castryck and Decru published *An efficient key recovery attack on SIDH* with Magma code recovering the Microsoft $IKEp217 challenge in ~6 minutes and SIKEp434 — believed to meet NIST level 1 — in ~62 minutes on a 2013 Intel Xeon core [3]. The maneuver was a *dimension jump*: SIDH's secret 1-dimensional isogeny was reconstructed from a *2-dimensional* isogeny between abelian surfaces, exploiting the auxiliary torsion-point images in SIDH's public transcript [2][7]. Within weeks Maino–Martindale extended it to arbitrary starting curves [5][7], and Robert gave a provably polynomial-time variant [6][7].

> **Thesis statement.** *The SIDH break was not a failure of the isogeny path problem but of the auxiliary-input protocol design. The same higher-dimensional machinery that destroyed SIDH now constitutes the fastest-growing constructive tool in isogeny cryptography, and the field's future belongs to schemes — SQIsign, CSIDH, and their higher-dimensional descendants — whose hardness assumptions do not expose torsion images of secret isogenies.*

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the arithmetic background: supersingular elliptic curves, isogeny graphs, the Deuring correspondence, and the class-group action. Section 3 describes our methodology for reconstructing and benchmarking the post-SIDH landscape. Section 4 contains the deep technical analysis in five subsections: the attack itself; the surviving hardness assumptions; SQIsign's construction; CSIDH's group action; and the new higher-dimensional signature and encryption schemes. Section 5 gives empirical performance figures and security-level arguments. Section 6 states the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Supersingular elliptic curves and isogeny graphs

Let *p* be a large prime and *E* an elliptic curve over the quadratic extension field 𝔽\_{p²}. *E* is **supersingular** when its endomorphism ring End(*E*) is a maximal order in the quaternion algebra *B*\_{p,∞} ramified at *p* and infinity, rather than an imaginary quadratic order [10]. An **isogeny** φ: *E* → *E′* is a non-constant rational map preserving the identity; its degree deg(φ) equals the size of its kernel for separable isogenies. The **supersingular ℓ-isogeny graph** has vertices the *j*-invariants of supersingular curves over 𝔽\_{p²} (roughly *p*/12 vertices) and edges the ℓ-isogenies between them; it is a regular Ramanujan expander graph, which makes random walks mix rapidly and underlies the pseudorandomness of isogeny-based constructions [1][10].

The foundational hardness assumption is the **supersingular isogeny path problem**: given two supersingular curves *E*₀ and *E*₁, find an isogeny φ: *E*₀ → *E*₁. The best known classical attack is the meet-in-the-middle algorithm of Delfs–Galbraith running in Õ(√*p*) time [6][10]; the best quantum attack is Tani's claw-finding algorithm at Õ(*p*^{1/4}). Neither was touched by the 2022 attacks — a fact of decisive importance [4][5].

### 2.2 SIDH and its auxiliary-point structure

SIDH [1] fixes *p* = 2^a·3^b·*f* ± 1 and *E*₀; Alice's secret is a cyclic 2^a-isogeny with kernel ⟨P_A + [s_A]Q_A⟩ on the public basis of *E*₀[2^a], Bob's a 3^b-isogeny. Each party publishes their image curve *and* the *images of the other party's torsion basis* under their secret isogeny: Alice sends φ_A(P_B), φ_A(Q_B) [1][2]. This auxiliary data was considered necessary for the exchange to complete — and it is precisely what the 2022 attacks consumed [2][7].

### 2.3 The Deuring correspondence

Deuring's theorem establishes a category equivalence between supersingular elliptic curves over 𝔽̅_p (up to isogeny) and maximal orders in *B*\_{p,∞} (up to ideal classes) [8][10]:

> **Theorem (Deuring correspondence).** *There is a bijection between isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves over* 𝔽̅\_p *and conjugacy classes of maximal orders in the quaternion algebra B\_{p,∞} ramified at p and ∞. Under this bijection, isogenies E → E′ correspond to left ideals of the associated orders, with deg(φ) = nrd(I\_φ).*

The *effective* Deuring correspondence — translating between ideals and isogenies algorithmically — is the engine of SQIsign [8][9]. The **endomorphism ring problem** (compute End(*E*) given *E*) is believed hard and is the security anchor of every surviving isogeny signature [5][10].

### 2.4 The commutative class-group action

For curves *E* defined over 𝔽_p itself (rather than 𝔽\_{p²}), the subring of 𝔽_p-rational endomorphisms is commutative: an imaginary quadratic order 𝒪 [13]. The ideal class group cl(𝒪) then acts *freely and transitively* on the set of 𝔽_p-isomorphism classes of such curves — a **hard homogeneous space** in the sense of Couveignes and Rostovtsev–Stolbunov [11][13]. CSIDH instantiates this with supersingular Montgomery curves *E_A*: *y*² = *x*³ + *Ax*² + *x* over 𝔽_p, with *p* = 4ℓ₁…ℓ_n − 1, and private keys are exponent vectors (*e*₁,…,*e_n*) acting via the ideals 𝔩_i above ℓ_i [11].

## 3 Methodology

We decompose each protocol into (i) its hardness problem, (ii) the exact public data it exposes, and (iii) the algebraic structure of the secret, asking whether the public data can reconstruct the secret via higher-dimensional isogeny interpolation [4][7]. Concretely: (1) reconstruct the Castryck–Decru [2] and Maino–Martindale [5] attack pipelines from published descriptions — auxiliary isogeny γ of degree *c* = *b* − *a*, Kani's lemma embedding into a (*b*,*b*)-isogeny, kernel recovery [4]; (2) classify assumptions by survival — public interfaces exposing only curves versus torsion images of secret isogenies [5]; (3) analyze SQIsign's KLPT pipeline and reported speedups [9]; (4) evaluate CSIDH against revised quantum-security estimates [13]. All parameters, timings, and sizes below are from the cited primary literature; no new implementations are claimed.

```haskell
-- The assumption-survival classifier used throughout this thesis.
-- Each scheme is reduced to (problem, publicInterface, secretStructure).
data Scheme = Scheme
  { hardnessProblem :: Problem
  , publicData     :: [PublicDatum]
  , secret         :: SecretObject
  }

exposesTorsionImages :: Scheme -> Bool
exposesTorsionImages = any isTorsionImage . publicData

survives2022 :: Scheme -> Verdict
survives2022 s
  | exposesTorsionImages s = Broken   -- SIDH, SIKE, B-SIDH [2][3][5]
  | otherwise              = Intact   -- SQIsign, CSIDH, CGL hash [5][11]
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Castryck–Decru–Maino–Martindale key-recovery machinery

The attack begins where SIDH's security argument ended: the adversary knows *E*₀, *E_B* = *E*₀/ker(φ_B), and the images φ_B(P_A), φ_B(Q_A) of Alice's torsion basis [2][7]. The key insight is that this data suffices to embed the secret isogeny φ_B into a higher-dimensional object rigid enough to betray it [2].

**Step 1 — the auxiliary isogeny.** Given deg(φ_B) = *a* and a smooth *b* > *a* with *c* = *b* − *a* small, the attacker builds an auxiliary isogeny γ: *E*₀ → *C* of degree *c* evaluable on the *b*-torsion; the refined Maino–Martindale–Panny–Pope analysis costs essentially *one* (*b*,*b*)-isogeny evaluation plus two γ evaluations [4][5].

**Step 2 — Kani's lemma.** The mathematical heart is a 1997 theorem of Ernst Kani on genus-2 curves with elliptic differentials [2]. Kani's lemma states, in the form used by the attack, that given isogenies φ: *E*₀ → *E* of degree *a* and γ: *E*₀ → *C* of degree *c* with *a* + *c* = *b*, there exists a (*b*,*b*)-isogeny

> **Theorem (Kani's lemma, attack form).** *Let φ: E₀ → E have degree a and γ: E₀ → C have degree c with a + c = b. Then the matrix* [[φ, γ̃],[−γ, φ̃]] *defines a (b,b)-isogeny F: E₀ × C → E × C′ on the product abelian surface, where φ̃, γ̃ are the dual isogenies and C′ is determined by the construction [2][7].*

Because the SIDH public data determines the action of φ_B on the *b*-torsion (via the published images of the torsion basis), the attacker can *evaluate* the (*b*,*b*)-isogeny *F* on enough points to recover its kernel — and the kernel of *F* encodes ker(φ_B) [4][5]. Robert's refinement made this provably polynomial-time, eliminating the heuristics in the parameter-tweaking step [6][7].

**Step 3 — kernel extraction.** From *F* the attacker recovers a generator of ker(φ_B), hence the shared secret [4]. The concrete numbers are devastating: the Magma implementation broke the $50,000 Microsoft SIKE challenge for the 217-bit prime in about 5 minutes, SIKEp434 in about an hour, and SIKEp751 in about 20 hours — all on a *single core* [3][7]. Later optimizations reduced even the largest parameters to under an hour [7].

| Target | Classical belief (pre-2022) | Attack time (single core) |
|---|---|---|
| $IKEp217 (217-bit) | ~2^{108} classical | ~5–6 minutes [3] |
| SIKEp434 (NIST level 1) | 2^{128} quantum / 2^{143} classical | ~62 minutes [3] |
| SIKEp751 (NIST level 5) | 2^{256} quantum | ~20 hours, later < 1 hour [7] |

David Jao, co-inventor of SIDH, confirmed the verdict plainly: the work "breaks SIKE," specifically the SIDH problem on which it rests — while noting that schemes "not based on SIDH, such as CSIDH and SQIsign, are not directly affected" [3]. B-SIDH, which shares SIDH's auxiliary structure, fell as well [3][5].

### 4.2 The Deuring correspondence and the surviving hardness assumptions

The attack's precision is also its limitation: it recovers *one specific* isogeny whose action on a large torsion subgroup is published. The generic problems — *find any isogeny between two given curves* (the isogeny path problem) and *compute the endomorphism ring of a given curve* — were never implicated [4][5]. Under the Deuring correspondence, these problems translate to quaternion-algebra problems:

1. **Endomorphism-ring problem.** Given *E*, compute a ℤ-basis of End(*E*), i.e. a maximal order 𝒪 ⊂ *B*\_{p,∞} with *E* ↔ 𝒪 [8][10]. *Status: intact* [5][10].
2. **Quaternion path / ideal problem.** Given two maximal orders (or an order and an ideal-class target), find a connecting ideal of prescribed norm [8]. This is the quaternionic avatar of isogeny path-finding. *Status: intact.*
3. **Class-group vectorization (CSIDH).** Given *E*, *E′* = [𝔞]⋆*E* in the CSIDH group action, recover the ideal class [𝔞] [11]. Reduces to a hidden-shift problem; Kuperberg's quantum algorithm gives subexponential but still superpolynomial cost, and recent analyses revised concrete estimates downward without breaking the scheme [13]. *Status: intact but with reduced margins.*

The pattern is clear: every surviving assumption is one whose public interface exposes *only curves* (or *j*-invariants), never torsion images of secret isogenies [5].

### 4.3 SQIsign: compact signatures from quaternions and isogenies

SQIsign (*Short Quaternion and Isogeny Signature*, ASIACRYPT 2020 [8]) is the **sole isogeny candidate** in NIST's additional-signature process [5][9]. Its assumption hygiene is exemplary: the secret is a trapdoor (a large-smooth-degree endomorphism, equivalently a secret ideal), the public key a single curve *E_A*, the signature a Fiat–Shamir non-interactive proof of knowledge of the secret endomorphism [8][10].

**The KLPT engine.** Signing requires producing an isogeny σ: *E*₁ → *E*₂ of *prescribed smooth degree* whose kernel encodes the challenge — the task solved by the Kohel–Lauter–Petit–Tignol (KLPT) algorithm operating in the quaternion algebra [8][9]. KLPT takes an input ideal *I* and outputs an equivalent ideal *J* ∼ *I* of smooth norm *D*, translated by the effective Deuring correspondence into an isogeny of degree *D*; improved prime-power-norm translation (Eurocrypt 2023) plus optimized 𝔽\_{p²} arithmetic cut signing cost by 3–4× [9].

**Sizes.** SQIsign achieves the smallest artifacts of any post-quantum signature proposal [9]:

| NIST level | Public key | Signature | Sign (orig. C impl.) | Verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 64 bytes | 177 bytes | ~2.5 s | ~50 ms [8] |
| III | 96 bytes | ~247 bytes | slower | ~100 ms [5] |
| V | 128 bytes | 335 bytes | slowest | ~200 ms [5] |

The AVX-512 vectorized implementation (2026) reports 2.69× faster signing and 3.18× faster verification at level I when combined with Qlapoti techniques [9]. The compactness shortens TLS handshakes and X.509 chains, motivating IoT-oriented profiles [9].

**The 2D turn.** In a reversal, the 2D isogenies that killed SIDH now accelerate SQIsign (SQIsignHD, SQIsign2D-West, SQIsign2DPush use (2,2)-isogenies for signing/verification [5][12]); one caveat: SQIsign2D-East suffers a specific vulnerability, while the main scheme has no known attacks [5].

```python
# Sketch of the SQIsign signing pipeline (De Feo et al., ASIACRYPT 2020) [8].
# Secret: endomorphism tau on E_A of smooth degree T (the trapdoor).
def sqisign_sign(sk, msg):
    E1, psi = commit(sk)          # random commitment isogeny psi: E_A -> E1
    c = H(E1, msg)                # Fiat-Shamir challenge -> cyclic subgroup
    # KLPT: find equivalent ideal of smooth norm encoding the challenge
    J = KLPT(sk.ideal, target_norm=smooth_bound(c))
    sigma = ideal_to_isogeny(J)   # effective Deuring correspondence [9]
    assert sigma.domain() == E1
    return (E1, sigma)            # verifier checks deg, domain, challenge
```

### 4.4 CSIDH and the commutative group action

CSIDH (ASIACRYPT 2018 [11]) applies the Couveignes–Rostovtsev–Stolbunov framework to *supersingular* curves over 𝔽_p. Restricting to 𝔽_p-rational endomorphisms collapses End to a commutative order whose class group acts simply transitively — a non-interactive DH analogue with 64-byte public keys at NIST level I and cheap key validation [11][13].

Why did CSIDH survive 2022? Its public data is a single Montgomery coefficient *A* ∈ 𝔽_p; no torsion-point images are ever transmitted, so Kani's lemma has nothing to grip [3][5]. Its challenges are instead *efficiency* (the highest latency among isogeny key exchanges [13]) and *margin erosion*: sublinear-Vélu (SQALE) and refined hidden-shift analyses revised quantum security estimates downward, prompting larger parameters [13]. CSI-FiSh (Beullens–Kleinjung–Vercauteren, ASIACRYPT 2019) answered the performance problem for signatures by precomputing the class-group structure for a fixed 512-bit prime, at the cost of a trusted precomputation [6].

```tla+
---- MODULE CSIDH ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS L            \* small odd primes l_1..l_n, p = 4*PRODUCT(L) - 1
VARIABLES curve,      \* Montgomery coefficient A in F_p
          priv        \* exponent vector (e_1..e_n), e_i in [-m, m]
GroupAction(a, e) ==  \* [l_1]^e_1 ... [l_n]^e_n star A
  FoldStar(a, e, L)
Safety == [](
  /\ curve \in FpCoefficients
  /\ \A i \in 1..n : priv[i] \in -M..M
  =>  GroupAction(curve, priv) \in FpCoefficients)
\* Key agreement: commutativity of cl(O) gives
\*   [a] star ([b] star E0) = [b] star ([a] star E0).  [11]
====
```

### 4.5 Higher-dimensional isogenies: from attack vector to design tool

The deepest irony of the post-SIDH era is that the attack's core primitive — evaluating (*d*,*d*)-isogenies between abelian surfaces — has become a *constructive* primitive. Laflamme's 2024 general isogeny-interpolation implementation, built to complete the attack for arbitrary base primes, explicitly notes that the technique "now has constructive applications as well as destructive applications" [4]. The new generation includes:

1. **SQIsignHD and binSIDH** use dimension-2-native signing/verification with primes *p* = *ABCf* − 1 [12].
2. **FESTA/QFESTA** reuse SIDH-style torsion structure where the leaked torsion is provably insufficient for Kani lifting [12].
3. **Threshold and adaptor signatures.** Robert's polynomial-time isogeny evaluation became a building block for threshold schemes combining higher-dimensional isogenies with error-correcting codes [6].
4. **OSIDH** (Colò–Kohel) uses orientations by imaginary quadratic orders to obtain a class-group action on an arbitrarily large subset of supersingular curves [14].

> **Theorem (informal, post-SIDH design rule).** *An isogeny protocol is plausibly immune to the Castryck–Decru–Maino–Martindale–Robert family iff its public transcript reveals no isogeny evaluated on a torsion subgroup large enough to determine the secret kernel via Kani lifting. Protocols satisfying this condition include SQIsign, CSIDH, and the 2D-native schemes; protocols violating it include SIDH, SIKE, and B-SIDH [3][5][7].*

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We consolidate the concrete, published figures that define the post-SIDH trade space.

**Attack economics (classical).** The Castryck–Decru Magma code is the relevant benchmark: $IKEp217 in ~5–6 minutes, SIKEp434 in ~62 minutes, SIKEp751 in ~20 hours on one 2013-era Xeon core, with later improvements compressing even SIKEp751 below one hour [3][7]. Robert's variant is asymptotically polynomial-time, removing the heuristic parameter search [6]. Maino–Martindale needs only a starting curve with known endomorphism ring [5][7].

**Signature compactness (SQIsign).** Public keys of 64/96/128 bytes and signatures of 177–335 bytes across NIST levels I/III/V make SQIsign the most compact post-quantum signature known [5][9]; the 2026 AVX-512 implementation achieves 2.69× signing and 3.18× verification speedups over the reference at level I [9]. NIST's second-round status report highlights SQIsign as the unique isogeny candidate advanced to Round 2 of the additional-signatures process [9].

**Key-exchange compactness (CSIDH).** 64-byte public keys at NIST level I with inexpensive public-key validation — a property most lattice and code schemes cannot match — at the price of the highest latency among isogeny key exchanges [11][13].

**Security reductions.** SQIsign's unforgeability reduces to the endomorphism-ring problem and a quaternion one-more-isogeny assumption in the random-oracle model [8][9]; CSIDH reduces to the class-group vectorization problem, with quantum security from hidden-shift hardness [11][13]. Neither reduction was invalidated by the 2022 attacks, since neither exposes the torsion-image oracle they require [3][5].

## 6 Limitations

This account has several boundaries that must be stated plainly.

1. **No new cryptanalysis is presented.** Our classification of surviving versus broken assumptions follows the published literature [2][3][4][5][6][7]; we independently verified the logical structure of the attack pipeline but executed no implementations.
2. **SQIsign's performance remains its weakness.** Even after 2–4× speedups, signing takes fractions of a second to seconds — far slower than ML-DSA — and side-channel-hardened constant-time implementations, which NIST's call explicitly values, remain an active research area [9].
3. **CSIDH's quantum margin is debated.** The hidden-shift quantum attack is subexponential; concrete estimates have been revised downward more than once, and conservative deployments now use larger primes (e.g., CSIDH-1024/1792) whose performance cost is significant [13].
4. **Higher-dimensional schemes are young**: thin cryptanalytic histories versus lattice finalists, and the SQIsign2D-East vulnerability warns the 2D design space contains traps [5][12].
5. **The endomorphism-ring assumption is less studied than LWE.** Isogeny cryptanalysis as a field is smaller than lattice cryptanalysis; "no known attack" carries less evidentiary weight here than in the lattice setting [10].

## 7 Conclusion

The SIDH break of 2022 was the most instructive failure in the history of post-quantum cryptography. It showed a protocol can fall not because its problem is easy — the isogeny path and endomorphism-ring problems remain unbroken [4][5] — but because its *public interface* leaks a structured evaluation of the secret that a change of dimension can invert [2][7]. Its ingredients — Kani's 1997 theorem, abelian surfaces, (*b*,*b*)-isogenies — were classical mathematics; the novelty was recognizing SIDH's auxiliary torsion points as a homing signal [2].

The field's response has been exemplary. Within four years, the weapon was converted into a tool: 2D isogenies now accelerate SQIsign, enable new signature and encryption designs, and power threshold constructions [4][6][9][12]. SQIsign stands as the most compact post-quantum signature known and the only isogeny candidate in NIST's additional-signature process [5][9]; CSIDH preserves the dream of 64-byte post-quantum key exchange on a commutative group action [11][13]. The design rule the episode teaches is now canonical: *never publish the image of a secret isogeny on torsion you do not control*. So long as that rule is honored, isogeny-based cryptography's future — written in quaternion ideals, class groups, and higher-dimensional abelian varieties — remains intact.
