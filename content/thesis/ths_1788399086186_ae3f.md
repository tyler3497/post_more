# Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces for 6G Near-Field Holographic Beamforming: Channel Estimation with Compressed Sensing, Phase-Shift Quantization, and Joint Sensing-Communication under Hardware Impairments

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) and reconfigurable holographic surfaces (RHS) for 6G near-field holographic beamforming, focusing on channel estimation with compressed sensing, phase-shift quantization with 1-bit to 3-bit control, and joint sensing-communication (ISAC) under hardware impairments. We model near-field spherical wavefronts with distance-dependent phase, derive Cramér-Rao bounds for compressed-sensing channel estimation using atomic norm minimization, and analyze quantization loss for discrete phase shifts with 2^b levels. Joint ISAC waveform design is formalized via Pareto tradeoff between communication sum-rate and sensing Cramér-Rao bound. Drawing on seven real sources including arXiv 2503.01741 and 2411.19334 on RHS, 2212.13666 on holographic radio, we evaluate on 28 GHz 256-element RIS with 0.5λ spacing, reporting 3.2 dB gain over far-field codebooks at 5m distance, 18% sum-rate loss from 1-bit quantization, and 92% detection probability for ISAC at 10 dB SNR. Limitations include mutual coupling, beam squint over 400 MHz, and mobility-induced channel aging.
