# Detonation Theory: The ZND Model, Chapman–Jouguet Theory, and Cellular Structure of Gaseous Detonations

## Abstract

Detonation is the most violent mode of gas-phase combustion: a supersonic shock wave coupled to exothermic reaction, propagating at kilometers per second with peak pressures tens of times ambient. This article reconstructs the three pillars of the classical theory. First, the Chapman–Jouguet thermodynamic theory, which selects a unique wave velocity from a continuum of conservation-law solutions by requiring the Rayleigh line to be tangent to the equilibrium Hugoniot, yielding the sonic condition at the wave's trailing edge. Second, the Zel'dovich–von Neumann–Döring (ZND) model, which resolves the wave's anatomy — a leading inert shock, the von Neumann pressure spike, an induction zone of radical buildup, and a finite-rate reaction zone terminating at the CJ sonic plane. Third, the modern cellular picture, in which transverse shocks, Mach stems, and triple points organize the front into a self-sustaining lattice whose fish-scale imprint on smoked foils reveals a characteristic cell width. We derive the jump relations, connect one-dimensional theory to multidimensional reality via induction-length and cell-size scalings, survey a century of empirical validation, and close with limitations and open problems.

## 1 Introduction

A reactive gas mixture admits two self-sustaining combustion waves. A *deflagration* creeps forward at centimeters to meters per second, pushed by heat conduction and species diffusion, with pressure nearly unchanged across it. A *detonation* slams forward at one to three kilometers per second — supersonic in the unburned gas — with pressure jumping by one to two orders of magnitude. The distinction is not one of degree but of mechanism: the detonation is a shock wave that ignites the gas it compresses, and the gas it ignites sustains the shock [2].

The first quantitative theory was thermodynamic. Chapman (1899) argued that among the continuum of wave speeds permitted by conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, nature selects the minimum-speed solution; Jouguet (1905–1906) showed that this minimum coincides with a minimum of entropy along the reaction Hugoniot and with a sonic condition behind the wave [6][8]. For four decades the Chapman–Jouguet (CJ) theory stood essentially alone, predicting detonation velocities to within a few percent of measurement while saying nothing about the wave's interior.

The Second World War changed that. Between 1940 and 1943, Zel'dovich, von Neumann, and Döring independently constructed a one-dimensional steady model with a finite-rate reaction zone: the ZND model [1]. A thin inert shock compresses the gas to the von Neumann spike; chemistry then runs to completion in a reaction zone whose end is the CJ sonic plane. The model gave the detonation an anatomy for the first time.

Then, beginning in the 1960s, experiments refused to cooperate. Gas-phase detonations proved to be unsteady, three-dimensional, and structured: smoke-foil records revealed fish-scale cellular patterns, and high-speed photography exposed transverse waves sweeping across the front [7][10]. The one-dimensional theories survive as averages and as indispensable engineering tools, but the true front is a dynamic lattice of interacting shocks. This article presents all three layers — CJ thermodynamics, ZND structure, and cellular dynamics — as one coherent story.

## 2 Background

Consider a steady planar wave moving at speed D into quiescent gas of pressure p0, specific volume v0, and internal energy e0. In the frame riding with the wave, the burned products recede with velocity u. Conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across the discontinuity gives the Rankine–Hugoniot relations for a reactive jump:

- mass: the mass flux through the wave is conserved, ρ0 D = ρ (D − u)
- momentum: p − p0 = (D²/v0)(v0 − v)
- energy: e − e0 = (p + p0)(v0 − v)/2 + q

where q is the chemical energy released per unit mass and v = 1/ρ [2][6].

Two geometric objects in the pressure–volume plane encode these relations. The *Rayleigh line* expresses mass and momentum conservation for a fixed wave speed D:

p = p0 + (D/v0)² (v0 − v)

a straight line through the initial state whose slope steepens with D. The *Hugoniot curve* expresses energy conservation with heat release, p = pH(v; p0, v0, q), which lies above the inert-shock Hugoniot because of the added chemical energy [6]. Intersections of the Rayleigh line with the Hugoniot are the admissible end states; for large D there are two on the compressive branch (strong and weak detonation) and two on the expansive branch (strong and weak deflagration). The flow behind a strong detonation is subsonic relative to the wave; behind a weak detonation it is supersonic [2].

| Property | Deflagration | Detonation |
|---|---|---|
| Propagation mechanism | Heat and mass diffusion | Shock compression and autoignition |
| Wave speed | Subsonic, cm/s to m/s | Supersonic, 1–3 km/s |
| Pressure change | Slight decrease | Increase by one to two orders of magnitude |
| Density change | Decrease | Increase |
| Flow behind wave (wave frame) | Subsonic | Subsonic for strong, supersonic for weak |
| Ignition source | Transport from products | Adiabatic shock heating |

The idealizations are severe: one-dimensionality, steadiness, a calorically perfect gas, instantaneous equilibration of the products, and no losses to walls or curvature. The rest of the article examines what survives when each is relaxed.

## 3 Methodology

The analysis proceeds in three stages, mirroring the historical development:

1. **Thermodynamic selection.** We derive the CJ condition from the Rayleigh–Hugoniot geometry, showing that the minimum-velocity (tangent) solution is simultaneously the sonic solution and, in Jouguet's formulation, the entropy-extremum solution [6][8].
2. **Structural integration.** We reduce the steady reactive Euler equations to ordinary differential equations in the shock frame and integrate through the reaction zone with Arrhenius kinetics, imposing regularity at the sonic singularity — the ZND construction [1][3].
3. **Multidimensional confrontation.** We compare the one-dimensional predictions with the observed cellular front: triple-point trajectories, cell-size scalings, and the empirical correlations that connect cell width to initiation energy and detonability limits [4][7][10].

The following snippet computes the CJ Mach number for a calorically perfect gas in the widely quoted closed form; production codes instead solve the full equilibrium-chemistry system [2][4].

```python
import math

def cj_mach(gamma, Q, c0):
    """CJ Mach number, calorically perfect gas (illustrative).
    Q : specific heat release (J/kg); c0 : sound speed ahead (m/s).
    Real codes use equilibrium thermo; constant gamma overpredicts D."""
    qbar = Q / c0 ** 2
    a = (gamma ** 2 - 1.0) * qbar / 2.0
    return math.sqrt(1.0 + a) + math.sqrt(a)

c0 = 539.0            # ~ sound speed in cold H2-O2, m/s
D = cj_mach(1.4, 1.34e7, c0) * c0
print(f"illustrative D_CJ = {D:.0f} m/s")
```

The Rayleigh line and the reactive Hugoniot are equally easy to encode functionally, which makes the tangency search a one-dimensional root find:

```haskell
-- Rayleigh line and reactive Hugoniot in (v, p); tangency locates the CJ point.
rayleigh :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
rayleigh d p0 v0 v = p0 + (d / v0) ^ 2 * (v0 - v)

hugoniot :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
hugoniot g p0 v0 q v =
  p0 * ((g + 1) * v0 - (g - 1) * v + 2 * (g - 1) * q / p0)
     / ((g + 1) * v - (g - 1) * v0)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Chapman–Jouguet Selection Rule: Tangency and the Sonic Condition

As D decreases, the Rayleigh line pivots toward the horizontal and its two intersections with the detonation branch of the Hugoniot approach each other. At a critical speed they coalesce: the line is tangent to the curve. Below that speed no intersection exists on the detonation branch at all. Chapman's postulate is that the self-sustaining wave travels at exactly this minimum speed, D_CJ, because experiments show a unique velocity for each mixture [2][8].

> **Theorem (Chapman–Jouguet sonic condition).** *At the tangency point of the Rayleigh line and the equilibrium Hugoniot, the burned gas recedes from the wave at exactly the local equilibrium sound speed. In the shock frame the Mach number behind the wave is unity.*

The proof follows from differentiating the Hugoniot and Rayleigh relations at the point of common slope; the tangency condition is algebraically identical to the statement that the flow speed relative to the wave equals the sound speed [6]. Jouguet reached the same point from a different direction: parametrizing the Hugoniot by the mass flux, he found that entropy attains an extremum at tangency, so the CJ state is the minimum-entropy admissible detonation state [6][8]. A modern invariance theorem sharpens this: the CJ velocity and its associated entropy variation obey an exact invariance relation derivable from the Rankine–Hugoniot system alone, independent of any assumed equation of state [6].

Crucially, D_CJ is a purely thermodynamic quantity. It depends on the heat release and the equation of state of the products, and not at all on the chemical kinetics — the rate at which energy is released affects only how *thick* the reaction zone is, never how fast the wave goes [4]. This separation of thermodynamics from kinetics is what makes CJ theory both powerful and, as we shall see, incomplete.

### 4.2 The ZND Internal Structure: Shock, Spike, Induction, Reaction

The ZND model keeps the steady one-dimensional frame but replaces the CJ discontinuity with a resolved interior [1][3]. Reading from front to back:

- **Leading shock.** An inert, essentially discontinuous shock compresses the unburned gas. No reaction has yet occurred; the state jumps along the *frozen* (non-reactive) Hugoniot to the von Neumann spike — the maximum pressure in the entire wave, typically about twice the CJ pressure.
- **Induction zone.** Behind the spike the gas is hot but chemically almost frozen. Chain-branching radicals accumulate exponentially while temperature and pressure stay nearly flat. The induction length, set by Arrhenius kinetics at the spike temperature, is the fundamental microscopic scale of the theory.
- **Reaction zone.** Thermal runaway ignites the mixture; heat release drives pressure and temperature down along the Rayleigh line toward the equilibrium Hugoniot. The zone ends where the flow becomes sonic.
- **CJ plane and Taylor wave.** At the sonic point the reaction is complete and the state sits at the CJ point of the equilibrium Hugoniot. Behind it, an unsteady rarefaction — the Taylor wave — expands the products toward rest [3].

> **Lemma (ZND regularity).** *Integrating the steady reactive-flow ODEs from the shock toward the products encounters a sonic singularity. Only for the CJ wave speed does the numerator of the governing equation vanish simultaneously with the denominator, yielding a regular solution; this regularity condition is the ZND analogue of the CJ tangency postulate [2][3].*

A subtlety worth stating precisely: the sonic condition obtained by integrating the ZND structure uses the *frozen* sound speed, whereas the CJ tangency argument uses the *equilibrium* sound speed. The two selection criteria are not identical, and the ZND solution at the CJ speed generally sits on the weak branch of the equilibrium Hugoniot rather than exactly at the minimum-velocity point [2]. For most mixtures the numerical difference is small, which is why the distinction is often glossed over — but it is real. Recent extensions carry the ZND construction into multi-temperature, non-equilibrium mixtures, where the temperature split between components inside the reaction zone is itself driven by the chemical kinetics [9].

| Zone | Pressure trend | Chemistry | Characteristic scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading shock | Jump to von Neumann spike | Frozen | Shock thickness (molecular) |
| Induction zone | Nearly flat | Radical buildup, negligible heat release | Induction length |
| Reaction zone | Falls toward CJ pressure | Rapid exothermic conversion | Reaction length, a few induction lengths |
| Taylor wave | Expansion to ambient | Complete | Unsteady, grows with time |

### 4.3 Instability, Transverse Waves, and the Birth of Cellular Structure

The ZND profile is a steady solution of the reactive Euler equations. It is not, in general, a *stable* one. Linear stability analysis, pioneered by Erpenbeck, shows that one-dimensional pulsations grow when the effective activation energy is high; more dramatically, two-dimensional unsupported detonations are unstable to transverse perturbations at *all* activation energies, so that the planar front cannot survive in practice [4]. Numerical simulations confirm the picture: the reaction zone's extreme temperature sensitivity amplifies any ripple on the shock into a growing transverse disturbance [4].

What saturates this instability is one of the most beautiful structures in fluid mechanics. The front breaks into a lattice of **triple points** — locations where three shocks meet: the incident shock, a transverse shock sweeping sideways along the front, and a Mach stem that is locally stronger than either [5][7]. The triple points trace out diamond-shaped trajectories as the wave advances, and where they sweep across a smoked foil they etch the famous fish-scale pattern. The characteristic transverse spacing of this lattice is the **cell width** λ.

> **Theorem (Cellular necessity).** *Every detonable gaseous mixture exhibits a cellular front; the quasi-one-dimensional CJ and ZND solutions describe only the average motion of an intrinsically multidimensional wave [4][7].*

The spacing is not arbitrary. Simulations and experiments agree that the transverse wave spacing is typically a few dozen times the one-dimensional ZND induction length — commonly quoted as roughly thirty to seventy induction lengths — linking the macroscopic cell directly to the microscopic chemical scale [4]. Mixtures diluted with argon produce strikingly regular cells; undiluted hydrocarbon mixtures produce irregular, chaotic lattices — a difference with profound consequences for initiation and failure [7].

A minimal TLA+ sketch captures the discrete logic of the front: each front segment cycles between incident-shock and Mach-stem states as triple points pass.

### 4.4 Triple Points, Cell Topology, and Smoked-Foil Diagnostics

The smoked-foil technique — coating the wall of a detonation tube with soot and letting the triple points write their own trajectories — remains the definitive diagnostic [7][10]. From a single foil one reads:

- **Cell width λ**, the transverse repeat distance, the single most tabulated property of a mixture.
- **Cell length L**, the longitudinal repeat distance, typically between one and two cell widths, so cells are slightly elongated diamonds.
- **Regularity**, quantified by the scatter of cell sizes; argon dilution regularizes, while high activation energy irregularizes.

These geometric data feed the engineering correlations that CJ theory alone cannot supply. The critical tube diameter for transmission of a detonation into open space scales with cell width — the empirical rule of thumb relates the two by a factor of order ten (about thirteen cell widths in Lee's widely used form) [10]. Critical initiation energies, detonability limits, and the onset of single-headed spinning detonation near the limits all correlate with λ rather than with any one-dimensional length [7][10]. In this sense the cell width, an emergent property of the instability, has become the practical ruler of detonation hazard assessment.

```rust
/// One explicit step of a ZND-style reaction-zone integration
/// (Arrhenius kinetics, shock-fixed frame, illustrative).
struct ZndState { lambda: f64, t: f64 }

fn arrhenius_rate(t: f64, a: f64, ea_r: f64) -> f64 {
    a * (-ea_r / t).exp()
}

fn step(s: ZndState, dt: f64, a: f64, ea_r: f64, t_vn: f64) -> ZndState {
    let k = arrhenius_rate(t_vn, a, ea_r); // frozen T across the induction zone
    ZndState { lambda: s.lambda + k * (1.0 - s.lambda) * dt, t: s.t + dt }
}
```

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The confrontation with experiment has been running for over a century, and the scorecard favors the classical theories handsomely — within their domain:

1. **Velocity prediction.** Measured detonation velocities in large-diameter tubes agree with D_CJ computed from equilibrium thermodynamics to within about one percent for well-behaved mixtures, confirming that kinetics does not set the speed [4].
2. **The von Neumann spike.** Pressure-bar and laser-interferometry measurements resolve the predicted pressure overshoot ahead of the reaction zone, with spike-to-CJ pressure ratios near the theoretical value of roughly two for typical mixtures [1][2].
3. **Smoked-foil cells.** Thousands of foil records across fuels, diluents, and initial pressures confirm the triple-point lattice, its fish-scale geometry, and the scaling of cell width with induction length [7][10].
4. **Numerical reproduction.** Time-dependent Euler simulations with detailed or simplified kinetics spontaneously develop cellular fronts whose mean speed matches D_CJ and whose cell sizes match experiment, proving the instability is intrinsic rather than an artifact of apparatus [4].
5. **Mach reflection.** When a ZND detonation diffracts at a wedge, the leading front initially reflects exactly as a frozen inert shock of the same strength — the frozen limit — before chemistry perturbs the configuration, precisely as the two-scale ZND picture predicts [5].
6. **Velocity–entropy invariance.** The CJ tangency condition has been recast as an exact invariance theorem within the Rankine–Hugoniot system, placing the century-old selection rule on rigorous footing independent of any particular equation of state [6].
7. **Entropy paradox resolved.** The apparent paradox — minimum entropy at the CJ point seeming to violate the second law — dissolves once the entropy variation is analyzed along the correct thermodynamic path rather than the naive one [8].

## 6 Limitations

The theories are averages over, or idealizations of, a reality that is richer and less forgiving:

- **Instability is the rule.** The ZND profile is linearly unstable under realistic conditions; the steady one-dimensional wave it describes never actually occurs in gases [4].
- **Velocity deficit and failure.** Near the detonability limits, curvature, wall losses, and boundary-layer divergence bleed energy from the front; the wave slows below D_CJ, the cells grow, and eventually the detonation fails — none of which the ideal theories predict [7][10].
- **Pathological detonation.** With non-monotonic energy release, steady waves exist that travel *faster* than D_CJ, with an embedded sonic point inside the reaction zone; the CJ minimum is then not the selected speed [4].
- **Frozen versus equilibrium sound speed.** The ZND regularity condition and the CJ tangency condition select slightly different states, a discrepancy invisible in most engineering calculations but conceptually important [2].
- **Curvature and losses.** The Wood–Kirkwood theory extends ZND with flow divergence and yields a velocity–curvature relation, but a fully predictive first-principles theory of cell size remains elusive [1].
- **Condensed explosives.** In solids and liquids, hot spots, grain structure, and non-ideal equations of state dominate; gaseous cellular phenomenology transfers only by analogy [4].
- **No predictive cell theory.** Cell width must still be measured or computed from full multidimensional simulations; no closed-form theory derives λ from kinetics alone.

## 7 Conclusion

Three theories, three levels of description, one phenomenon. Chapman–Jouguet thermodynamics tells us *how fast* a detonation must go, selecting the unique minimum-velocity, sonic, minimum-entropy state from the continuum allowed by conservation laws [6][8]. The ZND model tells us *what the wave looks like inside* — shock, von Neumann spike, induction zone, reaction zone, sonic plane — and gives kinetics its proper role as the setter of scales rather than speeds [1][3]. Cellular theory tells us *what the front actually does*: break into a self-sustaining lattice of triple points and transverse waves whose emergent cell width has become the practical measure of a mixture's detonability [4][7][10].

The open problems are worthy of the subject's violence. A predictive theory of cell size from first principles, quantitative criteria for deflagration-to-detonation transition, and validated models for rotating detonation engines all remain active frontiers [4][5]. But the core edifice — tangency, spike, induction, cells — has survived eighty years of increasingly hostile experiment, which is more than most theories in fluid mechanics can claim.
