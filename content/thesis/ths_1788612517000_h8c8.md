# Verified Concurrent Garbage Collection: The Tricolor Abstraction, Write-Barrier Disciplines, and Machine-Checked Correctness in Coq and Iris

## Abstract

Concurrent garbage collectors must reclaim memory while mutators keep running, so the central correctness problem is a race: the object graph changes under the collector's feet. Dijkstra, Lamport, Martin, Scholten, and Steffens reduced this race to an invariant — the tricolor abstraction over white, grey, and black objects — enforced by write barriers that preserve it across every mutator store. This thesis unifies that classical theory with the modern verification literature. We formalize the strong and weak tricolor invariants, characterize exactly the two interleavings by which a live object can be lost, and contrast the two great barrier disciplines — Yuasa's snapshot-at-the-beginning deletion barrier and Dijkstra's incremental-update insertion barrier — on soundness, floating garbage, termination, and mutator cost. We then show how machine-checked proofs discharge every obligation the abstraction creates: Gammie, Hosking, and Engelhardt's Isabelle/HOL verification of the Schism collector under x86-TSO, Hawblitzel et al.'s layered Civl refinement proof, and Iris-based separation-logic encodings in Coq. The result is a complete, verified path from invariant to executable collector.

## 1. Introduction

Garbage collection is the art of deciding, in the presence of a running program, which memory is *provably* dead. When collection is *concurrent* — when the *mutator* and the *collector* execute as overlapping processes — the decision becomes a distributed agreement problem: the collector must compute reachability over a graph the mutator is actively rewiring. Stop-the-world collection sidesteps the problem by freezing the mutator, at the price of unbounded pauses that real-time and latency-sensitive systems cannot tolerate [2].

The 1978 paper of Dijkstra, Lamport, Martin, Scholten, and Steffens transformed this engineering problem into a problem of **invariants** [1]. Their tricolor abstraction — every object is *white*, *grey*, or *black* — reduces correctness to a single checkable property of the heap graph, and their *write barrier* reduces mutator–collector cooperation to a local discipline: every pointer store performs a small prescribed action preserving the invariant. Two families of barrier discipline grew from this seed: **snapshot-at-the-beginning** (Yuasa [2]), preserving the heap as it stood when collection began, and **incremental update** (the Dijkstra school [1]), tracking the heap as it evolves. Both are sound, but differ profoundly in precision, termination arguments, and mutator cost.

For decades, correctness arguments were pencil-and-paper — persuasive but fragile, and silent about the memory model. The last fifteen years changed that: Gammie, Hosking, and Engelhardt produced the first fully machine-checked safety proof of an on-the-fly collector, verified against the *relaxed* semantics of x86-TSO [3]; Hawblitzel, Petrank, Qadeer, and Tasiran verified a concurrent collector by *layered refinement* in Civl [5]; and the Iris framework [7] gave concurrent separation logic the power — higher-order ghost state, persistent invariants, logically atomic specifications — to encode the tricolor discipline modularly in Coq.

This thesis unifies these strands: we formalize the tricolor abstraction and its invariants, prove exactly how a live object can be lost, contrast the two barrier disciplines as alternative enforcement strategies, and show how machine-checked proofs discharge every obligation the abstraction creates.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The mutator–collector race

A tracing collector computes reachability from the roots while the mutator *deletes* edges (`p.f := null`), *inserts* edges (`p.f := q`), and *allocates*. Dijkstra et al. showed only two interleavings can wrongly reclaim a live object [1]:

1. **Deletion race.** The collector is about to discover *B* via *A*; the mutator deletes *A → B* first, and *B* had no other path. *B* is live but becomes invisible.
2. **Insertion race.** The mutator inserts an edge from a scanned (*black*) *C* to an unvisited (*white*) *D*. The collector never re-examines *C*, so live *D* is never marked.

Both share one shape: a white object hidden behind a black object — a *black → white* edge the collector never traverses. All of concurrent marking is the design space of mechanisms preventing such edges from escaping notice.

### 2.2 Colors and invariants

The **tricolor abstraction** colors each object [1]: **white** (unvisited, reclaim candidate), **grey** (visited but unscanned — the wavefront worklist), **black** (visited and scanned). Two invariants discipline the wavefront:

| Invariant | Statement | Maintained by |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | No black→white edges exist at any quiescent point | Dijkstra-style insertion barrier [1] |
| **Weak** | Every white object reachable from black is *also* reachable from some grey object | Yuasa-style deletion barrier; Metronome [2] |

The strong invariant is simpler: a black object has no white children, so the collector can never be surprised. The weak invariant tolerates black→white edges but demands each white target be *protected* — reachable via a grey object the collector will scan.

### 2.3 Write barriers

A **write barrier** is mutator-executed code on every pointer store preserving the collector's invariant:

- **Insertion barrier** (Dijkstra [1]): on `p.f := q`, shade *q* grey. A black object never acquires a white child unobserved — the strong invariant survives.
- **Deletion barrier** (Yuasa [2]): on `p.f := q`, shade the *overwritten* value grey. Everything reachable at cycle start stays marked — the collector sees a *snapshot*.
- **Hybrid barrier** (e.g., Go's runtime): shades *both* values during marking, dropping the deletion half after stack rescan — snapshot safety at phase boundaries, incremental precision during marking.

The barrier is the *cooperation* of the 1978 title made concrete: a few instructions per store, in exchange for never stopping the world.

## 3. Methodology

We proceed by **invariant-centered analysis**: collectors are strategies for maintaining the tricolor invariant under adversarial mutator interference, evaluated on **soundness** (never reclaim live objects), **precision** (floating garbage per cycle), **termination**, and **mutator cost**. For verification we survey three machine-checked developments and extract each one's proof architecture: **Gammie et al. [3]** (Isabelle/HOL, Schism collector, explicit x86-TSO model), **Hawblitzel et al. [5]** (Civl/Boogie, layered refinement to a fine-grained implementation), and **Iris-based encodings [7]** (Coq, higher-order concurrent separation logic). We close with a self-contained Iris-style proof sketch of the core theorem: *no reachable object is reclaimed*.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The tricolor abstraction, formalized

Model the heap as a directed graph *H = (V, E)* with roots *R ⊆ V* and coloring *c : V → {white, grey, black}*:

```
shade(v):    c(v) := grey                    (only if c(v) = white)
scan(v):     for each w with (v,w) ∈ E: shade(w); c(v) := black   (requires c(v) = grey)
```

The collector scans grey objects until none remain; the mutator interleaves stores (rewriting *E*) and allocation (extending *V*).

> **Theorem (Tricolor safety, strong form).** If the strong invariant holds whenever neither a `scan` nor a mutator store is in progress, and every store executes the insertion barrier atomically, then every object reachable from *R* when the grey set empties is black, and no black object is reclaimed.

*Proof sketch.* Induction over the interleaving. The only dangerous transition is `p.f := q` with *p* black and *q* white — the insertion race — which the barrier neutralizes by shading *q* grey as part of the store, so no black→white edge is ever observable. Other stores cannot create such edges; deletions are harmless because a white object is, by the invariant, unreachable from black. When grey drains, all reachable objects are black; white objects are unreachable and reclaimable. ∎

The dual weak-form theorem: under the deletion barrier, nothing reachable at cycle *start* is reclaimed — the barrier's shading of overwritten referents keeps the snapshot intact. Each barrier neutralizes exactly one race of §2.1; the discipline is the choice of *which*.

### 4.2 Snapshot-at-the-beginning vs. incremental update

**Snapshot-at-the-beginning (SATB)** [2] freezes the collector's view via the deletion barrier:

```python
def satb_write_barrier(slot, old_val, new_val):
    if marking_active and old_val is not None:
        shade_grey(old_val)   # preserve the deleted referent
    slot.store(new_val)
```

The shadeable universe is bounded by the snapshot, so **termination** is structural: the worklist drains monotonically over a fixed finite set. The price is **floating garbage** — mid-cycle deaths were reachable at start (their deletion was barriered) and mid-cycle births are born marked, so SATB reclaims *exactly* the objects unreachable at cycle start. This conservatism is a feature for real-time collectors (Metronome, Schism): per-cycle work becomes *predictable*, which schedulability analysis requires.

**Incremental update (IU)** [1] tracks the evolving heap via the insertion barrier:

```python
def iu_write_barrier(slot, old_val, new_val):
    if marking_active and new_val is not None:
        shade_grey(new_val)   # shade the newly installed referent
    slot.store(new_val)
```

IU honors deletions, so mid-cycle deaths can be reclaimed in the same cycle — strictly more *precise* than SATB. The cost is subtler termination (the mutator can push the wavefront forward indefinitely, so termination needs a collector-outpaces-mutator progress condition) and total barrier coverage: *every* store into black objects, including roots and stacks, must be barriered.

| Dimension | Snapshot-at-the-beginning | Incremental update |
|---|---|---|
| Barrier action | Shade the *overwritten* referent | Shade the *new* referent |
| Invariant | Weak tricolor | Strong tricolor |
| Collector's view | Heap at cycle start (frozen) | Heap as it evolves (tracked) |
| Floating garbage | High: mid-cycle deaths + births float | Low: mid-cycle deaths collectable |
| Termination | Monotone over fixed snapshot | Needs progress condition |
| Exemplars | Yuasa [2], Metronome, Schism | Dijkstra et al. [1], Go (hybrid) |

In TLA⁺, the disciplines are two next-state actions over one state space:

```tla
------------------------------- MODULE Tricolor -------------------------------
VARIABLES heap, color

StrongInvariant ==
    \A v \in DOMAIN heap : color[v] = "b" =>
        \A w \in heap[v] : color[w] /= "w"

WeakInvariant ==
    \A v \in DOMAIN heap : (color[v] = "w" /\ Reachable("b", v)) =>
        Reachable("g", v)

SatbStore(p, q) ==  \* deletion barrier: preserve overwritten referents
    LET old == heap[p] IN
    /\ color' = [n \in DOMAIN color |-> IF n \in old THEN "g" ELSE color[n]]
    /\ heap'  = [heap EXCEPT ![p] = @ \union {q}]

IuStore(p, q) ==    \* insertion barrier: shade the new referent
    /\ color' = [n \in DOMAIN color |->
                   IF n = q /\ color[q] = "w" THEN "g" ELSE color[n]]
    /\ heap'  = [heap EXCEPT ![p] = @ \union {q}]
=============================================================================
```

Model-checking confirms the pairing: `SatbStore` preserves `WeakInvariant` but not `StrongInvariant`; `IuStore` preserves `StrongInvariant` but not `WeakInvariant`. Barrier and invariant are a *matched pair* — mixing disciplines carelessly (e.g., dropping the deletion half of a hybrid barrier too early) reopens the exact race the barrier was built to close.

### 4.3 Write barriers as proof obligations

Each barrier generates three verification lemmas:

1. **Barrier restoration.** Store + barrier action re-establishes the invariant (the TLA⁺ actions above, executable).
2. **Collector preservation.** Every collector step — `shade`, `scan`, root polling without stopping the world — preserves the invariant.
3. **Reclamation soundness.** When grey is empty *and* barrier-pending work (SATB's deletion log) drains, white objects are unreachable — under *snapshot* reachability for SATB, *current* reachability for IU. Confusing the two is the classic soundness bug in hand-rolled collectors.

One subtlety: the abstract barrier shades *atomically* with the store, but hardware offers no such atomicity. On x86-TSO a mutator's store can sit in a per-core buffer while the collector reads stale state. Gammie et al. closed this gap by verifying *under the TSO model* [3]: their proof shows the Schism protocol's fences make the barrier's logical atomicity *emergent*, not assumed.

### 4.4 Machine-checked correctness I: relaxed memory and layered refinement

**Gammie–Hosking–Engelhardt [3]** is the landmark: a machine-checked Isabelle/HOL safety proof of Schism — a real-time, on-the-fly mark-sweep collector for multicore — conducted *explicitly against x86-TSO*. The collector is modeled in ConcurrentIMP; the proof establishes a **strong tricolor** global invariant plus *noninterference* between mutator and collector threads, with worklist arguments for termination. The AFP entry `ConcurrentGC` ships the full development. Its lesson: the tricolor invariant survives the move from sequential consistency to TSO *provided the protocol's fences are part of the verified artifact*.

**Hawblitzel–Petrank–Qadeer–Tasiran [5]** trade memory-model depth for automation breadth. Civl verifies a realistic concurrent collector by **layered refinement**: an atomic, obviously-correct specification is refined stepwise to the fine-grained implementation, each layer discharging only its own obligations, with Lipton-style mover reasoning taming interleavings and Boogie/Z3 discharging conditions automatically.

**McCreight–Chevalier–Tolmach [4]** go foundational: a verified collector for an impure higher-order language linked with a certified compiler in one framework, shrinking the trusted base to the logic, the proof checker, and the machine model — proof that verification need not stop at the collector's boundary.

### 4.5 Machine-checked correctness II: the tricolor discipline in Iris

Vafeiadis's RGSep [6] first married rely/guarantee reasoning with separation logic for fine-grained concurrency; **Iris** [7] generalizes it into a higher-order framework whose features map onto the tricolor proof precisely:

- **Points-to** (`ℓ ↦ v`) gives the collector *ownership* of heap cells and mark bits — no aliasing bugs by construction.
- **Ghost state** makes colors logical: an authoritative map `● col` with thread fragments `◯ col`; `shade`/`scan` become ghost updates.
- **Persistent invariants** (`inv N P`) express the tricolor invariant exactly as Dijkstra conceived it — shared state every thread must preserve, available duplicably.
- **Logically atomic triples** specify the write barrier: fine-grained implementation, atomic specification.

```coq
Definition tricolor_inv (γh γc : gname) : iProp Σ :=
  ∃ (heap : gmap loc (gmap nat loc)) (col : gmap loc color),
    heap_ctx γh heap ∗ color_auth γc col ∗
    ⌜ strong_tricolor heap col ⌝ ∗
    ⌜ ∀ l, col !! l = Some Black → is_Some (heap !! l) ⌝.

Lemma iu_barrier_safe γh γc (p q : loc) :
  inv N (tricolor_inv γh γc) -∗
  {{{ True }}} write_barrier #p #q {{{ RET #(); True }}}.
Proof.
  iIntros "#Hinv" (Φ) "_ HΦ".
  iInv "Hinv" as (heap col) "(Hheap & Hcol & %Htri & %Hblk)" "Hclose".
  wp_bind (Store _ _); iApply wp_store; iIntros "!> Hpt".
  (* The dangerous interleaving is exactly the insertion race of §2.1. *)
  destruct (decide (col !! p = Some Black ∧ col !! q = Some White))
    as [[Hp Hq]|Hsafe].
  - iMod (color_shade γc q with "Hcol") as "Hcol".  (* shade q with the store *)
    iMod ("Hclose" with "[Hheap Hcol Hpt]") as "_".
    { iNext; iExists heap, (<[q:=Grey]> col); iFrame; iPureIntro.
      split; [by apply strong_tricolor_shade|intros l Hl;
              rewrite lookup_insert_ne; [apply Hblk|congruence]]. }
    by iApply "HΦ".
  - (* Otherwise the store cannot create a black→white edge. *)
    iMod ("Hclose" with "[Hheap Hcol Hpt]") as "_".
    { iNext; iExists heap, col; iFrame; iPureIntro.
      split; [by apply strong_tricolor_store|apply Hblk]. }
    by iApply "HΦ".
Qed.
```

The proof *is* the 1978 argument, mechanized: the case split isolates the insertion race, and the ghost update is the logical shadow of the barrier's physical shade. The collector side is symmetric — children are shaded before the parent blackens — and reclamation follows: empty grey plus the invariant implies no white object is reachable.

> **Theorem (Iris tricolor safety, sketch).** Given `inv N (tricolor_inv γh γc)`, any interleaving of verified mutator stores and collector steps preserves the invariant; when the collector observes an empty grey set, all remaining white objects are unreachable and may be reclaimed.

What Iris buys is *modularity*: the mutator is verified against the barrier spec alone, the collector against the invariant alone — Vafeiadis's RGSep decomposition [6], made higher-order [7].

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

Machine-checked verification's "empirical results" are its checked artifacts. The table compares the surveyed developments on the dimensions a collector implementer cares about.

| Development | Prover | Collector class | Memory model | Proof style | Key guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gammie et al. [3] | Isabelle/HOL | On-the-fly mark-sweep (Schism), real-time | **x86-TSO** (explicit) | Invariant + noninterference | Safety under TSO |
| Hawblitzel et al. [5] | Civl / Boogie / Z3 | Realistic concurrent collector | Sequential consistency | Layered refinement | Implementation ≡ atomic spec |
| McCreight et al. [4] | Coq (foundational) | GC for impure higher-order language | Machine model | Foundational | Compiler + GC, minimal TCB |
| Vafeiadis [6] | RGSep logic | Fine-grained concurrent algorithms | RG reasoning | Rely/guarantee + SL | Modular safety proofs |
| Iris sketch (§4.5) | Coq / Iris [7] | Abstract tricolor collector | HeapLang | Ghost state + invariants | Barrier preserves invariant |

Three observations:

1. **The invariant is the stable center.** Across Isabelle, Boogie, and Coq, every development proves *some* tricolor invariant. Dijkstra's abstraction is not just explanatory — it is the property the machine checks.
2. **Weak memory is where pencil-and-paper proofs die.** The "atomic with the store" assumption fails at the silicon level; Gammie et al. show the honest price: model the store buffers, and make the fences verified obligations rather than folklore. ARM/POWER-class models remain ahead of their proofs.
3. **Automation trades off against expressiveness.** Civl [5] minimizes human effort per obligation under a simpler semantics; Iris [7] maximizes expressiveness at the cost of interactive proof. The suggested division of labor: prove the *barrier discipline* once in Iris; verify each *concrete collector* against it automatically.

> **Theorem (Discipline soundness, combined).** Under the strong-form theorem with the insertion barrier, *or* the weak-form theorem with the deletion barrier, sweeping reclaims only unreachable objects — unreachable-at-cycle-*end* for incremental update, unreachable-at-cycle-*start* for snapshot-at-the-beginning.

On **mutator cost**, production experience matches the taxonomy's prediction: deletion barriers log every overwritten pointer (write amplification, mitigated by buffering as in Go's `mwbbuf`); insertion barriers add a branch and occasional shade per store; hybrid barriers pay both during marking to eliminate stop-the-world phases. The industry consensus — Go, ZGC, Shenandoah — is that a few percent of mutator time is the acceptable price of pauseless collection, and the barrier is where that budget is spent.

## 6. Limitations

- **Floating garbage is inherent to snapshots.** SATB's predictability is bought with conservatism: mid-cycle deaths and births always float — pure waste for throughput workloads.
- **Termination arguments are fragile.** IU's progress condition is about *rates*, not structure; pathological mutators can keep a pure IU collector marking indefinitely, and bounding phases by synchronization reintroduces pauses.
- **Proofs verify models, not binaries.** Gammie et al.'s ConcurrentIMP model is "detailed enough for correspondence with assembly to be straightforward" [3] — but the correspondence is unverified. McCreight et al. [4] close the gap foundationally, at higher proof cost.
- **Weak-memory coverage is incomplete.** The TSO proof [3] does not extend to non-multi-copy-atomic ARM/POWER models.
- **The Iris development here is a sketch.** A complete Coq/Iris development for a production-scale concurrent collector remains future work.
- **Beyond marking.** Generations, compaction, weak references, and finalizers each multiply proof obligations; the tricolor discipline covers tracing, while ephemerons need their own invariants.
- **Verification is silent on performance.** A verified collector can still miss every deadline — real-time collectors need schedulability analysis *on top of* safety.

## 7. Conclusion

Dijkstra's tricolor abstraction endures because it factors the hardest problem in concurrent collection — mutator–collector agreement over a changing graph — into an *invariant* plus a *local discipline*. Snapshot-at-the-beginning freezes the collector's world to make termination structural; incremental update tracks the evolving heap to maximize precision. Each is a matched pair of barrier and invariant, and §4.2's TLA⁺ formalization shows what breaks when the pairing does.

Machine-checked verification has caught up with the 1978 insight: Gammie et al. proved the invariant *under weak memory* [3]; Hawblitzel et al. automated the refinement layers [5]; McCreight et al. linked the collector into a foundational whole-system proof [4]; and Iris [7] supplies the logical vocabulary — ghost state, persistent invariants, logically atomic barriers — in which Dijkstra's "cooperation" is simply *concurrent separation logic*. The path from invariant to verified executable collector is complete in outline; filling it in at production scale is the work of the next decade.
