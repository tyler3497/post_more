# Hamiltonian Floer Homology and the Arnold Conjecture: Periodic Orbits, the Floer Equation, and Quantum Cohomology

## Abstract

The Arnold conjecture predicts that a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a closed symplectic manifold possesses at least as many fixed points as a smooth function has critical points, forging a bridge between symplectic rigidity and Morse theory. Andreas Floer's construction of an infinite-dimensional Morse homology for the symplectic action functional resolved the conjecture for a large class of manifolds and created one of the most powerful invariants in modern geometry. This article develops Hamiltonian Floer homology from first principles: the action functional on the loop space, its critical points as contractible periodic orbits, and the negative gradient flow lines governed by Floer's elliptic partial differential equation. We prove the energy identity, establish transversality and compactness modulo bubbling, and define the Floer chain complex over Novikov coefficients. The celebrated isomorphism between Floer homology and singular homology — via the Piunikhin–Salamon–Schwarz construction — is explained, yielding the Arnold conjecture as a corollary. We survey Lagrangian Floer homology, the Fukaya category, and symplectic cohomology, and discuss applications to the Weinstein conjecture, Hofer geometry, and mirror symmetry.

## 1 Introduction

In 1965, Vladimir Arnold made a striking prediction about the fixed points of symplectic maps [1]. Let $(M, \omega)$ be a closed symplectic manifold and let $\phi: M \to M$ be a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism — the time-one map of a time-dependent Hamiltonian flow. Arnold conjectured that the number of fixed points of $\phi$ is bounded below by the minimal number of critical points of a smooth function on $M$, a purely topological quantity. For a nondegenerate Hamiltonian diffeomorphism, the conjecture asserts at least $\sum_i b_i(M)$ fixed points, where $b_i$ are the Betti numbers; the stronger form predicts at least the minimal number of critical points of a Morse function.

> **Theorem (Arnold conjecture, weak form):** A nondegenerate Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a closed symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$ has at least $\sum_{i} \dim H_i(M; \mathbb{Z}_2)$ fixed points.

This is a rigidity statement of remarkable depth: it says that Hamiltonian dynamics cannot be too simple, and that symplectic topology constrains dynamics in ways invisible to ordinary differential topology. The conjecture resisted proof for over two decades. Partial results accumulated — Eliashberg's work on the torus, Hofer's results on $\mathbb{C}P^n$ — but the general case demanded new machinery.

That machinery arrived in 1988–1989, when Andreas Floer introduced an infinite-dimensional version of Morse homology for the **symplectic action functional** on the loop space of $M$ [2][3]. Floer's insight was that the fixed points of a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism are the critical points of a functional $\mathcal{A}_H$ on loops, and that the negative gradient flow of this functional is governed by an elliptic PDE — the **Floer equation** — whose solutions can be counted to define a homology theory. The resulting **Floer homology** is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopy and, miraculously, isomorphic to the ordinary singular homology of $M$. The Arnold conjecture follows immediately: the rank of Floer homology bounds the number of generators of the chain complex, i.e., the number of fixed points.

This article is a self-contained exposition of Hamiltonian Floer homology and its crowning application. We emphasize the analytic foundations — the Fredholm theory of the Floer operator, energy identities, Gromov compactness, and transversality — while keeping the geometric narrative in view. We then survey the vast landscape Floer's ideas opened: Lagrangian Floer homology and the Fukaya category, symplectic cohomology, and applications ranging from the Weinstein conjecture to homological mirror symmetry.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Symplectic manifolds and Hamiltonian dynamics

A **symplectic manifold** is a pair $(M, \omega)$ where $M$ is a smooth $2n$-dimensional manifold and $\omega$ is a closed, nondegenerate 2-form. Nondegeneracy means the map $TM \to T^*M$ given by $v \mapsto \iota_v \omega$ is an isomorphism; closedness ($d\omega = 0$) is the integrability condition that makes symplectic geometry rigid.

Given a time-dependent Hamiltonian $H: S^1 \times M \to \mathbb{R}$, the **Hamiltonian vector field** $X_H$ is defined by $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH_t$, and its flow $\phi_H^t$ consists of symplectomorphisms. The time-one map $\phi_H^1$ is a **Hamiltonian diffeomorphism**. The fixed points of $\phi_H^1$ correspond precisely to the 1-periodic orbits of $X_H$ — contractible loops $x: S^1 \to M$ satisfying $\dot{x}(t) = X_{H_t}(x(t))$.

A Hamiltonian diffeomorphism is **nondegenerate** if all its fixed points are nondegenerate, meaning $\det(\mathbf{1} - d\phi_H^1(x)) \neq 0$ at each fixed point $x$. Nondegenerate Hamiltonians are generic (dense) by a standard transversality argument.

### 2.2 Classical Morse homology

Floer's construction is an infinite-dimensional analogue of Morse homology, so we recall the finite-dimensional story. Let $f: M \to \mathbb{R}$ be a Morse function on a closed Riemannian manifold, with critical points of Morse index $\mu(x)$. The **Morse complex** $CM_*(f)$ is the free abelian group on critical points, graded by index, with differential counting isolated negative gradient flow lines:
$$\partial x = \sum_{\mu(y) = \mu(x) - 1} n(x, y)\, y,$$
where $n(x,y)$ counts flow lines from $x$ to $y$ modulo translation, with signs from coherent orientations. The identity $\partial^2 = 0$ follows from analyzing the boundary of one-dimensional moduli spaces of flow lines: broken trajectories. The homology $HM_*(f)$ is canonically isomorphic to $H_*(M)$ [4].

Floer's genius was to replicate this entire package with the **loop space** $\mathcal{L}M = C^\infty(S^1, M)$ in place of $M$, and the **symplectic action functional** in place of $f$.

### 2.3 The symplectic action functional

Fix a Hamiltonian $H$ and consider contractible loops $x \in \mathcal{L}_0 M$. Choose a capping disk $\bar{x}: D^2 \to M$ with $\bar{x}|_{S^1} = x$. The **action functional** is
$$\mathcal{A}_H(x) = -\int_{D^2} \bar{x}^*\omega - \int_{S^1} H_t(x(t))\, dt.$$
When $[\omega]$ vanishes on $\pi_2(M)$ (the **symplectically aspherical** case), the first term is independent of the capping and $\mathcal{A}_H$ is single-valued; in general one must work on a covering of the loop space or use Novikov coefficients, as we discuss in §4.1.

The critical points of $\mathcal{A}_H$ are exactly the contractible 1-periodic orbits of $X_H$. The $L^2$-gradient of $\mathcal{A}_H$ with respect to a compatible almost complex structure $J$ is $\nabla \mathcal{A}_H(x) = J_t(x)(\dot{x} - X_{H_t}(x))$, so negative gradient flow lines $u: \mathbb{R} \times S^1 \to M$ satisfy **Floer's equation**:
$$\partial_s u + J_t(u)\bigl(\partial_t u - X_{H_t}(u)\bigr) = 0,$$
with asymptotic conditions $\lim_{s \to \pm\infty} u(s, \cdot) = x^\pm$ for periodic orbits $x^\pm$. This is a nonlinear elliptic PDE of Cauchy–Riemann type, and its analysis is the technical heart of the theory [5].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Fredholm theory of the Floer operator

Linearizing Floer's equation at a solution $u$ yields the **Floer operator** $D_u$, a first-order elliptic operator on sections of $u^*TM$ over the cylinder. With suitable Sobolev completions ($W^{1,p}$ domain, $L^p$ target for $p > 2$), $D_u$ is Fredholm, and its index computes the difference of **Conley–Zehnder indices**:
$$\operatorname{ind} D_u = \mu_{CZ}(x^+) - \mu_{CZ}(x^-).$$
The Conley–Zehnder index $\mu_{CZ}(x)$ is a Maslov-type invariant of the periodic orbit, generalizing the Morse index to the symplectic setting; it is defined via the linearized Hamiltonian flow as a path in the symplectic group [6].

For generic $J$ and $H$, $D_u$ is surjective for all solutions, and the moduli space $\mathcal{M}(x^-, x^+; H, J)$ of Floer trajectories is a smooth manifold of dimension $\mu_{CZ}(x^+) - \mu_{CZ}(x^-)$.

### 3.2 Energy and compactness

The **energy** of a Floer trajectory is
$$E(u) = \int_{\mathbb{R} \times S^1} |\partial_s u|^2\, ds\, dt = \mathcal{A}_H(x^-) - \mathcal{A}_H(x^+),$$
the **energy identity**, which follows from differentiating the action along the flow. Energy bounds give $C^0$ bounds via a standard bubbling analysis: a sequence of trajectories with bounded energy has a subsequence converging to a **broken trajectory**, possibly with holomorphic sphere bubbles attached, by Gromov's compactness theorem [5].

In the symplectically aspherical or monotone settings, sphere bubbling is excluded by index considerations (bubbles carry positive Chern number, hence positive index, contradicting index bounds), and the moduli spaces admit compactifications by broken trajectories alone. This is what makes $\partial^2 = 0$ work: the boundary of a one-dimensional moduli space consists of once-broken trajectories, counted with signs that cancel.

### 3.3 The Floer complex

Assume $(M, \omega)$ is closed and symplectically aspherical for simplicity. The **Floer chain complex** is
$$CF_*(H) = \bigoplus_{x \in \mathcal{P}(H)} \mathbb{Z}_2 \cdot x,$$
graded by the Conley–Zehnder index, where $\mathcal{P}(H)$ is the set of contractible 1-periodic orbits. The differential counts rigid Floer trajectories:
$$\partial x^+ = \sum_{\mu_{CZ}(x^-) = \mu_{CZ}(x^+) - 1} \#\bigl(\mathcal{M}(x^-, x^+)/\mathbb{R}\bigr)\, x^-,$$
with counts mod 2 (signs require coherent orientations, constructed in [7]). The identity $\partial^2 = 0$ follows from the description of the boundary of one-dimensional moduli spaces. The homology $HF_*(H, J)$ is independent of the choices of $H$ and $J$: continuation maps induced by homotopies of Hamiltonians give canonical isomorphisms.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Beyond asphericity: Novikov rings and monotonicity

For general closed $(M, \omega)$, the action functional is multi-valued and sphere bubbling must be confronted. Two standard frameworks handle this:

- **Monotone symplectic manifolds**, where $[\omega] = \lambda c_1(TM)$ on $\pi_2(M)$ for some $\lambda \geq 0$. Here bubbling is controlled by the minimal Chern number $N$: trajectories of index difference less than $2N$ cannot bubble, and the complex is well-defined with a $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$-grading [3].
- **General closed manifolds**, where one works over the **Novikov ring** $\Lambda$ — formal power series in $T$ with exponents recording the symplectic area and Chern number of capping disks. The Floer complex becomes a module over $\Lambda$, and the theory is developed in full generality in [8].

The Novikov formalism elegantly absorbs the multi-valuedness of the action: changing the capping disk shifts the action by the area of a sphere, and the Novikov variable $T$ tracks exactly this ambiguity.

### 4.2 The Piunikhin–Salamon–Schwarz isomorphism

The deepest structural result is that Floer homology recovers ordinary homology. The **PSS isomorphism** [9]
$$\Phi: QH_*(M) \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} HF_*(H)$$
identifies Floer homology with **quantum homology** — the homology of $M$ with the quantum product, a deformation of the cup product counting holomorphic spheres. The map counts "spiked disks": half-infinite Floer cylinders asymptotic to a periodic orbit at one end and converging to a critical point of a Morse function at the other, with sphere bubbles allowed.

In particular, $HF_*(H) \cong H_*(M; \Lambda)$ as vector spaces. The Arnold conjecture is an immediate corollary: the Floer complex is generated by the fixed points of $\phi_H^1$, so
$$\#\operatorname{Fix}(\phi_H^1) \geq \dim_{\Lambda} HF_*(H) = \sum_i b_i(M).$$
The stronger form of the conjecture (bound by the minimal number of critical points of a Morse function) requires the refined statement that the Floer complex is chain-homotopy equivalent to the Morse complex, proved via the PSS maps being chain-level equivalences [9].

### 4.3 Lagrangian Floer homology and the Fukaya category

Floer simultaneously developed a **Lagrangian** version: for transverse Lagrangians $L_0, L_1 \subset M$, the complex $CF_*(L_0, L_1)$ is generated by intersection points, with differential counting holomorphic strips [2]. This theory obstructs Lagrangian displaceability and underlies the nearby Lagrangian conjecture program.

The algebraic structure was vastly enriched by Fukaya, Oh, Ohta, and Ono, who constructed the **Fukaya $A_\infty$-category** $\mathcal{F}(M)$: objects are (decorated) Lagrangians, morphisms are Floer complexes, and higher compositions count holomorphic polygons [10]. The Fukaya category is the central object of **homological mirror symmetry**, Kontsevich's conjecture relating $\mathcal{F}(M)$ to the derived category of coherent sheaves on a mirror manifold [11].

### 4.4 Symplectic cohomology and the Weinstein conjecture

For non-compact manifolds, particularly Liouville domains, **symplectic cohomology** $SH^*(M)$ is the Floer homology of Hamiltonians that grow linearly at infinity; it is the direct limit over Hamiltonians of increasing slope [12]. Viterbo's functoriality and the resulting long exact sequence
$$\cdots \to H^*(M) \to SH^*(M) \to SH^*_+(M) \to H^{*+1}(M) \to \cdots$$
encode deep symplectic information. Notably, $SH^*(M) = 0$ implies strong restrictions, and non-vanishing of symplectic cohomology was used by Viterbo to prove the **Weinstein conjecture** (existence of closed Reeb orbits) for a large class of contact hypersurfaces — one of the first major applications of Floer theory beyond Arnold's conjecture [13].

### 4.5 Spectral invariants and Hofer geometry

Floer homology carries a natural filtration by the action, giving rise to **spectral invariants** $c(a, H)$ for quantum homology classes $a$ — the minimal action level at which $a$ appears in the Floer complex [14]. These invariants are the foundation of modern quantitative symplectic topology: they define the Hofer metric's geodesics, prove the existence of the Calabi quasimorphism, and underlie Entov–Polterovich's theory of symplectic quasi-states. The resulting **persistent homology** perspective — Floer theory as a persistence module over the action filtration — connects symplectic topology to topological data analysis [15].

## 5 Empirical and Computational Aspects

While Floer homology is primarily a theoretical tool, several computational programs exist. For toric manifolds, the Floer theory can be computed combinatorially via the moment polytope [10]. Abouzaid's generation criterion gives conditions under which a finite collection of Lagrangians split-generates the Fukaya category, enabling concrete computations [16].

On the numerical side, the Floer equation's ellipticity makes it amenable to finite-element discretization, and pilot implementations compute Floer differentials for simple Hamiltonians on $S^2$ and $T^2$, verifying the Arnold bound numerically. A promising direction is the use of **machine learning** to predict holomorphic curve counts from almost-complex-structure data, though rigorous certification remains open.

```python
# Pseudocode: counting rigid Floer trajectories via discretized gradient flow
def floer_differential(orbits, H, J, index_diff=1):
    """Assemble the Floer differential over Z_2."""
    from collections import defaultdict
    diff = defaultdict(list)
    for x_plus in orbits:
        for x_minus in orbits:
            if cz_index(x_plus) - cz_index(x_minus) != index_diff:
                continue
            # Solve Floer's equation with asymptotic boundary conditions
            trajectories = solve_floer_pde(x_minus, x_plus, H, J)
            rigid = [u for u in trajectories if fredholm_index(u) == 1]
            if len(rigid) % 2 == 1:  # count mod 2, quotient by R-translation
                diff[x_plus].append(x_minus)
    return diff
```

## 6 Limitations

- **Analytic difficulty:** The full transversality and compactness package for general closed manifolds requires virtual perturbation techniques (Kuranishi structures [10] or polyfolds [17]), whose foundations span thousands of pages.
- **Computability:** Beyond toric and exact settings, Floer homology groups are rarely computable in practice; most applications use formal properties rather than explicit chain complexes.
- **Characteristic dependence:** Torsion phenomena in Floer homology over $\mathbb{Z}$ remain poorly understood compared to the field-coefficient theory.
- **Spectral gaps:** The relationship between spectral invariants and classical dynamical quantities (e.g., topological entropy) is largely conjectural.

## 7 Conclusion

Floer's infinite-dimensional Morse theory transformed symplectic topology from a subject of examples into a subject of invariants. The Arnold conjecture — once a distant dream — became a corollary of a general machine that continues to produce mathematics: Fukaya categories, symplectic cohomology, spectral invariants, and mirror symmetry. The central lesson is methodological: when a finite-dimensional variational problem resists direct attack, lifting it to an infinite-dimensional setting and developing the elliptic analysis to count gradient flow lines can unlock rigidity phenomena invisible at finite dimension. The program Floer began remains incomplete — the integral theory, the precise relation to string topology, and the computational frontier are active research areas — but its place as one of the deepest ideas in modern geometry is secure.
