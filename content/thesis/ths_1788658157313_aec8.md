# Discrete Differential Geometry for Graphics: The Cotangent Laplacian, Discrete Exterior Calculus, and Conformal Parameterization

## Abstract

Discrete differential geometry (DDG) recasts smooth differential geometry — the exterior derivative, Hodge star, Laplace–Beltrami operator, curvature — in a language exact on finite simplicial complexes rather than approximate by truncation. This thesis surveys the three pillars of DDG in geometry processing: the **cotangent Laplacian** [1, 2], **discrete exterior calculus (DEC)** [3, 4], which preserves Stokes' theorem *exactly* through a primal–dual construction, and **conformal parameterization** via discrete harmonic maps [5]. We derive the cotangent formula from the discrete Dirichlet energy, develop the discrete Hodge decomposition, state convergence theorems for the cotangent Laplacian under refinement with intrinsic Delaunay preprocessing [6], and validate mean curvature, Gaussian curvature, and conformal distortion numerically. Experiments on analytic surfaces demonstrate first-order convergence of the mean-curvature normal and machine-precision preservation of the discrete Gauss–Bonnet identity. We close with the theory's fundamental limits — the no-free-lunch theorem [4], negative cotangent weights, and the topology–geometry tension in global parameterization.

## 1 Introduction

Graphics speaks two languages: the physical world it simulates — light, fluids, elasticity — is governed by PDEs in the vocabulary of smooth differential geometry, while the computer manipulates only *finite* data: triangle meshes, point clouds, voxels. Reconciling these worlds is the founding problem of **discrete differential geometry (DDG)**.

The naive reconciliation — replacing derivatives with finite differences — destroys the *structure* of the continuous theory: invariants drift, and graph Laplacians fail to converge under refinement [4]. The philosophy of DDG, articulated in the SIGGRAPH course notes [7], is to construct discrete objects that are *exact* in their own right — discrete forms, Hodge stars, and curvatures satisfying the same structural identities (Stokes, the maximum principle, Gauss–Bonnet) — and only *afterwards* recover the smooth theory through convergence theorems.

Three constructions form the backbone of this program in graphics, and they are the subject of this thesis:

1. **The cotangent Laplacian**, the discrete Laplace–Beltrami operator with edge weights $\tfrac{1}{2}(\cot\alpha+\cot\beta)$ [1, 2] — the workhorse of smoothing, spectral analysis, and harmonic parameterization.
2. **Discrete exterior calculus (DEC)**, in which the discrete exterior derivative is the signed incidence matrix — making Stokes' theorem a *combinatorial identity* — and Hodge stars live on a circumcentric dual mesh [3, 4].
3. **Conformal parameterization**, flattening surfaces with minimal angle distortion, which reduces to a sparse linear system built from the cotangent Laplacian [5] and generalizes to discrete conformal equivalence classes [8].

These pillars are interdependent: the cotangent Laplacian *is* the DEC Laplacian for a particular inner product, discrete harmonic maps *are* its nullspace conditions, and curvature is extracted from it through the mean-curvature normal.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The smooth Laplace–Beltrami operator

Let $(M, g)$ be a smooth Riemannian surface. The **Laplace–Beltrami operator** $\Delta: C^\infty(M) \to C^\infty(M)$ is the divergence of the gradient,

$$\Delta f = \mathrm{div}\, \nabla f,$$

In exterior calculus, $\Delta = \star\, d\, \star\, d$. It is the Euler–Lagrange operator of the Dirichlet energy $E(f) = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_M |\nabla f|^2\, dA$: with fixed boundary values, the *harmonic* function $\Delta f = 0$ minimizes $E$. The mean-curvature vector is recovered through the action of $\Delta$ on the embedding coordinates $x: M \to \mathbb{R}^3$ [7]:

$$\Delta x = -2H\,\mathbf{n},$$

where $H$ is the mean curvature and $\mathbf{n}$ the unit normal. This single identity is the reason the discrete Laplacian is the gateway to discrete curvature.

### 2.2 Simplicial surfaces and piecewise-linear functions

A **simplicial surface** $S = (V, E, F)$ is a finite triangle mesh. A **piecewise-linear** (PL) function $f: S \to \mathbb{R}$ is linear on each triangle, determined by its vertex values $f \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$, with *constant* gradient per triangle. The Dirichlet energy then reduces to a quadratic form on vertex values:

$$E(f) = \frac{1}{2}\int_S |\nabla f|^2\, dA = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j)^2,$$

where the **edge weights** $w_{ij}$ depend only on intrinsic geometry — computing them explicitly leads directly to the cotangent formula [1, 6].

### 2.3 Prior work: from finite elements to intrinsic operators

Duffin derived the cotangent formula for triangulated planar regions [6]; Pinkall and Polthier [1] rediscovered it for *discrete minimal surfaces*. Meyer et al. [2] gave the definitive interpretation: cotangent weights are the stiffness matrix of piecewise-linear finite elements, and the diagonal *mass matrix* encodes per-vertex area, yielding the **discrete mean-curvature normal**, **discrete Gaussian curvature** (angle defect), and **principal curvatures**. Bobenko and Springborn [6] passed to the *intrinsic* Delaunay triangulation, guaranteeing positive weights and a maximum principle. Crane's thesis [4] and the SIGGRAPH 2006 course [7] unified these under DEC, while Lévy et al. [5] gave the practical flip-free conformal parameterization.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is axiomatic and constructive: define discrete operators satisfying exact structural identities, implement them as sparse matrices, and verify convergence against analytic solutions:

1. **Derivation of the cotangent formula.** Differentiate the Dirichlet energy of a PL function on one triangle w.r.t. vertex values to obtain the stiffness matrix — exact for PL functions, with no approximation made.
2. **DEC formalization.** We define the discrete exterior derivative $d_k$ as the transpose of the boundary operator $\partial_{k+1}$, so that Stokes' theorem $\langle d\omega, \Sigma\rangle = \langle \omega, \partial\Sigma\rangle$ holds *by definition*. The discrete Hodge star $\star_k$ transfers $k$-cochains on the primal mesh to $(n-k)$-cochains on a circumcentric dual mesh, and the DEC Laplacian is $\Delta_k = \star^{-1} d^T \star d + d \star^{-1} d^T \star$.
3. **Curvature extraction.** The discrete mean-curvature normal is defined through the *integrated* Laplacian, $K_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_j (\cot\alpha_{ij} + \cot\beta_{ij})(x_i - x_j)$, divided by a vertex area $A_i$ (Voronoi or barycentric), and Gaussian curvature through the angle defect $K_i = 2\pi - \sum_j \theta_j$.
4. **Conformal parameterization.** We minimize the discrete conformal energy $\int_M |dU|^2$ with boundary constraints, which reduces to solving $L\, U = 0$ in the interior with pinned boundary — a sparse positive-definite linear system when the mesh is Delaunay.

All operators are assembled as `scipy.sparse` matrices; linear systems are solved with sparse Cholesky or conjugate gradients.

## 4 The Discrete Laplacian, Discrete Exterior Calculus, and Conformal Maps

### 4.1 The cotangent formula from the Dirichlet energy

Consider a single triangle $T = (x_1, x_2, x_3)$ with angles $\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3$ and a linear function $f$ determined by vertex values $f_1, f_2, f_3$. Because $\nabla f$ is constant on $T$, the Dirichlet energy on the triangle is [6]:

$$E(f|_T) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{i \in \mathbb{Z}/3} \cot\theta_i\, (f_{i+1} - f_{i+2})^2.$$

Differentiating the total energy with respect to $f_i$ — the discrete Euler–Lagrange step — yields the **cotangent Laplacian** [1, 2, 6]:

> **Theorem: (Cotangent Laplacian via Dirichlet energy).** Let $S$ be a simplicial surface and $f: S \to \mathbb{R}$ piecewise linear. Then the gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy $E(f) = \frac{1}{2}\int_S |\nabla f|^2\, dA$ is governed by
>
> $$(\Delta f)(x_i) = \sum_{j: (i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j), \qquad w_{ij} = \begin{cases} \tfrac{1}{2}(\cot\alpha_{ij} + \cot\beta_{ij}) & \text{interior edge}, \\ \tfrac{1}{2}\cot\alpha_{ij} & \text{boundary edge}, \end{cases}$$
>
> where $\alpha_{ij}, \beta_{ij}$ are the angles opposite the edge $(i,j)$ in the one or two incident triangles. With the diagonal mass matrix $M_{ii} = A_i$ (vertex area), the operator $M^{-1}L$ with $L_{ij} = -w_{ij}$, $L_{ii} = \sum_j w_{ij}$ is the consistent discretization of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on $S$.

Geometrically, $\cot\theta$ penalizes differences along edges *opposite* large angles. On Delaunay meshes all weights are non-negative and the operator satisfies a **discrete maximum principle** [6]; Bobenko and Springborn [6] showed every simplicial surface admits a canonical *intrinsic* Delaunay triangulation with the same edge lengths, recovering the maximum principle without changing the geometry.

### 4.2 Discrete exterior calculus: exact Stokes on finite meshes

Integration pairs chains with cochains: $\langle \omega^k, \sigma_k \rangle$ is the "integral" of the $k$-form $\omega^k$ over the $k$-simplex $\sigma_k$. The discrete exterior derivative $d_k$ is *defined* as the coboundary operator — the transpose of the boundary operator $\partial_{k+1}$ — so that

> **Theorem: (Discrete Stokes theorem).** For any discrete $(k-1)$-form $\omega^{k-1}$ and any $k$-chain $\Sigma$,
>
> $$\langle d_{k-1}\,\omega^{k-1},\, \Sigma\rangle = \langle \omega^{k-1},\, \partial\,\Sigma\rangle.$$
>
> This identity holds *exactly*, for every mesh, by the definition of $d$ as the signed incidence matrix. It is not an approximation.

The metric enters through the **discrete Hodge star** $\star_k = \mathrm{diag}(|\star\sigma_k|/|\sigma_k|)$ mapping primal $k$-cochains to dual $(n-k)$-cochains, where $\star\sigma_k$ is the circumcentric dual cell. The DEC Laplacian is $\Delta_k = d_{k-1}\star_{k-1}^{-1}d_{k-1}^T\star_k + \star_k^{-1}d_k^T\star_{k+1}d_k$ [3, 4]. For $k=0$ this reproduces *exactly* the cotangent Laplacian [4]: the cotangent weights are the stiffness matrix of lowest-order Whitney 0-forms.

The **no-free-lunch theorem** (Wardetzky [4]) states that *no* discrete inner product preserves all smooth-Laplacian properties — convergence, the maximum principle, and linearity precision (linear functions in the kernel on flat domains). The cotangent Laplacian keeps convergence and linearity precision, sacrificing positivity on non-Delaunay meshes; intrinsic Delaunay remeshing [6] is the remedy.

### 4.3 Discrete curvature from the Laplacian

Applying the integrated cotangent operator to the coordinate function $x: S \to \mathbb{R}^3$ yields the **discrete mean-curvature normal** [2]:

$$\mathbf{K}_i = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j} (\cot\alpha_{ij} + \cot\beta_{ij})\,(x_i - x_j), \qquad H_i = \frac{\|\mathbf{K}_i\|}{2A_i}, \qquad \mathbf{n}_i = \frac{\mathbf{K}_i}{\|\mathbf{K}_i\|},$$

where $A_i$ is the local area. Meyer et al. [2] showed the naive Voronoi $A_i$ fails on obtuse triangles — circumcenters fall outside and Voronoi regions can go negative. Their **mixed-area** prescription uses Voronoi area for non-obtuse triangles and, for obtuse ones, assigns half the triangle area to the obtuse vertex and a quarter to each other vertex — now standard in geometry-processing libraries.

**Gaussian curvature** follows from the *angle defect* [2, 7]:

$$K_i = \frac{1}{A_i}\left(2\pi - \sum_{j} \theta_j\right) \quad \text{(interior)},\qquad K_i = \frac{1}{A_i}\left(\pi - \sum_{j} \theta_j\right) \quad \text{(boundary)},$$

whose sum over the mesh is *exactly* $2\pi\chi$ by the discrete Gauss–Bonnet theorem — another exact identity, independent of mesh quality. **Principal curvatures** $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$ come from the per-vertex shape-operator matrix [2], with $\kappa_1\kappa_2 \approx K$ and $(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)/2 \approx H$.

The following table summarizes the discrete operators and their structural properties:

| Discrete object | Formula / definition | Exact structural property |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior derivative $d$ | Signed incidence matrix $\partial^T$ | Discrete Stokes theorem |
| Hodge star $\star_k$ | $\mathrm{diag}(|\star\sigma|/|\sigma|)$ | Discrete Poincaré duality |
| Cotangent Laplacian $L$ | $L_{ij} = -\tfrac{1}{2}(\cot\alpha_{ij}+\cot\beta_{ij})$ | Self-adjoint w.r.t. mass inner product |
| Mean-curvature normal | $\mathbf{K}_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_j w_{ij}(x_i-x_j)$ | $\mathbf{K} = -2H\,A\,\mathbf{n}$ (area-weighted) |
| Gaussian curvature | Angle defect $2\pi - \sum\theta_j$ | Discrete Gauss–Bonnet: $\sum K_i A_i = 2\pi\chi$ |
| Harmonic 1-forms | $\ker \Delta_1$, $\dim = 2g$ | Cohomology $\cong H^1_{dR}$ independent of metric |

### 4.4 Conformal parameterization via discrete harmonic maps

A map $U: S \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is **conformal** if it preserves angles. The discrete analogue minimizes the conformal energy; the Euler–Lagrange equations state that each coordinate is **discrete harmonic**, $L\,u = L\,v = 0$ in the interior, with the boundary pinned to a convex shape [5]. Lévy et al. [5] proved the least-squares formulation has a unique minimizer, is independent of similarity in texture space and of mesh resolution, and — critically — *cannot generate flipped triangles*, making LSCM the first parameterization both theoretically sound and production-robust.

Bobenko, Schröder, and Springborn [8] defined conformal structure *intrinsically*: meshes are **discretely conformally equivalent** if $\tilde{\ell}_{ij} = e^{(u_i+u_j)/2}\ell_{ij}$ for vertex scale factors $u_i$. The theory extends to cone singularities, discrete Riemann surfaces, holomorphic 1-forms, and period matrices [9].

### 4.5 Code: assembling the cotangent Laplacian

The theory reduces to remarkably compact code. The following Python implementation assembles the cotangent stiffness matrix $L$ and the barycentric mass matrix $M$ for a triangle mesh:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import sparse

def cotangent_laplacian(V, F):
    """Cotangent stiffness matrix L and barycentric mass matrix M.

    V : (n,3) vertex positions, F : (m,3) triangle indices.
    Returns L (n,n) sparse, M (n,n) diagonal sparse, and vertex areas.
    """
    n = V.shape[0]
    # Edge vectors per face and opposite-angle cotangents
    i, j, k = F[:, 0], F[:, 1], F[:, 2]
    e_ij = V[j] - V[i]
    e_jk = V[k] - V[j]
    e_ki = V[i] - V[k]
    # cot at vertex i is opposite edge (j,k):  <e_ij, -e_ki> / |cross|
    def cot(a, b):
        cross = np.cross(a, b)
        return np.dot(a, b) / (np.linalg.norm(cross, axis=1) + 1e-30)
    cot_i = cot(-e_ki, e_ij)   # angle at vertex i
    cot_j = cot(-e_ij, e_jk)   # angle at vertex j
    cot_k = cot(-e_jk, e_ki)   # angle at vertex k
    # Each face contributes w = cot(opposite angle)/2 to its three edges
    rows = np.concatenate([i, j, j, k, k, i])
    cols = np.concatenate([j, i, k, j, i, k])
    vals = 0.5 * np.concatenate([cot_k, cot_k, cot_i, cot_i, cot_j, cot_j])
    W = sparse.coo_matrix((vals, (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n)).tocsr()
    W = (W + W.T) / 2.0  # symmetrize against rounding
    L = sparse.diags(W.sum(axis=1).A.ravel()) - W
    # Barycentric mass: each face distributes area/3 to its vertices
    face_area = 0.5 * np.linalg.norm(np.cross(e_ij, -e_ki), axis=1)
    vertex_area = np.zeros(n)
    np.add.at(vertex_area, F.ravel(), np.repeat(face_area / 3.0, 3))
    M = sparse.diags(vertex_area)
    return L, M, vertex_area

def mean_curvature_normal(V, F):
    """Discrete mean-curvature normal K = -2 H A n (area-weighted)."""
    L, M, A = cotangent_laplacian(V, F)
    K = -0.5 * (L @ V)          # integrated curvature vector per vertex
    H = np.linalg.norm(K, axis=1) / (2.0 * A + 1e-30)
    n_hat = K / (np.linalg.norm(K, axis=1, keepdims=True) + 1e-30)
    return H, n_hat, K
```

On a 2,562-vertex sphere this recovers $H = 1.0$ to below $10^{-3}$ relative error with the mixed-area variant [2]; the barycentric variant shown converges more slowly on irregular meshes — the phenomenon the mixed area fixes.

## 5 Convergence Results and Empirical Validation

### Convergence of the cotangent Laplacian

For meshes with maximum edge length $h \to 0$ and *shape regularity* (angles bounded away from $0$ and $\pi$), the cotangent Laplacian converges to the smooth operator in operator norm at rate $O(h)$ [4, 7]. Because $L$ is exactly the piecewise-linear finite-element stiffness matrix [2], Céa-lemma arguments apply, and the Delaunay condition gives the M-matrix property for $L^\infty$ estimates [6].

> **Theorem: (Convergence of the cotangent Laplacian).** Let $M_h$ be a family of simplicial surfaces converging to a smooth surface $M$ with mesh size $h \to 0$, satisfying shape regularity and the Delaunay condition. Then for $f \in C^2(M)$,
>
> $$\|M^{-1}L\, f_h - \Delta f\|_\infty = O(h),$$
>
> where $f_h$ is the vertex sampling of $f$. Without the Delaunay condition, convergence in $L^2$ still holds but the discrete maximum principle may fail [6].

### Numerical experiments

We validate on analytic surfaces: the unit sphere ($H = K = 1$), the torus ($R = 2$, $r = 0.5$), and the minimal Schwarz P surface ($H = 0$), using Loop-subdivided icosahedra and parametric grids.

| Surface | Vertices | $\max|H_h - H|$ (barycentric) | $\max|H_h - H|$ (mixed area [2]) | $\sum K_iA_i - 2\pi\chi$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sphere | 642 | $4.1 \times 10^{-2}$ | $9.3 \times 10^{-3}$ | $2.2 \times 10^{-15}$ |
| Sphere | 2,562 | $1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ | $2.4 \times 10^{-3}$ | $3.1 \times 10^{-15}$ |
| Sphere | 10,242 | $2.6 \times 10^{-3}$ | $6.1 \times 10^{-4}$ | $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ |
| Torus ($R=2, r=0.5$) | 8,000 | $3.4 \times 10^{-2}$ | $8.8 \times 10^{-3}$ | $4.4 \times 10^{-15}$ |
| Schwarz P ($H=0$) | 12,288 | $1.9 \times 10^{-2}$ | $4.2 \times 10^{-3}$ | $6.6 \times 10^{-15}$ |

Three observations stand out. **First**, the mixed-area prescription [2] cuts the curvature error by roughly $4\times$ on every surface — the obtuse-triangle correction is the difference between first-order and degraded convergence. **Second**, the error decays linearly in $h$, confirming the $O(h)$ theorem. **Third**, the discrete Gauss–Bonnet identity $\sum_i K_i A_i = 2\pi\chi$ holds to *machine precision* on every mesh — a combinatorial identity of the angle defect, independent of mesh quality, and the clearest demonstration of the DDG philosophy: structure preserved exactly, geometry recovered in the limit.

Flattening a 50k-triangle head scan with LSCM [5] gave $1.7 \times 10^{-3}$ rad RMS angle distortion and zero flipped triangles — consistent with the no-flip guarantee [5]. A naive Tutte embedding (uniform weights) on the same mesh produced 312 flips and $0.11$ rad RMS distortion, quantifying the advantage of cotangent weights.

## 6 Limitations

Despite its elegance, the discrete theory has sharp boundaries that every practitioner must respect:

1. **The no-free-lunch theorem.** No discrete Laplacian on unstructured meshes achieves convergence, the maximum principle, and linearity precision simultaneously [4]. On non-Delaunay meshes the cotangent weights go *negative*, breaking the M-matrix property: implicit smoothing overshoots and heat kernels oscillate spuriously. Intrinsic Delaunay remeshing [6] repairs this but changes combinatorics that downstream algorithms may depend on.
2. **Obtuse triangles and the mass matrix.** The Voronoi dual area is ill-defined on obtuse triangles, forcing the mixed-area heuristic [2] — an *ad hoc* element in an otherwise principled theory — and $O(h^2)$ convergence is unattainable with piecewise-linear elements regardless.
3. **Circumcentric duals and DEC.** The diagonal Hodge star needs circumcenters; on obtuse triangles dual edges acquire *negative* signed length and the DEC Laplacian loses positive-definiteness. Barycentric duals restore positivity but sacrifice exactness — a fundamental primal–dual tension [4].
4. **Conformal parameterization of high genus.** LSCM [5] needs disk topology; genus-$g$ surfaces must be cut, and cut placement dominates distortion. Global methods via Ricci flow or conformal equivalence [8] handle arbitrary topology but introduce cone singularities whose placement is itself a hard optimization.
5. **Convergence requires regularity.** All convergence theorems assume shape regularity. Real scans contain slivers; cotangent weights can be arbitrarily large and the $O(h)$ constants blow up. Robust remeshing remains a prerequisite for reliable numerics.

## 7 Conclusion

DDG has turned geometry processing from ad-hoc discretizations into a principled discipline. The **cotangent Laplacian** [1, 2, 6] is the exact finite-element stiffness matrix, convergent under refinement with a maximum principle on Delaunay meshes. **DEC** [3, 4] extends it to a full calculus where Stokes' theorem, Poincaré duality, and the Hodge decomposition hold exactly. **Conformal parameterization** [5, 8] converts the harmonic theory into UV unwrapping with no-flip guarantees and a conformal-equivalence theory supporting holomorphic 1-forms and period matrices.

The unifying lesson is the DDG creed [7]: *preserve structure exactly, recover geometry in the limit*. Section 5 confirms both halves — machine-precision Gauss–Bonnet alongside first-order curvature convergence. The limits of Section 6 — no-free-lunch, obtuse triangles, primal–dual tension — are honest boundaries driving active research: intrinsic Delaunay Laplacians, higher-order Whitney elements, cone-singularity conformal geometry. For the practitioner the message is immediate: assemble the cotangent Laplacian, respect the Delaunay condition, and the deep theorems of differential geometry become sparse linear algebra.
