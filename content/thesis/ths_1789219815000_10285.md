# De Novo Protein Design with Generative Diffusion Models: Backbone Hallucination, SE(3) Frame Diffusion, and Experimental Validation

## Abstract

The design of novel protein structures — *de novo* protein design — has undergone a phase transition from energy-function optimization to deep generative modeling. Diffusion models operating on residue frames in the special Euclidean group SE(3) now generate designable, diverse, and novel backbones with experimental success rates that exceed physically based approaches by orders of magnitude. This thesis develops the full stack: the differential geometry of diffusion on the SE(3) manifold, the RFdiffusion framework that fine-tunes the RoseTTAFold structure-prediction network into a generative denoiser [1], the ProteinMPNN inverse-folding network that converts backbones into sequences [2], and the all-atom extensions that model proteins together with ligands, metals, and nucleic acids [3]. We analyze why self-conditioning, non-invariant losses, and motif-conditioning are decisive for performance, present the experimental record — including a 2.9 Å cryo-EM structure of a designed influenza hemagglutinin binder and de novo small-molecule binders — and examine theoretical guarantees, failure modes, and open problems in the control, verification, and safety of generative molecular design.

## 1 Introduction

For half a century, the central dogma of computational protein design ran in one direction: given a target fold, search sequence space with a physics-based energy function — Rosetta — to find an amino acid sequence whose computed ground state approximates the target [2]. This pipeline produced landmark successes, yet its *experimental success rate* remained stubbornly low: designs frequently failed to express, fold, or function, and each design campaign demanded expert intervention and heroic experimental screening.

Two events broke this regime. First, deep learning solved structure prediction (AlphaFold2, RoseTTAFold), supplying a differentiable oracle that maps sequence to structure with near-experimental accuracy [7]. Second, diffusion models — the same machinery behind image generation — were transplanted into molecular space. The transplant was nontrivial: a protein is not an image. Its backbone is a chain of rigid-body frames, each residue contributing a rotation and translation, and any generative model must respect the SE(3) symmetry of physical space. The resolution, developed independently by several groups and culminating in RFdiffusion [1] and FrameDiff [5], was to diffuse *on the manifold itself*: Brownian motion on SO(3) for orientations, Gaussian diffusion in ℝ³ for translations, with SE(3)-equivariant score networks to reverse it.

> **Hypothesis:** The density of "designable" protein backbones — geometries realizable by some amino acid sequence — is learnable from the finite corpus of solved structures, and a diffusion model trained on that corpus can sample from it conditioned on arbitrary functional specifications (motifs, symmetries, binding epitopes).

This thesis argues that the hypothesis is essentially confirmed, and develops its consequences: how the geometry works, why particular architectural choices matter, what the experimental record shows, and where the framework still fails.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The protein design problem

A protein backbone of *N* residues is conventionally reduced to its Cα trace, or more precisely to *N* rigid frames **F**ᵢ = (**R**ᵢ, **t**ᵢ) with **R**ᵢ ∈ SO(3) and **t**ᵢ ∈ ℝ³, where the frame of residue *i* is constructed from its N–Cα–C atoms [5]. Designability of a backbone *X* = {**F**ᵢ} means there exists a sequence *S* ∈ {20 amino acids}ⁿ such that *X* is (approximately) the native state of *S*. The design problem factors classically into two stages:

1. **Backbone generation** — propose a novel, designable geometry.
2. **Inverse folding** — find sequences that fold into it [2].

Both stages are now deep-learned, and the coupling between them — validated by a third stage of *in silico* folding (AlphaFold2, ESMFold [4]) — defines the modern pipeline.

### 2.2 Denoising diffusion, briefly

A denoising diffusion probabilistic model defines a forward noising process *q*(**x**ₜ | **x**₀) that corrupts data into noise over *T* steps, and learns a reverse process *p*θ(**x**ₜ₋₁ | **x**ₜ) that denoises. The network typically predicts the clean data **x**₀ (or the added noise ε) from the noisy observation, minimizing an expected reconstruction loss. Sampling starts from pure noise and iterates the learned reverse kernel. The key design freedoms are the *state space*, the *noise schedule*, and the *parameterization* — and in protein design, all three required reinvention.

### 2.3 From hallucination to diffusion

The first deep generative protein designs used *hallucination*: optimizing a random sequence to maximize the confidence (pLDDT) of a structure-prediction network, effectively performing gradient ascent on the network's learned energy landscape [3]. Hallucination proved that prediction networks encode generative priors, but it was slow, mode-seeking, and hard to condition. Diffusion replaced optimization with sampling: rather than ascending a landscape, the model learns the landscape's gradient (the score) everywhere and integrates it. RFdiffusion [1] made the connection literal by fine-tuning RoseTTAFold itself as the denoiser.

> **Definition:** A *designable* backbone is one for which ProteinMPNN-designed sequences, when folded *in silico* by an independent predictor, recover the backbone with TM-score ≥ 0.5 and Cα-RMSD ≤ 2 Å [1].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The frame representation and SE(3) diffusion

Each residue is a frame in SE(3) = SO(3) ⋉ ℝ³. The forward process factorizes [5]:

- **Translations:** *q*(**t**ₜ | **t**₀) = 𝒩(**t**₀, σₜ² **I**), ordinary Gaussian diffusion in ℝ³.
- **Rotations:** *q*(**R**ₜ | **R**₀) = IGSO₃(**R**₀ · exp(**w** σₜ)), where **w** ∼ Uniform(SO(3)) and IGSO₃ is the isotropic Gaussian on SO(3) — Brownian motion on the rotation manifold.

At *t* = *T*, translations are an isotropic Gaussian cloud ("residue gas") and rotations are uniform; the reverse process must sculpt a coherent chain from this chaos. The denoiser is an SE(3)-equivariant network predicting the clean frames (**R̂**₀, **t̂**₀); equivariance guarantees that rotating the noisy input rotates the prediction identically, so the model cannot memorize absolute orientations — a physical symmetry is baked into the architecture rather than learned from augmentation.

```python
# Forward noising of one residue frame (schematic)
def forward_diffuse(R0, t0, t, T):
    sigma = noise_schedule(t, T)          # monotonically increasing
    t_t = t0 + sigma * randn(3)           # R^3 Gaussian diffusion
    w = uniform_so3_sample() * sigma      # Brownian motion on SO(3)
    R_t = R0 @ expm(skew(w))
    return R_t, t_t
# Reverse: network predicts (R0_hat, t0_hat); take a step toward it,
# then re-inject the noise level appropriate for t-1 (DDPM-style).
```

### 3.2 RFdiffusion: fine-tuning a predictor into a generator

RFdiffusion's decisive move was *not* training from scratch: it initialized the denoiser with RoseTTAFold's weights and fine-tuned on structure denoising [1]. RoseTTAFold's three-track architecture — 1D sequence, 2D pairwise, 3D coordinate tracks with SE(3)-Transformer updates — already reasons about protein geometry; fine-tuning repurposes that geometric competence for generation. Two further choices proved crucial [1]:

1. **Self-conditioning.** Inspired by recycling in AlphaFold2, the network is conditioned at each reverse step on its own prediction of the clean structure from the previous step, stabilizing long denoising trajectories.
2. **Non-invariant MSE loss.** The training loss on predicted frames is *not* invariant to the global reference frame; this seemingly unprincipled choice promotes continuity between adjacent timesteps and substantially improved *in silico* success rates.

### 3.3 Conditioning: motifs, symmetry, binders

Generation is steered by conditioning information injected during the reverse process:

| Conditioning mode | Specification | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional | Chain length only | Novel monomer folds |
| Motif scaffolding | Fixed functional residues (possibly discontiguous) | Enzyme active sites, epitopes |
| Symmetry | Cyclic/dihedral/icosahedral group | Nanoparticles, assemblies |
| Binder design | Target protein structure + hotspot residues | Therapeutics |

For binder design, the target is held fixed while the binder chain is diffused around it, with hotspot residues on the target biasing interfacial contacts. For motif scaffolding, the motif frames are noised on a slower schedule (or held fixed) while the scaffold is generated around them — solving 23 of 25 benchmark problems versus 15 for hallucination-based methods [1].

### 3.4 The design pipeline: generate → sequence → validate

A generated backbone is not yet a protein. The canonical pipeline is:

1. **RFdiffusion** samples backbones conditioned on the design task [1].
2. **ProteinMPNN** — a message-passing graph neural network for fixed-backbone inverse folding — designs 8–32 sequences per backbone at controlled sampling temperatures, achieving 52.4% native sequence recovery versus 32.9% for Rosetta [2].
3. **ESMFold/AlphaFold2** folds each designed sequence *in silico*; designs whose predicted structure matches the backbone (self-consistency TM-score) are advanced to experiment [4].

This *generate–sequence–validate* loop is the workhorse of the field; every stage is deep-learned, and the independent-validator stage is what keeps the generator honest.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The geometry of SE(3) diffusion and why the manifold matters

Diffusing Cα coordinates in ℝ³ — ignoring rotations — produces backbones whose local geometry (bond lengths, Ramachandran plausibility) must be repaired after the fact. The frame representation instead keeps the rigid N–Cα–C unit intact by construction: noise acts on *poses*, never on internal geometry. FrameDiff [5] supplied the principled foundations: an SE(3)-invariant forward process on multiple frames and an SE(3)-equivariant score network, proving that designable monomers up to 500 residues can be generated *without any pretrained structure-prediction network*. This matters theoretically — it shows the generative prior is learnable from structures alone — even though in practice RFdiffusion's fine-tuned predictor remains the stronger generator.

The choice of noise schedule interacts subtly with chain statistics. Translations diffuse with variance σₜ²**I** independent of residue index, so at high noise the chain is a Gaussian cloud with radius of gyration ∝ √*N*; the network must learn to collapse this cloud into a compact, hydrogen-bonded chain. Empirically, schedules that keep the chain partially collapsed at intermediate *t* yield higher designability — the network spends its capacity on secondary-structure packing rather than global compaction.

### 4.2 RFdiffusion's denoiser: what the fine-tuning actually transfers

RoseTTAFold was trained to predict structure from sequence and MSA; RFdiffusion fine-tunes it to *denoise* structure [1]. What transfers is the 3D track's geometric reasoning: invariant point attention (IPA) over residue frames, which updates each frame from its spatial neighbors. The 1D/2D tracks, originally fed by sequence and MSA, are repurposed to carry diffusion-timestep embeddings and self-conditioning inputs. Ablations in [1] show that random initialization collapses performance: the predictor's weights encode a prior over protein-like geometry — allowed torsion basins, packing densities, secondary-structure grammars — that pure denoising training on ~20k PDB chains cannot recover from scratch at comparable scale.

> **Theorem (informal, after [5]):** An SE(3)-invariant forward diffusion on *N* frames paired with an SE(3)-equivariant score network induces a generative distribution over backbones that is invariant to global rigid motions; consequently the model's capacity is spent entirely on *relative* geometry — the only physically meaningful degrees of freedom.

### 4.3 Inverse folding: ProteinMPNN and the sequence–structure gap

ProteinMPNN treats inverse folding as conditional generation on a residue graph: nodes carry backbone geometry, edges carry relative frame encodings, and an autoregressive (or order-agnostic) decoder emits amino acids [2]. Its robustness to *imperfect* backbones — trained with coordinate noise — is what makes it the universal downstream of diffusion generators: RFdiffusion backbones are idealized, and a sequence designer that demands crystallographic perfection would reject them all. The sampling temperature trades diversity for predicted confidence; the field's standard practice (temperature 0.1–0.3, 8+ sequences per backbone) reflects an empirical Pareto frontier between sequence recovery and designability [2].

A subtle point: ProteinMPNN's 52.4% sequence recovery is measured on *native* backbones. On generated backbones there is no native sequence; the operative metric is the *round-trip* self-consistency described in §3.4. This creates a methodological circularity — the validator (AlphaFold2) shares inductive biases with the generator's initialization (RoseTTAFold) — which is precisely why *experimental* validation, not *in silico* metrics, is the field's gold standard.

### 4.4 All-atom diffusion: ligands, metals, and nucleic acids

Backbone-only design cannot touch the majority of biology: enzymes bind small molecules, transcription factors bind DNA, metalloproteins coordinate ions. RoseTTAFold All-Atom (RFAA) extended the three-track architecture with 1D element-type, 2D bond, and 3D chirality tracks for arbitrary small molecules [3]. RFdiffusion All-Atom (RFdiffusionAA) then fine-tuned RFAA as a *conditional* denoiser: residue frames are diffused while the small molecule is held fixed, learning the distribution of proteins *conditioned on* biomolecular context. The model designed binders for haem, the cardiac drug digoxigenin, and the bilin chromophore — experimentally confirmed by a high-throughput *E. coli* screen in which bilins become fluorescent only when held in a designed pocket [3]. RFdiffusion2 further extended the frame representation to side-chain atoms, enabling *de novo* enzyme design with experimentally measured catalytic efficiencies approaching natural enzymes.

### 4.5 Flow matching and the efficiency frontier

Diffusion requires hundreds of reverse steps. FrameFlow [6] reframes backbone generation as *flow matching* on SE(3): instead of learning a score, the network learns a vector field transporting a base distribution to the data distribution along straight-line (geodesic) paths. The result: 5× fewer sampling steps with 2× better designability than FrameDiff [6]. This is not merely engineering — it suggests the diffusion SDE's stochasticity is inessential for this domain, and that deterministic transport on the manifold suffices. The efficiency gain compounds: cheaper sampling enables larger *in silico* screens, which is the dominant cost in real design campaigns.

## 5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

### 5.1 The experimental record

The RFdiffusion paper [1] experimentally characterized hundreds of designs across five task classes:

| Task | Scale tested | Success rate | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symmetric oligomers | 608 designs | 87 confirmed by SEC | nsEM |
| Motif scaffolding | 25 benchmarks | 23/25 solved | Functional assays |
| Binder design (5 targets) | 96 per target | ~19% | BLI / yeast display |
| Binder: influenza HA | HA_20 | atomic agreement | 2.9 Å cryo-EM, 0.63 Å backbone RMSD |
| Small-molecule binders | 3 ligands | confirmed subset | Fluorescence / binding assays |

The 19% binder success rate represents roughly a **100-fold improvement** over prior Rosetta-based binder design [1]. The cryo-EM structure of the designed binder HA_20 in complex with influenza hemagglutinin matches the computational model to 0.63 Å backbone RMSD — atomic-level agreement between a hallucinated protein and physical reality.

### 5.2 What the theory guarantees — and what it does not

The SE(3) formalism guarantees *invariance*, not *quality*. FrameDiff's theory [5] ensures the learned distribution respects physical symmetry; nothing in the diffusion objective bounds designability, novelty, or diversity. Empirically, designability correlates with:

- **pLDDT of the round-trip prediction** — the strongest single filter [1, 4];
- **Interface quality metrics** (for binders): predicted aligned error across the interface, buried surface area;
- **Novelty**: maximum TM-score to any PDB chain < 0.5 is the conventional "novel fold" threshold.

> **Hypothesis (scaling):** Designability and conditional controllability improve monotonically with denoiser capacity and training-set size, mirroring the scaling observed in protein language models [4]; the current frontier (RFdiffusion2, all-atom) is data-limited for protein–ligand complexes rather than capacity-limited.

No convergence or sample-complexity guarantees exist for SE(3) diffusion on proteins; the field operates, frankly, on experimental validation as its proof system.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Dynamics are absent.** Diffusion generates static structures; proteins function through conformational ensembles, allostery, and induced fit. Designing a *motion* — an enzyme's catalytic cycle, a transporter's alternating access — remains out of reach.
2. **The designability metric is circular.** Round-trip validation uses predictors sharing lineage with the generator. Adversarial backbones can fool AlphaFold2 while failing experimentally; the field lacks an independent, cheap, trustworthy oracle.
3. **Data scarcity for complexes.** RFAA's own authors note that protein–small-molecule structural data is orders of magnitude sparser than protein-only data, limiting RFdiffusionAA's generality [3].
4. **Membrane proteins and disorder.** The PDB training corpus underrepresents membrane proteins and intrinsically disordered regions; generated backbones inherit these biases, and designability collapses for transmembrane targets.
5. **Experimental throughput as the real bottleneck.** *In silico* generation is now essentially free; gene synthesis, expression, and biophysical characterization are not. The limiting reagent in 2026 is wet-lab bandwidth, not sampling steps.
6. **Dual-use.** The same pipeline that designs hemagglutinin binders could be steered toward harmful targets. Binder design against arbitrary proteins is now a commodity capability, and governance frameworks have not kept pace with the experimental success rates.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Generative diffusion on SE(3) has converted protein design from a craft of energy functions into a sampling problem with a learned prior — and the experimental record shows the prior is excellent. RFdiffusion [1], ProteinMPNN [2], and their all-atom successors [3] form a pipeline in which each stage is deep-learned and the whole is validated by independent folding oracles [4] and, decisively, by experiment. The theoretical contributions — principled diffusion on the frame manifold [5], flow matching as a faster transport [6] — are genuine advances in geometric generative modeling with lessons beyond biology.

The open frontier is *function*: from static backbones to dynamic machines, from binders to catalysts designed around transition states, from commodity generation to verified, safe deployment. The density of designable proteins appears to be learnable; the density of *useful* ones — conditioned on the full complexity of cellular context — is the problem of the next decade.
