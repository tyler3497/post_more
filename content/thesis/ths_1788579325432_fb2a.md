# SE(3) Diffusion and Flow Matching for Protein Backbone Generation: From RFdiffusion Fine-Tuning to Therapeutic Design Pipelines

## Abstract

The protein design problem — specifying amino-acid sequences that fold into prescribed three-dimensional structures with desired functions — has been transformed by generative models operating directly on SE(3)^N frame representations of protein backbones. This thesis develops the mathematical and computational foundations of diffusion-based generative protein design, tracing the lineage from AlphaFold2's invariant point attention (IPA) and frame-aligned point error (FAPE) loss through the fine-tuning of the RoseTTAFold structure prediction network into RFdiffusion, and onward to the simulation-free paradigm of Riemannian flow matching embodied by FrameFlow. We formalize denoising diffusion on SE(3) via Brownian motion on SO(3) and Gaussian perturbation of C-alpha coordinates, contrast score-matching diffusion with flow-matching vector-field regression, and analyze the critical design innovations — self-conditioning, non-invariant MSE denoising objectives, and the RFdiffusion-to-ProteinMPNN-to-AlphaFold2 validation triad.

---

## 1 Introduction

Protein design is the *inverse problem* of protein structure prediction. Where AlphaFold2 [1] maps sequence to structure with near-experimental accuracy, design requires inverting that map: given a desired backbone geometry **X** in SE(3)^N — a chain of *N* rigid-body frames, each encoding the C-alpha position and N–C-alpha–C orientation of a residue — find an amino-acid sequence **s** that folds into **X** and exhibits a target function such as high-affinity binding to a therapeutic target. Historically, this was the province of physics-based engines like Rosetta, requiring expert intervention, massive compute, and yielding success rates on the order of one in ten thousand designs.

Three developments jointly shattered that ceiling:

1. **High-fidelity structure predictors as oracles.** AlphaFold2 (Jumper et al., 2021) [1] and RoseTTAFold demonstrated that deep networks trained on the Protein Data Bank (PDB) can predict structures to atomic accuracy, and can therefore *validate* candidate designs in silico — the celebrated designability metric: does AlphaFold2 predict the designed sequence to fold into the designed backbone?
2. **Diffusion models on rigid-body frames.** Watson et al. (2023) [2] showed that fine-tuning the RoseTTAFold structure prediction network on a protein structure *denoising* task yields RFdiffusion, a generative model of protein backbones that solves — within one framework — unconditional monomer design, binder design, symmetric oligomer design, enzyme active-site scaffolding, and symmetric motif scaffolding.
3. **Inverse folding at scale.** ProteinMPNN (Dauparas et al., 2022) [3] converts generated backbones into sequences with 52.4% native-sequence recovery (versus 32.9% for Rosetta) and roughly 200-fold faster inference, completing the pipeline: **RFdiffusion (backbone) → ProteinMPNN (sequence) → AlphaFold2 (validation)**.

The pipeline's power was established experimentally: hundreds of designed symmetric assemblies, metal-binding proteins, and protein binders were characterized structurally and functionally, with a cryo-EM structure of a designed influenza haemagglutinin binder nearly identical to its computational model [2].

This thesis formalizes the generative-modeling core of this revolution. We develop diffusion and flow matching on the special Euclidean group SE(3), explain why the *frame representation* and *self-conditioning* are decisive architectural choices, and survey the rapid successor literature — notably FrameFlow (Yim et al., 2023) [4], which replaces diffusion's stochastic sampler with Riemannian flow matching to achieve a five-fold speedup and two-fold designability gain.

> **Theorem (Informal, Designability Decomposition):** Let p_backbone(X) be a generative backbone distribution, p_seq(s | X) an inverse-folding model, and v(X, s) the folding oracle mapping a sequence to its predicted structure. The end-to-end experimental success probability factors approximately as P_success ≈ P_designable · P_express · P_fold, where P_designable measures whether the folding oracle predicts the designed sequence to fold into the designed backbone. Improvements in the backbone prior p_backbone propagate multiplicatively through the pipeline.

This factorization explains why backbone generation — not sequence design — became the binding constraint, and why diffusion on SE(3) was the pivotal breakthrough.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Frame Representation of Protein Backbones

Following AlphaFold2 [1], each residue *n* is represented by a frame T^(n) = (r^(n), x^(n)) in SE(3), where r^(n) in SO(3) is a rotation constructed from the N, C-alpha, and C backbone atoms (Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization of the vectors along C-alpha–N and C-alpha–C bonds), and x^(n) in R^3 is the C-alpha translation. The full backbone is **T** = [T^(1), …, T^(N)] in SE(3)^N.

The frame representation confers two decisive advantages for generative modeling:

- **Rigid-body consistency.** Backbone bond lengths and angles are fixed by construction; the model reasons only in the 6N degrees of freedom that matter.
- **SE(3)-equivariance.** A network whose internal features transform correctly under global rotations and translations of the input cannot memorize absolute coordinates — it must learn *geometry*, generalizing across reference frames.

### 2.2 Invariant Point Attention and FAPE

AlphaFold2's structure module applies *invariant point attention* (IPA): attention weights are computed from scalar features while the aggregated values live in local 3D frames, making the output provably invariant to global rigid motions [1]. Training uses the *frame-aligned point error* (FAPE), which aligns predicted and true frames before measuring point distances — hence invariant to the global frame. IPA plus FAPE became the structural grammar reused, with modifications, by RFdiffusion and its successors.

### 2.3 Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

Denoising diffusion models define a forward noising process q(X_t | X_0) that corrupts data toward a tractable prior, and learn a reverse process p_theta(X_{t-1} | X_t) approximating q(X_{t-1} | X_t, X_0). Training minimizes a denoising objective E[||Xhat_0(X_t, t) − X_0||^2]. For Euclidean coordinates this is textbook DDPM. For protein backbones, the non-Euclidean part is the rotation manifold SO(3): forward noising of orientations proceeds by *Brownian motion on SO(3)* — an isotropic diffusion of the rotation matrix — and the reverse step learns to predict the clean frame.

### 2.4 RoseTTAFold and Fine-Tuning as a Generative Strategy

Rather than training a generative model from scratch, RFdiffusion fine-tunes the *RoseTTAFold* structure prediction network [2]. This is a striking transfer-learning claim: a network trained to map (sequence, MSA) to structure already encodes a strong prior over protein geometry; teaching it to *denoise* corrupted structures converts that discriminative prior into a generative model. The network's SE(3)-equivariant trunk, pre-trained on the PDB, provides precisely the inductive bias that score-matching on raw coordinates lacks.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Forward Noising on SE(3)^N

Training inputs are generated by noising PDB structures for up to 200 steps [2]:

- **Translations.** C-alpha coordinates receive isotropic 3D Gaussian noise with a variance schedule beta_t, exactly as in Euclidean DDPM.
- **Rotations.** Residue orientations undergo Brownian motion on SO(3), implemented via the isotropic Gaussian distribution on SO(3) (the IGSO(3) kernel) — sampling rotations whose geodesic distance from the identity grows with *t*.

The network receives the noisy frames **T**_t and the timestep *t*, and predicts the clean structure Xhat_0(**T**_t, t) — an SE(3)-equivariant denoiser.

```python
# Conceptual sketch: one RFdiffusion training step (PyTorch-like pseudocode)
# T0: (N, 4, 4) rigid frames sampled from the PDB
def training_step(model, T0, t):
    # 1. Forward noising: translations Gaussian, rotations Brownian on SO(3)
    x_t = gaussian_perturb(T0.translations, beta_schedule[t])
    r_t = igso3_perturb(T0.rotations, sigma_schedule[t])  # isotropic SO(3) diffusion
    T_t = Frames(x_t, r_t)
    # 2. Self-conditioning: reuse previous-step clean prediction (cf. AF2 recycling)
    X_prev = model.last_prediction.detach()  # self-conditioning input, stop-gradient
    # 3. Denoiser predicts clean structure WITHOUT frame alignment (MSE, not FAPE)
    X_hat = model(T_t, t, X_prev)
    # 4. Mean-squared error in the global frame: promotes timestep continuity
    loss = ((X_hat.atom_coords - T0.atom_coords) ** 2).mean()
    return loss
```

### 3.2 The Non-Invariant MSE Loss: A Deliberate Heresy

The most subtle methodological choice in [2] is the training objective. Structure-prediction training uses FAPE — invariant to global frame. RFdiffusion instead minimizes *un-aligned* mean-squared error between predicted and true atomic coordinates. Because MSE is **not** invariant to the global reference frame, the loss penalizes rigid drift between consecutive timesteps, forcing denoising trajectories to be *continuous* in the global coordinate frame. Empirically, this choice was one of two ablated features essential to in-silico success rates (the other being self-conditioning).

### 3.3 Self-Conditioning

Inspired by AlphaFold's recycling, the network is conditioned on its own prediction of the clean structure from the previous reverse step, Xhat_0(X_{t+1}, t+1). This provides a coarse target that stabilizes long trajectories: the model refines rather than re-solves at every step. During training, the self-conditioning input is computed with the network's own weights (with stop-gradient), mirroring the inference-time protocol [2].

### 3.4 Conditioning Mechanisms: From Unconditional to Functional Design

RFdiffusion handles a remarkable breadth of design tasks through conditioning on the noisy input itself [2]:

| Task | Conditioning strategy |
|---|---|
| Unconditional monomer | Pure noise; chain length specified |
| Topology constraints | Partially noised template; secondary-structure adjacency fixed |
| Motif scaffolding | Functional motif frames held fixed; only scaffold diffused |
| Binder design | Target structure provided as context; binder frames diffused against interface |
| Symmetric oligomers | Cyclic (C_n), dihedral (D_n), tetrahedral, octahedral, icosahedral symmetry applied to frames during sampling |
| Enzyme active-site scaffolding | Catalytic residues fixed; surrounding scaffold generated |
| Partial diffusion | Natural backbone noised to intermediate t, then denoised — diversifies while preserving fold |

Symmetry is enforced *during sampling* by averaging frame updates over the symmetry group — a clean exploitation of the SE(3) representation: symmetrization commutes naturally with rigid-body operations.

### 3.5 The Validation Triad: ProteinMPNN and AlphaFold2 in the Loop

A generated backbone is not yet a design. The pipeline closes the loop:

1. **ProteinMPNN** [3] designs sequences for the backbone (message-passing network on C-alpha-neighbor graphs; 52.4% native recovery, about 1 second per design).
2. **AlphaFold2** [1] (or ESMFold) predicts the structure of the designed sequence *without* the design backbone as input.
3. A design is *designable* if the predicted structure matches the designed backbone (typically RMSD below 2 angstroms and pLDDT above 80), and *novel* if dissimilar (TM-score below 0.5) to any PDB entry.

Only designs passing these filters advance to gene synthesis and experimental characterization — a filter that makes the roughly 20% end-to-end success rate in [2] remarkable, about a 100-fold improvement over prior Rosetta-based binder pipelines.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SE(3) Diffusion, Formalized

Let the data distribution be p_data(**T**) on SE(3)^N. Endow SE(3) with the product left-invariant metric, so that SE(3) behaves as the product manifold SO(3) × R^3 and geodesics decouple into rotation and translation components. The forward process on each residue's frame is a diffusion: standard Brownian motion on R^3 for translations and Brownian motion on SO(3) for rotations. At the final timestep the distribution is approximately the product of a wide Gaussian and the uniform (Haar) distribution on SO(3)^N.

The reverse process requires the *score* of the noised distribution, which the network approximates via the denoiser Xhat_0. Sampling discretizes the reverse process over 50–200 steps (RFdiffusion) — expensive, since each step evaluates a roughly 60M-parameter SE(3)-equivariant network. This cost is precisely what flow matching later attacks.

> **Definition (Forward SE(3) diffusion):** dT_t = −(1/2)β(t) T_t dt + sqrt(β(t)) dW_t, where W_t is Brownian motion on the product manifold: standard Brownian motion on R^3 for translations and Brownian motion on SO(3) for rotations. The reverse-time process is driven by the learned score, approximated through the equivariant denoiser.

### 4.2 From Score Matching to Flow Matching: FrameFlow

Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) is a *simulation-free* alternative to diffusion: instead of learning the score of a noised distribution, learn a vector field v_theta(**T**, t) whose integral curves transport a simple prior p_0 to the data distribution p_1. On Riemannian manifolds, the conditional flow-matching objective regresses v_theta against the closed-form conditional vector field of a geodesic interpolation between prior and data samples — no SDE simulation during training.

FrameFlow (Yim et al., 2023) [4] adapts this to SE(3)^N. With the product metric, the vector field decomposes per residue into translational and rotational components:

- **Translation:** vhat_R^(n)(**T**_t, t) = (xhat_1^(n)(**T**_t) − x_t^(n)) / (1 − t)
- **Rotation:** vhat_SO(3)^(n)(**T**_t, t) = log_{r_t^(n)}(rhat_1^(n)(**T**_t)) / (1 − t)

where (xhat_1, rhat_1) are the network's denoised predictions and log is the Riemannian logarithm on SO(3). Sampling integrates the learned ODE — deterministically — from t = 0 to 1.

Empirically, FrameFlow achieves **two-fold better designability** than its diffusion predecessor FrameDiff with **five-fold fewer sampling timesteps**, using a 16.8M-parameter model versus RFdiffusion's 59.8M [4]. It also reports a 23-fold sampling speedup over GENIE at higher designability. The lesson is sharp: the *generative paradigm* (diffusion vs. flow) matters less than the *geometric representation*, but simulation-free training and deterministic ODE sampling yield large practical wins.

### 4.3 Motif Scaffolding via Amortization and Guidance

Two strategies extend flow models to motif scaffolding (Yim et al., 2024) [6]:

- **Motif amortization:** train a *conditional* flow model with the motif as auxiliary input; during training only scaffold residues are corrupted. Data augmentation over all PDB motifs aids generalization.
- **Motif guidance:** repurpose an *unconditional* model by guiding motif residues toward their target positions along the ODE trajectory — a Bayesian posterior-sampling view analogous to classifier guidance in image diffusion.

Motif amortization wins on in-distribution motifs; guidance wins on flexibility, requiring no task-specific retraining.

### 4.4 ESMFold and the Language-Model Folding Loop

ESMFold (Lin et al., 2023) [5] replaces AlphaFold2's MSA-dependent Evoformer with the ESM-2 protein language model (up to 15B parameters): single sequence → ESM-2 embeddings → 48-block folding trunk → IPA structure module, yielding atomic-level structures at an order-of-magnitude speedup. It enabled the ESM Metagenomic Atlas of more than 617 million predicted structures. For design pipelines, ESMFold's speed makes it the workhorse *in-silico validator*: thousands of RFdiffusion backbones times ProteinMPNN sequences can be folded and filtered in hours rather than weeks, tightening the design–test loop by an order of magnitude [5].

### 4.5 Experimental Validation and Therapeutic Design

Watson et al. validated RFdiffusion designs at a scale unprecedented for de novo design [2]:

- **Symmetric assemblies:** 87 of 608 designed oligomers confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography, with negative-stain EM class averages matching design models.
- **Motif scaffolding:** 23 of 25 benchmark problems solved (versus 15 for prior hallucination methods).
- **Binders:** roughly 19% experimental success across five targets — including a designed binder to influenza haemagglutinin whose 2.9 angstrom cryo-EM structure matched the design at 0.63 angstrom backbone RMSD.
- **Metal-binding proteins:** symmetric motif scaffolding produced validated metal-coordination sites.

These numbers matter because they measure the *right* endpoint: not pLDDT or RMSD on a test set, but proteins that express, purify, and function. Binder design against therapeutic targets (viral antigens, cell-surface receptors such as the insulin receptor) is the direct on-ramp to biologics: RFdiffusion-generated binders are being developed as diagnostics, biosensors, and therapeutic candidates [7].

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Comparative Performance of Backbone Generators

| Method | Paradigm | Params | Sampling steps | Designability (relative) | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RFdiffusion [2] | SE(3) diffusion, fine-tuned RF | 59.8M | 50–200 | baseline | 19% binder success; cryo-EM validated |
| FrameDiff | SE(3) diffusion, from scratch | ~17M | 1000 | 0.5x FrameFlow | Proof of SE(3) diffusion concept |
| FrameFlow [4] | SE(3) flow matching | 16.8M | 100 (5x fewer) | 2x vs FrameDiff | 23x speedup vs GENIE |

| Motif-amortized FM [6] | Conditional flow matching | ~17M | 100 | task-dependent | Amortization vs guidance study |

### 5.2 The Self-Conditioning Ablation

Watson et al. ablate the two architectural innovations [2]: removing self-conditioning *or* replacing the global-frame MSE loss with FAPE each degrades in-silico success substantially; removing both collapses performance — establishing that *temporal coherence* of the denoising trajectory, not scale alone, drives the gains.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Equivariance of the Denoiser

> **Theorem (Equivariance preservation):** Let f_theta be an SE(3)-equivariant network mapping noisy frames **T**_t to clean-frame predictions Xhat_0. For any global rigid motion g in SE(3), f_theta(g·**T**_t, t) = g·f_theta(**T**_t, t). Consequently the learned reverse process is invariant to the global frame, and generated backbones are proper samples from a distribution on the quotient space SE(3)^N / SE(3).

*Proof sketch.* IPA layers are invariant by construction (attention over scalar pair features; outputs expressed in local frames) [1]; the frame-update heads apply predicted rigid transforms, which compose equivariantly. By induction over network blocks, the full trunk is equivariant. The reverse process driven by an equivariant vector field preserves the quotient structure, so samples are defined up to global rigid motion — exactly the physical symmetry of the problem.

### 5.4 Flow Matching Correctness on SO(3)

> **Theorem (Conditional flow matching on Riemannian manifolds, SE(3) special case):** Let the conditional path between prior sample T_0 and data sample T_1 be geodesic interpolation on SE(3) — spherical linear interpolation (slerp) on SO(3), linear interpolation on R^3. The conditional flow-matching loss E[||v_theta − u_t(·|T_1)||^2] has the same gradient as the intractable marginal flow-matching loss, so regressing the closed-form conditional field u_t trains v_theta to generate p_data [4].

This is the mathematical license for FrameFlow's simulation-free training: geodesics on the product manifold are available in closed form, so no SDE needs to be simulated to compute training targets.

## 6 Limitations

1. **PDB bias and the designability trap.** All models train on the PDB — a biased sample of soluble, crystallizable, evolutionarily successful proteins. "Designable" means "looks like the PDB to AlphaFold2"; genuinely novel physics (intrinsically disordered regions, membrane proteins, large conformational changes) lies outside the learned manifold. Designs inherit the PDB's blind spots.
2. **Backbone-only generation.** RFdiffusion and FrameFlow generate backbones; side chains are designed downstream by ProteinMPNN and validated by folding oracles. Joint all-atom generation (sequence plus side chains plus backbone) remains nascent, and catalytic geometry requiring sub-angstrom side-chain placement is still hard.
3. **Dynamics and flexibility.** The models generate *static* structures. Allostery, induced fit, and conformational ensembles — central to real protein function — are not modeled; binders are designed against a single rigid target conformation.
4. **Experimental attrition.** Even at roughly 20% success, four of five designs fail at expression, solubility, or binding. Failure modes (aggregation, off-target oligomerization) are poorly predicted in silico, and each design–build–test cycle costs weeks.
5. **Conditional control is coarse.** Topology and symmetry constraints are expressible; fine-grained functional specifications ("bind with K_d below 1 nM *and* cross the blood–brain barrier") are not yet directly conditionable — they emerge only through screening.
6. **Evaluation circularity.** Using AlphaFold2/ESMFold both to filter designs and to report designability risks optimistic bias: the validator shares the PDB prior with the generator. Experimental validation [2] remains the only non-circular metric.

## 7 Conclusion

The arc from AlphaFold2's frame representation [1] through RFdiffusion's fine-tuned SE(3) diffusion [2] to FrameFlow's Riemannian flow matching [4] constitutes one of the fastest translations of a mathematical idea — generative modeling on Lie groups — into experimental reality in the history of structural biology. The decisive insights were representational (frames, not coordinates), architectural (self-conditioning, IPA), and objective-level (the non-invariant MSE that enforces trajectory coherence). Around this core, ProteinMPNN [3] and ESMFold [5] supply the sequence-design and validation limbs of a pipeline that now produces experimentally confirmed binders, symmetric nanomaterials, and enzyme scaffolds at success rates two orders of magnitude above the Rosetta era.

Open frontiers are clear: all-atom co-generation of sequence and structure, modeling of conformational ensembles rather than static snapshots, conditioning on quantitative functional readouts, and breaking the PDB's inductive grip to access genuinely new protein physics. The mathematical machinery — diffusion and flow on Riemannian manifolds — is general; proteins were merely its first spectacular conquest.
