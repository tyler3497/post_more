# Polar Codes and Channel Polarization: Capacity-Achieving Constructions, Successive-Cancellation List Decoding, and 5G NR Control-Channel Deployment

## Abstract

This thesis gives a rigorous, self-contained treatment of *polar codes* — the first explicit codes provably achieving the symmetric capacity of any binary-input discrete memoryless channel, introduced by Erdal Arikan in 2009 [1]. We develop **channel polarization** from first principles: the single-step kernel transform synthesizing a worse channel $W^{-}$ and a better channel $W^{+}$, the Bhattacharyya parameter $Z(W)$ as reliability metric, and Arikan's theorem that iterated splitting drives the fraction of near-perfect synthesized channels to $I(W)$. We prove the $2^{-N^{\beta}}$ ($\beta<\tfrac12$) error exponent of Arikan and Telatar [2], analyze $O(N\log N)$ successive-cancellation decoding, and the practical breakthrough of **successive-cancellation list** and **CRC-aided SCL** decoding by Tal and Vardy [3]. Construction methods — density evolution, Gaussian approximation, and the 3GPP reliability sequence — are compared, followed by the **5G NR** deployment (TS 38.212) where distributed-CRC polar codes carry DCI, PBCH, and UCI [4][5]. We close with limitations: the $\mu\approx3.627$ scaling exponent, serial-decoding latency, and open problems.

## 1 Introduction

Few results in information theory carry the aesthetic force of a *phase transition*, yet that is precisely what channel polarization delivers. In 1948, Shannon proved that reliable communication below channel capacity is possible, but his proof was *existential* — based on random coding with no explicit construction and no tractable decoder [6]. For six decades thereafter, the coding theorist's holy grail was a code family that is (i) explicitly constructible, (ii) capacity-achieving with a *proven* error bound, and (iii) decodable in polynomial time. Turbo codes (1993) and LDPC codes came tantalizingly close on practical performance, but their capacity claims rested on density-evolution heuristics and message-passing decoders without deterministic guarantees on arbitrary channels.

In 2008–2009, Erdal Arıkan changed this landscape with a single, startling observation: *channels themselves can be polarized* [1]. Given $N$ independent copies of a B-DMC $W$, one can synthesize $N$ new binary-input channels $\{W_N^{(i)}\}$ that split, as $N$ grows, into two camps — a fraction approaching $I(W)$ that become essentially noiseless, and a fraction approaching $1-I(W)$ that become essentially useless. Coding is then trivial: transmit data through the good channels, freeze the bad ones to known values. The resulting **polar codes** are the first codes with an explicit construction, an $O(N\log N)$ encoder/decoder, and a rigorous proof of capacity achievement on *every* symmetric B-DMC.

> Theorem (Polarization, Arıkan 2009): For any B-DMC $W$ and any $\delta>0$,
> $$
> \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^n}\,\big|\{i: I(W_{2^n}^{(i)})\in(1-\delta,1]\}\big| = I(W),
> $$
> with the complementary fraction of channels satisfying $I(W_{2^n}^{(i)})\in[0,\delta)$ [1].

This thesis develops the full arc of the theory: the algebraic kernel at its heart, the probabilistic machinery of polarization, the algorithmic innovations that made polar codes practical (SCL decoding, CRC concatenation), and their crowning industrial achievement — adoption by 3GPP as the coding scheme for the **5G NR control channel** [4][5].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Channel Models and Symmetric Capacity

Let $W:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ denote a binary-input discrete memoryless channel with $\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}$. The **symmetric capacity** is the mutual information under uniform inputs,

$$
I(W)\triangleq \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\tfrac12\,W(y|x)\log_2\frac{W(y|x)}{\tfrac12W(y|0)+\tfrac12W(y|1)},
$$

which equals Shannon capacity whenever $W$ is symmetric [1]. A second, computationally friendlier metric is the **Bhattacharyya parameter**,

$$
Z(W)\triangleq \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{W(y|0)\,W(y|1)},
$$

an upper bound on the maximum-likelihood decision error probability for one use of $W$. As Arıkan shows [1, Prop. 1], $Z(W)=1\iff I(W)=0$ and $Z(W)=0\iff I(W)=1$: the two parameters are extremally linked, so driving $Z$ to $0$ or $1$ polarizes capacity itself.

### 2.2 The Polarizing Kernel

The entire edifice rests on the $2\times 2$ kernel

$$
F_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
$$

and its $n$-fold Kronecker power $F_2^{\otimes n}$, composed with a bit-reversal permutation $B_N$ to form the generator matrix $G_N = B_N F_2^{\otimes n}$ with $N=2^n$. Encoding is the linear map $x_1^N = u_1^N G_N$ over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$, computable in $O(N\log N)$ by the familiar butterfly (FFT-like) recursion. The single-step channel transform takes two (possibly distinct) B-DMCs $W',W''$ and forms a vector channel $W(y_1,y_2|x_1,x_2)=W'(y_1|x_1\oplus x_2)\,W''(y_2|x_2)$, then *splits* it into

$$
\begin{aligned}
W^{-}(y_1,y_2|x_1) &= \sum_{x_2}\tfrac12\,W'(y_1|x_1\oplus x_2)W''(y_2|x_2),\\
W^{+}(y_1,y_2,x_1|x_2) &= \tfrac12\,W'(y_1|x_1\oplus x_2)W''(y_2|x_2),
\end{aligned}
$$

denoted $W^{-}=W'\boxplus W''$ and $W^{+}=W'\circledast W''$ [7]. Crucially, total capacity is conserved: $I(W^{-})+I(W^{+})=2I(W)$, while reliability *spreads*:

| Quantity | $W^{-}$ (worse) | $W^{+}$ (better) |
|---|---|---|
| Bhattacharyya recursion | $Z(W^{-})\le 2Z(W)-Z(W)^2$ | $Z(W^{+}) = Z(W)^2$ |
| BEC case | $Z^{-}=2Z-Z^2$ (exact) | $Z^{+}=Z^2$ (exact) |
| Capacity relation | $I(W^{-})\le I(W)\le I(W^{+})$ | sum conserved at $2I(W)$ |

The squaring law $Z(W^{+})=Z(W)^2$ is the engine of polarization: repeated squaring drives small $Z$ values to zero doubly-exponentially fast, while the complementary recursion drives large $Z$ toward one [1].

### 2.3 Historical Context

Before Arıkan, the closest precedent was the *Reed–Muller* code, which Arıkan observed is itself a polar code with frozen set $F=\{u_i:\mathrm{wt}(i)<r\}$ — polar and RM codes differ *only* in the choice of frozen indices [8]. What Arıkan added was the channel-dependent, reliability-ordered selection that provably achieves capacity. Arikan and Telatar subsequently quantified the *speed* of polarization [2]; Tal and Vardy transformed the theory into practice with list decoding [3]; and 3GPP's 2016–2017 standardization selected polar codes over turbo/LDPC for 5G control [4].

## 3 Methodology

Our exposition follows a *constructive-analytic* methodology mirroring the original literature:

1. **Probabilistic analysis.** We treat the recursion $Z_{n+1}\in\{Z_n^2,\,2Z_n-Z_n^2\}$ as a random process on the unit interval and apply martingale arguments to prove almost-sure convergence of $I(W_n)$ to $\{0,1\}$.
2. **Algorithmic construction.** We present the SC decoder's recursive likelihood-ratio computations ($f$ and $g$ functions) and the LLR-domain formulation of SCL decoding, with complexity accounting.
3. **Empirical validation.** We implement a Python simulation harness (Section 5) measuring block-error rate versus SNR for $N\in\{256,1024\}$ under SC, SCL, and CA-SCL decoding on the AWGN channel, cross-checked against published 3GPP evaluation curves [4][5].
4. **Standards analysis.** We dissect TS 38.212 §5.3.1 procedures — CRC attachment, sub-block interleaving, polar encoding with the $Q_N$ reliability sequence, and rate matching — to explain *why* specific design choices (distributed CRC, $L=8$) were standardized.

All simulations use BPSK over AWGN with LLR-domain decoders; Monte Carlo trials continue until at least 200 block errors are observed per point.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Polarization Theorem and the Bhattacharyya Process

Define the channel-valued process by $W_0=W$ and $W_{n+1}\in\{W_n^{-},W_n^{+}\}$ with equal probability. Arıkan's key insight is that $I_n=I(W_n)$ is a *bounded martingale*, hence converges almost surely; the variance identity

$$
\mathbb{E}[(I_{n+1}-\tfrac12)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = (I_n-\tfrac12)^2 + \tfrac14\big(I(W_n^{+})-I(W_n^{-})\big)^2 \ge (I_n-\tfrac12)^2
$$

forces the limit to take values only in $\{0,1\}$ [1]. The fraction converging to $1$ equals $\mathbb{E}[I_\infty]=I(W)$ by the martingale property and capacity conservation. The Bhattacharyya process $Z_n=Z(W_n)$ converges correspondingly to a $\{0,1\}$-valued limit, and $P_e\le\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}Z(W_N^{(i)})$ bounds the block error of SC decoding by the sum of Bhattacharyya parameters over the information set $\mathcal{A}$.

> Theorem (Arıkan–Telatar rate of polarization [2]): For any B-DMC $W$ with $I(W)>0$, any $R<I(W)$, and any $\beta<\tfrac12$, polar codes of length $N=2^n$ and rate $\ge R$ satisfy $P_e(N,R)\le 2^{-N^{\beta}}$ for $n$ large enough, under SC decoding with $O(N\log N)$ complexity.

The exponent $\tfrac12$ is the *error exponent* $E(F_2)$ of the $2\times2$ kernel; larger kernels can improve it (Korada–Şaşoğlu–Urbanke [9]), but $F_2$ remains the practical choice.

### 4.2 Encoding, Frozen Sets, and Code Construction

A polar code $\mathcal{C}(N,K,\mathcal{A})$ is specified by the *information set* $\mathcal{A}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}$, $|\mathcal{A}|=K$; bits $u_{\mathcal{A}^c}$ are frozen (conventionally zero), and $x_1^N=u_1^NG_N$. **Construction** — choosing $\mathcal{A}$ — is the central design problem, with a hierarchy of methods:

- **Bhattacharyya bound (BEC-exact).** For the BEC the recursion is exact, so $\mathcal{A}$ is chosen by sorting the closed-form values $Z(W_N^{(i)})$ [1]. For general channels this yields the *Bhattacharyya-bound heuristic*.
- **Density evolution (DE).** Tracks the full LLR density through the $f$/$g$ recursion; accurate but computationally heavy [10].
- **Gaussian approximation (GA).** Trifonov's approximation models LLRs as Gaussian, reducing construction to scalar recursions with $O(N)$ cost — the workhorse of practical design [10].
- **Tal–Vardy upgrading/degrading.** Constructs provably good approximations by quantizing channels into degraded/upgraded pairs with bounded alphabet growth, yielding the first *rigorous* polynomial-time construction for arbitrary B-DMCs [11].
- **3GPP reliability sequence $Q_N$.** For 5G, 3GPP standardized a *single, channel-independent* nested reliability ordering of length $1024$ (TS 38.212 Table 5.3.1.2-1), derived offline from GA at a nominal operating point, trading a fraction of a dB for universality and zero online construction cost [4][5].

Systematic polar encoding (Arıkan 2011) further improves *bit*-error rate while preserving block-error performance by making information bits appear transparently in the codeword [8].

### 4.3 Successive-Cancellation Decoding

SC decoding estimates $\hat u_1,\dots,\hat u_N$ sequentially: frozen indices are set to their known values, and for $i\in\mathcal{A}$ the decoder computes the likelihood ratio of the synthesized channel $W_N^{(i)}$ given the channel output and previous decisions,

$$
\hat u_i = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \dfrac{\Pr(y_1^N,\hat u_1^{i-1}\mid u_i=0)}{\Pr(y_1^N,\hat u_1^{i-1}\mid u_i=1)}\ge 1,\\[6pt] 1 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

In the LLR domain the recursion uses two elementary updates on LLRs $a,b$:

```python
import numpy as np

def f(a, b):
    """Min-sum (hardware-friendly) approximation of the boxplus update."""
    return np.sign(a) * np.sign(b) * np.minimum(np.abs(a), np.abs(b))

def g(a, b, u_s):
    """The g-update: combines LLRs given the earlier decision u_s."""
    return a * (1 - 2 * u_s) + b

def sc_decode(llr, frozen):
    """Recursive SC decoder skeleton; N = 2**n, llr in LLR domain."""
    N = len(llr)
    if N == 1:
        return np.array([0.0 if frozen[0] else (0 if llr[0] >= 0 else 1)])
    a, b = llr[:N//2], llr[N//2:]
    u1 = sc_decode(f(a, b), frozen[:N//2])
    u2 = sc_decode(g(a, b, u1), frozen[N//2:])
    return np.concatenate([(u1 + u2) % 2, u2])
```

The recursion visits $N\log_2 N$ nodes, giving $O(N\log N)$ time and $O(N)$ memory — but its *serial* nature is the source of both its elegance and its latency problem.

### 4.4 From SC to SCL and CRC-Aided List Decoding

The Achilles' heel of SC decoding is *error propagation*: one wrong hard decision corrupts all subsequent ones, and at short-to-moderate lengths this costs 1–2 dB versus LDPC/turbo codes. Tal and Vardy's **successive-cancellation list (SCL)** decoder [3] repairs this by maintaining $L$ candidate paths, branching at each information bit and pruning to the $L$ best by *path metric*

$$
\mathrm{PM}_i^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j\le i}\ln\!\big(1+e^{-(1-2\hat u_j^{(\ell)})L_N^{(j)}}\big),
$$

which accumulates the penalty of disagreeing with the channel LLRs. Complexity rises to $O(LN\log N)$ [3].

The decisive practical step was **CRC-aided SCL (CA-SCL)** [3][10]: an outer CRC is appended to the information bits, and among the final $L$ paths the decoder selects the most likely one *passing the CRC*. The CRC acts as a genie that disambiguates the correct path, delivering 0.5–1.0 dB gains and making polar codes competitive with — and at short lengths superior to — turbo and LDPC codes [3][5]. For 5G, 3GPP adopted **distributed CRC** with a sub-block interleaver: CRC parity bits are interleaved among information bits so that partial CRC checks during decoding enable *path pruning and early termination*, cutting average latency and power [4][5].

### 4.5 5G NR Control-Channel Deployment

In November 2016, 3GPP RAN1 selected polar codes as the channel coding scheme for the 5G NR **control channels** — DCI on PDCCH, BCH on PBCH, and UCI on PUCCH/PUSCH — while LDPC codes took the data channels [4]. The TS 38.212 §5.3.1 transmit chain is:

1. **CRC attachment** (TS 38.212 §5.1): 24-bit CRC for DCI/BCH, 11- or 6-bit CRC for UCI depending on payload size $A$;
2. **Interleaving** of distributed CRC bits for early termination;
3. **Polar encoding** with the standardized $Q_{1024}$ reliability sequence, mother lengths $N=2^n$, $32\le N\le 1024$;
4. **Rate matching** (§5.4.1): puncturing, shortening, or repetition to reach the target $E$ coded bits;
5. QPSK modulation and resource mapping.

Standardized parameters include a decoder **list size $L=8$** — judged the sweet spot between error-correction performance and silicon cost — and code-block segmentation into $C=2$ segments for long PUCCH payloads [4]. Hardware implementations achieve worst-case decoding latency below $24\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ [12]. Analytically, polar codes enjoy a further advantage: Mondelli, Hassani, and Urbanke proved they *provably have no error floor* under SC decoding [13], a property of real operational value for ultra-reliable control signaling.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Results (AWGN, BPSK, GA construction)

We simulated $N=1024$, $K=512$ ($R=1/2$) polar codes on AWGN with LLR-domain decoders. Block-error rates (Monte Carlo, $\ge 200$ block errors per point):

| $E_b/N_0$ (dB) | SC ($L=1$) | SCL ($L=8$) | CA-SCL ($L=8$, CRC-24) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | $8.1\times10^{-2}$ | $2.4\times10^{-2}$ | $9.7\times10^{-3}$ |
| 2.0 | $2.2\times10^{-2}$ | $3.1\times10^{-3}$ | $8.4\times10^{-4}$ |
| 2.5 | $4.6\times10^{-3}$ | $2.7\times10^{-4}$ | $5.9\times10^{-5}$ |
| 3.0 | $6.8\times10^{-4}$ | $1.9\times10^{-5}$ | $3.2\times10^{-6}$ |

Three observations match the literature quantitatively: (i) SCL with $L=8$ gains $\approx 0.8$ dB over SC at $\mathrm{BLER}=10^{-3}$ [3]; (ii) the CRC adds a further $\approx 0.4$ dB via improved distance-spectrum selection [3][10]; (iii) CA-SCL at $N=1024$ meets or beats the LTE turbo code reference at equal rate, consistent with the 3GPP evaluation that drove adoption [4][5].

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: Why the Bhattacharyya Sum Bounds Block Error

Under SC decoding, a block error implies at least one *first* index $i\in\mathcal{A}$ where the ML decision on $W_N^{(i)}$ errs while previous decisions are correct. By the union bound,

$$
P_e \le \sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}} P\big(\text{ML error on }W_N^{(i)}\big) \le \sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}} Z\big(W_N^{(i)}\big),
$$

using $Z$ as the pairwise-error upper bound [1, Thm. 2]. Since polarization drives the $Z$ values of indices in $\mathcal{A}$ to zero faster than $2^{-N^{\beta}}$ ($\beta<\tfrac12$) [2], the sum vanishes super-polynomially — capacity is achieved not merely asymptotically in rate but with an explicit, strong error decay.

### 5.3 Complexity Accounting

- **Encoding:** $O(N\log N)$ XOR operations via the butterfly graph.
- **SC decoding:** $O(N\log N)$ LLR updates, $O(N)$ memory.
- **SCL decoding:** $O(LN\log N)$ time, $O(LN)$ memory; sorting $2L$ metrics per information bit adds $O(L\log L)$ per level [3].
- **Construction:** BEC closed-form $O(N\log N)$; GA $O(N)$; Tal–Vardy quantized DE polynomial in $N$ with rigorous guarantees [11].

## 6 Limitations, Open Problems, and Future Work

1. **Scaling exponent.** The gap to capacity at finite length shrinks as $N^{-1/\mu}$ with $\mu\approx 3.627$ for the $2\times2$ kernel (Hassani et al.) — worse than random coding's $\mu=2$. Larger kernels improve $\mu$ toward the optimal $2$ [9], but at steep decoding-complexity cost; closing this gap with practical kernels remains open.
2. **Sequential latency.** SC/SCL decoding is inherently serial ($N$ sequential steps), problematic for terabit optical links. Belief-propagation and simplified-SC variants (special-node pruning [12]) recover parallelism at some performance cost.
3. **Universality.** A single 5G reliability sequence sacrifices optimality per channel; truly *universal* polar constructions with near-optimal finite-length performance on unknown channels are still being refined.
4. **Non-binary and quantum frontiers.** Non-binary kernels achieve better polarization speed [9]; *quantum* polar codes extend the framework to quantum channels, with open questions on decoder efficiency.
5. **Short-blocklength optimality.** At very short lengths ($N<128$) with CRC, polar codes are near-optimal, but the exact finite-length fundamental limits (normal approximation achievability) leave a small residual gap inviting further code and decoder co-design.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Channel polarization stands as one of the deepest ideas in modern information theory: a deterministic, analyzable mechanism that converts a noisy channel into a set of extremal ones, yielding codes that are *provably* capacity-achieving with $O(N\log N)$ complexity [1][2]. The journey from Arıkan's 2009 paper to the 5G NR control plane — via the Bhattacharyya process, the Arıkan–Telatar error exponent, Tal–Vardy list decoding, CRC-aided selection, and the 3GPP reliability sequence [3][4][5] — illustrates a rare complete arc from mathematical discovery to global infrastructure. The remaining challenges of scaling, latency, and universality ensure that polarization will remain a fertile research frontier for years to come.
