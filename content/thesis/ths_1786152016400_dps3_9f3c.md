# Differentiable Physics Simulation with Contact: Linear Complementarity, Barrier Methods, and Implicit Differentiation for Robot Learning

## Abstract
Differentiable physics promises to turn simulation into a first-class learning substrate for robotics, providing analytic gradients of trajectories with respect to actions, states, and physical parameters. The core obstruction is contact: a nonsmooth, set-valued phenomenon with combinatorial mode switches. This thesis synthesizes three threads that define modern differentiable contact — *hard contact* via **linear and nonlinear complementarity problems (LCP/NCP)**, *soft-barrier contact* via **Incremental Potential Contact (IPC)** and log-barrier energies, and *gradient generation* via **implicit differentiation**. We provide unified notation, derive IFT-based gradients for both LCP and barrier formulations, analyze failure modes in gradient quality, and benchmark tradeoffs for trajectory optimization, policy optimization, and system identification.

---

## 1 Introduction
Robot learning is dominated by **contact-rich** tasks: locomotion, dexterous manipulation, assembly, and tool use. While model-free reinforcement learning succeeds by ignoring model derivatives, it pays with sample inefficiency and poor generalization. Differentiable simulation [1][2][3] offers an alternative: treat the simulator $s_{t+1} = f(s_t, u_t; \theta)$ as a differentiable layer.

For smooth dynamics this is trivial via automatic differentiation. For contact, three questions dominate:

1.  How to model *non-interpenetration* and Coulomb friction in a way that is both physically accurate and tractable?
2.  How to guarantee *intersection-free* trajectories under large timesteps and sharp geometry?
3.  How to obtain *useful* gradients through an otherwise nondifferentiable event?

> Theorem: *Gradient Usefulness vs. Physical Fidelity Tradeoff.* For any rigid contact model satisfying Signorini-Fichera non-penetration $0 \le \phi \perp \lambda_n \ge 0$, the exact Clarke Jacobian $\partial f/\partial u$ is almost everywhere sparse or zero under strict complementarity, while any $C^1$ relaxation that yields dense informative gradients must introduce $O(\kappa)$ constraint violation for barrier stiffness $\kappa < \infty$.

This thesis argues there is no single best contact model for differentiation; there are *operating points* on a Pareto front. We characterize them via three families: **LCP-based hard contact** [2], **convex-optimization / NCP second-order cone models** [3], and **barrier / IPC models** [4][5][6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Contact Dynamics as Complementarity
Time-stepping schemes for rigid bodies typically solve for post-impact velocities $v^+$ and contact impulses $\lambda$ Given generalized mass $M$, contact Jacobian $J$, bias forces $h$, and timestep $h_t$:

$$ M (v^+ - v^-) = J^T \lambda + h_t \tau $$

subject to normal complementarity:

$$ 0 \le \phi(q) \perp \lambda_n \ge 0 $$

and Coulomb friction: $\|\lambda_t\| \le \mu \lambda_n$, with *maximal dissipation* principle [3]. Stacking all contacts yields an *NCP*; polyhedral approximation of the friction cone linearizes it to an *LCP*:

$$ 0 \le w = A z + b \perp z \ge 0 $$

where $z$ concatenates $\lambda_n$ and friction multipliers. Solvers include Projected Gauss-Seidel (Tiny Differentiable Simulator [2]), Lemke, and Dantzig's algorithm extended for frictional LCP [1].

### 2.2 Incremental Potential Contact
IPC [4][5][6] replaces constraints by a $C^2$ barrier energy:

$$ B(d, \hat{d}) = \begin{cases} -(d-\hat{d})^2 \log(d/\hat{d}) & 0<d<\hat{d} \\ 0 & d \ge \hat{d} \end{cases}$$

with $d$ unsigned distance, $\hat{d}$ activation radius. Total incremental potential $E(q) = \frac{1}{2}\|q-\tilde{q}\|^2_M + h_t^2 \Psi(q) + \kappa \sum_{k} B(d_k(q))$ is minimized with filtered line search and continuous collision detection (CCD) to guarantee *non-intersection* [4]. Differentiable IPC [5] inherits this robustness while remaining smooth.

### 2.3 Why Gradients Fail
Zhong et al. [1] show four families — LCP, convex optimization, compliant, and position-based dynamics (PBD) — produce *incorrect* or vacuous gradients under naive autodiff. Two dominant sources:

*   **Time-of-Impact (TOI) discontinuity:** loss $l(q(t_{TOI}))$ is nondifferentiable when TOI crosses a timestep boundary unless CCD is differentiated.
*   **Complementarity-induced sparsity:** if $\phi > 0$, $\lambda_n = 0$, then $\partial \lambda_n / \partial q = 0$ almost everywhere, blocking learning that *making* contact would help.

## 3 Methodology

We consider a unified view: *solve* then *differentiate* via KKT.

### Formulation
All forward models solve $c(z^*; \theta)=0$ where $z^*$ are contact impulses / positions, $\theta = (q, v, u, \mu, M)$.

*   **LCP/NCP**: $c$ includes stationarity, $0 \le w \perp z \ge 0$, and SOC constraints $\|\lambda_t\| \le \mu \lambda_n$ [3].
*   **Barrier**: $c = \nabla E(z^*) = 0$.

We obtain gradients $dz^*/d\theta$ via **implicit differentiation**:

$$ \frac{\partial c}{\partial z^*} \frac{dz^*}{d\theta} + \frac{\partial c}{\partial \theta} = 0 \implies \frac{dz^*}{d\theta} = -\left(\frac{\partial c}{\partial z^*}\right)^{-1} \frac{\partial c}{\partial \theta} $$

This avoids unrolling solver iterations, which Brax and Warp [7] historically did with autodiff, incurring memory $O(T \cdot Iter)$ and biased gradients from truncated iterations.

### Central-Path Smoothing
Dojo [3] introduces a primal-dual interior-point method solving SOCP to tolerance $\epsilon$, with barrier parameter $\kappa_{IP}$. Instead of differentiating at $\kappa_{IP} \to 0$ (hard contact), differentiate at $\kappa_{IP}=10^{-4} \dots 10^{-1}$ to obtain *smoothed* gradients that remain feasible yet informative. Similarly, IPC smoothing uses $\hat{d}$ and barrier stiffness $\kappa$ as continuation parameters [4].

### Complementarity-Aware Gradients
Werling et al. [2] propose complementarity-aware gradients: when $z_i = 0, w_i > 0$, instead of $0$, use heuristic $dz_i/d\theta$ that would *activate* constraint if beneficial, avoiding saddle plateaus in trajectory optimization.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 LCP and NCP Contact Formulations for Differentiability
LCPs are attractive because they model *exactly* stick-slip transitions. NimblePhysics / DART [2] shows 87x speedup of analytic LCP gradients over finite differences by exploiting sparsity: active set size $m_a \ll m$.

Key properties:

*   **Correctness:** Under strict complementarity and non-degenerate $A_{\alpha\alpha}$, IFT gives exact Jacobians.
*   **Complexity:** Solving LCP is NP-adjacent worst-case, but with warm-start PGS or Dantzig, amortized $O(m_a^3)$ per solve.
*   **Limitation:** Non-uniqueness of Coulomb friction (Painlevé paradox) leads to *gradient selection* problem. Dojo [3] argues NCP with *second-order cone* preserves isotropy, while polyhedral LCP introduces artificial anisotropy biased by cone faceting.

| Model | Friction | TOI Handling | Grad Sparsity | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LCP (polyhedral) [2] | 4-8 facets | Backtracking CCD [1] | High without CA | Legged TO, Dexterous PGS |
| NCP-SOCP (Dojo) [3] | Exact cone | Variational integrator | Medium (IP smoothing) | Policy learning, sysID |
| Convex QP (MuJoCo) | Elliptic cone | Convex | Low | MJX/Brax RL |

*Table 1: Hard contact families.*

### 4.2 Barrier Energies and IPC for Intersection-Free Differentiability
Barrier methods rewrite hard constraints as *soft-but-guaranteed* potentials. Original IPC [4][6] guarantees intersection-free by combining:

1.  **Barrier activation** at $\hat{d}$ > 0 to create repulsive field before contact.
2.  **Continuous CCD** line-search filter rejecting steps causing penetration.
3.  **Lagged friction** with smoothed friction potential $D(v_t)$.

For differentiability, Huang et al. differentiable IPC achieves $C^2$ barrier with friction smoothing, enabling gradient-based inverse design of soft robots and garment draping.

**Orientation-aware IPC** [5] fixes artifact where distance-based barriers create spurious forces in rest configurations for non-parallel edges. It introduces *candidate-set* formalism: barrier depends on both distance and normal alignment, satisfying four desiderata: barrier property, locality, differentiable shape dependence, and *zero force at rest*.

For robot learning, barrier stiffness $\kappa$ controls tradeoff:

*   $\kappa \to \infty$: approaches hard contact but Hessian $\nabla^2 B \sim O(d^{-2})$ ill-conditioned → Newton CG iterations $\propto \kappa$.
*   $\kappa$ small: smooth gradients, fast solves, but noticeable *contact softness* and sinking.

### 4.3 Implicit Differentiation: IFT vs Unrolled Autodiff
Unrolled differentiation differentiates every solver iteration in JAX / Warp [7]. Pros: simple with `jax.grad`, GPU-parallel. Cons: *memory* $O(K)$, *biased* if stopped early, *unstable* for iterative complementarity solvers due to nonsmooth projections.

Implicit differentiation solves linear system at optimum:

```python
import jax.numpy as jnp
def implicit_lcp_grad(A_star, dA_dtheta, db_dtheta, active):
    # A_star = d c / d z at solution, active set
    # Solve adjoint: A_star^T * g = -dl/dz
    # Per [2][3] exploit sparsity of active LCP
    jac_active = jnp.linalg.solve(A_star[active][:, active], 
                                  -db_dtheta[active])
    dz = jnp.zeros_like(db_dtheta)
    dz = dz.at[active].set(jac_active)
    # complemented by complementarity-aware heuristic if active empty
    return dz
```

DojoLight [3] reports >10x reduction in complexity by merging collision detection and dynamics into single-level problem, avoiding bilevel implicit diff where $\partial p_{contact}/\partial q$ from GJK is non-unique for parallel faces.

Rust-style implementation for IPC barrier Hessian-vector product, leveraging $C^2$ property for Newton:

```rust
// Barrier HVP for IPC-style contact, per [4][5]
fn barrier_hvp(d: f64, dhat: f64, kappa: f64, grad_d: &Vec3, hvp_dir: &Vec3) -> (f64, Vec3) {
    if d >= dhat { return (0.0, Vec3::zeros()); }
    let t = d / dhat;
    // b = -(d-dhat)^2 * ln(d/dhat)
    let b_val = kappa * (dhat - d).powi(2) * (-t.ln());
    let db_dd = kappa * (2.0*(dhat - d)*t.ln() - (dhat-d).powi(2)/d);
    let d2b_dd2 = kappa * (2.0*t.ln() - 4.0*(dhat-d)/d + (dhat-d).powi(2)/(d*d));
    let grad = db_dd * grad_d;
    let hvp = d2b_dd2 * grad_d.dot(hvp_dir) * grad_d; // + db_dd * H_d * dir (omitted)
    (b_val, hvp)
}
```

> Theorem: *IFT Equivalence Under Strict Complementarity.* If LCP solution $z^*$ satisfies strict complementarity and $A_{\alpha\alpha}$ invertible on active set $\alpha$, then $dz^*/d\theta$ from IFT equals the limit of unrolled PGS gradients as iteration count $K \to \infty$ and TOI handling is $C^1$.

### 4.4 Smoothing, TOI, and Complementarity-Aware Gradients for Learning
Practical robot learning requires navigating two failure modes identified in [1]:

*   **TOI missing:** Without TOI, a ball tunneling through plane yields $\partial l/\partial v_0$ sign-flipped, converging to *two-bounce* local minimum. Solution: *continuous-time* collision detection whose $t_{impact}$ is differentiable via $\phi(q(t_{impact}))=0$ IFT [1][4].
*   **Zero gradients when separated:** Complementarity-aware gradient [2] injects $A_{\beta \alpha}$ coupling term when $w_i>0$ to suggest closing gap.

Training pipeline:

1.  Forward simulate with $\kappa_{small}$ (Dojo central path) or $\hat{d}_{large}$ (IPC) for stability.
2.  Compute IFT gradients at intermediate $\kappa$.
3.  Anneal $\kappa \to 0$ during optimization curriculum.
4.  Optionally randomize contact stiffness in MJX / Brax for policy robustness, analogous to implicit terrain in differentiable locomotion [2][3].

| Smoothing Strategy | Physical Error | Gradient Density | SysID Accuracy | Policy Transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard LCP exact [2] | 0 | 5-10% dense | High (w/ CA) | Poor w/o smoothing |
| Dojo IP $\kappa=1e-2$ [3] | $1e-3$m | 60% dense | Medium-High | Good |
| IPC barrier $\hat{d}=1e-3$ [4] | $\hat{d}/2$ | 90% dense | Low (soft bias) | Good, safe |
| Compliant (Brax) [1] | $k_p^{-1}F$ | 100% dense | Low | Sim-to-real gap |

*Table 2: Smoothing tradeoff empirically.*

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

We analyze benchmarks from Dojo, Nimble, and IPC suites [2][3][4].

**5.1 Gradient correctness:** On 2D bouncing ball optimal control (find $v_0$ achieving height $h^*$), models *without* TOI [1] yield $\partial l/\partial v_0$ error up to 180% and converge to wrong trajectory mode (Trajectory 2 vs 1). Adding TOI + IFT reduces error to <3% vs finite differencing of continuous-time physics.

**5.2 Performance:** NimblePhysics [2] reports single-core analytical Jacobian $87\times$ faster than finite diff for 1 timestep of Atlas humanoid with 4 contacts. DojoLight [3] reports order-of-magnitude complexity improvement over bilevel collision+ dynamics. Warp [7] GPU parallelization achieves 500 envs at 50 FPS for Laikago with TDS CUDA backend, enabling ARS rollouts entirely on GPU.

**5.3 Policy learning:** DiffLoco-style quadruped trained in MJX with smoothed contact achieves zero-shot hardware transfer [2], first demonstration for differentiable simulation. Key was *contact-scaled Ornstein-Uhlenbeck foot force randomization* and *tilted gravity slopes* for implicit terrain, compensating for MJX's flat-plane contact differentiability limits.

**5.4 Intersection-free guarantee:** IPC [4][6] maintains condition number 100x smaller than barrier-free augmented Lagrangian under high compression (empty table compression test), with CCD guaranteeing no tunneling even under $h_t = 1/30$s, critical for thin-shell robot grippers [5][6].

Proof sketch for Theorem (IFT equivalence):

1.  Under strict complementarity, active set $\alpha$ constant in neighborhood of $\theta$.
2.  Then LCP reduces to linear system $A_{\alpha\alpha} z_\alpha + b_\alpha=0$.
3.  Unrolled PGS converges to $z_\alpha = -A_{\alpha\alpha}^{-1} b_\alpha$ at linear rate $\rho<1$ if $A$ is P-matrix.
4.  Differentiating truncated iteration yields series $dz^{(K)}/d\theta = -\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} (I-A)^k db/d\theta$, whose limit = $A^{-1} db/d\theta$ = IFT solution.

## 6 Limitations

*   **Nonsmoothness is inherent.** IFT and barrier smoothing provide *informative* rather than *correct* subgradients; for multi-contact dexterous manipulation with >20 contacts, gradient variance remains high and sampling-based smoothing (e.g., randomized smoothing) may outperform deterministic IFT [1][3].
*   **Friction linearization bias.** Polyhedral LCP underestimates rotational friction and introduces drift under yaw; exact cone NCP [3] mitigates but requires SOCP solver less mature on GPU than PGS [7].
*   **Collision geometry differentiation.** Single-level optimization [3] improves over bilevel, but non-uniqueness for edge-edge parallel cases still yields discontinuous $\partial p_c/\partial q$. Log-barrier smoothing of narrow-phase [DojoLight] helps but adds tuning.
*   **Stiffness vs. efficiency** Barrier $\kappa$ and $\hat{d}$ induce ill-conditioning; preconditioned nonlinear CG [4] or sparse Cholesky in DiffPD [6] alleviate but scale poorly to full humanoid scenes with 10k+ triangles.
*   **Sim-to-real:** Compliant smoothing improves optimization landscape at cost of physical fidelity; direct transfer of policies trained in overly soft contact fails for high-impact tasks like jumping.

---

## 7 Conclusion
Differentiable physics with contact sits at the intersection of optimization, nonsmooth analysis, and robotics. This synthesis shows *LCP/NCP*, *barrier/IPC*, and *implicit differentiation* are not competing, but complementary layers:

*   Use **LCP/NCP** when physical accuracy and exact stick-slip matter and active set sparsity can be exploited [2][3].
*   Use **IPC-barrier** when intersection-free guarantees and codimensional (cloth, rods) coupling dominate, accepting softness during learning [4][5][6].
*   Use **implicit differentiation** in both cases, differentiating an intermediate relaxed KKT / central-path problem rather than unrolled solver iterates, with TOI-aware CCD and complementarity-aware heuristics for robot learning [1][2][3].

Future work: learned contact models (e.g., ContactNets) that regress $\phi, J, \mu$ from data while retaining complementarity structure; adaptive annealing schedules for $\kappa, \hat{d}$ co-optimized with policy learning; and hardware-accelerated SOCP interior-point solvers in Warp/JAX for real-time differentiable MPC with 100+ contacts.
