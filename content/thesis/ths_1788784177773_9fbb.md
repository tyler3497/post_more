# DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande: Neutrino Oscillation Physics, Mass Ordering, and CP Violation

## Abstract

The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) and Hyper-Kamiokande (HK) are the two flagship long-baseline neutrino oscillation facilities of the coming decade, tasked with resolving the neutrino mass ordering and discovering leptonic CP violation. DUNE sends a wideband 1.2 MW (upgradeable beyond 2 MW) beam from Fermilab over a 1300 km baseline to four 17 kton liquid-argon TPC far-detector modules at SURF, exploiting strong matter effects to determine the ordering at 5σ within 1–3 years of operation [2][3]. Hyper-Kamiokande, a 258 kton (187 kton fiducial) water-Cherenkov detector at Kamioka, receives a 2.5° off-axis narrowband beam peaking at 600 MeV from J-PARC over 295 km; with 27×10²¹ protons on target over 10 years it expects ≈10,000 νμ and ≈2,000 νe quasi-elastic events and can exclude CP conservation at 5σ for over 60% of δCP values [1]. This thesis develops the three-flavor oscillation formalism with matter effects, analyzes the octant–ordering–CP degeneracy structure, and quantifies the projected sensitivities of both experiments, showing that their combination — wideband long-baseline versus narrowband short-baseline, LArTPC versus water Cherenkov — breaks the degeneracies that limit each experiment alone.

## 1. Introduction

The 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics, awarded for the discovery of neutrino oscillations, established that neutrinos possess non-zero mass — the first confirmed departure of particle physics from the minimal Standard Model [5]. Over two subsequent decades, solar (SNO, Borexino), reactor (KamLAND, Daya Bay, RENO, Double Chooz), atmospheric (Super-Kamiokande), and accelerator (K2K, MINOS, T2K, NOvA) experiments measured the three mixing angles θ₁₂, θ₁₃, θ₂₃ and the two mass-squared splittings Δm²₂₁ and Δm²₃₁ with increasing precision, converging on the *three-flavor oscillation paradigm* [6]. Yet three fundamental unknowns remain:

1. The **neutrino mass ordering**: is the third mass eigenstate heavier (normal ordering, NO) or lighter (inverted ordering, IO) than the first two?
2. **Leptonic CP violation**: is the Dirac phase δCP different from 0 or π, so that νμ→νe oscillations differ from ν̄μ→ν̄e?
3. The **θ₂₃ octant**: is sin²θ₂₃ above or below 1/2?

These are not independent questions. In matter, the *Mikheyev–Smirnov–Wolfenstein (MSW) effect* generates an asymmetry between neutrino and antineutrino appearance probabilities that mimics genuine CP violation, with the sign of the matter-induced asymmetry flipped between orderings [7]. Disentangling the two effects is the central experimental challenge of the next generation, and it is precisely why two experiments with *deliberately different* systematics and baselines are required.

DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande embody complementary strategies. DUNE maximizes the matter effect with a 1300 km baseline and a wideband beam spanning the first and second oscillation maxima, at the cost of a complex, multi-interaction-channel event sample reconstructed in LArTPCs. Hyper-Kamiokande minimizes matter contamination with a 295 km baseline and a narrowband beam tuned to the first oscillation maximum, trading matter sensitivity for a clean, statistics-dominated νe appearance measurement in which quasi-elastic scattering dominates [1][6]. This thesis argues that neither experiment alone optimally resolves all three unknowns, and that their *joint* analysis — together with atmospheric-neutrino and reactor constraints — constitutes the definitive program for completing the leptonic mixing matrix.

> **Thesis claim.** The DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande programs, by combining a matter-dominated wideband measurement at 1300 km with a statistics-dominated vacuum-like measurement at 295 km, achieve mass-ordering determination at 5σ for all δCP values and CP-violation discovery at ≥3σ for a majority of δCP values, while controlling the ordering–octant–CP degeneracies that afflict either experiment in isolation.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The three-flavor framework

Neutrino flavor eigenstates |να⟩ (α = e, μ, τ) are related to mass eigenstates |νi⟩ (i = 1, 2, 3) by the Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata (PMNS) matrix U: |να⟩ = Σi U*αi |νi⟩. In the standard parameterization, U is written as a product of three rotations and a CP-violating phase δCP. Oscillation probabilities in vacuum follow from interference of the mass eigenstates:

> **Theorem (vacuum oscillation probability).** For ultra-relativistic neutrinos of energy E propagating a distance L, the flavor-transition probability is
>
> P(να→νβ) = δαβ − 4 Σi>j Re[UαiU*βiU*αjUβj] sin²(1.27 Δm²ij L/E) + 2 Σi>j Im[UαiU*βiU*αjUβj] sin(2.54 Δm²ij L/E),
>
> where Δm²ij ≡ m²i − m²j is in eV², L in km, and E in GeV. The imaginary term changes sign for antineutrinos and vanishes if δCP = 0 or π.

> **Proof.** Expand the evolved state |ν(L)⟩ = Σi U*αi e^(−im²iL/2E) |νi⟩, take |⟨νβ|ν(L)⟩|², and separate real and imaginary parts of the quartic products of mixing-matrix elements. The CP-odd term is proportional to the *Jarlskog invariant* J = c₁₂s₁₂c₂₃s₂₃c²₁₃s₁₃ sin δCP, which is the universal measure of CP violation in the lepton sector. ∎

Current global-fit best values (NuFIT 5.x–6.0 era) are summarized in Table 1 [6][8].

| Parameter | Best fit (NO) | Best fit (IO) | Precision driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| sin²θ₁₂ | 0.304–0.307 | 0.304–0.307 | Solar + KamLAND |
| sin²θ₁₃ | 0.0218–0.0220 | 0.0220–0.0222 | Daya Bay, RENO |
| sin²θ₂₃ | 0.57–0.58 | 0.57–0.58 | T2K, NOvA, SK |
| Δm²₂₁ (10⁻⁵ eV²) | 7.42 | 7.42 | KamLAND |
| Δm²₃₁ (10⁻³ eV²) | +2.52 | −2.50 | T2K, NOvA |
| δCP | ∼197°–230° | ∼278°–290° | T2K, NOvA |

Table 1. Representative global-fit values of the oscillation parameters. The δCP and octant entries remain the least constrained; values shown are indicative of recent fits.

### 2.2 Matter effects

In matter, coherent forward scattering of νe on electrons adds a potential V = ±√2 GF Ne (plus for neutrinos, minus for antineutrinos) to the Hamiltonian [7]. This *MSW effect* modifies the effective mixing angles and mass splittings, resonantly enhancing νe appearance for NO (and ν̄e appearance for IO) at long baselines. At DUNE's 1300 km baseline, the matter-induced asymmetry in P(νμ→νe) reaches ≈40% at the first oscillation maximum — comparable to the maximal CP-induced asymmetry — while at Hyper-Kamiokande's 295 km baseline the matter contribution is an order of magnitude smaller [4][7]. The complementarity is therefore structural: DUNE *must* disentangle matter from CP effects spectrally, whereas Hyper-Kamiokande measures a nearly pure CP asymmetry but requires external ordering information, supplied by its atmospheric-neutrino sample [1].

### 2.3 Predecessor experiments

T2K (295 km, off-axis, Super-Kamiokande far detector) demonstrated the off-axis narrowband technique and reported the first indications of CP violation, excluding CP-conserving values at 90% C.L. for certain δCP ranges. NOvA (810 km, off-axis, segmented scintillator) provided the first long-baseline data with significant matter effects, sharpening the ordering–octant tension: the two experiments' mild disagreements on δCP and the ordering are precisely the degeneracies the next generation must resolve. Daya Bay's sub-percent measurement of θ₁₃ (sin²2θ₁₃ ≈ 0.085) anchors the appearance probability scale for both DUNE and HK [6].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Beamlines and baselines

**DUNE / LBNF.** Protons from the Fermilab Main Injector (upgraded via PIP-II) strike a graphite target; pions and kaons are charge-selected by magnetic horns and decay in a 194 m decay pipe, producing a wideband νμ (or ν̄μ) beam with energies 0.5–8 GeV peaking near 2.5 GeV — covering both the first (2.6 GeV) and second (0.9 GeV) oscillation maxima at 1300 km. Initial beam power is 1.2 MW, with a planned upgrade beyond 2 MW. The near detector complex, 574 m downstream, comprises ND-LAr (a modular pixel-readout LArTPC), ND-GAr (a high-pressure gaseous-argon TPC in a magnetic field), and the on-axis SAND monitor, together constraining the unoscillated flux and cross sections [2][3].

**Hyper-Kamiokande / J-PARC.** 30 GeV protons strike a graphite target; the 2.5° off-axis geometry exploits two-body pion-decay kinematics to produce a narrowband beam peaking at ≈600 MeV — the first oscillation maximum at 295 km — while suppressing the high-energy tail and the intrinsic νe contamination to below 1%. The J-PARC Main Ring is being upgraded from 515 kW (2021) toward 1.3 MW near the start of HK data taking, via increased repetition rate (2.48 s → 1.16 s cycle) [6][8].

### 3.2 Far detectors

The detector technologies could hardly be more different, and the difference is the point.

| Feature | DUNE far detector | Hyper-Kamiokande |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | Liquid-argon TPC | Water Cherenkov |
| Modules | 4 × 17 kton | 1 × 258 kton (187 kton fid.) |
| Location | SURF, 4850 ft underground | Tochibora mine, 650 m overburden |
| Readout | Wire planes / pixels + X-ARAPUCA light | 20,000 × 50 cm PMTs (20% coverage) + 800 mPMTs |
| Strength | mm-scale tracking, e/γ separation | Huge mass, superb sub-GeV lepton ID |
| Beam energy | 0.5–8 GeV wideband | ≈0.6 GeV narrowband |

Table 2. Detector comparison. The LArTPC gives DUNE image-like event reconstruction across multiple interaction channels (quasi-elastic, resonant, deep-inelastic); HK's enormous mass gives it unmatched statistics in the quasi-elastic-dominated sub-GeV regime [2][5].

### 3.3 Analysis strategy

Both experiments perform a simultaneous spectral fit of νμ (ν̄μ) disappearance and νe (ν̄e) appearance samples, with oscillation parameters extracted via a binned likelihood marginalizing over flux, cross-section, and detector systematics constrained by near-detector data. The crucial methodological element is *spectral shape*: because matter and CP effects distort the energy spectrum differently — matter shifts the overall appearance normalization with a characteristic energy dependence, while δCP modulates the spectrum around the oscillation maximum — a wideband beam with good energy resolution can separate them [3][4].

A minimal numerical illustration of the vacuum appearance probability, the leading term both experiments fit, is:

```python
import numpy as np

def p_nue_appx(E_GeV, L_km, dcp_rad, s22t13=0.085, s2t23=0.97):
    """Leading-order nu_mu -> nu_e appearance probability (vacuum)."""
    Dm31 = 2.52e-3  # eV^2
    Dm21 = 7.42e-5
    D31 = 1.27 * Dm31 * L_km / E_GeV
    D21 = 1.27 * Dm21 * L_km / E_GeV
    lead = s22t13 * (s2t23 / 4.0) * np.sin(D31) ** 2
    # CP-odd interference term ~ sin(delta) * sin(D31) * sin(D21)
    J = 0.5 * np.sqrt(s22t13) * 0.5 * np.sin(dcp_rad)
    cp_odd = 8 * J * np.sin(D31) * np.sin(D21) * np.cos(D31)
    return lead + cp_odd

for E, L, label in [(2.6, 1300, "DUNE 1st max"), (0.6, 295, "HK 1st max")]:
    pne = p_nue_appx(E, L, -np.pi/2)
    print(f"{label}: P(nu_mu->nu_e) ~ {pne:.4f} at delta_CP=-90 deg")
```

The full analyses replace this approximation with exact three-flavor matter propagation (e.g., via Prob3++/GLoBES engines) folded through Geant4 detector simulations and CNN-based event selection [2].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The PMNS framework and the Jarlskog invariant

The magnitude of observable CP violation in oscillations is set by the Jarlskog invariant J ≈ 0.033 sin δCP — nearly three orders of magnitude larger than its quark-sector analogue (Jquark ≈ 3×10⁻⁵). If δCP is near-maximal (±90°), the νe appearance rate differs from ν̄e by tens of percent, well within reach of both experiments' statistics. The T2K and NOvA hints favor δCP ≈ −90° (NO) — the maximal-CP-violation scenario in which Hyper-Kamiokande can claim 5σ discovery in under three years given known ordering [1]. The Dirac phase is also the leading candidate source for *leptogenesis*: the same CP violation, transmitted through heavy right-handed neutrino decays in the early universe, may explain the cosmic matter–antimatter asymmetry — making δCP arguably the most consequential unmeasured parameter in particle physics.

### 4.2 Matter effects and the mass-ordering asymmetry

The matter potential introduces an effective shift Δm²₃₁ → Δm²₃₁ ± 2√2 GF Ne E, resonant when the vacuum and matter terms are comparable. For DUNE (L = 1300 km, ⟨ρ⟩ ≈ 2.8 g/cm³), the resonance sits squarely in the beam energy window, generating a ≈40% ordering-dependent asymmetry in the appearance rate. The projected sensitivity is striking: DUNE reaches 5σ mass-ordering determination in ~1 year of staged operation for the most favorable δCP, and within ~3 years even for the least favorable value — i.e., 5σ for *100%* of δCP values at only 70 kt·MW·yr exposure [3]. Hyper-Kamiokande's accelerator beam has weak ordering sensitivity, but its atmospheric-neutrino sample — upward-going multi-GeV neutrinos traversing the Earth's core — experiences the MSW resonance in the mantle and core, yielding independent ordering sensitivity that, combined with the beam data, breaks the δCP–ordering degeneracy [1][7].

### 4.3 Degeneracies: octant, ordering, and CP

Three discrete ambiguities haunt long-baseline fits:

1. **The octant degeneracy**: P(νμ→νμ) disappearance measures sin²2θ₂₃, symmetric under θ₂₃ ↔ π/2 − θ₂₃; appearance adds a sin²θ₂₃ factor that resolves it only with sufficient statistics.
2. **The ordering–δCP degeneracy**: (NO, δCP) and (IO, π − δCP) produce nearly identical appearance spectra at short baselines.
3. **The generalized hierarchy–octant–CP degeneracy**: an eightfold ambiguity in the full parameter space.

DUNE's wideband spectrum resolves (2) through the distinct energy dependence of matter versus CP effects; HK resolves (1) through high-statistics appearance plus atmospheric data, excluding the wrong octant at >3σ for 0.47 < sin²θ₂₃ < 0.55 [7]. The *combined* DUNE+HK fit collapses the degenerate manifold: studies show the joint analysis reduces the allowed (δCP, θ₂₃) volume dramatically and suppresses fake CP violation induced by non-unitarity or non-standard interactions, which neither experiment disentangles alone [7].

### 4.4 Complementarity: wideband versus narrowband

The design philosophies encode a deliberate division of labor:

- **DUNE**: high matter effect ⇒ unambiguous ordering; wideband beam ⇒ second-maximum coverage where the CP asymmetry is enhanced by a factor ≈3 relative to the first maximum, but diluted by lower flux; LArTPC ⇒ reconstruction of hadronic final states across CCQE, resonant, and DIS channels, enabling energy reconstruction over 0.5–8 GeV.
- **Hyper-Kamiokande**: low matter effect ⇒ clean CP asymmetry; narrowband beam ⇒ quasi-elastic dominance, minimizing cross-section model dependence at the cost of a single-L/E measurement; water Cherenkov ⇒ 187 kton fiducial mass, yielding the statistics (≈2,000 νe events) that make the measurement systematics-limited rather than statistics-limited after 10 years [1][5].

Neither strategy dominates the other across all parameters; together they span the space. This is the central methodological insight of the global program: *two baselines, two beams, two detector technologies* — a hedge against correlated systematics and model assumptions that a single mega-experiment cannot provide.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

No oscillation data from DUNE or HK exists yet — DUNE far-detector commissioning and HK operation are targeted for the late 2020s (HK construction began 2020, operation expected 2027 [8]) — so "empirical results" here means the experiments' sensitivity projections, derived from full simulation chains and documented in their Technical Design Reports and refereed sensitivity papers. These projections are the evidentiary basis on which the program was funded and approved.

**DUNE projections.** Under the collaboration's staging scenario [2][3]:

> **Theorem (DUNE ordering reach).** With the LBNF wideband beam and 40 kt fiducial LArTPC mass, DUNE determines the neutrino mass ordering at ≥5σ significance for 100% of true δCP values with 70 kt·MW·yr of exposure — approximately 1 year (best-case δCP) to 3 years (worst-case) of staged running.

> **Proof.** Full-simulation studies in the DUNE TDR (Vol. II) and the 2026 European Strategy input compute Δχ² between the best-fit and wrong-ordering hypotheses, marginalizing over all oscillation and systematic nuisance parameters. The 1300 km matter effect separates the NO/IO appearance spectra by Δχ² ≥ 25 for every δCP; the wrong-ordering fit cannot simultaneously accommodate the νe and ν̄e spectral shapes, so the minimum Δχ² over δCP exceeds 25. ∎

For CP violation, DUNE reaches 3σ discovery for 50% of δCP values at 300 kt·MW·yr, 5σ for 50% at 600 kt·MW·yr, and 3σ for 75% at 1000 kt·MW·yr [3]. The δCP resolution improves from 11°–27° (300 kt·MW·yr) to 7°–18° (1000 kt·MW·yr) depending on the true phase.

**Hyper-Kamiokande projections.** With the nominal 10-year exposure of 27×10²¹ POT (1:3 neutrino-to-antineutrino ratio) [1]:

- ≈10,000 νμ and ≈10,000 ν̄μ CCQE-like events; ≈2,000 νe and ≈800 ν̄e appearance events.
- CP conservation excluded at 5σ for **>60%** of δCP values after 10 years (assuming improved νe/ν̄e cross-section systematics via the intermediate water-Cherenkov detector).
- For maximal CP violation (δCP = −π/2) with known ordering, 5σ discovery in **under 3 years**; without external ordering constraints, ≈6 years when HK's own atmospheric ordering sensitivity is included.
- Wrong-octant exclusion at >3σ for 0.47 < sin²θ₂₃ < 0.55; Δm²₃₂ precision at the percent level.

The systematics frontier is explicit in the HK analysis: the measurement becomes systematics-limited, and the exclusion reach improves by *years* with reduced νe/ν̄e cross-section uncertainties — the raison d'être of the upgraded ND280 and the new intermediate detector at ~1 km [1][5].

**Joint sensitivity.** Combined DUNE+HK+JUNO fits project δCP precision approaching 5°–10° and ordering determination robust against non-standard interactions: the long-baseline matter measurement (DUNE) and the vacuum-like CP measurement (HK) over-constrain the (δCP, ordering) plane so that NSI-induced fake solutions, which shift the two experiments' contours differently, are excluded [7].

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

A candid assessment must confront the fragilities of the program.

1. **Cross-section and nuclear-model systematics.** Both experiments infer Eν from observed final states; the mapping depends on neutrino–nucleus interaction models (Fermi gas vs. spectral function, 2p2h, final-state interactions). DUNE's multi-GeV, multi-channel sample is especially exposed: mismodeled energy feed-down can *mimic* CP-violating spectral distortions. The near-detector suites (DUNE-PRISM off-axis capability, HK's intermediate detector spanning 1°–4° off-axis) are designed to constrain these models in situ, but residual uncertainties of 2–5% on the νe/ν̄e ratio remain the dominant sensitivity limiter [1][5].

2. **The ordering–δCP entanglement at 295 km.** Hyper-Kamiokande's headline CP reach assumes the ordering is known (or measured atmospherically). If the atmospheric ordering determination underperforms — e.g., due to larger-than-expected ν/ν̄ separation systematics — the CP discovery timeline slips from ~3 to ~6+ years [1].

3. **Three-flavor unitarity assumption.** All quoted sensitivities assume the PMNS matrix is unitary. Light sterile neutrinos or non-standard interactions can generate fake CP asymmetries and degrade the ordering reach (e.g., DUNE's 5σ ordering sensitivity can fall to 4σ in 3+1 scenarios with large θ₃₄) [3]. The short-baseline program (SBN at Fermilab, JSNS²) is the external control on this threat.

4. **Schedule and staging risk.** Both experiments are multi-decade civil-construction undertakings: DUNE's far-detector modules are staged, and HK's timeline targets 2027 first data [8]. Sensitivity-vs-time curves assume the staging plan holds; delays shift every milestone. Beam-power ramp profiles (1.2 → 2+ MW for LBNF; →1.3 MW for J-PARC) are technically demanding.

5. **Statistical interpretation.** Discovery claims rest on Wilks'-theorem Δχ² thresholds applied to highly non-Gaussian, degeneracy-riddled likelihoods. The collaborations mitigate this with Feldman–Cousins corrections and Bayesian cross-checks, but the mapping from Δχ² to σ near degenerate minima remains a methodological subtlety.

## 7. Conclusion

DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande are not redundant — they are *conjugate* experiments. DUNE's 1300 km wideband program turns the Earth itself into a spectrometer, using matter effects to pin down the mass ordering at 5σ within a few years and to separate genuine CP violation from matter-induced asymmetry across two oscillation maxima. Hyper-Kamiokande's 295 km narrowband program turns raw statistics into precision, measuring the nearly pure leptonic CP asymmetry with thousands of νe appearance events in a detector eight times the fiducial mass of its predecessor. Each experiment's weakness — DUNE's complex, systematics-heavy wideband sample; HK's ordering-blind short baseline — is the other's strength.

The quantitative bottom line, drawn from the collaborations' full-simulation TDR studies, is that the *joint* program achieves what neither can alone: 5σ ordering determination for all δCP values, 5σ CP-violation discovery for the majority of the δCP space, δCP measured to better than ~10°–20°, and the θ₂₃ octant resolved — while remaining robust against the sterile-neutrino and non-standard-interaction scenarios that would fake these signals in a single experiment [1][3][7]. If the T2K/NOvA hints of near-maximal CP violation hold, the discovery could come within the first years of operation. The three-flavor paradigm, completed by these two experiments, will either close the book on leptonic mixing or — through precision tensions with unitarity — open the first chapter of physics beyond it.
