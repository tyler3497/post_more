# Measure-Theoretic Foundations and Inference Engines of Probabilistic Programming Languages: From Quasi-Borel Semantics to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and Amortized Variational Inference

## Abstract

Probabilistic programming languages unify Bayesian modeling and general-purpose computation by treating program executions as *random variables* and denotations as *probability measures*. This thesis spans (i) the measure-theoretic and categorical semantics of higher-order probabilistic programs, (ii) the inference engines that exploit program structure, and (iii) compilation techniques that amortize inference. We start from Aumann's obstacle — the failure of cartesian closure in measurable spaces — and show how *quasi-Borel spaces* [1] and *quasi-Borel predomains* [2] furnish a commutative probability monad over a cartesian closed category, yielding adequate semantics for higher-order languages with soft conditioning. We contrast universal *trace-based* languages (Church [3], Anglican [4]) supporting arbitrary stochastic recursion against *differentiable* languages (Stan [7]) that trade universality for gradients. We treat Hamiltonian Monte Carlo [5] and the No-U-Turn sampler [6] geometrically, derive stochastic variational inference [10] as natural-gradient ELBO optimization, show how Pyro [9] embeds guides in effect-handler transformations, and analyze *inference compilation* [11] as amortized trace-space importance sampling with a precise correctness characterization.

## 1 Introduction

A probabilistic program is a program that denotes a probability distribution. The slogan is crisp; its realization is not. A language designer must answer three interlocking questions: *what does a program mean?* (semantics), *how do we compute with that meaning?* (inference), and *how do we make inference fast enough to matter?* (compilation and amortization). Each question exposes a deep fault line.

The semantic question collides with a classical negative result: the category **Meas** of measurable spaces and measurable maps is *not* cartesian closed [1][3], so higher-order programs — functions that accept or return random functions — admit no naive denotation as measurable maps. The inference question collides with geometry: posteriors over hundreds of dimensions concentrate on thin, curved manifolds (typical sets), defeating random-walk Metropolis–Hastings whose proposal variance must scale as *O*(1/*d*). The engineering question collides with universality: a Turing-complete language admits programs with unbounded, data-dependent randomness, for which no single inference algorithm is both correct and efficient.

This thesis is organized around the three traditions that advanced each frontier: **semantics** via quasi-Borel spaces [1] and predomains [2]; **gradient-based inference** via Stan [7] with HMC [5] and NUTS [6]; and **universal inference** via Church [3], Anglican [4], Pyro [9], and inference compilation [11]. We derive trace-based MCMC, geometric HMC, and variational objectives from a single measure-theoretic foundation, and characterize when inference compilation preserves posterior correctness.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Probability as a computational monad

Following Moggi's computational metalanguage, a probabilistic program of type *A* denotes a *computation* producing values in *A*. The Giry monad *P* maps a measurable space *X* to the space *P(X)* of probability measures on *X*; `return` is the Dirac measure and bind integrates a kernel [12]. The Giry monad is commutative — marginalization order is irrelevant — which semantically justifies the *commutativity* program equivalence that lets inference reorder independent samples [2].

The Giry construction breaks at higher order: there is no measurable structure on the function space *Y^X* making evaluation measurable in general [3]. The **quasi-Borel space (QBS)** model [1] resolves this by shifting the primitive from measurable *subsets* to admissible *random elements*: a QBS is a set *X* with a designated family *M_X ⊆ X^ℝ* of functions ℝ → *X* closed under precomposition with measurable maps and under countable gluing. The probability monad then takes the elegant form *P(X) = {[α, μ]}* where *α: ℝ → X* is a random element and *μ* a probability measure on ℝ, quotiented by change-of-variables equivalence [1]. The category **Qbs** is cartesian closed, and *P* is a commutative monad on it — the first model of higher-order probability with both properties.

### 2.2 Recursion, soft conditioning, and predomains

Real PPLs need *recursion* (stochastic memoizers, unbounded loops) and *soft constraints* (`observe` with densities, or `factor` scoring arbitrary log-weights). **Quasi-Borel predomains** [2] — chain-complete partial orders with compatible QBS structure — model both, yielding adequacy for recursive types and commutativity of the probabilistic powerdomain. Soft conditioning is handled by Staton's *s-finite* kernel semantics [12]: `observe d v` multiplies the trace weight by the density of *d* at *v*, allowing conditioning on measure-zero events that naive disintegration cannot express.

### 2.3 The two language families

| Feature | Universal trace-based (Church [3], Anglican [4], WebPPL) | Differentiable (Stan [7]) |
|---|---|---|
| Program class | Turing-complete; stochastic recursion, higher-order | Fixed-dimension continuous models; constrained types |
| Randomness model | Execution *traces*: sequences of addressed `sample` calls | Joint log-density ∇-differentiable over ℝⁿ |
| Conditioning | `observe` at arbitrary program points; soft factors | Observed data as inputs; log-density evaluation |
| Default inference | Trace-space MCMC, SMC, particle MCMC | NUTS [6] (HMC), ADVI [8] |
| Gradient access | None (black-box) | Reverse-mode AD through log-density |
| Memoization | Stochastic memoizer `mem` for nonparametrics [3] | Not supported |

This dichotomy — *universal but gradient-free* versus *differentiable but restricted* — organizes everything that follows. Pyro [9] and inference compilation [11] are attempts to dissolve it.

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *semantic derivation*: we fix the trace/measure semantics first, then derive each inference algorithm as a concrete Monte Carlo method on the semantic domain, and finally analyze its correctness and complexity. Concretely:

1. **Semantic core (§4.1).** Formalize traces and the QBS probability-monad semantics of `sample`/`observe`, fixing the target distribution each algorithm must approximate.
2. **Geometric inference (§4.2).** Derive HMC from symplectic geometry on the cotangent bundle, with NUTS [6] adaptively selecting trajectory length by recursive doubling.
3. **Variational inference (§4.3).** Derive SVI [10] as stochastic natural-gradient ascent on the ELBO, and show how Pyro [9] implements guides as effect-handler transformations.
4. **Amortized compilation (§4.4).** Formalize inference compilation [11] as learning a trace-space proposal *q_φ(x | y)* from prior traces, and characterize its correctness.

We validate each derivation against the primary literature [1]–[12] and the documented behavior of the Stan [7], Pyro [9], and Anglican [4] implementations.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Trace semantics: evaluation histories as the fundamental object

The operational heart of universal PPLs is the **trace**. In Church [3], program evaluation is sampling: each random choice (`flip`, `gaussian`, ...) extends an *evaluation history*. A trace *t* is a finite map from *addresses* (syntactic call sites, distinguished by their dynamic call stack) to sampled values, plus a weight *w(t)* accumulating the product of prior densities and likelihood factors from `observe` statements.

> **Theorem (Trace correctness, Wingate–Stuhlmüller–Goodman 2011).** For a Church program with finitely many addresses per execution, the distribution over return values induced by prior-weighted traces, conditioned on all observations, equals the denotational semantics in the s-finite kernel model [12].

*Proof sketch.* By induction on evaluation derivations: `sample d` extends the trace with a draw from *d* (kernel composition); `observe d v` multiplies the weight by the density *d(v)* (score). The normalization constant is the model's marginal likelihood. ∎

**Single-site Metropolis–Hastings on traces** is the canonical universal inference algorithm. A proposal selects one address uniformly, resamples its value from the prior (or a local kernel), and *re-executes* the program deterministically from that point: downstream addresses whose control flow is unchanged are *reused* (their values persist), while addresses that vanish or appear are added or removed with the appropriate dimension-changing Jacobian-free acceptance ratio:

```
α = min(1,  (w(t') · q(t|t')) / (w(t) · q(t'|t)) )
```

where *q* includes the address-selection and resampling probabilities. Because only the *suffix* of the trace is re-executed, proposals are cheap in practice — the key engineering insight of the "lightweight" MH implementations [3].

**Coroutines and continuations in Anglican.** Anglican [4] compiles its Clojure-like language via continuation-passing style so execution can be *suspended and resumed* at `sample`/`observe` checkpoints, yielding particle filters (SMC), particle MCMC (PMMH, particle Gibbs), and trace MH for free. The CPS/coroutine design makes inference *generic*: the algorithm never sees the model program, only the stream of checkpoints — a precursor to Pyro's effect handlers [9].

The **stochastic memoizer** `mem` [3] deserves special mention: `(mem f)` returns a *stochastically memoized* function that draws its return value once per distinct argument and reuses it — semantically, an infinite random table. This single construct expresses Dirichlet processes, Chinese restaurant processes, and infinite mixture models in a few lines, demonstrating that *universality plus memoization subsumes nonparametrics*.

### 4.2 Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and the No-U-Turn sampler in Stan

When the log-density is differentiable, geometry beats randomness. **Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)** augments the *d*-dimensional position *θ* with momentum *ρ ∼ N(0, M)* and defines the Hamiltonian

```
H(θ, ρ) = -log π(θ) + ½ ρᵀM⁻¹ρ = U(θ) + K(ρ)
```

Hamilton's equations *θ̇ = M⁻¹ρ*, *ρ̇ = ∇log π(θ)* generate a flow that (a) preserves volume (Liouville), (b) is reversible, and (c) conserves energy — so proposals along the flow are accepted with probability ≈ 1 and can travel *O*(1) distance in *O*(1) steps, versus *O*(1/√*d*) per step for random-walk Metropolis [5]. Betancourt's geometric analysis [5] reframes HMC as exploration of the *typical set*: the thin shell where the posterior concentrates. Divergent transitions — detected when the leapfrog integrator's energy error explodes — are not mere inefficiency but *diagnostics of geometric pathology* (e.g., funnel geometries in hierarchical models), a fact Stan surfaces to users as warnings.

The leapfrog integrator discretizes the flow with step size *ε*:

```python
def leapfrog(theta, rho, eps, grad_logp):
    rho   = rho + 0.5 * eps * grad_logp(theta)   # half momentum step
    theta = theta + eps * M_inv @ rho             # full position step
    rho   = rho + 0.5 * eps * grad_logp(theta)   # half momentum step
    return theta, rho
```

It is symplectic and reversible, so detailed balance holds up to the Metropolis correction. The two free parameters — *ε* and the number of steps *L* — are notoriously hard to tune by hand. **NUTS** [6] eliminates *L*: starting from the current state, it builds a trajectory by *recursive doubling*, simulating forward and backward in time, and stops at the U-turn criterion ⟨*θ⁺ − θ⁻*, *ρ⁻*⟩ < 0 (or for *ρ⁺*) or on numerical divergence. A slice variable selects the final state, preserving detailed balance without a Metropolis step. Stan [7] pairs NUTS with dual-averaging adaptation of *ε* during warmup — the most widely deployed "turnkey" Bayesian engine: the user writes a model block, and Stan's reverse-mode AD supplies ∇log π automatically.

*Why Stan restricts the language.* NUTS requires a fixed-dimensional, unconstrained, differentiable target. Stan's type system enforces this: parameters carry constraints transformed to unconstrained space with Jacobian corrections, and the model is a pure log-density function — the price of gradients. **Automatic Differentiation Variational Inference (ADVI)** [8] is Stan's second engine: a Gaussian variational family in unconstrained space, optimized with single-draw stochastic gradients.

### 4.3 Stochastic variational inference and Pyro's effect handlers

**Stochastic variational inference (SVI)** [10] scales variational Bayes to massive data. For a model *p(β, z, x)* with global parameters *β*, the mean-field ELBO is optimized by *stochastic natural gradients*: sample a minibatch, compute the noisy natural gradient of the locally optimized ELBO, and take a Robbins–Monro step. Natural-gradient preconditioning by the Fisher information makes the algorithm parameterization-invariant and recovers classical VB coordinate updates as a special case [10].

**Pyro** [9] lifts SVI from conjugate exponential families to *arbitrary programs* via **effect handlers**: `sample` and `param` statements are *effects* intercepted by handlers such as `trace`, `replay`, and `block`. A *guide* is an ordinary Python program; `Trace_ELBO` computes the ELBO from a single joint execution of model and guide, with gradients through reparameterized sites and score-function terms for discrete sites, while `TraceGraph_ELBO` adds Rao-Blackwellization. Because handlers compose, guides can be neural networks — dissolving the universal/differentiable dichotomy at the cost of requiring the user to design the guide.

> **Theorem (Handler-correctness, informal).** If every `sample` site in the model is matched by a site with the same name and support in the guide, and discrete sites use the score estimator, then the stochastic gradient of `Trace_ELBO` is an unbiased estimator of ∇ELBO [9].

### 4.4 Inference compilation: amortizing the trace

**Inference compilation (IC)** [11] attacks the remaining bottleneck: even fast per-model inference is too slow when the same model family must be queried repeatedly (e.g., real-time perception). IC *pre-trains* a neural proposal network *q_φ(x | y)* — an "inference artifact" — on *synthetic* data: run the *prior* program forward to generate traces *(x, y)* (latents, observations), then train the network to map observations to per-address proposals by minimizing *E_{p(x,y)}[−log q_φ(x|y)]*, i.e., the expected forward KL. At test time, run sequential importance sampling where each `sample` is proposed from the network:

```python
# inference compilation, test time (schematic)
def compiled_inference(program, obs, net):
    trace, log_w = {}, 0.0
    for addr, dist in program.run(obs, mode="propose"):
        prop = net.proposal(addr, obs, trace)      # amortized proposal
        x = prop.sample()
        trace[addr] = x
        log_w += dist.log_prob(x) - prop.log_prob(x)  # importance weight
        log_w += program.observe_logprob(addr, obs)   # likelihood factor
    return trace, log_w
```

> **Theorem (IC correctness).** For any fixed observation *y* with *p(y) > 0*, self-normalized importance sampling with proposal *q_φ(·|y)* is consistent for posterior expectations *E_{p(x|y)}[f]* provided *supp(p(·|y)) ⊆ supp(q_φ(·|y))*, regardless of *φ*. Training only affects *efficiency* (effective sample size), never asymptotic correctness [11].

This cleanly separates *amortization* (learning *φ* offline) from *validity* (importance weighting online) — the same separation Pyro exploits with amortized guides. The cost is *compilation generality*: the artifact is tied to the observation distribution seen in training; out-of-distribution observations degrade ESS gracefully (weights still valid) but can collapse it in practice.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Formal Results

We consolidate the formal guarantees and reported empirical behavior of each engine. All complexity statements are per *effective* sample.

### 5.1 Formal results

| Result | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| QBS cartesian closure | **Qbs** is cartesian closed; *P* is a commutative monad | [1] |
| Predomain adequacy | Quasi-Borel predomains model recursion + commutativity + soft constraints; contextual equivalence is sound | [2] |
| Trace MH correctness | Single-site MH on execution traces targets the exact posterior (detailed balance on trace space) | [3] |
| NUTS invariance | The NUTS transition kernel leaves *π* invariant; slice sampling on the doubling tree preserves detailed balance | [6] |
| SVI convergence | Stochastic natural-gradient ascent converges a.s. to a local ELBO optimum under Robbins–Monro steps | [10] |
| ADVI universality | Any differentiable Stan model admits a Gaussian variational approximation via unconstrained reparameterization | [8] |
| IC consistency | Self-normalized IS with learned proposals is consistent for any *φ* with adequate support | [11] |

### 5.2 Reported empirical behavior

- **NUTS vs. random-walk MH.** Hoffman and Gelman [6] report NUTS achieving multiples of the ESS per gradient evaluation of tuned HMC on hierarchical models, with HMC dominating random-walk MH by orders of magnitude in high dimensions; Neal's funnel shows NUTS's divergent-transition diagnostics exposing geometry that silently biases Gibbs samplers [5][7].
- **Stan at scale.** The Stan project [7] documents routine use on models with thousands of parameters, with warmup adaptation removing the *L*-tuning burden.
- **SVI at scale.** Hoffman et al. [10] demonstrate LDA on 3.8M Wikipedia articles in hours where batch VB was infeasible — per-iteration cost independent of dataset size.
- **Inference compilation speedups.** Le et al. [11] report 10–100× speedups over SMC baselines on Captcha-breaking and object-counting tasks, with millisecond-per-query artifacts after hours of offline training.

### 5.3 A unified complexity view

Let *d* be parameter dimension, *G* the cost of one gradient evaluation, *E* the cost of one program execution, and *n* the number of observations:

| Engine | Cost / effective sample | Scales with |
|---|---|---|
| Trace MH (universal) | *O*(E · trace-length) | Program structure; no gradient info |
| SMC / particle Gibbs | *O*(N · E · T) | Particles × time steps |
| HMC / NUTS | *O*(L · G), L adaptive | Gradient cost; geometry-aware |
| SVI | *O*(minibatch · E) amortized | Data size decoupled via subsampling |
| Inference compilation | *O*(E) per query + offline training | Training budget; ESS depends on *q_φ* quality |

The pattern is unmistakable: *structure buys speed*. Gradients buy geometry-awareness (Stan); minibatching buys data-scalability (SVI); learned proposals buy amortization (IC); coroutines buy genericity (Anglican).

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**Semantics.** Quasi-Borel predomains [2] handle recursion and continuous distributions, but *conditioning on measure-zero events* in higher-order settings remains delicate: disintegration is not functorial, and the s-finite kernel approach [12] validates `observe` operationally without a fully compositional account of Bayesian inversion at function types. *Question:* is there a cartesian closed category with a commutative probability monad *and* a natural conditioning combinator?

**HMC/NUTS.** NUTS assumes a fixed-dimensional, everywhere-differentiable target. Transdimensional models (mixtures with unknown components, nonparametric priors) fall outside Stan's language entirely; within it, *funnel geometries* and *multimodality* still defeat NUTS — divergences diagnose but do not cure. Riemannian HMC (Girolami & Calderhead) adapts the metric locally but requires third-order derivatives, rarely affordable.

**Variational inference.** SVI [10] and Pyro's guides [9] optimize a *non-convex* ELBO: mode-seeking behavior systematically underestimates posterior variance, and the score-function estimator for discrete latents can stall training. Designing guides remains an art — *the inference problem has been moved, not solved*.

**Universal trace MCMC.** Single-site MH mixes poorly on strongly correlated latents (the classic centered vs. non-centered parameterization problem), and re-execution costs scale with trace length. Gradient-guided universal inference is nascent.

**Inference compilation.** The artifact is only as good as its training distribution; *posterior degradation under distribution shift* is the central practical risk [11]. Formal guarantees for how much training suffices for a target ESS are absent — a PAC-style theory of amortized inference is an open frontier.

## 7 Conclusion

Probabilistic programming has matured from a provocative idea — *programs as distributions* [3] — into a stratified discipline with three load-bearing pillars. **Semantics** now rests on firm categorical ground: quasi-Borel spaces [1] and their predomain refinement [2] give higher-order probabilistic languages the denotational foundation that measurable spaces could not. **Inference** has two mature traditions: the *geometric* tradition of Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and NUTS [5][6][7], which turns gradients into efficient exploration of the typical set, and the *universal* tradition of trace-based MCMC and coroutine particle methods [3][4], which turns program execution itself into the proposal mechanism. **Amortization** — via stochastic variational inference [10], deep probabilistic programming with effect handlers [9], and inference compilation [11] — is dissolving the boundary between them, learning inference strategies from data rather than deriving them by hand.

The thesis of this thesis is that these are not competing paradigms but *projections of one object*: the trace/measure semantics of §4.1. HMC is trace-space dynamics restricted to differentiable densities; SVI is trace-space optimization of a variational bound; inference compilation is trace-space importance sampling with learned proposals. The measure-theoretic foundations are laid; the engineering of inference as *compilation* has just begun.
