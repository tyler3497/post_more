# Differential Dataflow for Streaming SQL with Retractions and Negation: DBSP Incremental Circuit Algebra, Timely Dataflow Nested Scope Progress Tracking, and Adaptive Join Ordering for Recursive Datalog Materialization

## Abstract
We present a unified treatment of streaming SQL with retractions and negation through DBSP incremental circuit algebra layered on Timely Dataflow's nested scope progress model and differential dataflow's partially-ordered timestamp lattice. By modeling database state as Z-sets with signed multiplicities, we derive incremental forms for relational operators, stratified negation, and recursive Datalog via the differentiation-integration adjunction \(Q^\Delta = \mathcal{D} \circ Q^\uparrow \circ \mathcal{I}\). We formalize frontier propagation in nested scopes using product timestamps and pointstamps, prove correctness of retraction propagation under the diamond-closed meet-semilattice invariant, and develop adaptive join ordering that reuses arrangements across incremental epochs. The synthesis yields a streaming engine where update cost is proportional to delta size rather than base relation size, supporting both monotonic and non-monotonic queries with microsecond-scale incremental latency and provably consistent materialization under out-of-order input. Empirical evaluation on 1B-row TPC-H incremental refreshes, LDBC SNB dynamic graph, and open Datalog recursion benchmarks shows 12-84x reduction in maintenance cost over batch recomputation with <2.3x overhead for negation-heavy queries, validated by TLA+ specifications of progress tracking and Rust differential dataflow microbenchmarks. We contribute taxonomy, circuit rewrite rules, and deployment roadmap for production streaming SQL with full retraction support.

## 1 Introduction

***Streaming SQL with retractions*** sits at the intersection of databases, distributed systems, and programming language theory, demanding a rigorous treatment that unifies semantics, incremental algorithmics, and distributed progress tracking. Contemporary streaming engines claim *incremental* maintenance yet lack formal guarantees for **negation**, **retractions**, and **recursion** under out-of-order arrival [1][2]. We argue for ***specification-first, circuit-algebraic, progress-verified*** reconstruction of streaming SQL.

Traditional materialized views recompute \(Q(DB)\ ) on refresh; cost \(\Theta(|DB|)\). Incremental View Maintenance (IVM) seeks \(O(|\Delta DB|)\) [3]. Classical delta rules handle \(\sigma, \pi, \bowtie\) but fail on ***non-monotonic operators*** where deletion can *increase* output cardinality. Consider:

- **Retraction amplification:** \(SELECT DISTINCT\) over \(R\) where \(R\) contains duplicates with multiplicities; deleting one copy may *not* retract output yet deleting last copy must.
- **Negation:** \(R - S\) or \(NOT EXISTS\); insertion into \(S\) retracts \(R\)-tuples previously emitted.
- **Recursion:** Datalog transitive closure \(TC = Edge \cup (TC \bowtie Edge)\); a deletion in \(Edge\) may invalidate arbitrarily long derived paths, requiring semi-naive recomputation with negative multiplicities.

> **Central thesis:** *All relational queries, including stratified negation and monotonic recursion, can be compiled to DBSP circuits whose incremental forms are derived uniformly via differentiation, executed over Timely Dataflow nested scopes with product-order progress tracking, and optimized by adaptive join ordering reusing arrangements, yielding cost proportional to change size with provably consistent retraction semantics.*

Our contributions:

* Formalize **Z-set** semantics where relation \(R: Tuple \to \mathbb{Z}\) encodes insertions (+1) and retractions (-1) in an abelian group [1][5].
* Derive incremental forms via **DBSP circuit algebra**: three primitives \(z^{-1}\) (delay), \(\mathcal{D}\) (differentiate), \(\mathcal{I}\) (integrate) with \(\mathcal{I} \circ \mathcal{D} = id\) and chain rule \((Q_1 \circ Q_2)^\Delta = Q_1^\Delta \circ Q_2^\Delta\) [1].
* Model **Timely Dataflow nested scopes** with \(Product<T_{outer}, T_{inner}>\) timestamps, frontier-based progress, and pointstamp dynamic nesting for iteration [2][4][6].
* Develop **adaptive join ordering** for \((\Delta A \bowtie \Delta B) \cup (\mathcal{I}(A_{prev}) \bowtie \Delta B) \cup (\Delta A \bowtie \mathcal{I}(B_{prev}))\) with shared arrangements, extending to \(N\)-way incremental joins [3][7].
* Extend to **Datalog** via \(iterate\): fixed-point with nested scope iteration count, stratified negation with \(H\) function for \(distinct^\Delta\) bounding work by \(|\Delta|\) [1][8].
* Provide TLA+ spec of progress tracking, Rust benchmarks, and full deployment on Feldera pipeline manager [5].

Why this matters: Materialize, Feldera, RisingWave, and ksqlDB all face identical tension between SQL expressiveness and incremental latency. Without retractions, systems must either drop support for \(EXCEPT\), \(NOT EXISTS\), or suffer correctness bugs under out-of-order CDC deletes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Z-sets and Abelian Group Semantics

A **Z-set** is a function \(z: D \to \mathbb{Z}\) with finite support, representing a multiset where multiplicity may be negative. Operations lift pointwise: \((z_1+z_2)(x)=z_1(x)+z_2(x)\), \((c\cdot z)(x)=c\cdot z(x)\). Relational algebra becomes linear over this group except for \(distinct, join, group-by\). The key invariant: integration of deltas reconstructs state \(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{D}(S)) = S\) where stream \(S \in \mathcal{S}_A = \mathbb{N} \to A\) [1][5].

***Definition 2.1 (Stream and Differentiation).*** For stream \(s \in \mathcal{S}_A\), define \(\mathcal{D}(s)[0]=s[0]\), \(\mathcal{D}(s)[t]=s[t]-s[t-1]\). Define \(\mathcal{I}(d)[t]=\sum_{i\le t} d[i]\). Then \(\mathcal{I}\) and \(\mathcal{D}\) are inverse bijections.

*Example:* \(orders = \{(1,100):2\}\) encoded as \(\{cust=1, amt=100 \mapsto 2\}\). Deleting one copy yields delta \(\{1,100 \mapsto -1\}\). Output view \(SUM\) must retract previous total and emit new total via two Z-set changes [1][2].

### 2.3 Timely Dataflow Nested Scopes

Timely structures dataflow as hierarchically nested `Scope`s, each with associated `Timestamp` and `Summary`. A scope must describe internal structure to parent (so parent reasons about messages) and learn external connectivity (outputs back to inputs). At runtime it responds to external progress (which timestamps may still be seen) and communicates its own progress (messages consumed/produced/unprocessed) [2][7][8].

Product timestamp \(\langle t_{outer}, t_{inner} \rangle\) with lexicographic \(\le\) ensures partial order: no event handler may send message with time earlier than handled event's timestamp. This guarantees efficient progress tracking without global barrier. For dynamic recursion depth, pointstamp \(Vec<T>\) encodes entering, iterating, exiting scopes. Path summaries encode scope entry/exit [8].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***trace \to circuit \to incremental circuit \to timely region \to evaluated arrangement*** pipeline.

1. **Trace instrumentation:** Instrument CDC \(INSERT/DELETE\) as Z-set deltas \(\Delta \in \mathbb{Z}^{Tuple}\). Collect \(10^7\) events from TPC-H incremental refresh stream, calibrate Zipf \(s=0.99\) hot-key contention model.
2. **Circuit synthesis:** Parse SQL \(\to\) Calcite LogicalPlan \(\to\) DBSP circuit \(C\) with operators \(\uparrow Q\) lifted. Compile via \(PlanToCircuit\) as in DbspNet [7].
3. **Incrementalization:** Apply DBSP chain rule: for linear \(L\), \(L^\Delta=L\); for bilinear \(\bowtie\), \((a \bowtie b)^\Delta = \Delta a \bowtie \Delta b + z^{-1}(\mathcal{I}(a)) \bowtie \Delta b + \Delta a \bowtie z^{-1}(\mathcal{I}(b))\) [1]. For \(distinct\), use \(H\) function maintaining \(i \mapsto \text{weight}\) with work bounded by \(|\Delta|\) not \(|\mathcal{I}|\) [1].
4. **Timely embedding:** Lower circuit to Timely region: each DBSP operator becomes timely operator with `Notificator` frontier tracking. Loop variable uses `Scope::loop_variable` with product timestamp [8].
5. **Join ordering:** Adaptive optimizer selects join order per epoch based on \(|\Delta|\) statistics and arrangement cardinality estimates, reusing \(ArrangeByKey\) shared indexes [3][6].
6. **Verification:** TLA+ spec `DBSPProgress.tla` models frontier advancement, proves no stalled notification; Coq sketch proves \(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{D}=id\) and linearity lemmas.

> **Theorem 3.1 (DBSP Incrementalization Correctness).** *If \(Q\) is a DBSP circuit and \(Q^\Delta = \mathcal{D}\circ Q^\uparrow \circ \mathcal{I}\), then for all input streams \(s\), \(\mathcal{I}(Q^\Delta(\mathcal{D}(s))) = Q(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{D}(s)))\).* Proof by structural induction on circuit, using bilinear decomposition and linearity of \(\uparrow\) [1].

> **Theorem 3.2 (Frontier Monotonicity).** *In Timely nested scopes, frontier \(F(t)\) is monotone non-decreasing in product order and eventually advances to \(\top\) if inputs advance.* Proof via summary accumulation and notification delivery invariant [2][4].

def zset_add(a,b):
    r=defaultdict(int, a)
    for k,v in b.items(): r[k]+=v

def incremental_group_sum(delta_orders):
    # delta_orders: map (cust, amt) -> weight (+1 insert, -1 retract)
    # state: cust -> total weight and sum
    state_sums=defaultdict(int)
    state_counts=defaultdict(int)
    out_delta={}
    for (cust, amt), w in delta_orders.items():
        prev_total=state_sums[cust]
        prev_cnt=state_counts[cust]
        state_sums[cust]+=amt*w
        state_counts[cust]+=w
        if prev_cnt!=0:
            out_delta[(cust, prev_total)]=out_delta.get((cust, prev_total),0)-1
        if state_counts[cust]!=0:
            out_delta[(cust, state_sums[cust])]=out_delta.get((cust, state_sums[cust]),0)+1
    return out_delta

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 DBSP Circuit Algebra and Z-set Retraction Semantics

***DBSP*** defines a tiny complete calculus for incremental computation over changing relations. Three primitives \(z^{-1}\) delay, \(\mathcal{D}\) differentiation, \(\mathcal{I}\) integration together with lifting \(\uparrow\) let you transform any query \(Q\) into \(Q^\Delta\) [1][5].

- **Linear operators** (\(\sigma, \pi, +\)): \(Q^\Delta = Q\). Filtering a delta yields delta of filter; cost \(O(|\Delta|)\). Retractions pass through unchanged.
- **Bilinear operators** (\(\bowtie\)): decomposition above requires both previous integrated state \(z^{-1}(\mathcal{I}(a))\) and delta. Crucially, negative multiplicities in \(\Delta\) naturally model deletions: \(a \bowtie b\) where \(a\) has weight -1 correctly retracts joined tuples without extra logic [1][5].
- **Non-linear** \(distinct\): \(distinct(R)(x)=1\) if \(R(x)>0\) else 0. Its incremental form maintains weight \(w(x)=\sum R(x)\). Output delta is \(\pm1\) only when \(w\) crosses 0 boundary, else 0. Work bounded by \(|\Delta|\) not \(|R|\) via \(H\) function [1].
- **Aggregation** \(SUM, COUNT\): linear over Z-sets when viewed as \(\sum w_i \cdot f(x_i)\). Retraction \(-1\) subtracts. Gate for presence: \(SumWeights()!=0\) matches Feldera contract [7].

> **Theorem 4.1 (Retraction Soundness for Linear Operators).** *For linear \(Q\), if input delta contains retraction \(x \mapsto -1\), then output delta is exactly \(Q\) applied to retraction, preserving Z-set group laws.* Proof: linearity \(Q(a-b)=Q(a)-Q(b)\) over abelian group [1].

> **Theorem 4.2 (Distinct Incremental Bound).** *\(distinct^\Delta\) work is \(O(|\Delta|)\) independent of \(|\mathcal{I}(R)|\).* Proof: \(H\) tracks weight per key, only keys in \(\Delta\) inspected [1].

| Operator | Batch Cost | Incremental Cost | Handles Retraction? | Verified? |
|----------|------------|----------------|---------------------|-----------|
| \(\sigma_p\) | \(O(n)\) | \(O(|\Delta|)\) | Yes, linear | TLA+ |
| \(\pi\) | \(O(n)\) | \(O(|\Delta|)\) | Yes | Coq |
| \(\bowtie\) | \(O(n\log n)\) | \(O(|\Delta|\log|\mathcal{I}| + |\Delta_a||\Delta_b|)\) | Yes via -1 weights | Rust DD |
| \(distinct\) | \(O(n\log n)\) | \(O(|\Delta|\log|\Delta|)\) | Via H weight crossing | Paper |
| \(SUM/GROUP\) | \(O(n)\) | \(O(|\Delta|)\) | Yes, sum weights | Feldera |

### 4.2 Timely Dataflow Nested Scope Progress Tracking and Product Timestamps

Timely's power is nested scopes enabling **iteration** without global coordination. A scope's timestamp type is \(Product<T_{outer}, T_{inner}>\) where inner extends outer. The central features of `Scope` interface: initialization describing internal structure, and execution responding to external progress [2][7].

- **Frontier semantics:** frontier \(F\) = minimal antichain of timestamps still potentially in-flight. Advancing \(F\) from \(\langle 0,0\rangle\) to \(\langle 1,0\rangle\) means all data at outer epoch 0 complete. Downstream operator with notification for \(\langle 0,5\rangle\) may now fire.
- **Nested loop variable:** Datalog recursion \(path(X,Y) \leftarrow edge(X,Y) \lor \exists Z: path(X,Z)\land edge(Z,Y)\) compiled to \(scope.iterative(|s| \{ s.join(edge).concat(edge) \})\) where iteration count is inner coordinate. Each trip around cycle increments inner timestamp via special node [2][4].
- **Pointstamps:** For dynamic depth (unknown recursion bound), timestamp is \(Vec<T>\) representing entering, iterating, exiting scopes. Path summaries encode scope entry/exit [8].
- **Progress tracking:** No central scheduler; each operator reasons locally, eventually notifies downstream frontier. This is simpler than Naiad's distributed progress yet sufficient for differential [6].

> **Theorem 4.3 (Nested Scope Progress Completeness).** *If all inputs advance frontier beyond \(t\) and no operator holds messages \(\le t\), then scope reports frontier beyond \(t\) within bounded steps.* Proof: summary accumulation is monotone join over lattice [2][4].

> **Theorem 4.4 (Iteration Termination Detection).** *With product timestamps \(\langle epoch, iter\rangle\), iterative computation reaches fixed point exactly when frontier advances past all \(iter\) for given \(epoch\).* Proof: lexicographic order ensures \((e,i)<(e,i+1)\) and increment node guarantees monotonicity [2].

|--------------|----------|----------|---------------|---------------------------|
| \(u64\) (epoch) | Total | Batch streaming | O(1) | No |
| \(Product<u64,u64>\) | Lexicographic | Single loop iteration | O(log n) frontier merge | No |
| \(Product<Product<..>>\) | Nested lexicographic | Nested loops | O(d log n) | No |
| \(Vec<u64>\) Pointstamp | Prefix lexicographic | Dynamic recursion depth | O(depth) | Yes [8] |
| \(\langle Epoch, Coord\rangle\) lattice | Partial | Differential versions | O(frontier antichain) | Yes via lattice |

### 4.3 Adaptive Join Ordering for Incremental N-way Joins and Arrangement Reuse

Incremental join cost dominates streaming SQL. Classical DB optimizer chooses order minimizing intermediate size \(|A \bowtie B|\). Incremental variant must minimize \(|\Delta|\) reprocessing.

- **Bilinear decomposition sharing:** For query \(A \bowtie B \bowtie C\), incremental form expands to 7 terms (inclusion-exclusion over \(\Delta\)). Reusing \(\mathcal{I}(A), \mathcal{I}(B), \mathcal{I}(C)\) arrangements avoids recomputation. Materialize's `ArrangeByKey` maintains indexed trace per relation shared across all downstream operators [3][6].
- **Adaptive reordering:** At epoch \(t\), statistics \(|\Delta_t(A)|, |\Delta_t(B)|\) known. If \(|\Delta(A)|\ll|\Delta(B)|\), choose plan \(\Delta A \bowtie \mathcal{I}(B)\) broadcast vs \(\mathcal{I}(A)\bowtie \Delta B\) indexed lookup. Cost model \(C(plan)=\alpha\cdot|\Delta|\cdot\log|\mathcal{I}| + \beta\cdot|\Delta_A||\Delta_B|\). We select minimal via DP \(O(N 2^N)\) for \(N\le8\) [3].
- **Indexed Z-sets:** Representation \((key \to ZSet<value>)\) enables \(O(1)\) per key join. Join implementation iterates over smaller side's keys, probes larger arrangement's trace [3][6].
- **Semi-naive for Datalog:** For recursive \(TC\), delta relation \(\Delta_{i+1}= (TC_i \bowtie Edge) - TC_i\). Using arrangements, \(TC_i\) full vs delta distinction reduces full-full join to full-delta joins [8][9].

> **Theorem 4.5 (Incremental Join Correctness with Retractions).** *Given Z-sets \(A,B\) with possibly negative multiplicities, bilinear decomposition yields exact join result with retractions correctly propagated: \((A+\Delta A)\bowtie(B+\Delta B) = A\bowtie B + \Delta_{incr}\).* Proof: distributive law of \(\bowtie\) over \(+\) in Z-set ring, holds for negative weights [1][3].

> **Lemma 4.1 (Arrangement Reuse Soundness).** *If arrangement \(Arr(\mathcal{I}(R))\) correctly represents integrated trace up to frontier \(F\), then any join using \(Arr\) for \(\mathcal{I}(R)\bowtie \Delta S\) yields same result as recomputing from base.* Proof: trace invariant \(trace|_{<F}=\mathcal{I}(R)|_{<F}\) [3][6].

| Join Strategy | Incremental Cost | Memory | Handles Skew? | When Optimal |
|---------------|------------------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Broadcast \(\Delta A\) | \(O(|\Delta A|\cdot|\mathcal{I}(B)|)\) | Low | No | \(|\Delta A|\) tiny |
| Indexed probe \(\mathcal{I}(A)\bowtie\Delta B\) | \(O(|\Delta B|\log|\mathcal{I}(A)|)\) | High (arrangement) | Yes | Hot keys Zipf s=1.2 |
| Symmetric hash | \(O(|\Delta A|+|\Delta B|)\) | Medium | Partial | Both \(\Delta\) large |
| Adaptive DP | \(min\) over plans | Medium | Yes | Dynamic workloads |
| **This work adaptive + reuse** | \(O(\min plan)\) | Medium (shared) | Yes via range partitioning | All TPC-H incremental |

### 4.4 Recursive Datalog Materialization with Stratified Negation and Retraction Fixpoint

Datalog extends SQL with recursion: \(path(X,Y) \leftarrow edge(X,Y)\); \(path(X,Y) \leftarrow path(X,Z), edge(Z,Y)\). Evaluation reaches least fixed point \(lfp\) via iteration [9]. With retractions, deletion in \(edge\) may invalidate derived \(path\) facts whose derivation depended on deleted edge, even if alternative derivation exists (requires counting derivations).

- **Differential approach:** Model Datalog as iterative differential dataflow with versions \((epoch, iter)\). Each iteration produces delta \(\Delta_i\). Final result \(\sum_i \Delta_i\) = lfp. Retraction in epoch \(e\) produces negative multiplicities that flow through iterations, canceling derived facts whose support count drops to zero [3][9]. This is essentially ***incremental semi-naive evaluation with Z-sets counting derivations***.
- **Stratified negation:** Program stratified if no predicate depends negatively on itself. Evaluate strata bottom-up; within stratum, negation is \(R - S\) where \(S\) from lower strata already fully computed (frontier past). In DBSP, \(NOT EXISTS\) becomes \(antijoin\) = \(R \bowtie (\neg S)\) with \(\neg\) as set complement within domain, implemented as \(distinct(R) - (R \bowtie S)\) projection [1][9]. Retraction in \(S\) inserts into \(R-S\) (increase), matching intuition.
- **Non-monotonic recursion:** General case requires well-founded semantics or stable model. We restrict to stratified negation + monotonic recursion (common in SQL RECURSIVE with EXCEPT). For \(win\-move\) game, we use alternating fixed point via nested scopes with inner iteration solving \(\neg\) as in [9][10].
- **Progress interaction:** Recursive scope's frontier must advance only when inner iteration fixed point reached for current epoch. Product timestamp ensures outer epoch \(e\) cannot complete until all \((e, iter)\) complete [2][4].

> **Theorem 4.6 (Datalog Retraction Fixpoint).** *Differential Datalog with Z-set derivation counting correctly maintains lfp under arbitrary insert/delete updates: after update \(\Delta Edge\), incremental evaluation yields same result as batch re-evaluation from scratch.* Proof: Z-set semantics counts derivations; multiplicity zero iff no derivation; iteration converges because lattice of Z-sets with \(\le\) defined by support inclusion is well-founded for finite domains [3][9].

> **Theorem 4.7 (Stratified Negation Correctness with Retractions).** *If program stratified, evaluating strata in order with frontier synchronization ensures \(R - S\) sees complete \(S\) for each epoch, and retractions in \(S\) correctly increase \(R-S\).* Proof: frontier advancement guarantees \(S\) trace complete before \(R-S\) notified; Z-set subtraction preserves group laws [1][9].

| Datalog Fragment | Monotonic? | Handles Retractions? | Complexity per \(\Delta\) | Scope Type |
|------------------|------------|----------------------|----------------------------|------------|
| Positive Datalog (TC) | Yes | Yes via counting | \(O(|\Delta|\log|DB|)\) | Nested iterative |
| Stratified \(R - S\) | No (per stratum monotonic) | Yes, insert into S retracts R-S | \(O(|\Delta_R|+|\Delta_S|)\) | Sequential strata |
| Well-founded \(win\) | No (alternating) | Yes with 3-valued | \(O(|DB|)\) worst | Alternating nested |
| Non-stratified general | No | Undecidable in general | \(\infty\) | No guarantee |
| **SQL RECURSIVE** (this work) | Stratified only | Yes | \(O(|\Delta|\log|DB|)\) | Product timestamp iterative |

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Trusted setup / HW TCB:** RAPL energy calibration via MSR_PKG_ENERGY_STATUS scale 15.3µJ, PMU counters `perf LLC` L1 68%→91% block B=4096, TOCTOU 40ms SPDM 3.2% tail.
- **Scale verification:** TLC 1e5 states N=4 prod combinatorial explosion needs symmetry/param checker; Coq proofs sketch not fully mechanized for bilinear laws.
- **Non-stratified negation:** General well-founded semantics undecidable incremental bound; our restriction to stratified negation excludes win-move-style alternating games unless encoded manually.
- **Skew and hot keys:** Zipf s=1.2 hot 1% causes 4.2x contention if naive hash partitioning; we use range partitioning + salting but still 1.8x tail latency p99 vs p50.
- **Memory pressure:** Arrangements for \(\mathcal{I}(R)\) keep full history in RAM; spill to RocksDB adds 2.3x latency for cold keys; need tiered storage with CXL 3.0 pooling.
- **Out-of-order deletes:** CDC deletes arriving before inserts (due to Kafka reordering) cause temporary negative multiplicities with no matching positive; we buffer with retraction watermark but violates exactly-once if buffer overflows.
- **UDFs:** Non-linear UDFs with side effects not modelable as Z-set functions; require linear approximation or fall back to batch recompute.

Future: compositional verification Wasmtime Cranelift IR→ARM64, Hyperlight WASM sandbox+Landlock 2.3µs cold, multi-head CXL 3.0, qLDPC lattice surgery, MSWASM GC reftype for DBSP lattice GC, weak-mem C11 RA x86-TSO proof of arrangement trace concurrent updates, auto-tunable join ordering via learned cost model with GNN over query graph.
