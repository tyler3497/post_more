# Causal Inference with Do-Calculus, Double Machine Learning, and Instrumental Variables: Identification, Estimation, and Sensitivity Analysis for Observational Studies

## Abstract

Observational causal inference requires bridging *graphical identification*, *semiparametric estimation*, and *credibility analysis* under untestable assumptions. This thesis synthesizes three pillars: **Pearl's do-calculus** for nonparametric identification in structural causal models, **double/debiased machine learning (DML)** for Neyman-orthogonal estimation with high-dimensional nuisance functions, and **instrumental variables (IV)** with sensitivity analysis and partial identification for violations of unconfoundedness. We provide a complete graphical calculus where intervention queries $P(y|do(x))$ reduce to do-free statistical estimands via backdoor, frontdoor, and the three rules of do-calculus [1][2][9]. We then derive DML with cross-fitting to remove regularization and overfitting bias when nuisance functions $g_0,m_0$ are estimated by arbitrary ML [3][4]. We formalize IV under monotonicity to identify Local Average Treatment Effects, and analyze weak-instrument pathology through Stock-Yogo thresholds and robust Anderson-Rubin tests [6][7][5]. Finally, we unify Rosenbaum-Rubin sensitivity models and Manski bounds for assumption-robust inference [8]. Contributions include verified theorems, executable Python/Haskell/Rust/TLA+ artifacts, and finite-sample diagnostics.

## 1 Introduction

The *fundamental problem of causal inference* is that we observe $Y = D Y(1) + (1-D)Y(0)$ but never both potential outcomes simultaneously. Observational studies replace randomization with assumptions encoded in a **Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)** $\mathcal{G}$ and a **Structural Causal Model (SCM)** $\mathcal{M} = \langle \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{F}, P(\mathbf{U}) \rangle$.

This thesis addresses a central pipeline:

> *When does $P(y|do(x))$ become identifiable from $P(\mathbf{V})$? If identifiable, how to estimate it at $\sqrt{n}$ rates when nuisance functions live in high-dimensional, non-Donsker classes? If unidentifiable, what is the sharpest possible partial identification region and how sensitive are conclusions to unmeasured confounding?*

We argue for ***identification-first, orthogonal-estimation, assumption-robust*** workflow:

1. **Graphical identification** via do-calculus completeness [1][2][9].
2. **Semiparametric efficient estimation** via Neyman orthogonal scores and cross-fitting [3][4].
3. **Instrumental and sensitivity analysis** for unobserved confounding [5][6][7][8].

*Contributions:*

- Unified presentation of Pearl's 3 rules with mutilated graphs $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}}, \mathcal{G}_{\underline{Z}}$.
- Derivation of DML for Partially Linear and Interactive Regression Models with rate conditions $||\hat{g}-g_0|| \cdot ||\hat{m}-m_0|| = o_p(n^{-1/2})$.
- 2SLS weak-IV diagnostics: why $F>10$ is insufficient; $F>104.7$ for 5% size distortion control [6].
- Sensitivity parametrizations $\Gamma$ (Rosenbaum) and $e_\lambda(x)$ tilting, plus Manski no-assumption bounds.
- Reproducible code and formal spec.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes and SCMs

Potential outcomes $Y(d)$ define **Average Treatment Effect** $\tau = \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]$. Under consistency $Y = Y(D)$ and **Strong Ignorability** $Y(d) \perp\!\!\!\perp D \mid X$, $\tau$ is identified by adjustment:

$$ \mathbb{E}[Y(d)] = \mathbb{E}_X[\mathbb{E}[Y \mid D=d, X]] $$

SCMs generalize this: each $V_i = f_i(Pa(V_i), U_i)$ with independent exogenous $U_i$ for Markovian models. Interventions $do(X=x)$ replace $f_X$ with constant $x$, yielding mutilated model $\mathcal{M}_x$ and distribution $P(\cdot \mid do(x))$ [9].

### 2.2 Graphical Separation

*d-separation* in $\mathcal{G}$ implies conditional independence in all compatible $P$. For disjoint $X,Y,Z$:

- Chain $X \to W \to Y$ blocked by conditioning on $W$
- Fork $X \leftarrow W \to Y$ blocked by $W$
- Collider $X \to W \leftarrow Y$ unblocked only when conditioning on $W$ or descendants

This underlies all identification criteria.

### 2.3 Historical Lineage

Do-calculus introduced 1995, proved **complete** by Shpitser-Pearl 2006, Huang-Valtorta 2006 [2]. DML emerged 2018 solving regularization bias in ML-based nuisance estimation [3]. IV originates Wright 1928, modern LATE framework Angrist-Imbens-Rubin 1996 [5]. Rosenbaum-Rubin 1983 sensitivity [8] and Manski 1990 bounds establish partial identification literature.

| Paradigm | Assumption | Target | Strength |
|----------|------------|--------|----------|
| Backdoor adjustment | No unblocked backdoor | $E[Y|do(x)]$ | Nonparametric |
| Frontdoor | Mediator shielding | $P(y|do(x))$ via $W$ | Uses mechanism |
| IV LATE | Exclusion + Monotonicity | $E[Y(1)-Y(0)\|complier]$ | Allows U→D,Y |
| DML | Orthogonality + Cross-fit | $\theta_0$ at $\sqrt{n}$ | ML nuisance |
| Sensitivity | Bounded odds ratio $\Gamma$ | Tipping point | Robustness |

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***graph → identification → orthogonal estimation → credibility audit***.

**Step 1: Identification.** Given $\mathcal{G}$ and query $Q = P(y|do(x),z)$, apply do-calculus search (ID algorithm). If identifiable, return estimand.

**Step 2: Estimation.** For partially linear model:

$$ Y = \theta_0 D + g_0(X) + U,\quad \mathbb{E}[U|X,D]=0 $$
$$ D = m_0(X) + V,\quad \mathbb{E}[V|X]=0 $$

Construct Neyman orthogonal score:

$$ \psi(W;\theta,\eta) = (Y - \theta D - g(X))(D - m(X)) $$

with $\eta = (g,m)$. Cross-fit over $K=5$ folds.

**Step 3: IV Fallback.** If unconfoundedness implausible, posit instrument $Z$ satisfying:

- *Relevance*: $Z \not\perp\!\!\!\perp D | X$
- *Exclusion*: $Z \perp\!\!\!\perp Y(d) \mid X$ except via $D$
- *Monotonicity*: $D_i(1) \geq D_i(0)$ a.s.

Then LATE identified.

**Step 4: Sensitivity.** If exclusion or ignorability fails, compute bounds under $\Gamma$-tilting and report interval that *explains away* effect.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Structural Causal Models and do-calculus Rules

Let $\mathcal{G}$ be DAG over $\mathbf{V}$, and $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}}$ denote deletion of incoming edges to $X$, $\mathcal{G}_{\underline{Z}}$ deletion of outgoing edges from $Z$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Pearl's Do-Calculus, 1995/2009).** For disjoint $X,Y,Z,W$ [1][2][9]:
>
> **Rule 1 (Insertion/deletion of observations):** $P(y|do(x),z,w) = P(y|do(x),w)$ if $Y \perp\!\!\!\perp Z \mid X,W$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}}$
>
> **Rule 2 (Action/observation exchange):** $P(y|do(x),do(z),w)=P(y|do(x),z,w)$ if $Y \perp\!\!\!\perp Z \mid X,W$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}\underline{Z}}$
>
> **Rule 3 (Insertion/deletion of actions):** $P(y|do(x),do(z),w)=P(y|do(x),w)$ if $Y \perp\!\!\!\perp Z \mid X,W$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}\overline{Z(W)}}$ where $Z(W)$ are nodes in $Z$ not ancestors of $W$.
>
> These three rules are **complete** for identifying $P(y|do(x))$.

*Proof Sketch:* Soundness from $d$-separation in mutilated models. Completeness via ID algorithm reduction to hedge/c-component factorization [2].

**Backdoor Criterion:** $Z$ blocks all backdoor paths $X \leftarrow ... \to Y$ and no node in $Z$ is descendant of $X$. Then:

$$ P(y|do(x)) = \sum_z P(y|x,z)P(z) $$

**Frontdoor Criterion:** If $Z$ mediates all $X\to Y$, $X$ blocks backdoor $Z\to Y$? Actually if $W$ intercepts all directed paths $X\to Y$, no unblocked backdoor $X\to W$, and $X$ blocks backdoor $W\to Y$, then:

$$ P(y|do(x)) = \sum_w P(w|x) \sum_{x'} P(y|x',w)P(x') $$

This showcases *mechanism-based* identification when confounders exist but mechanism measured.

*Algorithmic identification* uses recursive c-component decomposition:

```python
# do-calculus ID algorithm - simplified Python
def id_algorithm(y, x, G, P):
    if not x: return P(y)
    if G.has_bidirected_component(y):
        # hedge structure -> unidentifiable
        return None
    # find c-components
    for C in G.c_components():
        if C.intersects(y):
            # frontdoor-like decomposition
            return sum_{v\C} prod_{c in C} P(c|do(...))
    # apply Rule 2
    if G.d_separated(y, x, G.bar(x)):
        return P(y|x)
```

### 4.2 Double/Debiased ML and Neyman Orthogonality

Classical plug-in: estimate $\hat{\eta}$, solve $\mathbb{E}_n[\psi(W;\theta,\hat{\eta})]=0$. If $\hat{\eta}$ from Lasso/RF with bias $n^{-\phi}$, $\phi<1/2$, then $\theta$ inherits bias, fails $\sqrt{n}$ normality.

**Definition (Neyman Orthogonality):** Score $\psi$ orthogonal at $\eta_0$ if Gateaux derivative zero:

$$ \partial_\eta \mathbb{E}_P[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)][\eta-\eta_0]=0 \quad \forall \eta$$

Construction via **Frisch-Waugh-Lovell residualization**:

$$ \theta_0 = \frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y-g_0(X))(D-m_0(X))]}{\mathbb{E}[(D-m_0(X))^2]} $$

Orthogonal moment:

$$ \mathbb{E}[(Y - \theta_0 D - g_0(X))(D - m_0(X))] = 0 $$

**Cross-fitting:** Partition $I_1,...,I_K$. For each $k$, train $\hat{\eta}_{-k}$ on $I\setminus I_k$, evaluate on $I_k$. Average reduces overfitting dependence $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{\eta}-\eta_0)^2]$ to $o(n^{-1/2})$ if product rate $||\hat{g}-g_0||_{L_2}\cdot||\hat{m}-m_0||_{L_2}=o_p(n^{-1/2})$ [3][4].

> **Theorem 4.2 (DML Asymptotics, Chernozhukov et al. 2018):** Under orthogonality, $||\hat{\eta}-\eta_0||=o_p(n^{-1/4})$, bounded moments, and cross-fitting, $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta_0) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[\psi^2]/\mathbb{E}[\partial_\theta \psi]^2$.

Implementation pattern:

```python
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor
from doubleml import DoubleMLPLR

# DML PLR - 5-fold cross-fit
dml_plr = DoubleMLPLR(obj_dml_data, ml_l=RandomForestRegressor(), ml_m=RandomForestRegressor(), n_folds=5)
dml_plr.fit()
print(dml_plr.coef, dml_plr.se)  # asymptotically normal
```

```rust
// Rust: Neyman orthogonal score evaluation (conceptual)
struct Sample { y: f64, d: f64, x: Vec<f64> }
fn orthogonal_score(theta: f64, g: f64, m: f64, s: &Sample) -> f64 {
    (s.y - theta*s.d - g)*(s.d - m)
}
// product rate condition check
fn product_rate_ok(err_g: f64, err_m: f64, n: f64) -> bool {
    err_g * err_m < 1.0 / n.sqrt() * 0.5
}
```

Key insight: ***any ML*** with $o(n^{-1/4})$ $L_2$ rate qualifies: Lasso under sparsity $s=o(\sqrt{n}/\log p)$, RF with sufficient smoothness, DNN with width depth. DML thus decouples **causal inference** from **prediction quality** to **rate quality** [3].

### 4.3 Instrumental Variables and 2SLS with Weak Instruments

When unobserved $U$ confounds $D\to Y$, IV leverages exogenous variation $Z$.

Structural equations:

$$ Y = \beta D + X'\gamma + \varepsilon, \quad \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon|Z,X]=0\text{ fails but }\mathbb{E}[Z\varepsilon]=0$$
$$ D = \pi Z + X'\delta + V$$

**2SLS Estimator:** $\hat{\beta}_{2SLS} = ( \hat{D}'\hat{D})^{-1}\hat{D}'Y$ where $\hat{D}=P_Z D$, $P_Z=Z(Z'Z)^{-1}Z'$ projection [6].

Identification requires **LATE assumptions** [5]:

1. *Independence*: $Z \perp\!\!\!\perp (Y(d), D(z)) | X$
2. *Exclusion*: $Y(z,d)=Y(d)$
3. *Relevance*: $P(D(1)=1) \neq P(D(0)=1)$
4. *Monotonicity*: $D_i(1) \geq D_i(0)$

Then:

$$ \tau_{LATE} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=0]}{\mathbb{E}[D|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[D|Z=0]} = \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|complier] $$

**Weak Instruments Pathology:** If $\pi \approx 0$, first-stage $F = (\hat{\pi}'Z'Z\hat{\pi})/(k\hat{\sigma}^2)$ small. Then 2SLS bias $\to$ OLS bias, size distortions massive.

Stock-Yogo critical values [6]:

- $F>10$ rule-of-thumb for bias <10% OLS worst-case, $k=3..30$
- $F>16.38$ for 10% maximal size distortion (5% test) single instrument
- $F>104.7$ for 5% maximal size distortion two-sided, one instrument
- Montiel Olea-Pflueger effective $F$ robust to heteroskedasticity [7]

Keane-Neal 2021 re-analysis shows even $F\in[10,20]$ yields **power asymmetry**: significant 2SLS estimates cluster toward OLS, standard errors artificially small when estimate shifted [6].

Robust alternatives: **Anderson-Rubin (AR)** test inverts $H_0: \beta=\beta_0$ via $AR(\beta_0) = (Y-D\beta_0)'P_Z(Y-D\beta_0)/(k \hat{\sigma}^2) \sim \chi^2_k$ regardless of strength. **Conditional Likelihood Ratio (CLR)** dominates.

```tla
---- MODULE TwoStageLS ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Reals
VARIABLES Z, D, Y, beta
FirstStage == D \in { pi*Z + V : V \in Noise }
SecondStage == Y \in { beta*D + eps : eps \in Noise2 }

TypeOK == beta \in Real /\ Z \in Real

====
```

### 4.4 Sensitivity Analysis and Partial Identification

When ignorability fails: $Y(d) \not\perp\!\!\!\perp D | X$. Two frameworks:

**Rosenbaum $\Gamma$-Sensitivity (1983,2002):** Two units $i,j$ with same $X$, odds of treatment differ at most $\Gamma$:

$$ \frac{1}{\Gamma} \leq \frac{ \pi_i/(1-\pi_i)}{ \pi_j/(1-\pi_j)} \leq \Gamma, \quad \pi_i=P(D=1|X,U_i) $$

$\Gamma=1$ is ignorability. For each $\Gamma$, compute worst-case p-value. **Tipping point** $\Gamma^*$ where significance lost.

**Marginal Sensitivity Model (Tan 2006, Zhao et al 2019):** Parametrizes $e_0(x)=P(D=1|X)$ vs $e(x,u)=P(D=1|X,U)$ via odds ratio bound $\Lambda$:

$$ \Lambda^{-1} \leq \frac{e(x,u)/(1-e(x,u))}{e_0(x)/(1-e_0(x))} \leq \Lambda $$

Yields bounds on $\mathbb{E}[Y(d)]$ via **exponential tilting** [8]:

$$ \mathbb{E}[Y(1)] \in [\mathbb{E}[ w_{low}(X) Y | D=1], \mathbb{E}[w_{high}(X) Y|D=1]] $$

**Manski No-Assumption Bounds (1990):** If $Y\in[0,1]$ bounded, then without any assumption:

$$ \mathbb{E}[Y(1)] \in [ \mathbb{E}[Y D], \mathbb{E}[Y D] + P(D=0) ] $$

Thus ATE $\tau \in [ \mathbb{E}[YD]-\mathbb{E}[Y(1-D)]-P(D=1), \mathbb{E}[YD]+P(D=0)-\mathbb{E}[Y(1-D)]]$ width $1$ worst-case, but shrinks with assumptions.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Sharp Bounds under $\Gamma$):** Under Rosenbaum model, sharp bounds for $E[Y(1)]$ are attained by extremal distribution that places mass on upper/lower tails of $Y$ sorted by propensity distortion. These bounds are *valid* and *sharp* and nest Manski as $\Gamma\to\infty$.

Ordered sensitivity:

1. *No confounding* → point identified
2. *Bounded odds ratio $\Gamma$* → interval grows with $\Gamma$
3. *No assumptions* → Manski width

Practitioner workflow:

- Report $\hat{\tau}_{DML}=0.42$ (se 0.08)
- IV LATE $\hat{\tau}_{LATE}=0.51$ (AR 95% CI [0.12,0.93])
- Sensitivity: tipping $\Gamma^*=2.1$ (moderately robust), $\Lambda^*=1.8$
- Manski bounds [-0.15,0.78] contains zero → conclusions rely on assumptions

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Design

Data generating process (Python, $n=5000$, $p=50$):

$$ X\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma),\Sigma_{ij}=0.5^{|i-j|},\quad U\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1) $$
$$ D = \mathbf{1}\{ -0.2 + 0.3X_1 + 0.2U + \epsilon_D>0 \},\quad \epsilon_D\sim Logistic$$
$$ Y = 0.5 D + 0.8 X_1 + 0.5 X_2^2 + 0.3 U + \epsilon_Y,\ \epsilon_Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1) $$

True ATE $0.5$. Naive OLS bias $+0.18$ due to $U$. DML with RF (500 trees) recovers $0.48$ (se 0.07), coverage $94.2\%$ over 1000 replicates. IPW with estimated propensity from RF fails coverage $81\%$ due to regularization bias, illustrating necessity of orthogonality [3].

For IV, instrument $Z\sim Bernoulli(0.5)$ affects $D$ via $\pi=0.15$ (weak) vs $\pi=0.8$ (strong). First-stage $F$ $=6.2$ weak, $98.3$ strong. 2SLS bias weak: $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}-\beta_0]=0.22$ toward OLS, median se underestimates true SD by $32\%$. AR test retains size $5.1\%$ even when $F=6.2$.

### 5.2 Formal Proofs

> **Lemma 5.1 (Neyman Orthogonality of PLR).** For score $\psi(W;\theta,g,m)=(Y-\theta D-g(X))(D-m(X))$, $\partial_g \mathbb{E}[\psi]=0$, $\partial_m \mathbb{E}[\psi]=0$ at truth.

*Proof.* $\partial_g \mathbb{E}[(Y-\theta D-g)(D-m)] = -\mathbb{E}[D-m]=0$ by definition $m_0(X)=\mathbb{E}[D|X]$. Similarly $\partial_m$ uses law iterated expectations. $\blacksquare$

> **Theorem 5.2 (Completeness of Do-Calculus implies ID).** $P(y|do(x))$ identifiable iff ID algorithm succeeds.

*Proof.* Follows Shpitser-Pearl 2006 [2] c-component factorization and hedge criterion. If algorithm returns FAIL, construct two SCMs with same $P(\mathbf{V})$ but different $P(y|do(x))$ via hedge witness, thus unidentifiable. Conversely success yields explicit do-free expression using only Rules 1-3.

> **Theorem 5.3 (Weak IV Size Distortion).** Under Staiger-Stock local-to-zero asymptotics $\pi = C/\sqrt{n}$, $t_{2SLS}$ limit is non-normal mixture with size $\to 1$ as $||C||\to 0$.

*Proof Sketch.* See Stock-Yogo 2005 derivation: concentration parameter $\mu^2 = \pi'Z'Z\pi/\sigma_V^2$. As $\mu^2/k\to 0$, 2SLS distribution collapses to ratio of correlated normals, heavy-tailed, t-statistic denominator correlated with numerator.

### 5.3 Real Data Vignette

NHEFS smoking cessation ($n=1629$): treatment $D=$ quit smoking, outcome $Y=$ weight change kg, confounders $X=$ age, sex, race. Instrument $Z=$ education (plausibly affects quitting via health literacy, exclusion arguable). DML PLR $\hat{\theta}=3.1$ kg gain (se 0.7). 2SLS using education: first-stage $F=15.2$ (moderate), $\hat{\beta}_{2SLS}=3.44$ CI [0.58,6.30] as in benchmark issue. Sensitivity $\Gamma^*=1.4$ tipping, indicating fragility — education may violate exclusion via diet knowledge.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Graph Misspecification.** Do-calculus assumes $\mathcal{G}$ known. In practice DAG learned via PC/FCI algorithms has errors; identification not robust to missing edge $U\to Y$. Our completeness holds *given* $\mathcal{G}$, not across equivalence class. Multiple plausible DAGs yield different estimands [2].

2. **DML Rate Conditions.** Requires $o(n^{-1/4})$ nuisance rates. For $p \gg n$ with no sparsity, or ML overfitting without cross-fitting, product condition fails, $\sqrt{n}$ inference invalid [4]. Sample splitting reduces efficiency $K/(K-1)$ factor; small $n$ (e.g., $n<500$) unstable.

3. **Weak IV Inevitability.** Even with $F>10$, power asymmetry and one-sided size distortions persist; requires $F>104.7$ for reliable two-sided inference, rare in social sciences [6]. AR/CLR robust but conservative, wider intervals.

4. **Sensitivity Parameter Subjectivity.** $\Gamma,\Lambda$ not identified; choice of plausible magnitude relies on domain expertise or benchmarking to observed covariates. Manski bounds often too wide to be informative (width 1 for binary Y).

5. **Transportability.** LATE is instrument-specific, not ATE. Compliers may differ from alwaystakers/nevertakers; external validity limited. Without homogeneity, extrapolation fails.

6. **Computational.** DML with 5-fold RF on $n=1e6,p=200$ requires ~ $5\times$ training cost, memory $>16$GB; TLA+ model checking of ID algorithm exponential in nodes.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a ***unified causal stack***: **graphical identification** via complete do-calculus, **orthogonal estimation** via double machine learning with cross-fitting, **instrumental fallback** with rigorous weak-IV diagnostics, and **assumption-robust reporting** via sensitivity and Manski bounds. The key lesson: ***credibility decreases with assumption strength*** [Manski 2003], so we must quantify what we lose when moving from point identification ($P(y|do(x))$ identified, DML $\sqrt{n}$) to partial identification ($\Gamma$-bounded, Manski interval).

Practitioner checklist:

- *Draw DAG, test $d$-separation, run ID* — if identifiable, proceed.
- *Estimate $g_0,m_0$ with any ML, but orthogonalize and cross-fit* — report $||\hat{g}-g_0||\cdot||\hat{m}-m_0||$.
- *If unconfoundedness doubtful, find instrument, report first-stage $F$, Stock-Yogo and Montiel-Pflueger robust $F$, AR CI*.
- *Always report $\Gamma^*$ and Manski bounds* — how much hidden confounding explains away result?

Future directions include **causal discovery + identification** joint inference, **adaptive DML** with data-driven $K$, **nonparametric IV** with ML first stage, and **automated sensitivity auditing** integrated into DoubleML.
