# De Novo Protein Design with Generative Diffusion Models: Backbone Diffusion, Sequence Design, and Experimental Validation from RFdiffusion to Chroma

## Abstract

Generative diffusion models have transformed *de novo* protein design from physics-based fragment assembly into a learnable inverse problem. This thesis examines the 2023 convergence of structure diffusion on the special Euclidean group SE(3) — **RFdiffusion**, which fine-tunes the RoseTTAFold structure prediction network into a denoising backbone generator; **Chroma**, a programmable generative model framing design as Bayesian inference under external constraints; and **FrameDiff**, a principled SE(3)-invariant diffusion framework operating on residue frames without pretrained predictors — together with the inverse-folding sequence design of **ProteinMPNN** and the *in silico* validation oracle of **AlphaFold2**. We formalize the forward and reverse diffusion processes over residue frames, analyze self-conditioning and partial diffusion, and dissect designability metrics based on predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) and interface predicted template modeling (ipTM) scores. Empirically, RFdiffusion achieves roughly 20% experimental success across binder, symmetric-oligomer, and motif-scaffolding tasks — approximately a hundredfold improvement over Rosetta-based pipelines — with cryo-EM confirmation at 0.63 Å backbone RMSD to the design model.

## 1 Introduction

Protein design poses the *inverse* of the folding problem: given a desired structure or function, find an amino acid sequence that realizes it. For decades, the field relied on physically motivated heuristics — fragment assembly, rotamer libraries, and empirical energy functions embodied in the Rosetta suite [1]. These methods produced landmark successes (notably Top7 and designed enzymes), yet they remained laborious, expert-driven, and largely confined to scaffolds already sampled by natural evolution.

Two developments broke this ceiling in rapid succession. First, deep-learning structure prediction — AlphaFold2 [5] and RoseTTAFold — solved the *forward* problem at near-experimental accuracy, learning a differentiable map from sequence to structure. Second, **denoising diffusion probabilistic models**, proven in image synthesis, were adapted to the geometry of protein backbones. The year 2023 delivered three complementary milestones:

- **RFdiffusion** [1]: a RoseTTAFold network fine-tuned on a structure-denoising objective, yielding a general generative model of backbones conditioned on motifs, symmetries, and binding targets, with extensive wet-lab validation.
- **Chroma** [2]: a programmable generative model that diffuses in a compressed representation of polymer conformational statistics, exposing *conditioners* for symmetry, shape, substructure, semantics, and natural-language prompts — design as Bayesian inference.
- **FrameDiff** [4]: the first principled SE(3)-invariant diffusion framework over residue frames, achieving competitive designability with a small network and no pretrained predictor.

The modern pipeline that emerged is now canonical:

```
RFdiffusion / Chroma / FrameDiff (backbone) → ProteinMPNN (sequence) → AlphaFold2 (validation) → wet-lab assay
```

This thesis reconstructs that pipeline from first principles: the mathematics of diffusion on Lie groups, the architectural choices that made RFdiffusion work, the conditioning machinery of Chroma, the principled score-matching of FrameDiff, the inverse-folding machinery of ProteinMPNN [3], and the experimental evidence — including picomolar peptide binders [6] and atomic-accuracy cryo-EM confirmation [1] — that distinguishes this era from every prior wave of computational protein design.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Score-based diffusion in Euclidean space

Denoising diffusion models define a *forward* Markov process that progressively corrupts data $\mathbf{x}_0$ into noise $\mathbf{x}_T$, and learn a *reverse* process that restores structure. In the discrete-time DDPM formulation, the forward kernel is Gaussian:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\, \beta_t \mathbf{I}\big), \qquad t = 1,\dots,T$$

with a noise schedule $\{\beta_t\}$. By the reparameterization trick, $\mathbf{x}_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, where $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t (1-\beta_s)$ and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$. The network is trained to predict the added noise (equivalently, the score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$) with the simple objective:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\big[\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\rVert^2\big]$$

In the continuous-time score-SDE view, the forward process is $d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}$, and sampling proceeds by simulating the reverse-time SDE or the corresponding probability-flow ODE. The crucial property for proteins is that the *target distribution is unknown*: unlike image datasets, there is no corpus of "functional de novo proteins" to fit — the model must learn the *manifold of physically realizable backbones* from the Protein Data Bank and then sample *novel* points on it.

### 2.2 The residue frame representation

A protein backbone of $N$ residues is most compactly described not by all-atom coordinates but by $N$ **frames** — rigid-body transforms $T_i = (R_i, \mathbf{t}_i) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, where $R_i \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$ is a rotation and $\mathbf{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ a translation anchored at the Cα atom. Idealized N–Cα–C–O coordinates are placed via:

$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{global}} = R_i \, \mathbf{x}_{\text{local}} + \mathbf{t}_i$$

This representation, inherited from AlphaFold2's structure module, is *complete* for the backbone: given the $N$ frames and idealized local geometry, all backbone atoms are determined. Diffusion can then operate on $\mathrm{SE}(3)^N$ — $N$ coupled copies of the Lie group — rather than $\mathbb{R}^{3N \times 4}$, dramatically reducing the effective dimension while preserving geometric meaning. The forward process factorizes:

- **Translations**: standard Gaussian diffusion in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
- **Rotations**: Brownian motion on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, whose transition density has the closed form $\mathrm{IGSO3}(\sigma_t^2)$ — the isotropic Gaussian distribution on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.

### 2.3 Equivariance: the non-negotiable symmetry

Physical laws do not depend on the laboratory frame. A generative model of proteins must therefore be **SE(3)-equivariant**: rotating and translating the input must rotate and translate the output identically. Violating this forces the network to waste capacity learning the symmetry from data — infeasible for a group of dimension 6 acting on thousands of atoms. RFdiffusion inherits equivariance from the RoseTTAFold architecture; FrameDiff [4] *constructs* it explicitly by designing the score network to be equivariant and the forward process to be invariant, yielding an invariant marginal distribution $p_T$ and, consequently, an equivariant reverse process.

### 2.4 Inverse folding and the sequence gap

Backbone diffusion generates *shapes*, not *proteins*: a shape becomes a protein only when an amino acid sequence is found that folds into it — the **inverse folding** problem. ProteinMPNN [3] solves this with a message-passing graph neural network over the backbone, achieving 52.4% native sequence recovery versus 32.9% for Rosetta, and — critically — robustness to *imperfect* backbones, since diffusion-generated backbones are idealizations that deviate from native-like geometry.

### 2.5 AlphaFold2 as the validation oracle

Before any wet-lab experiment, designed sequences are validated *in silico*: the sequence is fed to AlphaFold2, and the predicted structure is compared to the design target. The community converged on quantitative gates [1][2][4]:

| Metric | Meaning | Typical design threshold |
|---|---|---|
| pLDDT | Per-residue confidence (0–100) | > 70 (mean) |
| PAE | Predicted aligned error between residue pairs | low inter-domain PAE |
| Cα RMSD | AF2 prediction vs. design backbone | < 2 Å |
| ipTM / pTM | Interface / global topology confidence | > 0.5–0.8 |

A design "passes" when AlphaFold2 *independently recapitulates* the intended structure — the **self-consistency** criterion. Because AF2 was trained only on natural proteins, self-consistency of a wholly novel backbone is a stringent, nearly adversarial test of designability.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 RFdiffusion: a structure predictor turned generator

RFdiffusion [1] begins from a deceptively simple insight: **a structure prediction network is already a denoiser**. RoseTTAFold maps (sequence, MSA) to structure; if the input structure is *corrupted with noise* and the target is the *clean* structure, the same architecture learns $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ — a denoising model whose iterates, run in reverse, generate new structures. Concretely:

1. **Forward process.** Native PDB backbones are noised with a Gaussian (translations) and IGSO3 (rotations) schedule over $T = 200$ steps.
2. **Network.** The RoseTTAFold three-track architecture (1D sequence, 2D pair, 3D coordinate tracks) is fine-tuned on the denoising objective. Crucially, the **mean-squared-error loss** is *not* invariant to the global reference frame, which the authors found promotes continuity between adjacent timesteps — the reverse trajectory traces a smooth path rather than jumping between plausible structures.
3. **Self-conditioning.** Inspired by AlphaFold2's recycling, at each reverse step the model is additionally conditioned on its own previous prediction of $\mathbf{x}_0$. This dramatically improves *in silico* success rates: the trajectory commits early to a topology and refines it, rather than dithering.

Conditioning is implemented by *fixing* part of the input: for **motif scaffolding**, the functional motif's frames are held at their native geometry (optionally with partial noise); for **binder design**, the target protein's coordinates are supplied as context and the binder's frames are diffused around specified hotspot residues; for **symmetric oligomers**, the asymmetric unit is diffused and symmetry operators applied. A Python sketch of the sampling loop:

```python
import torch

def sample_rfdiffusion(model, length, T=200, condition=None, self_cond=True):
    # Initialize: random translations, uniform SO(3) rotations
    x_t = torch.randn(length, 3)          # C-alpha translations
    R_t = random_uniform_so3(length)       # residue rotations
    x0_prev = None
    for t in reversed(range(1, T + 1)):
        beta = noise_schedule(t)
        # Predict clean structure (optionally self-conditioned)
        x0_hat = model(x_t, R_t, t, x0_prev if self_cond else None,
                       condition=condition)
        # DDPM-style reverse step toward x_{t-1}
        x_t, R_t = reverse_step(x_t, R_t, x0_hat, beta)
        x0_prev = x0_hat
    return x0_hat
```

The model's generality is its headline property: one set of weights handles unconditional monomers, topology-constrained generation, binders, symmetric assemblies, enzyme active-site scaffolding, and symmetric motif scaffolding for therapeutic and metal-binding proteins [1].

### 3.2 FrameDiff: principled diffusion on SE(3)

FrameDiff [4] asks the foundational question RFdiffusion sidesteps: *what is the correct mathematical formulation of diffusion over multiple rigid frames?* Yim et al. develop SE(3)-invariant diffusion models by:

- Defining a forward process on $\mathrm{SE}(3)^N$ that is **invariant** under simultaneous global rigid motions: $p_t(g \cdot \mathbf{T}) = p_t(\mathbf{T})$ for all $g \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$.
- Learning an **equivariant score** $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{T}, t)$ with an SE(3)-equivariant graph network, so that the reverse process is equivariant and the induced data distribution is invariant.
- Using a zero-center-of-mass subspace to remove the trivial translational degree of freedom.

Remarkably, FrameDiff achieves designable monomers up to **500 residues** with only 17.4M parameters and *no* pretrained structure predictor — a proof that the frame-diffusion formulation is sound in its own right, not merely a parasite on AlphaFold-era pretraining. Its successor FrameFlow [7] replaced diffusion with SE(3) flow matching, obtaining roughly twice the designability at five times fewer sampling steps.

### 3.3 Chroma: design as Bayesian inference

Chroma [2] takes a different and complementary route. Rather than diffusing full frame coordinates, it diffuses in a **compressed, polymer-statistics-aware representation**, synthesized back into 3D coordinates by learned layers from predicted inter-residue geometries. Three innovations stand out:

1. **Random graph neural networks** with sub-quadratic scaling ($O(N \log N)$), inspired by fast N-body methods: each residue connects to deterministic local neighbors plus stochastically sampled long-range neighbors with distance-based attachment propensity $c(D_{ij}) \propto D_{ij}^{-3}$, sampled via the Gumbel top-$k$ trick. This enables generation of thousand-residue complexes on standard hardware.
2. **Conditioners as Bayesian posteriors.** Chroma formalizes design as sampling from $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{c}) \propto p(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{c} \mid \mathbf{x})$: the unconditional prior times a conditioner likelihood. Classifier models trained on *noised* structures implement $p(\mathbf{c} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ for symmetries, shapes, substructures, secondary-structure content, and even natural-language annotations — composed at sampling time without retraining.
3. **Low-temperature sampling.** A general algorithm for sampling diffusion models at reduced temperature sharpens the distribution toward high-designability regions.

Chroma *jointly* models sequence and structure and was experimentally validated on **310 expressed proteins** [2].

### 3.4 ProteinMPNN: robust inverse folding

Given a backbone $\mathbf{X}$, ProteinMPNN [3] models $p_\theta(\mathbf{s} \mid \mathbf{X})$ autoregressively in a random decoding order with a message-passing encoder over the backbone graph. Two design decisions matter for the diffusion pipeline:

- **Noise-robust training.** Coordinates are perturbed during training, so the model tolerates the non-ideal geometry of generated backbones — diffusion outputs are not crystallographic structures, and a brittle inverse folder would fail on them.
- **Coupled positions.** Residues on single or multiple chains can be tied (homooligomer symmetry) or locked (interface hotspots), enabling binder and symmetric-complex sequence design.

The Rust-flavored pseudocode of the pipeline glue:

```rust
struct Design {
    backbone: Vec<Frame>,   // SE(3)^N from diffusion
    sequence: Vec<AminoAcid>,
    plddt: f32,
    rmsd_af2: f32,
}

fn design_pipeline(target: &Condition) -> Vec<Design> {
    let backbones = rfdiffusion_sample(target, n = 1000);      // or Chroma / FrameDiff
    let mut designs = Vec::new();
    for bb in backbones {
        let seq = protein_mpnn(&bb, temperature = 0.1, n_seqs = 8);
        for s in seq {
            let pred = alphafold2(&s);                          // oracle
            if passes_designability(&pred, &bb) { designs.push(Design{..}); }
        }
    }
    designs
}
```

### 3.5 The validation oracle

AlphaFold2 [5] closes the loop. For each designed sequence, a single-sequence (MSA-free) AF2 prediction is compared against the design backbone; designs with Cα RMSD < 2 Å and mean pLDDT > 70 proceed. For binders, AF2-Multimer-style interface metrics (ipTM) and interface PAE rank candidates. This *in silico* triage, applied at thousand-fold scale, is what converts diffusion's raw diversity into experimentally tractable shortlists.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Brownian motion on SO(3) and the IGSO3 kernel

The rotation component is where naive diffusion fails: adding Gaussian noise to rotation matrices leaves the manifold. The correct forward process is **Brownian motion on SO(3)**, with transition density given by the heat kernel expansion:

$$p_t(R' \mid R) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} (2\ell+1)\, e^{-\ell(\ell+1)t/2}\, \frac{\sin\big((\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})\,\omega\big)}{\sin(\omega/2)}$$

where $\omega$ is the rotation angle of $R^{-1}R'$. In practice this infinite series is truncated (or approximated by the **IGSO3** sampler with a numerically stable CDF inversion), giving a forward kernel from which one can sample and for which the score $\nabla_R \log p_t$ is tractable. The key subtlety handled by FrameDiff [4] is that the *joint* process over $N$ frames must remain invariant to a *single global* rigid motion while allowing *relative* motions between residues to diffuse — achieved by diffusing in the zero-center-of-mass subspace with a shared global rotation applied to the noise.

A TLA+ sketch of the schedule invariant the sampler must maintain:

```tla
------------------------------ MODULE DiffusionSchedule ------------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, Reals
CONSTANTS T  \* number of diffusion steps
VARIABLES t
Init == t = T
Next == /\ t > 0
        /\ t' = t - 1
\* The cumulative noise level must be monotone decreasing along reverse steps:
\* reverse step k (from t to t-1) strictly reduces expected noise.
MonotoneNoise ==
    LET alphaBar(k) == PRODUCT s \in 1..k : (1 - beta(s))
    IN  \A k \in 1..T : alphaBar(k) < alphaBar(k-1)
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_t /\ WF_t(Next)
=============================================================================
```

### 4.2 Self-conditioning and partial diffusion: two faces of trajectory control

**Self-conditioning** feeds the network's own previous estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^{(t+1)}$ as an auxiliary input at step $t$. Viewed information-theoretically, it converts the Markov chain into a *self-correcting* dynamical system: early steps fix a coarse topology, and later steps refine within its basin. RFdiffusion's ablations show this single trick accounts for a large share of its *in silico* success gain [1].

**Partial diffusion** runs the forward process only to an intermediate $t_0 \ll T$ on an *existing* structure, then denoises. This samples the *neighborhood* of a known fold — the basis of RFdiffusion's binder *optimization* mode, which improved Rosetta ddG estimates by 21–29% and contact molecular surface by up to 33% on glucagon and NPY peptide binders [6]. In Haskell, the idea is a pure function over trajectories:

```haskell
-- Partial diffusion: noise an existing backbone to t0, then denoise.
partialDiffuse :: Model -> Backbone -> Int -> Backbone
partialDiffuse model bb t0 =
    let noisy  = foldl (\x t -> forwardStep  t x) bb [1..t0]
        clean  = foldl (\x t -> reverseStep model t x) noisy (reverse [1..t0])
    in clean
-- t0 = T recovers unconditional generation; t0 small recovers local refinement.
```

Together these give diffusion models a *dial* between exploration and exploitation that energy-based methods lack: unconditional sampling for novelty, partial diffusion for affinity maturation.

### 4.3 Conditioning as Bayesian inference: motifs, symmetry, and binders

Chroma's framing — design as $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{c}) \propto p(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{c} \mid \mathbf{x})$ — unifies the conditional tasks [2]:

- **Motif scaffolding.** The conditioner $p(\text{motif} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ pins a functional substructure (e.g., an enzyme active site or a metal-binding motif) while the rest of the protein is freely generated. RFdiffusion solved **23 of 25** benchmark scaffolding problems, versus 15 for the prior state of the art (hallucination) [1].
- **Symmetric oligomers.** Conditioning on cyclic $C_n$, dihedral $D_n$, or cubic point-group symmetry generates the asymmetric unit; 87 of 608 RFdiffusion symmetric designs were confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography, with negative-stain EM validation [1].
- **Binder design.** The target structure and interface hotspots condition generation of a complementary binder. RFdiffusion binders to five targets (influenza HA, IL-7Rα, insulin receptor, PD-L1, TrkA) succeeded at rates roughly **two orders of magnitude** above previous Rosetta campaigns — one order attributable to diffusion itself, one to AF2 filtering [1].

> **Theorem (Designability concentration).** *Let $p_\theta$ be a reverse diffusion process whose terminal law concentrates on the manifold $\mathcal{M}$ of physically realizable backbones, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be the AlphaFold2 self-consistency filter. Under low-temperature sampling, the pushforward of the filtered ensemble concentrates on $\mathcal{M} \cap \mathcal{F}^{-1}(1)$ — the designable submanifold — at a rate controlled by the temperature parameter. In practice: unfiltered diffusion gives diversity; filtered, low-temperature diffusion gives experimentally tractable candidates.*

### 4.4 The designability metric and its discontents

The field's working definition — *AF2 recapitulates the design* — is powerful but circular in one precise sense: both the generator (RFdiffusion, via RoseTTAFold) and the filter (AF2) are trained on the PDB, so the filter may reward *PDB-likeness* rather than *foldability*. Three mitigations are now standard:

1. **Orthogonal predictors**: cross-check with ESMFold or RoseTTAFold, which have different failure modes.
2. **Experimental calibration**: RFdiffusion's reported ~19–20% wet-lab success rates were measured *after* filtering, establishing the filter's positive predictive value on real assays [1].
3. **Novelty controls**: FrameDiff samples were shown to generalize *beyond* any known structure (low TM-score to the PDB), proving the filter is not merely memorizing [4].

### 4.5 Noise-robust inverse folding

ProteinMPNN's contribution is easily underestimated. Diffusion backbones are *idealized*: bond geometries are perfect, and the frames lie exactly on the learned manifold rather than at crystallographic coordinates. A sequence-design network trained only on native structures would treat these as out-of-distribution. ProteinMPNN's coordinate-noise augmentation [3] explicitly trains the model to be invariant to such deviations, and its empirical payoff — rescuing previously failed Rosetta and AlphaFold-designed monomers, cyclic oligomers, and nanoparticles, confirmed by X-ray and cryo-EM — makes it the indispensable second stage of the pipeline.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Headline experimental results

| System | Task | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFdiffusion | Motif scaffolding (25 benchmarks) | 23/25 solved (hallucination: 15) | [1] |
| RFdiffusion | Symmetric oligomers | 87/608 confirmed by SEC + nsEM | [1] |
| RFdiffusion | Binders, 5 targets | ~19% success, ~100× over Rosetta | [1] |
| RFdiffusion | HA binder HA_20 | $K_D$ = 28 nM; cryo-EM 2.9 Å, 0.63 Å RMSD to design | [1] |
| RFdiffusion | Peptide binders (PTH, Bim) | picomolar affinity; PYY 24.5 nM | [6] |
| Chroma | Programmable generation | 310 proteins experimentally characterized | [2] |
| FrameDiff | Unconditional monomers | designable to 500 residues, 17.4M params | [4] |
| ProteinMPNN | Native sequence recovery | 52.4% vs. 32.9% (Rosetta) | [3] |
| FrameFlow | SE(3) flow matching | ~2× designability, 5× fewer steps than FrameDiff | [7] |

The **HA_20** result deserves emphasis: a cryo-EM structure of a computationally designed binder in complex with influenza hemagglutinin matches the design model to 0.63 Å backbone RMSD [1] — atomic-level accuracy from a model that began as Gaussian noise. The peptide-binder campaign [6] pushed further into therapeutically relevant space: *de novo* binders to parathyroid hormone and Bim with picomolar affinities, designed from nothing but the target peptide sequence and structure.

### 5.2 Why the numbers moved: a decomposition

Three factors compound multiplicatively:

1. **Backbone quality.** Diffusion explores the full SE(3)$^N$ manifold conditioned on the task, rather than assembling fragments; shape complementarity at interfaces is designed *directly* (partial diffusion raised contact surface 29–33% [6]).
2. **Sequence robustness.** ProteinMPNN converts ~50%+ of plausible backbones into expressible sequences [3], versus single-digit percentages for Rosetta protocols on novel folds.
3. **Oracle filtering.** AF2 self-consistency prunes the long tail of undesignable samples before any protein is expressed; the two-orders-of-magnitude binder improvement decomposes as ~10× from diffusion and ~10× from filtering [1].

### 5.3 A proof sketch: invariance implies equivariant generation

> **Theorem (Invariant forward ⇒ equivariant reverse).** *If the forward diffusion on $\mathrm{SE}(3)^N$ satisfies $p_t(g\mathbf{T}) = p_t(\mathbf{T})$ for all $g \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, and the learned score is equivariant, $\mathbf{s}_\theta(g\mathbf{T}, t) = g \cdot \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{T}, t)$, then the reverse-time SDE generates an SE(3)-invariant distribution.*

*Proof sketch.* The reverse SDE drift is $\mathbf{f} - g^2 \mathbf{s}_\theta$; invariance of the forward marginals implies the true score transforms equivariantly, and the learned equivariant score preserves this under the reverse flow. Integrating an equivariant vector field from an invariant initial law ($p_T$ uniform on the compact rotation factor, Gaussian on translations in the zero-CoM subspace) yields an invariant terminal law. ∎

This is the formal content of FrameDiff's construction [4] and the reason its 17.4M parameters suffice: the network never learns the symmetry — it is *built in*.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Backbone-only generation.** RFdiffusion and FrameDiff generate Cα frames; sidechains, ligands, and cofactors are absent. RFdiffusion-AA extends to all-atom contexts, but small-molecule and metal coordination remain frontier problems.
2. **Static structures, not ensembles.** Diffusion samples single conformations; allostery, induced fit, and conformational dynamics — central to enzyme and signaling function — are not modeled. A binder designed against one target conformation may fail against another.
3. **Oracle bias.** The AF2 filter inherits PDB biases: it rewards folds near the training distribution and may reject genuinely novel but foldable topologies (the novelty–designability trade-off documented in [4]).
4. **Function beyond binding.** Experimental validation concentrates on *binding* and *assembly* — the easiest functions to assay at scale. Catalytic rate enhancement by diffusion-designed enzymes remains largely undemonstrated.
5. **Data and license constraints.** All models train on the PDB (~200k structures), a biased, finite sample of protein space; Chroma's license restricts commercial use [2].
6. **Throughput and cost.** Reverse diffusion requires hundreds of network evaluations per backbone; even FrameFlow's 5× speedup [7] leaves large-scale campaigns GPU-intensive, and wet-lab validation (expression, SEC, BLI, cryo-EM) remains the true bottleneck.

## 7 Conclusion

The diffusion revolution in protein design is not a single model but a *pipeline* whose stages are now independently mature: principled SE(3) diffusion (FrameDiff [4]) and its flow-matching successors [7] formalize the geometry; RFdiffusion [1] demonstrates that fine-tuning a structure predictor yields a universal conditional generator with ~20% experimental success and atomic-accuracy cryo-EM validation; Chroma [2] shows that design is Bayesian inference under composable conditioners; ProteinMPNN [3] closes the sequence gap with noise-robust inverse folding; and AlphaFold2 [5] provides the *in silico* oracle that makes the whole enterprise economically viable. The picomolar peptide binders [6] and the 0.63 Å HA_20 structure are existence proofs that *de novo* proteins can now be specified almost as freely as images are prompted — constrained, validated, and experimentally realized. The remaining frontiers — all-atom and ligand-aware generation, dynamics, catalysis, and closed-loop experimental optimization — define the next thesis.
