# Univalent Foundations in Practice: Cubical Type Theory, Higher Inductive Types, and Certified Homotopy in Cubical Agda

## Abstract

##1 Introduction

There was, however, a foundational embarrassment at the center of the program. In Martin-Löf type theory extended with univalence **as an axiom**, the computational interpretation collapses: a closed `ℕ` term built with the axiom need not reduce to a numeral, and transporting along an equivalence from univalence is stuck, with no reduction rule to fire. The theory is consistent — Voevodsky's Kan simplicial sets model guarantees that — but not *computational*. For a program promising "convenient machine implementations" [1], this was a serious deficit: the flagship axiom could not be *run*.

The resolution came from **cubical sets**. Bezem, Coquand, and Huber modeled univalence in cubical sets within a constructive metatheory, showing its computational content was real. Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg then internalized the cube structure into the syntax itself, producing **cubical type theory** (CTT) [2]: points, lines, squares, and cubes manipulated directly; function extensionality *provable by definition*; univalence not postulated but **proved**, with definitional computation rules. Decomposing composition into homogeneous composition and generalized transport [4] made higher inductive types directly representable, and the resulting theory was implemented as **Cubical Agda** [3]: a full dependently typed language with native univalence and higher inductive types.

This article is a technical reconstruction of that trajectory, aimed at the reader who knows dependent type theory but has not internalized the cubical picture. Our central claim is methodological: cubical type theory converts univalent foundations from an *axiomatic* posture into an *engineering* one, and the discipline's maturity can be measured empirically — by what can now be formalized, and above all by what can now be **computed**. Our benchmark is the most demanding one available: synthetic homotopy theory, culminating in the Brunerie number — a natural number extracted from a synthetic proof that π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ [5], recently normalized to −2 by the Cubical Agda evaluator [6].

##2 Background

### Identity types and the homotopy interpretation

In Martin-Löf type theory, every type `A` and elements `x y : A` come with an **identity type** `x ≡ y` (written `Id_A(x,y)` in the literature), generated by `refl : x ≡ x` and eliminated by **path induction** `J`: to prove `P x y p` for all paths `p : x ≡ y`, it suffices to handle the case `p ≡ refl`. The eliminator computes definitionally on `refl`, and its most-used instance is **transport**: given `P : A → Type`, `p : x ≡ y`, and `t : P x`, we obtain `transport P p t : P y`.

The homotopy interpretation (Awodey–Warren; Voevodsky) reads this structure geometrically: a type is a *space*, a term is a *point*, an identification is a *path*, an identification between identifications is a *homotopy*, and so on — a weak ∞-groupoid. Under this reading, `J` is path induction in the topologist's sense, and transport is parallel transport along a path. The interpretation is not mere analogy: it is validated by models (simplicial sets, cubical sets) and it *suggests* new principles. The most important is univalence.

### The univalence axiom

Fix a universe `U` of small types. There is a canonical map

```
id-to-equiv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)
```

sending `refl` to the identity equivalence. **Univalence** asserts that this map is itself an equivalence:

> **Theorem:** *(Univalence axiom [1].)* For small types `A B : U`, the canonical map `id-to-equiv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence. Equivalently, `(A ≡ B) ≃ (A ≃ B)`: identity of types coincides with equivalence of types.

The consequences are sweeping. **Function extensionality** — pointwise equal functions are equal — follows from univalence [1]. The **structure identity principle** follows: to give an identification of groups (rings, categories, …) is to give an isomorphism, so all type-theoretic properties are automatically isomorphism-invariant. Mathematicians' informal practice of "identifying isomorphic structures" becomes a theorem rather than an abuse of language.

But in Book HoTT [1], univalence is an *axiom*: a postulated constant `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A ≡ B)` with no computation rule. Apply `transport` along `ua e` and the term is stuck — the evaluator has no rule for reducing it. Worse, **canonicity fails**: there exist closed terms of type `ℕ` in MLTT + univalence-as-axiom that do not reduce to numerals. The theory proves the right things but cannot run them.

### Higher inductive types

Ordinary inductive types are specified by *point constructors* (e.g., `zero`, `suc` for `ℕ`). **Higher inductive types (HITs)** additionally specify *path constructors*: identifications between points, identifications between paths, and so on. The circle is the prototypical HIT:

```
base : S¹        -- a point
loop : base ≡ base  -- a generating loop
```

together with an induction principle saying that to map out of `S¹` it suffices to give the image of `base` and a loop over it. Propositional truncation `∥A∥`, suspensions, pushouts, and tori are all HITs. Combined with univalence, HITs permit an entirely new "logic of homotopy types" [1]: spheres, fibrations, and exact sequences defined *inside* the logic, with homotopy groups `πₙ(A)` defined directly from iterated identity types. This is **synthetic homotopy theory**: homotopy theory done axiomatically, in the language, rather than analytically via simplicial sets.

### Cubical sets and the constructive model

Voevodsky's consistency proof for univalence uses Kan simplicial sets and classical logic; it explains *that* univalence is consistent but not *how it computes*. Bezem, Coquand, and Huber gave instead a model in **cubical sets** — presheaves on a category of cubes — carried out in a constructive metatheory. Cubes are computationally friendlier than simplices: they have a direct product structure (`Iⁿ`), and Kan *filling* conditions can be stated uniformly. Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg then took the decisive step: rather than merely modeling type theory in cubical sets, they **added the interval and cube structure to the type theory itself** [2].

##3 Methodology

### The cubical method

The cubical method replaces the inductively generated identity type with a **path type internalizing the interval**. The theory extends dependent type theory with:

We follow Cubical Agda [3], which decomposes CCHM composition into **homogeneous composition** (`hcomp`) and **generalized transport** (`transp`) [4]. This decomposition makes higher inductive types *directly representable*: constructors compute definitionally, and pattern matching on HITs works as programmers expect.

### Empirical method: synthetic homotopy theory as benchmark

A foundations program claiming computational meaning must be tested on hard computations:

1. **Formalize** synthetic homotopy theory in Cubical Agda (the `agda/cubical` library), tracking where definitional equalities replace manual proof steps.
2. **Measure computation**: normalize closed `ℤ` terms extracted from synthetic proofs — the Brunerie number experiment [6].
3. **Compare** against axiomatic Book HoTT on identical theorems (e.g., torus ≃ S¹ × S¹), counting obligations discharged by `refl`.
4. **Stress-test** with the 2024 synthetic cohomology development [7], whose new Brunerie-like numbers locate the current computational frontier.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Paths as functions out of the interval

The single most liberating idea of cubical type theory is that a path is not an inductively generated object but a **function of an interval variable**. Where Book HoTT declares `refl` and the eliminator `J`, CTT declares:

```agda
-- PathP A a₀ a₁: paths in the line of types A from a₀ to a₁
-- p : PathP A a₀ a₁   means   p : (i : I) → A i,  p i0 = a₀,  p i1 = a₁
```

Path application `p i` is ordinary function application. Reflexivity is the constant path `refl x = λ i → x`. And function extensionality — an *axiom* in Book HoTT, a *consequence of univalence* in [1] — becomes a **one-line definition**:

```agda
funExt : {f g : (x : A) → B x} → ((x : A) → f x ≡ g x) → f ≡ g
funExt h i x = h x i
```

Given pointwise paths `h x : f x ≡ g x`, the path between functions at interval point `i` is the function sending `x` to `h x i`. No axiom, no stuck transport: `funExt h i0` *is* `f` definitionally. This pattern — exchanging a transport problem for a *construction in one higher dimension* — is the cubical method in miniature, and it recurs at every level of the theory.

Path induction itself is recoverable: the eliminator `J` is definable from composition (see §4.2), and it satisfies its computation rule definitionally. Nothing of MLTT is lost; what is gained is that equalities *compute*.

### 4.2 Composition, Glue, and computational univalence

The interval alone does not make paths an ∞-groupoid: paths must *compose* and cubes must *fill*. CTT provides the **Kan operations**. In the Cubical Agda decomposition [4], these are:

- **Homogeneous composition** `hcomp`: given a type `A`, a partial element on a face formula `φ` (a system of compatible tubes), and a base cap, produce the composite — the missing lid of the open box.
- **Generalized transport** `transp`: given `A : I → Type` and `a : A i0`, transport `a` to `A i1`, with `transp (λ _ → A) = id` definitionally on constant lines.

Path composition is then a two-dimensional filling:

```agda
_∙_ : {x y z : A} → x ≡ y → y ≡ z → x ≡ z
p ∙ q i = hcomp (λ j → λ { (i = i0) → x
                         ; (i = i1) → q j }) (p i)
```

The system specifies the left tube (constant `x`) and right tube (`q`), and `hcomp` fills the square; the lid is the composite path. Squares, cubes, and higher fillers are handled uniformly — the cubical operations are *dimension-agnostic*, which is why the theory scales where simplicial approaches drown in combinatorics.

**Glue types** are the masterstroke. `Glue B [ φ ↦ (T , e) ]` equals `B` everywhere but *is* `T` on the face `φ`, where `e : T ≃ B` witnesses the identification. Univalence is then a construction, not a postulate:

```agda
ua : {A B : Type} → A ≃ B → A ≡ B
ua e i = Glue B (λ { (i = i0) → (A , e)
                  ; (i = i1) → (B , idEquiv B) })
```

At `i = i0` the Glue type is `A`; at `i = i1` it is `B`; the equivalence `e` glues the faces together. And crucially, transport along `ua e` **computes**:

```agda
uaβ : (e : A ≃ B) (x : A) → transport (ua e) x ≡ equivFun e x
uaβ e x = refl   -- holds definitionally: transport reduces to e x
```

> **Theorem:** *(Computational univalence [2].)* The canonical map `id-to-equiv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence, and transporting along `ua e` reduces definitionally to applying `e`. In particular, closed terms of type `ℕ` in cubical type theory evaluate to numerals (canonicity, Huber).

The canonicity result is the precise sense in which the cubical program succeeded where the axiomatic one could not: univalence finally *runs*.

### 4.3 Higher inductive types with definitional computation

In Book HoTT, HITs are specified by an *axiomatic schema*: each HIT comes with postulated constructors and a postulated induction principle whose computation rules hold only propositionally, so eliminations get stuck. In Cubical Agda [3, 4], HITs are native declarations with **definitional** computation on constructors. The circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

Pattern matching on `S¹` requires clauses for `base` and for `loop i`, where `i : I` is bound as an interval variable — the path constructor is matched *as a function of the interval*, exactly as §4.1 suggests. The universal cover (the "helix") is then definable by recursion:

```agda
sucPath : ℤ ≡ ℤ
sucPath = ua sucEquiv

helix : S¹ → Type
helix base     = ℤ
helix (loop i) = sucPath i
```

The encode–decode method [1] turns this fibration into an equivalence `(base ≡ x) ≃ helix x` for all `x : S¹`, yielding `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` — the integers as the winding numbers of loops, computed *inside* the theory. The proof that made the Cubical Agda paper [3] famous is the torus:

```agda
data Torus : Type where
  point : Torus
  line1 : point ≡ point
  line2 : point ≡ point
  square : PathP (λ i → line1 i ≡ line1 i) line2 line2
```

The equivalence `Torus ≃ S¹ × S¹` is established by maps defined by pattern matching on all four constructors, and the inverse proofs are **`refl` in each case** — the higher constructor `square` computes definitionally, so there is nothing left to prove. In Book HoTT the same theorem needed a delicate argument precisely because higher constructors did not compute. Via `ua`, the equivalence becomes an *identification* `Torus ≡ S¹ × S¹`, and every construction transports definitionally.

Propositional truncation `∥A∥` (with constructor `squash : (x y : ∥A∥) → x ≡ y`), suspensions `ΣA`, pushouts, and spheres `Sⁿ` are all instances of the same schema, and all compute. The HIT schema of [4] is what elevates Cubical Agda from a curiosity to a laboratory: synthetic homotopy theory can be *programmed*.

### 4.4 Synthetic homotopy theory: loop spaces, fibrations, and the road to π₄(S³)

With univalence and HITs computing, the synthetic program of [1] becomes practical. The homotopy groups are defined directly from identity types:

```
πₙ(A, a) := ∥ Ωⁿ(A, a) ∥₀      -- 0-truncation of the n-fold loop space
```

where `Ω(A, a) := (a ≡ a)`. The Eckmann–Hilton argument — that `π₂` and above are abelian — is a short cubical calculation about the interchange of two-dimensional composition. The Hopf fibration is constructed synthetically from the circle's multiplication; the James construction gives the Freudenthal suspension theorem; smash products acquire their symmetric monoidal structure.

The summit of the synthetic program is **Brunerie's thesis** [5]: a fully synthetic, constructive proof that

> **Theorem:** *(Brunerie [5].)* `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ`, and more generally `πₙ₊₁(Sⁿ) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ` for all `n ≥ 3`.

### 4.5 The Brunerie number as a computation

In axiomatic HoTT the Brunerie number is uncomputable *in practice*: its definition threads through the univalence axiom, and every transport along `ua` is stuck. Replayed in Cubical Agda, where `ua` computes, the number is just a program. Ljungström and Mörtberg [6] formalized a streamlined version of Brunerie's argument, avoiding the cohomology chapters, and obtained a new Brunerie number `β′ : ℤ` with `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/β′ℤ` proved. They then asked the evaluator to normalize `β′`:

> **Theorem:** *(Ljungström–Mörtberg [6].)* The Brunerie number `β′ : ℤ`, defined synthetically in Cubical Agda with `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/β′ℤ` proved, normalizes to **−2** in a few seconds.

(The sign is an orientation artifact; `+2` comes from a minor variant.) This is a landmark: the first non-trivial invariant of classical algebraic topology — the order of `π₄(S³)`, whose classical proof spans textbooks — **computed by evaluating a proof term**. Univalence does not merely hold; it *runs*, and what it runs is real mathematics.

##5 Empirical and Proof-Theoretic Evidence

The evidence for the cubical program is now threefold — metatheoretic, formalizational, and computational.

**Metatheory.** Huber proved *canonicity* for CTT via a cubical logical-relations (sconing) argument: every closed term of boolean or natural-number type reduces to a constructor — precisely the theorem that fails for MLTT + axiomatic univalence. The interval, composition, and Glue operations all have specified reduction behavior, so the evaluator never gets stuck on univalence the way Book HoTT's does.

**Formalization at scale.** The `agda/cubical` library is the living record: thousands of lemmas covering equivalences, univalence, HITs, synthetic homotopy theory, and synthetic cohomology, all machine-checked. The recurring experience is that proofs requiring pages of path algebra in Book HoTT collapse to `refl` once the cubical operations compute. The 2024 development of computational synthetic cohomology [7] pushed further, formalizing cohomology rings of spheres, tori, and projective spaces entirely in Cubical Agda.

Representative tasks compared:

| Task | Book HoTT (axiomatic) | Cubical Agda |
|---|---|---|
| Function extensionality | Axiom; stuck | Theorem; `funExt h i x = h x i` |
| Transport along `ua e` | Stuck term | Reduces to `e x` (`uaβ`) |
| Torus ≃ S¹ × S¹ inverse proofs | Non-trivial path algebra | `refl` on all constructors |
| Canonicity at `ℕ` | Fails | Holds (Huber) |
| Brunerie number `β` | Uncomputable (stuck) | Normalizes to −2 in seconds [6] |
| Cohomology ring computations | Axiomatic only | New computable "Brunerie-like" numbers [7] |

The last row points at the current frontier: the synthetic cohomology project [7] defines a family of numbers analogous to Brunerie's, some of which remain infeasible to normalize — a ready-made benchmark suite for the next generation of cubical evaluators.

##6 Limitations

Candor requires stressing what the cubical program has *not* achieved.

**Performance of normalization.** Computational univalence is not free univalence. Transports, compositions, and Glue unfoldings generate large proof terms, and normalization can be explosively slow: several Brunerie-like numbers of [7] remain infeasible to compute in Cubical Agda. The evaluator is a research artifact, not an optimizing compiler. Alternatives (`cubicaltt`, `redtt`, `cooltt`) explore different operational semantics, but none yet combines Cubical Agda's expressiveness with dramatically better performance.

**Expressiveness boundaries.** Cubical Agda's HIT schema does not cover every shape of higher inductive definition (notably certain indexed and infinitary HITs), and Glue's interaction with universe polymorphism requires care. The `--cubical` mode remains officially experimental; elaborator and termination checker occasionally disagree with geometric intuition.

**Foundational trade-offs.** Univalence is incompatible with classical principles such as the law of excluded middle in its naive form, and with uniqueness of identity proofs — the theory is *intrinsically* homotopical, which is a feature for homotopy theory and a cost for classical formalization. Moreover, the cubical picture, for all its computational virtues, is not the only story: the simplicial model retains conceptual primacy for many homotopy theorists, and the translation between cubical and simplicial synthetic arguments is still manual.

**Methodological immaturity.** Synthetic homotopy theory remains a young discipline: its library coverage is thin compared to classical algebraic topology, its proof automation is primitive, and its pedagogical materials assume fluency in both homotopy theory and dependent types — a rare combination. The Brunerie number computation is a proof of concept, not yet a technique.

---

##7 Conclusion

A decade ago, univalent foundations faced a dilemma: its central axiom could be *believed* but not *run*. Cubical type theory resolved it by refusing to treat univalence as primitive at all. By internalizing the interval, making paths into functions, and equipping the theory with Kan composition and Glue, Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg [2] turned univalence into a theorem with reduction rules; Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg's decomposition [4] made higher inductive types compute; and Vezzosi, Mörtberg, and Abel [3] put the apparatus into programmers' hands as Cubical Agda.

The payoff is empirical: the synthetic style of [1] now *executes* — the torus is a product of circles by `refl`, function extensionality is a definition, and the Brunerie number normalizes to −2 [6]. The machine does not merely check the mathematics; it *performs* it.

The road ahead is clear and demanding: faster cubical evaluators to conquer the infeasible Brunerie-like numbers of [7], a broader HIT schema, and the steady accumulation of synthetic libraries until the cubical laboratory covers what the classical textbooks do. The axiom has become a program. Now the program must scale.
