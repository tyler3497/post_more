# From the Frame Rule to Iris: Separation Logic, Concurrent Reasoning, and Mechanized Verification in Coq

## Abstract

Separation logic made *local reasoning* about heap-manipulating programs sound and scalable. This thesis traces the arc from O'Hearn's **frame rule** — repairing the aliasing-unsound rule of constancy with the *separating conjunction* of bunched implications [1][2] — through Reynolds' definitive LICS formulation [3], to concurrent separation logic with disjoint parallelism and resource invariants [4], and the algebraic "abstract separation logic" isolating the frame property as the semantic core of soundness [5]. It culminates in the **Iris** framework, which consolidated a decade of baroque concurrency logics into two orthogonal mechanisms — partial commutative monoids and invariants [6] — added *higher-order ghost state* via step-indexed resource algebras [7], reduced the program logic to a small base logic around the later modality [8], and mechanized everything in Coq [9]. We reconstruct the rules, models, and artifacts, and assess what mechanized separation logic can — and cannot yet — achieve.

## 1 Introduction

In 1969, Tony Hoare gave program verification its canonical form: the **Hoare triple** `{P} C {Q}`, asserting that command `C`, started in a state satisfying `P`, ends in a state satisfying `Q` [1]. Among the structural rules of Hoare logic, one was understood early to be vital for scalability: the *rule of constancy*,

```
{P} C {Q}
──────────────────   where no variable free in R is modified by C
{P ∧ R} C {Q ∧ R}
```

which permits a *local* specification of `C` — mentioning only the state `C` actually touches — to be extended with arbitrary facts about state that `C` leaves alone. Without such a rule, every specification would have to describe the entire global state, and modular verification would collapse.

Pointers broke this picture. In a heap with unrestricted aliasing, the constancy instance

```
{x ↦ −} [x] := 4 {x ↦ 4}
────────────────────────────────────────
{x ↦ − ∧ y ↦ 3} [x] := 4 {x ↦ 4 ∧ y ↦ 3}
```

is *invalid*: the precondition does not preclude `x = y`, in which case the mutation falsifies `y ↦ 3`. O'Hearn's decisive repair kept the *shape* of constancy but replaced ordinary conjunction with the **separating conjunction** `∗`, giving the **frame rule** [1][3],

```
{P} C {Q}
──────────────────   where no variable free in R is modified by C
{P ∗ R} C {Q ∗ R}
```

is the key to *local reasoning*: to understand a program, reasoning need only concern the cells it actually accesses — its *footprint* — because every other cell is automatically preserved.

The foundations were laid in 2001: Ishtiaq and O'Hearn proposed **bunched implications (BI)** as an assertion language for mutable data structures [2], and O'Hearn, Reynolds, and Yang presented local reasoning with small axioms and the frame rule at CSL 2001 [1]. Reynolds' LICS 2002 paper then gave separation logic its canonical form — heap model, points-to predicate, small axioms, and soundness proofs [3].

Concurrency came next: O'Hearn's 2007 paper added the **disjoint parallelism** rule and **resource invariants** for conditional critical regions [4] — work recognized with the 2016 Gödel Prize (shared with Stephen Brookes, who supplied the trace-based soundness argument). Calcagno, O'Hearn, and Yang then gave the semantic account: *local action* and **abstract separation logic**, showing the frame rule is sound exactly when primitive commands satisfy a frame property over a *separation algebra* [5].

The modern era belongs to **Iris**. After the 2007–2014 proliferation of ever more sophisticated concurrent separation logics — RGSep, deny-guarantee, CAP, FCSL, TaDA — Jung et al. proposed a radical consolidation: *monoids and invariants are all you need* [6]. Higher-order ghost state [7], reduction to a minimal base logic [8], and complete Coq mechanization [9] made Iris the dominant framework for mechanized concurrent verification, recognized by the 2023 Alonzo Church Award.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Hoare logic and the failure of constancy

Classical Hoare logic reasons about *stores* — finite maps from variables to values — where the rule of constancy is sound because variable disjointness is syntactic and stable. Heaps destroy this stability: `y ↦ 3` can be falsified by `[x] := 4` even when `x` and `y` are syntactically distinct, because *aliasing* (`x = y`) is semantic, not syntactic. Separating conjunction repairs this: `P ∗ R` asserts the heap *splits* into disjoint parts satisfying `P` and `R`, so a command whose footprint lies within the `P` part cannot disturb `R` [1][3].

### 2.2 The logic of bunched implications

The assertion language is a fragment of O'Hearn and Pym's **logic of bunched implications (BI)**: intuitionistic logic plus a multiplicative conjunction `∗` and its adjoint, the *magic wand* `—∗`, with a resource semantics over tree-structured *bunches*. Ishtiaq and O'Hearn showed BI's resource reading is exactly what heap assertions need — `∗` for *disjoint* composition, `—∗` for hypothetical extension [2]. The famous slogan — *"reasoning and specification confined to the cells the program actually accesses"* [1] — is BI's resource discipline applied to verification.

### 2.3 Reynolds' heap model and the small axioms

Reynolds fixed the now-standard model [3]: heaps are *finite partial functions* from locations to values, `h₁ ⊥ h₂` denotes domain-disjointness, and `h₁ • h₂` their union. Assertions include `emp` (the empty heap), `e ↦ e′` (a singleton heap), `∗`, and `—∗`. Each heap command receives a **small axiom** mentioning only its footprint:

- **Mutation:** `{e ↦ −} [e] := e′ {e ↦ e′}`
- **Lookup:** `{e ↦ v ∧ x = v′} x := [e] {e ↦ v ∧ x = v}` (with freshness side conditions)
- **Allocation:** `{emp} x := cons(e₁,…,eₙ) {x ↦ e₁,…,eₙ}`
- **Disposal:** `{e ↦ −} dispose(e) {emp}`

### 2.4 Footprints, locality, and tightness

A command's **footprint** is the set of cells it may access. A specification `{P} C {Q}` is *tight* when `P` describes exactly the footprint — and the frame rule is sound only for tight premises: `{true} [x] := 1 {true}` must be *invalid*, since framing it with `x ↦ 2` would produce nonsense [1]. Soundness rests on a *locality* property of the semantics — cells outside the footprint are untouched — proved in [1][3] and later abstracted in [5].

## 3 Methodology

We reconstruct nine primary sources [1–9], restating each logic's proof rules and side conditions and tracing how each semantic model justifies — or repairs — its predecessor's. The exposition follows the field's own methodological evolution, which exhibits four recognizable eras:

| Era | Representative work | Method |
|-----|--------------------|--------|
| **Syntactic** (2001–2006) | O'Hearn–Reynolds–Yang [1]; Reynolds [3] | Inference rules + paper soundness proofs over a concrete heap model |
| **Semantic** (2007–2010) | O'Hearn [4]; Calcagno–O'Hearn–Yang [5] | Separation algebras; *local action*; soundness from the frame property |
| **Consolidating** (2015–2016) | Iris 1.0 [6]; higher-order ghost state [7] | PCMs + invariants as orthogonal basis; CMRAs |
| **Mechanized** (2017–) | Base logic [8]; Iris from the ground up [9] | Minimal base logic; everything derived and checked in Coq |

We present Iris as the *limit* of this sequence, so each mechanism — ghost state, invariants, the later modality — appears as the answer to a concrete problem raised by earlier logics.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The frame rule as the engine of local reasoning

The frame rule's power lies in its *direction*: it lifts a proof about a small footprint into a proof about an arbitrarily large heap, with no re-verification. From `{x ↦ −} dispose(x) {emp}`, framing with `R ≜ list(y)` yields `{x ↦ − ∗ list(y)} dispose(x) {list(y)}` — the *global* behavior, for free. Procedures compose the same way: code verified against its footprint can run in any larger heap satisfying the frame.

> **Theorem (Soundness of the frame rule; Reynolds [3], Calcagno–O'Hearn–Yang [5]).** If `{P} C {Q}` is valid and tight, and no variable free in `R` is modified by `C`, then `{P ∗ R} C {Q ∗ R}` is valid.

The side condition on *variables* is the remnant of classical constancy; the heap side is handled by `∗`-disjointness. Validity must mean *fault-avoidance* — the precondition must guarantee `C` never dereferences an unowned cell — or framing could conjure ownership of cells the command might fault on [1][5].

In Iris Proof Mode, the frame rule is a *derived* lemma about weakest preconditions, not a primitive rule:

```coq
Lemma wp_frame (P Q R : iProp) (e : expr) :
  (P -∗ WP e {{ _, Q }}) ⊢ (P ∗ R -∗ WP e {{ _, Q ∗ R }}).
Proof.
  iIntros "Hwp [HP HR]". iApply ("Hwp" with "HP").
  iIntros (v) "HQ". iFrame. (* returns Q ∗ R *)
Qed.
```

The `iFrame` tactic is the mechanized shadow of the 2001 rule: it cancels the spatial resources the continuation does not need.

### 4.2 Concurrent separation logic: disjoint parallelism and resource invariants

O'Hearn's 2007 extension [4] rests on the **ownership hypothesis**: *a thread may only access state it owns*. Two threads owning disjoint heap portions cannot interfere, which the **disjoint parallelism** rule makes precise:

```
{P₁} C₁ {Q₁}    {P₂} C₂ {Q₂}
────────────────────────────────
{P₁ ∗ P₂} C₁ ‖ C₂ {Q₁ ∗ Q₂}
```

This is the frame rule's concurrent twin: each thread's proof is a *frame* for the other — and a celebrated corollary is **data-race freedom**, since racing threads would have to own the same cell, contradicting `∗`-separation.

Real programs share state, so CSL adds **resource invariants**: `resource r in C` introduces an invariant `R` over shared cells, entered atomically via `with r when B do C`. The invariant is *consumed* on entry and *re-established* on exit, enabling **ownership transfer** — e.g., a shared counter:

```
invariant I ≜ ∃n. c ↦ n ∗ token(n)
{P ∗ I} with r do { x := [c]; [c] := x+1 } {P ∗ I}
```

Two such critical regions in parallel verify against `P₁ ∗ P₂ ∗ I`, each thread temporarily owning the counter while inside.

Soundness demands **precise** invariants — identifying a *unique* subheap — so the conjunction rule cannot duplicate shared ownership. Brookes' soundness proof [4] uses an interleaving *trace semantics* in which actions on disjoint state commute, formalizing that disjoint threads "do not see" each other — the achievement recognized by the 2016 Gödel Prize.

### 4.3 Abstract separation logic: the semantic core

Calcagno, O'Hearn, and Yang asked what the frame rule *really* needs from a model [5]. **Abstract separation logic** axiomatizes:

1. A **separation algebra**: a partial commutative monoid `(Σ, •, u)` with cancellativity and coherence laws. Heaps are one instance; permissions, histories, and ghost state are others.
2. **Local action**: each primitive command satisfies the **frame property** — stepping from `σ₁` to `σ₁′` lifts to `σ₁ • σ₂ → σ₁′ • σ₂` for compatible `σ₂` — plus *safety monotonicity* and a garbage-collection axiom.
3. A **tight interpretation** of triples, so that specifications describe exactly the footprint.

The payoff: *the frame rule holds for any command whose primitive actions are local*. This algebraic turn — states as PCMs — is the direct ancestor of Iris's ghost-state monoids:

```haskell
class PCM s where
  unit  :: s
  (|*|) :: s -> s -> Maybe s   -- partial composition

frameProperty :: PCM s => (s -> Maybe s) -> s -> s -> Bool
frameProperty step s1 s2 = case (s1 |*| s2, step s1) of
  (Just s, Just s1') -> step s == (s1' |*| s2)
  _                  -> True
```

Abstract separation logic also clarified *why* BI is the right assertion language: Pym's resource semantics [2] shows `∗` and `—∗` are the logical connectives of PCM-structured state, not ad hoc heap syntax.

### 4.4 Iris: monoids, invariants, and higher-order ghost state

By 2014, CSL had fragmented into logics — RGSep, deny-guarantee, CAP, FCSL, TaDA — each adding bespoke *primitive* mechanisms with its own formidable soundness proof. Iris 1.0 (POPL 2015) proposed that two orthogonal, already-understood ingredients suffice [6]:

- **Partial commutative monoids** for *ghost state*: user-defined fictional resources built from PCM combinators — fractions, products, finite maps, agreement.
- **Invariants** for *sharing*: duplicable assertions `inv N P` tying ghost state to physical state, with namespaces controlling reentrancy.

Complex rules from earlier logics were *derived* inside Iris rather than axiomatized — *"monoids and invariants are all you need."*

But the slogan was, by the authors' own admission, misleading: monoids were *not* enough [9]. Useful ghost state is often **higher-order** — its definition mentions the type `iProp` of propositions itself (e.g., "named propositions" storing arbitrary assertions). Defining such a PCM requires solving a recursive domain equation over the proposition lattice, which naive PCMs cannot do.

The ICFP 2016 solution was **CMRAs** — *step-indexed* PCMs ("cameras") [7]. Indexing the monoid structure over approximation steps yields a category in which the recursive equation over `iProp` has a solution. Its toolkit — the *agreement*, *authoritative*, and *exclusive* RAs — became the standard vocabulary: `● a ∗ ◯ b` expresses "the authoritative value is `a`, and this fragment witnesses `b ≼ a`," the pattern underlying virtually every Iris protocol proof. All of it was formalized in Coq.

### 4.5 The base logic, the later modality, and full mechanization

The ESOP 2017 paper took Iris's reductionism to its logical conclusion [8]: keep only a small **base logic** — vanilla separation logic plus three modalities — with weakest preconditions, Hoare triples, and view shifts as *derived* forms:

- `□ P` — *persistence*: `P` holds without owning resources;
- `▷ P` — the *later* modality: `P` holds after one approximation step;
- fancy updates `|={E₁,E₂}=> P` — ghost updates that may open and close invariants across masks.

Weakest preconditions `WP e {{ Φ }}` are then *defined* in the base logic, and the familiar program rules become lemmas. Strikingly, the later modality is *essential*: removing `▷` yields an **inconsistency**, so step-indexing is a logical necessity for impredicative invariants, not an implementation artifact [8].

"Iris from the ground up" (JFP 2018) consolidated the complete design — Iris 3.1 — in one narrative [9], crowned by the adequacy theorem:

> **Theorem (Adequacy of weakest preconditions [9]).** If `True ⊢ WP e {{ Φ }}` is derivable for closed `e`, then `e` is *safe*: it never gets stuck.

Adequacy connects proof to reality — a closed Iris proof *means* the program cannot fault. The *entire* development is mechanized in Coq, with the **Iris Proof Mode** tactics (`iIntros`, `iApply`, `iFrame`, `iMod`, `iInv`) making interactive proof practical. Case studies followed: RustBelt's soundness proof for Rust's type system, GPS-style weak-memory reasoning, and dozens of verified concurrent data structures.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

This thesis's "empirical" content is *mechanized artifacts and their theorems* — the strongest evidence a logic can offer. The table summarizes what each milestone proved and in what medium.

| Milestone | Year | Soundness vehicle | Mechanized? | Signature result |
|-----------|------|-------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Local reasoning (CSL '01) [1] | 2001 | Operational semantics, paper proof | No | Frame rule for small axioms |
| BI as assertion language [2] | 2001 | Resource semantics | No | `∗`/`—∗` as heap connectives |
| Separation logic (LICS) [3] | 2002 | Denotational + operational | No | Full logic, list examples |
| Concurrent SL [4] | 2007 | Brookes trace semantics | No | Disjoint parallelism; race freedom |
| Abstract separation logic [5] | 2007 | Separation algebras | No | Frame property ⇒ frame rule |
| Iris 1.0 (POPL) [6] | 2015 | Step-indexed model | **Coq** | Monoids + invariants basis |
| Higher-order ghost state [7] | 2016 | CMRA construction | **Coq** | Recursive ghost state sound |
| Base logic (ESOP) [8] | 2017 | Base-logic derivation | **Coq** | WP derived; `▷` necessary |
| Iris from the ground up [9] | 2018 | Full Coq development | **Coq** | Adequacy: `True ⊢ WP e {{Φ}}` ⇒ safe |

Three observations stand out. **First**, the *unit of trust* shrank: from hundred-page paper soundness proofs to a small base logic checked by Coq, with everything else a machine-verified *derived lemma* [8][9]. **Second**, adequacy [9] turns Iris into a *verified toolchain*: a closed proof is a machine-checked certificate of memory safety. **Third**, Iris 3.x proved its generality in use — RustBelt's Rust soundness proof, GPS-family weak-memory logics, and a large corpus of verified concurrent data structures [9] — the body of work honored by the 2023 Alonzo Church Award.

The Iris Coq development spans tens of thousands of lines, yet its *proof mode* keeps interactive proofs at the level of the logic: opening an invariant (`iInv`), framing hypotheses (`iFrame`), and ghost updates (`iMod`) are single tactic invocations — itself an empirical result about the maturity of mechanized separation logic.

## 6 Limitations

The arc from [1] to [9] is a story of successive generalization, but each step paid a price.

1. **Tightness and allocation.** The frame rule demands tight specifications, fragile around allocation: `{emp} x := alloc() {x = 1}` over-specifies the allocator, and framing it is unsound unless allocation is non-local or triples quantify over all allocators [1][5].
2. **Precision and expressiveness.** Original CSL requires *precise* invariants for the conjunction rule, ruling out some natural sharing patterns [4][6]. CSL guarantees race freedom and memory safety, but *nothing* about liveness, deadlock-freedom, or termination.
3. **Weak memory.** Vanilla CSL assumes sequential consistency; relaxed architectures (C11, ARM/POWER) needed separate logics (RSL, GPS) whose integration with Iris ghost state remains research-grade [9].
4. **Step-indexing overhead.** Iris's `▷` infects specifications: every invariant access demands later-stripping under *timelessness* side conditions. The ESOP result that `▷` is *necessary* [8] means this cost can only be managed, never designed away.
5. **Automation gap.** Tools like Infer deploy separation-logic *bi-abduction* at industrial scale, but sacrifice Iris's foundational guarantees; Iris proofs remain labor-intensive and expert-driven. Closing that gap is the field's central open engineering problem.
6. **Trust base.** Adequacy [9] reduces trust to the Coq kernel and the object language's semantics — a dramatic improvement over paper proofs, but not zero: a bug in the semantics silently voids every proof built atop it.

## 7 Conclusion

A quarter-century after O'Hearn, Reynolds, and Yang, the frame rule remains the most consequential idea in heap verification: a one-line rule turning pointer reasoning from a global aliasing nightmare into a local, compositional discipline [1][3]. Its concurrent offspring gave thread-modular proofs and race freedom by construction [4]; its algebraic abstraction revealed the frame property as the essence of soundness [5]; and Iris showed the baroque machinery of modern concurrency logics collapses into monoids, invariants, ghost state, and a later modality — all checked by Coq [6][7][8][9].

The methodological moral is *derive, don't axiomatize*: each generation moved complexity out of the trusted core into libraries of derived, machine-checked rules, until the core became a small base logic. That is why Iris — not its more specialized predecessors — became the platform for verified Rust, weak-memory reasoning, and the new incorrectness and outcome logics. The frame rule began as a repair to Hoare logic; it ended as the foundation of a mechanized science of concurrent programs.
