# Yoneda, Adjunctions, and Computational Effects: A Categorical Reconstruction of Monads, Applicatives, and Arrows in Functional Programming

## Abstract
This thesis investigates yoneda, adjunctions, and computational effects: a categorical reconstruction of monads, applicatives, and arrows in functional programming through a mathematically rigorous lens, synthesizing contemporary advances in category theory with foundational theory. We develop formal models, prove convergence and complexity bounds, and evaluate empirical implications across benchmarks. Our analysis combines algebraic, probabilistic, and systems perspectives, establishing novel connections between theory and practice. Through extensive formalism, we demonstrate that careful design of underlying structures yields asymptotic improvements while preserving correctness guarantees. The work contributes definitional clarity, new theorems, and open problems that invite further investigation by the research community.
