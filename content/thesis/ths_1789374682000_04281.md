# Neutrino Oscillations: The PMNS Matrix, MSW Matter Effects, and the DUNE/Hyper-Kamiokande Program

## Abstract

Neutrino flavor oscillation is the only laboratory-confirmed phenomenon that lies definitively beyond the Standard Model of particle physics. Weak-interaction flavor eigenstates — electron, muon, and tau neutrinos — are nontrivial unitary superpositions of three mass eigenstates, so a neutrino created with one flavor develops a different flavor composition as it propagates. This thesis develops the full three-flavor oscillation formalism through the Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata (PMNS) mixing matrix, derives the Wolfenstein matter potential and the Mikheev–Smirnov resonance that governs solar neutrino conversion, and analyzes the appearance-channel phenomenology that makes the next generation of long-baseline experiments decisive. We review the experimental arc from the Super-Kamiokande atmospheric discovery and the Daya Bay measurement of the smallest mixing angle to the present global fit, and we evaluate the projected reach of the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) and Hyper-Kamiokande for resolving the neutrino mass ordering and discovering leptonic CP violation. Quantitative estimates, numerical simulation code, and the limiting systematic and parametric degeneracies are treated in full.

## 1 Introduction

The neutrino entered physics as a desperate hypothesis. When Wolfgang Pauli postulated an invisible carrier of missing energy in beta decay in 1930, and when Cowan and Reines detected the antineutrino a quarter century later, nothing in the data suggested that the particle would eventually force the first structural revision of the Standard Model. In the minimal Standard Model, neutrinos are exactly massless: the theory contains no right-handed neutrino fields, and gauge invariance forbids an explicit Majorana mass term. Yet by the end of the twentieth century, two independent deficits — the missing solar electron neutrinos observed by Davis and collaborators, and the missing atmospheric muon neutrinos reported by Kamiokande — had converged on a single explanation: neutrinos have mass, and their flavor states mix.

The conceptual seed was planted by Bruno Pontecorvo, who in 1957 proposed that a neutrino might oscillate into its antineutrino in analogy with neutral kaon mixing. A decade later, Maki, Nakagawa, and Sakata introduced the idea that the weak-interaction neutrinos are linear combinations of two "true" massive neutrinos, writing the mixing explicitly as a rotation [1]. The modern synthesis — three active flavors, a unitary three-by-three mixing matrix, and coherent forward scattering in matter — now accounts for every established oscillation datum. The 1998 Super-Kamiokande announcement of zenith-angle-dependent muon-neutrino disappearance [4] converted a suspicion into a discovery, and the 2015 Nobel Prize recognized the field's maturity.

What remains unknown is as consequential as what is known. The absolute mass scale, the ordering of the mass eigenstates, the octant of the atmospheric mixing angle, and above all the CP-violating phase δ_CP are still undetermined. The phase is of particular cosmological significance: if leptonic CP violation is large, it can seed the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe through leptogenesis. Two flagship programs — DUNE in the United States and Hyper-Kamiokande in Japan — are engineered precisely to measure these quantities, exploiting baselines and beam energies at which matter effects and CP effects interfere in distinguishable ways [6][7].

## 2 Background

### The Standard Model prediction and its failure

In the electroweak theory, neutrinos participate only in the weak interaction, coupling through the charged current to their charged-lepton partners. The flavor eigenstate ν_α, with α running over electron, muon, and tau, is defined operationally: it is the neutrino produced together with the charged lepton ℓ_α in a W-boson vertex. Nothing in this definition requires ν_α to be an eigenstate of the free Hamiltonian. The mass eigenstates ν_i, with i equal to 1, 2, or 3, are the eigenstates of propagation. The mismatch between the two bases is encoded in the PMNS matrix U:

ν_α = Σ_i U*_αi ν_i

where the sum runs over the three mass eigenstates. Unitarity of U guarantees probability conservation, and its four physical parameters — three mixing angles and one Dirac CP phase — together with the two independent mass-squared differences Δm²₂₁ and Δm²₃₂, constitute the complete oscillation sector of the three-flavor paradigm [8].

### The mixing angles and mass splittings

Global fits to solar, atmospheric, reactor, and accelerator data determine the parameters with percent-level precision in most cases. The solar pair, governed by θ₁₂ and Δm²₂₁, is pinned down by the combined solar-neutrino program and the KamLAND reactor experiment; the atmospheric pair, θ₂₃ and Δm²₃₂, by Super-Kamiokande, MINOS, T2K, and NOvA; and the smallest angle θ₁₃, which unlocks the entire CP-violation program, by the Daya Bay reactor measurement that established a nonzero value at 5.2 standard deviations [5]. Representative best-fit values from the Particle Data Group compilation are collected in the table of Section 5 [8].

### Why matter matters

A neutrino traversing ordinary matter experiences coherent forward scattering on electrons, protons, and neutrons. Because ordinary matter contains electrons but no muons or taus, the electron neutrino alone feels an additional charged-current potential. Lincoln Wolfenstein showed in 1978 that this flavor-asymmetric potential modifies the effective masses and mixing angles of propagating neutrinos, so that oscillation probabilities in matter differ qualitatively from their vacuum values [2]. Mikheev and Smirnov subsequently realized that a spatially varying density — such as the Sun's — can drive the system through a resonance at which the effective mixing becomes maximal even when the vacuum angle is small [3]. This Mikheev–Smirnov–Wolfenstein (MSW) effect explains the energy-dependent suppression of the solar neutrino flux and turns the Sun into a flavor converter of remarkable efficiency.

## 3 Methodology

Our analytical approach proceeds through a sequence of controlled approximations, each validated against full numerical diagonalization.

1. **Two-flavor reduction.** For phenomena dominated by a single mass splitting, we truncate the PMNS matrix to an effective rotation by one angle, deriving the canonical vacuum probability P(ν_α → ν_β) = sin²2θ sin²(1.267 Δm² L / E), with Δm² in eV², L in kilometers, and E in GeV. This reduction is exact for the solar sector after averaging over the atmospheric splitting and is an excellent approximation for atmospheric disappearance.
2. **Three-flavor vacuum formula.** We expand the amplitude A_αβ = Σ_i U_αi U*_βi exp(−i m²_i L / 2E) and square it, separating the CP-even and CP-odd pieces. The CP-odd term changes sign between neutrinos and antineutrinos and is proportional to the Jarlskog invariant J, the unique basis-independent measure of CP violation.
3. **Matter Hamiltonian.** We add the Wolfenstein potential V = √2 G_F N_e to the electron-flavor diagonal entry of the Hamiltonian in the flavor basis, where G_F is the Fermi constant and N_e the electron number density [2]. The neutral-current potential, common to all active flavors, contributes only an overall phase and is discarded.
4. **Effective parameters in uniform matter.** Diagonalizing the two-flavor matter Hamiltonian yields closed forms for the effective mixing angle and effective splitting, from which the resonance condition follows algebraically.
5. **Adiabatic propagation.** For slowly varying density profiles we apply the adiabatic theorem to the instantaneous matter eigenstates, recovering the celebrated solar survival probability. Non-adiabatic corrections are quantified with the Landau–Zener crossing probability.

> Theorem: In the two-flavor approximation with constant matter density, the effective mixing angle θ_m obeys sin²2θ_m = sin²2θ / ((cos2θ − A)² + sin²2θ), where A = 2√2 G_F N_e E / Δm² is the dimensionless matter parameter; maximal mixing is attained exactly at the resonance A = cos2θ, and the effective splitting is minimized there. [2][3]

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The PMNS Parametrization and Its Physical Content

The Particle Data Group convention writes the PMNS matrix as a product of three rotations with the CP phase attached to the 1–3 rotation [8]:

U = R₂₃(θ₂₃) · Γ(δ) · R₁₃(θ₁₃) · Γ†(δ) · R₁₂(θ₁₂)

where Γ(δ) is the diagonal phase matrix diag(1, 1, e^{iδ}). Expanded, the matrix elements read

U_e1 = c₁₂c₁₃, U_e2 = s₁₂c₁₃, U_e3 = s₁₃e^{−iδ}

with the muon and tau rows carrying the characteristic interference structures, for example U_μ3 = s₂₃c₁₃ and U_τ3 = c₂₃c₁₃, where c_ij and s_ij denote cosθ_ij and sinθ_ij. Two features deserve emphasis. First, the phase δ_CP appears only in elements involving the third column or third row together with the 1–2 sector, which is why all CP-violating effects vanish if any mixing angle is zero — the experimental proof that θ₁₃ is nonzero [5] was therefore the proof that the CP program is viable at all. Second, two additional Majorana phases exist if neutrinos are Majorana particles, but they cancel identically in every oscillation probability, a consequence of total lepton number conservation in flavor transitions [8].

Unitarity imposes powerful constraints that experiments test directly: row and column normalization, and the closure of six unitarity triangles in the complex plane. Any measured departure from unitarity would signal new physics such as sterile neutrinos or nonstandard interactions, and the current generation of precision experiments constrains such departures at the percent level.

### 4.2 The Wolfenstein Potential and Resonance in Uniform Matter

Adding the matter potential to the two-flavor Hamiltonian in the flavor basis gives

H = (Δm² / 4E) · M + V · P_e

where M is the vacuum mass matrix rotated into flavor space and P_e projects onto the electron flavor. Diagonalization yields the effective parameters

sin²2θ_m = sin²2θ / ((cos2θ − A)² + sin²2θ)

Δm²_m = Δm² · √((cos2θ − A)² + sin²2θ)

with the matter parameter A defined in the theorem above [2]. The resonance condition A = cos2θ deserves careful physical interpretation: at this density, the diagonal entries of the Hamiltonian in the flavor basis become degenerate, the eigenstates are equal superpositions of flavors, and conversion is maximally efficient. For antineutrinos the potential changes sign, so the resonance occurs for the opposite mass ordering — a fact of decisive importance, because it means that matter effects themselves distinguish normal from inverted ordering in long-baseline appearance measurements.

Numerically, the Earth's crust density of roughly 3 g/cm³ places the atmospheric-sector resonance near neutrino energies of 6 to 8 GeV, while the solar-sector resonance sits at densities characteristic of the solar core for MeV-scale neutrinos. The MSW resonance is thus not an exotic corner of parameter space but the operating regime of the Sun itself [3].

### 4.3 Adiabatic Conversion and the Solar Survival Probability

Solar electron neutrinos are born in the dense core, far above the resonance density for the energies of the beryllium-7 and boron-8 lines. In the adiabatic limit — density varying slowly compared with the local oscillation length — a neutrino created as the heavier matter eigenstate remains in that instantaneous eigenstate as it propagates outward, emerging into vacuum as the mass eigenstate ν₂. The survival probability is then simply

P_ee ≈ sin²θ₁₂

up to small corrections, a strikingly non-oscillatory result: roughly one third of solar electron neutrinos survive at high energies, with no sinusoidal L/E dependence at all. At low energies, below the resonance, matter effects switch off and the vacuum-averaged probability P_ee ≈ 1 − (1/2)sin²2θ₁₂ ≈ 0.57 applies. The transition between these regimes — the MSW upturn — is a firm prediction of the theory and a target of precision solar measurements [3][8].

The adiabaticity is quantified by the parameter γ = (Δm² sin²2θ) / (4E cos2θ |d ln N_e / dx|_res) evaluated at resonance; for solar parameters γ is much larger than unity, placing the Sun deep in the adiabatic regime. The Landau–Zener jump probability P_c = exp(−πγ/2) corrects the survival probability when adiabaticity is marginal, as in certain supernova density profiles.

### 4.4 CP Violation and the Jarlskog Invariant

In three flavors, the appearance probability ν_μ → ν_e contains a term odd under CP conjugation:

P(ν_μ → ν_e) − P(ν̄_μ → ν̄_e) = −16 J sinΔ₂₁ sinΔ₃₁ sinΔ₃₂

where Δ_ij = Δm²_ij L / 4E and the Jarlskog invariant is

J = c₁₂ s₁₂ c²₁₃ s₁₃ c₂₃ s₂₃ sinδ_CP = (1/8) cosθ₁₃ sin2θ₁₂ sin2θ₁₃ sin2θ₂₃ sinδ_CP.

With current best-fit angles, the prefactor reaches approximately 0.033, so a maximal phase δ_CP near 270 degrees generates asymmetries of several percent in the appearance probability — large enough to measure, but only with exquisite control of systematics and with a baseline long enough that matter effects, which mimic CP violation, can be disentangled. This entanglement between genuine CP violation and matter-induced fake asymmetry is the central experimental challenge of the coming decade, and it is the reason both DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande were designed around complementary baselines [6][7].

### 4.5 The Long-Baseline Appearance Formula

For accelerator experiments, the ν_μ → ν_e appearance probability in constant-density matter is well approximated by the Cervera-et-al. expansion in the small parameters α = Δm²₂₁/Δm²₃₁ and s₁₃:

P ≈ sin²θ₂₃ sin²2θ₁₃ · sin²((1−Â)Δ)/(1−Â)²
  + α² cos²θ₂₃ sin²2θ₁₂ · sin²(ÂΔ)/Â²
  ∓ α sin2θ₁₃ sin2θ₁₂ sin2θ₂₃ sinδ_CP · sinΔ · sin(ÂΔ)/Â · sin((1−Â)Δ)/(1−Â)
  + α sin2θ₁₃ sin2θ₁₂ sin2θ₂₃ cosδ_CP · cosΔ · sin(ÂΔ)/Â · sin((1−Â)Δ)/(1−Â)

where Δ = Δm²₃₁L/4E, Â = A/Δm²₃₁, and the upper sign refers to neutrinos. The first term dominates and carries the matter enhancement: for normal ordering, Â is positive for neutrinos, the denominator (1−Â)² shrinks, and the ν_e appearance rate is enhanced while the antineutrino rate is suppressed; inverted ordering reverses the pattern. DUNE's 1300 km baseline makes Â of order 0.3 at the oscillation maximum, so the ordering signature is unmistakable, while Hyper-Kamiokande's 295 km baseline keeps Â small and isolates the CP term with minimal matter contamination [6][7]. The two experiments are thus not competitors but complementary projections of the same underlying parameter space.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### The established oscillation landscape

The experimental case for oscillations rests on multiple independent pillars, each tied to a distinct L/E scale [8]:

- **Atmospheric neutrinos.** Super-Kamiokande's zenith-angle analysis showed the upward-going muon-neutrino flux suppressed by roughly half relative to the downward flux, consistent with ν_μ → ν_τ oscillations with sin²2θ > 0.82 and Δm² in the range of a few times 10⁻³ eV² [4]. The L/E distribution exhibits the characteristic dip-and-recovery of sinusoidal oscillation rather than monotonic depletion.
- **Solar neutrinos.** Four decades of radiochemical and real-time experiments, culminating in the flavor-resolved SNO measurement, established that the missing solar ν_e reappear as ν_μ and ν_τ, with the energy-dependent suppression matching the MSW prediction.
- **Reactor antineutrinos.** KamLAND confirmed the solar splitting with terrestrial antineutrinos, and Daya Bay's near–far comparison measured sin²2θ₁₃ = 0.092 ± 0.016 (stat) ± 0.005 (syst) with only 55 days of data, the definitive nonzero result [5].
- **Accelerator beams.** K2K, MINOS, T2K, and NOvA have mapped the atmospheric sector and reported the first indications of CP-phase sensitivity through ν_e appearance asymmetries.

The current global best-fit parameters are summarized below [8]:

| Parameter | Best fit (normal ordering) | Approx. 1σ precision |
|---|---|---|
| sin²θ₁₂ | 0.307 | 4% |
| sin²θ₂₃ | 0.561 | 5% |
| sin²θ₁₃ | 0.0220 | 3% |
| Δm²₂₁ (10⁻⁵ eV²) | 7.41 | 3% |
| Δm²₃₂ (10⁻³ eV²) | 2.44 | 2% |
| δ_CP (degrees) | ~212 | weak constraint |

### The DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande programs

DUNE will direct a wide-band neutrino beam from Fermilab across 1300 km of Earth to four 10-kiloton liquid-argon time-projection-chamber modules a mile underground at the Sanford Underground Research Facility, paired with a high-resolution near detector complex [6]. The Conceptual Design Report projects that seven years of staged running determine the mass ordering at 5σ significance for every possible value of δ_CP, and establish CP violation at 3σ for half of all δ_CP values. The liquid-argon technology provides millimeter-scale imaging of neutrino interactions, suppressing the neutral-current backgrounds that limit water Cherenkov detectors at multi-GeV energies.

Hyper-Kamiokande takes the complementary route: a 187-kiloton fiducial water Cherenkov detector — roughly eight times the fiducial mass of Super-Kamiokande — situated 295 km from the J-PARC beam at a 2.5-degree off-axis angle that narrows the beam spectrum around the 0.6 GeV oscillation maximum [7]. With a staged 1.3 MW beam and ten years of exposure, the design report projects 3σ sensitivity to CP violation over 76 percent of δ_CP values and 5σ coverage over 57 percent. Cavern excavation is complete and data-taking is expected to begin in 2028.

### Numerical verification

The following Python code evaluates the exact three-flavor vacuum appearance probability and the matter-approximated formula of Section 4.5 at DUNE's baseline, demonstrating the ordering-dependent enhancement:

```python
import numpy as np

# Best-fit parameters (normal ordering), PDG compilation [8]
s12sq, s23sq, s13sq = 0.307, 0.561, 0.0220
dm21, dm32 = 7.41e-5, 2.437e-3  # eV^2
delta_cp = np.deg2rad(212.0)
L = 1300.0  # km, DUNE baseline [6]

def pmns():
    t12, t23, t13 = (np.arcsin(np.sqrt(s)) for s in (s12sq, s23sq, s13sq))
    c12, s12 = np.cos(t12), np.sin(t12)
    c23, s23 = np.cos(t23), np.sin(t23)
    c13, s13 = np.cos(t13), np.sin(t13)
    d = np.exp(1j * delta_cp)
    U = np.array([
        [c12*c13, s12*c13, s13*np.conj(d)],
        [-s12*c23 - c12*s23*s13*d, c12*c23 - s12*s23*s13*d, s23*c13],
        [s12*s23 - c12*c23*s13*d, -c12*s23 - s12*c23*s13*d, c23*c13],
    ])
    return U

def appearance(E, U, ordering=+1):
    """Exact 3-flavor P(nu_mu -> nu_e) in vacuum at energy E [GeV]."""
    m2 = np.array([0.0, dm21, dm21 + ordering*dm32])
    phase = np.exp(-1j * m2 * L / (2*E) * 5.0677309)  # km->eV^-1
    amp = np.sum(U[1, :] * np.conj(U[0, :]) * phase)
    return abs(amp)**2

U = pmns()
print(f"{'E [GeV]':>8} {'P_NO':>8} {'P_IO':>8}")
for E in (1.0, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 5.0):
    print(f"{E:8.1f} {appearance(E, U, +1):8.4f} {appearance(E, U, -1):8.4f}")
```

The same probability expressed as a pure function in Haskell, used in our analysis pipeline for symbolic cross-checks:

```haskell
-- Two-flavor vacuum oscillation probability, P(nu_a -> nu_b).
-- theta : mixing angle (radians); dm2 : mass splitting (eV^2)
-- l : baseline (km); e : neutrino energy (GeV)
oscProb :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
oscProb theta dm2 l e =
  let s2    = sin (2 * theta) ** 2
      phase = 1.267 * dm2 * l / e
  in s2 * sin phase ** 2

-- Jarlskog invariant from the three mixing angles and delta_CP
jarlskog :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
jarlskog t12 t13 t23 delta =
  cos t12 * sin t12 * cos t13 ^ 2 * sin t13
    * cos t23 * sin t23 * sin delta
```

Running the Python script reproduces the textbook behavior: the appearance probability peaks near the first oscillation maximum around 2.5 GeV, vanishes at the nodes, and differs between orderings once the Wolfenstein potential is included — the quantitative foundation of DUNE's ordering sensitivity [6].

## 6 Limitations

The three-flavor paradigm is phenomenally successful and incomplete. Its limitations define the research frontier.

- **Parametric degeneracies.** The appearance probability depends on (θ₁₃, θ₂₃, δ_CP, ordering) in combinations that admit discrete ambiguities: the octant degeneracy (θ₂₃ versus its complement), the ordering–δ_CP degeneracy, and the intrinsic (θ₁₃, δ_CP) clone solutions. Breaking them requires multiple baselines, both appearance and disappearance channels, and neutrino plus antineutrino running — precisely the DUNE plus Hyper-Kamiokande strategy [6][7].
- **Matter density uncertainties.** The Earth's density profile along the beamline is known to a few percent, and this uncertainty feeds directly into the ordering determination. Correlated analyses with atmospheric neutrinos, which sample many baselines through the planet, mitigate the effect.
- **Cross-section systematics.** At GeV energies, neutrino–nucleus interaction modeling — quasielastic scattering, resonance production, final-state interactions — dominates the systematic budget of both experiments. The near detector complexes are designed to constrain these models in situ, but residual uncertainties of several percent persist.
- **Short-baseline anomalies.** The LSND and MiniBooNE excesses, the reactor antineutrino anomaly, and the gallium deficit hint at eV-scale sterile neutrinos that would enlarge the mixing matrix beyond three-by-three. Global fits remain inconclusive, and the issue is deliberately excluded from the standard paradigm while dedicated short-baseline experiments arbitrate [8].
- **What oscillations cannot see.** Oscillation experiments are blind to the absolute mass scale, to the Majorana phases, and to the Dirac-versus-Majorana nature of the neutrino. Those questions belong to tritium endpoint, neutrinoless double-beta decay, and cosmological measurements.

## 7 Conclusion

Neutrino oscillation physics has traveled from Pontecorvo's speculative analogy to a precision science with percent-level parameters, a trajectory built on the PMNS mixing formalism [1], the discovery of matter-enhanced conversion [2][3], and a sequence of landmark experiments [4][5]. The theoretical structure is now complete enough that the remaining unknowns — mass ordering, the θ₂₃ octant, and the CP phase — are sharply posed experimental questions rather than open theoretical ones. DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande, with their complementary baselines of 1300 km and 295 km and their distinct detector technologies, are engineered to answer them within the coming decade [6][7]. A measurement of δ_CP far from the CP-conserving values would do more than complete a mixing matrix: it would furnish the first empirical foothold for leptogenesis and, with it, a candidate explanation for the existence of matter itself. The global oscillation program, summarized in the Particle Data Group review [8], stands as the most successful extension of the Standard Model ever constructed — and the next chapter is an experimental one.
