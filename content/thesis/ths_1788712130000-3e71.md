# Photonic Neural Accelerators: Mach–Zehnder Interferometer Meshes, Phase-Change Synaptic Weights, and Analog-Domain Matrix Multiplication at the Speed of Light

## Abstract

Deep neural networks are dominated by matrix–vector multiplication, an operation constrained in electronics by the von Neumann bottleneck and the energy cost of data movement. This thesis develops the physics, mathematics, and engineering of *photonic neural accelerators*: silicon-photonic systems that execute neural-network linear algebra in the analog optical domain, where multiplication occurs through interference and summation through superposition, at propagation velocities approaching the speed of light. We formalize the singular-value-decomposition mapping that programs arbitrary real matrices onto cascaded Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes via the Reck and Clements unitary decompositions; analyze wavelength-division-multiplexed microring weight banks implementing the broadcast-and-weight protocol; and treat phase-change materials (notably Ge₂Sb₂Te₅) as nonvolatile photonic synapses enabling in-memory computing at femtojoule energies. Shot noise, phase-shifter precision, and converter resolution are shown to bound photonic multiply–accumulate operations to roughly 4–8 effective bits, and empirical results — from vowel-phoneme classification to tera-operations-per-second tensor cores — are critically assessed.

## 1 Introduction

The computational appetite of deep learning has doubled roughly every three to four months for over a decade [2], decisively outrunning transistor-level improvement. Modern models spend the overwhelming majority of their floating-point operations — routinely exceeding 90% in convolutional and transformer architectures — on a single primitive: dense matrix–vector multiplication (MVM), **y = Wx**. In digital electronics, each operation pays a compounded tax: fetching weights from memory, computing the multiply–accumulate, and writing partial sums back. The resulting *memory wall* means accelerators are frequently memory-bandwidth-bound, dissipating most energy on data movement rather than arithmetic [4].

Optics offers a qualitatively different trade. Photons do not interact in linear media, so parallel signals neither crosstalk nor dissipate energy in transit; propagation across a chip takes tens of picoseconds; and a single waveguide spans tens of terahertz, exploitable through wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM). Crucially, *interference itself is arithmetic*: coherent superposition performs addition, while a tunable beam splitter multiplies a field amplitude by a programmable complex coefficient. An entire matrix multiplication can thus be executed as light traverses a passive photonic circuit — latency set by time-of-flight, energy set by the laser and detectors rather than the operation count [1].

> **Theorem (Optical linear universality):** Any complex-valued N×N matrix **M** admits a singular value decomposition **M = UΣV†**, and each unitary factor **U**, **V†** can be realized exactly by a mesh of N(N−1)/2 tunable 2×2 interferometers (Reck decomposition [5]) or in a symmetric, balanced-loss rectangular arrangement (Clements decomposition [5]); the diagonal factor **Σ** is realized by per-mode attenuation or amplification. Hence any neural-network linear layer is implementable in the analog optical domain.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 surveys the background: silicon photonics device physics, the MZI as a programmable 2×2 unitary, and the landscape of optical neural-network proposals. Section 3 describes our methodology — analytical device models, decomposition algorithms, noise and precision analysis, and the empirical-benchmark framework used to compare reported systems. Section 4 is the deep technical dive: coherent MZI-mesh matrix multiplication, WDM microring weight banks and the broadcast-and-weight protocol, phase-change synaptic weights for in-memory computing, and the fundamental precision limits of analog optics. Section 5 evaluates the empirical record, from the first on-chip vowel classifier to commercial photonic inference blades. Section 6 confronts the limitations honestly: precision ceilings, loss accumulation, thermal control, and the stubborn dominance of conversion electronics. Section 7 concludes with a sober assessment of where photonic acceleration genuinely wins.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The matrix-multiplication bottleneck

A dense layer of a neural network computes **y = σ(Wx + b)**, where **W** is an M×N weight matrix, **x** an N-dimensional input, **b** a bias, and **σ** a pointwise nonlinearity. Training adds the backward pass, dominated by the transposed operation **Wᵀδ**. In digital hardware, the energy of a 32-bit MAC is dominated not by the multiplier (~pJ in modern nodes) but by SRAM/DRAM access (tens to hundreds of pJ per weight fetch). Photonic accelerators attack this asymmetry at its root: once weights are programmed into the optical fabric — as phase-shifter voltages, ring-resonator detunings, or nonvolatile phase-change states — the MVM is executed by the physics of propagation with no per-operation memory access at all [4].

### 2.2 Silicon photonics primitives

The material platform is silicon-on-insulator (SOI): silicon waveguides (n ≈ 3.47) clad in silica (n ≈ 1.44) confine 1.3–1.6 μm light by total internal reflection with bend radii below 10 μm and propagation losses of order 1–3 dB/cm in foundry processes. The elementary computational device is the Mach–Zehnder interferometer: light is split by a directional coupler, phase-shifted by Δθ in one arm and Δφ at the output, and recombined. Its transfer matrix is the general element of U(2):

$$T(\theta,\phi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2) & e^{i\phi}\cos(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix}$$

up to coupler-phase conventions. Phase shifting is achieved *thermo-optically* (resistive heaters, microsecond response, milliwatt static power, no added loss) or *electro-optically* (carrier depletion, tens of GHz, but with free-carrier absorption loss) [5]. Input encoding and output readout use modulators and photodiodes interfaced through DACs and ADCs [2].

### 2.3 Coherent versus incoherent architectures

Two philosophies divide the field. *Coherent* architectures (Shen et al. [1]) encode information in the complex amplitude (magnitude and phase) of light and compute with unitary meshes; they exploit interference for signed arithmetic but demand phase-stable lasers and careful calibration. *Incoherent* architectures (Tait et al. [3], Feldmann et al. [2]) encode information in optical *power* at distinct wavelengths and compute by wavelength-selective filtering and photodetection; they sacrifice the phase degree of freedom but gain robustness, straightforward WDM parallelism, and foundry compatibility. Both families now report tera-scale throughputs [4].

## 3 Methodology

This thesis synthesizes three methodological strands:

1. **Analytical device modeling.** We derive the MZI transfer matrix, compose mesh transfer functions, and construct the SVD-programming procedure: given **W**, compute **W = UΣV†**, decompose each unitary via Reck/Clements into per-MZI (θ, φ) settings, and map singular values σᵢ onto attenuators. A Python reference implementation is given in Section 4.1.
2. **Noise and precision analysis.** We model shot noise, phase-shifter quantization and drift, and ADC quantization, propagating them through the mesh to bound the effective bits of a photonic MAC and derive error scaling with mesh size N.
3. **Empirical benchmark framework.** Reported systems are compared on throughput (TOPS), efficiency (TOPS/W or fJ/MAC), effective precision, and task accuracy. Vendor claims are cross-checked against peer-reviewed work, and *passive-computation* energy is distinguished from *system* energy (lasers, DACs, ADCs, control), typically two to three orders of magnitude larger [2][6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Coherent MZI Meshes: SVD-Programmed Matrix Multiplication

The foundational result of the coherent approach is due to Shen et al. [1]: an arbitrary real-valued weight matrix **W** is programmed onto photonics by its singular value decomposition. The procedure is exact in the absence of hardware error:

1. Compute **W = UΣV†** with **U**, **V** unitary and **Σ** diagonal, σᵢ ≥ 0.
2. Decompose **U** and **V†** into MZI meshes. The *Reck decomposition* [5] nulls matrix elements column by column with a triangular mesh of N(N−1)/2 MZIs; the *Clements decomposition* [5] uses a symmetric rectangular mesh with the same device count but balanced optical path lengths, which equalizes insertion loss across modes and materially improves fidelity in fabricated devices.
3. Implement **Σ** with an array of tunable attenuators (or semiconductor optical amplifiers where gain is needed), one per mode.
4. Inject the input vector **x** as coherent amplitudes, let light propagate through **V† → Σ → U**, and detect the output field.

Because propagation through the mesh takes only the optical time-of-flight — under 200 ps across a millimeter-scale chip [6] — the *latency* of an N×N MVM is effectively constant in N, while *throughput* scales with the input-encoder modulation rate (tens of GHz). Reference decomposition:

```python
import numpy as np

def mzi_angles_from_unitary(U: np.ndarray):
    """Reck-style decomposition of unitary U into (row, col, theta, phi) settings."""
    N = U.shape[0]
    A = U.copy().astype(complex)
    settings = []
    for col in range(N - 1):
        for row in range(N - 1, col, -1):
            # Null A[row, col] via 2x2 rotation on rows (row-1, row)
            a, b = A[row - 1, col], A[row, col]
            if abs(b) < 1e-12:
                continue
            theta = 2 * np.arctan2(abs(b), abs(a))
            phi = np.angle(a) - np.angle(b)
            c, s = np.cos(theta / 2), np.sin(theta / 2)
            T = np.array([[np.exp(1j * phi) * s, np.exp(1j * phi) * c],
                          [c, -s]])
            A[row - 1:row + 1, :] = T @ A[row - 1:row + 1, :]
            settings.append((row - 1, row, theta, phi))
    return settings

def program_mesh(W: np.ndarray):
    """Map real matrix W onto mesh settings."""
    U, s, Vh = np.linalg.svd(W, full_matrices=True)
    return {
        "V_dagger_mesh": mzi_angles_from_unitary(Vh),
        "singular_values": s,          # -> per-mode attenuators
        "U_mesh": mzi_angles_from_unitary(U),
        "n_mzis": W.shape[0] * (W.shape[0] - 1),  # total MZIs
    }
```

The sobering scaling law is the device count: an N×N layer requires O(N²) MZIs and 2N(N−1) phase shifters. Each thermo-optic shifter dissipates milliwatts statically, so a 256×256 mesh needs tens of watts merely to *hold* its weights — one reason commercial systems moved toward electro-optic or nonvolatile programming [6]. Fabrication variations in coupler splitting ratios (±1–2%) must be calibrated out per device, an O(N²) procedure dominating bring-up time [5].

### 4.2 WDM Microring Weight Banks and the Broadcast-and-Weight Protocol

The incoherent alternative, developed by Tait, Shastri, Prucnal and colleagues [3], assigns each neuron's output to a unique wavelength carrier. In the *broadcast-and-weight* protocol:

- **Broadcast:** N wavelength channels (λ₁…λ_N), each carrying one neuron's activation as optical power, are multiplexed onto a single bus waveguide and broadcast to every downstream neuron.
- **Weight:** At each neuron, a *microring weight bank* — a cascade of microring resonators, each thermally or electrically tuned so its resonance sits slightly detuned from one channel wavelength — taps a controllable fraction of each channel's power. Detuning the ring changes its drop-port transmission continuously, implementing a signed weight when combined with a balanced photodetector pair (through-port minus drop-port).
- **Sum and fire:** The weighted powers are summed by total-power photodetection, and the resulting photocurrent drives an electro-optic modulator (the "neuron") imprinting the weighted sum — passed through the modulator's nonlinear transfer function — onto the neuron's own wavelength carrier.

Tait et al. proved something stronger than an analogy: the governing equations of this optoelectronic system are *isomorphic* to a continuous-time recurrent neural network (CTRNN) [3]. They demonstrated the isomorphism experimentally through bifurcation analysis — observing single-node bistability across a cusp bifurcation and two-node oscillation across a Hopf bifurcation — and used a neural compiler to program a simulated 24-modulator photonic CTRNN to solve a differential-equation task, projecting a 294× speedup over a CPU baseline. The key scaling advantage is fan-in: WDM packs tens to hundreds of synaptic inputs onto one waveguide, and the weight bank's footprint grows linearly in channel count rather than quadratically [3][4].

### 4.3 Phase-Change Synaptic Weights: Nonvolatile In-Memory Photonic Computing

Both MZI meshes and ring weight banks share a weakness: *volatile* weights. Thermo-optic shifters burn static power; electro-optic shifters need continuous bias. Phase-change materials (PCMs) — most prominently Ge₂Sb₂Te₅ (GST) — solve this by storing weights in atomic structure. GST switches between amorphous (low refractive index, low loss) and crystalline (high index) states via nanosecond optical or electrical pulses, exhibiting a large index contrast (Δn ~ 1–2) at telecom wavelengths while remaining nonvolatile for years without power [2].

Feldmann et al. exploited this in the landmark *photonic tensor core* [2]: a matrix of GST cells evanescently coupled to silicon-nitride waveguides, where each cell's crystallinity sets its optical transmission — a matrix element. Input vectors are encoded on the comb lines of an on-chip soliton microcomb, and convolution reduces to measuring transmitted power. Because the computation is a *passive transmission measurement*, its energy is limited only by the photodetector: ~17 fJ per MAC projected, 14 GHz operation, 2 tera-MAC/s demonstrated [2]. Multilevel programming (partial crystallization) gives multi-bit weights — 5-bit photonic memory cells were demonstrated by Li et al. — and nonvolatility means near-zero static power between inferences [2].

> **Theorem (In-memory photonic MAC bound):** In a PCM-based photonic tensor core, the energy per MAC is lower-bounded by the photodetection energy E_det ≈ (hν/η)·SNR² per output, independent of matrix dimension N; throughput scales as B·N² with modulator bandwidth B and WDM channel count N, until limited by waveguide loss and detector saturation.

A PCM synapse is a ~10–20 nm GST patch atop a Si or SiN waveguide; the evanescent guided mode samples the GST's complex index, and an integrated micro-heater or optical pulse toggles the phase state. Endurance (10⁶–10⁹ cycles), drift of intermediate states, and pJ–nJ write energies remain the principal device-level concerns [2].

### 4.4 Precision, Noise, and the Analog Bottleneck

The fundamental question for any analog accelerator is *effective precision*. Three limits dominate, summarized below; the table that follows gives typical magnitudes and mitigations.

| Error source | Scaling | Typical magnitude | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shot noise at photodetector | SNR ∝ √(P·T/hν) | 4–6 bits at mW powers, GHz rates | Higher laser power; balanced detection |
| Phase-shifter error (DAC + thermal drift) | σ_θ ~ 2π/2^b_DAC ⊕ drift | 6–8 bits effective | Calibration, thermal isolation, dithering |
| ADC quantization at readout | ENOB 6–10 bits at GS/s | Dominates system precision | Noise-aware training; analog accumulation |
| Coherent crosstalk / stray light | ∝ N² paths | Grows with mesh size | Clements symmetric mesh; calibration |
| Waveguide loss imbalance | ~2 dB/cm × path length | Mode-dependent attenuation | Loss-aware decomposition |

*Shot noise* is the hard floor: detecting a MAC result with B bits of precision requires distinguishing 2^B power levels, demanding photon counts scaling as ~4^B per measurement — an exponential energy cost capping practical photonic MACs at roughly 4–8 effective bits [4]. This suffices for *inference* (4-bit quantization is routine in digital accelerators) but is prohibitive for *training*, which needs high-precision gradient accumulation. The field has therefore converged on photonic *inference* accelerators trained digitally with quantization- and noise-aware methods — validated by the 76.7% vowel-classification accuracy of Shen's 56-MZI processor despite analog imperfections [1].

A second, subtler limit is *error accumulation with N*: each output traverses O(N) imperfect components, so uncorrelated per-MZI errors grow as √N while systematic calibration residuals grow linearly. Hardware-aware training — injecting measured device statistics into the training loop — recovers most lost accuracy and is now standard practice [4].

### 4.5 System Integration: From Lab Demos to Photonic Blades

The trajectory from Shen's benchtop processor to product is best exemplified by Lightmatter, founded by Harris and colleagues from the MIT group behind [1]. Their Envise architecture 3D-stacks a 12-nm CMOS control ASIC (SRAM, DAC/ADC drivers, RISC cores) atop a silicon-photonic compute die containing 256×256 photonic arithmetic units built from MZI meshes [6]. Two Envise blades reportedly process ~1.2 million ResNet-50 inferences per second at ~240 inferences/sec/Watt, with claims of 3× the throughput of an NVIDIA DGX-A100 [6]. The instructive lesson is architectural: the photonic die is deliberately *dumb* — passive interference fabric — while control, memory, and nonlinearities live in the electronic stack, conceding that the ADC/DAC boundary, not the optics, sets system efficiency. An efficient all-optical nonlinearity for deep cascaded networks without O/E/O conversion at every layer remains unsolved at competitive energy scales [4].

## 5 Empirical Evidence

The table below consolidates the key experimental milestones, distinguishing peer-reviewed results from vendor claims:

| System | Year | Architecture | Reported result | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shen et al., programmable nanophotonic processor [1] | 2017 | 56-MZI coherent mesh, SVD-programmed | 76.7% on 4-vowel classification vs ~90% digital baseline | ~5 bits effective |
| Tait et al., broadcast-and-weight network [3] | 2017 | 16-MRR weight bank, 4-node recurrent | CTRNN isomorphism via cusp/Hopf bifurcations; 294× projected CPU speedup (24-node sim) | Analog continuous |
| Feldmann et al., photonic tensor core [2] | 2021 | GST PCM cells + soliton microcomb, WDM | 2 TOPS at >14 GHz; ~17 fJ/MAC projected; parallel convolution | 5-bit PCM cells |
| Xu et al., photonic convolutional accelerator | 2021 | Microcomb + waveshaper, time-wavelength interleaving | 11 TOPS (Nature 589, 44–51) | 8-bit input |
| Lightmatter Envise blades [6] | 2021–23 | 256×256 MZI arithmetic units, 3D-stacked ASIC | ~1.2M ResNet-50 inf/s per 2 blades; ~240 inf/s/W; 3× DGX-A100 claim | Vendor-reported |
| Shastri et al. review consensus [4] | 2021 | Survey | Photonic MAC at fJ scale feasible; system efficiency dominated by E/O conversion | 4–8 bits typical |

Three patterns emerge. **First**, throughput records have advanced roughly an order of magnitude every two years, driven by WDM channel-count scaling. **Second**, the gap between *passive-computation* energy (fJ/MAC) and *system* energy (pJ/MAC including lasers, DACs, ADCs, control) remains two to three orders of magnitude — the honest metric is system-level TOPS/W, where photonics leads digital by roughly one order of magnitude on favorable workloads [2][6]. **Third**, demonstrated task complexity remains modest: no photonic system has yet run a billion-parameter model end-to-end [4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Precision ceiling.** Shot noise and converter resolution cap photonic MACs at ~4–8 effective bits — adequate for inference, insufficient for training, relegating photonics to the inference side of the workflow [4].
2. **Weak optical nonlinearity.** No photonic equivalent of a free, cascadable, low-energy transistor-like nonlinearity exists; deep networks need optical–electrical–optical conversion per layer, and the ADC/DAC energy at these boundaries dominates the system budget [4][6].
3. **Loss and scale.** An N×N coherent mesh needs O(N²) MZIs; waveguide loss (~2 dB/cm) accumulates along O(N)-length paths, demanding higher laser power that erodes the efficiency advantage as N grows [5].
4. **Thermal control burden.** Thermo-optic shifters dissipate milliwatts each; a large mesh needs watts of static power plus per-device calibration, and thermal crosstalk corrupts weight fidelity unless compensated [1][5].
5. **Fabrication variation.** Coupler splitting-ratio errors of ±1–2% break the ideal U(2) parameterization; every large mesh to date required individual device calibration, an O(N²) bring-up cost [5].
6. **PCM endurance and write cost.** Phase-change synapses introduce finite cycling endurance, drift of intermediate states, and pJ–nJ write energies — acceptable for rarely-updated inference weights, problematic for in-situ learning [2].
7. **System economics.** Silicon-photonic chips remain far costlier per area than CMOS logic, packaging (fiber attach, laser integration) dominates cost, and the software stack for mapping models onto analog hardware is immature versus CUDA/cuDNN [6].

## 7 Conclusion

Photonic neural accelerators rest on a genuine physical advantage: in the optical domain, matrix–vector multiplication is executed by interference and superposition during propagation, with latency set by time-of-flight and marginal energy set by detection rather than operation count. The mathematics is exact — the SVD maps any real matrix onto MZI meshes via the Reck or Clements decompositions — and the device physics is foundry-compatible. Three architectural families have each validated part of the vision: coherent MZI meshes for exact unitary programming [1][5], WDM microring weight banks with provable CTRNN isomorphism [3], and phase-change tensor cores pushing passive MAC energy toward tens of femtojoules with nonvolatile in-memory weights [2].

Yet the honest accounting is hybrid. The precision ceiling (~4–8 bits), the absence of a competitive all-optical nonlinearity, and the dominance of lasers, DACs, and ADCs in the system power budget mean photonics will not replace digital electronics for general computation. Its winning domain is narrower and still valuable: energy-constrained, latency-critical *inference*, where 3D-stacked photonic–electronic systems already claim order-of-magnitude efficiency gains on dense linear algebra [6]. The research frontier is therefore not "all-optical computing" but co-design of analog photonic fabrics with digital control: noise-aware training, calibration-free meshes, higher-endurance PCMs, and wafer-scale co-packaged optics.
