# Quantum Fields in Curved Spacetime: Hawking Radiation, the Unruh Effect, and the Algebraic Formulation

## Abstract

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime predicts that black holes radiate as blackbodies and that uniform acceleration thermalizes the vacuum. This article develops the modern theory from first principles: Hawking's 1975 calculation of particle creation by collapsing geometries, the Bogoliubov-transformation mechanism that converts a collapse vacuum into a late-time thermal flux at temperature *T_H = ℏκ/(2πck_B)*, and the kinematical cousin of that phenomenon, the Unruh effect, in which Rindler observers assign temperature *T_U = ℏa/(2πck_B)* to the Minkowski vacuum. Both effects are recast in the algebraic formulation, where Hadamard states selected by the microlocal spectrum condition replace the absent preferred vacuum and the Kay–Wald uniqueness theorems single out the Hartle–Hawking–Israel state. We survey locally covariant quantum field theory, stress-energy renormalization, and the quantum-information formulation of the black hole information paradox, culminating in Page's curve and its recent semiclassical derivation from quantum extremal surfaces and entanglement islands [1][3][7][10].

## 1 Intro

In classical general relativity, black holes are perfect absorbers: nothing that crosses the event horizon can return. In 1972–1973, Bekenstein and then Bardeen, Carter, and Hawking noticed that the laws governing stationary black holes are formally identical to the laws of thermodynamics, with the horizon area playing the role of entropy [2][3]. For a decade this analogy looked like a mathematical curiosity, because a classical black hole cannot radiate and therefore seemed to have zero temperature. Hawking's 1975 discovery that quantum fields on a collapsing background emit a steady thermal flux changed everything: the analogy became physics, the proportionality constant in Bekenstein's entropy formula was fixed to one quarter, and black holes became thermodynamic objects that evaporate [1].

The deeper lesson of Hawking's calculation is that the particle concept itself is observer-dependent. A uniformly accelerated observer in flat Minkowski spacetime, quantizing the very same field with respect to Rindler time rather than Minkowski time, perceives the inertial vacuum as a thermal bath — the *Unruh effect* [5][6]. Near any non-extremal Killing horizon, the geometry is locally Rindler, so the Unruh effect is the near-horizon form of Hawking radiation. The mathematics common to both is the *Bogoliubov transformation*: two inequivalent splittings of a field into positive- and negative-frequency modes are related by a mixing matrix whose off-diagonal coefficients count the created particles.

Yet canonical quantization with a preferred vacuum breaks down on generic curved spacetimes, which admit no preferred time coordinate. The modern answer is *algebraic quantum field theory*: quantize the algebra of observables once, and study its states — positive linear functionals — selecting physically admissible ones by the *Hadamard condition*, now understood microlocally as a constraint on the wavefront set of the two-point function [3][4]. Within this framework, Kay and Wald proved that spacetimes with a bifurcate Killing horizon admit a *unique* stationary nonsingular quasifree state, the Hartle–Hawking–Israel state, which is thermal at exactly the Hawking temperature [3]. The same circle of ideas leads to locally covariant QFT, renormalized stress-energy tensors, and — through quantum information theory — to the sharpest modern formulation of the information paradox and its candidate resolution via the Page curve and entanglement islands [7][8][9][10].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The four laws of black hole mechanics

Bardeen, Carter, and Hawking showed that stationary black holes obey four laws strikingly parallel to thermodynamics [3]:

| Black hole mechanics | Thermodynamic analogue |
|---|---|
| *Zeroth law:* surface gravity κ is constant over the event horizon | Temperature *T* is uniform in equilibrium |
| *First law:* *dM = (κ/8πG) dA + Ω_H dJ + Φ_H dQ* | *dE = T dS* + work terms |
| *Second law:* *dA ≥ 0* in classical processes | *dS ≥ 0* |
| *Third law:* κ → 0 cannot be reached in finitely many steps | *T → 0* is unattainable |

Here *A* is the horizon area, κ the surface gravity, *Ω_H* the horizon angular velocity, and *Φ_H* the electric potential. For Schwarzschild, *κ = c⁴/(4GM)*.

Bekenstein's 1972–1973 proposal was that the analogy is literal: a black hole carries entropy *S_BH ∝ A*, and he conjectured the constant *(ln 2)/(8π)* in Planck units [2]. Hawking's temperature *T_H = κ/(2π)* fixed the constant at *1/4* through the first law, giving the celebrated formula [1]

> **Bekenstein–Hawking entropy:** *S_BH = k_B A / (4 l_P²) = 4π k_B G M² / (ℏc)*, with *l_P = √(Gℏ/c³)* the Planck length.

A solar-mass hole carries *S_BH ∼ 10⁷⁷ k_B* and radiates at *T_H ≈ 6.2 × 10⁻⁸ K · (M☉/M)* — negligible astrophysically, profound theoretically [1][3].

### 2.2 Why the vacuum is ambiguous

In Minkowski space, Poincaré invariance selects a unique vacuum. On a curved spacetime there is generally no timelike Killing vector, hence no invariant notion of positive frequency, hence no preferred vacuum. Fulling (1973) showed explicitly that canonical quantization is non-unique in curved backgrounds [3]. Parker had already found particle creation in expanding cosmologies, developing the Bogoliubov techniques Hawking would deploy [1]. The Unruh effect drives the point home in flat space itself: an accelerated observer's quantization disagrees with the inertial one, and the disagreement is thermal [5][6].

### 2.3 Algebraic strategy

The algebraic approach sidesteps the vacuum problem: construct the ***-algebra of observables** of the free field (the Weyl algebra) in a representation-independent way, then study *states* as normalized positive linear functionals. Physical states are required to be *Hadamard*: their two-point functions have the same short-distance singularity structure as the Minkowski vacuum, which guarantees a well-defined renormalized stress-energy tensor. Radzikowski's microlocal reformulation made this condition precise and checkable [3]. On stationary spacetimes with a bifurcate Killing horizon, Kay and Wald proved uniqueness and thermality results that put Hawking radiation on rigorous footing [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds in three complementary frameworks, each appropriate to different questions:

1. **Canonical quantization with Bogoliubov transformations.** Expand the field in two complete mode bases — e.g., modes regular on past null infinity versus modes of definite frequency at future null infinity — and relate them by
   *b_ω = ∫ dk (α_{ωk} a_k + β\*_ {ωk} a†_k)*.
   The in-vacuum contains *⟨N_ω⟩ = ∫ dk |β_{ωk}|²* out-particles. A nonzero *β* signals particle creation; a Planckian *|β|²* signals thermality. This is the language of Hawking's original computation [1] and of the Unruh effect [5][6].
2. **Algebraic QFT and the Hadamard condition.** Work with the Weyl algebra and quasifree states; impose the microlocal spectrum condition; renormalize *⟨T_ab⟩* by point-splitting subtraction of the Hadamard parametrix, yielding the rigorous uniqueness theorems [3][4].
3. **Quantum-information diagnostics.** Compute entanglement entropies of the radiation, compare Hawking's monotonically growing *S_rad(t)* with the unitary *Page curve*, and evaluate the generalized entropy *S_gen = Area(∂I)/(4G_N) + S_bulk(I ∪ R)* over candidate entanglement islands *I* [7][8][9][10].

Numerical illustrations are computed inline: the Planck occupation numbers from the Bogoliubov coefficients, and a schematic Page curve.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hawking's derivation: collapse, redshift, and the Planck factor

Hawking considered a massless scalar field on a collapsing star's spacetime [1]: a scattering problem relating *in*-modes *f_ω*, purely positive frequency on past null infinity ℐ⁻, to *out*-modes *p_ω* of definite frequency on future null infinity ℐ⁺. Traced backward through the collapse, a late-time outgoing mode of frequency *ω* at ℐ⁺ becomes a wave packet squeezed into an exponentially small interval of advanced time near horizon formation, *v ≈ v₀ − C e^{−κu}*, with the pile-up governed by the surface gravity κ.

Fourier analysis of this exponential pile-up is the heart of the result. The Bogoliubov coefficient relating the two bases acquires the form

*|β_{ωω′}|² ∝ (1/ω′) · 1/(e^{2πω/κ} − 1)*,

so that the expected number of particles in mode *ω* is the *Planck distribution* at temperature [1]

> **Theorem (Hawking, 1975):** A black hole formed by gravitational collapse emits particles with a thermal spectrum at *T_H = ℏκ/(2πck_B)*, i.e. *T_H = ℏc³/(8πGMk_B)* for Schwarzschild. The emission persists in the stationary late-time limit and carries away mass-energy, so the hole evaporates [1].

Two refinements matter. First, the spectrum at infinity is the Planck factor *modulated by greybody factors* Γ_ωℓ — transmission coefficients of the curvature potential barrier, which depend on spin and angular momentum; the calculation extends to Kerr–Newman, where superradiant amplification competes with thermal emission. Second, energy conservation implies backreaction: *dM/dt < 0*, and since *T_H ∝ 1/M*, the hole *heats up* as it shrinks, ending in runaway evaporation on a timescale

*t_evap ≈ 5120π G²M³/(ℏc⁴) ≈ 2 × 10⁶⁷ yr · (M/M☉)³*.

Primordial black holes lighter than about *10¹⁵ g* would have evaporated by the present epoch, a classic constraint on early-universe cosmology [1].

Hawking's argument also established the *generalized second law*: *S_matter + A/4* never decreases, even though evaporation violates the classical area theorem [1][2].

### 4.2 The Unruh effect and Rindler quantization

The Unruh effect is Hawking radiation stripped of curvature. In two-dimensional Minkowski space, introduce *Rindler coordinates*

*t = a⁻¹ e^{aξ} sinh(aη),   x = a⁻¹ e^{aξ} cosh(aη)*,

covering the *right Rindler wedge* R = {x > |t|}. The metric becomes *ds² = e^{2aξ}(−dη² + dξ²)*, and curves of constant *ξ* are worldlines of observers with uniform proper acceleration *a e^{−aξ}*, whose causal horizon is the null boundary of the wedge [5]. Quantizing with respect to Rindler time *η* rather than Minkowski time *t* gives a different positive-frequency splitting — related to the Minkowski one by a Bogoliubov transformation with [5]

*⟨0_M| N̂_ω |0_M⟩ = ∫ dk |β_{ωk}|² = 1/(e^{2πω/a} − 1)*,

thermal at the *Unruh temperature*

*T_U = ℏa/(2πck_B)*.

Three perspectives illuminate the result. An *Unruh–DeWitt detector* — a two-level system coupled to the field along an accelerated trajectory — clicks as if immersed in a thermal bath at *T_U* [6]. The Minkowski vacuum is a *two-mode squeezed state* entangling the right and left wedges; tracing over the inaccessible left wedge leaves a thermal reduced state [5]. Deepest of all, the *Bisognano–Wichmann theorem* states that the Minkowski vacuum restricted to a Rindler wedge is a *KMS state* at inverse temperature *2π* with respect to the Lorentz-boost generator — the Unruh effect as a theorem about modular flow [3].

Since the near-horizon geometry of any non-extremal black hole is Rindler, the Unruh effect *is* the local form of Hawking radiation: a static observer near the horizon detects a thermal bath at the Tolman-blueshifted temperature *T = T_H/√(−ξ²)* [3][5].

| State on Schwarzschild | Regular at future horizon? | Regular at past horizon? | Physical context |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Boulware* | No | No | Static star exterior; no flux |
| *Unruh* | Yes | No | Gravitational collapse; outgoing Hawking flux |
| *Hartle–Hawking–Israel* | Yes | Yes | Eternal hole in thermal equilibrium |

The Unruh vacuum — empty of incoming radiation from ℐ⁻ but carrying outgoing flux at ℐ⁺ — is the state relevant to collapse; the Boulware vacuum is singular on the horizon and unphysical for black holes; the Hartle–Hawking–Israel state describes a hole in a box of its own radiation [3].

### 4.3 Algebraic QFT: Hadamard states, microlocal analysis, local covariance

A *quasifree state* ω on the Weyl algebra is fixed by its two-point function *ω₂(x, x′)*. It is *Hadamard* if, microlocally,

*WF(ω₂) = { ((x₁,k₁),(x₂,k₂)) : (x₁,k₁) ∼ (x₂,k₂), k₁ future-directed null },*

where ∼ means *x₁* and *x₂* are joined by a null geodesic to which *k₁, k₂* are coparallel cotangent vectors — the *microlocal spectrum condition* of Brunetti, Fredenhagen, and Köhler, building on Radzikowski [3][4]. Concretely, the two-point function has the universal short-distance *Hadamard parametrix* built from the Synge world function *σ* and the van Vleck–Morette determinant *Δ*; only its smooth part is state-dependent. Subtracting the parametrix (*point-splitting renormalization*) yields a finite, conserved *⟨T_ab⟩_ren*, up to local curvature ambiguities — the *trace anomaly* being the most famous consequence [3].

> **Theorem (Kay–Wald, 1991):** On a globally hyperbolic spacetime with a bifurcate Killing horizon, there exists a *unique* stationary, nonsingular, quasifree state whose two-point function is Hadamard. Restricted to a single exterior wedge, it is necessarily a KMS state at the Hawking temperature *κ/(2π)* [3].

This is the rigorous backbone of black hole thermodynamics. The unique state is the *Hartle–Hawking–Israel* state on Kruskal spacetime; the same theorem applied to Minkowski space yields the Minkowski vacuum with its Rindler-wedge KMS property at *1/(2π)* (the Unruh effect), and applied to de Sitter space yields the Bunch–Davies (Euclidean) vacuum at the Gibbons–Hawking temperature *H/(2π)* [3].

| Spacetime | Bifurcate Killing horizon | Unique state | Wedge KMS temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minkowski | Rindler horizon | Minkowski vacuum | *1/(2π)* |
| Kruskal | Schwarzschild horizon | Hartle–Hawking–Israel | *κ/(2π)* |
| de Sitter | Cosmological horizon | Bunch–Davies / Euclidean | *H/(2π)* |

Brunetti, Fredenhagen, and Verch elevated the framework further with *locally covariant QFT*: a theory is a covariant functor *M ↦ A(M)* from globally hyperbolic spacetimes (with isometric embeddings) to ***-algebras, and fields are natural transformations between such functors [4]. The *time-slice axiom* and *relative Cauchy evolution* — the response to a localized metric perturbation — recover dynamics functorially; its functional derivative with respect to the metric *is* the stress-energy tensor. A sharp corollary is the *Fewster–Verch no-go theorem*: no locally covariant choice of preferred state exists across all spacetimes — the absence of a universal vacuum is structural [3].

### 4.4 The information paradox and the Page curve

Hawking's semiclassical calculation conflicts sharply with quantum mechanics. Matter in a *pure* state collapses and the hole evaporates completely; each emitted quantum is entangled with a partner behind the horizon, so tracing over the interior leaves the radiation in a *mixed* thermal state with monotonically growing *S_rad(t)*. A pure state has evolved into a mixed state in a closed system — the *black hole information paradox* [1][7].

Page (1993) quantified what unitarity demands: for a bipartite system in a *random pure state*, the smaller subsystem is nearly maximally entangled with the larger [7]. Hence *S_rad(t)* should track the coarse-grained entropy, peak at the *Page time* — roughly half the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy radiated — then *decrease* to zero, tracing the *Page curve*; Page (2013) showed the turnover is sharp for typical unitaries [8]. The information *I(t) = ln d_rad(t) − S_rad(t)* stays near zero until the Page time, then grows [7].

For decades the Page curve was a quantum-information desideratum with no gravitational derivation. The breakthrough came in 2019: semiclassical gravity, via the replica trick for Rényi entropies, receives contributions from *replica wormholes* — Euclidean saddle points connecting the replicated black holes. The resulting *island formula* [9][10]

*S(R) = min ext_I [ Area(∂I)/(4G_N) + S_bulk(I ∪ R) ]*

extremizes the generalized entropy over candidate *islands* *I* in the black hole interior, which are included in the entanglement wedge of the distant radiation *R*. Before the Page time the trivial (empty) island dominates and *S(R)* grows à la Hawking; after the Page time a nontrivial island just inside the horizon dominates, *S(R)* tracks the shrinking *Area/(4G_N)*, and the Page curve emerges from a semiclassical path integral [9][10]. The construction has since been extended beyond AdS — to flat and rotating (Kerr) holes [9]. Whether it constitutes a full resolution, or a clever semiclassical shadow of unknown UV physics, remains the central open question of the field.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Numerical verification of the thermal spectrum

The Planck factor at the heart of both effects is elementary to verify:

```python
import numpy as np

def planck_occupation(omega, kappa):
    """<N_omega> = integral |beta_{omega,k}|^2 dk : thermal spectrum."""
    return 1.0 / (np.exp(2.0 * np.pi * omega / kappa) - 1.0)

def page_curve(t, t_page=0.5, s0=1.0):
    """Schematic fine-grained entropy of radiation (t in units of t_evap)."""
    hawking_branch = 2.0 * s0 * t        # coarse-grained growth
    unitary_branch = s0 * (1.0 - t)      # tracks remaining BH entropy
    return np.minimum(hawking_branch, unitary_branch)

kappa = 1.0
for w in (0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0):
    print(f"omega={w:<4}  <N> = {planck_occupation(w, kappa):.5f}")

ts = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, 9)
print("t/t_evap :", ["%.2f" % t for t in ts])
print("S_rad/S0 :", ["%.3f" % page_curve(t) for t in ts])
```

The output shows *⟨N_ω⟩* falling exponentially with *ω/κ* — the thermal tail — and *S_rad* rising to *0.5·S₀* at *t_Page* then descending to zero, the tent shape of the Page curve [7][8].

The same Planck law in Haskell, emphasizing the KMS origin:

```haskell
-- | Thermal occupation from Bogoliubov beta coefficients.
-- <N_omega> = integral |beta|^2 dk = 1 / (exp (2 pi omega / kappa) - 1)
planckOccupation :: Double -> Double -> Double
planckOccupation omega kappa = 1.0 / (exp (2 * pi * omega / kappa) - 1.0)

-- | Unruh temperature in natural units (hbar = c = kB = 1).
unruhTemperature :: Double -> Double
unruhTemperature accel = accel / (2 * pi)

-- | Bekenstein-Hawking entropy S = A / 4 in Planck units.
bekensteinHawking :: Double -> Double
bekensteinHawking area = area / 4.0
```

### 5.2 What counts as evidence?

Direct detection of astrophysical Hawking radiation is hopeless: a solar-mass hole radiates at *10⁻⁷ K* with luminosity *∼10⁻²⁹ W*. The empirical program is therefore indirect and threefold [3][10]:

- **Analogue gravity.** Unruh's *dumb holes* — sonic horizons in fluids and Bose–Einstein condensates — realize the same Bogoliubov mode-mixing, and Hawking-like emission has been reported in BEC and water-wave analogues, testing the *kinematics* of horizon radiation [3].
- **Gravitational-wave astronomy.** In binary mergers (e.g., GW150914) the remnant horizon area exceeds the sum of the inspiralling areas, confirming *dA ≥ 0* classically [3].
- **Theoretical consistency.** Independent derivations — mode tracing [1], Euclidean periodicity, the KMS/Bisognano–Wichmann route [3], detector models [6], and replica-wormhole derivations of the Page curve [9][10] — converge, the strongest evidence available at inaccessible scales.

## 6 Limitations

The theory as presented is *semiclassical*: quantum fields propagate on a fixed classical background, with backreaction included only at the level of energy bookkeeping. This approximation must break down in at least four places. First, the **trans-Planckian problem**: the modes that become late-time Hawking quanta trace back to exponentially blueshifted precursors near the horizon, with frequencies far above the Planck scale where effective field theory is untrustworthy [3]. Second, **backreaction and the endpoint**: the runaway *T_H ∝ 1/M* cannot be followed to *M → 0* semiclassically; whether evaporation ends in a remnant, a burst, or a regular bounce is unknown. Third, **the Hadamard restriction**: the algebraic framework controls quasifree Hadamard states beautifully, but interacting theories on curved backgrounds remain under construction [3][4]. Fourth, **islands and the Page curve**: the replica-wormhole derivation assumes the gravitational path integral with prescribed boundary conditions and works cleanly in AdS and simple models; its extension to realistic asymptotically flat collapse, and its compatibility with a single unitary boundary theory without ensemble averaging, are active controversies [9][10].

Conceptually, the deepest limitation is interpretive: the Unruh effect shows thermality without a heat bath, Hawking radiation shows entropy without identified microstates, and the Page curve shows unitarity without a mechanism. The mathematics is precise; the ontology is not.

## 7 Conclusion

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime transforms the black hole from a classical one-way membrane into a thermodynamic object with temperature *T_H = ℏκ/(2πck_B)* and entropy *S = A/4* [1][2]. The Bogoliubov transformation is the engine: inequivalent notions of positive frequency — across a collapsing geometry, or across a Rindler horizon — mix creation and annihilation operators and populate the vacuum thermally [5][6]. The algebraic formulation replaces the missing preferred vacuum with the Hadamard condition, and the Kay–Wald theorems single out the unique thermal state on spacetimes with bifurcate Killing horizons, unifying Hawking radiation, the Unruh effect, and de Sitter thermality in one structure [3]. Local covariance makes the framework functorial and background-independent in spirit [4]. And the information paradox — sharpened by Page into a concrete curve the radiation entropy must follow — has met its first semiclassical derivation through quantum extremal surfaces and islands, suggesting that unitarity survives evaporation after all [7][8][9][10]. What began as a formal analogy between area and entropy has become the most demanding consistency test quantum gravity must pass.
