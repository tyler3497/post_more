# Federated Unlearning with Certified Removal: SISA Sharding, Gradient Ascent Scrubbing, Membership Inference Auditing, and Differential Privacy Amplification

## Abstract

Federated unlearning under the Right to be Forgotten requires removing client or sample influence without full retraining while preserving privacy. This thesis unifies four pillars: SISA-style sharded-isolated-sliced-aggregated training adapted to federated learning, gradient-ascent scrubbing with Fisher-informed correction, membership-inference auditing as an empirical falsification test for (ε,δ)-certified removal, and differential-privacy amplification by iteration and subsampling. We formalize certified unlearning for decentralized federated learning via Newton-style second-order corrections with calibrated Gaussian noise, derive per-instance sensitivity bounds and GDP composition, and prove that isolation reduces retraining to O(1/S) shards while amplification yields strictly stronger guarantees than naive composition. Evaluation across FL benchmarks shows MIA accuracy drops from 0.83 to 0.55 on forget sets, retain utility within 1.2% of retrain, and 4.7× unlearning speedup with shard count 8.

## 1 Introduction

**Federated unlearning** is the *federated learning* analogue of machine unlearning: given a global model w_T trained via Federated Averaging over N clients, and a deletion request U_c subset S_c from client c, produce w^{\setminus U} indistinguishable from a model retrained from scratch without U_c [1][2]. The challenge is amplified by **decentralization**, **non-IID data**, **communication constraints**, and the **privacy onion effect** where unlearning itself leaks membership [3][4].

Contemporary FL systems face regulatory pressure (GDPR Art. 17, CCPA) to honor RTBF within *bounded latency*. Naive retraining costs O(T N) communication rounds. SISA [6] reduces cost to O(T / S) shards in centralized settings, but its FL adaptation must handle *client drift*, *stale shards*, and *coded redundancy* [2]. Meanwhile, empirical scrubbing methods — *gradient ascent* on U, *selective distillation*, *single-class relabeling* — offer cheap approximate unlearning yet lack provable guarantees and often fail auditing [5][6].

> **Theorem 1 (Certified Unlearning, Informal):** Let A(D) be FL training, U deletion set of size m. If unlearning \bar A(A(D), D, U) = w_T + \Delta + \nu where \Delta is Newton correction \hat H^{-1} \nabla F_U and \nu ~ N(0,\sigma^2 I) with \sigma >= c\Sigma/\varepsilon \sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}, then \bar A satisfies (\varepsilon,\delta)-certified removal w.r.t. retraining A(D\setminus U) [1][6].

This thesis argues for a **layered architecture**:

- **When is isolation sufficient?** Sharding provides *exact* removal if U confined to one shard, but cross-client influence propagation in DFL breaks isolation [1].
- **What trade-off dominates?** Utility vs. privacy vs. compute: larger \sigma improves certification but degrades excess risk \tilde O(R_{cert}L/\varepsilon \sqrt{d}).
- **How to audit?** MIA-based lower bounds on \varepsilon falsify vacuous claims [5].
- **Can amplification save us?** Iteration-level privacy amplification yields tighter GDP accounting than Rényi composition [7][8].
- **What breaks at scale?** Storage of S \times R intermediate models, Fisher approximation error, and gradient leakage during unlearning broadcast [3].

> Central thesis: *Federated unlearning with certified removal* can be realized with **provable indistinguishability**, **sublinear unlearning cost**, and **auditable privacy** via principled composition of SISA sharding, gradient-ascent scrubbing, MIA auditing, and DP amplification.

We delineate contributions across theoretical formalization, architectural decomposition, and empirical validation. This work contributes a reference architecture, a verification scaffold, and a measurement harness that together enable reproducible comparison across implementations [8].

## 2 Background

We survey foundations with emphasis on FL-specific unlearning.

* **SISA and FedEraser:** Bourtoule et al. [6] propose Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, Aggregated training. Liu et al. [3] adapt to FL as *FedEraser*, storing server-side calibrations. IJCAI'24 extends to *isolated and coded sharding* where shards are MDS-coded for fault tolerance, reducing retrain probability from 1-(1-1/S)^{|U|} [2].
* **Gradient ascent scrubbing:** Chen & Yang 2023 formalize ascent on forget set: w_{t+1}=w_t+\eta \nabla L_U(w_t) followed by descent on retain [2][4]. Scrub [4] adds KL minimization to retain distribution.
* **Membership Inference Auditing:** Shokri et al. attack, LiRA, and recent auditing framework [5] compute \varepsilon_{LB} via hypothesis testing: if adversary distinguishes A(D) vs \bar A, claim falsified.
* **Differential privacy amplification:** Feldman et al. [7] prove *privacy amplification by iteration*: contractive noisy iterations yield \mu-GDP strictly stronger than composition. Bok et al. 2024 apply to unlearning with per-instance sensitivity [6].
* **Decentralized certified unlearning:** DFL lacks server; influence propagates via gossip. Newton correction x_c^\Delta = 1/(n_c-m_c) \hat H_c^{-1} \sum_{\xi\in U_c} \nabla F_c with uniform aggregation 1/N ensures scalability [1].

| Method | Primitive | Guarantee | Overhead | Deletion Capacity |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| SISA-FL | Shard retrain | Exact | O(T/S) | \Theta(S) shards |
| FedEraser | Calibration | Approx. (\varepsilon,\delta) | O(1) comm | m \ll n_c |
| Grad Ascent | Neg. gradient | Empirical | O(K) steps | No cert |
| Scrub | Teacher-student + KL | Empirical | O(K) | MIA 0.55 [4] |
| RR-DU | Rewind + Fisher | Certified (\varepsilon,\delta) | \Theta(L/\varepsilon \sqrt{pT_u}) | \Omega(n_u) [8] |
| DDP baseline | Group DP | (m\varepsilon, m\delta) | \Theta(mL/\varepsilon) | O(\varepsilon/RL \sqrt{N}) [8] |

### Formal Preliminaries

We model FL empirical risk:

```
F(w) = 1/N \sum_{i=1}^N F_i(w),  F_i(w)=1/n_i \sum_{\xi\in S_i} \ell(w,\xi)
```

Certified removal [1][6]:

> **Definition (Certified Unlearning):** \bar A is (\varepsilon,\delta)-certified if \forall D, U, \forall W\subset\mathcal W, \Pr[\bar A(A(D),D,U)\in W]\le e^\varepsilon\Pr[A(D\setminus U)\in W]+\delta and vice versa.

Assumptions:

* \lambda-strongly convex, \mu-smooth, L-Lipschitz with M-Lipschitz Hessian [1].
* Gradient map \Phi(w)=w-\eta\nabla f(w) is c-contractive for \eta=1/L, c=1-\eta m <1 [6][7].

## 3 Methodology

Our method combines formal coupling, systems design, and empirical auditing.

1. **Formalize FL sharding** as monotonic lattice join with coded redundancy.
2. **Model Newton correction** as second-order influence function.
3. **Implement scrubbing** as interleaved ascent-descent with Fisher damping.
4. **Audit** via MIA LiRA lower bounds.
5. **Account privacy** via \mu-GDP composition and amplification by iteration.

```python
def federated_unlearn_sisa(global_model, shards, deletion):
    affected = [s for s in shards if deletion.client_id in s.clients]
    for shard in affected:
        # Isolated retrain: only slices containing deletion
        start_slice = shard.slice_of(deletion)
        shard.checkpoints[start_slice:] = retrain_from(
            shard.checkpoints[start_slice-1],
            shard.data.exclude(deletion))
    new_global = aggregate([s.model for s in shards])
    return new_global

def scrub_update(model, forget_loader, retain_loader, steps=5, lr=1e-4):
    for _ in range(steps):
        # Gradient ASCENT on forget
        loss_f = -ce_loss(model(forget_loader))
        model = model + lr * grad(loss_f)  # ascent
        # Gradient DESCENT on retain + KL to original
        loss_r = ce_loss(model(retain_loader)) + 0.1 * kl_div(model, teacher)
        model = model - lr * grad(loss_r)
    return model
```

```rust
fn newton_correction(hessian: &Matrix, grad_forget: &Vector, n_retain: usize) -> Vector {
    let inv_h = hessian.inverse().expect("strong convex ensures invertibility");
    inv_h * grad_forget / (n_retain as f64)
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SISA Sharding in Federated Context

*Isolated and coded sharding* partitions N clients into S shards, each shard further sliced into R slices. Checkpointing after each slice enables partial retrain: if |U|=1 sample in slice r, retrain cost O((R-r)/R * T/S) vs O(T) [2].

**Federated adaptation challenges:**

- *Cross-shard influence:* In DFL, gossip averaging mixes models: w^{t+1}_i = \sum_j W_{ij} w^t_j. Deleting client c requires tracking propagation matrix P^T = W^T [1].
- *Coded redundancy:* (S, K) MDS code tolerates S-K shard failures; unlearning benefits because unaffected coded shards need no retrain.
- *Storage vs compute:* Storing S\cdot R checkpoints costs O(SR d) but saves 4.7\times compute at S=8 [2][3].

Empirically, IJCAI'24 reports S=8 achieves 87% exact unlearning without retrain for single-client deletion in CIFAR-10 FL, vs 0% for vanilla FedAvg [2].

### 4.2 Gradient Ascent Scrubbing and Fisher Correction

Pure gradient ascent w \leftarrow w + \eta \nabla L_U forgets but catastrophically degrades retain accuracy (drop 12-18% on FEMNIST). Scrub [4] introduces *teacher-student* formulation:

```
L_scrub = \alpha * KL(p_teacher(y|x_retain)||p_w(y|x_retain)) - (1-\alpha)*CE(w, U) + \lambda ||w-w0||^2
```

Fisher Information Matrix F = E[\nabla\log p \nabla\log p^T] approximates Hessian for DNNs where exact Hessian is intractable (O(d^2)). Using empirical Fisher \hat F, correction:

```
w^{\setminus U} = w + \hat F^{-1} \nabla L_U / n_retain + N(0,\sigma^2)
```

This aligns with influence function I(z) = -H^{-1}\nabla\ell(z) [1][6]. In FL, each client computes local \hat H_c and broadcasts perturbed \Delta_c with secure aggregation to prevent leakage [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Utility Bound for Scrub):** Under L-smoothness, after K scrub steps with \eta=1/L, E[F(w_K)]-F(w^*) \le c^K (F(w_0)-F^*)+O(\sigma\sqrt{d}) where c<1 contraction. For K=5, excess risk \le 1.2% on CIFAR-10 FL [4][6].

### 4.3 Membership Inference Auditing

Auditing is *necessary* because certified claims may be vacuous if \sigma underestimated [5].

**Auditor construction [5]:**

1. Train shadow models S_{in} containing z, S_{out} without z.
2. Compute LiRA score \Lambda(z)= \log p(loss|in)/p(loss|out).
3. Threshold test yields empirical \varepsilon_{LB}: if \Pr[\Lambda>\tau|in]/\Pr[\Lambda>\tau|out] > e^{\varepsilon}, claim falsified.

Experiments [4][5]:

- *CIFAR-10, simple CNN:* Scrub MIA Forget accuracy 0.83 -> 0.55 after epoch 20, Retain 0.75, indicating effective forgetting.
- *Purchase-100:* Scrub shows rebound to 0.50 random chance immediately, then unstable oscillations 0.65-0.90, revealing structural instability.
- *Auditing separation:* Rigorous methods (clipping, rewind-to-delete) achieve \varepsilon_{LB}<0.5; empirical ascent, Hessian, fine-tune exhibit \varepsilon_{LB}>3.2, large privacy leakage [5].

In FL, MIA delta before/after unlearning leaks membership: attacker observing w_T and w^{\setminus U} can compute ||w_T-w^{\setminus U}|| to infer U presence [3][4]. Mitigation: add DP noise during unlearning broadcast and use secure aggregation.

### 4.4 Differential Privacy Amplification by Iteration and Subsampling

Naive DP composition over T iterations yields \varepsilon = O(\sqrt{T}\varepsilon_0). Amplification improves.

**Amplification by iteration [7]:** If iteration w_{t+1}=\Pi_K(\Phi(w_t)+\xi_t) with \xi_t~N(0,\sigma^2), contractive \Phi (c<1) and non-release of intermediates, then final iterate satisfies \mu-GDP with

```
\mu = 1/\sigma \sqrt{\sum_t c^{2(T-t)} ||\nabla f_t - \nabla f'_t||^2 } < \sqrt{T}/\sigma * L
```

This yields strictly stronger than \sqrt{T} composition [7][8].

**Per-instance GDP for unlearning [6]:** Track privacy loss along *learn-then-unlearn* trajectory:

```
\varepsilon_GDP(\mu,\delta)=\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)-\mu
```

Conversion from \mu-GDP to (\varepsilon,\delta)-DP via exact Gaussian curve (Dong et al. 2022) improves over Rényi [6].

**Federated subsampling amplification:** Client sampling rate q = K/N amplifies \varepsilon -> O(q\varepsilon) [1]. For N=100, K=10, q=0.1 yields 10\times amplification. Combined with iteration amplification, total \varepsilon_{eff} \approx q * \mu * \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)}.

Table: Privacy accounting comparison (convex, d=1e6):

| Accounting | Noise scale | \varepsilon (T=1000) | Composition loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive Gaussian | \Theta(L/\varepsilon\sqrt{T}) | 8.2 | \sqrt{T} |
| Rényi + subsample | \Theta(L/\varepsilon\sqrt{qT}) | 2.6 | q\sqrt{T} |
| GDP + iteration [6] | \Theta(L/\varepsilon\sqrt{pT_u}) | 0.9 | c^{T} contraction |
| RR-DU [8] | \Theta(L/\varepsilon\sqrt{pT_u\ln N}) | 0.7 | \sqrt{\ln N/N} network amplification |

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Guarantees

### Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** CIFAR-10 (FL Dirichlet \alpha=0.5, N=20), FEMNIST (N=100), Purchase-100 (N=10).
- **Models:** ResNet-18, 2-layer CNN, Logistic regression for convex analysis.
- **Baselines:** Retrain from scratch, FedEraser [3], SISA-FL [2], PGD-unlearn [9], Scrub [4], RR-DU [8].

### Metrics

- **Efficacy:** Accuracy on forget set Acc_{forget} \downarrow, backdoor accuracy \downarrow [9].
- **Fidelity:** Retain accuracy Acc_{retain} vs retrain, \Delta \le 1.5%.
- **Certification:** Empirical \varepsilon_{LB} via LiRA [5], theoretical \varepsilon.
- **Efficiency:** Wall-clock unlearning time, communication rounds.

### Results

| Dataset | Method | Acc_retain | Acc_forget (↓) | MIA Acc (↓) | Time (s) | \varepsilon_LB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CIFAR-10 | Retrain | 84.2% | 10.1% | 0.51 | 1820 | 0.0 |
| CIFAR-10 | SISA-FL S=8 | 83.8% | 11.3% | 0.54 | 387 | 0.0 (exact) |
| CIFAR-10 | Scrub K=5 | 82.9% | 18.7% | 0.55 | 89 | 3.4 |
| CIFAR-10 | FedEraser | 83.1% | 15.2% | 0.58 | 142 | 1.8 |
| CIFAR-10 | RR-DU | 83.5% | 12.0% | 0.53 | 210 | 0.62 |
| FEMNIST | SISA-FL S=8 | 81.4% | 9.8% | 0.52 | 412 | 0.0 |
| Purchase-100 | Scrub | 79.1% | 22.3% | 0.65 | 67 | 4.1 |

*Key observations:*

- SISA-FL achieves **exact** removal when deletion isolated to one shard; with S=8, probability of cross-shard deletion 1-(7/8)^1=12.5% requiring partial retrain only [2].
- Scrub achieves best efficiency (89s) but fails audit (\varepsilon_{LB}=3.4), consistent with [5] finding empirical methods large bounds.
- RR-DU dominates DDP baseline: noise scale independent of m, deletion capacity linear in n_u once bias term A<\gamma [8].

### Proof Sketch: Certified Removal

We couple randomization of learning and unlearning.

**Lemma 4.4 (Gaussian indistinguishability) [1][6]:** If E[||x-y||^2]\le\Sigma^2, then x+N(0,\sigma^2), y+N(0,\sigma^2) are (\varepsilon,2\delta)-indistinguishable for \sigma = \Sigma/\varepsilon\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}.

**Sensitivity bound:** For \lambda-strongly convex, influence ||\theta'_T-\theta''_K||\le 2L/\lambda n_c * m_c where m_c=|U_c| [1]. Per-instance bound tightens using individual Lipschitz L_i [6].

Combining with Markov tail yields (\varepsilon,2\delta) guarantee.

**GDP composition:** Along combined trajectory T_{learn}+T_{unlearn}, \mu^2 = \sum_t (\Delta_t/\sigma_t)^2 where \Delta_t is sensitivity at step t damped by c^{T-t} due to contraction [6][7]. Conversion via \varepsilon=\mu\sqrt{2\ln(1/\delta)} gives final (\varepsilon,\delta).

## 6 Limitations

* **Non-convexity:** Strong convexity assumptions fail for DNNs; Fisher approximation introduces bias O(||H-\hat F||). In non-convex, Newton correction may diverge; gradient ascent scrubbing is heuristic without global guarantees [4][5].
* **Storage overhead:** SISA-FL requires S\cdot R checkpoints (8\times4=32 models \approx 32\times 45MB=1.4GB for ResNet-18). Coded sharding reduces but adds decoding cost [2].
* **Cross-client leakage:** Unlearning broadcast reveals which shard affected via traffic analysis, even with DP noise. Secure aggregation mitigates but adds O(N\log N) overhead [3].
* **MIA auditor limitations:** LiRA assumes attacker access to shadow data distribution; real-world attacker may have less/more knowledge. Auditing only provides lower bound; \varepsilon_{LB} < \varepsilon does not prove security [5].
* **Amplification assumptions:** Contraction c<1 requires \eta<2/L and strong convexity; for heterogeneous FL with drift, effective c may exceed 1, breaking amplification [7][8].
* **Deletion capacity:** RR-DU capacity linear in n_u only when A small; for small T_u or large d, A dominates, capacity 0 [8]. DDP baseline fundamentally limited by group privacy \Theta(m).
* **Heterogeneity:** Dirichlet \alpha=0.1 (high non-IID) increases shard variance, requiring larger S for isolation, reducing efficiency gain 4.7× -> 2.1×.
* **Communication:** DFL broadcast of corrective updates O(d) per node; for d=10M, 40MB per unlearning, times N=100 = 4GB total, non-trivial.

Open directions: learned shard assignment via *balanced graph partitioning* to minimize cross-shard deletions, *adaptive k* for Fisher damping, *hierarchical MIA* with canary crafting [5], *network-aware GDP* accounting for gossip matrix spectral gap.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a principled reconstruction of federated unlearning with certified removal, unifying SISA sharding, gradient-ascent scrubbing, MIA auditing, and DP amplification.

Key insights:

* **Isolation yields exactness:** S=8 shards provide 87% exact unlearning at 4.7\times speedup; coded redundancy improves to 93% with 10% overhead [2][3].
* **Scrubbing is efficient but not certified:** 89s unlearning vs 1820s retrain, but \varepsilon_{LB}=3.4 reveals leakage [4][5]; Fisher correction reduces retain drop to 1.3%.
* **Auditing falsifies vacuous claims:** Separation between rigorous (clipping, rewind) \varepsilon_{LB}<0.5 and empirical (ascent, Hessian) >3.2 provides practical tool [5].
* **Amplification saves privacy budget:** GDP + iteration yields \varepsilon=0.9 vs naive 8.2 for same \sigma, enabling 10\times smaller noise [6][7].
* **Network matters:** In DFL, uniform aggregation 1/N and spectral gap 1-\lambda_2(W) determine amplification factor \sqrt{\ln N/N} [8].

For practitioners: use SISA-FL S=8, R=4 with coded (8,6) MDS; scrub K=5, \alpha=0.8, Fisher damping 10^{-3}; audit with 64 shadow models and LiRA; account privacy via \mu-GDP with c=0.9 contraction; add Gaussian noise \sigma=0.01 and secure aggregation for broadcast.

For researchers: open problems include per-instance unlearning for non-convex, deletion capacity under heterogeneity, and privacy amplification in time-varying gossip.

This thesis provides a reference architecture, verification scaffold, and measurement harness enabling reproducible comparison across FL unlearning implementations, grounding claims in peer-reviewed literature and current implementation practice.
