# Verifiable Delay Functions and Proof-of-Sequential Work: Wesolowski VDFs in Class Groups, Pietrzak Halving Protocol, and Time-Lock Puzzles from RSA Squaring with Transparent Setups

## Abstract
Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) formalize sequential hardness with succinct public verifiability, bridging proof-of-sequential-work (PoSW) and time-lock puzzles into a primitive with exponential evaluation-verification gap. This thesis synthesizes the two dominant algebraic VDF families—Wesolowski's single-element proof via adaptive-root decomposition and Pietrzak's logarithmic-size halving recursion—over groups of unknown order, particularly RSA groups and class groups of imaginary quadratic fields. We reconstruct security from the iterated squaring assumption for $y = g^{2^T}$ in unknown-order groups, formalize Fiat-Shamir compiled non-interactivity, and contrast trusted RSA setup versus transparent class-group setup with discriminant $\Delta < 0$. We prove correctness and soundness reductions under low-order and adaptive-root assumptions, analyze proof sizes ($1$ vs $O(\log T)$ elements) and verification ($2$ exponentiations vs $O(\log T)$), and evaluate transparent instantiations for randomness beacons, Chia consensus, and post-RSA agility. Dense technical treatment includes protocol specifications, attack considerations, parameter selection at 128-bit security, and open problems in sustainable trusted setup elimination.

## 1 Introduction

A **verifiable delay function** (VDF) is a function $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ requiring $T$ *inherently sequential* steps to evaluate, yet admitting public verification in time $\mathsf{poly}(\lambda,\log T)$, where $\lambda$ is the security parameter [1][4]. Introduced formally by Boneh, Bonneau, Bünz and Fisch in 2018 [4], VDFs distill decades of intuition from **time-lock puzzles** [5] and **proofs of sequential work** (PoSW) [6] into a single unique-output primitive suitable for decentralized randomness, leader election, and replication proofs.

The central difficulty is simultaneous:

* *Sequentiality*: No adversary with $\mathsf{poly}(T)$ processors in the Parallel RAM model can evaluate $f$ significantly faster than $T$ sequential steps.
* *Efficient verifiability*: Verification must be exponentially faster than evaluation.
* *Uniqueness / Soundness*: For each input $x$, there exists at most one acceptable $y$; forging incorrect $y'$ should be infeasible.

> Theorem: VDF Soundness - Let $G$ be a group of unknown order satisfying the adaptive-root assumption. For $g \leftarrow G$, $T$ delay, $\ell \leftarrow \mathsf{Primes}(2\lambda)$ derived via random oracle $H(g,y)$, and $\pi = g^{\lfloor 2^T / \ell \rfloor}$, if $\mathsf{Verify}(g,y,\pi)=1$ with $y \neq g^{2^T}$, then one can extract an $\ell$-th root of a random element, contradicting AR.

Prior attempts failed at conjunction. Iterated hashing $H^T(x)$ is plausibly sequential in the ROM but not efficiently verifiable without SNARKs, incurring heavy prover cost. Rivest-Shamir-Wagner (RSW) time-lock puzzles $y=x^{2^T}\bmod N$ obtain sequentiality from repeated squaring modulo an RSA composite [5], but lack public verifiability because only holder of $\phi(N)$ can quickly check exponent correctness.

Wesolowski [1] and Pietrzak [2] independently closed this gap in 2018 by constructing succinct proofs of correct exponentiation. Both treat the same algebraic delay function: starting from $g\in G$, compute $g^{2^T}$ via $T$ squarings. The verification problem *“prove $y=g^{2^T}$ without recomputing $T$ steps”* reduces to a **proof of exponentiation**. Remarkably, output size depends only on group, verification poly-logarithmic in $T$, while evaluation remains inherently sequential under the **RSW / iterated squaring assumption** [1][2][3].

This thesis makes four contributions:

1. Unifying formalism for VDFs, trapdoor VDFs, and PoSW.
2. Dense exposition of **Wesolowski** single-element proof: prime challenge $\ell \leftarrow H(g,y)$, quotient $q = \lfloor 2^T / \ell \rfloor$, remainder $r$, proof $\pi = g^q$, check $y = \pi^\ell g^r$ [1].
3. Detailed halving protocol analysis for **Pietrzak**: recursive statements $(x,y,T) \to (x_1,y_1,T/2)$ with random $r$, $x_1 = x^r \mu$, proof size $\log T$ [2][3].
4. Evaluation of **transparent setups** via class groups $Cl(\Delta)$ of imaginary quadratic orders where order unknown to anyone, eliminating RSA trusted deletion of primes $p,q$. We discuss discriminant generation, security bounds, and performance trade-offs.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Groups of Unknown Order

Both RSA groups $(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^*$ with $N=pq$ and class groups $Cl(\Delta)$ serve as instantiations [1][4].

* **RSA group**: order $\phi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)$ is hidden if $p,q$ discarded. Element representation compact, operations fast in hardware. *Requirement*: trusted setup to discard prime factors or via MPC ceremony (used by Chia, Filecoin, Ethereum). If factorization leaked, delay collapses: adversary computes $e = 2^T \bmod \phi(N)$, then $y = g^e$ in $O(\log T)$.
* **Class group $Cl(\Delta)$**: Let $\Delta<0$, $\Delta\equiv 1 \pmod 4$ fundamental discriminant. Binary quadratic forms $(a,b,c)$ with $b^2-4ac=\Delta$, modulo $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ action, form finite abelian group with unknown order approximating $\sqrt{|\Delta|}$. No trapdoor: even generator of setup cannot compute $|Cl(\Delta)|$ efficiently; transparent setup: $\Delta \leftarrow H(\mathsf{seed})$. Recent cryptanalysis indicates $| \Delta |\ge 1827$ bits for 128-bit security due to sub-exponential index calculus $L_{|\Delta|}[1/2,1]$; some deployments use 2048-3072 bits.

Security rests on:

* **Low-Order Assumption**: No efficient adversary finds $\mu \neq 1$, small $d$ with $\mu^d=1$.
* **Adaptive Root Assumption**: Given random $w$, adversary cannot produce $\ell$-th root for random prime $\ell$.
* **Iterated Squaring / RSW Assumption**: Computing $g^{2^T}$ requires $\Theta(T)$ sequential squarings.

### 2.2 VDF Syntax

Following Boneh et al. [4]:

* $\mathsf{Setup}(\lambda,T) \to pp=(ek,vk)$: public parameters. For RSA: $N$, $g$; for class group: $\Delta$, $g\in Cl(\Delta)$.
* $\mathsf{Eval}(pp,x) \to (y,\pi)$: computes $y = x^{2^T}$, proof $\pi$.
* $\mathsf{Verify}(pp,x,y,\pi) \in \{0,1\}$: accepts iff $y$ correct.

Properties: *Correctness*, *Soundness* (computational or statistical), *Sequentiality* $(p,\sigma)$-sequential for adversary time $\sigma(T)$ with $p(T)$ processors.

### 2.3 Fiat-Shamir and Random Oracle

Interactive public-coin protocols compiled via $c := H(x,y,\text{transcript})$ to non-interactive in ROM. Both Wesolowski and Pietrzak use challenges from $H$ to break prover ability to precompute.

Table 1 summarizes asymptotics.

| Construction | Group | Proof size | Verify cost | Prover extra | Setup | Assumption |
|--------------|-------|------------|-------------|--------------|-------|------------|
| RSW puzzle | RSA | - | - | - | trusted | factoring |
| Wesolowski | RSA / $Cl$ | 1 $\mathbb{G}$ | 1 exp $O(\lambda)$ | 1 div $\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor$ | transparent if $Cl$ | adaptive root |
| Pietrzak halving | $QR^+_N$ / $Cl$ | $\log T$ $\mathbb{G}$ | $O(\log T)$ exps | $O(T)$ optimized | transparent if $Cl$ | low-order |
| PoSW Mahmoody et al. | RO | $O(\log T)$ | $O(\log T)$ | $O(T)$ | none | ROM |

Another distinction is implementation complexity:

* Wesolowski requires **hash-to-prime** $\ell \in \mathsf{Primes}(2\lambda)$ with Miller-Rabin, subtle pitfalls if $\ell$ small.
* Pietrzak requires **signed quadratic residues** $QR^+_N$ to eliminate $-1$ order-2 attack, requiring strong primes $p=2p'+1$.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

We study VDF $f_T(g)=g^{2^T}$ over unknown-order group $G$, $g$ uniformly from $\mathcal{X}$ [1][2][4]. Delay parameter $T$ up to $2^{40}$ for Chia ($\approx$ 10 min to hour of squaring on optimized hardware), $T$ up to $2^{32}$ for Ethereum randomness beacon.

### 3.1 Trapdoor VDF Concept

Wesolowski introduces **trapdoor VDF** [1]: With trapdoor $\phi(N)$ or class number $h(\Delta)$, evaluation efficient; without, sequential. If setup process unknown even to generator (class group random discriminant), we obtain plain VDF. Construction:

- $y = g^{2^T}$
- Decompose exponent $2^T = q\ell + r$, $\ell = H(g,y)$, $0\le r < \ell$.
- Proof $\pi=g^q$.
- Verification: Check $y \stackrel{?}{=} \pi^\ell \cdot g^r$.

Since $\ell \approx 2\lambda$ prime (~128-256 bits), exponentiation $\pi^\ell$ requires $O(\lambda)$ multiplications, *independent* of $T$. Soundness follows: If adversary can forge $y' \neq y$ accepted, then extract $\ell$-th root of $g$ or find low-order element, contradicting adaptive-root / low-order [3].

### 3.2 Halving Formalism

Pietrzak protocol proves language $\mathcal{L}_T=\{(x,y): y=x^{2^T}\}$ recursively.

Given $T$ even (power of two), prover sends $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$. Verifier sends $r \leftarrow [\![1..2^\lambda]\!]$. Both compute:

$$x' = x^r \cdot \mu$$
$$y' = \mu^r \cdot y$$

Claim reduces to $y' = (x')^{2^{T/2}}$. Recursive $\log T$ times until $T=1$, final direct squaring check. Fiat-Shamir: $r = H(x,y,\mu,T)$. Each round $O(1)$ group elements; total proof $\{\mu_i\}_{i=1}^{\log T}$. Soundness statistical in group where $x$ honestly in $QR$, else requires *signed quadratic residues* $QR^+_N$ with strong primes to avoid low-order attack [2][3].

Both protocols admit optimization: use exponent splitting and windowing to produce proof essentially for free relative to $T$ squarings if using division algorithm with $O(T \log T)$? In practice, computing all $\pi_i$ costs about $O(T)$ muls extra, verified measurements show proof generation $8000\times$ faster than evaluation for $T=2^{20}$ [2].

Key algebraic identity underlying both:

*Wesolowski*: $g^{2^T} = (g^{\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor})^{\ell} \cdot g^{2^T \bmod \ell}$
*Pietrzak*: $(x^r\mu)^{2^{T/2}} = (x^{2^{T/2}})^r y$ if $y=x^{2^T}$ and $\mu=x^{2^{T/2}}$.

### 4.1 Wesolowski VDF in Class Groups

Wesolowski construction elegantly achieves ***optimal succinctness***: single group element proof [1]. Over class group $Cl(\Delta)$:

* **Setup**: Generate negative prime discriminant $\Delta = -p$ where $p$ prime, $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $p = \mathsf{HashToPrime}(\mathsf{seed})$. No prime generation mirroring RSA; transparency achieved because nobody knows class number. Security analysis requires $|\Delta| \approx 2048$ bits for 128-bit security due to Hafner-McCurley attack $L_{1/2}$. Composition of binary quadratic forms uses NUCOMP / NUDUPL: given forms $(a_1,b_1,c_1),(a_2,b_2,c_2)$, compute $\gcd$, compose, reduce to reduced form where $|b|\le a\le c$, $b\ge0$ iff $a=c$. Squaring dominance means we use optimized `nudupl`.

* **Eval**: Sequential $T$ squarings $y=g^{2^T}$, then compute $\ell=H(g,y)$ prime, $q=\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor$, $\pi=g^q$.

```rust
// Rust sketch of Wesolowski class group Eval
struct ClForm { a: BigInt, b: BigInt, c: BigInt, delta: BigInt }

impl ClForm {
    fn nudupl(&self) -> Self { /* optimized self-composition */ todo!() }
    fn nucomp(&self, other: &Self) -> Self { todo!() }
    fn pow(&self, exp: &BigInt) -> Self { /* binary exponentiation */ todo!() }
}

fn wesolowski_eval(g: ClForm, t: u64) -> (ClForm, ClForm) {
    let mut y = g.clone();
    for _ in 0..t { y = y.nudupl(); } // sequential T
    let l = hash_to_prime(&g, &y); // 256-bit prime
    let (q, r) = divmod_pow2(t, &l);
    let pi = g.pow(&q); // divide-and-conquer using saved intermediates
    (y, pi)
}

fn wesolowski_verify(g: ClForm, y: ClForm, pi: ClForm, t: u64) -> bool {
    let l = hash_to_prime(&g, &y);
    let r = mod_pow2(t, &l);
    let lhs = y;
    let rhs = pi.pow(&l).nucomp(&g.pow(&r));
    lhs.a == rhs.a && lhs.b == rhs.b
}
```

For $g^q$ where $q = \lfloor 2^T / \ell \rfloor$ huge (size $\approx 2^T$), naive exponentiation infeasible. Wesolowski observes prover already has intermediate powers $g^{2^i}$ for $i=0..T-1$ (saved during evaluation). Efficient computation uses divide-and-conquer: iteratively produce $q$ binary expansion. Cost about $O(T)$ group ops again, acceptable because $T$ dominates.

* **Verification**: $g$ public, check prime $\ell$, recompute $r=2^T \bmod \ell$ (via repeated square mod $\ell$ in $O(\log T)$), then check $y == \pi^\ell * g^r$. Class group multiplication constant small; verification ~ few ms vs minutes-hour evaluation.

**Advantages in class groups**: no need to trust RSA modulus generator. **Disadvantage**: class group operations ~ $5$–$10\times$ slower than RSA modular multiplication, and form compression nontrivial; proof element size $\approx 3\cdot |\Delta|/2$ bits after compression (~1 KB for 2048-bit $\Delta$). Nevertheless transparent property essential for public randomness beacons where ceremony compromise fatal [4][7].

### 4.2 Pietrzak Halving Protocol

Pietrzak addresses complementary design point: avoid heavy prime hashing and rely on low-order assumption only [2][3]. Protocol transcript size $O(\log T)$ but verification still polylog.

Recursive construction uses Schwartz-Zippel-like random linear combination to preserve correctness:

1. Verifier expects $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$
2. After challenge $r$, new statement $(x',y')$ where $x' = x^r\mu$, $y' = \mu^r y$ and remaining delay $T/2$. Honest prover if $y = x^{2^T}$ and $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$, then $y' = (x')^{2^{T/2}}$.

Soundness: If initial $(x,y)\notin \mathcal{L}_T$, then for random $r$ from large set, reduced $(x',y')\notin \mathcal{L}_{T/2}$ with overwhelming probability unless adversary finds low-order element. Iteration amplifies error exponentially: after $\log T$ rounds, cheating probability $\approx |\text{challenge}|^{-1}$.

Non-interactive version:

```python
def iterated_square(x, steps, N):
    y = x
    for _ in range(steps):
        y = (y * y) % N
    return y

def pietrzak_prove(x, T, N):
    assert T & (T-1) == 0
    pis = []
    y = iterated_square(x, T, N)
    cur_x, cur_y, cur_T = x, y, T
    while cur_T > 1:
        mu = iterated_square(cur_x, cur_T//2, N)
        pis.append(mu)
        r = hash_to_int(cur_x, cur_y, mu, cur_T) % (1<<128)
        cur_x = (pow(cur_x, r, N) * mu) % N
        cur_y = (pow(mu, r, N) * cur_y) % N
        cur_T //= 2
    return y, pis

def pietrzak_verify(x, y, T, pis, N):
    cur_x, cur_y, cur_T = x, y, T
    for mu in pis:
        r = hash_to_int(cur_x, cur_y, mu, cur_T) % (1<<128)
        cur_x = (pow(cur_x, r, N) * mu) % N
        cur_y = (pow(mu, r, N) * cur_y) % N
        cur_T //= 2
    return pow(cur_x, 2, N) == cur_y
```

Actual secure implementation uses subgroup $QR^+_N$ as $x = |\cdot|$: maps elements to signed quadratic residues to eliminate element $-1$ order 2 causing trivial attack. Requires $p,q$ strong primes.

Proof size for $T=2^{40}$, $N=2048$-bit: $\approx 40 \times 256\text{ bytes}=10$ KB (fits in block header). Verification $\approx 3$ RSA exponentiations dominating due to optimization: verifier only verifies $\log T - \delta$ rounds and squares final $2^{\delta}$ times directly; $\delta$ trade-off calldata vs compute gas cost critical for Ethereum smart contract implementation.

Comparison:

- *Wesolowski*: proof 1 element (~256 B RSA, ~1 KB class group), verify $O(\lambda)$ muls, prover extra $O(T)$ heavy division, assumption adaptive root (stronger).
- *Pietrzak*: proof $\log T$ elements (~10 KB), verify $O(\log T)$ exps, prover extra parallelizable, assumption low-order (weaker), statistically sound.

### 4.3 Time-Lock Puzzles RSA Squaring

Origin point: Rivest-Shamir-Wagner time-lock puzzle [5]. Construction:

- Generate RSA modulus $N=pq$, discard private.
- Message $m$, symmetric key $k$.
- Compute $t = T$ squarings, $N$, $x_0$, $W(0)=x_0$, $W(i+1)=W(i)^2 \bmod N$.
- Cipher: $K = W(T) + k$ concealed? Actually encrypt $k$ via $W(T)$, send $C = W(T) + k \bmod ...$ Standard formulation: puzzle is $(N, x_0, T, C = K + m)$ where $K = \text{PRF}(W(T))$.

Sequentiality intuition: Without factorization, computing $x_0^{2^T}\bmod N$ appears to require $T$ squarings; each squaring depends on previous result, not parallelizable due to mod multiplication data dependency. **Factorization shortcut**: Knowing $\phi(N)$, compute $e=2^T \bmod \phi(N)$, then $y=x_0^e$ via fast exponentiation $O(\log e)$. Security unproven under standard factoring but widely conjectured *sequential squaring assumption* [4][5]. Mahmoody-Moran-Vadhan proved time-lock puzzles impossible in ROM alone, requiring algebraic structure [6].

Relation to VDF: RSW puzzle lacked public verifiability: observer given $(N,x,y,T)$ cannot distinguish $y$ correct without either redoing $T$ squarings or knowing $\phi(N)$. *VDF transforms puzzle into publicly verifiable proof*.

Thus VDF = *publicly verifiable time-lock puzzle with deterministic unique output*. Time-lock encryption can still hide message: encrypt $m$ under $y$ derived key, revealing after $T$ squarings.

RLC-type puzzles edge: Hardware speed: RSA squaring minimal latency is multiplication mod $N$ ($2048$ bits) $\approx 5-50$ ns on ASIC, but Moore's law considered in LCS35 puzzle $t=796512...$ designed for 35-year delay [5]. Modern estimates for $A_{\max}=10$ adversary CPU clock advantage [4].

### 4.4 Transparent Setups & Applications

Trusted vs transparent setup is *deployment dividing line*.

**RSA trusted setup** drawbacks:

- Ceremony to generate $N=pq$ where nobody learns $(p,q)$. Multi-party RSA key generation requires at least one honest participant; if all collude, VDF breaks silently.
- Requires $2048$-$4096$-bit modulus for long-term security (NIST recommends $3072$ for 2030+).
- Quantum vulnerability: Shor breaks factoring, rendering delay instant to quantum adversary. Class groups also quantum-broken via Shor hidden subgroup, but parameters differ; neither post-quantum secure.

**Class group transparent setup** benefits:

- Sample negative discriminant $\Delta = -p$ with $p$ prime from public randomness; no secret. Order $h(\Delta) = O(\sqrt{|\Delta|})$ unknown; no trapdoor even to generator. Verified transparent.
- Implementation issues: Need reliable class group library. Must check $\gcd(a,\Delta)=1$.

**Applications**:

1. **Randomness beacon** [4]: $R_{i+1}= H_i( \text{VDF}( H(R_i)))$: VDF adds delay preventing last-revealer grinding outcomes. Ethereum's `RandDAO` replacement, Drand.
2. **Chia Proof-of-Space-Time** [2]: Chia uses Pietrzak VDF over $QR_{2048}$ with $T$ tuning to enforce spacetime; farmers must compute VDF sequentially after proof-of-space to extend chain.
3. **Resource-efficient blockchains**: Replace PoW energy waste with sequential time resource.
4. **Timestamping & Fair ordering**: Prevent front-running by requiring VDF delay before decryption of mempool transactions.

Table: Deployment examples.

| Project | VDF Type | Group | T | Proof Size | Verify on-chain |
|---------|----------|-------|---|------------|-----------------|
| Chia | Pietrzak | RSA 2048 QR+ | $2^{20..30}$ | 10KB | off-chain timelord |
| Ethereum beacon | Wesolowski | RSA 2048 / ClassGroup 2048 | $2^{28}$ | 32-256B | EIP-198 modexp ~500k gas |
| Drand LoE | Wesolowski | ClassGroup 2048 | $2^{20}$ | 128B | BLS agg + VDF |
| Filecoin PoRep | Pietrzak | RSA 2048 | $2^{22}$ | 2KB | SNARK wrapped |

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs / Analysis

### 5.1 Soundness Reductions

*Wesolowski* soundness reduces to adaptive root [1][3]. Suppose adversary $\mathcal{A}$ produces tuple $(g,y,\pi)$ accepted with $y\neq g^{2^T}$. Extract from two accepting transcripts with distinct challenges $\ell \neq \ell'$ (forking lemma) two equations $y = \pi^\ell g^r = {\pi'}^{\ell'} g^{r'}$. With $\gcd(\ell,\ell')=1$, using extended Euclidean find $\alpha,\beta$ s.t. $\alpha\ell+\beta\ell'=1$, derive $\ell$-th root of $g$, contradicting AR. Concrete bound: soundness error $\le O(q_H / 2^{\lambda})$.

*Pietrzak* statistical soundness holds in group of order unknown $> 2^\lambda$ except for low-order element $v$ with $v^d=1$, $d$ small. If adversary uses invalid $\mu$, then equation fails except with probability $1/|challenge|$ [2][3]. After $\log T$ rounds error $\le (1+\log T)/2^\lambda$ negligible.

### 5.2 Evaluation Parameters

On modern CPU, single 2048-bit RSA squaring $\approx 2 \mu s$, but class group NUDUPL $\approx 20 \mu s$. For $T=2^{40}$, naive CPU evaluation $\approx 20$ days (RSA) to months (class group). Dedicated ASIC squaring pipeline using Montgomery multiplication reduces latency to ~1-5 ns per squaring? Recent TCHES hardware study [7] reports 3.6x speedup over C++ baseline using redundant representation for class group squaring, achieving 9.1x vs CPU.

*Proof size & verification*:

- Wesolowski RSA: 256 bytes proof, verification 2 exponentiations.
- Wesolowski Cl: $\approx 1024$ bytes compressed, verification $\approx 3$ ms.
- Pietrzak RSA: $\approx \log_2 T = 40$ elements $\times 256$ B = 10 KB, verification 3 exponentiations with optimization.

### 5.3 Sequentiality Evidence

No proof from factoring alone; sequentiality *conjectured*. Evidence from allowing preprocessing $L$ generated by $A_0$ with unbounded power but limited parallel time $A_1$ cannot accelerate. Malicious acceleration via parallelism would require finding shortcuts to compute $g^{2^T}$ faster than $T$ squarings, which would also accelerate time-lock puzzle solving, contradicting 25 years cryptanalysis of RSW.

### 5.4 Formal properties checklist

1. **Completeness**: honest $\mathsf{Eval}$ yields $y=g^{2^T}$, $\pi=g^{\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor}$, verifier accepts due to Euclidean division identity $2^T = \ell q + r$.
2. **Soundness**: as above.
3. **Sequentiality**: $t$-sequential function iterated $g(x)=x^2$ yields $(t\cdot T)$-sequential $f_T$.
4. **Transparency**: class group setup coin-tossing protocol yields no trapdoor.

## 6 Limitations & Future Work

**Limitations**:

- **Parameter size uncertainty**: Class group security not standardized; doubling of required discriminant from 1665 to ~2048 bits due to Dobson et al. attack [3][7] increased proof sizes.
- **Quantum insecurity**: Both RSA and class group instances broken by Shor.
- **Hardware disparity**: Sequentiality claim depends on adversary not having $A_{\max}\gg 10$ over honest. Custom ASIC pipeline could centralize VDF evaluation.
- **Non-parallel proof generation**: Wesolowski proof requires storing intermediates or recomputing $q$ naive $O(T\log N)$ additional work.
- **Fiat-Shamir fragility**: ROM heuristic; instantiation with SHA-256 may introduce subtle grinding.

**Future directions**:

1. *Post-quantum transparent VDF*: explore MinRoot sequential permutation over $\mathbb{F}_p$ used by Ethereum VDF contest, proof via algebraic intermediate representation.
2. *Aggregatable / continuous VDFs (cVDFs)*: intermediate $g^{2^i}$ all verifiable continuously from single proof, useful for outsourcing VDF across shards.
3. *Transparent aggregate RSA setup*: using class groups of conductors or MPC for RSA with verifiable deletion.
4. *Optimized class group arithmetic*: Redundant representation and relaxed reduction strategies [7] promising low-latency hardware pipeline.
5. *Standardization and bias elimination*: define deterministic form compression, canonical discriminant generation via hash-to-prime.
6. *Proof-of-replication enhancements*: integrate VDF with proof-of-space-time for long-term data archiving.

## 7 Conclusion

Verifiable Delay Functions transform the venerable notion of sequential squaring from RSW time-lock puzzles into a primitive fit for trust-minimized distributed systems. The two leading instantiations—Wesolowski's single-element proof leveraging the adaptive-root assumption and Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol resting on the weaker low-order assumption—provide complementary points in the design space of *succinctness versus assumption strength*. When instantiated over class groups of imaginary quadratic orders, both achieve **transparent setup**, removing the last single point of trust that would otherwise undermine public randomness beacons and permissionless consensus.

Technically, both VDFs embody same insight: exponentiation structure $2^T = q\ell + r$ or halving random linear combination embeds verifiable proximity without revealing order. Implementations today power Chia's $>400$ PB network and Ethereum's emerging RANDAO beacon, with proof sizes from $256$ B to $10$ KB and verification in milliseconds—exponentially faster than the $T\approx 2^{30..40}$ sequential squarings that define evaluation.

Nevertheless, fundamental open problems remain about provable sequentiality from standard assumptions, post-quantum replacements, and hardware egalitarianism. Transparent class group instantiations, while ideal in model, suffer from conservative parameter growth and slower group operations; ongoing hardware acceleration work is essential to keep evaluation decentralized.

In synthesis, VDFs illustrate *cryptography leveraging time itself as a quantifiable computational resource*: not throughput, not parallelism, but the irreversible ordering of squaring operations becomes a building block.
