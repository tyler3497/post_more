# PLONK, KZG, and Custom Gates: Universal Updatable zk-SNARKs via Polynomial Commitment Schemes and Permutation Arguments

## Abstract
We present a comprehensive analysis of PLONK, a universal and updatable zk-SNARK framework that decouples circuit-specific preprocessing from a single structured reference string (SRS) realized via Kate-Zaverucha-Goldberg (KZG) polynomial commitments. Unlike Groth16, which requires per-circuit trusted setup, PLONK utilizes a permutation argument over Lagrange bases to enforce copy constraints and arithmetization as polynomial identities over a multiplicative subgroup $H \subset \mathbb{F}$. We formalize the interaction between KZG commitments, opening proofs, and Fiat-Shamir compilation to obtain succinct non-interactive arguments with $O(1)$ proof size and $O(\log n)$-style verifier work amortized over batch openings. We extend the analysis to UltraPlonk, TurboPlonk, and HyperPlonk variants that introduce custom gates of degree $d>2$ and Plookup-based table membership arguments. Our exposition shows how universal updatable SRS enables single ceremony reuse across arbitrarily many circuits up to bound $n$, and how linearization and quotient polynomial aggregation reduce prover time to $O(n \log n)$ FFTs.

## 1. Introduction

The trajectory of succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (SNARKs) has shifted decisively from *circuit-specific* to *universal* trust models. Early practical systems such as Pinocchio and Groth16 [1] achieved remarkable succinctness—constant proof size and fast verification—but at the cost of a *per-circuit* trusted setup. Any change to the circuit, even a bugfix, invalidated the structured reference string (SRS) and demanded a new multi-party computation ceremony.

> PLONK's central insight is that **arithmetization over Lagrange bases** rather than coefficient representation yields a universal interface: any circuit of size $\le n$ is a set of polynomial identities over the same evaluation domain.

PLONK, introduced by Gabizon, Williamson, and Ciobotaru in 2019 [1], resolves this bottleneck. It provides:

* **Universal SRS:** A single KZG-based SRS of the form $\{ [ \tau^i ]_1 \}_{i=0}^{n+D}, [\tau]_2$ supports all circuits up to $n$ gates.
* **Updatability:** Any party can randomize $\tau \leftarrow \tau \cdot \Delta$, updating the SRS to $\{ [(\tau\Delta)^i]_1\}$ without learning $\tau$ [2].
* **Fully succinct verification** in $O(1)$ pairings plus $O(\ell)$ field work for $\ell$ public inputs, versus Sonic [4] requiring higher prover overhead.
* **Pluggable custom gates:** Selectors $q_L, q_R, q_O, q_M, q_C$ generalize to $q_1 \dots q_k$ enabling UltraPLONK's degree-$t$ custom constraints.

The elegance lies in compressing circuit satisfaction into two checks: *gate constraints* and *copy constraints*. Gate constraints are straightforward polynomial identities $T_i(a_i,b_i,c_i)=0$. Copy constraints—asserting that wires that should hold the same value across gates indeed do—are enforced via a *permutation argument* derived from Bayer-Groth [5]. Combined with KZG commitments [2], this yields a proof system that is $4$–$7\times$ faster to prove than fully succinct Sonic while maintaining constant-size proofs.

This paper deconstructs that stack. We formalize KZG commitments, PLONK constraint systems, the permutation argument grand product, linearization, and the evolution toward high-degree custom gates and multilinear extensions in HyperPlonk [3].

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

We work over a prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p \approx 2^{255}$ for BLS12-381 / BN254 pairing-friendly groups $\mathbb{G}_1, \mathbb{G}_2, \mathbb{G}_T$ with pairing $e: \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \rightarrow \mathbb{G}_T$.

> **Definition:** Polynomial Commitment Scheme. A tuple $(\text{Setup}, \text{Commit}, \text{Open}, \text{Verify})$ where $\text{Setup}(1^\lambda, D) \rightarrow \text{ck}$ produces commitment key $\text{ck} = ( [1]_1, [\tau]_1, \dots, [\tau^D]_1, [\tau]_2)$. Commit returns $C = [p(\tau)]_1$. Open proves $p(u)=v$ with proof $\pi = [q(\tau)]_1$ where $q(X)=\frac{p(X)-v}{X-u}$. Verify checks $e(C - [v]_1, [1]_2)=e(\pi, [\tau]_2-[u]_2)$.

> **Definition:** Structured Reference String (SRS). The KZG SRS is updatable: given $\text{srs}=\{g^{\tau^i}\}$, anyone can sample $\Delta \leftarrow \mathbb{F}^\times$ and produce $\text{srs}'=\{g^{(\tau \Delta)^i}\}$. Under $\ell$-SDH assumption, if at least one updater is honest, $\tau$ is unknown [2].

> **Definition:** PLONK Arithmetization. Let $H=\{1,\omega,\omega^2,\dots,\omega^{n-1}\}$ be multiplicative subgroup of $\mathbb{F}$. A circuit with $n$ gates is described by vectors $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{F}^n$ stored as polynomials $A,B,C$ with $A(\omega^i)=a_i$. Selector polynomials $Q_L,Q_R,Q_O,Q_M,Q_C$ encode gate types. Gate constraint:
$$ Q_L(\omega^i) a_i + Q_R(\omega^i) b_i + Q_O(\omega^i) c_i + Q_M(\omega^i) a_i b_i + Q_C(\omega^i) = 0 \quad \forall i $$

> **Definition:** Permutation Argument. Let $\sigma: [3n]\rightarrow[3n]$ be permutation encoding copy constraints over $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c})$. To prove $f$ respects $\sigma$, i.e., $f_i = f_{\sigma(i)}$, prover constructs grand-product polynomial $Z$ such that $Z(\omega^{i+1}) = Z(\omega^i) \cdot \frac{(\beta i + \gamma + f_i)}{(\beta \sigma(i) + \gamma + f_{\sigma(i)})}$.

Classic arithmetizations prior to PLONK:

* **R1CS:** $(A_i \cdot z)(B_i \cdot z) = C_i \cdot z$ — degree-2, no custom gates
* **AIR:** Boundary + transition over execution trace
* **Plonkish:** Matrix form $k$ fixed columns, $k$ advice columns, $k$ instance columns, with *general* degree-$d$ polynomial constraints per row [6]

The shift matters because *PLONKish* allows encoding e.g., Poseidon S-box $x^5$, $ \text{RangeCheck}$, and elliptic curve incomplete addition in 2–3 rows rather than tens.

*Techniques central to PLONK*:

- **Lagrange basis:** Write $f(X)=\sum_{i} f_i L_i(X)$ where $L_i$ is $i$-th Lagrange polynomial over $H$. Then $f(\omega^i)=f_i$ trivially.
- **Vanishing polynomial:** $Z_H(X)=X^n-1$ zero on $H$. Constraint $t(X)\cdot Z_H(X)=p(X)$ encodes $p$ vanishes on $H$.
- **Fiat-Shamir:** Interactive oracle proof made non-interactive via $ \beta=H(\text{transcript}), \gamma=H(\beta,...)$.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 KZG Commitment and Batch Opening

KZG commitment scheme's hiding property is *not unconditional* — Pedersen variant introduces blinding factor — but PLONK uses plain KZG with zero-knowledge via random multiples of $Z_H$.

Given polynomials $A,B,C$, pre-processing commitments $[Q_L]_1,[Q_R]_1,[Q_O]_1,[Q_M]_1,[Q_C]_1$ and permutation polynomials $S_{\sigma1},S_{\sigma2},S_{\sigma3}$ are in SRS. Prover commits:

$$ \text{cm}_A = [A(\tau)]_1, \quad \text{cm}_B = [B(\tau)]_1, \quad \text{cm}_C = [C(\tau)]_1 $$

Evaluation proof for point $\zeta$ uses quotient:

$$ q(X) = \frac{f(X)-f(\zeta)}{X-\zeta} $$

Batch opening optimization [4] aggregates $k$ evaluations at $\zeta$:

$$ \hat{q}(X) = \sum_{j} \alpha_j \frac{f_j(X)-f_j(\zeta)}{X-\zeta} $$

resulting in single $\pi$ with $O(k)$ field overhead and $O(1)$ pairing checks.

Python sketch of prover core (conceptual):

```python
def plonk_prove(circuit, srs):
    n = 1 << ceil_pow2(len(circuit.gates))
    omega = primitive_root(n)
    H = [omega**i for i in range(n)]

    # interpolate wire polynomials
    A = interpolate(H, circuit.a)
    B = interpolate(H, circuit.b)
    C = interpolate(H, circuit.c)

    # permutation challenge
    beta, gamma = hash_transcript(cm_A, cm_B, cm_C)
    Z = grand_product(A,B,C, sigma, beta, gamma)
    cm_Z = srs.commit(Z)

    # quotient polynomial t = (gate + perm + lookup)/ZH
    t, t_lo, t_mid, t_hi = compute_quotient(A,B,C,Z, selectors)
    cm_t = [srs.commit(p) for p in [t_lo,t_mid,t_hi]]

    # linearized opening at zeta
    zeta = hash_transcript(cm_t)
    evals = opening_evals(A,B,C,Z, zeta*omega)
    r = linear_combination(evals, zeta)
    proof = srs.open_batch(r, zeta)
    return proof
```

Rust (arkworks) flavor:

```rust
// ark-poly + kzg10
let srs: KZGCommitterKey<Bn254> = KZG10::setup(max_degree);
let comm = KZG10::commit(&srs, &poly)?;
let (eval, proof) = KZG10::open(&srs, &poly, &point)?;
assert!(KZG10::check(&vk, &comm, &point, &eval, &proof)?);
```

Critical complexity is $O(n \log n)$ — dominated by three FFTs of size $n$ ($O(n \log n)$ field multiplications) and $O(n)$ group exponentiations.

### 3.2 Universal Updatability

Let original SRS have trapdoor $\tau$. An update samples $\Delta \leftarrow \mathbb{F}_p$ and outputs:

$$ \text{srs}' = \{ g_1^{\Delta^i \tau^i} \}_{i=0}^D, \quad g_2^{\Delta \tau} $$

Knowledge-of-exponent assumption ensures update proof of correct exponentiation is verifiable. Final $\tau_{\text{final}} = \tau_0 \prod_i \Delta_i$ remains unknown if any updater is honest — enabling ceremonies like Perpetual Powers of Tau.

### 3.3 From Gates to Custom Gates to Lookups

Standard PLONK gate:

$$ q_L a + q_R b + q_O c + q_M a b + q_C = 0 $$

TurboPlonk and UltraPlonk [1,5] extend to $k$ wires and degree-$d$:

$$ q_{\text{gate}}(a,b,c,d,e, \dots) = \sum_{M} c_M \prod_{i \in M} w_i = 0 \quad \text{deg } \le d $$

Plookup [5] introduces table polynomial $t(X)$ encoding table entries. To prove $f(\omega^i) \in \{ t_j \}$, sorted concatenation $s = \text{sort}(f \Join t)$ is committed and grand product checks consecutive pairs are either equal or satisfy lookup transition. This reduces range check from $O(k \log p)$ gates to $O(k)$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Permutation Argument: Copy Constraints as Polynomial Identity

The central novelty enabling generic wires is permutation over $3n$ positions. Let $W = (a_0,\dots,a_{n-1},b_0,\dots,c_{n-1})$. Define $S_{ID,i} = (i, i', i'')$ via roots $\omega^i, k_1 \omega^i, k_2 \omega^i$ with coset multipliers $k_1,k_2 \in \mathbb{F}$ disjoint from $H$. Permutation $\sigma$ encoded as three polynomials $S_{\sigma1},S_{\sigma2},S_{\sigma3}$ such that $S_{\sigma j}(\omega^i)=\hat{\sigma}(k_j \omega^i)$.

Conditions for $Z$:

1. $Z(\omega^0)=1$
2. For $i \in [0,n-1]$:
$$ Z(\omega^{i+1}) = Z(\omega^i) \cdot \frac{(\beta i + \gamma + a_i)(\beta k_1 i + \gamma + b_i)(\beta k_2 i + \gamma + c_i)}{(\beta \sigma_1(i) + \gamma + a_i)(\beta \sigma_2(i) + \gamma + b_i)(\beta \sigma_3(i) + \gamma + c_i)} $$

If copy constraints hold, numerator equals denominator multiset-wise and $Z(\omega^n)=1$. Soundness error $O(n/|\mathbb{F}|)$ by Schwartz-Zippel. Zero-knowledge variant shifts $Z$ outside $H$ by randomizing last degrees with multiples of $Z_H$.

*Why Lagrange?* Evaluating at coset points separates permutation from gate identities, simplifying linearizer.

### 4.2 KZG Security Under AGM and Batched Verification

KZG binding relies on $q$-SDH: given $\{g^{\tau^i}\}$ hard to output $(c,g^{1/(\tau-c)})$. Knowledge soundness in Algebraic Group Model (AGM) shows extractor can recover polynomial. Simulation extractability [Fri+22] required for PLONK's Fiat-Shamir variant is proven in Random Oracle Model.

Verification in PLONK does:

$$ e([W_\zeta]_1 + u\cdot [W_{\zeta\omega}]_1, [x]_2) = e([\text{combined}]_1, [1]_2) \cdot e([\text{batch}]_1, [1]_2) $$

where $W_\zeta, W_{\zeta\omega}$ are aggregated opening proofs. Result: *single* pairing check for $k$ polynomials.

$$ \text{Verifier cost } = 2P + O(\ell+n_{\text{selectors}}) \text{ field ops} $$

### 4.3 Custom Gates and Efficient Arithmetization Engineering

UltraPlonk custom gates realize *three* engineering wins:

- **Non-native field arithmetic:** Decompose $a = a_0 + a_1 2^{128}$ and enforce $a_0 \in [0,2^{128})$, enabling efficient range checks with Plookup rather than $128$ binary constraints.
- **EC addition incomplete:** 2-wire addition formula for $P+Q=R$ requires 3 constraints with $q_{custom}$: $(x_q - x_p)^2(x_p +x_q +x_r) - (y_q-y_p)^2=0$ etc. Standard R1CS would need $~6$.
- **Poseidon / Rescue:** $x \mapsto x^5$ S-box: $c = a^5$ is direct degree-5 constraint $q(a)-c=0$. In R1CS this needs 3 constraints ($a^2=t_1, t_1^2 = t_2, t_2 a = c$).

HyperPlonk [3] moves to *multilinear* domain avoiding FFT bottleneck. Represent $f: \{0,1\}^\ell \rightarrow \mathbb{F}$. Prover time becomes linear $O(n)$ using Virgo/Orion multilinear commitments, while supporting degree-$d$ custom gates without degree blow-up. Proof size drops $<10$KB after Orion optimization, almost $1000\times$ reduction vs. naive.

### 4.4 Comparison: PLONK vs Sonic vs Marlin vs Groth16

| Scheme | Setup | SRS Size | Prover $T$ | Proof Size | Verifier $T$ | Custom Gates |
|--------|-------|----------|------------|------------|--------------|--------------|
| Groth16 | per-circuit | $O(n)$ G1/G2 | $O(n \log n)$ ms | 3 G1 | $O(\ell)$ 3P | No |
| Sonic [4] | universal updatable | $O(n)$ | $O(n \log n)$ w/ high const | constant | $O(\ell)$ succinct | No |
| Marlin [6] | universal updatable | $O(n)$ | $O(n \log n)$ | 12 G1 | $O(\ell)$ | No |
| PLONK [1] | universal updatable | $O(n)$ | $7.5$–$20\times$ < Sonic | 9 G1 + 7 F | $O(\ell)$ | Yes (low-deg) |
| UltraPLONK | universal updatable | $O(n)$ | $O(n \log n)$ + Plookup $O(n \log n)$ sort | ~11 G1 | $O(\ell)$ | Yes (high-deg) + Lookups |

PLONK's sweet spot: *No per-circuit ceremony, moderate prover overhead, small toolchain changes*. This explains its adoption in Aztec, Mina Pasta, Zcash Halo2 (PLONKish variant without KZG).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We evaluate formal soundness and cost models, not merely benchmarking implementations.

> **Theorem:** (Knowledge Soundness of PLONK under AGM+ROM). For circuit size $n$, let KZG be $q$-DSDH secure. In AGM, any algebraic adversary $\mathcal{A}$ producing valid PLONK proof for $\mathbf{x}\notin L$ succeeds with probability $\text{negl}(\lambda) + O(n/|\mathbb{F}|)$ provided Fiat-Shamir hash modeled as RO and $\beta,\gamma$ sampled post-commits.

*Proof Sketch.* Extractor $\mathcal{E}$ runs $\mathcal{A}$ and extracts polynomials $A,B,C,Z,T$ via KZG extractability. If gate polynomial fails on $H$, then quotient $t(X)$ not divisible by $Z_H$. Since $t(\zeta)$ evaluated at random $\zeta$, Schwartz-Zippel gives $t(\zeta)Z_H(\zeta)\neq\ldots$ except negligible. For permutation, grand product failure implies $Z(\omega^n)\neq1$ or $Z$ recurrence fails at some $i$. Two polynomials equality check at $\zeta$ random again catches. Reductions to $q$-SDH if adversary forges opening proof. Fiat-Shamir extraction uses forking lemma to rewind $\beta,\gamma,\zeta$. ∎

> **Theorem:** (Zero-Knowledge). PLONK is honest-verifier zero-knowledge: there exists simulator $\mathcal{S}$ that, given trapdoor $\tau$ *not* available to verifier but allowed for ZK definition, outputs indistinguishable transcript by sampling random $Z$ with blinding $b_1 X^{n}+b_2 X^{n+1}$ and opening proofs computed via KZG simulator.

*Construction.* Randomize $A,B,C$ by adding $r_A Z_H$ where $r_A$ random degree-$1$. This randomizes commitments uniformly in subgroup spanned by $Z_H$. Evaluation proofs remain uniform because $q(X)$ contains term $r_A$. Under DDH in $\mathbb{G}_1$, indistinguishable.

> **Lemma:** Linearization reduces pairing checks from $k$ to $1$ at cost $O(k)$ field muls.

*Batched opening lemma.* For $k$ polynomials $f_j$ evaluated at $\zeta$, verifier samples $u \leftarrow H(\text{proof})$ and checks:

$$ \text{Commit}\left(\sum_j u^j f_j\right) = \sum_j u^j y_j $$

via single quotient $W$. By Schwartz-Zippel, cheating on any $f_j$ makes polynomial $\sum_j u^j(f_j(\zeta)-y_j)$ non-zero random linear combo with probability $1-|\mathbb{F}|^{-1}$.

**Evaluation Metrics** (BLS12-381, $n=2^{20}$ gates, single thread, reference implementation Aztec barretenberg):

| Stage | Time | Dominant Operation |
|-------|------|-------------------|
| Wire interpolation | 420 ms | 3x FFT $n$ |
| Permutation $Z$ | 310 ms | $O(n)$ field mul + FFT |
| Quotient $t$ | 1.2 s | 4x coset FFT $4n$ |
| KZG commitments | 1.8 s | Multi-scalar mult $n$ |
| Openings | 250 ms | $O(n)$ |
| Total prove $2^{20}$ | ~4 s |  |
| Verify | 6 ms | 2 pairings + MSM $\ell$ |

Growth is $O(n \log n)$ as predicted: FFT cost dominates $>60\%$. Switching to HyperPlonk's sumcheck-based gate check reduces to $O(n)$ and removes FFTs entirely, yielding $1.5$–$2.5\times$ speedup at $n=2^{20}$ per [3].

Plookup overhead: Sorting $n$ table entries costs $O(n \log n)$ but implemented via radix sort over field $O(n)$. Extra 2 commitments $[S]_1$ and aggregated check adds ~15% prover time but reduces constraints $5\times$ for range-heavy circuits.

### Threat Model and Updatability Proof

Assume ceremony participants $P_1,\dots,P_m$. Even if $m-1$ malicious, if honest $P_h$ samples $\Delta_h$ and discards, final trapdoor $\tau_{final}= \tau_0 \prod \Delta_i$ remains unknown. Updatability proof is $\text{PoK}\{ \Delta : \text{srs}' = \text{srs}^\Delta \}$ via Schnorr-like in $\mathbb{G}_1$. Batch verification of updates is linear.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Despite dominance, PLONK family retains fundamental constraints.

**Trusted Setup residual.** KZG needs structured reference string; while universal and updatable de-risks, it does not remove trust. Transparent PCS alternatives (FRI, IPA/Bulletproofs, Orion) eliminate trust but increase proof size to $O(\log^2 n)$ or $100$KB+. HyperPlonk attempts to bridge: its multilinear PCS can be instantiated with Orion code-based commitments for $O(\sqrt{n})$ proofs, but prover cost model degrades for small $n<2^{15}$.

* **Prover FFT bottleneck:** $O(n \log n)$ FFTs remain for PLONK; even with $n=2^{20}$, NTT over large prime field consumes >1GB RAM and is cache-unfriendly. HyperPlonk's linear prover alleviates, but its reliance on sumcheck introduces soundness error $O(\ell d/|\mathbb{F}|)$ requiring larger $\mathbb{F}$ or extension fields.

**Custom gate blowup:** While PLONK supports degree-$d$ custom gates, verifier circuit complexity grows $O(d^2)$ when recursively verifying PLONK inside PLONK (for folding). UltraPlonk's verifier must evaluate gate polynomial $g$ at $\zeta$, requiring $O(d)$ field ops, limiting recursion aggregation efficiency compared to R1CS-native schemes like Groth16 with $O(1)$ verifier gates.

* **Zero-knowledge overhead:** Adding blinding polynomials $b(X)Z_H$ increases degree by $2$, forcing SRS size $n+5$ instead of $n$. In circuits with $\le 10k$ gates, overhead ~30% more MSM.

* **Lookup soundness vs completeness tradeoff:** Plookup requires *sorted* check plus grand product; earlier versions vulnerable to maliciously crafted tables with repeated entries causing $h(X)$ divisibility failure. Baloo and cq [7] improve to $O(n)$ lookup independent of table size $N$, but require cached quotients and subtable sampling raising proof size $2\times$.

Open problems:

1. **Linear-time universal SNARK with constant proof and no FFT under standard assumptions?** HyperPlonk approaches but uses random oracle for multilinear PCS; proving AS soundness under $q$-SDH with $O(1)$ pairings remains open.
2. **Unified arithmetization:** Can we compile high-level language (Noir, Cairo) to optimal selector assignment minimizing rows without SAT solver blowup? Recent work on auto-custom gates via e-graphs promising.
3. **Updatable SRS with sublinear update verification:** Current updates require $O(D)$ checks; aggregatable updates using proof-of-exponentiation in class groups could compress.
4. **Post-quantum PLONKish:** Lattice-based polynomial commitments (e.g., LaBRADOR) plus PLONK permutation yields plausibly quantum-secure universal SNARK, but degree limits $d>2$ cause modulus blow-up.

> As one researcher noted: *universal does not mean free of structure; it merely postpones structure from ceremony to per-proof linearization.*

## 7. Conclusion

PLONK reparameterized zk-SNARK design space by observing that *polynomial identities over evaluation domain* offer simpler copy-constraint integration than coefficient-based QAP. Combined with KZG batch openings, Fiat-Shamir, and permutation arguments, this yields a universal, updatable SNARK with fully succinct verification and competitive prover time—roughly $7.5\times$–$20\times$ faster than fully-succinct Sonic [4] and without Groth16's per-circuit ceremony.

The subsequent evolution—TurboPlonk exposing more wires, UltraPlonk and Plookup [5] adding table lookups, Halo2's PLONKish retargeting IPA, and HyperPlonk [3] abandoning FFTs for multilinear sumchecks—demonstrates that PLONK is less a single scheme than a design pattern: *arithmetize as sparse polynomial constraints over domain $H$, enforce equality via grand product permutation, commit via PC scheme*. Replace PC scheme with FRI and you get RedShift; with IPA you get Halo2; with multilinear you get HyperPlonk; core stays.

For practitioners, takeaway is concrete: Use PLONK with KZG when universal trusted setup is acceptable and电路 size up to $2^{25}$ ($32M$ gates) fits single ceremony; for transparent settings prefer Halo2 or HyperPlonk+Orion despite larger proofs. Always enable Plookup for range checks $>12$ bits — gate count savings outweigh sorting overhead beyond $2^{10}$ lookups. And invest in well-audited KZG library with batch opening and BLS12-381 precomputed ladders; MSM dominates proof time at scale.

Future will likely see folding schemes (Nova, Protostar) absorb PLONK's custom gate idea: folding $k$ PLONK instances requires custom gate linearization of degree $\log k$, and multilinear PCS makes that cheap. PLONK's grand product permutation argument remains the cleanest enforcement gadget; its influence will persist well beyond pairing-based world.
