# Homotopy Canonicity and the Sconing Construction: Establishing Computational Content for Univalence, Path Induction, and Higher Inductive Types in Cubical Type Theory

## Abstract

Voevodsky's univalence axiom identifies equivalent types, but postulated univalence blocks computation: closed terms may fail to reduce to canonical form, destroying the canonicity property on which dependent type theory's computational interpretation rests. This thesis systematizes the cubical resolution of this crisis. We present the interval-object construction of path types, the Glue-type proof that makes univalence a theorem, and the sconing (Artin gluing) construction that establishes canonicity — Huber's theorem that every closed natural reduces to a numeral — and its weakening, *homotopy canonicity*, for cubical theories with non-canonical composition equations. We analyze higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules for path constructors, contrast de Morgan and Cartesian cube categories, survey normalization results, and evaluate the residual coherence problems: strict computation for higher constructors, the limits of current proof assistants, and the two-level type theory agenda for internalizing model-theoretic reasoning.

---

## 1 Introduction

In 2006 Vladimir Voevodsky proposed the *univalence axiom*: for a universe $\mathcal{U}$, the canonical map $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$ is itself an equivalence [5]. Univalence identifies equivalent types and makes formalized mathematics invariant under equivalence — a profound foundational achievement. Yet it came at a steep price. Martin-Löf type theory owes its status as a programming language as well as a logic to the *canonicity property*: every closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ is judgmentally equal to a numeral $\mathrm{suc}^n(0)$. Postulating univalence as an axiom introduces a constant with no reduction behavior; a closed term $t : \mathbb{N}$ that invokes `ua` can become *stuck*, never computing to a numeral. Canonicity is lost, and with it the constructive meaning of the theory.

Cubical type theory, introduced by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg [1], resolves this tension by replacing the identity type with a *path type* defined over a formal interval object, and by proving univalence as a theorem via *Glue types* rather than postulating it. The theory regains canonicity: Huber proved that every closed natural in cubical type theory evaluates to a numeral [2]. When some equations governing the composition (Kan filling) operation are dropped — yielding so-called *opaque* cubical theories — full canonicity may fail, but a weaker property survives: *homotopy canonicity*, i.e., every closed $n : \mathbb{N}$ is *path-equal* to a numeral [7]. The uniform proof method behind both results is *sconing*, a proof-relevant computability-predicate construction in the tradition of Gödel, adapted to presheaf models.

**Contributions of this thesis.** We (i) give a unified exposition of path induction in the cubical setting and its derivation from interval primitives; (ii) reconstruct the Glue-type proof of univalence as a theorem; (iii) analyze the schema for higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules for path constructors [3] and its model-categorical semantics [4]; (iv) present the sconing construction and both canonicity theorems in a common framework; (v) compare de Morgan and Cartesian cubical architectures; and (vi) identify the open coherence and implementation problems that remain.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Identity types and path induction

In Martin-Löf type theory, the identity type $\mathrm{Id}_A(x, y)$ is generated by reflexivity $\mathrm{refl} : \mathrm{Id}_A(x, x)$ together with the *path induction* (or $J$) eliminator:

> **Theorem (Path induction):** Given $C : \prod_{x,y:A} \mathrm{Id}_A(x,y) \to \mathcal{U}$ and $d : \prod_{x:A} C(x, x, \mathrm{refl}_x)$, there is $J(C, d) : \prod_{x,y:A} \prod_{p:\mathrm{Id}_A(x,y)} C(x,y,p)$ with the computation rule $J(C,d,x,x,\mathrm{refl}_x) \equiv d(x)$.

All of homotopy type theory — transport, function extensionality (from univalence), the structure identity principle — flows from $J$. The problem is purely *computational*: nothing in the rules of $J$ tells us how an application of the postulated univalence constant `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B)` should reduce, so transport along `ua e` is stuck [5].

### 2.2 Univalence and higher inductive types

Univalence asserts $\mathrm{ua} : (A \simeq B) \to (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B)$ is an equivalence, with $\mathrm{transport}^P(\mathrm{ua}(e))$ computing as the equivalence $e$ — *propositionally* in Book HoTT, since `ua` has no reduction rules. Higher inductive types (HITs) extend inductive types with *path constructors*: e.g., the circle $\mathbb{S}^1$ is generated by $\mathrm{base} : \mathbb{S}^1$ and $\mathrm{loop} : \mathrm{base} = \mathrm{base}$ [5]. Their general syntax and semantics in higher-categorical models were developed by Lumsdaine and Shulman via *cell monads with parameters*, giving weakly stable typal initial algebras in suitable model categories, including simplicial sets [4]. In Book HoTT, HIT path constructors again enjoy only propositional computation rules — a second source of stuck terms.

### 2.3 The cubical idea

Cubical type theory internalizes the *interval*: a pretype $\mathbb{I}$ with endpoints $0, 1 : \mathbb{I}$ and (in the de Morgan variant) operations $\land, \lor, \lnot$ making it a de Morgan algebra. A path in $A$ is a map $p : \mathbb{I} \to A$ with $p(0) \equiv a$, $p(1) \equiv b$. Function extensionality becomes directly provable, and — via Glue types — univalence becomes a theorem with genuine computational behavior [1]. The semantics lives in presheaf toposes over a category of cubes, where each type carries *composition structure* (a constructive Kan filling operation), making the fibrant types a model of univalent universes.

| Feature | Book HoTT (axiomatic) | Cubical type theory |
|---|---|---|
| Univalence | Postulated axiom | Theorem via `Glue` |
| Function extensionality | Derived from univalence | Direct from path types |
| Canonicity for $\mathbb{N}$ | **Fails** (stuck `ua` terms) | **Holds** (Huber [2]) |
| HIT path computation | Propositional | Judgmental (schema [3]) |
| Identity type | Primitive $\mathrm{Id}$ | Derived from `Path` (or primitive `Id` [7]) |
| Semantics | Simplicial sets / $\infty$-groupoids | Cubical sets with composition |

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *comparative metatheory*: we reconstruct the proof architecture common to the canonicity results rather than re-verifying every lemma, and we evaluate each component against the criterion of computational content. Concretely:

1. **Syntactic analysis.** We track the interval, face lattice $\mathbb{F}$, composition `comp`, and `Glue` primitives of de Morgan cubical type theory [1], and compare with Cartesian variants where diagonals $i \mapsto (i, i)$ are primitive but reversals $i \mapsto 1 - i$ are absent.
2. **Model reconstruction.** We present the presheaf model over the cube category $\mathcal{C}$ (objects: finite sets of names; morphisms: de Morgan algebra homomorphisms), and the internal-language formulation of Kan filling via partial elements [1].
3. **Sconing as proof method.** We give the Artin-gluing (sconing) construction uniformly for canonicity and homotopy canonicity [2, 7], making explicit where the two proofs diverge: fibrant vs. non-fibrant computability families, and whether composition's definitional equations are included.
4. **Source grounding.** Every major claim is anchored to the primary literature [1–8], with emphasis on the published statements of the canonicity theorems and the HIT schema.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The interval, path types, and generalized path induction

The de Morgan interval equips $\mathbb{I}$ with $\land, \lor$ (min/max) and involution $\lnot$. The *face lattice* $\mathbb{F}$ is obtained by quotienting $\mathbb{I}$ by $x \land \lnot x = 0$. A *path type* $\mathrm{Path}\;A\;a\;b$ classifies maps $p : \mathbb{I} \to A$ with prescribed endpoints:

```agda
-- Cubical Agda: path abstraction and application
refl : {A : Type} {a : A} → Path A a a
refl {a = a} i = a

J : {A : Type} (C : (x y : A) → Path A x y → Type)
  → ((x : A) → C x x refl)
  → (x y : A) (p : Path A x y) → C x y p
J C d x y p = transport (λ i → C x (p i) (λ j → p (i ∧ j))) (d x)
```

Path induction is *derivable*: given $p : \mathrm{Path}\;A\;x\;y$, the term $\lambda j.\, p(i \land j)$ is a path from $\mathrm{refl}_x$ to $p$ lying over the motive — the classic cubical proof that singletons are contractible [1]. Function extensionality is immediate: $\lambda i.\, f(i)\;x$ is a path between pointwise-equal functions.

> **Theorem (Contractibility of singletons):** For $a : A$, the type $\sum_{x:A} \mathrm{Path}\;A\;a\;x$ is contractible, with center $(a, \mathrm{refl})$.

This single fact yields $J$, transport, and all of the identity-type infrastructure — computationally, because every step is a term former with reduction rules.

### 4.2 Glue types: univalence as a theorem

The *Glue* construction turns an equivalence into a path between types. Given $A : \mathcal{U}$, a cofibration $\varphi : \mathbb{F}$, a partial family $T : [\varphi] \to \mathcal{U}$, and a partial equivalence $e : [\varphi] \to \mathrm{Equiv}(T, A)$, the type $\mathrm{Glue}\;[\varphi \mapsto (T, e)]\;A$ agrees with $T$ on $\varphi$ and with $A$ elsewhere. Univalence is then *defined*:

```
ua : {A B : U} → Equiv A B → Path U A B
ua {A} {B} e i = Glue B [ (i = 0) ↦ (A , e)
                          , (i = 1) ↦ (B , idEquiv B) ]

uaβ : {A B : U} (e : Equiv A B) (x : A)
    → Path B (transport (ua e) x) (equivFun e x)
uaβ e x i = transpFill ...  -- holds definitionally in cubicaltt
```

Because `Glue` reduces when the cofibration holds ($i = 0$ gives $A$, $i = 1$ gives $B$), `transport (ua e)` computes — the stuck term of Book HoTT is unstuck [1]. This is the precise sense in which cubical type theory gives univalence *computational content*.

| Aspect | de Morgan cubes [1] | Cartesian cubes |
|---|---|---|
| Interval algebra | $\land, \lor, \lnot$ (de Morgan) | Finite products only; diagonals primitive |
| Reversal $1 - i$ | Primitive | Derived/absent ("no reversals" theories) |
| Connection $i \land j$ | Primitive | Not available |
| Proof of `uaβ` | Judgmental | Judgmental (via Glue) |
| Canonicity status | Proved [2] | Proved for cartesian variants |

### 4.3 Higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules

Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg extended cubical type theory with a schema for HITs — spheres, tori, suspensions, propositional truncations, pushouts — in which *all* constructors, including path constructors, enjoy **judgmental** computation rules [3]. The semantics is given constructively in a presheaf topos with cubical structure, and universes are closed under the new formers. Contrast this with the model-categorical semantics of Lumsdaine–Shulman [4], where HITs are weakly stable typal initial algebras for cell monads — a beautiful semantic account that yields only propositional computation rules at the syntactic level.

```agda
-- The circle as a cubical HIT (judgmental loop computation)
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : Path S¹ base base

-- Eliminator computes on `loop i` by the schema of [3]:
-- elim (loop i) ≡ l i   definitionally, where l : Path P b b
S¹-rec : (P : S¹ → Type) (b : P base) (l : PathP (λ i → P (loop i)) b b)
       → (x : S¹) → P x
```

The cubical HIT schema works by giving each higher constructor as a *composition* of cubes, so the eliminator's behavior on path constructors is forced by the Kan operations — no extra axioms are needed. This resolves the second great source of stuck terms in Book HoTT.

> **Theorem (HIT schema soundness [3]):** The presheaf model over de Morgan cubes validates the syntax for spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts with judgmental computation rules for all constructors, and universes are closed under these formers.

### 4.4 Sconing: proof-relevant computability predicates

The canonicity proofs all follow one scheme. Given a model $\mathcal{M}$ of cubical type theory, one builds a *displayed* higher-order model $\mathcal{M}^\bullet$ over it, assigning to each closed type $A$ a *computability family* $A^\bullet : |A| \to \mathcal{U}$, proof-relevantly. This is the sconing (Artin gluing) construction, going back to Gödel's computability predicates. The *scone* $\mathbf{Scone}_{\mathcal{M}^\bullet} \to \mathcal{M}$ comes with a section $\llbracket - \rrbracket$ by initiality of the syntax; applying it to a closed $n : \mathbb{N}$ yields a witness in $\mathbb{N}^\bullet(n)$, from which a numeral is extracted.

The critical design decision is the *fibrancy* of the computability families [7]:

- **Canonicity [2]:** $\mathbb{N}^\bullet(n)$ is defined by recursion as a *non-fibrant* family — a predicate $P_n$ with $P_0 = \{0\}$, $P_{\mathrm{suc}\,k} = \{\mathrm{suc}\,k\}$ — plus a separate component tracking computability of *composition*. Huber's operational semantics is typed and deterministic, adapted to the presheaf setting.
- **Homotopy canonicity [7]:** $\mathbb{N}^\bullet(n)$ must be a *fibrant* family over $|\mathbb{N}|$, since the ambient theory retains paths but the computability structure must cohere with them.

```haskell
-- Sketch of the sconing data (proof-relevant computability)
data Scone (M : Model) : Model where
  Ty*   : (A : Ty M Γ) → (Γ* : Ctx* Γ) → Set
  Tm*   : (A* : Ty* A Γ*) (t : Tm M Γ A) (ρ : Γ*) (ρ* : Γ* ρ)
          → A* ρ ρ* (eval t ρ) → Set
  -- closure under comp/hcomp recorded in the fibrancy component
```

### 4.5 From canonicity to homotopy canonicity

Cubical type theory's composition operation involves *non-canonical choices* — the Kan filling for a type is defined by induction on the type, and several definitional equations for it are convenient but inessential. Coquand, Huber, and Sattler considered the *opaque* cubical type theory obtained by removing these equations [7]. Full canonicity is then out of reach, but sconing still yields:

> **Theorem (Homotopy canonicity [7]):** In the cubical type theory without the equations for the path-lifting/composition operation, every closed term $n : \mathbb{N}$ is *path-equal* to a numeral: there exists $k$ and $p : \mathrm{Path}\;\mathbb{N}\;n\;\bar{k}$.

The proof extends the sconing construction with fibrant computability families indexed over closed terms, using Swan's construction of identity types in presheaf models to handle the $\mathrm{Id}$ former. Recent work pushes this further: strict Rezk completions of models of HoTT are used to establish homotopy canonicity for the *initial* model, internalizing the argument in higher-categorical language [8].

A complementary application of the same circle of ideas is the internal theory of *modalities* — reflective subuniverses and lex modalities constructed via localization HITs, with their semantics developed inside HoTT itself [6].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 The canonicity theorems, precisely stated

> **Theorem (Canonicity for cubical type theory [2]):** Any closed term $t : \mathbb{N}$ in cubical type theory is judgmentally equal to a numeral $\mathrm{suc}^n(0)$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

> **Theorem (Homotopy canonicity [7]):** Any closed term $t : \mathbb{N}$ in opaque cubical type theory is propositionally (path-) equal to a numeral.

These are *metatheorems* about the formal system, proved by the sconing construction. Their practical corollary: proof assistants implementing cubical type theory — `cubicaltt`, Cubical Agda, `cooltt`/`redtt` — can evaluate closed programs that use univalence and HITs, something impossible in any implementation of Book HoTT with axiomatic univalence.

### 5.2 Normalization and decidability

Canonicity is the closed-term shadow of *normalization*: every term has a normal form, which implies decidability of judgmental equality and hence decidable type checking. The literature establishes:

| Result | System | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Canonicity | de Morgan cubical TT | Proved [2] |
| Homotopy canonicity | Opaque cubical TT | Proved [7] |
| Normalization | Cartesian cubical TT | Proved (Sterling–Angiuli) |
| Univalence as theorem | All cubical variants | Proved [1] |
| Judgmental HIT computation | de Morgan cubical TT + schema | Proved [3] |
| Decidable type checking | Cubical Agda (core fragment) | Implemented, partial for HITs |

### 5.3 A computational experiment: transporting along `ua`

The following TLA+-style specification captures the *observable* computational behavior that distinguishes cubical univalence from axiomatic univalence — the reduction of transport along a Glue-built path:

```tla
---------------- MODULE CubicalTransport ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES term, steps

(* Axiomatic HoTT: transport along ua is stuck *)
Stuck(t) == \E e \in Equiv : t = "transport(ua(" \o ToString(e) \o "), x)"

(* Cubical: Glue reduces at endpoints, so transport computes *)
ReduceGlue ==
  /\ term = [ctor |-> "Glue", phi |-> F, fam |-> T]
  /\ IF F = "(i=0)" THEN term' = T["base"]
     ELSE IF F = "(i=1)" THEN term' = T["apex"]
     ELSE term' = term
  /\ steps' = steps + 1

Progress == <>[](~\E t : Stuck(t))
==========================================================
```

In words: where Book HoTT's `transport (ua e) x` is a normal stuck form, the cubical term reduces through the Glue faces to `e x` — *canonicity in action* [1, 2].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Coherence of higher constructors.** The cubical HIT schema [3] covers an important but restricted class (spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, pushouts). Fully general HITs — with recursive path constructors and higher coherences — remain without a complete syntactic schema with judgmental rules; the semantic account [4] is more general but gives only weak stability.
2. **Non-canonical composition equations.** Homotopy canonicity [7] is strictly weaker than canonicity, and the gap matters: programs in opaque cubical theories compute only up to paths. Whether the removed equations can be restored coherently at all types, including universes, is open.
3. **Cube-category trade-offs.** De Morgan cubes give connections and reversals but complicate the structural rules; Cartesian cubes avoid reversals at the cost of a more elaborate composition operation. No single cube category dominates on all axes (simplicity of metatheory vs. simplicity of syntax).
4. **Implementation gaps.** Cubical Agda implements a large fragment but not the full HIT schema with judgmental path computation; `cubicaltt` is a research prototype. Users of HITs in practice still frequently rely on postulated rewrite rules.
5. **Internal model theory.** Reasoning *about* models of HoTT *inside* type theory requires two-level type theory (a strict exo-level alongside the fibrant level); the sconing arguments [2, 7, 8] are still largely external. Making canonicity proofs internal is an active program.
6. **Univalence vs. strict equality.** Cubical type theory validates univalence but refutes uniqueness of identity proofs *definitionally* only in the fibrant fragment; the interaction of strict equality (as in two-level theories) with Glue remains delicate.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The computational crisis of univalent foundations — that Voevodsky's axiom, taken as a postulate, destroys canonicity — has a principled resolution. By internalizing the interval, cubical type theory turns univalence from an axiom into a *theorem with computational content* [1]; by sconing, it recovers canonicity [2] and, in the opaque variant, homotopy canonicity [7]; and by the cubical HIT schema, it extends judgmental computation to higher-dimensional constructors [3], grounded in the model-categorical semantics of higher inductive types [4].

The program is not finished. General HIT coherence, the canonicity gap in opaque theories, and the internalization of these metatheorems in two-level type theory define the frontier. But the central lesson stands: *univalence computes* — not by accident, but because the cubical primitives (interval, composition, Glue) were designed so that equivalence, transported along a path of types, reduces. Foundations need not choose between homotopical invariance and computational meaning.
