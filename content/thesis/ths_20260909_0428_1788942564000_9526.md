# Categorical Semantics of Programming Languages: Monads, Adjunctions, and Enriched Categories from Moggi to Initial Algebras

## Abstract

Categorical semantics provides the unifying mathematical framework through which the meaning of programming languages can be defined compositionally and reasoned about rigorously. This thesis develops a comprehensive account of the categorical approach, spanning the foundational trinity of categories, functors, and natural transformations through their application to computational phenomena. We examine Eugenio Moggi's computational metalanguage, which models notions of computation as strong monads and separates values from computations via the Kleisli construction, providing a uniform semantic treatment of effects such as state, exceptions, nondeterminism, and continuations. We analyze adjunctions as the universal source of monads and the engine behind monadicity, enriched categories as the basis for domain-theoretic and linear resource-sensitive semantics, and the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence linking intuitionistic logic, typed lambda calculus, and cartesian closed categories. Finally, we present initial-algebra semantics, from the Goguen school's classical formulation to presheaf models of variable binding, showing how recursion and syntax themselves arise from universal constructions.

---

## 1 Introduction

The meaning of a programming language can be approached operationally (how programs execute), axiomatically (what properties they satisfy), or denotationally (what mathematical objects they denote). Categorical semantics belongs to the denotational tradition, but it elevates the enterprise: each type denotes an *object* and each well-typed term a *morphism* in a category whose structure mirrors the language's own [1]. Compositionality then becomes a theorem rather than a hope — the meaning of a compound phrase is built from the meanings of its parts by composition, pairing, and exponentiation.

Why categories? A programming language is, at heart, an algebra of *typed composition*: programs plug into contexts, functions consume arguments, modules link against interfaces. Category theory is precisely the mathematics of typed composition. Its payoff is threefold. First, **abstraction**: a theorem proved once for categories — the Yoneda lemma, say — applies simultaneously to domains, games, presheaves, and realizability models. Second, **structure identification**: effects, recursion, and binding are instances of universal constructions — *monads*, *adjunctions*, *initial algebras* — which is why the same patterns recur across unrelated languages. Third, **design guidance**: when a feature resists clean categorical description, that resistance is information, as the struggle to give monadic accounts of delimited control and effect handlers shows [6].

Section 2 fixes the vocabulary; Section 3 describes our method. Section 4 treats five pillars: monads and Moggi's metalanguage; adjunctions and monadicity; enriched categories and linear semantics; the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence; and initial-algebra semantics culminating in higher-order abstract syntax. Section 5 proves selected theorems; Sections 6–7 survey limitations and conclude.

## 2 Background

A **category** $\mathbf{C}$ has objects $A, B, \dots$ and morphisms $\mathrm{Hom}(A, B)$ with associative composition and identities $id_A$. Read $f : A \to B$ as a typed process. A **functor** $F : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}$ preserves composition and identities — it models type constructors and semantic translations. A **natural transformation** $\alpha : F \Rightarrow G$ assigns components $\alpha_A : F(A) \to G(A)$ such that every *naturality square* $\alpha_B \circ F(f) = G(f) \circ \alpha_A$ commutes: the formal expression of *parametricity*.

A category has **finite products** when it admits a terminal object $1$ and binary products $A \times B$; it is **cartesian closed** when each $- \times A$ has a right adjoint, giving exponentials $B^A$ with $\mathrm{Hom}(C \times A, B) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(C, B^A)$. CCCs are the semantic home of the simply typed $\lambda$-calculus.

The historical arc runs from Lawvere's functorial semantics (1963) through the Goguen school's initial-algebra treatment of abstract data types [8] and Scott's domain theory, to Moggi's monadic notions of computation [1][2] — popularized for programmers by Wadler [3][4][5] — and the presheaf models of binding of Fiore, Plotkin, and Turi [7]. These threads converge on one thesis: *the structure of a language is best understood as the structure of a category*.

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is synthetic: its method is the systematic application of categorical tools to semantic problems, verified by diagrammatic and equational proof. Four principles govern the work.

1. **Functorial semantics.** Each language fragment is interpreted as a structure-preserving map out of a *syntactic category* (objects are types, morphisms are terms modulo the equational theory) into a *semantic category*. Soundness is functoriality; completeness is fullness or faithfulness of the interpretation.
2. **Universal properties as definitions.** Products, exponentials, and initial algebras are defined by what they *do* — pairing, currying, unique recursion — so the definitions transport across models unchanged.
3. **Diagrammatic reasoning.** Equations between composites are proved by *diagram chasing*: decomposing large commuting diagrams into naturality squares, functoriality steps, and monad laws.
4. **Effect taxonomy via monad catalogs.** Following Moggi [1], effects are classified as instances of one schema — a *strong monad* on a category with finite products — and compared through monad morphisms and distributive laws rather than ad hoc reasoning.

Our "empirical" component (Section 5) consists of proof sketches detailed enough to formalize in a proof assistant; Haskell, Python, Rust, and TLA+ illustrations ground the abstractions in executable artifacts.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Monads, Kleisli Categories, and Moggi's Computational Metalanguage

> **Definition 1 (Monad).** A *monad* on $\mathbf{C}$ is a triple $(T, \eta, \mu)$ with endofunctor $T : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}$ and natural transformations $\eta : \mathrm{Id} \Rightarrow T$ (unit), $\mu : T^2 \Rightarrow T$ (multiplication) satisfying $\mu \circ T\mu = \mu \circ \mu T$ (associativity) and $\mu \circ T\eta = \mathrm{id}_T = \mu \circ \eta T$ (units).

![Figure 1: Monad structure — endofunctor T with unit eta and multiplication mu, and the monad laws as commutative diagrams.](public/thesis/ths_20260909_0428_1788942564000_9526-0.webp)

Moggi's insight: a *notion of computation* — partiality, state, exceptions, nondeterminism, continuations, I/O — is uniformly a monad [1][2]. The base category $\mathbf{C}$ models **values**; $T$ models **computations**. An effectful program $A \to B$ denotes $A \to T(B)$: from an input value, a *computation* of an output. The unit $\eta_A : A \to T(A)$ injects pure values; the multiplication $\mu_A : T(T(A)) \to T(A)$ flattens nested computations, capturing sequential composition of effects.

The standard catalog of computational monads [1]:

| Effect | Monad $T(A)$ | Unit $\eta_A(a)$ | Multiplication intuition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partiality | $A_\bot = A + \{\bot\}$ | $a$ | propagate $\bot$ |
| Exceptions | $A + E$ | $\mathrm{inl}(a)$ | first exception wins |
| Nondeterminism | $\mathcal{P}(A)$ | $\{a\}$ | union of choice sets |
| Global state | $(A \times S)^S$ | $\lambda s.\,(a, s)$ | thread the store |
| Continuations | $R^{(R^A)}$ | $\lambda k.\,k(a)$ | CPS composition |
| Interactive I/O | $\mu X.\, A + (X^I \times O \times X)$ | silent action | interleave actions |

Moggi's **computational $\lambda$-calculus** $\lambda_c$ has two judgments — $\Gamma \vdash_v v : A$ (values) and $\Gamma \vdash_c M : T(A)$ (computations) — plus sequencing $\mathtt{let}\; x \Leftarrow M \;\mathtt{in}\; N$, denoting Kleisli composition [1]. The **Kleisli category** $\mathbf{C}_T$ keeps the objects of $\mathbf{C}$ but takes morphisms $A \to T(B)$, composed as $g \circ_K f = \mu \circ T(g) \circ f$. Kleisli composition is associative *exactly because* of the monad laws (proved in Section 5).

Sequencing a value with a computation needs **strength**: a natural $t_{A,B} : A \times T(B) \to T(A \times B)$ coherent with $\eta$ and $\mu$ [1]. On $\mathbf{Set}$ every monad is canonically strong; on general CCCs strength is an extra axiom, and it is what makes $\lambda_c$ sound.

```haskell
-- Moggi-style metalanguage: the state monad T(A) = (A x S)^S
newtype State s a = State { runState :: s -> (a, s) }
instance Functor (State s) where
  fmap f (State g) = State (\s -> let (a, s') = g s in (f a, s'))
instance Monad (State s) where
  return a = State (\s -> (a, s))                       -- eta
  (State g) >>= k = State (\s -> let (a, s') = g s      -- mu via bind
                                 in runState (k a) s')
```

### 4.2 Adjunctions, Monadicity, and the Algebra of Computational Effects

> **Definition 2 (Adjunction).** $F \dashv G$ with $F : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}$, $G : \mathbf{D} \to \mathbf{C}$ consists of $\eta : \mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{C}} \Rightarrow GF$ (unit) and $\varepsilon : FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{D}}$ (counit) satisfying the **triangle identities** $G\varepsilon \circ \eta G = \mathrm{id}_G$ and $\varepsilon F \circ F\eta = \mathrm{id}_F$.

![Figure 2: An adjunction F ⊣ G with unit and counit, and the triangle identities as commuting diagrams.](public/thesis/ths_20260909_0428_1788942564000_9526-1.webp)

Every adjunction *induces* a monad: $T = GF$ with unit $\eta$ and multiplication $\mu = G\varepsilon F$ satisfies the monad laws by diagram chasing with the triangle identities (Section 5). Conversely, every monad arises from adjunctions in two canonical ways: the **Kleisli adjunction** $\mathbf{C} \rightleftarrows \mathbf{C}_T$ (computations) and the **Eilenberg–Moore adjunction** $\mathbf{C} \rightleftarrows \mathbf{C}^T$ (algebras for the monad — i.e., effect *handlers*). Monads are the "shadows" cast by adjunctions, which is why they pervade semantics.

| Adjunction $F \dashv G$ | Induced monad | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Free $\dashv$ forgetful (monoids) | List / free monoid | syntax from generators |
| $(-\times E) \dashv (-)^E$ | State $(-\times S)^S$ | environments as effects |
| $\Delta \dashv \lim$ (diagrams) | Codensity | Kan-extension effects |
| $F \dashv U$ for Lawvere theories | Finitary term monad | algebraic operations |

**Beck's monadicity theorem** characterizes which adjunctions are Eilenberg–Moore adjunctions: $G$ must create coequalizers of $G$-split pairs and reflect isomorphisms. Plotkin and Power used this machinery to show that *notions of computation determine monads*: from a signature of algebraic effect operations and equations, the monad is reconstructed as the free-algebra construction [6]. This inverts Moggi's direction — postulate operations (e.g., $\mathtt{get}$, $\mathtt{put}$ for state) and *derive* the monad — and underlies algebraic effects, where handlers are precisely Eilenberg–Moore algebras.

```python
# Kleisli composition: associative iff the monad laws hold
def kleisli_compose(mu, T, f, g):
    return lambda a: mu(T(g)(f(a)))   # g o_K f = mu . T(g) . f
```

### 4.3 Enriched Categories and Linear Resource Semantics

Ordinary categories are enriched over $\mathbf{Set}$: each $\mathrm{Hom}(A, B)$ is a set. An **enriched category** takes hom-*objects* in a monoidal category $\mathbf{V}$: we have $\mathbf{C}(A, B) \in \mathbf{V}$, composition $\mathbf{C}(B, C) \otimes \mathbf{C}(A, B) \to \mathbf{C}(A, C)$, and identities $I \to \mathbf{C}(A, A)$ satisfying enriched associativity and unit laws. The choice of $\mathbf{V}$ fixes the *flavor* of semantics:

- $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{Set}$: ordinary categories; classical denotational semantics.
- $\mathbf{V} = \boldsymbol{\omega}\mathbf{CPO}$: hom-objects are $\omega$-cpos, composition continuous — Scott domain theory, where least fixed points interpret recursion.
- $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{Cat}$: 2-categories, the home of rewrites between programs.
- $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{Ab}$: preadditive categories, supporting linear combinations of programs (quantum, probabilistic semantics).

Girard's **linear logic** [9] showed that weakening and contraction are not logical necessities but *resource policies*, modeled by symmetric monoidal closed categories with a comonad $!$ for reusable resources. Benton's linear/non-linear models factor the adjunction between cartesian and linear worlds. Enrichment buys what plain CCCs cannot: a semantic account of *how many times* a resource is used — exactly what strictness analysis, usage types, and quantum no-cloning demand.

```rust
// Enrichment over a resource monoid: morphisms carry usage counts.
struct LinMor<A, B> { run: Box<dyn Fn(A) -> B>, uses: u32 }
fn compose<A, B, C>(f: LinMor<A, B>, g: LinMor<B, C>) -> LinMor<A, C> {
    LinMor { run: Box::new(move |a| (g.run)((f.run)(a))), uses: f.uses + g.uses }
}
```

### 4.4 The Curry–Howard–Lambek Correspondence

The **Curry–Howard correspondence** identifies propositions with types and proofs with programs; **Lambek's** contribution was the third vertex: cartesian closed categories, making the correspondence a *functorial* equivalence between syntax and semantics [10].

| Logic (intuitionistic) | Type theory ($\lambda$-calculus) | Category theory (CCC) |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition $P$ | Type $P$ | Object $P$ |
| Proof of $P \vdash Q$ | Term $x{:}P \vdash t : Q$ | Morphism $f : P \to Q$ |
| Implication $P \supset Q$ | Function type $P \to Q$ | Exponential $Q^P$ |
| Conjunction $P \wedge Q$ | Product type $P \times Q$ | Categorical product |
| Cut / modus ponens | Substitution / application | Composition |
| Proof normalization | $\beta$-reduction | Equality of morphisms |

![Figure 3: The Curry–Howard–Lambek triangle: logic, typed lambda calculus, and cartesian closed categories.](public/thesis/ths_20260909_0428_1788942564000_9526-2.webp)

Formally, the syntactic category $\mathbf{C}_\lambda$ — objects are types, morphisms are terms modulo $\beta\eta$ — is the *free* CCC on the base types; interpretation into any CCC is fixed by the assignment on base types. Substitution lemmas become functoriality; $\beta$-reduction becomes equality of composites. The pattern generalizes: Moggi's $\lambda_c$ corresponds to CCCs with a strong monad [1][2], linear logic to symmetric monoidal closed categories [9].

### 4.5 Initial-Algebra Semantics and Higher-Order Syntax

Goguen, Thatcher, Wagner, and Wright proposed that *syntax* be understood as an **initial algebra** [8]. For a signature endofunctor $F$ (e.g., $F(X) = 1 + X \times X$ for leaf/node trees), an $F$-*algebra* is $(A, a : F(A) \to A)$; a homomorphism $h$ satisfies $h \circ a = b \circ F(h)$. An **initial** $F$-algebra $(\mu F, \mathit{in})$ admits exactly one homomorphism to every other $F$-algebra: the *catamorphism* $\llparenthesis f \rrparenthesis : \mu F \to A$, which *is* the recursion principle. Semantics is then *determined* by the target algebra, and compilers are homomorphisms between initial algebras of source and target signatures [8].

![Figure 4: Initial-algebra semantics — the F-algebra homomorphism diagram and the unique catamorphism from the initial algebra.](public/thesis/ths_20260909_0428_1788942564000_9526-3.webp)

> **Theorem 1 (Lambek's Lemma).** The structure map $\mathit{in} : F(\mu F) \to \mu F$ of an initial $F$-algebra is an isomorphism.

*Proof sketch.* Initiality applied to $(F(\mu F), F(\mathit{in}))$ yields a unique $u : \mu F \to F(\mu F)$; showing $\mathit{in} \circ u = \mathrm{id}$ and $u \circ \mathit{in} = \mathrm{id}$ by uniqueness of mediating maps gives the inverse. ∎ (Full chase in Section 5.)

Lambek's lemma says syntax is a *fixed point* of its own signature — $F(\mu F) \cong \mu F$ — which is why recursive types and inductive definitions coincide categorically.

Classical initial algebras handle first-order syntax but stumble on **variable binding**: naive endofunctors on $\mathbf{Set}$ cannot express scope. Fiore, Plotkin, and Turi moved to the **presheaf category** $[\mathbb{F}, \mathbf{Set}]$, where $\mathbb{F}$ is finite sets (contexts) [7]: a presheaf $\Lambda$ assigns to each context $n$ the terms $\Lambda(n)$ with at most $n$ free variables. Binding becomes context extension $(\delta X)(n) = X(n+1)$, and the $\lambda$-calculus is the initial algebra for $V + \delta(-) + (-)\times(-)$ on presheaves. Substitution is then a *monoid structure* on the presheaf, and the substitution lemma a monoid law — the categorical justification for de Bruijn-style implementations and modern higher-order abstract syntax.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We discharge the proof obligations incurred above. Each is a checkable equational fact validating a definitional principle of the deep dive.

> **Theorem 2 (Kleisli composition is associative).** For a monad $(T, \eta, \mu)$, $g \circ_K f = \mu \circ T(g) \circ f$ is associative with identities $\eta_A$.

*Proof.* $(h \circ_K g) \circ_K f = \mu \circ T(\mu \circ T(h) \circ g) \circ f = \mu \circ T\mu \circ T^2(h) \circ T(g) \circ f$ by functoriality, $= \mu \circ \mu_T \circ T^2(h) \circ T(g) \circ f$ by associativity, $= \mu \circ T(h) \circ \mu \circ T(g) \circ f = h \circ_K (g \circ_K f)$ by naturality of $\mu$. Units: $\eta \circ_K f = \mu \circ T\eta \circ f = f$ (right unit); $f \circ_K \eta = \mu \circ T(f) \circ \eta = \mu \circ \eta_T \circ f = f$ (naturality of $\eta$, left unit). ∎

> **Theorem 3 (Adjunctions induce monads).** If $F \dashv G$ with unit $\eta$ and counit $\varepsilon$, then $(GF, \eta, G\varepsilon F)$ is a monad.

*Proof.* Naturality of $\mu = G\varepsilon F$ follows from naturality of $\varepsilon$ and functoriality. Both sides of the associativity law reduce, via the interchange law in $\mathbf{Cat}$, to $G(\varepsilon \circ FG\varepsilon)F$; the middle unit cancels by the triangle identity $G\varepsilon \circ \eta G = \mathrm{id}$. The unit laws are the two triangle identities, whiskered. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (Lambek's Lemma, full version).** $\mathit{in} : F(\mu F) \to \mu F$ is an isomorphism.

*Proof.* Initiality on $(F(\mu F), F(\mathit{in}))$ gives a unique homomorphism $u : \mu F \to F(\mu F)$ with $u \circ \mathit{in} = F(\mathit{in}) \circ F(u)$. Then $\mathit{in} \circ u$ is an endomorphism of the initial algebra, as is $\mathrm{id}_{\mu F}$; by uniqueness, $\mathit{in} \circ u = \mathrm{id}$. Uniqueness of the mediating map into $(F(\mu F), F(\mathit{in}))$ similarly forces $u \circ \mathit{in} = \mathrm{id}_{F(\mu F)}$. Hence $u = \mathit{in}^{-1}$. ∎

> **Theorem 5 (Moggi's soundness for $\lambda_c$).** Every strong monad $(T, \eta, \mu, t)$ on a CCC yields a sound model of the computational $\lambda$-calculus [1][2].

*Proof sketch.* Interpret $\llbracket T(A) \rrbracket = T\llbracket A \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket \mathtt{let}\; x \Leftarrow M \;\mathtt{in}\; N \rrbracket = \mu \circ T(\llbracket N \rrbracket) \circ t \circ \langle \mathrm{id}, \llbracket M \rrbracket \rangle$. The $\beta_c$ axiom holds by the monad unit law plus strength coherence $t \circ \langle \mathrm{id}, \eta \rangle = \eta$; the associativity axiom by the monad associativity law plus the strength axioms over $\mu$. The rest is naturality and functoriality. ∎

## 6 Limitations

The categorical program has genuine boundaries. First, **full abstraction fails**: monadic semantics is typically adequate but not fully abstract — the continuation monad validates equations the operational semantics refutes once control is delimited [6]. Second, **monads do not compose**: no general monad structure exists on $T_1T_2$; composing effects needs distributive laws, which often do not exist (e.g., none for the list monad over itself), motivating transformers and algebraic effects [6]. Third, **size**: presheaf categories like $[\mathbb{F}, \mathbf{Set}]$ [7] strain set-theoretic foundations, requiring Grothendieck universes for full rigor. Fourth, **the operational gap**: denotational semantics identifies programs up to equational theory and abstracts away *cost* — quantitative refinements remain active research. Finally, **completeness is fragile**: dependent types, polymorphism, and effects each demand heavy extensions (comprehension categories, fibrations, indexed monads) of the clean CHL picture [10].

## 7 Conclusion

We have traced a single arc. Categories, functors, and natural transformations provide the ambient language. **Monads** — via Moggi's computational metalanguage — make computational effects first-class mathematical citizens, with the Kleisli category as the universe of effectful programs [1][2][3][4][5]. **Adjunctions** explain where monads come from and, through Beck monadicity and the Plotkin–Power program, how effect operations determine their monads [6]. **Enriched categories** calibrate resource-sensitivity, from domain-theoretic continuity to linear logic's accounting of use [9]. The **Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence** exhibits the identity of logic, language, and category [10]. And **initial-algebra semantics** — from Goguen's abstract data types [8] to Fiore–Plotkin–Turi presheaves [7] — shows that syntax and recursion are universal constructions.

The enduring lesson is methodological: when a language feature admits a clean categorical description — a monad, an adjunction, an initial algebra — that description predicts its equational theory, guides its implementation, and connects it to decades of mathematics. Features that resist such description carry a burden of justification the categorical ones do not. As languages absorb effects, resources, and dependency, the toolkit developed from Moggi to initial algebras remains the sharpest instrument for understanding what programs mean.
