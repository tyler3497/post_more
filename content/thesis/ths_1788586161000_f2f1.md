# Probabilistic Cardinality Estimation at Planetary Scale: HyperLogLog++ Register Merging, Theta Sketches in Apache DataSketches, Harmonic-Mean Error Analysis, and Distributed Union Semantics for 10^11 Distinct Elements

## Abstract

Counting distinct elements at planetary scale is infeasible with exact structures: a set of 10^11 unique identifiers consumes terabytes of memory and admits no trivially partitionable aggregate. This thesis surveys the family of probabilistic cardinality estimators that made sub-linear, mergeable distinct counting a solved engineering problem — from Flajolet, Fusy, Gandouet, and Meunier's HyperLogLog (HLL) and its refinements, through Heule, Nunkesser, and Hall's HyperLogLog++ (HLL++), to the Theta and KMV sketches formalized in the Apache DataSketches library by Cormode and Duffield. We derive the harmonic-mean register analysis that yields the signature 1.04/sqrt(m) standard error, analyze the sparse/dense representation switch and bias-corrected raw-estimator interpolation in HLL++, formalize the KMV (k-minimum-values) and adaptive-sampling Theta framework with its union, intersection, and difference operations, and prove the mergeability invariant that permits embarrassingly parallel aggregation. We close with production evidence from Apache Druid, Presto/Trino, and ad-tech counting pipelines operating at hundreds of billions of uniques per day.

## 1. Introduction

Consider the canonical ad-tech problem: *how many distinct devices saw this campaign in the last 24 hours?* A modern programmatic platform observes billions of bid requests per hour across dozens of data centers. The naïve answer — insert every device identifier into a hash set and read off `|set|` — collapses under three simultaneous pressures:

1. **Memory**: 10^11 distinct 64-bit identifiers occupy roughly 800 GB even before hash-table overhead.
2. **Distribution**: the stream is partitioned across shards, availability zones, and regions; no single node sees the whole stream.
3. **Query latency**: a dashboard must answer in milliseconds, not after a full-table scan.

Probabilistic cardinality estimation resolves all three by trading a *controlled, quantifiable* relative error — typically 1–2% — for memory footprints measured in kilobytes and a *mergeability* property that makes the aggregate a commutative monoid. The sketch for each partition is computed independently; sketches are combined with a bitwise-max (HLL) or a threshold-min (Theta) union, and the estimator is a pure function of the merged sketch.

> **Theorem:** A sketching scheme with merge function *M* and estimator *E* is *exactly mergeable* iff *M(S₁, S₂) = S(S₁ ∪ S₂)* and *E(M(S₁,S₂)) = E(S(S₁ ∪ S₂))*, i.e., merging commutes with union and the estimator is a function of the canonical sketch. Both HLL/HLL++ and Theta sketches satisfy this invariant.

This thesis gives the full mathematical machinery: the harmonic-mean analysis behind HLL's error bound, the bias corrections and sparse representation that define HLL++, the KMV/Theta framework that supports *arbitrary set operations* rather than unions alone, and the engineering evidence at 10^11-scale workloads.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The Problem Statement

Given a multiset *A* drawn from a universe *U*, the *cardinality* *n = |A*| (the number of distinct elements) is the statistic of interest. A cardinality sketch is a tuple *(S, U, E)* where *S: U → Sk* maps multisets to sketches, *U: Sk × Sk → Sk* merges sketches, and *E: Sk → ℝ⁺* estimates *n*. Desiderata:

- **Sublinear space**: |Sk| = O(log log n) or O(polylog n), independent of *n*.
- **Mergeability**: *U* is associative, commutative, idempotent.
- **Quantified error**: relative standard error *σ/n* with closed form.
- **One-pass**: the sketch is built in a single streaming pass.

### 2.2 Lineage of Ideas

The lineage begins with Flajolet and Martin's *Probabilistic Counting* (1985) [1], which observed that for a random hash of an element, the position of the leftmost 1-bit *ρ(w)* is geometrically distributed: *P(ρ(w) ≥ k) = 2^{-k}*. The maximum *R = max ρ(w)* over the stream satisfies *E[R] ≈ log₂ n*, so *2^R* estimates *n* — but with enormous variance. LogLog (Durand & Flajolet, 2003) [2] split the stream into *m = 2^p* registers (stochastic averaging) and averaged the maxima with a geometric mean; HyperLogLog (Flajolet et al., 2007) [3] replaced it with the **harmonic mean**, dramatically reducing the influence of outlier registers and achieving relative standard error *1.04/√m*.

In parallel, Bar-Yossef et al.'s *KMV* (k minimum values) framework (2002) [4] estimated *n* from the *k*-th smallest hash value: if the *k*-th order statistic of *n* uniform hashes is *vₖ*, then *n̂ = (k−1)/vₖ*. Cormode and Duffield's "An Improved Data Stream Summary: The Count-Min Sketch and its Applications" era culminated in the *Theta sketch* formalization [5], implemented in the open-source **Apache DataSketches** library, which generalizes KMV to *adaptive sampling* with a global threshold *θ* and supports union, intersection, difference, and A-not-B operations.

### 2.3 Why Exact Counting Fails at 10^11

| Approach | Memory at n = 10^11 | Mergeable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hash set (exact) | ~1.6 TB (16 B/entry) | Yes (set union) | Infeasible per partition |
| Sort + dedup (exact) | O(n log n) time | No | Latency in hours |
| Bitmap (exact) | 2^64 bits = impossible | Yes | Universe too large |
| HyperLogLog (p=14) | **12 KB** | Yes | σ/n ≈ 0.81% |
| HLL++ (p=14) | ~1–12 KB (sparse) | Yes | Better small-n accuracy |
| Theta sketch (k=4096) | ~33 KB | Yes | σ/n ≈ 1.56%, set ops |

At *n = 10^11*, a 12 KB HLL sketch with *p = 14* (m = 16384 registers) estimates cardinality with relative standard error *1.04/√16384 ≈ 0.81%* — a memory reduction factor of roughly **10^8** over the exact hash set.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The HyperLogLog Estimator

Let *h: U → {0,1}^64* be a hash function treated as uniform random. For element *v*, write *x = h(v)*; the first *p* bits select register index *j = ⟨x₁…xₚ⟩₂*, and *w = ⟨x_{p+1}…⟩₂* is the remainder. Define *ρ(w) = 1 +* (number of leading zeros of *w*). The sketch is an array *M[0…m−1]* of 6-bit registers, *m = 2^p*, initialized to 0:

```
M[j] := max(M[j], ρ(w))   for each streamed element
```

The **raw estimator** uses the harmonic mean of *2^{-M[j]}*:

> **Theorem:** Let *Z = (Σ_{j=0}^{m−1} 2^{-M[j]})^{-1}*. Then the HLL raw estimate *E = α_m · m² · Z* satisfies *E[E]/n → 1* as *m, n → ∞*, with relative standard deviation *σ/E → β_m/√m*, where *β_∞ = √(3 ln 2 − 1) ≈ 1.03896*.

The constant *α_m* corrects the systematic bias of the harmonic mean:

```
α_m = 0.7213 / (1 + 1.079/m)   for m ≥ 128
α_16 = 0.673, α_32 = 0.697, α_64 = 0.709
```

Small-range correction (linear counting): if *E ≤ 2.5m* and *V* = number of zero registers, use *E* = m · ln(m/V)*, the classic linear-counting estimator [6].

```python
import math

def hll_estimate(registers, p):
    m = 1 << p
    alpha = {16: 0.673, 32: 0.697, 64: 0.709}.get(
        m, 0.7213 / (1.0 + 1.079 / m))
    z = sum(2.0 ** -r for r in registers)
    E = alpha * m * m / z
    V = registers.count(0)
    if E <= 2.5 * m and V > 0:          # small-range correction
        E = m * math.log(m / V)          # linear counting
    # large-range correction omitted for 64-bit hashes
    return E
```

### 3.2 HyperLogLog++: Sparse Representation and Bias Correction

Heule, Nunkesser, and Hall (2013) [7] identified two weaknesses in HLL: poor accuracy at small cardinalities (the raw estimator is biased for *n < 5m/2*) and a fixed memory cost even when the stream is tiny. HLL++ adds:

1. **64-bit hashing** with *p = 25* effective precision in the *sparse* representation, eliminating the large-range correction entirely.
2. **Sparse representation**: instead of a dense array, store the set of *(idx, ρ)* pairs encoded as integers *k = ⟨x₁…xₚ⟩₂ : ⟨x_{p+1}…⟩₂* in a sorted list. Below a size threshold (e.g., 6m/8 bytes), the sketch is a fraction of the dense size.
3. **Bias-corrected estimation**: precomputed bias tables *bias(E_raw, p)* and interpolation give *E = E_raw − bias(E_raw)*, accurate down to *n ≈ 0*.
4. **Sparse-to-dense conversion**: when the temporary sparse set exceeds the threshold, materialize the dense register array once — amortized O(1) per element.

```java
// HyperLogLog++ sparse insertion (schematic, after Heule et al.)
void add(long hash) {
    if (sparse) {
        int k = encodeHash(hash, p, sp);   // p' = 25-bit sparse precision
        tmpSet.add(k);
        if (tmpSet.size() > THRESHOLD) { toDense(); }
    } else {
        int j = (int)(hash >>> (64 - p));
        byte r = rho(hash << p);
        if (r > M[j]) M[j] = r;
    }
}
```

### 3.3 Theta Sketches: The KMV Framework

Where HLL keeps only maxima, the Theta sketch retains actual hash *values* below a threshold, enabling **set operations beyond union**. The KMV sketch of rank *k* stores the *k* smallest hash values {*h₁ < h₂ < … < hₖ*}; the estimator is:

*n̂_KMV = (k − 1) / hₖ*

The Theta sketch generalizes this to *adaptive sampling*: each sketch carries a threshold *θ ∈ (0,1]*, retains all hashes *< θ*, and merges by taking the union of retained hashes and lowering *θ* to the *k*-th smallest value of the merged set [5]:

```
union(S1, S2):
    H = { h in S1.hashes ∪ S2.hashes : h < min(θ1, θ2) }
    θ  = k-th smallest value in H   (or min(θ1,θ2) if |H| ≤ k)
    return sketch(H, θ)
```

> **Theorem:** The Theta union is *exact*: the merged sketch equals the sketch that would have been built from the concatenated stream. Consequently, *intersection*, *difference* (A∖B), and *symmetric difference* sketches can be derived from retained hash values with the same *(k, θ)* semantics — something HLL register arrays cannot support, since maxima discard the information needed for intersections.

Error: the KMV/Theta estimator has relative standard error *1/√(k−2)*, slightly worse per-byte than HLL, but it buys set algebra.

### 3.4 Mergeability as a Monoid

Both families form commutative idempotent monoids:

- **HLL**: *M(S₁,S₂)[j] = max(M₁[j], M₂[j])* — registers are idempotent maxima; duplicates across partitions contribute nothing new. Associativity and commutativity follow from max.
- **Theta**: *union* as defined above; idempotent because re-adding the same hashes cannot lower *θ* further.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Harmonic-Mean Analysis: Why Not the Geometric Mean?

LogLog used the geometric mean *E_geo = α m · m · (Π 2^{M[j]})^{1/m}*. A single register corrupted by a hash anomaly — say one register records *ρ = 40* while the rest record *ρ ≈ 12* — multiplies the geometric mean by *2^{28/m}*, a distortion that grows with *n*. The harmonic mean inverts the terms: the estimator sums *2^{-M[j]}*, so an anomalously *large* register contributes a term near *2^{-40} ≈ 0* and is effectively *ignored*, while an anomalously *small* register (a register that never saw elements, *M[j] = 0*) contributes *1* and dominates the sum — which is precisely the signal linear counting exploits at small *n*.

Flajolet et al. [3] analyze the distribution of *Z = (Σ 2^{-M[j]})^{-1}* via Mellin transforms of the register-value distribution. The key lemma: for large *n*, register values are asymptotically independent with *P(M[j] ≤ k) ≈ exp(−n·2^{-k}/m)*. The harmonic mean's variance then follows from the variance of a sum of i.i.d. bounded variables, giving *Var(E)/E² → (3 ln 2 − 1)/m*, i.e., the celebrated *β_∞ = √(3 ln 2 − 1) ≈ 1.039*.

### 4.2 Register Merging at Scale: The Distributed Union

In production, merging is a tree reduction. Consider Presto/Trino's `approx_distinct(x)` (HLL, *p = 12* default, 4 KB per sketch): each worker builds a sketch per group-by key; the partial aggregation serializes 4 KB per group; the final aggregation takes register-wise maxima. For a query grouping by 10^7 keys, the shuffle carries ~40 GB of sketches instead of ~1.6 TB of raw identifiers — a 40× reduction — and the final estimate for the grand total is a max-reduce over the same sketches.

The merge is *order-independent* and *duplicate-insensitive*: if the same element appears on two shards (common in at-least-once delivery), the max operation absorbs the duplicate exactly. This makes HLL sketches *safe under reprocessing*, a property exact per-partition sets also have but per-partition *counts* do not — a subtle correctness trap in exactly-once vs. at-least-once pipeline design.

### 4.3 Theta Sketches and Arbitrary Set Expressions in Apache DataSketches

The DataSketches library [8] implements the Theta sketch as a first-class *set-expression engine*. Beyond union, it supports:

1. **Intersection** *A ∩ B*: retain hashes present in *both* sketches below *min(θ_A, θ_B)*; estimate *(|H|−1)/θ*.
2. **A-not-B** *A ∖ B*: retain hashes in *A* below *θ_A* absent from *B*'s retained set.
3. **Jaccard similarity** and inclusion–exclusion–derived distinct counts over arbitrary Boolean expressions.

This powers the canonical ad-tech funnel query — *devices that saw the ad AND visited the site but did NOT convert* — directly on sketches, without touching raw event logs. HLL cannot express this: from two register arrays you can recover |A ∪ B| but neither |A ∩ B| nor |A ∖ B|, because the maxima discard membership information. The price is memory: at equal error, Theta needs roughly 4–6× the bytes of HLL (storing 8-byte hashes vs. 6-bit registers), plus the retained-hash set enables the algebra.

```python
# Theta sketch union/intersection semantics (schematic)
def theta_union(sketches, k):
    vals = sorted({h for s in sketches for h in s.hashes
                   if h < min(s.theta for s in sketches)})
    theta = vals[k-1] if len(vals) > k else min(s.theta for s in sketches)
    return Sketch(vals[:k], theta)

def theta_intersect(a, b, k):
    theta = min(a.theta, b.theta)
    vals = sorted((set(a.hashes) & set(b.hashes)))
    vals = [h for h in vals if h < theta]
    return Sketch(vals[:k], theta)
```

### 4.4 Error Bounds, Confidence Intervals, and the 10^11 Regime

Both families give *relative* error independent of *n*: HLL *σ/n ≈ 1.04/√m*, KMV *σ/n ≈ 1/√(k−2)*. The estimates are asymptotically normal (by the CLT applied to the register sum / order statistics), so a 95% confidence interval is *n̂ ± 1.96·σ*.

At *n = 10^11* with HLL *p = 14* (*m = 16384*): *σ ≈ 0.0081 · 10^11 = 8.1 × 10^8*; the 95% CI spans roughly ±1.6 × 10^9 — a ±1.6% band. For ad-tech billing, where the question is "did reach grow 5% week-over-week," this is ample; for exact invoicing, sketches are *not* used — a boundary the industry respects.

Ertl's 2017 maximum-likelihood estimator [9] ("New cardinality estimation algorithms for HyperLogLog sketches") improves on the raw harmonic-mean estimator by treating the register histogram as a likelihood function over *n* and maximizing it — reducing error by ~15–30% at no extra memory cost, and extending naturally to *set operations on HLL sketches* (inclusion–exclusion via the joint register distribution), partially closing the gap with Theta sketches. This is the estimator implemented in several modern HLL libraries' "HLL with MLE" modes.

| Sketch | m or k | Memory | σ/n | Set ops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HLL, p=12 | m=4096 | 3 KB | 1.62% | Union only |
| HLL, p=14 | m=16384 | 12 KB | 0.81% | Union only |
| HLL, p=16 | m=65536 | 48 KB | 0.41% | Union only |
| HLL++ sparse, small n | — | <1 KB | varies | Union only |
| Theta, k=4096 | — | ~33 KB | 1.56% | Union ∩ ∖ |
| Theta, k=16384 | — | ~131 KB | 0.78% | Union ∩ ∖ |
| HLL + MLE [9], p=14 | m=16384 | 12 KB | ~0.65% | Union (+est. ∩) |

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Accuracy Validation

The canonical validation methodology, used in [3], [7], and [9], generates synthetic streams with known *n* (typically *n ∈ {10^2, …, 10^9}*), builds sketches with independent hash seeds, and reports the empirical relative RMSE over hundreds of trials against the theoretical *β/√m* curve. Results consistently show:

1. HLL raw estimator matches *1.04/√m* for *n > 5m/2*; below that, bias dominates and linear counting / HLL++ interpolation takes over.
2. HLL++ sparse mode tracks linear counting exactly at small *n* and converges to the raw estimator with bias correction through the transition region — the "bump" in relative error around *n ≈ m* visible in plain HLL is eliminated [7, Fig. 4].
3. Theta/KMV empirical error matches *1/√(k−2)* across the full range, with no small-*n* pathology, since the estimator *(k−1)/hₖ* is unbiased for all *n ≥ k* [4][5].

### 5.2 Production Evidence: Druid, Presto, and Ad-Tech

- **Apache Druid** implements HLL sketches as a first-class aggregator (`HLLSketchBuild`, `HLLSketchMerge`) [10]; its production deployments at ad-tech firms routinely estimate uniques over trillions of events per month. Druid's Theta sketch module [11] additionally supports sketch set operations for funnel analysis.
- **Presto/Trino** `approx_distinct` (HLL) and `approx_set` are among the most-called aggregate functions in interactive analytics; Meta's Presto fleet executes millions of such aggregations daily.
- **Ad-tech counting**: demand-side platforms count distinct devices per campaign per day in the 10^9–10^11 range using HLL sketches keyed by (campaign, day), merged across data centers. The mergeability invariant means each data center ships kilobytes per key per day; a central rollup produces global reach numbers with sub-1% error — the numbers that appear in advertiser dashboards.

### 5.3 Formal Mergeability Proof Sketch

> **Theorem:** Let *S(D)* be the HLL sketch of multiset *D*. For any partition *D = D₁ ∪ D₂*, *max-merge(S(D₁), S(D₂)) = S(D₁ ∪ D₂)*.

*Proof.* Register *j* of *S(D)* is *max_{v∈D, idx(v)=j} ρ(v)*. The max over *D₁ ∪ D₂* of the per-element values equals *max(max over D₁, max over D₂)* by associativity of max. Register-wise max of the two sketches therefore equals the sketch of the union. ∎

The Theta case is analogous with min over hash values and the adaptive threshold rule; exactness follows because the retained set after union is precisely the set of hashes below the merged threshold that the concatenated stream would have retained [5, §4].

## 6. Limitations

1. **No deletions**: registers record maxima; removing an element cannot lower a register. (Counting Bloom-filter variants support deletions but sacrifice mergeability or accuracy.)
2. **No intersections for HLL**: as shown in §4.3, HLL register arrays fundamentally cannot recover |A ∩ B|; Ertl's MLE-based inclusion–exclusion [9] gives an *estimate* but with wider error than Theta's exact retained-hash algebra.
3. **Hash quality dependence**: all analysis assumes a uniform random hash. Adversarial inputs against a weak hash (e.g., MurmurHash with crafted collisions) can skew estimates; production systems use seeded 64-bit hashes (xxHash, MurmurHash3_x64_128) and treat the seed as a deployment secret.
4. **Small-cardinality overhead**: HLL++ sparse mode mitigates but does not eliminate the constant-factor overhead; for *n < 100*, exact structures are smaller *and* exact.
5. **Merge compatibility**: sketches with different precision parameters (*p*, *k*) cannot be merged without downsampling to the coarser parameter; schema discipline is required in heterogeneous fleets.
6. **Not differentially private**: sketches leak approximate membership for small universes; they are *not* a privacy mechanism, only a compression mechanism.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Probabilistic cardinality estimation is one of the rare algorithmic ideas that moved from theory to planetary-scale infrastructure with its mathematical guarantees intact. The harmonic-mean analysis of HyperLogLog gives a closed-form error law — *1.04/√m* — that lets engineers *buy* accuracy by the kilobyte; HyperLogLog++ extends the operating range down to single digits with sparse representations and bias correction; and the Theta/KMV framework in Apache DataSketches trades a constant factor in memory for a full algebra of set operations. The mergeability invariant — a commutative idempotent monoid over sketches — is the deep reason these structures compose across shards, zones, and regions without coordination, making 10^11-scale distinct counting a kilobytes-per-partition problem. Open directions include tighter estimators (Ertl's MLE program [9]), learned sketch parameterizations, and formal privacy accounting for sketch releases — but the core problem, as posed in §1, is solved.
