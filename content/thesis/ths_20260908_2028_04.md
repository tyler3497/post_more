# Neural Audio Codecs: Residual Vector Quantization in EnCodec, Descript Audio Codec, and Autoregressive Generation versus Opus

## Abstract

This thesis develops the theory and evidence for **residual vector quantization (RVQ)** as the central bottleneck in modern neural audio codecs. We analyze EnCodec [1] — causal convolutional encoder–decoder, an RVQ cascade of 32 codebooks, and multi-scale complex-STFT discriminators — and the Descript Audio Codec (DAC) [2], which repairs RVQ pathologies via factorized low-dimensional codes with L2-normalized codebooks and balanced entropy, alongside their ancestor SoundStream [3]. We then trace how RVQ tokens enabled a second shift: autoregressive audio language models (AudioLM [4], VALL-E [5]) that treat compression as generation. A systematic comparison against Opus [6] on MUSHRA and ViSQOL grounds the tradeoff between learned and engineered codecs across bitrate, latency, and compute.

---

## 1 Introduction

The classical audio codec is an engineering monument: psychoacoustic models derived from masking experiments in the 1970s, MDCT transforms, entropy coding, and decades of hand-tuning. Opus, standardized as RFC 6716 [6], fuses two such monuments — the speech-optimized LPC layer SILK and the music-optimized MDCT layer CELT — and remains the dominant interactive codec. Yet classical codecs optimize a fixed analysis basis for an average signal; they cannot adapt their representation to the data distribution at inference time.

Neural audio codecs invert this design. Instead of designing the basis, they *learn* it: an encoder maps raw waveforms to a dense latent grid, a quantizer discretizes it, and a decoder reconstructs audio under a mixture of reconstruction and adversarial losses [1][2][3]. The quantizer is the crux. **Residual vector quantization (RVQ)** — a cascade in which each stage quantizes the residual error of the previous stage — provides bitrate scalability, a discrete interface for generative models, and a graceful degradation path, all in one mechanism [3].

> **Thesis statement.** RVQ is not merely a compression trick but the *interface layer* between perception and generation: by factorizing audio into hierarchical discrete tokens with known rate–distortion behavior, it simultaneously enables (i) low-bitrate perceptual coding competitive with or exceeding Opus, and (ii) autoregressive audio language models that synthesize speech and music from token sequences [4][5].

This thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 surveys the lineage from SoundStream to EnCodec to DAC. Section 3 formalizes the codec pipeline and its losses. Section 4 gives a four-part deep dive: EnCodec's architecture, the mathematics of RVQ and DAC's factorized codes, autoregressive generation, and the Opus DSP baseline. Section 5 compares bitrate–quality, latency, and complexity empirically and theoretically. Sections 6–7 address open problems and conclusions.

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 SoundStream: the RVQ codec template

SoundStream [3] established the architectural template that all subsequent neural codecs follow: a fully convolutional causal encoder, an RVQ bottleneck, a mirrored transposed-convolution decoder, and adversarial training with a mixture of waveform and spectral discriminators. Two training innovations proved durable. First, **quantizer dropout** — randomly truncating the RVQ cascade at a random depth per training example — yields a single model that spans bitrates (3–18 kbps in SoundStream) with negligible quality loss relative to bitrate-specific models [3]. Second, the combination of a *feature* reconstruction loss (multi-resolution mel or STFT L1/L2) with adversarial losses balances signal fidelity against perceptual plausibility: the generator need not reproduce the exact phase if a discriminator cannot tell the difference.

Crucially, SoundStream's subjective evaluations at 24 kHz showed 3 kbps SoundStream outperforming Opus at 12 kbps on MUSHRA and approaching EVS at 9.6 kbps [3] — the first quantitative demonstration that learned codecs could beat a standards-grade DSP codec at a quarter of the bitrate for speech-like content.

### 2.2 EnCodec: scale, discriminators, and entropy coding

EnCodec [1] (Défossez et al., 2022) refined the template along three axes. It introduced the **multi-scale complex-STFT (MS-STFT) discriminator**, which evaluates complex spectrograms at multiple time-frequency resolutions and proved substantially more effective than waveform discriminators for high-fidelity music. It scaled the RVQ cascade to 32 codebooks of 1024 entries (10 bits each) at a 75 Hz frame rate for 24 kHz mono, yielding bitrates from 1.5 to 24 kbps, plus a 48 kHz stereo model. And it trained a small Transformer language model over the RVQ codes to perform **entropy coding**, compressing the representation by up to 40% with zero additional distortion [1]. The public implementation [7] has made EnCodec the de facto reference codec for downstream generative research.

### 2.3 DAC: fixing the RVQ codebook

The Descript Audio Codec [2] (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2023) diagnosed and repaired three RVQ pathologies that degrade fidelity at 44.1 kHz: (i) **codebook collapse**, in which only a fraction of code vectors receive gradient updates; (ii) **bitrate inefficiency**, from poorly utilized codebooks; and (iii) poor generalization of periodic inductive bias. DAC's fixes — factorized codes (low-dimensional lookups projected back to full dimensionality), L2-normalized codebook vectors with cosine-similarity search, and k-means initialization — are the subject of Section 4.2. With 9 codebooks at 86 Hz frame rate, DAC achieves higher subjective quality than EnCodec at 8 kbps for full-band 44.1 kHz audio [2].

### 2.4 From codes to language: AudioLM and VALL-E

Once audio is a sequence of discrete tokens, the entire LLM machinery becomes applicable. AudioLM [4] (Borsos et al.) models RVQ tokens hierarchically — a coarse semantic stage followed by acoustic stages — and generates continuations that are coherent over tens of seconds. VALL-E [5] (Wang et al.) treats TTS as conditional language modeling over EnCodec tokens: an autoregressive model predicts the first quantizer's codes from phonemes plus a 3-second speaker prompt, and a non-autoregressive model fills in the remaining RVQ stages, achieving zero-shot voice cloning with in-context learning [5].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The codec pipeline

A neural audio codec is a triple $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{D})$: encoder, quantizer, decoder. Given waveform $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{T}$ at sample rate $f_s$,

$$\mathbf{z} = \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D},$$

where the frame rate $S$ is set by the encoder's total stride $M$, $S = f_s/M$. The quantizer maps each frame $\mathbf{z}_t$ to a tuple of code indices $(c_{t,1}, \dots, c_{t,N_q})$ and a reconstruction $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t$; the decoder outputs $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathbf{z}})$. The instantaneous bitrate is

$$R = S \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{N_q} \log_2 K_k \quad \text{bits/s},$$

with $K_k$ the size of codebook $k$. For EnCodec at 24 kHz: $M = 320$ (strides $2\times4\times5\times8$), $S = 75$ Hz, $D = 128$, $N_q = 32$, $K_k = 1024$ — hence $R = 75 \times 32 \times 10 = 24$ kbps at full depth, with quantizer dropout exposing 1.5, 3, 6, 12 kbps operating points [1].

```python
def residual_vector_quantize(z, codebooks):
    """RVQ: greedy residual cascade over per-stage codebooks.
    z: (D,) latent frame; codebooks: list of (K, D) tensors."""
    residual = z
    indices, quantized = [], 0.0
    for C in codebooks:
        # nearest neighbor in Euclidean (or cosine) metric
        idx = torch.argmin(((residual - C) ** 2).sum(-1))
        q = C[idx]
        indices.append(idx)
        quantized = quantized + q          # accumulate partial reconstructions
        residual = residual - q             # feed residual error forward
    return indices, quantized, residual
```

### 3.2 Training losses

Training minimizes a composite objective over the generator $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{D})$:

$$\mathcal{L}_G = \lambda_t \ell_t + \lambda_f \ell_f + \lambda_g \ell_g + \lambda_{fm} \ell_{fm} + \lambda_w \ell_w,$$

where $\ell_t$ is a time-domain L1 loss, $\ell_f$ a multi-scale spectral (mel/STFT) reconstruction loss, $\ell_g$ the adversarial hinge loss against the discriminators, $\ell_{fm}$ feature matching on discriminator intermediates, and $\ell_w$ the RVQ **commitment loss** $\|\mathbf{z} - \text{sg}[\hat{\mathbf{z}}]\|_2^2$ that keeps encoder outputs near their quantized values [1][2][3]. Discriminators are trained with the complementary hinge objective $\ell_d$ [1]. This recipe is remarkably stable across implementations; DAC adds periodicity-aware Snake activations and a multi-period waveform discriminator to sharpen harmonic structure [2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 EnCodec: convolutional encoder–decoder, RVQ cascade, and multi-scale STFT discriminators

EnCodec's encoder is a 1-D convolutional stack (strides $2, 4, 5, 8$ for a $320\times$ downsampling), followed by a two-layer LSTM and a final convolution producing $D=128$ latents at 75 Hz [1]. Causality is optional: the 24 kHz model is causal (suitable for streaming), the 48 kHz stereo model is non-causal. The decoder mirrors the encoder with transposed convolutions and an LSTM.

The RVQ cascade is the heart of the bitrate story. With 32 codebooks of 1024 entries, each frame carries up to 320 bits; dropping to $N_q' < 32$ stages (during training via quantizer dropout, at inference via stream adaptation) trades bits for a *hierarchically coarse* reconstruction: stage 1 captures gross spectral envelope, later stages encode fine detail — the discrete analog of scalable coding, but learned.

The decisive perceptual contribution is the **MS-STFT discriminator** [1]. Rather than judging waveforms, it judges *complex* STFTs — magnitude and phase jointly — at several window lengths. Ablations show it dominates waveform discriminators on music: multi-resolution windows catch both transient smearing and tonal ringing [1]. The result: EnCodec at 6 kbps achieves MUSHRA parity with Opus at 12 kbps on music, a 2:1 advantage for the learned codec [1].

A final, underappreciated component is the **LM entropy coder**. The RVQ indices are far from i.i.d.; a small Transformer trained with cross-entropy $\ell_l$ on the code sequence learns their redundancy and arithmetic-codes them, recovering up to 40% of the bitrate [1]. This is exactly the move classical codecs make with adaptive entropy coding — except the context model is learned rather than hand-designed.

### 4.2 RVQ mathematics: the cascade, codebook collapse, and DAC's factorized L2-normalized codes

Formalize stage $k$ of RVQ. Let $\mathbf{r}_0 = \mathbf{z}$, and for $k = 1..N_q$:

$$\mathbf{r}_k = \mathbf{r}_{k-1} - \mathbf{c}_{k,\, q_k}, \qquad q_k = \arg\min_j d(\mathbf{r}_{k-1}, \mathbf{c}_{k,j}),$$

with codebook $\mathcal{C}_k = \{\mathbf{c}_{k,j}\}_{j=1}^{K}$. The final quantized latent is $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \sum_k \mathbf{c}_{k,q_k}$, and the approximation error $\|\mathbf{r}_{N_q}\|$ decays geometrically when codebooks are well-matched to the residual distribution.

> **Theorem 1 (RVQ error decay).** *If each codebook $\mathcal{C}_k$ achieves a covering radius $\rho_k$ on the support of $\mathbf{r}_{k-1}$ (i.e., $\min_j d(\mathbf{r}_{k-1}, \mathbf{c}_{k,j}) \le \rho_k \|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|$), then $\|\mathbf{r}_{N_q}\| \le (\prod_k \rho_k)\,\|\mathbf{z}\|$. In practice, residual magnitudes shrink so fast that later codebooks specialize to micro-structure.*

In practice, vanilla RVQ suffers **codebook collapse**: with 1024 codes in 128 dimensions trained by straight-through estimation, a large fraction of codes are never the nearest neighbor of any residual and receive no gradient — dead codes, wasted bits. DAC [2] attacks this with three interlocking changes:

1. **Factorized codes.** Instead of nearest-neighbor search in the full $D$-dimensional space, DAC projects $\mathbf{r}_{k-1}$ to a low-dimensional space ($d \ll D$, e.g. 8), quantizes there with a small codebook, and projects back with a learned linear map. This concentrates codebook density where it matters and, per [2], dramatically improves codebook utilization.
2. **L2 normalization.** Code vectors are L2-normalized and nearest-neighbor search uses cosine similarity rather than Euclidean distance. This decouples *direction* (timbre/harmonic content, carried by the codes) from *magnitude* (loudness, carried by the residual scale), stabilizing training at 44.1 kHz where energy varies wildly across frames [2].
3. **Balanced entropy and improved discriminators.** DAC maximizes codebook entropy to force uniform code usage ("maximum entropy code representation" [2]) and pairs this with Snake-beta periodic activations plus a multi-period discriminator and a multi-band multi-scale complex-STFT discriminator, capturing both harmonic (periodic) and transient structure.

The payoff is concrete: DAC at 8 kbps / 44.1 kHz outperforms EnCodec on subjective and objective metrics while using only 9 codebooks at an 86 Hz frame rate — a *lower token rate* than EnCodec's 75 Hz × 32 codes, which matters enormously for the language models of Section 4.3, since sequence length is the dominant cost of autoregressive generation [2].

The table below summarizes the design points:

| Codec | $f_s$ (kHz) | Stride $M$ | Frame rate (Hz) | Codebooks | Bits/code | Target $R$ (kbps) | Discriminators |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SoundStream [3] | 24 | 320 | 75 | 8 | 10 | 3–18 (dropout) | multi-scale waveform + STFT |
| EnCodec [1] | 24 | 320 | 75 | 32 | 10 | 1.5–24 (dropout) | MS complex-STFT |
| EnCodec [1] | 48 (stereo) | 320 | 150 | 16 | 10 | 3–24 | MS complex-STFT |
| DAC [2] | 44.1 | 512 | 86 | 9 | 10 | 8 | multi-period + multi-band STFT |

### 4.3 Autoregressive generation over discrete tokens: AudioLM and VALL-E

RVQ turns audio into a matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \{1..K\}^{S \times N_q}$ — a sequence of frames, each a tuple of $N_q$ tokens. AudioLM [4] observes that this matrix has a natural hierarchy: the *first* quantizer's codes correlate with semantic content (phonemes, melody), while later stages carry acoustic detail (timbre, room). AudioLM therefore trains three Transformers: a semantic stage (from a self-supervised w2v-BERT tokenizer), a coarse acoustic stage conditioned on semantics, and a fine acoustic stage — generating minute-long piano and speech continuations that remain musically and linguistically coherent [4].

VALL-E [5] specializes this to TTS with a two-stage recipe over EnCodec tokens:

1. An **autoregressive** Transformer predicts the first-quantizer codes $\mathbf{c}_{:,1}$ from phonemes and a 3-second acoustic prompt of the target speaker.
2. A **non-autoregressive** Transformer predicts stages $2..N_q$ in parallel, conditioned on stage 1.

Trained on 60k hours of LibriLight, VALL-E exhibits in-context learning: a 3-second prompt suffices for zero-shot speaker cloning [5]. Note the architectural economy — the codec *is* the tokenizer, so no mel-spectrogram vocoder is needed. This is why DAC's lower frame rate (86 Hz with 9 stages) is strategically important: fewer, better-utilized tokens mean shorter sequences and cheaper language modeling [2][5].

### 4.4 Opus revisited: SILK, CELT, and the DSP baseline

Opus [6] is the codec neural methods must dethrone. Its design is a study in engineered adaptivity:

- **SILK** (speech layer): linear predictive coding with long-term prediction (pitch), noise shaping, and entropy coding; operates on 10/20/40/60 ms frames at narrowband to wideband.
- **CELT** (music layer): modified DCT with explicit energy/fine-structure separation and pyramid vector quantization; frames of 2.5–20 ms, down to 5 ms algorithmic delay.
- **Hybrid mode**: SILK handles low frequencies, CELT high frequencies, for super-wideband speech.

Opus spans 6–510 kbps, supports mono/stereo with dynamic switching, ships fixed- and floating-point implementations, and carries a 26.5 ms default algorithmic delay (reducible to 5 ms) [6]. It is royalty-free, bit-exact specified, and battle-tested in WebRTC, Discord, and WhatsApp.

Against this, neural codecs trade *generality of deployment* for *quality per bit on the training distribution*:

| Dimension | Opus [6] | EnCodec [1] | DAC [2] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best reported MUSHRA parity | baseline | ≈ Opus@12k with 6k (music) | > EnCodec@8k, 44.1 kHz |
| Minimum practical bitrate | ~6 kbps (NB speech) | 1.5 kbps (24 kHz) | 8 kbps (44.1 kHz) |
| Algorithmic latency | 5–26.5 ms | ~13–40 ms (causal) | higher (non-causal) |
| Decode compute | ~10–50 MHz CPU | real-time on 1 CPU core [1] | several GMACs/s |
| Royalty / spec | royalty-free, RFC | research license, no standard | research license |
| Generative reuse | none | tokens → AudioLM/VALL-E [4][5] | lower-rate tokens [2] |

The verdict is nuanced rather than triumphal. Neural codecs win the *rate–distortion* contest at low bitrates and add the generative interface; Opus wins on latency, compute, standardization, and out-of-distribution robustness. A codec is a contract, and Opus's contract is written in an RFC [6]; neural codecs' contracts are written in weights.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Results

### 5.1 What the papers report

- **SoundStream** [3]: at 24 kHz, 3 kbps SoundStream > Opus at 12 kbps on MUSHRA (speech), approaching EVS at 9.6 kbps. ViSQOL scores rise monotonically with SNR in joint compression–denoising experiments (2.93 at 0 dB to 3.58 at 15 dB input SNR).
- **EnCodec** [1]: state-of-the-art MUSHRA for speech and music at 1.5/3/6/12 kbps (24 kHz mono) and 6/12/24 kbps (48 kHz stereo); ablation confirms the MS-STFT discriminator and the LM entropy coder (up to −40% rate) are the dominant quality drivers.
- **DAC** [2]: at 8 kbps / 44.1 kHz, DAC surpasses EnCodec and prior RVQ codecs on both subjective ratings and objective metrics, with near-uniform codebook utilization attributable to factorized L2-normalized codes.
- **AudioLM** [4]: long-form generation rated near ground truth on acoustic quality for piano continuation, with semantic coherence from the hierarchical token design.
- **VALL-E** [5]: zero-shot TTS with 3-second prompts significantly outperforms prior zero-shot systems on naturalness (CMOS) and speaker similarity (SMOS).

### 5.2 A rate–distortion reading

Classical rate–distortion theory bounds any codec by $R(D)$ for the source; Opus approaches its bound with a fixed basis. Neural codecs instead learn an *empirical* $R(D)$ on the training distribution, and adversarial training lets them spend bits on *perceptual* rather than *waveform* fidelity — effectively redefining $D$ as a learned divergence. This explains the low-bitrate wins: at 3 kbps waveform fidelity is hopeless, but *plausible* reconstruction is achievable, which is what MUSHRA rewards. The cost is distribution shift: off-distribution, the learned $R(D)$ degrades while Opus's engineered $R(D)$ holds [1][2].

> **Proposition.** *Let $D_{\text{MUSHRA}}$ be a perceptual distortion. A neural codec trained with discriminator loss $\ell_g$ approximately minimizes $R + \lambda D_{\text{MUSHRA}}$ on the training distribution, whereas a classical codec minimizes $R + \lambda D_{\text{model}}$ for a fixed psychoacoustic $D_{\text{model}}$. The neural advantage is the gap $D_{\text{model}} - D_{\text{MUSHRA}}$ in-distribution; the neural risk is its generalization gap off-distribution.*

### 5.3 Complexity and latency arithmetic

EnCodec's real-time-on-one-CPU-core claim [1] rests on causal convolutions with modest channel counts; DAC's periodic activations and deeper stacks cost several GMACs/s — acceptable on a phone NPU, heavy on a microcontroller. Opus decodes in microseconds on fixed-point DSPs [6]. For interactive voice (the Opus stronghold), the neural latency budget is the sum of encoder lookahead, frame buffering ($1/75$ s ≈ 13.3 ms per frame for EnCodec), and decoder streaming delay — competitive with Opus's 26.5 ms only in the causal configuration. For streaming media (the DAC stronghold), latency is irrelevant and the neural quality-per-bit advantage dominates.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Out-of-distribution fragility.** Learned codecs overfit their training corpora; a codec trained on clean speech degrades on polyphonic music and vice versa, whereas Opus's mode switching is content-adaptive by construction [6]. Distributionally robust codec training remains open.
2. **Codebook pathology at scale.** DAC's factorization [2] mitigates collapse but does not eliminate it; as $N_q$ grows, later stages see near-noise residuals and learn degenerate codes. Principled per-stage rate–distortion codebook design is missing.
3. **Latency vs. quality.** Non-causal models (DAC, EnCodec-48k) win benchmarks but cannot stream; causal models pay a quality tax. Bridging this gap without doubling compute is unsolved.
4. **Standardization and patents.** Opus is an IETF standard with royalty-free patent grants [6]; neural codecs have neither. Deployment at internet scale requires a standards process that does not yet exist.
5. **Evaluation.** MUSHRA is expensive and ViSQOL correlates imperfectly with neural artifacts (burbling, phoneme hallucination — unlike DSP pre-echo or birdies). We need perceptual metrics calibrated on *neural* distortions.
6. **The generation–compression duality.** AudioLM/VALL-E show tokens are generative [4][5], but joint optimization of the codec for *both* reconstruction and LM-likelihood is barely explored; today's pipelines train the codec first and the LM second, leaving rate–distortion–*generativity* tradeoffs uncharted.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Residual vector quantization is the hinge on which the last five years of audio coding turned. SoundStream [3] proved the RVQ codec template; EnCodec [1] scaled it, sharpened it with multi-scale complex-STFT discriminators, and squeezed it with LM entropy coding; DAC [2] repaired its codebook geometry with factorized, L2-normalized codes; and AudioLM [4] plus VALL-E [5] revealed that the same discrete tokens are a language in which audio can be *generated*, not merely compressed. Against Opus [6] — still unmatched in latency, efficiency, and standardization — neural codecs have won the low-bitrate perceptual contest and opened the generative frontier, but they have not yet won the engineering contest. The next decade's codec will likely be neither pure DSP nor pure neural, but a hybrid that inherits Opus's contract discipline and the neural codec's learned perception. The RVQ cascade, with its hierarchical, bitrate-scalable discrete tokens, is the natural place to build it.
