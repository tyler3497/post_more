# The Freudenthal Suspension Theorem and Stable Homotopy in Homotopy Type Theory: Blakers–Massey, Joins, and Eilenberg–MacLane Spaces

## Abstract

Analytic homotopy theory proves theorems about topological spaces using point-set topology and CW complexes. *Synthetic* homotopy theory reverses the priority: it takes spaces, paths, and homotopies as primitive logical notions and derives the classical theorems inside the proof system itself — Martin-Loef type theory with Voevodsky's univalence axiom and higher inductive types. This thesis develops the **connectedness calculus** that organizes the synthetic program: *truncation levels*, *h-levels*, and *n-connected maps*. From these foundations we reconstruct three landmark results synthetically: the **Blakers–Massey excision theorem** for homotopy pushouts, the **Freudenthal suspension theorem** via the *James construction*, and the first unstable homotopy groups of spheres — the Hopf fibration giving π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ and Bruneri's number β : ℤ with π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/βℤ, shown constructively to equal 2. We explain Rijke's *join construction* as the engine of connectedness arguments, the machine-checked mechanization of Blakers–Massey in Agda, and the higher inductive **Eilenberg–MacLane spaces** K(G,n) grounding synthetic cohomology. We close with formal verification — including the Cubical Agda proof that π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ — and the open frontiers of synthetic stable homotopy theory.

##1 Introduction

In 1937, Hans Freudenthal observed a phenomenon that reshaped homotopy theory: the homotopy groups of spheres eventually *stabilize*. The suspension homomorphism E : π_{n+k}(Sⁿ) → π_{n+k+1}(Sⁿ⁺¹) becomes an isomorphism once n is large relative to k, so that for each fixed *stem* k the diagonal groups π_{n+k}(Sⁿ) are eventually constant. This discovery founded **stable homotopy theory**, the study of phenomena that persist under suspension. Sixteen years later, Blakers and Massey proved a far-reaching excision theorem controlling how much of the homotopy type of a pushout is visible in its gluing data — the theorem from which Freudenthal's result follows as a special case.

These theorems belong to *analytic* homotopy theory, proved with topological spaces and spectral sequences. The synthetic program asks whether the same theorems can be *derived logically* inside dependent type theory. The first spark was the Licata–Shulman encode–decode calculation of π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ [1]; the HoTT Book then built the core of synthetic homotopy theory — loop spaces, fibrations, the Hopf construction, truncations — and stated the Freudenthal and Blakers–Massey theorems as theorems of the logic [2].

But stabilization is a demanding test: a statement about *infinite families* of types with precise numerical thresholds. Proving it synthetically required new machinery — Lumsdaine's descent and flattening, Rijke's **join construction**, and Bruneri's synthetic **James construction** approximating ΩΣX — culminating in Bruneri's fully synthetic, constructive proof that π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ [3].

This thesis reconstructs that arc: the connectedness calculus, the Blakers–Massey theorem, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the first unstable groups, and the Eilenberg–MacLane spaces turning homotopy groups into cohomology. We track *machine-checked* status throughout — in the synthetic setting a proof is simultaneously a program, and every theorem below has, or approaches, a formalization in Agda or Cubical Agda [4].

##2 Background

### Truncation levels and h-levels

Every type in homotopy type theory carries an intrinsic notion of *homotopical complexity*, measured by Voevodsky's **h-levels** (homotopy levels). A type is of h-level −2 if it is contractible; of h-level −1 if it is a *mere proposition* (any two inhabitants are equal); of h-level 0 if it is an **h-set** (all identity types are propositions); and inductively of h-level n+1 if all its identity types are of h-level n [2].

The **n-truncation** ||A||ₙ is the universal n-type receiving a map from A, itself definable as a HIT with point constructor |–|ₙ and path constructors forcing all (n+1)-fold identity types to be trivial [2].

| h-level | Name | Character | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| −2 | contractible | a unique point, up to a unique path | the unit type **1** |
| −1 | mere proposition | at most one inhabitant | logical propositions, ∅ |
| 0 | h-set | identity types are propositions | ℕ, ℤ |
| 1 | groupoid | 1-morphisms with laws up to 2-cells | S¹, K(G,1) |
| 2 | 2-type | — | S², truncated spheres ||Sⁿ||₂ |
| n | n-type | ||A||ₙ ≃ A | ||A||ₙ for any A |


### Connectedness

A type A is **n-connected** when its n-truncation is contractible: ||A||ₙ ≃ **1**. A map f : A → B is **n-connected** when every fiber fib_f(b) := Σ_{a:A} (f(a) = b) is n-connected [2]. The low cases are familiar under other names:

- (−1)-connected maps are precisely the **surjections** (surjective on π₀).
- 0-connected maps induce surjections on π₀ with connected fibers.
- The n-sphere Sⁿ is (n−1)-connected [2] — a fact whose synthetic proof already uses the Freudenthal machinery in miniature.


### Loop spaces and homotopy groups

For a pointed type (A, a), the **loop space** Ω(A, a) := (a = a) carries concatenation as multiplication and path reversal as inversion. Iterating gives ΩⁿA, and the **n-th homotopy group** is the set-truncation

> πₙ(A, a) := ||Ωⁿ(A, a)||₀,


### The encode–decode stepping stone

The synthetic method was born in the calculation of π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ [1]: one defines a fibration over the circle by recursion, shows `encode` and `decode` are inverse equivalences, and concludes (base = base) ≃ ℤ. The *idea* — analyze a path space via a fibration with contractible total space — generalizes: wherever a classical proof studies a covering space, the synthetic proof studies a type family with a well-chosen total space [1][2].

```haskell
-- The circle as a higher inductive type
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base

-- A fibration over S¹ whose total space is contractible
code : S¹ → Type
code base      = ℤ
code (loop i)  = ua succEquiv i    -- transport along loop adds one

encode : (x : S¹) → (base ≡ x) → code x
encode x p = transport code p 0

decode : (x : S¹) → code x → (base ≡ x)
decode = S¹-ind (λ _ → refl) helixLift
```

##3 Methodology

The synthetic proofs below share a common toolkit. We describe each instrument before putting the ensemble to work.

### Higher inductive types as spaces

A **higher inductive type** is specified by point constructors *and* path constructors — identifications freely adjoined, then quotiented by the induction principle. Spheres, suspensions, joins, pushouts, and truncations all arise as HITs [2]. Their computational meaning is supplied by cubical type theories, where HITs compute definitionally; the schema of indexed cubical inductive types, with its canonicity theorem, secures the constructive status of every proof below [7].


### Fibrations, descent, and flattening

A fibration over a HIT is defined by induction on its constructors. The **flattening lemma** (Lumsdaine) computes the total space of a fibration over a pushout as a pushout of total spaces — the synthetic analogue of restricting a fibration to the pieces of a union — and it is the workhorse of the connectivity proofs: to show a map into a pushout is highly connected, one pulls back along it and flattens [2][4].


### The Blakers–Massey connectivity theorem


> **Theorem: (Blakers–Massey, synthetic form).** Suppose that for each x : X the type Σ_{y:Y} Q x y is m-connected, and dually for each y : Y the type Σ_{x:X} Q x y is n-connected, with m, n ≥ −1. Then for each x, y the **glue map** glue_{x,y} : Q x y → (inl x = inr y) is (m+n)-connected [2][4].


The synthetic proof proceeds by double induction on the pushout, analyzing the homotopy fiber of the glue map through a fibration that descent flattens into a span of fibers — exactly the situation the join connectivity theorem controls [4].

### The join construction

Rijke's **join construction** makes the join an operation on *maps*: given f : A → X and g : B → X, their join f ∗ g is the pushout of the pullback — a commutative, associative, unital operation whose idempotents are precisely the embeddings. The **join connectivity theorem** states that the join of an m-connected and an n-connected map is (m+n+2)-connected. Iterating join powers and taking the sequential colimit yields the image factorization of a map, and the same colimit *defines* the n-truncation ||A||ₙ — so the join engine underlies truncation itself [6].


```tla
---- MODULE JoinConnectivity ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES m, n
Init == m \in Nat /\ n \in Nat
JoinBound == m + n + 2
Next == m' = m \/ n' = n
(* Invariant: the join of an m-connected and an n-connected map is
   (m+n+2)-connected, and the bound is monotone in each argument. *)
Mono == \A m1, n1 \in Nat :
          (m1 >= m /\ n1 >= n) => (m1 + n1 + 2) >= JoinBound
====
```

### The James construction

For pointed X, the n-th James model JₙX is a HIT of words of length at most n with the basepoint collapsed; the inclusions form a tower whose colimit is the loop-suspension space:

> ΩΣX ≃ colimₙ JₙX.

Each finite stage has computable connectivity via the join machinery, so the tower itself *witnesses* the Freudenthal bound — and applied to X = S², it is what defines the Bruneri number in the computation of π₄(S³) [3].

### Mechanization as method

Every proof in this thesis is formalized or formalizable: Blakers–Massey was mechanized twice in Agda [4], and the James construction with the π₄(S³) computation was developed in HoTT-Agda and re-verified in Cubical Agda [3][7]. In the synthetic setting, a proof that type-checks cannot secretly appeal to point-set topology — the machine is the experiment.

##4 Deep Dive

###4.1 The Blakers–Massey Theorem and the Join of Maps

The Blakers–Massey theorem is the synthetic excision theorem. Classically, excision says that the relative homotopy groups of a well-behaved pair (X, A) depend only on a neighborhood of A; the Blakers–Massey theorem sharpens this to a *connectivity range*: if A and B are sufficiently connected subspaces covering X, the excision map is an isomorphism through a dimension computed from the two connectivities. In type theory the same content is expressed through the glue map of a pushout, and the proof is a triumph of the fibrational method.


> **Theorem: (Blakers–Massey, connectivity form).** Given maps f : C → A and g : C → B, form the pushout D = A ⊔_C B. If f is n-connected and g is m-connected, then the canonical map h : C → Σ_{a:A}Σ_{b:B} (inl a = inr b), h(c) = (f(c), g(c), glue c), is (n+m)-connected [2][4].

The synthetic proof uses double induction on the pushout with a codes fibration that descent flattens into a span of fibers — *two* distinct proofs were mechanized in Agda [4]. The theorem was later generalized to arbitrary higher topoi and modalities, with a proof inspired by the synthetic one [5].


###4.2 The Freudenthal Suspension Theorem and the James Construction


> **Theorem: (Freudenthal suspension theorem).** If X is n-connected, then η_X : X → ΩΣX is 2n-connected. Consequently the induced map π_k(X) → π_{k+1}(ΣX) is an isomorphism for k < 2n and a surjection for k = 2n [2].


> **Proposition.** For k, n : ℕ, the suspension map π_{n+k}(Sⁿ) → π_{n+k+1}(Sⁿ⁺¹) is an isomorphism if n > k+1 and surjective if n = k+1.

The synthetic proof runs through the James tower: the colimit equivalence ΩΣX ≃ colimₙ JₙX transfers the join-controlled connectivity of each finite stage to η_X [3]. Stabilization follows — for fixed stem k the system π_{n+k}(Sⁿ) is eventually constant — with the zigzag line of stability shown below.


| Stem k | Iso range | Stable value π_{n+k}(Sⁿ), n ≫ 0 |
|---|---|---|
| k = 0 | n > 1 | ℤ |
| k = 1 | n > 2 | ℤ/2ℤ |
| k = 2 | n > 3 | ℤ/2ℤ |
| k = 3 | n > 4 | ℤ/24ℤ |
| k = 4 | n > 5 | 0 |


```python
def freudenthal(n: int, k: int) -> str:
    """Stability of pi_{n+k}(S^n): iso if n > k+1, epi if n == k+1."""
    if n > k + 1:
        return "isomorphism"
    if n == k + 1:
        return "surjection"
    return "unstable"

for n in range(2, 7):
    print(f"n={n}: " + ", ".join(f"k={k}: {freudenthal(n, k)}" for k in range(4)))
# n=2: k=0: isomorphism, k=1: surjection, k=2: unstable, k=3: unstable
# n=3: k=0: isomorphism, k=1: isomorphism, k=2: surjection, k=3: unstable
# ...
```


###4.3 Whitehead Products, the Hopf Fibration, and π₃(S²)


> [–, –] : π_p(X) × π_q(X) → π_{p+q−1}(X),


> **Proposition.** For n ≥ 2, the map π_{2n−1}(Sⁿ) → π_{2n}(Sⁿ⁺¹) induced by suspension is surjective, and its kernel is generated by the Whitehead product [ιₙ, ιₙ], where ιₙ generates πₙ(Sⁿ).

The first application is the Hopf fibration. The **Hopf construction** builds, from any associative H-space, a fibration over its suspension; applied to the circle it yields S¹ → S³ → S², and the long exact sequence of homotopy groups gives π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ, generated by the Hopf map [2][3]. The Whitehead square [ι₂, ι₂] ∈ π₃(S²) is the integer ±2 — the Hopf invariant of the Whitehead square, and the seed of the Bruneri number.


###4.4 Bruneri's Number: the Computation of π₄(S³)


> J₁S² → J₂S² → J₃S² → ⋯ → ΩΣS² ≃ ΩS³


> **Definition (Bruneri number).** There is an explicitly defined β : ℤ such that π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/βℤ.


> **Theorem (Bruneri).** π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ, and more generally π_{n+1}(Sⁿ) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ for every n ≥ 3 [3].

The constructive subtlety is essential: one cannot in general extract a numeral from "∃n : ℕ" in type theory, so Bruneri *defines* β through the James tower — via smash products, the synthetic cohomology ring, the Hopf invariant, and the Gysin sequence — rather than extracting it from an abstract existence claim. The Hopf invariant narrows β to {1, 2}; computing the cohomology ring of ℂP² pins it to 2. The number is not proved to exist; it is *computed*, as the value of a closed term — later re-verified by machine in Cubical Agda [3].


###4.5 Eilenberg–MacLane Spaces and Synthetic Cohomology

Bruneri gave a strikingly compact alternative for the integral spaces — *truncated spheres* [3][8]:

> K(ℤ, n) := ||Sⁿ||ₙ,


> Hⁿ(X; G) := ||X → K(G, n)||₀,

the set of homotopy classes of maps into K(G, n). The H-space structure on the Eilenberg–MacLane spaces induces the group structure on cohomology, and a graded multiplication lifts to the **cup product** [8]. This is the machinery behind the ℂP² computation of the Bruneri number — a closed loop: truncations define the spaces, the spaces define cohomology, and cohomology computes the groups.

##5 Empirical Evidence and Formal Proofs

In synthetic homotopy theory, "empirical evidence" takes an unusual form: a theorem is *tested* by being formalized in a proof assistant. Because the synthetic proofs never appeal to point-set topology, a proof that type-checks is a proof whose every step is logically explicit — the machine is the experiment. The table below summarizes the verification status of the results developed in this thesis.

| Result | Synthetic proof | Machine-checked | System |
|---|---|---|---|
| π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ (encode–decode) | Licata–Shulman [1] | Yes | Agda (HoTT-Agda) |
| Blakers–Massey (both proofs) | Hou–Finster–Licata–Lumsdaine [4] | Yes | Agda |
| Freudenthal suspension theorem | HoTT Book §8.6 [2] | Partial | Agda (fragments) |
| Join connectivity theorem | Rijke [6] | Yes | Agda |
| James construction, Freudenthal via James | Bruneri [3] | Partial | HoTT-Agda (fragments) |
| Hopf fibration, π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ | Bruneri [3] | Yes | Agda |
| π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ (Bruneri number = 2) | Bruneri [3] | Yes | Cubical Agda |
| Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, cup product | Licata–Finster; Ljungström–Mörtberg [8] | Partial | Cubical Agda |

Three features deserve emphasis. **First**, the Blakers–Massey mechanization [4] formalized *two* complete proofs, exposing exactly where the classical argument hides uses of choice and excluded middle. **Second**, the Cubical Agda verification of π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ is a landmark: cubical HITs have *definitional* computation rules, so the proof assistant *evaluates* the Bruneri-number term to 2 — an evaluation made meaningful by the canonicity theorems of Cavallo and Harper [7]. **Third**, the synthetic cohomology development [8] shows the program scales: Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, the cup product, and the Gysin sequence form a reusable library.

The most striking datum is negative: π₅(S³) has not been computed synthetically — the Whitehead products and Toda brackets needed at the next stage resist current techniques, a precise measure of the program's frontier.

##6 Limitations

The synthetic program is powerful but bounded, and its boundaries are instructive.

**Constructive extraction is not automatic.** In constructive type theory one cannot in general extract a numeral from "∃n : ℕ, P(n)", so Bruneri *defines* β rather than merely proving its existence — proofs concluding "for some n" must be re-engineered to exhibit witnesses [3].

**Truncation destroys information.** Maps out of ||A||ₙ see only the first n homotopy groups, so arguments needing unstable information must work *before* truncating — which is why the James construction, not a truncated approximation, carries the Freudenthal proof [3][6].

**Higher inductive types are proof-theoretically delicate.** Their syntax and semantics — schemas, computation rules, coherence of higher constructors — remain active research; cubical type theory supplies computational meaning with canonicity [7], but the general schema is subtle.

**Performance and scale.** Agda and Cubical Agda formalizations are *slow* — the Blakers–Massey mechanization strains the type-checker and the π₄(S³) verification needs substantial resources [4] — correct, but not yet practical as everyday tools.

**The unstable frontier.** The synthetic theory reaches π₄(S³) and the first stable stems; the EHP sequence, Toda brackets, and the Adams spectral sequence have no synthetic counterparts yet. Whether the method can climb beyond the first unstable groups is the field's central open question.

##7 Conclusion

We have traced the synthetic reconstruction of a deep arc of classical homotopy theory. The **connectedness calculus** gives the language; the **Blakers–Massey theorem** gives excision, mechanized in Agda [4] and generalized to higher topoi [5]; the **join construction** drives the connectivity bounds and defines truncations [6]. The **Freudenthal suspension theorem** follows via Bruneri's synthetic **James construction** [3], yielding stabilization of the homotopy groups of spheres. At the edge of stability, the **Whitehead product** and **Hopf fibration** give π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ, and the same machinery, pushed one stage further, *computes* π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ — a closed term evaluated, then re-verified in Cubical Agda. Finally, **Eilenberg–MacLane spaces** turn the apparatus into a cohomology theory [8], closing the loop.

The moral is Voevodsky's: homotopy theory is not *applied* to type theory — it *is* type theory, seen from the right angle. Every result above is a theorem of logic, checkable by a machine that knows nothing of topological spaces. The frontier — higher unstable stems, synthetic spectra, a synthetic Adams spectral sequence — remains open, but the method has proved itself: where the classical topologist reaches for a spectral sequence, the synthetic homotopy theorist reaches for a fibration, a pushout, and an induction principle.
