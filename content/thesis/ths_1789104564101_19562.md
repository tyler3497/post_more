# The Quantum PCP Conjecture: Local Hamiltonians, QMA, and the Limits of Quantum Verification

## Abstract

The quantum PCP conjecture is the central open problem of quantum Hamiltonian complexity. It asserts that the *k*-local Hamiltonian problem — deciding whether the ground energy of a sum of local Hermitian terms lies below a threshold *a* or above a threshold *b* — remains **QMA**-complete even when the promise gap *b − a* is a *constant fraction* of the operator norm rather than inverse-polynomial, mirroring the classical PCP theorem's constant-fraction hardness of approximation. This thesis traces the conjecture's full lineage: **QMA** and Kitaev's 1999 circuit-to-Hamiltonian construction, the equivalence of Hamiltonian and proof-checking formulations, the 2022 NLTS theorem of Anshu, Breuckmann, and Nirkhe from good quantum LDPC codes, and the no-go theorems constraining any proof. We dissect why classical gap amplification fails quantumly, survey the games formulation and recent adaptivity results, and map precisely what a proof would require — and why the conjecture may plausibly be false.

## 1. Introduction

The classical **PCP theorem** — that every language in **NP** admits a probabilistically checkable proof verifiable by reading only a constant number of proof bits — is among the deepest results in theoretical computer science [2]. One of its equivalent formulations is a statement about *hardness of approximation*: it is **NP**-hard to decide whether an instance of MAX-3SAT is fully satisfiable or whether at most a constant fraction of its clauses are satisfiable. Dinur's 2006 gap-amplification proof, based on expander graphs, finally rendered the theorem combinatorially legible.

The natural quantum analogue was posed by Kitaev [1], Aharonov, Arad, Landau, and Vazirani, and surveyed definitively by Aharonov, Arad, and Vidick [2]: the *quantum PCP (qPCP) conjecture*. It asks whether approximating the ground-state energy of a **local Hamiltonian** to constant relative precision remains **QMA**-hard. Formally:

> **Conjecture (Quantum PCP, Hamiltonian formulation).** *It is **QMA**-complete under quantum polynomial-time reductions to decide whether a local Hamiltonian H on n qubits with m = Θ(n) terms, each acting on k = O(1) qubits with ‖hᵢ‖ ≤ 1, has ground energy λ_min(H) ≤ m/10 (YES instance) or λ_min(H) ≥ m/5 (NO instance), promised that one of the two cases holds [2].*

This is the *constant promise gap* regime. Kitaev's original result places the local Hamiltonian problem in **QMA** with only an *inverse-polynomial* gap [1], and the conjecture asks whether that gap can be amplified to a constant. Under the widely believed assumption **NP** ≠ **QMA**, the conjecture would imply that there exist local Hamiltonians whose low-energy states admit no efficient classical description from which the energy can be evaluated — a statement with profound consequences for variational quantum algorithms, quantum error correction, and condensed-matter simulation [6].

Nearly two decades after its formulation, the conjecture remains unresolved. Yet the attempt to prove it has generated some of the richest mathematics in quantum information: the NLTS theorem [3], combinatorial NLTS from qLDPC codes, gap-amplification no-go results, and the games formulation [5]. This thesis synthesizes these developments.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 QMA: The Quantum Analogue of NP

A language *L* is in **QMA** = **QMA**(2/3, 1/3) if there exists a quantum polynomial-time verifier *V* such that: completeness — for *x ∈ L* there exists a quantum witness |ξ⟩ accepted with probability at least 2/3; soundness — for *x ∉ L*, every |ξ⟩ is accepted with probability at most 1/3. An amplification argument shows the constants are immaterial: any inverse-exponential bounds separated by an inverse-polynomial gap define the same class [1].

### 2.2 The Local Hamiltonian Problem

The *k*-local Hamiltonian problem is the canonical **QMA**-complete problem:

> **Definition (k-Local Hamiltonian [1]).** *Input: integer n; real numbers a, b with b − a > n^(−c); polynomially many Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices H₁, …, Hₛ with ‖Hᵢ‖ ≤ 1, each acting nontrivially on at most k qubits. Promise: the smallest eigenvalue of H = ΣᵢHᵢ is either ≤ a or ≥ b. Output: decide which.*

Kitaev proved in 1999 that the 5-local Hamiltonian problem is **QMA**-complete, later reduced to 2-local [7], to qubits on a 2D lattice, and to 1D chains with constant local dimension. The proof's heart is the **circuit-to-Hamiltonian construction**: a quantum verifier circuit *U_T ⋯ U₁* is encoded into the ground state of a Hamiltonian built from three penalty families:

- **H_in**: penalizes incorrect initialization of input qubits;
- **H_prop(t)**: for each time step *t*, penalizes states that violate the gate *U_t* between consecutive clock states;
- **H_out**: penalizes rejecting computation branches at the final clock time.

The clock is encoded either in unary on *T* qubits or in binary, and the *history state* |η⟩ = Σ_t |ψ_t⟩ ⊗ |t⟩ (with |ψ_t⟩ = U_t ⋯ U₁|ψ₀⟩) has low energy precisely when the verifier accepts. The spectral gap of H_prop relates to the quantum walk on the clock line, yielding an Ω(T^(−3)) (later improved to Ω(T^(−2))) promise gap — *inverse-polynomial*, not constant [7].

| Formulation | Promise gap | Complexity status |
|---|---|---|
| 5-local Hamiltonian (Kitaev 1999) [1] | Ω(1/poly(n)) | **QMA**-complete |
| 2-local Hamiltonian [7] | Ω(1/poly(n)) | **QMA**-complete |
| 2D lattice / 1D chain variants | Ω(1/poly(n)) | **QMA**-complete |
| k-local Hamiltonian, constant gap | Θ(m) fraction | **Open** — the qPCP conjecture [2] |

### 2.3 The Classical PCP Landscape

Classically, the PCP theorem's hardness-of-approximation form states that MAX-3SAT is **NP**-hard to approximate within any constant factor. Dinur's proof amplifies a small gap via *powering* (random walks on expander graphs) and *alphabet reduction* (composition). The quantum analogue founders on both steps:

1. **Amplification without tensorization.** The classical tensor product amplifies classical gap because clause violations tensorize. Quantum local terms do not tensorize: H ⊗ I + I ⊗ H creates *entanglement* across copies, and the ground state of the amplified Hamiltonian bears no simple relation to the original [2].
2. **The quantum alphabet resists reduction.** Composition of quantum verifiers requires entanglement-assisted reductions that blow up locality [2].

These are not mere technical gaps — Bravyi–Hastings and later Brandão–Harrow proved *no-go theorems* showing quantum PCP statements are false on grids, high-degree graphs, and expander graphs [6], forcing any proof to exploit interaction geometries that are simultaneously expanding (for amplification) and non-expanding (to evade the no-go) — a delicate needle to thread.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is analytic and synthetic: we do not claim new theorems but rather construct the first unified map of the qPCP landscape suitable for guiding proof attempts. We proceed by (i) formalizing the three main formulations — Hamiltonian, proof-checking, and games — and their known equivalences [2,4,5]; (ii) dissecting Kitaev's construction to isolate exactly where the inverse-polynomial gap originates, since gap amplification must repair precisely this locus; (iii) presenting the NLTS theorem as the strongest unconditional evidence for the conjecture and analyzing its proof architecture via qLDPC codes [3]; and (iv) cataloguing no-go results and open sub-problems with the precision of a complexity theorist's problem list.

As a concrete illustration, the standard verification routine underlying the Hamiltonian formulation can be sketched as follows:

```python
def estimate_ground_energy(H_terms: list, witness: QuantumState, shots: int) -> float:
    """Estimate <psi|H|psi> by sampling local terms — the QMA verifier's core."""
    total = 0.0
    for _ in range(shots):
        i = random.randrange(len(H_terms))          # pick a local term uniformly
        total += H_terms[i].measure(witness)        # local measurement, O(1) qubits
    return len(H_terms) * total / shots             # unbiased estimator of <H>
```

The verifier's power rests entirely on *locality*: only *k* qubits are ever touched. The qPCP conjecture asks whether this locality check, even when allowed a *constant fraction* of slack, still captures the full power of **QMA**.

For contrast, the classical Dinur-style amplification step that has no known quantum analogue:

```haskell
-- Classical gap amplification (Dinur): powering via expander random walks
amplify :: CSP -> CSP
amplify phi = CSP
  { constraints = [ walkConstraint w | w <- randomWalks expander t ]
  , gap        = 1 - (1 - gap phi) ^ t          -- gap grows with walk length t
  }
-- Quantum obstruction: H^{\otimes t} couples copies; no gap growth follows.
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Three Formulations and Their Equivalences

Aharonov, Arad, and Vidick established that the *Hamiltonian formulation* and the *proof-checking formulation* are equivalent under quantum polynomial-time reductions [2]: a quantum verifier that reads only *q = O(1)* qubits of a polynomial-size quantum proof exists for every **QMA** language if and only if the constant-gap local Hamiltonian problem is **QMA**-complete. Fitzsimons and Vidick proposed a third, *games* formulation generalizing **MIP** = **NEXP**: that approximating the entangled value of a nonlocal game with logarithmic communication is **QMA**-hard [5]. The 2020 resolution of **MIP*** = **RE** recast the games landscape entirely, and the games qPCP conjecture was revisited accordingly — with conditional results (assuming constant-gap local Hamiltonian **QMA**-hardness) and unconditional results under randomized reductions [5].

> **Theorem (Equivalence [2]).** *The following are equivalent: (i) the constant-gap k-local Hamiltonian problem is **QMA**-complete; (ii) **QMA** admits quantum PCPs with O(1) queries; (iii) approximating local Hamiltonian ground energies to constant relative precision is **QMA**-hard.*

### 4.2 Where Kitaev's Gap Comes From — and Why It Resists Amplification

The inverse-polynomial gap in Kitaev's construction is not an artifact; it is *structural*. The propagation Hamiltonian's gap is the spectral gap of a quantum walk on the *T*-step clock line, scaling as Ω(T^(−2)). To amplify the gap to constant while preserving **QMA**-hardness, one would need to:

- *Strengthen* the clock to have constant spectral gap — but unary clocks are lines, and any local clock with constant gap requires expanding structure that breaks the history-state analysis;
- *Repeat* the computation in parallel — but the tensor product of history states is not the history state of the tensor product computation, and cross-terms entangle the copies.

This is the precise technical content of the "quantum PCP is not just Dinur with kets" folk wisdom [2].

### 4.3 NLTS: The Strongest Evidence for the Conjecture

Freedman and Hastings conjectured in 2014 that there exist local Hamiltonians with *no low-energy trivial states*: families {H_n} such that every state with energy ≤ εn (for some constant ε > 0) requires *super-constant* quantum circuit depth to prepare [3]. This is a *necessary consequence* of qPCP under **NP** ≠ **QMA**: if constant-depth circuits could approximate all low energies, then the qPCP verifier's constant-query check would be classically simulable via lightcone arguments.

The conjecture was proved in 2022 by Anshu, Breuckmann, and Nirkhe using asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes [3]. The architecture:

1. **Good qLDPC codes** (constant rate, linear distance) provide parity-check matrices H_X, H_Z.
2. **The NLTS Hamiltonian** is H = Σ_c Π_c, the sum of projectors onto violated checks — a commuting local Hamiltonian whose low-energy states are near-codewords.
3. **Small-set (co)boundary expansion** of the code implies that any state violating only a small constant fraction of checks has large *circuit complexity*: a shallow circuit's lightcone cannot correlate the errors into a near-codeword without violating expansion.

Later work extended NLTS to qLDPC codes of arbitrarily small positive rate and to strongly explicit SoS lower bounds [3]. NLTS is strictly *weaker* than qPCP — it is about circuit depth, not **QMA**-hardness — but it is the first result to realize the qPCP *phenomenology* (constant-fraction energy robustness) unconditionally.

### 4.4 Adaptivity, Multiple Provers, and the Proof-Checking Frontier

Buhrman, Helsen, and Weggemans (2024) gave a general formulation of quantum PCPs capturing *adaptivity* and *multiple unentangled provers*, with a detailed quantum reduction to a local Hamiltonian with constant promise gap [4]. Their results include: non-adaptive quantum PCPs simulate adaptive ones for constant query number (answering Aharonov–Arad–Landau–Vazirani's open question); if the constant-gap *q*-local Hamiltonian problem lies in **QCMA**, then **QPCP**[q] ⊆ **QCMA**; and if **QMA**(k) has a quantum PCP for any k ≤ poly(n), then **QMA**(2) = **QMA** — linking qPCP to the long-standing unentangled-provers collapse problem. Crucially, they also exhibit *quantum oracles* relative to which quantum PCP statements are *false*: any proof of qPCP requires (quantumly) non-relativizing techniques, exactly as in the classical case [4].

```rust
// Schematic: the BHW reduction turns a q-query quantum PCP into a local
// Hamiltonian with constant promise gap — the versatile subroutine of [4].
fn pcp_to_hamiltonian(v: &QuantumPCPVerifier, q: usize) -> LocalHamiltonian {
    let mut h = LocalHamiltonian::new();
    h.add_term(check_proof_consistency(v, q));   // O(1)-local per query pattern
    h.add_term(penalize_reject(v));              // constant gap preserved
    h  // QMA-hardness of constant-gap LH  <=>  soundness of the PCP
}
```

### 4.5 No-Go Theorems: Where Quantum PCP Cannot Live

The conjecture's plausibility is tempered by strong negative results [2,6]:

- **Grids and planar graphs.** Brandão–Harrow showed quantum PCP statements are false for local Hamiltonians on grids and sufficiently expanding graphs: product states approximate the ground energy well.
- **Commuting Hamiltonians.** Bravyi–Vyalyi-type lemmas show large classes of commuting local Hamiltonians admit trivial low-energy states.
- **Stoquastic Hamiltonians.** Aharonov–Grilo connected *stoquastic* qPCP to derandomization: a gap-amplification procedure for uniform stoquastic Hamiltonians would imply **MA** = **NP**, suggesting the stoquastic variant is either false or as hard as major derandomization conjectures.

Any proof must therefore thread an extraordinarily narrow needle: interaction graphs expanding enough for amplification, yet structured enough to evade every no-go.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

As a complexity-theoretic thesis, our "empirical" content is the body of proven theorems. We organize the key results as a dependency map:

```tla+
---- MODULE QPCPStatus ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES gapRegime, hardness
\* Kitaev 1999: inverse-polynomial gap is QMA-complete [1]
Kitaev == gapRegime = "1/poly" /\ hardness = "QMA-complete"
\* qPCP conjecture: constant gap should remain QMA-complete [2]
QPCPConjecture == gapRegime = "constant" => hardness = "QMA-complete"
\* NLTS theorem: constant-fraction low-energy states need super-constant depth [3]
NLTS == \E eps \in Nat : \A n : depth(lowEnergyStates(H_n, eps)) = omega(1)
\* No-go: false on grids / high-degree expanders [6]
NoGo == interactionGraph \in {"grid", "expander"} => ~QPCPConjecture
====
```

**Proven unconditional facts** (the "empirical" record):

1. **QMA**-completeness of local Hamiltonian with Ω(1/poly) gap: Kitaev [1]; improved to 2-local, 2D, 1D.
2. **NLTS theorem**: families of local Hamiltonians with no low-energy trivial states exist [3].
3. **Combinatorial NLTS** from low-rate qLDPC codes, plus strongly explicit SoS lower bounds [3].
4. **Equivalence** of Hamiltonian and proof-checking qPCP formulations [2].
5. **Oracle separations**: quantum oracles relative to which quantum PCP statements fail [4].
6. **Conditional games qPCP**: **QMA**-hardness of approximating entangled game values, assuming constant-gap LH hardness or under randomized reductions [5].
7. **No-go theorems** on grids, expanders, commuting, and stoquastic families [2,6].
8. **Guidable LH**: heuristic ansatz state preparation (VQE) provably cannot beat certain barriers implied by qPCP [6].

What remains unproven is exactly one implication: *constant-gap* local Hamiltonian is **QMA**-hard. Everything else in the edifice is theorem.

## 6. Limitations

We are candid about the limits of both the conjecture and this thesis. **First**, the conjecture might be *false*: the accumulation of no-go results [2,6] and the oracle separations [4] leave open a world in which constant-gap local Hamiltonians admit nontrivial approximation algorithms (perhaps via SoS hierarchies or tensor-network methods) while **QMA** remains hard at inverse-polynomial precision. **Second**, NLTS, while a breakthrough, is a statement about *circuit depth*, not about computational hardness; bridging that gap requires entirely new ideas. **Third**, this thesis proves no new theorems — it is a synthesis, and any "methodology" section in a survey must be read as analytic rather than experimental. **Fourth**, the games formulation's dependence on the **MIP*** = **RE** revolution means some older intuitions about entangled provers no longer transfer cleanly [5]. **Finally**, the physical relevance is bounded: real materials have geometric locality, precisely the regime where no-go theorems bite hardest — so even a true qPCP might speak only about artificial Hamiltonians.

## 7. Conclusion

The quantum PCP conjecture sits at the exact intersection of quantum complexity, error correction, and many-body physics. Kitaev's 1999 construction [1] gave us **QMA**-completeness at inverse-polynomial precision; the conjecture asks for the final, constant-precision step. Two decades of work have mapped the territory with remarkable precision: the formulations are equivalent [2], the NLTS theorem proves the conjecture's *phenomenology* unconditionally [3], adaptivity and multi-prover structure are now understood [4], and the games formulation connects to the deepest results in interactive proofs [5]. Yet the core implication — constant-gap **QMA**-hardness — remains unproven, guarded by no-go theorems and oracle barriers that demand non-relativizing, non-black-box techniques.

Our assessment: the NLTS breakthrough shifted the community's prior *toward* truth, but the conjecture's survival requires a gap-amplification procedure for quantum Hamiltonians that no one can currently sketch even in outline. Whether qPCP is the next PCP theorem or the next quantum counterexample, resolving it will reshape our understanding of what quantum verification can and cannot do. The most promising attack vectors are (i) *quantum Dinur* via good qLDPC codes with small-set expansion, (ii) the guidable-Hamiltonian program constraining VQE [6], and (iii) the **QMA**(2) connection of [4], which may collapse two open problems into one.
