# Score-Based Diffusion Models: Stochastic Differential Equations, Denoising Score Matching, and Classifier-Free Guidance

## Abstract

This thesis presents a rigorous, self-contained treatment of score-based generative modeling through continuous-time stochastic differential equations (SDEs), following the unification of Song et al. [4]. We derive the forward variance-preserving, variance-exploding, and sub-variance-preserving SDEs, prove the structure of the reverse-time SDE and the corresponding probability flow ordinary differential equation that shares all marginal distributions, and develop denoising score matching [1] from the Fisher divergence, showing its equivalence to the noise-prediction objective of DDPM [3]. We analyze annealed Langevin dynamics and predictor–corrector sampling as discrete realizations of these continuous dynamics, and give a Bayesian derivation of classifier-free guidance [6], interpreting the guidance weight as exponentiation of an implicit classifier. Finally, we examine consistency models [8] as amortized probability-flow-ODE solvers, discuss evaluation through Fréchet Inception Distance and exact likelihoods, and delimit the framework's theoretical and practical limitations.

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling asks a deceptively simple question: given samples from an unknown distribution $p_{\text{data}}(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, how do we produce new samples that are indistinguishable from the training data? Classical answers — likelihood maximization in normalizing flows, adversarial games in GANs, evidence lower bounds in VAEs — all suffer from a shared tension between training stability, sample quality, and tractable evaluation. Score-based generative models resolve this tension by abandoning direct modeling of the density in favor of modeling its *score*, the gradient of the log-density $\nabla_x \log p(x)$, and then sampling by simulating a stochastic process whose stationary distribution is the data distribution.

The intellectual lineage of this program is now clear. Hyvärinen's score matching [1, antecedent] showed that the Fisher divergence between a model score and the data score could be estimated from samples alone, without the partition function. Vincent [1] connected score matching to denoising autoencoders: learning to denoise is learning the score. Song and Ermon [2] scaled this insight to images with Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSNs), estimating scores at multiple noise levels and sampling with annealed Langevin dynamics. Ho, Jain, and Abbeel [3] showed that a discrete diffusion chain with a noise-prediction objective achieves GAN-competitive quality. Song et al. [4] unified all of these in a single continuous-time SDE framework, from which reverse-time SDEs, probability flow ODEs, and exact likelihoods all fall out as consequences. Classifier-free guidance [6] then provided the conditioning machinery behind modern text-to-image systems, and consistency models [8] addressed the remaining weakness: slow iterative sampling.

This thesis develops the complete theoretical edifice: the SDE framework, the training objectives, the samplers, the guidance mechanisms, and the distillation schemes that make the approach practical.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Score Functions and Score Matching

For a probability density $p(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, the *score function* is defined as

$$s(x) := \nabla_x \log p(x) = \frac{\nabla_x p(x)}{p(x)}.$$

The score is remarkable because it does not depend on the normalizing constant: for $p(x) = \tilde{p}(x)/Z$ with intractable $Z$, we still have $\nabla_x \log p = \nabla_x \log \tilde{p}$. This makes it the natural object for energy-based modeling, where $Z$ is generally intractable.

> **Theorem: Hyvärinen Score Matching.** Given samples from $p_{\text{data}}$, the Fisher divergence $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\frac{1}{2}\|s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}(x)\|^2]$ differs from the tractable objective
> $$J_{\text{SM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}\left[\text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x)) + \tfrac{1}{2}\|s_\theta(x)\|^2\right]$$
> by a constant independent of $\theta$, so minimizing $J_{\text{SM}}$ minimizes $J$.

The proof integrates by parts under mild regularity conditions. Two obstacles remain: the trace of the score Jacobian is expensive in high dimensions, and the data score is ill-defined when $p_{\text{data}}$ concentrates on a low-dimensional manifold. Both are cured by noise perturbation.

### 2.2 Denoising Score Matching

Vincent [1] proved that estimating the score of a *noise-perturbed* distribution $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}) = \int p_{\text{data}}(x)\,\mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 I)\,dx$ is equivalent to regressing a score network toward the tractable score of the perturbation kernel:

> **Theorem: Denoising Score Matching.** Under the same regularity conditions,
> $$\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}\sim q_\sigma}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\|s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})\|^2\right] = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\sigma^2 I)}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\|s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log p_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)\|^2\right] + C,$$
> where $C$ is independent of $\theta$.

Since $\nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log \mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 I) = -(\tilde{x}-x)/\sigma^2$, the objective reduces to a plain regression against the scaled noise:

$$L_{\text{DSM}}(\theta; \sigma) = \mathbb{E}_{x,\epsilon}\left[\left\|s_\theta(x + \sigma\epsilon, \sigma) + \frac{\epsilon}{\sigma}\right\|^2\right], \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).$$

Training a denoiser is training a score estimator. This is the *fundamental identity* on which all diffusion models rest [1][2]. Tweedie's formula makes the equivalence vivid: for Gaussian corruption, $\mathbb{E}[x \mid \tilde{x}] = \tilde{x} + \sigma^2 \nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})$, so the optimal denoiser directly yields the score.

### 2.3 From Langevin Dynamics to Diffusion

Given the score, sampling proceeds via the (overdamped) Langevin SDE $dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_x \log p(x)\,dt + dw$, whose stationary distribution is $p$ under standard dissipativity assumptions. Song and Ermon [2] observed that a single noise level is insufficient: at small $\sigma$ the score is accurate near the manifold but useless far from it; at large $\sigma$ it guides globally but recovers only coarse structure. Their solution — a *noise-conditional* score network $s_\theta(x, \sigma)$ over a geometric ladder $\sigma_1 < \cdots < \sigma_L$, sampled by *annealed Langevin dynamics* descending the ladder — was the first practical score-based image model [2].

The discrete-time counterpart is DDPM [3]: a forward Markov chain $q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$ and a learned reverse chain trained with the simplified noise-prediction loss $\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2]$. The noise predictor and the score are the same object up to scaling:

$$\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) = -\sigma_t\, s_\theta(x_t, t),$$

which unifies DDPM [3] and NCSN [2] as different parameterizations of one idea [4].

## 3 Methodology

We now work entirely in continuous time. Consider a forward diffusion process $\{x(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}$ defined by the Itô SDE

$$dx = f(x, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw, \tag{1}$$

with drift $f : \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$, scalar diffusion coefficient $g : [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}_+$, and standard Wiener process $w$. We choose $f, g$ so that $x(0) \sim p_{\text{data}}$ and $x(T) \sim \pi$, a tractable prior (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$). Three canonical choices, introduced in [4], are compared in the table below.

| SDE | Drift $f(x,t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | Prior $p_T$ | Discrete analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VP** (variance preserving) | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ | DDPM [3] |
| **VE** (variance exploding) | $0$ | $\sqrt{\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^2(t)}$ | $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\max}^2 I)$ | NCSN [2] |
| **sub-VP** | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)\,(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t \beta(s)\,ds})}$ | $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ | improved likelihood |

*Table 1: The three canonical forward SDEs. The VP drift is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process; the VE drift is pure Brownian motion with time-warped clock; sub-VP interpolates, provably upper-bounding the VP variance at all times and hence admitting tighter likelihood bounds [4].*

Let $p_t(x)$ denote the marginal density of $x(t)$. It evolves under the Fokker–Planck equation:

$$\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} = -\nabla_x \cdot \big(f(x,t)\,p_t(x)\big) + \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \Delta_x p_t(x). \tag{2}$$

The generative direction is supplied by Anderson's reverse-time theorem [4, Theorem 1]: for a forward SDE of the form (1), the time-reversed process $\bar{x}(t) := x(T - t)$ satisfies

$$dx = \big[f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w}, \tag{3}$$

where $\bar{w}$ is a Wiener process in reverse time. The *only* unknown in (3) is the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ at every noise level — precisely what a time-conditional score network $s_\theta(x, t)$ is trained to approximate.

**Training.** The continuous-time generalization of denoising score matching is

$$L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,T]}\,\mathbb{E}_{x(0)\sim p_{\text{data}}}\,\mathbb{E}_{x(t)\sim p_{0t}(\cdot\mid x(0))}\left[\lambda(t)\,\big\|s_\theta(x(t), t) - \nabla_x \log p_{0t}(x(t)\mid x(0))\big\|^2\right], \tag{4}$$

where the transition kernels $p_{0t}$ are Gaussian in closed form for all affine-drift SDEs (VP, VE, sub-VP). Choosing $\lambda(t) \propto 1/\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_x \log p_{0t}\|^2]$ balances the loss across noise levels; with the noise-prediction parameterization this recovers the DDPM objective in the continuous limit [4].

**Sampling.** With a trained $s_\theta$, generation means numerically integrating (3) backward from $x(T) \sim \pi$. The simplest scheme is Euler–Maruyama; the more powerful *predictor–corrector* (PC) sampler [4] alternates one reverse-SDE step (predictor) with several Langevin corrector steps using the same score network:

```python
def pc_sampler(score_fn, shape, T, num_corrector=2, eps=1e-5):
    x = torch.randn(shape) * sigma_max          # x(T) ~ prior
    timesteps = torch.linspace(T, eps, 1000)
    for t, t_prev in zip(timesteps[:-1], timesteps[1:]):
        # Predictor: Euler-Maruyama on the reverse SDE (3)
        drift = f(x, t) - g(t)**2 * score_fn(x, t)
        x = x + drift * (t - t_prev) + g(t) * math.sqrt(t_prev - t) * torch.randn_like(x)
        # Corrector: annealed Langevin dynamics at noise level t
        for _ in range(num_corrector):
            grad = score_fn(x, t)
            alpha = 2 * (sigma(t)**2) / norm(grad)**2   # SNR-adaptive step
            x = x + 0.5 * alpha * grad + math.sqrt(alpha) * torch.randn_like(x)
    return x
```

### The Probability Flow ODE

A striking consequence of (2) is that the *same* marginals $\{p_t\}$ are produced by a deterministic ODE. Eliminating the diffusion term via $\nabla_x p_t = p_t \nabla_x \log p_t$ and regrouping (2) into a continuity equation yields the **probability flow ODE** [4]

$$dx = \left[f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right] dt, \tag{5}$$

yields the **probability flow ODE** [4]: a deterministic flow whose trajectory distribution matches the SDE's marginals exactly. Equation (5) is the workhorse behind fast deterministic sampling (DDIM is its discrete counterpart), exact likelihood computation via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula

$$\log p_0(x(0)) = \log p_T(x(T)) + \int_0^T \nabla_x \cdot \tilde{f}_\theta(x(t), t)\,dt, \tag{6}$$

evaluated with Hutchinson's trace estimator, and latent-space interpolation along deterministic trajectories.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The VP SDE as the Continuous Limit of DDPM

The VP SDE is obtained by letting the number of DDPM [3] steps $N \to \infty$ with $\beta_t = \beta(t/N)/N$. With a linear schedule $\beta(t) = \beta_{\min} + t(\beta_{\max} - \beta_{\min})$ and the standard choices $\beta_{\min} = 0.1$, $\beta_{\max} = 20$ [4], the transition kernel is

$$p_{0t}(x(t)\mid x(0)) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(x(t);\, x(0)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \beta(s)ds},\, \Big(1 - e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s)ds}\Big)I\right),$$

so the variance is *preserved*: it converges to $I$ exactly, never exploding. The denoising target in (4) becomes $\nabla_x \log p_{0t} = -(x(t) - \alpha_t x(0))/\sigma_t^2$ with $\alpha_t = e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \beta}$, $\sigma_t^2 = 1 - \alpha_t^2$ — algebraically identical to the DDPM posterior, which is why every DDPM checkpoint is secretly a VP-SDE score model, and why DDPM's variational bound is a weighted integral of (4) [4].

The following Rust sketch implements the VP transition kernel exactly as used in training:

```rust
/// VP-SDE transition kernel statistics at time t in [0,1].
/// beta(t) = beta_min + t * (beta_max - beta_min)
fn vp_kernel(t: f64, beta_min: f64, beta_max: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    // Closed-form integral of the linear beta schedule
    let int_beta = beta_min * t + 0.5 * (beta_max - beta_min) * t * t;
    let alpha = (-0.5 * int_beta).exp();   // mean coefficient
    let sigma2 = 1.0 - (-int_beta).exp(); // variance
    (alpha, sigma2)
}
```

### 4.2 The VE SDE and the NCSN Connection

Setting $f \equiv 0$ gives the VE SDE $dx = \sqrt{d\sigma^2(t)/dt}\,dw$, whose solution is $x(t) = x(0) + \sigma(t) z$ with $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ — Brownian motion with a time-warped clock. With the geometric schedule $\sigma(t) = \sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t$, $\sigma_{\min} = 0.01$, $\sigma_{\max} = 50$ [4], this is precisely the NCSN noise ladder [2] in continuous time. The score target simplifies to $-(x(t) - x(0))/\sigma(t)^2$, and the annealed Langevin sampler of [2] is recovered as the corrector in the PC scheme with the predictor switched off. The VE SDE's likelihoods are typically worse than VP's (the exploding variance makes (6) numerically delicate), but its training is empirically more stable at high resolutions — a trade-off analyzed systematically in [5].

### 4.3 Annealed Langevin Dynamics: Why Annealing Is Necessary

Why not simply run Langevin dynamics on the data score at $\sigma \approx 0$? Three pathologies arise, all diagnosed in [2]:

1. **Manifold hypothesis.** If $p_{\text{data}}$ concentrates on a low-dimensional manifold, $\nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}$ is undefined off-manifold and Langevin chains started from noise never reach it.
2. **Mode isolation.** In a mixture $p = \sum_k \pi_k p_k$ with well-separated modes, the score in low-density regions is exponentially small; chains mix in geological time.
3. **Score misestimation.** In regions with no training data, a neural score is unconstrained — it extrapolates arbitrarily, and Langevin steps there are meaningless.

Perturbation solves all three: at large $\sigma$, $q_\sigma$ has full support with a well-defined, learnable score, and annealing $\sigma_L \to \cdots \to \sigma_1$ transfers the chain from the smoothed distribution to the true one. The geometric ladder in the original NCSN [2] is a discretization of this corrector chain; the modern view [4][5] is that predictor–corrector integration of (3) achieves the same effect with far fewer steps and principled step-size control.

### 4.4 Classifier-Free Guidance as Implicit Classifier Exponentiation

Classifier guidance [7] modifies sampling with the gradient of an auxiliary noisy classifier: $\tilde{s}_\theta(x_t, t, c) = s_\theta(x_t, t, c) + w\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p_\phi(c \mid x_t)$, which by Bayes' rule corresponds to sampling from $\tilde{p}(x_t \mid c) \propto p(x_t \mid c)\,p(c \mid x_t)^w$. Ho and Salimans [6] observed that the classifier's gradient can be rewritten *without any classifier*:

$$\nabla_{x_t}\log p(c \mid x_t) = \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid c) - \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t),$$

i.e., the difference between the conditional and unconditional scores. Training a single network on both (dropping the condition with probability $p_{\text{uncond}} \approx 0.1$–$0.2$) and forming at sampling time

$$\tilde{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\,\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big) \tag{7}$$

exactly implements classifier guidance with an *implicit* classifier — no auxiliary model [6]. The weight $w$ extrapolates *beyond* the conditional prediction ($w > 1$), sharpening the implied distribution at the cost of diversity; typical text-to-image systems use $w \in [5, 15]$ [5][6]. Setting $w = 1$ recovers pure conditional sampling, $w = 0$ the unconditional model.

### 4.5 Consistency Models: Amortizing the Probability Flow ODE

Iterative integration of (3) or (5) requires tens to hundreds of network evaluations per sample. Consistency models [8] amortize this cost by learning the *entire trajectory map* of the probability flow ODE: a function $f_\theta(x_t, t)$ satisfying the **self-consistency property** $f_\theta(x_t, t) = f_\theta(x_{t'}, t')$ for all $t, t'$ on the same PF-ODE trajectory, with boundary condition $f_\theta(x_\varepsilon, \varepsilon) = x_\varepsilon$. A single evaluation $f_\theta(x_T, T)$ maps noise directly to data. Training proceeds by **consistency distillation** from a teacher ODE or by standalone **consistency training** from data [8]. One-step consistency models reach FID 3.55 on CIFAR-10 and 6.20 on ImageNet-64, with multistep refinement trading compute for quality [8].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

**Unification theorem.** The central result of [4] is that NCSN [2] and DDPM [3] are Euler–Maruyama discretizations of the VE and VP SDEs, and that the DDPM variational bound equals a time-weighted integral of the denoising score matching objective (4). The proof writes the discrete ELBO as a sum of KL divergences between forward and reverse kernels, takes the continuous-time limit, and applies Girsanov's theorem to express the path-space KL as an expected squared score error. A corollary: any SDE sampler can be applied to any pretrained DDPM or NCSN checkpoint by identifying its SDE.

**Likelihoods.** Via the probability flow ODE and (6), Song et al. [4] report 2.99 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 with the sub-VP SDE — competitive with autoregressive models — alongside FID 2.20 and Inception score 9.89. The sub-VP SDE's variance upper bound is what makes the Hutchinson trace estimator in (6) numerically stable enough to achieve this.

**Guidance trade-offs.** Dhariwal and Nichol [7] showed classifier guidance lets diffusion models beat GANs on ImageNet (FID 3.94, versus BigGAN-deep's 6.95), establishing the fidelity–diversity Pareto frontier that CFG [6] later made practical without a classifier. FID as a function of $w$ is U-shaped: small $w$ under-conditions, large $w$ over-truncates. The operating point $w \approx 7.5$ popularized by Stable Diffusion sits near the FID minimum for natural-image benchmarks [5][6].

**Distillation.** Consistency distillation [8] reduces sampling to 1–2 network evaluations with only modest FID degradation (CIFAR-10: 3.55 one-step vs. 2.51 for the teacher with 35 steps), and standalone consistency training removes the teacher entirely. Progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) offers a complementary route, halving steps iteratively, but lacks the one-step guarantee of the consistency formulation.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Slow sampling is intrinsic, not incidental.** Every sampler for (3) or (5) inherits discretization error; high-fidelity generation needs dozens to hundreds of network evaluations. Distillation [8] mitigates but does not eliminate the gap to one-step generators, and multistep consistency sampling exhibits diminishing returns.

2. **Score estimation degrades where it matters most.** The DSM objective (4) weights regions by the *perturbed* density, not by their importance to generation. Low-density regions between modes — exactly where the reverse SDE must travel — receive little supervision. This manifests as disconnected-mode artifacts and per-sample likelihood estimates that are unreliable.

3. **Guidance distorts the learned distribution.** The CFG target (7) with $w > 1$ does not correspond to *any* valid diffusion of the data distribution: it exponentiates an implicit classifier, producing over-saturated colors and reduced diversity at high $w$ [6].

4. **Likelihood computation is fragile.** Equation (6) requires the divergence of a neural vector field estimated by Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator; variance is high, and for the VE SDE the exploding scale makes the integral numerically treacherous. Reported bits/dim numbers should be read as estimates with nontrivial error bars [4].

5. **Manifold and dimensionality assumptions.** The theory assumes continuous $\mathbb{R}^d$ with smooth densities. Discrete data (text, graphs) require ad-hoc embeddings or discrete-state extensions.

6. **Evaluation metrics lag the theory.** FID measures feature-space moment matching under an ImageNet-trained Inception network — a crude proxy for perceptual quality that guidance explicitly games. Likelihood and FID can and do disagree (Table 2), and neither captures conditional alignment, the quantity CFG optimizes.

## 7 Conclusion

Score-based diffusion modeling, unified by the SDE framework of Song et al. [4], is one of the rare cases in machine learning where a single mathematical object — the reverse-time SDE (3) — explains training (denoising score matching [1][2][3]), sampling (annealed Langevin dynamics, predictor–corrector schemes, probability flow ODEs), evaluation (exact likelihoods via (6)), and conditioning (classifier-free guidance as implicit classifier exponentiation [6][7]). The framework's practical triumphs — state-of-the-art FIDs, competitive likelihoods, one-step consistency models [8] — all flow from taking the continuous-time limit seriously rather than treating diffusion as a discrete algorithmic trick.

The open frontier is clear. Sampling speed remains the binding constraint: closing the gap between one-step consistency models and full SDE integration, understanding guidance-induced distribution shift, and developing score estimators accurate in the low-density regions the reverse process must traverse. The SDE lens that unified the field's past is the right lens for its future: every improvement — new forward processes, new samplers, new guidance schemes — can be stated and analyzed as a choice of drift, diffusion, and discretization.
