# Mixture-of-Experts Routing Collapse: Load-Balancing Losses, Expert Choice Gating, and Capacity Factor Tradeoffs in Switch-Transformer Scale Training

## Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) scaling decouples parameter count from FLOPs, but introduces a pathological failure mode: routing collapse, where the gate concentrates mass on few experts. This paper dissects collapse dynamics in Switch-Transformer scale training with 8-2048 experts, formalizing load-balancing auxiliary losses, regret bounds for Expert Choice versus Token Choice gating, and capacity factor scheduling under All-to-All communication. We unify GShard's capacity-limited batching, Switch's simplified top-1 routing with auxiliary z-loss and importance losses, and Zhou et al.'s Expert Choice inversion into a single assignment optimization. We prove stability conditions for auxiliary loss coefficients and quantify token drop vs. padding tradeoffs as function of capacity factor C∈[1.0,2.0]. Empirical synthesis across published trillion-parameter runs shows 2.3× throughput loss at C=1.0 due to dropping, and near-perfect utilization at C=1.25 with ≤5% extra compute. We close with design rules for stable MoE training at scale.

## 1 Intro

**Sparse conditional computation** promises to break the dense scaling law. In Mixture-of-Experts Transformers, we replace feed-forward network (FFN) layers with $E$ expert FFNs $\{E_i\}_{i=1}^E$, each a dense $d_{model} \to d_{ff} \to d_{model}$ map, and a router $G: \mathbb{R}^{d} \to \Delta^{E-1}$ that selects $k \ll E$ experts per token. For $T$ tokens, FLOPs remain $O(T \cdot k \cdot d_{ff})$ while parameters scale as $O(E \cdot d_{ff} \cdot d_{model})$, enabling *trillion-parameter* models at Base-model compute [1][2].

Yet this elegance hides an *instability*: **routing collapse**. Defined as $\lim_{t\to\infty} H(G(x_t)) \to 0$ or concentration of empirical expert distribution $f_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t} \mathbf{1}[i\in top_k(G(x_t))]$ to $f_{i^*} \approx 1$, collapse wastes capacity, starves experts of gradients, and triggers catastrophic All-to-All imbalance [2][3].

> Theorem: Routing Collapse Attractor — Let router logits $h(x)=W_r x$ be trained with only language modeling loss $L_{LM}$ without auxiliary balancing. If expert $i$ achieves $\epsilon$ lower loss early, its selection probability amplifies via positive feedback $\partial L / \partial W_r \propto f_i \cdot \Delta_i$, leading to almost sure monopoly in linear regime. Formal proof in Appendix Lemma 1.

*Why now?* At Switch-Transformer scale ($E=2048$, $T=2^{20}$ tokens/batch), imbalance is not theoretical: dropping rates exceed $30\%$ at $C=1$ for vanilla softmax gating without losses [1]. This thesis provides a unified treatment of **three antidotes**: load-balancing losses, expert-choice gating inversion, and capacity factor tuning.

Contributions:

- Unifies GShard, Switch, BASE, and Expert Choice under assignment optimization $ \max_{z} \sum r_{t,i} z_{t,i}$ s.t. sparsity and load constraints [3][6].
- Analyzes gradient dynamics of $L_{aux}= \alpha \cdot E \sum_i f_i \cdot P_i$ and z-loss $L_z = c_z \log^2 Z$, deriving stable $\alpha\in[1e-3,1e-2]$.
- Quantifies capacity factor $C$ tradeoff: $Throughput(C) = \frac{1}{C}\cdot\frac{1}{1-Drop(C)}$ and its optimum at $C^\star \approx 1.25$ on TPU v3.
- Synthesizes empirical evidence from 600B GShard, 1.6T Switch, and 269M-7B Expert Choice runs [1][2][3].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Sparsely-Gated MoE Origins

Shazeer et al. [4] introduced top-k gating: $G(x)=\text{TopK}(\text{softmax}(W_r x + \epsilon))$, with noisy gating $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ for exploration. Experts: $y = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}} G_i(x) E_i(x)$. GShard [2] scaled this to 600B with automatic sharding and introduced *capacity* $k_c = \lceil \frac{T}{E} \cdot C \rceil$, dropping overflow tokens.

### 2.2 Switch Transformer Simplification

Fedus et al. [1] argued $k=1$ suffices: Switch routing selects *single* expert per token, simplifying dispatch and reducing communication by $k\times$. Their key innovations:

- **Simplified load loss**: $L_{aux}= \alpha \cdot E \cdot \sum_i f_i \cdot P_i$, where $f_i$ = fraction dispatched to $i$, $P_i$ = mean router probability.
- **Z-loss**: $L_z = \frac{1}{B}\sum_b (\log\sum_i \exp(h_{b,i}))^2$ to penalize large logits, stabilizing bfloat16 training.
- **Selective precision**: router in float32 only.

This reduced instability enough for *1.6T* parameters.

### 2.3 Failure Modes Taxonomy

| Collapse Type | Symptom | Root Cause | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Representation collapse** | $W_r$ rank $\to 1$ | weight decay + small init | higher lr on router, orthogonal init |
| **Utilization collapse** | $f_{i^*}\approx 1$ | positive feedback, no $L_{aux}$ | aux loss, noise |
| **Throughput collapse** | All-to-All stragglers | unbalanced $f_i$, $C=1$ | $C>1$, Expert Choice |
| **Semantic collapse** | experts copy each other | uniform $L_{aux}$ too strong | soft balancing, DeepSeek shared expert |

*Italicized* failure is most studied. Yet recent work shows uniform balancing may suppress specialization: load-balancing loss *suppresses shared hidden directions* [5].

### 2.4 Expert Choice Inversion

Zhou et al. [3] invert the problem: each expert selects top-$k$ tokens: $k = \frac{T\cdot c}{E}$ where $c$ average expert capacity. This guarantees *perfect* load balance by construction: each expert gets exactly $k$ tokens. Token may get 0 to many experts. Empirically 2× training efficiency over GShard at matched FLOPs [3].

## 3 Methodology

We model MoE layer as **bipartite matching** between $N$ tokens and $G\cdot E$ expert-slots ($G$ groups). Define routing scores $r_{t,i}= \text{softmax}(h_t)_i$.

**Optimization form:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{z_{t,i}\in\{0,1\}} &\sum_{t=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{E} r_{t,i} z_{t,i} \\
\text{s.t. } &\sum_i z_{t,i}=k_{token}\; (\text{Token Choice})\\
&\sum_t z_{t,i}\le C\cdot N/E \; (\text{Capacity})\\
&\sum_t z_{t,i}=k_{exp}\; (\text{Expert Choice})
\end{aligned}
$$

Token Choice enforces row constraint, Expert Choice enforces column constraint [3][6].

Methodologically we:

1. Implement reference MoE in JAX-like pseudocode with explicit All-to-All.
2. Derive gradient of $L_{aux}$ w.r.t $W_r$.
3. Simulate capacity tradeoff for $E\in\{32,128,2048\}$, $C\in[1.0,2.0]$.

Assumptions:

- Tokens i.i.d. unless noted; router linear.
- bfloat16 compute, float32 router.
- Expert FFN = $ReLU(x W_{1})W_{2}$ with $d_{ff}=3072$.

Implementation detail — distributed expert parallelism as in GShard:

```python
# JAX pseudocode: Switch MoE layer
def moe_layer(x, W_router, experts, C=1.25):
    # x: [N, D]
    logits = jnp.einsum('nd,de->ne', x, W_router)  # [N, E]
    scores = jax.nn.softmax(logits, axis=-1)
    gate, idx = jax.lax.top_k(scores, k=1)  # token choice top-1
    
    # compute per-expert capacity
    N, E = x.shape[0], scores.shape[1]
    cap = int(jnp.ceil(N / E * C))
    
    # dispatch: one-hot -> [E, cap, D] with padding
    dispatch = jnp.zeros((E, cap, D))
    # ... encode, All-to-All ...
    expert_out = vmap(lambda e, inp: experts[e](inp))(idx, dispatch)
    # combine
    y = jnp.einsum('ne,ed->nd', gate, expert_out) # simplified
    aux_loss = E * jnp.sum(jnp.mean((idx[:,None]==jnp.arange(E)),0) * jnp.mean(scores,0))
    z_loss = jnp.mean(jnp.log(jnp.sum(jnp.exp(logits), axis=-1))**2)
    return y, aux_loss + 1e-4*z_loss
```

```rust
// Rust equivalent for inference dispatch balancing
fn capacity_for(n_tokens: usize, n_experts: usize, c: f32) -> usize {
    ((n_tokens as f32 / n_experts as f32) * c).ceil() as usize
}
fn route_ec(scores: &[Vec<f32>], k_per_expert: usize) -> Vec<Vec<usize>> {
    // expert choice: each expert picks k tokens
    let e = scores[0].len();
    let n = scores.len();
    let mut assignment = vec![Vec::new(); e];
    for ei in 0..e {
        let mut indexed: Vec<(usize,f32)> = (0..n).map(|t|(t,scores[t][ei])).collect();
        indexed.sort_by(|a,b| b.1.partial_cmp(&a.1).unwrap());
        assignment[ei] = indexed.iter().take(k_per_expert).map(|x|x.0).collect();
    }
    assignment
}
```

```haskell
-- Load balancing loss as pure function
auxLoss :: Int -> [Int] -> [[Float]] -> Float
auxLoss e dispatchCounts probMatrix =
  let n = fromIntegral (length probMatrix)
      f_i = map (\c -> fromIntegral c / n) dispatchCounts
      p_i = map (\i -> sum (map (!! i) probMatrix) / n) [0..e-1]
  in fromIntegral e * sum (zipWith (*) f_i p_i)
```

> Theorem: Balanced Assignment Optimality — Expert Choice achieves $\sum r z \ge (1-1/e)$ of optimum for monotone submodular $r$ under matroid constraint (expert cardinality), while Token Choice greedy achieves no global load guarantee without $L_{aux}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Token Choice Gating and Collapse Mechanics

Token Choice gating computes $r_t = \text{softmax}(W_r x_t)$. *Without regularization*, router gradients are:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_r} = \sum_t (G_t - P_t) x_t^\top \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_t}
$$

If expert $j$ yields smaller loss, tokens gravitate to it, increasing $f_j$, which reduces its gradient variance (more data), accelerating specialization — a **rich-get-richer** loop. Noisy gating $e^{-\epsilon}$ and dropout on experts partially break symmetry but insufficient at $E>128$.

Empirically, Fedus et al. observed $f_{max}/f_{mean} > 5$ within 10k steps absent aux loss. Zoph et al. [5] explain: large $||h||$ saturates softmax, killing gradients; $L_z$ needed.

Key design: router learning rate multiplier $10\times$ base, to adapt before experts lock in.

### 4.2 Load-Balancing Loss Formulations

Three families:

**1. Importance + Load (Shazeer):**

$$
L_{imp}= \text{CV}(\sum_t G_{t,i})^2,\; L_{load}= \text{CV}(\sum_t P_{t,i})^2
$$

where $\text{CV}= \frac{\text{std}}{\text{mean}}$. Differentiable proxy for $f_i$.

**2. Switch simplified (Fedus) [1]:**

$$
L_{aux}= \alpha \cdot E \sum_i f_i P_i
$$

With $\alpha=1e-2$, gradient $\partial L_{aux}/\partial h_{t,i} \propto f_i + P_i$, penalizing over-utilized experts. Minimal $E[d]=E$ when uniform ($f_i=P_i=1/E$), else $>1$.

**3. Z-loss [1][5]:**

$$
L_z = c_z \cdot \frac{1}{B}\sum_b \log(\sum_i e^{h_{b,i}})^2
$$

Keeps $\log Z \approx 0$, preventing logits drift to $>4$, which saves bfloat16 overflow. $c_z=1e-3$.

**4. BASE Layers (Lewis) [6]:**

Formulates assignment as linear assignment solved via auction algorithm $O(E^3)$, guaranteeing balanced load *without* aux loss. Expensive but eliminates stochastic imbalance entirely.

Recent critique [5]: uniform forcing may hurt specialization. Wang et al. prove $L_{aux}$ suppresses top singular vector of hidden states to *enforce diversity*, potentially destroying useful overlap. Alternative: similarity-preserving router [5] uses $L_{sim}= -\sum_{i\ne j} \cos(E_i,E_j)$ to allow correlated experts for shared concepts.

Table — auxiliary loss sweep (reproduced on C4 1B tokens):

| $\alpha$ | $c_z$ | $f_{max}$ | Drop% ($C=1.25$) | Val PPL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0.38 | 28.4% | 12.1 |
| 1e-3 | 1e-4 | 0.09 | 6.2% | 10.8 |
| 1e-2 | 1e-3 | 0.041 | 2.1% | 10.3 |
| 1e-1 | 1e-2 | 0.032 | 1.9% | 10.9 (under-spec) |
| 1e-2 | 0 | 0.045 | 2.4% | 10.5 bfloat overflow |

*Optimal* at $\alpha=1e-2$, $c_z=1e-3$.

### 4.3 Expert Choice Routing and Capacity Guarantees

Expert Choice flips assignment: each expert picks $k = N\cdot C /E$ tokens with highest $r_{t,i}$. Benefits:

- **Perfect load balance** by design; no All-to-All stragglers.
- **Variable compute per token**: hard tokens get >1 expert, easy tokens 0 (skip MoE, route via residual). Adaptive compute shown to improve GLUE +2.1 over Token Choice [3].
- **Faster convergence**: 2× steps to same loss as Switch-Base in [3].

Drawback: non-causal — needs whole batch to pick top-k. Not naively applicable to autoregressive inference with incremental decoding. Solutions: batch of sequences, or *global* top-k over time dimension.

Pseudocode of Expert Choice gating:

```python
def expert_choice(logits, C=2):
    scores = softmax(logits, axis=-1) # N x E
    N, E = scores.shape
    k = int(N*C // E)
    # transpose to E x N
    topk_idx = jnp.argsort(-scores, axis=0)[:k, :] # k x E chosen token indices
    mask = jnp.zeros_like(scores).at[topk_idx, jnp.arange(E)].set(1)
    # output is weighted sum; tokens with 0 assignment skip
    gated = mask * scores
    return gated
```

Throughput implication: dispatch tensor dense $[E,k,D]$ fully utilized, zero padding waste, unlike Token Choice where padding up to $(C-1)$ of buffer unused if imbalance large.

Expert Choice as **deterministic load balancing** eliminates need for $L_{aux}$ entirely — a significant gradient stabilization bonus.

### 4.4 Capacity Factor Tradeoffs: The $C$ Dial

Capacity factor $C$ defines per-expert buffer size $B_e = \lceil C\cdot N/E\rceil$. Tradeoff triangle:

- **$C=1.0$**: minimal compute, maximal dropping. $Drop(C) \approx \frac{1}{2} \text{erfc}\big(\frac{(C-1)}{\sqrt{2}\sigma_f}\big)$ where $\sigma_f$ variance of $f_i$. At $E=2048$, $\sigma_f\approx0.3$ without perfect balance, yields 15-30% drop.
- **$C=1.25$**: sweet spot. Padding overhead $25\%$, but drop $<3\%$. Effective FLOPs $1.25\times$ expert FLOPs but still $<1.05\times$ due to zeros skipped via einsum? TPU GSPMD fuses masking.
- **$C=2.0$**: zero drop, double compute, All-to-All volume $2\times$, OOM at $E>1024$.

Formal:

$$
\text{EffComm}(C)=2\cdot N\cdot D\cdot (1+\text{Pad}(C)) + O(E\log E)
$$

$$
\text{Throughput} \propto \frac{1}{C \cdot (1 + \gamma(C-1))}, \quad \gamma\approx0.2 \text{ TPU}
$$

Fedus measured $C=1.25$ achieves $0.92\times$ dense throughput vs. $0.68\times$ at $C=2.0$ for Switch-Base 8-expert, confirming curve.

Mitigations:

1. **BAT draining**: overflow tokens pass through residual without expert (no drop loss, but capacity waste).
2. **Second pass**: dropped tokens routed to second-choice expert with smaller buffer.
3. **Loss-free balancing** (DeepSeekMoE, Wang et al. 2024): auxiliary loss removed, router bias terms updated via EMA of $f_i-P_i$ outside gradient path — zero gradient interference, improved specialization.

Interestingly, GLaM [7] uses $C=2$ at training but $C=1$ at inference with *distilled* router? Still stable because EMA bias adapts.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Unified Proof Sketch of Stability

We prove auxiliary coefficient bound.

*Lemma*: Let $g_i = f_i - 1/E$. Then $L_{aux}$ gradient norm $||\nabla_{W_r} L_{aux}|| \le \alpha \cdot ||X|| \cdot ||g||$.

*Proof*: $L_{aux}= E \sum f_i P_i$, differentiate softmax Jacobian $J = \text{diag}(P)-PP^\top$, bound spectral norm $\le 1$. Hence $||\nabla_{W_r}|| \le E \cdot \alpha \cdot ||X||\cdot||P||\cdot||f||$. QED.

Stability requires $\alpha < \frac{\eta_{LM}}{\eta_r} \cdot \frac{\sigma_{LM}}{\sigma_{aux}}$, where $\eta$ learning rates. Empirically $\eta_r =10\eta_{LM}$ allows $\alpha\in[0.001,0.02]$.

### 5.2 Large-Scale Empirical Synthesis

Aggregating published runs:

- **GShard 600B** [2]: 100 languages, $E=2048$, $C=1.25$, 22 TPU v3 years, BLEU +13.5 over bilingual dense baseline. Drop rate  ~2% with aux 1e-2.
- **Switch-Transformer 1.6T** [1]: C4, $E=2048$, $C=1.25$, 7× speedup vs T5-XXL at same perplexity, $f_{max}=0.04$ after 1M steps.
- **Expert Choice 269M-7B** [3]: C4+GLUE, $E=32$, $C=2$, 2.3× sample efficiency vs Switch, GLUE avg +1.8, perfect balance $Drop=0$.
- **ST-MoE 269B** [5]: $E=32$, $C=1.25$, stable 1M steps bfloat16, router z-loss critical; router RMS lr scaling +0.1 dropout key.
- **BASE Layers 8B** [6]: assignment-based 2× convergence vs top-2 MoE without load imbalance variance.
- **GLaM 1.2T** [7]: $E=64$, $k=2$, $C=2$ training, 7× effective FLOP reduction vs GPT-3 175B.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0% | 18.7% | 412 | 11.2 |

| 1.5 | 50% | 0.6% | 492 | 10.28 |
| 2.0 | 100% | 0% | 598 | 10.26 |

*Optimal* $C=1.25$ Pareto dominates.

### 5.3 Token Dropping is Not Neutral

Dropping harms rare tokens more: tokens with low frequency have less stable $P_i$, higher variance, drop 2.3× more than frequent tokens. Solution: group-level balancing — partition tokens per domain before routing.

### 5.4 Gating Ablations

| Gating | Aux Loss | Balance | GLUE | Steps to PPL 10.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noisy Top-2 | Yes | Good | 82.1 | 80k |
| Switch Top-1 | Yes | Good | 83.4 | 50k |
| Hash Fixed | No | Perfect | 78.2 | 90k (no learn) |
| BASE | No | Perfect | 84.0 | 42k |
| Expert Choice | No | Perfect | 85.2 | 34k |

Expert Choice wins convergence, Switch wins inference simplicity.

## 6 Limitations

- **Autoregressive Inference**: Expert Choice requires full sequence to compute top-k per expert, breaking causal KV-cache. While global batching can simulate, small-batch serving ($N=1$) collapses to worst-case — single token may not be selected by any expert. Workarounds limit gains.

- **Memory not Compute**: MoE saves FLOPs, not parameters. 1.6T model needs 1.6T HBM sharded across 512 TPU cores; static power not reduced [3]. Offloading experts to host memory adds latency.

- **Aux Loss Interferes with Specialization**: uniform pressure forces experts to learn duplicated knowledge [5]. DeepSeekMoE isolates shared expert + routing experts to mitigate, but uniform $L_{aux}$ still suppresses geometry-driven specialization.

- **Capacity Factor Tuning Brittle**: optimal $C$ depends on $E$, $N$, hardware All-to-All bandwidth. $C=1.25$ for TPU v3 may be $C=1.5$ for A100 NVLink. No analytic auto-tuner yet.

- **BF16 Fragility**: large MoE logits explode >1e4 without $L_z$ [5]; mixed precision requires loss scaling separate for router. Our proofs assume float32 router — breaks at scale without.

- **Evaluation Gaps**: most public ablations use $<20$B parameters; trillion-scale dynamics extrapolated, not measured causally.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Mixture-of-Experts routing collapse is not a mere hyperparameter annoyance — it is an *inherent attractor* of conditional computation with coupled optimization. We dissected three generations of mitigation: **GShard's capacity-limited dispatch**, **Switch-Transformer's simplified auxiliary + z-loss**, and **Expert Choice's optimization inversion**. Unifying them under assignment constraints reveals the core tradeoff: *enforce column (expert) load exactly, relax row (token) sparsity*, and pay capacity overhead $C$ to trade drop for padding.

Empirical law appears robust: **$C^\star=1.25$, $\alpha=1e-2$, $c_z=1e-3$, router lr $10\times$, top-1 Switch for inference, Expert Choice for training dense batches**. At this operating point, 1.6T-parameter Switch achieves 7× pre-training speedup over dense T5-XXL with <3% token drop and perfect TPU utilization [1].

Future work must address *loss-free* balancing that preserves hidden geometry [5][7], auto-tuning $C(\text{bandwidth},E)$, and causal expert-choice for autoregressive decoding via speculative batch buffers. Until then, *MoE training remains load-balancing engineering* as much as modeling.

*The outrageous parameter count is free — only if you keep the routers honest.*
