# Itô Stochastic Calculus and Stochastic Differential Equations: Quadratic Variation, the Itô Formula, Girsanov's Theorem, and Strong and Weak Solutions

## Abstract

Itô's stochastic calculus extends Newtonian calculus to paths of unbounded variation, providing the rigorous language for diffusion processes, mathematical finance, and stochastic control. The central insight — that Brownian motion accumulates quadratic variation at unit rate — forces a correction term into the chain rule, the celebrated Itô formula. This article develops the theory from the construction of the Itô integral through the martingale representation theorem, Girsanov's change of measure, and the Feynman–Kac connection between stochastic differential equations and parabolic partial differential equations. We treat strong and weak existence and uniqueness, the Stroock–Varadhan martingale problem, and the Yamada–Watanabe principle, and we survey applications from Black–Scholes option pricing to stochastic filtering. Numerical methods (Euler–Maruyama, Milstein) and their convergence orders are discussed, along with the boundary between Itô and Stratonovich interpretations.

## 1 Introduction

Ordinary calculus fails for Brownian motion: its paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable, of infinite variation on every interval. Yet they possess a subtler regularity — **quadratic variation** accumulates deterministically. Itô's genius was to build an integral that respects this structure: the **Itô integral** $\int_0^t H_s\, dB_s$, defined for adapted integrands via $L^2$ isometry, is a martingale, and the chain rule acquires a second-order correction [1][2].

> **Theorem (Itô formula):** If $X$ is an Itô process $dX_t = \mu_t\, dt + \sigma_t\, dB_t$ and $f$ is twice continuously differentiable, then $f(X_t)$ is again an Itô process with $df(X_t) = f'(X_t)\, dX_t + \tfrac12 f''(X_t)\, d\langle X \rangle_t$, where $d\langle X \rangle_t = \sigma_t^2\, dt$ [2].

The extra term $\tfrac12 f''\, d\langle X \rangle$ is the signature of stochastic calculus — heuristically, "$dB_t^2 = dt$". This article develops Itô calculus as a complete theory: integral, formula, representation, change of measure, equations, and applications.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Brownian motion and quadratic variation

A **standard Brownian motion** $B$ is a continuous martingale with $B_0 = 0$ and independent Gaussian increments $B_t - B_s \sim N(0, t-s)$. Its paths have infinite first variation but finite **quadratic variation**: for partitions with mesh tending to zero,
$$\sum_i (B_{t_{i+1}} - B_{t_i})^2 \longrightarrow t \quad \text{in } L^2 \text{ and a.s.}$$
The limit defines $\langle B \rangle_t = t$. More generally, a continuous local martingale $M$ has quadratic variation $\langle M \rangle$, the unique increasing process making $M^2 - \langle M \rangle$ a local martingale [2][3]. Quadratic variation is the clock by which stochastic calculus runs: the Dambis–Dubins–Schwarz theorem represents every continuous local martingale as a time-changed Brownian motion, $M_t = \tilde B_{\langle M \rangle_t}$.

### 2.2 Construction of the Itô integral

For a simple adapted integrand $H = \sum_i \xi_i \mathbf{1}_{(t_i, t_{i+1}]}$ with $\xi_i$ being $\mathcal{F}_{t_i}$-measurable, the integral is defined algebraically: $\int_0^t H_s\, dB_s = \sum_i \xi_i (B_{t_{i+1}\wedge t} - B_{t_i\wedge t})$. The **Itô isometry**
$$\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_0^T H_s\, dB_s\right)^2\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T H_s^2\, ds\right]$$
extends the integral by density to all adapted $H$ with $\mathbb{E}\int_0^T H_s^2\, ds < \infty$, and by localization to $\int_0^T H_s^2\, ds < \infty$ almost surely [1][2]. Crucially, the integrand is evaluated at the *left* endpoint — this non-anticipativity makes the integral a martingale, unlike the Stratonovich integral which evaluates at the midpoint and obeys the ordinary chain rule.

### 2.3 Itô processes and the Itô formula

An **Itô process** has the form $X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t \mu_s\, ds + \int_0^t \sigma_s\, dB_s$. Its quadratic variation is $\langle X \rangle_t = \int_0^t \sigma_s^2\, ds$, and its covariation with another Itô process $Y$ is $\langle X, Y \rangle_t = \int_0^t \sigma^X_s \sigma^Y_s\, ds$. The **Itô formula** (multidimensional version: $df = \nabla f \cdot dX + \tfrac12 \mathrm{tr}(\sigma\sigma^\top D^2 f)\, dt$) follows from Taylor expansion to second order, with the second-order term surviving because $(dB_t)^2 \sim dt$ [2].

```python
# Pseudocode: Euler-Maruyama simulation of geometric Brownian motion
import numpy as np
def euler_maruyama(mu, sigma, X0, T, N, M):
    dt = T / N
    dB = np.sqrt(dt) * np.random.randn(M, N)      # Brownian increments
    X = np.empty((M, N + 1)); X[:, 0] = X0
    for n in range(N):
        X[:, n+1] = X[:, n] + mu*X[:, n]*dt + sigma*X[:, n]*dB[:, n]
    return X   # strong order 1/2, weak order 1
```

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Martingale representation and Girsanov

The **martingale representation theorem** states that every martingale $M$ in the Brownian filtration can be written $M_t = M_0 + \int_0^t H_s\, dB_s$ for a unique adapted $H$ — the Brownian filtration has the predictable representation property [2][3]. This is the foundation of hedging in finance: every contingent claim is replicable.

**Girsanov's theorem** describes how Brownian motion transforms under an equivalent change of measure [4]. If $d\mathbb{Q} = Z_T\, d\mathbb{P}$ with $Z_t = \mathcal{E}(\int \theta\, dB)_t$ the stochastic exponential, then $\tilde B_t = B_t - \int_0^t \theta_s\, ds$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion. Drift is not intrinsic — it depends on the measure. The Novikov condition $\mathbb{E}[\exp(\tfrac12\int_0^T \theta_s^2\, ds)] < \infty$ guarantees $Z$ is a true martingale. In finance, Girsanov produces the **risk-neutral measure** under which discounted asset prices are martingales, and option prices become discounted expected payoffs [7].

### 3.2 Stochastic differential equations: strong solutions

An **SDE** $dX_t = b(X_t)\, dt + \sigma(X_t)\, dB_t$, $X_0 = x$, is shorthand for the integral equation $X_t = x + \int_0^t b(X_s)\, ds + \int_0^t \sigma(X_s)\, dB_s$. Under global Lipschitz conditions on $b, \sigma$, Picard iteration yields a unique **strong solution** — a process adapted to the Brownian filtration [2][6]. The proof mirrors ODE theory, with the Itô isometry replacing the deterministic integral estimate and Gronwall's lemma closing the argument.

**Yamada–Watanabe** theory refines this: pathwise uniqueness plus weak existence implies strong existence and uniqueness in law [6]. Celebrated one-dimensional results give pathwise uniqueness under mere Hölder continuity of $\sigma$ (Yamada–Watanabe) or even the Engelbert–Schmidt conditions.

### 3.3 Weak solutions and the martingale problem

A **weak solution** allows the probability space and Brownian motion to be constructed along with $X$. Stroock and Varadhan's **martingale problem** reformulates the SDE: find a measure $\mathbb{P}^x$ under which $f(X_t) - f(x) - \int_0^t Lf(X_s)\, ds$ is a martingale for all smooth $f$, where $L = b \cdot \nabla + \tfrac12 \mathrm{tr}(\sigma\sigma^\top D^2)$ is the generator [5]. Under bounded measurable coefficients with uniform ellipticity, the martingale problem is well-posed — weak existence and uniqueness hold even when strong solutions fail (e.g., Tanaka's SDE $dX = \mathrm{sgn}(X)\, dB$, which has weak but no strong solution) [5][6].

### 3.4 Feynman–Kac and PDE connections

The **Feynman–Kac formula** represents solutions of parabolic PDEs probabilistically: for $\partial_t u + Lu = 0$ with terminal condition $u(T, \cdot) = g$,
$$u(t, x) = \mathbb{E}^x\left[g(X_T)\right],$$
where $X$ solves the SDE with generator $L$ [2][5]. This duality runs both ways: PDE regularity theory (Schauder estimates, Harnack inequalities) yields properties of diffusions, while probabilistic methods (coupling, Girsanov) prove PDE estimates. The Black–Scholes PDE is the prime applied example: the option price satisfies a parabolic equation whose probabilistic solution is the risk-neutral expectation [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Local time and the Tanaka formula

Brownian **local time** $L^a_t$ measures the density of occupation at level $a$: $\int_0^t f(B_s)\, ds = \int f(a) L^a_t\, da$ (occupation density formula). The **Tanaka formula** extends Itô's formula to the non-smooth function $|\cdot|$:
$$|B_t - a| = |B_0 - a| + \int_0^t \mathrm{sgn}(B_s - a)\, dB_s + L^a_t.$$
Local time appears as the correction for the kink — it is the continuous increasing process in the Doob–Meyer decomposition of the submartingale $|B - a|$ [2][3]. The Ray–Knight theorems describe the law of $a \mapsto L^a$ as squared Bessel processes, linking local times to branching processes.

### 4.2 Stochastic exponentials and change of numéraire

The **stochastic exponential** $\mathcal{E}(M)_t = \exp(M_t - \tfrac12\langle M \rangle_t)$ of a continuous local martingale $M$ is always a local martingale; it is a true martingale under Novikov's or Kazamaki's condition [3][4]. In finance, choosing different **numéraires** (e.g., the money-market account vs. a zero-coupon bond) corresponds to different equivalent martingale measures, and Girsanov's theorem computes the dynamics under each — the change-of-numéraire toolkit prices interest-rate derivatives [7].

### 4.3 Malliavin calculus: differentiation on Wiener space

**Malliavin calculus** differentiates random variables with respect to the Brownian path, giving an integration-by-parts formula on Wiener space. The Malliavin derivative $D_t F$ and the Skorokhod integral (its adjoint, extending Itô's integral to anticipating integrands) yield criteria for absolute continuity of laws: if the Malliavin covariance matrix is invertible, the law of $F$ has a smooth density. This provides a purely probabilistic proof of Hörmander's hypoellipticity theorem [3].

### 4.4 Stochastic control and backward SDEs

In **stochastic optimal control**, the value function satisfies the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation, and the verification theorem connects optimal feedback controls to PDE solutions. **Backward SDEs** (Pardoux–Peng), $dY_t = -f(t, Y_t, Z_t)\, dt + Z_t\, dB_t$ with terminal condition $Y_T = \xi$, run time backward: they represent semilinear PDEs via a nonlinear Feynman–Kac formula and underlie modern approaches to high-dimensional PDEs through deep learning (the deep BSDE method) [2].

### 4.5 Itô vs. Stratonovich and rough paths

The **Stratonovich integral** $\int H \circ dB$, defined via midpoint Riemann sums, obeys the ordinary chain rule and is the correct limit of smooth approximations (Wong–Zakai theorem) — natural for physics. It relates to Itô's via $\int H \circ dB = \int H\, dB + \tfrac12\langle H, B\rangle$. **Rough path theory** (Lyons) lifts this further: the path plus its iterated integrals (the "signature") determine SDE solutions pathwise, giving a deterministic solution theory robust to approximations and the foundation for regularity structures [3].

## 5 Empirical and Computational Aspects

| Scheme | Strong order | Weak order | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Euler–Maruyama | $1/2$ | $1$ | Simplest; unstable for stiff SDEs |
| Milstein | $1$ | $1$ | Adds $\tfrac12\sigma\sigma'(dB^2 - dt)$ correction |
| Stochastic Runge–Kutta | $1$ | $2$ | Higher weak order for expectations |

Strong convergence controls pathwise error; weak convergence controls errors of expectations $\mathbb{E}[g(X_T)]$ — the relevant notion for option pricing. Multilevel Monte Carlo (Giles) combines coarse and fine discretizations to achieve $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ complexity for root-mean-square error $\epsilon$, optimal up to logarithms. For high-dimensional semilinear PDEs, deep BSDE methods parametrize $(Y, Z)$ by neural networks and optimize the terminal condition mismatch [8].

## 6 Limitations

- **Beyond semimartingales:** Fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index $H \neq 1/2$ is not a semimartingale; Itô integration fails and rough-path or Skorokhod techniques are needed.
- **Jumps:** Lévy-driven SDEs require Itô's formula with jump corrections $\sum [f(X_{s-} + \Delta X_s) - f(X_{s-}) - f'(X_{s-})\Delta X_s]$; the martingale representation needs compensated jump measures.
- **Non-Lipschitz coefficients:** Superlinear growth (e.g., the $3/2$ model in finance) can cause explosions or non-uniqueness; tamed schemes restore convergence.
- **Filtration dependence:** The Itô integral's martingale property is filtration-relative; insider-trading models with enlarged filtrations need anticipating calculus.

## 7 Conclusion

Itô calculus turns the unruly paths of Brownian motion into a domain as orderly as smooth calculus — provided one pays the quadratic-variation toll in the chain rule. From the Itô integral's $L^2$ construction through Girsanov's change of measure to the martingale problem's weak solutions, the theory exhibits a rare unity: every analytic operation has a probabilistic meaning, and every probabilistic question admits an analytic formulation. Whether pricing derivatives, filtering signals, or proving hypoellipticity, the practitioner returns to the same foundations Itô laid in 1944 [1]: the integral, the formula, and the deep fact that $dB_t^2 = dt$.
