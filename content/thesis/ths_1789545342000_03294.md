# Self-Supervised Representation Learning: Contrastive Objectives, Masked Modeling, and the InfoNCE Lower Bound

## Abstract

Self-supervised representation learning has displaced purely supervised pretraining as the dominant paradigm for obtaining transferable visual and linguistic features. This thesis presents a unified account of the field's two principal families: discriminative contrastive methods that minimize a noise-contrastive estimation objective, and generative masked-modeling methods that reconstruct corrupted inputs. We derive the InfoNCE objective from the principle of noise-contrastive estimation and prove that it lower-bounds mutual information, tightening as the number of negative samples grows. We then dissect the engineering that made contrastive learning scale — the NT-Xent loss of SimCLR, the momentum encoder and dynamic dictionary of MoCo — and the surprising discovery that negatives are not strictly necessary: BYOL and SimSiam avoid collapse through architectural asymmetry and the stop-gradient mechanism, while Barlow Twins and VICReg prevent collapse through explicit redundancy reduction. Finally, we connect masked autoencoders (MAE) to contrastive learning through mask-induced alignment, analyze dimensional collapse as the field's central theoretical concern, and review joint-embedding predictive architectures (I-JEPA) as the emerging synthesis.

##1 Introduction

Modern deep learning was, for most of its history, an exercise in supervision. Architectures from AlexNet to the ResNet family were trained with hand-labeled objectives, and the field's progress was paced by the rate at which humans could annotate images, transcribe speech, and translate text. Self-supervised learning (SSL) rejects this dependency. Rather than learning from labels, SSL manufactures a *pretext task* from the data itself: predict a missing patch, discriminate an image from its distorted doppelgängers, or align two augmented views of the same observation. The encoder trained on such a task yields representations that transfer, with minimal labeled data, to classification, detection, segmentation, and beyond.

The intellectual history of the field converges on a deceptively simple question: *what should the geometry of a learned representation look like?* Contrastive learning answers that similar things should be near and dissimilar things far. Generative approaches answer that representations should contain enough information to reconstruct what was hidden. Non-contrastive methods argue that similarity alone suffices, provided collapse — the degenerate constant solution — can be held at bay.

This thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 establishes the background: noise-contrastive estimation, the InfoMax principle, and the mutual-information estimation landscape. Section 3 derives the InfoNCE objective and the SimCLR/MoCo methodology. Section 4 offers a deep dive into five pillars of the field: the InfoNCE lower bound, contrastive architectures, non-contrastive learning, redundancy reduction and dimensional collapse, and masked modeling with I-JEPA. Section 5 presents empirical results and formal proofs. Section 6 catalogs limitations, and Section 7 concludes with a synthesis of where the field stands.

##2 Background

### 2.1 The Label Bottleneck and the Promise of Self-Supervision

Supervised learning's appetite for labels is both its strength and its ceiling. Labeling ImageNet's 14 million images required years of coordinated human effort, and domains such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and low-resource languages offer no such luxury. Self-supervised learning exploits the *intrinsic structure* of data — spatial coherence in images, temporal continuity in video, distributional regularity in language — to generate supervisory signals for free. The resulting pretraining scales with compute and data rather than with annotation budgets, which is precisely why SSL became the substrate of the foundation-model era.

> **Lemma:** *A representation that is invariant to semantics-preserving transformations and discriminative with respect to semantics-changing ones is sufficient, under mild regularity conditions, to solve any downstream task whose label is a function of the invariant factors.*

This lemma — more folklore than formal theorem in much of the literature — captures the design philosophy of view-based SSL: augmentations define an equivalence relation on inputs, and the encoder is trained to respect it.

### 2.2 Noise-Contrastive Estimation

Long before "self-supervised learning" was a field, Gutmann and Hyvärinen's noise-contrastive estimation (NCE) offered a way to train unnormalized probabilistic models [1]. Rather than computing an intractable partition function, NCE recasts density estimation as binary discrimination between observed data and samples drawn from a known noise distribution. The model learns a score function proportional to the density ratio between data and noise, and this ratio is exactly what contrastive representation learning will later exploit.

Contrastive predictive coding (CPC), introduced by van den Oord, Li, and Vinyals, married NCE to representation learning across modalities — speech, images, text, and reinforcement learning [1]. CPC encodes observations into latents, aggregates them into a context with an autoregressive model, and trains the system to identify the true future latent among negatives. The loss it introduced — **InfoNCE** — would become the field's workhorse.

### 2.3 The InfoMax Principle and Mutual Information Estimation

The InfoMax principle, due to Linsker, states that a representation should maximize the mutual information between input and output subject to constraints. CPC operationalized this: maximizing the InfoNCE objective maximizes a lower bound on the mutual information between context and future observations [1]. Later work sharpened this connection into a rigorous estimator. The InfoNCE estimator, the NWJ (Nguyen–Wainwright–Jordan) estimator, and related variational bounds form a family of lower bounds on mutual information whose bias–variance trade-offs are now well characterized; notably, the InfoNCE bound becomes tighter as the number of negatives grows, at the cost of higher variance [8]. A formal proof that the InfoNCE estimator lower-bounds mutual information appears in the literature via an augmentation of the NWJ bound with auxiliary negative samples.

### 2.4 The Evaluation Protocols

A representation is only as good as its transfer. The community converged on several protocols:

1. **Linear evaluation (linear probing):** freeze the encoder, train a single linear layer on labeled data, and report accuracy. This is the gold standard for representation quality.
2. **Semi-supervised fine-tuning:** fine-tune the whole network on a small labeled fraction (e.g., one or ten percent of ImageNet) to measure label efficiency.
3. **Transfer learning:** evaluate on downstream tasks — object detection, semantic segmentation, dense prediction — often with frozen or fine-tuned backbones.
4. **kNN evaluation:** classify by nearest neighbors in embedding space with no training at all.

These protocols discipline the field: a method cannot claim victory on pretraining loss alone.

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 Deriving the InfoNCE Objective

Consider a set of N samples containing one positive drawn from the conditional distribution of a future observation given context, and N−1 negatives drawn from a proposal (typically the marginal) distribution. InfoNCE optimizes the categorical cross-entropy of identifying the positive:

$$\mathcal{L}_N = -\mathbb{E}\left[\log \frac{f_k(x_{t+k}, c_t)}{\sum_j f_k(x_j, c_t)}\right]$$

The optimal score function f is proportional to the density ratio between the conditional and the proposal distribution. Consequently, the loss is not merely a classification objective: it is a *density-ratio estimator*, and density ratios are the currency of mutual information.

> **Theorem:** *The InfoNCE objective lower-bounds mutual information: I(x_{t+k}; c_t) ≥ log(N) − L_N, and the bound tightens monotonically as N increases.*

*Proof sketch.* The bound follows from recognizing the InfoNCE estimator as a special case of the NWJ variational bound, augmented with the positive sample reused among the negatives for partition-function estimation. As N grows, the bias of the bound shrinks logarithmically, though its variance grows — the central tension analyzed in the InfoNCE literature [1][8].

### 3.2 SimCLR: A Simple Framework

SimCLR (Chen et al.) stripped contrastive learning to its essentials and, through exhaustive ablation, identified the design choices that matter [2]:

1. **Composition of augmentations:** random cropping and color distortion, applied stochastically, define positive pairs. No single augmentation suffices; the *composition* is critical.
2. **The NT-Xent loss:** for a minibatch of N images yielding 2N augmented views, the loss for a positive pair (i, j) is

$$l_{i,j} = -\log \frac{\exp(\mathrm{sim}(z_i, z_j)/\tau)}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp(\mathrm{sim}(z_i, z_k)/\tau)}$$

where sim is cosine similarity and τ is a temperature scalar. NT-Xent is InfoNCE with in-batch negatives and a temperature.
3. **The projection head:** representations are passed through a nonlinear MLP before the contrastive loss; the representation *before* the head transfers better than the one after it.
4. **Large batches:** with negatives drawn from the minibatch, batch sizes in the thousands (up to 8192) materially improve quality.

SimCLR with a ResNet-50 reached 76.5% top-1 linear accuracy on ImageNet, approaching supervised performance and demonstrating that labels were, for pretraining, optional [2].

### 3.3 MoCo: Momentum Contrast and the Dynamic Dictionary

MoCo (He et al.) observed that the number of negatives need not be shackled to the batch size [3]. It maintains a *queue* — a first-in-first-out dictionary of encoded keys from recent minibatches — and a *momentum encoder* whose parameters are an exponential moving average of the query encoder's. The momentum update keeps key representations consistent across iterations despite the queue's staleness, enabling tens of thousands of negatives on modest hardware. MoCo v2 incorporated SimCLR's MLP projection head and stronger augmentations, closing the gap without large batches [3].

| Method | Negatives | Key mechanism | Batch requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| SimCLR [2] | In-batch (2N−2) | End-to-end, shared encoder | Very large (4096+) |
| MoCo / v2 [3] | Queue (65536) | Momentum encoder + FIFO dictionary | Modest (256) |
| BYOL [4] | None | Momentum target + predictor | Moderate |
| SimSiam [5] | None | Stop-gradient + predictor | Small (even 64+) |
| Barlow Twins | None | Cross-correlation identity | Moderate |
| MAE [6] | None | Masked reconstruction | Standard ViT |

### 3.4 Collapse: The Adversary Within

Every method in this thesis fights the same enemy: **collapse**. The constant representation — mapping every input to the same vector — is a trivial global minimizer of pure similarity objectives. Contrastive methods repel it with negatives; non-contrastive methods must be subtler. Understanding collapse avoidance is understanding modern SSL.

##4 Self-Supervision: A Deep Dive

### 4.1 The InfoNCE Lower Bound and Density-Ratio Estimation

InfoNCE's elegance lies in its dual identity. As a loss, it is a simple softmax classifier distinguishing one positive from N−1 negatives. As an estimator, it targets the log density ratio between joint and product-of-marginals — the integrand of mutual information. Optimizing it therefore *maximizes a lower bound on mutual information* between views [1].

But the bound is a leaky vessel. Recent analysis of the gap between InfoNCE theory and practice shows that the bound's tightness depends on N logarithmically, while its variance can dominate in high dimensions; moreover, the representations that maximize the bound need not be the representations that transfer best, because mutual information with the *augmented view* includes nuisance information the downstream task would prefer discarded [8]. The temperature τ controls the hardness weighting of negatives: small τ sharpens attention onto the hardest negatives, large τ softens it. This single scalar mediates the alignment–uniformity trade-off.

> **Theorem (Alignment and Uniformity):** *On the hypersphere, the contrastive loss asymptotically decomposes into an alignment term, pulling positive pairs together, and a uniformity term, spreading representations over the sphere. Optimizing InfoNCE optimizes both simultaneously.*

This decomposition, established for the normalized contrastive objective, explains *why* negatives matter geometrically: without the uniformity pressure, all mass collapses to a point.

### 4.2 Contrastive Architectures: SimCLR, MoCo, and the Price of Negatives

SimCLR and MoCo are two answers to one engineering question: where do negatives come from?

Yet negatives carry a hidden tax.

### 4.3 Non-Contrastive Learning: BYOL, SimSiam, and the Stop-Gradient Mechanism

Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) stunned the field by discarding negatives entirely [4].

Exploring Simple Siamese Representation Learning (SimSiam) simplified further: no momentum encoder, no large batches — just a Siamese network with a predictor head on one branch and a **stop-gradient** on the other [5].

> **Lemma (Stop-Gradient as Implicit EM):** *With stop-gradient, the Siamese objective alternates between fitting the online network to fixed targets and updating the targets from the network — an EM-like procedure whose non-collapsed solutions are stable fixed points.*

The predictor head is essential: without the asymmetry it introduces, even stop-gradient collapses.

### 4.4 Redundancy Reduction and Dimensional Collapse: Barlow Twins and VICReg

A parallel lineage attacked collapse not with architecture but with *statistics*.

These methods expose a subtler failure mode than full collapse: **dimensional collapse**, in which representations occupy a low-dimensional subspace of the embedding space.

### 4.5 Masked Modeling and Joint-Embedding Prediction: MAE and I-JEPA

Masked Autoencoders (He et al.) brought BERT's masking paradigm to vision with two decisive design choices [6]:

1. **Asymmetric encoder–decoder:** the encoder — a Vision Transformer — processes *only the visible patches*; mask tokens are introduced late, in a lightweight decoder that reconstructs pixel values. Masking 75% of patches makes the encoder cheap (it sees one quarter of the tokens) and the task hard.
2. **High masking ratio:** at 75% masking, reconstruction cannot rely on local interpolation; it demands holistic, semantic understanding.

MAE's ViT-Huge reached 87.8% fine-tuning accuracy on ImageNet-1K, and its transfer performance surpassed supervised pretraining [6].

The Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA), proposed by LeCun and collaborators, synthesizes the traditions: like MAE it masks, but like BYOL it predicts *in representation space* rather than pixel space, using a context encoder, a target encoder with exponential moving average, and a predictor conditioned on mask position.

##5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 The Empirical Landscape

Linear evaluation on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50 unless noted) traces the field's ascent:

| Method | Year | Linear top-1 (ImageNet) | Negatives? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervised baseline | — | 76.5% | n/a |
| CPC [1] | 2018 | 48.7% | Yes |
| MoCo [3] | 2020 | 60.6% | Yes (queue) |
| SimCLR [2] | 2020 | 69.3% (76.5% w/ wider net) | Yes (in-batch) |
| BYOL [4] | 2020 | 74.3% | No |
| SimSiam [5] | 2021 | 71.3% | No |
| Barlow Twins | 2021 | 73.2% | No |
| VICReg | 2022 | 73.2% | No |
| MAE [6] (ViT-B fine-tune) | 2021 | 83.6% (fine-tuned) | No |

Two observations stand out. First, the gap between contrastive and non-contrastive methods, once thought fundamental, is empirically small — consistent with the unified gradient analysis showing little intrinsic gap between families [9]. Second, masked modeling dominates *fine-tuning* while contrastive methods historically dominated *linear probing*, suggesting the two families learn qualitatively different features: MAE captures fine-grained local structure; contrastive methods capture global semantics.

### 5.2 Proof: InfoNCE Lower-Bounds Mutual Information

We now give the central theoretical result in full.

> **Theorem:** *Let X, Y₁, …, Y_K be random variables with Y_j i.i.d., and let K−1 be the number of negative samples drawn from the marginal. Define the InfoNCE estimator with critic f. Then I(X; Y₁) ≥ I^NCE(X; Y₁), i.e., InfoNCE is a lower bound on mutual information, and the bound tightens as K grows.*

*Proof.* Augment Y₁ with auxiliary negatives Y₂…Y_K and apply the NWJ variational bound to the augmented variable. The NWJ bound states that for any critic f and any positive function a,

$$I(X;Y) \geq \mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)}[f(x,y)] - \mathbb{E}_{p(y)}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p(x)}[e^{f(x,y)}]}{a(y)} + \log a(y) - 1\right].$$

Choosing a to be the empirical partition estimate over the K samples (reusing the joint sample in the denominator, which is InfoNCE's signature deviation from NWJ) yields the InfoNCE estimator as a special case, and the inequality is inherited. The log(K) ceiling on the bound follows because a K-way classifier cannot convey more than log(K) nats of information about the positive's identity. ∎

The practical moral, emphasized in recent theory–practice analyses, is that the bound's tightness is necessary but not sufficient for good representations: the critic class, the augmentation distribution, and the temperature jointly determine which information the bound encourages the encoder to keep [8].

### 5.3 A Reference Implementation

The following PyTorch sketch implements the NT-Xent loss at the heart of SimCLR [2], illustrating how the InfoNCE bound is realized in practice:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def nt_xent_loss(z, temperature=0.5):
    # z: (2N, D) L2-normalized embeddings; rows 2k, 2k+1 are positives
    sim = torch.mm(z, z.t()) / temperature          # pairwise similarities
    n = z.shape[0]
    # mask out self-similarity
    mask = ~torch.eye(n, dtype=torch.bool, device=z.device)
    sim = sim.masked_fill(~mask, float('-inf'))
    # positives sit on the off-diagonals of each 2x2 block
    pos_idx = torch.arange(n, device=z.device)
    pos_idx = pos_idx - 1 + 2 * (pos_idx % 2 == 0)
    pos = torch.exp(torch.diag(sim[:, pos_idx]))
    denom = torch.exp(sim).sum(dim=1)
    return (-torch.log(pos / denom)).mean()
```

Note the structural similarity to cross-entropy: InfoNCE *is* classification, with the density ratio as the learned logit function.

### 5.4 Dimensional Collapse, Quantified

Dimensional collapse can be measured by the effective rank of the embedding covariance — for instance, via the entropy of its normalized eigenvalues or the count of singular values above a threshold. Empirically, MAE-trained encoders exhibit markedly lower effective rank than contrastive counterparts, and the U-MAE uniformity regularizer recovers rank while improving linear-probe accuracy on CIFAR-10, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1K [7]. VICReg's variance hinge provides the same service by construction, and the spectral unification shows that VICReg alone among major methods offers continuous control over the rank–invariance trade-off [9].

##6 Limitations and Open Problems

**The augmentation bottleneck.** View-based SSL inherits the biases of its augmentations. Random crops teach translation invariance but punish representations useful for localization; color jitter teaches color invariance that harms fine-grained recognition. The field lacks a principled theory of *which* invariances help which tasks, and augmentation design remains alchemy [8].

**Mutual information is not the target.** Maximizing a lower bound on I(view₁; view₂) keeps *shared* information, but shared information includes nuisance factors (backgrounds, watermarks, dataset artifacts) while task-relevant information present in only one view is discarded. The InfoNCE bound is a proxy, and proxies leak.

**Collapse theory is incomplete.** We have characterizations — stop-gradient as EM [5], redundancy reduction as spectral regularization [9], uniformity enhancement for MAE [7] — but no general necessary-and-sufficient conditions for collapse avoidance in nonlinear networks. The predictor head's stabilizing role in BYOL/SimSiam, in particular, still lacks a complete dynamical explanation.

**Evaluation myopia.** Linear probing on ImageNet rewards a narrow slice of representation quality. Dense prediction, robustness under distribution shift, calibration, and fairness properties of SSL features are comparatively underexplored, and different SSL families rank differently across these axes.

**Scaling laws for SSL are nascent.** Supervised learning and language modeling enjoy well-characterized scaling laws; how SSL representation quality grows with data, parameters, and negatives is far less mapped.

**The generative–discriminative divide persists.** MAE and I-JEPA reconstruct or predict; SimCLR and VICReg discriminate or decorrelate. The theoretical unification is underway [7][9], but no single objective yet dominates both linear probing and fine-tuning across modalities.

##7 Conclusion

Self-supervised representation learning has matured from a collection of tricks into a discipline with genuine theoretical footing. The InfoNCE objective — born in contrastive predictive coding as a multi-sample generalization of noise-contrastive estimation — provided the field's first unifying principle: *maximize a lower bound on mutual information between views* [1]. SimCLR and MoCo turned that principle into an engineering science of augmentations, projection heads, temperatures, and negative dictionaries [2][3]. BYOL and SimSiam then delivered the field's great surprise — that negatives, the load-bearing wall of the entire edifice, could be removed, with collapse held at bay by asymmetry and stop-gradients [4][5]. Barlow Twins and VICReg showed that explicit statistical regularization achieves the same end through redundancy reduction, exposing dimensional collapse as the deeper enemy [9]. Masked autoencoders revived the generative tradition and scaled it to state-of-the-art transfer [6], while theory revealed their hidden contrastive heart and their own vulnerability to collapse [7].

The emerging picture is one of convergence. Contrastive and non-contrastive, generative and discriminative, pixel-space and representation-space — these dichotomies are dissolving into a unified view in which every method navigates the same three-way trade-off among *invariance*, *informativeness* (rank, uniformity), and *efficiency*. The joint-embedding predictive architecture points to where the synthesis is heading: predict the missing, in representation space, without negatives and without pixels. If the last decade of SSL taught the field how to learn without labels, the next will determine what, precisely, is worth learning.
