# Compositional Program Semantics via Applied Category Theory: Algebraic Effects, Profunctor Optics, and String-Diagrammatic Reasoning

## Abstract

Category theory has evolved from a branch of pure mathematics into an engineering discipline for programming language design. This thesis develops a unified treatment of three of its most consequential applications: **(i)** *monads and algebraic effects* as the principled basis of effect systems, where effect handlers are identified with folds over the free monad induced by an equational theory; **(ii)** *profunctor optics* as a universal calculus of composable data accessors, derived uniformly from Tambara modules and the profunctor representation theorem; and **(iii)** *string diagrams* as a sound and complete graphical calculus for reasoning about program composition in symmetric monoidal categories. Our contributions are a *compositional normalization theorem* that fuses handler application with string-diagram rewriting, a syntactic decision procedure for optic lawfulness based on the dinaturality of coends, and machine-checked coherence arguments for the monoidal calculi we introduce. The thesis is grounded empirically in a Haskell reference implementation of handlers and optics, a TLA+ specification of the rewriting machinery with model-checked invariants, and measured data on handler-dispatch overhead and rewrite confluence. We conclude by delineating hard limits: coherence collapses outside strictification, full effect-row equality is undecidable, and scoped effects resist the algebraic fold semantics that make handlers tractable.

## 1 Introduction

Programming languages are *compositional artifacts*: programs are built from smaller programs, and their meanings should be built from the meanings of the parts. This requirement — compositionality — is the central demand of denotational semantics, and precisely what category theory formalizes. A category is an abstract theory of composition; everything else in the subject is elaboration of this idea.

This thesis argues that three independently developed strands of applied category theory have converged into a single coherent toolkit for program design:

1. **Monads and algebraic effects.** Moggi's insight that computational effects are *monads* [3] gave programming its first categorical account of side effects. Plotkin and Power's *algebraic effects* refined this: effects are operations of an equational theory, and the computational monad is the *free model* of that theory [1]. Plotkin and Pretnar's *effect handlers* then generalized exception handling to arbitrary algebraic effects: a handler is a *model* of the theory, and handling is the *unique homomorphism* out of the free model [1]. This single idea subsumes monad transformers, delimited continuations, coroutines, probabilistic programming primitives, and parser combinators [5].

2. **Profunctor optics.** Lenses, prisms, traversals, and their kin — collectively *optics* — are the functional programmer's answer to the problem of focusing on parts of immutable data. Milewski's observation that every optic admits a *profunctor representation* unified a sprawling zoo of types into a single interface. Román, Clarke, Elkins, Gibbons, Loregian, Milewski, and Pillmore then showed that optics are *coends* over monoidal actions, and that the profunctor representation theorem follows from Yoneda, co-Yoneda, and Tambara theory [2].

3. **String diagrams.** Since Joyal and Street's coherence theorem, the graphical language of monoidal categories has been known to be *sound and complete*: two morphisms are equal from the axioms of symmetric monoidal categories if and only if their string diagrams are equal up to planar deformation [3]. String diagrams turn algebraic proof into topological intuition, and they have been systematized as a rewriting theory in the monoidal algebra of Bonchi, Piedeleu, Sobociński, and Zanasi [4].

Our thesis is that these three strands are *mutually reinforcing*: effect handlers are folds, folds are monoid homomorphisms, string diagrams display that composition, and the profunctor representation of optics rests on the same universal-property machinery that makes handlers work.

Section 2 fixes background; Section 3 our methodology; Section 4 is the technical core (monads-as-monoids, handlers-as-folds, profunctor optics, string diagrams); Section 5 gives results and proofs; Sections 6–7 state limitations and conclude.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Categories, Functors, Natural Transformations

A **category** $\mathbf{C}$ has objects, hom-sets $\mathbf{C}(A,B)$, identities $\mathrm{id}_A$, and associative composition satisfying unit laws. A **functor** $F : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}$ maps objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving identities and composition. A **natural transformation** $\alpha : F \Rightarrow G$ is a family of morphisms $\alpha_A : FA \to GA$ satisfying the naturality square for every $f : A \to B$.


### 2.2 Monads

A **monad** on a category $\mathbf{C}$ is a triple $(T, \eta, \mu)$ where $T : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}$ is an endofunctor, $\eta : \mathrm{Id} \Rightarrow T$ is the unit, and $\mu : T^2 \Rightarrow T$ is the multiplication, satisfying the monad laws:

> **Theorem (Monad laws).** $\mu \circ T\mu = \mu \circ \mu T$ (associativity) and $\mu \circ T\eta = \mu \circ \eta T = \mathrm{id}_T$ (left/right unit).

Every adjunction $F \dashv G$ induces a monad $GF$, and every monad arises from its Eilenberg–Moore and Kleisli adjunctions. The **Kleisli category** $\mathbf{C}_T$ has hom-sets $\mathbf{C}_T(A,B) = \mathbf{C}(A, TB)$ with composition

$$g \circ_K f = \mu_C \circ Tg \circ f.$$ 

Kleisli arrows $A \to TB$ are *effectful functions*; the monad laws are exactly what make `do`-notation coherent.

### 2.3 Monoidal Categories and String Diagrams

A **(strict) monoidal category** $(\mathbf{C}, \otimes, I)$ equips $\mathbf{C}$ with a bifunctor $\otimes$ and unit object $I$, associative and unital on the nose. In a *string diagram*, morphisms are boxes, objects are wires, composition $g \circ f$ is vertical stacking, and the tensor $f \otimes g$ is horizontal juxtaposition [3]. The interchange law

$$(g_1 \circ f_1) \otimes (g_2 \circ f_2) = (g_1 \otimes g_2) \circ (f_1 \otimes f_2)$$

is then a *topological tautology*: sliding boxes along wires.

> **Theorem (Joyal–Street coherence).** In the free symmetric monoidal category on a signature, two terms denote the same morphism iff their string diagrams are related by planar isotopy respecting the braiding [3].

This theorem is the foundation of Section 4.4: it licenses us to *prove equations by drawing pictures*.

### 2.4 Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathbf{C}(A,B)$ | Hom-set of morphisms $A \to B$ |
| $T, \eta, \mu$ | Monad, unit, multiplication |
| $F \dashv G$ | $F$ left adjoint to $G$ |
| $\int^{M \in \mathbf{M}}$ | Coend over $\mathbf{M}$ |
| $\mathbf{Prof}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ | Category of profunctors $\mathbf{D}^{op} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ |
| $\mathrm{op} : A \hookrightarrow B$ | Effect operation with parameter $A$, arity $B$ |
| $\llbracket - \rrbracket$ | Denotational interpretation |

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines three mutually checking modes of rigor:

1. **Equational derivation.** Every representation theorem in Section 4 is derived by elementary categorical calculation — Yoneda, co-Yoneda, adjunctions, universal properties of free constructions — following the discipline of [2].

2. **Executable semantics.** We implemented the free monad, handler elaboration, and a profunctor-optics library in Haskell (GHC 9.6, `-O2`). The implementation is a *specification by construction*: each categorical definition has a corresponding Haskell definition, and the laws are tested as QuickCheck properties.

3. **Machine-checked rewriting.** The string-diagram rewriting system of Section 4.4 is specified in TLA+ and model-checked with TLC for confluence and termination on all diagrams up to a bounded size. The TLA+ specification is small enough to be fully displayed in Section 5.

We treat *compositionality* as our testable hypothesis: constructions agreeing on generators induce functors agreeing everywhere. Every theorem is a universal property, exercised by the implementation.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Monads as Monoids, Effects as Algebraic Theories

The endofunctor category $[\mathbf{C}, \mathbf{C}]$ is monoidal under functor composition, with unit $\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{C}}$. A **monoid** in a monoidal category is an object $M$ with multiplication $m : M \otimes M \to M$ and unit $e : I \to M$ satisfying associativity and unit diagrams. Instantiating at $M = T$:

> **Theorem (Monads are monoids).** A monad $(T, \eta, \mu)$ on $\mathbf{C}$ is exactly a monoid in the strict monoidal category $([\mathbf{C},\mathbf{C}], \circ, \mathrm{Id})$ [3].

*Proof sketch.* The monoid multiplication $m : T \circ T \to T$ is $\mu$; the monoid unit $e : \mathrm{Id} \to T$ is $\eta$. The monoid axioms unfold *syntactically* to the monad laws. ∎

In string-diagram notation, $\mu$ is a box merging two $T$-wires into one and $\eta$ creates a $T$-wire from nothing; associativity and the unit laws become the obvious topological identities. This is not a metaphor — by Joyal–Street coherence it is a *proof*.

**Algebraic effects** refine the picture. An *effect signature* $\Sigma$ is a set of operations $\mathrm{op} : A \hookrightarrow B$, read "takes a parameter of type $A$, yields a result of type $B$". An *algebraic theory* adds equations between terms built from operations. The **free monad** on $\Sigma$ is

```haskell
data Free sig a = Pure a | Op (sig (Free sig a))
```

with the evident monad instance. Plotkin and Power's correspondence states that the monad induced by the *free model* of the theory is the computational monad for the effect [1]:

- *Exceptions*: $\mathrm{raise} : E \hookrightarrow 0$ with no equations; the free monad is $A + E$.
- *State*: $\mathrm{get} : 1 \hookrightarrow S$, $\mathrm{put} : S \hookrightarrow 1$ with the four standard interaction equations; the free monad is $S \to (A \times S)$.
- *Nondeterminism*: $\mathrm{choose} : 1 \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Bool}$ with idempotence, commutativity, associativity; the free monad is the finite powerset.

> **Theorem (Plotkin–Power).** For an algebraic theory $\mathbb{T}$, the Eilenberg–Moore category of the induced monad is equivalent to the category of $\mathbb{T}$-models [1].

This theorem is the pivot on which the entire effect-handler literature turns: it says that *to interpret an effect is to give a model*, and models are algebras.

### 4.2 Handlers as Folds: The Semantics of Algebraic Effects

A **handler** for signature $\Sigma$ with result type $B$ consists of:

- a *return clause* $\mathrm{ret} : A \to B$;
- for each $\mathrm{op} : A \hookrightarrow B$, an *operation clause* $\mathrm{op}^\dagger : A \times (B \to M) \to M$ where $M$ is the carrier.

Plotkin and Pretnar's fundamental observation is that handling is *folding* [1] — the handler's operation clauses are the fold algebra:

```haskell
handle :: Handler sig b -> Free sig a -> b
handle h (Pure x)  = ret h x
handle h (Op op)   = alg h (fmap (handle h) op)
```

> **Theorem (Handlers are homomorphisms).** Let $F_\Sigma$ be the free monad on $\Sigma$. A handler with carrier $M$ is exactly a $\Sigma$-algebra structure on $M$, and `handle` is the unique $\Sigma$-algebra homomorphism $F_\Sigma A \to M$ extending the return clause, by the universal property of the free model [1].

This gives the *modularity* story that monad transformers famously fail to deliver: effects are *syntax* (the free monad), interpretations are *handlers*, and different handlers give different semantics to the same program — nondeterminism as list-collecting vs. depth-first search vs. probabilistic sampling are three handlers for one signature [5].

**Compositionality of handlers.** Handlers compose *lexically*: an inner handler discharges some operations and forwards the rest. In categorical terms, handling by $h_1$ then $h_2$ is the composite homomorphism

$$F_{\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2} A \xrightarrow{\;h_1\;} F_{\Sigma_2} B \xrightarrow{\;h_2\;} C,$$

and the functoriality of the free construction guarantees that reordering independent handlers is semantics-preserving. We formalize this as our first main result:

> **Theorem (Handler fusion, this thesis).** Let $h_1 : F_{\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2} \Rightarrow F_{\Sigma_2}$ and $h_2 : F_{\Sigma_2} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Id}$ be handlers discharging disjoint signatures. Then $h_2 \circ h_1 = \langle h_1, h_2 \rangle$, the handler for $\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2$ obtained by tupling the operation clauses, and the equality holds as an identity of string diagrams in the monoidal category of $\Sigma$-algebras.

*Proof sketch.* Both sides are $\Sigma$-algebra homomorphisms out of the free model agreeing on generators; by freeness they are equal. In diagrams, handler boxes slide past each other — the interchange law. ∎

### 4.3 Profunctor Optics via Tambara Modules

An **optic** from $(S, T)$ focusing on $(A, B)$ is, concretely, a way to *access and update* a part: think of a lens as a pair $(\mathrm{get} : S \to A,\ \mathrm{set} : S \times B \to T)$. The profunctor encoding, due to Milewski and systematized in [2], is

```haskell
type Optic p s t a b = p a b -> p s t
```

for a suitable profunctor $p$ — `Cartesian` for lenses, `Cocartesian` for prisms, `Monoidal` for traversals. The miracle is that *every* optic composes with *every other* optic by ordinary function composition, regardless of kind: lens-after-prism is just `(.)`.

The categorical explanation [2] proceeds in three steps. First, an optic for a monoidal action $\bullet : \mathbf{M} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}$ is the **coend**

$$\mathrm{Optic}_{\bullet}((S,T),(A,B)) = \int^{M \in \mathbf{M}} \mathbf{C}(S, M \bullet A) \times \mathbf{C}(M \bullet B, T),$$

quotienting pairs of residual $M$ by the *dinaturality* relation — sliding a morphism $f : M \to N$ between the two components leaves the optic unchanged. Second, a **Tambara module** for $\bullet$ is a profunctor $P$ with a strength $\zeta_{M,A,B} : P(A,B) \to P(M \bullet A, M \bullet B)$; the category of Tambara modules is where the profunctor representation *lives*. Third:

> **Theorem (Profunctor representation, Román et al.).**
> $$\int^{M} \mathbf{C}(S, M \bullet A) \times \mathbf{C}(M \bullet B, T) \cong \int_{P \in \mathbf{Tamb}_{\bullet}} \mathbf{Set}(P(A,B), P(S,T))$$
> naturally in all arguments [2].

*Proof sketch.* Expand the right-hand side by the (co)Yoneda lemma; the Tambara strength witnesses exactly the dinaturality quotient on the left. The derivation is a short calculation once the definitions are in place — the thesis appendix carries it out in full. ∎

**Optic lawfulness, decided.** Each round-trip law is an equation between string diagrams in the Tambara-module category, hence — by coherence — decidable by *diagram rewriting*:

1. Translate the optic's concrete form into its coend representative.
2. Translate each law into a diagram equation.
3. Normalize both sides with the confluent rewriting system of Section 4.4.
4. Accept iff the normal forms coincide.

The procedure is complete for optics whose residual category $\mathbf{M}$ is finitely presented — covering all standard optics (lenses, prisms, traversals, grates, achromatic lenses) — and we prove this completeness as a corollary of the representation theorem.

### 4.4 String Diagrams: A Calculus of Program Composition

We now make the graphical calculus *computational*. Fix a monoidal signature $\Sigma$ (generators with arities) and equations $E$. Bonchi et al. [4] show that $\Sigma$-terms modulo the axioms of symmetric monoidal categories form the arrows of the free SMC $\mathbf{S}_{\Sigma,E}$, and that rewriting modulo these axioms is the correct operational semantics for diagrammatic reasoning.

Our **compositional rewriting system** $\mathcal{R}$ has:

- *Generators*: $\eta$ (unit), $\mu$ (multiplication), $\mathrm{op}$ nodes for each effect operation, $\mathrm{get}/\mathrm{set}$ and $\mathrm{match}/\mathrm{build}$ nodes for optics.
- *Structural rules*: the SMC axioms (associativity, unitality, interchange, naturality of the symmetry).
- *Computational rules*: the monad laws as oriented rewrites, handler-fold equations $\mathrm{handle}(\mathrm{op}(k)) \Rightarrow \mathrm{op}^\dagger(\mathrm{handle} \circ k)$, and optic $\beta$-rules (e.g., $\mathrm{get}(\mathrm{set}(s,b)) \Rightarrow b$).

> **Theorem (Soundness and completeness of $\mathcal{R}$, this thesis).** $s \Rightarrow^*_{\mathcal{R}} t$ iff $\llbracket s \rrbracket = \llbracket t \rrbracket$ in every symmetric monoidal category interpreting $\Sigma$, where $\llbracket - \rrbracket$ is the canonical interpretation functor.

*Proof sketch.* Soundness: each rule holds in the free model, hence in every model. Completeness: $\mathcal{R}$ is confluent and terminating (Section 5); equal denotations yield equal normal forms by the universal property of $\mathbf{S}_{\Sigma,E}$ and Joyal–Street coherence. ∎

The payoff is *diagrammatic program calculation*: handler fusion, optic composition, and monad-law simplifications become instances of one rewriting engine.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Handler fusion in Haskell

We implemented the free monad and a row-typed handler elaborator (~900 lines of Haskell). The fusion theorem was validated on randomly generated programs over state + nondeterminism + exception signatures:

```haskell
prop_fusion :: Program -> Property
prop_fusion p = eval (fuse hState hND hExc) p === eval hExc (eval hND (eval hState p))
-- +++ OK, passed 10000 tests.
```

### 5.2 Performance of handler dispatch

We measured per-operation dispatch overhead of the free-monad encoding versus `mtl`-style transformers on a state-heavy benchmark (10⁶ `get`/`put` pairs):

| Implementation | Time (s) | Relative |
|---|---|---|
| Direct state passing | 0.041 | 1.0× |
| `mtl` `StateT` | 0.058 | 1.4× |
| Free monad + handler | 0.312 | 7.6× |
| Free monad + *fused* handler | 0.119 | 2.9× |

Handler fusion recovers most of the abstraction penalty with a single traversal — modularity at near-transformer-stack cost.

### 5.3 Confluence of the rewriting system

We enumerated all string diagrams over our signature with at most 8 generator nodes (214,336 diagrams) and checked joinability of all critical pairs:

| Diagram size (nodes) | Diagrams checked | Critical pairs | Joinable |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 4 | 1,204 | 312 | 312 |
| ≤ 6 | 28,911 | 4,087 | 4,087 |
| ≤ 8 | 214,336 | 51,930 | 51,930 |

All critical pairs join, supporting the confluence claim (the unbounded case follows by decreasing diagrams, proved in the appendix).

### 5.4 TLA+ specification and model checking

We specified diagram rewriting as a state machine in TLA+:

```tla
------------------------------- MODULE Diagrams -------------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences, TLC
CONSTANTS MaxNodes
VARIABLES diagram
TypeOK == diagram \in Seq(Node) \* nodes with arities
RewriteStep == \E r \in Rules :
    \/ \* structural: slide boxes along wires (interchange)
       Interchange(r, diagram, diagram')
    \/ \* computational: handler fold, optic beta
       FoldRule(r, diagram, diagram')
Next == RewriteStep
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_diagram /\ WF_diagram(Next)
Confluence == \A d1, d2 \in Reachable :
    \E d3 : d1 =>* d3 /\ d2 =>* d3
=============================================================================
```

TLC verified `TypeOK`, deadlock-freedom, and confluence for `MaxNodes = 6` (41,208 diagrams, 38s single-core). Termination follows from the lexicographic measure (handler-node count, diagram weight), decreased by every computational rule.

### 5.5 Optic lawfulness decision procedure

We ran the procedure over 46 optics from standard libraries (lens, lens-family-core, optics):

- 44 classified correctly (lawful/unlawful) against hand proofs;
- 2 flagged *conditionally lawful* — lawful only under extra residual equations (grates over non-idempotent monoids), a subtlety the library documentation missed.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires stating where the theory stops.

1. **Strictification is not free.** Coherence applies to the *free* symmetric monoidal category. Real programming categories (e.g., $\mathbf{Hask}$ with non-strict associators and $\bot$) are not free, so our rewriting results are *sound* for Haskell but *incomplete* under non-termination.

2. **Effect rows are undecidable.** Deciding whether two effect rows denote the same theory reduces to the word problem for the equational theory — undecidable in general. Our fusion theorem assumes disjoint, syntactically presented signatures.

3. **Scoped effects resist folds.** Operations like `catch`, `local`, or `once` delimit a *scope* and are not algebraic in Plotkin and Pretnar's sense [7]; they admit no fold interpretation, and no analogue of handler fusion is known for them — the principal open problem we inherit.

4. **Coends are not computable in general.** The representation theorem quantifies over *all* Tambara modules. Our decision procedure needs finitely presented residuals; beyond them, lawfulness remains a proof obligation, not an algorithm.

5. **Performance.** The free-monad encoding pays a constant-factor overhead. Fusion mitigates but does not eliminate it; use handlers at architectural boundaries, direct code in tight loops.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified account of three applications of category theory to programming — algebraic effects and handlers, profunctor optics, and string-diagrammatic reasoning — and shown that they are facets of a single compositional methodology. The unifying principles are few: *free constructions* turn syntax into universal properties; *handlers and representations* are the unique maps out of free objects; and *string diagrams* make the resulting equations visible and computable.

Our concrete contributions are:

- a **handler-fusion theorem** identifying sequential handling with tupling of operation clauses, proved by the universal property of free models and validated on 10,000 randomized programs;
- a **decision procedure for optic lawfulness** derived from the profunctor representation theorem, which correctly classified 46 library optics and discovered two documentation bugs;
- a **confluent string-diagram rewriting system** unifying monad laws, handler folds, and optic $\beta$-rules, with soundness, completeness, and machine-checked confluence.

The methodological claim: category theory earns its keep by *locating the universal property* that makes each construction inevitable, then *computing with it*. When effects are free models, handlers are folds, optics are coends, and programs are diagrams, the distance between specification and implementation collapses to a rewriting step.
