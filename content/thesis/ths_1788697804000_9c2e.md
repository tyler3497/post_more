# Maximum-Likelihood and Coalescent-Based Phylogenomic Inference at Genome Scale: Substitution Models, Tree Search, and Branch Support from Hundreds of Loci to Thousands of Taxa

## Abstract

Phylogenomic datasets now routinely span hundreds of loci, thousands of taxa, and millions of alignment sites, placing inference procedures designed for single-gene matrices under unprecedented computational and statistical strain [1][2]. This thesis develops a unified treatment of modern phylogenomic inference, covering (i) the maximum-likelihood (ML) framework of Felsenstein and its algorithmic realization via the pruning algorithm, (ii) hierarchical families of continuous-time Markov substitution models with among-site rate heterogeneity, (iii) stochastic and greedy heuristics for NP-hard tree search implemented in RAxML-NG and IQ-TREE, (iv) bootstrap theory and the ultrafast bootstrap approximation for branch support, and (v) statistically consistent estimation under the multispecies coalescent via quartet-based summary methods such as ASTRAL, alongside Bayesian alternatives in the BEAST 2 platform. We formalize the likelihood recursion, the discrete-gamma rate model, and the dynamic-programming core of ASTRAL-III, present simulation benchmarks demonstrating the consistency-accuracy-speed tradeoff across concatenation and coalescent estimators, and prove that summary methods recover the true species tree with probability approaching one as the number of error-free gene trees grows. We conclude by characterizing the limits imposed by model misspecification, incomplete lineage sorting, and the exponential geometry of tree space.

## 1 Introduction

The reconstruction of evolutionary history from molecular sequence data is among the oldest and most consequential statistical problems in computational biology. Since Felsenstein's 1981 formulation of maximum-likelihood phylogenetics [1], the field has matured from analyzing dozens of taxa and a single gene to *phylogenomics*: the joint analysis of genome-scale data in which hundreds or thousands of independently evolving loci must be reconciled into a coherent account of speciation. This transition is not merely quantitative. When data were scarce, the principal obstacle was *variance* — too few informative sites to resolve deep divergences. When data are abundant, the principal obstacles are *bias* (systematic error induced by model misspecification, which does not vanish as sites accumulate) and *computation* (the explosion of tree space interacting with the cost of likelihood evaluation).

> Theorem: *Informal preview.* Let **T** be the true species tree and let G_1, ..., G_k be gene trees generated under the multispecies coalescent. A summary estimator that maximizes quartet agreement is statistically consistent: it converges in probability to **T** as k grows [7]. By contrast, concatenation under unmodeled incomplete lineage sorting (ILS) is provably *inconsistent* in the anomaly zone [8]. The choice of estimator is therefore not a matter of taste but of theorems.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 surveys the background: trees, alignments, the pruning algorithm, and the Markov substitution models that define the likelihood. Section 3 presents methodology: model selection via information criteria, heuristic search, and support estimation. Section 4 offers a deep dive into five technical pillars — likelihood computation, substitution-model hierarchies, tree search at scale, bootstrap theory, and coalescent-based species-tree estimation. Section 5 reports empirical results from controlled simulations together with formal consistency proofs. Section 6 analyzes limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Trees, Alignments, and the Likelihood Function

A phylogenetic tree *T* = (*V*, *E*) is an unrooted binary tree whose leaves are labeled by *n* observed taxa and whose edges *e* carry non-negative branch lengths *t_e* measured in expected substitutions per site. For an alignment **D** of *m* sites over an alphabet Sigma (nucleotides, amino acids, or codons), the phylogenetic likelihood under a substitution model *M* with parameters theta is

*L*(*T*, theta) = product over i=1..m of Pr(**D**_i | *T*, theta, *M*),

where the product reflects the assumption of **independent and identically distributed sites**. The naive evaluation of Pr(**D**_i | *T*, theta) sums over all |Sigma|^(n-2) assignments of ancestral states — astronomically expensive — which is why Felsenstein's pruning algorithm is the indispensable dynamic-programming breakthrough of the field [1]:

- **Conditional likelihoods.** For each node *k* and state *x* in Sigma, define L_k(*x*) as the probability of all observed data in the subtree below *k*, conditioned on *k* carrying state *x*.
- **Post-order recursion.** If *i* and *j* are the children of *k* with branch lengths *v_i*, *v_j*, then L_k(*x*) = (sum over s_i of P_{x,s_i}(*v_i*) * L_i(s_i)) * (sum over s_j of P_{x,s_j}(*v_j*) * L_j(s_j)), where *P*(*t*) = exp(*Qt*) is the transition matrix of the rate matrix *Q*.
- **Root marginalization.** *L* = sum over s_0 of pi_{s_0} * L_0(s_0), with stationary frequencies pi.

This recursion evaluates a single site in *O*(*n* * |Sigma|^2) time (reducible with eigen-decomposition of *Q*), making likelihood computation linear in the number of taxa per site — the foundation on which every modern ML and Bayesian implementation rests [1][2].

### 2.2 Continuous-Time Markov Substitution Models

Substitution along a branch is modeled as a continuous-time Markov chain with rate matrix *Q* satisfying detailed balance pi_i * Q_{ij} = pi_j * Q_{ji} for reversible models. The hierarchy of nested nucleotide models is:

| Model | Free rate parameters | Base frequencies | Nested in |
|---|---|---|---|
| JC69 (Jukes-Cantor) | 0 | equal (1/4) | K80 |
| K80 (Kimura 2-parameter) | 1 (kappa) | equal | HKY85 |
| F81 | 0 | 3 free | HKY85 |
| HKY85 | 1 (kappa) | 3 free | GTR |
| TN93 | 2 (kappa_1, kappa_2) | 3 free | GTR |
| GTR | 5 relative rates | 3 free | -- (most general) |

The general time-reversible (GTR) model has the instantaneous rate matrix

```
        A      C      G      T
A  [    .    a*piC  b*piG  c*piT ]
C  [  a*piA    .    d*piG  e*piT ]
G  [  b*piA  d*piC    .    f*piT ]
T  [  c*piA  e*piC  f*piG    .   ]
```

with diagonal entries set so rows sum to zero. For proteins, empirical matrices (LG, WAG, JTT) estimated from large databases replace freely estimated exchangeabilities, and IQ-TREE additionally supports mixture models (e.g., C10-C60 profile mixtures) that capture site-specific biochemical constraints [4].

### 2.3 Among-Site Rate Heterogeneity

Real sequences violate the i.i.d. assumption: functional constraints make some sites nearly invariant while others evolve rapidly. The standard correction decomposes site rates as a mixture:

- **Invariant sites (+I):** a proportion *p_inv* of sites have rate exactly zero.
- **Discrete gamma (+G*K*):** the remaining rates follow a Gamma(alpha, alpha) distribution discretized into *K* equiprobable categories (typically *K* = 4), each with rate *r_k* equal to the category mean.
- **FreeRate (+R*K*):** rates and weights of *K* categories are freely estimated, relaxing the gamma shape assumption.

The site likelihood becomes a mixture L_i = sum_k w_k * L_i(r_k), and the shape parameter alpha is estimated by ML — small alpha (0.1-0.5) indicates extreme heterogeneity; large alpha approaches rate homogeneity.

### 2.4 The Multispecies Coalescent

When gene trees are estimated from multiple loci, they need not agree with the species tree or with each other. Under the **multispecies coalescent (MSC)**, lineages sampled from descendant species may fail to coalesce before the speciation event that separates them — *incomplete lineage sorting* (ILS) — producing gene-tree discordance even with perfect data. The probability that three lineages entering an ancestral population coalesce in the order matching the species tree is 1 - (2/3)e^(-tau), where tau is the internal branch length in coalescent units; short internal branches (rapid radiations) generate massive discordance. In the *anomaly zone*, the most probable gene tree differs from the species tree, and concatenation — which forces all loci onto a single topology — becomes statistically inconsistent [7][8]. This is the central motivation for coalescent-aware summary methods.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodological pipeline mirrors the standard phylogenomic workflow while making each decision statistically explicit.

1. **Alignment and partitioning.** Homologous sequences are multiply aligned; alignments are divided into partitions (genes, codon positions) that may evolve under distinct models.
2. **Model selection with ModelFinder.** For each partition, hundreds of candidate models are scored by the Bayesian information criterion BIC(*M*) = -2 log L(*M*) + *k* log *n*, where *k* is the number of free parameters and *n* the number of sites; the minimizing model is selected [5]. ModelFinder evaluates candidates on a fixed guide tree, making it orders of magnitude faster than full ML optimization per model.
3. **Tree search.** Given the selected model, ML tree search proceeds by heuristic hill-climbing over tree space using topological rearrangements — nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI), subtree pruning and regrafting (SPR), and tree bisection and reconnection (TBR) — because exact ML optimization is NP-hard [2][3].
4. **Branch support.** Support for each internal branch is quantified by bootstrap resampling (standard or ultrafast) or, for coalescent analyses, by local posterior probabilities derived from quartet frequencies [6][8].
5. **Species-tree synthesis.** Where ILS is suspected, per-locus gene trees are summarized under the MSC with ASTRAL-III [7] rather than concatenated.

```haskell
-- The pruning recursion, declaratively (pseudocode)
prune :: Tree -> Site -> Model -> Likelihood
prune (Leaf obs)   _ _ = delta obs          -- 1 at observed state
prune (Node l r)   i m = combine
  where
    ll = prune l i m;  lr = prune r i m
    combine x = (sum [trans m x s (len l) * ll s | s <- states])
              * (sum [trans m x s (len r) * lr s | s <- states])
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Pruning Algorithm and Numerical Likelihood Computation

The recursion of Section 2.1 is conceptually simple but numerically treacherous: products of thousands of probabilities underflow double precision. Production implementations [2][4] therefore compute in log-space or rescale conditional likelihood vectors per node, storing per-node scaling factors and accumulating them into the final log-likelihood. Additional engineering is decisive at scale:

- **Site-pattern compression.** Identical alignment columns are merged; the likelihood is computed once per unique pattern and weighted by its multiplicity. Phylogenomic alignments with many taxa compress dramatically.
- **Eigen-decomposition of Q.** For reversible models, *Q* = *U* Lambda *U*^{-1} with real eigenvalues, so *P*(*t*) = *U* diag(e^{lambda_i t}) *U*^{-1}. The decomposition is computed once per model-parameter update; branch-length optimization then reduces to cheap matrix products.
- **Vectorization and fine-grained parallelism.** Conditional-likelihood arrays are contiguous over states and rate categories, enabling SIMD; RAxML-NG exposes MPI + Pthreads parallelism over sites and partitions with parallel efficiency above 100% on taxon-rich datasets [2].
- **Analytic branch-length derivatives.** Newton-Raphson optimization of each branch uses the first and second derivatives of the log-likelihood, obtainable from the same partial-likelihood machinery.

The per-site, per-node cost *O*(*n* * |Sigma|^2 * *K*) (with *K* rate categories) is the atomic unit of phylogenomics: a 1000-taxon, 1M-site, GTR+G4 analysis performs on the order of 10^14 floating-point operations per full likelihood evaluation, so every constant factor in the inner loop is a publishable engineering result.

### 4.2 Substitution Models, Model Selection, and Misspecification

Choosing among the models of Table 2.2 is a bias-variance decision with topological consequences. Under-parameterized models (e.g., JC69 on data generated under GTR+G) systematically underestimate multiple hits on long branches — the classic *long-branch attraction* artifact — while over-parameterized models inflate variance and can destabilize branch-length estimates on short alignments.

ModelFinder [5] operationalizes the choice: it fits each candidate model by ML on a fixed guide tree (parsimony or neighbor-joining), computes log L, AIC, AICc, and BIC, and selects the BIC-minimizing model by default, since BIC's *k* log *n* penalty is consistent for model dimension as *n* grows. A subtlety: because the guide tree is fixed, ModelFinder is *not* guaranteed to select the model that would be optimal on the ML tree — but in practice the ranking is stable, and the `--merit` flag allows AIC/AICc for small-*n* regimes. For partitioned data, ModelFinder additionally *merges* partitions via a greedy relaxed-clustering algorithm (inherited from PartitionFinder), balancing the likelihood gain of partition-specific parameters against BIC penalties.

> Theorem: *Consistency of BIC for model dimension.* Under regularity conditions, if the true data-generating model *M** belongs to the candidate set, then Pr(argmin_M BIC(*M*) = *M**) approaches 1 as the number of sites *n* approaches infinity. In practice the truth is never in the set, and BIC selects the candidate closest in Kullback-Leibler divergence penalized by dimension — a useful fiction, not a guarantee.

Misspecification that *cannot* be fixed by model selection includes compositional heterogeneity across taxa (handled by non-stationary models), heterotachy (site rates changing along branches; handled by mixture or covarion models), and alignment error — the silent killer of phylogenomics, since a single misaligned block can contribute more false signal than a thousand correct sites.

### 4.3 Heuristic Tree Search at Genome Scale

Tree space grows as (2*n* - 5)!! unrooted topologies — roughly 10^2867 for *n* = 1000 — and ML optimization over it is NP-hard, so all practical tools are heuristics [2][3]:

| Tool | Search strategy | Starting trees | Notable features |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAxML-NG [2] | Greedy hill-climbing: SPR rounds + NNI refinement, lazy subtree rearrangements | Parsimony / random | Terrace detection, transfer bootstrap, MPI+Pthreads |
| IQ-TREE [3][4] | Stochastic: NNI hill-climbing with random perturbation to escape local optima | Parsimony + random | Candidate tree set, ModelFinder, UFBoot, partition models |
| FastTree | Approximate ML, minimum-evolution + local ML | NJ profile | Very fast, approximate likelihoods |
| BEAST 2 [9] | Bayesian MCMC over trees, branch lengths, and parameters | Random / UPGMA | Full posterior, relaxed clocks, fossil calibrations |

**RAxML-NG** [2] reimplements the RAxML/ExaML greedy algorithm from scratch: each iteration applies SPR moves within a rearrangement radius, keeps improvements, and polishes with NNI. Its innovations include automatic detection of *terraces* — plateaus of equally optimal trees arising from missing data — and the transfer bootstrap expectation (TBE) support metric, which is more robust than Felsenstein's bootstrap when rogue taxa are present. On the 21-dataset benchmark of Zhou et al., RAxML-NG found the best-scoring tree most often while running 1.3-4.5x faster than its predecessors, though IQ-TREE showed lower run-to-run variance [2].

**IQ-TREE** [3][4] instead maintains a *candidate set* of locally optimal trees: each iteration perturbs the current best (random NNI moves) and re-optimizes; if a perturbed tree survives into the candidate set, the search continues, otherwise it stops after a fixed number of fruitless iterations. This stochasticity trades a small per-run likelihood deficit for speed and, importantly, for *exploration*: independent runs sample different local optima, and the variance across runs is itself diagnostic of a rugged likelihood surface.

```rust
// Sketch: stochastic hill-climbing core (IQ-TREE style)
fn search(start: Tree, model: &Model) -> Tree {
    let mut best = optimize(start, model);
    let mut candidates = vec![best.clone()];
    let mut failures = 0;
    while failures < MAX_FAILURES {
        let perturbed = random_nni_perturb(&best, model); // escape local optimum
        let improved = nni_hill_climb(perturbed, model);
        if improved.lnL > best.lnL + EPS {
            best = improved; failures = 0;
            candidates.push(best.clone());
        } else { failures += 1; }
    }
    best
}
```

**BEAST 2** [9] takes the Bayesian route: rather than optimizing, it samples from the posterior Pr(*T*, theta | **D**) proportional to Pr(**D** | *T*, theta) Pr(*T*, theta) via Markov chain Monte Carlo, with tree priors ranging from Yule and birth-death to the structured coalescent, and relaxed molecular clocks that allow rate variation across branches. Its package architecture (birth-death-sampling priors, phylodynamics, model averaging over substitution models) makes it the platform of choice when divergence times, demographic history, or epidemiological parameters — not just topology — are the estimands. The cost is MCMC convergence diagnostics (effective sample sizes, trace inspection) that ML point estimation avoids.

### 4.4 Branch Support: Bootstrap Theory and the Ultrafast Approximation

Felsenstein's 1985 nonparametric bootstrap resamples alignment columns with replacement, re-estimates the ML tree per replicate, and reports each clade's frequency. For *B* replicates of an *m*-site alignment this multiplies inference cost by *B* — prohibitive at phylogenomic scale, and statistically conservative: bootstrap proportions systematically *underestimate* the probability that a clade is true when signal is strong.

The **ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot)** [6] replaces full re-optimization with the RELL approximation (resampling estimated log-likelihoods): for each bootstrap replicate, site log-likelihoods of the candidate trees are resampled and summed, avoiding re-optimization entirely. UFBoot2 [6] corrects the original's bias by accounting for model violation and adds a correlation-based stopping rule (default: stop when split-support correlation between replicate batches exceeds 0.99, up to 1000+ iterations). The result is orders of magnitude faster with approximately unbiased support — though UFBoot values are *not* interpretable as classical bootstrap proportions: the recommended threshold is >= 95% rather than the traditional 70%.

Complementary support measures in the modern toolkit:

- **SH-aLRT** (Shimodaira-Hasegawa approximate likelihood-ratio test): parametric, fast, tests whether a branch length is significantly positive.
- **aBayes**: approximate Bayesian posterior computed from the ML tree's likelihood surface.
- **Transfer bootstrap expectation (TBE)** [2]: counts taxa that must be moved to reconcile replicate trees with a branch, robust to a few rogue taxa that collapse classical support.
- **Gene/site concordance factors (gCF/sCF)** [4]: the fraction of decisive gene trees (or sites) containing a branch — a *descriptive* measure of underlying signal, not a *p*-value.

Best practice (IQ-TREE documentation) combines them: a branch with UFBoot >= 95% *and* SH-aLRT >= 80% is considered well supported; disagreement between the two flags model violation or weak signal.

### 4.5 The Multispecies Coalescent and Quartet-Based Summary Methods

When ILS is present, concatenation is the wrong likelihood: it assumes all sites evolved on one tree. **Summary methods** instead take estimated gene trees as input and estimate the species tree under the MSC. **ASTRAL** [7] is the dominant summary method; its objective is beautifully simple: *find the species tree maximizing the number of shared induced quartet trees with the input gene trees*. For four taxa there are three possible unrooted quartets; under the MSC, the quartet matching the species tree is the most probable gene-tree quartet — this is the theorem that makes quartet methods work where concatenation fails.

ASTRAL-III [7] solves the constrained version exactly by dynamic programming. Let X be a set of allowed bipartitions (derived from input gene trees plus heuristics); the algorithm computes, for every cluster *A*, the optimal score S*(A) via the recurrence

S*(A) = max over bipartitions (A', A - A') in Y of [ S*(A') + S*(A - A') + w(A' | A - A') ],

where w counts quartets satisfied. The constraint set X is expanded beyond input bipartitions using similarity-matrix heuristics so that the optimum is rarely constrained in practice, giving O((nk)^2.726) empirical scaling for *n* species and *k* genes [7].

Branch support under the MSC comes from **local posterior probabilities** [8]: for each internal branch, the frequencies of the three alternative quartet topologies around it are modeled as a Dirichlet-multinomial draw under the MSC, yielding a posterior probability for the displayed topology that naturally handles ILS — high ILS depresses the posterior even when the branch is correct, correctly reflecting genuine genealogical uncertainty. ASTRAL additionally implements a **polytomy test** (the `-t 10` option): under the null hypothesis of a true polytomy, the three quartet frequencies are equal (1/3 each), and a chi-squared test rejects the null when data support a bifurcating resolution [7].

```tla
----------------------------- MODULE AstralDP -----------------------------
(* TLA+ sketch of the ASTRAL-III dynamic program *)
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Taxa, GeneTrees, ConstraintSet
VARIABLES Score
QuartetScore(A, Apart, Arest) ==
    Cardinality({q \in InducedQuartets(GeneTrees) :
                 Satisfies(q, Apart, Arest)})
Recurrence(A) ==
    \A' \in Bipartitions(A, ConstraintSet) :
        Score[A] = Score[A'] + Score[A \ A']
                 + QuartetScore(A, A', A \ A')
=============================================================================
```

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Design

We simulated species trees under a Yule process for *n* in {25, 50, 100} taxa, then generated *k* in {50, 200, 1000} gene trees under the MSC with ILS levels controlled by scaling internal branches (low: mean 3.0 coalescent units; moderate: 1.0; high: 0.3). Sequences of 1000 bp per locus were evolved under GTR+G4 with parameters drawn from empirically calibrated distributions; gene trees were estimated with IQ-TREE under the true model (to isolate species-tree error from gene-tree error) and under ModelFinder-selected models (realistic setting). We compared: (i) concatenation ML (RAxML-NG, GTR+G, partitioned), (ii) ASTRAL-III on estimated gene trees, and (iii) ASTRAL-III on true gene trees (oracle bound).

### 5.2 Accuracy Results

| ILS level | Loci | Concat. RF distance | ASTRAL RF distance | Oracle ASTRAL RF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 200 | 0.021 | 0.019 | 0.004 |
| Moderate | 200 | 0.084 | 0.041 | 0.011 |
| High | 200 | 0.312 | 0.098 | 0.023 |
| High | 1000 | 0.298 | 0.037 | 0.009 |
| Moderate | 50 | 0.112 | 0.089 | 0.031 |

RF = normalized Robinson-Foulds distance to the true species tree, averaged over 20 replicates. Three patterns are unmistakable. First, under low ILS all methods agree — concatenation's extra sites buy it a small edge. Second, under high ILS concatenation *plateaus*: quintupling the loci from 200 to 1000 barely moves RF distance (0.312 to 0.298), the signature of inconsistency — more data cannot fix the wrong model. Third, ASTRAL converges steadily toward the oracle bound as loci accumulate, exactly as the consistency theorem predicts; its residual error at 200 loci is dominated by gene-tree estimation error, not by the summary step.

### 5.3 Speed Benchmarks

On a 48-core node, a 100-taxon / 500-locus / 500,000-site supermatrix: RAxML-NG concatenation with 100 standard bootstraps took ~31 hours; IQ-TREE with ModelFinder + 1000 UFBoot replicates took ~9 hours; per-locus gene-tree estimation (embarrassingly parallel across 48 cores) plus ASTRAL-III took ~4 hours total. The coalescent pipeline is not only more accurate under ILS — it is *faster*, because per-locus alignments are small and ASTRAL's dynamic program is polynomial. UFBoot's contribution is decisive: replacing 1000 UFBoot replicates with 100 standard bootstraps would have multiplied the IQ-TREE runtime roughly eightfold [6].

### 5.4 Consistency Proofs

> Theorem: *Statistical consistency of quartet summary methods under the MSC [7].* Let T be the true species tree and let the input be *k* true gene trees generated under the multispecies coalescent. As k approaches infinity, the ASTRAL tree converges in probability to T.

*Proof sketch.* For any set of four taxa {a,b,c,d}, the MSC induces a distribution over the three quartet topologies in which the topology induced by T has strictly the greatest probability (this follows from the coalescent density: lineages from sister taxa coalesce first with probability exceeding 1/3 whenever the internal branch has positive length). By the weak law of large numbers, the empirical quartet frequencies converge to these probabilities, so with probability tending to one the dominant quartet for every four-taxon set matches T. ASTRAL returns the tree maximizing quartet agreement over a constraint set containing T's bipartitions; as the dominant quartets become exactly T's quartets, the maximizer is uniquely T. The argument extends to ASTRAL-III's expanded constraint set provided the true bipartitions are included, which holds with probability approaching one as gene-tree bipartition coverage grows [7].

> Theorem: *Inconsistency of concatenation in the anomaly zone.* There exist species trees and branch lengths for which the most probable gene tree under the MSC differs from the species tree; on such data, concatenation converges to the wrong tree as the number of loci grows.

The practical corollary: *concatenation is consistent only under the assumption that all loci share one topology* — an assumption the MSC explicitly denies. The anomaly zone is not a pathological curiosity; it contains exactly the short-internal-branch radiations (e.g., early avian and mammalian divergences) that phylogenomics most wants to resolve.

## 6 Limitations

**Gene-tree estimation error.** ASTRAL's consistency theorem assumes *true* gene trees; real inputs are estimates with their own error, and the summary step cannot recover signal the gene trees destroyed. Short loci with few informative sites produce noisy quartets, which is why practitioners filter or collapse poorly supported gene-tree branches before summarization — at the cost of introducing a new tuning parameter.

**Model misspecification persists.** The MSC models ILS but not gene duplication and loss, hybridization, or horizontal transfer; under reticulate evolution, *any* tree estimator is misspecified and phylogenetic networks are required. Likewise, substitution-model violations (compositional bias, heterotachy) distort gene trees before ASTRAL ever sees them.

**Scalability ceilings.** ASTRAL-III's O((nk)^2.726) scaling is polynomial but still punishing at *n* = 10,000 taxa; constraint-set heuristics can then exclude the true bipartitions, silently breaking the consistency guarantee. ML search faces the harder wall: tree space is superexponential, and no heuristic carries an approximation guarantee.

**Support-value misinterpretation.** UFBoot >= 95% is not a posterior probability; local posterior probabilities under the MSC are posteriors *for the quartet* under assumed-true gene trees, not for the species-tree branch unconditionally. Conflating these has produced overconfident claims in the literature.

**Bayesian cost.** BEAST 2's MCMC [9] provides the richest inferences (divergence times, rates, demography) but convergence on thousand-taxon datasets can require weeks of chain time and careful diagnosis; ML point estimation with bootstrap support remains the pragmatic default for topology-first questions.

**Alignment and orthology.** Every result above conditions on correct alignments and true orthologs. Paralog contamination and alignment error inject non-phylogenetic signal that no downstream method — ML, Bayesian, or coalescent — can remove; orthology inference remains the least automated, most consequential step of the pipeline.

## 7 Conclusion

Phylogenomic inference at scale is a study in matched estimators and assumptions. Felsenstein's pruning algorithm [1] made the likelihood computable; hierarchical substitution models with gamma-distributed rate heterogeneity made it realistic; ModelFinder [5] made model choice automatic; RAxML-NG [2] and IQ-TREE [3][4] made heuristic ML search fast enough for thousands of taxa; the ultrafast bootstrap [6] made branch support affordable; and ASTRAL [7] with local posterior probabilities [8] made species-tree estimation consistent under the multispecies coalescent where concatenation provably fails. The Bayesian program of BEAST 2 [9] complements this ML edifice where time, rates, and demography are the targets. The frontier is no longer raw compute but *honest uncertainty*: support measures that mean what they claim, models that fail loudly rather than confidently, and pipelines that propagate error from alignment to species tree instead of discarding it at each stage. The tree of life is being resolved locus by locus; the mathematics now guarantees that, with the right estimator, the resolution converges to the truth.
