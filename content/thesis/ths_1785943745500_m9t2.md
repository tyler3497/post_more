# In-Context Learning as Implicit Bayesian Inference Over Concept Classes: Role of Pretraining Task Diversity, Sparse Attention Patterns, and Inductive Biases in Transformers from Meta-Learning Perspectives

## Abstract

We formalize in-context learning (ICL) in autoregressive Transformers as implicit Bayesian inference over a latent concept class underlying the pretraining distribution, extending Xie et al. [1] and its hierarchical generalization [2,3]. Under a mixture-of-HMM generative model for documents, pretraining induces a posterior predictor that, conditioned on a prompt of exemplars sharing a latent concept, approximates the Bayesian model average with vanishing excess risk as context length grows. We analyze the critical role of pretraining task diversity revealing a sharp threshold [4] beyond which the Transformer deviates from the Bayes-optimal estimator under the pretraining prior to an algorithm that generalizes to unseen tasks, mirroring ridge regression over the full task space rather than memorization of pretraining support. We dissect emergent sparse attention circuits [5,6] that implement this inference, contrasting implicit gradient descent interpretations [7] with Bayesian selection of function classes [8]. Empirical synthesis across linear, sparse, and nonlinear regression families shows that inductive biases of bounded-norm self-attention toward sparse variables enable sample complexity logarithmic in context length, providing a unified meta-learning account of why Transformers, but not recurrence, achieve strong ICL.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models conditioned on a prompt containing $k$ input-output pairs $(x_1, y_1), ..., (x_k, y_k), x_{k+1}$ generate $y_{k+1}$ consistent with the latent mapping without parameter updates. This *in-context learning* (Brown et al. 2020) is surprising because the objective is next-token prediction, not explicit meta-learning.

Two dominant explanatory frameworks compete:

1. **Bayesian inference view** [1,3]: ICL emerges because pretraining documents possess long-range coherence mediated by a latent concept $\theta$ (topic, style, task). The LM learns $p(\text{tokens}|\theta)p(\theta)$. At test time, the prompt provides evidence for $\theta$, and $p_{LM}(y|x_{prompt}) \approx \int p(y|\theta,x)p(\theta|\text{prompt}) d\theta$.

2. **Algorithmic / optimization view** [7,8,9]: Transformers trained on function classes *implement* learning algorithms (OLS, ridge, GD) in forward activations. Garg et al. [8] showed GPT-2 trained from scratch on linear functions achieves near-optimal least squares.

Are they contradictory? We argue they are duals: *under infinite data, ideal next-token predictor is Bayesian* [2]; under finite, structured pretraining, the architecture's inductive biases determine which Bayesian posterior is tractable to approximate and which algorithm it compiles to.

> **Central Question:** What conditions on pretraining distribution diversity and architecture induce a Transformer to approximate posterior inference over *arbitrary* concept classes rather than merely memorizing pretraining tasks, and how do sparse attention patterns mechanistically realize this inference?

We provide three contributions:

- A synthesis of Xie et al.'s HMM mixture theory with Raventós et al. task diversity threshold [4], proving a phase transition from Bayesian memorization to algorithmic generalization.
- A circuit-level analysis of how bounded-norm self-attention heads *create sparse variables* [5] with $O(\log n)$ sample complexity, enabling efficient concept selection [6].
- A comparative evaluation framework unifying linear, sparse linear, and decision tree tasks under same ICL lens, delineating where Bayes-optimal predictor diverges from empirical neural behavior.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 In-Context Learning Formalism

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be function class (e.g., linear: $f_w(x)=w^\top x$). Sample $f\sim \mathcal{D}_\mathcal{H}$, sample $x_i \sim \mathcal{D}_X$, form prompt $P = (x_1, f(x_1), ..., x_k, f(x_k), x_{query})$. Model $M_\phi$ outputs $\hat{y} = M_\phi(P)$. Loss $\mathbb{E}_{f,\{x_i\}}[(\hat{y}-f(x_{query}))^2]$.

Garg et al. [8] train $M$ from scratch on such prompts; surprisingly, 12-layer Transformer matches OLS up to $d=20$, while 2-layer MLP fails.

### 2.2 Latent Concept Generative Model [1]

Xie et al. model pretraining docs as Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) mixed over concepts $c$. Each concept $c$ defines transition matrix $T_c$ over hidden states $h_t$ and emission $o_t\sim O_c(\cdot|h_t)$. A document: sample $c\sim p(c)$, sample $h_{1:T}\sim T_c$, emit $o_{1:T}$.

Key property: *long-range coherence* — all tokens in a document share $c$. Language satisfies this: Wikipedia article shares topic.

Consequence: optimal next-token predictor trained to maximize $\log p(o_{1:T})$ must *invert* $p(c|o_{1:t})$ implicitly. ICL prompt is *unusual* — exemplars concatenated with delimiters — but if it exhibits same latent $c$ across exemplars, it lies in region where posterior $p(c|prompt)$ is sharp.

### 2.3 Task Diversity and Non-Bayesian Emergence

Raventós et al. [4] vary number of distinct pretraining tasks $N_{tasks}$. For linear regression tasks $w\sim \mathcal{U}(S^{d-1})$, they observe threshold $N^* \approx 2^{9}$ for $d=10$: below, Transformer behaves as Bayesian estimator with pretraining distribution as prior — i.e., it predicts using mixture over seen $w$'s, failing OOD; beyond $N^*$, it deviates from Bayes-optimal under pretraining prior and instead performs ridge regression over *all* $w$, generalizing to unseen.

This is crucial: *more diversity makes model less Bayesian w.r.t. pretraining distribution* but *more Bayesian w.r.t. broader meta-distribution*.

| Regime | $N_{tasks}$ | Behavior | Test Risk on Unseen $w$ |
|--------|-------------|----------|--------------------------|
| Memorization | $< 2^7$ | Posterior = mixture over memorized weights | $\Omega(1)$ |
| Bayesian ICL | $2^7$ - $2^9$ | Precise posterior over pretrain support | $\approx 0.3$ |
| Algorithmic / Ridge | $> 2^{11}$ | Implements closed-form estimator | $O(d/k)$ optimal |

### 2.4 Inductive Biases of Self-Attention

Edelman et al. [5] prove:

> **Theorem (Sparse Variable Creation):** A single bounded-norm self-attention head can represent any $s$-sparse Boolean function of context length $n$ with sample complexity $m=O(s \log n / \epsilon^2)$.

This explains why attention's inductive bias favors *sparse* patterns (p copying previous, induction heads) over dense. Empirical work [6] shows capabilities emerge abruptly when attention pattern is discovered; more heads increase lottery tickets for pattern discovery tenfold.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### Theoretical reduction

We consider pretraining loss:

$$\mathcal{L}(\phi) = -\mathbb{E}_{c\sim p(c)}\mathbb{E}_{o_{1:T}\sim \text{HMM}_c} \left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\phi(o_t|o_{<t})\right]$$

Optimal solution $p_\phi^* = p_{true}(\cdot) = \sum_c p(c) p_c(o_{1:T})$. Conditional future token given prompt $S_k$:

$$p_{LM}^*(y|P) = \sum_c p(y|c,x_{query}) p(c|P) = \mathbb{E}_{c\sim p(c|P)}[p(y|c,x_{query})]$$

Posterior sharpening:

$$p(c|P) \propto p(c)\prod_{i=1}^{k} p(o_i|c)$$

As $k\to\infty$, if concept distinguishable ($\text{KL}(p_{c_1}||p_{c_2})>0$), posterior concentrates on true $c^*$, achieving *in-context learning* zero-shot optimality.

We introduce pretraining task diversity knob $\beta$ where $p_\beta(c) = \text{Uniform}(\mathcal{C}_\beta)$, $|\mathcal{C}_\beta| = \beta$. Model capacity $C_\phi$ fixed. Effective risk decomposition from [2]:

$$\mathcal{R} = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[||M_\phi - M_{Bayes}^\beta||^2]}_{\text{Bayes Gap (approx + optimization)}} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[||M_{Bayes}^\beta - f^*||^2]}_{\text{Posterior Variance}}$$

First term decreases with pretraining prompts $N$ and model width $d$, second decreases with $k$ in-context examples exponentially: $O(e^{-k D_{KL}})$.

### Architecture implementations

- **Bayesian selection circuit:** Each head votes for concept $c$ via dot-product $q_{query}^\top k_{example}$. Logit $z_i \approx \log p(o_i|c)$. Softmax attention aggregates concept likelihood.

- **Gradient-descent circuit** [7]: Linear attention layers implement preconditioned GD: $W_{\ell+1}=W_\ell - \eta X X^\top W_\ell + \eta X y^\top$ where $X\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}$ is in-context data, captured in value stream.

Both are realizable in same weight matrices — divergence from pure Bayes emerges when optimizer prefers stable GD solution due to precision constraints in $bfloat16$.

We specify verification in Python and formal invariants in TLA+.

```python
# Simplified ICL Bayesian predictor in JAX style — educational
import jax.numpy as jnp

def bayesian_icl_predict(prompt_x, prompt_y, x_query, prior_concepts):
    # prior_concepts: list of (w_c, prior_logp)
    logposts = []
    for w,c_logp in prior_concepts:
        # likelihood under linear model p(y|x,w) = N(w^T x, sigma^2)
        ll = -0.5*jnp.sum((prompt_y - prompt_x @ w)**2) / 0.1
        logposts.append(ll + c_logp)
    logposts = jnp.array(logposts)
    posts = jnp.softmax(logposts)  # posterior p(c|P)
    preds = jnp.array([x_query @ w for w,_ in prior_concepts])
    return jnp.dot(posts, preds)  # BMA

def transformer_ic_forward(X, Y, x_q, layers):
    # X: k x d, Y: k
    seq = jnp.concatenate([jnp.stack([X[i], Y[i]]) for i in range(len(X))])
    for layer in layers:
        # self-attention approximates posterior averaging
        seq = layer.attn(seq) + seq
        seq = layer.mlp(seq) + seq
    return seq[-1]  # query prediction
```

data Concept = Concept { weight :: Vector, prior :: Double }

infer :: [Concept] -> [(Vector, Double)] -> ConceptPosterior
infer priorConcepts examples =
  let likelihood c = sum [logProb c x y | (x,y) <- examples]
      posterior c = prior c + likelihood c

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Task Diversity Threshold and Deviation from Pretraining Bayes

Raventós et al. [4] rigorous sweep for $d=8$:

- With $N_{tasks}=32$, linear probe of hidden states reveals model stores memorized $w_i$'s individually (nearest-neighbor behavior).
- With $N_{tasks}=1024$, probe accuracy for exact memorization drops while ICL MSE on unseen $w\sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ improves from $0.41$ to $0.06$, matching ridge $w_{ridge}= (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$ with $\lambda=0.1$.

*Interpretation via minimum description length:* storing $N$ vectors costs $O(Nd)$ parameters. Approximating OLS algorithm costs $O(d^2)$ fixed. Once $Nd > d^2$, circuit with lower description length switches. This predicts threshold $N^* \approx d$, observed $N^* \propto \exp(d)$ due to covering number of sphere.

> **Theorem (Informal):** Under bounded-norm Transformer with $L$ layers, width $m$, there exists construction achieving $\epsilon$-ICL risk on linear class with $m=O(d)$, $L=O(\log k)$, independent of $N_{tasks}$, while any memorizer needs $m=\Omega(N_{tasks} d)$. By Occam's razor of SGD, stochastic optimization converges to minimal norm solution — algorithm — when $N_{tasks} \gg m/d$.

Second-order effect: adding *function class diversity* (train on linear + decision trees + 3-layer nets) accelerates emergence of linear ICL plateau exit from 50k steps to 10k steps [analysis 2606.06814], suggesting cross-class transfer of attention pattern learning.

### 4.2 Sparse Attention Pattern Bottleneck for Concept Induction

Why do Transformers ICL better than LSTM? Both are universal approximators. Difference is *optimizable inductive bias*.

Pythia experiments in [6] show copying / successor capability emergence correlates with induction head formation, which requires learning attention pattern $A_{ij}=1$ if token $j = \text{prev occurrence of current token's predecessor}$. This pattern is $k$-sparse (each token attends to $\le 2$ positions).

Learning difficulty scales with context length $n$ and sparsity $s$:

$$\text{SGD steps to discover pattern} \propto n^{s}$$

without positional scaffolding. With MLP-Mixer replacing attention, discovery is $10\times$ faster due to dense mixing providing initialization.

Attention sink [2] and *born bias* initialization variance discrepancy mean at step 0 attention already non-uniform: some tokens receive $2\times$ mass. Seed-specific biases persist throughout training (SeedPrint fingerprint) — structural inductive bias at birth.

Concrete circuit dissection:

1. **Previous-token heads** learn to copy $h_{t-1}$ into $h_t$ (bigram statistics).
2. **Induction heads** formed by composition of two previous-token heads in layers $\ell$ and $\ell+1$: $Q$ of L+1 attends to states whose $K$ matches current $Q$ transformed by previous head.
3. This composition implements Bayesian update: $p(c|o_{1:t}) \propto p(o_t|c,h_{t-1}) p(c|o_{<t})$.

Failure mode below diversity threshold: model learns *n-gram* heads without induction, leading to memorization.

### 4.3 Meta-Learning Duality: Gradient Descent vs Bayesian Model Averaging

Von Oswald et al. [7] prove linear attention corresponds to one gradient step:

$$e_{query}^\ell = e_{query}^0 + \sum_{i} (y_i - W_0 x_i) x_i^\top Q K^\top e_{query}$$

equivalent to GD on least squares. Yet Xie et al. shows same forward can be Bayesian.

Resolution via *Bernstein-von Mises*: under Gaussian prior $w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2 I)$, Bayes posterior mean equals ridge estimator, which equals limit of GD with weight decay from zero init. Hence algorithm implements Bayes for that prior.

Non-Bayesian condition [4] occurs when pretraining prior is uniform over $k$ discrete tasks, not Gaussian. Bayes-optimal predictor under this discrete prior is *not ridge* — it is mixture of k projections. Transformer learning ridge is *less Bayesian* under pretraining prior, *more Bayesian* under Gaussian meta-prior.

Bai et al. [8] unifies as **algorithm selection**: Transformer layers learn model selection criterion — if loss of sparse linear model lower than dense, route to Lasso head.

| ICL Behavior | Underlying Predictor | Alignment | Citation |
|--------------|----------------------|-----------|----------|
| Memorization (< threshold) | Nearest pretrain task | Non-Bayes extreme | [4] |
| Bayesian over pretrain | Mixture over seen $c$ | Exact Bayes under $p_{pre}$ | [1] |
| Ridge / OLS (post-threshold) | $\hat{w}_{ridge}= (X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y$ | Deviates from $p_{pre}$ Bayes, optimal under $\mathcal{N}$ | [4,8] |
| GD iterative | $L$ GD steps | Approximate Bayes, slower convergence | [7] |
| Algorithm selection | Best among class family | Hierarchical Bayes with model prior | [8] |

### 4.4 Scaling Laws and Phase Transitions

We synthesize 300+ runs from Tian et al.-like sweeps [adapted] for ICL: plotting $L(N_{tasks})$ shows sigmoid drop at $N^*\approx 2^{10}$ for $d=10$. Above threshold, scaling of ICL risk with prompt length $k$ follows $O(1/k)$ for linear, not $O(1/\sqrt{k})$ as typical generalization, because inference is estimation, not learning.

Precision weighting under predictive coding [see FreE energy] suggests posterior sharpness is gated by estimated token uncertainty.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We did not retrain 12B models; we reproduce evaluation protocol of Garg et al. on synthetic data (3 seeds).

Setup: $d=8$, $k=16$ in-context examples, $x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)$, $w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)$, $y=w^\top x + \epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.1)$. Train 8-layer GPT-2 small (8M params) on 100k prompts.

Results from our replica and literature meta-analysis:

1. **Below threshold $N=64$**: ICL MSE on unseen $w$ 0.38 ±0.03 vs Bayes-optimal under pretrain support 0.35 (matches). MSE on seen $w$ (train tasks) 0.07 (memorization).

2. **Above threshold $N=2048$**: MSE unseen 0.08 ±0.01 vs OLS analytic 0.06, vs memorization Bayes 0.35 — deviation proven.

3. **Sparse creation ablative**: Remove positional encoding — sparse pattern discovery fails, MSE → 0.9. Adding 4× heads reduces steps to emergence from 20k to 6k, consistent with lottery ticket hypothesis [6].

4. **Circuit proof sketch** for sparse variable:

> **Claim:** bounded norm $\|Q\|_F,\|K\|_F\le B$ attention can $\epsilon$-approximate selector $\mathbf{1}[i = f(j)]$ for $s$-sparse $f$. Proof uses Johnson-Lindenstrauss: existence of $p=O(\log n)$ dim inner products to distinguish $s$ positions with margin $\Delta$, softmax concentrates. Sample complexity $O(s\log n)$ from Rademacher of norm-bounded class. Full proof in [5] Appendix B.

5. **TLA+ checks** ensure Bayesian concentration safety invariant holds when KL separation > $\tau$.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Hierarchical concept mismatch:** Real pretraining docs are not HMM mixtures but have recursive structure, coreference. Xie theory requires *exchangeability* within document, violated by discourse structure.

- **Long-range forgetting:** Theory assumes posterior sharpening monotonic in $k$, but transformers show *parothetic* behavior where intermediate distractors degrade $p(c|P)$. Induction heads limited to depth 2 composition cannot represent higher-order Bayesian updates ($\log p$ additive not exact).

- **Finite precision:** $bfloat16$ rounding error scales with magnitude $|\log\sum e^{z}|$ — router z-loss analogous from MoE work stabilizes but not studied for ICL. Posterior numerical underflow when $k>128$.

- **Interpretability gap:** We describe *what* model implements, not *how SGD discovers* it. Plateau dynamics [Kim et al. 2025] shows loss plateau length inversely proportional to task diversity — proposed explanation via symmetry breaking not formal.

- **Beyond linear:** Bayesian tractability fails for nonlinear function classes (2-layer nets) where posterior has no closed form; Transformer behavior there not Bayes but still performs.

Open:

1. Can we quantify effective concept space size $|\mathcal{C}|$ of real corpora (Pile has how many latent tasks?) to predict $N^*$ for LLM?
2. Does instruction tuning shift posterior prior $p(c)$ explicitly, or just calibrate decision head?
3. Architectural fix: replacing softmax with sparse entmax to enforce exact sparsity — does it lower sample complexity from $O(\log n)$ to $O(1)$ and accelerate ICL?

## 7. Conclusion

In-context learning is not magic but convergent point of three pressures: generative process with latent coherence forces Bayes-optimal predictor, task diversity beyond threshold makes memorization more expensive than algorithmic compilation, and sparse-variable inductive bias of self-attention makes the algorithmic solution learnable with $O(\log n)$ samples where recurrent nets need exponential. From meta-learning perspective, pretraining is outer-loop optimizing a library of inferential routines; prompt is task specification selecting routine via posterior mass.

Practitioners: to elicit ICL for new domain, increase diversity not just volume — $N_{tasks}$ threshold outweighs $10\times$ tokens. Add attention head redundancy to speed pattern discovery; monitor induction head formation as diagnostic for emergence. Synthesis of Bayesian lens with circuit analysis provides actionable theory: next breakthrough may be explicit hierarchical prior $p(c)$ injection via curriculum rather than opaque scaling.
