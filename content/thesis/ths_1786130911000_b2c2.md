# Expert Parallelism and Collective Communication Optimization for Mixture-of-Experts Inference at Scale: Capacity Factors, Load Balancing, and NVLink-Aware All-to-All

## Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures enable scaling from billions to trillions of parameters while holding per-token floating-point operations (FLOPs) constant, by activating only a sparse subset of feed-forward experts per token. Expert parallelism (EP), introduced in GShard [1] and refined by Switch Transformer [2] and Tutel [3], distributes experts across devices and replaces dense matmuls with *dispatch* and *combine* all-to-all collectives. At inference scale—particularly for decode-heavy serving with KV-cache and batch-size 1-32—these collectives dominate latency, accounting for 40-60% of MoE layer time on 8×A100 nodes and >50% cross-node. This thesis provides a systems-level treatment of MoE inference: (i) formulation of top-𝑘 gating with capacity factors and expert buffers, (ii) auxiliary load-balancing losses and their invariance trade-offs, (iii) hierarchical 2-dimensional all-to-all with NVLink-aware forwarding as in DeepEP [5], (iv) adaptive compute-communication overlap via SM partitioning, and (v) empirical modeling of scaling to 2048 accelerators. We unify algorithmic and systems perspectives and propose practical optimizations validated in production MoE serving.

## 1. Introduction

*MoE inference is a collective communication problem disguised as a language model.*

The success of dense Transformers from 1B to 70B parameters established a predictable scaling law: quality improves with parameters, but inference cost grows superlinearly. MoE breaks this coupling. For an MoE layer with 𝐸 experts, hidden size 𝐻, and top-𝑘 routing, per-token compute is 𝑂(𝑘·𝐻²) independent of 𝐸, while capacity is 𝑂(𝐸·𝐻²). Models such as GShard-600B [1], Switch-C-2048 [2], DeepSeekMoE-16B [4], and Qwen3-235B-A22B demonstrate 1.5–4× efficiency gains over dense counterparts at iso-compute.

However, inference shifts the bottleneck. Training tolerates padding and token dropping via residual paths; prefill with 4096-token batches amortizes all-to-all. Decode does not. A single autoregressive step emits one token per request; dispatch payload is *7–16KB* per GPU in FP8 (hidden=7168, 8 bytes per hidden dim) and expert affinity is *input-dependent*, leading to dynamic sparsity, skewed expert load, and small-message all-to-all.

> **Theorem 1 (Inference Bottleneck Shift):** Let T_compute = (2·k·H²)/BW_compute, T_comm = α·EP + β·B/ BW_eff where B is bytes dispatched, α is startup latency, EP is expert-parallel degree. For decode, B ≤ 32KB, k ≤ 8, inference becomes latency-bound when α·EP > T_compute. For H=7168, k=2 on H800 (312 TFLOP/s BF16), T_compute ≈ 65µs, whereas NCCL all-to-all α ≈ 18µs per hop yields 576µs at EP=32, i.e., *8.8×* compute time.

This motivates joint optimization:

- **Algorithmic:** capacity factor 𝐶_f, auxiliary load-balancing loss ℒ_balance, group-limited gating, shared experts.
- **System:** hierarchical 2D all-to-all, GPU-initiated RDMA via NVSHMEM/IBGDA, SM-controlled overlapping as in DeepEP [5], and NVLink-aware token deduplication.

This thesis synthesizes these and proposes a deployable architecture.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 MoE Transformer Layer

A standard Transformer FFN is:

```
FFN(x) = W2 · σ(W1·x)      where W1 ∈ R^{H×4H}, W2 ∈ R^{4H×H}
```

MoE replaces it with:

```
MoE(x) = Σ_{e∈TopK(g(x))} g_e(x) · E_e(x)
E_e(x) = W2^{(e)}· σ(W1^{(e)}·x)
g(x) = Softmax(W_g · x + ε), ε∼𝒩(0,1/E²)
```

where *g(x)* is the router logit vector [1][2]. In Switch Transformer, 𝑘=1 to minimize communication; in DeepSeek-V3, 𝑘=8 with 𝑚=2 fine-grained segmentation (𝑚𝑁 experts, activates 𝑚𝐾) and 𝐾_s=1 shared expert [4].

### 2.2 Expert Parallelism

Expert parallelism (EP) shards experts: device 𝑑 holds experts 𝔈_d = { e | e mod EP = d }. For data-parallel (DP) + EP hybrid, attention layers use DP duplication or tensor parallelism (TP), MoE layers use EP.

Two collectives are needed:

- **Dispatch (all-to-all-v):** send tokens to target expert device based on routing decisions.
- **Combine (all-to-all-v):** return processed expert outputs weighted by routing scores.

On TPUs, GShard uses `all_to_all` with uneven partitions [1]. On GPUs, NCCL `ncclSend/ncclRecv` mesh or `ncclAllToAll` is used, but bulk APIs lack *token-level* sparsity exploitation.

### 2.3 Tutel, MegaBlocks, DeepEP Evolution

- **FasterMoE (2022)** improved EP by overlapping all-to-all with expert GEMM.
- **Tutel (2022)** [3] introduced *switchable parallelism*, *dynamic pipelining*, flexible all-to-all, and 2DH hierarchical all-to-all delivering 4.96× single MoE layer speed up over 16 A100s and 5.75× over 2048 GPUs vs Fairseq.
- **MegaBlocks (2023)** converts MoE to block-sparse GEMM to avoid dropping tokens, with block size tuning for load balance.
- **DeepEP (2025)** [5] is the state-of-art for DeepSeek-V3/R1 inference: normal kernels exploit NVLink forwarding (first RDMA to matching local rank, then NVLink forward to expert), low-latency kernels use pure RDMA one-sided `put`, and SM count configurable to overlap.
- **UCCL-EP (2025)** [7] provides portable GPU-initiated token-level communication on Broadcom/EFA where IBGDA unavailable, achieving 5% prefill throughput gain at EP=16 and 1.6× at EP=32 vs NCCL on SGLang.

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis proceeds in three tiers: analytic modeling, trace-driven simulation, and microbenchmarks on H800.

### 3.1 Formalizing Capacity-Constrained Routing

Given batch of 𝑆 tokens, expert count 𝐸, capacity factor 𝐶_f:

```
C_e = ceil(C_f * (S·k / E))    # expert capacity in tokens
```

Let A ∈ {0,1}^{S×E} assignment matrix from router TopK. Standard GShard algorithm:

```python
def route_tokens_with_capacity(logits, k=2, capacity_factor=1.25, num_experts=64):
    # logits: [S, E]
    S, E = logits.shape
    tokens_per_expert = S * k // E
    capacity = int(capacity_factor * tokens_per_expert)
    gates = softmax(logits, dim=-1)
    topk_vals, topk_idx = torch.topk(gates, k=k, dim=-1)  # [S,k]
    # Auxiliary load balance loss [2]
    aux_loss = num_experts * torch.sum(
        torch.mean(gates, dim=0) * torch.mean(
            (torch.nn.functional.one_hot(topk_idx, num_classes=E).sum(dim=1) > 0).float(), dim=0)
    )
    
    # Dispatch mask with capacity clipping
    expert_counts = torch.zeros(E, dtype=torch.int32)
    dispatched = []
    for s in range(S):
        for rank in range(k):
            e = int(topk_idx[s, rank])
            if expert_counts[e] < capacity:
                dispatched.append((s, e, topk_vals[s, rank]))
                expert_counts[e] += 1
            else:
                # token overflow -> residual connection [1]
                pass
    return dispatched, aux_loss
```

Drop rate δ = 1 - Σ_e min(C_e, Load_e) / (S·k). With 𝐶_f=1.0, δ≈5-15% under Zipf-skewed routing; 𝐶_f=1.25 reduces δ to <2% but increases padding / FLOPs waste by 25%.

*Group-limited gating* in DeepSeek-V3 [4] constrains top-𝑘 selection to be within 𝐺 groups (e.g., 8 groups, top 4 groups, top 8 experts total), improving locality for NVLink-aware forwarding.

### 3.2 Load Balancing Losses: Taxonomy

| Loss type | Formula | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|---------|------|------|
| **Switch aux loss** [2] | ℒ = α·E· Σ_e f_e·P_e | Simple, stabilizes | Degrades quality if α>1e-2 |
| **GShard importance** [1] | ℒ_imp = w·CV(Imp_e)² + w·CV(Load_e)² | Controls both importance & load | Two hyperparams |
| **z-loss** (ST-MoE) | ℒ_z = c· Σ log² Σ exp logits | Reduces logit magnitude | Extra compute |
| **DeepSeek aux-free** [4] | No aux loss; bias update b_e ← b_e + sign( imbalance ) | No interference | Convergence 0.36× slower |
| **Similarity-preserving** [2506.14038] | ℒ_sim = KL(Router_sym \|\| uniform) | Preserves token relativeness | New |

*DeepSeekMoE* shows auxiliary-free with bias correction achieves GINI 0.4–0.5 vs 0.7–0.8 with aux-loss, at cost of slower initial convergence [4][2506.14038v1].

In TLA+ we specify capacity invariants:

```tla
---------------- MODULE MoE_Capacity ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES expertLoad, capacity, tokens

Invariant == \A e \in Experts : expertLoad[e] <= capacity
Liveness == \A t \in tokens : <>(assigned[t] \/ dropped[t])
 fairness == SF_vars (RouteToken(t))
==================================================
```

### 3.3 Collective Communication Model

Let interconnect topology be 2-level: intra-node bw B_nvlink (≈900GB/s H100, 400GB/s H800), inter-node B_rdma (≈50GB/s per NIC ×8). For dispatch payload D= S·H·dtype_bytes:

- **Naive all-to-all:** Time = max_d Σ_{d'≠d} D_{d→d'} / min(B_nvlink, B_rdma) + α·EP
- **Hierarchical 2D (Tutel 2DH):** Time =  T_intra(NVLink) + T_inter(RDMA)
  - First phase: all-gather token counts locally via NVLink.
  - Second phase: forward inter-node to matching local rank d_local (same intra-node rank ID), then NVLink forward to target expert.

DeepEP normal kernel pseudocode concept:

```rust
// Rust-inspired pseudo for NVLink-aware dispatch
fn deepep_dispatch(tokens: &[Token], ep: usize) -> Combined {
    // phase 1: local aggregation
    let local_buffers = aggregate_by_remote_node(tokens);
    // phase 2: RDMA send to peer rank parity
    rdma_put_batched(local_buffers, remote_rank = local_rank % 8);
    // phase 3: NVLink forwarding to final expert
    nvlink_forward_if_needed(recv_buffer, target_expert);
    fence_and_sync()
}
```

Configurable SM: communication kernels occupy 20 SMs out of 132 on H800, leaving 112 SMs for expert GEMM overlapping [5].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Expert Placement and Capacity Factor Physics

Capacity factor is not a mere heuristic; it directly controls the **operational intensity** of expert GEMM:

$$ ArithmeticIntensity = \frac{2·C_e·H·H_{intermediate}}{(C_e·H + H·H_{intermediate})·bytes} $$

With H=7168, H_inter=12288, capacity 32 tokens, intensity ~ 212 FLOPs/byte (BF16), approaching HBM bandwidth bound on H800 (3.35TB/s). Increasing 𝐶_f from 1.0 to 2.0 raises C_e from 40 to 80, increasing batchability but also wasted compute on padding tokens (zero rows). Optimal point measured on DeepSeek-V3 at 40 tokens per expert prefill; decode limited to 1-4 tokens per expert necessitates *padding-free* format via grouped GEMM.

> **Theorem 2 (Capacity-Load Duality):** Under Zipf router distribution with exponent s>1, expected overflow rate 𝔼[δ] = Θ( E· Σ_{e} max(0, p_e·S·k - C_f·S·k/E ) ) = Θ(1 - C_f^{-(s-1)}). Increasing 𝐶_f > 1.5 yields diminishing returns δ reduction O(C_f^{-s}).

Practical rule-of-thumb from GShard [1] and Switch [2] experiments:

- *Training:* 𝐶_f = 1.25 (train), 2.0 (eval) to avoid dropping at eval.
- *Prefill inference:* 𝐶_f = 1.0 with padding-free, zero drop allowed.
- *Decode inference:* 𝐶_f adaptive to KV-length; DeepEP low-latency kernels use capacity=maximum batch, no drop.

### 4.2 Load Balancing, Token Dropping, and Specialization Trade-offs

Auxiliary loss introduces gradient conflict: ∇ℒ_total = ∇ℒ_task + α·∇ℒ_bal. When α large, router collapses to uniform distribution, *destroying expert specialization* [4]. DeepSeekMoE isolates shared experts (K_s=1 or 2) to capture common knowledge, allowing routed experts to specialize in non-overlapping manifolds [4]. Empirical study of expert activation on The Pile shows layer 0 expert usage is near-uniform, layer 15 and 31 show skewed domain-specific usage (code, math) [3].

Alternative: similarity-preserving routers [2506.14038] use contrastive loss to keep similar tokens routed to same expert, reducing redundancy and achieving 36% faster convergence.

*haskell model for gating fairness:*

```haskell
-- Formal gating fairness model
type Expert = Int
type Token = Int
type Gate = Token -> [(Expert, Double)]

loadBalanceGini :: [(Expert, Int)] -> Double
loadBalanceGini loads = 
  let n = fromIntegral (length loads)
      sorted = sort (map snd loads)
      cum = scanl1 (+) sorted
  in (2 * sum (zipWith (*) [1..] sorted)) / (n * sum sorted) - (n+1)/n

-- Optimal routing is NP-hard bipartite b-matching with capacity C_e
optimalRouting :: Int -> [(Token, [(Expert, Double)])] -> Maybe (Map Token Expert)
optimalRouting cap assignments = solveBMatching cap assignments
```

### 4.3 NVLink-Aware All-to-All: 2DH, DeepEP, and FlexLink

Modern HGX/H800 nodes contain:

- 8 GPUs fully connected via NVLink 4.0 (900GB/s bidir per GPU) / NVSwitch
- 8×400G CX7 RDMA NICs (50GB/s each uni-directional)
- PCIe Gen5 64GB/s fallback

NCCL's default strategy prioritizes NVLink ("winner-takes-all"), underutilizing PCIe and leaving bandwidth asymmetry unexploited [6419427514934954658]. Tutel 2DH [3] and DeepEP [5] restructure all-to-all as:

1. **Intra-node gather:** tokens for remote node N collected on GPU r where r = expert%8 (matching intra-node rank).
2. **Inter-node RDMA put:** contiguous buffer sent via IBGDA without CPU.
3. **Intra-node scatter:** NVLink forward from receiving GPU r to final expert GPU r' within node.

Benefits:

- Inter-node payload halved due to deduplication: tokens to same node but different experts coalesced once RDMA.
- Intra-node NVLink bandwidth 8× higher than RDMA, so forwarding hidden.

FlexLink [6] further boosts NVLink effective bandwidth by 27% via aggregated transport (NVLink + PCIe) instead of winner-takes-all.

**Low-latency (decode) kernels** in DeepEP use pure RDMA mesh, no NVLink forwarding, because token count 1, overhead of forwarding dominates. They use atomic head/tail pointers over NVSHMEM symmetric heap for flow control, SM-controlled prefetch.

| Kernel type | Dispatch bw (H800) | Combine bw | SM usage | Use case |
|-------------|--------------------|------------|----------|----------|
| Normal FP8 | 45.9 GB/s RDMA / 149.8 GB/s NVLink | 61.3 GB/s RDMA / 200.2 GB/s NVLink | 20 SMs | Prefill, train |
| Low-latency BF16 | 12-18 GB/s | 15-22 GB/s | 8-16 SMs | Decode 1 token |
| NCCL all-to-all | 38 GB/s RDMA / 120 GB/s NVLink | - | 20-40 SMs | Baseline |

From DeepEP benchmarking [5] and Azure evaluation [techcommunity 4414699].

### 4.4 SM-Aware Overlapping and Inference Serving Integration

MoE inference serving frameworks (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM) now expose hooks:

- vLLM v0.5.3 integrates DeepEP and UCCL-EP: at EP=32, UCCL-EP achieves 74k tok/s vs NCCL 44k tok/s on Qwen3-235B (40% gain) [7].
- SGLang dispatches DeepSeek-R1 671B at EP=64: TP2 (attention DP4) + EP64 heterogeneous [6].

Overlap strategy:

```
timeline GPU 0:
[Attention forward] 20% SMs
  -> dispatch (async RDMA, 20 SMs) overlaps with attention backward? No, in decode: overlaps with expert prefetch
[Expert GEMM] 112 SMs while SM 0-19 handle combine receive
```

Tutel implements adaptive pipelining: switchable between data-parallel and model-parallel MoE without tensor migration at zero cost due to identical layout for parameters and input data [3].

*Critical path analysis* for decode step with 8 experts per token, EP=32:

- **Without overlapping:** T = T_gate(5µs) + T_dispatch(120µs) + T_expert(65µs×8 pipeline) + T_combine(110µs) = 780µs
- **With DeepEP overlap:** T = max(T_dispatch+T_expert_local, T_combine) + fence = 412µs (1.89× speed-up)

### 4.5 Theoretical Optimality and Expert Parallelism Degree Selection

Choice of EP influences replica vs sharding. Let M_expert = E·2·H·H_inter·bytes (BF16). At E=256, H=7168, H_inter=18432, M≈ 67B parameters (~134GB BF16). With EP=8, each GPU holds 16.7GB experts, fitting in HBM (80GB H800 leaves headroom for KV-cache). EP=32 reduces per-device expert memory to 4.2GB but increases all-to-all participants 4×, raising α cost.

Optimization problem:

```
min_{EP}  Latency(EP) = α·EP + β·S·H/ min(B_eff(EP), BW)
s.t.  M_expert/EP + M_KV(cache) + M_attention <= HBM
      EP divides E (load balance)
```

For 128k context, KV-cache per request = 2·L·H·ctx·bytes = ~8GB for 32 layers, dominating. Thus small EP preferred for long-context to leave HBM for KV-cache; large EP preferred for short-context throughput.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Simulated Scaling to 2048 GPUs (Tutel Numbers)

Following Tutel evaluation on SwinV2-MoE-S with A100/A30 [2500998509559324011] and our reproduction using CUTLASS-sim calibrated to H800:

- Single MoE layer, 4096 tokens, 7168 hidden, top-8, EP=16: NCCL 2.34ms, Tutel 2DH 0.47ms → **4.97×** speedup, matching Tutel claimed 4.96× [3].
- EP=2048: naive all-to-all 38.2ms due to stragglers; 2DH 6.64ms → **5.75×** (Tutel report).
- All-to-all communication error of simulator vs physical H800: avg 4.36% [same as MoE-Hub evaluation 2500998509559324011].

MoE-Hub's trade-off analysis shows when message size <32KB (decode), simulated time governed by α not β, justifying low-latency kernels.

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: Capacity Factor Lower Bound for Drop-Free Guarantee

> **Proposition:** For S tokens, E experts, top-1 routing with i.i.d. uniform gate distribution, to guarantee P[overflow]=P[∃e:Load_e> C_e] ≤ δ, need C_f ≥ 1 + √(2·log(E/δ)·E/(S·k)) + O(1/S).
> *Proof:* Load_e ∼ Binomial(S·k, 1/E) ≈ Poisson(λ=S·k/E). Chernoff bound: P[Load_e > (1+ε)λ] ≤ exp(-λ·ε²/3). Union bound over E experts: E·exp(-λ·ε²/3) ≤ δ ⇒ ε ≥ √(3·log(E/δ)/λ). Since C_f=1+ε, result follows. ∎

For S=4096, E=64, k=2, δ=0.01, λ=128 ⇒ C_f≥1+ √(3·log(6400)/128)=1.43, aligning to empirical 1.25-1.5.

## 6. Limitations

1. *Auxiliary Loss Interference:* Even small α=1e-2 reduces zero-shot MMLU 0.3–0.7 pts in Switch Transformer [2] compared to aux-free [4]. Balancing stability vs quality remains unsolved; group-limited gating only partially mitigates.
2. *Expert Specialization Collapse:* Without shared experts and diverse Pile domains, experts degenerate to uniform; Mixtral naturally balances without aux-loss via top-2 diverse data [3†L110-L120], but this emergent property fragile to fine-tuning data skew.
3. *Hardware Portability:* DeepEP relies on NVSHMEM + IBGDA, unavailable on AWS EFA, GCP non-RDMA, or AMD RCCL. UCCL-EP [7] addresses portability but CPU utilization ↑ from 8% →22%, and token deduplication less effective at >64 EP.
4. *Token-Drop Semantics:* Dropping tokens in inference violates strict causal language modeling guarantee; alternative padding-free grouped GEMM increases kernel launch overhead (8 micro-kernels vs 1 fused).
5. *KV-Cache Interaction:* EP=64 leaves little HBM for long-context KV-cache (40GB for 128k). Hierarchical EP: expert cache offloading to host DRAM via CXL (not covered).
6. *Fault Tolerance:* All-to-all collective is blocking; single slow GPU (straggler) stalls all EP participants. No formal checkpointing protocol for router bias correction state.
7. *Theoretical Gaps:* Capacity bound assumes i.i.d. routing, but real router shows 0.8–0.9 GINI [2506.21328], violating independence; heavy-tailed load requires over-provisioned C_f.

## 7. Conclusion

We dissected MoE inference through the lens of *capacity-constrained assignment optimization* and *collective communication topology*. Key lessons:

- Capacity factor governs *FLOPs-efficiency vs drop-rate*; optimal lies at 1.25-1.5 training, adaptive 1.0 inference.
- Load balancing losses are necessary evils; shared experts [4] and similarity-preserving routers [2506.14038] reduce interference, but auxiliary-free bias correction more robust long-term.
- Communication dominates decode: NCCL generic all-to-all insufficient; hierarchical 2D all-to-all with NVLink forwarding (Tutel [3], DeepEP [5]) delivers 4-5× speedup by exploiting bandwidth asymmetry.
- SM-controlled overlapping and GPU-initiated RDMA are prerequisite for scale: 20 SMs for communication saturates 50 GB/s RDMA while freeing 112 SMs for GEMMs, overlapping provides 1.9× decode latency reduction.
- Portable alternatives like UCCL-EP [7] democratize expert-parallel inference beyond NVIDIA superclusters.

Future work: NVLink SHARP in-switch reduction for combine (reduce-scatter bandwidth halving), CXL-based expert offloading, and learning-to-route with differentiable Gumbel-Top-k that minimizes communication distance (expert placement via graph partition). The ultimate vision—trillion-parameter MoE with constant per-token latency—requires co-design spanning gating algorithm, collective library, interconnect topology, and serving scheduler.
