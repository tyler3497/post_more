# Data Assimilation for Numerical Weather Prediction: Ensemble Kalman Filters, Four-Dimensional Variational Analysis, and Hybrid Ensemble-Variational Methods

## Abstract

Data assimilation is the statistical machinery that converts sparse, heterogeneous observations into dynamically consistent initial conditions for numerical weather prediction. This thesis develops the three families of algorithms that dominate operational practice: ensemble Kalman filters, four-dimensional variational analysis, and their hybrid descendants. Beginning from the Bayesian filtering formulation, we derive the Kalman gain and show how the ensemble Kalman filter of Evensen [2, 3] replaces analytical covariance propagation with a Monte Carlo sample, treating perturbed-observation and deterministic square-root variants, covariance localization through compactly supported correlation functions [10], and multiplicative inflation. We then construct 4D-Var as a nonlinear least-squares problem over the assimilation window, derive the adjoint equations governing the gradient of the cost function, and present the incremental formulation of Courtier, Thepaut and Hollingsworth [9] that made operational implementation affordable. Hybrid methods blending static climatological covariances with flow-dependent ensemble covariances are shown to unify the two paradigms [4], and the operational realization at ECMWF [7], incremental 4D-Var fed by an ensemble of data assimilations, is analyzed. The thesis closes with empirical evidence, fundamental limitations under strong nonlinearity and non-Gaussianity, and a perspective on emerging directions [6].

---

## 1. Introduction

Numerical weather prediction (NWP) is an initial-value problem. The atmosphere is a chaotic dynamical system whose deterministic predictability horizon is on the order of two weeks, and within that horizon the forecast skill is almost entirely controlled by the accuracy of the initial state [1]. Yet the true state of the atmosphere is never observed directly: the observing system, comprising roughly ten million daily observations from satellites, radiosondes, aircraft, buoys and surface stations, samples the state sparsely, irregularly, and through nonlinear observation operators with nontrivial error characteristics.

**Data assimilation (DA)** is the discipline that fuses this incomplete observational record with a numerical model's short-range forecast, the *background*, to produce the best statistical estimate of the atmospheric state, the *analysis* [1, 6]. Two intellectual traditions have competed and ultimately converged in operational practice:

1. **Sequential (filtering) methods**, rooted in estimation theory and culminating in the *ensemble Kalman filter* (EnKF) [2, 3], which propagates error statistics through an ensemble of model integrations.
2. **Variational (smoothing) methods**, rooted in optimal control theory and culminating in *four-dimensional variational analysis* (4D-Var) [9], which minimizes a cost function measuring misfit to observations distributed over a time window.

This thesis presents both families with full mathematical development, examines the practical devices (localization, inflation, adjoints, incremental outer loops) that make them operational, and shows how the modern *hybrid ensemble-variational* paradigm synthesizes them. Section 2 establishes the Bayesian background; Section 3 the methodology; Section 4 the deep technical dive; Section 5 empirical evidence; Section 6 limitations; and Section 7 the conclusion.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The state-space model

Let x_k in R^n denote the atmospheric state vector (winds, temperature, humidity, surface pressure, hydrometeors) at discrete time t_k, with n of order 10^8 for a modern global model. The evolution and observation models are

- **State dynamics:** x_k = M_{k-1:k}(x_{k-1}) + eta_k, where M is the (nonlinear) NWP model and eta_k is model error;
- **Observations:** y_k = H_k(x_k) + epsilon_k, where H_k is the observation operator mapping model space to observation space (dimension m, of order 10^7) and epsilon_k is observation error.

The filtering problem is to estimate the posterior distribution p(x_k | y_{1:k}); the smoothing problem estimates p(x_k | y_{1:K}) for K >= k. Under Gaussian and linear assumptions, the posterior is Gaussian and the Kalman filter gives the exact solution [1].

### 2.2 The classical Kalman filter

Given a forecast (background) mean x^f and error covariance P^f, and observation error covariance R, the analysis update is

> **Theorem (Kalman update).** Under linear-Gaussian assumptions, the minimum-variance unbiased estimator of the state given observations y is x^a = x^f + K (y - H x^f), with the **Kalman gain** K = P^f H^T (H P^f H^T + R)^{-1} and analysis covariance P^a = (I - K H) P^f.

The *innovation* d = y - H x^f measures the new information content of the observations; the gain K weights it against background uncertainty. For n ~ 10^8, storing and propagating the n x n matrix P^f is computationally infeasible, which motivates both the ensemble approximation and the variational reformulation [1, 5].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology follows the standard derivation chain of the field:

1. **Bayesian filtering formulation** - derive the exact recursive posterior and its Gaussian reduction to the Kalman equations.
2. **Ensemble approximation** - replace P^f by the sample covariance of N ensemble members (N ~ 50-100), yielding the EnKF [2, 3]; introduce *localization* [10] to suppress spurious long-range sample correlations and *inflation* to counteract systematic underestimation of spread.
3. **Variational formulation** - pose 4D-Var as nonlinear least squares over the window; derive gradients with the *adjoint model*; implement the *incremental* outer-loop/inner-loop algorithm [9] with tangent-linear resolution hierarchy.
4. **Hybridization** - combine climatological and ensemble covariances as B = beta_c^2 B_c + beta_e^2 (C circ B_e) [4]; survey operational hybrids, with emphasis on the ECMWF Integrated Forecasting System [7, 8].
5. **Evaluation** - cycling experiments on the Lorenz models for methodological validation, and documented operational evidence from ECMWF, the Met Office, NCEP and Environment Canada.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Bayesian Filtering Formulation

The filtering recursion follows directly from Bayes' rule. The *forecast step* propagates the posterior through the dynamics via the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation,

p(x_k | y_{1:k-1}) = integral p(x_k | x_{k-1}) p(x_{k-1} | y_{1:k-1}) dx_{k-1},

and the *analysis step* applies Bayes' theorem,

p(x_k | y_{1:k}) proportional to p(y_k | x_k) p(x_k | y_{1:k-1}).

When M and H are linear and all errors Gaussian, both densities remain Gaussian and the recursion closes analytically, reproducing the Kalman equations of Section 2.2 [1]. The analysis is then both the posterior mean and the maximum a posteriori estimator.

For nonlinear M, the extended Kalman filter (EKF) linearizes the dynamics about the trajectory and propagates P^f with the tangent-linear model M: P^f_k = M P^a_{k-1} M^T + Q. As Evensen [2] demonstrated, this linearization is both expensive (one TLM integration per state dimension in the naive form) and prone to unbounded instabilities when the linearization misrepresents error growth in strongly nonlinear regimes. The ensemble approach circumvents covariance propagation entirely: the forecast ensemble {x^{f,(i)}}_{i=1}^N is integrated with the *full nonlinear model*, and

P^f approx = (1/(N-1)) sum_{i=1}^N (x^{f,(i)} - xbar^f)(x^{f,(i)} - xbar^f)^T.

The sample covariance is rank-deficient (rank at most N-1) but flow-dependent: it encodes the *errors of the day*, i.e., the directions in which forecast error is currently growing [1, 5].

### 4.2 The Ensemble Kalman Filter: Localization and Inflation

#### 4.2.1 Update variants

The original *stochastic* EnKF [2] updates each member with perturbed observations y^{(i)} = y + epsilon^{(i)}, epsilon^{(i)} ~ N(0, R):

x^{a,(i)} = x^{f,(i)} + K (y^{(i)} - H x^{f,(i)}),   K = P^f H^T (H P^f H^T + R)^{-1}.

The perturbations are required so that the analysis ensemble has the correct covariance P^a in expectation; however, they introduce additional sampling noise. *Deterministic square-root* filters avoid perturbed observations [5, 12]:

| Variant | Update principle | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Stochastic EnKF | Perturbed observations y + eps^(i) | Evensen 1994, 2003 [2] |
| ETKF | Ensemble transform in N-dim space | Bishop et al. 2001 |
| EAKF | Adjustment of ensemble mean and spread | Anderson 2001 |
| EnSRF | Serial square-root update | Whitaker & Hamill 2002 [12] |
| LETKF | Local analysis at each grid point | Hunt et al. 2007 |

Square-root filters update the ensemble mean with the standard Kalman gain and transform the perturbations deterministically, X^a = X^f T, where the transform T is derived from the eigen-decomposition of the ensemble-space innovation covariance. They generally outperform the stochastic EnKF for small N and are now the operational standard [5].

```python
import numpy as np

def enkf_update(Xf, y, H, R, rng):
    """Stochastic EnKF analysis step (Evensen 2003)."""
    n, N = Xf.shape
    xf_bar = Xf.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    Xp = Xf - xf_bar                      # ensemble perturbations
    Pf = (Xp @ Xp.T) / (N - 1)            # sample forecast covariance
    K = Pf @ H.T @ np.linalg.inv(H @ Pf @ H.T + R)   # Kalman gain
    d = y[:, None] - H @ Xf               # innovations per member
    Eps = rng.multivariate_normal(np.zeros_like(y), R, size=N).T
    Xa = Xf + K @ (d + Eps)               # perturbed-observation update
    return Xa
```

#### 4.2.2 Localization

With N ~ O(50) and n ~ O(10^8), sample covariances contain severe *spurious correlations* between distant locations: two grid points 5000 km apart may show nonzero sample correlation purely by chance. **Covariance localization** damps these via the Schur (elementwise) product with a compactly supported correlation function [10]:

P^f_loc = rho circ P^f,

where rho_{ij} = C(dist(i,j) / L) and C is the fifth-order piecewise polynomial of Gaspari and Cohn [10], which is exactly zero beyond a cutoff radius (typically 1000-2000 km horizontally). Equivalently, in *domain localization* (LETKF), each grid point is analyzed using only observations within a local stencil, and the analysis is performed independently per grid point, which also yields excellent parallel scalability [5].

> **Theorem (Localization as rank enrichment).** The Schur product rho circ P^f with a positive-definite compactly supported rho is positive definite of rank up to n, restoring full rank to the rank-(N-1) sample covariance while preserving positive definiteness.

#### 4.2.3 Inflation

Ensembles systematically underestimate forecast-error variance because of (i) sampling error, (ii) unrepresented model error, and (iii) the variance-reducing effect of localization. Left unchecked, the filter trusts its background too much, rejects observations, and *diverges*. **Multiplicative inflation** rescales perturbations about the mean,

x^{f,(i)} <- xbar^f + lambda (x^{f,(i)} - xbar^f),   lambda > 1,

with lambda typically 1.01-1.10, possibly adaptive (e.g., estimated from innovation statistics) [5]. Additive inflation and stochastic physics (e.g., stochastically perturbed parametrization tendencies, SPPT) represent model error more physically and are standard in operational ensembles.

### 4.3 Four-Dimensional Variational Analysis: Adjoint and Incremental Formulation

#### 4.3.1 The 4D-Var cost function

Instead of cycling a filter, 4D-Var finds the initial state x_0 that best fits the background *and* all observations distributed over the assimilation window [t_0, t_K] by minimizing

J(x_0) = (1/2)(x_0 - x^b)^T B^{-1}(x_0 - x^b) + (1/2) sum_{k=0}^K (H_k(x_k) - y_k)^T R_k^{-1} (H_k(x_k) - y_k),

subject to x_k = M_{0:k}(x_0). Here B is the static background-error covariance. This is a *smoothing* problem: observations at the end of the window influence the estimated state at the beginning, exploiting the dynamics to extract maximum information [6, 7].

#### 4.3.2 The adjoint method

Minimization requires the gradient grad J(x_0). Direct finite differencing is impossible for n ~ 10^8. The **adjoint model** M^T, the transpose of the tangent-linear model linearized about the nonlinear trajectory, propagates sensitivities *backward* in time:

1. Forward: integrate the nonlinear model, storing the reference trajectory.
2. Compute departures d_k = H_k(x_k) - y_k and weighted adjoint forcings H_k^T R_k^{-1} d_k.
3. Backward: integrate the adjoint model forced by these terms; the accumulated adjoint state at t_0 equals grad J_o.
4. Add the background term: grad J = B^{-1}(x_0 - x^b) + grad J_o.

One forward plus one backward integration yields the *exact* gradient at the cost of roughly three model integrations. Developing and maintaining tangent-linear and adjoint codes for a full-physics NWP model is a major software investment, historically a decisive cost of 4D-Var [7, 9].

#### 4.3.3 Incremental 4D-Var

Courtier, Thepaut and Hollingsworth [9] showed that the minimization can be performed on the *increment* delta x_0 = x_0 - x^b using a simplified (lower-resolution) tangent-linear model, iterating *outer loops*:

J(delta x_0) = (1/2) delta x_0^T B^{-1} delta x_0 + (1/2) sum_k (H_k M_{0:k} delta x_0 - d_k)^T R_k^{-1} (H_k M_{0:k} delta x_0 - d_k),

where d_k = y_k - H_k(x_k) are the innovation departures computed at high resolution with the full nonlinear model. Each outer loop: (i) runs the high-resolution nonlinear trajectory, (ii) minimizes the quadratic inner-loop problem at reduced resolution with the TLM/adjoint, (iii) updates x^b <- x^b + delta x^a. ECMWF runs, for example, an outer loop at TCo1279 with inner loops at TL255, iterating three times [7]. The incremental formulation delivers an order-of-magnitude cost reduction and is the foundation of all operational 4D-Var systems [9].

### 4.4 Hybrid Ensemble-Variational Methods and Operational Systems

#### 4.4.1 The hybrid covariance model

Pure 4D-Var uses a static, climatological B that cannot represent flow-dependent errors of the day; pure EnKF suffers from rank deficiency and sampling noise. The **hybrid** combines both [4]:

B_hybrid = beta_c^2 B_c + beta_e^2 (C circ B_e),

where B_c is the static covariance, B_e the ensemble sample covariance, C a localization correlation matrix, and beta_c^2 + beta_e^2 = 1 are tunable weights. The variational minimization then inherits flow dependence through the ensemble term while retaining the full-rank stability of the static term. **4DEnVar** goes further, replacing the TLM/adjoint propagation of increments with ensemble trajectories: the increment is expressed as a linear combination of ensemble perturbations, so no adjoint model is needed at all [4].

---

#### 4.4.2 Operational systems

*ECMWF.* The IFS runs 12-hour window incremental 4D-Var [7]. Flow dependence enters through the **Ensemble of Data Assimilations (EDA)**: 50 members of 4D-Var at reduced resolution (TCo639) with perturbed observations, whose spread estimates the flow-dependent background-error *variances* used by the high-resolution deterministic 4D-Var [7, 8]. Model uncertainty in the EDA is represented with SPPT. This architecture, operational since June 2010, replaced evolved singular-vector perturbations for initial uncertainty and measurably improved probabilistic skill [7].

*Other centres.* The UK Met Office operates hybrid 4DEnVar; NCEP's GFS moved to hybrid EnVar; Environment Canada pioneered the operational EnKF with Houtekamer and Mitchell and later adopted hybrid formulations [5]. Limited-area systems such as HIRLAM demonstrated the variational path for regional NWP early on [11]. The community review by Houtekamer and Zhang [5] documents the fifteen-year maturation from experimental EnKF to operational staple.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Notes

### 5.1 Methodological validation on chaotic models

Cycling experiments on the Lorenz-63 and Lorenz-96 models provide the standard laboratory [6]. Key replicated findings:

- The stochastic EnKF converges to the Kalman filter solution as N -> infinity for linear models, and tracks the chaotic attractor with small N given localization and inflation [2].
- Deterministic square-root filters achieve equal or lower time-mean RMSE than the stochastic EnKF at fixed ensemble size, because they eliminate observation-perturbation sampling noise [12].
- 4D-Var with a window length matched to the error-doubling time outperforms 3D-Var and sequential filters in strongly observed regimes, but degrades when the window exceeds the tangent-linear validity range, necessitating multiple outer loops [9].
- Hybrid weights beta_e^2 in the range 0.25-0.75 consistently beat both pure-static and pure-ensemble endpoints on midlatitude test cases [4].

### 5.2 Operational evidence

Documented operational results include:

- Hamill and Snyder (2000) showed that a hybrid EnKF-3DVar analysis scheme reduced analysis and forecast error relative to either component alone, the founding empirical result of the hybrid programme.
- ECMWF's introduction of EDA-based flow-dependent variances and the hybrid system improved headline scores (e.g., 500 hPa geopotential anomaly correlation) and ensemble spread-skill reliability [7, 8].
- Houtekamer and Zhang [5] review multi-centre evidence that ensemble methods add the most value in data-sparse regions (Southern Hemisphere, oceans) and for rapidly developing systems where errors of the day dominate.

> **Theorem (Equivalence in the linear limit).** For linear dynamics and observation operators, Gaussian errors, and infinite ensemble size, the EnKF analysis mean converges to the Kalman filter analysis, and incremental 4D-Var converges to the Kalman smoother estimate at the start of the window [6, 9]. The methods differ only in how they approximate this ideal under operational constraints.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Gaussianity.** Both EnKF and 4D-Var are derived under Gaussian assumptions. For bounded, skewed, or multimodal variables (precipitation, humidity, clouds, chemical constituents), the Gaussian update can produce unphysical analyses; all-sky radiance assimilation remains an active frontier [6].
2. **Nonlinearity and window length.** The tangent-linear hypothesis fails for long windows and moist physics; incremental 4D-Var then requires many outer loops, eroding its cost advantage. Strongly nonlinear regimes motivate particle filters, which however suffer weight collapse in high dimensions [6].
3. **Rank deficiency and sampling noise.** Even with localization, N ~ 50 cannot span the unstable subspace of a 10^8-dimensional system; inflation tuning remains partly empirical [5].
4. **Balance and initialization.** Analysis increments can excite spurious gravity-wave noise; variational systems apply normal-mode or digital-filter initialization and weak-constraint terms to control it [7].
5. **Model error.** The perfect-model assumption (Q = 0) is false; stochastic physics and weak-constraint 4D-Var address it only approximately [5, 7].
6. **Adjoint maintenance.** Keeping TLM/adjoint codes consistent with an evolving nonlinear model is a permanent software burden, one reason centres are migrating toward adjoint-free EnVar formulations [4].
7. **Observation bias.** Systematic observation errors violate the unbiasedness assumption; variational bias correction (VarBC) estimates bias predictors inside the cost function [7].

---

## 7. Conclusion

Data assimilation for NWP has converged on a synthesis. The ensemble Kalman filter [2, 3, 5] made flow-dependent error statistics affordable by replacing covariance propagation with Monte Carlo integration, at the price of sampling noise managed through localization [10] and inflation. Four-dimensional variational analysis [9] made dynamically consistent smoothing operational through the adjoint method and the incremental formulation, at the price of static covariances and heavy software infrastructure. Hybrid ensemble-variational methods [4] combine the strengths of both, and the ECMWF system [7, 8] demonstrates that this synthesis works at planetary scale and operational cadence.

The frontier is defined by the assumptions the current synthesis still makes: Gaussianity, weak nonlinearity, and unbiased observations. Relaxing them - through particle filters with localization, weak-constraint formulations, coupled Earth-system assimilation, and the emerging fusion of data assimilation with machine learning [6] - will determine the skill of forecasts for the next decade. The quiet revolution of NWP [6] was, at its core, a revolution in data assimilation; the next one will be too.
