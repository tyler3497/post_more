# Causal Inference under Unmeasured Confounding via Proximal Causal Learning, Negative Controls, and Doubly Robust Deconfounding with Bridge Functions

## Abstract
Standard causal inference from observational data relies on no unmeasured confounding, i.e., exchangeability given measured covariates. This assumption is rarely credible. Proximal causal learning offers a formal framework for nonparametric identification and semiparametric inference when unmeasured confounders $U$ are proxied by two types of negative control variables: a negative control exposure $Z$ and a negative control outcome $W$. We review the proximal identification strategy via confounding bridge functions that solve Fredholm integral equations, derive the proximal g-formula, construct doubly robust and multiply robust estimators via the efficient influence function, and discuss adversarial minimax learning of bridge functions with neural networks and RKHS. The framework generalizes Robins' g-formula and unifies negative control methods, instrumental variables, and deconfounder methods, while providing testable robustness guarantees critical for modern high-stakes observational studies.

## 1 Introduction

The dominance of the potential outcomes framework in causal inference has institutionalized a convenient fiction: that we have measured all confounders. Formally, $Y(a) \perp\!\!\!\perp A \mid X$ for all treatment levels $a$. In epidemiology, economics, and algorithmic decision-making, this condition—*exchangeability* or *ignorable treatment assignment*—fails not by exception but by design.

> Unmeasured confounding is not a pathology of poor data collection; it is the default epistemic state of observational science.

Classical sensitivity analysis and instrumental variables (IV) provide partial remedies, but at substantial cost: the former bounds rather than points-identifies, the latter requires exclusion restrictions that are equally untestable and often implausible.

Proximal causal learning, introduced by Miao et al. (2018) and formalized in a potential outcomes framework by Tchetgen Tchetgen et al. (2020) and Cui et al. (2023), offers a third path. The key insight is *proximal*: although we cannot measure $U$, we may measure *proxies* that are differentially related to treatment and outcome. Specifically:

- **Negative control outcome** $W$: a variable that is affected by $U$ (like $Y$) but not causally affected by $A$.
- **Negative control exposure** $Z$: a variable that is associated with $A$ or $U$ but has no direct causal effect on $Y$ or $W$ except through $U$ and $A$.

Examples abound: in a study of smoking ($A$) on lung cancer ($Y$), past physician prescribing preference might serve as $Z$, and a future health outcome known not to be caused by smoking (e.g., traffic injury) as $W$. Both share latent confounding structure without being on the causal path.

This paper contributes a unified PhD-level synthesis of:

1. The negative control assumptions and their causal DAG representation
2. Nonparametric identification via outcome and treatment confounding bridge functions
3. Efficient influence functions and doubly robust estimation
4. Practical learning via minimax and deep learning formulations

We argue that proximal inference should replace naive covariate adjustment as the default sensitivity-aware workhorse for observational causal inference.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes and Unmeasured Confounding

Let $A \in \mathcal{A}$ be treatment, $Y$ outcome, $X \in \mathcal{X}$ measured covariates, $U \in \mathcal{U}$ unmeasured confounder. We observe O = $(X, Z, W, A, Y)$ with $Z,W$ proxies. The counterfactual $Y(a)$ denotes the outcome under intervention $do(A=a)$.

Under unmeasured confounding, we have:

$$ Y(a) \not\!\perp\!\!\perp A \mid X $$

but we may hope for *latent* exchangeability:

$$ Y(a) \perp\!\!\perp A \mid U, X $$

and the consistency $Y = Y(A)$. Without further structure, $E[Y(a)]$ is not identified.

### 2.2 Negative Controls: Formal Definitions

Following Lipsitch et al. (2010), Miao & Tchetgen Tchetgen (2018):

**Assumption 1 (Negative Control Conditions):**

1.  $Y(a,z) = Y(a)$ (Z does not affect Y)
2.  $W(a,z) = W$ (A and Z do not affect W)
3.  $(Z,A) \perp\!\!\perp (Y(a), W) \mid U, X$ (Latent unconfoundedness)
4.  $W \not\!\perp\!\!\perp U \mid X$ and $Z \not\!\perp\!\!\perp U \mid X, A$ (Proxy relevance)

These are strictly weaker than IV assumptions: $Z$ is *allowed* to be confounded by $U$, unlike a valid instrument. This relaxation is crucial for applicability.

| Variable | Role | Allowed to affect Y? | Relation to U |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Primary exposure | Yes | Confounded |
| **Z** | Negative control exposure / Treatment proxy | No (exclusion) | Associated |
| **W** | Negative control outcome / Outcome proxy | N/A | Associated |
| **U** | Unobserved confounder | Yes (via Y) | — |

The corresponding DAG (Figure 1) encodes $U \to \{A,Y,Z,W\}$, $A\to Y$, $X\to\{A,Y,Z,W\}$, with no edges $Z\to Y$ or $A\to W$.

### 2.3 Classical Failures and Linear Intuition

In linear structural models, early proximal ideas appeared as *double negative control adjustment* (Flanders et al. 2011). If:

$$ Y = \beta_a A + \beta_u U + \epsilon_y $$
$$ W = \gamma_u U + \epsilon_w $$
$$ Z = \delta_u U + \epsilon_z $$

then regressing $Y$ on $A$ and $Z$ with $W$ as an auxiliary outcome can remove bias even when $U$ is never observed. Proximal causal inference generalizes this linear insight to fully nonparametric settings.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

We aim to identify $\psi = E[Y(a)]$ or more generally $E[Y(a) \mid V]$ for $V\subseteq X$, and the Average Treatment Effect $\text{ATE} = E[Y(1)-Y(0)]$.

### 3.1 Confounding Bridge Functions

Identification hinges on existence of *bridge functions* that translate between proxy distributions.

> **Definition 1 (Outcome Bridge Function):** $h: \mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ is an outcome confounding bridge if
> $$ E[Y \mid Z, A, X] = \int h(w, A, X) dF(w \mid Z, A, X) \quad a.s. $$
> Equivalently: $E[Y - h(W,A,X) \mid Z,A,X] = 0$.

> **Definition 2 (Treatment Bridge Function):** $q: \mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a treatment confounding bridge if
> $$ E[q(Z,A,X) \mid W,A,X] = \frac{p(W)}{p(W\mid A,X)}\frac{p(A\mid W,X)}{p(A)}??? $$
> In its standard form (Cui et al. 2023):
> $$ \frac{1}{f(A\mid W,X)} = E\left[ q(Z,A,X) \mid W,A,X \right] $$
> or for binary $A$: $E[q(Z,A,X)\mid W,A,X]=1/f(A\mid W,X)$.

These are Fredholm integral equations of the first kind. They are ill-posed inverse problems: solutions may not exist, may not be unique, and estimators converge at slower than $\sqrt{n}$ rates without regularization.

**Completeness assumptions** guarantee uniqueness and existence:

- **Assumption 2 (Completeness):** For any square-integrable $g(U)$, $E[g(U)\mid Z,A,X]=0$ a.s. $\iff$ $g(U)=0$ a.s. Similarly $E[g(U)\mid W,A,X]=0$ $\implies$ $g(U)=0$. This formalizes that proxies are sufficiently informative about $U$.

### 3.2 The Proximal G-formula

**Theorem 1 (Proximal Identification via Outcome Bridge):** Under Assumptions 1-2 and existence of $h$ solving the outcome bridge equation, then

$$ \psi(a) = E[Y(a)] = \int\!\!\int h(w,a,x) dF(w\mid x) dF(x) $$

$$ E[Y \mid Z,A,X] = E[h(W,A,X)\mid Z,A,X] \implies E[Y(a)] = E[h(W,a,X)] $$

Similarly via treatment bridge:

$$ E[Y(a)] = E\left[ Y \cdot I(A=a) q(Z,A,X) \right] $$

This is the *proximal g-formula*, directly generalizing Robins (1986): when $W$ is empty and $h$ is $E[Y\mid A,X]$, we recover standard g-computation.

```python
# Conceptual minimax estimator for outcome bridge h_θ
# Following Dikkala et al. (2020), Ghassami et al. (2021): Deep Feature Proxy Learning

import torch

def minimax_bridge_loss(h, g, Z, A, X, W, Y, lambda_reg=1e-3):
    # h(W,A,X) outcome bridge
    # g(Z,A,X) adversarial test function (critic)
    residual = Y - h(W, A, X)  # E[residual | Z,A,X]=0
    # moment condition: E[ residual * g(Z,A,X) ] = 0 ∀ g
    moment = torch.mean(residual * g(Z, A, X))
    # L2 penalty for stability
    reg = lambda_reg * (torch.norm(list(h.parameters())[0])**2)
    # adversary maximizes, learner minimizes
    return moment - 0.5 * torch.mean(g(Z,A,X)**2) + reg

# Alternating SGD: max_g min_h
```

Doubly robust identification combines both bridges: even if one is misspecified, the functional remains valid if the other is correct.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Negative Controls as Design Principle

Negative controls originated in laboratory science; their causal formalization is due to Lipsitch et al. and Rosenbaum. Proximal learning operationalizes them as *proxies* rather than falsification tests.

Critical distinction from IV:

- **IV:** $Z \perp\!\!\!\perp U$, $Z \perp\!\!\perp Y(a) \mid A$ but $Z \to A$ is strong. Unconfounded instrument.
- **Proximal $Z$:** $Z \not\!\perp\!\!\perp U$, allowed to be confounded, but $Z \perp\!\!\perp Y \mid U,A,X$ and $Z \perp\!\!\perp W \mid U,A,X$.

Thus many *invalid* IVs are valid proximal $Z$'s. Similarly, many biased outcomes are valid $W$'s.

***Selection heuristic:***

1.  *Temporal negative controls:* Experiences after treatment that cannot be caused by treatment ($W$), or prior exposures that cannot affect future $Y$ except via $U$ ($Z$).
2.  *Spatial / provider controls:* Clinic prescribing patterns, physician preferences.
3.  *Outcome controls:* Negative control outcomes sharing confounding mechanism (e.g., other causes of death).

A failure mode: if proxies themselves are directly causally related ($Z\to W$), identification can still hold under modified conditions but requires *two-way* completeness—an active area (Ying et al. 2023).

### 4.2 Proximal Identification: From DAGs to Integral Equations

The proximal identification proof rests on two steps. First, latent ignorability allows:

$$ E[Y\mid Z,A,U,X] = E[Y(a)\mid U,X] $$

Second, completeness links observables to latents. Since $E[Y\mid Z,A,X] = E_{U\mid Z,A,X}[E[Y(a)\mid U,X]]$, the outcome bridge $h$ essentially inverts the operator $T: L_2(W\mid U) \to L_2(Z\mid U)$ defined by $T h = E[h(W)\mid Z]$.

Existence conditions relate to Picard's theorem for compact operators: if the conditional expectation operator $K_{W|Z}$ is Hilbert-Schmidt with singular system $(\sigma_j, \phi_j, \psi_j)$, then a solution exists iff $\sum_j \sigma_j^{-2} |\langle E[Y|Z],\psi_j\rangle|^2 < \infty$. This reveals ill-posedness: small singular values blow up estimation variance, necessitating Tikhonov or ridge regularization.

```
Operator view:
  U ──K_{Z|U}──> Z                     U ──K_{W|U}──> W
  |             ↑                      |             |
  E[Y(a)|U]    E[Y|Z]                  h(W) ------> E[Y|Z]
      ─── inversion via completeness ───
```

### 4.3 Bridge Functions: Estimation in Practice

Solving $E[Y-h(W,A,X)\mid Z,A,X]=0$ is not standard regression—it's a *conditional moment restriction*.

**Approaches:**

1.  **Parametric:** Assume $h(W,A,X; \alpha)$ linear or GLM; use two-stage GMM (Miao et al. 2018). Simple but brittle.
2.  **Kernel:** RKHS embedding with closed-form solution via kernel mean embeddings (Kallus et al. 2021). DRKPV and DRPMMR estimators achieve consistency without density ratios: $$ \hat h = \arg\min_h \sup_{g} E_n[(Y-h(W,A,X))g(Z,A,X)] - \|g\|^2_{\mathcal{H}} $$
3.  **Neural Minimax:** DeepMMR (Zhang et al. 2024) parameterizes $h_\theta$, $g_\omega$ as deep nets and solves $$ \min_\theta \max_\omega E_n[ (Y-h_\theta)g_\omega - g_\omega^2/2 ] $$ with spectral normalization for stability. Consistency guarantees follow from Rademacher complexity of critic class.

Python/Rust analogy for treatment bridge:

```rust
// Rust sketch: adversarial training loop
fn train_proximal(h: &mut Network, q: &mut Network, data: &Dataset) {
    for (z,a,x,w,y) in data.iter() {
        let h_w = h.forward(w,a,x);
        let q_z = q.forward(z,a,x);
        // outcome bridge loss via treatment bridge as Riesz representer
        let loss_dr = (y - h_w) * q_z + h_w; // doubly robust pseudo-outcome
        loss_dr.backward();
    }
}
```

A practical tip: cross-fitting (DML) is mandatory. Estimate bridges on fold $k^c$, compute pseudo-outcome on fold $k$, aggregate. This removes Donsker conditions and allows ML estimators with $o_p(n^{-1/4})$ rates.

### 4.4 Doubly Robust and Multiply Robust Inference

Let $h$ solve outcome bridge, $q$ solve treatment bridge. Then the efficient influence function (EIF) for $\psi(a)$ is, under non-parametric model $\mathcal{M}_{np}$ (Cui et al. 2023):

$$ \text{EIF}(O;\psi(a),h,q) = I(A=a)q(Z,A,X)(Y - h(W,A,X)) + h(W,a,X) - \psi(a) $$

Hence estimator:

$$ \hat\psi_{DR}(a) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n I(A_i=a) \hat q(Z_i,a,X_i)(Y_i - \hat h(W_i,a,X_i)) + \hat h(W_i,a,X_i) $$

**Theorem 2 (Double Robustness):** If either (i) $h$ correctly solves its integral equation, or (ii) $q$ correctly does, then $\hat\psi_{DR}$ is consistent for $E[Y(a)]$, assuming completeness and some regularity. If both are correct and estimated at $o_p(n^{-1/4})$, then $\sqrt{n}(\hat\psi_{DR} - \psi)$ is asymptotically normal and semiparametrically efficient, with variance = $E[\text{EIF}^2]$.

Extensions to *multiply robust* exist for longitudinal settings (Tchetgen Tchetgen et al. 2020): with time-varying $A_t$, we need sequential bridges $h_t, q_t$, and robustness holds if at each time point *either* $h_t$ *or* $q_t$ is correct ($2^T$ possibilities).

This double robustness mirrors standard AIPW but is *structurally different*: standard DR protects against outcome-regression vs propensity misspecification; proximal DR protects against two *different* solutions to ill-posed inverse problems representing the *same* latent confounding.

| Property | Standard DR (AIPW) | Proximal DR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nuisances | $\mu(A,X)=E[Y|A,X]$, $e(X)=P(A|X)$ | $h(W,A,X)$, $q(Z,A,X)$ |
| Consistency needs | $\mu$ or $e$ correct | $h$ or $q$ correct |
| Efficiency requires | Both | Both + cross-fit |
| Difficulty | Regression/classification | Inverse problems |

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs / Analysis

### 5.1 Identification Proof Sketch

We prove Theorem 1 quickly. Assume $E[Y\mid U,A,X]=E[Y(a)\mid U,X]$ (latent exchangeability) and $W\perp\!\!\perp (A,Z) \mid U,X$, $Y\perp\!\!\perp Z \mid U,A,X$.

If $h$ solves $E[Y\mid Z,A,X]=E[h(W,A,X)\mid Z,A,X]$, then by tower:

$$ E[h(W,A,X)\mid U,A,X] = E[Y\mid U,A,X] $$

holds via completeness (since $E[ E[h(W)|U]-E[Y|U] \mid Z,A,X]=0$ implies numerator zero). Then

$$ E[Y(a)] = E_{U,X}[E[Y(a)\mid U,X]] = E_{U,X}[E[Y\mid U,A=a,X]] = E_{U,X}[E[h(W,a,X)\mid U,X]] = E[h(W,a,X)] $$

which depends only on observed $F(W,X)$. Same logic yields treatment bridge form via importance weighting.

### 5.2 Synthetic Data Experiment Design

Following settings in Kallus et al. (2021) and Mastouri et al. (2021):

- Simulate $U\sim N(0,1)$, $X\sim N(0, I_d)$ with $d=5$
- $Z\mid U,X \sim N(\alpha_z U + \beta_z X, 0.5^2)$, $W\mid U,X\sim N(\alpha_w U + \beta_w X,0.5^2)$
- $A\mid U,X,Z \sim Bernoulli(\text{logit}^{-1}(\gamma_u U + \gamma_x X + \gamma_z Z))$
- $Y = \tau(A,X) + \lambda_u U + \epsilon$, with heterogeneous treatment $\tau(A,X)=A(1+0.5X_1)$ 

We compare:

1.  Naive IPW/G-computation (biased under $U$)
2.  Linear proximal (Miao et al. 2018)
3.  Kernel KPV (GMM + RKHS)
4.  DRKPV and DRPMMR (density-ratio-free)
5.  DeepMMR (NN)

Metrics: bias, RMSE, coverage of 95% CI based on EIF variance (with cross-fitting 2-fold).

**Expected result (stylized from literature):** Under correct linear specification, all proximal methods remove bias (>90% reduction vs naive). Under non-linear $h(U)$ misspecification, kernel and neural maintain coverage ~92-95% while linear drops to ~60%. Doubly robust version reduces RMSE 20-30% over single bridge when both bridges are estimable, and retains consistency when one bridge function class is misspecified—e.g., $h$ linear while true $h$ non-linear, but $q$ RKHS adequate.

Sample Python bootstrap SE:

```python
def dr_ci(Y, A, W, Z, X, h_hat, q_hat, a=1, B=500):
    import numpy as np
    psi_hat = np.mean((A==a)*q_hat(Z,a,X)*(Y-h_hat(W,a,X)) + h_hat(W,a,X))
    eff = (A==a)*q_hat(Z,A,X)*(Y-h_hat(W,A,X)) + h_hat(W,a,X) - psi_hat
    se = np.std(eff)/np.sqrt(len(Y))
    return psi_hat, (psi_hat-1.96*se, psi_hat+1.96*se)
```

### 5.3 Efficiency Bound

Under $\mathcal{M}_{np}$ (all observed distributions unrestricted but bridge exists), semiparametric efficiency bound for $E[Y(a)]$ is $\text{Var}(\text{EIF})$. This bound is *larger* than under unconfoundedness—paying price for not observing $U$. Ai & Shan (2025) show joint estimation of bridge + ATE via GMM with increasing sieve moments attains this bound, unlike plug-in sequential estimators which lose efficiency due to ignoring correlation between steps.

## 6 Limitations & Future Work

**1. Completeness is untestable.** Like IV relevance but stronger: it requires proxies to be *sufficiently* variable relative to $U$. In $L_2$ terms, completeness fails if proxy dimension < latent dimension, or measurement error too large. Diagnostic tests exist (based on singular values of empirical operator $\hat T$) but lack power. Without completeness, bridge uniqueness fails though *ATE* may still be identified if any solution yields same functional—*identification without uniqueness*.

**2. Ill-posedness and slow rates.** Since $T^{-1}$ is unbounded, minimax rates for $h$ can be as slow as $n^{-\beta/(2\beta+p)}$ where $\beta$ smoothness, $p$ dimension of $U$. This affects CI length. Tikhonov $L_2$ regularization $\| \hat T h - r\|^2 + \lambda \|h\|^2$ restores $\sqrt{n}$ for ATE even when $h$ itself converges slower, but tuning $\lambda$ via data-driven Lepski or balancing principle remains open for doubly robust versions.

**3. Proxy selection and validity.** Assumption $Z\perp\!\!\perp Y\mid U,A,X$ is untestable. If $Z$ has direct effect $\zeta$ on $Y$, bias = $\zeta E[q(Z)]$ proportional to direct effect. Recent work on *partially valid* proxies (Wang et al. ~2024) proposes sensitivity analyses indexed by proportion of valid proxies—akin to invalid IV literature with majority/plurality rules. Automatic proxy discovery from high-dimensional EHR using representation learning is frontier.

**4. Longitudinal and mediated confounding.** Proximal mediation analysis for natural direct/indirect effects requires nested bridges solving $E[h_1(W,M,A)\mid Z,A,X]$, $E[h_2(W,A)\mid Z,A,X]$. Multiply robust estimators require $4$ nuisance configurations correct out of $2^K$; efficient implementation is heavy. Extension to continuous-time proximal inference (e.g., survival analysis with time-varying $A(t)$) via proximal martingale theory (Ying et al. 2022) is nascent.

**5. Computational tractability.** Minimax neural estimators solve non-convex non-concave saddle-point problem $\min_\theta \max_\omega L(\theta,\omega)$. Convergence guarantees rely on two-timescale GDA or extragradient. Kernel methods scale $O(n^3)$. Scalable approximations (Nystrom, random Fourier features, neural tangent kernel regime) trade statistical for computational efficiency.

**Future:** Causal representation learning where latent $U$ is recovered via VAE/contrastive learning from proxies, then standard deconfounding applies; connections to *causal de Finetti* and deconfounder (Wang & Blei) critiques; integration with conformal inference for proximal prediction intervals.

## 7 Conclusion

Proximal causal learning reframes unmeasured confounding from an insurmountable barrier to a structured inverse problem solvable via proxies that are often already collected. By positing existence of a pair of negative control variables $(Z,W)$, completeness, and bridge functions solving Fredholm equations, we obtain nonparametric identification of average and conditional treatment effects via the proximal g-formula—strictly generalizing both standard g-formula and negative control bias correction.

The theory's elegance lies in its duality: two bridges, outcome $h(W,A,X)$ and treatment $q(Z,A,X)$, each sufficient alone, both together yielding double robustness, semiparametric efficiency, and practical adversarial learning algorithms. As observational datasets grow richer but randomized trials remain expensive or unethical, proximal methods provide the credible, testable-robust inference layer needed to move beyond naive no-unmeasured-confounding.

> From proxies to practice: we never observe the confounder, only its shadows—$Z$ and $W$—and yet, under completeness, shadows suffice to reconstruct the light.
