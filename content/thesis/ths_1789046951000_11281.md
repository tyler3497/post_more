# Succinct Zero-Knowledge Arguments: Sum-Check, KZG Commitments, and the Plonk Permutation Argument

## Abstract

Zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (zk-SNARKs) compress arbitrarily large computations into proofs of a few hundred bytes that verify in milliseconds, revealing nothing about the private witness beyond the truth of the statement. This thesis traces the theoretical spine of the modern zk-SNARK stack: the arithmetization of computation as arithmetic circuits and rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS); the sum-check protocol, the central interactive workhorse for delegating sums of multilinear polynomials; Kate–Zaverucha–Goldberg (KZG) polynomial commitments, which compress an entire polynomial into a single group element with constant-size evaluation proofs; and the Plonk permutation argument, which enforces global copy constraints between circuit wires with one grand-product polynomial. We show how these components compose into a universal zk-SNARK with an updatable structured reference string, how the Fiat–Shamir heuristic removes interaction, and how recursive composition — through Halo-style nested amortization and Nova-style folding schemes — yields incrementally verifiable computation without any trusted setup. Asymptotic cost models, formal soundness statements, and published performance figures are developed throughout, alongside an honest account of the limitations that remain.

## 1. Introduction

The dream of *verifiable delegation* — convincing a weak verifier that a large computation was performed correctly, in far less time than re-running it — was formalized in the theory of interactive proofs. A zero-knowledge *succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge* pushes this dream to its limit: the prover emits a single short message, the verifier checks it in time polylogarithmic in the computation size, and nothing about the secret witness leaks.

The modern lineage begins with pairing-based constructions achieving sublinear, and then constant-size, proofs [1]. The practical turn came with compilers from C programs to quadratic arithmetic programs and the first deployed systems [3], culminating in the flagship deployment of private payments, where transactions under one kilobyte verify in under six milliseconds [4]. Two subsequent ideas reshaped the field.

This thesis is organized as a single coherent narrative rather than a catalog. We develop each primitive from first principles — arithmetization, the sum-check protocol, KZG commitments, the Plonk permutation argument — and then show how they interlock: sum-check reduces high-dimensional claims to single evaluations, KZG compresses those evaluations into constant-size objects, the permutation argument glues wire values together across the whole circuit, and recursion folds everything into one continuously growing proof.

> **Theorem (Informal, zk-SNARK):** There exist argument systems for arithmetic-circuit satisfiability in which the proof consists of *O(1)* group elements, verification costs *O(1)* pairings plus time linear in the public input, and the prover runs in quasilinear time — under falsifiable pairing assumptions and a structured reference string.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The Security Properties

An argument system for a relation *R* involves a prover *P* and verifier *V*. We demand three properties:

- **Completeness.** If *(x, w) ∈ R*, an honest prover convinces an honest verifier with probability 1.
- **Knowledge soundness.** For every efficient cheating prover *P\** that convinces *V* with non-negligible probability, there exists an efficient *extractor* that recovers a valid witness *w*. This "argument of knowledge" formulation is weaker than a proof (soundness against unbounded provers) but is what all practical SNARKs achieve.
- **Zero knowledge.** There exists a simulator that, given only the statement *x*, produces transcripts indistinguishable from real proofs — formalizing that proofs leak nothing about *w*.

*Succinctness* adds the efficiency demand: proof size and verification time are *polylogarithmic* (ideally constant) in the circuit size. A *preprocessing* SNARK splits verification into an offline phase on the circuit and a fast online phase on the instance; a *transparent* SNARK uses only public randomness.

### 2.2 Arithmetic Circuits and R1CS

Computation is expressed over a large prime field **F**. An *arithmetic circuit* is a directed acyclic graph of addition and multiplication gates. The *rank-1 constraint system* (R1CS) flattens such a circuit into a system of bilinear equations: for matrices *A, B, C ∈ **F**^{m×n}* and witness vector *w ∈ **F**^n*,

> **Definition (R1CS satisfiability):** *(A, B, C)* is satisfiable if there exists *w* with first coordinate 1 such that *(Aw) ∘ (Bw) = Cw*, where *∘* denotes the Hadamard (entrywise) product.

Each constraint captures one multiplication gate: the *i*-th rows of *A* and *B* select the gate's left and right inputs from *w*, and the *i*-th row of *C* selects its output. Addition gates are folded into the linear forms for free. R1CS became the *lingua franca* of SNARK front-ends because it is simple to generate from programs [3] and simple to commit to with linear-time cryptography.

| Arithmetization | Constraint form | Expressiveness | Typical back-end |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1CS | *(Aw)∘(Bw) = Cw* | One mult. gate per constraint | Groth16, Spartan, Nova |
| QAP | *A(x)·B(x) − C(x) = H(x)Z(x)* | Same, as polynomial divisibility | Pinocchio [3] |
| Plonkish | *q_L a + q_R b + q_M ab + q_O c + q_C = 0* | Custom gates + lookups | Plonk [5], Halo2 |
| AIR | Transition constraints on traces | Uniform repeated computation | STARKs |

### 2.3 Interactive Proofs and the Fiat–Shamir Heuristic

Interactive proofs (*IP = PSPACE*) showed that interaction plus randomness buys enormous verification savings. The *sum-check protocol* (Section 4.2) is the purest expression of this idea and the engine inside GKR-style delegation, Spartan, and modern folding schemes. To obtain *non-interactivity*, the Fiat–Shamir heuristic replaces each verifier challenge with a cryptographic hash of the transcript so far; in the random-oracle model, the resulting non-interactive protocol inherits soundness.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis is an *analytic synthesis*, not an experimental report. Our method is to place the major constructions in one unified cost model and derive their trade-offs from the published definitions and theorems:

1. **Common parameters.** Let *n* be the number of constraints (or gates), *d* the degree bound, *v* the number of variables in sum-check instances, and *λ* the security parameter.
2. **Cost accounting.** Prover cost is decomposed into field operations (FFTs), multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), and group exponentiations; verifier cost into pairings, group operations, and field work; proof size into group and field elements.
3. **Security models.** We track exactly which idealization each result needs: the generic group model (GGM), the algebraic group model (AGM), the random-oracle model (ROM) for Fiat–Shamir, or knowledge assumptions such as *q*-SDH and power knowledge of exponent [1].
4. **Empirical grounding.** All concrete figures (proof sizes, verification times, prover speedups) are taken from the cited papers' own evaluations [4, 5, 7], reproduced here as reported rather than re-measured.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 From Arithmetic Circuits to R1CS

Each constraint captures one multiplication gate: the *i*-th rows of *A* and *B* select the gate's left and right inputs from *w*, and the *i*-th row of *C* selects its output; additions fold into the linear forms for free. R1CS became the *lingua franca* of SNARK front-ends because it is simple to generate from programs [3] and simple to commit to with linear-time cryptography.

![Arithmetic circuit compiled into R1CS constraint matrices](ths_1789046951000_11281-0.webp)

Two facts make R1CS cryptographically convenient. First, constraints are *linear in the witness* on each side of the Hadamard product, so a homomorphic commitment to the witness lets the verifier manipulate committed linear forms. Second, checking all constraints reduces to *one* randomized identity: for random *r*, *⟨r, (Aw)∘(Bw) − Cw⟩ = 0* holds with overwhelming probability exactly when every constraint holds.

### 4.2 The Sum-Check Protocol

The sum-check protocol solves a deceptively simple problem: given a *v*-variate polynomial *g* over **F** with individual degree at most *d*, and a claimed sum *H*, verify that

$$\sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^v} g(x) = H$$

without the verifier evaluating *g* at all *2^v* points. The protocol runs in *v* rounds. In round *i*, the prover sends the univariate polynomial

$$g_i(X_i) = \sum_{b_{i+1},\dots,b_v \in \{0,1\}} g(r_1,\dots,r_{i-1}, X_i, b_{i+1},\dots,b_v),$$

the verifier checks *g_i(0) + g_i(1) = H_{i−1}* (with *H_0 = H*), samples a random challenge *r_i*, and sets *H_i = g_i(r_i)*. After *v* rounds the claim is reduced to a *single evaluation* *g(r₁,…,r_v) = H_v*, which the verifier checks with one oracle query.

> **Theorem (Sum-check soundness):** If the initial claim is false, then for uniformly random challenges, the verifier accepts with probability at most *vd/|**F**|*, by the Schwartz–Zippel lemma applied round by round.

The protocol's economics are striking: the prover sends *v* univariate polynomials of degree *d* (communication *O(vd)*), and the verifier's work is *O(vd)* plus one evaluation. With multilinear *g* (*d = 1*), each message is just two field elements; zero knowledge is obtained by adding a random blinding polynomial that sums to zero over the hypercube.

```python
def sumcheck_round(g, r_prefix, remaining_vars):
    """One prover round of sum-check for multilinear g (d=1).
    Returns the univariate round polynomial g_i as (g_i(0), g_i(1))."""
    def partial_sum(fixed):
        return sum(g(r_prefix + [fixed] + list(bits))
                   for bits in product((0, 1), repeat=remaining_vars))
    return partial_sum(0), partial_sum(1)   # verifier checks s0 + s1 == H_prev

# Verifier loop: check s0+s1 == H, sample r_i, set H = s0 + r_i*(s1 - s0)
```

Sum-check is the reduction engine of the field: it is applied layer by layer through circuits in GKR-style delegation, applied to the randomized R1CS identity of Section 4.1 in Spartan to yield a *transparent* SNARK (no trusted setup) with an *O(log² n)* verifier, and its amortization spirit underlies Nova's folding of constraint checking across two instances.

### 4.3 KZG Polynomial Commitments

A polynomial commitment scheme lets a prover commit to *f ∈ **F**[X]* with a short string and later prove evaluations *f(z) = y* with short openings. The Kate–Zaverucha–Goldberg construction [2] achieves the optimal shape: *constant-size* commitments and *constant-size* evaluation proofs, at the cost of a structured reference string.

**Construction.** Let *e: **G**₁ × **G**₂ → **G**_T* be a pairing and *τ* a secret trapdoor. The SRS contains *{g^{τ^i}}* in **G**₁ and *h, h^{τ}* in **G**₂. To commit to *f* of degree ≤ *d*, compute *C = g^{f(τ)} = ∏ (g^{τ^i})^{f_i}*.

$$e(C \cdot g^{-y},\, h) \stackrel{?}{=} e(\pi,\, h^{\tau - z}).$$

Correctness follows from *f(τ) − y = q(τ)(τ − z)* lifted into the exponent. Binding reduces to the *q*-strong Diffie–Hellman assumption: forging an opening for a wrong value lets one compute *g^{1/(τ−z)}* for adversarial *z*, breaking *q*-SDH [2].

```rust
// KZG batch verification sketch: one pairing check for many openings
fn kzg_verify_batch(csrs: &SRS, commits: &[G1], zs: &[Fr], ys: &[Fr],
                    pi: &G1, r: Fr /* Fiat-Shamir challenge */) -> bool {
    // random linear combination: F(X) = Σ r^i (f_i(X) − y_i), single quotient at z
    let c_agg: G1 = commits.iter().zip(powers(r)).map(|(c, p)| c * p).sum();
    let y_agg: Fr = ys.iter().zip(powers(r)).map(|(y, p)| y * p).sum();
    // e(C_agg / g^y_agg, h) == e(π, h^{τ−z})
    pairing(&(c_agg - G1::generator() * y_agg), &csrs.h)
        == pairing(pi, &(csrs.h_tau - csrs.h * zs[0]))
}
```

Batching is where KZG earns its place in SNARKs: many polynomials opened at many points collapse, via random linear combinations, into *one* pairing equation — the technique Plonk's verifier exploits to keep verification to a constant number of pairings regardless of circuit size [5].

### 4.4 Plonk's Permutation Argument

Plonk [5] reframed SNARK arithmetization around a simple observation: rather than encoding each gate's wiring into the constraint matrices, put *all* wire values on a multiplicative subgroup *H = {1, ω, …, ω^{n−1}}* and enforce the wiring with a *permutation argument*.

Each gate contributes three wire values *(aᵢ, bᵢ, cᵢ)*. The gate's *logic* is enforced by a selector identity that must vanish on all of *H*:

$$q_L(X)\,a(X) + q_R(X)\,b(X) + q_M(X)\,a(X)b(X) + q_O(X)\,c(X) + q_C(X) = 0 \quad \text{on } H,$$

where *a(X), b(X), c(X)* interpolate the wire vectors and the *q*'s are preprocessed selector polynomials. The gate's *wiring* — the fact that, say, the output of gate 3 feeds the left input of gate 7 — is a permutation *σ* on the *3n* wire positions: position *i* must hold the same value as position *σ(i)*.

The permutation argument proves this with a single *grand-product polynomial Z(X)*. Define *Z(1) = 1* and enforce, for each *g ∈ H*,

$$Z(g\omega) = Z(g)\cdot \frac{(a_g + \beta g + \gamma)(b_g + \beta k_1 g + \gamma)(c_g + \beta k_2 g + \gamma)}{(a_g + \beta \sigma(g) + \gamma)(b_g + \beta \sigma(g) + \gamma)(c_g + \beta \sigma(g) + \gamma)},$$

with random challenges *β, γ* and coset shifts *k₁, k₂* separating the three wire domains. If all copy constraints hold, numerator and denominator multisets agree and the product telescopes to 1 around the subgroup; if any wire is miscopied, the rational function is non-constant and *Z* cannot simultaneously satisfy the recurrence and the boundary condition *Z(ωⁿ) = 1* except with negligible probability over *β, γ*.

> **Lemma (Permutation soundness, informal):** If the wire assignment violates any copy constraint, then for random *β, γ ∈ **F***, the grand-product identity fails on *H* except with probability *O(n/|**F**|)*.

The full Plonk prover runs in five rounds: commit to wire polynomials; commit to *Z*; evaluate at the Fiat–Shamir challenge *ζ*; form the *quotient polynomial t(X)* aggregating the gate identity, permutation identities, and boundary checks divided by *Z_H(X) = Xⁿ − 1*; split *t* into degree-bounded pieces; and open everything at *ζ* and *ζω* with batched KZG proofs [5]. The verifier's work is a constant number of pairings.

### 4.5 Recursion and Composition: Fiat–Shamir, Halo, and Nova Folding

A SNARK that can verify *another SNARK's* verifier circuit achieves *recursion*: proofs attest to the correctness of proofs, enabling incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) — a long computation *y = F^{(ℓ)}(x)* proved step by step with proof size independent of *ℓ*. The obstacle is the *recursion overhead*: the verifier circuit itself must be arithmetized, and pairing-heavy verifiers are expensive to express as circuits.

**Halo** [6] attacked the problem without any trusted setup. Its key primitive is a polynomial commitment from the inner-product argument with *amortized* verification: an untrusted helper supplies the linear-time components of many opening proofs, and one aggregation argument certifies all of them — so the *marginal* verification cost per proof is logarithmic. Over a *cycle of elliptic curves* (each curve's scalar field the other's base field), proofs efficiently reason about each other, deferring full verification to the end of the cycle: the first practical recursive composition with no trusted setup, with proof size and verification time independent of recursion depth [6].

**Nova** [7] took a more radical step: it eliminated SNARKs from the recursion entirely. A *folding scheme* reduces the task of checking *two* instances to checking *one*, without proving anything about the reduction in zero knowledge. For *relaxed* R1CS — *(AZ) ∘ (BZ) = u·(CZ) + E* with an error vector *E* and scalar *u* — folding two instances with challenge *r* is almost linear:

$$u = u_1 + r\,u_2,\quad W = W_1 + r\,W_2,\quad E = E_1 + r^2 E_2 + r\,T,$$

where the cross-term *T = A Z₁ ∘ B Z₂ + A Z₂ ∘ B Z₁ − u₁ C Z₂ − u₂ C Z₁* is committed by the prover before *r* is sampled.

| System | Setup | Recursion primitive | Prover/step | Proof growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plonk [5] | Universal updatable SRS | Verifier-in-circuit (pairings) | *O(n log n)* | Constant |
| Halo [6] | Transparent | Nested amortization of IPA | Amortized *O(log n)* marginal verify | Constant in depth |
| Nova [7] | Transparent | Folding of relaxed R1CS | 2 MSMs of size *O(\|F\|)* | Constant in depth |

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

**Formal guarantees.** The three pillars compose with cleanly separated soundness errors:

> **Theorem (Sum-check + KZG composition):** In the algebraic group model with a *q*-SDH-hard pairing group, the protocol that (i) reduces an R1CS instance to a sum-check claim, (ii) reduces the sum-check to a single polynomial evaluation via *v* rounds, and (iii) opens that evaluation with KZG, is knowledge-sound with error at most *vd/|**F**| + negl(λ)*, and perfect zero-knowledge when blinded.

> **Theorem (Plonk permutation, [5]):** Under the same assumptions, the grand-product argument is a sound proof that the wire assignment respects the copy-constraint permutation *σ*, with soundness error *O(n/|**F**|)*.

**Reported performance.** The concrete figures below are as published by the respective authors:

- **Zerocash** [4]: the first large-scale zk-SNARK deployment — transactions under 1 kB, verification under 6 ms, hiding origin, destination, and amount simultaneously.
- **Plonk** [5]: proof of 9 **G**₁ elements plus a handful of field elements (≈ 400–600 bytes depending on curve); verification dominated by a constant number of pairings; prover faster than Sonic by roughly *7.5–20×* in group exponentiations for the fully succinct verifier.
- **Groth (2010)** [1]: the original sublinear — and with large CRS, constant-size — pairing-based arguments, establishing the knowledge-of-exponent / computational power-Diffie–Hellman methodology that all subsequent pairing SNARKs inherit.
- **Nova** [7]: per-step prover cost of two multiexponentiations of size *O(|F|)* — the fastest prover in the IVC literature — with recursion overhead of just two in-circuit scalar multiplications; the IVC proof itself compresses to *O(log |F|)* via a Spartan-variant SNARK.

The qualitative lesson is a three-way trade-off with no dominated point: *pairing SNARKs* (Groth16, Plonk) minimize proof size and verifier time at the cost of a structured setup; *transparent* systems (Spartan-style, Halo, Nova) eliminate the setup at the cost of larger proofs or folding witnesses; and *recursion* converts per-step proving cost into one fold per step, making unbounded computation practical.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Trusted setup residue.** Even "universal and updatable" SRS ceremonies [5] require that at least one participant behaved honestly and destroyed their trapdoor share; a compromised ceremony breaks soundness retroactively for every proof made under it. Transparent systems [6, 7] avoid this but pay in proof size or prover complexity.
2. **Idealized security models.** Knowledge soundness is typically proven in the algebraic or generic group model, and non-interactivity via Fiat–Shamir in the random-oracle model — heuristic idealizations with known (contrived) separations from real hash functions, and extra costs in the quantum random-oracle model.
3. **No post-quantum security.** Every construction here rests on discrete-logarithm or pairing assumptions, broken by Shor's algorithm. Lattice- and hash-based transparent arguments exist but with substantially larger proofs; the succinct post-quantum frontier remains open.
4. **Prover-side walls.** Asymptotics hide constants: FFTs over hundred-million-gate circuits demand tens of gigabytes of RAM, and MSMs dominate wall-clock time. Recursion amortizes verification, not proving — someone still pays *O(|F|)* per step [7].
5. **Arithmetization mismatch.** Real programs are not field arithmetic. Bitwise operations, comparisons, and memory accesses explode in R1CS unless custom gates or lookups are available [5], and every abstraction layer between program and constraint system is a potential soundness gap in the *front-end*, outside the proof system's theorems.
6. **Recursion fragility.** Folding and amortization compose soundness errors across steps; deep recursion requires careful concrete analysis, and curve cycles constrain the choice of fields and security levels [6].

## 7. Conclusion

The modern zk-SNARK is not one invention but a composition of four: *arithmetization* turns programs into constraint systems; the *sum-check protocol* reduces global claims to local evaluations with logarithmic interaction; *KZG commitments* compress those evaluations into constant-size, batch-verifiable objects; and the *Plonk permutation argument* enforces the circuit's global wiring with a single grand product. Around this core, Fiat–Shamir removes interaction, universal updatable setups remove per-circuit trust.

Each step in this lineage was motivated by a concrete deployment pressure: Groth's sublinear arguments [1] made proofs short; SNARKs for C [3] made circuits programmable; Zerocash [4] made them real; KZG [2] and Plonk [5] made the setup reusable; Halo and Nova made proofs recursive. The remaining gaps — trusted-setup residue, idealized models, quantum vulnerability, and prover-side resource walls — define the research frontier.
