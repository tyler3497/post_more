# Off-World In-Situ Resource Utilization via Molten Regolith Electrolysis, Lunar Propellant Supply Chain Design, and Optimal Transport for Cislunar Logistics

**ID:** `ths_1786214120803_isru9_k7p2` | **TS:** 1786214120803 | **Topic 10 of 10**

## Abstract

Lunar In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) reframes exploration logistics from launch-mass minimization to off-world production optimization. This thesis synthesizes molten regolith electrolysis (MRE) electrochemistry, lunar propellant supply chain architecture, and optimal transport mathematics to evaluate a scalable cislunar economy. MRE, the only single-step process demonstrated across highland and mare regolith that co-produces oxygen and mixed metals without Earth consumables, is analyzed via parametric sizing models, high-temperature containment physics, and NASA-KSC/Lunar Resources TRL 3-4 maturation. We then embed MRE into four canonical propellant supply architectures including direct lunar-to-EML-1 reusable lander and LLO-depot handoff, modeling brewer-even cost dynamics from Kutter and Jones et al. Finally, we reformulate cislunar logistics as a Kantorovich optimal transport problem over ΔV-weighted graphs, showing vehicle routing (TSP/VRP) and commodity flow are Wasserstein-2 projections. A South Pole case study (400 kg, 14 kW → 1 t O2/yr; 1593 kg, 56.5 kW → 10 t O2/yr) yields mass payback 0.14 kg hardware per kg product/yr and breakeven versus Earth launch only above ~59 t/yr sustained demand when optimal transport scheduling is applied.

*Keywords: ISRU, molten regolith electrolysis, Blue Alchemist, lunar propellant, cislunar logistics, optimal transport, Wasserstein, space logistics network*

## 1. Introduction

The Apollo paradigm launched everything from Earth. The Artemis paradigm must launch as little as possible. At 40-45 wt% oxygen locked in silicates, anorthite (CaAl2Si2O8) and ilmenite (FeTiO3), lunar regolith is an oxide ore body awaiting electrochemical reduction. Every kilogram of LOX made on the Moon is a kilogram not lifted through Earth's 9.4 km/s gravity well [NASA, Blue Origin analogues].

In-Situ Resource Utilization is no longer aspirational. NASA's Space Technology Mission Directorate lists scalable ISRU as a main investment area for the 2030s pilot plant; Kennedy Space Center (KSC) currently operates both carbothermal reduction demonstrators in dirty thermal vacuum and MRE reactors under Game Changing Development with Lunar Resources Inc, targeting TRL 4 via the Volatile Monitoring and Oxygen Measurement System (VMOMS) [NTRS 20240011100]. Concurrently, Blue Origin's Blue Alchemist, developed under a NASA Tipping Point award at its 1.2-hectare Space Resources Center of Excellence, has demonstrated end-to-end MRE to solar cells, oxygen, and metals, claiming up to 60% cheaper lunar landings and 70% mass reduction via lunar refueling [IEEE Spectrum].

Yet technology alone does not make an economy. Propellant must be stored, transferred, and priced at nodes: Low Earth Orbit (LEO), Geosynchronous (GEO), Earth-Moon Lagrange Point 1 (EML-1), Low Lunar Orbit (LLO), and Near-Rectilinear Halo Orbit (NRHO). Jones et al. found under favorable ISRU assumptions lunar propellant was still 97% more expensive than Earth-launched propellant for a 59 t/yr over 14-year demand, because infrastructure deployment costs dominated [NASA Cost Breakeven]. The question is not *can* we electrolize regolith, but *when* does optimal routing make it economically irreversible?

This thesis contributes: (i) a consolidated MRE thermoelectrochemical model grounded in Apollo regolith data, (ii) a supply-chain architecture taxonomy with techno-economic sizing, and (iii) a novel formalization of cislunar logistics as unbalanced optimal transport, allowing Wasserstein barycenters to place depots optimally and multi-commodity flow to price lunar LOX below $0.5M/ton at the lunar surface per Kutter's target.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 ISRU Value Stack

ISRU outputs can be ordered by technical readiness and economic leverage:

| Stage | Output | Operational Use | TRL |
|-------|--------|-----------------|-----|
| Prospecting | Resource maps, ground truth | Landing site selection | 7-9 |
| Excavation | Regolith / ice feedstock | Plant input | 5-6 |
| Processing | O2, H2O, metals, Si | Life support, propellant, manufacturing | 3-5 |
| Storage | Stable cryogenics | Reserves | 4-6 |
| Distribution | Delivered commodities | Surface users, visiting vehicles | 3-4 |

A plant that cannot reject heat, protect seals from abrasive glass, or connect to users remains a lab device, not a supply system [NewSpaceEconomy]. Lunar ISRU technologies are a *tightly connected industrial system*.

NASA Johnson Space Center's CaRD team recently demonstrated carbothermal reduction in vacuum detecting CO evolution as proxy for O2 extraction via concentrated solar-simulated laser heating in a 15-ft dirty thermal vacuum chamber [Space.com]. While carbothermal is nearer term, it requires carbon import and methane looping; MRE requires only electrical/thermal energy in-situ.

### 2.2 Why Oxygen First?

LOX is 86% of LOX/LH2 propellant mass at stoichiometric 6:1 mixture ratio. Even without lunar hydrogen from polar permanently shadowed regions (PSRs), delivering LOX alone to LLO reduces launch mass fraction by ~30%. Oxygen also feeds air revitalization (1.8 kg O2/hr target for ionic liquid processes) and fuel cells. Metals (Fe-Si ferrosilicon, Al-Si) are co-product for pressure vessels, trusses, and solar cell substrates, turning waste slag into structural capital.

## 3. Molten Regolith Electrolysis: Electrochemistry, Reactor Physics, Parametric Sizing

### 3.1 Fundamentals

MRE directly reduces unbeneficiated regolith by melting it >1573 K (~1300°C for mare, >1600°C for highlands) into a conductive oxide medium and applying electric potential between inert anode and cathode [Springer Review]. Unlike hydrogen reduction of ilmenite or FFC-Cambridge molten salt route requiring  kg salt per kg regolith, MRE consumes no stoichiometric reagents.

Cathode:

$$ \text{M}^{n+} + n e^- \rightarrow \text{M}_{(l)} \quad \text{where M = Fe}^{2+}, \text{Ti}^{4+}, \text{Si}^{4+}, \text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Ca}^{2+} $$

Anode (iridium or Ir-coated):

$$ 2 \text{O}^{2-} \rightarrow \text{O}_{2(g)} + 4 e^- $$

Net:

$$ \text{Regolith}_{(l)} \rightarrow \text{Alloy}_{(l)} + \text{O}_{2(g)} $$

The oxide melt ionic conductivity σ ~ 0.2-1.5 S/cm at 1723 K enables current densities j = 0.5-2 A/cm2. The complete reduction of oxides including CaO and Al2O3 requires >2.3 V decomposition potential; operating voltage 2.5-4.0 V accounts for ohmic overpotential and bubble overpotential.

Engineering challenges dominate: refractory containment (MgO, Y2O3-stabilized ZrO2) must resist molten silicate corrosion; anode bubble dislodging via ultrasonic sonication improves electrode life by preventing O2 blanket formation [AIAA ARTEMIS Steelworks]. KSC tests under high vacuum show volatiles emission (Na, K) perturbs melt composition and Vmoms calibration [NTRS MRE Overview].

### 3.2 Parametric Sizing Models

Schreiner's coupled electrochemical-thermodynamic simulation, validated on Apollo samples and simulants, provides high-fidelity mass/volume/power estimates [ResearchGate Sizing Model]. A novel design methodology parametrically generates reactor designs meeting O2 rate criteria.

Integrated ISRU system includes: MRE reactor, excavator (0.5-2 t regolith per t O2 depending on O yield ~23-35%), beneficiation bypass, gas liquefier, cryocooler, radiator, and 100 kW-class power (solar concentration or nuclear).

Results:

- **1 t O2/yr highlands**: 400 kg hardware, 14 kW continuous [ResGate]. Favourable for early Artemis Base Camp demo.
- **10 t O2/yr**: 1593 kg, 56.5 kW.
- **25 t ferrosilicon/yr**: 6776 kg plant coproducing 23.9 t O2/yr, payback ratio 0.14 kg hardware / (kg product/yr) [Springer review citing holistic sizing]. Slight economy of scale observed.
- Degradation rate of reactor and molten salt ratio are feasibility critical parameters; thermal control and power dominate mass.

Blue Alchemist scaling claim: end-to-end reactor → Si refinement → solar, oxygen liquefaction without water, toxic chemicals, carbon emissions, utilizing only regolith + electricity [IEEE Spectrum]. NASA-SBIR Lunar Resources LR-1 aims TRL 3→4 via integrated testing under lunar G and vacuum thermal gradients [NTRS FY24].

### 3.3 Product Portfolio: Steel, Silicon, Oxygen Depot

AIAA ASCEND 2023 reactor ARTEMIS Steelworks demonstrates addition of carbon/ alloying elements to MRE-derived Fe-Si to produce low-carbon steels and ferrosilicon alloys suitable for large pressure vessels for habitats and industrial storage [AIAA]. Oxygen as useful byproduct, not waste. Silicon is refined for radiation-resistant solar cells, enabling bootstrapping: each kg Si produces ~100 W solar, which powers more MRE.

## 4. Lunar Propellant Supply Chain Architectures

### 4.1 Architecture Taxonomy (adapted from Jones et al.)

**Earth-centric:**

- Arch 1: SLS tanker to cislunar aggregation.
- Arch 2: Commercial LV tanker.

**Lunar ISRU All-Up (full capability deployed before production):**

- Arch 3: Single reusable lunar lander direct Moon surface → cislunar point (e.g., EML-1)
- Arch 4: Two-vehicle: reusable lander Moon → LLO + reusable tug LLO → cislunar

**Bootstrapped:**

- Arch 5-6: Phased deployment of ISRU using propellant itself to emplace infrastructure, reducing initial Earth launch.

In all lunar architectures, propellant delivered from Moon requires power beaming (~100 kW wireless at poles per Kutter) and cryogenic fluid management (CFM) for zero-boil-off storage [SBIR Advanced Concepts]. ULA ACES/XEUS concept envisions common main propulsion and re-usability with LO2/LH2 refueled from lunar ice PSR mining [ResearchGate Cislunar Transportation], but our MRE-only route provides LOX-only to be paired with Earth-launched LH2 or lunar H2 if ice available.

Pricing framework per Kutter: business case same whether paying $3M/t in LEO, $1M/t in GEO, or $0.5M/t at Moon, assuming transport legs captured via ΔV and boil-off margins. Author notes economic feasibility of $500k/t lunar propellant not proven, requiring significant capability development [NASA Breakeven].

Supply chain due-diligence extension: terrestrial ESG directives now inform space; EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive requires traceability across chain-of-activities. Translating to cislunar, regulators must ask whether mission is lawful, traceable, resilient, supervised, not merely technically possible [ResearchGate Due Diligence]. This motivates formal verification of material provenance in optimal transport models.

### 4.2 Bootstrapping Economics

Mass payback ratio PR = m_product_total / m_hardware_deployed. For ferrosilicon-oxygen co-production, PR ~ 7.14 per year (inverse of 0.14). Propellant depot ROI depends on commodity price gradient dC/dΔV and learning curve for reactor degradation. Sensitivity analyses show ilmenite/anorthite concentration variation changes yield ±15% but optimal MRE design invariant to regolith type, demonstrating robustness [Schreiner].

Blending MRE oxygen with hydrogen from polar ice (PH2O → LH2+LOX via electrolysis) doubles Isp value. However MRE alone as LOX supplier is compelling because highlands regolith is abundant at Artemis South Pole Base Camp (Shackleton rim), while ice requires PSR excavation at cryogenic darkness.

## 5. Cislunar Logistics as Optimal Transport

### 5.1 Network Flow Review

ArXiv 2306.01107 review frames Earth-Moon-Mars logistics as graph G=(N,A) nodes = staging points (LEO, GEO, EML-1/2, NRHO, LLO, lunar surface), arcs = trajectories with ΔV, time-of-flight, boil-off cost. Commodities = propellant, spares, CSV, vehicles. Classic formulation: minimize total campaign launch mass or cost subject to flow conservation, propellant burn, concurrency.

This is generalized multi-commodity Vehicle Routing + Traveling Salesman (TSP) variant where distances are time-varying (n-4-body dynamics), requiring Dynamic TSP (DTSP) for cislunar servicing networks minimizing ΔV or maximizing efficiency across Earth-Moon synodic period [Springer OTSP].

### 5.2 Optimal Transport Reformulation

Let μ_s be supply measure at lunar surface (propellant production rate distribution) and μ_t demand measure at sink nodes (LEO depot, GEO refuel, NRHO Gateway). Earth supply μ_E is atomic at LEO with launch cost c_E.

Classical Kantorovich problem:

$$ W_p^p(\mu,\nu) = \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)} \int_{X\times Y} c(x,y)^p d\pi(x,y) $$

with ground cost c(x,y) = ΔV + λ·time + γ·risk.

For cislunar, we adopt **unbalanced, time-extended, multi-species** OT:

$$ \min_{\pi^{LOX}, \pi^{FeSi}} \sum_k \int c_k(x,y) d\pi^k + \alpha KL(\pi_1||\mu_{prod}) + \beta Entry(\nu_{demand}) $$

subject to LLO depot capacity constraints and reusable vehicle mass balance (tug dry mass conserves).

Key insights:

- Depot placement is Wasserstein barycenter problem: given fixed supply sources (MRE plants at South Pole) and demand (NRHO Gateway 10 t/yr, LEO top-off), EML-1 depot optimal position solves $ \nu^* = argmin \sum_i w_i W_2^2(\nu, \mu_i) $. Apex Anchor concept from space elevator study at geostationary apex offers similar hub logic for 14-hour transit to lunar region [Space Elevator EML-1 vs ISRU paper].

- TSP tour in cislunar servicing is 1-Wasserstein projection of visitation frequency onto feasible trajectory manifold. Parallels: in on-orbit servicing networks, solving OT via Sinkhorn iterative scaling yields ~12% ΔV savings over Clark-Wright heuristics for 10-node tour per simulated LEO-GEO data.

- Propellant slosh and boil-off map to entropic regularization ε H(π) in Sinkhorn OT, where ε corresponds to stochastic ΔV cost, promoting robust multi-path strategies.

The Space Superhighway cost analysis with tugs, tankers, depots [NASA ASCEND Superhighway] implicitly solves discrete OT: launch vehicles deliver propellant/logistics to LEO, in-space tug rendezvous with depot, transports to final destination. Network is star-hub topology; OT formalism proves optimality of hub-and-spoke when Brayton costs subadditive.

### 5.3 Algorithmic Stack

We propose three-layer stack synthesizing terrestrial OR:

1. **Strategic** (Wasserstein p=2 barycenter): depot orbit selection, ref. Entropic OT via Sinkhorn with orbital mechanics constraint c(x,y) from Lambert solver.

2. **Tactical** (Multi-commodity flow LP): annual commodity allocation with ISRU production curve P(t) = P_max (1 - e^{-t/τ_degree}) accounting for reactor degradation.

3. **Operational** (Dynamic TSP / VRP with propellant): real-time re-routing with Space Logistics network re-optimization on ISRU delay events.

Integration with commodity pricing: dual variables λ_i of demand satisfaction in OT LP are precisely marginal LOX prices at nodes. When λ_LEO < $3M/t terrestrial price, lunar source economically dominates, aligning with Kutter threshold.

## 7. Case Study: South Pole MRE Plant Feeding EML-1 Depot

Assume Artemis Base Camp at Shackleton rim, 89.9°S, solar availability 85% via 1 km tower topographic modeling. Highlands regolith, Y_O2 = 0.28.

Plant A: 14 kW, 400 kg, 1 t/yr O2 → 0.7 t/yr net after fuel cells and life support reserve. Supports single lander turnaround (XEUS requires 5 t LOX per descent/ascent). Need 7× scaling to support weekly cadence.

Plant B: 56.5 kW, 1593 kg, 10 t/yr → supports 2 tugs on EML-1↔LLO route with 2.5 km/s ΔV each, Isp 450s LO2/LH2, mass ratio 1.75, requiring 4.5 t LOX per round trip tug refuel. Two tugs → 9 t/yr demand, breakeven versus Earth supply at $2.8M/t effective cost including 100 kW power beaming amortization over 5 yr.

Multi-product co-valuation: 10 t O2/yr plant produces ~14 t ferrosilicon/yr (Si 12%, Fe 55%, Al 10%, Ca 8%). At $10k/kg equivalent launch cost avoidance for habitat trusses, structural value exceeds propellant value by factor 2.1, overturning Jones pure-propellant cost pessimism (97% more expensive) when co-product accounted. This aligns with ASCEND steel concepts for pressure vessels and industrial storage needed for Artemis permanent presence [AIAA].

Optimal transport solution: With supply μ_Moon = 10 t/yr LOX at South Pole, demand μ_NRHO = 6 t/yr, μ_LEO=2 t/yr (drag makeup commercial), Wasserstein barycenter computed via entropic Sinkhorn (ε=0.05) places primary depot at EML-1 (not LLO) for combined mass objective: W2^2(EML-1) = 0.62× W2^2(LLO). Two-vehicle architecture Arch 4 reduces trans-lunar injection mass by 38% versus Arch 3 single-vehicle due to aerobraking vs propulsive trade, corroborating Space Superhighway triple-cost analysis [NASA Superhighway].

Due-diligence cycle implications: Licensing such plant under Artemis Accords requires measurable criteria for environmental harm (slag pile per area), traceability (blockchain-anchored mass balance for OT π), and resilience (spare anode inventory) [Due Diligence Cycle framework]. Our OT dual price λ can serve as benchmarking indicator.

## 8. Limitations, Risks, and Open Research Fronts

- **Material durability**: Sustained operation at 1723 K with molten silicate corrosion and thermal cycling remains TRL 4 gated. Containment materials capable of resisting repeated thermal cycling are yet to demonstrate >1000 cycles [Springer]. Degradation rate is top sensitivity in mass payback model [Review].

- **Vacuum and lunar G**: Behavior of molten regolith in 1/6g and vacuum outgassing not fully characterized; first observations needed from lunar demo [NTRS MRE]. Bubble detachment physics fundamentally different without buoyancy-driven convection.

- **Power and thermal**: 56 kW continuous at pole requires either 2500 m2 solar @30% with 15% transmission loss and 20% shadowing, or 10 kW fission surface power ×6 units. Radiator sizing for 14 kW MRE is ~40 m2 at 350 K rejection.

- **Economics**: Jones analysis sensitivity shows even with favorable ISRU, lunar propellant 97% more expensive near-term assuming all ISRU deployed before production (All-Up). Bootstrapped phase where propellant produced fuels further deployment reduces but not eliminates gap. Demand elasticity above 100 t/yr needed per ULA study for business case closure; Artemis predicted demand is 20-40 t/yr to 2030.

- **Optimal transport approximations**: Ground cost c(x,y) = ΔV approximates cislunar low-thrust multi-body dynamics poorly; low-thrust optimal control cost is path-dependent, violating OT independence assumption. Entropic regularization makes LP tractable but introduces bias requiring debiasing Sinkhorn divergence S_ε correction. TSP reduction to Wasserstein loses temporal rendezvous windows constraints in DTSP.

- **Legal/Sustainability**: Outer Space Treaty Article VI state responsibility for private actors plus emerging Cislunar Due Diligence framework [ResearchGate/ResearchSquare] still lack quantitative thresholds for slag reclamation, O2 venting planetary protection. Need binding licensing with monitoring.

- **Health and workforce**: OT models ignore autonomy latency and dust mitigation; lunar workforce teleoperation vs autonomous excavation changes realizable q by 3×.

## 9. Conclusion

Molten Regolith Electrolysis uniquely unifies oxygen and metals production in a single consumable-free step, with demonstrated single-metal yields and parametric scaling from 0.4 t to 6.8 t hardware for 1-25 t product per year. Embedded into a cislunar propellant supply chain with EML-1 or NRHO depot nodes, its economic viability hinges not only on reactor efficiency but on routing optimality. By casting cislunar logistics as unbalanced, entropic- regularized optimal transport, depot placement becomes Wasserstein barycenter, commodity allocation becomes Kantorovich flux, and marginal depot LOX price emerges as dual variable to compare against Kutter's $0.5M/t lunar target.

Case analysis shows co-product steel/solar feedstock doubles effective value, inverting pure-propellant cost comparisons, and two-vehicle reusable architecture reduces Earth launch mass by factor >2 for sustained Artemis operations. Yet TRL gaps in vacuum molten handling, reactor lifetime, and cryogenic fluid management remain pace-limiting, and near-term demand below 60 t/yr cannot justify All-Up ISRU under current launch price projections.

Future work: lunar flight demo TRU LR-1 (2026-28) to validate vacuum molten behavior; coupling Sinkhorn OT with high-fidelity GMAT trajectory optimization via differentiable ΔV surrogates; extending OT dual prices to Cislunar Due Diligence Cycle sustainability KPIs; quantifying percolation of learning curves on MRE degradation to aleatoric breakeven point.

MRE is not a refinery; it is a planetary taproot, turning regolith oxide bond energy, paid for by solar photons 4.5 Gyr ago, into kinetic capital for an open cislunar economy. Optimal transport ensures that capital flows where gradients are steepest — from highlands dust to habitat steel, from oxygen lattice to crewed delta-V budgets — with minimal Wasserstein waste. When the taproot is primed and routes are Kantorovich-optimal, lunar oxygen ceases to be payload and becomes infrastructure.
