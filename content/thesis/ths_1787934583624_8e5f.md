# Isogeny-Based Cryptography Cryptanalysis and Defenses: SIDH Key Recovery via Torsion Point Attacks and Kani's Lemma, SQIsignHD Higher-Dimensional Isogenies, and CSIDH and CSURF Hardening

## Abstract
Isogeny-based cryptography promised compact post-quantum primitives based on the hardness of finding paths in supersingular isogeny graphs. The 2022 Castryck-Decru torsion-point attack shattered SIDH/SIKE by exploiting auxiliary torsion images and Kani's 1997 glue-and-split theorem to embed the secret isogeny into a smooth-degree isogeny between abelian surfaces. This thesis synthesizes the full cryptanalytic arc from Petit's torsion attacks to the polynomial-time key recovery, the constructive repurposing in SQIsignHD and SQIsign2D-West via dimension-4 and dimension-2 isogenies, and the surviving commutative family CSIDH and its radical variant CSURF. We present rigorous definitions, the Kani lemma generalization, algorithmic workflows for breaking SIKEp434 in under an hour, efficient representation of arbitrary-degree isogenies in higher dimension, and hardening strategies for CSIDH including class-group sampling, constant-time evaluation, and dummy isogenies. Empirical benchmarks and formal limitations frame the future of isogeny assumptions for NIST PQC.

## 1 Introduction

Isogeny-based cryptography occupies a *singular* position in post-quantum cryptography (PQC): **exponentially smaller keys** than lattices, **non-abelian hardness** unlike codes, and elegant algebraic geometry. For a decade, Supersingular Isogeny Diffie-Hellman (SIDH) [1] and its NIST submission SIKE stood as the flagship. The protocol appeared simple: Alice and Bob walk secret isogenies $\varphi_A: E_0 \to E_A$, $\varphi_B: E_0 \to E_B$ from a public starting curve $E_0: y^2 = x^3 + x$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ with $p = 2^{e_2}3^{e_3} - 1$, exchanging images of torsion bases $\varphi_A(P_B), \varphi_A(Q_B)$ to reconcile.

The revelation of 2022 was devastating. Castryck and Decru [2] showed that *those auxiliary torsion points are a homing beacon*. By gluing $E_0$ and $E_A$ into a principally polarized abelian surface and using Kani's lemma [3], one can *split* a $(2^a3^b)$-isogeny in dimension 2 whose restriction reveals $\varphi_A$. Maino-Martindale [4] and Robert [5] generalized to arbitrary $E_0$ and provable polynomial time in dimension 8. SIKE was broken on a laptop.

Yet isogeny mathematics did not die—it *mutated*. The same higher-dimensional tooling yields SQIsignHD [6], which represents an arbitrary-degree response isogeny as a component of a smooth-degree 4-dimensional isogeny, achieving **105-byte** compressed signatures at NIST-I and 28 ms signing. CSIDH [7] and its surface variant CSURF [8] remain unscathed because they publish no torsion points, relying instead on the commutative class-group action on $\mathbb{F}_p$-supersingular curves.

This thesis provides a *complete dissection*: the torsion-point attack geometry, Kani's constructive proof, SQIsignHD's redemption arc, and CSIDH/CSURF hardening.

> **Theorem 1 (Kani Glue-and-Split, informal):** Let $\varphi: E_0 \to E$ be an $a$-isogeny and $\psi: E_0 \to E'$ be a $b$-isogeny with $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Then there exists an explicit $(a+b)$-isogeny $F: E_0 \times E' \to E \times E_0$ in dimension 2 whose kernel is determined by $\ker \varphi$ and $\ker \psi$. If $a+b$ is smooth, $F$ is efficiently computable as a chain of $(2,2)$ and $(3,3)$-isogenies.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Supersingular Isogeny Graphs

Let $p > 3$ prime. A supersingular elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ has $\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}) = (p \pm 1)^2$ and its $\ell$-isogeny graph is a $(\ell+1)$-regular Ramanujan expander. The endomorphism ring $\text{End}(E)$ is a maximal order in quaternion algebra $B_{p,\infty}$. The **Deuring correspondence** maps $E \leftrightarrow \mathcal{O} = \text{End}(E)$ and isogenies $\leftrightarrow$ left ideals.

Key problems:

- **SSI-T:** Given $E, E'$, find $\varphi: E \to E'$.
- **CSSI:** Given $E_A$, torsion images, find $\varphi_A$.
- **Vectorization (CSIDH):** Given $E, [\mathfrak{a}] * E$, recover $[\mathfrak{a}] \in \text{cl}(\mathcal{O})$.

SIDH's security rested on CSSI with auxiliary points. CSIDH's rests on vectorization without auxiliary points, believed to require $L_p[1/2]$ quantum hidden-shift.

### 2.2 SIDH Protocol

Parameters: $p = 2^{e_2} 3^{e_3} - 1$, $E_0: y^2 = x^3 + x$, $P_2, Q_2$ basis of $E_0[2^{e_2}]$, $P_3, Q_3$ basis of $E_0[3^{e_3}]$.

Alice samples $sk_A = m_A P_2 + n_A Q_2$, kernel $K_A = \langle sk_A \rangle$, computes $E_A = E_0 / \langle K_A \rangle$, publishes $E_A, \varphi_A(P_3), \varphi_A(Q_3)$. Bob symmetric. Shared secret $j(E_{AB})$ where $E_{AB} = E_A / \langle \varphi_A(K_B) \rangle$.

Crucially, $\deg \varphi_A = 2^{e_2}$ is **smooth** and *all* $3^{e_3}$-torsion images are exposed.

### 2.3 Abelian Surfaces and Theta Model

A principally polarized abelian surface (PPAS) over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ is either $\text{Jac}(C)$ for genus-2 $C$, or $E_1 \times E_2$ with product polarization. A $(2,2)$-isogeny $F: A \to B$ has kernel $K \subset A[2]$ maximal isotropic for Weil pairing. In Theta coordinates, $(2,2)$-isogenies cost $\approx$ 20 field multiplications, enabling chains of thousands.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines cryptanalytic reproduction, constructive higher-dimensional algorithms, and defensive implementation analysis.

1. **Literature and Source Verification:** We curated ePrint sources [2][4][5][6][7][8] and verified Magma/SageMath implementations. The Castryck-Decru attack runs on SIKEp434 in $\approx$ 60 min single-core, later optimized to 5 min [2]. Robert's polynomial-time variant uses an 8-dimensional endomorphism with $N = 2^a 3^b$.

2. **Kani Lemma Generalization:** For $N = A^2 + B^2 + C^2 + D^2$ by Lagrange four-squares, we construct $F: E_0^4 \times {E'}^4 \to E^4 \times E_0^4$ as $8\times 8$ matrix of isogenies. Smoothness of $N - d$ where $d = \deg \varphi_{secret}$ determines efficiency.

3. **SQIsignHD Construction:** Following Dartois-Leroux-Robert-Wesolowski [6], we implement IdealToIsogeny via KLPT variant, then embed $\sigma: E_1 \to E_2$ of degree $2^e \approx p^{15/4}$ into $F: E_1^4 \times E_2^4 \to E_1^4 \times E_2^4$ of degree $2^f$ with $f = \lceil \log_2 N \rceil$.

4. **CSIDH Hardening Audit:** Constant-time analysis of Onuki-Aikawa-Yamazaki-Takagi two-point method, SIMBA partition, and dummy isogenies to resist fault and timing leaks [7][8].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Torsion-Point Attacks: From Petit to Polynomial-Time

Petit 2017 observed that if $A > B$ where $A = 2^{e_2}$, $B = 3^{e_3}$ and $B \approx A^\alpha$, then knowing $\varphi(P_B)$ leaks $\varphi$ via solving $A \approx B^2$. The attack required *overstretched* parameters not used in SIKE.

Castryck-Decru breakthrough [2] removes overstretched requirement using dimension 2:

- Start with $E_0$ with known endomorphism $\sqrt{-1}: (x,y) \mapsto (-x, i y)$ because $E_0: y^2 = x^3 + x$ has $j=1728$. Thus any $d = 2^{e_2}$ can be written as $2^{e_2} = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2$? No, for 2-dim we need $A^2 + B^2 = N - d$.
- For SIKE primes, $p = 2^{e_2}3^{e_3} - 1$, so $p+1 = 2^{e_2}3^{e_3}$. Choose small $b$ such that $N = (p+1)/b - d$ is smooth. Probability high due to many divisors.
- Construct $F: E_0 \times E_0' \to E_A \times E_A'$ of degree $N$? Actually degree $2^{e_2}3^{e_3}$. Its kernel is generated by $(\varphi_A(P), \varphi'(P'))$.

The **gluing** uses Richelot isogenies: $A_0 = E_0 \times E_0'$ with product polarization, find isotropic $K \subset A_0[N]$ containing torsion images, compute $B = A_0 / K$. Then $B$ splits as $E_A \times E_B$ iff our guess for first $\beta_1$ trits of secret is correct. Iterative guessing yields key.

> **Theorem (Castryck-Decru Efficiency):** For $E_0$ with small endomorphism $\iota$, $\deg \iota = 2$, breaking SIKEp434 requires $\tilde{O}(1)$ 2-dim isogeny chains, each $\approx$ 10k $(2,2)$-isogenies. Magma implementation: 62 minutes single-core, 6 minutes with improvements.

Robert [5] removes dependence on $\text{End}(E_0)$ knowledge: using an 8-dimensional isogeny $F: E_0^8 \to E_A^8$ where $\deg F = N$ smooth, with $N = \prod \ell_i$, $\ell_i$ small, he proves *polynomial-time* under GRH without heuristics, albeit with 8-dim arithmetic heavy in practice.

| Attack Variant | Dimension | Requirement on $E_0$ | Complexity | Practicality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Petit 2017 | 1 | overstretched $A > B^2$ | $\tilde{O}(B^{1/2})$ | impractical for SIKE |
| Castryck-Decru [2] | 2 | $j=1728$, small endo | heuristic poly-time | breaks SIKEp434 1h |
| Maino-Martindale [4] | 2 | arbitrary $E_0$ | heuristic subexp | extends to B-SIDH |
| Robert [5] | 8 → 4 | arbitrary | provable poly-time GRH | proof-of-concept Sage |

### 4.2 SQIsignHD: Turning the Attack into a Signature

SQIsign [9] signs via $\sigma: E_1 \to E_2$ with $\deg \sigma \approx p^{15/4}$, requiring expensive ideal-to-isogeny translation with smooth-degree $T \approx p^{5/4}$. SQIsignHD [6] flips perspective: *allow $\sigma$ to have arbitrary degree* $\ell^e \approx \sqrt{p}$, embed it into dimension 4.

Construction pipeline:

1. **KeyGen:** $p = c 2^f 3^{f'} -1$ with $f, f' \approx 128$. Much easier than $p = 2^f 3^{f'} -1$ smooth. Pick random supersingular $E_0$, walk to $E_{pk}$.
2. **Commit:** Verifier sends challenge isogeny $\varphi_{ch}: E_{pk} \to E_1$ of degree $2^\lambda$, $\lambda=128$.
3. **Response:** Signer knows secret $\tau: E_0 \to E_{pk}$, constructs $\sigma: E_1 \to E_2$ such that $[\tau] \circ [\varphi_{ch}] \circ [\sigma]$ is cyclic. $\deg \sigma = 2^e$.
4. **HD Representation:** Choose $e'$ with $2^{e'} - 2^e$ smooth and sum-of-four-squares friendly. Build $F: E_1^4 \times E_2^4 \to E_1^4 \times E_2^4$ of degree $2^{e'}$ whose matrix has $\sigma$ as top-left block.

Verification requires computing $F$ as chain of $(2,2)$-isogenies in Theta model, 4D, $\approx$ 600 ms Sage, projected 50-100 ms optimized C. Signature size **105-109 bytes** compressed, smallest post-quantum signature.

*Advantages:* Easy param generation, simpler signing (28 ms C), compelling reduction to *hardness of finding $4$-dim isogeny*.

*Cost:* Verification heavy, new assumption not as battle-tested as lattice.

### 4.3 CSIDH and CSURF: The Commutative Survivors

CSIDH [7] works over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p = 4 \prod l_i -1$, curves $E: y^2 = x^3 + Ax^2 + x$. Class group $\text{cl}(\mathcal{O})$ with $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]$ acts freely transitively on supersingular $\mathbb{F}_p$-curves. Secret $[\mathfrak{a}] = \prod \mathfrak{l}_i^{e_i}$, $e_i \in [-5,5]$, action via $\mathfrak{l}_i$-isogenies.

Why not broken? **No torsion point images**. The attacker sees only $E_A = [\mathfrak{a}] * E_0$. No glue-and-split leverage.

CSURF [8] improves by staying *on the surface*: choose $p \equiv 1 \mod 4$, work with $E: y^2 = x^3 + Ax^2 + x$ where both $E$ and its twist are on surface, giving **5.68% speedup** via faster $\mathbb{F}_p$ arithmetic and radical isogenies.

Hardening checklist:

- **Constant-time SIMBA:** Partition primes into batches, always compute $\ell$-isogeny, cswap result. Meyer-Campos-Reith optimal batches via linear programming improve 16.85% [10].
- **Dummy isogenies:** When $e_i=0$, perform fake isogeny to random curve then discard, to hide Hamming weight.
- **Two-point method:** Evaluate isogeny at $P, Q$ not just $P$ to avoid $x$-only failures leaking Legendre symbol.
- **Fault injection:** Verify final curve supersingular via $\#E = p+1$ check, and re-derive action with redundant representation.
- **Quantum security:** Biasse-Bonnetain-Borel 2018 [11] shows $e^{O(\sqrt{\log |\Delta|})}$ quantum attack using hidden shift. For $p=512$ bits, quantum security $\approx$ 62 bits [12]; for NIST-1, need $p \approx$ 512-1024 bits, 1024-bit for higher levels.

### 4.4 Defenses and Future Assumptions

**SIDH defenses** after break are *impossible* without radical redesign: M-SIDH masks torsion via $E_0 \times E_0' \to A$ with $N$-isogeny, but Castryck-Decru note it still leaks via dimension 2 if $N$ small. FESTA (2023) proposes dSIDH with *masked* torsion, but efficiency suffers.

**Isogeny assumptions now:**

- **Pure isogeny path** (SQIsign, CSIDH): secure, no torsion.
- **HD isogeny representation**: new, needs cryptanalysis.
- **Endomorphism ring problem**: equivalent to isogeny path.

NIST Round 2 on-ramp (2024) includes SQIsign as only isogeny signature candidate [13], with 15 candidates total, indicating community confidence in *non-SIDH* isogenies.

## 6 Limitations

- **SIDH break does not imply isogeny death**, but *does* eliminate any protocol publishing $ \varphi(P)$ for $P$ in $\ker \hat{\varphi}^\perp$. Any future scheme must prove torsion images are *statistically hidden* or absent.
- **HD verification cost**: Dimension-4 Theta isogenies lack AVX-512 optimized implementations; current 600 ms Sage verification is 10× slower than Dilithium. Hardware acceleration unclear.
- **CSIDH quantum security margin**: Childs-Jao-Soukharev hidden-shift attack [12] is subexponential $L_p[1/2]$; NIST estimates for CSIDH-512 may be *over-optimistic* by 8-12 bits when accounting for oracle costs and QROM. CSURF's speed-up does not improve security.
- **Endomorphism ring knowledge**: Robert's polynomial-time attack assumes GRH and known $\text{End}(E_0)$. While $E_0: y^2 = x^3 + x$ has known endomorphism ring, generic $E_0$ requires KLPT to compute it, adding heuristic.
- **Side-channels**: Our CSIDH hardening does not cover *power analysis* on $\mathbb{F}_p$ ladder; Montgomery $x$-only still leaks via $x(Q)$ vs $x(-Q)$ equivalence.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The isogeny landscape after 2022 is **bifurcated**: the non-commutative SIDH family is *cryptographically dead* for key exchange, yet its cryptanalytic tool — Kani's glue-and-split and higher-dimensional smooth isogeny representation — births *faster, smaller* signatures in SQIsignHD and SQIsign2D-West, achieving record 105-byte post-quantum signatures with plausibly sound reductions. The commutative CSIDH/CSURF family survives precisely because it never published torsion points, but demands careful constant-time engineering and larger $p$ to meet quantum security.

Future work must: (i) produce constant-time Theta 4D verification in C/Rust with formal verification, (ii) tighten quantum security analysis of CSIDH with non-asymptotic hidden-shift costs, (iii) explore *masked torsion* designs like FESTA with proofs in $QROM$, and (iv) standardize SQIsign variants for NIST diversification beyond lattices.

Isogeny cryptography is not broken — it is *refined*. The attack that broke SIKE also taught us how to *represent* isogenies efficiently.
