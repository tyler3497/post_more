# Quantified Boolean Formula Solving: QCDCL, Expansion, and Dependency Schemes

## Abstract

Quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs) extend propositional logic with universal and existential quantification, yielding a PSPACE-complete decision problem with direct applications in planning, reactive synthesis, and bounded model checking. This thesis surveys the two dominant paradigms of modern QBF solving: **quantified conflict-driven clause learning (QCDCL)**, the search-based approach exemplified by DepQBF, and **expansion-based solving**, exemplified by RAReQS and its counterexample-guided abstraction refinement lineage. We develop the proof-theoretic foundations of both — Q-resolution and its extensions (long-distance resolution, QU-resolution) versus ∀Exp+Res and the IR-calculus family — and explain their exponential separations. We examine **dependency schemes**, which relax strict prefix ordering inside QCDCL and preprocessing, present generalized QCDCL with incompleteness-tolerant axiom rules, and treat preprocessing (blocked clause elimination in bloqqer, QRAT proof logging). A substantial section covers **strategy extraction and certificates**: how Q-resolution refutations yield decision-list strategies for universal variables, and how true instances produce Skolem certificates. We close with QBFEVAL competition evidence and open problems in DQBF and DQRAT verification. The central claim: no single paradigm dominates; progress depends on principled hybridisation guided by dependency analysis.

## 1 Introduction

The success of propositional SAT solvers over the past two decades has repeatedly inspired attempts to lift conflict-driven clause learning (CDCL) to harder logics. The most natural first step is the *quantified* Boolean formula: variables are bound by blocks of universal and existential quantifiers in a fixed prefix, and the resulting validity problem is PSPACE-complete [8]. QBF is the canonical encoding language for problems with adversarial or uncontrollable environment behaviour — conditional planning under uncertainty [9], reactive synthesis from temporal specifications, equivalence checking of partial designs, and conformant verification tasks.

Two architectural philosophies now dominate. **QCDCL** (quantified CDCL) generalizes DPLL+CDCL: decisions must respect the quantifier prefix, propagation is QBF-aware (unit and pure literal detection), conflicts yield learned clauses via *Q-resolution*, and satisfying leaves yield learned *cubes* via dual reasoning [1,2]. **Expansion-based solving**, by contrast, eliminates universal variables by instantiation — a formula ∀u.φ(u) is equivalent to φ(0) ∧ φ(1) — producing a propositional formula handed to a SAT oracle; refinements such as counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (CEGAR) avoid naive exponential blowup [4,5]. The two families are not merely engineering choices: they correspond to **mutually incomparable proof systems**, Q-resolution versus ∀Exp+Res, with exponential separations in both directions established by Janota and Marques-Silva [7].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes notation and reviews the core calculi: Q-resolution, long-distance resolution, QU-resolution, and the expansion hierarchy (∀Exp+Res, IR-calc, IRM-calc). Section 3 describes the methodology: how the thesis was developed from solver sources, benchmarks, and proof-complexity literature. Section 4 deep-dives into QCDCL engineering (generalized axioms, dynamic blocked clause elimination), expansion solvers (RAReQS, QESTO), dependency schemes (standard, triangle, quadrangle, resolution-path), and certificates via strategy extraction. Section 5 presents empirical evidence from QBFEVAL competitions and proof-theoretic separations. Section 6 discusses limitations — preprocessing soundness under dependency schemes, certificate size blowup, and portfolio fragility. Section 7 concludes with research directions including DQBF and DQRAT-based verification.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Syntax, semantics, and complexity

A *quantified Boolean formula* in prenex conjunctive normal form (PCNF) is written Π.φ, where Π = Q₁X₁ … QₙXₙ is the quantifier prefix (each Qᵢ ∈ {∃, ∀}) and φ is a propositional CNF matrix. Truth is defined recursively: ∃X.ψ is true iff some instantiation of X makes ψ true; ∀X.ψ is true iff ψ holds under *all* assignments to X. Deciding QBF truth is PSPACE-complete [8]; even the two-level ∃∀ fragment is Σ₂^P-complete.

For solvers, assignments must be *QCDCL assignments*: decisions follow prefix order, while propagated literals (unit/pure) may violate it [6]. A clause is *conflicting* under QBF-specific rules involving universal reduction; dually, a *cube* can be *satisfying* under symmetric conditions.

### 2.2 Q-resolution and its extensions

**Q-resolution** (Kleine Büning, Karpinski, Flögel, 1995) is the calculus underlying QCDCL, with two rules: **resolution** on an existential pivot (the resolvent must not be tautological) and **∀-reduction**, which deletes a universal literal u from a clause when every existential literal in the clause is quantified *before* u. Q-resolution polynomially simulates DPLL-based QBF search [1].

Three important extensions sharpen the picture:

1. **Long-distance resolution (LD-Q-Res)** [1,2]: resolution steps may merge complementary universal literals u, ¬u into the special literal u* when u is quantified *after* the pivot. Zhang and Malik showed this is exactly what conflict-driven learning in QBF needs [1].
2. **QU-resolution** [3]: universal variables may serve as resolution pivots. QU-Res is exponentially stronger than Q-Res, yet historically under-exploited inside QCDCL — DepQBF 6.0 harnesses it only partially via generalized axioms [3].
3. **Generalized Q-resolution axioms** [6]: instead of requiring φ[A] = ⊥ (resp. ⊤) under the current assignment, any *sound but incomplete* decision procedure may certify (un)satisfiability of φ[A], yielding a learned clause (gen-cl-init) or cube (gen-cu-init). This single mechanism integrates SAT-solver abstractions, expansion-based sub-solvers (Nenofex), and dynamic blocked clause elimination into one QCDCL loop [3,6].

### 2.3 Expansion-based proof systems

∀**Exp+Res** [7] refutes a QBF in two stages: (i) fully expand every universal variable — ∀u.φ becomes φ[u↦0] ∧ φ[u↦1] with fresh annotated copies x^α of each existential x depending on the universal assignment α; (ii) refute the resulting propositional formula by ordinary resolution. The annotation discipline is the subtle part: x^α and x^β are *distinct* variables unless the assignments α, β agree on all universals quantified before x. The **IR-calculus** (instantiation + resolution) and its merge-map extension **IRM-calc** [10] generalize this idea, allowing partial instantiations annotated with partial assignments and *merge* maps that unify compatible annotations.

> **Theorem:** (Janota–Marques-Silva [7]) ∀Exp+Res p-simulates *tree-like* Q-resolution but does *not* p-simulate unrestricted Q-resolution. Conversely, Q-resolution does not p-simulate ∀Exp+Res: parity-like formulas separate the systems.

### 2.4 Dependency schemes

The *standard* dependency relation x <_Π y declares every outer variable dependent on every inner one, but this is coarser than necessary. **Dependency schemes** compute safe over-approximations of true semantic dependence, permitting decision reordering inside QCDCL, stronger ∀-reduction, and more aggressive preprocessing.

The hierarchy runs from the **standard** and **triangle** relations (Samer–Szeider) through **quadrangle** dependencies — computable via biconnected components — up to the **resolution-path** dependency, the strongest efficiently characterizable relation [11]. In DQBF, where Henkin-style dependencies are explicit in the input, naive transplantation of QBF dependency schemes becomes *incomplete* — soundness meets incompleteness at the DQBF boundary [12].

### 2.5 Preprocessing: bloqqer, QBCE, QRAT

Modern QBF solving is unthinkable without preprocessing. **Bloqqer** implements *quantified blocked clause elimination* (QBCE): a clause C is blocked on literal l if every resolvent of C on l is a tautology; blocked clauses are satisfiability-preserving removable [13]. Bloqqer additionally performs (bounded) universal expansion, failed-literal detection, and equivalence reasoning, and it can *solve* many instances outright. Crucially, bloqqer logs **QRAT proofs** of every transformation, checkable by QRAT-trim — preprocessing is no longer a trusted black box. Its successor ideas appear in **QRATPre+** (strong redundancy via the QRAT⁺ system) and **HQSpre** [14].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 QCDCL: the search-based engine room

QCDCL generalizes the CDCL loop with two asymmetries that have no SAT analogue [1,3,6]: decisions respect the prefix while propagation (QBCP) need not, and *both conflicts and solutions are learned* — a conflict yields a clause by Q-resolution over the implication graph, while a *solution* yields a *cube* by dual term resolution. The solver maintains a clause database *and* a cube database, terminating on the empty clause or the empty cube.

The Zhang–Malik solver introduced *long-distance* resolvents to keep learned clauses asserting, and the *symmetric* treatment of satisfaction and conflict [1]. DepQBF refined this into a full QCDCL with dependency-aware reduction: with a dependency scheme D, ∀-reduction may delete u from a clause when no existential literal *D-dependent* on u remains, which shortens learned clauses and prunes the search [3].

```python
# Sketch: QCDCL main loop with generalized axioms (after Lonsing & Egly)
def qcdcl(phi):
    A = {}            # current partial assignment (QCDCL assignment)
    while True:
        status = qbcp(phi, A)          # unit + pure literal propagation
        if status == CONFLICT:
            C = analyze_conflict(phi, A)   # Q-resolution over implication graph
            if C == EMPTY_CLAUSE: return UNSAT
            learn_clause(C); backjump(A, C)
        elif status == SOLUTION:
            K = analyze_solution(phi, A)   # dual: cube learning
            if K == EMPTY_CUBE: return SAT
            learn_cube(K); backjump(A, K)
        elif incomplete_oracle(phi, A) == UNSAT:   # generalized axiom [6]
            learn_clause(gen_cl_init(A))
            backjump(A, ...)
        elif incomplete_oracle(phi, A) == SAT:
            learn_cube(gen_cu_init(A))
            backjump(A, ...)
        else:
            decide(phi, A)             # prefix-ordered decision
```

The generalized-axiom view of DepQBF 6.0 is the most important advance: it turns QCDCL from a fixed calculus into a *framework* in which arbitrary sound oracles (SAT abstractions, Nenofex expansion calls, dynamic QBCE checks) contribute lemmas, while Q-resolution remains the glue [6].

### 4.2 Expansion-based solving: RAReQS, QESTO, and CEGAR

Expansion attacks the universal quantifiers directly. The naive rule — replace ∀u.φ by φ[u↦0] ∧ φ[u↦1] — is complete but exponential. **RAReQS** (Recursive Abstraction Refinement QBF Solver) [5] avoids full expansion via CEGAR: maintain a propositional abstraction at the outermost level, ask a SAT solver for a candidate assignment, recursively check it against the inner subformula, and refine the abstraction with each counterexample. **QESTO** [4] restructures the same idea around *clause selection variables*: one SAT formula per quantification level, with learning anchored in the SAT solver's own conflict analysis. On QBFLIB benchmarks QESTO solved 133/276 instances versus DepQBF's 128 and RAReQS's 129 under identical conditions, while dominating the 2QBF track (53/65) [4].

> **Theorem:** (Soundness of recursive CEGAR) Each refinement step of RAReQS preserves the set of winning strategies of the existential player; the algorithm terminates with a correct SAT/UNSAT verdict because every counterexample eliminates at least one candidate from a finite candidate space [5].

Expansion's Achilles' heel is formulas whose refutations inherently need *dag-like* Q-resolution reasoning (e.g., pigeonhole-style counting under quantifiers): the ∀Exp+Res lower bounds bite exactly there [7]. Conversely, parity-flavoured formulas that are exponentially hard for Q-Res fall easily to expansion — the empirical complementarity is a theorem, not an accident.

### 4.3 Dependency schemes: from prefix order to true dependence

The standard dependency relation is brutally conservative: in ∃x₁…xₙ∀z∃a₁…aₙ, every xᵢ is declared dependent on z even when the matrix never connects them. Dependency schemes recover the lost freedom. The main relations, ordered by strength (fewer declared dependencies = stronger scheme):

| Scheme | Idea | Computability |
|---|---|---|
| Standard D^std | prefix order | trivial |
| Triangle D^△ | resolution-path of length ≤ 2 (Samer–Szeider) | polytime |
| Quadrangle D^quad | biconnected-component analysis of implication structure [11] | polytime |
| Resolution-path D^rrs | full resolution-path connectivity [11] | polytime (prototype costly) |

A scheme D is *sound* for a calculus if replacing <_Π by D in ∀-reduction and decision ordering preserves refutational soundness. The resolution-path scheme is the strongest known sound relation for Q-Res; whether it admits an efficient incremental implementation remains open [11]. In DQBF (dependency QBF), where Henkin-style dependencies are explicit in the input, Beyersdorff et al. showed that naive transplantation of QBF dependency schemes becomes *incomplete* — soundness meets incompleteness at the DQBF boundary [12]. This is not a defect but a deep fact: dependency is semantic, and calculi approximate it differently.

Practically, DepQBF's dependency-aware ∀-reduction is one of its signature advantages: learned clauses shrink because universal literals with no dependent existential literals vanish [3].

### 4.4 Certificates and strategy extraction

A solver's verdict is only as trustworthy as its certificate. For *false* QBFs, Q-resolution refutations support **strategy extraction**: from a refutation π of Π.φ one computes, in time polynomial in |π|, a *winning strategy* for the universal player — a function fᵤ per universal u mapping outer existential assignments to a Boolean value [2].

> **Theorem:** (Balabanov–Jiang [2]) Given a Q-Res refutation π of a false QBF φ, there is a winning universal strategy in which each fᵤ is representable as a *decision list* of size polynomial in |π|.

Decision lists — "if t₁ then c₁ else if t₂ then c₂ …" — are a weak computational model (subsumed by small circuits), and this weakness is a *feature*: it yields proof-complexity lower bounds. If every universal winning strategy for a formula family must compute a function outside the extraction model's power (e.g., parity outside AC⁰), then no short Q-Res refutation exists. Beyersdorff, Chew, and Janota used exactly this technique to prove the exponential lower bound for parity formulas in Q-Res, separating Q-Res from ∀Exp+Res [10]. The hierarchy generalizes: C-Frege + ∀red admits extraction into C-decision lists, giving a uniform lower-bound machine for strong QBF systems [10].

For *true* QBFs the dual certificate is a set of *Skolem functions* (existential strategies). DepQBF's QRPCert pipeline extracts AIGER circuits from Q-resolution proofs and validates them with an independent checker; on QBFEVAL-derived suites, certificates were extracted for over 90% of solved instances and over 80% validated [2]. Certificate validation remains the expensive step, which motivates DQRAT-based proof logging that unifies preprocessing and solving traces [12].

### 4.5 Preprocessing as proof: bloqqer, QBCE, and QRAT

Preprocessing in QBF is far more aggressive than in SAT, because quantifiers enable simplifications with no propositional analogue. **Quantified blocked clause elimination (QBCE)** [13] removes a clause C blocked on literal l when all Q-resolvents on l are tautologies; the quantified version must additionally respect ∀-reduction side conditions. Bloqqer layers QBCE with:

- **Bounded universal expansion** (the ∀Exp rule applied to small universal blocks),
- **Failed-literal detection** (probing assignments, SAT- or abstraction-based),
- **Equivalent-literal substitution and variable elimination** adapted to prefix constraints.

The striking engineering fact is that bloqqer *solves* a large fraction of QBFEVAL instances before any solver runs. Its QRAT proof logging was pioneering: every elimination step is justified in the QRAT proof system so that QRAT-trim can independently verify the transformation chain. **QRATPre+** [14] pushes further with strong redundancy properties (QRAT⁺) orthogonal to QBCE, and experiments show preprocessing order matters: QRATPre+ *before* bloqqer outperforms the reverse on most solvers.

A subtle interaction deserves emphasis: preprocessing under *dependency schemes* can be unsound if the scheme used by the preprocessor and the solver disagree. The certification stack (QRP → QRAT → DQRAT) is the community's answer — a single proof format expressive enough to log expansion, resolution, *and* dependency-aware steps — but full solver support for advanced dependency schemes in certificates is still listed as future work [2,12].

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Competition evidence: QBFEVAL and the portfolio effect

The QBFEVAL series (annual since the early 2000s, using the QBFLIB suite) consistently shows **no dominant solver**. Representative data points from the published literature:

- On QBFLIB application benchmarks with bloqqer preprocessing, QCDCL with *dynamic* QBCE (eager blocked-clause elimination inside the search, implemented in DepQBF) substantially outperformed traditional QCDCL and was competitive with expansion solvers; dynamic QBCE yields a variant of cube learning exponentially stronger than the traditional method [13].
- QRATPre+ preprocessing studies on QBFEVAL'18 instances: CAQE rose from 151 to 211 solved instances with preprocessing; DepQBF from 149 to 165 — while bloqqer-first pipelines pushed CAQE to 269 [14]. The lesson is twofold: preprocessing is the largest single performance lever, and its interaction with the downstream proof system is non-trivial.
- The QESTO evaluation [4] (276 QBFLIB + 65 2QBF instances, 800 s timeout): expansion/CEGAR solvers (Qesto 133, RAReQS 129) edged QCDCL (DepQBF 128) on general instances and dominated 2QBF (Qesto 53 vs DepQBF 30) — matching the theory that low-alternation formulas favour expansion.

### 5.2 Formal separations: the proof-complexity map

The empirically observed complementarity is underwritten by theorems [7,10]:

| Separation | Witness family | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Q-Res does not p-simulate ∀Exp+Res | parity / XOR formulas | expansion wins |
| ∀Exp+Res does not p-simulate Q-Res | pigeonhole / counting formulas | search wins |
| LD-Q-Res exponentially stronger than Q-Res | formulas needing long-distance steps | — |
| QU-Res exponentially stronger than Q-Res | universal-pivot formulas [3] | — |
| ∀Exp+Res p-simulates *tree-like* Q-Res | — | tree search ⊆ expansion |
| IR-calc / IRM-calc unify both | — | strictly stronger than each |

Strategy extraction converts these separations into *lower-bound technology* [2,10].

### 5.3 Applications

QBF solving earns its keep in domains where the ∃∀ alternation is intrinsic: **planning** (conditional/conformant planning compiles to ∃ plan ∀ contingencies; Rintanen's benchmarks remain a QBFLIB staple [9]), **reactive synthesis and verification** (strategy extraction *is* program synthesis — extracted Herbrand/Skolem functions are the controller), and **formal verification** (equivalence checking of partial designs). In each case the certificate is the deliverable, which is why the extraction machinery of Section 4.4 is not ornamental.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Portfolio fragility.** The state of the art is a *portfolio* of mutually incomparable solvers with heuristic per-instance selection. The exponential separations guarantee complementarity — and guarantee that any single solver family fails catastrophically on some structured family.
2. **Certificate cost.** Proof logging and validation can dominate runtime; DQRAT promises unification but solver support is incomplete, and dependency-scheme-aware certification is still future work [2,12].
3. **Preprocessing soundness under schemes.** Aggressive preprocessing interacts subtly with dependency schemes, and pipeline order (QRATPre+ vs bloqqer vs HQSpre) changes results unpredictably [14].
4. **Expansion blowup on deep prefixes.** CEGAR tames but does not eliminate exponential worst cases; dense universal blocks with many alternations still defeat RAReQS-style recursion, while QCDCL's prefix-ordered search handles them structurally.
5. **DQBF gap.** Dependency schemes sound for QBF become incomplete for DQBF [12], and certified DQBF solving is in its infancy.
6. **Benchmark bias.** QBFLIB over-represents certain encodings; solver rankings are encoding-sensitive, and preprocessing can mask the underlying calculus strength being measured.

---

## 7 Conclusion

QBF solving has matured from a single-paradigm field into a *proof-complexity-guided engineering discipline*. The central insight of the last fifteen years is that QCDCL and expansion are not competing implementations of one idea but realizations of **incomparable proof systems** — Q-resolution (with its long-distance and universal extensions) versus ∀Exp+Res and the IR-calculus family — and that their empirical complementarity on QBFEVAL benchmarks is a theorem, not an accident [7,10].

Three threads will shape the next decade. First, **dependency schemes** offer the most principled route to hybrid vigour: by computing true variable dependence, they let search-based solvers borrow expansion's freedom (out-of-order decisions, aggressive reduction) without leaving the Q-resolution proof system [3,11]. Second, **certification** must catch up with solving: DQRAT-based proof logging unifying preprocessing, expansion, and dependency-aware resolution is the prerequisite for trustworthy deployment in synthesis and verification [2,12]. Third, the **DQBF frontier** forces a re-examination of every soundness argument built on prefix order — an opportunity to rebuild QBF solving on semantic rather than syntactic foundations.
