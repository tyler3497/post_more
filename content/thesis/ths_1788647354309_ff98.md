# STARKs and the FRI Protocol: Low-Degree Testing, AIR Arithmetization, and Transparent Post-Quantum Succinct Arguments

## Abstract

Scalable Transparent Arguments of Knowledge (STARKs), introduced by Ben-Sasson, Bentov, Horesh, and Riabzev [1], are the first practical proof systems to simultaneously achieve transparency (no trusted setup), scalability (polylogarithmic verification, quasilinear proving), and plausible post-quantum security from collision-resistant hashing alone. Their core is a two-stage algebraic reduction: computation is *arithmetized* into an Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR) — polynomial constraints over a multiplicative subgroup of a finite field — and these constraints are folded into a single *composition polynomial* whose low-degree-ness is certified by the Fast Reed-Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity (FRI) [2]. This thesis gives a self-contained exposition of the STARK construction: AIR arithmetization and composition polynomials, the FRI commit/query phases with folding operators and distance preservation, the DEEP methodology and DEEP-ALI protocol that tightened soundness [3], and the Fiat-Shamir transform with Merkle commitments that compiles the IOP into a non-interactive argument. We compare proven versus conjectured soundness bounds, quantify query complexity, and contrast STARKs with SNARKs on transparency, proof size, and post-quantum security.

## 1 Introduction

The verification of outsourced computation is among the most consequential problems in modern cryptography. A *succinct argument of knowledge* allows a weak verifier to confirm, in time sublinear in the size of a computation, that a powerful prover executed the computation correctly — and, in the zero-knowledge variant, to do so while learning nothing beyond the truth of the claim. The practical realization of this vision began with **zk-SNARKs**, whose short proofs (hundreds of bytes) and millisecond verification enabled privacy-preserving cryptocurrencies such as Zcash. Yet classical SNARK constructions suffer from two structural liabilities: they require a **trusted setup** — a secret-bearing ceremony whose compromise destroys soundness — and they rely on number-theoretic assumptions (discrete logarithms, knowledge-of-exponent) that **quantum computers** are expected to break [1].

The **STARK** (Scalable Transparent Argument of Knowledge) paradigm, introduced by Ben-Sasson et al. in *"Scalable, transparent, and post-quantum secure computational integrity"* [1], eliminates both liabilities. A STARK requires no trusted setup — the reference string is the empty string — and its security reduces exclusively to the collision resistance of a hash function, an assumption believed to survive quantum adversaries. Verification time is polylogarithmic in the computation size, concretely *exponentially* faster than naive re-execution, and the prover runs in quasilinear time. The price is proof size: tens to hundreds of kilobytes rather than hundreds of bytes.

The STARK architecture rests on three mathematical pillars:

1. **Arithmetization**: the computation is encoded as an *Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR)* — a trace table of field elements together with polynomial constraints that every valid execution must satisfy [4].
2. **Low-degree testing**: the constraints are composed into a single *composition polynomial*, and the prover convinces the verifier that this polynomial is close to a Reed-Solomon code — i.e., that it is low-degree — using the **FRI** protocol [2], the asymptotically and concretely fastest known low-degree test.
3. **Fiat-Shamir compilation**: the interactive oracle proof (IOP) is rendered non-interactive by replacing verifier coins with hashes of Merkle commitments, in the random oracle model [1].

This thesis develops each pillar in depth: the mechanics of AIR arithmetization and composition polynomials (Section 4.1), the FRI protocol's commit/query structure and its proximity-gap analysis (Section 4.2), the DEEP methodology, the ALI protocol, and Fiat-Shamir Merkle compilation (Section 4.3), and the state of soundness analysis — what is *proven* versus what is *conjectured* (Section 4.4). Section 5 collects the principal theorems and concrete instantiations, Section 6 discusses limitations and open problems, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Interactive oracle proofs and polynomial IOPs

An *interactive oracle proof (IOP)* is an interactive protocol in which the prover sends messages to which the verifier has *oracle* access: rather than reading the whole message, the verifier reads only a small number of positions. The **proof length** $\ell(N)$ is the total size of prover messages; the **query complexity** $q(N)$ is the number of positions the verifier reads; and $r(N)$ is the round count [2]. For the FRI system, $q(N) = O(\log \ell(N))$ — exponentially fewer queries than proof bits. An IOP is *transparent* (or public-coin) when all verifier messages are public randomness broadcast to the prover, making it amenable to the **Fiat-Shamir transform** [1].

A **STIK** (Scalable Transparent IOP of Knowledge), per Definition 3.3 of [1], is an IOP satisfying perfect completeness, soundness error decaying exponentially in the query count, and a knowledge-extraction property: from any prover that convinces the verifier with non-negligible probability, an extractor can recover the witness.

### 2.2 Reed-Solomon codes

Fix a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ and a domain $D \subset \mathbb{F}_q$ of size $n$. The **Reed-Solomon code** $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}_q, D, \rho]$ consists of all functions $f: D \to \mathbb{F}_q$ that agree with a polynomial of degree less than $\rho n$ on $D$, where $\rho \in (0,1)$ is the *rate*. The relative minimum distance of the code is $1 - \rho$. The task of **low-degree testing** — deciding whether a given function is close to the code — is the central subproblem of every STARK, because constraint-satisfaction reduces to it.

### 2.3 Merkle commitments and the random oracle model

The prover commits to each oracle message via a **Merkle tree** over a collision-resistant hash function, sending only the root; verifier queries are answered by authentication paths [1]. This converts oracle access into a standard (non-oracle) proof. In the **random oracle model**, the Fiat-Shamir heuristic replaces each verifier coin with the hash of the transcript so far, yielding a non-interactive argument whose soundness follows from the soundness of the underlying IOP against state-restoration attacks.

### 2.4 The trusted-setup problem

Classical SNARKs compile linear PCPs through *linear-only encodings*, a step that requires embedding secret verifier queries into a common reference string — the trusted setup [6]. Polynomial IOPs avoid this: since efficient polynomial commitment schemes need no hidden queries, the compilation is **transparent** [6].

## 3 Methodology

This thesis follows a constructive methodology: we begin from the formal definitions of the objects involved (finite fields, Reed-Solomon codes, the folding operator), build the **AIR** arithmetization layer, reduce constraint satisfaction to a *proximity claim* on a composition polynomial, and then analyze the **FRI** and **DEEP-FRI/DEEP-ALI** proximity tests that decide that claim. Notation follows [1, 2, 3]:

- $\mathbb{F}_q$ — a finite field with $q$ elements; $D, D' \subset \mathbb{F}_q$ — evaluation domains.
- $\Delta(f, \mathrm{RS})$ — relative Hamming distance of $f$ from the Reed-Solomon code.
- $\rho$ — code rate; $\delta$ — distance (proximity) parameter; $\gamma$ — proximity gap.
- $\mathrm{Fold}[f, z]$ — the FRI folding of $f$ at challenge $z$ (Definition 4.2 below).
- $Z_S(X) = \prod_{\alpha \in S}(X - \alpha)$ — the vanishing polynomial of $S$.

We distinguish three kinds of guarantees throughout: **proven bounds** (theorems with unconditional proofs), **conjectured bounds** (notably the Johnson-bound regime list-decoding conjecture of [3]), and **empirical parameters** from the ethSTARK reference implementation [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Arithmetization: From Computation Trace to AIR Constraints and Composition Polynomials

The first stage of every STARK is *arithmetization*: the reduction of a claim about a deterministic computation to a claim about polynomials.

**The execution trace.** The prover first records the computation as a table $T$ with $w$ columns (registers) and $N = 2^h$ rows (time steps), entries in $\mathbb{F}$. Column $j$ is interpreted as the evaluations of a polynomial $p_j$ of degree less than $N$ on a multiplicative subgroup $H_0 = \langle g \rangle \subset \mathbb{F}^\ast$ of order $N$, the **trace domain**. This interpolation is well-defined because $|H_0| = N$ and the $p_j$ are unique.

**AIR constraints.** An *Algebraic Intermediate Representation* is a tuple $\mathcal{A} = (\mathbb{F}, w, h, d, s, g, I, \mathcal{C}_{\text{set}})$ where [4]:

- $w, h, d, s$ are integers: $w$ columns, $h = \log_2 |H_0|$, $d$ the maximal constraint degree, $s$ the number of constraints;
- $I \subseteq \{1,\dots,w\} \times \{0,\dots,2^h - 1\}$ is the set of **mask indices** — offsets into consecutive rows;
- $\mathcal{C}_{\text{set}} = \{C_1, \dots, C_s\}$; each $C_i = (Q_i, H_i)$ pairs a **constraint polynomial** $Q_i$ of total degree at most $d$ over the mask variables with an **enforcement domain** $H_i \subseteq H_0$ on which it must vanish.

A constraint $C_i$ asserts $Q_i(\dots, p_j(g^t x), \dots) = 0$ for all $x \in H_i$; i.e., the mask polynomial vanishes wherever the constraint applies. In the ethSTARK instantiation, $w = 12$, $d = 3$, $s = 52$, and the mask spans two consecutive rows, with $h = 20$ for a chain length of $98304 = 3 \cdot 2^{15}$ [4].

**Composition and quotients.** For each constraint, the prover forms the composition quotient

$$R_i(x) = \frac{Q_i(\text{mask}(x))}{Z_{H_i}(x)},$$

which is a polynomial (of bounded degree) *if and only if* the constraint is satisfied on its enforcement domain — division by the vanishing polynomial $Z_{H_i}$ exactly cancels the enforced zeros. A verifier-supplied random linear combination (the **composition coefficients** $\alpha_i$) merges all quotients into a single **composition polynomial**

$$C(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{s} \alpha_i \cdot R_i(x),$$

of degree less than $\rho |D|$ for a suitable rate $\rho$ when evaluated on a blowup domain $D \supset H_0$ with $|D| = N/\rho$ (the **LDE domain**). The integrity claim *"the trace satisfies all AIR constraints"* is thereby reduced to the single algebraic claim *"the composition polynomial $C$ is close to $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, D, \rho]$"* [1, 4]. This is precisely the proximity claim that FRI decides.

```python
def composition_polynomial(trace_polys, constraints, alphas, domain):
    """Compute the STARK composition polynomial C(x) = sum_i alpha_i * Q_i(x)/Z_{H_i}(x)."""
    C = [0] * len(domain)
    for alpha, (Q, Z_H) in zip(alphas, constraints):
        quotient = [q // zh for q, zh in zip(Q.evaluate(domain), Z_H.evaluate(domain))]
        C = [c + alpha * r for c, r in zip(C, quotient)]
    return C  # low-degree iff every constraint holds on its enforcement domain
```

### 4.2 The FRI Protocol: Commit Phase, Query Phase, Foldings, and Proximity Gaps

**The folding operator.** The heart of FRI is a dimension-halving reduction. Let $D \subset \mathbb{F}$ be $2$-smooth (closed under squaring up to sign pairing), and decompose $f: D \to \mathbb{F}$ into even and odd parts: there exist unique $g, h: D' \to \mathbb{F}$ with $f(x) = g(x^2) + x\,h(x^2)$ where $D' = \{x^2 : x \in D\}$. For challenge $z \in \mathbb{F}$,

> **Definition (Fold)** [5]: $\mathrm{Fold}[f, z](y) := g(y) + z \cdot h(y)$ for $y \in D'$.

Three facts make this operator useful [2, 5]:

1. **Degree halving.** If $f \in \mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, D, \rho]$ then $\mathrm{Fold}[f, z] \in \mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, D', \rho]$ for every $z$ — completeness.
2. **Local computability.** $\mathrm{Fold}[f, z](y)$ is determined by exactly *two* queries to $f$, at the two square roots of $y$ — efficiency.
3. **Distance preservation.** If $\Delta(f, \mathrm{RS}) \ge \delta$, then $\Delta(\mathrm{Fold}[f, z], \mathrm{RS}) \ge (1 - o(1))\,\delta$ except with small probability over $z$ — soundness.

**Commit phase.** The prover sends an oracle $f^{(0)} = f$ on domain $D_0 = D$; the verifier replies with random $z_0$; the prover sends $f^{(1)}$, purportedly $\mathrm{Fold}[f^{(0)}, z_0]$, on the halved domain $D_1$; and so on for $r$ rounds until the domain is tiny and the prover sends the final constant polynomial directly. Each oracle is Merkle-committed before the next challenge is drawn.

**Query phase.** The verifier samples random $x_0 \in D_0$ and checks, at each layer $i$, that $f^{(i+1)}(x_{i+1})$ is consistent with the two values of $f^{(i)}$ above $x_{i+1}$ under the folding formula with challenge $z_i$. A single query path costs $2$ queries per layer and $O(\log N)$ total; repeating for $q$ independent paths drives soundness error down exponentially [2].

**Proximity gaps.** The analysis hinges on the *proximity gap* $\gamma$: for instances far from the language, the reduction must map prover oracles to functions $\gamma$-far from the code, because $\gamma$ feeds directly into the soundness of the proximity test [3]. Classical FRI analysis via the **Johnson bound** yields $\gamma \approx 1 - \sqrt{\rho}$, while *conjectured* list-decoding radii would give $\gamma \approx 1 - \rho$ (nearly optimal); unconditionally, proximity gaps for Reed-Solomon codes are now known up to the unique-decoding radius [7].

### 4.3 DEEP Composition, the ALI Protocol, and Fiat-Shamir Merkle Compilation

**The DEEP method.** A subtle weakness of plain FRI: the verifier checks consistency only at points *inside* the evaluation domain, allowing a cheating prover's interpolating polynomial to behave well on $D$ while diverging wildly outside it — the "pretender" problem. **DEEP-FRI** [3] fixes this by *sampling outside the box*: after the oracles are committed, the verifier draws $z \in \mathbb{F}$ uniformly from the *whole field*, asks the prover for the claimed values of the interpolating polynomials at $z$, and applies a **QUOTIENT** operation,

$$\mathrm{QUOTIENT}(f, z, b)(x) = \frac{f(x) - b}{x - z},$$

which preserves low-degree-ness exactly when the claimed value $b$ is correct. A single out-of-domain consistency test lifts the proximity gap from $\gamma \le 1/8$ (prior ALI analyses) to $\approx 1 - \sqrt{\rho}$ unconditionally, and $\approx 1 - \rho$ under the list-decoding conjecture [3].

**ALI and DEEP-ALI.** Before FRI can run, the verifier must be convinced that the committed trace columns and the composition polynomial are *consistent* — that $C$ was genuinely built from the trace. The **Algebraic Linking IOP (ALI)** of [1] performs this check; **DEEP-ALI** [3] applies the DEEP technique to it, achieving the improved proximity gap with a single out-of-domain quotient test rather than many in-domain checks.

**Fiat-Shamir and Merkle compilation.** The full IOP — trace oracles, composition oracle, FRI layers — is compiled non-interactively: every verifier challenge (composition coefficients, FRI folding challenges $z_i$, DEEP point $z$, query paths) is derived as $\mathrm{Hash}(\text{transcript})$, where the transcript includes all prior **Merkle roots**. In the random oracle model this preserves soundness up to the standard Fiat-Shamir loss, and in practice the hash is instantiated with a STARK-friendly algebraic hash such as **Rescue** [4].

```rust
// Fiat-Shamir: derive each verifier challenge from the Merkle-committed transcript.
fn fiat_shamir_challenge(transcript: &[MerkleRoot], domain_sep: &[u8]) -> FieldElement {
    let mut h = RescueHash::new(domain_sep);
    for root in transcript { h.absorb(root); }
    h.squeeze() // public coin, unpredictable before the prover commits
}
```

### 4.4 Soundness Analysis: Query Complexity, Conjectured vs Proven Bounds

The soundness error of the compiled STARK decomposes into three terms: (i) the **proximity gap** $\gamma$ of the arithmetization + ALI reduction, (ii) the **FRI soundness error** per query, and (iii) the **Fiat-Shamir** and Merkle-binding losses. For $q$ FRI query repetitions, the acceptance probability of a cheating prover scales roughly as $(1 - \gamma)^q$ in the idealized analysis, so $q = \lambda / \gamma$ queries suffice for $\lambda$ bits of security — concretely, ethSTARK targets 80–100 bits with modest $q$ at rates $\rho \in \{1/8, 1/16\}$ [4].

> **Theorem (FRI soundness, proven regime)** [2, 5]: For rate $\rho$ and $r$ folding rounds, there exists $\delta^\* = \delta^\*(\rho) > 0$ such that if $\Delta(f^{(0)}, \mathrm{RS}) \ge \delta^\*$, then a single FRI query path rejects with probability at least $1 - (1 - \delta^\*/2)^r$ up to lower-order terms. Round-by-round soundness analyses [5] make the error accounting tight enough for concrete parameter selection.

The gap between proven and conjectured: the *Johnson bound* gives unconditional soundness only up to distance $1 - \sqrt{\rho}$, while the **proximity-gap conjecture** posits the dichotomy extends to capacity $1 - \rho$ [3, 7]. Practical STARKs (StarkWare's production systems, ethSTARK) operate in the conjectured regime — sound because no attack exploiting the gap is known, but honest about the theoretical debt. Recent work has *proven* proximity gaps up to the unique-decoding radius $(1-\rho)/2$ with false-witness bound $\epsilon = n$ [7], narrowing but not closing the gap.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The ethSTARK reference implementation [4] provides the canonical concrete instantiation: the statement proved is a chain of $3 \cdot 2^{15} = 98304$ Rescue hash evaluations, with $w = 12$ trace columns, $s = 52$ constraints of degree $d = 3$, trace domain $|H_0| = 2^{20}$, and LDE blowup to $|D| = 2^{23}$ at rate $\rho = 1/8$. The resulting proof is on the order of **tens of kilobytes** with verification in milliseconds — exponentially faster than re-executing $10^5$ hashes.

> **Theorem (STARK, informal)** [1, Thm. 1.1]: There exists a transparent IOP of knowledge for bounded-halting nondeterministic computations with quasilinear prover time, polylogarithmic verifier time and proof length, and soundness error $2^{-\lambda}$ for security parameter $\lambda$, assuming only a collision-resistant hash family.

> **Theorem (DEEP-FRI)** [3, Thm. 5.5]: Under the list-decoding conjecture, DEEP-FRI tests proximity to $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, D, \rho]$ with proximity gap $\gamma \approx 1 - \rho$; unconditionally, $\gamma \approx 1 - \sqrt{\rho}$ via the Johnson bound — a strict improvement over the $\gamma \le 1/8$ of prior analyses.

Key empirical and theoretical results:

1. **Transparency is realizable at scale.** StarkWare's production deployments (StarkEx, Starknet) have settled billions of dollars of transactions with STARK proofs, validating the quasilinear-prover/polylog-verifier asymptotics in adversarial production settings.
2. **Prover complexity is quasilinear.** The dominant costs are trace interpolation and LDE evaluation via NTTs ($O(N \log N)$) plus Merkle hashing of all oracles — concretely seconds to minutes for $N \sim 2^{20}$–$2^{24}$ on commodity hardware [1, 4].
3. **Verifier complexity is polylogarithmic.** Verification touches $O(q \log N)$ Merkle paths and $O(q)$ folding checks — milliseconds, independent of the computation's sequential depth [1].
4. **Zero-knowledge is cheap.** Randomizing the composition polynomial and trace commitments with bounded-independence randomness yields perfect or statistical zero-knowledge with negligible overhead [1].

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Proof size.** The central practical limitation: STARK proofs are $10^2$–$10^3 \times$ larger than SNARK proofs, which matters on-chain where calldata is priced per byte. Recursive STARKs and proof aggregation mitigate but do not eliminate this.
- **Prover overhead.** The LDE blowup ($8\times$–$16\times$ domain expansion) and Merkle-tree hashing of every oracle make proving concretely expensive; STARK-friendly hashes (Rescue, Poseidon) and hardware acceleration are active engineering frontiers.
- **Post-quantum nuances.** "Post-quantum secure" means *plausibly* secure against quantum adversaries: Grover's algorithm halves effective hash security (hence 256-bit hashes for 128-bit PQ security), and the Fiat-Shamir security reduction in the *quantum* random oracle model incurs additional loss terms that are still being tightened.
- **The proximity-gap conjecture.** The most important open theoretical problem in the area: prove that Reed-Solomon codes exhibit proximity gaps up to capacity $1 - \rho$ (the conjecture of [3]), which would put production STARK parameters on unconditional footing. Progress via [7] covers the unique-decoding regime; the Johnson-bound regime remains open.
- **Round-by-round soundness tightness.** While [5] simplified the round-by-round analysis, concrete security estimates for aggressive parameters (high rate, few queries) still rely on heuristic extrapolation rather than closed-form proven bounds.
- **Arithmetization ergonomics.** Writing AIRs by hand is error-prone; the ecosystem is converging on constraint DSLs (e.g., AirScript [4, 7]) and provably correct compilers, but verified end-to-end pipelines remain rare.

## 7 Conclusion

STARKs demonstrate that *transparency* and *post-quantum security* need not be sacrificed for *scalability*. The construction's elegance lies in its reduction pipeline: arbitrary computation becomes an AIR, AIR constraints become a composition polynomial, and the composition polynomial's low-degree-ness becomes a claim decidable by FRI — the fastest known proximity test for Reed-Solomon codes — strengthened by the DEEP methodology and compiled non-interactively through Fiat-Shamir Merkle commitments [1, 2, 3]. The theoretical foundations are unusually honest about their own boundaries: the line between proven soundness (Johnson-bound regime, round-by-round analyses) and conjectured soundness (list-decoding capacity) is explicitly drawn, and closing that gap is the field's central open problem. With production systems already settling real value and proof sizes shrinking through recursion, STARKs have moved from a 2018 theoretical breakthrough to the workhorse of transparent verifiable computation.
