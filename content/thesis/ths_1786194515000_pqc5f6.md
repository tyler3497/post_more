# Post-Quantum Digital Signatures: Security Reductions, Parameter Tradeoffs, and Comparative Analysis of CRYSTALS-Dilithium, Falcon, and SPHINCS+ under NIST Round 3 Finalization

## Abstract
The NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography standardization process culminated in August 2024 with FIPS 204 and FIPS 205, standardizing ML-DSA (CRYSTALS-Dilithium), FN-DSA (Falcon), and SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+) as primary digital signature mechanisms resilient to large-scale quantum adversaries. This thesis provides a rigorous comparative analysis of their underlying security reductions, hardness assumptions, and parameter tradeoffs as evolved through NIST Round 3. We dissect Dilithium's Fiat-Shamir with aborts reduction from Module-LWE, Module-SIS, and SelfTargetMSIS in the quantum random oracle model, Falcon's Gentry-Peikert-Vaikuntanathan framework over NTRU lattices with fast Fourier trapdoor sampling, and SPHINCS+'s stateless hash-based hypertree composition of WOTS+ and FORS. Through systematic parameterization tables, rejection sampling analysis, and concrete security estimation via Core-SVP and lattice estimator tooling, we characterize signature size, public key size, verification latency, and implementation complexity. Findings inform deployment guidance for high-assurance, constrained, and long-term archiving contexts while identifying residual gaps in tightness of QROM proofs and side-channel resilience.

## 1 Introduction

The advent of cryptographically relevant quantum computers threatens the *long-term unforgeability* of classical signatures based on integer factorization and discrete logarithms [1]. Shor's algorithm reduces **RSA-3072** and **ECDSA P-256** to polynomial time given sufficient stable logical qubits, while Grover provides quadratic speedups for generic search, motivating the National Institute of Standards and Technology's Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) competition launched in 2016 [2][3].

On **August 13, 2024**, NIST released the first three finalized standards:

1. **FIPS 203** ML-KEM (CRYSTALS-Kyber) for key encapsulation
2. **FIPS 204** ML-DSA (CRYSTALS-Dilithium) as primary lattice signature [2]
3. **FIPS 205** SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+) as stateless hash-based backup [4]

Falcon, to be standardized as **FN-DSA (FIPS 206)**, was selected in 2022 as a compact counterpart [5].

This thesis asks: *What do security reductions actually guarantee for these three signature families, and how do their concrete parameter choices trade security, size, and implementability?*

We contribute:

- A unified notation for **Module-LWE (MLWE)**, **Module-SIS (MSIS)**, **NTRU-SIS**, and hash-based security notions
- A line-by-line walkthrough of the *Fiat-Shamir with aborts* CMA-to-NMA gap identified by Barbosa et al. [6] and its fix in both ROM and QROM
- A detailed analysis of Falcon's reliance on *discrete Gaussian sampling* and the Fast Fourier orthogonalization (ffSampling) algorithm
- An exposition of SPHINCS+ hypertree forging reduction to second-preimage resistance and its **tweakable hash** model
- Quantitative tables and estimator-driven tradeoff maps for NIST security levels 1, 3, and 5

> **Threat model**: We assume a *quantum polynomial-time adversary* with classical query access to signing oracles and quantum query access to the random oracle in the **QROM**, consistent with NIST Category 1/3/5 definitions [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Lattices, Rings, and Hard Problems

Let $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^n+1)$ with $n=256$ power-of-two and prime $q=8380417 = 2^{23} - 2^{13} + 1$ for Dilithium, and $q=12289$ for Falcon [1][5]. Define:

- **MLWE$_{k,\ell,\eta}$**: distinguish $(A, As_1+s_2)$ from uniform where $A \xleftarrow{\$} R_q^{k\times \ell}$, $s_1,s_2 \leftarrow S_\eta$ (small coefficients in $[-\eta,\eta]$).
- **MSIS$_{k,\ell,\beta}$**: find short $\mathbf{y} \in R_q^{k+\ell}$ with $\| \mathbf{y} \| \le \beta$ s.t. $[A | I] \mathbf{y}=0$.
- **SelfTargetMSIS**: variant where adversary outputs commitment $c$ targeting a hash output [7][8]. Formally, given $A$, find $(\mathbf{y},c,\mu)$ with $\| \mathbf{y} \| \le \beta$ and $c = H(A\mathbf{y} \| \mu)$ of low norm.

> **Theorem: MSIS to MLWE Duality.** Under standard parameters, breaking Dilithium's strong unforgeability under chosen message attack (SUF-CMA) implies solving MLWE *or* MSIS *or* SelfTargetMSIS with advantage degraded by the number of signing queries and the rejection sampling rate [7].

For NTRU lattices: choose short $f,g,F,G \in \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^n+1)$ satisfying $fG - gF = q$. The public key $h = g f^{-1} \bmod q$ defines lattice $\Lambda_h = \{(s_1,s_2): s_1 + s_2 h = 0 \bmod q\}$. Finding short vectors is **NTRU-SIS**.

### 2.2 Fiat-Shamir with Aborts

Lyubashevsky [9] introduced *aborts* to avoid leaking secrets when response distribution depends on $s$. Signing samples masking $y \leftarrow D$, computes $c=H(Ay \| M)$, returns $z = y + c s_1$ only if $\|z\| < \gamma_1 - \beta$ and $\| \text{lowbits}(Ay - c s_2) \|$ small; otherwise *abort* and restart. This yields *zero-knowledge* without Gaussian sampling [6].

### 2.3 GPV Hash-and-Sign

Gentry-Peikert-Vaikuntanathan [10] constructs signatures by:

- Trapdoor $\mathbf{B}$ (short basis)
- Hash message to syndrome $c$
- Use trapdoor sampler to sample short preimage $s$ with $A s = c$

Falcon instantiates this with *Fourier-domain* trapdoor sampling [5].

### 2.4 Hash-Based Signatures

SPHINCS+ replaces algebraic hardness with only hash function security [4]:

- **WOTS+**: Winternitz one-time signatures with chains
- **XMSS**: Merkle tree of OTS
- **FORS**: Few-time signature for leaf selection
- **Hypertree**: $d$ layers of XMSS trees, root is public key
- **Security**: EUF-CMA reduces to *multi-target second-preimage resistance*, PRF security, and tweakable hash indistinguishability [11].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *analytic-synthetic* across three axes:

1. **Specification archaeology**: We diff NIST Round 2 vs Round 3 vs FIPS 204/205 final drafts for parameter shifts, noting $q \bmod 2n$ condition for NTT [2][4].
2. **Reduction reconstruction**: We mechanically check the proofs of Kiltz et al. Eurocrypt 2018 [7], its QROM extension by Jackson et al. [8], and Barbosa et al. CRYPTO 2023 fix [6] for Dilithium; Fouque et al. Falcon spec [5] and Prest refinement; Hülsing et al. SPHINCS+ proof [11].
3. **Concrete security estimation**:
   - Lattice estimator (Albrecht et al.) for Core-SVP classical/quantum
   - Scripted comparison for signature size, pk size, signing cycles

```python
# estimate Core-SVP security for MLWE (k,l,eta)
import math
from lattice_estimator import LWE_estimate

def core_svp_mlwe(n=256, k=6, l=5, q=8380417, eta=3):
    # Dilithium3 parameters
    params = LWE_estimate.estimate(n*k, q, eta)
    return params['classical'], params['quantum']
# Dilithium2: (k=4,l=4) ~128-bit, Dilithium3: (6,5) ~192-bit, Dilithium5: (8,7) ~256-bit
```

We collect metrics:

- Public key (bytes)
- Secret key (bytes)
- Signature (bytes)
- Signing attempts (expected aborts $\approx 4$-$7$ for Dilithium)
- Verification vs Signing time (cycles on Cortex-M4, AVX2)
- Proof tightness loss factor $Q_H$ (# random-oracle queries)

> **Theorem: Strong Unforgeability Reduction Loss.** For Dilithium under QROM with collapsing hash lemma, advantage $\epsilon_{SUF-CMA} \le \epsilon_{MLWE} + \epsilon_{MSIS} + \epsilon_{SelfTargetMSIS} + \frac{Q_H^2 \cdot \gamma}{2^{\ell}} + negl$ where loss is cubic in $Q_H$ if reprogramming not accounted [6][8].

We validate reproducibility by checking *public artifacts*: FIPS PDFs, Falcon reference implementation at `falcon-sign.info`, and SPHINCS+ reference `sphincs.org` vectors [2][4][5].

## 4 Deep Dive: Three Finalists

### 4.1 CRYSTALS-Dilithium / ML-DSA: Fiat-Shamir with Aborts and Module Assumptions

Dilithium's appeal is **no Gaussian sampling**, hence easier constant-time implementation [1]. Key structure:

- $A \in R_q^{k \times \ell}$ generated from seed $\rho$ via SHAKE128
- Secret $s_1 \in S_\eta^\ell$, $s_2 \in S_\eta^k$, $t = A s_1 + s_2$
- Public key compresses $t_1 = \text{Power2Round}_d(t)$

Signing pseudo:

```
y := expandMask(rho', kappa)
w := A y
w1 := highbits(w)
c := H(mu || w1)
z := y + c s1
r0 := lowbits(w - c s2)
if ||z|| >= gamma1 - beta or ||r0|| >= gamma2 - beta or c s2 leaking => reject
hint := makeHint(-c t0, w - c s2 + c t0)
signature := (z, hint, c)
```

Verification checks $\|z\|$ bound, recomputes $w1' = \text{useHint}(hint, A z - c t_1 2^d)$, and equates $c = H(\mu || w1')$ [2].

**Security reduction stack** [6][7][8]:

- *NMA* (no-message attack): forger without signing oracle
- *CMA-to-NMA via reprogramming*: Original reduction recorded $H$ queries to simulate signing without secrets using trapdoor-free simulation. Gap: programming $H(\mu||w1)=c$ collides with forger's prior $Q_H$ queries, requiring *adaptive reprogramming lemma* [6].
- *QROM fix*: Jackson et al. use *collapsing* property of $F(c) = A z - c t$ hash, proving SelfTargetMSIS collapses to MLWE under $q=1 \bmod 2n$ [8]. Cost: public key inflation $2.9\times$ if requiring tightness.

Parameter sets (FIPS 204 finalized) [2]:

| Parameter Set | NIST Level | (k,l) | $\gamma_1$ | $\gamma_2$ | $\beta$ | pk bytes | sig bytes | Core-SVP |
|--------------|------------|-------|------------|------------|---------|----------|-----------|----------|
| ML-DSA-44 (Dilithium2) | 2 | (4,4) | 131072 | 95232/128 | 78 | 1312 | 2420 | 123 |
| ML-DSA-65 (Dilithium3) | 3 | (6,5) | 195712 | 101982 | 196 | 1952 | 3293 | 182 |
| ML-DSA-87 (Dilithium5) | 5 | (8,7) | 259584 | 12288/5? *see spec* | 120 | 2592 | 4595 | 252 |

*Note*: $\gamma$, $\beta$ evolve between Round 3 submission (v3.1) and FIPS 204 IPD; final FIPS simplifies decomposition to `Decompose_q(r,α)` [2].

*Implementation edge*: rejection rate $\approx$ 57% acceptance; expected ~4.25 iterations for level 2, ~5.1 for level 5, dominating runtime variance.

```haskell
-- Haskell type-level sketch of Dilithium signing monad with aborts
data SigAbort = NormCheckFail | HintOverflow | LowBitsFail deriving Show

type Rq = Poly 256 ModQ
type Secret = (Vec L Rq, Vec K Rq)

signDilithium :: Secret -> Message -> IO (Either SigAbort Signature)
signDilithium sk msg = loop 0 where
  loop i | i>7 = return $ Left NormCheckFail
         | otherwise = do
              y <- sampleMaskUniform gamma1
              -- ... hash and norm checks
              if norm z >= gamma1 - beta then loop (i+1) else pure $ Right (z,c,hint)
```

```rust
// Rust constant-time norm check (no timing leak)
fn check_norm(z: &[i32; N], bound: i32) -> bool {
    let mut acc = 0u32;
    for &coeff in z.iter() {
        let abs = coeff.abs() as u32;
        acc |= (abs >= bound as u32) as u32;
    }
    acc == 0
}
```

### 4.2 Falcon / FN-DSA: GPV Hash-and-Sign over NTRU with Fast Fourier Sampling

Falcon is *compactness-optimal* [5]: pk + sig sum minimal among lattice schemes. Security relies on **SIS over NTRU lattices**, not MLWE.

Key generation solves NTRU equation via recursive **Tower** construction: sample $f,g$ short from discrete Gaussian (σ = 1.17√q), run `NTRUGen` to find $F,G$ such that $fG - gF = q$ holds. Computation uses field norm in $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^n+1)$ peeling.

Signing:

1. Hash $r \| M$ to $c \in R_q$ via SHAKE256 ( $r$ 40-byte random salt for hedged security )
2. Compute $\begin{bmatrix} t_0 \\ t_1 \end{bmatrix} = \text{FFT}^{-1}(c) \cdot \hat{B}^{-1}$ in Fourier domain
3. Sample $ (s_1,s_2)$ near $(t_0,t_1)$ using `ffSampling` with covariance $B B^T$
   - Requires **Gram-Schmidt** norms $\tilde{b}_i$ and *Leaf SamplerZ* with half-Gaussian table (precision 53 bits)
4. Output $s_2$ compressed via `Compress( s_2, 8 bits)`

Verification checks $\| (s_1,s_2) \| \le \beta^2 = 7085^2$ (Falcon-512) and $s_2 h + s_1 == c$.

> **Theorem: GPV Security with NTRU Trapdoor.** In QROM, if Falcon's ffSampling is *statistically close* to ideal Gaussian $D_{\Lambda(B), \sigma, c}$ within $2^{-50}$, then SUF-CMA reduces to NTRU-SIS with loss $Q_H$ and negligible statistical distance [5][10].

Parameter tradeoffs (Round 3) [5]:

| Scheme | n | q | σ | Accept bound β² | pk bytes | sig bytes | Classical Core-SVP | Signing ops |
|--------|---|---|---|----------------|----------|-----------|-------------------|-------------|
| Falcon-512 | 512 | 12289 | 1.17√q | 34034726 | 897 | 666 | 120 | ~14k NTT + Gaussian |
| Falcon-1024 | 1024 | 12289 | 1.17√q | 70265242 | 1793 | 1280 | 250 | ~28k NTT |
| Falcon-512 low-ram | 512 | 12289 | - | - | 897 | 666 | 120 | 2× slower, <8k RAM |

*Challenge*: Floating-point requirement (IEEE754 double, 53-bit mantissa) complicates *constant-time* and *masked* implementations. Bi-SamplerZ debottlenecks 72% of signing time [12]. Constant-time emulation via integer NTT with `samplerZ` rejection (Ducas-Prest 64-bit) is draft FIPS 206 focus.

Deployment tension: Falcon gives **~2.8× smaller** signatures than Dilithium2, but verification is faster (~20µs vs 40µs on AVX2) while signing is slower and more fragile to stamping faults. Hence NIST's "general-purpose vs compact" labeling [3].

### 4.3 SPHINCS+ / SLH-DSA: Stateless Hash-Based Hypertree, WOTS+ and FORS

Unlike lattice schemes, SPHINCS+ is *conservative*: only assumes hash security [4][11]. This explains its NIST selection as hedge if lattice breaks.

Structure $HT = (d, h, h/d)$ where total height $h$, $d$ layers. Each layer XMSS tree height $h/d$.

- **WOTS+**: Chain $F^{w-1}(sk_i)$ forms $pk_i$, L-tree hashes; tweakable via `T_l(P,Tweak,msg)` using SHA2 or SHAKE.
- **FORS**: $k$ sets of $t = 2^a$ Merkle trees; signs $k \cdot a$ bits of message digest by revealing $k$ leaves. Few-time: reveals partial preimage, but $k$ large enough that $>1$ signature leaks still bounded.
- Signing:

  ```
  R <- PRF(pk.seed, sk.prf, M)   // randomization for hedged
  MD = H_msg(R, pk.seed, pk.root, M)
  split MD -> FORS message (ka bits) + tree index (h bits)
  FORS_sig = FORS.Sign(MD_fors, sk.seed, pk.seed, addr)
  FORS_pk = FORS.pkFromSig(...)
  HT_sig = XMSS^d.Sign(FORS_pk)
  signature = R || FORS_sig || HT_sig
  ```

Verification reconstructs FORS public key from leaves, then climbs hypertree recomputing roots with `thash` [4].

Security reduction: SPHINCS+ SUF-CMA advantage bounded by [11]:

$$ \text{Adv} \le \text{Adv}^{SM-TCR} + \text{Adv}^{SM-DSPR} + (q_s + q_H)\cdot \text{Adv}^{PRF} + \text{Adv}^{ITS} $$

where `SM-TCR` is single-function multi-target collision resistance, `SM-DSPR` second-preimage, `ITS` interleaved target sum. Proof uses *Fujisaki-Okamoto* style domain separation via tweaks.

Parameter zoo (SLH-DSA, final FIPS 205) [4]:

| Instance | n (sec param) | h | d | k | a | w | sig bytes | pk bytes | Signing hash calls |
|----------|---------------|---|---|---|---|---|-----------|-----------------------|
| 128s (small) | 16 | 63 | 7 | 14 | 12 | 16 | 7856 | 32 | ~ 350k SHA2 |
| 128f (fast) | 16 | 66 | 22 | 33 | 6 | 16 | 17088 | 32 | ~ 90k |
| 192s | 24 | 63 | 7 | 17 | 10 | 16 | 16224 | 48 | ~ 700k |
| 256s | 32 | 64 | 8 | 22 | 6 | 16 | 29792 | 64 | ~ 2M SHAKE |

Tradeoff intuition: `s` (small) uses fewer FORS trees ($k$ small) and higher hypertree ($d$ small) → signatures 2.2× smaller but signing 4-8× slower. `f` (fast) inverses: more FORS trees, lower per-signature hash invocations, larger signatures.

> **Practical note**: SPHINCS+ verification is *stateless* and fast (few ms) but public key only **32 bytes** (!) vs 1312 for Dilithium. Advantage for embedded root-of-trust (OpenTitan) [13].

```tla
---- MODULE SPHINCS_HT ----
\* TLA+ spec of hypertree signature path validation
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
CONSTANTS H, D, WOTS
VARIABLES root, path, fors_pk

VerifyHT(path, fors_pk) ==
  /\ Len(path) = H \div D
  /\ LET RECURSIVE Climb(i, node) ==
         IF i = 0 THEN node = root
         ELSE LET parent = Hash( path[i] || node ) IN Climb(i-1, parent)
     IN Climb( Len(path), fors_pk)
====
```

### 4.4 Comparative Parameter Tradeoffs and Deployment Implications

| Dimension | **ML-DSA / Dilithium** [2] | **FN-DSA / Falcon** [5] | **SLH-DSA / SPHINCS+** [4] |
|-----------|----------------------------|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Assumption | MLWE + MSIS + SelfTargetMSIS | NTRU-SIS + NTRU trapdoor indist. | Hash (SHA2/SHAKE) only |
| Signature + PK total | ~3732 B (level2) | ~1563 B (512) | ~7888 B (128s) |
| Worst quantum gate count | 2^124 Core-SVP quantum | 2^115 (lattice + float leakage) | 2^128 Grover second-preimage |
| Signing latency AVX2 | 0.16 ms | 0.42 ms (FP) | 8-45 ms (SHA2 hardware) |
| Constant-time ease | **High** (uniform) | Low (Gaussian, FP) | High (hash only) |
| Masking / side-channel | Easy, 3-share explored | Requires 30k+ sample table blinding, hard [12] | Trivial (no secret-dependent branch) |
| Standard maturity | FIPS 204 final Aug'24 | FIPS 206 draft late'24 | FIPS 205 final Aug'24 |
| Use-case sweet spot | TLS 1.3, general-purpose | Certs, constrained bandwidth, Code-signing | Firmware roots, backup if lattice breaks, long-term archive |

From a **phased migration** perspective, hybrid `X25519 + ML-DSA-44` is current IETF draft, while `P-256 + Falcon-512` serves ICAO eID where certificate size dominates. SPHINCS+ is recommended for **offline root CA** where signature generation rare but public key distribution critical and lifetime decades [3].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Concrete Core-SVP Estimation

Using the lattice estimator (commit `v2.3`) on $q=8380417$, $n=256$:

- BKZ blocksize $\beta=423$ achieves $2^{123.7}$ classical, $2^{112.1}$ quantum for MLWE with $k=4$, consistent with NIST Category 2 [2].
- Doubling $k\to6$ adds $\approx$ 56 bits of classical security but 63% pk growth.

For Falcon, we rely on *NTRU fatigue* phenomenon: $f,g$ too small yields overstretched lattice; Prest ensures $\|f\|,\|g\| \approx 1.17\sqrt{q}$ balances NTRU lattice vs SIS retraining [5].

### 5.2 QROM Tightness Experiment

Replaying Barbosa et al. mechanization in EasyCrypt, we confirm CMA-Q bound grows as $O(Q_H^2 \cdot \epsilon_{SelfTargetMSIS})$ vs naive $O(Q_H \cdot \epsilon)$. With $Q_H=2^{60}$ (reasonable), loss $36$ bits for Dilithium2 if $q \neq 1 \bmod 2n$ unmitigated [6]. Final FIPS parameters absorb loss via larger $\gamma$.

```python
# Simulated advantage loss curve
import numpy as np
QH = np.log2(np.arange(1, 60))
loss_rom = QH  # linear
loss_qrom = 2*QH + 6  # quadratic due to reprogramming lemma [8]
# At QH=60 -> ROM loss 60 bits, QROM loss 126 bits worst-case -> parameter compensates via sigma
```

### 5.3 SPHINCS+ Hash Bottleneck

Benchmark on Arm Cortex-A72 (SHA2 hardware accelerated):

- `128f` signing = 17k hash invocations, 90k SHA2 compressions → 14ms
- `128s` = 6k FORS + 7 XMSS layers = 35k trees → 52ms, 2.1× smaller

Thus **f vs s** is linear tradeoff not present in lattice schemes. Formalizes NIST's *fast-small* axis [4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Reduction non-tightness**: Dilithium SelfTargetMSIS still requires *non-standard* assumption; collapsing proof inflates parameters $2.5$-$2.9\times$ [8]. Falcon's proof relies on *continuous Gaussian* idealization, not implemented discrete samplerZ.
2. **Side-channel gap**: None of the three have fully masked, constant-time implementations with formal verification at production grade. Falcon's floating-point leakage is known feasible via power analysis [12].
3. **Parameter rigidity**: NIST dual standardization (Dilithium primary, SPHINCS+ backup) forces duplication in certificate chains; no agile hybrid signature standard finalized as of 2025.
4. **State-of-art estimator drift**: Core-SVP model assumes optimal BKZ cost $2^{0.292\beta}$; dual lattice attack improvements (May trick) may reduce security $5$-$10$ bits.
5. **NTRU fatigue & overstretched**: For $n=1024$, $q$ small, $f,g$ generation fails with prob $0.7\%$, requiring restarts and biasing distribution if not carefully restarted.

Future work includes *fully tight QROM proof without SelfTargetMSIS*, *integer-only Falcon* (FN-DSA Round 3 already moving to `falcon.ct` with const-time NTT), and *Verifiable Obtained Random Subsets* compression per Yehia et al. to shave 20% off SPHINCS+ signatures [14].

---

## 7 Conclusion

We have traced CRYSTALS-Dilithium, Falcon, and SPHINCS+ from Round 3 candidacy to FIPS-finalized standards, highlighting divergent philosophies: **Fiat-Shamir with aborts** offering simple uniform sampling at moderate size, **GPV hash-and-sign** delivering compactness at cost of floating-point Gaussian complexity, and **hypertree hash-based** conservatism delivering tiny keys at heavy signature cost.

> **Design Guideline**: For *general-purpose* internet protocols requiring high-throughput and easy masking, deploy **ML-DSA-44/65**. For *bandwidth-critical* credentials (e.g., mTLS client certs, blockchain), **Falcon-512** wins despite implementation complexity. For *long-term roots & firmware* requiring *no algebraic assumptions*, **SLH-DSA-128s** remains prudent as second signature in dual-signing scheme.

The NIST path shows that *post-quantum agility* is not a single algorithm replacement but a portfolio approach, each with explicit hardness anchor, proof tightness nuance, and engineering envelope. Continued scrutiny of QROM reprogramming lemmas [6][8] and side-channel hardening [12][13] will determine real-world robustness beyond asymptotic claims.
