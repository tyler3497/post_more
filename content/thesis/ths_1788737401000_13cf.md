# Grid States and the Bosonic Road to Fault Tolerance: Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill Codes

## Abstract

The Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) code defines an oscillator qubit through simultaneous eigenstates of two commuting phase-space displacement operators, realizing codewords as Dirac combs on a square (or hexagonal) lattice whose spacing sets the correctable displacement radius [1]. Ideal GKP states are nonphysical; practice centers on finite-energy approximations with effective squeezing exceeding a ~10 dB threshold [2][3]. Stabilizer measurements are implemented through phase estimation, conditional-displacement feedback, or the small–big–small (sBs) protocol [4]. Experimental milestones progressed from the first grid-state QEC demonstration in 2020 [5] to real-time correction beyond break-even in 2023, with coherence gain $G = 2.27 \pm 0.07$ [6]. Concatenation with the surface code — the surface–GKP architecture — restores exponential logical-error suppression with thresholds around $9.9$–$10.5$ dB under circuit-level Gaussian noise [2][7]. This thesis develops the GKP formalism from lattice stabilizers through finite-energy deformations, derives the threshold behavior and the sBs mechanism, and surveys superconducting, trapped-ion, and photonic platforms, identifying squeezing quality, syndrome-extraction overhead, and leakage as the remaining barriers to scalable fault-tolerant computation.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum error correction (QEC) is conventionally conceived as a resource theory of redundancy: a logical qubit is distributed across many physical qubits, and noncommuting stabilizer measurements extract error syndromes without learning the encoded state. Bosonic codes invert this philosophy. Rather than multiplying the number of physical systems, they exploit the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space of a single quantum harmonic oscillator, embedding discrete logical information in continuous-variable degrees of freedom that are natively available in superconducting microwave cavities, trapped-ion motional modes, and optical fields [1][8].

Within the bosonic zoo — cat codes, binomial codes, rotation-symmetric codes — the GKP code occupies a distinguished position: it is the only bosonic code for which the full logical Clifford group is implementable with Gaussian unitaries alone [9], using quadrature measurements and controlled displacements, the native instruction set of continuous-variable platforms. Its codewords are *grid states*: superpositions of position eigenstates forming a lattice comb in phase space. Small displacement errors — the dominant failure mode under photon loss and dephasing — are detected by measuring each quadrature modulo the lattice spacing and corrected by recentering [10].

The physical unrealizability of ideal grid states is the central tension of the field. Every experiment and threshold theorem concerns *finite-energy* GKP states: damped, Gaussian-enveloped approximations whose sharpness is quantified in decibels of effective squeezing. The defining quantitative question is a threshold question: *how much squeezing is sufficient for error correction to help rather than hurt?* Analytical and numerical studies converge on approximately $10$ dB under additive Gaussian noise, the field's north star for two decades [2][11].

This thesis develops the GKP formalism from lattice stabilizers through finite-energy deformations, derives the threshold behavior and the sBs stabilization mechanism, and surveys the experimental state of the art in superconducting, trapped-ion, and photonic platforms.

## 2. Background

### The oscillator and its displacements

Consider a single bosonic mode with quadratures $\hat{q}, \hat{p}$ satisfying $[\hat{q}, \hat{p}] = i$ ($\hbar = 1$). The quadrature displacements $\hat{D}_q(u) = \exp(-iu\hat{p})$ and $\hat{D}_p(v) = \exp(iv\hat{q})$ shift $\hat{q}$ by $u$ and $\hat{p}$ by $v$. Two displacements commute up to a symplectic phase; the GKP construction selects two whose phase is a multiple of $2\pi$, rendering them *exactly commuting* stabilizers.

> **Theorem.** Let $\hat{S}_q = \hat{D}_q(2\sqrt{\pi})$ and $\hat{S}_p = \hat{D}_p(2\sqrt{\pi})$. Then $[\hat{S}_q, \hat{S}_p] = 0$, and the simultaneous $+1$ eigenspace of $\{\hat{S}_q, \hat{S}_p\}$ is a two-dimensional subspace of the oscillator Hilbert space, admitting a qubit decomposition.

*Proof sketch.* The commutator phase is $\exp(\pm 8\pi i) = 1$, so the stabilizers commute. The square lattice's fundamental domain has area $4\pi$ while one quantum state occupies $2\pi$ of phase space; the code space therefore encodes $\log_2(4\pi/2\pi) = 1$ qubit, rigorously via the Zak transform [1]. ∎

The ideal logical basis states are combs of position eigenstates:

$$|0_L\rangle \propto \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |q = 2n\sqrt{\pi}\rangle, \qquad |1_L\rangle \propto \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |q = (2n+1)\sqrt{\pi}\rangle.$$

The logical Pauli operators are the half-lattice displacements $\hat{X}_L = \hat{D}_q(\sqrt{\pi})$ and $\hat{Z}_L = \hat{D}_p(\sqrt{\pi})$, satisfying the Pauli algebra up to stabilizer-equivalence: $\hat{X}_L \hat{Z}_L = -\hat{Z}_L \hat{X}_L$ on the code space. Logical Cliffords — Hadamard (a $\pi/2$ rotation), phase gate (shearing symplectic map), and CNOT (controlled-$\hat{X}$ SUM gate) — are all Gaussian, i.e., generated by quadratic Hamiltonians [1][9].

| Concept | Square lattice | Hexagonal lattice |
|---|---|---|
| Stabilizer generators | $\hat{D}_q(2\sqrt{\pi}), \hat{D}_p(2\sqrt{\pi})$ | Three displacements at $120^\circ$, spacing $2\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt{3}}$ |
| Lattice spacing (correctable radius) | $\sqrt{\pi}/2 \approx 0.886$ | $\approx 0.977$ |
| Wigner cell area | $4\pi$ | $4\pi$ |
| Symmetry | $C_4$ (Hadamard natural) | $C_6$ (better vs. isotropic noise) |
| First experimental QEC demo | Superconducting cavity [5] | Same device, same paper [5] |

The hexagonal variant, with its denser sphere packing, offers a larger correctable displacement radius for isotropic noise such as photon loss, and both lattices were demonstrated in the landmark 2020 experiment [5].

### From ideal to physical: the envelope

Ideal codewords are eigenstates of the stabilizers with eigenvalue $+1$, but they carry infinite mean photon number. The standard regularization applies a Gaussian damping operator to the ideal state:

$$|\psi_{\Delta}\rangle \propto \hat{E}_{\Delta}\, |\psi_{\mathrm{ideal}}\rangle, \qquad \hat{E}_{\Delta} = \exp(-\Delta^2 \hat{n}),$$

with $\hat{n} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$. Each comb tooth is a Gaussian of variance $\Delta^2/2$ under an envelope of variance $1/(2\Delta^2)$. The effective squeezing is $s_{\mathrm{dB}} = -10\log_{10}(2\Delta^2)$; $\Delta = 0.22$ corresponds to roughly $10$ dB, and $\Delta \to 0$ recovers the ideal state weakly [2][12].

## 3. Methodology

The methodology of GKP error correction comprises three interlocking layers: the noise model that defines correctability, the finite-energy deformation that defines physicality, and the stabilization protocol that defines implementability.

### Noise model and the correctable radius

Under an additive Gaussian displacement channel — independent Gaussian shifts $(u, v)$ with variance $\sigma^2$ — a logical error occurs only when a shift exceeds half the lattice spacing, $|u| > \sqrt{\pi}/2$ or $|v| > \sqrt{\pi}/2$. The per-round logical error probability is the Gaussian tail $P_{\mathrm{err}} \approx 2\,Q(\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sigma))$, and fault tolerance reduces to whether repeated noisy correction suppresses $P_{\mathrm{err}}$ faster than physical noise accumulates it — the threshold analysis of Noh, Girvin, and Jiang for the surface–GKP concatenation, at approximately $10$ dB [2][7].

Photon loss, the dominant physical noise in cavities, is not a pure displacement channel. The canonical treatment converts loss to effective Gaussian displacement via the *twirl*: random stabilizer displacements symmetrize loss into a displacement channel of variance $\sigma^2 \approx \kappa t$ [11]. This underlies the result that GKP codes protect against pure loss at rates where other bosonic codes fail, shown by Albert et al. through semidefinite-programming bounds on the optimal recovery [13].

### The small–big–small (sBs) stabilization protocol

Direct measurement of the stabilizer eigenvalues requires resolving phase modulo $2\pi$ with a coherent ancilla interaction. The small–big–small protocol sidesteps this by implementing *engineered dissipation* whose steady states are the finite-energy GKP codewords. One sBs round for the $\hat{q}$ quadrature:

```python
# sBs stabilization round for the q-quadrature of a finite-energy GKP qubit
# ancilla: transmon qubit dispersively coupled to cavity mode
def sbs_q_round(cavity, ancilla, eps, big_disp):
    # (1) SMALL: conditional displacement by eps/2, entangling
    #     ancilla phase with cavity position modulo the lattice
    cdisplace(cavity, ancilla, alpha=eps / 2, axis='q')
    # (2) BIG: unconditional displacement by big_disp ~ sqrt(pi),
    #     mapping the error syndrome onto a large, resolvable shift
    displace(cavity, alpha=big_disp, axis='q')
    # (3) SMALL: second conditional displacement; ancilla measurement
    #     outcome heralds the modular position, resetting the ancilla
    cdisplace(cavity, ancilla, alpha=eps / 2, axis='q')
    syndrome_bit = measure(ancilla)   # 0/1 -> displacement feedback
    if syndrome_bit:
        displace(cavity, alpha=-big_disp, axis='q')  # recenter the comb
    reset(ancilla)
```

The small displacements ($\epsilon \ll \sqrt{\pi}$) probe the modular quadrature gently, the big displacement amplifies the syndrome into a resolvable signal, and the final small displacement disentangles the ancilla. Alternating $q$ and $p$ rounds pumps the oscillator toward the code manifold; this Markovian cycle was shown experimentally to suppress all logical error channels simultaneously [5][6]. Reinforcement learning was later used to optimize the pulse parameters in situ [6].

### Concatenation and decoding

A single GKP qubit corrects only small displacements; large errors and ancilla faults must be handled at a higher level. The surface–GKP architecture concatenates GKP qubits into a surface code, feeding the *analog* modular-displacement values (not just binary outcomes) into the surface-code decoder. This soft-information decoding substantially raises the threshold, because a GKP measurement reporting a displacement near the decision boundary is flagged as unreliable [2][7].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Geometry of phase-space error correction

The square-lattice GKP code is a two-dimensional classical repetition code embedded in phase space. Measuring $\hat{q} \bmod 2\sqrt{\pi}$ reveals the displacement modulo the lattice; the minimum-distance decoder applies $-\,\mathrm{argmin}_{k}|u - 2k\sqrt{\pi}|$, optimal for the Gaussian channel and implemented by sBs feedback. The hexagonal lattice replaces square decision regions with hexagonal Voronoi cells of larger inradius ($\approx 0.977$ vs. $0.886$), directly reducing the logical error rate against isotropic noise [5][11].

A subtlety arises from the envelope: $\hat{E}_{\Delta}$ does not commute with displacements, so finite-energy codewords are only approximate stabilizer eigenstates. The intrinsic logical error scales as $\exp(-\pi/(4\Delta^2))$ — negligible at $10$ dB ($\Delta \approx 0.22$) but dominant below $\sim 7$ dB — which is what makes the squeezing threshold a genuine phase transition rather than a gradual crossover [2][12].

### 4.2 The surface–GKP threshold

Noh, Girvin, and Jiang's analysis of the surface–GKP code remains the definitive fault-tolerance result for bosonic qubits [2]. Modeling each GKP qubit under i.i.d. Gaussian displacement noise of variance $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{gkp}}$ per gate, with a minimum-weight perfect matching decoder fed by analog GKP syndrome data, they find $\sigma_{\mathrm{th}} \approx 0.194$, i.e., $\approx 10.5$ dB of squeezing for ideal preparation, improving to $\approx 9.9$ dB with optimized decoding. Below threshold, logical error is suppressed exponentially in distance $d$; above it, correction is counterproductive. The threshold's robustness was confirmed under teleportation-based correction with noisy homodyne detection, where measurement inefficiency $\eta$ adds Gaussian broadening [7].

> **Theorem (informal, Noh–Girvin–Jiang).** For the surface–GKP code under i.i.d. Gaussian displacement noise, there exists $\sigma_{\mathrm{th}} > 0$ such that for $\sigma < \sigma_{\mathrm{th}}$ the logical error rate vanishes exponentially with code distance. Numerically $\sigma_{\mathrm{th}} \simeq 0.19$, i.e., $\sim 10$ dB squeezing.

The practical reading of this theorem is demanding: every operation — preparation, entangling gates, homodyne measurement — must contribute less than $\sim 0.19$ quadrature units of noise *combined*. Current experiments reach $9$–$12$ dB of effective squeezing in isolation, but gate and measurement noise still exceeds the budget, placing the community in the regime of *demonstrating* correction without yet *benefiting* from concatenation [6][14].

### 4.3 Noise models: Gaussian displacement versus photon loss

Two noise models dominate the literature, and their relationship is a frequent source of confusion. The *Gaussian displacement channel* is analytically tractable and yields clean thresholds, but it is an idealization. *Photon loss* — amplitude damping at rate $\kappa$ — is the physical process in cavities and fibers. The bridge is the stabilizer twirl: conjugating loss by random stabilizer displacements produces an effective displacement channel with variance $\sigma^2 \approx \kappa t$ for short times, plus a residual non-Gaussian component vanishing as squeezing increases [11][13].

Albert et al. showed via convex optimization over all recovery maps that GKP codes come closest to the ultimate performance limits for pure loss among known bosonic codes, outperforming cat and binomial codes at experimentally relevant loss rates [13]. This near-optimality against loss, combined with Gaussian Clifford gates, is the core of the GKP value proposition.

### 4.4 Experimental platforms and the state of the art

**Superconducting circuits** lead the field. The Yale/Devoret program demonstrated QEC of a grid-state qubit in a 3D microwave cavity with transmon ancilla in 2020, suppressing all logical error channels in agreement with theory [5]. In 2023, Sivak et al. achieved real-time QEC beyond break-even: a fully stabilized GKP qubit with lifetime $\sim 1.8$ ms, beating the best physical component by $G = 2.27 \pm 0.07$, via reinforcement-learned control and sub-microsecond FPGA feedback [6]. Logical two-qubit gates were later demonstrated through echoed conditional displacement (ECD) gates at $96$–$98\%$ fidelity [14].

**Trapped ions** encode GKP states in motional modes, with spin-dependent optical forces implementing conditional displacements. De Neeve et al. demonstrated dissipative stabilization of a grid-state logical qubit with autonomous feedback in 2022 [15]; Noh, Chamberlin, and Brennen developed the phase-estimation preparation protocol theoretically [16]. Ion coherence times are long, but gate speeds and motional heating limit performance.

**Photonic platforms** pursue GKP states for fault-tolerant measurement-based computing. Menicucci showed GKP qubits enable fault-tolerant one-way QC on continuous-variable cluster states with Gaussian measurements alone [17]; subsequent analyses placed required squeezing at $10$–$15$ dB for the macronode architecture [18]. Deterministic high-squeezing photonic GKP sources — via photon subtraction or breeding — remain the outstanding experimental challenge.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The experimental record of GKP error correction is a sequence of barrier crossings:

| Year | Result | Platform | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019–2020 | First QEC of a grid-state qubit [5] | Supercond. cavity | Logical error suppression, square + hexagonal |
| 2021 | Dissipative stabilization, trapped ion [15] | Ion motion | Autonomous feedback, no measurement |
| 2022–2023 | Beyond break-even real-time QEC [6] | Supercond. cavity | $G = 2.27 \pm 0.07$, $T_{1,\mathrm{logical}} \approx 1.8$ ms |
| 2024 | Logical two-qubit gates [14] | Supercond. cavity | ECD-based CNOT, $96$–$98\%$ |
| 2025+ | Photonic grid-state generation | Optics | Breeding/subtraction, $\sim 5$–$8$ dB |

The Sivak et al. beyond-break-even result is the field's most significant empirical proof point [6]. Break-even — the error-corrected qubit outliving its best constituent component — shows the QEC cycle removes entropy faster than control overhead injects it, the necessary (though not sufficient) condition for scalable fault tolerance. The gain of $2.27$ came from tantalum-based transmon fabrication, real-time FPGA feedback executing the sBs cycle in a few hundred nanoseconds, and model-free reinforcement learning optimizing pulse shapes against the true experimental cost function.

On the theoretical side, two results anchor the fault-tolerance claim. Glancy and Knill showed phase-estimation-based syndrome extraction achieves the correctable-radius bound up to the intrinsic envelope error [11]. The surface–GKP threshold theorem of Noh et al. supplies the composable guarantee: below $\sim 10$ dB of effective noise, concatenation suppresses logical errors exponentially [2]. Together with Baragiola et al.'s all-Gaussian universality result — a distillable magic state obtainable by GKP error correction of a thermal state, removing the need for non-Gaussian gates [9] — these complete the logical chain from a single stabilized oscillator to universal fault-tolerant computation.

> **Lemma.** Finite-energy GKP codewords with envelope parameter $\Delta$ incur an intrinsic logical error probability bounded by $\exp(-\pi/(4\Delta^2))$ per ideal correction round, independent of external noise [2].

> **Theorem.** The union of Gaussian operations and GKP error correction on thermal states is universal for quantum computation and, below the surface–GKP threshold, fault-tolerant [9][2].

## 6. Limitations

Despite two decades of theoretical development and striking experimental progress, significant limitations separate present GKP devices from scalable fault tolerance.

**The squeezing gap.** The $\sim 10$ dB threshold is a *total noise budget*, not a state-preparation specification. Experiments reporting $9$–$12$ dB characterize the prepared state in isolation; entangling gates and homodyne measurements add noise that pushes the total above threshold. Closing the gap requires simultaneous improvement of state quality, gate fidelity, and measurement efficiency [7][14].

**Non-Gaussian resource overhead.** While logical Cliffords are Gaussian, universality needs magic states, and GKP magic-state distillation carries substantial overhead. The Baragiola et al. protocol distills magic states from thermal states via GKP error correction [9], but its yield and fidelity requirements at realistic squeezing imply large distillation factories, partially offsetting the hardware efficiency motivating bosonic codes.

**Ancilla-induced errors.** The sBs protocol relies on a transmon ancilla whose errors — relaxation, dephasing, thermal excitation — propagate into the oscillator during conditional displacements. Fault-tolerant syndrome extraction demands ancilla errors be detectable or biased into correctable channels, addressed in theory by path-independent gates and flag qubits but only partially in practice [5][6].

**Leakage and non-Markovian effects.** Photon loss beyond the correctable radius causes logical errors no sBs cycling can fix; they must be caught by the outer code. The twirl-based reduction of loss to Gaussian noise is approximate, and residual non-Gaussian correlations between rounds violate the independence assumptions of threshold proofs [11][13].

**Platform trade-offs.** Superconducting cavities offer fast, high-fidelity control but suffer photon loss on millisecond timescales; trapped ions offer superb coherence but slow gates and motional heating; photonics offers room-temperature scalability but lacks deterministic high-squeezing GKP sources. No platform currently satisfies all requirements simultaneously [15][17][18].

## 7. Conclusion

The GKP code remains the most theoretically complete and experimentally validated bosonic route to fault-tolerant quantum computation. Its elegance lies in the alignment of code structure with physical noise: the small displacements that oscillators naturally suffer are exactly what lattice stabilizers detect and Gaussian feedback corrects. The progression from the 2001 proposal [1] through phase-estimation preparation [10], the first grid-state QEC demonstration [5], and beyond-break-even real-time correction [6] traces a steady conversion of mathematical possibility into laboratory reality.

The central target — roughly $10$ dB of effective squeezing within a total noise budget including gates and measurement — is within sight of leading experiments but not yet in hand for a full fault-tolerant gate set [2][7]. The remaining work is engineering as much as physics: better ancillas, faster feedback, higher-efficiency homodyne detection, and photonic GKP sources. If the squeezing gap closes, the surface–GKP architecture offers large-scale computation with dramatically reduced physical overhead relative to multiqubit codes — fulfilling the hardware-efficiency promise that has motivated bosonic error correction since GKP's original insight: a qubit need not be many things, if one thing is controlled well enough.
