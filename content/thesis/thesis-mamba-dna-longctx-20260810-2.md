# Mamba, Hyena, and Caduceus: State Space Models for Million-Context Genomics at Single-Nucleotide Resolution

## Abstract

Genomic foundation models have been bottlenecked by quadratic attention, limiting context to 512–4k tokens and discarding single-nucleotide resolution. The emergence of structured state space models (SSMs) — Mamba with selective gating, Hyena with implicit long convolutions, and Caduceus with reverse-complement equivariance — enables efficient modeling of DNA at scales up to $1\times10^6$ nucleotides on a single GPU. This thesis synthesizes the architectural evolution from linear-time-invariant S4 to input-dependent SSMs, compares their inductive biases for genomic data, and evaluates their empirical scaling to megabase contexts. We formalize selective SSM recurrences, BiMamba bidirectionality, and MambaDNA's RC-equivariant channel splitting, and show how these designs improve perplexity monotonicity with context length $L\in[1k,1M]$, zero-shot extrapolation $10-100\times$ beyond training length, and downstream variant-effect prediction. HyenaDNA demonstrates 160$\times$ training speedup versus FlashAttention and SotA on 12–17/18 Nucleotide Transformer datasets with orders of magnitude fewer parameters [2], while Caduceus exceeds 10$\times$ larger non-equivariant models on long-range VEP [3]. Recent benchmarking of Caduceus and Hawk versus 50M Transformers confirms matching performance with 1M-token single-GPU inference and superior length generalization [4]. We argue that SSMs are the basis for true genome-scale language modeling.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Context

The human reference genome spans 3.2 billion nucleotides. Regulatory elements such as enhancers can modulate promoters located 1M base pairs away [1][5]. Existing Transformer genomic models — DNABERT, Nucleotide Transformer, GENA-LM — cap context at ~512 to 36k bp (<0.001% of genome) due to $O(L^2)$ attention, and frequently rely on $k$-mer tokenization that collapses single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) [2].

> Motivation: A principled foundation model for genomics must satisfy three properties: (i) **linear or subquadratic $O(L)$ scaling** to reach chromosome-scale contexts, (ii) **single-nucleotide resolution** to capture SNP-induced functional shifts, and (iii) **strand- and direction-aware inductive bias** capturing that DNA is bi-directional and reverse-complement (RC) symmetric, with identical information on both antiparallel strands.

Mamba [1], HyenaDNA [2], and Caduceus [3][6] directly address this triad. Mamba introduces *selective* SSMs where parameters $\Delta, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}$ are functions of the input, enabling content-aware filtering — crucial for sparse regulatory motifs embedded in noisy intergenic repeats [1]. Hyena replaces attention with a pipeline of data-controlled implicit long convolutions generated by a small MLP, achieving sub-quadratic $O(L \log L)$ global receptive field at every layer without materializing $L\times L$ matrices [2]. Caduceus extends Mamba's causal kernel to a bidirectional, RC-equivariant MambaDNA block, enforcing $\text{MambaDNA}( \text{RC}(x)) = \text{RC}( \text{MambaDNA}(x))$ via weight sharing and channel-splitting, yielding first RC-equivariant long-range DNA LM family [3].

This thesis examines how selective SSMs scale to $L=2^{20}=1,048,576$ tokens, maintain perplexity improvements with length while Hyena's LTI convolution degrades, and enable genomic in-context learning for the first time.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Structured State Space Models

Classical SSM:

$$ h'(t) = \mathbf{A}h(t) + \mathbf{B}x(t) $$
$$ y(t) = \mathbf{C}h(t) $$

Discretized with zero-order hold (ZOH) [1]:

$$ \overline{\mathbf{A}} = \exp(\Delta \mathbf{A}) $$
$$ \overline{\mathbf{B}} = (\Delta\mathbf{A})^{-1}(\exp(\Delta\mathbf{A})-I)\cdot\Delta\mathbf{B} $$

Recurrence: $h_t = \overline{\mathbf{A}} h_{t-1} + \overline{\mathbf{B}} x_t$, $y_t = \mathbf{C} h_t$. Convolution view: $y = x * \overline{\mathbf{K}}$, $\overline{\mathbf{K}} = (\mathbf{C}\overline{\mathbf{B}}, \mathbf{C}\overline{\mathbf{A}}\overline{\mathbf{B}}, \dots)$. LTI property makes convolution efficient via FFT, but prevents content selection [1].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Selective SSM for DNA

Mamba enables selection by making $\mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t, \Delta_t$ linear projections of $x_t$ [1].

```python
# selective SSM core (PyTorch-like) for DNA
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

class SelectiveSSM(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model, d_state=16):
        super().__init__()
        self.D = d_model
        self.N = d_state
        self.A = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(d_model, d_state)) # diagonal structure
        self.B_proj = torch.nn.Linear(d_model, d_state)
        self.C_proj = torch.nn.Linear(d_model, d_state)
        self.delta_proj = torch.nn.Linear(d_model, d_model)
    
    def forward(self, x): # x: (B, L, D) ACGT embeddings
        B, L, D = x.shape
        A = -torch.exp(self.A) # stable negative
        Bt = self.B_proj(x)    # (B,L,N) input-dependent
        Ct = self.C_proj(x)    # (B,L,N)
        dt = F.softplus(self.delta_proj(x)) # (B,L,D)
        # ZOH discretize
        dA = torch.einsum('bld,dn->bldn', dt, A)  # (B,L,D,N)
        A_bar = torch.exp(dA)
        B_bar = torch.einsum('bld,b ln -> bldn', dt, Bt) if True else Bt
        # Parallel associative scan (hardware-aware)
        h = torch.zeros(B, D, self.N, device=x.device)
        ys = []
        for t in range(L):
            h = A_bar[:, t] * h + B_bar[:, t].unsqueeze(1) * x[:, t].unsqueeze(-1)
            y_t = torch.einsum('dn,bdn->bd', Ct[:, t], h) # C_t h_t
            ys.append(y_t)
        return torch.stack(ys, dim=1)

# DNA single-nucleotide embedding: vocab 4 + N + special
# Perplexity improves with L up to 1,048,576 [1]
```

Key property: $\Delta_t$ controls forgetting — large $\Delta_t$ $\rightarrow$ reset, focus on $x_t$; small $\Delta_t$ $\rightarrow$ persist state. This enables **selective copy** of regulatory motifs spaced by 100k irrelevant nucleotides, impossible for LTI convs [1].

### 3.2 BiMamba and MambaDNA (Caduceus)

BiMamba shares input/output projections for forward and reversed sequences [3]:

```haskell
-- Haskell-style description of BiMamba weight-sharing
data MambaBlock = MambaBlock { inProj :: Matrix, conv :: Conv1D, ssm :: SSM, outProj :: Matrix }

biMamba :: MambaBlock -> Sequence -> Sequence
biMamba m x = 
  let yFwd = mambaForward m x
      xRev = reverse x
      yRev = reverse (mambaForward m xRev) -- shared params
  in yFwd + yRev
-- MambaDNA adds RC split:
-- x split channel dim d into d/2, apply RC to second half
-- RC(x) = reverseComplement (ATGC complement)
```

MambaDNA [3][6] construction:

- Split $x \in \mathbb{R}^{L\times D}$ into $[x^{(1)}; x^{(2)}]$ along $D$.
- $x^{(2)}_{rc} = \text{RC}(x^{(2)})$: complement A↔T, C↔G then reverse length dimension.
- Process both chunks with same Mamba: $y^{(1)} = \text{Mamba}(x^{(1)}),\; y^{(2)}_{rc}=\text{Mamba}(x^{(2)}_{rc})$.
- Re-RC second output, concat: $y = [y^{(1)}; \text{RC}(y^{(2)}_{rc})]$.

This guarantees RC-equivariance with $1\times$ parameter cost vs data augmentation $2\times$ [3].

### 3.3 Hyena Operator

```rust
// Rust pseudocode for Hyena implicit long convolution
fn hyena_operator(x: &[f32], seq_len: usize, d_model: usize) -> Vec<f32> {
    // projections
    let q = linear_proj(x); // (L,D)
    let k = linear_proj(x);
    let v = linear_proj(x);
    // implicit filter generated by SIREN-like MLP on positional encoding
    let t = positional_encoding(seq_len); // (L, pos_dim)
    let h = mlp_filter(t); // (L, D) long kernel, O(L)
    // global convolution via FFT: y = (q * h_intermediate) gated by v
    let u = fft_conv(k, h); // O(L log L)
    let y = gated_mul(u, q, v); // D x gating as in Hyena
    y
}
// HyenaDNA stack: repeated Hyena → training 160x faster than Transformer at 1M [2]
```

Complexity: $O(L\log L)$ versus $O(L^2)$. Global receptive field each layer — crucial for 1M where Transformer layers would OOM even with FlashAttention [2][5].

Training uses **sequence length warmup** [1][2] to avoid instability at 1M: start 1k, progressive doubling. Gradient steps 20 with 330B tokens for Mamba DNA scaling study [1]; HyenaDNA pretraining on HG38 single-nucleotide masked/next-token [2][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Selection Mechanism as Biological Attention

- ***Content-aware gating*** replaces quadratic $QK^T$ with **$B_t = s_B(x_t), C_t = s_C(x_t)$** — allowing model to *ignore* 99% repetitive LINE/SINE elements in human genome and latch onto sparse transcription-factor binding motifs of 6–12 bp.
- **Why LTI fails on genomics:** HyenaDNA Figure 5 Right [1] shows perplexity improves for Mamba from 1k→1M but degrades for HyenaDNA; very long convolution kernel averages all positions, effectively low-pass filtering critical point mutations.
- **Empirical copy task:** Mamba solves selective copying and induction heads and extrapolates $>1M$ tokens indefinitely [1]. In DNA, this corresponds to recalling a promoter motif seen 800k bp earlier — analogous to enhancer-promoter looping.
- **Hardware-aware scan:** Avoids materializing $(B,L,D,N)$ state — $20GB$ for $L=1M, D=128, N=16$ would OOM; fuses discretization + scan in SRAM, 3× faster on A100 [1].

> Biological intuition: Human genes average 10–15 exons separated by 5k introns; selective SSM mirrors spliceosome's selective retention of exons.

### 4.2 Long-Range Dependencies and In-Context Genomics

- ***HyenaDNA enables soft-prompt tuning:*** With context up to 450k tok (HyenaDNA-small-32k up to 1M-large) [2][5],  $n$ examples of regulatory class are prepended as instructional tokens without weight updates — first demonstration of **in-context learning in genomics** [2].
- **Scaling evidence:** 
  - $L=1k \rightarrow 1M$ tokens: HyenaDNA context 500× larger than previous dense models [2].
  - Training: $160×$ faster than Transformer at 1M (sub-quadratic vs quadratic) [2][5].
  - Memory: Uses single-char tokenizer (vocab 5: A/C/G/T/N + special) enabling **single-nucleotide resolution** [2]; no k-mer collapse loses SNP identity.
- **Downstream:** State-of-the-art on 7/8 GenomicBenchmarks avg +10 accuracy points, 12/18 Nucleotide Transformer tasks with orders-of-magnitude less parameters/pretraining data [2].

Bulleted comparison of what breaks at scale:

- *Quadratic attention* OOMs beyond 32k on A100-40GB; needs chunking that breaks long-range interaction.
- *Long convolution LTI* aggregates noise, perplexity non-monotonic [1].
- *Selective SSM* maintains **constant-time per-step inference** (no KV-cache) and linear training, quality **monotonic** to 1M [1].

### 4.3 Reverse Complement Equivariance and Bidirectionality

- ***Definition:*** $f$ is RC-equivariant if $f(\text{RC}(x)) = \text{RC}(f(x))$. DNA sequenced on either strand with equal probability; model lacking this bias must learn motif and reverse-complement motif as separate patterns, wasting capacity [3][6].
- **Caduceus construction impact:**
  - *Parameter efficiency:* Shares Mamba parameters between forward/RC, improving sample efficiency vs data augmentation $2×$ compute [3].
  - *Bidirectional context:* Unlike causal Mamba NTP, Caduceus MLM uses BiMamba (forward+reverse) capturing both upstream enhancer and downstream terminator influence — essential for variant-effect prediction where causal SNP effect depends on genomic context both sides [3].
- **Empirical:** Caduceus outperforms HyenaDNA and 10× larger Transformers on long-range VEP from *Enformer* dataset (Avsec et al.) predicting gene expression influence of SNPs up to 100k bp away; exceeding 10× larger non-equivariant models [3].
- **Zero-shot extrapolation:** Caduceus and Hawk (another SSM) trained on 12 kbp, tested up to 120 kbp and 1 Mbp without fine-tuning maintains stable performance versus Transformer perplexity blow-up [4].

### 4.4 Million-Token Single-GPU Regime

- ***Single-GPU 1M:*** Both Caduceus/Hawk and HyenaDNA demonstrate 1M tokens on **single A100-40/80GB** [4][5]; earlier Transformer needed sharding.
- **Implications for labs:** Enables modeling entire bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) clone (~200k) or large topologically associating domain (TAD ~1M) in one forward pass [4].
- **Hierarchical regulation aligned:** Authors in [4] note SSM ability to preserve fine-grained nucleotide detail and long-range regulatory interaction aligns with genome's hierarchical regulation — again, selection allows coarse-grained ignoring at 1M scale while preserving nucleotide precision when needed.
- **Tradeoff table quantifies:** Training 1M large HyenaDNA requires A100-80GB vs tiny-1k T4; still subquadratic scaling makes feasible [5].
- **Future chemistries:** Combining Mamba-2 (more expressive state) with Caduceus RC-split promising for diploid/personal genome conditioning — unpublished directions but natural given Mamba-2 scaling improvements noted in BarcodeMamba ablations [7].

### 5.2 Theoretical Justification

> **Theorem 1 (Selective SSM Expressivity).** *Let $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ diagonal negative and input-dependent $\Delta_t = \tau_\Delta(x_t),\,\mathbf{B}_t,\,\mathbf{C}_t$. Then there exists parameter setting reproducing any deterministic content-aware gating that LTI SSM with fixed $(\Delta,\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C})$ cannot approximate with bounded state $N$ unless $N=\Omega(L)$.*

*Proof Sketch.* LTI convolution kernel $\overline{\mathbf{K}}$ is time-invariant; output $y_t = \sum_{k} \overline{\mathbf{K}}_{t-k} x_k$. To zero out irrelevant tokens arbitrarily located depending on content, need kernel zeros depending on input content — impossible with fixed kernel. Selective mechanism allows $\overline{\mathbf{A}}_t = \exp(\Delta_t \mathbf{A})$, making effective kernel input-dependent; setting $\Delta_t\approx 0$ (forget gate closed) preserves state indefinitely ignoring intermediate $x_{t+1:t+k}$, implementing selective copy. Full proof in [1] Appendix C and [3] Lemma 1 RC-equivariance construction. ∎

> **Theorem 2 (RC-Equivariance Parameter Efficiency).** *MambaDNA block with channel split D/2 preserves RC-equivariance exactly with same parameter count as single-direction Mamba, halving data augmentation cost.*

*Proof.* By definition, processing RC branch with shared parameters then RC-ing output commutes with RC input; concatenation equals RC output. Formal in [3] Theorem 3.1. ∎

### 5.3 Ablation: Without Bidirectionality/Equivariance

Caduceus ablations [3][6] show removing BiMamba or RC split drops performance on human-genome downstream by 4–9 points, larger degradation on VEP (requires both upstream/downstream). Without selection (S4 baseline), performance collapses on induction-head synthetic mirroring long-range regulatory recall.

## 6 Limitations

- **Pretraining still human-reference only:** All models evaluated pretrained on HG38 alone [2][5]; multi-species pangenome pretraining remains expensive even at linear scale. Biodiversity gap seen in BarcodeMamba — 70.2% unseen genus accuracy at 63.6% params of BarcodeBERT but limited to barcodes [7].
- **Interpretability of implicit filters:** Hyena implicit kernels $h(t)$ generated by MLP lack closed-form interpretation versus attention maps that biologists visualize for motif attribution. Attribution methods for Mamba selective $\Delta_t$ still nascent.
- **Selection kernel fusion maturity:** Mamba custom CUDA kernels/parallel scan not yet as universally optimized as FlashAttention-2; observed 3× speedup but deployment on TPUs less mature.
- **Bidirectional pretraining objective complexity:** Caduceus MLM requires masked language modeling strategies tuned for RC-equivariant split; causal NTP simpler but misses bidirectional context needed for many genomics tasks [3].
- **Context still < full chromosome:** Even 1M is 0.03% of human genome 3.2B; modelling full chromosome forward pass $>1M$ remains memory-heavy even at $O(L)$. Compression via hierarchical SSM (e.g., Mamba × pooling) unexplored for megabase→gigabase hierarchy.
- **Evaluation bias to human tasks:** GenomicBenchmarks and Nucleotide Transformer heavily human-centric; zero-shot generalization to underrepresented taxa (fungi, invertebrates) needs validation per BarcodeMamba findings of long-tail distribution shift [7].
- **Ethical:** 1M-context models could be misused for synthetic pathogen design; need careful release controls despite open GitHub availability [2][3].

## 7 Conclusion

The Triad of **Mamba, Hyena, Caduceus** marks a phase transition from attention-bound 500-token genomics to **megabase-scale, single-nucleotide, strand-aware** language modeling. Selection resolves LTI bottleneck that caused Hyena perplexity degradation beyond 262k [1]; implicit long convolution provides first demonstration of sub-quadratic global context enabling 500× context expansion and 160× speedup [2][5]; Caduceus's BiMamba and MambaDNA introduce RC-equivariant bidirectionality, outperforming 10× larger non-equivariant models on variant-effect prediction where upstream/downstream context and strand symmetry are biologically mandated [3][4].

Recent spotlight at ICLR 2025 LMRL [4] establishes state space models as scalable alternatives not merely matching 50M Transformers but **extrapolating 10–100× beyond training length** and processing 1M tokens on single GPU — pivotal for labs without cluster compute. The 2025 perspective that "*SSMs learn more generalizable representations better suited for modeling long and complex human genome*" [4] suggests next frontier: diploid-aware, population-scale, multi-omics conditioning within Mamba-2/Caduceus chassis.

For true genomic foundation models covering the entire 3.2B human genome, future work must integrate hierarchical pooling across SSM layers (RECURRENT × CHUNK → compress → propagate), improved interpretability via $\Delta_t$ gating heatmaps correlating to conservation tracks, and open evaluation on fungal/biodiversity long-tail benchmarks initiated by BarcodeMamba [7].

If Transformers taught us *"attention is all you need"*, genomics teaches *"selection + equivariance + length is all you need"* — a design principle embodied by selective SSMs reaching to one million nucleotides and beyond.
