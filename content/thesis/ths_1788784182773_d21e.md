# Single-Cell RNA Sequencing Analysis: Trajectory Inference, RNA Velocity, and Multimodal Integration ## Abstract Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) measures genome-wide expression in thousands of individual cells, but the measurement is *destructive*: each cell yields a single static snapshot, and its temporal past and future are lost. This thesis develops the three computational pillars that recover dynamics from such snapshots. **Trajectory inference** reconstructs branching differentiation topologies and assigns each cell a *pseudotemporal* ordering along lineages, from the principal-curve formulations of early Monocle to graph-abstraction methods such as PAGA and Slingshot. **RNA velocity** infers per-cell rates of expression change from the ratio of nascent (unspliced) to mature (spliced) transcripts via a system of first-order ordinary differential equations, progressing from the steady-state model of La Manno et al. to the likelihood-based dynamical model of scVelo. **Multimodal integration** jointly models paired transcriptomic, proteomic, and chromatin measurements with deep generative frameworks such as totalVI and MultiVI. We present the mathematics, estimation algorithms, complexity analyses, benchmark evidence, and failure modes of each pillar, and show how their synthesis — exemplified by CellRank's velocity-informed Markov chains — yields predictive cell-fate maps with quantified uncertainty. ## 1. Introduction The central methodological paradox of single-cell transcriptomics is that its richest dynamical inferences are drawn from *static, destructive measurements*. Droplet-based protocols such as 10x Chromium encapsulate single cells in oil droplets with barcoded beads, lyse them, and sequence the released mRNA; the cell is annihilated in the act of being measured [1]. Yet biologists seek to answer intrinsically temporal questions: which genes turn on and off as a hematopoietic stem cell commits to the erythroid lineage? What is the probability that a tumor cell in a pre-malignant state transitions to full malignancy? How fast is a developing neuron differentiating? Three complementary computational responses have matured into distinct sub-disciplines, treated here as a unified analytical pipeline: 1. **Trajectory inference (TI)** organizes cells along continuous paths that model differentiation. Given an $n \times p$ matrix of $n$ cells by $p$ genes, TI fits a *trajectory graph* — a set of vertices (cellular states) and edges (allowed transitions) — and assigns each cell a pseudotime coordinate along its lineage. More than 70 TI methods have been published, benchmarked systematically by Saelens et al. on 110 real and 229 synthetic datasets [2]. 2. **RNA velocity** recovers *directed* dynamics without time-course data by exploiting intronic reads. Nascent unspliced transcripts $u$ and mature spliced transcripts $s$ for each gene in each cell obey kinetic equations whose solution predicts the future expression state of the cell — an intrinsic "compass" pointing toward each cell's fate [3, 4]. 3. **Multimodal integration** combines scRNA-seq with parallel measurements — surface proteins (CITE-seq), chromatin accessibility (multiome ATAC), spatial barcodes — into joint latent representations, denoising each modality and enabling cross-modality imputation via variational autoencoders [5]. The thesis argues that these three pillars are interdependent: TI provides the topology, velocity the direction field, and multimodal integration the high-fidelity state space in which both are computed. Their synthesis — exemplified by CellRank's velocity-informed Markov chains — converts descriptive snapshot geometry into predictive, probabilistic cell-fate models. ## 2. Background and Related Work ### 2.1 From counts to manifolds The scRNA-seq analytical pipeline converts raw reads into a cell-by-gene count matrix $X \in \mathbb{N}^{n \times p}$ through alignment, barcode demultiplexing, and UMI deduplication. Standard preprocessing applies size-factor normalization, $\log(1+x)$ transformation, highly variable gene selection, PCA, and nonlinear embedding (UMAP/t-SNE), followed by Leiden clustering. This pipeline treats cells as independent samples; all temporal structure is latent. ### 2.2 Trajectory inference: a brief genealogy Early TI methods assumed simple topologies. **Monocle** (Trapnell et al., 2014) fit minimum spanning trees in a reduced space and ordered cells along the longest path [2]. **Slingshot** (Street et al., 2018) generalizes principal curves to branching lineages: it clusters cells, fits a minimum spanning tree on centroids, and smooths each lineage with a principal curve [6]. **Diffusion maps** (Haghverdi et al., 2016) frame cells as samples from a diffusion process, with diffusion pseudotime measuring random-walk distance from a root cell. **PAGA** (Wolf et al., 2019) tests inter-cluster connectivity against a random-edge null model, yielding a coarse topology graph that preserves both continuous and disconnected structure. **Monocle3** (Cao et al., 2019) scaled TI to millions of cells with a principal-graph algorithm (SimplePPT) [7]. Saelens et al.'s dynverse benchmark [2] evaluated 45 TI methods and concluded that method choice must be driven by the *expected trajectory topology*: methods excel within their designed topology class (linear, bifurcating, tree, graph) and degrade outside it. Stability under subsampling and scalability beyond $10^5$ cells sharply separate production-grade methods (Slingshot, Monocle3, PAGA) from the rest. ### 2.3 RNA velocity: from kinetics to vectors La Manno et al. (2018) observed that unspliced pre-mRNA $u$ and spliced mRNA $s$ follow the transcription–splicing–degradation cascade [3]: $$\frac{du}{dt} = \alpha(t) - \beta u, \qquad \frac{ds}{dt} = \beta u - \gamma s,$$ where $\alpha$ is the transcription rate, $\beta$ the splicing rate, and $\gamma$ the degradation rate. The **velocity** of gene $g$ in cell $c$ is $v_{gc} = \beta_g u_{gc} - \gamma_g s_{gc}$: positive velocity means upregulation, negative means downregulation. The original *velocyto* implementation assumed a universal splicing rate $\beta$ and estimated $\gamma_g$ per gene from the steady-state slope of the $s$–$u$ phase portrait ($ds/dt = 0$ implies $u = (\gamma/\beta) s$). Bergen et al. (2020) generalized this in **scVelo** with two estimation modes: a *stochastic* mode solving the full second-moment equations, and a *dynamical* mode that fits latent time $t_c$ per cell via expectation–maximization, allowing transcription rates to switch between induction and repression phases [4]. Subsequent extensions include UniTVelo (temporally unified latent time), MultiVelo and chromatin velocity (multi-omic kinetics), and SDE-based stochastic formulations [8, 9]. ### 2.4 Multimodal integration Paired measurements — CITE-seq (RNA + surface protein), 10x Multiome (RNA + ATAC) — motivated joint probabilistic models. **totalVI** (Gayoso et al., 2021) is a variational autoencoder modeling RNA counts with a negative-binomial likelihood and protein counts with a mixture separating foreground signal from ambient background, conditioned on a shared latent variable [5]. **MultiVI** (Ashuach et al., 2022) extends the framework to RNA+ATAC with modality-specific decoders and handles missing modalities by averaging modality-specific latent codes. --- ## 3. Methodology We formalize the three pillars in a common notation. Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ be the normalized expression matrix, $U, S \in \mathbb{N}^{n \times p}$ the unspliced and spliced count matrices, and $Y^{(m)}$ the count matrix of modality $m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ for multimodal data. ### 3.1 Trajectory inference as graph fitting A trajectory model is a tuple $\mathcal{T} = (G, \pi)$ where $G = (V, E)$ is a graph whose vertices are cellular states, and $\pi$ assigns each cell a position along an edge (lineage + pseudotime). TI algorithms optimize: $$\hat{\mathcal{T}} = \arg\min_{\mathcal{T}} \sum_{c=1}^{n} d\big(x_c, \mathcal{T}\big)^2 + \lambda \cdot \text{complexity}(\mathcal{T}),$$ where $d(x_c, \mathcal{T})$ is the projection distance of cell $c$ onto the trajectory and $\lambda$ penalizes topological complexity (number of branches, total edge length). Principal-curve methods instantiate this directly; graph-abstraction methods (PAGA) discretize it via cluster connectivity statistics; diffusion methods replace projection distance with random-walk distances on a $k$-nearest-neighbor graph. ### 3.2 RNA velocity estimation For each gene $g$, the steady-state model regresses $u_{gc}$ on $s_{gc}$ over cells in the extreme quantiles of expression, estimating the degradation-to-splicing ratio $\hat{\gamma}_g/\beta$ and computing residuals as velocities: ```python import numpy as np def steady_state_velocity(U, S, quantile=0.95): """u, s: (n_cells,) arrays for one gene. Returns velocity vector.""" # Fit steady-state line on extreme-quantile cells s_max = np.quantile(S, quantile) mask = S >= s_max gamma_over_beta = np.linalg.lstsq( S[mask, None], U[mask], rcond=None)[0][0] # Velocity = deviation from steady state: ds/dt = beta*u - gamma*s v = U - gamma_over_beta * S return v # positive -> upregulation, negative -> downregulation ``` The scVelo *dynamical* model instead maximizes the likelihood of $(u_{gc}, s_{gc})$ under the analytical solution of the ODE system with piecewise-constant transcription rate $\alpha_g(t) \in \{\alpha_{\text{off}}, \alpha_{\text{on}}\}$, jointly inferring per-cell latent time $t_c$ and kinetic parameters $\theta_g = (\alpha_g, \beta_g, \gamma_g)$ by EM [4]. Cell-level velocities $V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ are converted to a cell–cell **transition matrix** $P$: for cell $i$ with velocity $v_i$, the transition probability to neighbor $j$ is proportional to the cosine similarity between $v_i$ and the displacement $\delta_{ij} = x_j - x_i$: $$P_{ij} \propto \exp\left(\frac{\cos(v_i, \delta_{ij})}{\sigma}\right), \quad j \in \mathcal{N}(i),$$ restricted to the $k$-nearest-neighbor graph. This Markov chain is the substrate for fate prediction (CellRank) and velocity-graph visualization. ### 3.3 Multimodal generative modeling For cell $n$ with batch $s_n$, totalVI posits: $$z_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \quad \ell_n \sim \text{LogNormal}(\ell_\mu(s_n), \ell_\sigma(s_n)),$$ $$x_{ng} \sim \text{NB}\big(\ell_n \cdot f_\rho(z_n, s_n)_g,\, \theta_g\big),$$ $$y_{nt} \sim \pi_{nt}\,\text{NB}(\cdot \mid \text{background}) + (1-\pi_{nt})\,\text{NB}(\cdot \mid \text{foreground}),$$ where $f_\rho$ is a neural decoder and protein observations $y_{nt}$ mix foreground (true surface expression) and background (ambient antibody) components with mixing proportion $\pi_{nt}$ decoded from $z_n$ [5]. Inference uses amortized variational inference with encoder $q_\phi(z_n \mid x_n, y_n, s_n)$, optimized by SGD on the ELBO, scaling to millions of cells on GPUs. --- ## 4. Deep Dive ### 4.1 Splicing kinetics: steady-state versus dynamical velocity models > **Theorem (Velocity identifiability).** *In the steady-state model with universal splicing rate $\beta$, the velocity $v_{gc} = \beta u_{gc} - \gamma_g s_{gc}$ is identifiable up to a positive per-gene scale factor, provided cells are observed at both transcriptional steady states (fully induced and fully repressed) for gene $g$.* > **Proof.** At steady state, $du/dt = ds/dt = 0$ gives $u = \alpha/\beta$ and $s = \alpha/\gamma$, hence $u/s = \gamma/\beta$ is constant across cells at equilibrium. Fitting the upper-quantile regression of $u$ on $s$ recovers $\gamma_g/\beta$ up to the unobserved common $\beta$. Since $v_{gc} = \beta(u_{gc} - (\gamma_g/\beta) s_{gc})$, velocities are determined up to the positive factor $\beta$. ∎ The theorem exposes the model's fragility: genes whose induction or repression phases are too brief to reach equilibrium — **multiple rate kinetics (MURK) genes** — violate the premise and yield distorted or even sign-reversed velocities [9]. The scVelo dynamical model relaxes the equilibrium assumption by fitting the full transient solution: $$s_g(t) = s_0 e^{-\gamma_g t} + \frac{\beta_g}{\gamma_g - \beta_g}\left(u_0 e^{-\beta_g t} - u_0 e^{-\gamma_g t}\right) + \frac{\alpha_g}{\gamma_g}\left(1 - e^{-\gamma_g t}\right),$$ with EM alternating between assigning latent times $t_c$ and updating $\theta_g$. This recovers correct directionality in transient-dominated systems (e.g., rapid erythroid commitment) but at the cost of a non-convex optimization sensitive to initialization and preprocessing (moments computation over $k$-NN neighborhoods). A critical subtlety is that *velocity is estimated in gene space but interpreted in embedding space*. UMAP/t-SNE embeddings distort distances nonlinearly, so projected arrows can mislead about lengths and even directions; velocity-aware embeddings (VeloViz) exist, but projection onto precomputed embeddings remains the field standard — a major source of overinterpretation [9]. ### 4.2 Trajectory inference algorithms: from principal curves to graph abstraction TI methods differ along three axes: **topology class** (linear, bifurcation, tree, graph, disconnected), **rootedness** (does the method orient the trajectory?), and **scalability**. The table summarizes the principal families: | Method | Topology | Rooted? | Scaling (cells) | Key idea | |---|---|---|---|---| | Monocle / DDRTree | Tree | No (user root) | $\sim 10^4$ | Reverse graph embedding, MST longest path | | Slingshot | Branching lineages | No (user root) | $\sim 10^5$ | Cluster MST + principal curves | | Diffusion pseudotime | Manifold | Root cell required | $\sim 10^5$ | Random-walk distances on kNN graph | | PAGA | General graph | No | $\sim 10^6$ | Cluster connectivity vs. null model | | Monocle3 | Disjoint graphs | No (user root) | $\sim 10^6$+ | Principal graph (SimplePPT) | | Palantir | Branching | Start cell required | $\sim 10^5$ | Markov chain, branch probabilities | | CellRank | Any (velocity-guided) | Automatic via velocity | $\sim 10^6$ | Velocity transition matrix + macrostates | A decisive insight from the dynverse benchmark [2] is that *no method dominates across topologies*: Slingshot excels on bifurcating data, PAGA on complex graphs with disconnected components, Monocle3 at atlas scale. Moreover, TI is **unidentifiable with respect to direction**: without temporal labels or velocity information, reversing all pseudotimes yields an equally valid fit. Root selection — typically by marker-gene knowledge — is therefore not a minor parameter but the step that converts geometry into biology. PAGA's statistical formulation deserves emphasis. For clusters $i, j$ with $e_{ij}$ observed inter-cluster edges in the kNN graph, connectivity is tested against the configuration-model expectation: $$\text{connectivity}(i,j) = \frac{e_{ij}}{\hat{e}_{ij}}, \quad \hat{e}_{ij} = \frac{(\sum_k e_{ik})(\sum_k e_{jk})}{2\sum_{k<l} e_{kl}},$$ retaining edges that significantly exceed random expectation. This yields a topology tolerating disconnected trajectories — essential for datasets mixing unrelated tissues. ### 4.3 Multimodal integration with deep generative models The multimodal problem is harder than batch correction: modalities differ in noise physics (dropout-heavy RNA counts vs. ambient-contaminated protein counts vs. near-binary ATAC peaks), dimensionality, and information content. totalVI's solution is to make the *likelihood* modality-aware while sharing the latent code [5]. Its protein background model is the key innovation: by explicitly modeling ambient antibody counts, totalVI computes a **foreground probability** $1 - \pi_{nt}$ per cell–protein pair, separating true surface expression from noise — something ad hoc CLR normalization cannot do. MultiVI generalizes to RNA+ATAC by assigning each modality its own encoder and decoder, then averaging modality-specific latent samples: $$z_n = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_n|} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}_n} z_n^{(m)}, \quad z_n^{(m)} \sim q_{\phi_m}(z \mid y_n^{(m)}),$$ where $\mathcal{M}_n$ is the set of observed modalities for cell $n$. This averaging permits **missing-modality imputation**: a cell measured only in RNA can be mapped to the joint space and its ATAC profile decoded. Training uses a modality-alignment penalty so that $z_n^{(m)}$ for the same cell coincide across $m$. Joint modeling improves cell-type resolution where RNA alone is ambiguous — e.g., distinguishing exhausted from effector T cells via PD-1/TIM-3 protein levels with weak RNA correlates — and enables *in silico* gating on denoised protein expression with calibrated uncertainty [5]. ### 4.4 Synthesis: velocity-guided fate mapping with CellRank **CellRank** (Lange et al., 2022) fuses TI and velocity: it builds the velocity transition matrix $P$, optionally combined with a pseudotime-directed kernel, then coarse-grains the chain into **macrostates** via Generalized Perron Cluster Cluster Analysis (GPCCA), identifying initial and terminal states from the chain's spectral structure. Fate probabilities follow from absorption probabilities: $$F = (I - Q)^{-1} R,$$ where $Q$ governs transient states and $R$ transitions into terminal macrostates. The result is a per-cell probability distribution over fates — a *predictive* quantity, not merely descriptive ordering — with driver genes ranked by correlation of expression with fate probability. > **Theorem (Fate-probability consistency).** *If the velocity field $v(x)$ is the gradient of a potential with isolated attractors and the kNN graph samples the manifold densely, then as $n \to \infty$ and $k \to \infty$ with $k/n \to 0$, the CellRank absorption probabilities converge to the true basin-of-attraction membership of the underlying dynamical system.* > **Proof sketch.** Under dense sampling, the cosine-kernel transition matrix converges to the generator of the diffusion process following $v(x)$. Absorption probabilities of the discretized chain then converge to hitting probabilities of the continuous process, which for a gradient system equal basin membership. ∎ In practice the preconditions are approximate: velocity fields contain rotational components, rare states are sparsely sampled, and the kernel bandwidth $\sigma$ is heuristic. CellRank 2 addresses this with kernel ensembles, but the theorem clarifies what the method aspires to estimate. --- ## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs ### 5.1 Benchmark evidence The dynverse comparison [2] remains the definitive TI benchmark: 45 methods, 110 real and 229 synthetic datasets, scored on accuracy (correlation with ground-truth pseudotime, topology recovery), scalability, stability, and usability. Headline findings: 1. **Topology-matched methods win.** Slingshot and Monocle lead on bifurcating and tree topologies; generic methods trail by 10–25% in trajectory correlation. 2. **Scale separates the field.** Beyond $10^5$ cells most methods fail; Monocle3, PAGA, and Slingshot remain tractable, with Monocle3 reaching $10^6$-cell atlases. 3. **Ensembles help.** Combining two complementary topologies beats either alone. 4. **Stability is not guaranteed.** High-accuracy methods can show poor subsampling stability, so reported branchpoints may be sampling artifacts. For RNA velocity, ground truth requires time-resolved or lineage-traced data. Studies using metabolic labeling (scSLAM-seq) and joint lineage tracing report that steady-state velocyto correctly orients *slow, equilibrium-dominated* transitions (e.g., neurogenesis, the original La Manno et al. demonstration [3]) but fails on rapid induction cascades, where scVelo's dynamical mode recovers correct orientation for most tested genes [4, 8]. UniTVelo showed that unifying latent time across genes improves velocity-field consistency on hematopoiesis and pancreatic endocrinogenesis data [8]. ### 5.2 A worked example: pancreatic endocrinogenesis Consider the canonical Bastidas-Ponce et al. mouse pancreas dataset: progenitors differentiate through transient Ngn3-high endocrine progenitors into alpha, beta, delta, and epsilon cells. A standard analysis proceeds as: ```python import scanpy as sc, scvelo as scv adata = sc.read_h5ad("pancreas.h5ad") scv.pp.filter_and_normalize(adata) scv.pp.moments(adata, n_neighbors=30, n_pcs=30) scv.tl.recover_dynamics(adata) # dynamical model: EM per gene scv.tl.velocity(adata, mode="dynamical") scv.tl.velocity_graph(adata) # transition matrix P scv.pl.velocity_embedding_stream(adata, basis="umap") # Top dynamical genes drive the trajectory scv.tl.rank_dynamical_genes(adata) ``` The dynamical model identifies *Ngn3*, *Fev*, and lineage hormones (*Ins1*, *Gcg*) among top dynamical genes; the velocity stream plot orients the trajectory from multipotent progenitors through the Ngn3 bottleneck into terminal fates — an orientation pure pseudotime cannot determine without manual rooting. Feeding $P$ into CellRank recovers terminal macrostates matching the four endocrine fates, with fate probabilities correlating with lineage-tracing barcodes where available. ### 5.3 Complexity analysis | Step | Time | Memory | Notes | |---|---|---|---| | kNN graph ($n$ cells, $d$ PCs) | $O(n d \log n)$ | $O(nk)$ | HNSW/annoy approximate | | Moments (velocity smoothing) | $O(nk p_v)$ | $O(n p_v)$ | $p_v$ = velocity genes ($\sim$2k) | | Steady-state velocity | $O(n p_v)$ | $O(n p_v)$ | Per-gene least squares | | scVelo dynamical EM | $O(I \cdot n p_v)$ | $O(n p_v)$ | $I$ = EM iterations ($\sim$50–200) | | PAGA connectivity | $O(nk + C^2)$ | $O(nk)$ | $C$ = clusters | | totalVI training | $O(E \cdot n \cdot w)$ | $O(B \cdot w)$ | $E$ epochs, $w$ params, $B$ batch | --- ## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity 1. **Pseudotime is not time.** TI orders cells along a learned manifold; cell spacing reflects sampling density and transcriptional rate of change, not elapsed time. Kinetic interpretation of pseudotime derivatives is invalid without independent temporal calibration [2]. 2. **Velocity's equilibrium assumption is frequently violated.** MURK genes — with transient induction too brief for steady state — produce biased or sign-flipped velocities under velocyto; scVelo's dynamical mode mitigates but does not eliminate the problem, and its EM is sensitive to initialization and to moments-smoothing preprocessing [9]. 3. **Low unspliced counts.** Intronic reads are a small fraction of scRNA-seq libraries (often <20%); velocity estimates for lowly expressed genes are noise-dominated. Filtering to top dynamical genes is effectively mandatory, discarding most of the transcriptome. 4. **Embedding projection artifacts.** Velocity arrows are visualized by projecting high-dimensional vectors onto UMAP/t-SNE embeddings that distort distances nonlinearly. Arrows can appear coherent in embedding space while being inconsistent in gene space, and vice versa [9]. 5. **Multimodal missingness and batch confounding.** totalVI/MultiVI assume batch effects are captured by observed covariates; unobserved confounders (e.g., donor-specific biology correlated with batch) are absorbed into the latent space and can masquerade as biological signal. Missing-modality imputation inherits the biases of the reference modalities. 6. **Direction unidentifiability in TI.** Without velocity, lineage tracing, or time-course labels, trajectory direction is a user choice (root selection), not an inference. Published "discoveries" of developmental direction from pseudotime alone are circular when the root was chosen using the same marker genes used to validate the result. 7. **Scalability–fidelity tradeoffs.** Atlas-scale TI (Monocle3) and GPU-trained VAEs require aggressive approximations (principal graphs, amortized inference) whose approximation error is rarely quantified per dataset. --- ## 7. Conclusion Trajectory inference, RNA velocity, and multimodal integration form a complete computational stack for extracting dynamics from destructive single-cell snapshots: TI reconstructs the *topology* of cell-state transitions, velocity supplies the *direction field* from splicing kinetics, and multimodal generative models provide the *high-fidelity state space* in which both are estimated. The field's frontier lies in making these inferences *predictive and calibrated*: CellRank-style fate probabilities with uncertainty, optimal-transport couplings across real time points, and joint modeling of lineage barcodes with molecular state. The outstanding theoretical gap is a unified identifiability theory — precise conditions under which snapshot data determine the underlying dynamical system. Until that theory exists, every velocity arrow and pseudotime axis should be reported with the humility its assumptions demand: powerful hypotheses about cellular dynamics, not measurements of them. --- ## References [1] G.
