# Scalable Transparent Arguments via STARKs: FRI Low-Degree Testing, DEEP-ALI Arithmetization, and the Arithmetization Gap with SNARKs

## Abstract

Scalable Transparent ARguments of Knowledge (STARKs) achieve proving systems whose security rests solely on symmetric cryptography, eliminating the trusted setup that underpins pairing-based SNARKs while preserving post-quantum plausibility. This thesis dissects the STARK pipeline end to end: the Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR) that arithmetizes execution traces, the Fast Reed–Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity (FRI) that replaces algebraic-group low-degree tests with hash-committed folding rounds, and the DEEP composition technique that lifts the soundness of the Algebraic Linking IOP (ALI) from a small constant below 1/8 to a value arbitrarily close to 1 [1][2]. We formalize FRI's split-and-fold commit phase, quantify its soundness improvements over prior proximity tests, and contrast the AIR-based arithmetization gap with the circuit- and R1CS-centric encodings of classical SNARKs. Empirical estimates of prover work, proof sizes, and verifier complexity across representative trace lengths are derived and compared with Groth16-class and Plonk-class systems, locating precisely where STARKs pay their overhead and where transparency and plausible quantum resistance justify the cost.

## 1 Introduction

The search for **succinct** arguments of computational integrity has produced two dominant families: *SNARKs*, built on pairing-friendly curves with structured reference strings, and *STARKs*, built on interactive oracle proofs instantiated with hash-based commitments [1][2]. The difference is not merely engineering — it is a divergence in the mathematics of arithmetization: what it means to encode "this computation was executed correctly" as an algebraic claim, and what machinery certifies that claim.

Classical SNARKs arithmetize computation into rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS) or arithmetic circuits over an elliptic-curve scalar field. The prover commits to witness polynomials with homomorphic commitments (KZG, Pedersen) and the verifier checks evaluations through pairings. The price is a **trusted setup**: a ceremony that must generate, use, and verifiably destroy toxic waste. STARKs take a fundamentally different route. The computation is encoded as an *execution trace* — a two-dimensional table of field elements — whose correctness is expressed as an *Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR)*: a set of polynomial constraints on neighboring rows of the trace [3][4]. The trace columns are interpolated as polynomials over a large multiplicative subgroup, and correctness reduces to a single question:

> **The STARK reduction.** *Is the composition polynomial derived from the AIR constraints a low-degree polynomial?*

Answering that question succinctly is the job of the **FRI protocol** (Fast Reed–Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity) [1], and making the reduction sound with small overhead is the job of **DEEP-ALI** [2].

This thesis makes three contributions:

1. A self-contained formalization of the **FRI commit phase** — split-and-fold Merkle-committed rounds with random linear challenges — including its query phase and the folding consistency relation the verifier checks.
2. An exposition of **DEEP composition** and the **DEEP-ALI** protocol, which upgrades the Algebraic Linking IOP from a soundness constant below 1/8 to one arbitrarily close to 1 by sampling constraint evaluations *outside* the evaluation domain [2].
3. A precise account of the **arithmetization gap** between STARKs and SNARKs: what AIR can express naturally, what R1CS expresses naturally, and how each choice propagates into prover cost, proof size, and verifier time.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Proof systems and the setup spectrum

A zk-SNARK traditionally combines an *information-theoretic* core (an interactive proof) with a *cryptographic* commitment layer. In pairing-based SNARKs, that layer is a **trusted-setup** object: a structured reference string produced by a ceremony. **IOP-based** systems — STARKs are the flagship — instead commit with a **Merkle tree over a Reed–Solomon codeword**, using only a collision-resistant hash [1][5]. The consequence is *transparency*: no secret randomness in the parameters, and security reducing to hash-function security in the random-oracle model, widely regarded as plausibly post-quantum.

### 2.2 Reed–Solomon codes and the low-degree testing problem

Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a finite field and $S \subset \mathbb{F}$ a multiplicative subgroup of order $n$. The **Reed–Solomon code** $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S, \rho]$ is the set of functions $f: S \to \mathbb{F}$ that are evaluations of polynomials of degree $< \rho n$ ($\rho$ the *rate*). The **low-degree testing problem** asks: given oracle access to $f: S \to \mathbb{F}$, decide whether $f \in \mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S, \rho]$ or is $\delta$-far from every codeword. A *succinct* solution — prover quasilinear or better, verifier logarithmic — was the central bottleneck for transparent proof systems before 2018 [1][5].

### 2.3 Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR)

Where SNARKs arithmetize computation as circuits or R1CS, STARKs arithmetize computation as an **execution trace**. Consider a computation with $w$ state registers executed over $T$ steps. The trace is a $T \times w$ table; column $j$ records the evolution of register $j$. Correctness decomposes into:

- **Transition constraints**: for each row $i$, a low-degree polynomial $P(X, Y_1, \dots, Y_w)$ in the current row's variables $X$ and the next row's variables $Y$ must vanish: $P(x_i, x_{i+1}) = 0$.
- **Boundary constraints**: the first and last rows must satisfy specified values.

If $T$ divides into a coset of $\mathbb{F}^\times$, each trace column interpolates to a polynomial of degree $< T$. Let $Z(x)$ be the vanishing polynomial of the trace domain (for transition constraints, of all but the last point). Then each constraint yields a **quotient polynomial** $Q_k(x) = P_k(x)/Z(x)$, which is a genuine polynomial *if and only if* the constraint holds everywhere it must [3]. A random linear combination $\alpha$-weighted across all quotients forms the **composition polynomial**

$$C(x) = \sum_k \alpha_k \, Q_k(x),$$

whose degree is bounded by a public $D_{\max}$ precisely when *every* AIR constraint is satisfied. The entire STARK thus reduces to: **prove that $C(x)$ has degree at most $D_{\max}$**. That is a pure low-degree testing problem — the input FRI was designed to solve [1].

### 2.4 The Cairo virtual machine

The canonical real-world target of STARK arithmetization is the **Cairo CPU**: a Turing-complete von Neumann machine whose state (program counter, allocation pointer, frame pointer) and memory live in a large finite field, with a small instruction set [6]. The Cairo whitepaper formalizes the CPU's AIR — 51 constraint polynomials for instruction decoding, operand resolution, and the next-state relation — and that AIR's correctness has been *machine-checked in Lean* [7]. Cairo demonstrates the STARK thesis at scale: arithmetize one fixed CPU once, then prove arbitrary programs by proving their execution traces on it.

## 3 Methodology

Our approach is threefold: (i) formal exposition of the FRI commit/query phases with the folding relation made explicit; (ii) an account of DEEP composition and its application to ALI, showing exactly where out-of-domain sampling removes the soundness bottleneck; (iii) a comparative analysis of the arithmetization gap, grounded in published complexity bounds and prover measurements from the literature [1][2][5][8].

### 3.1 The FRI protocol, formally

FRI is an Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity for $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S, \rho]$. It proceeds in two phases [1].

**Commit phase (split-and-fold).** The prover claims $f^{(0)}: S^{(0)} \to \mathbb{F}$ is close to a polynomial of degree $< \rho|S^{(0)}|$. For round $i = 0, 1, \dots, r-1$:

1. The verifier samples a random challenge $\alpha^{(i)} \in \mathbb{F}$.
2. Write $f^{(i)}(x) = g^{(i)}_0(x^2) + x \cdot g^{(i)}_1(x^2)$ as the even/odd decomposition. The prover *folds*:
   $$f^{(i+1)}(y) = g^{(i)}_0(y) + \alpha^{(i)} \cdot g^{(i)}_1(y), \qquad y \in S^{(i+1)} = \{x^2 : x \in S^{(i)}\},$$
   halving both the domain and (claimed) degree.
3. The prover commits to each $f^{(i)}$ as a Merkle tree over its evaluations.

After $r$ rounds, $f^{(r)}$ is claimed to have tiny constant degree, and the prover sends it directly. The folding preserves low-degreeness *if and only if* the random challenge does not conspire with the prover's lies — which happens with probability bounded by the proximity gap analysis [1][9].

**Query phase.** The verifier samples $q$ random positions $s_0 \in S^{(0)}$ and checks *folding consistency* at each round: given Merkle openings of $f^{(i)}(s_i)$ and $f^{(i)}(-s_i)$, the verifier interpolates the unique degree-$<2$ polynomial through them, folds it with $\alpha^{(i)}$, and checks agreement with the opened value of $f^{(i+1)}(s_i^2)$ [8]. A single inconsistent fold reveals, with constant probability per round, that the original function was far from the code.

```python
# FRI fold: the core consistency relation checked by the verifier
def fold_even_odd(f_s, f_neg_s, alpha, s):
    # interpolate degree-<2 polynomial through (s, f_s), (-s, f_neg_s)
    g0 = (f_s + f_neg_s) / 2          # even part at s^2
    g1 = (f_s - f_neg_s) / (2 * s)    # odd part at s^2
    return g0 + alpha * g1            # must equal f_next(s^2)
```

> **Theorem (FRI soundness, round-by-round [9]).** *For the FRI IOPP over $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S, \rho]$ with proximity parameter $\delta$ below the unique-decoding radius, the round-by-round soundness error is bounded by*
> $$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rbr}} \le \max\!\left\{1 - \delta,\; \frac{2}{\rho |\mathbb{F}|} + \frac{\rho}{2}\right\}^r \cdot (\text{query terms}),$$
> *improving on the prior constant-factor distance loss per round of bivariate-testing-based proximity proofs.*

The prover's arithmetic complexity is *strictly linear* — $O(|S|)$ field operations — while the verifier's arithmetic complexity is *strictly logarithmic* — $O(\log |S|)$ — a first for proximity testing of Reed–Solomon codes [1].

### 3.2 Soundness accounting: commit, query, and the Fiat–Shamir transform

Each FRI query catches a $\delta$-far function with probability at least $\delta$, so $q$ queries drive soundness error to $\approx (1-\delta)^q$. With $\delta$ near $1-\sqrt{\rho}$ and $\rho = 1/8$–$1/16$, $q \approx 60$–$100$ queries deliver 80–100 bits of conjectured security. Fiat–Shamir compiles the protocol to a non-interactive argument; the hash function is the *only* cryptographic assumption — hence transparency and post-quantum plausibility [1][5].

### 3.3 DEEP composition: sampling outside the box

The original ALI protocol (the "linking" step that combines many AIR constraints into the composition polynomial) suffered a structural soundness bottleneck: its soundness constant was bounded by a small value below $1/8$, because the analysis required the constraint quotients to be evaluated *inside* the trace domain, where a cheating prover could hide inconsistencies in low-weight error patterns [2].

The **DEEP technique** (Domain Extension for Eliminating Pretenders) removes this bottleneck by evaluating the composed polynomial at a random point $z \in \mathbb{F} \setminus S$ — *outside* the evaluation domain. The quotient $Q_z(x) = (C(x) - C(z))/(x - z)$ is a genuine low-degree polynomial only when the prover's claimed $C(z)$ is correct, so one out-of-domain opening pins $C$ down with probability $1 - d/|\mathbb{F}|$ [2]. This yields **DEEP-ALI** (soundness constant arbitrarily close to 1, not capped below $1/8$) and **DEEP-FRI**, with linear prover and logarithmic verifier complexity retained [2].

### 3.4 Comparison methodology: the arithmetization gap

To compare STARKs with SNARKs we distinguish two gaps: (1) **expressiveness** — R1CS natively encodes bilinear relations; AIR natively encodes *uniform, local* transition relations over time, ideal for CPU traces but awkward for irregular logic without a VM [6][7]; (2) **efficiency** — AIR needs trace width $w$ (Cairo uses 25+ columns) with degree bounds scaling in $w \cdot T$, and the rate blowup $\rho^{-1}$ (4–16×) multiplies the trace before FRI runs [1][6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The FRI commit phase as a Merkle-folded pyramid

Picture the commit phase as a pyramid: the base is the full codeword of size $n = |S^{(0)}|$; each layer above is half the size, folded with a fresh challenge $\alpha^{(i)}$ and committed as its own Merkle tree. The query phase *threads* $q$ random vertical paths through the pyramid, opening sibling evaluations $f^{(i)}(s)$, $f^{(i)}(-s)$ at each layer and demanding fold-equality with the parent opening at $s^2$ [1][8]. This split-and-fold structure inherits the FFT's divide-and-conquer shape — which is why the prover is linear rather than quasilinear: prior proximity tests paid $O(n \log n)$ via bivariate testing with a constant *multiplicative* distance loss per round [1].

> **Theorem (distance preservation in FRI [1]).** *If $f^{(i)}$ is $\delta$-far from $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S^{(i)}, \rho]$, then with high probability over the random challenge $\alpha^{(i)}$, the folded function $f^{(i+1)}$ remains $\delta'$-far from $\mathrm{RS}[\mathbb{F}, S^{(i+1)}, \rho]$ with $\delta' \approx \delta$ — negligible distance loss per round — in contrast to prior PCPP constructions where $\delta' \le \delta/2$.*

### 4.2 From AIR constraints to the composition polynomial

The arithmetization pipeline is worth stating as an algorithm, because it is where the SNARK/STARK divergence begins:

1. **Trace generation.** Execute the program; record the $T \times w$ trace.
2. **Low-degree extension.** Interpolate each column over a subgroup $H$ of order $T$; extend evaluations to a domain $D$ of size $T/\rho$.
3. **Constraint quotients.** For each constraint $P_k$ of degree $d_k$, form $Q_k(x) = P_k(x)/Z_H(x)$; valid execution $\iff$ every $Q_k$ is a genuine polynomial.
4. **Random combination.** $C(x) = \sum_k \alpha_k Q_k(x)$ for random $\alpha_k$.
5. **Degree check.** Run FRI on $C$ over $D$ with bound $D_{\max} = \max_k(d_k T - T)$.

Step 5 is *exactly* the low-degree testing problem of §2.2. The "gap" in the thesis title refers to what happens between the programmer's intent and this pipeline: SNARKs cross the gap through circuits/R1CS and polynomial commitments; STARKs cross it through traces/AIR and FRI. Neither is free.

### 4.3 DEEP-ALI quotienting and out-of-domain sampling

In DEEP-ALI, after forming $C(x)$ the verifier samples $z \notin D$; the prover supplies $a = C(z)$ with Merkle openings of the trace columns at $z$. The verifier forms the *DEEP composition polynomial* $C'(x) = (C(x) - a)/(x - z)$, of degree $\deg(C)-1$ iff the prover was honest, and runs FRI on $C'$ [2]. Because $z$ lies outside $D$, no low-weight cheating pattern inside $D$ can match $C$ on $D$ and a false value at $z$ — Schwartz–Zippel bounds the collision probability by $\deg(C)/|\mathbb{F}|$. The ALI soundness constant moves from $< 1/8$ to $1 - O(d/|\mathbb{F}|)$ [2].

### 4.4 The arithmetization gap vs SNARKs, concretely

| Dimension | STARK (AIR + FRI) | SNARK (R1CS/Plonk + KZG) |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Transparent; hash only [1][5] | Trusted SRS / universal setup |
| Post-quantum plausibility | Yes (symmetric crypto) [5] | No (pairings / discrete log) |
| Native encoding | Uniform transition relations (CPU traces) [3][6] | Bilinear constraints; custom gates (Plonk) |
| Prover complexity | $O(T \log T)$ (dominated by NTTs); linear in FRI [1] | $O(n \log n)$ MSM + FFT |
| Proof size | 40–200 KB typical (FRI queries + Merkle paths) [8] | 0.2–1 KB (Groth16); ~1 KB (Plonk) |
| Verifier complexity | $O(\log^2 T)$ hashes [1] | $O(1)$ pairings (Groth16); $O(\log n)$ (Plonk) |
| Soundness basis | FRI proximity gap + DEEP; ~60–100 queries for 100-bit security [2][9] | Knowledge-of-exponent / AGM |
| Recursion friendliness | Good (no pairing cycles needed) | Requires pairing-friendly cycles |

The gap is about *where the succinctness lives*. SNARKs compress via the algebraic commitment — constant-size polynomial commitments. STARKs compress via the *IOP* — the verifier's logarithmic query complexity. STARKs pay in proof size (Merkle paths plus dozens of FRI queries vs three group elements — the ~100× gap [8]); SNARKs pay in the setup ceremony and quantum vulnerability.

### 4.5 Post-quantum transparent setup: what "transparent" buys

Because the STARK security argument reduces to the random-oracle model over a concrete hash, there is no number-theoretic assumption for Shor's algorithm to break [5]. With a 256-bit hash and ~100 bits of FRI/DEEP soundness, the system keeps a conservative post-quantum margin where pairing-based SNARKs keep none — which is why STARKs are the architecture of choice where long-lived integrity matters.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Complexity accounting

Let the trace have $T$ rows and $w$ columns with $n = T/\rho$ evaluation points. FRI proper is strictly linear in $n$ [1]; end-to-end, the $O(n \log n)$ NTTs of interpolation and LDE dominate wall-clock time. Proof size is dominated by $q$ query paths ($\log n$ hashes per layer plus trace openings). Representative figures:

| Trace length $T$ | Rate $\rho$ | Queries $q$ | Proof size (approx.) | Prover (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2^{16}$ | 1/8 | 60 | ~60 KB | < 1 s |
| $2^{20}$ | 1/8 | 80 | ~110 KB | ~2–5 s |
| $2^{24}$ | 1/16 | 100 | ~200 KB | ~20–60 s |

*(Order-of-magnitude figures synthesized from published STARK implementations and the survey literature [5][8]; exact values depend on hash choice, field, and constraint degrees.)*

### 5.2 Soundness proof sketch: why DEEP-ALI closes the gap

> **Theorem (DEEP-ALI soundness [2], informal).** *Let the AIR constraints have maximal degree $d$ and the field satisfy $|\mathbb{F}| \gg d$. If the prover's trace violates any AIR constraint, then DEEP-ALI rejects except with probability at most*
> $$\varepsilon \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{FRI}} + \frac{d + D_{\max}}{|\mathbb{F}|} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{FS}},$$
> *where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FRI}}$ is the FRI soundness error, the middle term is the Schwartz–Zippel cost of the out-of-domain sample $z$, and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is the Fiat–Shamir loss.*

*Proof sketch.* A violating trace yields $C$ either above the degree bound $D_{\max}$ (caught by FRI) or at/below it while failing constraints — impossible, since a degree-$\le D_{\max}$ function agreeing with all quotients on $D$ satisfies every constraint. The DEEP opening forces commitment to $C(z)$, $z \notin D$, *before* $z$ is revealed; a false opening makes $\deg(C')$ exceed the bound, which FRI detects [2]. ∎

### 5.3 The prover-cost ledger

Dominant prover costs: (1) interpolating $w$ trace columns — $w$ NTTs of size $T$; (2) the low-degree extension — $w$ NTTs of size $n = T/\rho$; (3) constraint evaluation over $D$; (4) the FRI commit phase — strictly linear [1]; (5) Merkle trees over all committed layers. In practice (2) and (5) dominate, which is why production STARKs invest in cheap hashes and NTT-friendly fields, and why 256-bit-field STARKs have largely given way to small-field variants with extension-field soundness boosting.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Proof size.** A 100 KB STARK proof is unusable where bandwidth is the constraint (e.g., per-transaction on-chain verification) — exactly where 200-byte Groth16 proofs dominate. Query batching and Merkle-path sharing mitigate but do not close the two-order-of-magnitude gap [8].
2. **Prover overhead at small scale.** The LDE blowup $\rho^{-1}$ and NTTs make STARK proving slower than Plonk/Groth16 for small computations; STARKs win asymptotically as $T$ grows, with a logarithmic verifier and no per-circuit setup [5].
3. **Arithmetization rigidity.** AIR demands *uniform* constraints. Non-uniform logic (memory access, range checks, bitwise ops) needs auxiliary columns, permutation arguments, or builtins — Cairo's builtins (Pedersen, ECDSA, range checks) exist precisely because raw AIR is inexpressive there [6].
4. **DEEP's field-size requirement.** Out-of-domain sampling needs $|\mathbb{F}| \gg d \cdot T$; small fields require extension-field techniques that complicate analysis and add prover cost [2].
5. **Conjectured proximity gaps.** The tightest FRI soundness analyses rely on list-decoding conjectures (approaching the Johnson bound); parameters are set conservatively [2][9].
6. **Fiat–Shamir in the quantum ROM.** Post-quantum claims assume Fiat–Shamir soundness against quantum adversaries — plausible but stronger than classical ROM soundness [5].

## 7 Conclusion

STARKs reframe succinct argumentation around one algebraic primitive — low-degree testing of Reed–Solomon codewords — executed with machinery needing nothing but a hash function. FRI's split-and-fold commit phase delivers the first *linear-prover, logarithmic-verifier* proximity test; DEEP composition lifts the algebraic linking step from a soundness constant below 1/8 to one arbitrarily close to 1; and AIR turns CPU execution traces into exactly the degree-bounded claim FRI certifies. The arithmetization gap with SNARKs is structural: SNARKs compress via algebraic commitments at the cost of trusted setup and quantum fragility; STARKs compress via interactive-oracle succinctness at the cost of proof size. Where transparency and long-term integrity outweigh bandwidth — recursive rollups, audited computation, post-quantum archives — the STARK/FRI/DEEP-ALI stack is the structurally correct choice.
