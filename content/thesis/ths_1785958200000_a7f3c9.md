# Post-Quantum Digital Signatures from Hash-Based and Lattice Trapdoors: SPHINCS+, CRYSTALS-Dilithium, and Falcon Under NIST PQC Standardization

## Abstract

We provide a unified cryptographic analysis of the three NIST post-quantum digital signature standards selected for stateless deployment: SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+), ML-DSA (CRYSTALS-Dilithium), and FN-DSA (Falcon). This thesis dissects their security reductions from minimal symmetric assumptions and module-lattice hardness (MLWE, MSIS, NTRU-SIS), formalizes hypertree and Fiat-Shamir-with-aborts constructions, and surveys trapdoor sampling via GPV and Fast Fourier LDL decomposition. We evaluate parameter instantiations for NIST levels 1,2,3,5, quantifying public-key, signature sizes, cycle counts, and side-channel posture, emphasizing trade-offs between conservative hash-based proofs and efficient lattice algebra. Proofs of strong unforgeability (SUF-CMA) in QROM are contextualized with recent collapsing-hash arguments and SelfTargetMSIS analysis, enabling implementers to select appropriate primitives for high-assurance firmware, TLS, and blockchain deployments under CNSA 2.0 timelines.

This work synthesizes designs finalized in FIPS 204 and FIPS 205 [1][2] and the draft FIPS 206 [8] for implementers migrating from ECDSA and RSA under NIST IR 8547 deprecation guidance [9].

## 1 Introduction

The imminent threat of **cryptographically relevant quantum computers (CRQC)** compels a transition from factoring and discrete-logarithm signatures to primitives resistant to *Shor's algorithm*. NIST's Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) standardization, initiated in 2016, concluded its third round in July 2022 selecting three signature families for standardization [9]: lattice-based *module-lattice* schemes and hash-based *stateless* schemes. National Security Memorandum 10 and NSA CNSA 2.0 mandate ML-DSA-87 for all classification levels by 2030, with LMS/XMSS and SLH-DSA approved for code-signing longevity.

*Digital signatures* must satisfy three properties: *correctness*, *strong existential unforgeability under chosen-message attack (SUF-CMA)*, and *non-repudiation*. Classical *Ed25519* achieves 64-byte signatures at 128-bit security, yet its security collapses entirely against a quantum adversary running Shor's algorithm in O(n^3) time. Post-quantum alternatives therefore trade larger bandwidth and state management for assumptions believed hard even for BQP machines.

This thesis addresses the central question: **how do SPHINCS+ (now SLH-DSA), CRYSTALS-Dilithium (now ML-DSA), and Falcon (now FN-DSA) achieve quantum resistance under disparate hardness foundations while meeting real-world performance constraints?** We argue that:

- **Hash-based security** offers the most conservative reduction to second-preimage resistance of SHA2/SHAKE, at cost of 7–50 KiB signatures and hypertree verification overheads.
- **Fiat-Shamir with Aborts** over module lattices achieves balanced sizes (1.3–2.6 KiB pk, 2.4–4.6 KiB sig) without Gaussian sampling, facilitating constant-time implementations [3][4].
- **GPV-style trapdoors** using NTRU lattices and Fourier-domain discrete Gaussian sampling (Falcon) yields minimal bandwidth (897 B pk, 690 B sig at NIST Level 1) but requires *floating-point arithmetic* and intricate LDL-tree precision analysis [7][10].

> Theorem 1 (Informal): Under the heuristic that SHA2-256 behaves as a random oracle with distinct-function multi-target second-preimage resistance (DM-SPR), and that Module-LWE and Module-SIS are hard for polynomial-time quantum adversaries at claimed module rank, the three NIST signature families achieve SUF-CMA in the quantum random oracle model (QROM).

Contributions of this monograph are:

1. A dense yet implementable description of WOTS+, FORS, and hypertree authentication;
2. A lattice-algebraic treatment of commitment-challenge-response rejection sampling and truncated Gaussian correctness;
3. A security reduction roadmap for SelfTargetMSIS collapsible hash proofs [5][6];
4. An empirical benchmark synthesis with deployment guidance for HSM, firmware, and blockchain contexts.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries

Let Z_q be the integer ring modulo q = 8380417 for Dilithium and q=12289 for Falcon. Let R_q = Z_q[X]/(X^256+1) for ML-DSA with NTT-friendly modulus satisfying q=1 mod 2n. For NTRU, consider R = Z[X]/(X^n+1) with n=512,1024. We denote centered modular reduction by [a]_q and infinity norm ||v||_inf.

> Definition 1 (Module-LWE): Distinguish (A, As+e) from uniform where A <- R_q^{k x l}, s <- S_chi^{l}, e <- S_chi^k. Decision-MLWE advantage must be negligible for all QPT adversaries.

> Definition 2 (Module-SIS): Given A <- R_q^{k x l}, find non-zero z in R_q^{l+k} with ||z||_inf <= beta and [A | I] z = 0.

### 2.2 Hash-Based One-Time Signatures

Lamport signatures [Lam79] and Winternitz optimization introduce *chaining*: private key elements sk_i hashed w-1 times to form public key pk_i = H^{w-1}(sk_i). Message split into base-w chunks determines iterations disclosed. **WOTS+** [1][2] introduces randomization via tweakable hash function F with bitmask XOR to defeat multi-target attacks while using only collision-resilient security, not collision resistance, improving tightness.

Historical stateful schemes *XMSS* and *LMS* (RFC 9708 family) maintain a state counter to avoid one-time key reuse. SPHINCS+ [2] eliminates state via *Forest of Random Subsets (FORS)*: a few-time signature where message subset selects k leaves from t=2^a trees, enabling 2^64 signatures per hypertree with negligible reuse probability due to pseudorandom path selection using *R*.

### 2.3 Lattice Trapdoors

Gentry-Peikert-Vaikuntanathan (GPV) [GPV08] frames signatures as: public trapdoor A basis of lattice Lambda_q^perp(A); signature for message hash c is short vector s with As = c. Babai's nearest-plane decoding using *Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization* and *discrete Gaussian sampling* D_{Lambda,sigma,c} yields distribution independent of secret basis. Falcon refines this to NTRU lattices where A = [1 | h], h = g f^{-1} mod q, with short secret pair (f,g,F,G) satisfying NTRU equation fG - gF = q and compact LDL tree preconditioning.

Dilithium circumvents Gaussians altogether via **Fiat-Shamir with Aborts** [Lyu12]: sample masking vector y <- [-gamma1,gamma1]^l uniformly, commit w = Ay, challenge c = H(mu || w), response z = y + c s1; reject if ||z||_inf or hints leak secret-dependent distribution. No inversion, no floating point, only uniform and centered binomial sampling—ideal for masking.

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis methodology integrates specification extraction, cryptanalytic reduction review, and performance metrology.

**Phase 1 – Specification Archeology.** We parsed *FIPS 204* [1], *FIPS 205* [2], *FIPS 206 draft* [8], original Dilithium spec [3], Falcon spec v1.2, and IETF LAMPS CMS drafts for SLH-DSA [11]. ASN.1 OIDs for `id-alg-ml-dsa-44/65/87`, `id-alg-slh-dsa-128s/128f/192s/192f/256s/256f`, `id-alg-fn-dsa-512/1024` were cross-referenced to ensure wire-format fidelity. Haskell prototypes for algebraic verification used `cryptonite`.

**Phase 2 – Reduction Mapping.** We constructed a dependency graph: MLWE -> IND-CPA KEM -> MSIS self-target -> collapsing hash [5][6] -> SUF-CMA. For hash-based, we modeled security as PRF indistinguishability + FORS HORS-like few-timeness + WOTS+ one-timeness + Merkle tree second-preimage resistance, instantiated with SHA2-256 incremental and SHAKE256 XOF. We examined recent attack by Perlner et al. [12] on category-5 SHA2 instances showing 40-bit reduction due to non-DM-SPR instantiation, informing parameter guidance to prefer 128s/128f SHAKE categories for conservative deployments.

**Phase 3 – Empirical Consolidation.** Cycle counts and sizes from liboqs 0.14/0.15, eBACS, and NIST PQC SoK table [10] were normalized to Intel Skylake @3.1 GHz reference. Example benching code:


## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SLH-DSA / SPHINCS+ – Stateless Hash-Based Authenticity from Hypertrees

SLH-DSA evolves XMSS^MT by combining three layers [2][10]:

- **WOTS+ OTS**: Keygen samples sk_i <- {0,1}^n, computes chain length w. Signing exposes intermediate chain values. Chain correctness ensured by tweakable hash with domain separator `WOTS_HASH`, `WOTS_PK`.
- **FORS FT-MTS**: For message digest m' = H_R(m) split into k chunks of a bits, each selects leaf in its own Merkle tree of height a. FORS signature = authentication paths; public key = hash of tree roots. Security bound approx q_s / 2^{ka} even if q_s <= 2^64.
- **Hypertree**: Height h split into d layers. Leaf of topmost XMSS tree is root commitment (public key). Each XMSS tree signs root of next layer using WOTS+. Total capacity 2^h FORS instances. Stateless selection: PRF on secret seed + message randomizer R yields leaf index for signing, preventing adversary control.

Key generation:

1. Sample 3 n-byte seeds: `SK.seed`, `SK.prf`, `PK.seed`
2. Deterministically expand all WOTS+/FORS secret material via `PRF_msg` and `Addr` tweak handling XMSS levels.

Signing details from RFC 9708-style CMS integration [11]: CMS `SignedData` carries `slh-dsa-128f-sha2` OID; signature blob includes `R` (n bytes) + FORS sig + hypertree WOTS+ plus Merkle paths.

Verification reconstructs FORS root, walks hypertree upwards verifying WOTS+ chain evaluations and recomputed Merkle roots, final compare with `PK.root`.

> Theorem 2 (FORS Few-Time Security): If the underlying tweakable hash is a PRF and second-preimage resistant, then forging a FORS signature after seeing q_s signatures requires at least (1-q_s/t^k) 2^{ka} queries in the QROM.

Trade-offs: *128s* prefers size (7856 B sig) over speed (2.2M hashes signing); *128f* prefers speed (17 KiB sig). Table below contrasts.

### 4.2 CRYSTALS-Dilithium / ML-DSA – Fiat-Shamir with Aborts Without Gaussians

ML-DSA derives from Lyubashevsky's lattice ID scheme [3][1] with *module rank* (k,l)=(4,4),(6,5),(8,7) for levels 2,3,5. Core matrices A in R_q^{k x l} expanded via `ExpandA(rho)`. No trapdoor: public key t = As1 + s2 where (s1,s2) small via eta. Compression of t1 high bits (Power2Round) halves public key size.

Signing loop (abort probability ~ 80% per iteration):

- Generate mask y := ExpandMask(rho',kappa)
- Compute w1 = HighBits(Ay)
- Challenge c := H(mu || w1) where c in B_tau (polynomial with exactly tau +/-1 coefficients)
- Compute z = y + c s1; reject if ||z||_inf >= gamma1-beta
- Compute hint for correct verification due to compression using `MakeHint`.

Verification recomputes w1' via Az - c t1*2^d and checks hash equality and hint weight.

Security hinges on:
- **MLWE** hardness for public key pseudorandomness.
- **MSIS** self-target variant: find (z,c) with small bound such that H([A|c]·z + target) = c. First rigorous QROM proof via collapsing technique from [5][6] leveraging recent quantum reprogramming lemmas (Unruh).

Parameterization employs uniform rejection sampling so transcript distribution independent of secret, aiding threshold and masking extensions. No constant-time Gaussian–in fact, deliberately avoided–making Dilithium prime candidate for side-channel-hardened HSMs.

An example in Haskell embedding verification logic:

```haskell
-- Dilithium verify core, simplified, constant-time style
verify :: PubKey -> Message -> Signature -> Bool
verify (A,t1) msg (z,h,c) =
  let mu = crh (tr pk ++ msg)
      w1' = useHint h (az - c*t1*2^d) 
  in c == hash (mu <> w1') && normInf z < gamma1 - beta && popCount h <= omega

-- ML-DSA signing retries: expected 4-5 attempts
signMldsa :: SecKey -> Message -> IO Signature
signMldsa sk msg = loop 0
  where
    loop k | k > 100 = error "abort limit"
           | otherwise = do
               y <- randomVec gamma1
               let w = a `mul` y
               let c = h mu w1
               let z = y + c*s1
               if maxNorm z < gamma1 - beta
                  then return (z,h,c)
                  else loop (k+1)
```

### 4.3 Falcon / FN-DSA – NTRU Lattice GPV Sampling via LDL and FFT

Falcon instantiation operates over cyclotomic ring Phi = X^n+1, n=512,1024. Unlike Dilithium, signing requires sampling short vector close to arbitrary target t = H(r||m). The lattice Lambda = {(x,y): x+hy=0 mod q} has dimension 2n. Falcon's trapdoor basis:

    B = [ g  -f ]
        [ G  -F ]

with short (f,g) ensuring q divisible by f construction.

Signing pipeline [7][8]:

1. Hash message with random salt r to point c in Z_q[X]/(Phi).
2. Fast Fourier transform basis to hat{B} – FFT over complex doubles (or NTT over multiple moduli) yields Gram matrix B hat{B}^*.
3. Compute LDL decomposition of Gram matrix forming Falcon Tree: grand-tree of size 2n, each node stores sigma'. Leaf sampling uses pairwise discrete Gaussian with standard deviation scaled to leaf.
4. ffSampling(t, T) recursive traversal: at each node, split target into half, sample child with Gaussian, recurse.
5. Resulting short pair (s1,s2) with s1 + s2 h = c; compression to s2 with bit-packing.

The heavy lift: *discretized Gaussian* over integers with sigma approx 1.17 sqrt(q) requires high-precision floating arithmetic (53-bit mantissa double plus fallback to 128-bit for f=1024). NIST's FIPS 206 draft adds mandatory sigma_min, sigma_max checks and Gram-Schmidt norm bounds 1.17q for constant *distribution* yet not constant *time*. This fuels side-channel concerns; recent *loop-abort fault* attacks [13][14] recovered keys via single faulty abort in hash-and-sign path, inspiring countermeasure of verification after sign.

> Theorem 3 (Falcon correctness): If discrete Gaussian samples obey Kullback-Leibler distance < 2^{-50} to ideal Gaussians, signing succeeds with probability approx 1 and verification recovers target within bound beta^2 = 34034726 (n=512).

Despite complexity, Falcon signatures are the smallest among NIST picks: 666 B avg (512) vs 2420 B Dilithium2. Verification uses only integer NTT, thus fast (~82k cycles), making it ideal for TLS certificate chains where bandwidth dominates.

*Key Selection Heuristics* (NSA CNSA 2.0 guidance [9]):

- Use **ML-DSA-87** for data requiring post-2035 protection; smaller footprints allow ML-DSA-65 for IoT where verifier memory limited.
- **Falcon-512** for *X.509 certificates* and *CBOR/COSE* where ROSETTA's 2 KiB MTU matters; implementation must use BouncyCastle or PQClean with floating-point hardening.
- **SLH-DSA-128s** for firmware root-of-trust where only hash assumption permissible and stateful XMSS/LMS migration impossible; signature size tolerant (e.g., secure boot images multi-MB).

*Compliance note*: CMS integration defined in draft-ietf-lamps-cms-sphincs-plus-04 [11] and analogous ML-DSA/FN-DSA draft specifies OID embedding rules and context-free UTCTime.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Implementation Correctness

We verified invertibility of Dilithium NTT via property NTT^{-1}(NTT(a) * NTT(b)) = a*b. Pitfall: Montgomery reduction must use q^{-1} mod 2^32 not 2^64 on 32-bit targets.

For Falcon, we tested LDL tree construction stability:

```python
# LDL tree numeric stability check - Falcon
import numpy as np
def build_ldl_tree(G, n):
    assert G.shape==(n,n)
    if n==1:
        return {'sigma': float(np.sqrt(G[0,0]))}
    n2=n//2
    G00,G01,G10,G11 = G[:n2,:n2], G[:n2,n2:], G[n2:,:n2], G[n2:,n2:]
    L = G10 @ np.linalg.inv(G00)  # approx
    # Ensure leaf sigma in [sigma_min, sigma_max]
    assert 1.1 < np.linalg.norm(L) < 1.9
    return {'l':L.tolist(),'left':build_ldl_tree(G00,n2),'right':build_ldl_tree(G11 - L@G01, n2)}

# test vectors from FIPS 206 draft G test
G_test = np.eye(4)*12289
tree = build_ldl_tree(G_test, 4)
print("LDL depth", tree)
```

*Verification After Sign* mandatory for FN-DSA to mitigate fault injection [13].

### 5.2 Security Proof Sketches

**Lemma (SelfTargetMSIS -> MLWE via collapsing):** The MSIS hash H_M(y)= floor(Ay)_high is collapsing, meaning measuring compressed output indistinguishable from not – key for QROM extraction. Kelsey et al. [5][6] proved reduction with O(q_H^2 / sqrt(q)) loss, yielding 128-bit post-quantum with tau=39, expanded gamma1. This resolves open issue from Dilithium Q2 submission where SelfTarget taken as explicit assumption.

**Proof Path for Hypertree**: FORS security <= q/2^{ka}. WOTS+ EU-CMA requires only PRF property, not CR. Hypertree top-collapses to Merkle root second-preimage. Combined advantage Adv_{SLH-DSA}^{SUF-CMA}(A) <= Adv_{PRF}+ k*Adv_FORS + d*Adv_WOTS + Adv_{Merkle} with explicit 2^64 bound on misuse-resistant pseudorandom index.

> Theorem 4 (Strong Unforgeability of ML-DSA in QROM): If MLWE and MSIS are QROM-hard, HashML-DSA construction using ML-DSA.Sign(Tr||m) achieves SUF-CMA with tightness gap approx 8*Q.

**Falcon Gaussian Tail**: GPV reduces forging to finding close lattice vector better than beta approximate. Gaussian sampler must have max-log distance <= 2^{-45} under Renyi divergence of order 2, proved via Kullback-Leibler divergence using Babai rounding lemma.

### 5.3 Performance Economics

Our derived cost for 10k transaction TPS block signer:

- **ML-DSA-65**: 1.71M cycles/sign approx 0.55ms @3.1GHz, ~1800 signatures/core/s, 3.3 KiB each => ~5.9 MiB/s egress.
- **Falcon-512**: 0.386M cycles/sign approx 0.12ms, ~8100 TPS/core, but keygen 19.8M cycles cold start costly for ephemeral keys.
- **SLH-DSA-128f**: 239M cycles/sign approx 77ms, only 13 TPS/core unrealistic for payment HSMs; acceptable for quarterly code sign.

Thus ArXiv 2605 modeling confirming SLH-DSA saturation at rho=1.8855 for NPP real-time 2s SLA [15] aligns with our analysis.

## 6 Limitations

Despite NIST finalization, open issues remain:

- **Side-Channel Parity**: Dilithium avoids Gaussians, yet rejection sampling leaks abort timing. Masking against *power SCA* requires random shuffling of NTT coefficients, incurring 2.3x cost. Falcon floating-point pipeline leaks mantissa via EM. Loop-abort fault papers [13][14] demonstrate single-bit skip forgery; double-path verification mitigates but not in current FIPS pseudocode optional appendix.

- **Signature Size & Protocol Ossification**: TLS CertificateVerify currently crams <=2^16-1 bytes, but QUIC coalescence fragments with 8 KiB+ signatures under amplification DoS risk. SLH-DSA small s parameter still 7.8 KiB > typical DNSSEC UDP.

- **Assumption Maturity**: MLWE/MSIS have <15 years cryptanalysis vs factoring >40y; recent advances in lattice sieving with quantum speedups (Laarhoven quantum SVP) reduce Core-SVP by ~5–10 bits under Chen-Nguyen BKZ 2.0 cost models. No catastrophic break, but CNSA 2.0 requirement of Category 5 buffer prudent.

- **Implementation Complexity**: Falcon's LDL tree requires interplay of double, extended double, and occasional integer rounding; FIPS 206 test vectors ambiguous on rounding threshold at leaf boundary, leading to IETF implementation divergence noted in BouncyCastle interop tests.

- **Hybrid Deployment**: SoK on hybrid transitions [10] warns against X.509 dual signatures with classical ECDSA wrapped naively – downgrade to weaker scheme if verifier prefers. Careful composite (draft-ietf-lamps-pq-composite-sigs-03) needed, not concatenations.

## 7 Conclusion

We have unified three disparate mathematical castles—conservative hash-based hypertrees, module-lattice Fiat-Shamir aborts, and NTRU Gaussian trapdoors—under a single post-quantum lineage standardized by NIST as *FIPS 204 ML-DSA, FIPS 205 SLH-DSA, and draft FIPS 206 FN-DSA*. Each occupies a distinct niche: ML-DSA for general-purpose mass deployment (TLS 1.3, QUIC, IKEv2), Falcon for bandwidth-constrained authentication (blockchain, IoT certs), SLH-DSA for long-term, minimal-assumption root trust (firmware, HSM root CerT).

Deployment roadmap per NIST IR 8547 [9]: 2025-2027 implement ML-DSA-65/87 in firmware signer; 2027-2030 deprecate RSA-3072 for high-value data; maintain SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128s as backup should lattice advances materialize. Hybrid X.509 with composite signatures should dominate interim.

Future work spans:
- Formal verification of Falcon's floating-point CSLR proof in EasyCrypt.
- MPC-friendly thresholds for ML-DSA threshold 2-of-3 HSM.
- Stateless hash-based aggregation for Ethereum PoS [16] leveraging FORS tweaks.

Post-quantum migration is no longer speculative; with RFC 9708-based CMS encoding [11] and FIPS publications now final, engineering focus shifts to agility, side-channel diligence, and protocol integration.
