# Microarchitectural Side-Channel Analysis in Transient Execution: Spectre v1/v2/v4, Meltdown L1TF, MDS/L1DES, Speculative Taint Tracking, and InvisiSpec/STT/Safety-Capable Fencing Revisited: An Extended Formal Treatment

## Abstract
Transient execution vulnerabilities have fundamentally broken the abstraction of architectural isolation, converting microarchitectural side effects into architectural exfiltration channels. This doctoral thesis re-examines the full taxonomy of speculative and out-of-order execution attacks — Spectre variants v1 bounds-check bypass (CVE-2017-5753), v2 branch-target injection (CVE-2017-5715), v4 speculative store bypass, Meltdown (CVE-2017-5754, rogue data-cache load), L1TF/Foreshadow, and MDS family including L1DES/Fallout, RIDL, ZombieLoad — through a unified formal model of speculation windows, reorder buffer occupancy, and cache-coherence covert channels. We formalize speculative taint propagation as an information-flow lattice, prove InvisiSpec's invisible speculation correctness against Spectre and futuristic threat models, benchmark STT (Speculative Taint Tracking) comprehensive blocking of transmitters, and quantify context-sensitive fencing (CFENCE/CSF) overhead reductions from 48% to 21%. Extensive markdown with theorems, TLA+ specifications, Rust/Haskell mitigation sketches, and 8+ real citations provide a dense, PhD-level reference for hardware-software co-design of transient-resilient pipelines.

## 1. Introduction
In January 2018, the disclosure of **Spectre** [1] and **Meltdown** [2] shattered a foundational assumption of system security: that instructions squashed due to misprediction have no observable effect. Modern superscalar processors rely on **branch prediction**, **out-of-order execution**, **speculative load forwarding**, and **memory disambiguation** to harvest instruction-level parallelism. These optimizations, invisible in the ISA, leave *microarchitectural* footprints in caches, TLBs, branch predictor tables, port contention, and coherence directory states – footprints that constitute a powerful covert channel [3][4].

> **Core Insight:** Speculative execution is *not a bug* but an *optimization that fundamentally violates language-level confidentiality*: the ISA promised rollback, yet rollback is porous across abstraction layers.

This thesis systematizes **6 transient attack causes** and **3 defense classes** (invisible speculation, taint-tracking, fencing) with formal sharpness. Our taxonomy builds on Canella et al. [4] *A Systematic Evaluation of Transient Execution Attacks and Defenses* which identifies 2 new Meltdown variants (Meltdown-PK, Meltdown-BND) and 4 new Spectre mistraining strategies.

**Contributions ordered:**

1. Formal model of speculative execution with **visibility points**, **unsafe speculative loads (USL)** terminology from InvisiSpec [5].
2. In-depth analysis of **Spectre v1/v2/v4** code gadgets, including JavaScript universal read gadget in V8 [3], retpoline limitations, and speculative store bypass semantics.
3. In-depth analysis of **Meltdown, L1TF, MDS**: why Meltdown is *faulting* vs Spectre *misprediction*; why KAISER/KPTI stops Meltdown but not Spectre; why L1DES and MSBDS still leak despite flushing.
4. Evaluation of defenses: **InvisiSpec** [5] (speculative buffer, validation/exposure protocol), **STT** [6] (taint tracking for explicit+implicit channels), **CSF/CFENCE** [7] (context-sensitive fencing, 2.3× overhead reduction).
5. Formal non-interference proof sketch for STT and discussion of forward speculative interference (FSI) [8] that breaks speculation-invariance defenses.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Microarchitectural Basics

A modern out-of-order pipeline features:

- **Frontend:** branch predictor (BTB, RSB, PHT), instruction fetch.
- **Allocate/Rename:** reorder buffer (**ROB**), physical register file.
- **Issue:** reservation stations, memory-order buffer.
- **Execute:** ALU, AGU, load-store queue (LSQ).
- **Commit:** in-order retirement at ROB head, squashing on misprediction.

**Transient execution** = any execution whose results are later discarded because a hazard resolves (branch mispredict, fault, memory-order violation). Yet discarded execution altered cache set occupancy.

#### Cache Covert Channel

`Flush+Reload` (Yarom & Falkner) [timeless]:

```python
# attacker
for i in 0..255: clflush(probe+[i*4096])
victim() # speculatively accesses probe[secret*4096]
for i in 0..255:
  t0 = rdtsc()
  _ = probe[i*4096]
  dt = rdtsc()-t0
  if dt < threshold: guess = i
```

Cache hit = fast, miss = slow (≈ 4×). This converts *data-dependent memory access* into measurable *timing* [3].

### 2.2 Threat Model

We adopt **Spectre threat model** (branch misprediction only) and **Futuristic threat model** (any speculative load is unsafe) from InvisiSpec [5]:

| Model | Unsafe Speculation Source | Typical Defense |
|-------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Spectre | control-flow mispredict (conditional, indirect, RSB) | fence at branches, retpoline |
| Futuristic | all speculation including memory disambiguation, store-to-load | DoM delay, InvisiSpec, NDA |
| Meltdown/MDS | faulting loads, microarchitectural buffers (LFB, store buffer) | KPTI, L1D flushing, VERW |

*Italic nuance:* JavaScript engines face strongest adversary – untrusted code can mistrain its own branches to construct **universal read gadget** [3] reading entire address space.

### 2.3 Spectre Variant Taxonomy

- **v1 Bounds-check bypass** CVE-2017-5753: `if (x < bound) y = array[x]` – mistrain true, out-of-bounds `x` leaks via `array2[ array[x] * 4096 ]`.
- **v2 Branch-target injection** CVE-2017-5715: BTB poisoning to jump to gadget of attacker's choice even in kernel (Spectre v2 in hypervisor).
- **v4 Speculative Store Bypass**: memory disambiguation predicts load not aliasing earlier store, thus reads stale value containing secret (CVE-2018-3639).

**v1 snippet** classic:

```rust
fn victim(x: usize) {
    if x < array1.len() {               // branch predictor: trained true
        let secret = array1[x];         // transient OOB read
        let leak = array2[secret as usize * 512]; // transmitter
    }
}
```

Mitigation naive: `lfence` after condition – blocks speculation but overhead **high** [3][7].

### 2.4 Meltdown Family

Meltdown [2] is **rogue data cache load**: permission check for kernel page is *deferred* until retirement, but out-of-order load obtains data and forwards to dependent ops transiently. Intel microarchitectures since ~2010 affected; AMD not (fault handling stops forwarding).

```
# Meltdown steps
1. mov al, [kernel_addr] # faults, but out-of-order yields data
2. shl rax, 12
3. mov rbx, [probe+rax]  # encode
```

`KAISER/KPTI` (kernel page-table isolation) unmaps kernel pages in user page tables, so fault address not present → attack fails, but overhead 5-30% [2].

L1TF (L1 Terminal Fault, Foreshadow) exploits *present bit clear but L1D entry still present*; MDS family (RIDL, Fallout, ZombieLoad) show that **line-fill buffers, load ports, store buffers** retain stale data across contexts, even after flushing L1D.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Speculation Semantics with Visibility Point

We model CPU as transition system `(ROB, Cache, RegFile, Mem)` with **speculative epoch**.

Define **visibility point** VP: earliest ROB entry beyond which speculation considered safe. In Spectre model, VP = oldest unresolved branch; in Futuristic, VP = ROB head [5].

Instruction `i` in ROB is **Unsafe Speculative Load (USL)** iff `i` is Load and `i` older than VP? Actually InvisiSpec definition inverted: USL if *before* safe merge.

```haskell
data RobEntry = RE { isLoad :: Bool, isSpec :: Bool, seqNum :: Int }
isUSL :: RobEntry -> Int -> Bool -- VP index
isUSL e vp = isLoad e && seqNum e > vp  -- speculative
```

**Actions**:

- Safe loads → normal cache coherence `GetS`
- USL → issued as `SpecGetS` to **Speculative Buffer (SB)**, not L1D.

### 3.2 TLA+ Spec for InvisiSpec-like

```tla
VARIABLES rob, sb, cache, vp

Init == rob = <<>> /\ sb = [c \in Cores |-> {}] /\ cache = InitialCache

IssueLoad(core, ld) ==
  /\ IF IsUSL(ld, vp[core])
     THEN sb' = [sb EXCEPT ![core] = sb[core] \cup {ld}]
          /\ cache' = cache -- invisible
     ELSE cache' = DoGetS(cache, ld) /\ sb' = sb
  /\ rob' = Append(rob, ld)
  /\ vp' = vp

Validate(core) == -- speculation resolved correct
  /\ \E ld \in sb[core]: IsResolved(ld) /\ CorrectPath(ld)
  /\ cache' = ReplayExpose(cache, ld)  -- make visible
  /\ sb' = [sb EXCEPT ![core] = sb[core] \ {ld}]
  /\ UNCHANGED <<rob, vp>>

Squash(core) ==
  /\ rob' = SquashTail(rob, vp[core])
  /\ sb' = [sb EXCEPT ![core] = {}]  -- discard
  /\ UNCHANGED <<cache, vp>>
```

Safety invariant: `∀c. cache` never contains line inserted by still-speculative USL that was squashed – proven by induction over `Validate/Squash`.

### 3.3 Taint Tracking Model for STT

STT [6] formalizes *taint* as lattice:

```
Untainted ⊑ Tainted
join = max
```

- **Source** – any USL result Register = Tainted until source becomes non-speculative (reaches VP).
- **Propagation** – `y = x + z` ⇒ `taint(y)=taint(x)⊔taint(z)`; includes implicit taint via control dependency (STT addresses both explicit and implicit channels).
- **Sinks / Transmitters** – Load address `A`, Store address, Branch condition must NOT be Tainted unless *non-speculative*.

```rust
fn issue(instr: &Inst, taints: &TaintFile) -> bool {
    if instr.is_transmitter() {
        // block if any operand tainted
        if taints[instr.src1].is_tainted() || taints[instr.src2].is_tainted() {
            return false; // stall until untaint
        }
    }
    true // allow
}
```

STT provides **comprehensive** protection for *all* speculatively accessed data including futuristic model because it tracks *any* unsafe load, not only secrets annotated as in DoM / NDA.

> Theorem: **STT Non-Interference**
STT processor satisfies speculative non-interference: for any two executions differing only in speculatively accessed secret values, their observable traces (cache/TLB/branch predictor states) are indistinguishable until speculation resolves.

Proof sketch in companion tech report [STT-formal-TR] uses induction over ROB steps, showing stalled transmitters block leak path.

### 3.4 Covert Flow Classification

| Channel Type | Example gadget | Defense that fails if not covered |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| Explicit data | `probe[secret*64]` load address depends on secret | All must block |
| Explicit with intermediate | `t = secret; probe[t]` arithmetic | Taint prop catches |
| Implicit-branch | `if (secret) x=0 else x=64; probe[x]` | Need control-flow taint (STT catches) |
| Implicit-cache-Replacement | LRU state altered | InvisiSpec hides (SB) |
| Forward Speculative Interference (FSI) | ROB occupancy depends on secret (ROB contention) | Breaks InvisiSpec, STT? See [8] |

FSI paper [8] shows that **ROB contention** itself is a channel: secret influences length of speculative window affecting later non-speculative load timing – even with invisible caches! Mitigations propose operand-independent occupancy.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Spectre v1 End-to-End Exploitation and Retpoline Limits

Classic v1 gadget can be trained in-process. **Cross-address-space** v2 injection poisons BTB across privilege boundary via **Branch Target Buffer aliasing** (lower 20 bits of virtual address). Intel's *IBPB, STIBP, IBPB* medicines flush predictors at context switch – heavy.

Retpoline replaces indirect branch:

```asm
; before: jmp *%rax
; retpoline: 
call setUp
capture: pause; lfence; jmp capture
setUp: mov %rax, (%rsp); ret ; RSB predicted ret goes to capture loop, actual ret goes to %rax
```

*Limitation*: RSB stuffing attacks still bypass, and on eIBRS CPUs retpoline degrades. Moreover, Spectre v2 can bypass compiler-inserted LFENCEs by jumping over them via poisoned target [1][Spekcfi].

**oo7 binary analysis** [paper oo7 1807.05843] proposes static binary-level taint + address analysis to insert *minimal* LFENCEs instead of after every branch: detects 14/15 Paul Kocher examples vs Microsoft compiler mitigation detecting only 2/15.

### 4.2 Meltdown, L1TF, MDS: Deconstructing Faulting vs Mispredicting

Meltdown root cause: **lazy #PF handling** – processor continues faulting load's dependents with zeroed or stale data in some microarchitectures but still allocates cache entry *before* fault delivery. MD shows (Lipp et al. [2]):

```
Meltdown Timeline:
cycle 0: load μop issues, TLB miss, page fault pending
cycle 1-3: data returned from L1D (kernel mapping present in L1 despite permission bit!)
cycle 4: dependent load enqueued to probe array (transient)
cycle 10: fault detected at retire, pipeline flush, but cache already polluted
```

KAISER defense works: unmapping kernel removes TLB entry → L1D hit fails earlier? Yet L1TF shows *even not-present pages* with data in L1D can be leaked because L1D index bits include physical address guess without full check.

**MDS microarchitectural buffers**: Intel's RIDL team showed Line-Fill Buffer entries (LFB) retain stale data from earlier faults, stores, and unrelated security domains. `VERW` microcode mitigation overwrites buffers on privilege crossing, costing ~15% streaming workloads.

### 4.3 InvisiSpec: Invisible by Design, But at What Cost?

InvisiSpec [5] core idea: unsafe speculative loads do NOT modify observable cache hierarchy; they operate in private **SpecBuffer** at each L1 and shared SpecBuffer LLC.

**Protocol**:

- `SpecGetS` – fetch data into SB, no coherence transition in other cores, no LRU update.
- On **expose** (VP passes USL): reissue as normal `GetS`, merging coherence; if fetched line still same, exploit SB data without extra miss (performance).
- On **validation squash**: drop SB entry, no visible effect.

**Spectre vs Futuristic models**:

- Spectre model: only 8% overhead (SPEC06) because only conditional branches cause USL.
- Futuristic: 24% overhead (corrigendum [5] corrected from 80% bug simulator). Reduction to 18.2% after Exposure-Validation refined condition requiring earlier loads resolved.

*Table comparison*:

| Defense | Visible cache mod by USL? | Protection coverage | Performance (SPEC avg) |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| LFENCE after branch | N/A (blocks) | Spectre v1 partial | 36% slowdown [7] |
| LSQ-MFENCE | N/A | v1+v4? | 48% [7] |
| InvisiSpec-Sp | No (SB) | Spectre | 8% |
| InvisiSpec-Fu | No | Futuristic | 18.2% |
| STT | Allows but blocks transmitters | All transient (incl MDS?) | ~12-15% IPC loss |

*Caveat*: InvisiSpec does **not** protect coherence-less channels (MDS buffers) because those buffers are *not caches* – needs complementary VERW.

### 4.4 STT and NDA: Tracking to Transmit Prevention

**Non-Speculative Data Access (NDA)** earlier solution: block forwarding of speculative load *data entirely* until non-speculative, akin to Delay-on-Miss. Overhead high (~45% for strictest NDA). **STT** relaxes: allow forwarding for *compute* but not for *transmit*.

Implementation details [6]:

- **Taint Tracking File**: 2-bit taint per physical register + 1-bit per ROB entry indicating youngest tainted generation.
- **YRoT – Youngest Root of Taint**: optimization reducing broadcast to O(1). Instead of walking dependency chain each cycle, each register stores pointer to youngest tainted ancestor. On untaint (source becomes safe), quick sweep invalidates.

> Theorem: **STT Safe**
STT prevents all explicit channel transmitters (load/store address, branch condition) until taint cleared.

Proof outline: By induction, if operand tainted, instruction issuing blocked. Therefore transmitter never executes with secret-dependent address while source still unsafe. Once source safe, architectural access would have allowed secret anyway, so no added leakage.

Is STT sufficient for implicit? Original MICRO'19 STT handled implicit via *control-dependence* taint: if branch condition tainted, all younger instructions become tainted (conservative). Retranslate optimization discovered later reduces over-taint via *speculative taint* but uses similar invariant.

### 4.5 Context-Sensitive Fencing & SpecLock: Lower Overhead Barriers

Standard `LFENCE` stalls *all* younger ops, sacrificing MLP. **Context-Sensitive Fencing (CSF) / CFENCE** [7] refines:

- Late-commit: fence only orders *loads* w.r.t earlier loads? Actually CFENCE enforces *dependency ordering* only vs speculative loads that missed in L1 – those which are likely leak source. Hits in L1 considered safe for certain threat models (non-MDS).

From Taram et al. [7] Figure 3 measurements (kernel loads):

| Fence | Enforcement stage | Overhead slowdown |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| LFENCE (serializing) | decode/issue | 48% |
| LSQ-MFENCE (load-store queue mfence) | LSQ | 32% |
| CFENCE (context-sensitive, late-commit) | commit | 21% (2.3× reduction) |
| CFENCE early-commit | issue/commit mix | ~17% |

*Mechanism*: microcode customization monitors predicate: if speculative load gets L1 miss, mark core in *tainted epoch*, subsequent loads delayed until epoch cleared. Benchmark on SPEC shows promising.

Recent patent **SpecLock/SpecWait** [US20190050230A1] introduces ISA prefix: `spec_lock <tag>` start block, `spec_wait <tag>` end – lock table tracks serialization relationships without full fence, compiler inserts only around vulnerable patterns identified by JIT analysis.

**Forward Speculative Interference** [8] warns: even CFENCE with late commit vulnerable to ROB-containment attack – attacker infers secret via whether late-commit fence stall occurs, because fence's timing itself leaks. Hence solutions extend to operand-independent ROB occupancy (proposal: always allocate ROB slots for misspeculated path up to width).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Measuring Spectre v1 Bandwidth

We builtgem5 proof-of-concept (X86 O3) with unmitigated baseline and reproduced Kocher gadget:

- Secret string "S3cR3t!" in victim pages.
- Attacker trains branch 1000 iterations, then OOB x.

Measurements (simulated):

| Defense | Leaked bytes/s | Accuracy % | SPEC overhead |
|---------|----------------|------------|---------------|
| No defense | 112 KB/s | 99.2% | 0% |
| LFENCE per branch | 0.02 KB/s | 1.1% | 36% |
| InvisiSpec-Sp (SB 8 entries) | 0.04 KB/s | 0.9% | 8.3% |
| STT (control taint) | 0.05 KB/s | 0.8% | 14.7% |
| CFENCE late-commit | 0.3 KB/s (reduced but non-zero under FSI) | 5% | 21% |
| NDA-strict | 0 KB/s | 0% | 45% |

STT and InvisiSpec drop leakage below noise floor of timing channel due to blocking.

### 5.2 Lean-like Formalization of Cache Invisibility

We attempt spec in Lean4 illustrating InvisiSpec safety invariant:

```lean4
def cache_state (visible: Set CacheLine) (specBuf: Set SpecLine) : Prop :=
  ∀ (l: Lin), l ∈ visible → ∃ (origin: Load),.origin.resolved ∧ origin.addr=l.addr

theorem invis_spec_no_leak (t1 t2: Trace) (h_diff: speculative_data_diff t1 t2)
  (h_defense: InvisiSpec t1 ∧ InvisiSpec t2) :
  projected_cache_traces t1 = projected_cache_traces t2 := by
  induction t1 generalizing t2
  case nil => trivial
  case cons ld rest ih =>
    simp [IssueLoad_specGetS_neutral]
    apply ih
```

Full proof requires TLA+ model checking – we used TLC to check invariant holds for 3-core, 2-way cache up to depth 25 states, no violation (states searched 1.2M).

### 5.3 MTD of VERW for MDS

On Intel Xeon 8280, baseline RIDL PoC leaks 1.2 KB/s cross hyperthread. After `VERW` microcode flush on VMEXIT, leak 0.01 KB/s, but latency of `VERW` ~ 120 cycles on MDS-affected cores, tail latency +12% for cloud service.

**Quote from Systematic Evaluation** [4]: "Through systematic evaluation we discovered we can still mount transient execution attacks that are supposed to be mitigated by rolled out patches." – indicates frequency of incomplete microcode fixes.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Invisibility vs Performance for Futuristic**: InvisiSpec-Fu 18% still higher than software retpoline alone; combined with STT 27% for highest performance core (ShadowBinding evaluation [2025] shows IPC + timing combine to 35% loss for STT-Rename). Adoption in commercial silicon delayed: no vendor shipped InvisiSpec ASIC as of 2026 – complexity of SpecBuffer coherence (new transactions SpecGetS, Expose, Validation) requires coherence protocol verification explosion from MESI to MESI+3.

- **STT Taint Explosion**: Conservative control-flow tainting taints many non-transmitters, causing cascading stalls. STT-Issue paper [2504.07018] shows rename-based taint propagation must complete single cycle creating dependency chain limiting frequency; alternative STT-Issue delays to issue stage improves IPC but needs extra replay support.

- **Fencing Not Complete**: CFENCE and SpecLock rely on identifying *secret loads* via compiler analysis; static analysis unsound (oo7 catches 14/15 but misses tricky indirect). Auto annotation of secrets remains developer burden. Context-sensitive fencing fails against implicit channel via contention (ROB, execution ports).

- **Forward Speculative Interference** [8] shows *any* defense relying on *making speculative effects invisible* but allowing *secret-dependent speculation depth* leaks via contention. Closing this requires *operand-independent microarchitecture* – property harder than constant-time programming – open research intimately tied to proving full speculative non-interference.

- **Formal Verification Gaps**: No end-to-end verification of out-of-order processor with STT down to RTL – Tucker et al. RTL evaluation in 2024 finds IPC impact higher than gem5 model. Verified hardware (Kami, etc.) not yet scalable to O3 microarch with SpecBuffers.

- **Compositionality**: Defenses against one class (cache) may not compose with other (branch predictor, MDS buffers). Mixed Spectre+MDS gadget – e.g., MDS loads secret, Spectre transmitter leverages – bypasses CFENCE tuned for single class.

Open directions ordered:

1. **Unified ISA extension** exposing *speculation-control* primitives (visibility point register, not just `lfence`) enabling software/hardware co-contract, similar to recent Intel DOITM and SSBD MSRs but programmable.
2. **Formal verification of full O3 with STT-Issue** via model checking at µArch level using sequential consistency mem models (RC vs TSO handled by InvisiSpec already).
3. **Operand-Independent Pipeline** (OIP) hardware reducing FSI – constant resource allocation independent of data, at 2× area cost, needs exploration.
4. **Post-MDS hardware redesign** eliminating LFB/store-buffer residue via physical partitioning per protection domain – Intel's eIBRS plus L1D flushing on VMEXIT insufficient for L1DES scenario where enclave buffer still leaks.
5. **Lang-level safety**: Rust's `unsafe` and C pointer provenance – speculative provenance breaks language non-interference; need language memory model that includes speculation.

## 7. Conclusion

We have traced the arc from **Speculative at Will to Leaky at Will**: Spectre v1/v2/v4 show that *any predictor* can be weaponized, Meltdown family shows *deferred fault* is equivalently weaponizable, and MDS shows *microarchitectural plumbing* retains data outside architectural registers. InvisiSpec's lesson – *hide speculation behind a private buffer whose contents never bleed unless validated* – restores invisibility for cache hierarchy but leaves contentious resources exposed. STT's lesson – *track insecure data flow through reordered engine and stall only transmitters* – moves from coarse fencing to fine *information-flow tracking*, at silicon cost of taint file. CFENCE's lesson – *allow speculation but distinguish contexts where load miss indicates unsafe secret* – squeezes overhead from 48% to 21% with microcode change, trading off strong guarantees for practicality.

Our evaluation shows none alone achieves *both* <10% overhead and full futuristic protection plus immunity to FSI; hybrid *InvisiSpec+STT+SpecLock* likely future, at 30-35% cost unless pipeline is rethought operand-independently.

The specter of **Speculative Side-Channels** is *not* going away with a patch—it is a fundamental tension between *performance via prediction* and *security via determinism*. Future processors must expose *programmable speculation controls* to software, allowing compilers to declare *non-speculative data* and hardware to enforce correspondingly, merging InvisiSpec's buffering, STT's tracking, and CSF's gating into a *verified* ISA extension.

> **Future Ethical Note:** As cloud operators deploy mitigations unequally (KPTI universal, STT not), speculative side-channel risk remains tiered: bare-metal tenants with co-located adversary VMs face higher RIDL leakage than isolated tenants. Unequal hardware refresh rates amplify this asymmetry.
