# Real-Time Decoding of Surface Codes and Quantum LDPC Codes Under Circuit-Level Noise: Union-Find, Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching, and Neural-Augmented FPGA Decoders

## Abstract
Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires classical decoding at the cadence of syndrome extraction, typically $0.5$–$1.1\ \mu s$ per round for superconducting qubits, with logical failure suppressed exponentially in code distance $d$. This thesis analyzes the real-time decoding stack for topological surface codes and emergent quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) bivariate bicycle (BB) codes under full circuit-level noise. We unify minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM), almost-linear-time Union-Find (UF), and neural-augmented ensemble decoders, and we characterize their hardware acceleration on FPGA and ASIC substrates. We prove complexity bounds, derive Helios-style distributed UF scaling $O(d^3)$ parallel resources yielding sublinear average-time $O(d\ \alpha(d))$ to $o(1)$ per-round latency, and evaluate single-shot and sliding-window strategies for BB codes with belief-propagation plus ordered-statistics decoding (BP-OSD) and transformer decoders. Empirical synthesis on Xilinx VCU129 reports $11.5\ ns$ per round for $d=21$ phenomenological and $23.7\ ns$ for $d=17$ circuit-level, and $124\ ns$ inference for recurrent NN with $550\ ns$ closed-loop feedback, meeting backlog-free criteria for million-cycle memory. We conclude with limits, open gaps, and a roadmap to modular 100-logical-qubit decoders.

## 1 Introduction
The threshold theorem establishes that arbitrary quantum computations can be executed with arbitrarily low logical error provided physical error $p < p_{th}$ and classical control can keep pace [1][2]. In practice, *keeping pace* is non-trivial. Superconducting transmon processors from Google Quantum AI, IBM, and academic labs extract a full syndrome round in $0.8$–$1.1\ \mu s$; each round produces $O(d^2)$ bits for surface codes and $O(n)$ for $[[n,k,d]]$ qLDPC codes [3][4]. If decoding latency exceeds generation rate, a backlog accumulates exponentially, stalling logical T-gate teleportation and lattice surgery.

This work bridges three lineages:

* **Topological matching:** Fowler's MWPM mapping of surface code errors to graph matching on ancilla defects [2][6], including correlated and pipelined reweighting [5].
* **Almost-linear clustering:** Delfosse–Nickerson Union-Find decoder mapping Pauli errors to erasure percolation, achieving $O(n\alpha(n))$ worst-case via disjoint-set union (DSU) with path compression and union-by-size [1][8].
* **qLDPC explosion:** Bravyi *et al.* bivariate bicycle codes achieving $[[144,12,12]]$ with weight-6 checks and $>10\times$ qubit saving vs. surface code at $p=0.1\%$ [9][10], forcing the decoder community to move beyond planar graphs to hypergraphs [13][14].

We focus on *real-time* meaning deterministic closed-loop latency $L_{closed} < T_{cycle}$ and sustained throughput $>1/T_{cycle}$, not amortized offline logical error rate (LER) alone.

> **Contribution snapshot:** (i) Formalization of backlog-free condition under stochastic circuit noise, (ii) comparative analysis of MWPM vs UF vs neural vs BP-OSD for surface and BB codes, (iii) hardware mapping to Helios hybrid tree-grid FPGA and quantized LSTM cores, (iv) synthesis results and open bottlenecks for 100–1000 logical qubits.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Surface Codes and Circuit-Level Noise
The rotated surface code $SC(d)$ encodes one logical qubit in $d^2$ data plus $d^2-1$ ancilla qubits with stabilizers $X^{\otimes 4}, Z^{\otimes 4}$ on plaquettes. Distance $d$ ensures $\lfloor (d-1)/2 \rfloor$ correctability. Under circuit-level noise each gate, idle, reset and measurement fails with Pauli depolarization $p$, measurement flips with $p_m$ [3][4]. The detector graph becomes a 3D space-time graph with vertices $V = \{ \text{detectors } (x,y,t)\}$ and edges weighted by $w_e = -\log( p_e/(1-p_e))$. For superconducting SI1000 model, hyperedge probabilities include hook errors and Y-correlated events [3][11].

*Key concept:* A logical error occurs iff the decoder's recovery chain $C_{rec}$ combined with physical error $E$ includes a non-trivial relative homology class connecting opposite boundaries or wrapping the torus.

### 2.2 Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching
Edmonds' blossom algorithm solves MWPM on general graph in $O(N^3\log N)$. Surface code decoding reduces to matching detection events (defects) on two decoupled graphs (X and Z). Fowler *et al.* [2][6] showed average $O(n^2)$ per round for $n=d^2$ lattice, using sparse-blossom and locality to achieve $O(1)$ parallel if enough cores. Correlated MWPM reweights edges using conditional probabilities $p(Z|X)$ achieved via tambourine or PyMatching's correlated stage [5].

> **Theorem 1 (MWPM threshold, phenomenological):** *For toric code with perfect measurements, MWPM threshold $p_{th}\approx 15.5\%$ for bit-flip, $ \approx 9.9\%$ for depolarizing $p$ under optimal reweighting. For circuit-level depolarizing, $p_{th}\approx 0.9\%–1.1\%$ with $d$ odd rotated code.*

This does not guarantee latency.

### 2.3 Union-Find: Erasure Conversion
Delfosse–Nickerson [1] reinterpreted Pauli errors as erasures with known locations after clustering. Steps:

1.  **Syndrome validation:** Grow odd clusters covering all defects by half-edges per synchronous round.
2.  **Peeling:** Extract a spanning forest in each even cluster, leaf-peel to find recovery inside erasure.

Complexity $O(n\alpha(n))$ almost-linear; inverse Ackermann $\alpha(n) \le 3$ for any $n < 10^{600}$. Practical threshold $9.9\%$ toric code, $2.6\%$ with faulty measurements, close to MWPM but $50$–$100\times$ faster in Python/C++ due to DSU pointer chasing, not blossom cycles [1][8].

### 2.4 qLDPC and Bivariate Bicycle Codes
Quantum LDPC generalizes stabilizers to sparse $H_X, H_Z \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m\times n}$ with bounded weight $w\le 8$. Bravyi *et al.* [9] defined BB codes via $l,m$ bicyclic: $H_X = [A | B], H_Z = [B^T | A^T]$ where $A = f_1(x,y), B = f_2(x,y)$ with $x^l = y^m = 1$ in group algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^l-1,y^m-1)$. Example $[[72,12,6]]$ uses $l=6,m=6$, $[[144,12,12]]$ gross code $l=12,m=12$, $k=12$ saving $12\times$ over $12$ surface patches. Threshold $0.7\%$ pseudo for memory, distance analysis depends on $\gcd$ polynomial $g(z)$ emphasized by Postema [10][16].

Decoding leaves planar matching: hypergraph BP struggles due to 4-cycle trapping sets and degeneracy [13][14]. BP-OSD (belief propagation + ordered statistics post-processing) lifts degeneracy by Gaussian elimination on most reliable $k$ columns [13]. Machine-learning AlphaQubit-2 recurrent transformer reduces worst-case BP-OSD tail from ms to $1\ \mu s$ [7][12].

## 3 Methodology
We formalize the decoder as a streaming algorithm $\mathcal{D}: \{ s_t \}_{t=1}^{T} \to \{ r_t \}$ where $s_t\in\{0,1\}^{m}$ is noisy syndrome and $r_t$ is Pauli frame update. Requirement: *backlog-free* if $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \Pr[T_{decode}(n) > n T_{cycle}] < \infty$ per Terhal-Aliferis criterion.

Our methodology triangulates:

*  **Complexity-theoretic:** Blossom vs DSU vs message-passing iteration bound.
*  **Hardware-in-loop:** VHDL / Vitis HLS synthesis for Helios tree-grid, BRAM sharing, and quantized LSTM matvec.
*  **Noise simulation:** Stim + PyMatching + custom sliding-window BP-OSD; circuit noise $p\in\{0.05\%,0.1\%,0.2\%\}$; SI1000; $10^4$–$10^7$ shots per point.
*  **Neural distillation:** Teacher = BP-OSD / MWPM ensemble on $10^8$ syndromes, student = 2-layer LSTM 512 hidden + linear head quantized to int6, distilled with focal loss.

Metrics: logical error per round $\epsilon_L$, latency $L_{p50}, L_{p99}$, LUT/FF/BRAM/DSP, power, and qubit overhead $\Lambda = d$ scaling exponent defined by $\epsilon_L \propto (p/p_{th})^{\lceil d/2\rceil}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 From Matching Graph to Detector Graph: Correlations
The simplified independent X/Z graphs neglect *Y* correlations. Pipelined correlated MWPM [5] stages:
- *Stage A (parallel):* For each edge $e$, compute correlation trigger $c_e = \sum_{e' \sim e} \lambda_{e,e'} s_{e'}$ where $\lambda$ = log-likelihood ratio from gate-level error propagation tables.
- *Stage B (parallel low-confidence):* Preliminary blossom on subgraph of edges with $w_e < \tau$.
- *Stage C (serial):* Full reweight $\tilde{w}_e = w_e - \log(1-p_{corr})$ and finish matching.

> **Lemma 1 (Reweighting invariance, Fowler-Pal er):** *If correlation correction $\Delta w_e$ satisfies $|\Delta w_e| < w_{min}/2$, then matching parity class is preserved with probability $>1-\exp(-O(d))$.*

This decoupling improves throughput to near-linear without changing logical threshold beyond statistical noise ($<0.02\%$ delta) [5].

*Python sketch for sparse blossom wrapper:*

```python
import pymatching
import numpy as np

def correlated_mwpm_decoder(detector_model, shots=10000):
    H = detector_model.check_matrix
    priors = detector_model.priors
    m = pymatching.Matching.from_check_matrix(H, weights=-np.log(priors))
    # Stage A: parallel reweight
    # correlation_matrix from stim circuit error model
    def reweight_callback(syndrome):
        # syndrome: bool array [m]
        # compute hyperedge likelihoods
        corr = correlation_reweight(syndrome, detector_model.hyperedges)
        # return edges, weights
        return corr
    # PyMatching supports callbacks via recalc?
    # simplified:
    for _ in range(shots):
        syndrome = sample_circuit_noise(detector_model)
        weights = reweight_callback(syndrome)
        correction = m.decode(syndrome, custom_weights=weights)
        yield correction
```

Iterative runtime: *Edmonds search* expands blossoms when alternating tree conflict arises; sparse implementation prunes edges exceeding radius $r\approx \log(1/p)$.

### 4.2 Union-Find Distributed Hardware Mapping — Helios
UF's algorithmic charm is locality. Delfosse's sequential growth [1] grows each odd cluster by half-edge. Liyanage *et al.* [11][18] reformulated as distributed cells on 3D lattice: each PU (processing unit) at $(x,y,t)$ maintains:

- *root pointer* parent[i], rank/size
- *parity* odd/even (XOR of defects in component)
- *support* growth radius $g$
- *fusion queue*

DSU merging steps use two-phase commit to avoid read-write races: `Find(u)` traverses to root with path halving, hardware-implemented via tree up-link network.

**Helios architecture:**

* *Grid:* $d\times d\times d$ PUs each with neighbor links 6 (+/- x,y,t).
* *Tree:* aggregation tree merging cluster parity bottom-up, latency $O(\log d)$ for collective odd detection.
* *Hybrid:* broadcast of growth increment via tree, fusion via grid.

Resulting average time: for $N=O(d^3)$ PUs, $T_{decode}=O(\gamma\log d)$ where $\gamma$ = max cluster diameter, which under $p < p_{th}$ has exponential tail $\Pr[\gamma > k] \le a\exp(-b k)$. Hence $\mathbb{E}[T]=O(\log d)$? Observed empirically $o(1)$ per round because larger $d$ adds parallel resources faster than cluster growth [11][18].

GFM comparison table of real-time performance compiled from [11][18][22][7]:

|---------|------|----------|-----------------|----------|-------------------|------|
| Sparse Blossom MWPM | Rot. Surf | 11 | $63\ \mu s$ | CPU (1 core) | $1.0\%$ | – |
| PyMatching v2 + Corr | Rot. Surf | 11 | $1.2\ \mu s$ | CPU 8-core | $0.95\%$ | – |
| UF (software) | Rot. Surf | 21 | $380\ ns$ | CPU FPGA soft | $0.85\%$ | – |
| Helios UF distributed | Rot. Surf | 21 | $11.5\ ns$ | Xilinx VCU129 | $0.78\%$ | 892k |

| Helios opt. large | Rot. Surf | 51 | $544\ ns$ | VCU129 | $0.78\%$ | 1.1M |

| LSTM int6 FPGA NN | Surf | 3 | $124\ ns$ infer, $550\ ns$ closed-loop | ZCU111 | $0.70\%$* | 54k |

*Rust HLS kernel for PU growth (Vitis):*

```rust
// Pseudo-HLS Rust equivalent of Verilog PU
#[derive(Clone)]
struct PuState {
    root: u32,
    odd: bool,
    growth: u8,
    parent_valid: bool,
}

fn pu_step(state: &mut PuState, nbrs: &[PuState; 6], syndrome_bit: bool) -> bool {
    // Find with path compression approximated
    let mut is_root = state.root == pu_id();
    if syndrome_bit {
        state.odd ^= true;
    }
    if !state.odd { return false; } // even cluster stops
    // grow by 0.5 edge
    state.growth = state.growth.wrapping_add(1);
    let mut fusion_needed = false;
    for nb in nbrs {
        if nb.growth + state.growth >= edge_weight(nb) {
            // union request
            if merge_root(state.root, nb.root) {
                fusion_needed = true;
            }
        }
    }
    fusion_needed
}
```

*Key engineering:* BRAM banking to store $3d^3$ edge weights, 67% reduction via logical-qubit resource sharing across time-division multiplexed patches [8][18].

> **Theorem 2 (Helios sublinear, Liyanage et al.):** *Given $O(d^3)$ PUs, distributed UF decodes $SC(d)$ with average round latency $T(d) = O\big( \mathbb{E}[\gamma] \big) = o(1)$ decreasing in $d$ under $p < p_{th}$. Worst-case $T_{wc}=O(d^3)$ but with probability $\exp(-\Omega(d))$.*

Proof sketch uses percolation: below threshold cluster size distribution has exponential tail, thus parallel growth steps bounded.

### 4.3 qLDPC, BB Codes, and Neural Augmentation
BB codes force hypergraph decoding. Our decoder stack:

1.  **SSDG:** Put Tanner graph $T = (Q \cup C, E)$ where $Q$ variables, $C$ checks. For $BB(n=144)$, $ |Q|=144, |C|=108$, degree 6.
2.  **BP (min-sum, 80 iter) with serial schedule:** $m_{v\to c}^{(i+1)} = s_c \oplus \sum_{c'\neq c} m_{c'\to v}^{(i)}$. Damping $\lambda=0.9$ reduces oscillation from trapping sets.
3.  **OSD-10 post:** If syndrome not satisfied, order LLRs $|L_v|$, perform Gaussian elimination on $H$ restricted to top $k = \dim\ker H$ bits, avoid $O(n^3)$ full.
4.  **Sliding window ($w=7$) single-shot:** Leverage redundant stabilizers to merge $w$ rounds before committing [16].

Neural augmentation replaces tail: AlphaQubit-2 uses transformer with recurrent cross-attention over detectors [7]. Input embeddings: detector id (learned) + time + basis. Training loss:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\log p(L_{true}|s) + \beta \cdot \text{latency penalty}$$

with focal $\beta$.

*Transformer decoding latency advantage:* BP-OSD worst-case tail $> 5\ ms$ for $[[144,12,12]]$ at $p=0.2\%$ due to Gaussian elimination failures; neural median $0.6\ \mu s$ on TPU, p99 $0.9\ \mu s$ with identical logical $\approx 1.5\times$ worse but $10\times$ faster worst-case [12][13].

*Haskell DSL for BB code description:*

```haskell

grossCode :: BBCode

parityCheck :: BBCode -> (Matrix, Matrix)

  let n = 2*l*m
      circulant p = circulantFromPoly l m p
      a = circulant aPoly
      b = circulant bPoly

-- preserves CSS commutativity A B^T + B A^T = 0
```

The **logical performance** of BB under circuit-level with our stack: at $p=0.1\%$ physical, $[[72,12,6]]$ AlphaQubit-2 achieves LER $5\times$ lower than BP-OSD [12]; $[[144,12,12]]$ trades $1.4\times$ higher LER for $k=12$ advantage: effective $12\times$ less volume than $12$ distance-12 surface patches ($12* 2d^2 \approx 3456$ vs $144$ physical).

### 4.4 Modular and Multi-FPGA Real-Time Feedback
Achilles *et al.* and Riverlane experiments show single-FPGA saturates at $d\approx 21$ due to LUT $>800k$ [18][20]. Multi-FPGA Helios [20] using 5× Xilinx VMK-180 decodes 100 logical $d=5$ patches $>1\ \mu s$ per round. Hybrid tree-grid mapping to SerDes interconnects incurs $40\ ns$ inter-FPGA hop. Critical path: quantization of edge-weight memory reduces bandwidth $30\times$ via Golomb-Rice coding of sparse syndromes [8].

> **Theorem 3 (Throughput bound, backlog):** *If $T_{decode}^{(p99)} < T_{cycle}$ and $T_{decode}$ iid subexponential with tail $\exp(-c t)$, then queue length $Q_T$ remains $O(\log T)$ w.h.p. and decoder is backlog-free.*

Proof uses Cramér bound + union bound over $T$.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup
Built $SC(d)$ rotated lattice via Stim [4] with SI1000: $p_1=p_2=p_m=0.1\%$, idle $p/10$, T1 sampling. Synthesis Vivado 2023.2, target 250 MHz. Host Intel Xeon + PCIe DMA to FPGA.

Datasets: (i) $10^6$ shots phenomenological per $(d,p)$, (ii) $10^5$ circuit-level per point, (iii) BB $10^4$ windowed shots.

### 5.2 Latency Scaling
Helios reproduces sublinear: $d=5$ $98\ ns$, $d=11$ $38\ ns$, $d=21$ $11.5\ ns$ phenomenological — counter-intuitive because larger codes expose more parallelism; cluster diameter grows as $O(\log d)$ below percolation. Circuit-level $d=17$ $23.7\ ns$ matches [11]. Large $d=51$ resource-optimized achieves $544\ ns$ but still $<1\ \mu s$, crossing backlog threshold.

Neural FPGA RN N [22] (distance-3): RNN hidden recurrence $h_{n+1}= \sigma(W_x x_n + W_h h_n + b)$, quantized int6, 4-stage pipeline, $124\ ns$ inference. Closed-loop includes quantum-classical feedback DAC/ADC $~200\ ns$, total $550\ ns$ deterministic on superconducting spool with 10k shots fidelity matching offline MWPM within $2\%$ logical fidelity [22].

For BB $[[72,12,6]]$ [12] : BP-OSD median $2.1\ \mu s$, p99 $18\ ms$; neural median $0.55\ \mu s$, p99 $0.88\ \mu s$ on TPUv5e. Logical error per cycle: BP-OSD $2.3e-4$, NN $5.1e-5$ at $p=0.1\%$. $[[144,12,12]]$ BP-OSD $1.2e-3$, NN $1.7e-3$ but 12-logical saves $12\times$ physical.

### 5.3 Proof of Finite Threshold Preserved Acceleration
Yoshida-Lake-Yamasaki 2026 [17] proved finite threshold for UF under circuit-level local stochastic via refined clustering framework separating error configurations by buffer $B = 2R+1$. Our distributed variant inherits same percolation argument because distributed DSU yields identical clustering ignoring parallelization order (confluent rewriting). Hence:

> **Theorem 4 (Finite threshold invariant):** *Helios distributed UF has $p_{th}^{dist}=p_{th}^{UF} >0$ identical to sequential UF up to equivalence of fusion order. Parallel average runtime quasi-polylogarithmic in code size.*

Implication: parallelism does not erode fault tolerance.

For target $\epsilon_L < 10^{-12}$ per cycle:

## 6 Limitations

*  **Correlated hook errors and Y:** Helios naive UF ignores hyperedge reweight; correlated UF heuristic loses $0.05\%$ effective threshold [8].
*  **Quantization bleed:** int6 NN degrades $p_{th}$ $0.7\%$ -> $0.65\%$ observed. Post-training quantization aware needed [22].
*  **Non-Clifford feedforward:** T-gate injection requires tracking $S$ correction depending on decoder state; PFU approximation fails [22]. Demands deterministic $550\ ns$ loop, not yet scaled to >5 logical.
*  **Connectivity realism:** BB codes assume degree-6 long-range $O(\sqrt{n})$ couplers; superconducting planar limited. Neutral atom Rydberg feasible [15], but shuttling adds latency.
*  **Multi-FPGA synchrony:** SerDes jitter causes $>10\ ns$ nondeterminism unless PTP-synced.
*  **Worst-case backlog tail:** Even exponential tail permits rare burst due to cosmic rays [3]; needs bomb detection and fallback.
*  **Verification:** TLA+ model unverified for actual VHDL; need co-simulation.

No decoder simultaneously achieves *all* of optimal LER, minimal latency, scalability to $O(1000)$ logical, and tolerance to long-range qLDPC.

## 7 Conclusion and Future Directions
We stitched a unifying narrative: *matching* founded surface decoding, *union-find* delivered almost-linear time via erasure mapping, *distributed Helios* reaped $10$–$100\times$ FPGA speedups to $11.5\ ns$, and *neural distillation* tamed qLDPC tail to $<1\ \mu s$, aligning with superconducting cycle times. Evidence:

1.  Below threshold $p$ percolation ensures parallel DSU growth sublinear, deciphering why larger $d$ can be faster.
2.  Correlated MWPM pipelining + sparse blossom preserves threshold within $2\%$ while parallelizing.
3.  BB $[[144,12,12]]$ achieves $>10\times$ savings but needs BP-OSD or transformer; most promising hybrid: BP warm-start + NN classifier for OSD selection, reducing Gaussian elimination to candidate pruning.
4.  Quantized LSTM on FPGA achieves first deterministic $550\ ns$ real-time feedback for $d=3$, enabling non-Clifford circuits [22].

Future work agenda:

*  **End-to-end ASIC:** 7 nm tape-out of UF+NEURAL PE array, $0.1\ nJ$/syndrome, targeting 1000 logical @ $1\ \mu s$.
*  **Single-shot qLDPC:** Leverage metacheck redundancy [16] to reduce syndrome rounds 30→1, requiring decoder aware of roll-forward.
*  **Riverlane-scale OS:** Decoders as microservices sharing BRAM across logical tiles, time-division multiplexed with dynamic allocation.
*  **AlphaQubit-3:** Graph neural net over hypergraph + detector attention, distilled to FPGA HBM.
*  **Formal guarantee:** Prove $T_{wc}$ bound $O(d^3)$ with exp tail implies bounded backlog expectation for any $T$ using Kingman queue.

The trajectory converges: modular superconducting + neutral-atom machines will host $\sim 100$ logical qubits before 2028. The classical co-processor, not the qubit count, will bottleneck fault tolerance. Designing decoders as *real-time cyberphysical systems* with TSN, deterministic latency, and learning, rather than as offline graph algorithms, is the critical path.
