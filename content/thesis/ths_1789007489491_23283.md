# The Rust Borrow Checker: Region-Based Lifetime Inference, Non-Lexical Lifetimes, and the Polonius Datalog Formulation

## Abstract

The Rust borrow checker is the most sophisticated static analysis routinely applied to production software: a whole-function, flow-sensitive, region-based lifetime inference engine enforcing memory safety and data-race freedom at compile time. This thesis reconstructs its lineage, from the original AST-based lexical-lifetime checker through RFC 2094's non-lexical lifetimes (NLL) — which re-grounded regions as sets of points in the Mid-level IR control-flow graph — to the Polonius formulation, a sound re-expression of the problem as Datalog over borrow, outlives, and points-to relations, now stabilizing as Polonius Alpha. We formalize inference as constraint solving over an outlives lattice, explain how MIR exposes true dataflow, characterize borrow-checker diagnostics (E0499, E0502, E0382) as infeasible-region reports, and examine the soundness argument relative to MIR's operational semantics. We exhibit the classic NLL/Polonius precision gaps — conditional reborrows, multi-branch lifetimes — with executable counterexamples, and argue the Datalog turn is a paradigmatic shift toward verifiable, declarative specification of ownership type systems.

## 1. Introduction

Few components of a production compiler carry as much conceptual weight as `rustc`'s borrow checker. It is simultaneously a *type inference engine*, a *dataflow analysis*, and a *constraint solver*. Its task is simple to state: given a function body, decide for every borrow expression the set of program points at which the resulting reference may be *live*, and reject the program if any point witnesses two conflicting live loans, a use after a loan expires, or a move of still-borrowed data [1]. The difficulty lies in being both *sound* (never accept an unsafe program) and *precise* (rarely reject a safe one) at ecosystem scale.

The history of the analysis is a study in representation. The original borrow checker (pre-2018) computed lifetimes as *lexical scopes*: a borrow was live for the entire remainder of its enclosing block. This was simple, compositional, and maddeningly coarse — it rejected programs where a mutable borrow dies before the end of its scope. RFC 2094, non-lexical lifetimes, changed the representation from *scopes* to *regions*: a region became a set of points in the control-flow graph, computed over the Mid-level IR (MIR) by constraint generation plus fixed-point inference [1][4]. Borrows could end mid-scope, and a large class of false positives vanished.

But NLL's formulation was operational and fragile: regions computed by an intricate hand-written inference algorithm with an informal soundness argument. The Polonius project, begun in 2018, asked the radical question: what if the whole analysis were *declarative*? Polonius re-specifies borrow checking as Datalog rules — Horn clauses over relations like `borrowRegion`, `pointsTo`, `outlives`, and `invalidates` — whose least fixed point *is* the borrow checker [3][8]. In 2023 the team reformulated Polonius ("Polonius Alpha") as a minimal re-architecture of NLL rather than a replacement, and as of 2026 that formulation is enabled on nightly rustc ahead of stabilization, with no known remaining soundness or performance blockers [7].

This thesis gives a comprehensive treatment of that arc: (i) region inference as constraint solving over the MIR CFG; (ii) the Polonius Datalog core, including the points-to re-interpretation of regions; (iii) borrow-checker errors as reports on infeasible constraint sets; (iv) the soundness story relative to MIR's operational semantics; and (v) the remaining precision limitations and the road to view types and place lifetimes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Ownership and the affine core

Rust's type system is *affine*: every value has one owner, values move by default, and the compiler tracks moves flow-sensitively. References `&T` / `&mut T` are *borrows* — temporary, non-owning views — governed by two rules: (1) either many shared borrows `&T` **or** one mutable borrow `&mut T` on a place, never both; (2) the referent must outlive the borrow (`'b: 'a` for `r: &'a T` into data of lifetime `'b`). Both are enforced *statically*, per function, before codegen.

### 2.2 From AST scopes to MIR points

The pre-NLL checker ran on the AST and treated each borrow as live for its entire lexical scope — so a borrow dead before end-of-scope still conflicted with later uses [1]. The key move was to analyze *MIR* instead: a CFG-based IR where drops, moves, borrows, and storage liveness (`StorageLive`/`StorageDead`) are all explicit statements and control flow is flattened into basic blocks [4].

In MIR, `let r = &mut v` is an `Rvalue::Ref` assignment at some *location* `B0/3` (block 0, statement 3). The checker no longer asks "in which scope is `r` live?" but "at which *locations* is this loan live?" — a set of CFG points computed by liveness analysis over regions.

### 2.3 Constraint generation: outlives and the lattice

NLL's inference works in two phases. First, *constraint generation* walks MIR and the type-checked types, emitting two kinds of constraints over region variables:

- **Outlives constraints** `'a: 'b`, meaning region `'a` must contain region `'b` (every point in `'b` is also in `'a`).
- **Liveness constraints** `'r` live at location `L`, meaning location `L` must belong to region `'r` — because a reference with region `'r` is *used* at `L`.

Second, *inference* computes the least fixed point: start every region empty, add required points, and propagate through outlives constraints until closure. The result is the minimal region assignment satisfying all constraints — minimality being exactly the precision story: a borrow is live at precisely the points forced by its uses and its outlives obligations [1][5].

> Theorem: (Minimality of NLL inference.) The NLL inference algorithm computes the pointwise-least region assignment satisfying the generated constraints, in the lattice of region variables ordered by subset inclusion.

Minimality makes NLL *complete relative to its own rule set*: if any sound assignment accepts the program, the minimal one does too. All false positives therefore trace to the *constraints themselves* being overly conservative.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology in this thesis is formal-expository with executable illustration:

1. **Representation analysis** — how each generation re-represents the problem: scopes → region sets over MIR points → Datalog relations over borrows.
2. **Constraint-level semantics** — the NLL and Polonius rule sets as constraint systems, showing how outlives constraints propagate liveness and produce conflicts.
3. **Differential testing by construction** — minimal Rust programs distinguishing the three generations (lexical, NLL, Polonius), explaining *why* each boundary sits where it does.
4. **Soundness anatomy** — the soundness argument with Stacked Borrows as the dynamic counterpart, stating precisely what "soundness relative to MIR" means [6].
5. **Source-grounded history** — every claim anchored in primary sources [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

Code listings are genuine Rust (or genuine MIR pseudocode / Datalog / TLA⁺ where labeled); the analysis assumes first-order logic, lattice theory, and basic compiler IRs.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The MIR substrate: why borrow checking moved off the AST

MIR is a *typed*, *CFG-based* IR produced after type checking, in which high-level constructs are desugared: autoref/autoderef become explicit `&` operations, and — critically — every *drop* and every *storage-liveness* transition is an explicit statement [4]. A region is then simply a subset of *locations* `(block, statement-index)`, with no scopes to leak liveness across. Two MIR features deserve special attention:

- **`StorageLive` / `StorageDead`.** The checker treats `StorageDead` as killing all loans on that local — the NLL treatment of unreachable `StorageDead`s required considerable care [1].
- **Two-phase borrows.** `vec.push(vec.len())` desugars into a reservation phase plus activation, modeled as a borrow kind whose mutable use begins only at activation, letting pervasive autoref patterns type-check [5].

### 4.2 Region inference as a constraint problem

Let us now make the NLL algorithm precise. For a function body, let **L** be the set of MIR locations and **R** the set of region variables (one per inferred lifetime in the body, plus one per lifetime parameter, plus `'static`). The analysis computes:

- `live(r) ⊆ L` — the set of locations where region `r` must be live;
- a constraint set `C` of *outlives* requirements `r₁: r₂`, i.e. `live(r₂) ⊆ live(r₁)`.

Generation emits, per MIR event:

| MIR / type event | Constraint emitted |
|---|---|
| Use of `v: &'r T` at `L` | `L ∈ live('r)` |
| Assignment `y = x`, `x: &'a T`, `y: &'b T` | `'a: 'b` at that point |
| Borrow `&'r p` at `L` | `L ∈ live('r)`; loan region is `'r` |
| Call `f::<'a>(x: &'a T)` | fresh region for `'a`; caller's outlives obligations |
| Drop of a value holding `&'r _` at `L` | `L ∈ live('r)` |

Inference computes the least solution: start `live(r) = ∅` (`'static` everywhere; lifetime parameters seeded by uses), then iterate `r₁: r₂ ∈ C ⇒ live(r₁) ⊇ live(r₂)` to fixed point. Monotonicity over a finite lattice guarantees the *least* fixed point — hence minimal regions [1].

Borrow *checking* is a separate pass: for each loan with region `'r`, at each `L ∈ live('r)`, verify no conflicting operation occurs at `L`. This is the *theory of diagnostics*: every E0499 is a witness that the minimal region assignment still contains a conflict — the constraint set admits *no* conflict-free assignment, and the compiler shows you the proof.

### 4.3 NLL's precision ceiling: the conditional-reborrow problem

NLL is minimal *given its constraints*, but the constraints encode a fateful approximation. The canonical example is the conditional reborrow [8]:

```rust
fn get_default<'r>(map: &'r mut HashMap<String, String>, key: String) -> &'r mut String {
    match map.get_mut(&key) {
        Some(v) => v,                       // reborrow flows to return
        None => {                           // ...but NLL keeps it live here too
            map.insert(key, String::new()); // ✗ conflicts under NLL
            map.get_mut(&key).unwrap()
        }
    }
}
```

This is rejected by NLL and Polonius Alpha (future work). Why? The `Some` arm's reborrow requires `'v_region: 'r`, where `'r` is the opaque return lifetime parameter — assumed to extend to any point reachable from function exit, so the outlives constraint drags the borrow's liveness across the *entire rest of the CFG*, including the `None` arm's use of `map`. The arms conflict even though on any *single execution path* there is none.

The root cause is NLL's lack of *path sensitivity*: `'v_region: 'r` applies uniformly at all points. A path-sensitive analysis would observe that on the `Some` path the reborrow flows to the return while on the `None` path the original borrow is dead. Polonius's points-to formulation (Section 4.4) is designed to recover this precision by tracking *which borrows* a region may contain *at each point* (`pointsTo(r, b, p)`), keeping the two paths' borrow sets distinct [8].

### 4.4 Polonius: the Datalog turn

Polonius reframes the analysis as *Datalog relations*: an extensional database (EDB) extracted from MIR and the type checker, plus intensional (IDB) relations defined by Horn clauses whose least model *is* the borrow-check result [3].

The core relations, in the NLL working-group thread's notation, are [8]:

- `borrowRegion(r, b, p)` — region `r` is the borrow region for borrow `b` at point `p`.
- `regionLiveOnEntryToStatement(r, p)` — region `r` is live on entry to the statement at `p`.
- `outlives(p, r₁, r₂, q)` — at `q`, region `r₁` must outlive `r₂`; the extra point `p` makes the relation *location-sensitive*.
- `killed(b, p)` — the path borrowed by `b` was reassigned at `p`, killing the loan.
- `pointsTo(r, b, p)` — the key innovation: at point `p`, a reference with region `r` may contain data derived from borrow `b`. Regions become *sets of borrows*, tracked flow-sensitively per point — a classic points-to analysis with borrows as abstract heap objects.
- `invalidates(b, p)` — borrow `b` is invalidated at `p` (a conflicting access while `b` is live).
- `subset(r₁, r₂, p)` — at `p`, region `r₁`'s borrow set is a subset of `r₂`'s.

Error detection becomes a single query: borrow `b` with `borrowRegion(r, b, _)` is erroneous if some point `p` has both `regionLiveOnEntryToStatement(r, p)` and `invalidates(b, p)`. Everything else is Datalog evaluation.

Three consequences follow. First, **declarative specification**: the checker's meaning is logical rules that can be read, audited, and *modeled* — a Datalog spec translates directly into proof assistants [7]. Second, **separation of concerns**: fact extraction (MIR → EDB) is decoupled from inference, so Polonius Alpha reuses NLL's extraction and replaces only the inference core — which is why stabilization became feasible where the from-scratch rewrite stalled [7]. Third, a **precision roadmap**: the points-to view exposes *why* NLL rejects the conditional reborrow, and per-point `pointsTo(r, b, p)` lets future work make outlives path-aware without redesigning the architecture.

The Datalog core, in the book's notation [3]:

```prolog
% A borrow is live at p if its region is live there and not killed.
loanLiveAt(b, p) :-
    borrowRegion(r, b, _),
    regionLiveOnEntryToStatement(r, p),
    !killed(b, p).

% A loan is invalidated by a conflicting access at a point where it is live.
error(b) :-
    loanLiveAt(b, p),
    invalidates(b, p).
```

The actual rule set is larger — subset propagation, placeholder handling for higher-ranked regions, two-phase borrow activation — but this is the conceptual skeleton: *liveness minus kills, intersected with invalidations*.

### 4.5 Soundness relative to MIR: what the checker actually guarantees

What does it mean for the borrow checker to be *sound*? The precise statement is relative to an operational semantics: **if the borrow checker accepts a MIR body, then every execution of that body is free of undefined behavior attributable to aliasing** — no use-after-free, no aliased mutation, no mutation under an active shared borrow [6].

The argument has two halves:

1. **Type soundness of the region system.** The outlives constraints must be *sufficient*: any execution respects the region assignment. Polonius's declarative rules make this half *mechanically checkable*: each Datalog rule validates against the operational semantics independently.
2. **The dynamic model: Stacked Borrows.** Jung et al.'s *Stacked Borrows* is an operational, lifetime-free aliasing model, deliberately *more permissive* than the checker, so accepted programs trivially satisfy it [6]. Soundness factors: borrow checker ⇒ Stacked Borrows discipline ⇒ no aliasing UB, with erased lifetimes licensing the optimizer's `noalias` assumptions.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 The three-generation differential corpus

Differential corpus of minimal programs (✓ = compiles, ✗ = rejected):

| Program | Lexical (pre-2018) | NLL (2018–) | Polonius Alpha (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Borrow dies before end of scope (Sec. 2.2) | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Conditional reborrow (Sec. 4.3) | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ (future work) |
| `get_default`-style two-arm mutable flow | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Reborrow through `&mut *x` in loop | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Disjoint field borrows (`&mut s.a`, `&mut s.b`) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Two-phase borrow (`v.push(v.len())`) | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Closure capturing disjoint fields | ✗ | ✓ (partial) | ✓ (partial) |

The pattern is instructive: NLL's gains came from *liveness precision*; Polonius Alpha is deliberately *minimal* at stabilization — a re-architecture preserving NLL's accepted language. The precision *frontier* moves only with follow-up work on path-sensitive outlives [7].

### 5.2 Performance: Datalog evaluation at compiler scale

A declarative formulation is only viable if it compiles real code fast. The original Polonius prototype used an off-the-shelf Datalog engine and was *too slow* — naive rule evaluation exploded on large functions [3]. Three responses made it viable:

1. **Rule optimization.** Rewrites of the rule set reduced intermediate relation sizes — e.g., fusing liveness into `pointsTo`.
2. **The Alpha re-architecture.** Instead of evaluating all of borrow checking in Datalog, Polonius Alpha reuses NLL's handwritten fact extraction and replaces only the inference core, bounding the Datalog problem to the location-sensitive fragment [7].
3. **Empirical validation.** Crater runs over the ecosystem corpus found performance "generally acceptable for stabilization" with no known soundness issues [7].

### 5.3 A TLA⁺ sketch of region fixed-point convergence

We model the NLL fixed-point computation in TLA⁺, where the state is the region assignment and the next-state relation applies all outlives propagations simultaneously:

```tla
---- MODULE RegionInference ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Regions, Locations, Outlives
VARIABLES live
Init == live = [r \in Regions |-> {}]
Propagate == live' = [r \in Regions |->
    live[r] \cup UNION {live[s] : s \in {t \in Regions : <<r, t>> \in Outlives}}]
Spec == Init /\ [][Propagate]_live
THEOREM Convergence == Spec => <>[](live' = live)
==============================
```

> Theorem: (Termination.) The `Propagate` step is monotone in the finite lattice `[Regions → SUBSET Locations]`; hence every behavior reaches a fixed point in at most `|Regions| × |Locations|` steps, and that fixed point is the least assignment satisfying the outlives constraints.

This is the formal shadow of the minimality claim in Section 4.2 — and exactly the property the Datalog formulation inherits for free: Datalog's least-fixed-point semantics *is* this computation, generalized from point-sets to borrow-sets.

### 5.4 Soundness proof obligations, stated precisely

A fully mechanized soundness proof must discharge four obligations [7]: (1) **fact-extraction fidelity** — EDB facts *over-approximate* operational behavior; (2) **rule soundness** — each Datalog rule is *valid*, and `outlives` propagation never *shrinks* a region below what some execution requires; (3) **error-query completeness** — any operational aliasing violation is derived by `error(b)`; (4) **erasure coherence** — the optimizer's assumptions (Stacked Borrows) follow from borrow-checker acceptance [6].

Obligations (1)–(3) are where Datalog pays for itself: statements *about Horn clauses*, amenable to proof assistants.

## 6. Limitations

Candor requires enumerating what the current system does *not* do:

- **Conditional reborrows remain rejected.** The motivating precision case for Polonius is still future work even in Polonius Alpha; the location-sensitive rules shipped are a re-architecture, not yet the precision upgrade [7].
- **No path sensitivity.** Regions are sets of points, and outlives constraints apply uniformly. Any analysis that needs "on this path but not that one" — beyond what the points-to relation currently exploits — is out of reach.
- **Higher-ranked regions are approximated.** Placeholders and universes (for `for<'a>` binders) require delicate handling; the Alpha work included explicitly "factoring out higher-ranked concerns" and rewriting invalid universe constraints, indicating this remains a soundness-sensitive corner [7].
- **Diagnostics lag the formulation.** Error messages still derive from NLL-era constraint structures; a Polonius-native diagnostic theory — explaining errors as Datalog derivations — does not yet exist.
- **Interprocedural reasoning is signature-bound.** Borrow checking is per-function; all cross-function reasoning flows through declared lifetime bounds. This is a feature for modularity but a hard ceiling on precision (no whole-program borrow inference).
- **The soundness proof is still informal.** Despite the Datalog formulation being designed for mechanization, the end-to-end mechanized proof remains future work — the "model Polonius Alpha" goal is listed as ongoing [7].
- **Performance cliffs persist.** Datalog evaluation can still behave pathologically on adversarial inputs; the roadmap's "optimize" step is not fully complete [3].

## 7. Conclusion

The journey from lexical scopes to Datalog is a story about *representation determining what can be known*. Lexical lifetimes could not see a borrow dying mid-scope, because scopes were the only vocabulary. NLL supplied the vocabulary of *points in a control-flow graph* and bought a decade of precision. Polonius supplies the vocabulary of *logic* — borrows, outlives, points-to as relations, inference as deduction — buying something more durable than precision: *verifiability*.

The 2026 stabilization of Polonius Alpha [7] is best understood not as the end of the story but as a change of medium. The accepted language barely moves; the *specification* moves from an intricate algorithm to Horn clauses. That move makes the remaining agenda — path-sensitive outlives, conditional reborrows, mechanized soundness, Polonius-native diagnostics — tractable. The next precision breakthrough will come not from a cleverer algorithm but from a more faithful representation. Polonius is that representation, and its least fixed point is still being computed.
