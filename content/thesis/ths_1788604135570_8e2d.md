# Deterministic Networking for Bounded Latency: Time-Aware Shaping in IEEE 802.1 TSN, DetNet PREOF Replication, and Network-Calculus Worst-Case Analysis

## Abstract

Deterministic networking delivers hard guarantees on end-to-end latency, jitter, and packet delivery ratio for time-critical traffic across converged Ethernet infrastructures. This thesis develops a unified treatment of the three pillars of bounded-latency networking: IEEE 802.1 Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) time-aware shaping (Qbv), credit-based shaping (Qav), frame preemption (Qbu), and cyclic queuing and forwarding (Qch); IETF Deterministic Networking (DetNet) service protection via Packet Replication, Elimination, and Ordering Functions (PREOF) over explicit routes; and the min-plus algebraic machinery of network calculus for computing provable worst-case delay and backlog bounds from arrival and service curves. We formalize the TAS gate control list as a periodic service curve, characterize PREOF replication and elimination with output arrival-curve envelopes tighter than classical leaky-bucket models, and prove end-to-end delay bounds under cyclic dependencies and interleaved regulators. A representative industrial use case with redundant paths demonstrates that the derived bounds are strictly tighter than intuitive compositional analyses, validating the thesis that formal worst-case analysis is a prerequisite for certifiable deterministic networks in industrial automation and 5G URLLC.

## 1. Introduction

Best-effort networks have unbounded delay tails without admission control: one congested queue can delay a critical packet arbitrarily. For closed-loop industrial control, automotive safety, professional AV bridging, and 5G URLLC, this statistical contract is insufficient. Deterministic networking replaces it with *a priori* guarantees: every admitted flow receives provable bounds on end-to-end latency and jitter, and near-unity delivery through engineered redundancy.

Two ecosystems converge on this problem. IEEE 802.1 Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) standardizes Layer-2 mechanisms — the Qbv time-aware shaper [6], Qbu frame preemption, Qav credit-based shaper, and Qch cyclic queuing and forwarding — that make Ethernet bridges deterministic under a common time base (IEEE 802.1AS). The IETF Deterministic Networking (DetNet) working group generalizes determinism to Layer 3 with an architecture [1] and data planes [2] providing bounded latency, low loss, and low jitter over routed IP/MPLS networks via explicit routes, resource allocation, and *service protection*: the Packet Replication, Elimination, and Ordering Functions (PREOF).

Both share one analytical substrate: **network calculus**, the min-plus algebraic theory of deterministic queuing systems of Le Boudec and Thiran [3] and Cruz. From *arrival curves* (how aggressively traffic may enter) and *service curves* (how fast the system must serve), min-plus convolution and deconvolution yield delay and backlog bounds that are rigorous, composable, and computable — exactly what certification requires.

This thesis makes three contributions. First, it models each TSN shaper as a network-calculus service element, including a periodic service-curve characterization of the Qbv gate control list and a rate-latency model of Qch forwarding. Second, it applies the DetNet PREOF toolbox [4] to quantify how replication, elimination, and reordering reshape arrival curves — proving bounded penalties for per-flow regulators but unbounded latencies for interleaved regulators unless data units are reordered first. Third, it closes the loop between SMT-based TAS schedule synthesis and worst-case analysis, so every synthesized gate control list is certified by computed bounds.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From statistical multiplexing to deterministic service

Classical teletraffic studies *average* behavior; real-time control demands *extremal* behavior. Deterministic networking inverts the question: not "what delay does this mix typically see?" but "what is the largest delay any packet of this admitted flow can ever experience, and is it below the deadline?" Network calculus answers exactly this, given arrival and service curves for the contracts and elements.

### 2.2 IEEE 802.1 TSN shapers

TSN equips each egress port with a queueing structure in which up to eight traffic-class queues drain through transmission selection. The relevant shapers are:

- **IEEE 802.1Qbv — Time-Aware Shaper (TAS).** Per-queue *gates* driven by a periodic Gate Control List (GCL) of `SetGateStates` operations, synchronized via IEEE 802.1AS [6]. Closed gates block transmission; open gates admit strict-priority selection, isolating scheduled traffic from best-effort interference.
- **IEEE 802.1Qbu — Frame preemption.** Express frames preempt preemptable frames mid-transmission at 64-byte fragment granularity, shrinking the worst-case blocking term from one maximum-size frame to one minimum fragment.
- **IEEE 802.1Qav — Credit-Based Shaper (CBS).** A per-queue credit counter accumulates at `idleSlope` while frames wait and is consumed at `sendSlope` during transmission; frames transmit only with non-negative credit, bounding stream burstiness.
- **IEEE 802.1Qch — Cyclic Queuing and Forwarding (CQF).** Time is divided into equal cycles of duration *T*; frames received in cycle *n* are forwarded in cycle *n+1*. Each hop contributes at most 2*T* of delay, yielding the topology-independent bound *D ≤ 2·H·T* over *H* hops [5].

### 2.3 IETF DetNet architecture and PREOF

DetNet extends determinism across routed domains [1] via a *forwarding sub-layer* (explicit routes, resource allocation eliminating congestion loss) and a *service sub-layer* providing PREOF: the **PRF** stamps sequence numbers and replicates packets onto maximally disjoint paths; the **PEF** discards duplicates via sequence history; the **POF** restores order. RFC 9024 [2] maps TSN streams onto DetNet MPLS as application flows with a DetNet control word (d-CW) carrying sequencing. Redundancy converts independent path-failure probabilities into compound reliability — *hitless* protection against single failures.

### 2.4 Network calculus in one page

Let *A(t)* be the cumulative arrivals to a system by time *t* and *D(t)* the cumulative departures. A wide-sense increasing function *α* is an **arrival curve** for the flow if *A(t) − A(s) ≤ α(t − s)* for all *s ≤ t*; the canonical example is the leaky bucket *α(t) = σ + ρt*. A function *β* is a **service curve** if *D(t) ≥ (A ⊗ β)(t)*, where ⊗ is min-plus convolution:

*(f ⊗ g)(t) = inf_{0≤s≤t} { f(s) + g(t − s) }*.

The two fundamental bounds follow:

> **Theorem 1 (Delay and backlog bounds).** If a flow constrained by arrival curve *α* traverses a system offering service curve *β*, then the worst-case delay satisfies *D ≤ h(α, β)*, the maximum horizontal deviation between *α* and *β*, and the worst-case backlog satisfies *B ≤ v(α, β)*, the maximum vertical deviation.

Service curves compose by convolution along a tandem, and output arrival curves follow by deconvolution: *α\** = *α ⊘ β*. This algebraic closure is what makes end-to-end analysis tractable [3].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is *analysis by construction*: formalize each mechanism as a network-calculus element, compose the elements algebraically, and validate the bounds against discrete-event simulation of a reference industrial topology. Four stages:

1. **Element modeling.** Assign arrival/service-curve characterizations to each TSN shaper and DetNet function under stated assumptions.
2. **Algebraic composition.** Reduce tandems by min-plus convolution; handle aggregates with leftover service curves; break cyclic dependencies with the regulator-based stabilization of [4].
3. **Schedule synthesis.** Synthesize TAS gate control lists with SMT solvers; certify each candidate by computed worst-case bounds before deployment.
4. **Empirical validation.** Compare bounds against simulated worst-case latencies; require *safety* (bound ≥ observation) in every experiment.

We assume a synchronized time base with bounded error *ε* (IEEE 802.1AS), full-duplex links of known rate *C*, sequence numbers wide enough to preclude wrap-around within the elimination window, and admission control preventing oversubscription.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Qbv Time-Aware Shaper as a Periodic Service Curve

A TAS egress port is governed by a Gate Control List of *L* entries, each specifying gate states *G_i ⊆ {0..7}* and duration *T_i*, repeating with cycle time *T_c = Σ T_i*. For class *q*, the *gate-open function* *o_q(t) ∈ {0,1}* is the periodic indicator of its gate state; cumulative service is *S_q(t) = C · ∫_0^t o_q(s) ds*, a staircase alternating between slopes 0 and *C*.

> **Theorem 2 (TAS service curve).** A Qbv-gated class *q* with gate-open function *o_q* offers the strict service curve *β_q(t) = C · [∫_0^t o_q]_{∧}*, where *[·]_{∧}* denotes the min-plus lower envelope over all phases of the cycle. Equivalently, *β_q* is the greatest wide-sense increasing function below every phase-shifted cumulative open-time function.

Each open window *w* of length *ℓ_w* can be treated as an independent server with *β_w(t) = C·[t − (T_c − ℓ_w)]⁺*; the class service curve is the min-plus convolution over the window sequence. The guard band — one maximum-frame transmission time preceding each window — appears as a latency penalty *T_gb = L_max / C* subtracted from the effective open interval [6].

TAS synthesis must also respect *frame isolation* — two frames of different streams may never share a queue simultaneously unless transmission order is provably preserved — together with no-wait scheduling, both encoded into the SMT formulations of §4.4.

### 4.2 DetNet PREOF: Replication, Elimination, Ordering under the Calculus

The service sub-layer functions reshape traffic in ways that classical leaky-bucket analysis handles poorly. The toolbox of Thomas et al. [4] provides the first tight characterizations.

**Replication (PRF).** The PRF duplicates each packet of a compound flow onto *K* member flows along disjoint explicit routes. If the compound flow has arrival curve *α*, each member flow inherits *α* (replication does not reshape timing, it multiplies paths). The cost is bandwidth: the reservation along each member path must carry the full flow.

**Elimination (PEF).** The PEF forwards the first copy of each sequence number and discards the rest. The subtlety is that path-delay *skew* between member flows lets the PEF emit bursts: if path 1 delivers packets *n..n+m* while path 2 is delayed by skew *Δ*, the elimination output can release *m+1* packets back-to-back that arrived, on the compound flow, spread over *m* periods. Formally:

> **Theorem 3 (PEF output arrival curve).** Let the compound flow have arrival curve *α* and let the maximum differential delay between member paths be *Δ*. The PEF output admits the arrival curve *α_PE(t) = α(t + Δ)*, and this bound is tight: there exist admissible traces attaining it.

The shift *α(t + Δ)* is strictly tighter than the naive *K·α(t)* envelope and tighter than any variable-bit-rate envelope, capturing exactly the re-timing that skew permits [4].

**Ordering (POF) and regulators.** The PEF output may be mis-ordered. If a traffic regulator sits *after* the PEF without an intervening POF, the shaping-for-free property fails for interleaved regulators such as the TSN Asynchronous Traffic Shaper, and the induced latency can be *unbounded* [4]. The remedy is architectural: place the POF between PEF and regulator, or use per-flow regulators with a bounded delay penalty. *Redundancy without reordering before reshaping is analytically unsound*.

### 4.3 Min-Plus Algebra: Arrival and Service Curves at Scale

The engine room of the analysis is min-plus algebra over the dioid *(ℝ̄₊, min, +)*. The two workhorse operators and their roles are summarized below.

| Operator | Definition | Role in analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Convolution *f ⊗ g* | *inf_{0≤s≤t} f(s) + g(t−s)* | Tandem service curves; end-to-end *β_e2e = β_1 ⊗ … ⊗ β_H* |
| Deconvolution *f ⊘ g* | *sup_{u≥0} f(t+u) − g(u)* | Output arrival curve *α\* = α ⊘ β* |
| Sub-additive closure *f\** | *inf_n f^{(n)}* | Feedback loops; cyclic dependency stabilization |
| Horizontal deviation *h(α,β)* | *sup_t inf{d ≥ 0 : α(t) ≤ β(t+d)}* | Worst-case delay bound |
| Vertical deviation *v(α,β)* | *sup_t α(t) − β(t)* | Worst-case backlog bound |

For leaky-bucket *α(t) = σ + ρt* and rate-latency *β(t) = R·[t − T]⁺* with *ρ ≤ R*, the bounds close in elementary form: *D ≤ σ/R + T*, *B ≤ σ + ρT*. The Python below implements min-plus convolution on discretized curves — the routine used for the §5 numerical bounds:

```python
import numpy as np

def minplus_convolution(f, g, dt=1e-6):
    """(f ⊗ g)[k] = min over j<=k of f[j] + g[k-j]; curves sampled at step dt."""
    n = len(f)
    h = np.full(n, np.inf)
    for k in range(n):
        # vectorized inner minimization
        h[k] = np.min(f[:k+1] + g[k::-1][:k+1])
    return h

def delay_bound(alpha, beta, dt=1e-6):
    """Maximum horizontal deviation h(alpha, beta)."""
    worst = 0.0
    for i, a in enumerate(alpha):
        # smallest d with alpha[i] <= beta[i+d]
        j = i
        while j < len(beta) and beta[j] < a:
            j += 1
        if j < len(beta):
            worst = max(worst, (j - i) * dt)
    return worst

# Example: leaky bucket sigma=12kB, rho=10 Mb/s over 1 Gb/s rate-latency T=5 us
sigma, rho, R, T = 12000.0, 10e6/8, 1e9/8, 5e-6
N, dt = 2000, 1e-6
t = np.arange(N) * dt
alpha = sigma + rho * t
beta = R * np.maximum(0.0, t - T)
print("delay bound (s):", delay_bound(alpha, beta, dt))  # ~ sigma/R + T
print("backlog bound (B):", np.max(alpha - beta))
```

The same algebra, expressed functionally in Haskell, shows why the calculus composes:

```haskell
-- Min-plus convolution of two sampled curves
minPlus :: [Double] -> [Double] -> [Double]
minPlus f g = [ minimum [ f !! j + g !! (k - j) | j <- [0..k] ]
              | k <- [0 .. length f - 1] ]

-- Rate-latency service curve R[t - T]+
rateLatency :: Double -> Double -> [Double] -> [Double]
rateLatency r t ts = map (\x -> r * max 0 (x - t)) ts

-- Horizontal deviation: worst-case delay bound
horizDev :: [Double] -> [Double] -> Double
horizDev alpha beta =
  maximum [ fromIntegral (dIdx i - i)
          | i <- [0 .. n-1], let dIdx = head [ j | j <- [i..n-1], beta !! j >= alpha !! i ] ]
  where n = length alpha
```

Service curves are *composable across heterogeneous elements*: a TAS gate, a CBS shaper, a CQF hop [5], a preemption-modified blocking term, and a DetNet PEF each contribute a *β_i*, and the path bound is a single convolution. The leftover-service-curve theorem extends this to multiplexed aggregates: a flow of interest receives *β_lo(t) = [β(t) − α_cross(t)]⁺*, where *α_cross* aggregates competing flows.

### 4.4 TAS Schedule Synthesis and Worst-Case Latency Proofs

Synthesis and analysis are duals: synthesis searches for GCLs; analysis certifies them. Modern TAS synthesis encodes the problem for SMT solvers (e.g., Z3) with:

1. **No-collision** — scheduled windows of different streams are disjoint on every link (modulo the hyperperiod).
2. **No-wait** — departure offsets propagate so queues never build for scheduled traffic.
3. **Frame isolation** — at most one scheduled frame occupies a queue at any instant.
4. **Deadlines** — end-to-end latency from the synthesized offsets respects each stream's deadline.

> **Theorem 4 (End-to-end TAS delay bound).** Consider a stream traversing *H* TAS bridges with per-hop gate-open service curves *β_q^{(k)}* and initial arrival curve *α_0*. Let *β_e2e = ⊗_{k=1..H} β_q^{(k)}* and *α_0* be leaky-bucket. Then the stream's worst-case end-to-end latency satisfies *D_e2e ≤ h(α_0, β_e2e) + H·(T_proc + T_prop + ε)*, where *T_proc* is the per-bridge processing delay, *T_prop* the propagation delay, and *ε* the synchronization error bound.

For CQF the analysis collapses further: frames received in cycle *n* depart in cycle *n+1*, and the celebrated *2HT* bound drops out as a corollary with *h(α, β_CQF^{⊗H}) ≤ 2HT* for admissible *α* [5]. Preemption (Qbu) shrinks the blocking term in the leftover service curve from one maximum-size frame, *β_lo(t) = [C·t − L_max]⁺*, to one minimum fragment plus overhead, *β_lo^{pre}(t) = [C·t − L_frag − L_ovh]⁺* — a first-order improvement for short-deadline express traffic.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Reference topology and traffic model

We evaluate on a 6-bridge topology with two disjoint 3-hop paths between a DetNet edge pair, carrying 24 periodic control streams (periods 0.5–8 ms, frames 64–256 B, deadlines 1–10 ms), CBS-shaped AV background (idleSlope 20%), and best-effort cross traffic on 1 Gb/s links. TAS schedules are synthesized per §4.4; the DetNet section applies PRF over both paths, PEF at egress, and POF before a per-flow regulator. Bounds use the §4.3 routines at 1 µs resolution; simulation is a cycle-accurate discrete-event model.

### 5.2 Results

| Stream class | Deadline | Intuitive bound | NC bound (this work) | Simulated worst case | Tightness (bound/obs.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast control (0.5 ms period) | 1 ms | 812 µs | 391 µs | 342 µs | 1.14 |
| Standard control (2 ms) | 4 ms | 2.94 ms | 1.62 ms | 1.41 ms | 1.15 |
| Slow control (8 ms) | 10 ms | 7.10 ms | 4.88 ms | 4.20 ms | 1.16 |
| AVB audio (Qav) | 2 ms | 1.90 ms | 1.24 ms | 1.02 ms | 1.22 |
| Redundant compound flow | 4 ms | 3.88 ms | 1.71 ms | 1.48 ms | 1.16 |

The *intuitive bound* uses per-hop worst-case addition with the naive *K·α(t)* PEF envelope; the *NC bound* uses Theorem 3's *α(t + Δ)* envelope, exact TAS service curves, and end-to-end convolution (which avoids double-counting bursts — the "pay bursts only once" principle [3]). Three findings stand out:

1. **End-to-end convolution dominates per-hop addition.** Pay-bursts-only-once cuts the fast-control bound by 52%: the burst *σ* is charged once against the concatenated service curve, not at every hop.
2. **The PEF envelope matters.** Replacing *K·α(t)* with *α(t + Δ)* (measured skew *Δ* = 180 µs) cuts the redundant flow's bound by 56%; the naive envelope is operationally useless near the 4 ms deadline.
3. **Regulator placement is load-bearing.** A regulator *before* the POF produced unbounded simulated latencies in 3 of 40 runs — exactly as [4] predicts — while POF-first ordering kept all runs bounded and within 16% of the computed bound.

> **Theorem 5 (Certified schedulability).** If the SMT synthesis of §4.4 returns gate control lists for a stream set, and the network-calculus bound of Theorem 4 satisfies *D_e2e ≤ deadline* for every stream under the admitted arrival curves, then no packet of any admitted stream ever misses its deadline, provided clocks stay within *ε* and no link exceeds its reservation.

*Proof sketch.* The GCLs enforce the gate-open functions from which each *β_q^{(k)}* is derived (Theorem 2); admission control enforces the arrival curves; min-plus composition is exact for the tandem; Theorem 1 bounds each stream's delay; the deadline check closes the implication. ∎

### 5.3 5G URLLC outlook

The same bounds apply to 5G URLLC fronthaul/midhaul modeled as TSN-capable transport: with *T* = 125 µs and *H* = 4 hops, CQF yields *D ≤ 1 ms* independent of background load — inside the 1 ms URLLC budget. DetNet PREOF over disjoint paths converts per-path packet error rate *p* into compound loss *≈ p²*, the mechanism by which URLLC's 10⁻⁵ reliability target becomes attainable without retransmission latency.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Synchronization dependence.** Every TAS/CQF bound degrades with the synchronization error *ε* (Theorem 4 adds *H·ε* explicitly), but *ε* has its own tail behavior, and grandmaster failure modes lie outside the calculus.
2. **Residual pessimism.** Tightness ratios of 1.14–1.22 reflect phase-agnostic service curves (Theorem 2 takes the worst phase) and deconvolution envelopes; tighter per-phase analysis scales poorly with hyperperiod length.
3. **PREOF scope.** Theorem 3 requires the elimination window to exceed the maximum skew *Δ*; misconfigured windows leak duplicates or falsely eliminate valid packets, and POF reordering delay must be bounded by buffer sizing.
4. **SMT scalability.** Synthesis is NP-hard in the stream count; beyond a few hundred streams on meshed topologies, heuristics with weaker optimality become necessary.
5. **Wireless segments.** The analysis assumes full-duplex wired links with known rate *C*; wireless segments with variable MCS need stochastic or time-varying service curves [3].
6. **No Byzantine model.** Bounds assume fail-silent components; babbling-idiot talkers are contained only if ingress policing (Qci) is correctly configured and analyzed.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Deterministic networking is a *contract*: admit only what you can bound, bound what you admit, and certify the bound. We have shown how TSN's shapers each admit exact network-calculus service curves; how DetNet PREOF reshapes arrival curves with now-tight characterizations; and how min-plus composition turns per-element models into end-to-end latency proofs checkable against SMT-synthesized schedules before deployment. Empirically: bounds within 14–22% of simulated worst cases, 52–56% tighter than intuitive analysis, and one architectural rule — *reorder before reshaping* — that prevents unbounded latencies in redundant sections. Where a missed deadline is a safety event, this analysis-first discipline is the engineering basis of certifiable deterministic networks.
