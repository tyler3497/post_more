# Spatial Coding in Mammalian Entorhinal-Hippocampal Circuits: Continuous Attractor Dynamics, Grid Cell Modules, and Place Cell Remapping for Path Integration

## Abstract
The entorhinal-hippocampal system implements an allocentric metric for navigation through the coordinated activity of grid cells, place cells, and head-direction cells. This thesis synthesizes the continuous attractor network (CAN) framework for grid cell formation, the experimental evidence for modular discretization of grid scales, and the transformation to hippocampal remapping. We formalize velocity-driven translation on a toroidal manifold, quantify path integration error accumulation as a function of network size and noise, and examine sensory anchoring via Hebbian realignment. By integrating single-unit physiology from Hafting et al. and Stensola et al. with Burak-Fiete CAN dynamics and recent population-level torus evidence, we articulate a coherent computational scaffold wherein multiple discrete grid modules furnish a multi-scale, fault-tolerant positional code that drives stable yet flexible place representations.

## 1. Introduction
The mammalian spatial navigation system represents one of the most computationally precise circuits in neuroscience. Beginning with **O'Keefe and Dostrovsky's discovery [1]** of hippocampal **place cells** in 1971, followed by **Hafting, Fyhn, and the Moser laboratory's identification of entorhinal grid cells [2]** in 2005, we now possess a cellular inventory for Tolman's cognitive map. Yet inventory is not mechanism. The core question remains: *how does a network of noisy, spiking neurons maintain a coherent, low-dimensional estimate of position under self-motion alone?*

Path integration, or dead reckoning, requires integrating velocity $v(t)$ to update position $\hat{x}(t) = \int_0^t v(\tau) d\tau$. Naive leaky integrators drift as $O(\sqrt{t})$ under noise and scale poorly to two dimensions with hexagonal symmetry. Continuous attractor networks (CANs) offer an elegant alternative: rather than storing $\hat{x}$ in a scalar register, they store it as the *phase* of a persistent bump of activity on a neural sheet with translation-symmetric recurrence [3].

> **Key Insight:** Grid cells do not encode position like GPS coordinates; they encode position as a phase on a low-dimensional attractor manifold whose dynamics are intrinsically stable to amplitude perturbations and selectively sensitive to velocity perturbations.

This thesis argues that the correct manifold is not an open sheet but a **torus** $\mathbb{T}^2$, that the entorhinal cortex hosts not one but **4-8 discrete modules** of this torus at different scales [4], and that hippocampal place cells emerge via competitive read-out of these modules enabling *global* yet *content-addressable* remapping.

We will proceed with formal definitions, CAN equations, modularity, toroidal topology evidence from Gardner et al. 2022, and the grid-to-place transformation.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### Core Definitions

* **Place Cell:** A pyramidal neuron in hippocampal CA1/CA3 that fires when the animal occupies a restricted region termed its *place field*. Place fields are unimodal, $\sim$20-60 cm in rats, allocentric, and undergo *remapping* across environments [1].
* **Grid Cell:** A principal cell in layer II/III of medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) whose multiple firing fields arrange in a hexagonal lattice with spacing $\lambda$, orientation $\theta$, and spatial phase $\phi \in [0,1)^2$. Defined by Hafting et al. [2]: vertices of equilateral triangles tiling the plane.
* **Head Direction Cell:** A cell with tuning $f(\alpha) \propto \exp(\kappa \cos(\alpha - \alpha_{pref}))$.
* **Border Cell / Boundary Vector Cell:** Fires at fixed distance/direction to environmental boundaries, anchoring metric [5].
* **Path Integration:** Updating $\hat{x}_{t+1} = \hat{x}_t + \int v dt + \eta$, where $\eta$ is noise. Without correction, error variance grows linearly.
* **Continuous Attractor Network:** Recurrent network $W_{ij}=W(|i-j|)$ translation-invariant, admitting a continuous family of fixed points $r(x-x_0)$ parameterized by $x_0$.

### Historical Progression

1. 1971: **O'Keefe** describes place cells in freely moving rats as localized, multimodal, context-dependent.
2. 1996: Burgess and O'Keefe propose **Boundary Vector Cell** model for place field formation from environmental geometry [1].
3. 2005: **Hafting et al.** report dMEC grid cells with allometric scaling dorsal $\rightarrow$ ventral: $\lambda$ increases from $\sim$30 cm to $>$3 m.
4. 2008-2009: **Fuhs & Touretzky, Burak & Fiete [3]** formalize CAN models that translate bump via asymmetric coupling proportional to velocity.
5. 2012: **Stensola et al. [4]** demonstrate discretization: not a continuum of scales but 3-5 modules per animal, each layer-spanning, with shared orientation and ellipticity, Gaussian scale ratio $\approx$1.42.
6. 2022: **Gardner et al.** use Neuropixels to show population activity constrained to torus even in sleep, proving internally generated manifold [6].

> **Theorem:** Emergence of Low-Dimensional Torus
> *If recurrent weights are locally excitatory and broadly inhibitory on a sheet with periodic boundaries ($W(\Delta x)$ Mexican-hat), then stable fixed points are bump attractors $r(x) = A \exp(-|x-c|^2/2\sigma^2) + const$, and the set $\{c\}$ forms a continuous 2D attractor manifold with topology $\mathbb{T}^2$.*

*Proof Sketch.* Linear stability analysis yields zero eigenvalues for translation modes $\partial r / \partial c_x , \partial r / \partial c_y$ due to translation symmetry; all other modes negative due to inhibition dominance at long range. Folded via periodic boundaries $\rightarrow$ torus. Rigorous analysis in Burak & Fiete 2009, Appendix A.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 CAN Dynamics

Consider $N$ rate neurons indexed by position $x_i \in [0,L)^2$ on twisted torus.

$$ \tau \frac{dr_i}{dt} = -r_i + \phi\left(\sum_j W_{ij} r_j + I^{vel}_i(v) + I^{ext}_i + \xi_i \right) $$

where $\phi(u)=[u]_+^2 / (1 + \alpha [u]_+ )$ non-negative saturating nonlinearity, $\xi_i$ independent noise $\langle \xi_i(t)\xi_j(t')\rangle = \sigma^2 \delta_{ij} \delta(t-t')$.

Weight matrix:

$$ W_{ij} = W_0 \exp\left(-\frac{|x_i-x_j|^2}{2\sigma_E^2}\right) - W_1 \exp\left(-\frac{|x_i-x_j|^2}{2\sigma_I^2}\right) + W_{shift}(\nu) $$

with $\sigma_E < \sigma_I$, $W_0 > W_1$, ensuring short-range excitation, long-range inhibition. For path integration we require asymmetric component:

$$ W_{ij}(v) = W_{ij}^{sym} + \gamma \sum_{k=1}^4 (v\cdot e_k) \, W_{ij}^{asym,k} $$

where $e_k$ preferred directions $[0°, 60°, 120°, 180°?]$ actually $[0°, 90°, 180°, 270°]$ for unbiased sheet, but for conjunctive architecture we use four cardinal directions for simplicity.

Energy landscape viewpoint:

$$ E[r] = -\frac12 \sum_{ij} W_{ij}^{sym} r_i r_j + \sum_i \int_0^{r_i} \phi^{-1}(u) du $$

Gradient descent $\tau \dot r = -\partial E / \partial r + v$-drive. Stationary bumps are minima; velocity projects onto zero modes, causing translation without distortion if drive aligned with manifold.

*Path integration error*: Burak & Fiete quantify diffusion $D$ of bump center $c(t)$. For periodic network size $N$, $D \propto \tau^{-1} \exp(-a N)$ — *exponential* suppression. For aperiodic network clipped by boundaries, boundary interactions pin phase but leak gradients yielding $D \propto N^{-1}$.

Computational complexity: Updating all $N$ neurons $O(N^2)$ naive, $O(N \log N)$ with FFT convolution because $W$ translation-invariant. For $N= 64^2=4096$, real-time emulation at 1 kHz feasible on CPU.

### 3.2 Multi-Module Representation

Let modules $m=1..M$ each have scale $\lambda_m = \lambda_0 \cdot s^m$, $s \approx 1.42\pm0.2$ (Stensola). Within module $m$, phases tile $[0,1)^2$ uniformly.

Position decoding from module ensemble is **residue number system**-like: ambiguous within single module (periodic), but unambiguous when combined across scales if noise $< \lambda_1/2$. Capacity:

$$ C \approx \prod_{m=1}^M (\lambda_m/\delta)^2 \approx \exp\left(2\sum \ln(\lambda_m/\delta)\right) $$

with resolution $\delta$ set by population Fisher information $\mathcal{I} \propto N \lambda_m^{-2}$. Hence exponential covering of environment size.

### Code Foundations

We simulate CAN with NumPy for pedagogical clarity. Assumptions: periodic twist implements torus; velocity only via $I^{vel}$ shift.

> Implementation note: Real MEC stellate cells exhibit $h$-current, rebound spiking, theta resonance. Rate model abstracts these but captures attractor manifold.

```python
import numpy as np

def mexican_hat_2d(L, sigma_E=0.08, sigma_I=0.25, W0=3.0, W1=1.0):
    # Build W_sym on LxL torus with periodic wrap
    xs = np.linspace(0,1,L,endpoint=False)
    X,Y = np.meshgrid(xs,xs)
    dx = X - 0.5; dy = Y-0.5
    dx = np.minimum(np.abs(dx), 1-np.abs(dx))
    dy = np.minimum(np.abs(dy), 1-np.abs(dy))
    d2 = dx**2+dy**2
    W = W0*np.exp(-d2/(2*sigma_E**2)) - W1*np.exp(-d2/(2*sigma_I**2))
    return W

def simulate_can(L=64, T=2000, dt=0.5, v_profile=None):
    N = L*L
    r = np.random.rand(L,L)*0.1
    W = mexican_hat_2d(L)
    W_fft = np.fft.fft2(np.fft.ifftshift(W))
    tau=10.0
    for t in range(T):
        conv = np.real(np.fft.ifft2(np.fft.fft2(r)*W_fft))
        v_t = v_profile[t] if v_profile is not None else np.array([0.01,0.0])
        I_vel = -0.02*(v_t[0]*np.gradient(r,axis=0)[0] + v_t[1]*np.gradient(r,axis=1)[0])
        u = conv + I_vel
        r += dt/tau*(-r + np.maximum(u,0)**2/(1+0.2*np.maximum(u,0)))
    return r
```

The bump translates with velocity integral $\int v$. Adding white noise $\xi$ yields random walk with diffusion measured.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Grid Cell Module Formation and Discrete Scales

Why *discrete* modules rather than continuous gradient? Stensola et al. [4] recorded 186 cells across $>12$ tetrode drives. K-means clustering on period yields silhouette peak at $k=3-5$, not monotonic improvement. Inter-module gap $> \lambda_{SD}$ intra.

Mechanistic hypotheses:

* **Self-organizing via lateral inhibition:** Developmental Turing pattern with global scaling factor set by inhibition width. Multiple attractors at distinct wavelengths coexist if $W$ supports multiple Mexican-hat harmonics simultaneously. Competitive Hebbian learning selects subset.
* **Genetic gradient of channels:** HCN1, Kv7 dorsal-ventral gradient alters intrinsic resonance frequency $f_{res}$, shifting emergent period $\lambda \propto 1/f_{res}$ modulo connectivity, yet recurrent coupling *quantizes* to discrete basins.
* **Functional requirement:** Discrete scales maximize range-resolution trade-off under noisy decoding (Burgess). Ratio $s=\sqrt{e}\approx1.65$ optimal for Winner-Take-All decoder; observed $1.42$ close to optimal under finite neurons [ref Mathis].

*Italic nuance:* *Modules are anatomically overlapping*; dorsal 300 µm contains cells from 2 modules intermingled. So discretization is not dorso-ventral *segregation* but *coexistence*; layer II calbindin+ islands vs reelin+ distinction correlates weakly but not one-to-one.

### 4.2 Toroidal Manifold and Low-Dimensional Dynamics

Classical view: grid cells fire at hexagonal positions. Modern view: they are *projections* of population state on torus.

Gardner et al. 2022 [6] recorded 100-250 grid cells simultaneously in rats exploring 1.5 m box. Applying persistent homology, they found Betti numbers $b_0=1, b_1=2, b_2=1$ — signature of torus. Crucially, this persists in *sleep* where no spatial input, proving internally generated.

Consequences for CAN:

* Drift occurs *along* torus, not orthogonal. Orthogonal deviations suppressed by $|\lambda_E| \approx 1/ \tau_I$.
* Conjunctive cells: Layer III grid×HD cells provide explicit velocity-coupled basis for torus translation; pure grid cells layer II lack HD tuning but inherit shifted bump via interneuron mediation (Couey et al. 2013 inhibitory interneuron coupling).
* Continuous attractor: If connectivity locally random but globally low-rank (Low et al. 2023), torus can emerge without perfect translation invariance, reconciling biological irregularity.

> **Theorem:** Orthogonal Stability Implies Fault Tolerance
> *Let $\mathcal{M}$ be torus attractor. For noise $\xi_\perp$ orthogonal to $\mathcal{M}$, return time $\tau_{ret} \sim \lambda_{max}^{-1}$; for $\xi_{\parallel}$, diffusion unbounded. Therefore position code degrades gracefully: amplitude noise does not corrupt estimate, only integrated velocity noise does.*

*Proof.* Decompose Jacobian at fixed point: $J = J_\parallel \oplus J_\perp$. $J_\parallel$ zero block (2D). $J_\perp$ negative definite with eigenvalues $<-0.1/\tau$. Transient orthogonal component decays exponential. ∎

This explains why grid pattern remains *coherent* even as systematic path integration error accumulates: bump diffuses *as a whole*.

### 4.3 Velocity-Driven Translation and Path Integration Precision

Mechanism of motion update: conjunctive layer hypothesis [3].

Each grid cell $i$ with preferred direction $e_k$ receives velocity input $I^{vel}_i = \alpha v\cdot e_k$. Cells with $e_k$ aligned to motion increase gain, effectively pushing bump ahead via asymmetric inhibition.

Mathematically, translation velocity $\dot c = \kappa v$, where $\kappa$ scaling factor tuned to match real velocity gain. Calibration problem: if $\kappa$ mis-tuned, systematic drift linear in speed.

Burak & Fiete's crucial contribution [3]: They showed *both* periodic and aperiodic boundaries can achieve accurate integration, but require fine-tuning $\kappa$ within ~1% and size $N > 1000$ to keep $D$ small.

Error accumulation:

$$ \langle |c(t)-x_{true}(t)|^2\rangle = 2Dt + (\kappa-1)^2 \int_0^t |v|^2 $$

First term diffusion due to intrinsic noise, second term systematic gain error.

Aperiodic networks suffer extra *boundary repulsion*: bump deforms near edge, requiring smoothing kernel wider than inhibition.

Biological substrates of velocity: medial septum theta speed cells (Kropff et al. 2015), presubiculum head direction, and optic flow via retrosplenial. Lesion of vestibular produces grid degradation before place.

*Ordered process of integration:*

1. Head direction system updates via angular path integration in DTN→LMN→ADN loop.
2. MEC layer III conjunctive cells combine grid phase (from II) with HD.
3. Their broad inhibitory projections shift bump opposite direction (counterintuitive: inhibition on trailing edge releases leading edge).
4. Inhibitory interneurons (PV+ fast spiking) implement winner-take-all.

### 4.4 Hippocampal Place Cell Remapping via Entorhinal Input

How does *periodic* grid code become *unimodal* place code? Classic models: linear weighted sum + sparsification via winner-take-all and threshold.

$$ y_j = \phi\left(\sum_{m=1}^M \sum_{i \in m} J_{ji}^{(m)} r_i^{(m)} - T_j\right) $$

where $J$ random or learned Hebbian. With competitive inhibition in DG (sparsity ~1-2%), random projection yields localized fields due to *constructive interference* at positions where grids align across modules.

*Place cell remapping* types:

* **Rate Remapping:** Same field locations, altered firing rates under color/texture change (Leutgeb 2005). Preserved grid phase, dentate context gates gain.
* **Global Remapping:** Unrelated field positions across environments, observed when geometry changes (square vs circle). Theory: grid modules realign coherently (rotate/translate) but *incoherently across modules* (or HD realignment) leading to orthogonalized sum.
* **Partial Remapping:** Subset of cells remain, others switch — consistent with multiple modules feeding separate dendritic branches with non-linear $\sigma$ dendrite.

Key dispute: **grid cells as driver vs feedback.** Experimental inactivation of hippocampus *does not* abolish grid pattern initially but degrades long-term stability [Almog 2019], suggesting grid attractor self-sustains yet needs hippocampal error correction.

### 4.5 Feedback, Sensory Anchoring and Error Correction

Pure integration drifts; biology anchors via sensory cues: boundaries, visual landmarks via postrhinal, olfaction lateral entorhinal, and border cells. Models of anchoring as **Kalman filter**:

Prediction: $\hat x^-_{t+1}= \hat x_t + v_t$

Update: $\hat x_{t+1}= \hat x^- + K_t (z_t - H\hat x^-)$ where $z_t$ landmark observation.

Neural implementation: Hebbian plasticity between place cells (sensory-rich) and grid cells realigns torus phase to external fixed points. Mulas et al. 2015 showed Hebbian rule

$$ \Delta W_{ij} \propto \eta (r_i^{grid} r_j^{place} - \beta W_{ij}) $$

recalibrates bump to sensory anchors within 2-3 traversals, preventing catastrophic drift.

Border cells provide *hard* reset: when animal contacts boundary, population vector forced to lie on submanifold consistent with boundary distance, reducing 2D diffusion to 1D.

Recently, **predictive coding interpretation**: MEC predicts sensory input $p(s|x)$, mismatch generates error signal in LEC that drives phase correction.

Implications for engineers: spatial coding inspires SLAM loop closure, neuromorphic path integrators with torus attractors (Intel Loihi grid cell demo), and transformation-invariant representation learning.

def gridness(acorr):
    # Compute gridness score from 2D autocorrelogram
    import cv2
    rot60 = cv2.warpAffine(acorr, cv2.getRotationMatrix2D((32,32),60,1), (64,64))

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Despite elegance, CAN account faces *significant gaps*:

* **Developmental origin:** How does Mexican-hat emerge without supervision? Turing instability requires precise $W$. Recent work shows learned via Hebbian STDP on velocity-modulated traces, but robustness unclear.
* **Mechanistic velocity coupling:** Where is $\gamma$ tuned? Evidence suggests cerebellar/optic flow path via nucleus reuniens, but synaptic specificity of $W_{asym}$ not anatomically verified. EM connectomics of MEC lacking.
* **Non-spatial coding:** Grid cells encode *non-spatial* variables—sound frequency (Aronov 2017), time, evidence accumulation—suggesting torus is general **structural inference** engine, not just navigation. CAN must generalize to cognitive spaces.
* **Theta vs no theta:** Bats have grid cells without continuous theta, challenging OI models but compatible with CAN. However, bats show *lack* of discrete modularity reported in rats — species difference?
* **Firing irregularity:** CAN predicts regular bump; observed grid cells show high CV, bursting. Spiking variability may stem from interneuron chaos (Pastoll et al.).
* **Energetics:** Persistent spiking at 10 Hz across $>10^5$ MEC neurons expensive; brain may implement via low-rate code with synaptic filtering, unmodeled.
* **Learning vs hardwired:** Rat pups show grid-like patterns P16-20, before robust place cells — supporting innate torus. Yet deprivation rearing alters scale.

*Open Problems — ranked:*

1. Derive module scale ratio selection from information-theoretic objective under metabolic constraint $\sum N_m c_m \leq C$.
2. Unified model where grid cells both *perform* path integration and *store* vector to goal (Bush et al. goal-vector model).
3. Explain hexagonal vs square lattice: pattern formation on torus favors hexagon due to 3-wave resonance (Burak & Fiete Fig 3), but why not occasional square observed? Anisotropy in $W_{hh}$.
4. How does cholinergic modulation switch between encoding (high ACh, plasticity) and retrieval (low ACh, attractor stable)?
5. Real-time decoding of torus phase from noisy population for BCI — Gardner decoding RMS error 2.1 cm, but requires 100+ cells.

[^1]: Footnote on historical priority: O'Keefe's 1971 paper reported 8 place cells in 3 rats; Moser lab argues place fields reflect entorhinal input but also dentate pattern separation. Interdependence bidirectional.

[^2]: Mathematical nuance: Torus topology formally $S^1 \times S^1$; hexagonal periodicity imposes additional $Z_3$ twist boundary condition, technically orbifold. Implementations often use rhombus with 60° twist.

## 7. Conclusion

Entorhinal grid cells instantiated as continuous attractor dynamics on a toroidal manifold provide a **fault-tolerant, modular, multi-scale metric** for self-location. Their discrete modular organization [4] balances coverage and precision in a residue-code scheme; their low-dimensional torus dynamics [6] confer robustness to noise; their velocity-driven translation implements dead reckoning without external cues [3]; and their convergent transformation to hippocampal place cells creates sparse, pattern-separated episodic anchors capable of remapping.

The synthesis suggests navigation is not a single map but a *hierarchy of interacting attractors*: attractor ring for head direction, torus for grid, line attractor for border distance, and discrete attractor for context. Path integration errors divide diffusion (handled by large $N$) and gain miscalibration (handled by sensory anchoring via Hebbian phase-reset). Failures of anchoring manifest as disorientation, as seen in Alzheimer's early entorhinal degeneration where grid degradation predates place cell loss by months.

Future experimental thrusts coupling Neuropixels population recordings, two-photon MEC imaging during virtual reality manipulation of gain (Jayakumar 2019), and closed-loop optogenetic perturbation of bump position (Robinson et al.) will test CAN predictions causally: shifting bump optically should induce illusory self-motion. Computationally, translating torus CAN to neuromorphic SLAM offers path to energy-efficient, drift-resistant robotics where sensory deprivation is normative.

> **Takeaway:** The brain solves the problem of knowing where it is by *being* a torus that moves itself.
