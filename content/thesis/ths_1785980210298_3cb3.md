# Algebraic Effects and Handlers: Row-Polymorphic Effect Systems in Koka, Multicore OCaml 5, and Evidence-Passing Compilation via Capability Semantics

*Author: anon#4821*

## Abstract

Algebraic effects and handlers reconstruct computational effects as algebraic operations interpreted by user-defined handlers, unifying exceptions, state, nondeterminism, and concurrency in a single abstraction. This thesis synthesizes Plotkin and Pretnar's foundational semantics with three contemporary implementations: Koka's row-polymorphic effect inference, Multicore OCaml 5's fiber-based runtime, and Xie and Leijen's evidence-passing compilation. We formalize handler semantics as homomorphisms from free algebras, present Koka's duplicate-label row calculus $\langle l \mid \mu \rangle$ with Hindley-Milner inference $\Gamma \vdash e : \tau \mid \epsilon$, and dissect OCaml 5's one-shot continuations and DWARF-compatible stack management. Central to modern performance is the shift from searching handler stacks to *evidence-passing*, where an implicit capability vector $w : \mathsf{evv}\; \epsilon$ witnesses handler existence. This capability semantics yields tail-resumptive optimization and direct C compilation without runtime search. We provide comparative benchmarks, type-safety proofs, and identify open challenges in effect safety, linearity, and polymorphism over evidence.

## 1 Introduction

Computational effects have historically been *ambient* — exceptions, I/O, and mutable state are handled by dedicated language constructs with ad-hoc semantics. Monads in Haskell restored compositionality but imposed strict sequencing and transformer complexity. **Algebraic effects**, introduced systematically by Plotkin and Power and extended to handlers by Plotkin and Pretnar [1], offer a third way: *effects as operations of an algebraic theory*, and *handlers as models* of that theory.

An effect signature $\Sigma$ declares operations $\mathsf{op} : A \to B$. A computation $M$ may *perform* $\mathsf{op}(V)$ without knowing its implementation. Enclosing $M$ in `handle M with H` installs a handler $H$ that interprets each $\mathsf{op}$ as a clause $\mathsf{op}(x,k) \mapsto M_{op}$, where $k$ reifies the *delimited continuation*.

> **Why does this matter now?** Multicore OCaml 5 shipped effect handlers to millions of developers to implement lightweight concurrency [3]. Koka demonstrates full effect inference in production compilers targeting C without GC [2]. And capability-passing translations [4][5] achieve *abstraction without regret*. The convergence of theory and industrial implementation merits a unified account.

This thesis contributes:

- A consolidated formal syntax and operational semantics for deep and shallow handlers.
- A deep dive into **row polymorphism** as implemented in Koka, contrasting it with Rémy/Wand extensible records [8].
- A runtime analysis of **Multicore OCaml 5**, including fiber layout, one-shot linearity, and `Effect.Deep` vs `Effect.Shallow`.
- A full account of **evidence-passing compilation** from Xie et al. [6][7] and its realization as capability semantics in Effekt [5].
- Proof sketches of type safety and effect safety, and empirical performance analysis.

*Italicized claims denote engineering folklore formalized herein; **bold** denotes definitional novelty.*

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Monads to Algebraic Theories

Moggi's monadic semantics modeled computation $A ! \Sigma$ returning $A$ with effects $\Sigma$ as $T_\Sigma A$. For algebraic effects, $T_\Sigma$ is the *free monad* on signature $\Sigma$ modulo equations $\mathcal{E}$. Plotkin and Pretnar [1] observed:

> **Theorem (Handlers as Homomorphisms).** *For signature $\Sigma$, each handler $H : \Sigma \Rightarrow D$ induces unique $T_\Sigma$-algebra homomorphism $\hat{H} : T_\Sigma A \to D$ such that handling is homomorphism application.*

Formally, `handle (op(V)) with H` reduces to $M_{op}[V/x][\lambda y.\, \text{handle }k\,y\text{ with }H / k]$ for deep handlers, forwarding shape via continuation wrapping.

This separates *syntax* (operations) from *semantics* (handlers). Exceptions become:

```haskell
-- Koka-like
effect exn { fun throw(e: string): a }
fun risky(): int <exn> { throw("boom") }
fun safe = handle risky() { throw(e) -> 42 }
```

Operations are *algebraic* when they commute with sequencing in prescribed way, unlike `call/cc`.

### 2.2 Effect Systems Taxonomy

| System | Effect Representation | Polymorphism | Composition |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|-------------|
| Java checked exns | sets | none | nominal |
| Haskell MTL | monad transformers | via typeclasses | quadratic |
| Koka [2] | rows $\langle l_1,...,l_n \mid \mu \rangle$ | row-polymorphic + duplicate labels | structural, inference |
| Effekt [5] | capabilities $\mathsf{Cap}$ | second-class fns | contextual modal |
| OCaml 5 [3] | extensible GADT `eff` | none (dynamic) | open handlers |

Row polymorphism [8] originated with Wand (1987) for records, refined by Rémy (1994) and Leijen (2005) for scoped labels. Its adaptation to effects, pioneered in Koka, treats $\epsilon$ as a row of labels.

### 2.3 Multicore OCaml and Koka Lineage

Multicore OCaml [3] aims for minimal overhead: OCaml has no prior non-local control beyond exceptions. Sivaramakrishnan et al. require: *(i) ≤1% slowdown on non-handler code, (ii) DWARF unwind compatibility, (iii) efficient fibers*. Koka [2] aims for *Perceus reference counting + evidence translation → C without runtime*.

Both converge on capability semantics: a handler instance is a *capability* that must be lexically available to perform.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Formal Syntax & Handler Semantics

**Syntax**:

$$
\begin{align*}
V &::= x \mid \lambda x.M \mid c(V_1..V_n) \\
M &::= \mathsf{return}\;V \mid V_1\,V_2 \mid \mathsf{let}\;x\leftarrow M_1\;\mathsf{in}\;M_2 \\
  &\mid \mathsf{op}(V) \mid \mathsf{handle}\;M\;\mathsf{with}\;H \\
H &::= \{\mathsf{return}\;x \mapsto M_r ;\; \overline{\mathsf{op}_i(x,k)\mapsto M_i}\}
\end{align*}
$$

**Typing**: $\Gamma \vdash M : A ! \epsilon$ where $\epsilon$ is effect row. Rule `T-Op`:

$$
\frac{\mathsf{op}: A_{\text{op}}\to B_{\text{op}}\in\Sigma \quad \Gamma\vdash V:A_{\text{op}}}{\Gamma\vdash \mathsf{op}(V): B_{\text{op}} ! \langle \mathsf{op}\mid\epsilon\rangle}
$$

**Operational semantics** – small-step with handler stack $K$:

- `handle (return V) with H ⟶ M_r[V/x]`
- `handle E[op(V)] with H ⟶ M_{op}[V/x][ (\lambda y. handle E[y] with H) / k ]` if $\mathsf{op}\notin\text{ops}(E)$ and $\mathsf{op}\in\text{dom}(H)$

> **Theorem 1 (Handler Correctness).** *Deep handling $\text{handle}(-)\text{ with }H$ is unique homomorphism extending $M_r$ and satisfying operation clauses as algebra operations. Shallow handling does not re-wrap $k$.*

Deep handlers automatically tail-recursively handle continuations; shallow handlers enable single-shot state machines but require explicit recursion. Koka uses deep by default; OCaml exposes `Effect.Deep` and `Effect.Shallow`.

Continuations are constrained:

- In OCaml 5: *one-shot* and *linear* — invoking `continue k` twice raises `Invalid_argument`. Permits fiber reuse.
- In Koka: *multi-shot* permitted (cloneable evidence), but tail-resumptive handlers compile to loops.

```ocaml
(* OCaml 5 – direct style concurrency *)
open Effect
open Effect.Deep

type _ eff += Fork : (unit -> unit) -> unit eff
type _ eff += Yield : unit eff

let run main =
  let queue = Queue.create () in
  let enqueue k = Queue.add k queue in
  let rec spawn f = match_with f () {
    retc = (fun () -> match Queue.take_opt queue with Some k -> continue k () | None -> ());
    exnc = raise;
    effc = fun (type a) (e: a eff) ->
      match e with
      | Fork g -> Some (fun (k: (a,_) continuation) ->
          enqueue k; spawn g; continue k ())
      | Yield -> Some (fun k -> enqueue k; 
          match Queue.take_opt queue with Some k' -> continue k' () | None -> ())
      | _ -> None }
  in spawn main
```

### 4.2 Row Polymorphism in Koka

Koka's novelty is **row polymorphism with duplicate labels and scoped labels** [2][8]. Effect rows:

$$
\epsilon ::= \mu \mid \langle \rangle \mid \langle l \mid \epsilon \rangle
$$

where $l ::= \mathsf{exn} \mid \mathsf{div} \mid \mathsf{st}\langle h\rangle \mid l_1$ is a label, $\mu$ a row variable. Duplicate labels are *distinguished by nesting*; `⟨exn,exn|μ⟩` is not equal to `⟨exn|μ⟩` — scoping resolves which `throw` matches which handler.

**Koka inference example**:

```koka
effect state<h> {
  fun get(): h
  fun set(h): ()
}

fun increment(): <state<h>> ()
  set(get() + 1)

// inferred: forall <h, mu> (a: int) -> <st<h> | mu> int
fun map(xs: list<a>, f: a -> <st<h> | mu> b): <st<h> | mu> list<b> {
  match xs { Nil -> Nil; Cons(x,xx) -> Cons(f(x), map(xx,f)) }
}
```

Critically, *row-polymorphic openness* provides effect polymorphism for free:

$$
\text{map} : \forall \alpha\beta\mu.\; (\text{list}\langle\alpha\rangle, \alpha\to\langle\mu\rangle\beta) \to\langle\mu\rangle \text{list}\langle\beta\rangle
$$

No explicit effect variable needed in `map` — it is polymorphic over tail $\mu$, solving monad transformer lifting. Leijen 2014 proves principal types with algorithm W extended for rows.

**Effect elimination**: `run : (() -> ⟨st⟨h⟩ | μ⟩ a) -> μ a` with `h ∉ fv(μ,a)` encapsulates state, analogous to Haskell `runST`, leveraging row scoping to ensure heap does not escape. This depends on *labeled* heap regions `h`.

### 4.3 Multicore OCaml 5 Effect Implementation

OCaml 5 implements handlers via **fibers** — heap-allocated stack segments (~16 words header + variable OCaml stack). OCaml stack ↔ C stack interaction must preserve DWARF.

Key design decisions [3]:

1. **No callee-saved registers**: OCaml uses caller-saved only, making `try`/`effect` installation a single push of exception/effect handler frame.
2. **Heap-allocated continuations**: `perform e` captures current fiber as continuation object with `exn_bucket` for GC roots.
3. **One-shot check**: header word has `CONT_MARK`. Second `continue` triggers fail.

Performance: Sivaramakrishnan et al. evaluate on opam switch benchmarks; mean overhead **1.02%** on code without handlers, vs **+12%** for naive CPS. Effective user-code uses 2-4 cycle handler lookup via cache of `(effect, fiber)` in `caml_state`.

Unimplemented feature — *effect safety* — intentionally omitted in OCaml 5.0 [discuss.ocaml.org 8554]:

```rust
// Rust pseudo for OCaml runtime
struct Fiber {
  parent: *mut Fiber,
  exn_handler: *mut ExnFrame,
  eff_handler: *mut EffFrame,
  stack_top: Value*,
}
fn perform(eff: Eff) -> ! {
  let mut cur = current_fiber();
  loop {
    if let Some(h) = cur.eff_handler.find(eff.tag) {
      cur.save();
      let k = make_cont(cur);
      h.invoke(eff, k)
    } else {
      cur = cur.parent; // bubbling
    }
  }
}
```

OCaml exposes low-level API without syntax: `Effect.perform`, `Effect.Deep.try_with`. Future effect types planned to rule out unhandled perform as exception.

### 4.4 Evidence-Passing Compilation via Capability Semantics

Xie, Brachthäuser, Schuster, Hillerström, Leijen — *Effect Handlers, Evidently* [6] and *Generalized Evidence Passing* [7] solve performance: **searching handler stack on every `perform` is O(depth)**.

#### From Handler Stack to Evidence Vector

Translate source $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ into System $F^{\text{ev}}$ where each term carries $w : \mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon$.

$$
\llbracket \Gamma \vdash e : \tau \mid \epsilon \rrbracket = w : \mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon \vdash \llbracket e \rrbracket : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket
$$

Operationally:

$$
\mathsf{evv}\;\langle l_1,...,l_n \mid \mu \rangle = \{ l_1 : \mathsf{ev}\;l_1, ..., l_n : \mathsf{ev}\;l_n, \mu : \mathsf{evv}\;\mu \}
$$

Where $\mathsf{ev}\;l$ is triple `(m, h, w')` — prompt marker, handler closure, handler evidence vector. Performing `op ∈ l` becomes:

```haskell
-- F^ev pseudo
perform op w = 
  let ev = w.l in           -- O(1) projection, not search!
  ev.h.op ev.m w'
```

No dynamic search; `w` threading is *capability passing* [4][5].

**Capability semantics** view [5]: effect type is not *what computation does*, but *which capability it requires*. In Effekt:

> Effects as Capabilities: effect types express which capabilities a computation requires [5].

This reverses variance: `A => B` (impure fn) vs `A -> B` (pure fn) in Effekt; second-class functions prevent capability escape.

#### Compilation Pipeline

Xie & Leijen [7]:

1. $\lambda_{\text{eff}} \to \lambda^p$ (multi-prompt delimited control with dynamic markers)
2. $\lambda^p \to \lambda^{\text{ev}}$ (evidence vectors, tail-resumptive optimization via `traverse`)
3. $\lambda^{\text{ev}} \to \text{System F}$ (monadic or direct-style)
4. System F → C (Perceus ref counting, no GC)

**Tail-resumptive optimization**: If handler is `op(x) -> k(e)` where `k` is called in tail position once, *no full continuation capture* needed. Koka detects automatically; stack allocation suffices.

```haskell
-- Koka monotonic counter, tail-resumptive
effect counter { fun inc(): int }
fun count(p: () -> <counter|e> a): <e> (a,int) 
  with handler {
    var c := 0
    inc() -> { c := c+1; resume(c) }
  }
```

Becomes in C:

```c
// Generated Perceus C – no heap closure
struct evv_counter { int64_t* counter; };
int kk_counter_inc(evv_counter* ev) { return ++(*ev->counter); }
```

**Formal rule** – evidence application:

$$
\frac{\Gamma; w:\mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon \vdash e : \tau}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda w.e : \mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon \to \llbracket\tau\rrbracket}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1 : \mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon\to\tau \quad \Gamma\vdash e_2 : \mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon}{\Gamma\vdash e_1\,e_2 : \tau}
$$

Preservation: $\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket$ is type-preserving and semantics-preserving up to $\beta$.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Safety Proofs (sketch)

Following Leijen [2] Lemmas 5.3–5.7:

> **Lemma (Effect Safety).** If $\Gamma\vdash e : \tau \mid \langle\rangle$ (total), then $e$ never performs unhandled operation at runtime.

Proof by induction on evaluation via evidence: total implies empty $\mathsf{evv}$, but $\mathsf{perform}$ requires $w.l$; contradiction. Koka enforces via typechecker.

OCaml 5 does *not* enforce statically yet — `perform` of unhandled effect raises `Unhandled`. Progress still holds modulo exception.

Preservation: If $\cdot\vdash M : A!\epsilon$ and $M\longrightarrow N$ then $\cdot\vdash N : A!\epsilon$. Key case: operation clause substitution preserves row unification due duplicate label scoping.

### 5.2 Performance Evaluation

Compiled from published benchmarks [3][4][6]:

| Benchmark | Handlers (search) | Ev-passing (Koka 3) | OCaml 5 fibers | Capability (Effekt) |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|----------------|---------------------|
| `count` 10M | 1420 ms | 88 ms (16×) | 210 ms | 95 ms |
| `n-queens` | 870 ms | 112 ms | — | 130 ms |
| `amb` | 310 ms | 45 ms | 98 ms | 52 ms |
| Web server (Eio) rps | — | — | 124k (1.8× Lwt) | — |

Tail-resumptive reduces `state` from O(n) heap allocs to in-place `Int` mutation in Koka's Perceus backend.

Space: evidence vectors allocated on stack proportional to lexical handler depth, not call depth; typical depth ≤3.

### 5.3 Expressivity Proof

All of `async/await`, `generators`, `probabilistic choice` encodable as handlers. Example `yield` in OCaml:

```ocaml
type 'a seq = unit -> 'a node
and 'a node = Nil | Cons of 'a * 'a seq

let to_seq (iter: (('a -> unit) -> unit)) : 'a seq =
  let module M = struct effect Yield : 'a -> unit end in
  let iter' = fun () -> iter (fun x -> perform (M.Yield x)) in
  (* handled lazily via suspension *)
  ...
```

Uniform encoding replaces ad-hoc builtins.

## 6 Limitations & Open Challenges

- **Effect safety gap in OCaml**: 5.0 shipped without row types; unhandled effects are runtime exceptions. Adding polymorphic effect variables à la Koka conflicts with OCaml's value restriction and separate compilation.

- **Capture and accidental handling**: With polymorphic rows `∀μ. ⟨op|μ⟩`, instantiating μ with `op` can cause inner handler to capture operation intended for outer. Koka solves via lexical scoping of duplicate labels; Effekt via branded capabilities; OCaml via generative effect declarations (`type _ eff += E`). Unification incomplete.

- **Linearity vs multi-shot**: OCaml enforces one-shot to enable cheap fibers; cloning breaks resource safety. Koka allows multi-shot but Perceus must trace clones. Linear capability system needed [Brač 22].

- **Performance of non-tail-resumptive**: Full continuation capture still expensive (GC scan of fiber). Xie et al. show evidence passing does not eliminate `yield bubbling` when not tail-resumptive; worst-case O(n) frames.

- **Interaction with GADTs / dependent types**: Row unification with GADTs is undecidable in general; Koka restricts to first-order.

- **Debuggability**: Stack traces across handlers lose structure in evidence translation — marker `m` recovery needed for source mapping.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Algebraic effects and handlers matured from Plotkin and Pretnar's categorical insight [1] into industrial features. **Row polymorphism** [2][8] provides principal inference and seamless effect polymorphism without transformers. **Multicore OCaml 5** [3] proves retrofitting can be backward-compatible and performant. **Evidence-passing compilation** [6][7] and its capability reading [4][5] achieve central optimization: turning dynamic handler search into static evidence projection $w.l$, enabling compilation to C and tail-resumptive handlers as loops.

The *capability* perspective — effects as requirements, handlers as providers of evidence — unifies these strands: type $\tau \mid \epsilon$ denotes computation needing $\mathsf{evv}\;\epsilon$, handling supplies evidence. Future work must close safety gap in OCaml, reconcile linearity with multi-shot, and scale row inference to dependent systems.

*Abstraction without regret* shifts from aspiration to engineering reality when evidence is passed explicitly.
