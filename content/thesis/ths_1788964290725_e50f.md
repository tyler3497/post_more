# Space Situational Awareness and Orbital Debris Tracking: Conjunction Assessment, Multi-Sensor Fusion, and Stochastic Catalog Uncertainty Propagation

## Abstract

Space situational awareness (SSA) is the discipline of detecting, tracking, and characterizing every resident space object (RSO) so that collisions can be predicted and avoided. More than 40,000 objects are now tracked by the U.S. Space Surveillance Network, while statistical models estimate on the order of one million fragments between 1 and 10 cm and over a hundred million below 1 cm. This article develops the full SSA pipeline: orbital dynamics and catalog representations (TLE/SGP4 versus high-fidelity numerical special-perturbations propagation); all-on-all conjunction screening and time-of-closest-approach determination; collision probability (Pc) computation for short-term encounters following Foster, Akella–Alfriend, Patera, Alfano, and Chan, including long-term and non-Gaussian extensions; multi-sensor orbit determination fusing radar, optical, and laser-ranging data; covariance realism and maneuver detection; and the operational burden imposed by mega-constellations. We prove the covariance-realism consistency proposition and the B-plane Pc integral formulation, present a Python implementation of miss-distance and linear covariance propagation, and close with the fundamental limits — force-model error, untracked lethal debris, and the self-sustaining collisional cascade first identified by Kessler and Cour-Palais.

## 1 Introduction

On 10 February 2009, the defunct Russian satellite *Cosmos 2251* collided with the operational *Iridium 33* spacecraft at 776 km altitude, generating more than 2,300 tracked fragments and demonstrating that conjunction risk is not a theoretical concern. A decade of mega-constellation deployment has since multiplied the traffic: the number of catalogued objects has grown by tens of thousands, and operational conjunction screening now produces thousands of *conjunction data messages* (CDMs) per day against individual large constellations [11]. Each warning demands a decision — maneuver or accept — under uncertainty that is itself uncertain.

SSA answers three coupled questions: *where is everything*, *where will it be*, and *how confident are we*. The first is the domain of the Space Surveillance Network (SSN) and its sensors; the second is orbit propagation; the third is covariance realism. The practical currency of the field is the *probability of collision*, Pc, computed at the time of closest approach (TCA) between a primary and a secondary object. Pc below roughly 10^-4 is usually accepted; above it, operators maneuver — each maneuver costing propellant, mission time, and, at constellation scale, fleet-wide coordination overhead.

This article treats SSA as an end-to-end estimation problem. Section 2 reviews orbital dynamics and catalog representations. Section 3 develops the conjunction-assessment methodology, from screening to the principal Pc algorithms. Section 4 deep-dives into propagator fidelity, covariance realism, multi-sensor fusion, maneuver detection, and the mega-constellation burden. Section 5 proves the core theorems and presents a reference implementation. Section 6 states the fundamental limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Orbital dynamics and propagation

A satellite's motion is governed by the two-body problem perturbed by Earth's oblateness (principally J2), atmospheric drag, solar radiation pressure (SRP), third-body gravity from the Moon and Sun, and higher-order geopotential terms. The workhorse analytic theory is SGP4/SDP4, derived from Brouwer–Lyddane mean-element theory with drag and resonance extensions, and distributed with the *two-line element* (TLE) catalog of mean Keplerian elements [10]. SGP4 evaluates in microseconds and is the only practical propagator for all-on-all screening of tens of thousands of objects, but its accuracy degrades rapidly: kilometer-level along-track errors after a few days are typical, and the model carries no rigorous covariance.

High-fidelity *special perturbations* (SP) numerical integration — Cowell or Encke formulations with 60×60 or higher geopotential, NRLMSISE-class atmospheric density, cannonball or panel SRP models, and solid-Earth/ocean tides — achieves tens-of-meters accuracy over a week for well-observed objects [10]. The dominant residual error source beyond 3–5 days is atmospheric density mismodelling during variable solar activity, which acts almost entirely along-track. Operators therefore maintain a two-tier architecture: SGP4/TLE for coarse screening, SP propagation with full covariance for high-interest conjunctions.

### 2.2 The catalog and its sensors

The public U.S. catalog (Space-Track) contains roughly 40,000+ tracked objects, mostly larger than 10 cm in LEO. *Statistical* environments — NASA's ORDEM 3.2 and ESA's MASTER — extend below the tracking threshold using radar beam-park statistics, returned surfaces, and breakup models [9]. The SSN's sensor mix is complementary:

- **Phased-array radars** (Space Fence on Kwajalein, the legacy FPS-85 and Globus II) provide all-weather, day-night LEO custody with high revisit.
- **Mechanical tracking radars** (Haystack and HAX, X-band) stare at fixed elevation to sample the small-debris population statistically and calibrate environment models.
- **Optical telescopes** (GEODSS sites at Socorro, Diego Garcia, and Maui) cover the GEO belt where radar sensitivity is insufficient.
- **Satellite laser ranging** (ILRS network) yields centimeter-level range to cooperative targets with retroreflectors, anchoring precise orbit determination.

No single sensor closes the problem: radars give accurate range and range-rate but poor angular resolution at range; optical gives exquisite angles but no range; laser gives range only to cooperative targets. Fusion is therefore mandatory, not optional.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Conjunction screening and TCA determination

All-on-all screening of *N* ≈ 40,000 objects implies ~10^9 pairs, so cascaded filters are used: an apogee–perigee overlap pre-filter, then coarse time-stepped distance checks with SGP4, then fine root-finding on the relative-distance function *d(t)* to locate TCA. At TCA the relative position and velocity define the *encounter frame* (B-plane): the plane perpendicular to the relative velocity vector **v**_rel, with miss distance *d_min* = |**r**_rel(TCA)| projected into it.

### 3.2 Collision probability for short-term encounters

The classical short-term encounter assumes (i) rectilinear relative motion during the encounter, (ii) Gaussian position uncertainty, and (iii) velocity uncertainty negligible relative to position uncertainty. Under these assumptions the 3-D problem collapses to a 2-D integral over the combined hard-body disk in the B-plane.

> **Theorem:** *(Foster–Chan Pc integral.)* Let the combined position uncertainty at TCA be Gaussian with B-plane covariance **C** (2×2) and nominal miss vector **μ** = (ξ, ζ). Let *R* be the combined hard-body radius. Then the probability of collision is
>
> *Pc* = (2π√det **C**)^-1 ∬_{x²+y² ≤ R²} exp(−½(**r**−**μ**)ᵀ**C**^-1(**r**−**μ**)) d**r**,
>
> i.e., the integral of the bivariate Gaussian miss-distance density over the hard-body disk.

*Proof sketch.* Rectilinear motion reduces the encounter to a single plane crossing; marginalizing the Gaussian state along **v**_rel leaves a bivariate Gaussian in the B-plane [2]. A collision occurs iff the realized B-plane crossing falls within the hard-body disk. ∎

Five algorithm families evaluate this integral:

| Method | Formulation | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foster (1992)** [2] | Polar 2-D quadrature in the B-plane, 0.5° angular steps | Reference standard; used for ISS/Shuttle | O(10^4) integrand evaluations; slow |
| **Akella–Alfriend (2000)** [5] | Closed-form series approximation | Analytic, fast, accurate for small *R*/σ | Degrades for large disks or small miss |
| **Patera (2001)** [6] | 1-D contour integral around hard-body ellipse | Mathematically equivalent to Foster, faster | Assumes miss > *R* |
| **Alfano (2005–2007)** [3] | Series expansion in *R*/σ with error-function terms | Fast; review benchmarked all methods | Series truncation error at extreme ratios |
| **Monte Carlo** | Direct sampling of the Gaussian (or GMM) | Handles non-Gaussian PDFs, long-term encounters | 10^6–10^8 samples for Pc ~ 10^-6; expensive |

Alfano's 2007 survey remains the definitive benchmark comparing accuracy and speed across these methods [3]. For *long-term* encounters — where relative motion curves appreciably during the encounter or objects linger near each other — Chan's monograph develops the full treatment, including non-spherical (ISS-shaped) hard bodies and time-integrated formulations [4]; Serra et al. recently recast the instantaneous Pc via characteristic-function inversion for improved numerical stability [7]. Operationally, the 18th Space Defense Squadron computes Pc with a Foster-family algorithm documented for Space-Track users [8].

### 3.3 Orbit determination and sensor fusion

Orbit determination (OD) is weighted nonlinear least squares (batch) or sequential filtering (extended/unscented Kalman filter) over heterogeneous measurements:

1. Collect radar range/range-rate, optical right-ascension/declination, and laser ranges over an arc.
2. Fit a trajectory with an SP force model, estimating the state plus consider parameters (drag coefficient, SRP coefficient).
3. Output the state *and* its covariance — the indispensable input to Pc.

Multi-sensor fusion weights each observation by its inverse noise covariance; optical angles constrain the orbit plane, radar ranges constrain energy and epoch, laser ranges anchor absolute scale. The pipeline from raw sensor tasking through OD to catalog update and CDM generation is illustrated above.

### 3.4 Stochastic uncertainty propagation

Covariance at epoch is useless unless it can be propagated to TCA. Three approaches dominate:

- **Linearized (STM):** **P**(t) = Φ(t,t₀)**P**(t₀)Φ(t,t₀)ᵀ. Cheap; valid for short arcs and small uncertainty.
- **Unscented transform:** 2n+1 sigma points propagated through the full nonlinear dynamics, then recombined. Captures second-order effects of drag nonlinearity at modest cost.
- **Polynomial chaos expansion (PCE):** projects the stochastic state onto orthogonal polynomial bases of the random inputs, giving a surrogate valid over long propagations and enabling analytic moment extraction. Costly to construct, cheap to evaluate.

The choice is governed by the *nonlinearity index*: drag-dominated LEO arcs of several days with large initial uncertainty break linearization first in the along-track component.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Analytic versus numerical propagation: the covariance bottleneck

TLE/SGP4 screening is necessary but insufficient for decision-quality Pc: SGP4 carries no covariance, so operators either attach empirically derived error statistics (e.g., from historical TLE-vs-precision-ephemeris comparisons binned by orbit regime) or re-fit an SP orbit from the underlying tracking data. The modern best practice — exemplified by the 18 SDS *special perturbations* catalog — maintains numerically integrated states with full covariances and issues CDMs in the CCSDS standard format with covariance included [8]. The bottleneck is not dynamics knowledge but *calibrated uncertainty*: an SP propagator with a wrong drag model produces a confident wrong answer.

### 4.2 Covariance realism

> **Theorem:** *(Covariance-realism consistency proposition.)* Let {**e**_i} be orbit-determination errors (estimated minus truth) with predicted covariances {**P**_i}. If the estimator is consistent, the Mahalanobis distances *m*_i = **e**_iᵀ**P**_i^-1**e**_i satisfy E[*m*_i] = tr(**I**) = *n* (the state dimension), and the empirical distribution of *m*_i matches χ²_n. Systematic deviation implies mis-modeled process noise or unmodeled biases.

*Proof sketch.* Under the Gaussian assumption, **e**_i ~ N(0, **P**_i) exactly when the filter's process and measurement noise models are correct; the quadratic form of a Gaussian is χ²_n with mean *n*. Vallado and Seago formalized this test for operational catalogs, and it remains the standard diagnostic: covariances that are systematically "too small" (the common failure mode for TLE-derived statistics) produce Pc values that are both wrong and overconfident [10]. ∎

In practice, realism is enforced by *covariance calibration*: scaling or inflating process noise (especially drag) until the χ² test passes on independent truth data such as laser-ranging residuals or operator ephemerides.

### 4.3 Sensor comparison

| Sensor | Type / band | Regime | Sensitivity / role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Space Fence** (Kwajalein) | S-band phased array | LEO | Tracks objects down to ~10 cm class; highest LEO revisit |
| **Haystack** | X-band mechanical dish | LEO (staring) | Detects ~1 cm-class debris statistically; environment-model calibration |
| **HAX** | Ku-band (co-located) | LEO (staring) | Complements Haystack for small-debris sampling |
| **GEODSS** (3 sites) | 1-m optical telescopes | GEO / deep space | Angles-only GEO custody; no range |

The staring radars never track individual small objects — they measure *flux*, constraining ORDEM/MASTER below the catalog floor [9]. This is how we know the lethal-but-untracked population (1–10 cm) numbers in the hundreds of thousands to a million: it is inferred, not catalogued.

### 4.4 Maneuver detection

Operational satellites maneuver; debris does not (except via drag and SRP). Losing custody after an unannounced maneuver is a leading cause of "lost" objects and false conjunctions. Detection methods include:

- matched-filter correlation of post-maneuver tracks against impulsive-burn hypotheses;
- sparse estimation of Δ**v** from TLE discontinuities or radar residual signatures;
- operator-provided ephemeris sharing (the cooperative solution, increasingly mandated for large constellations).

A missed maneuver corrupts both the mean state and the covariance: the filter absorbs the burn as process noise, inflating **P** and potentially masking a real conjunction behind a large, meaningless Pc dilution.

### 4.5 The mega-constellation collision-avoidance burden

A constellation of *M* satellites against a catalog of *N* objects generates O(*M*·*N*) screened pairs; with *M* ≈ 8,000+ (Starlink alone, still growing) the daily CDM volume per operator reaches the thousands. Mitigations in operational use:

1. **Autonomous onboard collision avoidance** — Starlink satellites maneuver without ground-in-the-loop, using uploaded CDMs and onboard GPS ephemerides.
2. **Operator ephemeris sharing** — sharing predicted (maneuver-inclusive) trajectories collapses the covariance of the primary object by orders of magnitude relative to ground-only tracking.
3. **Pc threshold tiering** — routine screening at 10^-5 with human review only above 10^-4, plus covariance-based dilution guards.

The burden is asymmetric: the constellation operator absorbs thousands of screenings to protect a few hundred genuine risks, while every maneuver perturbs the shared environment (new CDMs for everyone else) and consumes propellant budgeted against a fixed satellite lifetime.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Reference implementation: miss distance and linear covariance propagation

The following self-contained Python computes the TCA miss distance from two Cartesian state vectors under the rectilinear (short-term) assumption and propagates a covariance linearly with a two-body state-transition approximation:

```python
import numpy as np

MU = 398600.4418  # km^3/s^2

def miss_distance(r1, v1, r2, v2):
    """B-plane miss distance and TCA offset via linear relative motion."""
    r_rel = np.asarray(r1) - np.asarray(r2)
    v_rel = np.asarray(v1) - np.asarray(v2)
    dt_star = -np.dot(r_rel, v_rel) / np.dot(v_rel, v_rel)  # TCA offset [s]
    r_tca = r_rel + v_rel * dt_star
    d_min = np.linalg.norm(r_tca)
    v_hat = v_rel / np.linalg.norm(v_rel)
    d_bplane = r_tca - np.dot(r_tca, v_hat) * v_hat  # B-plane miss vector
    return d_min, dt_star, d_bplane

def stm_twobody_cw(r, v, dt):
    """6x6 STM via Clohessy-Wiltshire linearization about a circular orbit."""
    n = np.sqrt(MU / np.linalg.norm(r) ** 3)
    nt = n * dt
    s, c = np.sin(nt), np.cos(nt)
    Phi = np.zeros((6, 6))
    Phi[0, 0] = 4 - 3 * c;      Phi[0, 3] = s / n;          Phi[0, 4] = 2 * (1 - c) / n
    Phi[1, 1] = 1;              Phi[1, 3] = -2 * (1 - c) / n; Phi[1, 4] = (4 * s - 3 * nt) / n
    Phi[2, 2] = c;              Phi[2, 5] = s / n
    Phi[3, 0] = 3 * n * s;      Phi[3, 3] = c;              Phi[3, 4] = 2 * s
    Phi[4, 0] = -6 * n * (1 - c); Phi[4, 3] = -2 * s;       Phi[4, 4] = 4 * c - 3
    Phi[5, 2] = -n * s;         Phi[5, 5] = c
    return Phi

def propagate_covariance(P0, r, v, dt):
    """Linear covariance propagation: P(t) = Phi P0 Phi^T."""
    Phi = stm_twobody_cw(np.asarray(r), np.asarray(v), dt)
    return Phi @ np.asarray(P0) @ Phi.T

# Example: two LEO objects, 6x6 covariances (km, km/s), propagate 1 orbit
r1 = np.array([6678.0, 0.0, 0.0]); v1 = np.array([0.0, 7.725, 0.1])
r2 = np.array([6678.5, 2.0, 0.3]); v2 = np.array([0.05, 7.720, -0.08])
d_min, dt_star, d_bp = miss_distance(r1, v1, r2, v2)
P0 = np.diag([0.5**2]*3 + [5e-4**2]*3)  # 500 m / 0.5 mm/s 1-sigma
P_tca = propagate_covariance(P0, r1, v1, dt_star)
print(f"d_min = {d_min:.3f} km, TCA offset = {dt_star:.1f} s")
print(f"B-plane miss = {np.linalg.norm(d_bp):.3f} km")
print(f"along-track 1-sigma at TCA = {np.sqrt(P_tca[1,1]):.3f} km")
```

For the example geometry the linear TCA offset is a few hundred seconds and the along-track uncertainty — the dominant term — grows visibly even over that short arc, illustrating why drag-driven covariance growth, not the mean dynamics, usually decides Pc.

The altitude distribution of catalogued objects shows the classic double-humped LEO structure with concentrations near 800 km (Sun-synchronous band, FengYun-1C and Cosmos–Iridium fragments) and 1,400 km, plus the GEO ring. Environment models calibrated against Haystack/HAX staring data extend the census below the tracking floor: NASA's ORDEM 3.2 is the engineering reference for sub-10-cm flux [9], while ESA's annual environment report tracks the catalogued population's continued growth and the rising maneuver burden on operators [11].

### 5.3 The Kessler cascade

Kessler and Cour-Palais showed in 1978 that collisional fragmentation could become the dominant debris source, with fragments begetting fragments in a self-sustaining cascade once spatial density crosses a critical threshold [1]. The mechanism is now observed in miniature: the FengYun-1C (2007) and Cosmos–Iridium (2009) breakups each added thousands of fragments, measurably increasing the conjunction rate in their altitude bands.

![Kessler syndrome collisional cascade schematic](/thesis/ths_1788964290725_e50f-3.webp)

The cascade condition can be stated quantitatively: with spatial density *n*, mean collision cross-section σ, and relative velocity *v*_rel, the per-object collision rate λ = *n*σ*v*_rel must remain below the removal rate from atmospheric drag. In the 800–1,000 km band, drag removal times are centuries while λ has been climbing with catalog growth — the band where most analysts locate the highest long-term instability risk. Active debris removal of a handful of massive derelicts per year is the only intervention studies show can stabilize the projection.

## 6 Limitations

1. **The untracked lethal population.** Objects between 1 and 10 cm are largely invisible to the SSN yet carry enough kinetic energy (~10 km/s relative velocity) to destroy a satellite. No screening system protects against what it cannot see; shielding (Whipple bumpers) and statistical risk budgeting are the only defenses.
2. **Force-model error dominates long arcs.** Atmospheric density forecast error during geomagnetic storms can displace LEO predictions by kilometers within days. Covariance realism (§4.2) mitigates overconfidence but cannot create information that the physics withholds.
3. **Non-Gaussianity.** Breakup fragments, maneuver residuals, and long-propagated drag uncertainty are not Gaussian. Gaussian-mixture and PCE methods exist but are not yet operational at catalog scale.
4. **Data latency and custody.** Sensors revisit a given object on timescales of hours; a maneuver between revisits is invisible until the next track. Autonomous constellations that do not share ephemerides export this uncertainty to everyone else.
5. **Threshold politics.** A Pc of 10^-4 as a maneuver threshold is a convention balancing propellant against risk, not a derived optimum; different operators choose 10^-5 to 10^-3, and dilution-region pathologies (Pc *decreasing* as covariance grows) can mask genuinely dangerous events.

## 7 Conclusion

Space situational awareness is estimation under deep uncertainty: the dynamics are known to high precision, but the initial conditions, the force-model parameters, and the sub-catalog population are not. The field's central results — Foster's B-plane integral and its faster descendants [2][3][6], covariance-realism diagnostics [10], multi-sensor fusion architectures, and stochastic propagation via sigma points and polynomial chaos — form a coherent pipeline from photons on a detector to a maneuver decision. The pipeline works: catastrophic collisions remain rare, and operational screening now runs at industrial scale.

It works, however, only where it can see. The conjunction-assessment enterprise is bounded below by sensor physics (the ~10 cm LEO tracking floor), bounded in time by atmospheric unpredictability, and bounded in the aggregate by the Kessler instability [1]. Mega-constellations have converted SSA from a cataloging problem into a traffic-management problem, and the collision-avoidance burden now falls on autonomous systems and cooperative data sharing rather than on any single sensor or algorithm. The next decade's decisive advances will not be a better Pc integral — the mathematics is mature — but calibrated uncertainty at scale, universal ephemeris sharing, and removal of the massive derelicts that seed the cascade.
