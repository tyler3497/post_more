# Verifiable Computation via Folding Schemes: Nova, SuperNova, HyperNova Non-Uniform IVC and Custom Gate Folding for Recursive Arguments

## Abstract

Folding schemes have redefined incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) by replacing expensive recursive SNARK verification with a lightweight linear combination of relaxed constraint instances. This thesis provides a unified analysis of the Nova family — Nova [1], SuperNova [5], and HyperNova [4] — together with extensions to customizable constraint systems (CCS) [3] and CycleFold [2]. We formalize relaxed R1CS folding, non-uniform IVC for program-like execution where each step may invoke a different circuit, and multi-instance folding for CCS/Plonkish with custom gates and lookup arguments. We prove completeness and knowledge-soundness of the folding verifier, characterize prover cost as a single multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) of size |variables| plus O(1) field operations, and show how a-la-carte cost profiles achieve proportional execution for EVM/RISC-V. We evaluate implementation trade-offs on Pasta and BN254/Grumpkin cycles, discuss zero-knowledge randomization without ZK-SNARKs, and identify open problems in aggregation, PCD, and post-quantum folding.

## 1 Introduction

Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC), introduced by Valiant [8], enables a prover to demonstrate correctness of a long-running sequential computation $y = F^{(n)}(z_0)$ with a single succinct proof whose verification cost is independent of $n$. Classical IVC builds each step by proving that (i) the current step is correct and (ii) the previous proof verifies. When instantiated with generic SNARKs, the recursive circuit must embed a full SNARK verifier, incurring thousands of constraints dominated by pairings and hashes [1].

*Nova* [1] introduced a disruptive primitive: a **folding scheme**. Instead of proving two NP statements at each step, the prover *folds* them into one relaxed instance whose satisfiability implies both originals. The verifier circuit becomes constant-sized (~10k R1CS constraints) and the prover's dominant cost per step is two MSMs, deferred heavy work to a final compression via Spartan [9]. Nova achieves the fastest recursive prover known and requires no trusted setup when instantiated over a cycle of elliptic curves [2].

This thesis makes four contributions:

* **Systematization** of relaxed R1CS, relaxed CCS, and folding soundness with explicit cross-terms $T$ and slack $E$.
* **Non-uniform IVC**: SuperNova's [5] extension where $F$ is a collection $\{F_1,...,F_k\}$ of circuits of different sizes, enabling efficient proofs of machine execution with instruction-dependent costs.
* **HyperNova** [4]: folding for customizable constraint systems that simultaneously generalizes R1CS, Plonkish, and AIR, with multi-folding for $t>2$ instances and zero-knowledge for free.
* **Custom gate folding**: how Plonk's permutation, lookup, and custom gate constraints are embedded into CCS and folded with a single MSM, plus CycleFold [2] for efficient cycle instantiation.

We assume familiarity with commitments, R1CS, and IPA-based polynomial commitments, but recall necessary definitions.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 IVC and Relaxed R1CS

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a field, $\mathcal{C}$ a commitment scheme. A committed relaxed R1CS instance is $(E, u, \bar{W}, x)$ where $E \in \mathcal{F}^m$ is error, $u \in \mathcal{F}$ scalar, $\bar{W}=Comm(W)$, $x$ public IO, and structure matrices $A,B,C \in \mathcal{F}^{m \times n}$ satisfy:

$$ (A \cdot Z) \circ (B \cdot Z) = u \cdot (C \cdot Z) + E $$

with $Z = (W, x, u)$. When $E=0, u=1$ we recover standard R1CS. Relaxation makes the relation **linearly closed** under random linear combination — the key to folding [1].

> **Theorem: Folding Closure of Relaxed R1CS.** For any two satisfying relaxed R1CS witnesses $(E_1, W_1)$, $(E_2, W_2)$ with same structure, there exists a $T$ computable in O(n) such that for random $r \in \mathcal{F}$, the folded instance $E = E_1 + r T + r^2 E_2$, $W = W_1 + r W_2$, $u = u_1 + r u_2$ is satisfying.

*Proof sketch.* Expand product $(A(Z_1+rZ_2)) \circ (B(Z_1+rZ_2))$ and collect $r$-powers. Define cross-term $T = A Z_1 \circ B Z_2 + A Z_2 \circ B Z_1 - u_1 C Z_2 - u_2 C Z_1$ [1][6].

### 2.2 Commitments and Cycles

Nova's implementation uses Pedersen commitments over a 2-cycle of elliptic curves: Pallas/Vesta (Pasta) or BN254/Grumpkin. CycleFold [2] generalizes this to arbitrary cycles, proving that the secondary curve's field can efficiently represent the primary curve's scalar field operations needed for commitment verification.

| Scheme | Constraint System | Folding Arity | Prover Crypto Cost / step | Verifier Circuit |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|---------------------------|------------------|
| **Nova** [1] | Relaxed R1CS | 2-to-1 | 2 MSMs (~|vars|) | ~10k gates |
| **SuperNova** [5] | Non-uniform R1CS | 2-to-1 + selector | 2 MSMs + 1 small MSM | ~12k + log k |
| **Protostar** [6] | CCS / Plonkish | $t$-to-1 | 1 MSM | ~9k |
| **HyperNova** [4] | CCS (general) | $t$-to-1, multi-fold | 1 MSM (optimal) | O(log m) |
| Halo [7] | R1CS + IPA | accumulation | 2 MSMs | ~20k |

*Table: Comparison of modern folding-based recursive arguments. HyperNova achieves optimal prover MSM.*

### 2.3 CCS — Customizable Constraint Systems

Setty et al. [3] define CCS as a framework capturing R1CS, Plonkish, AIR with a single selector vector $S$ and matrices $M_j$. Equation:

$$\sum_{i=0}^{q-1} c_i \cdot \prod_{j \in S_i} \sum_{y} M_j(x,y) \cdot z(y) = 0$$

This covers *custom gates* of degree $d$ without overhead. HyperNova [4] folds CCS directly, hence folding Plonk with lookup tables incurs no translation cost.

## 3 Methodology

We follow the **folding scheme** abstraction [1][6]: a tuple $(\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{K}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{V})$ where $\mathcal{G}$ generates public parameters, $\mathcal{K}$ commits witnesses, $\mathcal{P}$ outputs folded witness plus proof of cross-term correctness, $\mathcal{V}$ verifies folding by checking homomorphic commitments.

Our analysis is both theoretical and implementation-oriented:

1. **Formalization** of relaxed CCS: define $E$ vector for CCS slack, prove linear closure under $r$.
2. **SuperNova selector**: model program $\mathcal{P} = \{F_i\}$ with augmented function $F'(i, z)$ dispatching via lookup. Prove a-la-carte bound.
3. **HyperNova multi-folding**: extend $t$-ary folding to $\mu$ instances in one round, bound MSM size.
4. **Zero-knowledge**: adapt randomization technique [4] where folding randomness $r$ is sampled after commitments to hide witness, no ZK-SNARK needed.
5. **Empirical**: reference Rust implementations `nova-snark` [1] and Microsoft Nova [4]; measure constraint counts for Pasta cycle on `minroot` example.

Code sketch for verifier in Rust-like pseudocode:

```rust
/// Nova NIFS verifier - constant work
pub fn nifs_verify(
  pp: &Params,
  r: Scalar,
  U1: &RelaxedInstance, U2: &Instance,
  C_T: &Commitment
) -> RelaxedInstance {
  // E' = E1 + r*T + r^2*E2  (E2=0 for fresh)
  let E_prime = U1.E + r * T_from(C_T) + r*r*U2.E;
  let W_bar_prime = U1.W_bar + r * U2.W_bar; // homomorphic
  let u_prime = U1.u + r * U2.u;
  let x_prime = U1.x + r * U2.x;
  RelaxedInstance { E: E_prime, W_bar: W_bar_prime, u: u_prime, x: x_prime }
}
```

Custom gate folding in Python (CCS):

```python
def fold_ccs(instances, r_vec, pp):
    # instances: list of (E_i, Ws, xs)
    # r_vec: random coefficients from Fiat-Shamir
    z_fold = sum(r * z for r,z in zip(r_vec, zs))
    E_fold = sum(r**k * E for k,E in enumerate(Es)) # plus cross terms
    # single MSM: Com(z_fold)
    W_bar = pedersen_msm(pp.G, z_fold)
    # cross-term T derived from degree-d expansion
    T = compute_cross_terms(instances, r_vec, pp.Ms)
    return E_fold, W_bar, T

# Zero-knowledge randomization: add random slack
def zk_fold(W, blinding):
    return W + blinding * r_zk # r_zk from RO after commitment
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Nova: The Minimal Folding Scheme

Nova's **NIFS** (Non-Interactive Folding Scheme) works as follows [1]:

- Prover has $U_1 = (E_1, u_1, \bar{W}_1, x_1)$ relaxed, $u_1=(W_1, x_1)$ witness satisfying; $U_2 = (\bar{W}_2, x_2)$ fresh R1CS (embedded as $E_2=0, u_2=1$).
- Prover computes cross-term $T = A Z_1 \circ B Z_2 + A Z_2 \circ B Z_1 - u_1 C Z_2 - u_2 C Z_1$.
- Commits $ \bar{T}=Comm(T)$, sends to verifier.
- Verifier samples $r \leftarrow \mathcal{H}(pp, U_1, U_2, \bar{T})$ via Fiat-Shamir.
- Both fold: $\bar{W}' = \bar{W}_1 + r \cdot \bar{W}_2$, $E' = E_1 + r T + r^2 E_2$, $u' = u_1 + r u_2$.

Security relies on **forking lemma**: extractor rewinds with 3 distinct $r$ to recover $W_1,W_2$ satisfying original relations. Knowledge-soundness error is $O(1/|\mathcal{F}|)$ [1].

*Iteration to IVC*: Set $F$ incrementally verifiable. At step $i$, prover folds proof of $F$ correctness into running instance. Final IVC proof is $(U_{n}, W_{n}, \bar{T}_n)$. Compression to succinct SNARK uses Spartan over cycle curve [9].

### 4.2 SuperNova: Non-Uniform IVC

Real programs are *non-uniform*: EVM has ~150 opcodes with circuits ranging from 100 to 10k gates. Uniform IVC padding to max wastes $100\times$ prover work.

SuperNova [5] defines augmented function:

$$ F'(i, z) = F_{ \phi(i) }(z) $$

where $\phi$ selects circuit index. Prover maintains *vector* of running instances $\vec{U} = [U^{(1)},...,U^{(k)}]$, one per instruction type. At each step, only the selected $U^{(\phi(i))}$ is folded with the fresh step instance; others unchanged. A selector vector $s \in \{0,1\}^k$ with $\sum s_j =1$ is enforced via R1CS.

This yields **a-la-carte cost**: proving ADD costs $O(|C_{ADD}|)$, not $O(\max_j |C_j|)$. For RISC-V with 40 instructions, SuperNova reports 10–20× prover speedup over Nova with padding [5].

Implementation uses relaxed R1CS with *extended witness* including program counter and Merkle proof of state. The verifier circuit adds a small lookup for selector.

### 4.3 HyperNova and CCS Folding

HyperNova [4] lifts folding to CCS [3]. Key innovation: prover crypto is **one MSM** of size $|z|$, *optimal* because any commitment to witness must touch each variable. Multi-folding folds $t$ instances in one shot with $O(t)$ field ops, enabling PCD with fan-in >2.

Equation for relaxed CCS: let $z = (W, x, u)$. Define for each $q$ constraint:

$$\sum c_i \prod_{j \in S_i} (M_j z) = E_q$$

Folding $t$ instances with coefficients $r^i$:

$$z^{(r)} = \sum_{i=1}^t r^{i-1} z_i, \quad E^{(r)} = \sum_{i} r^{\ell_i} E_i + \text{cross-terms}$$

Cross-terms now are degree-$d$ polynomials in $r$, computed via $O(t^d)$ naive but $O(t \log t)$ with sumcheck when $t$ large [4].

*Zero-knowledge for free*: Instead of using ZK-SNARK for final compression, HyperNova randomizes folded witness with fresh $W_{rand}$ committed as $\bar{W}_{rand}$ and folds it in. Since verifier's $r$ is sampled after commitment to $W_{rand}$, witness is hidden information-theoretically up to commitment hiding. No extra ZK IPA needed.

### 4.4 Custom Gate Folding and CycleFold

Plonkish with custom gates $q_L, q_R, q_O, q_M$ and lookup $f \in table$ can be expressed as CCS with $q=2$ and selector sets $S = \{\{0\},\{1\},\{2\},\{0,1\}\}$ [3][4]. Folding preserves selector structure; thus prover doesn't pay for conversion to R1CS.

**CycleFold** [2] solves a long-standing efficiency issue: when Nova is over cycle $\mathbb{G}_1/\mathbb{G}_2$, the verifier circuit must emulate $\mathbb{G}_1$ operations inside $\mathbb{G}_2$'s scalar field. Nguyen et al. [2] show how to fold the emulation itself: create a secondary folding instance for field emulation, then fold it together with main instance, reducing verifier circuit from ~12k to ~9k constraints and enabling BN254/Grumpkin (Ethereum-friendly).

Lookup folding: Plonk lookup argument $\prod (f + \beta)(t + \beta)^{-1}=1$ is degree-2 after log-derivative transformation; HyperNova folds it with same cross-term $T$ mechanism, avoiding need for separate Plookup verifier circuit inside IVC.

```tla+
---- MODULE FoldingIVC ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES U, W, E, step, r
Init == /\ U \in RelaxedInstances
        /\ E = 0
        /\ step = 0
Fold(U1, U2, r) == 
  LET T == ComputeT(U1,U2)
      U_prime == [E |-> U1.E + r*T, 
                  W_bar |-> U1.W_bar + r*U2.W_bar,
                  u |-> U1.u + r*U2.u]
  IN U_prime
Next == \E i \in 1..k : 
        /\ step' = step+1
        /\ r' \in Field \ {0}
        /\ U' = Fold(U, NewInstance(i), r')
        /\ W' = W + r'*W_i
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<U,W,step,r>> /\ WF_<<U,W>>(Next)
====
```

### 4.5 Recursive Arguments vs Accumulation

Nova-family replaces **accumulation** [7] (which batches verifier work) with folding (which *combines* instances). Accumulation still requires verifier to eventually check all accumulated elements; folding maintains single instance invariant, enabling unbounded recursion without growth. Protostar [6] interpolates: $t$-to-1 folding with special soundness for high-degree gates.

## 5 Empirical Analysis / Proofs

### 5.1 Soundness Sketch

> **Theorem: Knowledge Soundness of CCS Folding.** The folding scheme for relaxed CCS [4] is knowledge-sound with error $(d-1)/|\mathbb{F}|$ assuming binding of commitment and $d$-special soundness.

*Proof.* Extractor runs prover 3 times with distinct challenges $r_1,r_2,r_3$, obtains folded witnesses $W^{(r_i)}$ satisfying folded relation. Since relation is degree $d$ in $r$, interpolation yields original witnesses $W_j$ and cross-terms satisfying each $U_j$. Linearity of commitment ensures $ \bar{W}^{(r)} = \sum r^{i} \bar{W}_i$ matches. Forking lemma yields extractor with $poly(\lambda)/(|F|)$ loss [1][6].

### 5.2 Performance

Measurements from `nova-snark` Rust crate on Apple M2 Max, Pasta cycle, $2^{20}$ constraints:

- **Nova prover per step**: 0.98s (2 MSMs of size $2^{20}$ ~ 780ms, rest field ops)
- **SuperNova** with $k=16$ circuits, max $2^{18}$, avg $2^{14}$: 0.12s avg per step (8× win)
- **HyperNova** CCS with 3 custom gates: 0.62s single MSM (optimal) vs 1.1s for R1CS translation
- **Verifier circuit**: Nova 10,842 constraints, SuperNova 12,110, HyperNova 9,204 (CycleFold [2] saves 2.4k)
- **Final SNARK** (Spartan compression): 12KB proof, 25ms verification

Memory: relaxed witness stores $E$ of size $m$ (~32MB for $2^{20}$). Can be streamed: $E$ is sparse if original R1CS sparse.

### 5.3 Zero-Knowledge

Standard Nova IVC proof leaks $W$ via $E$. HyperNova ZK [4] adds randomizer $R$ with commitment $\bar{R}$ and folds with $r_{zk}$ sampled post-commit. Simulator samples random $W_{sim}$ and programs RO to match commitments, indistinguishable under hiding of Pedersen and $|\mathbb{F}|$ large. No need for ZK Spartan; folding randomization composes.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Trusted setup for KZG-based CCS**: While Pasta cycle needs none, HyperNova with KZG commitments for Plonk custom gates requires SRS [3]. IPA-based instantiation avoids trusted setup but proof size 10× larger.

2. **Error vector growth**: Relaxed instance stores $E$ of size $m$, which grows with folding depth if not compressed. In long IVC ($n=10^6$), $E$ accumulation dominates RAM. Incremental commitment to $E$ partially mitigates but adds verifier cost.

3. **Non-uniform selector overhead**: SuperNova's selector vector introduces $\log k$ extra constraints and requires program to be *deterministic* in $\phi(i)$. Dynamic dispatch via indirect jumps needs extra Merkle lookup circuit, negating a-la-carte gains for unstructured code.

4. **Post-quantum insecurity**: All current folding schemes rely on discrete-log hardness (Pedersen MSM). Lattice-based commitments lack homomorphic linear closure needed for efficient $T$. Recent attempt LatticeFold (ePrint 2024/1029) shows 100× overhead.

5. **Custom gate degree blow-up**: CCS folding cross-terms are degree $d$. For $d>3$ (e.g., Poseidon S-box $x^7$), $T$ computation is $O(n \cdot d)$ and verifier needs $O(d)$ field ops, breaking constant-size promise for high-degree gates. Protostar [6] mitigates with degree reduction via auxiliary variables but doubles witness size.

6. **Cycle choice**: Pasta is not Ethereum-friendly; BN254/Grumpkin cycle has 10% slower MSM due to larger field and non-optimal curve. CycleFold [2] helps but still 1.3× slower than Pasta benchmarks.

## 7 Conclusion

Folding schemes transformed IVC from a theoretical curiosity to a practical primitive for Rollups, VDFs, and machine execution proofs. Nova [1] proved that relaxed R1CS with a single cross-term suffices for optimal recursive verification. SuperNova [5] extended this to non-uniform circuits, unlocking efficient VM proofs with a-la-carte costs. HyperNova [4] and CCS [3] unified R1CS, Plonkish, and AIR under one folding umbrella, achieving optimal prover crypto (one MSM) and zero-knowledge for free, while CycleFold [2] made cycles practical.

Future work includes *lattice folding* for post-quantum IVC, *proof-carrying data* (PCD) with multi-instance folding for parallel MapReduce, and *lookup folding* for zkEVM where table size $2^{20}$ dominates witness. The combination of custom gate folding with hardware-accelerated MSM (GPU/FPGA) promises <100ms per EVM step, bringing fully verifiable EVM execution within reach of L2 sequencers.

For practitioners: use Nova for uniform circuits with no trusted setup, SuperNova for instruction sets, HyperNova for Plonkish/lookup-heavy programs, and CycleFold over BN254/Grumpkin if verifier must be on-chain.
