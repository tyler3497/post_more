# Reverse Mathematics and the Big Five Subsystems of Second-Order Arithmetic: Measuring the Axiomatic Strength of Ordinary Mathematics

## Abstract

Reverse mathematics is a research program in the foundations of mathematics that calibrates the set-existence principles required to prove the theorems of ordinary mathematics. Working in the language L2 over the weak base theory RCA0, the program has shown that a remarkable fraction of classical results — in analysis, algebra, combinatorics, and descriptive set theory — are equivalent to one of five canonical subsystems: RCA0, WKL0, ACA0, ATR0, and Pi11-CA0, the so-called *Big Five*. This article develops the technical machinery of the field: the syntax of second-order arithmetic, the definition of each of the Big Five, the anatomy of a reversal argument, landmark equivalences for Heine–Borel, Bolzano–Weierstrass, König's lemma, the perfect set theorem, and fragments of Ramsey's theorem, and the proof-theoretic and computability-theoretic analyses — ordinals, omega-models, Turing ideals, the low basis theorem — that make reversals possible. It further discusses Shore's program for extending reverse mathematics to uncountable structures, the zoo of combinatorial principles that escape the Big Five, and whether the convergence of ordinary mathematics onto five axioms is a deep fact or an artifact of theorem selection.

## 1. Introduction

Most of classical mathematics — real analysis, countable algebra, separable metric topology, infinitary combinatorics — can be formalized in the language L2 of *second-order arithmetic*, whose variables range over natural numbers and sets of natural numbers. The theory Z2 of full second-order arithmetic is vastly stronger than what ordinary mathematics requires, and the central question of the *Friedman–Simpson program* of reverse mathematics is sharp: *which set-existence axioms are actually needed to prove a given theorem?* [1][2]

The methodology is distinctive. One formalizes a theorem T in L2 and proves it in some subsystem S of Z2; then one proves the converse — that T implies the axioms of S over the base theory. The pair of implications yields an equivalence, and the theorem T is thereby *calibrated*: its exact set-existence content is measured. The empirical discovery driving the field is that the overwhelming majority of theorems of ordinary mathematics calibrate to exactly five systems, the **Big Five** [1]:

- **RCA0** — recursive comprehension, the computable base theory;
- **WKL0** — RCA0 plus weak König's lemma;
- **ACA0** — arithmetical comprehension;
- **ATR0** — arithmetical transfinite recursion;
- **Pi11-CA0** — Pi11 comprehension.

These five systems are linearly ordered by proof-theoretic strength, correspond to classical foundational programs (finitism, predicativism, predicative reductionism, impredicative set theory), and admit elegant computability-theoretic characterizations via omega-models. Yet the picture is not complete: Ramsey's theorem for pairs, Hindman's theorem, and a growing zoo of combinatorial principles inhabit the gaps between the Big Five, and Richard Shore's program extends the analysis beyond the countable into uncountable mathematics via generalized notions of computation [4][6][7].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The language of second-order arithmetic

The two-sorted language L2 has number variables *n, m, k*, set variables *X, Y, Z*, the constant 0, function symbols for addition and multiplication, and the order relation. Formulas are classified by quantifier structure: *arithmetical* formulas contain no set quantifiers and sit in the Sigma0n/Pi0n hierarchies, while formulas with leading blocks of set quantifiers over an arithmetical matrix are Sigma1n or Pi1n.

The full theory Z2 consists of the axioms of PA minus induction (PA minus), the second-order induction axiom, and the full comprehension scheme: for every L2 formula phi, the set of numbers satisfying phi exists. Z2 is the natural habitat of classical analysis, but it vastly exceeds the logical strength of the theorems it hosts. Reverse mathematics studies weakenings obtained by restricting comprehension.

### 2.2 RCA0: the computable base theory

**RCA0** (recursive comprehension axiom) consists of PA minus, induction for Sigma01 formulas, and comprehension for Delta01 formulas — sets definable by both a Sigma01 and a Pi01 formula. Equivalently, a set exists in RCA0 exactly when it is computable: the omega-model whose second-order part is REC satisfies RCA0 [1]. This gives RCA0 its foundational reading as *computable mathematics*, a finitistic-flavored base theory whose axioms are consistent with the statement that every total function from the naturals to the naturals is recursive [3].

> **Theorem:** RCA0 is Pi02-conservative over primitive recursive arithmetic (PRA). Every Pi02 sentence provable in RCA0 is already provable in PRA.

Full induction is unprovable in RCA0, and much technical work consists of carefully managing the induction available. Over RCA0, statements of ordinary mathematics are compared by provability, and equivalences are always *provable in RCA0* unless stated otherwise.

### 2.3 The remaining members of the Big Five

| Subsystem | Axiom added over RCA0 | Informal content |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------|
| WKL0 | Weak König's lemma | Every infinite binary tree has an infinite path |
| ACA0 | Arithmetical comprehension | Every arithmetically definable set exists |
| ATR0 | Arithmetical transfinite recursion | Arithmetical definitions can be iterated along any countable well-ordering |
| Pi11-CA0 | Pi11 comprehension | Every Pi11-definable set exists |

The five are strictly increasing in strength: RCA0 is properly weaker than WKL0, which is properly weaker than ACA0, which is properly weaker than ATR0, which is properly weaker than Pi11-CA0 [1]. Each corresponds to a classical foundational program: finitism (RCA0), compactness (WKL0), Weyl's predicativism (ACA0), predicative reductionism (ATR0), and full impredicativity (Pi11-CA0) [3].

### 2.4 Proof-theoretic ordinals

Each of the Big Five has a characteristic proof-theoretic ordinal measuring its transfinite induction strength [1]:

| Subsystem | Proof-theoretic ordinal |
|-----------|------------------------|
| RCA0 | omega raised to the omega (more precisely, below epsilon0; Pi02-conservative over PRA) |
| WKL0 | same as RCA0 (Pi02-conservative over PRA) |
| ACA0 | epsilon0 |
| ATR0 | Gamma0, the Feferman–Schütte ordinal |
| Pi11-CA0 | the Pi12 ordinal, far beyond Gamma0 |

The ordinal analysis explains why ATR0 is the natural home of theorems about well-orderings and transfinite constructions: its strength reaches exactly the Feferman–Schütte limit of predicativity.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The reversal schema

A reverse-mathematical analysis of a theorem T of ordinary mathematics proceeds in three stages:

1. **Formalization.** Express T as an L2 sentence, coding reals as Cauchy sequences, separable metric spaces by countable dense subsets, and open sets by enumerations of basic balls. The coding is itself a mathematical act and a source of subtlety.
2. **Forward proof.** Show that T is provable in a subsystem S of Z2, typically with S as weak as possible.
3. **Reversal.** Working in RCA0, assume T and derive the characteristic axiom of S. The reversal is the heart of the enterprise: it demonstrates that the axiom is not merely sufficient but *necessary*.

The slogan is: *T implies S over RCA0, hence T is equivalent to S over RCA0.*

### 3.2 Omega-models and computability

Most reversals are proved via *omega-models*: models of L2 whose first-order part is the standard natural numbers. An omega-model is determined by its second-order part, a *Turing ideal* — a collection of subsets of the naturals closed under Turing reducibility and join. The key correspondence [1][5]:

- The omega-models of RCA0 are exactly the Turing ideals.
- The omega-models of WKL0 are the Turing ideals closed under the low basis operation, equivalently the *Scott ideals* in the computability-theoretic reading.
- The omega-models of ACA0 are exactly the Turing ideals closed under the Turing jump.
- The omega-models of ATR0 are the Turing ideals closed under hyperjump iteration along well-orderings, i.e., containing all hyperarithmetic-in-the-ideal sets.
- The omega-models of Pi11-CA0 are the beta-models: omega-models correct about well-foundedness.

To prove that T does *not* imply S over RCA0, one constructs a Turing ideal satisfying T but not S. The flagship tool is the **Jockusch–Soare low basis theorem**: every nonempty Pi01 class contains a low member [5]. Iterating it builds an omega-model of WKL0 in which every set is low, hence which lacks the halting problem, establishing that WKL0 does not imply ACA0 over RCA0.

A miniature computational illustration of the reversal methodology — building a computable infinite binary tree with no computable path, the canonical separation of RCA0 from WKL0 — can be sketched in code:


The tree produced is computable and infinite, but any path through it computes a set diagonalizing against every computable function; hence the path is noncomputable, and the omega-model REC of RCA0 contains the tree but no path — a concrete witness that RCA0 does not prove weak König's lemma.

### 3.3 Weihrauch reducibility and the computational program

Modern reverse mathematics complements proof-theoretic reversals with *computability-theoretic reducibilities*, notably Weihrauch reducibility, which measures the uniform computational content of theorems viewed as multi-valued problems [5][7]. A theorem of the form "for every instance X there exists a solution Y" is a problem; one problem Weihrauch-reduces to another if solutions to the second uniformly compute solutions to the first. This refines the Big Five picture: principles equivalent over RCA0 can have distinct uniform strength. Shore's program pushes this furthest, proposing that computational complexity replace proof-theoretic strength as the organizing measure, enabling a reverse mathematics of *uncountable* structures via alpha-recursion theory and admissible sets [4][6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Big Five in detail

**RCA0** is the theory of computable mathematics. Its characteristic theorems are those provable by effective means: the intermediate value theorem for continuous functions on the unit interval, the existence of prime factorizations, the soundness and completeness of first-order logic for countable languages, and the infinite pigeonhole principle. An important subtlety is that RCA0 proves the *Baire category theorem* for complete separable metric spaces, showing that nontrivial nonconstructive content can already live at the base [1].

**WKL0** adds *weak König's lemma*: every infinite subtree of the full binary tree has an infinite path. Despite its modest statement, WKL0 calibrates a vast swath of analysis and topology. Landmark equivalences over RCA0 include the Heine–Borel covering theorem, the Brouwer fixed point theorem, the Hahn–Banach theorem for separable Banach spaces, Gödel's completeness theorem, the existence of prime ideals in countable commutative rings, and the compactness of the unit interval [1][5]. The computability-theoretic reading is that WKL0 corresponds to *compactness arguments*: its omega-models are Turing ideals in which every infinite binary tree has a path, buildable via the low basis theorem while keeping all degrees low.

**ACA0** adds comprehension for arithmetical formulas, equivalently the assertion that the Turing jump of every set exists. It calibrates the theorems of classical analysis that freely form sets by arithmetical definition: the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, the existence of maximal ideals in countable rings, the theorem that every bounded increasing sequence of reals converges, König's infinity lemma for finitely branching trees, and Ramsey's theorem for triples and higher exponents. The reversal for Bolzano–Weierstrass is paradigmatic: given an injection from the naturals to the naturals, the range exists by arithmetical comprehension exactly when sequential compactness holds [1].

**ATR0** adds arithmetical transfinite recursion: for any countable well-ordering and any arithmetical operator, the transfinite iterate of the operator along the ordering exists. Its characteristic theorems concern well-orderings and transfinite constructions: the perfect set theorem, the comparability of countable well-orderings, Lusin's separation theorem, and determinacy for open games [1].

**Pi11-CA0** adds comprehension for Pi11 formulas, the strongest of the Big Five and the natural setting for theorems whose statements quantify over countable well-orderings: the Cantor–Bendixson theorem, the theorem that every countable abelian group has a decomposition into divisible and reduced parts, Silver's theorem on Borel equivalence relations, and determinacy for Sigma01 games [1]. Its omega-models are the beta-models, correct about which linear orderings are well-founded, reflecting the impredicative character of Pi11 definitions.

### 4.2 Landmark reversals

The following table records a selection of the classical equivalences that constitute the empirical core of the program. Each equivalence is provable in RCA0 [1][5]:

| Theorem | Equivalent subsystem |
|---------|---------------------|
| Heine–Borel covering theorem | WKL0 |
| Brouwer fixed point theorem (unit cube) | WKL0 |
| Hahn–Banach theorem (separable case) | WKL0 |
| Existence of prime ideals in countable commutative rings | WKL0 |
| Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem | ACA0 |
| König's infinity lemma (finitely branching trees) | ACA0 |
| Every bounded monotone sequence of reals converges | ACA0 |
| Ramsey's theorem for triples (RT with exponent 3) | ACA0 |
| Perfect set theorem | ATR0 |
| Comparability of countable well-orderings | ATR0 |
| Ulm's theorem for countable abelian p-groups | Pi11-CA0 |
| Cantor–Bendixson theorem | Pi11-CA0 |

The perfect set theorem illustrates the ATR0 level: assuming every uncountable closed set has a perfect subset, one builds for any countable linear ordering a tree whose perfect kernel yields arithmetical transfinite recursion along the ordering [1]; conversely ATR0 proves the theorem by iterating the Cantor–Bendixson derivative.

Ramsey's theorem deserves special attention. For exponent 3 and above, RT is equivalent to ACA0, proved by the classical argument that homogeneous sets for triples compute the jump. For *pairs*, however, the situation is dramatically different: RT for pairs (RT2,2) is strictly weaker than ACA0, as Seetapun proved, and equivalent to no Big Five system — it lies properly between RCA0 and ACA0 [5]. The reverse mathematics of Ramsey's theorem for pairs became a research industry, generating the *zoo* of combinatorial principles — cohesiveness (COH), the ascending-descending sequence principle (ADS), the chain-antichain principle (CAC), stable Ramsey's theorem (SRT2,2) — that populate the region between RCA0 and ACA0 and motivated Hirschfeldt's monograph [5] and the Dzhafarov–Mummert textbook's extensive treatment of combinatorial reducibilities [7].

### 4.3 Proof-theoretic ordinals and the limits of predicativity

The proof-theoretic ordinals of the Big Five are not mere numerology; they explain *why* certain theorems calibrate where they do. ACA0's ordinal epsilon0 is the limit of predicative provability in the Gentzen sense: ACA0 proves transfinite induction exactly for ordinals below epsilon0. ATR0's ordinal Gamma0, the Feferman–Schütte ordinal, is the famous limit of predicativity: the supremum of what predicative systems can prove well-founded. Theorems requiring transfinite recursion along arbitrary countable well-orderings — the perfect set theorem, Borel determinacy fragments — need ATR0 precisely because they need induction up to Gamma0. Pi11-CA0 reaches the Pi12 ordinal, far beyond, into genuinely impredicative territory where well-foundedness of orderings is itself defined by a Pi11 condition [1].

The Haskell fragment below sketches the ordinal notation system for epsilon0 in Cantor normal form, whose well-foundedness ACA0 proves but RCA0 does not:

```haskell
-- Ordinal notations below epsilon_0 in Cantor normal form.
-- ACA_0 proves transfinite induction for this notation system;
-- RCA_0 does not. This is the proof-theoretic content of ACA_0.
data Ord = Zero | Plus Ord Ord | OmegaPow Ord deriving (Eq, Show)

-- omega^alpha + beta in Cantor normal form, alpha >= exponent of beta
cnf :: Ord -> Ord -> Ord
cnf Zero b = b
cnf a Zero = OmegaPow a
cnf a b    = Plus (OmegaPow a) b

-- epsilon_0 as the limit of omega, omega^omega, omega^(omega^omega), ...
epsTower :: Int -> Ord
epsTower 0 = OmegaPow Zero          -- omega
epsTower n = OmegaPow (epsTower (n-1))
```

### 4.4 Computability-theoretic underpinnings

The deepest explanation of the Big Five's robustness is computability-theoretic. Each subsystem corresponds to a natural closure condition on Turing ideals, and reversals are typically proved by constructing ideals with and without those closure properties. The toolkit includes:

- **The low basis theorem** (Jockusch–Soare): every nonempty Pi01 class contains a low member; iterating it yields omega-models of WKL0 with no non-low sets [5].
- **Jump inversion and cone avoidance**: techniques for building Turing ideals that avoid computing a given noncomputable set, used to separate principles.
- **Forcing with Pi01 classes**: the standard method for building omega-models of WKL0 plus the negation of stronger principles.
- **Hyperarithmetic analysis**: principles such as Halin's infinite ray theorem that imply ATR0 in omega-models but are weaker in full generality [6].
- **Beta-models and Pi11 comprehension**: ATR0 plus Pi11 comprehension corresponds to closure under the hyperjump and correctness about well-foundedness.

This computability-theoretic perspective is what makes reverse mathematics continuous with recursion theory, and it is the bridge Shore exploits to generalize the program beyond the countable [4][6].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The central empirical result of the Friedman–Simpson program is the *Big Five phenomenon*: of the hundreds of theorems analyzed, the overwhelming majority are equivalent over RCA0 to one of exactly five subsystems [1][2]. This is not a theorem but a striking regularity, and it demands explanation. Simpson's monograph documents the equivalences across analysis, algebra, combinatorics, and logic; the Dzhafarov–Mummert textbook extends the census to the modern zoo [1][7].

A second body of results concerns the *failure* of the Big Five to be exhaustive. The known exceptions cluster in infinitary combinatorics and in the region between RCA0 and ACA0:

1. **Ramsey's theorem for pairs** (RT2,2) is strictly between RCA0 and ACA0 and equivalent to no Big Five system; its stable version, its cohesive weakenings, and the associated principles COH, ADS, CAC, EM form a dense network of non-implications mapped in detail by Hirschfeldt [5].
2. **Hindman's theorem** is provable in ACA0, but its exact strength remains open; it is not known to imply ACA0.
3. **The Rival–Sands theorem, the Erdős–Rado theorem for pairs**, and other combinatorial principles occupy intermediate or incomparable positions.
4. **Theorems of hyperarithmetic analysis**, such as the infinite ray theorem of Halin, imply ATR0 over RCA0 in the sense of omega-models while remaining proof-theoretically weaker — a subtlety Shore and collaborators have explored [6].

A third direction is *Shore's program* for uncountable reverse mathematics [4]. Because L2 cannot quantify over uncountable sets, Shore replaces proof-theoretic strength with *computational complexity*: a theorem's strength is measured by the generalized Turing degrees needed to compute witnesses, using alpha-recursion theory. Analyzing uncountable algebra — vector space bases, prime ideals in uncountable rings — he finds that analogues of WKL0 correspond to compactness principles that climb into large-cardinal territory: the tree property at an uncountable kappa is equivalent to weak compactness [4][6]. Though its philosophical interpretation is contested [2], the program is the most serious attempt to export reverse-mathematical calibration beyond the countable.

> **Theorem:** Over RCA0, WKL0 is equivalent to the Heine–Borel covering theorem, the Brouwer fixed point theorem, and the separable Hahn–Banach theorem. Over RCA0, ACA0 is equivalent to the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem and to König's infinity lemma for finitely branching trees. Over RCA0, ATR0 is equivalent to the perfect set theorem and to the comparability of countable well-orderings. Over RCA0, Pi11-CA0 is equivalent to the Cantor–Bendixson theorem [1].

## 6. Limitations

The Big Five phenomenon, for all its success, faces serious methodological and philosophical limitations.

First, the framework is *intrinsically countable*. Theorems about uncountable sets, arbitrary topological spaces, or categories cannot be formalized in L2 at all, and Shore's computational generalization, while promising, abandons the proof-theoretic precision that gives classical reverse mathematics its bite; the choice of generalized computability notion is itself up for grabs, with no consensus candidate playing the role Turing computability plays in the countable case [4][2].

Second, *equivalence over RCA0 is coarse*. Principles equivalent over RCA0 can differ in uniform computational strength, as Weihrauch analysis reveals; the Big Five collapse distinctions that matter for computable mathematics, and the zoo between RCA0 and ACA0 shows the five systems are prominent landmarks in a much denser terrain [5][7].

Third, there are *coding sensitivities*. Whether a theorem calibrates to a given subsystem can depend on how its objects are coded in L2 — continuous functions by codes, open sets by enumerations — and different natural formalizations can yield different strengths. The framework measures the strength of *formalizations*, and the inference back to the informal theorem requires care.

Fourth, the *Sigma01 induction* of the base theory is a technical artifact with real consequences: some reversals need more induction than RCA0 provides, and the choice of base theory shapes the landscape.

Finally, there is the *sociological worry*: the Big Five phenomenon may partly reflect which theorems logicians choose to analyze. Theorems selected for their foundational interest, proved by standard compactness, jump, or transfinite-recursion arguments, will naturally calibrate to the systems named after those arguments. Whether the convergence is a deep fact about mathematical practice or an artifact of selection remains debated [2][3].

## 7. Conclusion

Reverse mathematics has achieved something rare in the foundations of mathematics: a precise, quantitative answer to the question of what axioms ordinary mathematics needs. The discovery that the theorems of analysis, algebra, and combinatorics distribute themselves across five canonical subsystems — RCA0, WKL0, ACA0, ATR0, and Pi11-CA0 — each with a clear computability-theoretic meaning, a characteristic proof-theoretic ordinal, and a classical foundational program as its philosophical correlate, is among the most striking empirical regularities in logic [1]. The reversal technique, the omega-model constructions, and the basis theorems that power it have become standard tools, and the program's extension into the zoo of combinatorial principles and into Shore's uncountable computational reverse mathematics keeps it at the frontier [4][5][6][7].

Open problems are commensurate with the achievement: the exact strength of Hindman's theorem, the structure of the region between RCA0 and ACA0, and whether a genuinely uncountable reverse mathematics can recover the precision of the countable theory. Whatever the answers, the Friedman–Simpson program has permanently changed the foundational landscape: we now know, theorem by theorem, exactly how much set existence ordinary mathematics requires — and the answer, remarkably often, is one of five.
