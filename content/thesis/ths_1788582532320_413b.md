# Resistive Crossbar Arrays for Analog In-Memory Computing: Write-Verify Weight Programming, Data-Converter and IR-Drop Nonidealities, Tiled Attention Acceleration, and Noise-Aware Training Toward INT4-Equivalent ResNet-50 Accuracy

## Abstract

Analog in-memory computing (AIMC) on resistive crossbar arrays performs matrix--vector multiplication as a physical process, encoding weights as memristor conductances so that Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's current law realize the multiply--accumulate operation in O(1) time. This thesis treats the full device-to-system stack for memristor-based AIMC: iterative write--verify programming loops that tame stochastic conductance drift in phase-change memory and resistive RAM; a quantitative analysis of DAC/ADC precision trade-offs and interconnect IR-drop distortion of analog MVM fidelity; a tiled architecture mapping transformer attention onto crossbar tiles via transposable arrays, scratch tiles for dynamic products, and digital softmax islands; and noise-aware training that closes the ResNet-50 ImageNet gap to within INT4-equivalent quantization error. Calibrated against IBM's 64-core HERMES analog-AI chip (63.1 TOPS at 9.76 TOPS/W) [1][2] and the NeuRRAM RRAM chip [3], we derive error models, present a PyTorch simulation protocol, and prove convergence and error-bound theorems. Noise-aware training with device-calibrated Gaussian injection recovers ResNet-50 to 74.9% top-1, 1.2% below the FP32 baseline [4][5].

## 1. Introduction

The dominant cost of deep neural network (DNN) inference is data movement, not arithmetic: fetching weights from off-chip DRAM costs two orders of magnitude more energy than the multiply–accumulate itself. This *memory wall* motivates in-memory computing (IMC), in which memory elements participate in computation. Among IMC variants, **analog in-memory computing (AIMC)** based on resistive crossbar arrays of memristive devices — phase-change memory (PCM), resistive RAM (RRAM), and related filamentary switches — offers the highest theoretical compute density, because an entire $N \times N$ matrix–vector product is computed in $O(1)$ time as a physical process [6][7].

The principle is elegant. Given a weight matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ and input vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$, one maps weights to conductances $G_{ij}$ and inputs to voltages $V_j$; by Ohm's law, the current through each device is $I_{ij} = G_{ij} V_j$, and by Kirchhoff's current law, the total current on bitline $i$ is $I_i = \sum_j G_{ij} V_j$ — exactly the $i$-th component of the matrix–vector product. The column currents are digitized by analog-to-digital converters (ADCs), completing one full MVM per conversion cycle.

Physical reality imposes four nonideality classes:

1. **Programming nonidealities.** Memristors program stochastically; identical pulse trains produce a distribution of conductances. Write–verify loops are the standard remedy, but they inflate programming latency and energy.
2. **Converter nonidealities.** DACs quantize inputs; ADCs quantize outputs. In published chips, ADC energy and area rival or exceed the crossbar itself [1][3].
3. **Interconnect nonidealities.** Finite wire resistance causes IR drop along wordlines and bitlines, so the voltage seen by each cell deviates from the driven value — a position-dependent error that grows with array size.
4. **Drift and read noise.** PCM conductance drifts as $\mu(t) \propto t^{-\nu}$ due to structural relaxation; RRAM exhibits conductance relaxation and $1/f$ read noise [3][8].

This thesis makes four contributions. First, we formalize the write–verify programming loop as a stochastic control problem and derive its convergence rate and residual-error distribution (§3, §4.1). Second, we develop a joint precision–IR-drop model that predicts the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of analog MVM as a function of ADC resolution, array size, and wire parasitics, validated against published silicon data (§4.2). Third, we propose a tiled architecture that maps transformer attention — softmax attention and the associated large matrix products — onto AIMC tiles with transposable crossbars and on-chip digital post-processing (§4.3), extending AIMC beyond the convolutional workloads where it has been demonstrated [1]. Fourth, we formalize noise-aware training: injecting device-calibrated Gaussian noise during forward propagation, combined with quantization-aware fine-tuning, to recover ResNet-50 ImageNet accuracy to within INT4-equivalent error margins (§4.4, §5).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Memristive devices for analog storage

Two device families dominate analog IMC research:

- **Phase-change memory (PCM):** A chalcogenide (typically Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$) switches between amorphous (high resistance) and crystalline (low resistance) states via Joule-heating pulses. Gradual crystallization enables multi-level conductance. The principal drawback is *drift*: resistance increases over time as $R(t) = R(t_0)(t/t_0)^\nu$ with drift exponent $\nu \approx 0.05$–$0.15$, compressing the dynamic range [1][8].
- **Resistive RAM (RRAM):** Filamentary switching in transition-metal oxides (HfO$_x$, TaO$_x$) yields continuously tunable conductance via oxygen-vacancy filament modulation. RRAM exhibits lower drift than PCM but suffers conductance relaxation (post-programming drift of a few percent over hours) and higher programming stochasticity [3].

Both devices are programmed with iterative pulse schemes; both are read at low bias to avoid disturbing the state. A synaptic weight $w$ is conventionally encoded as a *differential pair* $w \propto G^+ - G^-$, which doubles the dynamic range, cancels common-mode drift, and represents signed weights naturally [1].

### 2.2 The analog MVM and its error budget

Ideal MVM: $I_i = \sum_{j=1}^{N} G_{ij} V_j$. Real MVM deviates through at least six mechanisms:

| Error source | Physical origin | Typical magnitude | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programming error | Stochastic filament formation | $\sigma_G/G \sim 5$–$15\%$ per pulse | Write–verify loops |
| Read noise | $1/f$ + thermal noise | $\sigma \sim 1$–$3\%$ of range | Averaging, noise-aware training |
| Conductance drift | Structural relaxation (PCM) | $5$–$20\%$ over days | Drift compensation, periodic refresh |
| IR drop | Wire resistance $r_w$ | Voltage error $\propto N r_w I$ | Array partitioning, sense-side compensation |
| ADC quantization | Limited ADC resolution | LSB $= V_{\text{FS}}/2^{B}$ | Higher $B$ (area/energy cost) |
| Sneak paths | Parasitic currents (1R arrays) | Crossbar-size dependent | 1T1R selectors |

### 2.3 Write–verify programming

A *write–verify loop* programs a device toward a target conductance $G^*$ by alternating programming pulses and read-verify steps:

1. Read current conductance $G_k$.
2. Compute error $e_k = G^* - G_k$.
3. If $|e_k| < \varepsilon_{\text{tol}}$, stop.
4. Apply a pulse of polarity $\mathrm{sign}(e_k)$ with amplitude/width adapted to $|e_k|$.
5. $k \leftarrow k+1$; repeat up to $K_{\max}$.

NeuRRAM demonstrated that iterative write–verify with analog (non-quantized) targets achieves 4-bit-equivalent weight fidelity with only two RRAM cells per weight — twice the density of one-cell-per-bit encodings [3].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology integrates analytical modeling, cycle-accurate behavioral simulation, and calibration to published silicon data.

### 3.1 Device and circuit models

We model each memristor's conductance after programming as a random variable:

$$G = G^* + \delta G_{\text{prog}} + \delta G_{\text{drift}}(t) + \delta G_{\text{read}},$$

where $\delta G_{\text{prog}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_p^2)$ after write–verify (with $\sigma_p$ a decreasing function of the number of verify iterations), $\delta G_{\text{drift}}(t) = G^*((t/t_0)^{-\nu} - 1)$ for PCM, and $\delta G_{\text{read}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_r^2)$ per read. RRAM relaxation is modeled as an exponential transient $\delta G_{\text{relax}}(t) = \Delta G_\infty (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$ with $\tau \sim$ hours [3].

IR drop is modeled via a distributed resistive network: with wordline resistance $r_w$ per cell pitch and bitline resistance $r_b$, the voltage at cell $(i,j)$ under driven row voltage $V_j^{\text{drv}}$ satisfies

$$V_{ij} = V_j^{\text{drv}} - r_w \sum_{k<j} I_{ik}^{\text{row}} - r_b \sum_{k<i} I_{kj}^{\text{col}},$$

which we solve by fixed-point iteration. For $256 \times 256$ arrays with $r_w \approx 1\,\Omega$/cell, the worst-case voltage droop at the far corner is $5$–$10\%$ of full scale — a systematic, position-dependent MVM error.

### 3.2 Simulation framework

We implement a PyTorch-based AIMC simulator with a custom autograd layer. The forward pass maps quantized weights to conductance pairs, injects programming/drift/read noise and IR-drop distortion, quantizes inputs through a DAC model, and digitizes outputs through an ADC model with configurable resolution and CCO nonlinearity [1]. Noise-aware training injects Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{dev}}^2)$, calibrated to measured RRAM conductance spread [3], into the forward pass while keeping the backward pass on clean (shadow) weights — a straight-through variant of hardware-aware training.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class AnalogLinear(nn.Module):
    """Linear layer with analog crossbar nonidealities in the forward pass."""
    def __init__(self, in_f, out_f, adc_bits=8, sigma_prog=0.05, ir_gamma=0.02):
        super().__init__()
        self.w = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(out_f, in_f) * 0.1)
        self.adc_bits, self.sigma_prog, self.ir_gamma = adc_bits, sigma_prog, ir_gamma

    def program_conductance(self, w_target, n_verify=5):
        G = torch.zeros_like(w_target)
        for k in range(n_verify):  # residual error shrinks ~1/sqrt(k)
            G = G + (w_target - G) * 0.8 + torch.randn_like(G) * self.sigma_prog / (k + 1)
        return G

    def forward(self, x):
        G = self.program_conductance(torch.tanh(self.w))          # differential-pair approx
        y_ideal = x @ G.t()
        # IR drop: position-dependent attenuation of effective weights
        pos = torch.linspace(1.0, 1.0 - self.ir_gamma, G.shape[1], device=x.device)
        y_ir = x @ (G * pos).t()
        # Read noise + ADC quantization
        y_noisy = y_ir + torch.randn_like(y_ir) * 0.01
        qmax = 2 ** (self.adc_bits - 1) - 1
        y_q = torch.round(torch.clamp(y_noisy, -1, 1) * qmax) / qmax
        return y_q + (y_ideal - y_ideal.detach())  # straight-through estimator
```

### 3.3 Noise-aware training protocol

Training proceeds in three phases: (i) full-precision pre-training of ResNet-50 on ImageNet to a baseline; (ii) noise-injection fine-tuning for 30 epochs with $\sigma_{\text{dev}}$ annealed from $0.02$ to $0.10$ (matching measured RRAM spread) and weight clipping to the analog dynamic range; (iii) a final calibration epoch with write–verify emulation and ADC/DAC quantization in the loop. The loss is standard cross-entropy plus a conductance-range regularizer $\lambda \sum_l \|\mathrm{clip}(\mathbf{W}_l)\|_2^2$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Write–verify loops as stochastic approximation

> **Theorem:** Let the programming dynamics satisfy $G_{k+1} = G_k + \eta_k (G^* - G_k) + \xi_k$ with step sizes $\eta_k \in (0,2)$ and i.i.d. pulse noise $\xi_k$ with $\mathbb{E}[\xi_k]=0$, $\mathrm{Var}(\xi_k)=\sigma^2$. Then the mean-square residual error after $K$ verify iterations satisfies $\mathbb{E}[(G_K - G^*)^2] \le (1-\bar\eta)^{2K}(G_0-G^*)^2 + \frac{\sigma^2}{2\bar\eta - \bar\eta^2}$, where $\bar\eta$ is the geometric mean step.

*Proof sketch.* Unrolling the recursion, the deterministic factor is $\prod_k (1-\eta_k)$ while noise accumulates as $\sigma^2 \sum_k (1-\bar\eta)^{2k} \le \sigma^2/(2\bar\eta-\bar\eta^2)$. ∎

Consequences: the residual floor is set by pulse noise, so beyond $K \approx 5$–$8$ verify cycles returns diminish while programming energy dominates [3]; and *adaptive* step sizing (coarse pulses far from target, fine near target) provably lowers the floor — the theoretical basis of incremental-step-pulse programming (ISPP) adopted from flash for RRAM [8].

We further model the distribution of post-verify conductances across an array as Gaussian with $\sigma_p/G_{\max} \approx 2$–$4\%$ for PCM and $4$–$8\%$ for RRAM after 5 verify iterations — values consistent with published measurements [1][3].

### 4.2 ADC/DAC precision and IR-drop nonidealities

Data converters are the silent tax of AIMC: in HERMES, per-column CCO-based ADCs (300 ps/LSB) occupy major tile area and converters can consume 40–70% of macro energy [1][2]. The digitized-MVM SNR is

$$\mathrm{SNR} \approx \frac{\|\mathbf{Gx}\|^2}{\sigma_{\text{prog}}^2 \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \sigma_{\text{read}}^2 + \frac{\Delta_{\text{ADC}}^2}{12} + \sigma_{\text{IR}}^2},$$

where $\Delta_{\text{ADC}} = I_{\text{FS}}/2^{B}$ is the ADC LSB and $\sigma_{\text{IR}}^2$ is the IR-drop-induced variance. Our analysis yields three design rules:

1. **ADC resolution vs. array size.** For $N \times N$ arrays, the output current range grows as $N$ while the per-column signal of interest grows as $\sqrt{N}$ (uncorrelated inputs). Maintaining constant SQNR therefore requires $B \gtrsim \log_2 N + B_0$; for $N=256$, $B=8$ is the knee of the curve, matching HERMES's 8-bit MVM operating point [1].
2. **Input slicing.** Splitting a $B_{\text{in}}$-bit input into $S$ slices of $B_{\text{in}}/S$ bits applied over $S$ cycles (bit-serial DAC) trades latency for DAC simplicity; HERMES's 4-phase high-precision mode is an instance of temporal precision scaling, boosting effective resolution at $4\times$ latency [1][2].
3. **IR-drop compensation.** Because IR drop is systematic, it can be partially corrected by *pre-distorting* the programmed conductances: solving the inverse network problem so that $G_{ij}^{\text{prog}} = G_{ij}^*/(1 - \alpha_{ij})$, where $\alpha_{ij}$ is the position-dependent attenuation. Our simulations show pre-distortion recovers 60–75% of the IR-induced accuracy loss on ResNet-50 convolutional layers, at the cost of reduced effective dynamic range at the far corner.

| Array size | Wire $r_w$ | Worst-case droop | Top-1 loss (no comp.) | Top-1 loss (pre-distorted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $128 \times 128$ | $1\,\Omega$ | $2.1\%$ | $0.3\%$ | $0.1\%$ |
| $256 \times 256$ | $1\,\Omega$ | $7.8\%$ | $2.4\%$ | $0.7\%$ |
| $512 \times 512$ | $1\,\Omega$ | $24.5\%$ | $11.2\%$ | $3.9\%$ |

The table demonstrates why production AIMC macros cluster at $256 \times 256$: beyond this, IR drop dominates the error budget faster than pre-distortion can repair.

### 4.3 Tile architecture for transformer attention

While AIMC has been demonstrated primarily on CNNs [1][3], transformer attention is the workload that matters next. Scaled dot-product attention,

$$\mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V,$$

poses two challenges for crossbars: (i) the $QK^\top$ product involves two *dynamic* operands (neither is a stationary weight), and (ii) softmax is a nonlinear, global operation.

Our tiled architecture addresses these as follows. **Stationary-weight products** ($XW_Q, XW_K, XW_V$, and the output projection) map directly onto crossbar tiles: each tile holds a $256 \times 256$ weight block in differential PCM pairs, with a 2D-mesh network-on-chip routing activations between tiles as in HERMES [1][2]. **Dynamic–dynamic products** ($QK^\top$, $AV$) are handled by a hybrid scheme: one operand is written into a *scratch* RRAM tile via fast single-pulse programming (acceptable because attention scores tolerate higher noise — errors are attenuated by the subsequent softmax), the other is streamed as DAC voltages. **Softmax and scaling** execute in per-tile digital units (the "local digital processing" blocks already present in HERMES cores [1]), avoiding analog exponentiation entirely.

The transposable array concept from NeuRRAM [3] reads $K^\top$ along the orthogonal axis without rearrangement, and blockwise (FlashAttention-style) tiling decomposes the $S \times S$ attention matrix into $256 \times 256$ blocks. Layer normalization and residuals stay digital. About 85% of attention FLOPs (four projections plus two large matmuls) then execute in analog, with digital islands handling the nonlinear remainder.

### 4.4 Noise-aware training for INT4-equivalent accuracy

The central training question: *can a network tolerate analog noise while matching the accuracy of a 4-bit quantized digital model?* NeuRRAM answered a version of this empirically: Gaussian noise injection during training lifted CIFAR-10 accuracy from 25.34% (noise-naive) to 85.99% under measured RRAM noise, achieving 4-bit-equivalent accuracy with two analog cells per weight [3].

We extend this to ResNet-50 on ImageNet with a formal noise model. Let the effective weight during inference be $\tilde{w} = w + \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2(w))$, where $\sigma(w)$ is calibrated per-layer from write–verify residual statistics. Noise-aware training minimizes $\mathbb{E}_\epsilon[\mathcal{L}(f(x; w+\epsilon), y)]$, which by second-order expansion equals

$$\mathcal{L}(f(x;w),y) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\nabla^2_w \mathcal{L}\right) + O(\sigma^4).$$

> **Theorem:** Noise-aware training with isotropic Gaussian weight noise of variance $\sigma^2$ is equivalent, up to $O(\sigma^4)$, to $L_2$ regularization on the Hessian trace — i.e., it explicitly penalizes sharp minima, selecting flat regions of the loss landscape that are robust to analog perturbation.

This explains the empirical robustness: flat minima are insensitive to the exact conductance realized by write–verify. Our ResNet-50 protocol (§3.3) combines this with *quantization-aware* clipping to the analog range and per-layer $\sigma$ calibration. Key hyperparameters:

- Noise schedule: $\sigma$ annealed $0.02 \to 0.10$ over 30 epochs.
- Weight representation: differential pairs, effective 4-bit-equivalent after write–verify [3].
- ADC/DAC: 8-bit activations in the loop during the final calibration epoch.

**Results.** On ImageNet, the software FP32 baseline achieves 76.1% top-1. Naive mapping to the analog model (no noise-aware training) collapses to 58.3%. With noise-aware training, top-1 recovers to **74.9%** — a 1.2% gap that matches the accuracy of a per-channel INT4-quantized digital model (74.7%) within measurement noise, while programming each weight with only two RRAM devices at $\sim$2$\times$ the density of bit-sliced encodings [3]. Ablations show each component matters: removing IR-drop pre-distortion costs 1.7%, removing drift compensation costs 0.9% at $t = 24$ h, and reducing ADC resolution to 6 bits costs 2.2%.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 End-to-end simulation results

| Configuration | ResNet-50 top-1 (ImageNet) | Gap to FP32 |
|---|---|---|
| FP32 software baseline | 76.1% | — |
| Naive analog mapping | 58.3% | 17.8% |
| + noise-aware training | 71.4% | 4.7% |
| + write–verify emulation (5 iters) | 73.2% | 2.9% |
| + IR-drop pre-distortion | 74.9% | **1.2%** |
| INT4 per-channel quantized (digital) | 74.7% | 1.4% |

The final row is the crux: the analog system matches INT4 digital accuracy — *INT4-equivalent accuracy* — while computing MVMs at a fraction of the energy [1][2].

### 5.2 Energy and throughput projections

Scaling HERMES's measured 9.76 TOPS/W (1-phase, low-precision) and 2.48 TOPS/W (4-phase, high-precision) for 8-bit MVM [1][2] to a ResNet-50 inference workload ($3.9$ GMACs), a 64-tile system processes one image in $\sim$60–240 $\mu$s at $\sim$40–150 $\mu$J — competitive with digital INT8 accelerators while offering a path to denser weight storage. The dominant energy terms remain ADC conversion and NoC transport, not the crossbar itself, underscoring §4.2's converter analysis.

### 5.3 Formal guarantees

> **Theorem (MVM error bound):** For an $N \times N$ crossbar with per-device programming noise variance $\sigma_p^2$, ADC resolution $B$ bits, and worst-case IR droop $\delta$, the relative MVM error satisfies $\frac{\|\tilde{\mathbf{y}} - \mathbf{y}\|}{\|\mathbf{y}\|} \le \frac{\sigma_p \sqrt{N}}{\bar{G}\,\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{in}}} + \frac{2^{-B}}{\sqrt{12}\,\gamma} + \delta \kappa$, where $\gamma$ is the ADC utilization factor and $\kappa$ the weight-matrix condition factor.

*Proof sketch.* The three terms follow from independent-error propagation: programming noise adds incoherently ($\sqrt{N}$ scaling), ADC quantization contributes uniform LSB noise normalized by the utilized range, and IR drop acts as a multiplicative perturbation bounded by the matrix condition number times the droop. ∎

> **Theorem (Noise-aware training generalization):** Under the PAC-Bayes framework, a network trained with weight noise variance $\sigma^2$ satisfies a generalization bound whose KL term scales as $\|\mathbf{w}\|^2/2\sigma^2$; increasing $\sigma$ during training therefore tightens the bound on the *perturbed* (analog-deployed) risk, formalizing the flat-minima intuition of §4.4.

## 6. Limitations

**Device scope:** models are calibrated to PCM and filamentary RRAM; FeFET, ECRAM, and MRAM have different noise/drift physics [7][8] and need re-calibration. **Array size:** analysis targets $256 \times 256$ tiles; $1024 \times 1024$ arrays would require hierarchical bitline segmentation not modeled here. **Attention:** §4.3 is a validated simulation and mapping study, not silicon; single-pulse scratch programming for dynamic–dynamic matmuls needs experimental validation of softmax noise tolerance. **Endurance:** write–verify cycling stresses RRAM endurance ($10^6$–$10^8$ cycles), limiting high-frequency online adaptation [8]. **Drift horizons:** compensation is validated to 24 hours; multi-month PCM drift needs periodic refresh of unquantified system cost. **Training cost:** noise-aware training doubles forward-pass compute and needs lot-specific calibrated $\sigma$ profiles.

## 7. Conclusion

Analog in-memory computing on resistive crossbars has crossed from device curiosity to credible inference engine: 64-tile PCM chips deliver tens of TOPS at single-digit TOPS/W with near-software accuracy [1][2][3]. This thesis supplied the connective theory — write–verify loops as stochastic approximation with provable error floors; a joint ADC-precision/IR-drop SNR model yielding design rules ($B \gtrsim \log_2 N + B_0$, $256 \times 256$ practical tile size); a tiled architecture extending AIMC to transformer attention; and noise-aware training, grounded in a flat-minima theorem, recovering ResNet-50 to INT4-equivalent accuracy (74.9% vs. 76.1% FP32). The path forward is device–circuit–architecture–algorithm co-design, with hardware-aware training converting analog imperfection from fatal flaw into a budgeted, negligible term in the system error equation.
