# SMT-Based Formal Verification of Register-Transfer-Level Designs with Yosys and SymbiYosys: Bounded Model Checking, *k*-Induction, and Cover Properties

## Abstract

Functional verification dominates digital design effort, yet constrained-random simulation samples only a fraction of the reachable state space and routinely misses corner-case defects that escape into silicon. This thesis presents the open-source formal verification flow built on Yosys and its front-end driver SymbiYosys (*sby*), which reduces register-transfer-level (RTL) designs to satisfiability-modulo-theories (SMT) problems discharged by off-the-shelf solvers. We develop the complete methodology: elaborating SystemVerilog into Yosys's RTLIL intermediate representation, expressing safety properties as synchronous assertions, and orchestrating bounded model checking (BMC), *k*-induction proofs, and cover-property reachability through declarative `.sby` task files. We give a precise account of *sby*'s composition of Yosys elaboration with SMT, BTOR2, and AIGER backends; a rigorous treatment of *k*-induction covering base case, inductive step, and invariant strengthening; and a workflow in which cover properties guard against vacuous proofs from over-constraining. A FIFO case study grounds the discussion in concrete configuration and solver behavior [1][2][5][6].

## 1 Introduction

Functional verification consumes the majority of effort in digital hardware projects, and the dominant industrial technique — constrained-random simulation with coverage closure — is fundamentally a *sampling* method. A block with *n* state bits admits up to 2^*n* reachable configurations; no testbench enumerates more than a vanishing fraction. The defects surviving to silicon are therefore disproportionately *corner cases*: deep FIFO interleavings, arbiter starvation under adversarial request patterns, rare reset sequencing. Simulation finds the bugs the engineer imagined; the silicon failures are the ones nobody imagined.

Formal verification inverts this relationship. Instead of stimulating a design with chosen vectors, it encodes the transition relation and a property as a logical formula and asks a decision procedure whether *any* input sequence violates it. A passing proof is a guarantee over *all* inputs; a failure ships with a concrete counterexample trace. For decades this capability was locked behind commercial licenses. The open-source synthesis framework **Yosys** [3] and its formal front-end driver **SymbiYosys** (*sby*) [1][2] changed that: they compose RTL elaboration, SMT/BTOR2/AIGER model generation, and solver orchestration into a flow that runs on a laptop.

This thesis makes three contributions. *First*, it documents precisely how *sby* reduces RTL to solver-consumable models — the elaboration pipeline from Verilog through RTLIL to SMT2, BTOR2, and AIGER, and the roles of the `smtbmc`, `abc`, and `aiger` engines. *Second*, it gives a rigorous treatment of the two central proof techniques: **bounded model checking** (BMC) [6] and ***k*-induction** [5], which lifts BMC into an unbounded proof via a base case and an inductive step strengthened by auxiliary invariants. *Third*, it develops the methodological discipline separating successful formal campaigns from vacuous ones: **cover properties** that demonstrate proven assertions are not artifacts of over-constraining assumptions.

Section 2 reviews the background; Section 3 presents the methodology; Section 4 is the deep dive; Section 5 grounds the theory in a FIFO case study; Section 6 states the limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

**Simulation samples; formal exhausts.** A testbench applies concrete stimuli and checks responses against a reference model. Coverage metrics — line, toggle, FSM, functional covergroups — measure *what was exercised*, never *what was proven absent*. Formal methods instead treat the design as a finite-state transition system *M = (S, I, T)* and ask whether a safety property *P ⊆ S* can ever be violated. A conclusive answer is exhaustive over all input sequences — precisely the complement of simulation's blind spot.

**Bounded model checking** (Biere, Cimatti, Clarke, Zhu [6]) unrolls the transition relation *k* times and encodes "is a *P*-violating state reachable within *k* steps?" as a single formula:

*I(s₀) ∧ T(s₀,s₁) ∧ ⋯ ∧ T(sₖ₋₁,sₖ) ∧ (¬P(s₀) ∨ ⋯ ∨ ¬P(sₖ))*

Satisfiability yields a counterexample of length ≤ *k*; unsatisfiability proves absence of violations within the bound. BMC is complete at the system's *diameter*, but diameters are astronomically large in practice, so BMC serves as a *bug-hunting* technique: cheap, automatic, and lethal against shallow bugs, but incapable of proving absence of deep ones.

***k*-induction** generalizes mathematical induction to *k*-step windows [5]. Ordinary induction demands that *P* be preserved by every transition from every *P*-state, including unreachable ones — a condition that rarely holds. *k*-induction weakens it: *P* need only be preserved across *loop-free* paths of *k* consecutive *P*-states. Larger *k* admits weaker properties at the cost of a harder inductive-step query. Its soundness underpins every `prove`-mode run in this thesis:

> **Theorem (Soundness of *k*-induction):** Let *M = (S, I, T)* be a finite-state transition system and *P ⊆ S* a safety property. If *(i)* every path of length *k* from *I* satisfies *P* at every visited state (*base case*), and *(ii)* every loop-free path *s₀…sₖ* with *P* on *s₀…sₖ₋₁* also satisfies *P* on *sₖ* (*inductive step*), then *P* holds on all states reachable from *I*. [5]

**SMT solving and circuit formats.** Rather than bit-blasting everything to SAT, modern flows use *satisfiability modulo theories* solvers — Z3, Yices, CVC5, Boolector — that decide formulas over bit-vectors, arrays, and arithmetic, a natural match for word-level RTL. Yosys emits models in three interchange formats: **SMT2** (`write_smt2`, preserving word-level structure), **BTOR2** (`write_btor`, bit-precise word-level), and **AIGER** (`write_aiger`, fully bit-blasted And-Inverter Graphs for ABC's engines).

**Yosys and RTLIL.** Yosys [3] is an open-source synthesis suite whose internal representation, **RTLIL**, models designs as nets of coarse- and fine-grain cells. For formal work, Yosys elaborates Verilog/SystemVerilog into RTLIL and lowers verification constructs into dedicated **formal cells**: `$assert`, `$assume`, `$live`, `$fair`, `$cover`, plus `$anyconst`/`$anyseq` for nondeterministic signals. The `read -formal` frontend parses `assert`/`assume`/`cover` constructs inside `` `ifdef FORMAL `` regions [1][3].

**SystemVerilog assertions.** Properties are most portably expressed in the IEEE 1800 assertion subset [7]: `assert property`, `assume property`, `cover property` with clocking events, and temporal operators such as `$past`, `$stable`, `$rose`. The `smtbmc` engines consume the synthesizable subset; the fully general SVA language requires commercial Tabby CAD Suite parsers [2].

| Technique | Question answered | On success | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constrained-random simulation | Do chosen stimuli pass? | Confidence (coverage %) | Failing seed + waveform |
| Bounded model checking [6] | Violation within *k* cycles? | No bug up to depth *k* | Shortest counterexample ≤ *k* |
| *k*-induction [5] | Violation at *any* depth? | Unbounded proof of *P* | Counterexample or UNKNOWN |
| Cover properties | Is a scenario reachable? | Witness trace | Proof of unreachability |
| ABC PDR / IC3 | Violation at *any* depth? | Proof, no depth needed | Counterexample (often long) |

## 3 Methodology

**Property specification discipline.** Properties are embedded directly in the RTL, guarded so synthesis and simulation ignore them:

```verilog
module counter #(parameter WIDTH = 8)(
    input  wire             clk,
    input  wire             rst,
    output reg  [WIDTH-1:0] count
);
    always @(posedge clk) begin
        if (rst) count <= 0;
        else     count <= count + 1'b1;
    end

`ifdef FORMAL
    initial assume (rst);  // reset asserted in cycle 0

    always @(posedge clk) begin
        if ($past(rst))       assert (count == 0);
        else if ($past(!rst)) assert (count == $past(count) + 1'b1);
    end

    always @(posedge clk)
        cover (count == {WIDTH{1'b1}});  // max value is reachable
`endif
endmodule
```

Three constructs carry the specification burden. `assert` states what must hold; `assume` constrains the environment (here, reset behavior); `cover` asks the tool to *find* a trace reaching an interesting state. Nondeterministic stimulus uses `$anyconst` (stable-but-arbitrary, for configuration) and `$anyseq` (arbitrary each cycle, for adversarial inputs) — the formal analogue of constrained randomization, except the "randomization" is exhaustive.

**The `.sby` task file.** SymbiYosys is driven by a declarative configuration with four sections:

```ini
[options]
mode bmc
depth 20
expect pass

[engines]
smtbmc z3
smtbmc yices

[script]
read -formal counter.v
prep -top counter

[files]
counter.v
```

`[options]` selects the *mode* (`bmc`, `prove`, `cover`, `live`, …), the *depth* bound (default 20), multiclock handling, and the *expected* verdict — `expect fail` turns *sby* into a regression oracle for deliberately broken designs. `[engines]` lists solver backends raced in order; `[script]` is a Yosys elaboration script; `[files]` stages sources. `sby -f counter.sby` reports `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNKNOWN` and, on failure, emits a VCD trace under `counter_bmc/engine_0/` for GTKWave [1][4].

**The elaboration pipeline.** `read -formal` parses the design *with* formal constructs; `hierarchy -check -top` elaborates the module tree; `prep` runs elaboration and coarse-grain optimization; formal passes — `chformal`, `clk2fflogic`, `async2sync` — lower `$assert`/`$assume`/`$cover` cells and normalize clocks; finally `write_smt2 -wires`, `write_btor`, or `write_aiger` emits the solver model. Most *sby* failures are elaboration failures — a property referencing an optimized-away signal, or an unidentified clock — dying before any solver starts.

**The iterative workflow.** Formal campaigns proceed in a disciplined loop:

1. **Hunt with BMC.** `mode bmc` at depth 20–50 with a fast solver (Yices is frequently fastest). Fix every counterexample — these are real, usually shallow bugs.
2. **Prove with *k*-induction.** Switch to `mode prove`. An `UNKNOWN` verdict means the inductive step failed: the property holds on reachable states but is not *k*-inductive.
3. **Strengthen with lemmas.** Add helper assertions (inductive invariants). In prove mode *sby* proves all assertions simultaneously and lets each assume the others — *mutual induction* — so lemmas directly strengthen the induction hypothesis.
4. **Guard against vacuity with cover.** Over-constraining `assume`s admit proofs of anything. `mode cover` demonstrates that interesting behaviors — FIFO full, arbiter grant, pipeline flush — remain reachable, evidencing consistent assumptions.
5. **Close the loop in CI.** *sby* exits nonzero on unexpected verdicts, so tasks integrate directly into regression; the APB protocol checker, for example, runs `make prove`, `make cover`, and `expect fail` negative tests against injected bugs [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 From Verilog to SMT: The Elaboration Pipeline

The journey from RTL to a solver query crosses three representations. *Parsing* turns SystemVerilog into Yosys's AST; the `-formal` flag maps assertion constructs to formal cells instead of erroring. *Elaboration* (`hierarchy`, `proc`, `opt`, memory inference) resolves parameters, unrolls generates, and converts behavioral code into RTLIL — `$past` is implemented here via shadow registers, exactly as simulation would. *Formal lowering* rewrites the netlist for the model writer: `clk2fflogic` replaces edge-triggered flip-flops with explicit clock logic so the transition relation is a pure combinational function of state and inputs; `chformal` converts formal cells into each backend's representation; `opt_clean` removes dead logic that would bloat the formula.

Model emission decides word-level versus bit-level structure. `write_smt2 -wires` emits SMT-LIB 2 with bit-vector sorts, preserving adders and multiplexers as word-level operations so Z3's rewriting simplifies aggressively before bit-blasting. `write_btor` emits compact BTOR2; `write_aiger` emits pure And-Inverter Graphs for ABC. A useful mental model: *sby* is a compiler whose source language is RTL-plus-properties and whose targets are SMT2, BTOR2, and AIGER; `[engines]` selects the backend — a performance decision, not a semantic one.

### 4.2 Bounded Model Checking: Unrolling, Encoding, and Counterexamples

The `smtbmc` engine implements the Biere et al. scheme [6] almost literally. For bound *k*, the SMT2 model declares *k+1* copies of every state variable, asserts the initial-state predicate on copy 0, the transition relation between consecutive copies, and the disjunction of violations across all copies. One satisfiability question decides everything: `SAT` yields a model — concrete values per signal per cycle — converted into a VCD/FST trace; `UNSAT` is a *proof* of no violation within *k* cycles under the assumptions.

Two consequences follow. First, BMC counterexamples are *shortest*: solvers return minimal-length violating traces. Second, BMC is complete only relative to its bound — `PASS` at depth 20 says nothing about cycle 21. Depth selection is therefore a coverage argument: it should exceed the design's *sequential depth* (input-to-observable propagation plus internal pipeline/FIFO depth). Engineers commonly sweep depths 20 → 50 → 100 → 200 before attempting proof.

Encoding details drive performance. `$assume` constrains *every* unrolled copy. `$anyseq` becomes *free* variables per copy — this is what makes BMC exhaustive over inputs, as the solver chooses the adversarial sequence. `$anyconst` becomes one free variable shared across copies. And `expect fail` inverts verdict logic so a found counterexample is the *passing* outcome [4].

### 4.3 *k*-Induction: Base Case, Inductive Step, and Invariant Strengthening

Prove mode asks the unbounded question; `smtbmc` answers with *k*-induction [5] via two solver queries. The **base case** is BMC to depth *k*: *P* holds on all length-*k* paths from initial states. The **inductive step**: take *any loop-free* path *s₀…sₖ* — *s₀* arbitrary, *not* assumed reachable — on which *P* holds for the first *k* states, and ask whether *P* is forced on *sₖ*. If UNSAT, the Section 2 theorem applies: any reachable path extends backward *k* steps (or reaches an initial state, covered by the base case), and the inductive step propagates *P* forward one step at a time.

The *loop-free* qualifier is load-bearing: without it, paths looping through a *P*-violating state while *P* "holds" on the sampled window would defeat induction for properties that are in fact true. Engines therefore add *simple-path constraints* — pairwise disequalities between the *k+1* state copies — quadratic in *k* and a genuine cost driver at large depths.

An `UNKNOWN` verdict diagnoses *induction failure*: *P* is true but not *k*-inductive at the attempted *k*. Three remedies exist, in order of preference. **Increase *k*** — deeper windows admit weaker properties. **Strengthen with lemmas** — auxiliary assertions *L₁…Lₘ* that are (mutually) inductive; since *sby* assumes every asserted property in the inductive step, lemmas prune the unreachable states defeating induction. Finding lemmas is the creative core of formal verification: pointer-counter relations (e.g., `wr_ptr - rd_ptr == occupancy`), datapath bounds, one-hot disciplines. **Restructure the property** — split a conjunction into separately proved conjuncts; each may be inductive at small *k* even when their conjunction is not.### 4.4 Cover Properties: Reachability Witnesses and Vacuity Detection

Every `assume` is a loaded weapon pointed at the proof's validity. If the assumptions are *contradictory* — no input sequence satisfies them all — the BMC antecedent is unsatisfiable and *every* assertion proves vacuously, including assertions false in the real system. This **vacuity problem** is the most dangerous failure mode in formal verification: green checkmarks with false confidence.

**Cover properties** are the antidote. `cover (cond)` asks the tool to *find* a trace where `cond` holds — the dual of assertion checking. In `mode cover`, *sby* runs one reachability query per cover point, reporting *reached* (with witness trace) or *unreached*. A healthy formal testbench covers the design's interesting behaviors: resets complete, FIFOs fill and empty, arbiters grant every requester. An *unreached* cover point on a must-be-reachable scenario means the assumptions over-constrain (or the design is broken) — the campaign cannot be trusted until resolved.

Cover also provides **stimulus sanity for the proof itself**. When proving a FIFO never overflows, the environment assumption "never write when full" must not accidentally also block writes when *almost* full due to a fencing error — otherwise the proof succeeds while the real system overflows. A *reached* cover point on `write_enable && full` confirms the environment model still permits the dangerous scenario, making the proof's conclusion meaningful [1][4].

### 4.5 The Engine Landscape: smtbmc, ABC PDR, and BTOR2 Backends

*sby* is engine-agnostic by design; engine selection is the principal performance lever. The landscape divides along word-level versus bit-level reasoning and bounded versus unbounded algorithms.

| Engine backend | Algorithm | Strengths | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `smtbmc z3` | BMC + *k*-induction (SMT2) | Word-level rewriting; robust all-rounder | Default prove/bmc |
| `smtbmc yices` | BMC + *k*-induction (SMT2) | Often fastest on BMC queries | Bug-hunting sweeps |
| `smtbmc boolector` | BMC + *k*-induction (BTOR2) | Bit-vector/array specialist | Wide datapaths, memories |
| `abc pdr` | IC3/PDR (AIGER) | Unbounded proofs, no depth tuning; strong on control logic | Hard prove tasks |
| `abc bmc3` | BMC (AIGER) | Mature bit-level BMC | Cross-checking smtbmc |

The `smtbmc` engines share Yosys's SMT2/BTOR2 models and differ only in solver; since heuristics vary by problem shape, listing several lets *sby* race them and take the first conclusive answer. The `abc` engines consume AIGER and implement **PDR** (property-directed reachability / IC3), which builds inductive invariants incrementally as state-space clauses — often proving properties where *k*-induction stalls at `UNKNOWN`, particularly on control-heavy designs. PDR needs no depth parameter, but its counterexamples can be long, and AIGER witness-to-VCD conversion (via `aigsmt`, defaulting to Yices) occasionally fails on large witnesses.

The community-standard portfolio: `smtbmc yices` for fast BMC bug-hunting, `smtbmc z3` for the *k*-induction attempt, `abc pdr` as the heavy prover of last resort — with `mode cover` running throughout to keep the campaign honest [1][2][4].

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Arguments

We apply the full workflow to a representative design: a synchronous FIFO, depth 8, 8-bit data — the canonical first formal target. Its contract in the SystemVerilog assertion subset:

```systemverilog
`ifdef FORMAL
    always @(posedge clk)
        assert (occupancy <= DEPTH);              // no overflow

    always @(posedge clk)
        if ($past(rd_en) && $past(!rst))
            assert ($past(!empty));               // no underflow

    always @(posedge clk) begin
        assume (!wr_en || !full);                 // environment protocol
        assume (!rd_en || !empty);
    end

    always @(posedge clk) begin
        cover (full);                            // reachable behaviors
        cover (empty && $past(full));
        cover (occupancy == DEPTH/2);
    end
`endif
```

A representative campaign through the Section 3 workflow yields the following. (Figures are representative of the documented open-source flow on this design class; the *scaling behavior*, not absolute numbers, is the transferable lesson.)

| Step | Mode / engine | Depth | Verdict | Representative effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bug hunt | `bmc` / yices | 20 | **FAIL** → trace | < 1 s; finds pointer bug |
| Bug hunt (fixed) | `bmc` / yices | 50 | PASS | ~2 s |
| Proof attempt | `prove` / z3 | 10 | UNKNOWN | ~5 s; induction fails |
| Proof + lemma | `prove` / z3 | 10 | **PASS** | ~8 s; occupancy lemma added |
| Sanity | `cover` / yices | 30 | all reached | ~3 s; 3 witness traces |
| Cross-check | `abc pdr` | — | PASS | ~15 s; no depth needed |

The initial BMC failure is the methodology working as intended: an off-by-one in the full-condition logic produced a counterexample at depth 7, with `wr_en` asserted while `occupancy == DEPTH`. The first prove attempt returned `UNKNOWN` — no-overflow is true but not 10-inductive alone, because the inductive step admits unreachable states where pointers disagree with the occupancy counter. The remedy was one lemma under `` `ifdef FORMAL `` — `assert (occupancy == (wr_ptr - rd_ptr))` — proved by mutual induction alongside the property. The lemma prunes unreachable pointer/occupancy combinations, the conjunction becomes 10-inductive, and the proof closes. The cover run then confirmed `full`, the full-to-empty transition, and half-occupancy are all reachable — ruling out vacuity.

Three formal arguments summarize why this workflow is trustworthy. *First*, every BMC verdict is a theorem about the unrolled formula: `UNSAT` at depth *k* is a machine-checked proof of no violation within *k* cycles [6]. *Second*, every prove-mode `PASS` instantiates the *k*-induction soundness theorem, with base case and inductive step each discharged as separate UNSAT queries [5]. *Third*, cover reachability is the dual guarantee: a *reached* point witnesses assumption-set consistency with exercised behavior, bounding vacuity risk.

## 6 Limitations

The method's limits are as instructive as its power. **State-space explosion** is fundamental: BMC formula size grows linearly with depth but solver effort grows unpredictably, and *k*-induction's simple-path constraints are quadratic in *k* — designs beyond a few thousand state bits with deep sequential behavior routinely exhaust time or memory. Abstraction (black-boxing datapaths, narrowing widths, decomposing sub-proofs) becomes mandatory, and each abstraction is a potential soundness hole requiring its own justification.

**Practical incompleteness.** Solvers return `UNKNOWN` or time out on hard queries; PDR can diverge on complex arithmetic invariants; *k*-induction is complete only in the unreachable limit of large *k*. A failed proof is *not* evidence of a bug — distinguishing "true but unprovable at feasible cost" from "false" remains human judgment, aided by BMC's ability to find the bug if it is shallow.

**Over-constraining and vacuity** (Section 4.4) remain the dominant *methodological* failure: assumptions contradicting the real environment prove everything and mean nothing. Cover properties mitigate but cannot eliminate this risk, since cover points are engineer-chosen.

**Modeling gaps.** The open-source flow reasons about the RTL's *two-state* synchronous semantics; it does not model X-propagation pessimism, timing, metastability, power intent, or analog effects — proven RTL can still fail in silicon for physical reasons. Multi-clock and asynchronous logic need `multiclock on` and careful modeling. The free parsers support only the synthesizable SVA subset, and liveness properties (`s_eventually`) demand fairness reasoning that scales poorly [1][2][7].

**Human cost.** Good properties and lemmas demand deep design understanding; a formal testbench for a nontrivial block often rivals the RTL in effort. The open-source flow reduces *tool* cost to zero, not *expertise* cost — which is why the BMC-first workflow matters: it delivers value (real bugs, fast) before demanding proof expertise.

## 7 Conclusion

This thesis presented the open-source formal verification flow built on Yosys and SymbiYosys as a complete, rigorous alternative to simulation-only RTL verification. We traced elaboration from SystemVerilog through RTLIL to SMT2, BTOR2, and AIGER models; developed bounded model checking as exhaustive bug-hunting within a depth bound and *k*-induction — base case plus inductive step, strengthened by mutually-inductive lemmas — as the path to unbounded proof; and established cover properties as the safeguard against vacuous proofs born of over-constraining assumptions. The FIFO case study demonstrated the full *BMC-then-prove-then-cover* workflow with concrete configurations and the characteristic induction-failure-to-lemma resolution arc.

The broader significance is democratization. Formal verification was for two decades available only to teams with commercial licenses and dedicated methodology groups. The Yosys/SymbiYosys stack — free, scriptable, CI-integrable, documented in the open [1][2][3] — brings exhaustive hardware reasoning within reach of students, open-silicon projects, and small teams. Its limits are real: state-space explosion, solver incompleteness, modeling gaps, and the irreducible human cost of good properties. But within those limits it replaces sampling with proof — and for the corner-case bugs simulation cannot imagine, proof is the only technique that was ever going to find them.
