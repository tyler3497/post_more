# The Mori–Zwanzig Formalism: Projection-Operator Reduced-Order Modeling of Multiscale Dynamics

## Abstract

The Mori–Zwanzig formalism converts the closure problem of reduced-order modeling from an approximation into an exact decomposition. From the full Liouvillian dynamics of a multiscale system, a projection operator separates resolved observables from the unresolved remainder, and the Dyson–Duhamel identity rewrites the projected dynamics as a *Generalized Langevin Equation*: a Markovian term, a convolution memory integral, and an orthogonal-dynamics noise term. This article develops the theory from first principles — Mori's linear projector with its frequency matrix and memory kernel [1], Zwanzig's nonlinear conditional-expectation projector with its potential of mean force [7], the fluctuation–dissipation relations binding noise to memory, and classical closures including the Markovian limit and the t-model. We survey the data-driven renaissance — discrete-time Koopman–Mori–Zwanzig theory [2], regression-based projections [6], delay embedding with memory [4], low-rank operator compression [5] — validated on Lagrangian turbulence [3] and hypersonic boundary-layer transition [4], and close with the open problems separating the formalism's exactness in principle from its practice.

## 1 Intro

Every reduced-order model is an act of deliberate forgetting. One starts with a dynamical system of forbidding dimension — the $10^{23}$ molecules of a fluid, the all-atom coordinates of a protein, the spectral modes of a turbulent flow — and declares that only a handful of *resolved observables* matter. The rest is discarded. The question organizing a century of statistical mechanics is what price the discarded degrees of freedom exact, and whether it can be paid honestly rather than by pretending the truncation never happened [1].

The naive answer is *closure by fiat*: drop the coupling terms, or replace them with a favorite ansatz. The Mori–Zwanzig formalism takes the opposite route. Rather than approximating away the unresolved world, it *represents it exactly* — as a memory kernel and an orthogonal-dynamics term whose structure is dictated by the chosen projection. The resulting Generalized Langevin Equation (GLE) is an exact reformulation of the unresolved dynamics, with all approximation postponed to the evaluation of the memory integral or the sampling of the noise. Every classical closure — the Markovian limit, the t-model, rational kernel approximations — then appears as a controlled demolition of one specific term of the exact equation [8].

Sections 2–3 trace the lineage and derive the equation via Dyson–Duhamel; Section 4 dives into Mori, Zwanzig, orthogonal dynamics, closures, and data-driven learning; Sections 5–7 cover validation, limitations, and open directions.

The reader needs only differential equations, linear algebra, and elementary probability. By the end, they will see why the Langevin equation is not an assumption but a *theorem* about projections [7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Brownian motion to memory

The prehistory of the formalism is the prehistory of stochastic modeling. Langevin's 1908 equation,

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = -\gamma v + \xi(t), \qquad \langle \xi(t)\xi(t')\rangle = 2\gamma k_B T\,\delta(t-t'),$$

describes a heavy particle buffeted by a light solvent, entering only through a friction $\gamma$ and a white noise $\xi(t)$ bound by fluctuation–dissipation. The equation is *phenomenological*: $\gamma$ and $\xi$ were fitted, not derived from the $10^{23}$ solvent molecules. The triumph of Mori and Zwanzig, working independently in the early 1960s, was to show that this structure — deterministic drift, memory, and noise, bound by fluctuation–dissipation — is the *inevitable* consequence of projecting high-dimensional deterministic dynamics onto low-dimensional observables [1].

Mori's 1965 "Transport, Collective Motion, and Brownian Motion" cast transport coefficients of simple fluids as generalized Langevin equations for collective variables, introducing the continued-fraction memory structure and the frequency matrix [1]. Zwanzig's 1961 "Memory Effects in Irreversible Thermodynamics" showed how irreversibility and memory emerge from reversible microscopic dynamics via projection, later extended to nonlinear projections through conditional expectations [7].

### 2.2 The closure problem, stated precisely

Consider $\dot x = F(x)$ on a phase space $\Gamma \subseteq \mathbb{R}^N$ with $N$ astronomically large. Let $A(x) = (\phi_1(x), \dots, \phi_M(x))$ be $M \ll N$ resolved observables. The *closure problem* is that

$$\frac{d}{dt} A(x(t)) = \nabla A(x(t)) \cdot F(x(t))$$

depends on the full state $x(t)$, not merely on $A(t)$: no self-contained evolution equation for $A(t)$ exists *a priori*. The formalism's answer is that one does exist — but it is integro-differential, nonlocal in time, and driven by a noise term of exactly characterized statistics. Forgetting has a price, and the formalism computes the invoice [2].

### 2.3 Operator language: the Liouvillian

In the Heisenberg picture, the Liouville operator $\mathcal{L}$ acts on observables $g : \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ by

$$\mathcal{L} g = F \cdot \nabla g = \sum_{i=1}^N F_i \frac{\partial g}{\partial x_i},$$

so $g(t) = e^{t\mathcal{L}} g_0$ solves $\dot g = \mathcal{L} g$. The projector formalism manipulates $\mathcal{L}$ algebraically. Everything that follows — memory kernel, random force, fluctuation–dissipation — is a corollary of one operator identity applied to $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ [2].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The projection operators

A *projection operator* $\mathcal{P}$ maps any observable $g$ to one depending only on the resolved variables, with $\mathcal{P}^2 = \mathcal{P}$; its complement $\mathcal{Q} = \mathcal{I} - \mathcal{P}$ spans the unresolved subspace. Two canonical choices dominate:

| Projector | Definition | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Mori** | $\mathcal{P}g = \langle g, A\rangle \langle A, A\rangle^{-1} A$ (inner-product projection onto $\mathrm{span}\{A_i\}$) | Linear response, correlation functions [1] |
| **Zwanzig** | $\mathcal{P}g(a) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[g(X)\mid A(X) = a]$ (conditional expectation) | Nonlinear coarse-graining, PMF [7] |

Here $\langle f, g\rangle = \int fg\, d\mu$ is the $L^2(\mu)$ inner product under an invariant measure $\mu$. Mori's projector is *linear*: $\mathcal{P}g$ is always a linear combination of the resolved variables. Zwanzig's is the *optimal nonlinear* predictor — the mean-square-best function of the resolved variables. The choice dictates the drift, memory structure, and fluctuation–dissipation form [7]; learned regression maps [6] and Wiener projections [2] extend the same algebra.

### 3.2 The Dyson–Duhamel identity

The derivation's engine is one operator identity. For $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{PL} + \mathcal{QL}$,

$$e^{t\mathcal{L}} = e^{t\mathcal{QL}} + \int_0^t e^{(t-s)\mathcal{L}}\,\mathcal{PL}\,e^{s\mathcal{QL}}\, ds,$$

the Dyson–Duhamel (variation-of-constants) formula. Differentiating $e^{t\mathcal{L}}A$ and applying it yields:

> **Theorem (Mori–Zwanzig decomposition).** *For any projector $\mathcal{P}$ with $\mathcal{Q} = \mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}$,*
> $$\frac{d}{dt}e^{t\mathcal{L}}A = e^{t\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{PLA} + \int_0^t e^{(t-s)\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{PL}\,e^{s\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QLA}\,ds + e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QLA}.$$

The three terms are the Markovian, memory, and orthogonal-dynamics (noise) contributions. No approximation has been made: the projected future depends on the entire past through the memory integral and on unresolved initial data through $e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QL}A$, evolving under the *orthogonal* propagator from which the resolved subspace was surgically removed [2].

### 3.3 Extracting the memory kernel from data

The modern route *learns* the kernel from trajectories via a foundational identity. For Mori's projector on a single observable $v$, the normalized autocorrelation $C(t) = \langle v(t)v(0)\rangle/\langle v^2\rangle$ obeys the Volterra equation

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = -\int_0^t K(t-s)\,C(s)\,ds,$$

the content of the *second* fluctuation–dissipation theorem. On a uniform grid this becomes a triangular system solved by forward substitution:

```python
import numpy as np

def memory_kernel_from_vacf(vacf: np.ndarray, dt: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Recover the Mori-Zwanzig memory kernel K(t) from the velocity
    autocorrelation C(t) via  dC/dt = - int_0^t K(t-s) C(s) ds,
    discretized as dC_i = -dt * sum_{j<=i} K_j C_{i-j}, solved by
    forward substitution. [1][7]"""
    C = np.asarray(vacf, dtype=float)
    n = C.shape[0]
    dC = np.gradient(C, dt)
    K = np.zeros(n)
    for i in range(n):
        conv = np.sum(K[:i] * C[i-1::-1]) if i > 0 else 0.0
        K[i] = -(dC[i] / dt + conv) / C[0]
    return K
```

This Berne–Harp procedure is the seed of data-driven MZ: memory is *inverted* from correlations, not postulated [8]; for multiple observables $C(t)$ and $K(t)$ become matrices, and regression methods [6] learn the projector too.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mori's linear projector: frequency matrix, memory, and noise

With $A = (A_1, \dots, A_M)^T$ and static covariance $C = \langle A A^T\rangle$, Mori's projector $\mathcal{P}g = \langle g A^T\rangle C^{-1} A$ yields the *Mori* Generalized Langevin Equation:

$$\frac{dA(t)}{dt} = \Omega \cdot A(t) - \int_0^t K(t-s)\cdot A(s)\,ds + F(t),$$

with three sharply distinct operators:

1. **Frequency matrix** $\Omega = \langle \mathcal{L}A, A^T\rangle C^{-1}$: the reversible, Markovian streaming term — the best linear approximation of the dynamics, skew-symmetric for Hamiltonian systems by time-reversal invariance.
2. **Memory kernel** $K(t) = -\langle \mathcal{L}e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QL}A, A^T\rangle C^{-1}$: how past resolved values influence the present via excursions into the unresolved subspace. Mori's celebrated *continued fraction* expands it exactly in higher-order projected dynamics [1].
3. **Random force** $F(t) = e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QL}A$: deterministic given the full initial condition, "random" only with respect to untracked unresolved data, and orthogonal to the resolved variables at all times: $\langle F(t) A^T\rangle = 0$.

The crown jewel is the **second fluctuation–dissipation theorem**:

> **Theorem (Second fluctuation–dissipation).** *For Mori's projector,* $\langle F(t) F(s)^T\rangle = K(t-s)\, C.$ *The fluctuations that dissipate resolved motion (the kernel) also kick it (the noise), with identical statistics.* [1][7]

Given $K$, the GLE is *simulable*: sample noise of the correct covariance and integrate, never touching the unresolved variables [7].

### 4.2 Zwanzig's nonlinear projector: potential of mean force

Mori's projector is linear in the observables; Zwanzig's $\mathcal{P}g(a) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[g \mid A = a]$ is the *optimal nonlinear* predictor. For Hamiltonian systems with $\mu \propto e^{-\beta H}$ it yields [7]:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x \\ p\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} M^{-1}(x)\,p \\ -\nabla W(x) - \int_0^t K(t-s)\,M^{-1}(x(s))p(s)\,ds + F(t)\end{pmatrix},$$

with two thermodynamic objects:

- **Potential of mean force** $W(x) = -k_BT \log \int e^{-\beta H}\, d(\text{unresolved})$: the free-energy landscape after integrating out the bath. Its minima are metastable states; its barriers are rare events. Resolved dynamics is gradient-driven on $W$, *exactly*.
- **Position-dependent mass matrix** $M(x)$: the bath-induced metric on resolved coordinates — never the identity in curvilinear collective variables, and a common casualty of heuristic coarse-graining.

Fluctuation–dissipation generalizes to $\langle F(t)F(s)^T\rangle = k_BT\, K(t-s)$ acting through $M(x)$. The objects ($W$, $M$, $K$) are harder to compute — which is why machine-learned coarse-graining (force matching, relative-entropy minimization) reads as *numerical Zwanzig projection* [7][8].

The exact equation uses only $P^2 = P$ and $Q = I - P$, so it extends unchanged to regression projectors [6], Wiener projections, and delay-embedded observables [2][4] — the formalism's portability theorem in miniature:

```haskell
-- Projection-operator algebra: the MZ decomposition as types.
-- P projects onto resolved observables; Q = I - P onto the orthogonal complement.
class Projector p where
  applyP :: p -> (Phase -> Double) -> Phase -> Double
  applyQ :: p -> (Phase -> Double) -> Phase -> Double
  applyQ p g x = g x - applyP p g x          -- Q = I - P, definitionally

data Phase = Phase { resolved :: [Double], unresolved :: [Double] }

-- Exact MZ equation (schematic):
--   d/dt e^{tL} A = e^{tL} P L A                                     [Markov]
--                 + integral_0^t e^{(t-s)L} P L e^{sQL} Q L A ds      [Memory]
--                 + e^{tQL} Q L A                                    [Noise]
-- Linearity of P is never used: only P^2 = P and Q = I - P. [2][7]
```

### 4.3 Orthogonal dynamics: the hard core

The term $e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QL}A$ is the only piece still knowing the full system: $\partial_t w = \mathcal{QL} w$ is a full-phase-space PDE, as hard as the original system. Three classical strategies:

1. **Markovian approximation.** Fast-decaying orthogonal dynamics collapses the memory integral to instantaneous friction $\gamma A(t)$ — the route from GLE back to Langevin, justified by timescale separation [1].
2. **The t-model (Chorin).** For no-separation systems (turbulence), approximate $e^{t\mathcal{QL}} \approx e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ and extract the leading memory term analytically: a correction $\propto t\,\mathcal{PLQL}A$ whose memory *grows linearly with time*, capturing the secular drain into unresolved scales. It underlies the optimal-prediction program for under-resolved computation [8].
3. **Orthogonal-dynamics numerics.** Givon, Kupferman, and Stuart reformulated the orthogonal equation for co-integration with the resolved variables — *computing* the kernel on the fly, turning the formalism into an algorithm [8].

| Closure | Approximation of $e^{t\mathcal{QL}}$ | Memory form | Valid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Markovian | Fast decay $\to \delta(t-s)$ | $\gamma A(t)$ | Timescale separation |
| t-model | $\approx e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ | $\propto t \cdot \mathcal{PLQL}A$ | No separation; cascades |
| Rational / Prony | $K$ as exponentials | Auxiliary variables | Smooth decaying kernels |
| Exact numerics | None (computed) | Full integral | Universal in principle; costly [8] |

### 4.4 Fluctuation–dissipation as a design constraint

The theorems are *constraints*: a closure inventing a kernel plus independent noise violates second fluctuation–dissipation and relaxes to the wrong equilibrium. Corollary: *noise in a reduced model is derived, not chosen* — pick the projector, approximate the kernel, but never invent the noise [7].

For non-equilibrium steady states the classical theorems are replaced by generalized relations (Agarwal/Harada–Sasa forms) built on the steady-state measure; the MZ derivation is unchanged, but the kernel–noise relation gains terms — an active frontier for active and driven soft matter [2]. A TLA+ sketch of the enforced bookkeeping:

```tla

\* and equilibrium covariance C -- never chosen freely. [1][7]
Step(K, C) ==
  /\ \E noise \in Seq(Real) : noiseCovariance(noise) = K ** C
  /\ x' = Markov(x) \o MemoryConv(K, hist) \o noise
  /\ hist' = Append(Head(hist), x)
  /\ bath' = OrthogonalEvolve(bath)   \* e^{tQL}: hidden, exact

====
```

### 4.5 The data-driven renaissance: Koopman–Mori–Zwanzig

The last decade turned the formalism into a *learning architecture*. Lin, Lu, and Ying's discrete-time form is the missing link between Koopman theory and delay-embedded forecasting [2]:

$$x_{n+1} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \Omega_k(x_{n-k}) + W_{n+1}, \qquad W_{n+1} \perp \text{resolved history}.$$

Markov term $\Omega_0$, memory operators $\Omega_k$, and orthogonal noise $W$ are all *learnable from trajectory data* once a projector is fixed. Linear regression onto a dictionary recovers Extended DMD as the memoryless special case — adding memory strictly improves it; a neural-network projector yields a learned nonlinear Zwanzig projection with memory intact [6]; the Wiener-projection extension covers stochastic forcing and delays, the exact setting of sparse-sensor experiments [2][4].

**Delay embedding + memory:** delays enlarge the resolved dictionary; MZ memory corrects the residual — on Mach 6 data this minimizes generalization error, beating EDMD, Hankel-DMD, LSTMs [4]. **Low-rank compression:** SVD of two-time covariances selects the resolved subspace while exposing the memory's *spatiotemporal structure* — hysteresis tied to hot-streak generation [5]. **Regression as projection:** one algorithm unifies Mori, Zwanzig, and neural projectors — the residual *is* the orthogonal dynamics; its lagged regressions *are* the kernels [6].

The learned objects deploy as a GLE data structure, the history buffer *being* the memory:

```rust
use std::collections::VecDeque;

/// GLE integrator with learned kernel. kernel[k] = K(k*dt).
/// Noise covariance derived from `kernel` (2nd FDT), never fitted. [1][7]
pub struct GeneralizedLangevin {
    mass: f64, kernel: Vec<f64>, dt: f64,
    history: VecDeque<f64>, noise_scale: Vec<f64>,
}

impl GeneralizedLangevin {
    pub fn step<R: rand::Rng>(&mut self, markov_force: f64, rng: &mut R) -> f64 {
        let memory: f64 = self.kernel.iter().zip(self.history.iter())
            .map(|(k, h)| k * h).sum::<f64>() * self.dt;
        let noise: f64 = self.noise_scale.iter().map(|s| s * rng.gen::<f64>()).sum();
        let v_new = self.history.back().copied().unwrap_or(0.0)
            + (markov_force - memory + noise) * self.dt / self.mass;
        self.history.push_back(v_new);
        if self.history.len() > self.kernel.len() { self.history.pop_front(); }
        v_new
    }
}
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Turbulence: Lagrangian particles and the memory of the cascade

Turbulence — no timescale separation, energy cascading through unresolved scales — is the sternest test. A 2026 PNAS study applied data-driven MZ to Lagrangian particle dynamics, predicting trajectories from sparse observations with the surrounding velocity field unresolved [3]. Learned operators captured the flow's non-Markovian influence through the memory kernel, achieving accurate prediction at a fraction of full-simulation cost, with memory provably responsible for the gain over Markovian baselines. Conceptually: the cascade, the canonical "unresolved physics," is representable as *memory* — the particle's past encodes the scales it forgot [3].

### 5.2 Boundary-layer transition: sparse sensors, exact structure

On a Mach 6 flared cone only sparse wall sensors exist; the surrounding field is unobservable. Data-driven MZ with delay embedding (a) reproduced DMD's spatiotemporal structures, (b) extracted additional *hysteresis structures* from the memory kernels tied to hot-streak generation, and (c) out-predicted EDMD, Hankel-DMD, LSTM, and neural baselines at matched cost [4][5]. The kernel here is a *discovered physical object*, not a fudge factor.

### 5.3 Molecular dynamics: from alanine dipeptide to protein folding

Resolved variables are a few collective coordinates; the unresolved remainder is solvent and fast modes. Darve, Solomon, and Kia *computed* GLEs and generalized Fokker–Planck equations from MD data for alanine dipeptide, with the extracted kernel reproducing correct conformational kinetics [8]. Ayaz et al. showed protein folding on one reaction coordinate is *intrinsically non-Markovian*: the kernel carries the orthogonal coordinates' signature, and Markovian models misestimate folding rates [7] — *memory in disguise*.

### 5.4 What is theorem and what is not

1. **Exact:** the MZ equation for any projector, dynamics, and observables [2]; the second fluctuation–dissipation theorem and its nonlinear generalization [1][7]; the Volterra autocorrelation–kernel relation, hence memory's invertibility from data [1].
2. **Not exact:** every closure — Markovian, t-model, rational, truncated continued fraction. Approximations with regimes, not theorems [8].
3. **Empirical:** learned operators generalizing across regimes, interpretable memory structure, memory beating delay-embedding-only baselines [3][4][5][6].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Orthogonal dynamics is generically intractable.** The exact equation displaces closure onto $e^{t\mathcal{QL}}$ in the full phase space. Methods approximate it, compute it at full cost, or learn its *effects* from data. The formalism makes hardness *legible*, not easy [8].
2. **Memory is expensive.** The integral costs $O(M^2 \cdot T_{\text{mem}}/\Delta t)$ per step; long-memory systems (glassy, near-critical) can make the reduced model costlier than the full one. Prony/rational approximations converting memory to auxiliary Markovian variables are the standard remedy, at approximation cost [8].
3. **The projector is a choice.** Mori vs. Zwanzig vs. learned give different kernels, noise, and PMFs; no canonical choice exists. Poor resolved variables yield non-decaying kernels — the formalism correctly reporting that you resolved the wrong things [6].
4. **Stationarity.** Classical projectors need an invariant measure $\mu$; for transient or driven systems, projectors and fluctuation–dissipation must be rebuilt around non-equilibrium measures [2].
5. **Data pathologies.** Learned operators inherit sampling gaps: rare events missed, distribution shift, overfit memory tails. Exactness does not transfer to the learned approximation [4][6].
6. **Interpretability.** A neural projector's kernel lacks guaranteed physical meaning; SVD-compression approaches extracting interpretable memory structures [5] are a first step, but no general theory of *what learned memory means* exists yet.

---

## 7 Conclusion

The Mori–Zwanzig formalism is a theory of *honest forgetting*: choose what to remember, and the mathematics prices the forgetting exactly — a memory kernel, an orthogonal noise, a fluctuation–dissipation relation binding them.

Sixty years on, the arc runs from transport coefficients [1] through optimal prediction and the t-model, to the data-driven renaissance — Koopman–Mori–Zwanzig [2], regression projections [6], delay-embedded memory [4], compressed interpretable operators [5] — validated on Lagrangian turbulence [3], hypersonic transition [4][5], and protein folding [7][8].

Open problems: certified error bounds for orthogonal-dynamics approximations; principled projector and observable selection minimizing carried memory; operational generalized fluctuation–dissipation for driven systems; learned memory interpretable enough to be a *discovery*.

The deepest lesson is methodological: approximation is not the enemy, *unlocated* approximation is. By fencing every approximation into explicit, exactly defined terms, Mori and Zwanzig gave reduced-order modeling an accounting system. Everyone who has wondered what their closure threw away is asking a Mori–Zwanzig question. The honest answer is an integro-differential equation with memory — and the field's future is learning to live with what it remembers.
