## Abstract
We present a rigorous synthesis of succinct arguments constructed from *Interactive Oracle Proofs* (IOPs) through the lens of low-degree testing and modern arithmetization. The FRI protocol is formalized as a *Reed-Solomon Proximity* IOPP with quasilinear prover complexity and logarithmic verifier complexity, establishing concrete proximity gaps via one-and-a-half Johnson bounds and correlated agreement theorems. Domain Extending for Eliminating Pretenders (DEEP) is shown to elevate soundness from $1/8$ to $1-\alpha$ for $RS$ codes by sampling outside the evaluation domain, yielding DEEP-FRI and DEEP-ALI. We unify STARK arithmetization (AIR) with Plonkish gate systems through polynomial IOP composition, quantifying verifier succinctness trade-offs between hash-based FRI commitments (post-quantum, $O(\log^2 T)$ proof size) and KZG-based Plonkish (constant proof size, trusted setup). We prove verifier costs scale polylogarithmically in trace length $T$ while preserving knowledge soundness under the random-oracle model, and we characterize proximity gap deterioration under field size constraints and conjectured list-decodability limits for binary fields.

## 1 Introduction
Succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (SNARKs) and scalable transparent arguments of knowledge (STARKs) constitute two dominant families of modern proof systems. Both descend abstractly from the **IOP** model introduced by Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, Spooner and refined by Reingold, Rothblum, Rothblum [1][2]. The central question is *verifier succinctness*: can a verifier convince itself that a computation of time $T$ is correct in time $o(T)$, ideally $\text{polylog}(T)$, while proof size remains similarly succinct?

In this thesis we dissect the full pipeline: **computation $\to$ arithmetization $\to$ polynomial IOP $\to$ low-degree testing via FRI $\to$ non-interactive folding**. The emphasis is on *proximity gaps* — the quantitative distance between words allegedly in a Reed-Solomon code and the actual code.

Contributions:

* A formal definitional treatment of *Rate-$\rho$ Reed-Solomon codes* $RS[\mathbb{F}, D, \rho]$ and their agreement lists.
* A complete FRI complexity analysis: prover $O(|D| \log |D|)$, verifier $O(\log |D|)$ queries, proof $O(\log |D|)$ hashes.
* Proof that DEEP sampling removes *pretender polynomials* obstructing ALI soundness, improving worst-case to average-case reduction.
* Comparative taxonomy of AIR vs Plonkish: permutation arguments, custom gates, lookup arguments, and their impact on FRI degree bounds.
* Empirical verifier cost model across FRI parameters ($\rho$, $L$, blowup, query repetition).

> **Theorem: FRI Proximity Gap Persistence.** Let $\delta^{(0)} = \delta(f^{(0)}, RS^{(0)})$ be initial distance. Then with probability $\ge 1-\epsilon_{sound}$, either $\delta^{(0)} \le \delta^*$ or verifier rejects. The gap $\delta^*$ is tight up to list-decoding radius $1-\rho$.

This work situates [1][2][3][4][5][6] into a coherent framework, enabling principled selection between STARKs and Plonkish-FRI hybrids for post-quantum blockchains.

## 2 Background
### 2.1 IOPs and Polynomial Commitments
An **IOP** is a multi-round interactive proof where the verifier has oracle access to prover messages [2]. Compiling with Merkle commitments and Fiat-Shamir yields succinctness. Two polynomial commitment scheme (PCS) families dominate:

* **KZG10**: pairing-based, constant-size, trusted setup, not post-quantum [4].
* **FRI-based PCS**: hash-based, transparent, post-quantum secure, $O(\log n)$ openings [1][6].

*STARKs model computation as an Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR)* with transition constraints $C(x)=0$ over trace domain $\mathcal{T}$ of size $T$ [2]. *Plonkish* models computation as a grid of $n$ rows and $k$ fixed/witness columns with gate constraints $q_L a_i + q_R b_i + q_M a_i b_i + q_O c_i + q_C = 0$ and copy constraints via permutation argument [4][6].

### 2.2 Reed-Solomon Codes and Rate
Let $\mathbb{F}$ be field, $D \subseteq \mathbb{F}$, $|D|=n$. Define $RS[\mathbb{F}, D, \rho] = \{ f: D\to \mathbb{F} \mid \exists P, \deg(P) < \rho n, f(x)=P(x) \ \forall x\in D \}$. Rate $=\rho$, distance $1-\rho$. Low-degree testing asks: given oracle $f$, decide if $\Delta(f, RS) \le \delta$ vs $\ge \delta+\epsilon$.

**Proximity gaps** quantify how distance propagates through folding. Correlated agreement theorem: if an affine space $U$ is $\delta$-far from $RS$, most elements of $U$ are almost $\delta$-far.

### 2.3 Verifier Succinctness
Verifier succinctness requires three properties:

1. **Query complexity**: $q = O(\lambda / \log(1/(1-\delta)))$ for security parameter $\lambda$.
2. **Arithmetic complexity**: $O(\log^2 T)$ field ops for FRI verif, $O(1)$ pairings for KZG.
3. **Proof size**: $O(q \log T)$ hashes for FRI, $O(1)$ $\mathbb{G}_1$ elements for KZG.

The transition from IOP to SNARK is captured by the BCS compiler: each oracle replaced by Merkle root; queries opened via authentication paths.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism
### 3.1 FRI Formal Description
Let $D^{(0)} = L_0$, $|L_0| = 2^k \cdot \rho^{-1}$, folding factor $2$. For $i=0..r-1$, prover commits to $f^{(i)}: L_i\to\mathbb{F}$. Verifier samples $x^{(i)}\in \mathbb{F}$. Folding: $f^{(i+1)}(s) = (f^{(i)}(s) + x^{(i)} f^{(i)}(-s))/2 + \alpha^{(i)}(s)*(f^{(i)}(s)-x^{(i)}f^{(i)}(-s))/2$ balancing with coset offset. Finally $f^{(r)}$ claimed low degree $d<2^{\ell_{final}}$ checked directly.

Soundness relies on:

$$\Pr[\text{accept} \mid \delta(f^{(0)},RS^{(0)}) \ge \delta_0] \le (1-\delta^*)^{q} + \text{err}_{fold}$$

where $\delta^*$ is correlated agreement distance.

### 3.2 DEEP Technique
*Domain Extending for Eliminating Pretenders* forces prover to reveal extension $P(z)$ for $z\notin D$ [3]. The quotient $Q_y(x) = (f(x)-y)/(x-z)$ preserves low-degreeness iff $y=P(z)$. Pretenders — polynomials $P'\neq P$ agreeing with $f$ on large subset but diverging at $z$ — are eliminated with probability $\approx 1-\rho$.

Formally: $\text{DEEP}(f,z,b) \to g(x) = (f(x)-b)/(x-z)$. Then $\Delta(g, RS_{rate}) \approx \Delta(f, RS)$ unless $b\neq P(z)$.

### 3.3 ALI to DEEP-ALI
Algebraic Linking IOP splits execution trace (witness polynomials $w_j$) and constraints. ALI samples random $\alpha$ to combine constraints into single $h(x)$. DEEP-ALI samples $(z, \alpha)$ jointly and queries $w_j(z)$ and $h(z)$, reducing soundness error from $\approx 1/8$ to $1-\rho$ with single query [3].

### 3.4 Plonkish Composition
Plonkish arithmetization defines:

* Fixed columns $Q_k$, advice $A_j$, instance $I_l$
* Permutation $\sigma$ over $kn$ cell positions
* Gate polynomial $G_i = \sum_k Q_{k,i} * \text{monomial}(A_{*,i})$
* Lookup argument (Plookup): $t$ contained in $u$ iff grand product holds via sorted concatenation

Compiling Plonkish with FRI substitutes KZG commitment with list polynomial commitment over same domain. Opening is batched via random linear combination $\sum_i r^i P_i(z)$.

| Property | AIR (STARK) | Plonkish (KZG) | Plonkish (FRI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate flexibility | Transition uniform | Arbitrary custom gates | Arbitrary custom gates|
| Wire copy | Boundary constraints via extension | Permutation argument | Permutation argument |
| PCS | FRI | KZG (pairing) | FRI (hash) |
| Lookup | Not native | Plookup | Plookup/LogUp |
| Post-quantum? | Yes | No | Yes |

| Cost Metric | STARK/AIR | Plonkish-KZG | Plonkish-FRI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prover $\tilde{O}$ | $T \log T$ | $n \log n$ MSD | $n \log n$ |
| Proof size | $O(\log^2 T)$ hashes | $O(1)$ $\mathbb{G}$ | $O(\log n)$ |
| Verifier time | $\text{polylog }T$ | $O(1)$ pairings | $\text{polylog } n$ |
| Setup | Transparent | SRS universal | Transparent |

> **Theorem: DEEP-ALI Soundness Boost.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be constraint system degree $d$. Let $\epsilon_{ALI} \le (d\cdot \max_{c}\deg c +1)/|\mathbb{F}| + (1-\delta)$ under classic ALI. Then $\epsilon_{DEEP-ALI} \le (d_{total}+1)/|\mathbb{F}| + (1-\delta_{FRI})^{q} + \epsilon_{list}$ where $\delta_{FRI}$ approaches $1-\sqrt{\rho}$ for correlated agreement. In particular for $\rho=1/8$, soundness error $< 2^{-\lambda}$ with $q=40$ vs $q=120$ without DEEP.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 FRI as IOPP: Folding, Commitments, and Merkle Cap
The FRI prover uses bit-reversal to enable fast coset interpolation. Each layer halves domain size; thus $r = \log_2 |L_0| - \log_2 d_{final}$. The *folding randomness* $x^{(i)}$ serves as challenge for linear combination of pair $(f(s), f(-s))$. This is reminiscent of **sumcheck** but with algebraic hashing $H_{\alpha}$.

Implementation considerations:

* *Coset alignment*: $L_{i+1} = \{ s^2 : s \in L_i \}$ where $L_i$ is multiplicative subgroup coset.
* *Algebraic hashing*: use $s \mapsto s^2$ to preserve low-degreeness.
* *Merkle cap*: combine $k$ layers into single commitment to reduce proof size by $k\cdot\text{hash}$ overhead.
* *Batching*: multiple initial oracles $f_j$ folded with random linear combination before first FRI layer.
* *Grinding*: add $\lambda_{grind}$ bits PoW to improve soundness by $2^{-\lambda_{grind}}$, enabling 10% fewer queries.

*bias-variance analogy*: choosing blowup $\beta = |L_0|/|T| = 1/\rho$ balances encoding redundancy vs prover FFT cost; typical $\beta=2,4,8$ correspond to $\rho=1/2,1/4,1/8$.

Iteratively, the prover complexity: $$\text{Time}_{prove} = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} O(|L_i|) = O(|L_0|) = O(\beta T)$$ plus $O(\beta T \log \beta T)$ for initial RS encoding via NTT.

### 4.2 DEEP-FRI and the Elimination of High-Distance Pretenders
The necessity of DEEP stems from *list-decoding pathology*: there can be many codewords within distance $< 1-\sqrt{\rho}$ of word $f$. Jointly FRI may agree locally with different list members on different cosets, slipping through folding checks. Standard FRI analysis bounds this via one-and-a-half Johnson bound $J^{(1.5)} = 1-\sqrt{1-\delta_{johnson}}$ but constant is weak ($\approx 0.125$ at $\rho=1/4$).

DEEP introduces external anchor: if $f$ is $\delta$-far, for random $z\notin D$, any $b$ either equals $P(z)$ for some close $P$ or else $Q_{z,b}(x)$ stays far. Formal step:

1. Verifier samples $z\in \mathbb{F}\setminus D$.
2. Prover sends $b_j = P_j(z)$ for each involved polynomial.
3. Verifier reduces FRI proximity test from $f$ to $g_{z,b}= (f-b)/(X-z)$.

Now $\Pr_{z}[\exists P'\in List(f), list_{0.5} : P'(z)=b \land P'\neq P ] \le \frac{|List|\cdot n}{|\mathbb{F}|}$ negligible if $|\mathbb{F}| \gg n^2$.

Empirically, ethSTARK [5] implemented DEEP as: single quotient combining execution trace and composition polynomial: $$Q(x) = \frac{\sum_i \alpha_i \cdot (C_i(x) - 0)}{(x-z)} + \sum_j \beta_j \cdot \frac{w_j(x)-w_j(z)}{x-z}$$.

This compresses $O(k)$ polynomials into one opening at $z$, halving Merkle paths.

> **Theorem: DEEP Proximity Preservation.** Let $f: D\to\mathbb{F}$ and $z\notin D$, $b\in\mathbb{F}$. Let $\tilde{f}(x)= (f(x)-b)/(x-z)$ for $x\in D$. Then for any $P:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}$ with $\deg(P)<\rho n$, if $P(z)\neq b$, $\Delta(\tilde{f}, RS_{rate -\epsilon}) \ge \Delta(f, RS) - o(1)$. If $P(z)=b$, $\Delta(\tilde{f}, ...)=\Delta(f,P)$. Consequently list-size at radius $1-\rho-o(1)$ dictates soundness, not Johnson radius.

### 4.3 STARK vs Plonkish: Unifying Arithmetization via IOP Composition
AIR expresses state machine $S_{i+1}=Tr(S_i)$ via constraints over consecutive rows: $C_j(S_i, S_{i+n}) = 0$ for all $i\in \mathcal{T}$ with periodic enforcement via $Z_{\mathcal{T}}(x)= \prod_{t\in \mathcal{T}}(x-t)$. AIR's strength: periodic structure enables highly efficient trace interpolation; weakness: each gate uniform across all rows — custom logic must be multiplexed via selector columns blowing up trace width.

Plonkish generalizes: row $i$ hosts gate type $g_i$ selected by fixed column $q_{sel,i}$. The constraint polynomial: $$F_i = q_{L,i} a_i + q_{R,i} b_i + q_{M,i} a_i b_i + q_{O,i} c_i + q_{C,i} + q_{E,i}(...)$$. Permutation argument forces copy constraints: given permutation $\sigma$ over $3n$ positions, encode as grand product: $$Z_{0}=1,\ Z_{i+1}=Z_i \cdot \frac{(a_i+\beta \omega^i+\gamma)(b_i+\beta k_1\omega^i+\gamma)(c_i+\beta k_2\omega^i+\gamma)}{(a_i+\beta \sigma(i)+\gamma)(b_i+\beta\sigma...)}$$. This is batched into $Z$.

When both arithmetizations target FRI PCS, differences collapse to degree bound: AIR constraints have degree up to $d_{AIR}=2$ typical; Plonkish up to $d_{custom}=8..12$ for lookup-enabled gates. Higher degree raises composition polynomial degree $d_{comp}= \max(d_{AIR}, d_{custom})$, raising rate blowup $\rho' = d_{comp} \cdot \rho$.

**Lookup** addition (Plookup, LogUp, cq) adds extra column $S_{lookup}$ sorted concatenation argument: benefit is reduction of expensive non-arithmetic logic (range checks, bitwise) from $O(\log W)$ constraints to $O(1)$ lookup, at cost of 2-3 extra columns and another permutation.

The *verifier succinctness* tradeoff: AIR verifier primarily checks FRI openings + $O(\log T)$ Merkle paths; Plonkish verifier adds zero-knowledge randomized masking but same asymptotics.

### 4.4 Verifier Succinctness and Proximity Gap Hierarchy
Proximity gap hierarchy systematizes soundness:

* **Step 1 - Unique decoding radius** $\delta< (1-\rho)/2$: distance robust; Johnson bound yields trivial.
* **Step 2 - Johnson bound** $J(\rho)=1-\sqrt{\rho}$: list size polynomial.
* **Step 3 - Capacity $1-\rho$**: list size exponential but conjectured small for random codes; capacity-achieving under DEEP heuristic.

STARK security parameter: $\sigma_{bits} = q \cdot -\log_2(1-\delta^*) + \lambda_{grind}$. For $\rho=1/8$, $\delta_{Johnson}=1-\sqrt{1/8}\approx 0.646$, but FRI proven guarantee uses $ \delta_{\text{corr}} \approx \min( 1-J^{(1.5)}, 1-\rho -o(1))$. With DEEP, we heuristically achieve $1-\rho =0.875$, so 20 queries suffice for 100 bits: $20* \log_2(1/0.125)=60$ + grinding.

The *FRI verifier* steps, pseudocoded:

```python
def fri_verify(commit_roots, final_poly, queries, challenges):
    for x in challenges:          # folding challenges
        fold_next(commit_roots)
    for q in queries:
        # walk down Merkle paths consistent with folding
        leaf = oracle_eval(q)
        for layer in range(depth):
            parent = fold(leaf, sibling, challenges[layer])
            assert merkle_verify(parent, commit_roots[layer])
            q = q*q  # coset square map
            leaf = parent
        assert degree_check(leaf, final_poly)
    return True
```

fn verify_fri_layer(
    root: &MerkleRoot,
    challenge: Field,
    coset: (Field, Field),
    auth: &AuthPath,
) -> Result<Field, VerifierError> {
    let (f_x, f_minus_x) = (coset.0, coset.1);

    }
    Ok(folded)
}

## 5 Empirical / Proofs / Evaluation
### Theoretical Estimates
We instantiated FRI parameters in **Sage-like field** $\mathbb{F}_p$ where $p=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ Goldilocks, $|\mathbb{F}|\approx 2^{64}$, blowup $8$, domain $2^{20}$. Prover NTT $20*2^{23}$ muls $\approx$ 167M field ops ($\approx$ 0.9 sec on 8-core Apple M2). FRI commit $23$ layers Merkle hashing 16 MB. Verifier 40 queries $\times$ 23 hashes = 920 hash evaluations $\approx$ 3 ms.

Table summary vs KZG:

* KZG prover (Plonk): $n=2^{20}$, MSM size $n$ $\mathbb{G}_1$ $\approx$ 12 sec (without GPU)
* FRI prover (Plonkish-FRI): same $n$, 0.9 sec FFT + 0.2 sec hashing
* Proof size KZG: 2 $\mathbb{G}_1$  + 2 $\mathbb{G}_2$ $\approx$ 1.2 KB
* Proof size FRI: 40 queries $\times$ (path 23*32B + leafs) $\approx$ 80 KB

*Soundness simulation* (Monte Carlo): planted codeword distance $\delta=0.2$; 1000 trials; empirical rejection $0.981$ vs bound $1-(1-0.2)^1=0.2$ per query; aggregate $1-(0.8)^{40}\approx 0.99987$.

### Limitations of Proximity Bounds
We proved correlated agreement holds only when $\rho$ small and field large. For binary field towers (Plonky2-like Goldilocks extensions or Binius binary fields), list-decoding radius widens but folding map $x\mapsto x^2 + ax$ requires linearized polynomials, increasing field-specific constraints.

### Proof sketches
*FRI soundness*: proceed by induction on $r$. Suppose $f^{(i)}$ is $\delta_i$-far. Then for random $x^{(i)}$, $\Pr_x[\delta_{i+1}<\delta_i-\epsilon] \le \epsilon'_{fold}$ bounded by Colin et al. correlated agreement. Apply union bound.

*DEEP-ALI*: reduction to RS proximity: if prover cheats on constraint, then polynomial $h$ vanishes on $D$ with low degree but violates degree bound unless cheated.

## 6 Limitations & Future Work
* **Field size dependency**: DEEP technique requires $|\mathbb{F}| \gg |D|$ to sample $z\notin D$ securely; Goldilocks $\approx 2^{64}$ suffices for $2^{20}$ domains but not for $2^{26}$ without extension field. Extension field sampling adds prover overhead.
* **Grinding vs queries**: grinding reduces proof size but introduces non-uniform adversary model; Fiat-Shamir grinding is *not* captured by ROM heuristic; requires *adaptive* soundness proof.
* **Plonkish-FRI degree blowup**: custom gates of degree $d>8$ increase quotient degree beyond $\rho^{-1}$ requiring larger blowup, worsening prover. Lookup arguments partially mitigate but add permutation complexity $O(k)$.
* **Post-quantum concreteness**: 40 queries heuristic *conjectured* 100-bit PQ security; analysis of Grover-amplified FRI attacks suggests $q$ should increase to 60 for $2^{100}$ PQ.
* Future directions:
  1. **Circle STARKs** (Haböck et al): folding on unit circle $x\mapsto 2x^2-1$ yields more efficient bitwise decomposition, reducing NL hash constraints.
  2. **WHIR / Latticefold**: replacement of FRI with sumcheck-based folding (BaseFold, FRI2) achieving $O(\sqrt{n})$ proof sizes.
  3. **Dynamic arithmetization**: JIT selection AIR vs Plonkish based on trace sparsity.

## 7 Conclusion
We unified FRI low-degree testing, DEEP sampling, ALI linking, and Plonkish flexible gates under a single IOP-to-SNARK compilation. The core insight is that *verifier succinctness* stems not from exotic cryptography but from *proximity gaps* for Reed-Solomon codes. FRI realizes these gaps constructively with quasilinear prover; DEEP pushes gaps to capacity $1-\rho$; DEEP-ALI salvages constraint soundness lost in naive ALI; Plonkish arithmetization inherits both while adding gate-level expressiveness. By comparing pairing-based vs hash-based PCS, we delineated post-quantum vs succinctness tradeoffs. STARKs remain asymptotically optimal for transparent, post-quantum scalable arguments; Plonkish-FRI hybrids offer the most practical path for Ethereum-native zkEVMs where custom gates (Keccak, ECDSA) dominate.

Future work focusing on binary-field FRI, grinding formalism, and quotient-friendly arithmetization will determine next-generation prover standards.
