# Adaptive Low-Latency Video Streaming at Scale: CMAF Chunked Transfer, MPC-Based ABR, Pensieve Reinforcement Learning, QUIC 0-RTT Migration, and SVC Temporal Layer Switching

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, specification-first treatment of adaptive low-latency video streaming at scale: cmaf chunked transfer, mpc-based abr, pensieve reinforcement learning, quic 0-rtt migration, and svc temporal layer switching, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 production practice. We formalize cost models, invariants, and coherence protocols spanning adaptive video streaming systems, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE/NIST standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, and ACM DL. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and heterogeneous hardware traces including H100 CC, 96c Genoa, NVLink4 600GBps, RoCEv2 400G CX7, Micron7450 1.6TB NVMe ZNS, Loihi2 Oheo Gulch Lava0.8, USRP X310 6GHz IQ streams. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds, deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance.
