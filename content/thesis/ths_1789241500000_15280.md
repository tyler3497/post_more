# Univalent Foundations in Practice: The Univalence Axiom, Higher Inductive Types, and the Cubical Computational Interpretation of Homotopy Type Theory

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) interprets Martin-Löf dependent type theory as a synthetic language for spaces: types are spaces, terms are points, and identity proofs are paths, with higher identity proofs as homotopies between paths. This thesis develops the logical anatomy of the univalence axiom, which identifies equalities between types with equivalences between them, and of higher inductive types (HITs), which freely generate types by point, path, and higher-path constructors. We present the classical encode–decode method that computes π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ, the constructive cubical interpretation that restores computational canonicity through the Glue type former, and the synthetic homotopy program that has derived the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the Hopf fibration, and Brunerie's determination of π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/nℤ entirely inside type theory. Throughout we contrast axiomatic HoTT, where univalence is postulated and computation gets stuck, with cubical type theory, where univalence is a theorem with explicit computational behavior. We close with open problems: the constructivity of π₄(S³), normalization for all HITs, and scaling synthetic reasoning toward classical unstable homotopy theory.

## 1 Introduction

For most of the twentieth century, the *foundations of mathematics* were understood to be settled: Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with choice (ZFC) provided the lingua franca in which nearly all mathematical statements could be encoded. Yet encoding is not the same as *expression*. In set theory, isomorphic structures are related but distinct objects; every argument that "identification up to isomorphism" is harmless must be carried out by hand, and the encoding of higher-dimensional mathematical objects — spaces, homotopies, coherence data — is notoriously bureaucratic.

Homotopy type theory proposes a different settlement. It begins with *intensional Martin-Löf dependent type theory*, a formal system in which the identity type `x = y` is itself a type whose inhabitants are *proofs of equality*, and adds two new principles: **Voevodsky's univalence axiom**, which states that equivalences between types coincide with equalities between types, and **higher inductive types**, which allow types to be generated not only by point constructors but by path constructors and higher-path constructors. Under the *homotopy interpretation*, first articulated through the groupoid model of Hofmann and Streicher and developed systematically in the HoTT Book [1], these additions turn type theory into a *synthetic* theory of spaces: rather than defining a space as a set of points equipped with a topology, one reasons directly with points, paths, homotopies, and their higher analogues as primitive logical notions.

> **Theorem (The univalence slogan):** *In univalent foundations, to give an equality of types is to give an equivalence of types, and all constructions of type theory automatically respect this identification. Isomorphic structures are therefore identified once and for all, as a matter of logic rather than of convention.*

This thesis has three aims. **First**, to explain with precision what univalence says and why it breaks the computational canonicity of traditional type theory. **Second**, to show how higher inductive types supply a genuinely *synthetic* homotopy theory — one in which π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ can be proved without ever mentioning open sets, continuous maps, or the real line. **Third**, to present the *cubical* resolution of the computability problem: a reformulation of the theory in which univalence and function extensionality are provable theorems with computational content, vindicating the original vision of a foundation that is simultaneously invariant, logical, and executable [3][6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From intensional identity to paths

In Martin-Löf type theory, every type `A` comes equipped with an *identity type* `x =_A y` for `x, y : A`, governed by the eliminator known as **path induction** (the J-rule): to prove `P(x, y, p)` for all `x, y : A` and `p : x = y`, it suffices to prove it when `x ≡ y` and `p ≡ refl_x`.

Crucially, intensional type theory does *not* prove uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP): there may be many distinct inhabitants of `x = y`, and inhabitants of `p = q` for `p, q : x = y`, and so on without bound.

| h-level | Name | Characterization | Example |
|---------|------|------------------|---------|
| −2 | contractible | `Σ x:A, Π y:A, x = y` | unit type `𝟙` |
| −1 | mere proposition | `Π x y:A, x = y` | `n ≤ m` |
| 0 | set (h-set) | `x = y` is a proposition for all `x, y` | `ℕ`, `ℤ` |
| 1 | groupoid (1-type) | `x = y` is a set for all `x, y` | the circle `S¹` |
| 2 | 2-groupoid | one level higher | the 2-sphere `S²` |
| ∞ | ∞-groupoid | no truncation | a general type |

Voevodsky's h-level hierarchy distinguishes *sets* — types whose equality is a mere proposition — from genuinely higher types, a distinction invisible to set theory but central to univalent mathematics [1][7]. The subuniverse of h-sets in a univalent universe emulates classical set theory while higher types carry intrinsic homotopical content.

### 2.2 The univalence axiom

For types `A, B` in a universe `U`, there is a canonical map

```
idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)
```

sending `refl_A` to the identity equivalence. **Univalence** asserts that `idtoeqv` is itself an equivalence: equalities of types *are* equivalences of types. An immediate consequence is *function extensionality* — pointwise equal functions are equal — which is independent of plain Martin-Löf type theory but follows from univalence [1].

Univalence is a *structuralist* axiom: it enforces the principle that isomorphic structures are identical, so that any construction expressible in the theory automatically respects equivalence. Transporting along `ua(e) : A = B` moves not only points but entire structures — groups, rings, categories — along equivalences, with no manual coherence work [1].

But univalence has a computational cost. Added as an axiom, it introduces a closed term of type `ℕ` — for instance, one that inspects whether `ua(e)` is `refl` — that is provably equal to `0` yet does not reduce to `0`. *Canonicity fails*: not every closed term of natural-number type computes to a numeral.

### 2.3 Synthetic versus analytic homotopy theory

Classical algebraic topology is *analytic*: a space is a set with extra structure, and homotopical invariants are computed by manipulating that structure (singular chains, covering spaces, fibrations of sets). Synthetic homotopy theory inverts the priority: spaces, points, paths, and homotopies are taken as *primitive*, and consequences are deduced logically.

## 3 Methodology

Our exposition follows the standard methodology of the univalent foundations literature [1], combining three modes of argument.

1. **Axiomatic development.** We work in Martin-Löf type theory extended with the univalence axiom and a schema of higher inductive types, as presented in the HoTT Book [1]. This is the *logical* core: all synthetic proofs are carried out here and are valid in every model of the axioms.

2. **The encode–decode method.** Introduced by Licata and Shulman to compute loop spaces [2], this is the principal technique for *characterizing* identity types of HITs. One "guesses" the answer — e.g., that `Ω(S¹)` is `ℤ` — encodes it as a type family over the HIT, and proves by the HIT's induction principle that encode and decode are mutually inverse. The method is the synthetic analogue of constructing a universal cover.

3. **Model-theoretic and computational analysis.** We compare three semantic situations: (a) the *simplicial model*, which establishes consistency classically [6]; (b) the *cubical sets model* of Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg, which is constructive and yields a type theory where univalence computes [3]; and (c) the *Cartesian* cubical variant with a syntactic uniform Kan operation, whose canonicity and normalization have been established, including a mechanized proof in Agda [8]. Wherever possible we track which theorems hold *definitionally* (by computation) versus merely *propositionally* (up to a path).

We do not assume fluency with proof assistants, but all central arguments have been formalized: the π₁(S¹) computation in Agda by Licata and Shulman [2], the Freudenthal suspension theorem and the Hopf fibration in Brunerie's thesis [4], and the Cartesian cubical metatheory inside Agda-as-internal-language [8].

## 4 Univalence, Paths, and Higher Inductive Types

### 4.1 The anatomy of the univalence axiom

Univalence is best understood as a statement about the *universe* `U` as a space. In the homotopy interpretation, `U` is the classifying space of small types — the object classifier of the ∞-topos. The path space `A =_U B` is the space of *identifications* of `A` and `B`, while `A ≃ B` is the space of *equivalences*.

> **Theorem (Structure identity principle):** *For any notion of structure definable in univalent foundations — groups, rings, categories, metric spaces — the canonical map from identifications of structured types to structure-preserving equivalences is an equivalence. In particular, isomorphic groups are equal as groups, and every group-theoretic construction transports along group isomorphisms automatically [1].*

The proof is a one-line application of univalence to the type of structured objects. This is the precise sense in which univalent foundations *implement* mathematical structuralism: the "abuse of notation" by which mathematicians identify isomorphic objects becomes a theorem of the logic.

One subtle consequence is that univalence is *incompatible* with Streicher's axiom K (uniqueness of identity proofs): in a univalent universe containing the booleans, there are two distinct proofs of `𝟚 =_U 𝟚`, since the nontrivial automorphism of `𝟚` yields a nontrivial loop in the universe [1]. Univalence therefore forces the theory to be genuinely higher-dimensional.

```agda
-- The univalence axiom, stated in Agda-like syntax
postulate
  ua   : {A B : Type} → (A ≃ B) → (A ≡ B)
  uaβ  : {A B : Type} (e : A ≃ B) → coe (ua e) ≡ equivFun e
```

Here `coe : A ≡ B → A → B` is transport (coercion) along an identification; `uaβ` — which is *not* part of the axiom but is provable in cubical type theory — says that coercing along `ua e` computes as applying `e` [3].

### 4.2 Path algebra and the Eckmann–Hilton argument

Before tackling HITs, we need the *algebra of paths*. For `p : x = y` and `q : y = z`, path induction defines concatenation `p ∙ q : x = z`; every path has an inverse `p⁻¹`, and concatenation is associative and unital *up to higher paths* — the familiar coherence data of an ∞-groupoid, all generated mechanically by path induction [1].

> **Theorem (Eckmann–Hilton):** *Let `α, β : refl_x = refl_x` be 2-loops at `x`. Then `α ∙ β = β ∙ α`: the second homotopy group — and hence every higher homotopy group — is abelian.*

The proof is a jewel of synthetic reasoning. A 2-loop can be whiskered horizontally or composed vertically; the two compositions agree up to a 3-path (the interchange law), and both unit laws coincide, forcing commutativity [1]. No topology is invoked: the argument uses only the induction principles of identity types.

```haskell
-- Path concatenation and the Eckmann–Hilton interchange, schematically
(∙)  :: Path x y -> Path y z -> Path x z
whiskL :: Path x y -> Path2 p q -> Path2 (r ∙ p) (r ∙ q)
-- horizontal and vertical composition of 2-paths agree up to a 3-path:
interchange :: Path2 ((a ⊗ b) ∙ (c ⊗ d)) ((a ∙ c) ⊗ (b ∙ d))
```

### 4.3 Higher inductive types: the circle as a laboratory

An ordinary inductive type is generated by *point* constructors. A **higher inductive type** additionally has *path* constructors — identifications freely adjoined between terms — and potentially higher-path constructors, together with an induction principle that demands coherence data for every constructor [1]. The general schema was conceived at Oberwolfach in 2011 by Bauer, Lumsdaine, Shulman, and Warren, and developed by Brunerie and Licata for use in proof assistants [1].

The paradigmatic HIT is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The induction principle says: to define `f : (x : S¹) → P x`, give `b : P base` and a *dependent path* `l : b ≡[ P ]_loop b` lying over `loop`; the computation rules state `f base ≡ b` (definitionally, in implementations) and `apd f loop ≡ l` (propositionally) [1].

The critical subtlety is that HITs do *not* compute definitionally on path constructors in axiomatic HoTT: `apd f loop` is only propositionally equal to `l`.

### 4.4 The cubical computational interpretation

The breakthrough of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg was to replace the abstract interval of paths by a *formal interval* `I` with endpoints and de Morgan algebra structure, interpreting types as *cubical sets* — presheaves on a cube category equipped with a uniform Kan filling operation [3]. In this setting:

- A *path* in `A` is a map `I → A`; path types become *definable*, and function extensionality is provable by a direct cube manipulation.
- **Glue types** turn an equivalence `e : A ≃ B` (valid on a face of a cube) into a type that is `A` on that face and `B` elsewhere, with definitional equalities governing transport. Univalence is then a *theorem*: `ua e = Glue [φ ↦ (A, e)] B`, and `coe (ua e)` computes as `equivFun e` [3].
- Huber proved *canonicity*: every closed term of type `ℕ` in cubical type theory reduces to a numeral. Univalence no longer gets stuck [3][6].

> **Theorem (Computational univalence):** *In cubical type theory, there is a term `ua : (A ≃ B) → Path U A B` such that transporting along `ua e` is definitionally the application of `e`. Hence every use of univalence in a synthetic proof has an operational reading as a program transformation [3].*

Later work refined this picture. The *Cartesian* cubical type theory of Angiuli et al. replaces connections and reversal by diagonals and symmetries, gives a syntactic description of the uniform Kan operation with judgmental equalities per type former, and verifies the construction — including univalence of the universe — by a mechanized proof in Agda used as the internal language of cubical sets [8].

| Feature | Axiomatic HoTT | Cubical type theory |
|---------|---------------|---------------------|
| Univalence | postulated axiom | provable theorem |
| Function extensionality | consequence of UA | directly provable |
| Canonicity for `ℕ` | fails (stuck terms) | holds (Huber) [3] |
| HIT path computation | propositional only | computational via composition [8] |
| Model | Kan simplicial sets, classical [6] | Kan cubical sets, constructive [3] |
| Proof assistants | Coq/HoTT, Agda (axiomatic) | Cubical Agda, cubicaltt, RedPRL |

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

Synthetic homotopy theory earns its keep by *computing*. The flagship result is the characterization of the loop space of the circle, due to Licata and Shulman [2].

> **Theorem (Licata–Shulman):** *`Ω(S¹, base) ≃ ℤ`; equivalently, `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ` and all higher homotopy groups of `S¹` vanish.*

*Proof sketch (encode–decode).* Define `encode : ℤ → (base = base)` by `encode(n) = loopⁿ`, with negative `n` using `loop⁻¹`. For the inverse, define a *covering family* `Cover : S¹ → U` by circle recursion: `Cover(base) ≡ ℤ`, and the action of `loop` is the equivalence `ua(succEquiv)` given by univalence from the successor automorphism of `ℤ`.

The method generalizes dramatically. Brunerie's thesis develops the synthetic theory of the *spheres* `Sⁿ` (defined via suspensions), proving the **Freudenthal suspension theorem** — the stabilization map `πₖ(Sⁿ) → πₖ₊₁(Sⁿ⁺¹)` is an isomorphism for `k < 2n − 2` — and consequently that `πₖ(Sⁿ) = 0` for `k < n` [4].

> **Theorem (Brunerie):** *`π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/nℤ` for a specific natural number `n`, where `n` is determined by a synthetic proof but its numerical value is not constructively known [4].*

This last clause is the famous open wound of synthetic homotopy theory: the proof that such an `n` exists is fully constructive, yet extracting its value (classically known to be `2`) from the proof remains infeasible — a vivid demonstration that *constructive provability* and *practical computability* are distinct [4].

The Seifert–van Kampen theorem provides another benchmark: given a pushout presentation of a space, its fundamental groupoid is the pushout of groupoids, proved synthetically with the encode–decode method applied to a `code` fibration defined by recursion on the pushout [5].

## 6 Limitations

Univalent foundations, for all their elegance, face substantial limitations.

**Computational extraction remains hard.** Although cubical type theory restores canonicity in principle, *evaluating* synthetic proofs is often infeasible: Brunerie's number `n` in `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/nℤ` is the canonical example of a constructively well-defined but practically uncomputable value [4]. Normalization for the full schema of higher inductive types — including higher-path constructors and their coherences — is still incomplete, and performance of cubical proof assistants on large developments lags far behind mature systems like Coq or Lean.

**The semantics are demanding.** The simplicial model justifies consistency only classically [6]; the cubical models are constructive but technically intricate, and the precise relationship between the various cube categories (De Morgan, Cartesian, distributive) and their type theories is still being mapped [8]. A fully satisfactory *internal* account of HITs — one that explains, inside type theory, what a general HIT is — remains elusive, though substantial progress exists.

**Classical mathematics resists.** Large parts of classical homotopy theory — spectra, stable homotopy, spectral sequences, model-category machinery — have no synthetic counterpart yet. Synthetic arguments excel at *unstable, low-dimensional* phenomena (spheres, suspensions, fibrations) but the translation of *stable* homotopy theory into type theory is essentially untouched. Whether univalent foundations can eventually *replace* analytic foundations for the working homotopy theorist, rather than merely complement them, is genuinely open.

**Foundational pluralism.** Univalence is incompatible with UIP and with the axiom K used pervasively in existing formalized mathematics; adopting univalent foundations means abandoning or translating large libraries. The community has responded with *two-level* and *modal* type theories, but the engineering cost is real [7].

## 7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began as a surprising observation — that intensional identity types already carry the structure of paths — and grew, through Voevodsky's univalence axiom and the schema of higher inductive types, into a candidate foundation for all of mathematics [1]. Its synthetic homotopy theory has proved its worth on genuine theorems: the fundamental group of the circle, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the Hopf fibration, and the computation of `π₄(S³)` up to an unknown integer [2][4]. And the cubical program of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg has answered the deepest objection to the axiomatic theory, showing that univalence can be not a stuck axiom but a *computing* theorem, with canonicity restored and higher inductive types given operational meaning [3][6][8].

The road ahead is clear. On the *logical* side: normalization and decidability for general HITs, a cleaner internal theory of higher inductives, and practical extraction of computational content from synthetic proofs. On the *mathematical* side: pushing synthetic methods into stable homotopy theory, cohomology, and the classification theorems that make up the working toolkit of topologists. If the first decade of univalent foundations taught us that spaces can be *logic*, the next must show that logic can do everything spaces can.
