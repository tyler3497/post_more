# Quantitative Type Theory: Semiring-Graded Usage, Resource-Aware Realizability, and the Design Space of Linear Dependent Types

## Abstract

Quantitative type theory (QTT) annotates every variable binding in a typing context with an element of a resource semiring, recording how often — and whether at all — the variable may be consumed at run time. Where linear logic [1] bifurcated propositions into the reusable and the single-use, and McBride [2] observed that erasure demands a three-way distinction between compile-time, run-time, and no use at all, QTT unifies these strands into a dependent type theory in which usage is a first-class, compositional annotation. This article develops the design space systematically: semiring-graded contexts and their algebraic laws; the conflict between dependency and resource accounting, and its resolution; graded modal types as a coeffect discipline [4]; and resource-sensitive realizability built on quantitative combinatory algebras [3]. We survey the principal metatheoretic results — substitution and subject reduction with exact usage accounting [6], graded modal dependent types [5] — and their embodiment in compilers such as Idris 2 and Granule, closing with open problems in decidability, universe interaction, and quantitative effect reasoning.

## 1 Introduction

Type systems have always been silent bookkeepers: they track *which* values flow *where*, but not *how often* values are used. Linear logic changed the terms of this contract by treating hypotheses as resources that are consumed by proofs, with the exponential modality marking the boundary between the reusable and the single-use [1]. Yet the resource story remained stubbornly propositional. The moment types may *depend* on terms — the moment a variable can appear inside a type as well as inside a term — the naive transfer of linear discipline breaks down. A variable used in a type is used *statically*; a variable used in a term is used *dynamically*; and confusing the two destroys both erasure and soundness.

McBride's *I Got Plenty o' Nuttin'* made this tension explicit and proposed the decisive conceptual move: annotate each context entry not with a binary linear/unrestricted flag, but with a *usage quantity* drawn from an algebraic structure, distinguishing compile-time use (which must be erased) from run-time use (which must be accounted) [2]. Atkey then gave this idea a fully worked-out metatheory and semantics: quantitative type theory proper, with a resource semiring grading every binding and a realizability interpretation over quantitative combinatory algebras that tracks resources through evaluation [3].

The subsequent decade has turned QTT from a single system into a design space. Granule recast the same algebra as graded modal types over linear bases with indexed refinements [4]; Moon, Eades, and Orchard pushed graded modalities into full dependent types [5]; Choudhury, Eades, Eisenberg, and Weirich built a graded dependent calculus with a heap-based operational semantics and machine-checked-feeling usage invariants [6]; and Linear Haskell carried multiplicity polymorphism into a production compiler [7]. This article maps that space, proves its central theorems, and names its frontiers.

## 2 Background

The intellectual lineage of QTT runs through three distinct traditions that it fuses.

- **Linear logic and substructural type systems.** Girard's decomposition of intuitionistic implication into linear implication plus the exponential, $A \Rightarrow B \simeq\, !A \multimap B$, made resource consumption a logical primitive [1]. Type-theoretic descendants include linear and affine lambda calculi, uniqueness typing, and ownership systems; the perennial difficulty is scaling the discipline past the simply-typed fragment.
- **Coeffects and graded modalities.** Coeffect calculi annotate the *context* rather than the *result*: a judgment's requirements on its environment are graded by elements of a resource algebra, typically a semiring or lattice [4]. The graded necessity modality $\Box_r A$ internalizes this: a value of $\Box_r A$ may be unboxed exactly $r$ times. Dually to monadic effects, coeffects capture context-dependence — dataflow, liveness, usage, scheduling.
- **Dependent type theory and erasure.** In dependently typed languages, compile-time irrelevance is a correctness issue, not merely an optimization: implicit arguments, proofs, and indices must vanish before execution. McBride showed that erasure is itself a usage discipline — a variable used *only* in types has run-time multiplicity zero — and that the standard two-zone (erased vs. relevant) treatment is a degenerate case of a richer quantitative one [2].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is the standard one of programming-language metatheory, applied uniformly across the design space rather than to a single calculus:

1. **Fix the algebraic interface.** All systems are parameterized by a semiring $(R, +, 0, \cdot, 1)$ of *grades*. Contexts become lists of graded assumptions $x :^{\rho} A$ with $\rho \in R$. Context addition $\Gamma_1 + \Gamma_2$ adds grades pointwise; scaling $\rho \cdot \Gamma$ multiplies them. The typing rules are written once, against this interface [3][4].
2. **Separate the two roles of variables.** Every rule must answer: is this occurrence of $x$ a *type-formation* use or a *computational* use? QTT's answer is the quantity annotation on the binder: binders record the run-time budget, while a separate judgment tracks static occurrence [2][3].
3. **Prove resource-aware metatheory.** Substitution, weakening, and subject reduction are stated and proved with explicit grade arithmetic. The substitution lemma is the crux: substituting a term graded $\rho$ for a variable budgeted $\sigma$ must preserve the invariant $\rho \leq \sigma$ in the grade order [6].
4. **Give a quantitative semantics.** Atkey's approach interprets types as sets of realizers in a *quantitative combinatory algebra*, where application consumes a budgeted amount of resource, and soundness says well-typed programs respect their budgets [3]. Figure 3 sketches the categorical picture.
5. **Validate by implementation.** Granule, Idris 2, and Linear Haskell serve as existence proofs that the discipline survives contact with real compilers, and as sources of counterexamples when it does not [4][7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The resource semiring and annotated contexts

The single most consequential design decision in QTT is the choice of grade algebra. A *resource semiring* $(R, +, 0, \cdot, 1)$ supplies: addition for *splitting* a context between two subterms (as in the application rule), multiplication for *scaling* a context under a graded modality or binder, and distinguished elements $0$ (unused) and $1$ (used once). A preorder $\leq$ on $R$ — the *subusage* relation — permits weakening a budget: using a variable fewer times than permitted is always safe [3][6].

The canonical example is the three-element *erasure semiring* $\{0, 1, \omega\}$:

| $+$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\omega$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\omega$ |
| $1$ | $1$ | $\omega$ | $\omega$ |
| $\omega$ | $\omega$ | $\omega$ | $\omega$ |

with $0 \cdot x = 0$, $1 \cdot x = x$, and $\omega \cdot x = \omega$ for nonzero $x$. Here $0$ marks compile-time-only (erased) variables, $1$ marks linear run-time variables, and $\omega$ marks unrestricted ones. This is exactly the multiplicity lattice implemented by Idris 2 [2][6]. Richer semirings — the naturals for exact counting, products of semirings for multi-dimensional budgets, the tropical semiring for cost analysis — instantiate the same rules to different resource questions [4].

The application rule displays the algebra in action:

```haskell
-- Annotated application: budgets add across function and argument
--   Gamma |- f : (x :^sigma A) -> B      Delta |- a : A
--   ---------------------------------------------------
--        Gamma + sigma . Delta |- f a : B[a/x]
```

If the function consumes its argument $\sigma$ times, the argument's context is scaled by $\sigma$ and added to the function's context. *Every* structural rule of the theory is a theorem of semiring arithmetic; this is what makes QTT a framework rather than a single system [3][4].

### 4.2 The conflict between dependency and usage, and its resolution

The naive marriage of linearity and dependency fails for a precise reason. Consider $\Pi$-formation: to check $\Gamma \vdash (x : A) \to B$, we need $x$ available when checking $B$ — but $B$ is a *type*, evaluated at compile time, so $x$'s run-time budget must not be spent there. If dependency is allowed to consume linear resources, then either well-typed programs get stuck (a variable consumed by a type is unavailable to the term) or erasure becomes unsound (a variable needed at run time is erased because it appeared in a type) [2].

> **Theorem (McBride's separation):** In a quantitative dependent theory, every variable occurrence is classified as *static* (in types, kinds, and erased positions) or *dynamic* (in computationally relevant positions), and the usage budget recorded in the context governs dynamic occurrence alone. Type-formation uses never debit the run-time budget. [2]

QTT implements this via *quantity-annotated binders*: the $\Pi$-type $(x :^{\rho} A) \to B$ records that the *function*, when applied, will use its argument $\rho$ times at run time, while occurrences of $x$ inside $B$ are static and cost nothing. The variable rule requires the context to supply at least the demanded grade:

```idris
-- Idris 2: multiplicities on binders (0 = erased, 1 = linear, ω = unrestricted)
append : (0 n : Nat) -> (1 xs : Vect n a) -> (1 ys : Vect m a)
       -> Vect (n + m) a
append [] ys = ys
append (x :: xs) ys = x :: append xs ys
```

Here `n` is used only in types and is erased; `xs` is consumed exactly once, so the definition type-checks only because each recursive call threads the tail linearly. The compiler *erases* precisely the $0$-graded bindings, turning a metatheoretic invariant into a calling convention [6][7]. Figure 4 illustrates the erasure pipeline.

### 4.3 Graded modalities and coeffects

Granule's insight is that the same semiring algebra can be presented *modally* rather than *contextually*: instead of grading every assumption, introduce a graded necessity modality $\Box_r A$, the type of values usable exactly $r$ times [4]. Promotion boxes a term, scaling its context:

```haskell
-- Granule (simplified): promotion scales the context by the grade
--   r . Gamma |- t : A
--   -------------------
--   Gamma |- [t] : Box_r A
```

The modality is a graded exponential comonad: counit $\Box_1 A \to A$ extracts a single use, comultiplication $\Box_{r \cdot s} A \to \Box_r (\Box_s A)$ nests budgets, and the semiring laws become the comonad coherence laws [4]. Moon, Eades, and Orchard extended this to dependent types, where the grade $r$ may itself depend on terms — a *graded modal dependent type theory* in which modalities and $\Pi$-types interoperate [5]. Their system shows that the modal and contextual presentations are intertranslatable: $\Box_r A$ is definable as $(x :^{r} A) \to B$ sugar in one direction, while the modal presentation admits cleaner categorical semantics via graded comonads in the other.

The payoff is *usage polymorphism*: Granule functions can abstract over grades, so a single `map`-like combinator works for any usage discipline, with the semiring operations resolved at instantiation [4]. This recovers, inside the type system, the genericity that linear logic's binary $!$ modality lacks.

### 4.4 Quantitative realizability semantics

Syntax tells us which programs are well-budgeted; semantics tells us the budgets *mean* something. Atkey's semantics interprets QTT in *quantitative categories with families* (QCwFs), extending the standard CwF account of dependent types with a notion of resource consumption on morphisms [3]. Concretely, the realizability model works over *quantitative combinatory algebras* (QCAs): combinatory algebras equipped with a resource monoid such that application $e \cdot v$ is defined only when sufficient resource is supplied, and the $K$ and $S$ combinators have specified quantitative behavior.

> **Theorem (Quantitative soundness, Atkey):** If $\Gamma \vdash M : A$ in QTT over a resource semiring $R$, then $M$ is realized in every QCA over $R$ by a realizer that consumes resources within the budget recorded in $\Gamma$. In particular, a closed term of type $\mathsf{Nat}$ with the empty budget evaluates to a numeral while consuming no resources beyond those accounted. [3]

This refines ordinary realizability the way QTT refines ordinary type theory: the standard story says well-typed programs *compute*; the quantitative story says well-typed programs compute *within budget*. The construction also explains why the semiring — rather than, say, a mere monoid — is the right abstraction: addition interprets context splitting, multiplication interprets scaling, and distributivity interprets the interaction between the two [3].

### 4.5 From theory to compilers: multiplicities and erasure

The most stringent test of a type theory is a compiler that ships. Two production-adjacent systems validate QTT's core:

- **Idris 2** implements the $\{0, 1, \omega\}$ fragment of QTT as *multiplicities* on every binder. Multiplicity $0$ drives erasure of implicit arguments and proofs; multiplicity $1$ enforces linear resource protocols (file handles, session channels, in-place array update); $\omega$ is the default [2][6]. The case studies in Choudhury et al. — including a linear array API whose update operations are proven safe by their grades — demonstrate that exact-usage typing scales to realistic APIs [6].
- **Linear Haskell** takes the complementary route: rather than grading contexts, it grades *arrows*, with multiplicity-polymorphic function types $A \to_p B$ where $p$ ranges over multiplicities. The desugaring into GHC Core preserves the invariants, and the benchmarks show that linear stream fusion and mutable-array combinators achieve C-like performance with pure interfaces [7].

Both systems confirm McBride's central pragmatic claim: once usage is tracked, erasure is no longer a whole-program analysis but a *local* consequence of the typing derivation, and linearity is no longer an all-or-nothing whole-language commitment but a per-binder choice [2][7].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The metatheory of quantitative dependent types rests on three load-bearing results, each now established in at least one full system.

1. **Substitution with grade arithmetic (Choudhury et al.).** In LDC, if $\Gamma, x :^{\sigma} A \vdash M : B$ and $\Delta \vdash N : A$ with the grade of $N$'s usage bounded appropriately, then $\Gamma + \sigma \cdot \Delta \vdash M[N/x] : B[N/x]$. The proof proceeds by induction on the typing derivation, with each case discharging a semiring identity; the heap-based operational semantics then yields type soundness — well-graded programs never use a variable more often than its budget allows [6].
2. **Graded modal dependent types are conservative (Moon et al.).** The graded modal dependent theory admits the expected $\beta$-rules for $\Box_r$, and the modality commutes correctly with dependent elimination. Their categorical model validates the theory, establishing coherence of the modal and contextual presentations [5].
3. **Quantitative realizability is sound and adequate (Atkey).** The QCA model of [3] is not merely sound but *adequate* for the chosen notion of observation: two terms distinguished by the model are distinguished by their resource behavior. This gives a semantic criterion for when two grades are genuinely different — $\omega$ is not just "big $1$", because the model separates unbounded reuse from exact counting.

> **Lemma (Erasure correctness):** In the $\{0, 1, \omega\}$ fragment, erasing all $0$-graded binders and their occurrences preserves both typing (of the erased program, in the erased theory) and evaluation behavior. Hence compile-time irrelevance is a theorem, not an optimization heuristic. [2][6]

On the implementation side, Granule's type checker discharges grade constraints via an SMT-backed solver over the chosen semiring, and its case studies — information-flow security via graded modalities, session-type linearity, resource-bounded APIs — type-check in seconds [4]. Linear Haskell's benchmarks demonstrate that multiplicity polymorphism imposes no measurable overhead relative to hand-written unsafe code [7].

## 6 Limitations

The design space is far from closed. The principal limitations:

- **Decidability of grade constraints.** For the three-element erasure semiring, usage checking is decidable and cheap. For richer semirings — naturals with multiplication, polynomial grades — constraint solving becomes the bottleneck, and completeness of the solver is generally out of reach; Granule's SMT backend is sound but incomplete by necessity [4].
- **Interaction with universes and cumulativity.** Atkey's semantics is developed for a fixed universe structure; how quantitative grades interact with universe polymorphism, cumulativity, and typical ambiguity remains largely unexplored [3]. A universe-graded $\Box_r \, \mathsf{Type}$ raises coherence questions the current QCwF account does not answer.
- **Affine and relevant fragments.** The semiring presentation handles "at most once" (affine) usage awkwardly: the natural grades count exact uses, and weakening is a global preorder rather than a local structural rule. A fully satisfactory quantitative account of affinity — where discarding is permitted but duplication is not — is still missing [2].
- **Effects and quantitative reasoning.** QTT tracks *usage of variables*, not *cost of effects*. Marrying graded coeffects (context requirements) with graded monads (effect capabilities) inside a dependent theory — so that, e.g., a loop's iteration count and its heap effects are tracked by one discipline — is the subject of active work but has no canonical system yet [4][5].
- **Canonicity and normalization.** Choudhury et al. assume $\mathsf{Type} : \mathsf{Type}$ and hence forgo normalization; whether the standard normalization-by-evaluation arguments adapt to graded contexts — where the logical relation must itself be resource-aware — is open [6].

## 7 Conclusion

Quantitative type theory reframes a thirty-year-old question. Linear logic asked *which assumptions may be reused* [1]; McBride asked *which occurrences cost anything at run time* [2]; Atkey showed the two questions share one algebraic answer — a semiring of grades, threaded through contexts, modalities, and semantics alike [3]. The systems surveyed here — Granule's graded modalities [4], graded modal dependent types [5], the usage-aware operational semantics of LDC [6], and the industrial multiplicity polymorphism of Linear Haskell [7] — collectively demonstrate that the answer is robust: it survives dependency, it admits clean categorical and realizability semantics, and it compiles.

The frontier is now *composition*: of grades with universes, of usage with effects, of decidable fragments with expressive ones. If the history of linear logic is any guide — from a proof-theoretic curiosity in 1987 to the ownership discipline of a production borrow checker — the quantitative turn in dependent types is still in its early chapters, and the most consequential systems have yet to be written.
