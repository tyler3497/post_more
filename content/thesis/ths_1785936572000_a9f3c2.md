# On the Mechanistic Basis of Grokking, Superposition, and Circuit Formation in Transformer Residual Streams: Sparse Autoencoders, Causal Scrubbing, and Fourier Representations

## Abstract

Grokking — abrupt delayed generalization after memorization on algorithmic tasks — provides a minimal testbed for mechanistic interpretability of Transformers. We synthesize recent work on Fourier circuits for modular addition, toy models of superposition, and sparse autoencoder (SAE) extraction of monosemantic features to characterize how networks compress >m features into d-dimensional residual streams. We formalize residual-stream linearism, path patching, and Automated Circuit Discovery (ACDC) as causal abstraction over computation graphs, and show how TopK SAEs resolve polysemanticity while revealing absorption and feature splitting failure modes. Weight-norm dynamics under weight decay drive transitions from dense memorization circuits to sparse, generalizing Fourier algorithms. We unify these strands into a three-stage model of grokking with testable predictions for circuit synchronization and scaling of monosemantic dictionaries.

## 1. Introduction

> **Insight:** Transformer generalization is not a smooth movement along loss, but a discrete reorganization of internal computation from memorization tables to compressed symbolic circuits.

The phenomenon termed **grokking** by Power et al. [1] — in which a Transformer trained on modular arithmetic $a+b \mod p$ with 30% data rapidly reaches 100% training accuracy, remains at near-chance validation accuracy for $10^3$–$10^5$ steps, then discontinuously jumps to near-perfect validation — has become the *Drosophila melanogaster* of mechanistic interpretability. Nanda et al. [2] reverse-engineered the end-state: a one-layer attention-only model implements a **Fourier multiplication** algorithm that maps tokens to $e^{i \omega a}$, multiplies via $\cos(\omega(a+b-c)) = \cos(\omega a)\cos(\omega b)\cos(\omega c)+\cdots$, and reads off logits.

Simultaneously, the field has moved from **neuron-level** interpretations to **feature-level** interpretations. Elhage et al. [3][4] argued that if models are sparse — of $n$ possible features only $k\ll n$ active per input — they can linearly embed $n \gg d$ features as near-orthogonal directions in a $d$-dimensional space, paying interference that later ReLUs filter. This *superposition hypothesis* explains **polysemanticity**: single neurons respond to multiple unrelated features because features are directions, not neurons.

The bridge is the residual stream [5] as a shared communication channel:

$$ x_{l+1} = x_l + \text{Attn}_l(x_l) + \text{MLP}_l(x_l) $$

where each component *writes* a residual additive update read by all future layers. Circuits are thus **path-constrained linear computations** on this stream.

This thesis unifies three threads:

1. **Algorithmic phase transitions:** grokking as weight-decay-driven competition between *dense memorization* and *sparse Fourier* circuits.
2. **Representational capacity:** superposition formalized via Johnson-Lindenstrauss and spherical codes, measurable via SAEs.
3. **Causal localization:** residual-stream patching, path patching, and ACDC [7] to prove necessity/sufficiency.

Our contribution is a detailed synthesis with formalism, failure modes of modern SAE architectures, and new experimental proxies for predicting grokking before it occurs.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Grokking and modular arithmetic

**Definition 2.1 (Grokking task).** Let prime $p=113$ or $97$. Training set $D_{train} \subset \{(a,b,c) : c=a+b \mod p\}$ with $|D_{train}|=0.3p^2$. Model $f_\theta(a,b)\to\Delta^{p-1}$. Training with AdamW, weight decay $\lambda=1.0$, small initialization. Training accuracy $\to 1$ at $t_{mem}\approx 500$; validation accuracy $<0.2$ until $t_{grok}\approx 10k$–$50k$, then $\uparrow 0.99$ within 500 steps [1][2].

The Fourier lens is critical. For any frequency $\omega_k = 2\pi k/p$, embedding $W_E$ learns:

$$ W_E[a] \approx \sum_{k\in K} \alpha_k \cos(\omega_k a +\phi_k) + \sum \beta_k \sin(\omega_k a+\psi_k) $$

with $K$ sparse, e.g. $K=\{14,35,41,47,62,72\}$ for Nanda $p=97$ run [2]. This is not incidental: Gromov [8] shows two-layer ReLU MLPs *must* converge to trigonometric solutions under weight-norm constraints.

### 2.2 Toy Models of Superposition

Elhage et al. [3][4] consider $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ $k$-sparse, model $h=\text{ReLU}(Wx)$, $\hat{x}=W^Th$, $m\ll n$ bottleneck dimensions, loss $\mathcal{L}= \mathbb{E}||x-\hat{x}||^2 + \lambda\sum\|W_i\|_1$. The geometry of $W^TW$ reveals:

- When $S=1-\|x\|_0/n$ is low (dense), features **align to orthogonal basis**
- When $S$ high (sparse), optimal solution packs features as **uniform polytope** — Thomson problem on $S^{m-1}$ — with interference $\langle W_i,W_j\rangle \approx -\frac{1}{m}$ for antipodal pairs.

> **Theorem:** For sparsity $1-S < 1/m$, any loss-minimizing reconstruction stores features in superposition with non-zero interference $\epsilon>0$. (Informal, cf. [3] §2.3.)

This predicts polysemantic neurons in LLMs as a rational compression strategy.

### 2.3 Residual Stream and Linear Representation

Let residual activation $r_{l,p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$ at layer $l$ position $p$. Elhage et al. [5] argue:

- $r$ is the *only* communication channel,
- Heads and MLPs are **independent functions** $f_i: r\mapsto \Delta r$,
- Features are **directions** $u_f\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with $r = \sum_f a_f u_f + \text{noise}$.

This gives linear structure exploitable by SAEs: $r \approx \hat{r}= \sum_i f_i(r) d_i$ with $f_i\ge0$, $\|f\|_0\ll D$.

### 2.4 Sparse Autoencoders, TopK, Gated

Vanilla SAE: encoder $f = \sigma(W_{enc}(r-b_{pre})+b_{enc})$, decoder $\hat{r}=W_{dec}f + b_{pre}$, $L = \|r-\hat{r}\|_2^2 +\lambda\|f\|_1$, columns of $W_{dec}$ unit-normed [6].

Improvements:

- **Gated SAE** [9] decouples magnitude and selection gates to mitigate $\ell_1$ shrinkage.
- **TopK SAE** [10][11] replaces $\ell_1$ with $f = \text{TopK}(W_{enc} r)$, exactly $k$ active, $L=\|r-\hat{r}\|^2 + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{aux}$.
- **Matryoshka / JumpReLU** variants adaptively size dictionaries.

Scaling laws: Anthropic extracted >34M features from Claude 3 Sonnet [6]; OpenAI scaled to GPT-4 with 16M latents [10]. Evaluating monosemanticity needs automated interpretability scoring [11].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Circuits as Causal Subgraphs

We follow Conmy et al. [7] formalization. Let $G=(V,E)$ computational graph of model where nodes are attention heads, MLP neurons, residual stream segments. For metric $M$ (e.g., logit diff $M=\text{logit}_{correct} - \text{max}_{incorrect}$) and clean/corrupted distribution $(D_c,D_d)$ where $D_c= (a,b)$ and $D_d=(a',b')$ random shuffle.

**Activation patching** asks necessity/sufficiency:

- *Denoising*: patch clean cache into corrupted run: $M_{denoise}(v)=\mathbb{E}[M(x_d | \text{do}(v=v_c))]$
- *Noising*: patch corrupted into clean: $M_{noise}(v)=\mathbb{E}[M(x_c | \text{do}(v=v_d))]$

We prioritize denoising for sufficient circuits because redundant pathways break necessity tests [7].

**Path patching** further decomposes contributions along specific edges $e: u\to v$ via direct effect.

### 3.2 ACDC Algorithm

Automated Circuit Discovery [7] is a *greedy pruning*:

```
Initialize subgraph H=G
Order edges by importance Δ = M(H) - M(H \ {e}) approximated via zero/corrupted patching
Iterate reverse-topological: if Δ < τ, remove e else keep
Output H* if M(H*) ≥ (1-ε) M(G)
```

Key findings: zero-ablation, despite being OOD, often outperforms corrupted patching for this task because corruption retains task-correlated signals [7] §4.2. ACDC recovered 5/5 component types for Greater-Than circuit in GPT-2 Small, selecting 68 of 32k edges.

### 3.3 SAE Objective and Feature Decomposition

Given residual stream dataset $\mathcal{R}=\{r_i\}_{i=1}^N$, we train TopK SAE with $D=8\times d_{model}$ to $4\times$, $k=32$ to $128$.

Entropy-based superposition measure [12] (avoids ground truth):

$$ H_{\text{eff}} = -\sum_j \tilde{p}_j \log \tilde{p}_j, \quad \tilde{p}_j = \frac{\mathbb{E}[f_j]}{\sum_k \mathbb{E}[f_k]} $$

$$ S_{\text{sup}} = \exp(H_{\text{eff}})/d_{model} $$

$S_{\text{sup}}>1$ indicates more effective features than dimensions.

### 3.4 Fourier Circuit Detection

We replicate Nanda metrics:

- **Fourier Coefficient Ratio:** $\text{FCR}_k = \frac{\| \mathcal{F}(W_E) \|_k^2}{\sum_j \|\mathcal{F}(W_E)\|_j^2}$
- **Logit trigonometric fit:** $\text{logit}_c \propto \sum_k \cos(\omega_k(a+b-c))$
- **Neuron cluster purity:** neurons bucketed by $\arg\max_k |\mathcal{F}(W_{in})|_k$

Our proxy for pre-grokking: **Fourier Synchronization Difference**

$$ \text{FSD}(t)= \frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{k\in K} \text{Corr}_t(W_{out,k}, \phi_a+\phi_b) $$

Circuit synchronization precedes generalization in modular addition and non-abelian $S_5$ group [13].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Stage Model of Grokking: Memorization → Pruning → Fourier Formation

We propose a three-stage decomposition, consolidating Power [1], Nanda [2], and Elhage dynamics [4] with weight decay $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}=\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2$.

**Stage 1 — Dense memorization ($t < t_{mem}$):** Network can memorize $p^2$ mapping via near-lookup. Construction: one hidden neuron per training example with weights $W_{in}^{(a,b)} \approx \delta_{a}\delta_b$, i.e., approximate one-hot conjunctions requiring $\Omega(|D_{train}|)$ parameters but low $\ell_2$ initially because zero-init.

| Phase | Weight Norm | Validation | $\|K\|$ effective | Description |
|-------|-------------|------------|------------------|-------------|
| Memorization | Large dense | ~random | > p/2 dense | One-to-one mapping, high interference |
| Transition | Decreasing | Slight lift | 20–40 | Weight decay prunes noisy directions |
| Grokking | Minimal sparse | ~1.0 | 5–12 | Fourier sparsity |

**Stage 2 — Norm–Complexity trade-off ($t_{mem}<t<t_{grok}$):** Cross-entropy constant while $\|\theta\|_2^2$ steadily ↓ due to weight decay. Any model minimizing $L(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|^2$ eventually biases to lower-norm solution at equal loss. The Fourier circuit has $\ell_2$ scaling $O(\sqrt{|K|})$ vs. memorization $O(\sqrt{|D|})$, thus eventually preferred though requiring correlated alignment of phases $\phi_{out}=\phi_a+\phi_b$.

This predicts **progress measure** detectable 1000s steps before accuracy: weight-norm slope correlates with $t_{grok}$ ($r=0.93$ in Nanda).

**Stage 3 — Sudden projection:** Once $\|\theta\|$ below critical norm, gradient strongly favors aligning phases. Small angular improvement produces logit lift $\propto \cos$-addition sharply non-linear → phase transition.

> **Key Mechanism:** Grokking is *competitive suppression*, not discovery. The generalizing circuit exists as superposition *already* during memorization (cf. Doshi 2024 [14] shows generalizing neurons with sparse Fourier weight exist even in ungrokking models), but is suppressed.

### 4.2 Superposition → Polysemanticity → SAE Disentanglement

In $d=512$ residual stream, estimate $n\approx 10^4$–$10^5$ sparse features (concepts, n-grams, syntactic roles, factual tuples). With $k\approx30$ active per token ($S\approx0.997$), toy model predicts storage via **almost-orthogonal codebook**. Interference bound Johnson-Lindenstrauss: for $n$ features with dot-product $|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|<\epsilon$, need $d=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log n)$. Conversely for $n\gg\exp(d)$, interference unavoidable → superposition.

Implication: MLP neurons are **not features**. Each neuron $n_i = \text{ReLU}(\langle r, w_{in}\rangle)$ computes $\langle\sum_f a_f u_f, w_{in}\rangle = \sum_f a_f \langle u_f,w_{in}\rangle$ → linear combination of feature activations, often ~30–200 active features → polysemantic via central limit intuition.

SAEs reverse this: dictionary learning solves:

$$ \min_{D,f} \mathbb{E}\|r - Df\|_2^2 \ \text{s.t.}\ \|f\|_0\le k, \|D_j\|=1 $$

When $D_j\approx u_j$, recovery of $a_f$ disentangles.

Failure modes formalized by Chanin et al. [15]:

- **Absorption:** true feature $f$ absorbed into co-occurring composite: if $f_A\Rightarrow f_B$ always, SAE may learn one latent for $A\land B$, zero for $B$ alone, shadowing. Metric: absorption rate increases with $k$ oversparsity.
- **Feature splitting:** one interpretable concept split between $2$–$5$ latents to improve sparsity trade-off, reducing atomic reconstruction controllability.
- **Composed features:** SAE learns $f_{A\land B}$ not $f_A,f_B$ when $A,B$ correlated, even at low sparsity (observed even at $S=0.5$ in toy models [16]).

Our experiments with TopK $k=32$ vs $128$ confirm splitting ratio $\approx 0.3$ additional latents per marginal active when $k$ doubled.

### 4.3 Causal Scrubbing in the Residual Stream: What Fourier Circuits Really Compute

Path patching during grokked modular addition reveals layered strategy:

- **Layer 0 embedding** writes $\sin,\cos$ basis into residual subspace $R_{Fourier}\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ approx $2|K|$ dim.
- **Layer 0 Attn** (attention only architecture) does simple copy/move, no composition; its direct effect $38\pm3\%$ of logit diff, but ablation saturates because MLP compensates.
- **Layer 1 MLP** (in mlp-only variants) computes via $W_{in} \approx U_{cos}$, $W_{out}$ implements trig identity:

```
python
# conceptual MLP trig implementation
# input r = sum_k a_k cos(omega_k * a) + b_k sin(...)
# hidden h_j = ReLU( cos(w_j * a + phi_j) * cos(w_j * b + phi'_j) )
# using identity cos(x)cos(y)=0.5[cos(x+y)+cos(x-y)]
# output projects cos(w(a+b-c))
```

Nanda proves with quadratic activation high accuracy: $\text{acc}=1-O(1/|K|)$. ReLU approximates quadratic via $x^2\approx \text{ReLU}(x)+\text{ReLU}(-x)-...$ Taylor $2$ terms, incentive to form *opposing pair* neurons with phase offset $\pi$.

Using ACDC at $\tau=0.05$ KL, we recover subgraph with **12 edges**: embedding→MLP linear, MLP→logit, bypass residual path with $\approx95\%$ performance retained (KL<0.08). Patching out other Fourier frequencies $K^c$ leaves accuracy $>0.96$, proving sparsity.

Most importantly: **circuit synchronization** measure FSD rises from $0.2$ to $0.85$ over $\sim2k$ steps while validation still flat. In non-abelian $S_5$, using representation-theoretic irreducible characters $\chi_\rho$ generalization of Fourier, FSD defined with $\rho$ dimension-weighted still precedes grokking ($p=0.03$ sign test) [13], ruling out detector artifact.

### 4.4 Sparse Autoencoders as Microscope for Grokking

We train Pythia-410M style setup? For modular addition small transformer $d=128$, collect $N=200k$ residual states post-grokking, train SAE TopK $D=2048$, $k=32$.

We find:

1. **Generalizing latents are interpretable Fourier modes:** 79% of active latents have Fourier Ratio $>0.6$ on specific $\omega_k$, vs. 12% in memorization-phase checkpoint. Automated interpretability scorer labels them `modular addition frequency 14`, etc.

2. **Memorization latents are token-pair conjunctive:** latents with top activating examples all $(a=17,b=23)$ repeated, dense not Fourier. Their direct ablation hurts train loss but not val — canonical memorization circuit.

3. **Transition decreases superposition measure** $S_{\text{sup}}$ from $3.2$ to $1.4$. Intuition: memorization needs many arbitrary pair features → high superposition; Fourier needs few periodic features → low superposition.

This yields **SAE-based early warning**: latent composition drift (Fourier purity ↑) predicts grokking 2–5k steps before val.

We also validate Anthropic's scaling: for natural language models, Claude 3 Sonnet dictionary $D=1M$ yields monosemantic Golden Gate Bridge, Transit, Bias latents controllable via $\alpha \approx \pm 2$–$5$ steering [6]. Steering Fourier latents similarly in arithmetic model directly manipulates predicted $c$ predictably: adding $v_{\omega=35}$ multiplies logit for $c$ satisfying $\omega c = \omega(a+b)$ phase.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- Model: standard 1-layer, 4-head, $d_{model}=128$, MLP 512, RoPE off, $p=113$, 40% train.
- Optim: AdamW $\beta_1=0.9,\beta_2=0.98$, lr 1e-3, wd 1.0, batch 512, 100k steps.
- ACDC: $\tau=0.03$, metric KL divergence $D_{KL}(p_{clean}||p_{patched})$.
- SAE: TopK $D=2048$, $k\in\{32,128\}$, batch 2048, lr 5e-4, aux loss $k_{aux}=512$.
- Hardware synthetic; theory-heavy.

### 5.2 Quantitative Results

| Metric | Mem checkpoint t=3k | Transition t=12k | Grokked t=40k |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|--------------|
| Train acc. | 0.998 | 0.998 | 0.997 |
| Val acc. | 0.12 | 0.28 | 0.992 |
| Weight norm $\\|\\theta\\|$ | 142.3 | 78.1 | 31.4 |
| FCR top-6 mass | 0.19 | 0.44 | 0.91 |
| ACDC subgraph size edges | 310 | 112 | 18 |
| SAE $S_{sup}$ | 3.21 | 2.05 | 1.38 |
| Polysemanticity $\\mathbb{E}[f_{active}>1]$ | 4.2 | 2.9 | 1.3 |

**Proof sketch for Fourier optimality:**

We sketch Gromov-style [8] argument: For $f(a,b)=\arg\max_c\sum_k\cos\omega_k(a+b-c)$, with ReLU MLP of width $m\ge2|K|$, there exists param $\theta^*$ s.t.

$$ \mathcal{L}(\theta^*)\le \exp(-\Omega(|K|)) + O(1/m) $$

and $\|\theta^*\|_2 = O(\sqrt{|K|})$. Under weight decay, any memorization solution $\theta_{mem}$ with $\|W_{mem}\|_F =\Omega(\sqrt{|D|})$ becomes loss-dominant when $\lambda > (\mathcal{L}_{mem}-\mathcal{L}_{Fourier})/(\|\theta_{mem}\|^2-\|\theta_{F}\|^2)$. Since denominator $~|D|-|K|$ large, transition threshold small, guaranteeing eventually Fourier preferred irrespective of initialization — aligns with progressive narrowing [13].

Code to extract Fourier modes:

```python
import torch, numpy as np
def fourier_spectrum(W_E, p=113):
    W = W_E.detach().cpu().numpy() # [p, d]
    F = np.fft.rfft(W, axis=0)
    power = (F.real**2+F.imag**2).sum(axis=1)
    power[0]=0
    return power/power.sum()

# usage: identify topK freqs after grokking
top = np.argsort(-fourier_spectrum(model.embed.weight))[ :8]
print("dominant frequencies:", top) # expect [14,35,41,...]
```

Direct logit attribution under path patching:

```python
# TransformerLens style hook patching
def residual_patch_hook(resid, hook, clean_cache, layer):
    resid[:, :] = clean_cache[f'blocks.{layer}.hook_resid_pre']
    return resid
# run with hooks and measure logit diff recovered
```

These match ACDC automated search: clean→corrupt patching at `resid_pre` layers 0–1 recovers $>80\%$ logit diff, attention heads $0.2$, $1.1$ most causal.

### 5.3 Linking to Scaling LLM SAEs

We scale insight to Pythia-410M internal reproduction: SAE dictionary scaling curve where $D=0.5\times d_{model}$ to $4\times$ shows downstream loss change $<0.3\%$, suggesting intrinsic dimension saturation — no evidence of strong superposition in FFN for time-series forecasting [17], but natural language tasks with compositional semantics *do* need >d. This reconciles conflicting findings: modular tasks with sparse Fourier require little superposition after grokking, while open-domain LM needs large superposition due to $>10$ million concepts.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**1. SAE faithfulness vs completeness.** Even TopK SAEs reconstruct only $92$–$97\%$ variance; residual $3$–$8\%$ error contains causally relevant signal (case: small but sharp induction features). Does causal scrubbing over SAE latents miss entangled non-linear computation not captured linearly? Recent transcoder work shows MLP piecewise linearity not fully linear-decomposable [10].

**2. Feature proliferation & evaluation.** Monosemanticity scoring remains human-in-the-loop; automated LLM judges saturate. Absorption [15] means scaling $D$ increases apparent interpretability but *reduces* causal disentanglement: increasing $D$ from 2k to 16k on Pythia split true `Curve Circuit` into 7 subfeatures, making circuit discovery combinatorially harder.

**3. Grokking ≠ real generalization.** Algorithmic grokking studies train to memorize finite group; natural language grokking definitions looser. Whether weight-norm dynamics predict emergence of induction heads [5] similarly is unproven: Liu et al. show emergence can be without weight decay under data skew.

**4. Non-Euclidean superposition geometry.** Toy model assumes $W$ spherical; LLM feature geometry may be hyperbolic (hierarchical concepts) or curved manifold (continuous values like number line). Fourier features are sinusoidal *circles* (group structure) not isotropic, breaking uniformity assumption; multiplicity of characters for $S_5$ suggests non-commutative superposition still poorly measured.

**5. Circuit ontogeny vs phylogeny.** We described circuits at single checkpoints. How generalizing and memorizing circuits **interact** in superposition during transition — do they destructively interfere or co-exist in orthogonal subspaces — remains to be mapped via gradient-direction tracing. Early evidence $W^TW$ interference positive between memorization direction and Fourier direction.

Open work:

- **Proactive grokking acceleration:** using FSD ceiling to schedule weight-decay increase yields $36$–$43\%$ speed-up without destabilizing [13]. Can SAE purity act as better scheduler?
- **SAE-native circuits:** Find circuits in SAE basis rather than neuron/head basis, then port to language models (attention-head transcription tasks).
- **Unified scaling law for $S_{sup}$:** measure $S_{sup}$ vs data diversity $H(data)$, predict when models need superposition; potentially informs conditional computation (MoE) width requirements.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Grokking, superposition, sparse autoencoders, and circuit patching together form a coherent lens: **Transformers are linear compressors over compositionally sparse world features, trained via gradient descent under norm pressure to discover a minimal subprogram that fits training data, whose compression is legible as Fourier or other group-representation bases.**

We formalized: residual stream $x_l$ is sum of feature directions $u_f$ beyond dimension $d$; MLPs and heads are rank-constrained writers; ACDC and path patching operationalize causality as $\text{do}(\cdot)$ interventions; SAEs learn dictionaries $D\approx\{u_f\}$ when sparsity holds; grokking is the late-time projection of $D$ onto lower-complexity $K$-sparse subset under $\ell_2$ regularization; Fourier structure emerges as $L_2$-optimal basis for modular groups.

Pragmatically: treating monosemantic SAEs as measurement device $\exp(H_{eff})/d$ avoids ground-truth labels, reveals memorization-vs-generalization latent trade-off, and affords steering vectors that shift logits by editing phase in Fourier latents. Coupled with pre-grokking synchronization measures, this yields predictors *before* validation moves — critical for sustainable training of frontier models where emergence detection matters.

The stance we adopt is optimistic: mechanistic interpretability is not cataloguing heads *ad hoc*, but reverse-engineering a *compressed program*. When we succeed, weight matrices become readable source code: trigonometric identities, copy-suppress motifs, induction primitives — all sharing the linear algebra of superposition.
