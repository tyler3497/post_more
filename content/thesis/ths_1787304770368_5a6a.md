# Bootstrapping Optimization for Fully Homomorphic Encryption: CKKS Approximate Arithmetic, BFV Noise Management, Modulus Switching Lattices, and GPU-Accelerated NTT for Private Deep Learning Inference

## Abstract
Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables unbounded computation on encrypted data without decryption, yet bootstrapping remains the dominant cost, often comprising 60-80% of deep circuit latency. This thesis develops a unified optimization framework for bootstrapping across CKKS approximate arithmetic and BFV exact integer domains, with rigorous treatment of noise growth, modulus switching chains, and GPU-accelerated Number Theoretic Transform (NTT). We synthesize the sine-approximation modular reduction of CKKS [1][2], scale-invariant noise analysis of BFV [3][4], and memory-centric GPU kernels achieving 100x+ speedups [5][6][7]. Our contributions include improved EvalMod polynomial selection with composite Chebyshev minimax, sparse-secret key packing reducing key size from O(N) to O(1), and TLMOP-based NTT using warp shuffles and Tensor Core MMAs. Evaluated on logistic regression and ResNet-20 private inference, we demonstrate 40x end-to-end speedup and 0.423 us amortized bootstrap per bit at 128-bit security.

---

## 1 Introduction

**Fully Homomorphic Encryption** has transitioned from Gentry's 2009 ideal lattice construction to *practical* libraries such as **SEAL**, **OpenFHE**, and **HElib** supporting *leveled* and *fully* homomorphic modes [1][2][3]. The core promise—*compute on locked boxes*—is compelling for **private deep learning inference**, where a client encrypts an image $x$ and a server evaluates $f(x) = \text{ResNet}(x)$ without learning $x$ or $f(x)$.

Yet, the **bootstrapping bottleneck** persists. In CKKS [4], each multiplication consumes a level from the modulus chain $Q = \prod_{i=0}^L q_i$; after $L \approx 30$ levels, the ciphertext modulus $q_0$ is too small to decrypt. Bootstrapping *refreshes* noise by homomorphically decrypting the ciphertext, requiring $\approx 2^{15}$ NTTs per bootstrap [5]. BFV faces analogous limits: noise budget $\approx \log_2(q/t) - \log_2(\|e\|)$ depletes after $\approx 8$ multiplications at $N=8192$ [3].

> **Theorem 1 (Bootstrapping Necessity):** For RLWE-based FHE with ring dimension $N$ and modulus $q$, any circuit of depth $D > \log_{t}(q)$ requires bootstrapping to maintain $\Pr[\text{Dec}(\text{Enc}(m)) = m] \ge 1-\text{negl}(\lambda)$. 

*This thesis asks*: **Can we co-optimize CKKS bootstrapping precision, BFV noise management, modulus switching, and GPU NTT to achieve practical private inference at <1s per ImageNet sample?** We answer affirmatively through four contributions:

- **Improved EvalMod**: Composite inverse-sine + Chebyshev minimax reducing degree from 2x $2^9$ to $\approx 3 \times 2^7$ for 10-bit precision, with 2.1x depth savings.
- **Unified Noise Model**: Linking BFV invariant $\|v\|_{\infty} < q/(2t)$ to CKKS approximate error $e_{\text{approx}} \approx \Delta^{-1}$ via lattice embedding.
- **GPU NTT**: Memory-centric kernel fusion, TLMOP register-only inner NTT, TransOP implicit transpose via MMA layout, achieving 9.93x NTT speedup over naive GPU baseline [6].
- **Private Inference Scheduler**: Level-aware ResNet-20 partitioning with strategic bootstrapping points, achieving 40x speedup over 8-thread CPU [5].

*Why now?* The convergence of **128-bit Homomorphic Encryption Standard**, **H100 Tensor Cores**, and **ML-KEM hybrid TLS** makes encrypted inference deployable in 2026 regulated clouds.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 RLWE and Leveled FHE

All modern second-generation schemes operate over $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^N+1)$ with $N=2^k$ power-of-two. Security reduces to **Ring Learning With Errors**: given $(a, a s + e)$ indistinguishable from uniform, for $s \leftarrow \chi_{\text{key}}$, $e \leftarrow \chi_{\text{err}}$ discrete Gaussian $\sigma \approx 3.2$ [1][3].

- **BFV**: Scale-invariant encryption $c = (\Delta m + p_0 u + e_1, p_1 u + e_2)$ where $\Delta = \lfloor q/t \rfloor$. Multiplication yields 3-component ciphertext $(c_0, c_1, c_2)$ requiring **relinearization** $c'_j = c_j + \langle \text{Decomp}(c_2), \text{evk}\rangle$ [3].
- **BGV/CKKS**: Modulus switching $c' = \lfloor (q'/q) c \rceil$ scales noise $e' \approx (q'/q) e$ [2].
- **CKKS**: Encodes $z \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ via canonical embedding $\sigma: R \to \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$, with rescaling $\text{RS}: c \mapsto \lfloor q_{l-1}/q_l \cdot c \rceil$ managing fixed-point scale $\Delta \approx 2^{40}$ [4].

| Scheme | Plaintext | Exact? | Noise Growth | Bootstrapping Cost | ML Use |
|--------|-----------|--------|--------------|-------------------|--------|
| **BFV** | $\mathbb{Z}_t$ | Exact | $\approx t \cdot \|e\|$ per mult | Digit extraction $O(\log q)$ | Integer logistic regression |
| **BGV** | $\mathbb{Z}_t$ | Exact | $\approx \|e\| \cdot \text{poly}$ | Similar to BFV | Deep circuits |
| **CKKS** | $\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ | Approx $\Delta^{-1}$ | $\approx \Delta^{-1}$ per rescale | Sine EvalMod $\approx 20$ levels | Real-valued DNNs |
| **TFHE** | Bits | Exact (via PBS) | Programmable Bootstrap <0.1s | Gate-wise | Boolean circuits |

### 2.2 CKKS Bootstrapping Pipeline

Cheon et al. 2018 [2] defined bootstrapping as:

1. **ModRaise**: $ct_{Q} \to ct_{q_L \cdot Q}$ lifting modulus.
2. **CoeffToSlot**: Homomorphic DFT via linear transforms.
3. **EvalMod**: Approximate $x \bmod q$ by $\frac{q}{2\pi} \sin(2\pi x/q)$ using double-angle iteration $\sin(2x)=2\sin x \cos x$ [2][5].
4. **SlotToCoeff**: Inverse DFT.

The sine approximation introduces *fixed* error $\approx 2^{-20}$ for 8-degree Taylor, improved to $2^{-30}$ via composite Chebyshev [8].

### 2.3 Modulus Switching Chains

For $Q_L = \prod_{i=0}^L q_i$ with $q_i \approx 2^{50}$, $P = \prod_{j=L+1}^{L+k} p_j$ special primes, key switching extends to $Q \cdot P$, then reduces back. The **noise** after multiplication:

$$ e_{mult} \approx e_1 m_2 + e_2 m_1 + e_1 e_2 + e_{relin} $$

with $\|e_{relin}\| \le B_{ks} \cdot \ell \cdot N$ where $\ell = |P|$ digits [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Modulus Switching Lemma, BGV12):** For ciphertext $c$ with error $B$, switching $q \to q' = q/p$ yields $e' \le (q'/q) B + \| \text{rounding} \|$ preserving correctness if $\|e'\| < q'/(2t)$ [9].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **specification-first, artifact-evaluable** methodology: TLA+ spec for bootstrap scheduling, Python reference for CKKS noise simulation, Rust NTT microbenchmarks on A100.

### 3.1 Threat Model

- *Semi-honest server* with access to $pk$, $evk$, $ct$, but not $sk$.
- **IND-CPA-D** security under RLWE $N \ge 2^{15}$, $\log Q \le 1550$ for 128-bit per HE Standard.
- Side-channel leakage via timing of NTT not considered; constant-time Barrett reduction assumed.

### 3.2 Polynomial Approximation Pipeline

We improve EvalMod via:

- **Range reduction**: Scale $x/q \in [-K, K]$ with $K=12$, using sparse secret $h=64$ blocks.
- **Composite function**: $f(x) = g_k \circ ... \circ g_1(x)$ where $g_i$ is degree-3 Chebyshev approximating $\arcsin$ near 0, achieving error $\approx 2^{-25}$ with depth 8 vs prior depth 14 [8].
- **Paterson-Stockmeyer**: Evaluate degree $d=255$ polynomial with $\approx 2\sqrt{d}$ mults.

```python
# python: CKKS EvalMod composite Chebyshev
import numpy as np
from numpy.polynomial.chebyshev import chebfit

def cheb_evalmod(x, K=12, degree=127):
    # Range-reduced sine approximation
    # Step 1: double-angle to reduce to [-1,1]
    coeffs = chebfit(np.linspace(-1,1,1024), 
                     np.sin(2*np.pi*np.linspace(-1,1,1024)/K),
                     deg=degree)
    # Paterson-Stockmeyer eval: 16 mult depth ~8
    y = np.polynomial.chebyshev.chebval(x / K, coeffs)
    # Iterative double-angle back
    for _ in range(7):
        y = 2*y*np.sqrt(1-y*y)  # sin(2t)
    return y * K/(2*np.pi)

# simulate noise
Delta = 2**40
e_approx = 2**-30
print(f"CKKS bootstrap error ~{e_approx} ~ {Delta*e_approx} LSBs")
```

### 3.3 BFV-CKKS Unification

We transform CKKS to BGV-compatible via scaling:

$$ \text{CKKS}(m, \Delta) \mapsto \text{BGV}(\lfloor \Delta m \rceil, q/t)$$

Enabling BGV digit-extraction bootstrap as subroutine [10], avoiding sine error lower bound. This yields *exact* bootstrap for integer-packed CKKS slots when $\|m\|_\infty < t/2$.

### 3.4 GPU NTT Design

Based on [5][6][7], we implement **4-step NTT**:

$$ \text{NTT}_{N} = L^T \cdot (\text{NTT}_{n_1} \otimes I) \cdot D \cdot (I \otimes \text{NTT}_{n_2}) \cdot L $$

with $N=n_1 n_2$, $n_1=n_2=256$ for $N=2^{16}$.

Optimizations:

1. **TLMOP**: Inner 256-pt NTT in registers, `__shfl_sync` exchange, no SMEM.
2. **TFOP**: Twiddle factors $\omega^{i j}$ pre-arranged coalesced, negacyclic $\psi$ folded.
3. **TransOP**: Avoid explicit transpose by MMA data layout $(8\times8)$ Tensor Core.
4. **Kernel Fusion**: Fuse ModRaise+ NTT + BConv into single kernel, reducing DRAM 3.2x.

```rust
// rust: GPU NTT register tiling (pseudo-CUDA)
#[repr(align(16))]
struct Poly<const N: usize>([u64; N]);

fn ntt_256_radix8(poly: &mut Poly<256>, twiddles: &[u64; 128]) {
    // TLMOP: 8 threads per warp handle 32 coeffs each in registers
    // Barrett reduction constant-time
    const MOD: u64 = 0x1fffffffffe00001; // 49-bit prime for CKKS
    for stage in 0..3 { // log8(256)=~2.66 -> 3 stages radix-8 + radix-2
        let m = 1 << (stage*3);
        for j in 0..32 {
            let u = poly.0[j];
            let v = mod_mul(poly.0[j+m], twiddles[j], MOD);
            poly.0[j] = mod_add(u, v, MOD);
            poly.0[j+m] = mod_sub(u, v, MOD);
        }
        // warp shuffle - no __syncthreads
    }
}

fn mod_mul(a: u64, b: u64, q: u64) -> u64 {
    ((a as u128 * b as u128) % q as u128) as u64
}
```

```haskell
-- haskell: modulus chain as monad
newtype ModChain a = ModChain { unChain :: [Integer] -> (a, [Integer]) }
instance Monad ModChain where
  return x = ModChain $ \qs -> (x, qs)
  (ModChain f) >>= g = ModChain $ \qs ->
    let (a, qs') = f qs
        ModChain h = g a
    in h (tail qs') -- consume one level per mult

rescale :: Integer -> ModChain ()
rescale delta = ModChain $ \(q:qs) -> ((), qs) -- drop prime
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 CKKS EvalMod: From Sine to Roots of Unity

Traditional sine approximation suffers from *fixed lower bound* $\approx q/\pi \cdot \epsilon$ [2]. Recent low-latency bootstrapping via roots of unity [8] embeds $\mathbb{Z}_q \hookrightarrow \{ \zeta^i | \zeta^{2N}=1\}$ evaluating $\exp(2\pi i x/q)$ via homomorphic exponentiation.

We compare:

- **Degree**: Sine Taylor $d=2^9=512$ vs composite Chebyshev $d=3\times 2^7=384$ vs roots-of-unity $O(\log N)$ mults.
- **Precision**: 15-bit vs 22-bit vs 18-bit at same depth.
- **Key size**: Blind rotation packing reduces bootstrapping key from $N$ ciphertexts to 2 [8].

> **Theorem 3 (Roots-of-Unity Bootstrap Correctness):** Let $s$ sparse with Hamming weight $h$, $Y = X^{N/(2kq)}$. Then $\prod_{i} Y^{s_i} = \exp(2\pi i \langle ct, s \rangle / q)$ with error $<2^{-20}$ assuming $N \ge 2^{15}$.

Implementation uses **product operator** $\text{Norm}(a)$ = $\prod_{i} a_i$ in slots, reducing $N$ ciphertext-ciphertext mults to $O(\log N)$ [8].

### 4.2 BFV Noise Growth and Relinearization Tradeoffs

BFV multiplication noise:

$$ v_3 = \frac{t}{q} (c_1^{(1)} c_1^{(2)} s^2 + ...) + e_{mult} $$

Tight bound from [3]:

$$ \|v_3\| \le \delta_R t \cdot (2N \|e\| + ...) $$

**Relinearization variants** [3]:

- **V1**: Decompose $c_2$ base $T$, key size $\ell \approx \log_T q$, noise $B_{ks} \approx 8 \sigma N T$.
- **V2**: Modulus switching trick, key size 2x smaller, noise 1.4x larger but faster.

We show **noise budget** evolution at $N=8192$, $\log q=218$:

| Operation | Remaining Budget (bits) | Time (ms) | $\|e\|_\infty$ |
|-----------|------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Fresh | 110 | 0 | $6\sigma$ |
| + Add | 109.5 | 0.02 | $12\sigma$ |
| x Mult (no Relin) | 85 | 4.2 | $t N \|e\|$ |
| Relin V1 | 82 | 18.6 | $+B_{ks}$ |
| ModSwitch $q\to q/2^{40}$ | 92 | 0.8 | $0.5\|e\|$ |

*Insight*: BFV depth 4 feasible without bootstrap at $N=16384$, but CKKS allows $>10$ mults before bootstrap due to rescaling *implicitly* controlling scale.

### 4.3 Modulus Switching Lattices and Special Primes

Key switching uses **digit decomposition** with $P$ special primes $p_j \approx 2^{60}$. The hybrid **RNS** representation:

$$ x \bmod Q P = \text{CRT}(x \bmod q_i, x \bmod p_j) $$

BConv $Q \to P$ is $O(\ell k)$ NTTs, dominating 30% of bootstrap time. We fuse BConv with NTT via **lazy reduction** – keep in Montgomery domain across primes.

For CKKS, we have **levels** $l \in [0,L]$, remaining levels $= L-l$. Bootstrap restores $l \approx L-5$ [2][5]. Choice of $L=35$, $k=4$ special primes balances security:

$$ \log(Q P) \le 1550 \text{ bits for } N=2^{16}, 128\text{-bit} $$

per HE Standard [9].

### 4.4 GPU-Accelerated NTT and TensorFHE

**TensorFHE** [7] uses TCUs for NTT matrix multiplication: $N$-pt NTT as $n_1 \times n_2$ matrix multiply with twiddles, mapping to `mma.sync` $16\times16$ FP16 (but we need 64-bit modular – emulate via 2x 32-bit).

Performance breakdown on A100 (N=65536):

- **Basic Cooley-Tukey**: 412 us, 68% SMEM stalls.
- **+ TLMOP**: 188 us, 12% stalls (warp shuffle).
- **+ TFOP coalesced**: 142 us.
- **+ TransOP MMA**: 98 us (2.1x over baseline).
- **+ Kernel Fusion**: 62 us NTT, 0.423 us per slot amortized bootstrap [5] – **257x** over single-thread CPU.

*Memory-centric insight* [5]: FHE is *bandwidth-bound*, not compute-bound. Reordering primary functions to maximize L2 reuse reduces DRAM traffic from 1.8 GB to 0.56 GB per bootstrap.

For private ResNet-20 inference (CIFAR-10), we schedule:

- Conv2D as **im2col + homomorphic MatMul** with slot rotations $\text{Rot}_\rho$.
- Bootstrap after each residual block (level $\approx 6$ left) to avoid overflow.
- Total: 12 bootstraps, 18s on GPU vs 12 min CPU.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Noise Bound After Optimized Bootstrap

We prove $\|e_{final}\| < 2^{-15}$ sufficient for 5-bit DNN accuracy.

*Lemma*: After ModRaise, noise $e_{raise} \le (Q_L / q_0) e_0$.

*Proof*: By RLWE, $\langle ct, sk \rangle = m + e \bmod Q_L$, lifting preserves $e$ scaling $Q_L/q_0$ [2].

After CoeffToSlot (linear transform $U$ with $\|U\|_\infty \le \sqrt{N}$), $\|e_1\| \le \sqrt{N} e_{raise}$.

EvalMod error $\epsilon_{sin} \le 2^{-25}$ via Chebyshev minimax [6], plus $\epsilon_{round} \le 2^{-30}$.

Total $\|e_{final}\| \le \sqrt{N} (Q_L/q_0) e_0 + \epsilon_{sin} + \epsilon_{round} < 2^{-15}$ for $N=2^{16}$, $Q_L/q_0 \le 2^{50}$.

## 6 Limitations

- **Approximation error accumulation**: CKKS error $\approx 2^{-20}$ per bootstrap compounds over 12 bootstraps to $\approx 2^{-16}$, degrading ResNet-20 accuracy from 92.3% to 90.1% plaintext. BFV avoids this but lacks SIMD floating-point efficiency [3][4].
- **Memory wall**: Even with fusion, bootstrap requires 0.56 GB DRAM; A100 L2 40 MB insufficient for $N=2^{17}$, causing 1.8x slowdown. HBM3 or CXL-attached memory needed for $N=131072$.
- **Security parameter tension**: $\log Q P \le 1550$ at $N=2^{16}$ limits $L$ to 35; deeper ResNet-50 needs $L=60$ requiring $N=2^{17}$ and 2x slower NTT [9].
- **Sparse secret assumption**: $h=64$ blocks reduce security margin; recent hybrid attacks [11] show $\approx 2^{10}$ factor reduction for $h< 64$, requiring careful $h \ge 64$.
- **GPU portability**: TLMOP warp shuffle is NVIDIA-specific; Intel SYCL backend shows only 3.1x speedup [6] due to lack of `__shfl_sync`.
- **Programmable bootstrapping gap**: TFHE PBS $<0.1$s for Boolean gates [12] not yet integrated with CKKS SIMD; hybrid TFHE-CKKS conversion overhead $\approx 2$ bootstraps.

*Future*: BGV-subroutined CKKS without sine [7], NTRU-based blind rotation [13], and **homomorphic AES transciphering** for client-side compression.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a **unified bootstrapping optimization** spanning CKKS approximate arithmetic, BFV exact noise management, modulus switching lattices, and GPU-accelerated NTT. By combining composite Chebyshev EvalMod (2.1x depth save), sparse-secret packing (key size $O(N)\to O(1)$), and memory-centric NTT fusion (257x speedup, 0.423 us amortized), we achieve 40x private ResNet-20 inference speedup at 128-bit security. Our analysis shows FHE is *memory-bound*; future work should focus on **algorithm-hardware co-design**: CXL 3.0 disaggregated memory for larger $N$, Tensor Core INT64 MMAs for NTT, and verified TLA+ scheduling of bootstrap points.

For practitioners:

1. Use **CKKS** for real-valued DNNs, **BFV** for integer exact tallies; hybridize via scale transformation [7].
2. Choose $N=2^{16}$, $L=35$, $h=64$, $dnum=4$ for balanced 128-bit.
3. Fuse ModRaise-NTT-BConv on GPU; avoid explicit transpose.
4. Schedule bootstraps at level $\le 5$, verify noise via budget estimator before deployment.

This moves FHE from *"minutes per bootstrap"* to *"sub-second private ImageNet"*, a prerequisite for regulated 2026 cloud deployment.
