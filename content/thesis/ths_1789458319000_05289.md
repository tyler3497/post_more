# Functorial Architectures and the Geometry of Learning: Categorical Deep Learning, the Geometric Deep Learning Blueprint, and Sheaf Neural Networks on Graphs

## Abstract

Deep learning has achieved extraordinary empirical success, yet it has long lacked a unifying mathematical foundation. Two complementary programs now supply one: *geometric deep learning*, which derives architectures from symmetry groups and the geometry of their domains following Klein's Erlangen program, and *categorical deep learning*, which derives architectures from universal constructions — functors, lenses, and monads — in categories of parametric maps. This thesis develops the mathematical core of both programs and shows how they converge on graphs, where *sheaf neural networks* attach vector spaces (stalks) and linear restriction maps to nodes and edges, generalizing graph diffusion through the sheaf Laplacian. We present Bronstein's blueprint with its geometric priors of equivariance, deformation stability, and scale separation; the categorical reformulation of architectures as morphisms in Para(Smooth) with backpropagation as a functor; and the sheaf diffusion hierarchy of Bodnar et al., which provably expands the expressive power of graph networks in heterophilic and oversmoothing regimes. The result is a single functorial picture in which geometry supplies the *constraints* and category theory supplies the *compositional machinery* for architecture design.

## 1 Introduction

The last decade of machine learning has been an *experimental* revolution. Convolutional networks, recurrent networks, graph networks, and transformers each emerged from ad hoc engineering intuition, and their relationships were discovered only in hindsight — identical concepts were repeatedly reinvented and rebranded for want of unifying principles [1].

Two principled responses have crystallized. The first is **geometric deep learning** (GDL): most learning problems of interest are not generic functions in high dimensions — which would be cursed estimation problems — but carry *essential regularities* inherited from the low-dimensional structure of the physical world. Following Felix Klein's Erlangen program, these regularities are expressed as *symmetry*: a domain Ω acted upon by a group G of transformations that leave the task unchanged. The architecture is then derived from the pair (Ω, G), not guessed [1].

The second is **categorical deep learning** (CDL): architectures are *compositional* objects, and composition is the native subject matter of category theory. Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras showed that gradient descent itself is a *functor* from parametrized functions to update rules, giving backpropagation a compositional semantics [5]. Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi systematized this via lenses, parametric maps (Para), and reverse-derivative categories [7], while Gavranović's doctoral work rebuilt the supervised-learning pipeline — parametricity, bidirectionality, architectures, optimizers — as parametric weighted optics [3]. Most ambitiously, Gavranović, Lessard, Dudzik, von Glehn, Araújo, and Veličković argue that the *universal algebra of monads valued in a 2-category of parametric maps* subsumes both the top-down specification of constraints and the bottom-up specification of implementations in a single algebraic theory of all architectures [2].

These programs meet decisively on **graphs**, the most general discrete domain in the blueprint. There, cellular sheaf theory — vector spaces attached to vertices and edges with linear *restriction maps* between them — yields *sheaf neural networks* (SNNs), first introduced by Hansen and Gebhart [6] and developed into a full diffusion theory by Bodnar, Di Giovanni, Chamberlain, Liò, and Bronstein [4]. SNNs are simultaneously a geometric construction (the sheaf is a discrete vector bundle with connection) and a categorical one (sheaves are functors from a face-poset category into vector spaces). This thesis develops all three layers — the geometric blueprint, the categorical algebra, and the sheaf theory of graph networks — as three descriptions of one functorial architecture theory.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Categories, functors, and natural transformations

A **category** C consists of objects, morphisms between them, associative composition, and identity morphisms. A **functor** F: C → D sends objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving identities and composition. A **natural transformation** η: F ⇒ G is a family of morphisms η_X: F(X) → G(X) commuting with every morphism of C. These are the working tools of the entire thesis; we recall them only to fix notation.

Central to categorical deep learning is the category **Para(Smooth)** of *parametrized smooth maps*. An object is a Euclidean space; a morphism A → B is an equivalence class of pairs (P, f) with P a parameter space and f: P × A → B smooth, identified up to reparametrization of P [3][7]. A neural network layer *is* a morphism in Para(Smooth); a whole architecture is a composite morphism. Learning is thus literally *composition* in a category.

### 2.2 The geometric deep learning blueprint

The GDL blueprint organizes the most successful architectures around five geometric domains — the **5G**: **grids** (Euclidean domains with translation symmetry), **groups** (homogeneous spaces with group structure), **graphs** (combinatorial domains with permutation symmetry), **geodesics** (manifolds with isometry or gauge symmetry), and **gauges** (vector bundles where features transform under local gauge transformations) [1]. Each domain carries a symmetry group G acting on signals x: Ω → C, and the blueprint extracts three *geometric priors* from this data:

1. **Symmetry / equivariance**: layers must satisfy f(ρ_in(g) · x) = ρ_out(g) · f(x) for group actions ρ on input and output signal spaces. For grids this recovers convolution; for graphs it recovers permutation-equivariant message passing.
2. **Deformation stability**: small perturbations of the domain (diffeomorphisms, edge rewiring) should change the output only slightly — a Lipschitz condition in an appropriate metric.
3. **Scale separation**: hierarchical, multiscale processing (pooling, coarsening) that builds global structure from local neighborhoods.

From these priors the blueprint derives the standard recipe: *linear G-equivariant layers* ⊕ *local aggregation/pooling* ⊕ *pointwise nonlinearities*, stacked into multiscale hierarchies [1].

### 2.3 Cellular sheaves on graphs

A **cellular sheaf** F on a graph G = (V, E) assigns to every vertex v a vector space F(v) (the *stalk* at v) and to every edge e a vector space F(e), together with, for each incident pair v ≤ e, a linear **restriction map** F_{v≤e}: F(v) → F(e) [4][6]. The direct sum of vertex stalks is the space of 0-cochains C^0(G; F); the **coboundary** δ: C^0 → C^1 measures disagreement across edges; the **sheaf Laplacian** L_F = δ*δ acts node-wise as

> **Definition (Sheaf Laplacian):** L_F(x)_v = Σ_{u, v≤e} F_{v≤e}^⊤ (F_{v≤e} x_v − F_{u≤e} x_u).

Its kernel is the space of **global sections** H^0(G; F): assignments of stalk vectors consistent across every edge. Classical graph diffusion (and hence the GCN) is the special case of the *constant sheaf* with identity restriction maps; oversmoothing is the statement that heat flow on the constant sheaf converges to globally constant sections [4].

### 2.4 Lenses, optics, and backpropagation

**Backpropagation as functor** [5]: Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras construct a category Learn whose morphisms are *learners* — parametrized functions equipped with an update rule and a *request function* propagating desired outputs backward. Gradient descent (for fixed step size and suitable error) is a monoidal functor Para → Learn; the request function is the backward pass, and functoriality is the chain rule composed correctly across layers. Cruttwell et al. generalize this to **parametric lenses**: a learner is a lens (a forward *get* and backward *put*) internal to a reverse-derivative category, and composition of learners is lens composition [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *synthetic and functorial* rather than experimental: we take definitions and theorems from the three literatures and assemble them into one coherent architecture theory, checking at each step that the categorical, geometric, and sheaf-theoretic descriptions coincide on their common domain (graphs). Concretely:

1. **Categorical layer.** Architectures are morphisms in **Para(Smooth)**, training steps are lenses in a reverse-derivative category, and architecture *families* (RNNs, CNNs, GNNs) are algebras for endofunctors — monads in the 2-category of parametric maps [2][3].
2. **Geometric layer.** The blueprint's priors (equivariance, deformation stability, scale separation) become *constraints* on those morphisms: an architecture satisfies a geometric constraint when its layers are natural with respect to a group action — equivariance is a **naturality square** [1][2].
3. **Sheaf layer.** On graphs, we lift the trivial sheaf of classical GNNs to learnable cellular sheaves: restriction maps F_{v≤e} = Φ(x_v, x_u) are parametrized by a neural network, and diffusion proceeds along the learned sheaf Laplacian [4][6].

The three layers are glued by a single observation: **equivariance is naturality, message passing is a coboundary, and a learned sheaf is a learned geometry**. The functoriality theorems of categorical deep learning guarantee that anything built compositionally from equivariant, sheaf-respecting pieces inherits those properties automatically — no per-architecture proof required.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The 5G blueprint as a classification of architectures

The power of the blueprint is *taxonomic*: every major architecture is the instantiation of (Ω, G) for some domain and group.

| Domain | Symmetry group G | Signal | Architecture | Prior enforced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grid (images) | Translations (and rotations) | x: Z² → R^c | CNN | Translation equivariance |
| Group (spherical data) | SO(3) | x: S² → R^c | Spherical CNN | Rotation equivariance |
| Graph (molecules, social nets) | Permutations S_n | x: V → R^c | GNN / MPNN | Permutation equivariance |
| Geodesic (meshes, manifolds) | Isometries / gauge | x: M → R^c | Mesh CNN | Gauge equivariance |
| Gauge (bundles) | Local gauge group | sections of a bundle | Gauge CNN | Local gauge equivariance |

The blueprint's constructive claim is the converse: given a *new* domain with a known symmetry group, the same recipe *manufactures* a principled architecture rather than requiring a fresh invention [1]. Gauge-equivariant CNNs on manifolds and E(n)-equivariant graph networks are case studies of exactly this derivation.

> **Theorem (Blueprint universality, informal):** For any domain Ω with symmetry group G, the class of G-equivariant, deformation-stable, scale-separated networks is dense in the continuous G-equivariant functions on signals over Ω, provided the equivariant linear layers span the commutant of the group action.

The argument reduces universality to the representation theory of G: equivariant linear maps are intertwiners, and pointwise nonlinearities generate the algebra [1]. Crucially for our thesis, this argument is *functorial*: the assignment (Ω, G) ↦ {admissible architectures} is itself a functor from a category of geometric domains to a category of parametric maps.

### 4.2 Functorial architectures: Para, lenses, and the algebraic theory

We now make the categorical layer precise. In **Para(Smooth)**, a dense layer with weights W and bias b is the morphism (R^{m×n} × R^m, f) with f((W, b), x) = σ(Wx + b). Sequential composition concatenates parameter spaces; parallel composition is the monoidal product. *Weight tying* — the essence of convolution and of recurrent sharing — is a comonoid structure on objects, not an afterthought [3].

Backpropagation becomes structure, not algorithm. A **learner** A → B is a tuple (P, I, U, r) of parameter space, implementation I: P × A → B, update U: P × A × B → P, and request r: P × A × B → A. Fong et al. prove:

> **Theorem (Backprop as functor [5]):** Fix a step size ε > 0 and an error function e: R × R → R with ∂e/∂x(x₀, −) invertible. Then gradient descent defines a strict symmetric monoidal functor L_{ε,e}: Para → Learn.

Composition in Learn is exactly forward-then-backward composition; the request functions compose by the chain rule. Cruttwell et al. recast this as **parametric lenses**: in any reverse-derivative category, a learner is a lens whose *put* is the reverse derivative, and gradient-based optimizers (Adam, AdaGrad, Nesterov) are 2-cells modifying the lens [7].

The culminating step is Gavranović et al.'s **algebraic theory of architectures** [2]: both *constraints* (e.g., "be G-equivariant") and *implementations* (RNN unrollings, attention blocks) are captured by the *universal algebra of monads valued in a 2-category of parametric maps*.

```haskell
-- A learner as a parametric lens (conceptual Haskell sketch)
data Learner p a b = Learner
  { params  :: p
  , forward :: p -> a -> b        -- get  : implementation I
  , update  :: p -> a -> b -> p   -- put  : parameter update U
  , request :: p -> a -> b -> a   -- put* : backward request r
  }

-- Composition is lens composition; the chain rule is functoriality.
compose :: Learner p a b -> Learner q b c -> Learner (p, q) a c
compose f g = Learner
  { params  = (params f, params g)
  , forward = \(p, q) a -> forward g q (forward f p a)
  , update  = \(p, q) a c ->
      let b  = forward f p a
          b' = request g q b c       -- request propagates backward
      in (update f p a b', update g q b c)
  , request = \(p, q) a c ->
      request f p a (request g q (forward f p a) c)
  }
```

### 4.3 Sheaf neural networks: geometry learned on graphs

Classical GNNs implicitly assume the *constant sheaf*: every stalk is R^d and every restriction map is the identity.

- **Oversmoothing**: heat diffusion ẋ = −Δ_G x converges to ker(Δ_G) — constant on connected components. Depth destroys information *by theorem*, not by bad training.
- **Heterophily failure**: when neighbors belong to different classes, averaging neighbor features is the wrong inductive bias; the constant sheaf cannot represent "agree to disagree".

A **cellular sheaf** repairs both by giving each edge its own linear map between stalks.

> **Theorem (Sheaf separation hierarchy [4], informal):** There exist heterophilic node-classification tasks on which diffusion with a learned general sheaf converges to linearly separable representations, while diffusion under any trivial-sheaf GNN converges to a representation on which no linear classifier separates the classes.

On the practical side, the sheaf is *learned*: restriction maps F_{v≤e} = Φ(x_v, x_u) are produced by a small neural network from endpoint features, and the discretized diffusion layer

```python
# Sheaf diffusion layer (conceptual PyTorch sketch)
class SheafDiffusionLayer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, stalk_dim, hidden):
        super().__init__()
        # Phi: learnable restriction-map generator (the "learned geometry")
        self.phi = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(2 * stalk_dim, hidden), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden, stalk_dim * stalk_dim))

    def forward(self, x, edge_index):
        # x: (n, d) 0-cochain; build restriction maps per incident pair
        msgs = []
        for (v, u) in edge_index.t().tolist():
            Fv = self.phi(torch.cat([x[v], x[u]])).view(d, d)
            Fu = self.phi(torch.cat([x[u], x[v]])).view(d, d)
            # coboundary contribution: disagreement measured in the edge stalk
            msgs.append((v, Fv.t() @ (Fv @ x[v] - Fu @ x[u])))
        agg = scatter_add(msgs)          # assemble L_F x
        return x - self.eps * agg        # Euler step of sheaf heat flow
```

Hansen and Gebhart's original SNNs [6] already demonstrated that sheaf convolutions generalize GCNs and outperform them precisely where relations are *asymmetric or signed* — the regime where the constant sheaf is misspecified.

### 4.4 The convergence: three descriptions, one functor

We can now state the unifying thesis precisely as a commuting triangle:

- **Geometry** assigns to a domain (Ω, G) a *constraint theory*: equivariance equations, deformation-stability bounds, scale hierarchies [1].
- **Category theory** assigns to a constraint theory an *algebraic theory of architectures*: monads in parametric maps whose algebras are admissible networks [2][3].
- **Sheaf theory** assigns to a graph a *geometric object* — the cellular sheaf — whose Laplacian generates the admissible diffusions [4][6].

The triangle commutes: a G-equivariant sheaf diffusion layer is a morphism in Para(Smooth) satisfying the geometric constraint equations, and its backpropagation is the lens composite guaranteed by functoriality [5][7].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The thesis is mathematical, so "empirical" here means *theorems with computational content* plus the reported benchmark behavior of the cited works.

**Result 1 — GDL blueprint coverage.** Bronstein et al. show that CNNs, group-equivariant CNNs, Deep Sets, GNNs/MPNNs, RNNs, and transformers all instantiate the blueprint's recipe for their (Ω, G) pairs, and derive gauge-equivariant architectures on meshes as a novel prediction of the framework [1].

**Result 2 — Functorial backpropagation.** The functor L_{ε,e}: Para → Learn of [5] is proved by verifying functoriality directly: composition of parametrized functions maps to composite learners with the chain-rule request. Cruttwell et al. extend this to the full optimizer zoo — Adam, AdaGrad, Nesterov — as 2-cells on parametric lenses [7].

**Result 3 — Sheaf separation and heterophily.** Bodnar et al. prove the hierarchy theorem of §4.3: increasing sheaf generality monotonically expands the class-separation capacity of the infinite-time diffusion limit. Empirically, Neural Sheaf Diffusion models with learned restriction maps obtain competitive results on heterophilic benchmarks where constant-sheaf GNNs degrade, while retaining standard performance on homophilic ones [4].

**Result 4 — Sheaf generalization of GCN.** Hansen and Gebhart prove that sheaf neural networks *strictly generalize* graph convolutional networks: the GCN diffusion operator is recovered exactly for the constant sheaf, and SNNs outperform GCNs on synthetic tasks with asymmetric and signed relations, where the constant-sheaf assumption is violated by construction [6].

**Result 5 — Algebraic recovery of geometric constraints.** Gavranović et al. show that GDL constraints — including equivariance — are recovered as *equations* in their algebraic theory, and that standard architectures (RNNs as algebras for an endofunctor, with unrolling as the algebra structure map) are recovered as *models* of the theory [2].

## 6 Limitations

**Computational cost of sheaves.** Learning a d×d restriction map per incident node-edge pair costs O(|E|·d²) parameters and memory. Practical SNNs use small stalk dimensions, diagonal or orthogonal parametrizations, or shared Φ networks, each trimming the expressivity the hierarchy theorems promise [4][6].

**The blueprint is not a theorem.** The GDL blueprint is a *design methodology*, not a fully formal classification: "deformation stability" lacks a canonical metric on general domains, and the universality claim of §4.1 is proved only in special cases (compact groups, finite permutation groups). Turning the blueprint's recipe into machine-checkable equations inside the algebraic theory of [2] remains work in progress.

**Categorical overhead vs. engineering reality.** Para(Smooth), weighted optics, and 2-categorical algebra are powerful specification languages, but current deep learning frameworks (tensor-centric, array-programming) do not natively speak them. The implementations in [7] and [3] are proofs of concept; industrial adoption would require compiler-level support for lens composition and reverse-derivative categories.

**Sheaf learning is non-convex and fragile.** Jointly learning restriction maps and diffusion dynamics creates a difficult optimization landscape: the sheaf can collapse to the trivial (constant) sheaf during training, silently recovering an ordinary GNN. The infinite-time separation theorems describe *gradient flow on a fixed sheaf*, not the dynamics of *learning* the sheaf [4].

**Scope of the unification claim.** Our commuting-triangle picture is established rigorously on graphs and for compact symmetry groups. Extending it to the gauge (bundle) setting of the blueprint — where features are sections of vector bundles and restriction maps become parallel transport — requires infinite-dimensional categories of smooth maps and is an open research direction.

## 7 Conclusion

We have developed three interlocking mathematical perspectives on deep learning architecture design. **Geometric deep learning** derives architectures from symmetry: the pair (Ω, G) determines equivariant layers, deformation stability, and scale separation, unifying CNNs, GNNs, transformers, and gauge networks under Klein's Erlangen program [1]. **Categorical deep learning** derives architectures from composition: networks are morphisms in Para(Smooth), backpropagation is a functor into learners [5], optimizers are 2-cells on parametric lenses [7], and entire architecture families are algebras for monads in a 2-category of parametric maps [2][3]. **Sheaf neural networks** instantiate both on graphs: a cellular sheaf is a learnable discrete geometry whose Laplacian generalizes graph diffusion, whose global sections generalize consensus, and whose learned restriction maps provably expand expressive power in heterophilic and oversmoothing regimes [4][6].

The central claim of this thesis is that these are not three foundations but one: *geometry supplies the equations, category theory supplies the composition, and sheaf theory supplies the discrete geometric objects on which both act*. The practical moral is immediate — specify symmetries and sheaf structure first, and let functoriality guarantee that the pieces compose — and the research frontier is equally clear: machine-checkable blueprint equations, scalable sheaf parametrizations, and the gauge-theoretic extension of the whole picture. The Erlangen program, a century and a half after Klein, turns out to have been a design document for neural networks all along.
