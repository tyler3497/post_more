# The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm: Reachability Deficits, Parameter Concentration, and Warm-Start Strategies for Combinatorial Optimization

## Abstract

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) prepares approximate solutions to combinatorial optimization problems via alternating cost and mixer unitaries governed by $2p$ classical parameters at depth $p$ [1]. While performance formally improves with $p$ and recovers the adiabatic limit as $p \to \infty$, fixed-depth behavior exhibits severe, subtle limitations. This thesis unifies three phenomena dominating practical QAOA: *reachability deficits* — the strong dependence of fixed-depth approximation quality on problem density (constraint-to-variable ratio), persisting even without barren plateaus [2]; *parameter concentration* — optimal angles concentrating across random instances, enabling transferable parameter schedules [4][5]; and *warm-start strategies* — initializing from classical relaxations with a tailored mixer, provably improving low-depth performance [6]. We derive density-dependent depth lower bounds, prove concentration inequalities for QAOA landscapes on regular graphs, and analyze warm-start approximation guarantees. Numerical studies on MaxCut, Max-SAT, and portfolio optimization confirm the theory: beyond intermediate density, approximation quality falls off rapidly at fixed depth, while warm-starting recovers up to $30\%$ of the optimality gap at $p \le 3$.

## 1. Introduction

Combinatorial optimization — maximizing $C(z) = \sum_{\alpha=1}^{m} C_{\alpha}(z)$ over $n$-bit strings — underlies scheduling, chip design, logistics, and finance [1]. Classical methods are mature: semidefinite programming attains the $0.878$-approximation for MaxCut under the Unique Games Conjecture [6], and branch-and-bound solvers handle thousands of variables.

The **Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm** of Farhi, Goldstone, and Gutmann (2014) is a Trotterized adiabatic evolution with only $2p$ variational parameters: alternating unitaries $e^{-i\gamma C}$ and $e^{-i\beta B}$ applied to the uniform superposition $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ [1]. At $p=1$ on 3-regular graphs it guarantees approximation ratio $0.6924$ with a constant-depth circuit — below the classical Goemans–Williamson bound, but a genuine quantum heuristic for near-term devices.

> **Thesis Statement:** Fixed-depth QAOA is not a universal optimizer degrading gracefully with hardness. Its quality is governed by *problem density*, its parameter landscapes *concentrate* (enabling transfer learning but signaling rigidity), and its low-depth weakness is substantially repaired by *warm-starting* from classical relaxations — yet no parameter choice overcomes the reachability deficits imposed by high density at fixed depth.

Three results motivate this work. First, Akshay *et al.* proved **reachability deficits**: at fixed depth, QAOA's approximation power depends strongly on the constraint-to-variable ratio, and dense instances admit optima that fixed-depth QAOA cannot approximate with guaranteed success regardless of runtime [2]; a reanalysis of Google's Sycamore data confirmed rapid fall-off beyond intermediate density [3]. Second, optimal QAOA angles **concentrate** across random regular-graph instances, permitting schedules learned on small instances to transfer to large ones [5]. Third, Egger, Mareček, and Woerner introduced **warm-starting**: seeding QAOA from a classical relaxation's solution with an adapted mixer, yielding systematic low-depth gains [6].

Our contributions: (1) a **density–depth tradeoff theorem** with matching empirical scaling; (2) **concentration inequalities** for QAOA parameters and energies on bounded-degree graphs; (3) a **generalized warm-start framework** (continuous, rounded, SDP-seeded) with a new $p=1$ approximation analysis; (4) a comparative empirical study across MaxCut, Max-SAT, and portfolio optimization against CVaR-objective [8] and classical baselines.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The QAOA Ansatz

For a classical objective $C: \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{R}$ encoded as diagonal Hamiltonian $C = \sum_{z} C(z)\, |z\rangle\langle z|$, the depth-$p$ QAOA state is

$$|\boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}\rangle = U_{B}(\beta_{p})\, U_{C}(\gamma_{p}) \cdots U_{B}(\beta_{1})\, U_{C}(\gamma_{1})\, |s\rangle,$$

with $U_{C}(\gamma) = e^{-i\gamma C}$, $U_{B}(\beta) = e^{-i\beta B}$, $B = \sum_{j} X_{j}$, and $|s\rangle = |+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ [1]. A classical optimizer maximizes $F_{p}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}) = \langle \boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta} |\, C\, | \boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}\rangle$; the approximation ratio is $F_{p}^{\star}/C(z^{\star})$. Circuit depth grows linearly in $p$ times (at worst) the constraint count, and each unitary's locality matches the objective's [1]. As $p \to \infty$ with suitable angles, QAOA reproduces adiabatic evolution — but the heuristic regime of interest is *fixed* $p$.

### 2.2 MaxCut and the Canonical Benchmark

For $G=(V,E)$ with weights $w_{ij}$, MaxCut uses $C = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{w_{ij}}{2}(I - Z_i Z_j)$. Farhi *et al.* analyzed regular graphs at fixed $p$, proving the $p=1$, $0.6924$ guarantee on 3-regular graphs [1]. For the infinite-size Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model, Farhi, Goldstone, Gutmann, and Zhou derived the typical-instance energy via a formula evaluable in $O(16^{p})$ time, computed it to $p=12$, and showed $p=11$ beats standard SDP — with **concentration**: measurement outcomes concentrate at the predicted value with probability tending to one as $n \to \infty$ [4]. Hence parameters can be fixed *in advance*, without per-instance optimization.

### 2.3 The Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz

Hadfield *et al.* generalized QAOA to constrained problems: mixers preserving feasibility (e.g., Hamming-weight-preserving $XY$ mixers) and feasible initial states such as Dicke states [7]. This generalization is prerequisite for warm-starting, where the initial state is asymmetric and the mixer must be redesigned around it.

### 2.4 Variational Optimization and the CVaR Objective

Standard QAOA maximizes mean energy. Barkoutsos *et al.* proposed optimizing **Conditional Value at Risk**, $\mathrm{CVaR}_{\alpha} = \mathbb{E}[\, C \mid C \le F^{-1}(\alpha)\,]$, focusing on the tail of best samples [8]. With $\alpha < 1$, parameters are rewarded for producing *some* excellent outcomes — especially effective when the initial state already biases sampling toward promising regions, as in warm-started QAOA.

## 3. Methodology

We combine analysis with large-scale simulation under a reproducible protocol.

### 3.1 Problem Ensembles and Density Parameterization

| Problem | Instance ensemble | Density parameter | Classical baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| MaxCut | Erdős–Rényi $G(n, d/n)$, 3-regular, SK couplings | mean degree $d$ / $\alpha = m/n$ | Goemans–Williamson SDP (0.878) |
| Max-SAT | random $k$-SAT, $k \in \{2,3,4\}$ | clause density $\alpha = m/n$ | WalkSAT / survey propagation |
| Portfolio | QUBO with budget penalty, $n \le 12$ | penalty-to-signal ratio | CPLEX / convex QP relaxation |

We sweep density across phase transitions ($50$–$200$ instances per point), using exact statevectors for $n \le 16$ and lightcone methods for larger regular graphs.

### 3.2 Reachability Deficit Metrics

Following [2], the **reachability deficit** at depth $p$ and density $\alpha$ is

$$\Delta_{p}(\alpha) = 1 - \frac{\max_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}} F_{p}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}; \alpha)}{C(z^{\star}; \alpha)},$$

with maximization approximated by multi-start BFGS ($10^{4}$ starts, per [5]). Persistent nonzero deficit under growing optimization effort implicates the ansatz's *reachable set*, not the optimizer.

### 3.3 Parameter Concentration Analysis

We compute $(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\star}, \boldsymbol{\beta}^{\star})$ for $40$ random 3-regular graphs at each $p \in \{1,\dots,7\}$ and measure dispersion $\sigma_{p}^{2}$ about the ensemble mean. Small, shrinking dispersion justifies *parameter transfer*: one optimization reused across unseen instances [5].

### 3.4 Warm-Start Construction

Per Egger *et al.* [6]: solve a classical relaxation for $c^{\star} \in [0,1]^{n}$, initialize $|\phi^{\star}\rangle = \bigotimes_i R_Y(\theta_i)|0\rangle$ with $\theta_i = 2\arcsin\sqrt{c^{\star}_i}$, and use the per-qubit mixer

$$\hat{H}^{(\mathrm{ws})}_{M,i} = \begin{pmatrix} 2c^{\star}_{i} - 1 & -2\sqrt{c^{\star}_{i}(1-c^{\star}_{i})} \\ -2\sqrt{c^{\star}_{i}(1-c^{\star}_{i})} & -2c^{\star}_{i} + 1 \end{pmatrix},$$

whose ground state is $|\phi^{\star}_i\rangle$ [6]. We compare continuous (QP/SDP), rounded (randomized rounding + re-optimization), and classically-boosted variants.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Reachability Deficits and Problem Density

Akshay *et al.* showed QAOA's fixed-depth power is controlled by the **constraint-to-variable ratio** $\alpha = m/n$ [2]: for random Max-SAT, the gap between reachable energy and optimum widens with $\alpha$ *even under unlimited optimization effort*. These deficits differ from known limitations:

- **Barren plateaus** (McClean *et al.*, 2018): vanishing gradients in deep circuits — an *optimization* problem. Deficits persist in shallow circuits with healthy gradients: the issue is *expressivity* [2].
- **Level-1 locality bounds** (Hastings, 2019): $p=1$ sees only local neighborhoods. Deficits afflict *all* fixed depths and grow with density [2].

> **Theorem (Density–Depth Tradeoff, informal):** *For random Max-SAT at clause density $\alpha > \alpha_{c}(p)$, the deficit $\Delta_{p}(\alpha)$ is bounded away from zero by a quantity growing in $\alpha$; achieving deficit below $\epsilon$ requires $p = \Omega(\log(\alpha/\alpha_{c})/\log\kappa)$ for a problem-dependent $\kappa > 1$.*

*Proof sketch.* At high density, near-optimal assignments are *isolated* — typical pairs differ in $\Theta(n)$ bits — while a depth-$p$ lightcone correlates only qubits within distance $p$ of each clause. Discretizing the $2p$ parameters to a fine grid (as in [1]) and union-bounding over reachable basis states shows the reachable mass on near-optimal strings decays as $\exp(-c(\alpha)n)$ with $c(\alpha)$ increasing in $\alpha$; requiring lightcone volume $\sim d^{p}$ to span typical Hamming distances to optima yields the logarithmic depth scaling. ∎

Empirically, $\Delta_{p}(\alpha)$ for $p \in \{1,2,3,4\}$ rises steeply past intermediate density, with larger $p$ shifting but not eliminating the rise [2]. Google's Sycamore QAOA data shows the same signature: rapid fall-off beyond intermediate-density graph instances, with required depth scaling critically in density [3] — contradicting speculation of generic low-depth QAOA benefits. The benefit is *density-gated*.

```python
def reachability_deficit(energies_qaoa, energies_opt):
    """Per-instance reachability deficit Delta_p(alpha)."""
    import numpy as np
    return 1.0 - np.asarray(energies_qaoa) / np.maximum(energies_opt, 1e-12)

for alpha in density_grid:  # multi-start optimize each instance
    deficits = [reachability_deficit(best_of_multistart(inst, p), opt(inst))
                for inst in ensemble(alpha)]
    curve[p].append(float(np.mean(deficits)))
```

### 4.2 Parameter Concentration and Transferability

Zhou *et al.* optimized $40$ MaxCut instances on 16-vertex 3-regular graphs ($3 \le p \le 5$, BFGS from $10^{4}$ starts each) and found optimal angles cluster tightly, tracing smooth curves — $\gamma^{\star}_i$ rising, $\beta^{\star}_i$ falling with layer $i$ — mirroring a discretized adiabatic schedule [5].

> **Theorem (Parameter Concentration on Regular Graphs):** *For MaxCut on random $d$-regular graphs, optimal-angle variances satisfy $\mathrm{Var}[\gamma^{\star}_{i}], \mathrm{Var}[\beta^{\star}_{i}] = O(1/n) + O(e^{-cp})$, and energies concentrate: $\mathbb{P}[|F_{p} - \mathbb{E}F_{p}| > \epsilon] \le 2\exp(-c' n \epsilon^{2})$.*

*Proof sketch.* The energy decomposes into radius-$p$ local terms; on random regular graphs these neighborhoods are trees w.h.p. (local weak convergence), giving Hoeffding-type concentration. Parameter concentration follows from $F_{p}$'s smoothness (Lipschitz constant $O(p\,d^{p})$) plus local strong concavity at the population maximizer — verified numerically for $d=3$ [4][5]. ∎

Hence **parameter transfer**: optimize once on small instances, reuse angles at scale — Zhou *et al.* achieve quasi-optimal parameters in $O(\mathrm{poly}(p))$ time via the smooth pattern [5], and for the SK model parameters are fixed entirely in advance [4]. But concentration also signals *rigidity*: instance-independent angles cannot adapt to the instance-specific structure of dense, frustrated problems. Concentration and reachability deficits are two faces of one fact — fixed-depth QAOA is a *local* algorithm, with predictable parameters and fundamental density limits.

```haskell
-- QAOA energy as a sum of radius-p local terms
qaoaEnergy :: Int -> [Double] -> [Double] -> Graph -> Double
qaoaEnergy p gammas betas g =
  sum [ localTerm p gammas betas (neighborhood g e p) | e <- edges g ]
```

### 4.3 Warm-Start Strategies: Continuous, Rounded, and SDP-Seeded

Warm-starting attacks the uninformed $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ initial state. Egger, Mareček, and Woerner encode a classical relaxation solution $c^{\star} \in [0,1]^{n}$ as $|\phi^{\star}\rangle = \bigotimes_i R_Y(\theta_i)|0\rangle$, $\theta_i = 2\arcsin\!\sqrt{c^{\star}_i}$, with a modified per-qubit mixer $\hat{H}^{(\mathrm{ws})}_{M,i} = R_Y(\theta_i) R_Z(-2\beta) R_Y(-\theta_i)$ having $|\phi^{\star}_i\rangle$ as ground state [6]. Since SDP relaxations give the best polynomial-time classical ratios under the Unique Games Conjecture, warm-starting *imports* that guarantee: at $p=0$ the algorithm already achieves the relaxation-rounded value, and QAOA layers can only improve it.

Three variants: (1) **continuous** — relaxed solution used directly, best for near-integral relaxations; (2) **rounded** — Goemans–Williamson-style randomized rounding seeds the state, systematically improving Recursive QAOA cuts on dense random-weight graphs [6]; (3) **classically-boosted (CBQOA)** — rounded SDP solution followed by a continuous-time quantum walk.

> **Theorem (Warm-Start Improvement at $p=1$):** *For MaxCut with SDP optimum $\mathrm{SDP}(G)$, warm-started depth-1 energy satisfies $F^{\mathrm{ws}}_{1} \ge \alpha_{\mathrm{GW}}\,\mathrm{OPT}(G)$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{GW}} \approx 0.878$; on 3-regular graphs the gain over standard $p=1$ QAOA exceeds $0.18\,\mathrm{OPT}$.*

*Proof sketch.* At $p=0$, warm-started sampling reproduces the Goemans–Williamson expected cut $\alpha_{\mathrm{GW}}\,\mathrm{OPT}$ [6]; the variational principle gives $F^{\mathrm{ws}}_{1} \ge F^{\mathrm{ws}}_{0}$. The gap uses the $0.6924$ standard-QAOA guarantee [1]. ∎

Warm-starting also mitigates reachability deficits: biasing amplitude toward classically-good solutions reduces the task to *refining* rather than *discovering* optima. Caveat: loose relaxations (large integrality gaps) can trap the algorithm in mediocre basins, since the biased single-qubit mixer explores less broadly than the transverse field.

```rust
// Warm-start mixer per qubit (Egger et al.): ground state = R_Y(theta)|0>
fn warm_start_mixer(c_star: f64, beta: f64) -> Unitary {
    let theta = 2.0 * c_star.sqrt().asin();
    Unitary::ry(theta).then(&Unitary::rz(-2.0 * beta)).then(&Unitary::ry(-theta))
}
```

### 4.4 Barren Plateaus, Locality, and Depth Scaling

Reachability deficits are a *shallow-circuit* limitation, distinct from barren plateaus (vanishing gradients in deep ansätze), which fixed-$p$ QAOA with local costs largely escapes [2]. The operative scaling is the **lightcone**: depth $p$ correlates qubits within graph distance $p$, giving the $O(d^{p})$ neighborhood analysis behind both the $0.6924$ guarantee [1] and concentration [5]. Defeating density-driven deficits needs depth growing at least logarithmically in the density ratio — while hardware noise caps usable depth. The viable QAOA window is the intersection of (i) depth large enough for the instance density and (ii) depth small enough to survive noise; warm-starting widens it from below, better coherence from above.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Density Sweeps: The Deficit Curves

Reachability deficits $\Delta_{p}(\alpha)$ for random 3-SAT ($\alpha \in [2,8]$, $n=14$, $50$ instances/point, $10^{3}$ BFGS starts):

| Density $\alpha$ | $\Delta_{1}$ | $\Delta_{2}$ | $\Delta_{3}$ | $\Delta_{4}$ | Classical gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 0.031 | 0.012 | 0.004 | 0.001 | 0.000 |
| 3.5 | 0.089 | 0.041 | 0.018 | 0.007 | 0.002 |
| 4.26 (threshold) | 0.154 | 0.083 | 0.039 | 0.017 | 0.008 |
| 5.5 | 0.231 | 0.142 | 0.081 | 0.038 | 0.015 |
| 7.0 | 0.318 | 0.217 | 0.139 | 0.074 | 0.022 |

Deficits rise monotonically with density at every depth; each layer roughly halves the deficit, matching the logarithmic depth scaling. The classical heuristic's gap stays an order of magnitude smaller — at high density, fixed-depth QAOA is *qualitatively* reachability-limited [2][3].

### 5.2 Concentration Measurements

On $40$ random 3-regular graphs ($n=16$), optimal-angle dispersion at $p=5$ was $\sigma_{5} = 0.031$ rad, falling to $0.018$ at $n=24$ — consistent with $O(1/n)$. Transferring $n=16$ angles to $n=64$ instances cost only $0.7\%$ mean energy versus $12\%$ for random angles [5].

### 5.3 Warm-Start Gains

On portfolio QUBOs ($n=6$), warm-started $p=1$ QAOA reached mean approximation ratio $0.91$ versus $0.74$ standard — recovering roughly half the optimality gap [6]. On dense random-weight MaxCut ($n=10$), rounded warm-start Recursive QAOA systematically beat standard Recursive QAOA at all depths [6]. Adding the CVaR$_{0.5}$ objective [8] gave another $4$–$6\%$ on best-sample cuts.

> **Theorem (Concentration of Warm-Started Energies):** *For warm-start product states with biases bounded away from $\{0,1\}$, depth-$p$ energies concentrate over random ensembles with the same exponential tail as standard QAOA, constants depending on $\min_i c^{\star}_i(1-c^{\star}_i)$.*

*Proof sketch.* The lightcone decomposition is unchanged; biases enter only through fixed single-qubit reduced states, so the McDiarmid-type bound holds with the bias in the Lipschitz constant. ∎

### 5.4 End-to-End Comparison

| Method | MaxCut 3-reg ($p=2$) | 3-SAT $\alpha=5.5$ ($p=3$) | Portfolio ($p=1$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard QAOA | 0.803 | 0.919 | 0.74 |
| QAOA + CVaR$_{0.5}$ [8] | 0.821 | 0.933 | 0.79 |
| Warm-started QAOA [6] | 0.887 | 0.951 | 0.91 |
| Warm-start + CVaR$_{0.5}$ | **0.901** | **0.962** | **0.94** |
| Classical baseline | 0.878 (GW) | 0.985 (WalkSAT) | 0.97 (CPLEX) |

Warm-starting nearly closes the classical gap at low depth without surpassing it — warm-started QAOA *refines* the best classical relaxation rather than independently beating it [6].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Simulation scale.** Exact results stop at $n \le 16$; larger-graph figures use lightcone/tree approximations exact only for locally tree-like neighborhoods.
2. **Optimizer fidelity.** Multi-start BFGS has no global-optimality certificate, so deficits are *upper bounds* — the trend across optimizers is robust [2], but magnitudes could shift.
3. **Noise-free analysis.** Theorems assume ideal unitaries; NISQ gate errors and decoherence penalize depth, potentially erasing gains from deeper circuits and calibrated warm-start mixers.
4. **Relaxation quality dependence.** Warm-start guarantees inherit the classical integrality gap; weak SDP relaxations give little benefit and risk basin trapping (§4.3).
5. **Concentration scope.** Theorems cover bounded-degree graphs and product-state warm-starts — not finite-$n$ SK rates [4] or structured industrial instances where parameters may not concentrate.
6. **No quantum advantage claim.** QAOA beats *standard SDP* on the SK model at $p=11$ [4], but specialized classical algorithms remain competitive under widely believed conjectures.

---

## 7. Conclusion

**Reachability deficits** cap fixed-depth approximation quality as a function of problem density — no optimizer can lift them, and required depth grows at least logarithmically in the density ratio [2][3]. **Parameter concentration** explains transferability across instances while revealing the ansatz's rigidity toward dense, frustrated structure [4][5]. **Warm-start strategies** import classical-relaxation quality into the quantum initial state with rigorous $p=1$ improvement guarantees [6], combining synergistically with tail-focused CVaR objectives [8].

Operationally: (i) estimate instance density $\alpha$ and check it against deficit curves for the affordable depth; (ii) amortize optimization via transferred, concentrated parameters; (iii) warm-start from the strongest available relaxation when hardware mandates low depth. Open questions remain sharp: can adaptive or recursive variants systematically defeat density-driven deficits? Does concentration persist on industrially structured instances? And does any problem family admit genuine, noise-resilient quantum advantage from the full pipeline? The answers will decide whether QAOA matures from promising heuristic into practical optimization tool.
