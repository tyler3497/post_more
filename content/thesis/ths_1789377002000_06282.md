# Categorical Deep Learning: Functors, String Diagrams, and Geometric Priors

## Abstract

Deep learning architectures are traditionally presented as sequences of tensor operations tuned by gradient descent, yet beneath this operational view lies a rich compositional structure. This article develops the categorical foundations of deep learning, in which neural networks are morphisms in a symmetric monoidal category, training is a monoidal functor, and architectural design is constrained by geometric priors. We trace the progression from Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras' treatment of backpropagation as a functor between categories of parametrised functions and learners, through the lens- and optic-theoretic reformulations of gradient-based learning, to Gavranović's programme of parametric weighted optics and the categorical deep learning framework that treats every architecture as an algebra over a monadic signature. String diagrams supply the graphical syntax for composing models, while Bronstein's geometric deep learning blueprint shows how symmetry groups, domains, and equivariance constraints organise the space of architectures into grids, groups, graphs, geodesics, and gauges. We establish the functorial compositionality of gradient descent, show how equivariance linearises architecture search, and assess the programme's limits.

---

## 1 Introduction

A supervised learning algorithm searches over functions *A → B* parametrised by *P* to approximate an ideal *f : A → B* [1], updating parameters on examples *(a, f(a))*. This conceals a structural fact: the update rule **composes** — two learners trained end to end behave as the composite of their learning dynamics, and the chain rule is the infinitesimal shadow of a general compositionality.

Category theory is the mathematics of composition. A category consists of *objects* and *morphisms* between them, with an associative composition law and identity morphisms [4]. Functors are structure-preserving maps between categories, and monoidal categories add a parallel composition operator ⊗ alongside the sequential one. The thesis of categorical deep learning is that neural networks were categories all along: layers are morphisms, architectures are composite morphisms drawn as string diagrams, and backpropagation is not a trick of tensor calculus but a **functor** from a category of parametrised functions to a category of learners [1].

This programme has matured through three converging strands:

1. **Functorial learning.** Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras showed gradient descent defines a monoidal functor *Para(Euc) → Learn* [1], generalised by Cruttwell et al. to parametrised lenses and reverse derivative categories [2].
2. **Diagrammatic syntax.** String diagrams — the graphical calculus of monoidal categories — separate a model's *wiring* (syntax) from its *behaviour* (semantics). A compositional model pairs a diagram *D : I → O* in a syntax category with a monoidal representation functor *⟦·⟧ : S → C* assigning semantic content to each component [4].
3. **Geometric priors.** Bronstein, Bruna, Cohen, and Veličković organise neural architectures by the symmetries of their domains: invariance and equivariance under group actions yield the blueprint of grids, groups, graphs, geodesics, and gauges [3].

Gavranović's synthesis treats *all* architectures as algebras over a shared monadic signature, so the categorical model compiles directly into code [5][6]. This article develops these ideas from first principles, proves the central compositionality theorems, and evaluates the programme's explanatory and practical power.

## 2 Background

### Categories, functors, and monoidal structure

A **category** C has objects, morphisms *C(A, B)* with identities and associative composition; a **functor** *F : C → D* preserves identities and composition. A **monoidal category** adds a bifunctor *⊗* with unit *I* and coherent associator/unitors; it is **symmetric** when *A ⊗ B ≅ B ⊗ A* naturally.

In symmetric monoidal categories, morphisms admit a **string diagram** calculus — wires are objects, boxes are morphisms, and planar deformation preserves meaning: topology *is* algebra [4].

### The Para construction

Learning is parametrised. Given a monoidal category C, **Para(C)** has the same objects as C, but a morphism *A → B* is a pair *(P, f)* with *f : P ⊗ A → B* in C [4]. When *C = Euc*, the category of Euclidean spaces and smooth maps, *Para(Euc)* is the native habitat of neural networks: each layer is a smooth map whose parameter space holds its weights and biases.

### Lenses, optics, and bidirectionality

A **lens** *(A, A′) → (B, B′)* pairs a forward *get* *A → B* with a backward *put* *A × B′ → A′* [2][4]. Learners are lenses in disguise: the forward pass predicts, the backward pass propagates a *request* — a desired output change — through the model. Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras' key contribution was this *request function*: the backward channel carries requested output perturbations, from which gradient descent derives as a special case [1]. Optics generalise lenses by letting the residual live in an arbitrary monoidal category; **weighted optics** add a weight to the residual, recovering the full generality neural networks need [5].

### Reverse derivative categories

Cartesian differential categories axiomatise the forward derivative; **reverse derivative categories** (Cockett et al.) axiomatise the reverse derivative combinator *R*, assigning to each *f : A → B* a map *R[f] : A × B → A* satisfying the reverse chain rule among seven axioms [2][4]. Crucially, the reverse derivative exists in discrete settings such as Boolean circuits. This liberates gradient-based learning from calculus: what matters is the *combinatorial* structure of the reverse chain rule, not differentiability.

### Geometric deep learning

Bronstein et al. attribute successful architectures to their respect for domain symmetries [3]; their blueprint decomposes architecture design into five ingredients:

| Ingredient | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetry group 𝔊 | Transformations the task is invariant or equivariant to | Translations, rotations, permutations |
| Domain Ω | Space signals live on | Grid, graph, manifold |
| Signal space X(Ω) | Functions on the domain | Images, node features |
| Equivariant layers B | Maps commuting with group action | Convolutions, message passing |
| Pooling / coarsening | Scale separation, hierarchy | Strided convolution, graph pooling |

Equivariance, *f(ρ(g)·x) = ρ′(g)·f(x)*, organises CNNs, GNNs, group- and gauge-equivariant CNNs, and Transformers into one table [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is axiomatic: we define the categorical structures, exhibit the functors, and verify that standard deep learning emerges as their interpretation in *Euc*.

**Step 1 — Model the learner.** We formalise a learner *A → B* as a 4-tuple *(P, I, U, r)*: parameter space *P*, implementation *I : P × A → B*, update *U*, and request map *r* [1]. Learners form a symmetric monoidal category **Learn** under sequential and parallel composition.

**Step 2 — Exhibit the functor.** We construct the gradient-descent functor *L(ε,e) : Para(Euc) → Learn*, whose update descends the error gradient and whose request inverts the input-gradient into a requested perturbation. Functoriality — *L(g ∘ f) = L(g) ∘ L(f)*, *L(f ⊗ g) = L(f) ⊗ L(g)* — is the mathematical content of backpropagation [1].

**Step 3 — Diagrammatic syntax.** We encode architectures as string diagrams in *Para(Smooth)*: boxes are parametrised maps, wires carry activations forward and requests backward, and composition of diagrams is composition of learners by the coherence theorem.

**Step 4 — Geometric constraints.** We restrict the syntactic category to morphisms natural with respect to the domain's symmetry groupoid [3][6], converting an infinite architecture space into a structured one.

**Step 5 — Implementation.** The framework is prescriptive: parametric weighted optics compile to differentiable programs, validated here in Haskell (categorical core) and Python (numerics) [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Backpropagation as a Monoidal Functor

The central theorem of Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras deserves a precise statement.

> **Theorem:** *Fix a step size ε > 0 and an error function e satisfying the condition that, for every x₀, the partial derivative of e with respect to its first argument at (x₀, −) is invertible as a map ℝ → ℝ. Then gradient descent defines a strong symmetric monoidal functor L(ε,e) : Para(Euc) → Learn.*

*Proof sketch.* The non-trivial content is composition: given *I : P × A → B* and *J : Q × B → C*, the update of the composite must equal the composite of the updates, with the *request* *r_J(q, b, c)* supplying the training signal for the first learner. The *P*-component of the composite gradient factors through the Jacobian of *J*; the invertibility condition on *e* lets the request map convert the output-space gradient into a legitimate request. Monoidality follows because parallel composition yields block-diagonal Jacobians, so updates decouple across ⊗ [1]. ∎

The *request function* is thus the real innovation — gradients are merely one convenient encoding of requests.

In Haskell, the learner interface is direct:

```haskell
-- A learner: parameters p, inputs a, outputs b
data Learner p a b = Learner
  { implement :: p -> a -> b
  , update    :: p -> a -> b -> p
  , request   :: p -> a -> b -> a
  }

-- Sequential composition: requests flow backwards
compose :: Learner p a b -> Learner q b c -> Learner (q, p) a c
compose (Learner i1 u1 r1) (Learner i2 u2 r2) = Learner
  { implement = \(q, p) a -> i2 q (i1 p a)
  , update    = \(q, p) a c ->
      let b  = i1 p a
          b' = r2 q b c
      in (u2 q b c, u1 p a b')
  , request   = \(q, p) a c -> r1 p a (r2 q (i1 p a) c)
  }
```

Functoriality guarantees this coincides with backpropagation through any composite network — no per-architecture proof needed.

### 4.2 String Diagrams: Syntax Separated from Semantics

String diagrams are the *syntax* of monoidal categories [4]: data flows left to right along wires, parameters enter boxes from below, and the backward pass is the same diagram read right to left with requests on the wires. The network's compositional structure becomes visually checkable.

A compositional model *M = (D, S, C, ⟦·⟧)* separates concerns [4]:

- **Syntax** *S*: the diagram category — which boxes exist and how wires connect.
- **Diagram** *D : I → O*: this model's wiring.
- **Semantics** *C*: where behaviour lives (smooth maps, Boolean circuits, stochastic kernels).
- **Representation** *⟦·⟧ : S → C*: the interpreting monoidal functor.

Changing *C* from *Euc* to the category of Boolean circuits reinterprets the *same diagram* as a discrete learner — the reverse derivative exists there too [2]. Changing the representation functor from forward evaluation to interval arithmetic yields a verified-robustness analysis of the same architecture. The diagram is written once; functors supply meanings.

Cups and caps — the duality structure of compact closed categories — give the backward sweep a diagrammatic meaning: a cup *ε : A\* ⊗ A → I* contracts a request against an activation, making backpropagation the diagrammatic *transpose* induced by duals. Attention's query-key-value wiring is naturally a cup contracting queries against keys, followed by value aggregation.

### 4.3 Parametric Weighted Optics and the 2-Categorical View

Gavranović's programme unifies parametrisation and bidirectionality [5]: an **actegory** is a category with an action *• : M × C → C* of a monoidal category (Para is the case where the action is ⊗), and a **weighted optic** *(S, S′) → (A, A′)* pairs a residual object and weight with forward *S → M • A* and backward *M • A′ → S′* maps.

The composite construction — **parametric weighted optics** — models a neural network layer exactly: parameters are the actegory action, the forward pass is the *get*, the backward pass is the *put* carrying the weight (the cached activations needed for the reverse derivative). Crucially, this lives one categorical level up: parametric maps form a **bicategory** (2-category), where

- 0-cells are spaces (data types),
- 1-cells are parametric maps *(P, f) : A → B*,
- 2-cells are *reparametrisations*: maps between parameter spaces commuting with the implementations.

Two networks differing only by reparametrisation (say, permuting hidden units) are related by an invertible 2-cell — the bicategorical structure *proves* the permutation symmetry of parameter spaces. Optimisers become 2-cell dynamics on parameter spaces: Gavranović formalises momentum and Adam-style adaptivity as morphisms in an appropriate 2-category [5]. Every architecture is then an algebra for a monad whose operations are the layer combinators and whose laws are the coherence conditions of stacking.

This yields the prescriptive payoff: the categorical description *is* the implementation. The diagram compiles; there is no gap between the mathematics and the code.

### 4.4 Geometric Priors as Equivariance Constraints

Where category theory supplies *composition*, geometry supplies *constraint*: Bronstein's blueprint [3] beats the curse of dimensionality by exploiting the low-dimensional, highly symmetric structure of the physical world.

Formally, let 𝔊 be a group acting on the domain Ω, inducing an action on signals *x ∈ X(Ω)*. A layer *f : X(Ω) → X′(Ω)* is **equivariant** if *f(ρ(g)·x) = ρ′(g)·f(x)* for all *g ∈ 𝔊*, and **invariant** if *ρ′* is trivial. The five geometric domains and their characteristic architectures are:

1. **Grids** — Ω a discrete lattice, 𝔊 translations. Translation-equivariant linear maps are exactly convolutions. CNNs emerge *inevitably*.
2. **Groups** — Ω = 𝔊 itself (homogeneous spaces). Group convolutions integrate over the group, giving equivariance to rotations and roto-translations [3].
3. **Graphs** — 𝔊 the symmetric group permuting nodes. Permutation-equivariant layers are message-passing networks; invariant readouts give graph-level predictions. GNNs, Deep Sets, and Transformers sit on one spectrum [3].
4. **Geodesics** — Ω a Riemannian manifold. Equivariance upgrades to *isometry* equivariance; intrinsic operations (Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions, parallel transport) replace extrinsic coordinates.
5. **Gauges** — fields with local frame choices; layers must be *gauge equivariant*, commuting with position-dependent changes of frame. Gauge CNNs and lattice gauge-equivariant networks for physics are the frontier [3].

Categorically, equivariance is *naturality*: a layer is a natural transformation between functors from the one-object groupoid of 𝔊 into vector spaces. The blueprint's table is thus a classification of natural transformations — the programme searches for the right functor category before the right network.

The practical consequence is a dramatic reduction of architecture search: one searches over 𝔊-equivariant morphisms — a linear subspace of the parameter space, often orders of magnitude smaller — and sample complexity improves because the hypothesis class already encodes the task's symmetries [3].

### 4.5 Synthesis: The Categorical Deep Learning Framework

Gavranović et al. combine these strands into *categorical deep learning*: an algebraic theory of architectures [6]. The claim is striking: **every** neural architecture — dense, convolutional, recurrent, attentional, message-passing — is an algebra over a shared monadic signature in the bicategory of parametric maps. Architectures differ in *algebra structure*, not in kind.

Syntax, semantics, and learning are the three layers: a free monad of diagrams, algebras interpreting the signature, and the backprop functor lifting everything to learners with optimiser 2-cells [5][6].

Geometric priors enter as a *restriction of the signature*: only 𝔊-natural operations are admitted as generators.

Pythonic pseudocode illustrates the prescriptive compilation — a parametrised map as a first-class object:

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Callable
import torch

@dataclass
class ParaMap:
    """A 1-cell in Para(Smooth): params P, map P x A -> B."""
    params: torch.Tensor          # object P
    forward: Callable            # f : P x A -> B

    def then(self, other: "ParaMap") -> "ParaMap":
        # sequential composition threads parameters through
        p, q = self.params, other.params
        nq = q.numel()
        return ParaMap(
            params=torch.cat([q.flatten(), p.flatten()]),
            forward=lambda pq, a: other.forward(
                pq[:nq], self.forward(pq[nq:], a)),
        )

    def tensor(self, other: "ParaMap") -> "ParaMap":
        # parallel composition: independent parameter blocks
        np = self.params.numel()
        return ParaMap(
            params=torch.cat([self.params.flatten(),
                              other.params.flatten()]),
            forward=lambda pq, ab: (
                self.forward(pq[:np], ab[0]),
                other.forward(pq[np:], ab[1]),
            ),
        )
```

The reverse derivative — hence the learner — is derived *uniformly* for any composite of `then` and `tensor`: new architectures are new diagrams, and backpropagation comes for free.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### Theorem: compositionality of gradient descent

We now state the compositionality result in the generality of Cruttwell et al. [2]:

> **Theorem:** *Let C be a reverse derivative category. Then there is a functor Para(C) → Para(Lens(C)) sending each parametrised map to a parametrised lens whose backward pass is the reverse derivative. Gradient-based supervised learning is therefore compositional in any reverse derivative category.*

The proof replaces the analytic chain rule with the reverse chain rule axiom of reverse derivative categories. Since the category of Boolean circuits is a reverse derivative category [2], the theorem *proves* backpropagation-style learning applies to discrete circuits — a prediction no calculus-based account could make, confirmed by Wilson and Zanasi's reverse derivative ascent [4].

### Equivariance linearises architecture search

Consider image classification with translation symmetry. The space of linear maps on *n × n* images has dimension *n⁴*; the subspace of translation-equivariant maps (convolutions with *k × k* kernels) has dimension *k²*. For *n = 256, k = 3*, this cuts roughly four billion parameters to nine — a compression factor exceeding 10⁸, lossless for the task because any optimal equivariant solution lies in the natural-transformation subspace [3].

### String diagrams catch wiring errors

In the compositional-model view, a type error in a diagram — a wire of type *A* plugged into a box expecting *B* — is a *categorical* impossibility, caught at compile time in typed implementations of parametric optics [5]. This eliminates an entire class of wiring bugs that tensor frameworks surface only as cryptic runtime errors.

### Learners' languages and dynamical systems

Spivak showed that *Learn ≅ Para(SLens)* and that simple lenses embed into *Poly*, the category of polynomial functors [7]. A map in *Para(SLens)* then interprets as a generalised Moore machine — a *dynamical system* — and gradient descent becomes a proposition in the internal logic of the topos of dynamical systems [7], opening learning to coalgebraic methods such as bisimulation-based equivalence of learners.

## 6 Limitations

The categorical programme is powerful but incomplete. We enumerate its principal gaps honestly.

**Stochasticity.** Most categorical treatments model deterministic maps; real training is stochastic (minibatch sampling, dropout, stochastic depth). Markov categories supply the categorical probability theory, surveyed for ML by Shiebler et al. [4], but a *stochastic* Learn category with compositional reparameterisation gradients remains technically demanding.

**Generalisation.** Functoriality guarantees training composes but says nothing about generalisation: double descent, grokking, and scaling laws live in the *statistics* of the representation functor's image, not the syntax. The geometric blueprint addresses sample complexity through symmetry [3], but no categorical account of these phenomena exists yet.

**Expressivity vs. tractability.** The bicategorical and optic machinery is heavyweight. Parametric weighted optics require fluency in enriched category theory that few practitioners possess; the prescriptive compilation [5] is elegant but its performance engineering (kernel fusion, memory planning) lags far behind mature tensor compilers. Elegant syntax does not automatically yield fast kernels.

**The invertibility condition.** The original functor requires the error function's partial derivative to be invertible [1]; common losses satisfy this only with a careful request map. Later lens-based treatments [2] relax this at the cost of additional structure.

**Empirical validation gap.** The framework *predicts* compositional training for any parametric optic — verified for Boolean circuits [2][4] — but large-scale comparisons of categorically-designed architectures against hand-tuned baselines are scarce. The theory is ahead of the benchmarks.

## 7 Conclusion

Categorical deep learning reframes the field's central objects: networks are morphisms, architectures are string diagrams, training is a functor, and geometric priors are naturality constraints. The progression from "backprop as functor" [1] through reverse derivative categories and parametrised lenses [2], the survey unifying gradient-based, probabilistic, and equivariant learning [4], Gavranović's prescriptive foundations [5], the algebraic theory of all architectures [6], the topos-theoretic view of learners as dynamical systems [7], and Bronstein's geometric blueprint [3] constitutes a genuine research programme — mathematical without being merely formal, since every construction compiles to code.

The deepest lesson is methodological: the architecture space was never unstructured — we lacked the language to see its composition laws. String diagrams make composition visible; functors make learning compositional; equivariance makes the search tractable. Whether this language designs the next breakthrough architecture or remains a beautiful redescription depends on closing the empirical gap. But the direction is clear: the future of deep learning theory is *compositional*, and composition is the native subject of category theory.
