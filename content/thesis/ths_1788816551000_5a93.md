# The Evoformer at the Heart of AlphaFold2: Row–Column Attention over MSA Embeddings, Triangle Consistency Updates, and Invariant Point Attention in SE(3)

## Abstract

AlphaFold2 [1] reframed protein structure prediction as an end-to-end differentiable geometric inference problem and solved it with an architectural innovation—the *Evoformer*—coupled to a symmetry-respecting structure module. This thesis dissects the Evoformer's dual representation learning: row-wise gated self-attention over multiple sequence alignment (MSA) embeddings biased by a learned residue-pair representation, column-wise gated self-attention exchanging information across homologues, and an outer-product-mean bridge that funnels evolutionary covariation into the pair stack. We analyze the pair stack's triangle consistency machinery—triangular multiplicative updates and triangular self-attention—which enforces transitive, metric-compatible reasoning over residue triples and encodes the Euclidean triangle inequality as an inductive bias. We then develop the Structure Module's Invariant Point Attention (IPA), an attention operator over SE(3) backbone frames whose affinities are provably invariant to global rigid transformations, enabling equivariant frame updates and direct torsion-angle readout of all heavy atoms. We present CASP14 empirical results (median GDT_TS 92.4, median backbone RMSD 0.96 Å), formal invariance theorems with proof sketches, and a candid account of limitations including cubic complexity, single-conformer bias, and MSA-depth dependence.

## 1 Introduction

For five decades, protein structure prediction—the mapping from a one-dimensional amino-acid sequence to three-dimensional atomic coordinates—stood as computational biology's grand challenge [2]. The biological stakes are immense: structure determines function, and function determines therapeutic intervention. Yet the search space is astronomical: Levinthal's paradox notes that a 100-residue chain possesses roughly $10^{300}$ plausible conformations, while folding occurs in milliseconds *in vivo*.

Classical pipelines decomposed the problem into stages: build a **multiple sequence alignment (MSA)** of evolutionary homologues, extract pairwise co-evolution signals (residue contacts), and fold through fragment assembly or distance-geometry optimization. AlphaFold1 already exploited this pipeline with learned distance distributions [3]. AlphaFold2, introduced by Jumper *et al.* [1], demolished the staged architecture entirely. Its core principle:

> **The key principle of the building block of the network, named Evoformer, is to view protein structure prediction as a graph inference problem in 3-D space where the edges of the graph are defined by residues in proximity** [1].

Three conceptual stages organize the system:

1. **Input embedding** — construction of an MSA representation $m_{si}$ of shape $N_\text{seq} \times N_\text{res} \times c_m$ and a **pair representation** $z_{ij}$ of shape $N_\text{res} \times N_\text{res} \times c_z$, encoding everything known about residue *pairs*;
2. **The Evoformer** — a stack of 48 unshared blocks that iteratively refine *both* tensors, allowing evolutionary information and geometric reasoning to co-evolve;
3. **The Structure Module** — 8 weight-shared layers that decode an explicit 3D structure using **Invariant Point Attention (IPA)** over rigid residue frames in SE(3).

A **recycling** loop wraps the entire network: its own outputs are fed back as inputs for roughly four total passes, enabling iterative refinement at both training and inference time [4].

This thesis provides a rigorous, component-level account of the Evoformer and IPA. We derive the attention operators, analyze their computational complexity and symmetry properties, present the empirical record, prove key invariance theorems, and close with the method's fundamental limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Co-evolution and the geometry of contact

The foundational biological insight is **co-evolution**: residues that are physically adjacent in the folded structure exert selective pressure on each other, so mutations at one position are statistically compensated by mutations at the other across evolutionary time. Given an MSA of homologous sequences, columns that *covary* therefore carry noisy evidence about spatial contacts. Classical methods (direct coupling analysis, Potts models) formalized this as inverse statistical mechanics over the alignment; AlphaFold2 instead learns the mapping with attention.

### 2.2 From contact maps to end-to-end frames

AlphaFold1 predicted *distograms*—distributions over inter-residue distances—and minimized a learned potential [3]. AlphaFold2 predicts the structure directly. Each residue $i$ is assigned a rigid **backbone frame** $T_i = (R_i, t_i) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, constructed from its Cα position and the N–Cα / C–Cα vectors via Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization. The network predicts frame updates and backbone/side-chain torsion angles, from which all heavy-atom coordinates are reconstructed differentiably.

### 2.3 Symmetry: invariance versus equivariance

A protein's physics is independent of the laboratory coordinate system. Two distinct symmetry demands arise:

- **Invariance** — quantities like interatomic distances, attention weights, and the loss must not change when the whole system is rigidly transformed;
- **Equivariance** — predicted coordinates must *track* rigid transformations: rotating the input must rotate the output identically.

Most prior geometric networks achieved these properties through handcrafted invariant features (distance matrices) or expensive irreducible-representation machinery (SE(3)-Transformers [5]). AlphaFold2's IPA achieves invariance *by construction* with a lean, computationally tractable operator—one of the paper's deepest contributions.

| Property | Implementation in AF2 | Symmetry class |
|---|---|---|
| Pair representation $z_{ij}$ | Abstract, no coordinates | Invariant |
| MSA representation $m_{si}$ | Sequence-domain attention | Invariant |
| IPA attention weights | Point distances in local frames | Invariant |
| Backbone frames $T_i$ | Updated residually | Equivariant |
| FAPE loss | Frame-aligned, clamped | Invariant |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Representation shapes and initialization

The network consumes a target sequence of $N_\text{res}$ residues plus an MSA of $N_\text{seq}$ aligned sequences (clustered, with an additional extra-MSA stack for memory efficiency). Dimensions used in the original system:

- MSA representation: $m_{si} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_m}$, $c_m = 256$, over $N_\text{seq} \times N_\text{res}$;
- Pair representation: $z_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_z}$, $c_z = 128$, over $N_\text{res} \times N_\text{res}$, initialized from target-sequence features, residue relative-position encodings, and template features;
- Single representation (structure module): $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$, extracted from the first MSA row plus the pair diagonal.

### 3.2 The Evoformer block: two stacks, one conversation

Each of the 48 Evoformer blocks executes a fixed sequence of sub-operations. Conceptually, the block maintains a **conversation** between the MSA stack (evolutionary information) and the pair stack (geometric information):

**MSA stack** (all operations act on $m_{si}$):

1. **Row-wise gated self-attention** — for each sequence row $s$, attend over residue positions $i$. Attention logits receive an additive *bias* projected from the pair representation $z_{ij}$, so geometric beliefs steer evolutionary attention.
2. **Column-wise gated self-attention** — for each residue column $i$, attend over sequences $s$. This lets the network decide *which homologues are informative*, down-weighting redundant or distant sequences. Gating is computed from $m_{si}$ itself.
3. **MSA transition** — a two-layer feed-forward (GeLU) update per position.

**Bridge**:
4. **Outer product mean** — information flows MSA → pair. For each pair $(i,j)$, compute an average over sequences of an outer product of linear projections of $m_{si}$ and $m_{sj}$:
$$z_{ij} \mathrel{+}= \mathrm{Linear}\left(\frac{1}{N_\text{seq}}\sum_s \mathrm{flatten}\big(a_{si} \otimes b_{sj}\big)\right).$$
The outer product is deliberate: co-evolutionary *covariation* between positions $i$ and $j$ is, at its algebraic core, a second-order statistic, and the outer product is its natural neural encoding.

**Pair stack** (all operations act on $z_{ij}$):
5. Triangular multiplicative updates (outgoing, incoming);
6. Triangular self-attention (around starting node, around ending node);
7. Pair transition.

![Figure 1 — Evoformer dual-stack architecture overview](ths_1788816551000_5a93-0.webp)

### 3.3 The Structure Module

The structure module consumes the refined single representation $s_i$ and pair representation $z_{ij}$, initializes backbone frames $T_i$ at the origin (the "black hole" initialization), and applies 8 shared-weight layers. Each layer performs IPA over frames, updates $s_i$, predicts residual frame updates, and predicts torsion angles $\alpha$ that place all heavy atoms. A final loss and recycling complete the picture.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Row–column attention over MSA embeddings

The MSA tensor is two-dimensional (sequence × residue), and the Evoformer exploits this structure with *axial* attention—attention factored along each axis rather than flattened self-attention over $N_\text{seq} \cdot N_\text{res}$ tokens, which would be computationally ruinous.

**Row-wise gated self-attention with pair bias.** Fixing a sequence row $s$, queries, keys, and values derive from $m_{si}$ across residues $i$, with $h$ heads:
$$a_{sij}^h = \mathrm{softmax}_j\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\, q_{si}^{h\top} k_{sj}^h + b_{ij}^h\right), \qquad o_{si}^h = g_{si}^h \sum_j a_{sij}^h v_{sj}^h,$$
where $b_{ij}^h = \mathrm{Linear}(z_{ij})$ injects pair information and $g_{si}^h = \sigma(\mathrm{Linear}(m_{si}))$ is a learned gate. The pair bias is the crucial cross-talk: as the pair stack's geometric beliefs sharpen, they redirect *which residues* each sequence attends to.

**Column-wise gated self-attention.** Fixing residue column $i$, attention runs over sequences $s$ with no pair bias but with the same gating mechanism. Heuristically, the model *learns which aligned sequences are informative* for the target structure [6]—effectively performing learned, position-specific sequence weighting, a neural generalization of the sequence-reweighting heuristics of classical co-evolution analysis.

> **Theorem: Axial factorization preserves pairwise expressivity.** Row-plus-column attention over the $N_\text{seq} \times N_\text{res}$ grid can represent any interaction factorizable as a sum of a row term and a column term, and the pair-biased row attention additionally injects arbitrary $z_{ij}$-dependent modulation, so no pure row-residue interaction expressible in the flattened model is lost by the factorization.

*Proof sketch.* Flattened attention logits $\ell_{(s,i),(t,j)}$ decompose as $\ell^r_{s,i,j} + \ell^c_{i,s,t}$ up to the bias term; the row operator covers all $(i,j)$ dependence for fixed $s$, the column operator all $(s,t)$ dependence for fixed $i$, and $b_{ij}$ is a free function of the pair tensor. ∎

### 4.2 Triangle consistency updates

The pair representation $z_{ij}$ aspires to encode *geometry*: quantities like distances between residues $i$ and $j$. But Euclidean distances obey the **triangle inequality**—$d(i,j) \le d(i,k) + d(k,j)$—and a learned $z_{ij}$ updated independently per pair has no reason to respect it. The Evoformer's signature innovation is a set of operators that reason explicitly over *triangles* of residues $(i, j, k)$, enforcing transitive consistency.

**Triangular multiplicative update (outgoing).** Each edge $(i,j)$ aggregates information from the two adjacent edges of every triangle:
$$z_{ij} \mathrel{+}= g_{ij} \odot \sum_k a_{ik} \odot b_{jk},$$
where $a_{ik}, b_{jk}$ are linear projections of $z_{ik}, z_{jk}$ (followed by sigmoid gating $g_{ij}$), and $\odot$ is elementwise multiplication. The **incoming** variant symmetrically uses $a_{ki} \odot b_{kj}$. Note the deliberate economy: the dot product of attention is replaced by elementwise multiplication, yielding an $O(N_\text{res}^3 \cdot c_z)$ operation without the softmax overhead [7].

**Triangular self-attention.** Two symmetric attention variants complete the picture. Around the *starting* node: queries from $z_{ij}$, keys/values from $z_{ik}$, with a bias $b_{jk}$ derived from the third edge:
$$a_{ijk} = \mathrm{softmax}_k\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\, q_{ij}^\top k_{ik} + b_{jk}\right), \qquad o_{ij} = \sum_k a_{ijk}\, v_{ik}.$$
Around the *ending* node, keys/values come from $z_{ki}$ with bias $b_{ki}$. The third edge thus acts as a learned "triangle prior" on the attention distribution [7].

> **Theorem: Triangle updates propagate metric constraints.** If the pair representation encodes an upper bound on inter-residue distance, one outgoing multiplicative update with nonnegatively-gated projections implements a differentiable relaxation of the triangle-inequality closure $d_{ij} \leftarrow \min_k (d_{ik} + d_{kj})$, so repeated updates drive $z$ toward a metrically consistent fixed point.

*Proof sketch.* With $a_{ik}, b_{jk}$ encoding distances and $g_{ij}$ a soft minimum-selector over $k$, the sum $\sum_k a_{ik} \odot b_{jk}$ approximates $\min_k$ under the softmax-like concentration induced by training; the residual update with layer normalization implements the relaxation step. Iterating across 48 blocks yields the closure. ∎

The computational price is steep: triangle operations dominate the $O(N_\text{res}^3)$ time and memory footprint, which is why practical reimplementations (e.g., OpenFold) invest heavily in chunking and kernel fusion, and why chains beyond a few thousand residues remain challenging [4].

### 4.3 Invariant Point Attention in SE(3)

The structure module must reason in 3D while respecting Euclidean symmetry. IPA achieves this with an elegant construction. For each residue $i$ with backbone frame $T_i = (R_i, t_i)$, and for each of $N_\text{query}$ point queries $\vec{q}_i^p$, keys $\vec{k}_j^p$, values $\vec{v}_j^p$ expressed in *local* frame coordinates, the attention logits combine a standard scalar term with a geometric term:
$$a_{ij} = \mathrm{softmax}_j\left( w_L\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\, q_i^\top k_j + b_{ij} - \frac{\gamma^h w_C}{2}\sum_p \big\|T_i \circ \vec{q}_i^p - T_j \circ \vec{k}_j^p\big\|^2\right)\right),$$
where $T_i \circ \vec{q} = R_i \vec{q} + t_i$ maps local points into the global frame. Attention is thus modulated by the *squared 3D distance between query and key points*, evaluated in a common coordinate system—yet invariant, as we now prove.

> **Theorem: IPA affinities are invariant under global rigid transformations.** For any $T_\text{global} \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, the attention weights $a_{ij}$ computed from transformed frames $T_\text{global} \circ T_i$ equal those computed from $T_i$.

*Proof.* The squared-distance term transforms as
$$\big\|T_\text{global} \circ (T_i \circ \vec{q}_i^p) - T_\text{global} \circ (T_j \circ \vec{k}_j^p)\big\|^2 = \big\|T_i \circ \vec{q}_i^p - T_j \circ \vec{k}_j^p\big\|^2,$$
because a rigid transformation preserves Euclidean distances. The scalar term $q_i^\top k_j$ and bias $b_{ij}$ contain no coordinates. Hence every logit, and therefore every weight $a_{ij}$, is unchanged. ∎

The attention *output* then updates the single representation and predicts frame residuals, so frames evolve equivariantly while all learning signals remain invariant—a clean separation that avoids both the information loss of purely invariant features and the cost of irreducible-representation networks [2][5].

```python
# Invariant Point Attention (simplified, single head)
def ipa(s, z, T, gamma=1.0):
    # s: [N_res, c_s] single repr; z: [N_res, N_res, c_z] pair repr
    # T: [N_res, 4, 4] backbone frames in SE(3)
    q, k, v = linear_q(s), linear_k(s), linear_v(s)          # scalar attention
    qp, kp, vp = linear_qp(s), linear_kp(s), linear_vp(s)     # 3D points, local frames
    bias = linear_b(z)                                       # pair bias
    # map local points into the global frame, then squared distances
    Q = apply_frame(T[:, None], qp[:, None, :])              # [N, N, P, 3]
    K = apply_frame(T[None, :], kp[None, :, :])
    d2 = ((Q - K) ** 2).sum(-1).sum(-1)                       # [N, N]
    logits = (q @ k.T) / sqrt(c) + bias - 0.5 * gamma * d2
    a = softmax(logits, dim=-1)                              # invariant weights
    o_scalar = a @ v
    o_points = apply_frame(T, (a[..., None, None] * vp[:, None]).sum(1))
    return o_scalar, o_points
```

### 4.4 Structure readout and the FAPE loss

Each structure-module layer concludes by predicting, from the updated $s_i$: (i) a residual rigid update to $T_i$, and (ii) torsion angles $\alpha$ parameterizing backbone ($\phi, \psi, \omega$) and side-chain ($\chi$) dihedrals, from which all heavy-atom positions are placed differentiably. The principal training signal is the **Frame-Aligned Point Error (FAPE)**: for each residue frame, align the predicted frame with the true frame and compute the RMS error of all atoms under that alignment, clamped at 10 Å to limit outlier influence. Because the loss is computed *per local frame*, every residue receives a localized, well-conditioned gradient—dramatically stabilizing training relative to global-coordinate losses [1][8].

Auxiliary losses include the distogram prediction, masked-MSA reconstruction (a BERT-style objective over the alignment), and confidence heads predicting **pLDDT** (per-residue confidence, 0–100) and **pTM/PAE** (predicted TM-score and predicted aligned error matrices), which proved remarkably well-calibrated [8].

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 CASP14: the empirical record

AlphaFold2 entered CASP14 (2020) and produced a step-change the field had not witnessed in its 25-year history [1]:

| Metric | AlphaFold2 | Next-best entry |
|---|---|---|
| Median GDT_TS (87 domains) | **92.4** | ~72 |
| Median backbone RMSD (95% coverage) | **0.96 Å** | — |
| Targets with GDT_TS > 90 | majority | rare |

A GDT_TS of 92.4 with sub-angstrom RMSD is, for most practical purposes, experimental accuracy: side-chain rotamers, hydrogen-bond networks, and even zinc-binding sites were recovered without explicit ion modeling [1]. Subsequent validation at proteome scale—over 200 million predicted structures in the AlphaFold Database [8]—confirmed the method generalizes across the tree of life.

### 5.2 Formal results

We collect the chapter's formal claims:

1. **Axial expressivity** (Section 4.1): row–column factorization with pair bias loses no row-residue interaction expressible in the flattened model.
2. **Triangle closure** (Section 4.2): iterated multiplicative updates implement a differentiable relaxation of triangle-inequality closure, driving the pair representation toward metric consistency.
3. **IPA invariance** (Section 4.3): attention weights are invariant under global SE(3) transformations; frame updates are consequently equivariant.

> **Theorem: End-to-end symmetry of the structure module.** If all attention weights in the structure module are SE(3)-invariant (IPA theorem) and frame updates are predicted as functions of invariant quantities composed with current frames, then a global rigid transformation of the input frames produces the identically transformed output structure, while all losses (FAPE, distogram, pLDDT) remain unchanged.

*Proof sketch.* By induction over the 8 layers: invariant weights yield invariant single-representation updates; frame residuals predicted from invariant features compose equivariantly, $T_i \mapsto T_\text{global} \circ T_i$; torsion angles are invariant scalars. FAPE aligns frames before comparison, hence invariant. ∎

### 5.3 Ablation evidence

The original study and subsequent reimplementations confirm the architectural attributions: removing recycling degrades accuracy; replacing IPA with a non-geometric readout collapses fine stereochemistry; and the triangle operations are load-bearing for long-range contact consistency—models without them show degraded performance on multi-domain targets where transitive geometric reasoning matters most [1][4][7].

## 6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty demands we record where the architecture falls short:

- **Cubic complexity.** Triangular updates cost $O(N_\text{res}^3 \cdot c_z)$ time and $O(N_\text{res}^3)$-adjacent memory pressure, effectively capping routine predictions at a few thousand residues and motivating engineering efforts like OpenFold's chunking [4].
- **Single static conformer.** The network outputs one structure, not a conformational ensemble; intrinsically disordered regions, allosteric transitions, and folding dynamics are outside its native scope, though sampling schemes can partially recover diversity [2].
- **MSA-depth dependence.** Accuracy degrades for orphan proteins with shallow alignments; the entire Evoformer premise—extracting signal from evolutionary covariation—assumes homologues exist [8].
- **No ligands, ions, or nucleic acids** in the original formulation; AlphaFold-Multimer and AlphaFold3 later addressed complexes and general biomolecules.
- **Stereochemical imperfections.** Clashes and chirality violations occasionally survive; the network learns physics implicitly from PDB statistics rather than enforcing it, and confidence metrics (pLDDT/PAE) are less calibrated at multimer interfaces [6].
- **Interpretability.** Despite clean theorems about symmetry, *why* specific triangle updates resolve particular folds remains opaque; the model is a brilliant geometric reasoner whose reasoning traces we cannot fully read [2].

## 7 Conclusion

The Evoformer is a study in matching architecture to problem structure. Row–column attention respects the two-dimensional geometry of the MSA; the outer product mean encodes covariation in its native algebraic form; triangular updates impose the metric logic of Euclidean space; and Invariant Point Attention solves the symmetry problem with a construction of striking economy—distances in local frames, provably invariant, driving equivariant frame evolution. Together with frame-aligned losses and recycling, these ideas produced the CASP14 discontinuity and redefined what learned models can achieve in structural biology.

The deeper lesson transcends proteins: when a domain carries hard geometric structure—symmetries, inequalities, transitivity—the winning move is rarely to hope the network discovers it. It is to *build the geometry into the operator*, prove the invariance, and let learning fill in the rest. Future architectures for general biomolecular modeling, from diffusion-based successors to all-atom foundation models, inherit this blueprint.
