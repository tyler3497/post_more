# In-Memory Computing with Memristor Crossbars: ReRAM and PCM Analog Matrix-Vector Multiplication, Sneak-Path Mitigation, and Variation-Aware Weight Mapping for DNN Inference at 50 TOPS/W

## Abstract

The von Neumann memory wall has made weight data movement — not arithmetic — the dominant cost of deep neural network inference, with off-chip DRAM access consuming two orders of magnitude more energy than the multiply–accumulate operation itself. Memristor crossbar arrays offer a radical alternative: by encoding synaptic weights as device conductances and applying input activations as voltages, Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's current law evaluate an entire matrix–vector product in *O(1)* time in the analog domain. This thesis develops a complete engineering treatment of analog in-memory DNN accelerators built from resistive RAM (ReRAM) and phase-change memory (PCM): the physics of analog multiply–accumulate, bit-sliced precision management in the ISAAC, PRIME, and Newton architectures [1][2][3], sneak-path current suppression via bias schemes and 1S1R selectors [5][6][7], variation-aware conductance mapping under device stochasticity, and temporal conductance-drift compensation that preserves CIFAR-10 accuracy above 92.6% over a full day on real PCM hardware [4]. We show how co-design across device, circuit, architecture, and algorithm recovers near-digital accuracy while delivering order-of-magnitude gains in throughput and energy — with a principled roadmap to 50 TOPS/W-class efficiency.

## 1 Introduction

Deep neural network (DNN) inference is, at its core, a sequence of large matrix–vector multiplications interleaved with nonlinearities. In a von Neumann machine each weight must be fetched, multiplied, accumulated, and stored back — a *data-movement-dominated* workload. The energy hierarchy is sobering: a 32-bit ALU multiply costs roughly **1×**, while an off-chip DRAM access costs approximately **200×** [8]. For ResNet-50's 25 million parameters, a single inference moves hundreds of megabytes of weight traffic; the memory subsystem, not the ALUs, defines latency and energy.

This is the *memory wall*: processor performance has historically outpaced DRAM bandwidth and energy scaling, and DNN workloads expose the gap brutally. Digital near-memory architectures such as DaDianNao pushed computation next to eDRAM banks and still achieved only partial relief, because weights must ultimately be moved across wires to digital multipliers [1].

Memristors — two-terminal devices whose conductance is a persistent function of programming history — enable a fundamentally different proposition: **in-situ computing**. If a synaptic weight $w_{ij}$ is stored as a device conductance $G_{ij}$ at the intersection of wordline $i$ and bitline $j$, and activations $x_i$ are applied as voltages $V_i$, then each cell passes a current $I_{ij} = V_i G_{ij}$ by Ohm's law, and Kirchhoff's current law sums the currents along each bitline to yield

$$y_j = \sum_i x_i w_{ij} = \sum_i V_i G_{ij} = I_j,$$

an entire dot product computed in the physics of the array in a single step [1][9]. This thesis analyzes the full stack required to make that elegant equation an engineering reality: **analog MAC precision engineering** via bit-slicing (ISAAC, PRIME, Newton) [1][2][3], **sneak-path suppression** via bias schemes and 1S1R selectors [5][6][7], **variation-aware conductance mapping** under device stochasticity, and **temporal drift compensation** that preserves CIFAR-10 accuracy above 92.6% over a full day on real PCM hardware [4].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Memristive devices: ReRAM and PCM

Two device families dominate in-memory computing. **Filamentary ReRAM** (HfO$_x$, TaO$_x$, TiO$_2$) switches between a low-resistance state (LRS) and high-resistance state (HRS) by forming and rupturing oxygen-vacancy conductive filaments; ISAAC's 1T1R arrays favor TiO$_2$ and HfO$_x$ for their $R_{\text{ON}}/R_{\text{OFF}}$ ratio [3]. **Phase-change memory (PCM)** uses Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ (GST): a melt-quench pulse creates a disordered amorphous dome, while crystallizing pulses grow the low-resistance crystalline volume. The conductance is set by the amorphous fraction — intrinsically *analog and multilevel*, but subject to structural relaxation that causes conductance drift [4].

> **Theorem: Analog MAC Identity.** For an $m \times n$ crossbar with conductances $G_{ij}$ and input voltages $V_i$ with $V_i < V_{\text{th}}$ (the disturb threshold), the bitline current $I_j = \sum_i V_i G_{ij}$ equals the exact analog dot product of the input vector and the conductance column, independent of the array size, neglecting wire parasitics. [1]

### 2.2 The crossbar as a VMM engine

A crossbar places a memristor at every wordline–bitline intersection. Wordline drivers (DACs) convert activations to voltages; the array performs multiplication and summation physically; bitline digitizers (ADCs/sense amplifiers) return digital results. Signed arithmetic uses **differential pairs**: $w \propto G^+ - G^-$ via two devices or arrays, with subtraction in the analog current domain before digitization [4].

### 2.3 Non-ideality taxonomy

| Non-ideality | Origin | Impact on inference | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADC/DAC overhead | Interface cost scales exponentially with resolution | 50–70% of tile power in naive designs | Bit-serial inputs, bit-sliced weights, adaptive ADCs [1][3] |
| Sneak-path currents | Parasitic paths through unselected cells | Read margin collapse, write disturb | V/3 bias, 1T1R, 1S1R selectors [5][6] |
| IR drop / wire parasitics | Finite wordline/bitline resistance | Position-dependent voltage errors | Array size limits, compensation models [8] |
| Device-to-device variation | Stochastic filament/phrase formation | Weight perturbation $\Delta G$ | Write-verify, noise-aware training [4][10] |
| Stuck-at faults | Failed cells frozen at HRS/LRS | Large localized weight errors | Fault-aware remapping, redundancy |
| Conductance drift (PCM) | Structural relaxation of amorphous phase | Global weight decay over time | Global/column drift compensation [4] |
| Nonlinear $I$–$V$ | Non-ohmic conduction at read voltage | Dot-product distortion | Low-voltage linear read regime |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The weight-to-conductance mapping pipeline

Deploying a trained network onto a crossbar is a compilation problem:

1. **Quantization.** FP32 weights → $B_w$-bit fixed point (typically 8–16); inputs → $B_v$-bit.
2. **Conductance normalization.** Each weight column is scaled to $[G_{\min}, G_{\max}]$ (code below).
3. **Bit-slicing in space.** A device stores only $b_d$ bits (e.g., 2-bit cells); a $B_w$-bit weight spans $\lceil B_w / b_d \rceil$ columns, combined by digital shift-and-add [1].
4. **Bit-serial input in time.** ISAAC feeds 16-bit activations as 16 one-bit pulses — no multi-bit DAC, shift-add accumulation [1].
5. **Write-verify.** Iterative SET/RESET pulses with read-verify until $|G - G_{\text{target}}| < \epsilon$ [4].
6. **Calibration.** Drift-compensation coefficients fuse into batch-norm — zero runtime overhead [4].

### 3.2 Architectural case studies

- **ISAAC** (Shafiee et al., ISCA 2016) [1]: 12 tiles per chip; each tile holds 12 in-situ multiply-accumulate (IMA) units of 8 crossbar arrays ($128 \times 128$, 1T1R). Layers are pipelined across tiles with eDRAM buffers; shift-added partial sums pass through a sigmoid operator. Weights are dedicated per layer.
- **PRIME** (Chi et al., ISCA 2016) [2]: *morphable* ReRAM main memory — 64 subarrays per bank (memory, full-function, buffer). FF mats ($256 \times 256$) switch between storage (SLC) and compute (4-bit MLC) modes; positive/negative weights live in separate arrays with 6-bit reconfigurable sense amplifiers and analog subtraction in the column multiplexers.
- **Newton** (Nag et al., ISCA 2017, surveyed in [3]): ISAAC optimized — eDRAM shrunk from 64 KB to 21 KB, adaptive ADCs replacing SAR ADCs, Karatsuba divide-and-conquer cutting power ~25% (at 6.4% area), and Strassen-style MVM restructuring: **51% better power efficiency, $2.2\times$ computational efficiency** over ISAAC [3].

> **Design principle.** The crossbar itself is nearly free — $O(1)$ analog compute with $O(mn)$ parallelism. All engineering effort concentrates on the *interfaces*: reducing ADC/DAC resolution through bit-spreading, protecting the analog signal from parasitics and sneak paths, and absorbing device non-idealities in the digital periphery. [1][3]

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Analog MAC precision: the ADC wall and bit-spreading

The central cost equation of analog in-memory computing concerns the digitizer. For an array with $R$ active rows, $b_v$ input bits, and $b_w$ weight bits, the bitline can resolve up to

$$b_{\text{out}} = b_v + b_w + \lceil \log_2 R \rceil$$

bits of dynamic range. ADC power and area grow *approximately exponentially* with resolution [1], so a naive 16-bit × 16-bit VMM over 128 rows would demand a ~39-bit ADC — physically absurd. ISAAC's breakthrough was spreading the product across *space and time*:

- **Time spreading:** a 16-bit input becomes 16 single-bit cycles ($b_v \rightarrow 1$), so no multi-bit DAC is needed; partial products are shift-added digitally.
- **Space spreading:** weights are stored in 2-bit cells ($b_w \rightarrow 2$) across multiple columns; each column's ADC resolves only $2 + \lceil\log_2 128\rceil = 9$ bits, with column results shift-added digitally — far preferable to a 39-bit ADC, at the price of latency and replication.

Newton pushes this further with *adaptive* ADCs that match digitization precision to each layer's signal statistics, trimming the largest remaining power consumer [3].

> **Theorem: Quantization Noise Bound.** Let $\hat{y}_j$ be the bit-sliced crossbar output with per-column ADC step $\Delta$ and weight bit-slices $k = 0 \dots K{-}1$. Then $|\hat{y}_j - y_j| \le \frac{\Delta}{2} \sum_k 2^{k b_d}$. Keeping this bound below half an LSB of the target activation precision guarantees bit-exact equivalence with a digital $B_w$-bit datapath. [1]

### 4.2 Sneak-path currents: physics and suppression

In a passive (1R) crossbar, reading the selected cell at $(i,j)$ is corrupted by *sneak paths*: current flows from the selected wordline through unselected cells, across neighboring bitlines, and back — adding a data-dependent parasitic current to the sensed bitline. The readout margin

$$\text{RM} = \frac{V_{\text{out}}(\text{LRS}) - V_{\text{out}}(\text{HRS})}{V_{\text{WS}}}$$

degrades with array size; the worst case is the cell farthest from the drivers, where interconnect resistance compounds the problem [5].

**Biasing schemes.** During reads, unselected lines are biased to shrink the voltage across parasitic paths:

| Scheme | Selected WL / BL | Unselected WL | Unselected BL | Max unselected cell bias | Sneak suppression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floating | $V$ / 0 | floating | floating | undefined (leakage-dependent) | poor |
| V/2 | $V$ / 0 | $V/2$ | $V/2$ | $V/2$ | moderate |
| V/3 | $V$ / 0 | $V/3$ | $2V/3$ | $V/3$ | good |

The V/3 scheme keeps every unselected cell at only $V/3$ bias; combined with a nonlinear device, leakage collapses super-linearly [5].

**Device-level fixes** stack in order of effectiveness: (1) **1T1R** — an access transistor per cell eliminates sneak paths but sacrifices density and 3D stackability [1][3]; (2) **1S1R** — a two-terminal *selector* (ovonic threshold switching, insulator–metal transition, or tunneling barrier) with highly nonlinear $I$–$V$: negligible current below threshold, full conduction above. Tunneling selectors have shown read margins above 90% for 100 kbit arrays under V/3 bias (vs ~20% under V/2) [6]; OTS and VO$_2$ selectors switch in under 20 ns with sub-nA leakage [7]; (3) **selectorless nonlinearity** — self-rectifying bilayer stacks (HfO$_x$/SiO$_x$, HfO$_x$/graphite) achieve nonlinearity $\text{NL} = I(V_{\text{read}})/I(V_{\text{read}}/3)$ up to ~120 (vs ~5 single-layer), constraining sneak current at low bias [10].

The field consensus: compute-mode arrays (ISAAC's 1T1R) prioritize fidelity, while high-density storage-mode arrays (PRIME's ReRAM) combine V/3 biasing with selector technology [2][5].

### 4.3 Variation-aware weight mapping

Real devices are stochastic. Filament formation in ReRAM and amorphous-volume definition in PCM vary device-to-device ($\sigma_G/G$ of 5–20% is typical) and cycle-to-cycle; some cells are *stuck* at HRS or LRS. Four techniques make mapping variation-aware:

- **Write-verify programming** closes the loop at write time: iterative program-and-read pulses drive each cell into $|G - G_{\text{target}}| < \epsilon$, at the cost of one-time latency [4].
- **Differential encoding** $w \propto G^+ - G^-$ cancels common-mode drift and correlated variation; only the *difference* of the two noise processes survives [4].
- **Multi-memristive synapses:** averaging $N$ devices per synapse reduces programming noise by $1/\sqrt{N}$ without increasing power density, at the cost of area [4].
- **Noise-aware training:** injecting Gaussian conductance noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_G^2)$ into the forward pass flattens the loss landscape around the deployed weight vector, pre-hardening the network to on-hardware perturbation; crossbar-aware mixed-precision quantization extends this by assigning more bits where the network is most sensitive [2].

> **Theorem: Perturbation Robustness.** If $|G_{ij} - G_{ij}^{\star}| \le \delta$ and $|V_i| \le V_{\max}$, the MVM error obeys $\|\hat{\mathbf{y}} - \mathbf{y}\|_\infty \le n V_{\max} \delta$; a network trained with forward-pass weight noise $\ge n V_{\max}\delta$ retains training accuracy up to the quantization floor. Differential encoding halves the effective $\delta$ under correlated drift. [4]

```python
def diff_decode(Gp, Gn, scale):          # differential-pair decode
    return (Gp - Gn) / scale            # common-mode drift cancels
```

In TLA+, the mapping pipeline's write-verify loop is specified as a convergent action:

```tla+
WriteVerify(cell, target) ==
  LET err == Abs(ReadConductance(cell) - target) IN
    IF err < EPS THEN UNCHANGED vars
    ELSE /\ ProgramPulse(cell, Sign(target - ReadConductance(cell)))
         /\ UNCHANGED <<scale>>
Converges == <>(\A c \in Cells : Abs(ReadConductance(c) - Target(c)) < EPS)
```

### 4.4 PCM conductance drift and its compensation

PCM's multilevel states relax structurally: conductance decays as a power law,

$$G(t) = G_0 \left(\frac{t}{t_0}\right)^{-\nu},$$

with drift exponent $\nu$ larger for higher-resistance (more amorphous) states — the states that encode large-magnitude weights decay fastest [4]. Uncompensated, a ResNet mapped to PCM collapses from ~93.75% CIFAR-10 accuracy to random guessing (~10%) within roughly 1000 seconds, because the decaying weight magnitudes starve activations of signal as they propagate through the network [4].

Two complementary compensations restore accuracy: (1) **Global drift compensation (GDC)** — periodically read the summed current of a reference column subset; the ratio $I(t)/I(t_0)$ is a single scalar capturing the global conductance shift. Multiplying every crossbar output by this factor — *fused into existing batch-normalization parameters*, hence zero runtime overhead — holds accuracy above **92.6% for a full day** on real PCM hardware [4]; (2) **Per-column affine correction** — newer platforms (e.g., IBM's 14 nm Hermes cores) implement per-column affine correction in the local digital unit, absorbing drift and residual programming offsets and enabling 8-bit in-memory MVM precision [4].

Together, write-verify (spatial accuracy) and GDC (temporal accuracy) make PCM a viable analog substrate despite its physics.

### 4.5 Energy analysis: the road to 50 TOPS/W

An analog MAC's intrinsic energy is $\int V \cdot I\, dt$ — femtojoules per operation, since no data moves. *System* efficiency is dominated by the periphery: ADCs, drivers, shift-add trees, buffers. The lineage shows a clear trajectory:

| Architecture | Device / array | Key efficiency technique | Reported gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISAAC [1] | TiO$_x$/HfO$_x$ 1T1R, $128\times128$ | Bit-serial input, 2-bit cells, shift-add | 14.8× throughput, 5.5× energy, 7.5× density vs. DaDianNao |
| PRIME [2] | Pt/TiO$_{2-x}$/Pt, $256\times256$, 4-bit MLC | Morphable memory/compute, 6-bit reconfigurable SAs | ~2360× performance, ~895× energy vs. NPU baseline |
| Newton [3] | TaO$_x$, adaptive ADCs | Adaptive ADC, Karatsuba, 21 KB eDRAM, mapping constraints | +51% power efficiency, $2.2\times$ compute efficiency vs. ISAAC |

Reaching **50 TOPS/W** requires each layer of the stack to contribute: (i) 1T1R or high-nonlinearity 1S1R cells keep arrays dense without read-margin collapse; (ii) bit-spreading caps ADC resolution at 8–9 bits, the knee of the exponential cost curve; (iii) adaptive digitization attacks the remaining interface power; (iv) drift and variation compensation preserve accuracy so aggressive quantization ($B_w \le 8$) stays viable; (v) pipelined tile mapping sustains high utilization [1][3][4]. At the device level, a 128-row column performing 128 MACs per cycle at sub-pJ per analog MAC already sits in the tens-of-TOPS/W regime — the claim of this work is that the *system* can be engineered to keep it there.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

### 5.1 Architectural results

evaluated on CNN/DNN suites: **ISAAC** improves on digital baseline DaDianNao by **14.8× throughput, 5.5× energy, 7.5× computational density** — the first full end-to-end characterization of a crossbar CNN accelerator including all digital support logic [1]. **PRIME** reports **~2360× performance and ~895× energy** over an NPU baseline, proving morphable memory/compute subarrays extract PIM benefits without a dedicated accelerator die [2]. **Newton** adds **51% power efficiency and $2.2\times$ compute efficiency** over ISAAC via adaptive ADCs, buffer right-sizing, and Karatsuba/Strassen arithmetic restructuring [3].

### 5.2 Silicon evidence: PCM drift compensation at scale

The most demanding validation comes from fabricated PCM hardware. Joshi et al. mapped ResNet-class layers onto a PCM platform and tracked CIFAR-10 accuracy over time [4]:

| Condition | Accuracy at $t_0$ | Accuracy after 1 day | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideal (no drift) | 93.75% | 93.75% | reference model |
| Uncompensated drift | 93.75% | ~10% (random) within ~1000 s | $G(t) = G_0(t/t_0)^{-\nu}$ weight decay |
| Global drift compensation | 93.75% | **> 92.6%** | $I(t)/I(t_0)$ scale fused into batch-norm |

Accurate modeling of noise and drift *as functions of both time and conductance state* proved critical — a constant-drift model fails to reproduce the experimental accuracy evolution. IBM's 14 nm Hermes cores added per-column affine correction in the local digital unit, enabling 8-bit in-memory MVM precision [4].

> **Theorem: Drift-Corrected Accuracy Bound.** Let per-device drift satisfy $G_{ij}(t) = G_{ij}(t_0)\,d(t)\,(1+\epsilon_{ij}(t))$ with global factor $d(t)$ and residual $|\epsilon_{ij}(t)| \le \epsilon$. GDC exactly cancels $d(t)$; the residual output error is bounded by $n V_{\max} G_{\max} \epsilon$, independent of elapsed time. Hence accuracy is asymptotically *time-invariant* up to the residual, as observed experimentally. [4]

### 5.3 Error-propagation analysis

Composing per-layer bounds yields an end-to-end guarantee: for an $L$-layer network with 1-Lipschitz activations and per-layer MVM error $\eta_\ell$, output deviation satisfies $\|\hat{\mathbf{z}} - \mathbf{z}\| \le \sum_\ell \eta_\ell \prod_{k>\ell} \|W_k\|_2$. The sensitivity to later-layer spectral norms explains two empirical practices: compensation matters *most* in deep layers, and noise-aware training — which implicitly regularizes $\|W_k\|_2$ — is doubly effective [4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **The ADC wall persists.** Bit-spreading converts an exponential cost into linear latency/area, but the digitizer remains the largest power consumer in every published design; sub-8-bit and ADC-less readouts are active research, not solved engineering [1][3][8].
2. **IR drop bounds array size.** Wordline/bitline resistance makes effective read voltage position-dependent; with sneak paths, this caps practical arrays at $128$–$512$ rows without compensation models [5][8].
3. **Endurance and write cost.** ReRAM endurance ($10^9$–$10^{12}$ cycles) suffices for inference with occasional reprogramming, but on-chip training remains out of reach for PCM ($10^6$–$10^9$) [2][3].
4. **Temperature sensitivity.** Conductance, drift exponents, and selector thresholds shift with temperature; datacenter deployment needs thermal calibration loops absent from current prototypes [4].
5. **Variation floor.** Write-verify and differential encoding suppress but cannot eliminate variation; below ~4-bit effective precision, unhardened networks collapse [10].
6. **Mapping rigidity.** ISAAC-style per-layer crossbar dedication is excellent for inference throughput but makes the chip a fixed-function ASIC per network; model updates need reprogramming campaigns [1].

## 7 Conclusion

Analog in-memory computing with memristor crossbars is no longer a device curiosity: the ISAAC–PRIME–Newton lineage proves a full accelerator — arrays, ADCs, buffers, control — can be built and characterized end-to-end with order-of-magnitude gains over digital baselines [1][2][3]; and PCM hardware with drift compensation proves non-ideal physics can be absorbed by architecture and algorithm, holding >92.6% CIFAR-10 accuracy for a day on real silicon [4].

The path to **50 TOPS/W** is now a co-design checklist: high-nonlinearity 1S1R or 1T1R cells [6][7], bit-spread datapaths holding ADCs at 8–9 bits [1][3], write-verify plus differential mapping [4][10], drift calibration fused into batch-norm [4], and pipelined tile mapping [1]. Open problems — ADC-less readout, IR-drop-aware compilation, thermal robustness, and endurance for on-chip learning — define the next decade. The equation $I_j = \sum_i V_i G_{ij}$ is simple; making it trustworthy at scale is the discipline this thesis has mapped.
