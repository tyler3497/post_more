# Discrete Exterior Calculus and Spectral Methods on Simplicial Complexes: Hodge Decompositions, Persistent Laplacians, and Structure-Preserving Discretization

## Abstract

Discrete exterior calculus (DEC) provides a coordinate-free discretization of the calculus of differential forms on simplicial meshes, preserving Stokes' theorem, the Hodge decomposition, and the cohomology of the underlying manifold at the discrete level. This article develops the theory of DEC on simplicial complexes together with the spectral theory of combinatorial and persistent Laplacians. We construct the discrete Hodge star via circumcentric duality, derive the discrete codifferential and Hodge Laplacian, and state the combinatorial Hodge theorem identifying harmonic cochains with simplicial cohomology. We then turn to spectra: the up and down combinatorial Laplacians of Horak and Jost, their Betti-number nullities, and the persistent Laplacian of a pair of complexes, whose nullity recovers the persistent Betti numbers while its nonzero spectrum yields a strictly richer invariant than persistent homology, the harmonic barcode. Connections to discrete curvature through Regge deficit angles and Ollivier–Ricci optimal-transport curvature are drawn, and structure-preserving discretizations of Maxwell's equations and incompressible fluids are analyzed. Convergence theory, algorithmic costs, stability results, and open problems in multiscale persistent spectral geometry close the discussion.

The discrete exterior derivative is the *coboundary*, making Stokes' theorem an exact identity. The Hodge star, codifferential, wedge product, and Lie derivative need the mesh geometry, and that is where the theory gets interesting.

In parallel, a second program asks what *spectral geometry* means on a simplicial complex. The graph Laplacian generalizes to the *combinatorial Hodge Laplacian* on *k*-chains. Its kernel computes cohomology, its nonzero spectrum encodes geometry — a theory developed systematically by Horak and Jost [3]. Most recently, the *persistent Laplacian* of Wang, Nguyen, and Wei, analyzed by Mémoli, Sethi, and Wang [4], extends spectra to *pairs* and *filtrations* of complexes, producing the *harmonic barcode*: a multiscale invariant strictly refining persistent homology [5].

This article unifies these threads. Our aims are threefold: first, to give a self-contained exposition of DEC, from simplicial chains to the discrete Hodge decomposition; second, to develop the spectral theory of combinatorial and persistent Laplacians with full statements of the key theorems; and third, to survey structure-preserving applications — electromagnetism, fluids, discrete curvature, and topological data analysis — with an honest account of convergence theory and limitations.

## 3 Methodology

### From chains to discrete forms

In DEC, a **discrete differential k-form** is a primal *k*-cochain α ∈ *C^k(K)*: the number ⟨α, σ⟩ on each oriented *k*-simplex σ is read as the integral of the form over σ. The **discrete exterior derivative** is the coboundary, and *discrete Stokes' theorem* ⟨dα, σ⟩ = ⟨α, ∂σ⟩ holds *exactly* by definition. The operators that depend on the Riemannian metric in the smooth setting — Hodge star, codifferential, wedge product — are where the mesh geometry enters.

### The dual complex and the discrete Hodge star

A metric enters DEC through the **dual cell complex**. Given an embedded simplicial complex in which every simplex contains its circumcenter — a *well-centered* or Delaunay-type mesh — each primal *k*-simplex σ has a dual *(n − k)*-cell ⋆σ formed by the circumcenters of the *n*-simplices containing σ. The **discrete Hodge star** ⋆ : *C^k(K)* → *C^{n−k}(⋆K)* is then the diagonal operator

⟨⋆α, ⋆σ⟩ = (|⋆σ| / |σ|) ⟨α, σ⟩,

where |·| denotes *k*-dimensional volume. This is the discrete avatar of the smooth identity ⋆⋆ = (−1)^{k(n−k)} [2].

With the Hodge star in hand, the **discrete codifferential** δ : *C^k* → *C^{k−1}* is defined by

δ = (−1)^{nk+n+1} ⋆ d ⋆,

mirroring the smooth formula δ = ± ⋆ d ⋆, and the **discrete Hodge Laplacian** is

Δ = dδ + δd : *C^k* → *C^k*.

The following table summarizes the dictionary between the smooth and discrete theories.

| Smooth concept | DEC object | Discrete operator / identity |
|---|---|---|
| *k*-form ω ∈ Ω^k | Primal cochain α ∈ C^k | Evaluation on *k*-simplices |
| Exterior derivative *d* | Coboundary *d* | ⟨dα, σ⟩ = ⟨α, ∂σ⟩ (exact Stokes) |
| Hodge star ⋆ | Diagonal ⋆ via dual volumes | ⋆α on ⋆σ scaled by \|⋆σ\|/\|σ\| |
| Codifferential δ | δ = ±⋆d⋆ | Adjoint of *d* under the discrete L² inner product |
| Hodge Laplacian Δ = dδ + δd | Δ : C^k → C^k | Sparse symmetric positive semidefinite |
| Harmonic form, Δω = 0 | Harmonic cochain, Δα = 0 | Represents a cohomology class |

### The discrete Hodge decomposition

The centerpiece of the methodology is the following structural theorem, the discrete analogue of the classical Hodge decomposition on a compact oriented Riemannian manifold.

> **Theorem (Discrete Hodge decomposition).** Let *K* be a finite simplicial complex triangulating a compact oriented *n*-manifold, equipped with the discrete L² inner product induced by the Hodge star. Then the space of discrete *k*-forms decomposes orthogonally as
>
> *C^k* = im(*d* : *C^{k−1}* → *C^k*) ⊕ ker(Δ : *C^k* → *C^k*) ⊕ im(δ : *C^{k+1}* → *C^k*).
>
> Moreover, the map sending a harmonic cochain to its cohomology class is an isomorphism ker(Δ) ≅ *H^k(K; ℝ)*.

> **Proof (sketch).** Since δ is the adjoint of *d* under the Hodge inner product, ker(*d*) = (im δ)^⊥ and ker(δ) = (im *d*)^⊥, and Δα = 0 iff *d*α = 0 and δα = 0, because ⟨Δα, α⟩ = ‖dα‖² + ‖δα‖². The three-term splitting follows, and the orthogonal complement relations give the cohomology isomorphism [2]. ∎

This decomposition drives **Hodge-based vector field processing**: a discrete vector field (as a primal 1-form) splits uniquely into gradient, harmonic, and curl parts, enabling topology-aware smoothing and fluid simulation [2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The discrete Hodge star and circumcentric duality

The choice of the **circumcentric dual** over the barycentric dual is the load-bearing design decision of DEC [2]: only with circumcenters do primal and dual cells meet orthogonally, making the Hodge star diagonal and the codifferential a genuine adjoint. The price is **well-centeredness** — every simplex must contain its circumcenter — which in two dimensions is the Delaunay condition, and whose enforcement in higher dimensions is itself a nontrivial meshing problem [1].

When the mesh is not well-centered, the signed dual volumes can be negative, and the Hodge star ceases to be positive definite. Several remedies exist:

1. **Barycentric Hodge stars**, always positive but non-diagonal, coupling neighboring simplices.
2. **Galerkin Hodge stars** from Whitney form mass matrices, coinciding with FEEC inner products and inheriting their convergence theory.
3. **Delaunay Hodge stars**, diagonal and positive but restricted to Delaunay triangulations [1].

The convergence of DEC operators is subtle because the theory is formulated *intrinsically* on the mesh. For the diagonal Hodge star on well-centered meshes, the discrete Laplacian converges to the Laplace–Beltrami operator under refinement, with rates depending on mesh quality measures such as aspect ratio. A fully general convergence theory for all DEC operators remains open, motivating the Galerkin bridge to FEEC.

### 4.2 Spectral theory of combinatorial Laplacians

Forget the metric for a moment: the *combinatorial* Laplacian is defined purely from incidence. On *k*-chains with the orthonormal simplex basis, the **up-Laplacian** and **down-Laplacian** are Δ_k^up = ∂_{k+1} ∂_{k+1}^* and Δ_k^down = ∂_k^* ∂_k, and the **combinatorial Hodge Laplacian** is their sum. For *k* = 0, Δ_0^up is exactly the graph Laplacian *D − A*. Horak and Jost [3] gave the first systematic treatment: normalization, interlacing under wedge sums and joins, and relations between extremal eigenvalues and combinatorial invariants such as the chromatic number.

The foundational spectral-topological fact is Eckmann's discrete Hodge theorem:

> **Theorem (Combinatorial Hodge theorem).** For a finite simplicial complex *K*, ker(Δ_k) ≅ *H_k(K; ℝ)*, so the nullity of the *k*-th combinatorial Laplacian equals the *k*-th Betti number β_k.

Nonzero eigenvalues carry *geometric* information: they measure deviation from complete skeleta, control mixing of higher-dimensional random walks, and appear in higher Cheeger inequalities. The *spectrum* of Δ_k is thus a joint topological-geometric descriptor, and its eigenvectors underlie spectral embeddings, heat kernel signatures, and diffusion-based shape descriptors in geometry processing.

A Haskell encoding of the chain complex makes the combinatorial nature explicit:

```haskell
-- Simplicial chains over the reals, boundary as a sparse linear map
type Vertex  = Int
type Simplex = [Vertex]          -- ordered vertices fix orientation
type Chain   = [(Double, Simplex)]

-- Boundary of an oriented simplex: alternating sum of faces
boundary :: Simplex -> Chain
boundary s = [ (fromIntegral ((-1)^i), deleteAt i s)
             | i <- [0 .. length s - 1] ]
  where deleteAt i xs = take i xs ++ drop (i+1) xs

-- The up-Laplacian on k-chains: L = d d^* with the orthonormal basis
upLaplacian :: [Simplex] -> [Simplex] -> [[Double]]
upLaplacian kSimplices kp1Simplices =
  [ [ inner (coboundary a) (coboundary b)
    | b <- kSimplices ] | a <- kSimplices ]
  where
    coboundary t = [ (c, f) | (c, f) <- concatMap boundary kp1Simplices
                            , isFace t f ]
    isFace t f = all (`elem` f) t
    inner xs ys = sum [ c*d | (c,x) <- xs, (d,y) <- ys, x == y ]
```

The matrix assembled above is Δ_k^up in the simplex basis; adding the down term yields the full combinatorial Laplacian whose kernel dimension is β_k.

### 4.3 Persistent Laplacians and harmonic barcodes

Persistent homology studies a *filtration* — a nested sequence *K_1* ↪ *K_2* ↪ ⋯ — recording birth and death of homology classes as intervals in a *barcode*. It is *homological*: it sees only the Laplacian's kernel, discarding the nonzero spectrum. The **persistent Laplacian**, introduced by Wang, Nguyen, and Wei and developed by Mémoli, Sethi, and Wang [4], repairs this loss.

Given an inclusion of simplicial complexes *K* ↪ *L*, let *C_{q+1}^{K,L}* be the subspace of *(q+1)*-chains of *L* whose boundary lies in *C_q(K)*, and let ∂_{q+1}^{K,L} be the restricted boundary. The **q-th persistent Laplacian** is the operator Δ_q^{K,L} : *C_q(K)* → *C_q(K)* defined by

Δ_q^{K,L} = ∂_{q+1}^{K,L} (∂_{q+1}^{K,L})^* + (∂_q^K)^* ∂_q^K,

the sum of a *persistent up-Laplacian* and the ordinary *down-Laplacian* of *K*. When *K* = *L*, this reduces to the standard combinatorial Laplacian. The theory then delivers:

> **Theorem (Persistent Hodge theorem [4]).** For an inclusion *K* ↪ *L*, the nullity of Δ_q^{K,L} equals the *q*-th persistent Betti number β_q^{K,L} = dim im(*H_q(K)* → *H_q(L)*). Moreover, one has the persistent Hodge decomposition
>
> *C_q(K)* = im(∂_{q+1}^{K,L}) ⊕ ker(Δ_q^{K,L}) ⊕ im((∂_q^K)^*).

Mémoli, Sethi, and Wang further established a remarkable computational bridge: the persistent Laplacian is a **Schur complement** of the ordinary Laplacian of *L*. This yields a simple algorithm — compute the Laplacian of *L*, take the Schur complement with respect to the chain space of *K* — that is in some regimes significantly faster than standard persistence algorithms, and it uncovers a link with effective resistance and a *persistent Cheeger inequality* in the graph case [4].

The nonzero eigenvalues of Δ_q^{K,L} across the filtration trace the **harmonic barcode**: spectral curves detecting geometric events — cavity filling, tunnel thickening — *before* any homology class is born or dies. Wei and Wei [5] document that non-harmonic spectra distinguish point clouds that persistent homology cannot, and measurably improve machine learning models on protein engineering datasets where geometry, not just topology, determines function. Stability theorems [4] give Lipschitz-type eigenvalue bounds under interleaving distances, providing the robustness that made persistent homology practical.

### 4.4 Discrete curvature: Regge calculus and Ollivier–Ricci curvature

Curvature, the second-order invariant of Riemannian geometry, also admits discrete avatars — and DEC's metric apparatus (the Hodge star) is exactly what lets one write them down. Two traditions dominate:

- **Regge calculus** discretizes the Einstein–Hilbert action as a sum over codimension-two *hinges* of *area × deficit angle*, the deficit ε_h = 2π − Σ(dihedral angles) measuring non-flatness. On surfaces the vertex angle defect is the discrete Gaussian curvature, and discrete Gauss–Bonnet holds exactly.
- **Ollivier–Ricci curvature** takes an optimal-transport route [6]: for a metric space with random walk *m_x* at each point, κ(x, y) = 1 − W_1(m_x, m_y)/d(x, y) with *W_1* the Wasserstein-1 distance. On graphs it is positive on tight communities and negative across bottlenecks; Jost and Liu [6] tied it to clustering and curvature-dimension inequalities. Needing no embedding, it is the curvature of choice for network science and graph neural networks, where it diagnoses over-squashing and guides rewiring.

Both notions interact with the Laplacian: Forman's combinatorial Ricci curvature derives from a discrete Bochner–Weitzenböck formula, and Ollivier–Ricci lower bounds imply spectral gaps via discrete Bakry–Émery theory. The emerging picture is a *discrete Riemannian geometry* in which DEC supplies the differential operators, the combinatorial Laplacian supplies the analysis, and curvature supplies the comparison theorems.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### The persistent Laplacian in code

The Schur-complement formulation makes the persistent Laplacian directly computable. The following Python routine assembles the combinatorial Laplacians of a complex from its boundary matrices and extracts Betti numbers from the kernel — the *K* = *L* case of the persistent theory:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import null_space

def boundary_matrix(n_k, n_km1, faces):
    """Signed incidence: rows = (k-1)-simplices, cols = k-simplices."""
    B = np.zeros((n_km1, n_k))
    for j, f in enumerate(faces):          # f: list of (k-1)-face indices
        for i, sgn in f:                   # with orientation signs
            B[i, j] = sgn
    return B

def hodge_laplacian(Bk, Bk1):
    """Combinatorial Hodge Laplacian on k-chains.
    Bk: boundary k -> k-1 ; Bk1: boundary k+1 -> k."""
    down = Bk.T @ Bk if Bk.size else np.zeros((Bk.shape[1],) * 2)
    up = Bk1 @ Bk1.T if Bk1.size else np.zeros_like(down)
    return up + down

def betti_number(L, tol=1e-8):
    """dim ker L = multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue."""
    return null_space(L, rcond=tol).shape[1]

# Example: hollow tetrahedron (2-sphere triangulation)
# C2: 4 faces, C1: 6 edges, C0: 4 vertices
B1 = boundary_matrix(6, 4, [...])   # edge -> vertex incidences
B2 = boundary_matrix(4, 6, [...])   # face -> edge incidences
L0, L1, L2 = (hodge_laplacian(B0, B1) for B0, B1 in
              [(np.zeros((4, 0)), B1), (B1, B2), (B2, np.zeros((6, 0)))])
print(betti_number(L0), betti_number(L1), betti_number(L2))  # -> 1 0 1
```

The output β = (1, 0, 1) is the homology of the 2-sphere, recovered purely from the spectrum — a numerical shadow of the combinatorial Hodge theorem.

### Structure-preserving discretization of Maxwell's equations

In the language of forms, Maxwell's equations read *dE* = −∂_t*B*, *dB* = 0, *dD* = ρ, *dH* = *J* + ∂_t*D*, with constitutive laws *D* = ⋆_ε*E*, *B* = ⋆_μ*H* supplied by *material* Hodge stars. Because the discrete exterior derivative is the exact coboundary, the constraints *dB* = 0 and charge conservation hold *to machine precision* for all time — no spurious charges — with approximation entering only through the Hodge stars [2]. Yee's staggered-grid FDTD scheme is the special case of DEC on a cubical lattice; DEC extends it to unstructured simplicial meshes with the same guarantees.

### DEC fluids and vorticity advection

Elcott, Tong, Kanso, Schröder, and Desbrun built a **discrete, circulation-preserving simplicial fluid** simulator on DEC foundations [2]. Vorticity is a discrete 2-form; Kelvin's circulation theorem becomes exact because the discrete Lie derivative commutes with the discrete exterior derivative. The scheme is stable, exactly conserves circulation and (in the inviscid limit) energy, and handles complex domains without coordinate singularities.

### Stability and convergence

On the theoretical side, the key quantitative results are:

1. **Stability of persistent Laplacian spectra** [4]: Lipschitz-type eigenvalue bounds in terms of interleaving distances — the spectral analogue of the stability theorem of persistent homology.
2. **Convergence of DEC Laplacians**: on well-centered meshes with bounded aspect ratio, the diagonal-Hodge-star Laplacian converges to Laplace–Beltrami; the Galerkin (Whitney-form) Hodge star inherits the FEEC error analysis with optimal-order convergence.
3. **Empirical validation**: harmonic barcodes on Vietoris–Rips filtrations of molecular point clouds detect conformational transitions where the classical barcode is silent, improving regression on protein stability datasets [5].

## 6 Limitations

The theory, for all its elegance, faces concrete obstacles:

- **Mesh quality.** The diagonal Hodge star demands well-centered (Delaunay-type) meshes, hard to generate in three dimensions and sometimes requiring Steiner points. Non-diagonal alternatives sacrifice sparsity.
- **The wedge product.** No fully satisfactory discrete wedge exists: the primal–primal wedge is associative only up to vanishing refinement terms and fails the Leibniz rule exactly, complicating nonlinearities such as fluid convection.
- **Convergence gaps.** A unified error analysis covering the full DEC operator algebra — wedge, contraction, Lie derivative — on general complexes is incomplete; the Galerkin bridge covers only the linear elliptic case.
- **Cost of persistence.** Eigendecompositions per filtration value remain expensive for large Vietoris–Rips complexes despite the Schur-complement speedup [4].
- **Curvature ambiguity.** Regge, Ollivier–Ricci, and Forman curvatures differ quantitatively; the choice must be justified per application, and convergence to smooth Ricci holds only in restricted settings.

## 7 Conclusion

Discrete exterior calculus completes a century-long arc from Cartan's differential forms to a discrete calculus that *means it* when it writes *d² = 0*. Pairing the combinatorial exactness of the boundary operator with the metric content of the circumcentric Hodge star yields discretizations of Maxwell's equations and fluid mechanics that conserve the right quantities for the right reasons. The spectral program — combinatorial Laplacians, their Hodge-theoretic kernels, and persistent Laplacians across filtrations — turns the same simplicial data into a multiscale geometric microscope, the harmonic barcode, that sees strictly more than homology alone.

The frontier is *multiscale and statistical*: persistent spectral methods on filtrations with millions of simplices, learned Hodge stars adapting the discrete metric to data, discrete curvature flows uniformizing networks, and a convergence theory covering the whole DEC operator algebra. Discretization is not approximation of the continuum by the discrete, but the discovery that the discrete has its own exact geometry — and that geometry, once understood, computes.
