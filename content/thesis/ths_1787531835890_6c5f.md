# Formal Verification of CRDT Convergence under Byzantine Faults: State-based vs Op-based, Lean4 Mechanization, and Causal Stability Proofs

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of formal verification of CRDT convergence under Byzantine faults, synthesizing state-based and op-based models with Lean4 mechanization and causal stability proofs. We formalize convergence as join-semilattice monotonicity, causal delivery, and Byzantine-tolerant quorum intersection spanning distributed storage. Drawing on 7 authoritative sources including Shapiro's CRDT foundations, Byzantine CRDT extensions, and Lean4/Iris verification frameworks, methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale operation traces. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds for state-based CvRDT and op-based CmRDT under f < n/3 Byzantine replicas. Empirical results report convergence latency, metadata overhead, and verification coverage with 95% CI. Limitations include network asynchrony, state explosion in TLC, and trusted computing base assumptions, mapped to open problems.
