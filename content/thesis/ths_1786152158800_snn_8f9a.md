# Surrogate Gradient Learning in Deep Spiking Neural Networks: Temporal Credit Assignment, SpikeProp Generalization, and Compilation to Mixed-Signal Neuromorphic Hardware

## Abstract
Deep **Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)** promise order-of-magnitude energy efficiency by replacing dense multiply-accumulate (MAC) with sparse, asynchronous spike-driven accumulate (AC) operations, yet training remains obstructed by the non-differentiability of the spike mechanism. This thesis synthesizes the modern surrogate gradient framework as a principled relaxation of exact event-based differentiation, placing explicit emphasis on *temporal credit assignment* through Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) over Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) state graphs. We revisit **SpikeProp** (Bohte et al. 2002) as a first-spike-time gradient, show its generalization to multi-spike surrogate Jacobians, and provide a unified table of surrogate shapes with bias-variance trade-offs formalized via indicator χ. We then develop a compilation pipeline from trained deep SNNs to *Intel Loihi 2* neurocores and mixed-signal memristive crossbars, analyzing quantization, microcode programmable dynamics, graded-spike messaging, and three-factor learning constraints. We argue that surrogate gradients are not a heuristic trick but an *REINFORCE-like smoothed estimator* whose limit recovers true subgradient in distribution, and that temporal unrolling with eligibility traces offers the only scalable alternative to exact forward-mode spike-time differentiation. The thesis closes with empirical design rules and hardware-aware limitations essential for deploying deep SNNs at <1 mJ per inference.

## 1 Introduction

> **Neuromorphic computing is not about mimicking the brain for romantic reasons; it is about escaping the von Neumann memory wall by making time a first-class compute operand.**

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) have re-emerged from theory to deployable hardware due to two coincident trends: (i) the diminishing returns of Dennard scaling forcing event-driven computation, and (ii) the maturation of **surrogate gradient** training enabling ImageNet-scale accuracies with 2-16 timesteps [Neftci et al. 2019][Eff. SG 2023]. Yet textbook treatments still present SNN training as either *SpikeProp* first-spike learning or *ANN-to-SNN conversion*, obscuring the dominant paradigm that now powers all state-of-the-art: **direct training with smoothed Heaviside derivatives and temporal BPTT**.

Three historical strands converge:

1. *Timing-based learning*: SpikeProp defined error on output spike times $t_o^a$ vs $t_o^d$ under Spike Response Model (SRM), deriving $\partial t_o / \partial w_{ij}$ via implicit differentiation. Elegant, local, but brittle for deep networks with vanishing spike activity and nondifferentiable multi-spike regimes.
2. *Rate-based conversion*: Train ANN ReLU, map to IF neurons with weight normalization. Lossless at $T\to\infty$ but latency-inefficient and incompatible with temporal codes.
3. *Surrogate direct*: Keep forward pass exact $s[t] = \Theta(v[t]-V_{th})$, replace backward $\partial s / \partial v$ with smooth proxy $\tilde{g}'(v-V_{th})$. BPTT then propagates through membrane potential dynamics $v[t+1] = \beta v[t] + W s_{in}[t] - V_{th} s[t]$.

The last approach dominates because it reconciles *exact spiking forward* with *approximate but dense backward* gradients. This thesis makes three contributions:

- Formalization of **LIF dynamics as a discrete-time gated RNN** with explicit reset term, enabling precise temporal credit analysis via Jacobian products $\prod_{k=i}^{t} \beta (1-s[k])$.
- **Taxonomy of surrogate shapes** (fast sigmoid, exponential, piecewise linear, arctan, Gaussian) with slope annealing schedules proven to improve RL tasks $2.1\times$ in return [2510.24461], plus introduction of $\chi$ – proportion of non-zero gradient-bearing parameters – as tuning objective.
- End-to-end **mapping to Loihi 2**: 128 neurocores, 8k neurons/core, 32KB neuron + 128KB synapse SRAM, microcode-programmable LIF variants, graded-spike messaging, barrier-synchronized timesteps $\ge 200$ns [open-neuromorphic Loihi 2][2511.01553], and extension to analog mixed-signal crossbars with current-mirror LIF.

We preserve biological plausibility while demanding *systems rigor*: every trick must compile.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Artificial Neurons to Spiking Neurons
ANN ReLU neuron $y = \text{ReLU}(Wx+b)$ is *memoryless* and *real-valued*. LIF introduces state $v[t]\in\mathbb{R}^N$ and event $s[t]\in\{0,1\}^N$:

$$
\tau_m \dot{v} = -(v - V_{rest}) + I(t),\quad \text{if } v>V_{th}: s=1, v\gets V_{reset}
$$

Discrete Euler with $dt=1$ms yields standard deep learning compatible update widely used in **SpikingJelly, Norse, Lava, SLAYER** tutorial implementations [MDPI 2025].

*Energy argument*: MAC in 45nm = 4.6 pJ, AC = 0.9 pJ; sparsity of 85-95% on DVS-Gesture reduces effective ops $60\times$ on Loihi 2 vs Jetson Orin [BusinessWire Loihi 2 launch].

### 2.2 Why SpikeProp Stalled
SpikeProp (Bohte 2002) assumed single spike per neuron, SRM $u_j(t)=\sum_i w_{ij}\epsilon(t-t_i-d_{ij})$, error $E = \tfrac12\sum_o (t_o^a-t_o^d)^2$, gradient:

$$
\frac{\partial E}{\partial w_{ij}} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial t_j}\frac{\partial t_j}{\partial u_j}\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial w_{ij}},\quad \frac{\partial t_j}{\partial u_j} = -1 / \dot{u}_j(t_j)
$$

Limitation: *critical points where $\dot{u}_j(t_j)\to0$* → exploding gradients, inability to handle dead neurons (no spike → no derivative), and $O(N^2)$ event-driven simulation unsuited to tensor cores.

Modern view: SpikeProp is **special case of implicit differentiation** at spike times; surrogate BPTT generalizes it by allowing dense gradients even when $s[t]=0$, at cost of bias.

### 2.3 Neuromorphic Hardware Spectrum
- **Digital**: Intel Loihi 2 (Intel 4, 7nm), IBM TrueNorth (retired), SpiNNaker2 (ARM mesh)
- **Mixed-signal**: BrainScaleS-2 (analog neurons, digital routing), DYNAP-SE2, memristor $128\times128$ 1T1R TiO2 crossbars
- **Compilers**: Lava (Python→NxCore), LAVA-DL SLAYER, Rockpool, sinabs

Mixed-signal introduces *device mismatch $\sigma_{Vth}\approx 20-50$mV, limited conductance levels (4-8 bits), and IR-drop*, requiring hardware-in-the-loop surrogate fine-tuning.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **convergent analysis**:

1. **Analytical**: Model LIF as hybrid system, derive exact Jacobian $\partial v[t+1]/\partial v[t]$, $\partial v[t+1]/\partial W$, unrolled graph temporal credit length $T$.
2. **Functional**: Treat surrogate as *mollified distribution* $H_\sigma = \Theta * \kappa_\sigma$, $\kappa_\sigma\to\delta$ as $\sigma\to0$, gradient bias $\mathbb{E}[\tilde g'] - \delta_0$ quantified via Wasserstein-1.
3. **Systems**: Map quantized weights $W_q\in\text{INT8}$ and membrane constants $\beta_q$ to Loihi microcode; evaluate core partitioning with Lava profiler [2511.01553]; mixed-signal conversion via conductance $G_{ij}= G_{min}+(W_{ij}-W_{min})\Delta G$.

Metrics: accuracy vs timesteps $T$, $\chi$-density, spike rate $\rho$, energy-delay-product (EDP) on Loihi 2 EPL $E \approx N_{synops}\cdot 23.6\text{pJ} + N_{spikes}\cdot 71\text{pJ}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 LIF neuron dynamics

Standard discrete LIF with soft/hard reset:

$$
\begin{align*}
u[t] &= \beta \cdot v[t-1] + \sum_{j} W_{ij} s_j[t] + b_i - V_{th} s_i[t-1] \quad\text{(hard reset)}\\
v[t] &= u[t] \cdot (1 - s_i[t-1]) + V_{reset} s_i[t-1] \quad\text{alternative form}\\
s_i[t] &= \Theta(u[t]-V_{th})
\end{align*}
$$

where $\beta = \exp(-dt/\tau_m)\in[0.8,0.99]$. *Leak* provides fading memory.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Temporal Jacobian).** *For LIF with hard reset, $\frac{\partial v[t]}{\partial v[t-1]} = \beta (1-s[t-1]) - V_{th}\tilde g'(v[t-1]-V_{th})\beta ...$ approximated; exact for non-spiking intervals is $\beta^{k}$. Hence gradient decays exponentially $O(\beta^{T})$ – leak-induced vanishing.*

**Proof sketch:** induction on non-spiking segment. QED.

*Biological nuance*: Circulate-firing neurons [2605.27412] recirculate residual membrane over threshold to preserve subthreshold info, reducing dead neuron rate 23% on ImageNet SEW-ResNet34.

Code:

```python
# PyTorch LIF with surrogate hook
class LIF(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, beta=0.9, vth=1.0):
        super().__init__(); self.beta=beta; self.vth=vth
    def forward(self, x_seq): # x_seq: [T,B,N]
        B,N = x_seq.shape[1:]
        v = torch.zeros(B,N, device=x_seq.device)
        s_out=[]
        for t in range(x_seq.shape[0]):
            v = self.beta*v + x_seq[t] - self.vth*(s_out[-1] if s_out else 0)
            s = surrogate_heaviside(v-self.vth)  # forward = step, backward = sg
            s_out.append(s)
        return torch.stack(s_out), v
```

Haskell type-level encoding (for safety):

```haskell
-- Haskell: indexed LIF state
data LIF (n :: Nat) where
  LIF :: { beta :: Float, vth :: Float, v :: Vector n Float } -> LIF n
stepLIF :: KnownNat n => LIF n -> Vector n Float -> (LIF n, Vector n Bool)
stepLIF lif x = let u = scale beta (v lif) ^+^ x
                    s = fmap (> vth lif) u
                    v' = if s then reset else u
                in (lif {v=v'}, s)
```

### 4.2 Surrogate derivative shapes

Let $H(x)=\Theta(x)$. Surrogate $\tilde H_\alpha$ with $\tilde H_\alpha \to H$ pointwise as $\alpha\to0$ but derivative well-behaved:

| Name | Forward | Backward $\tilde g'(x)$ | Support | Bias |
|------|---------|---------------------------|---------|------|
| Fast Sigmoid | $H$ | $\frac{1}{(1+\alpha|x|)^2}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | low var |
| Exponential | $H$ | $\alpha e^{-\alpha|x|}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | sharp |
| Piecewise Linear | $H$ | $\max(0,1-\alpha|x|)$ | $[-1/\alpha,1/\alpha]$ | sparse |
| Arctan | $H$ | $\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\alpha}{1+(\alpha x)^2}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | heavy-tail |
| Gaussian | $H$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}$ | ~ $3\sigma$ | smooth |

**Key insight** from Chi-based tuning [OpenReview 2023]: define

$$
\chi(\alpha)=\frac{1}{|\theta|}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[|\nabla_{\theta_i} L| > \epsilon]
$$

Surrogate too sharp → $\chi\to0$ (dead gradient), too flat → biased direction. Optimal $\alpha^\star$ maximizes $\chi$ under validation accuracy constraint; adaptive scheduling $\alpha_t = \alpha_0 \cdot \gamma^{epoch}$ improves RL 2.1× [2510.24461].

> **Lemma 4.2:** *As $\sigma\to0$, $\tilde g'_\sigma \xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}'} \delta_0$ in distribution. For any test function $\phi\in C_c^\infty$, $\int \tilde g'_\sigma \phi \to \phi(0)$.*

Thus surrogate BPTT is *smoothed gradient* akin to Straight-Through Estimator (STE) in quant-aware training.

Rust microcode stub for Loihi 2 learning engine:

```rust
// Rust-like Loihi 2 microcode for surrogate-adapted three-factor rule
fn lif_update(state: &mut NeuronState, syn_in: i16, cfg: &Cfg) -> bool {
    let v = (cfg.beta * state.v as i32 + syn_in as i32) >> 8;
    let spk = v >= cfg.vth;
    state.v = if spk { cfg.v_reset } else { v as i16 };
    // on-chip learning hint: store trace e[t]=beta*e[t-1]+s_in[t]
    state.trace = (cfg.beta * state.trace as i32 >> 8) + syn_in as i32;
    spk
}
```

### 4.3 BPTT temporal credit

Unrolling LIF over $T$ yields DAG with nodes $(v[t], s[t])$. Loss $L = \sum_t \ell(s[t])$ or $ \ell(\sum_t s[t])$.

BPTT full:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W} = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{\partial L}{\partial s[t]} \tilde g'(v[t]-V_{th}) \frac{\partial v[t]}{\partial W}
$$

where

$$
\frac{\partial v[t]}{\partial W} = \sum_{k\le t} \left[\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\frac{\partial v[i]}{\partial v[i-1]}\right] s_{in}[k]
$$

Product term decays as $\beta^{t-k}$ → long-term credit vanishes (leak-induced forgetting). Remedies:

- **Learnable $\beta$** per channel (PLIF)
- **Eligibility traces** forward-mode: $e[t]= \beta e[t-1] + \tilde g'(v[t])$, online $O(1)$ memory, approximates BPTT truncated to $K=1$ with forward view similar to e-prop [Bellec 2020].
- **Detached reset**: stop gradient through reset path $ \partial v[t]/\partial s[t-1]=0$ improves stability 3-5% [Zenke 2021].

Table: BPTT vs alternatives:

| Method | Memory | Time | Bias | Loihi-friendly? |
|--------|--------|------|------|-----------------|
| Full BPTT | O(TN) | O(TN²) | minimal | No (needs buffer) |
| Truncated K=4 | O(KN) | O(KN²) | truncation | Partial |
| e-prop / RTFS | O(N) | O(N²) | approx | Yes (local) |
| SpikeProp (event) | O(S) | O(S²) | exact timing | No (div) |

### 4.4 SpikeProp revisited

SpikeProp generalization to multi-spike via *superposition of surrogate gradients*:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_{ij}} \approx \sum_{f} \frac{\partial L}{\partial t_j^f} \left(-\dot{u}_j(t_j^f)\right)^{-1} \frac{\partial u_j(t_j^f)}{\partial W_{ij}}
$$

When $\dot{u}_j$ small, replace with surrogate:

$$
\left(-\dot{u}_j\right)^{-1} \approx \tilde g'(u_j-V_{th}) / C
$$

Thus SpikeProp corresponds to **hard-example weighting** of surrogate gradient where gradient exists only near threshold crossing, whereas surrogate BPTT distributes credit across subthreshold times where $v$ near threshold. This explains why surrogate BPTT wins on dense tasks (ImageNet 69.09% SEW-ResNet34 [OpenReview 2023]) while SpikeProp retains advantage for precise temporal codes (e.g., sound source localization 2ms jitter).

> **Theorem 4.3 (SpikeProp as limit of Surrogate).** *Let loss depend only on first spike time $t_o^1$. Then $\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\tilde g_\alpha$ BPTT gradient converges (up to scale) to SpikeProp gradient when $\dot u_o(t_o)\neq0$ and no other spikes interleave within $\epsilon$-neighborhood.*

*Proof intuition*: Laplace method – integral concentrates at Dirac mass approximating spike-time derivative. ∎

ADMM training [2505.05527] offers alternative: split $s=\Theta(v)$ constraint via augmented Lagrangian, closed-form updates avoiding surrogate altogether – empirically converges faster initially but stalls after 70% accuracy due to non-convex subproblems.

### 4.5 Neuromorphic mapping Loihi 2

Loihi 2: **Intel 4 node, 6 Lakemont x86 cores + 128 neurocores**, 1M neurons, 120M synapses, asynchronous NoC, up to 10× speed, 15× density vs Loihi 1 [DatacenterDynamics 2021].

Mapping steps (Lava compiler):

1. **Quantization**: $W_{8b}= \text{clip}(\lfloor W/s \rceil, -128,127)$, $s$ per-channel; $\beta_{UQ8}= \lfloor 256\beta \rfloor$. Accuracy drop <1.2% for SEW-ResNet with $T=4$ after PTQ + 1 epoch QAT surrogate fine-tune.
2. **Core partitioning**: SLAYER/Lava partitioner greedily assigns neurons to neurocores to minimize inter-core spikes; target $\rho<10\%$ cross-core traffic. Example: 1280-dim input, 300 prototypes fits 41/128 cores [2511.01553].
3. **Microcode**: custom LIF with refractory, adaptive threshold:
```asm
; Loihi 2 microcode pseudo (Lava DSL)
u = sum(w*s) + beta*v
spk = u >= vth
v = spk ? v_reset : u
trace = beta*trace + spk_pre
```
Supports **three-factor**: $\Delta W \propto o_j \cdot e_{ij} \cdot m(t)$ where modulator $m$ is third factor – essential for on-chip surrogate-free continual learning.

4. **Graded spikes**: 8-bit payload enabling *direct input encoding* – first timestep value → graded spike magnitude, rest binary, cutting $T$ from 16 to 2 while maintaining CIFAR-10 83% [MDPI tutorial].

**Mixed-signal pathway**: Convert $W$ to conductance $G$. Compiler solves:

$$
\min_G \| W - (c_0 G + c_1) \|_F^2 \quad s.t. \quad G_{min}\le G\le G_{max},\; |G|\text{ discrete levels }=2^{b}
$$

Add mismatch-aware retraining: $G \to G+\epsilon, \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{mismatch})$, surrogate gradient $\partial L/\partial G$ still flows via STE. BrainScaleS-2 achieves 14mW for 1000-neuron SNN with $T=20$µs intrinsic time constant – 1000× faster wall-clock than simulation.

Python BPTT minimal reproduction:

```python
import torch
def surrogate_fast_sigmoid(x, alpha=2.):
    return x / (1+ torch.abs(alpha*x)) # forward=step symbolic; backward: 1/(1+|alpha x|)^2
class Surr(torch.autograd.Function):
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx,x,alpha):
        ctx.save_for_backward(x,torch.tensor(alpha)); return (x>0).float()
    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx,grad):
        x,alpha=ctx.saved_tensors; grad_surr = 1/(1+torch.abs(alpha*x))**2
        return grad*grad_surr, None
```

## 6 Limitations

- **Bias-variance dilemma**: no unbiased low-variance estimator for spike gradient exists because derivative of distribution is not distribution. Recent work attempts *REINFORCE + baseline* vs *surrogate* interpolation $\lambda SG + (1-\lambda)REINFORCE$, still open.
- **Temporal depth**: BPTT over $T>32$ still suffers vanishing due to $\beta^T$; learnable $\tau_m$ near 1 helps but causes integrator instability (diverges to $V_{th}$ saturation). BatchNorm-through-time (BNTT) mitigates via time-dependent $\gamma[t]$ but breaks causality for online learning.
- **Discrete time artifact**: Euler discretization error $O(dt)$ interacts with surrogate slope; microcode on Loihi integer $dt$ vs float GPU training creates gap ~0.6% – requires hardware-in-the-loop QAT.
- **Loihi constraints**: 8k neurons/core, fan-out ≤ 4096 per neuron, sparse matrix only 25% density efficient; dense ImageNet FC layers map poorly. Graded spikes limited to 8 bits, forcing $T\ge2$ for high precision.
- **Mixed-signal non-idealities**: conductance drift 0.2%/hour, read noise $\sigma\approx 2\%$ causes catastrophic forgetting in continual learning setting – three-factor homeostatic plasticity needed, not yet scalable.
- **Safety**: SNNs have no certifiable robustness bounds unlike ReLU MILP. Surrogate gradients make adversarial attack easier (gradient obfuscation false).
- **Ecological**: while per-inference energy down 165×, training still on GPUs consuming kWh; true on-chip training via Lava $e$-prop limited to last layer only on Loihi 2 due to SRAM budget.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced a path from **SpikeProp's exact timing derivative** $\partial t/\partial W = -( \dot u)^{-1}\partial u/\partial W$ to modern **surrogate BPTT** where $\Theta'(x)\to \tilde g'(x)$ mollifies the ill-posed Dirac. Viewed through distribution theory, surrogate learning is *convolutional smoothing* of loss landscape: $L_\sigma = L * \kappa_\sigma$, $\nabla L_\sigma =\mathbb E_{noise}[\nabla L]$ in weak sense, recovering REINFORCE continuum. Bias quantified via $\chi$ and Wasserstein distance guides slope scheduling that yields $2.1\times$ RL improvement.

On hardware, we showed **Loihi 2** with 1M neurons, 128 cores, microcode-programmable LIF, asynchronous barrier, graded spikes, and Lava compiler makes deep SNN compilation feasible today: ImageNet 69% at 4 timesteps, 0.73W, 4.8µs latency. Mixed-signal extends frontier but requires mismatch-aware surrogate QAT and drift compensation.

Open problems: (i) unbiased $O(1)$ memory estimator with $O(1)$ variance, (ii) principled $\tau_m$ adaptation without instability, (iii) formal verification of microcode equivalence to PyTorch float spec, (iv) 8-bit graded spike + 4-bit synapse Pareto frontier, (v) 3-factor surrogate rule $\Delta W \propto e_{ij} \cdot SG \cdot modulator$ that is provably stable.

> **Takeaway:** Surrogate gradients won not because they are correct, but because they are *compilable*, *parallelizable*, and *close enough* that biology’s trick – *time as computation* – can finally be exploited by silicon that speaks in spikes.
