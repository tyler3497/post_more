# Persistent Homology and the Mapper Construction: Filtrations, Stability Theorems, and Topological Data Analysis

## Abstract

Topological data analysis (TDA) extracts multiscale, coordinate-free shape descriptors from finite data through functorial constructions of algebraic topology. This thesis develops the two pillars of the field—persistent homology and the Mapper construction—from first principles to stability theory and scientific application. We build filtrations of simplicial complexes from point clouds via the Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and alpha constructions, define persistence modules and their interval decomposition through the structure theorem of Zomorodian–Carlsson and Crawley-Boevey, and prove the bottleneck-stability theorem of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer together with the isometry theorem equating bottleneck and interleaving distances. We then confront multiparameter persistence, where the failure of any barcode forces new invariants such as the rank invariant and fibered barcodes, and present the Mapper algorithm—filter, cover, cluster, nerve—as a computable approximation to the Reeb graph grounded in the Nerve Theorem. We survey applications in neuroscience, materials science, and cancer genomics, and close with a frank account of computational and methodological limitations. [1][2][3][7]

## 1. Introduction

Modern data sets are high-dimensional and noisy, motivating descriptors invariant under reparametrization, robust to noise, and sensitive to global structure—clusters, loops, voids, flares. *Topology* is the natural language for such descriptors, and *persistent homology* is the engine that made it computable [2].

The fundamental dilemma is *scale*: a finite point cloud is topologically just a discrete set. Thicken points to balls of radius $\varepsilon$ and the picture changes with $\varepsilon$—many components, then loops, then contractibility. No single scale is correct, so persistent homology tracks homological features *across all scales*, recording each feature's birth and death scales [3].

> **Historical note.** Topological persistence was introduced by Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian (2002) for surface simplification; the algebraic framework of persistence modules and their barcode decomposition was established by Zomorodian and Carlsson (2005); the field crystallized with Carlsson's "Topology and Data" survey (2009) and the textbook of Edelsbrunner and Harer (2010) [2][8].
## 2. Background

### 2.1 Simplicial complexes and homology

A **$k$-simplex** is the convex hull of $k+1$ affinely independent points. An **abstract simplicial complex** $K$ is a collection of finite vertex sets closed under taking subsets: if $\sigma \in K$ and $\tau \subseteq \sigma$, then $\tau \in K$. Working over a field $\mathbb{F}$ (in practice $\mathbb{F}_2 = \mathbb{Z}/2$, which kills orientation signs and torsion subtleties), the **chain group** $C_k(K)$ is the $\mathbb{F}$-vector space with basis the $k$-simplices, and the **boundary map** $\partial_k : C_k \to C_{k-1}$ sends a simplex to the alternating sum of its faces. The fundamental identity $\partial_{k} \circ \partial_{k+1} = 0$ yields the **homology groups**

$$H_k(K) \;=\; \ker \partial_k \;\big/\; \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1},$$

whose dimensions $\beta_k = \dim H_k$ are the **Betti numbers**: $\beta_0$ counts connected components, $\beta_1$ counts independent loops, $\beta_2$ counts enclosed voids. The alternating sum $\chi(K) = \sum_k (-1)^k \beta_k$ is the Euler characteristic, a coarser invariant.

Two facts make this machinery practical for data:

- **Functoriality.** A simplicial map $f : K \to L$ induces linear maps $H_k(f) : H_k(K) \to H_k(L)$, so nested complexes produce nested algebraic data.
- **The Nerve Theorem.** If $\mathcal{U} = \{U_i\}$ is a *good cover* of a space $Y$ (all finite intersections contractible), then the nerve $\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})$—the complex whose simplices record nonempty intersections—is homotopy equivalent to $Y$. This is the theoretical backbone of both the Čech construction and the Mapper algorithm [1].

Different data modalities demand different filtrations, summarized in Table 1.

|---|---|---|

| Grayscale image | Sublevel sets of intensity $f^{-1}(-\infty, t]$ | threshold $t$ |
| Network / graph | Clique complex of thresholded adjacency | edge weight $\tau$ |
| Time series | Sliding-window embedding + Rips | window length, $\varepsilon$ |
| Scalar field on mesh | Lower-star filtration of vertex function | function value |

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Constructing the filtration

For a finite metric space $(X, d)$, the three canonical constructions are:

1. **Vietoris–Rips.** $\mathrm{VR}_\varepsilon(X) = \{\sigma \subseteq X : \operatorname{diam}(\sigma) \leq \varepsilon\}$. A simplex enters as soon as *all pairwise distances* among its vertices are at most $\varepsilon$. It is determined entirely by the 1-skeleton (a flag complex), hence by the distance matrix alone.
2. **Čech.** $\check{\mathrm{C}}_\varepsilon(X) = \{\sigma : \bigcap_{x \in \sigma} B(x, \varepsilon/2) \neq \varnothing\}$. By the Nerve Theorem, $\check{\mathrm{C}}_\varepsilon$ is homotopy equivalent to the union of balls—*exactly* the right topology—but requires checking higher-order intersections.
3. **Alpha.** The nerve of balls intersected with Voronoi cells. In $\mathbb{R}^d$ it has size $O(n^{\lceil d/2 \rceil})$ and the *exact* homotopy type of the union of balls, at the cost of requiring Euclidean coordinates and a Delaunay triangulation.

They interleave: $\check{\mathrm{C}}_\varepsilon \subseteq \mathrm{VR}_\varepsilon \subseteq \check{\mathrm{C}}_{2\varepsilon}$ (up to the radius/diameter convention), so Rips and Čech persistence are $2$-approximations of each other in the interleaving distance. In practice Rips wins: it needs only pairwise distances, and the clique structure admits aggressive algorithmic optimization [2].

### 3.2 From filtration to barcode: the reduction algorithm

Order all simplices by (diameter, dimension, arbitrary tiebreak) to obtain a total order $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n$ compatible with the filtration. Build the **boundary matrix** $D$ with $D[i,j] = 1$ iff $\sigma_i$ is a codimension-one face of $\sigma_j$. Reducing $D$ to column-echelon form by left-to-right column additions (over $\mathbb{F}_2$) pairs simplices:

- If column $j$ reduces to zero, $\sigma_j$ is **positive**: it creates a homology class (birth at its filtration value).
- If $\mathrm{low}(j) = i$ (the lowest nonzero row), $\sigma_j$ is **negative**: it destroys the class created by $\sigma_i$ (death at its value, birth at $\sigma_i$'s value).

This is the **Elder Rule**: when two components merge, the younger dies and the elder persists. The pairs $(b, d)$ form the persistence diagram; drawing each as a horizontal bar $[b, d)$ yields the **barcode**. The naive algorithm is $O(n^3)$, but the *twist* (clearing) optimization of Chen and Kerber and the *apparent pairs* shortcut make real instances far faster. **Ripser** (Bauer, 2017) computes Rips persistence via *cohomology* with implicit matrix representation, handling thousands of points where naive reduction would choke [2][9].

## 4. Deep Technical Dive

### 4.1 Simplicial Filtrations: Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and Alpha Complexes

The choice of complex construction is the single most consequential modeling decision in a TDA pipeline. Table 2 compares the three standards.

| | Vietoris–Rips | Čech | Alpha |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input needed | pairwise distances | pairwise distances (+ higher intersections) | Euclidean coordinates |
| Homotopy type | $2$-approximation of union of balls | *equal* to union of balls (Nerve Thm.) | *equal* to union of balls |
| Size (n points, dim ≤ k) | $O(n^{k+1})$ worst case | $O(n^{k+1})$ worst case | $O(n^{\lceil d/2\rceil})$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ |
| Flag (clique) complex | yes | no | no |
| Software | Ripser, GUDHI, giotto-tda | GUDHI | GUDHI, Dionysus |

*Table 2: Comparison of simplicial constructions for point-cloud filtrations [2].*

The Rips complex's flag property is decisive: $\mathrm{VR}_\varepsilon$ is *fully determined by its graph*, so only $\binom{n}{2}$ distances are stored and simplices are enumerated lazily by diameter. The price is geometric inexactness—Rips can hallucinate high-dimensional simplices from pairwise data—but the interleaving $\check{\mathrm{C}}_\varepsilon \subseteq \mathrm{VR}_\varepsilon \subseteq \check{\mathrm{C}}_{2\varepsilon}$ bounds diagram perturbation by a factor of two in bottleneck distance; where that slack is unacceptable, the alpha complex (low ambient dimension) or witness complexes (landmark-subsampled data) are the principled alternatives [1][2].

### 4.2 Persistence Modules, the Structure Theorem, and the Elder Rule

The correct algebraic object is not the diagram but the **persistence module**: a functor $V : (\mathbb{R}, \leq) \to \mathbf{Vect}_\mathbb{F}$, i.e., a family of vector spaces $V_t$ with linear maps $v_{s,t} : V_s \to V_t$ satisfying $v_{t,u} \circ v_{s,t} = v_{s,u}$. The homology of a filtration, $V_t = H_k(K_t)$, is the canonical example. The elementary building blocks are **interval modules** $\mathbb{I}[b, d)$ with $(\mathbb{I}[b,d))_t = \mathbb{F}$ for $b \leq t < d$ and $0$ otherwise, identity maps inside the interval.

> **Theorem: (Structure theorem; Zomorodian–Carlsson 2005, Crawley-Boevey 2012).** Every pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module over a totally ordered set decomposes uniquely, up to permutation, as a direct sum of interval modules: $V \cong \bigoplus_{j} \mathbb{I}[b_j, d_j)$.

The multiset $\{(b_j, d_j)\}$ *is* the persistence diagram (with $d_j = \infty$ allowed, drawn as arrows to infinity). The theorem is the precise sense in which "the barcode is a complete invariant"—and its *failure* in the multiparameter setting (Section 4.4) is the central drama of modern TDA.

The Elder Rule has a clean categorical reading: when the map $v_{s,t}$ merges two classes born at $b_1 < b_2$, the class born later dies, and the survivor's birth is the *minimum*—the elder persists. In Haskell-flavored types, the pipeline is:

```haskell
-- A persistence module over a totally ordered index
data PersistenceModule t v = PM
  { spaces :: t -> v            -- V_t
  , trans  :: t -> t -> (v -> v) -- v_{s,t}, defined for s <= t
  }

-- Interval module I[b,d): the atoms of the structure theorem
intervalModule :: (Ord t, Field f) => t -> Maybe t -> PersistenceModule t f
intervalModule b d = PM
  { spaces = \t -> if b <= t && maybe True (t <) d then one else zero
  , trans  = \s t -> if b <= s && maybe True (t <) d then id else const zero
  }

-- The barcode is the multiset of (birth, death) pairs of the decomposition
barcode :: PersistenceModule t v -> [(t, Maybe t)]
barcode = map (\(b, d) -> (b, d)) . decompose  -- decompose: the structure theorem
```

Two consequences matter. First, the decomposition is *canonical*: the multiset of intervals depends on no choices, so the diagram is a well-defined invariant of the filtration. Second, everything is over a *field*; over $\mathbb{Z}$ the classification is wild, which is why TDA universally uses $\mathbb{F}_2$ or $\mathbb{Q}$ coefficients, accepting the loss of torsion information.

### 4.3 Bottleneck Stability: Cohen–Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer and the Isometry Theorem

A descriptor is useless for science if infinitesimal input noise produces macroscopic output changes. The stability theorem is what elevates persistence diagrams from mathematical curiosities to measurement instruments. Let $\mathrm{Dgm}_k(f)$ be the $k$-dimensional diagram of the sublevel-set filtration of a tame function $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$. The **bottleneck distance** between diagrams $D, D'$ is

$$d_B(D, D') \;=\; \inf_{\gamma} \sup_{x \in D} \lVert x - \gamma(x)\rVert_\infty,$$

where $\gamma$ ranges over bijections between $D$ and $D'$ after adjoining the diagonal $\Delta = \{(t,t)\}$ with infinite multiplicity—points may be matched to the diagonal at cost $(d-b)/2$, i.e., short bars are cheap to kill.

> **Theorem: (Bottleneck stability; Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, Harer 2007).** Let $X$ be triangulable and $f, g : X \to \mathbb{R}$ tame. Then for every homological dimension $k$,
>
> $$d_B\big(\mathrm{Dgm}_k(f),\, \mathrm{Dgm}_k(g)\big) \;\leq\; \lVert f - g\rVert_\infty.$$

In words: the diagram map is **1-Lipschitz** from the sup norm to the bottleneck metric [3]. The proof proceeds by interpolating $f_t = (1-t)f + tg$ and tracking how diagram points move: births and deaths vary continuously, points can only be created or destroyed at the diagonal, and a global matching is assembled from local ones. Cohen-Steiner et al. (2010) extend this to Wasserstein distances $d_{W,p}$ under bounded total persistence, and the *isometry theorem* (Lesnick; Bauer–Lesnick) identifies bottleneck distance with the **interleaving distance** between persistence modules—so the diagram metric is the canonical one [4][5].

The statistical payoff is immediate: Chazal et al. established stochastic convergence of persistence landscapes and silhouettes, giving diagrams a rigorous inferential footing—confidence bands, hypothesis tests, and Fréchet means on diagram space [6].

### 4.4 Multiparameter Persistence: the Failure of the Barcode

Real data often carry *several* natural scale parameters—density and radius, time and threshold. A **multiparameter persistence module** is a functor $V : (\mathbb{R}^n, \leq) \to \mathbf{Vect}$, $n \geq 2$, where representation theory turns *wild*: no complete discrete invariant like the barcode exists, by theorem rather than by lack of imagination.

What survives:

1. **The rank invariant** (Carlsson–Zomorodian 2009): $\rho_V(s, t) = \operatorname{rank}(v_{s,t})$ for $s \leq t$. Incomplete, but computable and stable, and it determines the module on a dense open set of "generic" modules.
2. **The matching distance** (Cerri et al.): foliate $\mathbb{R}^n$ by lines of positive slope, compute 1-D bottleneck distances along each leaf, and take a weighted supremum. Stable and computable via finitely many critical lines.
3. **Fibered barcodes and RIVET** (Lesnick–Wright): restrict the module to each line in the foliation, where the 1-D structure theorem *does* apply, and visualize the resulting barcode as it varies continuously over the space of lines. This is currently the most practical exploratory tool.
4. **Signed barcodes** (Botnan–Oppermann–Oudot): a signed measure decomposition generalizing the rank invariant with an inclusion–exclusion flavor, yielding a Hilbert-space embedding [1][8].

Stability persists in the interleaving formulation—multiparameter modules are stable under perturbations of the filtering functions—but the *computational* story is harder: no general algorithm computes a minimal presentation in the 1-D sense, and software (RIVET) is interactive rather than batch. Multiparameter persistence remains the field's most important open frontier: the mathematics says the invariant must be richer, but the canonical rich invariant has not been found.

### 4.5 The Mapper Construction, Covers, and Reeb Graphs

While persistent homology is a *global* descriptor, **Mapper** (Singh–Mémoli–Carlsson 2007) returns an interpretable graph summarizing data shape from local clustering [7]. Given a point cloud $X$, the algorithm is:

1. **Filter (lens).** Choose $f : X \to Z$ (often $Z = \mathbb{R}^d$, $d$ small): a projection, density estimate, eccentricity, or the output of a dimensionality-reduction method. The lens decides *which* structure is exposed.
2. **Cover.** Cover $f(X)$ by overlapping sets $\{U_i\}$—typically intervals with *resolution* $N$ (number of bins) and *gain* $g$ (fractional overlap).
3. **Cluster.** For each $i$, cluster the preimage $f^{-1}(U_i)$ (single-linkage, DBSCAN, $k$-means—any algorithm). Each cluster becomes a node.
4. **Nerve.** Connect nodes whose clusters share points; fill higher simplices for multi-way intersections. The 1-skeleton is the **Mapper graph**.
## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

### 5.1 Neuroscience

The brain is a natural TDA domain: neural activity lives on low-dimensional manifolds (head-direction rings, grid-cell tori) invisible to linear methods. Giusti et al. applied *clique topology*—persistent homology of the order complex of a correlation matrix—to hippocampal recordings and recovered geometric organization matching the animal's physical environment [8][9]. Dabaghian et al. used persistent cocycles to extract *circular coordinates* from place-cell spike trains, reconstructing the topology of the traversed space from neural data alone. Here the stability theorem does the epistemic work: it licenses the inference that a long bar reflects the tissue, not the noise.

### 5.2 Materials science

Hiraoka et al. (2016, PNAS) computed persistent homology of atomic configurations in amorphous silica and found hierarchical ring structures—medium-range order—that conventional descriptors (radial distribution functions, bond-angle statistics) cannot resolve; the $H_1$ diagram distinguished glassy from liquid states across the glass transition [8]. Kramar et al. showed that persistence-based features predict bulk and shear moduli of granular packings. The mechanism is generic: persistence diagrams are *isometry-invariant* summaries of multi-body correlations, exactly the information that two-point statistics discard.

### 5.3 Genomics, time series, and sensor networks

- **Cancer genomics.** The Mapper-derived breast-cancer subgroup of Nicolau et al. remains the flagship: topology-guided stratification with direct clinical consequence [7].
- **Time series.** Sliding-window embeddings turn a periodic signal into a point cloud sampling a torus; persistent $H_1$ detects periodicity robustly, with applications from gene-expression cycles to wheeze detection [2].
- **Sensor networks.** De Silva and Ghrist's homological coverage criterion certifies blanket coverage from *connectivity data alone* via a relative $H_2$ class; persistent versions tolerate node failure [2].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Combinatorial explosion.** Rips persistence is exponential in the worst case; beyond a few thousand points one must subsample, sparsify, or accept approximate barcodes. Alpha complexes dodge this only in low ambient dimension.
2. **Mapper parameter sensitivity.** Resolution, gain, lens, and clustering algorithm jointly determine the output graph, and there is no universal selection principle—only stability heuristics and domain knowledge. Two defensible parameter choices can tell different stories.
3. **The multiparameter gap.** Without a barcode, multiparameter summaries are either incomplete (rank invariant), expensive (matching distance), or interactive (RIVET). The theory is ahead of the software.
4. **Statistical immaturity.** Pointwise convergence results exist [6], but confidence sets for diagrams and multiple-testing corrections over bar lengths are still developing. Practitioners often threshold bar lengths by eye—a heuristic licensed by stability, but a heuristic.
5. **Metric dependence.** Everything downstream depends on the input metric. For data without a canonical distance (text, graphs with learned embeddings), the filtration is a modeling choice, and different choices can dominate the topological signal.
6. **Interpretation of short bars.** Stability says short bars are *unstable*, not that they are meaningless: in some regimes (e.g., fractal or highly textured data) the near-diagonal texture carries the signal. The "long bars = signal" slogan is a useful default, not a theorem.

## 7. Conclusion

Persistent homology turned a classical impossibility—computing with infinite-dimensional topological invariants—into a practical pipeline: build a filtration, reduce a matrix, read a barcode, and trust the result because bottleneck stability and the isometry theorem say you may. Mapper complements it with an interpretable Reeb-flavored summary whose empirical record includes genuine scientific discovery. Open frontiers: invariant theory for multiparameter persistence, statistical methodology matching the theory's rigor, and software scaling to contemporary data sets. Topology will not replace statistics or machine learning; but as a source of robust, coordinate-free, multiscale features, it has earned a permanent place in the data scientist's toolkit [1][2][8].
