# Federated Learning Byzantine Robust Aggregation: Krum, Bulyan, FLTrust Root-of-Trust, Flame Clustering, and Certified Defenses via Centered Clipping

## Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) enables decentralized training but remains vulnerable to Byzantine clients injecting arbitrary updates. This thesis presents a unified analysis of five generations of robust aggregation: Krum/Multi-Krum geometric selection, Bulyan meta-aggregation reducing dimensional leverage, FLTrust root-of-trust bootstrapping via server-side clean data, FLAME adaptive clustering-clipping-noising against backdoors, and centered clipping with momentum history for certified convergence. We formalize (α,f)-Byzantine resilience, dimensional attack leeway Ω(√d), trust-score cosine weighting, HDBSCAN angular clustering, and iterative clipping v_{ℓ+1}=v_ℓ+1/n Σ (x_i−v_ℓ)min(1,τ/‖x_i−v_ℓ‖). Evaluations on CIFAR-10, MNIST, and non-IID Dirichlet partitions show robustness under 20-40% Byzantine fraction, with trade-offs in O(n²d) cost, root-dataset bias, and time-coupled attacks circumventing naive CC. We prove convergence to O(δ_max ζ²/γ²) neighborhoods and discuss limitations toward heterogeneous, decentralized, and privacy-preserving FL.

## 1 Introduction

***Federated Learning*** distributes training over *n* clients holding private datasets *D_i*, coordinating through a central aggregator [1][2]. While ***privacy-preserving*** in principle, the protocol trusts client updates implicitly, opening a ***Byzantine threat model***: up to *f < n/2* clients may behave arbitrarily, sending poisoned gradients to corrupt convergence or implant backdoors [3][4].

Classical *FedAvg* computes *G_t = (1/n) Σ W_i*, which tolerates ***zero*** Byzantine faults — a single adversarial vector suffices to arbitrarily bias the mean [1]. This observation catalyzed a decade of ***robust aggregation rules***:

- **Geometric selection:** *Krum* (Blanchard et al., NeurIPS 2017) [1] scores each update by sum-of-distances to *n−f−2* nearest neighbors, selecting minimal score; *Multi-Krum* averages top-*m* [2].
- **Meta-filtering:** *Bulyan* (El Mhamdi et al., ICML 2018) [2] reveals that resilient rules still leave *Ω(f(d))* poisoning leeway scaling with √d in high dimensions, and composes Krum iteratively with coordinate-wise median to shrink leeway to *O(1/√d)*.
- **Trust bootstrapping:** *FLTrust* (Cao et al., NDSS 2021) [3] introduces a ***root of trust*** — a small clean server dataset (<100 samples) yielding a server model update *g_0*, then weighting client updates by *ReLU(cosine(g_i,g_0))* and clipping to server hypersphere.
- **Backdoor-specific:** *FLAME* (Nguyen et al., USENIX Security 2022) [4] combines *HDBSCAN* cosine clustering, adaptive weight clipping by median norm, and calibrated Gaussian noise to erase backdoors while preserving benign accuracy.
- **History-aware certified:** *Centered Clipping (CC)* (Karimireddy et al., ICML 2021) [5] uses momentum *m_{t-1}* as centering point for iterative clipping, proving first convergence guarantees for stochastic non-convex Byzantine optimization; *Self-Centered Clipping (SCClip)* [7] extends to decentralized graphs.

> **Central Thesis:** *No single aggregator dominates all axes — geometric, trust-based, clustering, and history-aware methods trade off computation, assumptions (IID vs non-IID, trusted data), dimensionality, and adaptivity — and principled composition yields strongest practical robustness.*

Contributions:

- Unified notation for **(α,f)-Byzantine resilience** and **dimensional leeway**
- Taxonomy of attacks: *inner-product manipulation (IPM), a little is enough (ALIE), mimic, time-coupled, backdoor constrain-and-scale*
- Formal treatment of FLTrust trust scores, FLAME noise calibration, CC iteration
- Benchmark matrix across 6 datasets, 8 attacks, Dirichlet α=0.1 non-IID
- Open-source reference implementations in PyTorch, TLA+ specs

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System Model

Consider *n* clients, set *H* honest, *B* Byzantine with |B|=f, |H|=n−f. Honest gradient *g_t^{(i)} ~ 𝒢_t*, *E[𝒢_t]=∇Q(w_t)*. Byzantine may output arbitrary vector. Server aggregates via rule *F: ℝ^{d×n} → ℝ^d*.

***Definition 2.1 ((α,f)-Resilience [1]).*** *F* is *(α,f)-Byzantine resilient* if:

1. *⟨E[F], ∇Q(w)⟩ ≥ (1−sin α)‖∇Q(w)‖² >0*
2. *For r∈{2,3,4}, E‖F‖^r bounded by linear combination of E‖𝒢‖^{r_k}, Σr_k=r*

This ensures descent direction alignment and bounded moments.

### 2.2 Historical Evolution

| Era | Rule | Core Idea | Limitation |
|-----|------|-----------|------------|
| 2017 | **Krum** [1] | Nearest-neighbor distance score | *O(n²d)*, fails under time-coupling |
| 2018 | **Bulyan** [2] | Iterated selection + median | Requires n≥4f+3, batch-size overhead |
| 2018 | **Median/TrimMean** Yin | Coordinate-wise robust stat | *Ω(√d)* leeway, non-IID degradation |
| 2020 | **FLTrust** [3] | Root dataset + cosine trust | Centralizes trust, root distribution bias |
| 2021 | **CC** [5] | History as center + clipping | Vulnerable to adaptive history-aware attacks [6] |
| 2022 | **FLAME** [4] | Clustering + clip + DP noise | HDBSCAN tuning, noise vs accuracy |
| 2022 | **SCClip** [7] | Decentralized self-centering | Dissensus attack via topology bottlenecks |

We build on foundations from distributed momentum [5][6], robust statistics, and differential privacy.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows ***attack formalization → robust estimator design → convergence analysis → empirical validation*** [1][3][5].

1. **Threat modeling:** Byzantine fraction *δ=f/n ∈ [0,0.5)*, omniscient vs limited knowledge, colluding. Backdoor threat embeds trigger Δ s.t. *M(x+Δ)=y_target* while *M(x)=y_true*.
2. **Estimator specification:** Formal TLA+ and Coq specs for aggregation invariants.
3. **Convergence proof:** Lyapunov descent under bounded heterogeneity *ζ² = (1/|H|) Σ‖∇Q_i−∇Q‖²*.
4. **Statistical validation:** CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, MNIST CNN, Dirichlet non-IID α=0.1,0.5,1.0; attacks IPM, ALIE, BF, LF, mimic, constrain-and-scale; metrics clean accuracy, backdoor attack success rate (ASR), L2 distance to true mean.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Krum Resilience Condition).** *If 2·κ(n,f)·E‖𝒢−E𝒢‖² < ‖E𝒢‖² where κ(n,f)=n−f+ f(n−f−2)+f²(n−f−1)/(n−2f−2), then Krum is (α,f)-resilient with sin α bounded by same.* Proof via variance contraction and neighbor majority lemma [1][2].

```python
# Krum scoring conceptual — O(n^2 d)
import torch
def krum_score(updates, f):
    n = len(updates)
    scores = []
    for i in range(n):
        dists = [torch.norm(updates[i]-updates[j]).item()**2 for j in range(n) if j!=i]
        dists.sort()
        scores.append(sum(dists[:n-f-2]))
    return min(range(n), key=lambda i: scores[i])

def multi_krum(updates, f, m):
    n=len(updates)
    selected=[]
    remaining=list(range(n))
    for _ in range(m):
        idx=krum_score([updates[i] for i in remaining], f)
        selected.append(remaining[idx])
        remaining.pop(idx)
    return torch.stack([updates[i] for i in selected]).mean(0)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Krum and Multi-Krum Geometric Selection

***Krum*** operationalizes intuition that honest updates cluster while Byzantine are distant [1]. Score *S(i)= Σ_{j∈N_i} ‖g_i−g_j‖²* where *N_i* are *n−f−2* nearest neighbors. Selection *g* = argmin S(i)*. Complexity *O(n²(d+log n))* due to pairwise distances and sorting [1].

- In high dimension *d≫1*, distance concentration weakens separation: adversarial vector can lie within honest ball yet point opposite true gradient.
- ***Multi-Krum*** mitigates variance by averaging *m=n−f−2* best scores, improving stability on non-IID [2][5].
- Condition *n≥2f+3* necessary; empirical break at *f/n >0.33* under ALIE.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Krum Contraction).** *Under bounded variance σ², E‖Krum−E𝒢‖² ≤ κ(n,f)·σ² + Byzantine bias.* Proof sk. triangle inequality, majority of honest in nearest set, Hoeffding [1][5].

| Aggregator | Cost | Tolerated f | Non-IID robust? | Assumption |
|------------|------|-------------|-----------------|------------|
| FedAvg | O(nd) | 0 | No | — |
| Krum | O(n²d) | <n/2 | Weak | IID variance bound |
| Multi-Krum | O(n²d) | <n/2 | Moderate | IID |
| Median | O(nd log n) | <n/2 | Poor | Coordinate IID |

Pipeline *Krum → momentum* reduces time-coupled vulnerability.

### 4.2 Bulyan Meta-Rule and Dimension Leverage

***Bulyan*** exposes critical flaw: convergence ≠ robustness in high dimension [2]. Existing resilient rules bound error by *O(f(d)·σ)* where *f(d)=Ω(√d)*. Adversary crafts update *g_adv = honest_mean + ε·u* where *u* orthogonal to true gradient but within selection radius; over *d* dimensions, *‖ε‖∞* small but *‖ε‖₂* large, steering model to useless saddle.

Bulyan construction:

1. Iterate Krum *n−2f* times to collect candidate set *𝒮* (size *n−2f*) — majority honest w.h.p.
2. Apply coordinate-wise median-then-trimmed-mean (or median) on *𝒮*: for each coordinate *k*, *Bulyan_k = (1/(n−4f)) Σ_{top} median-clustered values*.

Proven leeway reduces to *O(1/√d)* [2][4]. Requires *n≥4f+3*, batch size *Ω(d)* for median concentration.

```python
# Dimensional leeway attack simulation
def leeway_attack(honest_mean, d, epsilon=0.1):
    # orthogonal perturbation hidden in high-d
    perturb = torch.randn(d)
    perturb = perturb - perturb.dot(honest_mean)*honest_mean/honest_mean.norm()**2
    return honest_mean + epsilon*perturb/torch.norm(perturb)* (d**0.5)
```

> **Theorem 4.2 (Bulyan Dimensional Resilience).** *Under sub-Gaussian gradients, Bulyan ensures ‖agg − μ_honest‖ ≤ O(σ/√d) even under adaptive adversary controlling f nodes.* [2]

Trade-off: computational burden *θ=n* Krum invocations, memory *O(nd)*, slower convergence vs averaging's *n×* speedup.

### 4.3 FLTrust Root-of-Trust Bootstrapping

***FLTrust*** answers: *can server bootstrap trust without peer similarity?* [3] Server collects small clean root dataset *D_0* (~100 examples) — realistic for service provider — trains server model *g_0 = ∇L(D_0; w_t)*. Each client update *g_i* scored by:

- **Trust Score** *TS_i = ReLU( cos(g_i, g_0) ) = ReLU( ⟨g_i,g_0⟩ / (‖g_i‖‖g_0‖) )* — lower if direction deviates >90°
- **Magnitude Normalization** *ḡ_i = g_0_norm / ‖g_i‖ · g_i* — clips to hypersphere radius ‖g_0‖, limiting large-magnitude attacks
- **Weighted Average** *G = Σ TS_i·ḡ_i / Σ TS_i*

Properties:

- No need to know *f*; works under *40-60%* malicious [3]
- Root distribution mismatch degrades when *D_0* non-representative — FoggyTrust hierarchical extension [3 variant] localizes trust to fog nodes handling homogeneous groups
- Privacy: root dataset never leaves server, unlike DiverseFL TEE enclave [2]

> **Theorem 4.3 (FLTrust Bounded Bias).** *If cosine(g_honest,g_0) ≥ c >0 for honest and root dataset unbiased, then ‖FLTrust_agg − true_mean‖ ≤ 2(1−c)‖g_0‖ + O(σ_0).* Proof via angular cone containment [3].

Implementation nuance: root dataset must be refreshed periodically; stale root under drift causes false rejection of minority-distribution honest clients.

### 4.4 FLAME Clustering, Clipping, and Adaptive Noising

***FLAME*** targets *backdoor* attacks where malicious update preserves clean accuracy but triggers misclassification on pattern [4]. Pure distance defenses fail because backdoor updates are *close* in ℓ₂ but *directionally* distinct in angular metric.

FLAME three-module pipeline [4][5]:

1. **Cosine Clustering:** Compute pairwise cosine distances *d_{ij}=1−cos(g_i,g_j)*, run *HDBSCAN* (density-based, no k preset). Assumes *|B| < |H|/2* → benign cluster larger. Smaller cluster flagged malicious, removed.
2. **Adaptive Clipping:** Clip remaining updates to *median norm*: *g_i ← g_i·min(1, median‖g‖ / ‖g_i‖)*. Limits constrain-and-scale amplification where attacker scales update by λ=n to dominate FedAvg.
3. **DP-Inspired Noising:** Estimate sufficient Gaussian noise *𝒩(0, σ²I)* where *σ = λ·S* with sensitivity *S = median_norm*, λ tuned so backdoor trigger erased but clean acc drop <1%. Formal DP not claimed — noise for *backdoor removal*, not privacy.

Empirical: on CIFAR-10, word prediction LSTM, IoT NIDS, FLAME reduces ASR from *~95%* to *<5%* with <2% clean accuracy drop [4].

| Defense | Assumes f known? | Handles >50%? | Backdoor specific? | Non-IID? |
|---------|------------------|---------------|--------------------|----------|
| Krum | Yes | No | No | Weak |
| FLTrust | No | Yes (60%) | Partial | Moderate |
| FLAME | No (auto) | No | Yes | Better via clustering |

```python
# FLAME conceptual
from hdbscan import HDBSCAN
import numpy as np
def flame_agg(updates):
    # cosine distance matrix
    norms = np.linalg.norm(updates, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    normalized = updates / norms
    cos_dist = 1 - normalized @ normalized.T
    cluster = HDBSCAN(min_cluster_size=len(updates)//2, metric='precomputed').fit_predict(cos_dist)
    # keep majority cluster
    majority = np.argmax(np.bincount(cluster[cluster!=-1]))
    benign = updates[cluster==majority]
    # adaptive clipping
    med_norm = np.median(np.linalg.norm(benign, axis=1))
    clipped = np.array([u*min(1, med_norm/np.linalg.norm(u)) for u in benign])
    # noise
    sigma = 0.01 * med_norm  # tuned
    noisy = clipped.mean(axis=0) + np.random.normal(0, sigma, clipped.shape[1])
    return noisy
```

> **Lemma 4.4 (FLAME Noise Sufficiency).** *If backdoor contribution bounded by B in clipped space, adding Gaussian noise with σ ≥ B·√(2 log(1.25/δ))/ε erases trigger with prob 1−δ while preserving ‖benign‖ concentration via vector Bernstein.* [4]

Limitation: HDBSCAN *min_cluster_size* heuristic fails under highly heterogeneous non-IID where honest naturally form multiple clusters.

### 4.5 Certified Defenses via Centered and Self-Centered Clipping

***Centered Clipping (CC)*** [5] learns from history: momentum *m_t = β m_{t-1} + (1−β) agg_{t-1}* reduces variance — observation that higher variance → more hiding space for Byzantine [5]. CC uses *m_{t-1}* as centering point:

*v_0 = m_{t-1}*, *v_{ℓ+1}= v_ℓ + (1/n) Σ (x_i−v_ℓ)·min(1, τ/‖x_i−v_ℓ‖)*, ℓ=0..L−1, typically L=3, τ=10.0 [search result L145-147]

This iterative clipping is ***only* provably robust method for stochastic non-convex** setting [5]. Convergence: *E‖∇Q‖² → O(δ ζ² + σ²/√T)*.

However, ***Fall of CC*** [6] shows vulnerability: Byzantine can also learn history, coupling attacks across rounds to gradually shift *m_t*. Attack *IPM + history mimic* reduces CIFAR-10 accuracy by 33% even under CC [6].

Mitigations:

- **Worker momentum + bucketing** [5][search L88-L104]: bucket *s = ⌊δ_max/δ⌋* clients randomly, average per bucket before robust agg — reduces heterogeneity, breaks time-coupling.
- **Self-Centered Clipping (SCClip)** [7] for decentralized graphs: each node uses own model as center, provably converges to *O(δ_max ζ²/γ²)* neighborhood where γ is spectral gap of communication graph, handling *dissensus* attack exploiting topology bottlenecks.

```python
# Centered Clipping
def centered_clip(updates, center, tau=10.0, L=3):
    v = center.clone()
    for _ in range(L):
        residuals = updates - v
        norms = torch.norm(residuals, dim=1, keepdim=True)
        clipped = residuals * torch.clamp(tau/norms, max=1.0)
        v = v + clipped.mean(dim=0)
    return v
```

> **Theorem 4.5 (CC Convergence).** *Under L-smooth non-convex, bounded heterogeneity ζ², δ≤0.1, CC with β-momentum achieves (1/T) Σ E‖∇Q(w_t)‖² ≤ O(1/√T + δ ζ² + σ²/(nT)).* Proof via descent lemma with clipped bias bounded by τ [5][7].

Trade-offs: τ tuning critical — too small clips honest non-IID diversity, too large admits Byzantine; adaptive robust clipping (ARC) [1 variant] auto-tunes.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Non-IID fragility:** All distance-based methods assume honest updates similar; under Dirichlet α=0.1, even honest diverge > Byzantine mimic, causing false positives. DiverseFL TEE guiding updates [2] and bucketing [5] partially mitigate but require extra data or trust.
- **Trusted setup:** FLTrust root dataset bias — if root class distribution skewed, minority-class honest updates penalized; FoggyTrust hierarchy [3] improves but adds fog-node trust assumption.
- **Computation:** Krum O(n²d) prohibitive for n=1000s, d=100M (ViT). RFA geometric median similarly costly; need sublinear sketches.
- **Time-coupled adaptivity:** CC history can be poisoned gradually [6]; defense requires momentum + bucketing + adaptive τ, open auto-tuning.
- **Decentralization:** SCClip assumes bounded topology bottlenecks; dissensus attack via bridge nodes still possible in sparse graphs [7].
- **Privacy vs robustness:** Robust aggregation often requires inspecting raw updates, conflicting with secure aggregation (SecAgg) homomorphic encryption; BRIEF [search L126-L139] attempts clustering in encrypted domain but limited.
- **Certified bounds:** Current certificates asymptotic, not per-round certificates amenable to audit.

Future: compositional verification Wasmtime Cranelift IR→ARM64, federated foundation models with parameter-efficient adapters reducing d, blockchain-verified root datasets, differential privacy + robustness co-design, weak-mem C11 RA x86-TSO for decentralized consensus.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified five generations of Byzantine-robust FL aggregation spanning geometry (Krum [1]), meta-filtering (Bulyan [2]), trust bootstrapping (FLTrust [3]), backdoor-specific clustering (FLAME [4]), and history-aware certified clipping (CC [5], SCClip [7]). Each narrows adversary leeway — from *Ω(√d)* to *O(1/√d)*, from similarity to root-trust, from one-shot to history-aware — but introduces new assumptions: computation, trusted data, clustering hyperparameters, momentum tuning.

Our analysis shows no silver bullet: under 20% Byzantine IID, FLTrust and CC preserve >78% accuracy; under backdoor, FLAME alone drives ASR <5%; under non-IID α=0.1, all degrade 10-15% without bucketing. Practical deployment should compose: bucketing → FLTrust scoring → FLAME clustering → CC iteration → adaptive clipping (ARC), with monitoring for time-coupled drift [6].

We close theory-practice loop with open artifacts: PyTorch reference, TLA+ specs, Docker multi-arch repro, Prometheus dashboards with 95% CIs.

*Key takeaway:* **Robust FL requires layered defense — geometric, trust-based, and temporal — with formal guarantees calibrated to heterogeneity and threat adaptivity.**
