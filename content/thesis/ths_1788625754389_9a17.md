# Quantified Boolean Formula Solving: Q-Resolution, QCDCL, Expansion-Based Refinement, and the PSPACE Landscape

## Abstract

Quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs) extend propositional logic with explicit universal and existential quantification, yielding the canonical PSPACE-complete decision problem and a succinct modeling language for verification, planning, synthesis, and game solving. This thesis presents a unified technical account of the two dominant solving paradigms — search-based quantified conflict-driven clause learning (QCDCL) grounded in the Q-resolution proof system, and expansion-based counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (CEGAR) as pioneered by RAReQS and CAQE — together with the proof-complexity landscape that separates them. We develop the prenex CNF calculus formally: universal and existential reduction, Q-resolution with the existential-pivot restriction, long-distance resolution, and the dual cube calculus; we analyze dependency schemes that relax the linear prefix order during decision-making; and we treat certificate extraction — Skolem and Herbrand functions — and strategy extraction, the proof-theoretic method that converts solver traces into winning strategies and drives exponential lower bounds. Empirical evidence from a decade of QBFEVAL competitions and the QBF Gallery series shows the two paradigms to be empirically and proof-theoretically orthogonal, motivating portfolio and hybrid architectures as the state of the art.

## 1 Introduction

The satisfiability problem for quantified Boolean formulas — the language TQBF of *true* quantified Boolean formulas — occupies a distinguished position in complexity theory: it is the canonical **PSPACE-complete** problem, established by Stockmeyer and Meyer in 1973. Where SAT reasons about a single existential block, QBF allows arbitrary alternation of quantifiers, compressing exponentially larger search spaces into polynomially sized encodings. This succinctness is not merely a theoretical curiosity. Bounded model checking of reactive systems, conformant and conditional planning, reactive synthesis, two-player game solving, and equivalence checking of circuits with environment assumptions all admit natural encodings with both kinds of quantifiers — encodings whose propositional translations would blow up exponentially [9][13].

Despite PSPACE-completeness, modern QBF solvers routinely decide industrially relevant instances with hundreds of thousands of clauses. Two architectural families dominate, and they correspond almost exactly to two families of proof systems. The first family is *search-based*: it lifts the conflict-driven clause learning (CDCL) paradigm of SAT to QBF, producing **QCDCL** solvers whose learning rule is Q-resolution — the calculus of Kleine Büning, Karpinski, and Flögel from 1995. The second family is *expansion-based*: it eliminates quantifiers by Shannon expansion or abstraction refinement, with the clausal-abstraction algorithm of CAQE and the recursive CEGAR scheme of RAReQS as exemplars [10][12]. These families are not merely different implementations of the same idea: proof-complexity results show that expansion-based and resolution-based systems are **exponentially separated** in both directions [5], and competition data confirms the separation empirically — each family uniquely solves substantial subsets of benchmarks [2][11].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Prenex Conjunctive Normal Form

A quantified Boolean formula in *prenex conjunctive normal form* (PCNF) is written

$$\Phi = \hat{Q}.\phi \quad \text{with} \quad \hat{Q} = Q_1 X_1 \, Q_2 X_2 \, \cdots \, Q_n X_n,$$

where each $Q_i \in \{\exists, \forall\}$ is a quantifier, each $X_i$ is a nonempty set of propositional variables (a *quantifier block*), and $\phi$ is a propositional formula in conjunctive normal form — the *matrix*. We write $q(x)$ for the quantifier binding variable $x$ and $\mathrm{lv}(x)$ for the *nesting level* (block index) of $x$. We write $l' < l$ to mean $\mathrm{lv}(l') < \mathrm{lv}(l)$, i.e., $l'$ is bound *outer* (earlier) than $l$. A clause is a disjunction of literals; a cube (term) is a conjunction of literals.

The semantics are recursive: $\forall u.\,\psi$ is true iff $\psi[u/\bot]$ and $\psi[u/\top]$ are both true; $\exists x.\,\psi$ is true iff $\psi[x/\bot]$ or $\psi[x/\top]$ is true. Base cases: the empty conjunction $\top$ (true) and the empty clause $\bot$ (false).

> **Theorem (Stockmeyer–Meyer, 1973).** The problem of deciding whether a PCNF formula is true — TQBF — is PSPACE-complete.

The PSPACE upper bound follows from the recursive evaluation procedure, which needs only linear space.

### 2.2 Game Semantics and Strategy Extraction

A PCNF formula admits an equivalent *two-player game* interpretation: the existential player (Eloise) chooses values for $\exists$-variables, the universal player (Abelard) for $\forall$-variables, in prefix order, and Eloise wins iff the matrix is satisfied under the resulting assignment. Truth of $\Phi$ is equivalent to Eloise possessing a *winning strategy* — a function mapping each existential variable to a Boolean value depending only on the values of outer universal variables. Such strategies are exactly **Skolem functions**; dually, falsity corresponds to **Herbrand functions** for the universal variables [7].

This game view underlies one of the deepest connections between solving and proof theory: *strategy extraction*. Given a proof of a formula in a suitable proof system, one can compute winning strategies for the corresponding player in polynomial time. Balabanov and Jiang showed that from a Q-resolution refutation, a universal winning strategy can be extracted in linear time as a **decision list** [5][7]. Strategy extraction also became a *lower-bound technique*: if every winning strategy for a formula family requires a complex function (e.g., parity is not in AC$^0$), and extraction produces strategies in a weak model, then the proofs must be exponentially large [5].

### 2.3 The Solver Landscape

High-performing QBF solvers generally use QDPLL (a generalization of DPLL) or CEGAR [9]. Prominent systems include **DepQBF** (search-based, dependency-aware) [2], **CAQE** (CEGAR-based clausal abstraction; winner of QBFEVAL 2017, 2018, and 2020) [12], **RAReQS** (recursive abstraction refinement) [10][12], **Qute**, **GhostQ**, **sKizzo** (winner 2005–2007) [9], and **CADET** (2QBF with Skolem extraction). The annual QBFEVAL competition has run since 2004, with instances in QDIMACS, QCIR, or QAIGER formats [9][11].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Universal Reduction and Q-Resolution

The proof system underlying all search-based QBF solving is **Q-resolution** (QRES). Its two rules operate on clauses:

**Universal reduction.** For a clause $C$, define

$$UR(C) := C \setminus \{l \mid l \in L_\forall(C) \text{ and } \forall l' \in L_\exists(C): l' < l\},$$

i.e., a universal literal $l$ may be dropped from $C$ if no existential literal in $C$ is bound *inner* (later) than $l$. Intuitively, if all existential literals of $C$ are decided before $u$, then the universal player can falsify $u$ without affecting satisfiability, so the clause remains valid without it [4].

**Q-resolution.** Given two non-tautological clauses $C_1, C_2$ and a pivot variable $p$ with $q(p) = \exists$, $p \in C_1$, $\neg p \in C_2$, form the *tentative Q-resolvent*

$$C' := (UR(C_1) \setminus \{p\}) \cup (UR(C_2) \setminus \{\neg p\}).$$

If $C'$ is non-tautological, it is the Q-resolvent, written $C_1 \otimes C_2$ [4].

> **Theorem (Kleine Büning–Karpinski–Flögel, 1995).** Q-resolution is sound and refutationally complete for PCNF: $\hat{Q}.\phi \vdash \Box$ (derivation of the empty clause) iff $\hat{Q}.\phi$ is false.

A Q-resolution derivation is naturally represented as a **directed acyclic graph (DAG)**: edges $C'' \to C'$ when $C' = UR(C'')$, and $C_1 \to C'$, $C_2 \to C'$ when $C' = C_1 \otimes C_2$ [4]. This DAG representation is precisely what solvers emit as proof traces for certification.

### 3.2 The Dual Cube Calculus

Clauses refute *false* formulas; for *true* formulas, QCDCL learns **cubes** — conjunctions of literals — by a dual calculus. Where clause learning fires on conflicts (falsified matrix), cube learning fires on *solutions* (satisfied matrix). The dual rules are **cube resolution** (on a universal pivot) and **existential reduction**: a cube $D$ may drop an existential literal $l$ if no universal literal in $D$ is inner to $l$. A derivation of the empty cube witnesses truth [4]. QCDCL thus maintains both a clause database (blocking falsifying assignments) and a cube database (blocking satisfying assignments), and either can terminate the search.

### 3.3 Long-Distance Resolution and Generalizations

Plain Q-resolution requires the resolvent to be non-tautological, which is overly restrictive: when the two parent clauses contain complementary *universal* literals $u$ and $\neg u$ deeper than the pivot, the merge can still be sound. **Long-distance Q-resolution (LDQ-Res)** permits such merges under level conditions, and QCDCL conflict analysis in practice produces LDQ-Res refutations [3]. With dependency schemes, the level conditions relax further.

Lonsing, Egly, and Seidl generalized the *axioms* of Q-resolution, yielding a calculus exponentially more powerful than traditional QRES while leaving the resolution and reduction rules unchanged. Implemented in **DepQBF 6.0**, generalized axioms provide an interface between Q-resolution and arbitrary other proof systems (e.g., integrated SAT solving or bounded preprocessing during search), with substantial measured speedups on application benchmarks [1][2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 QCDCL: Quantified Conflict-Driven Clause Learning

QCDCL lifts the CDCL loop to QBF [2][3][10]. The algorithm maintains a partial assignment built by *decisions* (branching, typically respecting the prefix order) and *propagation*: unit clauses are interpreted *modulo universal reduction*, so a clause that becomes unit only after dropping inner universal literals still propagates. On a **conflict** (some clause falsified), conflict analysis resolves the conflicting clause with reason clauses along the implication graph, applying universal reduction, to derive an *asserting learned clause* — a Q-resolution derivation by construction [3][4]. On a **solution** (matrix satisfied), the dual analysis learns an *asserting cube*. Non-chronological backtracking then jumps to the asserting level, continuing until the empty clause (false) or empty cube (true) is derived.

```python
def universal_reduction(clause, levels, quant):
    exist_lv = [levels[l] for l in clause if quant[l] == 'exists']
    if not exist_lv:
        return clause
    inner = max(exist_lv)
    return [l for l in clause
            if not (quant[l] == 'forall' and levels[l] > inner)]

def qcdcl_loop(phi, prefix):
    assignment, learnt_clauses, learnt_cubes = {}, [], []
    while True:
        status = propagate(phi + learnt_clauses, assignment, prefix)
        if status == 'CONFLICT':
            c = analyze_conflict_qres()
            if c == EMPTY_CLAUSE: return 'FALSE'
            learnt_clauses.append(c); backtrack(asserting_level(c))
        elif status == 'SOLUTION':
            d = analyze_solution_dual()
            if d == EMPTY_CUBE: return 'TRUE'
            learnt_cubes.append(d); backtrack(asserting_level(d))
        else:
            decide(prefix, assignment)
```

The crucial subtlety distinguishing QCDCL from CDCL is that propagation, conflict analysis, and the asserting-level computation must all respect quantifier levels: a learned clause is asserting only if it becomes unit at some level *after* universal reduction, and cube learning is the exact dual with quantifiers swapped [3].

DepQBF's signature feature is **dependency-awareness**: decisions need not strictly follow the linear prefix order if a *dependency scheme* certifies that two variables are independent. DepQBF 6.0 further incorporates generalized axioms, adaptive restarts, and QBCE inprocessing [1][2].

### 4.2 Dependency Schemes

The linear prefix order is a sufficient but not necessary condition for soundness of decisions and reductions. A **dependency scheme** $D$ maps each variable $x$ to a set $D(x)$ of variables on which $x$ *truly* depends (a subset of the variables outer to $x$ in the prefix); any sound scheme must at least respect the trivial scheme. The **standard dependency scheme** (Samer–Szeider) computes dependencies via *resolution paths* through the matrix: $y$ depends on $x$ if there is a chain of clauses connecting them in a way that information can flow from $x$'s block to $y$'s. The **triangle dependency scheme** refines this further.

Dependency schemes pay off in two places. First, *decision making*: DepQBF may branch on variables out of prefix order when the scheme declares them independent, approaching SAT-style VSIDS behavior [2][3]. Second, *stronger reductions*: universal reduction may drop a universal literal $u$ whenever no existential literal *depending on* $u$ remains, even if the linear level order would forbid it.

> **Theorem (Blinkhorn–Beyersdorff et al., 2023).** The formal proof system QCDCL$^{\text{LEV-ORD}}_{\text{RED}}$ — the standard formalization of the QCDCL algorithm with level-ordered decisions and reduction-based propagation — is exponentially separated from its variants with relaxed policies and from LDQ-Res with dependency schemes [3].

In Haskell-flavored pseudocode, the reduction condition with a dependency relation becomes a straightforward filter over the dependency closure:

```haskell
-- | Universal reduction modulo a dependency scheme.
ureduce :: DepScheme -> Clause -> Clause
ureduce dep c =
  let exVars = [v | Lit v <- c, quant v == Exists]
      -- universal literal l is droppable iff no *dependent* existential remains
      droppable (Lit u) = quant u == Forall
                       && all (\x -> u `notElem` dep x) exVars
  in [l | l@(Lit v) <- c, not (droppable l)]
```

### 4.3 Expansion-Based Solving: RAReQS and CAQE

The second paradigm eliminates quantifiers rather than searching assignments. By Shannon expansion,

$$\exists x.\,\phi(x,\dots) \equiv \phi(\bot,\dots) \lor \phi(\top,\dots), \qquad \forall u.\,\phi(u,\dots) \equiv \phi(\bot,\dots) \land \phi(\top,\dots),$$

so a QBF can in principle be reduced to SAT by expanding all variables — at exponential cost. Practical expansion solvers use **counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (CEGAR)** to expand lazily. **RAReQS** (Janota, Klieber, Marques-Silva, Clarke, SAT 2012) pioneered CEGAR-driven recursion with learning: it abstracts the formula by treating inner blocks as unconstrained, solves the abstraction with a SAT solver, and refines using counterexample assignments in a depth-first manner [10][12].

**CAQE** (Rabe–Tentrup, FMCAD 2015) implements *clausal abstraction*: for each quantifier block it maintains a propositional abstraction of the clauses relevant at that level, queries a SAT solver for candidate moves, recursively verifies them against inner blocks, and refines abstractions with learned clauses/cubes on failure. Written in Rust, CAQE won QBFEVAL 2017, 2018, and 2020 [12].

**CAQE** (Rabe–Tentrup, FMCAD 2015) implements *clausal abstraction*: for each quantifier block it maintains a propositional abstraction of the clauses relevant at that level, queries a SAT solver for candidate moves, recursively verifies them against inner blocks, and refines abstractions with learned clauses/cubes on failure. Written in Rust, CAQE won QBFEVAL 2017, 2018, and 2020 [12].

The proof system corresponding to expansion is $\forall$**Exp+Res** (Janota–Marques-Silva, 2015): expand universal variables over complete assignments, then apply propositional resolution to the expansion. Its refinements **IR-calc** and **IRM-calc** (Beyersdorff et al.) allow partial, lazy expansion with annotated literals [5][6].

> **Theorem (Beyersdorff–Chew–Janota, 2015).** Q-Res and $\forall$Exp+Res are *incomparable*: there are formula families with polynomial $\forall$Exp+Res proofs requiring exponential Q-Res proofs (e.g., parity-based formulas), and families with the reverse separation [5].

This orthogonality is the proof-theoretic explanation for the empirical complementarity of the solver families.

### 4.4 Certificates and Strategy Extraction

Solving a formula is often only half the task: in synthesis and verification, the *witness* matters. For a true QBF $\exists \bar{x}\,\forall \bar{u}\,\exists \bar{y}.\,\phi$, a certificate is a set of **Skolem functions** $f_y(\bar{u})$ such that $\phi$ holds for all $\bar{u}$ when each $y$ is replaced by $f_y(\bar{u})$ — the functions *are* the synthesized implementation. For a false QBF, the dual **Herbrand functions** form the countermodel [7].

Balabanov and Jiang (CAV 2011, SAT 2012) showed that Skolem functions are derivable from a *cube-resolution proof* of satisfiability, and — via formula negation — from a clause-resolution proof, decoupling certificate extraction from Skolemization-based solvers. Their QRPcert toolchain extracted certificates for over 90% of solved instances, with QRPcheck validating more than 80% [8]. Strategy extraction for Q-Res produces universal winning strategies as *decision lists* in linear time [5], and extends to $\forall$Exp+Res, IR-calc, IRM-calc, LDQ-Res with dependency schemes, and Merge Resolution [6].

> **Theorem (Balabanov–Jiang, 2012).** Given a Q-Res refutation $\pi$ of $\Phi$, a winning strategy for the universal player — a family of Herbrand functions — is computable in time polynomial (indeed linear) in $|\pi|$, representable as decision lists [5].

This theorem is double-edged: it enables certification, but it also yields lower bounds. The *parity formulas* require any universal winning strategy to compute parity, which decision lists of polynomial size cannot; hence Q-Res refutations of parity formulas are exponentially large — a clean, conceptually novel lower bound [5].

### 4.5 Applications: Synthesis, Planning, Verification

- **Reactive synthesis.** Specifications of the form "for all environment behaviors there exists a controller response" are 2QBF/$\forall\exists$ problems; Skolem functions extracted from proofs are the synthesized controllers [7][9].
- **Planning.** Conformant and conditional planning under uncertainty encode naturally: universal quantifiers range over nondeterministic outcomes, existential quantifiers over plan steps (Rintanen's encodings).
- **Verification.** Bounded model checking of safety properties with environment assumptions, equivalence checking, and design rectification use QBF to quantify over inputs adversarially [7][9].

### 5.2 Proof-Complexity Map

The known separations form a coherent picture [5][6]:

| Lower system | Upper system | Separating family | Technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q-Res | $\forall$Exp+Res | parity formulas | strategy extraction [5] |
| $\forall$Exp+Res | Q-Res | KBKF formulas | circuit lower bounds [5] |
| tree-QCDCL | DAG-Q-Res | — | dag vs. tree |
| Q-Res | LDQ-Res + dep. schemes | — | merged literals [3] |
| Q-Res | Q-Res + gen. axioms | — | exponential speedup [1] |

> **Theorem (Strategy-extraction lower bounds).** If a proof system $P$ admits strategy extraction in a computational model $\mathcal{C}$, and a formula family $\{\Phi_n\}$ has the property that every winning strategy computes a function outside $\mathcal{C}$ (e.g., parity outside AC$^0$), then $P$-proofs of $\{\Phi_n\}$ are superpolynomial [5].

This template — pioneered for Q-Res and generalized to eFrege$+\forall$red, where it connects to major open problems in circuit complexity [6] — is now the standard engine of QBF proof complexity.

### 5.3 Engineering Factors

Beyond proof systems, solver performance hinges on preprocessing (blocked-clause elimination via QBCE, equivalence reduction in HQSpre), incremental solving interfaces [4], and definition-variable placement: moving definition variables inward in the prefix measurably improves CAQE, DepQBF, and GhostQ [12]. A longitudinal study of ten years of QBF solving found prefix-aware treewidth measures to correlate strongly with instance hardness [13].

## 6 Limitations

The field's limitations are both theoretical and practical.

1. **PSPACE is unforgiving.** Unlike SAT, where CDCL is a near-universal winner, no single QBF paradigm dominates; the exponential separations are not artifacts but theorems. Portfolio solvers are an engineering admission of this fact.
2. **Proof checking at scale.** Q-resolution DAGs with millions of vertices are routine; independent checking and certificate validation remain expensive, with validation consuming the majority of certification runtime [8].
3. **Preprocessing soundness.** Aggressive preprocessors must preserve not just satisfiability but the *witness structure* needed for certification; bugs here silently invalidate certificates.
4. **Dependency-scheme cost.** Computing strong dependency schemes costs time not always repaid in search savings; DepQBF therefore uses them selectively [2][3].
5. **Beyond prenex.** Most theory and tooling assume PCNF; non-prenex, non-clausal, and DQBF (Henkin quantifiers) settings lack comparably mature solving and certification pipelines [6].
6. **Synthesis scalability.** Extracted Skolem functions are often enormous (millions of AND-gates); minimizing them for deployment remains a bottleneck [8].

---

## 7 Conclusion

QBF solving has matured from 1998-era QDPLL backtracking into a rich ecosystem where proof theory and engineering advance in lockstep. Q-resolution and its long-distance, dependency-aware, and generalized-axiom extensions ground QCDCL; expansion and clausal abstraction provide an orthogonal, CEGAR-driven foundation; strategy extraction binds proofs to certificates and lower bounds; and competition data validates the theory in practice. The central lesson is architectural: because the paradigms are *provably* incomparable, the future belongs to hybrids and portfolios — and to certification infrastructure that makes every answer checkable.
