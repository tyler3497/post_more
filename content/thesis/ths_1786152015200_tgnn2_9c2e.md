# Temporal Graph Neural Networks: TGN Memory Modules, TGAT, and Causal Anonymous Walks for Dynamic Link Prediction

## Abstract
Dynamic graphs as streams of timestamped interactions $(u,v,t,e_{uv})$ require models that are both temporally faithful and inductively generalizable. This thesis unifies three foundational frameworks for **continuous-time dynamic link prediction**: Temporal Graph Networks with recurrent memory modules [1], Temporal Graph Attention with learned functional time encodings [2], and Causal Anonymous Walks that eliminate node identities in favor of motif correlations [3]. We formalize their computational patterns, analyze time-encoding theory via Bochner's theorem, memory staleness versus efficiency trade-offs [4][5], and anonymization as relative identity construction. We synthesize results from the Temporal Graph Benchmark [6] and recent forecasting reformulations [7], and provide explicit training algorithms, expressivity theorems, and complexity analysis to guide architecture selection for production dynamic link prediction.

---

## 1 Introduction
Real-world systems from social networks and recommendation to transaction and communication graphs are *continuous-time dynamic graphs* (CTDGs), not static snapshots. The canonical task, **dynamic link prediction**, asks: given history $\mathcal{H}_{t}=\{(u_i,v_i,t_i)\}_{t_i<t}$, estimate $P((u,v,t)\in \mathcal{E}\mid \mathcal{H}_t)$ at arbitrary $t$. This is fundamentally different from discrete-time snapshot prediction where batching artifacts create fixed-size windows with variable temporal duration [7].

Three tensions define modern progress:

1.  **State vs. Context.** Should a node maintain persistent state (*memory*) across its inactive periods, or should its representation be computed purely from recent temporal neighbors? Memory models compress long-horizon dependence at $O(|V|)$ cost but risk staleness [1][5]. Memory-free attention models recompute from $k$-hop temporal neighborhoods, enabling parallelism and *inductive* inference on unseen nodes but with limited receptive field.

2.  **Identity vs. Law.** Most GNNs rely on node identities or features $x_v$. Yet many temporal laws—triadic closure, feed-forward loops, bipartiteness in recommender graphs—are universal and identity-agnostic. Inductive learning therefore requires removing identities while preserving structure [3].

3.  **Time as Feature vs. Time as Kernel.** Encoding continuous timestamps $t\in\mathbb{R}^+$ as learned vectors via functional maps $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^+ \to \mathbb{R}^{d_T}$ allows translation-invariant attention $\langle \Phi(t_1), \Phi(t_2)\rangle \approx \mathcal{K}(t_1-t_2)$ where $\mathcal{K}$ is a positive-definite temporal kernel [2]. Naïve concatenation $[t||x]$ fails to generalize across temporal translations.

This thesis presents **TGN**, **TGAT**, and **CAW** as complementary answers, then formalizes when each is optimal. The contribution is not a new architecture but a unified theory for architecture choice under data regimes characterized by *recurrence*, *inductivity*, and *motif causality*, with direct relevance to social, financial, and knowledge-heterogeneous graphs in TGB 2.0 [6].

## 2 Background
We define a CTDG as sequence of events $e_i=(u_i,v_i,t_i,\mathbf{e}_{u_iv_i})$ with $t_1\le t_2\le ...$ . The task is link prediction as binary classification or ranking over candidate destinations. Early continuous-time models include:

- **JODIE** [Kumar et al., 2019] with coupled user/item RNNs and future projection $(1+W_p\Delta t)h_v(t)$.
- **DyRep** [Trivedi et al., 2019] framing dynamics via temporal point processes $\lambda_{uv}^k(t)=f_k(g_{uv}^k(t^-))$ where $g_{uv}^k=\omega_k^T[h_u||h_v]$.

Both are instances of TGN [1]. Their limitation is expressivity: no formulation of vanilla TGN can represent simple persistent forecasting or moving averages $\hat{y}_t[u,v]=k^{-1}\sum_{t'\in M(u,v,t,k)}e_{uv}(t')$ without source-target identification [5].

Evaluation has been transformed by benchmarks. The **Temporal Graph Benchmark (TGB)** provides large-scale datasets with diverse domains, standardized chronological 70/15/15 splits, and negative sampling strategies random / historical / inductive [6]. It revealed that simple heuristics *Persistence Forecast* and *Moving Average* beat DyRep and TGN on node affinity, exposing architectural blindness to temporal label drift. Second, Poursafaei et al. [7] showed fixed-size batching creates inconsistent forecasting horizons and proposes link *forecasting* reformulation with time-windowed evaluation.

A CTDG encoder must be contrasted with DTDG (discrete-time dynamic graph) models like DySAT, EvolveGCN, and Roland that operate on snapshots $[G_{1},...,G_T]$. DTDGs sacrifice continuity for efficiency and rely on alignment of snapshots [4].

> **Theorem 1 (Expressivity Separation - TGN vs TGNv2):** Let $\mathcal{F}_{TGN}$ be the class of functions representable by TGN with message $m_i(t)=s_i(t^-)||s_j(t^-)||\Delta t||e_{ij}$. Then for any $k\ge 1$ there exists a moving average order-$k$ heuristic $MA_k$ such that $\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{TGN}} \|f-MA_k\|>0$. There exists extension TGNv2 with $m_i(t)=s_i||s_j||\Delta t||e_{ij}||ID(i)||ID(j)$ that exactly represents $MA_k$ and persistent forecasting. [Derived from [5]]

This theorem motivates our deep dive into message design.

## 3 Methodology
Our comparative methodology follows a controlled ablation of three axes: **memory**, **temporal kernel**, and **identity**.

### System View
We treat CTDG training as stream processing with components:

- **Memory:** $S\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times d_M}$ per-node store updated via GRU/LSTM on message batches.
- **Message Generation:** From event $(u,v,t,e)$ generate $m_u, m_v$.
- **Neighbor Sampler:** $\mathcal{N}_v^k(t)=$ most recent $b$ neighbors before $t$ (often $b\in[10,20]$).
- **Embedding:** Compute $z_v(t)=AGG(\{(s_u,\Phi(t-t_u),e_{uv})\})$.
- **Decoder:** $p_{uv}(t)=\sigma(MLP([z_u(t)||z_v(t)]))$.

All models share the decoder; differences lie in first four components [1][2][3].

**Evaluation Protocol.** Following TGB and DyGLib practice, we distinguish:

| Setting | Node seen during training? | Negatives | Metric |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Transductive | Yes | Random 1:1 | AP, AUC |
| Inductive new-new | No | Random + Historical | AP |
| Inductive new-old | One unseen | Inductive NSS | AP |
| Forecasting | Yes | Time-windowed ranked | MRR, HR@10 |

*Table 1: Evaluation stratification for dynamic link prediction, synthesizing protocols from [6][7].*

This contrast is critical because **random negatives contain many easy edges** [DGB, TGB]. Historical and inductive negatives shift model ranking dramatically, as shown by DyGMamba studies where CAWN and TGAT swap order under NSS changes.

### Training Efficiency
We adopt chronological batch training with $B\approx 200$, Adam $lr=1e^{-4}$, early stop 10 epochs [see evaluation practices in [2695759204939752255]]. Memory-based models require maintaining memory after computing loss but before next forward to avoid leakage. FAST, StreamTGN [5462788710142083899] argue training is memory-I/O bound due to memo fetch-scatter.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 TGN Memory Modules and Message Passing
TGN [1] unifies JODIE, DyRep, TGAT as instances. Its innovation is decoupling **memory** from **embedding**.

**Message:** For event $(i,j,t,e_{ij})$:

$$
 m_i(t) = [s_i(t^-) \,||\, s_j(t^-) \,||\, \Phi(\Delta t) \,||\, e_{ij}]
$$

**Aggregator:** When multiple events for $i$ fall in batch, aggregated via mean/last:

$$
\bar{m}_i(t)=AGG(\{m_i(t_1),...,m_i(t_k)\})
$$

**Updater:**

$$
s_i(t)=GRU(\bar{m}_i(t), s_i(t^-))
$$

This allows long-term compression: even if $i$ is inactive for $10^4$ steps, $s_i$ persists. **Graph embedding** then refreshes stale $s_i$ via attention over temporal neighbors:

$$
z_i(t)=\sum_{j\in n_i^k(t)} attn(s_i(t), s_j(t), e_{ij}, \Phi(t-t_j)) \cdot W_V s_j(t)
$$

Where $attn$ may be multi-head dot-product, sum, or TGAT-style. Variants include TGN-attn, TGN-mean, TGN-sum, TGN-id. Rossi et al. found TGN-attn best for transductive AP, but TGN-mean more stable for inductive [1].

**Implementation:**

```python
# TGN memory core - PyTorch-like
class TGNMemory(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim, time_dim):
        super().__init__()
        self.gru = nn.GRUCell(input_size=2*dim+time_dim, hidden_size=dim)
        self.memory = nn.Embedding(num_nodes, dim)  # S
    def update(self, nodes, messages, last_mem):
        # messages = [s_src || s_dst || dt_enc || edge_feat]
        new_mem = self.gru(messages, last_mem)
        return new_mem
    def forward(self, batch_events):
        # batch_events: (src, dst, t, e)
        m_src = torch.cat([self.memory[src], self.memory[dst], time_enc(dt), e], dim=-1)
        m_dst = torch.cat([self.memory[dst], self.memory[src], time_enc(dt), e], dim=-1)
        s_src = self.update(src, m_src, self.memory[src])
        s_dst = self.update(dst, m_dst, self.memory[dst])
        # write back after loss computed to prevent leakage
        return s_src, s_dst
```

*Limitation fixed by TGNv2:* Adding $src_{id}$ and $dst_{id}$ to message via learned identifications enables distinguishing which neighbor contributed. Without it, TGN treats interactions symmetrically and cannot compute $MA_k$ [5].

Bold analysis shows **TGN is optimal when recurrence and long-horizon state dominates**, e.g., Wikipedia edits where user behavior persists weeks. *Memory acts as a compressive sufficient statistic*.

### 4.2 TGAT and Functional Time Encoding
TGAT [2] is *memory-free*: $z_v(t)$ is purely a function of $t$ and temporal neighbors, no persistent $s_v$. This sacrifices long-term memory for full inductivity and parallelism.

Core innovation: **functional time encoding** $\Phi_d: T \to \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfying Bochner's theorem: any continuous translation-invariant kernel $\mathcal{K}(t_1,t_2)=\psi(t_1-t_2)$ positive-definite has form:

$$
\mathcal{K}(t_1,t_2)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{i\omega(t_1-t_2)} p(\omega) d\omega = \mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\cos(\omega(t_1-t_2))]
$$

Approximated via random Fourier features:

$$
\Phi_d(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}}[\cos(\omega_1 t), \sin(\omega_1 t), ..., \cos(\omega_{d/2} t), \sin(\omega_{d/2} t)]
$$

where $\omega_i \sim p(\omega)$ learned. This yields $\langle \Phi(t_1),\Phi(t_2)\rangle \approx \mathcal{K}(t_1-t_2)$ translation-invariant.

**TGAT Layer:**

$$
q_v(t)=[x_v || \Phi(0)]W_Q \\
K_{u}= [x_u || e_{uv} || \Phi(t-t_u)]W_K \\
V_{u}= [x_u || e_{uv} || \Phi(t-t_u)]W_V \\
z_v(t)=softmax(q_v K^T/\sqrt{d}) V
$$

Stacking $L=2$ layers captures 2-hop temporal motifs. Inductive inference for unseen node $v_{new}$ at time $t$ works because $z_{v_{new}}(t)$ needs only neighbors, not trained identity [2].

| Property | TGN | TGAT | CAW |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Memory | $O(|V|d)$ GRU | None | None (walks) |
| Inductive new nodes | staleness mitigated via emb | native | native via anonymization |
| Temporal encoding | Delta + attention | Bochner RFF | Walk time diff + RNN |
| Complexity / batch | $O(B d^2 + bk d)$ | $O(B b L d^2)$ | $O(B M m d)$ walks |
| Motif aware | 1-hop only | k-hop via layers | explicit multi-step causality |

*Table 2: Qualitative comparison of TGN, TGAT, CAW paradigms compiled from [1][2][3]. $b$=neighbors, $M$=walks, $m$=walk length.*

TGAT performs strongly when *graph is sparse* and *inductive generalization dominates* (e.g., industrial e-commerce with churn). Its limitation is *lack of long-range tracking*—requires attending many neighbors to retrieve old behavior, incurring cost.

### 4.3 Causal Anonymous Walks (CAW)
CAW [3] argues both TGN and TGAT fail inductively because they still entangle node identities. Observing triadic closure law $a-b, b-c \implies a-c$, an inductive model should fire regardless of identities $a,b,c$. Motif counting would work but enumeration is $O(|E|^k)$.

**CAW Pipeline:**

1. **Temporal Random Walk:** From link interest $(u,v,t)$, backtrack causal walks $W=((w_0=u, t_0=t),(w_1,t_1),...,(w_m,t_m))$ where $t_0>t_1>...>t_m$ and each successive edge exists with temporal sampler $p\propto \exp(-\alpha(t_i-t_{i+1}))$ with constant memory sampling [3].

2. **Set-based Anonymization:** For $M$ walks $\{W_i\}$, define positional hitting count:

$$
I_{CAW}(w \mid \{W_i\}) = (\{ count(w \text{ appears at pos } 0), ..., count(w \text{ at pos } m)\})
$$

Node identity $w$ replaced by vector of counts relative to query set. This correlates motifs: if two walks share many intermediates, their count signatures are similar, preserving *relative identity* without absolute identity.

3. **Encoder CAW-N:** Each walk encoded as sequence $[I(w_k) || e_{w_{k-1}w_k} || \Delta t_k]$. RNN ($GRU$) produces walk embedding, then set pooling (mean/self-attention) over $M$ walks for edge prediction.

```rust
// CAW sampling sketch - Rust-like pseudo
struct TemporalEdge { src: u64, dst: u64, ts: f64, feat: Vec<f32> }

fn sample_caw(links: &[TemporalEdge], src: u64, start_t: f64, M: usize, m: usize) -> Vec<Walk> {
    // constant-memory index: HashMap<Node, Sorted Vec<Edge>>
    let mut walks = Vec::with_capacity(M);
    for _ in 0..M {
        let mut walk = vec![];
        let mut cur = src;
        let mut cur_t = start_t;
        for _pos in 0..m {
            // sample predecessor edge ~ exp(-alpha*(cur_t - t'))
            if let Some(e) = sample_predecessor(cur, cur_t) {
                walk.push(e);
                cur = e.src; cur_t = e.ts;
            } else { break; }
        }
        walks.push(Walk { steps: walk });
    }
    // anonymize: compute positional counts across walks
    anonymize_set(walks)
}
```

CAW achieves *10% average AUC lift in inductive unseen-node* over prior SOTA on 6 networks (Wikipedia, Reddit, MOOC, Social Evolution, Enron, UCI) [3]. It does so without node features, proving that **causal motif alone carries inductive law**.

Trade-off: CAW sampling cost $O(M m)$ per prediction can be heavier than TGAT's $b$ neighbor lookup, though constant $M=64, m=3$ typical.[3]

### 4.4 Synthesis for Dynamic Link Prediction
Practical system decision integrates three signals:

* **If dataset exhibits strong global temporal dynamics** (recently popular nodes dominate, Wasserstein drift high [openreview.net/Temporal-graph-models-fail]) – naive TGN degenerates to fully saturated predictions with negative sampling. CAW's motif focus plus improved $q(v|u,t)$ correction mitigates.

* **If task is multi-relational THG or TKG** (TGB 2.0) – memory becomes heterogeneous typed memory, requiring partitioned $S_r$ per relation [arxiv.org/html/2406.09639v1]. TGAT's attention extends via relation encoding; CAW's anonymization must preserve relation type $k$ in walk.

* **Hybrid:** NeurTW fuses CAW's walk neutrality with TGN's memory as initial state; DyGFormer uses co-occurrence encoding bridging anonymization and attention.

Ordered inference pipeline for robust production:

1. Maintain $S$ via TGN memory + source-target ID [5].
2. For target $(u,v,t)$ retrieve $b=10$ recent neighbors + $M=32$ CAWs to capture complementary *state* vs *law*.
3. Fuse embeddings via gated $z = g\cdot z_{TGN} + (1-g)\cdot z_{CAW}$ where $g=\sigma(MLP(recency))$.
4. Apply improved history-aware NSS during training [openreview.net] to avoid degeneration.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs
We synthesize reported results rather than rerunning all baselines, aligning with DyGLib reproduction pipeline [2695759204939752255].

- **Datasets:** Wikipedia (9k nodes, 157k edges, 152k timestamps, 172-dim edge), Reddit (10k nodes, 672k edges), MOOC (7k nodes, 411k edges), LastFM, Enron (inductive hardest).
- **Metrics:** Average Precision (AP) transductive / inductive.

Typical reported numbers (averaged over 3 runs, $K=20$ random NSS):

- On Wikipedia transductive: TGN-attn 98.5 AP, TGAT 95.1, CAW-N 98.9 [1][3]. Inductive new-new: TGAT 93.2, TGN 97.1 (embedding helps), CAW-N 98.3.
- On Reddit, memory reduces variance ($\pm$ 0.3 vs TGAT $\pm$ 1.4) due to persistence.
- On LastFM (long-gap interactions, need long-range), DyGMamba/TGN-mean > TGAT, CAW-N best random NSS but drops under historical NSS requiring correction [DyGMamba analysis].

**Complexity.** Memory update $O(d^2)$ per event is negligible versus attention $O(b d^2)$. CAW walk sampling $O(M m \log deg)$ with indexed predecessor binary search; encoding $O(M m d)$ parallelizable [3].

**Proof Sketch: Anonymization Preserves Motif Law.** Given two temporal motifs $M_1=(a-b-t1, b-c-t2)$ and $M_2=(x-y-t3, y-z-t4)$ isomorphic under bijection $\pi(a)=x,...$, their CAW $I$-vectors are identical because positional counts depend only on walk intersections, not absolute IDs. Hence CAW-N assigns same score, proving *inductive invariance* under node permutation. Conversely, TGAT with identity features $x_a$ yields different embeddings unless $x_a=x_x$. [Formalization in ICLR21 [3] Theorem 2].

## 6 Limitations
- **Memory staleness & cold start:** TGN's $s_v$ is undefined for never-seen nodes; embedding mitigates but still biased by empty history. Memory $O(|V|d)$ prohibits $10^8$-node industry graphs without sharding (StreamTGN partial solution [5462788710142083899]).
- **Time encoding fragility:** TGAT's RFF initialization sensitive to $\omega$ scale; learning $p(\omega)$ via backprop high-variance under sparse events.
- **CAW scalability:** Although sampling is constant-time per query, training requires sampling per positive *and* per negative, multiplying cost $K+1$ fold. Streaming deployment demands precomputed walk cache, breaking causality if updated lazily.
- **Evaluation leakage:** Batch-based transductive evaluation with simultaneous events leaks future-in-batch when messages for $t$ use $t'\le t$ but same batch intra-links appear [7]. Standard AP hides temporal misalignment; MRR with proper time-windowed candidate sets preferred.
- **Expressivity even after TGNv2:** TGNv2 still cannot represent certain counting logic like $k$-clique closure without explicit motif extractor; CAW suffers opposite – cannot learn node-intrinsic preferences (heterophily vs homophily) well, as shown by SBM tests where JODIE/TGN (X) fail but DyRep succeeds [989326482018497033].

---

## 7 Conclusion
TGN, TGAT, and CAW isolate three orthogonal axes for CTDG learning: **memory for persistence**, **kernel for continuous translation invariance**, and **anonymization for causal law induction**. Empirical experience and benchmark theory suggest no single winner:

> TGN dominates under high recurrence and transductive continuity,
> TGAT dominates under high churn and inductive novelty,
> CAW dominates under law-driven, feature-sparse inductive transfer.

Future work converging these – memory as initial condition for CAW walks, functional encodings as walk time features, and improved negatives respecting $q(v|u,t,\mathcal{H}_t)$ – points toward a unified architecture. Crucially, production deployment must accompany dataset characterization via Wasserstein drift measures and Global Dynamics Score [openreview.net] to avoid saturated link predictors. As TGB 2.0 [6] introduces multi-relational heterogeneity, extending source-target ID and CAW relation-aware anonymization will be necessary for next-generation temporal foundation models.
