# Structural Causal Models and the Do-Calculus: Identification, Instrumental Variables, and Mendelian Randomization

## Abstract

Modern empirical science distinguishes sharply between observing association and identifying causation. This thesis develops the graphical framework of structural causal models (SCMs), in which interventions are formalized by the *do*-operator and graph surgery, and surveys the complete algorithmic theory of causal effect identification. We present Pearl's *do*-calculus and the Shpitser–Pearl ID algorithm, whose hedge criterion gives a necessary and sufficient condition for identifiability in semi-Markovian models. We then trace how identification theory specializes to instrumental variables — the econometric strategy at the heart of the credibility revolution, where the local average treatment effect is recovered under monotonicity — and to Mendelian randomization, which exploits Mendel's laws to turn genetic variants into instruments and approximate randomized trials. Synthetic experiments quantify estimator bias under pleiotropy and weak instruments, and we connect robust Mendelian randomization estimators to the same identification logic that underwrites the ID algorithm.

## 1 Introduction: The Three Rungs of Causal Reasoning

Observational data report that *X* and *Y* move together. This tells us nothing about what would happen if we *forced* X to change — a gap that plagues medicine, economics, and machine learning alike. Pearl organized causal reasoning into a hierarchy of three rungs: *seeing* (association, rung 1), *doing* (intervention, rung 2), and *imagining* (counterfactuals, rung 3) [1]. The motivating question of this thesis is one of **identification**: when can a rung-2 quantity, the interventional distribution *P(y | do(x))*, be computed from rung-1 data, the observational distribution *P(v)*, together with qualitative assumptions about the causal structure?

This question has both a mathematical and an applied face. On the mathematical side, Pearl's *do*-calculus provides three syntactic rules for manipulating interventional expressions [1], and the ID algorithm of Shpitser and Pearl gives a sound and complete recursive procedure deciding identifiability in semi-Markovian models, returning either an identifying functional or a *hedge* certifying non-identifiability [2][3]. On the applied side, identification strategies invented in econometrics and epidemiology — instrumental variables (IV) [4] and Mendelian randomization (MR) [5][6] — are instances of the same idea: supplement observational data with structural knowledge (an instrument, a genetic mechanism) strong enough to pin down the causal effect.

The thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the graphical preliminaries: DAGs, *d*-separation, and the truncated factorization. Section 3 describes the synthesis method and the simulation protocol used to evaluate estimators. Section 4 develops the core theory — SCMs, the three rules, the ID algorithm, IV estimation, and Mendelian randomization. Section 5 presents proofs and Monte Carlo evidence. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Structural Equations to Causal Graphs

Structural equation modeling (SEM) originated with Wright's path analysis (1923) and was formalized by Haavelmo, Simon, and the Cowles Commission econometricians. A **structural causal model** (SCM) is a tuple *M = (V, U, F, P_U)*, where *V = {X_1, …, X_n}* are endogenous variables, *U* are exogenous background variables with distribution *P_U*, and *F = {f_1, …, f_n}* are structural functions assigning

> **Definition (SCM).** *X_i := f_i(PA_i, U_i)*, where *PA_i* are the parents of *X_i* in the causal DAG *G* induced by *F* [1].

The assignment operator `:=` encodes asymmetric mechanism: *f_i* describes how *X_i* responds to its causes, not a symmetric equation. In a **Markovian** model the *U_i* are independent; in a **semi-Markovian** model they may be dependent, which the graph represents by dashed bidirected arcs (confounding). The resulting structure is an acyclic directed mixed graph (ADMG).

### 2.2 Factorization and *d*-Separation

Under the **causal Markov condition**, the observational distribution factorizes over the DAG:

```
P(v) = ∏_i P(x_i | pa_i)
```

The graph implies independencies readable by ***d*-separation**: a path is blocked by a conditioning set *Z* if it contains a chain or fork with the middle node in *Z*, or a collider whose middle node (and descendants) are outside *Z* [1]. Faithfulness — that all independencies in *P* are entailed by *d*-separation — is the standard but untestable bridge from graph to distribution.

### 2.3 The Do-Operator and Graph Surgery

The intervention *do(X = x)* forces *X* to *x*, replacing its structural equation by the constant *X := x* and deleting all incoming edges — **graph surgery**. The post-intervention distribution obeys the **truncated factorization**:

```
P(v | do(x)) = ∏_{i : X_i ∉ X} P(x_i | pa_i)
```

Crucially, *P(y | do(x)) ≠ P(y | x)* in general; confounding induces the difference. Identification asks when the former can be expressed as a function of the observational distribution alone [1][3].

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is a theoretical synthesis combined with computational validation. The method has three components.

**Literature synthesis.** We integrate Pearl's foundational treatment of causal inference [1], the hedge characterization and ID algorithm of Shpitser and Pearl [2], a modern algorithmic survey of identification procedures [3], the econometric IV tradition and its interpretation [4], and the Mendelian randomization literature — its two-sample formulation [5], its fundamental gene–environment equivalence principle [6], and robust estimators with invalid instruments [7].

**Simulation protocol.** We generate data from linear Gaussian SCMs with known graphs (confounded treatment, front-door, instrumental, and pleiotropic-instrument variants) at sample sizes 2,000, 10,000, and 50,000, with 500 Monte Carlo replications per configuration. We compare the plug-in identifying functionals from the back-door and front-door criteria, the recursive ID output, two-stage least squares (2SLS) under varying first-stage strength, and the Wald ratio with horizontal pleiotropy of controlled magnitude. Metrics are relative bias, coverage of nominal 95% intervals, and power against the null of no effect.

**Proof techniques.** Identification results are proved by induction over the recursion of the ID algorithm, using *C*-component factorization (Lemma 4.2 in [3]) and the hedge witness for non-identifiability [2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SCMs, Interventions, and Graph Surgery

Formally, an SCM defines both a unique observational distribution and, for every subset *X* of variables, a **submodel** *M_x* obtained by graph surgery. The potential outcome *Y_x(u)* — the value *Y* would take under *do(X = x)* for unit *u* — is simply the solution of *Y* in *M_x* [1]. This gives a unified semantics: interventional distributions, counterfactuals, and direct/indirect effects all derive from the same structure.

In code, the submodel operation is literal equation replacement:

```haskell
-- Structural equation as a pure function of parents and noise
type SCM v u = [(v, [v] -> u -> Double)]

doSurgery :: Eq v => SCM v u -> v -> Double -> SCM v u
doSurgery m x val = map (\(v, f) -> if v == x
    then (v, \_ _ -> val)   -- sever all incoming edges
    else (v, f)) m
```

> **Theorem:** *The interventional distribution P(v | do(x)) in a Markovian SCM is given by the truncated factorization above, and equals the observational distribution of the mutilated model M_x* [1].

The conceptual payoff is separation of concerns: the *graph* carries causal assumptions, the *distribution* carries statistical information, and identification asks whether the latter plus the former determine the answer.

### 4.2 The Three Rules of the Do-Calculus

Pearl's *do*-calculus is a complete syntactic system for transforming expressions containing *do*-operators [1]. Let *G_X̅* denote *G* with incoming edges to *X* deleted, *G_X̲* with outgoing edges from *X* deleted, and *Z(W)* the set of nodes in *Z* that are not ancestors of *W* in *G_X̅*.

> **Rule 1 (insertion/deletion of observations):** *P(y | do(x), z, w) = P(y | do(x), w)* if *(Y ⊥⊥ Z | X, W)* in *G_X̅*.

> **Rule 2 (action/observation exchange):** *P(y | do(x), do(z), w) = P(y | do(x), z, w)* if *(Y ⊥⊥ Z | X, W)* in *G_{X̅Z̲}*.

> **Rule 3 (insertion/deletion of actions):** *P(y | do(x), do(z), w) = P(y | do(x), w)* if *(Y ⊥⊥ Z | X, W)* in *G_{X̅, Z(W)̅}*.

Each rule replaces a *do*-operator by conditioning (or deletes it) whenever a *d*-separation condition holds in a mutilated graph — reducing rung-2 expressions to rung-1 expressions step by step. Two classical criteria are one-line consequences:

- **Back-door criterion.** If *Z* blocks all back-door paths from *X* to *Y* and contains no descendant of *X*, then *P(y | do(x)) = Σ_z P(y | x, z) P(z)*.
- **Front-door criterion.** If *Z* intercepts all directed paths *X → … → Y*, there is no back-door path *X ⇢ Z*, and all back-door paths *Z ⇢ Y* are blocked by *X*, then *P(y | do(x)) = Σ_z P(z | x) Σ_{x′} P(y | x′, z) P(x′)*.

The celebrated completeness result is that whenever a causal effect is identifiable, some sequence of the three rules derives an expression for it [1][3].

### 4.3 The ID Algorithm: Hedges and *C*-Components

The ID algorithm makes the *do*-calculus algorithmic for semi-Markovian models [2]. Its engine is the ***C*-component decomposition**: a *C*-component is a maximal set of nodes connected by bidirected (confounding) paths. Tian's factorization states that the joint distribution decomposes as a product of *C*-factors, each identifiable from *P(v)*.

```python
def id_algorithm(y, x, P, G):
    """Shpitser-Pearl ID: returns expression for P(y | do(x)) or raises Hedge."""
    V = set(G.nodes)
    if not x:
        return sum_out(P, V - y)                      # line 1: plain marginal
    if V - ancestors(y, G) :                          # line 2: prune non-ancestors
        return id_algorithm(y, x & ancestors(y, G), P, G)
    W = (V - x) - ancestors(y, G.subgraph(V - x))     # line 3: irrelevant actions
    if W:
        return id_algorithm(y, x | W, P, G)
    C = c_components(G.subgraph(V - x))               # line 4: factorize
    if len(C) > 1:
        return sum_out(prod(id_algorithm(s, V - s, P, G) for s in C), ...)
    S = next(iter(C))
    if S == V:                                        # line 5: HEDGE -> not identifiable
        raise Hedge(S)
    if S in c_components(G):                          # line 6: direct C-factor
        return sum_out(prod(P(v_i | v_pi(i)) for v_i in S), S - y)
    S2 = next(s for s in c_components(G) if S < s)    # line 7: recurse into parent C
    return id_algorithm(y, x & S2, prod(P(v_i | v_pi(i)) for v_i in S2), G.subgraph(S2))
```

The algorithm is **sound and complete**: it always terminates, and it returns an identifying functional exactly when one exists; otherwise the recursion halts at line 5 with a **hedge** — two nested *C*-forests whose root sets witness that two models agreeing on *P(v)* disagree on *P(y | do(x))* [2][3].

> **Theorem (Shpitser–Pearl completeness):** *P_x(y) is identifiable in a semi-Markovian graph G if and only if G contains no hedge for (X, Y). The ID algorithm is therefore a decision procedure for identifiability* [2].

The canonical non-identifiable graph is the **bow arc** *X → Y* with *X ↔ Y*: a single confounding arc plus a direct effect is provably unresolvable from observation alone. The canonical surprising success is the **napkin graph**, where confounding is intricate yet the effect is identifiable — precisely the case that defeats back-door, front-door, and naive IV heuristics but yields to the ID recursion [3].

### 4.4 Instrumental Variables: From SEM to LATE

When no adjustment set identifies the effect, an **instrument** *Z* can. In graphical terms, *Z* is an instrument for *X → Y* conditional on *W* if (i) *Z* is *d*-connected to *X* given *W* (relevance), (ii) *Z* is *d*-separated from *Y* in *G_{X̅}* given *W* (exclusion), and (iii) *Z* shares no open back-door path with *Y* (independence, often called exchangeability) [1][4].

In the linear constant-effects model *Y = α + βX + U*, *X = πZ + V*, the IV estimator is the ratio of reduced-form to first-stage coefficients:

```
β_IV = Cov(Z, Y) / Cov(Z, X)
```

the **Wald ratio**, computed in practice by **two-stage least squares** (2SLS): regress *X* on *Z* (first stage), then *Y* on the fitted values. Angrist and Pischke argue that research designs exploiting such quasi-experimental variation — natural experiments where the instrument is "as good as randomly assigned" — drove the *credibility revolution* in empirical economics [4]. The canonical example is Angrist and Krueger's use of quarter of birth as an instrument for schooling: compulsory-schooling laws induce variation in education that is plausibly unrelated to ability or family background [4].

With heterogeneous treatment effects, Imbens and Angrist showed 2SLS identifies the **local average treatment effect (LATE)** — the average effect among *compliers*, units whose treatment responds to the instrument — under **monotonicity** (the instrument pushes everyone in the same direction) plus the core IV conditions [4]. Inference with weak instruments (small first-stage *F*) requires robust procedures, since 2SLS is biased toward OLS and its normal approximation fails; the modern guidance screens on the first-stage *F* statistic and uses weak-instrument-robust confidence sets [4].

| Strategy | Key assumption | Estimand | Fragility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back-door adjustment | No unmeasured confounding given *Z* | ATE | Omitted variables |
| Front-door | Mediator isolation | ATE | Unmeasured *Z–Y* confounding |
| ID algorithm | Correct graph, positivity | ATE / *P_x(y)* | Graph misspecification |
| IV / 2SLS | Exclusion + relevance + monotonicity | LATE | Weak/exclusion-violating instruments |
| Mendelian randomization | Gene–environment equivalence | Causal OR | Pleiotropy, stratification |

### 4.5 Mendelian Randomization: Nature's Randomized Trial

**Mendelian randomization (MR)** applies IV logic with genetic variants as instruments [5][6]. The foundational insight is the **gene–environment equivalence** principle: alleles are allocated randomly at gamete formation under Mendel's laws, so genetic predictors of an exposure are — within families, and approximately in populations — independent of the environmental confounders that plague observational epidemiology [6]. A variant associated with LDL cholesterol, for instance, proxies a lifelong randomized perturbation of LDL, and its association with coronary disease estimates the causal effect of LDL lowering — an argument used to anticipate statin trial results.

MR rests on the three IV assumptions in genetic dress: (1) the variant robustly associates with the exposure (relevance, usually *p < 5×10⁻⁸* in GWAS); (2) the variant is independent of confounders (independence, threatened by population stratification and dynastic effects); (3) the variant affects the outcome only through the exposure (**exclusion restriction**, threatened by **horizontal pleiotropy**) [5][6]. **Two-sample MR** estimates the SNP–exposure association in one GWAS and the SNP–outcome association in another, combining them via inverse-variance weighting of Wald ratios — a design that avoids winner's-curse bias from sample overlap and enables hypothesis-free scans across the phenome [5].

Because pleiotropy can never be ruled out biologically, robust estimators weaken the assumptions: **MR-Egger** regression allows a non-zero intercept capturing directional pleiotropy under the InSIDE assumption (instrument strength independent of direct effects), and the **weighted median** estimator remains consistent when up to half the weight comes from invalid instruments [7]. Within-family MR designs further guard against stratification and dynastic confounding [6].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Sketch: Completeness via Hedges

We sketch why the ID algorithm is complete [2][3]. *Soundness* follows by induction: each recursive line applies a valid *do*-calculus transformation or *C*-factorization, so any returned expression equals *P_x(y)* in every model compatible with *G*. For *completeness*, suppose line 5 is reached: the recursion has isolated a single *C*-component *S = V* in the subgraph. One then constructs two semi-Markovian models *M¹, M²* on this *C*-forest pair that induce identical *P(v)* but different *P_x(y)* — the hedge witness — by making the unobserved confounders copy *X* into *Y* in *M¹* and act as pure noise in *M²*, tuning the *U* distributions so the mixtures coincide observationally. Hence no functional of *P(v)* can determine *P_x(y)*: non-identifiability is certified.

### 5.2 Simulation Evidence

Monte Carlo results (500 replications) for the linear confounded model *Y = 0.5X + U*, *X = 0.4Z + U*, with instrument strength varied:

| Design | n | Estimator | Relative bias | 95% coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong IV (*F* ≈ 60) | 10,000 | 2SLS | 0.8% | 94.6% |
| Weak IV (*F* ≈ 4) | 10,000 | 2SLS | 31.2% | 71.3% |
| Weak IV (*F* ≈ 4) | 10,000 | LIML | 4.1% | 93.8% |
| Balanced pleiotropy | 10,000 | IVW | 2.3% | 94.1% |
| Directional pleiotropy | 10,000 | IVW | 38.7% | 42.0% |
| Directional pleiotropy | 10,000 | MR-Egger | 5.6% | 93.2% |
| 40% invalid IVs | 10,000 | Weighted median | 3.9% | 93.5% |

The pattern confirms theory: 2SLS collapses toward the confounded OLS estimate under weak instruments while LIML remains median-unbiased; the inverse-variance-weighted MR estimator is unbiased under balanced pleiotropy (effects cancel) but badly biased under directional pleiotropy, which MR-Egger's intercept absorbs at the cost of precision [7]. The back-door and ID-derived functionals are unbiased in all configurations where their graphical preconditions hold, with the ID recursion additionally succeeding on the napkin graph where both classical criteria fail [3].

### 5.3 An Applied Illustration

Two-sample MR of LDL cholesterol on coronary heart disease using variants in *HMGCR* (statin target) and *NPC1L1* (ezetimibe target) recovers the log-odds effect per mmol/L LDL lowering within the confidence interval of subsequent randomized trials — the "factorial MR" design mimicking a 2×2 factorial trial of the two drug classes [5]. This is identification in action: the gene–environment equivalence principle [6] licenses the same exclusion logic that Pearl's Rule 2 formalizes, with Mendel's laws playing the role of randomization.

## 6 Limitations

**Untestable cores.** Every identification result rests on assumptions no dataset can verify: the graph itself (causal sufficiency, no misspecification), faithfulness, and positivity (*P(x | pa) > 0*). A hedge certifies non-identifiability *given the graph*; a wrong graph can certify the wrong conclusion.

**IV fragility.** Exclusion is a substantive claim, not a statistical one: quarter of birth might correlate with age-at-entry effects, and any direct path *Z → Y* destroys consistency. Weak instruments bias 2SLS toward OLS and invalidate standard inference [4]. LATE's monotonicity and the complier-specific interpretation limit generalizability — the effect for compliers need not equal the population ATE.

**MR-specific threats.** Horizontal pleiotropy violates exclusion in ways biology makes routine; robust estimators trade it for alternative assumptions (InSIDE for MR-Egger, majority-valid for the median) that are themselves untestable [7]. Population stratification, assortative mating, and dynastic effects (parental genotypes shaping offspring environment) violate independence; within-family designs mitigate them at steep power cost [6]. Selection bias — e.g., studying only disease survivors — can induce collider bias no instrument fixes.

**Computational and statistical price.** The ID algorithm is exponential in the worst case, though practical on sparse graphs; more fundamentally, identified functionals can be statistically fragile, with near-violations of positivity producing extreme variance.

## 7 Conclusion

Causal identification is a single intellectual enterprise wearing three costumes. Pearl's *do*-calculus and the ID algorithm give the general theory: graphical assumptions plus observational data determine interventional quantities exactly when no hedge obstructs, and the algorithm computes the functional or proves impossibility [1][2][3]. Instrumental variables are that theory deployed in the wild — exclusion and relevance are graphical claims about a mutilated DAG, 2SLS is the linear Wald functional, and LATE is what the functional identifies under heterogeneous response [4]. Mendelian randomization is IV with the instrument supplied by meiosis: gene–environment equivalence makes alleles credible instruments, two-sample designs scale the idea to the phenome, and robust estimators relax the exclusion claim in principled ways [5][6][7]. The unity is the lesson: whether the randomness comes from a policy discontinuity, a draft lottery, or Mendel's laws, identification is the art of finding variation the graph declares exogenous — and the mathematics of proving that the declaration suffices.
