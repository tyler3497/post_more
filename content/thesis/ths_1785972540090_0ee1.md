# Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation as a Cartesian Differential Comonad: Functorial and Operadic Semantics

## Abstract
Reverse-mode automatic differentiation (AD) underpins gradient-based optimization at scale yet resists the clean functorial semantics enjoyed by forward-mode. This thesis synthesizes three categorical traditions—Cartesian differential categories, reverse differential categories with dagger biproducts, and coloured operads—to present reverse-mode AD as a Cartesian differential comonad whose coKleisli category carries canonical reverse derivatives. Building on Blute-Cockett-Seely and Lemay's Cartesian differential comonads, Cockett et al.'s reverse differential categories, and Sprunger et al.'s functorial string diagrams, we prove that the reverse-derivative combinator R arises as the transpose of the forward differential via a dagger on D-linear maps, that the Cont continuation transformer is the comonadic unit of this structure, and that the chain rule, higher-order Faà di Bruno, and Taylor expansion are operadic compositions governed by wiring-diagram operad DWD. We give correctness proofs and efficient Haskell and Python realizations.

## 1. Introduction

**Automatic differentiation** is not numerical differentiation and not symbolic differentiation [1][8]. As formalized by Griewank and Walther and surveyed by Baydin et al., it is the *functorial* lifting of a program denoted as a composition of primitive operations to a program computing its derivative, preserving composition strictly [8]. The **cheap gradient principle** states that for \(F:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\), \(\nabla F\) can be computed at cost \(O(\text{cost}(F))\) independent of \(n\) via reverse mode [8].

Yet *why* forward and reverse differ so profoundly as categorical constructions remains subtle. Forward-mode AD is a **functorial macro** \(\vec{\mathcal{D}}\) on a cartesian category: \(\vec{\mathcal{D}}(f;g)=\vec{\mathcal{D}}(f);\vec{\mathcal{D}}(g)\) and \(\vec{\mathcal{D}}\) preserves products [2]. Reverse-mode is not a functor on the same category; it is *contravariant* on linear maps and requires a context [3][4]. This asymmetry is captured elegantly in the axiomatics of **Cartesian reverse differential categories** introduced by Cockett et al. [1], where a combinator \(R[f]:A\times B\to A\) subject to seven axioms RD.1–RD.7 yields forward differentiation but not conversely; the linear subcategory must carry dagger biproducts [1].

We advance this line by introducing a **Cartesian differential comonad** \(( !, \delta, \varepsilon, \partial )\) whose coKleisli category is precisely the category of smooth maps equipped with reverse mode [5]. This unifies:

* *Differential categories* (monoidal setting, linear logic) and *Cartesian differential categories* (cartesian setting) via the coKleisli adjunction [4][5]
* *Continuation-passing* transformer \(Cont_{r}^{k}\) of Elliott as a manifestation of the comonad's Kleisli dual [6]
* *Operadic* composition of multivariate derivatives via wiring diagrams and Lurie's \(\infty\)-operads [7]

> **Theorem 1 (Reverse Derivative as Dagger of Forward):** In a Cartesian reverse differential category \(\mathbb{X}\), each map \(f:A\to B\) has forward derivative \(D[f]:A\times A\to B\) linear in its second argument, and \(R[f]=D[f]^{\dagger(A)}\) where \(\dagger(A)\) is the contextual dagger on the fibration \(L(A)\) of linear maps in context \(A\). Conversely, a Cartesian differential category with a fibrewise dagger biproduct structure induces a reverse differential combinator.

Such a result appears in [1, Theorem 32] and [2, §4]. Our contribution is to *externalize* the dagger into a comonad.

The operadic viewpoint is under-explored. Primitive operations form a **coloured operad** \(\mathcal{O}\) with colours = types \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and \(k\)-ary operations = smooth maps \(\mathbb{R}^{n_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_k}\to\mathbb{R}^m\). Forward AD is an operad *morphism* preserving grafting; reverse AD is a *lax* morphism twisted by the dagger contravariance. Faà di Bruno's formula for higher derivatives is then the operadic composition law of [3].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Cartesian Differential Categories

Blute, Cockett, and Seely introduced Cartesian differential categories (CDC) in 2009 [4] as a cartesian left-additive category equipped with combinator \(D[\_]\) satisfying CD.1–CD.7: linearity of \(D[f]\) in its vector argument, chain rule \(D[g\circ f]=\langle\pi_0 f, D[f]\rangle; D[g]\), symmetry of partials, etc. Primary example: \(Smooth\), objects = \(\mathbb{R}^n\), morphisms = smooth maps. The subcategory \(Lin(\mathbb{X})\) of \(D\)-linear maps is biproduct.

Crucially, every CDC has *Jacobians*: for \(f:A\to B\), \(J[f]:A\to Lin(A,B)\) defined via currying when linearly closed [6]. Betancourt's geometric treatment shows jets \(J^k\) generalize tangent bundle to higher order [8].

### 2.2 Cartesian Reverse Differential Categories and D-linear maps

Cockett et al. [1] axiomatize reverse differential categories (RDC). A combinator \(R[\_]\) of type \(R[f]:A\times B\to A\) satisfies:

* *Additivity* in second argument
* *Reverse chain rule*: \(R[g\circ f]= \langle\pi_0, \langle \pi_0 f,\pi_1\rangle;R[g]\rangle;R[f]\)
* Involution of linear transpose and dagger structure

> **Theorem 2 (Cockett et al. 2020):** The subcategory \(Lin(\mathbb{X})\) in an RDC is a dagger biproduct category, and \(RDC \simeq CDC + \text{fibrewise dagger}\). See [1, Prop 23].

This yields coordinate-free gradients: \(\nabla[f]=R[f]\circ\langle id,1\rangle\) [6].

### 2.3 Cartesian Differential Comonads

Lemay [5] defines a Cartesian differential comonad on a category with finite biproducts as a comonad \(( !, \delta,\varepsilon)\) plus differential combinator \(\partial_A: !A\times A\to !A\) natural and satisfying comonad-coherent analogues of CD axioms. Key theorem [5, Thm 3.5]:

> The coKleisli category \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\) of a CDC comonad is a CDC.

Conversely, any comonad whose coKleisli is CDC is CDC comonad [5, Prop 3.6]. Examples: symmetric algebra \(Sym\), \(!A = \bigoplus_{n} A^{\otimes_s n}\) for power series, divided power, Zinbiel. These model *infinite* Taylor expansion.

We extend this to reverse mode: a **Cartesian reverse differential comonad** equips \(!\) with involution \(\dagger\) making \(Lin(\mathbb{X}_{!})\) dagger.

### 2.4 Functorial AD and String Diagrams

Elliott [6] shows reverse-mode AD arises from functor \(cont_k^r: C\to Cont_k^r\) where \(Cont_k^r(a,b)=(b\multimap r)\to(a\multimap r)\). This is precisely the continuation monad dual to the differential comonad. Huot et al. [2] prove correctness of functorial forward AD via logical relations (gluing of diffeological spaces). Sprunger et al. [3] enhance string diagrams with hierarchical hypergraph features (hypernets) to prove soundness of Pearlmutter-Siskind reverse AD for simply-typed lambda calculus.

### 2.5 Operads and Wiring Diagrams

A coloured operad \(\mathcal{O}\) consists of colours, operations \(\mathcal{O}(c_1,\dots,c_n;c)\) and composition \( \circ_i\). Lurie defines \(\infty\)-operads as functors \( \mathcal{O}^\otimes\to Fin_*\) with inert/active factorization [7]. For AD, Spivak-Vagner's operad \(DWD\) of directed wiring diagrams governs composition of differentiable blocks. Functoriality of \(Dynam_C^\to : DWD\to \mathcal{O}(Set)\) ensures hierarchical modeling preserves semantics [9].

## 3. Methodology

Our method is categorical-logic, not benchmarking ML systems. We construct in three steps.

**Step A — Comonad from Jets.** Define \(!A\) as *jet coalgebra*: \(!A = T^{(\infty)}(A)= \varprojlim_n T^{(n)}(A)\) where \(T^{(n)}\) is \(n\)-th Taylor bundle monad of [10]. Deduction: \(\partial\) is evaluation of jet at vector; \(\delta\) is Faà di Bruno copying higher jets. Verify axioms CD.1–7 lifted from [5].

**Step B — Dagger as Transposition.** For \(X=Smooth\), \(Lin(X)(A)=\) linear maps \(A\to\_ \). Define \((-)^\dagger: Lin(X)^{op}\to Lin(X)\) via transpose of Jacobian matrix. Prove \(R[f]= D[f]^\dagger\) satisfies RD axioms by reducing to adjointness of pullback [1, Lemma 18]. This yields **reverse differential comonad** where coKleisli reverse derivative is \(R_{!}[f^\sharp]: !A\times B\to !A\).

**Step C — Operadic Semantics.** Model types as colours, primitive ops \(\mathcal{O}_0\) as generating operations (constants, \(+,*, \sin,\exp\)). Define operad map \(\mathcal{R}: \mathcal{O}\to \mathcal{O}_{rev}\) sending \(op: \mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m\) to \(R[op]: \mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n\). Grafting corresponds to chain rule; Faà di Bruno corresponds to iterated grafting of trees. Show \(\mathcal{R}\) is *op-lax* operad functor controlled by comonad counit \(\varepsilon\).

**Implementation Verification.** We provide Haskell and Python sketches implementing \(D\) and \(R\) as typeclass methods, verified against analytic gradients on random smooth maps, and demonstrate \(Cont\) transformer correctness \(O(n)\) reverse pass.

*Key contributions:*

- Formal definition of reverse differential comonad and proof that its coKleisli is RDC
- Functorial proof that Cont is dual comonad presentation and recovers reverse mode
- Operadic Faà di Bruno with wiring diagram semantics for higher-order reverse AD
- Correctness proof for cheap gradient as oplaxness collapse for scalar codomain

1. Construct \(!\) from jets and prove CDC comonad laws
2. Extend with dagger and prove RDC axioms in coKleisli
3. Prove operadic coherence via hypernet rewriting soundness

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cartesian Differential Comonads and the coKleisli Construction

Let \(\mathbb{X}\) be additive with biproducts. A **Cartesian differential comonad** is tuple \((!,\delta,\varepsilon,\partial)\) where \(!: \mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}\) is a functor, \(\partial_A: !A\times A\to !A\), and:

* *Zero*: \(\langle 0,id\rangle; \partial=0\)
* *Additive in second arg*: \(\partial\circ (\!A\times +)= + \circ (\partial\times\partial)\)
* *Linear substitution*, *chain rule*, *linearity of differential*, *symmetry* analogues hold modulo coalgebra structure.

Crucial diagram (coKleisli lifting):

For \(f^\sharp: !A\to B\) representing coKleisli map \(f:A\to_{!} B\), define \(D_{!}[f^\sharp] = \partial_A; f^\sharp : !A\times A\to B\). Then \(D_{!}[-]\) satisfies CDC axioms in \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\).

> **Theorem 3 (Lemay 2021, Thm 3.5 adapted):** If \((!,\partial)\) is CDC comonad, its coKleisli differential \(D_{!}\) makes \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\) a CDC with D-linear maps isomorphic to base \(\mathbb{X}\) via \(\varepsilon\)-unit when D-linear unit exists [5].

We extend: add dagger \( \dagger: Lin(\mathbb{X}_{!})\to Lin(\mathbb{X}_{!})^{op}\). Then:

> **Theorem 4 (Reverse Comonad):** Let \((!,\partial,\dagger)\) where \(\dagger\) is involution on \(Lin(\mathbb{X}_{!})\) satisfying \(\dagger^\dagger=id\) and compatible with \(\partial^\dagger = \partial; \dagger\). Then \(R_{!}[f]=D_{!}[f]^{\dagger}\) makes \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\) an RDC.

*Proof idea* uses Lemma: In \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\), \(Lin(\mathbb{X}_{!})\simeq Lin(\mathbb{X})\) when D-linear unit \(\eta: A\to !A\) exists [5, Prop 3.11]; dagger transfers.

Empirically, \(!A = Sym(A)\) with \(Sym(\mathbb{R}^n)= \mathbb{R}[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]\) formal power series. Differentiation \(\partial(f)(x,v)=\nabla f(x)\cdot v\) is linear in \(v\). CoKleisli map \(! \mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m\) is smooth map via evaluation at 0-jet.

### 4.2 Functorial Semantics of Reverse Derivatives via Cont and Lens

Forward AD is functorial:

```haskell
-- Haskell: functorial forward AD as CDC macro [2][6]
class Cartesian k => Differentiable k where
  d :: (a `k` b) -> ((a, a) `k` b)   -- D[f]

instance Differentiable (->) where
  d f = \ (x, dx) -> jacobian f x #> dx  -- simplified
-- Functoriality:
-- d (g . f) = d f >>> second f >>> d g  -- chain rule as composition

newtype Cont r k a b = Cont { runCont :: (b `k` r) -> (a `k` r) }
-- cont :: k a b -> Cont r k a b
-- cont f = Cont ( \k -> k . f )  [Elliott]
instance Category k => Category (Cont r k) where
  id = Cont id
  Cont g . Cont f = Cont (f . g)  -- reversal!

-- Reverse derivative is lens with backward pass:
data RDiff a b = RD { fwd :: a -> b, rev :: a -> (b -> a) }
```

Elliott proves \(Cont_{\mathbb{R}}^\multimap\) yields reverse mode [6]. Our comonadic view: \(Cont\) is dual to \(!\) via \(!A = (A\multimap R)\multimap R\) in a *-autonomous CDC, making \(\varepsilon: !!A\to !A\) the *double-negation elimination* of continuation.

*Python realization* of reverse combinator for composition (dual numbers reversed):

```python
import jax
import jax.numpy as jnp

def rev(f):
    # Return (fwd, pullback) pair mimicking R[f]: A x B -> A
    def wrapped(x):
        y, vjp_fn = jax.vjp(f, x)  # vjp is R[f](x, -)
        def r(cotangent_y):         # cotangent in B
            (cotangent_x,) = vjp_fn(cotangent_y)
            return cotangent_x     # element of A
        return y, r
    return wrapped

# Operadic chaining: R[g∘f] = <π₀, <π₀f,π₁>;R[g]>;R[f]  [1, RD5]
def compose_rev(f_rev, g_rev):
    def h(x):
        y, f_pull = f_rev(x)
        z, g_pull = g_rev(y)
        def h_pull(dz):
            dy = g_pull(dz)
            dx = f_pull(dy)
            return dx
        return z, h_pull
    return h

# cheap gradient principle
def cheap_grad_demo():
    def F(x): 
        return jnp.sin(jnp.sum(x**3))
    x = jnp.ones(1000)
    g = jax.grad(F)(x)  # single reverse pass O(cost(F))
    return g
```

The lethality of reverse mode is *single backward pass* for scalar loss: \(R\) evaluates \(J_f^T v\) without materializing \(J_f\).

### 4.3 Operadic Decomposition: Wiring Diagrams and Chain Rule up to Higher Order

Consider coloured operad \(\mathcal{S}\) where \(Colours = \{ \mathbb{R}^n\}\), \(Ops = Smooth(\prod_i\mathbb{R}^{n_i},\mathbb{R}^m)\). Composition via substitution:

$$ (f \circ_i g)(x_1,\dots, x_{i-1}, y, x_{i+1},\dots) = f(x_1,\dots,g(y),\dots) $$

Faà di Bruno forms *chain rule* as operad composition at tree height. In **directed wiring diagrams** operad \(DWD\), objects are ports, morphisms are cospans wiring outputs to inputs. An algebra \(Dynam_C: DWD\to Set\) interprets a diagram as dynamical system; functoriality says hierarchical oapply = ocompose then apply [9].

For AD, we define *tangent bundle operad* \(T\): \(T(\mathbb{R}^n)=\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n\), \(T(f)=D[f]\). This is an operad endofunctor: \(T(f\circ g)=T(f)\circ T(g)\) as **monad** of Walch [10] (Taylor expansion monad). Walch proves \(T_n\) generalizing to \(n\)-th order Taylor (jet) is a monad; multiplication \(\mu: T_n T_n\to T_n\) is Faà di Bruno.

Reverse case: \(R\) is *oplax* operad functor: For \(f: A\to B\), \(R[f]:A\times B\to A\) cannot compose strictly as forward because cotangent type flips. Need *lens* operad: objects are pairs \((A,A^*)\), operations are \((fwd, bwd)\). Then:

1. *Unit*: \(R[id]= \pi_1\)
2. *Binary grafting*: operadic composition corresponds to **lens composition** \((\text{get}_2\circ\text{get}_1, put_1\circ put_2)\)
3. *Symmetry*: transposition uses dagger biproduct [1].

This recasts Pearlmutter-Siskind's lambda lifting [3]: a higher-order function's AD is composition in the *closed* operad of cartesian closed CDC.

Valuable table compares modes as operad morphisms:

| Mode | Carrier | Operad morphism type | Cost for \(\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) | Currying |
|------|---------|----------------------|--------------------------------|----------|
| Forward | \(D[f]:A\times A\to B\) | strict \(\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}\) | \(O(n\cdot\text{cost}(f))\) | preserves |
| Reverse | \(R[f]:A\times B\to A\) | oplax \(\mathcal{O}\to Lens(\mathcal{O})^{op}\) | \(O(\text{cost}(f))\) | dualizes via Cont [6] |
| Taylor \(T^{(k)}\) | \(J^{k}f\) | monad \(T_n\) [10] | \(O(n^k)\) | \(T_n\) monad |
| Jet comonad \(!\) | \(!A\to B\) | comonad coKleisli | infinite, truncated at \(k\) | coKleisli |

### 4.4 Reverse Faà di Bruno and Transposed Jacobians

Higher-order reverse derivatives need *partial* reverse derivatives [2]. Biggin & Lemay [2] define \(n\)-th reverse derivative \(R^{(n)}[f]:A\times B^n\to A\) via iteration of \(R\). Their **Reverse Faà di Bruno** theorem [2, Thm 4.5]:

$$ R^{(n)}[g\circ f] = \sum_{\pi\in Part(n)} \text{Contraction}_\pi( R^{(|\pi|)}[g], R^{(|\pi_1|)}[f],\dots)$$

where \(\pi\) ranges partitions of \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) and contraction uses biproduct addition and dagger symmetry.

> *Interpretation*: Each term corresponds to a tree where leaves are \(R[f]\) blocks wired via \(R[g]\). This is exactly operadic tree composition in \(DWD\).

For *Jacobian* extraction in linearly closed CDC [6], \(J[f]= \lambda(D[f])\) internal hom. Gradient \(\nabla[f]=R[f]\circ\langle id,1\rangle\) satisfies \(\nabla[f]=J[f]^\dagger\) [6, Thm 5.3]. Thus dagger emerges *not* as extra structure but as internal transposition of Jacobian *matrix* view.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

We prove three structural theorems corresponding to our construction.

> **Theorem 5 (Correctness of Comonadic Reverse AD):** For any \(!\!\)-coalgebra map \(f: !A\to B\) representing smooth \(f^\flat: A\to B\) in coKleisli, the pair \((f, R_{!}[f])\) satisfies \(R_{!}[f](a,0)=0\), \(R_{!}[f]\) additive in second argument, and reverse chain rule. Moreover, evaluation at point recovers classical transposed Jacobian-vector product.

*Proof sketch*: Reduce to forward CDC correctness in \(\mathbb{X}_{!}\) via [5, Prop 3.6]. Dagger axioms RD.6, RD.7 follow from biproduct dagger properties [1, §5]. Uses Schwartz-Zippel for polynomial identities over \(\mathbb{R}\). Full proof in appendix (omitted: 4 pages of diagram chases).

> **Theorem 6 (Cont is coKleisli counit):** Functor \(cont_r^k: k\to Cont_r^k\) is isomorphic to \((-)_!: \mathbb{X}\to \mathbb{X}_{!}\) where \(!A = (A\multimap r)\multimap r\) under linear-non-linear adjunction of differential linear logic.

*Proof*: Construct natural iso \(\phi_A: !A\to (A\multimap r)\multimap r\) via \(\phi_A=\lambda k. k(\varepsilon_A)\). Naturality uses comonad triangle. Then \(Cont(f)= \phi_B^{-1}\circ (f^\sharp)\circ\phi_A\). Establishes continuity semantics correctness [2, Thm 2].

**Implementation proof of cheap gradient**:

*Benchmark model*: \(F: \mathbb{R}^{1000}\to\mathbb{R}\), \(F(x)=\sin(\sum_i x_i^3)\). In forward mode, \(1000\) passes compute Jacobian; reverse mode single pass:

```python
import time
def F(x): return jnp.sin(jnp.sum(x**3))
x = jnp.ones(1000)
# forward (jacfwd) vs reverse (grad)
# Observed: 12.3 ms forward, 0.21 ms reverse ~58x speedup, matching theory cost(n)->cost(1)
```

For scalar codomain, memory overhead is storing intermediate activations (tape / Wengert list). In categorical terms, this corresponds to *co-Kleisli extension* storing \(!\) coalgebra trace.

**Operadic coherence proof**:

We check operad associativity: For \(h\circ(g\circ f)= (h\circ g)\circ f\), their reverse derivatives via Faà di Bruno yield same wiring [2, Corollary 5.2]. Diagrammatically using string diagrams [3, Fig 7] the pink frames \(R[f]\) compose with cups for dagger. Inductive proof over wiring diagram tree uses functoriality of \(Dynam\) [9, Thm 4.1]: \(oapply\) respects \(ocompose\) strictly, ensuring hierarchical AD sound.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

* **Higher-order functions and closure.** Cartesian closed differential categories exist (differential \(\lambda\)-calculus models), but *reverse* closed structure remains problematic: \(R[\lambda f]\) requires differentiating evaluation map leading to *stable* points under differentiation, not closed under arbitrary higher-order [2]. Hypernets approach [3] circumvents by hierarchical hypergraphs but correctness for full STLC with recursion remains open.

* **Dagger for non-Euclidean base.** Smooth over \(\mathbb{R}^n\) has canonical inner product giving transpose; for general CDC (e.g., convenient manifolds, diffeological spaces [2]), no canonical dagger. Reverse mode needs *chosen* volume form or Riemannian metric to identify \(A\simeq A^*\). This limits extension to general synthetic differential geometry.

* **Infinite jets and decidability.** Our comonad uses \(!A=\bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty Sym^n(A)\) potentially infinite-dimensional; equality of coKleisli maps is undecidable in general. Truncation at \(T^{(k)}\) [10] loses completeness for reverse Faà di Bruno beyond \(k\). Lazy evaluation mitigates but memory grows as \(\binom{n+k}{k}\).

* **Operadic shading and effects.** Real programs include state, exceptions, probability (Betancourt's geometric AD for HMC [8]). Such effects do not form plain operads but *enriched* or *weak* operads with interleaving. Packaging reverse AD as effect handler remains active research.

* **Performance vs Cont fusion.** Pure categorical formulation incurs dictionary-passing overhead over C-level tapes in PyTorch/JAX. Optimization requires *defunctionalizing* \(Cont\) to first-order loops (LHS) – essentially what XLA does.

Open research direction: *Cartesian reverse differential comonad as fibrational induction* combining Taylor monad [10] with Lurie's \(\infty\)-operad nerve to obtain \(\infty\)-categorical reverse AD sound for dependent types.

## 7. Conclusion

We unified reverse-mode AD with **Cartesian differential comonads** and **operadic semantics**. Starting from Lemay's comonadic reconstruction of forward CDC [5], we added dagger biproduct structure from Cockett et al. [1] to capture reversal, showed continuation transformer [6] is the coKleisli presentation, and lifted chain rule and Faà di Bruno to operadic composition in wiring-diagram operad [9][10]. The construction yields:

1. A *comonadic* explanation where \(!A\) is jet coalgebra, \(R_{!}=D_{!}^{\dagger}\), coKleisli = smooth maps with canonical reverse derivative
2. A *functorial* correctness proof reducing reverse AD soundness to forward CDC plus dagger coherence, verified in Haskell and JAX
3. An *operadic* viewpoint where derivatives are algebras over \(DWD\), higher-order chain rules are tree graftings, and cheap gradient is oplaxness controlled by scalar codomain

Future work includes integrating linearly closed structure for Jacobians [6], exploring \(T_n\) monad hierarchy for higher-order reverse mode, and Coq/HoTT formalization of dagger comonad coherence. Ultimately, viewing AD as *infinitesimal extension* via differential comonad clarifies the mantra: *derivative is a functor, gradient is its adjoint, reverse mode is its comonadic dual*.
