# Mirror Symmetry of Calabi–Yau Threefolds: The A-Model, the B-Model, Gromov–Witten Invariants, and the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow Conjecture

## Abstract

Mirror symmetry is a duality relating pairs of Calabi–Yau threefolds whose Hodge numbers are interchanged, first observed by physicists and decisively established as mathematics through the enumerative predictions of Candelas, de la Ossa, Green and Parkes for rational curves on the quintic threefold [1]. This thesis surveys the two complementary mathematical formulations of the phenomenon: the **A-model**, governed by symplectic geometry and Gromov–Witten invariants counting holomorphic curves, and the **B-model**, governed by complex geometry and variations of Hodge structure through period integrals and Picard–Fuchs equations. We present the mirror map construction, Givental's proof of the mirror theorem [6], Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture relating the derived Fukaya category to the derived category of coherent sheaves [3], and the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow geometric proposal that mirror symmetry is fiberwise T-duality along special Lagrangian torus fibrations [4]. Enumerative confirmations, categorical results, and open problems including singular fibers and non-toric mirrors are discussed.

## 1 Intro

Few episodes in modern mathematics rival the arrival of *mirror symmetry*. In 1991, a group of string theorists — Philip Candelas, Xenia de la Ossa, Paul Green, and Linda Parkes — performed a calculation in conformal field theory that produced concrete integers purporting to count rational curves of every degree on a general quintic threefold [1]. Their numbers were startling: 2875 lines, 609250 conics, 317206375 twisted cubics, and 242467530000 quartic curves. Algebraic geometers, who had struggled for decades to compute even the first of these by hand, were confronted with a physics calculation that predicted *all* of them at once, and every subsequent verification by classical means confirmed the predictions [2].

The conceptual engine behind the calculation was a conjectured *duality* between two Calabi–Yau threefolds: the quintic threefold X itself, and a mirror partner Y constructed as a resolution of a quotient of a one-parameter family of quintics by a finite group. Quantities that are fiendishly difficult to compute on X — the *Gromov–Witten invariants* counting holomorphic curves — were identified with quantities on Y that are comparatively tractable: *period integrals* of the holomorphic three-form, governed by linear differential equations of Picard–Fuchs type. The bridge between the two sides is the **mirror map**, a change of coordinates on the moduli space of complex structures of Y fixed by a maximally unipotent monodromy condition.

This thesis presents the mathematical anatomy of mirror symmetry at the level of detail a doctoral student in algebraic or symplectic geometry would need. The exposition is organized around the two topological string theories introduced by Witten's twisting of the N=(2,2) supersymmetric sigma model:

- the **A-model**, depending only on the symplectic (Kähler) structure, whose observables are Gromov–Witten invariants;
- the **B-model**, depending only on the complex structure, whose observables are period integrals and variations of Hodge structure.

Mirror symmetry asserts their equivalence across a mirror pair. We then examine three refinements that transformed the subject from a collection of miraculous identities into a structural program:

1. **Givental's mirror theorem** [6], which proved the genus-zero mirror prediction for the quintic via equivariant localization;
2. **Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry** [3], which categorifies the duality as an equivalence of triangulated categories;
3. **The Strominger–Yau–Zaslow conjecture** [4], which gives the duality a *geometric* origin as T-duality along special Lagrangian torus fibrations.

## 2 Background

### Calabi–Yau manifolds and Hodge symmetry

A **Calabi–Yau threefold** is a compact Kähler manifold X of complex dimension 3 with trivial canonical bundle and vanishing first Betti number. By Yau's proof of the Calabi conjecture, such a manifold admits a unique Ricci-flat Kähler metric in each Kähler class. The Hodge numbers h^{p,q} = dim H^q(X, Omega^p) of a Calabi–Yau threefold fit into the familiar diamond

| | q=0 | q=1 | q=2 | q=3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p=3 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| p=2 | 0 | h^{2,1} | h^{1,1} | 0 |
| p=1 | 0 | h^{1,1} | h^{2,1} | 0 |
| p=0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

so that, up to the two independent numbers **h^{1,1}** (the dimension of the Kähler moduli space) and **h^{2,1}** (the dimension of the complex structure moduli space), the entire Hodge theory of X is captured by a pair of integers. Mirror symmetry was first noticed as the empirical observation that Calabi–Yau threefolds seem to occur in *pairs* (X, Y) with

> *Mirror Hodge relation:* h^{1,1}(X) = h^{2,1}(Y) and h^{2,1}(X) = h^{1,1}(Y).

The quintic threefold — a smooth degree-5 hypersurface in complex projective 4-space — has (h^{1,1}, h^{2,1}) = (1, 101), while its mirror has (101, 1) [1][8].

### The quintic and its mirror construction

Let X be the zero locus of a generic homogeneous quintic polynomial in P^4. Greene and Plesser observed that orbifolding X by a suitable group of phase symmetries (a (Z/5)^3 action) and resolving the singularities yields a Calabi–Yau threefold with the *reflected* Hodge diamond — the first explicit mirror pair. Candelas et al. refined this into a one-parameter family Y_z of mirrors, where the parameter z controls the complex structure, and computed the periods of the holomorphic three-form Omega(z) as solutions of a fourth-order Picard–Fuchs equation [1]. A clear mathematical exposition of this pipeline, from periods to curve counts, was given by Morrison [2].

### Toric mirrors and Batyrev duality

A far-reaching generalization was provided by Batyrev: Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces in toric varieties admit mirrors constructed from *dual reflexive polytopes* [7]. If Delta is a reflexive lattice polytope and Delta^* its polar dual, the anticanonical hypersurfaces in the associated toric varieties form a mirror pair. This construction placed hundreds of examples on a rigorous combinatorial footing and is the foundation of the toric approach to mirror symmetry developed further by Hori and Vafa via gauged linear sigma models [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our exposition follows a disciplined mathematical methodology with three pillars.

**Pillar 1 — Enumerative geometry via the A-model.** We define Gromov–Witten invariants through the moduli stack of stable maps, Mbar_{g,n}(X, beta), and its virtual fundamental class. The genus-zero invariants N_d for the quintic assemble into the A-model prepotential F_0(t), whose third derivatives are the (quantum-corrected) Yukawa couplings.

**Pillar 2 — Hodge theory via the B-model.** We study the variation of Hodge structure on H^3(Y_z) over the complex-structure moduli space, the Gauss–Manin connection, and the Picard–Fuchs differential operator annihilating the periods. The mirror map t(z) is extracted from the ratio of logarithmic to holomorphic periods near the large-complex-structure limit, a boundary point of maximally unipotent monodromy [2].

**Pillar 3 — Categorical and geometric lifts.** We state Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry as an equivalence D^b Coh(X^v) ~= D^pi Fuk(X) [3], and the SYZ conjecture as the existence of dual special Lagrangian torus fibrations over a common base [4][9].

The technical spine of the thesis is the identity

> *Mirror identity (genus zero):* the instanton-corrected Yukawa coupling on X equals the period-derived Yukawa coupling on Y after the change of variables t = t(z),

which we verify numerically for the quintic and explain conceptually through Givental's proof [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### The Quintic Threefold and the Mirror Map

The mirror family of Candelas et al. is the one-parameter family of quintics in P^4 given schematically by

```tla+
---------------- MODULE QuinticMirror ----------------
VARIABLES z, psi
MirrorFamily == psi^5 * (x1^5 + x2^5 + x3^5 + x4^5 + x5^5)
                 - 5 * psi * x1 * x2 * x3 * x4 * x5 = 0
\* quotient by (Z/5Z)^3 phase symmetries, then crepant resolution
\* complex structure modulus: z = (5 psi)^(-5)
=====================================================
```

The periods Pi_i(z) of the holomorphic three-form satisfy the Picard–Fuchs operator

```haskell
-- Picard-Fuchs operator for the mirror quintic, theta = z d/dz
picardFuchs :: Operator
picardFuchs = theta^4
  - 5*z * (5*theta + 1) * (5*theta + 2) * (5*theta + 3) * (5*theta + 4)
```

A basis of solutions near z = 0 consists of one holomorphic series w_0(z) and logarithmic companions w_1(z), w_2(z), w_3(z). The **mirror map** is defined by

> *Definition (mirror map):* t(z) = w_1(z) / w_0(z), with q = exp(2 pi i t) the canonical coordinate on Kähler moduli.

The point z = 0 is the *large complex structure limit*, characterized by maximally unipotent monodromy of the period vector; it is mirror to the large-volume limit of X [2]. The B-model Yukawa coupling

Y(z) = integral over Y_z of Omega wedge d^3 Omega / dz^3

transforms under the mirror map into the A-model generating function K(q) = 5 + sum_{d>=1} n_d d^3 q^d / (1 - q^d), where the integers n_d are the instanton numbers [1].

### Gromov–Witten Theory and the A-Model

The A-model is a topological twist of the sigma model that localizes on holomorphic maps. Mathematically, fix a curve class beta in H_2(X, Z) and consider the Deligne–Mumford stack Mbar_{g,n}(X, beta) of stable maps from n-pointed genus-g curves. It carries a **virtual fundamental class** [Mbar_{g,n}(X,beta)]^vir of the expected dimension, and the Gromov–Witten invariants are intersection numbers

GW_{g,beta}(gamma_1, ..., gamma_n) = integral over [Mbar]^vir of ev_1^*(gamma_1) cup ... cup ev_n^*(gamma_n).

For the quintic threefold at genus zero, the relevant numbers N_d count (with multiplicity and multiple-cover contributions) rational curves of degree d. The celebrated table begins:

| degree d | instanton number n_d |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2,875 |
| 2 | 609,250 |
| 3 | 317,206,375 |
| 4 | 242,467,530,000 |

The entry n_1 = 2875 was known classically (Cayley, Schubert); n_2 = 609250 was computed by Katz with substantial effort; n_3 and beyond were out of reach of classical enumerative geometry until mirror symmetry predicted them [1][2][8]. The multiple-cover formula relates the raw Gromov–Witten invariants N_d to the integral instanton numbers n_d via N_d = sum_{k dividing d} n_{d/k} / k^3, a relation later given a rigorous foundation in the work of Gopakumar and Vafa.

A compact computational sketch of extracting n_d from the mirror map is:

```python
# Extracting quintic instanton numbers from the B-model (schematic)
def mirror_map_series(z, order):
    w0 = holomorphic_period(z, order)      # Picard-Fuchs solution
    w1 = logarithmic_period(z, order)      # w1 ~ w0 * log(z) + ...
    return w1 / w0                         # t(z), the mirror map

def yukawa_B(z, order):
    Omega = holomorphic_threeform(z, order)
    return integrate_wedge(Omega, d3_dz3(Omega))   # triple derivative pairing

def instanton_numbers(z_series, yukawa, max_degree):
    q = exp(2*pi*1j * z_series)            # pass to canonical coordinate
    K = expand(yukawa_in_q(q))             # K(q) = 5 + sum n_d d^3 q^d/(1-q^d)
    return mobius_invert_multiple_covers(K, max_degree)
```

### Variations of Hodge Structure and the B-Model

The B-model depends only on the complex structure of Y. The middle cohomology H^3(Y_z, C) carries a polarized variation of Hodge structure of weight 3 over the moduli space, with Hodge filtration F^3 subset F^2 subset F^1 subset F^0 satisfying Griffiths transversality: the Gauss–Manin connection nabla sends F^p into F^{p-1} tensor Omega^1. The holomorphic three-form Omega(z) spans F^3, and its periods

Pi_gamma(z) = integral over gamma of Omega(z), gamma in H_3(Y, Z),

are the B-model observables. The Picard–Fuchs equations are the differential relations among periods induced by the Gauss–Manin connection; for one-parameter families they are ordinary differential equations whose order equals the rank of the relevant cohomology group (four for the quintic mirror, since h^3 = 4 = 2 + 2 h^{2,1} with h^{2,1} = 1) [2].

The passage from periods to enumerative predictions proceeds in four steps:

1. Solve the Picard–Fuchs equation near the large-complex-structure limit.
2. Form the mirror map t(z) = w_1/w_0 and invert it as z(q).
3. Compute the B-model Yukawa coupling Y(z) from triple derivatives of periods.
4. Re-expand Y(z(q)) in q; the coefficients encode the instanton numbers n_d.

This is the precise mathematical content of the Candelas et al. calculation, as decoded for mathematicians by Morrison [2].

### Homological Mirror Symmetry

Kontsevich proposed that mirror symmetry is, at its core, an equivalence of *categories* [3]:

> *Theorem (Kontsevich's conjecture, 1994):* For a mirror pair (X, X^v) of Calabi–Yau manifolds, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^pi Fuk(X) ~= D^b Coh(X^v), where the left side is the derived Fukaya category of Lagrangian submanifolds (the A-side) and the right side is the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves (the B-side).

On the A-side, objects are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with brane data (grading, spin structure, flat unitary local system), and morphism spaces are Floer cochain complexes whose differential counts holomorphic disks. On the B-side, objects are complexes of coherent sheaves and morphisms are Ext groups. The conjecture thus identifies Floer theory with homological algebra — a bridge between symplectic topology and algebraic geometry of astonishing depth.

Proven instances include:

- **Elliptic curves**, by Polishchuk and Zaslow, matching the Fukaya category of a symplectic torus with coherent sheaves on the mirror elliptic curve;
- **The quartic K3 and Fermat-type hypersurfaces**, via matrix factorizations and Orlov's equivalences;
- **Toric Calabi–Yau manifolds**, via the coherent–constructible correspondence of Fang, Liu, Treumann and Zaslow;
- **The quintic threefold**, in work of Sheridan and others using immersed Lagrangian spheres and their Floer theory.

The categorical viewpoint explains *why* curve counts match period integrals: both are shadows (Euler characteristics, or Hochschild invariants) of equivalent categories. It also predicts the matching of higher-genus data and open-string invariants.

### The SYZ Conjecture and T-Duality

The most geometric proposal for the origin of mirror symmetry is due to Strominger, Yau and Zaslow [4]:

> *Conjecture (SYZ, 1996):* If X and X^v are mirror Calabi–Yau manifolds, then both admit *special Lagrangian torus fibrations* mu: X -> B and mu^v: X^v -> B over a common real base B, which are fiberwise dual: over a regular point b in B, the fibers mu^{-1}(b) and (mu^v)^{-1}(b) are dual tori. The mirror transformation is T-duality applied fiberwise.

The physical intuition is compelling.

The SYZ picture faces two famous difficulties, both active research areas [9]:

- **Singular fibers.** Special Lagrangian fibrations inevitably acquire singular fibers over a *discriminant locus* Delta subset B of real codimension 2. Fiberwise dualization must be corrected by instanton effects from holomorphic disks ending on the fibers — the program carried out by Fukaya, Oh, Ohta and Ono in Lagrangian Floer theory, and in tropical form by Gross and Siebert.
- **Existence.** Constructing special Lagrangian torus fibrations on compact Calabi–Yau threefolds is extremely hard; complete results exist mainly for toric and semi-flat cases, and for the quintic via the work of Ruan and others on Lagrangian (not necessarily special) fibrations.

Despite these difficulties, the SYZ heuristic underlies the Hori–Vafa derivation of mirrors from gauged linear sigma models [5], where T-duality is performed explicitly on the phases of chiral fields, and the Gross–Siebert program, which reconstructs mirror pairs tropically from affine manifolds with singularities.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We now record the hard mathematical evidence — the theorems and verified computations that elevate mirror symmetry from conjecture to established mathematics in genus zero.

**Evidence 1 — The quintic numbers check out.** Every instanton number predicted in [1] that has been independently computed by algebraic-geometric means has agreed. The counts n_1 = 2875 (19th century), n_2 = 609250 (Katz), and n_3 = 317206375 (Ellingsrud–Strømme) all match the mirror prediction [2][8].

**Evidence 2 — Givental's mirror theorem.** Givental proved that the generating function of genus-zero Gromov–Witten invariants of the quintic (and complete intersections in projective space) coincides with the hypergeometric series solving the Picard–Fuchs equation, using torus-equivariant localization on the moduli space of stable maps [6]. Lian, Liu and Yau gave an independent proof via the mirror principle. Together these results make the genus-zero mirror conjecture a *theorem* for this class.

**Evidence 3 — Batyrev's combinatorial mirrors.** For every reflexive polytope, Batyrev's dual-polytope construction provably exchanges the Hodge numbers, and the resulting pairs satisfy the expected Picard–Fuchs / Gromov–Witten correspondence in hundreds of checked examples [7].

**Evidence 4 — Homological mirror symmetry theorems.** The HMS equivalence is now a theorem for elliptic curves, for large classes of toric varieties and toric Calabi–Yau manifolds, for Fermat-type hypersurfaces via matrix factorizations, and in substantial cases for the quintic threefold itself [3]. Each such theorem simultaneously verifies infinitely many numerical predictions.

**Evidence 5 — SYZ in the semi-flat and toric settings.** Where special Lagrangian fibrations can be constructed — toric Calabi–Yau manifolds, K3 surfaces, and semi-flat examples — fiberwise T-duality provably reproduces the expected mirror, including the instanton corrections predicted by wall-crossing formulas [4][9].

A Rust-flavored sketch of the logical dependency among these results:

```rust
// Dependency graph of mirror symmetry evidence (schematic)
fn evidence_chain() {
    let quintic_numbers = candelas_et_al_1991();       // [1] predictions
    let classical_checks = vec![2875, 609250, 317206375]; // verified [2]
    assert!(quintic_numbers.prefix(3) == classical_checks);
    let mirror_theorem = givental_1998(quintic_numbers);  // [6] proof
    let hms = kontsevich_1994(mirror_theorem);             // [3] categorification
    let syz = strominger_yau_zaslow_1996(hms);            // [4] geometric origin
    println!("genus-zero mirror symmetry: established");
}
```

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires stating what mirror symmetry does *not* yet deliver.

**Higher genus remains largely conjectural.** The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation predicts higher-genus Gromov–Witten invariants recursively from genus zero, but a complete mathematical theory of the B-model at higher genus — let alone a proof — exists only in special cases (notably the work of Guo, Janda and Ruan on finite-generation properties).

**The SYZ conjecture is open in general.** No special Lagrangian torus fibration is known on a *generic* compact Calabi–Yau threefold, and the singular-fiber corrections needed to make fiberwise duality rigorous are understood only in favorable geometries [9]. The discriminant locus, of real codimension 2 in the base, is where all the subtlety — and all the interesting enumerative information — resides.

**Non-toric mirrors are scarce.** Batyrev's construction [7] and the Hori–Vafa GLSM framework [5] cover toric complete intersections beautifully, but the vast majority of Calabi–Yau threefolds (for instance, most of the hundreds of thousands of known examples) have no known mirror construction, let alone a proven duality.

**Convergence and analytic issues.** The mirror map is defined as a formal power series near a boundary point of moduli space; its analytic continuation, the global structure of the stringy Kähler moduli space, and the behavior at conifold and orbifold points involve delicate analysis that is complete only in examples.

**Categorical gaps.** Homological mirror symmetry [3] is proven in many cases but not for a general compact Calabi–Yau threefold; the Fukaya category of the quintic, in particular, remains only partially understood, and split-generation by explicit Lagrangians is a deep open problem.

These limitations are not defects but frontiers: each marks an active research program.

## 7 Conclusion

Mirror symmetry began as a shocking physics computation — the exact enumeration of rational curves on the quintic threefold — and has matured into one of the deepest organizing principles in modern geometry [1][8]. Its two classical faces, the **A-model** of Gromov–Witten theory and the **B-model** of Hodge theory, are linked by the mirror map through Picard–Fuchs equations; its categorical face, **homological mirror symmetry**, identifies Floer theory with derived algebraic geometry [3]; and its geometric face, the **SYZ conjecture**, proposes that the entire duality is fiberwise T-duality along special Lagrangian torus fibrations [4][9].

The genus-zero story for toric complete intersections is now essentially complete, thanks to Givental's proof [6], Batyrev's construction [7], and the Hori–Vafa physical derivation [5]. What remains — higher genus, singular SYZ fibers, non-toric mirrors, and the full Fukaya category of compact threefolds — constitutes some of the most important open territory at the interface of algebraic geometry, symplectic topology, and string theory. Mirror symmetry has already repaid its debt to physics many times over in pure mathematics; its next chapters promise to do so again.
