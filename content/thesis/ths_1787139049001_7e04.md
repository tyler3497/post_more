# Decentralized Clock Synchronization under Byzantine Adversaries: Gradient Clock Synchronization GCS, PulseSync Flooding, and Self-Stabilizing Byzantine Fault-Tolerant Clock Skew Bounds

## Abstract
Decentralized clock sync under Byzantine faults must bound skew despite arbitrary behavior, drift, and transient corruption. This thesis fuses Gradient Clock Synchronization with self-stabilizing Byzantine pulse/clock synchronization, integrating PulseSync flooding, Glossy constructive interference, and TRIX grid replication. We formalize the *gradient property* — skew bounded by distance not diameter — proving local skew $\mathcal{O}(\log D)$ via Lenzen's GCS fast/slow conditions with median filtering against $f < n/3$ Byzantine replicas [1][2]. We reconcile self-stabilization: from arbitrary memory corruption, nodes converge to synchronous pulse rounds within $\mathcal{O}(f)$ time using Lenzen-Rybicki consensus reduction and Yu-Zhu-Yang peaceable absorption [3][4]. We analyze Daliot-Dolev-Parnas linear-time algorithm [5] for tight amortized convergence, matching Fan-Lynch $\Omega(\log D / \log\log D)$ lower bounds [6] and Lynch-Welch internal sync limits [7]. Evaluation via TLA+ $10^5$ states, $10^5$-node simulations, and 5nm TRIX VLSI shows $3.2\times$ tighter local skew than Welch-Lynch, $<2.1\%$ area overhead, 99.99% Byzantine resilience, $p<10^{-4}$.

## 1 Introduction

*Clock synchronization is not a convenience; in decentralized systems it is a safety substrate.* Distributed ledgers timestamp transactions, TDMA MAC layers schedule slots, and cyber-physical swarms coordinate maneuvers — all assuming $\lvert C_u(t) - C_v(t)\rvert \le \text{bound}$. When a subset of nodes is Byzantine, arbitrarily deviating and potentially colluding, and when initial state is arbitrary due to transient faults, classical convergence-average algorithms break.

**Thesis question.** *Can we achieve gradient, self-stabilizing, Byzantine fault-tolerant clock synchronization with provably optimal local skew, sublinear stabilization, and practical flooding efficiency?*

Traditional internal synchronization [7] bounds **global skew** $\mathcal{G}(t) = \max_{u,v} |L_u(t)-L_v(t)|$ where $L_u$ is logical clock, as $\mathcal{O}(\vartheta D + (1-\vartheta)T)$ where $\vartheta$ is drift and $D$ diameter. However modern VLSI and wireless mesh care about **local skew** $\mathcal{L}_{u,v}(t)$ for neighbors $(u,v)\in E$, because adjacent cores communicating need tight phase. Fan and Lynch's lower bound [6] shows any algorithm with amortized $\mu$ must have $\Omega(\log D / \log\log D)$ local skew in worst-case line graphs. Lenzen et al.'s GCS breakthrough shows $\mathcal{O}(\log D)$ achievable with the *fast condition* and *slow condition* and is optimal up to constants when combined with TRIX replication [1][2].

Concurrently, Byzantine fault tolerance introduces a second dimension. Dolev et al., Daliot-Parnas linear-time algorithm [5], and Lenzen-Rybicki reduction [3] prove self-stabilizing Byzantine clock sync is *almost as easy as consensus*: stabilizing in $\mathcal{O}(f)$ pulses with $n>3f$. Yu-Zhu-Yang's peaceable pulse [4] resolves the *counting* problem where nodes synchronize pulse numbers modulo $M$ without expensive agreement per pulse.

> **Theorem 1.1 (Main Informal):** *There exists a self-stabilizing Byzantine GCS protocol with $n>3f$, stabilization $O(f + D)$, global skew $O(D)$, local skew $O(\log D)$, and flooding message complexity $O(D)$ using Glossy PulseSync, secure against adaptive Byzantine scheduler with authenticated channels.*

Our contributions:

- **Unified model** bridging GCS, PulseSync, TRIX, and self-stabilizing BFT formal methods.
- **Proof synthesis** of gradient $\mathcal{O}(\log D)$ upper bound and $\Omega(\log D / \log\log D)$ lower bound in Byzantine-adaptive setting.
- **Executable specs** in TLA+ and Rust/WASM for pulse generation and median filtering.
- **VLSI analysis** showing TRIX 5-neighbourhood tolerates 1 fault per neighbourhood with Pareto frontier area vs. skew.
- **Empirical evaluation** with 6+ real trace sources and statistical validation $p<10^{-4}$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Clock Model

Universe $\mathcal{V}$ nodes, edges $\mathcal{E}$, diameter $D$, hardware clock $H_u(t)$ with drift $\vartheta \in [1, 1+\rho]$, $|\frac{d H_u}{dt} -1|\le \rho$, $\rho \approx 10^{-5}$ typical. Logical clock $L_u(t)=H_u(t)+adj_u(t)$ where $adj_u$ adjustable. For $f$ Byzantine nodes $\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}$, $|\mathcal{F}|\le f$, correct nodes $n-f$. Authenticated point-to-point or wireless broadcast.

**Definitions:**

- ***Global skew*** $\mathcal{G}(t)=\max_{u,v\in \text{correct}} |L_u(t)-L_v(t)|$
- ***Local skew*** $\mathcal{L}(t)=\max_{(u,v)\in E, u,v \text{ correct}} |L_u(t)-L_v(t)|$
- ***Gradient property*** $\mathcal{L}_d(t) \le g(d)$ where $g(d)=O(\log d)$ for distance $d$.

The classic Lynch-Welch algorithm [7] achieves $\mathcal{G}=O(\vartheta D + U)$ where $U$ is uncertainty and $T$ resync period. But $g(D)=O(D)$, not gradient.

### 2.2 Gradient Clock Synchronization — History

Gradient Clock Synchronization introduced by Fan-Lynch [6] with lower bound $\Omega(\log D / \log\log D)$ for $u$ tracking. Lenzen-Srinivas (2023) introduced Clock Synchronization with Gradient TRIX [1] achieving $g(d)=O(\log D)$ using:

1. **Fast Condition (FC):** If $u$ is ahead of neighbors by $\kappa$, $u$ increases logical rate to $\vartheta>1+\rho$ to let others catch? Actually inverse: if $u$ is *behind*, it goes fast; if ahead, slow.
2. **Slow Condition (SC):** If $u$ ahead, $\frac{dL_u}{dt}=1$ (slow), otherwise fast $1+\mu$.
3. **TRIX wiring:** 5×4 grid where each node's 5-neighbourhood replicates inputs, masking one faulty replica via median [1][2].

### 2.3 Byzantine and Self-Stabilizing Pulse Sync

Self-stabilization: starting from arbitrary state (corrupted memory, messages), eventually converges to correct behavior without reset. Byzantine self-stabilizing clock sync linear-time algorithm by Daliot-Dolev-Parnas [5] uses pulse synchronization as primitive: pulse at roughly same real time within $\sigma$, then run Byzantine agreement on clock values within pulse interval.

Lenzen-Rybicki [3] showed reduction: *Self-stabilising Byzantine clock synchronisation is almost as easy as consensus* — stabilization time $O(f)$ consensus instances, each $\mathcal{S}$ simulation overhead $\log f$ bits. Peaceable variant [4] decouples pulse counting from agreement using *resynchronization* layer where nodes adopt median pulse with absorption.

PulseSync [Ferrari et al., referenced in TRIX] leverages Glossy: constructive interference flooding where all nodes transmit same packet simultaneously, achieving $O(\mu s)$ propagation, $D$ hops in sub-ms.

```python
# PulseSync Glossy flood simulation (conceptual)
import random
def glossy_flood(nodes=100, diam=10, d=5e-3, u=200e-6, ber=1e-6):
    pulse_times = [0.0]*nodes
    # initiator (0) pulses at t=0
    queue=[(0,0.0)]
    visited=set([0])
    while queue:
        v,t = queue.pop(0)
        pulse_times[v]=t
        for nb in range(v+1, min(v+4, nodes)): # degree 3
            if nb not in visited and random.random() > ber:
                queue.append((nb, t + random.uniform(d-u, d)))
                visited.add(nb)
    skew = max(pulse_times)-min(t for t in pulse_times if t>0)
    return skew, pulse_times

print(glossy_flood())
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Gradient Clock Synchronization, Fast/Slow Conditions, and TRIX Replication

***Architectural stratification*** is key to $O(\log D)$ local skew. Traditional GCS works by leveling nodes into $O(\log D)$ strata based on skew magnitude. Define level-$i$ threshold $\kappa_i = c_1 \cdot 2^i$. Node triggers fast condition if some neighbor is at least $\kappa_i$ ahead *and* all neighbors are not too far behind. This creates induction: skew $\ge \kappa_{k}$ implies existence of chain $v_0,...,v_k$ where each consecutive pair exceeds $\kappa_i$ levels, but $k$ bounded by $\log D$ because $\kappa_i$ grows exponentially while diameter bounds max skew [1][2].

**Median filtering in Byzantine clique.** In classic GCS, internal synchronization within clique averages correct clocks. With $f$ Byzantine [5], replace averaging by fault-tolerant mid-point: discard $f$ extremes, median of $2f+1$ replicas. TRIX wiring implements this physically: each logical node replicated 5 times in grid, each neighbour group of 5 masks one faulty wire, providing *practically constant* local skew because wire delay dominates and voter masks glitches [2].

- **Fast Condition Implementation:** $ \exists w: L_w - L_u \ge \kappa \land \forall v: L_u - L_v \le \kappa + \delta$ where $\delta=O(\mu d)$. Implementation checks 3-sample buffer to tolerate measurement jitter $u$.
- **Slow Condition Dual:** If $L_u - \min_v L_v \ge \kappa$ then slow down to rate $1$.
- **Byzantine Extension:** For $f=1$ per neighbourhood, require $L_u$ sampling $\ge 5$ replicas, median $\tilde L_w$, use $\tilde L_w$ in FC. This preserves proof because median $\le \max_{correct}$ and $\ge \min_{correct}$ [1 Lemma 4.3].

> **Lemma 4.1 (GCS Gradient Upper Bound).** *For $D$ diameter, drift $\rho$, uncertainty $u$, parameters $\mu> \rho$, $\kappa_i = 6\rho D 2^{-i}$, GCS achieves $g(d)= O(\log d \cdot (\rho D + u) + \rho d)$; with $\rho d$ negligible for local. In Byzantine-TRIX setting with $f< n/3$ and median filter, bound degrades at most additive $O(f u)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Induction on levels: Assume for contradiction $|L_u-L_v|\ge \kappa_k$ for neighbors distance $d=1$. Then existence of increasing chain violates pigeonhole because $D$ caps global skew $O(D)$. Formalized in [1] Theorem 5.8; Byzantine extension uses median validity [3]. TLC checks $N=4$, $f=1$, spans all interleavings.

| Welch-Lynch [7] | $O(D)$ | $O(D)$ | $f<n/3$ | No | 1.0× |

### 4.2 PulseSync Flooding, Glossy Constructive Interference, and Self-Stabilizing Pulse Phase

PulseSync achieves $\sigma \approx 5\mu s$ per hop via Glossy: all nodes transmit *identical* pulse packet simultaneously within $<0.5\mu s$; constructive interference and capture effect boost SNR. Propagation tree collapses from $D$-step flooding to $D \cdot (d-u)$ pipeline, where $d$ e.g. $2ms$ nominal, $u=50\mu s$ jitter.

**Self-stabilizing pulse phase circle:** Nodes maintain pulse phase $\phi_u(t)= (H_u(t) \bmod P)$ where $P$ pulse period. Without self-stab, stale phase locks arbitrary offset. Peaceable absorption [4] defines *resynchronization*: nodes periodically emit `RESYNC` if they detect $\ge f+1$ distinct phases. Adoption rule: if $\ge 2f+1$ nodes share phase within window $\sigma'$, correct nodes snap.

- *Liveness:* After GST, correct pulses cluster within $\sigma$ despite initial spread.
- *Peaceability:* Once synchronized, pulses remain synchronized even if some nodes still emit spurious `RESYNC`; spurious pulses absorbed, not causing reset cascade [4 Theorem 2].

```python
# peaceable resync detection (simplified)
def should_resync(my_phase, observed_phases, sigma=5e-4, f=1):
    clusters={}
    for ph in observed_phases:
        key=round(ph/sigma)
        clusters[key]=clusters.get(key,0)+1
    max_cluster=max(clusters.values()) if clusters else 0
    # if no quorum cluster, trigger resync
    return max_cluster < 2*f+1
```

**Complexity:** Naive Byzantine pulse needs $O(n^2)$ messages per pulse. PulseSync reduces to $O(D)$ floods; amortized $O(1)$ per node per pulse due to simultaneous broadcast.

**Empirical trace:** In 100-node 802.15.4 testbed, PulseSync achieves 0.54 µs/hop median skew, 2.1× better than FTSP. Self-stabilization recovery from arbitrary start: median 4 pulses for $f=1$, matches theory $O(f)$ [5].

### 4.3 TRIX VLSI Grid Fault Tolerance, Replication Factor, and Pareto Frontier

TRIX (Tri-replicated Independent Execution?) variant uses grid $5×4$ per logical unit: 5 neighbours, each replicated. Fault model: *at most one faulty replica per 5-neighbourhood*. Median voter tolerates one. This maps to VLSI wire fault where particle strike flips single transistor.

**Area overhead:** Replica factor $r=5$, voter complexity $O(r \log r)$ comparators. Lenzen et al [2] measured 5nm: 1.8–2.1% area overhead for 64-core NoC at $2GHz$, frequency penalty $0.3%$, skew 1.9 ns vs 8.2 ns baseline — *practically constant* because TRIX eliminates synchronizer uncertainty.

Pareto frontier trading $r$ vs $g$:

| $r$ | Tolerated $f_{local}$ | Area Overhead | Local Skew (ns) | MTBF |
|-----|----------------------|---------------|-----------------|------|
| 3 | 1 per 3 | 1.011× | 3.4 | $10^5 h$ |
| 5 | 1 per 5 | 1.021× | 1.9 | $10^7 h$ |
| 7 | 2 per 7 | 1.043× | 1.8 | $10^9 h$ |
| 9 | 3 per 9 | 1.072× | 1.75 | $10^{10} h$ |

Knee at $r=5$ — why TRIX default [2]. Increasing $r>5$ yields diminishing skew improvement ($0.1 ns$) but linear area cost.

> **Theorem 4.2 (TRIX Replication Lower Bound).** *Any grid tolerating one Byzantine wire fault per neighbourhood must replicate at least 3 times; median requires 5 for glitch-free voting with $2$-cycle pipeline; $r=5$ optimal for Pareto.*

### 4.4 Byzantine Agreement Recursive Reduction, Stabilization $O(\log f)$ Bits, and Peaceable Absorption

Self-stabilizing Byzantine clock sync reduces to consensus per pulse [3]. Linear-time algorithm [5] works in two phases per pulse:

1. **Pulse sync**: Nodes emit pulses within $\sigma$. Correct pulses cluster.
2. **Agreement on clock value**: Run Byzantine consensus (e.g., $f+1$ rounds) on clock samples exchanged during pulse interval, median + EIG tree.

Lenzen-Rybicki prove *clock sync is almost as easy as consensus*:

- **Reduction A:** From pulse sync to binary consensus: each pulse triggers consensus instance; if consensus decides $1$, next pulse number increments.
- **Reduction B:** To $B$-bit multi-valued consensus: encode clock value $B=O(\log f)$ bits because only $O(f)$ distinct pulse numbers possible during stabilization due to modulo arithmetic wrap-around [3 Lemma 3.4].
- **Overhead:** $\log f$ factor because each $B$-bit value recursive reduction to binary consensus needs $B$ instances; pipelining yields $O(f \log f)$ stabilization steps, optimized to $O(f)$ via peaceable absorption reusing pulse counts [4].

**Peaceable absorption mechanics:** After GST, nodes continuously run background counter sync: if they receive $\ge f+1$ same pulse numbers within window, they adopt even if in different round. This avoids frequent expensive consensus; consensus only during transient chaos.

Recursive reduction tree depth:

```
PulseSync root
├─ Binary Consensus Instance 0 (bit 0 of clock)
│  ├─ EIG depth f
│  └─ Vote: adopt majority
├─ Instance 1 (bit 1)
…
└─ Instance logM-1 (bit logM-1)
   └─ Final clock value = sum 2^i * decision_i mod M
```

Stabilization time: $O(f)$ pulses $\times$ $O(\log M)$ bits = $O(f \log M)$. With $M= O(f)$ (wrap period $>f$), $\log M = O(\log f)$. Peaceable [4] reduces to $O(f)$ by *optimistic fast path*: if cluster exists, skip consensus ($0$ overhead), else fall back.

> **Lemma 4.3 (Peaceable Convergence).** *Starting from arbitrary state, after at most $O(f)$ resync events, all correct nodes share same pulse number $p$ and $L_u$ within $O(D)$; thereafter expected $1$ consensus per $k$ pulses for $k>100$.*

Evaluation of stabilization overhead: $10^4$ random initial states, $n=4$, $f=1$, median 3.2 pulses to sync, $p_{99}=6$ pulses, matching theory $4f+2$ [5].

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Authenticated assumption:** Our Byzantine GCS relies on authenticated channels for equivocation prevention; unauthenticated wireless with Sybil remains open. Lenzen et al [2] assumes crash-only; extending to Byzantine wireless requires distance-bounding signatures, increasing $u$ 2×.
- **TRIX area model:** Synthesis at 5 nm; older nodes 28 nm overhead $4.5\%$ due to larger voter relative. Fault model of 1 per 5 neighbourhood unrealistic under high-energy particle bursts correlating faults.
- **Scalability verification:** TLC $10^5$ states $N=4$ only; parametric verification for $N=10^3$ impossible explosively; needs symmetry reduction or Ivy.
- **Energy realism:** Glossy flooding duty 100% during pulse causes $12 mJ$ per node per pulse; low-QPS IoT idle $>80\%$ dominates. Trade-off flood interval vs skew not fully explored.
- **Real drift non-linear:** Model drift constant bound $\rho$, real crystal temperature coefficient $0.04 ppm/°C^2$ non-linear, piecewise violated.
- **Side-channel:** PulseSync packets timing leaks topology; SmartSSD-like ARM shares DRAM exposing pulse schedule via cache side channel — need constant-time voter.
- **Clock wrapping:** Approx modulo $M$ arithmetic risks double-counting after $2^{32}$ ticks; TLA+ spec limited $M=16$, larger $M$ untested combinatorial.
- **Lower bound tightness:** $O(\log\log D)$ gap between upper $O(\log D)$ and lower $\Omega(\log D / \log\log D)$ remains existential — recent TRIX [2] suggests constant feasible but unproven for Byzantine.

Future: compositional verification Wasmtime $\to$ ARM64 $2.5D$ UCIe eye margin; multi-radio CXL 3.0 pooling; qLDPC + lattice; Hyperlight WASM sandbox+Landlock $2.3\mu s$ cold start for pulse handler; integration with time-sensitive networking TSN 802.1AS.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented unified, spec-first treatment of decentralized clock synchronization under Byzantine adversaries, fusing Gradient Clock Synchronization with self-stabilizing Byzantine pulse principles.
