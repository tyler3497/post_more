# Gradual Typing: Cast Calculi, the Blame Theorem, the Gradual Guarantee, and Space-Efficient Coercions

## Abstract

Gradual typing reconciles static and dynamic typing inside a single language by introducing the unknown type `?` and replacing type equality with a *consistency* relation. This thesis presents the metatheory that makes gradual typing principled rather than ad hoc: the cast calculi that make runtime checking explicit, the *blame theorem* of Wadler and Findler which guarantees that runtime failures are always charged to the less precisely typed party, the *gradual guarantee* of Siek et al. which relates the behavior of programs that differ only in their annotations, and the space-efficient coercion representations that keep runtime checking from silently breaking tail calls. Each component is developed with formal definitions, the key theorems with proof sketches, and a survey of the empirical evidence — from Reticulated Python to the performance crisis documented by Takikawa et al. — that motivates the theory. The result is a self-contained account of why modern gradually typed languages check what they check, blame whom they blame, and cost what they cost.

---

## 1 Introduction

Static and dynamic typing are usually cast as rivals, yet working programmers constantly move between them: a prototype begins as an untyped script and hardens into a typed program, while even fully typed systems interact with dynamically typed configuration, serialization, and foreign code. *Gradual typing*, introduced by Siek and Taha [1], integrates the two disciplines within a single language. The programmer controls, expression by expression, which regions are statically checked and which are deferred to runtime — and can evolve code between the two regimes without rewriting it.

The 2006 paper supplied the static core: an *unknown type* `?` for absent type information, a *consistency* relation `~` generalizing type equality, and a *precision* order `⊑` formalizing what it means for one program to be "more typed" than another. But it left the hard questions open. When a runtime check fails, *who is at fault* — the typed or the untyped code? If a programmer adds annotations to a working program, does its behavior stay the same? And what does runtime checking *cost* — can it turn a tail-recursive loop into a space leak?

This thesis answers through four pillars. *Cast calculi* make runtime checks explicit as casts inserted during elaboration [1][2]. The *blame theorem* of Wadler and Findler [3] proves blame always lies with the less precisely typed side — "well-typed programs can't be blamed." The *gradual guarantee* of Siek, Vitousek, Cimini, and Boyland [4] pins down how behavior is preserved as annotations change, separating genuine gradual typing from mere optional typing. And *space-efficient coercions* — from Henglein's coercion calculus through Herman et al. to threesomes and blame-annotated coercions — ensure the runtime machinery does not asymptotically degrade the programs it monitors.

## 2 Background

### The unknown type and consistency

Gradual typing adds one type, the *unknown type* `?`, to an otherwise ordinary static type system, and replaces type equality with the *consistency* relation `~` — equality *up to occurrences of `?`* [1]:

> **Definition (Consistency).** `T ~ U` is the least relation with `? ~ T` and `T ~ ?` for every `T`; `B ~ B` for every base type `B`; and `T₁ → T₂ ~ U₁ → U₂` whenever `T₁ ~ U₁` and `T₂ ~ U₂`.

Consistency is reflexive and symmetric but deliberately *not transitive*: `Int ~ ?` and `? ~ Bool`, yet `Int ≁ Bool`. Transitivity would be fatal — if `?` were both top and bottom of a subtyping order, every type would relate to every other and static checking would be vacuous [1]. Consistency expresses *plausibility*: two types are consistent when they *could* coincide once the unknowns are resolved. Resolution is deferred to runtime, where casts perform the checks static reasoning could not.

### Precision: making "more typed" formal

*Precision* `T ⊑ U` ("`U` is more precise than `T`") makes type migration formal. `?` is the least precise element — `T ⊑ ?` for all `T` — and precision is congruent under constructors, so `(Int → ?) ⊑ (Int → Bool)` [1]. Precision lifts to terms: `e ⊑ e′` when `e′` replaces some annotations of `e` with more precise ones. Migration is then the search for `e′` with `e ⊑ e′` that still typechecks [4]. Unlike subtyping, precision is about *information content*, not substitutability: `Int ⊑ ?` even though the two are mutually consistent.

### Interoperation and contracts

Gradual typing builds on migratory typing: Tobin-Hochstadt and Felleisen showed how to move Scheme programs into Typed Scheme, placing higher-order contracts at the typed/untyped boundary with blame falling on the untyped side [5]. Findler and Felleisen's contracts [2] supplied the mechanism — a monitor that, on violation, *blames* either the term inside the contract or the surrounding context. Early gradual and hybrid systems all compiled to intermediate languages with explicit casts but *ignored blame*, leaving fault attribution unanswered [3].

### The cost question

Naive cast insertion has a quieter defect. In two mutually recursive functions with tail-position recursive calls, elaboration wraps each tail call in a cast, and the wrappers *accumulate* — a constant-space loop becomes linear-space [7]. The runtime representation of casts is thus a semantic obligation, not an implementation detail, leading directly to the coercions and threesomes of Section 4.5.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is the field's own: *make the dynamic checks explicit, then reason about the explicit calculus*. A gradually typed surface language elaborates into a *cast calculus* where every appeal to consistency becomes a syntactic cast `⟨T ⇒ U⟩ e`. The static system guarantees plausibility; the cast calculus guarantees *safety*, checking the rest at runtime and raising a *blame error* on failure.

We trace three generations. The first, implicit in Siek and Taha [1], uses higher-order wrappers: a function cast becomes a new function casting argument and result. The second, the *blame calculus* of Wadler and Findler [3], decorates each cast with a blame label `ℓ` and proves the blame theorem. The third replaces wrappers with *coercions* — first-class, composable cast representations in Henglein's style [6] — recovering space efficiency [7][8] and reunifying blame with coercions [9].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Consistency, precision, and the unknown type

The *Gradually Typed Lambda Calculus* innovates only in its type structure: ordinary static types plus `?`, with `~` wherever the simply typed calculus demands equality. Application, for instance, requires the operator's type to be *consistent* with some `T₁ → T₂`:

> **Rule (Application).** If `Γ ⊢ e₁ : T`, `Γ ⊢ e₂ : U`, `T ~ (V → W)`, and `U ~ V`, then `Γ ⊢ e₁ e₂ : W`.

Elaboration inserts the casts: `⟨T ⇒ (V → W)⟩ e₁` applied to `⟨U ⇒ V⟩ e₂`. The unknown type is a *bidirectional* membrane — values flow into `?` freely and out only under a check. The two relations are readily implementable, and every proof in Sections 4.3–4.4 is at bottom an induction over these definitions:

```haskell
data Ty = Dyn | TInt | TBool | TFun Ty Ty deriving (Eq, Show)

-- Consistency: equality up to Dyn. Reflexive, symmetric, NOT transitive.
consistent :: Ty -> Ty -> Bool
consistent Dyn _             = True
consistent _ Dyn             = True
consistent TInt TInt         = True
consistent TBool TBool       = True
consistent (TFun a b) (TFun c d) =
  consistent a c && consistent b d
consistent _ _               = False

-- Precision: t `precise` u means u is more precise. Dyn is least precise.
precise :: Ty -> Ty -> Bool
precise _ Dyn                = True
precise TInt TInt            = True
precise TBool TBool          = True
precise (TFun a b) (TFun c d) =
  precise a c && precise b d
precise _ _                  = False
```

| `~`       | `Int` | `Bool` | `?`   |
|-----------|-------|--------|-------|
| **`Int`** | ✓     | ✗      | ✓     |
| **`Bool`**| ✗     | ✓      | ✓     |
| **`?`**   | ✓     | ✓      | ✓     |

Every ✓ involving `?` becomes a runtime cast; every ✗ is rejected statically. Gradual typing is *not* dynamic typing with extra steps — it still rules out *implausibly* typed programs [4].

### 4.2 Cast calculi: from wrappers to the blame calculus

A cast `⟨T ⇒ U⟩ e` mediates between type `T` and a context expecting `U`, decomposing by type structure. Base-type casts check tags; casts to `?` *inject* a value with its tag; casts from `?` *project* by checking the tag; function casts become the classic wrapper:

```
⟨T₁ → T₂ ⇒ U₁ → U₂⟩^ℓ v  ⟶  λx. ⟨T₂ ⇒ U₂⟩^ℓ (v (⟨U₁ ⇒ T₁⟩^ℓ̄ x))
```

The domain cast runs *contravariantly*, and its blame label is *complemented* (`ℓ̄`): if the wrapped function fails because its *caller* supplied a bad argument, the caller — the context — is at fault [2][3]. The blame calculus makes labels syntactic: evaluation raises `blame ℓ` (positive — the term inside the cast is at fault) or `blame ℓ̄` (negative — the context is at fault). The Blame Theorem characterizes exactly when each can occur, via positive and negative subtyping:

> **Definition (Positive/negative subtyping).** `T <:⁺ U` and `T <:⁻ U`: base types relate to themselves under both; `?` to itself under both; ground types `G` (base types, `? → ?`) satisfy `G <:⁺ ?` and `? <:⁻ G`; functions relate by `T₁ → T₂ <:⁺ U₁ → U₂` iff `U₁ <:⁻ T₁` and `T₂ <:⁺ U₂`, with polarities swapped for `<:⁻`.

The two recombine into *naive subtyping* `T <:ₙ U`, under which a cast can never fail. Naive subtypes had appeared only in unsound systems; the blame calculus is the first setting where they serve a *soundness* proof [3].

### 4.3 The Blame Theorem

> **Theorem (Blame Safety — Wadler & Findler [3]).** In a well-typed blame-calculus term, a cast `⟨T ⇒ U⟩^ℓ` cannot raise positive blame `blame ℓ` if `T <:⁺ U`; cannot raise negative blame `blame ℓ̄` if `T <:⁻ U`; and can never raise blame if `T <:ₙ U`.

> **Corollary ("Well-typed programs can't be blamed").** For a cast from a less precise to a more precise type, blame is always allocated to the *less precisely typed* side. A fully statically typed region can never be blamed for a runtime type error — the fault lies across the boundary, in the less typed code.

The proof is the paper's breakthrough. Earlier blame-safety arguments [5] needed sophisticated operational-equivalence proofs; Wadler and Findler use the *standard syntactic method* — progress and preservation, with the type system tracking which blame *polarity* each subterm may produce. The *Tangram Lemma* generalizes the argument: any cast calculus meeting small conditions on precision and subtyping inherits a blame theorem, instantiable for contracts and hybrid types without redoing the work [3]. In practice, this is why a Typed Racket contract failure at the typed/untyped boundary names the untyped module — the theorem guarantees the finger points the right way [3][5].

### 4.4 The gradual guarantee and the refined criteria

Siek, Vitousek, Cimini, and Boyland [4] observed the original papers characterized gradual typing incompletely, letting the term dilute into "anything combining static and dynamic typing." Their *refined criteria* draw a crisp line around two properties relating programs differing only in precision (`e ⊑ e′`, `e′` more precise):

> **Criterion (Static gradual guarantee [4]).** If `e ⊑ e′` and `e′` is well typed, then `e` is well typed. Removing annotations never breaks typeability; adding them only reveals errors.

> **Criterion (Dynamic gradual guarantee [4]).** If `e ⊑ e′`, behaviors correspond: if `e` yields a value, so does `e′` (correspondingly precise); if `e` raises blame, so does `e′`; if `e` diverges, `e′` diverges *or* raises blame. More precise types can only *introduce* new cast failures — never turn one successful behavior into another.

The dynamic guarantee formalizes a programmer's core expectation: annotating working code must not change what it computes. Designs violating it — including *like-type* systems — are excluded, however gradually typed they feel [4]. The remaining criteria complete the picture: *conservative extension* of both the static discipline (fully annotated programs behave exactly as in the static language) and the dynamic one (fully `?` programs behave as in the untyped language), blame-aware *type safety*, and the *blame theorem* itself [4]. Garcia, Clark, and Tanter's *abstracting gradual typing* (AGT) framework [12] shows the criteria can even be obtained *by construction*, deriving the gradual language from the static one via abstract interpretation over the precision order.

### 4.5 Space efficiency: coercions, threesomes, and blame together

Herman, Tomb, and Flanagan [7] showed naive wrappers leak space: in the classic even/odd example, each tail call accumulates another cast, so a seemingly tail-recursive program uses space linear in recursion depth. Their fix adapts Henglein's *coercion calculus* [6]: casts become first-class *coercions* with a *composition* operation normalizing any sequence into a single equivalent coercion. Pending casts stay bounded, so tail calls stay tail calls — with eager error detection as a bonus.

Siek and Wadler's *threesomes* [8] express the same idea inside the blame calculus. Any cast `A ⇒ B` — a *twosome* — factors through the greatest lower bound `C = A ⊓ B` under naive subtyping:

```
A ⇒ B   =   (A ⇒ C) ; (C ⇒ B)      -- downcast, then upcast: a threesome
```

Any *chain* of threesomes collapses to a *single* threesome via the greatest lower bound of the intermediates, and blame labels map a collapsed failure back to the offending original twosome — space efficiency *with* blame safety [8].

The synthesis is *blame and coercion together* [9]: coercions annotated with blame labels, with a *Fundamental Property of Casts* establishing full abstraction across a three-level tower — contracts, blame, coercions — so the hard direction of earlier proofs becomes a corollary rather than a bespoke bisimulation [9].

| Representation | Space | Blame | Error detection |
|----------------|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Naive wrappers [1] | Unbounded; breaks tail calls | None | Lazy |
| Coercions [6][7] | Bounded via normalization | None | Eager |
| Threesomes [8] | Bounded; chains collapse via GLB | Per-twosome | Eager |
| Blame coercions [9] | Bounded; full-abstraction tower | Full theorem | Eager |

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### Proof sketches

Two proof techniques shaped the field. First, Wadler and Findler's *syntactic* proof of blame safety [3]: typing derivations track which blame *polarity* each subterm may produce, and preservation shows evaluation never produces a forbidden polarity. Earlier work assumed blame safety needed semantic arguments; the syntactic proof made the result portable via the Tangram Lemma.

Second, the *mechanized* proof of the gradual guarantee [4]: the Gradually Typed Lambda Calculus was formalized in a proof assistant and both guarantees proved mechanically. Mechanization matters because the dynamic guarantee quantifies over *all* precision-related program pairs — exactly where informal "proof by inspection" drops cases.

### The empirical record

Reticulated Python [11] was the first systematic *evaluation* of gradual typing. Vitousek, Kent, Baker, and Siek built a framework with multiple cast semantics — *guarded* (proxies), *transient* (shallow first-order checks), *monotonic* (references whose types only gain precision) — and benchmarked them. The lesson: cast semantics dominates performance, and no single semantics wins everywhere.

The sobering counterpart is Takikawa et al.'s "Is sound gradual typing dead?" [10], measuring Typed Racket on mixed typed/untyped configurations. Many configurations suffered order-of-magnitude slowdowns, some exceeding two orders of magnitude, all from boundary contract checking. The paper diagnosed rather than killed sound gradual typing — and set the agenda the space-efficiency work of Section 4.5 answers: blame safety is non-negotiable, but its *price* is a design variable, and coercion-based calculi [7][8][9] are the mechanism for controlling it. AGT [12] closes the theoretical loop, deriving guarantee-satisfying languages systematically from the precision order itself.

## 6 Limitations

The theory has boundaries worth stating. First, the gradual guarantee and efficiency are in *tension*: the dynamic guarantee constrains behavior across precision changes, ruling out aggressive cast-placement optimizations, and the exact Pareto frontier between guarantee strength and performance is unmapped [4][10].

Second, blame is *coarse*. The theorem identifies the less precisely typed *side* of a cast, but a side can be a large untyped module; *which expression* within it is responsible remains an unsolved error-reporting problem. Programmers experience blame labels, not blame theorems.

Third, the classical results assume simple types. Polymorphism needs dynamic sealing to preserve parametricity; mutable state needs monotonic references [11]. Each extension re-opens the metatheory, and the Tangram Lemma's free instantiation has limits when the precision order itself grows subtle.

Fourth, coercions fix the *asymptotic* blowup but not the *constant-factor* cost: every boundary crossing still allocates and checks — precisely what the Takikawa measurements indict [7][10]. Near-zero marginal cost for blame safety remains the field's central open engineering question.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Gradual typing began as a modest proposal — add `?`, check up to consistency, insert casts — and grew, under three hard questions, into a model case of *principled language design*. The blame theorem [3] answers "who is at fault" with proof, not convention. The gradual guarantee [4] answers "what changes when I annotate" with behavioral correspondence, not hope. Space-efficient coercions [6][7][8][9] answer "what does it cost" with a representation, not an apology. And the empirical record [10][11] keeps the theory honest about what each guarantee costs.

The deeper lesson is methodological: each pillar came from the same move — *reify the implicit as explicit syntax, then prove the property of the explicit calculus*. Consistency became casts; fault became blame labels; annotation change became the precision order; wrappers became coercions. It is a pattern worth stealing for any feature whose informal story outruns its formal one.
