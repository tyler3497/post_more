# Cubical Type Theory and Higher Inductive Types: Univalence via Glue Types, Kan Composition Operations, and Computational Interpretation of Homotopy Levels with Cubical Agda Case Studies

## Abstract

Cubical type theory resolves one of the deepest open problems in the foundations of mathematics and programming: giving *computational content* to Vladimir Voevodsky's univalence axiom. Where the simplicial-set model of homotopy type theory validates univalence only through classical reasoning, the cubical-sets model of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg reconstructs equality as *paths indexed by an abstract interval*, equips every type with Kan composition operations, and derives univalence as a theorem from the Glue type former [1]. This thesis presents a self-contained exposition of the resulting theory: the interval pretype and De Morgan algebra structure, path types with definitional function extensionality, homogeneous composition and generalized transport, Glue types as equivalence extension, and a general schema for higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules for higher constructors [2]. We analyze the decomposition of composition used by Cubical Agda [3], the homotopy canonicity theorem guaranteeing that closed natural numbers compute to numerals [4], and the Cartesian reformulation of the theory [5].

## 1 Introduction

Dependent type theory has long promised a single language for programming and proof, yet its treatment of equality has remained impoverished. In Martin-Löf type theory, the identity type is inductively generated by reflexivity alone, and its eliminator *J* provides only the weakest reasonable account of what it means for two things to be equal. Extensionality principles — function extensionality, propositional extensionality, and above all Voevodsky's *univalence axiom*, which identifies equalities of types with equivalences of types — must be postulated as axioms, and every postulate destroys computation: a closed term that mentions an axiom need not reduce to a canonical form [6].

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) embraced univalence as an axiom and developed a rich synthetic homotopy theory from it, including a synthetic proof that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers [6]. But the computational meaning of univalence remained obscure. Voevodsky, Kapulkin, and Lumsdaine validated univalence in the model of Kan simplicial sets, yet their argument uses classical logic in an essential way and therefore cannot explain how to *compute* with univalence [1].

Cubical type theory, introduced by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg (CCHM), changes the situation completely [1]. Instead of postulating univalence, it enriches the syntax with an abstract *interval* over which one can directly manipulate higher-dimensional cubes — points, lines, squares, cubes — and a *composition* operation that fills open boxes. From these ingredients, function extensionality becomes definitional, and univalence becomes a *theorem* proved via the Glue type former, which turns equivalences into paths between types. The theory is constructive end to end: Simon Huber proved that every closed term of natural-number type in cubical type theory reduces to a numeral, a result known as *homotopy canonicity* [4].

This thesis makes the following contributions:

1. A precise, self-contained account of the interval, path types, and the Kan composition operations, including the decomposition into homogeneous composition and generalized transport used by modern implementations.
2. A detailed reconstruction of the Glue type former and the proof of univalence from it, with attention to the definitional equalities that give univalence its computational behavior.
3. An exposition of the higher inductive type schema of Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, covering spheres, the torus, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts, with judgmental computation rules for higher constructors [2].
4. An analysis of the *computational interpretation of homotopy levels* — contractible types, propositions, sets, and groupoids — via the truncation hierarchy.
5. Extended case studies formalized in Cubical Agda [3]: the encode-decode proof that the loop space of the circle is the integers, the proof that the torus equals the product of two circles, and the extraction of executable content from univalence-based transport.

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 surveys the background: identity types, univalence, and the classical simplicial model. Section 3 describes our methodology: the cubical methodology of interval-indexed paths, partial elements, and composition. Section 4 is a deep dive into path types, Glue types, Kan operations, higher inductive types, and homotopy levels. Section 5 presents the empirical and formal results of our Cubical Agda case studies. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Identity types in Martin-Löf type theory

In Martin-Löf type theory, propositional equality is captured by the identity type family `Id A x y`, inductively generated by a single constructor `refl : Id A x x` and eliminated by the *J* rule, which states that to prove a motive depending on an equality proof it suffices to consider the reflexive case. This design is minimal and elegant, but it is also *proof-irrelevant in spirit*: the theory cannot distinguish different proofs of the same equality, and it validates uniqueness of identity proofs only if an additional axiom (Streicher's K) is assumed.

More damaging for practice, the identity type validates none of the extensionality principles programmers expect. Function extensionality — pointwise equal functions are equal — is independent of the theory, as is propositional extensionality. Each must be added as an axiom, and axioms block computation. A function defined by transporting along a postulated function-extensionality proof is *stuck*: it cannot reduce, because the axiom has no computational behavior [6].

### 2.2 The univalence axiom and its discontents

Voevodsky's univalence axiom states that the canonical map from equalities of types to equivalences of types is itself an equivalence. Concretely, for a universe `U` and types `A B : U`, there is a map `idtoeqv : (A = B) → (A ≃ B)`, and univalence asserts that this map is an equivalence, so that *equivalent types are equal* and, crucially, equal in a way that can be transported along [6].

Univalence unlocks a powerful abstraction principle: implementation details of a type do not matter, because any equivalence induces an equality along which programs and proofs transport. The HoTT book develops substantial mathematics from this axiom [6]. Yet as an axiom, univalence is computationally inert. The standard model in Kan simplicial sets validates it only via classical principles — in particular, the argument that the universe of Kan complexes is itself Kan uses the axiom of choice — so the model explains nothing about how a term using univalence should evaluate [1].

### 2.3 Cubical sets and constructive models

Cubical type theory replaces simplicial sets with *cubical sets*: presheaves on a category of cubes. A type is interpreted as a cubical set equipped with *Kan composition operations* that fill open boxes, and the interval object — representable on the one-dimensional cube — provides the abstract dimension variables over which paths are defined. The decisive technical advance of CCHM is that the Kan structure can be described *constructively*: no choice principles are needed, and the entire model lives in a constructive metatheory [1].

Two variants of the cube category dominate the literature. The original CCHM theory uses the *De Morgan* cube category, whose interval carries the structure of a De Morgan algebra: endpoints `0` and `1`, reversal, and binary meet and join operations satisfying the De Morgan laws [1]. A later reformulation by Angiuli, Brunerie, Coquand, Favonia, Harper, and Licata uses the *Cartesian* cube category — faces, degeneracies, symmetries, and diagonals, but no connections or reversal — and gives a syntactic account of a uniform Kan operation with judgmental equalities for each type former [5]. Cubical Agda implements a variation of the De Morgan theory in which composition is decomposed into homogeneous composition and generalized transport [2][3].

### 2.4 Homotopy levels

Homotopy type theory stratifies types by *homotopy level* (h-level): a type is contractible (level 0) if it has a center of contraction; a proposition (level 1) if any two elements are equal; a set (level 2) if its path types are propositions; and in general an *n*-type if its iterated path types are propositions at the appropriate depth. Truncation operations `∥ A ∥ₙ` force a type down to level *n*, with propositional truncation `∥ A ∥` being the most used: it *squashes* a type so that any two inhabitants become equal, which is exactly what is needed to define well-behaved quotients and existential statements [6]. In cubical type theory, truncations are higher inductive types, and their computation rules are judgmental [2].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is the *cubical methodology*: we work inside a type theory extended with an abstract interval and composition operations, and we derive extensionality principles as theorems rather than postulating them. This section describes the core instruments.

### 3.1 The interval pretype and path types

Cubical type theory introduces an *interval pretype* `I` with endpoints `i0` and `i1`. A *path* in a type `A` from `a` to `b` is a function out of the interval: `Path A a b` is the type of terms `p : I → A` with `p i0 = a` and `p i1 = b` definitionally. Path abstraction `λ i → t` and path application `p i` are the introduction and elimination forms. Unlike the inductive identity type, paths *compute*: applying a path abstraction to an endpoint reduces by beta reduction.

The immediate payoff is function extensionality. Given pointwise paths between `f` and `g`, the path between the functions is simply `λ i → λ x → p x i`:

```agda
funExt : {f g : (x : A) → B x}
       → ((x : A) → f x ≡ g x) → f ≡ g
funExt p i x = p x i
```

No axiom is invoked; the proof is a program that runs [3].

> **Definition:** A *path* `p : Path A a b` is a map out of the interval pretype `I` with `p i0 ≡ a` and `p i1 ≡ b` judgmentally. A *line* is a path in a constant family; a *dependent path* `PathP A a b` lies over a type family `A : I → Type`.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Path types and the algebra of equality

In cubical type theory, the familiar groupoid laws for equality are *programs* built from composition. Reflexivity is the constant path; symmetry reverses the interval variable; transitivity concatenates two paths by filling a square whose left and bottom faces are the given paths. Because composition computes, these operations satisfy their expected laws up to higher paths, and the higher paths themselves are constructed by filling cubes. The result is that every type carries the structure of a *weak ∞-groupoid*, but one whose operations are given by explicit Kan fillers rather than postulated coherences [1].

| System | Equality notion | Univalence | Function extensionality | Computation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Martin-Löf type theory | Inductive `Id`, eliminator `J` | Axiom (blocks reduction) | Axiom | Stuck on axioms |
| Book HoTT | Inductive `Id` + univalence axiom | Axiom | Provable from univalence | Stuck on univalence |
| CCHM cubical type theory | Path types over interval `I` | Theorem via Glue [1] | Definitional | All primitives compute |
| Cubical Agda | Path types + `Id` with comp. `J` | Theorem, `ua` computes [3] | Definitional `funExt` | Closed `ℕ` terms reduce to numerals [4] |

### 4.2 Glue types and the proof of univalence

```agda
ua e i = Glue B [ i = i0 ↦ (A , e) ]
```

> **Lemma:** Transport along `ua e` is *propositionally* equal to the forward function of `e`, and the computation is driven by the composition structure of Glue types, which is defined by case analysis on the cofibration.

### 4.4 Higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules

| HIT | Point constructors | Path constructors | Boundary conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circle `S¹` | `base` | `loop : base ≡ base` | Endpoints at `base` |
| 2-sphere `S²` | `base` | `surf : refl ≡ refl` (2-dim) | Boundary the constant loop |
| Torus `T²` | `point` | `line1`, `line2 : point ≡ point`; `square` filling their commutator | Square boundary is `line1 ∙ line2 ∙ line1⁻¹ ∙ line2⁻¹` |
| Suspension `Susp A` | `north`, `south` | `merid a : north ≡ south` for `a : A` | Endpoints at poles |
| Prop. truncation `∥A∥` | `∣a∣` for `a : A` | `squash : (x y : ∥A∥) → x ≡ y` | None (all points identified) |
| Pushout `A ⊔_C B` | `inl`, `inr` | `push c : inl (f c) ≡ inr (g c)` | Endpoints at the inclusions |

> **Theorem:** Every HIT in the schema of Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg has judgmental computation rules for all constructors, including higher-dimensional ones, and the universe is closed under these type formers [2].

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Case study I: the fundamental group of the circle

The signature achievement of synthetic homotopy theory is the computation `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ`: the loop space of the circle is equivalent to the integers [6]. In Cubical Agda this is proved by the *encode-decode* method. One defines a type family `Cover : S¹ → Type` with `Cover base = ℤ` and `Cover (loop i) = ua succEquiv i`, where `succEquiv : ℤ ≃ ℤ` is the successor equivalence.

### 5.2 Case study II: the torus is the product of two circles

A more demanding test is the equivalence `Torus ≃ S¹ × S¹`, where the torus is the HIT with point `point`, paths `line1` and `line2` from `point` to itself, and a two-dimensional `square` constructor witnessing their commutativity.

We can also phrase the verification as a temporal property of the proof development.

```tla+
---- MODULE TorusEquiv ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES dir
Init == dir = "t2c"
T2C == dir = "t2c" /\ dir' = "c2t"
C2T == dir = "c2t" /\ dir' = "t2c"
Next == T2C \/ C2T
RoundTrip == <>(dir = "t2c") /\ <>(dir = "c2t")
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_dir /\ WF_dir(Next)
====
```

### 5.3 Case study III: univalence-based program transport

The third case study demonstrates *executable* univalence. We define addition on unary natural numbers, establish the equivalence between unary and binary naturals, and transport the addition function along `ua` of this equivalence. In Cubical Agda the transported program reduces — the `transp` operations on the Glue type compute through the equivalence — whereas in book HoTT it would remain stuck on the univalence axiom [3][6].

### 5.4 Homotopy canonicity

Underwriting all of the above is Huber's *homotopy canonicity* theorem: any closed term of natural-number type in cubical type theory is judgmentally equal to a numeral [4]. The proof is a sconing construction — a proof-relevant logical-relations argument internalized through the cubical structure — and it is the precise sense in which univalence and HITs here are genuinely *constructive*.

## 6 Limitations

**Schema generality.** The HIT schema of Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg covers spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts, but not *all* higher inductive types definable in book HoTT — notably, HITs with recursive higher constructors of arbitrary complexity, such as localizations, lie outside the schema and require further theoretical work [2][6].

**Cube category trade-offs.** The De Morgan interval used by CCHM and Cubical Agda provides connections and reversal, which simplify many constructions, but the reverse and meet/join operations have no counterpart in the Cartesian formulation; conversely, the Cartesian theory's diagonals enable a cleaner syntactic account of the uniform Kan operation [5]. Neither variant dominates the other for all purposes, and results must sometimes be translated between them.

**Performance.** Composition computes, but not always efficiently. Transport along complex Glue types can produce large normal forms, and the general `transp`/`hcomp` machinery, while uniform, is not optimized in current implementations. Practical developments in Cubical Agda sometimes avoid heavy univalence-based transport for performance reasons, preferring manually constructed equivalences [3].

**Constructivity boundaries.** Homotopy canonicity holds for the core theory, but extensions — notably certain higher inductive types and impredicative principles — have not all been shown constructive. The metatheory remains an active research area [4].

**Universe levels.** As in all predicative type theories, universe polymorphism must be managed explicitly, and Glue types, HITs, and truncations all interact with level bookkeeping in ways that occasionally require manual annotation in Cubical Agda [3].

## 7 Conclusion

Cubical type theory transforms univalence from an axiom into a *mechanism*. By indexing equality over an abstract interval, equipping types with Kan composition operations, and introducing Glue types as equivalence extension, the theory of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg makes function extensionality definitional and univalence a theorem with genuine computational behavior [1]. The higher inductive type schema extends this computational reading to synthetic homotopy theory: spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts all compute judgmentally on their higher constructors [2]. Cubical Agda packages these ideas into a usable proof assistant in which the fundamental group of the circle, the torus decomposition, and univalence-based program transport are not just provable but *executable* [3], all underwritten by homotopy canonicity [4].

The Cartesian reformulation shows the theory is robust across cube categories [5], and the remaining frontiers — broader HIT schemas, performance of composition, and the full constructive metatheory — are active areas of research. What is settled is the central philosophical point: univalence *can* compute, and the cubical methodology shows exactly how. The equality of types is no longer a mystery to be postulated but a structure to be programmed.
