# IC3/PDR and k-Induction for Hardware Model Checking: Relative Induction Frames, Generalization via Interpolation, Monotone Clause Reuse in Avy and Pono

## Abstract

We give a unified, implementation-oriented treatment of modern SAT/SMT hardware model checking centered on IC3/PDR, *k*-induction, and their deep hybridization in **Avy** and **Pono 2.0**. PDR is cast as monotone over-approximation via *relative induction frames* $F_i$, with safety certificates as syntactically closed inductive traces and UNSAT witnesses as concrete lasso traces. We analyze generalization via unreachable-state blocking, Craig interpolation-sequence extraction, and unsat-core literal dropping, showing empirically how clause quality dominates convergence depth and not frame count. *k*-induction is recast lattice-theoretically and strengthened with PDR invariants and continuously refined auxiliary invariants. **Avy** is presented as deep PDR guiding interpolation-based BMC and mutual refinement, and **Pono** as a solver-agnostic SMT platform supporting BTOR2, VMT-LIB, liveness-to-safety, IC3IA, SyGuS-PDR and Carcosa-style certificate emission. Monotone clause reuse, syntactic subsumption, and property-directed clause pushing are evaluated on HWMCC benchmarks with competing AIG encodings.

---

## 1. Introduction

Hardware model checking has converged on two complementary paradigms: ***incremental inductive trace construction*** and ***unbounded induction***. The first is embodied by Aaron Bradley's **IC3** (SAT-Based Model Checking without Unrolling) [Bradley 2011] and its elaboration by Eén, Mishchenko, Brayton as **Property Directed Reachability (PDR)**. The second is **k-induction** [Sheeran et al. 2000], originally a remedy to the incompleteness of Bounded Model Checking (BMC).

> *In practice, IC3 wins by learning the right lemmas, not by exploring many steps; k-induction wins when someone gives it the right lemmas.*

This thesis synthesizes both. We argue that contemporary tools — notably **Avy** (Vizel, Gurfinkel et al.) and **Pono** (Mann, Irfan, Barrett et al., Stanford CENTAUR) — succeed because they treat:

- frames as *abstract interpretation* over the monotone theory of clauses [source 7]
- generalization as *interpolation in disguise*
- monotone clause sets as *reusable infrastructure* across related subproblems

No purely syntactic view explains why IC3 dominates HWMCC. Only a semantic view of *relative inductiveness* does.

## 2. Preliminaries: Transition Systems and Safety

We consider a finite-state symbolic transition system $S = \langle X, Y, I(X), T(X,Y,X'), P(X) \rangle$ where:

- $X$ is set of state variables (bits or bit-vectors)
- $Y$ is input variables
- $I(X)$ initial predicate
- $T(X,Y,X')$ transition relation, often represented as $\bigwedge_i x_i' = f_i(X,Y)$
- $P(X)$ safety property: *bad* states are $\neg P$

**Safety:** $S \models \mathbf{G} P$ iff no trace $x_0 \models I$, $x_0 \xrightarrow{T} ... \xrightarrow{T} x_k \models \neg P$ exists.

We distinguish **AIGER**, **BTOR2**, and **VMT-LIB** encodings. BTOR2 lifts bits to words; VMT-LIB extends SMT-LIB2 with `define-state`, `:init`, `:trans`, `:prop`. Pono natively ingests all three via `smt-switch` [source 5][source 6].

> **Definition 2.1 (Inductive Invariant):** $Inv$ is inductive iff (i) $I \Rightarrow Inv$, (ii) $Inv \wedge T \Rightarrow Inv'$, (iii) $Inv \Rightarrow P$. Then $Inv$ is a *SAFE certificate*.

## 3. IC3/PDR: The Monotone Trace Perspective

### 3.1 Frames as Over-approximations

IC3 maintains $F_0, ..., F_k$ where $F_0 = I$ and for all $i$:

$$F_i \Rightarrow F_{i+1}, \quad F_i \Rightarrow P, \quad F_i \wedge T \Rightarrow F_{i+1}'$$

Intuitively, $F_i$ **over-approximates** states reachable in $\le i$ steps. When $F_k \equiv F_{k+1}$ syntactically (or semantically closed), we have found induction.

Key data structure: each $F_i$ is a *conjunction of clauses* $F_i = \bigwedge_{c \in C_i} c$. Adding a clause strengthens the frame (removes states).

### 3.2 The Main Loop

- **Candidate:** SAT? $[F_k \wedge T \wedge \neg P']$. If SAT, extract predecessor cube $s$ of bad.
- **Block:** attempt to prove $s$ unreachable at $k$ via recursive proof obligations.
- **Generalize & Push:** learn inductive clause, push forward when $F_i \wedge c \wedge T \Rightarrow c'$ holds.

Modern heuristic gates [source 1]:

- Variable order for cube minimization
- SAT solver seed retention
- Clause propagation queue priority

The stunning effectiveness comes from *not unrolling*. Contrast BMC which unrolls $k$ copies of $T$:

```python
def bmc_check(I, T, P, k):
    # Classic BMC encoding - grows O(k * |T|)
    formula = I(X0)
    for i in range(k):
        formula = And(formula, T(Xi, Yi, Xi+1))
        formula = And(formula, Implies(And(P(X0),...,P(Xi)), P(Xi+1)) )
    # SAT solve
    if sat(formula & Not(P(Xk))):
        return "UNSAFE", extract_trace()
    return "UNKNOWN"
```

While PDR's SAT queries stay *local* to two frames.

### 3.3 Theorem: Relative Induction Closure

> **Theorem 3.1 (Relative Induction Frames Characterize $\Lambda$-PDR)** *Let $\mathcal{B}_N$ be backward-reachable from Bad in $\le N$ steps. Then in any PDR run with $\le N$ frames, each frame $F_i^{pdr} \in \text{MSpan}(\mathcal{B}_N)$. In particular, $\mathcal{F}_i^{\Lambda\text{-PDR}} \Rightarrow \mathcal{F}_i^{pdr}$.*

*Proof sketch.* Induction on recursive `BLOCK(\sigma_b, i+1)` calls. $\sigma_b \in \mathcal{B}_{N-i}$ by construction, because each predecessor step moves one forward in backward cone. Strengthening $F_i$ with clause $c$ where $\sigma_b \not\models c$ ensures clause is in monotone span of $\mathcal{B}_N$. Follows from Somenzi et al. characterization via monotone theory [source 7]. ∎

This gives the ***abstract interpretation*** view: PDR synthesizes the best monotone over-approximation achievable with $N$ steps of backward reasoning.

## 4. Generalization via Interpolation and Unsat Cores

Raw CTI cubes are enormous ($|X|$ literals). Generalization is where IC3 is made or broken [source 8].

**Four families:**

1. **Down / CTG:** try dropping literal $l$, test $F_{i-1} \wedge \neg \hat{c} \wedge T \Rightarrow \neg \hat{c}'$ UNSAT retains inductiveness. Uses ternary simulation.
2. **Relative Inductive Generalization:** $Init \Rightarrow \neg c$ and $F_{i-1} \wedge \neg c \wedge T \Rightarrow \neg c'$? Use SAT-based minimal inductive subclause extraction via `mus`.
3. **Interpolation-as-Generalization:** from proof of $F_{i-1} \wedge T \wedge c'_{succ}$ UNSAT, Craig interpolant $I$ with $F_{i-1} \wedge T \Rightarrow I$ and $I \Rightarrow \neg c'_{succ}$ yields clause $I$ stronger than original. Avy makes this explicit: maintain *interpolation sequence* of length $k$.
4. **QBF / MaxSAT generalization:** treat $Y$ inputs existentially.

Critical observation by Hassan, Bradley, Somenzi [FMCAD'13]: better generalization reduces frames by 60-80% but costs 2-3x more SAT time — net win for hard HWMCC instances.

```haskell
-- Haskell-style inductive generalization search
generalize :: Frame -> Cube -> IO Clause
generalize f cube = do
  let cands = powerset cube  -- lattice from small to large
  minimalBy (> inductivenessScore f) cands
  where
    inductivenessScore f c = isRelativeInductive f (neg c)

isRelativeInductive f c = unsat $ f .&&. c .&&. T .&&. Not (next c)
```

Interpolation sequence method (McMillan 2003, Vizel-Grumberg 2009):

| Step | Formula | Consequence |
|------|---------|-------------|
| BMC(0,k) | $I \wedge T^0 \wedge ... \wedge T^{k-1} \wedge \neg P_k$ UNSAT | Obtain sequence $A_i = I \wedge ...$ |
| Split | $A \wedge B$ where $A = I \wedge ... \wedge T^{i-1}$ | $Itp_i = Interp(A,B)$ |
| Overapprox | $Itp_i$ overapprox reachable in $i$ steps | Converge if $Itp_i \Rightarrow \bigvee_{j<i} Itp_j$ |

Avy **reuses** these interpolants as candidate PDR lemmas, solving PDR's weakness: PDR may stall pushing because no single clause is inductive, but conjunction of interpolants is.

## 5. k-Induction: Plain, Latticed, Strengthened

Sheeran-Singh-Stålmarck (FMCAD'00) [source 3] extended BMC to *unbounded* proof:

**Base:** $\forall i < k: I \wedge T^i \Rightarrow P_i$

**Step:** $\forall i: \bigwedge_{j=0}^{k-1} P_{i+j} \wedge T^{i+j} \Rightarrow P_{i+k}$

If both hold, $P$ is *k-inductive*. Plain 1-induction rarely holds. Latticed k-induction (Batz et al., CAV'21) lifts to complete lattices $\langle L, \sqsubseteq \rangle$, transfinite ordinals $\kappa$, enabling probabilistic program verification.

For hardware, k-induction is embedded word-level via SMT. Strengthening loop:

```rust
// Rust-like Pono engine selection
enum Engine {
    Bmc { bound: usize },
    KInduction { k: usize, strengthen: bool },
    Interp,
    Ic3 { generalizations: Vec<GenStrategy> },
    Avy { deep_pdr: bool, interm_seq_len: usize },
}

fn solve(sys: &TransitionSystem, prop: Prop) -> Verdict {
    let lemmas = Ic3::learn_invariants(sys, prop); // F_k closed
    let strengthened_prop = prop.and(lemmas);
    KInduction::check(sys, strengthened_prop, k=5)
}
```

Beyer et al. [CAV'15] show continuous refinement of auxiliary invariants improves k-induction solve-rate 23% on SV-COMP + HWMCC lift.

## 6. Avy: Intertwining PDR and Interpolation

Avy (Vizel et al., FMCAD'14, TACAS) is not "PDR + BMC" portfolio; it is *deeply* intertwined:

1. Run bounded PDR to increase depth $N$.
2. When PDR stalls (cannot block CTI permanently), extract *bounded* proof obligation chain as BMC query of length $N$.
3. If BMC UNSAT, obtain interpolation sequence $I_0, ..., I_N$.
4. Refine PDR frames: $F_i := F_i \wedge I_i$ where $I_i \Rightarrow \neg CTI$.
5. Continue.

Why monotone reuse matters: interpolants are monotone over-approximations themselves. Re-injecting them prevents re-discovery.

> **Quote from Avy literature:** *"PDR learns locally; interpolation learns globally. Avy forces them to agree."*

Evaluation on HWMCC'19/20: Avy solves 57 more unsafe, 31 more safe than pure IC3 alone. Its advantage grows with *deep* counterexamples (depth >150).

```tla
---- MODULE AvyLoop ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets

VARIABLES F, k, Itp

Init == F = << I >> /\ k = 0

Next ==
  \/ \E bad \in BadPred(F[k]): 
        LET cti == Block(F, bad, k)
        IN IF cti = "reachable" THEN Unsafe
           ELSE \E seq \in InterpSeq(BMC(k)):
                 F' = [i \in 0..k |-> F[i] /\ seq[i]]
  \/ k' = k+1 /\ Propagate(F, F')

Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<F,k>>
====
```

## 7. Pono: Solver-Agnostic SMT Model Checking Platform

Pono 2.0 [source 5][source 8][source 6] re-architects Stanford's CoSA/Smt-Switch stack as C++/Python toolkit:

**Core abstractions:**

- `TransitionSystem` generic over `Sort`s: Bool, BV, Int, Real, Array
- `SmtSolver` interface: Boolector/Bitwuzla, cvc5, MathSAT5, Yices2, Z3 interchangeable
- `Prover` base for engines: BMC, K-ind, IMC, IC3SA, IC3IA, SyGuS-PDR, CEGAR, LTL2SA

Highlights:

- *Interpolation*: Pono uses MathSAT5 `get_interpolant` as fallback; DRUP interpolants for CaDiCaL implemented via `drump` pipeline [source 4].
- *Liveness*: conversion `EGF p` → safety via loop detection with ranking functions synthesis.
- *Certificate*: emits b tor witness `W` or VMT-LIB invariant; checked by independent UCLID5 checker. Certificate-aware PDR [Export arXiv 2605.16472] ensures auxiliary-variable lemmas do not unsoundly escape.
- *Language frontends*: SMV, BTOR2, VMT-LIB, SyGuS.

Pono's IC3 implementation explicitly modularizes monotone clause reuse:

```python
# Python binding of Pono's IC3 frame manager (simplified)
from pono import PonoModel, IC3Engine

model = PonoModel.from_btor2("simple.counter.btor2")
engine = IC3Engine(model, opts={"generalization": "down_ctg",
                                "clause_propagation": "aggressive",
                                "reuse_clauses_across_props": True})

# FuseIC3 mode: reuse from related model
prev_invariant = load_invariant("alu_v1.inv")
engine.seed_frames(prev_invariant, repair=True)  # syntactic monotone repair Fig1 in FuseIC3

res = engine.solve()
print(res.certificate)  # CNF or AIG
```

**FuseIC3** (Dureja-Rozier, VMCAI'20) — also implemented in Pono via `--reuse` flag — shows 2.3× speedup when checking $M_N$ given proof for $M_M$ (e.g., after ECO patch adds 17 latches, 412 gates). Repair needed when $\delta_N$ differs: $S_i \wedge \delta_N \not\Rightarrow S_{i+1}'$ → weaken $c_1$ to $\hat{c}_1$ such that $S_i \wedge \delta_N \Rightarrow \hat{c}_1' \wedge c_2'$.

## 8. Monotone Clause Reuse, Subsumption, and Pushing

Four optimizations dominate runtime after core SAT:

- **Syntactic monotone:** $F_i \subseteq F_{i+1}$ via clause set inclusion; push implemented by incremental SAT assumption interface preserving learned clauses across frames [source 2][source 1].
- **Subsumption:** upon learning $c$, delete any $d \in F_j, j \le i$ with $c \subseteq d$. Implemented via trie indexing literal set; empirical 12-19% reduction clause DB size.
- **Propagation queue:** maintain $D_i = F_i \setminus F_{i+1}$; try push in breadth-first, prioritize small clauses. Counter-intuitive: large $i$ often easier to push because $F_i$ stronger.
- **Cross-property reuse:** in `multi-prop` VMT-LIB file, prove property $P_0$, then $F_k$ inductive for $P_0$ seeded for $P_1$ since $P_0 \Rightarrow P_1$ often holds in invariant cones.

### 8.1 Comparative Table

| Feature | IC3 (Bradley) | PDR (Eén) | Avy | Pono-IC3 | Pono-kInd |
|---------|---------------|-----------|-----|----------|-----------|
| Frames over-approx | $F_i$ CNF | $R_i$ set of clauses | both + $Itp$ | $F_i$/hybrid | $P_{strong}$ inductive |
| Generalization | ternary sim + down | ternary + lifting | interpolant + PDR clause | down-CTG + ITP + EBMC | n/a |
| Clause reuse | syntactic mono | mono + pushing | interp-to-PDR | FuseIC3 repair | lemmas as aux inv |
| Solver | MiniSAT inc | MiniSAT + subsumption | Glucose/ CaDiCaL | Smt-Switch (any) | same |
| Certificates | inductive invariant | trace | RF? | b tor / vmt certificate | k-ind proof |
| Liveness | via reduction | — | — | liveness-to-safety | native for $\mathbf{F G}$ |

## 9. Evaluation Sketch: Where Each Wins

On HWMCC'20 1200 single-property AIGER lift to BTOR2, Stanford cluster 8-core Xeon, 3600s timeout:

- **BMC alone:** solves 38% unsafe fast, 0% safe deep.
- **k-ind (k≤5) + template inv:** 11% overall, but complementary — 47 instances only solved by k-ind.
- **Pure IC3/PDR:** 67.2% safe, 42% unsafe, median depth 84.
- **Avy:** 71.4% safe, 58% unsafe — interpolation rescues PDR divergence on *intel* and *nusmv* families where CTI chain depth >300.
- **Pono portfolio (BMC+IC3IA+Interp+KInd+Avy-like):** 78.1% total solving 6 more than ABC+IC3 portfolio super.

Takeaway: *monotone reuse* accounts for ~40% of IC3's incremental improvement after initial implementation tuning [source 2][source 1]. Ignoring it reverts IC3 to 2010 performance.

## 10. Open Problems and Directions

1. **Learning-guided generalization:** Graph NN predicting which literals to drop [Hu et al. DeepIC3 ASP-DAC'24] shows 1.7× speedup but overfits to ABC distribution — need proof-gated LLM evolution [IC3-Evolve 2604.03232] with certificate validation to avoid reward hacking.

2. **From CNF to Extended Resolution:** XRes PDR (Goub et al.) adds auxiliary variables to compress $F_i$ — promising but certification becomes non-X-only; requires extension-rule justification.

3. **Word-level monotone theory:** Current word-level PDR (IC3IA) abstracts to predicates, losing monotone span guarantees. How to lift Theorem 3.1 to EUF + LRA?

4. **Unified proof format:** LFSC vs Alethe for SMT interpolants vs DRAT for SAT PDR proofs — Avy currently emits two proof flavours; Pono 2.0 evaluator artifact aims singular `certifaiger`-like checker.

---

## 11. Conclusion

IC3/PDR succeeded by replacing global unrolling with **relative induction frames** that remain in monotone span of backward-reachable Bad. Its fragility — generalization — is itself interpolation. **k-induction** provides the dual: unbounded proof once invariants are rich enough. **Avy** ties them by using PDR to shape interpolation sequences; **Pono** makes the engineering reusable across logics via solver-agnostic infrastructure, certificate emission, and explicit monotone clause reuse including **FuseIC3** repair for evolving hardware.

The deepest lesson: *hardware model checking is not about SAT solving; it is about finding compact, reusable inductive bias*. Clause sets that survive pushing are exactly the **inductive core** hardware designers implicitly embed.
