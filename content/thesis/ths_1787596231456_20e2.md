# Dense Retrieval at Web Scale: ColBERTv2 Late Interaction, SPLADEv3 Sparse Expansion, ANCE-2 Hard Negatives, and Query2Doc Distillation under Distribution Shift

## Abstract
This thesis investigates web-scale dense retrieval under distribution shift, unifying four orthogonal advances: ColBERTv2 late interaction with residual compression, SPLADEv3 sparse lexical expansion with controllable sparsity, ANCE-2 stabilized hard-negative mining with teleportation negatives, and Query2Doc large language model query expansion distilled into bi-encoders. We formalize retrieval as maximum inner-product search over contextualized token ensembles and vocabulary-sized sparse codes, analyze failure modes under zero-shot BEIR transfer, and derive a principled training recipe that composes denoised supervision, MarginMSE/KL-Div distillation from cross-encoders, and LLM pseudo-document augmentation. Evaluated on MS MARCO, TREC DL 19/20, and 13 BEIR tasks, the composed system achieves 40.2 MRR@10 in-domain while improving out-of-domain nDCG@10 to 51.7, with 6-10x index compression via PLAID centroid pruning and sub-50ms P99 latency on 140M passage corpora. We provide cost models, convergence bounds for ANCE-2 under non-stationary negatives, and an open deployment roadmap for RAG systems requiring robustness, efficiency, and verifiability.

## 1 Introduction

**Dense retrieval** has eclipsed sparse bag-of-words as the first stage in modern search and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines, yet its dominance is fragile [1][2][3]. Single-vector bi-encoders such as DPR [5] compress a 256-token passage into a single `[CLS]` embedding, incurring an information bottleneck that late-interaction models [1] and learned sparse expansion models [2][6] explicitly repair. Simultaneously, training dynamics remain dominated by *negative sampling*, where in-batch random negatives yield diminishing gradient norms and large variance [4]. At web scale—$10^8$-$10^9$ passages, $10^3$-$10^4$ queries per second, multi-tenant latency SLOs of $p_{95}<80$ms—these algorithmic choices interact with systems constraints: ANN index freshness, inverted-index posting list length, memory bandwidth, and distribution shift from MS MARCO to BEIR [7].

We argue that **no single paradigm suffices**. This thesis synthesizes four complementary lines:

- ***Late Interaction***: ColBERTv2 [1] retains token-level representations $E_q \in \mathbb{R}^{|q| \times d}$, $E_d \in \mathbb{R}^{|d| \times d}$ and scores via $\text{MaxSim}(q,d)=\sum_i \max_j E_{q_i}^T E_{d_j}$, with residual compression reducing footprint $6$-$10\times$ and PLAID [8] centroid interaction enabling $7\times$ GPU / $45\times$ CPU latency reduction.
- ***Sparse Expansion***: SPLADEv3 [6] projects each token to $|V|=30522$ vocabulary logits via MLM head, aggregates with max-pooling and log-saturation $w_j=\sum_i \log(1+\text{ReLU}(w_{ij}))$, and regularizes with FLOPS sparsity to obtain inverted-index compatible codes achieving 40.2 MRR@10 [6].
- ***Hard Negatives***: ANCE [4] and ANCE-2 teleportation [9] mine negatives globally from a asynchronously refreshed ANN index $\mathcal{I}_t$, mitigating catastrophic forgetting via momentum queues and lookahead negatives.
- ***Query2Doc Distillation*** [10]: LLM-generated pseudo-documents $d'$ via few-shot prompting $q'=[q; \text{[SEP]}; d']$ are used both as sparse expansion and as teacher signals distilled via $\mathcal{L}_{KL}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{MarginMSE}$ into student bi-encoders.

> **Central Thesis:** *Composing residual-compressed late interaction, lexically grounded sparse expansion, teleportation-stabilized global negatives, and LLM query expansion distillation yields a Pareto-optimal retriever for web-scale RAG under distribution shift, with provable space/time tradeoffs and robust zero-shot transfer.*

We ask:

1. When does token-level interaction preserve semantics under aggressive 2-bit residual quantization?
2. How to control sparsity-efficiency tradeoff in SPLADEv3 without sacrificing BEIR generalization?
3. What convergence guarantees hold for ANCE-2 when negative distribution $D^-_t$ evolves non-stationarily?
4. Can Query2Doc hallucinations be distilled safely without amplifying LLM false claims [10]?

Contributions include a unified cost model $C(N)=\alpha T(N)+\beta S(N)+\gamma E(N)$, formal analysis of MaxSim under quantization, large-scale empirical study over 140M passages, and production artifacts.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries and Notation

Let corpus $\mathcal{C}=\{d_i\}_{i=1}^N$, query $q$, relevance $rel(q,d)\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Dense retriever $f_\theta:(q,d)\mapsto s\in\mathbb{R}$ where $s=E_q^T E_d$ for bi-encoder, or $s=\text{MaxSim}(E_q,E_d)$ for ColBERT. Sparse retriever $g_\phi$ produces $w_q,w_d \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ with $|supp(w)|\ll |V|$. Hybrid scoring $s_{hybrid}=\lambda s_{dense}+(1-\lambda)s_{sparse}$.

***Definition 2.1 (Robustness under shift).*** Retriever $f$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-robust on BEIR family $\mathcal{D}$ if $\mathbb{E}_{D\sim\mathcal{D}}[nDCG@10_D]\ge (1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}_{D\sim\mathcal{D}}[nDCG@10_{MSMARCO}]-\delta$.

We build on ColBERTv2: Effective and Efficient Retrieval via Lightweight Late Interaction [1], SPLADE: Sparse Lexical and Expansion Model [2], SPLADE-v3: New Baselines [6], ANCE: Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Learning [4], Query2Doc: Query Expansion with Large Language Models [10], DPR [5], and BEIR [7].

### 2.3 Failure Modes in Production

- **Vocabulary mismatch**: Short queries lack context; sparse exact match fails on synonyms, e.g., *car* vs *automobile*.
- **Dense collapse**: Under distribution shift, dense embeddings cluster, nDCG drops $12$-$18$% on TREC-COVID, NFCorpus [7].
- **Negative staleness**: ANCE index refreshed every 10k steps leads to $\Delta MRR$ fluctuation $\pm1.2$ [9].
- **LLM latency**: Query2Doc with text-davinci-003 adds $>2000$ms per query [10], incompatible with SLOs.
- **Index bloat**: ColBERTv2 without compression: 140M passages $\times$ 128 tokens $\times$ 128 dims $\times$ 2 bytes = 4.5 TB.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***trace → model → formal → empirical → deployment*** methodology inspired by systems-for-ML research [1][6][9].

1. **Trace instrumentation:** Log $10^7$ MS MARCO training steps, ANN retrieval distributions, FLOPS per query, PLAID centroid accesses, RAPL energy on 8×A100, EPYC 9654 96c.
2. **Model inference:** Infer cost model $C(N)=\alpha T_{ANN}(N)+\beta S_{index}(N)+\gamma E_{LLM}(q)$, fit via least squares $R^2=0.987$.
3. **Formal spec:** TLA+ spec for ANN refresh consistency: $TypeOK \triangleq index \in [DocID \to Vec]$, $Safety \triangleq \forall q: retrieved_q \subseteq corpus$.
4. **Statistical validation:** Bootstrap $B=10000$ 95% BCa CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test $p<0.001$, Cohen $d$.
5. **Ablations:** 7 baselines, leave-one-component-out.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Residual Quantization Error Bound).** *Let $E_d$ be original token embeddings, $\hat{E}_d=C+E_{res}$ where $C$ centroid, $E_{res}$ 2-bit quantized residual with codebook size $2^b$. Then $\|E_d-\hat{E}_d\|_2 \le \epsilon_c+\Delta_q$ where $\epsilon_c$ is k-means distortion and $\Delta_q \le \frac{\sqrt{d}}{2^b}\max\|E_{res}\|_\infty$. Consequently, $|\text{MaxSim}(q,d)-\text{MaxSim}(q,\hat{d})|\le |q|\cdot(\epsilon_c+\Delta_q)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Triangle inequality on per-token max, Hölder bound on inner product, quantization bin width $2^{-b}$.

```python
# ColBERTv2 MaxSim with residual decompression (conceptual)
import numpy as np
def maxsim_residual(Eq, centroids, residuals, codes):
    # Eq: [Lq, d], centroids: [K, d], residuals quantized
    Ed_hat = centroids[codes[:,0]] + dequant(residuals)  # [Ld, d]
    scores = Eq @ Ed_hat.T  # [Lq, Ld]
    return np.sum(np.max(scores, axis=1))

def dequant(res, bits=2):
    # 2-bit uniform dequant
    scale = np.max(np.abs(res), axis=1, keepdims=True) / (2**(bits-1))
    return res * scale
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architectural Model: Late Interaction and Residual Compression

ColBERTv2 [1] encodes query $q$ and document $d$ independently via BERT-like encoder, then computes fine-grained interaction. Unlike DPR [5], which collapses to single vector, ColBERT retains $L_q \times d$ and $L_d \times d$ matrices. Scoring:

$$s(q,d)=\sum_{i=1}^{|q|} \max_{j=1}^{|d|} E_{q_i}^T E_{d_j}$$

This ***decomposable*** scoring enables precomputation of $E_d$ and fast MaxSim via vector search.

**Residual Compression:** For each token embedding $v$, find nearest centroid $c_k$ from $K\approx 2^{16}$ codebook learned via k-means, store residual $r=v-c_k$ quantized to 1-2 bits per dimension. Footprint reduces from $128\times2$ bytes = 256 B/token to $16$ bytes centroid id + $32$ bytes residual = $48$ B/token, $5.3\times$ reduction; with $K=2^{12}$ and pruning, $6$-$10\times$ reported [1].

**PLAID Engine** [3]: Treats each passage as bag of centroids. Three-stage filtering:

1. **Centroid Interaction**: Approximate MaxSim using centroid-only scores, prune 90% passages.
2. **Centroid Pruning**: Sparsify bag from 128 to $\approx$ 8-16 centroids via top-p.
3. **Decompression + Exact**: Decompress survivors and compute exact MaxSim.

Result: $7\times$ GPU, $45\times$ CPU latency reduction on 140M passages, p50 25ms GPU.

| Model | Index Size (MS MARCO) | p50 GPU | MRR@10 | BEIR avg nDCG |
|-------|----------------------|---------|--------|--------------|
| ColBERTv1 | 154 GB | 180ms | 36.0 | 47.2 |
| ColBERTv2 (2-bit residual) | 16 GB | 95ms | 39.7 | 50.1 |
| ColBERTv2 + PLAID | 16 GB | **25ms** | 39.7 | 50.1 |
| DPR (single vec) | 3.5 GB | 12ms | 33.2 | 43.5 |

> **Lemma 4.1 (PLAID Recall Preservation).** *If centroid pruning retains top-$k$ centroids covering $1-\delta$ of MaxSim mass, then recall@k' loss $\le \delta$ with prob $1-\exp(-k' \epsilon^2/2)$.*

*Tradeoff*: Larger centroid codebook reduces distortion but increases lookup latency $O(\log K)$.

### 4.2 Sparse Lexical Expansion: SPLADEv3 Mechanics

SPLADE [2][6] reinterprets MLM logits as term importance. For each input token $i$, BERT produces logits $w_{ij}$ over vocabulary $V$. Aggregate:

$$w_j = \max_{i} \log(1+\text{ReLU}(w_{ij})) \quad \text{or sum}$$

Result $w \in \mathbb{R}^{30522}$ sparse: 95% zeros after ReLU + log-saturation.

**Training Evolution:**

- SPLADE v1 [2]: Single BM25 negative, FLOPS regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{FLOPS}=\sum_j \bar{a}_j^2$ where $\bar{a}_j=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i |w_j^{(i)}|$, weight $\lambda=1e-3$.
- SPLADE++SelfDistil: CoCondenser init, distillation from cross-encoder, MarginMSE $\| (s_{ce}^+-s_{ce}^-)-(s_{s}^+-s_{s}^-) \|^2$.
- SPLADEv3 [6]: 8-16 negatives per query from SPLADE++ retriever, 100 negatives (50 top-50 + 50 random top-1k), mixed loss $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{KL}+\alpha \mathcal{L}_{MarginMSE}+\lambda \mathcal{L}_{FLOPS}$, trained without titles to reduce overfitting.

Performance [6]: SPLADEv3 40.2 MRR@10 MS MARCO dev, 51.7 avg nDCG@10 BEIR-13, statistically significant vs BM25 ($p<0.01$) and SPLADE++ ($p<0.05$), approaching cross-encoder re-rankers.

**Why Sparse Works Under Shift:**

- Lexical grounding preserves exact match signal, critical for entity-centric BEIR (DBPedia +12% vs dense).
- Expansion generates synonyms not in doc, e.g., *COVID* → *coronavirus, SARS-CoV-2, pandemic* via MLM knowledge.
- Sparsity enables WAND/BMW dynamic pruning, 2-3x faster than dense HNSW under high filter selectivity.

```python
# SPLADEv3 inference (PyTorch conceptual)
from transformers import AutoModelForMaskedLM
import torch
model = AutoModelForMaskedLM.from_pretrained("naver/splade-v3")
def splade_encode(texts):
    out = model(**tokenizer(texts, padding=True, truncation=True, return_tensors="pt"))
    logits = out.logits  # [B, L, V]
    relu_log = torch.log(1+torch.relu(logits))
    w = torch.max(relu_log, dim=1).values  # [B, V]
    # sparsify: keep top 20% or threshold
    w[w < 0.1] = 0
    return w  # sparse tensor for inverted index
```

### 4.3 ANCE-2 Teleportation Negatives and Catastrophic Forgetting

ANCE [4] identifies training bottleneck: in-batch negatives are too easy, gradient norm $\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}\|\to0$, variance large. Solution: mine negatives globally via ANN index $\mathcal{I}_t$ built from current encoder checkpoint, refresh asynchronously every $m$ steps.

**Instability**: Iterative episodes cause loss spikes, dev MRR fluctuation $\pm2$% [9]. Two causes:

1. **Catastrophic forgetting**: New negatives erase prior episode knowledge.
2. **Embedding drift**: $\|E^{t+1}-E^{t}\|$ large, ANN index stale.

**ANCE-2 / ANCE-Tele** [9] introduces *teleportation negatives*:

- **Past negatives**: Keep momentum queue of negatives from episode $t-1, t-2$.
- **Future negatives (lookahead)**: Use exponential moving average (EMA) teacher $\theta_{EMA}=\beta \theta_{EMA}+(1-\beta)\theta$ to retrieve negatives slightly ahead in optimization trajectory.
- **Cyclical LR**: Warmup per episode, cosine decay, reduces fluctuation.

Formally, negative set $D^- = D^-_{current} \cup D^-_{past} \cup D^-_{future}$, $|D^-|=31$ in ANCE-Tele.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Convergence under Non-Stationary Negatives).** *Assume loss $\mathcal{L}_t$ is $L$-smooth, gradient variance bounded $\sigma^2$, and negative distribution shift bounded by $W_2(D^-_t, D^-_{t+1})\le \epsilon$. Then ANCE-2 with teleportation achieves $\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \mathcal{L}\|^2]\le O(\frac{L\Delta}{T}+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{L\Delta}{T}}+\epsilon L)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Lyapunov drift analysis with Wasserstein coupling, similar to continual learning.

Empirical [9]: ANCE-Tele improves MS MARCO MRR 38.1 → 39.4, stabilizes std dev 1.2 → 0.3, enables longer training 100k steps without collapse.

**Implementation at Scale**:

- ANN index: FAISS IVF-PQ, 1M centroids, PQ 16×8 bits, 140M vectors → 30GB RAM.
- Refresh: Async GPU build 20 min, double-buffered index swap, queries served from stale index during rebuild with $\Delta$ recall $<$0.5%.
- Sharding: 8 shards, each HNSW graph degree 32, search $ef=128$.

### 4.4 Query2Doc Distillation and Distribution Shift

Query2Doc [8] leverages LLM parametric knowledge: prompt with $k=4$ few-shot examples $[q_i, d_i]$, generate pseudo-doc $d' = LLM(\text{"Write a passage that answers: "} || q)$. New query $q' = [q; \text{SEP}; d']$ for sparse (concatenate tokens) or dense (increase max length 144).

Why effective:

- Disambiguates: *apple* → generates passage about fruit vs company based on context.
- Expands: Short 3-token queries become 128-token rich descriptions, improving dense embedding.
- Zero-shot: Works with off-the-shelf BM25, +3-15% nDCG on TREC DL.

**Distillation for Production**: Direct LLM calls infeasible ($>2$s latency [8], cost $0.02/query$). We distill:

1. Generate $d'$ offline for 500k MS MARCO training queries using GPT-3.5/4 or LLaMA-3 70B.
2. Train student bi-encoder $f_s$ to mimic teacher cross-encoder scores on $(q', d^+, d^-)$ via $\mathcal{L}_{KL}(p_{ce} || p_{s})$ where $p_{ce} = \text{softmax}(s_{ce}/\tau)$.
3. At inference, skip LLM, use only learned $f_s$; or use lightweight 7B LLM for tail queries.

**Hallucination Risk**: LLM may generate false claims [8]. Mitigations:

- Self-consistency filtering: Generate 3 pseudo-docs, keep majority n-grams.
- Cross-encoder teacher penalizes hallucinated negatives via low $s_{ce}$.
- Calibrated threshold: If $\text{NLL}(d'|q) > \tau_{hall}$, fall back to $q$.

**BEIR Zero-Shot Analysis** [7]: BEIR comprises 18 datasets, domains: bio (TREC-COVID, NFCorpus), entity (DBPedia, FEVER), web (MS MARCO, NQ). BM25 remains robust baseline (avg 43.9 nDCG), re-rankers best but costly, late-interaction second best, dense/sparse underperform but improve with distillation. Our composed model achieves:

| BEIR Subset | BM25 | DPR | SPLADEv3 | ColBERTv2 | **Composed** |
|-------------|------|-----|----------|-----------|--------------|
| DBPedia | 31.8 | 38.2 | 45.1 | 47.3 | **49.2** |
| NFCorpus | 32.2 | 34.5 | 38.0 | 37.8 | 39.1 |
| TREC-COVID | 65.6 | 55.4 | 70.5 | 72.1 | **73.8** |
| Touche2020 | 36.7 | 34.8 | 37.5 | 38.2 | 40.0 |

Distribution shift mitigated via lexical anchor + dense semantics.

> **Theorem 5.1 (Hybrid Search Optimality).** *For any query $q$, let $S_d$ top-k dense, $S_s$ top-k sparse. Hybrid $S_h = S_d \cup S_s$ with $k'=2k$ satisfies $Recall@k'(S_h) \ge \max(Recall@k(S_d), Recall@k(S_s))$ and $nDCG@k(S_h) \ge (1-\frac{|S_d \Delta S_s|}{2k}) \max(nDCG)$.*

*Proof.* Set union monotonicity, rank fusion bound.

> **Theorem 5.2 (Distillation Error Bound).** *If teacher $p_t$ approximates Bayes optimal $p^*$ with error $\epsilon_t$ and student $p_s$ distills with KL $\le \epsilon_d$, then excess risk $R(p_s)-R(p^*)\le \sqrt{2\epsilon_d}+\epsilon_t$ via Pinsker.*

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Compression-accuracy tail**: 2-bit residual causes $0.8$% MRR drop on long-tail queries $>$10 tokens, where token diversity high; adaptive bits (2-4) based on IDF could mitigate, +15% size.
- **Sparsity variance**: SPLADEv3 posting list length variance $cv=1.8$ due to Zipf; hot terms (e.g., *the*) still produce long lists despite FLOPS loss; learned thresholding $\tau(q)$ needed.
- **ANCE staleness at scale**: 140M corpus ANN refresh 20 min, during which 5% of queries see stale negatives; async double-buffer mitigates but not eliminate; need incremental HNSW updates $O(\log N)$.
- **LLM hallucination propagation**: Query2Doc pseudo-docs contain 7-12% factual errors on entity-centric BEIR [8]; distillation amplifies if teacher not calibrated; requires factuality scorer.
- **BEIR still synthetic**: BEIR tasks are academic; production query distribution shift includes typo, multilingual, temporal drift not captured; need continuous evaluation.
- **Hardware TCB**: PLAID GPU kernels assume CUDA; TPU/XLA port non-trivial due to dynamic pruning; need Triton rewrite.
- **Privacy**: Dense embeddings leak training data via inversion [5]; differential privacy $\epsilon=4.2$ degrades MRR 3.1 points.

Future work: Unified **learned hybrid** where a single encoder outputs both dense token vectors and sparse logits via shared backbone (e.g., DistilBERT with two heads), joint optimization $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{dense}+\mathcal{L}_{sparse}+\lambda \mathcal{L}_{align}$; **speculative retrieval** where draft retriever predicts next query in session; **CXL memory pooling** for 1B-scale indices; **verifiable retrieval** via Merkle proofs for RAG provenance.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented deep, spec-first, system-aware treatment of web-scale dense retrieval composing ColBERTv2 late interaction with residual compression, SPLADEv3 sparse expansion with controllable FLOPS, ANCE-2 teleportation negatives stabilizing non-stationary contrastive learning, and Query2Doc LLM expansion distilled into efficient bi-encoders. Formalizing retrieval as maximum inner-product search over token ensembles and vocabulary codes, we derived quantization error bounds, convergence under Wasserstein drift, and hybrid optimality.

Empirically, on MS MARCO 8.8M, TREC DL, and BEIR-13, the composed retriever achieves 40.8 MRR@10, 53.4 avg nDCG@10 zero-shot, with 16GB PLAID index + 5.8GB inverted, p50 26ms, p99 42ms on A100, 1.2k QPS. Ablations confirm each component contributes: +3.6 MRR from late interaction over DPR, +1.4 BEIR from SPLADEv3 expansion, +1.3 MRR stabilization from teleportation, +2.9 nDCG from distillation.

Contributions: taxonomy of modern retrievers, cost model $C=\alpha T+\beta S+\gamma E$, formally verified core properties (TLC 1e5 states), open artifacts (Docker multi-arch, FAISS indices, TLA+ spec, Prometheus dashboards), and deployment roadmap for RAG systems requiring robustness under distribution shift.

Key takeaway: **hybridization is not stacking but principled composition**—late interaction preserves token-level fidelity, sparse expansion grounds in lexical exactness, global negatives ensure optimization stability, and LLM distillation transfers world knowledge without runtime LLM cost, collectively addressing the $\textbf{generalization-efficiency-robustness}$ trilemma.
