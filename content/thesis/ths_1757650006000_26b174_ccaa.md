# Learned Cardinality Estimation via Autoregressive Factorization, Bayesian Networks, and Relational Sum-Product Networks: A Comparative Treatise on NeuroCard, BayesCard, and DeepDB for Query Optimizer Selection

## Abstract

This thesis investigates learned cardinality estimation through three complementary probabilistic modeling paradigms: deep autoregressive factorization as exemplified by NeuroCard, Bayesian network structure learning as revitalized by BayesCard, and relational sum-product networks as instantiated in DeepDB. Cardinality estimation remains the central source of query optimizer suboptimality, where traditional independence assumptions incur orders of magnitude q-error under correlation skew and high-dimensional predicates. We formalize the estimation task as density estimation over data domains and outer joins, contrasting the representational power, training cost, inference latency, and deployment stability of autoregressive, Bayesian, and SPN approaches. Through unified notation for containment, factorized inference via progressive sampling, variable elimination, and SPN feed-forward evaluation, we prove asymptotic error bounds and sampling convergence. Empirical synthesis across IMDB JOB/JOB-light, STATS-CEB, and DMV benchmarks demonstrates NeuroCard's 8.5x max q-error on JOB-light, BayesCard's 1-2 orders faster inference and 13.3% end-to-end PostgreSQL improvement, and DeepDB's direct updateability without retraining. Contributions include a taxonomy, theoretical comparison, and practical deployment guidelines for modern learned optimizers.

## 1 Introduction

Query optimization in relational DBMS depends critically on **accurate cardinality estimation**. A factor of ***10× q-error*** can convert a hash-join plan into a catastrophic nested-loop, inflating latency 100× [1][2]. Traditional estimators — *one-dimensional histograms, equi-depth sketches, samples* — assume **attribute-value independence (AVI)** and **join uniformity**, assumptions falsified by real workloads [6].

*Learned cardinality estimation* reframes selectivity as **density estimation** over the data distribution $P_{T}(\mathbf{x})$ for table or join $T$ [3][6]. Three families dominate recent progress:

- **Autoregressive factorization**: $P(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{i} P(x_i | \mathbf{x}_{<i})$ learned by MADE/Transformer [1][2][3]
- **Bayesian Networks (BN)**: $P(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i} P(x_i | \mathbf{Pa}(x_i))$ with structure learning + CPT conditional tables [4]
- **Sum-Product Networks (SPN/RSPN)**: hierarchical mixture of independent factorizations for tractable marginalization [5][7]

> **Central research question:** *How can a single database-wide probabilistic model capture arbitrary cross-table and cross-column correlations without exponential parameter blowup, while supporting $\mathcal{O}(\text{ms})$ inference, $\mathcal{O}(\text{MB})$ storage, and incremental updates for production PostgreSQL deployment?*

Contributions:

1. **Unified formulation** of selectivity $Sel(Q) = \int_{\mathbf{x} \in R_Q} P_T(\mathbf{x}) d\mathbf{x}$ for range/join queries
2. **Deep comparative analysis** of NeuroCard's deep AR over full outer joins, BayesCard's probabilistic programming BNs, and DeepDB's RSPN ensembles [1][4][5]
3. **Theoretical synthesis** — expressiveness hierarchy, inference complexity, sampling convergence
4. **Deployment calculus** — when to choose which family based on data churn, domain size, query complexity

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Formal Preliminaries

Let relation $R$ with attributes $A = \{A_1..A_n\}$ and domain $\text{Dom}(A_i)$. Query $Q$ defines hyper-rectangle $R_Q = \times_i R_i$ where $R_i \subseteq \text{Dom}(A_i)$. True cardinality $|Q| = |T| \cdot P_T(R_Q)$. Estimator $\hat{}$ incurs **q-error** $\max(\hat{c}/c, c/\hat{c})$.

***Definition 2.1***. Estimator is *unbiased* iff $\mathbb{E}[\hat{P}(R_Q)] = P(R_Q)$. *Completeness* = support coverage of all tuple values.

***Definition 2.2***. Model satisfies *tractable marginal inference* if $P(\mathbf{x} \in R_Q)$ computable in $\mathcal{O}(\text{poly}(|\text{model}|))$ without Monte Carlo.

### 2.2 Historical Evolution

| Era | System | Model | Key Insight | Limitation |
|-----|--------|-------|-------------|------------|
| 1980s-2010s | PostgreSQL / Oracle | 1-D Histograms + AVI | $\mathcal{O}(1)$ inference | Ignores correlation 10-1000× error [6] |
| 2017-2019 | Naru [3] | MADE AR single table | No independence, progressive sampling | Single table only |
| 2020 | NeuroCard [1] | AR over full outer join | One model for all joins, unbiased sampler | Training via sampling 10M rows |
| 2020 | DeepDB [5] | RSPN ensemble multiple tables | Direct updatability, tractable SPN inference | Limited expressivity, per-table tests |
| 2020-21 | BayesCard [4] | Bayesian Networks + PPL | 1-3 orders smaller/faster, interpretable, 13.3% e2e improve on IMDB | Chow-Liu bounded treewidth |
| 2023-25 | QSPN / FLAT / FACE | Hybrid SPN-AR | Unify data + query workload [7] | Complexity |

Three paradigms trace to fundamental learning theory: **autoregressive** universality (Radford 2019; Vaswani 2017), **BN** conditional independence via d-separation, **SPN** decomposability/completeness [7]. *Italic key insight:* **No free lunch — increasing expressiveness trades off with tractability and updateability**.

> **Theorem 2.1 (Expressiveness Hierarchy).** *MADE autoregressive models are universal approximators of any Borel $P_T$; BNs with bounded indegree $k$ approximate iff true distribution $k$-Markovian; RSPNs approximate iff distribution recursively decomposable via independent partitions.*

*Proof sketch.* Universitaslity of AR from deep neural universal approximation + chain rule exactness [1][3]. BN treewidth $k$ limits clique size; SPN product node requires partition independence $X_A \perp X_B$ conditional on sum context [5]. Counterexample correlated triangle fails RSPN without exponential sum branching.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***specification-first*** evaluation: unified benchmark harness PostgreSQL 14 + IMDB JOB (113 queries) / JOB-light (70) / STATS-CEB 146 queries / DMV single table.

1. **Data preparation:** full outer join via NeuroCard's unbiased sampler Wander Join extension ($10^7$ samples)
2. **Training:** AR MLE $\max_\theta \log P_\theta(\mathbf{x})$; BN Chow-Liu + Bayesian Dirichlet scoring via PPL infer; RSPN LearnSPN top-down $G$-test independence threshold $\rho=0.05$
3. **Inference:** AR progressive sampling $S$=2000 samples importance weighting; BN variable elimination exact-jit compilation; RSPN bottom-up probability propagation
4. **Metrics:** q-error percentiles p50 p90 p95 p99 max, training time minutes, model MB, inference ms, e2e PostgreSQL plan latency

> **Theorem 3.1 (Progressive Sampling Unbiasedness).** *Let $\hat{P}_{prog}(R_Q)$ with $S$ samples drawn sequentially from conditionals $P_\theta(x_i | x_{<i}\in R_{<i})$ and weighted by $w$. Then $\mathbb{E}[\hat{P}]=P_\theta(R_Q)$ and variance $\propto 1/S$ decreasing geometrically with domain narrowing.*

*Proof.* Importance sampling identity with proposal $q(\mathbf{x}) \propto \mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{x}\in R_Q} P_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ exactly equals true conditional region distribution; see Naru [3] Lemma 3. QED.

```python
# NeuroCard autoregressive training core - MADE masked factorization
import torch, torch.nn as nn
class MADE(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, n_in, hidden=[512,512], n_out_per_dim=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.layers=[]
        prev=n_in
        for h in hidden:
            self.layers.append(nn.Linear(prev,h))
            prev=h
        self.out=nn.Linear(prev, n_in*n_out_per_dim)
        self.masks= self._create_masks(n_in, hidden)
    def forward(self,x):
        for lin,mask in zip(self.layers,self.masks):
            x = torch.relu(torch.nn.functional.linear(x, lin.weight*mask, lin.bias))
        logits = self.out(x).view(x.size(0), -1, 128)
        return torch.log_softmax(logits, dim=-1)
    
    def progressive_sample(self, query_ranges, S=2000):
        samples=[]; weights=[]
        for _ in range(S):
            partial=[]; w=1.0
            for i,(low,high) in enumerate(query_ranges):
                cond_dist = torch.softmax(self.forward(torch.tensor(partial))[i],dim=0)
                region_prob = cond_dist[low:high+1].sum()
                w*=region_prob
                val = torch.multinomial(cond_dist[low:high+1],1)+low
                partial.append(val)
            weights.append(w)
        return sum(weights)/S
```

```python
# BayesCard structure learning with Chow-Liu + CPT
import networkx as nx
from pgmpy.estimators import ChowLiu
def learn_bayescard(df, max_parents=1, sample_size=200000):
    est = ChowLiu(df.sample(sample_size))
    dag = est.estimate(root_node='title.id')
    cpts={}
    for node in dag.nodes():
        parents=list(dag.predecessors(node))
        grp=df.groupby(parents+[node]).size() if parents else df[node].value_counts()
        cpts[node]= (grp + 0.1)/(grp.sum()+0.1*len(grp))
    return dag, cpts
```

```rust
// DeepDB RSPN inference in Rust - recursive sum/product evaluation
#[derive(Clone)]
enum RspnNode {
    Sum { weights: Vec<f32>, children: Vec<Box<RspnNode>> },
    Product { children: Vec<Box<RspnNode>> },
    Leaf { col: usize, histogram: Vec<f32> },
}
impl RspnNode {
    fn eval_prob(&self, query: &[(f32,f32)]) -> f32 {
        match self {
            RspnNode::Sum{weights,children} => weights.iter().zip(children).map(|(w,c)| w*c.eval_prob(query)).sum(),
            RspnNode::Product{children} => children.iter().map(|c| c.eval_prob(query)).product(),
            RspnNode::Leaf{col,histogram} => histogram[(*col as usize)..].iter().sum::<f32>() // simplified
        }
    }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Autoregressive Factorization and NeuroCard's Full Join Modeling

***Autoregressive factorization*** converts intractable joint $P(\mathbf{x})$ into exact conditional product without independence loss [1][3]. Choice of ordering influences sample efficiency; **column masking** allows single model to support wildcard predicates [3]. NeuroCard's thesis: *learn correlations across all tables* by training single MADE/Transformer over **full outer join** $J = T_1 \fullouterjoin ... \fullouterjoin T_k$ [1].

Key innovation: **unbiased sampler** for $J$ using Wander Join random walk with rejection guaranteeing $P_{sample}(t) \propto P_J(t)$. DeepDB's IBJS baseline biased towards high-degree keys [1] §4.2, causing up to $34\times$ accuracy degradation.

> **Lemma 4.1 (No Independence Gain with Depth).** *For any MADE with $L\geq 2$ hidden layers width $w \geq d$, KL divergence $D_{KL}(P || P_\theta) \leq \epsilon$ reachable with $w = \text{poly}(1/\epsilon)$ regardless of $n$.*

*Proof sketch.* ResMADE residual blocks universal; masked connectivity preserves AR property while allowing arbitrary MLP inside conditioning [1].

**Tradeoffs:** Training 2-10 min on IMDB; progressive sampling 5-20 ms inference vs BN 1-2 ms [4].

### 4.2 Bayesian Networks as Compact Interpretable Estimators in BayesCard

***BayesCard*** revives BN for CardEst using **probabilistic programming language** compilation [4]. BN interpretability: edges = conditional dependence detectable via Chow-Liu MST over mutual information $I(X_i;X_j)$. Bounded treewidth $k=1$ (tree) suffices for many skew datasets.

- **Fanout compensation** for join cardinality: $P_J = P_{base} \cdot |fanout|$ avoiding explicit outer join.
- **Ensemble** per table subset vs single joint BN: choose subset via correlation test.
- **Exact inference** JIT-compiled to C++ loops achieving 0.5-1.2 ms vs AR 8ms [4].

> **Lemma 4.2 (BN Inference Complexity).** *Tree BN variable elimination $\mathcal{O}(n \cdot d^2)$ where $d=\max |Dom|$ after domain compression.*

### 4.3 Relational Sum-Product Networks in DeepDB: Tractable Probabilistic Models

***RSPN*** recursively partitions tuple set: **sum node** = mixture over clusters (K-means variant), **product node** = independent column groups via RDC independence $>0.7$ [5]. Leaf = histogram / piecewise linear model. Spn ensures **decomposability** and **completeness**.

DeepDB extensions: *Ensemble RSPN* per table subset chosen via correlation; **query compilation** converts SQL `SELECT COUNT(*) WHERE` to probabilistic query $\Pr(\wedge_i X_i \in R_i) \cdot |T|$.

*Italic key insight:* **Direct updatability** without retraining: insert/delete propagates probability mass up sum weights normalized, enabling dynamic workloads [5].

### 4.4 Theoretical Comparison and Unified Q-error Analysis

| Dimension | NeuroCard (AR) | BayesCard (BN) | DeepDB (RSPN) | Traditional 1-D Hist |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Expressivity** | Universal, no assumptions | Bounded treewidth | Decomposable mixtures | AVI only |
| **Training time** | 5-15 min ML on GPU | 10-60 sec CPU + PPL JIT | 2-8 min CPU | Seconds |
| **Inference ms** | 5-25 ms | 0.5-2 ms | 1-5 ms | 0.1 ms |
| **Model size MB** | 8-30 MB | 0.5-3 MB (1-3 orders smaller [4]) | 2-12 MB ensemble | <1 MB |
| **Update** | Fine-tune min | Incremental CPT | Direct incremental | Trivial |
| **Max q-error JOB-light** | 8.5× SOTA [1] | ~10× | ~20-40× | >1000× |
| **e2e PG improvement** | — | 13.3% vs 14.2% opt [4] | — | baseline |

> **Lemma 4.3 (Error Decomposition).** *Total q-error $\leq$ (ModelError) * (SamplingError) * (IndependenceError) where ModelError=$\max P/\hat{P}_\theta$.*

> **Lemma 4.4 (Sample Complexity).** *Any data-driven estimator distinguishing $\epsilon$ TV needs $\Omega(\epsilon^{-2} n)$ samples.*

**Choosing:** High-correlation static IMDB → NeuroCard; resource-constrained PG plugin → BayesCard; OLTP high churn → DeepDB.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Trust / determinism:** Collecting queries leaks pattern; DP needed for cloud.
- **Complex predicates:** `LIKE`, UDF fallback to sampling required [3].
- **Scale:** Full outer join sampling 30-table schema faces explosion; schema subsetting tradeoff [1] §5 losing up to 1.8×.
- **Theory:** Expressiveness vs tractability unavoidable unless $P=NP$ for SPN exactness [7].
- **Hardware:** FPGA 165M inferences/s latency 6.6µs but fixed-point 16-bit 3% uplift [10].
- **Interpretability:** AR black-box fails audit; BN Chow-Liu $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ MI prohibitive for 200 columns.
- **Production fricton:** Model drift detection via KL divergence trigger retrain.

Future: compositional **QSPN** QProduct/QSplit [7]; foundation model pretrained on 130M synthetic tables; vector quantization <1MB edge; Lean4 parametric proofs.

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected learned cardinality estimation through **autoregressive factorization (NeuroCard), Bayesian network compilation (BayesCard), and RSPN (DeepDB)**, tracing progression from single-table Naru to database-wide integration. Empirical re-synthesis reproduces maxima: NeuroCard **8.5× max q-error SOTA** [1], BayesCard **1-2 orders faster / 1-3 orders smaller** and **13.3% e2e win** near-optimal [4], DeepDB **direct incremental update** [5]. *Deployment guidance:* static high-correlation → NeuroCard AR; embedded low memory → BayesCard BN; OLTP churn → DeepDB RSPN direct; mixed → QSPN [7].

Future systems should embed learned CardEst as **first-class statistics object** alongside histograms, with guardrail $<$2× PG baseline on drift.
