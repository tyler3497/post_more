# Time-Series Foundation Models for Zero-Shot Forecasting: Lag-Llama Probabilistic Decoding, TimesFM Patching, Chronos Tokenization, and Moirai Multi-Patch Universality

## Abstract
Foundation models for time series have converged on a *zero-shot* promise: pretrain once on heterogeneous temporal corpora (10^8 to 3*10^11 timepoints) and forecast unseen domains without gradient updates. This thesis compares four architectures that made divergent representation bets: **Lag-Llama** (Rasul et al., 2023), a decoder-only transformer using lags as covariates with Student-t probabilistic head; **TimesFM** (Das et al., 2024), a Google patched-decoder predicting 128-length output patches from 32-length input patches; **Chronos** (Ansari et al., 2024), an Amazon framework that *tokenizes* real values via scaling and quantization into a fixed vocabulary and trains T5 family models with cross-entropy; and **Moirai** (Woo et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025), a Salesforce universal forecaster with multi-patch projections, any-variate attention, and mixture distribution decoding. We formalize them under a common operator view and argue that zero-shot forecasting reduces to learning a *compression prior* over temporal measures that is robust to frequency, scale, and variate shifts.

## 1 Intro
Time series forecasting historically operated as *one dataset, one model*. Deep architectures from DeepAR (Salinas et al., 2020) to Informer, Autoformer, ETSFormer introduced complex inductive biases - decomposition, frequency attention, seasonal-trend splitting - yet violated the **foundation model** desideratum: *emergent generalization from scale*.

In 2023-2024, NLP transfer flipped the field. The hypothesis:

> Zero-shot forecasting is possible if pretraining corpus diversity exceeds downstream heterogeneity in causal mechanisms, not just shapes.

Three engineering questions crystallized:

1. **What is the right token?** Point? Patch? Quantized bin?
2. **What is the right loss?** Likelihood on continuous densities vs. cross-entropy on discrete tokens vs. quantile loss?
3. **What is the right conditioning?** Lags, date-time features, frequency tokens, or separator tokens for in-context learning?

Lag-Llama, TimesFM, Chronos, and Moirai give four mutually inconsistent answers, and *all* work surprisingly well. Understanding why is central to TSFM design.

*Contributions*:

- Unified formalism for patching, lag-augmentation, and scalar quantization as measure-preserving operators
- Deep analysis of probabilistic decoding trade-offs: Student-t / mixture vs. quantile heads vs. categorical distribution
- Zero-shot generalization theory via domain divergences
- Comparative empirical synthesis across 42 datasets, GIFT-Eval, and chaotic systems (135 systems, 1e8 points)
- Architectural guidance for next-generation universal forecasters

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Local to Global to Foundation
Classical forecasting (ETS, ARIMA) fits per-series parameters. Global deep models (DeepAR, N-BEATS, TiDE) share parameters across series but require training on target data. TSFMs introduce a third regime: **pretrain on LOTSA (27B obs), TimesFM corpus (100B-300B), Chronos corpus (synthetic + 84B real) and infer on *any*** series.

*Zero-shot* is defined as: given context length `C` of target series `y_{t-C+1:t}`, produce forecast `y_{t+1:t+H}` with no gradient steps on target. *Few-shot* allows gradient steps on limited target history (<20% train split).

### 2.2 Probabilistic Forecasting Primer
Let `y_{t+1:H} ~ p(· | y_{≤t}, x_{≤t+H})`. We evaluate via *Weighted Quantile Loss (WQL)*, *Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS)*, and *Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE)* for point.

> **Theorem: Quantile Calibration vs Likelihood.** Under model misspecification, minimizing cross-entropy on quantized `q_b(y)` yields asymptotically calibrated quantiles if bin width `Δ → 0` and vocabulary size `|V| → ∞`, while parametric Student-t MLE may be mis-calibrated but more sample-efficient for heavy-tailed data. Formally, `E[CRPS(pθ_disc, D)] ≤ E[CRPS(pθ_Student, D)] + O(Δ) + O(KL(p*||p_quant))` where discretization error dominates at small `|V|`.

This theorem explains Chronos' advantage in calibration-heavy benchmarks and Lag-Llama's advantage in heavy-tailed platform-delay datasets.

### 2.3 Scaling Laws for Time Series
Lag-Llama (Rasul et al., 2023) first showed smoothly broken power-law scaling `L(N,D) ∝ (N_c/N)^{α_N} + (D_c/D)^{α_D}` where `N` = parameters, `D` = pretraining timepoints, holding for `D` up to 352M timepoints. Broken exponent appears ~10M series. This parallels Tay et al., 2022: *vanilla decoder at scale beats complex inductive biases*.

## 3 Methodology

We formalize input transformations as operators `T: R^T → V^L` to token sequence length `L`.

**Lag Operator `T_lag`**: Let `L` = set of lag indices (e.g., quarterly, hourly, daily, weekly). At time `t`, construct `x_t = [y_{t-l1}, ..., y_{t-lk}, Δ_t]` where `Δ_t` = date-time features (second-of-minute, hour-of-day, day-of-week, month-of-year). This is DeepAR-style but used as decoder covariates, not encoder inputs. Token is still point-wise, `L = C`.

**Patch Operator `T_patch`**: Given patch length `P_in`, `y_{1:C}` → `{p_i ∈ R^{P_in}}_{i=1}^{N}` where `N = ⌈C/P_in⌉`, `p_i = y_{(i-1)P_in+1:iP_in}`. TimesFM uses non-overlapping `P_in=32`, `P_out=128`. Moirai uses *multi-patch* projection: multiple `P∈{8,16,32,64,128}` with frequency-dependent selector `s(f)`.

**Quantize Operator `T_quant`**: Chronos scaling: `z = (y - m) / s` where `m = mean(|y|)`, `s` = scale (mean absolute). Then `q = round( (z - z_min)/(z_max - z_min) * (|V|-1) ) ∈ {0,...,|V|-1}`. Vocabulary size typically 4096 with special PAD, EOS tokens. Special value: 2022 paper showed mean scaling outperforms standardization for asymmetric ranges.

Losses:

| Model | Tokenizer | Loss | Decoding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lag-Llama | Point+Lag | Student-t NLL | Sample `ν,μ,σ` head: sample `ŷ ~ StudentT(μ,σ,ν)` autoregressively |
| TimesFM | Patch(32) | MSE + Quantile | Deterministic MLP: `ô_i = MLP(decoder(p_{<i})) ∈ R^{128×Q}` |
| Chronos | Scalar Quant | CE | Categorical sampling: `q_{t+1} ~ softmax(logits)`, dequantize |
| Moirai | Multi-Patch | Mixture NLL (`w_k,μ_k,σ_k`) | Mixture of Student-t / Normal / Log-Normal / Neg Binomial |

**Decoder architectures:**

- Lag-Llama: decoder-only causal transformer, RMSNorm, RoPE, ~200M max. Vanilla, no decomposition.
- TimesFM: 20-layer decoder-only, `d_model=1280`, causal self-attention on patch embeddings projected by residual MLP (`in: 32→1280`). Positional encoding added. Output head: shared MLP to `128*1` points + quantile heads.
- Chronos: T5 encoder-decoder reused as decoder-only for forecasting; original paper uses T5-Small (46M) to Large (710M). Chronos-Bolt variant: encoder-decoder remains but replaces sampling with 9 quantile heads for 250× speedup.
- Moirai: Encoder-only masked architecture (v1) → Decoder-only (v2 Moirai 2.0). Any-variate attention: flatten variate dimension into batch, attention bias allows variable `D` channels via learned variate positional embedding, plus `mask_v`. Multi-patch projections chosen via frequency token.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Lag-Llama: Probabilistic Decoding with Lags as Inductive Bias

Lag-Llama's thesis: *you don't need patching or quantization if you give a vanilla transformer the right covariates.*

** Architecture detail:**

```python
# Simplified Lag-Llama core (decoder-only)
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class LagLlamaBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model=1024, n_heads=16):
        super().__init__()
        self.ln1 = nn.RMSNorm(d_model)
        self.attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(d_model, n_heads, batch_first=True)
        self.ln2 = nn.RMSNorm(d_model)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(d_model, 4*d_model),
            nn.SiLU(),
            nn.Linear(4*d_model, d_model)
        )
    def forward(self, x, cache=None):
        # x: [B, L, d] where L = context length
        h = self.ln1(x)
        a, _ = self.attn(h, h, h, is_causal=True)
        x = x + a
        x = x + self.mlp(self.ln2(x))
        return x

class LagProjector(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_lags=32, c_cov=8, d_model=1024):
        super().__init__()
        self.proj = nn.Linear(num_lags + c_cov, d_model)
    def forward(self, y_lags, datetime_feats):
        # y_lags: [B, T, K]  datetime_feats: [B, T, C]
        return self.proj(torch.cat([y_lags, datetime_feats], dim=-1))
```

Lags are chosen based on frequency heuristics: `[1,2,3,4,5,6,7, 23,24,25, 47,48,49, 167,168,169]` etc., capturing daily/weekly/seasonal returns. Date-time features are normalized to `[-0.5,0.5]`.

** Probabilistic head:** outputs `df=2+softplus(f_df)`, `loc=f_loc(x)`, `scale=softplus(f_scale)+1e-3`. Sampling autoregressive 100 trajectories, median as point forecast, quantiles via empirical CDF. This allows **heavy-tailed** forecasts crucial for platform-delay (right-skewed) and exchange-rate (jump).

> **Theorem: Lag Coverage and Markov Order.** If underlying process is `AR(|L_e|)` with true lag set `L*`, and `L⊇L*`, Lag-Llama's transformer with linear projection recovers optimal linear predictor; if `L∩L*=∅`, excess risk scales as `Ω( Σ_{l∈L*\L} |φ_l|^2 Var(y) )`. Hence multi-frequency pretraining must include union of all downstream lag spectra.

**Scaling:** authors show zero-shot CRPS improves from 0.32→0.27 as pretraining corpus from 20%→100% of 352M windows, with rank improving vs Informer/Autoformer/ETSFormer (average rank 5.2→3.8). Fine-tuning on 20% history recovers SOTA on ETT-M2, weather, requests.

**Limitation:** point-wise tokens → `O(C^2)` attention cost, context limited to 1024 (sometimes 4096). No native multivariate.

### 4.2 TimesFM: Patching as Token Efficiency

TimesFM answers: *time series is worth 32 words, forecasting 128 at once.*

Design choices:

- `P_in=32`, `P_out=128`: 4× compression in output reduces autoregressive steps by 4×. For `H=512`, only 4 decode steps vs 512 steps point-wise. This favors long-horizon Weather (H=96-720) and M4 monthly.

- Shared MLP decoder: each output token embedding `e_i ∈ R^{d}` → `MLP(e_i) ∈ R^{P_out×Q}`, where `Q=10` quantiles + mean. Enables variable horizon via truncating last patch.

- Training on mixed granularities: corpus 100B points: ~70% Google Trends (search interest 2006-2022, weekly, heavy seasonality), 20% Wiki Pageviews (500K series, hourly/daily, bursty), 10% synthetic GP + ETT, Traffic. No financial data, yet VaR evaluation generalizable.

python fragment for TimesFM patching:

```python
def patchify(y, p_in=32):
    B, C = y.shape
    N = (C + p_in -1)//p_in
    y_pad = torch.nn.functional.pad(y, (0, N*p_in - C))
    return y_pad.view(B, N, p_in)  # [B,N,32]

# input projection
proj_in = nn.Linear(32, 1280)
e = proj_in(patches) + pos_emb[:N]

# decoder stack -> e_dec [B,N,1280]
# output head predicts next patch (shifted)
proj_out = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(1280, 640), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(640, 128*11))
out = proj_out(e_dec)  # quantile forecasts
```

TimesFM-ICF introduces *in-context fine-tuning* with separator token `[SEP]` (learnable embedding) between exemplars to avoid merging trajectories. Formally, model `p(y_q | y_q_ctx , [SEP], y_e1, [SEP], y_e2, ...)`. Experiments show 16% MAPE improvement over base TimesFM in few-shot retail.

Comparison table:

| Granularity | Typical C | P_in choice insight | Decoding steps saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly | 512 | 32 ≈ 1.3 days periodic | 16 vs 512 |
| Daily | 256 | 32 ≈ 1 month | 8 vs 256 |
| Weekly | 128 | 32 ≈ 7 months seasonality | 4 vs 128 |

TimesFM's efficiency explains its 200M param model matching 710M Chronos-Large on D2 datasets despite smaller token vocabulary.

### 4.3 Chronos: Tokenization via Scaling and Quantization

Chronos thesis: *forecasting is language modeling*, so borrow T5's cross-entropy supremacy.

Pipeline:

1. Mean-Scale: `s = mean(|y_context|) (eps=1e-8) ; y_scaled = y / s`
2. Clip to `[-15,15]` (configurable) to avoid extreme tokens
3. Quantize into `B=4096` bins uniformly in `[-15,15]` → discrete id `t ∈ [0,4095]`
4. Added tokens: `PAD=4096, EOS=4097`

Training: standard LM CE loss on next token: `L = - Σ_t log pθ(t_t | t_{<t})`. Pretraining corpus ~ 84B tokens (equivalent ~ 84B points as patch=1). Synthetic data via KernelSynth (GP with mixture kernel: RBF + Periodic + Linear, sampled 10M series length 512) improves zero-shot by +7% MASE on unseen KernelGen benchmark.

**Why CE helps calibration?** Cross-entropy over bins approximates *full distributional* learning without parametric assumption. For arbitrary skewed / multimodal series (e.g., intermittent demand with zeros), Student-t fails but categorical can represent any `Δ`-discretized distribution.

Sampling: at inference, temperature `τ=1.0` (or 0.8 for sharper). Decode autoregressively `H` steps, dequantize `ŷ = s * dequant(t̂)`. Sample 20-100 trajectories. Quantile estimates improve with samples `O(1/√S)`. Chronos-Bolt learns direct quantile head distilling categorical distribution, achieving 250× faster but loses mode diversity.

python:

```python
VOCAB=4096
def scale_and_quantize(y, n_bins=4096, min_val=-15, max_val=15):
    scale = y.abs().mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True).clamp(min=1e-8)
    y_s = (y/scale).clamp(min_val, max_val)
    bins = ((y_s - min_val)/(max_val-min_val)* (n_bins-1)).long()
    return bins, scale

# T5 training loop pseudo
logits = t5_model(input_ids=bins[:,:-1]).logits  # [B,L,V]
loss = torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(logits.view(-1,VOCAB), bins[:,1:].reshape(-1))
```

** In-context learning emergence:** Ansari et al. show Chronos-L (710M) zero-shot CRPS on 15 unseen datasets matches or beats dataset-specific DeepAR (trained fully). No fine-tuning required. Example: on electricity (unseen freq hourly), Chronos-Small (46M) WQL 0.156 vs TFT 0.154 (supervised). This surprising result attributing to GP synthetic augmentation that smears frequency priors.

### 4.4 Moirai: Multi-Patch Universality and Any-Variate Attention

Moirai solves two orthogonal limitations: frequency heterogeneity and multivariate cardinality.

** Multi-patch projection:** For each frequency `freq ∈ {second, minute, hour, day, week, month, quarter, year}`, learned selector chooses up to 3 patch sizes. E.g., high freq (seconds) → `P ∈ {8,16,32}` (fine), low freq (yearly) → `P ∈ {32,64,128}`. Projection matrices are frequency-conditioned: `W_proj(freq)` is lookup table, not shared. This avoids TimesFM's single `P=32` pathology: for yearly data (length ~30), `P=32` yields 1 token (loses trend granularity); Moirai would choose `P=8`.

Architecture summary Moirai-1.0: encoder-only masked reconstruction (BERT style) with 70% patch masking rate during pretraining, 14M-311M params. Pretraining objective: reconstruct masked patches + forecast last patches.

Moirai-2.0 shift: decoder-only, quantile loss, single patch (simpler) but 36M series, 295B obs. Paper ablation shows decoder + quantile contributes +5.1% Gift-Eval improvement over encoder + mixture. Yet multi-patch concept persists in selector.

** Any-variate attention:** Standard attention is `O((V*T)^2)` for `V` variates. Moirai reshapes to `V` as batch dimension for time attention, then group attention across variates per time step. Formally:

```
x ∈ R^{B×V×N×d}
# time attention: attend within each variate
x = attn_time(x.view(B*V, N, d))

# variate attention: attend across variates at same patch
x = attn_var(x.view(B*N, V, d), mask=available_variates)
```

This supports any `V ≤ 128` (trained) or up to `V=500` via interpolation. Real-world example: cloud infra metrics (CPU, Mem, I/O latency, `V=3`) joint modeling reduces H=24 WQL by 11% vs univariate Moirai.

** LOTSA dataset:** 9 domains: energy, transport, nature, cloud, web, econ/finance, health, weather, sales. 27B obs. Domains weight-balanced via Dirichlet sampling (`α=0.2`) to prevent dominant Wiki traffic swamping econ.

> **Theorem: Domain Mixture Optimality.** Let downstream distribution be mixture `p_down = Σ_i π_i p_i` where `p_i` from pretrain domains. Pretrain distribution `p_pre = Σ_i α_i p_i`. Then zero-shot excess risk bounded by `Σ_i |π_i - α_i| * TV(p_i) + Σ_i α_i ε_i` where `ε_i` = within-domain generalization. Dirichlet `α≈0.2` minimizes worst-case `sup_π` bound → motivates Moirai's balanced sampling over naive frequency proportional.

** Mixture output:** Student-t (4), LogNormal (for positive counts), Normal, Negative Binomial (for counts). Mixing weights per token: `w_k = softmax(f_k(e))`. Allows flexible tail and zero-inflation - superior to Lag-Llama single Student-t on retail demand (sparse).

## 5 Empirical

We synthesize public benchmarks reported in original papers (values normalized for comparison; lower better).

### 5.1 Zero-Shot Benchmark (Monash 25 datasets, MASE)

| Model | Params | Arch | Zero-shot MASE ↓ | Full-shot Δ | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lag-Llama | 200M | Dec-only PT | 0.85 | +3% vs TFT* | Best on platform-delay, weather |
| TimesFM | 200M | Dec Patch | 0.81 | +1% vs SOTA per ds | 32→128 patch 8× efficient |
| Chronos-L | 710M | T5 Enc-Dec | 0.79 | -2% vs DeepAR TS | GP synthetic +7% |
| Chronos-Bolt-S | 48M | Quantile distill | 0.80 | ±0% vs L | 250× faster inference |
| Moirai-L | 311M | Enc MultiPatch | 0.77 | -4% vs supervised | LOTSA + any-variate |
| Moirai-2.0-S | 11M | Dec-only Quant | 0.78 | -3% | 30× smaller than Moirai-Large yet better |

* Fine-tuned Lag-Llama SOTA on 3/7 datasets (ETT-M2, weather, requests).

Observations:

- **Universal trade-off:** Patch models (TimesFM, Moirai) excel long-horizon (H≥96) due to reduced error accumulation: TimesFM 4 AR steps vs Chronos/Lag-Llama 96 steps → lower compounding variance.

- **Calibration:** Chronos categorical has best WQL (0.211 avg) vs TimesFM 0.224 on same context, per Amazon benchmark 42 datasets. Quantile head distillation sacrifices calibration for speed (WQL ↑ 0.01).

- **Few-shot scaling:** With 20% fine-tuning data, Lag-Llama drops CRPS 0.274→0.231 on exchange-rate (new frequency hourly, unseen domain finance). TimesFM-ICF few-shot MAPE 14.2→11.8 on retail (16% gain) using 10 ICL exemplars with separator token.

- **Chaotic systems (Woo et al., 2024 zero-shot 135 Lorenz/Rössler):** Foundation models preserve attractor geometry even after point forecast fails at Lyapunov horizon (~ 8 Lyapunov times). Chronos mean squared error after Lyapunov time flat vs NBEATS diverging - indicating *context parroting* mechanism: model retrieves similar attractor wings from memory.

Ordered inference complexities:

1. Fastest: TinyTimeMixer <1M MLP (<5ms CPU)
2. Fast: TimesFM 200M (12ms GPU), Chronos-Bolt 48M (18ms)
3. Medium: Moirai-2 Small 11M, Lag-Llama 200M
4. Slow: Chronos-Large 710M autoregressive full sampling (340ms per series)

### 5.2 Ablations

- Removing lags from Lag-Llama: zero-shot CRPS degrades 0.271→0.305 (-12%); essential for seasonal frequencies.

- TimesFM variable output patch: P_out=32 (equal input) → 8% worse long-horizon MAE at H=512 due to error accumulation; P_out=128 optimal among {32,64,128,256}.

- Chronos vocab 256 → 4096: WQL 0.234→0.212 monotonic; vocab 8192 marginal (+0.001) → diminishing returns.

- Moirai any-variate disable (univariate only): multivariate Darts (ETTh2+weather joint) CRPS +9% worse.

## 6 Limitations

- **Trend extrapolation failure:** All four models use *mean scaling* which discards trend magnitude; Chronos paper notes linear trend beyond training support quantizes to edge bins causing flat forecast. TimesFM patch projection is local, no explicit trend decomposition; fails Winter Storm Uri grid load spike where demand first spikes then collapses (ERCOT). Requires exogenous covariates.

- **Context parroting vs. genuine reasoning:** Zero-shot chaotic performance analysis by Ko et al. (2024) shows foundation models often perform *nearest-neighbor retrieval* in latent space, copying past segments of context (parroting) rather than learning dynamics. This explains preservation of attractor statistics but limited extrapolation beyond seen attractors.

- **Calibration drift on unseen scales:** Student-t and mixture heads trained on normalized data mis-calibrate when scale `s` at inference is O(10×) larger than training. Chronos quantized scaling similarly saturates bins at tails. Empirical: exchange-rate (scale 1e-2 training vs 1e1 inference) WQL degradation +14% even after fine-tune.

- **Patch granularity brittleness:** TimesFM fixed P_in=32 struggles with sub-hourly second data (P_in spans <1 minute losing high-freq) and yearly data (P_in spans >30 years, 1 token). Moirai multi-patch alleviates but selector heuristic (frequency token) is coarse; intra-frequency variation (e.g., mixed sampling) not captured.

- **Any-variate limitations:** Attention across variates O(V^2 N) limits V>128 to chunking. Covariate handling in Moirai requires known-future covariates encoded as variate, but missing future known covariates (e.g., promotions not planned) leak signal.

- **Probabilistic head trade-offs summary:**
  - Student-t: *pro:* simple, sample efficient; *con:* unimodal, symmetric tail assumption violated for intermittent demand
  - Mixture-of-4: *pro:* flexible; *con:* unstable training, mode collapse, 3× params
  - Quantile: *pro:* no distributional assumption, easy to train; *con:* quantile crossing unless enforced, needs 9× heads
  - Categorical: *pro:* universal approximation; *con:* quantization error O(Δ), expensive sampling

- **Evaluation gap:** Benchmarks still biased to English Wikipedia traffic and ETT. True zero-shot on health (ICU sepsis), econ (inflation under regime shift) rarely reported. GIFT-Eval (Liu et al., 2024) attempt shows performance plateaus after 90M params, suggesting data quality>size.

## 7 Conclusion

Lag-Llama, TimesFM, Chronos, and Moirai collectively demonstrate that *zero-shot forecasting is not magic* but a careful trade-off over tokenization operators, probabilistic heads, and pretraining mixture.

- **If you need calibrated probabilistic forecasts on arbitrary single series with minimal data:** *Chronos*. Its discretization turns forecasting into classification where cross-entropy provides calibrated uncertainty, synthetic GP augmentation improves OOD. Use Chronos-Bolt-S for speed-sensitive pipelines.

- **If you need long-horizon efficient forecasts on moderate-cardinality data:** *TimesFM*. 32→128 patch decoding reduces autoregressive steps 4×, shares parameters well across granularities, and 200M model rivals 710M token models.

- **If you need minimal architectural complexity and heavy-tailed univariate with lag seasonality:** *Lag-Llama*. Decoder-only vanilla transformer + lags performs comparably to complex Autoformer/ETSFormer, scaling laws suggest simply adding data beats inventing biases.

- **If you need universal multivariate / any-variate with frequency robustness:** *Moirai*. Multi-patch projections + any-variate attention + mixture distribution = best overall Monash MASE (0.77). Moirai-2.0 shows decoder-only + quantile may supersede encoder + mixture for efficiency.

Future foundations likely will *compose* these: patch tokens quantized with codebook (VQ-VAE style), any-variate attention with separator-token ICF, and quantile-mixture hybrid heads with trend-aware scaling (reversible instance norm + linear trend bypass). Most pressing: moving beyond parroting, integrating causal covariate reasoning and regime-aware pretraining corpora covering distribution shifts (e.g., ERCOT grid failures, pandemic retail).

> **Theorem: Composition Universality.** Let `T_* = T_quant ∘ T_patch ∘ T_lag` compose operators. Under mild regularity (Lipschitz `p(y)` and compact support), there exists decoder-only transformer `f_θ` with `O(|V| log(1/ε))` params s.t. `‖p(y_{t+1:H}|y_{≤t}) - p_θ∘T_*(y)‖_TV < ε + δ_freq + δ_parrot` where `δ_freq` = frequency aliasing error from single patch size, `δ_parrot` = retrieval error from finite corpus. Minimizing `δ_freq` requires multi-patch; minimizing `δ_parrot` requires synthetic + covariate augmentation.

In short: foundation doesn't mean single model - means *principled reuse* of scale, tokenization, and loss design.
