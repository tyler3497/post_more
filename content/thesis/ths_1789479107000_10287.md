# Galois Representations and the Langlands Program: Modularity, Functoriality, and the p-adic Correspondence

## Abstract

The Langlands program is the grand organizing framework of modern number theory, predicting a deep reciprocity between automorphic representations of reductive groups and Galois representations of global fields. This thesis surveys four converging strands of that program: the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture on which p-adic Galois representations arise from geometry, the Taylor-Wiles method of modularity lifting that proved Fermat's Last Theorem and fuels ongoing automorphy lifting, the geometric Langlands correspondence that recasts reciprocity as an equivalence of categories of sheaves over moduli stacks of bundles and local systems, and the p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL(2) over Q(p), which replaces classical smooth representations with Banach-space representations and connects them to Galois representations through (phi, Gamma)-modules. We develop the core definitions, state precise theorems with hypotheses, outline proof architectures, and assess empirical and computational evidence, limitations, and open frontiers.

##1 Introduction

Few conjectural frameworks in mathematics rival the **Langlands program** in scope and coherence. Originating in Robert Langlands' 1967 letter to André Weil, it posits that arithmetic — encoded in Galois groups of number fields — and analysis — encoded in automorphic forms on reductive groups — are two faces of the same structure. The classical reciprocity conjecture asserts that every reasonable Galois representation of a global field arises from an automorphic representation, and conversely. In the fifty-plus years since, this vision has grown into several interrelated programs: the *arithmetic* Langlands correspondence, the *geometric* Langlands correspondence (a categorified version over function fields and complex curves), and the *p-adic* Langlands correspondence, which seeks a p-adic analytic analogue of the classical local correspondence.

This thesis treats four landmarks within this landscape:

1. The **Fontaine-Mazur conjecture**, which characterizes the p-adic Galois representations that come from geometry, and its unramified refinement studied by Boston [1].
2. **Modularity lifting** via the **Taylor-Wiles method**, the engine behind Wiles' proof of Fermat's Last Theorem and the Serre/Taniyama-Shimura-Weil theorems, extended beyond the classical setting by Calegari and Geraghty [2].
3. The **geometric Langlands correspondence**, where reciprocity becomes an equivalence between categories of D-modules on Bun(G) and coherent sheaves on LocSys of the dual group, with the local theory grounded in affine Kac-Moody algebras at the critical level [4].
4. The **p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL(2, Q(p))**, established by Breuil and Colmez, which attaches unitary Banach space representations of GL(2, Q(p)) to two-dimensional p-adic Galois representations via Colmez's functor and (phi, Gamma)-modules [3][6].

We proceed systematically: background machinery, methodology, a deep dive into each strand, an assessment of evidence and proofs, limitations, and conclusions.

##2 Background

### 2.1 Galois representations

Let F be a number field and G(F) the absolute Galois group of its algebraic closure over F. A **Galois representation** is a continuous homomorphism rho from G(F) into GL(n, A) for some topological coefficient ring A — typically a p-adic field such as Q(p), its algebraic closure, or a finite extension thereof. Because G(F) carries the Krull topology, continuity is a strong condition: it forces the representation to factor through a finite extension for finite coefficients, while for p-adic coefficients it encodes the deep arithmetic of towers of number fields.

At each finite place v of F, the local decomposition group G(F(v)) maps into G(F), and the **Frobenius elements** at unramified places provide the raw data of the representation. For an automorphic object pi, the Satake parameters at unramified places are expected to match the characteristic polynomials of Frobenius under the representation attached to pi — this matching is the heart of *reciprocity*.

> Theorem: **(Deligne, for modular forms)** To every normalized cuspidal eigenform f of weight k and level N there is attached a p-adic Galois representation rho(f) from G(Q) to GL(2, Q(p)-bar) such that for every prime l not dividing N*p, the trace of Frobenius at l equals the Hecke eigenvalue a(l) of f, and the determinant equals l^(k-1) times the nebentypus. This is the prototype of the global Langlands correspondence for GL(2).

### 2.2 Automorphic representations and L-functions

An automorphic representation is an irreducible admissible representation of the adelic points G(A(F)) occurring in the space of automorphic forms. To each such representation pi one attaches an **L-function** L(s, pi) via Euler products of local factors. The expected equality L(s, rho) = L(s, pi) for matching pairs is the analytic face of reciprocity.

### 2.3 p-adic Hodge theory

For a local field K with absolute Galois group G(K), Fontaine's theory of **p-adic periods** classifies p-adic representations by their associated filtered (phi, N)-modules. A representation is *crystalline*, *semistable*, or *de Rham* according as its associated module satisfies certain admissibility conditions. The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture asserts, roughly, that a global p-adic Galois representation that is *geometric* — de Rham at places above p — comes from algebraic geometry, and more precisely from the étale cohomology of an algebraic variety [5].

> Theorem: **(Petrov, 2020)** Any geometrically irreducible Q(p)-bar-local system on a smooth algebraic variety over a p-adic field K becomes de Rham after twisting by a character of the Galois group of K. Consequently the projective representation attached to such a local system over a number field automatically satisfies the hypotheses of the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture [5].

### 2.4 Deformation theory

Mazur's deformation theory parametrizes *lifts* of a residual representation rho-bar from G(F) to GL(n, F(p)-bar) into characteristic zero. The universal deformation ring R captures all such lifts subject to local conditions (unramified outside a finite set, prescribed behavior at p). On the automorphic side, a **Hecke algebra** T acts on a space of modular forms whose reductions give rho-bar. An *R = T theorem* asserts that the natural surjection R onto T is an isomorphism — every deformation is modular. This is the engine of modularity lifting.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Classical Langlands reciprocity and functoriality

The classical program associates to a connected reductive group G over a global field F its **Langlands dual group** G-hat, a complex reductive group whose root datum is dual to that of G. The reciprocity conjecture states that irreducible n-dimensional representations of the *Langlands group* of F (a hypothetical group surjecting onto G(F), unramified at almost all places) correspond bijectively to automorphic representations of GL(n, A(F)), compatibly with L-functions and epsilon factors. For general G, **functoriality** predicts that a homomorphism of dual groups induces a transfer of automorphic representations — the single most powerful unifying principle in the theory, subsuming quadratic reciprocity, class field theory, and the modularity of elliptic curves as special cases.

Key structural facts:

- *Unramified correspondence*: at an unramified place, the Satake isomorphism identifies the spherical Hecke algebra with the representation ring of G-hat, so the matching of Satake parameters with Frobenius eigenvalues is a theorem, not a conjecture.
- *Global matching*: for GL(n) over function fields, Drinfeld and Lafforgue proved the correspondence; over number fields it remains largely conjectural beyond GL(2) and a handful of cases (symmetric powers, endoscopic transfer).
- *Potential modularity*, surveyed in [7], weakens "modular" to "modular after a finite base change," and has become the central tool for proving cases of the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture and the Sato-Tate conjecture.

| Program | Coefficients | Galois side | Automorphic side | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classical local | complex / l-adic | Weil-Deligne representations | smooth representations of G(F(v)) | largely known (Harris-Taylor, Henniart) |
| Classical global | complex / l-adic | G(F) representations | automorphic representations | GL(2), GL(n) over function fields; open otherwise |
| Geometric | D-modules / sheaves | local systems on a curve | D-modules on Bun(G) | conjectural in full; many cases known |
| p-adic local (n=2, F=Q(p)) | p-adic Banach | 2-dim p-adic G(Q(p)) reps | unitary Banach reps of GL(2,Q(p)) | theorem (Breuil-Colmez) |

### 4.2 The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture

Fontaine and Mazur conjectured in 1995 that an irreducible p-adic representation rho of G(Q) that is unramified outside a finite set and *potentially semistable* at p — with distinct Hodge-Tate weights — is either of finite image up to twist or *modular*, i.e., attached to a cuspidal eigenform. The condition "potentially semistable" is the precise p-adic incarnation of "coming from geometry."

The conjecture is a p-adic refinement of the Fontaine-Mazur philosophy that *geometric implies automorphic*. Progress has been dramatic:

1. **Kisin's theorem** established the conjecture in nearly all cases for odd representations with distinct Hodge-Tate weights, using modularity lifting and the Khare-Wintenberger proof of Serre's conjecture.
2. **Calegari's work** on even representations showed that many even residual representations admit no geometric characteristic-zero lifts at all, proving the conjecture's "predicts nonexistence" direction in those cases.
3. The **unramified Fontaine-Mazur conjecture** — that unramified p-adic representations have finite image — was generalized by Boston to arbitrary pro-p coefficient rings, and recent work of Luo [1] reduces it to distinguished classes of p-adic analytic groups and interprets it via the virtually Golod-Shafarevich property.

The two-dimensional case over Q is now essentially a theorem (conditional on standard modularity lifting inputs), while the higher-dimensional and totally real cases remain active frontiers.

### 4.3 Modularity lifting and the Taylor-Wiles method

The proof of Fermat's Last Theorem rested on a **modularity lifting theorem**: if rho-bar is modular and rho is a "nice" lift (semistable, with cyclotomic determinant), then rho is modular. The proof goes through an *R = T theorem*, whose demonstration is the Taylor-Wiles method:

1. Choose auxiliary sets Q of **Taylor-Wiles primes** — primes q such that q is congruent to 1 modulo p and rho-bar(Frob(q)) has distinct eigenvalues. At such primes the local deformation problem is smooth and explicitly understood.
2. Compare the deformation ring R(Q) and Hecke algebra T(Q) at augmented level. A numerical coincidence — the rings have the same "size" measured by tangent-space dimensions — is arranged by choosing Q so that the number of primes balances the defect.
3. **Patch**: take an inverse limit over a tower of such Q to produce patched objects R(infinity) and M(infinity), where M(infinity) is a module over R(infinity) that is *free* over a power-series ring S(infinity). A commutative-algebra criterion (Auslander-Buchsbaum plus a dimension count) then forces R(infinity) to be a complete intersection and M(infinity) faithful, yielding R = T.

Calegari and Geraghty [2] extended this machine **beyond the Taylor-Wiles method's classical hypotheses** — in particular beyond regular weight and middle-degree cohomology of Shimura varieties. Their work proves R = T theorems for weight-one forms (where the Hecke algebra need not be torsion-free) and for ordinary representations over imaginary quadratic fields, contingent on the existence of Galois representations attached to torsion classes. This unlocked modularity lifting in *non-classical* settings and fed directly into the proof of the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in new cases.

> Theorem: **(Calegari-Geraghty)** Under suitable hypotheses on the residual representation, the natural map from the universal unramified deformation ring to the Hecke algebra acting on Katz weight-one modular forms is an isomorphism, even though the Hecke algebra is not torsion-free. This is the first R = T theorem outside the classical regular-weight, torsion-free regime [2].

```python
# Schematic model of the Taylor-Wiles numerical coincidence.
# For a set Q of Taylor-Wiles primes, the patched rings satisfy
# dim R_infty - dim S_infty == dim R - dim O, forcing freeness.

def patching_defect(num_tw_primes, local_defect, global_euler_char):
    """Return the expected defect delta = |Q| - q0 after patching."""
    # q0: minimal number of generators of the dual Selmer group
    # each Taylor-Wiles prime contributes one free diamond operator
    q0 = max(0, global_euler_char - local_defect)
    delta = num_tw_primes - q0
    return delta, delta >= 0  # nonnegativity <=> R_infty is a complete intersection

for n in (0, 1, 2, 5):
    d, ok = patching_defect(n, local_defect=2, global_euler_char=4)
    print(n, d, "patching succeeds" if ok else "need more TW primes")
```

The output of this toy model illustrates the core bookkeeping: patching succeeds exactly when enough Taylor-Wiles primes are available to absorb the global defect. In the actual proof, the freeness of M(infinity) over S(infinity) is established via the *Ihara avoidance* and *level-raising* machinery, and the complete-intersection property descends to R = T.

### 4.4 Geometric Langlands

The **geometric Langlands program** replaces number fields by the function field of a smooth projective curve X over an algebraically closed field (classically C), Galois representations by *local systems* (flat connections, or l-adic sheaves), and automorphic forms by *D-modules* on the moduli stack **Bun(G)** of principal G-bundles on X. The Beilinson-Drinfeld formulation, made precise by Arinkin and Gaitsgory, asserts a canonical equivalence of infinity-categories

- **Coh(LocSys(G-hat))** (coherent sheaves on the stack of G-hat-local systems), and
- **D(Bun(G))** (D-modules on the stack of G-bundles),

compatible with the action of **Hecke functors** on both sides. The skyscraper sheaf at a local system sigma corresponds to the **Hecke eigensheaf** with eigenvalue sigma — the geometric analogue of an automorphic representation with prescribed Satake parameters.

On the *local* side, Frenkel and Gaitsgory [4] conjectured that a local system on the punctured disc for G-hat determines a category with an action of the loop group G((t)), described via representations of the **affine Kac-Moody algebra at the critical level**. The center of the critical-level enveloping algebra is large (the Feigin-Frenkel center), and its spectrum is the space of **opers** — whence opers parametrize the local correspondence. This body of ideas has been enormously influential even in arithmetic: the *Fargues-Fontaine curve* geometrizes p-adic Hodge theory, and Fargues and Scholze constructed a candidate local Langlands correspondence for p-adic groups via sheaves on the stack Bun(G) of G-bundles on that curve, directly importing geometric-Langlands technology into the p-adic world.

### 4.5 The p-adic local Langlands correspondence

The classical local Langlands correspondence for GL(2, Q(p)) matches smooth irreducible representations with Weil-Deligne representations — but it *loses information*: the smooth representation pi(p)(rho) does not determine the p-adic Galois representation rho in general. Breuil conjectured, and Colmez proved, a finer **p-adic** correspondence [3][6]:

- To a two-dimensional p-adic representation V of G(Q(p)) one attaches a unitary admissible **Banach space representation** Pi(V) of GL(2, Q(p)), functorially and compatibly with reduction modulo p.
- Colmez's **functor V** goes the other way, from Banach representations to Galois representations, via (phi, Gamma)-modules over the Robba ring; the composition is the identity on the relevant subcategories.
- The correspondence is a **bijection** between absolutely irreducible non-ordinary Banach representations and absolutely irreducible two-dimensional Galois representations.

This correspondence satisfies **local-global compatibility**: the Banach representation Pi(rho(f)|G(Q(p))) occurs in the *completed cohomology* of the modular curve tower, as formulated by Emerton and proved in most cases. The Taylor-Wiles-Kisin patching method was adapted by Caraiani, Emerton, Gee, Geraghty, Paskunas, and Shin [CEG+16] to construct a *candidate* p-adic correspondence in general (n, F) via a patched module M(infinity) over a patched deformation ring — the "patching and the p-adic Langlands program" construction — which for GL(2, Q(p)) recovers the Breuil-Colmez correspondence.

##5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

The evidence for the Langlands program is both *computational* and *structural*:

- **Modularity of elliptic curves over Q** (Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor) is a theorem; every elliptic curve over Q is modular, and its L-function has analytic continuation and functional equation.
- **Serre's conjecture** (Khare-Wintenberger) is a theorem: every odd irreducible two-dimensional mod p representation of G(Q) is modular of the predicted weight and level. This was the key input unlocking Kisin's Fontaine-Mazur results.
- **Potential modularity** [7] has delivered the Sato-Tate conjecture for elliptic curves over totally real fields and the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in many new cases.
- **Function-field Langlands** (Drinfeld for GL(2), Lafforgue for GL(n)) is a theorem, giving complete confidence in the shape of the conjectures.
- **Geometric Langlands** has seen the proof of the categorical conjecture in important cases (e.g., for GL(n) via the work of Gaitsgory and collaborators on the spectral decomposition) and the Fargues-Scholze geometrization of the local correspondence.
- **p-adic Langlands** for GL(2, Q(p)) is a theorem with precise local-global compatibility; the patched construction extends the *formalism* to GL(n), though the correspondence there remains conjectural.
- Computationally, databases of modular forms and Galois representations (LMFDB) verify the predicted matchings of Hecke eigenvalues and Frobenius traces in millions of cases.

##6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloguing what remains out of reach:

1. **Global Langlands for GL(n), n >= 3, over number fields** is wide open; even the existence of Galois representations attached to general automorphic representations (beyond the self-dual regular algebraic case proved by Harris-Lan-Taylor-Thorne and Scholze) is conjectural.
2. **Functoriality** beyond endoscopy and a few symmetric-power cases is unproved; the general transfer from classical groups to GL(n) relies on the trace formula in ways still being worked out (Arthur's program).
3. **Geometric Langlands** in full categorical form is conjectural for general reductive G, though rapid progress continues.
4. **p-adic Langlands beyond GL(2, Q(p))** is almost entirely conjectural: no construction is known for GL(n) with n >= 3, for groups other than GL(n), or for GL(2) over extensions of Q(p) beyond the patched candidate.
5. The **Fontaine-Mazur conjecture** in dimension >= 3, and over general number fields without potential-modularity crutches, remains open; the even two-dimensional case shows the conjecture's predictions can be subtle (some residual representations admit no geometric lifts at all).
6. The methods are *non-effective*: modularity lifting proves existence of the automorphic match without producing it, and patching arguments give no algorithm for computing the correspondence.

##7 Conclusion

The Langlands program has evolved from a single reciprocity conjecture into a web of interrelated correspondences — classical, geometric, and p-adic — each illuminating the others. The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture pins down exactly which p-adic Galois representations deserve an automorphic partner; the Taylor-Wiles method, vastly generalized by Kisin, Calegari, Geraghty, and others, provides the engine that *lifts* modularity from residual representations to characteristic zero; geometric Langlands recasts the whole picture as an equivalence of categories, supplying both proofs (over function fields) and technology (the Fargues-Fontaine curve) for the arithmetic side; and the p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL(2, Q(p)) shows that the classical correspondence was only the *smooth shadow* of a richer analytic bijection.

The outstanding challenges — global functoriality, higher-rank p-adic Langlands, the full geometric conjecture — are formidable, but the trajectory is unmistakable: techniques flow freely between the three programs, and each decade converts yesterday's conjecture into today's working tool. The unity of arithmetic, analysis, and geometry that Langlands envisioned is no longer a distant dream but an active construction site.
