# Univalence and the Homotopy Interpretation of Type Theory: From Voevodsky's Axiom to Computational Cubical Models

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf's dependent type theory in *homotopy types*: types are spaces, terms are points, and identity types are path spaces [1][3]. Vladimir Voevodsky's *univalence axiom* is the linchpin — the canonical map $\mathrm{idtoeqv} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$ is an equivalence, so *equivalent structures may be identified* [1][8]. This article surveys univalence from its origins in Voevodsky's simplicial-set model, where it holds but does not compute [3][8], through higher inductive types and the landmark synthetic proof of Licata and Shulman that $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ [4], to the cubical type theories of Bezem–Coquand–Huber and Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg that render univalence *computational* [5][6]. We present the formal rules, key theorems with proof sketches, the Agda formalization of the circle as a higher inductive type, Huber's canonicity theorem [7], and the limitations of the program, including semisimplicial coherence and the status of general higher inductive types. We conclude with the open problems of univalent foundations [1].

## 1 Introduction

The correspondence between intensional Martin-Löf type theory and homotopy theory, first glimpsed by Hofmann and Streicher in the groupoid interpretation [1] and developed independently by Awodey–Warren and by Voevodsky around 2005 [3], rests on a single reinterpretation: a type $A$ is a *space*, a term $a: A$ is a *point*, and an identification $p: a =_A b$ is a *path* from $a$ to $b$ in that space [3]. The eliminator $J$ for identity types becomes path induction, and transport along a path becomes the familiar homotopy-theoretic action of paths on fibers. What type theory adds to homotopy theory is a *synthetic* language: instead of defining paths as continuous maps $I \to A$ inside set theory and then proving lemmas, one postulates the behavior of paths axiomatically and reasons with them directly [4].

The decisive innovation was Voevodsky's. Working in his model of type theory in Kan simplicial sets, he observed that the universe $\mathcal{U}$, whose points are (small) types, satisfies a property invisible to set-theoretic semantics: paths between types correspond to *equivalences* between types [8]. Elevating this to an axiom — the *univalence axiom* — yields a foundation in which *isomorphic mathematical structures can be identified*, a principle mathematicians have always wanted but which set theory cannot state [1]. The consequences are profound: function extensionality becomes provable, quotients and truncations become well-behaved higher inductive types, and entire proofs in algebraic topology can be mechanized in proof assistants such as Coq, Agda, and Lean with a fidelity impossible in ZFC [1][4].

This thesis surveys the field with attention to three watersheds:

1. **Voevodsky's univalence axiom** and its model in simplicial sets, where univalence is true but *not computational*: it is postulated as a constant with no reduction behavior [8].
2. **Synthetic homotopy theory** — the use of higher inductive types to define spaces like the circle $\mathbf{S}^1$ and spheres $\mathbf{S}^n$ *directly in the logic*, and the celebrated synthetic proof that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers [4][9].
3. **Cubical type theory**, the program initiated by Bezem, Coquand, and Huber [5] and developed by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg [6], which makes univalence a *theorem with computational content*, restoring canonicity and decidable type-checking [7].

> **Theorem:** The guiding thesis. In univalent foundations, the identity type $A =_{\mathcal{U}} B$ between types in a universe is equivalent to the type $A \simeq B$ of equivalences; synthetic homotopy theory internalizes classical results; and cubical models provide the missing computational interpretation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and identity

Dependent type theory, in Martin-Löf's formulation, is a framework in which every term has a type and types may depend on terms. The *identity type* is formed by the rule: for $A$ a type and $a, b: A$, there is a type $a =_A b$ (also written $\mathrm{Id}_A(a,b)$) with constructor $\mathrm{refl}_a: a =_A a$ and eliminator $J$ (*path induction*):

$$\frac{\Gamma, x: A, y: A, p: x =_A y \vdash C(x,y,p) \quad \Gamma, z: A \vdash d(z): C(z,z,\mathrm{refl}_z)}{\Gamma, x: A, y: A, p: x =_A y \vdash J(d, x, y, p): C(x,y,p)}$$

with computation rule $J(d, a, a, \mathrm{refl}_a) \equiv d(a)$. Intensionally, $J$ says that to prove something about all identifications it suffices to consider reflexivity — a principle that famously does *not* force uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP), as Hofmann and Streicher showed with the groupoid model [1][3]. The failure of UIP is the seed of the homotopy interpretation: if identifications need not be unique, then $a =_A b$ may contain many distinct *paths* [3].

### 2.2 The homotopy interpretation

Under the homotopy interpretation, the dictionary is [1][3]:

| Type theory | Homotopy theory |
|---|---|
| Type $A$ | Space (homotopy type) |
| Term $a: A$ | Point of $A$ |
| $p: a =_A b$ | Path from $a$ to $b$ |
| $p =_{a=_A b} q$ | Homotopy between paths |
| Dependent type $x: A \vdash B(x)$ | Fibration over $A$ |
| Transport $p_*: B(a) \to B(b)$ | Path lifting / parallel transport |
| $\Sigma$-type $\sum_{x:A} B(x)$ | Total space of fibration |
| $\Pi$-type $\prod_{x:A} B(x)$ | Space of sections |

Awodey and Warren proved that Martin-Löf type theory with identity types admits a model in any Quillen model category with suitable structure, interpreting identity types as *path objects* [3]. Voevodsky, working independently, constructed a model in the category of *Kan simplicial sets* — the classical combinatorial setting for homotopy theory — and proved it validates the rules of type theory with universes closed under the standard constructors [8]. The key move was interpreting a universe $\mathcal{U}$ as the simplicial set of *small Kan complexes*, with the universal Kan fibration classified by it.

### 2.3 Equivalences and the univalence statement

Voevodsky defined a map $f: A \to B$ to be an *equivalence* when all its homotopy fibers are contractible:

$$\mathrm{isequiv}(f) \;\equiv\; \prod_{y: B} \mathrm{isContr}\left(\sum_{x: A} f(x) =_B y\right), \qquad A \simeq B \;\equiv\; \sum_{f: A \to B} \mathrm{isequiv}(f).$$

Every identity map is an equivalence, so path induction yields a canonical map

$$\mathrm{idtoeqv}: (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \longrightarrow (A \simeq B).$$

> **Theorem:** **Univalence (Voevodsky).** For a universe $\mathcal{U}$, the map $\mathrm{idtoeqv}$ is an equivalence; i.e., $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B)$ [1][8].

Intuitively: *a path between spaces in the universe is the same as a homotopy equivalence between them*. Two equivalent types are, in the precise sense of the identity type of the universe, *equal*. This is the formal realization of the structuralist credo that mathematics is invariant under isomorphism [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *expository-synthetic*: we reconstruct the main line of development from primary sources — Voevodsky's simplicial model and the HoTT Book [1][8], the Licata–Shulman circle computation [4], Brunerie's work on the homotopy groups of spheres [9], and the cubical program [5][6][7] — verifying claims against the arXiv record and the mechanized libraries (HoTT-Coq, Agda cubical, Lean). Because HoTT is a *formal* discipline, the methodology of the field itself is proof: a theorem in this thesis is presented with its type-theoretic proof sketch, so that the reader can in principle machine-check it.

Concretely, we proceed by:

1. Stating the univalence axiom in its official form and deriving its headline corollaries — function extensionality, transport of structure, identification of isomorphic structures [1].
2. Introducing *higher inductive types* (HITs) as the synthetic definitions of spaces, with the circle as the running example, formalized in Agda-style syntax.
3. Reproducing the *encode–decode* method of Licata–Shulman for $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1)$ [4], the first genuinely synthetic homotopy-theoretic computation.
4. Explaining the cubical resolution of the computational problem: interval, faces, composition, and Glue types, with Huber's canonicity theorem as the metatheoretic payoff [5][6][7].

We cite only published or arXiv-archived sources with stable URLs, numbered and mapped inline.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Univalence, precisely stated, and its immediate corollaries

Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a universe. Voevodsky's univalence axiom asserts [1][8]:

$$\mathrm{ua}: \prod_{A, B: \mathcal{U}} \mathrm{isequiv}(\mathrm{idtoeqv}_{A,B}), \qquad \text{hence } (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B).$$

The axiom is *propositional* in the sense that its statement is a mere proposition? No — univalence for one universe implies univalence for all smaller universes, and the axiom itself has nontrivial higher structure, but any two proofs of univalence are equal: $\mathrm{isProp}(\mathrm{Univalence})$ is provable. Three corollaries follow swiftly [1]:

- **Function extensionality.** The map $\mathrm{happly}: (f = g) \to (f \sim g)$, where $f \sim g \equiv \prod_{x} f(x) = g(x)$ is pointwise homotopy, is an equivalence. Proof: univalence applied to fibers, or the standard argument via $\mathrm{idtoeqv}$ on singleton fibrations [1, §4.9].
- **Transport of structure.** Given $e: A \simeq B$ and any structure $P$ on $A$ (group structure, topology, …), there is a canonically induced structure $e_*(P)$ on $B$, *equal* to a structure obtained by transporting along $\mathrm{ua}(e): A =_{\mathcal{U}} B$. Mathematicians do this informally all the time; univalence makes it a theorem [1].
- **The universe is not a set.** The type $\mathbf{2}$ of booleans has a nontrivial automorphism $\mathrm{swap}: \mathbf{2} \simeq \mathbf{2}$, yielding by univalence a loop $p: \mathbf{2} =_{\mathcal{U}} \mathbf{2}$ distinct from $\mathrm{refl}$. Hence $\mathcal{U}$ has nontrivial path spaces — the failure of UIP at the universe is *necessary* for univalence [1].

A subtler point: univalence is *incompatible* with the principle that every type is a set (UIP globally), but it is perfectly compatible with classical logic for *propositions* (mere propositions, i.e., $(-1)$-types) [1]. This stratification by *h-level* ($n$-types: contractible, propositions, sets, groupoids, …) is Voevodsky's other great organizational idea: univalence lives naturally at all levels simultaneously [1][8].

### 4.2 Higher inductive types: the circle as a logical definition

Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point* constructors. *Higher* inductive types additionally have *path* constructors — identifications imposed between points [1][4]. The circle is the primordial example:

```agda
data S¹: Type where
  base: S¹
  loop: base ≡ base
```

The eliminator says: to define $f: \prod_{x: \mathbf{S}^1} P(x)$, it suffices to give $b: P(\mathrm{base})$ and a *dependent path* $\ell: b =^{P}_{\mathrm{loop}} b$ lying over $\mathrm{loop}$. Non-dependent recursion into $Y$ needs $y_0: Y$ and $q: y_0 =_Y y_0$. From this sparse data, synthetic homotopy theory blooms: the loop space $\Omega(\mathbf{S}^1, \mathrm{base})$ is *defined* as $\mathrm{base} =_{\mathbf{S}^1} \mathrm{base}$, and its group structure (concatenation, inverses) comes from the groupoid laws of identity types, proved once and for all by path induction [1][4].

Other HITs follow the same pattern [1][6]:

- **Spheres** $\mathbf{S}^n$: one point constructor and one $n$-dimensional path constructor (for $\mathbf{S}^2$, a 2-loop $\mathrm{surf}: \mathrm{refl}_{\mathrm{base}} = \mathrm{refl}_{\mathrm{base}}$).
- **Suspension** $\Sigma A$: north, south, and $\mathrm{merid}: A \to (\mathrm{north} = \mathrm{south})$, giving $\Sigma\mathbf{2} \simeq \mathbf{S}^1$.
- **Propositional truncation** $\|A\|$: the universal map $A \to \|A\|$ into a proposition, forcing all paths to be trivial — the type-theoretic *set of connected components* refined to a truth value [1].
- **Set truncation** $\|A\|_0$, **quotients**, **pushouts**: with univalence, quotients of sets by equivalence relations are *effective* [1].

The semantic subtlety is that HITs need *homotopy-coherent* elimination in models — handled in cubical sets by Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, who give HITs judgmental computation rules even for higher constructors [6].

### 4.3 The synthetic computation: $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$

The landmark of synthetic homotopy theory is Licata and Shulman's proof that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers [4], reproduced in the HoTT Book [1, §8.1]. The classical proof uses the covering space $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbf{S}^1$; synthetically, there are no covering *maps* — instead one defines a type family directly by circle recursion. Define $\mathrm{code}: \mathbf{S}^1 \to \mathcal{U}$ by

$$\mathrm{code}(\mathrm{base}) \equiv \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \mathrm{ap}_{\mathrm{code}}(\mathrm{loop}) = \mathrm{ua}(\mathrm{succEquiv})$$

where $\mathrm{succEquiv}: \mathbb{Z} \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ is the successor equivalence. *This is where univalence is load-bearing*: a path constructor in a type family demands a *path* in the universe, and only univalence turns the equivalence $\mathrm{succ}$ into the path $\mathrm{ua}(\mathrm{succ})$ [4]. Then:

> **Theorem:** (Licata–Shulman [4]) For all $x: \mathbf{S}^1$, there is an equivalence $\mathrm{encode}_x: (\mathrm{base} = x) \simeq \mathrm{code}(x)$, whence $\Omega(\mathbf{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$.

*Proof sketch.* Define $\mathrm{encode}: \prod_{x} (\mathrm{base} = x) \to \mathrm{code}(x)$ by path induction sending $\mathrm{refl}_{\mathrm{base}}$ to $0$, and $\mathrm{decode}: \prod_{x} \mathrm{code}(x) \to (\mathrm{base} = x)$ by circle induction, sending $n: \mathbb{Z} = \mathrm{code}(\mathrm{base})$ to $\mathrm{loop}^n$ and checking the loop case by a path-over argument using the fact that transporting along $\mathrm{ua}(\mathrm{succ})$ computes as successor. Both composites are identities by induction. Truncating gives $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1) \equiv \|\Omega(\mathbf{S}^1)\|_0 \simeq \mathbb{Z}$. ∎

The method — *encode–decode* — generalizes: Brunerie used it with the Hopf fibration $\mathbf{S}^1 \to \mathbf{S}^3 \to \mathbf{S}^2$ (defined via univalence from the H-space structure on $\mathbf{S}^1$) to compute $\pi_2(\mathbf{S}^2) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_3(\mathbf{S}^2) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$, and the higher groups up to the famous $\pi_4(\mathbf{S}^3) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ [9]. These are machine-checked in the HoTT-Coq and Agda libraries [5].

### 4.4 Cubical type theory: univalence made computational

The original univalence axiom is a *postulate*: $\mathrm{ua}(e): A =_{\mathcal{U}} B$ is a constant with no computation rule, so a closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ using univalence need not reduce to a numeral — *canonicity fails* [5][8]. The cubical program repairs this. Bezem, Coquand, and Huber built the first constructive model of univalence in *cubical sets* [5]; Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg then internalized it as a *type theory* in which univalence is provable [6].

The ingredients of cubical type theory (CTT) [6]:

- **The interval** $\mathbb{I}$, a De Morgan algebra with endpoints $0, 1$; paths are functions $\mathbb{I} \to A$, written $\mathrm{Path}_A(a, b)$.
- **Composition** ($\mathrm{comp}$): filling of open boxes — the constructive content of Kan fibrancy, giving transport and path algebra *definitionally well-behaved*.
- **Glue types** $\mathrm{Glue}\,[\varphi \mapsto (T, e)]\, A$: the cubical analogue of "attach $T$ to $A$ along the equivalence $e$ on the face $\varphi$". Univalence is then a *theorem*: given $e: A \simeq B$, the path $\mathrm{ua}(e): \mathrm{Path}_{\mathcal{U}}(A, B)$ is $\lambda i.\, \mathrm{Glue}\,[ (i = 0) \mapsto (A, e),\, (i = 1) \mapsto (B, \mathrm{idEquiv}) ]\, B$, and transporting along it *computes* — on $i = 0$ it is $e$ itself [6].

Consequences are immediate and dramatic: function extensionality holds *definitionally* in the sense of having a direct proof term with computational behavior; HITs like the circle get *judgmental* computation rules for all constructors including the higher ones [6]; and the theory is implementable — Cubical Agda is a full-scale proof assistant based on it.

### 4.5 Mechanization: Agda, Coq, Lean

The univalent foundations program has always been *implemented* mathematics [1]. The HoTT-Coq library formalizes the book's core; UniMath (Voevodsky's own library) develops univalent category theory; Cubical Agda provides native computational univalence; and Lean's mathlib-adjacent HoTT experiments explore the two-level and simplicial variants. The feedback loop is essential: Brunerie's computation of $\pi_4(\mathbf{S}^3)$ was *conjectured* by formalizing the proof and extracting the integer $n$ such that the Whitehead product $[\iota_2, \iota_2] = n \cdot \eta$ — the machine computed $n = \pm 2$ from the proof term, a genuine mathematical discovery mediated by proof assistants [9][1].

```haskell
-- Conceptual Haskell sketch of the univalence transport principle
-- (Haskell lacks dependent paths; this is analogy, not implementation)
transport:: (a ≃ b) -> P a -> P b   -- univalence: equivalences act on all structure
transport e = subst (ua e)            -- via the path ua(e): a ＝ b in the universe
```

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

"Empirical" in this field means *machine-checked*. The evidence for the claims above is not experimental data but formalization artifacts:

1. **The simplicial model (Voevodsky).** Kapulkin, Lumsdaine, and Voevodsky proved that the Kan simplicial set model validates type theory with univalent universes; the univalence theorem for simplicial sets is Theorem 9 of Bezem–Coquand–Huber in the cubical analogue [5], and Voevodsky's original notes establish it simplicially [8]. Consistency of univalence relative to ZFC with inaccessible cardinals follows.
2. **$\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1)$.** Formalized in Coq (HoTT library, `Pi1S1.v`), in Agda (Licata–Brunerie `Pi1S1.agda`), and presented in full detail in the HoTT Book [1, §8.1]; the proof type-checks and computes: `decode (encode loop)` reduces to `loop` [4].
3. **Canonicity for cubical type theory (Huber).** Huber proved that every closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ in CTT is judgmentally equal to a numeral, via a sconing / computability argument over the cubical-sets model [7]. This is the metatheoretic certificate that univalence in CTT is *computational*: no stuck constants.
4. **HITs in cubical type theory.** Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg gave syntax and semantics for spheres, torus, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts with judgmental computation rules for higher constructors and universes closed under them [6].
5. **Brunerie's thesis.** The computation of $\pi_n(\mathbf{S}^n)$ and $\pi_4(\mathbf{S}^3) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2$ is fully formalized in Agda; the *number* 2 was produced by normalizing a proof term — arguably the first theorem whose *numerical content* was discovered by a proof assistant's evaluator [9].

> **Theorem:** **Canonicity (Huber [7]).** In cubical type theory, if $\vdash t: \mathbb{N}$ then $t \equiv \mathrm{succ}^n(0)$ for a unique $n$, effectively computable. *Corollary:* CTT is consistent, and univalence computes.

A comparative summary of the three generations:

| Aspect | Book HoTT + UA axiom [1] | Simplicial model [8] | Cubical type theory [6] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Univalence status | Postulated constant | True in model | Provable theorem |
| Canonicity | Fails | N/A (model) | Holds [7] |
| Function extensionality | Provable, stuck | True | Provable, computes |
| HIT computation rules | Propositional | Coherent | Judgmental [6] |
| Proof assistants | HoTT-Coq, UniMath | — | Cubical Agda |

## 6 Limitations

Univalent foundations are not without serious open problems and genuine limitations:

1. **Semisimplicial types and infinite coherence.** There is no known internal definition of *semisimplicial types* (or complete semi-Segal types) in HoTT — the long-standing open problem of infinite coherence. Without it, internal $(\infty,1)$-category theory remains out of reach in Book HoTT, though two-level type theories and simplicial HoTT (Riehl–Shulman) attack it externally [1].
2. **HITs lack a general schema.** Book HoTT postulates HITs one at a time with propositional computation rules; a fully general, well-behaved schema for *all* higher inductive-inductive types with judgmental rules exists only in cubical settings [6], and even there, exotic HITs (localizations, higher truncations of universes) need care.
3. **Constructivity gaps in Book HoTT.** With univalence as an axiom, canonicity fails and transport along $\mathrm{ua}(e)$ is stuck; only cubical type theory restores computation [5][6][7]. The "Book" theory is thus a *specification*, CTT its *implementation*.
4. **Classical mathematics overhead.** Doing ordinary set-level mathematics (analysis, algebra) in HoTT requires disciplined use of sets ($0$-types) and propositions; while the HoTT Book develops substantial set-level mathematics [1, Ch. 10], the library ecosystem remains far smaller than set-theoretic counterparts.
5. **Performance of proof assistants.** Cubical Agda's interval and composition machinery, while theoretically clean, can be slow in practice; large synthetic-homotopy formalizations push the limits of normalization-by-evaluation [7].
6. **Univalence vs. choice.** The axiom of choice, in its type-theoretic form, is *inconsistent* with univalence in full generality (it forces all types to be sets); only restricted forms (choice for sets, i.e., for $0$-types) are consistent [1]. This is a feature for homotopy theorists but a limitation for classical mathematicians.

## 7 Conclusion

Voevodsky's univalence axiom transformed type theory from a programming-logic curiosity into a candidate *foundation of mathematics with intrinsic homotopical content* [1][8]. The trajectory surveyed here — from the path-space reading of identity types [3], through the univalence axiom and its simplicial model [8], to higher inductive types and the synthetic computation $\pi_1(\mathbf{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ [4][9], and finally to cubical type theory where univalence computes [5][6][7] — represents one of the most successful interactions between pure mathematics and computer science in recent decades. The open problems (semisimplicial coherence, general HIT schemas, scalable mechanization) are not signs of failure but the research frontier of a young field. Univalent foundations have already changed what a *proof* can be: a program that computes, a path that transports, a machine-checked argument that a space has the homotopy type it claims. The next decade will determine whether they can also change what *mathematics* is.
