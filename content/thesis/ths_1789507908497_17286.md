# Regularity Structures for Singular Stochastic PDEs: Rough Paths, Hairer's Theory of Modelled Distributions, and Renormalization

## Abstract

The classical theory of partial differential equations breaks down when the driving noise is so singular that nonlinear operations on the solution become meaningless: products of distributions, the KPZ nonlinearity, or the cubic term of the dynamical $\Phi^4$ model cannot be formed in the usual distribution sense. This article develops the modern pathwise solution theory for such *singular* stochastic PDEs, tracing a line from Lyons' theory of rough paths [6] through Hairer's 2013 solution of the KPZ equation [2] to the full framework of regularity structures [1] and its renormalization group [3]. We present the algebraic skeleton (index sets, graded spaces, structure groups, decorated trees), the analytic core (models, modelled distributions, the reconstruction theorem, Schauder estimates), and the probabilistic heart (the BHZ algebraic renormalization and the Chandra–Hairer analytic BPHZ theorem [4]) that together yield local well-posedness for a large class of subcritical semilinear equations, with convergent renormalized models arising as limits of regularized equations with diverging counterterms.

## 1 Intro

A stochastic partial differential equation is called *singular* when the driving noise is so irregular that the equation, as written, is ill-posed: some nonlinear operation it contains cannot be performed on the objects that classical solution theory produces. The archetype is the dynamical $\Phi^4_3$ model

$$\partial_t u = \Delta u - u^3 + \xi, \qquad (t,x) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{T}^3,$$

with $\xi$ space-time white noise. Counting parabolic regularity, $\xi$ lives in $\mathcal{C}^{-5/2-\kappa}$ for every $\kappa > 0$, the heat kernel raises regularity by $2$, and the mild formulation immediately asks for the cube of an object of regularity $-1/2-\kappa$ — a product that distribution theory does not define [1]. The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation

$$\partial_t h = \partial_x^2 h + (\partial_x h)^2 + \xi$$

in one space dimension is even more borderline: the solution $h$ is Hölder continuous, yet $(\partial_x h)^2$ is meaningless as a product of distributions [2].

Three generations of pathwise machinery now address this. *Rough path theory*, introduced by Terry Lyons in 1998 [6], enriches a low-regularity signal with its iterated integrals (its *signature*) and solves differential equations driven by it deterministically, with continuity of the solution map in the enriched metric. Hairer applied the same philosophy to KPZ in 2013 [2], factorizing the Cole–Hopf solution map through an auxiliary Polish space. Regularity structures [1] generalized this to space-time problems with genuinely *distributional* drivers, replacing the classical polynomial model by purpose-built models and Taylor jets by *modelled distributions*. Finally, the algebraic renormalization of Bruned, Hairer and Zambotti [3], with the analytic convergence theorem of Chandra and Hairer [4], made the theory a black box for subcritical semilinear equations.

This article is a self-contained exposition of that arc: the algebra, the analysis, and the renormalization.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Itô to pathwise: rough paths

Consider the rough differential equation $dy_t = V(y_t)\, dx_t$, where $x$ is merely $\alpha$-Hölder with $\alpha \le 1/2$, for instance a Brownian sample path. Young integration fails, and Itô integration sacrifices pathwise dependence on $x$. Lyons' insight was that the missing information is not the path itself but its *enhancement*: a multiplicative functional $\mathbf{X}_{s,t}$ taking values in a truncated tensor algebra, whose levels encode the iterated integrals

$$\mathbf{X}_{s,t} = \left(1,\; X_{s,t},\; \int_s^t X_{s,r}\otimes dX_r,\; \ldots\right),$$

satisfying the *Chen relation* $\mathbf{X}_{s,t} = \mathbf{X}_{s,u}\otimes \mathbf{X}_{u,t}$ and $\alpha$-Hölder-type bounds in the homogeneous $p$-variation metric with $p = 1/\alpha$ [6]. The collection of all levels is the *signature* of the signal. Differential equations are then formulated as fixed points in the enriched structure; Gubinelli's *controlled paths* extracted the essence: an integrand $y$ is *controlled* by $x$ if its increments are linear functions of $x$'s increments plus a smoother remainder, and a Riemann-type integral is defined by a sewing argument [5].

> **Theorem (Lyons' extension and continuity):** A $p$-rough path defined on levels $1$ through $\lfloor p \rfloor$ extends uniquely to all higher levels with consistent Hölder bounds, and the Itô–Lyons solution map $\mathbf{X} \mapsto y$ from rough paths to solutions of rough differential equations is continuous (indeed locally Lipschitz) in the $p$-variation metric [6].

A crucial lesson, already visible for KPZ, is that approximations of a rough path may need *renormalization*: Hairer's 2013 KPZ work exhibits a non-Gaussian rough path whose area process requires subtracting a diverging term before the approximations converge [2]. This was the germ of the full renormalization group.

### 2.2 Why classical distribution theory fails

For space-time white noise $\xi$ on $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ with parabolic scaling $\mathfrak{s} = (2,1,\ldots,1)$, we have $\xi \in \mathcal{C}^{-|\mathfrak{s}|/2-\kappa}_{\mathfrak{s}}$ where $|\mathfrak{s}| = d+2$. In $d = 3$ this gives regularity $-5/2-\kappa$. The Schauder estimate for convolution with the heat kernel gains exactly $2$ derivatives, so the stochastic convolution $K * \xi$ sits at $-1/2-\kappa$: a genuine distribution, not a function, and its cube is undefined. For KPZ in $d = 1$, $\xi \in \mathcal{C}^{-3/2-\kappa}$, $K * \xi \in \mathcal{C}^{1/2-\kappa}$, and $\partial_x(K*\xi)$ has regularity $-1/2-\kappa$; squaring it is equally impossible [2]. The obstruction is always the same: multiplication $ \mathcal{C}^\alpha \times \mathcal{C}^\beta \to \mathcal{C}^{\alpha\wedge\beta}$ is only continuous when $\alpha + \beta > 0$ (the classical Young condition), and singular SPDEs systematically violate it.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Regularity structures: the algebraic skeleton

A *regularity structure* is a triple $\mathscr{T} = (A, T, G)$ [1]:

- $A \subset \mathbb{R}$ is an index set containing $0$, bounded below, with no accumulation point.
- $T = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in A} T_\alpha$ is a graded vector space; $\tau \in T_\alpha$ has *homogeneity* $|\tau| = \alpha$.
- $G$ is a group of linear operators on $T$ such that, for $\tau \in T_\alpha$,
  $$\Gamma \tau - \tau \in T_{<\alpha} := \bigoplus_{\beta < \alpha} T_\beta, \qquad \Gamma \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1},$$
  where $\mathbf{1}$ spans $T_0$. The group implements *recentering*: changing the base point of a local expansion.

The prototype is the *polynomial* regularity structure, where $T_\alpha$ is spanned by monomials $X^k$ with parabolic degree $|k|_{\mathfrak{s}} = \alpha$ and $\Gamma_h X^k = (X + h)^k$. The revolutionary step in [1] is to *replace* polynomials by symbols purpose-built for the equation: a symbol $\Xi$ for the noise (of degree $-5/2-\kappa$ for $\Phi^4_3$), an abstract integration map $\mathcal{I}$ raising degree by $2$, and products of these, all subject to a *rule* guaranteeing that only finitely many symbols sit below any homogeneity. The space $T$ is then spanned by *decorated trees*: for $\Phi^4_3$,

$$\Xi,\quad \mathcal{I}(\Xi),\quad \mathcal{I}(\Xi)^2,\quad \mathcal{I}(\Xi)^3,\quad \mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(\Xi)^3),\;\ldots$$

with the homogeneity $|\mathcal{I}(\tau)| = |\tau| + 2$ and $|\tau \bar\tau| = |\tau| + |\bar\tau|$.

A *model* $Z = (\Pi, \Gamma)$ consists of maps $\Pi_z : T \to \mathcal{S}'$ and $\Gamma_{zz'} \in G$ satisfying the algebraic identities $\Pi_z \Gamma_{zz'} = \Pi_{z'}$, $\Gamma_{zz'}\Gamma_{z'z''} = \Gamma_{zz''}$, and analytic bounds of the form $|\langle \Pi_z \tau, \varphi_z^\lambda \rangle| \lesssim \lambda^{|\tau|}$ for rescaled test functions $\varphi_z^\lambda$, plus $\|\Gamma_{zz'} \tau\|_\beta \lesssim |z-z'|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha-\beta}$ for $\beta < \alpha = |\tau|$ [1].

### 3.2 Modelled distributions and the reconstruction theorem

A *modelled distribution* of order $\gamma$ is a map $f : \mathbb{R}^{d+1} \to T_{<\gamma}$ satisfying

$$\|f(z) - \Gamma_{zz'} f(z')\|_\alpha \lesssim |z - z'|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma - \alpha} \qquad \forall\, \alpha < \gamma,$$

the exact analogue of a Hölder bound for a Taylor jet, but in the abstract space $T$. The space is denoted $\mathcal{D}^\gamma$. The crown jewel of the theory is:

> **Theorem (Reconstruction, [1]):** For $\gamma > 0$ there exists a unique continuous linear map $\mathcal{R} : \mathcal{D}^\gamma \to \mathcal{S}'$ such that $|\langle \mathcal{R}f - \Pi_z f(z), \varphi_z^\lambda \rangle| \lesssim \lambda^\gamma$ uniformly in $z$ and $\lambda \in (0,1]$. If the model is *smooth*, $\mathcal{R}f(z) = (\Pi_z f(z))(z)$.

Reconstruction turns abstract jets back into concrete distributions; for $\gamma > 0$ the jet determines its limit uniquely, resolving the paradox of multiplying distributions by working at the level of the expansion. The calculus is completed by *multi-level Schauder estimates*: an abstract integration operator $\mathcal{K}$ on modelled distributions lifts convolution with singular kernels, gaining two degrees of regularity, and there are canonical products $\mathcal{D}^{\gamma_1} \times \mathcal{D}^{\gamma_2} \to \mathcal{D}^{\gamma_1+\gamma_2}$ whenever the homogeneities allow [1]. A semilinear equation $\partial_t u = \Delta u + F(u) + \xi$ is then lifted to a fixed-point problem

$$\mathbf{u} = \mathcal{K}(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u})) + \mathbf{P} u_0 \quad \text{in } \mathcal{D}^\gamma,$$

with $\mathbf{P}$ the abstract lift of the initial data, and $\mathcal{R}\mathbf{u}$ solves the original equation [7].

### 3.3 The renormalization group and BHZ

The models built canonically from mollified noise $\xi_\varepsilon$ *diverge* as $\varepsilon \to 0$: for instance $\mathbb{E}[(\Pi_z^\varepsilon \mathcal{I}(\Xi)^2)(z)] \sim \varepsilon^{-1}$. Renormalization acts on the model by a group element $M \in \mathfrak{R}$, the *renormalization group*, sending $(\Pi, \Gamma) \mapsto (\Pi M, \Gamma)$ in a way that subtracts the divergences while preserving admissibility [1]. In the original treatment this group was constructed indirectly. The breakthrough of Bruned, Hairer and Zambotti [3] was to enlarge the regularity structure so that renormalization becomes *canonical*: decorated trees carry a Hopf-algebraic structure — two Hopf algebras in cointeraction, one for *negative* renormalization (extracting divergent subtrees) and one for *positive* renormalization (recentering) — with the *twisted antipode* producing the BPHZ character, an algebraic Birkhoff factorization exactly as in perturbative quantum field theory [3]. The renormalized model is then $\Pi_z^M = \Pi_z \circ M$ for a character $M$ chosen as the *BHZ character*, the unique centered, translation-invariant character [7].

### 3.4 Convergence: the analytic BPHZ theorem

Algebraic renormalization is empty without convergence of the renormalized models. Chandra and Hairer proved a *black-box* analytic BPHZ theorem [4]: for stationary noises with sufficiently many moments (Gaussian included), the BPHZ-renormalized models built from $\xi_\varepsilon$ converge in probability in the model metric as $\varepsilon \to 0$. The proof uses multi-scale analysis in the spirit of constructive field theory, with new difficulties from *positive* renormalizations caused by recentering [4]. Combined with the continuity of the solution map in the model, this yields: solutions $u_\varepsilon$ of the regularized equations with diverging counterterms converge to a limit $u$ — the renormalized solution — independently of the mollifier up to finite renormalization [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The tree calculus for $\Phi^4_3$

For $\Phi^4_3$, assign $|\Xi| = -5/2-\kappa$ and generate symbols by the rule: polynomials $X^k$, the noise $\Xi$, products, and $\mathcal{I}(\tau)$ for non-polynomial $\tau$ with $|\tau| + 2 > 0$. The subcriticality of $\Phi^4_3$ manifests in the finiteness of negative-homogeneity trees:

| Symbol | Tree picture | Homogeneity |
|---|---|---|
| $\Xi$ | single noise node | $-5/2-\kappa$ |
| $\mathcal{I}(\Xi)$ | edge from noise node | $-1/2-\kappa$ |
| $\mathcal{I}(\Xi)^2$ | two edges, one root | $-1-2\kappa$ |
| $\mathcal{I}(\Xi)^3$ | three edges, one root | $-3/2-3\kappa$ |
| $\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(\Xi)^3)$ | planted cubic tree | $1/2-3\kappa$ |
| $\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(\Xi)^2)\,\mathcal{I}(\Xi)$ | mixed tree | $-2\kappa$ |

Only the first four trees have negative homogeneity and need stochastic construction; all others are built deterministically from the model via the analytic machinery [1]. The divergent constants are precisely the expectations of the negative trees: with mollification at scale $\varepsilon$,

$$C_\varepsilon^{(1)} = \mathbb{E}[(K * \xi_\varepsilon)^2(z)] \sim \varepsilon^{-1}, \qquad C_\varepsilon^{(2)} = \mathbb{E}[(K * \xi_\varepsilon)^3\text{-contractions}] \sim \log \varepsilon^{-1},$$

and the renormalized equation reads $\partial_t u_\varepsilon = \Delta u_\varepsilon - (u_\varepsilon^3 - 3C_\varepsilon^{(1)} u_\varepsilon) + \xi_\varepsilon$, with the logarithmic counterterm absorbed into the mass [1]. This is *Wick renormalization* in the language of trees: $M$ subtracts exactly the divergent subtree contractions.

A short program enumerates the negative-homogeneity trees and their scaling:

```python
from itertools import product

kappa = 0.01
def hom(symbol: str) -> float:
    # I raises by 2, products add, Xi = -5/2 - kappa, Xk = parabolic degree
    if symbol == "Xi":
        return -2.5 - kappa
    if symbol.startswith("I("):
        return hom(symbol[2:-1]) + 2.0
    if "*" in symbol:
        return sum(hom(s) for s in symbol.split("*"))
    raise ValueError(symbol)

trees = ["Xi", "I(Xi)", "I(Xi)*I(Xi)", "I(Xi)*I(Xi)*I(Xi)",
         "I(I(Xi)*I(Xi)*I(Xi))", "I(I(Xi)*I(Xi))*I(Xi)"]
for t in trees:
    h = hom(t)
    print(f"{t:28s}  |tau| = {h:+.3f}   {'NEGATIVE -> needs renormalization' if h < 0 else 'built deterministically'}")
```

The tree basis is naturally a functional data structure; in Haskell the decorated trees of [3] become:

```haskell
-- Decorated trees for the BHZ Hopf algebra (simplified)
data Decor = Noise | Poly [Int] | IntOp          -- node decorations
data Tree  = Node Decor [Tree]                   -- rooted tree
           | Graft Tree Tree                     -- forest product
           | One

homogeneity :: Tree -> Double
homogeneity One            = 0
homogeneity (Node Noise _) = -2.5 - kappa
homogeneity (Node IntOp [t]) = homogeneity t + 2.0
homogeneity (Graft t1 t2)  = homogeneity t1 + homogeneity t2
homogeneity _              = 0

-- negative renormalisation: extract divergent subforests (BHZ coproduct sketch)
extractNegative :: Tree -> [(Tree, Tree)]
extractNegative t = [(s, contract t s) | s <- subforests t, homogeneity s < 0]
```

### 4.2 KPZ: from Cole–Hopf to rough paths to regularity structures

Hairer's 2013 solution of KPZ [2] predates regularity structures and is the conceptual bridge from rough paths. The Cole–Hopf transform $Z = e^h$ converts KPZ into the *stochastic heat equation* $\partial_t Z = \partial_x^2 Z + Z\xi$, which is classically well-posed by Itô theory; but Cole–Hopf is a one-trick device that teaches nothing about the equation's own structure. Hairer instead constructed a *pathwise* solution: a factorization of the solution map as

$$\xi \;\xrightarrow{\text{measurable lift}}\; \mathbf{\Xi} \in \mathcal{X} \;\xrightarrow{\text{continuous}}\; h,$$

where $\mathcal{X}$ is an explicitly described Polish space of *enhanced noise* (a rough-path-like object containing the stochastic convolution, its Wick powers, and the notorious "area" term), and the second map is continuous [2]. The construction completely bypasses Cole–Hopf, coincides with the Hopf–Cole solution, and yields new regularity results plus differentiability in the initial condition. One striking byproduct was a *non-Gaussian* rough path whose area process requires a diverging renormalization for its approximations to converge — the first appearance of renormalization inside a rough-path object [2].

In regularity-structure language, KPZ becomes a routine fixed point: the structure contains $\Xi$ of degree $-3/2-\kappa$, $\mathcal{I}(\Xi)$ of degree $1/2-\kappa$, and the nonlinearity $(\partial_x h)^2$ lifts to a product in $\mathcal{D}^\gamma$ because the abstract derivative gains nothing but the product is formed *before* reconstruction. The renormalized approximating equations are

$$\partial_t h_\varepsilon = \partial_x^2 h_\varepsilon + (\partial_x h_\varepsilon)^2 + \xi_\varepsilon - C_\varepsilon, \qquad C_\varepsilon = \frac{c}{\varepsilon} + O(1),$$

converging to a one-parameter family of limits parametrized by the $O(1)$ part of $C_\varepsilon$, independent of the mollifier [7].

### 4.3 The BHZ Hopf algebra in cointeraction

The algebraic core of [3] deserves a closer look. Let $H^-$ be the Hopf algebra spanned by forests of decorated trees with only negative-homogeneity components, and $H^+$ the Hopf algebra governing recentering (positive renormalization). They *coact* on the space $T$ of trees: $\Delta^- : T \to H^- \otimes T$ extracts divergent subforests, and $\Delta^+ : T \to T \otimes H^+$ recenters at a new base point. A character $\ell$ of $H^-$ defines the renormalization map $M_\ell = (\ell \otimes \mathrm{id})\Delta^-$, and the *BPHZ character* is obtained by the twisted antipode $\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_-$, a variant of the Connes–Kreimer Birkhoff factorization [3]:

> **Theorem (BHZ, [3]):** There exists a unique character $\ell_{\mathrm{BPHZ}}$ of $H^-$, the BPHZ character, such that the renormalized model $(\Pi^{M}, \Gamma)$ is centered and translation invariant in law. The map $\ell \mapsto M_\ell$ identifies a finite-dimensional renormalization group acting continuously on admissible models.

This turns renormalization from an analytic art into algebra: once the rule generating the trees is fixed, the counterterms are *computed*, not guessed. The same machinery handles systems, non-Gaussian noises, and the generalized KPZ equation [7].

### 4.4 Schauder estimates and the abstract fixed point

The analytic engine that makes fixed points possible is the multi-level Schauder estimate [1]: if $K$ is a singular kernel of order $\beta$ (the heat kernel has $\beta = 2$), there is an operator $\mathcal{K} : \mathcal{D}^\gamma \to \mathcal{D}^{\gamma+\beta}$ lifting convolution, i.e. $\mathcal{R}\mathcal{K} = K * \mathcal{R}$, plus a compatible lift $\mathcal{K}'$ handling the polynomial part. Composition with smooth functions is also lifted: for $F$ smooth, there is $\mathbf{F} : \mathcal{D}^\gamma \to \mathcal{D}^\gamma$ with $\mathcal{R}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}) = F(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{u})$ whenever the reconstruction is a function [1]. Together with the product theorem, every *subcritical* semilinear equation — one for which the rule produces finitely many negative-homogeneity trees — admits a local solution theory that is continuous in the model. Subcriticality is the precise form of "the equation is not too singular": the scaling dimension of the nonlinearity must be strictly better than that of the noise term [7].

### 4.5 Paracontrolled distributions: the parallel universe

Simultaneously with [1], Gubinelli, Imkeller and Perkowski developed *paracontrolled distributions* [5], a first-order, Fourier-analytic alternative avoiding new algebraic foundations. Writing the product $fg = f \prec g + f \circ g + f \succ g$ via Bony's paraproduct, a solution is postulated to be *paracontrolled* by the enhanced noise: $u = u' \prec X + u^\sharp$ with $u^\sharp$ smoother. The resonant term $f \circ g$ is the only obstruction and is supplied as enhanced data — the exact analogue of the rough-path lift. Paracontrolled calculus recovered KPZ, $\Phi^4_3$, PAM and Burgers-type equations [5], and even yields a proof of the reconstruction theorem via paraproducts. It is lighter but inherently first-order; regularity structures handle arbitrary subcritical nonlinearities and higher-order expansions systematically.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

The theory's "empirical" content is its approximation theorem. Fix a mollifier $\rho$ and $\xi_\varepsilon = \rho_\varepsilon * \xi$. Build the canonical model $Z_\varepsilon$ from $\xi_\varepsilon$, apply the BHZ character to get the renormalized model $Z_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{BPHZ}}$, solve the abstract fixed point $\mathbf{u}_\varepsilon \in \mathcal{D}^\gamma$, and set $u_\varepsilon = \mathcal{R}\mathbf{u}_\varepsilon$. Then [4, 7]:

1. **Convergence of models:** $Z_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{BPHZ}} \to Z$ in probability in the model metric.
2. **Continuity of solutions:** $\mathbf{u}_\varepsilon \to \mathbf{u}$ in $\mathcal{D}^\gamma$, hence $u_\varepsilon \to u = \mathcal{R}\mathbf{u}$ in $\mathcal{C}^\alpha_{\mathfrak{s}}$.
3. **Identification:** $u_\varepsilon$ is the classical solution of the regularized equation with BHZ counterterms; e.g. for $\Phi^4_3$,
   $$\partial_t u_\varepsilon = \Delta u_\varepsilon - u_\varepsilon^3 + 3C_\varepsilon^{(1)} u_\varepsilon - C_\varepsilon^{(2)} + \xi_\varepsilon.$$
4. **Universality:** the limit $u$ depends on the mollifier only through finitely many constants (finite renormalization).

Concrete instances verified in the literature include: the dynamical $\Phi^4_3$ model and its invariant measure (the original target of [1], conjecturally the Glauber dynamics of 3D ferromagnets at criticality); KPZ with convergence to the Hopf–Cole solution [2]; the parabolic Anderson model and Burgers-type equations [5]; and the generalized KPZ equation as the black-box test case of [4, 7]. Hairer's work on regularity structures was recognized with the Fields Medal in 2014.

## 6 Limitations

- **Subcriticality is essential.** The rule must generate finitely many negative-homogeneity trees; supercritical equations (e.g. $\Phi^4_d$ for $d \ge 4$, KPZ in $d \ge 2$) are out of reach — the expansion would need infinitely many stochastic objects. Quasilinear singular SPDEs require the separate framework of Otto, Sauer, Smith and Weber, which avoids trees entirely [7].
- **Probabilistic input assumptions.** The analytic BPHZ theorem needs control of cumulants (or a spectral gap inequality, as in the Linares–Otto–Tempelmayr–Tsatsoulis approach) of the noise [4]; highly non-stationary or heavy-tailed drivers are not covered.
- **Local in time.** The fixed point is a contraction only on short intervals (or small data); global well-posedness needs extra structure, e.g. the "coming down from infinity" estimates for $\Phi^4_3$.
- **Combinatorial complexity.** Realistic systems generate thousands of trees; the BHZ Hopf algebra is explicit but computationally heavy, and hand-checking the rule's subcriticality is nontrivial.
- **Continuity only in the model.** Solutions are continuous in the enhanced noise, *not* in the raw noise — renormalization is unavoidable, and the physical meaning of finite renormalizations must be supplied externally.

## 7 Conclusion

Regularity structures completed a thirty-year arc from Lyons' rough paths [6] to a general solution theory for singular SPDEs. The division of labor is clean: *algebra* (decorated trees, Hopf algebras in cointeraction, the BPHZ character [3]) computes the renormalization; *analysis* (models, modelled distributions, reconstruction, Schauder estimates [1]) runs the fixed point; *probability* (the analytic BPHZ theorem [4]) supplies the convergent renormalized models. The payoff is a black box: given a subcritical semilinear equation, the theory manufactures its renormalized solution as the limit of regularized equations with diverging counterterms, recovering KPZ [2], $\Phi^4_3$, PAM and Burgers as special cases. Open frontiers remain the supercritical regime, quasilinear equations, and the long-time behavior of the constructed dynamics — but the pathwise philosophy that a singular equation is solved by *enhancing the noise, not taming it*, is now firmly established.
