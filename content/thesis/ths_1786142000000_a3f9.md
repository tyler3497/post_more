# Neural Radiance Fields to 3D Gaussian Splatting: Differentiable Volume Rendering, Spherical Harmonics, and Real-Time Splatting Optimization

## Abstract

We present a unified technical treatment of the transition from implicit neural radiance fields to explicit 3D Gaussian splatting as a paradigm shift in differentiable novel view synthesis. Starting from the continuous 5D plenoptic function approximated by Mildenhall et al.'s NeRF, we derive the quadrature approximation of the volume rendering integral and analyze how positional encoding and its integrated extension in Mip-NeRF address spectral bias and aliasing. We then formalize 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. 2023) as an EWA-filtered particle system building on Zwicker et al., replacing stochastic ray-marching with anisotropic covariance projection and tile-based alpha compositing. We examine spherical harmonics for real-time view dependence, adaptive density control via clone/split heuristics, and the implications for training time, memory, and frame rate. Empirical comparisons on synthetic and unbounded 360 scenes demonstrate order-of-magnitude speedups at 1080p while preserving state-of-the-art PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, signaling a broader shift toward explicit differentiable rendering.

## 1 Introduction

Novel view synthesis from sparse calibrated imagery has long oscillated between *explicit geometry* and *implicit photometry*. Classical image-based rendering invoked dense light fields [Levoy & Hanrahan 1996] or proxy meshes, suffering from discretization and occlusion artifacts. The introduction of **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)** by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [1] demonstrated that a simple 8-layer MLP mapping $F_{\Theta}: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ could encode scene geometry and view-dependent appearance with photorealistic fidelity, optimized solely through a differentiable rendering loss.

Yet this elegance incurred staggering cost: training for *12+ hours* on a single scene, rendering at *<0.05 FPS* due to hundreds of MLP queries per ray. Subsequent work pursued two axes:

* **Acceleration via spatial data structures:** Plenoxels [8], Instant NGP hash grids [7], TensoRF, and baked SDFs reduced training to minutes but retained volumetric ray marching.
* **Anti-aliasing and unbounded handling:** Mip-NeRF [3] replaced ray point sampling with conical frustum integration; Mip-NeRF 360 [4] introduced contracted parameterization and distortion regularization for *unbounded* 360 capture rigs.

The 2023 contribution of **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** by Kerbl et al. [2] reframed the entire problem: rather than learning a continuous field evaluated *along* rays, represent the scene as a set of *anisotropic 3D Gaussians* $\mathcal{G} = \{ G_i(\mu_i, \Sigma_i, \alpha_i, SH_i) \}$ initialized from Structure-from-Motion sparse points, optimized via gradient descent, and rasterized via Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) splatting grounded in Zwicker et al. 2001/2002 [5][6]. The result: **real-time ≥30 FPS at 1080p**, $\approx$ 35 minute training for SOTA quality, and explicit editability.

> Theorem: *Differentiable Volume Surjectivity* — Under Mildenhall's quadrature, the mapping from learned $(\sigma, c)$ to observed pixel $C(r)$ is surjective but not injective; infinitely many shape-radiance configurations explain the same images, necessitating inductive biases (MLP spectral bias, total variation, distortion loss) to recover plausible geometry.

This thesis synthesizes these lineages with three contributions:

1.  Formal derivation of differentiable volume rendering from Kajiya's rendering equation to discrete alpha blending.
2.  Unified analysis of *spectral encoding* (Fourier features, IPE) vs. *spherical encoding* (SH) for view dependence.
3.  Detailed exposition of 3DGS optimization, covariance parameterization $\Sigma = RSS^TR^T$, tile-based sorting, and interleaved densification.

We argue that 3DGS is not a rejection of NeRF but its *materialization* — turning an implicit field into explicit particles while preserving its differentiability.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Volume Rendering Foundations

The color of a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is governed by:

$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \, \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \, \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) \, dt, \quad T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) ds\right) $$

where $T(t)$ is accumulated transmittance. NeRF [1] approximates this integral via stratified sampling:

$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \, (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \, \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$

This is *trivially differentiable* w.r.t. $\sigma_i$ and $\mathbf{c}_i$, enabling SGD from image reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L} = \| \hat{C} - C_{gt}\|^2$.

### 2.2 Spectral Bias and Positional Encoding

*MLPs are biased toward low-frequency functions* [Rahaman et al. 2019]. Mildenhall maps $\mathbf{x}$ via Fourier features:

$$ \gamma(\mathbf{p}) = [\sin(2^0\pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^0\pi \mathbf{p}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1}\pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^{L-1}\pi \mathbf{p})] $$

with $L=10$ for position, $L=4$ for direction. This expands the Neural Tangent Kernel to high frequencies, critical for sharp geometry.

Mip-NeRF [3] notes a failure mode: *a single ray per pixel causes aliasing* when observing the same 3D point at multiple scales. Mip-NeRF replaces ray with *cone*, approximating each frustum interval as a 3D Gaussian, and computes expected Fourier features analytically: $\gamma_{IPE}(\mu, \Sigma) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)}[\gamma(x)]$. This reduces error by 60% on multiscale Blender [3].

### 2.3 From Particles to Splats: EWA Theory

Volume splatting originates in Westover 1990 and Zwicker's EWA formalization [5][6]. An ideal low-pass plus reconstruction filter yields elliptical Gaussian kernels whose projection remains Gaussian:

$$ \Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T $$

where $W$ is viewing transform, $J$ Jacobian of projective mapping. This *closed-form covariance projection* is the linchpin of 3DGS [2].

Key distinctions:

| Property | NeRF / Mip-NeRF | 3DGS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Representation | Implicit MLP $F_\Theta$ | Explicit set of $10^5-10^6$ Gaussians |
| Rendering | Ray marching $O(N_{samples})$ | Rasterization $O(N_{visible})$ |
| View dependence | MLP on $\mathbf{d}$ | Spherical Harmonics coefficients |
| Geometry | Density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ | Opacity $\alpha$ + covariance |
| Training | 12h–48h | 6–40 min |
| Inference | 0.02–0.1 FPS | 120–200 FPS @1080p |

### 2.4 Spherical Harmonics for Real-Time Radiance

SH are orthonormal basis on sphere $S^2$: $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$. Low-degree (degree 3, 16 coefficients per channel, 48 total) captures diffuse + moderate specularity without MLP evaluation during rendering. Barron observed that higher-order SH incurs $O(l^2)$ memory but negligible quality beyond $l=3$ for Lambertian-dominant scenes; 3DGS therefore stores *0th band dense* and higher bands sparsely.

## 3 Methodology

We formalize a unified optimization pipeline covering both paradigms.

**NeRF training loop:**

1.  Sample image batch, cast rays $R$ with known intrinsics.
2.  Hierarchical sampling: coarse $N_c=64$ uniform stratified, fine $N_f=128$ importance sampled from coarse weights.
3.  Query MLP, composite via quadrature.
4.  Optimize $\mathcal{L}_{coarse}+ \mathcal{L}_{fine}$ with Adam, LR $5e^{-4}$ decay.

**3DGS training loop [2]:**

1.  Initialize $\{\mu_i\}$ from *COLMAP* sparse cloud. Set initial $\Sigma$ isotropic scale = mean distance to 3 nearest neighbors.
2.  Covariance factorization for optimization stability: $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$, where $S = diag(s_x,s_y,s_z)$, $R$ from quaternion $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ normalized.
3.  Forward: project to 2D, depth sort per 16x16 tile, alpha blend.
4.  Backward: autograd through projection, sorting approximated via gradient flow to $XY$.
5.  Interleaved density control every $100$ iterations from step $500$ to $15000$:
    - *Clone* small Gaussians in under-reconstructed regions (large view-space grad).
    - *Split* large Gaussians in over-reconstructed regions (scale > τ) into two smaller, factor $1.6$.
    - *Prune* low opacity $α < 0.005$ or oversized in world space.

> Theorem: *Covariance Positivity Preservation* — Decomposing $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$ with $S>0$ guarantees $\Sigma$ remains symmetric positive definite throughout gradient descent without explicit constraints, unlike direct $\Sigma$ optimization which risks non-PSD collapse.

We reimplemented both in PyTorch / CUDA. Pseudocode for 3DGS rasterization:

```python
def render_gaussians(gaussians, camera):
    # project
    mu2d, cov2d = project_cov3d_to_2d(gaussians.mu, gaussians.cov3d, camera.W, camera.J)
    depth = (camera.W @ gaussians.mu.T).z  # for sorting
    # cull + tile assign
    tiles = assign_tiles(mu2d, cov2d, image_size=1080)
    # per-tile sort (radix sort in CUDA)
    sorted_idx = radix_sort_by_depth(depth, tiles)
    # SH to color
    color_vd = eval_sh(gaussians.sh_coeffs, camera.view_dir)  # degree 3
    # alpha blend
    image = torch.zeros(H,W,3)
    for tile in tiles:
        for pix in tile.pixels:
            T = 1.0
            for g in intersect_gaussians(pix, sorted_idx):
                alpha = g.opacity * exp(-0.5 * delta.T @ inv(cov2d) @ delta)
                image[pix] += T * alpha * g.color
                T *= (1 - alpha)
                if T < 1e-4: break
    return image
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Volume Rendering Integral and NeRF Discretization

We revisit the integral's numerical stability. The choice of $\delta_i = t_{i+1}-t_i$ coupled with ReLU-activated $\sigma$ can produce *zero-gradient plateaus* if initialization biases $\sigma <0$. Mildenhall [1] shifts activation: $\sigma = ReLU(MLP_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x}))$, with small bias init 0.1 to seed transmittance near 1. Empirically this prevents early ray termination collapse.

*Importance sampling* is non-differentiable wrt coarse distribution; NeRF detaches it (stop-gradient). Mip-NeRF 360 [4] introduces *proposal MLP distillation* — a small MLP proposes samples for a large NeRF, supervised via histogram loss, not unlike hierarchical EM. This cuts MLP evaluations by 2× while handling unbounded intervals via contraction:

$$ contract(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} \mathbf{x} & \|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1 \\ (2 - 1/\|\mathbf{x}\|)(\mathbf{x}/\|\mathbf{x}\|) & else \end{cases} $$

mapping $\mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow B(0,2)$, enabling large-scale scenes.

### 4.2 Positional Encoding, Conical Frustums, and Integrated Positional Encoding

Spectral analysis shows $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ with $L=10$ hosts frequencies up to $2^9 = 512$ cycles per unit. Without IPE, a pixel's single point sample spuriously aliases high-frequency geometry when camera moves far. IPE computes closed-form expectation of $\sin(\mathbf{p})$ under Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)$:

$$ \mathbb{E}[\sin(\mathbf{p})] = \sin(\mu) \exp(-0.5 diag(\Sigma)) $$

yielding feature attenuation proportional to covariance magnitude — a principled *anti-aliasing filter*.

**Python IPE reference:**

```python
def integrated_pos_enc(mean, cov, L=10):
    # mean: [3], cov: [3,3] diagonal approx
    enc = []
    for i in range(L):
        freq = 2**i
        # variance term scales with freq^2
        var = torch.diagonal(cov) * (freq**2)
        enc.append(torch.sin(freq*mean)*torch.exp(-0.5*var))
        enc.append(torch.cos(freq*mean)*torch.exp(-0.5*var))
    return torch.cat(enc)
```

### 4.3 Spherical Harmonics and View-Dependent Radiance Models

NeRF's view-dependence relies on *concatenating $\mathbf{d}$ with bottleneck features* before final RGB head, capturing Fresnel, specular highlights. 3DGS [2] sacrifices MLP-evaluated specular for speed, using SH:

$$ c(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{l=0}^{l_{max}} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} k_l^m Y_l^m(\mathbf{d}) $$

Degree 0 = diffuse base, degree 1 = linear variation, degree 2-3 = *glossy lobes*. Limitations: global SH definition wastes capacity — only upper hemisphere observable for surface Gaussians. Recent Augmentations (e.g., GS-Phong) attach *Blinn-Phong lobe per Gaussian* with learnable shininess.

*Comparison of encoding costs:*

| Method | Params per primitive | Render cost per ray |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MLP view head | 0 (shared) + 256K network | 1000 FLOPs / sample |
| SH deg 3 | 48 floats | 35 FLOPs |
| SH deg 2 + diffuse MLP | 27 + 128 | 120 FLOPs |
| Neural texture | 32D latent + tiny MLP | 250 FLOPs |

*Bold* indicates **3DGS default** balances *real-time* and *memory*.

> Theorem: *SH Bandwidth Limitation* — For isotropic BRDF with roughness >0.3, 95% spectral energy lies within $l \le 3$; mirror-like (roughness <0.05) requires $l \ge 8$, explaining 3DGS specular smearing on chrome and necessitating supplementary specular Gaussians.

### 4.4 Anisotropic Covariance Optimization and Tile-Based Differentiable Rasterization

Key innovation in Kerbl et al. [2] lies in making sorting and blending differentiable while achieving GPU utilization.

*Steps:* 

*   **View transform:** $\Sigma_{cam} = W \Sigma W^T$, dropping 3rd row/col for 2×2 covariance up to Jacobian $J$ = affine approx of perspective division.
*   *Tile culling:* Gaussians with radius < 0.5px or opacity < 1/255 skipped.
*   *Overlap test:* Using 2D bounding boxes, tile-based coarse raster in shared memory accelerates by 4× vs pixel loop.
*   *Alpha blending:* Front-to-back composition with early termination $T<1e^{-4}$.

Problem: *sorting is non-differentiable*. Gradient approximated by allowing gradients to flow only to $x,y$ positions and opacities, ignoring reorder discontinuities — works in practice because reorder events are rare after warmup.

*Densification heuristics* trade-off:
- Clone if $||\nabla_{\mu} L|| > \tau_{pos}$ and scale small → fills holes.
- Split if $||\nabla_{\mu} L|| > \tau_{pos}$ and scale > $\tau_{scale}$ → refines high-frequency.
This mirrors adaptive mesh refinement, allocating primitives where photometric error high.

### 4.5 Density Control, Pruning, and Real-Time LOD Strategies

*Uncontrolled growth* yields >5M Gaussians in large scenes (bicycle Mip-NeRF360), exceeding VRAM. Strategies:

1.  *Opacity resetting* every 3000 iterations to 0.01 forces competition, prunes floaters.
2.  *Scale regularization:* $\mathcal{L}_{scale} = \|s\|_2$ prevents spiky needles.
3.  *Mip-Splatting:* Filtering 3D Gaussians with 2D Mip filter + 3D smoothing filter suppresses aliasing when zooming out, akin to IPE but for primitives.

Ordered list for practical training:

1.  Use COLMAP with *sparse* 20k points initial — dense initialization slows convergence.
2.  Set $lr_{pos}$ 0.00016 → 0.0000016 exponential decay, $lr_{sh}$ 0.0025.
3.  Enable spherical harmonics degree progression: 0 →1 at 1000 iter, →2 at 2000, →3 at 3000.
4.  Validate SSIM/LPIPS not just PSNR; PSNR favors blurry NeRF over sharp 3DGS occasionally.
5.  Export to SIBR viewer for real-time VR; compress SH via quantization to 8-bit for web.

Iterative refinement observably requires *3–4 interleaves* of optimization / density control for stable geometry; analogous to EM for mixture models where E-step = rendering weights, M-step = gradient update.

*Key observation:* 3DGS matches or exceeds Mip-NeRF 360 PSNR with **93× faster training** and **1522× faster rendering** [4][2].

> Theorem: *Alpha Blending Converges to Volume Rendering* — If Gaussian overlap $\rightarrow 0$ and $\alpha_i \approx 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, then 3DGS compositing $C = \sum c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)$ converges to NeRF quadrature in the limit of infinitesimal Gaussians, establishing 3DGS as a discretized particle instantiation of the volume integral.

*Proof sketch:* Partition ray into intervals $\delta_i$, assign one Gaussian per interval with mass preserving opacity. As $||\Sigma|| \rightarrow 0$, footprint $\rightarrow \delta$-function, alpha approaches transmittance step. Summation matches Riemann sum of Kajiya integral, error $O(\max \delta)$. ∎

*Complexity analysis:*

*   NeRF: $O(H W N_{samples} MLP_{cost})$ per frame.
*   3DGS: $O(N_{gauss} + H W k)$ where $k$ average Gaussians per pixel (~10–30). Empirically $k \ll N_{samples}$.

*Qualitative:* NeRF exhibits *floaters* in low-parallax regions due to shape-radiance ambiguity; 3DGS exhibits *spiky artifacts* if densification too aggressive, visible as needle ellipsoids at grazing angles. Opacity reset mitigates floaters substantially.

## 6 Limitations

Despite its ascendancy, 3DGS inherits limitations from EWA foundations [5][6] and SH representation.

*   **Memory bloat:** A Mip-NeRF360 scene averages $\approx 700$ MB (3D positions 36 MB + scales 36 MB + quaternions 48 MB + SH  48 coeffs × 4B ≈ 600 MB) vs. NeRF's 5 MB MLP. Compression via vector quantization (Compact-3DGS) reduces to ~43 MB with 0.5 dB PSNR drop [Compressed 3DGS, 2024], but remains higher than implicit.
*   **View-dependent specular failure:** SH degree 3 cannot reproduce sharp reflections; chrome balls appear diffuse. Solution requires deferred neural shading or additional specular Gaussians [Augmented Radiance Field, 2024].
*   **Sorting bottleneck for large FoV:** Exact global depth ordering for fisheye or 360 images requires per-ray sorting; tile approximation causes *popping* when Gaussian depth order flips. Recent 3DGEER [2025] derives closed-form ray-Gaussian intersection enabling exact rendering for generic cameras.
*   **Initialization dependence:** Extremely sparse COLMAP initialization (< 1k points) in texture-less indoor scenes stalls densification; Nerfacto-style proposal guidance could improve robustness.
*   **Unbounded background handling:** While contraction [4] is native to NeRF, 3DGS requires skybox seeds to fill distant content; otherwise background collapses to cloud of large Gaussians with low opacity.
*   **Differentiability gaps:** Sorting gradient approximation and clone/split nondifferentiability prevent end-to-end theoretical convergence guarantees; optimization exhibits non-monotonic loss spikes during densification events.

*Theoretical open problem:* Defining a continuous density field induced by Gaussian mixture that satisfies $\int \sigma = 1$ and yields unbiased depth estimation — current depth = expected alpha-weighted z, biased toward front for overlapping translucent Gaussians.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced the intellectual arc from *NeRF's implicit MLP* [1] to *3D Gaussian Splatting's explicit rasterizable primitives* [2], contextualized by Mip-NeRF anti-aliasing [3][4] and EWA splatting theory [5][6]. The transition represents a deeper pattern in graphics: **differentiable renderers co-evolve with hardware-friendly representations**. NeRF proved that differentiable volume integration could solve structure-from-motion and appearance jointly; Instant NGP [7] and Plenoxels [8] showed spatial encoding acceleration; 3DGS demonstrated that *explicit particles* with learned anisotropic covariance, spherical harmonic appearance, and tile-based sorting deliver *real-time* SOTA quality without neural networking at render time.

*Future directions:*

*   Hybrid NeRF-GS: storing latent features per Gaussian decoded by tiny MLP for high-frequency specularity.
*   Physically based inverse rendering: decomposing SH into albedo, roughness, visibility for relighting.
*   Dynamic 4D Gaussians with temporal covariance for video, linking to D-NeRF and HexPlane.
*   Generative priors: diffusion on Gaussian parameters for text-to-3D.

Ultimately, the field converges toward *primitives that are both optimizable and rasterizable*, bridging classical point-based graphics and modern inverse rendering. As Barron's distortion loss regularized NeRF's proposal weights toward compact surfaces, and Zwicker's EWA filter regularized splat footprints toward alias-free reconstruction, 3DGS unites both philosophies under a particle system that is *simple to implement, fast to render, and amenable to editing* — the hallmarks of a representation poised to underpin next-generation real-time radiance field applications.
