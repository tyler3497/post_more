# Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems Composition: PLONK Permutation Arguments, Halo2 Lookup Tables, Nova Recursive Folding, and Accumulation Schemes for IVC

## Abstract
This thesis presents a compositional analysis of modern zero-knowledge proof systems, unifying PLONK's permutation-based arithmetization, Halo2's extended PLONKish framework with Plookup-style tables, and Nova's folding-based Incrementally Verifiable Computation. We formalize PLONK's universal updatable SRS, its Lagrange-basis copy constraints via grand product arguments, and custom gate extension to UltraPLONK. We then analyze Halo2's redesign that replaces KZG trusted setup with IPA-based commitment over Pasta cycles, enabling recursion without pairings, and its generalized subset lookup that eliminates permutation index bookkeeping. Finally we deconstruct Nova's relaxed R1CS folding, showing how two NP instances fold into one with O(1) verifier and two MSM prover cost, enabling IVC and PCD without succinct verification at each step. CycleFold and CCS/HyperNova generalizations are examined as accumulation schemes for program execution on EVM and RISC-V with a-la-carte cost profiles. Empirical and proof analysis demonstrates concrete prover/verifier tradeoffs and points to unified architectures for transparent, recursion-friendly SNARKs.

## 1 Introduction

Zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (zk-SNARKs) have transitioned from per-circuit trusted setups to *universal* and *transparent* systems that support recursion as a first-class primitive. Three paradigm shifts define this evolution:

1. **PLONK** [1] introduced a permutation argument over Lagrange bases that decouples circuit description from structured reference string (SRS), yielding a universal updatable SRS and fully succinct verification with ~400 bytes proofs.
2. **Halo2** [3][4] generalized PLONK into PLONKish arithmetization with *fixed*, *advice*, and *instance* columns, integrated **Plookup**-style subset arguments [2] for efficient range checks and bitwise operations, and removed pairing-based KZG in favor of IPA commitments over the Pasta cycle (Pallas/Vesta), achieving recursion without trusted setup [3].
3. **Nova** [5][6] reframed recursion itself: instead of verifying a SNARK inside another SNARK (expensive pairings or IPA verifier circuits), it introduced a *folding scheme* that reduces checking two NP instances to checking one, with prover dominated by two multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs) and verifier dominated by two group scalar multiplications.

The composition of these ideas — PLONK permutation, Halo2 lookups, Nova folding, and accumulation schemes for Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC) — constitutes the modern stack for zkVMs, zkRollups, and proof-carrying data (PCD).

> **Theorem 1 (Informal Composition):** A universal SNARK with custom gates and lookup tables over a cycle of curves, when combined with a folding scheme for relaxed R1CS/CCS, yields IVC where per-step prover cost is *a-la-carte* proportional to the executed instruction and recursive overhead is O(log n) hashes + O(k) scalars.

We contribute:

- A unified formalization of PLONK's grand product permutation and its extension to UltraPLONK lookup
- A reconstruction of Halo2's lookup as a generalized subset argument without explicit permutation indexing, and its security over IPA
- A step-by-step derivation of Nova's relaxed R1CS folding, error term `T`, and folding verifier circuit
- CycleFold [6] and HyperNova/CCS [7][8] as generalizations to customizable constraint systems
- Empirical cost models and proof sketches for knowledge soundness of composition

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Groth16 to Universal SNARKs

Groth16 [1] achieves 3 group elements and optimal verification but requires a per-circuit trusted setup. **Sonic** (Maller et al.) introduced universal SRS via permutation arguments based on Bayer-Groth [9]. PLONK simplified Sonic by focusing on *evaluations on a subgroup* rather than coefficients of monomials, reducing prover exponentiations by 7.5–20× in fully succinct mode [1].

Key primitives:

- **Lagrange basis** over multiplicative subgroup `H = {1, ω, ω^2, ..., ω^{n-1}}` with vanishing polynomial `Z_H(X)=X^n-1`
- **KZG commitment** [10]: `com(f)=[f(τ)]_1` with SRS `[τ^i]_1, [τ^i]_2`
- **Fiat-Shamir** compilation of 5-round protocol

### 2.2 PLONKish Arithmetization

Standard PLONK represents circuits via fan-in-2 fan-out-1 constraints:

```
q_L·a + q_R·b + q_O·c + q_M·a·b + q_C = 0
```

Selectors `q_*` are fixed polynomials encoding gate type. Wires `a,b,c` are advice polynomials. Copy constraints — that wire values appearing in multiple gates are equal — are enforced not via equality constraints but via a **permutation argument** over `H`.

### 2.3 Lookup Arguments

Plookup (Gabizon-Williamson 2020) [2] introduced efficient subset checks: prove every element of `f` (sorted advice) appears in table `t` (fixed). Halo2 generalizes to arbitrary-width relations, multiple columns combined via random challenge `β`, and tag columns for multiple tables [4].

### 2.4 IVC, PCD, and Accumulation

IVC (Valiant, Bitansky et al.) defines `(Gen, P, V)` where `P` proves `z_i = F(z_{i-1})` incrementally. PCD generalizes to DAGs of computations by mutually distrusting parties. **Accumulation schemes** (Bünz et al., Halo) defer expensive checks to final verifier. Nova's folding is an accumulation scheme where accumulation *is* folding and decider is final SNARK (Spartan-based) [5].

| System | SRS | Commitment | Recursion | Gate Model | Lookup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Groth16 | per-circuit trusted | KZG | No (requires cycle + pairing verifier) | R1CS | No |
| PLONK [1] | universal updatable | KZG | via cycle | custom gates | via Plookup extension [2] |
| Halo2 [3][4] | transparent (IPA) | IPA/Bulletproofs | native (no pairing) | PLONKish (fixed/advice/instance) | generalized subset |
| Nova [5] | transparent (cycle) | Pedersen MSM | folding (2 MSM) | relaxed R1CS | Origami extension [11] |
| HyperNova [7] | transparent | MSM | CCS folding (1 MSM) | CCS | CCS-encoded |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Formalizing PLONK Permutation Argument

We reconstruct PLONK per [1] with explicit zero-knowledge blinding. Let `n=2^k`. Public input `PI(X)` encodes public wires.

Define wire polynomials `a(X), b(X), c(X)` interpolated over `H` from witness. Define permutation `σ: H→H` mapping each wire occurrence to next occurrence in same copy-cycle. Let `S_{σ1}, S_{σ2}, S_{σ3}` be Lagrange-encoded permutation polynomials where `S_{IDi}(g)=ω^{i-1}g`.

The permutation check is encoded via grand product polynomial `Z(X)`:

```
Z(1)=1
Z(g·ω) = Z(g)· (a(g)+β·g+γ)(b(g)+β·k1·g+γ)(c(g)+β·k2·g+γ) / 
               (a(g)+β·Sσ1(g)+γ)(b(g)+β·Sσ2(g)+γ)(c(g)+β·Sσ3(g)+γ)
```

for `g∈H`, challenges `β,γ∈F`. Constraint polynomial `t(X)` aggregates gate + permutation identities divided by `Z_H`:

```
t = (qM·a·b+qL·a+qR·b+qO·c+PI+qC)/ZH
  + α·(... permutation product - ...)/ZH
  + α^2·L1·(Z-1)/ZH
```

Zero-knowledge: last `k=2` evaluations of `Z` are random, last rows not checked via factor `(X-h_{n-k})...` [1].

### 3.2 Halo2 PLONKish Extension and Lookup Subset Argument

Halo2's circuit is a rectangular grid: `n=2^k` rows, columns categorized [4]:

- **fixed**: selectors `q`, table columns `S`
- **advice**: witness `A`
- **instance**: public inputs

Equality enforced via permutation argument similar to PLONK but over arbitrary columns. Lookup for arbitrary width relation `R`: given compressed tuple via random `θ`:

```
θ-combine: Aθ = Σ θ^i·A_i , Sθ = Σ θ^i·S_i
```

Prove set `{Aθ(g)} ⊂ {Sθ(g)}` as multiset inclusion. Protocol [4]:

1. Permute `Aθ` to `A'θ` sorted by `Sθ` value, grouped same values contiguously
2. Build product column `Z` for subset argument: `Z(ω^0)=1`, `Z(gω)=Z(g)·(1+β)(θ-comb)` style product
3. Low-degree gates: degree ≤5 with challenge `β` to batch constraints

Generalizations: multiple tables via tag column, arbitrary polynomial expressions for input columns using relative references (saving advice columns), Pedersen homomorphism for cheap challenge combination.

### 3.3 Nova Folding for Relaxed R1CS

Relaxed R1CS instance: `(E, u, W, x)` where `W` witness, `x` public IO, `E` error vector, `u∈F` scalar, satisfying:

```
A·Z ∘ B·Z = u·C·Z + E   where Z=(W,x,1)
```

Standard R1CS is `u=1, E=0`. Folding two instances `U1=(E1,u1,W1,x1)`, `U2=(E2,u2,W2,x2)` with verifier randomness `r`:

```
W = W1 + r·W2
u = u1 + r·u2
E = E1 + r·T + r^2·E2
x = x1 + r·x2
```

Cross-term `T = A·Z1 ∘ B·Z2 + A·Z2 ∘ B·Z1 - u1·C·Z2 - u2·C·Z1`

Prover commits to `T` as `com_T`. Verifier computes `r = Hash(U1,U2,T)`. Folding verifier circuit is *constant* — only 2 group scalar multiplications to check commitment homomorphism [5]. This enables IVC: each step folds new instance `u_i` proving `z_i=F(z_{i-1})` into running `U`.

IVC construction:

```
U0 = trivial (E=0,u=1)
For i=1..n:
  prove U_i = F's R1CS for step i with witness W_i
  fold: U_{i+1} = Fold(U_i_folded, U_i; r_i)
Final decider: SNARK prove U_n is satisfiable (Spartan-based)
```

Zero-knowledge for free: randomize fold with blinding factors in `W`, no need for ZK SNARK at each step — folding randomization suffices [7].

### 3.4 Accumulation and CycleFold

When instantiating Nova over a cycle (Pallas/Vesta, BN254/Grumpkin), verifier's field operations must be expressed in other curve's scalar field. CycleFold [6] provides generic technique to handle mismatched field arithmetic efficiently: prove cycle curve operations inside circuit using non-native field emulation optimized via `E` decomposition and custom gates, maintaining `O(log n)` hash domination.

CCS (Customizable Constraint System) [8] generalizes R1CS/PLONKish/AIR:

```
Σ c_i · Π M_j·Z =0
```

HyperNova folding cost: *single* MSM of size `|variables|` — optimal for MSM commitment — and supports folding multiple instances at once, enabling non-uniform PCD (KiloNova [9]) where different circuits folded together.

## 4 Deep Dive: Composition Principles

### 4.1 Why Permutation Arguments Enable Universal SRS

*Traditional* R1CS equality is structural — wire index reused. PLONK decouples by labeling each gate occurrence as distinct evaluation point in `H` and enforcing equality via permutation product. Since `σ` is public circuit description encoded as fixed polynomials `Sσ`, SRS need not know `σ` — same `[τ^i]` works for any `n ≤ Nmax`. Updatability via powers-of-tau MPC [12] ensures if ≥1 contributor honest, trapdoor `τ` unknown.

> **Theorem 2 (PLONK Permutation Soundness):** If polynomial commitment is binding and `β,γ` random, then `Z` satisfying permutation identities implies `a,b,c` respect copy constraints defined by `σ` except with probability `≤ O(n/|F|)`.

*Proof sketch:* Follows Schwartz-Zippel for product argument; product equality across cosets enforces multiset equality of `(value + β·shift + γ)` tuples; if copy cycle broken, polynomials differ and product ratio ≠1 with high probability [1].

### 4.2 Halo2 Lookup Without Explicit Permutation Indexes

Unlike Plookup requiring same-size sorted sets and explicit index mapping, Halo2 lookup argument uses *subset* reasoning: inclusion proven via product over compressed values, no need to specify index for permutation. This yields:

- **Universality**: table size `|S|` may differ from `|A|`, filler with dummy or repeated entries
- **Efficiency**: degree ≤5 vs. PlonKup degree 6-7; Pedersen combination cheap
- **Flexibility**: multi-column lookups (`a,b` pair must be in table `(s0,s1)`) via `θ` compression, function property implicitly checks domain membership

Rust-like sketch for Halo2 lookup prover:

```rust
struct Halo2LookupProver {
    table: Vec<F>,          // fixed S
    advice: Vec<F>,         // A
    theta: F,               // random
    beta: F,
    gamma: F,
}

impl Halo2LookupProver {
    fn permute_pair(&self) -> (Vec<F>, Vec<F>) {
        // A' = sorted A, S' = permuted S aligning with A' runs
        let mut pairs: Vec<(F,F)> = self.advice.iter().zip(self.table.iter()).map(|(a,s)|( *a,*s)).collect();
        pairs.sort_by(|a,b| a.0.cmp(&b.0));
        let (a_prime, s_prime): (Vec<_>, Vec<_>) = pairs.into_iter().unzip();
        (a_prime, s_prime)
    }
    fn build_product(&self, a_prime: &[F], s_prime: &[F]) -> Vec<F> {
        let mut z = vec![F::one(); a_prime.len()];
        for i in 1..a_prime.len() {
            let lhs = F::one() + self.beta * (self.theta + a_prime[i-1]);
            let rhs = F::one() + self.beta * (self.theta + s_prime[i-1]);
            z[i] = z[i-1] * lhs / rhs;
        }
        z
    }
}
```

Python equivalent for PLONK grand product:

```python
import random

def build_Z(a,b,c, sigma, beta, gamma, omega, k1, k2, n):
    Z = [1]*n
    for i in range(1,n):
        g = omega**i
        num = (a[i-1]+beta*g+gamma)*(b[i-1]+beta*k1*g+gamma)*(c[i-1]+beta*k2*g+gamma)
        den = (a[i-1]+beta*sigma[0][i-1]+gamma)*(b[i-1]+beta*sigma[1][i-1]+gamma)*(c[i-1]+beta*sigma[2][i-1]+gamma)
        Z[i]=Z[i-1]*num/den
    return Z
```

### 4.3 Nova Folding as Deferral of Verification

Prior IVC (Halo, Zexe) verified pairing or IPA at each step — large verifier circuit (~10k constraints for pairing). Nova *defers* verification: folding does not check instances, only combines them linearly; final decider checks once. This is analogous to batch verification but with *knowledge soundness* preserved via forking lemma: if prover folds false instance, final folded instance unsatisfiable except with negligible probability over `r`.

Haskell-style type for folding:

```haskell
type RelaxedR1CS = (Commit E, F, Witness, X)
type FoldProof = (Commit T, Challenge r)

fold :: RelaxedR1CS -> RelaxedR1CS -> FoldProof -> RelaxedR1CS
fold (E1,u1,W1,x1) (E2,u2,W2,x2) (commit_T, r) =
  let W = W1 + r *^ W2
      u = u1 + r * u2
      x = x1 + r *^ x2
      e = E1 + r *^ T + (r^2) *^ E2
  in (commit e, u, W, x)
  where T = crossTerm (W1,x1) (W2,x2)

ivc :: (F -> F) -> Int -> Proof
ivc step n = foldl foldStep trivial [1..n]
  where foldStep acc i = fold acc (proveStep step i) (hashChallenge acc)
```

TLA+ invariant for folding accumulator:

```tla
---- MODULE NovaFold ----
VARIABLES U_folded, i, z_i
TypeOK == U_folded \in RelaxedInstances /\ i \in Nat

FoldStep ==
  /\ i < N
  /\ \E W_i, E_i, T:
       /\ IsValidStep z_i z_{i+1} W_i
       /\ T = CrossTerm(U_folded, (E_i,1,W_i,z_i))
       /\ U_folded' = Fold(U_folded, (E_i,1,W_i,z_i), RandomChallenge())
  /\ i' = i+1 /\ z_i' = F(z_i)

Safety == \A k \in 1..i: ExistsValidWitness k

Spec == TypeOK /\ [][FoldStep]_<<U_folded,i,z_i>>
====
```

### 4.4 Accumulation Schemes for PCD and Custom Gates

HyperNova [7] improves Nova's 2 MSM to 1 MSM by generalizing to CCS. CCS supports custom gates directly: selector polynomial becomes coefficient `c_i` in CCS sum. Folding custom gate with verifier input (e.g., lookup) requires handling extra verifier randomness rounds — addressed by Origami [11], first folding scheme for lookups, using verifier input for table.

CycleFold technique [6]: to prove Pallas operations inside Vesta circuit efficiently, decompose scalar multiplication into `GLV` + `WNAF`, use incomplete addition formulas optimized for circuit constraints, and batch range checks via lookup. This yields recursive overhead dominated by `O(log n)` Poseidon hashes and `O(k)` scalar multiplications, not pairing emulation.

Empirical: proving EVM step (SSTORE) vs. ADD has vastly different size; HyperNova *a-la-carte* cost means prover cost ∝ size of invoked instruction circuit, not union of all instructions — critical for zkVMs like Jolt where instruction set >50 [10].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Cost Model and Benchmarks

We instantiate models based on reported numbers [1][5][7] and community benchmarks (halo2 PSE, Nova Microsoft).

| Metric | PLONK (KZG) | Halo2 (IPA, Pallas) | Nova (Pallas/Vesta, relaxed R1CS) | HyperNova (CCS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proof size | ~400 bytes (3 G1 + 2 G2) | ~1-2 KB (IPA opening) | IVC proof ~ 2 G + fold proof 1 G + hash (compressed ~ 1-2 KB final Spartan) | ~ 1 MSM commitment |
| Prover | O(n log n) FFT, ~7-12s for 2^20 gates (GPU) | O(n log n) + MSM, ~ 20s for 2^20 | 2 MSM per fold (~0.5-1s for 2^20 R1CS) | 1 MSM per fold (optimal) |
| Verifier | 2 pairings + O(log n) | O(log n) IPA + hashes | 2 scalar muls + hash (constant) | 1 scalar mul |
| SRS / Setup | universal updatable, ~100MB for 2^20 | transparent (no SRS) | transparent (cycle, Pedersen) | transparent |
| Lookup overhead | +1-2 polynomials, degree +1 | +1 product column, degree 5 | Origami extra: 2 columns + cross | CCS encoded, no extra |

*Interpretation*: PLONK verification is pairing-heavy but fastest to verify on-chain (Ethereum precompiles). Halo2 verification logarithmic but no pairings — better for recursion. Nova folding verification is *tiny*, enabling 10k+ IVC steps with negligible recursive overhead.

### 5.2 Knowledge Soundness Sketch for Composition

**Lemma (PLONK):** Under dlog and q-SDH for KZG, PLONK is knowledge-sound in AGM [1].

**Lemma (Halo2 lookup):** Subset argument is sound if `β,θ` random and Pedersen binding; product equality enforces multiset inclusion except probability ≤ `n/|F|` [4].

**Lemma (Nova folding):** If relaxed R1CS instances `U1,U2` unsatisfiable, then folded `U` unsatisfiable with probability ≥ `1 - O(m/|F|)` over random `r`, where `m` is degree of error polynomial (2). This follows from Schwartz-Zippel on quadratic in `r`: `E(r)=0` has ≤2 roots [5].

**Theorem 3 (IVC Knowledge Soundness):** Composition of PLONKish circuit proving `F`, Halo2 lookup for range checks inside `F`, and Nova folding for `n` steps yields IVC with knowledge error `negl(λ)` if all constituent lemmas hold and hash modeled as RO.

*Proof sketch:* By induction on `i`. Assume `U_folded_i` accumulates `i` valid steps. Folding `i+1` with random `r` preserves validity if new step valid; if new step invalid, folded instance invalid except ≤2/|F| probability. Forking lemma extractor rewinds to obtain two different `r`, solving for witnesses of both `U1,U2`. Halo2 component inside each step uses same extraction as PLONK. Final decider SNARK extracts final witness. Union bound over `n = poly(λ)` maintains negligible error.

### 5.3 Real-World Deployment: Zcash, Scroll, Polygon

- **Zcash Orchard** (2021) deployed Halo2 with custom gates for Sinsemilla hash and lookup for 8-bit range checks, achieving ~8× speed-up over Groth16 setup ceremony [3].
- **Scroll zkEVM** uses PLONKish Ultra with 8 lookup tables for keccak, EVM memory, enabling keccak 8k gates vs 28k standard PLONK [13].
- **Nova-based zkVM** (Microsoft Nova + Jolt): folding EVM/RISC-V steps where each step is CCS, CycleFold handles Pallas/Vesta cycle, final Spartan compressed proof verified on-chain [5][6][10].

## 6 Limitations

**1. Trusted setup vs transparency tradeoff:** PLONK KZG gives fastest on-chain verification (2 pairings) but requires universal SRS ceremony (perpetual powers-of-tau). Halo2 IPA transparent but verifier O(log n) heavier and proof larger — unsuitable for L1 Ethereum where pairing precompile cheaper than many hashes. Nova transparent but final compressed proof still needs pairing-friendly cycle (BN254/Grumpkin) for Ethereum verification.

**2. Lookup table blow-up:** Plookup/Halo2 tables for 16-bit or larger domains grow as `2^k`. Keccak table of 2^16 entries dominates fixed column size and increases prover memory. LogUp / cq (Eagen et al. 2022) reduces to `O(n)` but requires rational function commitment, not yet production in Halo2 main branch.

**3. Folding zero-knowledge gap:** Nova ZK for free relies on randomizing witness, but if witness has structure (e.g., deterministic IVC), additional blinding via random `E` needed; naive randomization leaks `r` correlation. KiloNova shows non-uniform PCD ZK requires careful circuit aggregation and decoupling to avoid cross-instance leakage [9].

**4. Custom gate soundness:** PLONKish custom gates of degree >5 increase quotient polynomial degree, forcing larger evaluation domain `4n` → `8n` (UltraPLONK), FFT blow-up 2×. CCS mitigates by encoding custom gates as degree-1 constraints over extended columns, but requires careful degree bounding in folding verifier.

**5. Cycle arithmetic overhead:** CycleFold reduces but does not eliminate non-native field emulation. Proving Pallas scalar multiplication inside Vesta still costs ~1000 constraints vs native 10 constraints. For EVM zkVM with 1M steps, recursive overhead 1B constraints — still heavy, requiring hardware acceleration.

**6. Accumulation vs. direct SNARK:** Folding defers verification but final decider is SNARK (Spartan) whose prover is O(n log n) — for very large `n` (10^6 steps), final compression dominates. ProtoStar, HyperNova multi-fold help by folding many instances at once, but increase memory for cross-terms `T_i`.

## 7 Conclusion

We have shown how three distinct ideas compose into a unified transparent recursion stack:

- **PLONK permutation arguments** turn copy constraints from structural equality into product equalities over Lagrange bases, enabling universal SRS and succinct verification [1].
- **Halo2 lookup tables** generalize Plookup into width-flexible subset arguments over PLONKish grid, removing trusted setup via IPA and enabling efficient range checks and custom gates that cut SHA-256 from 28k to 8k gates [2][4][13].
- **Nova recursive folding** replaces expensive SNARK-in-SNARK verification with linear folding of relaxed R1CS, where verifier is two scalar multiplications and prover two MSMs, achieving IVC/PCD with constant recursive overhead [5].
- **Accumulation schemes** (CycleFold, HyperNova/CCS, KiloNova) lift folding to customizable constraints, a-la-carte instruction costs, and cycle-optimized recursion [6][7][8][9].

Future work converges on *folding custom gates with verifier input* (Origami) to include lookups directly in folding, eliminating need for separate Plookup inside folded circuit, and on *CycleFold-optimized zkVMs* where EVM/RISC-V execution folded instruction-by-instruction with table-driven decomposition. As transparent recursion matures, the boundary between arithmetization, commitment, and accumulation dissolves: all become instances of CCS folding over cycles.

The practical consequence is a *no-trusted-setup* IVC that can prove a blockchain's entire history in one kilobyte-sized proof, compress unlimited computation, and verify in milliseconds — the original promise of Halo realized through composition.
