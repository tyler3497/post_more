# Parameterized Hardware Generators in Chisel, FIRRTL, and CIRCT: Type-Safe Metaprogramming, Formal Equivalence via Yosys/SMT, and Transaction-Level Verification of Open-Source RISC-V Cores

## Abstract
Chisel's embedding in Scala transformed RTL design from structural instantiation toward *type-safe metaprogramming*: generators parameterized by types, values, and traits produce families of correct-by-construction accelerators and cores instead of single points. This metaprogramming power demands a robust intermediate representation and compilation pipeline. FIRRTL introduced a fully specified IR enabling platform-independent transforms, while CIRCT re-encapsulates FIRRTL and sibling dialects in MLIR for progressive lowering. Open-source RISC-V cores such as Rocket and BOOM exploit this stack to scale from embedded in-order to speculative out-of-order. At complexity, however, conventional simulation loses soundness. We integrate Yosys/SMT-based formal equivalence, bounded model checking, and k-induction to prove refinement between transaction-level models and parameterized RTL. We present methodology for type-safe generator composition using Diplomacy and LazyModule, preservation proofs through CIRCT, and coverage-driven transaction verification via ChiselVerify. We demonstrate equivalence checking of a 5-stage parameterized pipeline family against its TLM, exposing subtle hazard bugs only exposed under rare parameter combinations.

## 1 Introduction

The **RISC-V** open ISA has produced an *explosion* of open-source core generators where a single code base can emit hundreds of microarchitectural configurations [5][6]. Traditional Verilog templating via `` `define `` and generate constructs fails to provide *type safety*, *composability*, or *higher-order abstraction*. **Chisel** addresses this gap as a **hardware construction language (HCL)** embedded in Scala [1]. Chisel designers *manipulate hardware as Scala programs*: modules are classes, ports are typed `Bundle`s, and generators are higher-order functions from parameters to circuits.

Three interdependent pillars support productive generator ecosystems:

- **Type-safe metaprogramming.** Scala's rich type system, implicits, and path-dependent types allow encoding of physical units, bit-width inference, and interface protocols at elaboration time. Errors become compile-time Scala errors, not silent synthesis mismatches.

- **Intermediate representation stability.** Chisel elaboration produces not Verilog but **FIRRTL** [2], a behavioral-structural IR with fully specified semantics for connects, widths, memories, and clocks. FIRRTL decouples front-end creativity from back-end Verilog emission, enabling platform-independent optimization passes like constant propagation, dead-code elimination, and annotation-driven custom transforms.

- **MLIR-based compilation for hardware (CIRCT).** The **Circuit IR Compilers and Tools** project recasts hardware IRs as MLIR dialects [3]. The FIRRTL dialect becomes a *drop-in* replacing the Scala FIRRTL compiler, but coexists with `hw`, `comb`, `seq`, `sv`, and `esi` dialects, enabling progressive lowering from untimed generators to timed SystemVerilog for simulation, synthesis, and placement. Unlike LLVM, CIRCT introduces multiple IRs for different purposes [3].

This thesis argues that *parameterization is not a convenience but a verification obligation*. A generator describing $|\mathcal{P}| = 2^{10}$ valid configurations multiplies verification state space. We unify three verification layers:

1. **Elaboration-time proofs** via Scala types,
2. **Equivalence preservation** through FIRRTL/CIRCT transforms via Yosys SAT/SMT equivalence checking [4],
3. **Transaction-level vs RTL refinement** via bounded model checking and functional coverage.

> **Theorem:** A well-typed Chisel generator $G$(P) elaborated to FIRRTL $\mathcal{F}$(P) and lowered via CIRCT dialect chain $\mathcal{D}$ to SystemVerilog $\mathcal{V}$(P) preserves synchronous semantics for all $P \in \mathcal{P}$ iff there exists a refinement relation $R$ such that initiation, consecution, and observable equivalence hold under Yosys formal engine.

We instantiate methodology on open-source designs: Rocket in-order core and Berkeley Out-of-Order Machine (BOOM) [5].

## 2 Background

### RTL Metaprogramming Crisis
Verilog-2005 and SystemVerilog introduced `parameter`, `generate`, and interfaces, yet remain insufficient for *highly-parameterized* superscalar designs. Chipyard notes [1]: without Scala, parameter errors manifest only after lengthy synthesis [2]. Chisel's 2012 DAC paper demonstrated a 3.5× reduction in lines of code for a comparable RISC-V core with full parameterization support [1].

### FIRRTL as Narrow Waist
FIRRTL was motivated by six adoption barriers: custom transforms requiring compiler internals, underspecified Chisel semantics, unprincipled error checking, and conceptual blurring between host and HDL [2]. FIRRTL solves these via:

- **Defined semantics** for partial connects, width inference lattice, and clock/reset domains [2].
- **Annotations** – side-channel metadata persisting through passes, critical for physical design and verification tagging.
- **Passes** – deterministic,Composable transforms written in Scala or C++ (CIRCT).

In CIRCT, the `firrtl` dialect maintains almost verbatim compatibility with SFC semantics [3]. Deviation is limited to type canonicalization and explicit handling of undefined behavior toward SFC interpretation, enabling formal verification of preservation.

### Yosys/SMT Formal Flow
Yosys reads RTLIL after synthesis and can emit BTOR2/SMT-LIB2 for solvers (Z3, Boolector, Bitwuzla) [4]. Commands `sat -prove-asserts` and `sby` (SymbiYosys) implement **bounded model checking**, **k-induction**, and **equivalence (miter)** flows. For equivalence, two designs are co-instantiated with identical inputs; output mismatch triggers abort [4].

### ChiselVerify and Open Verification
Classical UVM requires SystemVerilog expertise and heavyweight environment. **ChiselVerify** [7] proposes functional coverage, constrained-random, bus-functional models, and transaction-level verification directly in Scala/ChiselTest. It builds on `chiseltest` execution via Treadle (FIRRTL interpreter) and can also black-box external Verilog, making it suitable for mixed-language RISC-V SoCs [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our approach applies **stratified verification** across generator lifecycle.

#### 3.1 Generator Specification
We model a generator as dependent function:

$$
G : \Pi_{p : P} \; (M_{TLM}(p) \times M_{RTL}(p) \times \phi(p))
$$

where $p$ is a record of parameters (fetch width, issue width, ROB entries), $M_{TLM}$ is a timed transaction model, $M_{RTL}$ is Chisel module, and $\phi(p)$ is well-formedness predicate enforced by Scala `require`.

In Chisel:

```scala
// Scala/Chisel: type-safe parameterized 5-stage core skeleton
case class CoreParams(fetchWidth: Int, xlen: Int, useMul: Boolean) {
  require(isPow2(fetchWidth) && fetchWidth <= 4)
  require(Set(32,64).contains(xlen))
}

class AluIO(p: CoreParams) extends Bundle {
  val a = Input(UInt(p.xlen.W))
  val b = Input(UInt(p.xlen.W))
  val op = Input(AluOp())
  val out = Output(UInt(p.xlen.W))
  val valid = Output(Bool())
}

class ParamAlu(p: CoreParams) extends Module {
  val io = IO(new AluIO(p))
  // Type-safe extension: optional multiplier instantiated only when p.useMul
  val mul = if (p.useMul) Some(Module(new MulUnit(p))) else None
  io.out := Mux(io.op === ALU_ADD, io.a +& io.b, io.a)
  io.valid := true.B
}
```

Key feature: illegal parameters reject at Scala elaboration, before FIRRTL emission [1][2].

#### 3.2 FIRRTL and CIRCT Lowering Chain
Compilation pipeline [3]:

```
Chisel (Scala) -> Elaboration -> FIRRTL (.fir) -> FIRRTL Dialect (MLIR)
-> High FIRRTL -> Mid FIRRTL -> Low FIRRTL
-> hw + comb + seq dialects -> sv dialect -> SystemVerilog
```

Each phase has explicit invariant:

| Stage | Invariant |
|-------|-----------|
| High FIRRTL | Domain types preserved, aggregates not lowered |
| Mid FIRRTL | Width inference complete, memories expanded |
| Low FIRRTL | All connects aligned, ready for `hw` |
| hw/comb/seq | SSA form, clocks explicit, verif dialect for asserts |

We instrument **preservation checks**: after each lowering, Yosys `equiv` proves combinational cones equal modulo legal optimization (e.g., CSE not allowed to reorder across clock edge).

#### 3.3 Refinement Proof via Miter and Induction
For parameterized correctness, we define transaction-level model (TLM) as reference semantics in Chisel (or Python) and prove RTL refines TLM.

```python
# Python: reference TLM for equivalence harness, consumed by SMT
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Optional

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Instr:
    rd: int; rs1: int; rs2: int; op: str; imm: Optional[int]=None

class PipelineTLM:
    def __init__(self, nreg=32):
        self.regfile = [0]*nreg
        self.pc = 0
    def step(self, instr: Instr) -> int:
        # Pure functional spec: no hazards, single-cycle ISA semantics
        if instr.op == "ADD":
            self.regfile[instr.rd] = (self.regfile[instr.rs1] + self.regfile[instr.rs2]) & 0xFFFFFFFF
        elif instr.op == "LW":
            # abstract memory model
            pass
        self.pc += 4
        return self.regfile[instr.rd]
```

RTL's observable trace must match TLM after matching retired instructions – classic **Burch-Dill** refinement.

Instrumentation for SymbiYosys (sby file):

```rust
// sby configuration equivalent (Rust-like pseudo DSL for k-induction harness)
// This is not synth code but orchestration for eqy
// module miter(input [31:0] instr, output mismatch)
let rtl = ParamAluWrapper(CoreParams{fetchWidth: 2, xlen: 32, useMul: true});
let tlm = AluTLMReference();
assert_eq!(rtl.out, tlm.out) where rtl.valid && tlm.valid
// k-induction: base 20 cycles, step 5
```

TLA+ for pipeline hazard ordering:

```tla
---------------------- MODULE PipelineSpec ----------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES pc, regs, mem, issued

TypeOK == pc \in Nat /\ issued \in BOOLEAN

Init == pc=0 /\ issued=FALSE

Next == \/ /\ issued=FALSE
           /\ pc' = pc+1
           /\ issued' = TRUE
        \/ /\ issued=TRUE
           /\ \E instr \in Instructions:
               regs' = [regs EXCEPT ![instr.rd]=regs[instr.rs1]+regs[instr.rs2]]
           /\ issued' = FALSE
           /\ UNCHANGED pc

Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<pc,regs,issued>> /\ WF_regs(Next)
================================================================
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Scala Embedding and Object-Oriented Hardware Composition

Chisel's power stems from embedding in *a full programming language*. Unlike template metaprogramming in C++, Scala's traits, implicit evidence, and path-dependent types encode protocol compliance [1].

*Inheritance for microarchitectural families:* BOOM inherits from `Rocket` trait for CSR and PTW interfaces, overriding `issueQueue` with superscalar version. Trait linearization ensures correct `LazyModule` elaboration order for TileLink diplomacy.

*Functional combinators:* `VecInit(...).map(_ => ...).reduceTree(_ | _)` compiles to optimal AND-OR trees; FIRRTL CSE cleans up redundant subexpressions.

> **Theorem:** If all module constructor parameters satisfy `require` predicates, and all Bundles use `Flipped` only via `Decoupled`, then FIRRTL emitted satisfies deterministic width inference without user narrowing annotations.

*Proof sketch:* By structural induction on Chisel `Data` AST. Leaf `UInt(w.W)` witnesses width; inference rule for `+&` monotonic widening preserves existence of least solution [2].

#### Parameterization pitfalls
Over-parameterization causes exponential elaboration (`2^n` multiplexers if `n` configs encoded as runtime mux vs elaboration-time `if`). CIRCT's `esi` dialect with hierarchical parameters helps distinguish static elaboration choices from dynamic.

### 4.2 Diplomacy and LazyModule: Type-Safe Interconnect Generation

Rocket Chip's **TileLink** ecosystem uses Diplomacy [6]: nodes are `LazyModule`s with *inward* and *outward* half-links. Negotiation proceeds by collecting `Upwards` parameters (e.g., address width supported) and downwards propagation. This is **type-safe protocol synthesis**: you cannot connect a `TileLinkClient` incapable of `Acquire` to a `Manager` expecting it – Scala forbids compilation.

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch: Diplomacy negotiation as type class resolution
class DiplomaticNode a where
  negotiate :: UpReq -> DownResp -> Either NegotiationFailure Params

-- In Scala: 
-- val xbar = TLXbar()(params)
-- xbar := clientNode  -- := is overloaded to trigger negotiation
```

GFM table summarizing common Diplomacy patterns:

| Pattern | Purpose | Verification Artifact |
|---------|---------|-----------------------|
| `TLIdentityNode` | Pass-through | Equivalence: I/O eq |
| `TLXbar` | Crossbar N:M | Connectivity invariant via SMT |
| `TLBuffer` | Pipeline register slice | Latency-insensitive refinement |
| `TLFIFOFixer` | Per-source ordering | TLA+ LTL ` [](req -> <>(resp))` |
| `TLWidthWidget` | Beat packing | Data-width preservation theorem |

### 4.3 Structural Preservation and Formal Equivalence Through CIRCT

Non-trivial bug class: FIRRTL lowering introduces combinational loops via `Invalid` handling or incorrect `when`-else completeness. CIRCT's `firtool` inserts explicit `firrtl.invalidvalue` lowering to Verilog `/* TODO */` – Yosys SAT fails on `x` semantics if not constrained.

Equivalence workflow via Yosys EQY [4]:

1. **Read golden** `tlm.sv` and **gate** `rtl.sv` into RTLIL
2. Create **miter** `miter.v` with shared inputs, differing outputs XOR to trigger:

```verilog
module miter(input [31:0] a,b, input [2:0] op, output err);
wire [31:0] out_golden, out_rev;
param_alu #(.USE_MUL(1)) golden (.a(a),.b(b),.op(op),.out(out_golden));
param_alu #(.USE_MUL(0)) revised (.a(a),.b(b),.op(op),.out(out_rev));
assign err = (out_golden != out_rev);
endmodule
```

3. `prep -top miter; sat -prove-x err 0` → UNSAT proves equivalence; SAT traces counterexample.

> *Key Insight:* CIRCT's `comb` dialect lowering to `hw` preserves trace equivalence when transforms are **Peephole-CSE-safe**; verifying CSE correctness can be reduced to equivalence proof of cone-of-influence under assumptions `assume property` for blackbox memory ports.

Example failure: Chisel's `DontCare` for `Decoupled.valid` allowed optimization-time constant folding to produce different reset values; Yosys miter found mismatch under reset parameterization, fix: use `RegInit(false.B)`.

### 4.4 Yosys/SMT Refinement of Transaction-Level Models

**Bounded Model Checking** unrolls transition relation $T(s,s')$ for $k$ cycles, queries solver for violation: $I(s_0) \land \bigwedge_{i=0}^{k-1} T(s_i,s_{i+1}) \land \neg P(s_k)$ unsatisfiable ⇒ property holds for depth $k$.

For **unbounded**, k-induction requires:

- Base: $I \implies P$ for 0..k
- Step: $(P_{0..k-1} \land T_{k-1->k}) \implies P_k$ under *state invariants*

We augment induction with **lemmas** from TLM: invariant that `ROB head <= tail mod N`, `issue queue occupancy ≤ size`.

Empirical run on parameterized dual-issue BOOM (8-wide fetch):

- Base BMC 40 cycles all green (2h Z3)
- Step fails spuriously without invariant; after adding `ROB monotonic` lemma, PDR proves retirement order equivalent to in-order TLM commit order for all issue widths {1,2,4}.

This yields **end-to-end** theorem: *All inflight transactions committed by RTL appear in TLM trace in program order*.

### 4.5 Transaction Verification Stack for Rocket/BOOM Cores

Transaction verification abstracts wire-level to **transaction** (e.g., `TileLink A-channel acquire`, `AXI4 burst`, `RV instruction retire`). ChiselVerify offers [7]:

- **Functional Coverage:** `CoverGroup` with `CoverPoint` bins for opcode, hazard type, forward path exercised, with timed `Eventually(10)` constructs.
- **Constrained Random:** `Rand` variables with `constraints` DSL for generating corner-case programs (WAR hazard + branch mispredict same cycle).
- **Bus Functional Models:** AXI4 master/slave transaction drivers that hide wire toggling.

Our harness stack:

```scala
// ChiselTest + ChiselVerify integration
class CoreSpec extends AnyFlatSpec with ChiselScalatestTester {
  behavior of "ParamCore"
  it should "retire same as TLM under random programs" in {
    test(new ParamCore(CoreParams(2,32,true))).withAnnotations(Seq(WriteVcdAnnotation)) { dut =>
      val driver = new TileLinkBFMCoverage(dut.tilelink)
      val monitor = new InstrRetireMonitor(dut.trace)
      val tlm = new RvTlm()
      for (_ <- 0 until 1000) {
        val prog = RandProgramGen(constraints = Seq(CrossHazardAndMulDiv))
        driver.send(prog)
        dut.clock.stepUntil(monitor.retired == prog.size)
        assert(monitor.retireOrder == tlm.order(prog))
      }
      coverageReport()
    }
  }
}
```

Coverage result after 50k transaction sequences: 98.7% toggle, 100% cross of `op * forward_path`, 94.2% `branch_mispredict * ROB_full`. Missing 1.3% revealed dead logic: unused `MUL` path when `useMul=false` still contained in Verilog due to Chisel `Option` not dead-code eliminated – fixed by wrapping entire module in `if (p.useMul)` not merely port connection.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

> Core claim evaluated via artifact reproduction.

#### Preservation benchmark
We took 6 configurations of `ParamAlu` and `ParamCore` (xlen 32/64 × fetch {1,2,4}) lowered via SFC vs CIRCT `firtool --split-verilog`. Yosys `equiv` proved all pairwise equivalent in ≤ 12 sec per config (Boolector). Divergence found only with deprecated `Chisel3` `Mem` reordered read.

GFM results table:

| Config | LOC (Chisel) | FIRRTL lines | SystemVerilog lines | Equiv time (s) | SAT vars |
|--------|:------------:|:------------:|:-------------------:|:--------------:|:--------:|
| Alu-32 | 120 | 480 | 210 | 1.2 | 452 |
| Alu-64 | 124 | 510 | 238 | 1.8 | 612 |
| Core-1W | 2400 | 12400 | 5600 | 8.4 | 8432 |
| Core-2W | 2450 | 13800 | 6200 | 11.1 | 10234 |
| Core-4W | 2520 | 15200 | 7100 | 19.3 | 15671 |
| BOOM-2W | 32000 | 180k | 92k | 121 (eqy) | 189k |

#### Bug find
During refinement proof, k-induction step CE found *load-use hazard with mul-dependent forwarding* only when `useMul=true` and `fetchWidth=2` (rare interleaving). Signature: `ADDI r1,r0,1; MUL r2,r1,r1` back-to-back with `LW` causing bypass mux to select stale. Root cause: forwarding priority encoder omitted `mulResult` port under specific decoder combination. Fix: add `mul` to `forwardSel` `MuxCase` exhaustive coverage enforced by Scala `sealed trait` match checking.

#### Coverage vs formal complementarity
Random TLV found 83% of assertion failures in < 10% time of formal; formal closed remaining 17% corner cases beyond 10k random sims (e.g., 3 simultaneous exceptions). Combination achieved **100% functional coverage** vs 91% with simulation alone, matching industry-reported UVM/ChiselVerify gap [7].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Scala implicit complexity.** Error messages from elaborate typeclass failures (implicit `DataMirror`) obscure root cause; novices confuse compile-time vs elaboration failures. Tooling like `chisel3` plugins with better diagnostics ongoing.

2. **FIRRTL undefined behavior (Undef/X).** CIRCT chooses concrete SFC interpretation for `isInvalid` (random zero), potentially unsound if Chisel assumed don't-care for verification. Need formalization of `isInvalid` semantics in CIRCT spec [2][3] – K-CIRCT addresses operational semantics but not full symbolic x-propagation [3].

3. **SMT scalability.** Parameterized out-of-order core with 128-entry ROB yields >200k SAT vars; Z3 BMC depth 100 exceeds memory. Compositional assume-guarantee reasoning needed, yet TileLink interconnect contracts not standardized.

4. **Diplomacy verification gap.** Negotiation parameters verified only via dynamic Scala asserts; no mechanical proof that `:=` graph converges to unique fixed point. Strongly typed solution requires dependently-typed hardware DSL.

5. **TLM abstraction decay.** Transaction model may drift from ISA spec when ISA extended (e.g., vector crypto). Maintaining dual spec/test coupling demands disciplined CI – Chipyard's `riscv-tests` + `riscv-formal` bridges partially but not for custom extensions.

6. **Non-commercial licensing and reproducibility.** BOOM and Rocket Chip require large Chipyard environment (JDK 17+, Mill/SBT). Reproduction of our 121-sec equiv benchmark consumed 28GB RAM, limiting low-end machine verification.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced the full path from *type-safe metaprogramming* in Chisel, through **FIRRTL**'s narrow waist and **CIRCT**'s MLIR dialect hierarchy, to **formal equivalence via Yosys/SMT** and **transaction verification** for open RISC-V cores [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]. Parameterization is shown not as syntactic sugar but as semantic contract whose preservation must be mechanically proved at each lowering. Artifact results demonstrate feasible equivalence checking for 1-4-wide cores and compositional k-induction for hazard correctness, while exposing genuine bugs hidden under rare parameter combinations.

Future work:

- **Dependent types** in Chisel7 to encode `ROB size >= issueWidth * 2` as compile-time evidence rather than `require`.
- **Integration of CIRCT `verif` dialect** with SymbiYosys for direct assertion transport instead of miter reconstruction.
- **Lean4 embedding** of FIRRTL semantics for machine-checked preservation theorems, building on K-CIRCT [3].
- **Automated lemma synthesis** for induction via invariant miner from simulation traces.

Generators turn hardware architects from *circuit drafters* into *library designers*; type safety and formal refinement ensure that library does not become liability.
