# Telomere to Telomere: String-Graph Assembly of HiFi Reads and the Pangenome Graph Revolution

## 1. Introduction

The Human Genome Project's "finished" sequence, announced in 2003, covered roughly 92% of the human genome [5][6]. The missing 8% was not an editorial choice but a technological wall: centromeric satellite arrays, the short arms of acrocentric chromosomes, and long segmental duplications consist of near-identical repeats whose length exceeds the reach of Sanger and short-read technologies. For nearly two decades, assemblers papered over these regions with *gaps* — runs of `N`s — and downstream genomics simply learned to ignore them.

Two sequencing revolutions dismantled that wall. Pacific Biosciences' **HiFi** (circular consensus) sequencing reads *≈15–25 kb* molecules at *>99.9%* accuracy (Q30+) [6], while Oxford Nanopore (ONT) produces **ultra-long reads** up to a megabase with more modest accuracy [6]. Accurate long reads revived the classical **overlap-layout-consensus (OLC)** paradigm and enabled the *string graph* formulation of assembly, in which reads are vertices and overlaps are directed edges. On this foundation, **hifiasm** introduced haplotype-aware error correction and phased string graphs, delivering haplotype-resolved human assemblies without sacrificing contiguity [1].

The culmination was the Telomere-to-Telomere (T2T) Consortium's **T2T-CHM13** assembly: a 3.055-billion-base-pair, gapless sequence of a human genome published in *Science* in 2022, adding nearly 200 Mbp of previously unread sequence [5]. Yet a single complete genome, however perfect, is a sample of one. Human genetic variation — especially structural variation — demands a *pangenome*: many genomes represented jointly as a **variation graph**. This thesis follows the complete arc: from reads, to string graphs, to haplotypes, to telomere-to-telomere chromosomes, and finally to graphs of genomes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The data: HiFi, ONT, and Hi-C

Three data types dominate modern assembly. **HiFi reads** combine length (15–25 kb) with accuracy comparable to short reads, so overlaps between reads almost always reflect true genomic adjacency rather than error [1][6]. **ONT ultra-long reads** sacrifice base accuracy for length, spanning repeats that HiFi cannot, but their recurrent error profiles historically defeated de novo correction [3]. **Hi-C** data provide chromatin contact frequencies that decay with genomic distance, furnishing chromosome-scale linkage between contigs and haplotypes [4].

### 2.2 Assembly graphs

The three classical paradigms are:

1. **Overlap-layout-consensus (OLC):** compute all-vs-all read overlaps, lay out reads along the implied superstring, derive consensus. Natural for long reads but historically *O(n²)* in overlaps.
2. **de Bruijn graphs:** shred reads into *k*-mers; nodes are *(k−1)*-mers. Memory-efficient for short reads but branchy and phasing-hostile.
3. **String graphs** (Myers): vertices are reads, edges are *irreducible* overlaps after transitive reduction. The string graph preserves exactly the assembly-relevant connectivity of the overlap graph while discarding redundant edges.

> **Theorem (String-graph reduction):** Let *G* be the overlap graph of a read set and *G\** its transitive reduction. Then every path in *G* corresponds to a walk in *G\** spelling the same string, so no assembly-usable overlap information is lost by working in *G\**.

Graph *simplification* then removes tips (dead ends), pops bubbles (allelic or paralogous divergence that rejoins), and merges linear chains into **unitigs** — maximal unbranched paths that become the assembly's contigs.

### 2.3 Diploidy and phasing

A diploid genome is two haplotypes, not one. Heterozygous sites split the assembly graph into bubbles; an assembler must decide which branch belongs to which haplotype. **Trio binning** partitions a child's reads into parental haplotypes using parent-specific *k*-mers before assembly [1]. **Hi-C phasing** instead assembles first and phases later, using contact signal to assign contigs or graph nodes to haplotypes [4]. Either way, the failure mode is *mispartition*: a homozygous read wrongly confined to one haplotype fragments the other.

### 2.4 Beyond the linear reference

Aligning all humans to one linear reference (GRCh38) creates **reference bias**: reads carrying non-reference alleles map poorly, and variants absent from the reference are invisible. A **variation graph** replaces the linear reference with a graph whose paths encode many genomes; a read maps to the path it resembles most [10]. This is the conceptual bridge from assembly to pangenomics.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The hifiasm pipeline

hifiasm [1] assembles HiFi reads in five stages:

1. **All-vs-all overlap** via minimizer seeding — *O(n)* in practice for HiFi data.
2. **Haplotype-aware error correction:** correct sequencing errors while *preserving* heterozygous alleles, so that corrected reads retain local phasing information.
3. **Phased string graph construction:** each vertex corresponds to one read (by ID); an edge joins two reads only if their overlap is consistent with the *same* haplotype. Heterozygous regions form haplotype-separated bubbles instead of collapsed tangles [1].
4. **Graph binning:** with global phasing from trio data or Hi-C, haplotype-specific markers are assigned to graph nodes; each haplotype's assembly is emitted by traversing the graph while discarding nodes marked for the other haplotype.
5. **Output:** a primary assembly (phased blocks resolvable from HiFi alone) and, with phasing data, fully haplotype-resolved contigs.

A key innovation is that mispartition is *repairable*: because the phased graph retains local phasing, a read lacking heterozygous alleles that was wrongly binned to one haplotype is recognized as belonging to both, and the break it would have caused is healed [1].

### 3.2 Double-graph hybrid assembly: hifiasm (UL)

HiFi reads alone cannot span repeats longer than the reads. **hifiasm (UL)** [2][12] builds *two* assembly graphs at different scales:

1. Correct HiFi reads and build the standard HiFi string graph.
2. Align ultra-long ONT reads to that graph; encode each ultra-long read as a **sequence of integers**, where each integer uniquely identifies a HiFi unitig it traverses. Nucleotide sequence is discarded at this step.
3. Build a **string graph of integer-encoded ultra-long reads** — the *integer graph* — producing ultra-long integer contigs that untangle HiFi graph structures.
4. **Graph incorporation:** remove from the HiFi graph all nodes already present on integer contigs, then merge the integer contigs back with the remaining nodes and edges.

The result is a final assembly graph in which tangles resolvable only by ultra-long reads become linear [2]. With trio or Hi-C data, the same graph-binning strategy yields haplotype-resolved T2T-scale assemblies; a polyploidy extension discards all other haplotypes' marked nodes when emitting each haplotype [2].

### 3.3 Direct assembly of noisy long reads: hifiasm (ONT)

A 2026 extension, **hifiasm (ONT)** [3], assembles ONT simplex reads directly rather than using them only to scaffold a HiFi graph. The core trick is informative-site clustering: true variant sites co-occur compatibly across overlapping reads, while recurrent sequencing errors do not, so a target read is corrected using only reads whose informative sites agree with its phase. The authors position this as a path to routine near-T2T assembly across research and clinical settings [3].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Haplotype-aware error correction and the phased string graph

Error correction is where most assemblers destroy phasing: correcting a read toward the *majority* allele converts heterozygous sites into homozygous ones, collapsing the very bubbles that encode haplotypes. hifiasm's correction instead consults the local pileup of overlapping reads and corrects only positions where the read disagrees with its *own* haplotype's consensus, keeping heterozygous alleles intact [1].

*Figure concept 1 — String-graph construction from HiFi reads: reads as vertices, overlaps as directed edges, unitig merging, and bubble resolution at a heterozygous locus.*

In the resulting phased graph, orange and blue reads carry haplotype-specific alleles while green reads come from homozygous regions; edges connect only same-haplotype reads. Unitigs are then maximal same-haplotype linear paths. The practical consequence, demonstrated across three human and five nonhuman datasets — including a *≈30-Gb hexaploid* California redwood — is that hifiasm frequently outperforms alternatives and consistently leads on haplotype-resolved assembly [1].

### 4.2 Global phasing: trio-binning, Hi-C, and ultra-long incorporation

Local phasing resolves heterozygous bubbles, but *blocks* separated by long homozygous stretches need global signal. Three complementary sources fill this role:

- **Trio binning** uses parental short reads to tag child reads with haplotype-specific *k*-mers before graph construction [1].
- **Hi-C** maps contact pairs to graph nodes; nodes in strong contact are assigned the same haplotype, enabling chromosome-long phasing from a single sample — crucially without pedigree data, so *de novo* mutations are phaseable too [4].
- **Ultra-long reads** provide the physical span to connect phased blocks across segmental duplications, encoded as integer paths through the HiFi graph [2].

The HiFi-plus-Hi-C combination is especially powerful for medically critical polymorphic regions such as **HLA** and **KIR**, whose diversity defeats reference-based analysis [4]. Typical usage is a single command:

```bash
# Trio-binned, haplotype-resolved assembly
hifiasm -o hg002.asm --h1 paternal.fq.gz --h2 maternal.fq.gz \
        -t 64 hg002.hifi.fq.gz

# Hybrid T2T: HiFi backbone + ultra-long ONT reads
hifiasm -o hg002.asm --ul ont_ul.fq.gz -t 64 hg002.hifi.fq.gz
```

### 4.3 The pangenome graph zoo: GFA, rGFA, minigraph, and the collapsed-graph problem

Assembly graphs describe *one* sample's reads. **Pangenome graphs** describe *many* samples' genomes. The community converged on the **GFA** (Graphical Fragment Assembly) format — segments (`S` lines) carrying sequence and links (`L` lines) carrying adjacencies — but GFA alone is a container, not a data model [7].

**rGFA** (reference GFA) extends GFA into a true pangenome model: one input genome is the *backbone*, and every segment carries stable-reference tags — `SN` (sequence name), `SO` (offset), `SR` (rank). This design deliberately **forbids collapsed graphs**: a segment may not correspond to two different reference intervals. The payoff is *orthology* and *stable coordinates*:

> **Theorem (rGFA coordinate stability):** In an rGFA graph, every segment projects to exactly one reference interval via its (SN, SO) tags. Consequently, paths through the graph convert unambiguously to linear coordinates, and adding new genomes never renumbers existing coordinates.

**minigraph** [7] constructs rGFA graphs incrementally: map each new haplotype to the growing graph with minimap2 plus a *graph wavefront alignment* algorithm, then augment the graph with the new sequence. It scales — 20 human genomes in about three hours — but by design it captures only variation **longer than 50 bp** and performs no base-level alignment, so SNPs and small indels pass through unmodeled [7].

The **vg toolkit** [10] takes the more general *path* model: a segment may lie on many paths or recur on one path (cycles allowed) — a *collapsed* graph. Collapsing is expressive but semantically hazardous: when two paralogous regions share one segment, orthology is undefined and stable coordinates require inspecting neighboring segments [7]. **Minigraph-Cactus** [8] splits the difference: minigraph builds the structural-variant skeleton, then the **Cactus** whole-genome aligner fills in base-level alignment, with **GFAffix** normalizing the result. The pipeline scaled to **90 human haplotypes** from the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium, and the authors show that using the complete CHM13 reference as backbone measurably improves accuracy [8].

### 4.4 From graphs to genotypes: mapping and structural-variant genotyping in vg

Graphs pay off when reads map to them. The vg toolkit demonstrated that mapping to a variation graph improves alignment over linear references by letting each read choose its most similar path [10]. The same machinery enables **structural-variant genotyping**: given an SV catalog (insertions, deletions, inversions), vg aligns reads to the graph and infers genotypes, tolerating small breakpoint errors. On catalogs of up to 97,368 variants, vg systematically produced the best genotype predictions against state-of-the-art SV genotypers [11]. Notably, graphs built directly from aligned *de novo* assemblies outperformed graphs built from intermediate VCF catalogs — assembly quality flows downstream into genotyping accuracy [11].

The minigraph work adds a cautionary empirical note: regions around structural variants are enriched for *false-positive SNP calls* made by linear-reference pipelines — 21% of false SNPs fell in SV-adjacent regions covering under 1% of confident territory — confirming that a meaningful share of short-read SNP errors are really misalignments involving SVs [7].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

**The T2T milestone.** T2T-CHM13 spans **3.055 billion base pairs** with gapless assemblies of all chromosomes except Y, adding nearly 200 Mbp of novel sequence — more than any reference release in the preceding twenty years [5]. The new sequence includes every centromeric satellite array (α-satellite monomers of ≈171 bp building ≈190 Mbp, 6.2% of the genome), the short arms of all five acrocentric chromosomes, and recently duplicated segmental duplications; annotation predicts 1,956 genes, 99 protein-coding [5]. The sample was a hydatidiform mole — effectively homozygous — sequenced with both ONT and PacBio HiFi and assembled with a string-graph approach, reaching consensus accuracy **Q73**, roughly one error per ten million bases [6]. Six papers in *Science* (31 March 2022) documented the sequence alongside companion studies [6].

**Assembly benchmarks.** hifiasm's phased graphs delivered leading haplotype-resolved assemblies across human and nonhuman data, including the 30-Gb hexaploid redwood [1]. The double-graph hifiasm (UL) extended this to T2T-scale diploid and polyploid assemblies [2][12], while the 2026 hifiasm (ONT) brought noisy simplex reads into de novo assembly [3]. Complementary tools fill adjacent niches: **HiCanu** targets segmental duplications, satellites, and allelic variants in HiFi data [3].

**Pangenome scale.** Minigraph-Cactus constructed graphs from 90 human haplotypes; replacing the GRCh38 backbone with CHM13 improved downstream accuracy — a direct, practical dividend of the T2T milestone [8]. A systematic comparison of construction methods (minigraph, pggb, mdbg, Bifrost, Minigraph-Cactus) confirmed minigraph's SV-scale speed and its SNP blindness, framing the current accuracy-versus-resolution tradeoff [9].

A standard contiguity metric used throughout these studies is **N50**:

```python
def n50(lengths):
    """Length L such that contigs >= L cover half the assembly."""
    s = sorted(lengths, reverse=True)
    half = sum(s) / 2
    c = 0
    for L in s:
        c += L
        if c >= half:
            return L
    return 0
```

## 6. Limitations

- **Resolution gaps.** minigraph/rGFA ignore SNPs and small indels entirely; base-level graphs (Cactus, pggb, vg) pay steep computational cost for that resolution [7][9].
- **Collapsing.** Collapsed graphs (vg, pggb) admit expressive representations but destroy orthology and stable coordinates; rGFA's non-collapsed discipline is simpler but cannot represent paralog collapse at all [7].
- **One complete genome is not a pangenome.** T2T-CHM13 is a homozygous mole genome — ideal for assembly, unrepresentative as a population reference; its 2022 release still lacked a gapless Y [5].
- **Phasing fragility.** Long homozygous runs break local phasing; Hi-C can switch haplotypes across them, and trio binning requires parental data that clinical samples often lack [1][4].
- **Repeat scale.** Repeats longer than HiFi reads remain graph tangles; ultra-long reads resolve them but add their own error and cost profiles [2][3].
- **Scalability.** All-vs-all overlap and whole-genome graph alignment remain memory- and compute-intensive at population scale [8][9].
- **Ecosystem inertia.** Most clinical pipelines speak VCF and linear coordinates; graph-native formats and coordinate projections are still maturing [10][11].

## 7. Conclusion

The trajectory from reads to pangenomes is a story of graphs growing up. The **string graph** turned long reads into contigs; **phased string graphs** turned contigs into haplotypes; the **double graph** turned haplotypes into telomere-to-telomere chromosomes; and **variation graphs** are turning chromosomes into populations. T2T-CHM13 proved that a complete human genome is achievable [5][6]; the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium's 90-haplotype graphs [8] prove that a complete *set* of human genomes is representable. The remaining work is engineering as much as science: base-level, non-collapsed, coordinate-stable graphs at population scale, with genotypers [11] and mappers [10] that make the graph — not the linear string — the default substrate of genomics.
