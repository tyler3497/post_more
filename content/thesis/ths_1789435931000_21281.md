# Spectral Sequences in Algebraic Topology: Exact Couples, the Leray–Serre and Atiyah–Hirzebruch Constructions, and Convergence Theorems

## Abstract

Spectral sequences are the principal algebraic machines for computing the homology or cohomology of a space, sheaf, or derived functor from filtered, local, or iterated data. This thesis develops the theory from first principles: filtered complexes and the bigraded exact couple of Massey, the derivation procedure producing pages E^r with differentials d^r of cohomological bidegree (r, 1−r), and the passage to the E^∞ page with its abutment filtration, edge homomorphisms, and criteria for strong convergence. We construct the two great geometric instances — the Leray–Serre spectral sequence of a fibration and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence for generalized cohomology theories — and work through classical computations: the cohomology ring of complex projective space, the homology of loop spaces, and the complex K-theory of spaces with even cells via collapse theorems. We then present Grothendieck's spectral sequence for composite functors and recover the Leray sheaf spectral sequence as a special case. Throughout we stress transgression, multiplicative structure, collapse criteria, and the extension problem separating E^∞ from the abutment.

##1 Introduction

Few instruments in modern mathematics repay study as richly as the spectral sequence. Introduced in rudimentary form by Jean Leray in the 1940s in his work on sheaf cohomology, and developed into a systematic computational tool by Jean-Pierre Serre in his 1951 doctoral thesis on the singular homology of fibrations, the spectral sequence converts the vague hope of computing global invariants from local data into a precise, page-by-page algorithm [1]. Each page E^r is a bigraded algebraic object equipped with a differential d^r; taking homology yields the next page, E^{r+1} = H(E^r, d^r). When the process stabilizes, the surviving page E^∞ encodes the associated graded of a filtration on the object of interest — the *abutment*.

The power of the device lies in its flexibility. The same formalism governs:

- the **Leray–Serre spectral sequence**, relating the (co)homology of the base, fiber, and total space of a fibration;
- the **Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence**, computing any generalized cohomology theory from ordinary cohomology with coefficients in the theory's values on a point;
- the **Grothendieck spectral sequence**, computing derived functors of a composite from the derived functors of the factors;
- and behind all of them, **Massey's exact couples** (1952), the elementary algebraic engine from which every one of these sequences can be derived [2].

Serre's original application already displayed the characteristic *reverse* use of the machine: knowing the homology of the total space and the base of a fibration, he extracted the homology of the fiber — in particular the homology of loop spaces — and with it, deep information about the homotopy groups of spheres [1]. Cartan and Eilenberg systematized the homological algebra; Grothendieck's 1957 Tôhoku paper made the composite-functor spectral sequence an organizational principle of the subject [7]; Atiyah and Hirzebruch (1961) showed that ordinary cohomology controls extraordinary theories through the skeletal filtration [5]. This thesis gives a unified, self-contained account: definitions, the exact-couple mechanism, the four classical constructions, convergence theory, worked computations, and honest limitations.

##2 Background

**Cohomological spectral sequences.** A *cohomological spectral sequence* consists of bigraded abelian groups E_r^{p,q}, defined for integers p, q and pages r ≥ r_0 (usually r_0 = 1 or 2), together with *differentials*

d_r : E_r^{p,q} → E_r^{p+r, q−r+1},   d_r² = 0,

and isomorphisms identifying E_{r+1}^{p,q} with the homology ker(d_r)/im(d_r) at bidegree (p, q). The *homological* variant uses subscripts and differentials d^r : E^r_{p,q} → E^r_{p−r, q+r−1}. A spectral sequence is *first quadrant* if E_r^{p,q} = 0 whenever p < 0 or q < 0; then for each fixed bidegree the differentials eventually vanish for degree reasons, and the groups stabilize to E_∞^{p,q}.

**Convergence and the abutment.** We write E_r^{p,q} ⇒ H^{p+q} to mean that each H^n carries a filtration

⋯ ⊇ F^p H^n ⊇ F^{p+1} H^n ⊇ ⋯

with E_∞^{p,q} ≅ F^p H^{p+q} / F^{p+1} H^{p+q}. The groups E_∞ thus determine only the *associated graded* of the abutment — the famous *extension problem* of recovering H^n itself is addressed in §6. Two canonical maps, the *edge homomorphisms*, connect the corners of the page to the abutment and are often the most directly usable output of the machine.

**Filtered complexes.** A *filtered cochain complex* is a complex C^* with a decreasing filtration ⋯ ⊇ F^p C^n ⊇ F^{p+1} C^n ⊇ ⋯ by subcomplexes, meaning d(F^p C^n) ⊆ F^p C^{n+1}. Its *associated graded* gr^p C = F^p C / F^{p+1} C inherits a differential. The spectral sequence of the filtration has

E_1^{p,q} = H^{p+q}(gr^p C),

and when the filtration is *bounded* (F^p C = C for p sufficiently negative, F^p C = 0 for p sufficiently positive) it converges strongly to H^*(C), with E_∞^{p,q} ≅ F^p H^{p+q}(C) / F^{p+1} H^{p+q}(C). Virtually every spectral sequence in topology arises this way: filter a space or a complex, and the machine records how the associated graded reassembles into the whole [3].

**Double complexes.** A first-quadrant double complex K^{*,*} with commuting differentials d_h d_v + d_v d_h = 0 has total complex Tot(K). Filtering by columns or by rows yields *two* spectral sequences with the same abutment H^*(Tot K):

- ^I E_2^{p,q} = H^p_h(H^q_v(K)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(Tot K),
- ^II E_2^{p,q} = H^p_v(H^q_h(K)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(Tot K).

This two-filtrations trick is the standard engine behind both the Leray–Serre construction (via Dress's singular double complex) and the Grothendieck construction (via Cartan–Eilenberg resolutions) [3][7].

**Exact couples.** An *exact couple* is a diagram of abelian groups

D —i→ D
 ↘j   ↗k
    E

exact at each vertex: im(i) = ker(j), im(j) = ker(k), im(k) = ker(i). Then d = j ∘ k : E → E satisfies d² = 0, because jkjk = j(ker j)k = 0. The *derived couple* has D' = im(i) ⊆ D, E' = H(E, d), with i' the restriction of i, j'(i(a)) = [j(a)], and k'([e]) = k(e) — all well defined by exactness [2][3]. Iterating yields a sequence of exact couples, and with compatible bigradings on D and E the E-terms assemble into a spectral sequence. This is Massey's great simplification: the entire apparatus of cycles, boundaries, and the classical Z_r^{p,q} / B_r^{p,q} bookkeeping is subsumed in one elementary diagram [2].

##3 Methodology

Our method throughout is *constructive and comparative*. For each of the four classical spectral sequences we:

1. **Identify the underlying filtration or double complex.** For Leray–Serre, the skeletal filtration of the base pulled back to the total space (Dress's singular double complex); for Atiyah–Hirzebruch, the skeletal filtration of a CW complex fed into a generalized cohomology theory; for Grothendieck, a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of an injective resolution.
2. **Extract the exact couple.** Long exact sequences of pairs in the filtration assemble into Massey's triangle, making the differentials d^r = j^r ∘ k^r and their bidegrees mechanical [2][3].
3. **Identify the E_2 page.** This is where geometry enters: the E_2 page must be recognizable — cohomology of the base with fiber coefficients, ordinary cohomology with theory coefficients, or iterated derived functors.
4. **Analyze differentials and convergence.** We exploit multiplicativity (the Leibniz rule), degree constraints (first-quadrant vanishing), and collapse theorems to force pages to stabilize, then solve the extension problem where the groups involved permit it.
5. **Verify by computation.** Classical test cases — projective spaces, loop spaces, K-theory — serve as both illustration and proof of concept, with one machine-checked page-turning calculation below.

A recurring technical theme is *naturality*: all constructions are functorial from the E_2 page onward, so maps of fibrations, spaces, or functors induce maps of spectral sequences. Comparison theorems (in the spirit of Zeeman) then allow differentials computed in universal examples to be transported everywhere [3].

##4 The Four Classical Constructions

### Exact Couples and the Mechanics of Derivation

Let (D, E, i, j, k) be a bigraded exact couple with cohomological bidegrees deg(i) = (−1, 1), deg(j) = (0, 0), deg(k) = (1, 0). The r-th derived couple (D^r, E^r, i^r, j^r, k^r) inherits bidegrees deg(i^r) = (−1, 1), deg(j^r) = (r−1, 1−r), deg(k^r) = (1, 0), since each derivation replaces deg(j) by deg(j) − deg(i). The differential

d^r = j^r ∘ k^r : E_r^{p,q} → E_r^{p+r, q−r+1}

therefore has total bidegree (r, 1−r): it moves r steps right and r−1 steps down. Setting E_r^{p,q} for the E-term of the (r−1)-st derived couple gives exactly a cohomological spectral sequence, with E_{r+1} = H(E_r, d^r) by construction [2][9].

The archetypal example comes from a filtered space ⋯ ⊆ X_p ⊆ X_{p+1} ⊆ ⋯. The long exact sequences of the pairs (X_p, X_{p−1}) in a generalized homology theory assemble into an exact couple with D^1_{p,q} = h_{p+q}(X_p) and E^1_{p,q} = h_{p+q}(X_p, X_{p−1}); the maps i, j, k are induced by inclusion, quotient, and the connecting homomorphism. This single construction, applied to the skeletal filtration, *is* the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence [6]. Massey's framework thus exhibits the AHSS and the Serre spectral sequence as siblings rather than distant cousins [2][4].

### The Leray–Serre Spectral Sequence

> **Theorem (Leray–Serre).** Let F → E → B be a Serre fibration with B path-connected. Then there is a first-quadrant cohomological spectral sequence of algebras, natural from E_2 on for maps of fibrations, with
>
> E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B; H^q(F)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(E),
>
> where H^q(F) denotes the local coefficient system given by the action of π_1(B) on the fiber cohomology. The differentials d_r : E_r^{p,q} → E_r^{p+r, q−r+1} are derivations for the product structure [1][3].

When π_1(B) acts trivially — a *simple* system of coefficients, automatic if B is simply connected — the E_2 page is the ordinary tensor product H^*(B) ⊗ H^*(F). The *transgression*

τ : E_r^{0,r−1} → E_r^{r,0}

is the restriction of d_r to the fiber axis landing on the base axis; it is the differential that carries the most readily interpretable geometric information, measuring the failure of fiber classes to extend to the total space [3].

The edge homomorphisms take the concrete form

- H^n(B) = E_2^{n,0} ↠ E_∞^{n,0} ↪ H^n(E), the pullback along the projection (inflation), and
- H^n(E) ↠ E_∞^{0,n} ↪ E_2^{0,n} = H^0(B; H^n(F)), restriction to the fiber.

Serre's 1951 thesis built this machine to attack the homotopy groups of spheres; its first triumphs were computations of H_*(ΩS^n) and of the cohomology of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, opening the era of systematic unstable homotopy theory [1].

### The Atiyah–Hirzebruch Spectral Sequence

> **Theorem (Atiyah–Hirzebruch).** Let A^* be a generalized cohomology theory represented by a spectrum A, and X a finite-dimensional CW complex with skeletal filtration X^{(p)}. Then there is a convergent cohomological spectral sequence
>
> E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X; A^q(pt)) ⇒ A^{p+q}(X),   d_n : E_n^{p,q} → E_n^{p+n, q−n+1},
>
> where A^q(pt) = π_{−q}(A). The construction is the exact couple of the skeletal filtration, and E_2^{p,q} is identified via the wedge decomposition X^{(p)}/X^{(p−1)} ≃ ∨ S^p [5][6][8].

The sequence is a machine for *bootstrapping ordinary cohomology into extraordinary theories*. Its most famous instance is complex topological K-theory KU, where Bott periodicity gives A^q(pt) = ℤ for q even and 0 for q odd. Hence nonzero rows occur only in even q, every even differential d_{2r} vanishes identically, and the first possibly nonzero differential is

d_3 = Sq^3 = β ∘ Sq^2 ∘ ρ,

the composite of mod-2 reduction ρ, the Steenrod square Sq^2, and the integral Bockstein β [5].

For a fibration F → E → B one may also run a *relative* Atiyah–Hirzebruch sequence with E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B; A^q(F)); when A is ordinary cohomology this recovers the Leray–Serre sequence, exhibiting Serre's construction as the ordinary-cohomology shadow of a fully general phenomenon [4][8].

### The Grothendieck Spectral Sequence for Composite Functors

> **Theorem (Grothendieck).** Let F : A → B and G : B → C be additive left-exact functors between abelian categories, with A and B having enough injectives, and suppose F sends injective objects of A to G-acyclic objects of B. Then for each A ∈ A there is a convergent first-quadrant cohomological spectral sequence
>
> E_2^{p,q} = (R^p G)(R^q F(A)) ⇒ R^{p+q}(G ∘ F)(A),
>
> natural in A [7].

The proof is the double-complex method in its purest form: take an injective resolution I^* of A, apply F to get a complex F(I^*), and choose a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution J^{*,*} — a double complex of injectives resolving each column, row, and homology object.

The low-degree terms yield the *five-term exact sequence*

0 → R^1G(F(A)) → R^1(G∘F)(A) → G(R^1F(A)) → R^2G(F(A)) → R^2(G∘F)(A),

the workhorse of applications [7].

E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y; R^q f_*(F)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X; F),

where R^q f_*(F) are the higher direct image sheaves.

| Spectral sequence | E_2 page | Differential bidegree | Abutment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leray–Serre (fibration F → E → B) | H^p(B; H^q(F)) | d_r : (r, 1−r) | H^{p+q}(E) |
| Atiyah–Hirzebruch (theory A^*, space X) | H^p(X; A^q(pt)) | d_n : (n, 1−n) | A^{p+q}(X) |
| Grothendieck (G ∘ F) | (R^p G)(R^q F(A)) | d_r : (r, 1−r) | R^{p+q}(G∘F)(A) |
| Leray sheaf (f : X → Y) | H^p(Y; R^q f_*(F)) | d_r : (r, 1−r) | H^{p+q}(X; F) |

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

**The cohomology of complex projective space.** Consider the circle bundle S^1 → S^{2n+1} → ℂP^n. The Serre spectral sequence has E_2^{p,q} = H^p(ℂP^n; H^q(S^1)), so only the rows q = 0, 1 are nonzero, and the only possible nonzero differential is the transgression d_2 : E_2^{p,1} → E_2^{p+2,0}. Let ι generate E_2^{0,1} ≅ ℤ and put u = d_2(ι) ∈ H^2(ℂP^n). By the Leibniz rule and the vanishing of d_2 on the base row,

d_2(ι · u^k) = d_2(ι) · u^k = u^{k+1},

so each d_2 : E_2^{2k,1} → E_2^{2k+2,0} is an isomorphism as long as both sides are nonzero. Since the abutment H^*(S^{2n+1}) vanishes above degree 2n+1, induction yields H^{2k}(ℂP^n; ℤ) ≅ ℤ for 0 ≤ k ≤ n, H^{odd} = 0, and u^{n+1} = 0. Hence

H^*(ℂP^n; ℤ) ≅ ℤ[u] / (u^{n+1}),   |u| = 2,

the truncated polynomial ring — recovered purely from the fibration, with no cell decomposition of ℂP^n assumed [1][3].

The page-turning in this computation is mechanical enough to verify by machine. Encoding the E_2 page rationally for n = 2 (basis in bidegrees (0,0), (2,0), (4,0), (0,1), (2,1), (4,1)) with d_2 : E_2^{2k,1} → E_2^{2k+2,0} an isomorphism for k = 0, 1:

```python
import numpy as np

basis = [(0,0),(2,0),(4,0),(0,1),(2,1),(4,1)]
idx = {b: i for i, b in enumerate(basis)}

def d2_matrix():
    # transgression d_2: E_2^{2k,1} -> E_2^{2k+2,0}, iso for k = 0, 1
    M = np.zeros((6, 6))
    M[idx[(2,0)], idx[(0,1)]] = 1.0
    M[idx[(4,0)], idx[(2,1)]] = 1.0
    return M

M = d2_matrix()
assert np.allclose(M @ M, 0.0), "d_2^2 must vanish"
rank = np.linalg.matrix_rank(M)
print("rank d_2 =", rank)
print("dim E_3 =", (6 - rank) - rank)
# surviving bidegrees: (0,0) and (4,1) -> total degrees 0 and 5 = H^*(S^5; Q)
```

The script outputs `rank d_2 = 2` and `dim E_3 = 2`: the surviving classes sit in bidegrees (0,0) and (4,1), i.e. total degrees 0 and 5, exactly H^*(S^5; ℚ). All later differentials vanish for degree reasons, so E_3 = E_∞, confirming the computation independently of the hand argument.

**Homology of loop spaces.** Apply the homological Serre sequence to the path fibration ΩS^m → PS^m → S^m with PS^m contractible. Then E^2_{p,q} = H_p(S^m; H_q(ΩS^m)) ⇒ H_{p+q}(PS^m), nonzero only in columns p = 0 and p = m. The differentials d^r : E^r_{p,q} → E^r_{p−r, q+r−1} can be nonzero only for r = m, giving isomorphisms d^m : E^m_{m,q} → E^m_{0,q+m−1} for all q ≥ 0 (everything must die to leave the contractible abutment). Hence H_{q+m−1}(ΩS^m) ≅ H_q(ΩS^m), and with H_0 = ℤ,

H_i(ΩS^m; ℤ) ≅ ℤ if (m−1) divides i, and 0 otherwise (m ≥ 3),

the classical Serre computation; for m = 2 one gets ℤ in every degree [1][3]. This is the *reverse* use of the spectral sequence par excellence: the unknown fiber is read off from the known total space and base.

**K-theory via Atiyah–Hirzebruch collapse.** For X = ℂP^n, the AHSS for complex K-theory has E_2^{p,q} = H^p(ℂP^n; ℤ) in even rows q and zero in odd rows. A differential d_{2r+1} : E_{2r+1}^{p,2k} → E_{2r+1}^{p+2r+1, 2k−2r} lands in an odd column, hence in zero; all differentials vanish and E_2 = E_∞. The filtration on K^*(ℂP^n) therefore has associated graded ⊕_j H^{2j}(ℂP^n; ℤ) in even total degree, and since every group involved is free abelian, all extensions split:

K^0(ℂP^n) ≅ ℤ^{n+1},   K^1(ℂP^n) = 0,

with the ring structure K^0(ℂP^n) ≅ ℤ[μ]/((1−μ)^{n+1}) detected by the Chern character. The same degree-parity argument collapses the AHSS for every space with cells only in even dimensions, e.g. even-dimensional smooth complete intersections in projective space [5][6].

**The five-term exact sequence.** From the low-degree corner of the Leray–Serre sequence one extracts, for simple coefficients,

0 → H^1(B) → H^1(E) → H^0(B; H^1(F)) → H^2(B) → H^2(E),

exact, where H^0(B; H^1(F)) is the invariants of the π_1(B)-action. This is frequently the only part of the machine needed in practice: it controls, for instance, when a cohomology class on the fiber extends to the total space and when a class on the base pulls back nontrivially [3][7].

##6 Limitations

**The extension problem.** E_∞ determines only the associated graded gr(H^n), not H^n itself. Both ℤ/4 (filtered 0 ⊂ 2ℤ/4 ⊂ ℤ/4) and ℤ/2 ⊕ ℤ/2 have associated graded ℤ/2 ⊕ ℤ/2; no spectral sequence alone distinguishes them. In practice one resolves extensions using freeness (as for ℂP^n above), multiplicative structure, or comparison maps to known cases — but the ambiguity is intrinsic to the formalism.

**Differentials are hard.** The E_2 page is usually computable; the differentials are not. Beyond degree-vanishing and the Leibniz rule, one needs extra input: Steenrod operations for the AHSS (d_3 = Sq^3, with higher differentials given by higher Massey products in the twisted case [8]), transgression theorems, or functoriality transporting differentials from universal examples. A spectral sequence with unknown differentials is a question, not an answer.

**Convergence subtleties.** For unbounded or non-first-quadrant filtrations, E_∞ need not compute the abutment: one needs Boardman's *conditional convergence*, i.e. vanishing of the derived limit lim^1 of the filtration, and even then E_∞ may compute the associated graded of the *completion* rather than the group itself. Whole-quadrant spectral sequences (as in Tate cohomology or periodic theories) demand particular care [3][9].

**Hypotheses are restrictive.** The Grothendieck sequence requires enough injectives and the acyclicity condition on F — false for many functors of interest without derived-categorical reformulation. The Leray–Serre theorem needs a Serre fibration; for arbitrary maps one must pass to homotopy fibers. The AHSS in the form stated needs finite-dimensional CW complexes, and its differentials, while theoretically stable cohomology operations, are explicitly known in very few cases [5][8].

##7 Conclusion

From Leray's sheaf-theoretic origins through Serre's thesis, Massey's exact couples, Grothendieck's Tôhoku synthesis, and Atiyah–Hirzebruch's extraordinary generalization, the spectral sequence has remained a single idea in many guises: *filter the object, compute the associated graded page by page, and reassemble*. The exact couple exhibits the mechanism in its barest form — one triangle, one derivation, iterated — while the Leray–Serre, Atiyah–Hirzebruch, and Grothendieck sequences show how geometry, stable homotopy theory, and homological algebra each supply the E_2 page that makes the machine turn.

The computations rehearsed here — the truncated polynomial cohomology of ℂP^n, the periodic homology of loop spaces, the collapse-driven K-theory of even cell complexes — are not museum pieces. They are the templates by which every modern application proceeds: identify the filtration, read the E_2 page, constrain the differentials by every available structure, and confront the extension problem honestly. Where differentials can be named — Sq^3, transgression, connecting homomorphisms — the spectral sequence computes; where they cannot, it precisely localizes our ignorance. That double character, as both algorithm and organized statement of what remains unknown, is why the spectral sequence endures as the central computational device of algebraic topology and homological algebra.
