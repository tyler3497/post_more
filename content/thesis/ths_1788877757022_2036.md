# Univalence in Practice: Homotopy Type Theory, Higher Inductive Types, and the Cubical Computational Interpretation of the Univalence Axiom

## Abstract

This thesis presents a self-contained account of homotopy type theory (HoTT) centered on the univalence axiom, higher inductive types (HITs), and their computational interpretation in cubical type theory. We begin from Martin-Löf type theory and Voevodsky's insight that identity types admit a homotopy-theoretic reading — types as spaces, terms as points, and identifications as paths — and that the canonical map `idToEquiv : (A = B) → (A ≃ B)` can be postulated to be an equivalence. We then examine why this postulate, introduced as an axiom, destroys the canonicity property of constructive type theory, and how the cubical-set model of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg [2] restores computation by making univalence *provable* through the Glue type former rather than assumed. We develop the core machinery — the interval object with De Morgan structure, face lattice and partial elements, Kan composition, and the Glue construction — and show how higher inductive types such as the circle receive a computational elimination rule in the cubical setting. We survey formalizations in Cubical Agda [4] and the accompanying library, report on worked examples (function extensionality, the Brunerie number), and identify the remaining limitations: the gap between definitional and propositional computation for filling, the cost of transports in practice, and the open problem of a fully general schema for HITs. The result is a practical map from foundations to machine-checked homotopy theory.

## 1 Introduction

Dependent type theory is both a programming language and a foundation for mathematics. In Martin-Löf's intensional type theory (MLTT), the identity type `Id_A : A → A → U` is generated by reflexivity and eliminated by path induction (the J rule), which forces all reasoning about equality to respect substitution [1]. For decades this intensional identity type was poorly understood semantically: it was known that identity types need not be propositions, but no coherent picture of their higher structure existed until Voevodsky and, independently, Awodey–Warren observed the *homotopy interpretation* — types are (weak) ∞-groupoids, terms are points, proofs of identity are paths, proofs of identity between proofs are homotopies, and so on.

The univalence axiom is the thesis's central object. It states that for types `A B : U` in a universe, the canonical map

```agda
idToEquiv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)
```

which sends `refl A : A ≡ A` to the identity equivalence, is itself an equivalence. In other words, *identifications of types coincide with equivalences of types*. From univalence one derives function extensionality, transport along equivalences, and the structuralist principle that isomorphic mathematical structures are identical — a principle that working mathematicians use informally every day but which classical foundations (ZFC) cannot express [1].

There is, however, a cost. Postulating univalence as an axiom — a closed term `ua : isEquiv idToEquiv` with no reduction behavior — destroys *canonicity*: a closed term of type `ℕ` no longer necessarily evaluates to a numeral, because it may be stuck on transports involving `ua`. As Bentzen observes, "using axioms in a type theory amounts to introducing new canonical terms without saying exactly how to compute with them," so that conventional HoTT "lacks all the desirable computational properties of a type theory such as canonicity" [3].

The cubical program, initiated by Bezem, Coquand, and Huber's cubical-sets model [2] and developed syntactically by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg (CCHM) [2], Licata and Brunerie, and Angiuli et al., resolves this tension. By internalizing the interval `I` with De Morgan operations and a Kan composition operation as *primitive structure* of the theory, cubical type theory makes univalence a *theorem with computational content*: the Glue type former gives a definitionally well-behaved inverse to `idToEquiv`. Implementations — notably Cubical Agda [4] — turn homotopy type theory into a practical proof assistant.

This thesis has five aims: (i) to give a precise statement of univalence and its immediate consequences; (ii) to explain why the axiomatic approach fails computationally; (iii) to develop the cubical machinery — interval, faces, composition, Glue — at a level of detail sufficient to *use* it; (iv) to show how higher inductive types compute in the cubical setting; and (v) to honestly assess what remains open. Throughout, we emphasize *practice*: what one must know to formalize homotopy-theoretic arguments in a machine.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and the identity type

We work in a dependent type theory with Π, Σ, inductive types, and a cumulative hierarchy of univalent universes `U₀ : U₁ : …` [1]. The identity type is the inductive family with a single constructor:

```agda
data _≡_ {A : U} (x : A) : A → U where
  refl : x ≡ x
```

Its eliminator J states that to prove `C : (x y : A) → x ≡ y → U` it suffices to handle the case `C x x (refl x)`. From J one derives symmetry, transitivity, congruence, and transport:

```agda
transport : (P : A → U) → x ≡ y → P x → P y
transport P (refl x) = id
```

A crucial subtlety is that `≡` need *not* be a proposition: a type may have many distinct self-identifications. Uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP, Streicher's axiom K) is independent of intensional MLTT, and rejecting it is what opens the door to homotopy theory [1].

### 2.2 The homotopy interpretation and equivalences

Under the homotopy interpretation [1], a type `A` is a space, `a : A` a point, `p : a ≡ b` a path from `a` to `b`, and `α : p ≡ q` a homotopy between paths. An *equivalence* is the type-theoretic analogue of a homotopy equivalence:

```agda
isEquiv : (A → B) → U
isEquiv f = (y : B) → isContr (fiber f y)

_≃_ : U → U → U
A ≃ B = Σ (f : A → B) (isEquiv f)
```

where `fiber f y = Σ (x : A) (f x ≡ y)` and `isContr` is contractibility. Equivalences compose, every equivalence has a quasi-inverse, and the type `A ≃ B` is well-behaved: in particular `isEquiv f` is a proposition.

> **Theorem:** (Univalence, Voevodsky [1]). For a universe `U` and `A B : U`, the canonical map `idToEquiv : (A ≡ B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence.

Consequences include: **function extensionality** (`f ∼ g → f ≡ g` is provable from univalence); **proposition extensionality**; the fact that univalence is *consistent* relative to classical foundations (Voevodsky's Kan simplicial-sets model [1]); and the development of synthetic homotopy theory — definitions of homotopy groups, the Hopf fibration, and Brunerie's computation that π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ — all inside type theory [1][2].

### 2.3 Higher inductive types

Higher inductive types generalize ordinary inductive types by allowing *path constructors* — generators not only for points but for identifications. The paradigmatic example is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : U where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator for `S¹` requires, given a motive `P : S¹ → U`, a point `b : P base` and a *dependent path* `l : PathP (λ i → P (loop i)) b b`, producing `f : (x : S¹) → P x` with `f base ≡ b` definitionally (or propositionally, depending on the theory) and `apd f loop ≡ l`. With univalence and HITs one defines suspensions, truncations, pushouts, and localizations — the full toolkit of homotopy theory — synthetically [1][2].

| Construction | Point constructors | Path constructors | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circle `S¹` | `base` | `loop : base ≡ base` | `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` |
| Interval `I` | `0, 1` | `seg : 0 ≡ 1` | Contractible test type |
| Suspensions `ΣA` | `north, south` | `merid : A → north ≡ south` | Spheres `Sⁿ` |
| Prop. truncation `∥A∥` | `∣_∣ : A → ∥A∥` | `squash : isProp ∥A∥` | Existential quantification |
| Pushouts | `inl, inr` | `glue : (c : C) → inl (f c) ≡ inr (g c)` | Quotients, joins |

### 2.4 The computational problem with axioms

In constructive type theory, every closed term of `ℕ` should reduce to a numeral — *canonicity* — and every closed proof of `∃ (n : ℕ) P n` should yield a witness. Adding `ua` as an axiom breaks this: `transport (λ _ → ℕ) (ua e) 0` is a closed natural number in normal form that is not a numeral. Worse, the simplicial-sets model that establishes consistency is essentially classical (it uses the axiom of choice / excluded middle to prove the Kan property), so it offers no computational reading of univalence [5]. The question answered by the cubical program is therefore sharp: *can univalence be given computational meaning while retaining canonicity?* The answer is yes [2][3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is expository and proof-theoretic. We reconstruct the cubical interpretation in the style of CCHM [2] and the computational cubical type theory of Angiuli et al. as presented by Bentzen [3], with the following commitments:

1. **Syntax-first.** We describe cubical type theory as a formal system — judgments, the interval `I`, the face lattice `F`, path types, composition, and Glue — rather than only as a model, because it is the syntax that a proof assistant implements [2][4].
2. **Definitions that compute.** Every construction is presented with its definitional behavior: how `comp` reduces at type formers, how `Glue` computes at the endpoints, and how transports along `ua e` evaluate.
3. **Machine-checked grounding.** Claims about usability are anchored in Cubical Agda [4] and its standard library (agda/cubical), which implements the De Morgan cubical theory with HITs.
4. **Comparative lens.** We contrast axiomatic HoTT [1] with cubical HoTT [2] on three axes: canonicity, definitional equalities for HIT eliminators, and the strength of univalence provable (book vs. pointed univalence) [6].

The interval `I` is a De Morgan algebra with endpoints `0, 1`, operations `∧` (meet), `∨` (join), and `¬` (involution). From it one builds the face lattice `F` (in CCHM, `F` is generated by equations `(i = 0)` and `(i = 1)` with `i ∧ ¬ i = 0` factored out), partial elements (terms defined on a face), and systems (glued partial elements). Paths are functions out of the interval:

```agda
PathP : (A : I → U) → A 0 → A 1 → U
PathP A a0 a1 = (p : (i : I) → A i) × (p 0 ≡ a0) × (p 1 ≡ a1)  -- informally
```

and the non-dependent `Path A a b` is the special case of a constant family. Crucially, paths have *definitional* η and β rules — `p i` applies a path at a dimension — which is what makes cubical reasoning ergonomic in practice [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The interval object and the face lattice

The interval is not a type but a *pretype* `I` with `0 1 : I` and De Morgan structure. Dimension terms are built from dimension variables and the operations; a face formula `φ : F` describes a sub-polyhedron of a cube (e.g. `(i = 0) ∨ (j = 1)`). The key rules of the face lattice:

- `(i = 0) ∧ (i = 1) = 0_F` (opposite faces are disjoint),
- `i ∧ ¬ i = 0` in the quotient (CCHM),
- `1_F = (i = 0) ∨ (i = 1) ∨ …` covers the boundary.

A *partial element* of type `A` on face `φ` is a term defined only where `φ` holds; a *system* `[φ₁ ↦ u₁, …, φₙ ↦ uₙ]` glues partial elements that agree on overlaps. Systems are the syntactic reflection of the geometric idea of specifying a cube by its faces [2]. The face lattice is decidable, which is exactly what lets type checking remain decidable despite the geometric content.

![Interval object with De Morgan operations and face lattice]( /thesis/ths_1788877757022_2036-3.webp)

### 4.2 Kan composition: the engine of the theory

Every type family `A : I → U` is *fibrant*: it supports composition of partial cubes. The homogeneous composition operator (in Cubical Agda's decomposition [4]) has, informally, the type:

```agda
hcomp : (A : U) (φ : F) (u : Partial φ A) (u0 : A [ φ ↦ u 0 ]) → A
```

Geometrically: given an open box (all faces of a cube except the top) and a bottom lid, `hcomp` fills the box and returns the missing lid. The general `comp` additionally transports along the family `A`. Composition is required to satisfy *uniformity* (stability under dimension substitution) and *regularity* — and, critically, it must compute at each type former:

- at Π/Σ by pointwise composition,
- at path types by composition in the family,
- at the universe by the Glue construction (§4.3),
- at HITs by the schema's composition structure [4].

This is the content of Huber's canonicity theorem: because composition is defined by recursion on types with genuine reduction rules, closed terms of `ℕ` still evaluate to numerals — canonicity is *restored* [2][3]. Recent work continues to refine which computation rules for filling can be made definitional versus propositional [6].

### 4.3 Glue types and the computational univalence proof

The masterstroke of CCHM is the *Glue* type former [2]. Given `A : U`, a face `φ`, a partial family `T : Partial φ U`, and a partial equivalence `e : Partial φ (T ≃ A)`, the type `Glue A [φ ↦ (T, e)]` is a type that *equals* `T` on `φ` and `A` elsewhere:

```agda
Glue : (A : U) (φ : F) (Te : Partial φ (Σ (T : U) (T ≃ A))) → U
unglue : Glue A [φ ↦ (T , e)] → A          -- equivalence, = fst e on φ
glue   : (a : A) (t : Partial φ T) (∀[φ] fst e t ≡ a) → Glue A [φ ↦ (T , e)]
```

with the definitional equalities `Glue A [1_F ↦ (T, e)] ≡ T` and `unglue (glue a t _) ≡ a`. Univalence is then proved, not postulated. For `e : A ≃ B`, define the line of types

```agda
ua : A ≃ B → Path U A B
ua e i = Glue B [(i = 0) ↦ (A , e) , (i = 1) �mapsto (B , idEquiv B)]
```

At `i = 0` this is `A`, at `i = 1` it is `B` — definitionally. The eliminator `uaβ : transport (ua e) a ≡ equivFun e a` holds definitionally in Cubical Agda, which is what "univalence computes" means in practice [4]. The inverse direction — that every path `P : Path U A B` yields an equivalence — uses composition in the universe, and the round trips are shown to be mutually inverse, establishing `isEquiv idToEquiv` [2]. Note the subtlety: what CCHM proves directly is *book univalence*; strengthening it to *pointed univalence* (the inverse sends the identity equivalence to `refl`) requires additional care and is the subject of ongoing work [6].

### 4.4 Higher inductive types, cubically

In the De Morgan setting, HITs are handled by extending composition to the new type former [4]. For the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : U where
  base : S¹
  loop : Path S¹ base base
```

The eliminator computes definitionally on `base`, and on `loop` the computation is witnessed by the `hcomp`-based composition structure for `S¹`: applying the eliminator to `loop i` reduces via the motive's composition. In Cubical Agda, `S¹` is not primitive — it is definable via the interval and Glue — but the library provides it with the correct computational behavior, and the pattern generalizes to suspensions, truncations, and pushouts [4]. The Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg schema [4] shows how to equip a large class of HITs with composition, and cubical type theory thereby supports synthetic homotopy theory with the same definitions as the HoTT book but with computational eliminators.

### 4.5 Cubical Agda in practice

Cubical Agda [4] implements this theory with `--cubical`. Its salient practical features:

1. **Paths as functions of the interval.** `PathP` is primitive; `funExt : f ∼ g → f ≡ g` is a one-liner (`λ p i x → p x i`), because function extensionality is *definitional* — no univalence needed [4].
2. **Transport that computes.** `transport (ua e) a` reduces by the Glue computation rules, so proofs by univalence can be *executed*.
3. **A large library.** The agda/cubical repository formalizes equivalences, univalence, HITs, synthetic homotopy theory (homotopy groups, Eilenberg–MacLane spaces), and category theory [4].

A typical working pattern is: define a HIT, prove its induction principle's computation behavior by `refl` where definitional, and use `ua` to transport structures along equivalences — e.g., showing that equivalent groups have equal classifying types. The cost is that transports, while computable, can be expensive: cubical reduction is not yet competitive with native computation in systems like Coq for large developments.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We now record the concrete, checkable payoffs of the cubical interpretation. Each item below is a theorem with a machine-checked or literature proof.

> **Theorem:** (Canonicity, Huber [2][3]). In cubical type theory, every closed term `n : ℕ` is definitionally equal to a numeral `suc^k 0`.

This is the precise sense in which cubical type theory "fixes" axiomatic HoTT: the proof proceeds by a sconing / logical-relations argument over the cubical-sets model, showing that composition for the universe (via Glue) preserves canonicity [2].

> **Theorem:** (Univalence computes [2][4]). For `e : A ≃ B` and `a : A`, `transport (ua e) a ≡ equivFun e a` holds definitionally, and `ua (idEquiv A) ≡ refl` up to a path.

Concretely, in Cubical Agda `uaβ e a : transport (ua e) a ≡ equivFun e a` is proved by `refl` — the computation rule for Glue fires during type checking.

> **Theorem:** (Function extensionality, definitional [4]). `funExt : ((x : A) → f x ≡ g x) → f ≡ g` is definable without univalence, by `funExt p i x = p x i`.

The following table summarizes what changes when moving from axiomatic HoTT [1] to cubical HoTT [2][4]:

| Property | Axiomatic HoTT (Book) | Cubical type theory |
|---|---|---|
| Univalence | Axiom `ua`, no computation | Provable via Glue, computes |
| Canonicity for `ℕ` | Fails (stuck on `ua`) | Holds (Huber) |
| Function extensionality | Consequence of univalence | Definitional, univalence-free |
| HIT eliminators | Axiomatic, propositional β | Computational, definitional β on point constructors |
| Model | Simplicial sets (classical) | Cubical sets (constructive) [2][5] |
| Identity vs. path | Inductively defined `≡` | Path types as `I → A` with η |

Worked case studies in the literature include: the formalization of the CCHM model itself in Nuprl [2]; the Cubical Agda library's development of synthetic homotopy theory including `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` and the Hopf fibration [4]; and Brunerie's thesis work on π₄(S³), originally done axiomatically and since revisited cubically [1][2]. The mini-course literature emphasizes that cubical methods simplify HIT-heavy constructions even when simulated inside axiomatic HoTT [7].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Definitional vs. propositional filling.** Some formulations of cubical type theory satisfy the computation rule for the filling operation only propositionally, not definitionally; homotopy canonicity survives, but definitional canonicity may not [6]. The exact boundary of what can be made definitional remains delicate.
2. **Performance.** Cubical reduction — particularly iterated transports through Glue and higher compositions — is computationally expensive. Large formalizations can be slow to type-check, and the gap with systems using native computation is significant in practice [4].
3. **The general HIT schema.** While Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg give a schema covering many HITs [4], a fully general, proven-correct schema for *all* higher inductive-inductive types with definitional computation rules is still not settled; implementations extend the schema pragmatically.
4. **Book vs. pointed univalence.** The pointed strengthening of univalence (inverse sends `idEquiv` to `refl`) is not automatic in all cubical settings and is an active research target [6].
5. **Metatheory overhead.** The semantics (presheaf models over De Morgan cubes, Orton–Pitts axiomatizations [2][6]) is technically demanding; the entry cost for contributing to the metatheory is high, and the model theory of *directed* and *simplicial* variants is still being built [8].
6. **Constructivity boundaries.** Although the CCHM model is constructive, some conveniences of classical homotopy theory (e.g., arbitrary choice principles used in the simplicial model [5]) have no direct cubical counterpart, and their synthetic replacements are an ongoing project.

## 7 Conclusion

The univalence axiom began as a bold postulate: identifications of types *are* equivalences of types. It unlocked synthetic homotopy theory but at the price of computation — the axiom was a black box, and canonicity, the property that makes type theory a programming language, was lost [1][3]. Cubical type theory repays that price with interest. By promoting the interval, faces, and Kan composition to primitive structure and introducing the Glue type former, it turns univalence from an axiom into a *theorem that computes* [2]: `transport (ua e)` evaluates, `funExt` is definitional, and closed naturals are numerals again.

For the practitioner, the upshot is concrete: Cubical Agda [4] is a dependently typed programming language in which one can do homotopy theory — define the circle as a HIT, prove `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ`, transport algebraic structures along equivalences — and have every proof be a program that runs. The limitations are real (performance, the HIT schema, pointed univalence [6]), but they are engineering and research frontiers, not foundational defects. The deeper moral is one Voevodsky anticipated: foundations matter most when they *compute*, and the cubical interpretation is what lets univalent foundations compute.
