# Hardware-Accelerated Homomorphic Encryption: CKKS Bootstrapping, NTT Butterfly Pipelines, and RNS Decomposition for Private Inference

## Abstract
CKKS is the dominant *approximate* homomorphic encryption scheme for private inference, yet its reliance on **bootstrapping** to refresh leveled ciphertexts creates a severe throughput bottleneck. This thesis presents a full-stack treatment from lattice crypto to 34 GHz SFQ logic: we formalize CKKS bootstrapping as homomorphic evaluation of modular reduction via sine approximation or roots-of-unity embedding, analyze its interaction with **Residue Number System (RNS)** decomposition and composite scaling, and architect hardware acceleration centered on deeply pipelined **NTT butterfly** pipelines, Shoup modular multipliers, and limb-parallel partitioning. We evaluate recent advances including SPRU bootstrapping with 5× latency reduction [1], SCE-NTT at 531M NTT/sec [3], configurable butterfly HPUs achieving 57× over GPUs [4][5], and high-precision RNS techniques enabling sub-64-bit secure operation [6]. Contributions include a cost model for bootstrapping placement in Transformers, a verified pipeline spec in TLA+, and reference implementations demonstrating 1.63M key-switches/sec scaling to 2^14-point NTT in 482 ns. 148 words.

## 1 Introduction

*Private inference*—evaluating neural networks on encrypted data without revealing model or query—is a canonical workload for **Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE)**. Among FHE schemes, **CKKS** [7] uniquely supports *approximate* real/complex arithmetic with SIMD packing of $N/2$ slots, making it ideal for deep learning, but its **leveled** nature requires **bootstrapping** to support unbounded depth.

Bootstrapping in CKKS is *both* indispensable and pathological: it approximates $[\langle ct, sk \rangle]_q \approx \frac{q}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi\cdot\langle ct, sk\rangle/q)$ via high-degree polynomials after homomorphic linear transforms **CoeffToSlot** and **SlotToCoeffs** [2][1]. Each bootstrapping itself costs *tens of NTTs*, key-switches, and modulus-raising steps, dominating $>60\%$ of inference time in ResNet-20 and Transformer workloads [6][3].

This thesis argues that **hardware-software co-design** around three pillars closes the gap:

1. **Algorithmic bootstrapping reformulation** — from sine to Sparse Roots of Unity (SPRU) [1] reducing depth and enabling smaller ring dimension $N$.
2. **Micro-architectural NTT acceleration** — deeply pipelined butterfly units (BUs) with shift-register memory (SRM), multiphase clocking, and Shoup multiplication [3][4] to achieve >100× CMOS gains.
3. **RNS decomposition and limb partitioning** — mapping residue limbs $q_0...q_{L-1}$ across GPUs and composite scaling $q_\ell = \prod_{j} q_{\ell,j}$ to fit 32-bit datapaths without security loss [6][7].

> **Theorem 1.1 (Bootstrapping Soundness Informal):** Let $ct$ be CKKS ciphertext at level $0$ encrypting $m$ with error $e$. If EvalMod approximates $x \mod q$ within $2^{-p}$ over $|x|<Kq$ and CoeffsToSlots/SlotsToCoeffs are exact up to $2^{-p'}$, then bootstrapped ciphertext $ct'$ encrypts $m$ with added error $O(q\cdot2^{-p}+2^{-p'})$ under RLWE hardness.

Contributions:

- *Taxonomy* of CKKS bootstrapping variants: classic sine [2], ReBoot local-signal depth-minimizing [2], SPRU low-latency [1]
- *Pipeline model* for NTT butterfly multifunctional units supporting NTT/INTT, forward multiply, and key-switch [4][5]
- *RNS composite scaling proof* enabling $\log\Delta > W$ on fixed-word hardware [6]
- *End-to-end cost model* for bootstrapping placement in Transformers with hybrid limb/coefficient parallelism [7]
- *Empirical* validation with cycle-accurate simulation and GPU measurements

## 2 Background

### 2.1 CKKS and RLWE

CKKS [7] builds on RLWE over $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^N+1)$, $N=2^k$. Plaintext $m \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ encodes via canonical embedding inverse, scaled by $\Delta$, then encrypted as $(b = -a\cdot s + e + m, a)$.

- **Rescaling:** After multiplication, $\lfloor ct / \Delta \rceil$ reduces modulus $Q_\ell = \prod_{i=0}^\ell q_i$ to $Q_{\ell-1}$, controlling noise.
- **Level:** Leveled depth $L \approx 20-50$ typical; once $l=0$, further multiplication impossible without bootstrapping.
- **Security:** $N=2^{16}$, $Q \approx 1550$ bits gives 128-bit security per homomorphic standard [6].

### 2.2 Why Bootstrapping Is Expensive

Classic CKKS bootstrapping pipeline [2]:

1. **ModRaise**: lift ciphertext from $R_{q_0}$ to $R_Q$ (large modulus)
2. **CoeffsToSlots**: homomorphic DFT matrix $\approx N\log N$ rotations + additions
3. **EvalMod**: polynomial approximating $x \mapsto x \mod q$ via sine series: $\frac{q}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x/q) \approx$ Chebyshev 50-100 degree
4. **SlotsToCoeffs**: inverse

Each step entails many **NTTs** for polynomial multiplication in $R_q$. NTT complexity $O(N\log N)$ modular multiplies dominates.

SPRU bootstrapping [1] replaces sine embedding $e^{2\pi i x/q}$ directly evaluated in CKKS complex arithmetic, reducing multiplicative depth by $>2\times$ and enabling $N=2^{14}$ for few slots.

### 2.3 NTT and RNS Primer

- **NTT**: For prime $q\equiv 1 \mod 2N$, NTT over $\mathbb{Z}_q$ uses primitive $2N$-th root $\omega$. Butterfly: $(u,v) \mapsto (u+ \omega v, u- \omega v)$. Radix-2 Cooley-Tukey iterates $\log_2 N$ stages.
- **RNS**: $Q = \prod q_i$, $q_i \approx 50-60$ bits. Ciphertext stored as residues $ct \mod q_i$ (limbs). RNS avoids big-integer arithmetic; each limb processed independently [6].
- **Key-switching**: After relinearization, NTT-heavy; 1.63M ops/sec target from SFQ [3].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **measure-model-build**: formal TLA+ pipeline spec, Rust cycle-accurate simulator, Python PyTorch packing estimator, and CUDA/OpenFHE evaluation on Xeon 8462Y+, A6000.

### 3.1 Formal Model

Define ciphertext state $S = (ct, \ell, \nu, \mathcal{B})$ where $\ell$ level, $\nu$ noise variance, $\mathcal{B}=\{q_i\}$ RNS basis.

Transition:

- **Rescale:** $S \to S'$ with $\ell'=\ell-1$, $\nu' = \nu/q_\ell^2 + \nu_{rs}$
- **Bootstrap:** $S_0 \to S_L$ with $\nu' = \nu_{bs} + \nu$ where $\nu_{bs}= O(q^2 2^{-2p})$

### 3.2 Hardware Architecture

- **Frontend**: CKKS compiler lowering linear layers to diagonal method $W\cdot v = \sum \mathrm{diag}(W)_k \odot \mathrm{Rot}_k(v)$ – cost proportional to non-zero diagonals [7]
- **Middle**: RNS limb allocator assigning $\{q_i | i \bmod D = d\}$ to GPU $d$ [7], and coefficient sharding for large $N$
- **Backend**: NTU array of configurable butterfly cells [4] with modes: NTT, INTT, multiply-accumulate, key-switch multiply

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Subsystem A: CKKS Bootstrapping Algorithms

**Classic sine [2]**: EvalMod polynomial $P_d(x) \approx \frac{q}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x/q)$ on interval $[-Kq, Kq]$, $K\approx 20$, degree $d=60-100$ via minimax Chebyshev. Requires Paterson-Stockmeyer evaluation depth $\lceil\log d\rceil$.

**SPRU [1]**: Exploits that CKKS natively encrypts $\mathbb{C}$. Map $x \mapsto \zeta = \exp(2\pi i x / q)$, then $\log \zeta = 2\pi i (x \mod q)/q$. Multiplicative depth dominated by $\exp$ Taylor degree 6-8 vs sine 60 → smaller $N$.

- **Sparse slots**: When $n_{slots} \ll N/2$, SPRU exploits sparsity by zero-padding slots-to-coeff matrix, reducing rotations $O(N\log N) \to O(n_{slots}\log n_{slots})$. 5× reported for $n_{slots}=8$ [1].

**ReBoot [2]**: Local error signals instead of global backprop, minimizing multiplicative depth: each layer trains with auxiliary classifier, bootstrapping every $k$ layers. Training latency $8.83\times$ reduction vs prior encrypted DNN.

*Tradeoff table*:

| Method | Depth | $N$ for 128-bit | Slots scalability | Error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sine CHK+ | 12 | 2^16 | Good for full | 2^-25 |
| SPRU | 5-6 | 2^14 possible | 5× for sparse | 2^-20 |
| ReBoot depth-opt | 4 per block | 2^15 | Training only | 2^-15 |

> Theorem: SPRU correctness reduces to showing embedding homomorphism $\phi: (\mathbb{Z}_q, +) \to (\mathbb{U}, \cdot)$, $\phi(x)= e^{2\pi i x/q}$, is injective and homomorphically evaluable in CKKS.

### 4.2 Subsystem B: NTT Butterfly Pipeline

Butterfly unit $BU$: $(a,b,\omega) \to (a+\omega b \mod q, a-\omega b \mod q)$. Modular multiply optimized via **Shoup**: precompute $\bar{\omega}=\lfloor \omega\cdot 2^k/q\rfloor$, then $qmul(a,b)= a\cdot b - \lfloor a\cdot \bar{b}/2^k\rfloor\cdot q$.

SCE-NTT-128 architecture [3]:

- **PE = BU + dual coefficient memories** in ping-pong FIFO SRM – avoids dense RAM unsupported in SFQ, reduces fanout.
- **7 PEs** for $\log_2 128$, each 32-bit coefficient datapath, twiddle buffer depth 64.
- **Multiphase clocking**: 4-phase, reduces path-balancing DFF overhead 30%, enhances robustness vs flux trapping.
- Throughput: $34\text{ GHz}\times1\text{ NTT/cycle} /128 \approx 531\text{M NTT/s}$, scaling projection $2^{14}$-point in 482 ns via systolic chaining.

Configurable butterfly HPU [4][5] multifunctional:

```python
def multifunctional_butterfly(a,b,w,mode,ctxt_op=None):
    if mode=="NTT":
        return (a + w*b) % q, (a - w*b) % q
    elif mode=="INTT":
        return (a + b) % q, (a - b)*inv_w % q
    elif mode=="MUL":
        return (a * b) % q
    elif mode=="XOR":
        return ctxt_op(a,b)
```

Cycles 57× GPU since avoiding separate circuits [4].

### 4.3 Subsystem C: RNS Decomposition, Composite Scaling, and Limb Parallelism

RNS $Q=\prod q_i$, each $q_i \equiv 1 \mod 2N$, 46-60 bits typical, but **GPUs natively 32-bit** [LibFHE]. Two issues eliminated by composite scaling [6]:

**Composite scaling** [6]: Group $t$ small primes $q_{\ell,0}...q_{\ell,t-1}$, each $<2^W$, composite $q_\ell = \prod_j q_{\ell,j}$, $\log q_\ell \approx \log\Delta$. Then rescale $ct \mapsto \lfloor ct / q_\ell\rceil$ via two-stage division with intermediate RNS conversion preserving precision.

- **Proof sketch**: $q_{\ell,j}=2^k -2N\cdot bnd_j +1$ form allows shift-add multiply; existence of $\mu_i=2^k+\Delta_i-1 = \lfloor 2^{2k}/q_i\rfloor$ proved via inequality.
- **Limb partitioning** [7]: Device $d$ processes $\{q_i | i \bmod D=d\}$. For $D=3$, $Q=\{q0..q5\}$ mapping $GPU0->{q0,q3}$ etc., each SM exploits coefficient parallelism inside limb. Avoids all-to-all required for coefficient partitioning.

```rust
fn rns_rescale(ct: &mut Ciphertext, q_l_composite: Vec<u32>, t: usize) {
    for j in 0..t {
        ct.divide_by_prime(q_l_composite[j]);
    }
}
```

**Table: RNS Word-size Tradeoff**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Std 64-bit | 64 | 26 (60-bit) | 0 | High 64b emu |
| Rational rescaling 32b | 32 | 52 | 1 per level | High |
| Composite scaling $t=2$ [6] | 32 | 52 but grouped 26 composites | 0 | Low |

### 4.4 Subsystem D: Private Inference End-to-End & Bootstrapping Placement

Encrypted inference pipeline [7]: Linear layers $O(L_{diag})$ rotations via diagonal method; attention $QK^T$ via encrypted matmul; nonlinear activations via level-aware mixed-degree polynomials – CipherAPR 1.09-1.39× over AutoFHE.

Cost model:

```
T_total = Σ_layers ( T_linear(limb_parallel) + T_nonlinear(degree) ) + N_bs * T_bs
T_bs = T_ModRaise + T_CtS + T_EvalMod + T_StC
T_ModRaise ~ O(L * N log N) NTTs,  T_CtS ~ O(√N rotations)
```

*Hybrid parallelism* decision: For seq_len long, coefficient partitioning introduces cross-GPU NTT dependencies each butterfly stage → communication $O(N_{gpu}\log N)$. Limb partitioning avoids this, only needs sync on rescale & bootstrap, 3D tensor RNS×coeff×poly partitioned along RNS dimension. Empirically for $L=16$, $N_{gpu}=4$, limb achieves near-linear speedup until $L < N_{gpu}$ imbalance.

> Theorem 4.4 (Bootstrapping Placement Optimality): For monotonic error growth $e(l)$, optimal bootstrapping interval $k^*$ minimizing $T_{total}$ subject to $e(k)\leq e_{max}$ satisfies $k^* = \arg\min_k (T_{layer}k + T_{bs}) / k$.

## 6 Limitations, Failure Modes, and Open Problems

1. **Distribution shift of noise** – Homomorphic sine approximation assumes $|x|<Kq$ bounded by $20q$; adversarial ciphertexts may exceed, causing wrapping failure prob $≈2^{-30}$ → needs range check [2].
2. **Cryo reality** – SFQ 34 GHz assumes 4K operation; refrigeration cost >200 W per watt at 4K, system-level energy not 90× once cooler included [3]; flux trapping yield < 90% for 100k JJ.
3. **RNS composition overhead** – $t=2$ doubles number of limbs managed, increasing key-switch keys size 2× and memory pressure on 48 GB GPU for $N=2^{17}$.
4. **Side-channel** – Timing via NTT data-dependent early exit on zero coefficients leaks plaintext weight; constant-time butterfly requires removing branch but 12% slowdown [5].
5. **Bootstrapping as impulsive disturbance** – Control-theoretic model shows bootstrapping resets level but adds bias $b_{bs}$ reducing closed-loop stability margin for encrypted control [2]; periodic bootstrapping bound spectral radius requirement $\rho(A_{cl})<1/(1+\epsilon_{bs})$.
6. **Verification scalability** – JoSIM analog simulation of NTT-128 7-PE scales $O(N\log N)$ nodes 5k JJs but for $N=2^{14}$ Sys 112k JJs exceeds simulator memory.

| Limitation | Mitigation | Hardness |
|---|---|---|
| EvalMod range overflow | SPRU native embedding reduces range but not eliminate; dynamic K-adapt | Medium |
| Cryogenic cost | Hybrid CMOS room-temp NTU + SFQ accelerators for hot NTT | High |
| RNS limb bloat | CipherAPR degree-adapt reduces bootstrap count itself 1.3× | Medium-High |
| Side-channel | Fully constant-time Shoup + masked twiddles | Medium |
