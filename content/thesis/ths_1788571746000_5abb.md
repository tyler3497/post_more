# Photonic Neural Network Accelerators: Mach–Zehnder Meshes, Microring Weight Banks, and Phase-Change Memory for Optical MAC Operations

## Abstract

Deep neural networks are dominated by multiply–accumulate (MAC) operations whose energy and latency are increasingly set by memory movement rather than arithmetic in electronic accelerators. Silicon photonics offers a fundamentally different trade: passive optical interference computes matrix–vector products in the time of flight of light through a chip, with weights held by phase shifters, microring resonators, or nonvolatile phase-change materials. This thesis develops the theory and practice of photonic MAC arrays across three canonical architectures — coherent Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes embedding unitary matrices via Reck and Clements decompositions, microring-resonator wavelength-division-multiplexed (WDM) weight banks implementing broadcast-and-weight neurons, and phase-change-material synapses enabling zero-static-power inference. We compare electro-optic and thermo-optic tuning, derive shot-noise and crosstalk limits on effective precision, and benchmark landmark demonstrations — Shen et al.'s nanophotonic vowel recognizer, Feldmann's all-optical spiking network, Xu et al.'s 11-TOPS accelerator — against electronic MAC arrays. Photonic accelerators win only inside a bounded envelope: moderate precision (4–8 bits), large vector dimensions, and stationary weights.

## 1. Introduction

The computational core of deep learning is the *general matrix multiply* (GEMM). For a dense layer **y** = **Wx** + **b**, the 2*mn* multiply–accumulate operations are dwarfed in energy cost by fetching **W** from memory — often 100× the energy of the arithmetic itself [2]. This *von Neumann bottleneck* is the architectural fact that neuromorphic photonics attacks directly.

Optics offers two intrinsic gifts for MAC arithmetic. First, **interference is multiplication by construction**: a coherent field traversing a beamsplitter network undergoes its linear transformation passively, at light speed, with no per-operation energy beyond the light itself [1]. Second, **wavelength-division multiplexing** supplies an orthogonal parallelism axis: dozens of independent streams coexist on one waveguide and are weighted simultaneously by wavelength-selective elements [4].

The field has converged on three silicon-photonic MAC architectures, each exploiting a different optical degree of freedom:

1. **Coherent MZI meshes** — networks of Mach–Zehnder interferometers synthesizing arbitrary complex unitary matrices via Reck or Clements decompositions of the SVD **W** = **UΣV†** [1].
2. **Microring-resonator (MRR) weight banks** — WDM "broadcast-and-weight" neurons where each wavelength carries one input and a bank of rings applies one weight per channel, summed in balanced photodetectors [4].
3. **Phase-change-material (PCM) synapses** — nonvolatile weights written into the optical loss of a waveguide segment coated with chalcogenide glass [3].

> **Thesis claim:** Silicon-photonic MAC arrays deliver a genuine energy and throughput advantage over electronic MAC arrays, but only inside a well-defined operating envelope bounded by achievable effective precision, weight stationarity, and optical loss per cascade stage.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The electronic MAC baseline

A digital 8-bit MAC in sub-10 nm CMOS costs tens of femtojoules, but the *system* energy per MAC in a GPU running a large GEMM is typically several picojoules once DRAM/SRAM traffic is included [2]. TPU-class systolic arrays reach ~100+ TOPS at 1–3 TOPS/W (8-bit) via weight-stationary dataflow. Any photonic competitor must therefore clear two bars: **throughput per unit area** and **system energy per MAC including lasers, detectors, modulators, and control electronics** — not just the optical core.

### 2.2 Why silicon photonics

Silicon photonics builds waveguides, modulators, and detectors in CMOS-compatible foundries, enabling monolithic co-integration with control electronics [2]. Key enablers: low-loss waveguides (~1–2 dB/cm in the C-band); carrier-depletion modulators exceeding 50 GHz; germanium photodetectors above 60 GHz; and dense WDM via microrings with quality factors over 10⁴.

> **Definition:** An *optical MAC array* is a photonic circuit whose steady-state transfer function implements **y** = **Wx**, with inputs **x** encoded as optical amplitudes, phases, or intensities and weights **W** encoded as interferometer phases, resonator detunings, or material-loss states.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis proceeds by constructive analysis:

1. **Transfer-matrix derivation** of the 2×2 MZI, with a proof sketch that Reck's triangular and Clements' rectangular decompositions embed any *N*×*N* unitary in *N*(*N*−1)/2 MZIs.
2. **Comparative architecture analysis** of MZI meshes, MRR weight banks, and PCM synapses along six axes: volatility, tuning bandwidth, encoding domain, sign representation, scalability, and precision.
3. **Noise-budget derivation** for effective number of bits (ENOB), combining shot noise, amplifier thermal noise, laser RIN, and crosstalk from coherent leakage and thermal drift.
4. **Empirical grounding** in published results: Shen et al. [1], Feldmann et al. [3], Xu et al. [5], Feldmann et al. (tensor core) [6], Tait et al. [4].
5. **Numerical experiment**: a Python simulation of a Clements-style MZI mesh verifying that phase-error injection reproduces the analytic ENOB bounds of Section 4.4.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mach–Zehnder interferometer meshes for unitary matrix multiplication

A 2×2 MZI comprises two 50:50 directional couplers separated by an internal phase shifter θ, plus an external phase shifter φ on one output arm. Its transfer matrix is

$$T(\theta, \phi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2) & e^{i\phi}\cos(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix}$$

up to global-phase conventions. Sweeping θ from 0 to π moves the splitting ratio continuously from cross to bar state; φ supplies the independent output phase required for universality.

> **Theorem (Reck 1994; Clements 2016):** Any *N*×*N* unitary **U** decomposes into a product of *N*(*N*−1)/2 two-mode transformations *T(θ, φ)*, each realizable by one MZI. Reck arranges them triangularly; Clements arranges them in a balanced rectangle with roughly half the optical depth.

The neural-network connection runs through the SVD: **W** = **UΣV†**, with **U**, **V** unitary and **Σ** diagonal. The photonic pipeline is therefore an MZI mesh for **V†**, a diagonal attenuation stage for **Σ**, and a second mesh for **U** [1]. Inputs are encoded as coherent field amplitudes on *N* waveguide modes; the product emerges at the output ports after tens of picoseconds of propagation. Shen et al. built exactly this with 56 programmable MZIs, programming phases from digital training to classify four vowel phonemes at 76.7% accuracy versus ~91.7% for a 64-bit digital reference [1].

The Clements rectangle matters because **optical depth is the master resource constraint**. Every MZI contributes insertion loss (0.1–0.5 dB) and phase error from fabrication variation and thermal drift. The Reck triangle forces an asymmetric depth — the deepest mode traverses 2*N*−3 MZIs — so the worst path dominates output SNR. The Clements rectangle balances all paths to ≈ *N* MZIs and is provably more robust to beam-splitter errors, making it the default topology in subsequent work.

```python
import numpy as np

def mzi(theta, phi):
    """2x2 Mach-Zehnder transfer matrix: internal phase theta, external phi."""
    s, c = np.sin(theta / 2.0), np.cos(theta / 2.0)
    return np.array([[np.exp(1j * phi) * s, np.exp(1j * phi) * c],
                     [c, -s]], dtype=complex)

def clements_4x4(thetas, phis):
    """Clements rectangular mesh, N=4: 6 MZIs acting on 4 modes."""
    assert len(thetas) == len(phis) == 6
    pairs = [(0, 1), (2, 3), (1, 2), (0, 1), (2, 3), (1, 2)]
    U = np.eye(4, dtype=complex)
    for (i, j), th, ph in zip(pairs, thetas, phis):
        T = np.eye(4, dtype=complex)
        T[np.ix_([i, j], [i, j])] = mzi(th, ph)
        U = T @ U
    return U

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
A = rng.standard_normal((4, 4)) + 1j * rng.standard_normal((4, 4))
U_target, _, _ = np.linalg.svd(A)          # e.g. the U factor of a weight SVD

prog = rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, 6), rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, 6)
U_mesh = clements_4x4(*prog)               # phases from Reck/Clements nulling
x = rng.standard_normal(4)
y_opt = U_mesh @ x                         # optical MAC: O(1) time in N

noisy = clements_4x4(prog[0] + rng.normal(0, 0.02, 6),
                     prog[1] + rng.normal(0, 0.02, 6))
err = np.linalg.norm(noisy @ x - U_mesh @ x) / np.linalg.norm(U_mesh @ x)
print(f"relative error from 0.02-rad phase noise: {err:.4f}")
print(f"effective bits lost: {-np.log2(err):.2f}")
```

The lesson: output error grows with mesh depth and per-element phase uncertainty, and every factor of two in error costs one bit of effective precision — the mechanism quantified in Section 4.4.

### 4.2 Microring-resonator WDM weight banks: the broadcast-and-weight protocol

Where MZI meshes exploit *coherence*, the Princeton broadcast-and-weight architecture exploits *wavelength parallelism* [4]:

1. **Broadcast** — all neuron outputs are multiplexed onto one waveguide, each on its own wavelength λᵢ.
2. **Weight** — at each receiving neuron, a *weight bank* of microrings taps the bus. Ring *i* is tuned slightly off λᵢ; combined with a balanced photodetector pair (through-port minus drop-port), the drop transmission implements a continuous weight *wᵢ* ∈ [−1, 1] [4].
3. **Sum and activate** — the photocurrent is Σᵢ *wᵢxᵢ*; a modulator or laser neuron applies the nonlinearity.

Tait et al. proved a strict mathematical isomorphism between this circuit and a continuous-time recurrent neural network — "neuromorphic" in the rigorous sense — demonstrated single-node bistability and two-node Hopf oscillation experimentally, and projected a **294× acceleration** over a CPU for a 24-node CTRNN differential-equation benchmark at ~106 mW system power, dominated by static ring tuning and pump lasers [4].

The weight bank's strengths are *fan-in* and *speed*: one bank sums dozens of WDM channels at photodetector bandwidth (tens of GHz), and many neurons share a single broadcast bus. Its characteristic weakness is **thermal crosstalk**: microheater heat spreads to neighboring rings, and each weight demands sub-picometer detuning accuracy. Feedback-stabilized rings have demonstrated ~5.1-bit weight accuracy [2] — the practical precision ceiling of the approach.

| Axis | MZI mesh (coherent) | MRR weight bank (WDM) | PCM synapse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight storage | Volatile (heater/DAC-held phase) | Volatile (heater-held detuning) | **Nonvolatile** (material phase) |
| Tuning bandwidth | kHz (thermo-optic) – GHz (electro-optic) | kHz (thermal) | Write ns–µs; read passive |
| Encoding | Complex field amplitude | Optical power per λ | Waveguide transmission |
| Negative weights | Unitary phase (free) | Balanced detection (through − drop) | Differential PCM cell pair |
| Fan-in | *N* (mesh width) | WDM channels (32–100+) | Wavelengths / spatial modes |
| Typical precision | 4–6 bits ENOB | ~5 bits | 4–5 bits (drift-limited) |
| Static power | ~mW per MZI heater | ~mW per ring heater | ≈ 0 |

### 4.3 Phase-change materials: nonvolatile photonic weights

Chalcogenides such as Ge₂Sb₂Te₅ (GST) and low-loss GSST switch reversibly between amorphous and crystalline states with large contrast in refractive index and absorption. A thin PCM patch on a silicon waveguide modulates modal transmission; partial crystallization yields **multilevel analog weights that persist with zero holding power** [3].

Feldmann et al. built an all-optical spiking neurosynaptic network on this principle — PCM synapses joining waveguide-integrated phase-change neurons, with WDM for scalability — and demonstrated supervised and unsupervised pattern recognition directly in the optical domain [3]. The platform scaled to the **integrated photonic tensor core** (2021): tera-MAC/s parallel convolution at >14 GHz bandwidth [6]. The decisive advantage is *weight stationarity without energy*: for inference, where weights are written once and read billions of times, PCM eliminates the static tuning power that otherwise dominates thermo-optic MZI and MRR arrays.

Two caveats are load-bearing. First, **write cost**: amorphization means locally melting the material (~600 °C), so frequent updates during training are energetically expensive and wear the cell (endurance ~10⁶–10⁹ cycles). PCM photonics is therefore inference-first; training stays electronic or in-situ optical. Second, **drift**: the amorphous state relaxes structurally over time, compressing multilevel spacing — the photonic analogue of drift in electronic PCM. Demonstrated multilevel precision remains ≈ 4–5 bits.

### 4.4 Tuning mechanisms, shot noise, and precision limits

**Electro-optic vs. thermo-optic tuning.** Silicon is centrosymmetric, so "electro-optic" tuning means the *plasma dispersion effect*: carrier depletion in a p–n junction shifts the index at tens of GHz, but Δn ~ 10⁻⁴ demands millimeter phase shifters or resonant enhancement plus free-carrier loss. **Thermo-optic** tuning via microheaters exploits silicon's large dn/dT ≈ 1.8×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ for full 2π shifts in compact devices — at ~10–30 mW per π and kilohertz bandwidths, with neighbor crosstalk. The industry compromise is now standard: thermo-optic (or MEMS) for slow weight programming, electro-optic or thin-film lithium niobate for fast input modulation [2] — matching the workload reality that *inputs change every cycle* while *weights change rarely*.

**Shot noise and the ENOB ceiling.** For one neuron output receiving optical power *P* over integration time *T*, detected photons *M* = η*PT*/*hν* give shot-noise SNR √*M* and

$$\mathrm{ENOB} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2(M) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{th}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{RIN}}}{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{shot}}}\right) - \log_2(\kappa)$$

with κ ≥ 1 for coherent crosstalk and phase-error accumulation through mesh depth. The brutal consequence: **each additional bit of precision demands 4× the optical energy per MAC**. Reaching 8-bit ENOB needs ~2¹⁶ photons per MAC per output — at 10 GHz, milliwatts of optical power per neuron before wall-plug laser efficiency (~5–20%) and splitting losses. This is why every serious demonstration operates at 4–8 bits [1][4][5]: physics, not engineering, sets the ceiling. Quantization-aware training recovers most of the accuracy lost to 4–6 bit weights, which is why the ceiling is tolerable rather than fatal.

**Error accumulation in deep meshes.** In a width-*N* Clements mesh each output traverses ~*N* MZIs. Independent per-MZI fractional error ε (1–3% beam-splitter imbalance) gives √*N*·ε output error; correlated phase drift (e.g., a temperature excursion) can reach *N*·ε. Wilkes et al.'s 60 dB-extinction auto-configured MZI shows local feedback can suppress static errors [2] — but feedback consumes control bandwidth and power that honest energy accounting must include.

> **Theorem (precision–energy scaling):** For a shot-noise-limited photonic MAC array, energy per MAC scales as 4^{ENOB}; electronic digital MAC energy scales roughly as (bit-width)². The photonic advantage therefore *widens* at low precision and *narrows* at high precision — the crossover for dense GEMM lies near 6–8 bits under realistic loss.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 What has actually been demonstrated

| Demonstration | Architecture | Key result | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shen et al., MIT [1] | 56-MZI coherent mesh, SVD-mapped | Vowel recognition, 76.7% (digital ref ~91.7%) | 2017 |
| Tait et al., Princeton [4] | MRR broadcast-and-weight CTRNN | Bistability/Hopf; 294× projected speedup, 106 mW | 2017 |
| Feldmann et al. [3] | PCM all-optical spiking network | Supervised + unsupervised learning, optical domain | 2019 |
| Feldmann et al., tensor core [6] | PCM photonic tensor core | Tera-MAC/s, >14 GHz, parallel convolution | 2021 |
| Xu et al. [5] | Microcomb WDM vector convolver | **>10 TOPS**, 250k-pixel convolutions, 88% digit accuracy, 8-bit | 2021 |

Xu et al. is the throughput landmark: interleaving temporal, wavelength, and spatial dimensions with an integrated Kerr microcomb, the accelerator sustains more than ten tera-operations per second — competitive with datacenter GPUs at 8-bit resolution [5]. Feldmann's tensor core complements it on the weight-stationary axis: PCM holds the kernel at zero static power while >14 GHz electro-optic bandwidth streams input data [6].

### 5.2 Comparison with electronic MAC arrays

For a dense 1024×1024 GEMM tile, the workhorse of transformer inference:

- **Electronic (TPU-class systolic array):** ~100–300 TOPS at 1–3 TOPS/W (8-bit), weight-stationary, arbitrary precision via digital accumulation, mature software. Energy dominated by SRAM/DRAM movement.
- **Coherent MZI mesh:** matrix–vector latency ≈ time of flight (~100 ps) — effectively *O(1)* in *N* versus *O(N)* systolic. Outstanding throughput per area *if* precision ≤ 6 bits and weights are stationary. But ~10⁶ MZIs each need a DAC and thermal stabilization; ~1024 stages at 0.1 dB/stage is physically prohibitive monolithically; and DAC/ADC conversion at the boundary dominates power at high bandwidth.
- **MRR weight bank:** 32–100-channel WDM fan-in at GHz rates, but thermo-optic tuning power and crosstalk cap bank sizes; system power is dominated by tuning and lasers, not the "free" optical MAC [4].
- **PCM tensor core:** zero static weight power with demonstrated tera-MAC/s [6]; write energy/endurance confines it to inference; drift caps precision near 4–5 bits.

The honest summary is a **conditional victory**: photonics wins the *throughput × energy* product for large, low-precision, weight-stationary linear algebra — the inner loop of inference — while electronics retains generality, precision, programmability, and the software ecosystem. The industry bet (e.g., Lightmatter's 3D-stacked processors) is that the inference inner loop is a large enough market; peer-reviewed evidence supports the physics but not yet the datacenter-scale economics.

### 5.3 Proof sketch: mesh universality implies GEMM completeness

*Claim.* Two Clements meshes plus a diagonal modulator stage implement any real **y** = **Wx**.

*Proof.* By SVD, **W** = **UΣV†** with **U**, **V** unitary and **Σ** real diagonal. By the Reck/Clements theorem, each unitary factor is a product of *N*(*N*−1)/2 MZI transfer matrices with suitable phases. The diagonal **Σ** is realized by per-mode modulators set to the singular values, normalized to the largest, with overall scale absorbed into laser power. Cascading yields transfer function **UΣV†** = **W** up to a known scalar. ∎

Programming a photonic network thus reduces to offline SVDs and phase loading — which is why Shen et al. trained digitally and transferred weights [1], and why in-situ backpropagation (Hughes et al., *Optica* 2018) remains an active frontier for closing the training loop optically.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Precision ceiling.** Shot noise, thermal crosstalk, drift, and coherent error accumulation bound demonstrated systems to ~4–8 effective bits; FP16/FP32 training and scientific computing remain out of reach for analog photonics.
2. **Loss-limited depth.** ~0.1–0.5 dB per MZI and ~1–2 dB/cm waveguide loss cap practical mesh widths at tens to low hundreds of modes without in-line amplification (which adds noise and power).
3. **Control overhead.** A 1024×1024 mesh needs ~10⁶ phase shifters with DACs, stabilization, and per-chip calibration against fabrication variation — a control plane whose area and power can exceed the optical core's, and which headline efficiency claims routinely omit.
4. **Nonlinearity and cascadability.** Linear optics does the MAC; the neuron needs a nonlinearity, and all-optical options are weak — most demonstrations pay the ADC/DAC toll at every layer via optoelectronic conversion.
5. **Volatility vs. write cost.** Thermo-optic/electro-optic weights are volatile and burn static power; PCM weights are nonvolatile but costly to rewrite and drift. No technology yet offers fast, nonvolatile, low-energy, high-endurance updates.
6. **Benchmark honesty.** Photonic TOPS/W figures frequently exclude laser wall-plug power, control electronics, and ADC/DAC energy. System-level energy per MAC is the only fair metric, and there the photonic advantage is real but far narrower than press releases suggest.

## 7. Conclusion

Silicon photonics reframes neural-network acceleration as *analog linear optics*: Mach–Zehnder meshes embed unitary matrices through SVD-derived decompositions, microring weight banks exploit WDM parallelism for broadcast-and-weight summation, and phase-change materials provide nonvolatile storage that eliminates static tuning power. The experimental record — Shen et al.'s 56-MZI vowel recognizer [1], Tait et al.'s neuromorphic isomorphism [4], Feldmann et al.'s all-optical spiking network [3], the 11-TOPS microcomb accelerator [5], and the PCM tensor core [6] — shows the core physics works and the advantages are real within their envelope.

That envelope is this thesis's central finding: **photonic MAC arrays beat electronic MAC arrays for large, low-precision (4–8 bit), weight-stationary matrix algebra, and lose outside it.** The 4^{ENOB} shot-noise energy scaling, loss-limited mesh depth, the volatility–endurance trade-off, and control-plane overhead jointly bound the region where optics wins decisively. The credible near-term trajectory is therefore the *photonic inner loop*: optical GEMM tiles for inference, trained electronically, with quantization-aware algorithms co-designed around 4–6 bit precision and PCM-held weights. Within that envelope, computing with light is not a relic of the 1980s optical-computing winter — it is the most energy-efficient known way to multiply a matrix by a vector.
