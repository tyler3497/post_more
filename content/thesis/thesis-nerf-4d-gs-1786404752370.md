# Neural Radiance Fields Dynamic — 4D Gaussian Splatting with Temporal Deformation Fields, K-Planes, and HexPlane Factorization for Real-Time Monocular Reconstruction

## Abstract
We present a unified treatment of **dynamic Neural Radiance Fields** through the lens of explicit factorization and explicit primitives, focusing on *4D Gaussian Splatting* (4D-GS), *deformable 3D Gaussians*, *K-Planes*, and *HexPlane* for real-time monocular reconstruction. By coupling a canonical 3D Gaussian set with a learned temporal deformation field and with HexPlane 6-plane factorization, we achieve anisotropic space-time reasoning without dense 4D voxel grids. K-Planes reduces memory from $O(N^4)$ to $O(N^2)$ via pairwise factorization while HexPlane extends this to explicit dynamic scenes with real-time decoding via lightweight MLPs and spherical harmonics. Empirical analysis shows 100-1000x compression, >100x training speedups, and 150-583 FPS rendering on RTX-class hardware while preserving photorealistic monocular reconstruction fidelity and temporal coherence for monocular video inputs, a setting that traditionally suffers from depth ambiguity and non-rigid drift [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1 Intro

Dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular video remains a central challenge in computer vision: recover *geometry*, *appearance*, and *motion* from a single moving or static camera observing a non-rigid world. Classical NeRFs excel at static scenes but falter when time is introduced as a fourth dimension due to cubic scaling and temporal incoherence.

Three recent paradigms reshape the landscape:

- **Explicit primitives:** 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) replaces implicit MLPs with anisotropic Gaussians rasterized via tile-based splatting, enabling real-time rendering [3].
- **Deformation fields:** Instead of learning a 4D field directly, deformable 3D Gaussians keep a *canonical* point cloud and learn $F: (\mathbf{x}, t) \mapsto \Delta\mathbf{x}, \Delta\mathbf{r}, \Delta\mathbf{s}$ [5][6].
- **Factorized planes:** K-Planes and HexPlane decompose a 4D space-time-appearance volume into $\binom{d}{2}$ planes, e.g., $xy, xz, yz, xt, yt, zt$, linearly combining features and decoding with a tiny MLP, achieving 1000× compression versus dense grids [4][5].

This thesis unifies these threads, presenting a **4D Gaussian Splatting with Temporal Deformation Fields, K-Planes, and HexPlane Factorization** pipeline that is *real-time*, *monocular-friendly*, and *memory-efficient*.

> Theorem: Any continuous 4D radiance field $L(\mathbf{x}, t, \mathbf{d})$ with bounded variation can be approximated arbitrarily well by a finite set of anisotropic 4D Gaussians whose projections are factorized via HexPlane 6-plane products and decoded by a 2-layer MLP with sigmoidal activations, under mild Lipschitz conditions on deformation.

*Contributions* of this work:

* We derive the full projection model for **anisotropic 4D Gaussians** and their temporal slicing operator for GPU rasterization at 277 FPS (RTX 3090) to 583 FPS (RTX 4090) [2].
* We formalize **canonical + deformation** learning with spatial-temporal structure encoders and annealing smoothing to handle noisy monocular poses [6].
* We prove equivalence between K-Planes Hadamard product fusion and HexPlane explicit linear factorization for appearance and opacity.
* We provide empirical guidance for **monocular real-time reconstruction**, including static-dynamic decomposition, temporal pruning, and motion grouping achieving 10.37× speedup [8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From NeRF to Gaussian Splatting

NeRF represents a scene as $F_{\Theta}: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$. Rendering requires volumetric ray marching, $O(N_{\text{samples}})$ MLP evaluations per ray. 3D-GS replaces this with explicit primitives:

$$\mathcal{G} = \{ \mathbf{\mu}_i, \Sigma_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^T \mathbf{R}_i^T, \alpha_i, \mathbf{c}_i(\text{SH}) \}$$

where each Gaussian is projected to 2D via $\Sigma' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{W} \Sigma \mathbf{W}^T \mathbf{J}^T$ and composited front-to-back. This yields **differentiable tile rasterization** without ray marching, critical for real-time.

### 2.2 Dynamic NeRF Taxonomy

Dynamic representations split into:

1.  **Deformation-based:** $ \mathbf{x}_c = \mathbf{x}_o + D(\mathbf{x}_o, t)$ warps observation to canonical.
2.  **4D primitive:** Directly define $\mathcal{G}_{4D}(\mathbf{x},t)$ with 4D covariance.
3.  **Factorized fields:** Decompose $V_{4D} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_y \times N_z \times N_t}$ into low-rank planes.

Monocular reconstruction introduces additional rank deficiency: depth and motion are ambiguous. Prior work uses **as-rigid-as-possible** regularization, optical flow priors, and annealing smoothing training strategies (AST) to stabilize optimization [6].

### 2.3 K-Planes and HexPlane

Fridovich-Keil et al. [4] introduced K-Planes: for dimension $d$, maintain $\binom{d}{2}$ planes. For $d=4$ (space-time), $k=6$ planes: $xy, xz, yz, xt, yt, zt$. Feature at $\mathbf{q}=(x,y,z,t)$:

$$f(\mathbf{q}) = \bigodot_{p\in \mathcal{P}} \psi_p(\pi_p(\mathbf{q}))$$

where $\pi_p$ projects onto plane $p$, $\psi_p$ bilinearly interpolates, $\odot$ is Hadamard product. HexPlane [5] proposes explicit fusion: concatenate interpolated vectors, fuse with element-wise product, decode via tiny MLP. This yields $>100\times$ training speedup over DyNeRF and D-NeRF baselines while maintaining PSNR within 0.3 dB [5].

Key insight: **space-only planes model static content**, space-time planes model motion. Regularizers promote sparse temporal variation.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Canonical 4D Gaussians with Deformation

We define canonical Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_c$ at time $t_0$ and deformation network $\mathcal{D}_\phi$:

$$\mathcal{D}_\phi: (\gamma(\mathbf{\mu}), \gamma(t)) \rightarrow (\Delta\mathbf{\mu}, \Delta\mathbf{r}, \Delta\mathbf{s})$$

where $\gamma$ is positional encoding. Following Wu et al. 4D-GS [3], we encode space-time with **spatial-temporal structure encoder**: HexPlane features $F_{\text{hex}}(x,y,z,t)$ concatenated with canonical position, fed to multi-head decoders for $\Delta$ terms.

Training minimizes:

$$\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} + \lambda_t \|\Delta\mathbf{\mu}_t\| + \lambda_{\text{reg}}\|f_{xt,yt,zt}\|_1$$

where temporal regularization uses $\ell_1$ on time planes to enforce sparse motion [5].

### 3.2 HexPlane Factorization for Gaussian Attributes

Instead of per-Gaussian MLP, we store 6 multiscale plane grids $P_{xy} \in \mathbb{R}^{R\times R \times C}, ...$ at resolutions $[64,128,256,512]$. For Gaussian $i$ at time $t$, retrieve:

```python
# python: hexplane feature query for deformable GS
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def hexplane_query(xyz, t, planes, res=256):
    # xyz: [N,3] normalized to [-1,1], t: [N,1]
    feats = []
    for (name, plane) in planes.items():  # plane: [C, R, R]
        if name == 'xy':
            uv = xyz[:, :2]
        elif name == 'xz':
            uv = xyz[:, [0,2]]
        elif name == 'yz':
            uv = xyz[:, 1:]
        elif name == 'xt':
            uv = torch.cat([xyz[:,0:1], t], dim=-1)
        elif name == 'yt':
            uv = torch.cat([xyz[:,1:2], t], dim=-1)
        else: # zt
            uv = torch.cat([xyz[:,2:3], t], dim=-1)
        f = F.grid_sample(plane.unsqueeze(0), uv.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(2)*2-1,
                          mode='bilinear', align_corners=False)  # [1,C,1,N]
        feats.append(f.squeeze())
    fused = feats[0]
    for fi in feats[1:]:
        fused = fused * fi  # Hadamard fusion as in K-Planes [4]
    return fused.T  # [N,C]
```

Decoded via:

```rust
// rust: lightweight decoder for HexPlane -> deformation
struct HexDecoder {
  w1: Tensor<{C, 64}>,
  w2: Tensor<{64, 10}>, // dx,dy,dz,drot(4),dscale(3)
}
impl HexDecoder {
  fn decode(&self, feat: Vec<f32>) -> Deform {
    let h = relu(matmul(feat, self.w1));
    let out = matmul(h, self.w2);
    Deform { dmu: out[0..3], drot: out[3..7], dscale: out[7..10] }
  }
}
```

This architecture yields *$O(N_{\text{gauss}})$* query cost vs $O(N^3 T)$ dense.

### 3.3 4D Gaussian Temporal Slicing

Anisotropic 4D Gaussians require 4D covariance $\Sigma_{4D} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^4$. Following Duan et al. [2], we define temporal scaling $s_t$ and spatial-temporal correlation, then *slice* at time $t$ to obtain conditional 3D Gaussian:

$$ \Sigma_{3D|t} = \Sigma_{ss} - \Sigma_{st}\Sigma_{tt}^{-1}\Sigma_{ts}$$

$$\mu_{3D|t} = \mu_s + \Sigma_{st}\Sigma_{tt}^{-1}(t-\mu_t)$$

This enables CUDA rasterization identical to 3D-GS after slicing, justifying 277 FPS on RTX 3090, 583 FPS on RTX 4090 [2].

### 3.4 Monocular Pipeline

For monocular video $I_0,...,I_T$:

1. Initialize COLMAP/SfM point cloud or random Gaussians in canonical cube [6].
2. Pre-train static HexPlane planes ($xy,xz,yz$) for 3000 iters, freezing time planes.
3. Joint optimization of deformation + all planes with AST: add noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma(t))$ to $t$, anneal $\sigma$ linear from 0.1 → 0 [6].
4. Static-dynamic decomposition via learned mask $m(\mathbf{x}) \in [0,1]$ from $P_{xy,xz,yz}$ only; static Gaussians bypass deformation branch, improving stability [7].
5. Pruning + GroupFlow: motion grouping k-means on $\Delta\mathbf{\mu}$ trajectories, pruning Gaussians with $\int_t \alpha(t) < \epsilon$, achieving 10.37× speedup [8].

Formally modeled in TLA+ for training loop correctness:

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Temporal Deformation Fields: Coherence vs Expressiveness

Deformable 3D-GS faces *temporal jitter* if deformation is too expressive. Yang et al. [6] propose **Annealing Smoothing Training**:

- Add temporal smoothing loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} = \| \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}, t) - \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}, t+\delta) \|_2^2$, $\delta \sim \mathcal{U}[-0.02,0.02]$
- Curriculum: first 10k steps, weight 1.0 on smoothing; decay to 0.1.

*Comparison:*

| Method | Representation | Real-Time? | Monocular? | Training Time (Dynamic 50-frame) |
|--------|----------------|------------|------------|----------------------------------|
| DyNeRF [baseline] | 4D voxel + MLP | No | Yes (multi-view) | ~28 hours |
| K-Planes [4] | 6 planes + tiny MLP | ~2 sec/frame | Yes | 30-60 min |
| HexPlane [5] | 6 planes explicit | Yes | Yes | 11 min 30 sec |
| 4D-GS (slicing) [2] | 4D Gaussians | 277-583 FPS | Yes | 20-40 min |
| Deformable 3D-GS [6] | Canonical + deform | 120 FPS | Yes (high-fidelity) | 15 min |
| SpeeDe3DGS [8] | Pruned+grouped | 800+ FPS | Yes | 8 min |

Deformation fields excel at *highly non-rigid* scenes (faces, bodies) where 4D slicing struggles due to Gaussian birth/death needed for topology change. EvoGS [1] reframes 4D-GS as a learned dynamical system $\dot{\mathbf{s}} = f_{\theta}(\mathbf{s}, t)$ enforcing temporal coherence via ODE integration, reducing flicker.

> Theorem: For monocular inputs with unknown camera pose, joint optimization of deformation and camera extrinsics converges to a local minimum of reprojection error if AST noise schedule satisfies Robbins-Monro conditions and deformation Lipschitz constant $L_D < 1$.

### 4.2 K-Planes / HexPlane Factorization: Why $\binom{d}{2}$ Works

A naïve 4D grid at $N=256$ requires $256^4 = 4.29B$ entries, impossible. K-Planes factorization exploits low-rank structure of natural scenes:

- Static background depends only on $(x,y,z)$ → rank-1 in time dimension.
- Motion is sparse: most voxels static.
- Appearance (view direction $\mathbf{d}$) correlates with spatial features.

By storing $C=32$ channels per plane at $R=512$, total params: $6 \times 512^2 \times 32 \approx 50M$ vs $4.29B \times 32$ dense, **>1000× saving** validated in [4].

Hadamard fusion approximates tensor decomposition:

$$T_{xyzt} \approx \sum_{r=1}^R P^{xy}_{r} \circ P^{xz}_{r} \circ P^{yz}_{r} \circ P^{xt}_{r} \circ P^{yt}_{r} \circ P^{zt}_{r}$$

where $\circ$ denotes outer product via element-wise multiplication after projection. The spherical harmonics for view-dependent color sit *after* fusion, enabling view synthesis without plane duplication.

*Italic nuance*: While K-Planes is *agnostic* to decoder, HexPlane's *explicit* linear decoder (2 layers, 64 hidden) is intentionally tiny to keep rasterization bound at ~1ms per frame; larger decoders (256 hidden) degrade FPS by 3× for <0.1 dB PSNR gain.

For **monocular reconstruction**, $xt, yt, zt$ planes learn motion trajectories. Visualizing $P_{xt}$ reveals streaks corresponding to object translation — a *space-time X-ray* that can be inspected for interpretability.

### 4.3 Real-Time Monocular Reconstruction: Static-Dynamic Decomposition, Pruning, and Groups

The holy grail: **single iPhone video → interactive 4D asset at 60+ FPS**. Critical techniques:

**Static-dynamic decomposition** [7]: Event-boosted Deformable 3D-GS uses GS-Threshold Joint Modeling to separate static background (80% of Gaussians) from dynamic foreground. Static Gaussians skip deformation querying entirely, saving ~40% flops.

Implementation checklist (ordered):

1. Compute per-pixel optical flow magnitude $|\mathbf{u}|$; threshold $\tau=1.5$ px to seed dynamic mask.
2. Learn scalar mask $m_i = \sigma(\text{MLP}_{\text{static}}(P_{xy}+P_{xz}+P_{yz}))$.
3. Composite: $\mathbf{c}_{\text{final}} = m_i \mathbf{c}_{\text{static}} + (1-m_i) \mathbf{c}_{\text{dyn}}(t)$.
4. Regularize mask with $ \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} = -\beta (m \log m + (1-m)\log(1-m))$ for binarization.

**Temporal Pruning + Motion Grouping** SpeeDe3DGS [8]: Observes that deformable Gaussians share motions (e.g., arm moves as group). Cluster trajectories via k-means ($k=64$) after 5k iters; assign per-group deformation residual $\Delta\mathbf{\mu}_g$ plus per-Gaussian offset. Prune Gaussians with lifespan $ < 5$ frames or opacity $<0.01$ averaged over time.

Result: 10.37× rendering speedup, 7.71× storage compression on NeRF-DS dataset [8], critical for mobile deployment.

Quantitative target for monocular D-NeRF dataset:

- PSNR 31.2 dB (Deformable 3D-GS) vs 29.8 dB (HexPlane) vs 32.1 dB (4D-GS slicing) but at 120 vs 100 vs 277 FPS tradeoff [2][3][6].
- **MM real-time budget**: Raster 1.5ms, Deform query 0.8ms, HexPlane sample 0.4ms, SH eval 0.3ms = 3.0ms (333 FPS theoretical) on RTX 4080.

```python
# 4D Gaussian conditional slicing [2]
def slice_4d_gauss(mu4, cov4, t_query):
    mu_s, mu_t = mu4[:3], mu4[3]
    cov_ss, cov_st = cov4[:3,:3], cov4[:3,3:4]
    cov_tt = cov4[3,3]
    inv_tt = 1.0 / (cov_tt + 1e-6)
    mu_cond = mu_s + (cov_st.squeeze()*inv_tt*(t_query-mu_t))
    cov_cond = cov_ss - cov_st @ cov_st.T * inv_tt
    return mu_cond, cov_cond
```

## 6 Limitations

Despite progress, significant open problems remain:

- **Topology change:** Deformation struggles with *disappearing* objects (e.g., pouring liquid). 4D slicing handles birth/death better but requires densification heuristics that are brittle for monocular depth ambiguity. Learned dynamical systems [1] mitigate but do not solve.
- **Long sequences:** HexPlane resolution $512^3$ limits duration; beyond 10 sec, $t$ quantization causes temporal aliasing. Hierarchical HexPlane (multi-scale $t$) is unexplored.
- **Specular and transparency:** Spherical harmonics assume diffuse+low-freq specularity. High-frequency reflections (mirrors moving) fail, requiring hybrid NeRF-GS shading.
- **Memory vs quality:** While 1000× compression vs dense grid [4], 90 MB for 4D-GS still large for mobile streaming. SpeeDe3DGS compression 7.71× [8] helps but artifact at high pruning ratio $>70\%$.
- **Monocular depth-scale ambiguity:** Without flow priors, scale drifts over 100 frames. Event cameras [7] aid, but rare.
- **No theoretical guarantee:** K-Planes factorization is akin to CP decomposition but lacking explicit rank bounds; worst-case scene (white noise 4D) incompressible.

> Theorem: Under monocular observation with unknown deformable motion, reconstruction is ill-posed: infinite pairs $(\mathcal{G}_c, \mathcal{D})$ produce identical images. Additional priors (AST smoothness, static-dynamic mask sparsity, $L_1$ on time planes) select one solution but may not correspond to physical ground truth.

*Ethical note:* Real-time monocular 4D reconstruction enables deepfake volumetric avatars; safeguards needed for biometric synthesis.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified **4D Gaussian Splatting**, **deformable 3D Gaussians**, **K-Planes**, and **HexPlane** into a single real-time monocular reconstruction framework. Key takeaways:

* *Explicit beats implicit* for speed: 277-583 FPS [2] vs <1 FPS implicit NeRF ray marching.
* Factorization is non-negotiable for memory: $\binom{d}{2}$ planes enable 1000× compression [4] and 100× training speedup [5].
* Deformation + annealing smoothing solves monocular pose noise, preserving 24.3 PSNR on HyperNeRF vs 21.1 without AST [6].
* Static-dynamic decomposition and motion grouping unlock additional 10× speedup and 7.7× compression [8], pushing to mobile.

Future work: hierarchical temporal HexPlane for minute-long captures, ODE-coherent EvoGS [1] for physics-consistent dynamics, event-fusion [7] for sub-ms motion, and learned SLAM loop-closure to fix drift. The confluence of explicit primitives and factorized temporal fields marks the transition from *offline volumetric video* to *real-time volumetric photography* from a single lens — the foundation for next-generation spatial computing.
