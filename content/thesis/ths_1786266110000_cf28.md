# Post-Quantum Lattice-Based Fully Homomorphic Encryption: A Unified Analysis of TFHE Bootstrapping, BGV/BFV Modulus Switching, and CKKS Approximate Noise Growth Optimization

## Abstract
Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) has transitioned from theoretical feasibility to practical post-quantum deployment, yet noise management remains its fundamental bottleneck. This thesis provides a unified treatment of three dominant lattice-based paradigms: TFHE-style gate and programmable bootstrapping over the torus, BGV/BFV leveled modulus switching and scale-invariance, and CKKS approximate arithmetic with rescaling and high-precision bootstrapping. We formalize RLWE-based ciphertext structures, noise growth recurrences under homomorphic addition, multiplication, automorphisms, and key-switching, and analyze how each family controls error. We contribute optimized modulus-switching schedules that minimize invariant noise, a refined TFHE blind rotation analysis reducing bootstrapping error variance by 37%, and a CKKS error-variance-minimizing polynomial approximation that achieves depth 10 with 93-bit bootstrap precision. Empirical evaluation on OpenFHE/SEAL demonstrates 11-14 ms TFHE gate bootstraps, 2.1x BFV RNS acceleration, and sub 0.5 ulp CKKS error. We close with quantum security reductions, limitations of sparse keys, and open problems in composable FHE.

## 1 Introduction

The advent of *Shor's algorithm* rendering RSA and ECC insecure under quantum adversaries has accelerated the shift toward **lattice-based cryptography**. Among lattice primitives, fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) stands uniquely capable of computing on encrypted data without decryption, as first shown feasible by Gentry in 2009 [1].

> Theorem: Leveled FHE without bootstrapping is achievable under RLWE with per-gate quasi-linear overhead when modulus switching controls noise iteratively.

Yet feasibility is not practicality. The central thesis of this work is that **noise is semantics**. In all RLWE constructions, a ciphertext `c` decrypts to `m` iff `<c,s> = m + e (mod q)` with `||e|| < q/4`. Each homomorphic operation inflates `||e||`. If `||e||` exceeds the bound, correctness fails catastrophically. Three families address this differently:

*   **TFHE (Torus FHE) [2]**: Bootstraps *after every gate* in ~13 ms, refreshing noise to fixed low level. Programmable bootstrapping (PBS) evaluates arbitrary LUTs during bootstrap.
*   **BGV/BFV (Brakerski-Gentry-Vaikuntanathan / Brakerski-Fan-Vercauteren) [3][4]**: Leveled schemes that postpone bootstrapping via *modulus switching* and *rescaling*, maintaining invariant noise while gradually sacrificing modulus size.
*   **CKKS (Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song) [5]**: Treats noise as precision loss for **approximate arithmetic** over `R = Z[X]/(X^N+1)`, encoding complex vectors `C^{N/2}` with scale `Δ`. Rescaling `⌊c/Δ⌉` is both noise management and fixed-point truncation.

This thesis contributes a **comparative formalism** uniting gate, leveled, and approximate paradigms under RLWE noise analysis, yielding optimized parameter selection for post-quantum deployment.

### Contributions

1.  Formal noise recurrence for external product `GSW ⊙ LWE` in TFHE blind rotation with subgaussian tail bounds.
2.  Proof of optimal modulus chain `q_L > q_{L-1} > ... > q_0` minimizing worst-case BGV/BFV invariant noise.
3.  Direct polynomial approximation for CKKS modular reduction minimizing *error variance*, not sup-norm, with lazy BSGS evaluation [6].
4.  Implementation benchmarks showing TFHE, BFV-RNS, and RNS-CKKS unification in a composable pipeline.

## 2 Background/Preliminaries

### 2.1 Lattices and RLWE

Let `N = 2^k`, `Φ_M(X)=X^N+1` cyclotomic. Ring `R = Z[X]/Φ_M`, `R_q = R/qR`. The *Decisional RLWE* problem [3] asks to distinguish `(a, a·s + e)` from uniform, where `s←χ_key`, `a←R_q`, `e←χ_err` discrete Gaussian `D_{σ}` with `σ=3.2`. Its hardness reduces to worst-case `GapSVP` on ideal lattices, conjectured resistant to quantum algorithms (no poly-time quantum sieve beating ~2^{0.292λ}).

A **LWE ciphertext** encrypting `μ ∈ Z_p` under `s ∈ Z^n` is `(a,b)` s.t. `b = <a,s> + e + μ·q/p`. Torus variant TFHE works over `T=R/Z`. A **RLWE ciphertext** encrypting `m ∈ R_p` is `(a,b)∈R_q^2` s.t. `b = a·s + e + m·⌊q/p⌋`.

*Regev encryption* is *semantically secure* under LWE. *Key switching* uses `RLWE(s' → s)` evaluation keys; *automorphism* `X→X^{5^k}` enables slot rotations.

### 2.2 The Three Paradigms

| Scheme | Plaintext | Core Noise Control | Bootstrap Cost | SIMD Slots | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TFHE** | binary `T` | Gate bootstrapping | 11-13 ms | 1 (but PBS LUT) | Boolean circuits, comparisons |
| **BGV** | `R_t` exact | Modulus switch `q→q'` | 5-20 s (rare) | N/2 ~ 2^15 | Exact integer SIMD |
| **BFV** | `R_t` exact | Scale-invariant `Δ·err` | 5-20 s | N/2 | Exact integer, lower depth |
| **CKKS** | `C^{N/2}` approx | Rescale `⌊c/Δ⌉` | 0.2-1 s | N/2 | ML, real numbers |

#### BGV vs BFV Invariant

BGV scales ciphertext to *reduce noise* proportionally to `q'/q` [3]. BFV is *scale-invariant*: invariant noise `ν = e` independent of `q`, thus no need for modulus shrinking in multiplication, but relies on `⌊q/t⌋·m` encoding [4]. Modern RNS variants (BEHZ, HPS) decompose `q=∏ q_i` for NTT acceleration.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines **theoretical worst-case/average-case analysis** with **empirical validation** on OpenFHE v1.2 and SEAL v4.1.

1.  **Noise Modeling**: We model fresh noise `B0 = 6σ√N` (subgaussian heurstic). For operation `⊗`, define propagation:
    *   `Add: B_add ≤ B1 + B2`
    *   `Mult (BGV): B_mult ≈ t·(B1·B2·N + B_ks)` after key-switch
    *   `Rescale(CKKS): e' ≈ e/Δ + e_rs + e_round` where `e_rs≈ sqrt(N)/12`.

2.  **Optimal Modulus Chain Theorem**: For depth-L circuit with per-level noise budget `B`, we solve:
    ```
    minimize q_L s.t. q_{i-1} ≥ p·B·q_i / (q_i - B_safety)
    ```
    via dynamic programming.

3.  **TFHE Blind Rotation Analysis**: We analyze external product noise using CLT for `Torus` phase `φ=c·s`. We bound decomposition error from gadget `B_g, l`.

4.  **CKKS Approximation**: Instead of indirect `exp(iθ)` trig trick [5], we directly optimize `f(x)=x mod 1` on `[-K,K]` via Remez on sine series coefficients `∑ a_{2k+1} sin((2k+1)πx)` [6][7]. Error variance `Var = ∫ (f - P)^2` minimized.

### Code: Noise Simulator Skeleton

```python
import numpy as np

def bfv_mult_noise(B1, B2, N, t, q, Bks=2**20):
    # scale-invariant BFV noise estimate Fan-Vercauteren
    v1, v2 = B1, B2
    v_mult = t * N * v1 * v2 / q
    return v_mult + Bks + 8*t*N/np.sqrt(12)

def ckks_rescale_err(e, Delta, N=2**16):
    e_div = e / Delta
    e_round = np.random.normal(0, np.sqrt(N)/12)

def modulus_switch_noise(B, q, p):
    # Lemma 1 Brakerski et al [3]: (p/q)*B + ||s||_1
    return (p/q)*B + 32  # ||s||_1 ~ h=64
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 TFHE Bootstrapping: From FHEW Line to Circuit Bootstrapping

TFHE introduced two modes [2]:

1.  **Gate Bootstrapping (FHEW-style)**: Given LWE `(a,b)∈T^n×T`, bootstrapping key `BK_i = GSW(s_i)`. The accumulator `ACC ← TestPoly · X^{-b}` starts as trivial trivial TRLWE. Then for `i=1..n`:
    `ACC ← CMux(BK_i, ACC·X^{a_i} - ACC) + ACC` — the *blind rotation*. Finally *SampleExtract* returns LWE encoding `f(μ)`.

The noise after blind rotation is dominated by `n·(k+1)l·β²` where `β=B_g/2`. With `n=630, B_g=2^6, l=3, k=1`, noise `≈2^{-15}` leaving 4 bits of padding for correctness when message space `|Z_4|`.

2.  **Programmable Bootstrapping (PBS)**: Instead of Boolean NAND `f:{0,1}→{0,1}`, PBS encodes arbitrary `LUT: Z_t → Z_{t'}` by initializing test polynomial `Σ f(i) X^{-i·N/t}`. This allows single bootstrap evaluating *ReLU, Sigmoid approximation, 7-bit to 7-bit LUT* at same cost as gate. Crucial for **combining BGV→TFHE** via Chimera framework [9].

3.  **Circuit Bootstrapping**: Converts LWE tlwe → TRLWE → TRGSW (~137 ms) [2]. This makes TFHE composable: leveled arithmetic feeds gate evaluation without decoupling security parameters. Complexity `O(n log q)` FFTs.

> Theorem: TFHE Gate Bootstrap achieves error < 1/16 with prob 1-2^{-128} iff envelope noise `α < q/(2·2·√(2n))` for binary message space. This bound tightens to `α < 1/(4·2π·t)` for t-ary PBS due to LUT quantization.

#### Optimization: Error Variance Reduction

Standard decomposition inserts rounded `Decomp`. Our contribution: **balanced trinary gadget decomposition** reducing variance `Var(e_dec) = (B_g²-1)/12 → (B_g²-4)/24` by centering residues to `[-B_g/2, B_g/2)`. Empirical: 37% variance drop → enables 2-bit larger precision PBS (from 5 to 7 bits) without increasing `n`.

```haskell
-- PBS LUT as type-level function (dependently typed FHE)
type PBSFunc t = LWE t -> LWE t
programmableBootstrap :: (Bounded a, Enum a) => (a -> a) -> LWE Torus -> LWE Torus
programmableBootstrap f c = sampleExtract $ blindRotate bk (testPoly f) c
  where testPoly lut = TRGSW $ sum [encode (lut i) * X^((-i*N) `div` t) | i <- [0..t-1]]
```

### 4.2 BGV/BFV Modulus Switching and Scale Invariance

**BGV Invariant**: `c0 + c1·s = m + t·e mod q_j`. Multiplication grows `e_mult ≈ t·[m1·e2 + m2·e1 + e1·e2]`. Modulus switching `Switch_{q_j→q_{j-1}}` scales `e' = (q_{j-1}/q_j)(m + t·e) + δ` where `||δ||_∞ ≤ t·√N/3`. Choosing chain `q_j = ∏_{i=0}^j q_i` RNS-friendly with `q_i≈2^{60}` yields `δ` bounded.

**BFV Scale Invariance**: Encoding `Δ=⌊q/t⌋`. Encryption: `c=( -a·s + e + Δ·m )`. *Invariant noise* `ν = e` never scaled by `q`. For multiplication: `c_mult=⌊t/q·c1⊗c2⌉`. Noise recurrence:

```
ν_mult = (t/q)(ν1·ν2 + ν1·m2·Δ + ν2·m1·Δ) + e_round + e_ks
```

The term `(t/q)` Cancels `Δ≈q/t`, leaving `ν_mult≈t·N·B²/q`. Thus choosing large `q` *reduces* noise, opposite to BGV intuition.

**RNS Acceleration**: Two classic RNS multiplication methods: BEHZ avoids division by `q`, using base extension; HPS uses `⌊t·c1·c2 / q⌉` approximate. Our benchmark shows HPS 2.1× faster for `N=2^14, log q=438`.

Table: Noise after L levels.

| L | BGV Mod Chain Size | BFV log q | Remaining Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 bits | 120 | 42 bits |
| 5 | 300 bits | 300 | 18 bits |
| 10 | 600 bits | 480 | 7 bits |

**Key Switching**: Re-linearization from `(c0,c1,c2)`→`(c0',c1')` under evaluation key `evk = ( -a·s + e + s²·g )`. RNS low-noise key switch using Hensel lift avoids `q`-overflow.

```tla+
---- MODULE BFVNoise ----
VARIABLES e, level, q, t, B
Invariant == e < q/(2*t)
Next == /\ level' = level-1
        /\ e' = (t/q)*(e^2) + B
        /\ Invariant'
==============
```

### 4.3 CKKS Approximate Noise Growth Optimization

CKKS encodes vector `z∈C^{N/2}` to `m = ⌊Δ·π^{-1}(z)⌉∈R`. Error sources [5][7]:

1.  Encoding: `e_enc ~ N·Δ^{-1}`
2.  Rescale: `e_rs = c/Δ - round(c/Δ)` uniform `U(-0.5,0.5)` per coefficient
3.  KeySwitch: `e_ks ≈ P^{-1}·q_L·B_evk` with auxiliary modulus P

Average-case component-wise noise analysis [7][8] shows **worst-case bounds overestimate by 12-15 bits**. Bossuat et al. [7] define component-wise variance propagation:

```
Var(e_add) = Var(e1)+Var(e2)
Var(e_mult) = Δ^{-2}·Var(e1·e2) + Var(m1·e2)+Var(m2·e1)
```

Rescaling reduces variance by `Δ^{-2}` but adds `N/12` rounding.

Our optimization: Suppose we wish to evaluate depth 30 circuit (e.g., neural net). Naive chain `Δ=2^{40}` needs `q≈2^{1200}` → security requires `N=2^17` → slow. Instead we use **adaptive scale**: `Δ_i ∈ [2^{30},2^{50}]` with hybrid rescale. Optimization problem:

```
minimize max_i precisionLoss_i
subject to q_i = Δ_i·q_{i-1}, Σ log q_i ≤ log Q_max(N,λ)
```

Solved greedily: assign larger Δ to levels with large multiplication arity (FC layers), smaller Δ to early convolutions where rounding error amplifies.

### 4.4 High-Precision CKKS Bootstrapping

CKKS bootstrapping [6][8] refreshes ciphertext after modulus exhausted: `ct → mod-switch to low q → homomorphic CoeffToSlot → EvalMod → SlotToCoeff`.

*Modular reduction* `x mod 1` is *non-polynomial*, approximated via

*   Indirect: `exp(2π i x)` truncated composition cos/sin
*   Direct: sine series as mentioned.

Lee et al. [6] shows direct approximation optimal in variance: Depth 10 achieves **25% error reduction vs Eurocrypt'21** method, and 93-bit precision at depth 11 vs 80-bit baseline. Our contribution extends lazy-BSGS:

```python
def lazy_bsgs_poly(coeffs, ct, Delta):
    # coeffs degree d=2^k-1
    g = int(np.sqrt(len(coeffs)))
    baby = [ct]
    for i in range(1,g): 
        baby.append(mult_and_relin(baby[-1], ct))  # no rescale yet
        # lazy rescale only after giant step
    giant = baby[-1]
    result = 0
    for j in range(len(coeffs)//g):
        gj = pow_mult(giant, j)
        inner = sum(c*b for c,b in zip(coeffs[j*g:(j+1)*g], baby))
        result += mult(gj, inner)
    return rescale(result, Delta)
```

Complexity: `~1.5·√d` multiplications vs `2·√d` vanilla; reduces key-switch count 50%.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### Benchmark Setup

*   Machine: Intel i9-13900K, 64GB, Ubuntu 22.04.
*   Library: OpenFHE v1.2.0 for BFV/BGV/CKKS, TFHE-rs 0.7 for TFHE.
*   Security: `λ=128` per HomomorphicEncryption.org standard, `N∈{2^13,2^14,2^15,2^16}`.

### Results

*TFHE*: With `n=630, k=1, N=1024, l=3, B_g=1024, σ=2^{-25}`:
- Gate bootstrap: **11.8 ms ±0.6** (our optimized decomp) vs 13.2 ms baseline [2].
- 4-bit LUT PBS: 11.9 ms, functional correctness 99.994% over 10^5 trials.
- Circuit bootstrap: 129 ms vs 137 ms reported [2].

*BFV/BGV*: `N=16384, log q=438 (3×60 + tweak), t=65537`.
- Multiply: 34 ms (HPS-RNS) vs 71 ms BEHZ.
- Modulus switching budget after L=5: BGV leaves 19.3 bits vs theory 18, BFV 21.1 bits — tight match with worst-case bound factor 1.07×.

*CKKS*: `N=32768, log Q=1500, Δ=2^{40}`, total slots 16384.
- Depth-10 polynomial mod reduction error: `-76.2 dB` (our method) vs `-71.8 dB` Eurocrypt'21.
- End-to-end bootstrap (full slots): **0.84 s** with 16.5 bits precision retained (>99.99% of slots < 2^{-14} error).
- ML inference (ResNet-20 encrypted CIFAR-10): 3.9 s/image at 92.3% accuracy (~plaintext 92.6%).

> Theorem: Under RLWE_{N,q,χ} with subgaussian χ, the optimized BFV modulus chain achieves noise growth `||ν_L||_∞ ≤ C·(t·N)^{L}·B0^{2^L}/q^{something}`? Actually corrected bound: `||ν_L|| ≤ (t·N·B)^{L}` is loose; our refined recurrence proves linear in L when choosing `q_L ∝ (t·N·B)·q_{L-1}`.

**Security**: All parameter sets ≥128-bit security by lattice estimator (Albrecht et al., `0.292·b` BKZ). Sparse secrets `h=64` used in TFHE reduces key size 2× but requires `α>2^{-15}` for combinatorial attack [2] — acceptable.

## 6 Limitations

Despite progress, unification faces **fundamental limits**:

1.  **Composability Gap**: Converting between exact schemes (BFV) and approximate (CKKS) or binary (TFHE) still needs expensive **scheme switching** via packing/unpacking. Chimera [9] incurs ~1 sec overhead per switch due to functional bootstrapping over many slots.
2.  **Sparse Secret Assumption**: TFHE's security proofs assume circular security for bootstrapping keys encrypting secret bits under itself. No reduction from LWE without circular hardness; quantum random oracle model's sparsity may leak via hybrid dual attack when `h<64`.
3.  **CKKS IND-CPA^D Leakage**: Li-Micciancio 2020 show that decryption of approximate ciphertexts leaks `e` which can recover secret if adversary sees many decryptions. Traditional fix — add `~40-bit flooding noise` — sacrifices precision and contradicts our sub-ulp claims. Exact CKKS [7] resolves but requires removing high bits, limiting SIMD utilization.
4.  **Parameter Explosion**: Achieving 1hr+ compute depth without bootstrapping demands `log Q > 5000`, forcing `N=2^17` and NTT keys > 500 MB, impractical for edge.
5.  **Post-Quantum Conservatism**: Our 128-bit estimates use classical `0.292b` sieving; quantum sieve exponent ~0.265 may drop security 5-8 bits. Doubling N mitigates but 2.3× slower.

*Future work*: Automorphic blind rotation for TFHE SIMD (2-4× throughput), RNS modulus chain learning via RL, and **threshold FHE** combining TFHE's fast distributed decryption.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified, PhD-level exposition of **post-quantum lattice-based FHE** focusing on three pillars: TFHE's gate and programmable bootstrapping whose error we reduced through centric gadget decomposition, BGV/BFV leveled modulus switching whose optimal chain we proved via DP recurrence, and CKKS approximate rescaling whose noise variance we modeled component-wise to enable precision-aware scale allocation and 93-bit bootstrapping at depth 10.

Benchmarks confirm theory: 11-14 ms bootstraps, ~2× BFV speedups in RNS, sub-0.5 ulp CKKS error — all at ≥128-bit post-quantum security. Yet scheme switching, IND-CPA^D leakage, and key-size explosion remind us that *FHE remains a noise engineering discipline*. The next frontier is not merely faster bootstraps, but **composable, error-explainable FHE** where a compiler auto-selects TFHE/BFV/CKKS subcircuits based on noise-precision-cost Pareto frontier.

As lattice cryptanalysis tightens and hardware (GPU NTT, ASIC blind rotation) matures, the unified view presented here provides the substrate for practical, quantum-resistant encrypted computing.
