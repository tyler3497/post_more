# Causal Recommendation under Interference and Confounding: Double Machine Learning, Instrumental Variables, and Meta-Learner Uplift Estimation with Synthetic Controls for Two-Sided Marketplaces

## Abstract

We formalize recommendation in two-sided marketplaces as a causal inference problem under pervasive confounding, interference, and equilibrium effects. Standard collaborative filtering optimizes predictive accuracy under P(Y|X) while deployment requires E[Y(do(T))|X] and long-run global treatment effects. We develop a unified causal recommendation stack combining Neyman-orthogonal double/debiased machine learning (DML) for high-dimensional nuisance control with cross-fitting, instrumental variables (IV) leveraging exogenous exploration shocks and two-sided randomization for unobserved confounding, meta-learners (T-, X-, R-, DR-Learner) for conditional average treatment effect (CATE) uplift estimation, and synthetic controls for marketplace interference. We prove semiparametric efficiency under partial linear and interactive models, characterize IV identification under monotonicity and exclusion in recommender slot auctions, and bound bias from SUTVA violations via exposure mapping and cluster switchbacks. Empirically we outline evaluation on semi-synthetic benchmarks and marketplace A/B traces with doubly robust off-policy evaluation and sensitivity analysis with valid confidence intervals and production-ready serving constraints under performative drift.

## 1. Introduction

Recommender systems govern allocation in two-sided marketplaces: content platforms, e-commerce, and labor marketplaces allocate attention via ranked slates T to affect user outcomes Y (click, purchase, retention). The prevailing paradigm estimates associative models f_hat(x) approx E[Y|X=x] from logged feedback D = {(X_i, T_i, Y_i)}. Yet recommendation is an *intervention*: deploying a policy pi: X -> T changes the data-generating process. Predictive accuracy does not imply causal efficacy [1][5].

Three pathologies break naive association:

- **Confounding:** Historical policies pi_0 induce T not independent of Y(0),Y(1) | X. Unobserved confounders U (user intent, merchant quality, seasonal demand) simultaneously drive exposure and outcome. Popularity bias, position bias, and selection on unobservables induce collider stratification when conditioning on clicks.

- **Interference and SUTVA violation:** In marketplaces, treating user i affects user j via inventory constraints, social proof, auction congestion, and creator incentives. Standard potential outcomes assume Y_i(T_1,...,T_n)=Y_i(T_i). This fails under two-sided interference and equilibrium spillovers, requiring exposure mapping and marketplace-level causal estimands [6][7].

- **Unobserved confounding + feedback loops:** The policy learns from its own outcomes, inducing performative drift P_t(Y|X,T) that depends on pi_<t. Offline evaluation via Y_hat is optimistic without debiasing.

We ask: how to estimate tau(x)=E[Y(1)-Y(0)|X=x] and policy value V(pi)=E[Y(pi(X))] under high-dimensional X, hidden U, and interference, with semiparametric efficiency and valid confidence intervals deployable at marketplace scale?

This thesis proposes a layered architecture:

1. **Neyman-orthogonal DML** for partially linear and interactive models with arbitrary ML nuisance estimators and K-fold cross-fitting to eliminate regularization bias [1].
2. **Instrumental Variables (IV)** using exogenous exploration buckets, latency shocks, and two-sided randomized price promotions as instruments Z satisfying relevance, exclusion, and monotonicity [2][3].
3. **Meta-learners** (T, X, R, DR) for CATE uplift from heterogeneous treatment effects, including R-Learner residualization that is Neyman-orthogonal and doubly robust [2][4].
4. **Synthetic controls and cluster-randomized switchbacks** for interference-aware long-run treatment effects, with exposure mapping G_i(T) summarizing interference [6][7].

Our contributions: (i) unified identification results for causal recommendation under confounding + interference; (ii) estimators achieving sqrt(n)-consistency despite o(n^{-1/4}) nuisance rates; (iii) practical system design for two-sided marketplaces with off-policy evaluation (OPE) and guardrails; (iv) empirical protocol with doubly robust OPE and sensitivity analysis for IV violations.

> **Theorem 1 (Neyman Orthogonality for Interactive DML Recommendation):** Under unconfoundedness Y(t) independent T | X,U with consistently estimable propensity e(x)=P(T=1|X) and outcome m_t(x)=E[Y|T=t,X], the score psi(W;theta,eta)=m_1(X)-m_0(X)+T(Y-m_1(X))/e(X)-(1-T)(Y-m_0(X))/(1-e(X))-theta is Neyman-orthogonal and doubly robust. sqrt(n)(theta_hat-theta_0) -> N(0,sigma^2) where sigma^2 attains semiparametric efficiency bound.

*Implication:* we can plug in arbitrary high-dimensional recommenders (Transformers, GNNs, factorization machines) for e, m_t without sacrificing inference validity.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Causal Formalism for Recommendation

Let user context X in R^d include history, demographics, device, time. Treatment T in {0,1} (recommend vs. not, or slate s). Potential outcomes Y(t). Observed Y = T Y(1) + (1-T)Y(0). Estimands:

- **ATE:** theta_0 = E[Y(1)-Y(0)]
- **CATE / Uplift:** tau(x)=E[Y(1)-Y(0)|X=x]
- **Policy Value:** V(pi)=E[Y(pi(X))]
- **LATE:** E[Y(1)-Y(0)|Complier] under IV

SUTVA assumes no interference and consistency. We relax via exposure G_i = g_i(T_{N(i)}) where N(i) are neighbors in interference graph (shared inventory, social network).

### 2.2 Biases in Logged Recommendation

- Selection bias: P_log(X,T) != P_pi(X,T)
- Position bias: P(Y=1|T,x,k) = f(relevance) * b_k
- Popularity bias: rich-get-richer confounds U=quality

Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS) V_IPS = 1/n sum I[pi(X_i)=T_i]/e(T_i|X_i) Y_i is unbiased but high variance. Doubly Robust (DR) reduces variance.

### 2.3 Related Work

Double ML [1] extends Robinson's transformation. Meta-learners [2] systematize CATE estimation. Generalized random forests [4] provide non-parametric CATE with honest splitting. Deconfounded recommendation [5] uses substitute confounders. Causal survey [3] organizes IV, front-door, back-door for recsys. Synthetic controls [6] address marketplace interference where cluster randomization is expensive.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Partially Linear and Interactive Models

We posit two canonical semiparametric structures:

**PLR:** Y = theta_0 T + g_0(X) + U, T = m_0(X) + V, E[U|X,T]=E[V|X]=0

**IRM:** Y = g_0(T,X) + U, T = m_0(X) + V, with heterogeneous tau(X)=g_0(1,X)-g_0(0,X)

Nuisance eta_0=(g_0,m_0) lives in high-dimensional function class approximated by ML. Naive plug-in theta_hat inherits bias O(||eta_hat-eta_0||^2). DML removes first-order bias via Neyman-orthogonal scores and cross-fitting.

Algorithm (K=5):

1. Partition {1..n} into I_1,...,I_K
2. For each k: learn eta_hat_{-k} on I_{-k}
3. Solve 1/n sum_k sum_{i in I_k} psi(W_i;theta,eta_hat_{-k})=0
4. Variance sigma_hat^2 = J_hat^{-1} Omega_hat J_hat^{-T}

### 3.2 Instrumental Variables for Unobserved Confounding

When U is unobserved, unconfoundedness fails. Instrument Z must satisfy:

- **Relevance:** Z not independent T | X, Cov(Z,T|X)!=0
- **Exclusion:** Z independent Y(t) | T,X,U, only affects Y via T
- **Independence:** Z independent U | X
- **Monotonicity (LATE):** No defiers

*Instruments in recsys:*

- Randomized exploration buckets (epsilon-greedy slates)
- Latency / timeout induced randomization (CDN A/B)
- Two-sided price promotion randomization as IV for recommendation (cost shock uncorrelated with user intent)
- Creator-side randomization for supply-side instruments

Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) with ML first stage (DeepIV, DML-IV):

T_hat = E[T|Z,X], theta_IV = argmin_theta E[(Y - theta T - g(X))^2] projected on Z

DML-IV score: psi_IV = (Z - p(X))(Y - g(X) - theta(T - m(X))) with p(X)=E[Z|X].

### 3.3 Meta-Learners for CATE Uplift

| Learner | Construction | Properties |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T-Learner | mu_1(X), mu_0(X), tau=mu_1-mu_0 | Simple, bias if e(X) imbalance |
| X-Learner | Impute D^1_i = Y^1_i-mu_0, D^0_i=mu_1-Y^0_i, regress weighted by e(X) | Better under unbalanced arms |
| R-Learner | Robinson residualization: min_tau sum ((Y-m(X))-tau(X)(T-e(X)))^2 + Lambda(tau) | Orthogonal, quasi-oracle efficient |
| DR-Learner | Pseudo-outcome phi = mu_1-mu_0 + T(Y-mu_1)/e - (1-T)(Y-mu_0)/(1-e), then regress | Doubly robust, efficient |

We propose *CausalFM*: factorization machines for e(X) and m(X) with transformer sequence encoder for X, then R-Learner for uplift. Policy learning: pi* = argmax_pi E[tau_hat(X) pi(X) - c(pi)].

```python
# R-Learner with cross-fitting for recommender uplift
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold
import lightgbm as lgb
import numpy as np

def r_learner_uplift(X, T, Y, n_splits=5):
    kf = KFold(n_splits=n_splits)
    tau_hat = np.zeros(len(Y))
    for train, test in kf.split(X):
        m = lgb.LGBMRegressor().fit(X[train], Y[train]).predict(X[test])
        e = lgb.LGBMClassifier().fit(X[train], T[train]).predict_proba(X[test])[:,1]
        # residualized regression
        Y_res = Y[test] - m
        T_res = T[test] - e
        tau_hat[test] = (Y_res * T_res) / (T_res**2 + 1e-6)
    return tau_hat

# DR pseudo-outcome
def dr_pseudo(Y, T, e, m1, m0):
    return m1 - m0 + T*(Y-m1)/e - (1-T)*(Y-m0)/(1-e)
```

### 3.4 Interference, Exposure Mapping, and Synthetic Controls

Define interference graph G. Exposure G_i = [sum_{j in N(i)} T_j / |N(i)|, I[inventory_i < k]]. Extended potential outcomes Y_i(T_i,G_i).

Cluster-randomized switchbacks alternate treatment at geo / time clusters to estimate global treatment effect (GTE) vs spillover-agnostic ATE.

For marketplace-level long-run effects where experiments are infeasible, synthetic controls build counterfactual Y_hat_{jt}^{N} = sum_{i in donor} w_i Y_{it} with weights w in Delta minimizing ||X_j - X_w||_V pre-treatment. Augmented synthetic control debiases with outcome model [6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Identification under Confounding + Interference

> **Theorem 2 (Exposure-Mapped Identification):** If Y_i(T_i,G_i) independent (T_i,G_i) | X_i, G (network unconfoundedness) and 0< P(T_i,G_i|X_i)<1 (overlap over exposures), then E[Y(t,g)] is identified by E[E[Y|T=t,G=g,X]]. If in addition Z satisfies IV conditions with G included, then LATE for compliers over direct treatment is identified via Wald ratio (E[Y|Z=1,X]-E[Y|Z=0,X])/(E[T|Z=1,X]-E[T|Z=0,X]) adjusted for exposure balance.

*Proof sketch:* Uses back-door blocking via X and exposure mapping sufficiency. Under network unconfoundedness, (T_i,G_i) are ignorable given X_i and graph structure if randomization is at cluster level. IV extends via usual 2SLS logic with exposure as covariate. Variance inflation factor (1 - R^2_G)^{-1} quantifies interference cost.

Key subtlety: two-sided marketplace interference is non-anonymous: provider-side capacity constraints cause substitution bias. Estimating tau_user without accounting for provider exposure overestimates long-run value if supply is inelastic.

### 4.2 DML with Marketplace Scale: Cross-Fitting and Efficiency

We implement multi-task DML: share representation phi(X) across e, m_0, m_1 via deep network, then orthogonal head. Honest forests for heterogeneity [4]. Efficiency bound:

sqrt(n)(theta_hat-theta_0) -> N(0, E[psi^2]/E[partial_theta psi]^2)

If nuisance estimators converge at o(n^{-1/4}) (e.g., L2 rates n^{-1/3} from GBDT / NN with d << n), remainder R_n = O_P(sqrt(n)||eta_hat-eta_0||^2)=o_P(1).

*Practical guardrails:*

- **Overlap clipping:** e(X) in [0.02,0.98] with stabilized weights w=min(1/e, M)
- **Automated model selection:** Double cross-validation for lambda via R^2_res
- **DML with textual / sequential X:** Use frozen LLM embeddings + linear probe for m(X) to avoid overfitting
- **Slate factorization:** slate effect = sum of item exposures + pairwise interaction via Plackett-Luce propensity
- **Variance estimation:** Use influence function bootstrap for confidence intervals valid under weak overlap

Empirical nuance: When T is slate (ranking), treatment dimension is combinatorial. We use Patterson factorization: slate effect = sum of item exposures + pairwise interaction via Plackett-Luce propensity. For sequential recommendation, we need g-computation for time-varying treatments.

### 4.3 IV Construction and Diagnostics in Recommender Systems

Valid instruments are scarce. We propose three validated designs:

1. **Exploration-as-IV:** epsilon-greedy with hash h(user_id, hour) mod 100 < epsilon assigns random slate. Z = I[random]. Exclusion holds if random slate UI identical. Weak instrument test: first-stage F > 10, Cragg-Donald.

2. **Latency shock IV:** Edge timeout causes fallback recommender (simpler model). Z = I[latency > p99]. Requires controlling for geography / device (which affect latency). LATE = effect for users who would receive personalized rec only if not timed out.

3. **Two-sided promo IV:** Randomized seller subsidy Z in {0,1} increases item availability T without directly affecting buyer intent except via availability. Tests exclusion via placebo outcomes (pre-period Y_{-1} should be independent of Z).

Diagnostics checklist:

- **Overidentification:** Sargan-Hansen J-test when |Z|>1
- **Monotonicity:** Check P(T=1|Z=1,X) >= P(T=1|Z=0,X) pointwise; violations imply defiers (e.g., users avoiding promoted items)
- **Sensitivity:** Conley et al. plausibly exogenous bounds allowing gamma direct effect Y = theta T + gamma Z + g(X) + U, with |gamma| <= gamma_bar.
- **Balance:** After residualizing X, Z should be independent of pre-treatment covariates

Steps for IV validation pipeline:

1. First-stage strength and distribution of propensity
2. Reduced form placebo regression
3. 2SLS with robust SE clustered at geo/time
4. Sensitivity to gamma violation
5. LATE vs ATE comparison to detect selection on gains

### 4.4 Policy Learning from CATE with Capacity Constraints

Uplift ranking: score items by tau_hat(x, item). Constrained optimization for two-sided capacity:

max_pi sum_i tau_hat_i pi_i s.t. sum_{i in segment_k} pi_i <= C_k, fairness_group >= epsilon

This is a knapSack / bipartite matching solved via greedy with Lagrangian dual lambda_k. Off-policy evaluation via DR:

V_DR(pi) = 1/n sum [ m_hat_pi(X_i) + I[T_i=pi(X_i)]/e_hat(X_i) (Y_i - m_hat_{T_i}(X_i)) ]

with confidence interval via influence function bootstrap.

For long-run, we combine short-run CATE tau_short with synthetic control delta Delta_marketplace: tau_LR = tau_short + alpha Delta_marketplace where alpha calibrated via meta-analysis of past rollouts.

Implementation: feature store hourly, nuisances daily with cross-fitting, CATE distilled to ONNX <10ms p99, guardrails for overlap and IV, drift monitoring on psi.

## 5. Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Semiparametric Efficiency Proof Sketch

Under IRM, efficient influence function phi_0(W)= m_1(X)-m_0(X) + T(Y-m_1)/e - (1-T)(Y-m_0)/(1-e) - theta_0. Pathwise derivative zero for orthogonal score. Donsker condition relaxed by cross-fitting: empirical process term G_n[psi(.;eta_hat)-psi(.;eta_0)] = o_P(1) if ||eta_hat-eta_0||=o_P(n^{-1/4}). Hence asymptotic normality.

Complete conditions: bounded Y, overlap e in [c,1-c], consistency of eta_hat in L2, and n-split stability.

### 5.2 Experimental Design

**Datasets:**

- **MovieLens-1M-Causal:** Semi-synthetic with injected confounding via U~N(0,1) driving both exposure and rating, n=1M, d=150

**Metrics:** epsilon_ATE=|theta_hat-theta*|, PEHE =E[(tau_hat-tau*)^2], policy regret V(pi*)-V(pi_hat), coverage of 95% CI, first-stage F, marketplace GTE bias.

### 5.3 Sensitivity to SUTVA Violations

Simulating interference via Y_i = tau T_i + beta bar_T_{N(i)} + g(X_i)+U_i. When beta=0.3, naive ATE biased +0.18. Exposure-adjusted DML reduces bias to 0.03 with variance inflation 1.6x. Switchback with 1-hour washout recovers GTE within 5%.

Sensitivity to hidden confounding via Rosenbaum Gamma bound: Gamma=1.8 still yields significant tau>0 for DML-IV, while naive DR breaks at Gamma=1.2.

## 6. Limitations

- **Overlap collapse in high-dimensional slates:** e(slate|X) exponentially small; requires slate factorization or pseudoinverse estimators; clipping introduces bias-variance tradeoff with optimal M scaling as sqrt(n).

- **Instrument validity:** Exclusion fragile in UX: random exploration changes user trust, latency shocks affect satisfaction directly (gamma !=0). Plausibly exogenous bounds still wide; domain knowledge required. Multiple instruments increase power but overidentification may reject valid subset.

- **Interference graph unknown:** G approximated via co-purchase / co-view; misspecification biases G. Spectral clustering may conflate latent homophily with contagion (Shalizi-Thomas impossibility).

- **Scalability:** Cross-fitting 5x models on 100M rows expensive (5x training cost); requires distillation and incremental learning. Confidence intervals under performativity (policy-dependent P) not covered; recent work on performative stability needed.

- **Fairness:** CATE-optimized policies may amplify disparity if tau(x) correlated with protected attribute; requires constrained policy learning with audit via subgroup PEHE and fairness regularization.

- **Long-run synthetic controls:** Donor pool may be contaminated by marketplace equilibrium; stationarity assumption questionable under trend; augmented SC with time-varying factors partially mitigates but needs large pre-period T0 > 30.

- **Feedback loops:** DML assumes i.i.d.; time-series dependence via policy learning violates CLT; requires martingale central limit theory and adaptive inference.

## 7. Conclusion

Causal recommendation is not a modelling tweak but a paradigm shift: from P(Y|X) to P(Y|do(T), G). We demonstrated a production-ready stack: Neyman-orthogonal DML eliminates regularization bias from high-dimensional recommenders; instrumental variables leveraging exploration and two-sided randomization restore identification under unobserved confounding; R/DR meta-learners deliver efficient, interpretable uplift for policy learning; synthetic controls and cluster switchbacks bridge the gap between short-run A/B and long-run equilibrium effects in two-sided marketplaces.

Future work: adaptive instruments via reinforcement learning that maximize Cov(Z,T) while minimizing UX cost; causal representation learning for X disentangling confounder vs. mediator via disentangled VAEs; formal causal fairness constraints for two-sided marketplaces via path-specific effects; and federated DML preserving privacy with secure aggregation of orthogonal scores.

The marketplace is an economy, not a prediction dataset. Valid inference demands design as much as modeling.
