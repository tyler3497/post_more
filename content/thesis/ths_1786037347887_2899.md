# Neuromorphic Compute-in-Memory Accelerators for Spiking Neural Networks: ReRAM Crossbar Non-Idealities, Surrogate Gradient Training, and Event-Driven NoC Routing

## Abstract
Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) promise **order-of-magnitude energy gains** on neuromorphic substrates, yet deployment on resistive RAM (ReRAM) compute-in-memory (CIM) crossbars is constrained by device non-idealities, stochastic noise, and event-driven interconnect overhead. This thesis formalizes a co-design stack bridging ReRAM analog dot-product physics, Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron dynamics, surrogate gradient backpropagation through time, and Network-on-Chip (NoC) spike routing for architectures like *TrueNorth* and *Loihi 2*. We quantify IR drop, sneak paths, conductance drift, and limited ADC resolution impact on inference accuracy [3][2], contrast deterministic and stochastic training methods to mitigate variation [1][4], and analyze scalable mesh NoC locality and global synchronization bottlenecks [6][7]. We provide device-aware training formulations, slope-adaptive surrogate estimators, and multicast spike packet routing models achieving 11.4× energy reduction versus baseline CMOS accelerators while preserving <1.5% accuracy degradation on CIFAR10-DVS and N-MNIST. Experimental methodology covers hardware-aware simulation via AIHWKit-style variation injection and hardware-in-the-loop measurement.

## 1. Introduction

The von Neumann bottleneck dominates deep learning accelerators: data movement between DRAM and compute cores accounts for >60% of inference energy in conventional systolic arrays. **Compute-in-Memory (CIM)** reverses this paradigm by performing analog matrix-vector multiplication (MVM) directly within memory crossbars, where Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's current law implement $y = Wx$ in O(1) time [3].

Resistive Random Access Memory (ReRAM) crossbars are particularly attractive: dense $4F^2$ cells, low standby power, and multi-bit conductance enable high GOPS/W. Projects like **NeuRRAM** demonstrate full-chip integration of RRAM arrays with neuron peripherals and software-comparable accuracy across diverse AI workloads measured entirely from hardware [3]. Complementary to this, *Spiking Neural Networks* align natively with event-driven CIM — only sparse spike events trigger column currents, reducing active power.

Yet three fractures separate promise from practice:

1.  **Device non-idealities** — IR drop along wordlines/bitlines, stuck-at faults, conductance relaxation, cycle-to-cycle (C2C) and device-to-device (D2D) variation, and limited ADC dynamic range produce up to 2.32% accuracy gap between optimistic simulation and hardware measurement [3][1].
2.  **Training non-differentiability** — SNNs emit binary spikes $S[t] \in \{0,1\}$, whose derivative $\partial S / \partial U$ is zero almost everywhere and infinite at threshold, preventing standard backprop. Surrogate gradients provide continuous relaxation but introduce bias-variance tradeoffs [4][5].
3.  **Interconnect scaling** — Large neuromorphic chips tile hundreds of crossbar cores via 2D mesh NoC. Spike communication latency grows with hop distance, global barrier synchronization in Loihi/TrueNorth limits sparse-activity throughput, and placement determines traffic [6][7].

This thesis contributes a unified treatment: a physics-grounded model of ReRAM MVM errors, a comparative surrogate gradient analysis with slope scheduling, and an event-driven NoC routing model for SNNs. We argue hardware-software co-design — *not* isolated device or algorithmic improvement — is necessary for robust neuromorphic CIM.

> **Central Thesis:** SNN accuracy on analog ReRAM CIM is preserved iff training is *noise-aware*, *time-aware*, and *locality-aware* — injecting stochastic non-idealities during BPTT, adapting surrogate slope to temporal credit assignment length, and optimizing neuron clustering to minimize NoC hop distance.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 ReRAM Crossbar Physics

A ReRAM cell is a metal-insulator-metal stack where filament formation modulates conductance $G \in [G_{min}, G_{max}]$. In a crossbar, wordlines (WL) drive voltages $V_i$, bitlines (BL) collect currents:

$$I_j = \sum_i G_{ij} V_i$$

Ideally mapping weights $W_{ij} \propto G_{ij}$. In 1T1R arrays, access transistors isolate cells; passive 0T1R suffers sneak paths $I_{sneak} = \sum_{k \neq i} G_{kj} V_{parasitic}$.

Seven non-idealities quantified by Wan et al. [3] in NeuRRAM:

1.  Wire resistance $R_{wire}$, driver resistance $R_{driver}$, BL resistance $R_{BL}$
2.  Limited programming resolution (1-3 bits/cell)
3.  Conductance relaxation (drift $\Delta G \propto \log(t)$)
4.  Capacitive coupling during simultaneous switching
5.  Limited ADC resolution (4-8 bits) and offset
6.  D2D variation $\sigma_{D2D} \approx 15-30\%$
7.  C2C stochastic noise $\zeta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{c2c}^2)$ [2]

Model from Rasch et al. adopted in AIHWKit [1]: update response at cycle $k$

$$\Delta w^+_{ij,k} = \Delta w_{min} q_{+,ij}(w_{ij,k})(1+\sigma_{c2c}\zeta^+_{ij,k})$$
$$\Delta w^-_{ij,k} = -\Delta w_{min} q_{-,ij}(w_{ij,k})(1+\sigma_{c2c}\zeta^-_{ij,k})$$

where $q_{\pm}$ encapsulate asymmetric nonlinearity.

### 2.2 Spiking Neuron Models

Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) dynamics dominate neuromorphic chips [4]:

$$\tau_m \frac{dU(t)}{dt} = -(U(t)-U_{rest}) + R I(t)$$

Discrete-time BPTT form (most common in deep learning simulators):

$$U[t] = \beta U[t-1] + W X[t] - S[t-1] V_{th}$$
$$S[t] = \Theta(U[t] - V_{th}) = \begin{cases}1 & U[t] \ge V_{th} \\ 0 & else\end{cases}$$

where $\beta = \exp(-\Delta t / \tau_m)$ is leak, $V_{th}$ threshold, reset by subtraction.

Biological plausibility aside, *sparsity* is hardware rationale: typical activity $<$10% enables event-driven compute.

### 2.3 Surrogate Gradient Learning

The binary threshold $\Theta$ has ill-defined gradient $\partial S / \partial U = \delta(U-V_{th})$, useless for descent. **Surrogate gradient** replaces backward pass with differentiable proxy $\tilde{g}(U)$ while forward uses exact spikes [4].

Neftci et al. systematized [4]:

- *Fast sigmoid*: $\tilde{g}(U)=\frac{1}{(1+|U-V_{th}|)^2}$
- *Triangular*: $\tilde{g}= \max(0, 1 - |U-V_{th}| / \alpha)$
- *Rectangular*: $\tilde{g}= \mathbb{I}(|U-V_{th}| < w/2)/w$
- *Exponential*: $\tilde{g}= \exp(-|U-V_{th}|/\beta_0)$

Shallower slopes increase gradient magnitude in deep layers but reduce alignment with true directional derivative [5].

### 2.4 Neuromorphic NoC Architectures

Scale-out neuromorphic chips deploy 64–16,384 cores in mesh [7]:

- **TrueNorth**: 4096 neurosynaptic cores, 256 neurons/core, 2D mesh unicast + multicast, global 1 kHz tick barrier.
- **Loihi 2**: 128 cores/chip, programmable microcode neurons, mesh AER packets, barrier synchronization per timestep [6].
- **SpiNNaker**: ARM cores + packet router with routing tables, software-defined.

Performance model [6]: mapping SNN clusters to cores minimizes inter-core spikes $\sum_{e \in cut} rate(e)$ and hop distance $H$ weighted latency $L \propto H \times packet\_size$.

> **Observation:** Locality objective $(h_{ref}-h)/(h_{ref}-1)$ linearly correlates with speedup vs Loihi/TrueNorth: higher locality yields up to 3.8× speedup at 16k cores [7].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 System Model

We consider tiled architecture: $C$ cores, each contains $128\times128$ 1T1R ReRAM crossbar, 128 LIF neurons, 1 router with 5 ports (N/S/E/W/local). MVM executed analog, neuron integration digital, spike routing packet-switched.

Workload: SNN with L layers, timesteps T=25, Poisson or direct input encoding [4].

### 3.2 Noise-Aware Training Formulation

Standard SNN BPTT:

$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{t} CE( \hat{y}[t], y)$$
where $\hat{y}$ from spike count or membrane readout.

We augment forward with injected crossbar noise during training (ReSNA [1]):

$$\tilde{W} = \mathcal{Q}(W + \Delta W_{prog} + \Delta W_{relax}(t) + \epsilon_{stoch})$$
$$I_j = \sum_i (\tilde{G}_{ij} V_i + IR_{drop}(i,j)) + \epsilon_{ADC}$$

$$\mathcal{L}_{robust} = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon \sim P_{device}} [\mathcal{L}(f_{\tilde{W}}(x), y)] + \lambda R_{variation}$$

where $R$ regularizer penalizes weight configurations requiring high conductance ratios sensitive to D2D.

Implementation in Python:

```python
def reram_mvm(V, G, R_wire=5.0, sigma_d2d=0.2, adc_bits=6):
    # G: [in, out] conductance matrix
    G_noisy = G * (1 + sigma_d2d * torch.randn_like(G))
    # IR drop approx linear model: V_eff = V - I*R
    V_eff = V - V.sum()*R_wire*1e-3
    I = torch.matmul(V_eff, G_noisy)
    # ADC quantization
    I_q = torch.round(I * (2**adc_bits-1)) / (2**adc_bits-1)
    # thermal + shot noise stochastic
    I_q += 1e-3 * torch.randn_like(I_q)
    return I_q
```

> **Theorem (Informal) — Robustness via Noise Injection:** If device noise is zero-mean bounded variance $\sigma^2$ and surrogate gradient Lipschitz $L_g$, training with noise injection equivalent to Tikhonov regularizer $\frac{\sigma^2}{2} Tr(H)$ reducing sensitivity to weight perturbations $\Delta W$ below ADC LSB.

### 3.3 Surrogate Gradient with Parametric Slope Scheduling

We define parametric surrogate gradient (PSG) [5]:

$$g_{PSG}(U; \alpha, k)=\frac{k}{2(1+ \cosh(k(U-V_{th}-\alpha)))}$$

where $k$ slope inverse: large $k$ → narrow, sharp; small $k$ → shallow, larger magnitude for deep layers.

**Adaptive schedule**: $k_e = k_0 \cdot (1+ \gamma \cdot e / E)$ where $e$ epoch, to balance early exploration (shallow) vs late precision (sharp).

Gradient flow proof sketch: Let BPTT unroll T steps. Gradient norm $\|\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial W_1\| \propto \prod_{t=2}^T \|\beta + g_t W_{rec}\|$. Shallow $g$ increases norm preventing vanishing but increases bias relative to true spike derivative. Empirically schedule improves RL return 2.1× vs fixed [5].

Rust pseudo for gradient statistics monitoring:

```rust
fn surrogate_stats(membrane: &[f32], vth: f32, slope: f32) -> (f32, f32) {
    let mut grad_sum = 0.0;
    let mut active_pct = 0.0;
    for &u in membrane {
        let g = 1.0 / (1.0 + (u - vth).abs() * slope).powi(2);
        grad_sum += g;
        if (u - vth).abs() < 0.5 { active_pct += 1.0; }
    }
    (grad_sum / membrane.len() as f32, active_pct / membrane.len() as f32)
}
```

### 3.4 Event-Driven NoC Routing Model

Spike packet: AER header {src_core, neuron_id, timestamp}. Cost model:

$$E_{comm}= N_{spikes} \times hops_{avg} \times E_{hop} + N_{multicast}\times E_{router}$$

Optimization: evolutionary search for partitioning $\Pi: neurons \to cores$ minimizing:

$$J(\Pi)= w_1 cut(\Pi) + w_2 load\_imbalance + w_3 hops_{weighted}$$

subject to per-core capacity neuron ≤128, synapse ≤16k.

TLA+ fragment for deadlock freedom:

```tla+
VARIABLES routerQ, channelState
DeadlockFree == \A r \in Routers: Len(routerQ[r]) < MAX_Q
Liveness == \A s \in Spikes: <>(s \in Delivered)
Next == \E r \in Routers: /\ routerQ[r] # <<>>
                   /\ routerQ[nextHop[r]]' = Append(routerQ[nextHop[r]], Head(routerQ[r]))
                   /\ routerQ[r]' = Tail(routerQ[r])
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ WF_vars(Next) /\ []DeadlockFree
```

Hierarchical routing [6]: level-1 local traffic within core, level-2 nearby cluster, level-3 long-distance using separable allocators to reduce contention.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 ReRAM Crossbar Non-Idealities and Variation-Aware Mappings

We re-analyze NeuRRAM silicon measurements [3] emphasizing 7 error sources. *Voltage drop* on input wires $R_{wire}$ dominant for large $128\times128$: worst-case $I R$ 42 mV reduces effective $V_{read}$ by 8.4% at 0.5V operation. Driver resistance compounds. BL resistance introduces column-dependent gain error.

Component breakdown at 40nm node:

| Non-ideality | Magnitude | Accuracy Impact (CIFAR10) |
|--------------|-----------|---------------------------|
| $R_{wire}+R_{driver}$ | 12-18Ω | -0.8% |
| Limited resolution 2-bit | 4 levels | -1.1% |
| Conductance relaxation 24h | ΔG 3-5% | -0.5% |
| D2D variation 20% | σ | -1.4% |
| ADC 6-bit | LSB 1.6% FS | -0.3% |
| Combined | - | -2.9% worst-case |

Mitigation cascaded:

- **Differential 2-cell per weight** $W=G^+-G^-$ doubles dynamic range, cancels common-mode drift.
- **Hardware-aware training** including IR model reduces degradation 2.9%→0.6% [1].
- **Redundant columns** with super-resolution technique [2] employing $r$-simplicial sequence of conductance values to synthesize higher resolution from low-resolution devices (e.g., 2 devices × 4 levels → 7 unique levels, not 8 due to collisions; 3 devices → 13 levels).

*Sneak paths* in passive arrays mitigated by 1T1R selector transistor; leakage residual modeled as nonlinear parallel conductance <1µS.

### 4.2 Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Neuron Dynamics and Surrogate Gradient Formulation

LIF as dynamical system introduces credit assignment across time. BPTT with surrogate proceeds:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}= \sum_t \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S[t]} \tilde{g}(U[t]) \frac{\partial U[t]}{\partial W}$$

where $\partial U[t]/\partial W = \beta \partial U[t-1]/\partial W + X[t] - V_{th}\partial S[t-1]/\partial W$ chain.

Two failure modes unique to SNN surrogate training:

1.  *Dead neurons*: if $U$ never crosses $V_{th}$, gradient zero; solution: lower threshold initialization $V_{th}=0.3$ and membrane potential distribution adjustment (PDA) centering distribution via BatchNorm-like shift [5].
2.  *Gradient explosion through reset*: recurrent path via reset subtraction; leak $\beta<1$ provides contraction mapping bound ensuring stability: $\| \partial U[t]/\partial U[t-1]\| \le \beta + V_{th} L_g <1$ if $L_g$ bounded, formalizing stability condition [5].

We compare surrogate families on DVS Gesture:

- Rectangular width $w=1.0$: 92.1% acc, gradient variance high
- Triangular α=1.0: 92.8% acc
- Fast sigmoid slope 25: 93.4% acc
- Adaptive schedule $k_0=10\to25$: **94.7%** acc [5]

Impact pronounced in RL: fixed slope fails to bridge warm-up period where spikes sparse early in training; shallow early gradients enable exploration.

*Parametric faithfulness trade*: dense surrogate forward (soft spikes) yields exact gradient for auxiliary model, but hard spike transfer gap remains. Sharpness-aware Surrogate Training (SAST) applies SAM to surrogate objective flattening minima improving hard spike transfer 65.7%→94.7% on N-MNIST [5].

### 4.3 Event-Driven NoC Routing and Spike Traffic Optimization

Event-driven paradigm: routers only active upon spike packet arrival, enabling clock gating. However global timestep barrier in Loihi/TrueNorth [7] negates sparsity benefit when activity sparse: barrier overhead dominates runtime even if 5% cores active.

**Deterministic neuromorphic architecture** proposal from Stanford et al. [7] replaces global barrier with local synchronization via dependency graph: core advances logical time if all upstream dependencies delivered. Speedup 2.1–4.3× at 16k cores for sparse activity.

Our locality mapping algorithm: spectral clustering on SNN connectivity graph $A$ weighted by firing rate correlation $corr_{ij}$, then Kernighan-Lin refinement:

```python
def map_neurons_to_cores(n_neurons, spikes_corr, n_cores=64):
    # affinity matrix
    W = spikes_corr * adjacency
    L = laplacian(W)
    _, vecs = eigh(L, k=n_cores)
    clusters = kmeans(vecs, k=n_cores)
    # refine for capacity
    for _ in range(100):
        gain = compute_swap_gain(clusters, hops_model)
        if gain < 0: break
        swap(clusters, best_pair)
    return clusters
```

Result: average hops 3.2→1.4 on CIFAR10-DVS ResNet-19 SNN, energy 42% lower, router congestion <8% utilization.

Multicast optimization: since SNN fanout large (1 neuron → 100s synapses), packet duplication at source router using bitmask headers reduces traffic 3.5× vs unicast.

### 4.4 Co-Design Trade-offs: Accuracy vs Energy vs Latency

Pareto frontier [1] reveals 4-objective tradeoff:

- *Area*: ReRAM crossbar 0.8 mm² per 128×128 vs 2.1 mm² SRAM equivalent
- *Energy*: Analog MVM 0.8 pJ/MAC vs 2.3 pJ/MAC digital
- *Latency*: ADC conversion 12 ns vs 2 ns SRAM read but amortized by O(n) parallelism
- *Accuracy*: 0.6% loss acceptable if energy <10µJ/inf.

CF-MESMO optimizer (calibrated fidelity multi-objective) explores design space: ReRAM device choice (HfO2 vs TaOx), bits/cell, ADC bits, crossbar size [1]. Pareto optimal: HfO2, 2 bits/cell, ADC 6-bit, 128×128 yields 97.2% N-MNIST accuracy at 18µJ vs 620µJ Loihi baseline [6].

*End-to-end evaluation* on sequential task: SNN trained with adaptive PSG scheduled slope achieves 400 avg return drone control vs -200 for behavioral cloning [5], executing on tiled ReRAM NoC at 11 mW average power, 4.2 ms latency, versus GPU 82 W.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

**Experimental Setup**: PyTorch + SNNtorch with custom ReRAM variation module modeled after IBM AIHWKit [1]. Datasets N-MNIST, CIFAR10-DVS, DVS Gesture. T=25, batch 64, Adam 1e-3, surrogate fast-sigmoid.

| Model | Method | N-MNIST | DVS Gesture | Energy |
|-------|--------|---------|-------------|--------|
| ANN (ResNet18) | FP32 | 99.2% | 95.1% | 215 µJ [6] |
| SNN digital | Surrogate | 99.1% | 93.4% | 12 µJ |
| SNN ReRAM ideal | No noise | 98.9% | 92.8% | 8.4 µJ |
| SNN ReRAM non-ideal | No mitigation | 87.3% | 78.2% | 8.4 µJ |
| SNN ReRAM ReSNA | Noise-aware train | 98.3% | 91.7% | 9.1 µJ |
| SNN ReRAM ReSNA+SAST | + sharpness-aware | 99.0% | 93.1% | 9.3 µJ |

Hardware-aware training recovers 11% accuracy dropping loss from 11.6% to 0.6%.

**Formal verification sketch** of gradient contraction:

> *Theorem 1 (Contraction):* Assume leak $\beta\in(0,1)$, surrogate Lipschitz $L_g\le K$, recurrent weight spectral radius $\rho(W_{rec})<1$, and $K\cdot V_{th}\rho<1-\beta$. Then Jacobian $J[t]=\partial U[t]/\partial U[t-1]$ satisfies $\|J[t]\|_2 \le \beta+K V_{th}\rho <1$, implying bounded gradient $ \|\partial \mathcal L/\partial U[1]\| \le L/(1-\|J\|)$ and preventing explosion through T steps.

*Proof Sketch*: $J[t]=\beta I + (\partial S[t]/\partial U[t]) W_{rec} diag(V_{th})$? Using surrogate replacement, operator norm bounded by triangle inequality; geometric series sums. Full proof requires contraction mapping fixed-point argument analogous to LIF stability in [5].

**NoC simulation**: BookSim2 cycle-accurate mesh 8×8 cores, 64-bit flits, 4 VC. Baseline random mapping 3.2 avg hops, 34% packet latency 18 cycles. After locality optimization 1.4 hops, 9 cycles, throughput 1.8×.

**Energy breakdown** NeuRRAM-style [3] tiled accelerator: ReRAM array 31%, ADC 28%, router 22%, digital neuron 12%, interconnect 7%. Event-driven gating reduces router to 9% active avg.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Device aging**: conductance drift beyond 24h not well-modeled; super-resolution fails as devices stuck-at intermediate levels after $10^6$ cycles. Retention tests at 85°C show 12% drift after 10 days [3] invalidating trained quantization points.

- **Surrogate bias formal guarantees lacking**: shallow slopes improve trainability but degrade alignment with true spike gradient; no unconditional guarantee of convergence to local optimum of hard SNN [5]. Second-order MAML meta-learning with twice-differentiable surrogate partially addresses [4] but doubles memory.

- **NoC scalability beyond 16k cores**: local synchronization dependency graph construction NP-hard; heuristic partitioning fails for highly recurrent SNNs with all-to-all connectivity (e.g., associative memory), locality 0.2 → speedup only 1.1× [7].

- **Dataset representativeness**: N-MNIST, DVS Gesture are low-resolution event streams; transfer to real-world asynchronous DVS with noise and temporal jitter untested. Behavioral cloning RL gap [5] remains large for long-horizon credit assignment >500 steps.

- **Security and adversarial robustness**: analog noise acts as implicit defense [2], but exploiting device-level non-idealities for adversarial attacks demonstrated [2] with 23% attack success increase. RRAM variation as PUF vs attack surface dual-use unresolved.

- **Open toolchains**: AIHWKit simulation omits capacitive coupling and driver resistance interplay; full SPICE co-simulation impractical for large SNN training iteration.

Ordered future work:

1.  Physics-informed neural compensator learning IR-drop correction table per column via on-chip calibration.
2.  Mixed-precision surrogate: sharp early layers, shallow deeper layers layer-wise schedule via gradient statistics.
3.  3-level hierarchical router as in DYNAPs [6] for multicast efficiency with software-programmable routing tables.
4.  Verification of deadlock-freedom for adaptive routing under spike burst traffic using model checking.

## 7. Conclusion

Neuromorphic ReRAM CIM for SNNs sits at intersection of device physics, learning theory, and interconnect architecture. We synthesized evidence that **variation-aware training** [1][3], **adaptive surrogate gradients** [4][5], and **locality-driven NoC mapping** [6][7] together recover software-level accuracy while retaining analog energy benefits. Key insight: treating ReRAM stochastic noise, surrogate bias, and NoC hop latency as first-class citizens in loss formulation — not afterthought — yields Pareto superiority over isolated optimizations.

The path forward is co-design: chips exposing programmable neuron parameters $\beta, V_{th}, k$ to compiler; training loops incorporating hardware-calibrated noise models; mappers exploiting spike correlation for placement. As deterministic local synchronization replaces global barriers [7], neuromorphic CIM scales to million-neuron SNNs at milliwatt power envelopes.

*Practical takeaway*: 2-bit/cell HfO2 1T1R 128×128 crossbars with 6-bit ADC, differential encoding, noise-injected PSG training with schedule $k=10\to25$, and spectral clustering mapping achieve <1% accuracy degradation from FP32 ANN at 12× energy reduction — making always-on event-driven perception feasible on edge.
