# Diffeomorphic Shape Registration: LDDMM, Geodesic Shooting, and the Geometry of Computational Anatomy

## Abstract

Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping (LDDMM) formulates anatomical shape comparison as the search for shortest paths — *geodesics* — on the infinite-dimensional Lie group of diffeomorphisms equipped with a right-invariant Sobolev metric [1][3]. This article develops the geometric core of the framework: the variational problem of Beg, Miller, Trouvé and Younes, its Euler–Lagrange system, and the Euler–Poincaré equation on diffeomorphisms (EPDiff) identified by Holm and Marsden [4]. We prove the conservation-of-momentum theorem underlying geodesic shooting [2], which compresses an entire registration to a single initial momentum, and derive the singular momentum-map solutions — landmarks, filaments and sheets — that make exact mesh-free computation possible [5][6]. Existence theory [7], kernel and metric design, empirical validation in neuroanatomy, and the framework's limitations are treated in turn: a self-contained account of why diffeomorphic registration is geometry first and optimization second.

---

## 1. Introduction

The intellectual lineage of computational anatomy begins with D'Arcy Thompson's *On Growth and Form* (1917), which proposed that biological shape differences be understood as coordinate transformations of a common template. Grenander's pattern theory later supplied the probabilistic language: anatomy as *deformable templates*, random objects generated by acting on a prototype with a group of transformations. Michael Miller's computational anatomy program made this concrete by insisting that the transformation group be the **diffeomorphism group** — smooth, invertible maps with smooth inverses — so that registration can never tear tissue, fold space, or fuse structures that were distinct.

Early dense registration methods, notably Christensen's viscous-fluid algorithm, produced diffeomorphic flows but with no associated notion of *optimal* flow: there was no least-action principle scoring one deformation against another, and therefore no metric geometry on shape space. The turning point was Dupuis, Grenander and Miller (1998), who established existence of solutions for variational problems on flows of diffeomorphisms penalized by kinetic energy measured in a Sobolev norm [7]. Faisal Beg's doctoral work then derived the Euler–Lagrange equations for dense image matching under least action and implemented the first algorithm solving them [1]; Miller, Trouvé and Younes subsequently showed that every optimal flow is a geodesic whose entire history is encoded in a single initial *momentum*, giving rise to geodesic shooting [2]. The synthesis of these ideas — variational calculus, Lie-group geometry, and momentum conservation — is the subject of this article.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The diffeomorphism group as a Lie group

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ (typically $d = 2, 3$) be the image domain and let $\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)$ denote the group of diffeomorphisms under composition. Its Lie algebra is the space $\mathfrak{X}(\Omega)$ of smooth vector fields, and a curve $\varphi_t \in \mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)$ is generated by a time-dependent Eulerian velocity field $v_t$ through the flow equation

$$\dot{\varphi}_t = v_t \circ \varphi_t, \qquad \varphi_0 = \mathrm{id}. \tag{1}$$

The velocity fields are taken in a Hilbert space $(V, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_V)$ defined by a positive self-adjoint differential operator $L: V \to V^*$ via $\langle v, w \rangle_V = \int_\Omega Lv \cdot w \, dx$. When $L$ dominates a Sobolev norm of order $s > d/2 + 1$, the embedding $V \hookrightarrow C^1_0$ guarantees that solutions of (1) are genuine diffeomorphisms [7]. Crucially, $V$ is then a **reproducing kernel Hilbert space** (RKHS) whose kernel $K = L^{-1}$ is the Green's operator — in practice a Gaussian or multi-scale smoothing kernel.

### 2.2 Group actions on anatomical data

Diffeomorphisms act on the objects of anatomy:

- **Images:** $(\varphi \cdot I)(x) = I \circ \varphi^{-1}(x)$ (right action);
- **Landmarks:** $\varphi \cdot \{q_i\} = \{\varphi(q_i)\}$ [6];
- **Curves and surfaces:** via currents, varifolds, or direct parameterization;
- **Tensor fields:** via push-forward of the Jacobian.

A *shape* is an orbit $\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega) \cdot I_0$ of a template $I_0$. The right-invariant metric

$$d_V(\mathrm{id}, \varphi) = \inf_{v: \, \dot{\varphi} = v \circ \varphi} \int_0^1 \|v_t\|_V \, dt \tag{2}$$

then descends to a true metric on shape space (modulo the isotropy subgroup fixing the template), which is what makes *metric* mapping possible at all [3].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The LDDMM variational problem

Given a template image $I_0$ and target $I_1$, Beg *et al.* [1] pose dense matching as

$$\min_{v: \, \dot{\varphi}_t = v_t \circ \varphi_t} C(v) \triangleq \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 \|v_t\|_V^2 \, dt + \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\|I_0 \circ \varphi_1^{-1} - I_1\|_{L^2}^2. \tag{3}$$

The first term is the **kinetic-energy action** enforcing least-action regularity; the second is the endpoint data-attachment term. Taking variations with respect to the vector-field parameterization yields the Euler–Lagrange system: a forward state equation (1), a backward adjoint (costate) equation transporting the endpoint image-mismatch force

$$f = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\,(I_0 \circ \varphi_1^{-1} - I_1)\, \nabla(I_0 \circ \varphi_1^{-1}),$$

and the optimality condition $v_t = -K \star m_t$, where $m_t = Lv_t$ is the **momentum** and $\star$ denotes convolution with the kernel. Beg's algorithm iterates this fixed-point system — forward flow, backward force transport, kernel smoothing — until convergence, and was the first code to compute dense matches satisfying the necessary conditions for least action [1].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 From Euler–Lagrange to geodesics

A decisive observation is that minimizers of (3) satisfy, on the open interval $(0,1)$, the *homogeneous* geodesic equation: the data term enters only through the endpoint (transversality) condition. Consequently every optimal deformation is a **geodesic** of the right-invariant metric (2), and registration reduces to the finite-dimensional-in-spirit problem of finding the right initial conditions for a geodesic flow. This is the conceptual pivot on which the rest of the theory turns.

### 4.2 EPDiff: the Euler–Poincaré equation on diffeomorphisms

Holm and Marsden identified the geodesic equation in Eulerian form as the **Euler–Poincaré equation associated to the diffeomorphism group** — EPDiff [4]. For the right-invariant Lagrangian $\ell(v) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|v\|_V^2$, reduction by symmetry gives, with $m = \delta\ell/\delta v = Lv$,

$$\partial_t m + \mathrm{ad}^*_{v_t} m = 0, \qquad v_t = K \star m_t, \tag{4}$$

or in coordinates,

$$\partial_t m + Dm \cdot v + (Dv)^T m + m\,\mathrm{div}\, v = 0. \tag{5}$$

This single equation unifies several classical PDEs: with the $L^2$ metric it is the **Euler equation of ideal fluids**; in one dimension with the $H^1$ metric it is the **Camassa–Holm** shallow-water equation; and it coincides with the averaged template matching equations of computer vision [4]. Holm and Marsden proved short-time well-posedness for smooth data and, more strikingly, showed that smooth solutions generically break up into *singular* measure-valued solutions — the subject of §4.4.

### 4.3 Geodesic shooting and conservation of momentum

> **Theorem (Conservation of momentum [2]).** Let $\varphi_t$ be a geodesic of the right-invariant metric on $\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)$ with Eulerian momentum $m_t = Lv_t$. Then
>
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\, \mathrm{Ad}^*_{\varphi_t} m_t = 0, \qquad \text{i.e. } m_t = \mathrm{Ad}^*_{\varphi_t^{-1}} m_0,$$
>
> where $\mathrm{Ad}^*$ is the coadjoint action on vector densities, $(\mathrm{Ad}^*_\varphi m)(x) = |\det D\varphi(x)|\, D\varphi(x)^T m(\varphi(x))$. The momentum is Lie-transported along the flow: **the entire geodesic is determined by the single initial datum $m_0$**.

This is the engine of **geodesic shooting** [2]. Define the Riemannian exponential $\mathrm{Exp}_{\mathrm{id}}: V \to \mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)$ by integrating EPDiff from initial velocity $v_0$ (equivalently $m_0 = Lv_0$) to time $1$. Registration then becomes optimization over the *initial momentum alone*:

$$\min_{m_0} \; \|m_0\|_{V^*}^2 + \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\,E_{\text{data}}\big(\mathrm{Exp}_{\mathrm{id}}(K \star m_0) \cdot I_0\big), \tag{6}$$

with $\|m_0\|_{V^*}^2 = \langle m_0, K \star m_0 \rangle_{L^2}$. Two further facts make this practical:

1. **Normal momentum constraint.** For image matching, optimal momenta are *normal* to the level sets: $m_0 = \alpha_0 \, \nabla I_0$ for a scalar field $\alpha_0$. The momentum has the *same dimension as the data* — one scalar per voxel — rather than a full vector field per time step [2].
2. **Constant-speed geodesics.** EPDiff is Hamiltonian, so kinetic energy $\|v_t\|_V^2$ is conserved along solutions; the time integral in (3) collapses to its value at $t = 0$, which is exactly the $\|m_0\|_{V^*}^2$ term in (6).

Shooting thus replaces optimization over a time-indexed path with optimization over one momentum field — a dramatic dimensionality reduction that also yields the deformation as an analyzable statistical object: initial momenta live in a *linear* space and can be averaged, PCA-decomposed, and regressed directly [2].

### 4.4 Singular solutions: the momentum map and diffeons

The most computationally consequential result of Holm–Marsden theory is the **singular solution ansatz** [4]:

$$m(x,t) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \int P_a(s,t)\,\delta\big(x - Q_a(s,t)\big) \, ds, \tag{7}$$

supported on points ($k=0$), filaments ($k=1$) or sheets ($k=2$) in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Holm, Trouvé and Younes proved that (7) is a **momentum map** $J: T^*\mathrm{Emb}(S,\mathbb{R}^n) \to \mathfrak{X}^*(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for the left action of diffeomorphisms on embeddings, hence a Poisson map; the parameters $(Q_a, P_a)$ consequently obey *canonical Hamiltonian dynamics* [5]:

$$\dot{Q}_a = \sum_b G(Q_a - Q_b)\,P_b, \qquad \dot{P}_a = -\sum_b (P_a \cdot P_b)\,\nabla G(Q_a - Q_b), \tag{8}$$

with $G$ the kernel. For landmarks ($k = 0$) this reproduces exactly the large-deformation landmark-matching system of Joshi and Miller [6] — landmarks are *zero-dimensional singular solutions of EPDiff*, the higher-dimensional generalizations of Camassa–Holm peakons. The practical payoff is exact, mesh-free registration of point sets: geodesic shooting on (8) requires no spatial grid at all.

### 4.5 Kernels, metrics, and the quotient geometry of shape

The operator $L$ — equivalently its kernel $K$ — is the *prior*: it decides which deformations are cheap. Common choices and their roles:

| Kernel | Form | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Gaussian $K_\sigma$ | $\exp(-\|x-y\|^2/2\sigma^2)$ | Single-scale smoothness; $\sigma$ sets feature size |
| Multi-scale sum $\sum_n c_n K_{\sigma_n}$ | weighted Gaussians | Coarse-to-fine; captures sulci and gyri jointly |
| Sobolev $(\alpha I - \Delta)^{-k}$ | Matérn-type | Explicit smoothness order; $k > d/2+1/2$ for $C^1$ flows |

Because $\|v\|_V^2 = \langle m, K \star m \rangle$, the metric penalizes momentum through the kernel: high-frequency momenta are expensive under smooth kernels, which is precisely the regularity that keeps $\varphi_t$ diffeomorphic. The induced distance descends to the shape quotient $\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)/\mathrm{Diff}_{I_0}(\Omega)$, giving a Riemannian structure on anatomical shape space itself — the foundation for geodesic regression, parallel transport of longitudinal change, and momentum-based morphometry [1][2].

| Method | Parameterization | Metric? | Guaranteed diffeomorphic? |
|---|---|---|---|
| LDDMM (Beg 2005) [1] | time-varying $v_t$ | right-invariant Sobolev; true geodesic distance | yes |
| Geodesic shooting (MTY 2006) [2] | initial momentum $m_0$ | same geometry, compressed | yes |
| Stationary velocity fields | single $v$ | log-Euclidean approximation | yes |
| Demons / optical flow | displacement field | none | no |

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Energy conservation along EPDiff geodesics (proof sketch)

*Claim.* $\frac{d}{dt}\|v_t\|_V^2 = 0$ along solutions of (4).

*Proof.* $\|v\|_V^2 = \langle m, v \rangle$ with $m = Lv$. Differentiating and using (5) together with $v = K \star m$,

$$\frac{d}{dt}\langle m, v \rangle = 2\langle \partial_t m, v \rangle = -2\langle \mathrm{ad}^*_v m, v \rangle = -2\langle m, [v, v] \rangle = 0,$$

by skew-symmetry of the Lie bracket pairing. ∎

Hence geodesics have constant speed, the action $\int_0^1 \|v_t\|_V^2 dt$ equals $\|v_0\|_V^2$, and the variational problem genuinely measures *length* — not an arbitrary regularization.

### 5.2 Euler–Poincaré reduction (proof sketch)

For a right-invariant Lagrangian $L(\varphi, \dot{\varphi}) = \ell(v)$, $v = \dot{\varphi}\circ\varphi^{-1}$, variations $\delta\varphi$ induce constrained variations $\delta v = \dot{w} + [v, w]$ with $w = \delta\varphi \circ \varphi^{-1}$ vanishing at endpoints. Stationarity $\delta\int \ell(v)dt = 0$ gives $\int \langle \delta\ell/\delta v, \dot{w} + \mathrm{ad}_v w \rangle dt = 0$, and integration by parts yields exactly (4). The coadjoint form $\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Ad}^*_{\varphi_t} m_t = 0$ follows by differentiating the pairing $\langle \mathrm{Ad}^*_{\varphi_t} m_t, w_0 \rangle = \langle m_t, \mathrm{Ad}_{\varphi_t} w_0 \rangle$ and using (4) — the computation at the heart of the shooting theorem [2].

### 5.3 Empirical validation

- **Algorithmic.** Beg *et al.* demonstrated dense matching of synthetic and brain images with semi-Lagrangian integration of the Euler equations, computing metric distances $\int_0^1 \|v_t\|_V dt$ between anatomical configurations [1]. Geodesic shooting reproduces these matches while optimizing only over $m_0$, with the momentum fields providing compact, comparable deformation signatures [2].
- **Neuroanatomical.** The momentum representation has been deployed for hippocampal morphometry in Alzheimer's disease, cingulate-gyrus studies in schizophrenia, and cardiac shape analysis — in each case the *initial momentum*, not the displacement field, serves as the biomarker, because it lives in a linear space amenable to standard statistics [3].
- **Landmarks and curves.** Exact shooting on the singular system (8) gives sub-voxel landmark accuracy without grids [6]; the framework extends to curves, surfaces and unlabeled point sets via currents and varifolds.

*Figure 4 — From pairwise to population: subject shapes register to a template via LDDMM geodesics; their momenta are parallel-transported to the template, where linear statistics (means, PCA) quantify group morphometric differences — the computational-anatomy pipeline.*

The landmark Hamiltonian system is simple enough to implement directly:

```python
import numpy as np

def gaussian_kernel(q, sigma):
    d2 = ((q[:, None, :] - q[None, :, :]) ** 2).sum(-1)
    return np.exp(-d2 / (2 * sigma ** 2))

def shoot_landmarks(q0, p0, sigma, steps=50):
    """Integrate Hamilton's equations (8) for landmark LDDMM geodesics."""
    dt, q, p = 1.0 / steps, q0.copy(), p0.copy()
    for _ in range(steps):
        K = gaussian_kernel(q, sigma)
        dq = K @ p                                   # qdot = K(q) p
        dK = (-K[..., None]
              * (q[:, None, :] - q[None, :, :]) / sigma ** 2)
        dp = -0.5 * np.einsum('i,ijk,k->ij', p, dK, p)  # pdot
        q, p = q + dt * dq, p + dt * dp              # symplectic Euler
    return q, p
```

## 6. Limitations

1. **Computational cost.** Kernel convolutions are naively $O(N^2)$; dense 3D shooting needs FFT-based or fast-multipole evaluation and careful GPU implementation.
2. **Local minima.** The shooting energy (6) is non-convex; poor initialization (e.g., large rotations) strands the optimizer. Affine pre-registration is essentially mandatory.
3. **Fixed topology.** Diffeomorphisms cannot tear, fuse, or create structures. Lesions, resections, and topological change require extensions such as metamorphosis, which augments the flow with an intensity residual.
4. **Kernel subjectivity.** The metric — and therefore every distance, mean and p-value — depends on the chosen $K$. Multi-scale kernels mitigate but do not eliminate this prior dependence.
5. **Photometric assumptions.** The $L^2$ data term assumes intensity correspondence; multi-modal or contrast-varying data need mutual-information or feature-based attachments.
6. **Statistical transport.** Comparing momenta across subjects requires parallel transport along inter-subject geodesics, itself a numerically delicate PDE problem.

---

## 7. Conclusion

LDDMM turned anatomical registration from a regularization problem into a *geometric* one: shapes are points in the orbit of a template under $\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega)$, distances are lengths of geodesics, and every optimal deformation is summarized by its initial momentum [1][2]. The Euler–Poincaré equation on diffeomorphisms [4] supplies the dynamics, the conservation-of-momentum theorem supplies the compression, and the singular momentum map [5] supplies exact finite-dimensional realizations for landmarks, curves and surfaces [6]. Existence theory [7] guarantees the variational problems are well-posed, and two decades of neuroanatomical application confirm that geodesic distance and momentum are biologically meaningful morphometric quantities [3]. The geometry does the heavy lifting; optimization merely walks downhill on a manifold that mathematicians already understand.
