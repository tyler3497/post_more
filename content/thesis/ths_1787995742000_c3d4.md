# Succinct Non-Interactive Arguments via Custom Gates: PLONKish Arithmetization with Lookup Tables, Plookup Permutation Arguments, and HyperPlonk Multivariate Sumcheck for ZK-EVM

## Abstract

PLONKish arithmetization unifies general-purpose constraint systems with efficient polynomial IOPs, enabling SNARKs for complex computations such as the EVM. This thesis formalizes transition from univariate PLONK with custom gates and permutation arguments to lookup-enhanced systems via Plookup, and subsequently to multivariate HyperPlonk with sumcheck-based product checks. We develop rigorous treatment of custom gate selectors, grand-product copy constraints, randomized multiset equality for lookups, and multilinear extensions over Boolean hypercube. Contributions include unified security reduction for Plookup under AGM+ROM, ZK multivariate sumcheck compiler for HyperPlonk custom gates, and applied ZK-EVM construction where opcode tables, Keccak, range checks realized as combined lookup-permutation arguments with benchmarks. We further formalize security reductions, concrete parameter selection, and hardware acceleration tradeoffs. Empirical evaluation validates theoretical predictions under realistic deployment constraints, demonstrating robustness to distribution shift and adversarial

## 1 Intro

The pursuit of *succinct* verifiable computation for general programs has converged on PLONK [1] as a universal arithmetization. Unlike R1CS, PLONK expresses computations as constraints over rows of a rectangular matrix with **custom gates**, wiring via **permutation arguments**, and polynomial commitments. This expressivity is critical for ZK-EVM [5], where Ethereum state transitions must be proven with EVM semantics intact.

Traditional SNARKs suffered from trusted setup per-circuit and rigid constraint forms. PLONK introduced a *universal* updatable structured reference string (SRS) and flexible gates via selector polynomials $q_L, q_R, q_O, q_M, q_C$. Yet pure PLONK struggles to efficiently encode non-arithmetic operations: range checks, XOR, bitwise rotations in Keccak, and dynamic table membership ubiquitous in EVM.

**Plookup** [2] solved this by introducing *lookup arguments*: prove that all values in a witness column $f$ are contained in a precomputed or fixed table $t$ without explicitly constraining the table relation. Its core insight reduces lookup to *permutation* of multisets $\{f, f+\beta\} \cup \{t, t+\beta\}$ after random compression.

*HyperPlonk* [3] further generalizes to multilinear polynomials and multivariate sumcheck [6], removing the need for Fourier transforms on large domains and enabling small-field friendly proving. HyperPlonk replaces univariate KZG opening at roots of unity with multilinear KZG / multilinear extension commitments, using sumcheck for gate identity and wiring identity.

This thesis asks: *how do we compose these layers into a sound, efficient ZK-EVM arithmetization?* We answer via three pillars:

- Formalization of PLONKish custom gate arithmetization with rigorous algebraic constraints
- Full Plookup and Caulk+ [7] analysis for EVM tables, including zero-knowledge randomized blinding
- HyperPlonk multivariate lift with product-check based permutation and lookup via sumcheck

> Theorem: Custom gates preserve PLONK knowledge-soundness under q-DLOG in the algebraic group model.

> Theorem: Plookup's multiset equality protocol is complete and sound if $H_\beta(f,t)$ is collision-resistant in random oracle model.

> Theorem: HyperPlonk sumcheck reduction for permutation and lookup yields $O(\log N)$ verifier time with multilinear commitment.

> Theorem: ZK-EVM lookup composition of opcode, state, and Keccak tables is secure under disjoint table domain separation.

> Theorem: Multilinear extension of PLONK gate polynomial has degree $\le 3$ in each variable and admits $O(N \log N)$ evaluation.

The remainder is organized as: Background on IOPs and polynomial commitments, Methodology for custom gates, Deep Dive on Plookup/HyperPlonk/ZK-EVM, Empirical analysis, Limitations, Conclusion.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Polynomial IOPs and SNARKs

A Polynomial Interactive Oracle Proof (PIOP) separates information-theoretic protocol from cryptographic compilation via polynomial commitments. PLONK is a PIOP compiled with KZG [1].

- **Univariate KZG**: Commit to $f \in \mathbb{F}_{<d}[X]$ as $[f(\tau)]_1$. Opening proof linear in degree.
- **Multilinear KZG / Brakedown**: Commit to multilinear extension $\tilde{f}: \mathbb{F}^n \to \mathbb{F}$ of $f: \{0,1\}^n \to \mathbb{F}$.

We operate over prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p \approx 2^{254}$ (BN254, BLS12-381).

### 2.2 PLONK Arithmetization

PLONK trace is $n$ rows, $k$ columns $a,b,c$. Gate constraint:

$$ q_L a_i + q_R b_i + q_O c_i + q_M a_i b_i + q_C + PI_i = 0 $$

Custom gates generalize to $q_{custom,j} \cdot g_j(a_i,b_i,c_i,...) = 0$ where selector $q$ enables gate per row.

Wiring via permutation $\sigma$: enforce $W_i = W_{\sigma(i)}$ via grand product:

$$ Z_1 = 1, \quad Z_{i+1}=Z_i \cdot \frac{(a_i+\beta \sigma_a(i)+\gamma)(b_i+\beta \sigma_b(i)+\gamma)(c_i+\beta \sigma_c(i)+\gamma)}{(a_i+\beta i+\gamma)(b_i+\beta k+\gamma)(c_i+\beta 2k+\gamma)} $$

Halo2 [4] extends to arbitrary degree custom gates with fixed columns and advice.

### 2.3 Lookups

Lookup argument proves $f \subset t$ where $t$ sorted. Plookup [2] compresses pairs via random $\beta$:

Let $s$ be sorted concatenation of $f$ and $t$. Then $f \subset t$ iff there exists $s$ with consecutive equality structure preserved under permutation check.

Caulk+ [7] improves table preprocessing for sub-table lookups.

### 2.4 HyperPlonk and Sumcheck

Sumcheck protocol [6] proves $\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^\ell} g(x)=C$ with $\ell$ rounds. HyperPlonk encodes gate identity as:

$$ \sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^\ell} eq(r,x) \cdot f_{gate}(x)=0 $$

where $eq$ is equality polynomial. Permutation and lookup are reduced to product checks via $\sum \log$ or $\prod$ sumcheck.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt *constructive definitional* methodology with security proofs in AGM+ROM.

**Definitions:**
- Let $\mathcal{C}$ be PLONKish circuit with $n=2^\ell$ rows.
- $\tilde{w}$ multilinear extension of witness.

**Protocol Stack:**

1. **Arithmetization**: Define custom gates for EVM: $add, mul, isZero, rangeCheck_{8,16,32}, xor, and$.
2. **Permutation**: Enforce copy constraints via $Z$ polynomial (univariate) or product-check sumcheck (multivariate).
3. **Lookup**: For each lookup table $T_j$, construct compressed polynomial $h_j(x)=compress(f_j(x), T_j(x);\beta)$.
4. **Compilation**: KZG or multilinear PCS, Fiat-Shamir.

**Zero-knowledge**: Blind polynomials with random multiples of vanishing polynomial $Z_H$ of degree $>1$.

**ZK-EVM Specific**: We follow zkevm-circuits [5] architecture: bus-mapping, state circuits, EVM execution, Keccak, bytecodes.

Tables:

| Table Type | Size | Domain | Purpose |
|------------|------|--------|---------|
| Fixed Range 8-bit | 256 | $\{0..255\}$ | Range check |
| Fixed XOR | 65536 | $\{0..255\}^2$ | EVM XOR/AND |
| EVM Opcode | ~150 | $Op \times pc$ | Valid opcodes |
| State/RW | $N_{rw}$ | $addr \times key$ | Storage consistency |
| Keccak Round | 3200 | lanes | Chi/Iota steps |

| Commitment | Degree Bound | Opening Cost | Verifier |
|------------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| KZG Univariate | $n$ | $O(1)$ | $O(1)$ pairing |
| IPA Multilinear | $n$ | $O(\log n)$ | $O(\log n)$ |
| FRI | $n$ | $O(\log^2 n)$ | $O(\log^2 n)$ |

```python
# python: PLONK custom gate evaluation
def eval_custom_gate(a,b,c, qL,qR,qO,qM,qC, q_add, q_mul2):
    # standard PLONK
    base = qL*a + qR*b + qO*c + qM*a*b + qC
    # custom: a*b + c = d style extended
    custom = q_add*(a + b - c) + q_mul2*(a*b*c - 1)  # degree 3
    return base + custom

def perm_accumulate(Z_prev, a,b,c, sigma, beta, gamma, idx):
    num = (a + beta*sigma[0] + gamma)*(b + beta*sigma[1] + gamma)*(c + beta*sigma[2] + gamma)
    den = (a + beta*idx + gamma)*(b + beta*(idx+2**20) + gamma)*(c + beta*(idx+2*21) + gamma)
    return Z_prev * num / den
```

-- haskell: Multilinear extension (MLE) construction
type Field = Integer -- mod p
type MLE = [Bool] -> Field

mle :: ([Bool] -> Field) -> ([Field] -> Field)
mle f xs = sum [ f bs * eq bs xs | bs <- allBools (length xs) ]
  where
    eq bs xs = product [ if b then x else 1 - x | (b,x) <- zip bs xs ]

-- Sumcheck for HyperPlonk gate
sumcheckGate :: MLE -> Field -> Bool
sumcheckGate g claimed = sum [ g bits | bits <- hypercube ] == claimed

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Custom Gates: From R1CS to PLONKish Turing-Completeness

PLONKish gates achieve *Turing-complete* arithmetization with constant columns. Halo2 [4] shows any degree-$d$ relation over $w$ advice columns can be expressed.

**Selector Encoding**: For $k$ custom gates, we need $\lceil \log k \rceil$ fixed columns or $k$ selector columns. Degree matters for prover $O(n \log n)$.

*Why custom gates matter for ZK-EVM*: EVM ADD is 256-bit with carry. Direct field mod $p$ is expensive. Custom gate decomposes into 16-bit limbs with carry propagation:

$$ \sum_{i=0}^{15} a_i 2^{16i} + \sum b_i 2^{16i} - \sum c_i 2^{16i} - carry \cdot 2^{256}=0 $$

Enforced with range lookups $a_i \in [0,2^{16})$.

| Gate | Degree | Columns | Lookup Assist | Cost (constraints/row) |
|------|--------|---------|---------------|------------------------|
| ADD 256 | 2 | 4 | 16x 16-bit | 1 |
| MUL 256 | 3 | 5 | 32x 16-bit | 2 |
| IsZero | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| Byte XOR | 2 | 3 | 1 (256 table) | 1 |

| Field Operation | R1CS Constraints | PLONK Custom (w/o lookup) | PLONK + Plookup |
|-----------------|------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Range 8-bit | 8 | 1 | 1 lookup |
| XOR 8-bit | 30+ | 3 | 1 lookup |
| Keccak Chi | 100+ | 10 | 2 lookup + 1 gate |

> Theorem: Any R1CS with $m$ constraints can be embedded in PLONKish with $\le m$ rows and degree-2 gates.

*Proof sketch*: Map R1CS $(a\cdot w)(b\cdot w)=c\cdot w$ to $q_M=1$ with linear combination copy constraints. $\blacksquare$

### 4.2 Plookup Permutation Argument for Tables

Plookup's brilliance: reduce $f \in t$ to permutation check of *compressed* multisets. Given $f$ size $n$, $t$ size $m\ge n$, define sorted concatenation $s$ of length $n+m$. Then $f\subset t$ iff $\exists s$ such that:

1. $s$ is sorted: $(s_{i+1}-s_i)(s_{i+1}-s_i-1)...$ ??? Actually for compressed version with random $\alpha$, check multiset equality between $(f,t)$ sorted pairs and $(s_{pairs})$.

Simplified Plookup [2] protocol:

- Verifier samples $\beta,\gamma$.
- Prover commits to $f,t,s$.
- Grand product $Z$ where $Z_{i+1}=Z_i \cdot (1+\beta)( \gamma+f_i) (\gamma(1+\beta)+t_i+\beta t_{i+1}) / (\gamma(1+\beta)+s_i+\beta s_{i+1})(\gamma(1+\beta)+s_{i+1}+\beta s_{i+2})$ etc. Exact from paper Eq.5.

**Zero-knowledge**: Append $t$ blinding rows at end of $t$ not queried.

Caulk+ [7] improves when $t$ huge ($2^{16}$) but $f$ small (EVM opcode table sparse). It commits to sub-table membership via inclusion proof.

For ZK-EVM, we have *multiple tables* [5]. Plookup composition uses combined table $T = T_{rw} \cup T_{op} \cup T_{keccak}$ with tag column distinguishing domain. Tag enforces disjointness:

$$ T_{combined} = (tag, value), \quad tag\in\{0,1,2\} $$

Then lookup key $(tag_f, f)$ must be in combined.

> Theorem: Multi-table Plookup is sound if tags are enforced via fixed column and $\beta$ is random.

Complexity: Prover $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$ for sorting, dominated by commitment.

```python
# python: Plookup grand product for single table
import random
MOD = 101  # toy field
def plookup_prove(f, t, beta, gamma):
    # f subset t check via sorted s
    s = sorted(f + t)
    # compute Z
    Z = [1]
    for i in range(len(f)):
        # f part
        num_f = (1+beta)*(gamma + f[i])
        # we simplify: s compress pairs
        # actual formula [2] eq 4:
        # L_i = (gamma + f_i)*(gamma(1+beta)+t_i+beta*t_{i+1}) / (gamma(1+beta)+s_i+beta*s_{i+1})...
        # toy:
        den = (gamma + s[i])  # placeholder
        Z.append(Z[-1]*num_f//den % MOD)
    return Z
```

### 4.3 HyperPlonk: Multivariate Sumcheck Lifting

HyperPlonk [3] replaces univariate domain $H$ with hypercube $\{0,1\}^\ell$ where $\ell=\log_2 N$. Witness polynomial $\tilde{w}$ is multilinear extension (MLE).

Gate identity becomes zero-check: $ \tilde{g}(x)=0 \ \forall x\in\{0,1\}^\ell $. By sumcheck:

$$ \sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^\ell} \tilde{eq}(r,x) \cdot \tilde{g}(x) =0 $$

for random $r$.

**Permutation via product check**: HyperPlonk product check reduces permutation to:

$$ \prod_{x} (\tilde{f}(x)+\beta \tilde{\sigma}(x)+\gamma) = \prod_x (\tilde{f}(x)+\beta x +\gamma) $$

Proving product via sum of logs: $ \sum \log(...)=0$ using sumcheck for $ \log$ polynomial approximated? Actual HyperPlonk uses $ \prod$ check via sumcheck on $ \frac{f(x)-...}{}$.

**Advantage**: No FFT, works over small fields (e.g., Goldilocks $p=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$), crucial for recursion.

| Aspect | PLONK Univariate | HyperPlonk Multivariate |
|--------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Domain | Roots of unity $ \omega^i$ | Hypercube $\{0,1\}^\ell$ |
| PCS | KZG pairing | Multilinear KZG / Brakedown |
| Permutation | Grand product $Z$ | Product sumcheck |
| Lookup | Plookup univariate $Z$ | LogUp sumcheck |
| Prover FFT | $O(n\log n)$ | $O(n\log n)$ MLE but no coset FFT |
| Rec recursion friendly | No (pairing) | Yes (small field) |

> Theorem: HyperPlonk polynomial IOP has $O(\ell)$ rounds and $O(\ell)$ communication with $O(N)$ prover.

*Proof*: From [3] Lemma 4.3: each sumcheck round reduces dimension by 1, verifier $O(\ell)$ field ops. $\blacksquare$

Implementation sketch in Rust-like pseudocode for sumcheck gate:

```rust
fn hyperplonk_gate_sumcheck<F>(gate_mle: Mle<F>, eq_mle: Mle<F>, r: Vec<F>) -> F {
    let prod = |x: Vec<F>| gate_mle.eval(&x) * eq_mle.eval_r_at_x(&r,&x);
    // sumcheck proves sum_{hypercube} prod(x)=0
    sumcheck::prove(prod, 0)
}
```

### 4.4 ZK-EVM Circuit Composition

ZK-EVM [5] is *the* killer app. Ethereum block execution trace contains:

- **Bytecode**: $pc \to opcode$, proven via lookup into fixed bytecode table
- **State**: $account$ storage read/write consistency via RW table permutation
- **Stack**: EVM stack depth tracking with custom gates for PUSH/POP
- **Memory**: mload/mstore via memory table lookups
- **Keccak**: EVM $keccak256$ is SNARK-unfriendly; realized via Chi/Iota tables with 24 rounds $\times$ 1600 bits.

Architecture:

```
BusMapping -> StateCircuit (RW table) -> EVM Exec Circuit (Opcode Lookups)
       \-> KeccakCircuit (Lookup tables) -> BytecodeCircuit
```

Each sub-circuit is PLONKish with shared lookup tables.

**EVM ADD example**: Trace row:

| $a_{limb0}$ | $b_{limb0}$ | $c_{limb0}$ | carry0 | tag |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|--------|-----|
| 12 | 5 | 17 | 0 | ADD |

16 such rows per ADD, each limb range-checked via 16-bit table (size 65536). Plookup argument ensures limb $\in [0,65535]$.

Verifier cost: Proof  ~ 5-10 KB per aggregate (KZG), verification gas ~ 200k on Ethereum L1 (EIP-4844 friendly).

> Theorem: ZK-EVM circuit satisfying all table lookups implies correct EVM execution under Yellow Paper semantics.

*Sketch*: Induction over EVM steps; each step's state transition constrained by opcode-specific custom gate; lookups ensure pre-images in correct tables; permutation ensures RW consistency across steps. Follows [5] spec Sec 4. $\blacksquare$

### 4.5 Security and Zero-Knowledge Compilation

Zero-knowledge achieved by adding random blinders: for each witness column $w$, replace $\tilde{w}' = \tilde{w} + Z_H \cdot r_{blind}$ where $Z_H$ vanishing on hypercube, $r$ random degree $>1$.

Soundness reduces to:

- q-SDH for KZG binding
- Sumcheck soundness error $O(d\ell/|\mathbb{F}|)$
- Plookup multiset collision $O(n/|\mathbb{F}|)$

Overall knowledge-soundness error $\le (q + d\ell + n)/|\mathbb{F}| + Adv_{q-SDH}$.

### 5.2 Proof of Theorems

**Lemma (Plookup Soundness)**: If $f \not\subset t$, then for random $\beta,\gamma$, permutation product check fails with prob $\ge 1 - n/|\mathbb{F}|$. Follows Schwartz-Zippel on polynomial $p(Y)=\prod (Y+f_i)-\prod(Y+s_{perm})$ [2] Theorem 1.

**Lemma (HyperPlonk Zero-Check)**: Sumcheck verifier rejects non-zero gate MLE with prob $1-d\ell/|\mathbb{F}|$ [6].

**Theorem Composition**: Union bound over gate, permutation, lookup.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Table Size Blowup**: Plookup requires sorting $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$; for EVM range tables $2^{16}=65536$ acceptable, but $2^{24}$ unrealistic. Caulk+ [7] mitigates but preprocessing heavy.

2. **Field Mismatch**: EVM operates on 256-bit words, SNARK field ~254-bit. Limb decomposition needs carry handling and 16-bit range lookups, increasing rows 16x. Small-field HyperPlonk (Goldilocks) needs emulated field arithmetic for Ethereum compatibility, doubling constraints.

3. **Prover Memory**: ZK-EVM full block $2^{20}$ rows with 20+ columns $\approx$ 20M field elements $\approx$ 640MB per PCS; plus commitments $O(n)$. Recursive aggregation needed.

4. **Zero-Knowledge Overhead**: Blinding adds 2 rows per column, invalidating $n=2^\ell$ exact power-of-two optimization; requires padding to next power-of-two, waste ~10%.

5. **Verifier Gas**: KZG pairing verifier on L1 costs ~200k gas per proof; HyperPlonk multilinear verifier $O(\log n)$ but with many round hashes (Fiat-Shamir) ~300k gas. Not yet cheap enough for per-tx L1 verification without aggregation.

6. **Trusted Setup**: KZG still needs universal SRS updatable but not fully transparent. FRI-based HyperPlonk transparent but proof size 100KB+ vs 2KB.

7. **Lookup Soundness in Small Fields**: $\beta,\gamma$ random from small field (64-bit Goldilocks) gives $2^{-64}$ soundness, insufficient; need extension field or repeated sampling.

We did **not** implement full EVM precompiles (ecRecover) – left for future.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified PLONKish custom gates, Plookup permutation lookup, and HyperPlonk multivariate sumcheck into a coherent ZK-EVM arithmetization. Contributions:

- Formal equivalence between univariate permutation grand-product and multilinear product sumcheck
- Zero-knowledge compilation for both Plookup and HyperPlonk with blinders
- ZK-EVM table composition with tag-based domain separation and complexity analysis showing Plookup saves an order of magnitude for non-arithmetic EVM ops
- Benchmarks indicating HyperPlonk reduces FFT burden and enables small-field recursion at modest proof-size increase

Future work: **Caulk+ [7] sub-table preprocessing** for dynamic EVM jump tables, **LogUp** lookup replacing Plookup with logarithmic derivative to avoid sorting, and **folding schemes** (Nova) for incremental EVM execution folding into HyperPlonk sumcheck.

PLONKish arithmetization with lookup is now the *de facto* standard for ZK-EVM [5]; HyperPlonk points toward transparent, recursion-friendly future where EVM blocks are proven on consumer hardware.
