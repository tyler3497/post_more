# Category-Theoretic Semantics for Effect Handlers: Graded Monads, Lawvere Theories, and Denotational Adequacy

## Abstract
This thesis develops a unified category-theoretic semantics for algebraic effect handlers using graded monads, Lawvere theories, and enriched models. Starting from Plotkin and Power's algebraic effects and Plotkin and Pretnar's handlers, we show how effect signatures form countable Lawvere theories whose free-model monads classify computations, and how grading by preordered monoids and categories refines these monads to track quantitative and protocol-sensitive usage. We present the category-graded framework CatEff where computations $\Gamma \vdash_f M : A$ are interpreted as $\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket \to T_f\llbracket A\rrbracket$, unify graded and parameterised monads as lax functors $\Sigma(\mathcal M)\to \mathbf{Cat}$, and prove soundness and adequacy of denotational models with respect to operational semantics, including completeness via handling constructs and generic effects.

## 1 Introduction

Algebraic effects, introduced by Plotkin and Power [1][2] and equipped with handlers by Plotkin and Pretnar [3], have become the dominant modular approach to computational effects. Operations are specified by a signature $\Sigma$; terms are built from operations and then interpreted by handlers corresponding to algebras for the theory. This separates syntax from semantics, allowing the same program to be executed, logged, replayed, or tested by changing the handler.

Two orthogonal developments have clarified this picture categorically. First, **Lawvere theories** provide the canonical algebraic presentation: an effect theory $T$ is a finite-product category where morphisms $n\to 1$ are $n$-ary operations and models are product-preserving functors $T\to\mathbf{Set}$ [4][5]. Free models yield monads $T X = \mu Y . X + \sum_{op} \coprod$. Second, **graded monads** refine monads by indexing $T_e$ over a pomonoid $(E,\cdot,I,\le)$ to track effect information such as cost, access levels, or protocols, with unit $\eta: A\to T_I A$ and multiplication $\mu_{e,f}: T_e T_f \to T_{e\cdot f}$ [6][7].

This thesis synthesizes three threads: graded monads for quantitative reasoning, countable and enriched Lawvere theories for algebraic effects, and denotational adequacy and completeness for effect handler calculi. Our contribution:

* We reconstruct Plotkin-Pretnar handlers as homomorphisms induced by the universal property of free models over a Lawvere theory, and lift to graded and category-graded variants [8].
* We explain Katsumata's graded monads, Atkey's parameterised monads, and category-graded monads of Orchard et al. as lax functors, following Fujii and Katsumata [6][9].
* We prove an adequacy square: operational reduction $\leadsto$ is sound for denotational interpretation in sets / $\omega$-cpos, and with handlers it becomes complete relative to Plotkin's handling construct [3][10].

The thesis is aimed at researchers in semantics and functional languages (Eff, Koka, OCaml 5) seeking principled foundations for effect tracking.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Algebraic Effects and Handlers

Moggi's monadic semantics models effectful computations as $A\to T B$. Plotkin and Power factor $T$ as free model monad of equational theory $(\Sigma,\mathcal E)$ where $\Sigma$ assigns to each $op$ arity $A_{op} \to B_{op}$. A computation is:

* `return V`
* `op(V; y. M)`
* sequential composition

A handler $H: C \Rightarrow D$ specifies clauses $op(x)(k)\mapsto M_{op}$ and $return(x)\mapsto M_{ret}$. Operationally:

> **Theorem (Handler Correctness Sketch):** Handling preserves typing and satisfies $\beta$-laws: `with H handle (op(V; y. M))` reduces to $M_{op}[V/x][\lambda y. \text{with H handle } M / k]$.

Plotkin and Pretnar's seminal draft shows exception handlers, state rollback, stream redirection, timeout, and nondeterminism all arise from same pattern [3]. Statistical divergence was handled via monadic reflection vs. delimitated control translations [1][10].

### 2.2 Graded Monads

Let $(M,\le,\cdot,1)$ be pomonoid. An $M$-graded monad on $\mathbb C$ is family $T: M\to [\mathbb C,\mathbb C]$, with:

* $\eta: Id \Rightarrow T_1$
* $\mu_{m,n}: T_m T_n \Rightarrow T_{m\cdot n}$
* approximations $T_{m\le n}: T_m\Rightarrow T_n$ natural

satisfying graded unit and associativity: $\mu_{1,m}\circ T_1\eta = id$, etc. Katsumata [2014] shows graded monads arise via lax functors $\Sigma(M)\to\mathbf{Cat}$ where $\Sigma(M)$ is one-object 2-category from $M$ [7][6]. Examples:

* **Writer grading** by $(\mathbb N,+,0)$ tracks cost.
* **Read levels** by lattice $(\mathcal L,\sqcup)$ tracks security.
* **List effect** graded by $2^L$ tracking invoked labels [8].

Graded monads unify effect systems: judgement $\Gamma \vdash_e M:A$ meaning effects below $e$.

### 2.3 Parameterised and Category-Graded Monads

Atkey parameterised monad $T: S^{op}\times S \times \mathbf C \to \mathbf C$ models pre/postconditions: $T_{s,s'} A$ maps $s$-state to $s'$-state producing $A$. Orchard et al. unified both via category-graded monad: $T_f$ indexed by morphism $f\in\mathcal S$, where $\mathcal S$ is grading category [7][8]. Objects are resources, morphisms are protocols. Thus $send_{int}: 1\to int$ etc. can express session types.

### 2.4 Lawvere Theories

Lawvere theory $L$ is bijective-on-objects finite-product preserving functor $\aleph_0^{op}\to L$, where $\aleph_0$ skeleton of $\mathbf{FinSet}$. Models are product-preserving $M:L\to\mathbf{Set}$ [4]. Algebraic theories $(\Sigma,\mathcal E)$ induce Lawvere theory $L_{\Sigma/\mathcal E}$ and monad $T_{\Sigma/\mathcal E}$ as free-model left adjoint. Enriched Lawvere theories in $\mathbf{V}$ generalize to $\mathbf{Cpo}$, $\mathbf{Met}$, etc. [5]. Hyland-Power showed discrete countable Lawvere theories classify computational effects.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is **syntactic theory $\to$ categorical model $\to$ adequacy**.

**Step A – Signature to Theory.** From signature $\Sigma$ construct countable Lawvere theory $L_\Sigma$ with morphisms $n\to m$ as $m$-tuple of terms in $n$ variables. Equations $\mathcal E$ quotient $L_\Sigma$. Free model monad $T_{(\Sigma,\mathcal E)} X = \text{Term}_\Sigma(X)/\mathcal E$ [2].

**Step B – Grading.** Choose pomonoid $M$ or small category $\mathcal S$. Lift $T$ to $M$-graded $T_m$ via distributive law of grading over theory, or via fibred semantics. Indexed graded monads of Kura et al. allow dependent effects where grade depends on value [9].

**Step C – Handlers as Models.** Define handler model $\mathcal A$ over theory as object $X$ with interpretation $\llbracket op\rrbracket_{\mathcal A}: \llbracket A_{op}\rrbracket \to T_{\Sigma'} \llbracket B_{op}\rrbracket$. Handling $M$ with $H$ interpreted via unique homomorphism $h: T_\Sigma \llbracket A\rrbracket\to \mathcal A$ [3].

**Step D – Denotational semantics.** Interpret base types in cartesian closed $\mathbf C$, effect theory as strong monad family, handlers as natural transformations, and prove:

* **Soundness:** $\Gamma\vdash M=N$ implies $\llbracket M\rrbracket=\llbracket N\rrbracket$
* **Adequacy:** $M\Downarrow$ iff $\llbracket M\rrbracket\neq\bot$ (domain case)
* **Completeness:** Semantic equality implies provable equality relative to handler equational theory [10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Graded Monads as lax 2-Functors and Effect Systems

Fujii et al. observe monad on $\mathbb C$ is lax 2-functor $1\to \mathbf{Cat}(\mathbb C,\mathbb C)$ [6]. Grading expands domain to $\Sigma(M)$. This 2-categorical view explains Eilenberg-Moore and Kleisli graded categories cleanly. In CatEff calculus [8], typing:

```
Γ ⊢f M : A   means   ⟦M⟧ : ⟦Γ⟧ → T_f ⟦A⟧
```

Composition requires $g\circ f$ grading. Key example: state protocols

| Grade | Meaning | Composite |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| τ_{s→t} | internal state change s to t | τ∘τ |
| send_int | emit int, state 1→int | send; τ |
| recv_int | receive int, state int→1 | recv; send |

Category-graded monads rule out ill-protocolled compositions not merely by abstract join $\sqcup$ but by existence of morphism. This mirrors Hoare triples: no morphism $f:s\to t$ => composition invalid.

Graded monads also enable **quantitative reasoning**. Kura-Gaboardi et al. [9] build indexed graded monad for dependent cost, probability bounds, expectation bounds. E.g., cost type `C[ Nat{e} ]` where `e` depends on argument.

### 4.2 Lawvere Theories for Handlers and Enrichment

Traditional Lawvere theory handles finitary effects with *generic effects* view: operation `op: A → B` corresponds to generic effect `g: 1→ T B^A` [2]. Diagrammatically:

```
      η
  X → T X
      ↓ T f
  T Y  with op_Y : (T Y)^? → T Y
```

Handlers interpret `T` as different monad $T'$. Hyland et al. [5] show enriched Lawvere theories solve domain-theoretic recursion: moving $\mathbf V=\mathbf{Cpo}$ gives least fixed points for `TX = μY. X + F Y`. Thus complete semantics requires enriched theories for recursion and iteration.

Consider table of theories to monads:

| Theory | Operations | Equations | Monad |
|--------|------------|-----------|-------|
| Exceptions E | raise_e : 0 | none | X+E |
| State S | get: S, put: S→1 | lens laws | (X×S)^S |
| Nondet | or:2 | semilattice | P_fin |
| I/O | read: Σ, write_σ:1 | none free | μY. X + Σ×Y + Σ→? |

This explains why handlers for state can implement exceptions via rollback: both are models of respective theories over same carrier.

Deep handlers vs shallow: deep handler recursively applies handling in continuation; shallow applies once then re-handles with possibly different algebra. Richly exhibitible as folds vs case splits over computation trees [3][10]. Deep handlers correspond to $T$-algebra homomorphism property:

> **Theorem (Free Algebra Universal Property):** For theory $\mathcal T$, carrier $X$, handling interpretation $\mathcal A$, there exists unique homomorphism $h: Free_{\mathcal T}(X)\to \mathcal A$ extending return clause.

This is precisely Plotkin-Pretnar handling semantics.

### 4.3 Combining Graded Monad and Lawvere Theory for Protocols

Sanada [8] defines **category-graded algebraic theory**: term judgement $\vdash_f t$ where $f$ morphism in $\mathcal S$. Rules: variable has grade id, operation $op$ applied to continuation graded $f$ yields $op;f$. Soundness: $\llbracket t\rrbracket \in T_f$. CatEff language:

```haskell
-- graded operation
op sendInt : unit -> int  with grade send_int

-- graded handler
handler protocolH : A!{send,recv} => B!{τ}
  = { sendInt(x)(k) -> ... ; recvInt()(k) -> ... ; return(x) -> ... }
```

Handling concatenates grading. Example `sendInt >> recvInt` graded by `send;recv`. If protocol expects receive before send, no morphism, term ill-typed. This lifts from effect tracking to behavioural typing akin to session types, but grounded in category theory rather than ad-hoc.

Dependent effect systems [9] generalise further: grade may refer to value `x:A` via fibration $p: \mathbb E\to \mathbb B$. Indexed graded monad $T_{e(b)} : \mathbb E_b \to \mathbb E_b$ gives refinement types `{ v:A | φ(e)}`.

*Methodological workflow* we follow:

1. **Signature** to Lawvere theory construction via free product completion
2. **Grading** by pomonoid/category assigning $T_f$ to each morphism grade
3. **Handling** as model interpretation yielding homomorphism $h: Free\to \mathcal A$
4. **Adequacy** proof by logical relations and TT-lifting to Cpo-enriched setting
5. **Completeness** via syntactic model reflection

### 4.4 Denotational Adequacy and Completeness

Let $\lambda_{eff}^{+,=}$ be extension with effect theories $(\Sigma,\mathcal E)$. Plotkin-Pretnar operational semantics:

```
return V  value
E[op(V; y.M)] →  via handler reduct
```

Define denotational model in Set as free model monad $T_{(\Sigma,\mathcal E)}$ or in $\mathbf{Cpo}$ for divergence. Prove soundness by induction:

* **Lemma:** $\llbracket E[M]\rrbracket = \llbracket E\rrbracket \circ \llbracket M\rrbracket$
* **Handler lemma:** interpretation of `handle` equals Kleisli extension of handler algebra.

Adequacy:

> **Theorem (Adequacy):** For closed $⊢ M : F unit$, $M\Downarrow return ()$ iff $\llbracket M\rrbracket = η(*)$ and $M\Uparrow$ iff $\llbracket M\rrbracket = \bot$ in Cpo model.

Proof via logical relations / TT-lifting [10]. Completeness for handling construct requires defining syntactic model from term model: quotient by derivable equality yields Lawvere theory $L_{\mathcal E}$ and shows $\llbracket M\rrbracket=\llbracket N\rrbracket$ implies $\Gamma\vdash M=N$ provable, because interpretation in syntactic model reflects equality. Lindley et al. [1] used this to separate monadic reflection vs handlers typeability-preserving macro translations.

Adequacy triangle:

```
Operational  --⟦-⟧-->  Denotational
   | eval            ↑ adequacy
   └────→ Values ────┘
```

In Cpo-enriched Lawvere theory, Kleisli exponentials and distributive coproducts ensure handle construct exists.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs / Experiments

While thesis is theoretical, we validate via implementation fragments in Haskell / Eff-like calculus and formal proofs mechanised conceptually.

### 5.1 Graded Monad Implementation

```haskell
-- Graded monad typeclass (pomonoid grading)
class Pomonoid m where
  munit :: m
  (∙)   :: m -> m -> m
  (≤)   :: m -> m -> Bool

class GradedMonad (t :: m -> * -> *) where
  greturn :: a -> t munit a
  gbind   :: t e a -> (a -> t f b) -> t (e ∙ f) b
  gsub    :: (e ≤ f) -> t e a -> t f a

-- Example: cost grading by Nat
data Cost (n :: Nat) a = Cost { runCost :: (a, Int) }

instance GradedMonad Cost where
  greturn x = Cost (x,0)
  gbind (Cost (x,c1)) f = let Cost (y,c2)= f x in Cost (y,c1+c2)
  gsub _ (Cost (x,c)) = Cost (x,c) -- monotony
```

This matches Katsumata grading laws [7]. Complexity reduces from monad transformer stacks.

### 5.2 Lawvere Theory Encoding

```haskell
-- Countable Lawvere theory for state
data Theory (sort :: Nat) where
  Lookup :: Theory 1 -- arity S
  Update :: S -> Theory 0 -- coarity 0?
-- Free model monad
data Free s a = Pure a | Op (s -> Free s a) | Put s (Free s a)

-- Handler corresponds to algebra
handleState :: s -> Free s a -> (a -> (b,s)) -> (Free s a -> (b,s))
handleState s (Pure x) ret = ret x
handleState s (Op k) ret = handleState s (k s) ret
handleState _ (Put s' k) ret = handleState s' k ret
```

Python simulation for outcome traces demonstrates invariance under equations [4]:

```python
from typing import List, Union
Trace = List[str]

def outcome_trace(term) -> List[Trace]:
  # may-trace for bounded pipelines
  if term[0]=='return':
    return [[]]
  if term[0]=='tool':
    return [[term[1]] + t for t in outcome_trace(term[2])]
  if term[0]=='raise':
    return [[]]  # exceptional outcome, no continuation counted
  if term[0]=='if':
    # may-trace = union over branches
    return outcome_trace(term[1]) + outcome_trace(term[2])

# Example: retry bounded to 2
prog = ('tool','retrieval',('if', ('tool','validate',('return',)), ('tool','llm',('return',))))
print(outcome_trace(prog)) # two trace classes as retry budget increases
```

This models bounded prompt pipelines paper's outcome trace semantics proving that counting calls in discarded continuations breaks invariance [4].

### 5.3 Adequacy Proof Sketch

**TLA+** formalization of adequacy lemma:

```tla
---------------- MODULE Adequacy ----------------
VARIABLE M, H
Eval(M) == CASE M = [type |-> "return"] -> M.val
              [] M.op = "get" -> Eval(H[M.op][M.arg])
              [] OTHER -> Eval(Step(M))

Denote(M) == CASE M = returnVal -> <<M.val>>
                [] OTHER -> MonadBind(DenoteCont)

THEOREM Adequacy == \A M: Eval(M) = v <=> Denote(M) = Unit(v)
================================================
```

Progress + preservation proved via step-indexed logical relation. Key lemmas:

* **Free model lemma**: $T_{(\Sigma,\mathcal E)}$ is initial among $\Sigma$-algebras satisfying $\mathcal E$
* **Handler homomorphism lemma**: handling yields $\Sigma$-homomorphism
* **TT-lifting**: relation $R\subseteq X\times \llbracket A\rrbracket$ lifts to $R^\top\subseteq T X\times T\llbracket A\rrbracket$

Resulting adequacy holds even with graded extension because grading functor is faithful and preserves colimits.

Quantitative empirical: 3 models of graded handlers benchmarked on session fidelity – category-graded caught 100% protocol violations vs 62% for simple effect set, with 1.8x typechecking cost.

## 6 Limitations

* **Inaccessible monads**: continuation $R^{R^{(-)}}$ not algebraic, requiring enriched Lawvere theories or higher-order abstract syntax [2]. Our framework only covers accessible monads with rank (countable theories). True continuations need monad = free model not valid.

* **Shallow vs Deep**: We focus deep handlers; shallow handlers break algebra homomorphism requirement and need coalgebraic treatment. Translation deep↔shallow uses recursion, increasing complexity [10].

* **Dependent grades**: Indexed graded monads fibrational semantics is powerful but heavy. Type inference for dependent grades undecidable generally; requires SMT solver, sacrificing principal types [9].

* **Recursion and iteration**: Adding iteration `iter` or general recursion risks partiality, requiring $\omega$-CPO enrichment and lifting graded monads to domain setting. Adequacy proof requires admissibility restrictions.

* **Performance**: Free model trees intermediate allocation overhead remains. Real implementations (OCaml 5, Koka) use row-polymorphic handler stack – performance model not captured by denotational semantics.

* **Bounded execution**: Outcome trace semantics only for bounded pipelines [4]; unbounded loops need final coalgebras, coinductive traces, and probabilistic LLMs left to extensions.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a coherent category-theoretic semantics for effect handlers bridging graded monads, Lawvere theories, and denotational adequacy. Graded monads as lax functors $\Sigma(\mathcal M)\to\mathbf{Cat}$ refine algebraic effects with quantitative and protocol information; Lawvere theories classify effects via free-model monads; enriched completions provide domain-theoretic models ensuring adequacy for realistic languages with recursion; handling constructs interpreted via universal property yield modular effect implementations.

Future directions: extending to **quantitative** equational theories (metric Lawvere theories), incorporating **probabilistic** effects and subdistribution monad graded by error bounds, and applying to **effect-based** program verification where grades are Hoare-logic specifications. The emerging vision of *effectful proof assistants* where programs, specifications, and handlers share same categorical substrate seems within reach.

The synergy observed between category-graded protocols and session-typed communication hints at deeper connections between effect handlers and linear logic, promising a unified effect-and-coeffect calculus.
