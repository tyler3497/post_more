# De Bruijn Graphs in Genome Assembly: k-mer Graph Construction, Minimizers, Compaction, and Pangenome Variation Graphs

## Abstract

The de Bruijn graph has dominated *de novo* genome assembly since Pevzner, Tang, and Waterman reduced fragment assembly to an Eulerian path problem in 2001, displacing the overlap–layout–consensus paradigm [1]. This thesis develops the full arc of de Bruijn-based assembly: *k*-mer graph construction from read sets, compaction into unitigs, minimizer sampling with provable density guarantees [2], succinct representations approaching the storage lower bound [7], and the generalization into pangenome variation graphs encoding species-wide diversity [3]. We prove the central correctness theorems — maximal non-branching paths partition the *k*-spectrum into uniquely reconstructible contigs — and analyze the complexity of construction, compaction, and minimizer chaining. Benchmarks from Velvet [8], minimap2 [2], and the vg toolkit [3] quantify the trade-offs between graph fidelity, memory, and speed. Fundamental limits: repeats beyond the spanning distance collapse information-theoretically, and paired-read repeat resolution is NP-hard. De Bruijn graphs succeed by converting a hard *layout* problem into a polynomial *touring* problem, and their descendants — compacted graphs, minimizer sketches, variation graphs — all answer one question: how to retain every shred of sequence evidence while discarding everything else.

## 1. Introduction

Genome assembly reconstructs an unknown string *S* over Σ = {A, C, G, T} from short substrings — reads — sampled from *S*. For twenty years the field used *overlap–layout–consensus* (OLC): compute all pairwise read overlaps, arrange reads into a layout, derive a consensus. Overlap detection is quadratic in read count and layout is Hamiltonian-like in the presence of repeats. Pevzner, Tang, and Waterman's 2001 paper [1] proposed a radical simplification: **stop matching reads against reads**. Decompose every read into its *k*-mers, build a graph whose edges are those *k*-mers, and observe that assembly becomes finding an Eulerian path — solvable in linear time. Repeats are not masked but *exploited*: a repeat is a vertex with multiple in- or out-edges, and resolving it is a question about which paths are consistent with the data.

> **Definition (de Bruijn graph of order k).** Given a read set ℛ, the de Bruijn graph *B(k)* has vertices = all (*k*−1)-mers and directed edges = all *k*-mers; edge *x₁…xₖ* connects vertex *x₁…xₖ₋₁* to *x₂…xₖ*.

Three consequences organize this thesis:

1. **Topology encodes sequence.** A path *v₀ → … → vₘ* spells the first (*k*−1)-mer followed by each edge's last character. Graph *traversal* is sequence *reconstruction*.
2. **Branching encodes ambiguity.** Vertices with in- or out-degree ≠ 1 mark *k*-mers with ambiguous context — errors, polymorphisms, repeats. Graph *simplification* (tip clipping, bubble popping, unitig compaction) removes error-induced ambiguity and merges unambiguous runs [4].
3. **Sampling encodes economy.** Retaining all *k*-mers is unnecessary for many operations; minimizer schemes keep a representative subset with provable density [2], accelerating overlap detection by orders of magnitude.

The arc from EULER [1] through Velvet [8], succinct graphs [7], and the variation graphs of the vg toolkit [3] is one story: the de Bruijn graph as universal substrate for representing sequence collections — first one genome, then all genomes of a species.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The k-mer spectrum and its fragility

Let reads of length ℓ sample *S* uniformly with per-base error rate ε. The *k*-spectrum of ℛ is the multiset of all (ℓ−*k*+1)*N* length-*k* read substrings. Error-free, every genomic *k*-mer appears; with errors, one substitution corrupts up to *k* overlapping *k*-mers, so the spurious fraction grows as ≈ *k*ε — for *k* = 55, ε = 0.01, over half the distinct *k*-mers can be spurious. Hence *k*-mer **counting and filtering** — discarding *k*-mers seen fewer than *c* times — is the mandatory first step of every de Bruijn assembler [4][8].

> **Theorem (Spectral fidelity under Poisson coverage).** With mean coverage λ, the count *X* of a true genomic *k*-mer follows Po(λ′) for λ′ = λ(ℓ−*k*+1)/ℓ, while distinct erroneous *k*-mers number ≈ *N*ℓ*k*ε. A threshold *c* ≈ λ′/2 separates true from spurious *k*-mers with probability → 1 as λ grows, provided *k*ε ≪ 1.

The count histogram is bimodal — a sharp peak near 0 (errors), a broad peak near λ′ (genomic *k*-mers) — and assemblers like Velvet choose the threshold at the valley [8].

### 2.2 Eulerian paths: the polynomial core

> **Theorem (Euler–Hierholzer).** A directed graph admits an Eulerian circuit iff every nonzero-degree vertex lies in one strongly connected component and in-degree equals out-degree everywhere. Such a trail is found in *O(|E|)* time.

Each read contributes its *k*-mers as edges, so the true genome is an Eulerian trail through the graph (error-free idealization). The NP-complete Hamiltonian layout of OLC is replaced by a linear-time Eulerian tour — a *complexity-class* improvement explaining EULER's order-of-magnitude speedups on bacterial genomes [1].

### 2.3 Minimizers: subsampling with guarantees

A *(w, k)*-minimizer scheme, introduced by Roberts et al. (2004) and popularized by minimap2 [2], selects from each window of *w* consecutive *k*-mers the *k*-mer minimal under a hash order (taken over both strands). Consecutive windows usually share their minimizer, yielding a sparse, evenly distributed sketch.

> **Definition ((w,k)-minimizer).** For sequence *S* and window length *w*, the minimizer of window *S[i..i+w+k−2]* is argmin{*h(m)*} over its *k*-mers, ties broken by position.

The **density** — expected fraction of positions selected — is 2/(*w*+1) for the random-order minimizer, provably optimal among forward schemes. Minimap2 [2] indexes all minimizers and chains collinear anchors, achieving ≥30× speedups over contemporary long-read aligners at higher accuracy, because minimizer seeding converts quadratic all-vs-all overlap detection into near-linear sketch comparison.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 k-mer graph construction pipeline

The canonical pipeline, refined by Velvet [8] and exposited by Compeau–Pevzner–Tesler [4]:

| Stage | Operation | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Counting | Enumerate *k*-mers with multiplicities | *O*(‖ℛ‖) time |
| 2. Filtering | Drop *k*-mers with count < *c* | *O*(distinct) |
| 3. Graph build | Insert surviving *k*-mers as edges | *O*(surviving) |
| 4. Simplification | Clip tips, pop bubbles, drop low-coverage edges | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) |
| 5. Compaction | Merge maximal non-branching paths into unitigs | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) |
| 6. Touring | Eulerian-ish traversal; mate-pair resolution | heuristic |

**Simplification** removes three artifact classes: *tips* (short dead ends from read-end errors), *bubbles* (parallel paths from SNPs or single-base errors), and chimeric low-coverage edges. **Compaction** merges every maximal path whose internal vertices have in-degree = out-degree = 1 into one *unitig* labelled by the spelled string — typically 10–100× fewer vertices with identical *k*-mer content [4].

```python
def build_debruijn(reads, k):
    """B(k): vertices are (k-1)-mers, edges are k-mers with counts."""
    from collections import defaultdict
    edges = defaultdict(int)
    for read in reads:
        for i in range(len(read) - k + 1):
            edges[read[i:i+k]] += 1
    return edges

def compact_unitigs(edges, k, min_cov=2):
    """Merge maximal non-branching paths into unitigs."""
    solid = {e: c for e, c in edges.items() if c >= min_cov}
    succ, indeg = {}, {}
    for e in solid:
        u, v = e[:-1], e[1:]
        succ.setdefault(u, []).append(v)
        indeg[v] = indeg.get(v, 0) + 1
    unitigs, seen = [], set()
    for e in solid:
        u = e[:-1]
        if indeg.get(u, 0) == 1 and len(succ.get(u, [])) == 1:
            continue  # internal vertex, not a unitig start
        s, cur = e, e[1:]
        while len(succ.get(cur, [])) == 1 and indeg.get(cur, 0) == 1:
            nxt = succ[cur][0]
            s += nxt[-1]
            cur = nxt
        if e not in seen:
            unitigs.append(s); seen.add(e)
    return unitigs
```

### 3.2 The minimizer-seeded overlap engine

Modern long-read assembly rarely builds *B(k)* over all *k*-mers. Following minimap2 [2]:

1. Compute *(w, k)*-minimizers of every read (defaults: *k*=15, *w*=10 short reads; *k*=19, *w*=19 noisy long reads).
2. Index minimizers: minimizer → list of (read, position, strand).
3. For each query, collect seed hits and **chain**: the max-weight subset of anchors increasing in both coordinates — *O(n log n)* sparse DP [2].
4. Chain scores feed overlap graphs or graph-based mapping.

Completeness holds because any exact match of length ≥ *w*+*k*−1 contains a shared minimizer (error-free case) — overlaps longer than the window are never missed.

### 3.3 From one genome to many: variation graphs

A **variation graph** generalizes the de Bruijn graph from one genome's *k*-spectrum to a *collection* of genomes: nodes carry sequences, edges carry adjacencies, each haplotype is a *path*. The vg toolkit [3] formalized the bidirected model with the GFA interchange format and showed graph mapping beats linear-reference mapping — 10M simulated read pairs mapped with higher sensitivity and specificity against a 1000 Genomes pangenome graph than BWA-MEM against the linear reference [3][5].

> **Definition (Variation graph).** A tuple (*V*, *E*, *P*): each *v* ∈ *V* labelled with a nonempty string, edges *E* connect node sides (bidirected), each *p* ∈ *P* a haplotype path. A *snarl* is a minimal subgraph separable by ≤ 2 boundary nodes — the graph analogue of a variant site.

Construction routes include *vg msga* (multiple alignment of contigs [3]), *minigraph* (minimizer-seeded incremental SV graphs), and *seqwish* (transitive reduction of all-vs-all minimap2 alignments) [5][6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Compacted de Bruijn graphs and unitig statistics

Compaction is lossless in *k*-mer content: the compacted graph spells exactly the same *k*-mer language. Its power is scale: for a human genome at *k* = 55, ~3×10⁹ edges collapse to ~10⁷ unitigs (~300× reduction), since most of the genome is non-branching. Repeats manifest as branching unitig endpoints; the count of branching vertices approximates the number of distinct repeat boundaries — a quantity coupled to the genome's repeat spectrum. The unitig N50 measures contiguity *before* any repeat resolution.

```rust
/// Compact a de Bruijn graph into unitigs; vertices with
/// indeg == outdeg == 1 are internal, all else are breaks.
pub fn compact_unitigs(
    out_edges: &HashMap<Kmer, Vec<Kmer>>,
    indeg: &HashMap<Kmer, usize>,
) -> Vec<String> {
    let mut unitigs = Vec::new();
    let mut visited = std::collections::HashSet::new();
    for (u, succs) in out_edges {
        if indeg.get(u).copied().unwrap_or(0) == 1 && succs.len() == 1 {
            continue; // internal vertex
        }
        for v in succs {
            let mut s = format!("{}{}", u, v.chars().last().unwrap());
            let mut cur = v.clone();
            while indeg.get(&cur).copied().unwrap_or(0) == 1
                && out_edges.get(&cur).map(|x| x.len()) == Some(1) {
                let nxt = &out_edges[&cur][0];
                s.push(nxt.chars().last().unwrap());
                cur = nxt.clone();
            }
            if visited.insert((u.clone(), v.clone())) { unitigs.push(s); }
        }
    }
    unitigs
}
```

### 4.2 Minimizer sampling: density theory and successors

The ordering function *h* determines quality: random hashes give density 2/(*w*+1); lexicographic order is biased (poly-A always selected); minimap2 uses an invertibly mixed hash for strand-symmetric, unbiased selection [2]. Key refinements:

- **Syncmers**: select *k*-mers whose minimal *s*-mer sits at a fixed offset — density ≈ 1/*w* with better conservation under mutation, improving sensitivity for divergent overlaps.
- **FracMinHash**: select *k*-mers with *h(m)* < *H/s* — density exactly 1/*s* in expectation, scaling with sequence *content* rather than length (basis of sourmash metagenomic sketching).

Chaining [2] solves: given anchors (*xᵢ*, *yᵢ*), find the max-weight chain with increasing coordinates, scored by match bonuses minus concave gap costs tolerating long indels.

| Scheme | Density | Robustness | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| (*w*,*k*)-minimizer | 2/(*w*+1) | moderate | minimap2 seeding [2] |
| Closed syncmer | ≈ 1/*w* | high | divergent overlaps |
| FracMinHash (1/*s*) | 1/*s* | moderate | metagenomic sketching |
| All *k*-mers | 1 | — | classic DBG assembly [1][8] |

### 4.3 Succinct and probabilistic representations

Explicit human *k*-mer graphs cost tens of GB. Conway and Bromage [7] showed near-optimal storage: edges of *B(k)* are implicitly enumerable (a *k*-mer *is* its address), so a bitmap over the 4ᵏ possible edges suffices — successors found by probing four bit positions. Their representation compresses the human graph from ~30 GB to under 2 GB, near the *m*·log(4ᵏ/*m*) lower bound. Companions:

- **BOSS**: the BWT of the edge set, with *O(k)*-time navigation via FM-index machinery.
- **Bloom-filter graphs** (Minia, MEGAHIT): ~4 bits per *k*-mer with a separate exact structure for branching *k*-mers; the controlled false-positive rate is provably harmless to traversal.

### 4.4 Assembly graphs meet pangenomes: GFA and the vg model

GFA became pangenomics' interchange format because assembly and variation graphs are the same object viewed differently — bidirected sequence graphs with embedded paths:

| Record | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `S` segment | Node with nucleotide label |
| `L` link | Adjacency between oriented segments |
| `P` path | Ordered oriented segments — a haplotype/contig |
| `W` walk | Path by stable segment IDs (GFA2/rGFA) |

The vg toolkit [3] adds the **snarl decomposition**: every variation graph decomposes uniquely into nested snarls and chains — a variant-centric coordinate system. Genotyping becomes: map reads, compute node/edge support, and at each snarl pick the best-supported haplotype path pair — systematically beating linear-reference SV genotypers [5]. Minimizers persist throughout: Giraffe seeds with minimizers against a haplotype index, and minigraph builds SV graphs by minimizer chaining [2][6].

### 4.5 Assembly algorithms: touring a simplified graph

Post-compaction strategies:

1. **Greedy Eulerian traversal** (EULER [1]): follow high-coverage edges; break at ambiguity — contiguity sacrificed for correctness.
2. **Paired-read threading**: mates of known insert size thread repeats; a repeat of length *r* is resolvable iff some pair spans it [1][4].
3. **Coverage-flow disambiguation**: edge multiplicities must be conserved as flow; repeats resolved via flow decomposition — used in SPAdes and metagenomic binners.

> **Theorem (Repeat resolvability bound).** Error-free, with read length ℓ and insert size *d*, a repeat of length *r* is unambiguously resolvable only if some spanning read pair covers it (*r* < ℓ + *d* − *k* + 1); no assembler resolves repeats beyond its longest spanning evidence — an information-theoretic barrier.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

**Correctness of compaction.** *Claim:* the compacted graph spells the same *k*-mer set, and Eulerian trails correspond bijectively, spelling identical strings. *Proof sketch:* compaction merges only paths with internal in-degree = out-degree = 1; any Eulerian trail entering such a path must traverse it fully — no alternative edge exists at internal vertices. Contracting preserves trail structure bijectively, and the merged label is exactly the edge-label concatenation. ∎

**Landmark results** (from the primary sources):

- **EULER vs. Celera (2001)** [1]: error-free assemblies of repeat-rich simulated bacterial genomes where OLC-based Celera misassembled — repeats *used*, not masked.
- **Velvet (2008)** [8]: 3 Mb bacterial genome from 36-bp reads, N50 > 100 kb, in minutes on a workstation — tip clipping and bubble popping became standard.
- **Minimap2 (2018)** [2]: 3–4× faster than short-read mappers at equal accuracy; ≥30× faster than long-read mappers at *higher* accuracy; concave gap costs improved SV-region alignment.
- **vg toolkit (2018)** [3]: ROC curves for 10M simulated pairs dominated BWA-MEM both genome-wide and in variant regions; SV genotyping achieved best F1 on insertions, deletions, inversions [5].
- **Succinct graphs (2011)** [7]: human de Bruijn graph ~30 GB → < 2 GB bitmap representation.

**Complexity** (*n* = total read length):

| Operation | Time | Space |
|---|---|---|
| *k*-mer counting | *O(n)* | *O*(distinct) |
| Eulerian tour (Hierholzer) | *O*(\|E\|) | *O*(\|E\|) |
| Unitig compaction | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) |
| Minimizer sketching | *O(n)* | *O*(n/(*w*+1)) |
| Minimizer chaining | *O*(m log m) | *O*(m) |
| Snarl decomposition | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) | *O*(\|V\|+\|E\|) |

## 6. Limitations

1. **The *k*-barrier.** Repeats longer than the spanning distance (*k*; ℓ+*d* with mates) collapse into one tangled subgraph — information-theoretic, not algorithmic. Long reads (HiFi, ONT) attack it by increasing ℓ.
2. **Error-induced explosion.** At ε ≈ 0.1 (early ONT), *k*ε ≈ 1: most *k*-mers are spurious, the count histogram merges, the graph becomes a hairball. This motivated A-Bruijn graphs over "solid strings" and the retreat to minimizer-seeded OLC for long reads.
3. **Diploidy.** Bubbles encode errors *and* heterozygosity; aggressive popping collapses haplotypes and loses phase. Diploid assemblers (hifiasm) retain both branches and phase with Hi-C/trio data.
4. **Minimizer pathologies.** In low-complexity sequence, density guarantees break: poly-A tracts reselect one minimizer, creating high-occurrence seeds that minimap2 must rescue with extra-anchor heuristics [5]. Density bounds are expected-case, not worst-case.
5. **Variation-graph scaling.** Human pangenome graphs reach 10⁸+ nodes; haplotype indexing (GBWT) is costly and graph alignment runs 2–5× slower than linear mapping. Nested snarls in segmental duplications still defeat clean genotyping.
6. **The Hamiltonian ghost.** Paired-end repeat resolution at arbitrary insert sizes is NP-hard (embeds Hamiltonian path in the scaffold graph); practice relies on real repeat graphs being *almost* series-parallel — an empirical fact with no theorem behind it.

## 7. Conclusion

The de Bruijn graph endures because it is the right *mathematical* object for assembly. It trades an NP-complete layout problem for a linear-time touring problem [1]; compacts the unambiguous backbone 100-fold [4]; samples minimizers to make overlap detection near-linear with provable density [2]; stores edges implicitly near the information-theoretic limit [7]; and threads haplotypes through shared nodes to generalize from one genome to the pangenome [3][6].

The frontier is integration: compacted graphs indexed by minimizers, variation graphs built by minimizer chaining of assemblies, genotypers threading reads through snarls with coverage-flow models. Each layer answers one design question — *what is the minimal structure preserving all sequence evidence?* The next decade asks whether one graph can serve simultaneously as assembler, mapper, variant caller, and pangenome coordinate system. From EULER to vg, the trajectory says yes — and the de Bruijn graph, now wearing variation-graph clothes, will remain the substrate of that unification.
