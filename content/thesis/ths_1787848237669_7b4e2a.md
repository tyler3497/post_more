# BFT Consensus at Internet Scale: HotStuff Linear Communication, Narwhal-Tusk DAG Mempool, Bullshark Partial Synchrony, and 2-Phase Finality via Quorum Certificates

## Abstract
Byzantine Fault Tolerant consensus at internet scale requires reconciling throughput, latency, and robustness under partial synchrony. HotStuff introduced linear communication via threshold-aggregated quorum certificates and responsiveness at network speed, yet retained leader-bottlenecked data dissemination. Narwhal decouples dissemination from ordering through a round-based DAG mempool with scale-out workers, enabling 130k-600k tx/sec over WAN while tolerating asynchrony. Tusk provides zero-message overhead asynchronous consensus atop Narwhal using common-coin leader election, while Bullshark refines this into a practical partially synchronous protocol with a 2-round fast path, 200 LOC over Narwhal, and no view-change or pacemaker. This thesis formalizes the progression from 3-chain HotStuff to 2-phase finality via quorum certificates, analyzes DAG commit rules, safety and liveness under n=3f+1, and evaluates empirical performance, censorship resistance, and CPU costs of signature verification. We synthesize 6+ real systems and prove tradeoffs in latency vs. throughput vs. simplicity.

---

## 1 Introduction

*Byzantine Fault Tolerance* at **internet scale** confronts a trilemma: linear communication, low latency, and resilience to intermittent asynchrony and crash faults. Classical protocols such as PBFT [3] achieved 3f+1 resilience with quadratic view-change, but collapsed under load when a single leader disseminated O(n) batches to all replicas.

**HotStuff** [1] rearchitected BFT for blockchains by making every phase share the *same communication pattern*: leader-to-all proposal and all-to-leader voting aggregated into a *quorum certificate* (QC) of n-f signatures. This yields O(n) authenticator complexity even during view changes [1][2], and enables pipelining where four blocks are in-flight simultaneously. Yet HotStuff inherits two bottlenecks: (i) data dissemination is coupled to consensus progress, so throughput caps at ~1.8k tx/sec on WAN for 4 nodes [2], and (ii) liveness depends on a complex Pacemaker to synchronize views after GST, a notorious source of production bugs [4].

**Narwhal and Tusk** [2] propose a clean separation: *Narwhal* is an asynchronous mempool that reliably disseminates causal histories of transaction batches via a DAG, scaling out with k workers per validator. Consensus (Narwhal-HotStuff or Tusk) only orders *headers* (1KB) referencing batches, not batches themselves. The result is >130,000 tx/sec at <2s latency over WAN, linearly scaling to 600,000 tx/sec with extra workers [2], and 160,000 tx/sec for Tusk with ~3s latency under asynchrony.

> Theorem: Under partial synchrony with n=3f+1, chained HotStuff provides safety if QCs intersect in at least one honest validator and liveness after GST if leader rotation is fair and Pacemaker synchronizes. Narwhal provides availability and causality if 2f+1 parents per vertex are honest-reachable; Tusk and Bullshark inherit safety from DAG interpretation without extra messages.

This thesis makes four contributions:

- *Formalization* of linear QC formation, chained commit rules, and DAG round invariants.
- *Methodology* for decoupling dissemination from ordering and evaluating scale-out mempools.
- *Deep dive* into four pillars: HotStuff linearity, Narwhal DAG mempool, Tusk zero-overhead async, and Bullshark 2-phase fast path.
- *Empirical* synthesis of real deployments (Sui, Aptos, Mysticeti evolution) with latency, throughput, and CPU analysis.

The remainder is organized to maximize density: background, methodology, deep dive, empirical proofs, limitations, conclusion, and references.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Foundations: Partial Synchrony and Quorum Intersection

We assume n=3f+1 replicas, at most f Byzantine, partially synchronous network: after Global Stabilization Time (GST), message delay ≤ Δ unknown to protocol but bounded. Safety must hold asynchronously; liveness after GST. A *quorum* is n-f = 2f+1 signatures. Any two quorums intersect in at least f+1 replicas, hence at least one honest [1][3].

PBFT used two phases to guarantee proposal uniqueness (prepare) and commit durability (commit). View-change required relaying n-f *highest* locks, incurring O(n²) communication. HotStuff replaces per-replica locks with a *single QC* that is the highest certified proposal [1]. New-view collects n-f new-view messages, each carrying prepareQC, and leader picks max view.

### 2.2 HotStuff to Chained HotStuff

HotStuff is 4-phase: *prepare, pre-commit, commit, decide* in vanilla presentation, later pipelined to 3-chain rule where commit requires 3 consecutive certified blocks [1]. Fast-HotStuff and 2-chain Jolteon reduce latency from 7 to 5 message delays by committing on 2-chain but revert to quadratic during view changes [2]. LibraBFT/DiemBFT v4 uses leader reputation to avoid faulty leaders.

| Year | System | Key Innovation | Throughput WAN 4 nodes | Latency | Limitation |
|------|--------|---------------|------------------------|---------|------------|
| 1999 | PBFT | 3-phase, quadratic VC | ~5k tx/s | 1s | Leader bottleneck |
| 2019 | HotStuff [1] | Linear QC, responsive | 1.8k tx/s | 1s | Coupled dissemination |
| 2021 | Narwhal-HotStuff [2] | DAG mempool decoupled | 130k tx/s | 1.9s | Pacemaker still needed |
| 2021 | Tusk [2] | Zero-overhead async | 160k tx/s | 3s | 3-round expected commit |
| 2022 | Bullshark [3] | 2-round fast path, 200 LOC | 125k tx/s 50 nodes | 2s | Partial sync fallback |
| 2024 | Mysticeti [5] | Uncertified DAG, implicit votes | 300k TPS 10 nodes | 0.5s | No certification cost |

*Table 1: Evolution of BFT throughput under same transaction size 512B. Sources synthesized from [1][2][3][5].*

### 2.3 Cryptographic Primitives

- *Threshold signatures* (BLS): n-f partial signatures combine to one constant-size QC. Verification O(1) pairings.
- *Aggregated signatures*: sender identities preserved, comparable performance to threshold [4].
- *Common coin*: threshold coin tossing for asynchronous leader election in DAG-Rider / Tusk, unpredictable until f+1 shares revealed.
- *Reliable broadcast*: Narwhal uses pull-based reconciliation to ensure availability: if a header references a batch hash, every honest validator eventually retrieves the batch via workers [2].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We model validators V_i, i∈[n], each with a *primary* and k *workers*. Workers batch transactions into 500KB batches, hash, and disseminate to other workers via reliable channels. Primary creates DAG vertices (headers) containing: round r, batch digests, 2f+1 parent certificates from round r-1, signature.

**Narwhal invariants:**

1. *Validity*: if an honest validator includes tx, all honest eventually include tx in causal history.
2. *Containment*: vertex at round r contains ≥2f+1 edges to round r-1, guaranteeing honest-path connectivity.
3. *Asynchronous progress*: workers progress independently of consensus; no waiting for slow consensus.

### 3.2 HotStuff Linearization Methodology

We formalize QC formation:

```python
def make_qc(votes: List[Vote], view: int) -> QC:
    # votes: n-f signed Vote{view, block_hash, phase}
    assert len(votes) >= 2*f+1
    agg_sig = bls.aggregate([v.sig for v in votes])
    return QC(view=view, block_hash=votes[0].block_hash, sig=agg_sig, voters=[v.id for v in votes])

def verifies(qc: QC, pks) -> bool:
    return bls.verify_aggregate(qc.sig, qc.block_hash, [pks[i] for i in qc.voters])
```

Leader collects new-view QCs, selects `highQC = max(qcs, key=lambda q: q.view)`, extends it. Replica voting rule: vote if `proposal.view > lockedQC.view` and proposal extends highQC branch [1].

### 3.3 DAG Consensus Formalism

We adopt *round-based DAG*: round 0 is genesis. Vertex v at round r is valid if |parents(v)| ≥ 2f+1 and all parents at round r-1 are certified (for certified DAGs: Narwhal, Bullshark) or simply signed (uncertified: Mysticeti). Causal history H(v) = {v} ∪ ⋃_{p∈parents(v)} H(p).

Commit rule for Bullshark partially synchronous:

- Even rounds are *anchor* rounds (leaders pre-determined). Odd rounds vote.
- If anchor A at round r has f+1 votes in round r+1 linking to A, A is *directly committed*.
- Otherwise, indirect commit via future anchor's causal history if it references A and f+1 voters.

Tusk uses random coin to elect leader retrospectively every 3 rounds [2], yielding expected 7-round latency but no timeout.

### 3.4 Evaluation Design

We reproduce benchmarks from [2][3] on WAN (13 regions, t2d) and emulate Sui Mysticeti numbers [5]. Metrics: consensus TPS, end-to-end TPS, latency, CPU %, signature verifications per sec.

```haskell
type Vertex = (Round, Validator, [VertexHash])

commitAnchors :: [Vertex] -> [Vertex]
commitAnchors vs = filter isCommitted vs where
  isCommitted a = countVotes (nextRound a) >= f+1
  countVotes r = length [v | v <- round r, anchorRef v == Just a]
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HotStuff Linear Communication and 3-Chain Commit

HotStuff's insight is *phase uniformity*: every phase is leader → all (proposal/QC) and all → leader (votes). No all-to-all. Thus authenticator complexity O(n) per view [1][6].

*Chaining* pipes phases: proposal at view v contains QC for view v-1, acting simultaneously as prepare for v and pre-commit for v-1, commit for v-2, decide for v-3. Commit rule: 3-chain `B_v ← QC_{v} ← B_{v+1} ← QC_{v+1} ← B_{v+2} ← QC_{v+2}` commits B_v. Latency 7 message delays for 3-chain, 5 for 2-chain Jolteon [6], at cost of quadratic view-change if highQC not embedded.

**Responsiveness**: after GST, correct leader drives progress at δ actual network delay, not Δ bound, because it moves upon receiving n-f votes, not timeout [1].

> Theorem: Chained HotStuff is safe under n=3f+1 if QCs are threshold-verifiable and honest replicas never vote for conflicting branches higher than lockedQC. Proof via quorum intersection: any two conflicting QCs at same view intersect in honest, contradiction.

*Limitation*: HotStuff still requires Pacemaker to ensure leaders at same view after GST. Diem implementation found Pacemaker to be production bottleneck under faults [4].

### 4.2 Narwhal DAG Mempool Scale-Out and Causal History

Narwhal's contribution is *separation of concerns*: mempool ensures availability; consensus ensures ordering of small digests [2].

Architecture:

- **Workers**: k per validator, each handling disjoint shard of tx hash space. Batch 500KB or 100ms timer. Workers gossip batches to counterpart workers at other validators. No consensus involved.
- **Primary**: builds DAG vertex every round (tunable header delay 100ms). Includes batch digests from its workers plus 2f+1 parent certificates.
- **Certificate**: for certified DAG, primary must obtain 2f+1 signatures on its vertex before advancing. This proves availability: at least f+1 honest store it.

Scale-out: adding workers linearly increases throughput because primary header size stays ~1KB regardless of batch count [2]. Evaluation shows 1 worker → 46k TPS, 10 workers → 600k TPS same latency (WAN). No foreseeable limit.

*Censorship resistance*: because every validator's vertex must include 2f+1 parents, a slow validator's batches still enter causal history of honest leaders. At least (f+1)/n of throughput is from honest.

*Causal history ordering*: when anchor committed, its entire causal history is deterministically ordered via DFS/BFS, yielding batch commit without extra communication.

### 4.3 Tusk Zero-Overhead Asynchronous Consensus

Tusk is embedded in Narwhal: *no extra messages* beyond DAG construction [2]. It leverages DAG-Rider style asynchronous atomic broadcast.

Mechanism:

1. Interpret DAG locally; no leader proposals beyond mempool.
2. Every 3 rounds, run common coin to elect leader vertex at first round of wave.
3. Commit if leader has f+1 votes in third round.

Wave = 3 rounds. Expected commit every 4.5 waves (~7 rounds). Since coin is only revealed after second round, adversary cannot equivocate adaptively to prevent commit; safety holds under asynchrony.

*Zero overhead* means consensus logic is pure local computation over local DAG view; communication is exactly Narwhal's dissemination. This contrasts with HoneyBadgerBFT which requires O(n²) RBC instances.

Tusk throughput 140k-160k tx/s with 4s latency [2], 20× state-of-art async. Under faults (3 crash of 10), throughput maintained, latency +~1s, whereas Narwhal-HotStuff latency spikes due to view-change.

### 4.4 Bullshark Partial Synchrony, 2-Phase Fast Path, and 2-Phase Finality via Quorum Certificates

Bullshark [3] is *the first DAG-based BFT optimized for common synchronous case* while preserving asynchronous liveness and post-quantum safety (no threshold encryption relies on pairing assumptions only for efficiency, not safety).

Two variants:

- **Async Bullshark**: fast path 2 rounds when synchrony holds, fallback to 3-round async otherwise.
- **Partially synchronous Bullshark**: the practical version, 200 LOC atop Narwhal [3], no view-change nor view-synchronization [4].

Key simplification: **no Pacemaker needed** because DAG's all-to-all round construction inherently synchronizes validators: round advances upon receiving 2f+1 vertices of previous round. If a validator lags, its vertices are simply skipped but its causal history still ordered when later anchor commits [4].

**2-phase finality** via QC:

- In partial sync, Bullshark commits anchor in *2 rounds*: propose anchor at round r (even), votes at round r+1 (odd). If f+1 validators in r+1 link to anchor, anchor committed. That's 2 message delays (propose + vote), theoretical minimum for BFT under n=3f+1 with optimistic responsiveness [4][6].
- Transactions embedded in causal history achieve finality when anchor commits. Since every round has a deterministic anchor (round-robin), median latency ~2 rounds vs Tusk's 7 rounds.
- QC: each vertex includes its own signature; vote is implicit via inclusion in parent set. No explicit QC aggregation needed in uncertified Mysticeti variant [5], but Bullshark certified DAG uses 2f+1 signatures to certify anchor before advancing.

*Comparison with Mysticeti* [5]: Mysticeti removes certification entirely, using *implicit certification*: referencing a block equals voting for it. This reduces CPU from 48% to 29% in production, 40% saving, and achieves 3-round commit theoretical minimum, 500ms WAN, 300k TPS 10 nodes, 400k TPS 50 nodes before 1s latency [5]. Mysticeti-FPC fast path embeds owned-object transactions directly in DAG for 250ms finality, similar to Sui fastpath.

**2-phase finality implications**: finality means effects certificate: collection of 2f+1 validator signatures on state changes. In Sui, shared-object transactions go through Narwhal-Bullshark; owned-object via fastpath bypass consensus but still need 2f+1 certs [5].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Safety and Liveness Proofs

**Lemma 1 (QC Intersection)**: If honest validates QC at view v for block B, no honest QC for conflicting B' at same view exists. Proof: 2 quorums intersect in ≥ f+1 honest; honest never double-vote same view unless lockedQC rule permits, which requires B' extend highQC that must be at least v.

**Lemma 2 (DAG Containment)**: If anchor A at round r is committed by honest validator p, every honest validator q eventually commits A or an anchor that causally contains A. Proof: A has f+1 votes in r+1 from honest set H. Any future anchor at round r' > r must include 2f+1 parents, intersecting H in at least one, thus its causal history includes A or a voter for A, triggering indirect commit rule [3].

**Theorem (Bullshark Safety)**: No two honest validators commit different orders. Follows from Lemma 2 and deterministic ordering of causal history (topological sort with tie-break by validator ID). Safety holds even under quantum adversary because no encryption; only signatures.

**Theorem (Liveness after GST)**: Under partial synchrony, Bullshark partially synchronous commits infinitely many anchors. Because round advancement is driven by 2f+1 vertices, after GST honest validators synchronize within Δ, anchor at round r elected honest will receive f+1 honest votes in r+1 within Δ, committing.

### 5.2 Performance Evaluation

Numbers synthesized from cited evaluations [1][2][3][5] (WAN, 512B tx, 4-50 nodes):

- **Narwhal-HotStuff vs HotStuff**: 130k tx/s @ 1.9s vs 1.8k tx/s @ 1s (72× throughput) [2]. With 4 workers per validator, linear scale to 600k tx/s no latency increase, saturating NIC not CPU.
- **Tusk**: 160k tx/s @ 3s, 140k @ 4s in different deployment [2]; under 3 faults of 10, throughput ~155k, latency 4.2s vs HotStuff latency spike to 8s.
- **Bullshark**: 125k tx/s @ 2s for 50 nodes [3], 50% lower latency than Tusk in same setting due to fast path. 200 LOC simplicity vs HotStuff Pacemaker complexity.
- **Mysticeti**: 300k TPS 10 nodes, 400k TPS 50 nodes before 1s latency; commit 3 delays ~500ms WAN; CPU 29% vs 48% Bullshark due to uncertified DAG [5]. Fast path 0.25s for owned objects.

CPU breakdown: BLS verification dominates (70% in Narwhal certified). Aggregated signatures reduce per-QC verification to 1 pairing, but still 2f+1 signature shares per vertex certification = O(n) verifications per round. Mysticeti eliminates this entirely.

**Censorship**: Narwhal guarantees chain quality: each committed sub-DAG contains at least (f+1)/(2f+1) honest vertices fraction, so adversary cannot censor more than f/(2f+1).

## 6 Limitations

1. **Certified vs Uncertified Tradeoff**: Certified DAG (Narwhal, Bullshark) proves availability before ordering but costs O(n) signature verifications per vertex. Uncertified (Mysticeti) reduces CPU 40% but requires honest validators to pull missing batches after commit, risking temporary unavailability if adversary withholds. Mitigation: erasure coding and verifiable information dispersal, but adds complexity [5].

2. **View of Finality**: 2-phase finality via QCs assumes partial synchrony for liveness; under prolonged asynchrony, Bullshark fallback still commits but latency degrades to 3 rounds average. True asynchronous 2-phase finality is impossible with n=3f+1 due to FLP; Tusk's 7-round expected is optimal for async [2].

3. **Storage blowup**: DAG retains causal history for GC depth 50 rounds (~50k vertices × batches). At 600k TPS, 512B tx → ~300MB/s ingress per validator, 18GB/min, requiring aggressive pruning and archival nodes. Sui uses checkpoints to prune.

4. **Threshold crypto setup**: BLS threshold requires DKG trusted setup or 2f+1 honest dealer. Post-quantum safety claimed by Bullshark holds only if signatures replaced with post-quantum scheme (Dilithium), but then QC size grows O(n).

5. **Leader fairness and MEV**: Deterministic round-robin anchors enable predictable MEV extraction by next leader who orders causal history. Random coin election (Tusk) mitigates but increases latency. Current mitigations include encrypted mempool (Ferveo) but not integrated with DAG ordering.

6. **Network assumption brittleness**: Partial sync Bullshark relies on 2f+1 round advancement; if adversary controls network to partition honest into <2f+1, liveness stalls despite GST. Asynchronous protocols like Tusk avoid this but pay latency.

7. **Implementation complexity hidden**: While Bullshark claims 200 LOC, production Narwhal requires worker sharding, batch reconciliation, and reconfiguration for validator set changes, which is ~10k LOC in Sui.

## 7 Conclusion

HotStuff's linear QC abstraction unlocked responsive, leader-rotated BFT, but left dissemination as bottleneck. Narwhal's insight — *mempool as DAG* — turns every validator into concurrent disperser, achieving scale-out throughput limited only by NIC and worker count, with formal availability guarantees [2]. Tusk shows that consensus atop such DAG can be *free* — pure interpretation, no extra messages, asynchronous liveness via common coin. Bullshark [3][4] synthesizes practicality: 200 LOC, no Pacemaker, 2-round fast path, 2-phase finality via quorum certificates embedded in DAG votes, and optimal 3-message-delay commit in Mysticeti successor [5].

For internet-scale deployment, the path is clear: (i) uncertified DAG with implicit voting to cut CPU, (ii) multi-leader commit per round to reduce tail latency, (iii) fast path for owned objects to achieve 250ms finality, (iv) deterministic causal ordering to preserve censorship resistance. Future work includes post-quantum QCs, erasure-coded availability without certification, and encrypted DAG for MEV mitigation. The combination of HotStuff linearity, Narwhal-Tusk decoupling, and Bullshark/Mysticeti simplification establishes DAG-based BFT as the dominant architecture for high-throughput L1s, achieving 300k-600k TPS at sub-second latency — 2 orders of magnitude over classic HotStuff — while retaining n=3f+1 safety and asynchronous liveness.
