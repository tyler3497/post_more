# Personalized Federated Learning under Statistical Heterogeneity: FedPer Representation Splitting, Ditto Multi-Task Regularization, pFedMe Moreau Envelopes, and Hypernetwork-Based Client Adaptation

## Abstract

Federated learning trains shared models from decentralized data without raw-data exchange, yet statistical heterogeneity — non-identically distributed client data — breaks the core assumption that a single global model can serve every client, producing the client-drift phenomenon that degrades FedAvg convergence. This thesis develops a unified treatment of *personalized* federated learning (PFL), in which each client obtains a model adapted to its own distribution while still benefiting from federation. We systematize four dominant architectural families: (i) *representation splitting* (FedPer [1], FedRep [4]), which partitions networks into shared base layers and client-local personalization layers; (ii) *multi-task regularization* (Ditto [2]), a bi-level objective anchoring personalized models to a global reference via proximal penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert v_k - w^\star\rVert^2$; (iii) *Moreau-envelope decoupling* (pFedMe [3]), which replaces each client's loss by its Moreau envelope to separate personalized-model optimization from global-model learning; and (iv) *meta-learning and hypernetwork adaptation* (Per-FedAvg [5], pFedHN [6]). We derive the governing objectives, compare convergence guarantees, present a unified empirical protocol on Dirichlet-partitioned CIFAR benchmarks, and characterize open limitations in privacy, heterogeneity estimation, and new-client cold start.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 The promise and the fracture

Federated learning (FL) was introduced as a privacy-preserving alternative to centralized training: $K$ clients hold private datasets $\mathcal{D}_k$ drawn from distributions $P_k$, a central server orchestrates training, and only model updates — never raw data — traverse the network [8]. The canonical algorithm, **Federated Averaging (FedAvg)**, broadcasts $w^t$, runs local SGD epochs on sampled clients, and re-aggregates by weighted averaging.

Reality is not IID. Keyboards, hospitals, phones, and banks each generate data whose *label distributions*, *feature distributions*, and *sample volumes* differ sharply — a phenomenon termed **statistical heterogeneity** [1][4]. When $P_k \neq P_j$, local objectives $F_k(w) = \mathbb{E}_{\xi \sim P_k}[\ell(w; \xi)]$ diverge in their minima, and local updates drift toward incompatible optima. The resulting **client drift** provably slows FedAvg, and the averaged model $w^\star$ may be a poor model for *every* individual client.

> **Theorem (Informal — client drift under heterogeneity).** Let $F(w) = \sum_k p_k F_k(w)$ with each $F_k$ $L$-smooth, and let $\Gamma = F(w^\star) - \sum_k p_k F_k^\star \ge 0$ quantify gradient dissimilarity at the optimum. Then FedAvg with $E$ local epochs converges at a rate degraded by a term $\Omega(E^2 \Gamma)$; the drift term vanishes only when $\Gamma = 0$, i.e., the homogeneous case. (Cf. Li et al., *FedProx*, MLSys 2020.)

### 1.2 Personalization as the resolution

Personalized federated learning reframes the goal: instead of one model for all, learn a *tuple* of models $\{\theta_1, \dots, \theta_K\}$, one per client, where federation supplies statistical strength — shared representations, shared priors, shared structure — while per-client adaptation supplies fit. The field organizes along two axes: **what is shared** (full models, representations, initializations, or generators) and **when personalization happens** (during joint training or as post-hoc fine-tuning).

This thesis covers four families in depth, derives their optimization structure, compares theoretical guarantees, and evaluates them under a controlled non-IID protocol.

### 1.3 Contributions

- A **unified notation** casting FedPer, Ditto, pFedMe, Per-FedAvg, and pFedHN as instances of regularized multi-task learning.
- **Derivations** of each method's bi-level or alternating structure, including pFedMe's Moreau mechanics and FedRep's alternating minimization–descent.
- A **comparative table** of convergence rates, communication costs, and heterogeneity assumptions.
- An **empirical protocol** on Dirichlet-partitioned CIFAR benchmarks with exact hyperparameters for reproducibility.
- A critical **limitations analysis**: privacy, heterogeneity estimation, and new-client cold start.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Federated optimization primitives

Let client $k$ hold $n_k$ samples, $n = \sum_k n_k$, $p_k = n_k/n$. FedAvg solves

$$\min_{w \in \mathbb{R}^d} \; F(w) := \sum_{k=1}^{K} p_k F_k(w), \qquad F_k(w) = \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_k} \ell(w; x_{k,i}, y_{k,i}).$$

Variants add proximal regularization (**FedProx**) or control variates (**SCAFFOLD**) to tame drift, but all pursue a *single* global model.

### 2.2 A taxonomy of statistical heterogeneity

Following Hsu et al. and subsequent benchmarks, non-IID structure decomposes into:

- **Label distribution skew** ($P_k(y) \neq P_j(y)$): clients see different class proportions. Simulated by **Dirichlet partitioning**: draw $\mathbf{q}_k \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha \mathbf{1}_C)$ and assign class-$c$ samples to client $k$ with probability $q_{k,c}$. Small $\alpha$ (e.g., $0.1$) yields extreme skew; $\alpha \to \infty$ recovers IID.
- **Feature distribution skew** ($P_k(x \mid y) \neq P_j(x \mid y)$): same labels, different input styles — e.g., handwriting in FEMNIST. Addressed by **FedBN** [Xiaoxiao Li et al., ICLR 2021], which keeps batch-normalization statistics local.

### 2.3 Multi-task learning as a unifying lens

Personalized FL is naturally a **multi-task learning** problem: $K$ related tasks (clients), each with its own risk $F_k$, coupled through shared structure. Classical MTL regularizers take the form

$$\min_{\{\theta_k\},\, \Omega} \sum_{k=1}^{K} F_k(\theta_k) + \mathcal{R}(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_K; \Omega),$$

where $\mathcal{R}$ encodes task relatedness. The six methods differ only in $\mathcal{R}$:

| Method | Shared structure $\Omega$ | Regularizer $\mathcal{R}$ | Venue |
|---|---|---|---|
| FedPer [1] | Base-layer parameters $B$ | Hard constraint: $\theta_k = [B; h_k]$, $B$ averaged | arXiv 2019 |
| FedRep [4] | Representation $B$ | Alternating min–descent on $(B, \{h_k\})$ | ICML 2021 |
| Ditto [2] | Global model $w^\star$ | $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert v_k - w^\star\rVert^2$ | ICML 2021 |
| pFedMe [3] | Global model $w$ | Moreau envelope $M_i(w) = \min_{\theta_i} f_i(\theta_i) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\theta_i - w\rVert^2$ | NeurIPS 2020 |
| Per-FedAvg [5] | Meta-initialization $w$ | MAML objective $f_i(w - \alpha \nabla f_i(w))$ | NeurIPS 2020 |
| pFedHN [6] | Hypernetwork $\varphi$ | $\theta_k = h(v_k; \varphi)$, embeddings $v_k$ | ICML 2021 |

## 3 Methodology

We now derive the four focal families. Notation: $w$ denotes shared/global parameters, $\theta_k$ or $v_k$ the personalized parameters of client $k$, $\lambda > 0$ a regularization coefficient, and $h_k$ a client-local prediction head.

### 3.1 FedPer: base + personalization layers

FedPer [1] views a deep network as the composition of **base layers** $\phi_B$ (feature extraction) and **personalization layers** $\phi_P$ (typically the top fully-connected layers). Only base-layer parameters are uploaded and averaged; personalization layers never leave the device. Formally, client $k$'s model is $\theta_k = (B, h_k)$ with

$$B^{t+1} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{S}_t} p_k B_k^t, \qquad h_k^{t+1} = h_k^t - \eta \nabla_{h} F_k(B^t, h_k^t),$$

where crucially the local update optimizes $B_k$ and $h_k$ **simultaneously** each round, and only $B_k$ is communicated. The authors motivate this by the empirical observation that lower layers learn domain-general features while upper layers encode task-specific decision boundaries — precisely where label skew concentrates — and partial upload reduces communication.

### 3.2 Ditto: bi-level multi-task regularization

Ditto [2] formulates personalization as the bi-level program

$$\begin{aligned}
&\min_{v_1, \dots, v_K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} h_k(v_k; w^\star) := F_k(v_k) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert v_k - w^\star\rVert^2 \
\text{s.t.} \quad &w^\star \in \arg\min_{w} G(F_1(w), \dots, F_K(w)),
\end{aligned}$$

where $G$ is any global objective (FedAvg in practice). The algorithm is modular:

1. **Global pass**: run any standard FL solver (FedAvg, FedProx, …) to update $w^t$.
2. **Personal pass**: each client updates its personalized model by a few SGD steps on $h_k(v_k; w^t)$, starting from $w^t$.

The scalar $\lambda$ is the *personalization dial*:

- $\lambda \to \infty$: $v_k \to w^\star$ — pure global learning.
- $\lambda \to 0$: $v_k \to \arg\min F_k$ — pure local learning.

> **Theorem (Ditto, linear setting [2], informal).** For linear regression with device relatedness $\tau$, there exists $\lambda^\star$ such that Ditto's solution is simultaneously the most accurate, the fairest (minimum cross-device variance), and the most robust to poisoning among the solution path — and $\lambda^\star$ decreases as local sample sizes grow, devices become less related, or adversarial strength increases.

This is Ditto's central theoretical claim: fairness and robustness, normally *competing* constraints, are reconciled by personalization, because each $v_k$ fits its own clean distribution while the $\lambda$-tether inherits the global model's statistical strength.

### 3.3 pFedMe: decoupling via Moreau envelopes

pFedMe [3] also personalizes through proximal regularization, but restructures the optimization so that personalized-model fitting is **decoupled** from global-model learning. Define the **Moreau envelope** of client $i$'s loss:

$$M_i(w) := \min_{\theta_i} \; f_i(\theta_i) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert \theta_i - w\rVert^2.$$

The envelope $M_i$ is a *smoothed* surrogate of $f_i$ — it is differentiable even when $f_i$ is not, with $\nabla M_i(w) = \lambda(w - \hat{\theta}_i(w))$ where $\hat{\theta}_i(w)$ is the proximal minimizer. pFedMe solves

$$\min_{w} \; M(w) := \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i M_i(w),$$

i.e., a standard federated objective **over envelopes**. The algorithm alternates:

1. **Personalized optimization** (local, $R$ steps): $\theta_i \approx \arg\min_{\theta} f_i(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\theta - w^t\rVert^2$.
2. **Global update**: $w^{t+1} = w^t - \eta \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_t} p_i \nabla M_i(w^t)$, approximated by $\lambda(w^t - \theta_i^{t,R})$.

> **Theorem (pFedMe convergence [3], informal).** Under smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, pFedMe achieves a **quadratic speedup** over prior PFL methods for strongly convex objectives and a **sublinear speedup of order $2/3$** for smooth nonconvex objectives, while the personalized models $\theta_i$ are maintained without extra communication rounds.

The key insight is analytical rather than architectural: because the Moreau envelope is $\lambda$-smooth with $\nabla M_i(w)$ computable from the local proximal solution, the server-side update enjoys the convergence theory of smooth federated optimization, while clients simultaneously refine personalized models for free.

### 3.4 Per-FedAvg: personalization as meta-learning

Per-FedAvg [5] observes that personalization is *adaptation*, and adaptation is what **model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML)** was built for. It optimizes the meta-objective

$$\min_{w} \; F(w) := \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i \, f_i\big(w - \alpha \nabla f_i(w)\big),$$

so that $w$ is an initialization from which each client reaches a good personalized model $\theta_i = w - \alpha \nabla f_i(w)$ in **one gradient step**. The challenge is the Hessian: $\nabla F_i(w) = (I - \alpha \nabla^2 f_i(w))\nabla f_i(w - \alpha \nabla f_i(w))$. Fallah et al. propose two tractable variants:

- **Per-FedAvg (FO)**: first-order approximation, dropping the Hessian term.
- **Per-FedAvg (HF)**: Hessian-free, approximating Hessian-vector products by finite differences of gradients.

> **Theorem (Per-FedAvg [5], informal).** For nonconvex $f_i$ with bounded gradient dissimilarity measured by total-variation or 1-Wasserstein distance between client distributions, Per-FedAvg finds an $\epsilon$-first-order stationary point of the meta-objective; the required communication rounds scale with the heterogeneity of the underlying distributions.

### 3.5 Hypernetwork-based adaptation: pFedHN

pFedHN [6] takes a generative view: a **central hypernetwork** $h(\cdot; \varphi)$ maps a low-dimensional client embedding $v_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ to a full model $\theta_i = h(v_i; \varphi)$. Training optimizes

$$\min_{\varphi, v_1, \dots, v_N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i\big(h(v_i; \varphi)\big),$$

with $\varphi$ updated at the server from aggregated client gradients and $v_i$ updated locally. Sharing occurs through $\varphi$ — the hypernetwork learns the *manifold* of client models — while $v_i$ encodes client identity. A variant, **pFedHN-PC**, keeps a personal classifier head per client and generates only the feature extractor. Empirically, t-SNE of learned embeddings recovers semantic structure (e.g., CIFAR-100 coarse-class groupings), and pFedHN outperforms FedPer.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 When should the split sit? FedPer vs. FedRep update scheduling

FedPer and FedRep agree on the architecture — shared representation, local heads — but disagree on **optimization dynamics**. FedPer updates $B$ and $h_k$ jointly each round. FedRep [4] instead performs **alternating minimization–descent**: freeze the representation $B$, solve (approximately) for the optimal head $h_k^\star(B)$ with many local steps, then take a gradient step on $B$ with heads fixed. The head is low-dimensional and client-specific, so it can be fit nearly exactly per round, giving the representation update a clean, low-variance signal. Collins et al. prove **linear convergence to the ground-truth representation with near-optimal sample complexity** in a linear model — a guarantee FedPer's simultaneous updates cannot match.

### 4.2 The $\lambda$ dial: Ditto and pFedMe as one continuum

Ditto and pFedMe look different — bi-level regularization vs. envelope smoothing — but both are governed by a single proximal coefficient $\lambda$ with identical endpoint semantics:

| Regime | Ditto $v_k$ | pFedMe $\theta_i$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\lambda \to 0$ | Pure local ERM | Pure local ERM |
| $\lambda \to \infty$ | Global model $w^\star$ | Global model $w$ |
| Intermediate | Bias–variance trade-off | Bias–variance trade-off |

The differences are operational. Ditto is **solver-agnostic**: the global model can come from any FL algorithm, and personalization is post-hoc local fine-tuning with an $\ell_2$ tether — trivially deployable on existing FL infrastructure. pFedMe is **integrated**: the global model is learned over envelopes, so $w$ is *already* the right anchor for personalization, and convergence theory covers the joint process. Ditto's fairness/robustness analysis is its distinguishing theoretical contribution; pFedMe's is its convergence rate.

### 4.3 Meta-learning vs. regularization: the adaptation-cost trade-off

Per-FedAvg and Ditto/pFedMe represent two philosophies. Regularization methods pay at **training time** (each client maintains a personalized model throughout) and deliver models that are ready at inference. Meta-learning pays at **adaptation time**: the federation learns only an initialization, and each client must run gradient steps on arrival — including *new* clients unseen during training, which regularization methods handle poorly. The price of Per-FedAvg is second-order information and sensitivity to the inner step size $\alpha$.

### 4.4 Hypernetworks and the geometry of the client manifold

pFedHN's contribution is conceptual: instead of regularizing models *toward* each other, learn a **generator** of the client-model manifold. The hypernetwork $\varphi$ is the shared knowledge; embeddings $v_i$ are coordinates on the manifold. This has two underappreciated consequences. First, *generalization to new clients* reduces to optimizing a low-dimensional $v_{\text{new}}$ rather than a full model — though it still requires communication rounds, unlike kNN-Per's single forward pass [7]. Second, the embedding space is *interpretable*: Shamsian et al. show t-SNE clusters align with semantic label hierarchies, suggesting the hypernetwork discovers the latent task structure that MTL theory assumes.

### 4.5 Privacy: what personalization leaks

A sober deep-dive must note that personalization *increases* privacy surface. FedPer never uploads heads, which protects the most client-specific parameters — but the shared base still admits gradient-inversion attacks. Ditto's personalized models never leave the client either, yet the global anchor $w^\star$ inherits standard FL leakage. pFedHN centralizes a generator capable of emitting any client's model at the server. None of these methods compose cleanly with differential privacy out of the box: DP noise calibrated for global utility may destroy the fine-grained signal that personalization needs.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Unified evaluation protocol

To compare the four families, we specify a reproducible protocol on **CIFAR-10** and **CIFAR-100** partitioned across $K = 100$ clients:

1. **Partitioning**: Dirichlet label skew with $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$ ($\alpha = 0.1$: each client dominated by 1–2 classes; $\alpha = 1.0$: moderate skew). Fixed seed $s = 42$; train/val/test split $6{:}2{:}2$ per client.
2. **Architecture**: ResNet-18; FedPer/FedRep personalize the final residual block + classifier.
3. **Federation**: $T = 200$ rounds, participation $C = 0.1$, $E = 5$ local epochs, batch size $64$, SGD with $\eta = 0.01$, momentum $0.9$, cosine decay.
4. **Personalization hyperparameters**: Ditto $\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$ on validation; pFedMe $\lambda = 15$, $R = 5$ inner steps [3]; Per-FedAvg $\alpha = 0.01$, HF variant; FedRep head-solve to tolerance $10^{-3}$.
5. **Metrics**: mean per-client test accuracy (the PFL objective), worst-10% client accuracy (fairness), and accuracy under a 10% label-flip poisoning attack (robustness) — the Ditto evaluation triad [2].

```python
def dirichlet_partition(dataset, num_clients=100, alpha=0.5, seed=42):
    """Label-skew partition: q_k ~ Dir(alpha * 1_C); client k gets
    class-c samples with probability q_{k,c}."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    labels = np.array(dataset.targets)
    num_classes = labels.max() + 1
    proportions = rng.dirichlet(alpha * np.ones(num_classes),
                                size=num_clients)  # (K, C)
    client_indices = [[] for _ in range(num_clients)]
    for c in range(num_classes):
        idx = np.where(labels == c)[0]
        rng.shuffle(idx)
        splits = (proportions[:, c] / proportions[:, c].sum()
                  * len(idx)).astype(int)
        splits[-1] = len(idx) - splits[:-1].sum()  # fix rounding
        start = 0
        for k, s in enumerate(splits):
            client_indices[k].extend(idx[start:start + s].tolist())
            start += s
    return client_indices
```

### 5.2 Expected outcomes and what they would prove

Based on the reported results in [1–6], the protocol should reproduce the following ordering at $\alpha = 0.5$ on CIFAR-10 (mean per-client accuracy):

| Method | Expected acc. | Fairness (worst-10%) | Robust (10% poison) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg (no personalization) | ~62% | ~38% | ~45% |
| FedAvg + fine-tune | ~74% | ~55% | ~52% |
| FedPer [1] | ~78% | ~60% | ~58% |
| Per-FedAvg (HF) [5] | ~79% | ~62% | ~57% |
| FedRep [4] | ~81% | ~65% | ~60% |
| pFedMe [3] | ~82% | ~66% | ~61% |
| Ditto [2] | ~81% | **~70%** | **~66%** |
| pFedHN [6] | **~83%** | ~66% | ~60% |

Three predictions carry theoretical weight: (i) **representation-splitting beats naive fine-tuning** by a clear margin, confirming that heterogeneity concentrates in the head; (ii) **Ditto dominates on fairness and robustness** while remaining competitive on accuracy, confirming the $\lambda^\star$ analysis of [2]; (iii) **accuracy gaps widen as $\alpha \to 0.1$** and vanish as $\alpha \to \infty$, confirming that all PFL gains are heterogeneity gains.

### 5.3 Proof sketch: why the Moreau envelope decouples

The pFedMe rate improvement rests on a classical observation. For $\lambda$-strongly-convex-in-$\theta$ inner problem, the envelope $M_i$ is $L_M$-smooth with $L_M \le \lambda$, and

$$\nabla M_i(w) = \lambda\big(w - \hat{\theta}_i(w)\big), \qquad \hat{\theta}_i(w) = \arg\min_\theta f_i(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\theta - w\rVert^2.$$

Hence the server update $w \leftarrow w - \eta \sum_i p_i \nabla M_i(w)$ is *exactly* federated gradient descent on a smooth objective — all the standard machinery (variance reduction, partial participation analysis) applies verbatim. The personalized models $\theta_i$ are byproducts of evaluating $\nabla M_i$, requiring no additional communication. The quadratic speedup for strongly convex $f_i$ follows because the envelope's condition number is controlled by $\lambda$ (chosen by the analyst) rather than by the raw $f_i$'s conditioning; the $2/3$-order sublinear speedup in the nonconvex case follows from the standard smooth nonconvex FL analysis applied to $M$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Heterogeneity is assumed, not estimated.** Every method tunes a personalization knob ($\lambda$, $\alpha$, split depth) against the *degree* of heterogeneity, yet no deployed FL system measures $P_k$-dissimilarity privately and cheaply. Dirichlet $\alpha$ is a simulator's fiction; real skew is unknown and time-varying.
2. **Cold start for new clients.** Ditto, pFedMe, FedPer, and FedRep all require a newcomer to participate in training (or at least in fine-tuning rounds) before obtaining a personalized model. Only meta-learning (Per-FedAvg) and embedding methods (pFedHN) offer a principled onboarding path.
3. **Privacy–personalization tension.** As analyzed in §4.5, finer personalization means more client-specific information is encoded in exchanged artifacts. Formal DP guarantees for PFL remain weak.
4. **Communication and compute asymmetry.** pFedMe's inner proximal solves, Per-FedAvg's Hessian-vector products, and pFedHN's hypernetwork all raise per-round client cost — precisely the devices least able to pay it.
5. **Evaluation monoculture.** CIFAR with Dirichlet skew dominates the literature; naturally partitioned benchmarks (FEMNIST, Shakespeare in LEAF) are used less often, and feature skew is rarely evaluated alongside label skew.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Personalized federated learning resolves the central tension of federated optimization — one model cannot serve distributions it averages over — by making heterogeneity a first-class modeling choice rather than a nuisance to be regularized away. The four families studied here represent distinct answers to *what should be shared*: FedPer and FedRep share representations and localize heads; Ditto shares a global anchor and localizes through proximal multi-task regularization, uniquely delivering fairness and robustness alongside accuracy; pFedMe shares the Moreau-envelope landscape itself, attaining the sharpest convergence rates; Per-FedAvg and pFedHN share *adaptation machinery* — initializations and model generators — offering the cleanest story for new clients.

The unifying lesson is architectural: **federation adds value in the representation, personalization adds value in the head**, and every successful method is, at bottom, a different mechanism for negotiating that boundary. Open problems — private heterogeneity estimation, cold-start onboarding, and DP-compatible personalization — will determine whether these methods graduate from benchmarks to the billion-device deployments that motivated federated learning.
