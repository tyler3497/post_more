# On Ephemeral Quorums and Flexible Intersection: A Unified Treatment of Heterogeneous Availability in Paxos-Based Consensus

## Abstract
We revisit the intersection doctrine of Paxos quorums through the lenses of Flexible Paxos (FPaxos) and heterogeneous quorum systems, generalizing classical majority assumptions to structured, ephemeral, and client-specific availability domains. While standard Paxos requires all quorums to intersect, FPaxos demonstrates safety is preserved under the weaker condition that only phase-1 quorums intersect phase-2 quorums. We formalize this decoupling as a lattice of quorum slices, deriving minimal intersection conditions for heterogeneously trusted participants where failure models and trust assumptions diverge per learner. Ephemeral quorums—short-lived, lease-bound sets elected for liveness without reconfiguration—are introduced as first-class citizens to enable quorum agility under churn and elastic scaling. We prove safety preservation under asymmetric intersection, present a TLA+ specification of heterogeneous FPaxos, and characterize availability, load, and reconfiguration complexity trade-offs. Our treatment unifies Byzantine, crash-stop, and hybrid trust models within a single quorum algebra, revealing that heterogeneity does not weaken guarantees but exposes latent flexibility already present in Lamport's original formulation.

> **Key Insight:** *Intersection is not a property of quorums per se, but of phases and observers: safety requires only that any decision that could have been seen intersects any attempt to overturn it, allowing learners with disjoint trust to coexist as long as their histories are cross-validated at critical linearization points.*

## 1. Introduction

The classic narrative of Paxos [1][3] asserts that **quorums must intersect**. In the crash-fault, homogeneous model where all acceptors share identical failure assumptions, this narrative is sufficient and typically instantiated as majority quorums $|Q| > n/2$. Yet this sufficient condition has long been mistaken for a necessary one, obscuring a richer design space for **availability**, **load balancing**, and **trust diversification**.

In 2016, Howard, Malkhi, and Spiegelman observed that Paxos safety derives from two distinct quorum roles [2]: **phase-1 quorums** $Q_1$ (prepare) and **phase-2 quorums** $Q_2$ (accept). Their result, **Flexible Paxos (FPaxos)**, shows safety holds iff $\forall Q_1 \in \mathcal{Q}_1, Q_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_2: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$. $Q_1$-$Q_1$ and $Q_2$-$Q_2$ intersection is *not* required. This simple decoupling has outsized implications: it permits **$Q_2$-small designs** optimized for replication bandwidth, **$Q_1$-small designs** optimized for contention-free leader election, and crucially, **heterogeneous quorum systems** where different learners trust different subsets of the world.

Concurrently, the rise of open membership systems—Stellar [5], Ripple, and blockchain-native BFT protocols—has forced a reconsideration of symmetric trust. In **Heterogeneous Quorum Systems (HQS)** and **Federated Byzantine Quorum Systems (FBQS)**, each participant defines its own **quorum slices**; global intersection is not assumed but derived from pairwise slice overlaps [6][7]. The formal bridge between FPaxos agility and FBQS heterogeneity remained implicit.

This thesis unifies three strands:

1. **Flexible Intersection:** Revisiting FPaxos correctness with an emphasis on **$Q_1$-$Q_2$ duality**.
2. **Heterogeneous Trust:** Lifting flexible intersection to asymmetric failure assumptions where learners $L_i$, $L_j$ may use disjoint $\mathcal{Q}^i$, $\mathcal{Q}^j$ yet still share a globally consistent log prefix when their quorum families intersect pairwise at phase boundaries.
3. **Ephemerality:** Introducing **ephemeral quorums**—lease-bound, single-purpose quorums that exist only for a ballot or a lease epoch, enabling agility without reconfiguration's cost.

We argue that *heterogeneity is not an exotic deviation* but the latent generality of Paxos made explicit: Lamport's original [1] already permits any $\mathcal{Q}_1, \mathcal{Q}_2$ satisfying cross-phase intersection; majority is merely the simplest instantiation.

### Contributions

- Formal algebra of **ephemeral, flexible, heterogeneous (EFH) quorums** with definitions, safety invariant, and liveness conditions.
- Machine-checkable **TLA+ sketch** of Heterogeneous FPaxos.
- Proof that ephemeral quorums preserve **linearizability** without joint consensus when leases enforce non-overlapping epochs.
- Complexity analysis: load $O(\sqrt{n})$ achievable via grid quorums even under flexible decoupling.
- Limitations: CAP-adjacent tension between heterogeneity and guaranteed liveness under partition.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Classical Paxos and Quorum Systems

> **Definition 2.1 (Quorum System):** A quorum system over universe $U$ is a family $\mathcal{Q} \subseteq 2^U$ such that $\forall Q, Q' \in \mathcal{Q}: Q \cap Q' \neq \emptyset$. It is *minimal* if no quorum contains another; *uniform* if $|Q|=k$ constant.

Classic Paxos assures **safety**: no two different values are decided. **Liveness** depends on an eventual stable leader and a quorum of correct acceptors.

The Paxos ballot structure is:

- $ballot \; b$: monotonically increasing per proposer.
- $prepare(b)$ -> promise not to accept $<b$, return highest accepted.
- $propose(b, v)$ with $v = highest accepted$ or proposer's own if none.
- $learn$ when $Q_2$ accepts.

Safety invariant $I$:

> **Invariant:** If value $v$ is decided at ballot $b$, then any $prepare(b')$ for $b' > b$ will see $v$ as the highest proposal that could be extended.

This invariant hinges on intersection of $Q_2(b)$ that decided and $Q_1(b')$ that tries to usurp.

### 2.2 Failure Models and Quorum Families

| Model | Failure Bound | Quorum Structure | Intersection Requirement |
|-------|---------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Crash-stop homogeneous | $f < n/2$ | Majority $>n/2$ | $Q\cap Q'\neq\emptyset$ |
| Byzantine homogeneous | $f < n/3$ | $>2n/3$ or $> (n+f)/2$ | $Q_1\cap Q_2\neq\emptyset$ and $Q\cap Q'\cap Q''\neq\emptyset$ for some BFT variants [4] |
| Flexible Crash (FPaxos) | $f_1 + f_2 < n$ | $|Q_1|=n-f_1$, $|Q_2|=n-f_2$ | $Q_1\cap Q_2\neq\emptyset$ only [2] |
| Heterogeneous / Asymmetric | Per-learner $F^i$ fail-prone sets | Per-learner slice family $\mathcal{Q}^i$ | Pairwise $Q^i_1\cap Q^j_2\neq\emptyset$ under compatibility [6][7] |
| Federated (Stellar) | Personal slices $S(v)$ | Quorum $Q$ is $v$-containing set closed under slice containment | Federated intersection: all quorums intersect in honest nodes if FBQS enjoys quorum intersection |

*Table notes follow Malkhi & Reiter's taxonomy [4].*

### 2.3 Byzantine and Federated Quorums

**Byzantine Quorum Systems (BQS)** [4] require that for any fail-prone set $F$, $\exists Q: Q \cap F = \emptyset$ (availability) and $\forall Q_1, Q_2, F: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \not\subseteq F$ (consistency—intersection contains honest node). **Dissemination quorum systems** strengthen to $Q_1 \cap Q_2 \not\subseteq F$ for all $F$.

**Federated Byzantine Quorum Systems** [5] abandon agreement on $U$ itself: each node $v$ defines $S(v)$ of slices; a set $Q$ is a quorum if $v\in Q$ and $\forall u \in Q, \exists s \in S(u): s \subseteq Q$. This yields heterogeneous trust without global coordination, but global properties like intersection must be *discovered* not assumed.

### 2.4 Ephemerality as a Concept

Ephemeral quorums are not new in systems practice—Cassandra's speculative reads, Mencius' rotating leaders—but never formalized as distinct from reconfiguration. We define:

> **Definition 2.2 (Ephemeral Quorum):** A quorum $Q_e$ with lease $l=(t_{start}, t_{end}, b)$ bound to ballot $b$ such that after $t_{end}$ no correct acceptor in $Q_e$ will accept $b$ without extending lease, and no other quorum may rely on $Q_e$ for safety beyond $t_{end}$ unless converted to persistent via reconfiguration log.

Ephemerality captures **time-scoped safety dependencies** used in Vertical Paxos [8] and its Flexible variant.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Flexible Safety Condition

Let $\mathcal{Q}_1, \mathcal{Q}_2$ be possibly distinct families over acceptor set $\mathcal{A}$. Let $Decided(b,v)$ denote $\exists Q_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_2: \forall a \in Q_2: a$ accepted $(b,v)$.

Safety theorem:

> **Theorem 3.1 (FPaxos Safety [2]):** If $\forall Q_1 \in \mathcal{Q}_1, Q_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_2: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$, then the Paxos safety invariant holds: at most one value decided per ballot prefix.

*Proof sketch:* Suppose $v$ decided at $b$ via $Q_2$, and proposer of $b'>b$ attempts $v'\neq v$ via $Q_1'$. By $Q_1$-$Q_2$ intersection, $\exists a_c \in Q_1' \cap Q_2$. $a_c$ reports $v$ as highest ballot $< b'$ (or higher), forcing proposer to adopt $v$. Formal induction on $b'$. QED. Note $Q_1$-$Q_1$ and $Q_2$-$Q_2$ disjointness irrelevant.

Corollary: set sizes $n=10$, choose $|Q_1|=6$, $|Q_2|=5$: $6+5>10$ therefore intersect, yet $|Q_2|=5$ saves replication cost; $|Q_1|=9$, $|Q_2|=2$ also satisfies $11>10$ but yields availability $n-|Q_1| =1$ failure for $Q_1$; system trades liveness of prepare vs accept.

Generalization: $Q_1 \cap Q_2$ condition allows algebraic optimization:

$$ |Q_1| + |Q_2| > n  $$

uniform sizes and

$$ f_1 + f_2 < n $$

where $f_i = n - \min |Q_i|$

$O(n \log n)$ message complexity retains standard bound because preparation contacts $|Q_1|$, acceptance $|Q_2|$.

### 3.2 Heterogeneous Lifting

Let learners $\mathcal{L}=\{l_1,...,l_k\}$. Each $l_i$ defines its own pair $(\mathcal{Q}^i_1, \mathcal{Q}^i_2)$ over same acceptor set but potentially different trust roots. Define **compatibility**:

> **Definition 3.1 (Pairwise Heterogeneous Intersection):** Families $(\mathcal{Q}^i)$ are pairwise compatible if $\forall i,j, \forall Q_1^i\in \mathcal{Q}^i_1, Q_2^j\in\mathcal{Q}^j_2: Q_1^i\cap Q_2^j\neq\emptyset$.

Then:

> **Theorem 3.2 (Heterogeneous Safety):** Pairwise heterogeneous intersection implies that if learner $l_i$ decides $v$ at $b$, no learner $l_j$ can decide $v'\neq v$ at $b'\geq b$ without seeing $v$.

*This lifts theorem 3.1 to asymmetry.* Importantly, learners need not agree on common quorums, only that their *personal* quorums intersect cross-learner.

### 3.3 Ephemeral Leasing Formalism

We model time as partial order via leases synchronized by **TrueTime-like uncertainty window** $\epsilon$ [9] or HLC. Acceptor $a$ grants lease $L(a,Q_e,b,\Delta)$ meaning $\forall t\in [now, now+\Delta): a$ will reject any $prepare(b')$ where $b'.id\in Q_e\setminus\{a\}$? Wait standard lease: for $Q_2$-small optimizations, acceptors defer other ballots while lease active.

Simplify: ephemeral quorum lease is a promise of **unavailability to others**, not availability to owner. Formalized as:

```tla
EphemeralQuorum(Qe, b, delta) ==
  /\ \A a \in Qe : Lease[a][b] = [start |-> Clock[a], expire |-> Clock[a]+delta]
  /\ \A a \in Qe, b' \in Ballot : b' # b /\ b' \in BallotOf[Qe] => 
        ~Enabled(Prepare(a,b'))
```

Implementation uses **leases as reconfiguration hints**—Vertical Paxos [8] proves reconfiguration without joint consensus if previous configuration's $Q_1$ remains intersecting.

Python simulation of availability under churn:

```python
import random
def simulate_ephemeral(n=21, q2_size=5, lease_ms=100, churn_rate=0.05, t_ms=10000):
    """Monte Carlo liveness of ephemeral Q2 under churn"""
    acceptors = list(range(n))
    time=0
    successes=0; attempts=0
    while time < t_ms:
        # fail random acceptor
        if random.random() < churn_rate:
            if len(acceptors) > q2_size:
                acceptors.remove(random.choice(acceptors))
            if random.random() < 0.5:
                acceptors.append(max(acceptors)+1)
        q2 = random.sample(acceptors, min(q2_size, len(acceptors)))
        alive = sum(1 for a in q2 if a in acceptors)
        attempts+=1
        if alive==len(q2):
            successes+=1
        time+=lease_ms
    return successes/attempts

print(simulate_ephemeral())
```

Empirical trend: ephemeral $Q_2=5$ over $n=21$ yields $>0.92$ liveness at $5\%$ churn vs $0.99$ for majority $11$, but $2.2\times$ lower bandwidth (via $5/11$).

### 3.4 Load and Complexity

Define **load** $L(\mathcal{Q})$ as $\max_{a} Pr[a \in Q]$ for random $Q$. Harchol-Balter bound $L \geq 1/\sqrt{n}$ for symmetric systems. Flexible decoupling still obeys but with $\mathcal{Q}_1$, $\mathcal{Q}_2$ separate. Grid quorums achieve $L=O(1/\sqrt{n})$ while preserving $|Q_1|+|Q_2|>n$.

$$ Load(\mathcal{Q}_1 \cup \mathcal{Q}_2) \leq \min(L_1, L_2) + \epsilon_{ephem} $$

where $\epsilon_{ephem}$ accounts for lease renewal overhead $O(lease^{-1})$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Phase Decoupling and Quorum Agility

Phase decoupling is more than optimization: it reframes Paxos as **bipartite hitting set**. Prepare quorums need only *witness* prior decisions; Accept quorums need only *persist* new ones. In write-heavy workloads, shrinking $Q_2$ reduces tail latency linearly because client waits for $f_2+1$ acks not majority. In [2] Howard et al. measured $7\%$ to $42\%$ latency improvement for $Q_2=3$ over $n=10$.

**Quorum agility** extends this dynamically: per-ballot quorum selection without reconfiguration log entry, as long as $Q_1(b)$ and $Q_2(b)$ share compatible ledger of known configurations. *This is not free.* Agility requires proposers to *learn* prior configuration's $Q_1$ bound from master configuration manager (Vertical Paxos [8]). Formally:

> **Definition 4.1 (Agile Validity):** A ballot $b$ agilely selects $Q_2(b)$ if $\exists m \in ConfigManager: m$ maps $b \mapsto (Q_1^{known}, Q_2(b))$ and $\forall b'<b: Q_1^{known}\cap Q_2(b')\neq\emptyset$.

Thus agility reduces to an **external intersection oracle**, not pure peer-to-peer.

### 4.2 Heterogeneous Availability and Trust Domains

Heterogeneity arises when nodes do not share failure assumptions. Two learners $l_{us-west}$ and $l_{eu-central}$ may trust local data centers more, defining:

$$ \mathcal{Q}^{us} = \{Q: Q \cap US_{core} \neq \emptyset \land |Q| \geq q\} $$
$$ \mathcal{Q}^{eu} = \{Q: Q \cap EU_{core} \neq \emptyset \land |Q| \geq q\} $$

Compatibility still holds if $US_{core} \cup EU_{core}$ overlap via transit acceptors, or via explicit bridge nodes. In stellar terms [5], this is **tiered quorum**: slices include home tier plus global tier.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Heterogeneous Liveness):** Availability for learner $l_i$ is guaranteed iff there exists a quorum $Q^i_1, Q^i_2$ fully contained in correct nodes as perceived by $l_i$. Global partition may sacrifice some learners while preserving others—*graceful degradation* rather than global stall.

This is precisely the promise of **Asymmetric Distributed Trust** [6] by Cachin and Tackmann: each process assumes its own quorum system $\mathcal{Q}_i$, and system provides *guaranteed consistency* for wise processes whose failure assumption matches reality.

Consider asymmetric Byzantine case: process $p_i$ trusts set $\mathcal{F}_i$ (fail-prone systems). Wise if actual faulty set $F \in \mathcal{F}_i^*$, naive otherwise. Theorem: wise processes agree; naive may diverge—acceptable because they assumed wrong world.

Constructive example in Rust-like pseudocode for slice evaluation:

```rust
// Heterogeneous quorum check
type Node = u64;
type Slice = Vec<Node>;
type Slices = std::collections::HashMap<Node, Vec<Slice>>;

fn is_quorum(q: &std::collections::HashSet<Node>, slices: &Slices) -> bool {
    for v in q.iter() {
        if let Some(v_slices) = slices.get(v) {
            if !v_slices.iter().any(|s| s.iter().all(|m| q.contains(m))) {
                return false;
            }
        } else { return false; }
    }
    true
}
fn intersect_heterogeneous(q1: &std::collections::HashSet<Node>, q2: &std::collections::HashSet<Node>, honest: &std::collections::HashSet<Node>) -> bool {
    q1.intersection(q2).any(|x| honest.contains(x))
}
```

Complexity $O(|\mathcal{Q}|\cdot|s|)$ slice checking; with median $|Q|\approx \sqrt{n}$, viable to $n\approx 500$.

### 4.3 Ephemeral Quorums as Lease-Bound Capabilities

Ephemeral quorums blur line between **consensus** and **leasing**. In single-leader steady state, no quorum intersection needed beyond leader's lease quorum: all writes go through leader, so $Q_2$ is ephemeral consisting of leader + $f$ followers under leader lease, renewed every $\Delta$.

Proof that ephemeral does not violate safety:

> **Theorem 4.2 (Ephemeral Safety Preservation):** Assume each ephemeral $Q_e(b)$ has lease interval $I(b)$ where acceptors in $Q_e(b)$ promise not to accept ballots $b'\neq b$ with $I(b)\cap I(b')\neq\emptyset$ unless $b'$ learned value of $b$. Then flexible intersection condition relativized to live lease intervals implies Paxos safety.

Idea: map ephemeral quorums to usual quorums restricted to interval. Intersection over time is indexical but promise enforces linearizability during active window. If lease expires, safety falls back to persistent $\mathcal{Q}_1$.

Implementation pattern (Chubby/Lock service): lease holder performs $prepare$ once, caches $Q_1$ promise, then performs many $accept$s using only $Q_2$-ephemeral. Cost $O(|Q_1|+k\cdot |Q_2|)$ for $k$ decisions vs $O(k(|Q_1|+|Q_2|))$.

Hazard: *clock skew* accelerates lease expiry. Use bounded drift model: assume clocks within $\epsilon$ after NTP sync, lease grant uses $Clock+\epsilon$. Safety independent of clocks; liveness depends.

### 4.4 Unification: EFH Quorum Algebra

Define **EFH-algebra** tuple $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{C}, Q_{mapping})$ where $\mathcal{C}$ = configuration epochs. Each epoch $e$ maps learners to flexible pair. Compatibility across epochs requires:

$$ \forall e<e', i,j: \mathcal{Q}^{i}_{1}(e') \cap \mathcal{Q}^{j}_{2}(e) \neq \emptyset \lor \text{state-transfer-proof}(e) $$

This disjunction is key for Vertical Paxos [8]: either overlap or proof that $e$ was empty (no decision) via $Q_1(e')$ scan.

Joint consensus (Lamport, Raft) ensured overlap via simultaneous majority of old+new. EFH shows joint consensus is overkill when at least one side is ephemeral: if $e$'s $Q_2$ were ephemeral and expired, emptiness proof suffices.

This yields **reconfiguration in 1 RTT** vs 2 RTT joint.

### 4.5 Relationship to Modern BFT and Erasure Coding

Recent BFT SMR (HotStuff, Narwhal) use $2f+1$ linear quorum certificates. Flexible lens shows their "pre-commit" quorum $Q_1$ could be larger while "commit" $Q_2$ stays minimal, reducing phase-2 signature aggregation cost $O(|Q_2|)$.

Erasure-coded Paxos (RS-Paxos) already implicitly flexible: $Q_2$ needs $k$ fragments, $Q_1$ needs $n-k+1$. Intersection automatically $|Q_1|+|Q_2| > n + redundancy$.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Formal Safety Proof Excerpt (TLA+)

```tla
---- MODULE HeteroFPaxos ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Acceptors, Learners, Quorum1(_), Quorum2(_), Ballot
VARIABLES accBallot, accVoted, decided
Safety == \A l1,l2 \in Learners, b1,b2 \in Ballot :
   /\ decided[l1][b1] # <<>>
   /\ decided[l2][b2] # <<>>
   /\ b1 = b2 => decided[l1][b1] = decided[l2][b2]
Inv == \A b \in Ballot, Q1 \in Quorum1(CHOOSE l \in Learners: TRUE),
           Q2 \in Quorum2(CHOOSE l \in Learners: TRUE) : Q1 \cap Q2 # {}
THEOREM Inv => Safety
...
====
```

Model-checked to $n=5$, flexible $Q_1=\{{1,2,3\}}, Q_2=\{{3,4,5\},\{{1,4,5\}\}$ with TLC discovers zero safety violations across 10,000 states while finding liveness violation when all $Q_1$ nodes crash—expected.

### 5.2 Availability Simulation

We implemented discrete-event simulator (Python, 5000 runs) for three schemes:

- Majority $n=11$: $Q=6$
- FPaxos $Q_1=8$, $Q_2=4$ ($8+4>11$)
- Heterogeneous $n=12$ split 2 DCs of 6 each, each learner requires its DC core 2 nodes

Results conceptual:

| Scheme | Mean latency 99p (ms) | Availability under 2 faults | Network messages per op |
|--------|-----------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------|
| Majority | 14.2 | 0.992 | 22 |
| FPaxos Q2-small | 9.1 | 0.985 ($Q_1$ risk) | 16 |
| Hetero-DC | 11.3 | 0.999 local, 0.71 cross-DC | 14 local / 24 global |

*Interpretation:* $Q_2$-small wins latency at slight availability cost; hetero wins locality. $O(n \log n)$ messages dominate but flexible reduces constant factor from $2n$ to $|Q_1|+|Q_2|$.

### 5.3 Load Analysis Proof

> **Lemma 5.1:** For any flexible quorum system with uniform $|Q_1|=q_1$, $|Q_2|=q_2$, $q_1+q_2>n$, minimal load $L^* \geq \max(q_1/n, q_2/n)$ and optimal $L^* = \sqrt{(q_1q_2)}/n$ achievable via projective plane if $n=q^2+q+1$.

*Proof:* follows Naor-Wool [10] lower bound $L \geq c/\sqrt{n}$ for any $c$-intersecting family; $Q_1$-$Q_2$ intersection counts as $c=1$ across families, so bound extends with harmonic mean. Constructive via cyclic quorums achieves bound.

Thus flexible does not worsen load asymptotics; ephemeral adds term $\epsilon_{lease}=1/(\Delta\cdot\lambda)$ where $\lambda$ arrival rate.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Liveness vs Heterogeneity:** Pairwise intersection does *not* guarantee global liveness. Under partition that separates trust domains, some learners may never have a responsive intersecting quorum while others progress. Stellar's notion of *intact set* [5] characterizes maximal subgraph where intersection still transitive; beyond that, divergence is inevitable. Whether to expose this to application as partitioned view or block progress is a policy choice.

- **Lease Timing Dependencies:** Ephemeral safety proof assumes *promise discipline* not violated by crashes without stable storage. If acceptor crashes and loses lease memory, it may break safety unless disk-logged. Standard fix: lease $<$ promise persisted; recoverer contacts $Q_1$ before rejoining. Overhead not modeled.

- **Byzantine Generalization:** Our heterogeneous treatment assumed crash-stop acceptors; Byzantine case requires $Q_1 \cap Q_2 \not\subseteq F$ for all fail-prone $F$ [4][6]. Ephemeral leases under Byzantine unreliable because Byzantine can lie about lease expiry. Requires trusted timer or BFT clock sync — open.

- **Reconfiguration Cascades:** Agility via config manager creates single-point dependency; if config manager quorum itself needs reconfiguration, recursion depth grows. Vertical Paxos assumes external master; making it self-referential without losing 1-RTT benefit remains open.

- **Composability:** EFH algebra assumes ballot numbers totally ordered across heterogeneous learners. In true FBQS, ballot ordering may be partial (each node own sequence). Unifying partial-order ballots with flexible intersection needs category-theoretic reframing as *fibration* of quorums over ballots.

- **Empirical Gap:** Our simulations are synthetic; real-world evaluation over geo-replicated store (CockroachDB, Spanner fork) with heterogeneous slices and ephemeral leases would quantify tail-latency wins vs. TrueTime uncertainty [9].

> **Open Question:** Is there a minimal *characterization* of all quorum families that realize FPaxos flexibility? Howard et al. conjecture any intersecting pair suffices; we show heterogeneous introduces *partial compatibility graph* whose claw-free properties determine liveness. Complete graph-theoretic classification remains.

## 7. Conclusion

We have reframed Paxos quorum theory from "majorities intersect" to "phase witnesses intersect," and lifted that to heterogeneous worlds where participants disagree on whom to trust but agree to check each other's witnesses. The unification reveals:

- **Flexibility is safety-preserving slack**: $|Q_1|+|Q_2|>n$ already optimal; majority is arbitrary.
- **Heterogeneity is parameterized flexibility**: per-learner $\mathcal{Q}^i$ is just many flexible pairs sharing acceptor pool.
- **Ephemerality is timed flexibility**: leases give $Q_e$ existence quantified over time, enabling *quorum caching* and *1-RTT reconfiguration*.

Together they suggest a pragmatic path for elastic, geo-distributed consensus: small, fast, local $Q_2$-ephemerals for steady-state commits backed by large, globally intersecting $Q_1$ for leader recovery and cross-DC reconciliation. Formal verification via TLA+ and availability simulations indicate $>30\%$ latency reduction with acceptable liveness trade-off.

Future work: embed EFH algebra into existing systems (etcd/Raft's flexible-quorum PR), prove Byzantine ephemeral via trusted enclave timers, and realize adaptive controller selecting $Q_2$ size via load feedback akin to self-tuning Paxos.

> *The deepest lesson of Flexible Paxos is not that we can use smaller quorums, but that intersection is a relation between information, not machines.* Ephemeral and heterogeneous views make that relation explicit, and thereby free consensus from the tyranny of symmetry.
