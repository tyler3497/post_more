# Spiking Neural Networks: Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Dynamics, Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity, and the Theory of Temporal Coding

## Abstract

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) constitute the third generation of neural network models, replacing continuous-valued activations with discrete, all-or-none spike events whose precise timing carries information [2]. This thesis develops the computational theory of spike-based networks. We formalize the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron as the canonical dynamical primitive within the spectrum of spiking models ranked by fidelity and cost [3]. We analyze spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) — the temporally asymmetric Hebbian rule mapped in hippocampal cultures [1] — as the principal unsupervised mechanism of spike-based learning, and the temporal-coding hypothesis, under which single spiking neurons outperform far larger sigmoidal networks, with evidence that biological vision is too fast for rate codes [9]. The non-differentiability of the spike — the central obstacle to supervised training — is addressed through surrogate-gradient methods [7] and eligibility propagation, rendering recurrent SNN learning online and biologically plausible [8]. Finally, we survey the neuromorphic substrates executing these models with milliwatt-scale efficiency — TrueNorth [4], Loihi [5], SpiNNaker [6] — and delineate the advantages of spike-based computation and the gaps before SNNs rival deep networks at scale.

## 1 Introduction

Deep learning rests on a convenient fiction: neuronal outputs are graded, real-valued, differentiable everywhere. The first generation — McCulloch–Pitts threshold gates — computed with binary outputs; the second — sigmoidal networks trained by gradient descent — conquered perception and language by exploiting differentiability. The third generation, formalized by Maass [2], abandons graded activations: neurons communicate through *spikes*, stereotyped pulses whose information resides not in amplitude but in number and precise timing.

Two forces motivate this shift. First, *energy*: the human brain performs on the order of 10¹⁴ synaptic operations per second within a ~20 W envelope, while a GPU performing comparable floating-point throughput draws hundreds of watts. Second, *time*: the sensory world is intrinsically temporal, and event-driven sensors deliver asynchronous streams of brightness changes rather than synchronous frames — a data modality for which clocked, frame-based networks are structurally mismatched.

The obstacles are precisely characterizable. The spike mechanism is discontinuous, so gradient-based learning collapses on contact. Biological plasticity is local in space and time, while backpropagation through time demands non-local storage and retrograde error signals. And "representation" must be rethought: when spikes cost energy, *silence* is informative, and the stimulus-to-spike-train code — rate, latency, rank order, phase — becomes a first-class design decision.

Section 2 traces the lineage from Hodgkin–Huxley models to efficient phenomenological neurons and neuromorphic hardware [3][4][5][6]. Section 3 establishes the methodological core: the formal SNN model, simulation paradigms, a taxonomy of neural codes, and the three learning families. Section 4 develops the deep theory — LIF dynamics, STDP [1], temporal coding [2][9], surrogate gradients [7][8] — Section 5 the empirical results and proofs, Section 6 the limitations, and Section 7 the road ahead.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From conductances to spikes

The quantitative study begins with Hodgkin and Huxley, whose 1952 conductance-based model of the squid giant axon remains the gold standard of fidelity — and the ceiling of cost: roughly 1,200 FLOPS per millisecond per neuron [3]. At the opposite pole sits Lapicque's 1907 integrate-and-fire neuron: a capacitor integrates current until threshold, then spikes and resets. Between them lies a spectrum of reductions — FitzHugh–Nagumo, Morris–Lecar, adaptive exponential IF — each trading fidelity for speed.

Izhikevich's 2004 comparison [3] made this trade-off systematic: enumerating *twenty* neurocomputational features of biological neurons, he showed a two-dimensional quadratic model reproduces all of them at ~13 FLOPS per millisecond — tens of thousands of cortical neurons in real time on a desktop PC:

$$ \dot{v} = 0.04v^2 + 5v + 140 - u + I, \qquad \dot{u} = a(bv - u) $$

with the spike-and-reset condition that when $v \geq 30$ mV, $v \leftarrow c$ and $u \leftarrow u + d$. The four dimensionless parameters $(a, b, c, d)$ select among the twenty firing patterns.

### 2.2 Neuromorphic engineering and the event-driven turn

Carver Mead's *neuromorphic engineering* proposed implementing neural dynamics directly in silicon physics, using subthreshold transistor currents as analogues of ionic currents. The communication protocol that emerged — the *address-event representation* (AER), transmitting each spike as a neuron address plus timestamp — exploits the defining property of spike traffic: *sparsity*.

Three platforms define the modern landscape. **SpiNNaker** discards the classical axioms of parallel design — memory coherence, global synchrony, determinism — routing 40-bit spike packets through a triangular mesh of ARM cores to simulate billion-neuron networks in real time [6]. **TrueNorth** integrates 4,096 neurosynaptic cores holding one million neurons and 256 million synapses, processing 400×240 video at 30 fps for 63 milliwatts [4]. **Loihi** adds the missing ingredient: a programmable microcode learning engine in each of its 128 neurocores, enabling on-chip plasticity with hierarchical connectivity, dendritic compartments, and synaptic delays [5].

### 2.3 Rate codes and their discontents

The classical interpretation of spike trains is the *rate code*: a neuron's firing rate, averaged over a window of tens to hundreds of milliseconds, encodes stimulus intensity. Rate codes are robust and map cleanly onto the graded activations of second-generation networks — many early "spiking" deep networks were simply rate-coded conversions of trained ANNs. But the rate hypothesis carries a hidden tax: averaging over time *is* latency.

## 3 Methodology

The methodological core of this thesis is the formalization of *what* an SNN computes, *how* it is simulated, *what* its spikes mean, and *how* it learns.

### 3.1 The formal computational model

We adopt the standard discrete formulation. Neuron $j$ maintains a membrane potential $v_j(t)$.

$$ S_j(t) = \sum_f \delta(t - t_j^f), \qquad t_j^f = \{t \mid v_j(t^-) \geq \vartheta\} $$

Synaptic input is filtered through a postsynaptic kernel $\epsilon$ (typically exponential, time constant $\tau_s$):

$$ \tau_m \frac{dv_j}{dt} = -(v_j - v_{\mathrm{rest}}) + R\, I_j(t), \qquad I_j(t) = \sum_i w_{ji} \sum_f \epsilon(t - t_i^f) + I^{\mathrm{ext}}_j(t) $$

with reset $v_j \leftarrow v_{\mathrm{reset}}$ upon spiking and an absolute refractory period $\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$ suspending integration.

### 3.2 Simulation paradigms

Two simulation strategies dominate:

1. **Clock-driven simulation.** Time advances in fixed steps $\Delta t$ (0.1–1 ms); every neuron updates every step: $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot T/\Delta t)$ regardless of activity. The paradigm of GPU frameworks and surrogate-gradient training [7], where differentiability demands a fixed computational graph.
2. **Event-driven simulation.** Computation occurs only at spike times; between events, membrane trajectories are predicted analytically. Complexity scales with the number of *spikes* — asymptotically optimal for sparse activity and the natural match for AER hardware [4][6] — but exact event prediction is tractable only for simple (typically linear) subthreshold dynamics.

### 3.3 A taxonomy of neural codes

| Code | Encoding | Latency | Spike cost | Robustness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Rate* | Spike count in window $T$ | $\sim T$ (slow) | High | High |
| *Time-to-first-spike* | Latency of first spike | $\sim$ ms (fast) | Minimal | Moderate |
| *Rank order* | Relative order of first spikes | Fast | Low | Moderate |
| *Phase* | Spike phase vs. background oscillation | Medium | Medium | High |
| *Population* | Distributed pattern across neurons | Medium | Medium | Very high |

Temporal codes buy speed and energy at the price of precision requirements — a trade-off quantified in §4.3 and §6.

### 3.4 The three families of learning

The spike-native formulations of the three learning paradigms:

- **Unsupervised local plasticity**, headed by STDP [1]: updates depend only on local pre/post spike timing — no global error signal, fully hardware-compatible.
- **Supervised surrogate-gradient learning** [7]: the spike nonlinearity is smoothed *only on the backward pass*, enabling BPTT in deep SNNs at the cost of a biased gradient.
- **Three-factor learning**, culminating in e-prop [8]: a global learning signal multiplies locally maintained *eligibility traces*, yielding online recurrent learning approaching BPTT without backward passes through time.

The following Python reference implementation of a discrete-time LIF layer — the workhorse of §4 — concretizes the formalism:

```python
import numpy as np

class LIFLayer:
    """Discrete-time leaky integrate-and-fire layer (Euler, step dt)."""
    def __init__(self, n, tau_m=20.0, v_th=1.0, v_reset=0.0,
                 v_rest=0.0, tau_ref=2.0, dt=1.0):
        self.n = n
        self.decay = np.exp(-dt / tau_m)
        self.v_th, self.v_reset, self.v_rest = v_th, v_reset, v_rest
        self.ref_steps = int(tau_ref / dt)
        self.v = np.full(n, v_rest)
        self.ref = np.zeros(n, dtype=int)

    def step(self, current):
        spiking = np.zeros(self.n, dtype=bool)
        active = self.ref == 0
        self.v[active] = (self.v_rest + (self.v[active] - self.v_rest)
                          * self.decay + current[active])
        fired = active & (self.v >= self.v_th)
        self.v[fired] = self.v_reset
        self.ref[fired] = self.ref_steps
        spiking[fired] = True
        self.ref[self.ref > 0] -= 1
        return spiking
```

> **Theorem (informal; after Maass [2]).** Networks of spiking neurons with programmable synaptic delays and thresholds are computationally universal, and with temporal coding a single spiking neuron can realize functions that require substantially larger networks of sigmoidal gates. The spike's discontinuity is therefore not a defect to be smoothed away but the source of the model's expressive power — provided learning can be made to work through it.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Neuron: A Canonical Dynamical Primitive

The LIF neuron endures as the field's workhorse because it is the *minimal* model retaining the three properties all spike-based computation needs: **integration** (inputs summate over time), **leak** (finite memory timescale $\tau_m$), and **threshold-reset** (all-or-none spike plus refractoriness).

The leak is the neuron's *clock*: with $\tau_m \approx 10$–$20$ ms, inputs arriving within milliseconds summate (coincidence detection) while older inputs are forgotten — the neuron weights its inputs *by recency*.

Canonical parameters [3]:

| Parameter | Symbol | Typical value | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Membrane time constant | $\tau_m$ | 10–20 ms | Integration / memory timescale |
| Firing threshold | $\vartheta$ | −50 mV (≈1 in normalized units) | Spike trigger |
| Reset potential | $v_{\mathrm{reset}}$ | −65 mV (≈0 normalized) | Post-spike state |
| Resting potential | $v_{\mathrm{rest}}$ | −70 mV | Leak target |
| Absolute refractory period | $\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$ | 1–2 ms | Maximum rate cap |

The refractory period bounds the firing rate at $1/\tau_{\mathrm{ref}} \approx 500$–$1000$ Hz and regularizes the dynamics; on chips like TrueNorth [4], where each core updates 256 neurons on discrete 1 ms timesteps, refractoriness keeps the fixed-point dynamics well-posed.

A far-reaching consequence of the LIF formulation is *sparsity by construction*: a neuron receiving weak input stays silent, consuming essentially no dynamic power on event-driven hardware — unlike a second-generation unit, which evaluates a dense multiply-accumulate every cycle regardless of input.

### 4.2 Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity: Hebb's Rule with a Clock

Hebb's postulate — *cells that fire together, wire together* — says nothing about order, but biology cares deeply about it. Markram and colleagues showed in 1997 that the *sequence* of pre- and postsynaptic spikes bidirectionally controls strength: pre-before-post potentiates, post-before-pre depresses.

Their result is the canonical STDP *learning window*, reproduced in preparations from insects to mammals ever since [1]:

- **Causal pairing** (pre precedes post by $\Delta t \in (0, \sim 20]$ ms) induces **long-term potentiation** (LTP), decaying with $\Delta t$.
- **Acausal pairing** ($\Delta t \in [-\!40, 0)$ ms) induces **long-term depression** (LTD).
- The coincidence window spans ~**40 ms**, with a strikingly sharp (~1 ms) LTP/LTD transition at zero lag [1].
- The effect is *input-specific* and depends on initial strength and cell type.

The standard phenomenological model, used in thousands of computational studies, writes the weight change for a spike pair with timing difference $\Delta t = t_{\mathrm{post}} - t_{\mathrm{pre}}$ as

$$ \Delta w = \begin{cases} A_+ \exp(-\Delta t / \tau_+) & \Delta t > 0 \quad \text{(LTP)} \\ -A_- \exp(\Delta t / \tau_-) & \Delta t < 0 \quad \text{(LTD)} \end{cases} $$

with $\tau_\pm \approx 20$ ms.

> **Proposition (competition through STDP).** In a neuron receiving correlated and uncorrelated inputs under pair-based STDP with balanced LTP/LTD windows, synapses carrying inputs that *predict* postsynaptic firing are potentiated while non-predictive synapses depress, producing receptive-field selectivity without any supervisory signal. The LTD window's typically larger integral acts as a homeostatic counterweight, preventing runaway potentiation.

Why a *timing*-dependent rule? Causality: pre-before-post ordering is statistical evidence that the presynaptic neuron *contributed to* the postsynaptic spike.

For neuromorphic engineering, STDP's locality is its supreme virtue: Loihi's per-core learning engine implements programmable plasticity of exactly this local form, updating weights from spike-timing traces without global communication [5].

### 4.3 Temporal Coding and the Expressive Power of Spikes

If STDP is the *learning* theory of SNNs, temporal coding is their *representational* theory.

1. **Universality.** Networks of spiking neurons with adjustable delays and thresholds simulate arbitrary Turing machines; no computational power is sacrificed by abandoning graded activations.
2. **Succinctness with temporal coding.** Functions computable by a *single* spiking neuron — exploiting the real-valued information in spike *times* — can require *hundreds* of sigmoidal hidden units. Where second-generation networks allocate their continuous degree of freedom to activation *values*, spiking networks allocate it to activation *times*, the richer resource.
3. **Noisy computation.** The framework extends to stochastic spiking — the biologically relevant regime of unreliable components [2].

The biological evidence is equally sharp. Thorpe, Fize, and Marlot [9] recorded human event-related potentials during ultra-rapid visual categorization and found reliable differential responses within **150 ms** of stimulus onset — allowing each neuron in the ventral-stream cascade roughly *one spike*. There is no time for rate averaging: the system must read *which neurons fired first*.

The engineering corollary: **latency codes are fast and sparse** — a time-to-first-spike network decides on the first spike wave, each neuron firing at most once. The costs are precise timing requirements, calibrated delays, and *training algorithms that assign credit through time* (§4.4).

### 4.4 Surrogate Gradients: Restoring Differentiability to the Spike

All the expressive power of §4.3 is moot without learning, and the obstacle is analytic: the spike function $S(v) = \Theta(v - \vartheta)$ has zero derivative everywhere except at threshold, where it is undefined. Backpropagating through it yields gradients that are exactly zero or ill-defined; early spike-time methods collapsed on silent neurons.

```python
import torch

class SurrogateSpike(torch.autograd.Function):
    """Binary spike forward; smooth surrogate derivative backward."""
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx, v, v_th=1.0, beta=10.0):
        ctx.save_for_backward(v)
        ctx.v_th, ctx.beta = v_th, beta
        return (v >= v_th).float()

    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx, grad_out):
        v, = ctx.saved_tensors
        # derivative of fast sigmoid, peaked at threshold
        grad_v = grad_out / (1.0 + ctx.beta * (v - ctx.v_th).abs()) ** 2
        return grad_v, None, None
```

The surrogate derivative is *peaked at threshold*: gradient flows to neurons near firing. Combined with BPTT over unrolled discrete-time dynamics (§3.2), this yields end-to-end training of deep SNNs approaching ANN accuracy on event-based benchmarks [7].

Surrogate gradients reinterpret elegantly as **three-factor rules**: surrogate derivative as postsynaptic gate, presynaptic spikes as second factor, backpropagated error as modulatory third factor — the bridge to biological plausibility, and to e-prop.

Bellec and colleagues' *eligibility propagation* (e-prop) [8] addresses the *learning dilemma* of recurrent SNNs: BPTT performs best but requires storing all states and propagating errors backward through time — biologically implausible and incompatible with online, on-chip learning.

$$ \frac{dE}{d\theta_{ji}} \;\approx\; \sum_t \underbrace{L_j^t}_{\text{learning signal}} \cdot \underbrace{e_{ji}^t}_{\text{eligibility trace}} $$

where the eligibility trace $e_{ji}^t$ is maintained locally at each synapse and the learning signal $L_j^t$ broadcasts error without traveling backward through recurrent connections. The result is **online** recurrent SNN learning approaching BPTT — demonstrated on demanding temporal tasks including TIMIT — directly mappable onto neuromorphic hardware [5][8].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: universality and succinctness

Maass's universality argument [2] proceeds by construction: threshold-tuned spiking neurons implement coincidence detectors; chains with tuned delays simulate Boolean gates with temporal wire encodings; a finite network thus simulates any Boolean circuit, hence any Turing machine.

### 5.2 Learning works: from STDP self-organization to surrogate benchmarks

The empirical record spans all three learning families. Unsupervised STDP in LIF networks develops Gabor-like receptive fields, direction selectivity, and stable assemblies — with no labels and no global signal [1]. Surrogate-gradient training has carried deep SNNs to competitive accuracy on neuromorphic benchmarks (N-MNIST, DVS-Gestures, Spiking Heidelberg Digits) while retaining order-of-magnitude spike-sparsity advantages [7].

### 5.3 The hardware verdict

The silicon results substantiate the theory's efficiency claims with unusual clarity:

- **TrueNorth** [4]: 1M neurons, 256M synapses, 5.4B transistors in 28 nm; 46 GSOPS/W; multi-object detection on 400×240 video at 30 fps for **63 mW** (~20 mW/cm² vs. 50–100 W/cm² for conventional processors). Architecturally an inference engine: synapses are non-plastic.
- **Loihi** [5]: 128 neurocores × 1,024 compartments, asynchronous NoC, 1–9 bit synapses, and the microcode learning engine TrueNorth lacks. Its headline result is algorithmic: a spiking convolutional Locally Competitive Algorithm solving LASSO with **over three orders of magnitude better energy-delay product** than CPU solvers — spike-based computation unambiguously outperforming conventional approaches.
- **SpiNNaker** [6]: the million-core machine simulating spiking networks in biological real time — no memory coherence, no global synchrony, no determinism.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Biased gradients.** Surrogate derivatives are not the derivatives of the forward computation; the resulting "gradient" is heuristic, and the theory of *when* surrogates work remains incomplete [7].
2. **Dead neurons.** A neuron that never spikes is invisible to learning under most surrogates, making initialization, threshold adaptation, and regularization load-bearing.
3. **Temporal credit assignment.** E-prop's factorization is approximate; over multi-second dependencies both truncated BPTT and eligibility traces degrade [8].
4. **Hardware constraints.** Loihi's 1–9 bit weights [5] quantize learning dynamics; TrueNorth's fixed connectivity forbids on-chip learning [4]; analog designs add device mismatch the theory assumes away.
5. **The benchmark gap.** On static datasets SNNs still trail ANNs, and rate conversion often erases the sparsity advantage. SNNs shine on *event-native* data — but the world has far more frames than events.
6. **Simulation cost.** Clock-driven training multiplies ANN cost by the timestep count ($T \sim 10$–$100$); event-driven simulation resists GPU acceleration.
7. **STDP is not enough.** Pair-based STDP cannot train deep hierarchies on supervised objectives; it is a powerful unsupervised prior, not a general learning algorithm [1].

---

## 7 Conclusion

This thesis has developed a unified account of spiking networks across four levels: the *dynamical* (LIF as temporal integrator [3]), the *plastic* (STDP as causal Hebbian learning [1]), the *representational* (temporal coding as the third generation's expressive power [2][9]), and the *algorithmic* (surrogate gradients [7] and e-prop [8] resolving the learning dilemma). The neuromorphic substrates — TrueNorth [4], Loihi [5], SpiNNaker [6] — show the theory cashing out in silicon: event-driven spike computation achieves efficiencies conventional architectures cannot approach, by exploiting the sparsity the theory predicts.

The deepest lesson is a reframing. Second-generation networks treat *time* as an inconvenience — sequences unrolled, dynamics discretized, learning batched over static snapshots. Spiking networks treat time as the *primary computational resource*: information lives in when spikes occur, learning in their relative timing, hardware in event sparsity. Maass's third generation [2] was prophetic: each generation moves computation from the *value* domain toward the *temporal* domain, toward machines that compute as nervous systems do — continuously, sparsely, online, in time.

The road ahead: credit assignment over behavioral timescales without backward passes [8]; hardware marrying Loihi's programmability [5] with TrueNorth's scale [4]; event-native benchmarks rewarding temporal computation; and a matured surrogate-learning theory replacing heuristic success with guarantees [7]. The spike, once a nuisance to be averaged away, is increasingly the atom of efficient intelligence.
