# Nonequilibrium Work Relations and the Thermodynamics of Information: The Jarzynski Equality, Crooks Fluctuation Theorem, and Landauer's Bound

## Abstract

Classical thermodynamics assigns free energy differences an operational meaning only through reversible processes, yet stochastic thermodynamics has shown that exact nonequilibrium equalities recover equilibrium information from arbitrarily fast driving. This article develops the mathematical core of that program. We state the Jarzynski equality, which expresses the equilibrium free energy difference between two states as the exponential average of the work over any switching protocol, and sketch its derivation from microscopic reversibility. We prove the Crooks fluctuation theorem, which constrains the ratio of forward to reverse work distributions and yields both the Jarzynski equality and the second law as corollaries. We formulate stochastic entropy production as the log-ratio of conjugate path probabilities, recovering ensemble irreversibility from trajectory-level symmetry. Finally, we show that Landauer's bound of k_B T ln 2 on bit erasure is a special case of the same structure, and we survey experimental confirmations from single-molecule pulling to colloidal feedback traps and quantum many-body systems. We close with the principal limits of the framework: estimator bias, coarse-graining, and the breakdown of microscopic reversibility.

---

## 1 Intro

The second law of thermodynamics is a statement about averages. It declares that the mean work required to drive a macroscopic system between two equilibrium states cannot fall below the free energy difference, with equality only in the reversible limit. For macroscopic bodies this inequality is effectively absolute, because relative fluctuations scale away with system size. At the mesoscale — single biomolecules, colloidal particles, molecular motors — fluctuations are comparable to mean values, and individual realizations routinely *violate* the macroscopic inequality even though the ensemble average respects it.

The 1990s produced a conceptual revolution in how these violations are understood. The fluctuation theorem of Evans, Cohen, and Morriss, and its steady-state form by Gallavotti and Cohen, showed that the *probability* of entropy-consuming trajectories is exactly constrained, not merely small. Jarzynski then discovered in 1997 that an exponential average of the nonequilibrium work over an arbitrary driving protocol equals the equilibrium free energy factor, with no near-equilibrium approximation [1]. Crooks strengthened this result in 1999 into a symmetry relating entire forward and reverse work distributions, from which the Jarzynski equality follows by integration [2]. Seifert subsequently unified these relations into stochastic thermodynamics, a trajectory-level theory in which entropy itself becomes a fluctuating quantity [3].

This article develops these results as one coherent mathematical structure. Our aims are to state the equalities precisely, to exhibit the change-of-measure argument from which they all flow, to connect them to Landauer's principle of information erasure, and to assess where the framework breaks down. The payoff is both practical — free energies from fast, irreversible computer simulations and single-molecule experiments — and foundational: irreversibility emerges from exact symmetries of reversible microscopic dynamics.

## 2 Background

Consider a classical system with microstate $x$ coupled to a heat bath at inverse temperature $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$. An external agent controls a parameter $\lambda$ appearing in the Hamiltonian $H(x; \lambda)$, driving it along a protocol $\lambda(t)$ from $\lambda_A$ at time $0$ to $\lambda_B$ at time $\tau$. The system begins in the equilibrium distribution at $\lambda_A$.

The equilibrium Helmholtz free energy is $F(\lambda) = -\beta^{-1} \ln Z(\lambda)$ with partition function $Z(\lambda) = \int dx\, e^{-\beta H(x;\lambda)}$. The free energy difference $\Delta F = F(\lambda_B) - F(\lambda_A)$ is the central equilibrium quantity we wish to recover from nonequilibrium data.

Following Sekimoto's stochastic energetics, work and heat are defined *along individual trajectories*. For a trajectory $x(t)$:

- **Work** is energy change caused by the external parameter: $W[x] = \int_0^\tau (\partial H/\partial\lambda)\, d\lambda$. Only the agent's intervention counts.
- **Heat** is energy change caused by the system's own motion: $Q[x] = \int_0^\tau (\partial H/\partial x)\, dx$, with the sign convention that positive $Q$ is heat absorbed *by* the system.
- The **first law** holds trajectory by trajectory: $W + Q = \Delta U$, where $U = H$ is the internal energy.

The dynamics are assumed **microscopically reversible**: the probability of a trajectory and that of its time reversal, under the conjugate protocol, satisfy a local detailed balance relation. For overdamped Langevin dynamics this is exact, and for Hamiltonian dynamics coupled to a bath it holds in the appropriate limit. This reversibility of the *dynamics* is what makes exact statements about *irreversible* processes possible.

The work $W$ fluctuates from realization to realization with distribution $P(W)$. Its mean obeys $\langle W \rangle \geq \Delta F$, the familiar second law, with strict inequality for any finite-time protocol. The fluctuation theorems refine this inequality into equalities.

## 3 Methodology

Our analytical workhorse is the **path probability measure**. Let $\mathcal{P}_F[x(\cdot)]$ be the probability density of a trajectory under the forward protocol, and $\mathcal{P}_R[\tilde{x}(\cdot)]$ the density of the time-reversed trajectory $\tilde{x}(t) = x(\tau - t)$ under the reversed protocol $\tilde{\lambda}(t) = \lambda(\tau - t)$, initialized in equilibrium at $\lambda_B$. Microscopic reversibility implies the fundamental identity

$$\frac{\mathcal{P}_F[x(\cdot)]}{\mathcal{P}_R[\tilde{x}(\cdot)]} = e^{\beta (W[x] - \Delta F)} = e^{\sigma[x]},$$

where $\sigma$ is the total entropy production defined below. Every result in this article is a corollary of this single change-of-measure formula.

Numerically, we study the overdamped Langevin equation

$$dx_t = -\frac{1}{\gamma}\nabla U(x_t; \lambda_t)\, dt + \sqrt{2D}\, dB_t,$$

with Einstein relation $D = k_B T / \gamma$, integrated by the Euler–Maruyama scheme. Work is accumulated as $dW = (\partial U/\partial\lambda)\, d\lambda$ along each trajectory. The following Python code simulates a harmonic trap dragged at constant velocity — a model with analytically known $\Delta F = 0$, ideal for validating estimators:

```python
import numpy as np

def simulate_dragged_trap(n_traj=20000, n_steps=2000,
                          k=1.0, gamma=1.0, beta=1.0, v=0.05, dt=0.001):
    D = 1.0 / (beta * gamma)
    W = np.empty(n_traj)
    for m in range(n_traj):
        x = np.random.normal(0.0, 1.0 / np.sqrt(beta * k))
        lam, lam_old, w = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0
        for _ in range(n_steps):
            lam_old = lam
            lam = lam + v * dt
            # dW = (dU/dlambda) dlambda with U = k(x - lambda)^2 / 2
            w = w - k * (x - lam_old) * (lam - lam_old)
            x = x - (k * (x - lam) / gamma) * dt \
                + np.sqrt(2.0 * D * dt) * np.random.normal()
        W[m] = w
    return W

def jarzynski_estimate(W, beta=1.0):
    """Free energy estimate from the exponential work average."""
    return -(1.0 / beta) * np.log(np.mean(np.exp(-beta * W)))
```

The exponential estimator is unbiased only asymptotically; in practice the Crooks crossing-point estimator of Section 4.2 converges far faster. A compact functional check of the fluctuation symmetry is:

```haskell
-- | Crooks symmetry: ratio of forward to reverse work densities.
crooksRatio :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
crooksRatio beta w deltaF = exp (beta * (w - deltaF))

-- | Integral fluctuation theorem: mean of exp(-sigma) over sampled
-- entropy productions should equal unity.
integralFT :: [Double] -> Double
integralFT sigmas =
  sum (map (exp . negate) sigmas) / fromIntegral (length sigmas)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Jarzynski equality

> **Theorem (Jarzynski, 1997).** For a system initially in equilibrium at $\lambda_A$ and driven to $\lambda_B$ by *any* protocol, under dynamics obeying microscopic reversibility,
>
> $$\big\langle e^{-\beta W} \big\rangle_F = e^{-\beta \Delta F},$$
>
> where the average is over forward trajectories [1].

*Proof sketch.* Discretize the protocol into short intervals. On each interval the system evolves under fixed $\lambda$, then $\lambda$ jumps. Writing the exponential work average as a path integral and inserting the equilibrium weight $e^{-\beta H}/Z$ at every time slice, the transition probabilities telescope against the Boltzmann factors by detailed balance. All intermediate partition functions cancel, leaving $Z(\lambda_B)/Z(\lambda_A) = e^{-\beta\Delta F}$. The Hamiltonian derivation is due to Jarzynski; the stochastic version follows the same pattern with Markov transition kernels.

Two consequences are immediate. First, Jensen's inequality gives $\langle W \rangle \geq \Delta F$: the second law is a corollary, not an axiom. Second, because $e^{-\beta W}$ is convex in $W$, the average is dominated by *rare* trajectories with $W < \Delta F$ — transient "violations" of the macroscopic second law that occur with probability suppressed as $e^{-\beta(\Delta F - W)}$. These rare events are not curiosities; they carry essentially all the statistical weight of the estimator, which is why naive finite-sample estimates are systematically biased upward, with bias growing roughly like the dissipated work $\langle W \rangle - \Delta F$.

### 4.2 The Crooks fluctuation theorem

> **Theorem (Crooks, 1999).** Let $P_F(W)$ be the work distribution under the forward protocol and $P_R(W)$ under the time-reversed protocol. Then
>
> $$\frac{P_F(W)}{P_R(-W)} = e^{\beta(W - \Delta F)},$$
>
> for all $W$ where both densities are nonzero [2].

*Derivation.* Group trajectories by their work value: $P_F(W) = \int \mathcal{D}[x]\, \mathcal{P}_/F[x]\, \delta(W - W[x])$. Under time reversal $W[\tilde{x}] = -W[x]$, and the change-of-measure identity converts $\mathcal{P}_F$ to $\mathcal{P}_R$ at the cost of exactly the factor $e^{\beta(W - \Delta F)}$.

The theorem's power lies in its symmetry. The two distributions cross precisely at $W = \Delta F$, where $P_F(\Delta F) = P_R(-\Delta F)$ — a *direct graphical readout* of the free energy requiring no exponential average. Integrating the Crooks relation against $P_R(-W)$ recovers the Jarzynski equality, so Crooks strictly implies Jarzynski. The relation also yields the optimal Bennett acceptance ratio estimator, which combines forward and reverse samples with minimum variance and is the workhorse of modern free energy computation [5].

### 4.3 Entropy production and stochastic thermodynamics

Seifert's stochastic thermodynamics elevates entropy itself to a trajectory quantity [3]. Define the **stochastic system entropy** $s(t) = -\ln p(x_t, t)$, where $p$ is the instantaneous ensemble density. Its change $\Delta s$ plus the **medium entropy change** $\Delta s_m = -Q/T$ (with $Q$ the heat absorbed by the system, so $-Q$ is dissipated) gives the **total entropy production**

$$\sigma[x] = \Delta s + \Delta s_m = \ln\frac{\mathcal{P}_F[x(\cdot)]}{\mathcal{P}_R[\tilde{x}(\cdot)]}.$$

The second equality is the deep one: entropy production *is* the log-likelihood ratio distinguishing forward from reversed dynamics. Its mean is a Kullback–Leibler divergence, $\langle \sigma \rangle = D_{KL}(\mathcal{P}_F \Vert \mathcal{P}_R) \geq 0$, which is the second law in information-theoretic dress. The integral fluctuation theorem $\langle e^{-\sigma} \rangle = 1$ follows by the same change of measure, and for steady states or time-symmetric protocols the detailed form $P(\sigma)/P(-\sigma) = e^{\sigma}$ holds — the Evans–Searles and Gallavotti–Cohen relations.

The family of results is summarized below:

| Relation | Fluctuating quantity | Statement | Regime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jarzynski equality | work $W$ | $\langle e^{-\beta W} \rangle = e^{-\beta\Delta F}$ | driven, initial equilibrium |
| Crooks fluctuation theorem | work $W$ | $P_F(W)/P_R(-W) = e^{\beta(W-\Delta F)}$ | conjugate protocols |
| Integral entropy FT | total entropy production $\sigma$ | $\langle e^{-\sigma} \rangle = 1$ | driven or steady state |
| Detailed entropy FT | $\sigma$ | $P(\sigma)/P(-\sigma) = e^{\sigma}$ | steady state / symmetric protocol |
| Hatano–Sasa relation | excess heat $Q_{ex}$ | $\langle e^{-\beta Q_{ex}} \rangle = 1$ | transitions between nonequilibrium steady states |

For transitions between nonequilibrium steady states, the total entropy production splits into housekeeping and excess parts, and the Hatano–Sasa relation constrains the excess heat alone — extending the framework beyond the equilibrium endpoints Jarzynski requires.

### 4.4 Landauer's principle and information thermodynamics

Landauer's principle (1961) states that erasing one bit of information — mapping two logical states deterministically to one — dissipates at least $k_B T \ln 2$ of heat. In the stochastic-thermodynamic language this is nearly a tautology with teeth. Model the bit as a particle in a symmetric double well, each well a logical state. Erasure merges the two wells into one; the Shannon entropy of the logical state drops by $\ln 2$ nats, so the system entropy change is $\Delta s = -\ln 2$ and the second law $\langle \sigma \rangle = \langle \Delta s \rangle + \langle Q_{diss} \rangle/(k_B T) \geq 0$ gives $\langle Q_{diss} \rangle \geq k_B T \ln 2$. Logical irreversibility (many-to-one mapping) forces thermodynamic cost; the bound is saturated only in the quasistatic limit.

The framework extends naturally to *feedback*. Sagawa and Ueda showed that when measurements with mutual information $I$ guide the protocol, the generalized relation $\langle e^{-\sigma - I} \rangle = 1$ holds, permitting $\langle \sigma \rangle \geq -\langle I \rangle$: information can be converted into extracted work, up to the information gained. This resolves Maxwell's demon quantitatively — the demon's memory must eventually be erased, and Landauer's bound collects the debt [9].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The equalities above are mathematical theorems under stated assumptions; their empirical content lies in how accurately real mesoscopic systems obey them. Three decades of experiments confirm the framework across scales:

| Year | System | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 2002 | RNA hairpin pulled by optical tweezers | Jarzynski equality recovers the folding free energy from strongly irreversible pulls [4] |
| 2005 | RNA hairpin, unfolding/refolding | Crooks relation verified; forward and reverse work histograms cross at $\Delta F$ [5] |
| 2010 | Colloidal particle driven along a spiral staircase | information-to-energy conversion; generalized Jarzynski equality with feedback confirmed [9] |
| 2012 | Colloidal particle in a modulated double well | mean dissipated heat approaches $k_B T \ln 2$ in the slow-erasure limit [6] |
| 2014 | Colloidal particle in a feedback trap | high-precision Landauer test; individual cycles can cost less than the bound, consistent with the Jarzynski equality [7] |
| 2025 | Ultracold atomic gas in the quantum many-body regime | Landauer's principle probed beyond single-particle systems [10] |

Several features deserve emphasis. First, the 2002 and 2005 single-molecule experiments demonstrated the *practical* payoff: equilibrium free energies extracted from pulling speeds far too fast for reversibility, validating steered molecular dynamics as a computational method. Second, the feedback-trap experiments confirmed the fluctuation-level subtlety that individual erasure cycles may dissipate less than $k_B T \ln 2$ — the bound constrains the *mean*, while the Jarzynski equality governs the full distribution. Third, the quantum many-body extension shows the principle surviving where the bath can no longer be treated as a structureless reservoir. The quantum fluctuation relations themselves, including the two-point measurement definition of quantum work, are reviewed in [8].

Numerically, the dragged-trap model of Section 3 reproduces all of this in silico: the Jarzynski estimator converges to the analytic $\Delta F = 0$ as trajectory count grows, the forward and reverse work histograms (for the reversed dragging protocol) cross at zero, and the empirical mean of $e^{-\sigma}$ approaches unity — a complete laboratory of the theory in a few dozen lines of code.

## 6 Limitations

The framework's exactness is conditional, and its limits are as instructive as its successes.

**Statistical convergence.** The Jarzynski estimator is dominated by exponentially rare trajectories. Its bias decays only as the inverse of the sample size with a prefactor that grows like $e^{\beta W_{diss}}$, where $W_{diss} = \langle W \rangle - \Delta F$ is the dissipated work. For strongly driven processes the estimator is practically useless without reverse-protocol data and the Bennett acceptance ratio. This is not a flaw in the theorem but a fact about the geometry of exponential averages.

**Microscopic reversibility.** All derivations assume the dynamics obey detailed balance at the trajectory level. Systems with odd-parity variables under time reversal (e.g., momenta in magnetic fields), non-Markovian baths with memory, or active-matter self-propulsion violate the standard assumptions and require modified relations. *Absolute irreversibility* — processes like free expansion where some reverse trajectories have strictly zero forward probability — introduces correction terms from singular path-measure components.

**Coarse-graining.** The entropy production computed from observed degrees of freedom is a *lower bound* on the true total; hidden slow variables and finite measurement resolution systematically underestimate dissipation. Apparent violations of fluctuation theorems in experiment are most often coarse-graining artifacts.

**Quantum regime.** Work is not a quantum observable, so the two-point projective measurement scheme used to define quantum work distributions destroys the very coherences whose thermodynamics one might wish to study. Fully coherent formulations of quantum fluctuation theorems remain an active research area [8].

**Interpretive debates.** The precise scope of Landauer's principle — whether it bounds erasure of *known* versus *unknown* data, and how it applies to asymmetric wells — continues to generate controversy, though the stochastic-thermodynamic derivation above pins down the standard case unambiguously.

---

## 7 Conclusion

A single mathematical object — the ratio of conjugate path probability measures — unifies the Jarzynski equality, the Crooks fluctuation theorem, the entropy production fluctuation theorems, and Landauer's bound on information erasure. Irreversibility, in this view, is not an extra postulate layered onto reversible dynamics but a measurable asymmetry *between* forward and reversed trajectory ensembles, quantified exactly by fluctuation symmetries valid arbitrarily far from equilibrium.

The practical legacy is secure: free energies from irreversible simulations and single-molecule experiments are now routine. The frontier has moved to thermodynamic uncertainty relations bounding the precision of molecular machines, thermodynamic speed limits constraining how fast distributions can be transformed, and machine-learning estimators — including annealed importance sampling, whose analysis runs directly through the Jarzynski equality — that turn the rare-event problem into a computational advantage. The deepest lesson endures: information is physical, erasure has a price, and the price list is written in the statistics of trajectories.
