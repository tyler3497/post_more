# Quantum Complexity Theory: BQP, QMA, Oracle Separations, and the Quantum PCP Conjecture

## Abstract

This thesis surveys the foundational complexity classes of quantum computation — *bounded-error quantum polynomial time* (BQP) and *quantum Merlin–Arthur* (QMA) — and the landmark results that position them relative to classical complexity theory. Beginning with the Bernstein–Vazirani formalization of quantum Turing machines and the uniform quantum circuit model, we develop the definitions of BQP and QMA and prove the principal containment relations BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE and BQP ⊆ QMA ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE, together with amplification arguments. We then present Kitaev's quantum Cook–Levin theorem establishing that the *local Hamiltonian problem* is QMA-complete, and examine the celebrated *Raz–Tal oracle separation* of BQP from the polynomial hierarchy via the Forrelation problem, the first relativized evidence that BQP ⊄ PH. Finally, we discuss the *quantum PCP conjecture* and its combinatorial core — the NLTS (No Low-energy Trivial States) conjecture of Freedman–Hastings — and the 2022 proof by Anshu, Breuckmann, and Nirkhe that NLTS Hamiltonians exist. The survey closes with limitations of relativization, the barrier of non-relativizing techniques, and open questions that remain the grand challenges of the field.

## 1 Introduction

Computational complexity theory asks which problems can be solved efficiently and, crucially, which cannot — where "efficient" is quantified through carefully defined *complexity classes* [1]. The discovery of quantum computing in the 1980s and 1990s forced a re-examination of this taxonomy: the physical model of computation itself changed, and with it, the frontier of efficient computation. *Quantum complexity theory* is the study of complexity classes defined using quantum computers and quantum information, with two classes at its center: **BQP**, the bounded-error quantum analogue of P, and **QMA**, the bounded-error quantum analogue of NP [2].

The stakes of this classification are immense. Shor's polynomial-time factoring algorithm places integer factorization in BQP, threatening the cryptographic assumptions of the modern internet; Grover's search gives a quadratic speedup for unstructured search that is provably optimal. Yet we cannot prove unconditionally that BQP is strictly more powerful than BPP, let alone P — separating BQP from P would imply separating P from PSPACE, far beyond current technique. What we *can* do, and what this thesis presents, is develop a rich structural theory: definitions with amplification theorems, containment hierarchies, *oracle separations* that relativize our ignorance into provable distinctions, complete problems for QMA, and the *quantum PCP conjecture*, arguably the deepest open problem in quantum complexity theory.

The thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 defines the quantum circuit model, BQP, and QMA, and proves the classical containments. Section 3 describes the proof techniques: amplification via Chernoff bounds, the SWAP test and phase estimation as verifiers, and diagonalization with oracles. Section 4 develops four deep topics: the uniform circuit model and error reduction, Kitaev's quantum Cook–Levin theorem, the Raz–Tal separation of BQP from PH via Forrelation, and the quantum PCP/NLTS frontier. Section 5 presents proof sketches of the main theorems. Section 6 discusses limitations — barriers of relativization and the dependence on unproven circuit lower bounds. Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2 Background

### The quantum circuit model

A *quantum circuit* on n qubits applies a sequence of *unitary gates* — two-by-two or four-by-four unitary matrices, typically drawn from a finite universal gate set such as {H, T, CNOT} — to the initial state |0⟩^⊗n, and concludes with a measurement of a designated output qubit in the computational basis. A *uniform family* {Q_n} of quantum circuits is one generated by a polynomial-time classical Turing machine that, on input n, outputs a description of Q_n with polynomially many gates [1]. Uniformity is essential: without it, the circuit description could encode arbitrary, even uncomputable, advice into each input length.

### BQP: bounded-error quantum polynomial time

> **Definition (BQP):** A language L ⊆ {0,1}* is in **BQP** if there exists a polynomial-time uniform family of quantum circuits {Q_n} such that for every x: if x ∈ L then Pr[Q_{|x|}(x) accepts] ≥ 2/3, and if x ∉ L then Pr[Q_{|x|}(x) accepts] ≤ 1/3.

The constants 2/3 and 1/3 are arbitrary: by running the circuit O(log(1/ε)) times and taking a majority vote, the error can be driven down to ε = 2^{-poly(n)} via the Chernoff bound [1]. This *amplification property* distinguishes BQP from the class PP, where the acceptance gap can be exponentially small and amplification is not obviously possible. Straightforward simulation arguments yield the containments:

| Containment | Reason |
|---|---|
| **BPP ⊆ BQP** | A classical coin flip is simulable by a Hadamard gate; reversible simulation of deterministic circuits embeds P ⊆ BQP [1] |
| **BQP ⊆ PP** | Accept iff the weighted sum of accepting paths exceeds threshold; Aaronson showed the tighter BQP ⊆ PP via PostBQP [8] |
| **BQP ⊆ PSPACE** | Simulate amplitudes depth-first; each amplitude is computable in polynomial space [1] |

Thus P ⊆ BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE — a chain in which *every* inclusion is conjectured to be strict, yet none is proven.

### QMA: quantum Merlin–Arthur

QMA is the quantum analogue of NP (technically, of MA). A polynomial-time quantum verifier Arthur receives, in addition to the input x, a *quantum witness* |ψ⟩ of poly(|x|) qubits from the prover Merlin [3][9]:

> **Definition (QMA):** L ∈ **QMA** if there exists a polynomial-time quantum verifier V such that: for x ∈ L there exists a witness |ψ⟩ with Pr[V(x,|ψ⟩) accepts] ≥ 2/3 (*completeness*), and for x ∉ L, for *all* |ψ⟩, Pr[V(x,|ψ⟩) accepts] ≤ 1/3 (*soundness*).

Key structural facts: QMA admits *error amplification* (via Marriott–Watrous in-place amplification, avoiding the exponential blowup naive repetition would cause on the witness), and BQP ⊆ QMA ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE. The class **QCMA**, with a *classical* witness but quantum verifier, satisfies QCMA ⊆ QMA; whether equality holds is open.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows the standard toolkit of structural complexity theory, adapted to the quantum setting:

1. **Uniform circuit construction.** We define classes via polynomial-time generated circuit families, and prove containments by explicit simulation: classical randomness via Hadamard gates, quantum amplitudes via Feynman path sums in polynomial space, and so on [1][8].
2. **Amplification.** The Chernoff bound converts constant gap into exponentially small error for BQP; for QMA we use the Marriott–Watrous amplification which reuses the witness register [3].
3. **Oracle separation by diagonalization.** To separate classes A and B *relative to an oracle*, we construct an oracle O and a language L^O such that L^O ∈ A^O ∖ B^O, enumerating all machines of the weaker class and diagonalizing against them. The celebrated Raz–Tal result instead separates via a distributional lower bound against AC⁰ circuits [4][8].
4. **Completeness via local reduction.** Kitaev's quantum Cook–Levin theorem reduces an arbitrary QMA verification circuit to a *local Hamiltonian*, mapping computation histories to ground states of a 5-local (later 2-local) Hamiltonian [2][3].
5. **Combinatorial NLTS arguments.** The NLTS theorem uses good quantum LDPC codes: their stabilizer Hamiltonians are shown to have no low-energy states preparable by constant-depth circuits [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Uniform Quantum Circuits and the Power of BQP

The equivalence between quantum Turing machines and uniform quantum circuits is the quantum Church–Turing thesis in action [1]. Bernstein and Vazirani (1993; journal version 1997) showed that any well-formed quantum Turing machine running in time T can be simulated by a uniform circuit family of size poly(T), and conversely. This robustness is what makes BQP a *natural* class rather than a model-dependent artifact.

> **Theorem:** BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE, and each inclusion is believed strict.

The most striking early evidence that BQP might exceed BPP came from *oracle* results: Bernstein and Vazirani exhibited *Recursive Fourier Sampling*, solvable with n quantum queries but requiring n^{Ω(log n)} classical queries [1], and Simon's problem gives an exponential oracle separation between BPP and BQP. Yet in the *unrelativized* world, we cannot even rule out BQP = P. The subsequent sections explain why relativized and combinatorial evidence must substitute for unconditional proof.

```python
# Schematic: uniform quantum circuit family for a BQP language L
def Q_n(x: str) -> bool:
    """Polynomial-time Turing machine generates circuit description;
    circuit has poly(n) gates from a finite universal gate set."""
    assert len(x) == n
    qubits = initialize(n, state='0' * n)
    for gate in universal_circuit_description(n):  # poly(n) gates
        qubits = apply_unitary(gate, qubits)
    outcome = measure(qubits[0])          # accept iff outcome == 1
    return majority_vote([outcome] * k)   # amplification, k = O(log 1/eps)
```

### 4.2 QMA and Kitaev's Quantum Cook–Levin Theorem

In 1999, Alexei Kitaev defined QMA (independently formalized by Watrous [9]) and proved its first complete problem — the quantum analogue of the Cook–Levin theorem [2][3]. The *k-local Hamiltonian problem* takes as input a collection of Hermitian terms {H_i}, each acting nontrivially on at most k of n qubits, plus thresholds a < b with inverse-polynomial promise gap b − a > 1/poly(n), and asks whether the ground-state energy of H = Σ_i H_i is at most a or at least b. This is the quantum generalization of MAX-k-SAT: instead of maximizing satisfied classical clauses, we minimize energy over entangled quantum states.

> **Theorem (Kitaev, 1999):** The 5-local Hamiltonian problem is QMA-complete [2][3].

*Proof sketch.* Containment in QMA: Merlin sends the ground state |ψ⟩; Arthur measures a random term H_i and accepts with probability related to the energy — phase estimation makes this rigorous. Hardness: given a QMA verifier circuit V = U_T ⋯ U_1, Kitaev constructs the *history state*

|η⟩ = (T+1)^{-1/2} Σ_{t=0}^{T} |t⟩ ⊗ U_t ⋯ U_1 |x⟩|ψ⟩|0⟩,

a uniform superposition over time steps of the computation, and a Hamiltonian H = H_in + H_out + H_prop + H_clock whose ground state encodes valid computations. The propagation terms H_prop check that consecutive time slices are related by the correct unitary — the quantum analogue of the Cook–Levin tableau constraints. The construction was later improved to 2-local Hamiltonians (Kempe, Kitaev, Regev 2006) and even to 1D chains [5].

The physical significance is profound: estimating ground-state energies of local quantum systems — the central task of condensed-matter physics and quantum chemistry — is QMA-hard, hence intractable for quantum computers unless BQP = QMA [6].

### 4.3 The Raz–Tal Oracle Separation: BQP vs the Polynomial Hierarchy

For decades, a central embarrassment of quantum complexity theory was the question: *is BQP contained in the polynomial hierarchy PH?* Aaronson (2010) gave oracle evidence for relation problems [8], but the decision-problem case resisted all attacks. In 2018, Ran Raz and Avishay Tal achieved the breakthrough [4].

The *Forrelation* problem, introduced by Aaronson (2009), asks: given black-box access to two Boolean functions f, g: {0,1}^n → {±1}, decide whether g is *correlated* with the Fourier transform f̂ of f (the YES case) or f and g are independent random functions (the NO case). Formally, the *forrelation* of f and g is Φ_{f,g} = 2^{-3n/2} Σ_{x,y} (−1)^{x·y} f(x)g(y). A single quantum query — one application of the Deutsch–Jozsa-like circuit — estimates Φ_{f,g} well enough to distinguish the cases, so Forrelation ∈ BQP [4][8].

Raz and Tal proved that *no* PH machine can solve it, even relative to an oracle encoding f and g. The reduction: PH corresponds, via standard translations, to quasipolynomial-size constant-depth (AC⁰) circuits. They constructed a distribution D over {±1}^N — a truncated Gaussian — such that (i) a polylog(N)-time quantum algorithm distinguishes D from uniform with Ω(1/log N) advantage, while (ii) no AC⁰ circuit of quasipolynomial size distinguishes D from uniform with advantage better than polylog(N)/√N. The proof blends Fourier analysis of Boolean functions, the Linial–Mansour–Nisan theorem, and stochastic calculus.

> **Theorem (Raz–Tal, 2018):** There exists an oracle A such that BQP^A ⊄ PH^A [4].

This was the first formal evidence that quantum computers might transcend *the entire polynomial hierarchy* — a far stronger statement than BQP ⊄ NP. It does not prove BQP ⊄ PH unconditionally (oracle results cannot, by the Baker–Gill–Solovay phenomenon), but it redirects the question: any proof that BQP ⊆ PH would require *non-relativizing* techniques.

### 4.4 The Quantum PCP Conjecture and NLTS

The classical *PCP theorem* states that NP = PCP(O(log n), O(1)): every NP proof can be rewritten so that a verifier checks only O(1) bits yet catches errors with constant probability. The *quantum PCP conjecture* (formulated circa 2005) asserts a quantum analogue: QMA = QPCP, i.e., the ground-state energy of a local Hamiltonian remains QMA-hard to approximate even up to *constant* relative error, with the proof encoded so that local measurements suffice. Equivalently, there exist *quantum locally testable codes* and Hamiltonians whose low-energy states are all highly entangled.

A necessary combinatorial step is the *NLTS conjecture* of Freedman and Hastings (2014) [7]: there exist families of O(1)-local Hamiltonians on n qubits such that *every* state with energy below some constant εn requires a quantum circuit of depth Ω(log n) to prepare — *no low-energy trivial states*. This rules out the most obvious obstruction to quantum PCP (product-state approximability of ground states).

For eight years NLTS resisted proof. The breakthrough came in 2022: Anshu, Breuckmann, and Nirkhe proved NLTS using *good quantum LDPC codes* — families with constant rate and linear distance, constructed by Panteleev–Kalachev and by Leverrier–Zémor in 2020–2021 [5]. The stabilizer Hamiltonian H = Σ (I − S_i)/2 of such a code has the property that low-energy states must violate few checks, hence lie near the code space; but codewords of a good qLDPC code are *well-spread* (no low-weight logical operators can be localized), so any shallow circuit preparing a low-energy state would imply a shallow circuit preparing a codeword — contradicting expansion properties of the code's Tanner graph. A self-contained simplification using classical LTCs followed (He–Nirkhe 2022).

> **Theorem (Anshu–Breuckmann–Nirkhe, STOC 2023):** NLTS Hamiltonians exist [5].

Quantum PCP itself remains open: NLTS concerns *circuit complexity* of low-energy states, while quantum PCP demands *hardness of approximation* for the ground energy. The gap between them — and whether the detectability lemma and gap amplification of Aharonov, Arad, Landau, and Vazirani [6] can be pushed to constant gap — is the central open problem of the area.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

We consolidate the main proof sketches and their quantitative content:

1. **Amplification (BQP).** If Pr[accept] ≥ 1/2 + δ for YES and ≤ 1/2 − δ for NO with δ ≥ 1/poly(n), then k = O(δ^{-2} log(1/ε)) repetitions with majority vote drive error below ε. Standard BQP definitions take δ = 1/6, so O(log(1/ε)) repetitions suffice [1].
2. **BQP ⊆ PP (Aaronson).** Define PostBQP = BQP with postselection; then PostBQP = PP [8]. Since postselection only helps, BQP ⊆ PP. The simulation writes the acceptance probability as a difference of two #P functions.
3. **QMA ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE.** The maximal acceptance probability of a QMA verifier equals the largest eigenvalue of an exponentially large PSD matrix; eigenvalue thresholding reduces to PP [3]. Marriott–Watrous amplification keeps the witness size polynomial.
4. **Kitaev's reduction.** The history-state Hamiltonian uses O(T) clock qubits and O(T) propagation terms, each acting on O(1) qubits; the promise gap scales as Ω(1/T^3), amplified to Ω(1/T^2) by the detectability lemma [2][6].
5. **Raz–Tal distinguishing advantage.** Quantum: Ω(1/log N). AC⁰ of size exp(polylog N): O(polylog(N)/√N) — an exponential gap in distinguishing power, lifted to an oracle separation by standard diagonalization [4].
6. **NLTS parameters.** The Anshu–Breuckmann–Nirkhe Hamiltonians are O(1)-local with Θ(n) terms; every state of energy ≤ εn (constant ε > 0) requires circuit depth Ω(log n) [5].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Relativization barrier.** The Raz–Tal separation holds *relative to an oracle* and therefore cannot be converted into an unconditional proof that BQP ⊄ PH; oracles exist with BQP^A = P^A as well. Any unconditional separation requires non-relativizing techniques such as arithmetization [4][8].
2. **No unconditional separations among the big five.** We cannot prove P ≠ BQP, BQP ≠ QMA, or even BPP ≠ BQP. All containments in P ⊆ BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE are conjectured strict but unproven — the central embarrassment persists despite the structural progress above [1].
3. **Quantum PCP remains open.** NLTS proves low-energy states are *complex*, not that the ground energy is *hard to approximate*. The known gap-amplification machinery (detectability lemma [6]) degrades parameters, and no constant-gap QMA-hard family of local Hamiltonians is known.
4. **Witness and uniformity subtleties.** QMA's definition depends on polynomial-size witnesses; QMA with exponentially small promise gap (preciseQMA) jumps to PSPACE. The distinction between QCMA and QMA (classical vs quantum witnesses) is unresolved and affects the interpretation of quantum advantage in verification [9].
5. **Physical realizability gaps.** Complexity-theoretic results assume fault-tolerant quantum circuits with arbitrary poly(n) depth; near-term devices are noise-limited, so asymptotic separations like Raz–Tal's do not directly imply practical advantage.

## 7 Conclusion

Quantum complexity theory has matured from the 1993 Bernstein–Vazirani definitions into a deep structural science. We now possess: a robust definition of BQP with amplification and a chain of containments into classical classes [1][8]; QMA as the quantum analogue of NP, with Kitaev's local Hamiltonian theorem as its Cook–Levin cornerstone [2][3]; the Raz–Tal oracle separation, the first rigorous evidence that quantum computation may transcend the entire polynomial hierarchy [4]; and the NLTS theorem, resolving an eight-year conjecture on the road to quantum PCP [5][7]. Yet the field's grand challenges — unconditional separations, the quantum PCP conjecture, the QCMA vs QMA question — remain open, each requiring techniques beyond relativization. The interplay between quantum error correction (good qLDPC codes), Hamiltonian complexity, and proof verification that powered the NLTS breakthrough suggests the next decade's progress will come from exactly such cross-disciplinary synthesis.
