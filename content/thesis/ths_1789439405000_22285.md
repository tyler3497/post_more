# Berkovich Analytic Geometry: Non-Archimedean Spaces, Skeletons, and Tropical Retractions

## Abstract

Berkovich analytic geometry enriches classical rigid geometry by adjoining to every scheme of finite type over a complete non-Archimedean field a Hausdorff, locally compact topological space whose points are multiplicative seminorms extending the given absolute value. This survey develops the theory from the spectrum M(A) of a commutative Banach algebra through the description of the affine and projective Berkovich lines as infinite trees, the analytification functor, and the construction of skeletons of semistable models with their canonical retractions τ: X^an → Sk(X). We explain the weight metric and essential skeleton of Mustaţă–Nicaise, the Kontsevich–Soibelman skeleton arising from pluricanonical forms, and the identification of tropicalization with the image of Berkovich space under the valuation map. We treat non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère equations, the skeleton of an abelian variety as a real torus, and the non-Archimedean SYZ picture proposed by Kontsevich and Soibelman, which places skeletons at the center of mirror symmetry for degenerating Calabi–Yau families.

##1 Introduction

Let K be a field complete with respect to a nontrivial non-Archimedean absolute value |·|: K → R≥0, with valuation ring R = {x : |x| ≤ 1}, maximal ideal m = {x : |x| < 1}, residue field k = R/m, and value group Γ = |K×| ⊂ R>0. Classical rigid analytic geometry, in the Tate tradition, endows the maximal spectrum of a Tate algebra with a Grothendieck topology whose points are too few to support a good topological theory: the underlying topological space is totally disconnected, and many natural analytic constructions fail to globalize.

Berkovich's fundamental insight [1] was to *enlarge* the point set. Instead of maximal ideals, one takes multiplicative seminorms bounded by the Banach norm. The resulting space M(A) is compact Hausdorff, and affinoid domains glue to give honest topological spaces — locally compact, locally arcwise connected — carrying sheaves of analytic functions. The payoff is a theory in which analytic spaces behave topologically like complex analytic spaces, while retaining the arithmetic precision of valuations.

This enrichment is not decorative. Points of a Berkovich space record *how* a function degenerates: a point of the affine line is a disc, and the space of all discs is a tree. When X is an algebraic variety, its analytification X^an remembers the combinatorics of degenerations of X over the valuation ring. A semistable model X of X determines a finite polyhedral complex — the *skeleton* Sk(X) — embedded in X^an, and there is a canonical strong deformation retraction τ: X^an → Sk(X) [2][7]. The homotopy type of X^an is therefore controlled by the combinatorics of the special fiber.

Three revolutions grew from this observation:

1. **Tropical geometry.** The tropicalization of a subvariety of a torus is the image of its Berkovich analytification under the coordinatewise valuation map. Berkovich spaces explain *why* tropical varieties are balanced polyhedral complexes [3][8].
2. **Birational geometry of degenerations.** The essential skeleton of Mustaţă–Nicaise [5], defined via the weight metric attached to a pluricanonical form, is independent of the choice of model and is a strong deformation retract of X^an. For Calabi–Yau degenerations it is a pseudomanifold — the non-Archimedean shadow of the SYZ base.
3. **Non-Archimedean mirror symmetry.** Kontsevich and Soibelman [7] proposed that the base of the SYZ fibration is the skeleton of the Berkovich space, equipped with an integral affine structure with singularities, and that the mirror can be reconstructed from the combinatorics of the skeleton. The non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère equation of Boucksom–Jonsson supplies the metric side of this picture [6].

This article develops these themes with full technical precision, from the definition of M(A) to the SYZ conjecture, and closes with a discussion of the theory's limitations and open problems.

##2 Background

### 2.1 Non-Archimedean Banach algebras and the spectrum

Let A be a commutative Banach K-algebra with unit, norm ‖·‖ submultiplicative and extending |·| on K. A *multiplicative seminorm* is a map |·|_x: A → R≥0 satisfying |f+g|_x ≤ max(|f|_x, |g|_x), |fg|_x = |f|_x·|g|_x, and |c|_x = |c| for c ∈ K. The *Berkovich spectrum* is

> **Definition:** M(A) = {|·|_x : A → R≥0 multiplicative seminorm, |f|_x ≤ ‖f‖ for all f ∈ A},

equipped with the weakest topology making every evaluation map x ↦ |f|_x continuous. Then M(A) is nonempty, compact, and Hausdorff [1, Theorem 1.2.1]. Every maximal ideal with its residue norm gives a point, but there are vastly more: M(A) is the completion of the classical rigid spectrum by "generic" points recording valuations.

The construction is functorial: a bounded homomorphism φ: A → B induces a continuous map M(φ): M(B) → M(A). Finite products, fiber products, and closed immersions behave as in scheme theory, but the underlying topology is now rich enough for genuine analysis — partitions of unity exist for suitable covers, and coherent sheaves have vanishing higher cohomology on affinoids.

### 2.2 The affine and projective lines as trees

The soul of the theory is visible already for A = K{T}, the Tate algebra of power series converging on the closed unit disc. Its spectrum A^1,an = M(K{T}) admits a complete classification [1, §4]. Points correspond to nested families of discs:

| Type | Description | Residue data |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | K-rational points x ∈ K | trivial |
| 2 | Gauss points ζ_{a,r} of discs D(a,r), r ∈ Γ | trdeg 1 over residue field |
| 3 | Discs D(a,r) with r ∉ Γ | rational function field in 1 variable |
| 4 | Decreasing nested discs with empty intersection | immediate extension |

The *Gauss point* ζ_Gauss = ζ_{0,1} is the seminorm f ↦ max_n |a_n| for f = Σ a_n T^n. It is the unique point fixed by the full automorphism group PGL_2(R), and it serves as the root of the tree. Every point x lies on a unique arc from ζ_Gauss outward, parametrized by the radius; the space A^1,an is a uniquely arcwise-connected tree — an infinite R-tree. Branching at a type-2 point is indexed by the projective line over the residue field k: each tangent direction corresponds to a residue disc. For the projective line P^1,an, obtained by gluing two copies of A^1,an, the same picture holds with the Gauss point as the central branching hub; the ends of the tree are the type-1 and type-4 points, compactifying the tree.

> **Theorem:** (Berkovich [1]) The Berkovich affine line A^1,an is a locally compact, uniquely path-connected tree. The retraction onto any finite subtree is continuous, and P^1,an is compact and contractible onto its Gauss point skeleton.

This tree structure is the local model for all of Berkovich geometry: Berkovich curves are, roughly, graphs with infinite branching permitted along a dense set of type-2 points [8].

### 2.3 Analytification

For X a scheme of finite type over K, the *analytification* X^an is constructed by gluing M(A) for affine charts Spec A, with structure sheaf built from the Banach algebra completions. As a set [8]:

> **Definition:** X^an = {(x, |·|_x) : x ∈ X, |·|_x a real valuation on κ(x) extending |·| on K},

topologized so that f ↦ |f|_x is continuous for regular functions f. The map X^an → X sending (x, |·|_x) ↦ x is surjective, and K-rational points embed as type-1 points. Analytification is functorial, preserves open and closed immersions, fiber products, and properness; the analytification of a proper variety is compact. Crucially, X^an is Hausdorff and locally compact — the pathologies of the Zariski topology are resolved by remembering valuations.

##3 Methodology

Our exposition follows the historical-logical arc of the subject: we define the analytic objects, compute them explicitly in dimension one, then ascend through skeletons, weight metrics, tropicalization, and the Monge–Ampère theory to mirror symmetry. Proofs are sketched with precise references; where computation illuminates theory, we give executable code.

The running conventions: K is algebraically closed, complete, nontrivially valued, with valuation written additively as val: K× → R (so |x| = e^{−val(x)}), valuation ring R, residue field k of characteristic zero unless stated. Models are flat, separated schemes X over Spec R with generic fiber X. A model is *strictly semistable* if it is covered by étale charts of the form Spec R[T_0,…,T_n]/(T_0···T_n − a) with a ∈ m nonzero [2][7].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Skeletons of semistable models and the retraction τ

Let X be a strictly semistable formal model with generic fiber X = X_η. Berkovich [2, §5] constructs a closed subset S(X) ⊂ X — the *skeleton* — together with a *strong deformation retraction* Φ: X × (0,1) → X onto S(X). The construction reduces by étale charts to the torus case: for the standard model Z_{n,a} = Spf R{T_0,…,T_n}/(T_0···T_n − a), the skeleton is the simplex

Δ = {(r_0,…,r_n) ∈ R≥0^{n+1} : Σ r_i = val(a)},

embedded via the monomial valuations |T_i| = e^{−r_i}, and the retraction sends a seminorm to the monomial seminorm recording only the values on the coordinates T_i. Gluing over charts, S(X) is identified with the *dual intersection complex* of the special fiber X_s: vertices correspond to irreducible components, edges to double intersections, faces to higher intersections, with edge lengths given by valuations of the local equations.

For a proper variety, every semistable model yields such a retraction, and different models give skeletons related by subdivisions. The retraction τ: X^an → Sk(X) is canonical once the model is fixed; it collapses each "tube" over a stratum of the special fiber onto the corresponding face of the dual complex. In particular:

> **Theorem:** (Berkovich [2, Theorem 5.2]) If X is a compact analytic domain with a strictly semistable formal model X, then X^an admits a strong deformation retraction onto the dual complex of the special fiber X_s. In particular X^an has the homotopy type of a finite simplicial complex.

For curves this becomes completely explicit: if X is a smooth projective curve with semistable model X, then Sk(X) ⊂ X^an is a *metric graph* — the dual graph of the special fiber with edge lengths −log|a| from the local equations xy = a — and X^an deformation-retracts onto it [3]. Removing finitely many type-1 points, X^an is locally isomorphic to A^1,an, and the skeleton is the minimal subgraph carrying the genus.

### 4.2 The weight metric and the essential skeleton

Skeletons depend on models; the *essential skeleton* does not. Let X be smooth projective over K with semiample canonical divisor, and let Ω be a nonzero pluricanonical form. Mustaţă and Nicaise defined the *weight function* wt_Ω: X^an → R ∪ {∞} measuring the order of vanishing of Ω along divisorial valuations; its minimality locus

Sk^ess(X) = {x ∈ X^an : wt_Ω(x) = min wt_Ω}

is a finite polyhedral complex — the *essential skeleton* — independent of all choices [5][6]. Temkin showed that the Kähler seminorm on pluricanonical forms extends this construction to arbitrary non-Archimedean ground fields and quasismooth spaces, with the maximality locus always a piecewise-linear subspace contained in every semistable skeleton [6].

> **Theorem:** (Nicaise–Xu [5]) Let X be smooth projective over k((t)) with semiample canonical divisor. The essential skeleton coincides with the skeleton of any minimal dlt model and is a strong deformation retract of X^an. If X is Calabi–Yau, Sk^ess(X) is a pseudomanifold.

Kontsevich and Soibelman had earlier constructed a canonical skeleton from the asymptotics of a holomorphic volume form along a degeneration — the *KS skeleton* — conjectured to carry the affine structure underlying SYZ mirror symmetry [7]. Nicaise–Xu prove the KS skeleton agrees with the essential skeleton for Calabi–Yau varieties, unifying the birational and symplectic perspectives: the pseudomanifold property is exactly what a candidate SYZ base must satisfy.

### 4.3 Tropicalization as the shadow of Berkovich space

Let T ≅ G_m^n be a split torus and X ⊂ T a closed subvariety. The *tropicalization map*

trop: X^an → R^n,  x ↦ (−log|x_1|_x, …, −log|x_n|_x),

is continuous, proper, and surjective onto the *tropical variety* Trop(X) — the image is a balanced polyhedral complex of pure dimension dim X [3][8]. This is the conceptual explanation of tropical geometry: tropical varieties are not combinatorial accidents but the value-group projections of analytic spaces. The balancing condition at each codimension-one face is the slope formula for the piecewise-linear functions log|f| on skeletons [3].

Baker, Payne, and Rabinoff proved the precise correspondence in dimension one: every finite subgraph of a Berkovich curve admits a *faithful* tropicalization — a homeomorphism onto its image that is an isometry for the skeletal metric — and every multiplicity-one tropicalization lifts to a subgraph of the curve [3].

```python
import math

def tropical_polynomial(coeffs, x):
    """Evaluate a tropical polynomial (min-plus) at x.
    coeffs: list of (exponent, coefficient) with coefficient = -log|c|."""
    return min(c + e * x for e, c in coeffs)

def newton_subdivision(coeffs):
    """Return the break points of a tropical polynomial in one variable:
    the tropical roots, i.e. the tropicalization of the zero set."""
    pts = sorted(coeffs)  # sort by exponent
    roots, lower = [], []
    for e, c in pts:
        while len(lower) >= 2:
            (e1, c1), (e2, c2) = lower[-2], lower[-1]
            # check convexity: slope must increase along lower hull
            if (c2 - c1) * (e - e2) >= (c - c2) * (e2 - e1):
                lower.pop()
            else:
                break
        lower.append((e, c))
    for (e1, c1), (e2, c2) in zip(lower, lower[1:]):
        roots.append((c1 - c2) / (e2 - e1))  # kink = tropical root
    return roots

# f = T^3 + p*T + p^2 over Q_p: coefficients in (exponent, valuation)
f = [(3, 0.0), (1, 1.0), (0, 2.0)]
print("tropical roots (valuations of zeros):", newton_subdivision(f))
print("trop(f) at x=0.5:", tropical_polynomial(f, 0.5))
```

The output lists the valuations of the zeros — the one-dimensional tropical variety — computed as the lower convex hull of the Newton polygon, exactly the data the retraction τ records on the skeleton.

### 4.4 Non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère equations

The metric half of mirror symmetry requires solving a Monge–Ampère equation on Berkovich space.

> **Theorem:** (Boucksom–Jonsson [9]) Let X be smooth projective and L ample. For every probability measure μ supported on the dual complex of an snc model, there exists a continuous semipositive metric φ on L^an with MA(φ) = μ, unique up to constants.

Applied to a degenerating Calabi–Yau family with trivial canonical bundle, the solution with μ the Lebesgue measure on the essential skeleton yields the non-Archimedean analogue of the Calabi–Yau metric — the metric whose existence the SYZ conjecture predicts on the base.

### 4.5 Skeletons of abelian varieties and the SYZ picture

For an abelian variety A over K, non-Archimedean uniformization (Raynaud, Bosch–Lütkebohmert) presents A^an as a quotient T^an/Λ of a torus by a lattice, after a finite extension.

Sk(A) = R^g / Λ_trop,

a strong deformation retract of A^an carrying a canonical integral affine structure and principal polarization [8].

Kontsevich–Soibelman [7] conjectured the general picture: for a maximally degenerating Calabi–Yau family X → D×, the essential skeleton Sk^ess(X) carries an integral affine structure with singularities along a codimension-two discriminant, the Gromov–Hausdorff limit of the Ricci-flat metrics collapses onto it, and the mirror family is built by gluing the Legendre-dual affine manifold.

```python
def skeleton_retraction_example(val_a, queries):
    """Retraction of points of the Tate curve G_m^an/(q^Z) onto its skeleton.

    The skeleton of a Tate elliptic curve with Tate parameter q is a circle
    of length val(q). A point with coordinate t retracts to val(t) mod val(q).
    """
    L = val_a  # length of the skeleton circle
    return [((v % L) + L) % L for v in queries]

# Tate parameter q with val(q) = 5; sample points at various valuations
print(skeleton_retraction_example(5.0, [-3.2, 0.0, 7.7, 12.5, -11.0]))
```

The circle R/5Z is the essential skeleton of the Tate curve; the map t ↦ val(t) mod 5 is the retraction τ, collapsing each affinoid torus fiber onto its base point — a one-line model of SYZ.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

The theory's central claims admit precise, verifiable formulations:

1. **Retraction theorem.** For X strictly semistable, the monomial retraction on each chart glues to a strong deformation retraction Φ: X × (0,1) → X onto S(X) [2]. The proof reduces via étale charts Z_{n,a} to the torus, where the retraction is the explicit map sending a seminorm to its monomialization; compatibility with étale morphisms [2, Theorem 5.2(vii)] glues the local retractions.
2. **Faithful tropicalization.** BPR prove that a finite subgraph Γ ⊂ X^an of a curve admits an embedding X ↪ T with trop|_Γ a homeomorphism and isometry [3, Theorem 8.1]. The proof builds rational functions separating the edges of Γ via the slope formula, then verifies injectivity on Γ by a potential-theoretic argument.
3. **Essential skeleton as retract.** Nicaise–Xu show the weight function attains its minimum on a subcomplex of every snc skeleton, that this subcomplex is independent of the model, and that the Berkovich retraction restricts to a strong deformation retraction onto it [5, Theorem 4.2.4]. The Calabi–Yau pseudomanifold statement follows from the MMP: minimal dlt models have dual complexes that are pseudomanifolds.
4. **Monge–Ampère solvability.** Boucksom–Jonsson solve MA(φ) = μ by a variational method: the energy functional E(φ) is maximized over semipositive metrics, and the maximizer's Monge–Ampère measure is the prescribed μ [6]. Uniqueness follows from a comparison principle for the non-Archimedean operator.
5. **Analytification as limit of tropicalizations.** Payne shows the natural map X^an → lim← Trop(X, ι) over all torus embeddings ι is a homeomorphism [4, Theorem 1.1], using that rational functions separate points of X^an and that the inverse limit topology coincides with the Berkovich topology.

*Computational check.* The two programs above are directly executable: the first computes the tropicalization of a p-adic polynomial via its Newton polygon, reproducing the image of the Berkovich zero set under trop; the second implements the skeleton retraction for a Tate curve. Both encode theorems — the Newton polygon theorem and Tate uniformization — as algorithms.

##6 Limitations

The theory has genuine boundaries:

- **Resolution hypotheses.** Skeletons and retractions require semistable or polystable models, whose existence in positive or mixed residue characteristic needs de Jong's alterations; the resulting skeletons are then only well-defined up to alteration. Functorial semistable reduction remains open in general.
- **Non-proper varieties.** For non-proper X there is no retraction of X^an onto a compact skeleton; one must work with generalized skeleta attached to a compactification plus boundary divisor, which are noncompact and technically heavier [8].
- **Higher-dimensional faithful tropicalization.** While curves enjoy a complete BPR dictionary, in higher dimensions faithful tropicalization preserves only the integral affine structure, not a metric; the full metric geometry of higher-dimensional skeletons is not captured by any single tropicalization.
- **The SYZ conjecture.** The Kontsevich–Soibelman picture — affine structure with singularities on the essential skeleton, metric collapse, mirror reconstruction — is proven only in special cases (abelian varieties, K3 surfaces via Gross–Siebert, some toric degenerations). The general Calabi–Yau case, including the existence of the singular affine structure on Sk^ess(X), remains conjectural.
- **Analytic vs. algebraic skeletons.** The Hrushovski–Loeser model-theoretic skeletons and the Temkin–Berkovich formal-model skeletons agree where both are defined, but a unified framework covering arbitrary analytic spaces without models is still being developed.

---

##7 Conclusion

Berkovich's spectrum M(A) turned non-Archimedean geometry from a Grothendieck-topological formalism into a genuine topological theory, and the dividend was a combinatorial spine inside every analytic space. The retraction τ: X^an → Sk(X) compresses the infinite tree-like bulk of the analytification onto the dual complex of a degeneration; the weight metric selects from all such spines a canonical one, the essential skeleton; tropicalization reads off the value-group shadow; and the Monge–Ampère theory equips the skeleton with the collapsed Calabi–Yau metric. In the Kontsevich–Soibelman vision, this skeleton *is* the base of the SYZ fibration, making Berkovich geometry the natural home of mirror symmetry for degenerations. What began as a technical fix — adding points to rigid spaces — became the bridge between birational geometry, tropical combinatorics, and symplectic duality.
