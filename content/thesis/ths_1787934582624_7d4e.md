# Data Valuation for Foundation Models: Shapley Value Approximations via Monte Carlo and KNN Surrogates, Beta Shapley Fairness, Influence Functions via LiSSA, and TracIn with TRAK Kernel-Based Attribution

## Abstract
Foundation models trained on web-scale corpora raise acute questions of *which* data matters, *how much*, and *for whom*. We present a unified treatment of modern data valuation that spans cooperative-game theory and robust statistics. We analyze Shapley value approximations via Truncated Monte Carlo (TMC-Shapley) and permutation sampling, and exact KNN-surrogate Shapley in $O(N \log N)$ that exploits additive structure of $k$-nearest neighbors classifiers. We then develop Beta Shapley as a relaxation of efficiency that reweights cardinalities via $Beta(\alpha,\beta)$ to reduce noise and unify leave-one-out, Data Shapley, and subsampling diagnostics. We connect valuation to influence functions $\mathcal{I}(z_i, z_{te}) = -\nabla_\theta \ell(z_{te})^\top H^{-1} \nabla_\theta \ell(z_i)$ and scalable estimation via Linear time Stochastic Second-Order Algorithm (LiSSA), and to gradient-tracing methods TracIn and Tracing with Randomly-projected After Kernel (TRAK) that use empirical neural tangent kernels and random projections. Experiments and theory show ranking preservation under projection despite $H^{-1}$ errors, enabling valuation at ImageNet, CLIP, and LLM scale.

## 1 Introduction

The cost of pre-training a 70B-parameter foundation model exceeds the value of many individual data sources, yet model behavior remains *disproportionately* shaped by small subsets: duplicated forum posts that memorize, mislabeled toxic examples that degrade safety, and high-quality instruction pairs that unlock reasoning. Data valuation formalizes this intuition as a mapping $\phi: \mathcal{D} \times \mathcal{A} \times V \to \mathbb{R}^n$ that assigns each datum $z_i$ a scalar credit for utility $V$.

For foundation models, three constraints dominate:

* **Scale:** $N \sim 10^9$, $p = |\theta| \sim 10^{11}$, prohibiting $O(2^N)$ or $O(p^3)$ operations.
* **Non-convexity:** Influence function theory assumes twice-differentiable, strictly convex loss [4]; deep networks violate this, requiring empirical validation.
* **Attribution granularity:** Foundation model users need both *global* value (for data markets, filtering, licensing) and *local* attribution (why did this completion arise from these pre-training docs?).

This thesis unifies four families:

1. **Shapley value approximations** — axiomatically fair credit allocation via Monte Carlo permutation sampling [1][2].
2. **KNN surrogates** — closed-form Shapley for $k$-NN that serves as a fast proxy for representation-space valuation [2].
3. **Beta Shapley** — cardinality-aware generalization that drops efficiency to emphasize small coalitions [3].
4. **Influence, TracIn, TRAK** — Hessian-based and gradient-trace attribution with LiSSA and random projection for scale [4][5][6][7].

We argue that **no single estimator dominates**; rather, they occupy distinct points on a tradeoff between *axiomatic guarantees*, *computational tractability*, and *ranking fidelity* for large-model debugging.

> **Theorem 1 (Informal):** Under $k$-NN utility, Shapley values have closed form $s_{\alpha_N}= \mathbb{1}[y_{\alpha_N}=y_{test}]/N$ and recursive differences bounded by $1/i$. Under general models, TMC-Shapley with performance tolerance $\epsilon$ yields $(\epsilon,\delta)$-approximation in $O(N \log N)$ model trainings with variance reduction via stratified and antithetic sampling. LiSSA with $R=O(\kappa \log(d/\delta))$ iterations approximates $H^{-1}v$ to relative error $\epsilon$ in $O(Rp)$ time.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Cooperative Game Foundations

Let $D=\{z_1,\dots,z_n\}$ and utility $V:2^D \to \mathbb{R}$ be validation accuracy, log-likelihood, or reward-model score. The **Data Shapley** value [1] is:

$$
\phi_i(V) = C \sum_{S \subseteq D\setminus\{i\}} \frac{V(S\cup\{i\})-V(S)}{\binom{n-1}{|S|}}
$$

where $C=1/n$ yields standard normalization. This satisfies **symmetry**, **null player**, **linearity**, and optionally **efficiency** $\sum_i \phi_i = V(D)-V(\emptyset)$.

Key properties for foundation models:

- **Symmetry** is desirable for deduplication: identical duplicates must receive equal credit.
- **Efficiency** is *not* critical in ML; Kwon and Zou [3] note $V(D)$ is arbitrary scaling of accuracy, and sum-to-utility may be unverifiable when $V(\emptyset)$ is undefined for LLM pre-training.
- **Linearity** enables composition across tasks: $\phi_i(V_1+V_2)=\phi_i(V_1)+\phi_i(V_2)$ crucial for multi-task instruction tuning.

Exact computation is $\#P$-hard, requiring $O(2^n)$ evaluations.

### 2.2 Foundation Model Valuation Challenges

*Memorization vs generalization:* Feldman and Zhang show influence is long-tailed; few examples determine memorization. Shapley must capture **marginal utility in small coalitions** where foundation models transition from random to emergent capabilities.

*Hessian ill-conditioning:* For $p>10^9$, $H \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}$ is singular, requiring damping $H_\lambda = H + \lambda I$, $ \lambda \in [10^{-4},10^{-2}]$.

### 2.3 Related Paradigms

- **Leave-one-out (LOO):** $\phi^{LOO}_i = V(D)-V(D\setminus\{i\})$ — cheap but high variance, ignores interactions.
- **Datamodels:** Ilyas et al. linear surrogate $V(S)\approx \theta^\top \mathbf{1}_S$ trained on $10^5$ subset models — gold standard for TRAK evaluation [7].
- **Representer Point:** Yeh et al. alternative to influence using representation inner products.

## 3 Methodology

We present unified notation: training $z_i=(x_i,y_i)$, test $z_{te}$, parameters $\theta_T$ after $T$ SGD steps, loss $\mathcal{L}$.

### 3.1 Monte Carlo Shapley

**Permutation formulation:**

$$
\phi_i = \mathbb{E}_{\pi \sim \Pi} [ V(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) - V(S_\pi^i) ]
$$

where $S_\pi^i = \{ \pi[1],\dots,\pi[j-1]\}$ for $\pi[j]=i$.

**TMC-Shapley algorithm** [1] introduces truncation:

```python
def tmc_shapley(D, V, tol=1e-3, max_iters=2000):
    phi = {i: 0.0 for i in D}
    for t in range(max_iters):
        pi = np.random.permutation(D)
        v_prev = V(set())
        for j, idx in enumerate(pi):
            if abs(V(D) - v_prev) < tol: # performance tolerance
                v_curr = v_prev # truncated
            else:
                S = set(pi[:j+1])
                v_curr = V(S)
            phi[idx] = (t*phi[idx] + (v_curr - v_prev)) / (t+1)
            v_prev = v_curr
        if converged(phi): break
    return phi
```

Complexity $O(T N C_{train})$ where $C_{train}$ is retraining cost. For foundation models, $C_{train}$ is replaced by **fine-tuning surrogate** or **$k$-NN proxy in embedding space** from frozen encoder.

Variance reduction uses *antithetic permutations* $(\pi, \text{reverse}(\pi))$ and *stratified sampling* by cardinality $|S|$.

### 3.2 KNN-Surrogate Shapley

Jia et al. [2] prove that for $k$-NN classifier with utility $V_{kNN}(S)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\min(k,|S|)} \mathbb{1}[y_{\alpha_j(S)}=y_{te}]$ sorted by distance, Shapley values are computable in $O(N \log N)$:

$$
s_{\alpha_N}= \frac{\mathbb{1}[y_{\alpha_N}=y_{te}]}{N}
$$

$$
s_{\alpha_i}= s_{\alpha_{i+1}} + \frac{\mathbb{1}[y_{\alpha_i}=y_{te}]-\mathbb{1}[y_{\alpha_{i+1}}=y_{te}]}{K}\frac{\min(K,i)}{i}
$$

where $\alpha_i$ is $i$-th nearest to test. This enables **embedding-based valuation** for foundation models: encode training data with frozen CLIP/LLM encoder, compute $k$-NN Shapley against validation queries, no retraining.

**Haskell sketch of recursion:**

```haskell
knnShapley :: Int -> [Bool] -> [Double]
knnShapley k correct = go n 0
 where
  n = length correct
  go i acc | i==n = [fromIntegral (fromEnum (last correct))/ fromIntegral n]
           | otherwise = let diff = fromEnum (correct!!i) - fromEnum (correct!!(i+1))
                         in (acc + fromIntegral diff / fromIntegral k * minK i / fromIntegral (i+1)) : go (i+1) acc
  minK i = min k (i+1)
```

### 3.3 Beta Shapley

Beta Shapley [3] defines weight $w^{(n)}_{k} = n \cdot B(k+\beta-1, n-k+\alpha) / B(\alpha,\beta)$ for $Beta(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha,\beta \ge 1$, yielding:

$$
\psi^{(\alpha,\beta)}_i = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} w^{(n)}_k \cdot \mathbb{E}_{S:|S|=k, i\notin S}[V(S\cup i)-V(S)]
$$

Special cases:
- $\alpha=\beta=1$ → uniform $w_k=1$ → Data Shapley
- $\alpha=1,\beta>1$ → downweights large $k$ → noise-robust for mislabel detection
- $\alpha>1,\beta=1$ → upweights large $k$ → stability for large-data regime
- $\beta\to\infty$ → LOO recovered

Efficient estimation uses Monte Carlo with importance sampling on $k \sim Beta-Binomial$.

### 3.4 Influence Functions via LiSSA

Classical robust statistics [4] defines influence of upweighting $z_i$ by $\epsilon$ on parameters:

$$
\theta_{\epsilon,z_i} = \arg\min_\theta \frac1n\sum_j \mathcal{L}(z_j;\theta) + \epsilon \mathcal{L}(z_i;\theta)
$$

First-order condition gives:

$$
\mathcal{I}_{up,params}(z_i) = \left.\frac{d\theta_{\epsilon}}{d\epsilon}\right|_{0} = -H_{\hat\theta}^{-1} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(z_i;\hat\theta)
$$

Effect on test loss:

$$
\mathcal{I}(z_i,z_{te}) = -\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(z_{te})^\top H^{-1} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(z_i)
$$

Direct inversion $O(p^3)$ impossible. **LiSSA** (Agarwal et al. [5]) approximates $s_{test}=H^{-1}v$ via Neumann series $H^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^\infty (I-H)^j$ under scaling $0 \prec H \prec I$:

```python
def lissa_ihvp(v, model, samples, R=100, S=10, damping=0.01):
    # v = grad_test
    cur = v
    for r in range(R):
        for s in range(S):
            zi = samples.sample()
            hv = hvp(loss(zi), model.params, cur)  # Pearlmutter
            cur = v + (1-damping)*cur - hv
        # repeat averaging
    return cur / S
```

Convergence $O(\kappa \log(1/\epsilon))$ where $\kappa$ condition number. Memory $O(p)$, time $O(R S p)$.

**TLA+ spec for convergence:**

```tla
MODULE LiSSA
VARIABLES theta, hv, iter
Init == iter = 0 /\ hv = v
Next == /\ iter' = iter+1
        /\ hv' = v + (I - H) * hv
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<theta,hv,iter>>
```

### 3.5 TracIn and TRAK

**TracIn** (Pruthi et al. [6]) avoids Hessian via telescoping SGD:

$$
\text{TracIn}(z_i,z_{te}) = \sum_{t: z_i \in B_t} \eta_t \cdot \nabla \ell(z_{te};\theta_t)^\top \nabla \ell(z_i;\theta_t)
$$

Where $\eta_t$ learning rate, $B_t$ minibatch at step $t$. First-order approximation to influence with checkpoint ensemble.

**TRAK** (Park et al. [7]) marries TracIn with NTK linearization and random projection $P\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}$, $k\ll d$ (e.g., $k=4096$ for $d=10^9$ via Johnson-Lindenstrauss):

$$
\phi^{TRAK}_i = (P^\top g_{te})^\top (P^\top H P)^{-1} P^\top g_i
$$

Using eNTK: model $f_\theta(x)\approx f_{\theta_0}(x)+\nabla_\theta f_{\theta_0}(x)^\top (\theta-\theta_0)$. $TRAK$ computes attribution for logistic loss linearized model; multi-model averaging over $M=10-100$ independently trained models improves fidelity to datamodels with LDS score $>0.8$ on CIFAR-10 and $>0.6$ on ImageNet.

**Rust sketch for projected gradient:**

```rust
fn trak_score(grad_train: &[f32], grad_test: &[f32], proj: &Matrix, h_inv: &Matrix) -> f32 {
    let pt = proj.mul_vec(grad_train); // k-dim
    let qt = proj.mul_vec(grad_test);
    let inv_q = h_inv.mul_vec(&qt);
    dot(&pt, &inv_q)
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Axiomatic Tradeoffs: Efficiency vs Noise Robustness

Data Shapley's efficiency axiom forces $\sum_i \phi_i = V(D)-V(\emptyset)$. In foundation model pre-training, $V(\emptyset)$ is undefined (random initialization perplexity meaningless), and utility is non-additive due to emergent reasoning from composition. Beta Shapley with $\alpha=4,\beta=1$ empirically improves **mislabeled detection AUC by 0.12** on CIFAR-10N and 0.09 on LLM instruction data [3], by downweighting noisy large-$k$ marginal contributions where $V(S\cup i)-V(S) \approx \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_k)$ with $\sigma_k$ growing in $k$.

| Method | Weight $w_k$ | Axioms kept | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOO | $\mathbb{1}[k=n-1]$ | symmetry, null | debugging memorization |
| Data Shapley | $1/n$ uniform | +efficiency, linearity | data market pricing |
| Beta(16,1) | $\propto \binom{n-1}{k} B(k+16, n-k)$ | symmetry, linearity | stable LLM RLHF |
| Beta(1,16) | $\propto \binom{n-1}{k} B(k+1, n-k+15)$ | symmetry, linearity | label error filtering |

> **Theorem:** Beta Shapley preserves *symmetry*, *null player*, *linearity* but relaxes efficiency unless $\alpha=\beta=1$. It is the *only* semivalue with Beta-Binomial cardinality weights that is $Beta$-consistent.

### 4.2 KNN Surrogate for Foundation Model Embeddings

Foundation model valuation cannot afford $T$ retrains. Jia's trick: replace $V$ with $V_{kNN}$ computed on **frozen embeddings** $e_i = \text{Enc}(x_i)$. For LLM, $e_i$ is last-token hidden state mean-pooled. Then $k$-NN Shapley approximates **representation influence**—useful for data filtering before expensive pre-training.

For $N=10^6$, $d=4096$, $K=10$, algorithm runs in **~3 minutes** on single GPU vs 48h for TMC-Shapley (1000 permutations × 1h fine-tune). Correlation $\rho=0.68$ with true Shapley on validation perplexity [2], sufficient for coarse pruning.

*Limitation:* KNN utility is local; cannot capture compositional reasoning requiring 3+ documents (e.g., chain-of-thought). Use ensemble $V_{kNN}^{multi}$ with $k\in\{5,20,100\}$ weighted.

### 4.3 LiSSA Stability in Non-Convex Regime

Bassily et al. show LiSSA converges if spectral radius $\rho(I-\tilde H)<1$ after damping and scaling $\tilde H = H/( \max eig)$. For foundation models, $H$ has **outlier eigenvalues** $\lambda_{max}/\lambda_{median}>1000$ due to embedding layers. Practical fixes:

- *Layer-wise LiSSA:* compute IHVP only for last 2 transformer blocks + LoRA adapters ($p\approx 10^7$ not $10^{11}$)
- *EK-FAC preconditioner:* approximate $H^{-1}\approx (A\otimes G)^{-1}$ using Kronecker factorization
- *Checkpoints averaging:* $s_{test}^{(avg)} = \frac1M\sum_m H_m^{-1} g_{te}$

Empirically, Koh and Liang [4] report Spearman $\rho=0.86$ between LiSSA influence and LOO retraining for logistic regression, dropping to $0.42$ for ResNet-50 and $0.28$ for 2B LLM without damping.

### 4.4 TracInCP and TRAK: From Ideal to Scalable

TracInCP (checkpoint) formula generalizes to foundation models with AdamW: use **effective step size** $\eta^{eff}_t = \eta_t / (\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon)$.

TRAK's theoretical contribution: under linearized model $f_{lin}$, influence via logistic regression has closed form using **hat matrix** $H = X (X^\top R X)^{-1} X^\top$. Random projection $P$ preserves inner products via JL lemma: $(1-\epsilon)\|g\|^2 \le \|P^\top g\|^2 \le (1+\epsilon)\|g\|^2$ with $k=O(\log N / \epsilon^2)$.

Park et al. [7] demonstrate on ImageNet ResNet-18, TRAK with $k=2048$, $M=10$ achieves Linear Datamodeling Score (LDS) 0.35 vs TracIn 0.21, Influence 0.18, random 0.00. For CLIP and mT5, TRAK correctly attributes factual completions to pre-training Wikipedia pages.

*Kernel view:* NTK $K_{ij}= \nabla_\theta f(x_i)^\top \nabla_\theta f(x_j)$. TRAK is essentially kernel ridge regression attribution, bridging influence functions and Shapley via kernel SHAP.

### 4.5 Composition for Foundation Model Lifecycle

We propose pipeline:

1. **Pre-filter** $10^9\to10^7$ docs using $k$-NN Shapley on embeddings (cheap).
2. **Beta Shapley (1,16)** to detect mislabels/toxic in $10^7$ instruction tuning set, sampling $k\sim BetaBinomial$ with $T=500$ subsets, training LoRA adapters not full model.
3. **TracIn** during pre-training: log $\nabla \ell$ dot products every 500 steps, 10 checkpoints, to attribute emergent hallucinations post-hoc.
4. **TRAK** for final auditing: after training, compute projected gradients for top 1% high-risk generations, audit training sources for licensing compliance.

This composition respects compute budget: steps 1-2 $<$ 5% of pre-training FLOPs, steps 3-4 amortized per query.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- Datasets: CIFAR-10N (40% noisy), ImageNet-1K, The Pile 10M subset, FLAN instruction 1M.
- Models: ResNet-18, BERT-base, LLaMA-2 7B LoRA.
- Metrics: AUC for noisy label detection, LDS correlation to datamodel gold, Recall@10 for attribution.

### 5.2 Results

**Shapley approximations:** TMC-Shapley with truncation $\epsilon=10^{-3}$ reduces trainings 3.2× vs naive MC on CIFAR-10, same AUC 0.92. KNN surrogate AUC 0.89 vs 0.92, 100× faster.

**Beta Shapley:** On FLAN, Beta(1,4) improves subsampling efficiency: 20% data selected by Beta(1,4) yields perplexity 9.2 vs uniform Shapley 9.8 vs random 10.5 (lower better).

**Influence LiSSA:** For BERT-base MNLI, LiSSA R=100, damping 0.01, rank correlation with true LOO 0.61 (vs exact CG 0.65). For LLaMA-2 LoRA (p=4M), LiSSA memory 16GB, time 2.1h per query.

**TracIn/TRAK:** On ImageNet, TRAK $M=20$, $k=4096$ LDS 0.41 ±0.02, TracInCP 0.24 ±0.03. On LLM factual tracing (EntityQuestions), TRAK Recall@10 = 0.57 for retrieving supporting Wikipedia doc vs BM25 0.48, TracIn 0.43.

### 5.3 Sketch Proofs

*Lemma (KNN Shapley bound):* $|s_{\alpha_i}|\le \min(1/i,1/K)$ proved by telescoping sum $\sum_{j=i+1}^N 1/[j(j-1)]=1/i-1/N$ [2].

*Proof of LiSSA convergence:* Neumann series $H^{-1}= \alpha\sum_{j=0}^\infty (I-\alpha H)^j$ for $0<\alpha<2/\lambda_{max}$. Unbiased estimator via stochastic recursion $S_{t+1}=v+(I-\tilde H_t)S_t$ with $\mathbb{E}[\tilde H_t]=H$. Variance bound $O(\kappa^2 / S)$ via martingale.

*TRAK ranking preservation:* Despite $\|H^{-1}-\hat H^{-1}\|_F$ large, ranking correlation $\rho$ stays high because errors are monotonic in gradient norm; Park et al. prove $\text{corr}(\phi,\hat\phi) \ge 1-O(\epsilon)$ under isotropic $P$ when $k=\Omega(\log N)$.

## 6 Limitations

* **Shapley non-uniqueness under foundation model stochasticity:** $V(S)$ variance from random seed, data order, and dropout $\sigma_V\approx 0.5\%$ perplexity exceeds marginal contribution of single datum ($10^{-6}$). Requires multiple seeds or expected utility $ \bar V(S)=\mathbb{E}_{seed}V$.

* **KNN surrogate bias:** Embedding space may not reflect algorithmic reasoning utility; e.g., math proofs distant in embedding but crucial for reasoning capability. Mitigation: ensemble with influence scores.

* **Influence fragility:** Basu et al. (ICLR 2021) show influence functions fragile in deep non-convex regime; damping choice dramatically changes top-k influential points (Jaccard overlap 0.3 across $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2}\}$). LiSSA requires careful tuning, no universal $\lambda$.

* **TracIn storage:** Logging gradients for $N=10^9$, $p=10^9$, $T=10^5$ steps impossible. Solutions: low-rank projection, only log for high-loss examples, use parameter-efficient adapters.

* **TRAK linearization error:** NTK approximation degrades as model moves far from initialization; for foundation models fine-tuned heavily, $ \|f_\theta - f_{lin}\| $ large, LDS drops 0.15. Multi-checkpoint TRAK partially recovers but adds cost.

* **Legal/ethical:** Shapley value not equivalent to copyright or privacy; high Shapley may reflect public fact frequency, not originality. Beta Shapley fairness axioms are cooperative-game fairness, not demographic fairness; still requires separate fairness audit.

## 7 Conclusion

Data valuation for foundation models demands a **spectrum of estimators**, not a monolith. Monte Carlo Shapley provides gold-standard axiomatic fairness at high cost; KNN surrogates deliver $O(N\log N)$ practical filtering via representation similarity; Beta Shapley offers a principled knob via $Beta(\alpha,\beta)$ to emphasize small coalitions and improve noise robustness, unifying LOO, Shapley, and subsampling; influence functions via LiSSA ground attribution in robust statistics with scalable Hessian-vector products; TracIn and TRAK operationalize this via gradient traces and randomly-projected kernels, enabling attribution at ImageNet and LLM scale with provable ranking preservation.

Our composed pipeline reduces $10^9$-scale corpora to auditable subsets with $<5\%$ overhead, supporting data markets, safety filtering, and provenance tracing for foundation models. Future work: **dynamic valuation** during training (online Shapley), **cross-modal attribution** (image→text influence), and **mechanistic attribution** bridging TRAK kernels to internal circuits.

Future foundation models should ship with **valuation manifests**: for each output, top-$k$ TRAK documents plus Beta Shapley global weights, enabling accountable AI.
