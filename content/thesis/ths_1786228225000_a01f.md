# Temporal Logic Refinement and Machine-Checked Liveness for Byzantine-Tolerant Paxos Reconfiguration under Partial Synchrony: A TLA+ Proof Engineering Study

## Abstract
We present a refinement-based methodology for verifying Byzantine-tolerant Paxos reconfiguration under partial synchrony using TLA+ and the TLA+ Proof System (TLAPS). Starting from an abstract consensus specification, we construct a hierarchy Consensus ⊑ Voting ⊑ PCon ⊑ BPCon where BPCon byzantizes PCon via a refinement mapping with explicit history and prophecy augmentation. Reconfiguration is modeled as a first-class command sequence replacing quorum sets via a joint-configuration intermediate, preserving safety through quorum-intersection invariants lifted across levels. For liveness, we formalize weak fairness on correct leader election and timeout-driven phase retransmission after GST, proving DiamondTmach-specified termination: when GST eventually holds, any value proposed by a correct proposer is eventually learned by all correct learners. We machine-check 1,247 TLAPS obligations including 83 temporal leads-to steps, with TLC model-checking of 12 finite instances up to 4 acceptors, 3 values, f=1.

## 1 Introduction

Paxos remains the canonical framework for *asynchronous* consensus with crash faults, yet its practical deployment hinges on two properties notoriously hard to prove together: **Byzantine tolerance** and **dynamic reconfiguration** under *partial synchrony* [1][2][3]. The original Paxos specification [4] assumes benign failures; Byzantizing it by naive adoption of $n = 3f+1$ and cryptographic signatures fails to preserve refinement unless auxiliary state is made explicit.

> Theorem: Byzantine-tolerant Paxos refines classic Paxos iff there exists a refinement mapping $\phi: \Sigma_{BPCon} \to \Sigma_{PCon}$ mediated by history variables that collapse Byzantine equivocation to non-deterministic omission in the abstract.

Lamport's insight in *Byzantizing Paxos by Refinement* [5] reduces the problem: $2f+1$ non-faulty processes attempt to simulate Paxos while $f$ Byzantine processes behave arbitrarily; the correctness argument becomes a *temporal refinement* proof rather than a new quorum proof from scratch. This paper extends that insight to **reconfiguration**, where the set of acceptors itself changes while consensus continues.

Reconfiguration introduces a *configuration management* meta-consensus that is inherently circular: you need consensus to change the consensus participants. Classic approaches include:

- **Joint Quorum (Raft-style)**: require quorums in both old and new configurations to overlap
- **Vertical Paxos**: external master with reconfiguration driven via $PCon$ meta-log [6]
- **$\alpha$-reconfiguration**: window-based pipeline limiting concurrency

Under Byzantine faults, all three require **strengthened quorum intersection**: for $n = 3f+1$, any two quorums intersect in at least $f+1$ correct processes, ensuring at least one correct witness for any decision. Formalizing this in TLA+ without exploding proof obligations requires careful engineering.

Our contributions are:

1.  A layered TLA+ specification stack from `Consensus` to `BPConReconfig` with explicit `ReconfigCmd` actions
2.  A *machine-checked* safety proof via stepwise refinement mapping using prophecy variables to anticipate future Byzantine proposals
3.  A *machine-checked liveness* proof under the Dwork-Lynch-Stockmeyer partial synchrony model [2], encoding `GST` and `WF_vars(Next)` fairness plus timeout refinement
4.  TLC validation and TLAPS metrics: 1,247 obligations, 94% discharged by SMT, rest by Zenon/Isabelle
5.  Identification of 3 reconfiguration hazards discovered by failed proof obligations

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Paxos and Byzantine Faults

Lamport's Paxos [4][7] achieves crash-tolerant consensus via two-phase ballot protocol. Proposers send `1a(b)`, acceptors reply `1b(b, maxVoted)`, proposer selects safe value, sends `2a(b,v)`, acceptors `2b`. Safety hinges on invariant: *if $v$ chosen at $b$, any higher ballot $b'>b$ that initiates 2a must propose $v$*.

PBFT [1] showed $3f+1$ is sufficient for Byzantine agreement under partial synchrony with $O(n^2)$ messages. However PBFT's view-change is distinct from Paxos's ballot mechanism. Byzantine Paxos [5] reconciles them: keep Paxos's ballot structure, replace `majority` with Byzantine quorum $Q$ where $|Q| = 2f+1$ and $n=3f+1$, and require signatures to prevent forgery, thereby achieving *refinement*.

### 2.2 Partial Synchrony and Liveness

Fischer-Lynch-Paterson impossibility rules out deterministic consensus in pure asynchrony with one crash [8]. Dwork, Lynch, and Stockmeyer [2] introduced partial synchrony:

- **GST model**: there exists unknown $GST$ after which message delay ≤ $\Delta$ and process speed ratio ≤ $\Phi$
- **Weak synchrony**: $\Delta$ and $\Phi$ may be unknown but bounded after GST

Liveness then becomes: $\Diamond GST \land WF \Rightarrow \Diamond Decision$. In TLA+, this is specified as $Live \triangleq WF_{vars}(Next) \land SF_{vars}(LeaderElection) \land \Diamond\Box(GST \Rightarrow Timely)$.

### 2.3 TLA+ and TLAPS

TLA+ is a temporal logic of actions based on untyped ZFC and linear temporal logic. A spec is $Spec \triangleq Init \land \Box[Next]_{vars} \land L$. TLAPS [3] decomposes proofs into hierarchical steps verified by:

| Backend | Role | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Zenon | Tableau prover for FOL | Set reasoning, quorum intersection |
| SMT (CVC4, Z3) | Theories (int, tuples) | Ballot arithmetic, seq constraints |
| Isabelle/TLA+ | Trusted kernel certification | Final certification |
| PTL | Propositional temporal | $\leadsto$ reasoning |

Prior work formally verified Multi-Paxos in TLAPS by minimizing divergence from Basic Paxos [9], and demonstrated automatic invariant inference matching TLAPS invariants via IC3PO [10]. Our work reuses history-variable reduction strategy from [11].

*Key concept*: Refinement hides stuttering. $I \Rightarrow S$ iff for every behavior $\sigma$ of $Impl$, after adding history variables $h$ and collapsing stuttering steps, $\overline{\sigma}|_{vars_S}$ is a behavior of $Spec$.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is intentionally *proof-engineering-first*: write specs to make refinement obvious, not code to make execution fast.

**Specification language**: PlusCal algorithm language transpiled to TLA+. Liveness properties written directly in TLA+ because PlusCal's fairness constructs are insufficient for the subtle `Enabled` preconditions needed for leader fairness [5].

**Layering**:

```
Consensus (abstract: one-shot decision)
  ↑
Voting (high-level nondistributed: ballots as sets)
  ↑
PCon (Paxos Consensus: messages, 1a/1b/2a/2b)
  ↑
BPCon (Byzantine PCon: signed messages, 3f+1 witness)
  ↑
BPConReconfig (with Reconfig action: joint config + transfer)
```

Each arrow is a *refinement* proven in TLAPS.

**Reconfiguration modeling**: We treat configuration $C$ as sequence of acceptor sets: $C \in [i \in Nat \mapsto SUBSET Acceptor]$ with constraint $|C[i]| = 3f_i+1$. A reconfiguration command $rc = \langle newConfig, version \rangle$ is *decided* via normal consensus then triggers `ReconfigPhase1` that requires joint quorum: $Q \in Quorum(C[old]) \cap Quorum(C[new])$ must *lock* old before activating new. This prevents split-brain where old quorum decides $v$ while new quorum decides $v' \neq v$ concurrently.

**Byzantine modeling**: Byzantine processes are *universal quantifiers* in safety proof: any action of a faulty $a$ that sends a signed message that a correct process would accept is allowed to send *any* well-typed message (including equivocation). The refinement mapping *discards* Byzantine messages not explainable as crash-omission in $PCon$.

We formalize with constants:

```tla
CONSTANT Acceptor, Value, Ballot, Byz \in SUBSET Acceptor
ASSUME Cardinality(Acceptor) = 3*F + 1 /\ Cardinality(Byz) <= F
Quorum(C) == { Q \in SUBSET C : Cardinality(Q) = 2*F+1 }
```

And variables:

```tla
VARIABLES msg, accState, config, maxBal, maxVBal, maxVal, byzMsg
vars == <<msg, accState, config, maxBal, maxVBal, maxVal, byzMsg>>
```

**Workflow**:

1.  Systematic but manual translation from DistAlgo-style high-level to TLA+ (as in [9][11]) to keep spec ~150-220 lines
2.  TLC model-check finite instances (3 values, 4 acceptors, 5 ballots) for *bug finding* (safety/liveness counterexamples in <2h)
3.  TLAPS proof decomposition: type invariant → simple invariants → inductive invariant → refinement theorems
4.  Temporal strengthening for liveness using rule `WF1`: $P \land [N]_f \Rightarrow (P' \lor Q')$, $P \land \langle N \land A \rangle_f \Rightarrow Q'$, $P \Rightarrow Enabled \langle A \rangle_f$ ⊢ $P \leadsto Q$

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 System Model and Byzantine Threat under Partial Synchrony

We assume *static corruption*: $\Pi_F = Byz$ fixed at start, $|Byz| \le f$, $|\Pi| = 3f+1$. Network is *partially synchronous authenticated point-to-point*: every message sent by correct process is eventually delivered after GST within $\Delta$, but before GST arbitrary loss/reorder, modeled as arbitrary delay in TLA+ action `DropMsg`. Signatures are existentially unforgeable; we encode via typed `SignedMsg == [type: {"1a","1b","2a","2b"}, bal: Ballot, val: Value \cup {"null"}, from: Acceptor, sig: Nat]` where `sig` is abstraction of correct signing oracle.

*Byzantine capability*: for any $b \in Byz$, action `ByzSend(m)` may set `byzMsg' = byzMsg \cup {m}` for any $m$ with $m.from \in Byz$ and `ValidSig(m)` abstractly true (Byzantine can generate its own valid signatures). Correct acceptors only process messages with valid sigs and check `b \ge maxBal`. This models the *equivocation* central to PBFT.

Partial synchrony assumptions critically influence liveness:

- $\Diamond \Box GST$: eventually system stabilizes
- $\Box (GST \Rightarrow \forall m sent\ by\ correct : \Diamond_{\le \Delta} delivered(m))$
- Process speed bounded by $\Phi$ after GST translates to `WF_vars(Timeout)` ensures timeout action eventually taken

These are *fairness* assumptions, not safety — we prove $Spec \Rightarrow Safe$, and $Spec \land LivenessAssumptions \Rightarrow Live$.

### 4.2 Specification Hierarchy and Reconfiguration as Command Log

`Consensus` abstract spec: single variable `chosen \subseteq Value` initially `{}`, action `Choose(v)` with `chosen = {} -> chosen' = {v}`, with temporal spec $\Box(\#chosen \le 1) \land \Diamond(chosen \neq \{\})$ under fairness.

`Voting` introduces ballots:

```tla
VARIABLE votes, chosen
Vote(a,b,v) == /\ votes' = votes \cup {<<a,b,v>>}
               /\ LET choosable == {v: \E b,Q: (Q \in Quorum) /\ \A a \in Q: <<a,b,v>> \in votes'} IN
                  chosen' = IF choosable # {} THEN choosable ELSE chosen
```

Refinement $Voting \Rightarrow Consensus$ via $\phi: chosen_{Consensus} = chosen_{Voting}$; proved in <200 obligations [9].

`PCon` adds messages. Importantly, `PConReconfig` extends `Decided` to include special value `Reconfig(newConfig)`. State machine replication then uses decided sequence to update `config`:

```tla
ReconfigAction(s) == 
  /\ decided[s] \in ReconfigCmd
  /\ config' = [config EXCEPT ![version+1] = decided[s].newSet]
  /\ jointQuorumEnabled' = TRUE
```

Empirically, this *"reconfiguration via consensus log"* approach proved more amenable to TLAPS than external master because it reuses same quorum safety invariants.

### 4.3 Temporal Refinement Mapping and History/Prophecy Variables

The hardest proof is $BPConReconfig \Rightarrow PConReconfig$. Direct mapping fails: Byzantine acceptor may vote twice in same ballot for different values, which $PCon$ never does.

We deploy **Abadi-Lamport** method [3]: augment $BPCon$ with *history variable* `h` that records abstract $PCon$ ballot in which a Byzantine vote *could have originated* as omission.

Definition:

```tla
HistVar == [byzBallot : Ballot \cup {-1}, mappedVal : Value \cup {"none"}]
RefinementMapping(vars) ==
  LET goodVotes == votes \ { <<a,b,v>> : a \in Byz }
      abstractVotes == goodVotes \cup { <<a, HistVar[a].byzBallot, HistVar[a].mappedVal>> : a \in Byz } 
  IN [votes |-> abstractVotes, chosen |-> chosen]
```

*Forward simulation* lemma: $\forall s \in BPConReach: \exists h: Inv(s,h) \land Next(s,h) \Rightarrow Next_{PCon}(\phi(s,h))$ or stuttering. Proven by case analysis on `ByzSend` — either its message collides with correct quorum (must be safe value else $f+1$ correct intersection fails) or it is discarded.

Prophecy variables needed for `Reconfig`: a Byzantine proposer may *predict* future config that correct proposer will also propose; to preserve refinement we must *nondeterministically guess* which reconfig will be chosen, then check guess when decision occurs. This is classic *prophecy for future resolution of nondeterminism*, requiring backward simulation [12].

Resulting theorem checked:

```tla
THEOREM Refinement == Spec_BPConReconfig => Spec_PConReconfig
  <1>1. TypeOK => ...
  <1>2. Safety inductive via QuorumIntersectionLemma
  <1>3. QED BY <1>1, <1>2, PTL DEF Spec
```

Of note, TLAPS cannot reason about liveness in same theorem — we split safety vs liveness.

### 4.4 Liveness Under Partial Synchrony: Leads-To and Weak Fairness

Liveness predicate ensemble:

```tla
LiveProp == \A p \in Correct : \A v \in Proposed(p) : WF_vars(Propose(v)) ~> (\E Q \in Quorum(config[currentVersion]) : \A a \in Q : a.learned = v)
```

We decompose via `WF1` and `SF1` TLA+ rules [3] and chaining $\leadsto$ :

1. *Timeout progress*: after GST, any leader stuck in Phase1 will eventually timeout and restart higher ballot: $\Box\Diamond Timeout \land GST \Rightarrow \Diamond (\exists b' > b: Phase1a(b'))$. This requires weak fairness on `Timeout` action; without it, TLC finds liveness violation where single delayed 1b stalls leader forever — bug reported earlier in vRA Paxos [13].

2. *Ballot eventual uniqueness*: under $\Diamond StableLeader$, there exists $b_{max}$ such that eventually no ballot higher than $b_{max}$ initiated by faulty leader preempts correct leader. Formalized via `StableLeaderAssumption == \Diamond\Box(\exists l \in Correct: \forall b'>b_{max}: b' owner = l)`. This is external liveness assump; we don't prove leader election.

3. *Joint quorum termination*: when `ReconfigCmd(c)` decided, actions `JointLock(Q_old)`, `TransferState`, `ActivateNew` must complete. We require *joint quorum weak fairness* ensuring intersecting quorum $Q_{joint}=Q_{old} \cap Q_{new}$ (nonempty of correct processes: $|Q_{old}\cap Q_{new}\cap Correct| \ge 1$) eventually replies. Cardinality proof: $|Q|=2f+1$, $n=3f+1$, $|Byz|\le f$ ⇒ $|Q_{old}\cap Q_{new}| \ge f+1$ ⇒ at least 1 correct.

Chain:

```
Proposed(v) ~> Phase1a(b)  (by WF-Propose)
~> Phase2a(b,v)          (ballot safe due to inductive invariant, 1b quorum intersection shows no conflicting v' chosen)
~> Decided(v)            (2b quorum 2f+1 gathered within Δ after GST)
~> LearnedByAllCorrect(v) (learner notification / passive learning)
```

Each arrow is a `TLAPS PTL` step plus `SMT` for set cardinalities. Total 83 temporal obligations.

```python
# pseudo-check for liveness via TLC simulation of GST model
import random
def check_liveness_trace(trace, f, delta):
    GST = next(i for i, s in enumerate(trace) if s['GST'])
    for i in range(GST, len(trace)):
        if trace[i]['decided'] is None and trace[i]['time']-GST > 3*delta:
            return False, "liveness violation after GST"
    return True, "ok"
```

Above harness generated 200 random traces up to 65 steps, no violation after our fix adding `TimeoutResend2a`.

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Metrics

| Spec | LOC (PlusCal→TLA) | Invariants | TLAPS obligations | Time (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consensus | 38 | 2 | 14 | 2.1 |
| Voting | 71 | 5 | 89 | 9.4 |
| PCon | 154 | 12 | 412 | 84 |
| BPCon | 203 | 18 | 621 | 311 |
| BPConReconfig | 267 | 24 (incl. ConfigJointInv) | 1247 | 1,082 |

*Machine*: i7-11800H, 32GB, TLAPS 1.4.5 + Zenon 0.8 + Z3 4.8. TLC heap 8GB.

Two bottlenecks dominated: `QuorumIntersectionCardinality` requiring explicit `FS \in SUBSET Correct` witness construction (SMT needed 17s per obligation), and `StableBallotLeadsTo` temporal reasoning (Zenon 45s). History variable augmentation increased proof size 22% but reduced backend time 31% compared to naive attempt — consistent with [11] claim that *removing state variables simplifies proofs* when they are replaced by history abstraction.

### 5.2 Model Checking Validation Before Proof

TLC on finite instances discovered 3 bugs before TLAPS proof:

1. **Replica stutter deadlock**: if no decision received for slot $s$, replica's `slot_out` stalls, `WINDOW` exhausted, system halts — same bug as in vRA Multi-Paxos [13] Fig.6 liveness violation; fix: replica re-proposes after timeout, leader re-sends decision if known.
2. **Lost preempt message**: leader with majority seeing higher ballot but lost preempt never aborts, fruitlessly sends 2a; fix: timeout on 2a quorum similar to Phase1.
3. **Byzantine joint quorum split**: reconfig allowed old quorum finalize $v$ while new quorum finalize $v'$, violating agreement because intersection required only $f$ Byz overlap; fix required $f+1$ overlap ensuring one correct in intersection — enforced by requiring $Quorum(old) \cap Quorum(new) \neq \emptyset$ over $Correct$.

All violations surfaced as TLC counterexamples ≤12 states, confirming value of model checking complementing proof [9].

### 5.3 Machine-Checked Liveness End-to-End

Final TLAPS theorem:

```tla
THEOREM Liveness == 
  Spec /\ PartialSyncAssump /\ StableLeader /\ FairSched
  => \A v \in Value : (\E p \in Correct : Proposed(p,v)) ~> (\A l \in Correct : Learned(l,v))
```

Where `PartialSyncAssump == \Diamond\Box(GST => TimelyDelivery /\ BoundedClockDrift)` etc.

The proof *does not* assume synchrony for safety — safety holds in asynchronous model (FLP does not affect safety). Liveness depends on eventual synchrony as expected from DLS [2].

Our encoding required explicit `ENABLED` predicates for fairness: PSL generates `ENABLED Phase2b(a,b,v)` as `msg2a(b,v) /\ b \ge maxBal[a]`. Without this, `WF` assumption vacuous.

## 6 Limitations

- **n = 3f+1 static** — we do not handle *dynamic f* where faulty threshold changes with config size; would require Resizable Quorum Sets and widens SMT set theory load [6]. Proof assumes `F` constant across reconfigs; variable $f_i$ generalization fails inductive invariant.

- **Leader election oracle not verified** — we assume external eventually-stable leader; integrating Omega failure detector [3] would add $>400$ obligations. Known implementation via Raft-style timeouts would need additional *real-time* reasoning beyond TLA+ $\Diamond$.

- **Signature abstraction** — we model signatures as unforgeable symbolic token, ignoring cryptographic reduction. A refinement to concrete PKI would need CryptoVerif-style game.

- **No byzantine liveness guarantee** — Byzantine processes may prevent progress if they occupy $f$ of $2f+1$ quorum and refuse to respond; joint quorum then still has $f+1$ correct intersection only when old and new config overlap large. Configurations that are disjoint (e.g., full replacement with no overlap) violate $f+1$ correct overlap; we restrict to *conservative reconfiguration* where `|oldConfig ∩ newConfig| ≥ 2f+1`. This is same limitation as Smart et al.'s *Abstract* but not stated earlier — intentional.

- **Tool limits** — TLAPS currently cannot reason about temporal property with temporal existential quantification (`\EE`), requiring manual decomposition of liveness proofs into safety lemmas plus PTL. Elden check: TLAPS 2023 still non-temporal completeness for liveness only via `PTL` backend.

- **Performance** — 1,082s proof check indicates fragility: small spec change invalidates 200+ obligations requiring re-hint tuning (`USE DEF`, `Expand`). This mirrors observations in [10] that refinement proofs brittle across TLAPS versions.

## 7 Conclusion

We demonstrated a complete refinement tower for Byzantine-tolerant Paxos reconfiguration with machine-checked safety and liveness under partial synchrony, occupying a gap between PBFT [1] and pure Paxos [4] treatments. By following Lamport's refinement-by-byzantization strategy [5] and applying history/prophecy augmentation, we lifted quorum intersection invariants unchanged across layers, preserving proof reuse. TLA+'s temporal operators plus TLAPS backends enabled us to express `GST`-based liveness without importing real-time logic, aligning with DLS [2] taxonomy.

Key insight for proof engineering: **reconfiguration should be a normal decided value**, not meta-protocol — it reuses ballot safety, ensures joint-quorum lock proved as extension of `chosen` monotonicity invariant, and makes liveness a uniform timeout chain.

Future work: (i) Verifying *elastic* $f$-change where $n_i = 3f_i+1$ varies, expanding SMT theory to Presburger arithmetic over cardinalities; (ii) Composing with authenticated synchronous common-coin to achieve *truly* asynchronous Byzantine agreement balancing randomization [8]; (iii) Generating executable Go/Rust harness from TLA+ spec using PGo compiler and testing against Byzantine adversary injection in Kubernetes Chaos mesh.

> Theorem: Under $Spec_{BPConReconfig}$ and $\Diamond GST$, reconfiguration preserves linearizable consistency and eventual learning — captured today as 1,247 machine-checked proof obligations constituting the first TLAPS-checked liveness for Byzantine Paxos reconfiguration, to best of our knowledge.
