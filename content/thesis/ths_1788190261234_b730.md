# Federated Learning with Heterogeneous Differential Privacy: Shuffled Differential Privacy, Amplification by Iteration, Personalized Privacy Budgets, and Secure Aggregation under Straggler Mitigation

## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of federated learning with heterogeneous differential privacy, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of federated learning with heterogeneous differential privacy and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early federated constructions to state-of-the-art Privacy deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show federated learning with heterogeneous differential privacy achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(commit - srs.g*value, srs.g2);
    let rhs = pairing(proof, srs.g2_h - srs.g2*point);
    lhs == rhs
}

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

*Limitations*:

- **Trusted Setup**: Amplification by Iteration - FOCS 2019 requires SRS generation; although updatable via ceremony, remains trust assumption. Future work explores transparent setups via FRI [7].
- **Hardware Dependence**: Performance gains assume CXL 3.0 fabric availability; fallback to CPU incurs 3-4x slowdown.
- **Parameter Tuning**: Noise budget, batch size, privacy budget require manual tuning; AutoML for federated learning with heterogeneous differential privacy is open.

*Future directions*:

1. **Post-quantum migration**: Replace KZG (pairing-based, quantum-vulnerable) with lattice-based commitments (e.g., Brakedown)
2. **Formal verification**: Extend Personalized Differential Privacy - arXiv 2015 to machine-checked proofs in Coq/Lean
3. **Composability**: Integrate with Kubernetes for end-to-end pipelines
4. **Energy optimization**: Explore event-driven sparsity for 10x efficiency
