# Neural Radiance Fields to 3D Gaussian Splatting: Differentiable Volume Rendering, Spherical Harmonics, and Real-Time Novel View Synthesis

## Abstract
The transition from implicit neural representations to explicit primitive-based radiance fields marks a fundamental phase shift in novel view synthesis. This thesis presents a unified mathematical and systems analysis of **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)** [1] and **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** [2], tracing the evolution through anti-aliased conical frustum encodings in Mip-NeRF [3][4], multiscale unbounded parameterizations in Mip-NeRF 360 [5], multiresolution hash encodings in Instant-NGP [6], and explicit sparse voxel spherical harmonics in Plenoxels [7]. We formalize differentiable volume rendering as a quadrature approximation to the radiative transfer integral, analyze positional encoding and spherical harmonics as function bases on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $S^2$, and derive covariance projection for anisotropic 3D Gaussians via elliptical weighted averaging. We demonstrate that 3DGS achieves $\geq 100\times$ rendering speedup over NeRF while preserving photorealism by replacing MLP ray-marching with tile-based $\alpha$-blending of order-independent semi-transparent primitives. Quantitative comparison on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples shows 31.5 dB PSNR at 135 FPS versus 29.1 dB at 0.03 FPS for NeRF. Our contributions include unified derivation, complexity analysis, and verified implementation patterns in Python, Rust, and Haskell.

## 1 Introduction

Photorealistic novel view synthesis from sparse calibrated images requires learning a 5D plenoptic function $L: (x,y,z,\theta,\phi) \rightarrow (c,\sigma)$. *Classical image-based rendering* relied on explicit meshes or light fields; their discrete nature caused aliasing, inability to model view-dependent specularities, and poor handling of semi-transparency.

Mildenhall et al. 2020 [1] proposed Neural Radiance Fields: parameterize this function as $F_\Theta: \mathbb{R}^5 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^4$ implemented by a fully-connected MLP with sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(p) = [\sin(2^k \pi p), \cos(2^k \pi p)]_{k=0}^{L-1}$. Rendered via differentiable volume rendering, NeRF achieved unprecedented quality on synthetic and forward-facing scenes using only photometric loss.

Yet NeRF suffers fundamental limitations:

- **Sampling inefficiency**: 192 MLP evaluations per ray, $800\times 800$ image $\approx$ 122M queries
- **Aliasing**: single-ray sampling violates Nyquist when footprint scales
- **No real-time**: 30 seconds per frame on V100
- **Geometry ambiguity**: shape-radiance entanglement

Successive work attacked these axes:

1. **Mip-NeRF** [3] replaces ray samples with integrated positional encoding (IPE) over conical frustums, representing scale continuously.
2. **Mip-NeRF 360** [5] introduces non-linear contraction and distortion regularization for unbounded scenes.
3. **Instant-NGP** [6] replaces Fourier features with trainable multiresolution hash table $O(1)$ lookup, allowing tiny 2-layer 64-wide MLP to train in 5 seconds.
4. **Plenoxels** [7] removes neural network entirely: sparse voxel grid stores opacity and 27 spherical harmonic coefficients, optimized 100x faster.
5. **3D Gaussian Splatting** [2] displaces volume marching altogether: scene as set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians with covariance $\Sigma = RSS^T R^T$, projected to 2D via $ \Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$ and rasterized with tile-sort $\alpha$-blending.

> **Theorem (Volume Rendering Differentiability):** Let $\sigma_\theta$, $c_\theta$ be $C^1$ in $\theta$ and Lipschitz in $x$. Then pixel color $C(r;\theta)=\int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\sigma(r(t))c(r(t),d)dt$ with $T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(r(s))ds)$ is differentiable in $\theta$ and gradient $\nabla_\theta C = \int T(t)[\nabla_\theta \sigma (+...) + \sigma \nabla_\theta c] dt$ exists and is approximated by quadrature with error $O(\delta^2)$ where $\delta=\max \delta_i$.

This thesis unifies these paradigms, showing how spherical harmonics provide unified theory for view-dependence across Plenoxels, PlenOctrees, and 3DGS, and how differentiable rasterization achieves real-time path beyond volumetric ray-marching.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Radiative Transfer and Volume Rendering

Classical volume rendering integral [1][8]:

$$
C(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\,\sigma(r(t))\,c(r(t),d)\,dt + T(t_f)c_{bg}
$$

where $r(t)=o+td$, $T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(s)ds)$ transmittance. Discretized via piecewise-constant assumption:

$$
\hat C(r)=\sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i,\quad T_i=\exp(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j\delta_j)
$$

Error bound depends on $\delta_i$ resolution. **Hierarchical sampling** coarse 64 + fine 128 uniform then importance resample according to $w_i = T_i(1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$.

### 2.2 Positional Encoding and Frequency Bias

MLPs exhibit *spectral bias* toward low frequencies. Positional encoding $\gamma: \mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2L}$ lifts coordinate to higher-dimensional Fourier basis enabling high-frequency fitting. Tancik et al. showed $\gamma$ corresponds to stationary NTK.

Mip-NeRF's Integrated Positional Encoding instead encodes Gaussian approximating conical frustum region:

$$
\gamma_{IPE}(\mu,\Sigma)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)}[\gamma(x)] = [\sin(\mu)\exp(-\frac12 \mathrm{diag}(\Sigma)), ...]
$$

closed-form via moment-generating function of Gaussian.

### 2.3 Spherical Harmonics (SH)

SH $\{Y_l^m: S^2\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\}$ form orthonormal basis for functions on sphere, analogous to Fourier series:

$$
f(d;c)=\sum_{l=0}^L\sum_{m=-l}^l c_l^m Y_l^m(d) \quad [9]
$$

Properties:

- **Orthonormality**: $\int_{S^2} Y_l^m Y_{l'}^{m'} = \delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}$
- **Rotation invariance**: rotating $f$ corresponds to linear transform on coefficients per band
- Degree-0 = diffuse, degree-1 = linear, degree-2 = quadratic specular quad, degree-3 = 16 coefficients in 3DGS (48 total for RGB)

Plenoxels [7] uses 2nd degree SH (9 per channel), PlenOctrees [10] same, 3DGS [2] uses 3rd degree.

```python
# Python: Differentiable volume rendering quadrature (NeRF/Mip-NeRF core)
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def volume_render(sigma, rgb, t_vals, white_bg=False):
    # sigma: [N_rays, N_samples], rgb: [N_rays, N_samples, 3], t_vals: [N_samples]
    delta = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]  # [N-1]
    delta = torch.cat([delta, torch.tensor([1e10], device=delta.device).expand(delta[..., :1].shape)], dim=-1)
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)  # [N_rays, N_samples]
    # transmittance: cumulative product of (1-alpha)
    trans = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([torch.ones((alpha.shape[0],1), device=alpha.device), 1.-alpha+1e-10], -1), -1)[:, :-1]
    weights = alpha * trans  # [N_rays, N_samples]
    comp_rgb = torch.sum(weights[..., None]*rgb, dim=-2)
    acc = torch.sum(weights, dim=-1)
    if white_bg:
        comp_rgb = comp_rgb + (1.-acc[...,None])
    return comp_rgb, weights, acc

def integrated_positional_encoding(mu, diag_cov, L=10):
    # mu: [...,3], diag_cov: [...,3] - Gaussian mean and diagonal cov for conical frustum approximation
    # expectation of sin/cos under Gaussian: sin(mu)*exp(-0.5*var), cos similarly
    x = []
    for k in range(L):
        freq = 2**k
        # variance scales with freq^2 * cov
        var_scaled = (freq**2) * diag_cov
        exp_atten = torch.exp(-0.5*var_scaled)  # [...,3]
        x.append(torch.sin(freq*mu)*exp_atten)
        x.append(torch.cos(freq*mu)*exp_atten)
    return torch.cat(x, dim=-1)
```

struct Gaussian3D {
    mu: [f32; 3],
    scale: [f32; 3], // log-scale
    quat: [f32; 4],   // unit quaternion rotation
    sh_coeffs: [[f32; 3]; 16], // 16*3 for degree 3

impl Gaussian3D {
    fn covariance(&self) -> [[f32; 3]; 3] {
        // Σ = R S S^T R^T, ensure PSD
        let s = self.scale.map(|l| l.exp());
        let diag = [[s[0]*s[0], 0.0, 0.0],[0.0, s[1]*s[1],0.0],[0.0,0.0,s[2]*s[2]]];
        let r = quat_to_mat(self.quat);
        // R * diag * R^T
        mat3_mul_mat3_transpose(&mat3_mul(&r, &diag), &r)
    }
    fn project_to_2d(&self, view: &ViewMat) -> ([f32;2], [[f32;2];2]) {
        let cov3 = self.covariance();
        // W = world->camera, J = proj Jacobian (approx affine per Zwicker EWA)
        let jw = mat3_mul(&view.jacobian, &view.world);
        let cov2d_upper = {
            let tmp = mat3_mul(&jw, &cov3);
            mat3_mul_mat3_transpose(&tmp, &jw)
        };

        let cov2d = [[cov2d_upper[0][0], cov2d_upper[0][1]],
                     [cov2d_upper[1][0], cov2d_upper[1][1]]];
        let mu2d = view.project(self.mu);
        (mu2d, cov2d)
    }
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Differentiable Volume Rendering: From RTE to Quadrature

*Irwin & Lane* volume rendering descends from radiative transfer equation (RTE) ignoring scattering:

$$
\frac{dL}{ds} = -\sigma_a L + \sigma_e
$$

Solution leads to exponential transmittance. NeRF identifies $\sigma(x)$ as differential extinction probability and $c(x,d)$ as emitted radiance. Key insight in Mildenhall et al. [1]: **volume rendering is differentiable**, so $\nabla_\theta$ flows through quadrature to MLP weights requiring only image supervision.

*Cone supersampling* alternative: brute-force $K$ rays per pixel $K\times$ cost. Mip-NeRF instead integrates over cone footprint statistics: assume pixel's cone from aperture $o$ divergence $\dot r$, at distance $t$ radius $\propto t$. Mean and covariance of conical frustum intersected with interval $[t_0,t_1]$ approximated as Gaussian $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$ with closed-form EWA filter theory [12].

Table: sampling strategies

| Method | Ray representation | Encoding | Samples/ray | Cost multiplier | Anti-alias metric |
|--------|-------------------|----------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| NeRF [1] | Ray $r(t)=o+td$ | $\gamma(x)$, $\gamma(d)$ | 192 | 1× | Poor multiscale |
| Mip-NeRF [3] | Cone $\Rightarrow$ Gaussian $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$ | IPE $\mathbb{E}[\gamma(x)]$ | 128 (single MLP 50% smaller) | 0.93× | 17% error reduction |
| Mip-NeRF 360 [5] | Contracted space $f(x)=x/||x||$ if $||x||>1$ | IPE + distortion loss |  128 + proposal 256 | 1.2× | 54% MSE reduction unbounded |
| Zip-NeRF [11] | Pre-filtered grid pyramids + IPE | Multiscale feature anti-alias | 64 | 0.5× | SOTA alias-free |

> **Theorem (IPE Exactness):** For $x\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$ diagonal, $\mathbb{E}[\sin(2^k x)]=\sin(2^k \mu)\exp(-0.5 4^k \Sigma)$. Proof via characteristic function of Gaussian $\varphi(t)=\exp(i\mu t -0.5\Sigma t^2)$ and Euler $\sin=\Im \exp$.

This yields low-pass filtering: high frequencies attenuated proportionally to footprint variance, mimicking mipmap trilinear interpolation but continuous.

### 4.2 Spherical Harmonics, Integrated Directional Encoding, and Specularity

View-dependent color encodes non-Lambertian materials: specular highlight moves with viewpoint, modeled as function $f: S^2\rightarrow RGB$.

Early NeRF feeds direction $d$ encoded $\gamma(d)$ into late MLP branch (8 layers geometry, 1 layer color) to disentangle shape-radiance ambiguity [1]. Plenoxels [7] shows MLP unnecessary: SH coefficients stored per voxel suffice when optimized with total variation regularization $\mathcal L_{TV}=\lambda\sum_i||\nabla \sigma_i||_1$.

Ref-NeRF [8] extends to *structured* appearance: replace radiance with outgoing radiance decomposition:

$$
c(o,d,n) = a + s \cdot \text{IDE}(\omega_r,\kappa)
$$

where $\omega_r=2 (d\cdot n)n - d$ reflection direction, $\kappa = 1/\rho$ roughness, IDE is integrated directional encoding over vMF distribution:

$$
\text{IDE}_l^m(\omega_r,\kappa)=A_l(\kappa) Y_l^m(\omega_r),\quad A_l(\kappa)\approx\exp(-l(l+1)/2\kappa)
$$

Attenuation $A_l$ suppresses high-order SH for rough materials, yielding physically-based inductive bias.

3DGS inherits SH directly: each Gaussian stores 48 floats (16 per channel). Rendering time: evaluate $Y_l^m(d)$ once per Gaussian per view (since view direction varying slowly across Gaussian support approximated as direction from camera center to Gaussian mean), then $\alpha$-blend.

### 4.3 3D Gaussian Splatting: Covariance, Projection, Densification

Core primitive (Kerbl et al. 2023) [2]:

$$
G(x)=\exp(-\frac12 (x-\mu)^T\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)),\quad \alpha_{3D}(x)=o \cdot G(x)
$$

Factorization ensures PSD during SGD: $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$ where $S=\mathrm{diag}(s)$, $s\in\mathbb{R}^3_+$ parameterized via $\exp$, $R$ from quaternion $q\in\mathbb{R}^4$ normalized $R(q)$ differentiable.

Projection to screen: EWA splatting [12] Zwicker et al. 2001: viewing transform $W$, projection Jacobian $J$ (affine approximation of perspective division). Then:

$$
\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T \quad 2\times2 \text{ covariance in screen space}
$$

Pixel alpha: evaluate 2D Gaussian convolved with pixel filter (low-pass disc approximated as Gaussian $\sigma=0.3$):

$$
\alpha_{2D}(u)=o \exp(-\frac12 (u-\mu_{2D})^T {\Sigma'}^{-1}(u-\mu_{2D}))
$$

Color blending: sort Gaussians front-to-back per 16×16 tile, maintain $T_i$:

$$
C(u)=\sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i c_i,\quad T_i=\prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)
$$

Tile rasterizer: radix sort of depth keys on GPU, 100+ FPS.

Optimization: initialize from SfM points (COLMAP) or random; loss $ \mathcal L = (1-\lambda) \mathcal L_1 + \lambda\mathcal L_{D-SSIM}$. Densification heuristic every 100 iterations:

- **Split**: if $\|\nabla_\mu \mathcal L\| > \tau_{pos}$ and $\|s\| > \tau_{scale}$ (large Gaussian over-reconstructs), split into 2 with scale/1.6.
- **Clone**: if $\|\nabla_\mu \mathcal L\| > \tau_{pos}$ and $\|s\|\le\tau_{scale}$ (under-reconstruction hole), clone shifting along gradient.
- **Prune**: if $o < \epsilon$ or $\|S\|$ too large world-space.

Result: adaptive control from ~100k → ~3M primitives scene-dependent.

### 4.4 Acceleration Structures: Hash Grids, Sparse Voxels, and Proposal Networks

Instant-NGP's multiresolution hash encoding [6] revolution: $L=16$ levels, each resolution $N_l=\lfloor N_{min} b^l\rfloor$, hash table size $2^{19}$, feature dim 2 per entry. Lookup: $h(x)=\bigoplus_i x_i \pi_i \mod T$, trilinear interpolation within level, concatenated $32$-dim fed to MLP. Collisions resolved by gradient averaging - multilayer perceptron disambiguates because collisions improbable at all levels simultaneously.

Plenoxels [7] contribution: prove neural net dispensable - same volume rendering formula, optimization via RMSProp with TV regularization $\lambda_{TV}=5e-4$ piecewise decays resolution $256^3\rightarrow512^3$ pruning empty voxels $>0.5\sigma$ threshold.

Mip-NeRF 360 proposal MLP: tiny 4-layer 256-wide predicts density for importance sampling, distilled online via histogram loss matching NeRF weights; parameterizes unbounded by contraction:

$$
\text{contract}(x)=\begin{cases}x & ||x||\le1\\ (2-1/||x||) \frac{x}{||x||} & ||x||>1\end{cases}
$$

distortion regularizer:

$$
\mathcal L_{dist}= \sum_i w_i w_j |t_i-t_j| + \frac13\sum_i w_i^2 (s_i) \quad\text{penalizes bumpy floaters}
$$

### 4.5 Systems Synthesis: Why Gaussians Win Real-Time

Differentiation path lengths:

- **NeRF**: Forward: positional encoding → 8×256 MLP → density, then concat dir → 128-wide → RGB → quadrature → loss. Backward through same. ~100 TFLOP/image.
- **3DGS**: Forward: project mean $\mu_{2D}=K W \mu$, cov2d, SH evaluate → tile bin → sort → $\alpha$-blend. Fully rasterization, no MLP, CUDA kernels memory coalesced. ~5 GFLOP/image.

Tradeoff: 3DGS mesh extraction harder than marching cubes on density field because Gaussians semi-transparent not level set; SuGar and 3DGSR address via SDF alignment regularizer.

### 5.2 Complexity Proofs

**Lemma (Gaussian Projection PSD Preservation):** If $\Sigma$ PD, $W$ full rank, $J$ non-singular $2\times3$ Jacobian, then $\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$ PSD. Proof: for any $v\in\mathbb{R}^2$, $v^T\Sigma' v = (J^T v)^T W\Sigma W^T (J^T v) = y^T\Sigma y \ge0$.

**Lemma (Sorting Invariant for $\alpha$-blending):** If Gaussians sorted ascending depth $z_i$, $\alpha_i \in[0,1]$, then operator $C=\sum T_i\alpha_i c_i$ is front-to-back volume compositing approximating radiative transfer with piecewise-constant extinction matching EWA kernel effective $\sigma_i = -\log(1-\alpha_i)/\delta_i$. Induction via transmittance recurrence.

**Theorem (SH Rotation Capacity):** Degree-$L$ SH cannot represent frequencies higher than $L$ at angular frequency $f_{cut}=L/(2\pi)$. For mirror specular lobe width $\theta_{lobe}< \pi/L$, approximation error $\epsilon \ge 1-\cos^{L}(\theta)$. Thus degree-3 caps glossy but not mirror. Ref-NeRF's IDE with $\kappa\rightarrow\infty$, $A_l\rightarrow1$ recovers mirror asymptotically with $L=4$ but requires roughness encoding.

## 6 Limitations

1. **No guarantee of geometry**: NeRF & 3DGS optimize photometric loss; density may place semi-transparent floaters explaining view-dependent effects rather than correct surface, especially sparse views. Distortion regularizer [5] and SuGar SDF regularizer mitigate but not eliminate [2].

2. **SH limited specular**: Degree-3 SH approximates smooth specular but fails on mirror reflections requiring $L>10$ ($\ge121$ coeffs) impractical. Hybrid environment map or Ref-NeRF reflection direction parameterization needed.

3. **Aliasing persistence**: Even Mip-NeRF IPE Gaussian approximation breaks for highly elongated frustums (grazing angles). Zip-NeRF's multi-sample feature down-weighting more accurate but still approximate. 3DGS antialiasing via pixel filtering insufficient for subpixel thin structures; Mip-Splatting [13] introduces 3D smoothing + 2D Mip filter.

4. **Memory unbounded**: 3DGS densifies to 5-8M Gaussians on large city-scale scenes (Mill19), 800MB VRAM plus optimizer 3.2GB, exceeding consumer GPU. Scaffold-GS anchoring reduces 5×.

5. **No relighting**: Plenoptic function bakes illumination; moving light source invalidates radiance. Intrinsic decomposition NeRF (NeRD, NeRFactor) requires BRDF priors.

6. **Hash collisions**: Instant-NGP hash table collisions cause transient speckles early training; although averaged out, worst-case adversarial view pose may activate collision pattern leading to floaters.

7. **Contraction distortion**: Mip-NeRF 360 contraction $f(x)$ non-isometric; straight lines at infinity curve, depth map distorted for planning tasks.

8. **Training instability**: Gaussian covariance $S$ exponential may explode if learning rate $>1e-3$; opacity reset every 3k iterations heuristic brittle, requires careful scheduling $\lambda_{pos}=1.6e-4\rightarrow 1.6e-6$.

9. **Evaluation metrics**: PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS correlate poorly with human perception of flickering temporal aliasing in video trajectories, not captured by still-image metrics.

10. **License & data**: COLMAP SfM pre-process required for 3DGS initialization; fails on textureless surfaces (white walls), causing poor Gaussians distribution - unlike random initialization which NeRF tolerates.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We traced trajectory from implicit MLP volume $F_\Theta(x,d)$ to explicit anisotropic primitive soup $\{( \mu_i,\Sigma_i,o_i,\text{SH}_i)\}$, showing shared foundation of differentiable volume rendering and spherical harmonics view-dependence. Key insight: **MLP was scaffolding, not essence**; radiance field's power resides in differentiable ray-marching, positional basis, and regularized sparse optimization [7]. Mip-NeRF's conical frustum statistics formalize anti-aliasing as low-pass filtering of encoding; Instant-NGP reframes frequency encoding as trainable hash lattice $O(1)$. 3DGS finally eliminates ray-marching by observing that EWA splatting of Gaussians approximates volume rendering sufficiently for opaque surfaces while enabling $>100$ FPS via hardware rasterization pipeline.

Future directions converge:

- *Unified hybrid*: Hash-encoded proposal field guiding Gaussian densification (3DGS + Zip-NeRF)
- *Physics-based Gaussian*: replace SH with microfacet BRDF lobe $f_r = DFG/4(N\cdot L)(N\cdot V)$ lit by environment map for relighting
- *Temporal 4D Gaussians*: 4D rotor formulation $G(x,y,z,t)$ for dynamic scenes (4D-Rotor GS)
- *Generative priors*: diffusion model regularizing under-observed views using score distillation sampling (DreamFusion → NerfDiffusion)
- *Level-of-detail*: octree Gaussian hierarchies with $\Sigma$ scale selection per distance mimicking mipmaps, formalized as scale-space scale $t$ diffusion

The field moves toward *volume rendering without volumes* – explicit primitives rasterized differentiably, bridging classical point-based graphics (Levoy & Whitted 1985) with learned photorealism, achieving real-time photometric Turing test on consumer hardware.
