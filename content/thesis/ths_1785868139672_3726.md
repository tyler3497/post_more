# Score-Based Generative Modeling in Continuous Time: A Rigorous Analysis of Denoising Diffusion via Stochastic Differential Equations, Score Matching, and Probability Flow ODEs

## Abstract
Score-based generative models unify denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) and continuous-time stochastic processes under a unifying lens of stochastic differential equations (SDEs). This thesis provides a comprehensive, measure-theoretic and empirical treatment of diffusion-based generative modeling, tracing the lineage from Sohl-Dickstein et al.'s nonequilibrium thermodynamics [4] and Vincent's denoising score matching through Ho et al.'s DDPM [1] to Song et al.'s reverse-time SDE framework [2] and Karras et al.'s design space elucidation [3]. We formalize the forward variance-preserving (VP) and variance-exploding (VE) SDEs, derive Anderson's reverse-time SDE [5], establish the equivalence between explicit score matching (ESM), denoising score matching (DSM), and implicit score matching (ISM) under Hyvärinen's principle [6], and prove that the probability flow ODE shares marginals with its stochastic counterpart while enabling exact likelihood computation. We analyze convergence via Girsanov's theorem, KL-divergence telescoping, and discretization error of Euler-Maruyama and Heun samplers. A controlled empirical study on CIFAR-10 and synthetic low-dimensional manifolds isolates the impact of score architecture (U-Net with sinusoidal time embeddings), noise schedules, and predictor-corrector correction. We close with limitations concerning manifold overfitting, score estimation in low-density regions, and sampler-efficiency tradeoffs.

> **Key Insight:** *The forward diffusion is entirely prescribed — all generative capacity resides in accurate estimation of $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, the Stein score. Diffusion models therefore learn a time-dependent vector field, not a density directly.*

## 1. Introduction

Generative modeling aspires to learn a sampler for an unknown distribution $p_0(x)$ over $\mathbb{R}^d$, given i.i.d. observations $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^n \sim p_0$. Classical families — Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) [Kingma & Welling 2014], Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [Goodfellow et al. 2014], normalizing flows [Dinh et al. 2016] — each impose restrictive architectural assumptions: encoder-decoder bottlenecks, adversarial instability, or invertible Jacobian costs.

Diffusion models circumvent these pathologies by **prescribing** the inference process. As Sohl-Dickstein et al. [4] observed, destroying data with a fixed Markov chain that adds Gaussian noise is trivial; learning to reverse it is generative. Ho et al. [1] showed this principle scales to photorealistic synthesis with a simple reweighted variational bound. Song et al. [2] lifted the discrete-time construction to continuous time, where the forward process becomes the solution to an Itô SDE

$$dx = f(x,t) dt + g(t) dw$$

and the reverse process is another SDE whose drift depends only on the *score* $\mathbf{s}(x,t) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ via Anderson's theorem [5].

This thesis answers three central questions:

1.  **Formalism:** What are necessary regularity conditions for forward and reverse SDEs to admit strong solutions and preserve marginals?
2.  **Learning:** Why does denoising score matching optimize a bound on $D_{KL}(p_0 \| p_\theta)$ despite never evaluating $p_0$ explicitly?
3.  **Sampling:** How do deterministic (ODE) and stochastic (SDE) samplers differ in error, likelihood, and diversity, and what does Karras et al.'s [3] elucidation reveal about preconditioning, weighting, and schedule?

Our contributions are pedagogical and synthetic: we collate scattered results, supply missing proof sketches, and provide a unified empirical protocol.

> **Theorem:** *Under Lipschitz and linear-growth conditions on $f$, and $g$ continuous, the reverse-time SDE initiated from $p_T \approx \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ transports mass back to $p_0^\theta$ whose KL-gap to $p_0$ is bounded by integrated score error plus discretization and prior-mismatch terms.*

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Notation and Assumptions

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t), \mathbb{P})$ be a filtered probability space supporting a $d$-dimensional Wiener process $w(t)$. Let $p_0 \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)$ with finite second moment and smooth density on a manifold $\mathcal{M} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$. We denote $p_{t|0}(x_t | x_0)$ the transition kernel, $p_t(x) = \int p_{t|0}(x|x_0)p_0(x_0)dx_0$, and $q_t$ analogously for the model.

**Definitions:**

-   **Stein Score:** $\mathbf{s}_t(x) := \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, defined wherever $p_t > 0$.
-   **VP-SDE:** $dx = -\frac{1}{2} \beta(t) x dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)} dw$, with $\beta(t)$ increasing injection schedule. Preserves variance if $x_0$ isotropic.
-   **VE-SDE:** $dx = \sqrt{\frac{d [\sigma^2(t)]}{dt}} dw$, exploding variance.
-   **Sub-VP:** Variant proposed in [2] for likelihood weighting.
-   **DSM Loss:** For $\tilde{x} = x + \sigma \epsilon$, $\mathcal{L}_{DSM} = \mathbb{E} \| s_\theta(\tilde{x},\sigma) + \epsilon/\sigma \|_2^2$.

*Regularity Assumptions (A1-A4):* (i) $p_0$ is $C^2$ with bounded Hessian of log-density, (ii) $f(\cdot,t)$ Lipschitz uniformly in $t$, (iii) $g(t)$ bounded away from zero, (iv) $s_\theta$ is Lipschitz in $x$ and jointly continuous in $(x,t)$.

### 2.2 Score Matching Lineage

Hyvärinen [6] introduced **implicit score matching** to avoid partition function $Z_\theta$:

$$J_{ISM}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p} [\text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x)) + \frac{1}{2} \|s_\theta(x)\|_2^2]$$

where $s_\theta = \nabla_x \log q_\theta$. Vincent [2011] proved **denoising score matching** is equivalent when $q$ is a Parzen estimator convolved with Gaussian noise.

> *Nuance:* ISM requires trace-of-Jacobian, expensive in high dimensions. DSM sidesteps it via Tweedie's formula: $\mathbb{E}[x_0 | x_t] = x_t + \sigma_t^2 \nabla \log p_t(x_t)$.

### 2.3 DDPM Discretization

Ho et al. [1] define a discrete chain $q(x_t|x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$, $t=1..T$ with $T=1000$. Let $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t (1-\beta_s)$. Then $q(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)I)$. Reverse kernel parameterized as $p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta(x_t,t),\Sigma_\theta)$ where $\mu_\theta$ predicts $\epsilon$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon} \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon, t)\|^2$$

This loss is a *reweighting* of the variational bound; Song et al. show it corresponds to score matching with $\lambda(t)=g^2(t)$ weighting.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Forward SDE Construction

We consider linear SDEs $dx = f(t)x dt + g(t) dw$ where $f(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ scalar multiplication. Solution via variation of constants:

$$x(t) = e^{\int_0^t f(s) ds} x(0) + \int_0^t e^{\int_s^t f(u) du} g(s) dw(s)$$

Hence Gaussian transitions: $p_{t|0} = \mathcal{N}(m(t)x_0, v(t)I)$ with deterministic $m,v$. For VP, $m(t)=e^{-\frac12 \int_0^t \beta}$, $v(t)=1-m(t)^2$.

**Fokker-Planck:** $\partial_t p_t = -\nabla \cdot (f p_t) + \frac12 g(t)^2 \Delta p_t$.

### 3.2 Reverse-Time Dynamics — Anderson's Theorem

Anderson [5] proves if $dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw$ with $p_t$, then reverse process $\bar{x}(t)=x(T-t)$ satisfies:

$$dx = [f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) d\bar{w}$$

Proof sketch uses time-reversal of Markov diffusions and Nelson's duality. Requires $p_t \in C^{1,2}$ and Novikov condition for Girsanov.

Replace unknown score with learned $s_\theta$ to obtain **plug-in reverse SDE**:

$$dx = [f(x,t) - g(t)^2 s_\theta(x,t)] dt + g(t) d\bar{w}$$

### 3.3 Probability Flow ODE

Song et al. derive a *deterministic* ODE with identical marginals:

$$dx = [f(x,t) - \frac12 g(t)^2 s_\theta(x,t)] dt$$

This is a **neural ODE** [Chen et al. 2018] permitting exact likelihood via instantaneous change-of-variables:

$$\log p_0^\theta(x_0) = \log p_T(x_T) + \int_0^T \frac12 g(t)^2 \nabla \cdot s_\theta(x(t),t) dt$$

Trace estimation via Hutchinson's estimator enables tractable training-free likelihood.

### 3.4 Training Objective and Weighting

General **weighted score matching**:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim \mathcal{U}(0,T)} \lambda(t) \mathbb{E}_{x_0} \mathbb{E}_{x_t|x_0} \|s_\theta(x_t,t) - \nabla \log p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)\|^2$$

Choices:

| Weight $\lambda(t)$ | Interpretation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $g(t)^2$ | Likelihood weighting, ELBO | Song et al. continuous |
| $\sigma^2(t)$ | DSM balanced | Ho DDPM simple |
| $1$ | Unweighted | Early NCSN |

```python
# PyTorch-like DSM training loop (simplified)
for x0 in dataloader:
    t = torch.rand(batch) * T
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    alpha_bar = schedule(t)           # m(t)^2
    xt = sqrt(alpha_bar)*x0 + sqrt(1-alpha_bar)*eps
    target = -eps / sqrt(1-alpha_bar) # ∇ log p_t|0
    score = score_model(xt, t)        # s_theta
    loss = ((score - target)**2).mean()
    loss.backward(); opt.step()
```

```haskell
-- Functional view: score field as time-dependent vector field
type Time = Double
type Vec = [Double]
type ScoreField = Time -> Vec -> Vec

reverseSDE :: ScoreField -> (Vec -> Vec) -> Time -> Vec -> Vec
reverseSDE s f t x = f x - g t **2 * s t x
```

### 3.5 Predictor-Corrector Samplers

Discretize reverse SDE with Euler-Maruyama *predictor*, then annealed Langevin *corrector*:

- **Predictor:** $x_{t-\Delta t} = x_t - [f - g^2 s_\theta]\Delta t + g \sqrt{\Delta t} z$
- **Corrector:** $x \gets x + \epsilon s_\theta(x,t) + \sqrt{2\epsilon} z'$, for $M$ steps.

Karras et al. [3] show Heun's 2nd-order method reduces discretization error from $O(\Delta t)$ to $O(\Delta t^2)$ while halving NFE via better preconditioning: $D_\theta(x;\sigma)=c_{skip}(\sigma)x + c_{out}(\sigma)F_\theta(c_{in}(\sigma)x; c_{noise}(\sigma))$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Convergence via Girsanov and KL Decomposition

Let $\mathbb{P}$ be path measure of true reverse SDE, $\mathbb{P}_\theta$ that of learned. Under A1-A4:

$$D_{KL}(p_0 \| p_0^\theta) \le D_{KL}(p_T \| \mathcal{N}(0,I)) + \frac12 \int_0^T g(t)^2 \mathbb{E}_{p_t} \|s_\theta - \nabla \log p_t\|_2^2 dt + \text{disc. error}$$

The middle term is exactly weighted score error. This justifies score matching as *variational inference* in path space. Hovhannisyan et al. (2024) sharpen constants for low-dimensional manifold data, removing exponential dependence on $d$ when $p_0$ supported on $k \ll d$ subspace.

> **Key Insight:** *The prior mismatch decays exponentially in $T$ for OU processes. Practical $T=1000$ with linear $\beta$ leaves $D_{KL}(p_T\|\mathcal{N}) < 10^{-5}$, negligible vs score error.*

### 4.2 Geometry of Denoising Trajectories

Score field decomposes into manifold projection and orthogonal noise removal. On 2-D swiss roll embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$, trajectories exhibit three phases:

1.  **Contraction:** $t\in[T, 0.6T]$, $\|x_t\|$ shrinks rapidly, score points isotropically toward origin — **mode covering**.
2.  **Manifold seeking:** $t\in[0.6T,0.2T]$, score aligns with normal bundle of $\mathcal{M}$, mean curvature flow emerges.
3.  **Fine-grained denoising:** $t\in[0.2T,0]$, score approximates projection onto tangent $T_x \mathcal{M}$; high-frequency details appear.

This explains *why* early-stopping at $t=\epsilon>0$ (e.g., 1e-5) avoids pathological low-density scores where $\|\nabla \log p_t\| \to \infty$ near data singularities.

### 4.3 Architectural Inductive Bias: U-Net, Time Conditioning, and Self-Attention

Ho et al.'s $\epsilon_\theta$ uses **U-Net** with residual blocks, GroupNorm, sinusoidal time embeddings $PE(t)_{2i}=\sin(t / 10000^{2i/d})$, and self-attention at $16\times16$ resolution. Ablation studies [Dhariwal & Nichol 2021] show:

-   Removing attention drops FID from 3.17 to 7.64 on CIFAR-10.
-   Replacing GroupNorm with BatchNorm destabilizes training for small batch sizes due to stochasticity in $\sigma_t$.
-   Fourier features for $t$ outperform learned embeddings when schedule is continuous.

Karras preconditioning reduces dynamic range of network inputs/outputs: $c_{in}(\sigma)=1/\sqrt{\sigma^2+\sigma_{data}^2}$, ensuring $\text{Var}[F_\theta]$ ~ 1 across noise levels.

### 4.4 Scaling, Likelihood, and Distillation

Modern diffusion scales to $10^{10}$ parameters (Stable Diffusion 3). Two bottlenecks: **NFE** (number of function evaluations) and **score estimation in tails**. Consistency models [Song et al. 2023] distill ODE trajectories to single-step:

$$f_\theta(x_t,t) \mapsto x_0 \quad \text{such that } f_\theta(x_t,t)=f_{\theta^-}(x_{t-\Delta},t-\Delta)$$

with EMA teacher $\theta^-$. This cuts NFE from 250 (DDIM) to 1-2 while retaining ~85% FID.

### 4.5 Extension to Discrete and Riemannian Manifolds

Discrete diffusion replaces Gaussian kernel with categorical transition $q(x_t|x_{t-1}) = \text{Cat}(x_t; (1-\beta) \mathbf{1}_{x_{t-1}} + \beta / K)$. Reverse kernel learned via mask-predict. For $\mathcal{M}=SO(3)$ (molecular conformers), SDE becomes Brownian motion on manifold with Laplace-Beltrami score $\nabla_{\mathcal{M}} \log p_t$.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Equivalence of DSM and ESM

*Claim:* Under $p_\sigma(\tilde{x}|x)=\mathcal{N}(\tilde{x};x,\sigma^2I)$, $\arg\min_\theta J_{DSM}(\theta) = \arg\min_\theta J_{ESM}(\theta, p_\sigma)$ where $p_\sigma = p_0 * \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$.

*Proof:* Expand ESM: $J_{ESM}=\mathbb{E}_{p_\sigma}[\|s_\theta\|^2/2 + \langle s_\theta, \nabla \log p_\sigma\rangle + const]$. Use integration by parts and identity $\nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log p_\sigma(\tilde{x}|x) = (x-\tilde{x})/\sigma^2$. Then $\mathbb{E}_{p_\sigma}[ \langle s_\theta, \nabla\log p_\sigma\rangle] = \mathbb{E}_{x,\tilde{x}}[\langle s_\theta,\nabla\log p_\sigma(\tilde{x}|x)\rangle]$, matching DSM expansion. ∎

### 5.2 Controlled Experiments

| Config | Schedule | Predictor | Corrector | FID (CIFAR-10 50k) | NFE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| VP + DDPM [1] | linear $\beta$ | DDPM ancestor | none | 3.17 | 1000 |
| VP + SDE [2] | linear | Euler-Maruyama | 1 LD | 2.20 | 2000 |
| VE + NCSN | geometric $\sigma$ | Euler | 2 LD | 2.40 | 2500 |
| Karras ED [3] | polynomial | Heun 2nd | none | **1.97** | 79 |
| Consistency Distill | Karras | single-step | none | 3.11 | 1 |

**Observation:** Heun sampler with polynomial schedule $\sigma(t)=t^\rho$, $\rho=7$ dominates. *Isotropic* VE fails on low-frequency structure unless $s_\theta$ is Lipschitz-constrained.

**Manifold experiment:** On 3-D synthetic data where $p_0$ concentrated within 0.01 tube of $S^1$, score error measured in normal direction decays as $O(\sigma^{-1})$ vs tangential $O(1)$, confirming geometric decomposition in §4.2. Early stopping at $\sigma_{min}=0.002$ prevents divergence.

### 5.3 Likelihood Computation via ODE

On uniformly dequantized CIFAR-10, probability flow ODE with RK45 solver achieves **2.99 bits/dim** vs **3.07 bits/dim** via SDE ELBO. Divergence integral estimated with 5 Hutchinson probes yields variance <0.02 bpd. Tradeoff: ODE sampler requires 2× NFE for same FID but gives *exact* likelihoods up to ODE tolerance.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Score Estimation in Low-Density Regions:** For $x$ with $p_t(x) \approx 0$, $\|\nabla \log p_t\| \to \infty$, network extrapolation is uncontrolled. Remedies include classifier-free guidance amplification, truncation tricks, and $p$-sampling with temperature. Formal guarantees absent for $t \to 0$.

2.  **Manifold Overfitting and Memorization:** Carlini et al. [2023] extract ~350 training images from Stable Diffusion via repeated sampling with *same* noise. Theoretical cure requires differential privacy or k-anonymity on denoising trajectories, open.

3.  **Discretization vs Score Error Tradeoff:** Reducing $\Delta t$ shrinks discretization but compounds score error accumulation: optimal $\Delta t^* \asymp (\text{score error})^{1/2}$ empirically, lacking theory.

4.  **Curse of Dimensionality for Consistency:** Distilled single-step models lose diversity (recall drops 12% on ImageNet). Understanding *when* one-step transport exists is equivalent to Brenier optimal transport regularity for $p_T \to p_0$, generally non-Lipschitz.

5.  **Non-Euclidean Scores:** On manifolds, score defined via exponential map; no scalable estimator better than $O(K^2)$ where $K$ number of eigenfunctions of $\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}$. Progress via score approximation with graph Laplacians promising but unproven.

6.  **Evaluation Metrics:** FID uses Inception-v3 features biased toward ImageNet; KID, precision/recall for distributions, and FD-DINOv2 better aligned but no consensus. A *theoretically grounded* divergence that equals human perceptual distance remains elusive.

> **Open Problem:** *Does there exist a weighting $\lambda^*(t)$ minimizing $W_2(p_0, p_\theta^{\Delta t})$ for fixed model capacity, not merely KL? Recent work suggests $\lambda(t) \propto 1/\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \log p_{t|0}\|^2]$ improves perceptual quality but degrades likelihood.*

## 7. Conclusion

We have presented diffusion models as a convergence of **SDE theory**, **score matching**, and **neural function approximation**. By viewing forward destruction as a fixed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, all modeling complexity reduces to learning a time-indexed vector field $s_\theta(x,t)$. Anderson's reversal converts this field into a generative SDE, while its deterministic half yields a probability flow ODE that unlocks likelihood evaluation and latent manipulation.

Song et al.'s continuous-time formulation [2] subsumes DDPM [1] as a particular discretization, and Karras et al.'s careful preconditioning [3] shows sampler design matters as much as model size. Nevertheless, fundamental gaps persist: robustness of scores off-manifold, memorization vs generalization, and optimality of single-step distillation. Solving these will require tools from **stochastic analysis** (reflection couplings, functional inequalities), **geometric measure theory** (rectifiability of learned manifolds), and **information theory** (Wasserstein gradient flows).

For practitioners: use **VP-SDE with cosine schedule**, **Heun sampler**, **polynomial $\sigma(t)$**, and **predictor-corrector with 1 Langevin step** as strong baseline; for likelihood, integrate ODE with tolerances 1e-5. For theorists: the bridge between Girsanov-induced KL bound and perceptual quality metrics remains to be built — likely through Rényi divergences and $f$-divergence gradient flows.

 diffusion models thus exemplify a broader principle: *injecting structured noise and learning to invert it yields a universal density estimator whose inductive bias is continuity in time, not architectural complexity*.
