# Verified Superoptimization via Equality Saturation: E-Graph Rewriting, Souper Enumeration, and Alive2 SMT Verification for Cranelift and LLVM Peephole Optimizations

## Abstract
We present a unified account of **verified superoptimization** that combines *equality saturation* over **e-graphs**, **CEGIS-driven synthesis** in Souper, and *bounded translation validation* in **Alive2** to derive provably correct peephole optimizations for LLVM and Cranelift. Central to our contribution is the reconstruction of congruence closure with deferred *rebuilding* from egg [1], the integration of e-class analyses for cost and semantics, and the encoding of LLVM IR refinement via Alive2 IR and Z3 SMT queries [3]. We demonstrate how **ISLE** [6] declarative lowering in Cranelift enables synthesis-to-production deployment while **VeriISLE** [8] proves ISA-specific correctness for aarch64 Wasm lowering. Evaluated against 47 latent LLVM bugs found by Alive2 and 85k Souper rewrites driving 4.4% code-size reduction in Clang, we argue equality saturation with verification closes the performance–correctness gap that hand-written InstCombine cannot. Our framework treats *superoptimization* not as exhaustive search but as a *saturation-closed* term rewriting system modulo theories, verified end-to-end.

## 1 Introduction

Modern compilers invest >20k lines of C++ in `InstCombine` alone [9], yet still miss 7,900 distinct optimizations discoverable by solver-aided search [2]. The paradox is familiar: **peephole optimizations** are local, but interactions are global; correctness depends on subtle semantics of *poison*, *undef*, *nuw/nsw*; and ordering heuristics cause non-confluence and oscillation.

We ask: *can we replace heuristic lists with a verified, saturating rewrite system that explores all equivalent programs simultaneously and extracts the cheapest provably correct one?*

Inspired by Tate et al.'s *equality saturation* and operationalized by Willsey et al. in **egg** [1], our answer is affirmative.

> **Theorem 1 (Confluence of Saturation):** Let `R` be a set of sound rewrites over language `L`. If equality saturation over e-graph `G` reaches fixpoint `G*`, then any term reachable by any finite sequence of `R` is represented in `G*`. Extraction w.r.t. monotone cost is globally optimal modulo `R`.

That distinguishes EqSat from greedy rewrite pipelines that suffer from *phase-ordering*.

*Contributions*:

- **Formal linkage:** We map Souper IR to LLVM IR semantics and encode its LHS→RHS as refinement in Alive2's logic [3], establishing refinement-preserving CEGIS.
- **E-graph engineering:** We reinterpret *rebuilding* [1] as amortized congruence maintenance for compiler IRs with *e-class analyses* for constant folding, bitwidth, and poison-ness.
- **Cranelift integration:** We show ISLE term rewriting [6] subsumes egg rewrites when extended with e-graph fuel; VeriISLE's SMT verification provides processor-specific assurance [8].
- **Empirical synthesis:** We replay data showing Souper finds 85k applications in LLVM build and Alive2 verifies against 21 new memory-model bugs [4].

**Organization:** §2 background, §3 methodology, §4 deep dive (four subsections), §5 empirical/proofs, §6 limitations, §7 conclusion, references.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Superoptimization History

Superoptimization dates to Massalin 1987: enumerating instruction sequences to find optimal implementations. Modern variant *synthesizing superoptimizers* [2] use SMT solvers to prune infeasible candidates, not brute force.

Souper [2] extracts slices from LLVM IR: small SSA DAGs computing a single value (`root`) with `var` and `const` leaves, typically up to 4-5 instructions. Each candidate RHS is enumerated in its own grammar, queried for **equivalence** via *IsAlive* SMT check, and costed by LLVM's `TargetTransformInfo`.

Key IR in Souper is Hoare-like *straight-line value*: `%a:i64 = var` `%b = add %a, C`. Operand width explicit.

### 2.2 E-Graphs and Equality Saturation

An **e-graph** compactly represents congruence relation over infinite terms. E-class = set of equivalent e-nodes; e-node = `f(c1...ck)` where `ci` are e-class ids. Invariant: *congruence* — if `a ~ b` then `f(a) ~ f(b)`.

Traditional re-build of congruence after each union is `O(n^2)` heavy. **egg** contribution [1]:

- *Deferred rebuilding*: maintain worklist of dirty e-classes; amortize closure, asymptotic speedup up to 3000× reported.
- *E-class analysis*: lattice data attached to class, e.g., *constant value*, *free variables*, *provenance*; `make` and `join` hooks.

Saturation loop:

```
G ← initial program
loop:
  matches ← E-matching of LHS patterns
  for m in matches: add RHS, union(LHS_class, RHS_class)
  rebuild(G)
until timeout / saturation / fuel exhausted
extract best via DP / ILP
```

Cranelift EG RFC [7] proposes same staged fuel for mid-end optimizations beyond instruction selection.

### 2.3 LLVM IR Semantics and Alive

LLVM IR refinement is not simple equality: `%x = undef` can be refined to any value; `poison` propagates differently [9]. Alive DSL [9] first captured this: source `=>` target guarded by precondition `Pre`. Z3 distinguishes *definedness*, *poison-freedom*, *memory refinement*.

Alive2 [3] is *bounded* translation validation: encodes unbounded loops by unrolling to depth `k`. Open source, drop-in `alive2` plugins for `opt`. Crucially avoids false alarms; reported 47 bugs in LLVM unit tests [3] and 21 memory bugs in CAV'21 extension [4].

### 2.4 Cranelift, ISLE, and VeriISLE

Cranelift is Bytecode Alliance's Wasm-native compiler. Its lowering defined in **ISLE** (Instruction Selection Lowering Expressions) DSL [6]: pattern-matching constructor rules:

```isle
(rule (lower (iadd x (iconst 1))) (aarch64_add_imm x 1))
```

VeriISLE [8] extends ISLE with specs: developer annotates rule with *specification term* in ISLE spectral DSL; SMT solver proves full equivalence, modular, incremental, compatible with evolving backend — first formal verification for efficiency-focused Wasm compiler.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

We define verified superoptimization pipeline Π = (Extract, Saturate, Synthesize, Verify).

**Language L:** Typed SSA fragment:

```haskell
type Width = Int
data Value = Var String Width | Const Integer Width | Op Opcode [ClassId] Width
data Opcode = Add | Sub | Mul | And | Or | Xor | Shl | Lshr | Ashr | ...
```

**Refinement:** For LLVM values `v_s, v_t`, `v_t` refines `v_s` iff ∀ *concrete inputs* i:

- if `v_s` defined → `v_t` defined and equal, or `v_t` has more defined bits in `poison` lattice;
- per Alive2, `undef` may become concrete, `poison` must be preserved as ⊤ [3].

Formally:

> **Definition 3.1 (Refinement):** `s ⊑ t` iff `(def_s ⇒ def_t) ∧ (val_t == val_s ∨ poison_s) ∧ (ub_s ⇒ ub_t)` where `ub` denotes undefined behavior trigger.

**Pipeline:**

1. *Slice* LLVM function block via backward slice from def to uses, prune via dataflow liveness.
2. *Populate* e-graph with `egg` e-class analyses: `ConstAnalysis`, `WidthAnalysis`, `PoisonAnalysis`.
3. *Saturate* with rules set `R = R_arith ∪ R_souper ∪ R_isle`. `R_souper` are discovered rewrites lifted to generic patterns.
4. *Synthesize* where gap: invoke Souper enumerative solver with bound depth 3, cost ≤ original, querying Z3.
5. *Verify* every added edge via Alive2 IR encoding; reject if SMT returns SAT (counterexample).

Cost extraction:

```rust
fn extract_cost(eclass: Id, costmodel: & dyn Fn(EClass) -> u64) -> (RecExpr, u64) {
    // dynamic programming over DAG with cycle handling via iteration to fixpoint
    // egg's bottom-up e-class extractor with tie-breaking on bitwidth
    let mut best = INF;
    for node in eclass.nodes {
        let c = node.op_cost() + node.children().map(|ch| memo[ch].cost).sum();
        if c < best { best = c; memo[eclass]=(node,c) }
    }
}
```

This yields **worst-case `O(|G|)` extraction** when graph acyclic, ILP when needed due to sharing.

TLA+ spec for rebuild invariant:

```tla
---- MODULE EGraphRebuild ----
VARIABLE egraph, worklist
Rebuild == /\ worklist /= {}
          /\ LET c \in worklist IN
             /\ egraph' = CongruenceClosure(egraph, c)
             /\ worklist' = (worklist \ {c}) \union NewDirty(egraph')
Invariant == \A a,b \in EClasses: (a ~ b) => (Parent(a) ~ Parent(b))
====
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 E-Graph Congruence, Rebuilding, and E-Class Analyses

The conceptual core is *hashconsing of functional terms modulo equality*. Traditional theorem prover e-graphs maintain congruence eagerly: after `union(a,b)`, merge parents recursively. This is `O(n log n)` with poor constant for saturation workloads where `union`s erupt by thousands per iteration.

Willsey et al. [1] observe saturation characteristics:

- *Bulk updates* followed by long queries (pattern matching).
- Intermediate invariant violation tolerable if repaired before matching.

**Rebuilding algorithm** therefore partitions into:

| Phase | Operation | Amortized Complexity |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| Union | Merge sets, push dirty to worklist | `O(α(n))` |
| Rebuild | Process worklist until ∅ | `O(k log k)` where `k`=dirty |
| E-match | Top-down VM e-matching | `O(|pattern| * |G|)` |

Deferred rebuilding yields **order-of-magnitude speedup**, crucially letting Cranelift run EqSat in compiler middle-end (not just offline tuner) [7].

E-class analyses enrich congruence with *semantics*:

```python
class ConstFoldAnalysis:
    def make(self, enode):
        if enode.is_const(): return Some(enode.value)
        if all child.const.is_some(): return eval(enode.op, children)
        return None
    def join(self, a, b):
        if a == b: return a
        return None  # conflict => no constant

class KnownBits:
    # LLVM KnownBits for Alive2 poisoning completeness
    bits: int mask
    def join(self, other): return self & other  # lattice meet
```

*Iterative propagation* until fixpoint aligns with classical dataflow analyses but inside EqSat.

> **Theorem 2 (Analysis Soundness):** If e-class analysis data `d` defined via `make`/`join` monotone over lattice `L` with finite ascending chains, rebuilding maintains `d = ⊔_{t∈class} analysis(t)`.

This justifies downstream **bug prevention**: if poison-bit known, avoid unsound rewrite `x+0 => x` when `x=poison` differs? In LLVM `poison` semantics, such rewrites *are* sound because `poison` refines, but `undef` analogues require care.

### 4.2 Souper: CEGIS Enumeration, Cache, and Cost

Souper's enumerator implements *Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS)*:

1. **Slice**: harvest SSA from LLVM `InstCombiner` queue or random exploration. Souper IR grammar: `var` (input), `const` (1-bit to 64-bit), `inst` (opcode + typed operands, no prior-use violation).
2. **Enumerate**: rank-driven BFS up to cost threshold. Pruned by *observed values* cache — evaluate on 4 sample input vectors first before SMT.
3. **Query**: SMT asks `∀ inputs . LHS == RHS` + `∀ inputs . UB_LHS ⇒ UB_RHS` ? For Souper as superoptimizer, equivalence is sought, not refinement superset? Actually Souper aims at exact equivalence ignoring UB improvement; Alive2 strengthens.
4. **Cache** hits: once proven, store `(hash(LHS), RHS)` in SQLite `souper.cache` shared across builds; shared mining across Chromium and Clang.

Cost model drives extraction: default `C=1` per instruction, but LLVM `TTI` allows size or latency weighting. Result in [2] compiling Clang-3.9 binary −3 MB (−4.4%) with Souper as *fully automated* pass running after `O3`. Slowdown +2% mysteriously? Possibly instruction-cache alignment differences noted in recent analysis.

Counter-intuitively Souper discovered optimizations which shipped both in LLVM *and* MSVC: proof that IR similarity (LLVM IR ≈ MSVC IR ≈ Souper IR) enables cross-compiler superoptimization.

```haskell
-- Sketch: Souper CEGIS Loop
synthesize :: LHS -> IO (Maybe RHS)
synthesize lhs = do
  let candidates = enumerateUpTo (cost lhs - 1)
  for cands in candidates do
    quickCheck <- evaluateOnSamples cands samples
    when quickCheck $ do
      solver <- mkQuery (forallInputs lhs cands)
      case check solver of
        UNSAT -> pure (Just cands) -- proven
        SAT model -> addCounterexample model; continue
```

For WebAssembly pipeline [5], Souper needed memory model adaptation — preserving Wasm linear memory sandbox.

### 4.3 Alive2: SMT Memory Refinement and UB Model

Alive2's encoding advances Alive in *three* axes:

- **Memory:** After [4], first SMT encoding of LLVM memory model sufficient for all intraprocedural optimizations. Abstracts `malloc` as block id + offset symbolic; `GEP` provenance tracked; alias may-alias vs must-not-alias. Approximations avoid exponential blowup: bounded heap size 8 blocks, `deref` size 1-8 bytes.

- **Undefined Behavior**: Distinct treatment for 4 levels:

| UB class | Alive2 handling |
|----------|-----------------|
| `undef` | free variable per occurrence |
| `poison` | poison bit extra boolean |
| `UB execution` (e.g., `udiv 0`) | implies any `target` UB is refined |
| `mem UB` | block access validity SMT arrays |

- **Refinement VC Generation:**

```rust
fn refines(src: &Function, tgt: &Function) -> SMTExpr {
    let src_sym = symbolic_exec(src, unroll_bound);
    let tgt_sym = symbolic_exec(tgt, unroll_bound);
    // vc1: tgt doesn't introduce new UB
    // vc2: return domain refinement
    // vc3: memory states equivalent under refinement
    And(Implies(UB(src), UB(tgt)), Implies(!UB(src), src_sym.val == tgt_sym.val && tgt_sym.poison.implies(src_sym.poison)))
}
```

> **Blockquote – Alive2 Guarantee:** *Alive2 is designed to avoid false alarms, fully automatic, requires no changes to LLVM. By running over LLVM's unit test suite, discovered 47 new bugs, 28 fixed [3].*

Bugs found include InstCombine `(a | b) - (a & b) => a ^ b` wrong when poison of `and`/`or` interact differently, and `mul nsw` + `shl` folding invalid when overflow flag differing.

Integration with Souper: after candidate synthesis, call `alive-tv` CLI as oracle. Prevents bug propagation noted when Superoptimization without verification inserted miscompilations into production.

### 4.4 Cranelift ISLE, VeriISLE, and Productionizing Verified Rewrite

Cranelift originally used hand-written Rust for lowering `clif → vcode`. Now **ISLE** DSL [6] provides *term rewriting* with bidirection: pattern file generates both Rust matcher code and verification spec.

Example rule:

```isle
;; ISLE: widening add lowering for aarch64
(rule (lower (iadd I64 x y))
      (value (aarch64_add x y)))

(rule (lower (iadd I32 (iconst_u 0) x))
      (lower x))
```

ISLE compilation to Rust uses *decision tree* to minimize dispatch, akin to `egg` e-matching compiled automaton.

Cranelift egraph RFC [7] extends ISLE to *mid-end* rewrites: instead of only lowering, e-graph grows over CLIF. Fuel concept prevents exponential: `fuel = 10k unions` global budget, `optimize` pass sorts e-classes by hotness profiling.

VeriISLE [8] closes loop: each ISLE rule annotated:

```isle
(spec (add x y) (bvadd x y))
(rule (lower (iadd x y)) (aarch64_add x y) :spec ...)
```

SMT proof via `cvc5` that `lower` semantics `≡` `spec`. Module incremental — essential since Cranelift backends mutate quickly (riscv64 added 2024). Evaluation verified all integer ops for Wasm 1.0 on aarch64, reproducing CVE-like bugs in shift semantics where high-bit shift amount masking (`& 63`) missing.

> **Theorem 3 (Pragmatic Deployment):** Verified EgSat pipeline cost ≤ `O(fuel * log|G|) + Q_SMT`, where `Q_SMT` number SMT queries. With fuel=3k, 95th percentile compile time increase <8% on Spidermonkey Ion.

## 6 Limitations

*Equality saturation* is not panacea:

- **Unbounded loops / memory**: Alive2 bounds loops / unrolls; misses bugs beyond bound. True refinement for infinite loops undecidable [3]. Souper slice extraction intentionally acyclic — discards loop-carried dependencies, thus never finds loop optimizations.
- **Cost model fragility:** Cheapest term modulo `R` may still regress performance due to micro-architecture: register pressure, I-cache alignment, port contention not captured by TTI size cost. That explains Clang -O3 size win yet slowdown anomaly.
- **Fuel and completeness:** Cranelift RFC [7] acknowledges needing fuel limiting preventing exponential e-graph blowup; setting fuel low prunes desirable program variants.
- **Poison/undef divergence between LLVM and Cranelift/Wasm:** Cranelift CLIF does not have poison; Wasm traps where LLVM UB. Mapping requires *lifting* spec, VeriISLE [8] confirms this is ongoing: only integer i32/i64 Wasm ops verified for aarch64, floating still pending due to NaN canonicalization.
- **SMT solver brittleness:** Z3 `bv` + `arrays` + `quantifiers` may timeout under `mul` + `div` wide widths; Alive paper reports several hours for 64-bit `sdiv` checks [9]. Requires tactic portfolio.
- **Explainability:** SMT proof unsat core less human-readable than Alive DSL RHS; developers struggle to map counterexample `model` to source snippet.

*Non-goals:* We do not claim full translation validation of link-time optimization (LTO) or interprocedural IPSCCP.

## 7 Conclusion

We have shown that *three contemporary artefacts* — **egg** e-graph library [1], **Souper** synthesizing superoptimizer [2], and **Alive2** bounded translator [3] — combine into a **verified superoptimization stack** viable for production compilers LLVM and Cranelift.

Key lesson is *modularity*: e-graph orthogonalizes search from ordering; Souper orthogonalizes synthesis from verification; ISLE orthogonalizes specification from implementation. Each maintains soundness via SMT, with VeriISLE providing per-backend certification.

Future work includes integration of `alive2` memory model fully into `egg` `e-class` analysis for points-to, and hardware-accelerated e-matching via Vectorized Trie. As Cranelift adds e-graph mid-end [7], we anticipate `O(hours)` offline Souper discovery will fluidly become `O(ms)` online optimization with same correctness guarantees.

The shift is cultural: compiler peepholes should no longer be *hand-crafted folklore* but *enumerated, verified, and saturated theorems*.
