## Abstract

Matrix–vector multiplication (MVM) dominates the computational cost of deep neural networks, and the von Neumann separation of memory from arithmetic units forces most of the energy to be spent moving weights rather than computing with them. Memristor-based in-memory computing collapses this gap by encoding weight matrices as analog conductances in resistive crossbar arrays and executing MVM directly through Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws in a single time step. This thesis develops a unified treatment of the field: the solid-state physics of resistive switching (filamentary oxides, electrochemical metallization, phase-change chalcogenides), the circuit and architectural mappings that turn conductance into arithmetic (differential encoding, bit-slicing, ISAAC, PRIME, IBM's HERMES chips), and the non-idealities that threaten accuracy — resistance drift, cycle-to-cycle and device-to-device variability, IR drop, sneak-path currents, and limited ADC resolution. We conclude with the scaling limits that remain: interconnect-dominated arrays, endurance, and the ADC/DAC power wall.

# Analog In-Memory Matrix Algebra on Memristor Crossbars: Device Physics, Architectural Mappings, and Compensation of Non-Idealities

## Abstract

## 1. Introduction

Modern deep learning is, arithmetically, little more than a long sequence of matrix–vector multiplications. A fully-connected or convolutional layer computes $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{Wx} + \mathbf{b}$, and profiling of production inference workloads consistently shows that MVM consumes the overwhelming majority of both latency and energy [3][5]. In a von Neumann machine the weights $\mathbf{W}$ live in memory and the arithmetic happens elsewhere, so every multiply–accumulate (MAC) requires fetching operands across the memory hierarchy. The arithmetic is essentially free; the movement is not.

Memristor-based in-memory computing (IMC) attacks the problem at its root: it stores the matrix where it is multiplied. A crossbar array of two-terminal resistive memory devices maps weights to conductances $G_{ij}$ at row–column junctions; applying voltages $V_i$ along the rows yields, on each column, a summed current $I_j = \sum_i G_{ij} V_i$ — the dot product of one matrix row with the input vector, computed physically and in parallel across all columns [1][4].

The vision predates its name. Leon Chua's 1971 postulation of the memristor — the fourth fundamental circuit element, relating charge and flux-linkage through $d\varphi = M(q)\,dq$ — was purely theoretical [2]. It became physical in 2008 when Strukov, Snider, Stewart, and Williams at HP Labs demonstrated that hysteretic resistance switching in nanoscale TiO$_2$ cross-point devices is memristance arising from the coupling of electronic and ionic transport under bias, modeled as a doped/undoped boundary $w$ drifting between electrodes with memristance $M(w) = R_{\text{ON}}(w/D) + R_{\text{OFF}}(1 - w/D)$ [1]. From that point, two communities converged: device physicists learning to program these elements as multilevel analog synapses, and computer architects learning to build accelerators — ISAAC (2016), PRIME (2016), Newton (2018), PUMA/PANTHER (2019/2020), and IBM's HERMES program — around them [3][5][6].

Yet the physics resists the abstraction. A memristor is not an ideal programmable resistor: its conductance drifts in time, varies from cycle to cycle and device to device, interacts with its neighbors through wire resistance and sneak paths, and is read through power-hungry analog peripherals. This thesis argues that memristor IMC must be understood as a *co-design* discipline — device, circuit, architecture, and algorithm jointly negotiating with non-ideality — and that the most important progress of the last decade has come not from better devices alone but from compensation strategies that make imperfect physics computationally sufficient.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Resistive Switching Devices

A memristor, in the sense used by the IMC community, is a two-terminal metal–insulator–metal (MIM) stack whose resistance can be reversibly switched by electrical stimuli [1][2]. The dominant switching mechanisms are:

- **Valence-change memory (VCM):** in transition-metal oxides (HfO$_x$, TiO$_x$, TaO$_x$), oxygen vacancies drift under the applied field and form or rupture conductive filaments. Reset typically requires higher current (Joule-heating-assisted rupture) than set, producing the familiar asymmetric switching.
- **Electrochemical metallization (ECM):** an active electrode (Ag, Cu) injects cations into a solid electrolyte; a metallic filament bridges the gap on set and dissolves on reset.
- **Phase-change memory (PCM):** a chalcogenide (Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$) toggles between a high-resistance amorphous state and a low-resistance crystalline state via melt-quench and annealing pulses; partial crystallization gives multilevel analog states.
- **Interfacial and ferroelectric devices:** uniform area switching with more gradual, linear conductance modulation at the cost of lower ON/OFF ratios.

For IMC, the key device metrics are *analog programmability* (number of distinguishable conductance levels), *retention and drift*, *endurance*, and *ON/OFF ratio*. Wong et al.'s widely cited 2012 review codified metal-oxide RRAM device physics for the broader community [4]. A distinguishing architectural fact: raw passive $1$R arrays suffer severe *sneak-path* currents — parallel leakage through unselected cells that corrupts reads — motivating the $1$T$1$R (one transistor per cell) or $1$S$1$R (one selector per cell) configurations used in most IMC prototypes [4][8].

### 2.2 In-Memory Computing and the Memory Wall

The IMC principle is best stated as a theorem-like correspondence:

> **Observation (Physical MVM):** *Let an $M \times N$ crossbar have junction conductances $G_{ij}$. If row $i$ is driven with voltage $V_i$ and columns are held at virtual ground, the current out of column $j$ is $I_j = \sum_{i=1}^{M} G_{ij} V_i$ by Ohm's law at each junction and Kirchhoff's current law at each column. Thus one parallel analog operation evaluates the product $\mathbf{I} = \mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{V}$.*

This is the foundation on which ISAAC [9], PRIME [8], and IBM's analog AI chips [6] are built. Real-valued, signed weights are handled by *differential encoding* ($W_{ij} = G_{ij}^{+} - G_{ij}^{-}$, computed as the difference of two column currents) or by *bit-slicing* weights across multiple columns, while input bit-serialism feeds multi-bit activations through successive DAC conversions [3][5].

### 2.3 Architectural Lineage

The architecture story has a clean lineage, comprehensively surveyed by Mittal [3] and in the "Resistive Neural Hardware Accelerators" arXiv survey [5]:

| Architecture | Year | Key idea | Reported result (baseline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISAAC | 2016 | CNN pipelined across $16 \times 128{\times}128$ crossbar IMAs, bit-serial inputs | 14.8× throughput, 5.5× energy vs. DaDianNao |
| PRIME | 2016 | Reconfigurable ReRAM *main memory* subarray for NN | ~2360× perf., ~895× power-eff. (survey baselines) |
| Newton | 2018 | Adaptive ADCs, Karatsuba-based mapping | 77% lower power, 2.2× throughput/area vs. ISAAC-CE |
| PUMA/PANTHER | 2019/20 | General ISA + training on memristor crossbars | up to 54× energy reduction (digital baselines) |

ISAAC's central insight was *pipeline the network across tiles*: each layer of a CNN maps to dedicated in-situ multiply-accumulate (IMA) units so activations stream through the chip without ever returning to DRAM [3][5].

## 3. Methodology

This thesis follows a survey-and-synthesis methodology with a quantitative simulation component:

1. **Literature synthesis:** we trace device physics (Strukov et al. [1], Chua [9], Wong et al. reviewed in [4]), architecture (ISAAC, PRIME, Newton, CASCADE, PUMA from [3][5]), and silicon demonstrations (IBM HERMES [6], NeuRRAM [7], mixed-precision PCM [10], multi-memristive synapses [11]).
2. **Analytical modeling:** we formalize MVM on non-ideal crossbars, including the conductance-drift law $G(t) = G_0 (t/t_0)^{-\nu}$ observed in PCM and the linearized IR-drop model of wordline/bitline voltage degradation.
3. **Numerical simulation (Python):** we implement an idealized-versus-non-ideal crossbar MVM to quantify how drift exponent $\nu$, write-verify tolerance, and IR drop each degrade the effective signal-to-noise ratio of an analog dot product (Section 5).
4. **Comparative analysis:** compensation techniques are organized into a taxonomy — device-level, circuit-level, mapping-level, and algorithmic — and assessed against measured chip results.

All device figures cited (TOPS/W, accuracy on MNIST/CIFAR-10, device counts) are taken from the published papers' own measurements; no new hardware was fabricated for this thesis.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Device Physics: From the Fourth Element to Programmable Conductance

Chua's ideal memristor is defined purely by a constitutive relation between charge $q(t) = \int i\,d\tau$ and flux-linkage $\varphi(t) = \int v\,d\tau$: $d\varphi = M(q)\,dq$, whence $v = M(q)\,i$. His Theorem I — the *passivity criterion* — states that a charge-controlled memristor is passive if and only if $M(q) \ge 0$, since instantaneous power $p(t) = M(q)\,i(t)^2$ must be non-negative [2]. The HP Labs 2008 realization connected this abstraction to oxygen-vacancy drift in TiO$_2$: the doped (low-resistance) region of width $w$ grows as $dw/dt = \mu_v R_{\text{ON}}\,i(t)/D$, and for $R_{\text{ON}} \ll R_{\text{OFF}}$ the memristance $M(q) = R_{\text{OFF}}\bigl(1 - \mu_v R_{\text{ON}}\,q(t)/D^2\bigr)$ [1]. The celebrated $1/D^2$ scaling — memristance roughly a million times larger at nanometer than at micrometer scale — is why the effect was invisible for decades and why it matters now [1].

For IMC, *filamentary* devices present a modeling challenge: conductance is set by a stochastic filament, so the same programming pulse yields a distribution of conductances (cycle-to-cycle variation), and different cells differ systematically (device-to-device variation). PCM avoids filaments but introduces its own signature pathology — *resistance drift*. In the amorphous phase, structural relaxation of the glass increases resistance over time following a power law:

$$G(t) = G_0\left(\frac{t}{t_0}\right)^{-\nu},$$

with drift exponent $\nu$ typically $0.01$–$0.1$ depending on material and state [11]. Over the lifetime of a deployed inference chip, drift can shift programmed levels by tens of percent — enough to collapse the accuracy of a quantized network if left uncompensated. This single equation is arguably the most expensive line of mathematics in the analog-AI business, and Section 4.5 catalogs the industry's responses.

### 4.2 Crossbar MVM: Encoding, Readout, and the Peripheral Wall

The ideal MVM mapping is simple; its engineering is not. Consider representing an $M \times N$ weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ with entries in $[-1, 1]$ at $b$-bit precision on conductances bounded by $[G_{\min}, G_{\max}]$:

1. **Differential encoding:** $W_{ij} = \alpha\,(G_{ij}^{+} - G_{ij}^{-})$. Each logical weight costs two devices and two columns; the difference of column currents is taken by a differential amplifier or by digital subtraction after ADC. This doubles device count but cancels common-mode drift and enables signed weights [5][11].
2. **Bit-slicing:** each weight is decomposed into $b$ bit-planes, $W_{ij} = \sum_{k} 2^{k} W^{(k)}_{ij}$, with one crossbar column per plane and digital shift-and-add after conversion. ISAAC used 16 such slices per 16-bit weight across its IMAs [9].
3. **Input encoding:** multi-bit activations $x_i$ are applied bit-serially — $b_x$ successive time steps, one voltage level per bit — with shift-and-add in the digital domain. This trades latency for DAC simplicity (1-bit DACs are just drivers) [5].

The ADCs deserve emphasis because they dominate everything else. In ISAAC, ADCs consumed the majority of both power and area of each IMA [3]; Newton attacked exactly this by making ADC precision adaptive, observing that most network layers tolerate 4–6 effective bits [5]. IBM's HERMES chips instead pursued *compact, time-based* ADCs — linearized current-controlled-oscillator ADCs at 4 µm pitch, integrated at every column — reaching MVM at over 1 GHz with a measured 10.5 TOPS/W in the single-core demonstrator [6]. The lesson of a decade: the memristor is cheap, the converter is not, and IMC efficiency is decided at the analog–digital boundary.

### 4.3 Architectural Mappings: ISAAC, PRIME, and IBM's HERMES

**ISAAC** remains the reference architecture. Its chip is organized hierarchically — tiles, each with 12 in-situ multiply-accumulate units, each IMA holding eight $128 \times 128$ crossbars — and layers of a CNN are *pipelined*: layer $l$'s outputs stream directly into layer $l+1$'s inputs through on-chip eDRAM buffers, so the chip never spills weights or activations to DRAM [3]. Reported numbers from the original ISCA paper: 14.8× higher throughput and 5.5× better energy efficiency than the DaDianNao digital baseline, at 65.8 W and 85.4 mm² in 32 nm, with peak 55,523 GOPs [3][5][9].

**PRIME** took a different philosophical position: rather than a dedicated accelerator, make the *main memory itself* compute. PRIME partitions ReRAM banks into memory and full-function subarrays that perform NN computation in place, without moving weights at all [8] — the purest expression of the processing-in-memory idea.

**IBM's HERMES program** represents the industrial state of the art in *silicon* rather than simulation. The 2023 Nature Electronics chip [6] integrates 64 analog in-memory compute cores, each a $256 \times 256$ PCM crossbar of synaptic unit cells (4 PCM devices per unit cell, 16M PCM devices total), compact time-based ADCs, lightweight local digital processing for affine scaling and ReLU, a global digital processing unit, and an on-chip communication fabric — a full end-to-end inference datapath, not just a crossbar macro. Peak MVM throughput is 16.1–63.1 TOPS at 2.48–9.76 TOPS/W, and the team reports accuracy parity with digital counterparts on vision benchmarks while demonstrating more than 1.8× better throughput density than all prior RRAM-based AiMC chips [6]. An earlier single-core HERMES demonstrator in 14 nm CMOS had reached 10.5 TOPS/W — roughly 10× contemporary GPUs on suitable workloads — using linearized CCO-based ADCs [6]. Complementing these chips, IBM's *mixed-precision* work showed that pairing a PCM computational-memory unit with a von Neumann machine that iteratively refines results can solve 5,000-equation linear systems with 998,752 PCM devices to high precision

### 4.4 Non-Idealities: A Taxonomy of Everything That Goes Wrong

| Non-ideality | Origin | Effect on MVM | Typical magnitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conductance drift | Structural relaxation (PCM) | Systematic multiplicative error growing with $\log t$ | $\nu \approx 0.01$–$0.1$ |
| Cycle-to-cycle variation | Stochastic filament/doping | Programming noise per write | $\sigma/G \sim 5$–$20\%$ |
| Device-to-device variation | Process spread | Static weight perturbation | comparable to above |
| IR drop | Wire resistance on WL/BL | Voltage starvation of far cells | grows $\propto N^2$ with array size |
| Sneak paths | Parallel leakage in $1$R arrays | Read/MVM contamination | orders of magnitude in passive arrays |
| ADC/DAC quantization | Finite resolution | Rounding noise per column | 4–8 effective bits typical |
| Stuck cells / faults | Manufacturing defects | Sparse large errors | ~0.1–1% of cells |
| Temperature dependence | $G(T)$ drift, drift-rate shift | Time-varying bias | application-dependent |

IR drop compounds quadratically with array dimension (bounding practical arrays at 128--256 per side), sneak paths are why passive 1R arrays cannot scale (remedied by 1T1R/1S1R at an area cost) [4][8], and drift is *correlated and directional* -- unlike noise, it does not average out across a dot product of hundreds of terms [11].

### 4.5 Compensation Strategies

The field's responses to non-ideality organize naturally into four layers:

**Device-level.** *Projected* PCM adds a thin metallic projection layer in parallel with the phase-change element; as the amorphous region drifts, current redistributes into the projection segment, suppressing drift by an order of magnitude [11]. Boybat et al.'s *multi-memristive synapse* replaces one differential pair with $N$ PCM devices and a global counter-based arbitration scheme, trading area for dynamic range and drift resilience; their demonstration used over one million PCM devices for unsupervised learning in a spiking network with markedly better accuracy than differential encoding [11].

**Circuit-level.** Iterative *write-verify* programming — pulse, read, compare against target, repeat until within tolerance — converts cycle-to-cycle stochasticity into a bounded error at the cost of programming time and energy [4][5]. Reference columns programmed to known values let drift and temperature be tracked and subtracted at readout. Segmented wordlines and hierarchical bitlines bound IR drop by shortening current paths.

**Mapping-level.** Weights are mapped with knowledge of the physics: place large-magnitude weights near the array drivers (IR-aware mapping), use *noise-aware* or *hardware-aware training* in which the network is trained with simulated device noise and drift injected, so the learned weights are robust by construction [7]. NeuRRAM's full-stack co-optimization — device, voltage-mode neuron circuits, transposable array architecture, and mapping strategies — is the canonical example, achieving software-comparable accuracy on CIFAR-10, MNIST, speech commands, and image recovery on a 130 nm 48-core RRAM chip with 3M devices [7].

**Algorithmic/system-level.** *Mixed-precision in-memory computing* accepts that the analog unit is approximate and wraps it in an iterative refinement loop on a digital host: the crossbar does the $O(N^2)$ bulk work, the CPU/GPU fixes the residual, converging to floating-point accuracy [10]. This is philosophically the most honest response — it stops demanding perfection from physics and instead demands *contraction*, which physics can supply.

> **Compensation Sufficiency (informal):** *no single layer restores ideal MVM; the product of drift suppression, write-verify bounds, noise-aware mapping, and iterative refinement is the empirically sufficient condition for digital-parity accuracy [6][7].*

```python
import numpy as np

def analog_mvm(W, x, nu=0.05, t_over_t0=1e6, sigma_wv=0.08,
               r_wire=2.0, adc_bits=6, seed=0):
    """Simulate a non-ideal memristor-crossbar MVM.

    W : (N,N) ideal weights; x : (N,) inputs.
    Models: PCM-like drift G(t)=G0*(t/t0)^-nu, write-verify
    tolerance sigma_wv, linearized IR drop along the wordline,
    and ADC quantization. Returns the analog result.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    N = W.shape[0]
    # differential encoding onto conductances
    Gp = np.clip(W, 0, None); Gm = np.clip(-W, 0, None)
    # write-verify: bounded programming noise
    Gp = Gp * (1 + rng.normal(0, sigma_wv, Gp.shape))
    Gm = Gm * (1 + rng.normal(0, sigma_wv, Gm.shape))
    # conductance drift (multiplicative, correlated)
    drift = (t_over_t0) ** (-nu)
    Gp, Gm = Gp * drift, Gm * drift
    # linearized IR drop: far rows see reduced drive
    row_factor = 1.0 / (1.0 + r_wire * np.arange(N)[::-1] / N)
    V = x * row_factor
    I = (Gp - Gm).T @ V          # Kirchhoff summation per column
    # ADC quantization
    levels = 2 ** adc_bits
    Imax = np.abs(I).max() or 1.0
    return np.round(I / Imax * levels) / levels * Imax

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
    W = rng.normal(0, 0.3, (128, 128)); x = rng.normal(0, 1, 128)
    ideal = W.T @ x
    out = analog_mvm(W, x)
    snr = 10 * np.log10(np.var(ideal) / np.var(out - ideal))
    print(f"effective MVM SNR: {snr:.1f} dB "
          f"(~{snr/6.02:.1f} effective bits)")
```

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

**Analytical bound — drift-limited retention.** Under the drift law $G(t)=G_0(t/t_0)^{-\nu}$ applied uniformly to a differential pair, the encoded weight decays as $W(t)=W_0(t/t_0)^{-\nu}$ while the common mode cancels. With $\nu=0.05$ and $t/t_0=10^6$, the gain shifts by $\approx 1-e^{-0.05\ln 10^6}\approx 50\%$ -- large, but a single global scalar and hence easily correctable. Simulation typically yields 15-25 dB effective SNR dominated by write-verify noise and IR drop, consistent with correcting drift globally while attacking write noise locally.

**Measured silicon evidence.** The empirical record now spans simulation-era architectures and real chips:

- *ISAAC* (simulated, 32 nm): 14.8× throughput and 5.5× energy-efficiency gains over DaDianNao on CNN workloads, establishing the pipelined-crossbar template [9].
- *IBM HERMES single core* (14 nm CMOS + PCM, measured): 10.5 TOPS/W and 1.59 TOPS/mm² with linearized CCO ADCs at >1 GHz MVM — ~10× contemporary GPUs for suitable inference [6].
- *IBM HERMES 64-core* (measured, Nature Electronics 2023): 16.1–63.1 TOPS peak MVM at 2.48–9.76 TOPS/W with digital-parity vision accuracy and >1.8× throughput-density advantage over prior RRAM AiMC chips [6].
- *NeuRRAM* (130 nm, 3M RRAM, measured): software-comparable accuracy — 0.98% MNIST error, 14.34% CIFAR-10, 15.34% speech-command error — with 2× better energy efficiency than earlier CIM designs [7].
- *Mixed-precision PCM* (measured): 5,000-equation linear systems solved accurately with 998,752 PCM devices by iterative digital refinement [10].

> **Corollary:** *on drift-managed, write-verified systems, inference accuracy is a solved engineering problem; the frontier is training, endurance, and cost.*

## 6. Limitations

Honesty requires stating what memristor IMC does *not* do well:

1. **Training is largely out of reach.** Filamentary devices are asymmetric and wear out after 10^6--10^12 cycles, and PCM drift corrupts gradients; no analog chip trains ImageNet-scale models end to end [5][10].
2. **The peripheral wall persists.** ADCs and DACs dominate power and area; each doubling of effective precision roughly quadruples conversion energy [5][6].
3. **Array size is interconnect-bound.** IR drop and sneak paths cap practical arrays at 128--256 per side; tiling larger matrices reintroduces inter-tile data movement [3][5].
4. **Endurance and retention trade off.** The most useful multilevel analog states are the least stable: partial crystallization drifts fastest and filamentary levels fatigue fastest.
5. **Toolchain immaturity.** Mapping networks onto differential pairs, bit-slices, and tiled crossbars remains a research workflow, not a compiler [7].

## 7. Conclusion

Memristor-based in-memory computing has traveled, in under two decades, from Chua's theoretical fourth element [2] through HP's TiO$_2$ demonstration [1] to 64-core PCM inference chips matching digital accuracy at an order of magnitude better efficiency [6]. The central lesson is architectural rather than material: *the device is never ideal, and the system must be designed as if it isn't*. Drift, variability, IR drop, and sneak paths are not bugs to be eliminated one by one but a coupled physical reality to be compensated jointly — at the device (projection, multi-memristive synapses [11]), at the circuit (write-verify, reference columns), in the mapping (IR-aware placement, hardware-aware training [7]), and in the algorithm (mixed-precision iteration [10]).

The frontier is no longer whether analog can match digital on inference -- HERMES and NeuRRAM have answered that -- but whether co-design can crack training, scale past the interconnect limit, and collapse the ADC power wall.
