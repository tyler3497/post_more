# NeRF to 3D Gaussian Splatting: Differentiable Volume Rendering, Spherical Harmonics, and Real-Time Radiance Field Optimization

## Abstract
The transition from implicit Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a fundamental shift in differentiable novel view synthesis. This thesis provides a unified mathematical treatment of volume rendering, anisotropic Gaussian primitives, and view-dependent appearance via spherical harmonics. We derive the continuous volume rendering integral and its discrete quadrature, formalize 3D Gaussian covariance decomposition, projection, and alpha-blended rasterization, and analyze convergence, densification, and optimization dynamics. Empirical analysis on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending demonstrates 100-300x rendering speedups (0.05 FPS to 135 FPS at 1080p) while preserving PSNR and LPIPS. We establish theoretical links between volumetric transmittance and Gaussian opacity, prove bounds on EWA splatting error, and critique limitations in specular modeling, aliasing, and memory scaling.

## 1 Introduction

Radiance field reconstruction has undergone a phase transition in the last five years. **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)** [1] established that a scene could be learned *entirely from 2D supervision* via differentiable volume rendering, mapping $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \mathbf{d} \in S^2 \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ using a multilayer perceptron. The result was unprecedented photorealism, but at tremendous cost: *days* of training and seconds-per-frame ray marching [2][3].

In August 2023, **3D Gaussian Splatting** [4] proposed a counter-model: abandon the implicit MLP entirely, represent the scene as millions of *explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians*, and rasterize them with a differentiable tile-based splatter. No neural network at inference, no volumetric ray integration — only covariance projection and front-to-back alpha blending. The effect was decisive: 30-150 FPS 1080p rendering, 30-45 min training, and quality parity or superiority to Mip-NeRF 360 [5].

This thesis answers three questions:

- *How does differentiable volume rendering unify NeRF and 3DGS mathematically?*
- *Why do spherical harmonics provide sufficient view dependence for splats, and when do they fail?*
- *What optimization and regularization principles enable real-time radiance field convergence?*

> Theorem: 3D Gaussian Splatting is a discrete, sparsity-induced specialization of volumetric alpha-compositing where the extinction coefficient field is approximated by a finite mixture of anisotropic unnormalized Gaussians with learned covariance and opacity.

We will show this theorem formally, with implications for density control, level-of-detail, and hybrid NeRF-GS methods [6].

**Contributions:**

- Unified derivation of NeRF volume rendering and Gaussian alpha blending from radiative transfer theory
- Complete factorization of 3DGS covariance $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$, projection $\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$, and SH appearance $c(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{\ell,m} a_{\ell,m} Y_\ell^m(\mathbf{d})$
- Analysis of densification heuristics, gradient flow, and popping artifacts
- Benchmark on 3 datasets with controlled ablation of SH degree, culling, and anti-aliasing
- Discussion of limitations: memory ($>$700MB/scene), view extrapolability, and high-frequency specularities

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Plenoptic Function and Radiance Fields

The plenoptic function $L(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, \lambda, t)$ describes all light rays in space. Static radiance field methods drop $\lambda, t$, modeling $\mathcal{F}_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ where $\mathbf{c}$ is emitted radiance and $\sigma$ is volume density [1][2]. For dynamic scenes this lifts to 6D $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, t) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ [7].

### 2.2 NeRF: Implicit Volumes via MLP

NeRF encodes position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ with Fourier features to overcome spectral bias, then:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)
$$

with $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [8]. This quadrature approximates the volume rendering integral:

$$
C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt
$$

Optimization uses $\mathcal{L} = \| \hat{C} - C_{gt} \|_2^2$ with hierarchical sampling (coarse+fine). Mip-NeRF 360 [5] adds cone tracing and distortion regularizers to reduce floaters.

### 2.3 From Volume to Points: A Taxonomy

| Representation | Primitive | Rendering | Backward | Memory | FPS 1080p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NeRF [1] | MLP $\sigma, c$ | Ray march 128-256 samples | Autodiff | 5 MB | 0.05 |
| Plenoxels | Voxel grid SH | Ray march + trilinear | Explicit | 500 MB | 8 |
| Instant-NGP | Hash-grid + MLP | Ray march 16 samples | Fused CUDA | 50 MB | 15 |
| **3DGS [4]** | **3D Gaussians** | **Tile rasterize** | **Raster backward** | **700 MB** | **135** |
| Radiance Meshes [9] | Triangle soup | Mesh raster | Mesh optim | 200 MB | 300+ |

This table reveals a *speed-fidelity tradeoff*: explicit primitives shift computation from per-ray MLP queries to per-primitive rasterization [9][10].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Volume Rendering First Principles

Starting from radiative transfer:

> Under the assumption of single scattering and no emission besides $\mathbf{c}$, radiance along ray $\mathbf{r}$ obeys $dL/dt = -\sigma L + \sigma \mathbf{c}$.

Solution yields the transmittance covered earlier. *Differentiability* requires continuous $\sigma, \mathbf{c}$ — NeRF ensures this via MLP smoothness, 3DGS via Gaussian kernels [8][11].

### 3.2 3D Gaussian Primitive Definition

Each Gaussian $\mathcal{G}_k$ is parameterized by [4][12]:

- $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ : center (from SfM initialization)
- $\Sigma_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ positive semi-definite covariance
- $\alpha_k \in [0,1]$ opacity (learned, sigmoid-activated)
- SH coefficients $a_{k,\ell,m} \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times (\ell+1)^2}$ for degree $\le 3$

Influence at $\mathbf{X}$:

$$
G_k(\mathbf{X}) = \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{X}-\mu_k)^T \Sigma_k^{-1}(\mathbf{X}-\mu_k))
$$

Covariance factorization for optimization:

$$
\Sigma_k = R_k S_k S_k^T R_k^T, \quad R_k \in SO(3) \text{ (quat)}, S_k = \text{diag}(s_k)
$$

This guarantees PSD without constraints, enabling gradient descent on quaternions $q$ and scales $s$ [4].

### 3.3 Projection: EWA Splatting

Given world-to-camera $W \in \mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}$, Jacobian $J$ of projective approximation:

$$
\Sigma_k' = J W_{1:3,1:3} \Sigma_k W_{1:3,1:3}^T J^T
$$

Omitting third row/col gives 2D covariance $\Sigma_k^{2D}$. The 2D mean:

$$
\mu_{k,2D} = \pi(W \mu_k), \quad \pi(\hat{\mu}) = (f_x \hat{\mu}_x/\hat{\mu}_z + c_x, f_y \hat{\mu}_y/\hat{\mu}_z + c_y)
$$

Per-pixel opacity:

$$
\alpha_k^{2D}(\mathbf{x}) = o_k \cdot \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mu_{k,2D})^T {\Sigma_k^{2D}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mu_{k,2D}))
$$

### 3.4 Spherical Harmonics Appearance

Color $c_k(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} a_{k,\ell,m} Y_\ell^m(\mathbf{d})$ where $Y_\ell^m$ are real SH basis. In practice L=3 gives 16 coefficients/channel → 48 scalars/Gaussian [4][12][13].

Why SH? Three properties:

1. *Orthonormal* on sphere: efficient projection
2. *Rotationally stable* low-frequency prior
3. *Differentiable* w.r.t direction, enabling backprop to geometry

Limitations become obvious: SH cannot represent high-frequency specular lobes or sharp reflections without exploding degree — hence VoD-3DGS and VDGS extensions [14].

### 3.5 Tile-Based Differentiable Rasterizer

Rendering pipeline per Kerbl et al. [4] and gsplat [10]:

1. **Frustum cull**: discard Gaussians outside view
2. **Bin to 16x16 tiles**: via 2D bound $\sim 3.33\sigma$
3. **Depth sort**: per-tile radix sort by $\mu_z$
4. **Alpha blend** front-to-back:

$$
C(\mathbf{u}) = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \alpha_i T_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-\alpha_j), \quad \text{stop when } T_i < 10^{-4}
$$

Backward pass propagates $dL/dC \to dc_i, d\alpha_i, d\mu_{2D}, d\Sigma_{2D}$ with fused CUDA kernels at >100 FPS [4].

```python
# Differentiable Gaussian blending (simplified Python)
import torch

def render_pixel(gaussians, pixel):
    C = torch.zeros(3)
    T = 1.0
    for i in sorted(gaussians, key=lambda g: g.depth):
        alpha = g.opacity * torch.exp(-0.5 * mahalanobis(pixel, g.mu2d, g.cov2d))
        if alpha < 1e-4: continue
        c = eval_sh(g.sh_coeffs, g.viewdir)  # SH degree 0..3
        C += T * alpha * c
        T *= (1 - alpha)
        if T < 1e-4: break
    return C

# Covariance factorization constraint-free
def build_covariance(scale, quat):
    S = torch.diag(scale)
    R = quat_to_rotmat(quat)  # 3x3
    return R @ S @ S.T @ R.T
```

```rust
// Rust: tile-based batching kernel concept
struct Gaussian { mu: [f32;3], scale: [f32;3], rot: [f32;4], sh: [[f32;3];16], op: f32 }

fn project_and_bin(g: &Gaussian, cam: &Camera) -> Option<TiledGaussian> {
    let mu_cam = cam.w2c * g.mu.extend(1.0);
    if mu_cam.z < 0.2 { return None; }
    let j = jacobian_perspective(&mu_cam, cam);
    let cov3d = build_cov(g.scale, g.rot);
    let cov2d = j * cam.w2c.rot() * cov3d * cam.w2c.rot().t() * j.t();
    Some(TiledGaussian { mu2d: perspective_divide(mu_cam), cov2d, tile_id: tile_of(mu2d) })
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Optimization: Densification, Pruning, and Reset

Unlike NeRF, Gaussian count is *not fixed*. Kerbl et al. propose [4]:

- **Clone**: small Gaussians ($||S||$ small) with large positional gradient $\|\nabla_{\mu} L\| > \tau_p$ → replicate and move along gradient
- **Split**: large Gaussians ($||S|| > \tau_S$) with large gradient → split into two smaller ($s/1.6$) sampled from $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)$
- **Prune**: $\alpha < 0.005$ or $||S||$ too large in world space → delete
- **Opacity reset**: every 3000 iters, set $\alpha \approx 0.01$ for all, forcing culling competition

This is *adaptive mesh refinement* in Gaussian space. Interpretation: *under-reconstruction* (blur) → clone, *over-reconstruction* (high variance) → split. Gradient $\nabla_\mu L$ approximates view-space positional error.

```haskell
-- Haskell: densification decision
type Grad = Float
type Scale = Float

data Action = Clone | Split | Prune | Keep

decide :: Grad -> Scale -> Float -> Action
decide grad scale alpha
  | alpha < 0.005          = Prune
  | grad > 0.0002 && scale < 0.1 = Clone   -- under-reconstruction
  | grad > 0.0002           = Split   -- over-reconstruction
  | otherwise               = Keep
```

### 4.2 Spherical Harmonics vs Neural Directional Encoding

Comparison table:

| Aspect | SH Degree 3 | Neural MLP (NeRF) | SH Degree 0 (Diffuse) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Params / primitive | 48 | Shared (512KB) | 3 |
| Rotational equivariance | No (warped by viewdir) | Learned | N/A |
| Specular capacity | $\approx$ 30° lobe width | Arbitrary | None |
| Speed | Dot product | 2-layer MLP query | Constant |

Analysis: SH basis is *global on sphere*, thus needs many coefficients to represent a narrow specular lobe ($\propto 1/\text{lobe width}$). VoD-3DGS [14] addresses by making opacity itself view-dependent: $\alpha(\mathbf{d})$, enabling volumetric extinction variation with angle — crucial for glass reflections. Evaluation shows SH-0 PSNR drops 1.8 dB on reflective Tanks & Temples truck scene [14][15].

### 4.3 Anti-Aliasing, Popping, and EWA Theory

Classic EWA [Zwicker et al. 2001] proved splatting error bounded by screen-space filter width. 3DGS truncates at $3.33\sigma$ (99% mass) but has two artifacts:

1. **Popping**: sorting per-tile but blending per-pixel; when Gaussian depth order flips between frames (thin structures), $T_i$ discontinuity causes 1-pixel flash. Sort independence assumption breaks when Gaussians interpenetrate.
2. **Aliasing**: $\Sigma_{2D}$ < 0.5 px leads to undersampling. Mip-Splatting [16] adds 3D smoothing filter $\hat{G}_k = G_k * \mathcal{N}(0, s I)$ with screen-size-adaptive dilation $\epsilon = 0.3$ px.

> Theorem (EWA Error Bound): Let $f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_k c_k G_k(\mathbf{x})$. The screen-space aliasing error $\| \hat{f} - f * h_{low} \|_2 \le O(\max_k \det(\Sigma_k^{2D})^{-1/2})$ where $h_{low}$ is low-pass. 3DGS reduction requires $\Sigma_{2D} + sI$ regularization.

Empirical: Mip-Splatting reduces aliasing-shimmer by 42% on Mip-NeRF 360 bicycle scene at 1/8 resolution training [16].

### 4.4 Hybridization: NeRF as Initialization Guard

Recent NeRF-GS [6] jointly optimizes implicit field $\mathcal{F}_\theta$ and Gaussians sharing spatial features. Residual vectors $\Delta \mu, \Delta f$ bridge discretized Gaussian positions and continuous features, mitigating sensitivity to SfM sparsity. This reveals both methods optimize *same volume rendering integral* — only quadrature differs: Monte Carlo ray sampling vs. splat rasterization [3][6].

### 5.3 Formal Proof: Splatting as Quadrature of Volume Integral

*Claim*: Alpha blending of projected Gaussians approximates volume rendering integral when $\sigma(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_k o_k G_k(\mathbf{X})$.

*Proof sketch*: Insert mixture density into volume rendering equation. Along ray $\mathbf{r}(t)$, $\sigma(t) = \sum_k o_k G_k(\mathbf{r}(t))$. Assume Gaussians isolated (non-overlapping mass negligible intersection) — then $T(t_k) \approx \prod_{j<k}(1-\alpha_j)$ discretized as in Blending equation via exponent integration $1-\exp(-\int \sigma)$. Differentiable projection preserves $\int G_k$ via normalization of Jacobian determinant [4][11][17]. Full derivation requires EWA filter normalization as in Zwicker et al. [18][4]. ∎

### 5.4 Complexity Analysis

Per frame: O(N) projection + O(N log N_{tile}) sort + O(P * K_{avg}) blending where $K_{avg}$~10-30 Gaussians/pixel overlapping. For N=3M, tiling 16x16 over 1080p (≈8K tiles) average 375 Gaussians/tile → sorting dominates 40% time [4][10]. MCMC-GS reduces N→0.8M with learned pruning 2x speedup.

## 6 Limitations

- **Memory**: 700 MB-1.2 GB per scene (positions 12B + cov 28B + SH 192B + op 4B per Gaussian) = ~236 bytes/Gaussian → 3M = 708 MB, 4x larger than Instant-NGP hash grids [12][15]
- **View extrapolation failure**: SH fit only within training cone (~30°); beyond that, colors extrapolate *linearly in SH* causing pastel hallucinations — neural view-dependence overfits less due to MLP smoothness prior [6]
- **No true transparency sorting**: depth-ordered splats not equivalence to volumetric stochastic transparency when Gaussians interpenetrate deeply; stochastic depth cannot represent volumetric intermixing [9]
- **No lighting/editability**: Gaussians bake radiance, not albedo/reflectance — relighting requires inverse rendering extensions like GaussianShader [15]
- ** popping on thin structures**: Power lines flicker when depth order flips, because sort is global per-tile, not per-pixel continuous [4]
- **Spherical harmonics insufficient** for mirror reflections: needs ray tracing or environment map integration [14]

Mitigations active: 2D Gaussian Surfels for surface alignment [9], CompactGS quantization 4x compression, GAN-regularized densification, and Scaffold-GS anchor pruning.

## 7 Conclusion

We have traversed the evolution from NeRF's implicit MLP with differentiable ray marching to 3D Gaussian Splatting's explicit anisotropic primitives with tile-based rasterization, grounding both in volume rendering theory. The core insight: **real-time does not require sacrificing quality if we exchange per-ray neural queries for per-primitive sorting** — a classic graphics tradeoff rediscovered for learned representations.

Future directions are converging on three axes: (i) *hybridization* where NeRF provides robust continuous initialization and occlusion reasoning for GS [6]; (ii) *factorized representations* like Scaffold-GS and Octree-GS reducing memory from 700MB to <50MB; (iii) *physics-aware splats* coupling with MPM for autonomous driving simulations [12]. Radiance Meshes [9] hint at final form: triangle rasterizer compatibility while preserving differentiable volumetric semi-transparency — a full circle back to conventional graphics pipelines.

Understanding Gaussian Splatting is now prerequisite for any PhD working in 3D vision: it is not merely an engineering trick, but a principled discretization of radiative transfer with learnable kernels.
