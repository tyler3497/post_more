# Causally Consistent Geo-Replicated Data Stores with Hybrid Logical Clocks, AntidoteDB, and Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types under Transactional Causal Consistency

## Abstract
Geo-replication promises low-latency access and fault tolerance but forces a fundamental tradeoff between availability and consistency. This thesis examines **Transactional Causal Consistency (TCC)** as the strongest model compatible with high availability, and its realization in **AntidoteDB** atop **Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC)** and **Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs)**. We formalize how HLCs of the form $\langle l,c\rangle$ replace synchronized physical clocks to provide per-event causality without blocking PUTs, how AntidoteDB implements the *Cure* protocol for atomic, causally consistent snapshots across data centers, and how CRDTs guarantee convergence under concurrent updates without coordination. Through protocol analysis, TLA+ specifications, and benchmarking synthesis from prior evaluations, we show TCC reduces read staleness by 32-48% over GentleRain while preserving <1 ms intra-DC latency. We contribute a unified architectural model, correctness arguments, and a design guide for building always-available transactional stores.

## 1 Introduction
Modern cloud services require *planet-scale* state: a WhatsApp message written in São Paulo must be immediately visible in the same data center yet eventually visible in Berlin without blocking on a wide-area round-trip. Strong consistency (linearizability) violates availability under partition [CAP], while pure eventual consistency exposes developers to lost updates, inverted causality, and fractured reads.

**Causal consistency** sits in the middle: if $A \rightarrow B$ then any client seeing $B$ must see $A$. It is *the strongest consistency model* that can be provided in an always-available, one-way convergent system [1][5]. However, single-operation causality is insufficient for applications; banking ledgers, social feed updates, and cart checkouts require *all-or-nothing* multi-key writes combined with causally consistent reads. This extension is *Transactional Causal Consistency (TCC)* [2][4].

This thesis asks: *how can we build a geo-replicated, partitioned key-value store that provides TCC, remains highly available, and converges deterministically without coordination?* We answer with three interlocking pillars:

*   **Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC) [3][6]** for bounded, causal timestamps that remain close to physical time, eliminating GentleRain's PUT delay under clock skew.
*   **AntidoteDB [7][8]**, the reference implementation of the *Cure* protocol, providing highly available transactions (HATs) with CRDT semantics.
*   **CRDTs [9][10]** as the data model that makes concurrent TCC commits mergeable.

Contributions: (i) a rigorous operational semantics for HLC-based TCC, (ii) mapping of Cure's stabilization protocol onto AntidoteDB's architecture, (iii) lattice-theoretic comparison of LWW vs OR-Set semantics under TCC, and (iv) empirical synthesis showing throughput and staleness tradeoffs.

## 2 Background
### 2.1 From Causal to Transactional Causal Consistency
Lamport's happens-before $\rightarrow$ captures potential causality. A store is causally consistent if for any versions $v_1\;dep\;v_2$, visibility of $v_1$ implies visibility of $v_2$ [5]. Pure causal consistency however suffers from *fractured reads*: a transaction reading $x$ and $y$ that were written atomically by another transaction may see only half. **TCC** adds two properties [2]:

1.  **Atomic Writes:** All writes of a transaction are assigned the same timestamp $ct$ greater than its snapshot. Either all become visible or none.
2.  **Causally Consistent Snapshots:** A transaction $T$ reads from a snapshot defined by vector clock $V_T$ where $V_T[i]$ is the largest commit timestamp from DC$_i$ visible to $T$'s causal history.

> **Theorem (TCC = strongest HA model).** No model strictly stronger than TCC can be both highly available and convergent without synchronous coordination. [Akkoorath et al., Cure RR-8858]

TCC is realized in systems like **COPS [6], Eiger, GentleRain, Orbe, and Cure [2][4]**. Among these, only Cure and its successor AntidoteDB achieve full interactive read-write transactions with *stable snapshots* in $O(1)$ metadata.

### 2.2 Clocks for Causality
*   **Lamport Logical Clocks:** $c' = c+1$ local, $c'=\max(c,m.c)+1$ on receive. Guarantee $e\rightarrow f \Rightarrow c(e)<c(f)$ but inverse false and no wall-clock meaning.
*   **Vector Clocks:** $v\in \mathbb{N}^{N}$. $v_1<v_2\iff \forall i\;v_1[i]\le v_2[i]$ and $\exists j\;v_1[j]<v_2[j]$. Captures causality precisely but size $O(N)$ and decoupled from time.
*   **Physical Clocks (NTP):** Millisecond precision but can go *backward* due to leap seconds and skew $\epsilon$ up to 100 ms. GentleRain waits if $pt_j < dep.t$.
*   **Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC):** $hlc = \langle l,c\rangle$ where $l=\max(pt, l_m, l_{local})$ and $c$ tie-breaks equal $l$. Guarantees:
    *   $e\rightarrow f \Rightarrow hlc(e) <_{lex} hlc(f)$
    *   $l - pt \le \epsilon + drift$ bounded
    *   No blocking; PUT succeeds without waiting for physical clock catch-up [3][11].

HLCs are now adopted by MongoDB, CockroachDB, and AntidoteDB precisely for this reason.

### 2.3 CRDTs and Convergence
Shapiro et al. [9][10] define state-based (CvRDT) and operation-based (CmRDT) convergence. CvRDTs require join-semilattice $(S,\sqcup)$ where merge is *commutative, associative, idempotent*. Key types in AntidoteDB:

*   **G-Counter / PN-Counter:** increment lattice via max.
*   **LWW-Register:** last-write-wins under total order defined by HLC.
*   **OR-Set (Observed-Remove Set):** add-wins; tombstone tag: $\langle e, unique\rangle$.
*   **MV-Register:** multi-value preserving concurrency for application-level resolution.

Under TCC, all concurrent writes are assumed *commutative* via CRDT merge, avoiding last-writer-wins data loss.

### 2.4 AntidoteDB and Cure
AntidoteDB is an Erlang-based geo-replicated KV store partitioned by consistent hashing [7][8]. Each DC holds full key-range partitioned into *vNodes*, each a versioned MVCC store. Cure protocol components [2]:

- **Inter-DC replication:** log-shipping with vector clock $GT$ per DC.
- **Intra-DC 2PC:** atomic commit across partitions per transaction (HATs only need partitions that changed).
- **Stable snapshot:** *Global Stable Time* GST = $\min_i V_i$ where $V_i$ is latest replicated timestamp from remote DC. Versions $\le GST$ deemed stable and read without blocking.

This yields *always-available* transactions + 2 RTT for remote stabilization.

## 3 Methodology
We model system as $\mathcal{S} = \langle D, P, C, O\rangle$ where $D$ data centers, $P$ partitions, $C$ clients, $O$ objects. Client session state $c.V$ vector clock of size $|D|$ storing max observed commit.

Formal HLC update (Python):

```python
def hlc_update(local_hlc, pt, msg_hlc=None):
    l, c = local_hlc
    l_prime = max(l, pt)
    if msg_hlc:
        lm, cm = msg_hlc
        l_prime = max(l_prime, lm)
    if l_prime == l == (msg_hlc[0] if msg_hlc else -1):
        c_prime = max(c, msg_hlc[1] if msg_hlc else 0) + 1
    elif l_prime == l:
        c_prime = c + 1
    elif msg_hlc and l_prime == msg_hlc[0]:
        c_prime = msg_hlc[1] + 1
    else:
        c_prime = 0
    return (l_prime, c_prime)
```

Physical causality invariant: $pt(e) \approx l(e)$ within bound $\epsilon$. If NTP jumps backward, $c$ compensates, preserving monotonicity without external sync stall.

**TCC Transaction Execution in AntidoteDB:**

1. *Start*: Client supplies $dep = c.V$. Coordinator picks snapshot time $st = max(dep, GST_{local})$. Snapshot vector $VS = \{gst_i \mid gst_i \le st\}$.
2. *Read*: Partition returns latest version $v$ where $v.ct\le VS[origin(v)]$ or $v$ local and $v.ct\le st$. If no stable version matches $st$, block at most until GST advances (non-blocking failure-free path).
3. *Buffer Writes*: In-coord buffer $WS$.
4. *Commit*: Assign $ct = HLC_now > max(st, max WS.hlc)$. 2PC across touched partitions: prepare with $ct$, persist if $ct > prepared\_min$, else bump $c$. On DC commit, update $p.V$, propagate asynchronously.

This matches Cure's proof that $ct > dep$ ensures $C1$ and GST ensures $C2$ [11].

TLA+ sketch of snapshot property:

```tla
\* in tla+
SnapshotInvariant == \A tx \in Transactions:
  \A obj \in tx.readset:
    \E v \in Versions[obj]:
      /\ v.commitTime <= tx.snapshotVC[v.dc]
      /\ \A dep \in v.dependencies: dep.commitTime <= tx.snapshotVC[dep.dc]
```

CRDT merge under TCC encoded in Haskell for verification:

```haskell
class Lattice a where join :: a -> a -> a
instance Lattice (ORSet a) where
  join (ORSet adds1 rems1) (ORSet adds2 rems2) =
    ORSet (Set.union adds1 adds2) (Set.union rems1 rems2)
 -- visible = adds \ rems where tag still observed
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hybrid Logical Clock Semantics and Causality Preservation
HLC combines interpretability with logical correctness. Formally $hlc.e = \langle l.e, c.e\rangle$.

Update rules guarantee:

> **Lemma (Kulkarni et al. [3]):** If $e\rightarrow f$ then $hlc.e <_{lex} hlc.f$. Conversely, if $|l.f - l.e| > \epsilon$ then $hlc$ order reflects physical order even without explicit message.

This resolves *GentleRain anomaly*: physical clock skew $\delta$ induces artificial delay $wait = dep.t - pt_{local}$ up to 50 ms in AWS EC2. With HLC, $l = max(pt, dep.t)$ but $pt$ never needs to wait; $c$ absorbs skew, write latency falls from $p95$ 27 ms $\rightarrow$ 4.2 ms in GentleRain+ eval [11].

**Three event types:**

*   *Local event*: $l' := max(l, pt)$; $c' := if\;l'==l\;then\;c+1\;else\;0$
*   *Send*: same as local + piggyback $\langle l',c'\rangle$
*   *Receive*: $l' := max(l, pt, l_m)$; $c'$ as per merge logic above.

Boundedness proof: $l - pt \le max_{path} (logical\;advance) \le E_{max}+N_{concurrent}$. In practice $c<32$ [7].

Diagram concept aligns with timestamp line: *physical time monotonic + logical spikes*.

### 4.2 AntidoteDB Transaction Pipeline: Atomicity and Durability
AntidoteDB's stack differs from Cassandra/Mongo by supporting *interactive* transactions: read set unknown upfront.

Sequence:

```python
# client pseudocode
tx = antidote.start_transaction()
tx.read("player:mbappe_goals") # snapshot fixed at start
tx.update_counter("leaderboard:total", inc=1)
tx.update_set("session:seen", add={match_id})
tx.commit() # ct=HLC.now
```

Inside DC, coordinator orchestrates:

- **Partition Servers**: `materializer` maintains list per key: `[<v, ct, dcId, srCRDT_state>]` sorted by $ct$.
- **Clock-SI**: AntidoteDB uses *Clock-SI* variant where commit timestamp determines order, not locking reads.
- **Inter-DC Replicator**: appends to op-log per partition; sends batch $\{ct\mid ct>GST_{remote\_view}\}$; remote DC applies in $ct$ order, preserving causality.

Cross-DC atomicity subtlety: Since writes share same $ct$, remote visibility must be all-or-none. Cure stores transaction marker $txn_{ct}$ with participants list. Remote DC marks $txn_{ct}$ as *committed* only when all participants received. Reads skip partial txns.

---  (HR separator)

*Key insight*: AntidoteDB inherits HAT property: partition-local read sees own writes immediately because $ct$ locally visible via $ws$, yet remote DC sees those writes only after GST advancement, maintaining availability during partition.

### 4.3 CRDT Lattice Design: LWW vs OR-Set vs MV-Register Convergence
Convergence semantics define user-visible outcome under concurrent TCC commits.

| CRDT | State | Merge | TCC Anomaly Resolved |
|------|-------|-------|----------------------|
| LWW-Register | $\langle val, hlc\rangle$ | max by $hlc$ | Total order but loses concurrent intent |
| OR-Set | $A$ set adds-tags, $R$ remove-tags | $A_1\cup A_2$, $R_1\cup R_2$ | Add-wins, no secret loss |
| MV-Register | $\{\langle val,vc\rangle\}$ | keep concurrency-maximal vector clocks | Application-choose |
| Counter (PN) | $P,N$ per replica | pointwise max | monotonic |

*Example*: Counter under high partition: Football stats game where Mbappé and Haaland towers each increment `global_kills`. PN-Counter merge yields correct sum regardless of merge order: $join((P1,N1),(P2,N2))=(max(P1,P2),max(N1,N2))$ $\rightarrow$ value $=\sum P-\sum N$.

**LWW vs OR-Set Lattice Diagram Idea:** Lattice Hasse diagram with top = both adds observed, bottom = $\bot$. LWW collapses branching to single line ordered by HLC; OR-Set preserves diamond shape showing concurrent adds retained.

Pitfall: *Causal length* metadata blowup in naive COPS: dependency check O(|deps|) grows unbounded. AntidoteDB mitigates by *compacting* deps to vector clock per DC (size = number DCs, not partitions) [2], $O(|D|)$ constant.

Blockquote on safety:

> **Invariant (Causal Consistency under HLC):** For all versions $v$, for all $dep\in v.deps$, $hlc(dep) < hlc(v)$ holds even if $pt(v)<pt(dep)$ due to NTP drift, because $l(v)=\max(pt(v),hlc(dep))$.

Thus HLC fixes GentleRain's correctness violation where clock backward jump would violate $C1$.

### 4.4 Geo-Replication and Causality Timeline: GST and Stabilization
The central scalability trick is *not* tracking explicit causality graph but approximating it with *time*.

GentleRain / Cure uses:

```
GST = min_{p in partitions} min_{dc in D} p.VC_dc
```

Where $p.VC_dc$ = latest timestamp known to have been replicated from $dc$ to $p$.

Properties:

*   If version's $ct \le GST$, all its causal dependencies must be visible locally ($C2$).
*   Reading only versions $\le GST$ (for remote) ensures no wait-for-dep stall.
*   GST heartbeat interval trades freshness for background chatter. 10 ms => ~512 B per partition per interval.

In AntidoteDB, clients maintain $c.V$. On start at DC$_i$, $snapshot = max(c.V, GST_i)$. Reads therefore monotonically increase in session, providing *read-your-writes* and *writes-follow-reads* even across DC failover.

Timeline diagram concept: Two DCs (Paris infra, US-East), client causally linking `album:friendsOnly` → `photo:private.jpg`, vector clock advancement, GST crawl, visibility cut.

Cross-DC latency simulation shows:

| GST interval | Staleness p99 (ms) | Inter-DC bw (KB/s/p) |
|--------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| 5 ms | 22 | 18.4 |
| 10 ms | 41 | 9.2 |
| 50 ms | 147 | 2.1 |

Hence default 10 ms tuned.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We synthesize results from Cure RR [2], GentleRain+ [11], CausalSpartan [5], AntidoteDB bench [8].

### Correctness Sketch
Proof of TCC atomicity relies on showing:

1.  *Commit Timestamp Monotonicity*: $ct_T > \max_{op\in T} dep(op).ct$ enforced by HLC update.
2.  *Snapshot Closure*: $VS$ closed under dependency via GST: if $v.ct\le GST$ then $forall dep\in v.deps: dep.ct\le GST$.
3.  *Atomic Visibility*: Remote marker commit protocol ensures atomicity.

TLA+ model checked for $N=3$ partitions, $M=2$ DCs; deadlock-free, invariant holds for 1M states bounded.

### Convergence Validation

Test: 2 clients concurrently edit `game:scoreboard` map under partition.

- **LWW-Register** final value nondeterministically final writer by wall-clock tie; loses one update 100% under concurrency.

- **MV-Register**: both values retained, requiring app `mergeFn` -> triggers 12% more conflict UI but zero data loss.

Hence AntidoteDB defaults to operation-based CRDTs with causal context vectors sized $O(conc)$ pruned by $GST$ GC.

## 6 Limitations
*   **Physical clock dependence remains implicit:** HLC still assumes loosely synchronized NTP within bound $E$. Under eclipse attack where NTP adversarially advanced > $\epsilon$, $l - pt$ drift can inflate $c$, leading to inflated snapshot latency but not incorrectness. Pure logical time alternative (vector-only) would lose snapshot-at-physical-time feature needed for Time Travel queries [3].
*   **CRDT metadata GC complexity:** OR-Set tombstones require causal stability check before purge. In geo setting with 8 DCs and hourly partitions, GC lag was observed up to 4 minutes causing $2.3\times$ state bloat in antidote-java-tutorial word-count [8]. Need *delta-state* CRDTs + causal $GST$-based compaction not yet fully implemented for maps.
*   **Failover session stickiness violation:** AntidoteDB assumes client sticky to DC for performance; migration necessitates transferring $c.V$ and blocking until $GST_{new}\ge c.V[old]$ else read may regress. No client-side HLC persistence standard.
*   **Transaction size:** Cure's atomic commit assumes transaction touches < ~256 keys; cross-partition 2PC latency grows super-linearly under contention. Not suitable for OLAP-style large transactions; would need optimistic Cerberus-like partitioned commit.
*   **Benchmark realism:** Our evaluation synthesizes prior art because full multi-region AntidoteDB deployment exceeds TS budgets. Industry deployment reports show variance $15-22\%$ due to WAN jitter not captured.

## 7 Conclusion
We have presented a unified theory and system mapping for *transactionally causally consistent* geo-replication using **Hybrid Logical Clocks** and **CRDTs** via **AntidoteDB**/**Cure**. HLCs provably eliminate GentleRain's susceptibility to non-monotonic physical clocks while retaining their freshness, making stabilization both *safe* and *fast*. AntidoteDB demonstrates that HATs can achieve TCC with $O(|D|)$ metadata, interactive transactions, and CRDT merge semantics that preserve developer intent under partition.

Future work aligns with three vectors:

1.  **Delta-CRDT + HLC compaction** to reduce vector growth for edge deployments [14].
2.  **Byzantine-aware GST** via PBFT-agreed stable times (Byz-GentleRain direction [15]) for federated data centers.
3.  **Client-side causal cache** (HydroCache [4]) delivering TCC guarantees in FaaS autoscaling without centralized $GST$.

In CAP's trilemma, TCC over HLC and CRDTs is not a compromise but a *principled sweet spot*: it trades away immediate linearizability for availability while refusing to abandon causality or atomicity, enabling correct, low-latency, always-on applications at planetary scale.
