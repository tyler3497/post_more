# DAGs Under Byzantine Faults: Mempool–Consensus Separation, Zero-Overhead Ordering, and the Throughput–Latency Frontier from Narwhal to Mysticeti

## Abstract

Classical Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus couples transaction dissemination with transaction ordering inside a single leader's proposal pipeline. The resulting protocols — from PBFT's three-phase commit to HotStuff's linear view-changes — saturate at a few thousand transactions per second and stall badly under faults. This thesis studies the DAG paradigm that displaced them: Narwhal's certified-DAG mempool, which separates dissemination from ordering and scales out linearly with workers; Tusk and Bullshark, which reach consensus with zero additional messages by interpreting local DAG views through wave-structured commit rules; Mysticeti, which discards per-block certification entirely to hit the three-message-delay latency lower bound; and the asynchronous common-subset lineage from HoneyBadgerBFT through Dumbo to Dumbo-NG, which resolved the batching-induced throughput–latency tension. We formalize commit rules and quorum-intersection safety invariants, give liveness arguments under asynchrony, compare measured wide-area performance across the protocol family, and argue that mempool–consensus separation plus uncertified dissemination defines the current Pareto frontier of Byzantine state machine replication.

## 1 Introduction

For two decades after Castro and Liskov's PBFT [8], practical Byzantine fault tolerance meant *leader-based* replication: a primary batches requests, broadcasts a proposal, and drives replicas through voting phases. This architecture has a structural weakness no phase-merging can fix. As Danezis et al. observe [1], **any protocol that minimizes metadata message count but relies on a leader to disseminate bulk transaction data ignores the load that leader inevitably becomes** — the bottleneck is not consensus messages (hundreds of bytes) but blocks (megabytes). The leader's bandwidth caps throughput.

Three breakthroughs (2016–2024) jointly dismantled this architecture:

1. **Asynchronous common-subset protocols** — HoneyBadgerBFT [5] proved liveness without timing assumptions is practical, replacing fragile view-changes with randomized agreement (ACS: *n* reliable broadcasts plus *n* binary agreements). Dumbo [6] and Dumbo-NG replaced the *n* ABAs with a single multi-valued validated Byzantine agreement (MVBA) and decoupled dissemination from agreement.
2. **Mempool–consensus separation** — Narwhal [1] factored BFT into a high-throughput asynchronous *mempool* building a certified DAG, plus an *ordering* layer handling only small DAG references. HotStuff over Narwhal jumped from ~1,800 to ~170,000 tx/sec on a WAN, scaling past 600,000 tx/sec with workers.
3. **Zero-overhead ordering and uncertified DAGs** — Tusk, Bullshark, and DAG-Rider [2,4] showed consensus needs *no additional messages*: validators locally interpret DAG edges to totally order vertices. Mysticeti [3] then removed per-block certification, reaching the three-message-delay lower bound — 0.5 s WAN commits at 100k+ TPS.

This thesis gives a unified account: (i) the certified DAG construction and mempool–consensus interface; (ii) formal wave-structured commit rules for Tusk, Bullshark, and Mysticeti with safety-invariant proof sketches; (iii) liveness arguments under asynchrony, including why randomness is unavoidable (FLP); (iv) head-to-head throughput/latency analysis against PBFT and HotStuff; and (v) honest limitations: garbage collection, censorship, cryptographic assumptions, and DoS surface.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System model and atomic broadcast

We consider *n = 3f + 1* validators, at most *f* Byzantine, targeting **Byzantine Atomic Broadcast (BAB)**: *Validity*, *Agreement*, *Integrity*, *Total Order*, plus eventual inclusion of every honest validator's proposals (fairness).

**Partial synchrony**: asynchronous until an unknown *global stabilization time* (GST), then bounded delay Δ. Safety always; liveness after GST. **Asynchrony**: no timing bound ever; by the FLP impossibility result, *deterministic* consensus is impossible, so liveness requires *randomization* (common coins, randomized agreement). Hence all asynchronous protocols here are randomized, while Bullshark also ships a deterministic partially synchronous variant.

The workhorse is the **quorum**: any two *2f + 1* sets intersect in ≥ *f + 1* validators, hence ≥ 1 honest one. Every commit rule below is quorum intersection applied to DAG edges.

### 2.2 Classical baselines: PBFT and HotStuff

PBFT [8] runs *pre-prepare / prepare / commit* with *O(n²)* votes per phase; view-changes replace faulty primaries but stall under attack. HotStuff [9] streamlined this to *O(n)* via threshold signatures collected at the leader, chaining phases across views. Yet it retains the leader bottleneck: the leader disseminates the full block. Measured WAN throughput of unmodified HotStuff is ~**1,800 tx/sec** [1] — the leader's egress bandwidth is the ceiling.

### 2.3 The asynchronous pioneers

**HoneyBadgerBFT** [5] assumes nothing about timing. It reduces atomic broadcast to *asynchronous common subset* (ACS): each validator reliably broadcasts (RBC) a batch — with erasure coding cutting Bracha's RBC from *O(|B|n²)* to *O(|B|n)* — then all run *n* parallel binary agreements (ABA) on *which* RBCs to include. Threshold encryption of batches prevents content-based censorship. Cost: ACS needs *O(log n)* expected rounds, so batches must be enormous to amortize it — excellent throughput, minute-scale latency.

**Dumbo** [6] replaced the *n* ABAs with one **MVBA**, cutting agreement rounds dramatically. **Dumbo-NG** restructured the protocol so dissemination and agreement run *fully concurrently*.

## 3 Methodology

This is an *analytic and comparative* study:

- **Primary-source reconstruction.** From the original papers [1–6] we extracted protocol pseudocode, commit rules, and theorems, re-deriving safety invariants (quorum intersection over DAG causal histories) in uniform notation.
- **Benchmark synthesis.** We compare only published wide-area measurements at comparable scale (10–106 validators), normalizing to sustained throughput (tx/sec) and commit latency, and reproduce Dumbo-NG's analytic formulas and the geometric wave-commit model of Tusk/Bullshark/DAG-Rider.
- **Executable models.** A Python simulation of the wave-commit process (expected latency from per-wave commit probabilities) and a Rust sketch of Bullshark's direct-commit rule, both included below.
- **Adversarial analysis.** For each protocol we identify worst-case Byzantine behavior against its liveness argument (leader equivocation, slow-anchor stalling, censorship of slow broadcasts) and how the mechanism (fallback leaders, MVBA quality, threshold encryption) neutralizes it.

All performance numbers are cited; we claim no new measurements.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Narwhal: The Mempool–Consensus Separation

Narwhal [1] is not a consensus protocol — it is a *mempool* with consensus-grade guarantees, so any consensus layer on top need only order tiny DAG references.

**The certified DAG.** In each *round*, every validator creates one *block* containing a batch of transaction digests (bulk data disseminated separately by workers), the round number, and references to ≥ *2f + 1* blocks of the previous round (*parents*). A block becomes *certified* upon collecting *2f + 1* signatures — a **certificate of availability** proving retrievability from ≥ *f + 1* honest validators. Two properties make this a sound consensus foundation:

- **Causal history agreement.** If an honest validator holds a certified block, every honest validator eventually holds it *and* its entire causal history. Certificates plus the *2f + 1*-parent rule keep honest DAG views *consistent*.
- **Timer-free round advancement.** A validator advances to round *r + 1* upon observing *2f + 1* certified blocks of round *r*. No timeouts, no leader election, no view-change: the DAG advances at *network speed* under asynchrony and faults.

![Narwhal certified DAG structure](/thesis/ths_1788791401735_0cdd-0.webp)

**Scale-out by workers.** The primary orders only block *headers*; bulk dissemination is sharded across *workers*. Throughput scales linearly with worker count because the consensus-critical path never touches bulk data. WAN measurements: HotStuff-over-Narwhal achieves **~170,000 tx/sec at ~2.5 s latency** versus ~1,800 tx/sec for baseline HotStuff, scaling to **~600,000 tx/sec** with more workers at constant latency [1].

**Two orderings, one DAG.** Narwhal-HS layers classic HotStuff on top (ordering digests only). **Tusk** instead embeds consensus *inside* the DAG with zero extra messages, reaching **~140,000 tx/sec at ~4 s latency** — ~*20× better than the prior asynchronous state of the art* [1] — and maintaining throughput under faults where HotStuff's view-changes collapse.

### 4.2 Tusk and Bullshark: Zero-Overhead Ordering on the DAG

Once Narwhal guarantees consistent DAG views, ordering is a *local* computation: each validator applies the same deterministic rule to its local DAG, and all derive the same total order. No votes, no phases — **zero message overhead** over DAG construction.

**Waves and leaders.** Rounds are grouped into *waves*; each wave's *leader* block is selected retroactively by a shared random coin — unpredictable to the adversary beforehand, identical for all honest validators afterward. The commit rule asks: *did enough of the DAG vote for this leader?*

> **Theorem: (Wave commit safety invariant).** Let *v* be the leader of wave *w*. If an honest validator *directly commits* *v* (observes ≥ *f + 1* — resp. *2f + 1* — wave vertices whose causal history contains *v*), then *v* belongs to the causal history of *every* leader committed in any later wave by any honest validator. All honest validators therefore order the same prefix before each committed leader.

*Proof sketch.* Quorum intersection: *2f + 1* parents per vertex and *f + 1* (or *2f + 1*) referencing vertices force committed leaders of any two honest validators to share causal history through ≥ 1 honest vertex. Committing a leader commits its *entire causal past*; uncommitted leaders are ordered retroactively when a later leader's history absorbs them. ∎

| Protocol | Wave | Leader | Commit rule | P(commit/wave) | Expected latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DAG-Rider [4] | 4 rounds | random coin | *2f+1* vertices reference leader | ≥ 2/3 | ~4 rounds |
| Tusk [1] | 3 rounds | random coin | *f+1* vertices reference leader | ≥ 1/3 | ~7.5 rounds |
| Bullshark (async) [2] | 4 rounds | steady-state + fallback | *f+1* references → direct commit | ≥ 2/3 + fast path | ~2 rounds (steady state) |

**Tusk** is the minimalist: 3-round waves, ≥ 1/3 commit probability per wave, expected commit every 1.5 waves (~7.5 rounds). **DAG-Rider** uses 4-round waves with a 2/3 commit probability and is post-quantum safe under a hash-based coin [4].

**Bullshark** [2] adds the key practical insight: in the *common synchronous case*, waiting for random fallback leaders is wasteful. Each wave has **two** leader slots — a *steady-state* leader (pre-determined round-robin, committable in 2 rounds when timely) and a *fallback* leader (random coin, preserving asynchronous liveness). During synchronous periods Bullshark commits every 2 rounds with **no view-change machinery**; under asynchrony it degrades gracefully to the randomized fallback. Its deterministic partially synchronous variant needs no common coin and was implemented in **~200 lines of code** atop Narwhal — **125,000 tx/sec at 2 s latency with 50 validators**, where the fully asynchronous state of the art paid ~50% higher latency [2].

![Bullshark wave structure with steady-state and fallback leaders](/thesis/ths_1788791401735_0cdd-1.webp)

```rust
/// Direct-commit check for a wave anchor (Bullshark, partially synchronous variant).
fn try_direct_commit(dag: &Dag, wave: usize, anchor: &VertexId, f: usize) -> CommitDecision {
    let vote_round = dag.rounds(wave).last(); // round r+1 after the anchor's round r
    let votes = vote_round.iter()
        .filter(|v| v.causal_history.contains(anchor))
        .count();
    // Quorum intersection: f+1 references force the anchor into every
    // future anchor's causal history (n = 3f+1).
    if votes >= f + 1 {
        CommitDecision::Commit(order_causal_history(dag, anchor))
    } else {
        CommitDecision::Skip // may be committed indirectly by a later anchor
    }
}
```

**Garbage collection** is handled by round-based pruning: once a wave's leaders are committed, all validators agree on the prefix and discard it. Bullshark ties collectability to committed rounds rather than wall-clock time, keeping GC safe under asynchrony [2].

### 4.3 Mysticeti: Uncertified DAGs and the Three-Delay Bound

Narwhal's certification costs a *2f + 1*-signature round trip per block *before* consensus starts. Since BFT consensus needs ≥ **three message delays** (Dolev–Reischuk), stacking it on a certified DAG costs *six* — latency doubled by construction.

**Mysticeti-C** [3] drops certification: validators multicast one signed block per round, no vote collection. This reopens two problems — *equivocation* and *data availability* — solved as follows:

- **Equivocation tolerance by exclusion.** The commit rule only commits blocks with sufficient *support*; equivocating validators' blocks are detected and excluded from future parent sets. For fast-path transactions, honest validators deliver *at most one* of two conflicting values, or a later honest block supersedes them [3].
- **Availability without certification.** A block counts as available once referenced by enough later blocks; missing data is fetched on demand. The commit rule never waits indefinitely on Byzantine sources.
- **Threshold clocks.** Rounds advance on *2f + 1* (uncertified) blocks — a clock ticking at network speed with a single message type and multicast pattern.

Mysticeti-C's **novel commit rule lets every block be committed without artificial gaps** — no mandated spacing between leaders. With **pipelined, multiple leaders per round** (every validator's block committable each round), it reaches the optimum:

> **Theorem: (Latency lower bound and Mysticeti-C optimality).** Any Byzantine consensus protocol needs at least 3 message delays to commit in the common case. Mysticeti-C commits in exactly 3 message delays in the steady state and under crash faults — it is latency-optimal.

![Certified vs uncertified DAG commit pipelines](/thesis/ths_1788791401735_0cdd-3.webp)

**Mysticeti-FPC** adds a *fast commit path* for transactions not needing total order (e.g., owned-object transfers, à la FastPay/Zef), woven into the DAG with minimal extra signatures. Results: **0.5 s WAN commit latency at 100k+ TPS** — the first Byzantine protocol under half a second at six-figure throughput — with graceful degradation under crash faults. Deployed on Sui (106 validators) replacing Bullshark, it cut p50 latency **80% (1,900 ms → 400 ms)** [3]. Follow-ups (Shoal++ [10], Adelie) designate *all* validators as leaders per round (saving ~0.5 RTT) and harden the uncertified DAG against Byzantine DoS.

### 4.4 Asynchronous Common-Subset Protocols: HBBFT to Dumbo-NG

The *asynchronous* lineage attacked the **throughput–latency tension** of batching. HBBFT's *O(log n)*-round ACS forces enormous batches to amortize agreement — great throughput, minute-scale latency — and the *f* slowest validators' broadcasts may be censored; HBBFT counters with *threshold encryption* at real computational cost [5].

**Dumbo-NG** [6] found the structural fix: run dissemination and agreement **fully concurrently** — validators continuously broadcast batches in slots while MVBA instances operate on tiny progress certificates alongside. Throughput then depends only on bandwidth, latency only on agreement speed:

```
tps(Dumbo-NG)      = nB / (nB/w + τ)
latency(Dumbo-NG)  ≈ nB/w + τ + 1.5 · T_BA
```

(*nB* = per-slot batch volume, *w* = bandwidth, *τ* = RTT, *T_BA* = MVBA latency.) Contrast HBBFT's `tps = nB / (nB/w + τ + T_BA + T_TPKE)`: the agreement term *vanishes from the throughput denominator*. The MVBA's **quality property** (output is an honest input with probability ≥ 1/2) guarantees every honest validator's broadcasts are eventually included — **censorship resistance with no threshold encryption** [6].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Head-to-head comparison

Published WAN measurements; throughput is sustained maximum, latency is submit-to-commit.

| Protocol | Model | Commit latency | Throughput | Latency | Amortized comm./tx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PBFT [8] | partial sync | 3 phases, *O(n²)* | ~1–2k tx/s | ~1 s | *O(n²)* |
| HotStuff [9] | partial sync | 3-chain, *O(n)* | ~1.8k tx/s | ~1 s | *O(n)* |
| HotStuff-over-Narwhal [1] | partial sync | same + mempool | **170k tx/s** (600k w/ workers) | 2.5 s | *O(n)* |
| Tusk [1] | **async** | ~7.5 rounds (exp.) | **140k tx/s** | ~4 s | *O(n)* |
| Bullshark [2] | async + PS fast path | 2 rounds (steady state) | **125k tx/s** (50 nodes) | 2 s | *O(n)* |
| Mysticeti-C [3] | partial sync | **3 delays (optimal)** | **100k+ tx/s** | **0.5 s** | *O(n)* |
| HoneyBadgerBFT [5] | **async** | *O(log n)* (exp.) | ~10–20k tx/s | minutes | *O(n)* |
| Dumbo-NG [6] | **async** | *O(1)* (exp.) | ~40k+ tx/s | ~2–9 s | *O(n)* |

Two conclusions. **Mempool–consensus separation is worth two orders of magnitude**: HotStuff-over-Narwhal's 1.8k → 170k tx/sec comes from removing bulk data from the consensus path, not a faster ordering rule. **The latency frontier moved from ~4 s (Tusk) to 0.5 s (Mysticeti)** almost entirely by deleting certification round trips — in DAG-BFT, *latency is a function of pre-commit round trips, not commit-rule sophistication*.

### 5.2 The wave-commit latency model

Expected commit latency follows a geometric distribution over per-wave commit probability *p*:

```python
def expected_commit_latency_rounds(p: float, wave_len: int) -> float:
    """Expected rounds until a wave commits, given per-wave commit prob p."""
    expected_waves = 1.0 / p            # geometric expectation
    return wave_len * (expected_waves - 0.5)

for name, p, w in [("DAG-Rider", 2/3, 4), ("Tusk", 1/3, 3), ("Bullshark-fallback", 2/3, 4)]:
    print(f"{name:20s} E[latency] = {expected_commit_latency_rounds(p, w):.1f} rounds")
# DAG-Rider            E[latency] = 4.0 rounds
# Tusk                 E[latency] = 7.5 rounds
# Bullshark-fallback   E[latency] = 4.0 rounds
```

This reproduces the published expectations (Tusk ~7.5 rounds [1]) and shows why Bullshark's steady-state path — *p = 1* every 2 rounds under synchrony — dominates: it replaces a geometric wait with a deterministic 2-round commit.

### 5.3 Liveness arguments under asynchrony

> **Theorem: (FLP — randomness is necessary).** No deterministic protocol guarantees both safety and liveness for Byzantine agreement under full asynchrony. Every asynchronous protocol in this thesis is therefore randomized.

The families randomize differently:

- **Tusk / Bullshark / DAG-Rider**: a *threshold coin* elects each wave's leader unpredictably. The adversary cannot target the leader in advance, so each wave commits with constant probability (*1/3* Tusk, *2/3* DAG-Rider/Bullshark-fallback) — expected constant rounds, liveness with probability 1.
- **HBBFT / Dumbo / Dumbo-NG**: *randomized ABA/MVBA* terminates in expected constant rounds; Dumbo-NG's MVBA **quality property** additionally defeats censorship without encryption [6].
- **Mysticeti-C**: *partially synchronous* and deterministic, with liveness after GST. Threshold clocks advance without timers, so the DAG never stalls on a slow leader; the multi-leader rule needs only *2f + 1* timely validators for progress.

Safety holds *even under full asynchrony* in all cases (post-quantum for DAG-Rider/Bullshark with a hash-based coin [2,4]) — the standard partial-synchrony contract.

## 6 Limitations

**Garbage collection.** A DAG that never forgets grows unboundedly. Round-based GC (Bullshark) and epoch checkpoints (Mysticeti) tie collectability to committed state — a subtlety behind real deployment bugs.

**Censorship and fairness.** Tusk-family protocols guarantee *eventual* inclusion, but asynchrony lets an adversary delay a targeted validator's blocks arbitrarily (its vertices never gather references in time). Dumbo-NG's MVBA quality and HBBFT's threshold encryption are the strongest answers; Mysticeti inherits weaker eventual fairness.

**Cryptographic assumptions.** Threshold coins need distributed key generation (trusted setup in the simplest form); DAG-Rider's post-quantum claim requires an information-theoretic coin [4]. Uncertified DAGs trade cryptographic weight for protocol complexity — Mysticeti's equivocation-exclusion is subtle, and Adelie was needed to harden it against equivocation-flooding DoS.

**Evaluation gaps.** Published numbers use different transaction sizes, validator counts, and topologies — §5.1 compares *across* studies, not within one testbed. Tail latency under *Byzantine* (not crash) faults is under-reported everywhere. Analyses assume *static* corruption; adaptive adversaries reacting to observed randomness are largely unaddressed.

**Complexity budget.** Bullshark's 200-line ordering layer is simple — atop Narwhal's certified-DAG machinery (certificates, worker sharding), which is not. Mysticeti inverts this: a trivial DAG (one message type, one multicast) with a sophisticated commit rule. Teams choose *where* to pay.

## 7 Conclusion

The decade from HoneyBadgerBFT to Mysticeti rewrote BFT around three ideas. **First**, dissemination and ordering are different problems: Narwhal showed a certified-DAG mempool at network speed, scaled with workers, lifts any ordering protocol by two orders of magnitude. **Second**, once the DAG exists, consensus is free: Tusk and Bullshark proved local interpretation of DAG edges — wave leaders plus quorum-intersection commit rules — totally orders the DAG with zero extra messages, with Bullshark's steady-state/fallback duality making it practical. **Third**, latency is dominated by pre-consensus round trips: Mysticeti's uncertified DAGs hit the three-message-delay bound — 0.5 s WAN commits at 100k+ TPS. In parallel, the asynchronous common-subset lineage (HBBFT → Dumbo → Dumbo-NG) discovered separation too, resolving the batching throughput–latency tension via concurrent dissemination and MVBA with the quality property.

Open problems remain at the theory–deployment boundary: aggressive yet provably safe GC, censorship resistance stronger than eventual fairness without heavy cryptography, and DoS-hardened uncertified DAGs. The direction is settled: *parallelize dissemination across all validators, order by interpreting the DAG, and never let bulk data touch the critical path of consensus.*
