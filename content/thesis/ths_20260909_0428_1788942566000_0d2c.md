# Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems and Reverse Mathematics: Arithmetization, Self-Reference, and the Big Five Subsystems of Second-Order Arithmetic

## Abstract

Gödel's incompleteness theorems established that no consistent, computably axiomatizable theory extending elementary arithmetic can be complete, nor can it prove its own consistency. This thesis reconstructs the theorems from first principles — Gödel numbering and arithmetization, the representability of primitive recursive functions, and the diagonal (fixed-point) lemma — and then reframes their legacy through two modern research programmes: **reverse mathematics**, which calibrates the exact set-existence axioms needed to prove ordinary theorems by establishing equivalences over the weak base theory RCA₀, and **ordinal analysis**, which measures theories by the supremum of provable well-orderings. We present the Big Five subsystems of second-order arithmetic (RCA₀, WKL₀, ACA₀, ATR₀, Π¹₁-CA₀) in an implication lattice with canonical reversals, Gentzen's ε₀-consistency proof of PA and the associated proof-theoretic ordinals, Löb's theorem and the derivability conditions, and the Paris–Harrington and Kirby–Paris independence results as natural mathematical witnesses to incompleteness. The thesis closes with the known limitations of each programme and open problems in the reverse-mathematical zoo.

## 1 Introduction

In 1931, Kurt Gödel demonstrated that the dream of a complete, self-certifying axiomatization of mathematics — Hilbert's program — was unattainable in principle. The **first incompleteness theorem** states that any consistent formal theory that is computably axiomatizable and strong enough to develop elementary arithmetic contains a true sentence it can neither prove nor refute [1]. The **second incompleteness theorem** states that such a theory cannot prove the arithmetical statement expressing its own consistency.

Gödel's argument rests on *arithmetization*: syntax itself — symbols, formulas, proofs — is encoded as natural numbers via **Gödel numbering**, so that metamathematical relations such as "is a proof of" become arithmetical relations representable inside the theory [4]. Once the theory can talk about its own syntax, a fixed-point construction (the **diagonal lemma**) produces a sentence asserting its own unprovability — self-reference made mathematically precise via the recursion theorem applied to definable predicates.

The second half of this thesis examines how proof theory internalized Gödel's limits. Two programmes dominate. **Reverse mathematics** asks which axioms are *necessary* to prove a given theorem: working in second-order arithmetic over the weak base theory RCA₀, it seeks equivalences between theorems and axioms. Astonishingly, the vast majority of classical theorems fall into exactly five equivalence classes — the **Big Five**: RCA₀, WKL₀, ACA₀, ATR₀, and Π¹₁-CA₀ [2]. **Ordinal analysis**, inaugurated by Gentzen's ε₀-consistency proof of PA, measures *how much* transfinite induction a theory proves; the proof-theoretic ordinal |PA| = ε₀ gauges consistency strength and computational power simultaneously [3]. These threads are joined by incompleteness itself: the Paris–Harrington theorem gives a *natural* combinatorial statement unprovable in PA [5], and Löb's theorem reveals the modal logic of self-referential provability underlying the second theorem.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Formal theories of arithmetic

We work with **L₁ = {0, S, +, ·, <}**. **Q** (Robinson arithmetic) axiomatizes the basic algebraic behavior of these symbols without induction. **Peano arithmetic (PA)** extends Q with the full induction schema. The schema stratifies by formula complexity: **IΣₙ** is induction for Σₙ-formulas, and PA = ⋃ₙ IΣₙ. Bounded induction, collection, and bounding principles (BΣₙ) give a fine-grained hierarchy of weak fragments, each with its own conservation and independence phenomena [6].

### 2.2 Primitive recursive functions and representability

A function f: ℕᵏ → ℕ is **primitive recursive** if built from zero, successor, and projections by composition and primitive recursion. Every primitive recursive function is **representable** in Q: there is a formula R_f such that Q proves R_f(n̄, y) ↔ y = f(n)̄ for all numerals n̄, and proves R_f functional [1]. Relations are handled analogously. This representability is the bridge between computability and provability on which arithmetization stands.

### 2.3 Second-order arithmetic and Hilbert's program

**L₂** has number variables x, y, z ranging over ℕ and set variables X, Y, Z ranging over subsets of ℕ, with a comprehension schema whose restriction yields the subsystems of §4.4 [2]. Hilbert sought a *finitary* consistency proof for infinitary mathematics; Gödel's second theorem showed no such proof can be formalized inside the system whose consistency is at issue [1]. Gentzen's later consistency proof for PA used transfinite induction up to ε₀ — unprovable in PA itself — showing exactly how much infinitary commitment consistency requires, and inaugurating ordinal proof theory [3].

## 3 Methodology

The methodology of this thesis is that of modern proof theory: *formalize, arithmetize, calibrate*.

**Arithmetization.** Fix an effective Gödel numbering ⌜·⌝ mapping syntactic objects to natural numbers such that substitution, concatenation, and proof-checking are primitive recursive [4]. Feferman's framework shows that for any reasonable axiomatization, the proof predicate Proof_T(x, y) can be chosen Δ₁ and the provability predicate Prov_T(y) ≡ ∃x Proof_T(x, y) Σ₁, satisfying the **Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions** [4]: (1) T ⊢ φ implies T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜φ⌝); (2) T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜φ → ψ⌝) → (Prov_T(⌜φ⌝) → Prov_T(⌜ψ⌝)); (3) T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜φ⌝) → Prov_T(⌜Prov_T(⌜φ⌝)⌝). These distill exactly what the second theorem needs from provability.

**Diagonalization.** The diagonal lemma: for every formula ψ(x) there is a sentence θ with T ⊢ θ ↔ ψ(⌜θ⌝), for any T ⊇ Q [1]. Applied to ψ(x) ≡ ¬Prov_T(x) it yields the Gödel sentence; applied to ψ(x) ≡ (Prov_T(x) → φ) it yields Löb's theorem.

**Reverse-mathematical calibration.** Fix base theory RCA₀; formalize a theorem τ of ordinary mathematics in L₂; prove τ in some S ⊇ RCA₀; then *reverse* it: RCA₀ ⊢ τ → (characteristic axiom of S). Success means S is exactly the axiomatic strength of τ [2].

**Ordinal calibration.** |T| = sup{α : α a primitive recursive notation, T ⊢ WF(α)} [3]. The value is sharp: PA proves TI(α) for all α < ε₀ but not TI(ε₀) — the latter would yield Con(PA), contradicting Theorem 2.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Gödel Numbering and the Arithmetization of Syntax

Assign distinct odd numbers to primitive symbols, then encode sequences via prime exponents: ⟨a₀, …, aₖ⟩ ↦ ∏ᵢ pᵢ^(aᵢ+1). Unique factorization makes decoding primitive recursive, so the predicates "n codes a term", "n codes a formula", "m codes a proof of the formula coded by n" are all primitive recursive and representable in Q [1][4].

```python
# Toy Gödel numbering: symbols -> odd codes, sequences -> prime exponents
from sympy import primerange

SYM = {'0': 1, 'S': 3, '+': 5, '*': 7, '=': 9, '<': 11,
       '(': 13, ')': 15, 'v': 17, '~': 19, '->': 21}

def seq_code(seq):
    """Code a finite sequence of natural numbers via prime exponents."""
    n = 1
    for p, a in zip(primerange(2, 10 ** 6), seq):
        n *= p ** (a + 1)          # +1 so that 0 is distinguishable
    return n

def godel_of_term(symbols):
    """Gödel number of a term given as a list of symbol codes."""
    return seq_code([SYM[s] for s in symbols])

print(godel_of_term(['S', '(', 'S', '(', '0', ')', ')']))
```

For computably axiomatized T, Proof_T(m, n) is primitive recursive, so Q represents it by Prf_T(x, y), and Prov_T(y) ≡ ∃x Prf_T(x, y) is Σ₁. Σ₁-ness is essential: true Σ₁ sentences are provable in Q (Σ₁-completeness), which powers derivability condition (1) and the proof that the Gödel sentence is true but unprovable [1]. Feferman [4] showed the construction is robust: any two standard provability predicates for the same theory are provably equivalent in PA.

### 4.2 The Diagonal Lemma and the First Incompleteness Theorem

Let diag map ⌜φ(x)⌝ to ⌜φ(⌜φ(x)⌝)⌝, represented by D(x, y). For given ψ, define θ(x) ≡ ∃y (D(x, y) ∧ ψ(y)) and let θ be θ(⌜θ(x)⌝). Then T ⊢ θ ↔ ψ(⌜θ⌝) [1]: *for every property ψ of codes, some sentence says "I have property ψ."*

```haskell
-- The diagonal lemma as a fixed-point combinator on codes
type Code = Integer

-- subst c d : code of the formula from substituting numeral d
--             for the free variable in the formula coded by c
subst :: Code -> Code -> Code
subst c d = undefined  -- primitive recursive; representable in Q

-- diagonal: given psi, return fixed point theta with
-- T |- theta <-> psi <theta>
diagonal :: (Code -> Code) -> Code -> Code
diagonal psi c =
  let theta = subst c c        -- self-application: quining
  in theta                     -- satisfies theta <-> psi theta
```

With ψ(x) ≡ ¬Prov_T(x) we obtain the **Gödel sentence** G_T, with T ⊢ G_T ↔ ¬Prov_T(⌜G_T⌝): "I am not provable in T."

> **Theorem 1 (Gödel's First Incompleteness Theorem):** Let T be consistent, computably axiomatizable, with Q ⊆ T. Then T ⊬ G_T and T ⊬ ¬G_T; moreover G_T is true in ℕ.

*Proof sketch.* If T ⊢ G_T, then T ⊢ ¬Prov_T(⌜G_T⌝) by the fixed point, yet the concrete proof gives T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜G_T⌝) by Σ₁-completeness — contradiction. If T ⊢ ¬G_T, then T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜G_T⌝), i.e. T proves ∃m Prf_T(m, ⌜G_T⌝); Σ₁-completeness makes this sound only if such an m exists — again contradicting consistency. Truth of G_T follows since it asserts its own unprovability [1]. Rosser's variant needs only plain consistency: the Rosser sentence asserts "for every proof of me there is a smaller proof of my negation" [1].

### 4.3 The Second Incompleteness Theorem, Löb's Theorem, and Reflection

Let Con(T) ≡ ¬Prov_T(⌜0 = 1⌝). Formalizing the proof of Theorem 1 *inside* T gives T ⊢ Con(T) → G_T, using the derivability conditions to push Prov_T through the logical steps [1][4]. Since T ⊬ G_T:

> **Theorem 2 (Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem):** Let T be consistent, computably axiomatizable, with Q ⊆ T. Then T ⊬ Con(T).

The argument relativizes: PA proves Con(IΣₙ) for each standard n, but no finite fragment proves its own consistency uniformly [6].

**Löb's theorem** is the deeper modal fact behind Theorem 2:

> **Theorem 3 (Löb):** If T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜φ⌝) → φ, then T ⊢ φ.

Diagonalize ψ(x) ≡ (Prov_T(x) → φ) to get θ with T ⊢ θ ↔ (Prov_T(⌜θ⌝) → φ); a short derivation with the derivability conditions collapses θ to φ. Theorem 2 is the case φ ≡ (0 = 1) [1]. Modal provability logic **GL** (with □(□p → p) → □p) is exactly the logic of Prov_T: GL ⊢ φ iff PA ⊢ φ* for all arithmetical realizations (Solovay's arithmetical completeness theorem) [1].

```tla+
---- MODULE Provability ----
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANT Phi            \* a sentence of the language of arithmetic
VARIABLE prov           \* prov[n] : is the formula coded by n provable?

\* Hilbert-Bernays-Loeb derivability conditions (schematic)
HB2 == \A a, b \in Nat :
         prov[impCode(a, b)] => (prov[a] => prov[b])
HB3 == \A a \in Nat : prov[a] => prov[provCode(a)]

\* Loeb's theorem: (Prov(<phi>) -> phi) provable implies phi provable
Loeb == prov[impCode(provCode(codeOf(Phi)), codeOf(Phi))]
        => prov[codeOf(Phi)]
====
```

### 4.4 The Big Five: Subsystems of Second-Order Arithmetic

**RCA₀** (Δ⁰₁-comprehension + Σ⁰₁-induction) is the base camp: its ω-models are exactly the Turing ideals, and its provably total functions are the primitive recursive ones [2].

| System | Added axiom (over RCA₀) | Proof-theoretic ordinal | Canonical reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RCA₀** | Δ⁰₁-comprehension, Σ⁰₁-induction | ω³ | — (base theory) |
| **WKL₀** | Weak König's lemma: every infinite binary tree has a path | ω³ (Π⁰₂-conservative over RCA₀) | Heine–Borel theorem; Gödel completeness theorem; Brouwer fixed point |
| **ACA₀** | Arithmetical comprehension | ε₀ | Bolzano–Weierstrass; full König's lemma; RT³₂ |
| **ATR₀** | Arithmetical transfinite recursion | Γ₀ (Feferman–Schütte) | Perfect set theorem; comparability of well-orderings |
| **Π¹₁-CA₀** | Π¹₁-comprehension | far beyond Γ₀ | Cantor–Bendixson theorem; Σ⁰₁-determinacy |

The implications RCA₀ → WKL₀ → ACA₀ → ATR₀ → Π¹₁-CA₀ are strict, and each system is characterized by ordinary theorems provably equivalent to it over RCA₀ [2]:

- **WKL₀** captures *compactness arguments*: Heine–Borel, the completeness theorem for countable languages, the separable Hahn–Banach theorem, and maximal ideals in countable commutative rings. Harrington's Π¹₁-conservativity of WKL₀ over RCA₀ makes these "finitistically reducible" [2].
- **ACA₀** captures *sequential compactness and arithmetical definability*: Bolzano–Weierstrass, the Turing jump, König's lemma for finitely branching trees.
- **ATR₀** captures *transfinite recursion*: the perfect set theorem, comparability of countable well-orderings, Ulm's theorem for countable abelian groups [2].
- **Π¹₁-CA₀** captures *hyperarithmetical* mathematics: Cantor–Bendixson and open determinacy.

```rust
// The Big Five as a strict implication lattice (conceptual model)
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord)]
enum BigFive { Rca0, Wkl0, Aca0, Atr0, Pi11Ca0 }

impl BigFive {
    fn proof_theoretic_ordinal(&self) -> &'static str {
        match self {
            BigFive::Rca0 | BigFive::Wkl0 => "omega^3",
            BigFive::Aca0 => "epsilon_0",
            BigFive::Atr0 => "Gamma_0 (Feferman-Schutte)",
            BigFive::Pi11Ca0 => "psi_0(Omega_omega) scale and beyond",
        }
    }
    fn strictly_above(&self, other: BigFive) -> bool {
        use BigFive::*;
        matches!((self, other),
            (Pi11Ca0, Atr0) | (Atr0, Aca0) | (Aca0, Wkl0) | (Wkl0, Rca0))
    }
}
```

The Big Five phenomenon is *empirical*: reverse mathematics could have found hundreds of incomparable systems — and below ACA₀, in the "zoo" of Ramsey-theoretic principles (RT²₂, ADS, CAC, COH), it does [7]. That the mainstream of classical analysis, algebra, and topology collapses onto five levels remains the subject's central mystery [2].

### 4.5 Ordinal Analysis and Consistency Strength

Gentzen proved Con(PA) by assigning each PA-proof an ordinal α < ε₀ and showing cut-elimination strictly decreases it; well-foundedness of ε₀ then rules out a proof of 0 = 1 [3]. The analysis is *optimal*: PA proves TI(α) for every α < ε₀ but not TI(ε₀), which would yield Con(PA).

The **proof-theoretic ordinal** |T| simultaneously gauges consistency strength, computational power (the provably total recursive functions of PA are exactly the fast-growing hierarchy F_α for α < ε₀), and Π⁰₂-consequences via Schütte cut-elimination [3]:

| Theory | Proof-theoretic ordinal | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| PRA | ω^ω | Finitistic reasoning |
| PA, ACA₀ | ε₀ | Limit of ω, ω^ω, ω^(ω^ω), … |
| ATR₀ | Γ₀ | Feferman–Schütte ordinal |
| Π¹₁-CA₀ | ψ₀(Ω_ω) scale | Beyond predicative provability |

```python
# Fast-growing hierarchy: PA proves totality of F_alpha for alpha < epsilon_0
def F(alpha, n):
    """Fast-growing hierarchy on toy ordinal notations."""
    if alpha == 0:
        return n + 1
    if isinstance(alpha, int):          # successor ordinal k+1: iterate
        r = n
        for _ in range(n):
            r = F(alpha - 1, r)
        return r
    tag, k = alpha
    if tag == 'w':                      # limit: diagonalize at n
        return F(('w', n), n)
    raise ValueError("unknown notation")

print(F(2, 3))   # F_2 already Ackermann-like; F_omega dominates it
```

Ordinal analysis converts Gödel's negative result into positive calibration: no single theory captures all truth, but theories can be linearly ordered by the transfinite induction they afford [3].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Natural independence: the Paris–Harrington theorem

Let PH(k, m, n, N) state that every coloring f: [N]ⁿ → k has a *relatively large* homogeneous H ⊆ N with |H| ≥ m (|H| ≥ min(H)). The **Paris–Harrington principle** ∀k,m,n ∃N PH(k, m, n, N) is a true Π⁰₂ statement of finite Ramsey theory, provable in ZF, but:

> **Theorem 4 (Paris–Harrington):** PA ⊬ ∀k,m,n ∃N PH(k, m, n, N).

In PA the principle implies Con(PA): the Paris–Harrington function grows so fast that its totality yields TI up to ε₀ [5][8]. By Theorem 2, PA cannot prove it. Kirby and Paris [6] made the result *accessible* via indicator arguments in nonstandard models, and Ketonen–Solovay calibrated the growth rate against the fast-growing hierarchy below ε₀.

### 5.2 Reversals: the method in action

Representative equivalences over RCA₀ [2]:

1. **WKL₀ ↔ Heine–Borel theorem.** The reversal codes a pathless binary tree into an open cover of [0,1] with no finite subcover.
2. **ACA₀ ↔ Bolzano–Weierstrass.** The reversal extracts the Turing jump from a purported modulus of convergence.
3. **ATR₀ ↔ perfect set theorem.** The reversal builds the hyperarithmetical hierarchy along a purported counterexample.
4. **Π¹₁-CA₀ ↔ Cantor–Bendixson theorem.** The reversal codes Π¹₁-comprehension into the Cantor–Bendixson rank [2].

Each reversal shows the set-existence axiom is not merely sufficient but *necessary*, pinning the theorem's logical strength exactly.

### 5.3 Beyond the Big Five: the Ramsey-theoretic zoo

RT²₂ resists classification: strictly between ACA₀ and RCA₀, it neither implies WKL₀ (Liu) nor follows from it, and decomposes as SRT²₂ + COH [7]. Hirschfeldt and Shore showed ADS and CAC split similarly into stable and cohesive parts, with WKL₀ incomparable with all of them — a genuine *zoo* below ACA₀ [7]. The zoo refines the Big Five picture without overturning it: the mainstream of analysis still lands on the five levels, while the interval (RCA₀, ACA₀) hosts many incomparable principles. Whether SRT²₂ implies RT²₂ remains the flagship open problem [2][7].

### 5.4 Proof-theoretic calibrations

Gentzen's |PA| = ε₀ extends across the spectrum: |PRA| = ω^ω, |ATR₀| = Γ₀, and impredicative theories reach the Bachmann–Howard ordinal and beyond via collapsing functions [3]. A landmark bridge: **WKL₀ is Π⁰₂-conservative over PRA** (Sieg), so the compactness arguments of ordinary analysis add no new Π⁰₂ consequences beyond finitistic reasoning — a partial vindication of Hilbert's program after Gödel [2].

## 6 Limitations

**The second theorem's hypotheses are delicate.** Theorem 2 requires the derivability conditions; nonstandard provability predicates (Rosser-style or "slow" provability) can evade it — Feferman constructed binumerations for which T proves its own "consistency" [4]. Willard showed weak theories lacking total exponentiation can prove the consistency of stronger ones. The theorem concerns *standard* provability, not every arithmetized consistency notion.

**Ordinal notation dependence.** The values |T|_Π⁰₁ and |T|_Π⁰₂ depend on the chosen notation system; only |T|_WF is notation-independent, and it gauges consistency strength only coarsely [3].

**The Big Five may be an artifact of the sample.** The zoo below ACA₀ [7] shows incomparable systems proliferate once one leaves the classical mainstream. Whether the Big Five reflects a deep fact about mathematical practice or the limited diet of digested theorems remains debated.

**Incompleteness is not a practical obstacle.** The independent statements — Gödel sentences, Paris–Harrington, Goodstein — are remote from working mathematics; no mainstream theorem is known independent of ZFC. Incompleteness limits *foundational completeness*, not mathematical practice.

## 7 Conclusion

Gödel's incompleteness theorems, proved by arithmetizing syntax and diagonalizing the provability predicate, established permanent limits on formalization: consistency implies incompleteness, and consistency is unprovable from within [1]. But the theorems were not merely negative. **Reverse mathematics** calibrates theorems against the Big Five subsystems, revealing that ordinary mathematics organizes into five sharply separated levels of set-existence strength, with exact reversals certifying each placement [2]. **Ordinal analysis** calibrates theories against transfinite induction, assigning PA the ordinal ε₀ and recovering consistency, computational power, and Π⁰₂-consequences from a single invariant [3]. Natural independence results — Paris–Harrington [5][8], Kirby–Paris indicators [6] — show incompleteness is visible in finite combinatorics, not only metamathematics. Open frontiers remain: the structure of the zoo below ACA₀ [7], notation-independence of proof-theoretic ordinals, and the ultimate explanation of the Big Five phenomenon. Gödel showed that no axiom system finishes mathematics; reverse mathematics and ordinal analysis show that we can nonetheless map, with precision, exactly how far each system reaches.
