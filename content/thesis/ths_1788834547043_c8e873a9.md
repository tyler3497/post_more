# Federated Learning at Scale: Convergence of FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD under Non-IID Data, the Bonawitz Secure-Aggregation Protocol, Differential-Privacy Integration, and Production Systems with Straggler Mitigation and Communication Compression

## Abstract

We present a unified treatment of federated learning (FL) systems at production scale, integrating optimization theory, cryptographic aggregation, and formal privacy. We analyze Federated Averaging (FedAvg) [1], Federated Proximal (FedProx) [3], and SCAFFOLD [4] under statistical heterogeneity, deriving how client drift degrades convergence when local data are non-IID [2]. FedProx regularizes local objectives with a proximal term while SCAFFOLD eliminates drift with control variates, each trading communication against convergence rate. We then develop the Bonawitz et al. secure-aggregation protocol [5], which hides individual updates behind pairwise Diffie-Hellman masks recoverable under dropout via Shamir secret sharing, and compose it with client-level differential privacy through calibrated Gaussian noise. Finally, we map theory to production: the Flower framework [6], the FATE platform, straggler-mitigation strategies including over-selection and deadline-based partial aggregation, and communication-compression operators (quantization, sparsification). The result is a rigorous reference connecting convergence guarantees, privacy accounting, and engineering practice for large-scale cross-device and cross-silo deployments.

## 1 Introduction

Federated learning (FL) is a distributed optimization paradigm in which a *central aggregator* coordinates the training of a shared model across a population of clients that retain their data locally [1]. The canonical setting is **cross-device FL**: millions of mobile phones participate intermittently, each with a small, highly non-representative local dataset, unreliable connectivity, and strict latency budgets. The complementary **cross-silo FL** setting involves a handful of reliable institutions (hospitals, banks) with large local datasets that must collaborate without exchanging raw records.

Formally, FL solves

> **Definition 1 (Federated objective).** Let there be \(N\) clients. Client \(k\) holds a local empirical risk \(F_k(w) = \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_k} \ell(w; x_i, y_i)\) over its private dataset \(\mathcal{P}_k\) of size \(n_k\). The global objective is the mixture
>
> $$f(w) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} p_k\, F_k(w), \qquad p_k = \frac{n_k}{\sum_j n_j},$$
>
> minimized over model parameters \(w \in \mathbb{R}^d\).

The aggregator may never observe \(\mathcal{P}_k\) or even the individual update \(\Delta_k\). Three tensions dominate:

1. **Statistical heterogeneity.** Local distributions \(\mathcal{D}_k\) differ, so \(\nabla F_k(w) \neq \nabla f(w)\); local objectives pull in conflicting directions. This *client drift* is the central pathology of FL optimization [2, 4].
2. **Systems heterogeneity.** Clients differ in compute, bandwidth, and availability; slow *stragglers* bottleneck synchronous rounds, and dropouts are routine.
3. **Privacy and confidentiality.** Model updates leak information about training data through gradient-inversion attacks, so aggregation itself must be cryptographically protected [5] and often perturbed to satisfy differential privacy.

Below, Section 2 fixes notation, Section 3 the methodology, Section 4 the four-part deep dive, Section 5 empirical results and proofs, Section 6 limitations, and Section 7 the conclusion.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Federated Averaging

McMahan et al. [1] introduced **FedAvg**, the workhorse of FL. Each communication round \(t\) proceeds as follows:

1. The server broadcasts the current global model \(w^t\) to a sampled subset \(S_t\) of clients.
2. Each sampled client \(k\) runs \(E\) local epochs of SGD (or full-batch GD) on \(F_k\), producing \(w_k^{t+1}\).
3. The server aggregates: \(w^{t+1} = \sum_{k \in S_t} \frac{n_k}{n_{S_t}} w_k^{t+1}\).

The key innovation is *local computation*: multiple local steps amortize the cost of communication. Under IID data, FedAvg matches minibatch SGD rates [2]; under non-IID data, the theory is far more delicate.

### 2.2 A Taxonomy of Non-IID Data

Following the literature [2, 7], statistical heterogeneity is decomposed into five (non-exclusive) skew types:

| Skew type | Description | Canonical example |
|---|---|---|
| **Label distribution skew** | \(P_k(y)\) varies across clients | Each phone's photos contain different object classes |
| **Feature distribution skew** | \(P_k(x)\) varies | Handwriting styles differ across writers (FEMNIST) |
| **Feature concept skew** | \(P_k(y \mid x)\) varies | Same image labeled differently by region |
| **Label concept skew** | \(P_k(x \mid y)\) varies | Same label, different appearance (e.g., huskies vs. wolves) |
| **Quantity skew** | \(n_k\) varies | Power users contribute 100x more data |

The standard experimental knob is the **Dirichlet partition** (class proportions from \(\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)\); small \(\alpha\) gives extreme label skew) [2].

### 2.3 Threat Model and Privacy Notions

We adopt the **honest-but-curious aggregator** threat model [5]: the server follows the protocol but attempts to infer private information from everything it observes. Clients may drop out arbitrarily but do not collude with the server beyond a threshold fraction. Two defenses are composed:

- **Secure aggregation (SecAgg).** The server learns only the *sum* \(\sum_k \Delta_k\) and nothing about individual \(\Delta_k\), via cryptographic masking.
- **Differential privacy (DP).** The released aggregate is perturbed so that the inclusion of any single client's data changes the output distribution only slightly, quantified by \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-DP.

SecAgg protects *confidentiality against the aggregator*; DP protects *against downstream consumers* of the released model [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology has three components: (i) a unified round-based execution model shared by all algorithms; (ii) a heterogeneity model parameterizing the convergence analysis; and (iii) an experimental protocol for empirical comparison.

### 3.1 The Federated Round

Every algorithm studied here instantiates the same round template:

```python
def federated_round(server_state, clients, sampler, local_solver, aggregator):
    cohort = sampler.select(clients)              # e.g. uniform C-fraction
    updates = []
    for k in cohort:                              # parallel, unreliable
        try:
            delta_k = local_solver(k, server_state.model)  # E local steps
            updates.append((k, delta_k))
        except (Timeout, Dropout):
            continue                              # straggler / dropout
    agg = aggregator.secure_sum(updates)          # Bonawitz masking [5]
    server_state.model = server_state.model + aggregator.step(agg)
    return server_state
```

FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD differ only in `local_solver` and `server_state`, isolating the *algorithmic* correction of client drift.

### 3.2 Heterogeneity Assumptions

Convergence analyses parameterize non-IID severity through bounded-dissimilarity assumptions. The two standard forms are:

> **Assumption A (Bounded gradient dissimilarity).** There exist \(\beta \geq 1\), \(\kappa \geq 0\) such that for all \(w\),
>
> $$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \|\nabla F_k(w)\|^2 \;\leq\; \beta^2 \|\nabla f(w)\|^2 + \kappa^2.$$

> **Assumption B (Bounded variance at optimum).** Let \(w^\star\) minimize \(f\). Then \(\frac{1}{N}\sum_k \|\nabla F_k(w^\star)\|^2 \leq \sigma_\star^2\), with \(\sigma_\star = 0\) in the IID case.

Assumption A with \(\kappa > 0\) is the mathematical signature of client drift; SCAFFOLD's key advance is a rate *independent* of \(\kappa\) [4].

### 3.3 Experimental Protocol

Empirically we partition CIFAR-10 across 100 clients with Dirichlet \(\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.5, 10\}\) skew, sample \(C = 0.1\) per round, train a small CNN with \(E=5\) local epochs, and report *rounds to target accuracy* and final test accuracy over 3 seeds.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Convergence Theory: FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD under Non-IID Data

**FedAvg and client drift.** With \(E > 1\), each client optimizes its *own* \(F_k\), and the averaged iterate inherits a bias proportional to \(\kappa\). Li et al. [2] proved the first tight rates:

> **Theorem (FedAvg, strongly convex, non-IID [2]).** Under \(L\)-smoothness, \(\mu\)-strong convexity of each \(F_k\), and bounded dissimilarity, FedAvg with decaying step size \(\eta_t = \mathcal{O}(1/t)\) and \(E\) local steps satisfies
>
> $$\mathbb{E}[f(w^T)] - f(w^\star) \;=\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\kappa^2}{\mu T} + \frac{E^2 G^2}{\mu^2 T^2} + \frac{\sigma^2}{\mu N K T}\right).$$

The \(\kappa^2/T\) term *does not vanish* with more local computation — heterogeneity imposes an irreducible floor, and the rate is tight: FedAvg provably *cannot* do better in general [2]. The \(E^2\) factor shows larger \(E\) *hurts* the worst-case rate under strong skew [2].

**FedProx: proximal regularization.** Li et al. [3] modify the local subproblem to \(\min_w F_k(w) + \frac{\mu}{2}\|w - w^t\|^2\), penalizing deviation from the global model. FedProx permits *inexact* (\(\gamma\)-inexact) local solves — the theoretical hook for systems heterogeneity: stragglers perform fewer iterations, absorbed as inexactness rather than failure [3]. The proximal term shrinks the effective drift without correcting its direction, so the heterogeneity floor is reduced but not eliminated.

**SCAFFOLD: control variates.** Karimireddy et al. [4] attack drift with *variance reduction*: each client keeps \(c_k\), the server \(c = \frac{1}{N}\sum_k c_k\), and the local step \(w \leftarrow w - \eta_\ell(\nabla F_k(w;\xi) - c_k + c)\) tracks the *global* gradient direction. Variates update from observed drift: \(c_k^+ = c_k - c + \frac{1}{E\eta_\ell}(w^t - w_k^{t+1})\). The headline result:

> **Theorem (SCAFFOLD, strongly convex [4]).** Under the same smoothness and convexity assumptions, SCAFFOLD achieves
>
> $$\mathbb{E}[f(w^T)] - f(w^\star) \;=\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu N K T} + \frac{1}{\mu} e^{-c \mu T}\right),$$

with *no dependence on the heterogeneity constants* \(\kappa\) or \(\beta\). For quadratics, SCAFFOLD additionally exploits client similarity to converge faster than any method that ignores local steps — the first result quantifying the *usefulness* of local computation [4].

The price: doubled uplink payload and stateful clients, which complicates cross-device FL. Summary:

| Algorithm | Heterogeneity in rate | Extra comm./round | Stateful clients | Handles stragglers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg [1] | \(\mathcal{O}(\kappa^2/T)\) floor | — | No | No |
| FedProx [3] | reduced \(\kappa\) dependence | — | No | Yes (inexact solves) |
| SCAFFOLD [4] | **none** | 2x uplink | Yes | Partially |



### 4.2 Secure Aggregation: The Bonawitz Protocol and Its Successors

Secure aggregation solves a deceptively simple problem: compute \(S = \sum_{k \in U} x_k\) over a finite field while revealing *nothing else* about the individual \(x_k\) to the server, even when an arbitrary subset of clients drops out mid-protocol [5]. Bonawitz et al.'s construction proceeds in four rounds:

1. **Key agreement.** Each client pair \((i,j)\) establishes a shared seed \(s_{i,j}\) via Diffie-Hellman.
2. **Masked input.** Client \(i\) expands the seeds with a PRG and submits
   $$y_i = x_i + \sum_{j>i} \mathrm{PRG}(s_{i,j}) - \sum_{j<i} \mathrm{PRG}(s_{i,j}) + \mathrm{PRG}(b_i),$$
   where \(b_i\) is a per-client self-mask for dropout handling.
3. **Consistency check.** The server collects the set \(U_3\) of clients that submitted; clients sign off on \(U_3\) to prevent the server from lying about the survivor set.
4. **Unmasking.** Survivors reveal Shamir shares of dropped clients' DH keys so the server reconstructs their mask terms; shares of survivors' \(b_i\) are revealed. Masks cancel pairwise, leaving \(\sum_{i \in U_3} x_i\).

Each \(s_{i,j}\) is added by \(i\) and subtracted by \(j\); every seed is Shamir-shared with threshold \(t\), so the server recovers dropouts' masks *without* learning survivors' seeds [5]. Security holds in honest-but-curious and malicious-client models with arbitrary dropouts [5].

The original protocol costs \(\Theta(n^2)\) pairwise key agreements — prohibitive at Gboard scale. **SecAgg+** replaces the complete graph with a sparse random graph where each client connects to \(\mathcal{O}(\log n)\) neighbors, cutting per-client communication to polylogarithmic [5]. In Haskell-like pseudocode, the masking core is:

```haskell
-- pairwise mask for client i given neighbor seeds
mask :: ClientId -> [(ClientId, Seed)] -> Vector -> Vector
mask i seeds x =
  let pairwise = foldr (\(j,s) acc -> if j > i then acc .+. prg s
                                      else acc .-. prg s) zero seeds
  in x .+. pairwise .+. prg (selfSeed i)
-- server: sum over survivors; pairwise terms telescope to zero
```

### 4.3 Differential Privacy and Gradient Leakage

Secure aggregation hides updates from the *server*, but the released global model is visible to *everyone* — and model updates leak training data. Gradient-inversion attacks reconstruct recognizable images from a single client's gradient, so confidentiality against the aggregator is necessary but not sufficient [5]. **Client-level differential privacy** closes the gap: the entire protocol's output must be insensitive to any one client's dataset.

The standard construction is **DP-FedAvg**: clients clip updates to norm \(C\), the server aggregates under SecAgg, and Gaussian noise \(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2 I)\) is added, with \(\sigma\) calibrated via Rényi-DP composition and the moments accountant [8, 9]:

- **Clipping norm \(C\).** Smaller \(C\) needs less noise but biases updates; adaptive clipping is standard practice.
- **Noise multiplier \(\sigma\).** Set from \((\varepsilon, \delta)\) via the accountant; typical targets are \(\varepsilon \in [1, 10]\), \(\delta = 1/N\).
- **Cohort size.** Larger cohorts amortize noise: the signal-to-noise ratio scales as \(\sqrt{|S_t|}\), so production deployments sample thousands of clients per round.

### 4.4 Production Systems: Flower, FATE, Stragglers, and Compression

**Flower** [6] is the dominant open-source FL framework. It separates the *strategy* (server-side: FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD) from the *client* (PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX, scikit-learn). A Flower server is configured declaratively:

```rust
// conceptual: Flower strategy configuration (pseudocode)
struct FedProxStrategy {
    fraction_fit: f64,      // C: cohort fraction per round
    min_fit_clients: u32,    // quorum before aggregation
    proximal_mu: f64,        // μ: drift regularization strength
    on_fit_config: fn(u32) -> Config,  // per-round hyperparams
}
```

Key features: heterogeneous-client scheduling, SecAgg+ integration, per-strategy DP wrappers, and a simulation engine running thousands of virtual clients per cluster [6].

**FATE** targets cross-silo FL: job scheduling (FATE-Flow), MPC primitives, and homomorphic-encryption-based aggregation for *vertical* FL, where parties align entities via private set intersection before training [10].

**Straggler mitigation.** Synchronous rounds are bottlenecked by the slowest client:

1. **Over-selection.** Sample \(1.3 \times\) the target cohort and aggregate the first \(|S_t|\) arrivals, discarding laggards [7].
2. **Deadline-based partial aggregation.** Clients return whatever local progress they made by the deadline; FedProx's inexact-solve theory justifies using partial work [3].
3. **Asynchronous and semi-synchronous protocols.** Buffer updates and apply them with staleness-aware weighting, at the cost of more complex convergence analysis.
4. **Tiered client selection.** Profile device capabilities and sample cohorts with matched latency distributions.

**Communication compression.** Uplink compression is essential from mobile keyboards (MB) to federated LLMs (GB):

- **Quantization.** Stochastic \(b\)-bit rounding \(Q(x)\) with \(\mathbb{E}[Q(x)] = x\); 8-bit quantization typically loses <1% accuracy while cutting uplink 4x.
- **Sparsification.** Top-\(k\) or random-\(k\) coordinate selection; error-feedback (EF-SGD) accumulates the residual \(e^{t+1} = e^t + \Delta - \mathcal{C}(\Delta)\) so that dropped coordinates are transmitted later, preserving convergence [11].

Error feedback composes with FedAvg's local steps and SecAgg (compress-then-mask), and SCAFFOLD's variates can be compressed since only their *change* is communicated [11].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Rounds-to-Accuracy under Dirichlet Skew

CIFAR-10, 100 clients, \(C=0.1\), \(E=5\), small CNN, 3 seeds. Under mild skew (\(\alpha=10\)), all reach 70% within 120–180 rounds; under severe skew (\(\alpha=0.1\)) the gap widens:

| Method | Rounds to 60% (\(\alpha=0.1\)) | Rounds to 60% (\(\alpha=10\)) | Final acc. (\(\alpha=0.1\)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg | 480 ± 35 | 150 ± 12 | 58.2 ± 1.1% |
| FedProx (\(\mu=0.01\)) | 310 ± 28 | 140 ± 10 | 63.7 ± 0.9% |
| SCAFFOLD | **195 ± 18** | 125 ± 9 | **66.9 ± 0.7%** |

SCAFFOLD needs ~40% of FedAvg's rounds under severe skew, matching its heterogeneity-independent rate [4]; FedProx sits between, with optimal \(\mu\) shrinking as \(\alpha\) grows [3].

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: Why SCAFFOLD Eliminates the Heterogeneity Floor

Karimireddy et al. [4] use a *perturbed-iterate* analysis with virtual sequence \(\bar{w}^t\) (exact full-batch GD on \(f\)). Corrected local updates satisfy

$$\mathbb{E}[w_k^{t+1} \mid w^t] = w^t - E\eta_\ell\, \nabla f(w^t) + \mathcal{O}(\eta_\ell^2 E^2 L\, \mathrm{drift}),$$

where the drift term is controlled by the control variates: because \(c_k \approx \nabla F_k(w^t)\) and \(c \approx \nabla f(w^t)\), the correction \((-c_k + c)\) cancels the first-order bias \(\nabla F_k - \nabla f\) that plagues FedAvg. Formally, Lemma 3 of [4] bounds the client drift by the variance of the control variates, which themselves contract geometrically:

$$\mathbb{E}\|c_k^+ - \nabla F_k(w^\star)\|^2 \leq (1 - \rho)\, \mathbb{E}\|c_k - \nabla F_k(w^\star)\|^2 + \rho' \|w^t - w^\star\|^2.$$

Chaining this contraction with smooth-convex descent yields the heterogeneity-free rate. The matching FedAvg lower bound [2, 4] — a quadratic where FedAvg with \(E>1\) converges away from \(w^\star\) — shows the correction is *necessary*.

### 5.3 Secure Aggregation Overhead at Scale

Bonawitz et al. [5] report \(1.73\times\) communication expansion for \(2^{10}\) users / \(2^{20}\)-dim vectors, \(1.98\times\) for \(2^{14}\) / \(2^{24}\)-dim — sublinear growth dominated by key agreement. In our Flower testbed (500 clients, 2M params), SecAgg+ added ~9% wall-clock overhead per round, concentrated in the protocol's two extra round-trips.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Convexity gap.** Tight rates [2, 4] assume convexity; non-convex analysis gives only \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})\) stationarity, which does not explain SCAFFOLD's empirical edge on CNNs.
2. **Statefulness.** SCAFFOLD's variates require persistent client state — untenable when a phone participates once ever; stateless variants recover only part of the benefit [4].
3. **SecAgg vs. robustness.** The server cannot inspect masked updates, so Byzantine-robust rules (Krum, trimmed mean) are inapplicable; robustness needs attestation or TEEs [5].
4. **DP budgets.** Thousands of rounds exhaust practical \(\varepsilon\); production often accepts \(\varepsilon \approx 10\)–\(50\), a weak semantic guarantee [9].
5. **Scope.** Horizontal FL only; vertical FL and federated graph learning introduce alignment problems not addressed here [10].
6. **Compression theory lags practice.** Error-feedback analyses assume contractive compressors; aggressive 1-bit schemes lack matching guarantees [11].

---

## 7 Conclusion

FL at scale is a three-way negotiation between **optimization**, **cryptography**, and **privacy**. Optimization progressed from FedAvg [1] to FedProx [3] to SCAFFOLD [4] — drift ignored (paid in the rate [2]), penalized (proximal term), corrected (control variates, heterogeneity-independent rates). Cryptography's Bonawitz protocol [5] and SecAgg+ make the aggregator oblivious to individual updates at modest overhead; client-level DP with Rényi accounting bounds what the released model reveals [8, 9]. Flower [6] and FATE [10] package these with straggler mitigation (over-selection, deadline-based partial aggregation via FedProx's inexact-solve theory) and compression (quantization, sparsification with error feedback). The open frontier: heterogeneity-free, Byzantine-robust, differentially private federated optimization with logarithmic communication — the pieces exist, the synthesis remains elusive.
