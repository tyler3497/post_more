# Federated Learning at Scale: Convergence of FedAvg under Non-IID Data, Client-Drift Correction via SCAFFOLD and FedProx, and Privacy-Preserving Secure Aggregation Protocols

## Abstract

Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model across millions of decentralized clients without centralizing their data, but three obstacles dominate the optimization landscape: the convergence of federated averaging (FedAvg) under statistically heterogeneous (non-IID) data, the systematic bias of **client drift** induced by many local epochs, and the privacy exposure of cleartext model updates. This thesis unifies these threads. We explain the $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ convergence analysis of FedAvg on non-IID data under proper client sampling and rescaled averaging [2]; formalize client drift as a persistent bias surviving even full-batch gradients and full participation, and show how SCAFFOLD's control variates $(c - c_i)$ implement stochastic client-variance reduction to eliminate it [3]; analyze FedProx's proximal term $\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|w - w^t\|^2$ and its $\gamma$-inexactness framework for variable client work [4]; and present the double-masking secure aggregation protocol that reveals only the *sum* of updates while tolerating arbitrary client dropout via Shamir secret sharing [5]. Through theorem sketches, a comparative complexity table, and the empirical record, we locate where each method dominates and where the privacy–utility–communication trilemma remains unresolved [6].

## 1. Introduction

Modern machine learning draws power from data, yet the most valuable data is increasingly *non-collectable*: it lives on billions of devices and inside institutional silos bound by privacy law. Federated learning, introduced by McMahan et al. [1], leaves data where it was generated, broadcasts a shared model, lets clients compute local updates, and aggregates them centrally. In **federated averaging (FedAvg)**, each selected client runs several epochs of SGD on its own data before returning updated weights; the server averages them into a new global model. This reduces communication rounds by 10–100$\times$ versus synchronized SGD [1] and underpins production systems such as mobile keyboard prediction.

Yet the mechanism that makes FedAvg communication-efficient — multiple *local* steps — is its deepest difficulty: each client optimizes a *different* objective $F_k$, so local trajectories drift toward local minimizers rather than the global one. Zhao et al. [7] quantify the damage: under highly skewed non-IID partitions, CNN accuracy collapses by up to **11% on MNIST, 51% on CIFAR-10, and 55% on keyword-spotting**, traced to *weight divergence* — the distance between centralized and federated weights from the same initialization, bounded by the Earth mover's distance (EMD) between each client's class distribution and the population distribution.

Three research programs attack this from complementary angles, integrated here:

1. **Convergence theory** for FedAvg: under which sampling schemes, averaging weights, and step sizes does FedAvg recover SGD-like $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ rates under non-IID data? [2]
2. **Client-drift correction**: SCAFFOLD's control variates [3] and FedProx's proximal regularization [4], which cancel or constrain drift.
3. **Privacy-preserving aggregation**: cryptographic protocols ensuring the server observes only the *aggregate* of updates [5], composed with differential privacy [6].

We present the convergence machinery in unified notation, compare communication and computation complexities in one table, and analyze the trade-offs that persist after all corrections are applied. We follow the survey [6] in distinguishing *cross-device* FL (millions of unreliable edge devices) from *cross-silo* FL (tens of reliable institutions).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Problem formulation

With $N$ clients, each holding private data $D_k$ and local objective $F_k(w) := \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_k}\, \ell(w; x, y)$, and importance weights $p_k \ge 0$, $\sum_k p_k = 1$ (typically $p_k \propto |D_k|$), the global objective is

$$\min_w\; F(w) := \sum_{k=1}^{N} p_k\, F_k(w).$$

**Non-IID data** means the $D_k$ differ in distribution [2]. Heterogeneity is measured by gradient dissimilarity $\|\nabla F_k(w) - \nabla F(w)\|$ and the EMD between local and global class histograms [7]. The tightest analyses assume each $F_k$ is $L$-smooth and $F$ is $\mu$-strongly convex; nonconvex results yield only stationary-point guarantees [2].

### 2.2 The FedAvg algorithm

Each round $t$ of FedAvg [1]: (1) the server broadcasts $w_t$ to a sampled cohort $S_t$ of $K$ clients; (2) each $k \in S_t$ runs $E$ epochs of SGD on $F_k$ from $w_t$, producing $w_{t,E}^k$; (3) the server averages the returned models into $w_{t+1}$. Li et al. [2] show the averaging scheme is load-bearing: naive averaging of sampled clients is *biased* toward their objectives, whereas the **rescaled average** $\sum_{k \in S_t} p_k \tfrac{N}{K}\, w_t^k$ recovers unbiasedness and admits an $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ rate.

### 2.3 Weight divergence and the statistical challenge

Zhao et al. [7] define *weight divergence* as the distance between centralized-SGD and FedAvg weights from identical initialization after equal total samples, and bound it by a function of the EMD between each device's class distribution and the population's, modulated by learning rate, synchronization frequency, and gradient norms. Two consequences: divergence grows continuously with skew (it is no adversarial artifact), and it is *repairable* without full centralization — a 5% globally shared dataset recovers up to **30% accuracy on CIFAR-10** [7], at the price of reintroducing a centralized collection step. Heterogeneity is thus a *first-order* term in FL analysis, not a nuisance.

### 2.4 The privacy setting

In vanilla FL, updates travel in cleartext, and even an **honest-but-curious** server can inspect and invert individual updates — gradients of deep networks can be inverted to recover training inputs, the canonical motivation for secure aggregation [5]. Privacy mechanisms operate on two layers [6]:

- **Cryptographic**: the server learns $\sum_i x_i$ and nothing about any individual $x_i$ (secure aggregation).
- **Statistical**: even the aggregate leaks nothing about an individual client's data (differential privacy), via clipped per-client contributions plus calibrated noise — the DP-FedAvg lineage refined by McMahan et al. through the moments accountant [6].

## 3. Methodology

We reconstruct the central convergence arguments of the four key papers in unified notation and compare them using the numbers those papers report — the standard "proof as experiment" methodology of optimization surveys. We (1) formalize the FedAvg argument [2] by decomposing the averaged iterate into an SGD-like update plus drift/variance terms; (2) formalize client drift [3] and show how SCAFFOLD's correction makes each local step estimate the *global* gradient direction; (3) analyze the FedProx subproblem $\min_w\, h_k(w; w^t) := F_k(w) + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\|w - w^t\|^2$ with $\gamma$-inexact solutions, letting stragglers contribute partial work; (4) decompose secure aggregation [5] into Diffie–Hellman pairwise masking, self-masking, and Shamir-share dropout recovery; and (5) synthesize the three layers into one comparison of round complexity, per-round cost, and privacy guarantees.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 FedAvg convergence under non-IID data

Li et al. [2] show FedAvg is *not* doomed by heterogeneity: with the right scheme it inherits SGD's $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ rate on strongly convex smooth objectives, with constants exposing exactly where heterogeneity bites. With $\bar w_t = \sum_k p_k w_t^k$ the virtual full-participation average, small learning rates make $E$ local steps approximate one large step along averaged local gradients plus $\mathcal{O}(\eta_t^2 E^2)$ error. Under **Scheme II** — $S_t \sim \mathcal{U}(N,K)$ with rescaled averaging — the expected update equals the full-participation update, so partial participation only inflates variance, which the decaying step size absorbs:

$$\mathbb{E}[F(\bar w_T)] - F(w^\star) = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{T}\right).$$

The constant hides gradient dissimilarity and an $E^2$ factor: longer local epochs amplify drift.

> **Theorem (FedAvg, non-IID, informal [2]):** *Let each $F_k$ be $L$-smooth, $F$ be $\mu$-strongly convex, gradients bounded. With $S_t \sim \mathcal{U}(N,K)$, rescaled averaging, and $\eta_t = \mathcal{O}(1/t)$, FedAvg converges as $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ under full and partial participation; the constant depends polynomially on $E$ and the dissimilarity bound.*

Caveats: strong convexity and bounded gradients fail for deep networks, whose nonconvex analysis yields only $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ stationary-point rates; linear speedup in $E$ is not retainable in the minimax sense — local steps buy communication savings, not faster optimization; and the sampling scheme is essential — the original stale-weight averaging of [1] and with-replacement uniform schemes admit weaker or no guarantees [2]. FedAvg convergence is *fragile but attainable*: uniform-without-replacement sampling, rescaled averaging, decaying steps, modest $E$.

### 4.2 Client drift and SCAFFOLD control-variate correction

Karimireddy et al. [3] open with a sharp negative result: drift is not caused by stochastic noise or partial participation — it persists **even with full-batch gradients and full participation**. With heterogeneous objectives, $E$ local steps on $F_k$ move $w_t^k$ toward $\arg\min F_k$; averaging these models fixes the *wrong* operator. Their quadratic counterexample has FedAvg converge, at arbitrarily small step sizes, to a point bounded away from the optimum: heterogeneity induces *bias*, not just variance.

SCAFFOLD [3] corrects this with control variates from variance-reduction (SAGA, SVRG). Each client keeps $c_i$ estimating its own gradient direction; the server keeps $c$ estimating the average. Local updates become

$$y \leftarrow y - \eta_\ell\, \big(g_i(y) - c_i + c\big),$$

so $(c - c_i)$ — the estimated drift — is subtracted and the corrected step estimates the *global* gradient. Variates are updated either by exact local gradients (**Option I**: $c_i^+ \leftarrow \nabla F_i(w_t)$) or by reusing the trajectory (**Option II**: $c_i^+ \leftarrow c_i - c + \tfrac{1}{E\eta_\ell}(w_t - y_i)$), which needs no extra gradient passes [3].

SCAFFOLD converges in communication rounds at the *centralized* SGD rate, is **unaffected by client sampling**, and — a first — *provably exploits client similarity*: on quadratics with similar $F_k$ it needs strictly fewer rounds than any method unable to benefit from local steps [3]. Heterogeneity is reinterpreted as *client variance*, and SCAFFOLD performs *client-variance reduction* exactly as SVRG performs sample-variance reduction. Cost: one extra $d$-dimensional vector per client and variate communication — roughly double the per-round payload, repaid many times over by fewer rounds in heterogeneous regimes.

### 4.3 FedProx: proximal regularization and $\gamma$-inexactness

FedProx [4] tackles statistical *and* systems heterogeneity with a drop-in modification of FedAvg: each client solves the regularized subproblem

$$\min_w\; h_k(w; w^t) := F_k(w) + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\,\|w - w^t\|^2,$$

anchoring local solutions near the global model and directly bounding drift ($\mu = 0$ recovers FedAvg). The deeper innovation is **$\gamma$-inexactness**: $w^\ast_k$ is $\gamma$-inexact if $\|\nabla h_k(w^\ast_k; w^t)\| \le \gamma \|\nabla h_k(w^t; w^t)\|$, $\gamma \in [0,1)$. This formalizes "the client did as much work as it could" — fast devices drive $\gamma \to 0$, stragglers stop early, and *both contribute* instead of stragglers being dropped. Li et al. [4] prove convergence under statistical and systems heterogeneity jointly, degrading gracefully in $\gamma$.

Empirically, FedProx achieves **22% higher absolute test accuracy on average than FedAvg in highly heterogeneous settings**, with far more stable curves [4]; the proximal term additionally strengthens convexity of the inner problems, accelerating the local solves. The price is $\mu$ itself: too small leaves drift unchecked, too large freezes local progress, and theory gives only heuristic guidance.

| Method | Heterogeneity handled | Rate (strongly convex) | Extra state / cost | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg [1][2] | Mild (correct sampling) | $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ | None | Rescaled averaging, decaying $\eta$ |
| SCAFFOLD [3] | Arbitrary statistical | SGD-like rounds | $d$-dim variates/client | Control variates $(c - c_i)$ |
| FedProx [4] | Statistical + systems | $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$, $\gamma$-dependent | Proximal term $\mu$ | $\tfrac{\mu}{2}\\|w - w^t\\|^2$ penalty |

### 4.4 Secure aggregation: double masking, dropout resilience, and cost

Secure aggregation (Bonawitz et al. [5]) closes the privacy gap with elementary cryptography. Each client $u$ quantizes its update $x_u$ to integers modulo a large prime $p$ (typically $p \ge 2^{32}$, making quantization error negligible [5]) and masks it twice:

1. **Pairwise masks**: $u$ and $v$ agree on seed $s_{u,v}$ via Diffie–Hellman; $u$ adds $\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v})$, $v$ subtracts it. Summed over clients, every pairwise mask appears with both signs and **cancels exactly**.
2. **Self mask**: $u$ adds $b_u = \mathrm{PRG}(s_u)$, known only to itself, defeating a server colluding with all-but-one client.

The server sums $\tilde x_u = x_u + b_u + \sum_v \pm \mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v})$, leaving $\sum_u x_u + \sum_u b_u$. Seeds are distributed via Shamir $t$-out-of-$n$ secret sharing: if a client drops out, survivors reveal shares of its *pairwise* seeds (to subtract unmatched masks); if it survives, they reveal its *self* seed instead — never both. Security holds in honest-but-curious and malicious models **even if an arbitrarily chosen subset of users drops out at any time** [5].

> **Theorem (Secure aggregation, informal [5]):** *For $n$ clients, dimension $d$, 16-bit inputs, the protocol reveals only $\sum_u x_u$ (plus negligible quantization error), tolerates arbitrary dropouts under a $t$-out-of-$n$ threshold, and expands communication by $1.73\times$ at $n = 2^{10}$, $d = 2^{20}$ and $1.98\times$ at $n = 2^{14}$, $d = 2^{24}$ over cleartext.*

The $\sim$2$\times$ overhead and $\mathcal{O}(n)$ per-client seed agreement are the headline costs. Note the optimization interaction: secure aggregation *requires* plain-sum aggregation, ruling out per-client server-side adaptivity — though treating the *summed* update as a pseudo-gradient recovers adaptivity at the server [6].

### 4.5 Differential privacy over the aggregation channel

Cryptography hides updates from the server, but the *aggregate itself* can leak through model-inversion and membership-inference attacks. The complementary layer is **client-level differential privacy**: clip each update to $\ell_2$ norm $S$ (bounding sensitivity) and add Gaussian noise scaled to $S$, tracking the budget with the moments accountant [6]. Composition with secure aggregation is nearly ideal — noise is added *distributively* before masking, so the server never sees the noiseless sum — but utility demands large cohorts (hundreds per round), since noise variance shrinks only with cohort size [6].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proof Sketches

We consolidate the literature's quantitative claims; all numbers are reported by the cited papers on their own benchmarks.

### 5.1 Proof sketch of the FedAvg rate

Write one round as $\bar w_{t+1} = \bar w_t - \eta_t E\, g_t + e_t$, with $g_t$ the mean local gradient and $e_t$ the drift error of sequential versus simultaneous steps. Smoothness gives

$$\mathbb{E}[F(\bar w_{t+1})] \le \mathbb{E}[F(\bar w_t)] - \eta_t E\, \mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(\bar w_t)\|^2 + \tfrac{L\eta_t^2E^2}{2}\mathbb{E}\|g_t\|^2 + \text{(drift)}.$$

Drift is $\mathcal{O}(\eta_t^3 E^3)$ under bounded gradients and dissimilarity; with $\eta_t = \beta/(t+\kappa)$ it is summable, and SGD telescoping yields $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$. Partial participation adds variance $\propto (N-K)/(K(N-1))$, likewise absorbed. SCAFFOLD's proof [3] reuses this skeleton with drift *removed* by control variates — hence heterogeneity-independent constants; FedProx's [4] adds a $\gamma$-dependent inexactness term.

### 5.2 Reported magnitudes

- **Communication**: FedAvg reaches target accuracy in 10–100$\times$ fewer rounds than synchronized SGD [1].
- **Heterogeneity damage**: pathological non-IID splits cost up to 11% (MNIST), 51% (CIFAR-10), 55% (KWS) accuracy [7]; 5% shared data recovers ~30 points on CIFAR-10 [7].
- **Drift correction**: SCAFFOLD needs substantially fewer rounds than FedAvg, with the gap growing in skew; unique in provably *benefiting* from client similarity [3].
- **Proximal stabilization**: FedProx gains 22% absolute accuracy on average in highly heterogeneous settings, with lower run-to-run variance [4].
- **Privacy cost**: secure aggregation costs 1.73–1.98$\times$ communication at cohorts $2^{10}$–$2^{14}$ [5]; $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP needs cohorts of hundreds to keep accuracy loss to a few points [6].

```python
# FedAvg round with rescaled averaging (Scheme II, [2]);
# SCAFFOLD corrected local step ([3]); FedProx subproblem gradient ([4])
import numpy as np

def fedavg_round(w, clients, K, eta, E):
    S = np.random.choice(len(clients), K, replace=False)       # S_t ~ U(N, K)
    acc, norm = 0, 0
    for k in S:
        wk = w.copy()
        for _ in range(E):
            wk -= eta * clients[k].grad(wk)                    # local SGD
        a = clients[k].p * len(clients) / K                    # rescaled p_k * N/K
        acc, norm = acc + a * wk, norm + a
    return acc / norm

def scaffold_local_step(y, g_i, c_i, c, eta_l):
    return y - eta_l * (g_i(y) - c_i + c)                      # drift-corrected [3]

def fedprox_subproblem_grad(w, w_t, grad_Fk, mu):
    return grad_Fk(w) + mu * (w - w_t)                         # proximal anchor [4]
```

The taxonomy is now concrete: FedAvg fixes the *averaging*, SCAFFOLD fixes the *gradient estimate*, FedProx fixes the *local objective* — while secure aggregation wraps the whole round in a cryptographic envelope, orthogonal to all three.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Convexity gap.** The tight rates assume strong convexity and bounded gradients, which deep networks violate; nonconvex extensions give only stationary-point guarantees, and no nonconvex lower bound separates the methods.
2. **Hyperparameter fragility.** FedProx's $\mu$ interacts with $E$ (the proximal term's influence scales with $E$ [4]); mis-tuning reproduces the drift it was meant to cure. SCAFFOLD's variates need warmstarting to avoid early instability [3].
3. **Correction overhead.** SCAFFOLD doubles per-client state and payload; secure aggregation doubles communication with $\mathcal{O}(n)$ per-client seed agreement — at FL scale, constants are the budget [5].
4. **The trilemma.** DP noise demands large cohorts, secure aggregation demands extra communication, drift correction demands extra state. No analysis characterizes the *joint* rate of a DP + SecAgg + drift-corrected system [6].
5. **Threat-model boundaries.** Secure aggregation does not stop malicious-client poisoning, and Byzantine-robust (nonlinear) aggregators are incompatible with its sum-only view — reconciling robustness with privacy is open [6].
6. **Personalization.** All three methods optimize one global model, which may serve no client well under extreme heterogeneity; personalized FL is an active frontier [6].

## 7. Conclusion

Federated learning at scale is governed by one structural tension: the local steps that make it communication-efficient let client objectives diverge, and the aggregation that makes it collaborative exposes clients to the server. The tension is *manageable* but not *eliminable*.

On optimization, FedAvg converges at $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ under non-IID data exactly when sampling and averaging preserve unbiasedness [2]; SCAFFOLD's control variates provably remove drift by reducing client variance [3]; FedProx's proximal term and $\gamma$-inexactness tame drift amid stragglers and variable compute [4]. The record supports the theory: 10–100$\times$ communication savings [1], 22% accuracy recovery in heterogeneous regimes [4], drift bounds tied to measurable distributional distances [7].

On privacy, double-masking secure aggregation [5] reduces the server's view to the bare sum at $\sim$2$\times$ communication cost with full dropout robustness, composing cleanly with client-level differential privacy [6].

The frontier is composition: a system correcting drift, tolerating stragglers, aggregating securely, and guaranteeing DP exists in pieces, but its joint rate, communication lower bounds, and malicious-participant robustness are unknown. Every large-scale FL deployment is therefore an engineered compromise over the privacy–utility–communication trilemma — and the mathematics surveyed here maps exactly what is traded.
