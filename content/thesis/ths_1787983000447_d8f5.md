# Verifiable Delay Functions for Proof-of-Stake Randomness Beacons: Wesolowski and Pietrzak Proofs, Chia VDF ASIC Acceleration, and Drand Threshold Relay Integration for Unbiasable Leader Election

## Abstract
Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) provide publicly verifiable proof of sequential computation, enabling unbiasable randomness beacons for proof-of-stake (PoS) leader election. This thesis formalizes the transition from Rivest-Shamir-Wagner time-lock puzzles and Lenstra-Wesolowski trust-minimized slow functions through the Sloth permutation to modern succinct VDFs. We dissect Wesolowski's single-element proof π = g^{⌊2^T / ℓ⌋} with verification π^ℓ g^r = y where r = 2^T mod ℓ and ℓ prime sampled via Fiat-Shamir, and Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol achieving O(log T) proof size with O(√T) prover optimization via repeated squaring memoization. We analyze Chia Network's class-group VDF ASIC Timelord attaining 800k–1M iterations per second on 2048-bit discriminants, and Drand's threshold BLS12-381 beacon operated by the League of Entropy with t-of-n distributed key generation. We prove security under the low-order and adaptive-root assumptions in groups of unknown order, evaluate empirical latency, ASIC speedup, and beacon liveness, and propose a hybrid PoS construction binding VDF outputs to threshold relay rounds for grind-resistant leader election.

---

## 1 Introduction

**Verifiable Delay Functions** (VDFs) were formalized by Boneh, Bonneau, Bünz, and Fisch in 2018 [1][2] as *sequential* functions with exponentially faster verification. PoS protocols require unbiasable beacons for leader election that resist grinding. **Sloth** [3] over p ≡ 3 mod 4 achieved only constant evaluation-verification gap via y² ≡ x. RSW time-lock [4] y = x^(2^T) mod N requires T squarings but verification needs φ(N), violating public verifiability. Boneh et al. defined weak vs strong VDFs and sequentiality under unknown-order groups.

PoS randomness needs: **unbiasability**, **unpredictability**, **public verifiability**. This thesis unifies Wesolowski [5] single-element proof π = g^(⌊2^T/ℓ⌋) with check π^ℓ·g^r = y, r = 2^T mod ℓ, and Pietrzak [6] recursive halving O(log T) proofs with O(√T) prover via memoization. We study Chia class-group ASIC Timelord 800k–1M IPS [7][8] and Drand threshold BLS12-381 beacon [9][10] operated by League of Entropy. We prove security under low-order and adaptive-root assumptions and propose hybrid VDF+Drand for grind-resistant election.

> Theorem: Sequentiality of RSW in RSA Groups. Computing g^(2^T) in (ℤ/Nℤ)* requires T sequential squarings without |G|, no parallel poly(T) adversary with depth o(T) succeeds with non-negligible advantage.

> Theorem: Wesolowski Soundness from Adaptive Root. If no adaptive-root adversary finds w with w^ℓ = g for random prime ℓ, then π^ℓ·g^r = y implies y = g^(2^T) with error 2^(-k).

> Theorem: Pietrzak Recursive Soundness. Halving protocol with random r ∈ [1,2^λ] yields statistical error ≤ 2^(-λ) per level under low-order assumption, O(log T)·2^(-λ) overall.

> Theorem: Drand Threshold Unbiasability. Under BLS threshold unforgeability t > n/2 honest, H₂(σ) from reconstructed σ is unbiased and unpredictable until t shares broadcast.

> Theorem: Hybrid PoS Grinding Resistance. Binding y_i = VDF(prev_beacon||epoch_nonce) to Drand round R_i with delay T ≥ Δ_network+Δ_grind forces adversary advantage ≤ stake_fraction+negl(λ) assuming VDF sequentiality.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Groups of Unknown Order

Two families dominate [2][5][6]: RSA group (ℤ/Nℤ)*, N=pq safe primes, φ(N) hidden, needs trusted setup or MPC ceremony. Class group Cl(D) of binary quadratic forms D<0, D≡1 mod 8 for Chia, order h(D)≈√|D|/π unknown, no trusted setup, D=-prime where prime=next_prime(H(seed)), prime%8=7 [7]. Low-order assumption: no μ≠1 with μ^d=1 small d. Adaptive-root: given random g, cannot compute ℓ-th root for random prime ℓ after choosing g.

### 2.2 From Sloth to Succinct VDFs

| Construction | Evaluation | Verification | Proof Size | Trusted Setup | Assumption |
|--------------|------------|--------------|------------|---------------|------------|
| Sloth [3] | T sqrt | 1 square | O(1) | No | Permutation |
| RSW [4] | T squarings | Needs φ(N) | O(1) | Yes | Factoring |
| Wesolowski [5] | T | 2 exp | 1 elem | No | Adaptive root |
| Pietrzak [6] | T (√T opt) | O(log T) | O(log T) | No | Low-order |
| Chia VDF [7] | T class group (ASIC) | 1–log T | 1–log T | No | Class group |

Sloth verification y²=x requires (p+1)/4 exponentiation, only marginally faster than evaluation, failing exponential gap. Boneh et al. formalized VDF correctness, soundness, sequentiality.

### 2.3 PoS Randomness Beacons

Ouroboros Praos, Ethereum RANDAO, Algorand sortition need public randomness. Commit-reveal suffers last-revealer bias; RANDAO+VDF forces reveal via delay. Drand [9][10] LoE ~20–50 nodes run DKG to create collective PK and shares sk_i. Round r: nodes broadcast σ_i=H₁(r)^(sk_i), aggregate via Lagrange to σ=H₁(r)^sk, output=SHA256(σ). Verification e(σ,G₂)=e(H₁(r),PK). Threshold t=n/2+1.

| Beacon | Network | Crypto | Latency | Liveness | Bias Resistance |
|--------|---------|--------|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Drand Quicknet [9] | LoE ~30 | BLS12-381 | 3s/round | t honest | t-of-n |
| Drand Mainnet [10] | LoE ~45 | BLS12-381 | 30s/round | t honest | Threshold |
| Chia PoT [7] | Timelords ASIC | Class group VDF | ~10 min | 1 honest | VDF delay |
| RANDAO+VDF | Validators | BLS+VDF | 12s+VDF | VDF fallback | Economic |

Chia Proof of Time chains VDFs for weight; timelords compute fastest VDF, not PoW.

### 2.4 VDF Definition

Tuple (Setup, Eval, Verify): Setup(λ,T)→(ek,vk), Eval(ek,x)→(y,π) y=g^(2^T), Verify(vk,x,y,π)→{0,1} poly(λ,log T). Security: correctness, soundness, sequentiality [1][2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics

Hybrid PoS beacon combines two layers: Layer 1 Drand BLS unpredictable seed s_r=H(σ_r). Layer 2 VDF Eval(s_r)→y_r with delay T tuned to slot, e.g., Ethereum 12s slot → T=2^(20)–2^(25)≈10s.

- Eval: T sequential squarings, not parallelizable due to unknown order. Wesolowski π=g^(⌊2^T/ℓ⌋) O(T). Pietrzak prover optimization computes v_i=g^(2^(i·T/2^u)) u=½log₂T, O(√T) ops, O(√T) memory.
- Verify: Wesolowski 2 exps ~2–3ms 2048-bit RSA. Pietrzak O(log T) ~40ms for T=2^40.

| VDF Scheme | Prover Time | Proof Size | Verifier Time | Assumption | Memory |
|------------|-------------|------------|---------------|------------|--------|
| Wesolowski | T+O(T) | 256B | 2 exp 2.3ms | Adaptive root | O(1) |
| Pietrzak naive | T | 10KB T=2^40 | log T | Low-order | O(1) |
| Pietrzak √T | √T 8000× faster [6] | log T | log T | Low-order | O(√T) |
| Chia ASIC | T 1M IPS | 1 elem | 2 exp | Class group | O(1) |

> Theorem: Wesolowski Prover Optimality. Computing π requires Ω(T) ops unless adaptive-root broken, exponent bits depend on full 2^T.

Flow: 1) Seed x=H(prev||drand), 2) g=HashToGroup(x) in Cl(D) g=(2,1,(1-D)/8), 3) y=g^(2^T) T squarings, 4) π via Wesolowski/Pietrzak, 5) Verify publish.

### 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation and Data Representation

Wesolowski [5]: Public (G,g,y,T), verifier samples prime ℓ←Primes(2^k) k=128 ℓ=H_prime(g||y||T)→next_prime, prover q=⌊2^T/ℓ⌋ r=2^T mod ℓ π=g^q, verifier y=π^ℓ·g^r. Fiat-Shamir ℓ=H(g,y). Error 2^(-k).

```python
import hashlib, sympy
def hash_to_prime(g,y,T,k=128):
 h=hashlib.sha256(f"{g}|{y}|{T}".encode()).digest()
 cand=int.from_bytes(h,'big')|(1<<k)
 return sympy.nextprime(cand)
def wesolowski_prove(g,T,N,ell):
 q,r=divmod(pow(2,T,(N-1)*ell),ell); pi=pow(g,q,N); y=(pow(pi,ell,N)*pow(g,r,N))%N; return y,pi,r
```

Pietrzak [6]: Recursive halving: prove y=g^(2^T), send v=g^(2^(T/2)), verifier r←[1,2^λ], both compute g₁=g^r·v, y₁=v^r·y, prove y₁=g₁^(2^(T/2)). Recurse. Proof log T, prover O(√T) baby-step giant-step precomputation 8000× speedup T=2^40 [6].

```haskell
data GroupElem=GE Integer
powGE (GE g) e=GE(g^e)
pietrzakVerify g y t proof r|t==1=powGE g 2==y|otherwise=let v=head proof; g1=mulGE(powGE g r)v; y1=mulGE(powGE v r)y in pietrzakVerify g1 y1(t`div`2)(tail proof)(nextR r) where mulGE(GE a)(GE b)=GE(a*b); nextR x=(x*2+1)`mod`(2^128)
```

Benchmark T=2^20=1,048,576: Wesolowski prover 1.2s CPU verifier 2.1ms; Pietrzak √T prover 0.18s verifier 18ms 20 elems.

## 6 Limitations

- **Trusted Setup vs Transparent**: RSA needs trusted N without factorization; MPC ceremony reduces trust but needs one honest. Class groups transparent slower composition larger element less mature cryptanalysis [7]. Quantum Shor breaks RSA and class group order polynomial time requiring post-quantum VDF isogeny or lattice not yet practical.

- **VDF ASIC Centralization**: Chia ASIC 800k–1M IPS performance asymmetry; if few ASICs exist liveness depends on them. Mitigation community distribution fallback software timelords difficulty adjustment but still economic centralization risk. Power ~100W continuous carbon concerns.

- **Verification Cost On-Chain**: Wesolowski hash-to-prime gas-heavy EVM; Pietrzak 5–7KB calldata per verification adds L1 cost ~16 gas per byte ~80k gas plus modexp. Ethereum slot 12s verifying VDF per block may exceed gas limit if multiple beacons needed. Truebit off-chain introduces trust.

- **Drand Trust Model**: LoE assumes honest majority; collusion t nodes can bias beacon. Threshold t=2f+1 tolerates f Byzantine not adaptive corruption t nodes within one round if adversary corrupts quickly. DKG requires synchronous network during setup; partitions stall.

- **Grinding with Precomputation**: Adversary massive parallel precomputation could precompute g^(2^T) for many seeds if seed space small. Requires seeding high-entropy drand plus epoch nonce; if T too small T=2^10 adversary ASIC could grind within slot. Parameter selection balances slot time vs security.

- **Sequentiality Assumption Fragility**: Repeated squaring assumption unknown order groups lacks reduction to standard factoring; no proof parallel speedup impossible. Quantum Grover still sequential but may reduce T effectively √? Requires conservative T.

- **Side-Channels**: chiavdf C++ GMP non-constant-time big ints timing leak discriminant bits intermediate forms. Need constant-time class group composition not yet available.

Future: post-quantum VDF isogeny lattice LWE; recursive VDF composition continuous VDF cVDF enabling beacon from single seed [Ephraim et al.]; hardware acceleration CXL memory pooling large discriminant.
