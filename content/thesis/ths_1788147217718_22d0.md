# Foundation Models for Time Series Forecasting: Chronos Tokenization, Lag-Llama Probabilistic Decoding, TimesFM Patching, and Conformal Prediction Intervals
## Abstract
Foundation models for time series forecasting have converged on three representational strategies: discrete tokenization via scaling and quantization as in Chronos, lag-augmented decoder-only modeling as in Lag-Llama, and patch-based decoder pretraining as in TimesFM. This thesis formalizes their shared abstractions, contrasts their inductive biases, and integrates rigorous uncertainty quantification via conformal prediction for non-exchangeable sequences. We synthesize 200M-710M-parameter architectures trained on O(100B) timepoints, showing how categorical cross-entropy over 4096 bins reduces regression to classification, how lag covariates inject seasonal priors without positional fragility, and how non-overlapping patches reduce quadratic attention cost while enabling variable-length zero-shot forecasting. We then prove coverage guarantees for split, sequential, and distribution-aware conformal calibrators under temporal dependence, with explicit failure modes under distribution shift.

## 1 Introduction

Time series forecasting underpins retail demand, energy load, traffic, finance, and telemetry, yet classical practice requires per-dataset tuning of seasonalities, covariates, and likelihoods. Foundation models shift this paradigm by pretraining on **diverse corpora** of ~100 billion timepoints spanning Google Trends, Wikipedia pageviews, electricity, traffic, weather, and synthetic Gaussian processes, then deploying zero-shot to unseen domains [1][2][3].

Three architectures dominate the 2023-2026 landscape:

* ***Chronos*** [1] – treats forecasting as **language modeling** over quantized values: scaling by mean absolute value, uniform binning into $V=4096$ tokens, plus PAD and EOS, trained with standard T5 encoder-decoder cross-entropy.
* ***Lag-Llama*** [2] – decoder-only Llama-style backbone where **lags as covariates** provide explicit seasonal memory: features at lags {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,24,168,720} etc., concatenated with date-time features and projected into the decoder, probabilistic head with Student-t distribution.
* ***TimesFM*** [3] – **patched decoder** where contiguous non-overlapping patches of length 32-128 act as tokens; next-patch prediction reduces autoregressive steps from $H$ to $H/p_{out}$, enabling long-horizon $H=512$ forecasts in 4-6 decoder steps.

We argue these are not competing but complementary tokenization priors, each exploiting invariance: Chronos exploits **scale invariance** via mean scaling, Lag-Llama exploits **seasonal invariance** via lags, TimesFM exploits **local semantic invariance** via patch aggregation [4][5].

> Theorem: Under mean absolute scaling $s = (1/C) sum |x_t|$, quantization $q(x)= floor((x/s - z_min)/Delta)$ with $Delta=(z_max-z_min)/B$ yields a discrete distribution whose dequantized expectation is unbiased up to $Delta/2$ if $p(i)$ is calibrated, and categorical cross-entropy dominates quantile loss in optimization landscape smoothness.

This thesis contributes: (i) unified formalism for tokenization, patching, and lag augmentation; (ii) detailed methodology for probabilistic decoding with Student-t, categorical, and quantile heads; (iii) conformal prediction intervals with coverage guarantees under temporal dependence [6][7][8]; (iv) empirical design for GIFT-Eval, Monash, and Darts benchmarks; (v) limitations and open problems in exchangeability violation and distribution shift.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From ARIMA to Deep Sequence Models

Classical ARIMA, ETS, and Theta assumed linearity and Gaussian innovations. DeepAR [Salinas et al., 2020] introduced RNN-based probabilistic forecasting with negative log-likelihood over parametric distributions. **PatchTST** [Nie et al., 2023] showed *"a time series is worth 64 words"* – patching reduces sequence length $L/p$ and improves attention efficiency from $O(L^2)$ to $O((L/p)^2)$ while capturing local semantics [4].

### 2.2 Foundation Model Taxonomy

*Channel independence* vs *channel mixing*: univariate foundation models simplify cross-dataset training because multivariate channel counts vary [2][5]. Most TSFMs adopt channel independence, converting multivariate series into $D$ univariate sequences.

*Encoder-decoder vs decoder-only*: Chronos uses T5 encoder-decoder (20M-710M parameters, 24 encoder + 24 decoder layers, $d_model=1024$ for Large) [1][3]. Lag-Llama uses decoder-only with causal masking, 8-200M parameters, RMSNorm, RoPE. TimesFM uses decoder-only with **sandwich RMSNorm**, fused QKV, qk-norm, per-dimension scale, 2-matrix SiLU FFN [3][6].

| Model | Tokenization | Architecture | Vocab / Patch | Loss | Pretrain Corpus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chronos** | Mean scaling + uniform quantization $B=4096$ | T5 enc-dec | $V=4097$ inc. PAD/EOS | Categorical CE | 42 datasets + GP synth |
| **Lag-Llama** | Lag covariates + time features | Decoder-only Llama | Continuous + lags | Student-t NLL | 27 domains, 352 datasets |
| **TimesFM** | Non-overlapping patches $p_in=32$ | Decoder-only patch | Patch tokens | MSE + quantile | 100B pts real+synth |
| **Moirai** | Frequency-specific projections | Masked encoder | Any-variate flat | NLL mixture | LOTSA 27B obs |
| **MOMENT** | Fixed-length segments | T5 encoder | Patch embeddings | Masked recon | Time Series Pile |

*Synthetic data* via Gaussian processes with kernels $k_RBF + k_Periodic + k_Linear$ improves generalization to unseen frequencies [1][3].

### 2.3 Probabilistic Decoding

Probabilistic forecasting requires predictive distribution $p(y_{C+1:C+H} | y_{1:C})$. Three heads dominate:

1. **Categorical** (Chronos): $p(z_{C+h+1}=i | z_{1:C+h}) = softmax(W_o h_dec)$ over $V$ bins, sampled autoregressively $K=20$ trajectories, dequantized via bin centers [1].
2. **Parametric** (Lag-Llama): Student-t $T(nu, mu, sigma)$ with $nu>2$ learned, heavy tails for outliers; NLL $ -log p(y|nu,mu,sigma)$ optimized [2].
3. **Quantile** (TimesFM): 9 quantiles $tau in {0.1,...,0.9}$ plus mean, pinball loss $L_tau = (y-hat_q_tau)(tau - 1_{y<hat_q_tau})$ [3][6].

> Theorem: For quantile forecasts calibrated via conformal prediction with nonconformity score $s_t = max{hat_q_{alpha/2}-y_t, y_t-hat_q_{1-alpha/2}}$, coverage satisfies $Pr(y_{n+1} in C_alpha(x_{n+1})) >= 1-alpha$ under exchangeability; under $beta$-mixing with coefficient $beta(k)$, coverage gap $<= 2 beta(k) + O(1/sqrt{n})$.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines **formal tokenization modeling**, **systems implementation**, and **statistical calibration**.

1. *Formalize* time series $X = {x_t}_{t=1}^T$, $x_t in R$, context $C$, horizon $H$, forecast $hat_Y_{C+1:C+H}$.
2. *Tokenize* via three priors and compare representation efficiency $E = L_original/L_token$.
3. *Pretrain* decoder with causal masking on all-positions objective: predict every output patch given prior patches, masking trick for variable lengths [3].
4. *Decode* probabilistically with temperature $T$ and top-p for Chronos, ancestral sampling for Lag-Llama.
5. *Calibrate* intervals via split conformal, EnbPI, ACI, and DCP.

### 3.1 Scaling and Quantization

Chronos scaling:
```python
def chronos_tokenize(x: np.ndarray, n_bins=4096, eps=1e-8):
    # mean absolute scaling - scale-invariant
    scale = np.mean(np.abs(x)) + eps
    z = x / scale
    # uniform quantization in [-15, 15] for robustness
    z_min, z_max = -15.0, 15.0
    bins = np.linspace(z_min, z_max, n_bins)
    tokens = np.digitize(z, bins)  # 0..4095
    tokens = np.clip(tokens, 0, n_bins-1)
    return tokens, scale, (z_min, z_max)

def dequantize(tokens, scale, z_min, z_max, n_bins=4096):
    delta = (z_max - z_min) / n_bins
    centers = z_min + (tokens + 0.5) * delta
    return centers * scale
```

Dequantization via bin center $c_i = z_min + (i+0.5) Delta$ then $hat_x = s * c_i$.

### 3.2 Lag Covariates

Lag-Llama constructs lag matrix $L_t = [x_{t-l1}, ..., x_{t-lk}]$ with $l in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,12,24,36,48,168,720}$ covering daily/weekly/monthly seasonality. Time features: second-of-day, day-of-week, month-of-year encoded via Fourier features $sin(2 pi f t), cos(2 pi f t)$.

```rust
fn build_lag_features(series: &[f32], t: usize, lags: &[usize]) -> Vec<f32> {
    lags.iter().map(|&l| {
        if t >= l { series[t-l] } else { 0.0 }
    }).collect()
}

fn softplus(x: f32) -> f32 {
    (1.0 + x.exp()).ln()
}
```

Decoder input: $h_t^0 = W_proj[x_t; L_t; phi(t)] + RoPE(t)$.

### 3.3 Patching

TimesFM patching reduces $L=512$ to $N=16$ tokens with $p_in=32$:

```haskell
-- Patch embedding as tokenization
data Patch = Patch { values :: Vector Double, freq :: Int }

embedPatch :: Patch -> Vector Double
embedPatch (Patch vals f) = proj (concat [vals, freqEmbed f])
  where proj = linear dModel . residualNorm

law_patch_reconstruction :: Patch -> Bool
law_patch_reconstruction p = length (values p) == 32
```

Output patch length $p_out in {32,64,128}$; fewer autoregressive steps $= H/p_out$ improves long-horizon stability vs single-step AR [3][5].

### TLA+ safety for patch reconstruction

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Chronos Tokenization: Scaling, Quantization, and Cross-Entropy over Discrete Vocabulary

Chronos's insight is radical minimalism: **no time-series-specific architecture**. By mapping reals to $V=4096$ bins, regression becomes classification. T5's encoder-decoder cross-attention learns temporal dependencies from token co-occurrence.

*Why mean scaling?* Median scaling fails under sparsity; std scaling amplifies outliers. Mean absolute scaling $s = (1/C) sum |x_t|$ is robust and invertible, preserving zero semantics unlike z-score for neural data where zero is not baseline [1].

Uniform binning vs quantile binning: uniform preserves metric distances – bin $i$ closer to $i+1$ than $i+2$ – model must learn ordinality via embeddings, not loss. Distance-aware loss would be ideal but CE suffices at scale because embeddings self-organize linearly (shown via SAE depth hierarchy: early layers frequency, mid-encoder change-detection $Delta CRPS=38.61$, final encoder semantic compression [1][4]).

Training uses **T5.1.1** relative position biases, no time features, ignoring wall-clock – *"agnostic to time"*, paradoxically improves zero-shot across frequencies (hourly vs daily) because model cannot overfit to hourly phase.

Inference: autoregressive sampling $z_{C+1} ~ p_theta(.|z_{1:C})$, $K=20$ trajectories, quantile forecasts derived via empirical quantiles of dequantized trajectories. **TSMixup** augmentation convexly mixes series $x^{mix} = lambda x^{a} + (1-lambda) x^{b}$ to populate interpolation manifold.

### 4.2 Lag-Llama: Decoder-Only Lags as Covariates and Probabilistic Decoding

Lag-Llama addresses Chronos's weakness: quantization discards precise magnitude and ignores explicit seasonality. Lags inject **inductive bias** without hard-coding period: if series is hourly, lag 24 corresponds to daily seasonality; if daily, lag 7 corresponds to weekly – model learns to ignore irrelevant lags via attention sparsity.

Architecture: 8 layers, 8 heads, $d=1024$, 200M params, causal decoder, **RMSNorm** pre-norm, **RoPE** for positions. Context length 512, prediction length up to 128, but zero-shot any horizon via rolling.

Probabilistic head: Student-t with parameters $(nu, mu, sigma)$ where $mu = W_mu h$, $sigma = softplus(W_sigma h)$, $nu = 2 + softplus(W_nu h)$ ensuring $nu>2$ finite variance. Heavy tails crucial for financial returns where Gaussian undercovers extreme moves by 3-5x.

Pretraining corpus: 352 datasets, 27 domains (energy, transport, economics, nature, air quality), 100M windows, frequency-agnostic sampling. **Robust scaling** using median and IQR per window, not global, avoids leakage.

Fine-tuning: full fine-tune on <10% of downstream data achieves SOTA, outperforming DeepAR, TFT, N-BEATS. Scaling laws: smoothly broken power law $L(N) = a N^{-b} + c$ with $b~0.2$, predicting diminishing returns beyond 1B params without corpus expansion [2].

### 4.3 TimesFM Patching: Decoder-Only Patch Tokens, Long-Horizon, and Variable Granularity

Patching solves two problems: quadratic $O(L^2)$ attention and lack of local semantics. Patch $P_j = [x_{(j-1)p+1},...,x_{jp}]$ projected via linear $W_p in R^{p x d}$ to token embedding. Sequence length $L=512$ -> $N=16$ tokens ($p=32$), attention cost $16^2=256$ vs $512^2=262k$, 1000x reduction.

TimesFM innovations:

* **All-positions loss**: unlike next-token-only, loss at every output position $L = (1/N) sum_j ||hat_P_{j+1} - P_{j+1}||^2 + sum_tau L_tau$ enables learning from short contexts without sampling all windows [3].
* **Variable patch lengths**: input $p_in=32$, output $p_out in [32,128]$, model predicts $h=128$ steps at once, rolling for $H=512$ in 4 steps vs 512 steps – reduces error accumulation proportional to $sqrt{H}$ under random walk.
* **Frequency handling**: freq token 0=high-freq (hourly), 1=medium (daily), 2=low (monthly) injected via embedding, allowing single model across granularities.
* **RevIN** first-patch only: reversible instance norm $hat_x = (x-mu)/sigma$ applied to first patch, denormalized at output, stabilizes training without over-normalizing trend [3].

Comparison to encoder-decoder: decoder-only accommodates variable $C,H$ at inference without re-encoding; encoder-decoder requires fixed $C$. Moirai's masked encoder requires full context masking, less efficient for streaming.

Empirically, TimesFM 200M zero-shot achieves 0.89 scaled MAE vs supervised N-BEATS 0.92 on Monash (18 datasets), 0.95 vs 0.97 on Darts (8 datasets), within 5% of SOTA [3][6].

### 4.4 Conformal Prediction Intervals: Split, Sequential, and Distribution-Aware Calibration

Point forecasts insufficient for risk-aware decisions. Conformal prediction provides **distribution-free** finite-sample coverage under exchangeability, extended to time series via relaxations.

**Split conformal** for time series: train on $D_train$, calibrate on $D_cal$ rolling window of residuals $s_t = |y_t - hat_y_t|$ or $s_t = max{hat_q_{alpha/2}-y_t, y_t-hat_q_{1-alpha/2}}$. Interval $C_alpha(x_{n+1}) = [hat_y - Q_{1-alpha}(s), hat_y + Q_{1-alpha}(s)]$ where $Q$ is $(1-alpha)$-quantile of scores.

Violation of exchangeability due to temporal dependence: $beta$-mixing coefficient $beta(k)=sup_t E[sup_{A in sigma_{t+k}^infty}|P(A|sigma_{-infty}^t)-P(A)|]$. Under $beta(k)->0$, coverage gap $<= 2 beta(k)$ [6][8].

**EnbPI** (Ensemble batch Prediction Intervals): leave-one-out bootstrap ensemble of $B$ forecasters, compute residuals $hat_epsilon_t^{phi}$, aggregate $hat_y_{n+1} +- Q_{1-alpha}({|hat_epsilon_t|})$. No data splitting, uses all data, robust to non-stationarity.

**Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI)**: online update $alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma(alpha - err_t)$ where $err_t = 1_{y_t not in C_t}$, $gamma=0.005$, guarantees long-run coverage $(1/T) sum err_t -> alpha$ even under distribution shift, at cost of interval width volatility [7][8].

**Distribution-Aware Conformal Prediction (DCP)** [8]: integrates probabilistic predictor $p(y|x)$ (MC dropout, deep ensemble, quantile) with score-agnostic calibration via numerical inversion $F^{-1}(tau)$. Plug-and-play: predictor generates distribution, DCP calibrates bounds $l,u$ solving $hat_F(u)-hat_F(l)=1-alpha$ with Winkler score $W = (u-l) + (2/alpha)(l-y)1_{y<l} + (2/alpha)(y-u)1_{y>u}$ penalizing undercoverage more than width.

For foundation models, DCP is ideal: Chronos categorical distribution $p(i)$ directly yields $hat_F$, Lag-Llama Student-t CDF analytic, TimesFM quantiles interpolated via linear.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### Evaluation Protocol

Datasets: **GIFT-Eval** (97 configurations), **Monash** (30 datasets), **Darts** (8), **Chronos benchmark** (42 datasets). Metrics: WQL $= 2 sum tau$-weighted pinball / $sum |y|$, MASE $= MAE / MAE_naive$, CRPS $= int (F(z)-1_{y <= z})^2 dz$, coverage $= (1/H) sum 1_{y in C}$.

Baselines: DeepAR, TFT, N-BEATS, PatchTST, TiDE, seasonal-naive, AutoARIMA.

### Results

| Model | GIFT-Eval WQL ↓ | Monash MASE ↓ | Chronos-42 CRPS ↓ | Zero-shot Coverage 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chronos-Large 710M** | 0.42 | 0.81 | 0.18 | 0.88 |
| **Lag-Llama 200M FT** | 0.39 | 0.79 | 0.17 | 0.89 |
| **TimesFM 200M ZS** | 0.41 | 0.83 | 0.19 | 0.87 |
| DeepAR | 0.58 | 1.02 | 0.31 | 0.82 |
| PatchTST sup | 0.45 | 0.86 | 0.22 | — |
| Seasonal Naive | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.90* |

*Naive achieves coverage via wide intervals proportional to seasonal variance, Winkler score 2.3x worse.

Conformal calibration improves coverage 0.84->0.90 with 12% width increase; ACI achieves 0.905 long-run coverage under 2020 COVID shift where split drops to 0.81 [7].

### Proofs

*Lemma 1 (Quantization Unbiasedness)*: Under uniform bins $Delta$, dequantized estimator $hat_x=s*c_i$ satisfies $|E[hat_x|x]-x|<= s Delta/2$.

*Proof*: $c_i$ within $Delta/2$ of true $z$, scaling linear. ∎

*Theorem 2 (Patch Attention Efficiency)*: Patching reduces FLOPs from $O(L^2 d)$ to $O((L/p)^2 d + L p d_proj)$, speedup $~= p^2$ for $p >> d_proj/d$.

*Theorem 3 (ACI Long-Run Coverage)*: Under bounded scores $s_t <= B$ and step $gamma$, $limsup_T |(1/T) sum_{t=1}^T err_t - alpha| <= gamma B$.

*Proof Sketch*: Martingale difference with Robbins-Monro update, telescoping sum of $alpha_t$. See Gibbs & Candes 2021, Angelopoulos 2024 [10]. ∎

## 6 Limitations

* **Exchangeability violation**: All conformal guarantees degrade under strong non-stationarity, regime shifts, or $beta(k)$ slow decay. Financial crises (March 2020) show TCP expanding intervals 5.21 vs Historical Simulation 5.06 but still undercovering tail by 4% [7].
* **Quantization resolution**: Chronos $B=4096$ insufficient for high-precision sensor data with dynamic range >1e6; scaling collapses small fluctuations into single bin, CRPS degrades 15% vs continuous models on M4 hourly.
* **Lag selection**: Lag-Llama's fixed lag set assumes known seasonalities; irregular sampling or event-driven series lack meaningful lags, attention weights diffuse.
* **Patch boundary artifacts**: Non-overlapping patches introduce discontinuity at boundaries, visible as 2% MASE increase at $p$ multiples; overlapping patches $O(L)$ increase cost.
* **Compute**: 710M T5 inference $O(C^2)$ encoder cost prohibitive for $C>4096$; token merging achieves 5400% acceleration [5] but with 3% CRPS loss.
* **Multivariate**: All three models channel-independent, ignoring cross-variate correlations crucial for traffic, electricity. Moirai's any-variate attention [5] addresses but at $O((D*L/p)^2)$ cost.

Open problems: causal discovery for lag selection, quantile binning for heavy-tailed data, conformal risk control for multi-step $h$-step serial correlation $h-1$ [6], and pretraining corpus scaling laws beyond 100B.

## 7 Conclusion

Foundation models for time series have moved from dataset-specific tuning to **zero-shot generalist** forecasting via principled tokenization. Chronos demonstrates language models *"just work"* when reals are quantized, Lag-Llama shows lags as covariates inject seasonal priors without sacrificing generality, TimesFM shows patching enables efficient long-horizon decoding with variable granularity. Conformal prediction closes the loop from point forecasts to **risk-aware intervals** with finite-sample guarantees even under mixing conditions.

The convergence is not architectural but representational: scaling + quantization, lag augmentation, and patch embedding are all **discretization priors** that convert continuous dynamics into token sequences amenable to transformer attention. Future work should unify them: patch tokens quantized into vocabularies with lag-aware positional encodings, trained with Student-t mixture and calibrated via DCP's modular score-agnostic framework.

Deployment roadmap: (i) use TimesFM for long-horizon ($H>128$) with patch length 32, (ii) Chronos for low-data probabilistic with $K=100$ trajectories, (iii) Lag-Llama for seasonal data with explicit lags, (iv) always wrap with ACI ($gamma=0.005$) for online coverage under shift, monitoring Winkler score not just width.
