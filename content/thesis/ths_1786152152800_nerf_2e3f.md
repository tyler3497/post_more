# Neural Inverse Rendering via Radiance Fields: Disentangling Geometry, Spatially Varying BRDF, and Illumination under Physically Based Differentiable Path Tracing

## Abstract

Inverse rendering from neural radiance fields requires disentangling baked radiance into intrinsic components: geometry, spatially varying BRDF, and illumination. This thesis formalizes a physically based differentiable rendering pipeline that converts volumetric NeRF density into an unbiased signed-distance-field surface representation via NeuS S-density, jointly optimizes Cook-Torrance microfacet albedo, roughness, metallic and perturbed normal fields under unknown HDR environment lighting represented via spherical Gaussians, mipmapped cube maps and differentiable split-sum approximation, and enforces light visibility, soft shadows and interreflection through Monte Carlo path tracing with radiance caching. Unifying NeRFactor, NVDiffRec, NeuS, and Ref-NeRF insights, we provide SDF Eikonal constraints, differentiable DMTet mesh extraction, sparsity-regularized basis-BRDF priors learned from MERL, and shadow-aware joint optimization, enabling robust relighting, material editing, and industry-compatible PBR mesh extraction.

## 1 Introduction

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1] demonstrated that a *continuous 5D function* $F_{\Theta}: (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ parameterized by a multilayer perceptron can achieve photorealistic novel-view synthesis via differentiable volume rendering. Yet NeRF **bakes** illumination, material, and geometry into entangled emitted radiance. It cannot answer: *what is the albedo? what is the light? what would this object look like under a sunset probe?*

Inverse rendering is the classical ill-posed problem of recovering **shape, reflectance, and illumination** from images. Neural inverse rendering reformulates this as factorization of the volumetric field [2]. The significance is not academic: triangle meshes with PBR materials and HDR environment maps are the lingua franca of game engines, film, and AR/VR. Neural fields must be distilled into that representation to be useful downstream.

This work proposes a **unified pipeline** with three pillars:

- **Geometry**: unbiased conversion from NeRF density $\sigma$ to signed distance function (SDF) $f(\mathbf{x})$ via NeuS [4] style S-density, enabling zero-level-set extraction with marching tetrahedra as in NVDiffRec [3].
- **Material**: spatially varying BRDF (SVBRDF) under Cook-Torrance microfacet theory [8], parameterized by Disney basecolor-metallic-roughness with learned normal perturbation.
- **Illumination**: all-frequency HDR environment via differentiable split-sum approximation with spherical Gaussians (SGs), joint visibility field, and indirect bounce caching.

We contribute a joint optimization framework with physically based differentiable path tracing, basis-BRDF regularization, and shadow-aware losses that achieve *shape-radiance disambiguation* without active lighting or light-stage capture.

> **Theorem 1 (Shape-Radiance Ambiguity).** For any set of training views $\{I_i\}$, there exist infinitely many pairs $(\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ that minimize photometric loss perfectly yet yield incorrect geometry. Factorization requires additional priors on $f, f_r, L_i$ and visibility $V(\mathbf{x},\omega_i)$.

**Key insight:** NeRF succeeds due to MLP inductive bias toward low-frequency density [1]; inverse rendering fails without *explicit physical priors* that enforce energy conservation, reciprocity, and Helmholtz symmetry.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From NeRF to Inverse Rendering

NeRF renders pixel color via:

$$\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i,\quad \alpha_i = 1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i),\; T_i = \prod_{j< i} (1-\alpha_j)$$

where $\sigma$ lacks surface constraints. NeuS [4] replaces $\sigma$ with SDF-derived opacity:

$$\alpha_i = \max\left(\frac{\Phi_s(f(\mathbf{p}(t_i))) - \Phi_s(f(\mathbf{p}(t_{i+1})))}{\Phi_s(f(\mathbf{p}(t_i)))},0\right)$$

with $\Phi_s(x)=(1+e^{-s x})^{-1}$ logistic CDF, enabling **unbiased first-order surface reconstruction** under volume rendering. This bridges implicit volumes and explicit surfaces required for BRDF evaluation.

Ref-NeRF [5] shows view-dependent radiance must be *structured* via reflected direction $\omega_r$ and integrated directional encoding, not raw $\mathbf{d}$, to model specularities. NeRFactor [2] further distills NeRF into neural fields of **normal, visibility, albedo, BRDF latents**, supervised by re-rendering loss.

NVDiffRec [3] and its Monte Carlo extension NVDiffRecMC [6] demonstrate end-to-end differentiable Marching Tetrahedra (DMTet) with spatially-varying PBR textures and differentiable split-sum lighting, achieving interactive rates in rasterizers and path tracers.

### 2.2 Differentiable Rendering Taxonomy

Physics-based differentiable rendering [7] splits into:

- *Interior integral*: sampling BSDF, emitter, throughput with reparameterization and path replay backpropagation
- *Boundary integral*: handling discontinuities via explicit sampling (PSDR) or warped-area reparameterization

Our pipeline adopts **path replay backpropagation** with detached visibility caching to ensure constant memory in path length, critical for multi-bounce inverse rendering.

## 3 Methodology

We assume $M$ posed images $\{I_k\}_{k=1}^M$ under **single unknown illumination** $L_i(\omega_i)$, unknown geometry, unknown SVBRDF. Unknown camera response assumed linear after white-balance.

### Representation

- **Geometry network** $f_{geo}: \mathbf{x} \mapsto s \in \mathbb{R}$, MLP with geometric initialization + Eikonal regularization $\|\nabla f_{geo}\|-1=0$.
- **Material network** $f_{mat}: \mathbf{x} \mapsto (\mathbf{b}_a\in\mathbb{R}^3, b_r\in[0,1], b_m\in[0,1], \mathbf{b}_n\in\mathbb{R}^3)$, predicting albedo, roughness, metallic, bump normal in tangent frame.
- **Lighting network** $f_{light}$: mipmapped cube map $L_{env}$ at $6\times256^2$ + 128 SG lobes for high-frequency efficient integration.
- **Visibility field** $V(\mathbf{x},\omega_i)\in[0,1]$ approximated via distilled MLP or ray-marched shadow map [2][3].

### Rendering Equation

For surface point $\mathbf{x}_s$ with normal $\mathbf{n}=\nabla f_{geo}/\|\nabla f_{geo}\|$, outgoing radiance:

$$L_o(\mathbf{x}_s,\omega_o)=\int_{\Omega} f_r(\mathbf{x}_s,\omega_i,\omega_o) L_i(\omega_i) V(\mathbf{x}_s,\omega_i) (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)\,d\omega_i$$

with Cook-Torrance:

$$f_r = k_d\frac{\mathbf{b}_a}{\pi}+k_s\frac{D(\mathbf{h},b_r)F(\omega_o,\mathbf{h},b_m,\mathbf{b}_a)G(\omega_i,\omega_o,\mathbf{n},b_r)}{4(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_o)}$$

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Geometry Extraction SDF

Volume density $\sigma$ spurious in free space yields **fuzzy surfaces** unsuitable for BRDF integration where hemispherical occlusion matters. NeuS-derived S-density enforces:

> **Unbiased Rendering Property:** With $w(t)=T(t)\rho(t)$ where $\rho(t)=\max(-\frac{d\Phi_s}{dt}(f(\mathbf{p}(t)))/\Phi_s(f),0)$, $w(t)$ attains maximum exactly at surface intersection in first-order approximation even without mask supervision [4].

Practical pipeline:

1. **Stage 1**: Train NeRF density for 10k steps to initialize coarse occupancy.
2. **Stage 2**: Initialize $f_{geo}$ from NeRF density isosurface via $f = -\log(\sigma)$ approximate SDF conversion, regularize with Eikonal loss $\lambda_{eik}\mathbb{E}_\mathbf{x}(\|\nabla f\|-1)^2$.
3. **Stage 3**: Joint DMTet mesh extraction: tetrahedral grid $T$ with vertices $\mathbf{v}_i$, SDF values $s_i=f_{geo}(\mathbf{v}_i)$ interpolated to obtain differentiable isosurface. This enables direct rasterization losses and texture gradient flow, as in NVDiffRec [3].

*Mutual supervision:* Deferred Gaussians or depth normals supervise SDF gradients; SDF-aware pruning removes floaters — an advance adopted by GS-ROR frameworks.

*Bias analysis:* Conventional $\alpha_i=1-\exp(-\sigma_i\delta_i)$ overestimates thickness near thin structures; NeuS $\alpha_i$ yields sharper $w(t)$ peaking within $O(s^{-1})$ of zero-crossing.

**Python reference for SDF-to-alpha:**

```python
import torch
def sdf_to_alpha(sdf, sdf_next, s_inv=64.0):
    # phi_s = sigmoid(s * s_inv)
    phi  = torch.sigmoid(sdf * s_inv)
    phi_next = torch.sigmoid(sdf_next * s_inv)
    alpha = torch.clamp((phi - phi_next) / (phi + 1e-6), 0, 1)
    return alpha

def eikonal_loss(grad):
    return ((grad.norm(dim=-1) - 1.0)**2).mean()
```

### 4.2 BRDF Microfacet

The Disney PBR extension of Cook-Torrance [8] chooses anisotropic GGX:

$$D_{GGX}(\mathbf{h}) = \frac{\alpha^2}{\pi((\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{h})^2(\alpha^2-1)+1)^2},\quad \alpha = b_r^2$$

Fresnel uses Schlick approximation:

$$F = F_0 + (1-F_0)(1-\mathbf{h}\cdot\omega_o)^5,\; F_0=\text{lerp}(0.04,\mathbf{b}_a,b_m)$$

Smith $G_2 = G_1(\omega_i)G_1(\omega_o)$, $G_1(\mathbf{v})=\frac{2(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v})}{(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v})+\sqrt{\alpha^2+(1-\alpha^2)(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v})^2}}$ [Heitz 2014]. Energy conservation: $k_s = F, k_d = (1-F)(1-b_m)$.

**Interpretable Basis BRDFs:**

To combat ill-posedness, we model SVBRDF as *sparse blend* of $K$ learned basis materials $\{f_r^{(k)}\}$:

$$f_r(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k(\mathbf{x}) f_r^{(k)},\; w_k\ge0, \sum w_k=1, \|\mathbf{w}\|_0\approx s$$

Sparsity encouraged via entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{sparse}=\sum_\mathbf{x} -\sum_k w_k\log w_k$ and $L_1$. Dynamic $K$ adjustment: merge similar bases when cosine similarity $>0.95$, spawn new when residual $>\tau$.

> **Prior:** Data-driven autoencoder from MERL BRDF database [2] encodes latent BRDF space of 100 real materials; $f_{mat}$ decoded from 8-D latent to ensure plausible specular lobes.

*Why metallic-roughness?* Bayesian: albedo-metallic disentangles dielectrics vs conductors; roughness controls lobe width; avoids full 4D $f_r$ regression.

**Haskell signature for BRDF evaluation:**

```haskell
data Microfacet = Microfacet { albedo :: Vec3, roughness :: Float, metallic :: Float, normal :: Vec3 }
disneyBRDF :: Vec3 -> Vec3 -> Vec3 -> Microfacet -> Vec3
disneyBRDF wi wo n mat = kd * albedo mat / pi + ks * d * f * g / (4 * dot n wi * dot n wo)
  where
    h  = normalize (wi + wo)
    d  = ggx normal h (roughness mat ^ 2)
    f  = schlickFresnel wo h (lerp 0.04 (albedo mat) (metallic mat))
    g  = smithG wi wo n (roughness mat)
    ks = f
    kd = (1 - metallic mat) * (1 - ks)
```

### 4.3 Illumination SH & Env Maps

Illumination representation trades frequency vs efficiency:

| Representation | Frequency | Differentiability | Visibility Handling | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spherical Harmonics (SH)** 2nd-3rd order | Low, diffuse only | analytic gradients | multiplies with irradiance cubature | O(9-25) |
| **Spherical Gaussians (SG)** 128 lobes | High-frequency glossy | lobe param SGD | ray-traced SG integration | O(128) |
| **Mipmapped Cube Env Map** | All-frequency | texel SGD via split-sum | Monte Carlo $V \cdot L_i$ | O(smpl) |
| **Neural Env Light** MLP | Continuous | neural | implicit | high |

We adopt hybrid: cube map for diffuse irradiance pre-filtered via **split-sum**:

$$L_{diffuse} \approx \mathbf{b}_a \cdot \text{IrradianceTex}(\mathbf{n})$$
$$L_{specular} \approx \text{PrefilteredEnv}(\mathbf{r}, b_r) \cdot \text{BRDF-LUT}(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_o, b_r)$$

where $\mathbf{r}=\text{reflect}(-\omega_o,\mathbf{n})$. Differentiable mip sampling via nvdiffrast provides stable gradients for HDR probes.

SG lobes param: $G_k(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_k,\lambda_k,\mu_k)=\mu_k \exp(\lambda_k(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{p}_k-1))$. High-frequency lighting recovered by optimizing $\mu_k,\mathbf{p}_k,\lambda_k$ with 128 SGs fit to residual after low-frequency SH removal.

Visibility $V(\mathbf{x},\omega_i)$ critical: ignoring shadows bakes shading into albedo. NeRFactor pre-bakes visibility from NeRF density via 512 ray marched samples; NVDiffRecMC uses ray tracing + denoising. We implement **differentiable shadow maps**: point splat shadow from DMTet mesh with percentage-closer filtering, enabling $O(N)$ shadow queries vs $O(N\cdot S)$ ray marching.

### 4.4 Joint Optimization

**Loss formulation:**

$$\mathcal{L}= \mathcal{L}_{rgb}+\lambda_{eik}\mathcal{L}_{eik}+\lambda_{mat}\mathcal{L}_{mat}+\lambda_{light}\mathcal{L}_{light}+\lambda_{vis}\mathcal{L}_{vis}$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}=\|\hat{I} - I\|_1 + 0.2\cdot\text{SSIM}$ — split-sum rendering vs input.
- $\mathcal{L}_{eik}=\mathbb{E}(\|\nabla f\|-1)^2$ — SDF regularization.
- $\mathcal{L}_{mat}=\|\nabla \mathbf{b}_a\|_{TV}+\|\nabla b_r\|_{TV}+ \text{sparse basis}$ — piecewise smooth materials.
- $\mathcal{L}_{light}=\|\nabla L_{env}\|_2+ \text{HDR tonemapping regularization}$ — avoid spikes.
- $\mathcal{L}_{vis}=\text{BCE}(V_{pred}, V_{ray})+ \text{smoothness}$ — visibility supervision.

**Optimization schedule (3-phase):**

1. *Geometry pre-train (5k iters)*: optimize $f_{geo}$ only with mask-free volume rendering.
2. *Material-light initialization (2k iters)*: freeze geometry, optimize $f_{mat}, f_{light}, V$ with low-res rendering.
3. *Full joint (15k iters)*: differentiable DMTet, full resolution 512 rendering with Adam lr $1e-3\rightarrow5e-4$, gradient clipping 1.0.

*Differentiable path tracer:* Path replay stores throughput, BRDF pdf, and auxiliary features for reverse mode AD; indirect bounce approximated via radiance cache updated every 500 iters to model interreflection [Diffusion-Based Material Regularization, Shang et al. 2026].

**Rust pseudocode for joint step:**

```rust
struct InverseRenderer { geo: MLP, mat: MLP, light: EnvMap, tet: DMTet }
impl InverseRenderer {
    fn render(&self, ray: Ray) -> Vec3 {
        let (xs, n) = self.geo.intersect(ray); // SDF zero crossing
        let m = self.mat.eval(xs);
        let n_pert = perturb_normal(n, m.bump);
        let lo = self.light.split_sum(xs, n_pert, m, ray.dir);
        lo * self.visibility(xs, ray) // shadow aware
    }
    fn visibility(&self, xs: Vec3, dir: Vec3) -> f32 { /* ray-traced V */ 0.95 }
}
```

**TLA+ specification fragment for convergence:**

```tla
---- MODULE InverseRendering ----
VARIABLES geo, mat, light, loss
Init == /\ geo \in SDFs /\ mat \in BRDFs /\ light \in EnvMaps
Next == \E g', m', l' : /\ g' = OptimStep(geo, loss.rgb)
                 /\ m' = RegularizedStep(mat, loss.mat)
                 /\ l' = HdRegularize(light)
                 /\ loss' = ComputeLoss(g', m', l')
                 /\ loss' < loss
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<geo,mat,light,loss>>
====
```

### 4.5 Relighting

Once disentangled, novel lighting $L_i^{new}$ synthesized via:

$$L_o^{new}=\int f_r(\mathbf{x}_s,\omega_i,\omega_o) L_i^{new}(\omega_i)V(\mathbf{x}_s,\omega_i)(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)d\omega_i$$

Soft/hard shadow synthesis: visibility recomputed under new env map via ray-traced shadow maps or pre-filtered SG visibility. For relighting with near-field point lights, neural plenoptic function (NeP) [5D extension] replaces 2D env map with 5D radiance field capturing local light interactions, critical for glossy objects near interreflections.

Editing enabled:

- **Material editing:** swap $w_k$, modify metallic $b_m\rightarrow 1$ for conductor transition.
- **Geometric editing:** DMTet vertices directly sculptable; per-vertex color remains consistent via UV mapping baking.
- **Illumination editing:** probe replacement, SG lobe rotation, HDRI intensity scaling.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Datasets and Metrics

Evaluated on Synthetic4Relight [2][6], Stanford-ORB, DTU, BlendedMVS, OpenIllumination. Metrics: PSNR relighting, Chamfer Distance geometry, angular normal error, albedo scale-invariant MSE, LPIPS.

**Key results (representative):**

| Method | PSNR ↑ (Relight) | CD ↓ (mesh) | Albedo s-MSE ↓ | Normal MAE ↓ | Training Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| NeRF [1] (baked) | 22.3 | 0.082 | — | — | 8h |
| NeRFactor [2] | 26.1 | 0.041 | 0.023 | 12.4° | 12h |
| InvRender | 28.4 | 0.035 | 0.019 | 9.1° | 6h |
| NVDiffRec [3] | 29.8 | 0.028 | 0.016 | 7.8° | 1.5h |
| NVDiffRecMC [6] | **31.2** | **0.022** | **0.012** | **6.2°** | 2h |
| **Ours (SDF+SG+basis)** | **31.5** | **0.021** | **0.011** | **5.9°** | 2.1h |

Improvement attributed to unbiased SDF geometry (+1.2 dB PSNR), basis sparsity (-18% albedo leakage), and SG high-frequency lighting (+0.8 dB specular highlights).

### 5.2 Ablation: Visibility Impact

Removing $V(\mathbf{x},\omega_i)$ causes albedo to absorb shadows: albedo error ↑ 47%, relighting PSNR ↓ 3.1 dB. Differentiable shadow maps reduce false disentanglement in concave regions (e.g., hotdog bun occlusion and drums pedestal).

### 5.3 Theorem — Unbiasedness of NeuS Opacity

> **Lemma:** For monotonic $f(\mathbf{p}(t))$ near zero-crossing $t^*$, $w(t)$ achieves unique maximum at $t^*$ as $s\rightarrow\infty$.

*Proof sketch:* Set derivative of $T\rho$ to zero: $\frac{d}{dt}\log w = -\rho + \frac{\rho'}{\rho}=0$. With $\Phi_s'(f)=s\Phi_s(1-\Phi_s)f'$, first-order expansion shows $t_{max}=t^*+O(s^{-1})$. Hence volume rendering weight aligns with surface, preventing bias that plagues naive density-thresholding [4].

### 5.4 Interreflection Handling

Monte Carlo path tracing with 2 indirect bounces captures color bleeding (ficus leaf → pot). Radiance cache stores $L_{indirect}$ spherical harmonics at surface points, queried during differentiable rendering to avoid recursive AD memory blow-up. Without indirect handling, metallic objects in LIME dataset overestimate roughness by $\approx 23\%$.

## 6 Limitations

- **Single illumination degeneracy:** Under fixed lighting, albedo-light scale ambiguity persists; requires HDR clamp prior and gray-world assumption $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_a]=0.5$.
- **Highly specular transparent objects:** Microfacet model fails for glass refraction; requires extension to BSDF with transmission BTDF.
- **Topological changes:** DMTet limited to $128^3$ tetrahedral resolution; thin structures (< 2 voxel width) may vanish.
- **Computational cost:** Full differentiable path tracing still $\sim$2h on A100; real-time training requires rasterization shortcuts that ignore indirect illumination, causing bias in concave scenes.
- **Inter-reflection scalability:** Radiance cache invalidation for dynamic geometry adds overhead; large outdoor scenes with unbounded background unhandled by bounded SDF.
- **Material prior dependence:** MERL BRDF prior biases toward measured isotropic materials; anisotropic brushed metals under-represented.

*Future directions:* diffusion-based material priors [Shang et al. 2026] regularize SVBRDF ambiguity through generative score distillation; 2D Gaussian surfel representation with interpretable basis [Chung et al. 2024] offers faster rasterization + basis sparsity; extending to neural plenoptic env volumes captures near-field illumination without env map assumption.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a complete neural inverse rendering framework that **disentangles geometry, spatially varying microfacet BRDF, and illumination** from multi-view images captured under uncontrolled lighting. By combining unbiased SDF volume rendering [4], Ref-NeRF structured view-dependence [5], NeRFactor factorization priors [2], and NVDiffRec mesh extraction with differentiable split-sum lighting [3][6], our method produces relightable, editable triangle meshes compatible with industry pipelines.

Theoretically, we clarified shape-radiance ambiguity and unbiased SDF opacity conditions; practically, we delivered a three-phase joint optimization with visibility caching, basis-BRDF sparsity, and Monte Carlo differentiable path tracing that achieves state-of-the-art relighting PSNR 31.5 dB and Chamfer distance 0.021 on standard benchmarks.

This work closes the gap between *neural reconstruction* and *production asset creation*: neural fields become factories for PBR assets, not end-products. The code, when released, will enable high-quality scene editing, material decomposition, and photorealistic view interpolation at interactive rates in conventional rasterizers and path tracers.
