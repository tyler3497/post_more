# Bayesian Phylogenetics: Markov Chain Monte Carlo Tree Inference, the Coalescent Process, and Molecular Clock Models

## Abstract

Bayesian phylogenetics recasts the reconstruction of evolutionary history as posterior inference over tree topologies, divergence times, and substitution-model parameters. This thesis develops the full inferential machinery: the Felsenstein likelihood under general time-reversible substitution models with gamma-distributed rate heterogeneity; Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) exploration of the superexponential tree space via NNI, SPR, and TBR proposal operators with Metropolis–Hastings acceptance; convergence diagnostics including average standard deviation of split frequencies and effective sample sizes; Kingman's coalescent and its multispecies extension for modeling gene-tree discordance under incomplete lineage sorting; and strict versus uncorrelated relaxed molecular-clock models with Bayesian model averaging. We review the *BEAST framework for joint species-tree estimation, fast adaptive clock operators yielding order-of-magnitude efficiency gains, and nonparametric coalescent inference, and we close with identifiability limits and convergence pathologies documented in large-scale empirical studies.

---

## 1 Introduction

The statistical reconstruction of phylogenies underwent a profound transformation in the late twentieth century. Felsenstein's pruning algorithm [see 7] rendered the likelihood of a tree computable in time linear in the number of taxa for any fixed topology, yet the number of unrooted binary topologies on *n* taxa, *(2n − 5)!!*, grows superexponentially, so that exhaustive search is impossible for any nontrivial *n*. Maximum-likelihood heuristics therefore dominate the frequentist landscape, but they report only a point estimate and its bootstrap support.

The Bayesian turn — initiated in the mid-1990s by Rannala and Yang, Mau, Newton, and collaborators, operationalized by Larget and Simon's BAMBE, and industrialized by the release of MrBayes [7] and BEAST — treats the tree itself as a random variable with a posterior distribution. The inferential target is

$$P(\tau, \mathbf{t}, \theta \mid D) = \frac{P(D \mid \tau, \mathbf{t}, \theta)\, P(\tau, \mathbf{t})\, P(\theta)}{P(D)},$$

where $\tau$ is the topology, $\mathbf{t}$ the branch lengths or node ages, $\theta$ the substitution-model and clock parameters, and $D$ the multiple sequence alignment. The marginal likelihood $P(D)$ is intractable, so inference proceeds by constructing a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is exactly this posterior.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the substitution-model and prior background. Section 3 derives the MCMC machinery — the Metropolis–Hastings kernel, heated chains, and topological proposals. Section 4 deep-dives into four pillars: the likelihood recursion, the geometry of tree-space proposals, the coalescent hierarchy, and clock models. Section 5 surveys empirical and theoretical results, Section 6 the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Continuous-Time Substitution Models

Sequence evolution is modeled as a continuous-time Markov chain on the state space $\{A, C, G, T\}$ with rate matrix $Q$. The transition probabilities over a branch of length $t$ are $P(t) = e^{Qt}$. The hierarchy of models trades realism for estimability:

| Model | Free parameters | Key assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Jukes–Cantor (JC69) | 0 | Equal base frequencies, equal rates |
| Kimura 2-parameter (K80) | 1 | Transition/transversion bias |
| Hasegawa–Kishino–Yano (HKY85) | 4 | Unequal base frequencies + ti/tv ratio |
| General time-reversible (GTR) | 8 | Fully general reversible rates [2] |

The GTR model of Tavaré, combined with gamma-distributed rate heterogeneity across sites (+Γ, Yang 1994) and a proportion of invariable sites (+I), remains the workhorse of empirical analyses [2, 7]. Under +Γ, each site's rate is drawn from a discretized gamma distribution with shape parameter $\alpha$; small $\alpha$ implies extreme among-site heterogeneity. Model selection among these candidates is itself a Bayesian problem, addressed by Bayes factors and stepping-stone marginal-likelihood estimation [2].

### 2.2 Priors on Trees and Parameters

A posterior requires priors. The standard tree priors are the *Yule* (pure-birth) process and the *birth–death* process with speciation rate $\lambda$ and extinction rate $\mu$, possibly with incomplete sampling fraction $\rho$. For divergence-time estimation with fossils, the fossilized birth–death process and sampled-ancestor models [5] allow fossils to attach as tips *or* as direct ancestors of other samples — a relaxation of the classical assumption that all samples are tips. Branch-rate priors for relaxed clocks (lognormal, exponential, gamma) complete the hierarchy [2, 3].

### 2.3 What the Posterior Summarizes

The MCMC output is a *sample* from the posterior, not a single tree. Standard summaries include:

- **Posterior clade probabilities**: the frequency with which a bipartition appears in the sample;
- **Maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree**: the sampled tree maximizing the product of clade posteriors;
- **Consensus trees**: majority-rule or strict summaries of the bipartition frequencies;
- **Credible sets**: the smallest set of topologies containing $1 - \alpha$ posterior mass.

> **Theorem:** *Posterior consistency of topology.* Under identifiability of the substitution model and with the number of sites $\to \infty$, the posterior probability of the true unrooted topology converges to 1 almost surely, provided the prior assigns positive mass to a neighborhood of the truth. Finite-sample behavior, however, is governed by branch-length geometry and model adequacy.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Metropolis–Hastings Kernel on Tree Space

The chain proposes a move from state $x = (\tau, \mathbf{t}, \theta)$ to $x'$ via a proposal density $q(x' \mid x)$ and accepts with probability

$$\alpha(x \to x') = \min\left(1, \frac{\pi(x')}{\pi(x)} \cdot \frac{q(x \mid x')}{\pi(x')}{q(x' \mid x)}\right) = \min\left(1, \frac{P(D\mid x')P(x')}{P(D\mid x)P(x)} \cdot \frac{q(x \mid x')}{q(x' \mid x)}\right),$$

where $\pi$ is the unnormalized posterior. The intractable marginal likelihood cancels. Detailed balance, $\pi(x) K(x \to x') = \pi(x') K(x' \to x)$, guarantees that the posterior is the unique stationary distribution of an irreducible, aperiodic chain.

A minimal illustration of the acceptance logic in Python:

```python
import math, random

def mh_accept(log_post_new, log_post_old, log_q_fwd, log_q_rev):
    """Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability (log scale)."""
    log_r = (log_post_new - log_post_old) + (log_q_rev - log_q_fwd)
    return min(1.0, math.exp(log_r))

def mcmc_step(state, propose, log_posterior):
    proposal, log_q_fwd, log_q_rev = propose(state)
    a = mh_accept(log_posterior(proposal), log_posterior(state),
                  log_q_fwd, log_q_rev)
    return proposal if random.random() < a else state
```

### 3.2 Metropolis-Coupled MCMC

Tree space is rugged: the posterior is multimodal in topology, and a single chain can become trapped on a suboptimal peak. MrBayes therefore runs *Metropolis-coupled* MCMC ($MC^3$) [7]: $m$ chains sample from tempered posteriors $\pi_i(x) \propto P(D\mid x)^{\beta_i} P(x)$ with $0 < \beta_m < \dots < \beta_1 = 1$, and states are periodically swapped between chains with the acceptance probability

$$\alpha_{\text{swap}} = \min\left(1, \frac{\pi_i(x_j)\pi_j(x_i)}{\pi_i(x_i)\pi_j(x_j)}\right).$$

Heated chains flatten the likelihood surface and traverse valleys freely; swaps let the cold chain inherit their discoveries. In practice `nchains=4` with the default heating scheme is standard [7].

### 3.3 Convergence Diagnostics

A chain that has not converged yields biased summaries. The community standards are:

1. **Average standard deviation of split frequencies (ASDSF)** across independent runs: $< 0.01$ indicates topological convergence [7];
2. **Effective sample size (ESS)** per parameter: $> 200$ (conservative; some studies accept $> 100$) [2];
3. **Potential scale reduction factor (PSRF)** $\approx 1.0$ across runs;
4. **Trace inspection** in Tracer: stationarity, no trends, good mixing;
5. **Burn-in**: the initial transient — often the first 10–25% of samples — is discarded.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Likelihood Surface and the Felsenstein Pruning Recursion

The likelihood factorizes over alignment sites under the usual independence assumption: $P(D \mid \tau, \mathbf{t}, \theta) = \prod_{s} P(D_s \mid \tau, \mathbf{t}, \theta)$. For a single site, Felsenstein's pruning recursion computes the conditional likelihood $L_v(a)$ — the probability of the data in the subtree below node $v$ given that $v$ carries state $a$ — bottom-up:

$$L_v(a) = \prod_{u \in \text{children}(v)} \sum_{b} P_{ab}(t_{vu})\, L_u(b),$$

with $L_v(a) = 1$ if leaf $v$ is observed in state $a$ (0 otherwise), and the site likelihood $\sum_a \pi_a L_{\text{root}}(a)$. The algorithm is $O(n \cdot s \cdot k^2)$ for $n$ taxa, $s$ sites, and $k$ states, and its repeated evaluation dominates every MCMC iteration — the motivation for BEAGLE GPU acceleration [2, 3].

The likelihood surface over branch lengths is smooth and unimodal for fixed topology, but the *joint* surface over topologies is discrete, vast, and multimodal: adjacent topologies in NNI distance can differ enormously in likelihood, producing the ruggedness that makes proposal design critical.

### 4.2 MCMC Operators on Tree Space: NNI, SPR, and TBR

Topological proposals rearrange the tree while (usually) preserving or perturbing branch lengths. The three classical operators form a nested hierarchy of move sizes:

| Operator | Neighborhood size | Character |
|---|---|---|
| NNI (nearest-neighbor interchange) | $O(n)$ | Swaps adjacent subtrees across one internal branch; smallest move |
| SPR (subtree pruning and regrafting) | $O(n^2)$ | Detaches a subtree and reattaches it elsewhere |
| TBR (tree bisection and reconnection) | $O(n^3)$ | Bisects the tree and reconnects the halves arbitrarily |

NNI moves are cheap and accepted frequently but explore slowly; TBR moves can leap across valleys but are almost always rejected in high dimensions. Practical samplers therefore mix operators: local branch-length sliders and NNI for fine-tuning, SPR for medium-range moves, and narrow/wide *exchange* operators that swap subtrees between adjacent or distant nodes. For relaxed-clock models, specialized variants of the narrow exchange operator traverse tree space up to 40% more efficiently than the generic version [3].

A subtle point: proposals must be *reversible* with computable Hastings ratios. Naive SPR implementations that forget the regraft-position Jacobian silently bias the stationary distribution. Modern implementations (MrBayes, BEAST2, RevBayes) derive proposals as deterministic involutions on an augmented state space, making the Jacobian explicit [7].

> **Theorem:** *Ergodicity of mixed proposals.* A Metropolis–Hastings chain combining an irreducible topological proposal (e.g., NNI, which connects the full tree graph) with positive-density continuous proposals on branch lengths and model parameters is $\pi$-irreducible and aperiodic, hence converges in total variation to the posterior from any starting state.

### 4.3 Kingman's Coalescent and the Multispecies Coalescent

Phylogenetics above the species level treats the tree as the object of inference; *population* phylogenetics treats the tree as a random realization of a demographic process. Kingman's *n*-coalescent models the ancestry of $n$ sampled gene copies backward in time in a panmictic Wright–Fisher population of effective size $N_e$ [4]:

- When $k$ lineages are present, each pair coalesces independently at rate $1/(2N_e)$ per generation (diploid), so the waiting time to the next event is $\mathrm{Exp}\left(\binom{k}{2}/(2N_e)\right)$;
- the coalescing pair is chosen uniformly at random;
- multiple mergers have probability $O(1/N_e)$ and vanish in the diffusion limit.

> **Theorem:** *Kingman (1982).* As $N \to \infty$ with time rescaled in units of $N$ (haploid) generations, the ancestral process of a finite sample converges weakly to a pure-death Markov chain in which only binary mergers occur, with the rates above. Genealogies are therefore *exchangeable* random binary trees with independent exponential waiting times.

The expected time to the most recent common ancestor is $4N_e(1 - 1/n)$ generations, and the total tree length has expectation $4N_e \sum_{k=2}^{n} 1/(k-1)$ — the engine behind Watterson's estimator and Tajima's $D$.

The **multispecies coalescent (MSC)** embeds this process inside a species tree: lineages evolve within species branches and may fail to coalesce before a speciation event, producing *incomplete lineage sorting* (ILS) — gene trees that disagree with the species tree [1, 6]. The probability of the concordant gene tree decays as internal species-tree branches shorten, and in the *anomaly zone* the most probable gene tree differs from the species tree, rendering concatenation and majority-vote estimators statistically inconsistent. The full Bayesian solution, *BEAST, co-estimates the species tree, divergence times, effective population sizes, and all gene trees jointly [1]:

$$P(S, \mathbf{G}, \Theta \mid D) \propto \left[\prod_i P(d_i \mid g_i)\, P(g_i \mid S, \Theta)\right] P(S)\, P(\Theta),$$

where $P(g_i \mid S, \Theta)$ is the MSC density. This is the gold standard for recent radiations where ILS is severe, at the cost of formidable computation [1, 6].

A modern nonparametric direction places flexible priors directly on the coalescent's demographic function, estimating $N_e(t)$ trajectories without parametric assumptions [4] — the Bayesian analogue of skyline-plot methods.

### 4.4 Strict and Relaxed Molecular Clock Models

A *strict* clock constrains all branches to share one substitution rate $\mu$, so branch lengths in substitutions are proportional to time. Rate heterogeneity across lineages — the empirical rule rather than the exception — motivates *relaxed* clocks, in which each branch draws its own rate:

| Clock model | Branch-rate distribution | Correlation |
|---|---|---|
| Strict | degenerate at $\mu$ | — |
| Uncorrelated lognormal (UCLN) | $\mathrm{LogNormal}(\mu, \sigma)$ i.i.d. | none |
| Uncorrelated exponential (UCED) | $\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)$ i.i.d. | none |
| Autocorrelated | rates evolve along branches | parent–child |
| Random local clocks | piecewise-constant shifts | local |

Choosing among these is a model-selection problem. Because marginal likelihoods are sensitive to prior choice, Li and Drummond introduced *model averaging* over clock models within a single MCMC, letting the chain visit clock models in proportion to their posterior support and computing Bayes factors from visit frequencies [2]. Their mammalian analysis (1,056 genes, GTR+Γ, 50M-step chains) found decisive support for relaxed over strict clocks in most genes — while honestly reporting that 5.1% of genes failed to converge even after 200 million steps, a sobering datum on the limits of current operators [2].

Operator design for clocks has since advanced dramatically: Douglas, Zhang, and Bouckaert's adaptive scheme — comparing three rate parameterizations, learning operator weights on the fly, and deploying AVMVN and Bactrian kernels — achieved up to **65×** efficiency gains on continuous clock parameters, with clock-aware narrow-exchange variants improving tree-space traversal by up to 40% [3]. The lesson is general: in Bayesian phylogenetics, *the proposal is the algorithm*.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Simulation Benchmarks

Simulation studies with known true trees are the primary validation tool. Under correctly specified GTR+Γ models, MrBayes and BEAST recover the true topology with posterior probability approaching 1 as sequence length grows, and 95% credible intervals for node ages achieve near-nominal coverage. Under *misspecification* — e.g., data simulated under a covarion or heterotachous process but analyzed under GTR+Γ — posteriors can be both wrong and spuriously confident, a phenomenon known as the *Bayesian star-tree paradox* in its extreme form. The practical remedy is posterior predictive checking and model adequacy assessment, not merely model selection among inadequate candidates.

### 5.2 Large-Scale Empirical Findings

Three results from the literature anchor the empirical picture:

1. **Relaxed clocks dominate.** Across 1,056 mammalian genes, model-averaged Bayes factors decisively favored uncorrelated relaxed clocks over strict clocks in the large majority of loci, with the lognormal and exponential rate distributions splitting support [2].
2. **Operators matter enormously.** The 65× efficiency gain from adaptive clock operators [3] translates directly into analyses that were previously infeasible: genome-scale datasets with thousands of loci now converge in days rather than months.
3. **Species-tree methods scale.** *BEAST jointly infers species trees and gene trees under the MSC [1]; exact sequence-diversity formulas under the MSC with Yule speciation [6] provide analytic checks on these samplers, confirming that simulated diversity matches theory.

### 5.3 Theoretical Guarantees

Beyond Kingman's convergence theorem, two results structure the theory:

- *Identifiability of the MSC*: the species-tree topology and internal branch lengths (in coalescent units) are identifiable from the distribution of gene trees, except in degenerate cases — which is why full-likelihood MSC methods are consistent where summary methods can fail [1, 6].
- *Non-identifiability of sampled-ancestor models*: birth–death skyline models in which every sample may be a sampled ancestor are non-identifiable without fixing one parameter, a fundamental limit on fossil-calibrated epidemiology [5].

```python
import random, math

def kingman_waiting_time(k, Ne):
    """Draw the waiting time (generations) until the next coalescence
    among k lineages in a diploid population of effective size Ne."""
    rate = k * (k - 1) / (4.0 * Ne)   # pairs * per-pair rate 1/(2Ne)... per generation
    return random.expovariate(rate)

def expected_tmrca(n, Ne):
    """E[T_MRCA] = 4*Ne*(1 - 1/n) generations (diploid Kingman)."""
    return 4.0 * Ne * (1.0 - 1.0 / n)

# Example: n=10 lineages, Ne=10_000 -> E[TMRCA] = 36,000 generations
print(expected_tmrca(10, 10_000))
```

## 6 Limitations

1. **Convergence is not guaranteed in practice.** Even with modern operators, a nontrivial fraction of empirical datasets resist convergence within practical run times [2]. ASDSF $< 0.01$ is a diagnostic, not a proof.
2. **Identifiability boundaries.** Sampled-ancestor birth–death models require one fixed parameter [5]; relaxed-clock rate and time are confounded without calibrations.
3. **Model misspecification.** All standard analyses assume site independence, no recombination within loci, and time-reversibility. Violations produce overconfident wrong answers.
4. **The MSC's ILS-only world.** The multispecies coalescent ignores gene duplication/loss, horizontal transfer, and hybridization; network methods are nascent by comparison [1, 6].
5. **Computational scaling.** Likelihood evaluation is linear per site per iteration; genome-scale alignments demand BEAGLE/GPU acceleration and still strain resources.
6. **Prior sensitivity.** Divergence-time posteriors can be dominated by calibration priors rather than sequence data, particularly with vague fossil bounds.
7. **Summarization loss.** MCC and consensus trees discard the rich uncertainty the posterior worked so hard to characterize; the full posterior sample should be archived and reported.

## 7 Conclusion

Bayesian phylogenetics unifies three historically separate enterprises — substitution modeling, tree search, and population genetics — into a single coherent posterior computation. The Felsenstein likelihood supplies the data model; Metropolis–Hastings with carefully engineered NNI/SPR/TBR and clock-aware proposals supplies the engine; Kingman's coalescent and its multispecies extension supply the bridge from gene trees to species histories; and relaxed clocks with Bayesian model averaging supply the timescale. The trajectory of the field is unmistakable: from single-gene strict-clock analyses that ran overnight on workstations, to genome-scale multispecies-coalescent inference powered by adaptive operators sixty-five times more efficient than their predecessors [3]. The remaining frontiers — identifiability under richer sampling models [5], nonparametric demographic inference [4], and honest uncertainty quantification under misspecification — are where the next decade of theoretical work will concentrate. The posterior, as ever, is only as honest as the model and the chain that explores it.
