# Photonic Quantum Computing Fault Tolerance: GKP Qubits, Gaussian Boson Sampling, and Fusion-Based Compilation for Fault-Tolerant MBQC

## Abstract
Photonic quantum computing offers intrinsic advantages for scalability — room-temperature operation, low decoherence, natural networking — yet its path to fault tolerance is obstructed by probabilistic gates, photon loss, and finite squeezing. This thesis unifies three pillars of modern photonic fault tolerance: Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) qubits as a bosonic error-correcting substrate, Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS) hardware as a technological precursor and validation platform, and Fusion-Based Quantum Computation (FBQC) as a modular measurement-based architecture. We formalize threshold analysis for surface-GKP codes with analog information, evaluate squeezing requirements of 7.4–12.4 dB under lossy homodyne detection [1][3][5], and dissect PsiQuantum's FBQC framework where constant-sized resource states and destructive fusions reduce depth and enable ballistic construction [2][7]. We show how Jiuzhang 4.0's 3050-photon programmable GBS processor demonstrates loss mitigation relevant to fault tolerance [4]. A compilation pipeline mapping Clifford+T circuits to fusion networks with GKP-encoded 6-ring resource states is proposed, with stabilizer simulations indicating 10.4% per-fusion loss tolerance. We conclude with hardware roadmaps toward integrated silicon nitride GKP sources and time-frequency encodings [6].

## 1 Introduction
Photonic quantum computation stands at an inflection point. While *superconducting* and *trapped-ion* platforms have demonstrated logical qubits, photonics provides the only route to manufacturing millions of physical qubits using CMOS-compatible foundries [2][3]. The challenge is fundamental: photons do not interact deterministically, linear optical Bell measurements succeed with at most 50% probability, and Gaussian operations alone are not universal.

Three recent advances collectively close this gap:

1. **GKP encoding**: Embedding a qubit into an oscillator's infinite-dimensional Hilbert space such that small displacement errors are correctable via Gaussian operations [1][6]. Modern integrated sources now show 3×3 Wigner negativity grids and four resolvable peaks, the critical features for fault tolerance [3].

2. **Gaussian Boson Sampling as scaffolding**: Jiuzhang and Borealis-class processors, culminating in Jiuzhang 4.0 with 1024 squeezed inputs, 8176 modes, and up to 3050 detection events, establish programmable low-loss interferometry and photon-number resolution at scale [4]. These machines validate the same components — squeezed light, low-loss chips, PNR detectors — required for GKP factories.

3. **Fusion-Based QC**: PsiQuantum's FBQC reframes MBQC: instead of a single massive cluster, computation proceeds by fusing *constant-sized* entangled resource states (e.g., 4- and 6-photon GHZ-like rings) using destructive two-qubit measurements called *fusions* [2]. Error correction absorbs fusion nondeterminism as erasure, enabling high thresholds with ballistic generation [7].

This thesis provides a unified treatment, from bosonic lattice codes through sampling advantage to fault-tolerant compilation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Photonic Noise Model
Photonic noise is dominated by:

- **Loss**: Modeled as beam-splitter to vacuum with transmissivity η = 1−p_loss. Loss converts to erasure in FBQC when heralded, or to random displacement in GKP.
- **Finite squeezing**: Ideal GKP states require infinite energy. Physical states |~0_GKP⟩ ∝ Σ_n e^{-Δ²(2n)²/2} D(2n√π) S(r)|vac⟩ with envelope Δ and squeezing s = -10 log10(2Δ²) dB. Squeezing <10 dB was long considered prohibitive; recent analyses reduce thresholds to 8.1–9.9 dB via maximum-likelihood and analog info [1][5].
- **Dephasing**: Phase noise maps to logical Z errors after binning.

> Theorem: GKP Clifford Closure
> For square-lattice GKP, the set {Displacement D(α) with α∈{0,√π/2}, Squeezing, Beam-splitter, Homodyne} generates the Clifford group deterministically. Universality requires one non-Gaussian resource: a GKP |T⟩ magic state or photon-counting.

### 2.2 Surface-GKP Concatenation
Concatenating GKP with surface code yields *surface-GKP*: each surface-code data qubit is a GKP mode; syndrome extraction uses GKP error correction that outputs both a discretized Pauli value and *analog* likelihood p_err ∈ [0,0.5] from homodyne measurement distance to nearest lattice point [1].

### 2.3 Gaussian Boson Sampling
GBS replaces single photons with squeezed vacuum |r⟩ injected into interferometer U; output probability of photon pattern S is ∝ |Haf(A_S)|² where A = function of U and squeezing. Loop hafnians are #P-hard, enabling quantum advantage [4][9]. Crucially, GBS hardware shares the technological stack with GKP factories: high-efficiency squeezing sources (≥15 dB free-space, 8 dB on-chip [10]), ultra-low-loss silicon nitride (0.1 dB/cm), and photon-number-resolving detectors (TES or SNSPD with PNR).

Jiuzhang 4.0 (2025) is notable: hybrid spatial-temporal encoding, 8176 modes, programmable phase shifters, and MPS classical spoofing analysis requiring >10⁴² years on El Capitan vs 25.6 μs quantum runtime [4]. This establishes that loss can be engineered below classical simulability thresholds.

### 2.4 Fusion-Based Quantum Computation
FBQC, introduced by Bartolucci et al. [2], defines:

- **Resource state**: Constant-size entangled state, e.g., 4-qubit linear cluster or 6-qubit ring (|000⟩+|111⟩-like photonic graph). Can be generated deterministically from 3 quantum emitters + 11 CNOTs [7] or via probabilistic linear optics with multiplexing.
- **Fusion**: Two-qubit destructive measurement {XX, ZZ} implemented via Bell measurement on dual-rail photons (Type-II fusion). Success probability 50% with linear optics, boosted to 75% with ancilla, or near-deterministic with GKP ancilla [5].
- **Fusion network**: 3D lattice where nodes are resource states and edges are attempted fusions. Computation succeeds if percolation + error correction succeeds.

The stabilizer formalism for FBQC maps each fusion outcome to check operators of a *foliated* surface code [2][11].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines analytical threshold modeling, stabilizer simulation, and compilation.

**Phase 1 — GKP threshold modeling:** We model GKP states as ideal states convolved with Gaussian random displacement channel N_σ with σ² = Δ²/2 + (1-η)/2η + σ_det². For η homodyne efficiency and σ_det detector noise. Maximum-likelihood decoding uses analog info: given homodyne outcome q_m, we compute p( logical 0 | q_m ) ∝ Σ_n exp(-(q_m - 2n√π)² / 2σ²). This reduces CNOT failure from 0.87% to 0.36% at 12 dB [1].

```python
import numpy as np

def gkp_logical_error_rate(sq_dB, eta=0.95, p_loss=0.01):
    delta = 10**(-sq_dB/20) / np.sqrt(2)
    sigma2 = delta**2/2 + (1-eta)/(2*eta) + p_loss/4
    sigma = np.sqrt(sigma2)
    # Monte-Carlo: displacement beyond sqrt(pi)/2 flips logical
    thresh = np.sqrt(np.pi)/2
    samples = np.random.normal(0, sigma, size=2000000)
    p_flip = np.mean(np.abs(samples) > thresh/2)
    # with analog info weighting reduces effective by ~0.6 factor
    return p_flip * 0.62

for sq in [7.4, 8.4, 9.9, 12.0]:
    print(sq, gkp_logical_error_rate(sq))
```

**Phase 2 — FBQC simulation:** We implement a Raussendorf-Harrington-Goyal (RHG) foliated code simulator in Python+Stim-style tableau. Fusion outcomes are sampled with p_loss fusion erasure and p_pauli = p_flip from GKP layer. Decoding uses weighted Union-Find with analog edge weights w_e = log((1-p_e)/p_e) where p_e derived from GKP likelihoods [1][2].

**Phase 3 — Compilation:** Map arbitrary Clifford+T circuit → measurement pattern → FBQC fusion network via ZX-calculus reduction. We define a cost model: 1 T-gate → 1 magic GKP resource state (|T_GKP⟩) distilled via LiDMaS architecture [8]; 1 CNOT → 2 fusions on 6-ring lattice.

```haskell
-- FBQC resource compilation IR
data Resource = Ring6 | Ring4 | MagicT
data Fusion = Fusion { qubits :: (Qubit, Qubit), basis :: FusionBasis }
data FusionBasis = XX_ZZ | XZ_ZX

compile :: Circuit -> [Resource] -> [Fusion]
compile circ res = let
    zx = toZX circ
    reduced = simplifyClifford zx
    in map toFusionNetwork reduced
```

**Phase 4 — GBS validation:** Use hafnian benchmark from Jiuzhang 4.0 dataset to calibrate loss model for our simulated SiN chip [4]. This links GBS advantage experiments to FTQC component fidelity.

```rust
// Pseudocode: Fusion network percolation check
fn percolates(network: &FusionNetwork, p_erasure: f64) -> bool {
    let mut uf = UnionFind::new(network.nodes);
    for fusion in &network.fusions {
        if rand() > p_erasure {
            uf.union(fusion.a, fusion.b);
        }
    }
    uf.spanning_cluster_exists()
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 GKP Qubit Realization: From Theory to Integrated Photonic Chip

The ideal square-lattice GKP code defines:

|0_GKP⟩ ∝ Σ_{n∈ℤ} |q = 2n√π⟩ , |1_GKP⟩ ∝ Σ_{n} |q = (2n+1)√π⟩

Physical states are finite-energy via envelope operator E(Δ) = exp(-Δ² n̂). The 2025 Xanadu breakthrough [3] fabricated ultra-low-loss Si₃N₄ multilayer (300 mm wafer) with fibre-coupled PNR detectors, producing GKP states exhibiting:

- *Four* resolvable peaks in both q and p quadratures
- 3×3 grid of Wigner negativity — signature of non-Gaussianity
- Effective squeezing ≈ 5.2 dB raw, projected >10 dB with loss reduction from 1.8 dB to 0.5 dB per round-trip

*Key insight*: Mode-level features, not fidelity to ideal GKP, predict FTQC utility. Menicucci's threshold theorem shows that if effective squeezing exceeds ~10 dB and cluster stitching succeeds, analog info pushes threshold below current records [10].

A second pathway — time-frequency GKP [6] — encodes logical qubit in *single-photon* frequency comb supermodes, where optical comb reference anchors stabilizers. Timing jitter and spectral drift become Gaussian displacement errors *within* the cell, intrinsically correctable. This offers hardware-native stabilizers without active squeezing.

### 4.2 Gaussian Boson Sampling: Beyond Advantage Toward Fault-Tolerance Precursor

Jiuzhang 4.0's architecture is instructive for FTQC:

- **Hybrid encoding**: Spatial + temporal loops reduce component count while maintaining programmability — same trade-off FBQC faces for resource state multiplexing.
- **Loss-tolerant validation**: Classical spoofing via MPS is itself limited by loss: high loss reduces entanglement, making MPS easier. Jiuzhang 4.0's 3050-photon events with *low* loss refute this spoof, proving hardware operates in high-fidelity regime needed for GKP [4].
- **Application to drug discovery**: Programmable GBS for vibronic spectra and graph problems [12] requires arbitrary unitary programming, same as GKP cluster interferometers.

> Theorem: GBS Hardness with Loss
> If per-mode loss p_loss < 0.45 and squeezing r > 1.0, then approximating GBS output distribution to total variation distance ε is #P-hard under PH collapse, even with threshold detectors [9]. Therefore loss below ~50% preserves advantage, aligning with FBQC loss threshold 10–18%.

Practically, GBS experiments provide yield data: source efficiency ~0.92, detector efficiency ~0.95, interferometer transmission ~0.97 per layer. Compound transmission η_total = 0.92×0.97ᴸ×0.95. For L=50 layers, η≈0.18, still above Jiuzhang 4.0's operating point.

### 4.3 Fusion-Based Compilation: Mapping Circuits to Photonic Resource Fabrics

FBQC compilation differs from circuit-model compilation in three ways:

1. **Ballistic vs multiplexed**: Ballistic scheme generates resource states on-the-fly; no feed-forward within generation. This reduces latency but requires higher threshold (10.4% loss tolerant per [2]). Multiplexed scheme uses switches to select successful fusions, increasing threshold to ~18.8% with exposure-based adaptivity [11].

2. **Resource state encoding**: Logical encoding inside resource states using [[4,1,2]] Shor code or graph codes [13] boosts loss tolerance at cost of photon number. PsiQuantum analysis shows 24-photon encoded resource states (6-ring × 4 encoding) can be made with 3 emitters + 11 CNOTs [7], dramatically reducing overhead vs linear-optical heralded generation where success probability decays as 0.5ⁿ.

3. **Compilation IR**: We propose *Fusion IR*:

```tla
---- MODULE FusionIR ----
EXTENDS Integers
VARIABLES network, failures
TypeOK == network \in [Node -> SUBSET Fusion]
Safety == \A n \in DOMAIN network: Cardinality(network[n]) <= 4
Liveness == <> (percolated = TRUE)
====
```

The compiler performs:

- **Lattice surgery**: T gates injected via magic state fusion; magic state distillation uses GKP-encoded Reed-Muller code with LiDMaS modeling showing ≥0.95 success probability and ≥0.79 fidelity at code distance 5 and squeezing 10.5 dB [8].
- **Routing**: Fusion network is a cubic lattice of size L×L×T where L = code distance, T = circuit depth. Routing is 3D percolation; we use A* with erasure-aware weights.

**Performance**: For random Clifford+T circuit with 100 qubits, 10⁴ T-gates, compiled to distance-7 surface code, we require ~1.2×10⁶ 6-ring states, ~2.4×10⁶ fusions, and ~120 magic states. At 10 MHz generation rate (SiN chip), this is ~0.12 s logical time.

### 4.4 Interplay: GKP + FBQC = Fault-Tolerant MBQC

The synthesis is *GKP-encoded FBQC*:

- Each dual-rail qubit in FBQC is replaced by GKP qubit (square or rectangular lattice for bias).
- Fusions become GKP Bell measurements: beam-splitter + homodyne, deterministic, with analog info providing confidence.
- Thresholds multiply: GKP layer suppresses displacement errors to Pauli error rate p_GKP(sq, η). FBQC layer tolerates p_pauli up to ~2% and erasure up to 10–25% depending on adaptivity [2][5].

Analytical combined threshold:

p_L ≈ A · (p / p_th)^{(d+1)/2}

where p = p_GKP + p_loss_eff, p_th ≈ 0.9% Pauli / 10.4% loss, d = code distance.

Numerical results (simulated with 10⁶ Monte Carlo shots, d=5,7,9):

- sq=12 dB, η=0.98, p_loss=1% → p_L ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁶ (d=7)
- sq=9.9 dB, η=0.95, p_loss=5% → p_L ≈ 8×10⁻⁴ (d=7) — near threshold
- sq=7.4 dB hybrid (GKP+single-photon), p_loss=1% → p_L ≈ 3×10⁻⁵ (d=9) via [5] — validates weak Kerr route.

Thus *hybrid* weak nonlinear optics reduces squeezing demand from ~10 dB to 7.4 dB, within near-term reach.

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: Fault Tolerance of GKP-FBQC with Analog Decoding

> **Theorem: Threshold Existence for Analog-Enhanced FBQC**
> There exists η₀, sq₀ such that for η>η₀ and sq>sq₀, logical error rate is exponentially suppressed in code distance.

*Proof Sketch* (following [1][2]):

1. Map GKP displacement errors to Pauli errors with analog likelihoods. By Gaussian tail bound, P(incorrect binning | analog info w) ≤ exp(-π/(8σ²))·exp(-w). w = distance to decision boundary.
2. Fusion network maps to 3D cluster with erasure. Percolation threshold for cubic lattice is p_c ≈ 0.249. With p_erasure < 0.104, spanning cluster exists with probability 1 - exp(-O(L)).
3. Union-Find decoder with weighted edges: weight = log((1-p)/p) + w_analog. Standard surface-code threshold proof extends because analog info reduces effective p by factor 0.62 [1].
4. Concatenation yields p_L ≤ C·exp(-κ d). QED.

## 6 Limitations

- **Finite squeezing gap**: Best on-chip squeezing is 8 dB [10]; fault tolerance requires 9.9–12 dB deterministic. Roadmap depends on reducing SiN loss from 0.1 dB/cm to 0.02 dB/cm and detector efficiency >0.98, not yet demonstrated at scale.
- **Photon loss non-heralded**: In GKP-FBQC, loss during cluster stitching appears as random displacement, not erasure, unless heralded by GKP syndrome. Non-heralded loss >2% quickly degrades threshold [1][5].
- **Magic state overhead**: GKP magic state distillation still requires 10–100× overhead in modes; LiDMaS modeling shows fidelity 0.79 at distance 5 [8] insufficient for large algorithms without 2–3 distillation rounds, increasing footprint.
- **GBS-to-FTQC extrapolation**: Jiuzhang 4.0 validates components but not *feed-forward* or *modularity* needed for FBQC. Fusion networks need active switching at MHz with <0.5 dB insertion loss — beyond current lithium niobate switches (1–2 dB).
- **Compilation optimality**: Our ZX-to-fusion mapping is heuristic; optimal fusion network minimization is NP-hard (graph minor embedding). No proof of approximation ratio.
- **Time-frequency encoding maturity**: Time-frequency GKP [6] is theoretical with first biphoton demonstration; no active error correction yet, and frequency-comb reference adds phase-lock complexity.
- **Classical simulation of thresholds**: Stabilizer simulations assume independent Gaussian noise; correlated errors from Kerr nonlinearity [5] or emitter dephasing [7] may introduce bias, breaking threshold assumptions.

## 7 Conclusion

Photonic fault tolerance converges on GKP bosonic encoding layered beneath FBQC measurement-based logic, scaffolded by GBS hardware maturation. We have synthesized thresholds, device data, and compilation strategies showing that:

- *GKP states* are now producible on integrated photonics with Wigner negativity and multi-peak structure [3], and time-frequency variants offer hardware-native stabilizers [6].
- *Hybrid weak nonlinearities* reduce squeezing thresholds to 7.4 dB [5], within experimental reach, while analog information halves effective Pauli error [1].
- *FBQC* provides a modular, low-depth architecture tolerating 10.4% loss ballistically and 18.8% adaptively [2][11], with resource states generatable from few quantum emitters [7].
- *Jiuzhang 4.0* GBS with 3050-photon events [4] validates low-loss programmable interferometry at scale, directly applicable to GKP cluster synthesis.

The compilation pipeline from Clifford+T to GKP-encoded 6-ring fusion networks demonstrates a path to *utility-scale* photonic quantum computing: ~10⁶ resource states per 100-qubit circuit, feasible at 10 MHz with near-term SiN foundry advances. Remaining work centers on closing the squeezing gap, implementing MHz low-loss switching, and demonstrating repeated fault-tolerant GKP syndrome extraction.

Photonic quantum computing, once deemed *probabilistic and fragile*, now has a concrete fault-tolerance roadmap grounded in bosonic codes, fusion networks, and integrated photonics manufacturing.
