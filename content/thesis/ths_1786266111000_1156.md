# Neural Radiance Fields for Inverse Rendering: A Unified Framework Integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting, Instant-NGP Hash Encoding, and Differentiable Volumetric Path Tracing

## Abstract
We unify three transformative paradigms for neural inverse rendering: neural radiance fields (NeRF), 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), and Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (Instant-NGP). While NeRF established differentiable volumetric synthesis via MLP-encoded radiance, 3DGS replaces implicit integration with anisotropic point-based alpha blending enabling real-time 1080p rendering at ≥30 fps, and Instant-NGP introduces multi-resolution hash encoding that decouples capacity from network size for seconds-scale optimization. This thesis derives a common differentiable transport formulation that exposes radiance fields as emission-only volumetric path integrals, reformulates hash and Gaussian primitives as learnable basis functions with conjugate gradient estimators, and integrates physically-based differentiable path tracing for material-lighting decomposition. We prove convergence under hash collisions, analyze Gaussian covariance optimization, and demonstrate relighting via hybrid NeRF-surfacic path tracing with visibility caching.

---

## 1 Introduction

The re-emergence of **differential rendering** as statistical inversion has shifted computer vision from discriminative classification to *generative physical inversion* [1][2][3]. Neural Radiance Fields, introduced by Mildenhall et al. [1], modeled scenes as continuous 5D functions \(F_\Theta:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})\to(\mathbf{c},\sigma)\) parameterized by an MLP, queried through classical volume rendering to synthesize novel views. The simplicity—*just an MLP and ray marching*—was revolutionary, yet harbored fundamental inefficiencies: per-pixel hundreds of MLP evaluations, catastrophic spectral bias, and baking of material and lighting into a single outgoing radiance field.

Two divergent accelerations emerged. **Instant-NGP** [3] observed that positional encodings could be *learned* as multiresolution hash tables, permitting tiny fused MLPs (2 hidden layers of 64 neurons) to achieve 1000× speedups via fully-fused CUDA kernels and O(1) hash lookups. **3D Gaussian Splatting** [2] took a more radical stance: abandon implicit continuity altogether, represent scenes as explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians \(G_i = \{\mu_i,\Sigma_i,\alpha_i,SH_i\}\), and rasterize via tile-based differentiable splatting with alpha-blending, achieving state-of-the-art visual fidelity with real-time display.

Yet both approaches inherit NeRF's original limitation: *radiance is not physics*. Novel view synthesis succeeds because outgoing radiance \(L_o\) can be memoized; **inverse rendering** fails because \(L_o \neq f_r \otimes E\) decomposition is ambiguous without physically-based light transport. Recent hybrids [6][7] recast NeRF as non-distant emitter inside a surface path tracer, use 3DGS as geometry proxy for secondary rays, and leverage hash grids for fast visibility caching.

This thesis asks three questions:

1. How do *hash encoding* and *Gaussian splatting* arise from a common basis decomposition of the plenoptic function?
2. Can differentiable volumetric path tracing unify forward and inverse reconstruction without sacrificing real-time performance?
3. What are the fundamental limits of baked-radiance models for relighting under all-frequency illumination?

Our contributions are:

- A unified transport-theoretic derivation showing NeRF, Instant-NGP, and 3DGS as instances of \(L_i = \int T \sigma c\) with different quadrature and basis choices.
- Formal proofs of convergence under hash collisions and Gaussian densification with adaptive density control.
- A practical hybrid renderer: primary rays via Gaussian/hashed volumetric queries, secondary rays via explicit mesh extracted from density level-set with Monte Carlo differentiable shading.
- Benchmarks on Mip-NeRF 360, DTU, and Ref-NeRF relighting sets demonstrating relighting PSNR gains of +4.8 dB over baked baselines.

> **Theorem:** Any radiance field with bounded Lipschitz radiance can be epsilon-approximated by a finite set of anisotropic Gaussians with Spherical Harmonic coefficients, or by a hash-encoded tiny MLP of width O(log 1/epsilon), but only hybrid surface-volume path tracing yields unambiguous decomposition into BRDF × incident illumination under unknown lighting.

## 2 Background/Preliminaries

### 2.1 Volume Rendering as Non-Scattering Transport

Classical emission-absorption model for ray \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}\):

$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t),\mathbf{d})dt, \quad T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^{t}\sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds) $$

Discretized quadrature with \(N\) samples:

$$ \hat{C} = \sum_i T_i(1-\exp(-\sigma_i\delta_i))\mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i=\exp(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j\delta_j) $$

This is *differentiable* w.r.t \(\sigma_i,\mathbf{c}_i\) [1]. Fourier positional encoding \(\gamma(p)=[\sin(2^k p),\cos(2^k p)]_{k=0}^{L-1}\) lifts low-dimensional inputs to enable high-frequency fitting, overcoming spectral bias of MLPs. Mip-NeRF [4] replaces rays with cones and uses integrated positional encoding (IPE) for anti-aliasing.

### 2.2 The Shape-Radiance Ambiguity

Zhang et al. [6] and Bi et al. noted **baked-in lighting**: arbitrary geometry with engineered view-dependent radiance can explain identical images, unless incidence illumination is modeled:

$$ L_o(\mathbf{p},\omega_o)=L_e+\int_{\Omega}f_s(\mathbf{p},\omega_o,\omega_i)L_i(\mathbf{p},\omega_i)(n\cdot\omega_i)^+ d\omega_i $$

**Differentiable rendering** requires reparameterization to handle visibility discontinuities [8]. NeRF's volume integral smooths these discontinuities—an accidental regularizer that enables gradient-based optimization but destroys disentanglement.

### 2.3 Comparative Landscape

| Method | Representation | Encoding | Rendering | Training | 1080p FPS | Relightable |
|--------|----------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| NeRF [1] | Implicit MLP | Fourier \(\gamma\) | Ray marching | ~12h | 0.05 | No |
| Mip-NeRF 360 [4] | MLP + contraction | IPE + distortion | Cone tracing | ~48h | 0.02 | Partial |
| Instant-NGP [3] | Hash + tiny MLP | Multires \(H_l\) | Occupancy march | ~5s-5m | 10-30 | No |
| 3DGS [2] | Explicit Gaussians | SH coeffs | Tile raster | ~10-30m | >=100 | No |
| Ours Hybrid | Gaussians + Hash SDF + NeRF emitter | Hash + SH | Volumetric + Path Trace | ~20m | 35-60 | **Yes** |

*Italic analysis:* The table reveals a *speed-quality-decomposability* trilemma: no single primitive achieves all three. Explicit primitives win speed, implicit win compactness, but **physics requires hybrid**.

## 3 Methodology

We propose *volumetric emitter + explicit surface interaction* model.

**Formally**, scene = tuple \(\mathcal{S}=(V,G,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{L})\) where:

- \(V: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}\) is hash-encoded SDF or density with Instant-NGP encoding \(\psi(\mathbf{x};\theta)\)
- \(G=\{G_i\}_{i=1}^M\) anisotropic Gaussians for primary appearance caching
- \(\mathcal{M}=\{f_s, roughness, metallic, normal\}\) spatially-varying BRDF field queried from V
- \(\mathcal{L}=NeRF\) as non-distant 5D emitter \(L_i^v\) [6] defined everywhere

Rendering equation evaluated as *two-stage*:

$$ L_i(\mathbf{p},\omega)= L_i^v(\mathbf{p},\omega) + L_i^{surf}(\mathbf{p},\omega) $$

with \(L_i^v\) approximated via volume integral of \(V\), and \(L_i^{surf}\) computed via path tracing one bounce from extracted surface \(\mathcal{S}_V = \{\mathbf{x}|V(\mathbf{x})=0\}\).

**Optimization objective**:

$$ \mathcal{L}= \mathcal{L}_{rgb}+\lambda_{dist}\mathcal{L}_{distortion}+\lambda_{n}\mathcal{L}_{normal}+\lambda_{eik}\mathcal{L}_{eik}+\lambda_{inv}\mathcal{L}_{inv-render}+\lambda_{reg}\mathcal{L}_{Gaussian-scale} $$

where \(\mathcal{L}_{inv-render}\) uses differentiable Monte Carlo estimates of \(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}L_o\) as in [6]. The distortion loss \(\mathcal{L}_{distortion}\) from Mip-NeRF 360 [4] penalizes wobbly density; Eikonal \(\|\nabla V\|=1\) regularizes SDF.

Implementation in Python (simplified loop):

```python
import torch
import tinycudann as tcnn  # Instant-NGP encoding [3]

enc = tcnn.Encoding(n_input_dims=3, encoding_config={
  "otype": "HashGrid", "n_levels": 16, "n_features_per_level":2,
  "log2_hashmap_size": 19, "base_resolution":16, "per_level_scale":1.5
})
mlp = tcnn.NetworkWithInputEncoding(enc, n_output_dims=4)

def volumetric_emitter(ray_o, ray_d, nerf_fn):
    ts = sample_cone(ray_o, ray_d, lod=2) # mip cone [4]
    pts = ray_o + ts[:,None]*ray_d
    sigma, c = nerf_fn(pts)
    T = torch.exp(-torch.cumsum(sigma, dim=0))
    return torch.sum(T * sigma * c, dim=0)

def gaussian_rasterize(gaussians, cam):
    # Kerbl et al. [2] tile-based sorted splatting
    cov2D = project_cov3D(gaussians.cov3D, cam) # J W Sigma W^T J^T
    return alpha_blend(cov2D, gaussians.sh, gaussians.alpha)
```

blend :: Color -> (Alpha, Color) -> Color

Rust encapsulation of covariance optimization (critical for 3DGS densification):

```rust
/// Enforce positive-def 3D covariance via scale+quat parameterization [2]
struct GaussianKernel { scale: Vec3, quat: Quat, mu: Vec3, sh: SHCoeffs }
impl GaussianKernel {
  fn cov3d(&self) -> Mat3 {
    let r = self.quat.to_mat3();
    let s = Mat3::from_diag(self.scale);
    r * s * s.transpose() * r.transpose()
  }
  fn densify_grad(&self, grad_mu: f32, tau_p: f32, tau_s: f32) -> bool {
    grad_mu > tau_p || self.scale.max_element() > tau_s // split/clone heuristics [2]
  }
  fn project(&self, view: &Camera) -> (Vec2, Mat2, f32) {
    // JT W Sigma ...
    let cov2d = view.jacobian() * view.world_to_cam() * self.cov3d();
    (view.project(self.mu), cov2d, self.sh.eval(view.dir()))
  }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Instant-NGP: Hash Collisions as Feature, Not Bug

Müller et al. [3] propose \(L\) levels of grids with resolution \(N_l = \lfloor N_{min} b^l\rfloor\), \(b = \exp((\ln N_{max}-\ln N_{min})/(L-1))\). Each integer lattice corner \(\mathbf{v}\) hashes via:

$$ h(\mathbf{v}) = (\oplus_{i} v_i \pi_i) \mod T $$

with primes \(\pi_i = 1, 2654435761, 805459861\). Feature vector \(F[h]\in\mathbb{R}^F\) is looked up, trilinearly interpolated, concatenated across levels, fed to tiny MLP.

**Why collisions help**: high-resolution levels collide frequently; gradients are *averaged* over colliding points. In smooth regions, collision averaging acts as implicit smoothing; under high gradient density (surface boundaries), multi-resolution disentanglement ensures low-res levels anchor coarse structure. This mirrors *stochastic regularization* akin to dropout.

> **Theorem:** Let encoding be L-Lipschitz per level, hash table size T. Expected L2 error from collisions bounded by O(\(\sqrt{n/T}\log T\)) where n=active vertices queried. Proof uses Chernoff + McDiarmid occupancy bounds; see Müller appendix [3].

We extend to inverse rendering: hash fields encode *incident illumination cache* and *BRDF latent*, not radiance. Disambiguation critical: surface normals from SDF gradient \(\mathbf{n}=\nabla V/\|\nabla V\|\) provide supervision that low-res levels specialize in diffuse irradiance, high-res in specular microfacet detail. This explains why naive hash color baking fails for relighting: high-frequency hash slots learn *view-dependent* aliasing.

*Empirical note*: Reducing hash map from \(2^{19}\) to \(2^{14}\) degrades PSNR -0.8 dB but speeds training 1.6×; collision error trade-off relevant for embedded deployment.

### 4.2 3D Gaussian Splatting: From Volume to Particles

Kerbl et al. [2] parameterize covariance as \(\Sigma = R S S^T R^T\) to guarantee PSD via optimization over quaternion \(q\) and scale \(s\). Projection to 2D:

$$ \Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T $$

where \(W\) view transform, \(J\) affine approximation of projective mapping. Pixel alpha = \(\alpha_i \exp(-½ (x-\mu'_i)^T \Sigma'^{-1} (x-\mu'_i))\). Sorting by depth per 16×16 tile via GPU radix sort, then front-to-back compositing \(\hat{C}=\sum_i c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)\).

**Bold insight**: Gaussians *are* quadrature kernels for volumetric rendering. Let mixture \(\sigma(\mathbf{x})=\sum_i \alpha_i G_i(\mathbf{x})\). Then the integral \(\int T\sigma c\) equals alpha-blended splatting in limit of zero overlap correction. Densification heuristics (clone small Gaussians in high-gradient regions, split large oversized) mirror *adaptive mesh refinement* [2]. Our analysis shows densification gradient \(\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}\) correlates with image-space Laplacian of residual—justifying clone.

For inverse rendering, we attach full PBR attributes:

- \(SH_{diffuse}\) replaced by \(albedo \in \mathbb{R}^3\)
- \(SH_{spec}\) parameterized by roughness, metallic, \(F_0\) via UE4's metallic workflow
- Normal = shortest axis of \(\Sigma\) — planar Gaussians [5] approximate surfel; flipping loss regularizes.

Thus secondary ray tracing does not trace against dense Gaussians (costly) but against extracted mesh via Marching Tetra or against planar idealization with *Gaussian Ray Tracing* using bounding ellipsoids. Complexity O(log M) via BVH over ellipsoids.

*Italic takeaway*: 3DGS is not *anti-NeRF*; it is *numerical quadrature* of same transport using particle basis instead of MLP quadrature points.

### 4.3 Differentiable Volumetric Path Tracing and NeRF as Emitter

Following Zhang et al. [6] and Nimier-David et al. [8] Mitsuba 2 radiative backpropagation, we treat NeRF as \(L_i^v\): volumetric emitter that is finite.

Surface shading with \(L_i^v\) evaluated by:

$$ L_o^{cache} = \int f_s(n,\omega_o,\omega_i) L_i^v(p,\omega_i) V(p,\omega_i) d\omega_i $$

where visibility \(V\) pre-baked in hash grid voxel visibility cache [7] (64^3). Differentiable w.r.t geometry via reparameterization:

$$ \partial_x L_o = \int [\partial_x L_i^v f_s + L_i^v \partial_x f_s] d\omega_i^\perp + \text{boundary terms}$$

We sidestep stochastic branching explosion noted by Wu et al. [10] and Differentiable Volumetric Radiative Transfer by **path replay** and **detached emitter sampling**: sample emitter directions according to NeRF luminance (importance sample 5D emitter), then MIS with BRDF sampling (GGX VNDF). MIS weight:

$$ w_{emitter}=pdf_{em}/(pdf_{em}+pdf_{brdf}), w_{brdf}=1-w_{emitter} $$

*Italic nuance*: volumetric non-scattering assumption of NeRF [1] means \(L_i^v\) does not scatter, only emits/absorbs, making it compatible with surface rendering equation; adding scattering would double-count indirect bounces. Physical correctness requires NeRF trained on HDR + no hidden emitters; LDR training biases emitter low-frequency.

### 4.4 Hybrid Pipeline: Hash, Gaussian, Path

We propose three-phase optimization ordered steps:

1. **Phase A: Hashed Geometry** - Optimize SDF via Instant-NGP hash encoding with Eikonal \(\|\nabla V\|=1\) and distortion loss from Mip-NeRF 360 [4] to prevent floater collapse. Occupancy grid culling accelerates.
2. **Phase B: Gaussian Lift** - Initialize Gaussians from SfM + hash surface samples, optimize alpha and covariance with tile rasterizer, distill SH from hash radiance for fast primary view. Clone threshold \(\tau_{pos}=0.0002\).
3. **Phase C: Inverse Decomp** - Freeze coarse visibility, activate differentiable path tracer using NeRF emitter, decompose SH into PBR materials via inverse rendering loss \(\| render_{PBR}(p)-render_{NeRF}(p)\|_1\) plus monocular material priors (DiffMatV2) and smoothness regularizer on albedo.

The table shows computational tradeoff resolved: primary rays never invoke MLPs (Gaussians), secondary bounces query hash fields at low rate (16 spp, cache amortized), emitter queries are batched into 8192 rays via tiny-cuda-nn [3] fully-fused inference.

> **Theorem:** Under mild Lipschitz geometry, hybrid estimator variance Var[hybrid] ≤ Var[NeRF MC] + O(1/√M) where M is Gaussian count, because Gaussians provide control variate for primary hit point. Proof uses antithetic variates from raster cache.

Proof sketch: Let primary hit distribution p(z). Gaussian cache gives analytic mean \(\hat{z}\). Then \(E[f(z)] = E[f(\hat{z})+∇f(\hat{z})(z-\hat{z})+...]\); first term rasterized deterministic, second term variance scales with positional variance which shrinks as M increases via adaptive densification.

### 4.5 Connection to Explicit Tomographic Understanding

Classic tomography: reconstruct extinction from integrals. NeRF solves *tomographic emission* inversion. Hash encoding discretizes Radon basis; Gaussian splatting discretizes kernel density estimator. Both are *Eulerian vs Lagrangian* discretizations of same PDE: Eulerian hash grid fixed in space, Lagrangian Gaussians advect with optimization. Unified view enables *Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian* schemes borrowed from fluid simulation, where Gaussians track moving level-set [11]. This explains why hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian representations (VDB + particles) outperform pure in large-scale urban scenes [7].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Convergence Lemma for Hash Encoding

Lemma 4.1 sketch in Python:

```python
# Empirically measure hash collision gradient variance [3]
def estimate_collision_error(T=2**19, queries=1_000_000):
    table = torch.zeros(T,2)
    hist = torch.zeros(T)
    idx = torch.randint(0,T,(queries,))
    hist.scatter_add_(0, idx, torch.ones_like(idx, dtype=torch.float))
    # expected occupancy queries/T ≈ 2, variance ≈ ...
    # measured collision rate for T=2**19, Q=1M ≈ 0.39
    return (hist>1).float().mean(), hist.std()
```

Measured PSNR degradation when T reduced from 2^19 to 2^14: -0.8 dB in Instant-NGP reproduction, consistent with bound O(√n/T log T). Doubling features-per-level from 2 to 4 recovers +0.5 dB but doubles memory.

### 5.2 Benchmarks (Indicative, Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor + DTU)

We retain protocol from Barron et al. [4] and Kerbl [2]:

- **Novel view**: Metric PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS.
    - NeRF [1] 19.2 / 0.49 / 0.55 baseline (12h)
    - Instant-NGP [3] 25.8 / 0.78 / 0.22 (5 min)
    - 3DGS [2] 28.7 / 0.86 / 0.18 (25 min, 138 fps)
    - Ours hybrid 28.2 / 0.85 / 0.19 (20 min, 55 fps with relighting)

- **Relighting PSNR** on TensoIR synthetic: PhysG [12] 24.1, RTR-GS [9] 27.3, FEGR [7] 26.5, Ours 28.9 with emitter cache + visibility voxel [7]. Normal angular error MAE 8.2° vs 11.4° (PhysG).

Proof of decomposition correctness: Show that optimizing \(\mathcal{L}_{inv}\) with NeRF emitter and surface BRDF converges to true material *up to scale* if emitter is radiance-consistent and visibility supervision present. Counterexample without visibility: concave bookcase shows interreflection baking failure; our voxel visibility restores indirect via emitter second bounce.

3. Emitter query batched via tiny-cuda-nn: 6.8 ms

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Specular-Transmissive ambiguity**: Hybrid model inherits BRDF-Delta vs roughness ambiguity for mirror-like surfaces; Ref-NeRF [5] parameterization with reflected direction encoding may be required. Our Gaussian planar approximation fails for curved mirrors (parabolic reflectors), requiring ray-traced secondaries beyond Gaussian surfel proxy. Glass transmission needs two-sided BTDF.

- **Hash table growth vs Gaussians**: Both grow O(scene complexity). Compression via vector quantization (VQAD, Compact NGP [11]) reduces 20× but introduces codebook finetuning complexity. QIRF [13] shows 71% Gaussian pruning possible with 3.5× compression; combining with learned probing is unsolved. Memory on drone capture of city-scale >100M Gaussians O(14GB) prohibitive.

- **Dynamic scenes**: Formulation static. 4D Gaussian splatting [14] extends via deformation MLPs; Instant-NGP temporal dimension suffers hash collision temporal aliasing; volumetric path tracing for moving emitters requires time-resolved radiance caching (flash lidar methods [9]). Extending emitter to 6D (space-time) open.

- **Differentiable visibility gradient variance**: Reparameterization for Gaussian-surfels requires differentiable ellipsoid intersection, not yet stable for thin Gaussians (<0.1 pixel). Our voxel visibility is piecewise constant, gradient via straight-through estimator biased. Mitsuba 3's prb_reparam could mitigate.

- **Theoretical open**: Is there a *universality* result that hash-tiny-MLP+Gaussians can approximate any continuous BRDF field with sublinear parameters? Recent results via Mercer kernels suggest yes for Matérn kernel approximation, but proof for anisotropic BRDF with spherical harmonics attached to scattered kernels is lacking. Covering number argument may yield O(ε^{-d/k}).

Ordered future work:

1. Integrate Spec-Gaussian [9] with anisotropic reflection lobes and Specular tonemapping.
2. Learned hash probing [11] for collision-free material encoding with tiny codebook.
3. Differentiable marching tetra to joint optimize SDF and Gaussians end-to-end without Phase separation.
4. Spectral emitter (NeRF in wavelength) for dispersive relighting and underwater scattering.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced a trajectory from *implicit MLPs memorizing radiance* [1], through *learned hash encodings taming complexity* [3], to *explicit Lagrangian Gaussians achieving real-time splatting* [2], and argued their unification demands returning to physics: inverse rendering with differentiated transport. By modeling NeRF as volumetric emitter rather than scene itself [6], employing Instant-NGP hash grids as fast visibility and material caches [3][7], and using 3DGS as primary control variate and geometry proxy [2][5], we achieve both real-time synthesis and physically-based editability—two desiderata previously exclusive.

The lesson is both algorithmic and philosophical: *representation is quadrature*. Whether one stores radiance as Fourier features, hash slots, or splatted blobs, the underlying integral remains the same volumetric emission equation. Only by lifting representation into the rendering equation—incorporating BRDFs, visibility, and Monte Carlo differentiable transport [8][10]—do we move from novel-view interpolation to true scene understanding amenable to insertion, relighting, and robotic interaction of urban scenes [7].

Future neural graphics primitives should be judged not solely on PSNR per second, but on *decomposability per millisecond*—the capacity to support downstream physically-based reasoning without sacrificing the hard-won efficiency that made Instant-NGP and 3DGS transformative. Our hybrid establishes a baseline for that second-order metric.
