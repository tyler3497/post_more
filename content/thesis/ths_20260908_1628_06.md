# Chiplet Interconnects: UCIe, Die-to-Die Link Training, BoW, and Coherence Fabrics for Multi-Die Packages

## Abstract

The exhaustion of economically viable monolithic scaling has forced system-on-chip architecture onto a new trajectory: disaggregation into chiplets joined by specialized die-to-die interconnects inside advanced packages. This thesis presents a rigorous treatment of the three dominant interconnect philosophies — the open UCIe standard, the parallel BoW PHY from the Open Compute Project, and vendor-proprietary fabrics such as AMD Infinity Fabric and Intel AIB — analyzing each across physical layer design, link-training protocols, bandwidth density, energy per bit, and cache-coherence support. We derive quantitative models for shoreline bandwidth, derive the link-training state machine required for reliable die-to-die bring-up, and demonstrate how CXL.mem and CXL.cache semantics ride atop UCIe to implement coherent multi-die memory fabrics. Our comparative analysis establishes that while UCIe targets an interoperable chiplet marketplace, no single standard simultaneously optimizes cost, density, and latency; coherent fabrics remain the least standardized and most performance-critical frontier.

## 1 Introduction

For five decades, the semiconductor industry extracted performance through the steady contraction of the transistor. That trajectory has fractured along two axes. First, the *economic* scaling described by Moore's law has slowed: below the 7 nm node, mask costs exceed hundreds of millions of dollars and defect density makes large monolithic dies yield poorly. Second, the *physical* reticle limit caps a single die at roughly 850 mm²; flagship accelerators and server CPUs can no longer be expressed on one reticle.

The industry's answer is the *multi-die package*: a system-in-package (SiP) in which specialized dies — compute tiles, cache tiles, I/O dies, high-bandwidth memory stacks — are integrated side by side (2.5D) or stacked (3D). AMD's EPYC processors proved the commercial viability of this model, Intel's Ponte Vecchio extended it to 47 chiplets combining 2.5D Co-EMIB and 3D Foveros integration [8], and TSMC's CoWoS and SoIC flows now package some of the largest AI accelerators in production.

Yet disaggregation transfers the hardest problem from the transistor to the *wire*: the die-to-die (D2D) interconnect must simultaneously deliver enormous bandwidth density, sub-nanosecond latency, sub-pJ/bit energy, and, for memory coherence, strict ordering and snoop semantics. Three design traditions compete:

1. **UCIe (Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express)** — the industry consortium standard (founded March 2022 by Intel, AMD, Arm, Google Cloud, Meta, Microsoft, Qualcomm, Samsung, TSMC, ASE), defining a complete physical layer, protocol stack, and software model that reuses PCIe and CXL semantics [2][3].
2. **BoW (Bunch of Wires)** — an OCP/ODSA open PHY specification emphasizing parallel, source-synchronous signaling optimized for low cost on organic substrates [4][5].
3. **Proprietary fabrics** — AMD's Infinity Fabric, Intel's AIB/EMIB ecosystem, and TSMC's LIPINCON, each tuned to a vendor's packaging stack [6][7].

This thesis unifies these threads: the physics of D2D signaling, the link-training discipline that makes packages boot reliably, the protocol layers that carry coherence, and the empirical evidence from shipping products.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Monolith to Package

Classical SoC design assumed on-die global wires with energy cost scaling as $CV^2$. In a package, the channel is shorter (millimeters rather than centimeters) and the substrate is electrically quieter than a board, permitting simpler signaling: lower-swing CMOS drivers, reduced equalization, and in some cases single-ended transmission. The design space thus spans *serial, high-speed SerDes* (UCIe standard package), *parallel source-synchronous buses* (BoW, AIB), and *ultra-dense 3D face-to-face interfaces* (Foveros, hybrid bonding, UCIe-3D).

The key figures of merit are:

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandwidth density** | Gb/s per mm of die edge (2.5D) or per mm² (3D) | Shoreline is the scarce resource |
| **Energy per bit** | pJ/bit end-to-end | Power budget is thermally capped |
| **BER** | Bit error rate at full rate | Determines FEC/retransmit need |
| **Latency** | ns, PHY + link layer | Critical for coherence traffic |
| **Reach** | mm over package routing | Sets floorplan freedom |

### 2.2 Prior Art

The idea of multi-chip modules is old — IBM's Thermal Conduction Modules of the 1980s — but modern chiplet economics began with Intel's EMIB (embedded multi-die interconnect bridge), a small silicon bridge in an organic substrate achieving silicon-density routing between adjacent dies [8]. AMD's 2017 EPYC demonstrated that a *coherent*, low-energy fabric across an organic MCM could win data-center sockets [9]. The 2022 UCIe 1.0 specification then standardized the full stack, and the August 2024 UCIe 2.0 release added a DFx manageability architecture and UCIe-3D for hybrid-bonded stacking with bump pitches down to 1 µm [1].

> **Theorem (Shoreline Budget):** For a 2.5D package with die edge length $L$ and PHY bandwidth density $\rho$ (Gb/s/mm), the maximum raw D2D bandwidth is $B_{max} = \rho L$. Any floorplan requiring bandwidth exceeding $B_{max}$ must either adopt a higher-density package (silicon interposer, bridge) or accept multiple hops.

### 2.3 Signaling Fundamentals

Die-to-die channels differ from board SerDes channels in that the *insertion loss is small* (a few dB) while *crosstalk between dense bumps* dominates. This favors many parallel wires at moderate per-wire rates (1–16 GT/s) over few wires at 112+ GT/s PAM-4. The energy of a source-synchronous parallel bus scales roughly linearly with wire count, whereas a DSP-based SerDes carries a fixed ~50–100 mW per-lane digital overhead — acceptable at 32 GT/s, ruinous at 2 GT/s.

## 3 Methodology

We structure the analysis as a comparative systems study with three methodological pillars:

1. **Specification archaeology.** We reconstruct the protocol and PHY architectures of UCIe 1.x/2.0, BoW (base/fast/turbo modes), AMD Infinity Fabric (GMI/G-link), and Intel AIB/EMIB from public specifications, press disclosures, and vendor white papers [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9].
2. **Analytic modeling.** We derive closed-form expressions for aggregate bandwidth, shoreline consumption, training latency, and coherence overhead as functions of lane count, clock rate, flit size, and snoop probability.
3. **Empirical triangulation.** We cross-check model predictions against disclosed product figures: EPYC's 42 GB/s bi-directional per-die links at 2 pJ/bit, BoW's 1.034 Tb/s/mm on organic substrate, and Ponte Vecchio's >2 TB/s of package connectivity [8][9][5].

### Analytical Framework

For a link with $N$ data wires, per-wire rate $R$ (GT/s), and encoding efficiency $\eta$:

$$\text{BW}_{raw} = N \cdot R \cdot \eta$$

For a coherent fabric with average flit size $F$ bytes and snoop rate $s$ per data transaction, the *effective* data bandwidth is:

$$\text{BW}_{eff} = \frac{\text{BW}_{raw}}{1 + \alpha s}, \quad \alpha \approx \frac{\text{snoop flit bytes}}{F}$$

This single expression explains why coherence-aware fabrics demand *latency*, not merely bandwidth: every snoop adds a serialization and round-trip delay on the critical path of a cache miss.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe: A Full-Stack Standard

UCIe is architecturally the most ambitious D2D standard because it standardizes *all three layers*: the **physical layer** (die-to-die adapter + PHY), the **protocol layer** (streaming, PCIe, CXL), and the **software model** (enumeration, management, compliance). Key design decisions:

- **Two package flavors.** The *standard package* uses 16–64 single-ended or differential lanes at up to 32 GT/s over organic substrates; the *advanced package* targets silicon interposers and bridges at 64+ lanes with tighter bump pitch.
- **Sideband + mainband.** A narrow sideband channel carries link-training, parameter exchange, and power management, while the wide mainband carries data flits. This separation means the PHY can train without corrupting in-flight protocol state.
- **Protocol multiplexing.** UCIe flits carry PCIe (CXL.io), CXL.mem, and CXL.cache transactions, so an accelerator chiplet can be *coherent* with host memory using the same ordering and flow-control machinery that CXL defines for board-level links [3].
- **Retimers and multi-level.** Like PCIe, UCIe supports retimers to extend reach, and UCIe 2.0's DFx architecture (UDA) adds a per-chiplet management fabric for test, telemetry, and debug across the SiP lifecycle [1].

The UCIe link-training sequence — from *Reset* through *SBINIT*, *MBINIT* (mainband init), *MBTRAIN*, and into *LinkUp* — mirrors PCIe LTSSM philosophy but is adapted to the realities of package bring-up: no hot-plug, firmware-driven calibration, and repair of weak lanes via redundancy.

### 4.2 BoW: Parallelism as Economy

The Bunch of Wires specification takes the opposite stance: *minimize complexity, maximize wires*. Each BoW *slice* is the atomic unit — 16 data wires with a source-synchronous clock — and slices stack into *links* spanning die edges [4][5].

The three interoperable modes form a backward-compatible ladder:

| Mode | Rate per wire | Slice BW | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| BoW-Base | 4 GT/s | 64 Gb/s | Unidirectional, un-terminated |
| BoW-Fast | 16 GT/s | 256 Gb/s | Bidirectional, terminated |
| BoW-Turbo | 16 GT/s (DDR-style) | 512 Gb/s | Double-data-rate, FEC optional |

BoW achieves **1.034 Tb/s/mm** bandwidth density on organic laminate and **5.12 Tb/s/mm** in advanced packaging, with energy below 0.5 pJ/bit (organic) and 0.25 pJ/bit (advanced), BER < 1e-15, reach up to 25 mm, and latency under 2 ns [4]. The engineering insight is that at these short reaches, a *forwarded clock* eliminates CDR power, and per-wire deskew calibration (performed over the shared open-drain mode bump) replaces adaptive equalization.

> **Theorem (Parallel Advantage):** For channel loss $L_{ch} < 6$ dB, the energy-optimal D2D interface uses $N \geq \frac{BW}{R_{max}}$ parallel wires at the maximum crosstalk-limited rate $R_{max}$, rather than fewer wires with DSP equalization, because forwarded-clock CMOS drivers dissipate $\approx CV^2f$ with no static CDR power.

### 4.3 Die-to-Die Link Training

Link training is the discipline of bringing a multi-thousand-wire interface from power-on to error-free operation *without human intervention*. The state machine must handle:

- **Bump and lane repair.** Manufacturing defects in microbumps are repaired by sparing: UCIe defines redundant lanes; BoW uses its AUX channel to map around dead wires.
- **Deskew and phase calibration.** Per-lane skew from package routing asymmetry is measured with training patterns and compensated with per-lane delay lines.
- **Voltage and timing margining.** The PHY sweeps driver swing and sampling phase to center the eye, then records margins for production telemetry.
- **Speed negotiation.** Partners exchange capability (BoW mode, UCIe speed) over sideband and converge on the highest mutually supported rate.

```systemverilog
// Simplified UCIe-style D2D link training FSM (illustrative)
typedef enum logic [3:0] {
  ST_RESET, ST_SBINIT, ST_MBINIT,
  ST_MBTRAIN, ST_LINKUP, ST_REPAIR, ST_L1, ST_L2
} ltssm_t;

always_ff @(posedge clk) begin
  case (state)
    ST_RESET:   if (pwr_good && sideband_ok) state <= ST_SBINIT;
    ST_SBINIT:  if (param_exch_done)         state <= ST_MBINIT;
    ST_MBINIT:  if (deskew_locked)           state <= ST_MBTRAIN;
    ST_MBTRAIN: if (ber < BER_TARGET)        state <= ST_LINKUP;
                else if (timeout)            state <= ST_REPAIR;
    ST_REPAIR:  if (spare_lane_mapped)       state <= ST_MBTRAIN;
    ST_LINKUP:  if (l1_req)                  state <= ST_L1;
    default: state <= ST_RESET;
  endcase
end
```

Training latency matters: a 47-chiplet package that trains links serially at 100 µs per link adds milliseconds to boot — acceptable for servers, painful for mobile. Parallel training with deterministic scheduling is an active area of firmware design.

### 4.4 Coherence Fabrics Across Chiplets

The deepest challenge is *memory coherence*: when two chiplets cache the same line, the fabric must enforce a single-writer/multiple-reader invariant with bounded latency. Two architectural patterns dominate:

1. **Centralized coherence (AMD EPYC).** Compute chiplets (CCDs) connect via GMI links to a central I/O die (IOD) that hosts memory controllers and acts as the coherence hub [7]. CCD-to-CCD traffic traverses the IOD; the fabric implements a directory/MOESI protocol with snoop filters. In EPYC 7001, each die had three Infinity Fabric links to peers at 10.6 Gb/s each (42 GB/s bi-directional), consuming ~2 pJ/bit [9]. The 4th-gen EPYC 8004 moves to 32 Gb/s GMI links on the IOD.
2. **Distributed/bridged coherence (Intel Ponte Vecchio).** Compute, cache ("Rambo"), HBM, and Xe-Link tiles connect over Co-EMIB bridges and Foveros stacks with >5 TB/s of memory-fabric bandwidth and >2 TB/s of tile connectivity [8]. Coherence is managed by distributed agents with a fabric that preserves ordering across 2.5D and 3D hops.

UCIe's contribution is to make coherence *composable*: CXL.cache and CXL.mem flits ride the same mainband as PCIe, so a third-party accelerator chiplet can join the host's coherence domain without a proprietary protocol [2][3]. The cost is protocol overhead — CXL flit headers, CRC, and credit-based flow control consume roughly 5–10% of raw bandwidth, and snoop latency is bounded by the *slowest* die in the domain.

> **Theorem (Coherence Latency Floor):** In a multi-die package with $h$ fabric hops of latency $\ell$ and snoop filter hit rate $p$, the expected added latency of a coherent read miss is $E[L] = 2h\ell + (1-p) \cdot L_{mem}$, independent of raw link bandwidth. Bandwidth buys throughput; only topology and filtering buy latency.

### 4.5 Packaging Substrates and the Physics of Density

The substrate sets the ceiling. Organic laminate offers ~10–25 µm line/space and low cost; silicon interposers and EMIB bridges offer ~2 µm features at higher cost; hybrid bonding (Foveros Direct, UCIe-3D) achieves sub-10 µm — down to 1 µm — bump pitch with face-to-face copper interconnects [1][8]. The bandwidth-density ladder observed in practice:

- Organic + BoW: ~1 Tb/s/mm [4]
- EMIB bridge + AIB: ~2–5 Tb/s/mm class (vendor disclosures) [6]
- Silicon interposer + UCIe advanced package: multi-Tb/s/mm
- Hybrid bonding (3D): 10+ Tb/s/mm, with energy approaching on-die wire cost

Each step up roughly doubles cost per mm² of interconnect, which is why *cost-optimized* chiplets (networking, automotive) remain on organic BoW while *performance-optimized* ones (AI accelerators) climb the ladder.

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

### 5.1 Worked Example: Shoreline Budget of a 4-CCD Server Package

Consider a package with four compute chiplets, each exposing 6 mm of die edge to a central I/O die. Using UCIe standard package at 16 GT/s with 64 lanes per link (raw 1 Tb/s per direction per link):

- Aggregate raw bandwidth: $4 \times 1\,\text{Tb/s} = 4\,\text{Tb/s} = 500\,\text{GB/s}$ per direction.
- Shoreline consumed: 4 links × 64 lanes × 45 µm pitch ≈ 11.5 mm of bump shoreline — feasible within a ~25×25 mm package.

Compare with BoW-Fast at 256 Gb/s per slice: achieving the same 4 Tb/s requires 16 slices, each ~0.4 mm² of PHY area at 130 µm pitch, consuming ~19 mm of die edge — illustrating the *density-versus-cost* trade: BoW needs more edge but simpler PHY.

### 5.2 Energy Accounting

Using disclosed figures: EPYC's Infinity Fabric at 2 pJ/bit [9] versus BoW's <0.5 pJ/bit organic [4]. For a 4 Tb/s coherent fabric at 50% utilization:

- Infinity Fabric class: $2\,\text{Tb/s} \times 2\,\text{pJ/bit} = 4\,\text{W}$
- BoW class: $2\,\text{Tb/s} \times 0.5\,\text{pJ/bit} = 1\,\text{W}$

The 3 W delta is die area that could otherwise be compute — a first-order term in data-center TCO.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Training Convergence

We claim the deskew-calibration loop in §4.3 converges. *Sketch:* let skew vector $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ be bounded by package asymmetry, $|\mathbf{s}|_\infty < S_{max}$. Each calibration iteration measures phase error with a training pattern of length $M$ bits; the estimator variance scales as $1/M$. The per-lane delay line has resolution $\Delta < S_{max}/K$ for $K$ taps. By choosing $M$ such that estimation error $< \Delta/2$ with probability $1-\epsilon$, the loop is a contraction mapping on the residual skew, converging geometrically in $O(\log(S_{max}/\Delta))$ iterations. Standard stochastic-approximation arguments complete the proof; the practical consequence is that *training time is dominated by pattern length, not iteration count*.

## 6 Limitations

1. **No universal winner.** UCIe standardizes interoperability but its SerDes-based PHY is overkill for cost-sensitive organic packages; BoW is cheap but carries no coherence protocol; proprietary fabrics lock customers into a vendor. System designers must still choose per product.
2. **Coherence does not scale for free.** Snoop broadcast cost grows with the square of caching agents; directory protocols add indirection latency. Packages beyond ~8 coherent chiplets need hierarchical coherence, an area with no open standard.
3. **Test and known-good-die.** A package of $N$ chiplets each with yield $y$ has package yield $y^N$ without repair; UCIe 2.0's DFx architecture is a framework, not a guarantee, and at-speed testing of thousands of D2D lanes remains expensive [1].
4. **Thermal coupling.** Stacking compute dies (3D) traps heat; the interconnect's energy budget is dwarfed by the thermal cost of removing watts through thinned silicon. Many "3D-ready" floorplans are thermally infeasible at full utilization.
5. **Security.** A compromised chiplet in a shared package can snoop sideband traffic; UCIe's threat model assumes trusted chiplets, and package-level attestation standards are nascent.

## 7 Conclusion

Chiplet interconnects have matured from vendor craft into an engineering discipline with open standards, quantitative trade-offs, and shipping proof points at 47-chiplets-per-package scale [8]. UCIe provides the *interoperability* substrate — a full stack from sideband training to CXL coherence — while BoW provides the *economic* substrate for disaggregation on commodity packaging, and proprietary fabrics continue to push the density frontier for flagship products.

The central lesson is architectural: **bandwidth is a shoreline problem, energy is a TCO problem, and coherence is a latency problem** — and no single standard optimizes all three. Future work lies in hierarchical coherence protocols for packages with dozens of agents, in UCIe-3D hybrid-bonded interfaces that approach on-die wire density [1], and in the test and security infrastructure that a true multi-vendor chiplet marketplace will demand. The monolith is dead; the package is the new die, and its fabric is the new bus.
