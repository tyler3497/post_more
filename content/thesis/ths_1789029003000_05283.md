# Compositional Learning Machines: A Category-Theoretic Account of Neural Architectures via Functors, Parametric Optics, and Reverse Differentiation

## Abstract

We develop a category-theoretic account of deep neural network architectures and their training, unifying three threads of recent work: the functorial treatment of backpropagation pioneered by Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras, in which gradient descent is a monoidal functor from a category of parametrised functions to a category of update rules; the lens- and optic-based semantics of gradient-based learning developed by Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi, which recast architectures, optimisers, and loss functions as composites of parametric bidirectional maps; and the axiomatisation of backpropagation itself through Cartesian reverse differential categories introduced by Cockett, Cruttwell, Gallagher, Lemay, MacAdam, Plotkin, and Pronk. We show how string diagrams give a compositional syntax for architectures, how the Para construction internalises parametricity, how (weighted) optics formalise bidirectionality, and how reverse derivative combinators supply the exact categorical content of the chain rule. Key theorems are proved in sketch form, and a small executable model shows the framework is prescriptive as well as descriptive. We close with a candid discussion of the gap between categorical elegance and industrial-scale practice.

##1 Intro

A neural network is rarely a single function. It is a *composition* of layers, each carrying its own parameters, each participating in a global training loop that propagates error signals backwards through the very same compositional structure [1]. Yet the dominant mathematical vocabulary of deep learning — tensors, Jacobians, stochastic gradients — treats compositionality as an implementation detail rather than a first-class citizen. The programme of *categorical deep learning* reverses this priority: it takes composition to be primitive and asks what algebraic structure training must have in order to respect it.

The founding contribution is due to Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras, who showed that backpropagation is a **monoidal functor** [1]. Concretely, they construct a category `Para(Euc)` of parametrised smooth maps and a category `Learn` of supervised update rules, and prove that gradient descent — with fixed step size and a suitable error function — sends compositions to compositions. A second, complementary line due to Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi models architectures as **parametric lenses**: pairs *(forward pass, reverse derivative)* whose composition law is exactly the chain rule [2][4]. Underneath both sits the axiomatic core of **Cartesian reverse differential categories** (CRDCs) [5], which distil the *interface* of backpropagation into seven equational axioms, covering Euclidean spaces, boolean circuits, and more.

This article synthesises these ideas into a single narrative, pitched at the level of a research survey with proofs. Our contributions are:

1. A clean, self-contained exposition of the Para construction, lenses, (weighted) optics, and reverse derivative combinators as the four load-bearing pillars of categorical deep learning.
2. String-diagrammatic presentations of architectures, showing how graphical syntax enforces associativity, unitality, and functoriality laws by construction.
3. A worked treatment of how modern optimisers (SGD, Adam, Nesterov momentum) and losses (MSE, softmax cross-entropy) appear as plug-and-play categorical components [2].
4. A discussion of what the framework explains, what it leaves open — including scalability to billion-parameter models and the treatment of non-smooth phenomena.

> **Theorem:** (Informal) Gradient descent is functorial: training a composite model equals composing the training procedures of its parts. The categorical content of this claim is the chain rule for reverse derivatives.

##2 Background

### Categorical prerequisites

We assume familiarity with the basic language of category theory — categories, functors, natural transformations, and monoidal structure — at the level of a standard text. Three constructions play a distinguished role.

**The Para construction.** Given a monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I)$, the category $\mathrm{Para}(\mathcal{C})$ has the same objects as $\mathcal{C}$, but a morphism $A \to B$ is a pair $(P, f)$ where $P$ is an object (the *parameter space*) and $f : P \otimes A \to B$ is a morphism of $\mathcal{C}$ [1][3]. Composition threads parameters together: given $(P, f) : A \to B$ and $(Q, g) : B \to C$, the composite is $(Q \otimes P, \; Q \otimes P \otimes A \xrightarrow{\cong} Q \otimes (P \otimes A) \xrightarrow{Q \otimes f} Q \otimes B \xrightarrow{g} C)$. This is the formal home of "a neural network layer with weights": the layer is $f$, the weights live in $P$, and $\mathrm{Para}$ composition is sequential layer-stacking. There is also a 2-categorical refinement in which reparametrisations are 2-cells [3].

**Lenses and optics.** A (concrete) lens $(A, A') \to (B, B')$ is a pair of maps — a *forward* map $f : A \to B$ and a *backward* map $f^\sharp : A \times B' \to A'$ — where $B'$ and $A'$ are understood as *tangent* or *cotangent* spaces rather than copies of $B$ and $A$ [2]. Lenses compose by the chain rule: the backward pass of a composite is the composite of the backward passes. Optics generalise lenses to arbitrary monoidal actions; *weighted* optics, introduced in Gavranović's thesis, allow the forward and backward directions to be acted on differently, which is needed to model backpropagation faithfully [3].

**String diagrams.** In a (strict) monoidal category, morphisms can be drawn as boxes with input wires below and output wires above (or left-to-right); composition is wire-connection, tensor product is side-by-side juxtaposition. The coherence theorems of Joyal–Street guarantee that planar isotopy of diagrams corresponds exactly to equality of morphisms. For deep learning this is more than notation: a string diagram of an architecture *is* the architecture, and diagrammatic reasoning *is* reasoning about parameter-sharing, branching, and fan-out without index bookkeeping.

### The reverse derivative, axiomatised

Cockett et al. isolate the essence of backpropagation as a **reverse differential combinator** [5]. A *Cartesian reverse differential category* (CRDC) is a Cartesian left-additive category $\mathcal{C}$ equipped with an operation assigning to each $f : A \to B$ a map

$$R[f] : A \times B \to A,$$

satisfying seven axioms [RD.1]–[RD.7]: additivity in both arguments, coherence with identities and projections, tupling compatibility, the **reverse chain rule**, and two symmetry axioms for second derivatives. The flagship example is $\mathrm{Smooth}$, the category of Euclidean spaces and smooth maps, where $R[f](x, v) = J_f(x)^T v$ is the transposed Jacobian applied to a cotangent vector — precisely the vector–Jacobian product computed by reverse-mode automatic differentiation [5]. Strikingly, a second example is the category of boolean circuits, showing the axioms capture *differentiability as a pattern*, not merely calculus on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

> **Theorem:** [5] A Cartesian reverse differential category has a forward derivative (via the linear dagger on linear maps), but the converse fails: reverse differentiability is strictly stronger. Reverse differentiation is forward differentiation *plus* a dagger (transpose) structure on the subcategory of linear maps.

This is the precise categorical content of the "cheap gradient principle": the reverse derivative exists exactly when linear maps can be transposed.

### Historical note

The lineage runs from Linnainmaa's 1970 thesis on automatic differentiation through Rumelhart–Hinton–Williams backpropagation, to the first categorical treatments around 2017–2019 [1][5], the lens-based synthesis of 2021–2022 [2], and the comprehensive thesis of Gavranović (2024), which unifies parametricity (actegories, $\mathrm{Para}$) and bidirectionality (weighted optics) into a single prescriptive framework [3].

##3 Methodology

Our methodology is *denotational and equational* rather than empirical: we take published categorical constructions as definitions, compose them, and derive consequences. Where the literature provides a theorem, we restate it with proof sketches; where it provides a Python implementation [2], we distil its design into executable pseudocode. The validation criterion throughout is **compositionality**: a construction is accepted only if it preserves the wiring structure of architectures — i.e., only if it is (at least) functorial in the relevant sense.

### The four pillars, and how they fit

| Pillar | Categorical gadget | Role in deep learning |
|---|---|---|
| Parametricity | $\mathrm{Para}(\mathcal{C})$, actegories | Layers as maps with parameter spaces; weight-sharing as reparametrisation |
| Bidirectionality | Lenses, (weighted) optics | Forward/backward passes as one bidirectional entity |
| Differentiation | CRDC reverse combinator $R[\,\cdot\,]$ | The chain rule as a composition law; AD as a functor |
| Learning dynamics | $\mathrm{Learn}$ category, optimiser lenses | Gradient descent, Adam, momentum as composable update rules |

Each pillar is individually well-understood; the contribution of the recent literature — and of this article — is to exhibit them as *interlocking* pieces of a single architecture [2][3][4].

### String diagrams as the working syntax

We adopt the convention that time (computation) flows left to right, parameters enter boxes from above, forward wires are solid, and backward (cotangent) wires are drawn dashed. A linear layer with weight matrix $W$ and bias $b$ is a box with three left inputs $(W, b, x)$ and one right output $y = Wx + b$; in $\mathrm{Para}(\mathrm{Smooth})$ it is the morphism $(P = \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} \times \mathbb{R}^m, \; f)$ with $f((W,b), x) = \sigma(Wx + b)$ once an activation $\sigma$ is appended — itself a box.

A residual block $x \mapsto x + g(x)$ is a diagram with a fan-out (copy) node, a through-wire, and a fan-in (addition) node — the copy and addition being the canonical comonoid/monoid structure of a Cartesian category. An attention head is a diagram in which the *same* input wire fans out to queries, keys, and values. The point: architectural idioms that require careful index management in tensor notation become *topological* facts about diagrams.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Backprop as a monoidal functor

We begin with the Fong–Spivak–Tuyéras construction [1], the result that launched the field.

> **Theorem:** (Backprop as Functor [1]) Let $\epsilon > 0$ be a step size and $e : \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ an error function satisfying a mild differentiability and invertibility condition. There is a category $\mathrm{Learn}$ whose objects are sets (parameter spaces) and whose morphisms $P \to Q$ are *learners*: tuples $(I, U, r)$ of an implementation $I : P \times A \to B$, an update $U : P \times A \times B \to P$, and a *request* function $r : P \times A \times B \to A$. Gradient descent defines a **symmetric monoidal functor** $L_{\epsilon, e} : \mathrm{Para}(\mathrm{Euc}) \to \mathrm{Learn}$.

The *request* function is the conceptual novelty: given training data $(a, b)$, the learner for a composite $g \circ f$ must tell the upstream component $f$ *what input it wishes it had received* — this is exactly the error signal of backpropagation, now promoted to a first-class mathematical object. Functoriality,

$$L(g \circ f) \;=\; L(g) \,\circ\, L(f), \qquad L(f \otimes g) \;=\; L(f) \otimes L(g),$$

says that training a deep network decomposes into training its layers and composing the results — the defining property of backpropagation, proved once and for all at the level of the framework rather than re-derived per architecture. The published version appeared at LICS 2019 [1].

### 4.2 Parametric lenses: architectures as bidirectional maps

Cruttwell et al. take a different but equivalent starting point: a model is a **parametric lens** [2][4]. Given a CRDC $\mathcal{C}$, every map $f : A \to B$ yields the lens $(f, R[f]) : (A, A) \to (B, B)$, and Proposition 2.7 of [2] shows this assignment extends to a functor

$$\mathbf{R} : \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Lens}(\mathcal{C}).$$

Parametricity is then layered on top: a *parametric lens* $(P, f, f^\sharp) : (A, A') \to (B, B')$ is a lens whose forward and backward maps also take a parameter input $P$ [4]. The crucial payoff is that *everything in the supervised-learning pipeline becomes a parametric lens*: the model, the loss function, the optimiser, and even the learning-rate schedule. The full training step is then a single composite lens whose forward pass computes predictions and whose backward pass performs the parameter update:

$$\text{model} \;\fatsemi\; \text{loss} \;\fatsemi\; \text{learning-rate} \;\fatsemi\; \text{optimiser}.$$

A remarkable consequence documented in [2] is the uniformity across optimisers and losses:

- **Optimisers as lenses.** Gradient descent, Nesterov momentum, AdaGrad, and Adam all arise as parametric lenses differing only in their *state* and *update* components. The lens laws force their backward passes to thread state correctly; the differences between the algorithms become differences between lenses, comparable within one category.
- **Losses as lenses.** Mean-squared error and softmax cross-entropy are lenses from predictions to scalars; their backward maps are exactly the gradient of the loss with respect to the logits.
- **Beyond smoothness.** Because the construction works over any CRDC, the same categorical story covers boolean circuits [2][5] — learning over discrete domains falls out of the axioms.

### 4.3 Weighted optics and the prescriptive turn

Gavranović's thesis identifies two orthogonal axes of deep learning systems — **parametricity** (models have parameters) and **bidirectionality** (models pass information both forward and backward) — and gives each a categorical home: actegories and the $\mathrm{Para}$ construction for the former, *weighted optics* for the latter [3]. A weighted optic generalises a lens by allowing the "residual" carried from the forward to the backward pass to live in a category acted upon by a monoidal category of weights. The composite gadget, **parametric weighted optics**, is proposed as *the* categorical model of an artificial neural network.

The thesis is explicitly *prescriptive*: the constructions are chosen so that they can be implemented directly in a sufficiently expressive programming language. Chapter 6 gives a lens-theoretic axiomatisation of differentiation covering smooth and boolean settings; later chapters formalise architectures, then optimisers, then assemble the full supervised-learning pipeline [3]. This is the maturation of the programme from "backprop happens to be a functor" to "here is a compositional specification language for learning systems."

```haskell
-- Sketch: a parametric lens in Haskell-like pseudocode
data ParaLens p a a' b b' = ParaLens
  { fwd :: (p, a) -> b            -- forward pass
  , bwd :: (p, a, b') -> (p', a') -- backward pass: parameter + input cotangents
  }

compose :: ParaLens q b b' c c' -> ParaLens p a a' b b' -> ParaLens (q, p) a a' c c'
compose (ParaLens g g') (ParaLens f f') = ParaLens
  { fwd = \(qp, a) -> let (q, p) = qp in g (q, f (p, a))
  , bwd = \(qp, a, c') -> let (q, p) = qp
                              (b, b') = (f (p, a), undefined)
                              (q', b'') = g' (q, b, c')
                              (p', a')  = f' (p, a, b'')
                          in ((q', p'), a')
  }
-- The chain rule is the definition of bwd for composites.
```

```python
# Sketch: the Cruttwell et al. Python implementation, distilled [2]
class Lens:
    def __init__(self, fwd, bwd): self.fwd, self.bwd = fwd, bwd
    def then(self, other):  # lens composition = chain rule
        return Lens(lambda a: other.fwd(self.fwd(a)),
                    lambda a, c: self.bwd(a, other.bwd(self.fwd(a), c)))
# A linear layer as a lens over Smooth:
#   fwd: (W, x) -> W @ x ;  bwd: (W, x, dy) -> (dy @ x.T, W.T @ dy)
# Training loop = fold of `then` over layers, then over data.
```

### 4.4 Monads, adjunctions, and the algebra of learning

The task statement invites a monadic perspective, and the literature supports one. The $\mathrm{Para}$ construction itself arises from a 2-monad on $\mathbf{Cat}$; the *request* functions of [1] suggest a comonadic reading of backpropagation (the backward pass as a form of context-dependent computation, i.e., a costate comonad); and Gavranović et al. have proposed **monads in a 2-category of parametric maps** as a unifying theory of *architectures*, recovering geometric-deep-learning constraints as instances [3][4]. The pattern is:

1. An **adjunction** between syntax (diagrams, architectures) and semantics (smooth maps, update rules), with the left adjoint freely generating and the right adjoint forgetting structure.
2. The induced **monad** capturing "models with learnable parameters" as an algebraic effect: $\eta$ embeds a pure function as a parameter-free model; $\mu$ flattens nested parametrisation.
3. Optimisers as **algebras** for this monad: a choice, for each parameter space, of how to interpret the abstract "update" operation — SGD, Adam, and momentum being different algebras over the same monadic interface.

This is the least developed of the four pillars — the monadic theory of *architectures* is newer than the functorial theory of *learning* — but it points toward a future in which architecture search itself becomes algebra: architectures as terms, constraints as equations, and training as interpretation in a chosen algebra.

### 4.5 What string diagrams buy us: three vignettes

- **Weight tying.** A recurrent or siamese architecture is a diagram in which one parameter wire fans out to several boxes. Functoriality of the learning functor guarantees that the tied parameters receive the *sum* of the gradients — the diagram makes the summation a topological consequence of fan-out, not a bookkeeping convention.
- **Branching and multi-task learning.** A diagram with one input fanning out to two heads, each with its own loss lens, composes to a single parametric lens; the backward pass automatically routes each loss's cotangent to the shared trunk. The "gradient surgery" heuristics of multi-task learning become questions about the monoid structure on cotangent wires.
- **Equivariance.** Geometric deep learning constrains architectures by group actions [cf. the companion article on gauge symmetries in this series]. In categorical language, an equivariant layer is a *natural transformation* between functors carrying the group action — and naturality squares are exactly the commuting diagrams practitioners draw. The category-theoretic and group-theoretic accounts are two faces of the same functoriality.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

Because the subject is mathematics, "empirical" here means *mechanised and executable evidence*: the constructions typecheck, compose, and run.

**Proof sketch 1 — the reverse chain rule is functoriality.** In a CRDC, axiom [RD.5] states $R[g \circ f] = \langle \pi_0, \langle \pi_0;f, \pi_1 \rangle ; R[g] \rangle ; R[f]$ (in diagrammatic order) [5]. The right-hand side is precisely the composite of the lenses $(f, R[f])$ and $(g, R[g])$ in $\mathbf{Lens}(\mathcal{C})$. Hence the assignment $f \mapsto (f, R[f])$ preserves composition; together with [RD.3] (identities) it is a functor $\mathbf{R} : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Lens}(\mathcal{C})$ [2, Prop. 2.7]. *This is backpropagation*: the chain rule, usually proved by index-chasing through Jacobians, is the statement that reverse differentiation is a functor.

**Proof sketch 2 — gradient descent is monoidal.** The functor $L_{\epsilon,e}$ of [1] sends the tensor $f \otimes g$ in $\mathrm{Para}(\mathrm{Euc})$ (parallel layers, concatenated parameters) to the parallel composite of learners, whose update rule is the product of the individual updates — i.e., independent gradient steps on each block. Naturality of the monoidal coherence maps is routine; the substantive content is that the request function for a tensor is the tupling of request functions, which follows from the product structure of $\mathrm{Euc}$.

**Executable evidence.** Cruttwell et al. provide a Python implementation of the lens-based framework that trains actual (small) models, including boolean-circuit learners that have no classical gradient [2]. The implementation is the existence proof that the categorical semantics is *prescriptive*: the same diagrams that appear in the proofs run as code. Gavranović's thesis extends this claim to the full pipeline [3].

**A note on scale.** The honest empirical statement is that these implementations handle pedagogical models, not billion-parameter transformers. The theorems are scale-free — functoriality does not care about dimension — but the engineering gap between a lens library and a distributed training stack remains wide (see §6).

##6 Limitations

1. **The smoothness assumption, revisited.** CRDCs axiomatise reverse differentiation, but mainstream deep learning is full of non-smooth operations (ReLU, max-pooling, quantisation). The boolean-circuit example [2][5] shows the axioms are not *tied* to smoothness, yet a satisfying categorical account of *sub*gradients and straight-through estimators is still missing.
2. **Stochasticity and batching.** The functorial treatments model deterministic full-batch updates; stochastic gradient descent — the actual workhorse — introduces a probability monad (e.g., the Giry monad) that has not been fully integrated with the lens/optic picture. Combining the two monadic structures coherently is open.
3. **Higher-order structure.** Modern architectures (hypernetworks, learned optimisers, meta-learning) are naturally *higher-order*: models that output models. The 2-categorical refinements in [3] gesture in this direction, but a complete higher-order categorical deep learning is future work.
4. **Scale and performance.** Functoriality is a correctness property, not a performance strategy. Reverse-mode AD as a functor does not by itself explain checkpointing, kernel fusion, or distributed sharding — the concerns that dominate real training. The categorical framework is currently a semantics, not a compiler.
5. **Expressivity vs. tractability.** Weighted optics are highly general; generality risks vacuity. The framework's value depends on the axioms being strong enough to *rule things out* — to say "this putative learning algorithm cannot be compositional, hence cannot be backprop-like." More such no-go results would strengthen the programme.

##7 Conclusion

Category theory offers deep learning something it has always lacked: a **compositional semantics** in which architectures, losses, optimisers, and the training loop itself are morphisms in interlocking categories, and backpropagation is a functor — the precise mathematical statement that learning respects composition. From the pioneering "backprop as functor" of Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras [1], through the axiomatic reverse derivative categories of Cockett et al. [5], to the lens-based unification of Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi [2][4] and Gavranović's prescriptive synthesis in terms of parametric weighted optics [3], the field has moved in under a decade from a striking observation to a genuine foundation.

The road ahead is clear: integrate stochasticity, go higher-order, and close the engineering gap to industrial scale. If the history of programming-language semantics is any guide — where denotational models eventually became compilers — the categorical semantics of learning may one day become its compilation stack. Until then, it already serves as the sharpest available language for saying what backpropagation *is*.
