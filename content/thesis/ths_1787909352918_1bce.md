# Silicon-Photonic Interconnect Collective Communication: Microring Resonator Wavelength Division Multiplexing for AllReduce, DPU-Offloaded In-Network Hierarchies, and Topology-Aware Rail-Optimized Rings on DGX GH200

## Abstract
We analyze silicon-photonic interconnects for collective communication, focusing on microring resonator (MRR) wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), optical circuit switching, and topology-aware AllReduce on DGX GH200. MRRs enable wavelength-selective routing with 8–32 channels per waveguide, 512 Gb/s per fiber demonstrated via Kerr combs [5], and thermal tuning challenges of ~0.09 nm/°C. Circuit-switched photonics reduces multi-hop electrical losses but incurs reconfiguration latency (e.g., 200 µs) that must be overlapped with communication [7]. We frame AllReduce ring, tree, and hierarchical algorithms under rail-optimized fabrics where each rail aligns 32 nodes one hop away [4]. NVLink-C2C provides 900 GB/s coherent CPU-GPU bandwidth, 7× PCIe Gen5 [4]. In-network aggregation via DPUs/SmartNICs or switch-centric ISAs can offload reduction, but requires careful deadlock-free scheduling, wavelength-contention avoidance, and loss/power budgeting. We formalize reduction associativity, schedule feasibility, and failure fallback without claiming new experiments.

## 1 Introduction

Photonic interconnects promise to decouple compute scaling from copper's distance and energy limits, enabling 70% interconnect power reduction and 10× bandwidth density in recent analyses. This thesis synthesizes silicon-photonic WDM with collective communication for LLM training.

> **Central thesis:** Microring-based WDM and circuit-switched optics can accelerate AllReduce only if wavelength assignment, thermal stability, and reconfiguration scheduling respect loss budgets and rail-aligned topology.

The shift from pluggable transceivers to co-packaged optics (CPO) and photonic interposers places modulation/detection inside GPU/switch packages, removing DSP overhead [2][5]. GH200 exemplifies electrical scale-up via NVLink Switch System combining 256 Grace Hopper superchips into 1 exaflop, 144 TB shared memory with 900 GB/s NVLink-C2C per superchip [4].

Collective communication remains bottleneck: synchronous AllReduce waits for slowest participant. Optical degree constraints limit static topologies, while naive per-step reconfiguration incurs $|\\Pi| \\times T_{recfg}$ cumulative penalty [7]. SWOT-style overlap partially reconfigures circuits during transmission to hide 200 µs switching.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Microring Resonators and WDM

Microring resonator filters tune >20 nm for 8 WDM channels at 200 GHz spacing, with off-resonant pass-through loss critical [photonic switch ref]. Index contrast enables 10×10 µm rings vs 100×100 µm pads, pad-limited switch area. Waveguide crossings use multimode interference to reduce crosstalk.

Kerr frequency combs generate 32 wavelengths per fiber, 16 Gb/s per wavelength → 512 Gb/s aggregate, BER < $10^{-12}$ [5]. Architecture scales to >100 channels, bandwidth density 1.4 Tb/s/mm² reported for 3D interposers [2]. Thermal shift 0.09 nm/°C requires active locking; heaters consume ~30 mW per ring, motivating wavelength-locking monitors.

Insertion loss budget: grating coupler 2–3 dB, MRR drop 0.5–1 dB per stage, waveguide crossing 0.1 dB, propagation 2 dB/cm. Total path loss must stay <10–12 dB for link closure with external laser (ELSFP) at 5 pJ/bit [2][5].

### 2.2 DGX GH200 and Rail Optimization

GH200 Superchip combines 72-core Grace CPU (480 GB LPDDR5) + H100 GPU (96 GB HBM3) via NVLink-C2C 900 GB/s, 7× PCIe Gen5 bandwidth at 1/5 power [4]. NVLink Switch System forms two-level non-blocking fat-tree, every GPU accesses peer memory at 900 GB/s. 256 superchips provide 144 TB shared, 18k cores.

Rail-aligned fabric: each group of 32 nodes rail-aligned, traffic per rail one hop away from other 31 nodes in scalable unit (SU), inter-rail via spine [4][DGX SuperPOD docs]. Rail optimization maps collective's ring dimension to physical rail to avoid spine contention. This contrasts with torus or dragonfly where all-to-all stresses different phases.

## 3 Methodology

We follow **device → link → fabric → collective → fallback**.

1. **Device model:** MRR resonance $\lambda_{res}=n_{eff} L / m$, tunable via heater $\Delta\lambda \approx 0.09 \cdot \Delta T$ nm. Lorentzian drop response, 3 dB bandwidth 250 GHz (2 nm) for 400 GHz channel spacing to keep crosstalk < -25 dB [5].
2. **Link budget:** $P_{rx}=P_{tx}-IL_{gc}-N_{mrr}IL_{mrr}-N_{cross}IL_{cross}-\alpha L$. Require $P_{rx}>$ sensitivity + margin. E.g., $P_{tx}=0$ dBm, losses 8 dB → -8 dBm Rx, needs APD or high-resp Ge PD.
3. **Fabric construction:** Wavelength-selective switch (WSS) with $N$ inputs, $N$ outputs, $M$ wavelengths per input, $L$ MRRs per crosspoint. Rearrangeably non-blocking, reconfigured at slot start via centralized arbitration for contention resolution [MRR switch].
4. **Collective mapping:** AllReduce algorithms (ring, tree, hierarchical) expressed as sequence $\Pi$ of communication steps, each demanding topology $C_i$. Joint optimization of $\{C_i\}$ and flow schedule avoids enumerating combinatorial space by expressing topologies as sum of adjacency matrices and powers [7].
5. **In-network offload:** DPU/SmartNIC or switch-centric ISA performs reduction. SCIN proposes in-switch accelerator directly accessing attached accelerator memory via co-designed fabric to reduce unnecessary bounce-back to initiating GPU [6][SCIN].
6. **Reconfiguration overlap:** SWOT overlaps partial circuit updates with data transmission, reducing completion time 20% for 8-node AllReduce with 400 Gb/s links, 200 µs $T_{recfg}$ [7].

> **Theorem 3.1 (Reduction Associativity).** *If operator $\oplus$ associative and commutative, hierarchical reduction yields same result as ring regardless of partition.* Proof by induction over reduction tree, standard for AllReduce.

// Microring wavelength assignment - simplified arbitration
struct Wavelength { channel: usize, lambda_nm: f64 }
struct MRR { tuned_lambda: f64, loss_db: f64, heater_mw: f64 }
fn assign_wavelength(requests: &[Request], available: &[Wavelength]) -> Option<Vec<(RequestId, Wavelength)>> {
    // centralized arbitration resolves contention: at most one flow per (port,wavelength)
    let mut used = std::collections::HashSet::new();
    let mut mapping = Vec::new();
    for req in requests {
        let w = available.iter().find(|w| !used.contains(&(req.dst_port, w.channel)))?;
        if used.insert((req.dst_port, w.channel)) { mapping.push((req.id, w.clone())); }
    }
    Some(mapping)
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics

State $S=(L, F, C, R)$ where $L$ photonic link state (tuned wavelengths, temperature), $F$ flows, $C$ circuit configuration, $R$ reduction progress.

Costs:

- **Insertion loss:** $IL_{total}=IL_{gc}+N_{mrr} \cdot 0.7 + N_{cross}\cdot0.1 + 2L_{cm}\cdot 2$. Must <12 dB for direct detection.
- **Power:** Laser 100 mW + heaters $L \cdot 30$ mW + modulator driver. CPO reduces from pluggable 5 pJ/bit to 5 pJ/bit still? Actually Ayar TeraPHY Gen3 claims 5 pJ/bit [2].
- **Reconfiguration:** $T_{recfg}=200$ µs typical MEMS OCS, 43.4 ns demonstrated for nanosecond switch with 12.4 ns label processing + 6 ns driver [nanosecond switch].
- **Thermal:** $\Delta\lambda=0.09 nm/C$ × 85°C range = 7.65 nm shift, exceeds channel spacing → requires active locking.

WDM contention constraint: for $L=1$, switch behaves like AWGR; for $L>1$, multiple wavelengths share ports → need contention resolution via centralized arbiter at slot start [MRR switch].

Rail-aligned optimization maps collective dimension to rail: each GPU's NVLink domain (8 GPUs) handles intra-node reduction via NVLink 900 GB/s, then inter-node via rail-aligned InfiniBand 400 Gb/s per rail, avoiding spine oversubscription. GH200's NVLink Switch System extends this to 256 GPUs with 900 GB/s all-to-all [4].

### 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation

**WDM routing:** 32-channel Kerr comb provides independent information channels on same fiber [5]. Each transmitter can modulate any wavelength, but receiver must filter via MRR. Wavelength assignment problem: bipartite matching between flows and wavelengths per port, NP-hard with additional crosstalk constraints, solved heuristically via greedy + backtracking.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Wavelength-Contention Freedom).** *If arbiter ensures at most one flow per (output_port, wavelength) per slot, no wavelength collision occurs.* Proof by construction of arbitration table [MRR switch].

**AllReduce algorithms:**

- **Ring:** $2(n-1)$ steps, bandwidth-optimal for large messages, latency $2(n-1)\alpha$. Rail-optimized ring places neighbors on same rail to keep one hop.
- **Tree (binary):** $2\log n$ steps, latency-optimal for small messages, but requires $2×$ bandwidth.
- **Hierarchical:** intra-rail reduce-scatter (NVLink), inter-rail all-reduce (photonic), intra-rail all-gather. Reduces inter-rail volume by factor of rail size (32).
- **In-network:** DPU or switch performs reduction: data stays in network, not bounced to GPU. NVLink SHARP equivalent NVLS relies on GPU load to trigger reduction, causing unnecessary back-transfer; SCIN ISA directly accesses accelerator memory, enabling true in-switch reduction and in-network quantization (INQ) to 8-bit [SCIN].

**Optical circuit switching:** Static topology limited by optical degree (few ports). Naive intra-collective reconfiguration (Strawman-ICR) switches at each algorithmic step, maximizing spatial efficiency but incurring $|\Pi| \times T_{recfg}$ stop-and-wait. SWOT overlaps partial reconfigurations with transmissions, modeling sequence as sum of adjacency matrices $A_i$ and powers $A_i^k$ to bound completion time [7]. Near-optimal sequences constructed with symmetric expanders.

> **Lemma 4.2 (Schedule Deadlock Freedom).** *If collective's dependency graph is acyclic and wavelength assignment respects port ordering, photonic schedule is deadlock-free.* Proof by topological order of DAG, no circular wait on wavelengths.

**DPU offload:** BlueField-3 DPU provides 16 Arm A78 cores, line-rate steering via DOCA Flow, hardware acceleration for bypass flows. In-network AllReduce can use DPU as aggregation point, freeing host CPU. However, DPU memory bandwidth (DDR5) limits reduction size; hierarchical approach aggregates first in DPU SRAM.

```python
# SWOT overlap intuition - from [7]
def swot_overlap(collective_steps, T_recfg=200e-6, link_bw=400e9):
    total = 0
    for i, step in enumerate(collective_steps):
        transmit_time = step.bytes*8 / link_bw
        if i+1 < len(collective_steps) and needs_different_topology(step, collective_steps[i+1]):
            # start reconfig during tail of transmission
            total += max(transmit_time, T_recfg) * 0.8  # 20% saving via partial overlap
        else:
            total += transmit_time
    return total
```

### 4.3 Composition, Pipelining, and Runtime Interaction

Pipeline: $Tensor \rightarrow Shard \rightarrow WDMAssign \rightarrow OCSConfig \rightarrow Transmit \rightarrow Reduce \rightarrow Gather$.

- **Batching:** Tensors fused to amortize $T_{recfg}$. Fused computation-collective overlaps GPU kernel execution with networking via GPU-initiated networking, hiding latency without extra kernel launches [6].
- **Credit flow:** Each rail credits $Q_{max}=4096$ packets to prevent buffer overflow in photonic switch electrical buffers.
- **Backpressure:** Electrical buffering at ToR holds packets during OCS reconfiguration. NACK triggers retransmission, but nanosecond switch achieves 43.4 ns control loop: 12.4 ns label + 3 ns driver + 6 ns rise/fall + 16 ns margin [nanosecond switch].
- **Thermal control loop:** MRR heaters adjusted via PID using on-chip monitors, wavelength-locking scheme demonstrated [MRR switch]. Control loop runs every 1 ms, heater power tracked for power budget.

Topology-aware rail optimization: GH200's rail-aligned fabric ensures that AllReduce ring's $k$ neighbors are within same SU, one hop away. Inter-SU traffic traverses spine, but hierarchical reduction minimizes it: intra-SU reduce-scatter reduces data by $32×$ before spine crossing [4].

Failure and fallback:

- **Link failure:** Optical path loss exceeds budget → fallback to electrical InfiniBand rail. Circuit-switched fabric maintains millisecond-level failover with hot-standby GPU substitution [dOCS].
- **Wavelength drift:** MRR detuned → packets dropped, NACK, retransmit on alternate wavelength. Wavelength monitor triggers re-lock.
- **DPU failure:** In-network aggregation falls back to host-based ring. SCIN ISA direct memory access fails safe to GPU-initiated path.

Composition with NVLink-C2C: Grace CPU memory 480 GB LPDDR5 at 1/8 power per GB vs DDR5, coherent via ATS, enables GPU oversubscription of CPU memory. AllReduce can stage shards in CPU memory to reduce HBM pressure, accessing at 900 GB/s [4].

### 4.4 Resource Accounting and Loss/Power Budgeting

Quantitative model without claiming new experiments:

- **Bandwidth:** 32 λ × 16 Gb/s = 512 Gb/s per fiber [5]; 100+ λ scalable → >1 Tb/s. Photonic interposer 114 Tbps aggregate, 1.4 Tb/s/mm² [2].
- **Energy:** Pluggable 30 pJ/bit → CPO 5 pJ/bit [2]. Laser 100 mW / 512 Gb/s = 0.2 pJ/bit dominant.
- **Latency:** OCS reconfiguration 200 µs (MEMS) vs 43.4 ns (nanosecond switch). For 8-node AllReduce with 10 MB shards, transmission 200 µs per step → reconfiguration overhead 100% if naive, 20% saving via SWOT [7].
- **Loss budget example:** Tx 0 dBm, GC 2.5 dB ×2 =5 dB, MRR 0.7 dB ×4 =2.8 dB, crossings 0.1×10=1 dB, waveguide 2 dB/cm×0.5 cm=1 dB → total 9.8 dB, Rx -9.8 dBm, needs APD sensitivity -14 dBm → margin 4.2 dB feasible.

> **Lemma 4.3 (Loss/Power-Budget Feasibility).** *A photonic path is feasible iff $P_{tx} - IL_{total} \ge P_{sens}+M$.* Where $M$ margin 3 dB for thermal drift and aging.

> **Lemma 4.4 (Reduction Associativity).** *Hierarchical AllReduce result equals flat ring if operator associative.* Proof by tree reduction equivalence.

Operational considerations: panel-scale photonic interconnects on glass substrate enable unlimited tiling, all-directional communication without active repeaters, 7 Tbps+ aggregate [3]. This enables supernode abstraction where network becomes schedulable resource, exposing ring/mesh/dragonfly topologies per workload phase [dOCS].

## 6 Limitations

- **Thermal sensitivity:** MRR resonance drifts 0.09 nm/°C, 85°C range exceeds channel spacing. Active locking required, heater power overhead not negligible at scale.
- **Reconfiguration latency:** 200 µs MEMS OCS dominates small-message AllReduce (few µs transmission). Nanosecond switches exist but limited port count and higher loss.
- **Loss budget:** Large fanout WSS with many MRR stages exceeds 12 dB, requiring amplification or APD, increasing power.
- **Control plane:** Centralized arbitration for contention resolution becomes bottleneck at >1000 GPUs, needs distributed scheme.
- **DPU memory:** BlueField DDR bandwidth ~100 GB/s vs HBM 3 TB/s, limits in-network reduction size; hierarchical staging needed.
- **Standardization:** ELSFP external laser source serviceability, CPO assembly yield, and UCIe optical mapping still evolving [2].
- **Failure handling:** Hot-standby GPU failover millisecond-level [dOCS], but optical path verification not instantaneous; fallback to electrical incurs performance hit.

Future: glass substrate photonic interposer for panel-scale tiling [3], Kerr comb scaling to >100 λ [5], switch-centric ISA for direct accelerator memory access enabling INQ [SCIN], fused computation-collective to hide latency [6], and near-optimal topology sequence construction avoiding combinatorial enumeration [7].

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected silicon-photonic collective communication: microring WDM provides 512 Gb/s per fiber via 32 λ [5], scaling toward 100+ λ and 1.4 Tb/s/mm² interposer [2]; circuit switching enables topology control (ring/mesh/dragonfly) per workload phase but requires overlapping 200 µs reconfiguration with transmission to remain practical [7]; GH200's NVLink-C2C 900 GB/s and rail-aligned 32-node SU [4] constrain AllReduce mapping to preserve one-hop locality; in-network aggregation via DPU or switch-centric ISA [SCIN][6] reduces bounce traffic but must respect associativity, deadlock freedom, wavelength contention, and loss/power feasibility. Panel-scale glass photonics offers path to unlimited tiling and 7 Tbps aggregate without repeaters [3]. No single layer suffices; end-to-end feasibility demands joint optimization of device tuning, link budget, fabric arbitration, collective algorithm, and failure fallback.

Contributions: WDM contention model with centralized arbitration, loss/power budget feasibility condition, rail-optimized hierarchical AllReduce mapping, reconfiguration overlap analysis via adjacency matrix powers [7], and literature-grounded quantification without new experiments.

*Takeaway:* Photonics moves bottleneck from bandwidth to control: wavelength assignment and reconfiguration scheduling determine AllReduce performance more than raw line rate; rail-aligned electrical topology remains essential for locality.
