# Homological Mirror Symmetry: Fukaya Categories and Matrix Factorizations

## Abstract

Homological mirror symmetry (HMS), conjectured by Kontsevich in 1994, recasts the duality between mirror Calabi-Yau manifolds as an equivalence of triangulated categories: the derived Fukaya category of Lagrangian branes on a symplectic manifold X and the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on its mirror X^\vee. In Landau-Ginzburg mirror symmetry, the B-side is refined to the category of matrix factorizations of a superpotential W, a Z/2-graded dg category equivalent by Orlov's theorem to the triangulated category of singularities of the singular fiber. This article surveys the categorical foundations of both sides — the A_\infty-structure of the Fukaya category, Eisenbud's matrix factorizations, and the derived categories of the second kind — then examines the explicit bridges between them: the localized mirror functor, Orlov's equivalences, and comparison of categorical invariants such as Hochschild homology via the Kapustin-Li formula. We review the proven cases — elliptic curves, the quintic threefold, punctured surfaces, and orbifold projective lines — and identify the analytical and algebraic obstructions that keep the general conjecture open.

##1 Introduction

The statement of mirror symmetry familiar to physicists — two distinct string compactifications on Calabi-Yau manifolds X and X^\vee producing the same physical observables — is astonishing as a claim about *numbers*. Closed-string mirror symmetry predicts that the Yukawa couplings (trilinear integrals over X^\vee) coincide with generating functions of genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of X. That two elements of an uncountable set, formal power series with integral coefficients, should agree is the kind of coincidence that begs for a structural explanation [2].

Kontsevich's 1994 ICM proposal provided that explanation by lifting the numerical predictions to an equivalence of categories. The *objects* are D-branes, the morphisms are open strings, and the mirror map becomes an A_\infty-quasi-equivalence between:

- the **A-side**: the derived Fukaya category DFuk(X), whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with flat vector bundles (brane data), and whose morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups; and
- the **B-side**: the bounded derived category D^b Coh(X^\vee) of coherent sheaves on the mirror.

Kontsevich's slogan was that HMS is *"less of a coincidence"* than numerical mirror symmetry: it asserts the coincidence of connected components in the (informal) moduli space of triangulated categories — a countable set — rather than of arbitrary power series [2].

The modern formulation, however, is substantially richer. Mirror symmetry in its Landau-Ginzburg incarnation replaces the B-side by a category of **matrix factorizations** of a superpotential W: pairs of maps between projective modules whose composition is multiplication by W. By Orlov's theorem, this is the same as the *triangulated category of singularities* of the zero fiber of W [1]. Meanwhile the A-side has grown from the original Fukaya category to include wrapped and partially wrapped variants, Fukaya-Seidel categories of Lefschetz fibrations, and topological models. This article explains how these two sides are built, how they are compared, and where the proofs actually stand.

##2 Background

### 2.1 The Fukaya category

Let (M, ω) be a symplectic manifold of real dimension 2n. An **object** of the Fukaya category Fuk(M) is a *Lagrangian brane*: a Lagrangian submanifold L (of middle dimension n, so ω vanishes on TL) satisfying additional technical conditions — orientability, a grading (a lift of the Lagrangian Grassmannian map to a fixed cover), and a spin structure — together with a flat unitary vector bundle E → L, the Chan-Paton data. For Calabi-Yau manifolds, c_1(TM) = 0, and the grading makes homological degrees well defined.

For two transversely intersecting branes L_0, L_1, the **morphism space** is the Floer cochain complex CF*(L_0, L_1), generated over a Novikov coefficient ring by the intersection points L_0 ∩ L_1. The differential μ^1 counts pseudoholomorphic strips (bigons) with boundary on the two Lagrangians:

> Theorem: The Floer differential squares to zero, μ^1 ∘ μ^1 = 0, whenever the Lagrangians satisfy suitable unobstructedness hypotheses; its cohomology HF*(L_0, L_1) is the morphism space in the homotopy category H(Fuk(M)).

The **composition maps** μ^d : CF*(L_{d-1}, L_d) ⊗ ... ⊗ CF*(L_0, L_1) → CF*(L_0, L_d)[2−d] count rigid pseudoholomorphic disks with d+1 boundary marked points, mapped cyclically onto the Lagrangians. These satisfy the A_\infty-relations:

```
μ^1(μ^1(x)) = 0
μ^1(μ^2(x,y)) = ± μ^2(μ^1(x),y) ± μ^2(x,μ^1(y))
μ^2(μ^2(x,y),z) − μ^2(x,μ^2(y,z)) = ± μ^3-bracket terms ± μ^1(...)
```

so Fuk(M) is an **A_\infty-category**: associativity of composition holds only up to coherent homotopy, with higher corrections governed by moduli spaces of disks. The derived Fukaya category DFuk(M) is obtained by passing to twisted complexes and taking the split-closure (Karoubi completion), making it a triangulated category. For elliptic curves this construction was worked out in complete detail, establishing the first full proof of HMS: the Fukaya category of a symplectic two-torus is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror elliptic curve, with intersection numbers matching Ext degrees via theta-function identities [5].

### 2.2 Landau-Ginzburg models and matrix factorizations

A **Landau-Ginzburg model** is a pair (X, W) where X is a (possibly non-compact) complex manifold and W: X → C is a holomorphic function, the *superpotential*. In the physics of N = (2,2) theories, B-branes in the LG model are described algebraically. Eisenbud's classical construction assigns to such a model its **matrix factorizations**:

> Definition: A matrix factorization of W over a ring B is a pair of finitely generated projective B-modules E_0, E_1 together with B-linear maps p_0: E_0 → E_1 and p_1: E_1 → E_0 such that p_1 ∘ p_0 = W · id_{E_0} and p_0 ∘ p_1 = W · id_{E_1}.

The name is justified: when B is a regular local ring and W is a non-zero-divisor, p_0 p_1 is the scalar matrix W · I, literally a factorization of the potential into matrices. Matrix factorizations organize into a Z/2-graded dg category MF(B, W), with even morphisms given by pairs of maps preserving the decomposition and odd morphisms by pairs swapping it, and a differential δ(χ) = q ∘ χ − (−1)^{|χ|} χ ∘ p.

The deep significance of MF(W) is **Orlov's theorem** [1]: the homotopy category of matrix factorizations is equivalent to the *triangulated category of singularities* D^b_sg(X_0) = D^b Coh(X_0) / Perf(X_0) of the zero fiber X_0 = W^{−1}(0), the quotient of the bounded derived category by perfect complexes. Since perfect complexes encode everything "smooth," the quotient measures precisely the singularity of the fiber. The graded (equivariant) version relates D-branes of type B in graded LG models to derived categories of coherent sheaves on the projective hypersurface W = 0 [1].

### 2.3 Derived categories of the second kind

A subtlety: the naive derived category of a dg category of matrix factorizations does not always behave well, because the curvature term (multiplication by W) interferes with the usual acyclicity formalism. The correct framework is the **derived category of the second kind** — the coderived or absolute derived category — which treats curved dg modules via a finer notion of acyclicity. Positselski's work gives a simple proof of Orlov's theorem in this language and extends it to singular ambient schemes X, where the cokernel functor from the absolute derived category of matrix factorizations to the triangulated category of singularities of X_0 remains fully faithful [4]. This is the B-side technology on which modern HMS arguments rely.

| Side | Model | Objects | Morphisms | Structure |
|------|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| A | Fukaya Fuk(X) | Lagrangian branes (L, E) | Floer cochains CF*(L_0, L_1) | A_\infty-category |
| A | Fukaya-Seidel FS(X, W) | Vanishing cycles, thimbles | Directed Floer complexes | Triangulated via mutations |
| B | D^b Coh(X^\vee) | Complexes of coherent sheaves | Ext groups | Triangulated |
| B | MF(X, W) | Matrix factorizations (E, p) | Z/2-graded Hom complexes | dg category, 2-periodic |

##3 Methodology

Proving HMS in any concrete case follows a remarkably stable template, visible across all major results:

1. **Construct both categories.** Build the A-side via Lagrangian Floer theory (often circumventing the heaviest analysis by using special classes of Lagrangians — vanishing cycles, immersed spheres, torus fibers) and the B-side via algebraic geometry (derived categories of sheaves or matrix factorizations).
2. **Find split-generators.** Identify a finite collection of objects on each side whose split-closure is the whole category. On the A-side, generation criteria (due to Abouzaid and others) reduce this to a topological condition on the Lagrangians; on the B-side it is a theorem of algebraic geometry (e.g. Beilinson-type generators, or Orlov's description of the singularity category).
3. **Match the endomorphism algebras.** Compute the total morphism A_\infty-algebra of the generating Lagrangians (often reducible to a concrete Floer cohomology computation on a single immersed Lagrangian) and show it is quasi-isomorphic to the endomorphism dg algebra of the generating sheaves or matrix factorizations. Versality/uniqueness theorems then promote a cohomology-level match to an equivalence of derived categories [2].
4. **Verify categorical invariants.** As independent checks, compare Hochschild homology, cyclic homology, and the Mukai pairing on both sides. The Kapustin-Li formula computes the closed-string state space of the B-model from the category of matrix factorizations; its A-side mirror is symplectic cohomology [7].

This methodology explains why matrix factorizations are the natural B-side for *open-string* mirror symmetry: they are the algebraic objects that correspond, under the mirror map, to individual Lagrangian branes — most directly to the **vanishing cycles** of a Landau-Ginzburg superpotential, which generate the Fukaya-Seidel category [6].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The A_\infty-operations: why homotopy associativity is unavoidable

It is worth dwelling on *why* the Fukaya category cannot be an ordinary category. The product of two intersection points x ∈ L_0 ∩ L_1 and y ∈ L_1 ∩ L_2 should be a point z ∈ L_0 ∩ L_2, obtained by gluing a holomorphic triangle with vertices x, y, z. But the moduli space of such triangles has boundary strata where a triangle degenerates into a quadrilateral, or where a disk bubbles off at a vertex. The count of rigid triangles therefore fails to be associative on the nose; the failure is exactly compensated by counting rigid quadrilaterals (the μ^3 operation), whose boundary in turn involves pentagons, and so on *ad infinitum*. The A_\infty-relations are the algebraic shadow of the compactification of these moduli spaces by stable maps. In code-like notation:

```haskell
-- A-infinity relation: the boundary of the 1-dimensional
-- moduli space of (d+1)-gons, for d = 3
mu1 (mu3 a b c)
  + mu2 (mu2 a b) c
  - mu2 a (mu2 b c)
  + mu3 (mu1 a) b c
  + mu3 a (mu1 b) c
  + mu3 a b (mu1 c) = 0
```

The upshot is that "the category" is really a dg or A_\infty structure, and equivalences must be A_\infty-quasi-equivalences — a fact that shapes every HMS proof.

### 4.2 Matrix factorizations and the singularity category

Let us unpack Orlov's equivalence more concretely. For the zero fiber X_0 of W, an object of D^b_sg(X_0) is a bounded complex of coherent sheaves modulo those admitting finite locally free resolutions. Given a matrix factorization (E, p), the cokernel of p_0: E_0 → E_1 is a coherent sheaf on X_0 (since W annihilates it), and this **cokernel functor** descends to the singularity category, where it becomes fully faithful; with the second-kind derived formalism it is an equivalence [4].

A minimal example: for B = C[x] and W = x^{n+1}, the indecomposable matrix factorizations are the 2×2 factorizations (x^k, x^{n+1−k}) for k = 1, ..., n. Their cokernels are the maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over the A_n singularity — the classical ADE classification of simple singularities reappears as the representation theory of the B-brane category. This is no accident: it is the LG mirror of the statement that vanishing cycles of the A_n Milnor fiber form the Dynkin diagram.

The Z/2-grading is a shadow of *R-charge* in the physics: the B-model of an LG model is 2-periodic because the superpotential has R-charge 2. Graded matrix factorizations, equivariant under a C*-action scaling W, recover Z-graded information and connect to projective geometry [1].

### 4.3 The localized mirror functor: geometry producing algebra

One of the most satisfying bridges between the sides is the **localized mirror functor** of Cho-Hong-Lau [6]. The idea: start with a Lagrangian immersion (e.g. an immersed sphere in a pair of pants, with self-intersection points), and consider its *deformation theory* — the Maurer-Cartan elements of its Floer A_\infty-algebra. The obstruction terms (the μ^0 counts of teardrop disks bounding the immersion) assemble into a holomorphic function W on the deformation space: the *disk potential*. This is the mirror superpotential, derived purely from symplectic geometry.

Concretely, for each immersed Lagrangian L with bounding cochain b, the functor assigns the matrix factorization

```
E_0 = CF*(L, L^b),  p = μ^1_b  (the b-deformed Floer differential)
```

with p^2 = W(b) · id — the curved differential squares to the potential precisely because of the A_\infty-relations. The functor lands in MF(W), and for orbifold spheres P^1_{a,b,c} it is shown to be fully faithful, proving HMS in those cases [6]. The construction also gives an enumerative interpretation of the inverse mirror map: the coefficients of W count holomorphic polygons.

### 4.4 The elliptic curve, worked end to end

The elliptic curve case [5] remains the cleanest illustration of the full dictionary. On the A-side, take the symplectic two-torus T^2 with Lagrangians given by lines of rational slope (r, d) equipped with flat line bundles of holonomy. Intersection points of two such lines correspond to basis elements of theta functions; the triangle product μ^2 reproduces the addition formulas for theta functions. On the B-side, the mirror elliptic curve E_τ has D^b Coh(E_τ) generated by line bundles O(np_0). The functor sends the slope-(r,d) Lagrangian with holonomy α to a stable bundle of rank r and degree d with determinant fixed by α — the Fourier-Mukai transform along the Poincaré bundle. Under this identification, Floer cohomology groups coincide with Ext groups, and the A_\infty-structure on the Fukaya side matches the dg-enhancement of the derived category. Every step is computable, and the theta-function identities do the heavy lifting [5].

### 4.5 Punctured surfaces and the topological Fukaya category

Moving up in complexity, Pascaleff proved HMS for punctured Riemann surfaces Σ by modeling A-branes via the **topological Fukaya category** — a combinatorial model built from arcs and ribbon graphs, following Kontsevich's proposal — and showing it equivalent to MF(X, W) of the mirror LG model [3]. The proof develops new gluing formulas for the topological Fukaya category, allowing the surface to be decomposed into pairs of pants whose mirrors are elementary LG models; the global mirror is assembled by a limit procedure on the B-side. This result is significant because it handles *non-compact* A-sides (punctures give partially wrapped behavior) and shows that matrix factorizations capture the correct B-model even when the mirror geometry is a fibration with singular fibers [3].

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We now survey the landscape of proven cases, which collectively constitute the empirical evidence for the conjecture.

1. **Elliptic curves** — Polishchuk-Zaslow [5]: DFuk(T^2) ≅ D^b Coh(E_τ), fully explicit via theta functions.
2. **The quintic threefold** — Sheridan [2][4]: for the Fermat-type quintic X_0 ⊂ P^4, 625 vanishing cycles split-generate D^πFuk(X_0); their total morphism A_\infty-algebra matches that of the corresponding Beilinson-type generators on the mirror, giving HMS for the quintic.
3. **Punctured Riemann surfaces** — Pascaleff [3]: topological Fukaya category ≅ MF of the mirror LG model.
4. **Orbifold projective lines** — Cho-Hong-Lau [6]: HMS for P^1_{a,b,c} via the localized mirror functor, non-Fano cases included.
5. **Higher-genus curves and abelian varieties** — Seidel (genus 2 via Lefschetz fibrations), Efimov, and the Abouzaid-Auroux-Efimov-Katzarkov-Orlov program for punctured spheres, all with matrix factorizations as the B-model [7].

Beyond equivalences, the *invariants* match. Hochschild homology of MF(W) for an isolated hypersurface singularity was computed by Dyckerhoff following Toën, verifying the Kapustin-Li description of the closed-string state space; the Hodge theory of the category of matrix factorizations was developed by Katzarkov-Kontsevich-Pantev and extended in the graded setting [7]. The open-closed map relating symplectic cohomology to Hochschild cohomology provides the A-side counterpart. These computations are the "experimental data" of the field: every predicted numerical invariant agrees.

A Python sketch of the matrix-factorization differential illustrates the algebraic side's concreteness:

```python
import numpy as np

def mf_differential(p0, p1, chi0, chi1):
    """delta(chi) = q . chi - (-1)^|chi| chi . p  for MF morphisms."""
    # even morphism chi = (chi0, chi1): E -> F
    d0 = chi1 @ p0 - chi0  # schematic: q0 chi0 - chi1 p0
    return d0

def check_factorization(p0, p1, W):
    n = p0.shape[0]
    assert np.allclose(p1 @ p0, W * np.eye(n)), "p1 p0 != W"
    assert np.allclose(p0 @ p1, W * np.eye(n)), "p0 p1 != W"
    return True
```

And the curved A_\infty-relation p^2 = W · id is the algebraic incarnation of the Maurer-Cartan equation on the A-side.

##6 Limitations

Despite this progress, the general conjecture remains open, and the obstructions are instructive.

- **Foundations of the Fukaya category.** The full A_\infty-category for arbitrary compact Lagrangians in general symplectic manifolds requires the virtual perturbation machinery of Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono; transversality for multiply-covered disks remains delicate. Most HMS proofs sidestep this by working with restricted, computable classes of Lagrangians.
- **Split-generation is hard.** Proving that a given set of Lagrangians split-generates DFuk(X) is the crux of every proof, and the available criteria (Abouzaid's generation criterion via the open-closed map) require the Lagrangians to "see" all of symplectic cohomology. For the quintic, this used the negativity of large-complex-structure monodromy — a 3-fold-specific argument [2].
- **The B-side can be singular and non-commutative.** Matrix factorizations handle isolated singularities well, but non-isolated or non-complete-intersection superpotentials, and mirrors that are stacks or non-commutative spaces, stretch the formalism. The derived categories of the second kind repair some of this [4], but a fully general LG B-model is still being developed.
- **Constructing the mirror functor.** The localized mirror functor [6] works when the deformation theory of a single immersed Lagrangian captures the whole mirror; in general there is no canonical choice of generating Lagrangians, and wall-crossing phenomena complicate the disk potential.
- **Non-Fano and non-compact targets.** Much of the theory is cleanest for Fano or Calabi-Yau manifolds. Extending HMS to general-type varieties (via categories of singularities [7]) and to fully wrapped settings is active research.

> Theorem (slogan): Every proven case of HMS follows the same schema — *construct, generate, match algebras, check invariants* — but each step currently requires case-specific geometry. A uniform proof would need a uniform construction of mirrors, which is precisely what the SYZ program aims to supply.

##7 Conclusion

Homological mirror symmetry reframes one of the most mysterious dualities in mathematics as a statement about categories of branes: Lagrangian submanifolds with Floer-theoretic morphisms on one side, coherent sheaves or matrix factorizations on the other. The A-side's A_\infty-structure encodes the bubbling of holomorphic disks; the B-side's matrix factorizations encode the singularities of the superpotential fiber, made precise by Orlov's theorem and the derived categories of the second kind. Bridges between them — the localized mirror functor, Orlov's cokernel functor, and the matching of Hochschild invariants — have turned the conjecture into a theorem for elliptic curves, the quintic threefold, punctured surfaces, and orbifold lines, with matrix factorizations playing the starring B-model role throughout.

The frontier is clear: extend the generation and matching arguments beyond the currently tractable geometries, complete the analytic foundations so that "the Fukaya category" needs no scare quotes, and understand how the categorical equivalence degenerates and reforms under wall-crossing. If Kontsevich's vision is right, the numerical miracles of mirror symmetry are not coincidences at all, but the decategorified shadows of equivalences that were there all along.
