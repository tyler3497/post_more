# Garbled Circuits at Scale: From Yao's Point-and-Permute to Half-Gates, Authenticated Garbling, and Silent-OT Preprocessing

## Abstract

Yao's garbled circuits remain the workhorse of constant-round secure two-party computation, yet the naive construction's four ciphertexts per gate made it impractical for decades. This thesis traces the engineering arc that turned garbling into a deployable primitive: point-and-permute indexing, the free-XOR global-offset trick of Kolesnikov and Schneider, row-reduction techniques, and the half-gates construction of Zahur, Rosulek, and Evans, which achieves two ciphertexts per AND gate while remaining free-XOR compatible. We then examine how authenticated garbling replaces the cut-and-choose paradigm to obtain malicious security with a single garbled circuit, and how silent oblivious-transfer extension via pseudorandom correlation generators pushes preprocessing communication to the sublinear regime. Drawing on protocol measurements and security proofs from the literature, we quantify communication costs per AND gate across scheme generations, analyze assumption trade-offs from circular correlation robustness to the learning-parity-with-noise assumption, and identify remaining limitations in standard-model security, malicious-secure OT preprocessing, and side-channel robustness.

## 1 Introduction

Secure two-party computation (2PC) asks two mutually distrustful parties, holding private inputs $x$ and $y$, to compute $f(x,y)$ while revealing nothing beyond the output. *Andrew Yao* introduced the garbled-circuit technique at FOCS 1986 "How to Generate and Exchange Secrets" [1]; the term "garbled circuit" was coined by Beaver, Micali, and Rogaway in STOC 1990 [2].

> **Theorem:** Any efficiently computable Boolean function $f$ admits a constant-round secure two-party protocol in the semi-honest model assuming only the existence of oblivious transfer (OT) and a pseudorandom function family.

The classical construction associates each wire $w$ with two random $\kappa$-bit labels $W_w^0, W_w^1$ and replaces every gate $g$ with a *garbled truth table*: four doubly-encrypted rows, one per input combination, permuted randomly so the evaluator cannot tell which row decrypts. The evaluator obtains one label per input wire — its own directly, the garbler's via oblivious transfer — and propagates labels gate by gate without ever learning the underlying wire values.

For decades this remained a beautiful theory with a prohibitive price tag: four $\kappa$-bit ciphertexts per gate plus trial decryption of every row. The modern era of garbling is the story of squeezing that cost — first to three ciphertexts, then to two, then to *zero* for XOR gates (free-XOR), and finally to two ciphertexts per AND *with* free XOR (half-gates). Parallel to this, cut-and-choose gave way to authenticated garbling for malicious security with a single circuit, while silent-OT preprocessing attacked the remaining bottleneck: the input-independent correlated randomness consumed by oblivious transfer.

This thesis synthesizes these developments. **Section 2** reviews the classical construction and point-and-permute; **Section 3** sets out a unified cost model; **Section 4** develops five deep dives; **Section 5** collects measurements and theorems; **Section 6** confronts limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The classical Yao construction

Let $C$ be a Boolean circuit with wires $\mathcal{W}$ and gates $\mathcal{G}$. The garbler $G$ samples for each wire $w$ two independent labels $W_w^0, W_w^1 \in \{0,1\}^\kappa$. For a binary gate $g$ with input wires $a,b$ and output wire $c$, the garbler encrypts the appropriate output label under the two input labels:

$$T_{a,b}^g[i,j] = \mathsf{Enc}_{W_a^i}\bigl(\mathsf{Enc}_{W_b^j}(W_c^{g(i,j)})\bigr), \qquad i,j \in \{0,1\}.$$

The four ciphertexts are randomly permuted; the evaluator $E$, holding exactly one label per input wire, trial-decrypts all four rows to find the one that opens. Output decoding tables map final output labels back to plaintext bits.

Two problems are immediate: (i) the evaluator trial-decrypts up to four rows per gate, and (ii) communication is $4\kappa$ bits per gate. *Point-and-permute* [2] addresses (i): each label carries a random *select bit* $p_w^b$, and the table is ordered by the pair $(p_a^i, p_b^j)$. The evaluator indexes the correct row directly and performs a *single* decryption — leaking position information uncorrelated with semantics, a pattern recurring throughout garbling research.

### 2.2 Oblivious transfer and the protocol skeleton

A full 2PC protocol wraps garbling with oblivious transfer: $G$ sends its own input labels directly, while $E$ obtains its input labels through 1-out-of-2 OT. The IKNP OT-extension protocol (Crypto 2003) reduced many OTs to a few base OTs plus symmetric-key operations — a prerequisite for large-scale deployment [3].

### 2.3 The formal garbling-scheme abstraction

Bellare, Hoang, and Rogaway (CCS 2012) formalized garbling schemes as $(\mathsf{Gb}, \mathsf{En}, \mathsf{Ev}, \mathsf{De}, \mathsf{ev})$ with security notions of *privacy*, *obliviousness*, and *authenticity* — the evaluator's inability to forge a valid garbled output, central to **Section 4.4**.

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis follows a comparative, cost-driven methodology:

1. **Unified cost model.** For each scheme we record ciphertexts per AND/XOR gate, hash/AES invocations for garbling and evaluation, and the underlying assumption (random oracle, circular correlation-robust hashing, LPN, standard-model primitives) — see **Table 1**.
2. **Assumption auditing.** We trace what each optimization demands of the hash function: correlation robustness for classical garbling, *circular* correlation robustness for free-XOR (Choi et al. showed circularity is unavoidable [4]), and weak CCR instantiable in the standard model [5].
3. **Protocol-level accounting.** For malicious security we compare cut-and-choose (many circuits, statistical security $2^{-s}$) against authenticated garbling (one circuit, information-theoretic MACs from TinyOT preprocessing). For OT we compare IKNP extension against silent-OT extension via pseudorandom correlation generators, measuring communication as a function of the number $n$ of OTs.
4. **Empirical anchoring.** Reported timings (e.g., WRK's 37 ms malicious AES evaluation) are taken from the original publications and interpreted comparatively.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Point-and-Permute: Indexing Without Trial Decryption

Point-and-permute is deceptively simple yet load-bearing. Each label carries a random *permute bit* $p \in \{0,1\}$: a wire's two labels are $(W_w^0, p_w^0)$ and $(W_w^1, p_w^1)$ with $p_w^0 \neq p_w^1$. Garbled rows are sorted by $(p_a, p_b)$, so the evaluator holding $(W_a, p_a), (W_b, p_b)$ opens row $2p_a + p_b$ directly.

The security argument is a standard *real-vs-random* hybrid: because $p_w^0, p_w^1$ are an independent random permutation of $\{0,1\}$, the row index reveals nothing about truth values. Crucially, point-and-permute composes with free-XOR: the global offset $\Delta$ is chosen with least significant bit $1$, so the permute bit of $W_w^1 = W_w^0 \oplus \Delta$ is always the complement of that of $W_w^0$.

- **Cost:** zero extra ciphertexts; one decryption per gate instead of up to four.
- **Subtlety:** select bits must be refreshed per garbling; labels are one-time.

### 4.2 Free-XOR: The Global Offset

Kolesnikov and Schneider's ICALP 2008 breakthrough observed that XOR gates need no garbling at all [6]. The garbler samples a single global secret $\Delta \in \{0,1\}^\kappa$ and sets

$$W_w^1 = W_w^0 \oplus \Delta \qquad \forall w \in \mathcal{W}.$$

For an XOR gate with inputs $a,b$ and output $c$, define $W_c^0 = W_a^0 \oplus W_b^0$. The evaluator holding $W_a^{x}, W_b^{y}$ computes $W_a^{x} \oplus W_b^{y} = W_c^{x \oplus y}$ — the correct label, with *no communication and no cryptographic operations*. This cuts garbled-circuit size by factors of $2$–$4$ on practical circuits.

Kolesnikov and Schneider proved security in the random-oracle model and conjectured that *correlation robustness* would suffice. Choi, Katz, Kumaresan, and Zhou refuted the claim: because the adversary can query the hash on inputs related by the *unknown* global $\Delta$, a *circular* variant of correlation robustness is required — and they defined exactly the notion that suffices [4]. Applebaum later realized free-XOR garbling in the standard model under LPN [7].

> **Theorem:** (Choi et al.) The Kolesnikov–Schneider construction is a secure garbling scheme if the hash function $H$ is circular 2-correlation robust; plain correlation robustness does not suffice.

One caution: the *global* secret $\Delta$ is a side-channel magnet. Hazay demonstrated horizontal power-analysis attacks extracting $\Delta$ from the garbler's device with as few as eight nonlinear gates under simulation — free-XOR can be "quite expensive" under physical access [8].

### 4.3 Half-Gates: Two Ciphertexts per AND, XOR Still Free

Before half-gates, the state of the art was fractured. *Garbled row reduction* (GRR3, 1999) cut each gate to 3 ciphertexts; GRR2 (Pinkas et al., AsiaCrypt 2009) reached 2 ciphertexts via polynomial interpolation but was *incompatible* with free-XOR, because it defined output labels as pseudorandom functions of input labels. Practitioners therefore used free-XOR + GRR3: 0 ciphertexts for XOR, 3 for AND.

Zahur, Rosulek, and Evans (EuroCrypt 2015) resolved the tension by splitting each AND gate into two *half-gates* — AND gates in which one party effectively knows one input [9]:

- **Garbler half-gate.** The garbler knows input $a$'s truth value semantics. It sets the output label directly as a hash of the known input label, transmitting one ciphertext that lets the evaluator recover the output label when $a=1$ and derive it locally when $a=0$:
  $$T_G = H(W_a^0) \oplus H(W_a^1) \oplus (p_b \cdot \Delta), \qquad W_c^0 = H(W_a^0).$$
- **Evaluator half-gate.** Symmetrically, with the roles exchanged and one more ciphertext $T_E$.

The full AND gate costs exactly **2 ciphertexts**, XOR gates remain free, and the construction is provably *optimal* within a natural class of garbling schemes capturing all known practical techniques — a $2\kappa$-bit lower bound per AND gate [9]. The price is computation: the evaluator performs two hash invocations per AND gate rather than one. Empirically the trade wins: up to 25% less time, 33% less bandwidth, and 20% less energy on benchmark circuits [9].

The lower bound stood for six years until Rosulek and Roy's "three halves" construction (Crypto 2021) bypassed it with *slicing and dicing* — splitting wire labels into halves and processing them separately — reaching $1.5\kappa + 5$ bits per AND gate with free-XOR compatibility intact [10]. Half-gates were also recently shown instantiable in the *standard model* from weak circular correlation robustness, itself realizable from indistinguishability obfuscation plus one-way functions [5].

```python
# Half-gate garbling of an AND gate (garbler side, garbler half-gate)
def garble_and_half_gates(Wa0, Delta, Wb0, pa0, H):
    # point-and-permute: lsb of labels are the select bits
    Wa1 = xor(Wa0, Delta); Wb1 = xor(Wb0, Delta)
    pa1, pb0, pb1 = 1 - pa0, lsb(Wb0), lsb(Wb1)
    # Garbler half-gate: garbler knows semantics of input a
    TG = xor(H(Wa0), H(Wa1), pb0 * Delta)   # 1 ciphertext
    Wc0 = H(Wa0)
    # Evaluator half-gate: evaluator's select bit pb selects
    TE = xor(H(Wb0), H(Wb1), pa0 * Delta)   # 1 ciphertext
    We0 = H(Wb0)
    Wd0 = xor(Wc0, We0)                     # free-XOR combination
    return (TG, TE), Wd0
```

### 4.4 Authenticated Garbling: Malicious Security Without Cut-and-Choose

Semi-honest garbling collapses against a malicious garbler who crafts a corrupt circuit — the classic remedy was *cut-and-choose*: garble $s$ circuits, open a random half, evaluate the rest, and take a majority vote. Lindell and Pinkas's protocol needed $\approx 160$ circuits for $2^{-40}$ cheating probability on AES — a 20-minute computation in 2009.

*Authenticated garbling*, introduced by Wang, Ranellucci, and Katz (CCS 2017, "WRK"), eliminates cut-and-choose [11]: a *function-independent preprocessing* phase distributes **information-theoretic MACs** on every wire label, then a *single* circuit is garbled and transmitted. For each wire $w$ the parties hold a public masked value $\Lambda_w = \lambda_w \oplus v_w$; the garbler holds key $K_w[\Lambda_w]$ and the evaluator holds MAC $M_w[\Lambda_w] = K_w[\Lambda_w] \oplus \Lambda_w \Delta_B$ under its global key $\Delta_B$.

Each garbled AND gate carries, alongside half-gate ciphertexts, *authenticated* cross-terms binding the computation to the MACs; any deviation by a malicious garbler requires forging a $\kappa$-bit MAC. The preprocessing is instantiated with a heavily optimized **TinyOT** protocol (Nielsen et al.), producing authenticated Beaver triples at high throughput.

The numbers rewrote expectations: maliciously secure AES evaluation in **37 ms total with 1 ms online time**; amortized over 1024 executions, 6.7 ms per AES [11]. Follow-ups pushed further: Katz et al. (Crypto 2018) realized the authenticated-garbling functionality at roughly 10 garbled circuits' cost given OT correlations; Ishai, Lu, and Ostrovsky (2022) reduced this to **1.31 garbled circuits** using VOLE and multiplication-triple correlations, or 2.25 using VOLE alone [12]. The online phase is *non-cryptographic*: XORs and table lookups.

| Approach | Circuits sent | Assumption | AES-class cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cut-and-choose (Lindell–Pinkas) | $\approx 3s$ ($s=40$) | OT + CR hash | $\sim$20 min (2009) |
| Authenticated garbling (WRK) | **1** | TinyOT preprocessing | 37 ms total, 1 ms online |
| Katz et al. Crypto 2018 | $\approx 10$ equivalent | OT correlations | comparable |
| Ishai–Lu–Ostrovsky 2022 | $\approx 1.31$ equivalent | VOLE + MT | best known |

### 4.5 Silent-OT Preprocessing and Pseudorandom Correlation Generators

Every garbled-circuit protocol consumes oblivious transfers — one per evaluator input bit — and every authenticated-garbling preprocessing consumes correlated randomness at circuit scale. IKNP-style OT extension made each OT cost a few hash evaluations, but *communication* remained linear in the number of OTs.

*Silent OT extension* breaks the linear barrier. Boyle, Couteau, Gilboa, Ishai, Kohl, and Scholl introduced **pseudorandom correlation generators (PCGs)**: the parties interact briefly to establish short correlated seeds, then *silently* expand them locally into long pseudorandom correlated strings — $n$ random OTs from $O(\lambda \log n)$ communication [3]. The construction rests on the **learning-parity-with-noise (LPN)** assumption: a sparse-noise vector masked by a public matrix yields pseudorandomness that the other party, holding correlated seed material, decodes into matching OT correlations. The blueprint:

1. **Seed setup (interactive, sublinear).** A handful of base OTs plus a small computation of the LPN seed correlation.
2. **Silent expansion (local).** Each party runs $\mathsf{Expand}$ on its seed; no communication. Outputs satisfy the OT correlation pseudorandomly.
3. **Online phase.** The garbled-circuit protocol consumes the correlations exactly as it would IKNP-extended OTs.

Boyle et al. (CCS 2019) further reduced the seed-setup interaction to **two rounds**, giving the first concretely efficient 2-round OT extension [13]. The blueprint composes cleanly with authenticated garbling: the expensive part (malicious-secure seed generation) is *amortized* over the tiny seed, while the bulk expansion is local and therefore immune to active attack.

**ABY** (Demmler, Schneider, Zohner, NDSS 2015) combines arithmetic sharing, Boolean (GMW) sharing, and Yao sharing with OT-based conversions, choosing the cheapest representation per subcircuit [14]. **TinyTable** (Damgård et al.) reduces the online phase to pure table lookup — one element exchanged per wire — foreshadowing the function-secret-sharing compression techniques that followed [15].

```rust
// Silent-OT PCG blueprint: seed setup once, expand silently per batch
struct PcgSeed { k0: Vec<u8>, k1: Vec<u8> }       // short correlated seeds
fn silent_expand(seed: &PcgSeed, n: usize) -> OtBatch {
    // LPN-based expansion: no communication, O(n log n) local work
    let sparse = lpn_decode(&seed.k0, n);          // sparse noise vector
    let dense  = prg_expand(&seed.k1, n);         // dense pseudorandom mask
    OtBatch::from_correlated(sparse, dense)
}
// Online: consume OtBatch exactly like IKNP-extended OTs in Yao's protocol
```

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

**Communication cost per AND gate across generations** ($\kappa = 128$):

| Scheme | XOR cost | AND cost | Hash ops (eval) | Assumption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classical Yao (1986) | $4\kappa$ | $4\kappa$ | 4 trial dec. | PRF / RO |
| Point-and-permute (BMR90) | $4\kappa$ | $4\kappa$ | 1 | PRF / RO |
| GRR3 (NPS99) | $3\kappa$ | $3\kappa$ | 1 | PRF / RO |
| GRR2 (PSSW09) | $2\kappa$ | $2\kappa$ | 1 | PRF (no free-XOR) |
| Free-XOR + GRR3 (KS08) | **0** | $3\kappa$ | 1 | circular CR hash |
| **Half-gates (ZRE15)** | **0** | **$2\kappa$** | 2 | circular CR hash |
| Three-halves (RR21) | **0** | $1.5\kappa+5$ | 3 | circular CR hash |

> **Theorem:** (Half-gates optimality, Zahur–Rosulek–Evans.) In the model of garbling schemes with free-XOR compatibility and a single global offset, any AND gate requires at least $2\kappa$ bits of communication. The half-gates construction meets this bound.

Measured end-to-end effects reported in the literature:

1. **Half-gates vs. free-XOR+GRR3:** up to 33% bandwidth, 25% runtime, and 20% energy savings across benchmark circuits including AES and edit distance [9].
2. **Authenticated garbling (WRK):** maliciously secure AES in 37 ms total / 1 ms online; 6.7 ms amortized per AES over 1024 runs [11].
3. **Silent OT extension:** sublinear communication in $n$; millions of OTs from kilobytes of seed communication [3]. Two-round seed setup removes the round-complexity penalty of earlier PCG protocols [13].
4. **Security theorems:** free-XOR requires *circular* correlation robustness [4]; half-gates meet the $2\kappa$ lower bound [9]; authenticated garbling reduces malicious garbler security to MAC unforgeability plus TinyOT security [11]; PCG security reduces to LPN [3].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Standard-model foundations remain impractical.** Half-gates and free-XOR are proven secure under circular correlation-robust hashing, whose only standard-model instantiation uses indistinguishability obfuscation — a feasibility result, not a deployment path [5].
2. **Malicious-secure preprocessing is not silent.** PCG seed setup is cheap but its malicious-secure generation still requires interaction and consistency checks; only the *expansion* is immune to active attack. End-to-end malicious silent preprocessing remains open.
3. **The global offset is a side-channel liability.** Free-XOR's single $\Delta$ amplifies physical attacks: horizontal power analysis on the garbler's device can recover $\Delta$ from a handful of nonlinear gates [8]. Masking or refreshing $\Delta$ reintroduces cost.
4. **One-time labels and state.** Garbled labels cannot be reused; every evaluation re-garbles. Reusable garbled circuits exist only under heavy assumptions and are far from practical.
5. **Circuit-dependent costs persist.** Garbling is linear in circuit size, and per-AND communication is bounded below by $1.5\kappa+5$ bits in the free-XOR-compatible regime [10]. Highly nonlinear computations (comparisons, AES S-boxes) still dominate budgets — the motivation for mixed-protocol frameworks like ABY [14].
6. **Selective-failure attacks on OT.** Malicious OT extension must guard against selective failure, where a corrupt sender crafts correlations that abort depending on the receiver's choice bit; silent-OT constructions inherit this concern in seed setup.

## 7 Conclusion

Four decades of garbling research form a coherent optimization narrative. *Point-and-permute* removed trial decryption; *free-XOR* removed cryptography from linear gates; *row reduction* and then *half-gates* compressed each AND gate to two ciphertexts; *authenticated garbling* replaced the cut-and-choose replication tax with a single MAC-authenticated circuit; *silent-OT preprocessing* pushed the correlated-randomness bottleneck into the sublinear regime. Together they moved maliciously secure two-party computation from a 20-minute AES evaluation to milliseconds — roughly five orders of magnitude from protocol engineering alone.

The frontier now lies in foundations and robustness: standard-model instantiations of the correlation-robustness notions efficient garbling actually uses, malicious-secure silent preprocessing that preserves the sublinear promise end to end, and side-channel-hardened implementations that protect the global offset without surrendering its efficiency.
