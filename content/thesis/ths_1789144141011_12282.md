# Pauseless Heap Management: Immix Mark-Region Design, the LXR Collector, and Concurrent Barriers in Shenandoah and ZGC

## Abstract

Automatic memory management remains the dominant determinant of tail latency in managed-language services, yet the three canonical tracing collectors — mark-sweep, semispace, and mark-compact — each sacrifice one of space efficiency, collection speed, or mutator locality. This thesis traces a modern line of escape from that trilemma. We examine **Immix** [1], a *mark-region* collector that reclaims memory at the coarse granularity of blocks subdivided into lines, using a line-mark histogram to reclaim contiguous free regions and *opportunistic defragmentation* to blend copying with marking in a single pass. We then examine **LXR** [2], which generalizes RCImmix's line accounting into a whole-heap reference-counting design augmented with a snapshot-at-the-beginning trace and concurrent copying, achieving both sub-millisecond-scale pauses and throughput competitive with the best stop-the-world collectors. Finally we dissect the barrier machinery of the pauseless production collectors **Shenandoah** [4] and **ZGC** [6]: Brooks-pointer indirection and load-reference barriers in the former, colored-pointer encodings over multi-mapped heap views and load barriers in the latter. We formalize how write barriers preserve the correctness of concurrent collectors, why generational refinement shifts responsibilities between load and store barriers, and where the remaining frontier lies.

## 1. Introduction

A tracing garbage collector performs three separable functions: it **identifies** live objects by following the object graph from a root set, it **reclaims** storage no longer reachable, and it **manages locality** by placing contemporaneously allocated objects contiguously so that caches, TLBs, and prefetchers reward the mutator. The canonical collectors each optimize two of these at the expense of the third [1]:

- **Mark-sweep** (MS) never moves objects, so collection is fast and space overhead is minimal, but it cannot relocate live objects and therefore sacrifices allocation contiguity and locality.
- **Semispace** (SS) copies survivors into a clean space, giving excellent locality, but halves the usable heap and pays a copying cost on every collection.
- **Mark-compact** (MC) achieves both locality and space efficiency, but pays a heavyweight collection cost that renders it uncompetitive in practice.

The *mark-region* family, introduced in Immix by Blackburn and McKinley at PLDI 2008 [1], attacks the dichotomy structurally rather than by tuning. Instead of reclaiming at object granularity (mark-sweep) or heap-partition granularity (semispace), it reclaims at a **region granularity** — contiguous blocks, each subdivided into lines — and tracks liveness with a per-line mark histogram. A block with no marked lines is reclaimed wholesale without sweeping; a block with *some* marked lines retains only those lines, and surviving objects in nearly-empty blocks may be *opportunistically* relocated to defragment. In a single pass, Immix mixes marking and copying.

A decade later the same lineage produced **LXR** (Zhao, Blackburn, McKinley, PLDI 2022) [2]: fusing *reference counting* with Immix's line-bytemap machinery and a *snapshot-at-the-beginning* (SATB) trace for cyclic garbage — achieving both low pauses and throughput competitive with the best stop-the-world collectors.

The industry converged on a different answer: keep tracing collectors but make them **concurrent**, crucially making **evacuation concurrent**. Shenandoah [4] uses Brooks-pointer indirection plus write barriers; ZGC [6] uses *colored pointers* over a multi-mapped heap with load barriers. Both accept a permanent per-access barrier tax in exchange for pauses that do not grow with heap size [6].

The unifying theme: **the barrier is the mechanism and the collector is the policy**. Once marking and relocation run concurrently with the mutator, every design reduces to *how the mutator interacts with objects whose location or liveness metadata is in flux* — SATB write barriers, load-reference barriers, colored-pointer decode.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The trilemma and the generational hypothesis

Collectors exploit two regularities. First, the **generational hypothesis**: most objects die young, so the young generation should be reclaimed frequently and cheaply. Second, **allocation contiguity**: contemporaneously allocated objects are accessed together, so contiguous allocation improves mutator cache locality [1]. Mark-sweep violates the second, semispace the first, mark-compact the third. Mark-region design recovers all three by making the reclamation unit the *line* rather than the object, while the allocation unit remains the contiguous block [1].

### 2.2 Reference counting and its classic defects

Reference counting (RC) maintains a per-object counter of inbound references; zero means dead. RC is *local*, *incremental*, and prompt — but pure RC **never reclaims cycles**, and naive implementations pay synchronization on every pointer mutation [2]. Deferred, coalescing RC batches increments and decrements and a backup trace handles cycles. RCImmix [3] married RC to Immix's line-marked heap; LXR [2] pushed this to whole-heap RC with *sticky* line accounting, where the line bytemap doubles as generation metadata.

### 2.3 Barriers: the mutator-collector interface

Any collector that does work concurrently with the mutator must interpose on mutator memory operations. A **write barrier** executes on every pointer store; a **read/load barrier** executes on every pointer load. Their roles differ by design:

| Barrier | Trigger | Canonical use |
|---|---|---|
| SATB write barrier | object field store | Capture the overwritten reference so a concurrent trace sees a consistent snapshot [2] |
| Generational write barrier | object field store | Record old-to-young pointers in a remembered set |
| Brooks/forwarding write barrier | object field store | Redirect stores to the to-space copy during concurrent evacuation [4] |
| Load-reference barrier (LRB) | reference load | Resolve forwarding pointers, preserve to-space invariant in Shenandoah [4] |
| ZGC load barrier | reference load | Decode color bits, remap stale pointers, mark live [6] |

A subtle point emphasized by [2]: *short GC pauses do not imply low latency*. On lusearch at a 1.3x heap, Shenandoah achieved sub-millisecond median pauses yet only 15K QPS with 78ms p99 latency — its concurrent machinery consumed the machine — while LXR delivered 119K QPS at 3.0ms p99 [2]. Barrier tax and concurrency tax must be measured in application terms.

### 2.4 Concurrent correctness: snapshot-at-the-beginning

A concurrent tracer faces the *lost object* problem: the mutator may overwrite the only pointer to an object before the tracer reaches it. **Snapshot-at-the-beginning** (SATB) defines liveness with respect to the graph as it existed when the trace began: the write barrier records every overwritten reference and new objects count as live, so everything reachable at snapshot time is retained [2]. SATB is *conservative* (it keeps floating garbage) but never reclaims live objects; both LXR and Shenandoah rely on SATB write barriers for concurrent marking [2, 4].

## 3. Methodology

Our method is comparative and mechanistic: we reconstruct each design from its primary publication, formalize barrier protocols as state machines over pointer representations, and evaluate claims against the reported evidence [1, 2, 4, 5, 6]. We center on **invariants** — the to-space invariant, the color-bit protocol, line-mark accounting — because each barrier exists solely to maintain one invariant under concurrent mutation, and on the *responsibility migration* generational refinement forces between load and store barriers [6]. We close with a TLA+ sketch of the concurrent-evacuation invariant.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Immix: the mark-region breakthrough

Immix organizes the heap into **blocks** (32 KB), each subdivided into **lines** of 256 bytes, and maintains a *line-mark table* — one byte per line recording whether any live object spans that line [1]. Allocation is bump-pointer within a block, so contemporaneous objects land contiguously, recovering the locality that mark-sweep sacrifices. Collection proceeds as follows:

1. **Mark**: trace from roots, marking headers and the line-mark bytes of every line each live object spans.
2. **Recycle by histogram**: a block with zero marked lines is **recycled wholesale** with no per-object sweeping; a partially marked block keeps only its marked lines, the rest becoming allocatable holes.
3. **Opportunistic defragmentation**: sparsely populated blocks have their survivors *copied* to a clean block in the same pass, freeing the source — marking and copying blended, with no separate compaction phase.

> **Theorem (Region reclamation):** If a block contains no marked lines after marking, no live object occupies it, and it may be recycled without examining any object within — so sweeping cost is proportional to *blocks*, not objects [1]. Empirically, on 20 DaCapo/SPEC benchmarks, Immix matched or beat mark-sweep, semispace, and mark-compact on total, mutator, and GC time at every heap size, with the largest advantage in tight heaps [1]. Block granularity also proved ideal for **pinning** at line rather than object granularity, minimizing pinning overhead [3].

A generational Immix adds a contiguous nursery; because the mature space copies only *opportunistically*, GenImmix avoids generational semispace's full-copy cost while retaining promptness [1].

### 4.2 LXR: reference counting grown up

LXR [2] is best understood as the convergence of three ideas: RCImmix's line-accounted reference counting [3], Immix's opportunistic copying [1], and a SATB backup trace. Its central data structure is the **Immix line bytemap**, which LXR repurposes to encode per-line *generations* — the same byte array that Immix uses for marks now tracks object age, giving a whole-heap generational structure without a separate nursery.

The collection cycle: **(1) RC epoch** — coalesced increments/decrements processed in bulk; zero-count objects free immediately into Immix lines, and line counters detect fully free lines and blocks (the *immediacy* of RC: short-lived objects reclaimed without any trace). **(2) SATB trace for cycles** — a concurrent snapshot trace seeded only from objects that *survived* the RC phase (the *implicitly dead* optimization), far cheaper than a full-heap trace. **(3) Concurrent copying** — survivors in fragmented blocks are relocated with barriers maintaining referential integrity.

LXR bounds the classic RC weaknesses directly: the *decrement avalanche* of recursively freeing a dead list is parallelized [2], cycles fall to the SATB trace rather than trial deletion, and coalescing amortizes synchronization — only the first increment and last decrement per epoch are processed, and most short-lived objects die with zero collector-visible barrier traffic.

> **Theorem (LXR correctness sketch):** Under SATB, any object live at trace start or created during it is retained; the RC phase reclaims only zero-count objects, unreachable in the snapshot. Hence no live object is reclaimed, and all non-cyclic garbage is reclaimed promptly. The empirical headline of [2]: LXR matches G1's throughput (119K vs 112K QPS) while delivering dramatically better tail latency than Shenandoah (13.1ms vs 127.8ms p99.99 — the pauseless collector's concurrency tax dwarfed its pause advantage).

### 4.3 Write barriers, read barriers, and the SATB discipline

The write barrier is the workhorse of concurrent collection. In its SATB form it is conceptually simple:

```python
def satb_write_barrier(obj, field, new_val):
    old_val = obj[field]          # the overwritten reference
    if gc.concurrent_marking:
        satb_buffer.append(old_val)   # keep the snapshot alive
        if old_val is not None:
            mark_gray(old_val)        # ensure the tracer visits it
    obj[field] = new_val
```

The slow path costs one branch plus a buffer append; the fast path is a single flag check. The generational write barrier has the same shape but records the *field location* (the old-to-young edge) into a remembered set:

```python
def generational_write_barrier(obj, field, new_val):
    obj[field] = new_val
    if is_old(obj) and is_young(new_val):
        remembered_set.add(address_of(obj, field))
```

Shenandoah's **load-reference barrier** (LRB) inverts responsibility: on every reference *load*, the barrier checks whether the reference points into the evacuation set and resolves it through the forwarding pointer, CAS-updating the field so future loads take the fast path [4, 5]. The LRB maintains Shenandoah's **to-space invariant** — *every* reference the mutator observes points to the to-space copy — letting evacuation proceed concurrently; it also supports compressed 32-bit oops on 64-bit builds [5].

ZGC puts *all* barrier responsibility on loads: its load barrier must (a) decode and strip color bits, (b) remap stale pointers, and (c) mark loaded objects live. Generational ZGC *migrates* responsibilities: the load barrier keeps color-stripping and remapping, while **store barriers** take over color-bit installation, remembered-set maintenance, and liveness marking [6] — a deep lesson that the optimal barrier split depends on which operation is cheaper to slow down.

```rust
// Sketch of a load-reference barrier fast path (Shenandoah-style)
fn load_ref_barrier(slot: *mut *mut Obj) -> *mut Obj {
    let r = unsafe { *slot };
    // fast path: not in evacuation set -> return as-is
    if !in_evacuation_set(r) { return r; }
    // slow path: resolve forwarding pointer, heal the slot
    let fwd = brooks_forwarding_pointer(r);
    if !fwd.is_null() {
        unsafe { cas(slot, r, fwd); }
        return fwd;
    }
    r
}
```

### 4.4 Shenandoah: concurrent evacuation via indirection

Shenandoah divides the heap into fixed-size **regions** and performs *concurrent* marking, evacuation, and reference updating [4]. The enabler is the **Brooks pointer**: an extra indirection word in every object header pointing to the object's current location. GC threads copy objects from the evacuation set to free regions and swing the Brooks pointer; mutator stores go through the barrier to the to-space copy. The only stop-the-world pauses are brief root-scanning phases — initial mark, final mark, evacuation bookends — so pauses are roughly *independent of heap size* [4].

The hazard is a mutator reading a field from the from-space copy mid-copy and observing a torn object; Shenandoah resolves it with the to-space invariant enforced by the LRB. The price — per-load barrier, one extra header word per object, and the concurrent CPU of evacuation — is the very tax that made Shenandoah lose on *latency* (though not *pauses*) to LXR on lusearch [2]. Generational Shenandoah (JEP 404) [5] concentrates evacuation on young regions, recovering throughput while reusing the LRB unchanged.

### 4.5 ZGC: colored pointers and the load barrier

ZGC takes the opposite architectural bet: instead of per-object indirection, it encodes GC metadata **in the pointer itself**. On 64-bit Linux, ZGC reserves a large virtual address range and maps the *same physical pages* at multiple virtual addresses — the **multi-mapping** trick [6]. Each mapping is a *view*: for example, three views called Marked0, Marked1, and Remapped. A colored pointer's high bits select the view; the low bits are the offset. Loading through the Marked0 view *means* "this reference was last seen in marking phase 0"; the load barrier inspects the color bits and acts accordingly:

| Color bits | Meaning | Load-barrier action |
|---|---|---|
| Marked0 / Marked1 | Object marked in that phase | If current phase differs, mark through current view |
| Remapped | Object (maybe) relocated | If pointer targets the relocation set, remap to to-space address |
| Finalizable | Needs finalizer processing | Route to slow path |

Because the views alias the same physical memory, "remapping" is pure address arithmetic — no per-object forwarding table in the fast path [6]. When relocation frees a region, its memory is reused *immediately*, before all stale pointers are fixed: stale colored pointers resolve through the Remapped view until the load barrier heals them. This keeps ZGC's pauses flat from megabytes to terabytes [6].

Colored pointers consume address bits — hence the 64-bit-only, large-reserved-range requirement (up to 16TB heaps) and the delicate interaction with compressed oops. Generational ZGC addresses throughput with young/old generations and the barrier migration of §4.3 [6].

We can express the core concurrent-evacuation invariant, shared in spirit by both Shenandoah and ZGC, in TLA+:

```tla+
---- MODULE ConcurrentEvacuation ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES heapLoc,    \* object |-> current location
          mutRefs,    \* mutator-visible references
          evacSet      \* objects being relocated

ToSpaceInvariant ==
    \A r \in mutRefs : heapLoc[r.obj] = r.loc
    \* every reference observed by the mutator denotes the current location

Evacuate(o, newLoc) ==
    /\ o \in evacSet
    /\ heapLoc' = [heapLoc EXCEPT ![o] = newLoc]
    /\ mutRefs' = {Heal(r) : r \in mutRefs}  \* barrier heals stale refs
    /\ UNCHANGED evacSet

THEOREM Safety == []ToSpaceInvariant
=====================================
```

The `Heal` step *is* the load barrier (ZGC) or LRB (Shenandoah): a per-load function mapping stale references to current locations. Model-checking this invariant is how one gains confidence the fast path cannot leak a from-space reference — the single bug class that corrupts the heap silently.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The published evidence forms a coherent arc from Immix through LXR to the production pauseless collectors:

1. **Immix vs. the classics** [1]: across 20 DaCapo/SPECjvm98/SPECjbb2000 benchmarks on a Core 2 Duo, Immix (and its generational variant) achieved the best or tied-best geometric-mean total time, mutator time, and GC time at every heap size tested. The mechanism is visible in the data: Immix's block-granular recycling gives mark-sweep-like collection speed, while bump allocation in blocks gives semispace-like mutator locality.
2. **LXR vs. G1 and Shenandoah** [2]: on lusearch at a tight 1.3x heap, LXR delivered 119K QPS — matching G1 (112K), dwarfing Shenandoah (15K) — with 3.0ms p99 latency versus 78.0ms. Pause percentiles favored Shenandoah at the median; the latency inversion is the paper's central caution: pauseless ≠ low-latency when concurrency consumes the machine.
3. **Shenandoah's pause scaling** [4]: pause times are dominated by root scanning and remain in the low milliseconds as heap size grows, validating the concurrent-evacuation architecture for SLA-bound services — at the cost of roughly one extra header word per object and continuous barrier overhead.
4. **ZGC's heap scaling** [6]: pause times do not grow with heap or live-set size (sub-10ms design goal, with work ongoing toward 1ms), and heaps scale from 8MB to 16TB. The multi-mapping design keeps heap overhead low because relocation reuses memory immediately without waiting for pointer healing.

On the *proof* side, the key results are invariant-based: the SATB correctness argument [2], the to-space invariant maintained by Shenandoah's LRB [4], and ZGC's color-bit protocol in which every stale pointer stays dereferenceable through its view until healed [6] — the properties a TLA+ model like §4.5's checks mechanically.

## 6. Limitations

No design escapes tradeoffs; the modern collectors redistribute rather than eliminate them.

- **Barrier tax is permanent.** Every pointer load in ZGC and every reference load in Shenandoah pays for the barrier fast path — a few instructions and, on the slow path, a potential cache miss. For pointer-chasing workloads this is a measurable throughput cost that no amount of concurrency recovers.
- **Concurrency tax.** Concurrent marking, copying, and barrier slow paths consume CPU that the mutator could have used. The LXR paper's lusearch result [2] is the canonical demonstration: Shenandoah's pauses were excellent and its latency was terrible, because evacuation saturated the machine.
- **Immix fragmentation bounds.** Opportunistic defragmentation is heuristic: a pathological heap of pinned or long-lived sparse blocks can defeat it, and the line granularity (256 bytes) means small-object fragmentation within lines is invisible to the histogram. Immix bounds but does not eliminate fragmentation.
- **LXR's complexity.** Fusing RC, SATB tracing, generational line accounting, and concurrent copying is a large trusted computing base with a subtle interaction surface (e.g., decrements racing the SATB trace's liveness decisions), and its evaluation is narrower than G1's decades of production experience.
- **Colored-pointer address pressure.** ZGC's design consumes virtual address bits for colors and requires OS support for multi-mapping; it was Linux/x86-64-only for years and interacts poorly with compressed oops. Shenandoah's Brooks word costs space on every object and complicates object layout for the JIT.
- **Generational pauseless collection is still young.** Generational Shenandoah [5] and Generational ZGC are recent refinements; their heuristics for tenuring age, young-generation sizing, and barrier-splitting are active research, not settled engineering.

## 7. Conclusion

The trajectory from Immix to LXR to Shenandoah and ZGC is garbage collection growing up: from choosing *which* stop-the-world tradeoff to accept, to restructuring reclamation around regions and lines, to fusing reference counting with tracing, to making the mutator a participant in collection through barriers. The deepest lesson: **the barrier protocol is the collector**. SATB write barriers, load-reference barriers, and colored-pointer load barriers are not optimizations layered onto a collector — they *are* the concurrent-correctness mechanism, and their fast-path cost, slow-path frequency, and responsibility split determine real-world behavior more than any policy heuristic.

Two frontiers stand out. First, **generational pauseless collectors** [5, 6] are renegotiating the load/store barrier split, whose optimum is not yet understood theoretically. Second, **hardware support** — tagged memory, capability machines — could collapse the barrier tax all current designs pay in software. Until then: measure latency, not pauses; count the barrier tax in application throughput; and remember the cheapest collection is the one the line histogram lets you skip entirely.
