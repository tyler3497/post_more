This article reconstructs the Montague architecture in full technical detail, traces the crisis precipitated by Geach's donkey sentences and its resolution in dynamic semantics (Kamp's Discourse Representation Theory, Heim's File Change Semantics, Groenendijk and Stokhof's Dynamic Predicate Logic), and evaluates modern reunifications — monadic semantics, variable-free combinatorics, and dependent-type pragmatics [2][3][4] — which argue that compositionality survives precisely by being generalized. # Compositionality Under Pressure: Montague's Intensional Logic, Quantifier Scope, and the Donkey-Anaphora Challenge to Typed Lambda-Calculus Semantics

---

## Abstract

Richard Montague's 1973 "Proper Treatment of Quantification in Ordinary English" [1] advanced one of the most audacious claims in twentieth-century science: that natural languages admit the same kind of mathematically precise syntax-semantics interface as the artificial languages of logicians, via higher-order intensional type logic and the principle of compositionality. Half a century later, two phenomena have tested that claim more severely than any other: *quantifier scope ambiguity*, where a single surface string admits multiple logically distinct readings, and *donkey anaphora*, where pronouns escape the c-command domain of their apparent binders and falsify the predictions of static first-order translation. This article reconstructs the Montague architecture in full technical detail, traces the crisis precipitated by Geach's donkey sentences and its resolution in dynamic semantics (Kamp's Discourse Representation Theory, Heim's File Change Semantics, Groenendijk and Stokhof's Dynamic Predicate Logic), and evaluates modern reunifications — monadic semantics, variable-free combinatorics, and dependent-type pragmatics [2][3][4] — which argue that compositionality survives precisely by being generalized. The thesis defended is that the dynamic and type-theoretic developments do not refute Montague's program; they complete it.

---

## 1 Introduction

In 1970 Richard Montague declared:

> "There is in my opinion no important theoretical difference between natural languages and the artificial languages of logicians; indeed, I consider it possible to comprehend the syntax and semantics of both kinds of language within a single natural and mathematically precise theory." [1]

Montague produced a *fragment* of English — syntactic rules, semantic types, translation rules into an intensional higher-order logic (IL), and model-theoretic truth conditions over possible worlds and times. PTQ (1973) showed that *every man* is uniformly a generalized quantifier of type **⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩**, combined with the rest of the sentence by typed functional application and λ-abstraction [1]. The engine is the **principle of compositionality**: meaning is a *homomorphism* from a syntactic algebra to a semantic algebra, each syntactic rule *S_i* paired with a semantic rule *T_i* over constituent translations. Two features define the system: a strict **type discipline** (τ(α) must be a function type matching τ(β) for application *α'(β')* to go through), and **intensionality** via the ^/ˇ operators over a type *s* of world-time indices [1].

Two phenomena placed this machinery under pressure it could not obviously withstand:

1. **Quantifier scope.** *Every man loves a woman* yields surface scope (∀ > ∃) and inverse scope (∃ > ∀) from one string. PTQ's quantifying-in rules generate scope mechanically, yet attested possibilities — islands, branching and cumulative readings — outran the original rule inventory.
2. **Donkey anaphora.** Geach's sentences — *If a farmer owns a donkey, he beats it*; *Every farmer who owns a donkey beats it* — contain pronouns whose antecedents neither c-command them nor bind them with the right quantificational force [9]. No static translation of the antecedent supplies the universally-quantified, discourse-persistent referents the pronouns demand: a counterexample to meaning-from-static-logical-form.

The history of formal semantics since 1973 is the attempt to save compositionality from these two phenomena — first by enriching the *level* of composition (storage, raising, underspecification), then the *notion of meaning* (dynamic update, discourse referents), and finally the *algebraic setting* of composition itself (monads, variable-free combinators, dependent types) [2][3][4][6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Intensional logic: types and operators

Montague's IL has three basic types — *e* (entities), *t* (truth values), *s* (world-time indices) — with functional types **⟨a,b⟩**. An **intension** of type *a* is an object of type **⟨s,a⟩**. The operators: *^α* ("cap") maps α : *a* to *^α* : *⟨s,a⟩*, the function from each index to α's extension there; *ˇα* ("cup") maps α : *⟨s,a⟩* back to *a*, the intension's value at the current index.

### 2.2 The PTQ category-to-type map

| Category | Symbol | Type | Denotation |
|----------|--------|------|------------|
| Sentence | S | *t* | *walks'(john')* |
| Term / NP | T | *⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩* | *λP. P(john')* |
| Common noun | CN | *⟨e,t⟩* | *λx. man'(x)* |
| Intransitive VP | IV | *⟨e,t⟩* | *λx. walks'(x)* |
| Transitive verb | TV | *⟨e,⟨e,t⟩⟩* | *λy. λx. loves'(x)(y)* |
| Determiner | DET | *⟨⟨e,t⟩,⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩⟩* | *λP. λQ. ∀x (P(x) → Q(x))* |

The type *⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩* is the signature move: *every man* denotes the **set of properties** every man has. Proper names are *lifted* to the same type — *λP. P(john')* — making subject position type-uniform [1].

> **Theorem (Type-uniform composition, Montague 1973).** Every well-formed PTQ derivation determines, via the translation rules *T_i*, a unique (up to βη-equivalence) IL term of its root category's type. [1]

### 2.3 De re / de dicto

*John seeks a unicorn* is ambiguous [1]: **de dicto**, *seek'(^λP. ∃x (unicorn'(x) ∧ P(x)))(john')* — John seeks the *intension* of *a unicorn*, none need exist, with *seek'* taking a property-intension of type *⟨s,⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩⟩*; **de re**, *∃x (unicorn'(x) ∧ seek'(^λP. P(x))(john'))* — a particular unicorn is sought. The ^ operator renders the ambiguity *inside* the compositional system — the technical sense of Montague's "intensional" logic.

## 3 Methodology

This thesis uses **formal reconstruction with empirical triangulation**:

1. **Reconstruction.** PTQ, Cooper storage, Quantifier Raising, DRT, File Change Semantics, Dynamic Predicate Logic, and monadic/dependent-type semantics are stated in a uniform typed λ-calculus metalanguage, so differences appear as differences in *types*, *composition rules*, or *semantic values* [2][4].
2. **Diagnostics.** Proposals face a fixed battery: (D1) scope flexibility; (D2) donkey conditionals and relative-clause donkeys; (D3) cross-sentential anaphora; (D4) weak crossover; (D5) the proportion problem.
3. **Computation.** Derivations are realized as executable λ-terms (Haskell), scope enumeration (Python), and dynamic update as state transition (TLA+).
4. **Complexity.** Following Morrill [8], each interpretation problem is placed in the complexity hierarchy.

The criterion is **conservative extension**: preserve PTQ's successes (generalized quantifiers, intensionality, compositional derivation) while covering the failing diagnostics, with minimal new primitives.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 PTQ's machinery and the quantification problem it solved

Pre-PTQ semantics was a patchwork: *most*, *few*, *exactly three* resisted first-order regimentation, and syntax-to-semantics mapping was paraphrase. PTQ's innovations:

1. **Generalized quantifiers as the uniform NP type**, directly implementable:

```haskell
-- Determiner denotations, PTQ fragment (Haskell embedding)
every :: (Entity -> Bool) -> (Entity -> Bool) -> Bool
every p q = all (\x -> not (p x) || q x) domain
a     p q = any (\x -> p x && q x) domain
lift  :: Entity -> ((Entity -> Bool) -> Bool)  -- proper-name lifting
lift j = \p -> p j
```

2. **Quantifying-in rules (S14–S16).** A term phrase is "quantified into" a sentence containing a free variable (*he_n*); different derivations nest operators differently — anticipating Cooper storage and Quantifier Raising.
3. **Intensional attitude verbs.** *Believe* relates an individual to a proposition-*intension* *⟨s,t⟩*, blocking substitution of coextensives and dissolving Frege's puzzles compositionally.

But quantifying-in is a *syntactic* scope mechanism grafted onto a *semantic* type theory, with hard-to-constrain generative power. Cooper (1975, 1983) replaced it with **quantifier storage**: quantifiers are held in a store during bottom-up derivation and discharged at clausal nodes, *retrieval order* fixing scope:

```python
# Cooper storage: scope as retrieval order
import itertools
store = [("every", "man", "x"), ("a", "woman", "y")]
for order in itertools.permutations(store):
    f = "loves(x, y)"
    for det, noun, v in reversed(order):
        f = (f"forall {v} ({noun}({v}) -> {f})" if det == "every"
             else f"exists {v} ({noun}({v}) & {f})")
    print(f)
# forall x (man(x) -> exists y (woman(y) & loves(x, y)))  -- surface scope
# exists y (woman(y) & forall x (man(x) -> loves(x, y)))  -- inverse scope
```

> **Theorem (Storage completeness).** Every PTQ quantifying-in derivation has a Cooper-storage derivation with the same IL translation; storage additionally generates exactly the operator permutations the retrieval constraints permit. [1]

Storage keeps the homomorphism while admitting surface constituency underdetermines scope — as does May's (1977) **Quantifier Raising**, where QPs covertly move to clausal peripheries at Logical Form.

### 4.2 Scope: islands, underspecification, and effects

Naive storage and QR underconstrain scope. Decisive data:

- **Islands.** *A student from every department left*: *every department* cannot take matrix scope as free QR would allow — locality constraints on retrieval/movement are mandatory.
- **Cumulative readings.** *Three hunters shot at five tigers* has a cumulative reading (three hunters and five tigers in shooting events, pairwise unspecified) no linear quantifier ordering expresses — motivating polyadic quantifiers.
- **Underspecification.** Hole Semantics and Minimal Recursion Semantics answer the *n!* explosion by representing scope as a *partial constraint order*: one underspecified representation, resolved by further constraints.

Shan's type-theoretic moral [2]: scope is an *effect*. Meanings lift uniformly into a monad *M* — continuation monad for scope, state monad for anaphora, reader monad over indices for intensionality — and one rule, *bind*/*unit*, replaces proliferating lexical stipulations:

```haskell
-- Monadic semantics: one composition rule for many "effects" (after Shan [2])
compose :: Monad m => m (a -> b) -> m a -> m b
compose mf mx = do { f <- mf; x <- mx; return (f x) }
newtype Cont r a = Cont { runCont :: (a -> r) -> r }  -- scope = continuations
everyC :: (Entity -> Bool) -> Cont Bool Entity
everyC p = Cont (\k -> every p k)
```

> **Theorem (Monadic unification, Shan 2002).** Intensionality, quantification, binding, and focus — each demanding global lexical revision ("generalizing to the worst case") classically — are uniformly monadic effects over one unchanged composition rule. [2]

The monadic program *generalizes* Montague rather than abandoning him: the homomorphism survives, into a Kleisli category rather than sets.

### 4.3 Donkey anaphora: rupture and dynamic repair

Geach's sentences, named by Kamp [9]: (1) *If a farmer owns a donkey, he beats it.* (2) *Every farmer who owns a donkey beats it.* Static Montague analysis gives (1) roughly *∀x ∀y ((farmer(x) ∧ donkey(y) ∧ own(x,y)) → beat(x,y))* — decent truth conditions, but the pronouns sit outside every binder's scope, and the antecedent existential cannot yield the pronouns' *universal* force (*every* farmer beats *every* donkey he owns). Binding crosses the conditional boundary, which static sentence-level composition cannot cross.

Kamp's **Discourse Representation Theory** (1981) and Heim's **File Change Semantics** (1982) [9] changed what meanings *are*: discourses map to *updates* on information states.

- **DRT.** Interpretation builds a **Discourse Representation Structure** *⟨U, Con⟩*. Indefinites *introduce* referents; pronouns *pick up* accessible agreeing referents; implication/quantification are *relations between DRSs* (duplex conditions). In (2), *x* and *y* are introduced once and the duplex condition quantifies over embedding extensions — the pronouns' universal force is free.
- **FCS.** The state is a *file*: cards (assignments) partitioned by introduced referents. Indefinites add cards; definites/pronouns require *familiar* cards — the definiteness contrast falls out of the dynamics [9].
- **Dynamic Predicate Logic** (Groenendijk & Stokhof 1991) internalized dynamics in classical FOL by redefining connectives: *∃x φ* is a *random assignment* passing *x* onward; *φ → ψ* is externally static, internally dynamic. The donkey conditional *∃x (farmer(x) ∧ ∃y (donkey(y) ∧ own(x,y))) → beat(x,y)* receives the universal reading — the existential *donates* its witness across the boundary.

DPL-style update as an executable state-transition system:

```tla+
---- MODULE DonkeyUpdate ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES refs, conds
Init == refs = {} /\ conds = {}
Introduce(r) == /\ r \notin refs /\ refs' = refs \cup {r} /\ UNCHANGED conds
Assert(c)    == /\ conds' = conds \cup {c} /\ UNCHANGED refs
Next == \/ \E r \in {"x","y","z"} : Introduce(r)
        \/ \E c \in {"farmer(x)","donkey(y)","own(x,y)","beat(x,y)"} : Assert(c)
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<refs, conds>>
(* Introducing x,y before asserting beat(x,y) keeps the pronoun's
   referent accessible: y is in refs when beat is asserted. *)
====
```

> **Theorem (Dynamic donkey equivalence).** Under DPL's dynamic →, the donkey conditional is true in input state *g* iff every antecedent-output state extends to one satisfying *beat(x,y)* — every farmer beats every donkey he owns. Universal force is a theorem of the dynamics, not a stipulation.

Dynamics also explains cross-sentential anaphora (D3): *A man walked in. He whistled.* works because sentence one's output state — *x* bound — is sentence two's input state. Meaning composes *across* sentence boundaries, impossible for static sentence-semantics.

### 4.4 Reconciliation: monads, variable-free semantics, dependent types

Dynamic semantics looked like Montague's refutation. Three reunifications show the opposition is apparent.

**1. Monadic dynamics (Shan [2]).** The state monad *S → (A × S)* reproduces DPL/DRT update inside compositional typed λ-calculus; nondeterminism models the existential's witnesses; their combination models donkey anaphora. New effects change the monad, not the lexicon [2].

**2. Variable-free dynamics (Shan [3]; Jacobson).** Indices and assignments — the machinery making binding look non-compositional — are eliminable via combinators (*S*, *K*, *W*, Geach's *g*):

```rust
// Variable-free binding: Geach's combinator g (Rust sketch)
fn geach<A, B, C, F>(f: F) -> impl Fn(Box<dyn Fn(A) -> B>) -> Box<dyn Fn(A) -> C>
where F: Fn(B) -> C
{
    move |inner: Box<dyn Fn(A) -> B>| Box::new(move |a: A| f(inner(a)))
}
// The pronoun becomes the identity on the threaded argument:
// no assignment functions, no indices, only combinators.
```

Shan [3] gives donkey anaphora with no assignment functions and predicts novel *donkey weak crossover* effects distinguishing dynamic binding from E-type accounts.

**3. Dependent-type pragmatics (McAdams & Sterling [4]).** In Martin-Löf type theory the donkey sentence is proof-relevant: *(p : Σ(x:E). Farmer(x) × Σ(y:E). Donkey(y) × Owns(x,y)) → Beats(π₁(p), π₃(p))*. The indefinite contributes a Σ-type whose *witness* the pronoun projects — the DRT discourse referent from general-purpose dependent types. As Sundholm noted, nothing here is ad hoc: the notions predate the donkey problem [4]. The *require* rule extends this to presupposition generally [4].

Categorical work presses further: DisCoCat donkey sentences in **Rel** [6], and sheaf-theoretic unification with DRS merge as colimit [5].

| Framework | Sentence meaning | Binding | Composition |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|-------------|
| PTQ (1973) [1] | IL term : *t* | λ-binding, quantifying-in | Application |
| Cooper storage | IL term + store | Retrieval order | Apply + discharge |
| DRT (Kamp) [9] | DRS update | Referents, accessibility | DRS merge |
| FCS (Heim) [9] | File update | Novelty/familiarity | Change potential |
| DPL (G&S) | Assignment relation | Dynamic ∃, → | Relational composition |
| Monadic (Shan) [2] | Kleisli morphism | State + nondet. monads | Monadic bind |
| Variable-free [3] | Combinator term | Geach combinators | Combinatory application |
| Dep. types [4] | Dependent function | Σ-projection | Π/Σ elimination |

> **Theorem (Conservative extension, synthesis).** Each row matches PTQ on the fragment's core predictions and strictly extends coverage on D1–D5. None abandons the syntax-to-semantics homomorphism; each redefines its *codomain*.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 The proportion problem

The sharpest static/dynamic wedge is D5: *Most farmers who own a donkey beat it.* Unselective dynamic binding (quantification over farmer-donkey *pairs*) predicts truth iff *most pairs* beat. Counter-scenario: ten one-donkey farmers beat theirs; one farmer owns a hundred donkeys and beats none. Intuition: *true* (most *farmers* beat their donkeys); pair-counting: *false* (10 of 110 pairs). Brasoveanu [7] resolves it by distinguishing *plural reference* (non-atomic individuals) from *plural discourse reference* — quantificational dependencies as plural information states (sets of assignments), à la van den Berg and Muskens's Compositional DRT. Only plural info states get relative-clause donkeys with multiple singular anaphors right [7].

### 5.2 Crossover without c-command

Static theory derives weak crossover from c-command. DPL derives it from left-to-right dynamics: a pronoun evaluated *before* its antecedent is introduced finds no referent [3]. Shan [3] further predicts *donkey* weak crossover contrasts adjudicating dynamic vs. E-type accounts — a testable novel prediction.

### 5.3 Complexity

| Formalism | Problem | Complexity |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Lambek calculus | Provability | **NP-complete** [8] |
| Lambek + additives | Provability | PSPACE |
| DPL (propositional) | Satisfiability | Decidable (FOL translation) |
| DRT (full) | Satisfiability | NEXPTIME-hard |

Dynamics does not buy coverage with tractability. The monadic/type-theoretic reformulations [2][4] partly aim to recover a *compositional* complexity profile.

### 5.4 Worked derivation: the donkey conditional

1. *a farmer* ↦ *∃x farmer(x)* — random assignment, *x* introduced.
2. *a donkey* ↦ *∃y donkey(y)* — *y* introduced.
3. *owns* composes: *∃x (farmer(x) ∧ ∃y (donkey(y) ∧ own(x,y)))*.
4. Dynamic →: antecedent output states become consequent input states.
5. *he* ↦ *x*, *it* ↦ *y* — pronouns read dynamically bound referents.
6. *beats* asserts: every antecedent-output state extends to *beat(x,y)*.

Result: *∀x ∀y ((farmer(x) ∧ donkey(y) ∧ own(x,y)) → beat(x,y))* — the universal reading static analyses could not produce, with no extra stipulation.

## 6 Limitations

**1. Weak vs. strong readings.** Dynamics predicts the *strong* reading, but *Every person who has a credit card pays with it* is naturally *weak* (one card, not all). Neither DRT nor DPL derives weak readings without added machinery (situations, plural individuals [7]).

**2. Dynamics ≠ pragmatics.** Dynamic semantics models *linguistically encoded* context change; real anaphora needs world knowledge and salience (*John bought a car. The engine was faulty* — bridging). Update rules underdetermine referent selection; the probabilistic-pragmatics interface is programmatic [4][5].

**3. Cross-linguistic coverage.** The debate is overwhelmingly English/German/Dutch. Logophoricity (Ewe, Yoruba), zero anaphora (Japanese, Mandarin), switch-reference (Mojave) stress the architecture in barely explored ways.

**4. Cost of reunification.** Monadic and dependent-type reconstructions [2][4] buy compositionality with heavier types — continuations, state monads, Σ/Π-types. Whether the human parser computes with monads is unknown; full-system parsing complexity is uncharted [8].

## 7 Conclusion

Montague's thesis [1] survived its two gravest challenges by changing shape. Scope forced auxiliary structure onto the homomorphism (stores, raised LFs, underspecified constraints); donkey anaphora forced dynamic *values* — updates, assignment relations, monadic computations, dependent functions over witnesses [2][3][4][9]. The five-decade pattern is unmistakable: every successful successor is a **conservative generalization** of PTQ — DRT generalizes the assignment, DPL the connectives, monadic semantics the category of composition, dependent types the witness [2][4]. None abandons the homomorphism. The history since 1973 is not Montague's refutation but his research program *working as intended*: an exact framework, stressed by recalcitrant data, extending itself without breaking.

The open question is no longer whether compositionality survives dynamics — it does, in at least four technical senses — but whether generalized compositionality still constrains theory enough to explain. That is the next fifty years' question.
