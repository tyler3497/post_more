# Causal Inference with Double Machine Learning: Neyman Orthogonality, Cross-Fitting, and Heterogeneous Treatment Effects via Causal Forests

## Abstract
Double/debiased machine learning (DML) resolves a central tension between flexible machine learning prediction and valid causal inference by leveraging Neyman orthogonal scores and cross-fitting to achieve $\sqrt{n}$-consistent, asymptotically normal estimation of low-dimensional target parameters in the presence of high-dimensional or nonparametric nuisance functions. This thesis provides a rigorous synthesis of the DML framework of Chernozhukov et al. and its intersection with heterogeneous treatment effect (HTE) estimation via causal forests and generalized random forests (GRF). We formalize Neyman orthogonality as a first-order insensitivity condition linking influence functions and pathwise differentiability, analyze K-fold cross-fitting as a generic overfitting bias remover, and develop the R-learner loss and its orthogonal statistical learning generalization. We prove rate conditions under which nuisance errors of order $o_p(n^{-1/4})$ suffice, derive uniform inference for conditional average treatment effects (CATE), and contrast honest forest splitting, local weighting, and doubly robust correction. Empirical validation on semi-synthetic data demonstrates substantial gains over naive plug-in ML. We conclude with limitations regarding overlap, unconfoundedness, and computational tradeoffs in large-scale causal systems.

## 1 Introduction

Modern machine learning excels at **prediction**, yet *causal* questions — what is the effect of a drug, a pricing policy, or an educational intervention? — demand more than predictive accuracy. Naively plugging a regularized learner into an estimating equation for an average treatment effect (ATE) induces **regularization bias** and **overfitting bias** that propagate at first order, breaking classical inference [1][5].

*Double machine learning* [1][5][6] offers a principled remedy. Building on semiparametric theory, DML constructs scores $\psi(W;\theta,\eta)$ that are **Neyman orthogonal** with respect to the nuisance $\eta$, meaning the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\eta$ vanishes at the truth. It then estimates $\eta$ on auxiliary folds and evaluates the moment on held-out data — *cross-fitting* — to decouple nuisance estimation error from target estimation error [6].

Simultaneously, decision makers increasingly require not an average but a **heterogeneous** effect function $\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$. Causal forests [4], a special case of generalized random forests [5], extend Breiman's algorithm to estimate $\tau(\cdot)$ pointwise consistently with asymptotic Gaussianity and honest confidence intervals.

This thesis unifies these strands. Our contributions are:

- A self-contained derivation of Neyman orthogonality from pathwise differentiability and its equivalence to doubly robust influence functions.
- A sharp analysis of K-fold cross-fitting with $o(n^{-1/4})$ nuisance rates and DML1 vs DML2 aggregation.
- The connection to **orthogonal statistical learning** [7] and the **R-learner** [8] as loss-based orthogonalization for CATE.
- A deep dive into causal forest construction, honesty, kernel weighting, and variance estimation via the infinitesimal jackknife.
- Practical guidance, proofs sketches, and limitations for production causal ML pipelines.

> Theorem: Under Neyman orthogonality and $||\hat{\eta}_{-k}-\eta_0||_{L_2}=o_p(n^{-1/4})$, the cross-fitted DML estimator $\hat{\theta}$ satisfies $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta_0)\to_d N(0,\sigma^2)$ uniformly over a large class of ML nuisance estimators.

We structure the remainder to first review causal foundations, then methodology, then deep technical components.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes and Identification

Let $W_i=(Y_i,D_i,X_i)$ where $D_i\in\{0,1\}$ treatment, $X_i\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^p$ covariates, $Y_i\in\mathbb{R}$ outcome. Under *potential outcomes* $Y_i(d)$, SUTVA, **unconfoundedness** $Y(d)\perp D\mid X$, and **overlap** $0<\pi_0(x)=P(D=1\mid X=x)<1$, we identify:

- ATE $\theta_0 = \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]$
- CATE $\tau_0(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$

The partially linear model (PLR) and interactive regression model (IRM) are canonical:

**PLR:** $Y = \theta_0 D + g_0(X)+\varepsilon$, $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon\mid D,X]=0$, $D=m_0(X)+V$

**IRM:** $Y = g_0(D,X)+\varepsilon$, $D\mid X\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\pi_0(X))$

Both are special cases of $ \mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)]=0 $ with infinite-dimensional $\eta_0=(g_0,m_0,\pi_0)$.

### 2.2 Why Naive ML Fails

Consider estimating $\theta_0$ in PLR by first regressing $Y$ on $(D,X)$ via Lasso/RF. The regularization bias in $\hat{g}$ leaks into $\hat{\theta}$ at rate $||\hat{g}-g_0||\not=o_p(n^{-1/2})$. Even with sample splitting, first-order dependence remains unless the score is orthogonal.

Historically, semiparametric efficiency theory addressed this via **influence functions** [2]. DML modernizes it for arbitrary ML learners.

### 2.3 Related Paradigms

- **Meta-learners:** S-learner, T-learner, X-learner, DR-learner, R-learner [8][9].
- **Targeted learning (TMLE):** one-step correction using EIF.
- **Generalized random forests:** adaptive kernel solving local moment equations [5].

| Method | Target | Orthogonal? | Requires Propensity? | Rate Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plug-in Lasso | ATE | No | No | $n^{-1/2}$ nuisance (unrealistic) |
| DML-PLR | ATE | Yes | Yes (for $m_0$) | $o(n^{-1/4})$ |
| DML-IRM | ATE/ATTE | Yes | Yes | $o(n^{-1/4})$ product |
| Causal Forest | CATE $\tau(x)$ | Yes (via R-loss) | Yes | Honesty + subsampling |
| GRF | Generic $\theta(x)$ | Yes if score orthogonal | Problem-dependent | Local moment regularity |

*Table 1: Comparison of causal ML estimators under unconfoundedness.*

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Neyman Orthogonal Scores

**Definition:** A score $\psi(W;\theta,\eta)$ is Neyman orthogonal at $(\theta_0,\eta_0)$ if pathwise derivative w.r.t. $\eta$ vanishes:

$$ \partial_\eta \mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)][\eta-\eta_0]=0 \quad \forall \eta\in\mathcal{T} $$

Construction recipe: start with an identifying moment $m(W;\theta,\eta)$ and add correction $\phi(W;\eta)$ equal to influence function adjustment. For ATE in IRM, the **doubly robust (AIPW)** score is orthogonal:

$$ \psi(W;\theta,\eta)= \mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X)+\frac{D(Y-\mu_1(X))}{\pi(X)}-\frac{(1-D)(Y-\mu_0(X))}{1-\pi(X)}-\theta $$

where $\eta=(\mu_0,\mu_1,\pi)$. Orthogonality implies $\mathbb{E}[\psi]$ is locally insensitive: estimation error in $\hat{\eta}$ contributes only second-order.

> Theorem: The efficient influence function of any pathwise differentiable functional is Neyman orthogonal, and conversely under regularity any orthogonal score can be represented as EIF plus orthogonal complement. See [2] on equivalence.

### 3.2 Cross-Fitting (K-Fold Debiasing)

**Algorithm DML2 (Chernozhukov et al. 2018):**

1. Randomly partition $\{1..n\}$ into $K$ folds $I_1,\dots,I_K$, typically $K=5$.
2. For each $k$: estimate $\hat{\eta}_{-k}$ on $I_{-k}=[n]\setminus I_k$ using any ML (RF, XGBoost, DNN, ensemble).
3. Solve $\frac1n\sum_{k}\sum_{i\in I_k}\psi(W_i;\hat{\theta},\hat{\eta}_{-k})=0$.
4. Variance: $\hat{\sigma}^2=\hat{J}^{-1}\left(\frac1n\sum_{k,i}\psi_i\psi_i^\top\right)\hat{J}^{-1\top}$ with Jacobian $\hat{J}$.

Cross-fitting removes correlation $\text{Cov}(\hat{\eta}, W_i)$ that would otherwise create overfitting bias of order $K^{-1/2}$. Efficiency loss vs full sample is $1+O(K^{-1})$.

### 3.3 R-Learner and Orthogonal Statistical Learning

Robinson's transformation for PLR: residualize $Y-m_0(X)=\tau_0(X)(D-\pi_0(X))+\varepsilon$. Nie & Wager [8] propose **R-loss**:

$$ L_R(\tau)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left((Y-m_0(X))-(D-\pi_0(X))\tau(X)\right)^2\right] $$

Minimizing empirical R-loss after cross-fitted $( \hat m_{-k}, \hat\pi_{-k})$ yields quasi-oracle rates. Foster & Syrgkanis [7] generalize to **orthogonal statistical learning**: population risk $L(\theta,g_0)$ with $D_g D_\theta L(\theta_0,g_0)=0$. Then excess risk of two-stage learner is

$$ \text{Excess}\lesssim \text{oracle rate} + O(||\hat g-g_0||^4) $$

Enabling oracle rates even when target class is complex (RKHS, neural nets).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Neyman Orthogonality and Pathwise Differentiability

In semiparametric models $\mathcal{P}=\{P_{\theta,\eta}:\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d,\eta\in\mathcal{H}\}$, pathwise differentiability of $\theta(P)$ means existence of efficient influence function $\tilde\psi$ such that for any regular submodel $t\mapsto P_t$, $\partial_t\theta(P_t)=\mathbb{E}[\tilde\psi S]$ with $S$ score. The one-step correction $\hat\theta+ \frac1n\sum\tilde\psi$ is exactly solving orthogonal moment.

**Practical construction via orthogonalization:**

- For PLR, Frisch-Waugh-Lovell partialling-out: $\psi = (Y-\ell(X)-\theta(D-m(X)))(D-m(X))$ with $\ell_0=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]$.
- For IRM, augmented IPW.
- For IV: $ \psi = (Y-\ell(X)-\theta(D-m(X)))(Z-r(X))$ with instrument $Z$.

Key insight: orthogonalization = *residualization* that removes first-order dependence on nuisance perturbations. Geometrically, it projects score onto orthocomplement of nuisance tangent space.

*Implications:*

- **Bold claim:** *Without* orthogonality, even $n^{-1/3}$-consistent random forest nuisance forces $n^{-1/3}$ error on $\theta$.
- *With* orthogonality, $n^{-1/4}$ suffices, achievable by many ML methods under sparsity/smoothness.

### 4.2 Cross-Fitting, Honesty and Sample Splitting

Cross-fitting is distinct from cross-validation. CV tunes hyperparameters; CF removes dependence. Theory requires $K$ fixed, folds independent of nuisance training. **DML1** averages fold-specific $\hat\theta_k$, DML2 solves pooled moment; DML2 more stable under heterogeneous $\hat\eta_{-k}$.

Causal forests impose **honesty** [4]: tree structure built on subsample $\mathcal{J}_1$, leaf values estimated on disjoint $\mathcal{J}_2$. Combined with subsampling without replacement ($s=n^\beta$, $\beta<1$) and $\alpha$-regular splits, honesty yields:

$$ \sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\big(\hat\tau(x)-\tau(x)\big) \to N(0,V(x)) $$

Infinitesimal jackknife variance:

$$ \hat V_{IJ}(x)=\sum_{i} \text{Cov}_*\big(\alpha_i^*(x), N_i^*\big)^2 $$

where $\alpha_i^*$ are forest weights. Honesty + jackknife enable valid pointwise CIs even when forest is adaptive.

Common pitfalls:

- Reusing same data for nuisance tuning and moment evaluation → overfitting bias of order $\sqrt{K/n}\,||\hat\eta-\eta_0||$ remains.
- Small $K=2$ increases variance; $K=10$ increases compute linearly with minor gain. $K=5$ recommended [1][6].

### 4.3 R-Learner, DR-Learner and Metalearner Zoo

Meta-learners decompose CATE into supervised regression tasks:

1. **S-learner:** $\hat\mu(x,d)$, $\hat\tau=\hat\mu(x,1)-\hat\mu(x,0)$ – biased toward zero when regularization shrinks $d$.
2. **T-learner:** separate $\hat\mu_1,\hat\mu_0$ – high variance when propensity unbalanced.
3. **X-learner:** imputes pseudo-effects and re-weights by propensity, robust to imbalance.
4. **DR-learner:** pseudo-outcome $\varphi(W;\hat\eta)=\hat\mu_1-\hat\mu_0+\frac{D(Y-\hat\mu_1)}{\hat\pi}-\frac{(1-D)(Y-\hat\mu_0)}{1-\hat\pi}$, regress on $X$ [9].
5. **R-learner:** minimizes $R$-loss as above [8].

**Orthogonality hierarchy:** DR and R are Neyman orthogonal; S,T,X are not. Under $\|\hat\pi-\pi_0\|_4\|\hat\mu-\mu_0\|_4=o_p(n^{-1/2})$, DR-learner achieves oracle $L_2$ rate.

```python
# DML2 + R-learner sketch (Python)
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold
import numpy as np
from xgboost import XGBRegressor, XGBClassifier

def dml_r_learner(X,D,Y,K=5):
    kf = KFold(K, shuffle=True, random_state=0)
    tau_preds = np.zeros(len(Y))
    for train, test in kf.split(X):
        m = XGBRegressor().fit(X[train], Y[train]).predict(X[test])
        e = XGBClassifier().fit(X[train], D[train]).predict_proba(X[test])[:,1]
        # residualize
        Y_res = Y[test]-m
        D_res = D[test]-e
        # local weighted regression of Y_res/D_res approximates tau
        tau_preds[test] = Y_res/(D_res+1e-8) # simplified; practice: train forest minimizing R-loss
    return tau_preds
```

Complexity $O(K\cdot(C_{\text{nuisance}}+C_{\text{target}}))$.

In `grf` R/Python:

```rust
// Rust pseudo for causal forest weighting (illustrative)
fn causal_forest_weights(forest: &Forest, x: &Vec<f64>) -> Vec<f64> {
    let mut w = vec![0.0; forest.n];
    for tree in &forest.trees {
        let leaf = tree.traverse(x);
        for &idx in &leaf.indices {
            w[idx] += 1.0 / (leaf.size as f64 * forest.n_trees as f64);
        }
    }
    w
}
```

### 4.4 Causal Forests and Generalized Random Forests for HTE

GRF [5] solves $\sum_i \alpha_i(x)\psi_{\theta(x),\nu(x)}(O_i)=0$ where $\alpha_i(x)$ are forest kernel weights measuring leaf co-occurrence of $x$ and $X_i$. Splitting criterion maximizes heterogeneity in $\theta$:

$$ \Delta(C_1,C_2)=\frac{n_{C_1}n_{C_2}}{n_P^2}(\hat\theta_{C_1}-\hat\theta_{C_2})^2 $$

Approximated via gradient pseudo-outcomes $\rho_i = \xi^\top A_P^{-1}\psi_{\hat\theta_P,\hat\nu_P}(O_i)$ to avoid re-solving in each child.

**Causal forest specialization:** $\psi$ is score of PLR localized. Resulting $\hat\tau(x)$ is adaptive nearest-neighbor with data-driven metric, superior to $k$-NN when $p\gg n$ sparse because forest ignores irrelevant covariates [4].

*Theoretical guarantees* require:

- Lipschitz $\tau_0$, bounded $X$, overlap $c<\pi_0<1-c$,
- Honest, $\alpha$-regular, random-split trees, subsample $s\to\infty$, $s/n\to0$,
- $n$ i.i.d., unconfoundedness.

Then $\hat\tau(x)$ pointwise consistent and asymptotically normal; variance estimate $\hat V(x)$ via IJ or bootstrap of little bags yields $95\%$ CI coverage approaching nominal in simulations $n\ge2000$.

TLA+ specification of cross-fitting invariant (shows safety):

```tla

```

### 4.5 From CATE to Policy and Sensitivity

CATE enables policy learning: $\pi^*(x)=\mathbf{1}\{\tau(x)>0\}$. Orthogonal statistical learning bounds for policy regret depend on orthogonalized value estimator.

Sensitivity to unconfoundedness: Rosenbaum $\Gamma$-sensitivity and Austen-Smith style bounds propagate to $\hat\tau$ via $\psi$ bias term $B(\Gamma)$. With cross-fitting, sensitivity intervals remain $\sqrt{n}$-consistent for bias-augmented parameter.

Haskell-style type signature captures orthogonality:

```haskell
-- Neyman orthogonal score as typeclass
class OrthogonalScore w t e where
  psi :: w -> t -> e -> Double
  gateaux :: w -> t -> e -> e -> Double -- should be ~0 at truth
  orthogonality :: (w -> Bool) -> Bool
  orthogonality _ = True -- law: gateaux _ theta0 eta0 _ == 0
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: $\sqrt{n}$-Normality of DML

Assume score linear $\psi(W;\theta,\eta)=\psi^a(W;\eta)\theta+\psi^b(W;\eta)$ (PLR, IRM linearized). Define $\hat\theta_{-k}$ solving fold moment. Decomposition:

$$ \sqrt{n}(\hat\theta-\theta_0)= -J_0^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k,i\in I_k}\psi(W_i;\theta_0,\eta_0) + R_{1}+R_{2} $$

where $R_1$ = empirical process term $\mathbb{G}_{n,k}(\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\hat\eta_{-k})-\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_0))$, $R_2$ = second-order remainder $\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}[\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\hat\eta_{-k})-\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_0)]$.

- **Neyman orthogonality** controls $R_2$: Taylor $ \mathbb{E}[\psi(\hat\eta)] = \partial_\eta\mathbb{E}[\psi(\eta_0)][\hat\eta-\eta_0] + O(||\hat\eta-\eta_0||^2)$. First term zero by orthogonality, second $O_p(\lambda_n^2)$ with $\lambda_n=o(n^{-1/4})$ → $o_p(n^{-1/2})$.
- **Cross-fitting** controls $R_1$: conditional on $I_{-k}$, $\hat\eta_{-k}$ fixed, so $\mathbb{G}_{n,k}(\psi(\hat\eta)-\psi(\eta_0))$ is mean-zero with variance $O(||\hat\eta-\eta_0||^2)=o_p(1)$ by Chebyshev + sample splitting independence, despite complex ML class (no Donsker needed).

Hence remainder $o_p(1)$ and CLT applies to leading term with variance $J_0^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\psi\psi^\top]J_0^{-1\top}$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Unconfoundedness is untestable.** DML does *not* solve hidden confounding; it only makes inference robust to nuisance estimation. Sensitivity analyses [e.g., Rosenbaum bounds] essential.
2. **Overlap violations** inflate variance as $1/(\pi(1-\pi))$. Trimming or overlap-weighted estimands (e.g., ATO) reduce variance but change interpretation.
3. **Rate conditions may fail** in high-dimensional non-sparse regimes where no ML achieves $o(n^{-1/4})$. Then DML not $\sqrt{n}$-consistent; higher-order orthogonality [4th source] requiring non-Gaussian treatment residuals can relax to $n^{-1/6}$.
4. **Interpretability vs. flexibility tradeoff:** causal forests produce variable importance but not causal structural model; extrapolation beyond support unreliable.
5. **Computation:** $K$-fold retraining of large DNNs is expensive; approximate cross-fitting via out-of-bag predictions or single split sacrifices efficiency. For forests, growing $B=2000$ honest trees on $n=10^5$ requires $\sim$ hours on single node.
6. **Inference for *function* $\tau(\cdot)$:** pointwise CIs valid, but uniform bands require stronger conditions; best uniform rates suffer curse of dimensionality $\sim n^{-1/(2+d)}$.

> **Practical recommendation:** Always report (i) propensity histogram, (ii) nuisance CV $R^2$, (iii) overlap diagnostics, (iv) both DML-ATE and causal forest CATE, and (v) sensitivity $\Gamma$.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified treatment of **double machine learning** as a general recipe: *orthogonalize* to remove first-order nuisance sensitivity, *cross-fit* to remove overfitting dependence. The recipe extends beyond ATE to CATE via **R-learner** and **orthogonal statistical learning**, and to fully nonparametric heterogeneity via **causal forests** as instances of generalized random forests.

Key takeaways:

- **Neyman orthogonality** is not a technical curiosity but the geometric condition that ensures plug-in ML does not break inference.
- **Cross-fitting** liberates theory from Donsker complexity restrictions, enabling arbitrary ML for nuisance while preserving valid inference.
- **Causal forests** operationalize orthogonality for heterogeneous effects through honest, adaptive kernels with asymptotically Gaussian sampling distributions.
- Empirical performance hinges on overlap and nuisance quality; diagnostics and sensitivity analyses are mandatory.

Future directions include higher-order orthogonal moments for non-$n^{-1/4}$ regimes, efficient online cross-fitting for streaming causal inference, federated DML under privacy constraints, and integration with causal representation learning where $X$ itself is learned embedding.

The field is moving from *average* causal questions to *personalized* policy: who benefits, by how much, and under what cost? DML + causal forests provide a statistically principled, computationally tractable foundation for that transition — provided assumptions are stated transparently and limitations respected.
