# Sketching the Deluge: Turnstile Streaming Algorithms, from Count-Min and Count Sketches to HyperLogLog and the AMS Second-Moment Estimator

## Abstract

Modern data infrastructure ingests streams far too large to store: network telemetry, search queries, clickstreams, and sensor feeds arrive at rates that make even a single pass with polylogarithmic memory the only viable computation model. This thesis develops the foundational theory and practice of *sketching* under the turnstile streaming model, in which updates may increment or decrement arbitrary coordinates of an implicit frequency vector. We present the Count-Min sketch [1] and the Count sketch with their one-sided and unbiased frequency-estimation guarantees, the AMS second-moment sketch of Alon, Matias, and Szegedy [3] with its median-of-means analysis and near-optimal space complexity, and the HyperLogLog cardinality estimator of Flajolet, Fusy, Gandouet, and Meunier [2], whose harmonic-mean aggregation achieves relative standard error 1.04/√m with kilobytes of memory. We unify these algorithms as linear sketches, prove their core concentration guarantees, quantify the space–accuracy tradeoffs and matching lower bounds, report empirical behavior on synthetic heavy-tailed streams, and close with open problems in adversarial robustness and optimal frequency-moment estimation.

## 1 Introduction

The streaming model of computation was born of necessity. When the input is a sequence of billions of updates that can be examined only once, and when the working memory available is measured in megabytes rather than the terabytes the raw data would occupy, classical algorithms that assume random access collapse. The seminal 1996 paper of Alon, Matias, and Szegedy [3] posed the problem in its now-canonical form: *approximate the p-th frequency moment of a data stream in space polylogarithmic in the stream length*. That paper, together with the earlier probabilistic counting work of Flajolet and Martin, founded a field whose practical descendants now run inside Redis, BigQuery, Druid, and every major network-monitoring stack.

This thesis is a systematic exposition of the four pillars of single-pass summarization. First, the **turnstile model**, in which the stream is a sequence of updates *(i, Δ)* applied to coordinates of an implicit frequency vector *f*, with Δ permitted to be negative; its strict variant requires all frequencies to remain nonnegative. Second, the **Count-Min sketch** [1], the workhorse for point queries with a one-sided error guarantee. Third, the **Count sketch**, which trades the one-sided guarantee for an *unbiased* estimator with an ℓ₂-norm error bound — strictly stronger whenever the frequency vector is heavy-tailed. Fourth, the **AMS sketch** [3], which estimates the *second* frequency moment *F₂ = Σᵢ fᵢ²* — the "surprise index" — in *O(ε⁻² log(1/δ) log n)* space, within a near-tight bound that Kane, Nelson, and Woodruff later refined. Finally, **HyperLogLog** [2], which estimates the *zeroth* frequency moment *F₀* — the number of distinct elements — with relative standard error *1.04/√m* using only *m* small registers, and whose engineering refinements at Google [4] turned it into an industry standard.

> **Theorem:** The four families of sketches surveyed here are all *linear*: each maintains the product *Mf* of a (random, implicitly defined) sketch matrix *M* with the frequency vector *f*, so sketches of disjoint or differenced streams compose by addition and subtraction.

We proceed from foundations to deep technical analysis, then to proofs, empirical evaluation, and limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Streaming Models

Three progressively more general models structure the field:

- **Cash-register (insertion-only) model.** Updates are insertions *(i, +1)*; frequencies are monotone nondecreasing. Easiest, but unrealistic for streams with deletions or corrections.
- **Strict turnstile model.** Updates *(i, Δ)* with arbitrary integer Δ are allowed, but the frequency vector must remain coordinatewise nonnegative at all times: *fᵢ ≥ 0* [5]. This captures insert/delete streams such as network flows that begin and end.
- **General (nonstrict) turnstile model.** The final vector *f* may have negative coordinates. Count-Min fails here; the Count sketch and the AMS sketch survive.

The algorithm sees *x₁, x₂, …, xₘ* with *xₜ ∈ [n]* (or updates *(iₜ, Δₜ)*), uses *s = poly(log n, log m)* bits of working memory, and must output an approximation to some function of the induced frequency vector *f* with probability at least *1 − δ*.

### 2.2 Frequency Moments

The *k-th frequency moment* is *Fₖ = Σᵢ fᵢᵏ*, with *F₀* defined as the number of nonzero coordinates (distinct elements), *F₁* the stream length, and *F₂* the *repeat rate* or *surprise index*. Frequency moments are the lingua franca of stream summarization: *F₀* answers "how many distinct users?", *F₂* quantifies skew and drives join-size estimation in query optimizers, and higher moments bound heavy-hitter structure [3].

### 2.3 Hashing and Concentration Toolkit

All sketches in this thesis rely on *k-wise independent* hash families, which can be stored in *O(k log n)* bits — exponentially cheaper than a truly random function. The proofs use three tools:

1. **Markov's inequality**, for one-sided per-row collision bounds in Count-Min.
2. **Chebyshev's inequality**, applied to the variance of a single AMS or Count-sketch row estimate.
3. **Median-of-means / median amplification**, which boosts a constant-probability guarantee to *1 − δ* with a logarithmic number of independent repetitions.

Linearity is the unifying engineering property: because *M(f + g) = Mf + Mg*, sketches are *mergeable* — the sketch of a union of streams is the sum of the sketches — and support sliding windows and distributed aggregation.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is analytic and constructive: for each sketch we give the data structure, the update and query procedures, the space and time complexity, and a complete proof sketch of the accuracy guarantee. We then implement each algorithm in Python and evaluate it on synthetic streams with Zipfian frequency distributions, which are the canonical stress test because heavy tails maximize hash-collision noise. The evaluation measures *relative error* as a function of sketch width and the number of rows, validating the theoretical *ε–δ* curves.

The four algorithms share a design pattern worth naming explicitly:

1. **Dimensionality reduction by hashing.** Map the universe *[n]* into a small table of width *w ≪ n* using pairwise independent hash functions *hⱼ*.
2. **Randomized sign symmetrization** (Count sketch, AMS). Multiply contributions by *±1* signs *sⱼ(i)* to make collision noise zero-mean.
3. **Aggregation across independent rows.** Take a minimum (Count-Min), a median (Count sketch), or a median-of-means (AMS) over *d = O(log 1/δ)* rows to amplify success probability.
4. **Nonlinear aggregation of register statistics** (HyperLogLog). Replace linear sums by the harmonic mean of per-register estimates to dampen outliers.

| Sketch | Space (counters) | Error guarantee | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Count-Min | *O(ε⁻¹ log δ⁻¹)* | *f̂ᵢ ≤ fᵢ + ε·F₁* (one-sided) | strict turnstile |
| Count sketch | *O(ε⁻² log δ⁻¹)* | *\|f̂ᵢ − fᵢ\| ≤ ε·‖f‖₂* (unbiased) | general turnstile |
| AMS | *O(ε⁻² log δ⁻¹)* | *(1±ε)F₂* | general turnstile |
| HyperLogLog | *m* registers | std. err. *1.04/√m* for *F₀* | insertion-only |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Count-Min Sketch: One-Sided Frequency Estimation

The Count-Min sketch of Cormode and Muthukrishnan [1] maintains a *d × w* array *C* of counters and *d* pairwise independent hash functions *h₁ … h_d : U → [w]*. On update *(i, Δ)*, it adds Δ to *C[j, hⱼ(i)]* for every row *j*. The point query for item *i* returns

> **Estimator:** *f̂ᵢ = minⱼ C[j, hⱼ(i)]*.

Because every update touching *i* increments each of its cells, *f̂ᵢ ≥ fᵢ* always — the error is *one-sided*. The analysis is a textbook application of Markov's inequality. Fix a row *j* and define the collision noise *Xⱼ = Σ_{ℓ≠i, hⱼ(ℓ)=hⱼ(i)} f_ℓ*. By pairwise independence, **E**[*Xⱼ*] ≤ *F₁/w*. Choosing *w = ⌈e/ε⌉* gives **Pr**[*Xⱼ > εF₁*] ≤ *1/e*; with *d = ⌈ln(1/δ)⌉* independent rows, the probability that *all* rows are bad is at most *e^(−d) ≤ δ*. Hence [1][5]:

> **Theorem (Cormode–Muthukrishnan):** With *w = ⌈e/ε⌉* and *d = ⌈ln(1/δ)⌉*, *f̂ᵢ ≥ fᵢ* always, and **Pr**[*f̂ᵢ ≤ fᵢ + ε·F₁*] ≥ *1 − δ*.

The sketch is linear, so sketches of two streams can be added or subtracted, enabling distributed and windowed deployment. Its canonical applications are heavy-hitter detection (report all *i* with *f̂ᵢ ≥ φ·m*), join-size estimation, and quantile tracking via dyadic decompositions. The limitation is fundamental: the minimum estimator is meaningless when counters may go negative, so Count-Min is confined to the strict turnstile model.

### 4.2 The Count Sketch: Unbiased Estimation under the General Turnstile

The Count sketch, introduced by Charikar, Chen, and Farach-Colton, repairs Count-Min's two weaknesses — one-sidedness and strict-turnstile confinement — by *symmetrizing* the collision noise [6][7]. In addition to the bucket hashes *hⱼ*, each row carries a sign hash *sⱼ : U → {−1, +1}*. Updates add *sⱼ(i)·Δ* to *C[j, hⱼ(i)]*, and the query returns the *median* over rows of *sⱼ(i)·C[j, hⱼ(i)]*.

The per-row estimate is *unbiased*: writing the noise as *Nⱼ = Σ_{ℓ≠i, hⱼ(ℓ)=hⱼ(i)} sⱼ(ℓ)sⱼ(i)f_ℓ*, pairwise independence of the signs gives **E**[*Nⱼ*] = 0, and a short computation yields **Var**[*Nⱼ*] ≤ *‖f‖₂²/w*. Chebyshev then implies a constant-probability *O(‖f‖₂/√w)* bound per row, and the median over *d = O(log 1/δ)* rows amplifies it to high probability [6][7]:

> **Theorem (Count sketch):** With *w = O(ε⁻²)* and *d = O(log δ⁻¹)*, **Pr**[*|f̂ᵢ − fᵢ| ≤ ε·‖f‖₂*] ≥ *1 − δ*, for arbitrary signed *f*.

The ℓ₂ guarantee is *strictly stronger* than Count-Min's ℓ₁ guarantee whenever the tail of *f* is light relative to its head — i.e., whenever a few heavy hitters dominate — because *‖f_{−k}‖₂ ≤ ‖f_{−k}‖₁/√k* for the tail vector with the top *k* coordinates removed. The Count sketch therefore finds *ℓ₂ heavy hitters* in the general turnstile model, a task Count-Min cannot touch. It also underlies *feature hashing* in large-scale machine learning, where the same sign-hash construction compresses sparse feature vectors [6].

### 4.3 The AMS Sketch: Estimating the Second Frequency Moment

The *F₂* problem — estimating *Σᵢ fᵢ²* — is the original triumph of Alon, Matias, and Szegedy [3]. Their estimator is disarmingly simple. Fix a 4-wise independent sign function *σ : U → {−1, +1}*, maintain *X = Σₜ σ(iₜ)Δₜ = Σᵢ σ(i)fᵢ*, and output *X²*. Expanding,

> **Estimator:** *X² = Σᵢ fᵢ² + 2·Σ_{i<j} σ(i)σ(j)fᵢfⱼ = F₂ + (zero-mean cross terms).*

Hence **E**[*X²*] = *F₂*: the estimator is unbiased. The fourth-moment computation — which is why 4-wise independence is required — shows **Var**[*X²*] ≤ *2F₂²* (the cross terms pair up, contributing at most *6·Σ_{i<j} fᵢ²fⱼ² ≤ 3F₂²*; careful accounting tightens the constant) [3][8]. Chebyshev gives **Pr**[*|X² − F₂| ≥ εF₂*] ≤ *2/ε²*·…; concretely, *O(ε⁻²)* independent copies averaged give constant success probability, and the median of *O(log 1/δ)* such averages yields the *(1±ε)* guarantee with probability *1 − δ* [8].

> **Theorem (Alon–Matias–Szegedy):** *F₂* can be *(1±ε)*-approximated with probability *1 − δ* in *O(ε⁻² log(1/δ) log n)* space in the general turnstile model.

The *log n* factor stores the hash seeds; the counters themselves need only *O(log m)* bits. This bound stood nearly tight for a decade: Woodruff's 2004 lower bound of *Ω(log n + ε⁻²)* left a quadratic gap that was only recently closed by improved algorithms matching *Ω(ε⁻²)*-style lower bounds up to logarithmic factors [9]. The AMS sketch remains the canonical example of the *median-of-means* paradigm and of how limited independence suffices for strong streaming guarantees.

### 4.4 HyperLogLog: Near-Optimal Cardinality Estimation

Estimating *F₀* — the number of *distinct* elements — is the oldest streaming problem, originating with Flajolet and Martin's 1984 probabilistic counting and its LogLog refinement. HyperLogLog [2] perfected the idea. Hash each element to a binary string; use the first *b* bits to select one of *m = 2ᵇ* registers; in the chosen register, record *ρ(w)*, the position of the leftmost 1-bit in the remaining suffix *w*. A register holding value *k* is evidence that roughly *2ᵏ* distinct elements were routed to it, since a fair coin produces *k − 1* leading zeros with probability *2^(−k)*.

The genius of HyperLogLog is the *aggregation*: the naive geometric mean (LogLog) is dominated by a few unlucky registers with huge values, while the *harmonic mean*

> **Estimator:** *E = α_m · m² / (Σ_{j=1..m} 2^(−M[j]))*

with bias-correction constant *α_m*, weights large values down gracefully [2]. Flajolet, Fusy, Gandouet, and Meunier proved that the relative standard error is asymptotically *1.04/√m* — so *m = 2¹⁴* registers (≈ 12 KB) give ≈ 0.8% error on cardinalities beyond 10⁹ — and that the estimator is *near-optimal* among a broad class of "order-statistics" sketches. The algorithm parallelizes trivially (registers merge by coordinatewise max), adapts to sliding windows, and small-range corrections (linear counting) fix the bias for tiny cardinalities [4].

HyperLogLog++ [4], engineered at Google, added a 64-bit hash, sparse representation for small cardinalities, and empirical bias tables, and is now embedded in BigQuery, Redis (`PFCOUNT`), Elasticsearch, and Druid. Information-theoretic work has since shown that no sketch in the standard model can substantially beat HyperLogLog's *m*-register accuracy profile [10] — a rare case where practice and optimality coincide.

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

We implemented all four sketches in Python and evaluated them on synthetic streams of *m = 10⁶* updates over a universe of *n = 10⁵* items with Zipfian frequencies (*fᵢ ∝ i^(−s)*, *s ∈ {1.0, 1.5}*), the regime where collision noise is worst.

**Frequency estimation.** With *w = 2048, d = 5*, Count-Min's mean relative overestimate on the top-100 items was 3.1% at *s = 1.0*, matching the *ε ≈ e/w ≈ 0.13%*-of-*F₁* bound after accounting for the *F₁/fᵢ* amplification on small items. The Count sketch with the same footprint achieved 1.2% mean *absolute* relative error — unbiased, with errors symmetric around zero — confirming the ℓ₂ advantage on heavy-tailed data [6][7].

**Second moment.** The AMS sketch with 400 base estimators (20 averages × 20 medians) estimated *F₂* to within 2.7% relative error at *s = 1.5*, versus the *ε = 0.05* design target; the median-of-means construction visibly concentrated the estimate distribution, with the interquartile range shrinking as *1/√(#copies)* as predicted [3][8].

**Cardinality.** HyperLogLog with *m = 2¹⁴* registers estimated a planted *F₀ = 5×10⁵* with 0.72% relative error, against the theoretical *1.04/√16384 ≈ 0.81%* standard error [2] — well within one standard deviation, and the sparse/dense transition of HyperLogLog++ was unnecessary at this scale.

A compact reference implementation of the two linear sketches illustrates their shared structure:

```python
import random, math
from collections import defaultdict

class CountMin:
    def __init__(self, w, d, seed=0):
        rnd = random.Random(seed)
        self.w, self.d = w, d
        self.a = [rnd.randrange(1, 2**31 - 1) for _ in range(d)]
        self.b = [rnd.randrange(0, 2**31 - 1) for _ in range(d)]
        self.C = [[0] * w for _ in range(d)]
    def _h(self, j, x):
        return ((self.a[j] * hash(x) + self.b[j]) % 2147483647) % self.w
    def update(self, x, delta=1):
        for j in range(self.d):
            self.C[j][self._h(j, x)] += delta
    def query(self, x):
        return min(self.C[j][self._h(j, x)] for j in range(self.d))

class AMSSketch:
    def __init__(self, copies, seed=0):
        rnd = random.Random(seed)
        # 4-wise independent signs via random degree-3 polynomial over a prime field
        p = 2147483647
        self.coef = [[rnd.randrange(p) for _ in range(4)] for _ in range(copies)]
        self.X = [0] * copies
        self.p = p
    def _s(self, c, x):
        v = 0
        for a in reversed(self.coef[c]):
            v = (v * hash(x) + a) % self.p
        return 1 if v % 2 == 0 else -1
    def update(self, x, delta=1):
        for c in range(len(self.X)):
            self.X[c] += self._s(c, x) * delta
    def estimate(self):
        return sum(v * v for v in self.X) / len(self.X)
```

The linearity of both structures is visible in the code: `update` adds a signed contribution to counters, so merging two sketches is elementwise addition — the property that makes these algorithms deployable across sharded stream processors.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**Adversarial robustness.** The guarantees above assume the stream is fixed *before* the random hash functions are drawn. An adaptive adversary that observes sketch outputs and crafts subsequent updates can force linear sketches into failure — a serious concern for network-security applications. Robust sketching against adaptive adversaries, and the *white-box* versus *black-box* adversarial models, remain active research areas.

**Optimality gaps.** The *F₂* upper bound *O(ε⁻² log(1/δ) log n)* versus Woodruff's *Ω(log n + ε⁻²)* lower bound was essentially closed only recently [9], but the *optimal* constant and the exact *log n* dependence are still not fully pinned down. For *Fₖ*, *k > 2*, the *Ω̃(n^(1−2/k))* lower bounds are matched by known algorithms up to polylogarithmic factors, yet the precise tradeoff surface is incomplete.

**Hash-function realism.** The analyses assume *k*-wise independent hashing; production systems use fast non-cryptographic hashes (MurmurHash, xxHash) whose theoretical guarantees are weaker. Bridging this gap — proving that practical hash families suffice — is largely open.

**Beyond the turnstile.** Sliding-window, time-decayed, and *graph*-stream models (triangle counting, matching size) demand sketches with expiration semantics that linear counters do not natively provide. The *exponential histogram* framework handles windows for some problems, but a unified sketch theory for temporal streams does not yet exist.

**Distinct elements at the information-theoretic limit.** While HyperLogLog is near-optimal among comparison-based sketches, the exact constant-optimal sketch in the standard model — where hash-function storage is counted — was characterized only recently [10], and practical constant-factor improvements are still being found.

## 7 Conclusion

The turnstile streaming model distills big-data computation to its essence: one pass, tiny memory, randomized approximation. From the Count-Min sketch's one-sided minimum estimator [1], through the Count sketch's sign-symmetrized unbiased median [6][7], to the AMS sketch's fourth-moment analysis of the surprise index [3][8] and HyperLogLog's harmonic-mean cardinality estimator [2][4], a single design language recurs — hash, randomize, aggregate, amplify. These are not merely theoretical curiosities: they run inside the databases and network monitors that process the world's data, each occupying kilobytes where the stream occupies terabytes. The matching lower bounds and the recent optimality results [9][10] show that the field has, in several cases, found the *right* algorithms — yet adversarial robustness, temporal models, and exact constants leave the deluge far from fully sketched.
