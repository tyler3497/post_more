# The Conformal Bootstrap: Crossing Symmetry, Semidefinite Programming Bounds, and the 3D Ising Critical Point

## Abstract

The conformal bootstrap is a non-perturbative program that determines the data of a conformal field theory — operator dimensions, spins, and operator product expansion coefficients — from the rigid requirements of conformal symmetry, crossing symmetry of four-point functions, and unitarity alone. We survey the modern formulation of the bootstrap: the conformal block decomposition of scalar correlators, the crossing equations as linear constraints on OPE data, Rychkov's linear programming method for bounding operator dimensions, and the reformulation as a polynomial matrix program solvable by the arbitrary-precision SDPB solver. The crowning application is the three-dimensional Ising conformal field theory, where the bootstrap has localized the leading scaling dimensions to an isolated island at (Δσ, Δε) = (0.5181489(10), 1.412625(10)), with precision exceeding every Monte Carlo and perturbative renormalization-group determination in history. We discuss mixed-correlator systems that isolate the theory completely, the O(N) vector models, and the complementary analytic bootstrap of large-spin double-twist operators. The article is self-contained at the level of a physics graduate student familiar with quantum field theory.

## 1 Introduction

Most of quantum field theory is studied perturbatively: one begins from a free fixed point, introduces a coupling, and computes observables as a series in that coupling. This strategy fails spectacularly at strongly coupled fixed points, where no small parameter exists. The three-dimensional Ising critical point — governing water at its liquid-vapor critical point, binary fluid mixtures, and uniaxial magnets — is precisely such a theory: the Wilson-Fisher fixed point sits at coupling of order unity, and perturbation theory in the epsilon expansion is an asymptotic series requiring sophisticated resummation [4].

The conformal bootstrap offers an entirely different route. Its premise, formulated in the 1970s by Polyakov and collaborators and revived in 2008 by Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi [2], is that the axioms of conformal field theory — conformal invariance, the existence of an operator product expansion (OPE) with convergent radius, crossing symmetry, and unitarity — are so restrictive that they nearly determine the theory's data outright. Rather than constructing the theory from a Lagrangian, the bootstrap *carves out* the space of consistent theories by excluding what the axioms forbid. The surviving region, at sufficient numerical precision, collapses to a tiny island: the 3D Ising conformal field theory (CFT), characterized to world-record precision [7].

This article develops the full machinery. Section 2 reviews conformal kinematics and the OPE. Section 3 derives the crossing equations and the linear and semidefinite programming formulations that make them numerically tractable. Section 4 presents the deep dive: the numerical bootstrap for the 3D Ising model, the mixed-correlator revolution, and the analytic bootstrap at large spin. Section 5 collects the precision results, and Section 6 discusses the limitations of the program.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Conformal symmetry and primary operators

A conformal field theory in d spacetime dimensions is a quantum field theory invariant under the conformal group SO(d+1,1), which includes translations, rotations, dilatations, and special conformal transformations. Local operators are organized into representations of this group labeled by a scaling dimension Δ and a spin l. A *primary operator* O(x) transforms covariantly under conformal transformations; all other operators (*descendants*) are derivatives of primaries and carry no independent dynamical information.

Unitarity imposes lower bounds on dimensions. In a unitary CFT, scalar primaries satisfy Δ ≥ (d-2)/2, with equality for a free field, and spinning operators satisfy the *unitarity bound*

> **Theorem:** In a unitary CFT in d dimensions, a spin-l primary with l > 0 obeys Δ ≥ l + d − 2. Operators saturating the bound are conserved currents, ∂·J = 0.

The complete dynamical content of a CFT — its *CFT data* — consists of the spectrum {Δi, li} of primary operators and the OPE coefficients λijk appearing in three-point functions. The bootstrap program aims to constrain or determine this data without ever writing down a path integral.

### 2.2 The operator product expansion

The operator product expansion asserts that the product of two local operators at nearby points can be expanded in a complete set of local operators:

φ(x1) φ(x2) = Σk λφφk |x12|^(Δk − 2Δφ) [1 + descendants] Ok(x2).

Crucially, in a CFT the OPE converges whenever x1 and x2 can be separated from all other insertions by a sphere — a theorem of Mack and, in modern form, Pappadopulo, Rychkov, Espin, and Rattazzi. This convergence is what elevates crossing symmetry from a formal identity to a numerically powerful constraint.

### 2.3 Four-point functions and conformal blocks

Conformal invariance fixes the coordinate dependence of a four-point function of identical scalars up to a function g(u, v) of the two conformal cross ratios u = (x12²x34²)/(x13²x24²) and v = (x14²x23²)/(x13²x24²). Inserting the OPE in the (12)(34) channel yields the *conformal block decomposition*

g(u, v) = ΣO λ²φφO gΔ,l(u, v),

where gΔ,l(u, v) is the *conformal block*: the universal contribution of a primary of dimension Δ and spin l together with all its descendants. Dolan and Osborn derived closed-form expressions for these blocks, and their efficient numerical evaluation via recursion relations underpins every modern bootstrap computation [1].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The crossing equation

The same four-point function can be expanded in the (14)(23) channel by pairing the operators differently. Equality of the two expansions — *crossing symmetry* — gives, after stripping a conventional prefactor,

ΣO λ²φφO FΔ,l(u, v) = 0, where FΔ,l(u, v) = v^Δφ gΔ,l(u, v) − u^Δφ gΔ,l(v, u).

This is the crossing equation: a single functional equation encoding infinitely many constraints. The unknowns are the CFT data {Δ, l, λ²}; the functions FΔ,l are known. Unitarity requires λ²φφO ≥ 0 for all exchanged operators, with the identity operator contributing λ² = 1 at Δ = l = 0.

### 3.2 The linear programming bound

Rychkov's insight [2][3] was to turn crossing into an optimization problem. Choose a linear functional α acting on functions of the cross ratios, normalized so that α(F0,0) = 1 on the identity contribution. If one can find α such that α(FΔ,l) ≥ 0 for every operator allowed by a hypothetical spectrum, then applying α to the crossing equation gives 1 + Σ λ² α(FΔ,l) = 0, a contradiction — so the hypothetical spectrum is ruled out.

In practice, α is taken as a linear combination of derivatives evaluated at the crossing-symmetric point z = z̄ = 1/2 (with u = zz̄, v = (1−z)(1−z̄)), truncated to some finite derivative order Λ. The positivity conditions α(FΔ,l) ≥ 0 for all Δ above a gap and all spins become constraints on the finitely many coefficients of α. Searching over α is a *linear program* after discretizing Δ, and its feasibility directly bounds the dimension of the lowest scalar: if no functional exists for an assumed gap Δε, that gap is excluded.

> **Theorem:** Let S(Δgap) be the set of unitary spectra in which all scalars other than φ and the identity have Δ ≥ Δgap. If there exists a functional α with α(F0,0) > 0 and α(FΔ,l) ≥ 0 for all (Δ, l) in S(Δgap), then no unitary CFT exists with scalar gap Δgap.

### 3.3 Semidefinite programming reformulation

The linear programming approach suffers from a discretization artifact: positivity must hold for a continuum of Δ. Simmons-Duffin resolved this by exploiting the analytic structure of conformal blocks [6]. The derivatives of FΔ,l at the symmetric point can be written as a positive prefactor times polynomials in Δ (for each fixed spin l), using the pole structure of blocks in Δ. The bootstrap constraints then become:

Find y such that P⁰l(x) + Σn yn Pⁿl(x) ⪰ 0 for all x ≥ 0 and all l,

where x parametrizes the dimension above the unitarity bound and the Pⁿl are polynomial matrices. This is a *polynomial matrix program*, a class of semidefinite programs. The SDPB solver — parallelized, arbitrary-precision, and specialized to this structure — evaluates such programs orders of magnitude faster than general-purpose solvers, enabling derivative orders Λ = 43 and beyond where the original approach stalled at Λ ∼ 20 [6].

    #    P_l(x) = P0_l(x) + sum_n y_n Pn_l(x) >= 0, x >= 0

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The 3D Ising kink and the birth of the island

Applying the single-correlator bootstrap to ⟨σσσσ⟩ in three dimensions with external dimension Δσ produces an upper bound on the dimension Δε of the lowest Z₂-even scalar. The resulting bound curve Δε(Δσ) exhibits a sharp *kink* — a sudden change of slope — at Δσ ≃ 0.518 [4]. The kink sits precisely where independent Monte Carlo and epsilon-expansion estimates place the Ising model, strongly suggesting that the 3D Ising CFT saturates the bound: it is the *extremal* theory, the one pushed hardest against the axioms.

El-Showk, Paulos, Poland, Rychkov, Simmons-Duffin, and Vichi exploited this in 2012 by imposing an additional physical assumption — that σ and ε are the *only* relevant operators (Δ < 3) — and scanning the allowed region. The kink sharpened into a small isolated *island* in the (Δσ, Δε) plane: a closed allowed region surrounded on all sides by exclusion [4]. As the derivative order Λ increased, the island shrank dramatically, converging toward a point.

![OPE and crossing symmetry diagram](public/thesis/ths_1789515128383_19280-0.webp)

### 4.2 Mixed correlators: bootstrapping the full system

The single correlator ⟨σσσσ⟩ leaves a residual degeneracy: the island's size is limited by the assumption about the spectrum. Kos, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin removed this limitation by bootstrapping the *mixed system* of correlators ⟨σσσσ⟩, ⟨σσεε⟩, and ⟨εεεε⟩ simultaneously [5]. Crossing symmetry of the mixed four-point function ⟨σσεε⟩ relates the OPE coefficients λσσε, λεεε, and λσσσ = 0 (by Z₂ symmetry) through a matrix of conformal blocks, and unitarity demands that the matrix of squared OPE coefficients be positive semidefinite — a strictly stronger condition than positivity of each coefficient separately.

The mixed system yields a *three-dimensional* exclusion plot in the space (Δσ, Δε, λεεε/λσσε), and with the single assumption that σ and ε are the only relevant scalars, the allowed region collapses to a tiny island with no free parameters at all. Kos, Poland, Simmons-Duffin, and Vichi then scanned over the OPE coefficient ratio, incorporating the physical fact that only one operator sits at each dimension, obtaining [7]

| Quantity | Bootstrap value |
|---|---|
| Δσ | 0.5181489(10) |
| Δε | 1.412625(10) |
| λσσ ε | 1.0518537(41) |
| λεε ε | 1.532435(19) |

These remain the most precise determinations of the 3D Ising critical exponents in existence — roughly two orders of magnitude tighter than the best Monte Carlo results — achieved with no lattice, no Feynman diagrams, and no resummation.

### 4.3 The O(N) vector models and universality

The same machinery extends to CFTs with continuous global symmetry. Bootstrapping the four-point function of the O(N) fundamental scalar φi produces bounds on the leading singlet and traceless-symmetric scalars, with kinks identifying the O(N) Wilson-Fisher fixed points for N = 2 (the XY / superfluid helium universality class) and N = 3 (the Heisenberg magnet) [5][7]. The precision islands give

- O(2): (Δφ, Δs) = (0.519088(17), 1.51136(22)),
- O(3): (Δφ, Δs) = (0.51928(32), 1.5957(28)),

in agreement with high-precision Monte Carlo and Borel-resummed perturbation theory, but obtained purely from symmetry and consistency. The agreement across methods constitutes one of the strongest validations of the bootstrap program: three entirely independent non-perturbative techniques converging on the same numbers.

### 4.4 The analytic bootstrap and large-spin perturbation theory

Complementary to the numerics, the *analytic bootstrap* studies crossing symmetry in the lightcone limit u ≪ v ≪ 1, where the crossed channel is dominated by the identity and the direct channel by large-spin operators. Fitzpatrick, Kaplan, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin showed that consistency demands infinite towers of *double-twist* operators with twists τn,l = 2Δφ + 2n + γ(n, l), where the anomalous dimensions γ(n, l) decay as inverse powers of a conformal spin variable [8]. In AdS language, these are weakly bound two-particle states orbiting at large angular momentum, and their binding energies are computed by tree-level graviton and gauge-boson exchange — a direct CFT derivation of bulk locality.

Komargodski and Zhiboedov proved general convexity and monotonicity theorems for these large-spin trajectories from crossing and unitarity alone [9], establishing that the analytic bootstrap is not merely an approximation scheme but a source of rigorous asymptotic theorems about every CFT.

### 4.5 The extremal functional method

Beyond ruling spectra out, the bootstrap can *read off* spectra. When a bound is saturated — as at the Ising kink — the optimal functional α* of the dual program has double zeros precisely at the dimensions of operators in the extremal spectrum. El-Showk and Paulos developed this *extremal functional method* into a practical spectroscopy tool: the zeros of α* reveal the low-lying operator dimensions and OPE coefficients of the theory living at the kink, reconstructing the 3D Ising spectrum operator by operator from pure consistency conditions.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The numerical results of the bootstrap program are best appreciated as a table of determinations that improved monotonically with computational investment:

1. **2008 — the first bound.** Rattazzi et al. prove that in any 4D unitary CFT, the lowest-dimension scalar in the φ × φ OPE satisfies Δ ≤ 2 + 0.7√(Δφ − 1) + 2.1(Δφ − 1) + 0.43(Δφ − 1)^(3/2), the first rigorous non-perturbative bound of its kind [2].
2. **2012 — the 3D Ising island.** With Λ = 20 derivatives, El-Showk et al. obtain Δσ = 0.5182(2), Δε = 1.413(1) [4], already competitive with the best Monte Carlo of the era.
3. **2014 — mixed correlators.** Kos, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin shrink the island by an order of magnitude using the ⟨σσσσ⟩, ⟨σσεε⟩, ⟨εεεε⟩ system [5].
4. **2015 — SDPB.** The semidefinite program solver pushes to Λ = 43 with 1024-bit precision, giving Δσ = 0.518151(6), Δε = 1.41264(6) from the single correlator alone [6].
5. **2016 — precision islands.** Scanning over OPE coefficient ratios, Kos et al. reach Δσ = 0.5181489(10), Δε = 1.412625(10): relative precision of two parts per million on Δσ, the sharpest known determination of any strongly coupled critical exponent [7].

A key structural result deserves emphasis: these are *rigorous* exclusion statements at each finite Λ (up to controlled numerical error), and the bounds improve monotonically as Λ increases. The convergence of the island's center across methods — single correlator, mixed correlator, extremal functional — and its agreement with Monte Carlo constitute the empirical proof that the bootstrap is genuinely solving the theory, not merely bounding it.

## 6 Limitations

The bootstrap program, for all its successes, faces significant limitations that define the current research frontier.

- **No Lagrangian, no dynamics.** The bootstrap constrains CFT data but does not explain *why* a particular theory exists or provide a microscopic derivation of the fixed point. It is a theory of consistency, not of origin.
- **Computational cost.** High-precision islands require SDPB runs with thousands of bits of precision and derivative orders above 40, consuming millions of CPU hours. Each new correlator system multiplies the cost, and spinning correlators (needed for the stress tensor or conserved currents) are substantially harder.
- **Assumption dependence.** The sharpest islands assume knowledge of the low-lying spectrum — e.g., that σ and ε are the only relevant operators. Without such input, bounds are weaker; the program rules out, but isolating a theory requires physical insight about which operators to assume.
- **Limited scope of analytic control.** The analytic bootstrap governs large spin rigorously, but the phenomenologically crucial low-spin data (Δσ, Δε themselves) remains accessible only numerically. A fully analytic solution of the 3D Ising CFT is not in sight.
- **Beyond unitarity.** Most bootstrap technology assumes reflection positivity. Non-unitary theories — the Lee-Yang edge singularity, disordered systems, de Sitter holography — require the *gluing* of positivity differently, and the numerical bootstrap there is far less developed.
- **Higher dimensions and gauge theories.** Bootstrapping 4D gauge theories like QCD at its conformal window, or 6D (2,0) theories, remains extremely challenging: the relevant correlators involve spinning operators with complicated tensor structures, and the computational cost grows steeply.

## 7 Conclusion

The conformal bootstrap has transformed our understanding of what quantum field theory can determine without perturbation theory. From Rychkov's linear programming bounds [2][3] through the SDPB revolution [6] to the precision Ising island [7], the program has produced the most accurate critical exponents in physics from nothing but symmetry, crossing, and unitarity. The mixed-correlator bootstrap [5] demonstrated that the axioms can *isolate* a theory completely, while the analytic bootstrap [8][9] revealed the universal large-spin structure hidden in every CFT.

The deepest lesson is philosophical as much as technical: the space of consistent quantum field theories appears to be remarkably rigid, with isolated theories like the 3D Ising CFT sitting at kinks and corners of the allowed region, as if the axioms themselves select the theories realized in nature. Whether this rigidity extends to carve out the Standard Model's own strongly coupled sectors — or quantum gravity via the holographic bootstrap — is the open challenge of the coming decade. The bootstrap has shown that consistency is far more constraining than anyone dared to hope; the task now is to find out how far that constraint reaches.
