# Byzantine-Resilient Federated Learning: Trimmed Mean, Krum, and Bulyan Aggregation Under Label-Flipping Adversaries

## Abstract
Federated learning enables collaborative training across decentralized clients without sharing raw data, yet its reliance on client-supplied gradients exposes it to Byzantine failures where malicious participants craft arbitrary updates. This thesis provides a systematic treatment of Byzantine-resilient aggregation for federated learning under label-flipping adversaries, focusing on three canonical robust operators: coordinate-wise Trimmed Mean, Krum and its Multi-Krum variant, and the two-stage Bulyan meta-aggregator. We formalize the federated threat model, quantify the poisoning leeway of each aggregator in high-dimensional parameter spaces, and derive convergence guarantees under $f$ Byzantine clients among $n$ total participants. We dissect how label-flipping manifests as a data-poisoning attack that induces biased but statistically plausible gradients, contrasting it with model-poisoning and sign-flipping attacks. Through analytical comparison, we show that Trimmed Mean achieves order-optimal statistical rates when $f < n/2$ but suffers $\Omega(f/n)$ bias under adaptive adversaries, that Krum guarantees $(\alpha,f)$-Byzantine resilience at the cost of using a single update per round, and that Bulyan reduces the dimensional leeway from $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ to $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ while requiring $n \ge 4f+3$. We synthesize empirical protocols, proof sketches, and implementation considerations in Python, Rust, and TLA+, highlighting trade-offs for non-IID data and providing guidelines for deploying robust federated systems.

## 1 Introduction
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for privacy-preserving collaborative machine learning [1][2]. In its canonical form, a central server orchestrates training by broadcasting a global model $w_t$ to $n$ clients, each computing a local gradient or model delta $V_i$ on private data $D_i$, and aggregating via Federated Averaging. The assumption of *honest-but-curious* clients fails in open, cross-device deployments where clients may be compromised, buggy, or incentivized to degrade model utility. Such behavior is modeled as *Byzantine* failures, where up to $f$ clients can send *arbitrary* vectors $V_i^* \in \mathbb{R}^d$ [3][4].

A particularly insidious yet practical class of Byzantine behavior is the **label-flipping attack**, a data-poisoning strategy in which the adversary permutes labels in its local dataset, e.g., $y \mapsto (y+1) \mod C$ or $y \mapsto C-1-y$, while faithfully executing the learning algorithm [5]. Unlike model-poisoning, label-flipping produces consistent updates harder to detect.

This thesis examines three foundational Byzantine-resilient aggregation rules under label-flipping:

- **Coordinate-wise Trimmed Mean** [3], which discards $\beta$ fraction extremes per dimension,
- **Krum** [4], which selects the gradient minimizing the sum of distances to its $n-f-2$ nearest neighbors,
- **Bulyan** [6], which combines iterative Krum selection with coordinate-wise median to bound dimensional leeway.

We argue that understanding their interplay is crucial for modern FL security. Trimmed Mean is statistically optimal but vulnerable to *a little is enough* attacks [7]. Krum is provably resilient but inefficient in benign settings. Bulyan achieves strong resilience at higher sample complexity. Label-flipping sits at the intersection: it is weak enough to be mitigated by trimmed statistics, yet structured enough to exploit non-IID heterogeneity.

Our contributions are:

1. Unified formalism of Byzantine FL with label-flipping threat model.
2. Comparative analysis of Trimmed Mean, Krum, Multi-Krum, and Bulyan with resilience proofs and dimensional leeway bounds.
3. Empirical design for evaluating under MNIST, CIFAR-10, and FEMNIST non-IID partitions.
4. Implementation artifacts and limitations for production FL.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Federated Learning Model
We consider synchronous FL with $n$ clients and a trusted server. At round $t$, server broadcasts $w_t$. Each honest client $i$ computes $V_i = \nabla F_i(w_t)$ where $F_i$ is local empirical loss. Server aggregates $w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta_t \cdot Aggr(\{V_i\}_{i=1}^n)$. Standard $Aggr = Mean$ is optimal when $f=0$, achieving linear speedup $O(1/n)$ [1].

*Byzantine clients* $B$, $|B| = f$, may send $V_i^* \neq \nabla F_i(w_t)$, arbitrarily chosen, potentially colluding and omniscient of honest updates [4][6].

> **Theorem: Byzantine Resilience Definition (Blanchard et al. 2017):** An aggregation rule $Aggr$ is $(\alpha,f)$-Byzantine resilient if for any input vectors where $f$ are Byzantine, the output $Aggr$ satisfies (i) $\langle \mathbb{E}[Aggr], g \rangle \ge (1-\sin\alpha) \|g\|^2$ where $g = \mathbb{E}[\nabla F]$, and (ii) moments of $Aggr$ are bounded by honest moments. This implies convergence of SGD despite Byzantine workers when $\alpha < \pi/2$ [4].

### 2.2 Label-Flipping Attack Taxonomy
Label-flipping is categorized as *data poisoning* [5][8]:

- **Static Label-Flipping (SLF):** Flip label $c_{src} \to c_{target}$ for all samples of class $c_{src}$.
- **Dynamic Label-Flipping (DLF):** Adversary flips labels based on confidence, e.g., flipping only low-confidence samples to maximize loss.
- **Symmetric vs. Asymmetric:** Symmetric randomizes labels uniformly; asymmetric targets specific class pairs to induce targeted misclassification.

In federated settings, even $f/n = 0.1$ label-flipping clients can drop global accuracy from $94\%$ to $<70\%$ on CIFAR-10 under FedAvg [8]. Unlike Gaussian noise attacks, label-flipping preserves gradient norm distribution, evading simple norm clipping.

### 2.3 Related Robust Aggregation

| Aggregator | Resilience Condition | Breakdown | Complexity | Leeway in $d$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mean | $f=0$ | $0$ | $O(nd)$ | $\infty$ |
| Coordinate-wise Median [3] | $f < n/2$ | $0.5$ | $O(nd \log n)$ | $O(\sqrt{d})$ |
| Trimmed Mean [3] | $f < n/2$ | $\beta$ | $O(nd \log n)$ | $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ [6][7] |
| Krum [4] | $2f+2 < n$ | $(n-2)/2$ | $O(n^2 d)$ | $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ |
| Multi-Krum [4] | $2f+2 < n$ | $m = n-f-2$ | $O(n^2 d)$ | $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ |
| Bulyan [6] | $4f+3 \le n$ | $0.25$ | $O(n^2 d)$ | $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ |

*Table: Comparison of Byzantine-resilient aggregators. Breakdown is maximal $f/n$ tolerated; leeway denotes adversary's ability to shift output within variance bound in high dimensions.*

**Key insights:**

- **Trimmed Mean** removes $\beta = f/n$ largest and smallest values per coordinate, then averages remainder. Yin et al. prove order-optimal error $O(f/(n\sqrt{k}) + 1/\sqrt{kn})$ for strongly convex losses [3].
- **Krum** computes score $s(i) = \sum_{i \to j} \|V_i - V_j\|^2$ over $n-f-2$ nearest neighbors, selects $i^* = \arg\min s(i)$. Multi-Krum averages top $m$ lowest scores for better utility [4].
- **Bulyan** iteratively applies Krum to select $\theta = n-2f$ gradients, then applies coordinate-wise trimmed mean (or median) on selected set. Mhamdi et al. show Bulyan reduces leeway from $\Omega(f(d))$ to $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ where $f(d) \sim \sqrt{d}$ [6].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is analytical and constructive. We do not conduct new large-scale training in this thesis batch but synthesize methodology from prior verified literature [1][3][4][6][7].

### 3.1 System Model and Assumptions

We assume:

1. *Honest majority* but not overwhelming: $n=50$, $f \in \{5,10,15\}$ ($10\%-30\%$ Byzantine).
2. *Synchronous rounds*: server waits for all $n$ updates before aggregation.
3. *Non-IID partition* via Dirichlet $\alpha=0.5$ as in FedAvg literature [1][8], exacerbating label-flipping detection difficulty.
4. *Omniscient adversary*: Byzantine clients know honest $V_i$ distributions and collude, stronger than label-flipping alone, to test worst-case [7].

We define label-flipping formally: For client $k \in B$, original dataset $D_k = \{(x_i, y_i)\}$, poisoned dataset $D_k^* = \{(x_i, \pi(y_i))\}$ where $\pi: [C] \to [C]$ is permutation with $\pi(c) \neq c$. Local gradient $V_k^* = \nabla \frac{1}{|D_k^*|} \sum \ell(w; x_i, \pi(y_i))$.

### 3.2 Aggregation Operators Formalized

**Definition 3.1 (Coordinate-wise $\beta$-Trimmed Mean):** For vectors $\{V_i\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, for each coordinate $j$, sort $(V_1[j], ..., V_n[j])$, remove largest and smallest $\beta n$ elements, average remainder: $TrMean_\beta[j] = \frac{1}{(1-2\beta)n} \sum_{i \in U_j} V_i[j]$ [3].

**Definition 3.2 (Krum):** Score $s(i) = \sum_{j \in N_i} \|V_i - V_j\|^2$ where $N_i$ are $n-f-2$ closest vectors to $V_i$. $Krum(V_1...V_n) = V_{i^*}$ where $i^* = \arg\min s(i)$. $m$-Krum = $\frac{1}{m} \sum_{k=1}^m V_{i_k^*}$ for $m$ smallest scores [4].

**Definition 3.3 (Bulyan):** Let $S_0 = \{V_i\}$. For $t=1$ to $n-2f$: select $i_t = Krum(S_{t-1})$, add to selection set $\mathcal{S}$, remove from $S_{t-1}$. Then $Bulyan = TrMean_{\text{or median}}$ over $\mathcal{S}$ per coordinate with trimming $f$ on each side. Requires $n \ge 4f+3$ to ensure $\theta = n-2f \ge 2f+3$ [6].

### 3.3 Evaluation Protocol

We propose evaluation mirroring standard FL benchmarks [1][5][8]:

1. **Datasets:** MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10, FEMNIST (62-class).
2. **Attacks:** SLF $0 \to 9$, DLF confidence-based, plus combined label+Gaussian for comparison.
3. **Metrics:** Test Error Rate (TER), Attack Success Rate (ASR), Aggregation Error $\|Aggr - \bar{V}_{honest}\|$, convergence rounds to $90\%$ of clean accuracy.
4. **Baselines:** FedAvg, Median, TrimMean ($\beta=0.1$), Krum, Multi-Krum ($m=10$), Bulyan.

Implementation stack uses Flower framework for orchestration, PyTorch for local training.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Trimmed Mean: Statistical Optimality and Its Fragility

Trimmed Mean's power lies in robust statistics. Yin et al. prove that under sub-exponential gradients, coordinate-wise $\beta$-trimmed mean achieves minimax optimal rate $ \tilde{O}(\frac{f}{n} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{kn}})$ where $k$ local samples [3]. 

However, Baruch et al.'s *A Little Is Enough* (LIE) attack shows trimmed mean is vulnerable when adversary shifts each coordinate by $z \cdot \sigma$ where $\sigma$ is empirical std and $z \sim 0.5$ [7]. Because trimming only removes extremes per coordinate, a coordinated small shift across many dimensions accumulates to $\Omega(z \sqrt{d})$ drift in $\ell_2$, enough to prevent convergence in non-convex neural nets.

For label-flipping, the gradient bias is *structured*: flipping $0 \to 9$ on MNIST induces gradient that pushes decision boundary to confuse those two classes, not random noise. Trimmed Mean can mitigate symmetric flipping because poisoned gradients are still outliers in affected class weights, but fails on non-IID where honest clients with rare labels also appear as outliers.

```python
def trimmed_mean(vectors: list[torch.Tensor], beta: float) -> torch.Tensor:
    # vectors: list of n x d
    stacked = torch.stack(vectors)  # n x d
    n = stacked.size(0)
    k = int(beta * n)
    sorted_vals, _ = torch.sort(stacked, dim=0)
    # remove k smallest and largest per coordinate
    trimmed = sorted_vals[k:n-k]  # (n-2k) x d
    return trimmed.mean(dim=0)

# Example: n=50, f=10, beta=0.2 removes 10 extremes per side
# Under LIE, adversary estimates sigma and sends mean + 0.5*sigma
```

### 4.2 Krum and Multi-Krum: Geometric Selection

Krum's intuition: in high dimensions, honest gradients cluster, Byzantine vectors lie far. By minimizing sum of distances to nearest $n-f-2$ neighbors, Krum selects a vector *close* to true barycenter even if $f$ vectors are arbitrary [4].

> **Theorem: Krum Resilience:** If $n > 2f+2$ and honest gradients $V_i$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb{E}[V_i]=g$, $\mathbb{E}\|V_i-g\|^2 \le \sigma^2$, then Krum output $\hat{V}$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}\|\hat{V}-g\|^2 \le O(\sigma^2)$ and $\langle \mathbb{E}[\hat{V}], g\rangle >0$, guaranteeing descent [4].

Proof sketch uses that for honest $i$, its $n-f-2$ nearest neighbors contain at least $n-2f-2$ honest vectors, bounding distance to true mean. For Byzantine $j$, even if it mimics honest cluster, its score is at least as large as honest's with high probability when variance bounded.

**Limitation:** Krum uses *one* client per round, losing linear speedup and amplifying variance, especially under non-IID where honest clients disagree. Multi-Krum averages $m$ best scores, recovering speedup $m/n$ but still discards $n-m$ updates [4][9].

Label-flipping evades Krum when $f$ is small: flipped gradients may remain within honest cluster's radius because they are derived from real data (albeit mislabeled), so distance-based filtering fails. Empirical work shows Krum's accuracy drops $8-12\%$ under $20\%$ SLF on FEMNIST non-IID [8], while TrimMean drops only $3-5\%$.

### 4.3 Bulyan: Two-Stage Defense and Dimensional Leeway

Mhamdi et al. identified that convergence alone is insufficient: in $d \gg 1$, adversary can force convergence to *ineffective* models within $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ ball of true optimum while still satisfying Krum's resilience condition [6]. They formalize *leeway*: maximum coordinate-wise shift adversary can induce without being detected.

Bulyan's two-stage design:

1. **Selection:** Iteratively apply Krum $n-2f$ times to obtain $\mathcal{S}$ of size $\theta = n-2f$. This ensures at most $f$ Byzantine remain in $\mathcal{S}$ (since Krum majority honest).
2. **Sanitization:** Compute coordinate-wise median of $\mathcal{S}$ (or trimmed mean with $f$ trimming). Since median of $\theta$ vectors where at most $f$ Byzantine has bounded influence $O(1/\sqrt{d})$, overall leeway shrinks to $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ [6].

Condition $n \ge 4f+3$ ensures $\theta \ge 2f+3$, needed for median's breakdown.



*Haskell pure specification enables property-based testing of resilience.*

Bulyan is strongest theoretically but expensive: $O(n^2 d)$ for $n-2f$ Krum iterations, plus requires larger $n$ for same $f$. Under label-flipping, Bulyan excels because median step removes residual bias that survives Krum selection.

### 4.4 Label-Flipping Adversary: Formal Analysis and Countermeasures

Label-flipping attack can be modeled as distribution shift: honest client $i$ samples $(x,y) \sim \mathcal{D}_i$, Byzantine $k$ samples $(x, \pi(y)) \sim \mathcal{D}_k^\pi$. Its gradient $\nabla F_k^\pi(w) = \mathbb{E}_{x,y}[\nabla \ell(w; x, \pi(y))]$ equals honest gradient plus bias $b(w) = \nabla F_k^\pi - \nabla F_k$.

For cross-entropy loss with softmax, flipping $c_{src} \to c_{tgt}$ increases loss on $c_{src}$ samples by pushing probability mass to $c_{tgt}$. Bias $b$ is concentrated on weights connecting penultimate layer to logits $c_{src}, c_{tgt}$, not isotropic. 

Countermeasures beyond aggregation:

- **Honest Score Client Selection (HSCS):** Use server-side validation set to score client updates by loss reduction; exclude low-score clients [5]. Requires $O(n)$ validation inference per round.
- **Loss-based Clustering:** Cluster clients by loss on server's trusted dataset; label-flippers form separate cluster [8].
- **Spectral Defenses:** Project updates onto low-dimensional subspace estimated from history; label-flipping updates have larger orthogonal residual [9].

**TLA+ specification** for FL round with Byzantine detection:

```tla
---- MODULE FederatedByzantine ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets, Reals
CONSTANTS n, f, d
VARIABLES round, updates, globalModel, byzSet

ByzantineCondition == Cardinality(byzSet) <= f

KrumScore(i) == 
  LET neighbors == CHOOSE S \in SUBSET (DOMAIN updates \ {i}) : Cardinality(S) = n - f - 2
  IN Sum({Norm(updates[i] - updates[j]) : j \in neighbors})

Next == 
  /\ \E newUpdates \in [1..n -> Real^d]: 
     /\ \A i \in 1..n \ byzSet : newUpdates[i] = HonestGradient(globalModel)
     /\ \A i \in byzSet : newUpdates[i] \in Real^d \* arbitrary
  /\ updates' = newUpdates
  /\ globalModel' = globalModel - eta * Aggr(updates')
----
```

*TLA+ models safety: global model never diverges beyond bound despite $f$ Byzantine.*

### 4.5 Unified View: Trade-offs Under Non-IID

Non-IID exacerbates all defenses. When Dirichlet $\alpha=0.5$, honest clients may hold disjoint label sets; their gradients naturally diverge, inflating pairwise distances and causing Krum to select *majority label* clients disproportionately, starving minority classes [5][8]. Trimmed Mean suffers less because per-coordinate trimming preserves minority signals if $f < n/2$ but still biased when minority clients < $f$.

Bulyan's requirement $n \ge 4f+3$ becomes more restrictive in cross-device FL where $n$ per round is $50-100$ and $f$ unknown. Recent work *FedRo* analyzes tradeoff of estimating $\hat{f}$: overestimating increases aggregation error proportional to $\hat{f}/(n-\hat{f})$ [10], while underestimating breaks resilience.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Resilience Proof Sketch for Trimmed Mean

We adapt Yin et al. [3]:

*Lemma:* Let honest vectors $V_i$ have sub-Gaussian tails with mean $g$ and variance proxy $\sigma^2$. For $\beta = f/n$, trimmed mean $T$ satisfies $\|T - g\|_\infty \le O(\sigma \sqrt{\log d / n} + f/n \cdot \Delta)$ where $\Delta$ bounds Byzantine magnitude.

*Proof idea:* Sorting per coordinate ensures Byzantine values at extremes are removed if $f < \beta n$. Remaining set contains at most $f$ Byzantine among $n-2f$ survivors, bounding influence. Union bound over $d$ dimensions yields $\ell_\infty$ bound, converted to $\ell_2$ via $\|x\|_2 \le \sqrt{d}\|x\|_\infty$. Optimal rate follows when $\beta \asymp f/n$.

### 5.2 Krum Convergence

Blanchard et al. show Krum is $(\alpha,f)$-resilient with $\sin \alpha = O(\sigma/\|g\|)$ when $2f+2 < n$ [4]. SGD with Krum then satisfies:

$$\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t+1})] \le F(w_t) - \eta (1-\sin\alpha)\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + \eta^2 L \sigma_{krum}^2 /2$$

Telescoping yields $\frac{1}{T}\sum \|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 \to O(\sigma_{krum}^2)$.

### 5.3 Bulyan Leeway Bound

Mhamdi et al. prove: For any Byzantine strategy, Bulyan output $B$ satisfies $\|B - \bar{V}_{honest}\|_\infty \le O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}})$ where $\bar{V}_{honest}$ is true mean of honest gradients, versus $\Omega(\sqrt{d})$ for Krum/Median alone [6]. Proof uses that median of $\theta$ vectors with at most $f$ Byzantine lies within convex hull of honest medians, and coordinate-wise median's error scales inversely with $d$ due to concentration.

### 5.4 Empirical Protocol (Proposed)

We propose following protocol (not executed in this batch due to time constraints, but designed for reproducibility):

1. Setup Flower simulation with $n=50$, ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10.
2. Partition via Dirichlet $\alpha=0.5$.
3. Adversary $f=10$ runs SLF $1 \to 7$ (cat→horse) and DLF.
4. Measure TER after 1000 rounds, 5 seeds.

Expected results from literature [8][9]:

- FedAvg: TER $45\%$ under $20\%$ SLF (clean $15\%$).
- TrimMean $\beta=0.2$: TER $18-20\%$.
- Krum: TER $25-28\%$ (non-IID penalty).
- Multi-Krum $m=10$: TER $20-22\%$.
- Bulyan: TER $16-18\%$, best but requires $n=50 \ge 43$ for $f=10$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Dimensionality curse:** All three aggregators' theoretical guarantees degrade with $d$. Bulyan's $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ leeway is asymptotically good but constants matter; for $d=10^7$, $1/\sqrt{d} \approx 0.0003$ but multiplied by gradient norm $10^2$ yields $0.03$ shift per round, accumulating over $1000$ rounds.

2. **Non-IID indistinguishability:** No purely update-based aggregator can distinguish honest non-IID outliers from Byzantine without auxiliary data or assumptions on heterogeneity boundedness [5][10]. Label-flipping that mimics non-IID is fundamentally hard.

3. **Adaptive adversaries:** *A Little Is Enough* shows trimmed mean can be broken by small coordinated shifts [7]. Our analysis assumes omniscient but not adaptive to defense history; in practice, adversary can learn defense via observing global model.

4. **Communication and privacy:** Robust aggregation requires receiving full vectors, incompatible with secure aggregation that hides individual updates. FORTA attempts DFT-coded Krum [11] but introduces numerical error.

5. **Single-server trust:** We assume trusted server. Decentralized Byzantine resilience (GUANYU) requires $n \ge 6f+...$ for both workers and servers [12], doubling requirements.

6. **Label-flipping is weak:** Stronger model-poisoning (e.g., Fang et al.'s optimization-based attack) breaks TrimMean/Krum even when label-flipping fails [13]. Defending only label-flipping gives false sense of security.

## 7 Conclusion

Byzantine-resilient federated learning under label-flipping adversaries reveals a nuanced trade-off between statistical efficiency, computational cost, and dimensional robustness. Trimmed Mean offers optimal statistical rates and simplicity but remains vulnerable to coordinated small shifts and non-IID confusion. Krum provides provable $(\alpha,f)$-resilience via geometric selection at the cost of discarding $n-1$ updates per round and sensitivity to heterogeneity. Bulyan achieves the strongest known leeway bound $O(1/\sqrt{d})$ by combining selection with median sanitization, yet demands $n \ge 4f+3$ and $O(n^2 d)$ computation.

For practical deployments facing label-flipping:

- Use **Multi-Krum with $m \approx n/2$** as baseline when $n \ge 2f+2$ and data moderately IID.
- Prefer **Trimmed Mean $\beta = f/n$** when communication efficient and adversary is non-adaptive data poisoner.
- Deploy **Bulyan** when security paramount and $n$ large enough, especially for high-dimensional models where dimensional leeway matters.
- Augment with **server-side validation scoring** [5] or **loss-based clustering** [8] to handle non-IID.

Future directions include learned aggregation weights [14], spectral defenses that estimate benign subspace [9], and coded secure aggregation that preserves privacy while enabling robustness [11]. Ultimately, Byzantine resilience is not a single aggregator but a *system property* requiring defense-in-depth, from data attestation to robust optimization.
