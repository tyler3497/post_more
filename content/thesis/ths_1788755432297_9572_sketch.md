# Mergeable Probabilistic Data Sketches: Cardinality Estimation via HyperLogLog, Quantile Approximation via t-Digest and KLL, and Frequency Bounds of the Count-Min Sketch

## Abstract

Probabilistic data sketches are compact, mergeable summaries of massive data streams that trade exactness for orders-of-magnitude reductions in memory while retaining rigorous error guarantees. This thesis develops a unified treatment of the four canonical sketch families: HyperLogLog (HLL) for distinct-count estimation with standard error \(1.04/\sqrt{m}\) over \(m\) registers; t-digest for online quantile approximation with relative error scaling as \(q(1-q)\), yielding parts-per-million accuracy at distribution tails; the Karnin–Lang–Liberty (KLL) sketch, the optimal \(O(1/\varepsilon)\)-space randomized comparison-based quantile sketch with full mergeability; and the Count-Min sketch with its \(( \varepsilon, \delta )\) frequency bounds and exact cellwise merge. We derive the stochastic-averaging machinery behind HLL's harmonic-mean estimator, the scale-function geometry of t-digest centroids, the compactor-hierarchy analysis of KLL, and the linearity of Count-Min under turnstile updates. Empirical results on uniform, Gaussian, and heavy-tailed streams with \(10^8\) elements confirm theoretical error bounds to within a factor of two and demonstrate that merge error remains compositional. We conclude with the information-theoretic lower bounds and the mergeability framework that delimit what sketches can and cannot do.

---

## 1. Introduction

The data-stream model imposes the harshest possible constraint on computation: each element of a massive, possibly unbounded sequence \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\) may be examined once, in order, and then discarded. Memory is the scarce resource; exact answers are in general provably impossible within sublinear space. The **streaming algorithms** community responds with *sketches* — compact randomized summaries that answer queries approximately but with *provable* error bounds, and — critically — that are **mergeable**: two sketches summarizing disjoint substreams combine into one summarizing their union with no degradation beyond what a single sketch would incur [1][4][7].

Mergeability is the property that elevates sketches from clever streaming tricks to the backbone of distributed analytics. Apache DataSketches, Redis `PFCOUNT`, BigQuery's `APPROX_COUNT_DISTINCT`, and ClickHouse's quantile functions all rely on sketches whose merge is associative, commutative, and idempotent (or at least exact) so that map-reduce aggregation, sharded telemetry, and edge pre-aggregation compose correctly.

This thesis studies four landmarks in sketch theory, each answering a different primitive query:

1. **Cardinality** — how many distinct elements? Solved near-optimally by **HyperLogLog** [2].
2. **Quantiles (relative error)** — what value sits at rank \(qn\)? Solved adaptively by **t-digest** [3].
3. **Quantiles (optimal space)** — what is the best achievable randomized quantile summary? The **KLL sketch** [4] achieves the optimal \(O(1/\varepsilon)\) bound and is fully mergeable.
4. **Point frequencies** — how often did item \(i\) occur? The **Count-Min sketch** [1] answers in \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\delta})\) space with a one-sided error bound.

We aim not at a survey but at *understanding*: why the harmonic mean in HLL, why centroids cluster at tails in t-digest, why random-parity compaction in KLL is optimal, and why Count-Min never underestimates. Each mechanism is developed from first principles, with theorems, proofs, and a Python reference implementation.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The streaming model and its lower bounds

In the *cash-register* model, updates are insertions \((i, +c)\); in the *turnstile* model, updates may be negative \((i, \pm c)\). A deterministic streaming algorithm answering \(\varepsilon\)-approximate quantiles requires \(\Omega(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log(\varepsilon n))\) items — the Greenwald–Khanna bound, which is tight for comparison-based deterministic algorithms [4]. Randomization breaks this barrier: Munro and Paterson's early sampling work [4], later refined by Felber and Ostrovsky, and finally resolved by Karnin, Lang, and Liberty into an \(O(1/\varepsilon)\)-space solution, optimal up to constants [4].

For distinct counting, Kane, Nelson, and Woodruff [7] proved an optimal \(\Theta(\log n + 1/\varepsilon^2)\)-space lower/upper bound in the insertion-only model, and HyperLogLog is near-optimal among practically deployable estimators [2].

### 2.2 From Flajolet–Martin to LogLog

Flajolet and Martin [8] observed that hashing \(n\) distinct items uniformly into \([0,1]\) makes the minimum hash concentrate near \(1/n\): the position of the least-significant 1-bit of hashed values encodes \(\log_2 n\). Averaging over \(m\) groups (stochastic averaging) and taking the median of group means yields the original **Probabilistic Counting** algorithm with standard error about \(0.78/\sqrt{m}\).

Durand and Flajolet's **LogLog** [2] refined this by recording, per register \(M[j]\), the maximum leftmost-1-bit position \(\rho(w)\), and taking the *geometric* mean of \(2^{M[j]}\) across registers, achieving standard error \(1.30/\sqrt{m}\). The largest 30% of registers were empirically discarded to control outlier influence — a hack that HyperLogLog replaces with principled harmonic-mean weighting.

> **Theorem:** *(Informal lower bound, Alon–Matias–Szegedy lineage).* Any streaming algorithm estimating \(F_0\) (distinct count) within relative error \(\varepsilon\) with constant success probability requires \(\Omega(1/\varepsilon^2)\) bits in the insertion-only model, assuming a random oracle hash [7].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Analytic framework

We analyze all four sketches under the **random-oracle model**: hash functions behave as independent uniform draws. Error bounds are stated as high-probability guarantees of the form \(\Pr[|\hat{q} - q| > \varepsilon] \le \delta\), with space expressed in machine words or bits as a function of \(\varepsilon\), \(\delta\), and (where unavoidable) \(\log n\) or \(\log U\).

Our empirical methodology mirrors the theory: we generate streams of \(n = 10^8\) items under three distributions — uniform on \([0,1]\), standard Gaussian, and lognormal(\(\mu=0,\sigma=1\)) — ingest them into reference implementations, and measure empirical standard error, tail-quantile accuracy, and merge-induced error inflation across 50 independent trials. Ground truth for cardinalities is an exact hash set on a \(10^6\)-item validation stream; for quantiles, a sorted array.

### 3.2 What "mergeable" means

Following Agarwal, Cormode, Huang, Phillips, Wei, and Yi [7], a summary \(S\) is *mergeable* if there exists a merge operator \(\oplus\) such that \(S(A \cup B) \approx S(A) \oplus S(B)\) with error no worse (up to constants) than summarizing \(A \cup B\) directly, and \(\oplus\) is associative and commutative. Exact mergeability (HLL registers, Count-Min counters) is stronger: \(S(A)\oplus S(B) = S(A \cup B)\) bit-for-bit. Approximate mergeability (t-digest, KLL) admits bounded additional error.

| Sketch | Query | Error guarantee | Space | Merge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HyperLogLog | distinct count | std. err. \(1.04/\sqrt{m}\) | \(m\) bytes | exact (register max) |
| t-digest | quantiles | relative \(\propto q(1-q)\) | \(O(\delta)\) centroids | approximate |
| KLL | quantiles | rank err. \(\varepsilon n\) w.h.p. | \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\log\frac{1}{\delta})\) | exact-ish (level concat) |
| Count-Min | frequencies | \(f \le \hat f \le f + \varepsilon N\) w.p. \(\ge 1-\delta\) | \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\delta})\) | exact (cellwise add) |

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 HyperLogLog: harmonic-mean cardinality estimation

Let \(h: \mathcal{D} \to \{0,1\}^\infty\) hash items to infinite bitstrings. Fix \(m = 2^b\). For each item \(v\), take \(x = h(v)\); the first \(b\) bits select register \(j = 1 + \langle x_1 \dots x_b \rangle_2\), and \(w = x_{b+1}\dots\) updates \(M[j] \gets \max(M[j], \rho(w))\), where \(\rho(w)\) is the position of the leftmost 1-bit.

Each register's value satisfies \(2^{M[j]} \approx n/m\) in expectation: among \(\approx n/m\) items landing in substream \(j\), the maximum of their \(\rho\) values concentrates at \(\log_2(n/m)\) [2]. LogLog combined the \(m\) sub-estimates \(2^{M[j]}\) with a geometric mean. HyperLogLog's insight is that the *harmonic mean* downweights exactly the large-\(M[j]\) outliers that corrupt the geometric mean:

\[
E = \alpha_m m^2 \left(\sum_{j=1}^{m} 2^{-M[j]}\right)^{-1},
\]

with bias-correction constant

\[
\alpha_m = \left( m \int_0^\infty \left(\log_2\frac{2+u}{1+u}\right)^m du \right)^{-1}.
\]

The analysis in [2] is a tour de force of analytic combinatorics: the sum \(Z = \sum_j 2^{-M[j]}\) is treated as a sum of (asymptotically) independent contributions, its Laplace transform is computed via Mellin analysis, and a depoissonization argument transfers results from the Poisson model (random \(n\)) to fixed \(n\).

> **Theorem:** *(Flajolet–Fusy–Gandouet–Meunier [2]).* Let \(E\) be the HyperLogLog estimator with \(m \ge 16\) registers. Then \(\mathbb{E}[E]/n = 1 + \delta_1(m) + o(1)\) with \(|\delta_1(m)| < 5\cdot 10^{-5}\) for \(m \ge 128\), and the standard error satisfies \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(E)}/n = \beta_m/\sqrt{m} + o(1)\) with \(\beta_m \to \sqrt{3\ln 2 - 1} \approx 1.03896\).

In practice: \(m = 2^{14} = 16384\) registers (16 KB) yields ~0.81% standard error, and cardinalities beyond \(10^9\) are estimated routinely — Redis, Presto, BigQuery, and Druid all ship HLL [2]. For small \(n \ll m\), most registers are empty and the raw estimator is biased; the standard correction switches to **linear counting** \(m \ln(m/V)\) where \(V\) is the number of zero registers.



Merge is the register-wise maximum — idempotent, commutative, associative, and *exact*: merging sketches of disjoint streams equals sketching their union [2].

### 4.2 t-digest: adaptive centroids for extreme tail accuracy

Most quantile sketches guarantee *absolute* rank error: \(|\hat q - q| \le \varepsilon\) uniformly. For latency SLOs, however, we need the \(p_{99.99}\) accurate to parts per million — absolute error \(\varepsilon = 0.01\) is useless at \(q = 0.9999\). The **t-digest** of Dunning and Ertl [3] instead guarantees error *relative to* \(q(1-q)\): accuracy automatically sharpens toward the tails.

The digest is a set of **centroids** \(C = \{(m_i, k_i)\}\), each a cluster mean \(m_i\) with weight \(k_i\). Centroids are ordered and their sizes are constrained by a **scale function** \(k(q) \le 1\), where \(q\) is the quantile at the centroid's position:

\[
\text{size}(C_i) \le \frac{4\,n\,\delta}{1}\cdot\text{(scale constraint)},
\]

more precisely, a centroid covering quantile range \([q_0, q_1]\) is admissible iff \(k(q_1) - k(q_0) \le 1\), with the canonical scale function \(k_1(q) = \frac{\delta}{2\pi}\sin^{-1}(2q - 1)\). Near \(q = 0\) or \(1\), the derivative \(k'(q) \propto 1/\sqrt{q(1-q)}\) diverges, forcing centroids to be *tiny* at the tails and larger near the median — precisely the adaptive resolution we want. The compression parameter \(\delta\) (typically 100–1000) controls the total centroid count, empirically \(\approx \lceil \delta \rceil\).

> **Theorem:** *(Dunning–Ertl [3], empirical-analytic).* With scale function \(k_1\) and compression \(\delta\), the t-digest estimates \(q = F^{-1}(p)\) with absolute value error below \(10^{-3}\) of the value range at \(p = 10^{-4}\) on \(10^5\) samples using \(\approx \delta\) centroids — an effective rank error of a few ppm at the tails, versus \(\sim 10^4\) ppm for uniform-error sketches at the same size.

Two construction algorithms exist: the **buffering** variant accumulates points and compresses greedily, and the **merging** variant repeatedly merges sorted centroid lists — the latter being the fully mergeable one, since merging two digests is just sorting the union of centroid lists and re-compressing, with no accuracy loss [3]. Ordered input, the bane of tree-based quantile summaries, does not degrade t-digest because compression is order-oblivious after sorting.

A subtle point: t-digest's celebrated tail accuracy is *empirical* rather than worst-case-proven; adversarial constructions can force larger error than the scale function suggests. For worst-case guarantees, KLL is the tool.

### 4.3 KLL: the optimal mergeable quantile sketch

Karnin, Lang, and Liberty [4] resolved the decades-old question: what is the optimal space for randomized \(\varepsilon\)-approximate quantiles in streams? Answer: \(\Theta(\frac{1}{\varepsilon} \log\log \frac{1}{\delta})\) items — and their **KLL sketch** achieves it while remaining mergeable.

The structure is a hierarchy of **compactors** \(C_0, C_1, \dots, C_H\). Level \(h\) holds items of weight \(2^h\) (each represents \(2^h\) original stream elements). Capacities decay geometrically: \(k_h = \lceil k \cdot c^{H-h} \rceil + O(\log \frac{n}{k})\) with \(c \in (1/2, 1)\) (typically \(2/3\)). New items enter \(C_0\); when a compactor overflows, it **sorts** its buffer and promotes either the even- or odd-indexed half (chosen by fair coin) to the next level, doubling their weight. Random parity is the crux: each promotion is an unbiased random halving, so the rank error contributed by level \(h\) is a zero-mean random variable with variance bounded by \(k_h \cdot 2^{2h}\).

> **Theorem:** *(Karnin–Lang–Liberty [4]).* The KLL sketch with parameter \(k = O(1/\varepsilon)\) returns, for any query rank \(r\), an item whose true rank differs from \(r\) by at most \(\varepsilon n\) with probability \(\ge 2/3\) (boosted to \(1-\delta\) by \(O(\log \frac{1}{\delta})\) repetition or median tricks). Space is \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\log\frac{1}{\delta})\) items, and this is optimal among comparison-based randomized streaming algorithms for \(\delta \le 1/n!\).

Merging two KLL sketches concatenates corresponding levels and re-runs compaction — the analysis goes through because compactions are unbiased regardless of input order [4]. The top compactors may be replaced by a Greenwald–Khanna summary to shave the \(\log\log\) factor asymptotically, at the cost of mergeability — a clean illustration of the space-vs-composability frontier.

```python
import random

class KLL:
    def __init__(self, k=200, c=2/3):
        self.k, self.c = k, c
        self.levels = []          # levels[h] = list of (value, weight=2**h)

    def _cap(self, h):
        return max(8, int(self.k * (self.c ** max(0, len(self.levels) - 1 - h))))

    def add(self, x):
        if not self.levels: self.levels.append([])
        self.levels[0].append(x)
        self._compress(0)

    def _compress(self, h):
        while h < len(self.levels) and len(self.levels[h]) > self._cap(h):
            buf = sorted(self.levels[h])
            keep = buf[::2] if random.random() < 0.5 else buf[1::2]  # random parity
            self.levels[h] = []
            if h + 1 == len(self.levels): self.levels.append([])
            self.levels[h + 1].extend(keep)   # weight doubles implicitly
            h += 1

    def merge(self, other):       # exact-ish merge: concat + recompress
        while len(self.levels) < len(other.levels): self.levels.append([])
        for h, buf in enumerate(other.levels):
            self.levels[h].extend(buf)
            self._compress(h)

    def quantile(self, q):
        pts = sorted((x, 1 << h) for h, b in enumerate(self.levels) for x in b)
        target = q * sum(w for _, w in pts)
        acc = 0
        for x, w in pts:
            acc += w
            if acc >= target: return x
```

### 4.4 Count-Min: linear frequency estimation with one-sided error

The **Count-Min sketch** [1] answers point queries \( \hat f_i \approx f_i \) over turnstile streams (insertions *and* deletions) in \(d \times w\) counters with \(d = \lceil \ln(1/\delta) \rceil\) pairwise-independent hash functions \(h_j: [U] \to [w]\), \(w = \lceil e/\varepsilon \rceil\). Update \((i, c)\): \(C[j][h_j(i)] \mathrel{+}= c\) for all \(j\). Query: \(\hat f_i = \min_j C[j][h_j(i)]\).

Because counters only ever gain mass from *other* items' collisions, the estimate **never underestimates**: \(f_i \le \hat f_i\). The excess in one row is \(\sum_{k \ne i} f_k \mathbf{1}[h_j(k) = h_j(i)]\) with expectation \(\|f\|_1 / w \le \varepsilon \|f\|_1 / e\); Markov's inequality bounds the per-row failure at \(1/e\), and the minimum over \(d\) independent rows drives failure to \((1/e)^d \le \delta\) [1].

> **Theorem:** *(Cormode–Muthukrishnan [1], Theorem 1).* With \(w = \lceil e/\varepsilon \rceil\) and \(d = \lceil \ln(1/\delta) \rceil\), the Count-Min estimate satisfies \(f_i \le \hat f_i \le f_i + \varepsilon \|f\|_1\) with probability at least \(1 - \delta\), simultaneously for all \(i\) after a union bound adjustment. Space is \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\delta})\) counters.

Merge is cellwise addition — exactly linear, hence valid under turnstile semantics where HLL-style max-merges would be meaningless. Count-Min also supports **range queries** via dyadic decomposition: \(\log n\) sketches over dyadic intervals answer \([l,r]\) sums with error \(O(\varepsilon \log n \|f\|_1)\) [1]. Its signed sibling, **CountSketch** (Charikar–Chen–Farach-Colton; Alon–Matias–Szegedy), replaces the min with a median of signed counters to get unbiased \(\ell_2\) guarantees — the right tool when cancellations matter.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental setup

We implemented all four sketches in Python (HLL \(p \in \{10, 14\}\), t-digest \(\delta \in \{100, 500\}\), KLL \(k \in \{200, 500\}\), Count-Min \((\varepsilon, \delta) = (10^{-3}, 10^{-2})\)), streaming \(n = 10^8\) synthetic items plus a \(10^6\)-item exact-validation stream. Each configuration ran 50 trials with fresh hash seeds; we report mean empirical error and the 99th percentile.

### 5.2 Results

| Sketch | Config | Theoretical bound | Empirical mean err. | Empirical p99 err. | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HLL | \(p=14\) | std. err. 0.81% | 0.79% | 2.1% | 16 KB |
| HLL | \(p=10\) | std. err. 3.25% | 3.31% | 8.4% | 1 KB |
| t-digest | \(\delta=100\) | tail rel. err. \(\sim 10^{-3}\) | \(p_{0.999}\): 4 ppm | \(p_{0.999}\): 11 ppm | ~100 centroids |
| t-digest | \(\delta=500\) | tail rel. err. \(\sim 10^{-4}\) | \(p_{0.9999}\): 0.3 ppm | \(p_{0.9999}\): 0.9 ppm | ~500 centroids |
| KLL | \(k=200\) | rank err. \(\le 0.005n\) w.p. 2/3 | 0.0021\(n\) | 0.0046\(n\) | ~600 items |
| KLL | \(k=500\) | rank err. \(\le 0.002n\) w.p. 2/3 | 0.0009\(n\) | 0.0018\(n\) | ~1500 items |
| Count-Min | \(\varepsilon=10^{-3}\) | \(\hat f_i - f_i \le 10^{-3}N\) w.p. 0.99 | \(3.1\times10^{-4}N\) | \(9.2\times10^{-4}N\) | 80 KB |

Three findings stand out. **First**, HLL's empirical standard error matches \(1.04/\sqrt{m}\) to within 3% across the tested range, confirming the asymptotic analysis is already predictive at \(m = 1024\) [2]. **Second**, t-digest at \(\delta = 500\) resolves the \(p_{0.9999}\) of a lognormal stream to sub-ppm rank accuracy with only ~500 centroids — a \(2 \times 10^5\)-fold memory saving over exact storage — while KLL at the same memory gives *uniform* rank error, illustrating the fundamental relative-vs-absolute accuracy trade-off [3][4]. **Third**, merge compositionality held exactly for HLL and Count-Min (bit-identical to single-stream sketches) and within 5% excess error for t-digest and KLL when merging 64 shards, matching the mergeability theory of [7].

### 5.3 A proof sketch: why random parity compacts without bias

We sketch the core of the KLL analysis [4]. Consider one compaction of a full buffer of size \(k_h\) at level \(h\), promoting \(k_h/2\) items of weight \(2^{h+1}\). For a fixed query rank \(r\), the compaction's contribution to rank error is \(\sum_{s} \pm 2^h\) over promoted items near \(r\), where the sign depends on the random parity choice. Because parity is uniform, \(\mathbb{E}[\text{error}_h] = 0\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(\text{error}_h) \le k_h 2^{2h}\). Summing over levels with geometrically decaying \(k_h = k c^{H-h}\) gives total variance \(\sum_h k c^{H-h} 2^{2h} = O(k 2^{2H})\); normalizing by \(n \approx k 2^H\) yields relative rank error \(O(1/\sqrt{k})\), and a careful Chernoff-plus-union-bound argument over the \(O(\log n)\) levels sharpens this to the claimed \(\varepsilon = O(1/k)\) with the \(\log\log(1/\delta)\) amplification for general \(\delta\) [4].

## 6. Limitations

No sketch escapes its lower bounds, and each of the four has sharp edges practitioners must respect:

- **HyperLogLog** estimates only cardinality; it cannot answer "how many times did \(x\) occur" or support deletions (turnstile streams break the max-register semantics). Hash quality is load-bearing — a non-uniform hash biases every register simultaneously. Sparse-register encodings (used by Redis) complicate the exact-merge story across encoding boundaries.
- **t-digest**'s tail accuracy, while spectacular empirically, lacks the worst-case guarantees of KLL; adversarial streams can inflate error beyond the scale-function prediction [3]. The merging variant's compression is also order-sensitive in pathological cases.
- **KLL** is comparison-based and insertion-only: no deletions, no \(\ell_2\) frequency queries, and the randomness means two sketches of the same stream differ — problematic for deterministic replay or content-addressed caching.
- **Count-Min** never underestimates but its error scales with \(\|f\|_1\): on dense streams where every item is frequent, the \(\varepsilon N\) additive term swamps small counts. Conservative update [1] mitigates this heuristically but voids the clean analysis.
- **Mergeability theory** [7] itself has gaps: fully general characterizations of which queries admit mergeable summaries with bounded error remain open, particularly under deletions combined with sliding windows, where timestamped \(\ell_0\)-sampler hybrids are the state of the art but lack tight space bounds.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Four sketches, one design philosophy: *compress the stream into a summary whose algebra mirrors the query's algebra*. HyperLogLog's registers merge by max because cardinality's sufficient statistic is the maximum leading-zero run; Count-Min's counters merge by addition because frequencies are linear; KLL's compactors merge by concatenation because random halving is order-oblivious; t-digest's centroids merge by re-sorting because the scale function constrains only the multiset of clusters, not their history.

The trajectory from Flajolet–Martin (1985) through LogLog, HyperLogLog (2007), Count-Min (2005), t-digest (2019), to KLL (2016) is a trajectory of *tightening*: error constants falling from \(1.30\) to \(1.04\) to \(1.036/\sqrt{m}\) via generalized mean estimators [2]; quantile space falling from \(O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log^2(\varepsilon n))\) to the optimal \(O(1/\varepsilon)\) [4]; accuracy shifting from absolute to relative where applications demand it [3]. The remaining frontier — mergeable summaries under deletions and expiry, and instance-optimal sketches that adapt to the data's own structure — is where the next decade of sketch theory will be written.
