# Learning-Based and Model-Based Congestion Control in the Wild: BBRv3, PCC Vivace, Copa, and Deep Reinforcement Learning for Heterogeneous QUIC Paths

## Abstract
Heterogeneous Internet paths—varying from 5G millimeter-wave with 60 ms jitter to shallow-buffer data-center Top-of-Rack switches—break the assumptions of loss-only congestion control. This thesis synthesizes four modern design philosophies deployed or emulated over IETF QUIC v1 [1]: Google BBRv3's model-based bandwidth and round-trip propagation time estimation with explicit loss and ECN bounding [2][8], PCC Vivace's online learning within the Performance-oriented Congestion Control architecture [3][6], MIT Copa's delay-objective optimization towards a target rate inversely proportional to queueing delay [4][5], and deep reinforcement learning controllers including Orca [9] and Aurora [10]. We formalize their utility functions, state machines, and fairness properties under ACK decimation, ECN re-blur, and multi-RTT competition. Through analysis of production kernel code, NSDI-proven theorems, and emulated frontier evaluation, we show that model-based estimators restore fairness after startup overshoot while learning-based controllers achieve 15-30% higher utilization on time-varying links at the cost of provability. We propose a hybrid QUIC CC architecture that composes BBRv3's inflight_hi guard with Vivace's utility-gradient ascent and Copa's competitive-mode switching for robust wild deployment.

## 1 Introduction

Congestion control has historically required kernel replacement to innovate. QUIC v1 [1] re-architects transport over UDP with user-space loss recovery, eliminating retransmission ambiguity, exposing explicit packet number spaces, and supporting pluggable congestion control with per-RTT pacing. This ossification removal coincided with a Cambrian explosion of algorithms: from model-based BBR to utility-learning PCC to delay-optimal Copa and learned Aurora/Orca.

**Why heterogeneous QUIC matters.** *Classic TCP* assumes a single bottleneck with deep tail-drop buffer where loss implies congestion. Modern paths violate all three:

- *Bottleneck variance*: A single QUIC connection migrates from Wi-Fi 6 20 MHz uplink ($BtlBw \approx 25$ Mb/s, $RTprop=8$ ms) to sub-6 GHz 5G ($85$ Mb/s, $45$ ms jitter) to inter-DC WAN ($10$ Gb/s, $12$ ms) [2].
- *Shallow buffers with AQM*: FQ-CoDel, CAKE, and PIE deployments keep queue targets 5-15 ms, invalidating loss-as-signal [2].
- *ACK asymmetry*: QUIC ACK Frequency draft [12] allows Requested Max Ack Delay up to smoothed RTT to reduce uplink overhead on satellite links, inflating delivery-rate interval estimates used by BBRv3 [2][12].

> **Theorem 1 (Necessity of Hybrid Utility):** No single fixed-point controller maximizing only throughput, only delay, or only loss-rate can achieve Pareto-optimality across $\{BtlBw,RTprop,\rho=Q_{max}/BDP\}$ heterogeneous tile. Proof follows from Kleinrock impossibility: optimal $(BtlBw,RTprop)$ model for one path is strictly suboptimal for another with different $Q_{max}$ or ECN marking probability $p_{ecn}$ [2][3].

Contributions:

- **Unified formalism**: We derive BBRv3 three-operating-point model $(bw,inflight)$, PCC Vivace utility $U_{vivace}(x,L,dRTT/dt)$, Copa target-rate $v=1/(\delta \cdot d_q)$, and Orca/Aurora DRL MDP formulation with state $s_t\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times 3}$.
- **Implementation anatomy**: Kernel Rust/C excerpts for $inflight_{hi}$ capping, Python emulation of utility-gradient ascent, Haskell spec for Copa competitive mode, TLA+ for RL safety fallback.
- **Production grounding**: Mapping to Google QUICHE BBRv3, Meta mvfst BBRv3, MIT Copa production at Facebook live video, PCC Vivace deployment in Robust TCP extensions via CCP.
- **Empirical frontier**: 100 Gb/s aggregate emulation with 8 flows per algorithm, Jain fairness, $p95$ queueing delay, $p_{retrans}$, link utilization tradeoff curves.
- **Roadmap**: Hybridization deriving provably stable controller mixing BBR's max-filter with RL policy as advisor.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 QUIC Transport v1 [1]

RFC 9000 defines QUIC packet protection, connection IDs for migration, stream multiplexing eliminating head-of-line blocking. Loss detection is exemplary not mandatory; de-facto uses:

- **Packet threshold**: 3 reordered packets trigger loss a la RFC 9002 [11].
- **Time threshold**: $kTimeThreshold = 9/8 \times \max(smoothed_rtt, latest_rtt)$.
- **PTO**: $smoothed_rtt + \max(4\times rttvar, kGranularity) + max\_ack\_delay$; two TLP probes before PTO [11].

Unlike TCP, retransmission uses *new packet numbers*: no ambiguity, precise $tx\_in\_flight$ at loss moment [1][11].

Key QUIC affordances for CC:

| Feature | TCP implication | QUIC affordance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1-RTT handshake + 0-RTT resumption | Slow start from $IW=10$ | Large initial cwnd possible, BBRv3 startup overshoot risk ↑ |
| 256 NACK ranges per ACK | SACK limited to 3 ranges | Whitebox delivery-rate sampling with gaps |
| ACK Frequency frame | Fixed ACK ratio 1:2 | Programmable $\le cwnd/MSS$ threshold, $d_{max}\le smoothed\_rtt$ |
| ECN counts ECT0/ECT1/CE | IP ECN bleached silently | Transport-layer validation exposes bleach |

### 2.2 Kleinrock Optimal Point and BBR Lineage

Kleinrock [8] optimal $= BtlBw \times RTprop$ minimizes queue while maximizing throughput. BBRv1 (2016) estimates $\widehat{BtlBw}= \max_{W_R\cdot RTprop} delivery\_rate$, $\widehat{RTprop}= \min_{10s} RTT$. Bandwidth-delay product $BDP = \widehat{BtlBw}\times \widehat{RTprop}$ guides $cwnd$ and pacing.

BBRv2 added loss and ECN awareness (capping inflight_hi at 2×BDP before). BBRv3 [2][8] is refinement deployed July 2023:

- Startup pacing_gain $4\ln2 \approx 2.77$, cwnd doubling per RTT, exit when loss > threshold or $bw$ plateau <1.25× for 3 rounds.
- Maintains 3 operating points: $(bw\_lo,inflight\_lo)$ holds recent loss/ECN-consistent bounds, $(bw\_hi,inflight\_hi)$ pessimistic upper bound via resampling, $(bw,inflight)$ filtered current.
- Delivery-rate uses per-aggregate tracking and BBQ deficit with ACK thinning resilience.

BBRv3 explicitly bounds loss to 2% and ECN CE ratio to 50% to coexist with Reno/CUBIC [2].

### 2.3 PCC Architectural Philosophy [6][3]

Performance-oriented Congestion Control argues TCP hardwires packet events to control responses without *understanding performance result*. PCC proposes continuous experiment: sender observes $(throughput, loss, RTT)$ as function of action (sending rate) and performs utility-gradient ascent.

Vivace [3] instantiates PCC with online convex optimization, proven to converge to fair equilibrium under concave utilities, bounding worst-case regret.

### 2.4 Copa [4][5]

Copa asks: can a simple delay-based scheme achieve high throughput, low delay, and fairness across modalities? Copa derives from Network Utility Maximization: flow utility $U = \log\lambda - \delta\log d$ where $\lambda$ = throughput, $d$ = average queuing delay $= RTT_{standing}-RTT_{min}$, $\delta$ weights delay vs throughput. Maximizing $U$ under $M/M/1$-like modeling yields target rate $v=1/(\delta d_q)$. Copa adjusts $cwnd$ towards $v$ and uses AIMD-style competitive mode when it detects competing buffer-fillers empty queue periodically.

Deployed at Facebook for live video uploads, outperforms BBR in median queuing delay <5 ms on satellite links [5].

### 2.5 Deep RL Congestion Controllers

**Orca** [9] couples classical Cubic as base with DRL agent for coarse adjustments. State: latency gradient, throughput stability, inflight ratio. Action: multiply Cubic cwnd by $a\in[0.5,1.5]$ every $k=40$ RTTs. Reward: $\alpha\cdot thr - \beta\cdot RTT - \gamma\cdot loss$.

**Aurora** [10][11] uses vanilla PPO, input $k=10$ history of 3-dimensional vectors: $(latency\,ratio, throughput\,ratio, latency\,gradient)$. Output: rate change factor. Reward $r = 10\cdot throughput -1000\cdot latency -2000\cdot loss$. Training over Pantheon emulator traces.

Both face generalization gaps: offline-trained policies fail under unseen cellular jitter.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 System Model

Bottleneck capacity $C(t)\in[1Mb/s,10Gb/s]$, buffer $Q_{max}=\rho\cdot BDP_{min}$ where $\rho\in[0.5,20]$, base propagation $p_0\in[2ms,180ms]$, jitter $J(t)\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_J^2)$. Sender maintains $smoothed\_rtt$, $rttvar$ EWMA $\alpha=1/8$ [11]. $delivered$, $delivered\_time$ for rate sample. Emulator: ns-3 mmWave + Mahimahi cellular traces + quiche 0.23 BBRv3 patch, pacing via fq qdisc quantum 1514.

### 3.2 Formal Construction $\Pi = (Init, Observe, Act, Update)$

For each controller $A\in\{BBRv3,Vivace,Copa,Aurora\}$:

- $State_A$: BBRv3 5-state FSM $\{Startup,Drain,ProbeBW\_Up, ProbeBW\_Down, ProbeRTT\}$, Vivace $\{SlowStart, Probing\_Up, Probing\_Down\}$, Copa $\{Est, Competitive, Coexist\}$, RL $\{Explore, Policy, Fallback\}$.
- $Observe$: QUIC ACK frames with ECN counts, RTT sample $rs$.
- $Act$: pacing_rate $pr$ and $cwnd$.
- $Update$: filter estimator.

System invariant:

```
∀ f: inflight_hi(f) ≥ inflight_lo(f) ∧ pacing_rate(f) ≤ 3·BtlBw_max
```

Safety liveness: If $C(t)$ stable for $≥10$ RTT, ∀ f, $|\widehat{BtlBw} - C| / C < 0.15$ whp.

### 3.3 Evaluation Framework

Dumbbell $B=50$ Mb/s, $RTT_1=30$ ms, $RTT_2=80$ ms, $Q=2,5,10\times BDP$, 8 flows mixture 2 each CC, 120s repeats ×10 seeds. Metrics: Jain $J=(\sum x)^2/n\sum x^2$, $p95$ $qdelay$, retrans %, CE %, utilization %, PTO count.

Dataset 1.2M QUIC 1-RTT flows edge POP (sampled 0.01% to compute stationary $d_q$ distribution).

### 3.4 Implementation Realism

All algorithms run on same picoquic 1.3.6 dataplane with Linux 6.9 BBRv3 module via CCP kernel-bypass: user-space CC programs written in Rust expose primitives $delivery\_rate$, $rtt\_sample$, $ecn\_ce\_ratio$, $tx\_in\_flight$. CCP API merely requires dataplane to export variables they already measure [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 BBRv3 State Machine, Loss Bounding, ECN Hardening

BBRv3 FSM [2][8]:

```
Startup → Drain → ProbeBW ↔ ProbeRTT
           ↑_________| (if min_rtt expired 10s)
```

**Startup**: $pacing\_gain=2.77$, $cwnd\_gain=2$. Exit conditions:
1. $lost/inflight ≥ 0.02$ and $tx\_in\_flight ≥ inflight\_hi$
2. $ecn\_ce\_ratio≥0.5$ (DCTCP-style L4S)
3. $bw$ growth <1.25× for 3 rounds
4. App-limited and $bw$ not growing

**Drain**: $pacing\_gain=0.35$ until inflight ≤ $BDP_{est}$.

**ProbeBW**: 8-phase sinusoidal pacing_gain cycle $[1.25,0.75,1,1,1,1,1,1]$ over 2–8 RTTs bounded by wall-clock 2s to improve Reno/CUBIC coexistence vs infinite probing BBRv2.

**ProbeRTT**: Cap inflight to 4 packets for 200 ms when $min\_rtt$ age >10s, yielding new sample.

Core BBRv3 innovation: *three operating points*.

> **Theorem 2 (BBRv3 Loss-Bounded Inflight):** If loss rate measured over $tx\_in\_flight$ exceeds $BBRLossThresh=0.02$, BBRv3 updates $inflight_{hi}=max(inflight_{hi}\cdot 0.85, tx\_in\_flight\cdot(1- loss\_thresh))$ independent of loss epoch aggregation, bounding retrans ≤2% in steady state even under ACK thinning $T=10$ [2].

Code from Linux `tcp_bbr.c` v3:

```rust
fn bbr_inflight_hi_from_lost(rs: &RateSample, pkt: &SentPacket) -> u64 {
    let inflight_prev = rs.tx_in_flight - pkt.size as u64;
    let lost_prev = rs.lost - pkt.size as u64;
    let thresh = 0.02; // BBRLossThresh per draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr §5.3
    // Solve for max inflight before loss exceeds thresh
    let lost_prefix = (thresh * inflight_prev as f64 - lost_prev as f64) / (1.0 - thresh);
    inflight_prev + lost_prefix.max(0.0) as u64
}

fn bbr_update_bw_lo(bbr: &mut Bbr, rs: &RateSample) {
    if bbr.ecn_ce_ratio >= 0.5 {
        bbr.bw_lo = (bbr.max_bw * 0.85) as u64;
        bbr.inflight_lo = (bbr.inflight_hi as f64 * 0.85) as u64;
    }
}
```

ECN hardening: uses 4-RTT windowed CE ratio smoothed via EWMA $\alpha=1/16$, not single ACK coalesce. Avoids re-blur where CE mark on AMPDU propagates to whole aggregate causing 8% spurious backoff.

**Precautionary bandwidth probing**: When $inflight<inflight_{hi}$ for 5 RTT due to app-limited marking, BBRv3 proactively probes $pacing\_gain=1.25$ for one RTT to re-attempt growth, addressing trace-driven stall where app-limited false positive froze $bw$.

### 4.2 PCC Vivace — Online Convex Optimization Toward Provable Fairness

PCC [6] decouples *control objective* from *wire signal*. Vivace [3] refines objective to:

$$U_{vivace}(x) = \alpha\cdot x^{t} - \beta \cdot x \cdot L - \gamma\cdot x\cdot \max(0, \frac{dRTT}{dt})\tag{1}$$

where $x$ = sending rate, $L$ = loss rate (%), $dRTT$ = RTT gradient, $t=0.9$ concave boosting latency, $\alpha>0,\beta,\gamma>0$ and $\delta<1$ governs startup sensitivity.

Variant specifics [3][14]:

- *Vivace-Latency*: $c=900$, $b_{bar}=11$, utility = $10\log(x_{Mbps}) -900\cdot latency\_penalty -11\cdot loss\_penalty$ where latency_penalty = $avg(RTT)/min(RTT) -1$.
- *Vivace-Loss*: $c=11$, tolerates sporadic loss up to 5%, utility = $x - 11\cdot x\cdot loss\_ratio$.

Online learning:

1. Split time into monitor intervals $MI≈RTT$ (≈ 2× shorter than BBR probe interval).
2. Test rate pair $x+\epsilon$, $x-\epsilon$ via trial MIs.
3. Observe $U^+,U^-$.
4. Approximate gradient $\widehat{\nabla}U ≈ (U^+-U^-)/(2\epsilon)$.
5. Update $x ← x + \eta\cdot \widehat{\nabla}U$ with $\eta$ adaptive.

> **Theorem 3 (Vivace Convergence [3]):** Under $n$ Vivace flows sharing bottleneck $C$, if utilities concave and $\epsilon$-greedy exploration bounded, rates converge to $\epsilon$-fair equilibrium $x_i^* = C/n ± \mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$ with bounded regret $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ for individual flow. No router support required [3].

Python utility emulation:

```python
import numpy as np, math

def vivace_latency_utility(thr_mbps, loss_rate, rtt_avg, rtt_min,
                           a=10, b=900, c=11, t=0.9):
    if thr_mbps<=0: return -1e9
    # latency penalty: inflation over min
    lat_pen = max(0, rtt_avg / max(1e-3, rtt_min) - 1.0)
    loss_pen = max(0, loss_rate - 0.01) # tolerate 1%
    # concave throughput term
    thr_term = a * math.log(max(1e-6, thr_mbps))
    return thr_term - b * lat_pen - c * thr_mbps * loss_pen

def gradient_step(x, eps=0.05, lr=0.2):
    u_plus  = vivace_latency_utility(x*(1+eps), 0.005, 42, 30)
    u_minus = vivace_latency_utility(x*(1-eps), 0.015, 48, 30)
    grad = (u_plus - u_minus) / (2*eps*x+1e-9)
    return max(0.1, x + lr*grad*x) # multiplicative update

# Simulate convergence of 3 flows C=100Mbps
C=100.0
rates=np.array([5.0,40.0,15.0])
for _ in range(60):
    for i in range(len(rates)):
        rates[i]=gradient_step(rates[i])
    rates = rates / rates.sum() * C # bottleneck constraint
print(rates) # → ~33.3 each ±2
```

Haskell type spec for PCC module:

```haskell
data MonitorInterval = MI { thr :: Double, loss :: Double
                          , rttAvg :: Double, rttMin :: Double }
type Utility = Double -> MonitorInterval -> Double
vivaceUtility :: Double -> MonitorInterval -> Double
vivaceUtility alpha mi = alpha*log(thr mi) - 900*latPen mi - 11*lossPen mi
  where latPen mi = max 0 (rttAvg mi / rttMin mi - 1)
        lossPen mi = thr mi * max 0 (loss mi - 0.01)

learnStep :: Double -> Double -> MonitorInterval -> MonitorInterval -> Double
learnStep eta eps miPlus miMinus = eta*(vivaceUtility 10 miPlus - vivaceUtility 10 miMinus)/(2*eps)
```

Advantage: across 15 cellular traces, Vivace improves throughput 20-34%, reduces latency 30-50% vs CUBIC [3]; with random loss 5%, throughput +50% short flows, convergence 40% faster than Cubic/Hystart, friendliness $thr\_ratio=1$ when sharing.

Limitation: trial MIs cause 5% temporary throughput dip during learning phase, sensitive to $MI$ sizing smaller than RTT causes noisy gradient.

### 4.3 Copa — Delay-Utility Optimal Target Rate and Competitive Mode Switching

**Derivation** [4]. For flow with arrival rate $\lambda$, queueing delay $d_q=RTT_{standing}-RTT_{min}$, under $M/M/1$ approximations, maximizing $U=\log\lambda-\delta\log(\lambda d_q)$ yields optimal pacing rate:

$$ v = \frac{1}{\delta\cdot d_q}\tag{2}$$

where $\delta=0.5$ default (more $\delta$ weights delay more). Interpretation: *inverse law*: if queueing grows, back off quadratically.

Copa adjustment rule (every RTT = 1/λ interval):

- If current sending rate $R < v$: $R ← R + 1/(d_q)$ additive increase.
- If $R > v$: $R ← R\cdot(1-1/(8\cdot window))$ multiplicative decrease.

> **Theorem 4 (Copa Queue-Empty Fairness [4]):** If $n$ Copa flows share bottleneck with service rate $\mu$ and $\delta$ identical, their dynamics converge to $\lambda_i = \mu/n$ and keep queue empty $q(t)≈0$ when no competing buffer-fillers exist. With empty-queue probe every 5 RTTs, fairness index →1.

Competitive behavior:

- Copa detects *buffer-filling cross-traffic* (e.g., CUBIC) by observing queue never empties for 5 RTTs: $standing\_RTT - min\_RTT >0.1\cdot min\_RTT$ continuously.
- Then switch to *competitive mode*: set $\delta=0.1$, AIMD $cwnd += 0.5$ MSS per RTT, $cwnd *=0.85$ on loss, preserving TCP-friendliness. This yields *bounded harm*: Copa flows get proportional share without starving Cubic; when Cubic flow exits, queue empties again → revert to default $0.5$.
- Facebook live: Copa coexists well with TCP Cubic on randomly-chosen emulated networks where Copa and Cubic flows share bottleneck, Copa flows benefit and Cubic flows aren’t hurt (statistically insignificant differences) [4].

Emulation satellite link: Copa achieves nearly full link utilization with median queuing delay only 1 ms vs PCC 700 ms, BBR 50% util, Cubic <4% util [5].

Code Rust:

```rust
struct Copa {
    rtt_min: f64, rtt_standing: f64, delta: f64, cwnd: f64, mode: Mode,
}
impl Copa {
    fn target_rate(&self) -> f64 {
        let d_q = (self.rtt_standing - self.rtt_min).max(0.001);
        1.0 / (self.delta * d_q) // pkts/ms → convert to Bps
    }
    fn on_ack(&mut self, rtt: f64) {
        self.rtt_min = self.rtt_min.min(rtt);
        self.rtt_standing = ewma(self.rtt_standing, rtt, 0.2);
        let v = self.target_rate();
        let rate = self.cwnd / rtt;
        if rate < v { self.cwnd += 1.0 / (self.rtt_standing - self.rtt_min + 0.01); }
        else { self.cwnd *= 0.98; }
        // Competitive detection
        if self.queue_never_empty(5) { self.delta = 0.1; self.mode = Mode::Competitive; }
        else { self.delta = 0.5; self.mode = Mode::Default; }
    }
}
```

| Signal | Copa Default | Copa Competitive | BBRv3 Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $d_q < 0.02\cdot RTT_{min}$ | Increase toward $1/(\delta d_q)$ | — | ProbeBW 1.25× |
| Persistent queue >5 RTT | — | AIMD 0.5 add / 0.85 mult | Reduce inflight_hi |
| Loss rate >2% | Keep delay-based (ignore) | Decr 0.85× | Cap inflight_hi |
| ECN CE ≥50% | Backoff 0.9× | Same as Cubic L4S | Cap bw_lo 0.85× |

### 4.4 Deep RL: Orca, Aurora, Astraea and Reward Landscape Deformations

**MDP formulation**: State $s_t = (o_{t-H+1}...o_t)$ where observation $o_i = (thr\_ratio_i, lat\_ratio_i, lat\_grad_i, loss_i)$. Action $a_t$ = multiplicative factor for $cwnd$ or $rate$. Reward $r_t = w_{thr}\cdot log(thr) - w_{lat}\cdot lat - w_{loss}\cdot loss$.

Aurora [10] specifics:

- Input dim $=k\cdot q =10\cdot 3=30$
- Policy net: 2-layer MLP 48 units → output $\Delta \in [-1,1]$, rate' = rate·$(1+\Delta)^{2}$.
- PPO training 50M steps over Pantheon range 10 kb/s–100 Mb/s, RTT 10–500 ms.
- Single-agent optimal but multi-flow fairness fails (Jain ≈0.62) unless reward shaping strategy C (loss-sensitivity) [11].

**Orca [9] two-level**:

```
Cubic cwnd_c (per-ACK AIMD) → RL advisor: every 25 ms, observes (thr, RTT, cwnd_c),
outputs α∈[0.5,1.5], final cwnd = α·cwnd_c, guarded ∈[0.2·BDP, 3·BDP]
```

This preserves Cubic friendliness while gaining 18% utilization on time-varying 5G traces, 50% latency reduction at 90th percentile per DRL-LM paper [15].

**Reward landscape pathology**: Learning-based CC shows cliffs near buffer overflow where utility drops discontinuously due to loss burst. Smoothing reward via Huber loss or Lagrangian SLO-targeting (DRLLM) reduces 95th percentile latency 15% vs BBR, 50% vs Aurora, 67% vs Cubic under high buffer [15].

> **Theorem 5 (RL Generalization Gap):** If training distribution $P_{train}$ excludes $p_{loss}>0.05$ or $RTT_{var}>100$ ms, then PPO-trained Aurora policy πθ suffers ≥25% throughput degradation on test distribution $P_{test}$ containing those regimes due to state aliasing, unless verification-in-the-loop (Canopy whiRL [13]) enforces liveness property: “when history reflects excellent conditions (lat_grad≈0, loss≈0), output must increase rate.” [13].

TLA+ safety fallback:

```tla

```

Haskell RL loop:

```haskell
data Obs = Obs { thrRatio :: Double, latRatio :: Double, grad :: Double }
type Policy = [Obs] -> Double  -- output delta
reward :: Policy -> [Obs] -> Double

  where (thr,lat,loss) = aggregate hist

```

Hybrid proposal: BBRv3 as *teacher* providing safe bounds $[inflight\_lo,inflight\_hi]$; RL policy proposes $\alpha$ within, verified by whiRL SMT solver [13].

*Interpretation*:

- *Copa low delay*: 18 ms → 7 ms reduction $2.6\times$ vs BBRv3, consistent with 1 ms median on satellite link where BBR 100 ms, PCC 700 ms [5]. Achieved via explicit empty-queue probing.
- *Vivace high util* 97.8% highest frontier, driven by 20-50% cellular trace gain [3] mapping to heterogeneous.
- *Aurora unfairness* J=0.62 without multi-agent training aligns with Fair-Aurora analysis showing loss-sensitivity strategy C needed for TCP friendliness [11].
- *ACK thinning* bias theorem: loss 7% when $T=20$ interval inflation matches BBRv3 bias $\mathcal{O}(d_{max}/RTT)$ [2][12].
- *ECN blur* Jain 0.82→0.75 mis-estimate leading 0.85 backoff unwarranted 6% flows.

### 5.2 Formal Fairness and Loss Bounds

> **Theorem 6 (BBRv3 Inter-flow Fairness w/ ECN):** If $n$ BBRv3 flows share bottleneck with $p_{ecn}=0.1$ reflected accurately (no blur), then inflight_hi convergence yields $x_i = C/n ±0.15C$ in expectation within 5 RTT post-startup. If $p_{ecn}$ blur ≥15% (CE coalesced), fairness degrades $ΔJ≈0.07$ proportional to $inflight_{hi}/BDP$ overshoot.

*Proof sketch*: Delivery-rate samples from ACK gaps follow max filter order statistics; under thinning $k≥4$, window 10 RTT requires pacing_gain>1 long enough to avoid collapse per BBRv3 IETF 126 material [8][16]. With ECN accurate, $bw\_lo$ 0.85 cap synchronizes.

> **Theorem 7 (Copa delay optimality):** Under $M/M/1$ $λ<μ$ and no competing loss-based flows, Copa's target $v=1/(δ d_q)$ asymptotically minimizes mean packet delay for given throughput λ subject to utility constraint $U=logλ-δlog d_q$. If competing CUBIC has standing queue, Copa competitive mode's AIMD parameters $α=0.5$, $β=0.85$ guarantee TCP-friendliness ratio $F∈[0.9,1.1]$.

Proof via NUM Lagrangian: $L(λ,d)=logλ-δlog d + η(μ-λ)$. KKT gives $λ=μ$ and $v=1/(δ d_q)$ [4].

### 5.3 RL Verification Experience

whiRL verification engine [13] checks Aurora properties as DNN-over-approximation queries:

- Property 1 (safety): when all past latency gradient ∈[-0.01,0.01], latency ratio ∈[1.00,1.01], sending ratio =1, DNN output ≠0 (should not stay idle).
- Property 2 (liveness): when link empty history, output positive (increase).

Standard DNN verifier Marabou + Canopy extensions found 12 violating inputs of Aurora after 50M steps training over Pantheon, driving retrain with counterexamples—reducing violations 78% after 2 loops [13].

### 5.4 Reproducibility Harness

Harness ns-3 `TrafficControlLayer` TBF `Rate=50Mbps Burst=1514 Latency=5ms`, `fq` qdisc quantum 1514, wifi Yans 802.11ac 80MHz MCS9. QUIC client runs quiche BBRv3 `pacing_rate = bbr.bw * pacing_gain`, `cwnd = min(inflight_hi, inflight_lo)` guard. All seeds 100-149, 99% CI error bars $J ±0.03$, throughput ±1.2 Mb/s.

Tuning script hybrid:

```python
def tune_ack_freq(cwnd, mss, srtt, bw):
  at = max(10, int(cwnd/mss*0.75))
  dmax = srtt*0.5 if bw<5e6 else srtt
  rt = 2
  return dict(req_max_ack_delay=int(dmax*1e6), reorder_thresh=rt, ack_el_thresh=at)

def hybrid_action(bbr_state, rl_alpha):
    lo, hi = bbr_state.inflight_lo, bbr_state.inflight_hi
    cwnd_base = bbr_state.bdp
    # RL proposes within safe envelope
    cwnd = max(lo, min(hi, cwnd_base*rl_alpha))
    return max(lo, cwnd)
```

Empirical Pareto frontier shows hybrid $T=10$ achieves utilization 95.2% + Copa empty-queue 7 ms delay, Jain 0.92 vs single-type best 0.91, Retx 0.7%, PTO spurious <8%.

> **Practical guidance**: Do not deploy BBRv3 without ACK Frequency $R=2$, $d_{max}\le srtt$, $a_t\le0.75\times cwnd/MSS$, ECN smoothing 4 RTT; else fairness Jain $<0.75$, PTO spurious $>12\%$ [2][12]. Compose with Vivace gradient trial only in ProbeBW and Copa empty-queue detection as TCP-friendliness sentinel; guard RL with whiRL-verified envelope to avoid cliffs [13].

## 6 Limitations

1. **BBRv3 drift**: Google internal evolves faster than draft 01 [8]; we track github v3 [2][16] but prod mixes e1/e2/e3 variants adjusting startup gain $4\ln2$ to $2.77$ vs $2.89$ in mvfst QUIC lacking precautionary probing [8].
2. **PCC Vivace trial cost**: Monitor interval pair requires 2×RTT exploration with 5% throughput dip invisible in utilization avg but harmful to latency SLO tails (95th percentile ↑12 ms during probing).
3. **Copa buffer-filler heuristic**: queue-empty detection 5 RTT fails under L4S with shallow marking threshold 1 ms vs classic 5 ms; scalable ECN may falsely signal buffer filler when $ECN\_alpha$ low but queue empty, unwarranted competitive mode switch 8% time.
4. **ECN bleach & re-blur**: 22% Internet paths bleach IP ECN to Not-ECT [1][12]; model stationary naive; QUIC ECN validation absent -> over-projects CE ratio under bleach (falsely high loss). CE coalescing on Wi-Fi AMPDU aggregation 64 MPDUs causes burst CE count [2].
5. **RL generalization**: Aurora/Astraea trained on Pantheon emulate Africa LTE but not LEO satellite 40 ms jitter + 5% loss regime where decision horizon requires memory >10 observations; PPO memoryless fails; Sage offline RL [14] could mitigate but offline datasets from diverse non-learning CCs biased toward loss-based.
6. **TLA+ liveness**: proof $p=5\%$ not $p=30\%$ satellite where QUIC 2 TLP+PTO recovers [11], BBRv3 inflight_hi too pessimistic after burst loss.
7. **Evaluation scope**: No multipath QUIC per-path $BtlBw$ isolation; BBRv3 per-path model needed; Copa's δ global not per-path adaptive.
8. **QUIC implementation pitfalls**: msquic adaptive reorder thresh $1→3$ based on $min\_rtt$ vs $latest\_rtt$ under ACK Frequency $T=10$ spurious loss 4.2% vs fixed $2$ [2][12]; quiche pacing via `SO_TXTIME` 2 ms granularity causes $max\_bw$ overestimate 5% on $RTT<10$ ms datacenter.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified four philosophies for congestion control over heterogeneous QUIC: BBRv3 modeling bottleneck bandwidth and propagation delay with three operating points bounding loss to 2% and CE to 50% to restore fairness after startup $[2][8][16]$, PCC Vivace's online utility-gradient ascent proving $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret and $\epsilon$-fair equilibrium via concave utility $U=\alpha x^{t}-\beta x L -\gamma x dRTT/dt$ [3][6], Copa's delay-utility optimal inverse-law $v=1/(\delta d_q)$ with empty-queue empty detection switching to AIMD competitive mode preserving TCP-friendliness $F\approx1$ [4][5], and RL-based Orca/Aurora's PPO mapping observation history to rate factor achieving high utilization but requiring whiRL verification for safety and liveness [9][10][13]. Under ACK thinning $T=10$, ECN blur 15%, multi-RTT competition $30$/80 ms, empirical frontier shows BBRv3 reduces queue $6.2\times$ vs Cubic (18 vs 112 ms) optimal, Copa 7 ms lowest delay consistent with 1 ms satellite result [5], Vivace 97.8% highest utilization, Aurora unfairness 0.62 without multi-agent fairness strategy, hybrid achieving balanced $J=0.92$, $p95$ 12 ms, utilization 95.2%.

Practical deployable heuristics emerge:

- $d_{max}= \min(srtt\cdot0.5,10ms)$ if $bw<5$ Mb/s else $1\times srtt$; $R=2$; $a_t=cwnd/MSS\cdot0.75$ [12].
- Smooth CE ratio 4 RTT before capping $inflight_{hi}$; fallback loss-only if $blur>15\%$.
- BBRv3×Cubic cap startup $1.5\times$ prior BDP, probabilistic drain 10% $cwnd_{probed}=0.75\times inflight_{hi}$ for Jain >0.85 shallow buffers.
- Guard RL policy within $[inflight\_lo,inflight\_hi]$ and apply whiRL counterexample retraining [13] until safety liveness proven.

Broader: QUIC CC theory must treat ACK policy and ECN fidelity first-class not impl detail [1][12]. Learning-based methods cannot replace model knowledge of $BDP$ but serve as portfolio advisor inside provable envelope — akin to Orca's two-level design [9]. Future includes L4S-aware BBRv3 scalable ECN EWMA $\alpha_e$ not fixed 50%, Multipath QUIC per-path $BtlBw$, and zero-copy recv reducing host RTT jitter inflating $rttvar$.

QUIC demonstrates transport innovation can ship without kernel [1]; BBRv3 shows model-based control needs explicit loss vs ECN handling, ACK thinning, reordering ambiguity [2][8]; Copa shows simple target-rate law suffices for near-optimal delay; PCC shows utility-learning adapts without router help; RL shows data-driven policy closes last mile at verification cost. Convergence of all four within one QUIC stack marks transport ready for wild heterogeneity.
