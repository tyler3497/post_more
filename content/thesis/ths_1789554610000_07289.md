# Mean Field Games: Lasry–Lions Monotonicity, the Master Equation, and Propagation of Chaos

## Abstract

Mean field games (MFG) provide a rigorous mathematical framework for the analysis of *N*-player stochastic differential games in the limit of infinitely many indistinguishable, individually negligible agents. This thesis develops the core analytic spine of the theory: the derivation of the coupled forward-backward Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman and Fokker–Planck system characterizing mean field equilibria, the *Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition* and the uniqueness theorem it furnishes, and the *master equation* — an infinite-dimensional PDE on Wasserstein space whose solution serves as the decoupling field of the entire hierarchy. We present the Lions derivative on the space of probability measures, global well-posedness results for the master equation under monotonicity, the extension to common noise via stochastic MFG systems, and the quantitative *propagation of chaos* that justifies the mean field limit with explicit convergence rates for Nash equilibria of the finite-player games. Discrete-time and finite-state variants, crowd motion and energy market applications, and the modern learning-theoretic viewpoint (fictitious play, reinforcement learning) are treated, concluding with the principal open problems, most notably the regularity of the master equation with common noise.

## 1 Intro

Consider a game with a very large number of players, each controlling a private stochastic state — position, wealth, inventory, or infection status — while interacting with the *aggregate* behavior of the population rather than with any individual opponent. Examples abound: pedestrians navigating a crowded plaza, banks lending in an interbank market, producers bidding into an electricity spot market, individuals choosing social distancing levels during an epidemic. When the number of players *N* is in the thousands or millions, direct computation of Nash equilibria through coupled Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman systems of *N* equations is hopeless. Mean field game theory, introduced independently by Lasry and Lions and by Huang, Malhamé and Caines in 2006–2007, replaces the intractable *N*-player problem by a *limit* problem in which a single representative agent optimizes against the evolving statistical distribution of the population, and that distribution is in turn generated by the collective optimal behavior of all agents [1][2].

The mathematical object at the heart of the theory is the **mean field game system**: a coupled forward-backward system of partial differential equations consisting of a backward Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation for the value function of the representative agent and a forward Fokker–Planck (Kolmogorov) equation for the evolution of the population density. The coupling is *forward-backward in time*: the value function is computed backward from a terminal cost, while the density is transported forward from an initial distribution, and each equation feeds into the other. Such forward-backward structures are notoriously delicate — existence typically follows from fixed-point or variational arguments, while uniqueness is far from automatic.

Uniqueness is delivered by the **Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition**, a structural assumption on the coupling costs with a transparent economic meaning: agents dislike crowded regions (congestion) or, in the opposite regime, are attracted to them. Under monotonicity, the MFG system admits at most one classical solution, and moreover the *N*-player Nash equilibria converge to the mean field equilibrium as *N* grows [3].

The deepest layer of the theory is the **master equation**, introduced by Lions in his Collège de France lectures. It is a PDE posed on the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space of probability measures, governing the value of a representative agent as a function not only of time and private state but of the *entire population distribution*. Its solution acts as a decoupling field: evaluating the master equation's solution along the flow of the population density recovers the MFG system, and through it one constructs approximate Nash equilibria for the finite-player games with quantitative error bounds. The master equation is thus the MFG analogue of the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation in classical stochastic control, elevated to the space of measures [2][4].

This thesis presents a self-contained, research-level exposition: the *N*-player model and the formal mean field limit; the derivation and analysis of the forward-backward system; the Lasry–Lions monotonicity and the uniqueness theorem; the Lions derivative and the master equation, including its well-posedness theory and the common-noise extension; propagation of chaos and convergence rates via coupling arguments; finite-state and discrete-time variants; applications; learning dynamics; and the open problems that define the frontier.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The N-player stochastic differential game

Fix a finite horizon *T* and a state space *R^d*. For *N* players indexed by *i*, each player controls a private state process *X^i* evolving according to

```text
dX_t^i = b(X_t^i, alpha_t^i, m_t^{N}) dt + sigma dW_t^i,   X_0^i = xi^i,
```

where *alpha^i* is the player's control, *W^i* are independent Brownian motions, *xi^i* are i.i.d. initial conditions, and *m_t^N = (1/N) sum_j delta_{X_t^j}* is the *empirical measure* of the population. Each player minimizes an expected cost

```text
J^i(alpha) = E[ integral_0^T f(X_t^i, alpha_t^i, m_t^N) dt + g(X_T^i, m_T^N) ],
```

where the running cost *f* and terminal cost *g* depend on the empirical distribution of all players. A **Nash equilibrium** is a profile of (possibly randomized, possibly closed-loop) strategies from which no player can profitably deviate unilaterally.

For fixed *N*, the value functions of the *N* players satisfy a coupled system of *N* Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations — the **Nash system** — whose analysis is essentially intractable for large *N*. The mean field program sidesteps this curse of dimensionality by passing to the limit *N* approaching infinity.

### 2.2 The mean field heuristic

As *N* grows, each individual player has negligible influence on the empirical measure *m^N*.

### 2.3 Historical development

The theory was launched in 2006–2007 by three seminal contributions: Lasry and Lions' series of papers introducing the PDE system and the monotonicity condition [3]; Huang, Malhamé and Caines' "Nash certainty equivalence" principle for large-population stochastic dynamic games [2]; and Lions' lectures at the Collège de France, where the master equation was first formulated.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 From the N-player game to the MFG system

Fix a deterministic flow of measures *m = (m_t)*.

```text
u(t,x) = inf_alpha E[ integral_t^T f(X_s, alpha_s, m_s) ds + g(X_T, m_T) | X_t = x ],
```

with dynamics *dX_s = b(X_s, alpha_s, m_s) ds + sigma dW_s*.

```text
-partial_t u - nu Delta u + H(x, nabla u, m) = 0,    u(T,x) = g(x, m_T),
```

where *nu = sigma^2 / 2* and the Hamiltonian *H(x,p,m) = sup_a { -b(x,a,m) dot p - f(x,a,m) }* encodes the optimized tradeoff between drift and cost.

```text
partial_t m - nu Delta m - div( m D_p H(x, nabla u, m) ) = 0,    m(0) = m_0.
```

The **MFG system** couples these two equations: *u* depends on *m* through the coupling costs, and *m* depends on *u* through the optimal feedback.

### 3.2 Solution strategy

Our methodology proceeds in four stages, each corresponding to a section of the deep dive:

1. **Uniqueness via monotonicity.** Prove that the Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition on the couplings *(F, G)* forces at most one classical solution of the MFG system, via an energy-type identity obtained by testing the difference of two solutions.
2. **Lifting to the master equation.** Introduce the Lions derivative on Wasserstein space and write the PDE for the value *U(t, x, mu)* of the game started at time *t* with private state *x* and population law *mu*; show that *u(t,x) = U(t, x, m_t)* recovers the MFG system.
3. **Common noise.** Extend the system to environments with aggregate shocks, where the measure flow becomes random and the master equation acquires second-order derivatives in the measure variable.
4. **Quantitative convergence.** Use coupling arguments and the master equation's regularity to prove propagation of chaos and explicit rates at which *N*-player Nash equilibria converge to the mean field limit.

### 3.3 Standing assumptions

Throughout, the Hamiltonian *H* is smooth, strictly convex and coercive in the momentum variable; the diffusion is non-degenerate (*nu > 0*); the initial law *m_0* has finite second moment and a smooth density; and the coupling functions *F(x, m)* and *G(x, m)* are smooth in *x* and continuous in *m* for the Wasserstein topology.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Lasry–Lions monotonicity and the uniqueness theorem

The couplings *F(x, m)* (running cost) and *G(x, m)* (terminal cost) encode how each agent's cost depends on the population.

```text
integral ( F(x, m_1) - F(x, m_2) ) d(m_1 - m_2)(x)  >=  0,
```

and similarly for *G*.

> **Theorem:** (Lasry–Lions uniqueness). Assume the Hamiltonian is strictly convex in the momentum variable and the couplings *F, G* satisfy the Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition. Then the second-order MFG system admits at most one classical solution *(u, m)*.

*Idea of proof.* Let *(u_1, m_1)* and *(u_2, m_2)* be two solutions. Subtracting the HJB equations, multiplying by *(m_1 - m_2)*, integrating over space-time, and integrating by parts against the difference of the Fokker–Planck equations produces an identity in which the strict convexity of *H* contributes a non-negative term proportional to the squared difference of the gradients, while the monotonicity of *F* and *G* contributes non-negative boundary terms. The sum of non-negative terms being zero forces *nabla u_1 = nabla u_2* and *m_1 = m_2* wherever the density is positive, whence uniqueness. This "energy" or "duality" argument is the workhorse of the entire theory: every uniqueness or stability estimate in MFG is a variation on it [1][3].

Monotonicity also yields *a priori* estimates: the same computation applied to a single solution against a reference flow gives *L^2* bounds on *nabla u* that are the starting point for existence via Schauder fixed-point arguments.

### 4.2 The master equation on Wasserstein space

The master equation lifts the value function to the space *P_2(R^d)* of probability measures with finite second moment, equipped with the Wasserstein-2 metric.

```text
-partial_t U - nu Delta_x U + H(x, nabla_x U, mu)
   + integral div_y[ partial_mu U(t,x,mu)(y) ] d mu(y)
   - integral D_p H(y, nabla_x U(t,y,mu), mu) dot partial_mu U(t,x,mu)(y) d mu(y) = 0,
```

with terminal condition *U(T, x, mu) = G(x, mu)*.

> **Theorem:** (Global well-posedness under monotonicity). Under the Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition and sufficient smoothness of the data, the master equation admits a unique global classical solution *U*. The pair *(u(t,x), m(t))* defined by *u(t,x) = U(t,x,m_t)* and the Fokker–Planck flow solves the MFG system, and *U* serves as its decoupling field.

The proof, completed in the monograph of Cardaliaguet, Delarue, Lasry and Lions [4], proceeds by representing *U* through the MFG system itself: for each initial condition *(t_0, x, mu)*, solve the MFG system on the remaining horizon with initial law *mu*, and set *U(t_0, x, mu) = u(t_0, x)*.

Without monotonicity, only *local-in-time* classical solutions are known in general; global well-posedness for the fully nonlinear master equation arising with volatility control remains completely open [8].

### 4.3 Common noise and stochastic MFG

In many applications — financial markets, epidemics, energy systems — all agents are simultaneously subjected to *aggregate shocks*: interest rate moves, new variants, weather events.

The master equation with common noise acquires additional second-order terms in the measure variable, involving the second Lions derivative *partial_{mu mu} U* contracted against the common-noise volatility.

### 4.4 Propagation of chaos and quantitative convergence

**Propagation of chaos**, in the sense of Kac and Sznitman, is the statement that symmetric *N*-particle systems become asymptotically independent: the joint law of any fixed number *k* of particles converges to the *k*-fold product of the limiting one-particle law as *N* grows [10]. For MFG, the relevant question is sharper: do the *Nash equilibria* of the *N*-player game converge to the mean field equilibrium, and at what rate?

The master equation is the key instrument.

The workhorse estimate is a **coupling argument**: construct on the same probability space the *N*-player Nash trajectories and *N* i.i.d.

| Setting | Quantity | Typical rate | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open-loop Nash, smooth data | value convergence | *O(N^{-1/2})* | master equation method [4] |
| Closed-loop Nash, monotone couplings | equilibrium convergence | *O(N^{-1/2})* up to logs | Delarue–Lacker–Ramanan [11] |

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 The coupling proof of propagation of chaos

We sketch Sznitman's synchronous coupling for the McKean–Vlasov equation, the template for all quantitative MFG convergence. Consider *N* exchangeable particles with mean field interaction

```python
# dX^i_t = b(X^i_t, mu^N_t) dt + sigma dW^i_t,  mu^N_t = (1/N) sum_j delta_{X^j_t}
```

and *N* i.i.d. copies *bar{X}^i* of the nonlinear limit process *d bar{X}_t = b(bar{X}_t, mu_t) dt + sigma dW^i_t* driven by the *same* Brownian motions. Writing the difference and using Lipschitz continuity of *b* with constant *L*,

```text
|X^i_t - bar{X}^i_t| <= L integral_0^t ( |X^i_s - bar{X}^i_s| + W_1(mu^N_s, mu_s) ) ds.
```

Decomposing *W_1(mu^N_s, mu_s) <= W_1(mu^N_s, bar{mu}^N_s) + W_1(bar{mu}^N_s, mu_s)*, where *bar{mu}^N* is the empirical measure of the i.i.d. copies, the first term is controlled by the average coupling distance and the second is a pure fluctuation term of order *N^{-1/2}* by the law of large numbers in Wasserstein space. Grönwall's inequality then yields *E|X^i_t - bar{X}^i_t|^2 <= C(T,L)/N*, i.e. chaos propagates with rate *1/sqrt(N)* in *W_2* [10]. The MFG refinement replaces the fixed drift *b* by the feedback *D_p H(x, nabla_x U(t,x,mu))*, where the master equation's Lipschitz regularity in *mu* is exactly what the coupling needs — this is why master equation regularity *is* the convergence theory [4].

### 5.2 Fixed-point iteration for the forward-backward system

Numerically, the MFG system is solved by iterating between the backward HJB equation and the forward Fokker–Planck equation. The following Python prototype implements this Picard iteration on a one-dimensional periodic grid with a congestion coupling, using damping for stability:

```python
import numpy as np

def solve_mfg_1d(nx=200, nt=100, T=1.0, nu=0.1, beta=1.0, n_iter=60, damp=0.5):
    """Picard fixed point for the 1D periodic MFG system.
    Coupling F(x, m) = beta * m  (Lasry-Lions monotone for beta >= 0)."""
    dx, dt = 1.0 / nx, T / nt
    x = np.linspace(0, 1, nx, endpoint=False)
    m = np.ones((nt + 1, nx)) / nx          # initial guess: uniform flow
    m[0] = np.exp(-40 * (x - 0.5) ** 2); m[0] /= m[0].sum()

    for it in range(n_iter):
        # --- backward HJB: -u_t - nu u_xx + 0.5 (u_x)^2 = beta * m ---
        u = np.zeros((nt + 1, nx))
        for n in range(nt - 1, -1, -1):
            ux = (np.roll(u[n + 1], -1) - np.roll(u[n + 1], 1)) / (2 * dx)
            uxx = (np.roll(u[n + 1], -1) - 2 * u[n + 1] + np.roll(u[n + 1], 1)) / dx ** 2
            u[n] = u[n + 1] + dt * (-nu * uxx + 0.5 * ux ** 2 - beta * m[n])
        # --- forward FP: m_t - nu m_xx - div(m * (-u_x)) = 0 ---
        m_new = np.zeros((nt + 1, nx)); m_new[0] = m[0]
        for n in range(nt):
            ux = (np.roll(u[n], -1) - np.roll(u[n], 1)) / (2 * dx)
            flux = m_new[n] * (-ux)                       # optimal drift
            div = (np.roll(flux, -1) - np.roll(flux, 1)) / (2 * dx)
            mxx = (np.roll(m_new[n], -1) - 2 * m_new[n] + np.roll(m_new[n], 1)) / dx ** 2
            m_new[n + 1] = m_new[n] + dt * (nu * mxx - div)
            m_new[n + 1] = np.clip(m_new[n + 1], 0, None)
            m_new[n + 1] /= m_new[n + 1].sum() * dx * nx / nx
        m = damp * m_new + (1 - damp) * m                 # damped update
    return x, u, m

# The iteration contracts under small T or small beta; monotonicity (beta >= 0)
# guarantees the limit, when it converges, is the unique equilibrium.
```

For the congestion coupling the iterates converge to the crowd-averse equilibrium: mass spreads away from regions of high density, and the value function develops the characteristic "repulsion" gradient. In practice, Newton or multigrid methods replace Picard iteration, and variational formulations via the Benamou–Brenier dynamic optimal transport viewpoint admit augmented Lagrangian solvers [7].

## 6 Limitations

The theory's strength is also its boundary. **First**, global classical well-posedness of the master equation is known essentially only under monotonicity (Lasry–Lions or displacement) or for short horizons; the fully nonlinear master equation with volatility control is wide open [8]. **Second**, the Lasry–Lions condition rules out *attractive* interactions — herding, conformity, network effects — where multiple equilibria are the rule and selection principles are poorly understood. **Third**, quantitative convergence rates for closed-loop equilibria with *common noise* remain fragmentary, and the regularity theory of the master equation with common noise (second-order Lions derivatives) is the subject's most important open analytic problem. **Fourth**, the learning theory (fictitious play, RL) assumes exact or near-exact best responses and monotone structure; sample-efficient learning of MFG equilibria with function approximation and without monotonicity has essentially no rigorous foundation. **Fifth**, most rigorous results require non-degenerate idiosyncratic noise and smooth data; first-order (deterministic) MFG, state constraints, and singular couplings (e.g. density constraints in crowd motion) demand viscosity or variational techniques with weaker conclusions [7]. Finally, the mean field limit itself can fail: with multiple MFG equilibria, *N*-player equilibria may converge to different limits along subsequences — monotonicity is doing real work in every convergence theorem.

## 7 Conclusion

Mean field games resolve the tension between strategic sophistication and population scale by replacing *N* coupled optimization problems with a single forward-backward system, and then with a single PDE on the space of measures. The Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition is the linchpin: it yields uniqueness of the MFG system, global well-posedness of the master equation, stability of fictitious play learning, and quantitative convergence of *N*-player Nash equilibria through propagation of chaos. The master equation, with its Lions derivatives on Wasserstein space, is both the deepest analytic object in the theory and its most powerful applied tool, furnishing approximate equilibria for otherwise intractable large games.

The frontier is clear: common-noise regularity of the master equation, equilibrium selection without monotonicity, convergence of model-free multi-agent learning, and the fully nonlinear volatility-controlled theory. What began as a pragmatic approximation for large games has become a meeting point of PDE theory, stochastic analysis, optimal transport, and game theory — and its core ideas now propagate far beyond the models that spawned them.
