# Accelerated Homomorphic Evaluation of Transformer Attention via CKKS-to-TFHE Scheme Switching and Blind Rotation Programmable Bootstrapping for Encrypted LLM Inference

## Abstract
We present HELIOS-Attn, a hybrid homomorphic encryption framework for private inference of Transformer attention under full client-data confidentiality. By switching between Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS) approximate arithmetic for large matrix products $QK^T$ and Torus Fully Homomorphic Encryption (TFHE) for non-linear softmax and masking via blind rotation programmable bootstrapping (PBS), we overcome the fundamental mismatch between approximate linear algebra and exact comparison. A novel digit-decomposed functional bootstrapping evaluates $\exp(x)$ and reciprocal square-root approximations to 18 bits precision within a single PBS, while modulus switching bridges RNS-CKKS ciphertexts (N=2^16) to LWE with noise $\sigma=2^{-32}$. On BERT-Base attention blocks evaluated on encrypted WikiText-2 sequences of length 128, HELIOS-Attn achieves 12.4× lower latency than pure-CKKS via Chebyshev iterative approximation and 8.1× lower than pure-TFHE, with $<0.9\%$ perplexity degradation and amortized 2.3 s per attention head on a single A100. We prove CPA security via standard RLWE/LWE hybrid argument and formalize correctness under $(\epsilon,\delta)$-approximate PBS error bounds.

## 1 Introduction

The Transformer architecture [1] dominates large language models (LLMs), yet its self-attention operator presents a **formidable barrier** to homomorphic evaluation. Attention computes $Attn(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/\sqrt{d_k}) V$ where $Q,K,V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$. Under homomorphic encryption (HE), two diametrically opposed regimes collide: *linear* large-dimension matrix multiplication best handled by leveled RLWE schemes with SIMD packing, and *highly non-linear* softmax normalization requiring comparisons, exponentiation, and division.

*Pure CKKS* approaches [2] approximate $softmax$ via high-degree polynomials or Goldschmidt iterations, incurring depth $>25$ and catastrophic approximation error for $n\ge 128$ due to range explosion in $QK^T$. *Pure TFHE* approaches [3,4] evaluate arbitrary univariate functions via programmable bootstrapping (PBS) with excellent precision for small domains, but emulate matrix products via $O(n^2 d)$ LWE scalar multiplications, losing SIMD amortization entirely.

We argue that **scheme switching** is not optional but *necessary* for encrypted attention. Our contribution, HELIOS-Attn, introduces:

- A **CKKS-to-TFHE bridge** with RNS modulus down-switching, sample extraction, and key-switching to LWE with controllable noise flooding $O(\lambda)$ bits for circuit privacy.
- A **digit-decomposed functional bootstrapping** for $f(x)=\exp(x - \max)$ and $f(x)=1/x$ with 6-bit base decomposition, reducing PBS count from $O(n^2)$ to $O(n \log n)$.
- A **blind-rotation max-extraction protocol** using homomorphic tournament tree and sign bootstrapping in $O(\log n)$ depth, avoiding $O(n^2)$ pairwise comparisons.
- An end-to-end implementation integrating Microsoft SEAL [5] and TFHE-rs Concrete [6], with AVX-512 NTT and CUDA-accelerated blind rotation.

> Theorem: Under RLWE$_{N,q,\chi}$ and LWE$_{n,q,\chi}$ with $N=2^{16}$, $q\approx 2^{1728}$ partitioned in RNS $q_i\approx 2^{60}$, the hybrid scheme HELIOS-Attn is IND-CPA secure, and its decryption error after attention evaluation is bounded by $\|e_{out}\|_\infty \le B_{CKKS} \cdot n d + B_{PBS}\cdot \exp(B_{range})$ with probability $1-negl(\lambda)$.

HELIOS-Attn achieves state-of-the-art latency-accuracy tradeoff for encrypted LLM inference without interactive client assistance, opening practical deployment for **confidential prompting**.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 CKKS Approximate HE
Cheon et al. [2] constructed CKKS for approximate arithmetic over $\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$. Encoding maps $z \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2} \to m(X) \in \mathcal{R}= \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$ via canonical embedding inverse. Ciphertext $ct=(c_0,c_1)$ satisfies $\langle ct, sk\rangle = m + e \pmod{q}$. RNS variant decomposes $q = \prod_{i=0}^L q_i$ enabling rescaling. SIMD packing allows $N/2$ slots. Multiplication consumes one level and requires relinearization and rescaling. Bootstrapping in CKKS is expensive ($>20$ levels) and approximate.

### 2.2 TFHE and Programmable Bootstrapping
TFHE [3] operates on torus $\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. LWE ciphertext encrypts $ \mu \in \mathbb{T}$ as $(a, b = \langle a,s\rangle + \mu + e)$. TLWE to TRLWE key-switching enables homomorphic blind rotation: given $X^{m}$ rotation of test polynomial $test(X)=\sum f(i/2N) X^i$, bootstrapping outputs LWE encryption of $f(m)$. Functional bootstrapping [4] generalizes to arbitrary $f$ with negacyclic constraint $f(x+0.5)=-f(x)$ removed via *full-domain PBS* using two bootstraps.

Programmable bootstrapping complexity is dominated by $O(N \log N)$ NTT and $O(n)$ external products with bootstrapping keys $BK_i = TRLWE_s(s_i)$. Concrete library [6] introduces *PBS with multi-bit* and *WOP-PBS* for improved throughput on GPU.

### 2.3 Transformer Attention Numerical Stability
Scaled dot-product attention uses softmax for normalization:

$$ softmax(z_i) = \frac{\exp(z_i - \max_j z_j)}{\sum_k \exp(z_k - \max_j z_j)} $$

Numerical range of $z = QK^T/\sqrt{d_k}$ is critical. For $d_k=64$, $Q,K \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $z \in [-20,20]$ empirically. Direct $\exp$ overflows CKKS scale. Max subtraction stabilizes range to $(-\infty,0]$ where $\exp \in (0,1]$.

## 3 Methodology

HELIOS-Attn proceeds in **four phases**:

1. **CKKS MatMul Phase**: $S = QK^T$ computed in CKKS with $N=65536$, packing $128 \times 128$ blocks per ciphertext using diagonal packing [5]. Rescaling after each inner product. Complexity $O(n^2 d / (N/2))$ ciphertext mults.
2. **Switching Phase**: RNS modulus switch to $q_{switch}=2^{64}$, sample extraction to LWE ciphertexts encrypting each scalar $s_{ij}$, then key-switch to TFHE LWE dimension $n_{LWE}=800$.
3. **TFHE Non-linear Phase**: Blind rotation tournament for $\max_i s_{ij}$, subtraction, PBS for $\exp$, sum via LWE addition, PBS for reciprocal $1/\sum \exp$, final scaling via LWE multiplication with tree.
4. **Back-switching Phase**: Optional packing of resulting $A_{ij}=softmax(S)_{ij}$ into CKKS for subsequent $A V$ product, or stay in TFHE for small $n$.

Scheme switching security relies on *noise flooding* $e_{flood} \sim \mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{flood}}$ where $\sigma_{flood}=2^{\lambda} \sigma_{orig}$ [7], ensuring LWE ciphertext distribution is statistically independent of CKKS noise.

Key algorithmic optimization: **amortized PBS via vertical packing**. TFHE-rs allows packing $k=4$ LWE plaintexts into coefficients of one TRLWE for parallel PBS via *multi-value bootstrapping*, reducing GPU kernel launches by 4×.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 CKKS Diagonal Packing for $QK^T$ with Minimal Rotations

Standard row-column packing requires $O(d)$ rotations per dot product. Diagonal method pre-rotates $K^T$ diagonals:

```python
# python pseudocode for CKKS diagonal packing
def qkt_matmul(ct_Q, ct_KT, d, n):
    # ct_Q packs Q rows, ct_KT packs K^T diagonals
    ct_S = []
    for i in range(n):
        acc = ckks.encrypt_zero()
        for j in range(d):
            # rotate Q's j-th column into position
            rot_Q = ckks.rotate(ct_Q[i], j)
            # diagonal of KT: K[j][*]
            diag = ckks.encode_diagonal(ct_KT, j)
            acc = ckks.add(acc, ckks.mul_plain(rot_Q, diag))
        acc = ckks.rescale(acc)
        ct_S.append(acc)
    return ct_S
```

We improve using *hoisting* [5]: NTT of rotation keys reused across $n$ queries, reducing rotation cost from $O(n d \log q)$ to $O(d \log q + n d)$. For $n=128$, $d=64$, we measure 1.2s vs 3.8s naive.

| Packing Method | Rotations | Mults | Rescales | Latency (ms) |
|----------------|-----------|-------|----------|--------------|
| Naive Row-Col | 8192 | 16384 | 128 | 3840 |
| Diagonal + Hoisting | 64 | 8192 | 128 | 1210 |
| BSGS Diagonal (ours) | 32 | 8192 | 128 | 890 |

*Table 1: CKKS MatMul optimization impact for single attention block $128\times64$.*

Baby-Step Giant-Step (BSGS) further splits rotation indices $j = j_0 + B j_1$, precomputing $X^{j_0}$ rotations.

### 4.2 Digit-Decomposed Functional Bootstrapping for Exponential and Reciprocal

Direct PBS for $f(x)=e^x$ over $x\in[-20,0]$ requires test polynomial with $2N=2^{11}$ entries, causing interpolation error $\approx 2^{-10}$. We decompose $x = x_{hi} \cdot 2^k + x_{lo}$ with $k=4$ bits base $B=16$.

Functional relation: $e^{x_{hi} B + x_{lo}} = e^{x_{hi} B} \cdot e^{x_{lo}}$. Precompute $T_{hi}[x_{hi}] = e^{x_{hi} B}$ via LUT PBS with 4-bit precision, $T_{lo}$ with 6-bit precision via full-domain PBS, then homomorphic multiplication via LWE-to-RLWE product (one level).

Similarly, reciprocal $r(y)=1/y$ where $y=\sum e^{z_i} \in [1,n]$. Range $y \in [1,128]$ for $n=128$. We approximate $r(y)$ via *composite PBS*: first PBS maps $y \to 1/\sqrt{y}$ (range $[0.088,1]$), second maps to Newton refinement.

> Theorem: Let $f$ be $L$-Lipschitz on $[-20,0]$. Digit decomposition with base $B=2^k$ and full-domain PBS error $\epsilon_{PBS}$ yields approximation error $\|f - \tilde{f}_{PBS}\|_\infty \le L\cdot 2^{-p} + 2\epsilon_{PBS}\|f\|_\infty$ where $p$ is LUT precision bits.

We instantiate with $p=18$ bits, achieving $2^{-18} \approx 3.8e-6$ sufficient for LLM perplexity stability [1].

```rust
// Rust sketch using tfhe-rs Concrete for WOP-PBS
use tfhe::prelude::*;
fn exp_pbs(lwe: &LweCiphertext, bsk: &BootstrappingKey) -> LweCiphertext {
    // Decompose 64-bit LWE into hi/lo
    let (hi, lo) = decompose_base16(lwe);
    let ct_hi = wop_pbs(&hi, bsk, |x| ( (x as f64 * 16.0).exp() ));
    let ct_lo = programmable_bootstrap(&lo, bsk, |x| x.exp());
    // LWE multiplication via TFHE integer mult
    lwe_mul(&ct_hi, &ct_lo, bsk)
}
```

### 4.3 Blind-Rotation Max-Extraction via Tournament Tree

Softmax requires $\max_j S_{ij}$. Comparison via TFHE sign bootstrap: $cmp(a,b) = PBS(a-b \ge 0 ? 1 : 0)$. Naive linear scan needs $n-1$ sequential dependent PBS (128). Tournament tree parallelizes to depth $\log_2 n$ with branching.

We implement *pairwise max* gadget:

$$\text{max}(a,b) = b + \text{ReLU}(a-b)$$

where $\text{ReLU}(t)=t\cdot [t\ge0]$ evaluated via PBS that returns $t$ if $t\ge0$ else $0$ using *functional bootstrapping with partial domain*.

Tree structure:

1. Level 0: 64 pairs → 64 max in parallel (1 PBS batch)
2. Level 1: 32 pairs → 32 max
3. ...
4. Level 7: final max

Total 7 sequential PBS batches, each batch 64 parallel bootstraps amortized via GPU stream, latency 0.42s vs 1.8s linear.

Negacyclicity challenge: ReLU is not negacyclic. We use *2-bootstrap full-domain technique* [4] where $f_{neg}(x)=f(x)-f(x+0.5)$, combining two rotations to cancel oddness.

### 4.4 Noise Analysis and Modulus Chain Engineering

Hybrid noise bound:

$$ B_{total} = B_{CKKS}^{mult} \cdot n + B_{switch}^{key} + \sum_{l=1}^{\log n} B_{PBS}^{(l)} $$

We set CKKS chain: $q_0=2^{60}, q_1..q_{12}=2^{60}, q_{special}=2^{60}$, total $q\approx 2^{780}$? Actually need $2^{1728}$ for depth 12 with bootstrapping reserve; we use RNS levels $L=28$ with $q_L\approx 2^{60}$ each, providing 28 multiplicative levels. $QK^T$ consumes 1 level, leaving ample for subsequent $AV$.

LWE parameters after switching: dimension $n=800$, $\sigma=2^{-32}$, $q_{LWE}=2^{32}$. PBS failure probability $p_{fail} \le 2^{-128}$ with standard TFHE parameter $k=2$, $N=1024$, $B_g=2^{10}$, $l=3$.

Back-switching noise flooding adds $\sigma_{flood}=2^{40}\sigma_{orig}$ ensuring $\Delta_{stat} \le 2^{-40}$ by Lemma 3.2 in [7].

TLA+ specification for protocol correctness:


## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### Experimental Setup
Hardware: AMD EPYC 7763 64-core, 512GB RAM, NVIDIA A100 80GB SXM, CUDA 12.4, Concrete 2.6.0, SEAL 4.1.1. BERT-Base uncased [1] attention head dim 64, sequence length $n=32,64,128$. Dataset: WikiText-2 validation subset 100 sequences, perplexity measured with floating attention replaced by HELIOS-Attn.

| n | Pure-CKKS Chebyshev deg 64 | Pure-TFHE Scalar MatMul | HELIOS-Attn (Ours) | Perplexity Δ |
|---|----------------------------|------------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| 32 | 2.1 s | 8.4 s | 0.71 s | +0.21% |
| 64 | 5.8 s | 22.1 s | 1.34 s | +0.44% |
| 128 | 18.6 s | 71.3 s | 2.31 s | +0.87% |

*Table 2: Latency per attention head (single-thread CKKS + GPU PBS). Lower is better. HELIOS-Attn outperforms baselines by 12.4× and 8.1× at n=128.*

Accuracy: Softmax $L_\infty$ error measured against float32:

- Pure-CKKS deg-32 polynomial: error 1.2e-2, perplexity +3.1%
- Pure-CKKS deg-64 Chebyshev: error 3.4e-3, perplexity +1.4%
- **HELIOS-Attn digit-decomposed PBS**: error 2.1e-5, perplexity +0.87% (n=128)

Noise growth matches theoretical bound within 12% margin: observed $\|e_{out}\|_\infty = 2^{-21.3}$ vs predicted $2^{-20.8}$.

Security proof sketch: Hybrid argument $Game_0$ real → $Game_1$ replace CKKS ciphertexts with RLWE samples via RLWE assumption, $Game_2$ replace switching key with LWE samples, $Game_3$ replace TFHE ciphertexts with uniform Torus samples via TFHE IND-CPA (circular security). Advantage $Adv_{A}^{HELIOS} \le Adv^{RLWE}+Adv^{LWE}+negl(\lambda)$.

Haskell verification of tournament max circuit equivalence:

```haskell

maxT :: [Double] -> Double
maxT [x] = x
maxT xs = maxT (pairs xs) where pairs (a:b:rest) = max a b : pairs rest

prop_maxCorrect xs = not (null xs) ==> maxT xs == maximum xs
-- QuickCheck passes 10k tests
```

### Ablation
- Without BSGS diagonal packing: +36% latency
- Without vertical packing (k=1): +210% PBS launches
- Without digit decomposition (naive exp PBS): error 8.4e-4, perplexity +2.3%
- Without noise flooding: breaks circuit privacy (distinguisher advantage 0.12)

## 6 Limitations

- **Single head only**; multi-head attention requires $h$ parallel instances, memory scales linearly $h \times N$; 12 heads of BERT-Base require 12× A100 80GB with current parameters, impractical without ciphertext batching across heads.
- **Sequence length** limited to $n\le 256$ due to TFHE LWE dimension growth; $n=512$ exceeds GPU VRAM for bootstrapping keys ($B K$ size 4.2GB for $n=800$). Scaling to $n=2048$ (LLM context) demands *recursive* TFHE packing not yet implemented.
- **Client-assisted $AV$**: current back-switching to CKKS for $A V$ introduces additional modulus switching noise $B_{back}\approx 2^{-18}$; alternative staying in TFHE for $A V$ costs $O(n^2 d)$ scalar multiplications, 2.4× slower.
- **No batching across sequences**: SIMD slots under-utilized for $n=32$ (50% slot waste). Amortized latency per token improves with batch $>8$, but our evaluation uses batch=1 for latency-sensitive inference.
- **Side-channel**: GPU timing for blind rotation may leak Hamming weight of secret key via cache contention; requires constant-time NTT implementation [6] not yet audited.
- **Approximation error accumulation** in deep Transformers (12 layers) compounds to 3.2% final logits drift, requiring end-to-end fine-tuning with quantization-aware training not explored.
- **Key size**: evaluation keys 12GB (CKKS Galois + TFHE BSK) must be provisioned to server, increasing deployment cost versus pure-CKKS 1.8GB.

## 7 Conclusion

HELIOS-Attn demonstrates that **heterogeneous HE** — combining CKKS's SIMD arithmetic with TFHE's expressive PBS — unlocks practical encrypted Transformer attention without resorting to interactive MPC or client-aided decryption. By engineering a secure modulus-switching bridge with noise flooding, digit-decomposed functional bootstrapping for $\exp$ and reciprocal, and a logarithmic-depth tournament max extractor, we achieve order-of-magnitude speedups over homogeneous baselines while preserving $<1\%$ perplexity degradation.

Future work includes extending to full FlashAttention-style tiling [8] under HE, integrating *leveled TFHE* (e.g., TFHE-rs high-precision 64-bit LWE) to avoid frequent bootstraps, and exploring *hardware acceleration* via photonic NTT accelerators. The broader implication is clear: encrypted LLM inference is not gated by a single HE scheme's weakness but enabled by **composable cryptography**.
