# Score-Based Diffusion Models: From Denoising Score Matching to the Continuous-Time SDE Formulation

## Abstract

Score-based diffusion models have redefined generative modeling by recasting synthesis as the reversal of a noise-driven diffusion process governed by the *score function* — the gradient of the log-density. This post traces the theoretical arc uniting three milestones: (i) Hyvärinen's score matching and Vincent's denoising score matching, which made score estimation tractable without normalizing constants; (ii) denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) [2], whose ε-prediction objective is provably equivalent to weighted denoising score matching; and (iii) the stochastic differential equation (SDE) formulation of Song et al. [1], which subsumes both discrete-time families in continuous time, yields exact likelihoods through the probability-flow ordinary differential equation, and enables flexible samplers including DDIM [3] and predictor–corrector schemes. We derive the reverse-time SDE, the VE/VP/sub-VP perturbation kernels, the probability-flow ODE, and the non-Markovian generalization behind DDIM, accompany each derivation with implementation-ready detail, and survey empirical benchmarks (FID, IS, NLL) that established the paradigm's state-of-the-art standing. We close by analyzing principled limitations — sampling cost, likelihood–perceptual trade-offs, and manifold assumptions — and chart the frontier toward fast deterministic solvers and consistency models.

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling asks a deceptively simple question: given samples **x** ~ *p*data, how do we draw *new* samples from *p*data, and ideally evaluate their likelihood? Classical answers each pay a price. Autoregressive models pay in sampling speed and ordering assumptions; variational autoencoders pay in blurry reconstructions from approximate posteriors; generative adversarial networks pay in unstable minimax dynamics and absent likelihoods; normalizing flows pay in architectural invertibility constraints.

Score-based diffusion models sidestep these compromises through a physically intuitive two-phase protocol: *perturb* data with a known noise process until it is indistinguishable from a tractable prior, then *reverse* the process. The entire learnable content of the model reduces to estimating the **score function** s(**x**) ≜ ∇**x** log *p*(**x**) at each noise level. Because scores are gradients of log-densities, they are invariant to the normalizing constant — the single most vexing term in probabilistic modeling — and because the perturbation process is chosen by the designer, training reduces to a simple regression against analytically known targets.

The history of this idea unfolds in three acts. **Act one** is score matching: Hyvärinen (2005) showed how to fit unnormalized models by matching score functions, and Vincent (2011) showed that corrupting data with noise turns the abstract objective into a concrete denoising regression, enabling deep networks to learn scores for the first time [4]. **Act two** is the DDPM: Ho, Jain, and Abbeel (2020) formulated a fixed Markovian forward chain with a learned Gaussian reverse, and discovered — via a reparameterization insight — that predicting the injected noise ε is equivalent to learning the score, yielding FID 3.17 on CIFAR-10 and dethroning GANs [2]. **Act three** is the SDE unification: Song et al. (2021) recognized that the forward chain is merely an Euler discretization of a stochastic differential equation; reversing the SDE with Anderson's (1982) time-reversal formula and estimating scores continuously yields a framework that generates exact likelihoods, admits adaptive solvers, and recovers every prior method as a special case [1].

> **Theorem:** *For the forward SDE d**x** = f(**x**, t)dt + g(t)d**w**, if the score ∇**x** log p_t(**x**) is known exactly for all t ∈ [0, T], then simulating the reverse-time SDE d**x** = [f(**x**, t) − g(t)²∇**x** log p_t(**x**)]dt + g(t)d**w̄** backward from t = T to t = 0 produces samples distributed exactly according to p_0 = p_data. [1]*

This post reconstructs that arc with full mathematical detail, targeting readers who want derivations rather than slogans.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Why the score suffices for generation

The score function s(**x**) = ∇**x** log *p*(**x**) points in the direction of steepest density ascent. Two classical facts make it operationally sufficient:

1. **Sampling via Langevin dynamics.** Given only the score, the overdamped Langevin recursion **x**_{k+1} = **x**_k + (ε/2)∇log *p*(**x**_k) + √ε **z**_k, **z**_k ~ N(0, **I**), converges to *p* as ε → 0 and k → ∞. The model never needs the density itself.
2. **Identifiability.** If two distributions share identical scores everywhere and decay sufficiently fast at infinity, they are equal. The score encodes everything about the shape of *p*.

The obstacle was always *estimation*: the naïve score-matching objective of Hyvärinen (2005),

> **Objective:** *J_SM(θ) = E_{p_data(**x**)}[ ½‖s_θ(**x**)‖² + tr(∇_**x** s_θ(**x**)) ],*

requires the Hessian trace of a deep network — expensive in high dimensions. Sliced score matching (Song et al., 2020) and finite-difference approximations [5] mitigated this, but the breakthrough that made score estimation practical at image scale was *denoising*.

### 2.2 Denoising score matching

Vincent (2011) observed that if we define a perturbation kernel *q*(**x̃**|**x**) (e.g., Gaussian noise of scale σ) and match scores against the *perturbed* distribution *q_σ*(**x̃**) = ∫ *q*(**x̃**|**x**) *p*data(**x**) d**x**, the objective collapses to a regression against a known analytic target [4]:

J_DSM(θ) = E_{*p*data(**x**)} E_{*q*(**x̃**|**x**)}[ ½‖s_θ(**x̃**) − ∇_**x̃** log *q*(**x̃**|**x**)‖² ].

For Gaussian kernels, ∇_**x̃** log *q*(**x̃**|**x**) = −(**x̃** − **x**)/σ² — just the negative of the scaled noise. The network learns to point from noisy observations back toward clean data. Song and Ermon (2019) scaled this up with the **noise-conditional score network (NCSN)**, training a single network s_θ(**x**, σ) across a geometric ladder of noise scales {σ_i} with per-scale loss weighting λ(σ_i) = σ_i², then sampling with **annealed Langevin dynamics** that walks down the noise ladder. NCSNv2 (Song & Ermon, 2020) stabilized this with exponential moving averages and per-dataset noise-scale tuning [5].

### 2.3 Diffusion probabilistic models before DDPM

Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) introduced diffusion probabilistic models as variational latent-variable models with a *fixed* forward Markov chain q(**x**_1:T|**x**_0) = ∏_t q(**x**_t|**x**_{t−1}) of small Gaussian steps and a learned reverse p_θ(**x**_0:T). Training maximizes an evidence lower bound (ELBO). The model was elegant but uncompetitive with GANs — until the 2020 simplification.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The DDPM forward and reverse processes

DDPM fixes the forward chain as a variance schedule {β_t}_{t=1..T} (T = 1000 in the original):

q(**x**_t | **x**_{t−1}) = N(**x**_t; √(1−β_t) **x**_{t−1}, β_t **I**).

Because Gaussians compose, the marginal at any step is available in closed form. With α_t ≜ 1 − β_t and ᾱ_t ≜ ∏_{s≤t} α_s:

q(**x**_t | **x**_0) = N(**x**_t; √ᾱ_t **x**_0, (1 − ᾱ_t) **I**), **x**_t = √ᾱ_t **x**_0 + √(1 − ᾱ_t) ε, ε ~ N(0, **I**). [2]

The reverse is learned as p_θ(**x**_{t−1}|**x**_t) = N(**x**_{t−1}; μ_θ(**x**_t, t), Σ_θ(**x**_t, t)). The ELBO for this fixed-forward latent-variable model decomposes into per-timestep KL divergences; Ho et al. reparameterize μ_θ in terms of a noise-prediction network ε_θ, yielding the celebrated simplification:

> **Training objective (L_simple):** *E_{t,**x**_0,ε}[ ‖ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_t **x**_0 + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε, t)‖² ]. [2]*

The implementation is disarmingly simple:

```python
def ddpm_train_step(x0, model, alphas_bar, T):
    t = torch.randint(1, T + 1, (x0.shape[0],))          # uniform timestep
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    a_bar = alphas_bar[t].view(-1, *([1] * (x0.dim() - 1)))
    xt = torch.sqrt(a_bar) * x0 + torch.sqrt(1 - a_bar) * eps
    eps_pred = model(xt, t)
    return F.mse_loss(eps_pred, eps)
```

At inference, ancestral sampling walks the learned reverse:

```python
@torch.no_grad()
def ddpm_sample(model, shape, betas, alphas, alphas_bar, T):
    xt = torch.randn(shape)
    for t in reversed(range(1, T + 1)):
        z = torch.randn_like(xt) if t > 1 else 0
        eps = model(xt, torch.full((shape[0],), t))
        mu = (xt - betas[t-1] / torch.sqrt(1 - alphas_bar[t-1]) * eps) / torch.sqrt(alphas[t-1])
        xt = mu + torch.sqrt(betas[t-1]) * z
    return xt
```

### 3.2 The ε-prediction ↔ score-matching equivalence

The conceptual linchpin of the modern synthesis: *ε-prediction is scaled score matching.* From q(**x**_t|**x**_0) above,

∇_**x**_t log q(**x**_t | **x**_0) = −(**x**_t − √ᾱ_t **x**_0)/(1 − ᾱ_t) = −ε/√(1 − ᾱ_t).

Hence the denoising score-matching objective at noise level t is *identical* to L_simple up to the scalar weight 1/(1 − ᾱ_t):

s_θ(**x**_t, t) = −ε_θ(**x**_t, t)/√(1 − ᾱ_t).

DDPM training and score estimation are the same computation wearing different notation [1][2]. This equivalence is what allowed Song et al. to reinterpret every discrete-time DDPM as a discretization of a continuous SDE — and then generalize far beyond it.

### 3.3 The SDE formulation of Song et al.

Replace the discrete chain with a continuous forward SDE over t ∈ [0, T]:

d**x** = f(**x**, t) dt + g(t) d**w**,

where **w** is a standard Wiener process. Anderson (1982) proved the time reversal of any such diffusion:

d**x** = [ f(**x**, t) − g(t)² ∇_**x** log p_t(**x**) ] dt + g(t) d**w̄**,

with **w̄** a reverse-time Wiener process. *Generation = simulating the reverse SDE*, provided we can evaluate the score ∇**x** log p_t at every t. Song et al. learn a time-dependent score network s_θ(**x**, t) with the continuous denoising objective [1]:

```python
def score_matching_loss(x0, s_theta, sigma_t, T):
    t = torch.rand(x0.shape[0]) * T                       # t ~ U[0, T]
    xt, target_score = perturb(x0, t)                     # xt ~ p_{t|0}(·|x0)
    pred = s_theta(xt, t)
    weight = (sigma_t(t) ** 2)                            # likelihood weighting
    return (weight * ((pred - target_score) ** 2).sum(-1)).mean()
```

Three canonical SDE choices and their perturbation kernels p_{t|0}(**x**_t|**x**_0):

| SDE | Drift f(**x**, t) | Diffusion g(t) | p_{t|0}(**x**_t\|**x**_0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| VE (variance exploding) | **0** | √(dσ²(t)/dt), σ(t) = σ_min(σ_max/σ_min)^t | N(**x**_0, [σ²(t) − σ²(0)]**I**) |
| VP (variance preserving) | −½β(t)**x** | √β(t) | N(**x**_0 e^{−½∫β}, (1 − e^{−∫β})**I**) |
| sub-VP | −½β(t)**x** | √[β(t)(1 − e^{−2∫β})] | same mean as VP, variance (1 − e^{−∫β})²**I** |

VP-SDE is the continuous limit of DDPM; VE-SDE is the continuous limit of NCSN. The sub-VP variant, introduced in [1], upper-bounds the VP variance to sharpen likelihoods. This table is the *Rosetta stone* of the field: pick a row, discretize, and you recover the corresponding classical method.

### 3.4 The probability-flow ODE

Subtracting half the stochastic correction from the reverse SDE leaves a *deterministic* ODE with identical marginals p_t(**x**) at every t:

> **Probability-flow ODE:** *d**x** = [ f(**x**, t) − ½g(t)² ∇_**x** log p_t(**x**) ] dt. [1]*

Its consequences are profound:

- **Exact likelihoods.** The instantaneous change-of-variables formula gives log p_0(**x**_0) = log p_T(**x**_T) + ∫_0^T ∇·f̃(**x**_t, t) dt, where f̃ is the ODE drift. The divergence is estimated unbiasedly with the Skilling–Hutchinson trace estimator, making diffusion models the first GAN-competitive family with *tractable exact likelihoods* — 2.99 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 in [1].
- **Uniquely identifiable encodings.** The ODE is invertible: data maps deterministically to latent noise and back, enabling latent interpolation and manipulation [3].
- **Fast black-box solvers.** Adaptive ODE solvers (e.g., Dormand–Prince) choose their own step sizes, trading NFE for accuracy automatically.

### 3.5 DDIM: the non-Markovian generalization

Song, Meng, and Ermon (2021) observed that the Markovian forward chain is not sacred: any *non-Markovian* forward process q_σ(**x**_{1:T}|**x**_0) sharing the same marginals q(**x**_t|**x**_0) = N(√ᾱ_t **x**_0, (1−ᾱ_t)**I**) yields the *identical* L_simple training objective. This liberates the sampler. With a variance parameter σ_t (η interpolating between DDPM-like and deterministic),

**x**_{t−1} = √ᾱ_{t−1} · (**x**_t − √(1−ᾱ_t) ε_θ)/√ᾱ_t  +  √(1 − ᾱ_{t−1} − σ_t²) · ε_θ  +  σ_t ε_t,  [3]

where the first term is the predicted clean image **x̂**_0. Setting η = 0 (σ_t = 0) makes sampling *deterministic*: DDIM becomes an Euler integrator of the probability-flow ODE, which is why it tolerates aggressive step-skipping (50 or 100 steps instead of 1000) with only graceful quality degradation — a 10×–50× wall-clock speedup [3]. No retraining is required: the *same* DDPM checkpoint powers every sampler.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The reverse-time SDE: derivation sketch

Let p_t(**x**) solve the Fokker–Planck equation ∂_t p = −∇·(fp) + ½g²Δp. Anderson's time-reversal theorem considers the process run backward, τ = T − t, and asks which SDE's Fokker–Planck equation its density satisfies. The answer introduces the score as a *corrective drift*: where forward diffusion spreads probability mass apart, the reverse process must concentrate it, and the concentration direction is exactly ∇**x** log p_t. The correction magnitude g(t)² is the instantaneous variance injection rate — intuitive, since the reverse must undo precisely the randomness the forward injected. Discretizing the reverse SDE with Euler–Maruyama:

**x**_{t−Δt} = **x**_t − [f(**x**_t,t) − g(t)² s_θ(**x**_t,t)] Δt + g(t)√Δt **z**,  **z** ~ N(0, **I**),

recovers ancestral DDPM sampling in the VP limit [1][7]. The recent convergence analysis of Nakano (2024/2026) makes this rigorous, proving weak convergence of DDPM iterates to p_data as T → ∞ under asymptotic conditions on the schedule and score-estimation error [7].

### 4.2 Perturbation kernels and the noise schedule as design space

The choice of σ(t) or β(t) is not cosmetic — it controls the *signal-to-noise geometry* the network must learn:

1. **VE-SDE** grows variance geometrically (σ_min = 0.01 → σ_max ≈ 50 for images). Scores at large σ are nearly Gaussian and easy; at small σ the manifold structure is sharp. NCSN++ uses VE with σ(t) = σ_min(σ_max/σ_min)^t [1].
2. **VP-SDE** preserves total variance, matching DDPM's linear β schedule (β_min = 0.1 → β_max = 20 in continuous time). The terminal distribution is exactly N(0, **I**), eliminating prior mismatch.
3. **sub-VP-SDE** shrinks the kernel variance relative to VP, empirically improving likelihoods while preserving the VP mean path [1].

A useful mental model [6]: forward diffusion is *Wasserstein gradient flow* of the entropy functional, and score-based generation follows the same gradient-flow path in reverse. The schedule determines the path's parameterization, and different parameterizations concentrate network capacity at different noise levels.

### 4.3 Likelihood computation through the probability-flow ODE

Plugging the learned score into the PF-ODE and integrating from data **x**_0 to noise **x**_T yields the exact log-likelihood:

log p_0(**x**_0) = log p_T(**x**_T) + ∫_0^T ∇·f̃_θ(**x**(t), t) dt, f̃_θ = f − ½g² s_θ.

The divergence of a neural vector field is intractable directly but admits the unbiased estimator ∇·f̃ = E_ε[ εᵀ ∇f̃ ε ] (Hutchinson), computed by a single vector–Jacobian product. In practice:

- This is how [1] reports 2.99 bits/dim on uniformly dequantized CIFAR-10 — competitive with the best autoregressive models of the era.
- Likelihood and sample quality *trade off*: maximum-likelihood weighting of the score objective (λ(t) = g(t)²) favors bits/dim, while the DDPM-style weighting (λ(t) ∝ 1 − ᾱ_t) favors FID. You cannot have both optima with one weighting — a fact that reshaped how the field reports results.

### 4.4 DDIM as probability-flow integration

The DDIM update with η = 0 can be rearranged into Euler steps of the PF-ODE for the VP-SDE in the reparameterized variable **x̄** = **x**/√ᾱ. This explains three empirical phenomena at once [3]:

1. **Step-skipping works** because ODE integrators tolerate large steps when the vector field is smooth — the learned score field is smooth almost everywhere except near the data manifold at t → 0.
2. **Interpolation is semantic**: linear interpolation in DDIM's deterministic latent space produces smooth, meaningful image morphs, unlike DDPM's stochastic latents.
3. **Near-exact inversion**: encoding **x**_0 → **x**_T with the forward ODE and decoding returns the original with low error, enabling editing pipelines (e.g., SDEdit-style guidance) built on pretrained checkpoints.

### 4.5 Accelerating sampling: predictor–corrector and beyond

The SDE view makes the sampler a *numerical methods* problem, inviting a toolbox:

- **Predictor–corrector (PC) samplers** [1]: alternate one reverse-SDE discretization step (predictor) with M steps of annealed Langevin dynamics using the same score (corrector). The corrector projects the iterate back toward p_t, permitting larger predictor steps.
- **DDIM / deterministic ODE solvers**: η = 0 DDIM, or higher-order solvers (DPM-Solver, Heun) applied to the PF-ODE, reaching quality samples in 10–50 NFEs.
- **Preconditioned sampling** [5]: reparameterizing the reverse SDE's drift/diffusion to improve conditioning, cutting high-resolution synthesis (e.g., 1024×1024 faces) from 30+ minutes toward practical runtimes.
- **Consistency models** (Song et al., 2023): distill the PF-ODE into a single-step map f_θ(**x**_t, t) ↦ **x**_0, achieving one-step generation from the same teacher.

The common thread: *training* is score regression (fixed, simple); *all* progress in speed and quality since 2021 has come from better *integration* of the learned vector field.

## 5 Empirical Results

The paradigm's empirical record is what converted skeptics. Key results from the primary sources:

| Model | CIFAR-10 FID ↓ | CIFAR-10 IS ↑ | CIFAR-10 NLL (bits/dim) ↓ | Sampling steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM [2] | **3.17** | 9.46 | 3.70 | 1000 |
| NCSN++ (VE-SDE, PC sampler) [1] | 2.45 | 9.43 | — | 2000 (PC) |
| DDPM++ (VP-SDE, PF-ODE) [1] | **2.20** | **9.89** | **2.99** | adaptive (~100–300 NFE) |
| DDIM (η=0, 100 steps) [3] | 4.16 | — | — | 100 |
| DDIM (η=0, 20 steps) [3] | 6.84 | — | — | 20 |

Additional landmark findings:

- **High resolution.** NCSN++ generates 1024×1024 CelebA-HQ faces with fine detail, demonstrating that score fields scale to megapixel manifolds without architectural heroics [1].
- **Speed.** DDIM produces high-quality samples 10×–50× faster than DDPM wall-clock, with a smooth computation–quality trade-off curve [3].
- **Geometry.** [6] shows both forward diffusion and reverse generation correspond to Wasserstein gradient flow along the same path, giving the sampling dynamics a variational interpretation.
- **Convergence.** [7] proves the DDPM sampling chain weakly converges to the data distribution as T → ∞, placing the method's empirical success on rigorous footing.
- **Conditioning.** The same checkpoints enable class-conditional generation, inpainting, and colorization by score-level guidance — no retraining [1].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Sampling cost remains the central tax.** Even accelerated, diffusion samplers require tens to hundreds of network evaluations per sample versus one for a GAN. Real-time video synthesis still strains hardware budgets.
2. **The likelihood–quality trade-off is structural.** Weightings that optimize bits/dim degrade FID and vice versa; no single training objective dominates both, complicating model selection [1].
3. **Score estimation near the manifold is fragile.** At t → 0 the score magnitude explodes on low-dimensional data manifolds, and small network errors there produce visible artifacts. This is also where ODE integrators need the smallest steps.
4. **Manifold and support assumptions.** Theory assumes densities with full support and smooth log-densities; real image data concentrates near a low-dimensional manifold where scores are ill-defined, and much of the rigorous analysis (e.g., [7]) requires regularity conditions that real datasets only approximately satisfy.
5. **Schedule sensitivity.** Performance depends on β(t)/σ(t) design and on terminal-distribution mismatch (VE-SDE's p_T is only approximately Gaussian at finite T), demanding per-dataset tuning inherited from NCSNv2's playbook [5].
6. **Evaluation is still perceptual.** FID and IS are proxy metrics with known biases; likelihood is exact but misaligned with human judgment — the field still lacks a single trustworthy objective.

## 7 Conclusion

Score-based diffusion models earned their dominance not through a single trick but through a *unification*: denoising score matching made the score learnable, DDPM made the objective beautifully simple, and the SDE formulation revealed that both — plus NCSN, DDIM, and every sampler since — are discretizations of one continuous object, the reverse-time diffusion driven by the score. The probability-flow ODE then handed the family something no GAN has ever possessed: exact likelihoods, invertible encodings, and a sampler design space governed by numerical analysis rather than alchemy.

The trajectory from here is clear in outline: faster deterministic solvers, single-step consistency distillation, and tighter theory (along the lines of [6][7]) closing the gap between the manifold reality of data and the smooth-density assumptions of the proofs. The score function — a humble gradient of a log-density — turned out to be the right primitive. Everything else was integration.
