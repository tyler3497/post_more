# Numerical Relativity and Binary Black Hole Waveforms: The BSSN Formalism, Template Banks, and LIGO Parameter Estimation

## Abstract

The first direct detection of gravitational waves, GW150914, rested on an edifice built in two movements: the numerical solution of the full nonlinear Einstein equations for inspiralling black holes, and the statistical machinery that extracts weak signals from detector noise. This thesis presents a unified treatment of that edifice. We derive the 3+1 ADM decomposition, expose the instabilities that stalled binary black hole evolution for decades, and develop the Baumgarte-Shapiro-Shibata-Nakamura (BSSN) reformulation whose conformal decomposition and auxiliary connection functions yielded long-term stable evolutions. We examine the moving-puncture gauge conditions, 1+log slicing and the Gamma-driver shift, that made merger simulations routine. From Newman-Penrose-extracted waveforms we follow the path into the LIGO detection pipelines: template bank construction, matched filtering, fitting factors, and full Bayesian parameter estimation with Bilby and LALInference. The synthesis shows how theoretical relativity, numerical analysis, and Bayesian statistics converged to convert strain time series into the first observational census of merging black holes.

## 1 Introduction

The announcement on 11 February 2016 that the Advanced LIGO interferometers had observed the transient gravitational-wave signal GW150914 on 14 September 2015 marked the birth of gravitational-wave astronomy [1]. The signal, sweeping upward in frequency from roughly 35 Hz to 250 Hz with a peak strain of order 10^{-21}, matched the general-relativistic prediction for the inspiral, merger, and ringdown of a binary black hole system.

This thesis traces the complete chain from field equations to astrophysical inference. The story has three acts. First, the *formulation problem*, how to write Einstein's equations in a form amenable to stable numerical evolution, culminating in the BSSN formalism. Second, the *gauge problem*, how to choose coordinates that do not crash into singularities, solved in practice by the moving-puncture method. Third, the *data-analysis problem*, how to search noisy interferometer output for signals and invert them for source parameters, solved by template banks, matched filtering, and Bayesian parameter estimation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The 3+1 decomposition

The starting point is the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) decomposition, which foliates four-dimensional spacetime into spacelike hypersurfaces labeled by time t. The four-metric is written in terms of a spatial three-metric gamma_{ij}, a lapse function alpha, and a shift vector beta^i; the dynamics are encoded in the extrinsic curvature K_{ij}. Einstein's equations split into four *constraint equations*, which initial data must satisfy, and six *evolution equations* advancing gamma_{ij} and K_{ij} forward in time:

- The metric evolves as partial_t gamma_{ij} = -2 alpha K_{ij} + Lie_beta gamma_{ij}
- The extrinsic curvature evolves through curvature, gauge, and Lie derivative terms

The constraints are mathematically preserved by the evolution equations: if satisfied on the initial slice, the continuum solution satisfies them for all time.

### 2.2 The long instability

Through the 1980s and 1990s, this clean mathematical picture collapsed on contact with computation. All attempts to evolve black hole spacetimes in three dimensions with the ADM system failed, the failure announced by explosive growth of constraint violations. The ADM evolution system possesses zero-speed and constraint-violating modes in its characteristic structure; numerical error excites these modes and they grow without bound, making the long evolutions a binary inspiral requires impossible.

The field responded with a program of *reformulation*: rewriting Einstein's equations, through the addition of multiples of the constraints and the introduction of auxiliary variables, into systems with better hyperbolic structure. The most successful product was the system introduced by Shibata and Nakamura in 1995 and popularized by Baumgarte and Shapiro in 1998 and 1999, now known as the BSSN formalism [5].

### 2.3 The 2005-2006 breakthrough

The decisive advance came in 2005-2006, when three groups independently achieved the first stable evolutions of binary black holes through merger. Pretorius used a generalized-harmonic formulation with constraint damping and excision; Campanelli, Lousto, Marronetti, and Zlochower, and independently Baker and collaborators, used the BSSN formulation with a radical simplification of the singularity treatment called the *moving-puncture method*. These simulations produced the first complete inspiral-merger-ringdown waveforms and agreed with post-Newtonian predictions in the early inspiral, establishing numerical relativity as a predictive science.

## 3 Methodology

The methodology of this thesis is synthetic: we reconstruct the pipeline from field equations to parameter estimates, exhibiting the mathematical structure at each stage and the interfaces between stages. The exposition follows the actual order of operations in a modern analysis:

1. **Initial data.** Solve the constraints for two black holes in quasi-circular orbit via the puncture method: the conformal factor splits into a singular Brill-Lindquist piece plus a regular correction from an elliptic solver.
2. **Free evolution.** Advance the BSSN variables with high-order finite differencing and Runge-Kutta integration, 1+log slicing and Gamma-driver shift, on adaptive mesh refinement grids tracking the punctures.
3. **Waveform extraction.** Compute the Newman-Penrose scalar Psi_4 on extraction spheres, decompose into spin-weighted spherical harmonics, and integrate twice in time to obtain the strain.
4. **Template construction.** Build waveform template banks spanning the binary parameter space, using analytic approximants calibrated to numerical relativity or surrogate models interpolating directly between simulations.
5. **Search and detection.** Correlate detector strain against the bank with the noise-weighted matched filter, rank triggers by signal-to-noise ratio and chi-squared vetoes, and estimate the false alarm rate by time-slide studies.
6. **Parameter estimation.** Evaluate the Bayesian posterior over source parameters with stochastic samplers to produce credible intervals for masses, spins, distance, and orientation.

Each subsequent section develops one or more of these stages in depth.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The BSSNOK Formalism: Conformal Decomposition and Evolution

The BSSN reformulation splits the extrinsic curvature into its trace K and trace-free part A_{ij}, then performs a *conformal decomposition* of the spatial geometry: a conformal factor phi = (1/12) log(det gamma_{ij}), or the evolved variable chi = e^{-4 phi} regular at punctures; a conformal metric tilde gamma_{ij} = e^{-4 phi} gamma_{ij} with unit determinant; and a conformal trace-free extrinsic curvature tilde A_{ij}.

The decisive innovation is promoting the *conformal connection functions* tilde Gamma^i = tilde gamma^{jk} tilde Gamma^i_{jk} = -partial_j tilde gamma^{ij} to independent evolved variables. Second derivatives of the metric are reorganized so that the principal part of the Ricci tensor becomes a wave operator on the conformal metric, tilde gamma^{lm} partial_l partial_m tilde gamma_{ij}, with remaining second derivatives expressed through first derivatives of tilde Gamma^i. This tames the instability by giving the system clean hyperbolic structure; the auxiliary constraints det(tilde gamma) = 1, tr(tilde A) = 0, and the definition of tilde Gamma^i serve as evolution diagnostics [5].

The evolution equations, in vacuum, read:

partial_t tilde gamma_{ij} - Lie_beta tilde gamma_{ij} = -2 alpha tilde A_{ij}

partial_t phi - Lie_beta phi = -(1/6) alpha K

partial_t K - Lie_beta K = -D^i D_i alpha + alpha (tilde A_{ij} tilde A^{ij} + (1/3) K^2)

partial_t tilde A_{ij} - Lie_beta tilde A_{ij} = e^{-4 phi}[alpha R_{ij} - D_i D_j alpha]^{TF} + alpha(K tilde A_{ij} - 2 tilde A_{ik} tilde A^k_j)

partial_t tilde Gamma^i - Lie_beta tilde Gamma^i = (second derivatives of the shift, first derivatives of alpha and K, and products of connection functions)

> **Theorem (Strong hyperbolicity of BSSN with puncture gauge):** The BSSN evolution system, coupled to 1+log slicing and the Gamma-driver shift condition, is strongly hyperbolic. Its characteristic fields propagate at physical speeds (zero, the speed of light, and gauge speeds set by the slicing), and the initial value problem is well posed. Consequently, constraint violations introduced by numerical error cannot grow faster than the physical modes permit, which is the analytic foundation of the long-term stability observed in binary black hole simulations [5].

The practical consequence is that BSSN codes with high-order finite differencing can evolve binaries for dozens of orbits through merger and ringdown while constraint violations converge toward zero with resolution [4].

### 4.2 Moving Punctures: 1+log Slicing and the Gamma-Driver Shift

Black holes contain singularities, and no coordinate system can evolve into one. The traditional remedy, *excision*, removed a region inside the apparent horizon, but required delicate boundary conditions on a moving inner boundary. The moving-puncture method sidesteps excision by exploiting puncture initial data: the conformal factor diverges as psi ~ m/(2r) at the puncture while all other variables remain regular. The 2005-2006 breakthrough was allowing this singular point to move across the grid, provided the evolved variable vanishes at the puncture (the chi = psi^{-4} variant) and the gauge conditions collapse the lapse near the singularity.

The standard gauge choices are:

- **1+log slicing:** partial_t alpha - beta^i partial_i alpha = -2 alpha K. Where the extrinsic curvature trace grows large near the puncture, the lapse collapses toward zero, freezing the evolution before the slice can reach the singularity.
- **Gamma-driver shift:** partial_t beta^i = (3/4) B^i, partial_t B^i = partial_t tilde Gamma^i - eta B^i. The shift is driven by the dynamics of the conformal connection functions, counteracting slice-stretching as the holes orbit, with a damping parameter eta of order unity over mass.

Together these conditions are singularity-avoiding and computationally robust. The punctures orbit, merge, and the remnant settles to a stationary state, all without excision. This combination underlies the large simulation catalogs feeding modern waveform models, including recent GPU-accelerated codes such as AthenaK, which employ the closely related Z4c formulation with the same moving-puncture gauge philosophy [6].

### 4.3 Waveform Extraction and Numerical Relativity Surrogates

Gravitational waves are extracted from the simulation via the Newman-Penrose scalar Psi_4, the component of the Weyl tensor that falls off as 1/r and encodes the outgoing transverse-traceless radiation. On coordinate spheres at large radius, Psi_4 is decomposed into spin-weight -2 spherical harmonics, Psi_4 = sum_{l,m} psi^{lm} {}_{-2}Y_{lm}, and the strain modes h_{lm} are obtained by double time integration, with careful handling of integration constants and low-frequency drift.

A single high-accuracy simulation costs weeks of supercomputer time, so direct simulation-per-analysis is infeasible. The community therefore builds *surrogate models*: reduced-order representations trained on catalogs of numerical relativity waveforms that evaluate any waveform in the covered parameter region in milliseconds, including higher-order modes and precession effects.

Reanalyses of GW150914 and GW170104 with fully precessing numerical relativity surrogates extracted information from subdominant harmonics that conventional approximants miss, placing the sources 10 to 20 percent farther away than originally estimated and pinning the effective inspiral spin of GW150914 closer to zero [7]: a first step toward eliminating semi-analytic approximations from parameter estimation.

### 4.4 Template Banks, Matched Filtering, and Fitting Factors

Detection of a known waveform shape in stationary Gaussian noise is governed by the *matched filter*: the noise-weighted inner product <a, b> = 4 Re integral [a-tilde*(f) b-tilde(f) / S_n(f)] df, where S_n(f) is the one-sided power spectral density. The matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio rho = <s, h> / sqrt(<h, h>) is the optimal detection statistic by the Neyman-Pearson lemma. Because the coalescence time and phase are unknown, the *match* maximizes the normalized overlap over these extrinsic parameters, and the *fitting factor* is the maximum match over all templates in the bank.

A template bank must cover the astrophysical parameter space densely enough that no real signal loses more than a few percent of its signal-to-noise ratio to bank discreteness. The traditional criterion is a *minimal match* of 0.97, corresponding to at most a 10 percent loss in detection volume. The broad PyCBC search bank spans total masses from 2 to 500 solar masses with dimensionless spins up to 0.9 [11]; recent machine-learning approaches use masked autoregressive normalizing flows to sample template probability densities for precessing binaries and higher-order-mode systems, producing banks of millions of templates in hours [10].

| Method | Placement principle | Strengths | Representative use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geometric (lattice) | Parameter-space metric, minimal match | Optimal efficiency at low dimension | Aligned-spin inspiral banks |
| Stochastic | Random proposals, mismatch rejection | Handles curved, high-dimensional spaces | PyCBC broad all-sky search |
| Normalizing-flow (mbank) | Learned template probability density | Millions of templates in hours; precession, higher modes | Next-generation precessing searches |

### 4.5 Bayesian Parameter Estimation with Bilby and LALInference

Detection answers *whether* a signal is present; parameter estimation answers *what* it is. The Bayesian formulation begins with the likelihood of the strain data d given source parameters theta,

L(d | theta) proportional to exp(-(1/2) <d - h(theta), d - h(theta)>),

with the same noise-weighted inner product that defines the matched filter, multiplied by prior distributions encoding astrophysical expectations, and explored with stochastic samplers. The output is the posterior distribution p(theta | d), from which credible intervals for every parameter are derived by marginalization.

Two software ecosystems dominate this task. *LALInference*, the pioneering library, enabled the parameter estimates of the first two observing runs. *Bilby*, introduced in 2019, reimplemented the full infrastructure in modular Python with support for multiple samplers and interfaces to real open LIGO-Virgo data [8], and was validated by reproducing the published parameter estimates for all eleven signals of the first gravitational-wave transient catalog, GWTC-1 [9]. Modern analyses combine Bilby's sampling infrastructure with numerical relativity surrogate waveform models, closing the loop from Einstein's equations to astrophysical measurement.

A compact illustration of the core numerical operations, the noise-weighted inner product and the matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio, is given below:

```python
import numpy as np

def inner_product(a, b, psd, df):
    """<a|b> = 4 Re int a*(f) b(f) / S_n(f) df."""
    return 4.0 * np.real(np.sum(np.conj(a) * b / psd)) * df

def matched_filter_snr(strain, tmpl, psd, df):
    h = tmpl / np.sqrt(inner_product(tmpl, tmpl, psd, df))
    return inner_product(strain, h, psd, df)

def log_likelihood(d, h, psd, df):
    r = d - h
    return -0.5 * inner_product(r, r, psd, df)
```

Credible intervals from posterior samples, in a systems language:

```rust
/// Summarize one-dimensional marginal posterior samples.
pub fn credible_interval(samples: &[f64], level: f64) -> (f64, f64, f64) {
    let mut s = samples.to_vec();
    s.sort_by(|a, b| a.partial_cmp(b).unwrap());
    let n = s.len();
    let lo = ((1.0 - level) / 2.0 * n as f64) as usize;
    let hi = ((1.0 + level) / 2.0 * n as f64) as usize;
    let median = s[n / 2];
    (s[lo], median, s[hi.min(n - 1)])
}
```

```tla+
          THEN triggers' = triggers \cup {[segment |-> seg, template |-> tmpl, snr |-> rho]}
          ELSE UNCHANGED triggers

Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<strain, bank, triggers, background>>
=============================================================================
```

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

The empirical anchor of the entire pipeline is GW150914. On 14 September 2015 at 09:50:45 UTC, the Hanford and Livingston interferometers recorded a coincident transient with a combined matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio of 24 and a false alarm rate below one event per 203,000 years, exceeding 5.1 sigma [1]. Bayesian parameter estimation yielded the source properties summarized below [2]:

| Parameter | Median and 90% credible interval |
|---|---|
| Primary mass m_1 | 36 (+5, -4) solar masses |
| Secondary mass m_2 | 29 (+4, -4) solar masses |
| Final black hole mass | 62 (+4, -4) solar masses |
| Final spin | 0.67 (+0.05, -0.07) |
| Luminosity distance | 410 (+160, -180) Mpc |
| Redshift | 0.09 (+0.03, -0.04) |
| Radiated energy | 3.0 (+0.5, -0.5) solar masses |
| Primary spin magnitude | < 0.7 (90% probability) |

Three distinct lines of evidence connect this observation to the numerical relativity program. First, a *direct comparison* of the strain data against a catalog of numerical relativity simulations, bypassing semi-analytic approximants entirely, reconstructed the binary parameters with all quadrupolar and octopolar modes included; the recovered parameters agreed with the template-based analysis [4]. Second, *tests of general relativity* confirmed the signal's internal consistency: the remnant mass and spin inferred separately from the inspiral and post-inspiral phases agree, the post-peak data match the least-damped quasi-normal mode of the inferred remnant, and the graviton Compton wavelength is constrained above 10^13 km [3]. Third, the simulation enterprise continues to advance: new-generation GPU-accelerated codes such as AthenaK, using the Z4c formulation with moving-puncture gauge, now reproduce GW150914-class mergers roughly two orders of magnitude faster than the leadership machines available in 2016 [6].

## 6 Limitations

The pipeline described here, for all its empirical success, faces significant limitations that define the current research frontier.

**Parameter-space coverage.** Numerical relativity catalogs densely cover comparable-mass, low-eccentricity binaries with moderate spins, but high mass ratios, highly precessing configurations, and eccentric mergers remain sparsely sampled. Surrogate models cannot be trusted outside their training domain, and template banks inherit the approximants' blind spots, particularly for eccentric binaries where fitting factors degrade [11].

**Waveform systematics at high signal-to-noise ratio.** Statistical uncertainty shrinks as signal-to-noise ratio grows, but systematic differences between waveform families do not. For loud events, the choice of approximant or surrogate can dominate the error budget; the GW150914 surrogate reanalysis, which moved the distance by up to 20 percent, warns that current error bars may understate model uncertainty [7].

**Computational cost.** A single high-accuracy numerical relativity simulation still requires weeks on hundreds of cores; next-generation detectors will observe hundreds of thousands of events per year, making direct numerical relativity follow-up of every event impossible. Reduced-order and machine-learning surrogates mitigate but do not eliminate this scaling problem.

**Beyond general relativity.** The entire template infrastructure assumes Einstein's theory, and numerical relativity in modified gravity remains in its infancy, so tests of general relativity are necessarily null tests within parameterized deviations rather than comparisons against complete alternative theories.

## 7 Conclusion

The detection and characterization of GW150914 was the convergence of three mature technologies: the BSSN reformulation of Einstein's equations, which rendered the two-body problem numerically tractable; the moving-puncture gauge conditions, which made merger simulations robust and routine; and the matched-filtering and Bayesian inference apparatus, which converted interferometric strain into astrophysical parameters.

The quantitative payoff is a new observational window: two black holes of approximately 36 and 29 solar masses merged 410 megaparsecs away, radiating three solar masses of energy in a fraction of a second, leaving a 62 solar mass remnant spinning at 67 percent of extremality, all inferred from a strain signal of 10^{-21} [1][2].

The frontier now lies in scale and fidelity: covering the full binary parameter space with simulations, eliminating waveform systematics as the dominant error, and extending the entire edifice to the hundreds of thousands of events the next generation of detectors will deliver.
