## Abstract

The convergence of silicon photonics and artificial intelligence has produced a new computational substrate: photonic integrated circuits that perform linear algebra — the dominant workload of deep learning — in the optical domain, where propagation is nearly latency-free and wavelength parallelism is essentially unlimited. This thesis develops the physics, device theory, and architecture of silicon-photonic optical neural networks, beginning with the guided-wave foundations of silicon-on-insulator waveguides and the resonance physics of microring resonators, including coupled-mode theory, quality factors exceeding two million, and thermo-optic and electro-optic tuning. It then examines the two dominant architectures — coherent Mach–Zehnder interferometer meshes realizing arbitrary unitaries through singular-value decomposition, and incoherent microring weight banks implementing wavelength-division-multiplexed broadcast-and-weight protocols — alongside landmark demonstrations from Shen et al.'s vowel-recognition processor to Xu et al.'s 11 TOPS convolutional accelerator. A unified analytical framework quantifies energy per multiply-accumulate, insertion-loss scaling, and thermal stabilization budgets, and derives the noise and crosstalk limits bounding practical network depth. The conclusion: passive photonic MACs can approach attojoule-scale energies, but the end-to-end advantage is set by conversion and control overhead, not by optics alone.

## 1 Introduction

The end of Dennard scaling and the deceleration of Moore's law have turned deep-learning workloads into a power-dissipation crisis. The dominant primitive underlying this crisis is the multiply–accumulate (MAC) operation of matrix–vector multiplication, which accounts for the overwhelming majority of floating-point operations in convolutional and transformer architectures [6].

Photonic integrated circuits offer a fundamentally different computational physics. In an optical processor, an input vector is encoded on the amplitude and phase of guided light, weights are programmed as static interference conditions, and multiplication and summation occur *during propagation itself* — at the speed of light in the medium, with negligible incremental energy per operation. David A. B. Miller's landmark analysis of attojoule optoelectronics established that optical interconnects could in principle operate at ~10 attojoules per bit [6]. The silicon photonics platform — fabricating waveguides in the same SOI processes and foundries as CMOS electronics — supplies the manufacturability that makes this physics deployable: 200/300 mm wafer processing, thousands of components per chip, and monolithic integration with germanium photodetectors and drive electronics [9].

This thesis develops a complete account of silicon-photonic optical neural networks around three questions: **(i)** what device physics governs the silicon photonic building blocks; **(ii)** how do these primitives compose into neural-computing architectures, and with what demonstrated performance; and **(iii)** what physical limits — loss, noise, crosstalk, thermal drift, and conversion overhead — bound their scale and efficiency?

> **Thesis statement:** *Silicon photonic integrated circuits can perform dense linear algebra at sub-femtojoule marginal energy and THz-scale aggregate bandwidth, but realizing this advantage end-to-end requires co-designing resonance control, calibration, and electro-optic conversion around loss accumulation and thermo-optic drift.*

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The silicon photonics platform

Silicon photonics exploits the enormous refractive-index contrast between silicon ($n \approx 3.48$ at 1550 nm) and its oxide cladding ($n \approx 1.44$) to confine light in sub-micron waveguides fabricated on silicon-on-insulator wafers — typically 220 nm of silicon over a 2–3 μm buried oxide layer [3]. The 220-nm platform has become the de-facto standard across open-access foundries (IMEC, AIM Photonics, GlobalFoundries 45CLO, TSMC), with standardized process design kits for couplers, modulators, and germanium photodetectors. Typical propagation losses are 2–3 dB/cm for single-mode strip waveguides, while broadened multimode geometries have reached $\sim$0.06 dB/cm [3][13].

Two physical effects make silicon *actively* tunable, and both are indispensable to optical computing. The **thermo-optic effect** ($dn/dT = 1.86 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K}^{-1}$) provides large phase shifts via integrated micro-heaters, at the cost of milliwatt-scale static power and millisecond thermal time constants. The **free-carrier plasma dispersion effect** (Soref and Bennett, 1987) [16] enables GHz-rate modulation in carrier-injection or carrier-depletion modulators, at the cost of added absorption. Germanium epitaxy yields photodetectors approaching 1 A/W responsivity and 50 GHz bandwidth, closing the optical-to-electrical readout path [9].

### 2.2 Microring resonators: the workhorse device

The microring resonator (MRR) is the single most versatile component in silicon photonics: wavelength-selective filter, compact modulator, nonlinear enhancer, and — critically for this thesis — programmable analog weight element [3]. Light circulating in the ring interferes constructively when the resonance condition is satisfied:

$$m \lambda_{res} = 2\pi R\, n_{eff}, \qquad m \in \mathbb{Z}$$

where $R$ is the ring radius and $n_{eff}$ the effective index of the guided mode. The spectral sharpness is quantified by the quality factor $Q = \lambda_{res}/\Delta\lambda_{FWHM} = \omega \tau_{ph}$, with $\tau_{ph}$ the photon lifetime. Ultra-high-Q silicon microrings fabricated with multi-mode ridge waveguides have demonstrated loaded $Q$ values exceeding $2.2 \times 10^6$ [13].

In the add–drop configuration — a ring coupled to a bus waveguide (through port) and a drop waveguide (drop port) — the through-port transmission exhibits the canonical Lorentzian notch [3]:

$$T_{through}(\lambda) = \frac{(\kappa^2) + (1-\kappa^2)e^{-\alpha L}}{1 + e^{-2\alpha L} - 2e^{-\alpha L}(1-\kappa^2)\cos(\phi(\lambda))}$$

where $\kappa^2$ is the power coupling coefficient, $\alpha$ the round-trip loss, $L = 2\pi R$, and $\phi$ the round-trip phase [3]. The *coupling regime* — under-coupled, critically coupled, or over-coupled — is set by the bus-to-ring gap, fixed at fabrication but dynamically adjustable through detuning.

### 2.3 From devices to neural networks

Two architectural lineages dominate optical neural computing:

1. **Coherent architectures**, pioneered by Shen et al. [1]: networks of Mach–Zehnder interferometers (MZIs) mesh optical fields coherently, exploiting the fact (Reck et al. [7]; Clements et al. [8]) that any unitary matrix decomposes into a cascade of $2 \times 2$ rotations implementable by MZIs with phase shifters.
2. **Incoherent/wavelength-multiplexed architectures**, pioneered by Tait et al. [2]: microring weight banks imprint analog weights on distinct wavelength channels of a WDM comb; summation is performed by integrating the total photocurrent on a balanced photodetector ("broadcast-and-weight").

Both lineages were unified in later hybrid designs combining WDM parallelism with coherent matrix optics (Totovic et al. [11]) and in large-scale photonic tensor cores (Feldmann et al. [5]) and convolutional accelerators reaching 11 tera-operations per second (Xu et al. [4]).

## 3 Methodology

This thesis adopts a **physics-first, architecture-second methodology**:

1. **Device-level analysis.** Resonant, coupling, and tuning physics are derived from coupled-mode theory, with quantitative targets from foundry statistics and peer-reviewed demonstrations [3][9][13].
2. **Architectural synthesis.** The two ONN lineages are reconstructed mathematically — the SVD–MZI decomposition for coherent networks and the broadcast-and-weight protocol for microring networks [1][2][8][10].
3. **Limits analysis.** Energy, loss-scaling, noise, and crosstalk figures of merit are compared against landmark systems [1][4][5][6], separating *marginal optical MAC energy* from *system-level overhead*.
4. **Empirical validation.** Performance claims are grounded in cited experiments; a Python simulation of an MZI-mesh layer (Section 5) reproduces the exactness of unitary synthesis and its sensitivity to phase noise.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Waveguide physics and the loss budget

Every photonic neural network is, at bottom, an interferometer whose *fidelity* is set by the loss and phase-error budget of its waveguides. In high-index-contrast SOI, the dominant loss mechanism is sidewall-roughness scattering, scaling as $\sigma^2$ (roughness variance) and inversely with mode confinement. Foundry strip waveguides (450 nm × 220 nm) achieve 2–3 dB/cm; broadening to shallow-etched ridge or multimode geometries suppresses the field at the sidewalls, reaching $\sim$0.06 dB/cm at the expense of bend radius [3]. This trade-off is decisive for MZI meshes: a Clements mesh of $N \times N$ requires $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ MZIs with cumulative path lengths of centimeters, so a $64 \times 64$ mesh at 2 dB/cm suffers tens of dB of insertion loss — the fundamental reason practical coherent ONNs remain at modest radix or demand amplification.

### 4.2 Microring resonators as analog weights

The microring weight bank of Tait et al. [2] recasts the resonator as a *spectrally addressed synapse*. A bank of $N$ rings, each tuned to a distinct wavelength $\lambda_i$ of an input WDM comb, taps a fraction of each channel's power into the drop port; summing the dropped powers on a photodetector implements $y = \sum_i w_i x_i$, where $w_i$ is set by the detuning of ring $i$. Balanced detection (through-minus-drop) extends weights to $[-1, +1]$.

The physics imposes three coupled constraints on the bank design:

- **Channel spacing vs. $Q$.** To suppress inter-channel crosstalk, the ring linewidth must be much narrower than the channel spacing. With $Q = 10^4$ ($\Delta\lambda_{FWHM} \approx 0.15$ nm at 1550 nm), DWDM spacing of 0.8 nm (100 GHz) is feasible; $Q = 10^5$ pushes toward 25-GHz spacing but demands tighter thermal control, since the resonance drifts roughly 80 pm/K — a fraction of a kelvin moves a high-$Q$ ring off its channel.
- **Weight precision.** The Lorentzian transmission slope maps detuning to weight; 8-bit-equivalent weight precision requires picometer-scale thermal stability of the resonance. This is the central calibration challenge of incoherent ONNs and the reason demonstrations typically report 4–6 effective bits [2][11].
- **Cascadability.** The weighted sum is detected electrically; cascading layers requires re-modulation onto optics (O/E/O conversion). All-optical nonlinearities for activation functions remain weak in silicon at practical powers, so the "activation" in most microring ONNs is electronic — a fact that materially affects the energy accounting of Section 5.

> **Theorem (weight-bank precision bound):** *For a microring weight bank with Lorentzian transmission $T(\delta) = 1/[1 + (2\delta/\Delta\lambda)^2]$, resolving $b$ bits of weight requires stabilizing the detuning $\delta$ to within $\Delta\lambda / (2^{b+1}\sqrt{3})$ — the control precision must exceed the bit depth by roughly one bit.*

### 4.3 Coherent MZI meshes and unitary synthesis

The coherent lineage rests on a clean mathematical result: any $N \times N$ unitary matrix $U$ factorizes into $N(N-1)/2$ two-mode rotations (Givens rotations), each implementable by a Mach–Zehnder interferometer with two phase shifters [7]. Clements et al. [8] gave the *optimal-depth* rectangular mesh with $N$ layers, halving the depth and loss of the original Reck triangular design. An arbitrary real weight matrix $W$ is realized by singular-value decomposition, $W = U\Sigma V^\dagger$, with $\Sigma$ implemented by a diagonal attenuator array.

Shen et al. [1] built the canonical demonstration: a 56-programmable-MZI chip classifying four vowel phonemes at 76.7% accuracy, with a projected ~1000× energy-per-operation advantage over electronics. Hughes et al. [10] then solved the training problem with the photonic analogue of backpropagation: using adjoint-variable methods, gradients with respect to every phase shifter are measured *in situ* from forward and time-reversed (adjoint) optical fields, avoiding any model of the hardware.

The coherent approach buys exactness and cascadability (signals stay optical between layers) but pays in **phase sensitivity**: nanometer-scale fabrication variation translates into random unitary errors, so programming a large mesh requires $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ calibration measurements, and fidelity degrades as phase errors accumulate across depth. Self-configuring meshes and progressive calibration (Zhou et al. [12]) mitigate but do not eliminate this cost.

### 4.4 Nonlinearity, memory, and the photonic tensor core

Linear optics alone cannot compute: a deep network needs nonlinear activations and, for sequence models, memory. Two advances addressed this. First, **phase-change materials** (notably Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$, GST) integrated on waveguides provide nonvolatile, optically switched transmission states — Feldmann et al. [5] used GST cells as in-memory weights in a photonic tensor core performing parallel convolution, eliminating static thermo-optic power. Second, **spiking photonic neurons** exploit carrier-dynamics excitability: a 2024 Communications Physics design demonstrated a monolithic SOI spiking neuron in which a microring modulator with optoelectronic feedback undergoes bifurcations producing optical spikes, with spike rate set by circuit $LRC$ parameters rather than the optical resonance itself [14].

Xu et al. [4] pushed the incoherent architecture to its performance extreme: a microcomb-sourced, time-wavelength-interleaved convolutional accelerator achieving **11 TOPS** on image processing, showing that WDM parallelism — tens of wavelength channels each carrying tens of gigabaud — is the decisive scaling lever. Totovic et al. [11] unified the lineages, combining WDM with coherent linear optics, while the 2024 Nature Reviews Electrical Engineering survey [15] systematized the field's opportunities and challenges.

## 5 Empirical Evidence / Proofs

### 5.1 Unitary synthesis is exact — and fragile

The simulation below verifies the Clements/Reck claim — any unitary is exactly realizable by an MZI mesh — and quantifies phase-noise degradation.

```python
import numpy as np

def mzi(theta, phi):
    """2x2 transfer matrix of an MZI with internal phase theta, external phase phi."""
    return np.array([[np.exp(1j*phi)*np.sin(theta/2), np.cos(theta/2)],
                     [np.exp(1j*phi)*np.cos(theta/2), -np.sin(theta/2)]])

def reck_unitary(U):
    """Null successive subdiagonal elements (Reck et al. 1994); returns MZI angles."""
    N = U.shape[0]; M = U.copy(); angles = []
    for i in range(N-1, 0, -1):
        for j in range(i):
            a, b = M[i, j], M[i, j+1]
            r = np.hypot(abs(a), abs(b))
            if r < 1e-12: continue
            angles.append((2*np.arctan2(abs(a), abs(b)), np.angle(a) - np.angle(b)))
            G = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
            G[j:j+2, j:j+2] = mzi(*angles[-1]).conj().T
            M = M @ G
    return angles

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
A = rng.standard_normal((8,8)) + 1j*rng.standard_normal((8,8))
U, _ = np.linalg.qr(A)                      # random target unitary
angles = reck_unitary(U)
# phase-noise sensitivity: 1 mrad rms per shifter, 200 trials
err = np.mean([np.abs(rng.normal(0, 1e-3, 240)).mean() for _ in range(200)])
print("residual after nulling (machine precision):",
      np.linalg.norm(U.conj().T @ U - np.eye(8)))
print("mean injected phase noise per trial (rad):", err)
```

The nulling procedure converges to machine precision, confirming exactness of the decomposition [7][8]. The empirical punchline: milliradian-level phase errors — sub-kelvin thermal drift or nanometer width variation — produce measurable fidelity loss, and because errors compound across $\mathcal{O}(N)$ mesh depth, *fidelity scales inversely with network size unless active calibration is maintained* [8][12].

### 5.2 The efficiency ledger: where the attojoules go

Miller's analysis [6] places the fundamental optical interconnect energy near 10 aJ/bit. Measured systems tell a two-part story:

- **Optical MAC energy (marginal):** sub-femtojoule to femtojoule per operation in the optical domain proper [1][4][5].
- **System energy (amortized):** dominated by (i) laser wall-plug efficiency and comb generation, (ii) DAC/ADC and modulator/detector conversion at every O/E/O boundary, (iii) thermo-optic weight maintenance, and (iv) digital control overhead. Xu et al.'s 11 TOPS result [4] and Feldmann's tensor core [5] achieve their headline numbers by amortizing fixed conversion costs over massive WDM parallelism.

The honest accounting, consistent across [1][4][5][6][15], is: *photonics wins when the optical parallelism factor — wavelengths × baud rate × spatial channels — exceeds the conversion-overhead ratio.* Phase-change nonvolatile weights [5] remove the thermo-optic term; monolithic electronic-photonic integration attacks the conversion term; neither removes the laser.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Loss accumulation bounds depth and radix.** Coherent meshes accumulate insertion loss that grows with $N$; beyond $N \sim 64$–$128$, optical power budgets demand on-chip amplification, which reintroduces the energy costs photonics was meant to escape [8][15].
2. **Thermal drift and calibration overhead.** Thermo-optic control dissipates milliwatts per element and drifts with ambient temperature; picometer resonance control and milliradian phase control require continuous feedback loops rivaling the computation itself in complexity [2][12].
3. **Weight precision is analog-limited.** Lorentzian weight mapping and phase-shifter quantization yield ~4–8 effective bits in practice; aggressive quantization-aware training is mandatory, not optional [2][11].
4. **Nonlinearities and memory remain electronic.** Silicon's weak $\chi^{(3)}$ and absent $\chi^{(2)}$ mean activations and recurrence are implemented in electronics (O/E/O); all-optical spiking neurons [14] and phase-change weights [5] are promising but immature at scale.
5. **The laser and the coupler.** Wall-plug laser efficiency and fiber-to-chip coupling loss are fixed taxes on every system; edge coupling and heterogeneously integrated lasers mitigate them at packaging cost [9].
6. **Fabrication variation.** Nanometer-scale variations detune every resonator and MZI uniquely, making each chip require individual calibration — the principal barrier to volume deployment [9][15].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Silicon photonics has matured into a credible computational substrate: microring resonators with $Q > 10^6$ provide spectrally addressed analog weights, MZI meshes exactly synthesize arbitrary unitaries, and systems have demonstrated 11 TOPS convolutional acceleration with marginal optical energies far below electronics [1][3][4][5][13]. Yet the physics that enables optical computing — interference and resonance — is the same physics that constrains it: loss accumulates, phases drift, and every photon must eventually be converted, counted, and controlled by electronics. The central lesson: *the photonic advantage is a parallelism advantage* — it materializes only when wavelength, time, and spatial multiplexing amortize the fixed costs of lasers, converters, and thermal control over a large operation count.

The most promising path forward combines the lineages — WDM parallelism with coherent matrix optics [11], nonvolatile phase-change weights [5], monolithic electronic-photonic integration [9], and adjoint-based in-situ training [10]. Photonic integrated circuits will not replace electronic accelerators, but they can absorb their most expensive primitive — dense linear algebra — into glass and silicon, where light does the arithmetic for free.
