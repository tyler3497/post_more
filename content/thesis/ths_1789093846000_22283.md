# AlphaFold and the Protein Structure Prediction Problem: Evoformer Attention, the Structure Module, and Diffusion-Based Design

## Abstract

Protein structure prediction — inferring the three-dimensional fold of a polypeptide from its amino acid sequence — was for five decades the central unsolved problem of computational structural biology. In 2020, AlphaFold2 solved it to experimental accuracy at CASP14, achieving a median backbone RMSD below 1 Å on the hardest free-modeling targets [1]. This thesis dissects the architecture behind that breakthrough: the **Evoformer**, which couples a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) representation to a pairwise geometric representation through row- and column-wise attention and triangle updates that enforce metric consistency; and the **structure module**, which decodes these representations into 3D coordinates using *invariant point attention* over rigid-body frames, trained with the frame-aligned point error (FAPE) loss and *recycling*. It then traces the diffusion revolution: **AlphaFold3** (Abramson et al., 2024), which replaces the structure module with a diffusion-based all-atom generative model over biomolecular complexes [2], and **RFdiffusion**, which inverts the problem to perform *de novo* protein design by diffusing in SE(3) frame space [3]. Finally, it confronts the limits: dynamics, disorder, and the gap between static snapshots and the conformational ensembles biology uses.

## 1 Introduction

In 1973, Christian Anfinsen summarized the thermodynamic hypothesis that made protein folding a well-posed computational problem: the native structure of a protein is determined by its amino acid sequence alone, occupying the global minimum of the Gibbs free energy [4]. The hypothesis was a promise and a provocation. Levinthal's paradox had already quantified the absurdity of blind search: a 100-residue chain has roughly $10^{100}$ possible conformations, yet folding occurs in milliseconds [5]. Somewhere between sequence and structure lay a code — and fifty years of computational biology was an attempt to crack it.

Progress was glacial and then sudden. Through the 2010s, contact-prediction methods exploited a beautiful evolutionary signal: when two residues are in physical contact in the 3D structure, mutations at one position are compensated by mutations at the other, producing *coevolutionary* correlations in multiple sequence alignments. Direct-coupling analysis (DCA) and deep residual networks (notably RaptorX) turned alignments into contact maps [6]. AlphaFold1 (2018) pushed this line to its limit with distance *distributions* rather than binary contacts.

Then AlphaFold2 (AF2) arrived at CASP14 in 2020 and the problem essentially ended for single-domain globular proteins. Its summed z-score of **244.0** more than doubled the second-place method's 92.1 [7]; for 88 of 97 targets it achieved GDT_TS scores above 80, meaning the predicted backbone was within a few angstroms of experiment. Median Cα RMSD was **0.96 Å** — comparable to the width of a carbon atom. The resolution of the structure prediction problem was not achieved by better physics, but by a neural architecture — the **Evoformer** — that learns to reason about residue–residue geometry as a *graph inference problem in 3D space* [1].

This thesis examines that architecture in depth (§2–§4), derives the key mathematical machinery (§3–§4), surveys the empirical evidence (§5), and closes with the frontier: diffusion-based all-atom modeling (AlphaFold3, §4.4), diffusion-based *design* (RFdiffusion, §4.5), and the open problems that remain (§6).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Anfinsen to CASP

The protein structure prediction problem comes in flavors of increasing difficulty, as formalized by the Critical Assessment of Protein Structure Prediction (CASP) competition:

- **TBM (template-based modeling):** a detectable homolog exists; the problem is aligning and refining.
- **FM (free modeling):** no template exists; the fold must be predicted *ab initio*.

Before AF2, FM targets were essentially hopeless: best GDT_TS scores hovered around 30–50, barely better than random packing. The dominant signal was evolutionary: an MSA of homologous sequences, from which coevolution statistics were extracted. Methods like DCA [8] computed precision matrices over the alignment; convolutional networks learned to map these to contact maps. All of this treated the alignment as a *fixed feature* — covariance matrices, mutual information — computed once and fed to a network.

### 2.2 Transformers and geometric priors

AF2's radical move was to feed the network the **raw MSA** as a categorical tensor and let attention discover the relevant statistics end-to-end [1]. The architectural insight draws on the transformer [9]: attention is a differentiable, content-based lookup that can express arbitrary pairwise relationships. The Evoformer specializes this with **geometric inductive biases** — the triangle updates of §3.2 — which hard-code the triangle inequality, the fundamental constraint of metric geometry, into the representation [8].

### 2.3 The residue-gas representation

AF2 represents each residue as a **rigid body** (a rotation $R_k \in SO(3)$ and translation $\mathbf{t}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ defining the backbone frame from Cα–C–N geometry) plus torsion angles $\chi$ for side chains — the *residue gas* [1, Fig. 3e]. This is the decoder's state: invariant point attention updates these frames, and a final refinement places all atoms. The frame formulation is crucial because it makes the loss (FAPE, §3.3) **SE(3)-invariant**: a rigid-body transformation of the whole prediction changes nothing.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Evoformer: two representations, four operations

The Evoformer maintains two coupled representations through $N=48$ blocks:

| Representation | Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| MSA representation $\{m_{si}\}$ | $S \times L \times c_m$ | Sequence $s$, residue $i$; evolutionary context |
| Pair representation $\{z_{ij}\}$ | $L \times L \times c_z$ | Directed edge $i \to j$; geometric relations |

($S \approx 512$ sequences, $L$ residues, $c_m = 256$, $c_z = 128$.) Each block applies, **in series**, operations that alternate between the two representations [1, Fig. 3a]:

1. **Row-wise gated self-attention** on the MSA: each sequence's residues attend to each other — capturing intra-sequence context.
2. **Column-wise gated self-attention** on the MSA: at each residue position, sequences attend across homologs — this is where the network *learns* coevolution, discovering which homologs carry signal.
3. **Triangle multiplicative update and triangle self-attention** on the pair representation (see §3.2).
4. **Outer-product mean**: the MSA representation updates the pair representation via an element-wise outer product summed over sequences, $\mathbf{z}_{ij} \mathrel{+}= \text{Linear}(\mathbf{m}_i \otimes \mathbf{m}_j)$, applied *in every block* so the pair representation continuously absorbs evolving evolutionary information.

The single representation handed to the structure module is the first row of the MSA stack (the query sequence), projected to 384 channels [1].

### 3.2 Triangle updates: baking in metric geometry

> **Theorem (informal):** *In any metric space, distances satisfy the triangle inequality $d_{ij} \le d_{ik} + d_{kj}$. Any consistent pairwise representation of a molecular geometry must respect it.*

The Evoformer's most distinctive innovation is operations on the pair representation that take triples $(i, j, k)$ into account — the **triangle multiplicative update** (in two directions, "outgoing" and "incoming") and **triangle self-attention** around a starting and ending node [1, Fig. 3c]. For the edge $z_{ij}$ being updated, the network gates information through all intermediate $k$:

$$\tilde{z}_{ij} = \sum_k g(z_{ik}) \odot h(z_{kj}),$$

conceptually enforcing that the relation $i \to j$ be *consistent* with the path $i \to k \to j$ for every $k$. This is a learned, soft version of the triangle inequality — a **geometric prior** that biases the representation toward realizable geometries and away from contradictory pairwise claims. Ablation studies show removing the triangle updates costs several GDT points, confirming they are not cosmetic [1].

```python
# Pseudocode: triangle multiplicative update (outgoing), Evoformer block
def triangle_multiplicative_update(z, c=128):
    # z: [L, L, c] pair representation
    z = layer_norm(z)
    a = linear_a(z) * sigmoid(linear_gate_a(z))   # [L, L, c]
    b = linear_b(z) * sigmoid(linear_gate_b(z))   # [L, L, c]
    # contract over intermediate k: z_ij += sum_k a_ik * b_jk
    update = einsum('ikc,jkc->ijc', a, b)
    return z + sigmoid(linear_out_gate(z)) * linear_out(update)
```

The $O(L^3)$ cost of these updates is the dominant computational expense of AF2 and the reason long chains are expensive.

### 3.3 The structure module: invariant point attention and FAPE

The structure module converts the single and pair representations into 3D coordinates. Its core is **invariant point attention (IPA)** [1, Fig. 3d]. Each residue carries a backbone frame $T_k = (R_k, \mathbf{t}_k)$; IPA computes attention where queries, keys, and values include *3D points expressed in each residue's local frame*:

> **Definition (IPA):** *Given frames $\{T_i\}$, IPA computes attention weights $a_{ij}$ from scalar features, then aggregates values that include points $T_i^{-1} \circ \vec{\mathbf{x}}_j$ — the position of residue $j$'s query points expressed in frame $i$. Because everything is computed in local coordinates, the output is invariant to global rotation and translation.*

This is the decoder-side mirror of the Evoformer's geometric reasoning: where the Evoformer reasons about geometry *implicitly* in pair space, IPA reasons *explicitly* in 3D, updating each residue's frame from the frames of the residues attending to it.

The module is trained with the **frame-aligned point error (FAPE)** [1, Fig. 3f]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{FAPE}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{frames}} N_{\text{atoms}}} \sum_{k} \sum_{i} \min\left(d_{\text{clamp}}, \left\lVert T_k^{-1} \circ \mathbf{x}_i - T_k^{\text{true }-1} \circ \mathbf{x}_i^{\text{true}} \right\rVert\right). $$

Each predicted frame $T_k$ is aligned to its true counterpart, and every atom's error is measured in that local frame, clamped at $d_{\text{clamp}} = 10$ Å. FAPE has three properties that make it the right loss:

1. **SE(3)-invariance** — global rigid motions cost nothing, so the network never wastes capacity on absolute placement.
2. **Locality** — errors are penalized per-frame, so a wrong domain orientation does not drown out correct local geometry (the classic failure of global RMSD).
3. **Clamping** — beyond 10 Å, errors saturate, focusing training on getting *most* atoms right rather than obsessing over the worst outliers.

### 3.4 Recycling and self-distillation

**Recycling** feeds the outputs — the MSA representation, pair representation, and predicted 3D structure — back through the *entire* network (with shared weights) up to 3–4 times at inference [1]. It is iterative refinement: each pass corrects inconsistencies the previous pass left, analogous to a learned optimizer unrolled in depth. Ablations attribute roughly **1.5 GDT points** to recycling [1].

**Self-distillation** trains on ~350,000 predicted structures from UniClust30 in addition to the ~170,000 experimental structures in the PDB. The network learns from its own confident predictions on sequences with no known structure, massively expanding the effective training distribution — a key driver of AF2's generalization to novel folds.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The attention economy: what row/column attention actually learns

Column-wise attention is where coevolution is *rediscovered*. At a fixed residue $i$, the query sequence's representation queries all homologs $s$: sequences that share subfamily-specific features with the query receive high weight. Probing studies show the Evoformer's column attention recovers contact-like structure without ever being supervised on contacts — the network invents its own, richer statistics than mutual information [7]. Row-wise attention, meanwhile, propagates constraints along each sequence, allowing the model to resolve *which* pairs of positions the column statistics implicate.

### 4.2 Why the pair representation is a directed graph

The pair representation is explicitly interpreted as **directed edges** in a residue graph [1, Fig. 3b]: $z_{ij}$ need not equal $z_{ji}$. Directionality matters because geometric relations are asymmetric under the frame formulation — the vector from $i$'s frame to $j$ differs from the reverse. The outer-product mean then acts as message passing from node features (MSA rows) to edge features, repeated 48 times: a deep graph neural network where the "messages" are learned bilinear interactions.

### 4.3 pLDDT and PAE: confidence as a first-class output

AF2 predicts its own accuracy. **pLDDT** (predicted local distance difference test), per residue, estimates the Cα lDDT against the (unknown) truth; **PAE** (predicted aligned error) estimates the expected positional error of residue $x$ when the structure is aligned on residue $y$. Empirically, pLDDT $< 50$ marks disorder — AF2's low-confidence regions correlate strongly with intrinsically disordered regions [11]. This turned a limitation into a feature: the *AlphaFold Protein Structure Database* (200M+ structures) ships pLDDT as a disorder annotator [10].

### 4.4 AlphaFold3: replacing the structure module with diffusion

AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al., *Nature* 2024) keeps the Evoformer-like trunk but replaces the IPA structure module with a **diffusion-based generative model** over *all atoms* — proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, and modified residues jointly [2]. Key changes:

- **No more residue frames.** AF3 operates directly on atomic coordinates with a *Pairformer* (MSA-free, simplified Evoformer) and a diffusion module that denoises noisy coordinates conditioned on the representations — a direct descendant of denoising diffusion probabilistic models [9].
- **Unified biomolecular scope.** Because atoms are atoms, the same model predicts protein–ligand poses (dramatically better than docking tools on PoseBusters), protein–nucleic acid interfaces, and antibody–antigen complexes — where sampling 1,000 seeds improves DockQ substantially [2].
- **MSA-free capability.** AF3 shows that cross-entity evolutionary information is not essential: chemistry and physics learned from data suffice for many interactions [2].

The diffusion formulation also naturally yields *ensembles*: different noise seeds give different samples, a first step toward modeling conformational diversity rather than a single structure.

### 4.5 RFdiffusion: inverting the arrow — from prediction to design

If AF2 maps sequence → structure, **RFdiffusion** (Watson et al., *Nature* 2023) maps *specification → structure*: it is a generative model of protein backbones obtained by fine-tuning the RoseTTAFold structure-prediction network on a **denoising diffusion** objective in SE(3) frame space [3]. Forward diffusion corrupts backbone frames $(R_i, \mathbf{t}_i)$ with noise on $SO(3) \times \mathbb{R}^3$; the network learns the reverse process, conditioned on design constraints:

- **Unconditional generation** of novel folds;
- **Motif scaffolding**: hold a functional motif fixed, diffuse the rest;
- **Binder design**: generate a backbone that complements a target surface;
- **Symmetric oligomers**: cyclic and dihedral assemblies.

Experimental validation was unprecedented: >80% success rates across tasks, including a 2.9 Å cryo-EM structure of a designed influenza-hemagglutinin binder matching the design at 0.63 Å backbone RMSD [3]. The pipeline — RFdiffusion (backbone) → ProteinMPNN (sequence) → AF2 (validation) — is now the standard *de novo* design stack, and it earned David Baker a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 CASP14: the numbers

| Metric | AlphaFold2 | Best competitor | Typical pre-2020 FM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summed z-score | **244.0** | 92.1 | — |
| Targets with GDT_TS > 80 | 88/97 | — | rare |
| Median Cα RMSD (backbone) | **0.96 Å** | ~2.8 Å | >4 Å |
| RMSD < 1.6 Å (fraction) | ~50% | — | ~0% |

[1, 7]. A GDT_TS of 90 means 90% of Cα atoms are within a few Å of experiment — the regime of *experimental* error. For 25% of targets the all-atom RMSD was below 1.5 Å.

### 5.2 AlphaFold3 benchmarks

AF3 substantially exceeds specialized tools across interaction classes [2]: on PoseBusters (protein–ligand), pocket-aligned ligand RMSD < 2 Å far exceeds state-of-the-art docking; on protein–nucleic acid interfaces, interface lDDT beats nucleic-acid-specific predictors; on antibody–antigen DockQ, it markedly improves over AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3. Critically, these gains come from a **single unified model**, not a suite of specialists.

### 5.3 Ablation evidence for the architecture

Jumper et al. report ablations isolating each component's contribution [1]: removing the MSA (single-sequence input) collapses accuracy; removing triangle updates or the outer-product communication degrades GDT by multiple points; recycling adds ~1.5 GDT; self-distillation on UniClust30 predictions improves generalization to novel folds. The lesson: accuracy is not in any one trick but in the *composition* — evolutionary reasoning, geometric priors, invariant decoding, and iterative refinement stacked together.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Dynamics, not structures.** Proteins are ensembles; AF2 predicts a single conformation, typically the lowest-energy state represented in the PDB. Allosteric transitions, folding pathways, and excited states are invisible [11].
2. **Intrinsically disordered regions.** Low pLDDT flags disorder, but AF2 cannot describe the *distribution* of disordered conformations — only its ignorance of them.
3. **Complexes and stoichiometry.** AF2 was trained on monomers; AlphaFold-Multimer and AF3 extend to complexes, but inter-chain arrangement and large assemblies remain harder than single chains [2].
4. **Mutational effects.** AF2 is largely insensitive to point mutations — it predicts the wild-type fold for most mutants — limiting its use for variant-effect prediction without augmentation [1].
5. **Data bias.** The PDB over-represents crystallizable, soluble, static structures; membrane proteins, NMR ensembles, and rare folds are under-represented, and performance follows the bias [7].
6. **No physics, no guarantees.** AF2 is an interpolator over the PDB's manifold, not a simulator. It cannot fold a sequence whose fold class is absent from its training distribution's neighborhood, and it offers no energy function to rank alternatives.

## 7 Conclusion

AlphaFold2 closed a fifty-year problem by reframing it: not as energy minimization, but as **geometric inference over evolutionary data**. The Evoformer's coupled representations — MSA attention discovering coevolution end-to-end, triangle updates enforcing metric consistency — feed an invariant point attention decoder trained with FAPE, a loss that sees geometry the way geometry is. Recycling and self-distillation compound the gains. AlphaFold3 then showed the structure module itself was replaceable: diffusion over all atoms unifies proteins, nucleic acids, and ligands in one generative framework [2]. And RFdiffusion showed the arrow runs both ways — from *predicting* the structures evolution found to *designing* structures evolution never tried [3].

The remaining frontier is the one Anfinsen's hypothesis always implied but never captured: proteins do not have *a* structure; they have a Boltzmann ensemble of them, and function lives in the ensemble. Diffusion models, which natively produce distributions rather than points, are the natural tool — and the arc from AF2's single structure to AF3's sampled ensembles to RFdiffusion's designed backbones suggests the next decade belongs to generative models of the full conformational landscape.
