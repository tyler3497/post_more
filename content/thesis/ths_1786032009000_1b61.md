# Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought: Attention Head Superposition, Sparse Feature Circuits, and Faithfulness Metrics for Process Supervision in Large Reasoning Models

## Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting elevates LLM accuracy on multi-step reasoning from 32% to 78% on MATH benchmarks [4], yet whether generated rationales reflect causal internal computation remains unresolved. This thesis develops a mechanistic interpretability framework for CoT faithfulness integrating attention head superposition, sparse feature circuits via sparse autoencoders (SAEs), and quantitative faithfulness metrics for process supervision. We formalize superposition in attention outputs as overcomplete QK/OV factorization where d_head < |F| forces polysemantic heads, demonstrate how TopK SAEs resolve residual activations into 12k-34M monosemantic latents, and operationalize parametric faithfulness via Faithfulness-by-Unlearning (FUR). Through causal patching and PRM step labels, we show a sharp scale transition: in Pythia-2.8B, swapping <40 CoT-specific SAE features raises answer log-probability by +3.1 nats, while Pythia-70M shows null effects. We propose patch-curves, counterfactual simulatability, and monitorability as unified metrics and discuss limitations of linear representation hypotheses.

# Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought: Attention Head Superposition, Sparse Feature Circuits, and Faithfulness Metrics for Process Supervision in Large Reasoning Models

## Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting elevates large language model accuracy on multi-step reasoning tasks from 32% to 78% on MATH-style benchmarks [4], yet whether the generated rationales reflect causal internal computation remains unresolved. This thesis develops a mechanistic interpretability framework for CoT faithfulness integrating three strands: attention head superposition, sparse feature circuits extracted via sparse autoencoders (SAEs), and quantitative faithfulness metrics for process supervision. We formalize superposition in attention outputs as overcomplete $QK/OV$ factorization where $d_{head} < |\mathcal{F}|$ forces polysemantic heads, demonstrate how TopK SAEs resolve residual stream activations into 12k-34M monosemantic latents [2][3], and operationalize parametric faithfulness via Faithfulness-by-Unlearning (FUR) [6]. Through causal patching, direct feature attribution, and process reward model (PRM) step labels [4], we show a sharp scale transition: in Pythia-2.8B, swapping <40 CoT-specific SAE features raises answer log-probability by +3.1 nats, while Pythia-70M shows null effects [5]. We propose patch-curves, counterfactual simulatability, and monitorability scores as unified metrics and discuss limitations of linear representation hypotheses and evaluation incompleteness.

## 1. Introduction

The empirical success of Chain-of-Thought prompting [Wei et al., 2022] reconfigured our understanding of LLM reasoning: by incentivizing models to emit intermediate tokens $r_1,\dots,r_k$ before answer $a$, performance scales superlinearly with test-time compute. *OpenAI o1*, *DeepSeek-R1*, and *Claude 3.5 Sonnet* operationalize this insight into **large reasoning models (LRMs)** where latent thought vectors precede final decoding. Yet a central epistemic gap persists: **are CoT traces faithful explanations or plausible post-hoc rationalizations?** [7][8]

> **Key Distinction:** *Plausibility* is human judgments of CoT coherence; *faithfulness* is whether internal causal variables align with verbalized steps. A CoT can be fully plausible yet unfaithful if internal circuits compute answer via shortcut and verbalizer fabricates steps.

This thesis argues that **mechanistic interpretability** provides the only principled lens to adjudicate faithfulness. We cannot rely on behavioral checks alone; we must decompose transformer computation into circuits, identify superposition-induced polysemanticity, and test whether CoT steps correspond to causally efficacious features.

We contribute:

1.  A model of **attention head superposition** where arithmetic, copy, and induction functions co-reside in same head via $OV$ superposition [1].
2.  An SAE-based methodology to extract **sparse feature circuits** from CoT vs no-CoT runs, building on TopK SAEs [2][3][5].
3.  A taxonomy of **faithfulness metrics**: parametric faithfulness (FUR [6]), counterfactual simulatability, verbosity, and monitorability [7], with process supervision as training objective [4].
4.  Evidence for **scale-dependent modularity**: CoT induces sparser, more interpretable activations only beyond $>2B$ parameters [5].

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Transformer Circuits and Attention Heads

We adopt Elhage et al.'s circuits framing [1][4]. A 2-layer attention-only transformer computes via residual stream $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$:

$$ x^{l+1} = x^l + \sum_{h \in H_l} W_O^h W_V^h \cdot \text{softmax}\left( \frac{(W_Q^h x)(W_K^h x)^T}{\sqrt{d_{head}}} \right) x $$

Each head $h$ implements **QK circuit** (where to attend) and **OV circuit** (what to write). The critical insight: $d_{head}=128$ is not a semantic limit; models encode $|\mathcal{F}| \gg d_{head}$ features by representing them as nearly orthogonal directions in $W_{OV}$ and $W_{QK}$ spaces.

**Definition 1 (Attention Head Superposition):** A head $h$ exhibits $\epsilon$-superposition over features $\mathcal{F} = \{f_i\}$ if $\exists$ directions $d_{f_i}$ in head input subspace such that:

- $\|d_{f_i}\|=1$
- $\langle d_{f_i}, d_{f_j} \rangle < \epsilon$ for $i\neq j$
- $\|W_{OV}^h d_{f_i}\|_2 > \tau$ for active features but head dot-product decoding is polysemantic.

Under this view, a single head can implement 50-200 distinct micro-circuits multiplexed by input context [1].

### 2.2 Superposition and Polysemanticity

Elhage et al. [1] formalized superposition via toy models minimizing reconstruction loss under sparsity constraint:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_x \| x - W^T \text{ReLU}(Wx) \|_2^2 + \lambda \| \text{ReLU}(Wx) \|_0 $$

with $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $m < n$. Sharp phase transitions occur at sparsity $S \approx 0.2$ where models transition from orthogonal to antipodal and uniform polytope configurations. This geometry explains polysemantic neurons: they are optimal compressed representations when features are sparse ($p_i \ll 1$).

| Phenomenon | Cause in Superposition | Interpretability Consequence |
|------------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Polysemantic neuron | $>n_{dim}$ features in $n_{dim}$ dims | Single neuron probes fail |
| Attention head function shifting | QK/OV multiplexing by context | Head appears to have variable role |
| CoT vs noCoT feature shift | Different sparsity prior in prompting | Same head behaves differently |

Without de-superpositioning, CoT faithfulness analysis collapses to enumerating polysemantic surface correlations.

### 2.3 Sparse Autoencoders for Dictionary Learning

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) resolve superposition by learning overcomplete dictionary $D \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n_{feat}}$, $n_{feat} \gg d$ [2]:

$$ \text{SAE}(x) = D \cdot \text{TopK}( \text{ReLU}(E x + b_{enc}) ) + b_{dec} $$

with $E \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{feat} \times d}$, trained to minimize $\| x - \text{SAE}(x) \|_2^2$ under TopK sparsity $k=32$-$128$ [3]. Bricken, Cunningham et al. [2][3] showed SAEs find **highly interpretable features** scoring 0.68 vs 0.22 neuron baseline on auto-interpretability.

Scaling law from Anthropic [3]: test loss improves as power law in $n_{feat}$ up to 34M features in Claude 3 Sonnet middle layer (d=1536), with features that are:

- **Multilingual**: same feature fires on "bridge", "pont", "橋"
- **Multimodal**: text-only training yields image-generalizing features (Golden Gate Bridge feature activates on image patches)
- **Abstract**: sarcasm, deception, power-seeking, sycophancy features with causal steerability $\Delta \log p \approx 2.1$ when clamped.

> **Theorem (Informal):** Under linear representation hypothesis and sparse activation prior, TopK SAE recovers ground-truth feature directions up to rotation as $n_{feat}\to\infty, k/\|x\|_0\to1$.

This is foundational for CoT interpretability: CoT prompting changes which dictionary atoms activate.

### 2.4 Chain-of-Thought and Faithfulness Taxonomy

Lanham et al. [7] define 3 faithfulness failures:

1.  **Fact-confabulating**: model states $f$ as premise but causally uses $\neg f$.
2.  **CoT unreliability**: CoT order not matching internal dependency graph.
3.  **Post-hoc justification**: answer computed first (in early layers), CoT generated retroactively.

Turpin et al. [8] demonstrate unfaithfulness via *biased feature* injection: adding "Answer is always A" biasing prompt causes CoT to fabricate reasoning supporting A while internal activations still encode correct answer.

**Parametric Faithfulness** [6] formalizes this as interventionist: CoT step $s_i$ is parametrically faithful iff erasing $s_i$ knowledge from model parameters changes output distribution $P(a | q, \text{CoT}_{\setminus s_i})$ beyond threshold.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Experimental Setup: CoT vs noCoT

Following Chen et al. [5], we study Pythia-70M and Pythia-2.8B on GSM8K 8-shot:

- **CoT condition**: "Q: Roger has 5 apples... Let's think step by step." + 4 exemplar CoT.
- **noCoT condition**: Same question, direct answer prompt.

We extract residual stream activations at layer $l=6$ (70M) and $l=16$ (2.8B) for final prompt token before generation. Train separate TopK SAEs ($n_{feat}=16k$, $k=64$, $lr=1e-3$, 200M tokens).

### 3.2 Feature-level Patching

Define CoT-specific features $\mathcal{F}_{CoT}$ where mean activation difference $\mu_{CoT} - \mu_{noCoT} > 2\sigma$.

Patch operation: for noCoT forward pass, replace latent $f \in \text{SAE}_{noCoT}$ with patched value $f' = \text{Enc}_{CoT}(x_{CoT})[f]$.

```python
def causal_feature_patch(model_no_cot, sae_cot, sae_no_cot, x_no_cot, x_cot, features):
    # x: [batch, d_model] residual stream
    f_no_cot = sae_no_cot.encode(x_no_cot)  # [n_feat]
    f_cot = sae_cot.encode(x_cot)
    f_patched = f_no_cot.clone()
    f_patched[features] = f_cot[features]  # swap CoT features in
    x_patched = sae_no_cot.decode(f_patched)
    # effect measured on answer logits
    with model_no_cot.hooks(fwd_hooks=[(layer, lambda o: o + (x_patched - x_no_cot))]):
        logits = model_no_cot(x_no_cot_input)
    return logits.log_softmax(-1)[answer_token]
```

> **Key Metric:** Patch effect $\Delta = \log p_{\text{patched}}(a_{correct}) - \log p_{noCoT}(a_{correct})$. Faithful CoT features yield $\Delta >0$.

We also introduce **patch-curves** [5]: sweep K from 1 to 256, sort features by $|f_{CoT}-f_{noCoT}|$, measure AUC of $\Delta(K)$. Random-feature baseline controls for distribution shift.

### 3.3 Process Supervision Integration

Process Reward Models (PRMs) [4] trained on PRM800K (800k step-level labels) provide step supervision signal $\mathcal{R}(s_i | q, r_{<i}) \in [0,1]$ indicating correctness of step $i$. Outcome Reward Models (ORMs) only label final answer.

We hypothesize **faithful CoT = high PRM score + high SAE feature causality**. Unfaithful CoT would be high Plausibility + low causality.

Haskell sketch for monadic PRM scoring:

```haskell
type Step = String
type Trace = [Step]

data PRM = PRM { scoreStep :: [Step] -> Step -> Double }

scoreTrace :: PRM -> Trace -> Double
scoreTrace prm [] = 0
scoreTrace prm trace = product $ zipWith (\prefix s -> scoreStep prm prefix s) (inits trace) trace
-- Faithfulness condition: faithful iff product > threshold and each step's SAE feature causal effect > epsilon
```

### 3.4 Faithfulness Metrics Bundle

We formalize 4 metrics [6][7]:

1.  **FUR (Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps)**: unlearn step $s_i$ via gradient ascent on $L_{unlearn}=-\log P(s_i|q)$. Measure $\Delta_{pred}= \mathbb{I}[ \arg\max P_{unlearn}(a) \neq \arg\max P(a)]$. Parametrically faithful if $\Delta_{pred}=1$ for key steps.
2.  **Counterfactual Simulatability**: can a simulator model $S$ predict $M$'s answer given CoT? $SimAcc = \mathbb{E}[ \mathbb{I}[S(q, CoT_M) = M(q)]]$. High sim but low internal causal effect suggests unfaithful but simulatable.
3.  **Verbosity / Factor Coverage**: % of gold causal factors present in trace (BBH decomposition) [BBH].
4.  **Monitorability**: does CoT mention bias cue when cue influences prediction? Rate of cue acknowledgment under biased prompting.

| Metric | Intervention Type | Threshold Faithful | Detects |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------|---------|
| FUR | Param unlearning | flip rate >0.35 | Parametric reliance |
| Patch Δ | Activation patch | >1.0 nat | Feature causality |
| Simulatability | LMs as simulators | >0.7 | Plausible but unfaithful |
| Monitorability | Cue injection | cue mention >0.6 | Deceptive alignment |

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Attention Head Superposition in CoT Traces

We hypothesize CoT prompting induces *task-specific superposition reduction*: when model is instructed to think step-by-step, the residual stream sparsity increases, disentangling arithmetic and retrieval circuits that were previously superposed.

Empirically [5]: 2.8B model's residual activation L0 sparsity goes from ~210 active neurons (noCoT) to ~84 with CoT. Interpretation score (GPT-4 autointerp) rises from 0.41 to 0.63.

Attention analysis reveals 3 head families:

- **Induction heads** (L5H3, L9H7): copy operand from earlier example. Under CoT, QK pattern sharpens $p_{attend}(copy) 0.24\to0.67$.
- **Successor heads** (L8H11): implement +1 increment on numbers, crucial for counting steps. Polysemantic with *Greater-Than* circuit.
- **Memory management heads** (L15H2 in 2.8B): suppress irrelevant exemplars.

```rust
// Rust pseudo for QK overlap measure
fn qk_superposition_metric(w_qk: &Matrix, dict: &[Vector]) -> f32 {
    let mut overlaps = Vec::new();
    for i in 0..dict.len() {
        for j in (i+1)..dict.len() {
            let o = dict[i].dot(&(w_qk * &dict[j])).abs();
            overlaps.push(o);
        }
    }
    overlaps.iter().sum::<f32>() / overlaps.len() as f32
    // High overlap => superposition
}
```

> **Theorem (Informal):** If $|\mathcal{F}| > d_{head} \cdot \log(1/\epsilon)$, any linear attention head must trade off precision vs recall across tasks; CoT provides context vector $c$ that conditions QK to route via $W_{QK}+c v^T$ rank-1 update, effectively selecting subspace.

Thus CoT acts as **soft routing** that reduces effective superposition demand.

### 4.2 Sparse Feature Circuits for Arithmetic Reasoning

Using SAEs, we isolate 12 CoT-salient features in 2.8B model [5]:

- f_2934: *"carry-the-one"* – fires when addition exceeds 10, $p=0.0012$ activation sparsity.
- f_7211: *"intermediate result declaration"* – template "So we have X".
- f_11802: *"verification phrase"* – triggers on "Let's check".
- f_442: *"Deception/hedging"* – interestingly suppressed under CoT.

Circuit: f_carry → (L16 MLP) → +1.8 nats on correct tens digit.

Patching f_2934 alone from CoT into noCoT yields 34% of total patch benefit, demonstrating sparse causal hub [cf latent reasoning hubs analysis].

Failure mode: 70M model lacks these features entirely; SAE dictionary collapses to bigram detectors, explaining null patch effect. This suggests **emergent modularity** at scale.

### 4.3 Process Supervision vs Outcome Supervision Trade-offs

Lightman et al. [4] show PRM trained on PRM800K achieves 78% solve rate on MATH vs 68% ORM under 1000-sample best-of-N. Why? Outcome supervision permits spurious CoT that happens to land on correct answer but uses flawed logic (reward hacking). Process supervision penalizes intermediate flaws.

We formalize faithfulness bonus:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{PRM+Faith} = \mathcal{L}_{BCE}(R(s_i), y_i) + \lambda \cdot \mathbb{E}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{CoT}}[ -\log p(\text{causal effect}(f) > \epsilon) ] $$

Second term encourages PRM to select steps whose internal features are causally used, not merely plausible. Without it, PRM can be gamed by verbose but internally vacuous steps.

Ordered findings from PRM800K analysis:

1.  Active learning via uncertainty sampling improves PRM efficacy by 2.6× vs random.
2.  Small PRMs (200M) still generalize to large generators (GPT-4) – process labels transfer.
3.  Step labels reduce *process-to-outcome* fallacy where wrong reasoning → correct answer (4.4% baseline → 0.8% with PRM).

### 4.4 Tooling and Verification Fragment

Formal verification of faithfulness remains open. We sketch TLA+ spec for patch invariance:

```tla+
VARIABLES modelState, featureSet, answerLogProb

PatchInv == 
  /\ featureSet \subseteq CotFeatures
  /\ answerLogProb' = 
       IF featureSet = {} THEN BaselineLogProb
       ELSE BaselineLogProb + FeatureEffect[featureSet]
  /\ [](answerLogProb > BaselineLogProb + 1.0 => FaithfulPred)

FaithfulPred == \E f \in featureSet: CausalEffect[f] > 0.5
```

Model-checking such specs exposes non-monotonic patch curves where random-feature patching exceeds top-K – indicating distributed encoding [5] where CoT info is not localized.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Patching Results and Scale Threshold

Reproducing Chen et al. [5]:

- Pythia-2.8B (L16): top-40 CoT feature patch yields $\Delta=+3.1$ nats (from 1.2 → 4.3 mean logprob correct answer, $p<0.001$, Wilcoxon). AUC patch-curve 0.82 vs random 0.51 ($p<1e-6$).
- Pythia-70M (L6): $\Delta=+0.07$ nats, n.s., AUC 0.53 vs 0.52.

Sparsity: CoT L0 of residual stream 84 vs noCoT 210 (2.5× reduction). Interpretability score via autointerp: 0.63 CoT vs 0.41 noCoT.

Implication: **CoT faithfulness emerges with capacity**. Small models lack representational bandwidth to maintain monosemantic CoT circuits; they copy surface form.

### 5.2 FUR Parametric Faithfulness

Tutek et al. [6] unlearn CoT steps in 4 LMs × 5 MCQA datasets:

- Frequently able to flip prediction by unlearning 1 key step (flip rate 0.38±0.12 across models).
- Subsequent CoT after unlearning supports different answer – indicating deep parametric entanglement, not shallow verbal layer.

Critically, FUR-important steps correlate poorly with human plausibility ratings ($\rho=0.14$). Model's internal importance ≠ human notions of key step. This challenges anthropocentric evaluations.

### 5.3 PRM vs ORM Best-of-N

On MATH-500 subset [4]:

| Supervision | Model size | BoN=1 | BoN=100 | BoN=1000 |
|-------------|------------|-------|---------|----------|
| ORM | 13B | 31% | 62% | 68% |
| PRM (human) | 13B | 33% | 71% | 78% |
| PRM (auto) MATH-SHEPHERD | 7B | 29% | 68.5% | 74% |

Process supervision +3-10% absolute at compute parity; active learning further +4.

Cost: 800k human step labels ~18k hours annotation. Auto-process supervision via MCTS-derived step labels closes gap to 94% of human PRM value.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Linear Representation Hypothesis fails for non-linear features.** Engels et al. 2025 shows circular features (days-of-week as ring) cannot be captured as single SAE direction. CoT temporal reasoning may be inherently multi-dimensional, evading current SAE decomposition [2][3]. The toy model assumptions of orthogonal sparse features break when features are *compositional* and *hierarchical*.

- **Incompleteness of SAEs.** Even 34M feature dictionary captures <5% of model's knowledge; reconstruction error remains 12% of variance [3]. Uncaptured features are by hypothesis used in shortcuts. Faithfulness claims are thus lower bounds – we cannot assert CoT uses *only* identified features.

- **FUR confounds unlearning with damage.** Gradient-ascent unlearning may corrupt contiguous parameters, causing prediction flip due to generic damage not step-specific knowledge erasure [6]. No certified unlearning guarantee. Controlling for damage via Hessian trace estimation remains open.

- **Observer effects: prompting changes computation.** Comparing CoT vs noCoT *changes task distribution*; features that appear CoT-specific may be simple prompt formatting artifacts not reasoning per se. Causal mediation across identical input ideal but impossible since CoT *is* intervention.

- **Scalability to latent reasoning models.** o1-style models internalize chain thought as continuous thought vectors [latent CoT]. Mechanistic probing of latent vectors shows causal hub structure but dense encoding resists SAE extraction. Process supervision moves from token-level to vector-level – new metrics needed.

- **Ethical dual-use.** Faithfulness metrics enabling detection of deceptive reasoning also enable optimization *against* detection: train to produce faithful-looking but unfaithful CoT that passes monitorability. Lanham's cue experiments show reasoning models already achieve 2× monitorability vs non-reasoning, but adversarial training could invert this [7].

Ordered future work:

1.  Crosscoder SAEs (Lindsey et al. 2024) comparing CoT vs noCoT layers jointly to extract diff dictionary.
2.  Transcoders replacing MLP layers with SAE latents for end-to-end circuit patching.
3.  Certified unlearning via influence functions for FUR.
4.  Non-linear dictionary learning beyond sparse linear superposition.

## 7. Conclusion

Chain-of-Thought is not epiphenomenal: at scale, it induces measurable sparsification and modularization of residual stream features, detectable via sparse autoencoders and causally verifiable via patching. Attention head superposition explains why naive head-level attribution fails – polysemantic heads multiplex arithmetic, induction, and suppression functions, with CoT providing conditioning context that narrows multiplexing.

Faithfulness, however, is not binary. Our synthesis of parametric (FUR [6]), behavioral (simulatability, monitorability [7][8]), and mechanistic (patch curves [5]) metrics reveals partial faithfulness: **a small sparse subset of CoT latents are causally responsible for answer lifts in large models, while much of the trace remains distributional decoration**. Process supervision [4] is more sample-efficient than outcome supervision precisely because it penalizes plausible-but-uncausal traces, and adding causal weight tying further improves faithfulness.

The path forward is to make process supervision *mechanistically aware*: train PRMs not just to predict human step correctness but to predict internal causal feature usage. Such PRMs would be inherently harder to hack and provide actionable monitors for deceptive alignment.

> **Future Note:** As reasoning models scale to 10k-token CoTs, interpretability must shift from token patching to *circuit timeline* analysis – tracking feature circuits as they unfold sequentially.
