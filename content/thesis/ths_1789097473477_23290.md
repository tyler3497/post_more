# Persistent Homology: Filtrations, Barcodes, and Stability Theorems in Topological Data Analysis

## Abstract

Persistent homology has emerged as the central computational tool of topological data analysis, providing a multiscale framework for extracting robust topological features from finite metric data. This thesis develops the theory from first principles: we construct simplicial complexes from point clouds via Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and alpha filtrations; define persistence modules and prove the existence of the barcode decomposition for pointwise finite-dimensional modules; and explicate the correspondence between barcodes and persistence diagrams. We then prove and contextualize the stability theorem of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer, which bounds the bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams by the sup-norm distance of the underlying functions, together with its algebraic generalization to interleavings of persistence modules. Computational methods are treated in depth, including the matrix reduction algorithm and practical speedups via cohomology, clearing, and Morse reductions. We report empirical benchmarks on synthetic manifolds and molecular conformation data, and survey applications in neuroscience, materials science, and machine learning via persistence landscapes.

## 1 Introduction

Modern datasets routinely inhabit high-dimensional spaces where classical statistical summaries fail to capture *shape*: the presence of loops, voids, and higher-dimensional cavities that constrain the geometry of the data-generating process. Topological data analysis (TDA) addresses this gap by equipping point clouds with topological signatures that are simultaneously computable, interpretable, and stable under perturbation [1]. At its core lies a deceptively simple observation: a single topological space tells us little, but a *growing family* of spaces — a **filtration** — tells us which features are signal and which are noise.

The notion of topological persistence was formalized by Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian, who showed that by tracking the lifetime of homological features across a filtration, one can separate meaningful topology from sampling artifacts [1]. Given a simplicial complex built on top of data, homology groups $H_k$ record the $k$-dimensional holes: connected components ($k=0$), loops ($k=1$), voids ($k=2$), and beyond. As the filtration parameter advances, features are *born* and later *die*; the resulting multiset of birth–death intervals is the **barcode**, equivalently visualized as a **persistence diagram** of points in the plane [2].

> **Theorem (Informal stability):** *Small perturbations of the input data produce only small changes in the persistence barcode, when measured in the bottleneck distance.* [2]

This thesis presents a self-contained treatment of the subject. Section 2 recalls the algebraic topology prerequisites and the simplicial constructions that convert data into filtrations. Section 3 develops the algebraic theory of persistence modules, the structure theorem, and the matrix reduction algorithm. Section 4 dives into filtration geometry (Vietoris–Rips versus Čech versus alpha), diagram metrics, the stability theorems, and vectorizations for machine learning. Section 5 reports empirical results: computational benchmarks and two application studies. Section 6 confronts the limitations of the framework, and Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Simplicial complexes and homology

A **simplicial complex** $K$ is a collection of finite vertex sets closed under taking subsets: every face of a simplex in $K$ is also in $K$, and any two simplices intersect in a common face. The $k$-chains $C_k(K)$ form a vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$ (typically $\mathbb{Z}/2$ for computation), and the **boundary operator** $\partial_k: C_k \to C_{k-1}$ satisfies $\partial_{k-1}\circ\partial_k = 0$. The $k$-th **homology group** is the quotient

$$H_k(K) = \ker \partial_k \,/\, \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1},$$

whose dimension $\beta_k$ is the $k$-th **Betti number**: $\beta_0$ counts connected components, $\beta_1$ counts independent loops, $\beta_2$ counts enclosed voids. Working over a field makes each $H_k$ a vector space, which is essential for the algebraic structure theory that follows [4].

### 2.2 From point clouds to complexes

Given a finite point cloud $P \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ and a scale parameter $r \geq 0$, three standard constructions produce complexes:

- **Vietoris–Rips complex** $\mathrm{VR}_r(P)$: a simplex spans any subset of points with all pairwise distances $\leq 2r$. Combinatorially simple but potentially enormous.
- **Čech complex** $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r(P)$: a simplex spans points whose $r$-balls have a common intersection. Homotopy equivalent to the union of balls (by the nerve lemma), but expensive to test.
- **Alpha complex** $\alpha_r(P)$: the nerve of the intersection of $r$-balls with Voronoi cells — geometrically faithful and small in low dimensions.

The sandwich $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r \subseteq \mathrm{VR}_r \subseteq \check{\mathrm{C}}_{\sqrt{2}r}$ (in Euclidean space) quantifies how well Rips approximates Čech, a fact with deep consequences for approximation algorithms [3].

### 2.3 Filtrations and persistence modules

A **filtration** is a nested family $\{K_r\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}}$ with $K_r \subseteq K_s$ for $r \leq s$. Applying $H_k$ yields a **persistence module**: vector spaces $V_r = H_k(K_r)$ with linear maps $v_r^s: V_r \to V_s$ induced by inclusion, satisfying $v_s^t \circ v_r^s = v_r^t$. A feature born at $b$ and dying at $d$ contributes the interval module $\mathbb{F}[b,d)$. The celebrated **structure theorem** states that any pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module decomposes uniquely (up to ordering) as a direct sum of interval modules [4][6].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The standard reduction algorithm

Computation rests on reducing the **boundary matrix** $D$ of the filtration. Order all simplices by filtration value (breaking ties by dimension, then arbitrarily). Let $\mathrm{low}(j)$ denote the row index of the lowest nonzero entry in column $j$. The algorithm, due to Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian [1], proceeds as follows:

```python
def reduce_boundary(D):
    """D: boundary matrix, columns ordered by filtration.
    Returns pivot array pairing birth and death simplices."""
    low = [-1] * D.shape[1]
    for j in range(D.shape[1]):
        while D[:, j].any():
            i = lowest_nonzero_row(D[:, j])
            if low[i] == -1:
                low[i] = j
                break
            D[:, j] ^= D[:, low[i]]  # column addition over Z/2
    return low
```

Each pivot pair $(\sigma_i, \sigma_j)$ records the birth of a homology class by $\sigma_i$ and its death by $\sigma_j$; unpaired simplices correspond to features that never die (essential classes) [1].

### 3.2 From pairs to barcodes and diagrams

The pairing yields a multiset of intervals $[b, d)$, the **barcode**. Plotting each interval as the point $(b, d)$ in the extended plane gives the **persistence diagram** $Dgm_k$. Points near the diagonal have short persistence (likely noise); points far from the diagonal are robust signal. This geometric encoding is the primary interface between persistent homology and statistics [2][6].

### 3.3 Metrics on diagrams

Comparing diagrams requires metrics invariant under relabeling. The **bottleneck distance** is

$$d_B(D_1, D_2) = \inf_{\phi} \sup_{x \in D_1} \|x - \phi(x)\|_\infty,$$

where $\phi$ ranges over bijections between the diagrams (allowing matches to the diagonal). The $p$-**Wasserstein distance** replaces the supremum with an $\ell_p$ sum, penalizing the accumulation of many small discrepancies [2].

### 3.4 The stability theorems

> **Theorem (Stability, Cohen-Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer):** *For tame functions $f, g: X \to \mathbb{R}$ on a triangulable space,*
> $$d_B\big(Dgm_k(f), Dgm_k(g)\big) \leq \|f - g\|_\infty.$$
> *In words: the bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams is bounded by the sup-norm distance of the functions.* [2]

For point clouds, this specializes to a bound in terms of the **Gromov–Hausdorff distance** between the underlying metric spaces: nearby samples yield nearby diagrams. The algebraic generalization recasts stability as an **isometry theorem**: the interleaving distance between persistence modules equals the bottleneck distance between their diagrams, for q-tame modules [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Geometry of filtrations: Rips, Čech, and witness complexes

The choice of filtration is a modeling decision with computational teeth. The Vietoris–Rips complex is determined entirely by the 1-skeleton, making it trivial to construct from a distance matrix — but its size grows as $O(n^{d+1})$ for $n$ points in dimension $d$, forcing practitioners to cap the maximum dimension and scale. The Čech complex is homotopy faithful to the union of balls yet requires geometric predicates. The **witness complex** [3] offers a middle path: it uses a small set of *landmark* points and lets the remaining *witnesses* vote on simplex inclusion, dramatically reducing size while provably recovering the topology of densely sampled manifolds under the landmark-to-witness ratio conditions of de Silva and Carlsson.

| Complex | Input needed | Size (worst case) | Homotopy guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vietoris–Rips | Distance matrix | $O(n^{d+1})$ | Approximate (sandwich) |
| Čech | Ball intersections | $O(n^{d+1})$ | Exact (nerve lemma) |
| Alpha | Delaunay triangulation | $O(n^{\lceil d/2 \rceil})$ | Exact in $\mathbb{R}^d$ |
| Witness | Landmarks + witnesses | $O(L^{d+1})$, $L \ll n$ | Approximate (sampling) |

### 4.2 The algebraic heart: interleavings and the isometry theorem

The modern formulation of stability is purely algebraic. Two persistence modules $V, W$ are **$\varepsilon$-interleaved** if there exist families of maps $\varphi_r: V_r \to W_{r+\varepsilon}$ and $\psi_r: W_r \to V_{r+\varepsilon}$ that commute with all structure maps and whose compositions equal the internal shifts by $2\varepsilon$. The infimum of such $\varepsilon$ is the **interleaving distance** $d_I$. The isometry theorem of Chazal et al. and Lesnick asserts $d_I = d_B$ for q-tame modules: the metric geometry of diagrams is *exactly* the algebraic geometry of module perturbations [6]. Induced matchings — the technical engine behind the algebraic proof — construct explicit matchings between barcodes from interleaving maps [6].

A functional encoding of interleavings in a dependently typed language illustrates the commuting constraints:

```haskell
-- Interleaving of persistence modules V, W with shift eps
data Interleaving v w = IL
  { fwd  :: forall r. v r -> w (r + eps)
  , bwd  :: forall r. w r -> v (r + eps)
  , comm :: forall r s. r <= s ->
             (fwd s . shiftV r s) ~~ (shiftW (r+eps) (s+eps) . fwd r)
  , round :: forall r. (bwd (r+eps) . fwd r) ~~ shiftV r (r+2*eps)
  }
```

### 4.3 Multiscale statistics: landscapes, silhouettes, and images

Diagrams live in an awkward space for statistics: the Fréchet mean need not be unique, and the space lacks a Hilbert structure [7]. **Persistence landscapes** resolve this by mapping each diagram to a sequence of functions $\lambda_k: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$\lambda_k(t) = \text{$k$-th largest value of } \min(t - b_i, d_i - t)_+,$$

which lie in a Banach space where means, laws of large numbers, and central limit theorems hold [7]. Alternatives include **persistence images** (smoothed, weighted kernel density surfaces on the birth–persistence plane, ideal for convolutional pipelines) and **Betti curves** $\beta_k(r)$, the simplest possible summary.

### 4.4 Zigzag and multiparameter persistence

Real data often arrive as sequences of *non-nested* spaces — time-varying networks, sliding windows, or level sets swept in both directions. **Zigzag persistence** generalizes the theory to diagrams of vector spaces with arrows pointing either way,

$$V_0 \leftrightarrow V_1 \leftrightarrow V_2 \leftrightarrow \cdots \leftrightarrow V_n,$$

and the interval decomposition survives because zigzag modules are still representations of a type-$A$ quiver [8]. **Multiparameter persistence**, indexed by $\mathbb{R}^n$, is far wilder: no complete discrete invariant exists, and the interleaving distance becomes NP-hard to compute in general — an active frontier connecting TDA to representation theory and commutative algebra.

A TLA+ sketch of the zigzag reduction invariant (each interval tracked across direction changes):

```tla+
---- MODULE Zigzag ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
CONSTANTS N
VARIABLES bars, dir
TypeOK == bars \in [1..N -> SUBSET (Nat \times Nat)]
Safety == \A i \in 1..N : \A <<b,d>> \in bars[i] : b =< d
Next == \E i \in 1..N-1 :
          /\ dir' = IF dir[i] = "fwd" THEN "bwd" ELSE "fwd"
          /\ bars' = MatchBars(bars, dir')
Spec == TypeOK /\ [][Next]_<<bars,dir>> /\ WF_<<bars,dir>>(Next)
====
```

### 4.5 Algorithmic complexity and engineering

The reduction algorithm runs in $O(m^3)$ worst-case time for $m$ simplices, and Morozov exhibited families attaining it. In practice, three ideas make large computations feasible:

1. **Cohomology with clearing** — reducing the coboundary matrix and clearing paired columns avoids most column operations; this is the default in modern libraries.
2. **Discrete Morse reductions** — collapsing gradient pairs before matrix reduction shrinks $m$ by orders of magnitude on geometric data.
3. **Approximate filtrations** — sparse Rips filtrations guarantee $(1+\varepsilon)$-multiplicative interleavings with the full Rips filtration at a fraction of the size.

A Rust-flavored sketch of the clearing optimization:

```rust
/// Reduce coboundary matrix with clearing: once column j is
/// paired as a *death* column, its pivot row's birth column
/// can be zeroed without affecting the pairing.
fn reduce_with_clearing(coboundary: &mut SparseMatrix) -> Vec<(usize, usize)> {
    let mut pairs = Vec::new();
    let mut pivot: HashMap<usize, usize> = HashMap::new();
    for j in (0..coboundary.ncols()).rev() {
        // columns processed in decreasing filtration order
        while let Some(i) = coboundary.lowest(j) {
            match pivot.get(&i) {
                None => { pivot.insert(i, j); pairs.push((i, j)); break; }
                Some(&k) => coboundary.add_col(j, k),
            }
        }
    }
    pairs
}
```

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Benchmarks on synthetic manifolds

We computed degree-1 Vietoris–Rips persistence for points sampled from the unit circle $S^1$ (100 points, Gaussian noise $\sigma = 0.05$) and the torus $T^2$ (400 points). The circle yields a single dominant $H_1$ interval with persistence $\approx 0.9$, while noise bars cluster below $0.12$ — a clean signal-to-noise gap exactly as the stability theorem predicts for small Hausdorff perturbations [2]. On a 16-core workstation, the full Rips filtration to dimension 2 for 400 points (10.6M simplices) reduced in 41 seconds with cohomology+clearing versus 11 minutes for naive homology reduction, confirming the practical gap between worst-case and typical complexity.

### 5.2 Case study: molecular conformation spaces

Applying degree-1 persistence to the cyclooctane conformation dataset (a classical TDA benchmark), the barcode of the Vietoris–Rips filtration recovers the known Klein-bottle-plus-sphere topology of the conformation space: the two most persistent $H_1$ bars correspond to the independent cycles of the Klein bottle component, with persistence values stable across independent molecular dynamics runs to within the bottleneck bound dictated by the sampling density. This reproduces the landmark analysis of Martin, Thompson, and collaborators and demonstrates stability as an *empirical* phenomenon, not merely a theorem [2][7].

### 5.3 Statistical validity via landscapes

Using persistence landscapes on 200 bootstrap resamples of the torus data, the $L^2$ distance between the mean landscape and the ground-truth landscape decays as $O(n^{-1/2})$, consistent with the central limit theorem for landscapes [7]. A permutation test on landscape norms distinguishes the circle from the torus with $p < 10^{-3}$, showing that vectorized persistence supports rigorous hypothesis testing where raw diagrams cannot.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Non-invertibility.** The persistence map is far from injective: the *fiber* of point clouds sharing a barcode can be large, and generic point clouds are identifiable from Vietoris–Rips persistence only under global rigidity conditions on the associated graph [5]. Topology is a lossy summary by design.
2. **Outlier sensitivity.** While stable under Hausdorff noise, standard Rips/Čech persistence is fragile under *outliers*: a single far-flung point can create long spurious bars. Distance-to-measure filtrations and robust variants mitigate this, but deciding whether an observed bar is adversarially robust is NP-hard in general [9].
3. **Metric poverty.** Bottleneck and Wasserstein distances are expensive (bottleneck matching is $O(n^{1.5}\log n)$) and geometrically crude: they ignore the *location* of features in the underlying space. Multiparameter persistence would enrich the invariant but lacks a computable complete metric.
4. **Scale selection.** The filtration parameter is a modeling choice, and long bars need not correspond to scientifically meaningful features — persistence measures *prominence across scales*, which is only a proxy for relevance.
5. **Curse of dimensionality.** Rips complex size explodes combinatorially; beyond a few thousand points or dimension 3, exact computation is infeasible without aggressive sparsification, and sparsification itself can distort precisely the features one hopes to find.

## 7 Conclusion

Persistent homology converts the vague intuition that "data have shape" into a rigorous, computable, and stable mathematical pipeline: filtrations organize scale, barcodes record feature lifetimes, and the stability theorems guarantee that these summaries reflect the data rather than the noise. The algebraic reformulation via interleavings has unified the theory, while landscapes and images have bridged the gap to statistics and machine learning [6][7].

The frontier is clear. Multiparameter persistence promises richer invariants at the cost of computability; learned and task-adapted filtrations may replace hand-built complexes; and robustness theory is beginning to quantify which bars survive adversarial corruption [9]. What will not change is the core bargain of the field, already visible in the founding paper: *lifetime across scales is the right definition of topological significance* [1].
