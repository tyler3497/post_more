# Certified Adversarial Robustness of Deep Classifiers: Tight ℓ₂ Guarantees via Randomized Smoothing, Deterministic Lipschitz Verification, Branch-and-Bound Completeness, and the Accuracy–Robustness Pareto Frontier at ImageNet Scale

## Abstract

Adversarial perturbations — imperceptible to humans yet catastrophic for deep classifiers — demand guarantees stronger than empirical robustness: *provable certificates* that no perturbation within a norm ball can alter a prediction. This treatise unifies the three dominant certification paradigms.

---

## 1. Introduction

The existence of adversarial examples — inputs perturbed by imperceptible noise that flip a classifier's prediction — was established in the early 2010s and has since matured from a curiosity into a central obstacle for deploying neural networks in safety-critical settings.

```
∀ δ with ‖δ‖_p ≤ ε:   f(x + δ) = f(x)
```

or decline to answer (abstain). A method is *sound* if every certificate it emits is valid; it is *complete* if it never abstains on a truly robust input. The literature splits into two camps.

This thesis develops all three strands with mathematical precision:

1. The **tight ℓ₂ certificate** of randomized smoothing and its Monte Carlo certification algorithm.
2. **Deterministic incomplete verification**: Lipschitz margins, interval-bound propagation, and CROWN-style linear relaxations.
3. **Complete branch-and-bound verification**: the β-CROWN bound propagation scheme and its extension to general nonlinearities.
4. The **accuracy–robustness Pareto frontier** — what certificates actually cost in clean accuracy at ImageNet scale, and how training moves the frontier.

We treat each strand as both theory and engineering, because certified robustness is unusual in machine learning: its claims are *theorems*, and its benchmarks are *proofs that ran*.

---

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The verification problem

Let f: ℝᵈ → 𝒴 be a trained classifier and fix a norm ‖·‖_p and radius ε > 0. The **local robustness property** at input x with label c = f(x) is:

> **Definition (local robustness).** f is (ε, p)-robust at x iff argmax f(x′) = c for every x′ with ‖x′ − x‖_p ≤ ε.

Two metrics organize the empirical literature:

- **Empirical (attack) accuracy**: fraction of test points on which a strong attack (e.g., PGD) fails to find a misclassified perturbation.
- **Certified accuracy**: fraction of test points on which the verifier *proves* robustness, i.e., the certificate radius exceeds ε.

Certified accuracy is always a lower bound on true robust accuracy, which is in turn a lower bound on attack accuracy — a chain of inequalities every certified-defense paper must respect.

### 2.2 Why certification is hard

A feedforward ReLU network is a piecewise-linear function whose linear regions grow exponentially in depth. To prove robustness at x, one must bound the network's output over an entire ε-ball — an *infinite* set — using only finitely many computations.

### 2.3 The three paradigms at a glance

| Paradigm | Sound? | Complete? | Scales to ImageNet? | Norm flexibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized smoothing [1] | Yes (w.h.p.) | No (abstains) | **Yes** | Primarily ℓ₂ |
| Lipschitz / IBP bounds | Yes | No | Yes | ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞ |
| Convex relaxations (LP/SDP) | Yes | No | Partially | ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞ |
| Branch-and-bound (α,β-CROWN) [4] | Yes | **Yes** | No (small/medium nets) | ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞ |

> **Theorem (informal, field consensus):** No known polynomial-time verifier is both complete and tight on large networks; every practical method sits somewhere on the soundness–completeness–scalability trilemma. The contribution of the last decade has been to push the Pareto surface of this trilemma outward.

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis proceeds along three tracks:

1. **Formal derivation.** We restate the tight smoothing certificate with its Neyman–Pearson proof sketch, the Lipschitz-margin argument, the ReLU convex-relaxation hierarchy, and the branch-and-bound correctness invariant, so that each claim can be checked against the primary sources [1][3][4][5].
2. **Algorithmic specification.** We present the Monte Carlo certification procedure (`Certify`), a Haskell model of bound propagation as an abstract interpretation, and a Rust-flavored interval-bound propagation kernel, together with a TLA+ specification of the smoothing contract. These are faithful simplifications, not production code.
3. **Empirical protocol.** We report certified-accuracy numbers using the community's standard protocol: Monte Carlo estimation with Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals at failure probability α = 0.001, abstention when the lower confidence bound is inconclusive, and radius sweeps over σ ∈ {0.25, 0.50, 1.00}. All figures cited are from the referenced works' published evaluations [1][2][4].

**Notation.** Φ denotes the standard normal CDF; σ the smoothing noise level; p_A the (lower-bounded) probability of the top class; p_B the (upper-bounded) probability of the runner-up; W⁽ᵏ⁾, b⁽ᵏ⁾ the weights and biases of layer k.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Randomized Smoothing and the Tight ℓ₂ Certificate

Randomized smoothing converts *any* base classifier f into a **smoothed classifier** g by majority vote under isotropic Gaussian noise:

```
g(x) = argmax_{c ∈ 𝒴}  P_{δ ∼ 𝒩(0, σ²I)} [ f(x + δ) = c ]
```

The noise level σ controls the tradeoff: larger σ yields larger certified radii but degrades clean accuracy, because the base classifier must classify *noisy* inputs correctly.

> **Theorem (Cohen et al., 2019 — tight ℓ₂ certificate):** Suppose that for input x, the smoothed classifier's top class c_A satisfies P(f(x+δ) = c_A) ≥ p_A and every other class c satisfies P(f(x+δ) = c) ≤ p_B, with p_A > p_B. Then g(x + δ′) = c_A for all perturbations with ‖δ′‖₂ < R, where
>
> ```
> R = (σ / 2) · ( Φ⁻¹(p_A) − Φ⁻¹(p_B) )
> ```
>
> Moreover, this radius is **tight**: no larger radius can be certified from (p_A, p_B, σ) alone. When p_A > 1/2 one may take p_B = 1 − p_A, giving the simplified form R = σ · Φ⁻¹(p_A).

*Proof sketch.* The Neyman–Pearson lemma characterizes the worst-case classifier consistent with the observed class probabilities: the adversary's optimal strategy is a likelihood-ratio test, i.e., a halfspace in the Gaussian geometry.

Three remarks give this theorem its force. First, it is **classifier-agnostic**: f may be any function, including a deep network, a decision tree, or a human — the certificate depends only on measurable probabilities, never on f's architecture.

The certification algorithm is a two-stage Monte Carlo procedure:

```python
def certify(f, x, sigma, n0=100, n=100_000, alpha=0.001):
    """Return (predicted_class, certified_radius) or ABSTAIN."""
    # Stage 1: identify the likely top class with n0 samples
    counts0 = Counter(f(x + randn(d) * sigma) for _ in range(n0))
    c_hat = counts0.most_common(1)[0][0]
    # Stage 2: lower-bound P(f(x+delta) = c_hat) with n samples
    counts = sum(1 for _ in range(n) if f(x + randn(d) * sigma) == c_hat)
    pA_lower = clopper_pearson_lower(counts, n, alpha)
    if pA_lower > 0.5:
        radius = sigma * norm_ppf(pA_lower)   # tight certificate
        return c_hat, radius
    return ABSTAIN
```

With n = 100,000 samples per input, Cohen et al. certified a ResNet-50 on ImageNet at **49% top-1 accuracy under ℓ₂ radius 0.5** (= 127/255 per-pixel) — the first non-vacuous certificate on ImageNet, where every deterministic verifier fails to scale [1].

### 4.2 Deterministic Lipschitz Bounds and Interval Propagation

Deterministic methods certify the *base* network directly, with no sampling and no failure probability. The oldest idea is the **Lipschitz margin**: if f's logit-difference function h(x) = f_{c_A}(x) − max_{c≠c_A} f_c(x) is L-Lipschitz in the ℓ₂ norm, then robustness holds within radius h(x)/(2L).

**Interval Bound Propagation (IBP)** takes the opposite approach: propagate axis-aligned boxes [l⁽ᵏ⁾, u⁽ᵏ⁾] through each layer. For an affine layer the bounds transform exactly; for a ReLU, [l, u] maps to [max(0, l), max(0, u)].

**CROWN and DeepPoly** occupy the middle ground: instead of intervals, propagate *linear* upper and lower bounds per neuron, z ↦ αz + β, choosing the bounding lines adaptively to minimize volume.

```
x ≥ 0,   x ≥ z,   x ≤ (u / (u − l)) · (z − l),
```

the convex hull of the ReLU graph over [l, u]. CROWN (Zhang et al., 2018) back-substitutes these linear bounds through the network to obtain a certified lower bound on the logit margin; DeepPoly and DeepZ use the same geometry in forward mode with zonotopes.

> **Theorem (Convex relaxation barrier — Salman et al., 2019):** All common single-neuron convex relaxations of ReLU networks — interval bounds, the triangle relaxation, and their duals — are unified in one framework, and the *optimal* per-neuron relaxation still leaves a strictly positive gap to the true robust margin on natural networks. Closing the gap requires **multi-neuron relaxations** that capture joint constraints across neurons, at sharply higher computational cost.

In other words, incomplete verifiers cannot be made arbitrarily tight by cleverer per-neuron bounds; the barrier is structural. The only way past it is to stop relaxing and start *splitting* — which leads to complete verification.

A compact Haskell model captures bound propagation as abstract interpretation over a lattice of interval-linear bounds:

```haskell
-- Bounds as pairs of affine functions of the input
data Bound = Bound { lower :: Affine, upper :: Affine }

-- ReLU relaxation: tight triangle when unstable
reluBound :: (Double, Double) -> Bound -> Bound
reluBound (l, u) b
  | u <= 0    = Bound (const 0) (const 0)          -- stably inactive
  | l >= 0    = b                                 -- stably active
  | otherwise = Bound lowerLin upperLin            -- unstable: triangle
  where
    lowerLin = scale alpha . lower $ b             -- adaptive slope α ∈ [0,1]
    upperLin x = (u / (u - l)) * (eval (lower b) x - l)
    alpha = if -l > u then 0 else 1               -- CROWN heuristic
```

The parameter α is exactly what α-CROWN later learns by gradient ascent [4] — a one-line heuristic elevated into an optimization problem.

### 4.3 Branch-and-Bound Complete Verifiers: from LP-BaB to α,β-CROWN

A **complete verifier** must decide every instance: either produce a proof of robustness or exhibit a counterexample. For ReLU networks, the classical route encodes each ReLU x = max(0, z) with a binary variable in a mixed-integer linear program (Tjeng et al., 2017).

The bottleneck is the bounding subroutine. LP-based BaB solves a linear program per node: tight but far too slow on GPUs. **β-CROWN** (Wang et al., 2021) [4] replaces the LP with bound propagation that *natively encodes split constraints*.

> **Theorem (β-CROWN soundness and dominance):** For any fixed set of neuron splits, the β-CROWN bound — obtained by maximizing over β ≥ 0 and the CROWN α slopes — is sound, is at least as tight as the LP relaxation with the same splits at the optimum, and is computed by GPU-parallel bound propagation in time comparable to a single CROWN pass.

Empirically, β-CROWN with BaB is up to **three orders of magnitude faster** than LP-based BaB at equal timeout rates, and on an adversarially trained MNIST network it shrank the gap between verified accuracy and attack accuracy from 35% (SDP-based) to 12% while running 47× faster [4].

The framework has since been generalized.

The core BaB loop, stripped to its invariant, is:

```
BaB(domain D, property P):
    bound ← IncompleteBound(D)          # sound over-approximation
    if bound proves P on D:  return VERIFIED
    if SampleAttack finds x ∈ D violating P:  return FALSIFIED with x
    pick unstable neuron n in D         # branching heuristic
    return BaB(D ∩ {z_n ≤ 0}, P) ∧ BaB(D ∩ {z_n ≥ 0}, P)
```

Soundness follows from the incomplete bound; completeness from finiteness of the split tree and the attack's eventual discovery of counterexamples in falsifiable leaves. The art — and the entire research program — lies in the branching heuristic and the bounding subroutine.

### 4.4 The Accuracy–Robustness Pareto Frontier at ImageNet Scale

Certification is not free. Every known method trades clean accuracy for certified radius, tracing a **Pareto frontier** that no current technique crosses. The tradeoff has both theoretical and empirical faces.

**Theory: is the tradeoff intrinsic?** Tsipras et al. [6] constructed a simple distribution where any classifier with high standard accuracy provably has small adversarial accuracy — an *inherent* tension arising because robust classification requires ignoring predictive but brittle features.

**Practice: moving the frontier.** The frontier is navigated by two knobs: the smoothing noise σ and the training procedure.

The quantitative picture at ImageNet scale (ResNet-50, certified top-1 accuracy):

| Method | σ | Certified acc. @ r = 0.5 | Certified acc. @ r = 1.0 | Clean acc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian augmentation [1] | 0.25 | ~45% | ~25% | ~65% |
| Gaussian augmentation [1] | 0.50 | **49%** | ~35% | ~57% |
| Gaussian augmentation [1] | 1.00 | ~40% | ~40% | ~44% |
| SmoothAdv adversarial training [2] | 0.50 | ~55% | ~42% | ~60% |
| Deterministic IBP / CROWN training | — | ~0% (vacuous) | ~0% | — |

*(Figures are representative of the published evaluations in [1][2]; exact numbers vary with architecture and sample budget.)*

Two patterns stand out. First, **σ tunes along the frontier but does not shift it**: larger σ buys radius at the direct expense of clean accuracy. Second, **better training shifts the frontier outward**: SmoothAdv dominates Gaussian augmentation at every radius.

## 5. Empirical Evidence and Formal Results

We consolidate the headline quantitative claims, each traceable to its source:

1. **Tightness of the ℓ₂ certificate.** Cohen et al. prove that no certificate derived solely from (p_A, p_B, σ) can exceed R = σ/2·(Φ⁻¹(p_A) − Φ⁻¹(p_B)); the Neyman–Pearson halfspace construction attains it [1]. This is a rare *optimality* result in a field accustomed to inequalities with slack.
2. **ImageNet feasibility.** 49% certified top-1 accuracy at ℓ₂ radius 0.5 on ResNet-50 with σ = 0.5, n = 100,000, α = 0.001 — the first non-vacuous ImageNet certificate [1]. No deterministic verifier has produced a comparable ImageNet result.
3. **SmoothAdv dominance.** Adversarial training of the smoothed classifier beats every prior provable ℓ₂ defense on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, with additional gains from pre-training and semi-supervised data [2].
4. **The convex barrier.** The optimal single-neuron convex relaxation provably cannot close the verification gap; multi-neuron relaxations are necessary and expensive [3].
5. **β-CROWN acceleration.** Up to 1000× speedup over LP-based branch-and-bound; verified-vs-attack accuracy gap on MNIST (ε = 0.3) reduced from 35% to 12% at 47× less runtime than SDP verification [4]. VNN-COMP 2021 winner; GenBaB extension to general nonlinearities won VNN-COMP 2023 and 2024 [5].
6. **The tradeoff's theoretical status.** An inherent accuracy–robustness tension is provable in a stylized setting [6]; whether it binds on real image distributions remains contested.

A TLA+ specification of the smoothing contract makes the probabilistic guarantee precise at the systems level:

```tla
---------------------------- MODULE SmoothingCert -----------------------------
EXTENDS Reals, TLC
CONSTANTS Sigma, Alpha, Radius
VARIABLES pA, pB, certificate

TypeOK == /\ pA \in 0..1 /\ pB \in 0..1 /\ pA > pB
Soundness ==
    /\ TypeOK
    /\ certificate = (Sigma / 2) * (InvPhi(pA) - InvPhi(pB))
    => \A d \in Ball(Radius) : Classify(x + d) = Classify(x)
(* InvPhi is the standard normal quantile; Ball(Radius) the l2 ball. *)
(* Monte Carlo estimation refines pA, pB with Clopper-Pearson intervals   *)
(* at confidence 1 - Alpha; abstention is modeled as stuttering.         *)
=============================================================================
```

And a Rust-flavored sketch of the IBP kernel shows why deterministic certification is cheap enough to run inside the training loop:

```rust
/// Interval bound propagation through one affine + ReLU layer.
fn ibp_layer(w: &Matrix, b: &Vector, l: &Vector, u: &Vector) -> (Vector, Vector) {
    let (zl, zu) = affine_bounds(w, b, l, u);   // exact for affine maps
    let l2: Vector = zl.iter().map(|&v| v.max(0.0)).collect();
    let u2: Vector = zu.iter().map(|&v| v.max(0.0)).collect();
    (l2, u2) // sound over-approximation; wrapping error compounds per layer
}
```

## 6. Limitations

**ℓ∞ and the limits of Gaussian smoothing.** The tight certificate is an ℓ₂ result, and this is no accident: Gaussian noise is rotationally symmetric, and the Neyman–Pearson argument exploits exactly that symmetry.

**The cost of completeness.** Branch-and-bound is complete but worst-case exponential; in practice it verifies networks with thousands — not hundreds of millions — of neurons. The ImageNet-scale frontier belongs entirely to smoothing and (vacuous) IBP.

**Sampling cost and abstention.** Certifying a single ImageNet input at α = 0.001 consumes ~10⁵ forward passes — minutes on a GPU per image, hours for a test set.

**The relaxation gap persists.** Even β-CROWN leaves a verified-vs-attack accuracy gap (12% on MNIST at ε = 0.3) [4]; the convex barrier [3] explains why cheap methods cannot close it and BaB can only close it by paying exponential search.

**Distribution shift and adaptive adversaries.** Certificates are statements about the *fixed* classifier on the *fixed* input distribution.

**The tradeoff debate.** Whether the accuracy–robustness Pareto frontier reflects a law of nature [6] or an artifact of current training is unresolved.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Certified adversarial robustness has grown from an impossibility-tinged curiosity into a discipline with theorems, competitions, and ImageNet-scale results.

The through-line is a trilemma: **soundness, completeness, scalability — pick two cheaply.** Smoothing picks soundness and scalability; BaB picks soundness and completeness; IBP picks scalability and hopes.

The most promising direction is their convergence: certified training that co-designs the network for the verifier, smoothing distributions matched to threat models beyond ℓ₂, and branch-and-bound heuristics learned rather than hand-crafted.
