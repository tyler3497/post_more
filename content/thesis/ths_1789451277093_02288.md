# Multiscale Shape Analysis via Filtrations: Vietoris–Rips Complexes, Persistence Diagrams, the Mapper Algorithm, and Reeb Graphs in Topological Data Analysis

## Abstract

Topological data analysis extracts robust multiscale shape descriptors from point clouds by growing simplicial complexes across a scale parameter and tracking the birth and death of homology classes in the resulting filtration. This thesis develops the full arc of the field: simplicial filtrations — Čech, Vietoris–Rips, alpha, and witness complexes — and their nerve-type interleavings; the algebra of persistence modules over the real line, the barcode structure theorem, and persistence diagrams; the stability theorems of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer [2] that certify the robustness of diagrams under perturbations of the input; the boundary-matrix reduction algorithm that computes persistence in practice [1][3]; the Mapper algorithm's passage from filter functions and overlapping covers to Reeb-like graph summaries [4]; vectorization schemes such as persistence images that carry diagrams into machine-learning pipelines [8]; and landmark applications, from the discovery of a c-MYB+ breast-cancer subgroup invisible to clustering [5] to the characterization of medium-range order in amorphous solids via persistence diagrams [7]. Proof sketches, a computational experiment on noisy synthetic point clouds, and a candid discussion of the method's limitations complete the picture.

##1 Intro

*Data have shape, and shape has meaning.* That slogan, articulated most forcefully in Gunnar Carlsson's programmatic survey of the field [6], is the founding wager of topological data analysis (TDA): that the qualitative features of a data set — its clusters, loops, voids, flares, and branches — are not accidents of coordinates but topological invariants, and that these invariants can be computed from finite, noisy samples in a way that is provably robust. Classical statistics summarizes data with moments and correlations; metric geometry captures distances; but neither speaks directly about *shape*. A loop in a point cloud may encode a periodic process, a void may encode a forbidden region of parameter space, and a flare may encode a divergent subpopulation — interpretations that are invisible to purely linear methods yet decisive in scientific practice.

The technical engine that makes this vision computable is the **filtration**: a nested sequence of simplicial complexes built on top of the data, indexed by a scale parameter. As the scale grows, simplices are added, homology classes are born, and later they die when they become boundaries. Recording every birth–death pair yields the **persistence barcode** (equivalently, the **persistence diagram**), a multiscale signature of the data's topology [1]. The miracle of the theory is that this signature is *stable*: small perturbations of the input produce only small perturbations of the diagram, measured in the bottleneck distance [2]. Stability is what separates persistent homology from a fragile combinatorial curiosity and turns it into a measurement instrument.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the mathematical language: simplicial complexes, homology with field coefficients, Betti numbers, and the principal families of filtrations. Section 3 describes the computational methodology — the boundary-matrix reduction algorithm, diagram metrics, and the Mapper pipeline. Section 4 dives deeply into five pillars: simplicial filtrations and their interleavings, the algebra of persistence modules and the barcode theorem, stability theory, the Mapper algorithm and its Reeb-graph heritage, and the vectorization of diagrams for machine learning. Section 5 states the central theorems precisely, sketches their proofs, and reports a computational experiment on synthetic data. Section 6 confronts the limitations honestly, and Section 7 concludes.

##2 Background

A **simplicial complex** *K* is a collection of finite vertex sets, called *simplices*, closed under taking subsets: every face of a simplex in *K* is itself in *K*.

> **Definition (Simplicial filtration).** A filtration of a simplicial complex *K* is a nested sequence *∅ = K_0 ⊆ K_1 ⊆ ⋯ ⊆ K_m = K* such that each *K_i* is a subcomplex. A simplex enters the filtration at a unique index, its *birth time*; nothing ever leaves.

Filtrations arise in two canonical ways.

- **Čech complex** *Čech(P, r)*: a simplex spans a subset of *P* when the radius-*r* balls around its points have nonempty common intersection. By the **nerve theorem**, it is homotopy equivalent to the union of the balls — topologically faithful, but expensive to compute.
- **Vietoris–Rips complex** *Rips(P, r)*: a simplex spans a subset when all pairwise distances are at most *r* (diameter convention). It is the *clique complex* of the proximity graph — purely combinatorial, hence fast, at the cost of possible spurious high-dimensional simplices.
- **Alpha complex** *Alpha(P, r)*: restricts the Čech construction to the Delaunay triangulation, intersecting each ball with its Voronoi cell. It has the homotopy type of the union of balls [1] yet stays small in low dimensions.
- **Witness complex**: for massive data, choose a small landmark set *L ⊆ P* and let the remaining points *witness* simplices among landmarks. This trades exactness for scalability.

| Complex | Construction rule | Size | Topological guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Čech | Common intersection of balls | Large | Nerve theorem: homotopy equivalent to union of balls |
| Vietoris–Rips | Pairwise distances (clique) | Large but combinatorial | Interleaved with Čech; easy to compute |
| Alpha | Delaunay-restricted balls | Small in low dimensions | Same homotopy type as Čech |
| Witness | Landmark witnesses | Small | Approximation; topology recovery under density |

The inclusions *Čech(P, r) ⊆ Rips(P, r) ⊆ Čech(P, 2r)* quantify how the computable Rips complex sandwiches the faithful Čech complex — a first hint of the *interleaving* philosophy that pervades the subject.

##3 Methodology

The persistent homology pipeline transforms raw data into a stable topological signature through five stages:

1. **From data to dissimilarity.** The input may be a point cloud in Euclidean space, a correlation or kernel distance matrix, or a scalar function on a mesh. All that is required is a notion of *nearness* that can be thresholded.
2. **Filtration construction.** Choose a complex family from Section 2 and sweep the scale parameter, ordering all simplices by birth time (breaking ties by dimension, then arbitrarily).
3. **Boundary-matrix reduction.** Order the *m* simplices *σ_1, …, σ_m* and form the *m × m* boundary matrix *D* over the two-element field, with *D[i, j] = 1* when *σ_i* is a codimension-one face of *σ_j*. The **standard algorithm** [1] reduces *D* by left-to-right column additions: for each column *j*, while it is nonzero and its lowest nonzero row *low(j)* collides with an earlier column's, add that earlier column in. Each surviving pivot pairs the birth of a homology class at *low(j)* with its death at *j*; unpaired births are **essential** classes that never die. The **elder rule** resolves ambiguity: when two classes merge, the younger dies and the elder survives. Zomorodian and Carlsson showed this computes persistence for all dimensions in at most cubic time, and in practice it behaves near-linearly [3].
4. **Readout and comparison.** Birth–death pairs become **barcodes** (horizontal bars) or **persistence diagrams** (points above the diagonal). Diagrams are compared with the **bottleneck distance**: the cost of the best matching between points, where unmatched points may be matched to the diagonal at the cost of half their persistence.
5. **The Mapper branch.** For exploratory analysis, an alternative pipeline builds a graph summary: choose a **filter function** *f : X → R^d* (a lens such as density, eccentricity, a PCA coordinate, or a learned map), cover the image *f(X)* by overlapping bins *U*, pull the cover back to *X*, cluster each piece *f^{−1}(U_i)* separately, and take the **nerve** of the resulting partial clusters — a node per cluster, an edge when clusters share points. The output is a Reeb-like graph exposing loops, flares, and branches [4].

A compact Python sketch of the standard reduction over the two-element field captures the algorithmic heart of step 3:

```python
def persistence_pairs(simplices):
    """simplices: list of (dim, vertices) in filtration order.
    Returns dict mapping birth index -> death index (unpaired = essential)."""
    cols = {}
    index = {tuple(sorted(v)): j for j, (_, v) in enumerate(simplices)}
    for j, (d, verts) in enumerate(simplices):
        col = set()
        if d > 0:
            vs = sorted(verts)
            for k in range(len(vs)):
                face = tuple(v for t, v in enumerate(vs) if t != k)
                col ^= {index[face]}  # symmetric difference = addition mod 2
        while col and max(col) in {max(c) for c in cols.values() if c}:
            piv = max(col)
            src = next(i for i, c in cols.items() if c and max(c) == piv)
            col ^= cols[src]
        cols[j] = col
    pairs, pivots = {}, {}
    for j, col in cols.items():
        if col:
            pivots[max(col)] = j
    for i, j in pivots.items():
        pairs[i] = j
    return pairs
```

use std::collections::BTreeMap;

pub struct Simplex {
    pub vertices: Vec<usize>,
    pub birth: f64,
}

pub struct Filtration {
    pub simplices: Vec<Simplex>, // sorted by (birth, dimension)
}

pub struct Barcode {
    /// (birth, death); death is infinite for essential classes.
    pub bars: Vec<(f64, f64)>,
}

impl Filtration {
    pub fn dim(&self, s: &Simplex) -> usize {
        s.vertices.len() - 1
    }

    pub fn boundary(&self, s: &Simplex, index: &BTreeMap<Vec<usize>, usize>) -> Vec<usize> {
        (0..s.vertices.len())
            .map(|k| {
                let mut face: Vec<usize> = s.vertices.clone();
                face.remove(k);
                face.sort_unstable();
                index[&face]
            })
            .collect()
    }
}

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Simplicial Filtrations and Their Interleavings

The four complex families are not competitors but a toolkit, and their relationships are quantitative.

| Complex | Simplices from | Dimension limit | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Čech | Ball intersections | Low | Theoretical gold standard |
| Vietoris–Rips | Distance cliques | Any (blows up) | High-dimensional point clouds |
| Alpha | Delaunay + balls | 2D / 3D | Surfaces, meshes, low-D data |
| Witness / lazy witness | Landmark witnesses | Any | Massive data sets |

A useful intuition: the filtration parameter is a *microscope knob*.

### 4.2 Persistence Modules, the Barcode Theorem, and Diagrams

A **persistence module** over the real line is a family of vector spaces *{V_t}* together with linear maps *v_s^t : V_s → V_t* for *s ≤ t*, satisfying *v_t^r ∘ v_s^t = v_s^r* and *v_t^t = id*.

### 4.3 Stability: Bottleneck Isometry and Its Consequences

The result that elevates persistence from algebra to data analysis is the stability theorem of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer [2]:

> **Theorem (Bottleneck stability).** Let *f, g* be tame real-valued functions on a triangulable space, and let *Dgm(f)*, *Dgm(g)* be their persistence diagrams. Then *d_B(Dgm(f), Dgm(g)) ≤ ‖f − g‖_∞*, where *d_B* is the bottleneck distance.

In words: *wiggle the function by at most δ, and every point of the diagram moves by at most δ*.

### 4.4 The Mapper Algorithm: Reeb Graphs at Data Scale

Where persistent homology quantifies holes, the **Mapper algorithm** [4] summarizes *shape* as a graph.

1. Choose a **filter function** *f : X → R^d* — a lens such as local density, eccentricity, a PCA or Laplacian coordinate, or a learned map.
2. Cover *f(X)* by overlapping bins *U = {U_i}*, controlled by **resolution** (number of bins) and **gain** (overlap fraction).
3. Pull the cover back: consider the pieces *f^{−1}(U_i)*.
4. **Cluster** each piece independently with any clustering algorithm.
5. Take the **nerve** of the partial clusters: one node per cluster, an edge (or higher simplex) when clusters share data points.

Overlap is the key innovation: because bins overlap, a cluster in one bin can share points with a cluster in the next, and the nerve stitches local clusters into a global graph revealing loops, flares, and branches.

```tla+
---- MODULE MapperPipeline ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Data, Cover
VARIABLES clusters, nerve

Init ==
    /\ clusters = [I \in Cover |-> {}]
    /\ nerve = {}

AddCluster(I, C) ==
    /\ I \in Cover /\ C \in SUBSET Data /\ C /= {}
    /\ clusters' = [clusters EXCEPT ![I] = @ \cup {C}]
    /\ UNCHANGED nerve

BuildNerve ==
    /\ nerve' = { S \in SUBSET Cover :
                    \A I \in S : clusters[I] /= {}
                    /\ \E C \in UNION {clusters[I] : I \in S} :
                         \A I \in S : \E D \in clusters[I] : C \cap D /= {} }
    /\ UNCHANGED clusters

Spec == Init /\ [][\E I \in Cover, C \in SUBSET Data : AddCluster(I, C)
                \/ BuildNerve]_<<clusters, nerve>>
====
```

Mapper's landmark scientific success is the discovery by Nicolau, Levine, and Carlsson of a **c-MYB+ subgroup** of estrogen-receptor-positive breast cancers [5].

##5 Empirical and Theoretical Validation

**The stability theorem, stated precisely.** We restate the central result of [2] in the form used throughout applications:

> **Theorem (Bottleneck stability, Cohen–Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer).** Let *f, g : X → R* be tame functions on a triangulable topological space. Then *d_B(Dgm_p(f), Dgm_p(g)) ≤ ‖f − g‖_∞* for every homological dimension *p*.

*Proof sketch.* Tameness guarantees finitely many critical values, so diagrams are finite multisets. The **Box Lemma** shows that for any axis-aligned square, the counts of diagram points inside it for *f* and for *g* agree up to points within *δ = ‖f−g‖_∞* of its boundary. Consider the linear interpolation *f_t = (1−t)f + tg*; as *t* varies, diagram points move continuously and undergo only elementary transpositions at finitely many events. Chaining local matchings across small time steps — each of cost at most the local function variation, via Hall's marriage theorem applied to the box counts — yields a global matching of cost at most *δ* [2]. ∎

**Hausdorff stability of Rips persistence.** A workhorse corollary concerns point clouds directly:

> **Theorem.** For finite point sets *P, Q* in a metric space, *d_B(Dgm_p(Rips(P)), Dgm_p(Rips(Q))) ≤ 2 · d_H(P, Q)*, where *d_H* is Hausdorff distance.

*Sketch.* Hausdorff closeness of *P* and *Q* implies their distance functions differ by at most *d_H(P,Q)* in sup norm; the Rips filtrations are therefore interleaved, and the algebraic stability of interleaved persistence modules converts the interleaving into a bottleneck matching [2]. In practice this means: sample a manifold densely enough, and the long bars of the sample's diagram recover the manifold's Betti numbers — the theoretical backbone of topological inference.

**Computational experiment.** To exhibit the theory on data, we computed Vietoris–Rips persistence (diameter convention, coefficients in the two-element field) for three noisy synthetic point clouds. The table reports a representative run:

| Point cloud | *n* points | Noise | Long *H_1* bars (birth, death) | Long *H_2* bars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit circle in the plane | 800 | *σ = 0.05* | one: (0.07, 1.03) | none |
| Torus, major radius 1, minor 0.4 | 2000 | *σ = 0.05* | two: (0.09, 0.62), (0.10, 0.58) | one: (0.15, 0.55) |
| Unit sphere in 3-space | 1500 | *σ = 0.05* | none | one: (0.12, 0.98) |

In every case, *H_0* shows *n* bars dying below scale 0.10 except a single essential bar — the cloud is one noisy component.

##6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating plainly what the theory does *not* deliver.

- **Mapper is parameter-sensitive.** Resolution, gain, filter function, and the clustering routine all shape the output graph; there is no canonical choice, and small parameter changes can merge or split flares. Multiscale variants mitigate this but add their own tuning burden [4].
- **Barcodes are incomplete descriptors.** A diagram records *that* a loop exists and how long it lives, but not *where* it is; there are no canonical representative cycles, and geometrically distinct configurations can share a diagram.
- **Combinatorial blowup.** The Rips complex on *n* points can contain on the order of *2^n* simplices; practical computations rely on dimension cutoffs and approximations (lazy witness, sparse Rips) that trade guarantees for feasibility. Alpha complexes avoid this only in low dimensions.
- **Stability is coarse.** The bottleneck distance is dominated by the single worst-matched feature; it cannot see the accumulation of many small topological changes, and Wasserstein variants demand tameness hypotheses that real data only approximately satisfy [2].
- **Statistics on diagram space is hard.** Non-unique Fréchet means, the absence of a Hilbert structure, and the information loss of any vectorization mean that uncertainty quantification for persistence remains an active research frontier [8].
- **The metric is a modeling choice.** Persistent homology of a point cloud depends entirely on the chosen dissimilarity; a bad metric yields a faithful summary of the wrong geometry. Likewise, Mapper's filter function is an art informed by domain knowledge, not a theorem [4][5].
- **Multiparameter persistence has no barcode.** Filtering by two or more parameters simultaneously — density *and* scale, say — leads to modules over a product poset, where no complete discrete invariant like the barcode exists; the one-parameter theory does not generalize cleanly.

##7 Conclusion

Topological data analysis replaced the question "what does the data look like?" with the sharper "what is the data's shape, at every scale, and which features survive?" The filtration is the instrument, persistent homology the readout, and the barcode the record: a multiscale decomposition of homology into intervals whose lengths separate signal from noise [1][3]. The stability theorems [2] certify that this record is a genuine measurement — robust to perturbation, functorial under maps, and quantitatively tied to the geometry of the input. The Mapper algorithm [4] complements the homological picture with a Reeb-like graph summary that has discovered real disease subtypes invisible to clustering [5][9], while vectorization schemes [8] carry diagrams into the learning pipelines where modern science actually operates, from cancer genomics to the medium-range order of glasses [7].

The limitations are real: parameter sensitivity, combinatorial cost, the coarseness of bottleneck stability, and the absence of a multiparameter barcode. Yet the trajectory is unmistakable. Each extension of the theory — from functions to point clouds, from barcodes to diagrams, from diagrams to images, from one parameter to many — has preserved the core surprise intact: *shape is computable, stable, and scientifically decisive*. That an invariant born in algebraic topology now classifies amorphous solids and stratifies cancer patients is a testament to the unreasonable effectiveness of qualitative mathematics in the age of data.
