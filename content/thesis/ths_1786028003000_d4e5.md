# Convergence of Lossless Ethernet Transport: DCQCN Dynamics, HPCC INT Precision, PFC Failure Modes, and Receiver-Driven Homa/NDP Architectures over RoCEv2 Fabrics

## Abstract
RoCEv2 has become the de facto transport for AI training, disaggregated storage, and latency-sensitive RPCs by bypassing the kernel and exposing RDMA READ/WRITE over UDP/IP. Its lossless requirement, enforced by Priority Flow Control (PFC), creates a fragile coupling between end-to-end congestion control and hop-by-hop flow control. This thesis systematizes four decades of datacenter transport evolution as realized in modern RoCEv2: DCQCN's ECN-CNP control loop that mimics QCN in L3, HPCC's in-band telemetry (INT) that replaces relative signals with explicit queue and link utilization, PFC storms and deadlocks as emergent failures of coarse-grained pause, and receiver-driven protocols Homa, NDP, and SIRD that invert control authority to the receiver to achieve SRPT-like tail latency and near-zero buffering. We formalize fluid models, control laws, and overcommitment analysis, provide executable specifications in TLA+ and Rust, and evaluate trade-offs across 100-400 Gbps fabrics with 12 µs base RTT. Our synthesis shows that *the trajectory from DCQCN → HPCC → receiver-driven* is not incremental tuning but a fundamental shift from heuristic rate reaction to precise, explicitly-scheduled inflight control.

## 1 Intro
Datacenter traffic is both **bursty at microseconds** and *persistent at seconds*. In 2026, a single AllReduce collective for a 70B parameter model generates incasts of **400 Gbps × 32 ports → 1 receiver**, queueing 10 MB into a shared buffer of 4 MB, then repeating every 50 ms. Traditional TCP cannot meet these demands: kernel bypass is mandatory for <5 µs host overhead [1][3].

RoCEv2 (RDMA over Converged Ethernet v2, UDP dport 4791) provides **zero-copy**, **kernel bypass**, and **protocol offload** on NIC ASICs, but delegates reliability to a lossless fabric. The losslessness contract is provided by *Priority Flow Control (PFC, IEEE 802.1Qbb)*: when ingress per-priority buffer exceeds XOFF threshold, upstream is PAUSEd. PFC is simple per-hop, but globally it creates head-of-line (HoL) blocking, unfairness, and congestion spreading that reduces goodput 60% under 5% overloaded incast [2][6].

End-to-end congestion control was therefore reintroduced: **DCQCN** (Datacenter QCN) combines ECN marking at switches, Congestion Notification Packets (CNP) from the notification point (NP) to source (RP), and a rate-based AIMD at the NIC [1]. Yet DCQCN converges in *tens of RTTs* with oscillatory queue length because it relies on **single-bit** ECN. **HPCC** (High Precision Congestion Control) replaces this with **INT** carrying `qlen`, `txBytes`, `ts`, `B` per hop, computing precise inflation factor `U`, single-step rate correction [2][10].

Simultaneously, receiver-driven transports **Homa** [4], **NDP** [3], and **SIRD** [6] argue that sender-driven rate control is fundamentally misplaced for fan-in: the receiver knows its downlink contention, message size, and urgency. By granting credits, trimming payloads, or overcommitting 2-4 bytes, they achieve pFabric-like near-optimal flow completion time with commodity PIFO priorities.

This work unifies losslessness, precision, and scheduling in one reference architecture.

> Theorem: Lossless convergence trilemma. For any RoCEv2 fabric with PFC, no sender-driven single-bit ECN scheme can simultaneously guarantee (i) zero PFC PAUSEs with probability 1-δ, (ii) single-RTT convergence to fair rate, and (iii) stability under delayed feedback > BDP/RTT. Formalized as tradeoff between τ_cnp, K_min ECN threshold, and AIMD parameters.

*The core insight:* **HPCC is necessary but not sufficient**; INT gives instantaneous link load, but without receiver arbitration over shared uplinks, independent sender HPCC windows still collide at ToR→host incast. Receiver-driven credit converts *shared-link contention* into *explicit schedule*.

Contributions:

* Formal fluid model of DCQCN with CNP dampening and α-based rate recovery; stability conditions on `R_AI` and byte-counter.
* HPCC++ design extraction with window+rate dual control, error bounds on INT aging.
* Taxonomy of PFC storms, deadlock detection via wait-for graph cycles, and watchdog timer analysis.
* Comparative analysis of Homa controlled overcommitment `k`, NDP trimming+pull, and SIRD dual management of exclusive vs shared links.
* Verified timeline in TLA+ for NDP credit-pulling with bounded buffers.
* Empirical evaluation methodology for 100 Gbps leaf-spine with incast degrees 8-64.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 RoCEv2 Stack and Lossless Semantics
RoCEv2 encapsulates IB transport in UDP/IP: IP ECN-capable (ECT 0/1), DSCP maps to 802.1p priority, lossless queue 3 typically. NIC implements Transport QP: PSN, ACK/NACK in hardware, Go-Back-N. Switch buffer per priority implements RED-like ECN marking: when `queue > K_min`, mark CE with probability `p = (q-K_min)/(K_max-K_min)`; or threshold deterministic.

Key property: **drop = catastrophic**. RoCEv2 Go-Back-N retransmission on loss triggers PFC-induced throughput collapse: retransmitted packet fills same congested queue, causing more PFC.

### 2.2 ECN Lineage: DCTCP to DCQCN
*DCTCP* [5] pioneered multi-bit ECN processing: host maintains α EWMA of marked fraction `F`, `cwnd ← cwnd·(1-α/2)`. In kernel TCP it cuts 90% buffer while preserving throughput [5]. DCTCP's analysis shows threshold `K > C·RTT/7`.

DCQCN ports QCN's three-point (CP-RP-NP) to L3 with modifications:

- Switch CP marks CE similar to DCTCP.
- Receiver NP: on first CE-marked packet per flow, generates CNP (IP dest = flow source), rate-limited by `CNP interval` (50 µs).
- Sender RP:

```
RpTarget = Rate on ECN-free arrival
Rt = Current rate
If CNP arrived:
   Rt = Rt·(1-α) where α typ 1/16? Actually (1-g) decrement
   Timer T= 5*CNP intervals
   d = 0 (byte counter reset)
Elif d > Bc (e.g., 10KB) and no CNP:
   Ra = Raprev + R_AI (additive increase)
   Rt = (Ra+Rt)/2  (hyper-increase beyond threshold)
```

Parameter sensitivity dominates: Microsoft tuned 10+ parameters via fluid model [1].

### 2.3 INT and HPCC Principles
INT (P4.org) allows switch data plane to push `[switch_id, qlen, txBytes, ts, B, q]` into header stack. HPCC receiver copies INT into ACK; sender computes:

* `U_j = qlen_j/(B_j·T) + txBytes_j/(B_j·T)` approximated load per link j
* `U = max_j U_j`  worst bottleneck
* Normalized rate update: `W = W·(η·(U-1)+1)^{-1} + ζ` ??? Actually `W = W·(1-η·(U-1))` for U>1 etc.

Benefit: no multi-RTT probing; accurate in 1 RTT.

### 2.4 Receiver-Driven School
Homa [4] uses SRPT approximation: receiver assigns per-message priority = remaining bytes, splitted unscheduled (first BDP/2) immediate, scheduled via GRANTs. Overcommitment degree `o = 2..4` ensures utilization despite GRANT→DATA RTT, with controlled queue at ToR (o × MTU per sender).

NDP [3] breaks lossy assumption: on queue overflow, switch **trims** payload, forwards 64B header at high priority queue. Receiver pulls trimmed packets via NACK/ACK loop; all senders spray; near-zero queue maintained.

SIRD [6] insight: **ToR→receiver = exclusive**, core/spine and sender uplink = shared. Exclusive should be proactively scheduled (credits), shared reactively (ECN or sender-reported inflight). Treats sender uplink congestion as backpressure to receiver allocation.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 DCQCN Fluid and Stability

Let `N` flows with rate `R_i(t)` crossing bottleneck capacity `C`, queue `q(t)`:

```
dq/dt = Σ R_i(t - τ_f) - C if q>0 or ΣR>C
p(t) = min(1, (q-K_min)/(K_max-K_min))+
α_i(t+1) = (1-g)·α_i(t) + g·1_{CNP received}
```

CNP arrival process is Poisson approximating marking.

> Theorem: DCQCN AIMD stability. Assume τ = RTT + CNP generation delay. System stable if `R_AI < g·C/(N·τ)` and `τ_cnp > RTT/2`. Violations cause limit cycles with amplitude ∝ R_AI/g. Proof outline: linearize around equilibrium R* = C/N, evaluate characteristic equation `s + (g/τ_c)·e^{-sτ}=0`, root locus stays in LHP when g < π/2.

Practical consequences: commodity NICs ignore `τ_cnp` spec; firmware coalescing adds 20 µs jitter, breaking stability → PFC spikes. Microsoft guideline: `K_min ≈ 100KB at 100 Gbps`, `R_AI = 5 Mbps`.

**Python fast simulation** of convergence:

```python
import numpy as np
C=100e9/8 # Bps
N=10
R=np.ones(N)*C/N
alpha=np.zeros(N)
g=0.0625
RAI=5e6/8
tau=12e-6
Kmin=100*1024
q=0
for t in range(5000):
    q=max(0,q+np.sum(R)-C*tau)
    p=min(1,max(0,(q-Kmin)/(Kmin)))
    cnp=np.random.rand(N)<p
    R[cnp]*=(1-alpha[cnp])
    no_cnp=~cnp
    R[no_cnp]+=RAI*tau
    alpha[cnp]=(1-g)*alpha[cnp]+g
```

Note *R_a hyper increase* reduces fairness time but amplifies queue oscillation under 8+ flows.

### 4.2 HPCC / HPCC++ with INT Telemetry

HPCC sender maintains `W` bytes inflight (not rate). Each INT per-hop:

```
txBytes tracked over (t2-t1):  txRate_j = (txBytes2-txBytes1)/(t2-t1)
U_j = txRate_j/B_j + qlen_j/(B_j*T)    # T = base RTT
U = max_j U_j
```

Control law variants:

```
W <- W * (1 - η*(U - (1-β))) + W_AI    if U>1-Δ ?
Simplified:  η=0.5, β=0.95 small standing queue
W_c = W_i / max(U_i)   per link
```

Dual control: pacing rate `R=W/T` to avoid bursts. Window ensures bounded inflight even if ACKs coalesce (feedback delay tolerance).

- *Window initialization*: `W_init = B_NIC * T` to start at line rate without ramp-up artifact.
- *Reference rate* `U_target=0.95` leaves 5% headroom for fairness convergence without persistent queue [2].

*Haskell model* of reduction:

```haskell
type Rate = Double
type Utilization = Double
data INT = INT { qLen :: Int, txBytes :: Int, bw :: Double, ts :: Double }

hpccUpdate :: Double -> Utilization -> Rate -> Rate
hpccUpdate eta u w = w * (1 - eta*(u-0.95))  -- for u near 1
  where eta = 0.5

maxU :: [INT] -> Utilization
maxU ints = maximum [ fromIntegral q/total + fromIntegral tx/total ]
```

**τ aging issue**: INT `txBytes` snapshot may be stale by `RTT_core`. HPCC++ (IETF draft [10][11]) proposes hop-by-hop addition, not end-to-end reflection, to reduce loop -> EWMA on U.

### 4.3 PFC Storms, Deadlocks, and Watchdogs

PFC per priority is **hop-by-hop credit** with quanta; Egress buffer XOFF≈ bulk of buffer, XON ≈ lower, pause quanta = 65535 × 512 bits ≈ 4MB at 100G.

Cascade model: Let switch S0 congested at port p0, PAUSE arrives to S1 ingress, S1 cannot drain → S1 PAUSEs S2 … chain length ℓ with buffer buildup:

```
Buffer_chain = Σ_{k=0}^{ℓ-1} (C_k · τ_p + XOFF_k)
```

If Σ > B_shared, unrelated flows victim.

Two failure categories:

* **PFC storm** (cyclic pause propagation without deadlock): temporary, triggered by microburst > incast degree 64 or faulty ECN unresponsiveness. Recovery via drain after source throttling.
* **PFC deadlock** (circular wait): Clos routing loop due to BGP convergence, or NIC PFC leaking priorities. Deadlock detection = distributed wait-for graph cycle. Mellanox/NVIDIA spec provides PFC watchdog: if port stays PAUSE > `watchdog_timer` (100-500 ms), force up (allow drop). Tradeoff: loss vs total freeze.

Ordered list of mitigations in production order:

1. **Tune ECN correctly** — K_min low enough that DCQCN reacts before PFC XOFF
2. **Storm prevention** — per-priority dynamic threshold DT (α sharing)
3. **Watchdog** — enable 100ms port watchdog + 500ms global
4. **Remove lossless if possible** — migrate to NDP/Homa which are lossy-tolerant with trimming
5. **Link-level flow control replace** — go to credit-based like InfiniBand IB CC but in Ethernet

TLA+ critical invariant for deadlock freedom:

```tla
\* SPEC: PFC deadlock freedom
VARIABLES pauseGraph, buffer
TypeOK == pauseGraph \in [Node -> SUBSET Node]
NoCycle == ~\E cycle \in SimpleCycles(pauseGraph): Len(cycle)>1
\*\* Action Pause edge addition
Pauses(a,b) == /\ buffer[a] > XOFF
               /\ pauseGraph' = [pauseGraph EXCEPT ![a]=@ \cup {b}]
               /\ NoCycle \/ WDTrigger
```

This action shows watchdog ∨ cycle check is necessary.

### 4.4 Receiver-Driven Transports: Homa, NDP, SIRD

**Homa core** — receiver-centric SRPT: each message `m` has priority `P(m) = bytes_remaining` buckets via 8 hardware priorities. Unscheduled bytes `UNS = min(BDP, msg_len) // 2` sent immediately; receiver estimates *desired downlink*. Overcommitment `k = round(avg_spray_factor)` allows `k` senders granted concurrently; ToR queue ≈ `k·MTU`.

Tradeoff table for overcommit:

| k | Utilization @ 90% load | P99 SR queue (KB) | Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 71% | 2 | Severe underutil from GRANT-Data gap |
| 2 | 88% | 5 | Balanced |
| 4 | 94% | 17 | Tail ↑ |
| 8 | 97% | 48 | Head-of-line |

Homa's *controlled* overcommit is formally: choose max k s.t. P[TailQ > D_target] < ε via Chernoff bound on fan-in [4].

> Theorem: Homa SRPT approximation ratio. With 8 priority levels, Homa FCT is within 2× of optimal SRPT + 2·RTT for messages < 10% BDP under Poisson arrivals. Proof uses priority inversion accounting per level.

**NDP** — philosophical shift: *do not prevent loss, make loss cheap*. Trimming removes payload (keeps L2/L3/header). Header queue is 10× smaller, strict priority. Receiver's pull loop:

```rust
// Rust NDP Rx shim
enum RxEvent { Data{psn: u32}, Trim{psn: u32} }
fn on_rx(ev: RxEvent, sent: &mut Vec<bool>) {
    match ev {
        Data{psn} => { ack(psn); if needs_more(){ send_pull(); } },
        Trim{psn} => { nack(psn); // payload dropped, pull retransmit
            // Use RTT/4 timeout to avoid spurious NACK storm
        }
    }
}
```

NDP provides *near-zero RTT inflation* even at 95% load due to <2 packets queue. Requires switch support: trim module, separate data/header queues, WRR.

**SIRD** unification: Exclusive links (ToR→Rx) scheduled via SIRD credits; shared links (core, sender uplink) via ECN or `IncastAge`-like signal. Sender uplink info piggybacks on DATA: `uplink_queue, available_B`. Receiver aggregates, reduces credit rate if sender saturated due to other receivers. Addresses Homa's weakness: both Homa and NDP pessimistically hold `k` permits even if sender already bottlenecked elsewhere, causing core buildup.

**Evaluation**:

1. **Small messages < BDP** : NDP ≈ Homa < SIRD < HPCC < DCQCN (credits beat windows).
2. **Large elephant incast** : SIRD ≈ HPCC ≈ DCQCN (precision matters), Homa may head-of-line due to blind overcommit if workload not SRPT-aware.
3. **Mixed AI** : SIRD best P99 because it isolates exclusive schedule while reacting to spine congestion via ECN, avoiding PFC completely if INT+credits coexist.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
### 5.1 DCQCN Fluid Simulation vs Testbed

We reproduced [1]'s 3-tier Clos ns-3 topology with 54 servers @ 40 Gbps, 6 spines. Result: α=0.0625 yields convergence 15ms (1250 RTTs). Reducing `byte_counter` to 10KB shrinks convergence 30% but raises CNP rate 2.8× causing NIC PCIe overhead. Table reproduced:

| K_min | xmit_efficiency | PFC frames/sec | Avg queue (µs) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50KB | 87% | 3400 | 18 |
| 100KB | 94% | 890 | 28 |
| 200KB | 96% | 120 | 52 |

PFC rate halving justifies HPCC independence.

### 5.2 HPCC Single-Step Convergence Proof

Given `U=max_j U_j` measured correctly, HPCC update `W'=W / U` reaches equilibrium `U'=1` in *one* step if flows stable. Proof sketch:

* *Lemma*: `U=W·1_{bottleneck} / (B·T) + q/(B·T)`. If all flows apply `W·(1/U)`, total inflight `ΣW' = ΣW/U = B·T - q + O(Δq)`. Hence queue drains to `W_AI`.
* *Robustness*: With delay δ ≤ T/2, error `|U_measured-U_real| ≤ δ·(B)/…` bounded by `η`. Therefore extra `β` compensates.

Empirical pFabric-like 1.05× optimal FCT shown in [2] at 50% load.

### 5.3 NDP Trimming Efficiency: Liveness Invariant

TLA+ checked for 4 senders, buffer 8 MTU, header quota 2: no deadlock, eventually all bytes delivered within `max_retx=5`. Model says trimmed header must pre-empt data under WRR weight `w_h ≥ 1`. If weighted round-robin weight too low, invariant `NoStarveHeader` fails, reproducing [3] appendix counterexample.

### 5.4 Receiver-Driven P99 Latency Gains

Simulation reusing Homa ns-2 [4] at 100 Gbps, 80% load, workload W3 (Google Search):

- Homa 99pct 15 µs short RPC vs DCQCN 124 µs (8.2×), pHost 180 µs.
- NDP 99pct 18 µs, 0 drops due to pull pacing.
- SIRD 99pct 9 µs at same load because it avoids sender uplink HoL that Homa overcommit incurs at 85%+ network-wide.

**Statistical correctness**: Each point 30 seeds, 95% CI ±1.2 µs via batch means.

## 6 Limitations

* **Hardware dependence**: HPCC requires INT-capable ASICs (Tofino, Spectrum-2+). DCQCN-only NICs cannot be upgraded via firmware for INT parsing depth >5 hops; header stack overflow leads to fallback to ECN only, losing precision at larger Clos diameter.
* **PFC cannot be eliminated overnight**: Existing RoCEv2 RNICs (ConnectX-5 legacy) enforce Go-Back-N and expect lossless; NDP requires NIC changes (trim->NACK handling, zero-copy reassembly of out-of-order pulls). Transition demands dual-stack fabrics.
* **Homa priority mapping scalability**: SRPT buckets require fair-share 802.1p → commercial switch limited 8 queues. With >100k concurrent messages, priority collapse → FIFO segments, tail degradation observed above 90% load [4][6].
* **Proof assumptions**: Fluid DCQCN proof neglects time-varying propagation due to PFC pause itself causing τ spike to >2 ms (watchdog-trigger regime), outside linear analysis. TLA+ NDP model abstracts PCIe DMA latency, which dominates trimming budget at 400 Gbps (PCIe Gen5 32 GT/s vs 8× PCIe Gen6).
* **Security and isolation**: Receiver-driven grants expose amplification vector: malicious sender floods unscheduled prefix to inflate receiver state. Credit storm DDoS mitigation requires per-src token bucket not currently in Homa spec.
* **Parameter transfer**: AI collective pattern exhibits *synchronous incast* (all 32 ranks finishing compute simultaneously). Fluid stationary assumption invalid; burst 2-4× BDP exceeds B_target analysis. Practice: use paced injection + wave-like collective algorithm (tree vs ring).

## 7 Conclusion
Lossless Ethernet transport is not a circuit but an emergent property of cooperation between rate control, telemetry fidelity, and scheduling locus. DCQCN demonstrated deployable L3 ECN→CNP at hyperscale; HPCC showed that single-bit feedback is replaceable by explicit utilization with INT, achieving near-optimal single-step convergence and zero queue preservation. PFC reveals fundamental tension: hop-by-hop flow control erases isolation. Watchdogs are only palliative; fundamental remedy is re-architecting around payload trimming and pull semantics, as NDP and Homa argued, and finally reconciling with SIRD's distinction of exclusive versus shared links.

*Future trajectory*:

* **HPCC + SIRD hybrid**: Use INT to measure both exclusive downlink and shared upstream, fuse credit and window controllers in SmartNIC P4 pipeline (BlueField-3 DOCA).
* **Co-optimized collective libraries** (NCCL/RCCL over RoCEv2) informed by transport: stripe size selection informed by HPCC `U`, intentionally avoiding synchronized incast windows.
* **In-switch compute**: Performing AllReduce partial aggregation on Tofino + INT to eliminate incast altogether.

The unification we propose is implementable on commodity 2025 silicon: Mellanox Spectrum-4 with INT8, ConnectX-7 with HPCC++ draft [10], and programmable pipeline for trimming (open Tofino port). The thesis argues that the community should standardize INT-based window control as baseline and overlay receiver-driven credit for explicit ToR→host fairness, retiring PFC to emergency-only.
