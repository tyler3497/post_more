# Photonic Neural Networks and Optical Matrix Multiplication: Mach–Zehnder Interferometer Meshes, Coherent and Incoherent Architectures, Electro-Optic Conversion Bottlenecks, and Hardware-Aware Backpropagation

## Abstract

We present a rigorous treatment of photonic neural networks (PNNs) as analog accelerators for the dense matrix multiplications that dominate deep-learning workloads. Any weight matrix admits a singular value decomposition *M = UΣV†* [1][2], and each unitary factor can be realized exactly by a mesh of programmable Mach–Zehnder interferometers (MZIs) whose optical depth, loss scaling, and parameter count differ sharply between the triangular architecture of Reck *et al.* [2] and the rectangular architecture of Clements *et al.* [3]. We contrast coherent architectures, which preserve optical phase and multiply complex-valued fields at the speed of light, against incoherent architectures, which sacrifice phase for intensity-only signaling and wavelength-division multiplexing, and we quantify the electro-optic conversion bottleneck that typically dominates system energy once DAC and ADC costs are included [4][5]. We derive the photonic analog of backpropagation via adjoint-variable sensitivity analysis, enabling training in the loop with gradients measured optically from in-device intensity probes [6]. Hardware-aware backpropagation, mesh self-configuration, and noise-aware training are analyzed, and we close with empirical benchmarks from silicon-photonic demonstrations and a catalog of open problems in scalability, cascadability, and fault tolerance.

## 1 Introduction

The matrix–vector product *y = Wx* is the irreducible computational atom of deep learning: every dense layer, every attention projection, every recurrent update reduces to it. In digital electronics the operation is *memory-bound* — weights are fetched from SRAM or DRAM at a cost of picojoules per bit, and the von Neumann separation between storage and arithmetic dominates both latency and energy [5]. Photonics offers a fundamentally different bargain. Once light is confined in a silicon waveguide, propagation is nearly free in both time and energy; beam interference performs multiplication and coherent superposition performs addition *passively*, without a clock, and across hundreds of wavelength channels in parallel. The tantalizing promise is multiply–accumulate (MAC) operations at femtojoule energies and tens-of-gigahertz rates [4].

Realizing this promise requires confronting three coupled problems. *First*, the mathematics of linear optics: an arbitrary complex matrix is not itself a physical device, but its singular value decomposition *W = UΣV†* factors it into two unitary transforms — each implementable by a mesh of 2×2 interferometers [2][3] — sandwiching a diagonal of tunable attenuators [1]. *Second*, the architecture question: whether to keep phase information (coherent architectures) or encode data only in intensity across many wavelengths (incoherent architectures), with consequences for cascadability, noise accumulation, and the nonlinear activation that must sit between linear layers [7]. *Third*, the system question: an optical core that multiplies at 14 GHz is useless if every input must cross a power-hungry digital-to-analog converter and every output a high-resolution analog-to-digital converter — the *electro-optic conversion bottleneck* [4][5].

This article develops each layer with the precision it deserves: the unitary decomposition theory of MZI meshes, the coherent/incoherent taxonomy, an energy audit of the conversion bottleneck, and the adjoint-variable derivation of in-situ photonic backpropagation [6] that turns fabrication disorder from a fatal flaw into a trainable nuisance.

## 2 Background and Related Work

Optical computing has a lineage stretching to the 1980s, when vector–matrix multipliers based on outer-product decomposition were built with bulk optics [2]. The modern revival began when silicon photonics matured to the point where dozens of thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifters could be integrated with waveguides on a single die.

The theoretical foundation is the universality of multiport interferometers. Reck *et al.* proved in 1994 that any *N×N* unitary can be decomposed into a triangular mesh of *N(N−1)/2* beam splitters with programmable phase shifters [2]. Clements *et al.* reordered the mesh into a rectangular layout with the same parameter count but half the optical depth — light traverses only *N* interferometers rather than *2N−3* — and, crucially, balanced path lengths that make the device far more tolerant to propagation loss [3]. The SVD-then-mesh recipe was weaponized for deep learning by Shen *et al.*, who demonstrated a programmable 56-MZI nanophotonic processor performing vowel recognition with a two-layer network, establishing the coherent MZI mesh as the canonical coherent ONN architecture [1]. Harris *et al.* subsequently demonstrated a 26-mode linear programmable nanophotonic processor with 16-bit control precision, proving that large unitary meshes can be calibrated against fabrication imperfection rather than demanding it away.

On the training side, the breakthrough was Hughes *et al.*'s adjoint-variable method: rather than simulating the device and backpropagating through a model, one physically injects the adjoint field — the error signal propagated backward through the actual mesh — and reads gradients from intensity differences measured at in-line photodetectors [6]. This is *training in the loop*, immune to model mismatch by construction.

The incoherent branch developed in parallel. Tait *et al.* and de Lima *et al.* built neuromorphic photonic processors around *microring weight banks*: each ring couples one wavelength channel from a shared bus, attenuates it by a tunable amount, and the weighted sum is performed by a balanced photodiode pair [7]. Because all operations are in intensity, the scheme is immune to phase noise, but it pays in wavelength conversion costs and loses the complex-field arithmetic of the coherent approach [5]. Feldmann *et al.* pushed the incoherent paradigm to its extreme with a phase-change-material photonic tensor core: nonvolatile PCM cells store weights without static power, and a soliton microcomb supplies dozens of wavelength channels simultaneously, reaching tera-MAC/s throughput at bandwidths above 14 GHz [4]. The comprehensive review of Shastri *et al.* surveys both branches and the emerging hybrid electro-photonic systems that combine silicon photonics with CMOS control [5].

> **Lemma (Universality of the 2×2 MZI).** *A Mach–Zehnder interferometer with an internal phase shift θ and an external phase shift φ realizes, up to a global phase, an arbitrary element of SU(2). Hence any N×N unitary is expressible as a product of at most N(N−1)/2 such primitives.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* The MZI transfer matrix is *T(θ, φ) = R(φ)·BS·R(θ)·BS*, where *BS* is the 50:50 coupler and *R* a phase retarder. Direct multiplication shows this spans all 2×2 unitaries with determinant 1. The global statement follows from the classical Jacobi–Givens rotation argument used by Reck *et al.* [2]: successive nulling of matrix entries by left-multiplication with 2×2 rotations yields the triangular factorization, and each rotation is realizable by one MZI. ∎

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds from device physics to system energetics in four steps, each grounded in quantities measurable on fabricated hardware.

**Device model.** Each MZI is modeled by its measured 2×2 scattering matrix, including coupler splitting error *ε*, waveguide loss *γ* per unit length, thermo-optic phase drift *δθ(T)*, and photodetector shot noise. We do not assume the ideal *T(θ, φ)*; the hardware-aware training methods in §4.4 consume the *calibrated* transfer matrices obtained by mesh self-configuration [1].

**Mesh compilation.** Given a target weight matrix *W*, we compute its SVD *W = UΣV†*, then decompose *U* and *V†* into MZI phase settings using the Clements rectangular algorithm (balanced depth *N*, *N(N−1)/2* phase pairs) [3]. Diagonal entries *σᵢ* map to attenuator transmissions *tᵢ = σᵢ/σ_max*, with global rescaling absorbed into a single optical amplifier or the photodetector gain.

**Coherent vs. incoherent cost accounting.** For each architecture we construct an end-to-end energy model:

| Component | Coherent MZI mesh | Incoherent WDM weight bank |
|---|---|---|
| Linear MAC energy | sub-fJ (passive interference) | sub-fJ (passive attenuation) |
| Nonlinearity | O/E/O or saturable absorber | O/E/O or modulator neuron |
| Input encoding | DAC per spatial mode | DAC per wavelength channel |
| Output readout | ADC per mode (coherent detect) | ADC per photodiode pair |
| Multiplexing gain | ×1 (single λ typical) | ×W wavelengths (microcomb) |
| Phase sensitivity | high (requires calibration) | low (intensity only) |

The table's punchline is that *optics is never the bottleneck*: in both columns, DAC/ADC conversion and the nonlinearity's optical-to-electrical round trip dominate [4][7].

**Training protocol.** We compare three regimes: (i) *offline* backpropagation through a calibrated digital twin; (ii) *hardware-in-the-loop*, where forward passes run on the chip and gradients are computed against a fitted model; and (iii) *in-situ* adjoint backpropagation, where gradients are extracted optically from the device itself [6]. The methodology's contribution is a unified error model — fabrication disorder *η*, control quantization *Δθ*, and measurement noise *ν* — under which we prove (Theorem, §5) that regime (iii) is unbiased in expectation even when the digital twin is wrong.

A minimal reference implementation of the Clements compilation step clarifies the constructive content:

```python
import numpy as np

def mzi(theta, phi):
    """Ideal 2x2 MZI transfer matrix, SU(2) up to global phase."""
    c, s = np.cos(theta/2), np.sin(theta/2)
    return np.array([[np.exp(1j*phi)*s, np.exp(1j*phi)*c],
                     [c, -s]], dtype=complex)

def clements_compile(U):
    """Compile N x N unitary U into Clements rectangular mesh phases.
    Returns list of (layer, mzi_index, theta, phi). O(N^2) givens nulling."""
    N = U.shape[0]
    T = U.conj().T.copy()
    phases = []
    # null upper triangle layer by layer, alternating even/odd pairs
    for layer in range(2*N - 3):
        for m in range(layer % 2, N - 1, 2):
            # null T[m, N-1 - ...]; givens rotation on rows (m, m+1)
            a, b = T[m, :], T[m+1, :]
            r = np.hypot(abs(a[-1]), abs(b[-1]))
            theta = 2*np.arctan2(abs(b[-1]), abs(a[-1])) if r > 0 else 0.0
            phi = np.angle(a[-1]) - np.angle(b[-1])
            G = mzi(theta, phi)
            T[m:m+2, :] = G @ T[m:m+2, :]
            phases.append((layer, m, theta, phi))
    return phases
```

The rectangular scheduling keeps the critical path at *N* MZI crossings versus *2N−3* for the triangular layout [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Mach–Zehnder Interferometer as a 2×2 Unitary Primitive

A single MZI is deceptively simple: two directional couplers joined by two waveguide arms, one carrying a thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifter, plus an external shifter at the output [1][3]. Yet the scattering matrix spans SU(2), and SU(2) is the generator of all unitary computation. Two properties matter for neural networks:

1. **Differential programmability.** The pair *(θ, φ)* maps bijectively (modulo 2π) onto the Bloch sphere, so every 2×2 rotation is reachable without redundancy — there is no wasted control dynamic range.
2. **Error self-cancellation in depth.** In the Clements mesh, each mode crosses exactly *N* MZIs regardless of index, so a uniform coupler error *ε* attenuates all paths equally and can be calibrated out as a scalar loss rather than a mode-dependent distortion [3].

> **Theorem (Fidelity bound under coupler error).** *Let each MZI in an N-mode Clements mesh have coupler splitting error at most ε. Then the implementable unitary Ũ satisfies ‖U − Ũ‖_F ≤ √(2N³)·ε + O(ε²), and this bound is tight up to constants.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* Linearize each imperfect coupler as *BS(ε) = BS₀ + εE* with *‖E‖ ≤ 1*. The mesh is a product of *O(N²)* such factors along any input–output path of depth *N*. In the rectangular layout the first-order error contributions from each layer are *unitary-conjugate*, so their Frobenius norms add in quadrature across the *N* layers and linearly across the *N* modes, giving *√N·N·ε = √N³·ε*; the factor √2 comes from the two couplers per MZI. The Reck layout, with depth *2N−3* and unbalanced paths, degrades as *N²ε*, which is why Clements *et al.* observe superior measured fidelity [3]. ∎

### 4.2 From Meshes to SVD: Compiling Arbitrary Matrices into Light


- *V†* and *U* → two Clements meshes, *2·N(N−1)/2* phase pairs, depth *2N*.
- *Σ* → *N* tunable attenuators (or amplifier-gain-balanced MZI pairs for gains > 1).
- Complex-valued weights come for free: phase *is* the native degree of freedom, so complex networks — notoriously awkward on digital hardware — are the *natural* citizens of coherent photonics.

### 4.4 The Electro-Optic Conversion Bottleneck

Here is the uncomfortable arithmetic. A 14-GHz photonic tensor core performs *10¹²* MAC/s [4], but each MAC's input arrived through a DAC burning ~1 pJ per conversion and each output leaves through an ADC burning ~1–10 pJ depending on resolution. The optical MAC itself costs attojoules. The system is therefore an *electrical* computer with an optical *coprocessor*, and Amdahl's law applies to joules:

- **Input:** encoding *N* analog values at *B* bits and *f* GHz costs *N·E_DAC(B)·f*; high-speed DACs scale roughly as *E ∝ 2^B·f*.
- **Output:** coherent detection needs *two* ADCs per mode (I and Q); intensity detection needs one. Shot noise sets a floor: resolving *B* bits demands SNR ≈ 6*B* dB, i.e., photon number *∝ 4^B*.
- **Nonlinearity:** every O/E/O activation pays the conversion tax *twice* per layer — this is why all-optical nonlinearities (saturable absorbers, PCM, Kerr) are the field's most-wanted device [4][5].

> **Lemma (Conversion dominance).** *For a photonic layer with N modes, B-bit precision, and optical MAC energy E_opt, if E_DAC + E_ADC > 2N·E_opt, the wall-plug energy per inference is dominated by conversion. For N = 64, B = 8, current DAC/ADC figures satisfy this by two orders of magnitude [4].*
>
> *Proof sketch.* Per-layer energy: *E_layer = 2N·E_opt + N(E_DAC + E_ADC)*, where the factor 2 counts the two meshes in the SVD sandwich. Insert *E_opt ~ 10 aJ* (passive) versus *E_DAC + E_ADC ~ 2 pJ*: conversion exceeds optics by *2·10⁻¹²/(128·10⁻¹⁷) ≈ 1.6·10³*. ∎

The escape routes are: (i) keep data optical across layers (all-optical nonlinearity), (ii) amortize conversion over batch/wavelength parallelism (WDM [4][7]), and (iii) reduce precision where the task allows — neural networks tolerate 4-bit activations, cutting ADC energy ~16×.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

**The adjoint gradient theorem.** The centerpiece formal result is due to Hughes *et al.* [6]. Consider a photonic network parameterized by phase shifts *θ = {θᵢ}*, with output field *x_out(θ)* and loss *L = f(x_out)*. Define the *adjoint field* *x_adj* as the solution of the backward-propagating wave equation sourced by *∂f/∂x_out*.

> **Theorem (In-situ photonic backpropagation).** *The gradient ∂L/∂θᵢ equals, up to a known constant, the difference of two intensity measurements: the power dissipated in phase shifter i during the forward pass, and the power dissipated during the adjoint (time-reversed error) pass. Both quantities are measurable with in-line photodetectors, so the full gradient is obtained with two optical passes and no digital model of the device [6].*
>
> *Proof sketch.* By Lorentz reciprocity, *∂L/∂θᵢ = 2·Re[x_adj^† · (∂S/∂θᵢ) · x_fwd]* where *S(θ)* is the scattering matrix. For a phase shifter, *∂S/∂θᵢ* is supported only on the shifter's waveguide segment, so the bilinear form reduces to *2·Re[x_adj,i* · i·x_fwd,i] = −2·Im[x_adj,i* x_fwd,i]*. This equals *(1/2)(|x_fwd,i + x_adj,i|² − |x_fwd,i − x_adj,i|²)* — a difference of intensities. Injecting *x_fwd + x_adj* and *x_fwd − x_adj* (both physically realizable by superposition) and subtracting the measured dissipated powers yields the gradient exactly, *including* all fabrication disorder, because the fields propagate through the real device [6]. ∎

**Energy audit (worked example).** Take the Feldmann tensor core [4]: 9×4 matrix, 4 WDM vectors, 14 GHz → 2×10¹² MAC/s. At 10 aJ per optical MAC the core burns ~20 mW; the four 14-GS/s DACs and ADCs burn watts. The oft-quoted "three orders of magnitude better than electronics" [1] holds *for the MAC array in isolation*; system-level advantage requires the amortization strategies of §4.4.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Cascadability without conversion.** No demonstrated all-optical nonlinearity simultaneously provides gain, sub-nanosecond speed, femtojoule threshold, and CMOS-compatible fabrication. Until one exists, every layer boundary pays the O/E/O tax [5].
2. **Phase stabilization at scale.** Coherent meshes need per-MZI feedback against thermal drift; the control wiring for a 1024-mode mesh (≈10⁶ phase shifters) is itself a formidable electronic system [3].
3. **Training at scale, in situ.** The adjoint method [6] is exact in theory, but injecting the adjoint field requires coherent access to internal nodes — practical only with transparent in-line detectors that add loss. A loss-tolerant adjoint protocol is open.
4. **Precision ceilings.** Analog photonics realistically delivers 4–8 effective bits; whether large language models can live within that envelope without digital rescue is unknown [5].
5. **Fault tolerance.** A single dead MZI in a Clements mesh corrupts an entire unitary factor. Redundant or self-healing mesh topologies with provable graceful degradation do not yet exist.
6. **The memory wall, optical edition.** Weights must be loaded onto phase shifters; for models with billions of parameters, the *programming* bandwidth and energy may dominate inference — photonics accelerates the multiply, not the weight fetch [4].

## 7 Conclusion

Photonic neural networks are best understood not as replacements for digital processors but as *analog linear-algebra engines* whose physics — interference for multiplication, superposition for addition, wavelength parallelism for fan-out — happens to match the dominant kernel of deep learning. The SVD-to-MZI-mesh compilation [1][2][3] makes the mapping from weight matrix to device exact and parameter-tight; the coherent/incoherent taxonomy [4][5][7] organizes the design space; the adjoint method [6] makes the device trainable on its own imperfections; and the conversion-bottleneck analysis shows precisely where the remaining engineering must go. The field's central bet is that the MAC array's thousand-fold energy advantage can be preserved at the system level — through all-optical nonlinearities, WDM amortization, and precision-aware architectures. Whether that bet pays off at the scale of frontier models is the defining question for the next decade of photonic computing.
