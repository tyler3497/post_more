# Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems: PLONK Arithmetization, STARKs, and Recursive Composition via Folding Schemes

## Abstract

Zero-knowledge proof systems have diverged into two dominant design families: pairing-based SNARKs built on **KZG polynomial commitments** and **PLONK-style arithmetization with permutation arguments**, and hash-based STARKs built on **FRI low-degree testing** and **Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR)**. This thesis analyzes both families' cryptographic machinery — PLONK's five-round protocol, grand-product permutation argument, and batched pairing checks versus STARK trace interpolation, composition polynomials, and Merkle-committed FRI folding — contrasts their prover/verifier asymptotics, and examines how recursive composition via accumulation and the **Nova folding scheme** for relaxed R1CS transforms these primitives into incrementally verifiable computation. We survey trusted-setup tradeoffs from circuit-specific CRS to universal updatable SRS to fully transparent hash-based setups, and quantify the concrete cost of each choice. The result is a unified reference for selecting and composing modern zero-knowledge proof systems.

---

## 1 Introduction

A *succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge* (SNARK) allows a prover to convince a verifier that a computation was performed correctly, producing a proof far smaller than the computation itself and verifiable far faster than re-execution. The theoretical foundations date to Micali's CS proofs and Kilian's PCP-based arguments, but the modern era began with pairing-based preprocessing SNARKs such as Pinocchio and Groth16 [1].

Two architectural revolutions reshaped the field after 2016:

1. **Universal SNARKs with permutation arguments.** PLONK (Gabizon, Williamson, Ciobotaru) replaced the rigid quadratic arithmetic program (QAP) with a flexible *Plonkish* arithmetization: gates are arbitrary polynomial identities over wire vectors, and wiring between gates is enforced by a grand-product permutation argument. A single updatable structured reference string (SRS) serves all circuits [2].
2. **Transparent STARKs with FRI.** STARKs (Ben-Sasson et al.) eliminated the trusted setup entirely, compiling algebraic intermediate representations into Reed–Solomon proximity proofs via the FRI low-degree test, realized with Merkle commitments and collision-resistant hashing [3].

A third development — **recursive composition** — turned one-shot proofs into *incrementally verifiable computation* (IVC): a proof that proves the verification of a prior proof, compressing arbitrarily long computations into constant-size attestations. The Nova folding scheme achieved this with the smallest recursion overhead known, avoiding SNARKs entirely in the inner loop [4].

This thesis develops the full technical stack: arithmetization, commitments, permutation arguments, low-degree testing, recursion, and the asymptotics and setup assumptions that govern real deployments.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Arithmetization: From R1CS to Plonkish

Classical preprocessing SNARKs encode computation as rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS): matrices \(A, B, C\) with \(Az \circ Bz = Cz\). R1CS is expressive but rigid — every constraint is a single bilinear product gate, so complex operations (bit decomposition, range checks) balloon into thousands of constraints.

PLONK generalizes to **Plonkish** arithmetization. A circuit of \(n\) gates and three wires per gate yields three wire polynomials \(a(X), b(X), c(X)\) interpolated over a multiplicative subgroup \(H = \{1, \omega, \dots, \omega^{n-1}\}\). Gate semantics are enforced by a *selector polynomial identity*:

```text
q_M(X)·a(X)·b(X) + q_L(X)·a(X) + q_R(X)·b(X)
  + q_O(X)·c(X) + q_C(X) = 0   for all X in H
```

where the \(q\) polynomials are preprocessed selectors (multiplication, left, right, output, constant). Custom gates (TurboPlonk/UltraPlonk) extend this identity to higher-degree terms and lookup arguments, compressing expensive sub-circuits into single gates [2].

### 2.2 Polynomial Commitment Schemes

Both families need a way to commit to polynomials and later prove evaluations at verifier-chosen points:

- **KZG commitments** (Kate–Zaverucha–Goldberg): constant-size commitments and constant-size evaluation proofs via pairings; require a trusted SRS containing powers \(g^{\tau^i}\). Batching reduces many openings to one pairing equation [5].
- **FRI-based commitments**: commit by Merkle-rooting the Reed–Solomon codeword evaluations; openings are authentication paths plus a logarithmic folding transcript. No trusted setup; security reduces to collision resistance of the hash [6].
- **IPA / DARK**: inner-product arguments (Halo 2) and groups of unknown order (DARK compilers) offer transparent logarithmic alternatives [7].

### 2.3 IOPs and the Compiler Paradigm

Modern constructions separate into two layers [7]:

1. An *information-theoretic* protocol — typically a **polynomial IOP** or **algebraic IOP** — that is statistically sound against unbounded provers with oracle access to polynomials.
2. A *cryptographic compiler* that instantiates the oracles with a commitment scheme (KZG, Merkle+FRI, IPA), trading statistical soundness for computational soundness.

PLONK is a polynomial IOP compiled by KZG; STARKs are algebraic IOPs compiled by Merkle+FRI. This separation is what makes HyperNova-style generalization possible: one arithmetization, multiple compilers [8].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds in three stages. First, we reconstruct the PLONK protocol round-by-round, identifying exactly where the permutation argument, quotient polynomial, and opening batching contribute to prover cost and proof size. Second, we develop FRI and AIR from first principles, deriving the soundness error as a function of query count and code rate, and compare the resulting asymptotics against pairing-based systems. Third, we formalize recursive composition: accumulation-based recursion (Halo 2), FRI-based recursion (Plonky2/Plonky3), and folding-based IVC (Nova/HyperNova), quantifying recursion overhead — the number of extra constraints the prover pays per step to fold verification.

We ground asymptotic claims in published prover benchmarks and parameter choices from deployed systems (Aztec, StarkNet, Polygon Zero), and we state concrete security assumptions for each family: algebraic group model with pairing-friendly curves versus the random-oracle model with collision-resistant hashes.

> **Theorem (IOP-to-argument compilation).** Any public-coin polynomial IOP for a relation \(\mathcal{R}\) with soundness error \(\epsilon\) and query complexity \(q\), compiled with an extractable polynomial commitment scheme, yields an argument of knowledge for \(\mathcal{R}\) with soundness error \(\epsilon + \mathrm{negl}(\lambda)\), where the prover's cost grows by the commitment and opening overhead and the verifier's by the opening checks.

This compiler theorem explains why protocol designers optimize the *information-theoretic* layer (fewer rounds, fewer queries, lower degree) while cryptographers optimize the *commitment* layer (smaller openings, cheaper verification).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 PLONK's Permutation Argument and Constraint System

The core innovation of PLONK is enforcing *copy constraints* — the requirement that two wire positions in different gates carry the same value — without the wiring-specific QAP of Groth16. Copy constraints are expressed as a permutation \(\sigma\) over the \(3n\) wire positions; the witness satisfies the constraints iff the wire-value vector is invariant under \(\sigma\).

The prover commits to a **grand-product polynomial** \(Z(X)\) accumulating:

\[ Z(\omega X) = Z(X)\,\frac{(a(X)+\beta X+\gamma)(b(X)+\beta k_1 X+\gamma)(c(X)+\beta k_2 X+\gamma)}{(a(X)+\beta\,\sigma_1(X)+\gamma)(b(X)+\beta\,\sigma_2(X)+\gamma)(c(X)+\beta\,\sigma_3(X)+\gamma)} \]

with \(Z(1) = 1\) and the boundary condition \(Z(\omega^n) = 1\). If the permutation identity holds at every \(X \in H\), then each numerator/denominator ratio telescopes and the copy constraints hold; if any wire is misrouted, the grand product fails with overwhelming probability over random \(\beta, \gamma\). The polynomials \(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3\) encoding \(\sigma\) are preprocessed, so wiring costs the prover only one extra committed polynomial.

The full five-round protocol is:

| Round | Prover sends | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | \([a],[b],[c]\) | Wire commitments |
| 2 | \([z]\) | Permutation grand product |
| 3 | \([t_{lo}],[t_{mid}],[t_{hi}]\) | Quotient polynomial (degree \(3n\)) |
| 4 | 7 field evaluations | Openings at \(\zeta, \zeta\omega\) |
| 5 | \([W_\zeta],[W_{\zeta\omega}]\) | Batched KZG opening proofs |

The quotient polynomial \(t(X)\) divides the combined constraint identity by the vanishing polynomial \(Z_H(X) = X^n - 1\); its existence proves all gate and permutation identities vanish on \(H\). The verifier's final check is a single batched pairing equation:

\[ e(W_\zeta + u\cdot W_{\zeta\omega},\,[x]_2) = e(r\cdot[u]_1 - [W]_1,\,[1]_2) \]

for linearization scalar \(u\) and combined evaluation \(r\), keeping verification constant-size. A reference implementation of the grand-product accumulator in Python:

```python
def grand_product(a, b, c, s1, s2, s3, beta, gamma, omega, n, k1, k2, F):
    Z = [F(1)]
    for i in range(n):
        num = (a[i] + beta * omega**i + gamma)
        num *= (b[i] + beta * k1 * omega**i + gamma)
        num *= (c[i] + beta * k2 * omega**i + gamma)
        den = (a[i] + beta * s1[i] + gamma)
        den *= (b[i] + beta * s2[i] + gamma)
        den *= (c[i] + beta * s3[i] + gamma)
        Z.append(Z[-1] * num * F(1) / den)
    assert Z[n] == F(1), "copy constraints violated"
    return Z
```

Zero knowledge is obtained by adding random blinding multiples of \(Z_H(X)\) to each wire polynomial, hiding the witness behind the evaluations the verifier never sees [2].

### 4.2 KZG Commitments vs. FRI Low-Degree Testing

KZG and FRI solve the same problem — binding a prover to a low-degree polynomial — under opposite assumptions:

- **KZG** (pairing-based): commitment \(C = g^{f(\tau)}\) is a single group element; an evaluation proof \(\pi = g^{q(\tau)}\) for \(q(X) = (f(X)-f(z))/(X-z)\) verifies via \(e(C - g^{f(z)}, g) = e(\pi, g^{\tau - z})\). Proof size is *constant* (48 bytes on BLS12-381), but the SRS must contain \(g^{\tau^i}\) for \(i\) up to the maximum degree, generated in a trusted (multi-party) ceremony. If \(\tau\) leaks, soundness collapses entirely.
- **FRI** (hash-based): the prover commits to evaluations of \(f\) over a domain \(L_0\) via Merkle root, then *folds*: given challenge \(\alpha\), \(f_{i+1}(x^2) = \tfrac{1}{2}(f_i(x) + f_i(-x)) + \tfrac{\alpha}{2x}(f_i(x) - f_i(-x))\), halving the domain and degree each layer until a constant. The verifier spot-checks consistency along \(\lambda\) random paths. No secrets exist to leak — the setup is a hash function.

The price of transparency is size: a FRI proof carries \(\lambda\) Merkle paths of length \(\log|L_0|\), i.e. \(O(\lambda \log d)\) hashes, typically tens to hundreds of kilobytes, versus KZG's hundreds of bytes. Conversely FRI needs no pairings and no elliptic-curve arithmetic at all — it works over any field with a large smooth multiplicative subgroup, e.g. the 64-bit Goldilocks field, where NTTs are dramatically faster than on pairing curves.

> **Theorem (FRI soundness, informal).** If \(f_0 : L_0 \to \mathbb{F}\) is \(\delta\)-far from every Reed–Solomon codeword of rate \(\rho\), then one FRI query round rejects with probability at least \(\delta(1-\rho)/2 - o(1)\); with \(\lambda\) independent queries the soundness error is \(\approx (1 - \delta(1-\rho)/2)^\lambda\). DEEP-FRI sampling tightens this to conjectured \((1-\delta)^\lambda\) [6].

Round-by-round soundness analyses confirm FRI's security under standard proximity-gap assumptions, with recent work extending guarantees beyond the unique-decoding radius [9].

### 4.3 STARKs: AIR Arithmetization and the IOP Compiler

STARK arithmetization uses **AIR (Algebraic Intermediate Representation)**: computation becomes an execution trace — a table with \(w\) columns (registers) and \(T\) rows (steps) — constrained by *transition polynomials* \(C_i\) of bounded degree \(d\) that must vanish on every pair of consecutive rows, plus *boundary constraints* fixing public inputs and outputs. This is ideal for uniform, repetitive computation (VMs, hash chains, zkEVM step functions) where the same transition repeats \(T\) times.

The STARK pipeline:

1. **Trace interpolation**: each column becomes a polynomial over a subgroup of size \(T\).
2. **Low-degree extension**: evaluate on a larger domain (blowup factor \(1/\rho\), typically 4–16×) to form the Reed–Solomon codeword.
3. **Composition polynomial**: combine all transition constraints into one via random linear combination (the ALI protocol), then divide by the transition vanishing polynomial.
4. **FRI**: prove the composition polynomial has degree \(< \rho|L|\).
5. **Fiat–Shamir**: hash the Merkle roots to derive all challenges non-interactively.

AIR's regularity is both its strength and weakness: uniform steps give tiny per-step prover overhead and natural recursion, but irregular control flow (as in general circuits) must be padded into the trace, wasting prover work. Plonkish arithmetization is the mirror image — flexible per-gate semantics at the cost of a trusted setup [2].

A minimal Haskell sketch of the FRI verifier's query check:

```haskell
-- verify one FRI query path against layer commitments
verifyQuery :: [MerkleRoot] -> [Challenge] -> Field -> [(Field, AuthPath)] -> Bool
verifyQuery roots alphas x0 layers = and (zipWith3 checkLayer roots alphas (zip layers (tail layers)))
  where
    checkLayer root alpha ((y0, path0), (y1, path1)) =
      merkleVerify root x0 y0 path0
      && merkleVerify root (x0^(2::Int)) y1 path1
      && y1 == foldStep alpha x0 y0 y0'   -- y0' = sibling evaluation
    foldStep a x ev op = (ev + op) * inv 2 + a * (ev - op) * inv (2 * x)
```

### 4.4 Recursive Composition: Nova Folding Schemes and Accumulation

Recursion answers a scaling question: how do we prove \(\ell\) sequential steps without a proof linear in \(\ell\)? Two paradigms exist:

**Accumulation-based recursion** (Halo 2, BCMS20): the inner proof is a polynomial IOP whose verifier is expressed *inside* the next circuit, with an accumulator carrying deferred pairing checks. Halo 2 uses the IPA commitment (no trusted setup) over a cycle of curves so the verifier's curve arithmetic can itself be proved.

**Folding-based IVC** (Nova): instead of verifying a SNARK inside a SNARK, Nova *folds* two R1CS instance-witness pairs into one. Given a running relaxed-R1CS instance \(U\) and a fresh step instance \(u\), the prover sends a cross-term commitment \(\bar T\); both parties derive challenge \(r\) and compute the folded instance \(U' = U + r\cdot u\) (with \(\bar E' = \bar E + r\bar T + r^2\bar E_u\)). After \(\ell\) steps, a single relaxed-R1CS instance attests to the entire chain; one final SNARK (e.g. Spartan) compresses it to \(O(\log |F|)\) size [4].

Nova's breakthroughs: (i) **no SNARK in the loop** — folding needs no FFTs and no trusted setup; (ii) **minimal recursion overhead** — folding one step costs the circuit only *two scalar multiplications* on the curve; (iii) prover work per step is dominated by two multiexponentiations of size \(O(|F|)\), the fastest in the literature. HyperNova generalizes folding from R1CS to **CCS (customizable constraint systems)**, which subsumes Plonkish, R1CS, and AIR without overhead, and adds *CycleFold* for efficient curve-cycle instantiation [8].

```rust
// Nova-style fold of committed relaxed R1CS instances (simplified)
struct RelaxedInstance { comm_w: G1, comm_e: G1, u: Scalar, x: Vec<Scalar> }
fn fold(U: &RelaxedInstance, u: &RelaxedInstance, r: Scalar, t: G1) -> RelaxedInstance {
    RelaxedInstance {
        comm_w: U.comm_w + u.comm_w * r,            // W' = W + r*w
        comm_e: U.comm_e + t * r + u.comm_e * r * r, // E' = E + rT + r^2*E_u
        u: U.u + r * u.u,
        x: U.x.iter().zip(&u.x).map(|(a,b)| a + b*r).collect(),
    }
}
```

**FRI-based recursion** (Plonky2, Plonky3): STARK verifiers are mostly hashes and field arithmetic, so expressing a STARK verifier *inside* another STARK circuit is cheap — Polygon's Plonky2 recursion verifies proofs in ~tens of thousands of Goldilocks gates, enabling proof trees that aggregate thousands of proofs with fast, parallel proving.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Asymptotic Comparison

| System | Prover | Proof size | Verifier | Setup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Groth16 | \(O(n \log n)\) (\(n\) MSMs + FFTs) | ~192 B (3 \(\mathbb{G}_1\)) | \(O(m)\) + 3 pairings | Per-circuit trusted |
| PLONK (KZG) | \(O(n \log n)\), ~9–11 FFTs | ~400–600 B | \(O(1)\) pairings | Universal updatable SRS |
| STARK (FRI/AIR) | \(O(T \log^2 T)\) hashes | 45–200 KB | \(O(\log^2 T)\) | Transparent (hash) |
| Halo 2 (IPA) | \(O(n \log n)\) | \(O(\log n)\) KB | \(O(\log n)\) | Transparent (DLOG) |
| Nova IVC (per step) | 2 MSMs of size \(O(\|F\|)\) | \(O(\|F\|)\) → \(O(\log\|F\|)\) after compression | \(O(\|F\|)\) → succinct | Transparent (DLOG) |
| Plonky2 recursion | ~1–2 s per 2^20-gate proof (Goldilocks) | ~200 KB | ~ms (native), ~10^4 gates (in-circuit) | Transparent (hash) |

*(\(n\) = circuit gates, \(T\) = trace length, \(m\) = public inputs; concrete numbers from published benchmarks on commodity hardware.)*

### 5.2 Sketch: Knowledge Soundness of the PLONK Permutation Argument

We sketch why the grand-product check enforces copy constraints. Fix wire values \(\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}^{3n}\) and permutation \(\sigma\). Define \(f_i = v_i + \beta \omega^{\lfloor i/n \rfloor} + \gamma\) style terms (with coset shifts \(k_1, k_2\)) and \(g_i = v_i + \beta\sigma(i) + \gamma\). The prover's \(Z\) satisfies \(Z_{i+1} = Z_i \cdot f_i/g_i\) with \(Z_0 = 1\). If \(\mathbf{v}\) is \(\sigma\)-invariant, the multisets \(\{f_i\}\) and \(\{g_i\}\) coincide, the product telescopes, and \(Z_n = 1\). Conversely, if \(\mathbf{v}\) is not invariant, then as formal polynomials in \(\beta, \gamma\) the multisets differ, and by Schwartz–Zippel the verifier's random \(\beta, \gamma\) expose the mismatch except with probability \(\leq 3n/|\mathbb{F}|\). The extractor rewinds the algebraic prover to recover the wire polynomials, establishing knowledge soundness in the Algebraic Group Model [2].

### 5.3 Sketch: Nova IVC Completeness

Relaxed R1CS generalizes \(Az \circ Bz = Cz\) with an error vector \(E\) and scalar \(u\): \(Az \circ Bz = u\cdot Cz + E\). Folding preserves satisfiability: if \((U,W)\) and \((u,w)\) are satisfying with cross-term \(T = A(z_1 \circ z_2) + A(z_2 \circ z_1) - u_1 C z_2 - u_2 C z_1\), then the folded pair \((U', W')\) satisfies the relaxed equation by direct expansion in \(r\). Induction over \(\ell\) steps yields IVC completeness; zero knowledge follows from the hiding commitments and a final zk-SNARK compression step [4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Trusted-setup fragility (KZG/PLONK).** Security rests on an SRS ceremony (e.g. Aztec's powers-of-tau). A compromised trapdoor \(\tau\) permits undetectable forgeries. Universal updatable SRS mitigates but does not remove this trust assumption; auditors must verify ceremony transcripts.
2. **Proof-size gap.** STARK proofs are 100–500× larger than PLONK proofs. For on-chain verification where calldata is priced per byte, this dominates cost; STARK verification gas routinely exceeds pairing-based alternatives by an order of magnitude.
3. **Prover memory.** Both FFT-based (PLONK) and Merkle-tree-based (STARK) provers need \(O(n)\) or \(O(T/\rho)\) memory with large constants; GPU/FPGA acceleration helps MSMs and NTTs but Merkleization remains bandwidth-bound.
4. **Quantum posture.** KZG, IPA, and Nova rely on discrete-log hardness and fall to Shor's algorithm; only hash-based FRI/STARK constructions are plausibly post-quantum — at the cost of the size gap above.
5. **Recursion ergonomics.** Expressing verifiers in-circuit demands curve cycles (Nova, Halo 2) or arithmetization-friendly fields (Plonky2); mismatched fields incur expensive non-native arithmetic, and soundness of the composed system requires careful Fiat–Shamir domain separation across layers.
6. **AIR rigidity.** Uniform execution traces penalize irregular programs; Plonkish custom gates and lookup arguments (UltraPlonk, plookup) recover efficiency but reintroduce trusted-setup dependence.

---

## 7 Conclusion

PLONK's permutation argument and FRI's low-degree testing represent two answers to the same question — how to bind a prover to structured computation — optimized for opposite ends of the trust/efficiency spectrum. KZG-compiled Plonkish systems deliver minimal proof size and constant-time verification at the price of a universal trusted setup; FRI-compiled AIR systems deliver transparency and post-quantum plausibility at the price of proof size. Recursive composition, and Nova's folding paradigm in particular, shows the dichotomy is not the end of the story: by replacing SNARK verification with algebraic folding, IVC achieves the smallest known recursion overhead with no trusted setup and no FFTs, while HyperNova's CCS folding unifies Plonkish, R1CS, and AIR under one recursive framework. The practical frontier is now hybrid: STARK-proven steps folded or aggregated by FRI recursion, compressed by a final succinct SNARK — systems that inherit transparency where it matters and succinctness where it counts.
