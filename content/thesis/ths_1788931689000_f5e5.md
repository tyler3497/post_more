# Deterministic Network Calculus: Min-Plus Algebra, Arrival and Service Curves, and Tight Delay Bounds for Time-Sensitive Networking

## Abstract

## 1 Introduction

Modern safety-critical systems — automotive zonal architectures, industrial control loops, avionics backbones, and the TSN–5G converged edge — require *deterministic* communication: provable, per-packet, worst-case delay bounds holding under every admissible arrival pattern. Stochastic models cannot certify a braking command or a robot control loop; only a calculus of *guarantees* can. Deterministic network calculus, pioneered by Cruz in his two-part 1991 "calculus for network delay" [3] and systematized into (min, +) algebra by Chang [10], Le Boudec, and Thiran [1], provides exactly that: an algebra in which flows are cumulative functions, servers are linear operators, and performance bounds are closed-form expressions in arrival and service curves.

The central move of the theory is to replace classical queueing analysis — which tracks *events* — with an analysis of *functions*. A flow is its cumulative arrival function $A(t)$; a server is a *service curve* $\beta$ via $D \ge A \otimes \beta$; traffic is constrained by an *arrival curve* $\alpha$ with $A \otimes \alpha \ge A$, i.e. $A(t) - A(s) \le \alpha(t-s)$ [1][2]. Delay and backlog bounds are *horizontal and vertical deviations* between $\alpha$ and $\beta$, the output flow inherits $\alpha^* = \alpha \oslash \beta$, and a tandem collapses into one server whose service curve is the convolution of the parts [2][4].

Section 2 establishes the background: the (min, +) semiring, arrival and service curves, and standard curve families. Section 3 describes the analysis methodology. Section 4 is a deep dive into the algebraic core — the three fundamental bounds and their tightness, tandem analysis and PBOO, the SFA/PMOO/TFA family for feed-forward networks, and leftover service with shapers and packetizers. Section 5 applies the theory to TSN, deriving service curves for TAS, CBS, ATS, and CQF with a full numerical example. Section 6 confronts the limitations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The (min, +) Dioid

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the nondecreasing functions $f: \mathbb{R}^+ \to \mathbb{R}^+ \cup \{+\infty\}$, and $\mathcal{F}_0$ those with $f(0) = 0$. Define [2][5]:

- **Aggregation:** $(f \oplus g)(t) = \min(f(t), g(t))$.
- **Convolution:** $(f \otimes g)(t) = \inf_{0 \le s \le t} \{f(t-s) + g(s)\}$.
- **Deconvolution:** $(f \oslash g)(t) = \sup_{u \ge 0} \{f(t+u) - g(u)\}$.

$(\mathcal{F}, \oplus, \otimes)$ is a *dioid*: $\oplus$ commutative and idempotent with neutral element $+\infty$; $\otimes$ associative with neutral element $\delta_0$; $\otimes$ distributes over $\oplus$. The order $f \le g \iff f \oplus g = f$ is pointwise domination — smaller arrival curves and larger service curves tighten bounds. All proofs rest on *isotonicity* and the *residuation* adjunction $(f \otimes g \le h) \Leftrightarrow (f \le h \oslash g)$, the sense in which deconvolution inverts convolution [2]. Pitfall: $\oslash$ is neither commutative nor associative in the naive sense [4].

### 2.2 Cumulative Functions, Arrival Curves, Service Curves

Consider a flow at a network element's ingress with *cumulative function* $A(t)$ (total data in $[0, t)$) and departures $D(t)$, both in $\mathcal{F}$, lossless so $D \le A$. Two geometric deviations carry the performance semantics [1]:

- **Backlog** at time $t$: $b(t) = A(t) - D(t)$, the *vertical* deviation.
- **Virtual delay** at time $t$: $d(t) = \inf\{\tau \ge 0 : A(t) \le D(t+\tau)\}$, the *horizontal* deviation.

A flow is *$\alpha$-constrained* (has arrival curve $\alpha$) if for all $0 \le s \le t$, $A(t) - A(s) \le \alpha(t-s)$, equivalently $A \otimes \alpha \ge A$. The canonical arrival curve is the *leaky-bucket* (affine) curve $\alpha(t) = \min(M + pt, rt + b)$ for $t \ge 0$, where $M$ is the maximum packet size, $p$ the peak rate, $b$ the burst tolerance, and $r$ the sustainable rate — the IntServ TSPEC 4-tuple $(p, M, r, b)$ [1]. Concave piecewise-linear arrival curves are exactly the envelopes enforceable by cascades of leaky buckets [1].

A server *offers service curve* $\beta$ (a *minimum* service curve) if $D \ge A \otimes \beta$ for all admissible arrivals. The canonical family is the *rate-latency* curve $\beta_{R,T}(t) = R \cdot [t - T]^+$, modeling a link of rate $R$ with scheduling latency $T$. A constant-rate work-conserving server of capacity $C$ offers $\beta(t) = Ct$ in the fluid model; with packetization the tight curve is $\beta(t) = [Ct - L^{\max}]^+$, where $L^{\max}$ is the maximum packet length [2][5].

### 2.3 Historical Lineage

The theory descends from Cruz's 1991 two-part calculus: Part I analyzed network elements in isolation, Part II composed them into network-wide bounds via *network service curves* [3]. Le Boudec's application to IntServ guaranteed-service networks [1] and the Le Boudec–Thiran monograph made it an engineering discipline with algorithmic content [2][7]; modern work extends it to TSN/DetNet, avionics (AFDX), networks-on-chip, and TSN–5G convergence [6][8][10].

## 3 Methodology

A DNC analysis proceeds in four methodological steps, each justified by a theorem.

**Step 1 — Constrain the arrivals.** For each flow $f$, determine an arrival curve $\alpha^f$: from a *traffic contract* (TSPEC, TSN stream reservation), a *regulator* (a greedy shaper enforces $\alpha$ by construction, $D = A \otimes \sigma$ [1]), or a measurement-based empirical envelope with its concave hull [1]. Flows sharing a link aggregate by ordinary sum $\sum_f \alpha^f$.

**Step 2 — Characterize the servers.** Derive a minimum service curve $\beta$ per element: from first principles (work-conserving link $\Rightarrow$ rate-latency), by *leftover service* ($\beta^{\text{res}} = [\beta - \alpha^{\text{cross}}]^+$ for cross-traffic $\alpha^{\text{cross}}$ [5][8]), or by modeling a shaper as an exact-service element.

**Step 3 — Compose.** For servers $1, \dots, H$ in series, the *concatenation theorem* gives $\beta^{\text{e2e}} = \beta^1 \otimes \cdots \otimes \beta^H$ [2][4] — the *pay bursts only once* (PBOO) principle: bounding against $\beta^{\text{e2e}}$ is no worse than summing per-node bounds [2].

**Step 4 — Bound.** From $\alpha$ and $\beta$ [2][5][7]:

> **Theorem (Performance bounds).** Let a flow with arrival curve $\alpha$ traverse a server offering service curve $\beta$. Then:
> 1. **Backlog:** $b(t) \le \sup_{s \ge 0}\{\alpha(s) - \beta(s)\} = (\alpha \oslash \beta)(0) =: v(\alpha, \beta)$, the *vertical deviation*.
> 2. **Delay:** $d(t) \le \inf\{\tau \ge 0 : \forall s \ge 0,\ \alpha(s) \le \beta(s+\tau)\} =: h(\alpha, \beta)$, the *horizontal deviation*.
> 3. **Output:** the departure flow has arrival curve $\alpha^* = \alpha \oslash \beta$.

Moreover, these bounds are *tight*: for given $\alpha, \beta$ one can construct admissible $A, D$ attaining them [2]. Tightness is what elevates DNC from a bag of inequalities to a *calculus* — the output curve $\alpha^*$ of one stage is the input curve of the next, so bounds propagate through topologies without loss of the worst-case semantics [7].

For feed-forward networks with interfering flows, three algebraic strategies compete. **TFA** (total flow analysis) bounds each server under the aggregate and sums per-node delays; **SFA** (separated flow analysis) computes per-flow residual service per node, then applies PBOO; **PMOO** (pay multiplexing only once) convolves servers *before* subtracting cross-traffic, paying the multiplexing penalty once [5]. Tightness ranking: PMOO $\le$ SFA $\le$ TFA, at increasing algebraic cost.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Three Fundamental Bounds, Precisely

Let us fix notation and prove the bounds in the form used by implementations. Work in $\mathcal{F}_0$ with the convention $f(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$ where needed.

> **Theorem (Backlog bound).** If $A$ is $\alpha$-constrained and the server offers $\beta$, then for all $t$, $A(t) - D(t) \le \sup_{s \ge 0} \{\alpha(s) - \beta(s)\}$.

*Proof sketch.* From $A \otimes \alpha \ge A$ and $D \ge A \otimes \beta$:
$$A(t) - D(t) \le \sup_{0 \le s \le t}\{A(t) - A(t-s) - \beta(s)\} \le \sup_{s \ge 0}\{\alpha(s) - \beta(s)\},$$
using $A(t) - A(t-s) \le \alpha(s)$. ∎

> **Theorem (Delay bound).** Under the same hypotheses, $d(t) \le h(\alpha, \beta) = \inf\{\tau \ge 0 : \alpha(s) \le \beta(s+\tau)\ \forall s \ge 0\}$.

*Proof sketch.* The virtual delay $d(t)$ is the smallest $\tau$ with $A(t) \le D(t+\tau)$. From $D \ge A \otimes \beta$ and the arrival constraint, a standard manipulation shows $A(t) \le D(t + h)$ for $h = h(\alpha, \beta)$: the shifted service curve dominates the arrival curve pointwise, so the infimum defining $D(t+h)$ can absorb the burst [1][5]. ∎

> **Theorem (Output characterization).** The departures satisfy $D(t) - D(s) \le (\alpha \oslash \beta)(t-s)$: the output flow has arrival curve $\alpha^* = \alpha \oslash \beta$.

This is the engine of *envelope propagation*: bursts grow as flows traverse servers, and $\alpha \oslash \beta$ quantifies exactly how much [2][7].

**Worked miniature.** Take $\alpha(t) = b + rt$ and $\beta(t) = R[t-T]^+$, $R > r$. Then $v(\alpha,\beta) = b + rT$, $h(\alpha,\beta) = T + b/R$, and $\alpha^*(t) = b + rT + rt$: delay decomposes into a *latency term* $T$ and a *burst term* $b/R$, the burst paying the rate exactly once.

```python
import numpy as np

def minplus_conv(f, g):
    n = len(f)
    return np.array([np.min(f[t::-1][:t+1] + g[:t+1]) for t in range(n)])

def backlog_bound(alpha, beta):
    return np.max(alpha - beta)          # v(alpha, beta)

def delay_bound(alpha, beta, dt):
    n = len(alpha)
    for k in range(n):                   # smallest shift tau = k*dt
        if np.all(alpha <= np.r_[np.zeros(k), beta[:n-k]]):
            return k * dt
    return np.inf
```

### 4.2 Tandem Analysis: Concatenation and Pay Bursts Only Once

> **Theorem (Concatenation / PBOO).** If server $i$ offers $\beta^i$ ($i = 1, 2$), the tandem offers $\beta^{\text{e2e}} = \beta^1 \otimes \beta^2$.

*Proof.* $D_2 \ge D_1 \otimes \beta^2 \ge (A \otimes \beta^1) \otimes \beta^2 = A \otimes (\beta^1 \otimes \beta^2)$ by isotonicity and associativity of $\otimes$ [2][4]. ∎

The economic content is the inequality $h(\alpha, \beta^1 \otimes \beta^2) \le h(\alpha, \beta^1) + h(\alpha \oslash \beta^1, \beta^2)$: the left side charges the burst $b$ once, while the right side charges it at every hop, because the output curve $\alpha \oslash \beta^1$ carries the burst inflated by the first node's latency [2]. For $H$ identical rate-latency servers with latency $T$ and a leaky-bucket flow, PBOO gives $d \le HT + b/R$ versus $H(T + b/R)$ for naive summation — a factor-$H$ improvement on the burst term, decisive in deep TSN topologies [8].

A subtlety: convolution is commutative, but *residual* service curves are not symmetric, and FIFO multiplexing breaks the clean picture (Section 6). The *sub-additive closure* $\bar{\alpha} = \inf_{n \ge 1} \alpha^{(n)}$ is needed whenever $\alpha \otimes \alpha \ne \alpha$; assuming idempotency without verifying sub-additivity is a classic proof bug [2].

### 4.3 Leftover Service, Shapers, and Packetizers

In real networks a flow of interest shares servers with cross-traffic. The *leftover service* theorem is the workhorse:

> **Theorem (Leftover service, arbitrary multiplexing).** If an aggregate with arrival curve $\alpha^{\text{agg}} = \alpha^f + \alpha^{\text{cross}}$ traverses a server offering $\beta$, then flow $f$ alone is guaranteed $\beta^f = [\beta - \alpha^{\text{cross}}]^+$.

*Proof idea.* During any backlog period of the aggregate, the server emits at least $\beta$; cross-traffic can consume at most $\alpha^{\text{cross}}$, so the remainder — truncated at zero — lower-bounds what $f$ receives [5][8]. Recent TSN–5G analyses use exactly this form for multi-priority residual service: $\beta_{\text{res}}(t) = [\beta(t) - \sum_{f} \alpha_f(t)]^+$ [8].

*Greedy shapers* are the dual primitive: a shaper with shaping curve $\sigma$ transforms $A$ into $D = A \otimes \sigma$, i.e. it offers the *exact* service curve $\sigma$. A shaper after a multiplexer *removes* the burstiness increase inflicted by interference, at the cost of a *shaping delay* bounded by $h(\alpha^{\text{in}}, \sigma)$ [1][6] — the analytic justification for TSN's Asynchronous Traffic Shaping (ATS): per-flow or interleaved regulators after multiplexing stages restore per-flow envelopes and recover scalability [6]. The interleaved regulator (IR) — one FIFO queue for an aggregate, each flow with its own shaping curve — was only recently given exact network-calculus service curves, with direct application to IEEE 802.1Qcr ATS [6].

*Packetizers* convert fluid bounds to packet reality: a packetizer with maximum packet length $L^{\max}$ adds at most $L^{\max}/R$ to delay bounds and is exactly modeled by the curve adjustments noted in Section 2.2 [2].

### 4.4 Service Curves for TSN Mechanisms

TSN standardizes a toolbox of egress mechanisms, each with a distinct DNC model [9][10]:

| Mechanism (IEEE) | Principle | Service-curve model $\beta(t)$ |
|---|---|---|
| Time-Aware Shaper, 802.1Qbv (TAS) | TDMA-like gate schedule, period $T_{\text{cyc}}$, open windows $W$ per cycle | Staircase: $\beta(t) = R \cdot \sum_k [\,t - kT_{\text{cyc}}\, \text{ clipped to } W\,]^+$; often lower-bounded by a rate-latency curve with $T = T_{\text{cyc}} - W + \text{GB}$ |
| Credit-Based Shaper, 802.1Qav (CBS) | Per-class credit, idleSlope/sendSlope | Rate-latency $\beta_{R,T}$ with $R = \text{idleSlope}$, $T$ from credit bounds and interference |
| Async Traffic Shaping, 802.1Qcr (ATS) | Per-flow/interleaved regulators, no global clock | Greedy shaper $\beta = \sigma$ (exact); removes burst cascade [6] |
| Cyclic Queuing & Forwarding, 802.1Qch (CQF) | Two queues alternate each cycle $T_c$ | Per-hop delay in $[T_c, 2T_c]$; end-to-end $= (H+1)T_c$ structurally |
| Frame Preemption, 802.1Qbu | Express frames preempt preemptable | Fragments guard band; reduces $T$ in TAS/CBS models |

For TAS, the gate-control list partitions each cycle into per-class open intervals; the exact service curve is a *staircase*, and worst-case latency includes the *guard band* (GB), sized to the maximum interfering frame [9]. In DNC terms the GB is absorbed into the latency term $T$ of a rate-latency lower bound; recent TSN–5G work models TAS with scheduling delay $T_0$, initial degradation $\delta_0$, and recovery rate $\omega$ [8].

CBS is the subtlest: credit evolves with idle/send slopes, and the worst case couples credit bounds with maximum higher-class interference; the resulting rate-latency curve's $T$ encodes the longest blockage while credit recovers [9][10]. NC-based worst-case delay computation for CBS is involved enough to serve as a benchmark for automated TSN timing analysis [10].

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

### 5.1 Worked End-to-End Example: Three-Hop TSN Line

A control-data flow (CDT class) traverses three TSN bridges in series:

- Flow: $\alpha(t) = b + rt$ with $b = 12\,\text{kbit}$, $r = 1\,\text{Mbps}$.
- Each bridge egress (TAS): $R = 1\,\text{Gbps}$, cycle $T_{\text{cyc}} = 500\,\mu\text{s}$, CDT window $W = 50\,\mu\text{s}$, guard band GB $= 12\,\mu\text{s}$ [9].
- Rate-latency lower bound per bridge: $T = T_{\text{cyc}} - W + \text{GB} = 462\,\mu\text{s}$, $\beta^i(t) = R\,[t - 462\,\mu\text{s}]^+$.

**PBOO computation.** $\beta^{\text{e2e}} = \beta^1 \otimes \beta^2 \otimes \beta^3 = R\,[t - 1.386\,\text{ms}]^+$. Delay bound:
$$d \le h(\alpha, \beta^{\text{e2e}}) = 1.386\,\text{ms} + \frac{12\,\text{kbit}}{1\,\text{Gbps}} \approx 1.398\,\text{ms}.$$

**Naive per-hop summation** gives $3 \times (462\,\mu\text{s} + 12\,\mu\text{s}) = 1.422\,\text{ms}$ — close here because the burst is small; with $b = 1.2\,\text{Mbit}$ PBOO gives $2.586\,\text{ms}$ versus $4.986\,\text{ms}$ naive, nearly $2\times$ better. PBOO's advantage scales with burstiness and path length [2][8].

**Backlog bound:** $v(\alpha, \beta^{\text{e2e}}) = b + r \cdot 1.386\,\text{ms} \approx 13.4\,\text{kbit}$ per bridge — dimensioning the CDT queue.

**With cross-traffic:** add an AVB aggregate $\alpha^{\text{AVB}}(t) = 200\,\text{kbit} + 100\,\text{Mbps}\cdot t$ under strict priority (CDT > AVB). In the TAS model the AVB traffic is excluded from the CDT window by construction (gates closed), so the residual curve is $\beta^{\text{res},i} = \beta^i$ — the formal DNC counterpart of "TAS isolates CDT from AVB/BE interference" [9]. Without TAS (pure strict priority), the residual curve would carry the AVB burst in its latency term ($T' = T + b_{\text{AVB}}/R$), and PMOO would then pay that multiplexing penalty once across the tandem rather than per hop [5].

### 5.2 Tightness and What "Empirical" Means for a Calculus

DNC theorems are *worst-case exact*: the bounds are tight in the sense that adversarial arrival/departure pairs attain them [2]. "Empirical validation" therefore takes a specific form:

1. **Simulation conformance:** discrete-event simulators (e.g. OMNeT++ TSN models) fed with worst-case traffic patterns produce delays approaching — never exceeding — the analytic bounds; the gap measures analysis pessimism, not unsoundness [9].
2. **Tightness case studies:** for leaky-bucket/rate-latency tandems, explicit adversarial traces (burst at $t=0$, then rate $r$) attain $h(\alpha, \beta^{\text{e2e}})$ exactly.
3. **Tool comparison:** algebraic DNC tools implementing TFA/SFA/PMOO on feed-forward benchmarks establish the ranking PMOO $\le$ SFA $\le$ TFA on bound values, with PMOO strictly improving SFA on nested-interference topologies [5].

### 5.3 Proof: PMOO Dominates SFA

> **Theorem.** For a feed-forward network, the PMOO delay bound is no larger than the SFA bound.

*Proof sketch.* SFA computes per-node residuals $\beta^{f,i} = [\beta^i - \sum_{\text{cross at } i} \alpha^{\text{cross},i}]^+$ and applies PBOO. PMOO convolves first and subtracts once. The key inequality $[\beta^1 \otimes \beta^2 - \alpha]^+ \ge [\beta^1 - \alpha]^+ \otimes [\beta^2 - \alpha]^+$ holds because convolution distributes the subtraction across both factors' latency terms; hence the PMOO end-to-end curve dominates the SFA curve pointwise, and $h(\alpha, \cdot)$ decreases in its second argument. ∎

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**The FIFO blind-multiplexing gap.** Leftover service assumes *arbitrary* multiplexing — the worst case over all interleavings. Under FIFO the analysis is far harder: exact FIFO residual service curves exist only for special cases (two flows, specific curve shapes) [5]. Since most TSN egresses are FIFO *within* a class, this is the largest source of avoidable pessimism in per-class analyses.

**Non-tightness of residual service.** $[\beta - \alpha^{\text{cross}}]^+$ is valid but generally non-tight: it assumes cross-traffic adversarially consumes service exactly when the flow of interest needs it. Tighter variants exist but leave the deterministic framework [5].

**Scalability of the algebra.** Min-plus convolution of piecewise-linear curves is quadratic in the number of segments; exact deconvolutions on large feed-forward topologies are expensive, so tools resort to rate-latency/affine approximations that trade tightness for speed [5]. Near-linear-time PMOO-style analysis for general curve families remains open.

**Non-feed-forward topologies.** The SFA/PMOO edifice assumes feed-forward routing. Cyclic dependencies require fixed-point methods (time-stopping, iterative bounds) whose convergence and tightness are poorly understood deterministically [5][7].

**TSN modeling fidelity.** Published TAS service curves abstract away gate-control-list *phase* relationships between consecutive bridges; coordinated schedules can beat the bounds significantly, and a DNC exploiting *global* schedule knowledge — rather than per-node worst cases — is an active frontier [8][9]. Frame preemption (802.1Qbu) fragments the guard band and its exact DNC model is still being refined [10].

**Packet-level and physical effects.** DNC is a fluid theory patched with packetizer terms; cut-through vs. store-and-forward, PHY overhead, and clock-synchronization error (802.1AS) enter only as latency constants. A unified treatment of synchronization uncertainty inside the (min, +) algebra does not yet exist.

## 7 Conclusion

Deterministic network calculus reduces worst-case performance to algebra: constrain arrivals with $\alpha$, characterize servers with $\beta$, and read delay, backlog, and output envelopes as horizontal deviation, vertical deviation, and deconvolution. Concatenation and PBOO make end-to-end analysis compositional; leftover service extends it to multiplexed networks; the SFA/PMOO hierarchy trades algebraic effort for tightness. Applied to TSN, the framework yields certifiable delay bounds for TAS, CBS, ATS, and CQF — the basis of TSN stream admission control and offline schedule synthesis [6][8][9][10].

Its limits are equally crisp: FIFO multiplexing resists tight analysis, residual service is pessimistic by construction, cyclic topologies need fixed points, and computational cost pushes tools toward approximations. These are the honest price of *determinism* — a bound holding for every admissible trace cannot exploit the average case. As safety-critical systems converge on TSN, DetNet, and TSN–5G integration, that price remains worth paying, and closing the tightness gaps is the central research agenda of the field.
