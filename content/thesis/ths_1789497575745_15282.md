# Coalgebraic Methods in Formal Verification: Bisimulation, Codata, and Coinductive Proof in Proof Assistants

## Abstract

Coalgebras provide a uniform mathematical account of state-based, potentially non-terminating behavior: an F-coalgebra is a pair (X, c) where c : X → F X observes rather than constructs states. This thesis surveys coalgebraic methods in formal verification, centered on bisimulation as the canonical notion of behavioral equivalence, codata as final coalgebras of polynomial functors, and coinductive proof as mechanized in contemporary proof assistants. We review the categorical core — final coalgebras, Aczel–Mendler bisimulations, and the coinduction principle dual to induction via the Knaster–Tarski theorem — alongside operational incarnations: guarded corecursion with the cofix tactic in Rocq/Coq, copatterns and sized types in Agda, and corecursion up-to in Isabelle/HOL. Case studies span stream calculi, process-calculus bisimilarity, coinductive operational semantics for divergence, and up-to techniques that scale coinductive proofs to realistic systems. We report mechanized results, analyze the limitations of syntactic guardedness, and trace the convergence of guarded recursion with step-indexed program logics.

##1 Introduction

Modern verification targets are reactive: operating-system schedulers, network protocols, stream processors, and compilers whose executions never terminate but must behave correctly forever. Inductive methods, which reason from base cases upward through finite constructions, are structurally mismatched to such systems — there is no base case, only perpetual observation. Coalgebra supplies the dual viewpoint. Where an algebra F X → X explains how to *build* data, a coalgebra X → F X explains how to *observe* it [1]. This single reversal reorganizes verification: specifications become descriptions of observable behavior, equivalence becomes bisimilarity, and proof becomes coinduction.

The coalgebraic program rests on three pillars. First, **bisimulation**: two states are equivalent when they admit a relation closed under observations, capturing Milner's insight that concurrent processes should be compared by what they do rather than how they are built. Second, **codata**: infinite structures such as streams, infinite trees, and perpetual processes arise as *final coalgebras* — universal domains into which every behavior maps uniquely. Third, **coinductive proof**: the proof principle dual to induction, mechanized in proof assistants through guarded corecursion, copatterns, and parameterized coinduction libraries.

This thesis makes five contributions: a self-contained exposition of the categorical foundations (functors, coalgebras, homomorphisms, finality, and the Knaster–Tarski duality of induction and coinduction); a comparative analysis of codata in Rocq/Coq, Agda, and Isabelle/HOL; mechanized case studies spanning streams, operational semantics, and compiler correctness; a treatment of up-to techniques from Sangiorgi's respectfulness to parameterized coinduction; and an honest assessment of limitations.

> Theorem: (Coinduction proof principle). Let (νF, out) be a final F-coalgebra. For any x, y in νF, if there exists a bisimulation R relating x and y, then x = y.

Section 2 reviews the background; Section 3 the methodology; Section 4 the theory in depth; Section 5 the empirical case studies; Section 6 the limitations; Section 7 concludes.

##2 Background

**Algebras versus coalgebras.** Work in Set or a category of types. An endofunctor F assigns to each object X an object F X and to each arrow f : X → Y an arrow F f : F X → F Y, preserving identities and composition. An *F-algebra* is a pair (X, a) with a : F X → X: it folds structure into values, the familiar setting of initial-algebra semantics for inductive datatypes. An *F-coalgebra* is a pair (X, c) with c : X → F X: it unfolds a state into one step of observable behavior [1]. Deterministic automata are coalgebras for F X = 2 × X^A, pairing acceptance with transitions; labeled transition systems are coalgebras for F X = P(A × X); streams over A are coalgebras for F X = A × X.

**Homomorphisms and finality.** A homomorphism f : (X, c) → (Y, d) is a map f : X → Y with d ∘ f = F f ∘ c, preserving one-step behavior. A *final* coalgebra (νF, out) admits exactly one homomorphism from every coalgebra — it is the universe of all abstract behaviors. Concretely, the set A^ω of streams with out(σ) = (head σ, tail σ) is final for F X = A × X: any stream automaton maps uniquely into it by recording successive observations [1].

**Bisimulation.** Aczel and Mendler define a bisimulation between (X, c) and (Y, d) as a relation R ⊆ X × Y with γ : R → F R making the projections homomorphisms. For functors preserving weak pullbacks — including all polynomial functors — this coincides with relation lifting: R is a bisimulation when related states have related observations, i.e., (x, y) ∈ R implies (c(x), d(y)) ∈ Rel(F)(R). Two states are *bisimilar* when some bisimulation relates them; on a final coalgebra, bisimilarity coincides with equality. This yields the coinduction proof principle stated in Section 1: to prove equality of infinite objects, exhibit a bisimulation — never inspect the infinite objects directly.

**Knaster–Tarski and duality.** On a complete lattice, a monotone operator Φ has a complete lattice of fixed points. The *least* fixed point μΦ is the intersection of all pre-fixed points (Φ(X) ⊆ X) — the inductive reading, supporting proof by induction. The *greatest* fixed point νΦ is the union of all post-fixed points (X ⊆ Φ(X)) — the coinductive reading, supporting proof by coinduction: to show x ∈ νΦ it suffices to exhibit a post-fixed point X containing x, that is, a set closed under one step of Φ. Bisimilarity is the greatest fixed point of the monotone operator B(R) = {(x, y) | observations related by Rel(F)(R)}; proving bisimilarity is therefore proving membership in a greatest fixed point [5].

**Behavioral differential equations.** Rutten's stream calculus specifies streams by initial value σ(0) and derivative σ′, defines operators such as sum and convolution product by such equations, and proves their identities by exhibiting bisimulations up to algebraic structure [1] — the coalgebraic analogue of structural recursion.

##3 Methodology

Coalgebraic verification proceeds in five disciplined steps.

1. **Model the system as a coalgebra.** Identify the functor of observations — e.g., F X = P(Act × X) for a protocol, F X = A × X for a stream processor. The functor choice fixes what counts as observable.

2. **Specify behaviorally.** Properties are stated as relations on states or as predicates on the final coalgebra. Safety and liveness over infinite executions become coinductive predicates: "diverges", "never deadlocks", and "produces infinitely many outputs" are greatest fixed points of monotone rule functionals.

3. **Prove by exhibiting bisimulations.** The core proof act is relational: propose a candidate R, then verify the progression condition R ⊆ B(R). In a proof assistant this becomes either a guarded cofix proof or an explicit bisimulation relation together with a once-and-for-all lemma that bisimilarity implies equality [8].

4. **Scale with up-to techniques.** Raw bisimulations are often infinite or unwieldy. Up-to techniques close the candidate relation under context, transitivity, or congruence without additional proof burden, shrinking obligations dramatically. Soundness of each technique must itself be established — the subject of Section 4.4.

5. **Mechanize with productivity guarantees.** Every corecursive definition must be productive: each finite observation must be computable in finite time. Enforcement differs per assistant — syntactic guardedness in Rocq/Coq, sized types in Agda, friendliness in Isabelle/HOL — and the methodology adapts accordingly.

This methodology is *dual* to inductive verification at every step: construction is replaced by observation, least fixed points by greatest, termination by productivity.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Final Coalgebras and the Coinduction Principle

The final coalgebra theorem legitimizes codata: for polynomial functors, and more generally accessible functors on locally presentable categories, the final coalgebra exists as the limit of 1 ← F 1 ← F² 1 ← ⋯. Lambek's lemma states that the structure map out : νF → F(νF) of a final coalgebra is an isomorphism: infinite behavior is *fully* described by one-step observation plus the behavior of successors — no information is lost or gained in unfolding.

Categorically, coinduction is the universal property: finality delivers the unique morphism from any coalgebra into the universe of behaviors, identifying bisimilar objects:

> Theorem: (Bisimulation implies equality). Let R be a bisimulation on the final coalgebra (νF, out) with (x, y) ∈ R. Then x = y in νF.

The proof is a one-line categorical argument — the projections of R are homomorphisms into νF, and finality forces them equal — yet its mechanization is where all the difficulty lives, because proof assistants must check the *productivity* of the relation's unfolding. The gap between the platonic principle and its syntactic enforcement is the central engineering problem of coinductive proof [3][9].

### 4.2 Bisimulation: From Process Calculi to Verified Compilers

Bisimulation entered verification through concurrency: Milner's CCS equates processes when each matches the other's transitions — *strong bisimilarity* — with the weak variant abstracting from silent steps. Applicative, environmental, probabilistic, and timed bisimilarities all instantiate the same coalgebraic pattern with different functors [5].

The π-calculus has been formalized in higher-order abstract syntax inside Coq, yielding a proof environment in which a substantial chapter of strong late bisimilarity was proved formally, with coinductive types giving proofs widely regarded as more natural than the on-paper originals [11]. Name-passing concurrency — binding plus fresh-name generation — is exactly where the coalgebraic discipline pays off.

A landmark application is compiler correctness. Leroy and Grall gave the operational semantics of a small imperative language coinductively: terminating evaluations by the usual inductive big-step rules, diverging evaluations by coinductive rules, unifying both in a single judgment [7]. Semantic preservation becomes a simulation — a one-sided bisimulation — between source and target behaviors, including infinite ones. With divergence defined coinductively rather than via domain theory, the development mechanizes in Rocq/Coq with modest means: syntactic definitions, constructive logic, classical axioms only where marked [7]. This is the template for verified compilers treating non-termination as first-class behavior.

### 4.3 Codata in Proof Assistants: Guardedness, Copatterns, and Sized Types

**Rocq/Coq: syntactic guardedness.** Coinductive types are introduced by CoInductive declarations; corecursive values by cofix. The kernel accepts a cofix only when every corecursive call is *guarded* — a direct argument of a constructor. This decidable criterion is incomplete: productive definitions such as tree mirror, whose recursion sits under map, are rejected [3]. The cofix tactic introduces the coinductive hypothesis, usable only on constructor-guarded subgoals; the Guarded command rechecks the proof term at Qed, and automation often produces unguarded uses whose failure surfaces only there [9]. Two engineering responses have emerged. First, the *bisimulation-relation style*: define an explicit relation R, prove it a bisimulation, and prove once and for all that bisimulations imply equality — moving the guardedness burden out of every proof and into a single lemma [8]. Second, *first-order guarded coinduction*: a Coq plugin elaborates user-friendly coinduction principles into guarded proof terms automatically, choosing the principle from the shape of the goal [9].

```coq
CoInductive Stream (A : Type) : Type :=
| SCons : A -> Stream A -> Stream A.

CoInductive stream_eq {A} : Stream A -> Stream A -> Prop :=
| stream_eq_intro : forall x s t,
    stream_eq s t -> stream_eq (SCons x s) (SCons x t).

Lemma map_id : forall (A : Type) (s : Stream A),
  stream_eq (smap (fun x => x) s) s.
Proof.
  cofix CH. intros A [x s'].
  simpl. constructor. apply CH.
Qed.
```

**Agda: copatterns and sized types.** Agda replaced delay-and-force coinduction with *coinductive records* and *copatterns*: infinite values are defined by observation rather than construction [4]. The termination checker doubles as a productivity checker, requiring corecursive calls to be guarded by record constructors; *sized types* refine this by indexing codata with a bound on how many observations are guaranteed, admitting definitions — such as stream processors that consume input before producing output — that plain guardedness rejects.

```agda
record Stream (A : Set) : Set where
  coinductive
  field head : A
        tail : Stream A

zipWith : (A → B → C) → Stream A → Stream B → Stream C
head (zipWith f s t) = f (head s) (head t)
tail (zipWith f s t) = zipWith f (tail s) (tail t)
```

Copatterns make the coalgebra structure literal: a stream *is* its observations, and defining a stream is defining a coalgebra [4].

**Isabelle/HOL: corecursion up-to.** The (co)datatype package supports codatatypes with *corecursion up-to*: definitions may call already-registered *friendly* operations beneath constructors, where friendliness is a semantic productivity condition discharged once per operation [10]. Reasoning uses *coinduction up-to*, the proof dual. This trades Coq's rigid syntax for an extensible, semantically justified framework — at the cost of a heavier metatheory.

| Assistant | Codata | Corecursion check | Coinduction style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rocq/Coq | CoInductive types | syntactic guardedness by constructors | cofix; bisimulation relations; elaborating plugins [9] |
| Agda | coinductive records | productivity checker; sized types | copatterns; definitional unfolding [4] |
| Isabelle/HOL | codatatype package | friendly operations | corecursion and coinduction up-to [10] |

### 4.4 Up-To Techniques and Parameterized Coinduction

A bisimulation candidate for realistic systems is rarely closed under progression on the nose; it is closed *up to* something — transitivity, context, bisimilarity itself. An *up-to technique* is a function A on relations such that R ⊆ B(A(R)) still implies R ⊆ νB. Not every A is sound — the classic counterexample is weak bisimulation up to weak bisimilarity — so the theory characterizes the sound ones. Sangiorgi's *respectfulness* and Pous's *compatibility* (A ∘ B ⊆ B ∘ A) give compositional criteria: compatible techniques compose, forming a rich dictionary of enhancements usable in combination [5][6].

The categorical account pushes further: in an ordered setting, up-to techniques are themselves coalgebras, and parameterized coinduction internalizes the enhancement into the proof rule. The Paco library for Coq implements *parameterized coinduction*: coinductive predicates take an extra parameter accumulating knowledge, and the proof rule permits strengthening that parameter by any *compatible* closure — recovering up-to reasoning inside the assistant with machine-checked soundness [9]. Bonchi, Petrișan, Pous, and Rot give the general theory: coinduction up-to instantiates a broader categorical pattern covering lattice-theoretic and fibrational presentations [6]. In practice these techniques make coinduction scale from textbook streams to compilers: they shrink candidates from infinite closures to small, comprehensible cores.

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

**Case study 1: streams and Moessner's theorem.** Niqui and Rutten's coinductive proof of Moessner's theorem — that a sieve-and-sum construction on the naturals yields the stream of powers — was formalized in Coq by Krebbers, Parlant, and Silva as a nontrivial exercise in coinductive reasoning [8]. Two styles were compared: the bisimulation-relation style, which establishes "bisimulation implies equality" once, and the elementwise style via the nth-element function. The former concentrates guardedness in one lemma; the latter avoids coinduction but sacrifices the equational character. Notably, the same bisimulation proved generalizations of the original theorem — a recurring dividend: the right relation proves more than was asked [8].

**Case study 2: coinductive operational semantics.** Leroy and Grall's development defines big-step evaluation coinductively, proves it equivalent to small-step reduction sequences (finite and infinite), uses it to state type soundness, and proves semantic preservation for compilation down to an abstract machine — all inside Coq [7]. Its logical requirements are strikingly *modest*: syntactic definitions, constructive logic, classical axioms only where marked. Divergence — traditionally the province of domain theory — becomes a coinductive predicate usable by working engineers. This is the empirical core of the thesis: coinduction is the right tool for the most practically important infinite behavior, non-termination.

**Case study 3: guarded recursion and adequacy.** Clouston, Bizjak, Grathwohl, and Birkedal's guarded λ-calculus internalizes productivity in the type system via the later modality and Löb induction; its denotational semantics in the topos of trees yields an adequacy theorem: well-typed programs evaluate as their denotations prescribe [2]. The accompanying program logic reasons about contextual equivalence of coinductive programs — the meeting point of guarded recursion and step-indexed logical relations that underpins modern program logics.

**A worked proof.** The proof exhibits R = {(map f (map g s), map (f ∘ g) s) | s a stream} and checks progression: heads agree by definition, tails stay in R by the coinductive hypothesis. In Coq this is a compact cofix proof; relationally, it is a proof that R is a bisimulation plus bisimulation-implies-equality. Either way, no infinite object is ever inspected — only one-step observations and closure.

```haskell
data Stream a = SCons a (Stream a)

-- The final coalgebra: every A-valued stream coalgebra maps uniquely into it.
unfold :: (s -> (a, s)) -> s -> Stream a
unfold step s = let (x, s') = step s in SCons x (unfold step s')
```

**Synthesis.** A pattern emerges: coalgebraic formalization demands more up front — functor, relation lifting, and productivity made explicit — but pays off in *compositionality*. Bisimulations compose, up-to techniques combine, finality supplies canonical representatives. The record shows coinduction scaling precisely where induction cannot reach: the infinite.

##6 Limitations

**Syntactic guardedness is incomplete.** Coq rejects productive definitions whose corecursion passes through auxiliary functions — tree mirror, with recursion under map, fails the guard check [3]. Semantic alternatives (complete partial orders, sized types, friendly operations) recover expressiveness at the cost of heavier obligations.

**cofix is fragile in practice.** The coinductive hypothesis's restricted usage interacts badly with automation: tactics apply it in unguarded positions, with failure surfacing only at Qed, far from the offending step [9]. Coinductive proofs are brittle under maintenance in a way inductive ones are not.

**Up-to techniques demand metatheory.** Each enhancement needs a compatibility proof; the dictionary must be built and trusted. Parameterized coinduction internalizes this reasoning but requires definitions to pass through library-specific constructions rather than the assistant's native coinductive types [9].

**Classical reasoning gaps.** Some coinductive developments need excluded middle or choice where their inductive counterparts do not — Leroy's development marks classical proofs explicitly [7]. The constructive content of coinduction remains subtler than that of induction.

**Automation is nascent.** No analogue of inductive proof search exists for bisimulation discovery: proposing the candidate relation remains a manual, creative act. Decision procedures cover only restricted classes such as finite-state systems.

**Performance.** Guardedness checking and the unfolding of coinductive proof terms can be expensive; large coinductive developments stress kernel reduction, and sized-type inference in Agda can produce cryptic errors.

##7 Conclusion

Coalgebraic methods reorganize formal verification around observation rather than construction. Bisimulation gives the canonical equivalence for state-based systems; final coalgebras give canonical domains for infinite behavior; coinduction gives the proof principle dual to induction. Mechanized in Rocq/Coq through guarded corecursion, in Agda through copatterns and sized types, and in Isabelle/HOL through corecursion up-to, these ideas have verified stream calculi, process calculi, operational semantics, and compiler correctness for diverging programs [1][4][7][8][10].

The trajectory points toward convergence: the later modality meets step-indexed logics; up-to techniques meet parameterized coinduction; syntactic guardedness yields to semantic productivity [2][6][9]. Open problems are practical — bisimulation discovery, robust cofix engineering, richer decision procedures — but the mathematics is stable. Wherever verification must speak of the infinite, coalgebra is the native language.
