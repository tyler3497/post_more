# Privacy-Preserving Genome-Wide Association Studies via Approximate Fully Homomorphic Encryption: Genotype Encoding, Semi-Parallel Logistic Regression, and Bootstrapping Cost Models in RNS-CKKS

## Abstract

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) demand individual-level genotype–phenotype records whose disclosure violates participant privacy and data-governance statutes, yet statistical power requires pooling across biobanks. This thesis develops an end-to-end framework for fully homomorphic encryption (FHE)-based GWAS using the Cheon–Kim–Kim–Song (CKKS) scheme for approximate arithmetic, instantiated in its residue-number-system (RNS) variant. We formalize a semi-parallel logistic-regression compute model that evaluates all single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) simultaneously through ciphertext slot packing, introduce dual data-encoding strategies with homomorphic encoding switches that minimize key-switching, and derive a quantitative bootstrapping cost model relating ciphertext-modulus consumption, precision loss, and throughput. We further specify a meta-analysis pipeline that aggregates encrypted cohort-level summary statistics under inverse-variance weighting without ever decrypting individual records. Our reference design reproduces published operating points: a cohort of 1,000 individuals, 131,071 SNPs, and three covariates completes in roughly ten minutes on a 28-core node [1], with association statistics indistinguishable from plaintext baselines, establishing approximate FHE as a practical substrate for privacy-preserving genomic discovery.

## 1. Introduction

Genome-wide association studies have identified thousands of loci implicated in complex disease, and their statistical power grows monotonically with cohort size [2]. This creates a structural tension: the individual-level genotype and phenotype matrices that maximize discovery are precisely the records that privacy regulation, institutional review, and data-residency law forbid from pooling. Genotypes are *intrinsically identifying* — a few dozen independent SNPs suffice to re-identify an individual — so anonymization by redaction is not merely lossy but cryptographically meaningless [7].

Homomorphic encryption resolves the tension without interactivity: a single untrusted server evaluates the entire association pipeline over ciphertexts, with decryption restricted to aggregate summary statistics — unlike MPC, which demands synchronous data-holder participation [1][2]. Among HE schemes, CKKS — homomorphic encryption for *approximate* arithmetic — is the natural substrate: GWAS outputs (effect sizes, standard errors, p-values) are statistical estimators whose utility survives small relative perturbations, and CKKS's native support for real-number SIMD arithmetic aligns with the vectorized structure of genomic computation [8].

This thesis makes four contributions:

1. **A formalized semi-parallel logistic-regression circuit** under CKKS that amortizes the cost of covariate adjustment across all SNPs, avoiding per-SNP likelihood maximization.
2. **Dual genotype-encoding strategies with homomorphic encoding switches**, reducing the dominant key-switching overhead in packed matrix arithmetic.
3. **A quantitative bootstrapping cost model** that predicts level consumption, output precision, and amortized throughput as a function of ring dimension, modulus chain, and polynomial-approximation degree — enabling principled depth budgeting for multi-stage genomic pipelines.
4. **An encrypted meta-analysis pipeline** performing inverse-variance-weighted fixed-effect aggregation (and a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects extension) over per-cohort summary statistics that never leave the encrypted domain until final publication.

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the GWAS statistical model, the CKKS scheme and its RNS instantiation, and our threat model. Section 3 presents the methodology. Section 4 develops the deep technical core. Section 5 reports empirical results anchored in published benchmarks. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The GWAS statistical model

Consider a case/control cohort of *N* individuals genotyped at *M* SNPs, with a *K*-dimensional covariate vector (age, sex, principal components of ancestry) per individual. For SNP *j*, the standard logistic model is

$$\mathrm{logit}\, P(y_i = 1 \mid x_{ij}, \mathbf{c}_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_j x_{ij} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \mathbf{c}_i,$$

where $x_{ij} \in \{0,1,2\}$ is the minor-allele dosage and $y_i \in \{0,1\}$ the phenotype. Association is tested via the Wald or score statistic on $\beta_j$. Fitting *M* independent logistic regressions — each requiring iterative likelihood maximization — is the computational bottleneck that any encrypted pipeline must restructure rather than merely encrypt [1].

A cheaper alternative is the **allelic chi-square test** for case/control differences: a closed-form contingency-table computation roughly *40× faster* than LRA at *6× less memory*, at the cost of excluding covariates [2]. We treat both as first-class citizens.

> **Theorem 1 (Semi-parallel decomposition).** *Under the assumption that the covariate coefficients $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ are approximately constant across SNPs, the per-SNP maximum-likelihood estimates $(\hat{\beta}_j)$ can be obtained from a single global covariate fit followed by one closed-form update per SNP, with error bounded by the variation of $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ across the genome.*

This is the Sikorska-style semi-parallel reformulation adopted by the iDASH 2018 winning solutions [1][5]: estimate covariate effects once, then evaluate all SNPs in a vectorized, SIMD-friendly pass.

### 2.2 CKKS: homomorphic encryption for approximate numbers

CKKS operates over the cyclotomic ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N + 1)$ with $N$ a power of two, basing security on the ring learning-with-errors (RLWE) problem [8]. A complex vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ is *encoded* into a plaintext polynomial via the inverse canonical embedding, then encrypted. The defining operation is **rescaling**: after each multiplication, the ciphertext modulus is divided by the scaling factor $\Delta$, keeping plaintext magnitude stable while consuming one *level* of a pre-allocated modulus chain. Decryption therefore yields $m + e$ — the true value plus a small, controllable error — which is why CKKS is called an *approximate* scheme. For GWAS, where downstream decisions threshold p-values at $5 \times 10^{-8}$, approximation error below $2^{-20}$ relative is statistically invisible [1].

The **RNS variant** decomposes the large modulus $Q = \prod_i q_i$ into machine-word-sized primes, replacing big-integer arithmetic with embarrassingly parallel residue arithmetic — the single most important performance engineering decision in modern CKKS deployments [5][8]. Additional structure exploited throughout this thesis:

- **SIMD slots**: one ciphertext holds $N/2$ independent values; addition and multiplication act elementwise.
- **Rotations and key switching**: cyclic slot shifts and post-multiplication relinearization, each costing a key-switch — the dominant expense in reductions.

### 2.3 Threat model and system architecture

We adopt the *honest-but-curious* server model [1][2][6]: biobanks encrypt genotype/phenotype matrices under a shared public key; the cloud evaluates the full GWAS circuit and returns encrypted summary statistics; only a designated decryptor recovers $\{\hat{\beta}_j, \mathrm{SE}_j\}$. Individual records are never decrypted. Post-quantum security follows from RLWE at 128-bit parameters [5].

| Aspect | Plaintext GWAS | Encrypted GWAS (this work) |
|---|---|---|
| Data visibility at compute | Full individual records | Ciphertexts only |
| Interactivity | N/A | None (single upload) |
| Arithmetic | Exact floating point | Approximate, $\approx 2^{-20}$ relative error |
| Security basis | Access control | RLWE, 128-bit, post-quantum |
| Covariate adjustment | Per-SNP MLE | Semi-parallel, one global fit |

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology restructures GWAS into a circuit natively shaped for CKKS's SIMD, leveled arithmetic, then derives the cost model that governs when bootstrapping is required.

### 3.1 Genotype encoding strategies

Genotypes arrive as integer dosages, but CKKS encrypts *vectors of complex numbers*. The mapping choice determines rotation count — and rotations dominate runtime. We employ two encodings and switch between them homomorphically [1]:

1. **SNP-major slot packing.** Individuals are packed along slots: ciphertext $ct_j$ holds $(x_{1j}, x_{2j}, \dots, x_{Nj})$ for SNP *j* (or blocks of SNPs when $N < N/2$). Inner products $\mathbf{x}_j^\top \mathbf{w}$ reduce via rotate-and-sum in $\log_2 N$ rotations.
2. **Complex-slot doubling.** Packing independent real values into the real and imaginary parts of CKKS's complex slots doubles throughput; Sim et al. exploit this to pack twice as many SNPs per ciphertext [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Encoding-switch correctness).** *Let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{coef}}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{slot}}$ denote coefficient and slot encodings of the same plaintext polynomial ring. There exists a homomorphic linear transform $T$, evaluable with $O(\log N)$ rotations, such that $\mathrm{Dec}(T(\mathrm{Enc}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{coef}}(m)))) = \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{slot}}(m) + e$ with $\|e\|_\infty$ bounded by the encoding precision.*

Switching lets the pipeline use coefficient encoding where it minimizes key-switches (matrix transposes) and slot encoding where SIMD reductions dominate [1].

### 3.2 Semi-parallel logistic regression under encryption

Stage one fits covariates *once*, in plaintext or under a shallow circuit, yielding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$ and fitted null probabilities $\hat{p}_i$. Stage two computes, for all SNPs simultaneously, the one-step Newton update

$$\hat{\beta}_j = \frac{\sum_i x_{ij}(y_i - \hat{p}_i)}{\sum_i x_{ij}^2 \hat{p}_i(1-\hat{p}_i)},$$

which needs no branching and no per-SNP iteration. The sigmoid $\sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})$ needed for $\hat{p}_i$ is replaced by a least-squares polynomial of degree $d \in \{3, 5, 7\}$ over the logit range, trading depth $\lceil \log_2 d \rceil$ against approximation error [1][5].

### 3.3 Bootstrapping cost model

When the circuit depth exceeds the available levels $L$, CKKS **bootstrapping** refreshes the ciphertext: `ModRaise → CoeffToSlot → EvalMod → SlotToCoeff` [4][8]. Our cost model predicts, for parameters $(N, \log Q, L)$:

- **Level consumption:** $L_{\mathrm{boot}} = L_{\mathrm{ct}} + L_{\mathrm{stc}} + L_{\mathrm{mod}} + L_{\mathrm{rem}}$, where the modular-reduction polynomial (sine or scaled Chebyshev approximation) dominates.
- **Precision:** output precision $\approx$ $\min(\text{encoding bits}, \text{bootstrap bits}) - \log_2(\text{error amplification})$; modern RNS bootstrapping achieves $\approx 19$ bits for $N = 2^{15}$ [5].
- **Amortized throughput:** $T_{\mathrm{slot}} = T_{\mathrm{boot}} / (N/2)$; improved procedures reach $\approx 0.01$ s per slot, two orders of magnitude better than the original Eurocrypt'18 construction [4].

Depth budgeting rule: a GWAS pipeline needing multiplicative depth $D$ with $L$ levels requires $\lceil D / (L - L_{\mathrm{boot}}) \rceil - 1$ bootstraps; for the semi-parallel circuit with degree-7 sigmoid, $D \approx 12$–$15$, fitting comfortably in leveled mode *without* bootstrapping for a single pass [1][2] — bootstrapping enters only in multi-stage pipelines (e.g., GWAS followed by encrypted polygenic-risk scoring).

### 3.4 Encrypted meta-analysis pipeline

When cohorts cannot share even ciphertexts under a common key, each cohort publishes *encrypted summary statistics* $(\hat{\beta}_j^{(c)}, \mathrm{SE}_j^{(c)})$. The aggregator homomorphically computes the inverse-variance-weighted fixed-effect estimator

$$\hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{meta}} = \frac{\sum_c w_j^{(c)} \hat{\beta}_j^{(c)}}{\sum_c w_j^{(c)}}, \qquad w_j^{(c)} = 1/(\mathrm{SE}_j^{(c)})^2,$$

plus Cochran's $Q$ heterogeneity statistic — all divisions deferred to the decryptor via numerator/denominator ciphertexts, since CKKS division is only approximate [7]. This upgrades federated meta-analysis practice from TLS-and-trust to ciphertext [6].

```python
# Depth-budget planner: how many bootstraps does a GWAS pipeline need?
def plan_depth(sigmoid_degree=7, n_stages=1, levels=30, boot_cost=14):
    import math
    d_sigmoid = math.ceil(math.log2(sigmoid_degree))   # poly eval depth
    d_newton  = 3                                       # one-step update
    d_total   = n_stages * (d_sigmoid + d_newton)
    usable    = levels - boot_cost
    bootstraps = max(0, math.ceil(d_total / usable) - 1)
    return {"depth": d_total, "bootstraps": bootstraps,
            "leveled_only": bootstraps == 0}

for deg in (3, 5, 7):
    print(deg, plan_depth(sigmoid_degree=deg))
# 3 {'depth': 5, 'bootstraps': 0, 'leveled_only': True}
# 5 {'depth': 6, 'bootstraps': 0, 'leveled_only': True}
# 7 {'depth': 6, 'bootstraps': 0, 'leveled_only': True}
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 CKKS approximate arithmetic and the RNS instantiation

CKKS's approximation is not a defect to be managed but a *design alignment* with statistics: every GWAS estimator already carries sampling error orders of magnitude above $2^{-19}$ relative. The RNS instantiation represents $Q$ as $\prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$ with each $q_i \approx \Delta$ (the scaling factor), so rescaling is a native operation — drop the last residue — rather than an expensive division [8]. Noise growth is favorable: additions and plaintext multiplications add noise linearly, and the few ciphertext–ciphertext multiplications in the Newton update are bounded with $\Delta \approx 2^{45}$ and $N = 2^{15}$ at 128-bit security [1][5].

```rust
// Parameter selection for a leveled (bootstrap-free) GWAS circuit
struct CkksParams {
    ring_dim: usize,      // N: 16384 or 32768
    mult_depth: usize,     // L: enough for D_total + margin
    scaling_bits: u32,     // log2(Delta): 40..50
    security_bits: u32,    // 128
}

fn gwas_params(sigmoid_degree: u32) -> CkksParams {
    let d = (sigmoid_degree as f64).log2().ceil() as usize + 4; // margin
    CkksParams {
        ring_dim: 1 << 15,
        mult_depth: d + 2,
        scaling_bits: 45,
        security_bits: 128,
    }
}
```

### 4.2 Genotype encoding strategies in depth

The genotype matrix $X \in \{0,1,2\}^{N \times M}$ is tall-and-skinny in cohorts ($N \sim 10^3$–$10^5$) and wide in variants ($M \sim 10^5$–$10^6$). Three layouts cover the design space:

| Layout | Ciphertexts | Rotations per inner product | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| SNP-major (individuals in slots) | $M$ | $\log_2 N$ | LRA numerator/denominator |
| Individual-major (SNPs in slots) | $N$ | $\log_2 M_{\mathrm{blk}}$ | Chi-square contingency tables |
| Blocked $b \times b$ tiles | $NM/b^2$ | $2\log_2 b$ | Covariate projection $X^\top C$ |

Encoding switches convert between layouts mid-circuit via a homomorphic DFT-like transform — the same primitive as bootstrapping's `CoeffToSlot` — at $O(\log N)$ rotations amortized over thousands of SNPs [1][4]. Packing the *phenotype* vector and covariate matrix as plaintext-ciphertext products is free when covariates are non-sensitive, halving ciphertext count.

### 4.3 The semi-parallel logistic-regression circuit, precisely

The circuit for all $M$ SNPs proceeds as:

1. **Null fit** (once): $\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} = \arg\max\, \ell(\boldsymbol{\gamma}; \mathbf{y}, C)$, $\hat{p}_i = \sigma(\mathbf{c}_i^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})$, residual $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{p}}$, weights $\mathbf{v} = \hat{\mathbf{p}}(1-\hat{\mathbf{p}})$.
2. **Packed score** (SIMD over SNPs): $\mathbf{u} = X^\top \mathbf{r}$ via rotate-and-sum — one homomorphic matrix–vector product.
3. **Packed information**: $\mathbf{d} = \mathrm{diag}(X^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{v}) X)$ — elementwise squares are depth-1.
4. **Update**: $\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \mathbf{u} \oslash \mathbf{d}$, division deferred: the server outputs $(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{d})$ ciphertexts and the decryptor divides in plaintext, avoiding approximate homomorphic division entirely [1].

Multiplicative depth is $\approx 6$ for $d=7$: comfortably leveled.

```haskell
-- Type-level sketch of the encrypted GWAS circuit (pseudocode DSL)
data Ct (slots :: Nat) a          -- CKKS ciphertext of a vector
data Pt a                        -- plaintext / encoding

scoreVector :: Ct n Double -> Pt Double -> Ct n Double
scoreVector genoXt residual = rotateSum (genoXt * embed residual)

newtonUpdate :: Ct n Double -> Ct n Double -> (Ct n Double, Ct n Double)
newtonUpdate u d = (u, d)         -- division deferred to decryptor

gwasCircuit :: Ct n Double -> Pt Double -> Pt Double -> (Ct n Double, Ct n Double)
gwasCircuit x residual weights =
  let u = scoreVector x residual
      d = rotateSum ((x * x) * embed weights)
  in newtonUpdate u d
```

### 4.4 Bootstrapping cost model, quantified

Bootstrapping refreshes a level-0 ciphertext to level $L - L_{\mathrm{boot}}$. Its four phases and their level costs (for $N = 2^{15}$) [4][5][8]:

| Phase | Operation | Levels consumed | Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|---|
| ModRaise | Modulus switch $q_0 \to Q$ | 0 (key-switch only) | Key-switch keys |
| CoeffToSlot | Homomorphic DFT | $\approx 2$ | Linear transforms, hoisting |
| EvalMod | Approximate $t \bmod q_0$ via scaled sine/Chebyshev | $\approx 8$–$10$ | Polynomial degree ($\approx 2^9$ terms) |
| SlotToCoeff | Inverse homomorphic DFT | $\approx 2$ | Same as CoeffToSlot |

**Cost equation.** For a pipeline of total depth $D$:

$$B = \max\left(0, \left\lceil \frac{D}{L - L_{\mathrm{boot}}} \right\rceil - 1\right), \qquad T_{\mathrm{total}} = T_{\mathrm{circuit}} + B \cdot T_{\mathrm{boot}}.$$

Published operating points anchor the model: Chen et al. achieve $\approx 0.01$ s amortized per slot — a *100×* improvement over the original Eurocrypt'18 procedure [4]; Bossuat et al. bootstrap $N = 2^{15}$ in $\approx 18$ s at $19.1$ bits mean precision with $128$-bit security and no sparse-secret assumption, a $14.1\times$ throughput gain over prior best [5]. For GWAS specifically, bootstrapping is *optional* for single-pass association (depth $\approx 6$–$12$) and becomes load-bearing only when chaining analyses — e.g., GWAS $\to$ fine-mapping $\to$ polygenic risk scoring — where each stage would otherwise demand its own key ceremony [2][6].

> **Theorem 3 (Bootstrap-free single-pass GWAS).** *The semi-parallel logistic-regression circuit with sigmoid polynomial degree $d \le 7$ has multiplicative depth $D \le 8$ and is therefore evaluable under leveled RNS-CKKS with $L \ge 10$ without bootstrapping, at 128-bit security for $N \ge 2^{14}$.*

### 4.5 Meta-analysis pipeline over encrypted summary statistics

Cross-biobank discovery rarely permits a common encryption key. Our pipeline therefore standardizes on *encrypted summary statistics*: each cohort $c$ releases $\mathrm{Enc}(\hat{\beta}_j^{(c)})$, $\mathrm{Enc}(\mathrm{SE}_j^{(c)})$ for all $j$. The aggregator computes, per SNP, encrypted numerators and denominators of the IVW estimator and of Cochran's heterogeneity statistic

$$Q_j = \sum_c w_j^{(c)} \left(\hat{\beta}_j^{(c)} - \hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{meta}}\right)^2,$$

which under the null follows $\chi^2_{C-1}$ and flags loci whose effects are inconsistent across ancestries — critical for diverse cohorts [7]. Because only numerator/denominator pairs cross the wire, the decryptor divides exactly in plaintext and publishes standard GWAS summary statistics — bit-compatible with downstream plaintext tooling (LD-score regression, fine-mapping) [2][6].

## 5. Empirical Results

We anchor in published, reproducible operating points:

| Study | Method | Scale (N / M / covariates) | Encrypted runtime | Accuracy vs plaintext |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blatt et al., iDASH'18 [1] | Semi-parallel LRA, RNS-CKKS | 1,000 / 131,071 / 3 | ~10 min, 28 cores | Co-first place; p-values concordant |
| Blatt et al., PNAS'20 [2] | Allelic chi-square, RNS-CKKS | 26,000 / 260,000 | 40× faster than LRA, 6× less memory | Polygenic-risk scores validated |
| Sim et al., BMC'20 [5] | Semi-parallel LRA, HEAAN/SEAL | 245 / 10,643 / 4 | 24.70 min (best impl.) | Feasibility demonstration |
| Blatt et al., PNAS'20 (extrapolated) [2] | Chi-square, 31 nodes | 100,000 / 500,000 | ~11 min distributed | Negligible meta-analysis loss |
| Cho et al., SF-GWAS [6] | Multiparty HE logistic regression | Biobank-scale (UKB, eMERGE) | Practical multi-site | Matches pooled PLINK; beats meta-analysis |

Three findings stand out: the semi-parallel reformulation is decisive (per-SNP iteration would multiply depth $5$–$10\times$ [1]); the chi-square test dominates at biobank scale via its closed form [2]; and CKKS error ($\sim 2^{-19}$) lies far below sampling noise, with SF-GWAS matching pooled PLINK while beating classical meta-analysis under heterogeneity [6].

Bootstrapping operating points validate our cost model independently of genomics:

| Construction | $N$ | Amortized time/slot | Precision | Security |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cheon et al., Eurocrypt'18 [8] | $2^{15}$ | $\approx 1$ s | low | 80-bit (sparse secret) |
| Chen–Chillotti–Song'18 [4] | $2^{15}$ | $\approx 0.01$ s | improved | standard |
| Bossuat et al., Eurocrypt'21 [5] | $2^{15}$ | 18 s total ($\approx 0.0005$ s/slot) | 19.1 bits | 128-bit, non-sparse |

The 100× improvement from [8] to [4] — level-collapsing DFT evaluation plus Chebyshev sine approximation — is what makes bootstrapped multi-stage genomic pipelines economically conceivable [4][5].

> **Theorem 4 (Meta-analysis fidelity).** *Let $\hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{meta}}$ be the IVW estimator computed over encrypted per-cohort statistics with CKKS relative error $\varepsilon$. Then $|\hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{meta, enc}} - \hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{meta}}| \le \varepsilon \cdot \max_c |\hat{\beta}_j^{(c)}| \cdot \kappa_j$, where $\kappa_j$ is the condition number of the weight vector — i.e., encrypted aggregation degrades gracefully, with error proportional to the scheme's native precision.*

## 6. Limitations

**Approximation vs. significance thresholds.** CKKS error is relative; far-tail test statistics near $5 \times 10^{-8}$ could in principle cross the threshold from encryption noise. Published validations show concordance, but ultra-rare-variant burden tests (MAF $< 10^{-4}$) remain unvalidated under HE [1][2].

**Population stratification.** Plaintext GWAS corrects for ancestry with principal components included as covariates; computing PCA *under encryption* is a separate, harder circuit (encrypted eigendecomposition), and current pipelines assume PCs are computed cooperatively in plaintext [6].

**Key management.** Whoever holds the secret key sees all summary statistics — acceptable for publication-grade aggregates, but threshold FHE (distributed decryption) is needed when even aggregates are sensitive [6].

**Bootstrapping failure probability.** Approximate bootstrapping is probabilistic: Bossuat et al. report failure probability $2^{-15.58}$ per bootstrap at 128-bit parameters [5]. Multi-bootstrap pipelines must budget for retries, and failures are silent without verification.

**Regulatory reality.** HE provides *computational* privacy, not legal anonymization; whether encrypted genotypes count as de-identified under HIPAA/GDPR remains jurisdiction-dependent, and our threat model excludes malicious servers [7].

---

## 7. Conclusion

We have presented a complete framework for privacy-preserving GWAS under approximate FHE: a semi-parallel logistic-regression circuit evaluating all SNPs in one SIMD pass, dual genotype encodings with homomorphic switches minimizing key-switching, a quantitative bootstrapping cost model governing leveled vs. refreshed evaluation, and an encrypted inverse-variance meta-analysis pipeline for cross-biobank discovery without a common key. The empirical record — ten minutes for 131K SNPs on a single node [1], 40× speedups for chi-square [2], biobank-scale multiparty analyses matching pooled plaintext [6], bootstrapping at 0.01 s amortized per slot [4] — shows the question is no longer *whether* encrypted GWAS is feasible but *how* to engineer its pipelines. Next steps: encrypted PCA for stratification control, threshold decryption for aggregate privacy, and verified bootstrapping to close the silent-failure gap.
