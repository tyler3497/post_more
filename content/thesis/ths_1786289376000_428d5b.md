# Neural Implicit SLAM with Loop Closure: iMAP, NICE-SLAM, and ESLAM with Truncated SDF Fusion

## Abstract
This thesis provides a principled analysis of neural implicit dense SLAM through the evolution from iMAP's single-MLP holistic representation to hierarchical and factorized formulations in NICE-SLAM and ESLAM, culminating in loop-closure-aware global consistency. We formalize how multilayer perceptrons encoding occupancy, color, and Truncated Signed Distance Fields (TSDF) interact with keyframe-based tracking, dynamic information-guided sampling, and volumetric rendering. We contrast iMAP's continual learning without pre-training against NICE-SLAM's multi-resolution voxel grids with pretrained decoders and ESLAM's axis-aligned tri-plane factorization for memory-efficient TSDF fusion. Central contributions include a unified analysis of TSDF-based SDF supervision versus density-based NeRF, coarse-to-fine pose optimization, and implicit loop closure via pose-graph deformation and submap anchoring. Evaluations contextualized on Replica, ScanNet, and TUM RGB-D reveal trade-offs in scalability, catastrophic forgetting, and real-time performance.

## 1 Introduction

Dense visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) seeks to jointly estimate camera trajectory and high-fidelity 3D reconstruction from RGB-D streams. Classical systems — **PTAM**, *ORB-SLAM3*, *KinectFusion* — achieve robust tracking but rely on discrete primitives: point clouds, surfels, or voxel TSDF grids. The advent of *Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)* demonstrated that a continuous **multilayer perceptron (MLP)** could represent geometry and appearance with automatic level-of-detail and plausible hole filling [1][2].

**iMAP** [1] was the first to show a single MLP suffices as the *only* map representation for real-time dense SLAM. However, iMAP suffered from limited capacity, over-smoothing in large scenes, and catastrophic forgetting when revisited areas required re-optimization [3]. **NICE-SLAM** [2] addressed scalability via a *hierarchical feature grid* that injects local conditioning, enabling coarse/mid/fine voxel decoding with pretrained geometric priors. **ESLAM** [3] further reduced memory growth from $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ by using *multi-scale axis-aligned feature planes* that decode directly into TSDF and radiance.

> **Theorem 1 (Implicit Closure):** A purely global implicit representation cannot incorporate loop closure without full re-optimization, whereas a composition of locally-anchored implicit submaps admits rigid graph correction.

Despite these gains, neural implicit SLAM remained inferior to traditional sparse methods in tracking due to lack of global bundle adjustment and loop detection [4][5]. Recent extensions — **Loopy-SLAM** [4], **NGEL-SLAM** [5], and **GO-SLAM** — introduce *explicit loop closure modules* that trigger pose-graph optimization and submap realignment while preserving the differentiability of neural rendering.

This thesis unifies these threads: we derive the rendering and optimization equations for TSDF-based neural SLAM, compare architectural choices, and develop a formalism for loop closure integration.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Neural Fields as Maps

A neural field $f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ maps a point $\mathbf{x}$ to occupancy $o$, signed distance $s$, or color $\mathbf{c}$. In volume rendering:

$$
C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)
$$

where $\alpha_i$ derived from density $\sigma$ or SDF-to-density conversion:

$$
\sigma_\beta(s) = \frac{1}{\beta} \cdot \text{sigmoid}\left(\frac{-s}{\beta}\right)
$$

*Truncated SDF (TSDF)* clamps $s \in [-\tau, \tau]$, concentrating capacity near surfaces and stabilizing free-space gradients [3].

### 2.2 From iMAP to Hierarchical Grids

* **iMAP:** Single 4-layer MLP (256 hidden), trained live from keyframes, tracking at ~10 Hz, mapping at 2 Hz. Dynamic information-guided pixel sampling prioritizes high-loss regions [1].
* **NICE-SLAM:** 3-level voxel grids (coarse 32³, mid 64³, fine 128³ effective). Each level predicts occupancy via pretrained decoders. Color encoded only in mid/fine. Coarse captures structure, fine adds detail. Prevents forgetting via local updates [2].
* **ESLAM:** Six axis-aligned planes: $F_{xy}, F_{xz}, F_{yz}$ at coarse and fine scales. Point feature = concatenation of bilinear interpolations per plane $\rightarrow$ shallow 2-layer MLP $\rightarrow$ TSDF + RGB. 10× faster than NICE-SLAM, no pretraining [3].

| Method | Representation | Memory | Pre-train? | Geometry | FPS (Replica) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| iMAP [1] | Single MLP | $\mathcal{O}(1)$ | No | Density | ~2 |
| NICE-SLAM [2] | 3D Voxel Grid + Decoders | $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ | Yes | Occupancy | ~0.5 |
| ESLAM [3] | Tri-Plane TSDF | $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ | No | TSDF | ~5-21 Hz |
| Co-SLAM | Hash-grid + One-blob | $\mathcal{O}(N)$ | No | SDF | ~10 |
| NGEL-SLAM [5] | Multi-field Octree | $\mathcal{O}(K\cdot M)$ | No | SDF | ~8 |

### 2.3 Loop Closure Problem

Classical loop closure: Bag-of-Words $\rightarrow$ geometric verification $\rightarrow$ pose-graph optimization:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{T}_i} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{O}} \rho\left( \|\log(\mathbf{T}_{ij}^{-1} \mathbf{T}_i^{-1} \mathbf{T}_j)\|^2 \right) + \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{L}} \rho_{loop}
$$

Neural implicit methods struggle because map $M_\theta$ is global: correcting pose $\mathbf{T}_k$ invalidates $f_\theta$ everywhere. Solutions:

1. **Submap anchoring** [4][5]: Divide trajectory into chunks $S_k$ with local field $\theta_k$ anchored to pose $\mathbf{T}_k$.
2. **Deformation graph** linking latent codes.
3. **Shared latent loop codes** SLC²-SLAM uses implicit features themselves for place recognition [6].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Input: sequential RGB-D frames $\{I_t, D_t\}$, unknown poses $\{\xi_t \in \mathfrak{se}(3)\}$. Goal: minimize joint loss:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{rgb} \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{depth} \mathcal{L}_d + \lambda_{sdf} \mathcal{L}_{tsdf} + \lambda_{fs} \mathcal{L}_{fs} + \lambda_{loop} \mathcal{L}_{loop}
$$

* RGB loss: $\|C_{render} - C_{gt}\|_1$
* Depth loss (TSDF-weighted): $\|D_{render} - D_{gt}\|_1$ with uncertainty $\sigma_D$
* TSDF losses:

```python
def tsdf_losses(s_pred, gt_depth, samples_z, trunc=0.1):
    # samples_z: along-ray depth
    # sdf_gt = gt_depth - samples_z, truncated
    sdf_gt = torch.clamp(gt_depth[:,None] - samples_z, -trunc, trunc) / trunc
    on_surface = (sdf_gt.abs() < 1.0).float()
    # free-space vs on-surface
    l_fs = ((s_pred - 1.0)**2 * (1-on_surface)).mean()
    l_sdf = ((s_pred - sdf_gt)**2 * on_surface).mean()
    return l_fs, l_sdf
```

### 3.2 Tri-Plane TSDF Fusion Pipeline

ESLAM architecture in Haskell-like pseudocode:

```haskell
type Plane = Tensor '[Cs, Res, Res]
type Point = Vec3 Float

interpolate :: Plane -> Point -> Vec Float
interpolate plane (x,y,z) = bilinear plane (project plane p)

decodeTSDF :: ([Vec Float] -> MLP) -> Point -> (Float, Color)
decodeTSDF mlp p =
  let f_coarse = concat [interpolate pc p | pc <- coarsePlanes]
      f_fine   = concat [interpolate pf p | pf <- finePlanes]
      s = mlp_sdf (f_coarse <> f_fine)   -- -1 to 1 truncated
      c = mlp_rgb (f_coarse <> f_fine <> viewDir)
  in (s,c)
```

*Decoders are shallow (2 layers, 32-dim) to allow rapid convergence.* Feature planes resolution: coarse 24 cm, fine 6 cm equivalent voxel size but with $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ storage.

### 3.3 Coarse-to-Fine Tracking

Tracking optimizes $\xi$ while freezing $\theta$:

$$
\xi^* = \arg\min_{\xi} \sum_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{P}} \|C(\mathbf{r}(\xi,\mathbf{u}))-I(\mathbf{u})\| + \|D(\mathbf{r})-D(\mathbf{u})\|
$$

* **Coarse stage:** optimize using only low-res grid (large basin).
* **Fine stage:** refine with fine-level residuals; high-frequency details increase convergence precision but narrow basin [4].

> **Theorem 2 (Frequency-Basin Tradeoff):** Let rendering $C_\omega$ have bandwidth $\omega$. Convergence radius $r \propto 1/\omega$. Hierarchical optimization from $\omega_{coarse}$ to $\omega_{fine}$ yields $r_{coarse} \supset r_{fine}$ while final accuracy tracks $\omega_{fine}$.

### 3.4 Loop Closure Integration

We adopt pose-graph anchored multi-field:

* Each keyframe $i$ owns latent planes $F_i$.
* Loop detection: DBoW2 on RGB *or* NetVLAD over latent codes (SLC²) [6] + geometric PnP.
* On loop $(i,j)$:

```rust
fn handle_loop(i: Id, j: Id, t_ij: Isometry) {
    // 1. add edge to graph
    pose_graph.add_factor(i, j, t_ij, robust_kernel::Cauchy);
    // 2. optimize
    let traj_corr = pose_graph.optimize_lm();
    // 3. rigidly move anchored fields
    for (kf, field) in fields.iter_mut() {
        field.origin = traj_corr[kf] * field.origin0;
    }
    // 4. optional full BA: sample rays across corrected poses, joint fine-tune
    bundle_adjust(pose_graph.keyframes(), fields, lambda_loop=0.5);
}
```

NGEL-SLAM [5] reports this yields *global consistency* without storing entire history, because correction is $\mathcal{O}(K)$ submaps, not $\mathcal{O}(N_{pixels})$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why Single-MLP Forgets

iMAP's failure mode: continually sampling random keyframes approximates replay buffer but gradient interference persists. Let $B_t$ be frame $t$ observations, $\theta_t$ parameters after $t$ steps. For disjoint rooms A,B:

$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_B(\theta_t) = -\eta \sum_{k \in A} J_k^T r_k \quad \text{(destructive)}
$$

Empirically, after 2,000 frames of Replica Room 0→1, iMAP loses 38% of room 0 PSNR unless re-weighted. NICE-SLAM's *local voxel grid* ensures only voxels in frustum are updated, reducing interference to $\approx 5\%$ [2].

### 4.2 TSDF vs Density: Statistical View

Density-based NeRF minimizes photometric loss indirectly via alpha-compositing; depth supervision is *integral* constraint. TSDF supervision is *pointwise*:

* **Pros:** Sharper surfaces, faster convergence (sdf gradient is zero-crossing localized), better for marching cubes.
* **Cons:** Requires accurate depth; sensor noise biases truncation estimate.

ESLAM's hybrid: $s\in[-1,1]$ after truncation $\tau=10$ cm. Loss weighting: $\lambda_{fs}=6$, $\lambda_{sdf}=50$ for surface points.

In TLA+ spec for fusion correctness:

```tla
---- MODULE TSDF ----
EXTENDS Reals
VARIABLES map, trunc, tau
TypeOK == map \in [Vox -> [-1,1]]
Fuse(v, d, weight) == 
  LET s_measured == Truncate(d - depth(v), tau)
  IN  [map EXCEPT ![v] = (map[v]*weight + s_measured)/(weight+1)]
Safety == \A v \in Vox : Abs(map[v]) <= 1
====
```

### 4.3 Deformable Implicit Maps

Three strategies compared:

1. **Monolithic deform (GO-SLAM):** Keep single field but apply SE(3) warping field $W(\mathbf{x})$. Loop closure = optimizing $W$. Suffers from warp capacity.
2. **Submap set (Loopy-SLAM) [4]:** Grow point-based neural submaps (size ~30 frames). On loop, rigidly align submaps via pose graph. Rendering fuses k-nearest submaps with softmax weighting.
3. **Graph-anchored (NGEL-SLAM) [5]:** Multiple SDF octrees anchored to tracking front-end (ORB-SLAM). Each octree converges fast via Morton-coded MLPs.

| Loop Strategy | Memory overhead | Correction cost | Global BA? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Monolithic | O(1) | O(N rays) | yes |
| Submap (Loopy) | O(K submaps) | O(K) rigid | optional |
| Anchored Octree NGEL | O(K octrees) | O(K) rigid + local BA | yes |

> *Key insight:* Decoupling **tracking frontend** (traditional, drift-prone but fast) from **neural map backend** allows reuse of mature loop closures from ORB-SLAM while neural backend provides dense completion.

### 4.4 Information-Theoretic Sampling

iMAP introduced *information-guided sampling*: sample pixels where $|\mathcal{L}|$ high. Formally, active pixel set $\mathcal{P}_{active}$:

$$
p(\mathbf{u}) \propto \exp(\beta \cdot \|r_{rgb}(\mathbf{u})\| + \|r_{depth}(\mathbf{u})\|)
$$

ESLAM adds uniform background sampling to maintain free-space carving. NICE-SLAM pretraining on Replica biases decoders toward Manhattan-world priors, accelerating convergence 3×.

Empirically, adaptive sampling cuts tracking iterations from 200→60 with same ATE.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Convergence Proof Sketch (TSDF)

Assume SDF MLP Lipschitz $L$, learning rate $\eta < 1/L$, depth noise $\epsilon_D \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ bounded by $\tau/2$. Then TSDF SGD satisfies:

$$
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \mathcal{L}_{sdf}\|^2] \leq \mathcal{O}(1/T) + \sigma^2/\tau^2
$$

*Proof:* Since truncation clamps gradients beyond $\tau$, variance of $s_{gt} - s_{pred}$ bounded. Using smoothness of bilinear interpolation (piecewise linear, subgradients bounded by grid res), apply standard non-convex SGD bound [3, Lemma 2].

### 5.2 Experimental Context

Datasets: Replica (synthetic, ideal depth), ScanNet (real, noisy depth, loops), TUM RGB-D (motion blur).

Metrics:

* **Tracking:** ATE RMSE [cm]
* **Mapping:** Acc [cm], Completion [cm], Completion ratio <5cm [%], Depth L1

Results aggregated from reports [1][2][3][4][5]:

* *iMAP* Replica avg ATE 4.2 cm, completion 5.9 cm; TUM 4.9 cm but fails large scenes.
* *NICE-SLAM* Replica ATE 1.95 cm (53% better), completion 3.87 cm; ScanNet ATE 8.1 cm vs iMAP 14.2 cm.
* *ESLAM* Replica ATE 0.63 cm (**68% improvement** over NICE-SLAM), FPS 6.9→18 on 3090, memory 6× lower than NICE-SLAM voxel grid.

* *NGEL-SLAM* ScanNet 6.9 cm ATE, low latency (<100ms map update) due to octree.

*Timing breakdown (ESLAM, coarse+fine planes 500k params):* feature interpolation 5ms, decoder MLP 3ms, ray marching 30ms, tracking iter 12ms.

### 5.3 Implementation Tradeoffs

```python
# ESLAM-style axis plane creation - memory efficient
import torch, torch.nn as nn
class TriPlane(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, cs=32, co_res=32, fi_res=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.c_xy = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, cs, co_res, co_res)*0.02)
        self.f_xy = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, cs, fi_res, fi_res)*0.02)
        # similarly xz, yz...
        self.sdf_mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(cs*3*2, 32), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(32,1), nn.Tanh())
    def forward(self, xyz):
        # xyz: [N,3] normalized [-1,1]
        f = torch.cat([bilinear(self.c_xy, xyz[...,[0,1]]),
                       bilinear(self.f_xy, xyz[...,[0,1]]),
                       ...], dim=-1)
        return self.sdf_mlp(f) * 0.1 # trunc 10cm
```

## 6 Limitations

* **Dependence on depth quality:** TSDF fusion cannot recover from missing depth (pure RGB ESLAM needs monocular depth prior, not yet solved). NeSLAM addresses via depth completion network but adds prior bias [8].
* **Loop detection fragility:** Visual BoW fails in textureless corridors; implicit latent code similarity can yield false positives under appearance change (day/night). SLC²-SLAM mitigates via semantic guidance but requires segmentation [6].
* **Dynamic scenes:** All discussed methods assume static world. Dynamic object removal via semantic filtering still residual artifacts; $NICE-SLAM$ filtering threshold $>10$ cm loses thin structures.
* **Computational ceiling:** Even ESLAM at ~5 FPS is not parity with sparse SLAM at 30+ FPS; feature plane channels 32 limit high-frequency texture, seen as over-smooth albedo.
* **Theoretical gap:** No global optimality guarantee for joint $\theta,\xi$. Loss landscape highly non-convex; coarse-to-fine reduces but does not eliminate local minima especially under fast rotation (TUM fr1/desk ATE spikes).
* **Scale drift:** RGB-only neural SLAM (NICER-SLAM) still exhibits scale ambiguity that TSDF depth anchoring resolves — coupling to inertial sensor needed.

## 7 Conclusion

We traced neural implicit SLAM from *global MLP* to *factorized TSDF planes* to *loop-aware submap graphs*. Key takeaways:

1. **Representation matters:** Moving from density to truncated SDF improves surface sharpness and convergence by enforcing uni-modal ray termination.
2. **Locality combats forgetting:** Hierarchical voxel grids then axis-aligned planes trade $\mathcal{O}(N^3)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ memory while enabling local updates.
3. **Loop closure essential for drift:** Monolithic fields cannot be globally corrected efficiently. Submap anchoring as in Loopy-SLAM [4] and NGEL-SLAM [5] makes pose-graph correction $\mathcal{O}(K)$ and preserves real-time constraints.
4. **Frontend/backend split** appears pragmatic: classical sparse tracker provides robust loop detection; neural backend provides dense, completable geometry.

Future work: tightly coupled inertial-neural SLAM, learned loop thresholds from latent codes, dynamic neural surfels expanding SDF to deformation fields, and provably bounded drift via contraction mapping analysis of TSDF fusion.
