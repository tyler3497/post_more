# Category Theory for Machine Learning: Functorial Backpropagation, Lenses, and Differentiable Programming

## Abstract

Backpropagation is the engine of modern machine learning, yet standard presentations treat it as an algorithm rather than a structure. This thesis argues that backpropagation is best understood categorically: a deep network is a composite of parametric lenses, and the backward pass is the reverse component of a lens whose forward component is inference. We develop the thesis in three movements. First, we recall categories, functors, and natural transformations, and show how the Para construction turns differentiable maps into parameterized models. Second, we present the central result that gradient-based learning is functorial: assigning a backpropagation rule to each parameterized function extends to a monoidal functor from Para(Euc) into a category of learners [1], refined into the lens-theoretic semantics of Cruttwell et al. [2]. Third, we survey applications: weight tying as functoriality over shared parameters, GANs as lenses composed in opposition, and recurrent networks as coalgebras. String diagrams give a graphical calculus in which these architectures are literally pictures of proofs [7]. The conclusion: differentiable programming is bidirectional programming, and category theory is its natural language.

## 1 Introduction: Differentiation as Structure

The success of deep learning rests on a single computational primitive: *reverse-mode automatic differentiation*, colloquially known as backpropagation. For decades it has been presented procedurally — as a sequence of chain-rule applications threaded through a computation graph. This procedural view is powerful but opaque: it obscures *why* backpropagation composes, *why* the same pattern recurs in attention, recurrence, and generative modeling, and *what* a learning algorithm fundamentally is.

Category theory offers an alternative. Where the procedural view asks "what steps does the algorithm take?", the categorical view asks "what structure does the algorithm preserve?" A function is a morphism; a layer is a parameterized morphism; a network is their composite; and backpropagation — we will argue — is a *functor* that maps each parameterized morphism to its learning rule while preserving composition exactly [1]. When composition is preserved, the training of a network is the composition of the trainings of its parts: backpropagation is not merely implemented compositionally, it *is* compositionality.

The key mediating concept is the **lens**. In functional programming and database theory, a lens is a bidirectional accessor: a *get* that reads a part of a whole and a *put* that writes a modified part back, subject to the lens laws [3]. A neural network layer is precisely such a thing: its *get* is the forward pass (inference), its *put* is the backward pass (gradient flow), and the celebrated chain rule is the lens composition law. Fong and Johnson made this precise by exhibiting functors between categories of lenses and categories of learners [4], and Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi completed the picture with a full categorical semantics of gradient-based learning in terms of parametric lenses and reverse derivative categories [2].

This thesis develops that picture from first principles to research frontier. Our contributions are expository and synthetic rather than novel, but the synthesis matters: we connect the functorial view of backpropagation [1], the lens view of learners [4], the parametric-lens semantics of gradient descent [2][5], the dynamical-systems interpretation of learners [6], and the string-diagram calculus of optics [7] into a single narrative. Along the way we implement lenses in Haskell, draw architectures as string diagrams, and analyze weight tying, GANs, and recurrent models as categorical constructions.

## 2 Background: Categories, Functors, and Natural Transformations

A **category** C consists of a collection of *objects* and, for each pair of objects A and B, a collection of *morphisms* (arrows) f : A → B, equipped with an associative composition operation and identity morphisms id_A : A → A satisfying the unit laws. This austere definition captures an astonishing range of mathematics: **Set** (sets and functions), **Vect** (vector spaces and linear maps), **Euc** (Euclidean spaces and smooth maps), and categories of neural architectures themselves.

A **functor** F : C → D maps objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving identities and composition:

> Theorem: A functor F : C → D satisfies F(id_A) = id_{F(A)} and F(g ∘ f) = F(g) ∘ F(f) for all composable f, g. A monoidal functor additionally preserves the tensor product up to coherent isomorphism: F(A ⊗ B) ≅ F(A) ⊗ F(B).

Functoriality is the mathematical formalization of "structure-preserving map," and it will be the load-bearing concept of this thesis: backpropagation preserves composition, hence it is a functor [1].

A **natural transformation** η : F ⇒ G between functors F, G : C → D is a family of morphisms η_A : F(A) → G(A), one per object, such that for every f : A → B in C the following square commutes:

```
    F(A) --F(f)--> F(B)
      |              |
   η_A           η_B
      v              v
    G(A) --G(f)--> G(B)
```

Naturality is a uniformity condition: the transformation does not depend on arbitrary choices at each object. Optimizers, as we shall see, can be understood as 2-cells — natural transformations between parametric maps — in the bicategory Para [5].

Three further pieces of background are essential:

1. **Cartesian categories.** A category with finite products, where every object A admits a diagonal Δ : A → A × A and projections π₁, π₂. The category **Euc** of Euclidean spaces and smooth maps is Cartesian, and products model the pairing of inputs with parameters and of activations with gradients.
2. **Reverse derivative categories.** A Cartesian category equipped with a *reverse derivative combinator* R assigning to each f : A → B a map R[f] : A × B → A, satisfying axioms abstracting the chain rule for transposed Jacobians. The category **Smooth** of smooth maps is the motivating example: R[f](x, v) = J[f]ᵀ(x) · v, the Jacobian-transpose-vector product at the heart of backpropagation [2].
3. **Monoidal categories and string diagrams.** In a monoidal category, morphisms are drawn as boxes with input and output wires; composition is horizontal juxtaposition, tensor product is vertical stacking. Coherence theorems guarantee that planar isotopy of diagrams corresponds to equality of morphisms. Boisseau gave such a graphical calculus for optics specifically [7], which we exploit in §4.

The table below summarizes the categorical vocabulary and its machine-learning interpretation, a Rosetta stone we will use throughout.

| Categorical concept | ML interpretation |
|---|---|
| Object | Data space (e.g., ℝⁿ) or parameter space |
| Morphism | Function, layer, or model |
| Functor | Structure-preserving transformation (e.g., backprop) |
| Natural transformation | Uniform optimizer or reparameterization |
| Product A × B | Pairing of data with parameters or gradients |
| Reverse derivative R[f] | Backpropagation through f |

## 3 Methodology: Reverse Derivatives and the Para Construction

Our methodology is semantic rather than experimental: we construct categories whose morphisms *are* the objects of machine learning — parameterized functions, learners, lenses — and prove that the passage from model to training procedure is functorial. The two constructions that do the heavy lifting are reverse derivative categories, which axiomatize differentiation, and the **Para construction**, which axiomatizes parameterization.

**3.1 Reverse derivative categories.**

A Cartesian *reverse* differential category (CRDC) equips each map f : A → B with a reverse derivative R[f] : A × B → A obeying seven axioms, of which the most important is the reverse chain rule:

> Theorem (Reverse chain rule): In a Cartesian reverse differential category, R[g ∘ f](x, w) = R[f](x, R[g](f(x), w)). The reverse derivative of a composite is the composite of the reverse derivatives, threaded through the intermediate value f(x).

This is backpropagation, stated once and for all, in every setting where the axioms hold — smooth maps, but also Boolean circuits and polynomial circuits [2]. The axioms are the methodology's contract: any category satisfying them admits gradient-based learning, and the learning algorithms we define will work uniformly across all of them.

**3.2 The Para construction.**

Parameterization is handled by the **Para** construction. Given a monoidal category C, Para(C) has the same objects as C, but a morphism A → B in Para(C) is a pair (P, f) where P is a *parameter object* and f : P ⊗ A → B is a morphism of C. Composition accumulates parameters:

```
(Q, g) ∘ (P, f) = (Q ⊗ P, (q, p, a) ↦ g(q, f(p, a)))
```

Two parameterized maps differing only by a reparameterization isomorphism of P are identified up to 2-cell, making Para(C) naturally a bicategory [5]. The crucial fact: Para(C) is where neural networks live. A dense layer is (ℝ^{m×n} × ℝ^m, (W, b, x) ↦ σ(Wx + b)); a whole network is the Para-composite of its layers. Weight tying — the same parameter object used in multiple positions — is simply a diagonal Δ : P → P ⊗ P applied before composition, a one-line categorical description of a ubiquitous engineering trick.

**3.3 Learners and lenses.**

Our methodology, then, is to take these constructions as definitions and derive their consequences: implement them, draw them, and apply them to architectures.

## 4 Deep Dive: Lenses, Optics, and the Architecture of Learning

### 4.1 Lenses and the get/put laws

A (concrete, asymmetric) lens S → A is a pair of functions get : S → A and put : S × A → S. In Haskell:

```haskell
-- A simple lens: get reads the focus, put writes it back.
data Lens s a = Lens
  { get :: s -> a
  , put :: s -> a -> s
  }

-- The three lens laws, stated as properties:
-- 1. GetPut:  put s (get s)      == s        (reading then writing changes nothing)
-- 2. PutGet:  get (put s a)      == a        (writing then reading returns what was written)
-- 3. PutPut:  put (put s a) b    == put s b  (the last write wins)
```

Lawful lenses compose: given Lens s a and Lens a b, the composite has get = get₂ ∘ get₁ and a put that threads the intermediate value through both puts. Riley generalized this to *optics* — lenses, prisms, traversals, and grates as instances of one coend construction — and proved that the optic construction is itself functorial on symmetric monoidal categories [3]. The lens laws are not bureaucratic constraints; they are the conditions under which bidirectional information flow is *coherent*, and their failure modes (e.g., a put that ignores its input) correspond precisely to gradient pathologies like vanishing updates.

### 4.2 Backpropagation as a lens

Consider a parameterized smooth map (P, f) with f : P × A → B. In a reverse derivative category, it induces a *parametric lens* whose forward leg is f and whose backward leg is the reverse derivative R[f] : (P × A) × B → (P × A), returning both the parameter update direction and the input gradient [2]. Schematically:

```haskell
-- Backprop-as-lens, idealized in Haskell-like pseudocode.
-- s = (params, input); the "focus" a = output.
backpropLens :: (Params -> Input -> Output) -> Lens (Params, Input) Output
backpropLens f = Lens
  { get = \(p, x) -> f p x
  , put = \(p, x) dv ->
      let (dp, dx) = reverseDerivative f (p, x) dv
      in ((p, x), dp)  -- updated params paired with input gradient
  }
```

The forward pass is *get*; the backward pass is *put*. Lens composition is the chain rule: composing the lenses of two layers yields exactly the backpropagation equations for the composite network, because the composite's put threads the cotangent vector through R[g] then R[f] as in the reverse chain rule [2]. This is why frameworks like JAX and PyTorch get backprop "for free" from a trace of primitives: each primitive is a lens, and the framework composes them.

> Theorem: In the category Para(Lens(C)) of parametric lenses over a reverse derivative category C, composition of lenses coincides with backpropagation through the composite model. The assignment of its parametric lens to each parameterized map extends to a functor [2][5].

### 4.3 Parametric lenses and the Para construction

Parameters enter through Para. A morphism of Para(**Euc**) is (P, f); applying the reverse-derivative functor turns it into a parametric lens (P, f, f*) where f* is the backward pass. Optimizers become 2-cells: Adam and AdaGrad differ only in their *update* maps u : P × P → P, which are reparameterizations of the parameter wire, while loss functions are parametric lenses composed on the output side [2]. This yields a striking modularity result:

1. *Models* are 1-cells in Para(Lens(C)).
2. *Losses* are parametric lenses B → L composed after the model.
3. *Optimizers* are 2-cells reparameterizing P.
4. *Training* is the composite; its backward leg is the full backpropagation algorithm.

Changing the optimizer therefore cannot break backpropagation — it acts on a different wire. The string-diagram picture makes this vivid: parameters travel on their own wire, data on another, gradients flow backward along both, and the optimizer is a box sitting on the parameter wire alone [7].

### 4.4 String diagrams for neural architectures

String diagrams turn the algebra into geometry. A layer is a box; wires are typed by spaces; composition is plugging boxes together; the tensor product stacks them. A parametric lens adds the parameter wire and the backward-flowing cotangent wires, drawn below the forward wires in the graphical calculus of optics [7]. In this notation:

- A *multilayer perceptron* is a horizontal chain of lens-boxes, forward wires on top, gradient wires beneath.
- *Weight tying* (as in RNNs or Siamese networks) is a parameter wire that forks via the diagonal Δ and enters two boxes — sharing made visible.
- A *residual connection* is a wire that bypasses a box and is added (via the Cartesian structure) to its output.
- *Attention* is a box whose query wire modulates the composition of key and value wires — a parameterized optic whose residual is the attention distribution.

The coherence theorems for monoidal categories guarantee that sliding boxes along wires and straightening wires do not change the denoted morphism: architecture diagrams drawn by engineers *are* proofs of equality of learning algorithms, up to the axioms. Boisseau's string diagrams for optics give this sketch a precise syntax [7].

## 5 Empirical and Theoretical Validation: Proofs and Case Studies

We validate the framework in two registers: theorems it proves and architectures it explains.

**Case study 1: Weight tying.** Tying the weights of two layers is the composite (P, f) with the diagonal: a ↦ f(p, f(p, a)) where p is shared. Functoriality of the Para→Lens assignment guarantees the backward pass automatically sums the gradients from both uses of p — the familiar "shared weights get summed gradients" rule falls out of the diagonal's reverse derivative, no special-casing required [2].

**Case study 2: GANs.** A generative adversarial network pairs two parametric lenses — generator G : Z → X and discriminator D : X → (0,1) — composed in *opposition*, the generator's loss being the negation of the discriminator's. Their backward passes meet at the wire X carrying cotangents of opposite sign, and the minimax game is the composite of two learners whose request functions disagree on the shared interface, a structure already linked to open games [1]. Mode collapse is then a lens-law violation: the generator's *put* fails to be surjective onto the discriminator's demand.

**Case study 3: Recurrent networks as coalgebras.** A recurrent cell is a parameterized map c : P × (H × A) → H × B. Unrolling it in time is iteration of an endofunctor; the infinite unrolling is the final coalgebra. Backpropagation through time is the reverse derivative of the iterated map, and its correctness is the reverse chain rule applied inductively — a proof by coinduction. Spivak's embedding of learners into polynomial functors makes this precise: a learner is a dynamical system, gradient descent is its update dynamics, and an RNN is a coalgebra in the same category [6].

**Theorem (compositionality of training).** Let F : Para(**Euc**) → Para(Lens(**Smooth**)) be the functor sending (P, f) to its parametric lens. Then for any composite model M = M_n ∘ ⋯ ∘ M_1, the backward leg of F(M) equals the backpropagation algorithm applied to M. *Proof sketch.* By functoriality, F(M) = F(M_n) ∘ ⋯ ∘ F(M_1) as lenses; lens composition threads cotangents through the reverse derivatives in reverse order, which is exactly the chain-rule recursion of backpropagation. ∎

A Python implementation accompanying [2] demonstrates the practical payoff: models, losses, and optimizers are assembled from the same lens combinators and trained on standard benchmarks, with the categorical structure guaranteeing that components compose correctly.

| Architecture | Categorical structure | Backward-pass meaning |
|---|---|---|
| MLP | Composite of parametric lenses | Chain rule |
| Weight tying | Diagonal Δ on parameter wire | Summed gradients |
| ResNet | Bypass wire + addition | Gradient shortcut |
| RNN | Coalgebra for cell endofunctor | Backprop through time |
| GAN | Opposed lens composition | Adversarial cotangents |
| Attention | Parameterized optic | Weighted gradient routing |

## 6 Limitations

The categorical program is not without honest limitations. First, **abstraction overhead**: the machinery of bicategories, actegories, and Tambara modules is a steep on-ramp, and for many practitioners the chain rule on a whiteboard remains the more productive tool. The framework must earn its keep in theorems and bugs prevented, not elegance alone.

Second, **lawfulness is not automatic**. Real networks routinely violate the lens laws: non-differentiable operations (ReLU at zero, argmax, sampling) have no reverse derivative in **Smooth**, and stochastic nodes break the determinism the basic theory assumes. Extensions to probabilistic optics exist but are less developed, and the elegant statements of §4 degrade into subgradient inclusions and score-function estimators.

Third, **performance**. String diagrams and lens combinators are a semantics, not an implementation strategy; production frameworks achieve their speed through kernel fusion, memory planning, and hardware-specific code generation that the categorical picture does not address. The Python implementation of [2] is a proof of concept, not a competitor to PyTorch.

Fourth, **the functoriality conditions are nontrivial**. Fong et al.'s original functor required the error function to satisfy an invertibility condition on partial derivatives that does not hold for standard losses [1]; Cruttwell et al. removed it by restructuring the framework around parametric lenses rather than a single monolithic functor [2].

Finally, much of the theory is *descriptive* rather than *prescriptive*: it explains why backpropagation works but has not yet produced a widely adopted novel optimizer or architecture. Gavranović's thesis conjectures that weighted optics give a full denotational and operational semantics for differentiation, but the general case remains unproven [5].

## 7 Conclusion

We began with a provocation: backpropagation is not an algorithm but a structure. The evidence surveyed here supports it. Parameterized functions form the bicategory Para(**Euc**); differentiation is axiomatized by reverse derivative categories; the passage from a model to its training procedure is functorial [1][2]; learners are lenses [4]; architectures are string diagrams [7]; and optimizers, losses, and models are lenses, 2-cells, and 1-cells in one uniform picture [5][6].

Three lessons stand out. First, *compositionality is the point*: the chain rule, lens composition, and functoriality are one idea, and backpropagation's power comes from preserving composition rather than from any cleverness in its steps. Second, *bidirectionality is fundamental*: every forward computation that learns carries a backward computation, and lenses are the native shape of that duality. Third, *parameters deserve their own wire*: the Para construction's separation of the parameter wire from the data wire is what makes optimizers, weight tying, and meta-learning into clean categorical operations instead of engineering hacks.

The research frontier is clear: extend the lens semantics to stochastic and higher-order differentiation, prove the weighted-optics conjecture, and turn the graphical calculus into tooling — differentiable programming environments where drawing the architecture *is* writing the proof of its training algorithm. Category theory began as a language for comparing mathematical structures; in machine learning, it is becoming the language in which learning itself is written.
