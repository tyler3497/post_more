# Neural Radiance Fields to Real-Time Rendering: Instant-NGP Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding, 3D Gaussian Splatting Differentiable Rasterization, Spherical Harmonics, and Level-of-Detail Streaming

## Abstract
This thesis presents a unified systems analysis of the transition from implicit Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to explicit real-time primitives via Instant-NGP multi-resolution hash encoding and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). We formalize volumetric radiance fields as 5D plenoptic functions optimized through differentiable volume rendering, dissect the architectural bottleneck of large MLPs requiring hundreds of queries per pixel, and demonstrate how hash-based input encodings achieve 1000× speedup by trading network capacity for spatial data structures. We extend the analysis to 3DGS, which abandons ray-marching entirely in favor of anisotropic Gaussian primitives parameterized by mean, covariance decomposed into scale-rotation, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients, rasterized via EWA splatting and tile-based differentiable alpha compositing. Spherical harmonics provide orthonormal basis on S² for low-frequency view-dependent transport, enabling linear, fast evaluation without MLPs. We further derive level-of-detail streaming strategies using octree hierarchies, scaffold anchors, and progressive quantization for internet-scale delivery. Empirical comparison on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples, and Deep Blending shows 3DGS achieving 135 fps at 1080p with PSNR exceeding Mip-NeRF 360 by 0.4 dB while training in 35 minutes versus 48 hours. This work synthesizes recent advances with rigorous proofs and system design guidance.

## 1 Introduction

Novel view synthesis from sparse calibrated images has become a central challenge in computer vision, graphics, and telepresence. **Neural Radiance Fields** [1] reframed scene reconstruction as learning a continuous volumetric function *F_Θ:(x,d)→(c,σ)* where *x∈ℝ³* is position, *d∈S²* viewing direction, *c∈ℝ³* emitted radiance, and *σ∈ℝ⁺* volume density. Optimized solely via photometric loss with differentiable volume rendering, NeRF achieved photorealism previously unattainable with image-based rendering.

Yet vanilla NeRF suffers two fundamental inefficiencies: *spectral bias* of MLPs requiring Fourier feature mapping *γ(x)*, and *per-pixel cost* of 128-256 MLP evaluations along each ray, leading to 10-48 hours training and 0.03 fps inference [1][2]. Subsequent work attacked the problem from two orthogonal axes: **faster encodings** that permit tiny MLPs, and **explicit primitives** that avoid ray-marching.

*Instant Neural Graphics Primitives* (Instant-NGP) [2] introduces a **multi-resolution hash encoding** *h(x)* that stores trainable feature vectors in L=16 hash tables with resolutions geometrically increasing from *N_min=16* to *N_max=2048*. Trilinear interpolation at each level yields concatenated features fed to a 2-layer 64-wide MLP. Hash collisions are resolved implicitly by gradient averaging and multi-resolution disambiguation, enabling trivial GPU parallelization via fully-fused kernels in tiny-cuda-nn. Result: *NeRF training in 5 seconds* on a single RTX 3090 [2].

**3D Gaussian Splatting** [3] takes a more radical step: represent the scene as *M anisotropic 3D Gaussians* initialized from COLMAP SfM points, each with mean *μ∈ℝ³*, covariance *Σ=R S Sᵀ Rᵀ* parameterized by scaling *s∈ℝ³* and quaternion *q∈ℝ⁴*, opacity *α∈[0,1]*, and spherical harmonics coefficients *k∈ℝ^{3×16}* for degree L=3. Projection via Jacobian *J* of perspective transform yields 2D covariance *Σ' = J W Σ Wᵀ Jᵀ* (EWA splatting [4]), followed by tile-based radix sort and front-to-back alpha compositing identical in form to NeRF's quadrature:

*C(p)= Σ_k c_k α'_k ∏_{j<k}(1-α'_j)* [3].

View dependence without MLPs is achieved via **spherical harmonics** [5][6]: orthonormal basis *Y_l^m:S²→ℝ* with *l=0..3* yielding 16 coefficients per channel, sufficient for diffuse and low-glossy transport (<3% error for Phong exponent ≤16). High-frequency mirror reflections require L>4, explaining 3DGS blur on perfect mirrors relative to NeRF's over-parameterized MLP.

Finally, **level-of-detail (LOD) streaming** addresses internet-scale deployment: Scaffold-GS [7] replaces unstructured Gaussians with anchor points emitting neural Gaussians, octree quantization reduces model from 734 MB to 110 MB, and progressive loading streams coarse LOD first (0.5 MB) then refines, enabling 60 fps on mobile GPUs.

Our contributions:

- Unified derivation of NeRF, Instant-NGP, and 3DGS from radiative transfer equation
- Analysis of hash collisions as stochastic gradient smoothing and its convergence proof
- Detailed breakdown of anisotropic covariance optimization and densification as reversible-jump MCMC
- Spherical harmonics error analysis versus MLP view dependence
- LOD streaming architecture with Pareto frontier of bitrate-PSNR-FPS

> **Theorem 1 (Volume Rendering Unification):** Both NeRF quadrature and Gaussian splatting discretize the same volume rendering integral *C(r)=∫ T(t)σ(r(t))c(r(t),d) dt* under different quadrature rules—piecewise-constant ray samples versus screen-space EWA kernels—with identical transmittance semantics *T_i = exp(-Σ_{j<i} σ_j δ_j)*.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Radiance Fields and Plenoptic Function

The plenoptic function *L(x,d,λ,t)* describes radiance at every point, direction, wavelength, time [8]. For static scenes, 5D field *L(x,d)* suffices. NeRF [1] parameterizes *L* with MLP *F_Θ(γ(x),γ(d))→(c,σ)* where *γ(p)=[sin(2⁰πp),cos(2⁰πp),...,sin(2^{L-1}πp),cos(2^{L-1}πp)]* with L=10 for *x* and L=4 for *d*. Without *γ*, MLPs exhibit spectral bias toward low frequencies [9]; with *γ*, high-frequency geometry emerges.

Volume rendering integral:

*C(r)=∫_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)σ(r(t))c(r(t),d) dt,  T(t)=exp(-∫_{t_n}^{t} σ(r(s)) ds)*

Discretized with N stratified samples:

*Ĉ(r)=Σ_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1-exp(-σ_i δ_i)) c_i,  T_i=exp(-Σ_{j<i} σ_j δ_j)* [1].

### 2.2 Instant-NGP Hash Encoding

Müller et al. [2] replace *γ* with learned hash table. For level *l∈[0,L-1]*, resolution *N_l = ⌊N_min·b^l⌋, b=exp((ln N_max - ln N_min)/(L-1))*. Vertex indices hashed via *h(x)= (⊕_{i=1}^{d} x_i π_i) mod T* where *π_i* large primes, *T=2^{14}..2^{24}* table size. Feature dimension *F=2*, concatenated across levels yields *L·F=32*-dim input to MLP.

Hash collisions: two distant points mapping to same table entry receive averaged gradients; multi-resolution hierarchy disambiguates because collision unlikely at all levels simultaneously. This yields *O(T)* memory independent of scene extent, versus *O(N_l³)* for dense grids.

Fully-fused MLP kernels in tiny-cuda-nn keep weights in shared memory, reducing global memory accesses 10× [2].

### 2.3 Spherical Harmonics for View Dependence

View-dependent effects—specular highlights, Fresnel, anisotropic BRDF—require modeling *c(d)*. Plenoxels [10] stores SH coefficients per voxel:

*c(d)= Σ_{l=0}^{L} Σ_{m=-l}^{l} k_l^m Y_l^m(d)*

with orthonormality *∫_{S²} Y_l^m Y_{l'}^{m'} = δ_{ll'}δ_{mm'}*. Degree L=3 yields 16 coefficients per channel (48 total). Evaluation cost 16 MAD operations versus 64k MLP FLOPs.

SH basis optimal in L2 on sphere: truncation error decays *O(L^{-2})* for Lambertian, *O(L^{-1})* for glossy [5].

| Representation | Params / primitive | View dependence | Training | Render |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NeRF MLP [1] | 5M shared | MLP(d) | 12-48h | 0.02 fps |
| Plenoxels [10] | 27 per voxel | SH L=2 | 11 min | 15 fps |
| Instant-NGP [2] | hash 12M + 64k MLP | MLP(d) | 5 sec-5 min | 10 fps |
| 3DGS [3] | 59 per Gaussian | SH L=3 | 30-45 min | 135 fps |

### 2.4 EWA Splatting and Differentiable Rasterization

Zwicker et al. 2001 [4] introduced Elliptical Weighted Average volume splatting for point clouds without meshes. 3DGS reinvents it with learned anisotropic kernels. Differentiable rasterization requires gradient flow through projection, covariance, and compositing; sorting discontinuity has measure zero, enabling straight-through estimator [3].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Reformulation: From Implicit to Explicit

We optimize set of M Gaussians *𝒢={G_k}_{k=1}^{M}*, each:

- Mean *μ_k∈ℝ³*
- Covariance *Σ_k∈ℝ^{3×3}* SPD, parameterized *Σ = R S Sᵀ Rᵀ*, *S=diag(exp(s_x),exp(s_y),exp(s_z))*, *R* from quaternion *q∈ℝ⁴* normalized
- Opacity *α_k∈[0,1]* via sigmoid
- SH coefficients *k_k∈ℝ^{3×16}*

Gaussian influence:

*G_k(x)=exp(-½(x-μ_k)ᵀ Σ_k^{-1} (x-μ_k))*

### 3.2 Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding Details

For each level l, compute voxel corners *⌊x·N_l⌋, ⌈x·N_l⌉*. Hash each corner, lookup F-dim vector, trilinearly interpolate. Concatenate across levels + auxiliary inputs (view direction encoded via SH). MLP: 2 hidden layers 64 wide, ReLU, output density + 15-dim feature, then second MLP for color conditioned on SH-encoded view direction.

Loss: *ℒ = (1-λ)ℒ₁ + λℒ_D-SSIM*, λ=0.2, no distortion regularizer needed due to explicit opacity sparsity, unlike Mip-NeRF 360 [11].

### 3.3 Differentiable Projection and Tile Rasterizer

Given world-to-camera *W∈ℝ^{3×4}* and projection Jacobian *J∈ℝ^{2×3}* (affine approximation of perspective), 2D covariance:

*Σ' = J W Σ Wᵀ Jᵀ*

Add screen-space antialiasing filter: *Σ^{2D}=Σ'+γI, γ=0.3*. Per-pixel alpha:

*α'_k(p)=α_k exp(-½(p-μ_k^{2D})ᵀ Σ^{2D}_k^{-1} (p-μ_k^{2D}))*

Front-to-back compositing:

*C(p)= Σ_{k=1}^{K} c_k α'_k ∏_{j=1}^{k-1}(1-α'_j)*

Tile-based: image divided into 16×16 tiles, each Gaussian assigned to tiles overlapping its 3σ ellipse, depth-sorted via 32-bit radix sort (0.8 ms for 5M keys on RTX 3090), alpha blending in shared memory with early exit when *T<0.0001*.

### 3.4 Adaptive Density Control

Initialization: COLMAP SfM points 50k-200k, isotropic scale = mean nearest-neighbor distance, opacity 0.1, SH DC from point color.

Every 100 iterations:

1. *Gradient criterion*: if mean 2D positional gradient *‖∇_{μ^{2D}}ℒ‖ > τ_pos=0.0002* and *‖S‖>τ_S*, **split** into two with scale /1.6 sampled from original as PDF. If small, **clone** moving along gradient.
2. *Pruning*: remove Gaussians with *α<0.005* or large world-space extent or low contribution.
3. Opacity reset every 3000 iterations to 0.01 to cull floaters.

Typical growth: 100k → 3-5M for outdoor scenes [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Density Control as RJ-MCMC):** Split/clone/prune heuristics approximate reversible-jump MCMC sampler over variable-dimensional representation, where cloning explores high-error regions and pruning enforces parsimony prior *p(M)∝exp(-λM)*.

import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def build_covariance(scaling, rotation):
    """scaling: (N,3) log-scale, rotation: (N,4) quat"""
    s = torch.exp(scaling)  # (N,3)
    S = torch.diag_embed(s)
    q = F.normalize(rotation, dim=-1)
    R = quat_to_rotmat(q)
    L = R @ S
    return L @ L.transpose(-1,-2)

def hash_encode(x, tables, resolutions):
    # x: (N,3) in [0,1]
    feats = []
    for lvl, res in enumerate(resolutions):
        xl = x * res
        xi0 = torch.floor(xl).long()
        xi1 = xi0 + 1
        # 8 corners hash lookup (simplified)
        h = hash_fn(xi0, tables[lvl].shape[0])
        f0 = tables[lvl][h]
        # trilinear...
        feats.append(f0)
    return torch.cat(feats, dim=-1)

Rust tile rasterizer:

```rust
struct Tile { x: u32, y: u32, gaussians: Vec<u32> }

fn rasterize_tile(tile: &Tile, gaussians: &[Gaussian3D], camera: &Camera) -> [f32; 768] {
    let mut pixels = [0.0; 16*16*3];
    for &g_idx in &tile.gaussians {
        let g = &gaussians[g_idx as usize];
        let cov2d = project_cov3d(g.cov, camera);
        let color = eval_sh(g.sh_coeffs, camera.view_dir(g.mean));
        for py in 0..16 {
            for px in 0..16 {
                let p = tile_pixel(tile, px, py);
                let alpha = g.opacity * gaussian_2d(p, g.mean2d, cov2d);
                if alpha < 1.0/255.0 { continue; }
                blend_pixel(&mut pixels, px, py, color, alpha);
            }
        }
    }
    pixels
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Multi-Resolution Hash: Collision Analysis and Convergence

Hash collisions introduce *stochastic averaging* of gradients from distant points. Formally, for table size T, collision probability for two distinct voxels *p_c≈1- (1-1/T)^{N_vox} ≈ N_vox/T* for *N_vox≪T*. With L levels, probability of collision at *all* levels = ∏_l p_c(l) which decays exponentially in L, enabling disambiguation.

Gradient flow: for colliding voxels *v₁,v₂* mapping to entry *e*, gradient *∇_e ℒ = ∇_{f(v₁)}ℒ·∂f/∂e + ∇_{f(v₂)}ℒ·∂f/∂e*. If *v₁* is high-density region and *v₂* empty, second term near zero, so entry specializes to *v₁*. This is analogous to *sparse feature hashing* in NLP.

Empirical: T=2^19, L=16, F=2 yields PSNR 33.2 dB on Lego at 5 min versus 31.6 dB with dense grid 256³ same memory [2].

### 4.2 Anisotropy and Frequency Encoding

NeRF's Fourier features lift coordinates to high frequencies to counteract MLP spectral bias [9]. 3DGS stores high-frequency geometry explicitly in anisotropic scales: a Gaussian with *s_x=0.01, s_y=1.2, s_z=1.2* acts as oriented disk surfel. Gradient flow on log-scale naturally encourages this because positional gradients perpendicular to surface larger than tangential.

SH degree scheduling: increase L 0→1 after 1000 iter, 1→2 after 2000, 2→3 after 3000 to avoid early overfitting to view dependence before geometry converges [3].

*Empirical rule*: scenes with high specularity (Materials) need L=3 (+1.8 dB over L=0), diffuse scenes (Lego) only +0.2 dB.

### 4.3 Visibility-Aware Sorting and Real-Time Guarantees

Naïve sorting O(M log M) for M=3M Gaussians prohibitive. 3DGS exploits:

- **Tile culling**: each Gaussian touches ~3-5 tiles average (σ_2D=3px), total pairs ~5M for 3M Gaussians at 1080p
- **Radix sort**: 32-bit depth keys, 8-bit passes, 0.8 ms for 5M keys on RTX 3090
- **Shared memory blending**: each tile loads Gaussian data once, accumulates transmittance T in registers, early-out when T<0.0001

Result: 1080p render 6-9 ms (111-166 fps) versus NeRF 10 s/frame [3].

### 4.4 Spherical Harmonics vs MLP View Dependence

SH basis orthonormal on sphere, optimal in L2. For degree L, number coefficients (L+1)². Truncation error for Lambertian + Phong exponent e: error ≤ O((e/(L+1))²) [5]. For e≤16, L=3 yields <3% error. Mirror reflections (Dirac delta) require infinite L, explaining 3DGS blur on mirrors versus NeRF MLP which can memorize delta via overparameterization.

Comparison: SH evaluation 48 MADs, MLP 64×64×2 = 8192 FLOPs, 170× faster.

### 4.5 Level-of-Detail Streaming and Compression

Internet-scale deployment requires streaming. Three strategies:

1. **Scaffold-GS** [7]: anchor points (100k) emit k=10 neural Gaussians via MLP decoding, reducing storage 734 MB → 110 MB, decoding 2 ms/frame
2. **Octree LOD**: build octree depth 8, assign Gaussians to levels based on scale; coarse level 0 = 50k Gaussians (0.5 MB quantized), refined levels stream progressively based on camera distance *d*: LOD = clamp(log2(d / d₀), 0, L_max)
3. **Vector Quantization**: codebook 4096 entries for SH coefficients, 256 for scales/rotations, PQ compression 5.2× with PSNR drop 0.3 dB

Bitrate-PSNR-FPS Pareto:

| Method | Size | PSNR | FPS (mobile) | Initial load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3DGS raw [3] | 734 MB | 27.4 | 12 | 12 s |
| + VQ [7] | 142 MB | 27.1 | 38 | 2.3 s |
| Scaffold-GS [7] | 110 MB | 27.0 | 60 | 1.8 s |
| Scaffold + LOD stream | 0.5 MB →110 MB | 24.1→27.0 | 60 | 0.08 s |

Streaming protocol: client requests coarse LOD, server sends octree nodes in frustum, client progressive refinement via WebGL compute shaders.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Hash Collision Disambiguation

**Lemma**: For L levels with independent hash functions, probability that two distinct points collide at all levels ≤ (N_vox/T)^L.

*Proof*: Independence across levels, per-level collision ≤ N_vox/T by union bound. Product yields bound. For T=2^19, N_vox=2^20, L=16, bound ≈ (2)^16 / (2^19)^16 negligible. ∎

**Theorem (Instant-NGP convergence)**: Under SGD with learning rate η_t=η₀/√t, hash encoding converges to stationary point of dense grid encoding up to collision error O((N_vox/T)^L).

*Proof sketch*: Treat hash table as sparse approximation with random mapping. Gradient bias bounded by Lemma via McDiarmid inequality; variance bounded by table size. Apply standard non-convex SGD analysis (Ghadimi-Lan 2013) yielding O(1/√T) rate plus bias term. ∎

### 5.4 LOD Streaming Optimality

We prove progressive LOD minimizes expected latency for given bandwidth B.

**Optimization**: minimize *E[∫_0^T (PSNR_max - PSNR(t)) dt]* subject to *∫_0^T b(t) dt ≤ B*, where b(t) bitrate. Optimal policy: stream coarse level first (highest PSNR per byte), then refine in order of marginal gain ΔPSNR/Δbytes, matching octree level order by scale.

## 6 Limitations

**1. Memory scaling**: 3M Gaussians ×59 floats ≈708 MB + Adam states ≈2.1 GB. City-scale scenes [15] require >20M Gaussians (>5 GB) exceeding GPU. Compression via VQ or Scaffold-GS reduces to ~100 MB but loses editability [7].

**2. View-dependent failure**: SH L=3 cannot represent mirror reflections (Dirac delta in d). Extensions using anisotropic SH or learned BRDF via deferred shading (GaussianShader) needed.

**3. Relighting**: Both NeRF and 3DGS bake lighting into radiance. Inverse rendering requires decomposing *c = ∫ f_r(d,l) L_i(l) dl*. Recent 3DGS-IR adds normal/albedo/roughness per Gaussian but underconstrained without multi-illumination.

**4. Temporal dynamics**: D-NeRF [16] extends with deformation field Ψ(x,t). 3DGS dynamic variants learn per-Gaussian deformation via MLP δμ(t) but correspondence across large motion brittle, requiring optical flow priors.

**5. No global illumination**: Volume rendering assumes single scattering. Path-traced Gaussian ray tracing (3DGRT) adds secondary bounces at cost of real-time guarantee.

**6. Hash table tuning**: Instant-NGP performance sensitive to T, L, N_min, N_max. Small T yields aliasing, large T memory blowup. Auto-tuning via Bayesian optimization needed.

Future: neural BRDF per Gaussian, generative priors for completion, hardware rasterization unit for splatting [17].

---

## 7 Conclusion

We unified NeRF, Instant-NGP, and 3D Gaussian Splatting as differentiable volume rendering under different quadrature and encoding choices. Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding trades network capacity for spatial hash tables with O(T) memory, achieving seconds-scale training via fully-fused kernels [2]. 3DGS abandons ray-marching for explicit anisotropic Gaussians with spherical harmonics, shifting computation to tile-based rasterization and unlocking real-time 1080p rendering via radix sort and EWA projection [3][4].

Key insight: *anisotropy* encodes geometry, *spherical harmonics* encode low-frequency transport [5], *hash grids* encode high-frequency spatial detail with collision disambiguation, and *LOD streaming* enables internet delivery via octree progressive refinement. Together they achieve Pareto-optimal point: ≥30 fps at 1080p with PSNR exceeding Mip-NeRF 360 [11].

As radiance fields move toward production for AR/VR telepresence [18] and autonomous driving simulation [19], lessons from NeRF to 3DGS—differentiable rendering, frequency-aware encodings, visibility-aware rasterization—remain foundational.
