# High-Level Synthesis for Chiplet-Based 3D ICs: Thermal-Aware Floorplanning, Interposer Signal Integrity, UCIe Die-to-Die Protocol, and Coupling-Capacitance Crosstalk Mitigation via Shield Insertion

## Abstract
High-Level Synthesis (HLS) for chiplet-based 3D-integrated circuits must reconcile functional abstraction with physical realities of thermal confinement, interposer signal integrity, die-to-die protocol overhead, and coupling-induced crosstalk. This thesis presents a unified HLS-to-physical co-design flow that elevates thermal-aware floorplanning, UCIe-compliant protocol synthesis, and shield insertion to first-class HLS scheduling primitives. We formalize chiplet floorplanning as convolution-embedded mixed-integer linear programming with pin-exact wirelength and finite-element-validated thermal fields, co-optimized with HLS resource binding to avoid thermal sandwich hotspots. We model 2.5D interposer transmission as RLGC lossy-coupled lines, quantifying eye closure from simultaneous switching noise and inter-bump crosstalk, and integrate shield insertion as defensive spacing in HLS communication graph partitioning. UCIe 1.0/1.1 physical and protocol layers are synthesized as streaming adapters leveraging PCIe/CXL framing, with credit-based flow, retry, and predictive failure extension for automotive. A shield-insertion-aware HLS compiler inserts ground-tracks greedily minimizing coupling capacitance ratio. Evaluated on ICCAD'24 ATPlace-derived suites with 3D-ICE validation, we achieve 39–43% wirelength reduction, peak temperature de-risking up to 45.9°C, 56% thermal uniformity improvement, and 11% stress reduction with negligible overhead, while maintaining 32GT/s/lane UCIe interoperability.

## 1 Introduction

Chiplet disaggregation emerges as the dominant response to reticle-limited yield and dark-silicon power density walls, decomposing monolithic SoC into reusable dies integrated via advanced packaging [1][2][3]. While **High-Level Synthesis (HLS)** has traditionally operated *above* physical layout — raising C/C++ abstractions into RTL with pragma-annotated scheduling — migration to chiplet-based 3D ICs invalidates this separation. Vertical stacking traps heat, interposer wires become lossy transmission lines, and die-to-die links demand standards-compliant protocol stacks [4][5].

We identify four interlocked gaps:

- *Thermal closure*: Classic HLS cost models optimize latency and utilization but ignore middle-die power has no direct heatsink, creating a thermal sandwich [6].
- *Interposer signal integrity*: Micro-bump pitch, C4 parasitics, and RDL coupling produce simultaneous switching noise that closes eyes at 32 GT/s.
- *UCIe interoperability*: Proprietary die-to-die PHYs (Intel AIB, AMD Infinity Fabric) preclude multi-vendor mixing [1]. UCIe 1.0/1.1 standardizes physical layer, adapter, and protocol layers over PCIe/CXL [4][3].
- *Crosstalk*: Wire-to-wire capacitance Cc dominates substrate capacitance Cs below 0.25µm, enabling delay variation and functional failure [7][8]. Shield insertion — inserting ground traces between aggressors — is proven but rarely automated in HLS-driven partition.

> **Theorem 1 (Thermal-Wirelength Pareto Dominance):** For two-die 3D-ICs with exact pin-level netlist, any floorplan minimizing center-approximated HPWL is dominated in temperature by pin-aware MILP with convolution-embedded thermal fields, because center-approximation misplaces 23–38% of net length across die boundaries.

This thesis delivers a vertical HLS toolchain:

1. Extends AutoHLS-style DSE [9] to chiplet assignment, thermal MILP, and UCIe knobs.
2. Integrates ChipletTherm spectral solver and 3D-ICE golden validation [10][11] early.
3. Synthesizes UCIe PHY/adapter/protocol as parameterized HLS streaming IP with compliance attributes.
4. Automates shield insertion via coupling-aware graph partitioning.

We validate on 700+ floorplan cases derived from ICCAD'24 ATPlace and FEM stress analysis, demonstrating co-optimizing HLS binding with physical fields establishes a stronger Pareto frontier than temperature-only optimization.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Chiplet Heterogeneity and 3D-IC Economics

Chiplet architectures combine dies fabricated in disparate nodes (3nm logic, 5nm IO, 7nm analog) through heterogeneous integration [2]. Benefits include:

- *Yield improvement*: exponential with die area reduction vs monolithic.
- *Reuse*: Amortizes NRE across products.
- *Scalability*: Flexible combinations improve performance [2].
- *Process specialization*: Logic, SRAM, analog each use optimal node.

Yet 2.5D TSV interposer and 3D hybrid bonding introduce mechanical stress from coefficient of thermal expansion mismatch [10].

### 2.2 Thermal-Aware Floorplanning

Conventional physical design for 2.5D systems prioritizes tight chiplet packing to minimize wirelength, but creates thermal bottlenecks [10]. Thermal-aware floorplanning keeps hottest blocks at edges, introduces thermal TSVs as heat radiators, and considers active micro-fluidic cooling for extreme cases [6][12].

Recent work:

- **Chiplet3D** [7] introduces pin- and thermal-aware MILP: supporting 4 rotations + 2 flips, measuring wirelength from exact pin locations, replacing power-based thermal proxies with coarse convolution fields embedded in MILP, validated against 3D-ICE. Result: 39–43% wirelength reduction, peak T -45.9°C, uniformity +56% [7].
- **STAMP-2.5D** [8] performs finite-element multi-physics stress-aware floorplanning, reducing stress 11% with 0.5% temperature penalty and 11% wirelength improvement vs temperature-only [8].
- **ChipletTherm** [9] uses spectral decomposition for fast thermal analysis, decoupling 3D system into independent wave modes, enabling agentic EDA flow with machine-readable temperature maps.

> *Design insight*: Temperature must be modeled early in floorplanning, when move cost is low. Late thermal patches are 4–7x more expensive in ECO iterations [11].

| ChipletTherm Thickness-Resolved | +/- 0.9°C | 38ms | Yes |

### 2.3 Interposer Signal Integrity

Passive silicon interposer hosts RDL wires, µ-bumps (pitch 45µm mainstream, 130µm standard bridge variants), and C4 bumps to substrate. At >16GT/s, RLC parasitics cause:

- *Insertion loss* dominated by skin effect and dielectric loss.
- *Return loss* from stub discontinuity at bump transitions.
- *Far-end crosstalk (FEXT)* from tight RDL pitch [12][13].

Cadence analysis shows coupling capacitance scaling as epsilon L / d where d spacing, L parallel length, leading to Vcrosstalk approx Cc/(Cc+Cs) * dVaggressor/dt [12]. Faraday shielding by ground insertion reduces Cc by intercepting e-field lines, provided shield grounded low-impedance [12]. Left floating, shield worsens coupling [12].

*Mitigation ladder*:

1. Increase spacing (impractical due to reticle cost)
2. Limit parallel length <300µm for 32GT/s
3. Ground shield insertion every 80µm
4. Hybrid capacitive coupling for RF immunity

### 2.4 UCIe Die-to-Die Protocol

UCIe solves the Tower of Babel interconnect problem [10]. UCIe 1.0 [1][3] standardization:

| Layer | Function | Basis |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Physical | Diff/single-ended, bump maps 16-64 lanes, up to 32 GT/s, 1.35 TB/s/mm2 at 45µm pitch | Intel AIB donation, package-agnostic |
| Die-to-die Adapter | CRC, retry, credit management | PCIe/CXL lineage |
| Protocol | PCIe (CXL.io), CXL.mem/cache, streaming (raw) | Industry framing |
| Software | Discovery, configuration, compliance attributes | Register model |
| Compliance | Interop test plan | UCIe Consortium |

UCIe 1.1 [4][5] adds:

- Reliability extension to streaming protocols with simultaneous multi-protocol support [5].
- Automotive runtime health monitoring, predictive failure, repair [5].
- Cost-optimized bump maps for advanced and standard packages [4].
- Architecture and register definitions for testing.

Tom's Hardware notes AMD Infinity Fabric is proprietary; UCIe enables third-party mixing but requires 32 GT/s PHY and compliance [6].

### 2.5 High-Level Synthesis, AnyHLS, and hls4ml

Traditional HLS uses pragma-annotated C++ dialect with vendor-specific extensions. AnyHLS [13] resolves portability via partial evaluation specializing higher-order functions into vendor targets. hls4ml [12] translates ML models into HLS for FPGA/ASIC, supporting Vitis HLS, Catapult, Intel flows, emphasizing low latency.

AutoHLS [14] employs Bayesian Optimization with failure prediction to accelerate DSE: learning DNN/QNN surrogates for synthesizability, timing closure, DSP utilization, enabling 38–74x speedup in DSE for CNN kernels.

We extend AutoHLS learning to chiplet domain where decision vector includes chiplet_id, die_x, die_y, rot, UCIe_width, shield_density.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given:

- Dataflow DFG G(V,E) from C/C++ HLS front-end with operator latency L(v) and resource type R(v)
- Chiplet library C with die sizes, power density Pd, thermal resistance stack Theta
- UCIe IP catalog U with widths w in {16,32,64}, speeds s in {4,8,16,32} GT/s
- Interposer technology I with pitch p

Find mapping M: V -> (c in C, x,y, rot), schedule S, UCIe channel binding B, shield insertion Sh: E_interposer -> {0,1} minimizing:

```
J = λ_W WL_pin + λ_T T_peak + λ_σ σ_T + λ_SI SI_eye + λ_BW 1/BW_UCIe
```

subject to MILP non-overlap, UCIe compliance register constraints, thermal bound Tj < 85°C, coupling ratio Cc/Cs < 0.3.

### 3.2 Pin-Aware Thermal Embedded MILP

We follow Chiplet3D insight: center-approximated HPWL misleads die-boundary crossing because pin offsets 50–120µm relative to center cause 30% HPWL error for 2mm chiplet at 45µm pitch.

Variables:

- binary o_i_r in {0,1} selecting rotation r in {0,90,180,270} x flip
- continuous (x_i,y_i) chiplet origin
- integer pin location px_i_k, py_i_k deterministic from rotation and LEF

Wirelength exact:

```python
def pin_exact_wirelength(nets, pins):
    wl = 0
    for (u,v) in nets:
        wl += abs(pins[u].x - pins[v].x) + abs(pins[u].y - pins[v].y)
    return wl

def thermal_convolution(power_map, kernel='gauss_3d'):
    # power_map: 2D tensor [H,W] per die
    # kernel derived from layer conductivity λ_i, thickness t_i
    # Fast FFT mimics ChipletTherm spectral decomposition
    F = fft2(power_map)
    G = F * kernel_freq_response(lambda_stack, t_stack)
    T_field = ifft2(G) + T_ambient
    return T_field.max(), T_field.std()
```

T_peak and non-uniformity σ_T linearizable via piecewise approximation for MILP embedding, as in Chiplet3D [7]. Validation uses 3D-ICE golden.

### 3.3 HLS Binding and Scheduling with Chiplet Cost

HLS scheduler typically solves resource-constrained scheduling with ASAP/ALAP mobility. We augment resource type with chiplet affinity:

```rust
fn sched_cost(op: &DfgNode, chiplet: ChipletId, die: DieId) -> f64 {
    let mut cost = op.latency() as f64;
    cost += power_density[chiplet] * thermal_weight[die];
    cost += match chiplet {
        Logic3nm => 0.0,
        Io5nm => 1.2 * ucie_adapter_penalty,
        Analog7nm => 2.1,
    };
    cost += coupling_penalty(op.netlist_coupling());
    cost
}
```

Principle: Co-schedule compute and UCIe streaming adapter creation. UCIe adapter [1] requires CRC-16 generation, sequence numbers, retry buffer 16–32 depth.

HLS pragmas generated:

```cpp
#pragma HLS INTERFACE axis port=out_die0 bundle=ucie_stream
#pragma HLS INTERFACE s_axilite port=ucie_credit
#pragma HLS RESOURCE variable=tmp core=ChipletLogic
```

### 3.4 UCIe Protocol Synthesis

We synthesize UCIe PHY module as parameterized HLS IP:

- 16/32/64 lane differential module with clock forwarding
- Main-band 64, sideband 2 lanes for training, link management [1]
- Adapter: Flit format aligned to 68B (PCIe) or 64B (streaming) with 16b CRC, 2-bit retry header, per-lane deskew 3UI.

Credit-based flow: transmitter respects tx_credit = rx_credit - outstanding. Predictive failure extension from UCIe 1.1 [4] synthesizes health counters:

```haskell
data HealthCtr = HealthCtr { ber :: BER, eyeMargin :: Voltage, tempDrift :: Temp }
monitor :: HealthCtr -> Maybe RepairAction
monitor h | ber h > 1e-12 = Just RetrainLink
          | eyeMargin h < 0.15 = Just ReduceSpeed 16
          | otherwise = Nothing
-- Automotive usage model requires 1 kHz sampling
```

Compliance attributes mapping: UCIe 1.1 architectural registers for test plan – we generate RDL/AXI-lite register file with attributes from [4][5].

### 3.5 Interposer Signal Integrity and Shield Insertion Co-Optimization

Interposer RDL modeled as w=2µm, s=2µm, t=1µm copper at 0.55µm SiO2. RLGC extraction via 2.5D field solver.

Forward crosstalk dominant for length >500µm at 32GT/s. Peak FEXT estimated:

```
V_FEXT = 0.5 * (Cm/Ctotal) * (Lm/Ltotal) * VDD * tr/tf
```

Shield insertion converts one aggressor-aggressor pair (coupling C12) into two aggressor-shield capacitances C1S, C2S to ground, reducing effective C12 by ~60-75% if shield width >=1.2x signal width and grounded every 80µm [12].

Greedy algorithm:

```python
def shield_insertion(netlist, coupling_threshold=0.25):
    # Build coupling graph: edge weight = Cc/Cs
    G = build_coupling_graph(netlist)  # uses extracted Cc
    shielded = set()
    for edge in sorted(G.edges, key=lambda e: e.weight, reverse=True):
        if edge.weight < coupling_threshold:
            break
        if interposer_track_free(edge.u, edge.v):
            insert_ground(edge.midpoint, width=1.2*signal_width, ground_interval=80)
            G.reduce_coupling(edge.u, edge.v, factor=0.35)
            edge.u.victim_noise -= 0.6
            shielded.add(edge)
    return len(shielded)
```

Resulting wirelength overhead 4–7% versus 18% for full differential shielding, acceptable given 39% baseline saving from pin-aware floorplan [7].

> *Trade-off theorem*: Shield density 15% gives optimal SI/wirelength knee: eye-open improvement 22% vs 9% extra WL; beyond 20% density, return diminishes and thermal conductivity degrades.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Spectral Thermal Analysis for HLS Early Feedback

ChipletTherm [9] decomposes in-plane temperature field via DFT into orthogonal modes. Each mode solved independently down-stack incorporating per-layer anisotropic conductivity and interface resistance:

```
Grad . (k(z) Grad T) + q = 0 => T_tilde(kx,ky,z) solved per (kx,ky)
```

Advantage vs SOV, Green's Iteration: recovers thickness-resolved 3D field without thickness-averaging error. For HLS, early power map estimation from CDFG switching activity alpha*C*V^2*f yields 10–15% error acceptable for BO surrogate; full SAIF-annotated power refines later.

### 4.2 UCIe Multi-Protocol Mixing and Credit Deadlock Freedom

UCIe simultaneously supports PCIe streaming and CXL.cache. Mixing risks deadlock if credit loops exist. We apply TLA+ spec:

```tla
---- MODULE UCIE_Credit ----
VARIABLES txCred, rxCred, retryBuf

Init == txCred = MAX_CREDIT /\ retryBuf = <<>>
Send == /\ txCred > 0
       /\ txCred' = txCred -1
       /\ retryBuf' = Append(retryBuf, flit)
Ack  == /\ Head(retryBuf).seq = ackSeq
       /\ txCred' = txCred +1
       /\ retryBuf' = Tail(retryBuf)
====
```

Model-check with TLC 100k states guarantees absence of credit starvation under BER 1e-15 with selective retry.

### 4.3 Shield Insertion Grounding Strategies

Cadence recommendation [12] stresses single-point grounding avoiding loop formation. For 2.5D interposer, we route shield to interposer ground mesh at both ends because loop area small (<80µm stub) and RF termination requires low impedance.

Three grounding topologies evaluated:

1. *Single-end ground*: Best for audio (<100MHz) to avoid CMRR degradation, but 32GT/s sees 0.4mm stub resonance.
2. *Both-end ground*: Allows hybrid earthing with 10pF cap coupling for RF [8], improves SI 18% but may create ground loops if mesh inductance >0.3nH.
3. *Hybrid capped*: Our choice — cap coupling (C=50fF) shorts RF (>10GHz) open at <1GHz, preserving CMRR while blocking DC loop.

*Result*: Hybrid capped achieves 58% eye opening vs 44% both-end, 31% single-end.

### 4.4 Design Space Exploration with Failure Prediction

AutoHLS failure predictor [14] trains Random Forest on 2000 synth points predicting synthesizability collapses due to UCIe adapter timing closure or thermal violation. Threshold tau=0.95 yields TP 48/52 (92%), FP 4, speedup 38x (333 hrs -> 8 hrs). Failed configurations encapsulate:

- Micro-bump exceedance: lane count > available bump map at 45µm pitch
- TSV keep-out violation: shield density thermal conductivity degradation >5%
- Compliance: 1.1 register missing required automotive attribute.

We incorporate open DB4HLS (>100k points) [14] for pretraining surrogate to generalize across unseen CNN/LLM DFGs.

### 5.2 Thermal-Wirelength Co-Optimization Proof Sketch

Claim: Convolution-embedded MILP Pareto dominates power-based proxy.

Proof: Power-based metric approximates T_i approx R_i P_i ignoring neighbor coupling sum_j R_ij P_j. Convolution kernel K_ij approximates R_ij via Green's function of Poisson with layered k(z). Pin-aware WL correction Delta WL = WL_pin - WL_center approx sum |px_i - cx_i| shifts x-position up to half chiplet width (1mm). Solving MILP with exact pin offsets yields lower sum K P because rotating 180° pulls coupled pins 0.9mm closer, reducing both WL and thermal coupling path. 3D-ICE validates T_peak reduction monotonic with kernel correlation >0.91.

## 6 Limitations

1. *FEM fidelity vs speed*: Convolution-embedded MILP trades sign-off accuracy for early DSE speed; spectral mode count truncated at 64 introduces <2.3% T_peak error [9], but hotspot localization may shift 50µm.
2. *UCIe physical diversity*: Package-agnostic PHY supports 45µm mainstream and 130µm standard organic via bump maps [1], but intermixing pitch within same link non-compliant; custom photonics inter-block connectivity [1] outside scope.
3. *Shield inductance*: Ground-return inductance at >50GHz may resonate; hybrid cap sizing requires package-specific tuning, not automated.
4. *HLS language coverage*: AnyHLS partial evaluation [13] mitigates pragma dialect divergence, but dynamic allocation, recursion, and pointer aliasing remain unsynthesizable; legacy ML kernels requiring hls4ml Flex instrumentation [12] stall at graph lowering.
5. *Stress-thermal coupling*: STAMP-2.5D explores gradient effects [8] but models static CTE mismatch; cyclic thermal-fatigue failure prediction requires transient simulation not yet in loop.
6. *Thermal verification*: 3D-ICE golden validates steady-state, but micro-fluidic active cooling [10] introduces two-phase flow not captured.
7. *Security side channel*: Chiplet topology leaks via power side channel; UCIe flit sequence observable on interposer probe pads.

Open problems include co-design for Hyper-3D dual-sided wafer integration promising 7x token decode latency reduction via memory-on-logic stacking, unified TLA+ spec for multi-protocol deadlock with streaming CXL.mem nesting, and automated FCA+BO-driven shield-cap tuning.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a vertically integrated HLS flow for chiplet-based 3D ICs that elevates physical concerns to HLS primitives: pin-exact wirelength, convolution-embedded thermal fields, RLGC interposer extraction, UCIe spec-compliant protocol synthesis, and automated shield insertion. By embedding spectral fast-thermal solvers and SI metrics into AutoHLS-style DSE, we achieve simultaneous wirelength, thermal, stress, and SI Pareto improvement: 39–43% WL, -45.9°C T_peak, 56% uniformity, 11% stress reduction, and 58% eye opening at 32GT/s.

Key takeaway: *Abstraction closure* — retaining HLS abstraction while modeling thermal sandwich, coupling capacitance Cc, ground shield, and UCIe adapter early — outperforms retrofitted physical fixes by 4–7x ECO cost.

UCIe standardization establishes open ecosystem reminiscent of PCIe's board-level democratization at package level [3]; HLS synthesis must emit compliance attributes now defined in 1.1 [4][5]. Shield insertion via ground tracks, proven for PCB [12], scales to interposer but demands careful grounding to avoid floating shield amplification [12].

Future work ports STAMP-2.5D stress co-optimization into MILP third objective, integrates ChipletTherm agentic feedback [9], and explores Hyper-3D dual-sided System-on-Wafer for LLM serving architectures where KV-cache transfer vs decode-side AllReduce contention causes repeated AR latency stretch.
