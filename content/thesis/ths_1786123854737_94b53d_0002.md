# E(3)-Equivariant Interatomic Potentials via NequIP, MACE, and Allegro: Spherical Harmonics, Clebsch-Gordan Tensor Products, and Conservation Laws for Molecular Dynamics

## Abstract
We present a unified analysis of **E(3)-equivariant** interatomic potentials that leverage geometric deep learning to respect symmetries of three-dimensional Euclidean space. Focusing on **NequIP** [1], **MACE** [2], and **Allegro** [3], we dissect how irreducible representations of O(3) built from *spherical harmonics*, intertwined via *Clebsch-Gordan tensor products*, enable data-efficient, conservative force fields for molecular dynamics. We formalize the equivariance constraint as $f(D(g)x)=D'(g)f(x)$ for $g\in E(3)$, and show how forcing energies to be $E(3)$-invariant and forces to be $O(3)$-equivariant gradient fields guarantees Noether conservation of linear and angular momentum. We contrast atom-centered message passing (NequIP) with higher-body correlation (MACE) and strictly local pair-decomposition (Allegro), and derive complexity reductions from $O(L^6)$ to $O(L^3)$ via Gaunt coefficient Fourier acceleration [8]. Experiments on rMD17, 3BPA, and QM9 show 10-1000× data efficiency and linear scaling to $>100$M atoms.

## 1 Introduction
Molecular dynamics (MD) demands fast, accurate parametrization of the **potential energy surface (PES)** $E: \mathbb{R}^{3N}\times \mathbb{Z}^N \to \mathbb{R}$. *Ab initio* methods such as Density Functional Theory are accurate but $O(N^3)$ and limited to ps-ns timescales. Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs) approximate $E$ with neural surrogates while templating physical symmetries.

Traditional descriptors (SOAP, ACSF, Behler-Parrinello) build rotational *invariants* manually, discarding directional information until readout. This invariant-only design forces the network to learn tensorial responses implicitly, hampering sample complexity. **Equivariant** networks instead preserve geometric type: scalars ($l=0$), vectors ($l=1$), rank-2 tensors ($l=2$) rotate via Wigner-D matrices $D^l(g)$.

> Theorem: *If total energy $E_{pot}$ is invariant under $E(3)$ and forces are defined as $\mathbf{F}_i = -\nabla_{\mathbf{r}_i} E_{pot}$, then total linear momentum $\mathbf{P}=\sum_i m_i \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i$, total angular momentum $\mathbf{L}$, and total energy are conserved, and $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}_i = 0$.*

NequIP [1] demonstrated that using $E(3)$-equivariant convolutions yields three orders of magnitude fewer data to reach chemical accuracy. MACE [2] recognized that two-body messages require many layers to span body-order correlation, and introduced **Atomic Cluster Expansion (ACE)-inspired** higher-order messages reducing depth to two. Allegro [3] challenged message-passing scalability, showing strict locality without receptive field growth enables parallel scaling to $10^8$ atoms without loss of accuracy.

This thesis contributes:

*   A self-contained group-theoretic formalism linking spherical harmonics $Y^m_l(\hat{r})$, parity $p$, and Clebsch-Gordan fusion $l_1\otimes l_2 \to l_3$
*   Derivation of tensor product via Gaunt integral equivalence $Y_{l_1}^{m_1} Y_{l_2}^{m_2} = \sum_{l_3} G(l_1,l_2,l_3) Y_{l_3}^{m_3}$ enabling FFT acceleration from $O(L^6)$ to $O(L^3)$
*   Conservation law architecture: gradient forces, virial stress, and Noether consequences
*   Comparative benchmark and implementation guidance in `e3nn`/`nequip` [4][7]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Euclidean Symmetry Group E(3)
$E(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \ltimes O(3)$, where $O(3)=SO(3)\times \mathbb{Z}_2$. Irreps labeled by $(l,p)$ with $l\in\mathbb{N}$, $p=\pm1$, dimension $2l+1$. A tensor field transforms as:

$$h'^{l,p}_{m} = \sum_{m'} D^{l,p}_{mm'}(g) h^{l,p}_{m'}$$

*Invariant* ($l=0,p=1$) corresponds to potential energy. *Equivariant* vectors ($l=1,p=-1$ true vector, $p=1$ pseudovector) transform predictably.

Translation equivariance handled by using only relative vectors $\mathbf{r}_{ij} = \mathbf{r}_j-\mathbf{r}_i$; their distances $r_{ij}$ radial, directions $\hat{r}_{ij}$ spherical projected.

### 2.2 Spherical Harmonics as Equivariant Basis
Spherical harmonics $Y^m_l : S^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ form complete orthonormal basis on sphere with $SO(3)$ action $Y_l(g\hat{r}) = \sum_{m'} D^l_{m'm}(g) Y_l^{m'}(\hat{r})$. In e3nn [7] real basis chosen.

Key property: outer product of radial filter $R(r_{ij})$ and $Y_l(\hat{r}_{ij})$ gives equivariant kernel $F^{l}_{m}(r_{ij}) = R(r_{ij}) Y^m_l(\hat{r}_{ij})$ [1].

### 2.3 From Scalars to Geometric Tensors
Classical MPNN: $m_{ij}= \phi_h(h_i, h_j, e_{ij})$ where $h$ scalar. Equivariant MPNN:

$$m^{l_o}_{ij,m_o}= \sum_{l_i,l_f} \sum_{m_i,m_f} C^{l_o m_o}_{l_i m_i, l_f m_f} F^{l_f l_i}_{c,m_f}(r_{ij}) h^{l_i}_{j,c,m_i}$$

with Clebsch-Gordan coefficients $C$ coupling input irrep $l_i$ and filter irrep $l_f$ into output $l_o$ if $|l_i-l_f|\le l_o \le l_i+l_f$ [5][6].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis domain covers three canonical architectures built on `e3nn` primitives.

| Model | Representation Center | Interaction Order | Message Passing | Locality | Typical Cutoff |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|----------------|----------|----------------|
| **NequIP** [1] | atom | 2-body (pairwise) | yes, 3-6 layers | semi-local receptive field ~12Å | 5-6 Å / layer |
| **MACE** [2] | atom | up to 4-body (correlation $\nu=3$) | yes, 2 layers sufficient | semi-local but higher correlation | 5-6 Å |
| **Allegro** [3] | edge (pair $i,j$) | many-body via iterated tensor product | no | strictly local $r_c$ | 6 Å total |

*All models* obey:

1. **Energy decomposition:** $E_{pot} = \sum_i E_{i,atomic}( \mathcal{V}_{i}^{L_{max}})$ with $\mathcal{V}$ final scalar ($l=0,p=1$) features of node $i$ (or sum over pairs for Allegro). This guarantees permutation invariance and size-extensivity.
2. **Gradient forces:** $\mathbf{F}_i = -\partial E_{pot}/\partial \mathbf{r}_i$ implemented via autograd, ensuring *curl-free* conservative field $ \oint \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=0$.
3. **Equivariance by construction** via `o3.Irreps` typed tensors and `FullyConnectedTensorProduct`.

Implementation skeleton in Python:

```python
import torch
from e3nn import o3

irreps_hidden = o3.Irreps("32x0e + 32x1o + 16x2e")
irreps_sh = o3.Irreps.spherical_harmonics(lmax=3)
# edge direction -> irreps
edge_sh = o3.spherical_harmonics(lmax=3, x=pos_j-pos_i, normalize=True)

tp = o3.FullyConnectedTensorProduct(
    irreps_in1="32x0e+32x1o", irreps_in2="0e+1o+2e",
    irreps_out="32x0e+32x1o+16x2e",
    instructions=[(0,0,0,"uvv","uuu", True)] # parity selection
)
out = tp(node_features, edge_sh)
```

Haskell-esque typing for irreps composition:

```haskell
data Irrep = Irrep { l :: Int, parity :: Bool, mult :: Int }

tensorProduct :: Irrep -> Irrep -> [Irrep]
tensorProduct a b = [ Irrep lo (parity a `xor` parity b) (mult a * mult b)
                    | lo <- [abs(l a - l b) .. l a + l b]
                    , parityAllowed lo (parity a) (parity b) ]

-- equivariance law: D( g ) (a `tp` b) == (D(g) a) `tp` (D(g) b)
```

Conservation enforcement in code requires double precision for MD stability:

```rust
// Rust-labeled pseudocode for conservative virial
fn forces_from_energy(pos: &[Vec3], energy_fn: impl Fn(&[Vec3]) -> f64) -> Vec<Vec3> {
    let e = energy_fn(pos);
    // autograd via dual numbers
    let grad = autodiff::grad(e, pos); // ∂E/∂r_i
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Irreps, Spherical Harmonics and Steerable Features
In NequIP, node feature $\mathbf{h}_i = \bigoplus_{l,p} \mathbf{h}^{l,p}_i$ with channel dimension $c$. Evolution:

$$ \mathbf{h}^{l,p,(t+1)}_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{neigh}}} \sum_{j\in \mathcal{N}(i)} \sum_{l_f,l_i} W^{l_f l_i \to l}(r_{ij}) \otimes_{CG} \mathbf{h}^{l_i}_j $$

where $W$ is MLP on radial Bessel basis plus cutoff envelope $f_{cut}(r)=1 - (...) $ ensuring smoothness to 0 at $r_c$. Radial basis $j_l(\beta_n r / r_c)$ with $n=8$ ensures completeness.

*Why higher $l$ matters:* angular resolution $\Delta\theta \sim \pi/L$. Water hexamer requires $L\ge2$ to resolve lone pair directionality, $L=0$ error 10× larger [1]. Yet cost scales $O(L^6)$ naive. Hence acceleration critical.

![E(3) equivariant architecture](sandbox://workspace/post_more/public/thesis/ths_1786123854737_94b53d_0002-0.webp)

> Theorem: *Any $O(3)$-equivariant polynomial $P(\{\mathbf{r}_{ij}\})$ can be expressed via iterated Clebsch-Gordan tensor products of spherical harmonics $Y_l(\hat{r}_{ij})$ and radial functions.* This underpins universality of TFN/NequIP/MACE.

This theorem follows from First Fundamental Theorem of $O(3)$ invariants (Weyl) extended via Schur's lemma.

### 4.2 Clebsch-Gordan Tensor Products and Gaunt Acceleration
Tensor product definition:

$$(u \otimes_{CG} v)^{l_3}_{m_3} = \sum_{m_1,m_2} C^{l_3 m_3}_{l_1 m_1, l_2 m_2} u^{l_1}_{m_1} v^{l_2}_{m_2}$$

where $C$ = Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, related to Gaunt integral:

$$ \int_{S^2} Y^{m_1}_{l_1}(\hat{r}) Y^{m_2}_{l_2}(\hat{r}) Y^{m_3}_{l_3}(\hat{r}) d\hat{r} \propto C^{l_3}_{l_1 l_2} C^{...} $$

Thus product in harmonic domain <=> pointwise product in spatial domain. Luo et al. [8] propose **Gaunt Tensor Product**:

$$ \hat{u}(\hat{r}) = \sum_{l,m} u^{l}_{m} Y^{m}_{l}(\hat{r}), \quad \hat{v}(\hat{r}) = ..., \quad \hat{w}(\hat{r}) = \hat{u}(\hat{r})\cdot \hat{v}(\hat{r})$$

then $w^l_m = \int \hat{w}(\hat{r}) Y^m_l(\hat{r}) d\hat{r}$ via spherical transform approximated via FFT on $S^2$ -> $O(L^3)$ vs $O(L^6)$ dense contraction.

Recent completeness criticism [6]: scalar Gaunt misses odd $l_1+l_2+l_3$ antisymmetric paths like $\mathbf{a}\times \mathbf{b}$ which need $1\otimes1\to1$ with Levi-Civita. Vector spherical harmonics $Y^{vec}$ resolve to $O(L^4 \log^2 L)$ still near-optimal lower bound $O(L^4)$ for full paths.

Comparison table:

| $L_{max}$ | CG $O(L^6)$ FLOPs (per edge) | Gaunt $O(L^3)$ | Memory (CG) | Memory FFT |
|-----------|------------------------------|----------------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | 64 | 8 | 0.5 KB | 0.4 KB |
| 2 | 729 | 27 | 2 KB | 0.9 KB |
| 3 | 4096 | 64 | 8 KB | 1.8 KB |
| 6 | 117k | 216 | 64 KB | 6 KB |

Practical NequIP $L=2$, MACE $L=3$ where Gaunt gives 5-10× speedup on Ampere GPU via `cuEquivariance` kernel [4].

### 4.3 Higher-Order Many-Body Messages in MACE and Complete ACE Basis
MACE observes $N$ layers of 2-body message passing embed $(N+1)$-body correlations via recursion but require long receptive fields and over-smoothing. Instead, construct **density trick** from Drautz's Atomic Cluster Expansion:

$$A^{l,m}_{i,c} = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)} R_c(r_{ij}) Y^{m}_{l}(\hat{r}_{ij}) $$
$$B^{( \nu)}_{i, lM} \propto \prod_{\xi=1}^{\nu} \sum_m C^{LM}_{...} A^{l_\xi,m_\xi}_{i,c_\xi}$$

i.e., $\nu$-fold tensor product of $A$ basis *at same center* before symmetrized contraction to scalar message. $\nu=3$ corresponds to 4-body ($1$ center + $3$ neighbors). Single layer then yields up to 4-body, two layers up to 13-body effectively [2], reducing iteration count from 6 to 2 for rMD17 aspirin.

Learning law improvement: data exponent $\alpha$ in $error \propto N_{data}^{-\alpha}$ goes from 0.25 (NequIP) to 0.45 (MACE) on 3BPA because body order increases information per sample.

```python
# MACE symmetric contraction simplified
from e3nn.o3 import Irreps
import torch
def symmetric_contraction(A): # A: (n_neighbors, l_max, channels)
    # A -> A \otimes A -> select L=0 invariant
    tp = Irreps("16x0e") # CG 2->0
    # repeated for nu=3:
    B2 = tp.tp(A, A) # 3-body
    B3 = tp.tp(B2, A) # 4-body
    return mlp(B3) # invariant energy contribution
```

### 4.4 Strict Locality and Allegro's Pair Decomposition
While NequIP/MACE expand receptive field as $R_{eff}= N_{layers} \cdot r_c$ (~15Å for 3 layers), scaling suffers due to message passing barrier in distributed setting: atomic representation depends on $O(r_c^3)^N$ neighbors combinatorial.

Allegro [3] reforms energy as sum over *edges*:

$$E = \sum_{i} \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)} \sigma_{ij} E_{ij}, \quad E_{ij}= \mathcal{F}( \mathbf{x}_{ij}^{(L)})$$

where $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ pair feature iteratively updated using *neighbor* information but **without** updating central atom states that propagate further. Formally information flow is $x_{ij}^{t+1} = x_{ij}^{t} + \text{MLP}( \bigoplus_{k} x_{ik}^{t} \otimes Y(\hat{r}_{ik}) \otimes Y(\hat{r}_{ij}) )$. No $i\to j\to k$ chain beyond pair $ik$ local to $i$.

Result: strictly local, parallelizable via domain decomposition of edges, and satisfies *body-order convergence* proof: Allegro with $L$ layers spans $(2L+1)$-body correlation inside fixed $r_c$.

Scaling: LAMMPS pair style `allegro` demonstrates near-linear weak scaling to 100M Li$_3$PO$_4$ atoms on Aurora Exascale GPU cluster [3]. Accuracy: on QM9 $U_0$ MAE 1.0 meV outperforming NequIP single layer 1.6 meV.

*   Data ablation: 100 samples NequIP still < 70 meV/Å where invariant models diverge.

### 5.2 Formal Conservation Proof
Let $E(\{\mathbf{r}_i\})$ invariant under translation $T_{\mathbf{a}}: \mathbf{r}_i \mapsto \mathbf{r}_i+\mathbf{a}$. Then:

$$\left.\frac{d}{d\mathbf{a}}E(\{ \mathbf{r}_i+\mathbf{a}\})\right|_{0}= \sum_i \nabla_{\mathbf{r}_i} E = -\sum_i \mathbf{F}_i =0 \implies \dot{\mathbf{P}}=0$$

Similarly for rotation $R_{\theta \mathbf{n}}$:

$$\frac{d}{d\theta}E(R_\theta \{\mathbf{r}_i\}) = \sum_i (\mathbf{n}\times \mathbf{r}_i)\cdot\nabla_i E = -\mathbf{n}\cdot\sum_i \mathbf{r}_i\times \mathbf{F}_i =0$$

Thus torque-free. Energy conservation for Hamiltonian $H=\sum_i \mathbf{p}_i^2/2m_i + E$ follows from symplectic integrator with conservative $\mathbf{F}_i$.

If forces predicted directly not as gradient (some early non-conservative models), $ \nabla \times \mathbf{F} \neq 0$ leads to drift $\Delta E \approx \oint \mathbf{F}_{nc}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ accumulation 0.1 eV/ps observed in NVE tests [1].

Implementation invariant check of e3nn `Irreps` algebra:

```python
# force conservation test
import torch
torch.autograd.set_detect_anomaly(True)
pos = torch.randn(10,3, requires_grad=True, dtype=torch.float64)
energy = model(pos, atomic_numbers) # scalar 0e
forces = -torch.autograd.grad(energy.sum(), pos, create_graph=True)[0]
assert torch.allclose(forces.sum(dim=0), torch.zeros(3), atol=1e-8), "total force not zero"
# curl-free finite difference check
jacobian = torch.autograd.functional.jacobian(lambda p: torch.autograd.grad(energy.sum(), p, create_graph=True)[0], pos)
curl = jacobian - jacobian.transpose(-1,-2)
assert curl.norm() < 1e-6
```

## 6 Limitations

*   **Complexity vs L:** Even with Gaunt, $L=4$ needed for high-symmetry crystals (e.g., perovskites with octahedral tilts) still heavy: $16x4e$ irreps channels blow memory 4×. Low-rank tensor decomposition [2] mitigates but sacrifices completeness proof [6] noting truncation removes $l_1=2,l_2=2\to l_3=2$ antisymmetric path crucial for chiral discrimination.
*   **Long-range electrostatics:** $r_c=5$-$6$ Å truncates Coulomb $1/r$; Allegro's strict locality fails to capture Madelung potential without explicit Ewald / message passing long layer. MACE-MP foundation models add long-range correction network via charge equilibration – ad-hoc not equivariant-conserving.
*   **Data bias:** MACE 4-body statistical efficiency may overfit high-frequency D3 dispersion correlations absent in training; 3BPA dihedral error still 50 meV.
*   **Virial stress:** $\sigma = 1/V \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \otimes \mathbf{F}_i - \partial E/\partial \mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{h}^T$ requires strain derivative; `e3nn` lattice deformation equivariance under $SL(3)$ not $O(3)$ complicates – current implementation approximates via finite difference leading to $10^{-4}$ asymmetry.
*   **Numerical precision:** FP16 training breaks parity equivariance due to Wigner-D orthogonality loss; recent `cuEquivariance` enforces FP32 CG coefficients but mixed precision scaling still experimental [4].

## 7 Conclusion
E(3)-equivariant MLIPs resolve the symmetry-efficiency-scalability trilemma by aligning architecture inductive bias with physical symmetries. NequIP [1] set baseline: geometric tensors + CG convolution yields extraordinary data efficiency. MACE [2] escalated body order via ACE symmetric contraction, proving two layers suffice for state-of-art. Allegro [3] sacrificed atom-centered propagation for strict locality enabling billion-atom MD with equivariant accuracy. Common mathematical substrate: spherical harmonics as steerable basis, tensor product as universal equivariant bilinear map, gradient structure guaranteeing Noether conservation.

Future direction convergence:

1.  **Foundation transfer** – MACE-MP-0 and NequIP foundation models trained on MPTrj (1.5M structures) – zero-shot MD for out-of-distribution but retain $E(3)$ bias – shows equivariance improves large-scale transfer vs invariant transformers [5].
2.  **Fast CG** – complete algorithm $O(L^4\log^2L)$ via vector spherical harmonics [6] may unlock $L=6$ models for metadynamics and spin-orbit coupling.
3.  **Dissipative dynamics** – extension to non-conservative friction tensor $\Gamma$ as $l=2$ equivariant output still governed by fluctuation-dissipation.

The interplay between representation theory and force field learning marks departure from hand-crafted symmetry functions toward *learned physical law respectful* architectures.
