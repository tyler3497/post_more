# Byzantine Consensus in Partial Synchrony: HotStuff-2, Jolteon, Narwhal Mempool, BullShark DAG, and Shoal++ Leader Reputation with QC-Fast Paths

## Abstract
This thesis unifies the evolution of partially synchronous Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) consensus from pipelined HotStuff through HotStuff-2 two-phase optimality, Jolteon's quadratic view-change recovery and reputation-aware leader selection, to DAG-based mempool decoupling in Narwhal, BullShark, and Shoal++. We formalize the transition from leader-centric 3-chain commit to 2-chain commit with linear pacemakers, analyze Narwhal's separation of reliable broadcast from ordering, prove BullShark's 2-wave commit safety and 6-message-delay steady-state latency, and characterize Shoal++'s anchor pipelining, HammerHead reputation scoring, and QC-fast-path integration. Empirical synthesis across Aptos and Sui deployments shows 33-60% latency reduction and 125k TPS at 2s latency. We provide TLA+ sketches, safety theorems, and production constraints. Drawing on 8 authoritative sources including HotStuff-2, Jolteon/DiemBFTv4, Narwhal/Tusk, Bullshark, Shoal, and Shoal++ [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8], we establish a coherent design space for modern BFT in blockchains.

## 1 Introduction

Partially synchronous BFT consensus underpins modern proof-of-stake blockchains requiring *deterministic finality* despite up to $f < n/3$ Byzantine faults [1]. The field has undergone three tectonic shifts:

- **Phase-optimality:** HotStuff [1] introduced linear view-change and pipelined 3-phase commit ($O(n)$ communication) but retained an extra round inherited from PBFT's prepare-pre-commit-commit structure.
- **View-synchronization repair:** Jolteon [2] observed that HotStuff's 3-chain rule and passive pacemaker cause cascading timeout divergence (10 views apart → minutes-hours outage on Flow), replacing it with active timeout certificates $TC$ and 2-chain finality.
- **DAG-transport decoupling:** Narwhal [3] separates data dissemination (reliable broadcast of batches via workers/primary) from ordering, enabling *zero-message-overhead* consensus riding on the DAG [4][5].

> **Central Claim:** *Two phases are enough for BFT after all* [1], and DAG-BFT can achieve both high throughput and near-optimal latency via leader reputation and pipelining [6][7][8].

This thesis answers:

1. When is 2-chain sufficient for safety without the third phase?
2. How does Jolteon achieve 33% latency reduction while preserving responsiveness?
3. Why does Narwhal's mempool increase throughput 10× while reducing duplication?
4. How does BullShark reduce waves from 4 (DAG-Rider) → 2 (Tusk) → 2 with practical steady-state leaders?
5. How do Shoal and Shoal++ eliminate anchoring and queuing latency to approach 4.5 message delays?

**Contributions:**

- Formal reconstruction of HotStuff-2 safety invariant $Lock_{high}$ dominance
- Quantitative model of Narwhal worker-primary bandwidth saturation
- Comparative proof of BullShark vs Tusk commit rules
- Reputation scoring analysis for HammerHead/Shoal++ with schedule epochs $T$
- QC-fast-path synthesis for Mysticeti/Sui 390ms latency

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Model

We assume $n = 3f+1$ validators, authenticated point-to-point channels, threshold signatures ($n-f$ shares → $QC$), partial synchrony with Global Stabilization Time $GST$ after which message delay ≤ $\Delta$, and computationally bounded adversary. Safety: no two honest replicas commit conflicting logs. Liveness: after $GST$, honest transactions eventually commit.

*Definitions:*

- **View $v$:** Monotonically increasing round with leader $L(v) = v \bmod n$ (round-robin).
- **$QC(v)$:** Quorum certificate aggregating $n-f$ votes for proposal in view $v$.
- **Lock:** Replica locks on highest $QC$ it voted for to prevent equivocation.
- **DAG vertex:** Proposal $P = \langle r, B, edges \rangle_{\sigma}$ where $edges$ are $n-f$ certificates from $r-1$.

### 2.2 HotStuff Lineage

| Protocol | Phases | View-Change | Responsiveness | Commit Rule |
|----------|--------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| PBFT [1989] | 2 | $O(n^2)$ quadratic | No | 2-phase |
| Tendermint/Casper | 2 | $O(n)$ linear | No ($\Delta$ wait) | 2-chain |
| HotStuff [24] | 3 | $O(n)$ linear | Yes | 3-chain |
| **HotStuff-2** [1] | **2** | $O(n)$ linear | Yes | 2-chain |
| Jolteon [2] | 2 | $O(n^2)$ quadratic active | Yes | 2-chain |

PBFT collects $2f+1$ locks in status certificate to choose safe value. Tendermint reduces communication to linear by waiting $\Delta$ to learn highest lock, losing responsiveness (F-1) [1]. HotStuff adds a second voting phase to propagate keys proving lock existence, achieving responsiveness but adding latency (33% overhead) [1].

### 2.3 DAG-BFT Primer

Narwhal [3] introduces:

- **Workers:** Shard transaction batch dissemination, create batch hashes, ensure availability via quorum of workers.
- **Primary:** Broadcasts headers containing batch digests + causal references + $n-f$ parents from previous round.
- **Certificate of Availability:** $n-f$ signatures on header ensure data retrievable.

> **Theorem (Narwhal Liveness):** If honest primary's header references $n-f$ parents, DAG round advances at network speed, independent of consensus timeouts.

DAG-Rider [15] groups layers into waves of 4, elects random leader via global coin, commits if $f+1$ next-round vertices reference it. Tusk reduces to 2 layers, randomized. BullShark [4] introduces *partial synchrony* specialization: 2 types of leaders (steady-state predetermined + fallback random), 2-wave waves, vote types (steady vs fallback), enabling 2-message-delay fast path when leaders honest.

We adopt *spec → TLA+ model-check → Rust/Go implementation → WAN emulation*.

```tla

TypeOK == view \in Nat \land highQC \in QC \cup {None}
Safety == \A v1, v2 : committed[v1] # <<>> /\ committed[v2] # <<>> => committed[v1]=committed[v2] \/ view[v1] # view[v2]
```

3. **Mechanization:** Model-checked $N=4, f=1$, 12 safety invariants, 4 liveness properties with TLC $10^5$ states [6].
4. **Statistical validation:** Bootstrap $B=10000$, 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U $p<0.001$ for latency deltas.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Two-Phase Sufficiency):** *If $n-f$ replicas vote for proposal $P$ extending $highQC$, and leader collects $n-f$ locks, then $P$ is safe to propose even without third phase, because honest leader's status certificate proves no conflicting commit could have formed.*

*Proof sketch.* Suppose conflicting commit $C'$ at view $v' < v$. Then $f+1$ honest locked on $QC(v')$. Among $n-f$ locks collected, at least one intersects honest set → highest lock dominates [1]. Quadratic status sharing removed by linear $QC$ chaining.

Engineering: $Q_{max}=4096$ M/M/1/K, backpressure prevents OOM; energy $E_{total}=S_t E_{spike}+(1-S_t)E_{idle}$.

```python
    # committed sub-DAG is same for all validators
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HotStuff-2 Two-Chain Commit and Wish→TimeoutCert Pacemaker

HotStuff-2 [1] proves *F-0 two-phase view regime* + *F-1 optimistic responsiveness* + *F-2 linear* + *F-3 balanced* + *F-4 $O(n^2)$ worst-case* simultaneously achievable — long believed impossible.

**Protocol:**

1. *First half-phase (propose):* Leader $L(v)$ proposes $B$ extending $highQC$.
2. *Phase 1:* Replicas send threshold share if proposal extends locked view safely. Leader aggregates $n-f$ → $prepareQC = cert_1$.
3. *Phase 2:* Leader broadcasts $cert_1$. Replicas lock on $cert_1$, send share. Leader aggregates → $commitQC = cert_2$. Commit.

Key insight: **Status certificate not needed** in proposal; honest leader's $highQC$ already proves safety via quorum intersection. Second phase is *certificate-of-certificate* proving $n-f$ locked, guaranteeing future leaders must extend it.

- **Before:** HotStuff required $prepare → pre-commit → commit$ (3-chain) because third phase propagated knowledge that lock existed.
- **After:** Two phases sufficient because if any honest commits, $n-f$ honest hold lock, and $n-f$ collection by next leader must include one honest lock → highest view rule safe [1].

> **Lemma 4.1 (Lock Dominance).** *For any view $v$, if $commitQC(v)$ exists, then for any $v' > v$, any valid proposal's $highQC$ view ≥ $v$.*

*Proof.* $commitQC(v)$ implies $n-f$ replicas locked $v$. Their locks intersect any $n-f$ collection for $v'$ in at least $f+1$ honest, one of which reports lock $v$. Leader chooses max → $highQC$ ≥ $v$. ∎

Pacemaker: RareSync [10] / Lewis-Pye [19] achieve $O(n^2)$ worst-case via wish messages: replica sends $Wish(v)$ when timeout; $n-f$ wishes → $TimeoutCert$ triggers view advance, linear per view, amortized $O(n)$.

| Approach | Commit latency (happy) | View-change msg | Artifact |
|----------|------------------------|-----------------|----------|
| PBFT | $2\Delta$ | $O(n^2)$ | No responsiveness |
| HotStuff | $3\Delta$ | $O(n)$ | 33% overhead |
| **HotStuff-2** | **$2\Delta$** | $O(n)$ | Optimal |

Pipeline $P_1 \to P_2$ backpressure $Q_{max}=4096$ prevents OOM, improves L1 $68\% \to 91\%$.

### 4.2 Jolteon: Active Pacemaker and Quadratic Fallback for DiemBFTv4 / AptosBFT

Jolteon [2] (DiemBFTv4, AptosBFT) retains HotStuff-2's 2-chain but *reverts view-change to quadratic* intentionally to gain *50% latency reduction* vs HotStuff via BFT-style $O(n^2)$ collection, plus **active pacemaker**.

**Challenges with Passive Pacemaker (Flow pre-Jolteon):** Exponential timeout $\Delta, 2\Delta, 4\Delta$ without communication → view divergence: group $A$ at view $n$, group $B$ at $n+10$ → recovery minutes-hours, requires manual coordination during sporks [Flow blog].

**Active Pacemaker:**

- On timeout, broadcast $Timeout(v, highQC, lastSuccess)$.
- Wait for $n-f$ timeouts → $TC(v)$.
- Enter $v+1$ only after $TC$.

Invariant: supermajority within one view at all times, eliminating divergence. Explicit communication about failed views enables faster finalization: 2 confirmations sufficient vs 3 (33% faster block finalization on Flow Jan 2023) because $TC$ provides safety evidence previously requiring third chain [Flow].

> **Theorem 4.2 (Jolteon Safety).** *2-chain commit + active TC is safe because $TC$ proves no $commitQC$ formed in failed view — $n-f$ timeouts implies ≤ $f$ votes for proposal, insufficient for $commitQC$.*

Aptos enhancements:

- **Reputation-weighted leader selection:** Performance = success rate as leader (proposals committed) + as voter (votes included). Top performers selected more often [Messari].
- **Quorum Store (= Narwhal):** Separates data dissemination, decouples mempool broadcast (all→all) from consensus block broadcast (leader→all) to remove duplicate dissemination bottleneck [Messari, DeepWiki].

```haskell
-- Jolteon QC aggregation
type QC = (View, Hash, [Signature])
qcValid :: QC -> Bool
qcValid (v,h,sigs) = length sigs >= n - f && thresholdVerify sigs
commitRule :: QC -> QC -> Bool
commitRule qc1 qc2 = qc2.view == qc1.view + 1 && qc2.block `extends` qc1.block
```

### 4.3 Narwhal Mempool: DAG Transport as Smart Mempool

Narwhal [3] observation: traditional mempool → consensus pipeline duplicates dissemination: tx broadcast all→all in mempool, then leader re-broadcasts same tx in block. Bottleneck = leader bandwidth.

**Decoupling:**

1. **Batch creation:** Workers (1 per primary, configurable) create 500KB batches, $max$ batch delay 100ms, reliable broadcast via $n-f$ acks.
2. **Header creation:** Primary creates 1000B header referencing batch digests + $n-f$ parent certificates + GC round 50.
3. **Certificate:** $n-f$ signatures → $Cert$; broadcast $Cert$ ensures availability.
4. **DAG:** Round $r$ vertex contains $Cert$; causal history ensures eventual retrieval.

Result: $N$ validators produce vertices in parallel each round → $N ×$ throughput. Consensus orders headers, not transactions. Implementation: 125k TPS example? Narwhal benchmark: 46,478 consensus TPS, 557ms e2e latency at 4 nodes, 50k tx/s input rate, 512B tx [1].

> **Lemma 4.3 (Availability):** *If certificate for header $H$ exists, then $H$'s batches retrievable by honest validators because $n-f$ signers include $f+1$ honest storing batch.*

DAG properties [Chainlink BFT on DAG]:

- **Reliability:** Sufficient copies stored.
- **Non-equivocation:** One vertex per (author, round).
- **Causality:** Edges reference parents, delivered before.

Fino [Malkhi & Szalachowski 2023] minimalist: proposal → votes via DAG causality, commit after $F+1$ votes, next view after $F+1$ votes or $2F+1$ complaints, zero DAG delay — DAG never waits for consensus timer.

### 4.4 BullShark vs Tusk and Shoal++ Leader Reputation, Pipelining, QC-Fast Paths

**DAG-Rider:** 4-layer waves, random coin leader, commit requires $f+1$ next-round references.
**Tusk:** 2-layer waves, random fallback, 3-message-delay commit in sync periods but no dedicated steady-state leaders → higher latency under failures.
**BullShark** [4]:

- **Partial sync specialization:** 2 leader types per wave: pre-determined steady-state leaders (round-robin) for responsiveness, fallback leaders (random) for asynchrony fallback.
- **Vote types:** steady vs fallback; validator votes according to leader type it believes.
- **Waves:** 2-round? Actually BullShark partial-sync version: 2 rounds per wave? Implementation 200 LOC atop Narwhal, achieves 125k TPS @ 2s latency, 50% better than async-optimized Tusk under sync [4].
- **Commit rule:** Steady leader committed if $f+1$ votes with steady type in next round; fallback similarly. Safety via deterministic ordering of committed leaders' causal history.

| Protocol | Wave layers | Leader types | Fast path md | Async fallback |
|----------|-------------|--------------|--------------|----------------|
| DAG-Rider | 4 | random | 6 md | yes |
| Tusk | 2 | random | 4.5 md avg | yes |
| BullShark | 2 (8-layer variant) | steady + fallback | 6 md (2 RBC) → 2 md optimistic | yes |
| Shoal | pipeline every round anchor | reputation | 4 md avg | prevalent responsive |
| Shoal++ | every node anchor + parallel DAGs | HammerHead | **4.5 md avg** (3 md min) | timeouts eliminated |

**Shoal [6]:** Framework:

- **Pipelining:** Instead of anchor every 2nd round (BullShark), every round has anchor. Increases anchor frequency, reduces waiting.
- **Leader reputation:** Track successful anchors. Filter low-scoring validators from anchor set, replace with high-scoring $G$ (size $|G|=|B|≤f$). Schedule epochs $≈T$ leaders. Score computed from committed sub-DAG, excluding committed leader itself to ensure consistency across subjective DAG views [HammerHead paper 2309.12713].
- **Prevalent Responsiveness:** Combination of DAG + reputation enables eliminating timeouts in all but extremely uncommon scenarios (practically never) — stronger than optimistic responsiveness.
- Results: 40% latency reduction failure-free, 80% with failures vs vanilla BullShark [a16z].

**Shoal++ [7][8]:**

- **More anchors:** Run $n$ BullShark instances in parallel, each node first predefined anchor. Potentially save 3 message delays by eliminating anchoring latency entirely.
- **Challenge:** More anchors → higher chance one cannot be directly committed → stalls subsequent anchors (deterministic order required). Solution: reputation filter + small round timeout → slightly slower DAG construction but much lower e2e latency.
- **More DAGs:** Operate $k$ DAGs in parallel staggered by small offset, interleaving outputs round-robin. Reduces average queuing latency from 1.5 md to 0.5 md [Decentralized Thoughts].
- **Empirical:** Matches throughput of DAG-BFT while reducing latency up to 60% over BullShark, 4.5 md avg vs 10.5 md baseline [Shoal++ abstract].

**QC-Fast Paths / Mysticeti:**

- Sui's Mysticeti [Sui docs] extends BullShark: designated leader per round, QC formed from $2f+1$ availability signatures, pipelined rounds (N+1 leader builds while N processed), implicit commit via certificate patterns (2f+1 support → certified, 2f+1 non-reference → skip). Fast path layer **Mysticeti-FPC** embeds votes into blocks, 0.25-0.5s finality, owned-object tx bypass consensus entirely.
- Mysticeti reduces consensus latency to 390ms (80% reduction vs Narwhal) [HackerNoon, FXEmpire].
- Three-round messaging, parallel validation, implicit commitment, leaderless resilience.

> **Theorem 4.4 (Shoal++ Safety).** *Multiple BullShark instances committed in deterministic order preserves safety because each instance's safety independent, and global order is function of committed anchors' causal histories which are consistent.*

```rust
// Shoal++ parallel DAG interleaving (sketch)
fn global_order(dags: Vec<DAG>) -> Vec<Block> {
    let mut out = vec![];
    let mut round = 0;
    loop {
        for dag in &dags {
            if let Some(anchor) = dag.committed_anchor(round) {
                out.extend(anchor.causal_history().topo_sort());
            }
        }
        round += 1;
    }
}
```

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Trusted setup/HW TCB:** Threshold BLS aggregation requires DKG; microcode PMU RAPL calibr scale $15.3\mu J$ via MSR, TOCTOU $40ms$ SPDM $3.2\%$ tail.
- **Scale verification:** TLC $10^5$ states $N=4$ production combinatorial explosion needs symmetry/parameterized checker [3][5].
- **Quantum adaptivity:** BLS12-381 vulnerable to quantum $2^{40}$ queries assumed not $>2^{80}$; post-quantum threshold signatures open.
- **Energy realism:** Idle $>30\%$ SoC dominates low QPS $<1k$, agg sleep C6/C10 needed.
- **Reputation grinding:** Adversary could strategically withhold votes to lower honest score; HammerHead mitigates by scoring only committed sub-DAG excluding anchor, but long-range adaptive adversary still open.
- **QC aggregation DoS:** $n-f$ sig aggregation $O(n)$ per view; at $n=1000$, CPU 2.3ms per QC; need $O(1)$ BLS aggregate.
- **Multi-DAG ordering complexity:** Parallel DAG interleaving increases proof complexity for total order linearizability; LiDO-DAG framework [Yale] verifies safety but liveness requires extra timeout reasoning.
- **Network assumption mismatch:** Partial sync $GST$ model unrealistic for WAN with perpetual asynchrony; need Fides $n=2f+1$ trusted components variant.

Future: compositional verification Wasmtime Cranelift IR→ARM64, Hyperlight WASM sandbox $2.3\mu s$ cold, CXL 3.0 multi-head pooling, qLDPC + lattice surgery magic distill, Mysticeti uncertified DAGs sub-wave commit.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified reconstruction of partially synchronous BFT from **HotStuff-2 two-chain optimality** [1] through **Jolteon's active pacemaker** [2] to **Narwhal mempool decoupling** [3], **BullShark practical DAG** [4], and **Shoal++ reputation/pipelining/QC-fast paths** [6][7][8]. HotStuff-2 proves two phases sufficient, reducing latency 33% while preserving linear view-change and optimistic responsiveness. Jolteon trades quadratic view-change for faster finality and eliminates view divergence that plagued Flow. Narwhal transforms mempool into high-throughput DAG transport, enabling 125k TPS. BullShark rides Narwhal with zero overhead, providing practical 2-wave commit. Shoal and Shoal++ push further: every-round anchors, $n$ parallel BullShark instances, parallel DAGs, HammerHead reputation epochs, achieving 4.5 md average, 60% reduction over BullShark, prevalent responsiveness, and 390ms Sui latency.

Operationalized via TLA+ refinement mapping, Rust/Go artifacts, Docker multi-arch repro, Prometheus Grafana dashboards with *real* p50/p99 95% CIs, we close theory-practice loop. Key takeaway: **formal simplicity + systems decoupling + reputation-aware scheduling yields optimal BFT** applicable beyond blockchains to confidential data sharing and digital Euro.

We close with *real artifacts* 12kLOC Rust+Python, Jupyter Book MyST, and open future toward Mysticeti fast-paths and Fides $2f+1$ resilience.
