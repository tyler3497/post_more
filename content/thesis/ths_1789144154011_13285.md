# Capability Hardware: CHERI's Architectural Memory Safety, Compartmentalization, and the Morello Evaluation

## Abstract

Memory-safety vulnerabilities have dominated the exploitation landscape for half a century, accounting for roughly two-thirds of high-severity defects in mainstream software stacks. The Capability Hardware Enhanced RISC Instructions (CHERI) architecture addresses this crisis at the instruction-set level by replacing integer pointers with *capabilities* — unforgeable, hardware-enforced references carrying bounds, permissions, and validity metadata under a strict monotonicity discipline. This thesis presents a rigorous treatment of CHERI's protection model: the capability format and its compressed-bounds encoding, provenance validity, the monotonicity theorem, sealed capabilities for object-capability compartmentalization, and the hybrid and pure-capability software models. We then analyze the industrial-scale evaluation on Arm's Morello prototype — a CHERI-enabled Neoverse N1-class research processor — covering performance measurements, software-porting experience, and compartmentalization case studies including network-stack isolation. Finally, we compare CHERI against contemporary alternatives — Rust's language-level ownership, Arm's Memory Tagging Extension, and AddressSanitizer — on the axes of spatial and temporal safety, overhead, and deployability, and we delineate the architecture's residual limitations: pointer-size growth, revocation costs for temporal safety, and capability-leakage vectors observed in real compartmentalized systems.

## 1. Introduction

Memory unsafety is arguably the most durable defect class in computing. Microsoft's Security Response Center estimates that approximately 70% of the vulnerabilities it patches each year are memory-safety bugs; Google reports similar figures for Chromium. A striking empirical claim — advanced by Microsoft's analysis of its 2019 vulnerability corpus — holds that CHERI, combined with complementary measures, would have mitigated at least two-thirds of that year's memory-safety vulnerabilities requiring security updates [9].

Conventional mitigations live in software: AddressSanitizer (ASan) instruments code with redzones and quarantine at heavy cost, and can therefore never be a production defense; stack canaries and control-flow integrity narrow particular channels but leave spatial violations intact. Memory-safe languages such as Rust attack the problem at the type-system level, but the trillion-line legacy of C and C++ cannot be rewritten. CHERI takes a third path: it *extends the architecture itself*, so that pointers become first-class protection primitives enforced by hardware on every access [1][3].

The research program behind CHERI spans more than a decade. It began with FPGA-based MIPS prototypes running a capability-aware FreeBSD (CheriBSD) [3], matured through a series of ISA revisions culminating in the CHERI ISAv3/v5/v6 technical reports [1], and reached industrial scale with Arm's Morello prototype — a CHERI-enabled Neoverse N1-class processor, SoC, and board shipped in January 2022 under the UK's Digital Security by Design (DSbD) programme [7][9]. The NSA's *Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps* now cites CHERI alongside Rust and MTE as a recommended direction for memory safety [7].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 surveys background: capability systems, fat pointers, and the hybrid capability-system model. Section 3 states the analytical methodology. Section 4 is the deep dive: capability format, provenance and monotonicity, sealed capabilities, and compartmentalization models. Section 5 presents empirical results from Morello and related evaluations. Section 6 catalogs limitations. Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### Capability Systems and Fat Pointers

Capability-based addressing is among the oldest ideas in protection — from the Chicago Magic Number Machine and the Plessey System 250 to KeyKOS and EROS. Classical capability machines, however, demanded a global capability namespace incompatible with flat-address-space C programs. Two research lines converged in CHERI:

1. **Fat-pointer literature** (e.g., HardBound, SoftBound): augmenting C pointers with base/bounds metadata to enforce spatial safety. These designs demonstrated that fine-grained bounds checking is *feasible*, but struggled with metadata storage overhead, compatibility, and strictness (Intel MPX, the industrial attempt, was deprecated partly for correctness gaps [1]).
2. **Hybrid capability systems**: CHERI fuses capabilities with paged virtual memory, yielding "a program-managed fat pointer capability model" that runs unmodified flat-address-space code while enabling fine-grained protection [3].

### The Protection Gap in Commodity ISAs

Commodity ISAs expose memory as a flat array of bytes addressable by integers. Any confused-deputy or bounds violation becomes a primitive for exploitation because *nothing distinguishes a valid reference from a forged integer*. CHERI's central insight is that the ISA should reify *authority* — the provenance chain by which a pointer was derived — as a first-class, hardware-checked quantity.

> **Theorem (Informal — CHERI Monotonicity):** Any capability derived from a set of capabilities grants no more authority than the capabilities it was derived from. Authority can only decrease along derivation chains.

This monotonicity property, enforced jointly by the capability encoding and the instruction semantics, is what makes capabilities unforgeable in practice and underpins every higher-level guarantee [1].

## 3. Methodology

This thesis follows a **primary-source synthesis methodology**: claims are grounded in the CHERI ISA technical reports [1], the early Morello microarchitecture performance report [2], the original CHERI capability-machine report [3], peer-reviewed and preprint evaluations of Morello software [4][5], the ECOOP 2023 Rust-for-Morello work [6], the NSA memory-safety roadmap analysis [7], and secondary reporting of Arm's own evaluation claims [9]. Where quantitative figures are cited (porting effort, mitigation rates, MTE overhead), they are attributed to the reporting source rather than independently measured. Comparative claims (CHERI vs. Rust vs. MTE vs. ASan) are organized along five axes: spatial safety, temporal safety, overhead, legacy compatibility, and compartmentalization support.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Capability Format: Bounds, Permissions, and the Tag Bit

A CHERI capability is a *fat pointer with hardware-enforced integrity*. In the 128-bit CHERI model (as deployed on Morello/AArch64), a capability comprises:

| Field | Width | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Address / offset cursor | 64 bits | The pointer value used by arithmetic |
| Compressed bounds (base, top) | ~27 bits (CHERI Concentrate) | Spatial extent of the referent object |
| Permissions mask | ~12–18 bits | Load, Store, Execute, LoadCap, StoreCap, System |
| Object type (`otype`) | 24 bits | Sealed-capability type for domain crossing |
| Sealed bit | 1 bit | Distinguishes sealed from unsealed capabilities |
| **Tag bit** | 1 bit, *out-of-band* | Validity: set only by legitimate capability creation |

The **tag bit** is the linchpin of unforgeability. It is stored in memory physically separate from the capability's data bits — in a shadow tag table consulted on every load — and is cleared by *any* non-capability-aware write to the capability's storage [1]. Consequently:

- Casting an integer to a pointer produces a capability with a *clear* tag, which traps on dereference.
- Bitwise manipulation or `memcpy` through non-capability instructions destroys validity.
- Capabilities can only enter the system through derivation from existing valid capabilities (e.g., `CSetBounds`, `CAndPerm`) or from the root capabilities (`PCC`, `DDC`) held in privileged registers.

**CHERI Concentrate** compresses base and top bounds into roughly 27 bits by exploiting the redundancy between the address cursor and the bounds: bounds near the cursor need few bits, and precision scales with object size [1]. The decompression occurs "in parallel with address generation arithmetic," so that, as Arm's Richard Grisenthwaite explains, "the bounds check is timed in a very similar way to a normal TLB hit" — bounds checking is *not* on the critical path in the Morello pipeline [9].

Permissions refine authority per-pointer rather than per-page. A `char*` used only for reading can be stripped of `Store` permission; function pointers carry `Execute`; capabilities to capabilities require `LoadCap`/`StoreCap`. This per-pointer least privilege is strictly finer than page-table protection and composes with virtual memory rather than replacing it.

### 4.2 Provenance Validity and Monotonicity

CHERI's security rests on two intertwined invariants [1]:

1. **Provenance validity:** every valid (tagged) capability in the system is transitively derived from a root of trust — the initial capabilities granted to a process by the kernel and loader. There is no instruction that manufactures a tagged capability from arbitrary data.
2. **Monotonicity:** each derivation step can only *narrow* rights: `CSetBounds` can shrink but never grow bounds; `CAndPerm` can only clear permission bits. No instruction widens authority.

```
Derivation chain (authority strictly non-increasing):

  DDC (whole address space)
   └─ CSetBounds → heap arena capability
        └─ malloc: CSetBounds → per-allocation capability [base, base+size)
             └─ CAndPerm → read-only capability passed to callee
```

The consequence is *deterministic spatial safety*: an out-of-bounds access cannot succeed by construction, because the dereferenced capability's bounds are checked by hardware on every load and store. Unlike probabilistic schemes, there is no tag-guessing attack, no redzone-gap window, and no reliance on allocator behavior [1][7].

**Temporal safety** is more subtle. Early CHERI provided it only weakly, through *capability revocation* and the local/global distinction constraining capability flow [8]. Full deterministic temporal safety — defeating use-after-free even when the attacker holds a stale capability — requires revocation mechanisms (sweeping memory to invalidate capabilities to freed objects, as in Cornucopia/CHERIvoke), which carry nontrivial cost. We return to this in §6.

### 4.3 Sealed Capabilities and Object-Capability Compartmentalization

Beyond memory safety, CHERI provides a **software-defined compartmentalization** substrate via *sealed capabilities* [1][5]:

- **Sealing** binds a code capability and a data capability together under an *object type* (`otype`), producing an opaque sealed pair that cannot be dereferenced or modified — only *invoked*.
- **Invocation** (`CCall` / `CInvoke`) atomically unseals the pair, switches the program-counter capability (`PCC`) and default data capability (`DDC`) to the callee's, and transfers control. This is the hardware primitive for *cross-domain calls*.
- Unsealing requires a capability with the `Seal`/`Unseal` permission for the corresponding `otype`, so only an authorized *compartment manager* can mint entry points.

Sealed capabilities implement the **object-capability model** in hardware: compartments hold only the capabilities they are granted, authority flows explicitly through sealed entry points, and the monotonicity theorem guarantees a compartment cannot amplify its own rights. Compartmentalization models built atop this include:

- **Library compartmentalization:** isolating risky libraries (parsers, codecs, TLS) within a process using a CHERI-aware dynamic linker (`libdl` extensions) [5].
- **Network-stack compartmentalization:** a 2025 case study isolated applications, TCP/IP libraries, and network drivers on CheriBSD/Morello, finding that CHERI "has the potential to enhance security while maintaining performance in embedded-like environments" [5].
- **Process and VM compartmentalization:** CheriBSD supports capability-sandboxed processes with per-compartment address-space views.

Critically, compartmentalization is *scalable*: unlike page-table-based isolation, which pays TLB and context-switch costs per boundary, CHERI domain transitions are designed as fast-path register operations [1].

### 4.4 Hybrid vs. Pure-Capability Software Models

CHERI supports incremental adoption through two ABI models [1][2]:

| Model | Pointer representation | Compatibility | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid** | Integers remain integers; only annotated pointers become capabilities | Near-zero porting; interoperates with unmodified libraries | Opt-in: only capability-annotated code is protected |
| **Purecap** | *Every* pointer is a capability | Requires full recompilation and porting | Complete spatial safety by construction |

The Morello evaluation explored both: SPECint benchmarks were run under multiple code-generation models, and language runtimes such as MicroPython were ported in both hybrid and purecap variants — with purecap showing measurable but (per the porters) acceptable overhead, e.g., roughly 2× wall-clock on AES-256 in the purecap interpreter relative to hybrid [4 in second search → MPLR'23]. This dual-model strategy is CHERI's answer to the deployability dilemma that killed fat-pointer predecessors: *security can be adopted compartment by compartment rather than flag-day*.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Morello: Industrial-Scale Evaluation

Arm's Morello — a CHERI-enabled Neoverse N1-class prototype SoC and board, shipped January 2022 under DSbD with £70M government plus £117M industry investment — is the first evaluation of CHERI composed with a contemporary high-performance microarchitecture and full software stack, at a scale "unobtainable via ISA emulators or hardware simulators" [2][7].

Key reported results:

- **Performance:** The living technical report *Early performance results from the prototype Morello microarchitecture* (UCAM-CL-TR-986) tracks SPECint across code-generation models on both baseline and modified FPGA designs [2]. The headline architectural result is that capability enforcement — bounds decompression, tag checks, permission checks — is engineered *off the critical path*, timed like a TLB hit [9].
- **Porting effort:** The CheriBSD X11/KDE desktop — some 6 million lines of code — was ported to Morello in *three months by a single engineer*, changing less than 0.03% of the codebase [9]. This is the strongest evidence for CHERI's "incrementally adoptable" claim.
- **Mitigation rate:** The same porting exercise yielded an assessed vulnerability-mitigation rate of **73.8%** — closely corroborating Microsoft's independent finding that CHERI plus complementary measures would have mitigated at least two-thirds of 2019's memory-safety vulnerabilities [9].

### 5.2 Formal Assurance

CHERI's protection model has been subjected to unusual formal scrutiny for an architecture extension: the ISA semantics have been formalized (notably in Sail/Isabelle), and the monotonicity and provenance properties have been machine-checked against the model [1]. This matters because the security argument is *architectural*: if monotonicity holds in the ISA model and the hardware implements the model, whole classes of exploit are ruled out by construction rather than by testing.

### 5.3 Comparative Evaluation: CHERI vs. Rust vs. MTE vs. ASan

| Axis | CHERI | Rust | Arm MTE | AddressSanitizer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial safety | **Deterministic**, hardware-enforced per-pointer bounds | Static (safe subset); `unsafe` blocks unchecked | Probabilistic (4-bit tags); intra-object overflows missed | Deterministic within redzones; gaps at boundaries |
| Temporal safety | Via revocation/quarantine (costly); precise with Cornucopia-style sweeping | Ownership/borrow checker (safe subset); `unsafe` escapes | Probabilistic tag mismatch on UAF | Quarantine delays reuse; not production-safe |
| Runtime overhead | Low single-digit % targeted; pointer ops off critical path [2][9] | ~Zero for safe code; `unsafe` unprotected | **1–2%** estimated [7] | 2–3×; test-only |
| Legacy C/C++ | **Recompile, mostly unmodified** (<0.03% churn) [9] | Rewrite required | Recompile + allocator support | Recompile + instrumented allocator |
| Compartmentalization | **Hardware sealed capabilities**; fast domain crossing [1][5] | Process/thread isolation only | None | None |
| Production deployable | Yes (prototype stage) | Yes | Yes (Pixel 8+) | No |

Three observations follow:

1. **CHERI and Rust are complementary, not rivals.** Rust's ownership model gives *temporal* safety that CHERI's spatial focus lacks, while CHERI hardens Rust's `unsafe` escape hatches: "Rust for Morello" demonstrates always-on memory safety *even in unsafe code* by compiling Rust to CHERI capabilities [6][8]. The NSA roadmap explicitly notes that "some hardware features like MTE are going to be needed even in systems written in [memory-safe languages]" because of `unsafe` code [7].
2. **MTE is cheaper but weaker.** At 1–2% overhead, MTE is the pragmatic near-term deployment [7], but its 4-bit tags are probabilistic and it cannot express sub-object bounds or compartmentalization. CHERI is deterministic at somewhat higher (but still modest) cost.
3. **ASan is a testing tool, not a defense.** Its overhead confines it to CI; CHERI aims to be the *production* realization of ASan's intent.

## 6. Limitations

A candid assessment must record where CHERI falls short of its ambitions:

1. **Pointer-size doubling.** Purecap doubles pointer size (64→128 bits), inflating cache footprint and memory bandwidth for pointer-heavy workloads. The Morello performance report tracks this cost explicitly across SPECint [2]; workloads dominated by pointer chasing pay the most.
2. **Temporal safety remains the hard problem.** Deterministic use-after-free protection requires *revocation* — sweeping memory to invalidate outstanding capabilities — whose cost scales with heap size and allocation rate. Without it, CHERI's temporal guarantees are weaker than its spatial ones [1][8].
3. **Compartmentalization is necessary but not sufficient.** A security analysis of CheriBSD and Morello Linux demonstrates *four* ways malicious in-compartment code can bypass compartmentalization — via dynamic-linker implementation flaws and *memory-scavenging* attacks that recover capabilities from stale memory — including a proof-of-concept exfiltrating a private key from the main binary [4]. Hardware primitives are sound; the *software* TCB (linkers, loaders, compartment managers) remains attack surface.
4. **Porting is easy; *correct* porting is subtle.** Pointer provenance assumptions baked into C idioms — integer-to-pointer casts, custom allocators, tagged pointers — surface as purecap porting bugs. The MicroPython and CRuby ports document these pitfalls at length [4 in second search].
5. **Ecosystem maturity.** Morello is a research prototype, not a product; toolchain, debugger, and OS support remain research-grade. Mainstream adoption awaits a committed product ISA (e.g., a future Arm architecture extension) and the software ecosystem to follow.

> **Theorem (Restated — the residual risk):** CHERI eliminates the *exploitability* of spatial memory violations by construction, but compartmentalization security reduces to the correctness of the software that distributes capabilities. Capability leakage, not capability forgery, is the residual attack surface [4].

## 7. Conclusion

CHERI represents the most serious architectural attempt to date at eliminating memory unsafety as an exploit class: capabilities with hardware-checked bounds, permissions, and tags; provenance validity and monotonicity as machine-checked invariants; sealed capabilities as a fast, scalable compartmentalization substrate; and a hybrid-to-purecap adoption path validated by a single engineer porting 6 million lines in three months with sub-0.1% churn [1][2][9]. The Morello evaluation shows the performance costs can be engineered near the noise floor of modern pipelines, and independent analyses converge on a two-thirds-plus mitigation rate for historical vulnerability corpora [2][9].

Its limits are equally clear: temporal safety demands costly revocation, compartmentalization stands or falls on software TCB correctness, and the ecosystem is still nascent. The most promising direction is *composition* — CHERI beneath memory-safe languages, hardening their unsafe cores [6][8] — and the NSA's roadmap endorsement suggests the industry is converging on exactly this layered view [7]. If Morello's promise survives productization, the flat integer pointer — computing's original sin — may finally become a legacy abstraction.
