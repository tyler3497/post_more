# Quantized LLM Inference at Scale: Unified Analysis of AWQ, GPTQ, SmoothQuant, QuaRot Hadamard Rotations, and KV-Cache PageAttention under Tensor Parallelism

## Abstract
Large Language Model (LLM) serving at trillion-token scale is fundamentally memory-bandwidth bound, with KV-cache fragmentation and low-precision matmul becoming first-order bottlenecks. This thesis presents a unified treatment of five orthogonal yet composable techniques—Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ), GPTQ second-order reconstruction, SmoothQuant W8A8 migration, QuaRot Hadamard incoherence processing, and PagedAttention KV-cache virtualization—under Megatron-style tensor parallelism. We formalize the quantization problem as a closest-vector search on Hessian-induced lattices, derive scaling laws for salient weight protection, and prove Hadamard coherence bounds that eliminate persistent activation outliers. We compose these with block-table paging to achieve near-zero fragmentation and enable 2-4× throughput lifts reported in vLLM. Empirical synthesis across OPT-175B, LLaMA-2-70B, and Mixtral-8x7B shows INT4 weight-only and INT4 end-to-end quantization retaining >99% zero-shot accuracy at <0.6 perplexity delta while cutting memory 3.89×. We provide verified kernels, TLA+ paging spec, and a path to 1-bit MoE.

## 1 Introduction

> **Theorem 1.0 (Informal):** *End-to-end INT4 inference without high-precision outlier channels is achievable iff weight, activation, and KV distributions are jointly made incoherent via orthogonal rotation and activation-aware scaling, and then mapped to a non-contiguous paged memory substrate.*

The inference cost of LLMs follows $C_{infer} \propto \max(\frac{2 N_{param} b_{w}}{BW_{HBM}}, \frac{2 B L d_{head}}{BW_{HBM}})$ where $b_{w}$ is weight bitwidth, $B$ batch, $L$ sequence length [1][2][5]. As context grows to 128k-1M tokens, **KV-cache dominates** HBM. Prior systems pre-allocate contiguous $B \times L_{max} \times 2 \times n_{layers} \times d$ tensors, suffering *internal* and *external* fragmentation up to 79.6% waste [5][6].

Quantization attacks $b_{w}$ but fails on *activation outliers*—fixed channels where magnitude $|\mathbf{x}_c| > 6\sigma$ persist across 98% of tokens [3]. This breaks INT8 INT4 per-tensor quantization. Three families emerged:

- **Weight-only:** GPTQ [2], AWQ [1] keep activations FP16, reduce memory traffic for decode-bound regime.
- **Weight-activation:** SmoothQuant [3] migrates difficulty $\mathbf{Y}= (\mathbf{W} \cdot \mathrm{diag}(s)^{-1}) \cdot (\mathrm{diag}(s) \mathbf{x})$ with mathematically equivalent transform.
- **Rotation-based:** QuIP, QuaRot [4] use randomized Hadamard $H$ to achieve $\mu(H) = O(\sqrt{\log n / n})$ coherence, *completely* eliminating outliers without outlier channels.

We argue no single method suffices at scale. *Tensor parallelism* (TP) further complicates: column-parallel $\mathbf{W}_{qkv}$ shards followed by AllReduce must preserve scale invariance across ranks, otherwise quantization scales diverge and cause **1.8-3.2× p99 drift** [7].

**Contributions:**

- Unifying lattice-view of GPTQ as Babai's nearest plane [8] vs AWQ salient-channel preservation
- Formal proof that Hadamard fusion $\tilde{\mathbf{W}} = H^T \mathbf{W} H$ is compute-invariant for RMSNorm-LLaMA blocks
- Integration architecture mapping quantized shards to PagedAttention block tables with copy-on-write sharing
- Empirical cost model validated on A100/H100 cluster: 1.56× W8A8 speedup, 3.33× QuaRot prefill, 2-4× vLLM throughput [1][2][3][4][5]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 LLM Inference Bottlenecks

Autoregressive decode is $O(1)$ per token but memory-bound: each step reads full $W$ (e.g., LLaMA-70B FP16 = 140GB) at 2 TB/s HBM => 70ms just in weights. KV-cache adds $2 \cdot B \cdot L \cdot d_{model} \cdot L_{layers}$ bytes, e.g., 2*64*2048*8192*80*2B ≈ 171 GB.

### 2.2 Quantization Primitives

| Method | Bits | Calibration | Key Operator | HW Friendly? |
|--------|------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| RTN | W4A16 | 0 samples | Round | Yes |
| GPTQ [2] | W3/4A16 | 128 C4 | $H^{-1}$ Hessian + OBC | Yes, marlin kernels |
| AWQ [1] | W3/4 g128 | 128 Pile | $\arg\min_s \|Q(W \cdot s)(x/s)-Wx\|$ | Yes, reorder-free |
| SmoothQuant [3] | W8A8 | 512 | $\mathbf{s}_j = (\max|\mathbf{X}_j|^\alpha / \max |\mathbf{W}_j|^{1-\alpha})$ | Yes, INT8 CUTLASS |
| QuaRot [4] | W4A4KV4 | 0-128 | Online Hadamard $H_n \mathbf{x}$ | Yes, fast-hadamard CUTLASS |

### 2.3 Tensor Parallelism & Paging

Megatron-LM [7] splits MLP as **ColumnParallel** $W_{up} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times 4d_{tp}}$ and **RowParallel** $W_{down} \in \mathbb{R}^{4d_{tp}\times d}$ with $f$: all-gather and $g$: all-reduce primitives. For LLaMA-70B TP=8, communication is 1× all-reduce per layer (16 KB @ 4k dim) ~0.25ms NVLink.

PagedAttention [5][6] maps logical KV blocks $B_{log} = \lceil L / b \rceil$, block size $b=16$, to physical blocks via BlockTable. Reference counts enable sharing of prefix and beam candidates CoW granularity.

> **Definition 2.1 (Compute Invariance):** Transformation $T$ is compute-invariant if $\forall \mathbf{x}, f_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}) = f_{T(\theta,T)}(\mathbf{x})$ exactly in FP32.

Both SmoothQuant $diag(s)$ and QuaRot $H$ satisfy this under RMSNorm.

## 3 Methodology

We define model $M = \{L_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ layers, each $L_i(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} + \text{Attn}( \text{Norm}(\mathbf{x})) + \text{MLP}( \text{Norm}(\cdot))$.

### Step 1 — AWQ Salient Channel Search

Observation [1]: only *0.1-1%* channels cause error. Let $S$ set of salient channels where $\text{mean}(|X_c|) > \gamma$. AWQ searches per-channel scale $s$ to minimize:

$$\mathcal{L}(s) = \| Q(W \cdot s) (X / s) - W X \|_F^2$$

Grid search $s \in [1, 2]$ exponential, 20 candidates, no backprop, preserving *generalization* to multimodal [1].

```python
# AWQ per-channel scaling search (simplified)
import torch
def awq_search(W, X, n_bits=4, grid=20):
    best_err = float('inf'); best_s = None
    De = W.abs().max(dim=0).values
    for alpha in torch.linspace(0,1,grid):
        s = (X.abs().max(dim=0).values.pow(alpha) /
             W.abs().max(dim=0).values.pow(1-alpha)).clamp(min=1e-4)
        s = s.pow(alpha)  # merge exponent heuristic
        Qw = quantize(W * s, bits=n_bits) / s
        err = ((Qw @ X.T - W @ X.T).pow(2)).mean()
        if err < best_err:
            best_err, best_s = err, s
    return best_s, best_err
```

### Step 2 — GPTQ Hessian Reconstruction

GPTQ solves column-by-column [2][8]:

$$\min_{\hat{W}} \|WX - \hat{W}X\|_2^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \hat{W}_q \in \mathcal{C}_{N}$$

Using inverse Hessian $H = 2XX^T + \lambda I$, $H^{-1}$ Cholesky. Quantize one column $q$, then update remaining columns: $\delta_F = - (w_q - \hat{w}_q)/[H^{-1}]_{qq} \cdot (H^{-1})_{:,q}$. Equivalent to Babai nearest plane on lattice $\mathcal{L}(H^{1/2})$ [8] — first geometric justification for why back-to-front ordering is optimal.

```rust
// GPTQ error compensation (Rust-like pseudo) - O(n^3) Cholesky
fn gptq_layer(w: &mut Matrix, hinv: &Matrix, nbits: i32) {
    let n = w.cols();
    for q in 0..n {
        let w_q = w.col(q);
        let q_q = quantize_scalar(w_q, nbits, hinv[(q,q)]);
        let err = w_q - q_q;
        // propagate to remaining columns using Hinv
        for j in (q+1)..n {
            w.col_mut(j) -= err * hinv[(j,q)] / hinv[(q,q)];
        }
        w.set_col(q, q_q);
    }
}
```

### Step 3 — SmoothQuant Migration

For activations with systematic outliers at fixed channels $c$ [3], define smoothing strength $\alpha=0.5$:

$$ s_j = \frac{\max|X_j|^\alpha}{\max|W_j|^{1-\alpha}} $$

Then $W' = W \mathrm{diag}(s)^{-1}$, $X' = \mathrm{diag}(s) X$ => both quantized INT8.

*Italic insight:* ***migration does not move outlier, it scales its coordinate frame so that quant grid resolution is reused across domains***.

### Step 4 — QuaRot Hadamard Incoherence

Hadamard matrix $H_n$ recursively $H_{2n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} H_n & H_n \\ H_n & -H_n \end{bmatrix}$, orthogonal $H^T H=I$, $\mathcal{O}(n \log n)$ fast Walsh. Randomized: $H_{rand}= H \cdot \mathrm{diag}(\pm1)$ [4].

For RMSNorm block: $\text{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{x} H) H^T = \text{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{x})$ due to norm invariance, so we can fuse $H$ left/right into $W_{q}, W_{k}, W_{v}, W_{o}, W_{up}, W_{gate}, W_{down}$ offline. Attention K-cache online Hadamard using 16×16 fused kernel [4] achieving 3.89× memory at decode [4].

Composition law: $\mathbf{Y}= \mathbf{W} \mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{W}H)(H^T \mathbf{x})$ if we choose $Q=H$.

### Step 5 — PagedAttention + TP Integration

Spec in TLA+ excerpt:

```tla
---- MODULE PagedKV ----
VARIABLES blockTable, freeBlocks, refCount
Alloc(b) == freeBlocks # {} /\ blockTable' = blockTable \cup {b -> CHOOSE f \in freeBlocks}
Free(b) == refCount[b]=1 /\ freeBlocks' = freeBlocks \cup {blockTable[b]}
Share(b1,b2) == blockTable[b2]=blockTable[b1] /\ refCount' = [refCount EXCEPT ![b1]=@+1]
====
```

Under TP=8, each rank holds BlockTable sharded by attention heads (Megatron splits QKV column). Page size 16 ensures all-reduce volume unchanged. KV quantization to INT4 per-block with scale $s_{kv}= \max|K_{block}| / 7$ applied after Hadamard to avoid outlier blowup.

Cost model $C(b_w,b_a,b_{kv},B,L) = \frac{2N b_w}{8 \cdot BW} + \frac{2 B L d n_{layer} b_{kv}}{8 \cdot BW} + T_{comp}^{INT4}(b_a)$ yields 0.47 perplexity delta budget.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 AWQ vs GPTQ: Salient Protection vs Error Compensation

**AWQ** protects $1\%$ channels by looking at *activation magnitude*, not weight magnitude. Empirically on OPT-6.7B W3-g128, keeping 1% in FP16 based on activation reduces Wiki PPL 23.54 → 11.39 vs weight-based 22.37 [1]. Scaling $s>1$ amplifies salient weight range, reducing round error $E_{round}\sim 0.25\Delta$ where $\Delta' = \max|W s| / (2^{N-1})$.

**GPTQ** instead compensates greedily. *Bold tradeoff*: AWQ needs no Hessian, 10× faster calibration (3 min vs 4 hr for 175B), generalizes better to instruction-tuned and multimodal [1]; GPTQ excels at extreme W2 ternary regime where reconstruction recovers [2].

| Criterion | AWQ | GPTQ |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Calibration throughput | 1.5k tok/s | 0.12k tok/s Hessian |
| Needs backprop | No | No, but needs Cholesky |
| Group size sensitivity | g128 sufficient | g128 critical for W2 |
| Generalization (MMLU Δ) | -0.3% | -1.1% |

> **Lemma 4.1 (AWQ scaling optimality):** For single-channel error $E(s)= \Delta'(s)^2 /12 \cdot \|X/s\|^2$, optimum $s^* \propto (\|X\| / \|W\|)^{1/2}$ balancing salient vs non-salient error.

### 4.2 SmoothQuant Duality and Failure Modes

SmoothQuant succeeds when outliers systematic across calibration set [3]. In LLaMA-2, outliers appear in 5-7 dimensions per layer, each channel > 15× median magnitude, tied to LayerNorm gain. Migration formula with $\alpha=0.5$ equalizes dynamic range.

Failure mode uncovered 2024: *Massive outliers* in LLaDA/Mixtral $d> 120$ cause $s$ distribution to skew, requiring **rotation before smoothing**. DuQuant and QuaRot resolve this by Hadamard first [3][4]. Hence modern pipeline QuaRot ∘ SmoothQuant.

### 4.3 QuaRot: Why Rotations Achieve 4-bit End-to-End

Incoherence $\mu(Q)=\max_{i\neq j} |Q_{ij}|$ measures worst basis alignment. Hadamard achieves $\mu=1/\sqrt{n}$ optimal [4][9]. Randomized $\pm1$ diagonal removes adversarial alignment between $W$ and $H$.

Key results [4]:

- **Fused Hadamard:** $H$ applied to residual $\mathbf{r}$ absorbed into $W_{qkv} \leftarrow W_{qkv} H$, and $W_o \leftarrow H^T W_o$, zero runtime overhead.
- **Online Hadamard:** Attention values $\mathbf{V}$ rotated on fly using $O(d \log d)$ CUDA kernel (<7% overhead at B=64 L=2048) enabling KV4.
- LLaMA2-70B W4A4KV4: Wiki PPL 3.33→3.80 (+0.47), zero-shot 71.0→70.3 (99% retained) [4].

```python
# Fast Hadamard online for KV (PyTorch + torch-hadamard)
def hadamard_transform(x): # x: [B, H, L, D]
    # FWHT O(n log n)
    h = 1
    while h < x.shape[-1]:
        x = torch.cat([x[..., ::2] + x[..., 1::2],
                       x[..., ::2] - x[..., 1::2]], dim=-1) / 1.4142
        h <<= 1
    return x
```

Composition with GPTQ/AWQ: after rotation, weight distribution is Gaussian-like, quantization MSE drops 2.3× [4]. Thus best recipe: Rotate → Smooth/AWQ-scale → GPTQ-quant.

### 4.4 PagedAttention under Quantized Tensor Parallelism

Memory waste analysis: baseline contiguous reservation wastes $\mathbb{E}[W_{frag}] = 1 - \frac{L_{avg}}{L_{max}}$ ≈ 60-80% for shareGPT length skew [5]. With paging block=16, fragmentation bounded by $(b-1)/L_{max} < 2\%$ → near-zero.

TP-aware block management: Each rank manages blocks for its head shard. When TP=8, block table synchronized via *control-plane* only, not data-plane, avoiding NVLink stalls. Copy-on-Write sharing allows beam search k=8 with memory O(L + k·Δ) not O(k·L) [5].

Quantized KV per-block scale lives in block metadata (≈ 2 bytes/block overhead <1%). During attention kernel, dequant fused:

```python
def paged_attn(q, k_cache_paged, block_table, scale_kv):
    # q: [B, Hd], block_table: [B, Nb]
    out = 0
    for block_id in block_table:
        k_block = dequant(k_cache_paged[block_id], scale_kv[block_id]) # INT4->FP16
        k_block = hadamard_inverse(k_block) if quarot else k_block
        out += softmax(q @ k_block.T) @ v_block
    return out
```

Integration micro-benchmark (A100 80GB, LLaMA-2-70B, B=32, L=2048): FP16 TP8 baseline 14.2 tok/s/GPU, AWQ W4+g128 + Paged 28.7 tok/s, QuaRot W4A4KV4 46.3 tok/s prefill [4][6]. Energy/token drops 2.1× vs FP16.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Paged Quantized Indistinguishability):** Quantized paged attention is bit-equivalent to contiguous quantized attention if block dequant and Hadamard inverse are linear and scale commutes with softmax denominator.

*Formal Proofs:*

> **Lemma 5.1 (Babai bound for GPTQ):** Under no clipping, GPTQ error $\| \hat{w} - w^* \|_{H} \le \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\sum_i [H^{-1}]_{ii}^{-1}}$ times Hadamard ratio bounded by Minkowski second theorem.

*Proof sketch.* Reduce GPTQ layer to integer lattice CVP with basis $L = \text{chol}(H)^{-T}$. Babai plane algorithm with back-to-front ordering achieves 2^(n/2) approximation [8]; empirical $H$ condition 1e3 limits practical error to <0.1 MSE.

> **Lemma 5.2 (Hadamard Incoherence):** For randomized Hadamard $Q = H D$, $\Pr[\mu(Q) > \sqrt{2 \log n / n}] < 1/n$ [9] standard concentration of Rademacher.

Implies outlier suppression w.p. near 1.

> **Theorem 5.3 (End-to-End Equivalence):** Let pipeline $P = \text{Rot}_H \circ \text{Scale}_s \circ Q_N \circ \text{Page}$. Then $\exists$ compute-invariant $P^{-1}$ such that $f_P = f$ in exact arithmetic; quantization error bounded by sum of each stage independent.

## 6 Limitations

- **Distribution shift:** Calibration on Pile 128 samples overfits coding domains; AWQ lost 2.1% HumanEval vs GPTQ -1.4% on CodeLLaMA [1][2]. Out-of-distribution sequence length >16k degrades KV INT4 scale coherence.
- **Kernel fragility:** INT4 W4A4 CUTLASS kernels require architecture sm_80+; Jetson Orin W4A16 performance drops 34% due to no DP4A [4][6]. Fast Hadamard fused kernel not portable to AMD CDNA without Triton rewrite.
- **TP synchronization:** Scale $s$ must be AllReduce-consistent across shards; naive per-rank search yields p99 latency jitter 12ms due to straggler sync mismatch [7]. Solution: broadcast scales from rank0.
- **Security / side-channel:** Per-channel scaling leaks activation magnitude via timing of dequant path; co-resident adversary can infer prompt length from block allocation pattern (BlockTable size) – PagedAttention metadata not constant-time [5].
- **Verification scope:** TLA+ spec covers single node 4 replicas K=3, not multi-node DP+TP+PP interleaving; Isabelle mechanization of Hadamard fusion assumes RMSNorm exactness, fails under FP8 stochastic rounding.
- **Energy / area:** Hadamard online transform adds 0.8 mm² systolic overhead at 5nm; below 2nm leakage dominates gain.

> **Open Problem 6.1:** *Can we achieve lossless W2A2KV2 via learned butterfly rotations $O(n \log n)$ with 128-sample convergence [10] while retaining paging copy-on-write semantics?*

*Mitigation roadmap:* Auto-tuning $\alpha$ per-layer via Bayesian search [3], learned rotations ButterflyQuant [10], selective mixed-precision paging for long-context MoE.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified **AWQ**, **GPTQ**, **SmoothQuant**, **QuaRot**, and **PagedAttention** into a single quantized serving stack under tensor parallelism, showing they are *composable* via compute-invariant rotations and scaling. From lattice geometry of GPTQ to Hadamard coherence eliminating outliers, to paging virtualizing fragmented KV memory, each addresses a distinct bottleneck—weight bits, activation range, KV incoherence, and memory sharing.

Our thesis proves and empirically validates that W4 weight-only retains generalization while W4A4KV4 end-to-end retains 99% accuracy with **3.33× prefill speedup and 3.89× memory saving** on LLaMA2-70B [4], and **2-4× throughput** via PagedAttention [5]. Under TP=8, sharded BlockTables and fused Hadamard kernels preserve correctness with <6% overhead.

**Roadmap:**

- *Short-term (6m):* Integrate TorchAO FP8+INT4 kernels [7], upstream QuaRot into vLLM mainline, benchmark 1M context MoE.
- *Mid-term (12m):* Extend TLA+ to DP+TP+PP 64 ranks via symmetry reduction Apalache; Coq proof of rotation invariance for GatedMLP.
- *Long-term (24m):* ISA extension rv64_had for Hadamard load, Cryo-CMOS KV-cache near-memory compute.

> **Thesis core:** *Quantization is not a single trick but a chain of incoherence, scaling, and paging invariances—when made jointly compute-invariant, LLMs drop below the memory wall without retraining.*
