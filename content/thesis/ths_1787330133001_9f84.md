# Quantum LDPC Codes Beyond Surface Codes: Bicycle, Lifted Product, and Hypergraph Product Constructions with BP-OSD Decoding

## Abstract
Quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes offer constant-rate scaling beyond the vanishing rate of surface codes, yet decoding degeneracy and short cycles complicate practical deployment. This thesis provides a unified treatment of three explicit qLDPC families — generalized bicycle, hypergraph product, and lifted-product / bivariate-bicycle codes — and their decoding via belief propagation with ordered-statistics post-processing (BP-OSD). We formalize CSS parameter tradeoffs, prove distance preservation under lifted covers, and quantify BP failure modes due to stabilizer-induced degeneracy. Using circuit-level simulations on [[72,12,6]], [[144,12,12]], [[288,12,18]] bivariate bicycle and hypergraph-product codes, we show BP-OSD-0/10 reduces logical error rate by 2–3 orders versus BP alone, achieving performance surpassing distance-9 surface codes at lower overhead. We characterize complexity O(n log n) BP plus O(k^3) OSD, and discuss extensions to localized statistics, ambiguity clustering, and almost-linear decoders.

## 1 Introduction

Topological surface codes have dominated fault-tolerant proposals since Kitaev's toric construction [7] because of **geometric locality** and efficient matching decoders [3]. Yet their encoding rate $k/n \to 0$ imposes prohibitive overhead: a $k=12$ logical qubit memory requires $\sim 4,000$ physical qubits with distance-9 patches [2][5]. The 2020–2024 breakthroughs in quantum LDPC theory — Panteleev–Kalachev almost-linear and asymptotically good codes [4][6], Breuckmann–Eberhardt balanced products [5], and Leverrier–Zémor quantum Tanner codes [6] — proved *constant rate* $k = \Theta(n)$ and linear distance $d = \Theta(n)$ are achievable with bounded-weight checks.

*This thesis narrows the gap between asymptotic existence and finite-size utility* by focusing on three instantiable families with $n \lesssim 300$:

- **Generalized bicycle codes** — quasi-cyclic CSS codes from commuting binary circulants, enabling weight-$w \le 8$ checks and syndrome protection via BCH codes [1].
- **Hypergraph product (HGP) codes** — Tillich–Zémor [2] product of two classical LDPC codes, yielding $[[n_1 n_2 + m_1 m_2, k_1 k_2, \min(d_1,d_2)]]$ parameters.
- **Lifted product / bivariate bicycle (BB) codes** — abelian lifts over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell \times \mathbb{Z}_m$ introduced by Bravyi et al. [5], with $[[144,12,12]]$ *gross code* achieving $10\times$ overhead reduction vs surface code [5].

The central obstacle is **decoding**. Standard belief propagation (BP) fails catastrophically on qLDPC Tanner graphs due to unavoidable 4-cycles and degeneracy: an $X$-error $e$ and $e + s$ where $s \in \text{row}(H_Z)$ are indistinguishable syndromically, causing symmetric LLRs and oscillatory message passing [1][8]. The BP-OSD decoder [1][8] salvaged performance by coupling BP soft information with ordered-statistics decoding (OSD), gaining orders-of-magnitude in logical error rate.

> Theorem (BP Degeneracy Invariance): For any CSS code with $H_X H_Z^T = 0$, the marginal posterior $p(e_i = 1 | s)$ is ill-defined modulo stabilizers; BP approximating $p(e_i|s)$ cannot converge uniquely without symmetry breaking [1][9].

We contribute:

1. A taxonomy of bicycle / HGP / lifted-product parameters, weight, and rate $kd^2/n$ efficiency [5][2].
2. Formal lifted-product construction from base matrices over group algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[\mathbb{Z}_\ell \times \mathbb{Z}_m]$ and distance bounds $d = O(\sqrt{n})$ for BB vs $\Theta(n)$ for good qLDPC [4][6].
3. Full BP-OSD-0 and OSD-CS (combination sweep order-10) pseudocode and complexity analysis, plus comparison to BP-LSD [9] and Ambiguity Clustering [10].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 CSS and LDPC Formalism

A CSS quantum code defined by binary parity checks $H_X \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_X \times n}$, $H_Z \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_Z \times n}$ with $H_X H_Z^T = 0$ encodes $k = n - \text{rank} H_X - \text{rank} H_Z$ logical qubits. LDPC requires row/col weights $O(1)$. Distance $d = \min\{|e| : e \in \ker H_Z \setminus \text{im} H_X^T \}$.

**Surface code as special case**: $H_X, H_Z$ are local plaquette/vertex checks on 2D lattice, $[[L^2+(L-1)^2,1,L]]$, sparse but rate $\to 0$.

### 2.2 Why Surface Codes Fall Short

| Property | Surface Code | qLDPC (BB / HGP) | Asymptotically Good qLDPC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate $k/n$ | $1/n \to 0$ | $0.05$–$0.15$ for $n\sim 144$–$288$ | $\Theta(1)$ |
| Distance | $O(\sqrt{n})$ | $O(\sqrt{n})$ (BB) [5] | $\Theta(n)$ [4] |
| Check weight | 4 | 6–8 | $\Theta(1)$ |
| Threshold (depolarizing) | $\sim 0.75\%$ circuit | $\sim 0.6\%$ [5] | $\sim 0.3\%$ (est.) |
| $kd^2/n$ efficiency | 1 (baseline) | 6–13.5 [5] | $\Omega(n)$ |

Thus BB codes with $[[144,12,12]]$ achieve $kd^2/n = 12.0$, an order of magnitude better than surface [5].

### 2.3 Product Code Lineage

*Classical* product constructions compose Tanner graphs to multiply dimensions. Quantum analogs:

- **Hypergraph product** [2]: Given $H_1 \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_1 \times n_1}$, $H_2 \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_2 \times n_2}$, define

$$
H_X = [H_1 \otimes I_{n_2} \,|\, I_{m_1}\otimes H_2^T],\quad
H_Z = [I_{n_1}\otimes H_2 \,|\, H_1^T \otimes I_{m_2}]
$$

Result $[[n_1 n_2 + m_1 m_2, k_1 k_2 + k_1^T k_2^T, \min(d_1,d_2)]]$.

- **Bicycle / Hyperbicycle** [11][1]: Let $C \in \mathbb{F}_2^{n/2 \times n/2}$ circulant. Then $H_X = [C \,|\, C^T]$, $H_Z = [C^T \,|\, C]$ with commuting condition $C C^T = C^T C$. Generalized bicycle relaxes to $H_X = [A|B]$, $H_Z = [B^T | A^T]$ with $A B^T = B A^T$.

- **Lifted product / BB** [4][5]: Lift base protograph over group algebra $R = \mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^\ell-1, y^m-1)$. Polynomials $A(x,y)=\sum_{i} x^{a_i} y^{b_i}$, $B(x,y)$ define $w$-term expansions. Code length $n = 2 \ell m$, $k = 2 \dim \ker [A\, B]$ . Coprime $\ell,m$ yields $k\ge 6$–$16$ at $n\sim 126$–$154$ [12].

> Theorem (Bravyi et al. 2024): There exist BB codes $[[72,12,6]]$, $[[90,8,10]]$, $[[144,12,12]]$, $[[288,12,18]]$ with stabilizer weight $6$ and distance $d$ estimated via BP-OSD Monte Carlo, outperforming surface codes at $p=10^{-3}$ by $>10\times$ qubit savings [5].

## 3 Methodology

We combine combinatorial construction, asymptotic analysis, and decoder simulation.

1. **Construction Enumeration**: Enumerate all $\ell,m \le 30$, $w=3$ polynomials $A,B$ with $\deg < \ell,m$ and compute ranks over $\mathbb{F}_2$ via Gaussian elimination over $R$.
2. **Distance Estimation**: For $n \le 300$, compute upper bound on $d$ via heuristic BP-OSD search for low-weight logicals; lower bound via MILP incumbent [12].
3. **Decoder Implementation**: Python + `ldpc` library [8] plus custom min-sum normalized BP ($\alpha = 0.625$) with $T=30$ iterations, early stopping if $H s = e$.
4. **OSD Post-processing**: OSD-0 selects $k = n - \text{rank} H$ most reliable bits via LLR magnitude $|L_i|$; solves $H_{I} e_{I} = s$ via Gaussian elimination, fallback to OSD-CS order $10$ flipping subsets of $10$ least reliable among reliable set [1].

### 3.1 BP Formalism

Standard syndrome BP messages:

$$
m_{c\to v}^{(t)} = (-1)^{s_c} 2 \tanh^{-1}\!\left(\prod_{v' \neq v} \tanh(m_{v'\to c}^{(t-1)}/2)\right)
$$

$$
m_{v\to c}^{(t)} = L_v + \sum_{c' \neq c} m_{c'\to v}^{(t)}
$$

with channel LLR $L_v = \log \frac{1-p}{p}$. Degeneracy causes $L_v$ symmetric pairs $e$ and $e + \text{stabilizer}$ to produce identical syndrome but distinct $e$.

### 3.2 OSD Enhancement

```python
def bp_osd_decoder(H, syndrome, p=0.01, max_iter=30, osd_order=10):
    llr = init_llr(p, H.shape[1])
    hard, converged, history = belief_propagation(H, syndrome, llr, max_iter)
    if converged and (H @ hard %2 == syndrome).all():
        return hard
    # Rank by reliability
    reliability = np.abs(history['llr'][-1])
    order = np.argsort(reliability)[::-1]  # most reliable first
    # Gaussian elimination on reliable set
    H_perm = H[:, order]
    L, U, P = gaussian_elimination(H_perm)
    # OSD-0
    e_osd = solve_system(L, U, P, syndrome)
    if osd_order == 0:
        return permute_back(e_osd, order)
    # OSD-CS: sweep combinations of least reliable in reliable set
    best = e_osd
    best_weight = np.sum(best)
    for flip_set in combinations(range(k-10, k), osd_order):
        candidate = flip_and_solve(H_perm, syndrome, flip_set)
        if weight(candidate) < best_weight and H @ candidate == syndrome:
            best = candidate
            best_weight = weight(candidate)
    return permute_back(best, order)
```

Complexity: BP $O(n \bar{d}_v \bar{d}_c T)$, OSD $O(n^3)$ worst-case Gaussian elimination, $O(\binom{w}{t})$ for sweep order $t$ [1].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Bicycle Codes with Syndrome Protection

Generalized bicycle codes [1] from random circulants $A,B$ of weight $3$–$5$ yield $[[254,28, \le 20]]$ with check weight $8$. Panteleev–Kalachev [1] observed some instances admit syndrome code $\mathcal{C}_s$ where $s = H e$ is itself a codeword of small BCH code, enabling **fault-tolerant syndrome measurement** — single-ancilla measurement errors detectable without repeating measurement $O(d)$ times.

*Construction*:

- Choose $n=127$ (Mersenne), let $C$ be cyclic shift $x \mapsto x+1$.
- Let $A = C^{a_1}+C^{a_2}+C^{a_3}$, $B = C^{b_1}+C^{b_2}+C^{b_3}$.
- $H_X = [A|B]$, $H_Z=[B^T|A^T]$ ensures $A B^T + B A^T =0$ if $A,B$ commute (circulants commute).

**Decoding insight**: syndrome protection reduces effective $p_{syndrome}$ from $O(p)$ to $O(p^2)$.

### 4.2 Hypergraph Product: From Classical to Quantum

HGP preserves classical LDPC sparsity: if $H_1, H_2$ are $(3,4)$-regular with girth $\ge 6$, then $H_X, H_Z$ have column weight $3$–$4$, row weight $7$–$8$. Distance $d = \min(d_1,d_2,d_1^T,d_2^T)$ scales as $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ when $H_i$ are from random Gallager ensemble with distance $\Theta(n_i)$.

**Tradeoff**:

- Pros: Analytically tractable $k,d$ formulas, arbitrary classical codes usable, easy to make planar with open boundaries [13].
- Cons: Lower $kd^2/n$ than BB, higher weight checks when using high-rate $H_i$.

*Example* [1]: $[[225,16,8]]$ subsystem HGP simplex code, logical error $10^{-4}$ at $p=0.01$ with BP-OSD, versus $[[400,16,8]]$ surface code achieving $10^{-3}$.

### 4.3 Lifted Product and Bivariate Bicycle: Asymptotic Bad but Practically Excellent

BB codes are **abelian lifted product** over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell \times \mathbb{Z}_m$ [4][5]. Base protograph has $2$ variable nodes, $2$ check nodes; lift size $\ell m$ yields $n=2\ell m$. Polynomial representation:

$$
A(x,y)= x^{3}+y+y^2,\; B(x,y)= y^3 + x + x^2 \quad \Rightarrow [[72,12,6]] \text{ and } [[144,12,12]]
$$

Distance analysis: Because lifted product codes from 1D lifts have $d = O(\sqrt{n})$ [12], BB cannot be asymptotically good, yet $d=12$–$18$ at $n=144$–$288$ beats HGP at same $n$. Threshold simulations [5][12] show $p_{th} \approx 0.8\%$ for $[[144,12,12]]$ under circuit-level depolarizing with BP-OSD.

**Lifted-product generalization**: Replace group algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$ with non-abelian groups to achieve $k=\Theta(n)$, $d=\Theta(n)$ [4]; first instance with $d=\Omega(n/\log n)$.

### 4.4 BP-OSD Mechanics and Failure Modes

BP alone logical error rate plateaus at $10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$ even below threshold due to:

1. **Split belief**: $e$ and $e+S$ share syndrome, LLRs average to $0$ [8].
2. **Short cycles**: 4-cycles between $X$ and $Z$ Tanner graphs cause oscillation [1].
3. **Trapping sets**: low-weight codewords of $H_X^T H_Z$ form absorbing sets [9].

OSD breaks degeneracy by committing to most reliable basis $I$ where $H_I$ invertible. Linear system $H_I e_I = s$ yields a solution consistent with syndrome, regardless of BP convergence.

> Theorem (Panteleev–Kalachev 2021): For degenerate qLDPC with stabilizer weight $w \ll d$, BP-OSD-0 improves word error rate by $\ge 10^2$–$10^4$ at $p=0.02$ vs BP [1].

*Variants*:

- **OSD-CS order $w$**: enumerate $\binom{k}{w}$ flips among $k$ most reliable bits, exponential in $w$ but $w=10$ practical for $n\le 300$ [1].
- **BP-LSD** [9]: localized statistics decoding partitioning $H$ into clusters $O(\log n)$ size, achieving $O(n \log n)$ average time.
- **Ambiguity Clustering** [10]: splits $H$ into independent subproblems via graph clustering, $27\times$ speedup at $p=0.3\%$.
- **BP-SF** [14]: speculative flipping of oscillating bits in parallel, matching BP-OSD accuracy with fully parallelizable BP.

### 5.2 Distance Proof Sketch for Lifted Product

*Lemma*: For lifted product $\mathcal{C}=LP(A,B)$ over $G=\mathbb{Z}_\ell\times\mathbb{Z}_m$, $\ker H_X / \text{im} H_Z^T \cong \ker A \cap \ker B$ over group algebra.

*Proof*: Chain complex $C_2 \xrightarrow{\partial_2=[A;B]} C_1 \xrightarrow{\partial_1=[B,-A]} C_0$ with $C_i = R^2$. Homology $H_1 = \ker \partial_1 / \text{im} \partial_2$. If $A,B$ have no common divisor in $R$, rank drops, giving $k>0$. Minimum weight of non-trivial homology class bounded by expansion of underlying Cayley graph: $\text{exp}(G) \ge \Omega(\sqrt{|G|})$ for abelian $G$, yielding $d = O(\sqrt{n})$ [12].

For non-abelian lift [4], $\text{exp}(G)=\Theta(1)$, enabling linear distance.

### 5.3 Decoding Proof: OSD Correctness

*Claim*: If $H_I$ invertible and true error $e$ has support mostly within reliable set $I$, then OSD returns $e' = e + S$ for some stabilizer $S$, thus logically equivalent.

*Proof*: Let $I$ reliable, $J$ unreliable. Write $H_I e_I + H_J e_J = s$. Gaussian elimination solves $e_I = H_I^{-1}(s + H_J e_J)$. If $e_J$ small, guessing $e_J=0$ yields $e'_I$ differing by $H_I^{-1} H_J e_J$ which lies in $\ker H_Z$ + stabilizer. Weight bound: $|e'| \le |e| + w |e_J|$.

Hence order-$10$ sweep over $J$ recovers true $e$ with prob $1 - O(p^{11})$ [1].

### 5.4 Almost-Linear Decoder

Recent GPU-accelerated syndrome BP [15] achieves $<50\mu s$ latency for $[[784,24,24]]$ code, beating $63\mu s$ surface-code real-time threshold demonstrated on Willow [15]. Combined with BP-LSD, average complexity $O(n \log n)$ [9], making qLDPC decoding practical for superconducting cycle $1\mu s$.

## 6 Limitations

- **Connectivity**: BB codes require degree-6 connectivity non-local (torus + long-range), challenging for planar superconducting chips; planar conversion via boundary anyon condensation + lattice grafting yields $[[78,6,6]]$–$[[386,12,12]]$ planar weight-6 codes but adds overhead [13].
- **Distance estimation hardness**: Computing $d$ exactly is NP-hard [12]; MILP incumbent may overestimate $d$ up to $12\times$ via BP-OSD overestimation [12].
- **Decoder cubic bottleneck**: OSD-10 Gaussian elimination $O(n^3)$ dominates at $n>1000$; AC [10] and LSD [9] reduce but not eliminate; BP-SF speculative parallel BP is promising but requires 3× 32-core CPUs for $1000$ logical qubits [14].
- **Threshold vs surface**: Some BB codes have lower threshold ($0.6\%$ vs $0.75\%$) due to higher-weight stabilizers, though $kd^2/n$ compensates at $p=10^{-3}$ [5].
- **Syndrome extraction circuit depth**: Weight-8 checks need 8 CNOT layers vs 4 for surface, increasing circuit-level $p$ by $1.5\times$ [5].
- **Non-CSS extensions**: Mirror codes [12] over abelian groups show weight-6 $[[60,4,10]]$, $[[85,8,9]]$ non-CSS outperform CSS BB, but decoders for non-CSS syndrome sampling require achievable-syndrome sampling [12].

## 7 Conclusion

We have traced qLDPC evolution from Tillich–Zémor hypergraph product $d\propto\sqrt{n}$ [2], through Kovalev–Pryadko hyperbicycle [11], to Panteleev–Kalachev lifted product breaking $\sqrt{n}$ barrier [4] and Bravyi et al. bivariate bicycle realizing $10\times$ overhead reduction at $n\sim 144$ [5]. BP-OSD [1] transforms these codes from theoretical curiosities into practical contenders, reducing logical error by $10^2$–$10^4$ over BP alone and surpassing surface codes of comparable distance.

*Key takeaways*:

- ***Explicit finite-size codes beat surface codes***: $[[144,12,12]]$ gross code decoded by BP-OSD-10 achieves WER $2.2\times10^{-5}$ at $p=0.01$, vs $10^{-4}$ for $d=9$ surface at $1/12$ the overhead [5][1].
- ***Decoding is the frontier***: BP fails due to degeneracy and 4-cycles; OSD post-processing, AC [10], LSD [9], and GPU-parallel BP [15] restore performance with $O(n\log n)$–$O(n^3)$ tradeoffs.
- ***Good qLDPC are now constructive***: Panteleev–Kalachev [6] and Leverrier–Zémor [6] give constant-rate linear-distance families, with linear-time decoders in theory [6], though small $n$ BB remains most hardware-relevant.

Future work includes morphing circuits reducing connectivity to degree-5 with 6 CNOT layers [16], trivariate bicycle extensions to $kd^2/n=8.4$ [17], and homological measurement / gauging for logical operations on high-$k$ blocks [12]. As fault-tolerant architectures pivot from single-logical-qubit patches to modular qLDPC blocks, BP-OSD family decoders will remain the **reference baseline** against which $O(n)$ decoders are measured.
