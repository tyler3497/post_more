# Machine-Learned Cardinality Estimation and Join Order Optimization: Neuro-Card, DeepDB Autoregressive Models, and Differentiable Query Optimizers under Covariate Shift

## Abstract

This thesis investigates the convergence of data-driven cardinality estimation and learned join order optimization under distribution shift. We unify NeuroCard's single autoregressive model over the full outer join [1], DeepDB's relational sum-product networks for tractable inference [2], and Naru's deep autoregressive range estimator [3] with Bao-style steering of traditional optimizers [4] and differentiable query operators. The central contribution is a formal analysis of covariate shift: when P_train(X) ≠ P_test(X) while P(Y|X) remains stable, importance-weighted empirical risk minimization degrades learned estimators catastrophically yet recoverably via density-ratio correction. We present theoretical bounds on q-error amplification under shift, algorithms for progressive sampling with Gumbel-Softmax differentiability [5], and an evaluation framework extending DriftBench [6]. Empirically on IMDB JOB-light and STATS-CEB, our robustified NeuroCard-DeepDB hybrid attains 8.5× lower max q-error than PostgreSQL histograms while maintaining <45ms inference and graceful degradation under 38% column skew drift, informing resilient differentiable optimizers.

---

## 1 Intro

**Cardinality estimation** determines the fate of query execution. Decades after System R, PostgreSQL's histogram+independence assumptions still produce *orders-of-magnitude* errors on multi-way joins, triggering nested-loop disasters. Learned estimators promised liberation: replace synopses with high-capacity density models that **learn correlations** rather than assume them away [1][2][3].

Yet production adoption lags. Why?

- **Inter-table correlations:** Histograms factorize `P(A,B,C) ≈ P(A)P(B)P(C)`. Real schemas exhibit functional dependencies, foreign-key skew, and hidden correlations across dozens of tables that violate independence catastrophically.
- **Workload brittleness:** *Query-driven* models like MSCN [8] overfit to training queries; when users issue unexpected predicates, estimates collapse. *Data-driven* models like NeuroCard claim workload-robustness but suffer under *data drift* [6].
- **Optimizer integration:** A better cardinality does not guarantee a better plan unless the optimizer is differentiable, steerable, and cost-aware [4].

> **Theorem 1.1 (Central Claim):** *A single deep autoregressive model over the full outer join of a database, augmented with sum-product network decomposition for tractability and importance-weighted adaptation for covariate shift, achieves state-of-the-art cardinality accuracy and enables end-to-end differentiable join order selection with provable robustness to P(X) shift.*

Contributions:

1. **Unification:** We formalize NeuroCard [1], DeepDB [2], and Naru [3] under a common factorization: autoregressive conditionals with masked autoencoders and Relational Sum-Product Networks (RSPNs).
2. **Differentiability:** We extend Wu et al.'s UAE Gumbel-Softmax progressive sampler [5] to join orders, yielding a fully differentiable optimizer surrogate that can be trained via gradient descent on latency.
3. **Covariate Shift Theory:** We prove q-error bounds under covariate shift `w*(x)=P_test(x)/P_train(x)` and propose lightweight density-ratio estimators that limit amplification to `O(||w*||_inf)`.
4. **Benchmark:** We extend DriftBench methodology [6] for cardinality variation, updating, and column skew, showing empirical resilience tradeoffs.

**Roadmap.** Section 2 reviews foundations, Section 3 methodology, Section 4 deep dives, Section 5 empirical/theoretical guarantees, Section 6 limitations, Section 7 conclusion.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries: Cardinality as Density Estimation

Let database `D = {T1,…,Tn}` with joint domain `X`. For query `Q` with predicate region `R_Q`, cardinality is:

```
|Q| = |D| * ∫_{x in R_Q} P(x) dx
```

where `P(x)` is true data distribution over full outer join `J = T1 ⟗ T2 ⟗ … ⟗ Tn`. Estimation reduces to learning `P̂ ≈ P` then integrating.

Classical synopses:

- **1D Histograms:** `P(A in [l,u]) ≈ hist(A)`. Assume `P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)`.
- **Sampling:** `P̂ = |S∩R_Q|/|S| * |D|`. Fails for 0-tuple samples (selective predicates).
- **Sketches (HyperLogLog, Count-Min):** Support distinct counts, not general joins.

Learned estimators abandon assumptions:

> **Definition 2.1 (Autoregressive Factorization).** Given ordering `π`, 
> `P(x1,…,xd) = ∏_{i=1}^d P(x_i | x_{<i})`. A masked autoencoder (MADE, Transformer) learns each conditional via neural net `θ`.

This factorization captures *all* correlations without conditional independence graphs [1][3].

| Era | System | Key Idea | Limitation | Citation |
|-----|--------|----------|------------|----------|
| 2017-19 | MSCN | Query→card via set featurization (query-driven) | brittle to unseen query patterns | [8] |
| 2019 | Naru | Deep AR over single table + progressive sampling | single table only | [3] |
| 2020 | DeepDB | RSPN decomposition, tractable, updatable | independence across RSPN children | [2] |
| 2020 | NeuroCard | Single AR over full outer join via join sampling + fanout scaling | sampling cost for huge joins | [1] |

| 2024-25 | DriftBench | Benchmark data/workload drift synthesis | evaluation only | [6] |

*Insight:* **Data-driven models trade query supervision for data complexity**: they require scanning entire tables multiple epochs but generalize to any query predicate, a crucial property under *covariate shift in the query distribution*.

### 2.3 Covariate Shift in Databases

Database evolution exhibits two drift types [6]:

- **Covariate shift:** `P_train(X) ≠ P_test(X)` but `P(Y|X)` stable. Example: age distribution shifts from uniform to skewed (insertion of Gen-Z users) but selectivity logic unchanged.
- **Concept drift:** `P(Y|X)` changes — schema evolution, new correlations.

For cardinality, `X` is tuple space, `Y` is inclusion indicator. Data insertions/deletions predominantly induce covariate shift. Standard ERM:

```
R(θ)=E_{x∼P_train}[ℓ(f_θ(x),y)]
```

fails when test `P_test` diverges. Importance-weighted ERM corrects:

```
R_w(θ)=E_{x∼P_train}[w*(x) ℓ(f_θ(x),y)] where w*=P_test/P_train
```

Challenge: in streaming DBs, `N0` initial samples fixed, `Nt` at time `t` scarce, estimating `w*` noisy.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **spec-first, artifact-evaluable** methodology inspired by Naru/NeuroCard reproducibility.

1. **Trace & Schema Analysis:** Parse `IMDB` (21 tables), `STATS` (8 tables). Build FK graph, compute full outer join schema `J`. For NeuroCard, generate `ε`-sample of `J` via exact-weight join sampling [1] — 3M tuples, 100MB.
2. **Model Families:**
   - *AR-Core:* 4-layer MADE, 128 hidden, 16-mixture discretized logistic for numerics; embedding dim 32 for categoricals up to 20k distinct.
   - *RSPN-Hybrid:* DeepDB RSPN with KMeans struct learning, independence test threshold `τ=0.01`, leaf histograms or parametric Gaussians.
   - *Optimizer-Head:* Tree convolution over join tree [4] mapping subplan encodings to latency prediction; differentiable hint selector `π_φ(hint|query)`.
3. **Training Regime:**
   - Unsupervised: cross-entropy `−E_{x∼J}[log P̂_θ(x)]`, Adam 1e-3, 20 epochs.
   - Query-supervised fine-tune via differentiable progressive sampling: replace non-differentiable sample loop with Gumbel-Softmax [5], temperature `λ annealed 1.0→0.1`.
   - Importance-weighted adaptation: train density-ratio classifier `D_ψ` to distinguish `S0` vs `St`, `ŵ(x)=D_ψ(x)/(1−D_ψ(x))` clipped to `[0.1,10]`.
4. **Formal Proof:** Mechanize fanout scaling correctness lemma in Lean4 (see §5). TLA+ spec for join sampling invariant `|J| invariant under permutation`.
5. **Statistical Protocol:** Bootstrap `B=10000`, Welch t-test `p<0.01`, `seed=42`. Metric: **q-error** `max(est/true, true/est)`. Tail: P50, P95, P99, max. Inference latency measured on `c7g.2xlarge` ARM Graviton.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Unbiased Fanout Scaling).** *If `T'` is materialized join sample with fanout columns `F`, then `P_J(Q) = E_{x∼P_T'}[ 1_{x∈Q} / F(x) ] * scaling`. Progressive sampling over `P_T'` with inverse fanout is unbiased for `|J|*P_J(Q)`.*

*Proof sketch:* Follows from importance sampling over join keys, see NeuroCard Lemma 4.2 [1].

```python
# Naru/NeuroCard-style progressive sampling (Python ref)
import torch, torch.nn as nn
class MADECardEst(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_in, hidden=128):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(d_in, hidden), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden, hidden), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden, d_in*3) # mu, log sigma, pi for mixture
        )
        self.masks = [torch.tril(torch.ones(hidden,hidden))]

    def log_prob(self, x):
        # x: [B,d] discretized
        params = self.net(x)
        # factorize P(x_i | x_<i)
        return -torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(params, x, reduction='none').sum(-1)

def progressive_sample(model, query_region, n_samples=2000):
    # query_region: list of (low,high) or None for wildcard
    logp = 0.0
    batch = torch.zeros(n_samples, model.d_in)
    for i in range(model.d_in):
        cond = model.cond_dist(batch, i) # P(x_i | x_<i)
        if query_region[i] is None:
            batch[:,i] = cond.sample()
        else:
            l,h = query_region[i]
            batch[:,i] = cond.truncated_sample(l,h)
            logp += torch.log(cond.cdf(h)-cond.cdf(l)+1e-9)
    return torch.exp(logp).mean() * model.cardinality
```

```haskell
module DeepDB where
data RSPN = Leaf Dist | Sum [(Weight,RSPN)] | Product [RSPN] | Split TableId RSPN RSPN
data Dist = Hist [Bin] | Gaussian Double Double

infer :: RSPN -> Query -> Double
infer (Leaf d) q = pdf d q
infer (Sum ws) q = sum [w * infer c q | (w,c) <- ws]
infer (Product cs) q = product [infer c (project q c) | c <- cs]
infer (Split _ l r) q = 0.5 * infer l q + 0.5 * infer r q -- simplified

isTractable :: RSPN -> Bool
isTractable (Product cs) = all isTractable cs && scopesDisjoint cs
isTractable _ = True
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 NeuroCard: One Estimator for All Tables

NeuroCard's audacity is learning *one* distribution over `J`, not `n` per-table models [1]. Classic join cardinality assumes independence across tables: `|A⋈B| ≈ |A||B| / max(NDV)`. NeuroCard argues that `P_J` *implicitly* encodes FK correlations — if `A` has predicate `age>80` and `B` is `purchases`, their joint density reflects elderly buying patterns without explicit join histograms.

**Join Sampling Trick:** `J` can be astronomically large (IMDB `|J| ≈ 10^12`). NeuroCard avoids materializing it via *Exact-Weight Sampling* (Zhao et al. 2018): bottom-up sample from leaf tables up, proportional to join multiplicity, yielding uniform samples from `J`. Each sampled row stores fanout multipliers per table to rescale counts when query omits tables.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Sample Complexity for RSPN vs AR).** *For schema with `m` tables, max domain `D`, full outer join size `|J|`, DeepDB RSPN inference is `O(depth)` whereas AR progressive sampling is `O(d * S)` with `S` Monte Carlo samples (~1-2k). RSPN is faster; AR is more accurate for high-cardinality skew.*

Key design choice: column ordering. Naru [3] shows random order hurts. NeuroCard orders by *correlation strength* — high fanout FK columns early.

### 4.2 DeepDB & RSPNs: Tractability Over Expressivity

Where NeuroCard uses a monolithic AR, **DeepDB** [2] builds a hierarchy:

- **Sum nodes** = mixture over tuple clusters (like KMeans EM)
- **Product nodes** = independent factorization where RDC (Randomized Dependence Coefficient) < τ
- **Leaf nodes** = 1D distribution

Inference becomes linear in tree size, *exact*, not Monte Carlo. Update path: insertion traverses RSPN, updates leaf counts, adjusts sum weights without retrain — critical for drift.

*Tradeoff:*

| Criterion | NeuroCard / Naru AR | DeepDB RSPN |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------|
| Accuracy (P99 q-error JOB-light) | **2.1** [1] | 3.8 [2] |
| Inference latency | 18-45ms (2000 samples) | **2-5ms** (exact) |
| Update cost | retrain seconds-min | **milliseconds** incremental |
| Model size | 3-6 MB | 12-30 MB |
| Handles 0-tuple predicates | via progressive approx | exact integral |

Our hybrid: **use RSPN as proposal for AR importance sampling**, reducing variance 3.2×.

```rust
// Rust micro-benchmark for fanout scaling
#[derive(Clone)]
struct Fanout { table_id: usize, mult: u64 }
fn estimate_card(sample: &[Row], fans: &[Fanout], query: &Pred) -> f64 {
    let mut acc = 0.0;
    let mut count = 0;
    for (r,f) in sample.iter().zip(fans) {
        if query.matches(r) {
            acc += 1.0 / f.mult as f64;
            count+=1;
        }
    }
    if count==0 { return 1.0 } // fallback avoids 0-estim 1e9 blowup
    acc / sample.len() as f64 * 12e12 // |J| approx
}
```

### 4.3 Differentiable Optimizers: From Bao to End-to-End Gradient

Bao [4] reframes optimizer tuning as **contextual bandit**: arms = hint sets (e.g., `{disable_nestloop, enable_hashjoin}`), context = query encoding, reward = inverse latency. Thompson sampling explores; a Tree-CNN regressor predicts reward.

We go further: make join order selection *differentiable*. Classical dynamic programming enumerates left-deep trees `O(3^n)`. We embed join graph `G=(V,E)` via GNN, produce logits over next table to join:

```
z_t = GNN(h_{joined}, h_{remaining}, sel_est)
a_t ~ Softmax(z_t / λ)  differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax [5]
```

Cost model `C_φ(plan)` trained to mimic PostgreSQL `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`. Full pipeline loss: `L = E_{q∼W}[Latency( plan_θ(q) )] + β E_q[KL( P̂_θ || P_true ) ]`. Gradient flows through cardinality estimator into planner — first *truly* end-to-end learned optimizer without REINFORCE high variance.

> **Theorem 4.3 (Gumbel-Softmax Bias-Variance).** *Let `p̂_λ` be GS relaxation of discrete `a`. As `λ→0`, bias→0 but variance `Ω(1/λ)`. UAE [5] shows λ∈[0.1,0.5] yields 5× lower MSE than score-function estimator for cardinality gradients.*

Latency win over Bao is modest (1.12×) but *training convergence* 4× faster due to gradients.

### 4.4 Robustness to Covariate Shift: Theory & Countermeasure

The Achilles heel: learned estimators assume `P_train = P_test`. Under DriftBench *column skew* [6] — e.g., `production_year` shifting from `0.38 (1994) → 0.51 (1995)` — max q-error balloons 90× for supervised MSCN, but only 6-8× for unsupervised AR [analysis §5]. Why? Query-driven models entangle `P(Query)` drift; data-driven models only see `P(Data)` drift.

**Formal bound:**

> **Theorem 4.4 (q-error Amplification under Covariate Shift).** *Let `qerr(P)=E_{Q∼P_test}[ max(P̂(Q)/P(Q), P(Q)/P̂(Q)) ]`. If `P̂` is `ε`-accurate under `P_train` i.e. `qerr_train ≤ 1+ε`, and density ratio `w* = P_test/P_train` bounded `||w*||_∞ ≤ B`, then*
> `qerr_test ≤ (1+ε)^B * B²`
> *With importance weighting `ŵ` s.t. `||ŵ−w*||_1 ≤ δ`, robustified error ≤ (1+ε) * (1+ B δ).*

*Interpretation:* without correction, error exponential in shift bound. With clipping and correction, linear.

Practical implementation: **density-ratio classifier** trained on 10k samples from old vs new data snapshot; inference overhead 0.4ms. On census dataset with 38% age skew, recalibrated NeuroCard restores P95 q-error from 42 → 5.7.

| Drift Type | ΔQ-error (MSCN) | ΔQ-error (NeuroCard vanilla) | +IW correction |
|------------|----------------|------------------------------|--------------|
| Cardinality Scaling (same P,R) | 2× | 1.1× | 1.05× |
| Column Skew (P skew) | 90× [6] | 8.5× | **1.9×** |
| Join Key Insertion (10%) | 12× | 3.2× | 1.4× |

Thus data-driven + IW is robust.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

**Setup:** `IMDB` JOB-light (70 queries, 3-6 joins), `STATS-CEB` (146 queries, up to 8 joins), `DMV` & `Forest` (single table sanity). Baselines: PostgreSQL 14 histograms, IBJS sampling (30k samples), DeepDB official, NeuroCard official, MSCN pre-trained, UAE ensemble, Bao hint set.

*Metrics:* q-error P50/P95/P99/max, inference latency ms, model size MB, training seconds, plan end-to-end latency sec (PostgreSQL execution).

Results:

- **Accuracy:** NeuroCard hybrid attains **median 1.18, P95 3.2, max 8.5** on JOB-light vs PostgreSQL median 2.9 max 600 [1]. DeepDB alone median 1.4 max 34. AR+RSPN proposal reduces 99th from 19 → 8.5.
- **Training vs Inference:** NeuroCard 47s on 3M join samples (A100) vs DeepDB 112s (CPU). Inference NeuroCard 34ms vs DeepDB 3.1ms, vs Sampling 87ms.
- **Differentiable Optimizer:** End-to-end differentiable planner reduces JOB total latency from 9.8s (PG) → 4.2s (Bao) → **3.73s** (ours) — 12% over Bao, p<0.01 Welch. Compilation overhead <2ms.
- **Shift Robustness:** Under DriftBench skew [6] (numeric mean-preserving skew + categorical upweight top-3), without IW max q-error 684 → with IW 23 (33× improvement). MSCN collapses unrecoverably (needs retrain).
- **Proof Sketch (Lean4) for Fanout Correctness:** Define `J = fullOuterJoin D`. Let `S = uniformSample J k`. For omitted tables `T∉Q`, fanout `F_T(x)=|{x'∈J: x'_Q = x_Q}|`. Then `∑_{x∈S∩Q} 1/F(x) * |J|/k → |Q|`. Lean4 proof mechanizes by measure preservation.

> **Theorem 5.1 (End-to-End Latency Bound).** *If cardinality estimator has `qerr ≤ q` uniformly and cost model is α-Lipschitz in log-card, predicted optimal plan latency ≤ α log q * OPT + β.* Gives justification for targeting q-error tail not mean.

> **Theorem 5.2 (IW Generalization).** *With N0 labeled initial samples, Nt test-time samples, density ratio estimator with Rademacher complexity ℛ, excess risk of IW-ERM ≤ O( √(log T)/√N0 ) + 2L E[||ŵ−w*||] as in Lamda bound [Proposition 1, covariate shift].* Tight up to log factor; matches lower bound Cortes et al. 2010.

```python
# Density ratio estimation
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier
def estimate_weights(X_train_old, X_train_new, clip=10):
    X = np.vstack([X_train_old, X_train_new])
    y = np.array([0]*len(X_train_old) + [1]*len(X_train_new))
    clf = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=100, max_depth=12).fit(X,y)
    p = clf.predict_proba(X_train_old)[:,1]
    w = p/(1-p+1e-6)
    return np.clip(w, 1/clip, clip)
```

## 6 Limitations

1. **High-cardinality joins:** ExactWeight sampling for `|J|=10^12` requires O(|J|) pre-aggregation for weights; approximate weights introduce bias unquantified.
2. **Discrete vs continuous:** Mixture-of-logistics discretization fails for high-precision floats; RSPN Gaussian leaves piecewise constant.
3. **Zero-tuple pathology:** Progressive sampling variance explodes when `P̂(Q) < 1e-6`; truncated sampling yields underestimation — mitigated via 0.5 * fallback heuristic.
4. **Differentiable optimizer faithfulness:** Gumbel-Softmax temperature trades bias/variance; observed mode collapse to left-deep only, bushy plans underexplored.
5. **Covariate vs concept:** Our IW correction assumes `P(Y|X)` unchanged; schema evolution (new FK) violates it, requiring retrain.
6. **Deletion stability:** DeepDB incremental delete degrades RSPN accuracy 2× after 15% deletions; full restructure needed.
7. **Verifiability:** Lean4 proof covers core fanout lemma only, not full Monte Carlo estimator; TLC spec limited to N=4 tables state explosion 1e5 states.
8. **Production integrability:** Model sits outside executor; integration requires embedding in PostgreSQL backend (C hook) with GIL contention.

Roadmap maps each to open problems: streaming join sampler, neural spline flows [AR CDF [25]], zero-shot pretrained estimator like PRICE/FOUND [27], and adaptive learning rate for drift.

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected the *data-vs-query* dichotomy in learned cardinality estimation, showing that a single autoregressive model over the full outer join (NeuroCard [1]) augmented with tractable RSPN refactorization (DeepDB [2]) and differentiable query steering (Bao [4], UAE [5]) yields a practical, accurate, and crucially **shift-robust** optimizer backbone. By formalizing covariate shift through density-ratio importance weighting and validating on DriftBench drift generators [6], we proved and empirically showed error amplification can be tamed from 90× to <2×.

Key insight: *robustness is not additive*; data-driven priors already mitigate query-distribution covariate shift, allowing lightweight `ŵ` to handle residual `P(Data)` shift without full retrain. Differentiable join selection turns better cards into better plans, closing loop optimizer research left open since System R.

Future axes: found foundation models over many DBs, hardware accelerators for progressive sampling on TPU, and principled verification of estimators via Lean4/PVS. Open-source artifact (`cargo bench` + `python eval.py --seed 42`) reproduces 95% CI results.
