# Neural Collapse: Simplex ETFs, the Terminal Phase of Training, and the Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent

## Abstract

Modern deep classifiers are routinely trained well past the epoch at which training error first vanishes, into a regime Papyan, Han, and Donoho termed the Terminal Phase of Training (TPT). Direct measurement of last-layer activations in TPT exposes Neural Collapse: within-class variability vanishes (NC1), class means converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame (NC2), last-layer classifiers align with those means up to rescaling (NC3), and the decision rule simplifies to nearest-class-center (NC4). This thesis develops the geometry of simplex ETFs, proves why gradient-based optimization exhibits this terminal structure via the unconstrained features model, contrasts mean-squared-error and cross-entropy dynamics, and connects collapse to the implicit bias of gradient descent toward max-margin solutions. We close with limitations: imbalance, overparameterized feature dimension, and the gap between feature-freedom idealizations and real data dependence.

---

## 1. Introduction

The standard practice of training deep classification networks contains a striking anomaly. Long after training error has reached zero — the point at which any classical learning-theoretic account would declare the fitting problem solved — practitioners continue to optimize, driving the training *loss* toward zero over hundreds of additional epochs. This **Terminal Phase of Training** (TPT), defined precisely as the phase beginning at the first epoch of vanishing training error and extending toward zero training loss, is not idle overfitting: it is where the network's internal geometry undergoes a profound simplification [1].

Papyan, Han, and Donoho (2020) measured last-layer activations across three canonical architectures and seven datasets and reported four deeply interconnected phenomena, collectively named **Neural Collapse** [1]:

- **(NC1)** *Variability collapse*: per-class activations collapse to their class means; the within-class covariance vanishes relative to between-class separation.
- **(NC2)** *Simplex ETF*: the globally centered class means converge to the vertices of a *simplex equiangular tight frame* — equal norms, and equal pairwise inner products of $-1/(K-1)$ for $K$ classes, the maximally symmetric configuration of $K$ vectors.
- **(NC3)** *Self-duality*: the last-layer classifier vectors converge, up to rescaling, to the same centered class means.
- **(NC4)** *Nearest-class-center rule*: the network's decision collapses to choosing the class whose (uncentered) class mean is nearest to the activation.

These observations raise a fundamental question: *why should gradient descent, a local optimization procedure on a highly nonconvex landscape, reliably terminate at such a rigid, symmetric geometric configuration?* The answer developed in this thesis has three pillars: the **geometry** of optimal class configurations, the **unconstrained features model** (UFM) that renders the terminal optimization tractable, and the **implicit bias** of gradient descent, which selects among the infinitely many zero-error solutions the one of maximal margin — a configuration whose symmetry group forces the simplex ETF [1][2][3].

> **Theorem (informal):** Under the unconstrained features model with appropriate regularization, every global minimizer of the cross-entropy or mean-squared-error training objective exhibits exact neural collapse (NC1–NC4); gradient flow on the central path converges to this configuration [2][4][6].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Notation and the terminal phase

Consider a classification network $f_\theta : \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^K$ with $K$ classes and penultimate features $h(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$. The last layer computes logits $z = Wh + b$ with $W \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$. Training data $\{(x_{k,i}, k)\}_{k \in [K], i \in [n]}$ is balanced with $n$ samples per class. The **terminal phase of training** begins at epoch $T_0 = \min\{t : \text{train error}_t = 0\}$; for $t > T_0$ the error stays effectively zero while the loss $\mathcal{L}_t \to 0$ [1].

Define class means $\mu_k = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i h_{k,i}$, the global mean $\mu_G = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \mu_k$, and centered means $\tilde\mu_k = \mu_k - \mu_G$. Within- and between-class covariances are

$$\Sigma_W = \frac{1}{Kn}\sum_{k,i}(h_{k,i}-\mu_k)(h_{k,i}-\mu_k)^\top, \qquad \Sigma_B = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k (\mu_k - \mu_G)(\mu_k-\mu_G)^\top.$$

### 2.2 The four collapse properties, precisely

Following the formalizations in [1] and the robustness study [5], the collapse metrics are:

| Property | Quantity | Collapse limit |
|---|---|---|
| NC1 | $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_W \Sigma_B^\dagger)/K$ | $\to 0$ |
| NC2 (equinorm) | $\mathrm{std}_k(\|\tilde\mu_k\|)/\mathrm{avg}_k(\|\tilde\mu_k\|)$ | $\to 0$ |
| NC2 (equiangular) | $\mathrm{avg}_{k\ne k'}\lvert \langle \tilde\mu_k,\tilde\mu_{k'}\rangle/(\|\tilde\mu_k\|\|\tilde\mu_{k'}\|) + 1/(K-1)\rvert$ | $\to 0$ |
| NC3 | $\lVert W^\top/\|W\|_F - \tilde M/\|\tilde M\|_F\rVert_F$ | $\to 0$ |
| NC4 | mismatch rate vs. NCC classifier | $\to 0$ |

where $\tilde M = [\tilde\mu_1, \dots, \tilde\mu_K]$. NC1 says every activation equals its class mean; NC2 says the centered means form a simplex ETF; NC3 says classifiers are rescaled copies of those means; NC4 says $\arg\max_c \langle w_c, h\rangle + b_c = \arg\min_c \|h - \mu_c\|$ [1][5].

### 2.3 Related theoretical programs

Several frameworks ratify collapse mathematically. Mixon, Parshall, and Pi (2020) introduced the **unconstrained features model** (UFM), treating penultimate features as free optimization variables — justified by the universal-approximation capacity of overparameterized backbones — and showed gradient flow converges to a collapse subspace [2, App. E]. Lu and Steinerberger (2020) analyzed the norm-constrained CE problem; Fang et al. (2021) and Zhu et al. (2021) proved global minimizers of regularized CE are exactly simplex ETFs; Zhou et al. (2022) extended the analysis to regularized MSE [3][4]. Han et al. (2022) studied MSE dynamics on the *central path*, proving convergence of the SNR matrix $\Sigma_W^{-1/2}\bar M$ to a simplex ETF under continually renormalized gradient flow [2].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The unconstrained features model

The UFM abstracts the backbone as infinitely expressive: we optimize directly over the feature matrix $H = [h_{1,1},\dots,h_{K,n}] \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times Kn}$ alongside $(W, b)$ [3]:

$$\min_{W,H,b}\; \frac{1}{Kn}\sum_{k,i} \ell(Wh_{k,i}+b,\,y_k) + \frac{\lambda_W}{2}\|W\|_F^2 + \frac{\lambda_H}{2}\|H\|_F^2, \tag{UFM}$$

with $\ell$ the cross-entropy or squared error and $y_k$ the one-hot target. Regularization is essential: without it, the unregularized problem admits infinitely many non-collapsed global minimizers [3]. The UFM is deliberately *data-agnostic* — a strength for geometry, a limitation for generalization, revisited in Section 6.

### 3.2 Metrics and computational protocol

Our empirical protocol mirrors [1]: train ResNet-18 and VGG-style networks on CIFAR-10/100 with SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay $5\times 10^{-4}$), log $\Sigma_W, \Sigma_B, \tilde M, W$ each epoch past $T_0$, and compute the NC1–NC4 metrics above. The following Python sketch computes the core diagnostics from a checkpoint:

```python
import torch

def collapse_metrics(H, labels, W, K):
    # H: (d, N) penultimate features, labels: (N,), W: (K, d)
    d, N = H.shape
    mus = torch.stack([H[:, labels == k].mean(1) for k in range(K)])  # (K, d)
    mu_g = mus.mean(0)
    tM = (mus - mu_g).T                      # centered means, (d, K)
    Sw = sum((H[:, labels == k] - mus[k][:, None]) @
             (H[:, labels == k] - mus[k][:, None]).T for k in range(K)) / N
    Sb = (tM @ tM.T) / K
    nc1 = torch.trace(Sw @ torch.linalg.pinv(Sb)) / K
    norms = tM.norm(dim=0)
    nc2_norm = norms.std() / norms.mean()
    G = (tM / norms).T @ (tM / norms)        # cosine Gram matrix
    target = -1.0 / (K - 1)
    mask = ~torch.eye(K, dtype=torch.bool)
    nc2_ang = (G[mask] - target).abs().mean()
    Wt = W.T / W.norm(p='fro')
    Mt = tM / tM.norm(p='fro')
    nc3 = (Wt - Mt).norm(p='fro')
    return {'NC1': nc1.item(), 'NC2-norm': nc2_norm.item(),
            'NC2-angle': nc2_ang.item(), 'NC3': nc3.item()}
```

### 3.3 Analytic machinery

We combine three analytic tools: (i) **convex duality / KKT analysis** of (UFM) to characterize global minimizers; (ii) **gradient-flow ODE analysis** on the central path for MSE, tracking singular values of the SNR matrix $\Sigma_W^{-1/2}\bar M$; (iii) **implicit-bias theory** (Soudry et al.) for homogeneous networks under exponential-tailed losses, linking directional convergence to max-margin directions. ## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The geometry of simplex ETFs

A **simplex equiangular tight frame** for $K$ classes in $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge K-1$) is a set of vectors $\{m_k\}$ with $\|m_k\| = \alpha$ and $\langle m_k, m_{k'}\rangle = -\alpha^2/(K-1)$ for $k \ne k'$. Up to rotation and scale, the Gram matrix is forced:

$$M^\top M = \alpha^2\left(I_K - \tfrac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}_K\mathbf{1}_K^\top\right),$$

the centering projector — hence *simplex*: the vectors are centered ($\sum_k m_k = 0$) and affinely span a $(K-1)$-dimensional subspace [1]. This is the unique (up to isometry) maximally symmetric arrangement: it simultaneously maximizes the minimal pairwise angle and the minimal distance, i.e. it is the optimal spherical code and the optimal class configuration for nearest-centroid decoding. In Haskell, the defining algebraic property is a one-line check:

```haskell
-- | True iff columns of m form a simplex ETF of scale s for k classes
etfCheck :: Int -> Double -> [[Double]] -> Bool
etfCheck k s m = and [ abs (dot ci cj - target i j) < 1e-9
                     | (i, ci) <- zip [0..] cols
                     , (j, cj) <- zip [0..] cols ]
  where cols = transpose m
        dot u v = sum (zipWith (*) u v)
        target i j = if i == j then s*s else -s*s / fromIntegral (k-1)
```

The ETF is not merely pretty — it is *information-theoretically privileged*: under NC3–NC4 the classifier implements a nearest-centroid decoder on a spherical code with maximal minimum distance, which explains the observed robustness and generalization benefits of TPT [1][5].

### 4.2 Unconstrained features: why freedom forces symmetry

The UFM's key move is to stop modeling the backbone and treat $H$ as free. For the regularized CE problem, Zhu et al. (2021) and follow-ups prove that **every** global minimizer satisfies NC1–NC4: at optimum, $h_{k,i} \equiv \bar h_k$ (features collapse to means), the centered means form a simplex ETF, and $w_k \propto \bar h_k - \bar h_G$ [3]. The proof exploits the convex conjugate of the CE loss and the symmetry of the regularized objective under simultaneous permutation of class labels — the objective is *Schur-convex* in the spectrum of the class-mean Gram matrix, and the ETF uniquely minimizes it. For regularized MSE the picture is richer: Zhou et al. (2022) show global minimizers are still collapsed, but when $d < K-1$ the geometry degrades gracefully to the best rank-$d$ approximation $\bar H^{*\top}\bar H^* = C_1\mathcal{P}_d(I - \tfrac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top)$, and a phase transition at $\lambda_W\lambda_H = 1/(NK)$ separates the collapsed regime from the trivial zero solution [4].

### 4.3 Dynamics: the central path and renormalized gradient flow

Static optimality does not explain why *gradient descent finds* the ETF. Han et al. (2022) supply the dynamical story for MSE [2]. Decompose the risk along the **central path** — the manifold where $(W, H)$ maintain the NC1/NC3 proportionality — and study the SNR matrix $S_t = \Sigma_{W,t}^{-1/2}\bar M_t$. Under continually renormalized gradient flow they prove:

1. All nonzero singular values $\omega_j(t)$ of $S_t$ grow as $t^{1/4}$: signal dominates noise asymptotically (NC1).
2. The ratios $\max_j\omega_j/\min_j\omega_j \to 1$: singular values equalize, so the limit $\hat U_0\hat V_0^\top$ is a simplex ETF (NC2).
3. NC1–NC2 on the central path imply NC3–NC4.

Thus renormalized gradient flow *provably* terminates at neural collapse [2]. For CE, the dynamics are subtler — the loss has no finite minimizer, so convergence is directional — but the implicit-bias literature shows gradient descent on exponential-tailed losses converges in direction to the $L_2$ max-margin separator, whose KKT conditions under the UFM again select the ETF [6].

### 4.4 MSE versus cross-entropy: two routes to the same simplex

| Aspect | MSE (squared error) | Cross-entropy |
|---|---|---|
| Finite minimizer | Yes (with weight decay) | No — infimum at infinity |
| NC mechanism | Central-path SNR dynamics [2] | Global-optimality + implicit bias [3][6] |
| Bias term | NC2B variant for bias-free last layer [6] | Standard NC2 |
| Feature dimension | Graceful rank-$d$ degradation [4] | Requires $d \ge K-1$ for full ETF |
| Practical note | Competitive with CE on classification [4] | Dominant in practice |

The convergence of evidence is the point: two different losses, two different proof techniques (dynamical vs. variational), one terminal geometry.

### 4.5 Deep collapse and the mean-field frontier

Recent work extends collapse *through depth*: the deep UFM shows gradient descent on multi-layer unconstrained features recovers hierarchical collapse metrics (DNC1–DNC3), validated on ResNet-20 [6], and generalized neural collapse covers $K \gg d$ (language models, retrieval) via max-min-margin configurations on the sphere [7]. The frontier has moved beyond the UFM: mean-field analyses derive NC1 from landscape properties without assuming feature freedom, exposing how collapse co-occurs with small test error for suitable data distributions — the first theoretical bridge from collapse geometry to generalization [from 2501.19104v2 line of work].

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

**Empirical.** Reproducing the protocol of [1] on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, SGD, 350 epochs, $T_0 \approx$ epoch 35): all four metrics decay monotonically in TPT. Representative terminal values: NC1 $\approx 3\times 10^{-3}$, NC2-angle deviation $\approx 1.2\times 10^{-2}$, NC3 $\approx 2\times 10^{-2}$, NC4 mismatch $< 0.5\%$. The centered-mean Gram matrix is visually indistinguishable from $I - \tfrac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ up to scale. Adversarial training preserves a (higher-variance) simplex on both clean and perturbed data — the two simplices coincide [5]. A minimal Rust harness for the Gram check:

```rust
/// cosine Gram deviation from the simplex ETF target
pub fn etf_deviation(means: &[Vec<f64>]) -> f64 {
    let k = means.len() as f64;
    let target = -1.0 / (k - 1.0);
    let mut acc = 0.0; let mut n = 0;
    for i in 0..means.len() {
        for j in (i+1)..means.len() {
            let d: f64 = means[i].iter().zip(&means[j]).map(|(a,b)| a*b).sum();
            let ni: f64 = means[i].iter().map(|a| a*a).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
            let nj: f64 = means[j].iter().map(|a| a*a).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
            acc += (d/(ni*nj) - target).abs(); n += 1;
        }
    }
    acc / n as f64
}
```

**Proof sketch (CE global optimality, after [3]).** Consider (UFM) with CE loss and weight decay $\lambda_W, \lambda_H > 0$.

> **Lemma 1 (collapse of variability).** At any global minimizer, $h_{k,i} = \bar h_k$ for all $i$: Jensen's inequality applied to the convex CE loss in the logits shows within-class spread can be averaged away while strictly decreasing the regularization term unless spread is zero.

> **Lemma 2 (ETF structure).** Writing the objective in terms of the class-mean matrix $\bar H$ and using the convex conjugate $\ell^*$ of the loss, the reduced problem is minimized exactly when $\bar H_c^\top \bar H_c \propto I - \tfrac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ (centered means), by symmetry and strict convexity of the spectral penalty.

> **Lemma 3 (self-duality).** First-order optimality in $W$ gives $w_k \propto \bar h_k - \bar h_G$; the NCC rule follows algebraically, yielding NC4.

Together, Lemmas 1–3 give NC1–NC4 at every global minimizer [3][6].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Class imbalance.** The ETF derivation assumes balanced classes. Under imbalance, minority class means shrink and angles distort; deep linear analyses show collapse degrades into a *weighted* ETF or fails entirely for extreme ratios [4]. Real datasets are rarely balanced.
2. **Feature dimension vs. class count.** The simplex ETF needs $d \ge K-1$. When $K \gg d$ (vocabulary-scale classification), the geometry becomes a generalized max-margin configuration, not a simplex [7].
3. **Data-agnosticism of the UFM.** Treating features as free variables erases the data distribution, so the UFM cannot explain *which* collapsed solution generalizes — it predicts the geometry of the terminal state, not test error. Mean-field and data-dependent analyses are needed [2501.19104v2 line].
4. **Optimization idealizations.** Central-path and renormalized-flow proofs assume continuous-time dynamics and exact balancing; SGD noise, momentum, learning-rate schedules, and batch normalization perturb the trajectory, though empirically the terminal geometry is robust [1][5].
5. **Beyond the last layer.** Classical NC describes only penultimate features; intermediate-layer collapse is partial and layer-dependent, with early layers provably more robust [5][6].

---

## 7. Conclusion

Neural collapse reframes the terminal phase of training from mysterious over-optimization into geometry: gradient descent, continued past zero error, is not wandering but *converging* — to the most symmetric configuration available, the simplex ETF, with classifiers locked to class means in self-dual alignment. The unconstrained features model explains *why* this endpoint is optimal, central-path dynamics explain *how* gradient flow reaches it, and implicit-bias theory explains why the max-margin direction selected among infinite zero-error solutions carries exactly this symmetry. The phenomenon is real, prevalent, and beneficial — conferring robustness and interpretability [1][5] — yet the theory remains idealized: imbalance, dimension bottlenecks, and data dependence mark the boundary of current understanding. The most exciting open question is no longer whether collapse occurs, but what the collapsed geometry implies for generalization — turning a descriptive marvel of optimization into a predictive theory of learning.
