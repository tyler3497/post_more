# Higher Representation Theory: 2-Quantum Groups, Soergel Bimodules, and Categorified Knot Invariants

## Abstract

Categorification replaces numbers by vector spaces, vector spaces by categories — and representation theory by *higher* representation theory, where algebraic relations become isomorphisms between functors. We survey the categorification of quantum groups via Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier's diagrammatic 2-category $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$, its 2-representations via cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebras, and Webster's construction of knot homologies categorifying Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants. We explain Soergel bimodules as the categorification of the Hecke algebra, Elias–Williamson's diagrammatic calculus and their proof of the Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity conjectures via Hodge theory of Soergel bimodules, and the role of categorical actions in modular representation theory (Chuang–Rouquier) and higher Schur–Weyl duality. We close with the frontier: categorification at roots of unity, $p$-dg structures, and the search for a categorified 3d TQFT unifying knot homology with four-dimensional gauge theory.

## 1 Introduction

The Jones polynomial assigns to a knot a Laurent polynomial; Khovanov (2000) assigned to it a *bigraded homology* whose Euler characteristic is the Jones polynomial [5]. This was the first hint of a vast program: **categorification** lifts quantum invariants to homological ones, and the representation theory of quantum groups $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ lifts to **2-representation theory** of a 2-category $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$.

The program has two pillars. First, Khovanov–Lauda (2009) and Rouquier (2008) independently defined the **2-quantum group**: a diagrammatic 2-category whose Grothendieck group recovers Lusztig's idempotented quantum group $\dot{\mathbf{U}}_q(\mathfrak{g})$ [1][2]. Second, Soergel bimodules categorify the Hecke algebra, and Elias–Williamson's diagrammatic Soergel calculus plus Hodge theory proved the Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity conjectures for arbitrary Coxeter groups [4].

> **Thesis.** Higher representation theory is the discovery that the *relations* of quantum algebra are shadows of higher morphisms: the quantum Serre relations become isomorphisms of 1-morphisms, the Hecke quadratic relation becomes a decomposition of Bott–Samelson bimodules, and positivity of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials becomes the hard Lefschetz theorem. Categorification does not just refine invariants — it explains them.

## 2 Background: What Categorification Means

### 2.1 The decategorification dictionary

| Categorified | Decategorified via |
|---|---|
| abelian category $\mathcal{C}$ | Grothendieck group $K_0(\mathcal{C})$ |
| exact functor $F$ | linear map $[F]$ on $K_0$ |
| natural isomorphism $F \simeq G$ | equality $[F] = [G]$ |
| 2-category $\mathcal{U}$ | 1-category (its $K_0$) |

A **categorification** of an algebraic object $A$ is a category (or 2-category) $\mathcal{A}$ with $K_0(\mathcal{A}) \cong A$, where the operations on $A$ lift to functors. The lift is never unique, but the *right* lift reveals hidden structure — gradings, positivity, functoriality.

### 2.2 Quantum groups and their idempotented form

Lusztig's **idempotented form** $\dot{\mathbf{U}}_q(\mathfrak{g})$ replaces $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ by a category with objects the weight lattice $X$ and morphisms $1_\mu \dot{\mathbf{U}} 1_\lambda$ — it has no unit, but categorifies cleanly: objects become the objects of a 2-category. The defining relations (quantum Serre) are:

$$\sum_{a+b = 1 - \langle i, j^\vee\rangle} (-1)^a E_i^{(a)} E_j E_i^{(b)} = 0 \qquad (i \ne j).$$

In the 2-category, this becomes a *direct sum decomposition* of 1-morphisms — an isomorphism, not an equation [1].

## 3 Methodology: The KLR 2-Category

### 3.1 Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier diagrams

The 2-category $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ has objects $\lambda \in X$ (weights), 1-morphisms generated by $\mathcal{E}_i\mathbf{1}_\lambda: \lambda \to \lambda + \alpha_i$ and $\mathcal{F}_i\mathbf{1}_\lambda: \lambda \to \lambda - \alpha_i$, and 2-morphisms given by planar diagrams: upward/downward strands labeled by $i \in I$, with dots, crossings, cups, and caps, modulo local relations (KLR relations, adjunction, bubble relations) [1][2].

```python
# Schematic: KLR 2-morphism generators
# dot on i-strand  ->  polynomial generator x_i (degree 2)
# crossing i/j     ->  divided-difference-like operator (degree -<i,j>)
# cup/cap          ->  biadjointness of E_i and F_i
def klr_relations(i, j):
    if i == j:
        return "nilHecke: crossing^2 = 0, dot-slide relations"
    elif cartan(i, j) == 0:
        return "far-commutativity: crossings slide through"
    else:
        return "braid relation with error terms (quantum Serre)"
```

> **Theorem (Khovanov–Lauda; Rouquier).** $K_0(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})) \cong {}_{\mathcal{A}}\dot{\mathbf{U}}_q(\mathfrak{g})$, Lusztig's integral idempotented quantum group [1][2].

Webster proved **non-degeneracy**: the Hom spaces of $\mathcal{U}$ have exactly the expected graded dimensions, by constructing enough 2-representations [3].

### 3.2 2-representations and cyclotomic KLR algebras

A **2-representation** assigns to each weight $\lambda$ a category $\mathcal{V}_\lambda$ and to $\mathcal{E}_i, \mathcal{F}_i$ functors satisfying the KLR relations up to coherent isomorphism. The universal such: modules over **cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebras** $R^\Lambda(\beta)$ — finite-dimensional graded algebras presented by the same diagrams, with cyclotomic relation killing sufficiently dotted strands [2][3]. These categorify irreducible highest-weight modules $V(\Lambda)$.

### 3.3 Knot homologies

Webster's construction: a tangle labeled by representations gives a complex of functors between 2-representations; closing gives a complex of vector spaces — a **knot homology** whose Euler characteristic is the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant [3]. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ this recovers Khovanov homology; for $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, Khovanov–Rozansky homology. Functoriality under cobordisms follows from the 2-categorical structure — the original motivation for the entire program.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Soergel bimodules and the Hecke algebra

Let $(W, S)$ be a Coxeter system and $R$ its reflection representation ring. For $s \in S$, the **Bott–Samelson bimodule** $B_s = R \otimes_{R^s} R(1)$; the monoidal category $\mathbb{SBim}$ of **Soergel bimodules** (direct summands of tensor products of $B_s$) categorifies the Hecke algebra:

> **Theorem (Soergel).** $K_0(\mathbb{SBim}) \cong \mathcal{H}_W$, with $[B_s]$ mapping to the Kazhdan–Lusztig generator $b_s = H_s + v$. Indecomposable self-dual bimodules $B_w$ correspond to the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis $\{b_w\}$ [4].

The **Soergel categorification theorem** identifies characters: $\mathrm{ch}(B_w) = b_w$.

### 4.2 Elias–Williamson diagrammatics and Hodge theory

Elias and Williamson gave a *generators-and-relations* presentation of $\mathbb{SBim}$ by planar diagrams (colored strands, dots, trivalent vertices), making computations feasible in arbitrary type [4]. Their crowning achievement: a **Hodge theory of Soergel bimodules** — an analogue of the Kähler package (Poincaré duality, hard Lefschetz, Hodge–Riemann) for the "cohomology" $\mathrm{Hom}(B_w, B_e)$.

> **Theorem (Elias–Williamson, 2014).** For any Coxeter group, the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials have non-negative coefficients.

This proved conjectures of Kazhdan–Lusztig (1979) in full generality — including non-crystallographic types where no geometry (no flag variety) exists. The proof is purely algebraic/diagrammatic: hard Lefschetz for Soergel bimodules forces the coefficients of $b_w$ in the standard basis to be positive [4]. *Positivity, a numerical miracle, is explained as a Hodge–theoretic inevitability.*

### 4.3 Categorical actions and modular representation theory

Chuang and Rouquier's **$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-categorification** axiomatizes categorical actions of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ (adjoint functors $E, F$ with specified endomorphisms). Their theory derives **derived equivalences** between blocks of symmetric groups — Broué's abelian defect group conjecture for $\mathfrak{S}_n$ — from the higher structure [6]. This is higher representation theory as a *tool*: the 2-categorical action manufactures equivalences invisible at the decategorified level.

### 4.4 Roots of unity and the small quantum group

At a root of unity, $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ develops a large center and a finite-dimensional *small* quantum group. Categorifying at roots of unity (Khovanov's hopfological algebra; Elias–Qi) requires $p$-dg structures — differentials with $d^p = 0$ instead of $d^2 = 0$. The resulting theories promise categorified 3-manifold invariants at roots of unity (Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev), a major open frontier.

### 4.5 Higher Schur–Weyl duality and categorical Howe duality

Classical Schur–Weyl duality relates representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and $\mathfrak{S}_d$ via commuting actions on $(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes d}$. Its categorification — due to Chuang–Rouquier, Shan, and Webster — lifts this to commuting categorical actions of 2-quantum groups on categories of modules over cyclotomic Hecke algebras. Categorical Howe duality (Cautis–Kamnitzer–Licata) goes further: a 2-representation of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{sl}_m)$ on categories built from $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ data yields derived equivalences underlying knot homologies, with the commuting actions of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{sl}_m)$ and $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$ categorifying the classical $(\mathrm{GL}_m, \mathrm{GL}_n)$ Howe duality on exterior algebras. These higher dualities are the engine behind functorial tangle invariants: the braiding functors of §3.3 are shadows of Rickard complexes built from the categorical $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-action, and their coherence is guaranteed by the 2-categorical relations.

## 5 Empirical and Computational Aspects

Diagrammatic calculus is genuinely computational: the KLR and Soergel relations are rewriting systems, and software (e.g., SageMath's diagrammatic modules, custom Python) computes Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials in exceptional types where geometric methods fail. Williamson used these techniques to find the first *counterexamples* to Lusztig's conjectured bound on characteristics for modular representations — torsion in intersection cohomology, invisible to characteristic-zero Hodge theory, detected diagrammatically. The lesson: **diagrams compute what geometry cannot**.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Non-degeneracy is hard.** Proving a diagrammatic 2-category has the "right size" requires constructing enough 2-representations — achieved for finite/affine type, open in general Kac–Moody type.
2. **Roots of unity.** The $p$-dg categorification program is technically formidable and incomplete.
3. **Functoriality gaps.** Full functoriality of Khovanov–Rozansky homologies under link cobordisms (up to sign) required deep work; the 2-categorical framework clarifies but does not trivialize it.
4. **Geometric vs. diagrammatic.** The diagrammatic Soergel category is defined for all Coxeter groups, but its identification with geometric Soergel bimodules (and hence with perverse sheaves) uses deep Hodge theory available only in crystallographic type.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Higher representation theory turned quantum algebra into higher category theory: the 2-quantum group $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ categorifies $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$, its 2-representations yield knot homologies, and Soergel bimodules plus Hodge theory explain Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity. The pattern is uniform — **equations become isomorphisms, numbers become graded dimensions, and positivity becomes hard Lefschetz**. The frontiers (roots of unity, modular categorification, 3d TQFT) ask how far this pattern extends [1][2][3][4].
