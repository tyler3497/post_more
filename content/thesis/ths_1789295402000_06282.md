# Certified Adversarial Robustness of Neural Networks: αβ-CROWN Bound Propagation, Marabou SMT Verification, Lipschitz Certification, and Randomized Smoothing

## Abstract

Adversarial examples expose a fundamental fragility of deep neural networks: imperceptible input perturbations can flip predictions with high confidence. Certified robustness replaces empirical, attack-dependent defenses with mathematical proof, guaranteeing that *no* perturbation within an ℓp ball alters the network's decision. This thesis unifies the four pillars of modern certified robustness: **(1)** αβ-CROWN, the GPU-accelerated bound-propagation verifier combining optimized linear relaxation (α), branch-and-bound split constraints (β), and general-nonlinearity extensions, which won the International Verification of Neural Networks Competition (VNN-COMP) in 2021, 2023, and 2024 [1][2]; **(2)** Marabou, the complete SMT-based verifier extending Reluplex with lazy ReLU splitting and simplex-driven deduction [5]; **(3)** Lipschitz-constant certification, where 1-Lipschitz architectures convert geometric decision margins into certificates [4]; and **(4)** randomized smoothing, the only certification method demonstrated at ImageNet scale [3]. We derive the β-CROWN Lagrangian dual, the Marabou Reluplex pivot rule, the Lipschitz margin theorem, and the smoothing radius theorem; we include executable Python, Haskell, Rust, and TLA+ artifacts; and we benchmark MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet results. The synthesis reveals a completeness–soundness–scalability trilemma and charts paths toward certifying foundation models.

## 1 Intro

Deep neural networks are deployed in safety-critical loops — aircraft collision avoidance (ACAS Xu), medical imaging, and autonomous driving — yet they remain vulnerable to adversarial examples: perturbations δ with ‖δ‖p ≤ ε that are invisible to humans but flip the network's output [4]. Empirical defenses such as adversarial training improve *measured* robustness but offer no guarantees; stronger attacks routinely break them. The field therefore split into two complementary questions:

1. **Verification:** given a network *f* and an input *x*, *prove* that ∀δ with ‖δ‖p ≤ ε, argmax *f(x+δ)* = argmax *f(x)* — or produce a counterexample.
2. **Certified training:** train networks that are *provably* robust by construction, minimizing a worst-case loss over a tractable outer approximation of the perturbation set.

This thesis treats both. We organize the landscape into four technical lineages and show how they relate through a single formalism — the **adversarial polytope**: the set *Z*ε(*x*) = {*f(x+δ)* : ‖δ‖p ≤ ε} of reachable logits. Complete verifiers (Marabou) decide membership properties of *Z*ε exactly; bound-propagation verifiers (αβ-CROWN) over-approximate it with convex relaxations and refine via branch-and-bound; Lipschitz methods bound its diameter by a global constant; randomized smoothing enlarges a smoothed version of *f* until the polytope becomes trivially classifiable. Each lineage trades off *completeness*, *tightness*, and *scalability* differently, and understanding that trade-off is the thesis's central contribution.

> **Definition:** A verifier is *sound* if every property it proves holds, *complete* if every true property is eventually proven, and *certified* if it emits a machine-checkable proof artifact. Randomized smoothing and Lipschitz methods are not verifiers in this strict sense — they are *certification* methods that produce a robustness radius per input without solving a decision problem.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The verification problem

Consider a feedforward ReLU network *f* : ℝ^n → ℝ^m with *L* layers, *f(x)* = *W^(L)* σ(*W^(L−1)* … σ(*W^(1)x* + *b^(1)*) … + *b^(L−1)*) + *b^(L)*, where σ = ReLU. For classification with true label *y**, robustness on *B*ε(*x*) = {*x+δ* : ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε} reduces to proving, for every *y* ≠ *y**,

```
f_{y*}(x') − f_y(x') > 0,   ∀ x' ∈ B_ε(x)
```

Equivalently, the minimum of the *specification function* *g_y(x')* = *f_{y*}(x') − f_y(x')* over the input box must be positive. The exact minimum is NP-complete even for shallow ReLU networks, so all practical methods either **over-approximate** the reachable set (sound but incomplete) or **search** it exhaustively (complete but exponential in the worst case).

### 2.2 Historical lineages

The field's genealogy matters because each modern tool inherits specific relaxations:

- **Reluplex (2017):** extended the simplex method with lazy handling of ReLU constraints *y* = max(0, *x*), splitting only when pivoting fails. Sound and complete; scaled to ACAS Xu [5].
- **Convex outer adversarial polytope (2018):** Wong and Kolter relaxed each ReLU into a triangle of linear constraints, turning the robust-loss LP dual into a second neural network — the first *trainable* certificate [4].
- **CROWN / α-CROWN (2018–2021):** generalized bound propagation to arbitrary architectures via auto_LiRPA, and *optimized* the relaxation's free parameters (α) by gradient ascent, tightening bounds dramatically [1][2].
- **β-CROWN (2021):** encoded branch-and-bound split constraints directly into bound propagation through Lagrangian multipliers β, eliminating the LP solver from the inner loop and enabling GPU-accelerated *complete* verification three orders of magnitude faster than LP-based BaB [2].
- **GenBaB / general nonlinearities (2024–2025):** extended branch-and-bound beyond ReLU to sigmoid, tanh, GeLU, sine, and multi-dimensional nonlinearities (LSTM multiplications, Vision Transformers), winning VNN-COMP 2023 and 2024 [1].
- **Marabou (2019):** productionized Reluplex with deduction rules, parallel splitting, and support for max-pooling and sigmoids — the reference complete SMT-style verifier [5].
- **Randomized smoothing (2019):** Cohen, Rosenfeld, and Kolter proved a *tight* ℓ2 radius for Gaussian smoothing, certifying ImageNet — the only method to do so [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is comparative and formal. For each of the four lineages we (a) state the core theorem with proof sketch, (b) derive the algorithm's computational structure, (c) implement a minimal executable artifact, and (d) report published benchmark numbers with citations. We do not re-run large verifiers; instead we reproduce the *mathematical core* of each method in compact code so the reader can verify soundness directly.

### 3.1 The β-CROWN Lagrangian

The key insight of β-CROWN [2] is that branch-and-bound split constraints — e.g., "ReLU *i* is active, *x̂_i* ≥ 0" — can be absorbed into bound propagation via Lagrange multipliers instead of an LP solver. For a ReLU network and specification *g(x)*, consider the LP relaxation with triangle constraints and split sets *S⁺*, *S⁻*:

> **Theorem (β-CROWN dual):** Let α parameterize the lower linear bound of each unstable ReLU and let β ≥ 0 be multipliers on split constraints. Then
>
> ```
> min g(x)  ≥  max_{α,β≥0}  d(α, β)
> ```
>
> where *d(α, β)* is computable by a single backward pass — the same complexity as backpropagation — and gradient ascent on (α, β) tightens the bound. Any feasible (α, β) yields a *sound* lower bound.

This is why αβ-CROWN is GPU-friendly: the inner bounding problem is just a differentiable backward pass, and BaB becomes "split a neuron, re-optimize (α, β) by gradient ascent, repeat" — no simplex, no LP.

### 3.2 The Marabou Reluplex calculus

Marabou treats verification as satisfiability of linear constraints plus ReLU constraints [5]. The simplex core maintains a tableau; when a ReLU pair (*b*, *f*) with *f* = ReLU(*b*) is violated and cannot be repaired by pivoting, the search *splits*: one branch asserts *b* ≤ 0 ∧ *f* = 0, the other *b* ≥ 0 ∧ *f* = *b*. Deduction rules (e.g., bound tightening from interval arithmetic) prune branches before splitting. The result is a *complete* decision procedure: UNSAT means the property holds; SAT yields a concrete counterexample.

### 3.3 Lipschitz certification

If *f* is *L*-Lipschitz, then |*f(x+δ)* − *f(x)*| ≤ *L*‖δ‖. For classification with margin *m(x)* = *f_{y*}(x)* − max_{y≠y*} *f_y(x)*:

> **Theorem (Lipschitz margin):** If *f* is *L*-Lipschitz in ‖·‖p, the prediction at *x* is robust for all ‖δ‖p < *m(x)* / (√2 *L*).

Training 1-Lipschitz networks (orthogonal convolutions, spectral normalization) makes certification a single forward pass [4]. The price is expressivity: tight Lipschitz bounds on standard architectures remain loose, and enforcing *L* = 1 constrains capacity.

### 3.4 Randomized smoothing

Given any base classifier *f*, define the smoothed classifier *g(x)* = argmax_c ℙ_{δ∼N(0,σ²I)}[*f(x+δ)* = *c*]. Cohen et al. [3] prove, via the Neyman–Pearson lemma:

> **Theorem (Tight smoothing radius):** If *g* returns class *c_A* with probability *p_A* ≥ ℙ[*f(x+δ)* = *c_A*] and the runner-up satisfies *p_B* ≤ max_{c≠c_A} ℙ[*f(x+δ)* = *c*], then *g(x+η)* = *c_A* for all ‖η‖₂ < *R*, where
>
> ```
> R = (σ/2) · (Φ⁻¹(p_A) − Φ⁻¹(p_B))
> ```
>
> with Φ⁻¹ the inverse standard Gaussian CDF. This radius is *tight*: no larger radius can be certified from (*p_A*, *p_B*) alone.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 α-CROWN: optimizing the relaxation

Standard CROWN propagates linear bounds backward: each layer's pre-activation is bounded by *A x + b* ≤ *z* ≤ *Ā x + b̄*. For an unstable ReLU with pre-activation bounds [*l*, *u*] (l < 0 < u), the triangle relaxation permits any lower line through the origin with slope α ∈ [0, 1]:

```
α·x̂ ≤ ReLU(x̂) ≤ (u/(u−l))·(x̂ − l),   for x̂ ∈ [l, u]
```

α-CROWN treats every α as a learnable parameter and maximizes the final bound by gradient ascent — a few dozen steps of Adam on a backward pass. On CIFAR-10 ResNets this closes most of the gap between naive interval bound propagation (IBP) and the LP optimum, at backprop cost [1][2].

```python
# Minimal α-CROWN-style bound for one unstable ReLU layer (PyTorch)
import torch

def crown_relu_bound(l, u, W_out, alpha):
    # l, u: pre-activation bounds; W_out: outgoing weights (row vector)
    slope_up = u / (u - l)
    # upper bound is fixed triangle edge; lower bound slope is optimized
    A_up = torch.where(W_out >= 0, slope_up * W_out, alpha * W_out)
    b_up = torch.where(W_out >= 0, -slope_up * l * W_out,
                       torch.zeros_like(W_out))
    # gradient ascent on alpha tightens the certified lower bound
    return A_up, b_up
```

*Listing 1: The optimizable ReLU relaxation at the heart of α-CROWN.*

### 4.2 β-CROWN: splits without an LP solver

Branch-and-bound partitions the input domain by fixing unstable ReLUs to one phase. Classical BaB (e.g., BaBSB) solves an LP per subdomain — the bottleneck. β-CROWN instead dualizes the split constraints: for each split neuron *i* in subdomain *S*, add multiplier β_i ≥ 0 on the violated phase constraint. The dual objective *d(α, β)* is concave in (α, β); projected supergradient ascent converges to the LP optimum *of that subdomain* while executing only matrix multiplications. Infeasible subdomains are detected when the dual exceeds zero, pruning them without further branching [2]. The completeness argument is standard BaB: splits eventually fix all ReLUs, at which point the relaxation is exact.

Branching heuristics matter enormously. GenBaB [1] extends this to general nonlinearities by pre-optimizing *branching points* (not just 0 for ReLU but interior points of sigmoid/tanh/GeLU) and estimating improvement via linear-bound shortcuts — enabling verification of Vision Transformers and AC optimal power flow graphs.

```haskell
-- Branch-and-bound skeleton: the search all complete verifiers share
data Phase = Active | Inactive deriving (Eq, Show)
data Subdomain = Sub { fixed :: [(Int, Phase)], lbound :: Double }

bab :: (Subdomain -> Double)   -- sound bounding oracle (beta-CROWN dual)
    -> (Subdomain -> Maybe Int) -- branching heuristic
    -> Subdomain -> Either Counterexample Certified
bab bound pick sd
  | bound sd > 0        = Right Certified          -- proven robust here
  | otherwise = case pick sd of
      Nothing -> Left (extractWitness sd)          -- all ReLUs fixed: exact
      Just i  -> case (bab bound pick (fixPhase i Active sd),
                       bab bound pick (fixPhase i Inactive sd)) of
        (Right _, Right _) -> Right Certified
        (Left w, _)        -> Left w
        (_, Left w)        -> Left w
```

*Listing 2: Complete BaB in Haskell — the control flow Marabou and αβ-CROWN both implement, differing only in the bounding oracle.*

### 4.3 Marabou: SMT solving for networks

Marabou's engine is a *lazy* SMT solver [5]: the theory solver is a simplex variant extended with ReLU-aware pivoting (Reluplex), and the SAT layer manages case splits. Its deduction engine propagates bounds — if *l_b* ≥ 0 for ReLU pair (*b*, *f*), the pair is *fixed active* with no split needed. On ACAS Xu (45 networks, 5 inputs, 6 hidden layers of 50 ReLUs), Marabou proves safety properties in seconds to minutes; on CIFAR-10 CNNs it is complete but far slower than αβ-CROWN, reflecting the CPU-simplex vs GPU-bound-propagation divide. Marabou's strength is *generality*: arbitrary piecewise-linear constraints, network-level queries beyond robustness (e.g., output-range analysis), and proof-producing runs.

```rust
// Reluplex-style pivot decision, simplified
enum ReluState { Active, Inactive, Unfixed }

fn relu_pivot(b_val: f64, f_val: f64, state: &mut ReluState) -> Action {
    match *state {
        ReluState::Unfixed if b_val < 0.0 && f_val > 0.0 => {
            // violated and unrepairable by simplex pivot: must split
            Action::Split
        }
        ReluState::Unfixed => Action::Pivot, // simplex can still repair
        _ => Action::Propagate,              // fixed phase: linear reasoning
    }
}
```

*Listing 3: The Reluplex decision at each violated ReLU — pivot, propagate, or split.*

### 4.4 Lipschitz certification and the expressivity tax

Wong and Kolter's convex outer polytope [4] showed that the *dual* of the robust LP is itself a network, enabling certified training. The modern Lipschitz line pushes further: architectures with *L* = 1 by construction (orthogonal layers, GroupSort activations) certify via one margin computation. The trade-off is the **expressivity tax**: enforcing small Lipschitz constants provably limits the functions representable, and clean accuracy drops. Randomized smoothing pays a different tax — inference cost (thousands of Monte Carlo samples) and the ℓ2-only guarantee.

### 4.5 Randomized smoothing at scale

Smoothing's genius is *black-box*: it needs no access to *f*'s weights, only samples. Certification uses a Clopper–Pearson lower bound on *p_A* from *n* ≈ 10⁵ samples, then the radius formula. With σ = 0.5, Cohen et al. certified 49% top-1 ImageNet accuracy at radius 0.5 [3] — still the only ImageNet-scale certificate. Later work (Salman et al.) showed the smoothed classifier's Lipschitz constant *is* the certificate, unifying smoothing with §4.4, and "denoised smoothing" prepends a denoiser so off-the-shelf classifiers can be smoothed without retraining.

```tla
---- MODULE SmoothCert ----
\* TLA+ model of the smoothing certification decision procedure
EXTENDS Reals, Naturals
CONSTANTS Sigma, PA, PB, Epsilon
PhiInv(p) == (* inverse Gaussian CDF, axiomatized *)
Radius == (Sigma / 2) * (PhiInv(PA) - PhiInv(PB))
Certified == Radius > Epsilon
THEOREM Soundness == (PA + PB <= 1 /\ PA > PB) => (Certified => Robust)
  \* Robust: no adversarial example exists within Epsilon (Neyman-Pearson)
====
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We compare the four approaches on published benchmark results (CIFAR-10, ε = 8/255 unless noted; numbers from the cited papers, not re-measured here).

| Method | Complete? | CIFAR-10 certified acc. (ε=8/255, ℓ∞) | ImageNet? | Time per property |
|--------|-----------|----------------------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| IBP training | No (sound) | ~30% | No | ms |
| CROWN / α-CROWN | No (sound) | ~40% (bounds only) | No | seconds (GPU) |
| αβ-CROWN + BaB | **Yes** | ~60–65% verified | No | seconds–min (GPU) [1][2] |
| Marabou | **Yes** | ~55% verified (small CNNs) | No | minutes–hours (CPU) [5] |
| Lipschitz (1-Lip nets) | No (sound) | ~35–45% | No | ms |
| Randomized smoothing (σ=0.5) | No (probabilistic) | ~50% (ℓ2, r=0.5) | **Yes: 49% top-1** [3] | ~10⁵ samples |

*Table 2: The completeness–tightness–scalability trilemma. No method dominates on all axes.*

Three proof sketches anchor the theory:

1. **β-CROWN soundness:** weak duality gives min *g* ≥ *d(α, β)* for any feasible (α, β); gradient ascent only improves the bound. Completeness follows from BaB exhaustiveness [2].
2. **Marabou completeness:** Reluplex is a complete decision procedure for ReLU constraints (Katz et al.); splits partition the search space finitely; deduction preserves satisfiability [5].
3. **Smoothing tightness:** the Neyman–Pearson lemma shows the worst-case classifier consistent with (*p_A*, *p_B*) is a half-space test, whose robustness radius is exactly the stated formula — hence tight [3].

A unifying observation: αβ-CROWN's dual *d(α, β)*, Wong–Kolter's dual network [4], and the smoothing radius are all *dual certificates* — lower bounds on the primal robust optimum derived from a convex dual. The field's progress is largely the story of making these duals tighter and cheaper.

## 6 Limitations

- **Scalability wall:** complete verifiers handle networks with ~10⁵–10⁶ neurons (VNN-COMP 2024); billion-parameter transformers remain out of reach. Branch-and-bound is worst-case exponential, and hard instances (e.g., deep ResNets at large ε) still time out [1].
- **Relaxation gap:** even αβ-CROWN's optimized bounds leave a gap to the true optimum on deep networks; the convex relaxation barrier (Salman et al.) proves single-neuron relaxations cannot close it — multi-neuron relaxations (k-ReLU, PRIMA) help but cost more.
- **Norm restriction:** deterministic verifiers target ℓ∞ almost exclusively; smoothing certifies ℓ2 but with a probabilistic guarantee (failure probability ~10⁻³) and no protection against ℓ∞ attacks of comparable perceptual size.
- **Distribution shift:** certificates hold for the *fixed* network and norm ball; they say nothing about semantic perturbations (rotations, lighting) or adaptive attackers outside the ball.
- **Proof checking:** Marabou can emit proofs, but αβ-CROWN's GPU-optimized bounds are rarely independently checked — a soundness bug in auto_LiRPA would silently invalidate certificates. Verified proof checkers for bound propagation are an open problem.
- **Training cost:** certified training (IBP, CROWN-IBP, Lipschitz) inflates training time 5–50× and reduces clean accuracy by 5–20 points relative to standard training.

## 7 Conclusion

Certified adversarial robustness has matured from a theoretical curiosity into an engineering discipline with annual competitions, production tools, and ImageNet-scale results. The four lineages surveyed here are converging: αβ-CROWN absorbed the dual-network idea of Wong–Kolter [4] and the completeness of Marabou-style search [5] into a GPU-accelerated framework [1][2]; randomized smoothing [3] was reinterpreted as Lipschitz certification of the smoothed classifier, bridging the probabilistic and deterministic worlds. The central open challenge is *scale with guarantees*: certifying foundation models will require compositional reasoning (certify components, compose certificates), verified proof artifacts for GPU bound propagation, and threat models beyond ℓp balls. Until then, the trilemma stands — pick completeness, tightness, or scalability — but the frontier moves every year, and the tools surveyed here are the instruments pushing it.
