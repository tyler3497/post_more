# From Preterms to Proofs: Elaboration, Universes, Termination, and the Metatheory of Dependent-Type Proof Assistants

## Abstract

This thesis surveys the engineering and metatheory of dependent-type proof assistants — **Lean 4**, **Coq/Rocq**, and **Agda** — through the five mechanisms that make them trustworthy: elaboration of preterms into kernel terms via metavariables and unification, universe polymorphism with cumulativity, termination and productivity checking, the distinction between definitional and propositional equality, and the metatheory of the **Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC)**. We trace how a single surface declaration becomes a proof object checkable by a small kernel, explain why Lean 4's elaborator leans on higher-order unification while Coq's kernel rests on the *predicative calculus of cumulative inductive constructions* (pCuIC), and show how Agda's size-change termination checker and sized types guarantee logical consistency. Case studies drawn from the Lean4Lean, MetaCoq, and pCuIC soundness projects demonstrate that the line between a programming language and a proof system is the metatheory.

---

## 1 Introduction

A modern proof assistant is a paradox in a box: it must be expressive enough to formalize mathematics, automatic enough that humans tolerate it, and sound enough that its verdicts mean something. The three systems examined here — Lean 4, Coq (recently rebranded Rocq), and Agda — all answer this paradox the same way architecturally: a *rich, ambiguous surface language* elaborated down to a *small, checkable kernel calculus*, with a wall of metatheory in between.

The user's view is deceptively simple. One writes:

```haskell
-- Agda: a proof that addition is associative, by induction
+-assoc : ∀ (x y z : ℕ) → (x + y) + z ≡ x + (y + z)
+-assoc zero    y z = refl
+-assoc (suc x) y z = cong suc (+-assoc x y z)
```

Behind this snippet, the elaborator must infer implicit arguments, insert universe levels, solve metavariables by higher-order unification, verify that the recursive call is on a *structurally smaller* argument, and finally hand a complete term to a kernel that rechecks everything from scratch. If any stage fails, the whole edifice collapses — and, worse, a bug in the trusted kernel can prove `False`.

This thesis examines the five pillars of that pipeline:

1. **Elaboration** — how preterms become terms via metavariables and unification [1].
2. **Universe polymorphism and cumulativity** — how `Type₀ : Type₁ : ⋯` avoids Girard's paradox while staying usable [4][5].
3. **Termination/productivity checking** — how totality preserves logical consistency [6][7].
4. **Definitional vs. propositional equality** — the two notions of "equal" every assistant juggles.
5. **Metatheory of CIC** — confluence, subject reduction, and the certified typecheckers of MetaCoq and Lean4Lean [2][3].

> **Theorem:** (The Trust Thesis) *A proof assistant is only as trustworthy as the metatheory connecting its elaborator, its kernel calculus, and its termination analysis. Verifying the kernel alone is necessary but not sufficient.*

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Calculus of Constructions and its inductive extension

All three systems descend from the **Calculus of Constructions (CoC)** of Coquand and Huet: a dependently typed λ-calculus with an impredicative sort `Prop`, a hierarchy of predicative universes `Typeᵢ`, dependent products `Π`, and definitional equality given by β-reduction. The **Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC)** adds (co)inductive types with recursors, and the **predicative calculus of cumulative inductive constructions (pCuIC)** — Coq's actual kernel theory — adds universe polymorphism and *cumulativity* for inductive types [4].

The three assistants differ in surface philosophy but converge on the kernel pattern:

| System | Surface language | Kernel theory | Key elaboration device |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lean 4** | Lean (functional programming + tactics) | Dependent type theory w/ inductive types | Higher-order unification, `Expr.mvar` [1][2] |
| **Coq/Rocq** | Gallina + Ltac/Rocq tactics | pCuIC: predicative cumulative CIC | Evar-based refinement, universe constraints |
| **Agda** | Dependently typed functional programming | Martin-Löf type theory + sized types | Metavariables, pattern unification, coverage checking |

### 2.2 The elaborator/kernel split

Every system separates an *untrusted* front end from a *trusted* kernel. The elaborator takes a **preterm** — syntax with holes, implicit arguments, overloading, and notations — and produces a fully explicit **kernel term**. The kernel then typechecks the term with a small, ideally verified, algorithm. This split is what lets Lean 4's elaborator be a sprawling metaprogramming framework [1] while its kernel fits on a few pages: elaborator bugs produce ill-typed or unexpected terms, but (in the ideal) never unsoundness, because the kernel rechecks everything.

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *comparative metatheory with engineering grounding*: for each of the five pillars we (a) state the formal rule or algorithm, (b) show how each of the three systems implements it, and (c) cite the mechanized or paper proof that justifies it. We draw on primary sources: the de Moura–Avigad–Kong–Roux elaboration paper [1], the Lean4Lean verified typechecker [2], the MetaCoq project [3][9], the pCuIC consistency proof [4], the FSCD 2018 cumulative inductives paper [5], Abel's MiniAgda [6], Agda's size-change termination implementation [7], and the Agda reference manual [8].

We treat *definitional equality* as the kernel's conversion relation (decided by reduction) and *propositional equality* as the internal identity type (requiring proof terms), and we analyze termination as a *logical* requirement — non-termination plus Curry–Howard yields a proof of `False`.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Elaboration with metavariables and higher-order unification

Elaboration is the process

$$
\text{preterm} \times \text{expected type} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{elab}\;}\; \text{kernel term},
$$

mediated by **metavariables** — typed holes `?m : Γ ⊢ T` that the elaborator creates and later assigns. In Lean 4, every omitted implicit argument, every overloaded notation, and every tactic goal becomes an `Expr.mvar`; the kernel *bans* metavariables entirely [2]. A typical pipeline is:

1. **Pre-elaboration**: parse to `Syntax`, expand macros (interleaved with elaboration in Lean 4).
2. **Bidirectional elaboration**: `elabTerm : Syntax → Option Expr → TermElabM Expr` infers or checks against an expected type.
3. **Constraint generation**: each use of a metavariable emits a unification constraint, e.g. `?m ≟ f x`.
4. **Unification**: a higher-order unifier (pattern fragment + heuristics, with postponement) assigns metavariables.
5. **Kernel check**: the resulting closed `Expr` is rechecked by the small trusted kernel.

The hard case is **higher-order unification** (HOU), which is undecidable in general. When elaborating `eq.subst H₂ H₁` with `H₂ : a = b`, the elaborator must unify the motive `?P : A → Prop` with `λ x, R (f a x) (f x a)` — a classic HOU problem solved by Miller's *pattern* fragment when the metavariable is applied to distinct bound variables, and by heuristics plus backtracking otherwise [1]. Lean 4's unifier is deliberately *eager but recoverable*: it defers constraints it cannot solve yet (`postpone`), retries after other assignments land, and reports stuck goals rather than looping forever.

```python
# A cartoon of the elaborator's constraint loop (Python pseudocode)
constraints = ["?P a ≟ R (f a a) (f a a)", "?P b ≟ R (f a b) (f b a)"]
assignment  = {}
while constraints:
    c = constraints.pop(0)
    solved, delta = try_pattern_unify(c, assignment)
    if solved:
        assignment.update(delta)          # ?P := λ x, R (f a x) (f x a)
    elif is_rigid_rigid(c):
        raise UnificationFailure(c)       # genuine type error
    else:
        constraints.append(c)             # postpone; retry later
```

Coq's elaborator (*refiner*) works similarly with *evars*, but additionally accumulates **universe constraints** (`Typeᵢ ≤ Typeⱼ`) solved by a graph-based algebraic solver. Agda goes further toward *typed* elaboration: its metavariables carry their types, and its unifier is essentially Miller pattern unification extended withη and datatype-aware heuristics, tightly coupled to its interactive case-splitting machinery.

> **Theorem:** (Elaboration soundness, informal) *If the elaborator produces a term `t` for expected type `T`, and the kernel accepts `Γ ⊢ t : T`, then the surface declaration is well-typed — elaborator bugs cannot manufacture kernel-accepted falsehoods.* The kernel recheck is the firewall [1][2].

### 4.2 Universe polymorphism and cumulativity

Russell's paradox forces stratification: `Type : Type` is inconsistent (Girard's paradox), so the assistants use an infinite hierarchy

$$
\text{Prop} : \text{Type}_0 : \text{Type}_1 : \text{Type}_2 : \cdots
$$

with **cumulativity**: if `i ≤ j` then `Typeᵢ ≤ Typeⱼ`, and every term of `Typeᵢ` is also a term of `Typeⱼ`. Writing explicit levels everywhere would be unbearable, so all three systems offer **universe polymorphism**: definitions are implicitly (Lean, Coq) or explicitly (Agda) quantified over level variables, with constraints like `u ≤ max u v` collected during elaboration and generalized at the end.

Coq goes furthest with **cumulative inductive types** [5]: different universe instances of the *same* inductive definition are related by subtyping. Given `list@{u} A` and `list@{v} A` with `u ≤ v`, we have `list@{u} A ≤ list@{v} A`, provided constructors match in shape and their arguments are covariant. The pCuIC consistency proof [4] builds a set-theoretic model (in ZFC plus countably many strongly inaccessible cardinals) showing this cumulativity — and the whole calculus — is sound. Formally:

> **Theorem:** (Timany–Sozeau [4]) *pCuIC — the predicative calculus of cumulative inductive constructions — is consistent relative to ZFC + countably many strongly inaccessible cardinals. The proof proceeds by a set-theoretic model in which cumulativity of inductive types is interpreted by genuine subset inclusion.*

Agda instead keeps universes explicit (`Set₀ : Set₁ : ⋯`) and leans on **cumulativity of `Set`** plus universe polymorphism via `Level`. Lean 4 sits in the middle: `Prop : Type : Type 1 : ⋯` with `Sort u` polymorphism and definitional proof irrelevance for `Prop` — any two proofs of the same proposition are *definitionally* equal, which dramatically simplifies unification.

A subtle engineering point: universe level metavariables are solved by a dedicated constraint solver *before* term unification finishes, because a stuck level can block otherwise solvable term constraints. Lean 4's `Level` is itself an expression language (`max`, `imax`, `+1`) with its own definitional equality — a miniature elaboration problem nested inside the main one.

### 4.3 Termination and productivity checking

Under Curry–Howard, a non-terminating program is a circular proof. All three systems therefore require recursive definitions to be *total*:

- **Coq** uses the syntactic **guard condition**: recursive calls must be on structurally smaller arguments, guarded by constructors (`fix` with structurally decreasing principal argument).
- **Agda** uses the **foetus/size-change termination checker** [7]: it builds a call graph whose edges carry size-change matrices, completes the graph under composition, and demands that every *idempotent* call matrix from a function to itself has a strictly decreasing argument. This is strictly more liberal than lexicographic structural recursion and is justified by the size-change principle of Lee, Jones, and Ben-Amram.
- **Sized types** [6] internalize the argument in the type system: `ℕⁱ` is the type of naturals of "size" `< i`, and `minus : [i] → SNat i → SNat ∞ → SNat i` typechecks only because the recursive call happens at a smaller size `j < i`. Agda supports `--sized-types` (inconsistent with `--guardedness`, per the manual [8]) and `--termination-depth` for bounded lookahead.

For **coinductive** types (streams, infinite trees), the dual requirement is **productivity**: every finite observation must terminate. Coq enforces syntactic guardedness (corecursive calls under constructors); Agda offers sized types or constructor-guarded corecursion (the two are mutually exclusive under `--safe` [8]).

The metatheoretic payoff is normalization: well-typed terms reduce to canonical forms, which yields *canonicity* (a closed term of `ℕ` is a numeral) and hence *consistency* (no closed term of `⊥`). This is why termination checking is not an optimization — it is load-bearing for logic.

> **Theorem:** (Size-change principle, Lee–Jones–Ben-Amram, as implemented in Agda [7]) *If the completed call graph of a set of mutually recursive functions has the property that every idempotent call matrix on a self-loop shows strict decrease in some argument position, then all calls terminate.*

### 4.4 Definitional versus propositional equality

Every assistant maintains **two** equalities, and confusing them is the source of half of all beginner suffering:

| | Definitional (`≡`, judgmental) | Propositional (`=`, `≡` the type) |
|---|---|---|
| Decided by | The kernel, via reduction (β, ι, δ, ζ) | The user, via proof terms |
| Evidence | None — it *holds* silently | A term `p : a = b` (e.g. `refl`) |
| Used in | Conversion rule, unification, typechecking | Rewriting (`subst`, `transport`, `cong`) |
| Cost | Decidable (in practice, with fuel limits) | Arbitrary proof effort |

The **conversion rule** is the bridge:

$$
\frac{\Gamma \vdash t : A \qquad \Gamma \vdash A \equiv B}{\Gamma \vdash t : B}
$$

If `A` and `B` are *definitionally* equal — equal after unfolding definitions and computing — then `t` can be silently retyped. Lean 4's unifier exploits this aggressively: `2 + 2` and `4` are definitionally equal, so `show 2 + 2 = 4` elaborates with `rfl` alone. Propositional equality is the inductive family with a single constructor `refl : a = a`; to transport along `p : a = b` one must invoke the eliminator (`Eq.subst`, `J`), which is a *proof step*, not a computation.

**Proof irrelevance** (Lean's `Prop`, Coq's `Prop`/`SProp`) collapses the distinction one level up: any two proofs of the same proposition are definitionally equal, so proof *content* never blocks unification. Agda has no universe of proof-irrelevant propositions by default (there is a `--prop` option), which is why Agda unification must sometimes compare proof terms structurally.

> **Theorem:** (Subject reduction / preservation) *If `Γ ⊢ t : T` and `t ↠ t′` by the kernel's reduction relation, then `Γ ⊢ t′ : T`. Definitional equality is exactly the congruence closure of reduction — it never changes what a term proves, only how it computes [3][9].*

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

Three landmark verification efforts put these ideas to the test:

1. **MetaCoq** [3][9] formalizes PCUIC — the *polymorphic cumulative* calculus of (co)inductive constructions — inside Coq itself, and proves **confluence of reduction, type preservation, and principality of typing**, culminating in a verified typechecker and a verified erasure procedure to untyped λ-calculus (the core of extraction). Notably, the project *found a real bug* in Coq's kernel handling of cumulativity during formalization — empirical proof that metatheory pays rent.
2. **Lean4Lean** [2] verifies a typechecker for Lean's kernel in Lean itself. It pins down the exact `Expr` datatype (`bvar`, `fvar`, `mvar`, `sort`, `const`, `app`, `lam`, `forallE`, `letE`, `lit`, `mdata`, `proj`), proves that metavariables are *banned* from kernel terms, and handles the locally-nameless vs. de Bruijn gap that the real kernel navigates. Its existence narrows Lean's trusted base from "the whole elaborator" to "the kernel + this spec."
3. **pCuIC consistency** [4] gives the set-theoretic model justifying Coq's cumulative inductives, and the FSCD 2018 paper [5] shows the implementation in Coq 8.7 matches the theory.

On the termination side, Agda's size-change checker [7] is validated daily by the Agda standard library: thousands of definitions pass through call-graph completion, and the `--termination-depth` knob [8] exists precisely because the default depth-1 analysis rejects some genuinely terminating programs — a measurable completeness gap.

A comparative scorecard:

| Property | Lean 4 | Coq/Rocq | Agda |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elaboration HOU | Pattern + heuristics, postponement [1] | Evar refinement + universe solver | Pattern unification + case splitting |
| Universe cumulativity | `Type u ≤ Type v`, proof irrelevance | Full inductive cumulativity (pCuIC) [4][5] | `Set` cumulativity, explicit levels |
| Termination | Structural (`decreasing_by`/`termination_by`) | Guard condition | Size-change principle [7], sized types [6] |
| Kernel verified | Lean4Lean (in progress) [2] | MetaCoq typechecker + erasure [3] | — (kernel = typechecker, small) |
| Definitional `Prop` irrelevance | Yes | Yes (`Prop`/`SProp`) | Opt-in (`--prop`) |

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires listing what these systems *cannot* do:

- **Higher-order unification is undecidable.** Every elaborator resorts to heuristics, postponement, and occasional mysterious failures. A goal that is "obviously" solvable can get stuck because the unifier made an unlucky eager choice — the price of expressiveness [1].
- **Termination checking is incomplete.** Termination is undecidable, so all checkers reject some terminating programs. Coq's guard condition rejects perfectly good definitions (e.g. certain nested recursions); Agda's size-change analysis can need `--termination-depth` tuning [8].
- **Universe polymorphism leaks.** Universe constraints can escape into error messages (`stuck at solving universe constraint u ≤ max u v`), and cumulative inductives add genuine algorithmic complexity to conversion checking [5].
- **The elaborator is unverified.** MetaCoq and Lean4Lean verify the *kernel*, not the elaborator. A bug in Lean 4's 100k-line elaborator can still produce a wrong-but-kernel-accepted term — soundness holds, but *faithfulness* (the term means what the user wrote) does not.
- **Metatheory gaps remain.** Full normalization proofs for pCuIC with all of Coq's features (e.g. primitive integers, template polymorphism edge cases) are still being completed; the trusted base includes the compiler (OCaml/C) that runs the kernel.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Dependent-type proof assistants survive on a division of labor: an *ambitious, heuristic, unverified elaborator* that turns human-friendly preterms into explicit terms via metavariables and unification; a *small, principled, increasingly verified kernel* implementing CIC/pCuIC with cumulativity; a *termination analysis* that keeps logic consistent; and a *two-level equality* that separates computation from proof. The case studies — MetaCoq's verified typechecker and erasure [3][9], Lean4Lean's kernel specification [2], the pCuIC model [4], and Agda's size-change termination [6][7] — show a field moving from "trust me" to "check the proof."

The open frontier is clear: verify the elaborator, not just the kernel; close the metatheoretic gaps around cumulative inductives and sized types; and make universe levels and termination arguments *invisible* when they are routine and *inspectable* when they are not. The trust thesis stands: a proof assistant is only as trustworthy as the metatheory connecting its parts — and that metatheory is, increasingly, itself machine-checked.
