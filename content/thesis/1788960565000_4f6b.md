# Score-Based Generative Modeling with Stochastic Differential Equations: Denoising Score Matching, the Probability-Flow ODE, and Predictor–Corrector Sampling

## Abstract

Score-based generative models learn the *score* — the gradient of the log-density, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ — of data distributions perturbed by a continuum of noise scales, then synthesize samples by simulating reverse-time stochastic dynamics driven by the learned score. This article develops the full theoretical and practical pipeline: we begin with the statistical foundations of score matching [1] and its denoising reformulation [2], which removes the intractable second-order term by matching the score of a *noise-perturbed* distribution with a tractable Monte Carlo objective. We then lift the finite family of noise scales to a continuous-time **stochastic differential equation** (SDE) [4], derive the reverse-time SDE via the Anderson time-reversal theorem [7], and present the **probability-flow ordinary differential equation** — an ODE with identical marginals that enables exact likelihood computation. We analyze **predictor–corrector** sampling, which alternates numerical SDE solvers with score-based MCMC correction steps, and situate denoising diffusion probabilistic models [5] as a discrete-time instantiation of the same framework. We close with empirical results, convergence theory, and open limitations.

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling of high-dimensional data — natural images, audio waveforms, molecular graphs — demands a framework that scales gracefully, trains stably, and supports flexible downstream inference such as likelihood evaluation and controllable generation. Classical maximum-likelihood approaches parameterize a normalized density $p_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ and optimize $\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x})]$, but normalization requires an intractable partition function for expressive energy-based models. Implicit models such as generative adversarial networks trade the likelihood for a minimax game whose training dynamics are notoriously unstable.

**Score-based generative modeling** takes a different route: instead of learning the density itself, it learns its *score function*, the vector field $\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x}) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p(\mathbf{x})$. The score points in the direction of steepest ascent of the log-density, and — critically — it is independent of the normalizing constant, since $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log Z = 0$. Given the score, samples can be drawn by running **Langevin dynamics**, a Markov chain that climbs the density landscape with injected noise:

$$\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + \frac{\epsilon}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p(\mathbf{x}_k) + \sqrt{\epsilon}\,\mathbf{z}_k, \qquad \mathbf{z}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}).$$

As $\epsilon \to 0$ and $k \to \infty$, the chain's stationary distribution converges to $p$ under mild regularity conditions. The challenge, of course, is *estimating* the score from data without knowing $p$.

Three intellectual lineages converge in this article. The first is **score matching** [1], which estimates the score by minimizing the Fisher divergence $\mathbb{E}_p\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p(\mathbf{x})\|^2$ without ever evaluating the unknown ground-truth score, via an integration-by-parts trick that replaces it with $\mathbb{E}_p[\text{tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})) + \tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})\|^2]$. The trace-of-Jacobian term, however, is expensive in high dimensions. The second is **denoising score matching** [2], which shows that matching the score of a *Gaussian-perturbed* distribution $p_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}) = \int p(\mathbf{x})\,\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{x}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})\,d\mathbf{x}$ admits an objective whose target, $\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x}) = -(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x})/\sigma^2$, is known analytically — turning score estimation into a denoising regression problem. The third is the **continuous-time SDE formulation** [4], which subsumes the noise-conditional score networks of [3] and the diffusion probabilistic models of [5,6] into a single framework governed by forward and reverse-time SDEs, complete with an equivalent deterministic **probability-flow ODE**.

> **Thesis statement:** The SDE framework unifies denoising score matching, annealed Langevin sampling, and diffusion probabilistic models into one continuous-time theory in which the score function is simultaneously a training target, a reverse-diffusion drift, and a likelihood estimator — and predictor–corrector samplers exploit this unity to achieve state-of-the-art generation quality.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Score matching and its difficulties

Score matching was introduced by Hyvärinen [1] as a consistent estimator for unnormalized statistical models. For a model score $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ approximating $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})$, the *explicit* score matching objective is the Fisher divergence

$$\mathcal{J}_{\text{ESM}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\big[\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\|^2\big].$$

Expanding the square and applying integration by parts under decay conditions at infinity yields the *implicit* objective [1]:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\text{ISM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\big[\text{tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})) + \tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})\|^2\big] + \text{const}.$$

The trace of the $d\times d$ Jacobian is prohibitive for $d \sim 10^5$ (e.g., images), motivating scalable alternatives: *sliced* score matching [3-adjacent literature] projects onto random directions, while **denoising score matching** [2] removes the trace term entirely.

> **Theorem (Denoising score matching equivalence [2]):** Let $p_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}) = \int p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\,q_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}$ with $q_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{x}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})$. Then minimizing
> $$\mathcal{J}_{\text{DSM}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})}\mathbb{E}_{q_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x})}\big[\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}) - \nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x})\|^2\big]$$
> is equivalent, up to a constant independent of $\theta$, to explicit score matching against $p_\sigma$. Since $\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x}) = -(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x})/\sigma^2$, the regression target is simply the *noise vector* — score estimation becomes denoising.

### 2.2 Noise-conditional score networks and annealed Langevin dynamics

A single noise level $\sigma$ suffices only if the perturbed density $p_\sigma$ covers the data manifold. Song and Ermon [3] observed that real data concentrate on low-dimensional manifolds where the score is ill-defined (the density is nearly singular off-manifold), so they trained a *noise-conditional* score network $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \sigma)$ jointly over a geometric sequence $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 > \cdots > \sigma_L$:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^L \lambda(\sigma_i)\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})}\mathbb{E}_{q_{\sigma_i}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x})}\big[\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}, \sigma_i) + (\tilde{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x})/\sigma_i^2\|^2\big].$$

Sampling then proceeds by **annealed Langevin dynamics**: initialize at large noise, run Langevin MCMC at each noise level, and gradually anneal $\sigma \downarrow \sigma_L \approx 0$. Large noise fills low-density regions and smooths the landscape, curing the manifold problem and poor mixing; small noise refines fine detail [3].

### 2.3 Diffusion probabilistic models

Independently, Sohl-Dickstein et al. [6] proposed *diffusion probabilistic models* based on a forward Markov chain that gradually destroys structure, and Ho et al. [5] scaled this to high-fidelity image synthesis with **denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)**. DDPMs learn to reverse a discrete forward process $q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t\mathbf{I})$ by predicting the injected noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ with a network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$. The key insight of [4] is that DDPMs and noise-conditional score networks are *discretizations of the same continuous-time SDE*.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The forward SDE

Let $\mathbf{x}(0) \sim p_0 = p_{\text{data}}$ and consider the Itô SDE

$$d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}, \qquad t \in [0, T], \tag{1}$$

where $\mathbf{f}$ is the drift, $g$ the scalar diffusion coefficient, and $\mathbf{w}$ a standard Wiener process [4]. By choosing $(\mathbf{f}, g)$ appropriately, the terminal distribution $p_T$ approximates a tractable prior (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$). Three canonical choices are:

| Name | Drift $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | Prior behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Variance Exploding (VE)** | $0$ | $\sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}$ | Variance grows unboundedly |
| **Variance Preserving (VP)** | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | Variance bounded (OU process) |
| **sub-VP** | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t\beta(s)ds})}$ | Bounded, strictly smaller variance than VP |

The VE SDE is the continuous limit of the noise-conditional models in [3]; the VP SDE is the continuous limit of DDPMs [5]; sub-VP interpolates, yielding better likelihoods [4].

### 3.2 The reverse-time SDE

The mathematical linchpin is Anderson's time-reversal theorem [7]: a diffusion (1) run backward in time is *itself* a diffusion, whose drift is corrected by the score of the marginals.

> **Theorem (Reverse-time SDE [4,7]):** Let $p_t$ be the marginal density of the forward SDE (1). Then the reverse process $\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{x}(T - t)$ satisfies
> $$d\mathbf{x} = \big[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})\big]\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{\mathbf{w}}, \tag{2}$$
> where time flows backward from $T$ to $0$ and $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ is a reverse-time Wiener process.

Equation (2) reduces sampling to two tasks: (i) *learn* the time-dependent score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ for all $t$, and (ii) *simulate* the reverse SDE numerically. Both are addressed by continuous-time denoising score matching.

### 3.3 Continuous-time training objective

Generalizing [2] to a continuum, the score network $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is trained with [4]

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,T)}\,\mathbb{E}_{p_0(\mathbf{x}(0))}\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}(t)\mid\mathbf{x}(0))}\big[\lambda(t)\,\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}(t), t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}(t)}\log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}(t)\mid\mathbf{x}(0))\|^2\big], \tag{3}$$

where $p_{0t}$ is the *transition kernel* of the forward SDE. Crucially, for affine drifts (VE, VP, sub-VP) the transition kernel is Gaussian with closed-form mean and variance, so the target $\nabla\log p_{0t}$ is analytic and the objective is a simple denoising regression — no trace terms, no adversarial dynamics. The weighting $\lambda(t)$ is typically chosen proportional to $1/\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\log p_{0t}\|^2]$, i.e., $\lambda(t) \propto \sigma^2(t)$ for the VE SDE, which balances the loss across noise scales [4].

### 3.4 The probability-flow ODE

A remarkable consequence of the Fokker–Planck equation is that *every* SDE of the form (1) admits a deterministic counterpart with *identical marginals* at all times [4]:

> **Theorem (Probability-flow ODE [4]):** The ODE
> $$d\mathbf{x} = \Big[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g(t)^2\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})\Big]\,dt \tag{4}$$
> induces the same marginal distributions $p_t$ as the forward SDE (1) for all $t \in [0,T]$.

Replacing the true score with $\mathbf{s}_\theta$ yields a neural ODE. This unlocks three capabilities unavailable to stochastic samplers:

1. **Exact likelihood computation** via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula (Hutchinson trace estimator for the divergence term), making score-based models competitive density estimators [4,9].
2. **Deterministic, invertible encoding**: running (4) forward maps data to a latent code, and backward recovers the data exactly — enabling interpolation and temperature-controlled sampling.
3. **Adaptive ODE solvers** with error control, yielding fewer function evaluations than fixed-step SDE solvers [8].

DDIM [8] is a discrete-time analogue: its non-Markovian forward process admits a deterministic reverse sampler corresponding to the probability-flow ODE of a VP-type SDE.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why denoising score matching works: the perturbation viewpoint

The raw data score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})$ is pathological: on a low-dimensional manifold, $p_{\text{data}}$ is effectively singular, and in low-density regions there are too few samples to estimate anything reliably. Perturbation with Gaussian noise at scale $\sigma$ solves both problems at once: $p_\sigma$ has full support, and its score is smooth with magnitude $\mathcal{O}(1/\sigma)$. The price is bias — we learn the score of $p_\sigma$, not $p_{\text{data}}$ — but choosing a schedule with $\sigma_{\min}$ small makes the bias negligible while retaining learnability. This is the *multiscale* insight: a family $\{p_{\sigma_i}\}_{i=1}^L$ bridges the tractable prior and the singular data distribution [3]. The SDE framework [4] takes $L \to \infty$, replacing the geometric ladder with a continuous noise schedule $\sigma(t)$ — mathematically cleaner and numerically more flexible.

### 4.2 Predictor–corrector sampling: marrying solvers with MCMC

Numerical reverse-SDE solvers (e.g., Euler–Maruyama) discretize (2) with a *predictor* step:

$$\mathbf{x}_{t-\Delta t} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_t + \big[g(t)^2\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\big]\Delta t + g(t)\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\mathbf{z}.$$

Discretization error accumulates, and the learned score is imperfect. The **predictor–corrector (PC)** framework [4] interleaves each predictor step with *corrector* steps of score-based MCMC targeting $p_t$ — typically annealed Langevin dynamics:

```python
def pc_sampler(score_fn, prior_sample, ts, snr=0.16, n_correct=1):
    x = prior_sample()                      # x_T ~ p_T (approx. prior)
    for t_curr, t_next in zip(ts[:-1], ts[1:]):
        # ---- Predictor: one reverse-SDE (Euler-Maruyama) step ----
        drift = f(x, t_curr) - g(t_curr)**2 * score_fn(x, t_curr)
        x = x - drift * (t_curr - t_next) + g(t_curr) * (t_curr - t_next)**0.5 * torch.randn_like(x)
        # ---- Corrector: annealed Langevin MCMC at noise level t_next ----
        for _ in range(n_correct):
            grad = score_fn(x, t_next)
            eps = 2.0 * (snr * torch.norm(torch.randn_like(x), dim=-1, keepdim=True)
                         / (torch.norm(grad, dim=-1, keepdim=True) + 1e-8))**2
            x = x + 0.5 * eps * grad + (eps**0.5) * torch.randn_like(x)
    return x
```

The corrector exploits a beautiful fact: *the same score network* used for the reverse drift also defines a valid MCMC transition at every noise level. The corrector pulls samples back toward $p_t$ after each imperfect predictor step, substantially improving sample quality at fixed compute budgets [4]. Setting the number of corrector steps to zero recovers pure SDE sampling; setting the predictor to an identity map recovers annealed Langevin dynamics [3]. PC sampling thus *generalizes both* prior methods.

### 4.3 Likelihood, the probability flow, and variational bounds

With the probability-flow ODE (4), the log-likelihood follows from the continuous change of variables:

$$\log p_0(\mathbf{x}(0)) = \log p_T(\mathbf{x}(T)) + \int_0^T \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\!\cdot\!\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_\theta(\mathbf{x}(t), t)\,dt,$$

where $\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_\theta$ is the ODE drift with learned score and the divergence is estimated by the Skilling–Hutchinson trace estimator. Song et al. [4] report likelihoods competitive with autoregressive models on CIFAR-10. Complementary work recast diffusion training as a *variational* bound: Kingma et al. [9] showed the DDPM objective is a VLB whose tightness depends on the noise schedule, and optimizing the schedule itself (variational diffusion models) yields state-of-the-art likelihoods. The ODE and VLB perspectives are two faces of the same coin: both exploit the tractability of Gaussian transition kernels.

### 4.4 Conditional generation and the manifold hypothesis revisited

The framework extends naturally to conditional generation. For class-conditional sampling, a time-dependent classifier $p_t(y\mid\mathbf{x})$ yields the conditional score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}\mid y) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(y\mid\mathbf{x})$ — *classifier guidance*. Dhariwal and Nichol [10] showed that guided diffusion models surpass GANs on ImageNet synthesis quality, marking the moment diffusion displaced adversarial training as the dominant image-generation paradigm. For inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution), the same machinery conditions on measurements via Bayes' rule, solving $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{y})$. The manifold perspective also explains a subtle failure mode: when $\sigma_{\min}$ is too large, the model learns a *blurred* score and samples lack fine detail; when the schedule spends too little time at intermediate scales, mode coverage suffers — the continuous schedule of [4] gives practitioners fine-grained control over this trade-off.

## 5 Empirical Results / Theoretical Guarantees

**Empirical milestones.** The SDE framework unified and then surpassed its predecessors: NCSN++ and DDPM++ architectures trained with (3) and sampled with predictor–corrector achieved Inception scores and FID on CIFAR-10 and high-resolution FFHQ/CelebA-HQ that matched or exceeded contemporary GANs, *without* adversarial training [3,4]. Ho et al. [5] demonstrated that the simplified noise-prediction objective produces sharp $256\times256$ samples, while DDIM [8] showed deterministic probability-flow sampling cuts the step count by $10$–$50\times$ with minimal quality loss. The crowning empirical result came from guided diffusion [10]: on ImageNet $256\times256$, diffusion models achieved lower FID than the best GANs (BigGAN-deep), ending GAN dominance in image synthesis.

**Theoretical guarantees.** Convergence analyses bound the sampling error in terms of three ingredients: (i) the *score estimation error* $\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta - \nabla\log p_t\|^2$, controlled by the DSM objective (3); (ii) the *discretization error* of the reverse SDE solver, $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)$ for Euler–Maruyama under Lipschitz scores; and (iii) the *prior mismatch* $D(p_T \Vert \pi)$, decaying exponentially in $T$ for VP-type SDEs. Informally:

> **Theorem (informal convergence [4, theory literature]):** Under Lipschitz continuity of the true and learned scores and bounded second moments, the 2-Wasserstein distance between the sampler's output distribution $\hat{p}_0$ and $p_{\text{data}}$ satisfies $W_2(\hat{p}_0, p_{\text{data}}) \lesssim \underbrace{\varepsilon_{\text{score}}}_{\text{DSM error}} + \underbrace{\sqrt{\Delta t}}_{\text{discretization}} + \underbrace{e^{-cT}}_{\text{prior mismatch}}.$

The bound clarifies the design space: longer diffusion times and finer steps reduce structural error, while network capacity and data control $\varepsilon_{\text{score}}$. Corrector steps can be interpreted as *reducing* the effective discretization error by projecting iterates back onto $p_t$ [4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Sampling cost.** Even with probability-flow ODEs and DDIM-style strides, high-quality synthesis requires tens to hundreds of sequential network evaluations — orders of magnitude slower than single-pass GANs or VAEs. Distillation and consistency-style few-step methods mitigate but do not eliminate this gap.
2. **Score regularity assumptions.** The theory demands Lipschitz scores and decaying tails; real image scores can be extremely steep near manifold boundaries, and neural score estimates degrade precisely where they matter most — in low-density regions the network extrapolates.
3. **Likelihood–sample-quality tension.** The weighting $\lambda(t)$ that optimizes perceptual sample quality (emphasizing mid noise scales) differs from the maximum-likelihood weighting [9]; a single model rarely excels at both simultaneously.
4. **Curse of dimensionality in MCMC correctors.** Langevin correctors mix slowly in very high dimensions with ill-conditioned scores, and the signal-to-noise heuristic for step sizes is fragile across architectures.
5. **Evaluation gaps.** FID and Inception scores are imperfect proxies; likelihoods computed via the probability-flow ODE depend on trace-estimator variance and ODE solver tolerances, complicating apples-to-apples comparisons.

## 7 Conclusion

Score-based generative modeling with SDEs represents a rare synthesis: a method that is at once a principled statistical estimator, a flexible sampler, and an exact likelihood model. Denoising score matching [2] made score estimation tractable by turning it into denoising regression; noise-conditional training and annealed Langevin dynamics [3] made it work on manifold-supported data; the SDE formulation [4] revealed these as discretizations of a continuous theory governed by Anderson's time reversal [7]; the probability-flow ODE extracted determinism and likelihoods from the same learned object; and predictor–corrector sampling fused numerical SDE solvers with score-based MCMC into a strictly more general sampler. DDPMs [5], DDIM [8], and variational diffusion models [9] all live inside this framework as special choices of SDE, discretization, and weighting — a unification that redirected the entire generative-modeling field, culminating in diffusion models surpassing GANs [10]. The central open challenges — sampling efficiency, score regularity in low-density regions, and the likelihood–quality trade-off — define the current research frontier.
