# Homotopy Type Theory and Univalent Foundations: Cubical Type Theory, Higher Inductive Types, and Synthetic Homotopy Theory

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory reinterprets Martin-Löf's identity types as spaces of paths and augments them with Voevodsky's *univalence axiom*, which identifies equality of types with equivalence of types, and with *higher inductive types* that present spaces by generators and relations. This essay surveys the resulting univalent foundations: the homotopy interpretation of the identity type and its path induction principle, the stratification of types into homotopy truncation levels, the univalence axiom and its principle of structural invariance, higher inductive types such as the circle, suspensions and propositional truncations, and cubical type theory — the constructive semantics in which univalence computes [1][2]. We trace the synthetic homotopy theory developed in this setting: the encode-decode computation of the fundamental group of the circle, the Hopf fibration, the Blakers–Massey theorem, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, and Brunerie's calculation of the fourth homotopy group of the 3-sphere [3][4][5]. We assess the mechanization landscape in Agda, Coq and Cubical Agda, and close with open problems: canonicity under axiomatic univalence, the semantics of higher inductives, and the still-unnormalized Brunerie number [9].

## 1. Introduction

In the late 1990s, Hofmann and Streicher observed that the identity types of intensional Martin-Löf type theory admit a *groupoid* model: proofs of identity behave like morphisms, with composition, inverses and units satisfying the groupoid laws up to higher identity proofs. This was the first hint that dependent type theory has intrinsic homotopical content. The hint became a program a decade later, when Awodey and Warren generalized the observation to *weak higher groupoids*, and Vladimir Voevodsky — working from the model of type theory in Kan simplicial sets, and from his proof that the universe of small Kan complexes is itself a Kan complex — proposed the **univalence axiom**: for types in a universe, *equality coincides with equivalence*.

Voevodsky's proposal was radical. It made precise a practice mathematicians had always followed informally — identifying isomorphic structures — and elevated it to a logical principle. If two types are equivalent, there is a *path* between them in the universe, and every construction respects that path. As Awodey puts it, univalence is best read not as conflating equivalence with equality but as *expanding* the notion of identity to that of equivalence, yielding a principle of structural invariance for all of mathematics [7].

The Institute for Advanced Study hosted a special year on Univalent Foundations in 2012–2013, during which a large collaboration wrote the first systematic exposition, *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*, known as the HoTT book [1]. The book did two things at once. First, it developed the *homotopy interpretation*: types as spaces, terms as points, identity proofs as paths, iterated identities as higher paths. Second, it showed that this interpretation supports genuine *synthetic* homotopy theory — proofs of theorems about spheres, fibrations and homotopy groups carried out entirely inside type theory, with no reference to topological spaces, simplicial sets, or any analytic model.

The most celebrated early payoff was Brunerie's thesis [3]: a fully type-theoretic proof that the fourth homotopy group of the 3-sphere is the cyclic group of order two, a result whose classical proof dates to Pontryagin in the 1930s. That a proof assistant's logic could reproduce one of the hard-won computations of classical algebraic topology — and define the *Brunerie number*, a closed term of type natural numbers whose value *is* the theorem — demonstrated that univalent foundations are not merely an alternative notation but a working mathematical environment.

Yet the original formulation had a wound. Postulating univalence as an axiom destroys *canonicity*: a closed term of type natural numbers need not reduce to a numeral, because the axiom blocks computation. Voevodsky himself regarded a *constructive* interpretation of univalence — one in which the axiom computes — as the central open problem of the program. Its solution arrived in 2016 with **cubical type theory** [2]: a reformulation of dependent type theory around an interval object, in which paths are functions out of the interval, univalence is *provable*, and canonicity is restored.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and the identity type

We work in intensional Martin-Löf type theory with a hierarchy of universes. For a type `A` and terms `a b : A`, the **identity type** `a =_A b` (often written `Id_A(a,b)`) is generated by a single constructor, reflexivity `refl_a : a = a`, and governed by the elimination rule of *path induction*: to prove a property `P` of all paths, it suffices to prove it for reflexivity. In Agda-like notation:

```agda
data _≡_ {A : Type} (a : A) : A → Type where
  refl : a ≡ a

J : {A : Type} {a : A} (P : (b : A) → a ≡ b → Type)
  → P a refl → {b : A} (p : a ≡ b) → P b p
J P d refl = d
```

Path induction says that the type of paths *based at* `a` — the type `Σ(b : A). a = b` — is contractible. From `J` one derives the groupoid operations: path *concatenation* `p ∙ q`, path *inverse* `p⁻¹`, and proofs that these satisfy the unit, inverse and associativity laws — but only *up to higher paths*, not definitionally [1].

### 2.2 Homotopy levels

Not all types use their higher structure. Voevodsky stratified types by **homotopy level** (h-level), or equivalently by *truncation level*:

| Level | Name | Characterization |
|---|---|---|
| −2 | Contractible | `Σ(c : A). Π(x : A). x = c` |
| −1 | Mere proposition | `Π(x y : A). x = y` |
| 0 | Set | `Π(x y : A). isProp(x = y)` |
| 1 | Groupoid | identity types are sets |
| n+1 | `(n+1)`-type | identity types are `n`-types |

A *mere proposition* has at most one inhabitant up to identity; a *set* has identity types that are mere propositions, recovering the ordinary set-theoretic notion of equality. The hierarchy is cumulative: every `n`-type is an `(n+1)`-type. Crucially, the universe `U` itself is *not* a set: univalence will populate `A =_U B` with one path per equivalence [1].

### 2.3 Equivalences

An **equivalence** of types must be defined with care. Voevodsky's definition — a map `f : A → B` is an equivalence when all its homotopy fibers `Σ(a : A). f(a) = b` are contractible — is a mere proposition, hence well-behaved. The naive definition via a quasi-inverse, `Σ(g : B → A). (f ∘ g ~ id) × (g ∘ f ~ id)`, is *not* a mere proposition in general, and in fact contradicts univalence; the repair is the *half-adjoint* equivalence, which adds one coherence datum and is again propositional [1]. We write `A ≃ B` for the type of equivalences. There is always a canonical map in one direction:

> **Definition (idtoeqv).** By path induction on `p : A =_U B`, transporting along the identity family yields `idtoeqv(p) : A ≃ B`, with `idtoeqv(refl) = id-equiv`.

The **univalence axiom** asserts that this map is itself an equivalence:

> **Axiom (Univalence).** For `A B : U`, the map `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence. Equivalently, `(A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B)`.

Its inverse, usually written `ua`, turns every equivalence into a path between types. Transport along `ua(e)` then *computes* — up to a path — as application of `e` [1][7].

## 3. Methodology

This essay is a *survey and reconstruction*: it systematizes results from the primary literature rather than reporting new experiments. Our method has four components.

1. **Logical reconstruction.** We present the core definitions — identity types, h-levels, equivalences, univalence, higher inductive types — in the notation of book HoTT [1], checking each definition against the original sources for fidelity.
2. **Proof-chain analysis.** Synthetic homotopy theory is cumulative: the computation of `π₁(S¹)` feeds the Hopf fibration, which feeds the James construction, which with Blakers–Massey yields the Freudenthal theorem and the `π₄(S³)` calculation [3][5]. We trace these dependencies explicitly.
3. **Proof-theoretic comparison.** We contrast *axiomatic* univalence (postulated, canonicity lost) with *cubical* univalence (proved from interval and glueing structure, canonicity retained), following [2] and the analysis of cubical variants [8].
4. **Formalization audit.** We survey what has actually been mechanized — the Blakers–Massey theorem fully formalized in Agda [4], Brunerie's thesis as pen-and-paper type theory with a computational gap [3][9], the HoTT book reals and integers in Cubical Agda [6] — distinguishing proved, formalized, and merely sketched claims.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The identity type as a path space

The homotopy interpretation reads the formation rule for identity types geometrically:

- a type `A` is a *space*;
- a term `a : A` is a *point*;
- a proof `p : a = b` is a *path* from `a` to `b`;
- a proof `α : p = q` is a *homotopy* between paths, and so on.

Under this reading, path induction is the type-theoretic avatar of the fact that the *based path space* is contractible: any path with fixed left endpoint can be contracted to the constant path.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, **function extensionality** — pointwise equal functions are equal — is *provable from univalence* but independent of plain Martin-Löf type theory; it says the function type respects the path structure of its codomain. Second, **uniqueness of identity proofs** (UIP), which would collapse every type to a set, is *refuted* by univalence: the two-element type has a nontrivial self-equivalence (negation), hence by univalence a nontrivial loop in the universe, so `U` is not a set [1]. The universe is genuinely higher-dimensional.

### 4.2 Univalence and structural invariance

Univalence is often summarized as "equivalent types are identical," but its real force is the **invariance principle**: every construction in HoTT automatically respects equivalence [7]. Given `e : A ≃ B` and `t : P(A)` for any type family `P` over the universe, transport along `ua(e)` yields `e_* t : P(B)`. There is no need to prove separately that each definition is isomorphism-invariant; invariance is a *theorem of the logic*.

Philosophically, univalence resolves a tension Baez and Dolan once summarized as "never mistake equivalence for equality." Awodey's analysis [7] argues the axiom does not collapse the distinction but *enlarges* identity: in a structural foundation, what it means for two mathematical objects to be "the same" was always equivalence-invariant sameness, and univalence makes the logic match the practice. Whether univalent HoTT thereby constitutes an *autonomous* foundation — interpretable without recourse to set-theoretic models — remains debated [7], but its independence from any particular model is precisely what the cubical semantics [2] was built to secure.

### 4.3 Higher inductive types: spaces from constructors

Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point* constructors. **Higher inductive types** (HITs) additionally allow *path* constructors — identifications imposed between terms — so that a type can be presented the way a topologist presents a CW complex: generators and relations. The paradigmatic example is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

Its induction principle says that to define `f : Π(x : S¹). P(x)` it suffices to give `f(base)` and a *path over* `loop` relating the value at `base` to itself — the dependent version of the familiar fact that maps out of the circle correspond to loops. From this alone, the encode-decode method computes the loop space: define a type family `code : S¹ → Type` by `code(base) = ℤ` and transport along `loop` as successor; then `Ω(S¹, base) ≃ ℤ`, i.e., the fundamental group of the circle is the integers [1].

The HIT toolkit is small but expressive:

- **Suspensions** `ΣA`, with `Sⁿ⁺¹ = ΣSⁿ`, building all spheres from the booleans;
- **Pushouts**, the homotopical replacement for unions, from which cofibers and smash products follow;
- **Propositional truncation** `∥A∥`, collapsing a type to the mere proposition of its inhabitation — the bracket type that internalizes "there merely exists";
- **Set quotients** and **truncations** `∥A∥ₙ`, forcing a type down to a given h-level.

HITs thus serve both as synthetic spaces and as well-behaved data types with native computation rules in cubical implementations [6].

### 4.4 Cubical type theory: computation restored

Axiomatic univalence breaks canonicity because `ua(e)` is a stuck constant. **Cubical type theory** [2] repairs this by changing what a path *is*. The theory adds:

- an **interval** type `I` with endpoints `0` and `1` and de Morgan algebra structure (meet, join, reversal);
- **path types** `PathP A a b`, literally functions `p : I → A` with `p 0 = a` and `p 1 = b`;
- **composition** operations expressing that every open box has a filler (the type-theoretic Kan condition);
- **glueing**, which turns an equivalence `e : A ≃ B` into a path `ua(e) : Path U A B` *with computational behavior*.

In Cubical Agda this looks as follows:

```agda
-- paths are interval-indexed; composition fills open boxes
_∙_ : {x y z : A} → x ≡ y → y ≡ z → x ≡ z
p ∙ q i = hcomp (λ j → λ { (i = i0) → x
                        ; (i = i1) → q j })
                (p i)

-- univalence computes: transport along ua(e) acts as e
ua : {A B : Type} → A ≃ B → A ≡ B
ua = Glue -- (schematic: glueing a partial equivalence into a total path)

transport : {A B : Type} → A ≡ B → A → B
transport (ua e) a = equivFun e a   -- holds definitionally on refl-paths
```

Because `ua` is *defined* by glueing rather than postulated, transport along `ua(e)` reduces — on closed terms — to application of `e`, and **canonicity holds**: every closed term of type `ℕ` evaluates to a numeral [2]. Function extensionality is directly provable. The cubical sets model gives the whole theory a constructive semantics [2], and subsequent work has mapped the zoo of cube categories against their proof-theoretic and homotopical behavior [8].

### 4.5 Synthetic homotopy theory: from Blakers–Massey to π₄(S³)

With univalence and HITs in place, classical homotopy theory can be replayed *synthetically* — inside type theory, with no topological model in sight. Each level of the tower reuses the previous one [3][5].

1. **The Hopf fibration.** A fibration over `S²` is determined by fibers over the poles and an equivalence over each meridian. Taking both fibers to be `S¹` and the meridian equivalence to be multiplication by the meridian point gives the Hopf fibration, whose total space is `S³`. This yields `π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ` [3].
2. **The James construction.** For any type `A`, a sequence of approximations `JₙA` has colimit equivalent to `ΩΣA`, the loop space of the suspension. Applied with Blakers–Massey connectivity estimates, it shows `π₄(S³)` is cyclic: `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/nℤ` for a specific natural number `n` [3].
3. **The Brunerie number.** The integer `n` is defined as the image of the Whitehead product `[id_{S²}, id_{S²}] ∈ π₃(S²)` under the equivalence `π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ` coming from the Hopf fibration. A separate argument shows `|n| = 2`, hence `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ` [3].
4. **Blakers–Massey.** The connectivity theorem for pushouts was fully mechanized in Agda by Hou, Finster, Licata and Lumsdaine [4], a proof notable for being *essentially new*: its synthetic form generalized to arbitrary higher toposes and modalities, beyond the classical statement.

The frontier continues: Ljungström has constructed a new Brunerie number for `π₅(S³)`, showing it equals `ℤ/nℤ` for a constructively defined `n ∈ {1, 2}` — with the normalization that would decide the value still defeating current proof assistants [9]. The gap between *defining* the number and *computing* it measures exactly how far computational univalent foundations still have to go.

## 6. Limitations

Univalent foundations are young, and several limitations are structural rather than incidental.

- **Canonicity vs. axioms.** Book HoTT with postulated univalence lacks canonicity; closed natural-number terms can be stuck on `ua`. Cubical type theory restores canonicity [2], but at the cost of a more elaborate judgmental apparatus (interval, faces, composition, glueing) whose metatheory — normalization, decidability of conversion — is technically demanding and still being consolidated across cubical variants [8].
- **Higher inductive types, semantically.** The *specification* of HITs (formation, constructors, induction, computation rules) is well understood syntactically, but their *semantics* — in which models which HITs exist, and whether the induction principle captures initiality — remains subtle. Different cubical implementations support different HIT schemas, and the general schema is an active research area [2][6].
- **Agreement with classical homotopy theory.** That synthetic theorems *mean* the classical theorems rests on the interpretation of HoTT in spaces (simplicial sets, cubical sets). While the interpretation is established for book HoTT and fragments of cubical theory, a complete, watertight agreement theorem for full cubical type theory with general HITs is still being worked out [8].
- **Computational feasibility.** The Brunerie number exposes a gap between *constructive definability* and *feasible computability*. Cubical normalization of the term defining `π₄(S³)` has not terminated in practice; the number is computable in principle but not in reach [9]. Synthetic proofs can be computationally intractable even when they are logically pristine.
- **Foundational autonomy.** Whether univalent foundations can be presented and justified without leaning on set-theoretic or category-theoretic models — the question pressed by Ladyman and Presnell — is philosophically open, whatever one's view of the technical results [7].

None of these limitations is a refutation; several are the ordinary growing pains of a fifteen-year-old program. But they mark the boundary between what univalent foundations have achieved and what they have merely promised.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began with a structural observation about identity proofs and became, via Voevodsky's univalence axiom, a candidate foundation for all of mathematics [1][7]. Cubical type theory answered the program's deepest technical objection by making univalence compute [2]. And synthetic homotopy theory — from the fundamental group of the circle through Blakers–Massey and Freudenthal to Brunerie's `π₄(S³)` — has shown that the great computations of twentieth-century algebraic topology can be replayed, and sometimes generalized, inside type theory [3][4][5]. The open problems are now computational and semantic rather than conceptual: normalize the Brunerie number, settle the semantics of general higher inductives, and complete the agreement between synthetic and classical homotopy theory [8][9]. If the next decade resolves them, univalent foundations will have done something no foundation since Zermelo–Fraenkel has managed — not merely to encode existing mathematics, but to change the medium in which it is proved.
