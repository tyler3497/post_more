# Quantum Error Mitigation for Near-Term Devices: Zero-Noise Extrapolation, Probabilistic Error Cancellation, and the Sampling-Overhead Frontier

## Abstract

Quantum error mitigation (QEM) is the principal software layer extending the computational reach of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware before fault tolerance arrives. This thesis develops a unified theoretical account of the two foundational mitigation families introduced by Temme, Bravyi and Gambetta — zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) and probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) — and analyzes the sampling-overhead frontier that determines their practical regimes. We review Richardson-type extrapolation with digital noise scaling by unitary folding, quasiprobabilistic channel inversion with its characteristic gamma-squared sampling overhead, and sparse Pauli-Lindblad noise learning that renders PEC deployable on real processors. We prove the unbiasedness of ideal PEC, bound the bias-variance tradeoff of polynomial ZNE, and show how total sampling cost scales as the product of per-gate one-norms squared over circuit volume. We survey recent overhead-reduction methods — Pauli error propagation, control variates, and blockwise noise aggregation — and benchmark their scaling against hardware results. We conclude that the mitigation frontier is not accuracy but affordability: unbiased estimation is achievable, yet its price grows exponentially in circuit volume, forcing a disciplined choice between biased-but-cheap and unbiased-but-expensive estimators.

## 1 Intro

The noisy intermediate-scale quantum era is defined by a single uncomfortable fact: the processors exist, they are programmable, and they are wrong. Gate infidelities of order 10^-3, finite coherence times, readout errors, and crosstalk conspire so that the expectation value extracted from a deep circuit bears only a decaying resemblance to the ideal value [1]. Quantum error correction, the asymptotic answer, demands orders of magnitude more physical qubits and lower physical error rates than are currently available. Between the hardware we have and the hardware we want sits a software layer — quantum error mitigation — that trades classical sampling effort for accuracy without adding a single qubit.

Temme, Bravyi and Gambetta's 2017 letter introduced both schemes in one stroke: extrapolation to the zero-noise limit and error cancellation by resampling circuits under a quasi-probability distribution [1]. Nearly a decade later ZNE and PEC remain the twin poles of the field, and their rivalry is economic: ZNE is cheap and biased; PEC is unbiased and expensive. The currency is *sampling overhead* — the factor by which circuit executions must grow to hit a target precision.

This thesis (1) unifies both methods in one mathematical framework for direct bias-variance-overhead comparison, (2) derives the scaling laws of the sampling-overhead frontier, including the per-gate negativity product behind PEC's exponential cost, and (3) surveys modern reduction techniques — Pauli error propagation, control variates, blockwise aggregation — grounded in published hardware benchmarks [2][3][4][5][6].

The argument is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews NISQ noise models and the estimation problem. Section 3 formalizes the mitigation framework. Section 4 develops the two methods and their modern extensions in depth. Section 5 proves the key guarantees and reports empirical scaling numbers. Section 6 catalogs the limitations honestly, and Section 7 concludes with guidance for practitioners.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The NISQ noise landscape

A NISQ processor implements a target unitary as a sequence of imperfect physical operations. The dominant error mechanisms are:

- **Depolarizing noise**: with probability p the state is replaced by I/2 — the canonical symmetric model.
- **Amplitude/phase damping**: T1 relaxation and T2 dephasing from environmental coupling.
- **Coherent errors**: systematic over-rotations that accumulate quadratically in depth before twirling.
- **Readout errors**: classical bit-flips in measurement, handled by separate calibration.
- **Crosstalk and leakage**: correlated errors violating the locality assumptions of efficient noise learning.

Pauli twirling (randomized compiling) converts coherent and general Markovian errors into stochastic Pauli channels — the enabling simplification for practical mitigation, since a twirled gate is well described by a learnable Pauli channel [3].

### 2.2 The estimation problem

Near-term algorithms — VQE, quantum classifiers, Trotterized dynamics — reduce to estimating <O> = Tr[O rho_ideal]. The device samples rho_noisy instead. Mitigation builds an estimator <O>_mit from noisy executions plus classical post-processing, spending *classical* repetitions rather than extra qubits or coherence [1].

### 2.3 Mitigation is not correction

Error *correction* encodes redundantly and, below threshold, suppresses logical error exponentially in code distance. Error *mitigation* accepts the noisy distribution and reshapes the estimator. Because the unmitigated signal decays exponentially with depth, any fixed-shot mitigation eventually drowns in variance — so the field's central question is not *whether* mitigation works, but *how far in circuit volume* it works at fixed shot budget.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Formal circuit model

Consider an ideal circuit U = U_d ... U_2 U_1 acting on n qubits, with the observable O measured at the end. The physical implementation applies noisy gates:

```
rho_k = E_k(U_k rho_{k-1} U_k^dagger),   rho_0 = |0><0|^n
```

Each E_k is a CPTP noise channel. The experimenter may execute the noisy circuit — or *modified* variants (folded, twirled, resampled) — N times at unit quantum cost per shot; classical post-processing is free, and the shot budget N is the scarce resource.

### 3.2 The two estimator families

**Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE).** Measure E(lambda) at amplified noise levels {r_0=1 < r_1 < ... < r_K}, fit a model, and evaluate at lambda = 0. The estimator is *biased* by model mismatch but costs only a small constant factor more shots than the unmitigated run [1][2].

**Probabilistic error cancellation (PEC).** Write each inverse noise channel as E_k^{-1} = sum_j q_{k,j} B_{k,j} with possibly negative coefficients. Sample B_{k,j} with probability |q_{k,j}|/gamma_k, weight outcomes by gamma_k sign(q_{k,j}), and average. The estimator is *unbiased*, but its variance is amplified by gamma^2 = (prod_k gamma_k)^2, where gamma_k = sum_j |q_{k,j}| is the *negativity* of the decomposition [1][3].

### 3.3 The sampling-overhead metric

For single-shot variance sigma^2 and target standard error epsilon, N = sigma^2/epsilon^2 shots are needed. The sampling overhead eta is the ratio of N for the mitigated estimator to N for the ideal experiment: eta = gamma^2 for PEC, and sum_k beta_k^2 (times shot allocation) for order-K ZNE [2]. Everything that follows is a study of eta.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Richardson extrapolation and polynomial ZNE

Suppose the noise-scaled expectation admits a Taylor expansion around zero noise:

```
E(lambda) = E(0) + a_1 lambda + a_2 lambda^2 + ... + a_K lambda^K + O(lambda^{K+1})
```

Richardson's deferred approach to the limit forms a linear combination of measurements at scaled noise levels that cancels the first K powers. With scale factors r_0 < r_1 < ... < r_K and weights beta_k solving the Vandermonde system sum_k beta_k r_k^j = delta_{j0} for j = 0..K, the Richardson estimator

```
E_Rich = sum_{k=0}^{K} beta_k E_hat(r_k)
```

giving |E_Rich - E(0)| = O(lambda^{K+1}) absent statistical noise [1]. The price is Var[E_Rich] = sigma^2 sum_k beta_k^2/N_k, and the Vandermonde system is exponentially ill-conditioned in K — so practical ZNE rarely exceeds cubic order, and higher order buys lower bias at variance cost with an exponentially worsening exchange rate.

Polynomials are not the only model class: since deep noisy circuits converge to the depolarizing fixed point, an exponential ansatz E(lambda) = a + b exp(-c lambda) often extrapolates more stably, and adaptive protocols that select the model by statistical evidence outperform fixed choices [2].

> **Theorem: ZNE bias-variance tradeoff.** Let E be (K+1)-times differentiable with bounded derivatives and let E_hat(r_k) be independent estimators with variance sigma^2/N_k. Then the K-th order Richardson estimator satisfies bias O(lambda^{K+1}) and variance sigma^2 (sum_k beta_k^2 / N_k), where the weights beta_k grow in magnitude at least exponentially in K for fixed scale-factor spacing. In particular, eliminating bias to order lambda^{K+1} amplifies sampling cost by a factor exponential in K.

```python
import numpy as np

def richardson_weights(scale_factors):
    # Solve sum_k beta_k * r_k**j = delta_{j0} for j = 0..K
    r = np.asarray(scale_factors, dtype=float)
    K = len(r) - 1
    V = np.vander(r, K + 1, increasing=True).T  # V[j,k] = r_k**j
    rhs = np.zeros(K + 1)
    rhs[0] = 1.0
    beta = np.linalg.solve(V, rhs)
    return beta  # variance amplification = sum(beta**2)

# Example: scale factors [1, 3, 5] -> quadratic Richardson
b = richardson_weights([1.0, 3.0, 5.0])
print('weights:', b, 'variance factor:', np.sum(b**2))
```

### 4.2 Digital noise scaling: folding, unoptimization, and inverted circuits

ZNE requires the ability to *amplify* noise controllably. Analog approaches stretch control pulses in time, which demands pulse-level access. The digital alternative, introduced by Giurgica-Tiron et al., is *unitary folding*: replace a gate or subcircuit G by G (G^dagger G)^n, which implements the same unitary (up to the noise) while executing 1 + 2n times as many noisy gates [2]. Global folding (fold the whole circuit) yields integer scale factors r = 1, 3, 5, ...; local folding (fold individual gates, possibly at random) yields fine-grained fractional scale factors and better averaging over gate-dependent noise.

Pelofske and Russo proposed *circuit unoptimization*: rewriting the circuit into a unitarily equivalent, gate-heavier form that generates an exponentially large family of folded variants, suppressing coherent-error bias and resisting server-side compiler simplification [7]. *Inverted-circuit ZNE* appends the inverse circuit to measure the error strength epsilon directly and extrapolates in measured epsilon; with randomized compiling it beats standard ZNE on simulators and real hardware [8].

Digital ZNE benchmarks report 18X-24X error reductions at larger qubit counts than prior studies [2] — but with a structural weakness: when E(lambda) has saturated at the depolarizing fixed point for all accessible lambda, every model fits a flat line and the zero-noise estimate is artifact. ZNE is a small-noise expansion that knows nothing beyond its radius of convergence.

### 4.3 Quasiprobability decomposition: the machinery of PEC

PEC inverts noise exactly. For each noisy gate with channel E_k, find implementable CPTP maps B_{k,j} and real coefficients q_{k,j} with E_k^{-1} = sum_j q_{k,j} B_{k,j} [1]; since a channel inverse is generally not CP, some coefficients are negative — hence *quasi*-probability. The Monte Carlo protocol:

1. For each gate k, sample index j_k with probability p_{k,j} = |q_{k,j}| / gamma_k, where gamma_k = sum_j |q_{k,j}|.
2. Execute the circuit with B_{k,j_k} substituted for the ideal gate at every position.
3. Multiply the measured eigenvalue of O by the global sign s = prod_k sign(q_{k,j_k}) and the global weight Gamma = prod_k gamma_k.
4. Average over many random circuit instances.

The reweighted average is unbiased for <O>_ideal, with single-shot variance inflated by Gamma^2. For N_g gates of strength epsilon, gamma_g = 1 + 2 epsilon + O(epsilon^2), so Gamma ~ exp(2 epsilon N_g) and eta = Gamma^2 ~ exp(4 epsilon N_g) — the hard wall of PEC: unbiasedness is for sale, and its price doubles roughly every 1/(4 epsilon) gates [1][3].

> **Theorem: PEC unbiasedness and overhead.** Let each noise channel E_k be invertible with quasiprobability decomposition of one-norm gamma_k, and let the sampling protocol above be executed. Then the estimator <O>_PEC satisfies E[<O>_PEC] = <O>_ideal exactly, and Var[<O>_PEC] = Gamma^2 Var_ideal / N + O(1/N) cross terms, with Gamma = prod_k gamma_k. The sampling overhead relative to the noiseless experiment is eta = Gamma^2.

```rust
// Sketch of the PEC Monte Carlo loop (one shot)
fn pec_shot(
    gates: &[GateDecomp], // per-gate quasiprobability decompositions
    rng: &mut impl Rng,
) -> (f64, f64) {
    let mut log_gamma = 0.0_f64;
    let mut sign = 1.0_f64;
    let mut circuit = Vec::with_capacity(gates.len());
    for g in gates {
        let j = g.sample_index(rng); // prob |q_j| / gamma
        log_gamma += g.gamma.ln();
        sign *= g.coeffs[j].signum();
        circuit.push(g.ops[j]);
    }
    let outcome = execute_noisy_variant(&circuit); // +-1 eigenvalue
    let weight = log_gamma.exp() * sign;
    (weight * outcome, weight) // accumulate numerator; denominator -> 1
}
```

Decomposing into the full Pauli basis is canonical but expensive; a *sparse* basis adapted to the learned noise model is what makes PEC practical.

### 4.4 Sparse Pauli-Lindblad noise learning

PEC's unbiasedness assumes the true channel is known; full process tomography scales exponentially. Van den Berg et al. model each gate layer's noise by a *sparse Pauli-Lindblad* generator [3]:

```
E = exp(L),   L(rho) = sum_k lambda_k (P_k rho P_k - rho)
```

The sum runs over sparse low-weight Paulis with learnable rates lambda_k >= 0. Twirling justifies the Pauli form, sparsity makes learning polynomial, and the Lindblad exponential keeps the fit physical. Crucially, exp(-L) admits a quasiprobability decomposition of *minimal* one-norm, directly minimizing PEC overhead for the learned model [3].

Demonstrated on superconducting processors, this converts PEC from a theoretical ideal into a calibrated protocol: learn the sparse generator, invert it quasiprobabilistically, pay gamma^2. Residual bias is then dominated by *model mismatch* (non-Markovianity, drift, weight-3+ terms) rather than statistical noise [3].

### 4.5 The sampling-overhead frontier

All modern PEC research attacks Gamma^2, and since the one-norm factorizes over gates, every per-gate reduction compounds exponentially. Three directions define the state of the art:

- **Pauli error propagation (Scheiber et al.).** For Clifford circuits, propagate Pauli errors classically to the measurement and compress the decomposition before sampling. Exact and cheap via the Clifford-Pauli normalizer structure, with substantial reductions for Clifford-dominated circuits and graceful degradation as non-Clifford count grows [4].
- **Control variates (CV4Quantum).** Import a classical statistics trick: subtract a correlated, cheaply computable control variate of known mean from the PEC estimator. In realistic simulations, over half the estimations needed >50% fewer samples for equal precision — a constant factor multiplying through the exponential [5].
- **Blockwise aggregation (MoSAIC).** Partition the circuit into noise-aligned blocks, learn an effective block noise model variationally, and invert once per block. At 50 qubits, gamma drops from 23.044 (layerwise PEC) to 6.654 — roughly 12x less variance at fixed budget — validated on 156-qubit hardware [6].

| Method | Bias | Overhead scaling | Key requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZNE (Richardson, order K) | O(lambda^{K+1}) | sum beta_k^2, exp. in K | Smooth E(lambda); small noise |
| Standard PEC | 0 (ideal model) | Gamma^2 = prod gamma_k^2, exp. in volume | Accurate channel knowledge |
| PEC + sparse Pauli-Lindblad | model mismatch only | minimized per-gate gamma | Twirlable Markovian noise |
| PEC + Pauli propagation | 0 (Clifford) | reduced effective Gamma | Clifford-dominated circuit |
| PEC + control variates | 0 | c * Gamma^2, c < 1 | Good control variate |
| MoSAIC blockwise PEC | variational approx. | block-aggregated gamma^2 | Block-learnable noise |

Every row is a point on the bias-variance-overhead Pareto surface; none dominates another.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Guarantees

*PEC* is unbiased under exact noise knowledge with overhead exactly Gamma^2; under a learned sparse Pauli-Lindblad model, residual bias is bounded by the diamond-norm distance between true and learned channels times the observable norm [3]. *ZNE* of order K has bias O(lambda^{K+1}) and variance amplification sum_k beta_k^2 — for scale factors {1,3,5}, quadratic Richardson amplifies variance by order 10^1 and cubic by over 10^2, which is why the literature converges on low-order extrapolation [1][2].

In the small-noise limit the two agree to extrapolation order — both are linear maps acting as identity on the noiseless component while annihilating the first K noise orders. They diverge where it matters: at large circuit volume, where ZNE's Taylor model breaks down and PEC's overhead explodes.

### 5.2 Reported benchmarks

The empirical record, drawn from the cited literature, quantifies the frontier:

1. **Digital ZNE** achieves 18X-24X error reductions over unmitigated circuits at larger qubit counts than prior studies, via unitary folding and adaptive extrapolation [2].
2. **Inverted-circuit ZNE** with randomized compiling cuts RMSE 3x versus standard ZNE on depolarizing-noise simulators, with closer medians and fewer outliers on real hardware [8].
3. **Sparse Pauli-Lindblad PEC** demonstrated on superconducting processors, establishing learnability and practical inversion at scale [3].
4. **Blockwise MoSAIC**: gamma = 6.654 vs 23.044 for standard PEC at 50 qubits (~12x variance reduction at equal budget), validated on 156-qubit hardware — the largest PEC-based hardware demonstration to date [6].
5. **CV4Quantum** cut required samples by >50% in over half of simulated PEC estimations — a model-agnostic gain composing with any decomposition [5].
6. **Pauli-propagation PEC** significantly reduces overhead for Clifford circuits via exact classical preprocessing, degrading gracefully with non-Clifford count [4].

The pattern is coherent: ZNE delivers order-of-magnitude suppression cheaply for shallow circuits, while PEC stays exact but fights an exponential whose base keeps shrinking.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloging where the framework strains.

**Model mismatch.** PEC's unbiasedness concerns the *modeled* noise; drift, non-Markovian memory, and residual coherent errors become bias, and characterizing them needs the tomography sparsity avoids [3].

**ZNE's radius of convergence.** Extrapolation is analytic continuation from lambda >= 1 to 0, valid only while E(lambda) is smooth. At large depth the curve saturates and extrapolation manufactures precision from a flat line — a limitation of information, not fitting [2][8].

**The exponential wall.** Gamma^2 ~ exp(4 epsilon N_g) is the actual, tight cost. A 1000-gate circuit at 10^-3 error per gate pays Gamma^2 ~ e^4 ~ 55; at 10^-2 per gate it pays e^40 ~ 10^17. Mitigation extends NISQ reach by a *constant factor* in circuit volume, not asymptotically.

**Learning cost and drift.** Noise learning needs dedicated benchmarking and assumes the model still describes later computation circuits; on drifting hardware the model stales, and relearning consumes device time — a meta-overhead rarely reported [3].

**Composability gaps.** Twirling for PEC can interfere with the coherent structure dynamical decoupling exploits, and the sampling budgets of composed methods multiply.

---

## 7 Conclusion

ZNE and PEC are complementary answers to one question: *how much classical sampling will you spend to recover signal from noise?* ZNE spends little and accepts bias — the right tool when noise is small and E(lambda) has not saturated [1][2]. PEC spends exponentially in circuit volume and buys exact unbiasedness; sparse Pauli-Lindblad models made it real, and Pauli propagation, control variates, and blockwise aggregation keep shrinking its cost [3][4][5][6].

The sampling-overhead frontier — the Pareto surface trading bias against variance against shots — is the proper object of study. The practitioner's rule: twirl your circuits, learn a sparse noise model, extrapolate where noise is small, cancel where the budget allows, and never trust an estimator whose overhead you have not computed. Fault tolerance will move the frontier, not abolish it.
