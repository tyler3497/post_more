# Floquet Engineering and Discrete Time Crystals: Periodically Driven Quantum Matter

## Abstract

Periodically driven quantum systems admit no ground state and no Gibbs ensemble, yet they host robust phases of matter with no equilibrium counterpart. Floquet's theorem factorizes the evolution operator into a periodic micromotion operator and stroboscopic evolution under a time-independent Floquet Hamiltonian H_F, accessible perturbatively through the Floquet-Magnus and van Vleck high-frequency expansions. Truncated expansions govern a long-lived prethermal regime whose heating time grows exponentially in the drive frequency, a rigorous result of Abanin, De Roeck, Ho, and Huveneers. This thesis develops Floquet theory from first principles, derives the leading expansion terms, analyzes Floquet topological phases including the anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator, and gives a complete treatment of discrete time crystals — the Khemani-Lazarides-Moessner-Sondhi and Else-Bauer-Nayak mechanism of spontaneous discrete time-translation symmetry breaking, the 2017 trapped-ion and NV-center experiments, and the 2021 Google Sycamore realization — closing with exact-diagonalization methodology, proof sketches, and open problems.

## 1. Introduction

The twentieth-century edifice of condensed matter physics rests overwhelmingly on the assumption of equilibrium: a time-independent Hamiltonian H furnishes the spectrum, the ground state anchors zero-temperature physics, and the Gibbs state exp(-beta H) governs finite temperature. Periodically driven — *Floquet* — systems shatter this taxonomy in a controlled way: the Hamiltonian satisfies H(t+T) = H(t), so time translation is not absent but *discrete*, and discrete symmetries can be broken, gauged, and topologically classified like any other.

The consequences have been far-reaching. By 2011, Lindner, Refael, and Galitski had shown that circularly polarized light can imprint a nontrivial Chern number on the bands of an otherwise trivial semiconductor quantum well — a topological insulator engineered purely by the drive, with no static lattice capable of producing it [8]. By 2016, the joint theoretical proposal of Khemani, Lazarides, Moessner, and Sondhi, together with the independent framework of Else, Bauer, and Nayak, established that periodically driven, disordered, interacting spin systems can spontaneously break the discrete time-translation symmetry of their own drive — a *discrete time crystal* — exhibiting rigid subharmonic response at multiples of the driving period that is robust against perturbations and persists in the thermodynamic limit [3][4]. These ideas culminated in landmark experiments: the 2017 observation of discrete time-crystalline signatures in a chain of trapped ytterbium ions by Zhang, Yao, and Monroe's group, and the 2021–2022 realization of time-crystalline eigenstate order on Google's Sycamore superconducting processor [9][10].

The theoretical engine is the Floquet-Magnus expansion, a systematic high-frequency expansion constructing the effective stroboscopic Hamiltonian order by order in the inverse drive frequency [1][7]. Its modern understanding, sharpened by rigorous heating bounds of Abanin, De Roeck, Ho, and Huveneers, reveals that driven many-body systems do not thermalize to infinite temperature immediately: they first enter a *prethermal* regime whose lifetime is exponentially long in the drive frequency [6]. Prethermalization is what makes Floquet engineering experimentally viable — and discrete time crystals observable.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the mathematical background: Floquet's theorem, the quasienergy spectrum, the stroboscopic Floquet Hamiltonian, and the micromotion operator. Section 3 develops the methodology — the Floquet-Magnus and van Vleck expansions, convergence results, and the prethermalization bound — alongside a numerical exact-diagonalization recipe. Section 4 undertakes the deep dive: Floquet topological phases, the anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator, discrete time crystals in many-body localized and prethermal variants, and the micromotion gauge structure. Section 5 presents empirical results and proof sketches, Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Floquet's Theorem

Consider the time-dependent Schrödinger equation i d/dt |psi(t)> = H(t) |psi(t)> with H(t+T) = H(t) for some period T. Floquet's theorem, the temporal analogue of Bloch's theorem, asserts that the evolution operator U(t,0) admits the factorization [1]

> **Theorem:** There exists a Hermitian operator H_F, the Floquet Hamiltonian, and a T-periodic unitary operator P(t) = P(t+T), the micromotion operator, such that U(t,0) = P(t) exp(-i H_F t). Equivalently, the solutions of the Schrödinger equation can be written |psi_alpha(t)> = exp(-i epsilon_alpha t) |u_alpha(t)> with |u_alpha(t+T)> = |u_alpha(t)>.

The eigenvalues epsilon_alpha of H_F are the *quasienergies*, defined modulo 2 pi / T, exactly as crystal momenta are defined modulo reciprocal lattice vectors. The eigenstates |u_alpha(t)> are the Floquet modes. The stroboscopic dynamics — observation at integer multiples of the period — is governed entirely by H_F: U(nT, 0) = exp(-i H_F nT), while the intra-period micromotion is encoded in P(t).

A crucial subtlety is that H_F is not unique: the branch of the matrix logarithm defining H_F from U(T,0) = exp(-i H_F T), and the choice of stroboscopic frame (initial phase t_0), generate a *gauge structure* [7]. Physical observables at stroboscopic times are gauge independent, but the decomposition into macromotion and micromotion is not, and this gauge freedom is precisely what the van Vleck expansion exploits to define a t_0-independent effective Hamiltonian.

### 2.2 The Stroboscopic Floquet Hamiltonian

Formally, H_F[t_0] is defined by the relation

U(t_0 + T, t_0) = T exp(-i integral from t_0 to t_0+T of H(t) dt) = exp(-i H_F[t_0] T),

where T denotes time ordering [1]. The dependence on t_0 is explicit: different stroboscopic frames yield unitarily related Floquet Hamiltonians, H_F[t_0'] = W H_F[t_0] W^dagger with W = U(t_0', t_0). This unitary equivalence guarantees identical quasienergy spectra but different eigenvectors — and, importantly, different truncations of any perturbative expansion.

### 2.3 Heating and the Absence of Equilibrium

A generic isolated driven many-body system is expected, over long times, to absorb energy from the drive without bound, approaching the infinite-temperature maximally mixed state [6]. For ergodic driven systems the Floquet eigenstates satisfy a Floquet eigenstate thermalization hypothesis and locally resemble infinite-temperature states. The profound question — answered only in the last decade — is *how fast* this heating proceeds: exponentially slowly in the drive frequency, the content of the prethermalization theorem of Section 3.3.

### 2.4 Many-Body Localization as a Stabilizer

Many-body localization (MBL) offers a route to driven phases that never heat at all. In an MBL system, strong disorder and interactions produce an extensive set of quasi-local integrals of motion, the l-bits, which prevent thermalization entirely [3]. A periodically driven MBL system can then host Floquet eigenstates with long-range order in *time* — the discrete time crystal. Whether MBL survives in the thermodynamic limit above one spatial dimension remains contested [6], and the time-crystal program inherits this controversy.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The Floquet-Magnus Expansion

The Floquet-Magnus expansion is obtained by applying the Magnus expansion to the time-ordered exponential defining U(T, t_0). Writing U(T,t_0) = exp(Omega), with Omega = sum over k of Omega_k, the Floquet Hamiltonian is H_F = (i/T) Omega. The first terms are [1]

- **Zeroth order:** H_F^(0) = (1/T) integral from 0 to T of H(t) dt, the naive time average.
- **First order:** H_F^(1) = (1/(2 i T)) integral from 0 to T dt_1 integral from 0 to t_1 dt_2 [H(t_1), H(t_2)], capturing the non-commutativity of the Hamiltonian at different times.
- **Second order:** a combination of nested commutators [H(t_1), [H(t_2), H(t_3)]] with polynomial weight functions, scaling as T^2.

Each successive term carries an additional power of the period T, so for high-frequency driving (small T relative to local energy scales), the series is asymptotic and its optimal truncation yields an effective Hamiltonian accurate up to exponentially small corrections [6][7].

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import expm, logm

def floquet_hamiltonian(H_of_t, T, dim, n_steps=2000):
    """Compute H_F from U(T,0) = T exp(-i int H dt) via Trotterized product."""
    dt = T / n_steps
    U = np.eye(dim, dtype=complex)
    for k in range(n_steps):
        t = (k + 0.5) * dt
        U = expm(-1j * H_of_t(t) * dt) @ U
    HF = 1j / T * logm(U)  # principal branch
    return (HF + HF.conj().T) / 2  # symmetrize numerical noise

def magnus_orders(H_of_t, T, n_steps=2000):
    """Zeroth- and first-order Floquet-Magnus terms."""
    ts = np.linspace(0, T, n_steps)
    dt = ts[1] - ts[0]
    Hs = [H_of_t(t) for t in ts]
    H0 = sum(Hs) * dt / T
    H1 = np.zeros_like(H0)
    for i, t1 in enumerate(ts):
        for j in range(i):
            H1 += (Hs[i] @ Hs[j] - Hs[j] @ Hs[i]) * dt * dt
    H1 = H1 / (2j * T)
    return H0, (H1 + H1.conj().T) / 2
```

The code above illustrates the central computational strategy: exact construction of U(T) by Trotterization followed by the matrix logarithm, benchmarked against truncated Magnus terms. For a driven two-level system H(t) = (Delta/2) sigma_z + (A/2) cos(omega t) sigma_x, the exact quasienergies can be compared with the Magnus approximants to verify the expected O(T^(n+1)) error scaling.

### 3.2 The van Vleck (High-Frequency) Expansion

An alternative expansion, systematized by Bukov, D'Alessio, and Polkovnikov, works in the extended (Sambe) Hilbert space of T-periodic functions, where the quasienergy operator Q = H(t) - i d/dt acts [7]. Block-diagonalizing Q in the Fourier-harmonic basis via a unitary rotation yields an effective Hamiltonian H_eff *independent* of the stroboscopic frame t_0, at the price of a kick operator K(t) dressing states and observables. The two expansions agree at stroboscopic times up to the computed order; their difference is a gauge transformation. The high-frequency analysis classifies driving protocols where the infinite-frequency Floquet Hamiltonian differs from the time average — the Kapitza pendulum, the Harper-Hofstadter model of atoms in artificial gauge fields, and the Haldane-type Floquet Chern insulator [7].

### 3.3 Prethermalization: Rigorous Heating Bounds

The landmark rigorous result of Abanin, De Roeck, Ho, and Huveneers establishes that for a lattice system with local interactions of strength J driven at frequency omega >> J, there exists a quasi-conserved effective Hamiltonian H_* (a truncated high-frequency expansion) such that energy absorption is suppressed for a time tau_* that grows *exponentially* in omega/J [6]. The mechanism is transparent: absorbing one quantum omega of energy from the drive requires a collective rearrangement involving on the order of omega/J local degrees of freedom, an event whose amplitude is exponentially suppressed by locality. Consequences:

1. Local observables relax rapidly to the Gibbs state of H_* — the prethermal plateau.
2. The plateau persists for tau_* ~ exp(const * omega/J).
3. Eventual heating to infinite temperature is unavoidable but parametrically delayed.

This theorem is the rigorous foundation of Floquet engineering: it guarantees that effective Hamiltonians computed by truncated high-frequency expansions describe real, long-lived physics, not formal artifacts.

### 3.4 Exact Diagonalization of the Floquet Unitary

The numerical workhorse of the field is exact diagonalization of U(T) for spin chains of up to roughly 20 sites (Hilbert space dimension ~10^6 with symmetries). Diagnostics include:

- **Quasienergy level statistics:** the ratio of consecutive gaps r = min(delta_n, delta_{n+1}) / max(delta_n, delta_{n+1}) distinguishes Poisson (MBL, <r> approx 0.386) from circular orthogonal ensemble (ergodic, <r> approx 0.53) statistics.
- **Eigenstate entanglement entropy:** volume law for ergodic Floquet eigenstates, area law for MBL ones.
- **Spectral pairing:** in the time-crystal phase, quasienergies come in pairs separated by pi/T, the spectral signature of period doubling [3][4].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Floquet Topological Phases: The Driven Chern Insulator

Topology enriches the Floquet setting because the quasienergy spectrum is periodic: gaps open at quasienergy zero *and* at the zone boundary epsilon = pi/T, permitting edge modes with no static analogue. The paradigmatic example is graphene irradiated by circularly polarized light: the Peierls substitution generates, at order 1/omega, an effective imaginary next-nearest-neighbor hopping — the Haldane mass term — opening a gap at the Dirac points with Chern number C = +/- 1 [7][8]. The resulting chiral edge states circulate unidirectionally around the sample.

### 4.2 The Anomalous Floquet-Anderson Insulator

Even more striking is the anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator (AFAI) of Titum, Lindner, Rechtsman, and Refael [8]: a 2D driven lattice in which *all* bulk Floquet bands carry zero Chern number, yet robust chiral edge modes traverse the quasienergy zone boundary. The invariant is not any band's Chern number but a *winding number of the full-period evolution operator*, counting net edge chirality across all gaps including the anomalous pi/T gap.

| Phase | Bulk invariant | Edge signature | Static analogue? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floquet Chern insulator | Chern numbers of Floquet bands | Chiral edge modes in 0-gap | Yes (Haldane model) |
| Anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator | Winding of U(T) | Chiral modes in pi-gap, trivial bands | **No** |
| Discrete time crystal | Spectral pi/T pairing | Subharmonic edge response | **No** |
| Floquet symmetry-protected topological | Projective representation of G x Z | Fractionalized boundary modes | Partial |

### 4.3 Discrete Time Crystals: Spontaneous Breaking of Time Translation

The 2012 proposal of Wilczek — a ground state with periodic motion — was ruled out in equilibrium by the no-go theorems of Bruno and of Watanabe and Oshikawa [2].

> **Theorem:** In a periodically driven, many-body localized spin system with an emergent Ising symmetry, the Floquet eigenstates exhibit long-range spatiotemporal order: local observables oscillate at integer multiples of the driving period, and the quasienergy spectrum displays rigid pi/T pairing robust to arbitrary weak perturbations preserving the period.

The canonical model is the driven random-field Ising chain with imperfect pi-pulses:

U_F = exp(-i H_int T_int) exp(-i (pi/2 - epsilon) sum_i sigma^x_i),

where H_int contains disordered Ising couplings and longitudinal fields. For epsilon = 0, each pulse perfectly flips every spin; eigenstates are cat superpositions (|s> +/- |-s>)/sqrt(2) with quasienergies split by exactly pi/T. For small nonzero epsilon, many-body localization protects the pairing: local perturbations cannot mix the cat states because doing so requires rearranging an extensive number of l-bits, and the subharmonic response at period 2T is *rigid* — its frequency is locked to exactly half the drive frequency, independent of epsilon, up to a critical perturbation strength [3][4].

### 4.4 Prethermal Time Crystals and the Role of Heating

Not all time crystals require many-body localization. Else, Bauer, and Nayak showed that an emergent symmetry of a prethermal effective Hamiltonian can stabilize time-crystalline order for exponentially long times [4]. The 2017 trapped-ion experiment realized precisely this prethermal variant: with long-range interactions, true MBL is not expected, yet period-doubled magnetization oscillations persisted for ~100 driving periods, with a variance peak in the subharmonic response marking the phase boundary [9]. A prethermal time crystal is thus a finite-lifetime phenomenon with lifetime exponential in a control parameter — operationally identical to the MBL variant on experimental timescales.

### 4.5 Micromotion: The Gauge Structure of Driven Dynamics

Beyond stroboscopic times, the full dynamics U(t,0) = P(t) exp(-i H_F t) involves the micromotion operator P(t). Bukov, D'Alessio, and Polkovnikov emphasized that non-stroboscopic observables require *dressed* operators O_dressed = P^dagger(t) O P(t), computable perturbatively in T [7]; neglecting micromotion gives quantitatively — sometimes qualitatively — wrong predictions for time-averaged currents and heating rates. The Floquet gauge structure relates the stroboscopic Hamiltonian to the van Vleck one by the kick operator: H_F[t_0] = exp(-S(t_0)) H_eff exp(S(t_0)).

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Benchmarks

1. **Trapped ions, 2017 (Zhang et al., Nature):** 10 ytterbium-171 ions with programmable long-range Ising interactions, driven by three-step Floquet sequences (imperfect global pi-pulse, disordered Ising evolution, disordered longitudinal fields), showed period-doubled magnetization oscillations persisting ~100 periods, with the subharmonic Fourier peak sharpening at the transition [9].
2. **Nitrogen-vacancy centers, 2017 (Choi et al., Nature):** ~10^6 NV centers in diamond with dipolar interactions and disorder showed rigid subharmonic response consistent with a *prethermal* discrete time crystal stabilized by high-frequency driving rather than MBL [9].
3. **Google Sycamore, 2021–2022 (Mi et al., Nature):** A 20-qubit superconducting processor implemented the kicked-Ising Floquet unitary with site-resolved disorder; eigenstate-resolved measurements demonstrated MBL time-crystalline order with pi/T spectral pairing on a programmable quantum processor [10].
4. **Photonic waveguides (Rechtsman group):** Floquet topological insulators and the anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator, with direct imaging of chiral edge transport [8].
5. **Ultracold atoms (Eckardt review):** Shaken optical lattices realizing the Harper-Hofstadter Hamiltonian, Floquet-Bose-Hubbard physics, and dynamical localization — the cleanest testbed for high-frequency expansion predictions [7].

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: Exponential Heating Bound

We sketch the Abanin et al. argument [6]. For H(t) = H_0 + V(t) with V T-periodic, local, and bounded, seek a time-periodic rotation Q(t) = exp(S(t)) such that the rotated Hamiltonian has vanishing low-order Fourier harmonics up to order n_*. Each order suppresses the residual drive by ~J/omega while increasing operator range linearly; optimizing n_* ~ omega/J yields a residual drive ~ exp(-const * omega/J). Energy absorption is driven by the residual, so tau_* >= exp(const * omega/J).

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Rigidity of the pi/T Pairing

In the MBL time crystal, write the Floquet unitary in the l-bit basis as U_F = U_0 V, where U_0 = prod_i sigma^x_i is the perfect pi-pulse and V = exp(-i D) with D quasi-local in the l-bits tau^z_i. The eigenstates of U_0 are cat states |{tau}> +/- |{-tau}> with quasienergies split by exactly pi/T. Matrix elements of D between the two cat partners are exponentially small in system size, while intra-sector elements merely renormalize quasienergies — so perturbation theory preserves the pi/T splitting up to exponentially small corrections: the *rigidity* of the time crystal [3].

```rust
// Conceptual: detecting pi/T spectral pairing in quasienergies
fn spectral_pairing(quasienergies: &[f64], period: f64) -> f64 {
    let target = std::f64::consts::PI / period;
    // fraction of quasienergies with a partner at distance ~= pi/T
    quasienergies.iter()
        .filter(|&&e| quasienergies.iter()
            .any(|&f| ((e - f).abs() - target).abs() < 1e-8))
        .count() as f64 / quasienergies.len() as f64
    // -> 1.0 deep in the DTC phase, -> 0.0 in the trivial phase
}
```

## 6. Limitations

The theory faces significant limitations. First, the Floquet-Magnus expansion *diverges* for generic many-body systems: it is asymptotic, and only its optimal truncation carries physical meaning [1][6]. Second, MBL stability in the thermodynamic limit — the foundation of the eternal time crystal — remains unresolved; rare thermal regions (avalanches) may destabilize MBL above one dimension, reducing the MBL time crystal to a long-lived prethermal phenomenon [6]. Third, real experiments suffer drive imperfections, environmental coupling, and finite coherence times; the Sycamore realization probed eigenstate order within a finite coherence window rather than infinite-time rigidity [10]. Fourth, Floquet topological invariants characterize the evolution operator, not a Hamiltonian, demanding interferometric or tomographic measurement protocols far more involved than equilibrium spectroscopy [8]. Fifth, the classification of interacting Floquet symmetry-protected phases is complete only for certain symmetry groups and dimensions. Finally, engineering driven fractionalized phases — fractional Chern insulators, spin liquids — remains aspirational, as heating competes directly with the small gaps of topological order.

## 7. Conclusion

Periodically driven quantum matter has matured from a curiosity of differential equations into a central frontier of condensed matter physics. The Floquet-Magnus and van Vleck expansions provide the computational language; the prethermalization theorem guarantees this language describes long-lived reality; Floquet topological phases — culminating in the anomalous Floquet-Anderson insulator, with no static analogue — prove that driving creates genuinely new orders of matter; and discrete time crystals show that even time itself, reduced to a discrete symmetry by the drive, can be spontaneously broken with experimental rigidity. The arc from Wilczek's 2012 proposal through the 2016 breakthroughs to the trapped-ion, NV-center, and superconducting-processor realizations of 2017–2022 is among the fastest theory-to-experiment cycles in modern physics [2][3][4][9][10].

Open problems abound: the fate of MBL in higher dimensions, the classification of interacting driven topological phases, driven fractionalized phases, and prethermalization in open Floquet systems. The central lesson stands: *time dependence, when made periodic, is not a complication to be removed but a resource to be engineered.*
