# Derived Categories as Triangulated Worlds: Beilinson Resolutions, Tilting Equivalences, and Bridgeland Stability Conditions

## Abstract

The derived category of an abelian category was introduced to repair the failure of exactness in homological algebra, but it has since become an object of independent geometric and representation-theoretic interest. This article develops three pillars of the modern theory: Beilinson's resolution of the diagonal, which exhibits the bounded derived category of projective space as generated by a full strong exceptional collection of line bundles and yields a computable spectral sequence for every coherent sheaf; tilting theory and Rickard's derived Morita theorem, which characterize when two algebras or varieties share equivalent derived categories and reduce derived equivalence to the existence of a tilting complex; and Bridgeland's stability conditions, which equip a triangulated category with a complex manifold of slicings and central charges, complete with walls, chambers, and Harder–Narasimhan filtrations. We give precise statements with proofs of the Beilinson spectral sequence, the derived Morita equivalence, and Bridgeland's deformation theorem, situate them within Fourier–Mukai theory and Orlov's singularity categories, and close with the principal open problems, including the existence of stability conditions on Calabi–Yau threefolds.

##1 Introduction

Homological algebra begins with a discomfort: global sections, tensor products, and Hom are only half exact. Verdier's derived category repairs this by inverting quasi-isomorphisms, making derived functors honest functors. What began as bookkeeping became a profound invariant: smooth projective varieties can share equivalent derived categories of coherent sheaves without being isomorphic — Mukai's Fourier-Mukai transform on abelian varieties was the first shock — and classifying *derived equivalences* is now central to algebraic geometry and representation theory [4].

The axiomatic setting is the **triangulated category**: an additive category with a suspension autoequivalence and distinguished exact triangles satisfying Verdier's axioms. Triangulated categories are famously defective — cones are not functorial — and the modern response regards them as shadows of dg or stable infinity-categorical enhancements. Yet the shadow retains enormous structure, and three results form its load-bearing walls.

First, **Beilinson's resolution of the diagonal** [1][7]: on projective space, the structure sheaf of the diagonal in the product admits a finite resolution whose terms are exterior tensor products of the sheaves in a full strong exceptional collection. The immediate corollary is that every coherent sheaf on projective space is canonically built from twists of the structure sheaf, via an explicit spectral sequence. Second, **tilting theory and derived Morita theory**: Happel observed that classical tilting modules induce equivalences of derived categories, and Rickard proved the definitive criterion — two rings have equivalent derived categories if and only if one is the endomorphism ring of a tilting complex over the other [3]. Third, **Bridgeland stability conditions** [2]: a stability condition on a triangulated category is a slicing into semistable pieces together with a central charge, and the space of all such conditions carries a natural complex-manifold structure, partitioned into chambers by walls where the Harder–Narasimhan filtration jumps.

This article is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the triangulated axioms and the construction of derived categories. Section 3 describes our methodological stance: generation by exceptional collections, geometrization of functors via Fourier–Mukai kernels, transfer via tilting complexes, and parametrization of hearts via stability. Section 4 develops the three pillars in depth, and Section 5 states and proves the three central theorems. Section 6 discusses limitations and open problems.

##2 Background

### Triangulated categories

A **triangulated category** $\mathcal{T}$ is an additive category equipped with an autoequivalence $[1]\colon \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{T}$, the *shift* or *suspension*, and a collection of **distinguished triangles**

$$X \xrightarrow{u} Y \xrightarrow{v} Z \xrightarrow{w} X[1]$$

satisfying Verdier's axioms TR1–TR4 [2]:

- **TR1.** Every morphism fits into a distinguished triangle; triangles isomorphic to distinguished ones are distinguished.
- **TR2 (rotation).** $X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]$ is distinguished iff its rotation $Y \to Z \to X[1] \to Y[1]$ is.
- **TR3.** Morphisms between the first two terms of distinguished triangles extend to morphisms of triangles.
- **TR4 (octahedron).** For composable $X \to Y \to Z$, the three cones fit into a distinguished triangle.

The octahedral axiom is the triangulated analogue of the third isomorphism theorem; applying a cohomological functor to a distinguished triangle yields a long exact sequence.

### Derived categories

For an abelian category $\mathcal{A}$, the **derived category** $D(\mathcal{A})$ is obtained from the homotopy category $K(\mathcal{A})$ by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms: morphisms are roofs $X \xleftarrow[\sim]{s} Z \xrightarrow{f} Y$, the shift is degree shift, and distinguished triangles are mapping-cone triangles. The bounded variants $D^b(\mathcal{A})$, $D^+$, $D^-$ restrict the cohomological amplitude. For a scheme $X$ we write $D^b(X) = D^b(\mathrm{coh}(X))$ for the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves.

Two structural facts govern everything that follows. First, the **Grothendieck group** $K_0(\mathcal{T})$ is generated by objects modulo $[Y] = [X] + [Z]$ for every distinguished triangle, with $[X[1]] = -[X]$. Second, when $\mathcal{T}$ is Hom-finite over a field, the **Euler form** $\chi(E, F) = \sum_i (-1)^i \dim_k \mathrm{Hom}(E, F[i])$ descends to $K_0(\mathcal{T})$, categorifying intersection theory.

### Enhancements and the cone problem

The cone in a triangulated category is not functorial, so the standard remedy is an **enhancement** — a dg category or stable infinity-category with $\mathcal{T}$ as homotopy category — in which cones are functorial up to coherent homotopy. We work at the triangulated level but flag arguments that secretly use an enhancement.

| Feature | Abelian category | Triangulated category | dg enhancement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact sequences | Short exact sequences | Distinguished triangles | Cofiber sequences |
| Cone | Cokernel, functorial | Non-functorial | Functorial up to homotopy |
| $K_0$ relations | $[B]=[A]+[C]$ for $0\to A\to B\to C\to 0$ | $[Y]=[X]+[Z]$ for triangles | Same as triangulated |
| Derived functors | Need resolutions | Built in | Built in |

##3 Methodology

Because $D^b(\mathbb{P}^n)$ has a proper class of objects, the working method is *indirect*: control the category through generators and a functor-geometry dictionary.

1. **Generation by exceptional collections.** An object $E$ is *exceptional* if $\mathrm{Hom}(E, E[i]) = 0$ for $i \neq 0$ and $\mathrm{End}(E)$ is a division ring; a sequence is *exceptional* if additionally $\mathrm{Hom}(E_j, E_i[k]) = 0$ for $j > i$, *strong* if $\mathrm{Hom}(E_i, E_j[k]) = 0$ for $k \neq 0$, and *full* if it generates the whole category. A full strong exceptional collection linearizes the category into modules over a finite-dimensional algebra.
2. **Fourier-Mukai kernels.** Every natural exact functor $D^b(X) \to D^b(Y)$ is a transform $\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(-) = R\pi_{Y*}(\mathcal{E} \otimes^L L\pi_X^*(-))$ for a kernel $\mathcal{E} \in D^b(X \times Y)$; composition is convolution of kernels, and $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ is the kernel of the identity. Resolving $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ resolves the identity functor itself [1].
3. **Tilting transfer.** A tilting complex $T$ over $A$ packages an equivalence $D^b(A) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{End}(T))$; a tilting bundle on $X$ exhibits $D^b(X)$ as modules over its endomorphism algebra [3][6].
4. **Parametrized hearts.** Bridgeland's formalism treats the *space* of hearts (via slicings) as a geometric object, with wall-crossing controlling moduli of stable objects [2].

> **Theorem (Beilinson's lemma, generation criterion).** *Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a triangulated category and $E_1, \dots, E_n$ an exceptional collection. If the right orthogonal $ \langle E_1, \dots, E_n \rangle^\perp $ vanishes, then the collection is full.*

*Proof.* The generated subcategory is right admissible, giving a semi-orthogonal decomposition $\mathcal{T} = \langle \langle E_i\rangle^\perp, \langle E_i\rangle \rangle$; vanishing of the orthogonal kills the first factor. ∎

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The triangulated world and its defects

Before celebrating the three pillars, one must understand what triangulated categories *cannot* do, because each pillar is partly a workaround. The non-functoriality of the cone means that a morphism of triangles extending a commutative square is not unique, so diagram chases that are routine in abelian categories require care. A sharper defect: the cone construction does not preserve quasi-isomorphisms of the input data in any canonical way, which is why derived functors must be constructed via resolutions (K-injective or K-projective) rather than directly. Verdier's original construction of $D(\mathcal{A})$ as a localization of $K(\mathcal{A})$ already exhibits the pattern that dominates the subject: *pass to a larger, better-behaved category of complexes, then quotient back down*.

The rotation axiom TR2 deserves emphasis because it is the engine of homological computation. From a single distinguished triangle one extracts, by rotation, the long exact sequence of a cohomological functor, and the octahedral axiom TR4 guarantees that the standard filtration arguments (spectral sequences of filtered complexes, Postnikov towers) go through. In code, the rotation can be rendered as pure data:

```haskell
-- A distinguished triangle as data; rotation is an involution up to sign.
data Triangle obj mor = Triangle
  { src :: obj, mid :: obj, cone :: obj
  , f   :: mor            -- src -> mid
  , g   :: mor            -- mid -> cone
  , h   :: mor            -- cone -> src[1]
  }

rotate :: Triangle o m -> Triangle o m   -- TR2: (f,g,h) |-> (g,h,-f[1])
rotate (Triangle x y z f g h) = Triangle y z (Shift x) g h (negShift f)
```

Orlov's **triangulated category of singularities** $D_{\mathrm{sg}}(X) = D^b(\mathrm{coh}(X)) / \mathrm{Perf}(X)$ shows how Verdier quotients create new worlds: it vanishes for smooth $X$ and measures the failure of perfectness for singular $X$, identified by Orlov with matrix factorization categories from Landau-Ginzburg models [5].

### 4.2 Beilinson's resolution of the diagonal

Let $\mathbb{P}^n$ be projective $n$-space over a field $k$, with projections $p_1, p_2\colon \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^n$ and diagonal $\Delta \subset \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^n$. Beilinson's fundamental insight [7] is that $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ admits a finite resolution by sheaves pulled back from the two factors:

$$0 \longrightarrow \Omega^n(n) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}(-n) \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow \Omega^1(1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}(-1) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O} \boxtimes \mathcal{O} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_\Delta \longrightarrow 0,$$

where $\Omega^p$ is the sheaf of Kähler $p$-forms. Exactness is a Koszul computation: locally the diagonal is cut out by a regular sequence [1].

Two consequences follow. First, applying the Fourier-Mukai transform with this kernel yields the **Beilinson spectral sequence** (Theorem 1 below), reconstructing every sheaf from its cohomology against the bundles $\Omega^p(p)$. Second, the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(-n), \dots, \mathcal{O}$ form a **full strong exceptional collection**, giving an exact equivalence $D^b(\mathbb{P}^n) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}A)$ with $A = \mathrm{End}(\bigoplus_{i=0}^n \mathcal{O}(-i))$, a finite-dimensional algebra of global dimension $n$ described by a quiver with relations. The Euler form on this collection is upper-triangular with ones on the diagonal, a numerical shadow of exceptionality:

```python
from math import comb
import numpy as np

def euler_matrix_Pn(n):
    """chi(O(-i), O(-j)) on P^n: upper-triangular, 1s on diagonal."""
    M = np.zeros((n + 1, n + 1), dtype=int)
    for i in range(n + 1):
        for j in range(n + 1):
            if j >= i:
                M[i, j] = comb(n + (j - i), n)  # h^0(P^n, O(j-i))
    return M

print(euler_matrix_Pn(3))
# [[ 1  4 10 20]
#  [ 0  1  4 10]
#  [ 0  0  1  4]
#  [ 0  0  0  1]]
```

The strictly upper-triangular part records the nontrivial extensions the quiver relations must kill. Canonaco and Karp extended this to weighted projective stacks, producing derived autoequivalences of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces [1]. The moral: *a resolution of the diagonal is a presentation of the identity functor, hence of the entire category*.

### 4.3 Tilting complexes and derived Morita theory

Classical Morita theory says that two rings have equivalent module categories iff one is the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated projective generator over the other. **Derived Morita theory** asks the same question for derived categories, and the answer, due to Rickard, is both more flexible and more subtle: projective generators are replaced by **tilting complexes** [3].

> **Definition.** *A complex $T \in K^b(\mathrm{proj}(A))$ over a ring $A$ is a **tilting complex** if (i) $\mathrm{Hom}_{D(A)}(T, T[i]) = 0$ for all $i \neq 0$, and (ii) $T$ generates $K^b(\mathrm{proj}(A))$ as a triangulated category, i.e. the smallest thick subcategory containing $T$ is the category of perfect complexes.*

Condition (i) says $T$ is *self-orthogonal*, a homological rigidity; condition (ii) says it is *large enough* to see everything perfect. Rickard's theorem [3] states that for rings $A, B$ the following are equivalent: (a) there is a triangle equivalence $D^b(A) \simeq D^b(B)$; (b) there is a two-sided tilting complex ${}_A M_B$ with $-\otimes_A^L M$ an equivalence; (c) $B \cong \mathrm{End}_{D(A)}(T)$ for a tilting complex $T$ over $A$. In particular, derived equivalence is detected by a *single object* — a striking compression of information.

Geometrically, a **tilting bundle** $\mathcal{T}$ on $X$ ($\mathrm{Ext}^i(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}) = 0$ for $i > 0$, generating $D^b(X)$) gives $D^b(X) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{T}))$ via $R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{T}, -)$. The Beilinson collection $\bigoplus_{i=0}^n \mathcal{O}(-i)$ is itself a tilting bundle on $\mathbb{P}^n$: the resolution of the diagonal *produces* the tilting object, and the tilting object *encodes* the equivalence. Happel's theorem — a tilting module of projective dimension at most one induces $D^b(A) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{End}_A(T))$ — is the two-term case, and the Brenner-Butler theorem describes how module categories tilt inside the equivalence [6].

Derived equivalence is strictly coarser than Morita equivalence, yet preserves Hochschild cohomology, algebraic K-theory, and the center; the derived Picard group becomes the symmetry group of the noncommutative space.

### 4.4 Fourier–Mukai transforms: functors as geometry

Mukai's equivalence $D^b(A) \simeq D^b(\hat{A})$ between an abelian variety and its dual revealed that derived categories remember *less* than varieties but in a structured way. Orlov's representability theorem asserts that every fully faithful exact functor $D^b(X) \to D^b(Y)$ between smooth projective varieties is a Fourier-Mukai transform for a unique kernel, with composition given by convolution of kernels and $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ as the two-sided unit. Beilinson's resolution thus computes the unit of this monoidal structure explicitly, a fact exploited in [1] to build derived autoequivalences from spherical twists and line-bundle twists.

$$\Phi_{\mathcal{F}} \circ \Phi_{\mathcal{E}} \;\cong\; \Phi_{\mathcal{F} * \mathcal{E}}, \qquad \mathcal{F} * \mathcal{E} = R\pi_{13*}(\pi_{12}^*\mathcal{E} \otimes^L \pi_{23}^*\mathcal{F}),$$

and $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ is the two-sided unit for convolution. Beilinson's resolution thus computes the unit of this monoidal structure explicitly — a fact exploited in [1] to construct derived autoequivalences as compositions of spherical twists and line-bundle twists. The group of autoequivalences generated this way acts on $D^b(X)$ the way the mapping class group acts on a surface: faithfully enough to be interesting, mysteriously enough to remain largely unknown.

### 4.5 Bridgeland stability conditions

Fix a triangulated category $\mathcal{T}$. A **stability condition** $\sigma = (Z, \mathcal{P})$ consists of a group homomorphism $Z\colon K_0(\mathcal{T}) \to \mathbb{C}$, the *central charge*, and a **slicing** $\mathcal{P}$: full additive subcategories $\mathcal{P}(\phi) \subset \mathcal{T}$ for each phase $\phi \in \mathbb{R}$, such that [2]:

1. If $0 \neq E \in \mathcal{P}(\phi)$ then $Z(E) = m(E)\, e^{i\pi\phi}$ for some mass $m(E) > 0$ — semistable objects of phase $\phi$ map into the corresponding ray;
2. $\mathcal{P}(\phi + 1) = \mathcal{P}(\phi)[1]$ — phases are periodic under shift;
3. If $\phi_1 > \phi_2$ and $A_i \in \mathcal{P}(\phi_i)$ then $\mathrm{Hom}(A_1, A_2) = 0$ — no maps go backward in phase;
4. Every nonzero $E \in \mathcal{T}$ admits a **Harder–Narasimhan filtration**: a tower of distinguished triangles with factors $A_i \in \mathcal{P}(\phi_i)$ and strictly decreasing phases $\phi_1 > \cdots > \phi_n$.


```haskell
-- A slicing: phases index additive subcategories; HN filtrations decompose objects.
type Phase = Double
slicing :: Phase -> [Object]              -- P(phi): semistables of phase phi
centralCharge :: Object -> Complex Double -- Z(E) = m(E) * exp(i*pi*phi)
hnFiltration :: Object -> [(Phase, Object)] -- decreasing phases
```

Equivalently, a stability condition is a bounded t-structure (a heart $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{P}((0,1])$) plus a stability function $Z$ on $\mathcal{A}$ with the Harder-Narasimhan property.

Bridgeland's deformation theorem [2] is the centerpiece (proved as Theorem 3 below): $\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{T})$ is stratified by **walls**, real-codimension-one loci where two stable objects acquire equal phase. Crossing a wall, the HN filtration jumps — this is *wall-crossing*, governed by wall-crossing formulas (Kontsevich–Soibelman, Joyce). The group $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{T})$ acts on $\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{T})$ by isometries, and the quotient parametrizes noncommutative complex structures on $\mathcal{T}$.

Two examples anchor the theory. For a smooth projective curve, classical slope stability assembles with the standard heart into a stability condition, and $\mathrm{Stab}(D^b(C))$ is completely understood [2]. For K3 surfaces, Bridgeland constructed a distinguished component $\mathrm{Stab}^\dagger$ of dimension equal to the rank of the algebraic Mukai lattice, later shown simply connected by Bayer-Macri with its boundary described by wall-crossing.

##5 Empirical Results / Proofs

We now state the three load-bearing theorems with proofs or proof sketches at the level of the research literature.

> **Theorem 1 (Beilinson spectral sequence).** *Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^n$. There is a spectral sequence with*
> $$E_1^{p,q} = H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{F} \otimes \Omega^{-p}(-p)) \otimes \mathcal{O}(p) \implies \mathcal{F},$$
> *concentrated in the range $-n \le p \le 0$, converging to $\mathcal{F}$ in degree zero.*

*Proof.* Resolve $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$ by the Beilinson complex $B^\bullet$; then $\Phi_{B^\bullet}(\mathcal{F}) \simeq \mathcal{F}$ since $B^\bullet \simeq \mathcal{O}_\Delta$. Computing $\Phi_{B^{-p}}(\mathcal{F}) = R\Gamma(\mathcal{F} \otimes \Omega^p(p)) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-p)$ and re-indexing gives the $E_1$ page; the stupid filtration of $B^\bullet$ gives convergence, and finiteness bounds the $p$-range. ∎

**Corollary.** *$\mathcal{O}(-n), \dots, \mathcal{O}$ is a full strong exceptional collection in $D^b(\mathbb{P}^n)$, and $D^b(\mathbb{P}^n) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}A)$ for $A = \mathrm{End}(\bigoplus_{i=0}^n \mathcal{O}(-i))$.*

> **Theorem 2 (Rickard's derived Morita theorem).** *Let $A, B$ be rings. The following are equivalent: (i) there exists a triangle equivalence $D^b(A) \simeq D^b(B)$; (ii) there exists a two-sided tilting complex ${}_A M_B$ such that $-\otimes_A^L M$ is an equivalence; (iii) $B \cong \mathrm{End}_{D^b(A)}(T)$ for some tilting complex $T \in K^b(\mathrm{proj}(A))$.*

*Proof sketch.* (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (ii): self-orthogonality makes the derived endomorphism dg-algebra $\mathbf{R}\mathrm{End}_A(T)$ formal, hence quasi-isomorphic to the ordinary algebra $B$; Keller's lifting promotes $T$ to a two-sided tilting complex [4]. (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) is immediate. (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii): an equivalence $F\colon D^b(A) \to D^b(B)$ sends the canonical tilting complex $A$ to a tilting complex $T = F(A)$; the generation hypothesis forces the quasi-inverse to be of tensor type. ∎

> **Theorem 3 (Bridgeland's deformation theorem).** *Let $\mathcal{T}$ be triangulated of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$ with a fixed lattice quotient $v\colon K_0(\mathcal{T}) \to \Lambda$. Then $\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{T})$ is a complex manifold, and the forgetful map $\mathcal{Z}\colon \mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{T}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda, \mathbb{C})$, $\sigma \mapsto Z$, is a local homeomorphism onto its image on each connected component.*

*Proof sketch.* Fix $\sigma = (Z, \mathcal{P})$ with the support property. For $Z'$ close to $Z$, define $\mathcal{P}'$ from semistables of nearby $Z'$-phase; the support property uniformly bounds destabilizing masses, so HN filtrations persist for $\|Z' - Z\|$ small, giving a continuous local section of $\mathcal{Z}$. Uniqueness of HN filtrations gives local injectivity. ∎


##6 Limitations


1. **The enhancement problem.** The cone is not functorial, and Verdier quotients can collapse information; arguments invoking "the" cone implicitly choose enhancements. For schemes the canonical dg enhancement exists, but some triangulated categories admit none.
2. **Constructing tilting complexes is hard.** Rickard's theorem is a *characterization*, not a construction: finding all tilting complexes with prescribed endomorphism ring is wild, and mutation of tilting complexes via silting theory [6] is the active frontier.
3. **Stability conditions need not exist.** For a general smooth projective threefold, existence of *any* stability condition on $D^b(X)$ is unknown; the conjectural Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality for tilt-stable objects (BMT conjecture, now theorems in several cases) is the precise obstruction.
4. **Derived categories do not determine varieties.** Mukai's equivalence shows non-isomorphic varieties share derived categories; Bondal-Orlov reconstruction recovers varieties with ample canonical bundle, but Fano and Calabi-Yau varieties admit nontrivial derived partners.
5. **Computability.** The Beilinson spectral sequence is explicit, but for a general $X$ without a full exceptional collection there is no comparable algorithm, and wall-crossing formulas, while theoretically complete, are combinatorially explosive.

##7 Conclusion

Derived categories began as a technical fix for non-exact functors and matured into a landscape with its own geography. Beilinson's resolution presents the identity functor finitely and explicitly; Rickard's derived Morita theorem detects derived equivalence by single rigid objects; Bridgeland's stability conditions reveal a complex manifold of abelian hearts stratified by walls. The outstanding challenges — stability on threefolds, classification of tilting complexes, the reconstruction problem — ensure the subject stays active for decades.
