# Liquid Neural Networks and Closed-Form Continuous-Time Dynamics: From LTC to CfC for Adaptive Edge Control

## Abstract
This thesis presents a rigorous analysis of liquid neural networks from liquid time-constant networks (LTCs) to closed-form continuous-time networks (CfCs) for adaptive control at the edge. We formalize LTC dynamics as input-dependent linear time-varying systems with bounded hidden states, derive stability conditions via Lyapunov arguments, and show how numerical ODE solvers bottleneck deployment. We then derive the CfC approximation as a tightly bounded closed-form gating mechanism that replaces iterative solvers with an explicit interpolation between zero and infinite horizon behaviors, achieving 1-5 orders of magnitude speedup while preserving expressivity. We contextualize both within Neural Circuit Policies (NCPs) inspired by C. elegans wiring, evaluate on time-series, robotics, and flight tasks, and provide deployment guidance for neuromorphic and microcontroller targets. Contributions include unified notation, proof sketches, complexity analysis, and failure modes relevant to real-time edge adaptation.

## 1 Intro

*Liquid neural networks* reimagine recurrent computation through the lens of *time-continuous dynamical systems* whose **time constants are themselves state- and input-dependent**. Introduced by Hasani et al. [1][2], Liquid Time-Constant Networks (LTCs) draw inspiration from the synaptic transmission dynamics of small nervous systems, most notably *C. elegans*, where membrane potentials evolve according to first-order linear dynamics modulated by nonlinear interlinked gates.

The central motivation is adaptivity. Classical discrete-time RNNs — LSTMs, GRUs — assume fixed discretization and lack principled handling of irregularly sampled, noisy, or intervened time-series. Continuous-time models, and Neural ODEs in particular [3], provide elegance but pay a steep cost: **forward passes require numerical differential equation solvers** and backward passes require adjoint methods or through-solver backprop, inflating latency and memory.

> Theorem: Bounded Liquid Dynamics — For LTC hidden states $x_i(t)$ evolving under $dx_i/dt = -(1/\tau_i + f_i(x,I,\theta)) x_i + f_i(x,I,\theta) A_i$ with $\tau_i>0$, $f_i \in [0,1]$, $A_i$ bounded, trajectories remain in $[\min(0,A_i^{min}), \max(0,A_i^{max})]$ for all $t \ge 0$ given $x_i(0)$ inside [1][2].

LTCs address this by **explicitly parameterizing varying time-constants** coupled to hidden state, yielding stable and bounded behavior, superior trajectory-length expressivity, and empirical gains on time-series prediction [1]. However, they remain ODE-solver dependent.

Closed-form Continuous-time Neural Networks (CfCs) [4][5] resolve this bottleneck by **approximating the true LTC solution in closed-form**, replacing solver iterations with a sigmoid-gated interpolation. Combined with Neural Circuit Policies (NCPs) [6], a four-layer sparsely recurrent wiring motif, LTCs and CfCs achieve robust out-of-distribution generalization for flight, driving, and medical tasks with as few as 19 liquid neurons.

This thesis unifies LTC→CfC evolution, provides a dense technical deep dive, and maps implications for **adaptive edge control** where latency <10ms, power <1W, and online adaptation are non-negotiable.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Continuous-Time Recurrent Models

Continuous-time RNNs (CT-RNNs) define hidden evolution as:

$$ \frac{dx(t)}{dt} = - \frac{x(t)}{\tau} + f(x(t), I(t), \theta) $$

where $\tau$ is time-constant, $I(t)$ input, $f$ nonlinearity. Funahashi and Nakamura [7] established universal approximation for continuous trajectories. Neural ODEs [3] generalize this: $dx/dt = f_\theta(x,t)$, solved by black-box ODE solvers.

Strengths: handling irregular sampling, adaptive computation depth, memory-efficient adjoint gradients. Weaknesses: **solver overhead**, stiffness, non-Lipschitz pathologies, poor deployment on edge hardware lacking floating-point ODE libraries.

### 2.2 Biological Motivation: Why Liquid?

*C. elegans* has 302 neurons, yet performs complex sensorimotor behaviors. Its synapses communicate via *graded potentials* and *electronic* coupling, not just spikes, leading to time-constants that depend on presynaptic activity [2][6]. Hasani et al. formalized this as:

- **Presynaptic stimulus-dependent conductance**
- **Reversal potentials $A_{ij}$** bounding postsynaptic influence
- **Sparsity** and **recurrency** reflecting connectomic priors

This yields **liquid time-constants**: $\tau_{sys} = 1/(1/\tau_i + \sum_j w_{ij} \sigma(x_j))$, varying instantaneously with network activity — unlike static $\tau$ in CT-RNNs.

### 2.3 Related Lineages

- **CT-RNNs, ODE-RNNs, GRU-D**: early continuous-time handling of missing data [3]
- **Neural Circuit Policies (NCPs)**: wiring LTC/CfC neurons into sensory $N_s$, inter $N_i$, command $N_c$, motor $N_m$ layers with 90% sparsity [6]
- **Liquid AI LFM**: industrial extension to foundation models, e.g., LFM2-450M VL running on Jetson Orin [8]

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a unified notation:

- $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$: hidden membrane potentials
- $I(t) \in \mathbb{R}^m$: time-varying input
- $\tau \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^D$: learnable base time-constants, parameterized as $\tau_i = \exp(\theta_{\tau,i})$
- $A \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: learnable bias/reversal matrix
- $f$: $D$-dim nonlinearity, typically $\sigma(W_{rec} x + W_{in} I + b)$

### 3.1 LTC Formalism

Per Hasani et al. [1][5], LTC cell:

$$ \frac{dx_i(t)}{dt} = -\left[\frac{1}{\tau_i} + f_i(x(t), I(t), \theta)\right] x_i(t) + f_i(x(t), I(t), \theta) A_i \tag{1} $$

where $f_i \in (0,1)$. This is a **bilinear time-varying system**. For fixed $f_i$, solution is:

$$ x_i(t) = (x_i(0)-A_i) e^{- (1/\tau_i + f_i) t} + A_i $$

With time-varying $f_i$, integral form:

$$ x_i(t) = x_i(0) e^{-\int_0^t (1/\tau_i+f_i(s))ds} + \int_0^t f_i(s) A_i e^{-\int_s^t (1/\tau_i+f_i(v))dv} ds $$

Implementation uses **fused semi-implicit Euler** with $L_{ode}=6$ internal steps [1]:

```python
def ltc_cell(x_prev, I_t, W_rec, W_in, b, tau, A, L=6):
    h = x_prev
    dt = 1.0 / L
    W_in_x = W_in @ I_t
    for _ in range(L):
        f = torch.sigmoid(W_rec @ h + W_in_x + b)  # (D,)
        # semi-implicit: h = (h + dt*f*A) / (1 + dt*(1/tau + f))
        h = (h + dt * f * A) / (1 + dt * (1/tau + f))
    return h
```

Properties proved in [1][2]:

- **Boundedness**: $x_i(t) \in [\min(0,A_i), \max(0,A_i)]$
- **Lipschitz**: LTC map is Lipschitz in $x$, $I$
- **Trajectory length** $\uparrow$ exponentially with depth, surpassing NODEs and CT-RNNs

### 3.2 From ODE to Closed-Form: CfC Derivation

Numerical integration cost is $O(L_{ode} \cdot D^2)$ per step, plus adaptive step overhead. Hasani et al. [4] observe LTC solution interpolates between two regimes:

- $t \to 0$: $x(t) \approx x(0)$
- $t \to \infty$: $x(t) \to A$ exponentially with rate $f$

They propose approximation:

$$ x(t) \approx \underbrace{\sigma(-f(x,I;\theta_f) t)}_{\text{gate}} \odot g(x,I;\theta_g) + \underbrace{[1-\sigma(-f t)]}_{1-\text{gate}} \odot h(x,I;\theta_h) \tag{2} $$

Interpreted as:

- $g$: $t=0$ branch (initial value network)
- $h$: $t=\infty$ branch (asymptotic value network)
- $f$: time-constant / gating network modulating decay, with shared backbone

**Three-headed architecture** [4][5]:

```rust
// Rust-like pseudocode for CfC
struct CfC {
    backbone: MLP, // shared embedding e = backbone([x;I])
    f_head: Linear, // time-modulator
    g_head: Linear, // zero-time
    h_head: Linear, // infinite-time
}
fn cfc_step(x: Tensor, i: Tensor, t_delta: f32) -> Tensor {
    let e = backbone.concat(x,i).tanh();
    let f = sigmoid(f_head(e)); // (D,)
    let g = g_head(e).tanh();
    let h = h_head(e).tanh();
    let gate = sigmoid(-f * t_delta); // soft exponential
    gate * g + (1.0 - gate) * h
}
```

Variants [4]:

- **CfC full**: as above with backbone
- **CfC-noGate**: drops $(1-\sigma)$ mixing
- **CfC-mmRNN**: mixes recurrent state with LSTM cell
- **Mixed CfC**: hybrid with LSTM gates to avoid vanishing gradients

The key theoretical contribution: **tight bound** $|x_{true}(t)-x_{CfC}(t)| \le C e^{-\lambda t}$ with explicit $C,\lambda$ depending on $\tau, f$ Lipschitz constants [4][5].

### 3.3 Neural Circuit Policies Wiring

NCP [6] stacks CfC/LTC neurons into 4 layers:

1. $N_s$ sensory (8-16 neurons, fully feedforward)
2. $N_i$ inter (e.g., 12-20, 50% recurrent)
3. $N_c$ command (e.g., 6-10, highly recurrent >80%)
4. $N_m$ motor (4-6, readout)

Connectivity sampled:

```haskell
-- Haskell pseudocode for NCP wiring sampler
sampleNCP :: Int -> Int -> Int -> Int -> Prob -> IO AdjMatrix
sampleNCP ns ni nc nm p = do
  -- binomial(n_target, p) per source
  s_i <- binomial ns ni p
  i_c <- binomial ni nc p
  c_c <- binomial nc nc 0.8  -- dense recurrent in command
  c_m <- binomial nc nm p
  ensureConnected [s_i,i_c,c_c,c_m]
```

Sparsity ~90% vs dense RNNs → **5-10× FLOPs reduction**, crucial for microcontrollers [8].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 4.1 Expressivity and Trajectory Length Analysis

Hasani et al. [1] quantify expressivity via **expected trajectory length** $\mathcal{L}(F)$ after $k$ layers/steps. For LTC:

$$ \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{LTC}] \ge \alpha \cdot (\sigma_w \sqrt{k})^{L} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{prev}] $$

where $\sigma_w$ is weight variance, $L$ depth, $\alpha$ depends on $f$ gating. Empirically, LTC trajectory length grows **exponentially faster** than NODEs and CT-RNNs, implying more distinct input-output mappings with same parameter count [1].

> Theorem: Universal Approximation for LTC — Any $C^1$ dynamical system $dx/dt = F(x)$ on compact $I\times U$ can be approximated arbitrarily closely by an LTC network with $n$ hidden units and $n$ outputs [2].

This hinges on showing LTC's hidden state can emulate $F$ via appropriate $W_{rec}, W_{in}, A$. Proof sketch: partition state space, assign LTC units to local linearizations, use gating $f$ to interpolate.

*CfC retains this*: since CfC approximates LTC solution uniformly with bound $\epsilon$, approximation error composes additively: $\|F - CfC\| \le \|F-LTC\| + \|LTC-CfC\| \le \epsilon_1+\epsilon_2$ [4].

Practical consequence: **19-neuron NCP CfC** successfully learns lane-keeping from vision, where 100k-param LSTM fails OOD [6].

### 4.2 4.2 Stability, Boundedness, and Adaptive Time-Constants

Boundedness is non-negotiable for edge safety. From (1), since $f_i \ge 0$:

$$ \frac{d}{dt} (x_i - A_i)^2 \le -2(1/\tau_i) (x_i-A_i)^2 $$

Thus $A_i$ is globally asymptotically stable equilibrium for frozen $f_i$. With varying $f_i$, Lyapunov $V=\frac12 \sum_i (x_i-A_i)^2$ yields $\dot V \le 0$ outside compact set.

| Property | LTC | CfC | LSTM |
|----------|-----|-----|------|
| Hidden bound | $[\min(0,A),\max(0,A)]$ provable | Same, via gate $\in[0,1]$ | $[-1,1]$ via tanh but cell unbounded |
| Time-constant | Input-dependent $1/(1/\tau+f)$ | Implicit via $\sigma(-f t)$ | Fixed discrete step |
| Solver | $L_{ode}$ steps | None, closed-form | None |
| FLOPs/step | $O(L_{ode} D^2)$ | $O(D^2)$ | $O(4 D^2)$ |
| Lipschitz | Controlled by $\tau$ | Controlled by $f,g,h$ Lipschitz | Can explode |

*Italic insight*: *liquid time-constants provide intrinsic timescale adaptation* — fast $f \approx 1$ → small effective $\tau_{sys}$, responsive to transients; slow $f \approx 0$ → large $\tau_{sys}$, long memory without explicit gates.

For edge control, this means **no manual timescale tuning** for e.g., quadrotor gust rejection vs hovering drift.

### 4.3 4.3 Closed-Form Gating: Deriving Equation (2)

We derive (2) rigorously. Start with integral solution of LTC (1). Let $w(t)=1/\tau + f(t)$. True solution:

$$ x(t)=e^{-\int_0^t w(s)ds} x_0 + \int_0^t e^{-\int_s^t w(v)dv} f(s) A ds $$

Hasani et al. [4] bound integral by replacing $f(t)$ with learned time-modulator and bounding exponential via sigmoid. Key lemma:

$$ e^{-t f} \approx \sigma(-f t) + \delta(t), \quad |\delta| \le 0.1 \ \forall t,f $$

Empirically tight for $f \in [0,10]$, $t \in [0,1]$ after normalization.

Thus:

$$ x(t) \approx \sigma(-f t) \odot x_0 + (1-\sigma(-f t)) \odot A_{eff} $$

Replace $x_0$ and $A_{eff}$ with learned functions $g(x,I)$ and $h(x,I)$ to increase capacity beyond scalar $A$.

TLA+ spec for CfC gate safety (no solver divergence):

```tla
---- MODULE CfC ----
EXTENDS Reals
VARIABLES x, t, f, g, h
CfCNext == 
  /\ f \in 0..10
  /\ t \in 0..1
  /\ LET gate == 1 / (1 + Exp(f*t)) IN
     x' = gate * g + (1 - gate) * h
  /\ t' = t + 0.01
  /\ UNCHANGED <<f,g,h>>
Safety == [](x \in -1..1)
====
```

This spec checks boundedness irrespective of solver.

### 4.4 4.4 Edge Deployment: Quantization, Neuromorphic, Real-Time

Deployment targets: Cortex-M4/M7 (256KB RAM), Jetson Orin Nano, Intel Loihi-2 [8][9]. Considerations:

- **Quantization**: CfC $f,g,h$ heads are MLPs with tanh/sigmoid; 8-bit post-training quantization yields <2% accuracy drop on PhysioNet Sepsis [4] due to bounded activations.
- **Irregular sampling**: Edge sensors (IMU 200Hz, GPS 1Hz, camera 30Hz) produce non-uniform $\Delta t$. CfC naturally consumes $\Delta t$ as input to gate: $\text{gate}=\sigma(-f \cdot \Delta t)$. LTC would require re-solving ODE per $\Delta t$; CfC is O(1) [4].
- **Online adaptation**: Liquid networks exhibit **few-shot adaptation** — NCPs trained on summer driving adapt to winter without retraining due to input-dependent $\tau$ [6].

Benchmark table (from [4][5][6][9]):

| Task | Model | Params | Solver steps | Latency (Jetson) | OOD AUC |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|------------------|---------|
| PhysioNet Mortality | LSTM | 120k | — | 3.2ms | 0.82 |
| | LTC | 45k | 6 | 12.1ms | 0.85 |
| | CfC | 45k | 0 | **1.8ms** | **0.87** |
| Drone navigation | LSTM | 200k | — | 8.5ms | fail |
| | NCP-LTC 19n | 6k | 6 | 9.2ms | 0.71 |
| | NCP-CfC 19n | 6k | 0 | **2.1ms** | **0.88** |
| Walker2d | ODE-RNN | 80k | adaptive ~20 | 22ms | 0.65 |
| | CfC-mixed | 80k | 0 | **3.5ms** | 0.78 |

> Theorem: CfC Speedup — For $L_{ode}$ fused steps, CfC inference is at least $L_{ode}$× faster in FLOPs and eliminates $O(L_{ode})$ sequential dependencies, enabling parallel scan [4].

Memory: NCP 90% sparse → weight storage $\propto 0.1 D^2$ vs dense $D^2$; fits in 64KB SRAM for 32-unit CfC.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Closed-Form Approximation Bound

Claim from [4] Theorem 2: Let $x(t)$ true LTC solution, $\tilde x(t)$ CfC (2). Then $\exists C_1,C_2>0$ s.t.

$$ \|x(t)-\tilde x(t)\| \le C_1 e^{-\mu t} + C_2 \sup_s \|f(s)-\tilde f(s)\| $$

*Proof sketch*:

1. Write error $e(t)=x(t)-\tilde x(t)$
2. Differentiate using LTC ODE and CfC ODE surrogate
3. Apply Grönwall inequality: $\|e(t)\| \le \int_0^t e^{-\int_s^t w} \|\Delta f\| ds$
4. Bound $e^{-\int}$ by $\sigma$ approximation lemma: $|e^{-a}-\sigma(-a)| \le 0.05$ for $a\in[0,5]$ (numerical)
5. Compose Lipschitz bounds of $g,h$ networks

Full proof in Appendix A of [4].

### 5.2 Empirical Validation Strategy

To replicate [1][4][6]:

1. **Irregular time-series**: PhysioNet 2012, 45k ICU stays, 37 variables, 80% missing. CfC AUC 0.87 vs LSTM 0.82 [4]
2. **Control**: Closed-loop lane keeping in VISTA simulator, NCP-CfC 19 neurons recovers from off-road, LSTM crashes [6]
3. **Edge**: Deploy ncps PyTorch model → ONNX → TensorRT on Jetson Orin, measure latency/power; CfC 2.1ms vs LTC 9.2ms meets 30Hz control loop

```python
from ncps.torch import CfC
from ncps.wirings import AutoNCP
import torch
# NCP wiring: 8 sensory, 12 inter, 6 command, 4 motor
wiring = AutoNCP(16, 4, sparsity_level=0.9)
model = CfC(input_size=20, units=wiring.units, wiring=wiring)
x = torch.randn(2, 50, 20)  # batch, seq, features
dt = torch.rand(2,50,1)*0.1 # irregular dt
y,_ = model(x)  # CfC handles dt internally via gate
print(y.shape) # (2,50,4)
```

### 5.3 Complexity

- **Time**: LTC $O(B T L_{ode} (D_{in}D + D^2))$, CfC $O(B T (D_{in}D + D^2))$ → $L_{ode}$≈6× saving, plus no adaptive loop divergence.
- **Space**: $O(D^2)$ weights, $O(BD)$ hidden, vs ODE adjoint $O(B T D)$ checkpointing.
- **Energy**: Loihi-2 CfC mapping reduces dynamic power 4.2× vs LSTM due to sparse event-driven updates [9].

## 6 Limitations

- **Approximation tightness**: CfC bound degrades for highly stiff $f$ with rapid switching ($f(t)$ discontinuous). $\sigma(-f t)$ smoothing loses sub-millisecond transients; LTC with $L_{ode}$=6 may still be needed for power electronics [4].
- **No closed-form for coupled $f$**: Derivation assumes $f_i$ depends only on $x,I$ not $dx/dt$; for second-order liquid synapses, closed-form fails.
- **Training instability**: Shared backbone with three heads causes gradient interference; authors use **separate optimizers** per head or gradient clipping [4]. Mixed CfC+LSTM helps vanishing but adds parameters.
- **Theoretical gap — sparsity vs expressivity**: NCP sparsity improves efficiency but no PAC bound yet proving 90% sparsity preserves universal approximation; empirical only [6].
- **Hardware**: True neuromorphic benefit requires Loihi-2 toolchain; Jetson/GPU still dense matmul, sparsity speedup <2× due to lack of sparse kernels [9].
- **Safety**: Boundedness ≠ stability under adversarial input. Adversarial $I(t)$ can drive $f \to 0$ → $\tau_{sys} \to \tau$ large, causing lag and overshoot in closed-loop. No robust control certificate yet.

Future work: hybrid LTC-CfC adaptive solver switching, second-order liquid synapses with analytic bounds, hardware-aware NCP NAS.

## 7 Conclusion

LTCs introduced **liquid time-constants** as a biologically grounded mechanism for adaptive, bounded, expressive continuous-time modeling [1][2]. Their Achilles' heel — ODE solver dependence — was alleviated by CfCs, which derive a **tight closed-form gating approximation** replacing integration with explicit interpolation between initial and asymptotic networks [4][5]. Combined with NCP wiring [6], this yields sub-10k-parameter agents capable of out-of-distribution generalization for autonomous flight and driving, deployable on microcontrollers and neuromorphic chips with millisecond latency.

For adaptive edge control, the LTC→CfC trajectory teaches a principle: **embed physics (time-constants) as learnable, input-dependent modulators rather than static hyperparameters**, then seek closed-forms not via symbolic integration of arbitrary ODEs (intractable) but via **structurally motivated approximation of interaction kernels** — here, synaptic transmission. The result is not just faster inference but *qualitatively different* adaptivity: online timescale reconfiguration without retraining.

Open directions include composition with state-space models (Mamba-style selective CfC), safety certificates via contraction theory, and Liquid Foundation Models scaling laws [8]. As edge autonomy demands more from less, liquid networks provide a rigorous, efficient, and biologically plausible substrate.
