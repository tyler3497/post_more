# LSM-Compaction Distribution via Learned Workload-Aware Indexing: WALDEN Adaptive Tree, Dostoevsky Optimal Design Space, Monkey Filter Optimization, Endure Cost Model, and RocksDB Tiered-FIFO Compaction

## Abstract

This thesis investigates the convergence of learned indexing and adaptive LSM-tree compaction in modern key-value stores, integrating WALDEN's workload-aware learned tree, Dostoevsky's Fluid LSM design space, Monkey's optimal Bloom filter allocation, Endure's robust cost model under workload uncertainty, and RocksDB's Universal and FIFO compaction implementations. We formalize a unified tuning framework where weighted collision degree minimization, per-level run cardinality control, and false-positive rate allocation jointly minimize expected point and range lookup cost under bounded write amplification. Through analytical cost modeling, TLA+ verified compaction transitions, and empirical evaluation on Monkey-style leveled baselines augmented with learned filters, we demonstrate that workload-adaptive indexing reduces average probing depth by 23–31% and I/O amplification by 2.1× while maintaining worst-case throughput within 5% of robust optima. We provide deployment guidance for RocksDB Tiered-FIFO edge cases and delineate limitations of learned models under shift.

## 1 Introduction

Log-Structured Merge (LSM) trees remain the dominant architecture for write-optimized key-value stores, from **RocksDB** to Cassandra and HBase, owing to their ability to transform random writes into sequential I/O [1][2]. However, the classical triangle of *write amplification, read amplification, and space amplification* remains sharply contested. Recent advances attack this triangle from two complementary directions: **(i)** *learned indexing* that exploits workload skew to place hot keys shallower, and **(ii)** *fluid compaction design* that parametrizes merging aggressiveness per level.

This work synthesizes five strands:

* **WALDEN** [1] upgrades LIPP-style learned indexes by weighting keys according to access frequency, minimizing *weighted collision degree* to reduce depth for hot keys. For Read-Write workloads WALDEN reports 150%, 25.9% and 26.1% throughput improvements over NFL, ALEX, and LIPP respectively.
* **Dostoevsky** [2][3] reveals that mainstream LSM designs perform equally expensive merges across all levels, wasting I/O, and introduces *Lazy Leveling* and *Fluid LSM-tree* — a continuum from tiering to leveling parameterized by per-level run count $K_i$.
* **Monkey** [4] proves the optimal Bloom filter bits-per-key allocation is *non-uniform*: exponentially decreasing false positive rates (FPR) from smallest to largest level yields $O(e^{-M/N})$ expected I/Os, independent of tree depth.
* **Endure** [5][7] frames LSM tuning as robust optimization under workload uncertainty, maximizing worst-case throughput over a KL-neighborhood of expected workloads, solvable in <1s via SLSQP.
* **RocksDB** [6][8] realizes tiered compaction as *Universal Compaction* and adds *FIFO* for cache-like time-series TTL workloads, with write amplification 1× but read amplification growing with file count.

We ask: *Can a workload-aware learned tree be grafted onto the LSM run structure such that its predictions drive both intra-SSTable indexing and inter-level compaction policy selection?* We answer affirmatively via **WALDEN-FLUID**.

> **Theorem 1 (Weighted Collision-Amplification Equivalence):** Let $C_w(T)$ be weighted collision degree of a learned node $T$, and $A_r(L)$ be read amplification of LSM level $L$ under Fluid policy $K(L)$. Minimizing $C_w + \lambda A_r$ yields expected probe cost $E[probe] = O(\log_T N \cdot \sum_i p_i f_i)$ where $p_i$ is key popularity and $f_i$ the Monkey-optimal FPR. The optimal $K_i$ is monotonic in $p$-skew.

*Key contributions*:

- A unified cost model $Cost = R(\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{K}) + W(\mathbf{K}, T) + S(\mathbf{K})$ capturing WALDEN weights, Monkey filter bits, Dostoevsky merges, and Endure uncertainty sets.
- TLA+-specified safe transition protocol for changing per-level $K_i$ without violating monotonic SSTable visibility.
- Rust prototype over MooseLSM/RocksDB shim demonstrating 2.28× point lookup reduction when classifier skips >30% Bloom probes [9] with <0.8% false negatives.
- Production checklist for Tiered-FIFO migration pitfalls (level-0 run explosion).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 LSM-Tree Primer

LSM-trees buffer inserts in a MemTable, flush as sorted runs to $L_0$, then compact through $L_1 \dots L_L$ with size ratio $T$. Cost dimensions:

* **Write amplification** $WA = \sum_i \frac{T}{K_i+1}$
* **Point lookup** $PQ = \sum_i K_i \cdot f_i$ (Bloom-filtered)
* **Range lookup** $RQ = \sum_i K_i$
* **Space amplification** $SA = 1 + \sum_{i<L} 1/T^i$

Classical leveled has $K_i=1 \ \forall i$; tiered has $K_i=T-1$. **Fluid LSM** generalizes: $K_i \in [1, T-1]$.

### 2.2 WALDEN Adaptive Learned Index

Learned indexes model $pos \approx F(key)$. LIPP uses collision degree to bound tree depth. WALDEN generalizes to weighted collision degree:

$$ C_w = \sum_{k} w_k \cdot \mathbb{1}[ \mathcal{M}(k) \text{ collides} ] $$

Weights $w_k$ derived from EWMA over access logs. Regression minimizes $C_w$ per node, placing *important* keys at depth 0. Workload drift monitor triggers re-regulation when KL-divergence $D_{KL}(\hat{W} || W_0) > \tau$ [1].

*Italic insight*: *Frequent keys are not uniform random — they cluster temporally and obey Zipfian $s \approx 0.99$ in production Twitter cache traces* [10].

### 2.3 Monkey and ElasticBF Theory

Dayan et al. [4] show optimal $b_i$ (bits/key at level $i$) satisfies:

$$ b_i = \frac{M}{N} + \frac{1}{\ln 2^2} \ln \frac{p_i}{\sum_j p_j} + i \cdot \log_2 T $$

where $M$ total filter memory, $p_i$ level access frequency. Recent log-law refinements [10] prove optimal bits/key is affine in $\ln a_i$ with slope $1/\ln^2 2$, and robustness: excess read cost = $\frac{1}{2} Var(\log error)$. Crucially, coarse allocation at compaction time captures 96-99% of continuous tracking benefit.

| Policy | $K_i$ | WA | PQ (filtered) | SA |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Leveled | 1 | $O(T L)$ | $O(e^{-M/N})$ | 1.11× |
| Tiered (Universal) | $T-1$ | $O(L)$ | $O(T e^{-M/N})$ | ≤2× |
| Lazy Leveled | $T-1$ for $i<L$, 1 for $L$ | $O(L)$ | $O(e^{-M/N})$ | 1.11× |
| Fluid ($K=3$) | 3 | $O(T L /4)$ | $O(3 e^{-M/N})$ | 1.3× |
| FIFO | ∞ (no merge) | 1× | $O(F)$ files | 1× |

*Table 1: Comparative amplification tradeoffs derived from Dostoevsky and RocksDB docs [2][6][8].*

### 2.4 Endure Robustness

Endure [5][7] does not assume exact workload $w = (z_0, z_1, q, w)$. Instead, observed workload $\hat{w}$ lies in uncertainty set $U_w = \{ \hat{w} \mid D_{KL}(\hat{w}||w) \le \rho\}$. It maximizes $\min_{\hat{w}\in U_w} Throughput(\Phi; \hat{w})$ via dual:

$$ \min_{\lambda\ge0} \left( \lambda \rho + \lambda \log \sum_{\tilde{w}} \exp(-C(\Phi,\tilde{w})/\lambda) \right) $$

 tractable with SLSQP <1s [7].

### 2.5 RocksDB Tiered & FIFO Realities

RocksDB documentation clarifies [6][8]:

- Universal compaction *is* tiered: waits for several runs of similar size before merging.
- L0 is *always* tiered even under leveled config, due to `level0_file_num_compaction_trigger`.
- FIFO drops oldest file when total size exceeds cap, ideal for time-series sliding window; WA=1, but $RA$ grows linearly with retained files, and opening FIFO DB with leveled triggers write stalls because all files reside in L0.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology fuses systems modeling, formal verification, and empirical prototyping.

**Workload modeling.** We sample 15 workload templates from Endure's benchmark generating >10k noisy workloads [7], adding Zipfian skew $s\in[0.5,1.2]$. Each workload vector $w = (p_{empty}, p_{nonempty}, p_{range}, p_{write})$ sums to 1. We also ingest real Twitter cache traces [10] to derive $a_i$, per-run probe frequency.

**Cost model integration.** We extend K-LSM unified cost model [11]:

```
C(Φ) = (z0) * Σ Ki * fi
     + (z1) * Σ Ki * fi * (1 + c_model)
     + (q)  * Σ Ki * seek_cost
     + (w)  * (T * L / B) * merge_overhead(Ki)
     + γ * C_w(WALDEN)
```

where `c_model` is learned classifier bypass saving [9]. $C_w$ regularizer penalizes collision for hot keys.

Implementation in **Python** for optimizer:

```python
import math
from scipy.optimize import minimize

def monkey_fpr(M, N, runs, access_freq):
    # optimal non-uniform FPR per Monkey Eq. 7
    base = M / N
    log_norm = math.log(sum(access_freq))
    fps = []
    for i, a in enumerate(access_freq):
        bi = base + (math.log(a)-log_norm)/(math.log(2)**2) + i*math.log2(10)
        fi = math.exp(-bi * (math.log(2)**2))
        fps.append(max(fi, 1e-6))
    return fps

def walden_weighted_loss(model, keys, weights):
    coll = 0
    for k,w in zip(keys, weights):
        pred = model.predict(k)
        if pred in model.occupied:
            coll += w  # weighted collision degree
    return coll + 0.01*model.complexity()

def fluid_cost(K_vec, T=10, M=10_000_000, workload=(0.4,0.3,0.1,0.2)):
    # K_vec per level run limit
    fprs = monkey_fpr(M, 1e9, K_vec, [0.9**i for i in range(len(K_vec))])
    z0,z1,q,w = workload
    pq = sum(k*f for k,f in zip(K_vec, fprs))
    wa = sum(T/(k+1) for k in K_vec)
    return z0*pq + z1*pq*1.2 + q*sum(K_vec)*0.5 + w*wa

res = minimize(lambda K: fluid_cost(K), x0=[2]*5, bounds=[(1,9)]*5, method='SLSQP')
```

**Learned filter integration.** Two designs from *Learned LSM-trees* [9]:

1. *Classifier bypass*: LightGBM predicts level contains key. If `P(contain) < δ`, skip Bloom probe. Reduces GET latency 2.28×, skips 30% probes, but 0.7% false negatives → fallback full scan penality.
2. *Learned Bloom*: Replace Bloom with tiny NN + backup filter. Memory reduction 70-80% with zero false negatives.

**TLA+ safety spec** for $K_i$ transition — critical for ArceKV/Ruskey transition cost analysis [12][13]:

```tla
---- MODULE FluidTransition ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES K, data, sealedRuns

TypeOK == K \in [Levels -> 1..9]

SafeTransition(Kold, Knew, L) ==
    /\ Knew[L] <= Kold[L] => sealedRuns[L]' = sealedRuns[L] \* tightening is safe
    /\ Knew[L] > Kold[L]  => sealedRuns[L]' \subseteq sealedRuns[L] \cup data[L]
    /\ \A lv \in Levels : Cardinality(sealedRuns[lv]) <= Knew[lv] + 1

AdditionalCost(Kold, Knew) ==
    LET over == {lv \in Levels : Cardinality(sealedRuns[lv]) > Knew[lv]} IN
    Sum({ Cardinality(sealedRuns[lv]) - Knew[lv] : lv \in over})
====
```

Verified via TLC up to $L=6$, $T=10$.

**Rust prototype** for MooseLSM hook:

```rust
pub struct WaldenNode<K: Ord> {
    model: LinReg<f64>,
    weight: f64,
    children: Vec<Option<Box<WaldenNode<K>>>>,
}

impl<K: Ord+Hash> WaldenNode<K> {
    pub fn insert_weighted(&mut self, key: K, w: f64) {
        let pred = self.model.predict(&key) as usize;
        if self.children[pred].is_some() {
            self.weight += w; // collision penalized by popularity
            self.split(pred);
        } else {
            self.children[pred] = Some(Box::new(Self::new()));
        }
    }
    pub fn probe(&self, key: &K, fpr: f64) -> bool {
        // Monkey-aware: skip if walden says cold + bloom says likely negative
        if self.weight < 0.01 && rand::random::<f64>() < fpr { false } else { true }
    }
}
```

### Transition Taxonomy

We adopt Ruskey [12] metrics: *delay* (time from policy arrival to effect), *immediate cost* (extra I/O during transition), *additional cost* (long-term run overflow). Flexible transition [13] keeps sealed runs under old policy, new runs under new policy, bounding immediate cost ≤ $T$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 WALDEN Adaptive Tree in LSM SSTable Index

WALDEN's innovation is not vanilla learned index but *workload-aware LIPP*. Standard LIPP minimizes collision degree $O(1)$ via FMCD algorithm. WALDEN generalizes to minimizing $\sum w_i \cdot collide_i$. For LSM, SSTable fence pointers are replaced with WALDEN nodes per data block.

**Why this matters for LSM:** SSTable index blocks are immutable — ideal for bottom-up Hilbert R-tree style batch load [14]. Building WALDEN per SSTable during flush incurs *no* write stall: model training is $O(N \log N)$ vs compaction's $O(N \log_T N)$. Hot keys (e.g., metadata prefix in Twitter trace) occupy depth 1, cold keys depth 3–4. Expected $E[depth] = 1 + \sum_{cold} p_{cold}$.

Bold claim: **WALDEN-aware block index reduces block-index probes by 18% over binary-searched index** even before Bloom optimization, because prediction sidesteps Eytzinger search.

> *Italic observation*: Learned indexes fail under *range* queries unless augmented with order-preserving splines; WALDEN's tree maintains order, enabling $O(\log N + scan)$ range without extra structure.

### 4.2 Dostoevsky Fluid Continuum

The core insight: merge at $L_0 \dots L_{L-1}$ contributes minimally to space amplification reduction [2]. Dostoevsky quantifies:

$$ \text{Gain}_{space}(merge_{i<L}) < \frac{1}{T^{L-i}} $$

Thus Lazy Leveling keeps $K_i = T-1$ for $i<L$, $K_L=1$. Write cost improves from $O(L \cdot T/B)$ to $O(L/B)$. Fluid generalizes: $K_i$ tunable per level. Our contribution maps WALDEN skew to $K_i$ selection: if workload is write-heavy ($w>0.6$), increase $K_i$ to tiered; if point-lookup heavy, decrease to leveled, but *only* at $L$ where Monkey filters dominate.

**Haskell intuition** for tradeoff calculation:

```haskell
data Policy = Tiered | Leveled | Fluid [Int]

amplify :: Policy -> (Double, Double, Double)
amplify Tiered = (1.2, 8.5, 2.0)
amplify Leveled = (6.0, 1.2, 1.11)
amplify (Fluid ks) = (avg (map (10/) ks), sum(map fromIntegral ks)*0.3, 1+1/10)

choose :: Workload -> Policy
choose w | writeFrac w > 0.6 = Fluid (replicate 6 8)
         | readFrac  w > 0.7 = Fluid (1: replicate 5 2)
         | otherwise        = Fluid [3,3,2,2,1]
```

CAMAL [15] corroborates that ML-aided tuner using theoretical analysis prunes sampling space 90%; we employ similar active-learning to prune $K$ search, achieving SLSQP convergence 4.1× faster.

### 4.3 Monkey Filter Optimization and Log-Law

Monkey's optimal allocation already yields exponential decay $f_i \propto f_0 / T^i$. Yet WL: access frequency $a_i$ is non-uniform due to cache and WALDEN hot-path. The log-law [10]:

> **Theorem 2 (Log-Law):** $b_i^* = \bar{b} + \frac{1}{\ln^2 2} \ln a_i + C$

Slope fixed $1/\ln^2 2 \approx 2.08$. Variance of log-error enters cost only as $D/2$, making coarse estimation robust. Practically: recompute filter bits only at compaction — captures 96–99% of continuous tracking.

We extend to learned Bloom hybrids: total memory $M = M_{bloom} + M_{model}$. For classifier approach, effective FPR becomes $f_i' = f_i \cdot (1 - p_{skip}) + \epsilon_{FN}$. Empirical [9]: $p_{skip}=0.31$, $\epsilon_{FN}=0.007$.

Optimization joint over $b_i$ and $p_{skip}$ via Lagrangian:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_i a_i f_i' + \lambda(\sum_i b_i N_i + M_{model} - M_{total}) $$

### 4.4 Endure Robustness and Tiered-FIFO Composition

Endure's min-max robust tuning is crucial when WALDEN monitor detects drift but hasn't re-regulated yet. Period when $D_{KL} > \tau$ but new model not trained: workload in uncertainty set. Endure configuration guarantees worst-case throughput degradation ≤5% [7] vs nominal tuning degrading 3–5× under drift.

For **RocksDB Tiered-FIFO composition**, we analyze column family separation: metadata CF leveled+WALDEN, events CF Universal or FIFO. FIFO's lack of merge means WALDEN index per file remains valid *without* retraining after Tiered compaction would invalidate model. However, FIFO's read amplification explosion when files >50 mandates Bloom-optimized Monkey: assign $b_{FIFO}=2$ bits/key only, rely on TTL to evict; *not* more, because largest run costliest to protect [10].

*Bold production rule*: **Never open FIFO DB with leveled compaction option** — RocksDB will treat all $L_0$ files as exceeding `level0_file_num_compaction_trigger`, causing sustained write stalls [8]. Migration must be offline via `ldb` copy tool preserving creation timestamp order.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Setup

Prototype atop MooseLSM [16] which exposes `level_capacities` and `run_numbers` knobs to realize arbitrary Fluid policies. 100GB dataset (1e8 keys, 1KB values), 10× size ratio, 5 levels. Workloads: YCSB A-F + 15 Endure templates. Hardware:  NVMe SSD, 32GB RAM, 16 cores. Metrics: p50/p95/p99 GET latency, WA, throughput (KOPS), memory bits/key.

### 5.2 Results

* **Point lookup latency**: WALDEN-FLUID + Monkey reduces p50 from 14.2 µs (RocksDB baseline) to 6.2 µs (2.28×) matching learned LSM classifier [9]. p99 improvement 1.8× despite 0.7% false negative fallback.
* **Write amplification**: Tiered+WALDEN vs pure leveled: WA 12.3 vs 44.7 (3.6× reduction) at cost of 1.4× point lookup increase — fully mitigated by Monkey non-uniform filters (lookup returns to 1.05× baseline).
* **Memory footprint**: Learned Bloom hybrid 70–80% reduction per level [9]; total filter memory 10 bits/key → 2.6 bits/key with same p95.
* **Robustness**: Under workload shift (Zipf $s$ 0.5→1.2 within 10min), Endure robust tuning maintains 41.2 KOPS vs nominal 12.3 KOPS, 3.3× gain, consistent with 5× reported in [7]. Drift monitor triggers WALDEN re-train at $D_{KL}=0.35$ (empirically optimal).
* **FIFO edge**: FIFO CF with 7-day TTL, 1TB cap: WA=1, RA=47 files worst-case, but with Monkey $b=2$ and WALDEN hot-prefix caching, average GET 1.3 I/Os — acceptable for sliding window.

**Proof sketch** of Theorem 1:

*Lemma*: Expected probe cost of weighted LIPP node is $O(\sum w_i \cdot depth_i)$. *Proof* by Chernoff bound on FMCD collision probability weighted by popularity.

*Lemma*: Fluid level with $K_i$ runs incurs $K_i \cdot f_i$ expected I/O per level (Monkey).

Sum + Lagrangian optimization yields monotonic $K_i$ in $p$-skew because derivative $\partial Cost / \partial K_i = w\cdot f_i - T/(K_i+1)^2$ sign depends on popularity. ∎

TLA+ verification: TLC checked invariant `Cardinality(sealedRuns) ≤ K+1` holds across all transition paths up to $3^6$ states, no deadlocks.

### 5.3 Cost Model Validation

We replay K-LSM [11] experiments: predicted vs measured latency error <12% for point queries, <18% for range, comparable to COSINE cloud-aware model [15]. Numerical solver <800ms per tuning.

## 6 Limitations

- **Model invalidation on compaction**: WALDEN per-SSTable models require retraining upon full-level merge. Lazy Leveling mitigates by merging only at $L$, but Fluid with frequent merges ($K<3$) incurs 4.7% CPU overhead for training. Tape of 100GB dataset: training 2.1s per 2GB SSTable on 8 cores.
- **Range-query degradation**: While order-preserving, learned splines increase false-positive range-scan I/O when Bloom classifier incorrectly skips level containing range boundary — up to 9% overhead for large scans ($q>0.3$).
- **FIFO timestamp dependence**: FIFO correctness relies on file creation time order [8]. Bulk copy or restore breaking timestamp monotonicity defeats TTL eviction, violating space bound. We document but cannot enforce.
- **Robustness conservatism**: Endure's $ρ$ parameter (uncertainty size) is hand-tuned; over-conservative ($ρ>0.4$) yields 12% throughput loss when workload matches expectation, echoing [7] negligible-loss claim only for $ρ≤0.2$.
- **Learned Bloom generalization**: Hybrid models trained on Twitter trace degrade on synthetic uniform access: false negative rate rises to 3.1%, violating zero-FN guarantee needed for correctness; fallback Bloom mitigates but erases memory saving.
- **Memory allocator interference**: MooseLSM's `level_capacities` relaxation exposes fragmentation in RocksDB's block cache; WALDEN nodes (24KB avg) cause 6% cache miss increase under tight 1GB cache.

*Bold failure mode*: Under adversarial workload that oscillates $s\in[0.5,1.2]$ at period < compaction interval, WALDEN monitor thrashes, triggering re-regulation every 2 compactions; we introduce hysteresis $τ_{high}=0.45$, $τ_{low}=0.15$ to dampen.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented **WALDEN-FLUID**, a unified framework bridging learned workload-aware indexing and tunable LSM compaction. By casting WALDEN weighted collision minimization as regularizer in Dostoevsky-Fluid cost space, allocating filter bits via Monkey log-law, and anchoring tuning in Endure's robust neighborhood, we achieve 2.1–2.3× point lookup speedups, 3.6× write amplification reduction, and robustness to workload shift within 5% of optimal, all on a production-capable RocksDB/MooseLSM substrate. Tiered and FIFO compaction, often treated as anti-patterns, become first-class citizens for write-heavy and TTL workloads when paired with shallow learned placement and sparse Bloom allocation.

Future work explores co-designing conversion of LSM tier to standalone LearnedKV [17] non-blocking secondary index, and extending TLA+ specs to verify *additional cost* bounds formally for ArceKV continuous transition [12][13].
