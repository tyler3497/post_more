# Scalable Causal Discovery from Interventional Data: Differentiable DAG Learning with NOTEARS, Interventional Markov Equivalence, Invariant Causal Prediction, and Do-Calculus Automation

## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of scalable causal discovery from interventional data, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of scalable causal discovery from interventional data and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early scalable constructions to state-of-the-art NOTEARS deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show scalable causal discovery from interventional data achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(commit - srs.g*value, srs.g2);
    let rhs = pairing(proof, srs.g2_h - srs.g2*point);
    lhs == rhs
}

## 6 Limitations and Future Work
