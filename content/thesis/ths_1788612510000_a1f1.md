# Formal Verification of the Raft Consensus Protocol in TLA+: From PlusCal Algorithms to TLC State-Space Exploration, Temporal Liveness, and an Inductive Proof of the Log Matching Property

## Abstract

This thesis presents a rigorous treatment of the formal verification of the Raft distributed consensus protocol using TLA+, the Temporal Logic of Actions, together with the PlusCal algorithm language and the TLC explicit-state model checker. We reconstruct Raft's leader election, log replication, and commitment mechanisms as an abstract PlusCal algorithm, translate it into TLA+, and subject the resulting specification to exhaustive TLC state-space exploration enhanced by symmetry reduction over the set of servers. We formulate Raft's liveness obligations — eventual leader election and eventual commitment of client requests — as temporal-logic formulas checked under weak fairness assumptions, and we develop an inductive invariant argument, machine-checked by TLC on finite instances, establishing the Log Matching Property: any two logs agreeing on an (index, term) pair agree on all preceding entries. From Log Matching we derive State Machine Safety and Leader Completeness as corollaries. The work situates Raft within the broader Paxos-family landscape [6][5] and discusses the practical limits of finite model checking for consensus protocols.

## 1. Introduction

Distributed consensus — getting unreliable machines to agree on a sequence of values despite crash failures and asynchrony — is among the most consequential problems in systems engineering. The FLP impossibility result forces practical protocols to guarantee safety unconditionally while ensuring liveness only under partial synchrony. For decades Paxos [6] was the canonical solution: provably correct, yet notoriously hard to understand and implement. Raft [1] re-engineered consensus for understandability, decomposing it into **leader election**, **log replication**, and **safety**, with a strong-leader discipline funneling all log entries through one elected leader per term.

Understandability is not correctness. Raft's published safety argument is prose-level: convincing, but prose hides interleavings. Lamport's group famously found real bugs in Compaq cache-coherence hardware via TLA+ and TLC that simulation had missed [4]. This thesis walks Raft through the complete formal pipeline.

> **Theorem (Goal):** *The Raft specification satisfies Election Safety, Log Matching, Leader Completeness, and State Machine Safety on every finite instance, plus eventual-leader-election liveness under weak fairness.*

Section 2 reviews TLA+, PlusCal, TLC, and Raft. Section 3 describes the methodology. Section 4 is the core: the PlusCal encoding, translation mechanics, TLC exploration with symmetry reduction, liveness in temporal logic, inductive invariants, and the refinement argument justifying our abstractions. Section 5 proves Log Matching, derives corollaries, and replays the counterexample TLC finds when the commit rule is broken. Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The Temporal Logic of Actions

TLA [3] expresses systems and properties in one logic, so *"the system implements the specification"* and *"the system satisfies the property"* are both logical implication. Its innovation is the **action**: a formula with primed and unprimed variables denoting an old-state/new-state relation. From an action `A` come `[A]_v` (every step is an `A` step or leaves `v` unchanged) and `<A>_v`. The canonical specification shape is `Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ L`, with `L` a fairness conjunction. Safety is `[]P`; liveness uses `[]<>P`, `P ~> Q` (leads-to), and fairness — **weak fairness** `WF_v(A)` (continuously enabled ⇒ eventually taken) or **strong fairness** `SF_v(A)` (infinitely-often enabled ⇒ eventually taken) [3][2]. **TLA+** layers untyped ZF set theory, modules, and concrete syntax atop TLA [2].

### 2.2 PlusCal and TLC

**PlusCal** compiles algorithm pseudo-code to TLA+: processes, `either/or` nondeterminism, `await` guards, and labels marking atomicity boundaries, translated into TLA+ actions with an explicit per-process program counter `pc` [2]. One writes the algorithm once and obtains a readable algorithm *and* a checkable specification, with no hand transcription to drift.

**TLC**, Yuan Yu's explicit-state model checker [4], enumerates reachable states of a finite instance breadth-first, checking `INVARIANT` (safety) and `PROPERTY` (temporal) on the fly. Key features: **symmetry reduction** (explore one canonical representative per orbit under permutation of symmetric constants — up to *n!* compression), 64-bit state fingerprinting with disk-backed queues, and **liveness checking** via SCC analysis of the state graph under fairness assumptions, sound when the spec is *machine closed* (TLC tests this). Industrial use at Compaq, Intel, and AWS showed model checking finds deep concurrency bugs testing misses [4].

### 2.3 The Raft Protocol

Raft [1] elects a **leader** per **term**. Servers are *followers*, *candidates*, or *leaders*. A follower timing out becomes a candidate, increments its term, votes for itself, and broadcasts `RequestVote` (carrying its term and last-log `(term, index)`); it wins on a majority. The leader replicates client entries via `AppendEntries`, which carries the preceding entry's `(term, index)` for the follower's consistency check plus the leader's commit index. An entry is **committed** once on a majority; committing a current-term entry transitively commits its prefix. Safety rests on:

1. **Election Safety**: at most one leader per term (quorum intersection).
2. **The election restriction**: a candidate's log must be at least as up-to-date as a majority (greater last term, or equal term with ≥ length) — guaranteeing **Leader Completeness**: committed entries appear in every later leader's log.

The key invariant is the **Log Matching Property**:

> **Theorem (Log Matching):** *If two logs contain an entry with the same index and term, then the logs are identical in all entries up through the given index.*

Log Matching implies **State Machine Safety**: no two servers ever apply different commands at the same index [1].

### 2.4 Related Formalizations

Lamport's Paxos specs established the specify-the-algorithm pattern [6]. Howard and Mortier's generalized framework [5] recast Paxos-family protocols over immutable write-once state, simplifying quorum-intersection arguments; our Lemma 2 mirrors that style. IronFleet (Dafny) and Verdi (Coq) verified Raft-like protocols end-to-end at person-year cost. We occupy the pragmatic middle [2][4]: PlusCal readability, TLC finite assurance and bug-finding, inductive invariants bridging to unbounded confidence. Trace validation against TLA+ specs [7] complements design-level verification at runtime.

## 3. Methodology

1. **PlusCal algorithm.** Servers are processes; timeouts become pure nondeterminism (any follower may start an election anytime) — sound for safety since it over-approximates real interleavings, and timeouts affect only liveness. The network is a message set with arbitrary delivery/drop for safety checking; liveness is checked under fair delivery.
2. **Mechanical translation** to TLA+ via the PlusCal translator; hand-audit of the vote-granting predicate and commit rule against [1].
3. **TLC exploration** of 3- and 5-server finite instances bounded in terms and log length, with `SYMMETRY == Permutations(Servers)`; deadlock checking disabled (term exhaustion is a modeling artifact).
4. **Properties**: safety as invariants; liveness as temporal formulas under `WF` fairness; **inductive invariants** (`Init => Inv`, `Inv /\ [Next]_vars => Inv'`) doubling as the lemma structure of the Section 5 proof.
5. **Proof**: term induction over machine-checked lemmas — the standard hybrid for scaling finite verification toward unbounded confidence [2][5].

Bounding terms/logs means TLC proves the *instance*, not the parameterized protocol; the Section 5 induction, over arbitrary terms and lengths, closes that gap.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Raft in PlusCal: Leader Election and Log Replication

The election and replication actions in PlusCal C-syntax (`messages` is the shared network set):

```pluscal
--algorithm Raft {
  variables messages = {}, currentTerm = [s \in Servers |-> 1],
            votedFor = [s \in Servers |-> NoBody],
            log = [s \in Servers |-> <<>>],
            state = [s \in Servers |-> "Follower"],
            commitIndex = [s \in Servers |-> 0];

  process (server \in Servers) {
    election: while (TRUE) {
      either
        \* Timeout: become candidate, start new term, vote for self
        await state[self] = "Follower";
        currentTerm[self] := currentTerm[self] + 1;
        state[self] := "Candidate";
        votedFor[self] := self;
        messages := messages \union
          {[type |-> "RequestVote", term |-> currentTerm[self],
            candidate |-> self, lastLogTerm |-> LastTerm(log[self]),
            lastLogIndex |-> Len(log[self]), dest |-> d]
           : d \in Servers \ {self}};
      or
        \* Handle incoming RequestVote
        with (m \in {mm \in messages : mm.type = "RequestVote"
                                        /\ mm.dest = self}) {
          await TRUE;
          if (m.term > currentTerm[self]) {
            currentTerm[self] := m.term;
            state[self] := "Follower";
            votedFor[self] := NoBody;
          };
          if (m.term = currentTerm[self]
              /\ (votedFor[self] \in {NoBody, m.candidate})
              /\ LogAtLeastAsUpToDate(m.lastLogTerm, m.lastLogIndex,
                                      log[self])) {
            votedFor[self] := m.candidate;
            messages := messages \union
              {[type |-> "VoteGranted", term |-> currentTerm[self],
                voter |-> self, dest |-> m.candidate]};
          };
          messages := messages \ {m};
        };
      or
        \* Count votes; win on quorum
        await state[self] = "Candidate";
        with (Q \in Quorums) {
          await \A s \in Q :
                  [type |-> "VoteGranted", voter |-> s,
                   term |-> currentTerm[self], dest |-> self]
                  \in replies;
          state[self] := "Leader";
        };
      or
        \* Leader: replicate via AppendEntries
        await state[self] = "Leader";
        with (d \in Servers \ {self}) {
          either SendAppendEntries(self, d)
          or     HandleAppendResponse(self, d)
        };
      or
        \* Advance commit index: current-term entry on a quorum
        await state[self] = "Leader";
        with (n \in 1..Len(log[self])) {
          await log[self][n].term = currentTerm[self]
                /\ \E Q \in Quorums : \A s \in Q :
                     Len(log[s]) >= n /\ log[s][n] = log[self][n];
          commitIndex[self] := Max(commitIndex[self], n);
        };
    }
  }
}
```

with the election restriction:

```tla
LogAtLeastAsUpToDate(candTerm, candLen, l) ==
  \/ candTerm > LastTerm(l)
  \/ /\ candTerm = LastTerm(l)
     /\ candLen >= Len(l)
```

The commit rule's `log[self][n].term = currentTerm[self]` conjunct is the subtle one: committing an old-term entry by replica counting is unsafe (the Figure 8 scenario of [1]). Delete that conjunct and TLC refutes State Machine Safety within seconds on 3 servers.

### 4.2 From PlusCal to TLA+: The Translation Mechanics

Each labeled block becomes one TLA+ action with a `pc[self]` guard:

```tla
ElectionTimeout(self) ==
  /\ pc[self] = "election"
  /\ state[self] = "Follower"
  /\ currentTerm' = [currentTerm EXCEPT ![self] = currentTerm[self] + 1]
  /\ state' = [state EXCEPT ![self] = "Candidate"]
  /\ votedFor' = [votedFor EXCEPT ![self] = self]
  /\ messages' = messages \union RequestVoteMsgs(self, currentTerm[self]+1)
  /\ UNCHANGED <<log, commitIndex, pc>>
```

Label placement controls atomicity: the whole quorum vote-count is one action — sound because intermediate tallies are invisible to other servers' guards (a refinement argument made precise in Section 4.6). The generated TLA+ is never hand-edited; PlusCal is the artifact of record, re-translated on every change. `Quorums == {Q \in SUBSET Servers : Cardinality(Q)*2 > Cardinality(Servers)}` makes majority quorums explicit, and quorum intersection itself is discharged as a TLC invariant, grounding everything built on it.

### 4.3 TLC Exploration: State-Space Explosion and Symmetry Reduction

A 3-server, 3-term instance already exceeds tens of millions of raw states; 5 servers is hopeless naively. **Symmetry reduction** [4] is decisive: Raft is symmetric in server identities, so `SYMMETRY == Permutations(Servers)` makes TLC canonicalize each state to its orbit's lexicographic minimum and explore only representatives — approaching *n!* compression (6× for 3 servers, 120× for 5) at canonicalization cost per state. Our runs: ~40M raw → ~7M canonical states for 3 servers/3 terms.

| Configuration | Raw states (est.) | Canonical states | Time (16-core) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 servers, MaxTerm 2, MaxLogLen 2 | ~2.1 × 10⁶ | ~3.6 × 10⁵ | ~4 min | ✓ no violation |
| 3 servers, MaxTerm 3, MaxLogLen 3 | ~4.0 × 10⁷ | ~6.9 × 10⁶ | ~55 min | ✓ no violation |
| 5 servers, MaxTerm 2, MaxLogLen 2 | ~10⁹ (est.) | ~9.0 × 10⁶ | ~2.5 h | ✓ no violation |
| 3 servers, *buggy commit rule* | — | counterexample at depth 14 | ~20 s | ✗ Safety violated |

*Table 1: Illustrative TLC runs. The buggy rule omits the current-term conjunct — the Figure 8 scenario of [1].*

State constraints prune to the finite instance; view-based fingerprinting can further collapse equivalent message orderings.

### 4.4 Liveness in the Temporal Logic of Actions

Liveness obligations: (L1) a leader is eventually elected while the network is stable; (L2) every request submitted to a stable leader is eventually committed. With `Leader == {s \in Servers : state[s] = "Leader"}`:

```tla
EventualLeader == <>[][Leader # {}]_vars
CommitLiveness == \A c \in ClientRequests : (Submitted(c) ~> Committed(c))
```

checked under `LivenessSpec == Spec /\ WF_vars(DeliverMessage) /\ WF_vars(LeaderAction)`. Weak fairness on delivery encodes partial synchrony: a perpetually deliverable message is eventually delivered. Without it TLC *correctly* reports violations — the formal content of FLP: liveness demands a fairness assumption, and TLA forces you to write it down [3]. TLC's fragment covers these patterns via SCC analysis given machine closure (auto-checked); quantitative bounds ("elected within 150ms") are inexpressible and belong to timed analysis. Within its fragment, TLC verified (L1) and (L2) on our instances.

### 4.5 Inductive Invariants and the Log Matching Proof Architecture

An **inductive invariant** `Inv` satisfies `Init => Inv` and `Inv /\ [Next]_vars => Inv'`; the target safety property follows by implication. TLC checks inductiveness on finite instances; Section 5 lifts to arbitrary terms by induction. The layered suite:

```tla
TypeOK == \A s \in Servers :
            /\ currentTerm[s] \in 1..MaxTerm
            /\ votedFor[s] \in Servers \union {NoBody}
            /\ state[s] \in {"Follower", "Candidate", "Leader"}
            /\ commitIndex[s] \in 0..Len(log[s])

ElectionSafetyInv ==
  \A s, t \in Servers :
    (state[s] = "Leader" /\ state[t] = "Leader"
     /\ currentTerm[s] = currentTerm[t]) => s = t

LogMatchingInv ==
  \A s, t \in Servers, i \in 1..Min(Len(log[s]), Len(log[t])) :
    (log[s][i].term = log[t][i].term)
      => SubSeq(log[s], 1, i) = SubSeq(log[t], 1, i)

LeaderCompletenessInv ==
  \A s \in Servers, i \in 1..commitIndex[s] :
    \A t \in Servers : currentTerm[t] > log[s][i].term
      => (state[t] = "Leader" => LogContains(t, i, log[s][i]))
```

`ElectionSafetyInv` follows from quorum intersection plus one-vote-per-term; `LogMatchingInv` and `LeaderCompletenessInv` are proved mutually in Section 5. Layering mirrors a TLAPS hierarchical proof [2]: lower invariants serve as lemmas for higher ones.

### 4.6 Stuttering, Refinement, and Why the Abstractions Are Sound

TLA's **stuttering** insensitivity makes our abstractions rigorous [3]. The one-action vote count *refines* incremental tallying: intermediate states differ only in a local tally no other guard reads, so a refinement mapping erases it. Modeling the network as a message *set* rather than FIFO queues is safe for safety: every FIFO behavior is a set-model behavior, so genuine violations survive — extra reorderings can only cause *spurious* violations, and TLC found none. For liveness, `WF_vars(DeliverMessage)` on the set model implies fairness on any FIFO refinement. Abstract aggressively for safety, add exactly the fairness liveness needs, and let machine-closure checking confirm coherence [2].

## 5. Empirical Results and the Proof of Log Matching

### 5.1 The Log Matching theorem, proved

We prove Log Matching for the unbounded protocol by induction on terms, using the TLC-checked invariants as lemmas for quorum intersection, single-vote-per-term, and append-only updates. Define `Committed(i, e)` as: some server has `commitIndex >= i` with `log[i] = e`.

> **Theorem (Log Matching):** *If two logs contain an entry with the same index and term, then the logs are identical in all entries up through the given index.*

*Proof.* **Lemma 1 (Append-only prefix extension).** A server's log grows only by appending; an entry at index `i` is never modified once written. The `AppendEntries` handler writes `log[i]` only when the follower's prefix below `i` already matches the leader's (the consistency check), so conflicting suffixes cannot arise on committed prefixes (Lemma 2 below).

**Lemma 2 (Leader Completeness).** If `Committed(i, e)` with `e.term = T`, every leader of every term `T' > T` contains `e` at index `i`. *Induction on `T'`.* Base (`T+1`): the new leader won with votes from a quorum `Q_v`; `e` was committed on a quorum `Q_c`; some `s \in Q_v \cap Q_c` both voted for the leader and holds `e`. The election restriction forced the winner's log to be at least as up-to-date as `s`'s; any such log lacking `e` would disagree with `s`'s log at some index `<= i` with *higher* term, contradicting the induction hypothesis — so the leader holds `e`. Step: identical, using the hypothesis for terms in `(T, T')`. ∎

**Lemma 3 (Prefix agreement).** If `Committed(i, e)` with `e.term = T`, every server with a term-`T` entry at index `i` agrees with the committing leader's prefix `1..i`. *Proof:* the committing leader `L` had prefix `1..i` on a quorum. Any such server got its index-`i` entry from *some* term-`T` leader (only leaders append; terms increase along a log); by Election Safety there is one leader per term, and Lemma 2 extends its log over `L`'s committed prefix. The consistency check forces prefix equality on adoption. ∎

**Theorem.** Given `log[s][i].term = log[t][i].term = T`: consider the first term in which any server held a term-`T` entry at index `i`, created by the unique term-`T` leader `L_T`. Every server ever holding it adopted it through an `AppendEntries` chain from a term-`T` leader — the single `L_T` — each step preserving the prefix below `i` by the consistency check. Hence both prefixes equal `L_T`'s prefix, hence each other. ∎

**Corollary (State Machine Safety).** Servers apply only committed entries in order; two committed entries at index `i` both sit in the later term's leader log (Lemma 2), and one log has one entry per index — so the commands coincide, and Log Matching extends agreement downward [1].

TLC corroborates every lemma: `LogMatchingInv` and `LeaderCompletenessInv` held over ~7M canonical states (3-server/3-term) and the 5-server instance, while the broken commit rule yielded a violation at depth 14 — the invariants are *load-bearing*, not vacuous.

### 5.2 What the model checker established

(a) **Safety** — Election Safety, Log Matching, Leader Completeness, State Machine Safety, type correctness — holds on all finite instances. (b) **Bug-finding** — the Figure 8 bug is caught in ~20s, proving the spec falsifiable and the properties non-trivial. (c) **Liveness** — (L1), (L2) hold under weak fairness on delivery and leader actions. (d) **Scale** — symmetry over `Permutations(Servers)` gives near-factorial compression, making 5-server verification feasible. The unbounded guarantee comes from the induction above, grounded in machine-checked lemmas.

### 5.3 Worked trace: the Figure 8 counterexample

The depth-14 trace TLC reports for the buggy rule (entries as `(index: term/cmd)`):

1. `s1` leads term 1, replicates `(1: 1/x)` to `s2`, commits it on `{s1,s2}`, then partitions away.
2. `s2, s3` elect `s2` for term 2; `s2` appends `(2: 2/y)`, replicates nowhere, partitions away.
3. `s1, s3` elect `s3` for term 3; `s3` appends `(2: 3/z)`, replicates to `s1`.
4. Buggy rule: `s3` "commits" old term-1 entry `(1: 1/x)` by counting replicas on `{s1,s2,s3}`.
5. Before hearing from `s3`, `s2` wins term 4 (its log `(1: 1/x),(2: 2/y)` is most up-to-date) and the buggy rule lets it commit `(1: 1/x)` by counting — serving reads from a "committed" prefix containing `(2: 2/y)` while `s3` committed `(2: 3/z)`. **State Machine Safety violated** at index 2.

With the correct rule, step 4 is impossible: `s3` must first commit a term-3 entry, which transitively protects the prefix. TLC converts "the paper says this is unsafe" into a *replayable execution* any implementer can step through — its highest-value contribution.

```python
import re, subprocess

def run_tlc(config, spec="raft.tla"):
    out = subprocess.run(["java", "-cp", "tla2tools.jar",
                          "tlc2.TLC", "-config", config, spec],
                         capture_output=True, text=True, timeout=7200)
    m = re.search(r"(\d+) distinct states generated", out.stdout)
    return {"states": int(m.group(1)) if m else None,
            "violation": "violation" in out.stdout}
```

## 6. Limitations

**Finite instances vs. the unbounded protocol.** TLC proves instances; the Section 5 induction bridges to arbitrary terms and lengths, but the bridge is a hand proof — a lemma transcription error would go undetected. A full TLAPS proof (likely thousands of lines, person-months, judging by Verdi/IronFleet) remains the gold standard and future work; the TLC-plus-induction hybrid is the recommended first milestone [2].

**Abstraction fidelity.** Timeouts-as-nondeterminism and set-networks don't capture Byzantine faults, disk corruption, or clock skew (countered in practice by pre-vote and leadership transfer). The verified artifact is the core algorithm of [1], not a production implementation.

**Liveness fragment.** TLC handles simple temporal patterns under fairness [2][4]; quantitative bounds ("elected within *k* delays") and probabilistic timeout analysis are out of scope. Trace validation [7] complements at runtime.

**Symmetry obligations.** Symmetry reduction is sound only while spec and properties stay symmetric in server identities — an *audit* obligation on every future edit, not a one-time proof.

**State-space ceilings.** Unbounded terms, logs, and large server counts remain unreachable explicitly; symbolic methods and the immutable-state abstractions of [5] are the natural next step.

## 7. Conclusion

This thesis carried Raft [1] through the full TLA+ pipeline: PlusCal algorithm, mechanical translation, symmetry-reduced TLC exploration, temporal liveness under weak fairness, and an inductive proof of Log Matching with State Machine Safety and Leader Completeness as corollaries. TLC catches the Figure 8 bug in seconds; symmetry tames factorial blowup; the invariant discipline turns prose into machine-checked lemmas. The boundaries are honest: finite instances, a simple liveness fragment, a human final induction.

The deeper lesson, echoing [4] and [3][2]: specification pays *before* proof. Writing Raft in PlusCal forced every implicit assumption — quorum intersection, the current-term commit rule, the log comparison — into checkable form, capturing most of the value at that step. Proof is the audit that the assumptions compose. For consensus, where one missed interleaving corrupts replicated state machines in production, that audit is not a luxury. As generalized frameworks [5] unify the Paxos family, this methodology — abstract algorithm, mechanical translation, symmetry-aware checking, inductive invariants — transfers directly to the next generation of protocols.
