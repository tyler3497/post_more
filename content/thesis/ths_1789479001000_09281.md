# Cubical Type Theory and Synthetic Homotopy: Kan Composition, Higher Inductive Types, and a Computational Reading of Univalence

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf dependent type theory through a homotopical lens, identifying types with spaces, terms with points, and identifications with paths. At its center stands Voevodsky's *univalence axiom*, which identifies equivalences between types with identifications in the universe, yielding an invariant conception of mathematical foundations. This article develops the univalent foundations program in full technical detail: the groupoid structure of identity types, the statement and consequences of univalence, cubical type theory as a computational interpretation in which univalence is *provable*, higher inductive types as the synthetic construction kit of spaces, and the flagship synthetic results of homotopy theory — the computation of the fundamental group of the circle and the fourth homotopy group of the three-sphere. We explain how constructive cubical models (Bezem–Coquand–Huber; Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg) resolve Voevodsky's constructivity conjecture, how Cubical Agda mechanizes these ideas with judgmental computation rules, and where the remaining open problems lie: general schemas for higher inductive types, universe hierarchies, and the coherence of higher paths.

##1 Intro

The foundations of mathematics were, for most of the twentieth century, synonymous with *set theory*: every object is ultimately a set, and equality is primitive and extensional. This picture is enormously successful, yet it clashes with mathematical practice, which is overwhelmingly *structural* — a group theorist cares about groups up to isomorphism, a topologist about spaces up to homotopy equivalence. In ZFC, isomorphic structures are *not* equal, so every structural statement needs a manual check that constructions "respect isomorphism." This mismatch between foundations and practice is the problem the univalent foundations program sets out to solve [1].

The program's starting point is a discovery from the early 2000s: Martin-Löf's *dependent type theory* admits a faithful homotopical interpretation in which types are spaces, terms are points, dependent types are fibrations, and elements of the *identity type* `Id_A(x, y)` are paths from `x` to `y` in the space `A` [1]. Developed by Awodey, Warren, Voevodsky, and others, this "homotopy interpretation" revealed that intensional type theory already carries a rich higher-dimensional structure: identity proofs compose and reverse like paths, with higher paths-between-paths. Hofmann and Streicher had shown in 1998 that the identity type gives every type the structure of a groupoid — a 1-dimensional shadow of an infinite-dimensional *∞-groupoid* structure.

Vladimir Voevodsky's contribution was twofold. First, he formulated the *univalence axiom*: for types `A` and `B` in a universe `U`, the canonical map from identifications `A = B` to equivalences `A ≃ B` is itself an equivalence. Informally, *equivalent types are identical* — the foundations finally say what mathematicians always meant. Second, he exhibited a model of type theory with univalence in Kan simplicial sets, establishing consistency relative to ZFC with suitable universes [1]. From these ingredients sprang *synthetic homotopy theory*, in which spaces are constructed and homotopy groups computed inside type theory itself, without ever mentioning topological spaces or singular homology.

This article is a self-contained technical exposition of the resulting edifice: the core type theory (§2), the synthetic methodology and cubical models (§3), a deep development of univalence, cubical type theory, HITs, synthetic homotopy, and implementation (§4), proof sketches of the flagship theorems (§5), limitations and open problems (§6), and a concluding assessment (§7). It is aimed at readers comfortable with dependent types and basic algebraic topology.

##2 Background

### Martin-Löf type theory and the identity type

Martin-Löf's intensional type theory is a *dependently typed* λ-calculus organized around the judgments `Γ ⊢ A type` and `Γ ⊢ a : A`. Its novelty for foundations is the *identity type* `Id_A(x, y)`: a type whose inhabitants are *proofs of identification* between terms. It is introduced by reflexivity `refl_x : Id_A(x, x)` and eliminated by the *induction principle* `J` — path induction — which reduces a property `P(x, y, p)` of an arbitrary path `p : x = y` to the reflexivity case. Computationally, `J` pattern-matches on reflexivity, giving the theory its constructive character.

Iterating the identity type yields *higher identifications* — elements of `Id (p, q)` for `p, q : x = y` — behaving as *homotopies between paths*, ad infinitum. Hofmann and Streicher proved the groupoid laws: paths have inverses, composition is associative up to a higher path, and unit laws hold up to higher paths [1]. The homotopy interpretation makes this geometry literal: types are *spaces*, terms are *points*, identifications are *paths*.

### Equivalence, contractibility, and the h-level hierarchy

Voevodsky introduced a stratification of types by *homotopy level* (h-level). A type `A` is *contractible* (h-level 0) if it has a center of contraction to which every point is identified; it is a *mere proposition* (h-level 1) if any two of its inhabitants are identified; a *set* (h-level 2) if all its identity types are propositions; a *1-groupoid* (h-level 3) if identity types are sets; and so on. This hierarchy internalizes the homotopical complexity of a type as a purely type-theoretic notion.

An *equivalence* `A ≃ B` is a function `f : A → B` whose fibers are all contractible — equivalently, a function with a quasi-inverse. This is a *property* in the mere-propositional sense once appropriately truncated: being an equivalence is itself a mere proposition, so there is no ambiguity about "which" equivalence is meant. The type of all equivalences `A ≃ B` is then a well-behaved object of study.

### The univalence axiom

For a universe `U` of (small) types, there is a canonical map

> `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)`

defined by path induction, sending `refl_A` to the identity equivalence. The *univalence axiom* asserts that `idtoeqv` is itself an equivalence:

> **Axiom (Univalence):** For all `A, B : U`, the type `A =_U B` is equivalent to `A ≃ B`.

### Models: from simplicial sets to cubical sets

Voevodsky, with Kapulkin and Lumsdaine, constructed a model of type theory with univalence in *Kan simplicial sets*, with types interpreted as Kan complexes and the universe as a Kan complex of small Kan complexes. This established consistency, but the construction used classical logic essentially — the Kan condition for the universe required non-constructive choices — leaving open Voevodsky's *constructivity conjecture*: that univalence could be given computational meaning, so that closed terms of natural-number type still compute to numerals.

The resolution came from *cubical sets*. Bezem, Coquand, and Huber built the first model of univalent type theory in a constructive metatheory: a presheaf model over a cube category with an explicit *composition* operation witnessing the Kan condition [4]. Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg internalized this as a *type theory* — cubical type theory — where interval, composition, and glueing are syntactic primitives, univalence is provable, and canonicity holds [2]. Orton and Pitts later axiomatized the topos-theoretic conditions for such models [7].

##3 Methodology

Our exposition follows the *synthetic* methodology of the HoTT book [1]: spaces are not built out of points-with-topology but are *postulated* as higher inductive types with specified points, paths, and higher paths, and all reasoning proceeds by the induction principles these types provide. The method has three pillars.

**Pillar 1: The homotopical reading of type theory.** Every type-theoretic concept receives its homotopical meaning, and definitions are chosen to be *homotopy invariant*. A "subset" becomes a predicate valued in mere propositions; a "quotient" becomes a higher inductive type with path constructors; an "algebraic structure" becomes a type of structured types, with univalence guaranteeing that isomorphic structures are identified. Invariance is thus enforced by the language itself rather than by external discipline.

##4 Deep Dive

###4.1 The Univalence Axiom and its Consequences

Univalence combines two ideas: *invariance under equivalence* and *a complete characterization of identity in the universe*. The map `idtoeqv` sends `refl` to the identity equivalence; univalence supplies its inverse `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B)`. *transport* along `ua(e)` turns an equivalence `e : A ≃ B` into a coercion, and *every* type family `P : U → U` respects equivalence: `e : A ≃ B` yields `P(A) ≃ P(B)`. This is the structure identity principle made internal: isomorphic groups are equal as groups, equivalent categories are equal as categories, and any property expressed in the theory is automatically isomorphism-invariant — eliminating the "transport hell" of set-theoretic formalizations.

###4.2 Cubical Type Theory: Interval, Composition, Glueing

Cubical type theory (CTT) extends Martin-Löf type theory with an *interval* pretype `I` having endpoints `0` and `1` and de Morgan algebra structure (meet, join, reversal) [2]. A *path* in `A` from `a` to `b` is a function `p : I → A` with `p 0 = a` and `p 1 = b` judgmentally. Path types are thus *defined* rather than inductively generated, and function extensionality is immediate: a path between functions is a function into a path type, by swapping arguments.

The heart of the theory is the *composition* operation. Given a *partial* cube — a family defined on all faces of a cube except one — composition produces the missing face together with the *filler*, the interior of the cube. Schematically:

```haskell
-- Composition: from an open box, produce the missing lid
comp : (i : I) -> (A : I -> Type) -> ((j : I) -> Partial (j = 0 \/ i = 1) (A j))
     -> A i
```

| Primitive | Syntax | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Interval | `I`, `0`, `1`, `∧`, `∨`, `~` | Domain of paths; de Morgan structure |
| Face lattice | `φ : F` | Partial elements; cofibrations |
| Path types | `PathP A a b` | Identity as functions from `I` |
| Composition | `comp i A u` | Kan filling; transport; `J` |
| Glueing | `Glue φ T A e` | Equivalences as paths; univalence |
| HIT schema | `data` with path constructors | Spheres, truncations, quotients [3] |

###4.3 Higher Inductive Types

Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point constructors* (e.g., `zero` and `suc` for `ℕ`). *Higher inductive types* (HITs) additionally admit *path constructors* — specified identifications between terms — and the induction principle must then handle these higher constructors with appropriate coherence data. The *circle* is the paradigmatic example:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The induction principle says: to define `f : (x : S¹) → P x`, give `b : P base` and `l : transport P loop b ≡ b`. The non-dependent special case (recursion) maps the circle into any type `A` by choosing a point `a : A` and a loop `p : a ≡ a`; the induced map `S¹ → A` sends `loop` to `p`. This is exactly the universal property of the circle as the free ∞-groupoid on one loop.

###4.4 Synthetic Homotopy Theory: Computing Homotopy Groups

The acid test of synthetic homotopy theory is the computation of homotopy groups of spheres — results that classically require singular homology, spectral sequences, or Serre fibrations. In HoTT they are proved by pure type-theoretic reasoning.

**The fundamental group of the circle.** Define `code : S¹ → Type` by circle recursion with `code base = ℤ` and `loop` acting as the successor equivalence `succ : ℤ ≃ ℤ`. The *encode–decode* method shows `(base = x) ≃ code x` for all `x : S¹`: `encode` maps a path to its transport action on `0 : ℤ`, and `decode` is defined by circle induction, sending `n : ℤ` to the `n`-fold composite `loopⁿ`. At `x = base` this yields `(base = base) ≃ ℤ`, i.e., `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ`, with path composition as addition [1] — short, constructive, and fully mechanized.

| Synthetic construction | Result | Classical analogue |
|---|---|---|
| `code` fibration over `S¹` | `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` | Covering-space theory |
| Hopf construction on `S¹` | Hopf fibration `S¹ → S³ → S²` | Hopf invariant one |
| LES of a fibration | `π₂(S²) ≅ ℤ`, `π₃(S²) ≅ ℤ` | Serre spectral sequence |
| James construction, Whitehead products | `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2` (Brunerie) | Freudenthal, Serre |
| Blakers–Massey (synthetic) | Connectivity of pushouts | Blakers–Massey theorem |
| Cohomology via Eilenberg–MacLane HITs | `Hⁿ(X; ℤ)` synthetic | Singular cohomology |

###4.5 Implementation: Cubical Agda and Computational Content

*Cubical Agda* extends Agda with cubical primitives — interval, faces, composition, glueing — making it a proof assistant with *native* univalence and a general HIT schema [5]. Here `ua : A ≃ B → A ≡ B` is a defined, computing function; transporting along `ua e` applies `e`. Function and propositional extensionality and quotient types are all *proved*, not postulated. HITs use ordinary `data` syntax plus path constructors, with eliminators generated with judgmental computation rules on higher constructors [3][5].

The practical payoff is visible in formalization projects: the HoTT-book reals as a higher inductive-inductive type check *without postulates or holes*, and the resulting terms compute — Cauchy sequences of rationals can be normalized to approximations [8]. Angiuli, Harper, and Wilson's *computational higher type theory* provides the semantic counterpart: a Martin-Löf-style meaning explanation in which types are complete programs and identifications are explicit coercions, validating canonicity — every closed boolean computes to a value — even in the presence of higher-dimensional structure [6].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We now state precisely the flagship theorems and sketch their proofs in the encode–decode style. These are *proofs*, not experiments: in univalent foundations, a constructive proof is an algorithm, and several have been executed by machine.

> **Theorem:** (Loop space of the circle) There is an equivalence `(base =_{S¹} base) ≃ ℤ` sending path composition to integer addition. Hence `π₁(S¹, base) ≅ ℤ`. [1]

> *Proof sketch.* Define `code : S¹ → Type` by `code base := ℤ` and `ap code loop := ua succ`. Define `encode : (x : S¹) → (base = x) → code x` by `encode x p := transport code p 0`, and `decode : (x : S¹) → code x → (base = x)` by circle induction, sending `n : ℤ` at `base` to `loopⁿ` (the `n`-fold composite of `loop`, with inverses for negative `n`); the path-constructor case requires showing `decode` respects `loop`, which follows from the definition of `code` on `loop`. One checks `encode` and `decode` are mutually inverse by path induction and circle induction respectively. ∎

> **Lemma:** (Hopf fibration) There is a fibration over `S²` with fiber `S¹` and total space `S³`. [1]

> *Proof sketch.* The circle carries an H-space structure: `μ : S¹ → S¹ → S¹` defined by circle induction using the equivalence `x ↦ x + n` on fibers. The Hopf construction turns an H-space `A` with `A ≃ S¹` into a fibration over the suspension `ΣA` with fiber `A`; here `ΣS¹ ≃ S²` and the total space is shown equivalent to the join `S¹ * S¹ ≃ S³`. ∎

> **Theorem:** (Brunerie) `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2`. [1][6]

> *Proof sketch.* The James construction gives a filtration of `ΩΣA` whose colimit is the loop space; applied to `A = S²`, the Whitehead product `[i₂, i₂] : π₃(S²)` is analyzed via the Blakers–Massey theorem to show that a certain map `S³ → S²` has Hopf invariant `±2`. The *Brunerie number* `β` is defined as a specific integer extracted from a higher encode–decode argument; the proof that `|β| = 2` involves large higher-dimensional coherences. The integer was subsequently computed by evaluating the corresponding closed term in a cubical implementation — a term of type `ℕ` normalizing to `2`. ∎

> **Theorem:** (Canonicity for cubical type theory) Every closed term `⊢ t : ℕ` in cubical type theory evaluates to a numeral `sucᵏ zero`. [2][6]

> *Proof sketch.* By a computability / realizability argument over the cubical sets model: types are interpreted as computability predicates closed under the Kan operations, and the interpretation of composition preserves computability. Since `ua` and HIT eliminators are interpreted by computable operations (glueing, initial-algebra recursion), the fundamental lemma yields that closed naturals denote numerals. ∎

These results jointly demonstrate the program's central claim: *univalence is computational*. The same axiom that restructures the foundations also runs.

##6 Limitations

The program's achievements are real, but so are its limitations — several acknowledged as open problems in the HoTT book itself [1].

**No general schema for HITs (yet).** Ordinary inductive types enjoy a precise schema (strictly positive functors, W-types). For HITs the book offers examples but no schema; the difficulty is the *coherence data* for higher path constructors at arbitrary dimension. Cubical type theory gives semantics for particular HITs (spheres, torus, suspensions, truncations, pushouts) with judgmental computation rules [3], and Cubical Agda implements a schema — but a fully general, proven-correct schema for arbitrary higher constructors, especially *inductive-inductive* and *higher inductive-recursive* types, remains incomplete, and universes closed under arbitrary HITs are still delicate.

**Universe management.** Univalence is stated per universe, and HITs must land in the right universe to avoid paradox. Cubical Agda's universe polymorphism interacts subtly with glueing: `Glue` raises universe levels in ways that complicate large constructions (e.g., the Rezk completion or higher topos structure). Managing `Setω`-style hierarchies while keeping canonicity is ongoing engineering.

**Performance of cubical computation.** Composition and transport in cubical type theory compute, but often *slowly*: a proof that normalizes in principle may be infeasible in practice, because Kan filling duplicates subterms across faces. The Brunerie number computation succeeded only after careful optimization; naive cubical programs routinely exhaust memory. Improving the operational behavior of `comp` and `transp` — e.g., via the "regular" cubical theories or rewrite-rule approaches — is active research.

**Coherence and strictness.** Path algebra in HoTT is *weak*: associativity of `∙` holds only up to a higher path, and managing infinite towers of coherences by hand is impractical. Cubical type theory improves this (some laws hold definitionally), but not all. Two-level type theories (exo/strict equality alongside homotopical equality) attempt to recover strict reasoning where needed, at the cost of complicating the core theory.

**Classical mathematics gap.** Large parts of classical mathematics — choice principles, excluded middle for arbitrary propositions, impredicative encodings — are independent of or incompatible with univalence in its pure form. While consistent extensions exist (e.g., adding LEM for mere propositions yields a classical subuniverse of sets), the synthetic theory cannot simply import classical proofs; each must be re-examined for constructivity and invariance. The library of formalized univalent mathematics, though growing, remains far smaller than its set-theoretic counterpart.

##7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began with a simple observation — that Martin-Löf's identity type already *is* the path space of homotopy theory — and grew, through Voevodsky's univalence axiom, into a candidate new foundation for all of mathematics [1]. Since 2013 the technical development has been remarkable: cubical type theory turned univalence from an axiom into a theorem with computational content [2]; constructive cubical-set models settled the constructivity conjecture [4][7]; higher inductive types made spaces directly definable [3]; and synthetic proofs computed genuine homotopy groups, culminating in the machine-checked evaluation of the Brunerie number [6]. Cubical Agda has turned the theory into a working environment in which univalent mathematics is done, not merely discussed [5][8].

The philosophical payoff matches the technical one. Univalence internalizes the structuralist practice of mathematics: equivalent objects are identical, constructions respect equivalence automatically, and the "invariance" that set-theoretic foundations could only approximate becomes a theorem of the logic. Whether univalent foundations will displace set theory as the *working* foundation remains open — the limitations of §6 are substantial — but the program has permanently changed the conversation. It showed that foundations can be *homotopical*, *computational*, and *structural* all at once, with space as a primitive notion reasoned about through its paths.

The frontier is clear: general schemas for higher inductive types, performant cubical computation, larger-scale formalized synthetic mathematics, and a deeper understanding of the (∞,1)-categorical semantics of universes. The univalent foundations program is no longer a proposal; it is a research field.
