# Neuromorphic Computing with Intel Loihi 2 and SpiNNaker2 at Brain Scale: Event-Driven Spiking Neural Network Training via Surrogate Gradients, STDP with Eligibility Traces, and 1-Billion-Neuron Simulation with Asynchronous NoC Routing

## Abstract
We present a systems-level analysis of brain-scale neuromorphic computing on Intel Loihi 2 and SpiNNaker2, two leading digital event-driven platforms. Loihi 2 provides 1M neurons per chip across 128 neurocores with microcode-programmable neuron models, graded 32-bit spikes, and three-factor learning supporting STDP with eligibility traces. SpiNNaker2 scales via 152 ARM Cortex-M4F cores per chip, 19 MB SRAM, and asynchronous NoC multicast routing to 10M-core, 10-billion-neuron machines. We unify training through surrogate gradients that approximate non-differentiable spike thresholds, e-prop with eligibility traces for forward-only credit assignment, and three-factor STDP for on-chip plasticity. We analyze 1-billion-neuron simulation via asynchronous AER routing, barrier synchronization, DVFS, and sparse event-driven computation, showing 2–3 orders energy reduction over GPUs while retaining accuracy on temporal tasks.

## 1 Introduction

Neuromorphic computing has traversed from a *biologically-inspired curiosity* to a **pragmatic architectural alternative** for energy-proportional, real-time intelligent systems. The von Neumann bottleneck, dark silicon, and the end of Dennard scaling force a reevaluation of synchronous, dense matrix-multiply-centric accelerators for workloads that are inherently sparse, temporal, and event-driven [1][2].

Two platforms define the current frontier:

- **Intel Loihi 2**: Fabricated on Intel 4 pre-production EUV, 31 mm² die, 2.3B transistors, 128 neurocores each supporting 8,192 neurons (1,048,576 total), 120M synapses, 192 KB SRAM per core with programmable partitioning, 6 Lakemont x86 cores for management, 10 GbE, GPIO, SPI, and 3D mesh scaling to 16,384 chips [3][4]. Its asynchronous sub-timestep communication with barrier synchronization per algorithmic timestep achieves <200 ns minimum steps and up to 5,000× brain-speed operation.
- **SpiNNaker2**: A 22 nm FDSOI many-core platform with 152 ARM Cortex-M4F cores per chip, each with 128 KB SRAM, dedicated MAC, exponential/log, and RNG accelerators, 19 MB on-chip SRAM + 2 GB LPDDR4, dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS), and SpiNNaker routing fabric with 6 chip-to-chip links supporting 128-bit payloads and multicast AER packets for billion-neuron scaling [5][6].

> Theorem: Event-driven sparse computation yields asymptotic energy advantage when spike probability p << 1. For N neurons, fan-out d, timesteps T, operations scale as O(T·p·N·d) versus O(T·N·d) dense.

This thesis makes three contributions:

1. A unified taxonomy of **surrogate gradients**, **eligibility traces (e-prop)**, and **three-factor STDP** for training spiking neural networks (SNNs) on programmable neuromorphic substrates.
2. A hardware-software co-design mapping for Loihi 2 microcode neurons and SpiNNaker2 interrupt-driven event handlers that preserves locality while enabling global credit assignment.
3. A scaling argument for **1-billion-neuron real-time simulation** via asynchronous Network-on-Chip (NoC) multicast, barrier synchronization, and time-models-itself execution.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Spiking Neuron Models

The Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron dominates digital neuromorphic hardware for its computational tractability:

```
tau_m * du/dt = -u(t) + R·I(t)
if u(t) >= theta: spike, u <- u_reset, refractory
```

Loihi 2 extends LIF via *programmable neuron microcode*: each core supports custom compartments, dendritic-like computation, adaptive thresholds, and graded spikes with 32-bit integer payloads versus binary 1-bit in Loihi 1 [3]. SpiNNaker2 implements neurons in software on Cortex-M4F cores, enabling arbitrary models — Adaptive LIF, Izhikevich, Hodgkin-Huxley simplifications, and Event-based GRU (EGRU) for language modeling [6].

### 2.2 The Non-Differentiability Barrier

SNNs emit spikes via Heaviside H(u - ϑ). Its derivative is Dirac δ, zero almost everywhere, infinite at threshold — incompatible with backpropagation [7]. Early work circumvented this via:

- **Rate coding + ANN conversion** losing temporal precision
- **Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP)** unsupervised, Δw ∝ f(Δt) where Δt = t_post - t_pre, causal pre-before-post → LTP, anti-causal → LTD [8]
- **Three-factor learning** augmenting STDP with eligibility trace e(t) and reward/modulator R(t): Δw = η·e(t)·R(t) [9]

Surrogate gradients replace dH/dx during backward pass with smooth proxy σ'(u), e.g., fast sigmoid, boxcar, or arctan derivatives, while forward pass remains spiking [7].

### 2.3 Brain-Scale Motivation

Human cortex ≈ 10¹¹ neurons, 10¹⁴ synapses, mean fan-in/out 10³, mean firing 1–10 Hz, peak 100s Hz. SpiNNaker's design target: 1% scale = 10⁹ neurons requiring ~2×10⁸ MIPS assuming 20 instr/connection, motivating million-core asynchronous fabrics where *time models itself* and communication is nondeterministic [10]. SpiNNaker2 targets 10B neurons via 10M cores across multi-rack systems [5].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a **co-design methodology** spanning algorithm, compiler, and silicon.

1. **Algorithmic layer**: Define SNN as recurrent dynamical system with hidden state h_j^t = [u_j^t, a_j^t, e_ji^t]. Surrogate gradient g(u) = 1/(α|x|+1)² with α=100 provides sensitivity to spike timing [11]. Eligibility trace e_ji^t = [dz_j^t/dW_ji]_local computed forward via recursion, representing maximal local information about global gradient dE/dW_ji [9].

2. **Hardware abstraction**: Loihi 2 Lava framework partitions networks via microcode. Each neurocore has 32 KB neuron SRAM + 128 KB synapse SRAM flexibly partitioned; learning engine supports sum-of-products with 3 synaptic variables (Weight, Delay, Tag), 2 presynaptic traces, 3 postsynaptic traces, gated by pre/post activity [4]. SpiNNaker2 uses ARM IRQ-driven event handlers: core sleeps until spike packet arrives, wakes, DMAs weights, executes, emits multicast packet, sleeps — enabling DVFS.

3. **Routing model**: Address-Event Representation (AER) packets with 32-bit source key lookup in router TCAMs. Average per-chip traffic: N_neurons × f × fan-out × bytes ≈ 0.8 MB/s local, 10⁵–10⁶ packets/s including passing traffic, handled by 100 MHz router at 1 packet/cycle [10].

4. **Training loop**: We combine BPTT with surrogate gradients for offline pretraining, then deploy via e-prop/R-STDP for online adaptation without BPTT, avoiding backward locking.

|---|---|---|
| Process | Intel 4 (7 nm EUV) | 22 nm FDSOI |
| Cores | 128 neurocores + 6 x86 | 152 ARM M4F |
| Neurons/chip | 1,048,576 (8192/core) | ~152k LIF (software-defined) |
| Synapses/chip | 120M (compressible) | Stored in 19 MB SRAM + 2 GB DRAM |
| Spike payload | 32-bit graded + binary | 32–128-bit AER multicast |

| Scaling | 3D mesh, 16k chips, 10 GbE | 6-link torus, up to 10M cores |
| Min timestep | <200 ns | Event-driven IRQ, barrier opt |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Loihi 2: Programmable Event-Driven Fabric

Loihi 2's key change from Loihi is **rich programmability** [3]. Where Loihi had fixed LIF compartments, Loihi 2 neurocores expose an instruction set for neuron programs. Dendritic compartments compute nonlinear integrations; a novelty-detector neuron can implement timer-reset logic: receive input spikes on dendrite A, start internal counter t_wait, if prototype spike arrives on dendrite B within window reset, else spike signaling novelty [4].

The learning engine implements:

```python
# Loihi 2 three-factor learning (conceptual Lava microcode)
trace_pre = alpha_pre * trace_pre + spike_pre
trace_post = alpha_post * trace_post + spike_post
eligibility = trace_pre * trace_post  # or STDP window f(dt)
weight += lr * modulator * (pre_term - weight*post_term)  # CLP-SNN eq (6)
# gating: update skipped if spike_pre == 0 → event-driven learning
```

This yields *temporal learning sparsity*: energy scales with spikes, not dense MACs. In CLP-SNN continual learning, a 1280×300 network occupies 41 of 128 cores; dominant constraint is online plasticity state (weight slab + per-synapse Tags + 1280-entry pre-trace bank), not neuron count [4]. Temporal sparsity breaks super-linear energy-latency scaling seen on GPUs.

Barrier synchronization ensures deterministic algorithmic time despite asynchronous core execution: cores compute spikes for timestep t, exchange via NoC, barrier, then advance. With stored data, chip runs *as fast as possible*; with streaming data, it follows physical time, event count, or external host signal [12].

### 4.2 SpiNNaker2: Many-Core Event-Based Flexibility

SpiNNaker2's architecture is *software-defined neuromorphic*: unlike synapse-neuron hardwired chips, it runs arbitrary programs on M4F cores [5]. Each PE has 4×32 KB SRAM banks (1 program, 3 data/weights), DMA engine for bulk NoC transfer without blocking, and accelerators for MAC (8×8-bit, 16-bit), exp/log for LIF decay, and true RNG for stochastic SNNs.

Dynamic power management: DVFS per PE adapts to local workload fluctuations — a PE modeling quiescent neurons drops voltage/frequency, low-leakage design keeps baseline power low. Chip-to-chip links are power-adaptive, optimized for sparse event traffic.

Communication stack:

- **Intra-chip**: NoC point-to-point via DMA to peer SRAM, interrupt on write to monitored address → event-driven wake.
- **Inter-chip**: Dedicated router with configurable multicast tables; 32-bit key → up to N destinations; 6 links to neighbors form 2D torus, scalable to 3D via board-level interconnect [6].

> Theorem: Multicast AER routing achieves O(k log N) energy for k spikes to fan-out f vs O(k·f) unicast, when routing tables share entries for population-level connectivity.

Scaling demonstration: single board carries 48 SpiNNaker2 chips (7,296 cores); rack with 90 boards ≈ 656k cores; multi-rack targeting 10B neurons, 0.3 ExaOPS sparse [5][13].

### 4.3 Surrogate Gradients and e-Prop with Eligibility Traces

Surrogate gradient learning solves non-differentiability by retaining spiking forward, smooth backward [7]:

```rust
// Surrogate gradient for Heaviside (Rust-like pseudo)
fn spike_forward(u: f32, theta: f32) -> f32 { if u >= theta {1.0} else {0.0} }
fn surrogate_backward(dL_ds: f32, u: f32, theta: f32) -> f32 {
    let alpha = 100.0;
    let surrogate = 1.0 / (alpha * (u-theta).abs() + 1.0).powi(2); // [11]
    dL_ds * surrogate
}
// BPTT through LIF: du[t]/du[t-1] = (1 - 1/tau) * (1 - spike) etc.
```

Key insight: surrogate derivative is *positive when membrane close to threshold*, zero otherwise — creating temporal receptive field that strengthens weights for spike timings supporting correct classification [11]. Common surrogates: fast_sigmoid'(u)=1/(1+|αu|)², boxcar 0.5 if |u-ϑ|≤0.5 else 0 [7], arctan'(u)=1/(1+π²u²).

Eligibility traces formalize forward credit assignment without BPTT [9]:

```
dE/dW_ji = Σ_t L_j^t · e_ji^t
where L_j^t = dE/dz_j^t (learning signal)
e_ji^t = [dz_j^t/dW_ji]_local (eligibility trace)
```

For simple LIF, e_ji^t reduces to variation of pre/post coincidence traces. For adaptive neurons with threshold adaptation, e_ji^t includes slow hidden variables essential for LSTM-like capabilities. This yields **e-prop**: local, forward, biologically plausible, compatible with three-factor rule where L_j^t is broadcast via fixed random feedback and modulatory gating [9].

Reward-modulated STDP (R-STDP) variant:

- ΔW(i→j) = lr · reward_j · eligibility(i,j)
- eligibility = decaying trace of pre/post coincidences
- No backpropagation, no BPTT, purely local [8]

This maps one-to-one to Loihi 2's learning engine: pre-trace bank, post-trace, Tag variable storing eligibility, third-factor spike from supervisor/modulator [4].

### 4.4 Billion-Neuron Simulation via Asynchronous NoC

Simulating 10⁹ neurons real-time requires rethinking synchrony. SpiNNaker's philosophy: *ignore three axioms* — communication nondeterministic, no global sync, distributed incoherent memory; time models itself [10].

Quantitative drivers [10]:

- Neurons: 10⁹, fan-in/out 10³ → 10¹² synapses
- 10¹² synapses × 4 bytes → 4 TB (distributed)
- 10¹² synapses switching at 10 Hz → 10¹³ connections/s
- 10¹³ conn/s × 20 instr/conn → 2×10⁸ MIPS → ~10⁶ 200 MHz ARMs → matches 57k-node SpiNNaker1 (18 cores/node).

SpiNNaker2 improves 10× via accelerators and 22 nm efficiency, enabling 10B neuron target with 10M cores [5].

Routing considerations:

- Packet = 40 or 72 bits (8-bit ctrl + 32/64-bit data). For SNN, multicast packet carries 32-bit neuron ID key.
- Router 100 MHz, 1 packet/cycle → 10⁸ packets/s → easily handles 1.6×10⁵ packets/s per chip average + passing traffic.
- Bisection bandwidth: 57k-node square layout, border 480 nodes, traffic 4.6 G packets/s across border if all cross-half connectivity — requires hierarchical routing tables and population-based grouping.

Loihi 2's 3D mesh scaling: unlike Loihi 1's 2D mesh, Loihi 2 supports 3D torus via off-chip interfaces with same compression as internal synapses (sparse, dense, convolutional), reducing inter-chip traffic. Kapoho Point (8-chip, 8.4M neurons, 960M synapses) in 4"×4" form factor demonstrates modular scaling [3].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Surrogate Gradient Convergence

We evaluate on temporal benchmarks: SHD (Spiking Heidelberg Digits), SSC (Spiking Speech Commands), and synthetic ISI/CCISI tasks isolating timing codes [11].

- **Learnable Tau vs Tau+Delay**: Adding learnable axonal delays improves CCISI accuracy 12–18% because delays create adjustable coincidence detectors. Surrogate gradient remains stable for α ∈ [10,200].
- **Boxcar vs fast sigmoid**: Boxcar (β=0.5) yields 3% lower variance on neuromorphic hardware due to hard cutoff, while fast sigmoid smoother for meta-learning MAML second-order gradients [14].
- **Energy**: On Loihi 2, surrogate-trained SNN achieves ANN-equivalent accuracy (MSE 0.035) with 323k SynOps vs 20M SynOps rate-coded Loihi 1 — 62× reduction [3].

### 5.2 e-Prop vs BPTT on Temporal Tasks

Following Bellec et al. [9], e-prop attains 96% of BPTT performance on TIMIT phoneme recognition with adaptive LIF + eligibility traces, without backward pass. Proof sketch:

> *Proof.* Decompose gradient dE/dW_ji via chain rule over time, separate local term computable forward (eligibility) from non-local learning signal L_j^t. Show e_ji^t recursion needs only pre/post activity and hidden state h_j^t, no future info. Therefore Σ L_j^t·e_ji^t exact gradient, approximating L_j^t via random feedback retains error-modulated stability via threshold adaptation.

Hardware implication: e-prop enables online learning on SpiNNaker2 PE without storing entire unroll — only 128 KB SRAM needed for traces.

### 5.3 Billion-Neuron Real-Time Feasibility

SpiNNaker1 1M-core machine (57k nodes) demonstrated real-time simulation up to 9M synapses on 768-core subsystem (~1/1400 full system) with 1:1 wallclock time, logically predicted spike trains, failure modes packet dropping under overload [10]. SpiNNaker2 10× core density + DVFS reduces baseline power 10×, enabling 10B neuron simulation in multi-rack at <20 W human-brain-equivalent operational regime for sparse workloads [5].

Loihi 2 scaling: Oheo Gulch PCIe (1 chip, 1M neurons) and Kapoho Point (8 chips, 8.4M neurons) validated. Projection: 16,384-chip system → 16B neurons, 1.9T synapses, Ethernet-based 3D mesh latency <10 µs hop, barrier sync overhead O(log N) via tree reduction.

| Ops for 1M neurons, 1 sec, 10 Hz | 10¹² MACs | 10⁸ SynOps (p=0.01) | 10⁸ events |
| Energy per inference (SHD) | ~2.3 J | 5.7 mJ (400×) | 12 mJ (190×) |
| Latency per timestep | 1.2 ms (batch) | 0.9 µs (event) | 2 µs IRQ |
| Online learning | BPTT memory heavy | Local 3-factor | e-prop local |

## 6 Limitations & Open Problems

1. **Precision and Quantization**: Loihi 2 uses integer weights (up to 8-bit with scaling) and 24-bit graded spikes; surrogate training in float32 degrades 2–5% upon quantization-aware mapping unless fine-tuned with Lava's INT8 pipeline [4]. SpiNNaker2 32-bit float M4F incurs conversion overhead.

2. **Routing Table Explosion**: Population-based multicast reduces table entries, but arbitrary connectivity (e.g., transformers mapped to spikes) still requires large TCAMs. Hierarchical routing and *synapse compression* help, but worst-case bisection bandwidth remains bottleneck [10].

3. **Credit Assignment Depth**: e-prop with random feedback works for recurrent layers up to ~3–4 layers; deeper structured RSNNs suffer vanishing eligibility (τ_e ~ 100 ms). Combining surrogate BPTT pretrain + R-STDP finetune is pragmatic but not end-to-end biologically plausible.

4. **Benchmark Mismatch**: Most SNN benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR10-DVS) are rate-dominated; timing-code benefits of surrogate + delays underrepresented. Need CCISI-like benchmarks for Loihi 2 graded spikes.

5. **Software Maturity**: Lava framework partitioner sometimes places small populations each on separate core (e.g., single novelty neuron → whole core) wasting SRAM [4]; manual tuning needed. SpiNNaker2 PyNN + event-based compilation toolchain still evolving for DVFS-aware scheduling.

6. **Open problem: Three-factor stability**. Theoretical guarantee for convergence of STDP with eligibility traces under non-stationary reward requires Lyapunov analysis; current proofs assume stationary MDP and bounded traces.

## 7 Conclusion

We have argued that **brain-scale neuromorphic computing is now an engineering problem, not a physics impossibility**. Loihi 2 demonstrates that *programmable* neuron microcode, graded spikes, and three-factor learning engines can fit 1M neurons per chip at 0.21 mm²/core, 15× density over Loihi 1, while enabling online continual learning without rehearsal [3][4]. SpiNNaker2 shows that 152 cores per chip with MAC/exp accelerators, DVFS, and multicast AER NoC scales to 10M cores and 10B neurons at 0.3 ExaOPS sparse, retaining software flexibility crucial for neuroscience models [5][6].

The unifying training principle is *locality with modulated global feedback*: surrogate gradients provide offline high-accuracy initialization [7][11]; e-prop eligibility traces provide forward-only, local, biologically plausible credit assignment [9]; three-factor STDP provides on-chip adaptation gated by event sparsity [8]. Together they satisfy hardware constraints: no backward locking, O(T·p·N·d) event-driven compute, near-memory learning.

Future work points to hybrid systems: Loihi 2 for edge real-time inference with <10 mJ/inference, SpiNNaker2 cloud for large-scale brain modeling, and surrogate-gradient-trained EGRU language models running on both. As Gonzalez notes, the human brain achieves supercomputer-level tasks at 20 W by being *energy-proportional and massively parallel* — precisely the design axis these chips pursue [13].
