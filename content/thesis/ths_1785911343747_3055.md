# Speculative Out-of-Order Execution at Scale: Reorder Buffer Design, Tomasulo's Register Renaming, and Memory Disambiguation in Modern Superscalars

## Abstract
This thesis investigates speculative out-of-order execution at scale: reorder buffer design, tomasulo's register renaming, and memory disambiguation in modern superscalars through a mathematically rigorous lens, synthesizing contemporary advances in chip design with foundational theory. We develop formal models, prove convergence and complexity bounds, and evaluate empirical implications across benchmarks. Our analysis combines algebraic, probabilistic, and systems perspectives, establishing novel connections between theory and practice. Through extensive formalism, we demonstrate that careful design of underlying structures yields asymptotic improvements while preserving correctness guarantees. The work contributes definitional clarity, new theorems, and open problems that invite further investigation by the research community.
