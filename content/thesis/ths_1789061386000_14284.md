# Timed Automata and Zone-Based Model Checking: Region Abstractions, Difference Bound Matrices, and the Reachability Algorithms of the UPPAAL Toolchain

## Abstract

Timed automata, introduced by Alur and Dill in 1990, extend finite-state automata with finitely many real-valued clocks, providing a rigorous mathematical model for real-time systems in which correctness depends on quantitative timing. This thesis develops the full arc from theory to industrial-strength verification: the region-equivalence construction that renders reachability decidable and PSPACE-complete; the zone-based symbolic representation of clock valuations as conjunctions of difference constraints; difference bound matrices (DBMs) with their Floyd–Warshall canonicalization and constant-time symbolic operations; finite abstractions based on maximal-constant and LU-bound extrapolation; the more recent simulation-based approach to abstraction; and the architecture of the UPPAAL model checker, which industrialized these ideas through its compact DBM library, on-the-fly forward reachability with passed and waiting lists, and engineering refinements such as active-clock reduction and minimal constraint systems. We prove the region construction correct as a time-abstract bisimulation, analyze the complexity of the DBM operation suite, and evaluate the empirical consequences of abstraction choice on termination and state-space explosion, drawing on published experiments from the UPPAAL, TChecker, and verified-checker lineages [1][2][3][4][7].

---

## 1. Introduction

Few problems in computer science are as unforgiving as *timing*. A protocol that responds correctly but one millisecond too late is not merely slow — it is wrong. Aircraft collision-avoidance systems, automotive bus protocols, medical infusion pumps, and industrial schedulers all demand that correctness arguments account for quantitative time. Classical finite-state models, which abstract away time entirely, cannot express such requirements; continuous-time models without decidability results cannot be checked automatically. **Timed automata** occupy the rare and valuable middle ground: they model real-valued time explicitly, yet their core decision problems remain decidable [1].

A timed automaton is a finite-state Büchi automaton augmented with a finite set of real-valued *clocks*. All clocks advance synchronously with time. Transitions carry *guards* — conjunctions of comparisons between a clock and an integer constant — and *resets* that set selected clocks to zero. Locations carry *invariants* restricting how long the automaton may dwell in them. Despite the infinite, dense state space induced by real-valued clocks, Alur and Dill showed that the language-emptiness problem — and with it, reachability — is decidable, via the celebrated **region construction** that collapses the uncountable valuation space into finitely many equivalence classes [1].

The region graph, however, is exponential and serves mainly as a proof device. Practical verification migrated to **zones**: convex sets of valuations defined by conjunctions of *difference constraints* of the form *x − y ≺ c*. Zones are represented canonically as **difference bound matrices (DBMs)**, supporting delay, guard intersection, reset, and emptiness checks as efficient matrix operations [2]. Because zones alone do not guarantee termination of forward exploration, tools apply **extrapolations** — widenings parameterized by the maximal constants appearing in the automaton, and more recently by separate lower and upper bounds per clock (LU-extrapolation) [5]. An alternative line of research replaces extrapolation by **simulation relations** between zones, yielding the subsumption-based algorithms implemented in TChecker and recently mechanized in verified checkers [3][4][7].

This thesis presents these ideas as a unified technical narrative. Section 2 fixes the formal syntax and semantics. Section 3 develops the methodology: regions, zones, DBMs, and the forward reachability algorithm. Section 4 treats four deep technical topics — region-graph correctness, the DBM algebra, extrapolation hierarchies, and the UPPAAL toolchain architecture. Section 5 combines empirical findings with proof sketches. Sections 6 and 7 address limitations and conclusions.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Syntax and concrete semantics

Let *X* be a finite set of clocks and let *C(X)* denote the set of clock constraints: conjunctions of atomic formulas *x ∼ c* or *x − y ∼ c* with *x, y ∈ X*, *c ∈ ℕ*, and *∼ ∈ {<, ≤, =, ≥, >}*. A **timed automaton** is a tuple *A = (L, l₀, Σ, X, E, I)* where *L* is a finite set of locations, *l₀ ∈ L* the initial location, *Σ* a finite alphabet, *X* the clock set, *E ⊆ L × C(X) × Σ × 2^X × L* the edge set, and *I : L → C(X)* assigns each location an invariant — a conjunction of upper bounds *x < c* or *x ≤ c* [1][2].

A clock valuation is a map *v : X → ℝ≥₀*. The semantics of *A* is the timed transition system whose states are pairs *(l, v)* with *v ⊨ I(l)*, generated by two rules:

1. **Delay:** *(l, v) → (l, v + d)* for *d ∈ ℝ≥₀* whenever *v + d′ ⊨ I(l)* for all *0 ≤ d′ ≤ d*.
2. **Action:** *(l, v) —a→ (l′, v[r := 0])* for an edge *(l, g, a, r, l′)* whenever *v ⊨ g* and *v[r := 0] ⊨ I(l′)*.

### 2.2 Regions: the finite quotient

Alur and Dill's key insight is that only finitely many *qualitative distinctions* among valuations matter for future behavior: the integer parts of clocks up to a per-clock maximum constant *cₓ*, whether each fractional part is zero, and the *ordering* of the fractional parts. Formally, *v ≅ v′* (region equivalence) iff for every clock *x*:

- either *⌊v(x)⌋ = ⌊v′(x)⌋*, or both *v(x), v′(x) > cₓ*;
- *frac(v(x)) = 0 ⟺ frac(v′(x)) = 0*;
- for all *x, y*: *frac(v(x)) ≤ frac(v(y)) ⟺ frac(v′(x)) ≤ frac(v′(y))* [1].

The quotient of the state space by *≅* is the **region graph**, a finite automaton whose reachability is equivalent to reachability in the original timed automaton. The number of regions is bounded by

|*L*| · |*Σ*| · |*X*|! · 2^|*X*| · ∏ₓ (2cₓ + 2),

which is exponential in the number of clocks — adequate for a decidability proof, ruinous for an implementation [1][2].

### 2.3 Complexity landscape

The foundational results, all from Alur and Dill [1], are worth stating precisely:

| Problem | Nondeterministic TA | Deterministic TA |
|---|---|---|
| Language emptiness / reachability | **PSPACE-complete** | PSPACE-complete |
| Universality | *Undecidable* (Π¹₁-hard) | PSPACE-complete |
| Language inclusion | *Undecidable* | PSPACE-complete |
| Closure under complement | No | Yes (Muller acceptance) |

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology mirrors the standard architecture of a zone-based timed model checker [2][5][7]:

1. **Formalize** the automaton and the query (a reachability property *E◇φ* in UPPAAL's query language, a subset of TCTL).
2. **Symbolize** sets of valuations as *zones* — conjunctions of difference constraints *x − y ≺ c* — represented as *difference bound matrices*.
3. **Explore** the symbolic state space forward: from a symbolic state *(l, Z)*, compute delay successors, guard intersections, and resets purely on DBMs.
4. **Abstract** zones through extrapolation or simulation to force termination of the exploration, preserving soundness (every abstract counterexample refines to a concrete one) and completeness (every concrete reachable state is covered).
5. **Decide** the query by checking whether any abstract symbolic state satisfies *φ*.

The forward reachability procedure, in the style of UPPAAL's passed/waiting-list algorithm [2][7], is:

```tla
---- MODULE ZoneReachability ----
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANTS Locs, InitLoc
VARIABLES passed, waiting
Init == passed = {} /\ waiting = {<<InitLoc, ZeroZone>>}
Next ==
  \/ \E s \in waiting :
       waiting' = (waiting \ {s}) \cup Succ(s) \ passed
       /\ passed' = passed \cup {s}
  \/ UNCHANGED <<passed, waiting>>
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<passed, waiting>>
====
```

Here *Succ* computes all delay and discrete successors of a symbolic state as DBM operations, and set difference against *passed* is mediated by a *subsumption* test *Z ⊆ Z′*: a symbolic state is discarded when an already-explored, larger zone at the same location covers it. Subsumption is what converts exponential blowup into practical termination [3][7].

Python reference implementation of the DBM canonicalization at the heart of every operation:

```python
def canonicalize(dbm):
    """Floyd-Warshall all-pairs shortest paths; O(n^3).
    dbm[i][j] = (bound, strict) encoding x_i - x_j <= bound
    (strict: '<' vs '<=').  After this pass every entry is
    the tightest entailed difference constraint."""
    n = len(dbm)
    for k in range(n):
        for i in range(n):
            for j in range(n):
                cand = add(dbm[i][k], dbm[k][j])
                if cand < dbm[i][j]:
                    dbm[i][j] = cand
    return dbm

def is_empty(dbm):
    """A zone is empty iff its constraint graph has a
    negative cycle; equivalently, some diagonal entry
    of the canonical DBM is negative."""
    return any(canonicalize(dbm)[i][i] < (0, False)
               for i in range(len(dbm)))
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The region construction as a time-abstract bisimulation

The region equivalence *≅* is not merely finite — it is a **time-abstract bisimulation** on the concrete transition system. This is the precise content of the decidability theorem, and it deserves a proof rather than a slogan.

> **Theorem (Region bisimulation).** Let *v ≅ v′* with *v ⊨ I(l)*. Then (i) for every delay *d* there exists *d′* with *(l, v+d) ≅ (l, v′+d′)* (and both respect the invariant), and (ii) for every edge *(l, g, a, r, l′)*, *v ⊨ g* implies *v′ ⊨ g*, and *v[r := 0] ≅ v′[r := 0]*.

*Proof sketch.* Guards compare clocks against integer constants or differences against integer constants; the integer-part and zero-fractional clauses of *≅* make guard satisfaction invariant under *≅*, and resets map integral valuations to integral ones, preserving the equivalence. For delays: the ordering clause on fractional parts guarantees that as time elapses, the *sequence of region boundaries crossed* — the order in which fractional parts hit integers — is identical for *v* and *v′*; choosing *d′* to cross the same number of boundaries yields a region-equivalent valuation. ∎

The corollary is immediate: the region graph faithfully simulates the timed automaton for all *qualitative* properties (reachability, and in fact all of TCTL without exact-time equalities across unbounded horizons) [1][2].

### 4.2 The DBM algebra: zones as constraint graphs

A zone *Z* over clocks *X* is encoded by introducing a reference clock *x₀ ≡ 0* and storing, for every pair *(i, j)*, the tightest bound *mᵢⱼ ∈ (ℤ ∪ {∞}) × {<, ≤}* such that *Z ⊨ xᵢ − xⱼ ≺ᵢⱼ mᵢⱼ*. Viewed as a weighted directed graph — vertices are clocks, edge *j → i* carries weight *mᵢⱼ* — a DBM is consistent (nonempty zone) exactly when the graph has **no negative cycle**, and the canonical form is the all-pairs shortest-path closure [2].

The symbolic operations required by forward exploration are:

| Operation | Effect on zone | DBM cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Delay** *Z⇑* | remove all upper bounds on clocks (*xᵢ ≤ ∞*) | *O(n²)* |
| **Guard intersect** *Z ∧ g* | tighten entries per atomic constraint | *O(n²)* + re-canonicalize |
| **Reset** *Z[r := 0]* | set row/column of *x ∈ r* to copy *x₀* | *O(n²)* |
| **Emptiness** | negative-cycle test | *O(n³)* (one canonicalization) |
| **Inclusion** *Z ⊆ Z′* | entrywise *≼* on canonical forms | *O(n²)* |

Canonicalization dominates at *O(n³)*, but *n* is the number of clocks plus one — small in practice — and most operations avoid full re-canonicalization: after tightening a single bound, only paths through the affected vertex need updating, giving *O(n²)* [2][5].

### 4.3 Extrapolation hierarchies: from *k*-normalization to *aLU*

Zones alone do not terminate forward search: consider a loop that delays by one time unit per iteration — the zones *x ≥ 0, x ≤ 1*, *x ≥ 1, x ≤ 2*, … are pairwise incomparable and the search never stabilizes. A **widening** maps each zone to a coarser one drawn from a finite family, applied after every successor computation [3][5].

The classical **k-extrapolation** (*M*-extrapolation) caps every bound at the maximal constant *M* appearing anywhere in the automaton: bounds above *M* are relaxed to *∞*, bounds below *−M* to *−M*. The coarsened zones are finite in number, hence termination, and the abstraction is sound and complete for reachability [2].

Behrmann et al. observed that a single global *M* is brutally coarse: a clock compared only against small constants in guards is extrapolated as if it ranged up to the global maximum. **LU-extrapolation** instead tracks, per clock *x*, the maximal *lower* bound *L(x)* and maximal *upper* bound *U(x)* occurring in the automaton, and extrapolates asymmetrically: upper bounds above *U(x)* are dropped, lower bounds below *−L(x)* are dropped [5]. Since *L(x), U(x) ≤ M* typically with strict inequality, the LU abstraction is *finer* — it distinguishes more zones — while still finite. Herbreteau, Srivathsan, and Walukiewicz then identified the **aLU abstraction** as the *largest* (coarsest) abstraction with respect to LU-bounds that remains sound and complete for reachability; although aLU-images of zones need not be convex, they showed how to use the abstraction *without materializing it*, via a direct zone-inclusion test against the abstract semantics [4].

The hierarchy, from coarsest to finest (all sound and complete for reachability):

*k*-extrapolation ≺ LU-extrapolation ≺ aLU-abstraction ≺ (simulation-based, zone inclusion) ≺ identity

where each step preserves termination whenever the previous one does, and each refinement can exponentially reduce the explored abstract state space on suitable models [3][4].

### 4.4 The UPPAAL toolchain: from DBM library to industrial model checker

UPPAAL — developed jointly at Uppsala University and Aalborg University — is the canonical realization of this theory, and its architecture is worth studying as a software artifact [2][5]:

- **Modeling language.** Networks of timed automata extended with *binary* and *broadcast* synchronization channels, *committed* and *urgent* locations (time may not pass / interleavings are prioritized), bounded integer variables, arrays, and C-like functions. These extensions are compiled down to the core timed-automaton product on the fly.
- **DBM library.** A standalone, heavily optimized difference-bound-matrix package: canonical and minimal-constraint representations, active-clock projection, and the LU-extrapolation operators. The library is the performance-critical kernel; everything else is search strategy [2][6].
- **Search engine.** On-the-fly forward reachability with unified *passed* and *waiting* lists, subsumption-based pruning, and partial-order-style reductions (committed locations). Queries are drawn from a pragmatic TCTL subset: *E◇φ* (reachability), *A□φ* (safety), *E□φ*, *A◇φ*, and leads-to *φ ⇝ ψ*.
- **Query-driven abstraction.** Bounds *L(x), U(x)* are computed per query from the constants actually relevant, so the abstraction adapts to the property rather than the whole model.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 What the experiments show

The survey literature and tool papers report a consistent picture across benchmark suites (Fischer's mutual exclusion, FDDI token ring, CSMA/CD, the *RCS* and *B&O* industrial cases) [3][4][5]:

1. **Zones beat regions by orders of magnitude.** No competitive tool enumerates regions; the region graph is a proof device. Zone-based exploration with subsumption explores symbolic states whose count is typically *far* below the region count, because a single zone aggregates exponentially many regions.
2. **LU beats *k*.** Moving from global-maximum extrapolation to per-clock LU-bounds reduces the number of explored abstract states dramatically on models where clocks have heterogeneous constant ranges — the common case — because fewer spurious distinctions survive widening [5].
3. **aLU beats LU.** Herbreteau et al.'s experiments show the aLU abstraction exploring strictly fewer abstract states than LU-extrapolation on the standard benchmarks, with the inclusion-based implementation adding negligible overhead per step [4].
4. **Simulation-based search wins on liveness and hard cases.** The TChecker line, replacing extrapolation by a *≼_LU* simulation preorder between zones, handles reachability *and* liveness (Büchi) uniformly and extends to weighted and diagonal-constraint variants where extrapolation is awkward [3].

### 5.2 Proof: termination of extrapolated forward search

> **Theorem (Termination).** Forward reachability with *k*-extrapolation (hence LU and aLU) terminates on every timed automaton.

*Proof sketch.* The extrapolation maps every zone into the finite set of *k*-bounded DBMs over *X* — there are finitely many matrices with entries in *(ℤ ∩ [−k, k]) × {<, ≤} ∪ {∞}*. Subsumption discards any symbolic state *(l, Z)* for which a previously explored *(l, Z′)* has *Z ⊆ Z′*. Along any infinite exploration path, infinitely many symbolic states share a location (pigeonhole), and among their finitely many distinct extrapolated zones two must satisfy inclusion — the later is subsumed. Hence the waiting list empties. ∎

### 5.3 Proof: soundness and completeness of the abstraction

> **Theorem (Abstraction correctness).** Let *α* be a widening that is *extensive* (*Z ⊆ α(Z)*) and *region-compatible* (if *v ∈ Z* then the region of *v* is contained in *α(Z)*'s region closure). Then *(l, v)* is reachable in *A* iff some explored *(l, Z)* has *v ∈ Z*.

*Proof sketch.* Soundness: by induction on the exploration, every concrete run is simulated by the abstract run obtained by replacing each concrete successor with its extrapolated zone — extensiveness guarantees the concrete valuation is covered at each step. Completeness: if *(l, v)* with *v ∈ Z* is explored, reverse the simulation: region-compatibility implies each abstract step's guard and invariant conditions hold for some concrete valuation in the zone, and time-abstract bisimilarity (Section 4.1) lifts the abstract path to a concrete one. ∎

Together, termination and correctness give a decision procedure: run the search; the query *E◇φ* holds iff an explored symbolic state satisfies *φ* [2][3].

## 6. Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what zone-based timed model checking *cannot* do:

1. **Diagonal constraints and updates.** Guards of the form *x − y ∼ c* are supported by zones natively, but *updates* like *x := y + c* or *x := x + 1* leave the zone fragment — the image of a zone under a general update is not a zone. Extrapolation-based termination then breaks; the simulation approach extends further but not universally [3].
2. **Stopwatches and hybrid dynamics.** Clocks that can be *stopped* (rate 0 or 1) make reachability undecidable — this is the celebrated result of Cassez and Larsen on stopwatch automata. UPPAAL supports stopwatches only for restricted analyses (e.g., schedulability with over-approximation), not full verification [2].
3. **Liveness and full TCTL.** Forward zone reachability decides safety and reachability; general liveness (Büchi acceptance, nested temporal operators) needs the simulation-based or region-based algorithms, at higher cost [3].
4. **Parametric and probabilistic extensions.** Parametric timed automata (unknown constants) have undecidable reachability in general; probabilistic timed automata require entirely different (MDP-based) machinery.
5. **Worst-case complexity is real.** PSPACE-completeness is not a formality: crafted models do explode, and abstraction choice, variable ordering, and modeling discipline (few active clocks, committed locations, small constants) remain the difference between seconds and infeasibility [4][5].
6. **Verified implementations lag.** Mechanized correctness proofs of the full DBM + search stack (as in the Isabelle/HOL verified checker [7]) cover a subset of UPPAAL's modeling language; the industrial tool itself is trusted, not proven.

## 7. Conclusion

From Alur and Dill's region construction to UPPAAL's industrial DBM engine, the story of timed-automata verification is the story of a single idea refined across three decades: *the infinite can be tamed by the right finite quotient, and the right quotient for engineering is not the finest one.* Regions give decidability; zones give practicality; LU-bounded abstractions and simulations give termination without surrendering the precision that makes verification useful. The DBM — a matrix, a constraint graph, a Floyd–Warshall closure — remains the unglamorous hero: every diagram in this thesis's figures, every guard intersection and delay step in every industrial case study, bottoms out in *O(n³)* shortest paths over a handful of clocks [1][2][5].

Open challenges persist at the frontier surveyed by Bouyer et al.: unifying extrapolation and simulation techniques, pushing verified checkers to full UPPAAL expressiveness, and extending zone methods to richer dynamics without losing decidability [3][7]. But the core achievement stands: real-time systems — where a millisecond is the difference between correct and catastrophic — can be modeled faithfully and verified automatically, at industrial scale, today.
