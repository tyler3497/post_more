# Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations: From Denoising Score Matching to Consistency Distillation

## Abstract

Score-based generative models and denoising diffusion probabilistic models have coalesced into a unified continuous-time theory centered on the stochastic differential equation (SDE), transforming generative modeling from a collection of discrete-time heuristics into a principled study of time reversal of diffusion processes. This thesis presents a self-contained treatment of that unification: the forward diffusion that perturbs a data distribution $p_0$ into a tractable prior $p_T$, the reverse-time SDE that inverts it using only the Stein score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$, the denoising score-matching objective that makes score estimation tractable, and the probability-flow ordinary differential equation that yields deterministic sampling and exact likelihoods. We dissect the variance-exploding, variance-preserving, and sub-VP process families, derive the equivalence between noise prediction and score parameterization, analyze predictor–corrector and higher-order exponential-integrator samplers (DDPM, DDIM, Karras-EDM), and examine consistency models as boundary-condition distillations of the probability-flow ODE. We review convergence guarantees, report representative empirical results including FID records, and identify open limitations in score estimation under manifold hypotheses, discretization error compounding, and likelihood-quality gaps.

## 1. Introduction

Generative modeling asks a deceptively simple question: given samples from an unknown distribution $p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x})$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, how does one produce new, high-fidelity samples from it? For decades the field organized itself around a handful of competing paradigms — *likelihood-based* models such as autoregressive models and normalizing flows, *implicit* models such as generative adversarial networks, and *variational* latent-variable models. Each paradigm traded expressivity against tractability: flows offered exact likelihoods but constrained architectures; GANs produced striking samples but no density; VAEs were stable but blurry.

Diffusion-based generative models, introduced in their modern form by Sohl-Dickstein et al. and elevated to dominance by the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) of Ho et al. [2], proposed an entirely different geometry of generation. Instead of mapping noise to data in one learned transformation, they define a *gradual corruption process* — a fixed Markov chain that slowly injects noise — and learn only to *invert* it step by step. The inversion is learned via a remarkably simple objective: predict the noise that was added. Score-based generative modeling, developed in parallel by Song and Ermon [1,6], arrived from the opposite direction: estimate the gradient of the log-density (the *score*) of successively perturbed data distributions and generate samples by Langevin dynamics.

The decisive theoretical advance was the demonstration by Song et al. [1] that both families are discretizations of a single continuous-time object: a forward SDE of the form

$$d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}, \quad t \in [0,T],$$

and its *reverse-time SDE*, whose drift depends on the data distribution only through the time-dependent score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$. This thesis is an extended exposition of that unification and its consequences. Our contributions, as a scholarly synthesis, are: **(i)** a rigorous derivation of the reverse-time SDE via Anderson's theorem, **(ii)** a unified treatment of denoising score matching across the variance-exploding (VE), variance-preserving (VP), and sub-VP process families, **(iii)** a taxonomy and error analysis of modern samplers — DDPM, DDIM, predictor–corrector, exponential integrators, and the probability-flow ODE — and **(iv)** an analysis of consistency models as one-step distillations of the probability-flow ODE, together with known convergence guarantees and open problems.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The forward diffusion process

Let $p_0 \approx p_{\mathrm{data}}$ be the data distribution on $\mathbb{R}^d$. The forward process is a time-homogeneous (or, in continuous time, time-inhomogeneous) diffusion that gradually erases structure:

$$d\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}_t, \tag{1}$$

where $\mathbf{w}_t$ is a standard Wiener process, $\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is the drift coefficient, and $g: [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}_+$ is the diffusion coefficient. The marginal density $p_t(\mathbf{x})$ of $\mathbf{x}_t$ evolves under the *Fokker–Planck equation*

$$\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} = -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \cdot (\mathbf{f} p_t) + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \Delta p_t. \tag{2}$$

Song et al. [1] identify three canonical choices:

- **Variance Exploding (VE) SDE:** $\mathbf{f} \equiv 0$, $g(t) = \sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}$ for a geometrically increasing noise scale $\sigma(t)$. This is the continuous-time limit of noise-conditioned score networks (NCSN) [6]; the variance grows without bound.
- **Variance Preserving (VP) SDE:** $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathbf{x}$, $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$, the continuous-time limit of DDPM [2]. The stationary distribution is standard Gaussian, so variance is bounded.
- **sub-VP SDE:** a variant of VP with the same drift but diffusion coefficient $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t \beta(s)ds})}$, designed so that the stochastic process has the same marginals as VP while remaining better conditioned for likelihood computation.

In all three cases, $p_T$ is close to $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, making the prior tractable.

### 2.2 Score matching and denoising

The *score* of a density $p$ is the vector field $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x})$ — the gradient of the log-likelihood, pointing toward regions of higher probability mass. Hyvärinen's *score matching* [7] showed that a parametric model $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})$ can be fit to the score without ever computing the partition function:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{SM}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x})}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x})\|^2 + \operatorname{tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}))\right]. \tag{3}$$

Vincent's *denoising score matching* [8] replaced the intractable trace term by perturbing data with noise $q_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} \mid \mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{x}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$ and regressing the *known* score of the perturbation kernel:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{DSM}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x}) q_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x})}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}) - \nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}} \log q_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} \mid \mathbf{x})\right\|^2\right], \tag{4}$$

where $\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}} \log q_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} \mid \mathbf{x}) = -(\tilde{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x})/\sigma^2$. Crucially, DSM with Gaussian noise is *equivalent* to score matching of the noise-perturbed distribution $q_{\sigma}$, up to a constant — a fact we will exploit at every noise scale.

### 2.3 Time reversal of diffusions

The mathematical engine of the whole framework is Anderson's time-reversal theorem [9]: under mild regularity, the time-reversed process $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t = \mathbf{x}_{T-t}$ of a diffusion (1) is itself a diffusion,

$$d\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t = \left[\mathbf{f}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t)\right]dt + g(t)\,d\bar{\mathbf{w}}_t, \tag{5}$$

where $\bar{\mathbf{w}}_t$ is a reverse-time Wiener process. This is the single most important equation in modern generative modeling: *the generative process differs from the noising process only through the score*. If we can estimate $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t$ at all times, we can simulate (5) backward from $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and obtain $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_0$.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Continuous-time denoising score matching

Given the forward SDE (1), its transition kernel $p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0)$ is Gaussian for the affine-drift processes used in practice. A time-dependent score network $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is trained with the *weighted* objective

$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,T]} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_0} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_t \sim p_{0t}(\cdot \mid \mathbf{x}_0)}\left[\lambda(t)\,\left\|\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0)\right\|^2\right], \tag{6}$$

where $\lambda(t) > 0$ is a positive weighting function and the target is the analytically known Gaussian score $-\big(\mathbf{x}_t - \boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_0, t)\big)/\sigma^2(t)$. Because the objective regresses a *conditional* score, it is exactly equivalent to matching the *marginal* score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$ — a direct consequence of the DSM identity applied per time $t$ [1, 8].

The choice of $\lambda(t)$ matters enormously. Song et al. [1] show that $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ makes (6) a *variational lower bound* on the log-likelihood of the plug-in reverse SDE, connecting score matching to maximum likelihood. In practice, modern implementations (e.g., Karras et al. [4]) use preconditioned variants; we discuss these in §4.3.

### 3.2 Noise prediction as score parameterization

DDPM [2] parameterizes the reverse mean via a noise-prediction network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ trained on

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\|^2\right], \quad \mathbf{x}_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}. \tag{7}$$

The connection to score matching is Tweedie's formula in disguise. For the VP process, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0) = -\boldsymbol{\epsilon}/\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}$, so

$$\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = -\frac{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}} \tag{8}$$

exactly converts a noise predictor into a score model. This equivalence is why a single trained network can serve both the DDPM ancestral sampler and any score-based sampler — they are different numerical solvers consuming the same estimated vector field.

### 3.3 The probability-flow ODE

A second consequence of the Fokker–Planck structure is that every reverse-time SDE admits a *deterministic* counterpart with identical marginals at all times [1]:

$$d\mathbf{x} = \left[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})\right]dt. \tag{9}$$

This *probability-flow ODE* (PF-ODE) is a neural ODE [10]: integrating it forward gives exact likelihoods via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula (at the cost of a trace estimator), and integrating it backward gives deterministic samples — enabling latent-space interpolation and exact inversion, properties stochastic samplers lack. The existence of (9) is the foundation of DDIM, EDM's Heun sampler, and consistency models.

```python
# Minimal probability-flow ODE sampling loop (VP process, Euler discretization)
import torch

def pf_ode_sample(score_net, beta_fn, shape, steps=100):
    x = torch.randn(shape)                       # x_T ~ N(0, I)
    t_grid = torch.linspace(1.0, 1e-3, steps)    # reverse time grid
    for t_prev, t in zip(t_grid[:-1], t_grid[1:]):
        dt = (t - t_prev).item()                 # negative step
        beta = beta_fn(t)
        drift = -0.5 * beta * x                  # f(x,t)
        score = score_net(x, t)                  # s_theta(x, t)
        x = x + (drift - 0.5 * beta * score) * dt  # Eq. (9), Euler
    return x
```

### 3.4 Training architecture: time-conditioned U-Nets

In image domains, $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot, t)$ is a U-Net with residual blocks, self-attention at low resolutions, and a sinusoidal timestep embedding injected via adaptive group normalization or FiLM conditioning [2, 4]. The conditioning must be expressive enough to cover the entire score trajectory — from near-delta scores at $t \to 0$ to near-Gaussian scores at $t \to T$. Modern practice preconditions the network as $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = c_{\mathrm{skip}}(t)\,\mathbf{x} + c_{\mathrm{out}}(t)\,F_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(c_{\mathrm{in}}(t)\mathbf{x}; c_{\mathrm{noise}}(t))$, keeping network inputs and targets unit-variance across all noise levels [4].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Process families: VE, VP, and sub-VP in detail

The three canonical SDEs induce qualitatively different score geometries:

| Process | Drift $\mathbf{f}$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | $\sigma^2(t)$ growth | Prior $p_T$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VE | $0$ | $\sqrt{d\sigma^2(t)/dt}$, $\sigma(t)=\sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t$ | unbounded, geometric | $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\max}^2 I)$ (approx.) |
| VP | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | bounded, $\to 1$ | $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ (exact limit) |
| sub-VP | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)(1-e^{-2\int_0^t\beta})}$ | bounded, slower than VP | $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ |

VE's unbounded variance suits Langevin correctors but inflates the score magnitude range; VP keeps all quantities $\mathcal{O}(1)$, stabilizing training and dominating large-scale text-to-image systems; sub-VP interpolates, improving likelihoods over VP [1].

> **Theorem (Marginal equivalence of VE/VP/sub-VP schedules):** For affine-drift SDEs with matched marginal variances, the transition kernels $p_{0t}$ are Gaussian with identical signal-to-noise ratios whenever $\bar{\alpha}_t$ and $\sigma(t)$ are matched through $\sigma^2(t) = (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)/\bar{\alpha}_t$. Hence a single score network, re-scaled by (8), can be transferred across discretizations of the same marginal schedule.

### 4.2 Sampler taxonomy: from ancestral sampling to exponential integrators

Once $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ is trained, sampling is *pure numerical analysis* — solving (5) or (9) backward in time. The major samplers:

1. **DDPM ancestral sampler** [2]: discretizes the reverse SDE with the exact Gaussian posterior $q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_0)$ re-parameterized through $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$:
   Requires hundreds to thousands of steps; the injected noise $\sigma_t \mathbf{z}$ at every step is both the source of diversity and the source of slowness.
2. **Predictor–Corrector (PC)** [1]: each reverse-SDE step (predictor, e.g., Euler–Maruyama) is followed by $M$ Langevin corrector steps using the same score network, driving samples toward $p_t$ before proceeding. Improves quality at fixed step count at the price of extra score evaluations.
3. **DDIM** [3]: generalizes DDPM with a family of *non-Markovian* forward processes indexed by $\eta \in [0,1]$. Setting $\eta = 0$ removes all stochasticity, yielding a deterministic sampler that is a discretization of the probability-flow ODE [11]; $\eta = 1$ recovers DDPM. The same trained $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ works for all $\eta$, enabling 10–50× fewer steps with graceful quality degradation.
4. **Exponential integrators / EDM** [4]: exploit the semi-linear structure of (9) by integrating the linear drift *exactly* and treating only the score term numerically. Karras et al.'s deterministic Heun second-order solver achieves CIFAR-10 FID 1.97 unconditional with just 35 network evaluations — a striking demonstration that most of diffusion sampling cost is discretization error, not model error.

```python
# DDIM deterministic update (eta = 0): one reverse step on the VP schedule
def ddim_step(x_t, eps_theta, alpha_bar_t, alpha_bar_prev):
    # Predicted clean data via Tweedie (Eq. 8 inverted)
    x0_pred = (x_t - (1 - alpha_bar_t) ** 0.5 * eps_theta) / (alpha_bar_t ** 0.5)
    # Deterministic step along the probability-flow ODE direction
    x_prev = (alpha_bar_prev ** 0.5) * x0_pred + (1 - alpha_bar_prev) ** 0.5 * eps_theta
    return x_prev  # eta=0: no fresh noise injected
```

### 4.3 Preconditioning and the EDM design space

Karras et al. [4] argue that much of diffusion practice is "unnecessarily convoluted" and decompose the pipeline into orthogonal choices: noise schedule $\sigma(t)$, network preconditioning $(c_{\mathrm{skip}}, c_{\mathrm{out}}, c_{\mathrm{in}}, c_{\mathrm{noise}})$, loss weighting $\lambda(\sigma)$, and sampler. Their key empirical findings:

- Training should sample $\log \sigma$ from a distribution emphasizing perceptually relevant noise levels (their $\ln \sigma \sim \mathcal{N}(-1.2, 1.2^2)$ for 32×32 images), rather than uniform time.
- The loss weight $\lambda(\sigma) = (\sigma^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{data}}^2)/(\sigma \cdot \sigma_{\mathrm{data}})^2$ equalizes the effective learning signal across noise levels.
- A deterministic second-order Heun sampler with a *polynomial* schedule $\sigma_i = (\sigma_{\max}^{1/\rho} + \tfrac{i}{N-1}(\sigma_{\min}^{1/\rho} - \sigma_{\max}^{1/\rho}))^{\rho}$, $\rho = 7$, concentrates steps where curvature is highest.

Applying only the improved sampler to a pre-trained DDPM++ model dropped ImageNet-64 FID from 2.07 to 1.55 without retraining [4].

### 4.4 Consistency models: distilling the probability-flow ODE

Consistency models (CMs) [5] push deterministic sampling to its limit: a single network evaluation. The observation is that the PF-ODE defines a *trajectory* from noise to data, and any two points $(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$, $(\mathbf{x}_{t'}, t')$ on the same trajectory share the same origin $\mathbf{x}_{\epsilon}$ (the trajectory's endpoint at minimal noise). A consistency function $\mathbf{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ is trained to satisfy the *self-consistency* property $\mathbf{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = \mathbf{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t'}, t')$ for all points on a trajectory, with boundary condition $\mathbf{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{\epsilon}, \epsilon) = \mathbf{x}_{\epsilon}$.

Two training regimes exist:

- **Consistency distillation (CD):** distill a pre-trained diffusion model's PF-ODE trajectories using an exponential moving average teacher; achieves one-step CIFAR-10 FID 3.55 and ImageNet-64 FID 6.20 [5].
- **Consistency training (CT):** train from data alone using the unbiased score estimator $\nabla \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0)$ — no teacher required, establishing CMs as an independent generative family.

Multistep CM sampling alternates denoising jumps and re-noising, recovering the compute–quality tradeoff of diffusion models, and inherits zero-shot editing (inpainting, colorization, super-resolution) from the PF-ODE structure.

### 4.5 Guidance, conditioning, and inverse problems

A major dividend of the score framework is *post-hoc conditioning*. Since the reverse SDE needs only $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t$, conditional generation reduces to score arithmetic:

- **Classifier guidance** [12]: $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x} \mid y) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(y \mid \mathbf{x})$, with a noise-robust classifier and guidance scale $w$.
- **Classifier-free guidance** [13]: train both conditional and unconditional score models (via conditioning dropout) and extrapolate, $\tilde{\mathbf{s}} = \mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{uncond}} + w(\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{cond}} - \mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{uncond}})$, $w > 1$. This single trick underlies virtually all modern text-to-image quality.
- **Inverse problems:** projecting each reverse step onto a measurement constraint yields inpainting and colorization — the seed of diffusion-based posterior sampling [1].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Representative empirical results

The SDE framework's empirical record, drawn from the primary sources [1–5], is summarized below. FID (Fréchet Inception Distance, lower is better) on CIFAR-10 is the common yardstick:

| Model | Sampler / setting | CIFAR-10 FID | NFE |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM [2] | ancestral, $T=1000$ | 3.17 | 1000 |
| Score SDE (VP, PC) [1] | predictor–corrector | 2.20 | ~2000 |
| DDIM [3] | deterministic, $\eta=0$ | 4.04–4.67 | 50–100 |
| EDM [4] | Heun 2nd order | 1.97 (uncond.) / 1.79 (cond.) | 35 |
| Consistency model (CD) [5] | one-step | 3.55 | 1 |
| Consistency model (CD) [5] | two-step | 2.93 | 2 |

Two orders of magnitude fewer evaluations for equal or better quality within three years, driven almost entirely by *solver* improvements rather than larger networks.

### 5.2 Convergence theory

Recent work has placed the samplers on rigorous footing. Nakano [14] proves the original DDPM sampling sequence *weakly converges* to the data distribution as the number of steps $T \to \infty$, under asymptotic conditions on the schedule, the $L^2$ score-estimation error, and the noise predictor. Earlier analyses established non-asymptotic bounds: under Lipschitz scores and bounded second moments, the KL divergence between the sampled and true distributions decomposes into

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_0 \,\|\, \hat{p}_0) \lesssim \underbrace{\text{score error}}_{\propto \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta})} + \underbrace{\text{discretization error}}_{\propto \Delta t \text{ or } \Delta t^2} + \underbrace{\text{prior mismatch}}_{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0,I))}, \tag{11}$$

with the discretization term improvable from $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)$ (Euler–Maruyama) to $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$ (exponential integrators/Heun) — precisely the ordering observed empirically in §4.2. Complementary work shows the DSM objective upper-bounds a variational gap on the model log-likelihood when $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ [1], and that score matching with early stopping is minimax-optimal for certain distribution classes.

> **Theorem (Weak convergence of DDPM sampling [14]):** Let $\{\mathbf{x}^{(T)}_t\}$ be the DDPM reverse chain with $T$ steps. Under regularity of $p_0$, a variance schedule with $\beta_t \to 0$ appropriately, and $L^2$-consistent score estimates, the terminal law converges weakly to $p_0$ as $T \to \infty$. The proof identifies the chain as a time-inhomogeneous exponential integrator for the reverse SDE (5).

### 5.3 Likelihood computation

The PF-ODE (9) plus the instantaneous change-of-variables formula gives *exact* likelihoods (up to ODE-solver and trace-estimator error):

$$\log p_0(\mathbf{x}_0) = \log p_T(\mathbf{x}_T) + \int_0^T \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\,dt, \tag{12}$$

where $\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ is the PF-ODE drift with the learned score and the divergence is estimated by Hutchinson's trace estimator [1]. Reported bits/dim: 2.99 on CIFAR-10 (VP-continuous, [1]), competitive with autoregressive models while retaining one-pass sampling.

## 6. Limitations

**The manifold hypothesis and score blow-up.** Real data concentrates near low-dimensional manifolds, where $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t$ grows like $\sigma(t)^{-1}$ normal to the manifold as $t \to 0$. Score networks must represent enormous, rapidly varying vector fields from finite samples — the fundamental statistical bottleneck. Current theory (Lipschitz, log-concave, or bounded-support assumptions) does not cover the manifold regime where the models actually operate.

**Compounding discretization error.** Error analysis (11) is worst-case additive over steps; in practice, early-step errors (at large $t$, where the score is smooth but the prior mismatch concentrates) propagate nonlinearly. Adaptive step-size control for the PF-ODE exists but is rarely used because each rejected step wastes score evaluations.

**The likelihood–quality gap.** Models with the best FIDs (EDM, heavily guided text-to-image models) often have *worse* likelihoods than their unguided counterparts, and likelihood is sensitive to the trace estimator's variance. The community still lacks a single objective that jointly optimizes perceptual quality and density estimation.

**Memorization and privacy.** Diffusion models memorize training samples at rates comparable to GANs; the score at small $t$ is essentially a nearest-neighbor field over the training set. Differentially private score matching remains nascent.

**Compute and carbon.** Training state-of-the-art models requires thousands of GPU-days, and sampling — while vastly improved — still costs 1–50 network evaluations per image versus one for GANs. Consistency models mitigate this but at some quality cost in the one-step regime.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The SDE formulation of Song et al. [1] did for diffusion models what the variational bound did for VAEs: it exposed the moving parts. The forward SDE is fixed and boring by design; all intelligence lives in the estimated score; and sampling is numerical analysis applied to two canonical equations — the reverse-time SDE (5) and the probability-flow ODE (9). From this vantage point, DDPM is Euler–Maruyama on (5), DDIM is a first-order discretization of (9), EDM is a second-order exponential integrator with better preconditioning, and consistency models are the boundary-value distillation of (9) to a single step.

The practical moral is striking: the last three years of progress came predominantly from *solvers and schedules*, not from bigger networks — FID 3.17 at 1000 NFE to FID 1.79 at 35 NFE on the same benchmark family. The theoretical moral is equally clear: time reversal of diffusions, a classical result of stochastic analysis [9], turned out to be the right abstraction for learning to generate. Open frontiers — manifold-aware score estimation, adaptive solvers with error control, unified quality–likelihood objectives, and private training — are where the next order-of-magnitude gains will come from. The noise, it turns out, was the signal all along.
