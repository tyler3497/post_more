# IO-Aware Exact Attention: FlashAttention Tiling, Online Softmax, and the HBM–SRAM Memory Hierarchy

## Abstract

Self-attention dominates the cost of modern Transformers, yet its quadratic time and memory complexity is only part of the story: on contemporary GPUs, *wall-clock* performance is governed not by floating-point throughput but by data movement between high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and on-chip SRAM. This thesis develops the theory and practice of **IO-aware exact attention**, centered on the FlashAttention family [1][2][3]. We formalize the GPU memory hierarchy as a two-level red–blue pebble game [5], derive the block-tiling schedule that keeps hot operands resident in SRAM, and show how **online softmax** [4] permits exact softmax normalization over sequentially processed row blocks. We prove that tiled attention achieves $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$ HBM accesses — versus $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ for the naive formulation — and is optimal for a broad range of SRAM sizes $M$. We analyze the backward pass under recomputation, the parallelism redesign of FlashAttention-2, and the asynchronous warp-specialized execution of FlashAttention-3 on Hopper GPUs. Empirically, these methods yield 2–4$\times$ wall-clock speedups and up to 20$\times$ memory reduction at sequences up to 64K, with throughput approaching 75% of peak device FLOPs [3].

## 1. Introduction

The Transformer [6] is the universal substrate of contemporary machine learning, yet scaled dot-product attention exhibits time and memory complexity quadratic in sequence length $N$. For years the community's response was *approximate* attention — sparse patterns, low-rank factorizations, kernelized estimators — trading quality for asymptotic FLOPs. A striking empirical fact punctures this narrative: many approximate mechanisms, despite superior big-O complexity, are **slower in wall-clock time** than exact attention on modern hardware [1]. The resolution is that counting FLOPs is the wrong cost model. GPUs are not FLOPs-limited for attention; they are *memory-bandwidth-limited*.

Consider the NVIDIA A100: 312 dense TFLOPs of FP16 Tensor Core throughput against roughly 1,555 GB/s of HBM2 bandwidth (2,039 GB/s on the 80 GB SXM variant), with per-SM SRAM of 164 KB and a 40 MB L2 [7]. The naive implementation materializes the full $N \times N$ score matrix $S$ and probability matrix $P$ in HBM, forcing $\Theta(N^2)$ elements through a bus three orders of magnitude slower than on-chip SRAM. The missing principle, articulated in [1] and refined in [2][3], is **IO-awareness**: design the algorithm around *reads and writes between memory-hierarchy levels*, minimizing HBM accesses even at the cost of extra FLOPs.

This thesis presents a unified treatment of IO-aware exact attention: (i) a formalization of attention's IO complexity under the Hong–Kung red–blue pebble game [5], with lower bounds and the optimality regime of tiling; (ii) a self-contained derivation of **online softmax** [4] as the streaming reduction enabling exact blocked softmax; (iii) analysis of the FlashAttention forward and backward schedules, including the recomputation trick eliminating $O(N^2)$ storage; (iv) the architectural evolution through FlashAttention-2 and FlashAttention-3; and (v) a synthesis of the empirical record.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Scaled Dot-Product Attention

Given $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ derived from input $X$, attention computes

$$\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V,$$

with row-wise softmax. The naive implementation forms $S = QK^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, then $P = \mathrm{softmax}(S)$ and $O = PV$. FLOPs scale as $O(N^2 d)$ but, critically, memory as $O(N^2)$: both $S$ and $P$ are stored in HBM because backpropagation needs $P$ (or its recomputation) for gradients with respect to $Q$, $K$, and $V$.

### 2.2 The GPU Memory Hierarchy

| Level | A100 (Ampere) | H100 (Hopper) | Bandwidth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registers (per SM) | 256 KB | 256 KB | tens of TB/s |
| Shared memory / L1 (per SM) | up to 164 KB | up to 228 KB of 256 KB | tens of TB/s |
| L2 cache | 40 MB | 50 MB | several TB/s |
| HBM (device) | 40/80 GB HBM2/HBM2e | 80 GB HBM3 | 1.5–3.35 TB/s [7][8] |

Aggregate SRAM on an A100 is roughly **18 MB** — less than a single $N \times N$ attention matrix at $N = 8192$ in FP16 (134 MB). Everything that does not fit in SRAM transits the HBM bus, and this transit dominates runtime for memory-bound kernels. The FlashAttention papers model this as a **two-level hierarchy**: fast, small SRAM of size $M$ versus slow, large HBM, with HBM accesses as the cost metric.

### 2.3 The Red–Blue Pebble Game and IO Complexity

Hong and Kung's red–blue pebble game [5] formalizes IO complexity: a computation DAG is pebbled with red pebbles (fast memory, at most $M$) and blue pebbles (slow memory); placing a red pebble on a node whose predecessors are blue incurs IO cost. Applied to attention [1]: standard attention needs $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ HBM accesses (reading $Q, K, V$; writing $S$, $P$, $O$), while tiled attention achieves $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ and is provably optimal when $d \le M \le Nd$. The gap is largest exactly where it matters: long sequences with modest head dimension $d$ and realistic SRAM sizes $M$.

### 2.4 Online Softmax

Softmax couples all elements of a row, seemingly forbidding block-wise computation: the normalizer $\ell(x) = \sum_i e^{x_i - m(x)}$ depends on the global row maximum $m(x)$. Milakov and Gimelshein [4] showed both statistics can be maintained **incrementally**. For $x = [x^{(1)}, x^{(2)}]$ with per-block maxima $m^{(j)}$ and normalizers $\ell^{(j)}$:

$$m = \max(m^{(1)}, m^{(2)}), \qquad \ell = e^{m^{(1)} - m}\ell^{(1)} + e^{m^{(2)} - m}\ell^{(2)},$$

and $\mathrm{softmax}(x^{(j)}) = e^{x^{(j)} - m} / \ell$. This *online* (safe) softmax is numerically stable, exact, and streaming — the primitive that lets attention accumulate over key/value blocks without materializing full score rows.

### 2.5 Tiling and Recomputation

Tiling partitions operands so each block fits in fast memory; recomputation (gradient checkpointing) discards intermediates and regenerates them in the backward pass, trading FLOPs for memory. FlashAttention's insight is that for attention these compose into something qualitatively new: the backward pass needs only the $O(N)$ softmax statistics $(m, \ell)$ — not the $O(N^2)$ matrix $P$ — so recomputation converts a quadratic-memory algorithm into a linear-memory one while tiling simultaneously slashes HBM traffic.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The Tiled Forward Pass

Let $M$ be the SRAM capacity in elements. Partition $Q$ into $T_r = \lceil N/B_r \rceil$ blocks $Q_i \in \mathbb{R}^{B_r \times d}$ and $K, V$ into $T_c = \lceil N/B_c \rceil$ blocks $K_j, V_j$. SRAM must hold one $Q$, one $K$, one $V$, and one output tile — four $B \times d$ blocks — hence

$$B_c = \left\lceil \frac{M}{4d} \right\rceil, \qquad B_r = \min\left(\left\lceil \frac{M}{4d} \right\rceil, d\right).$$

For each query block $i$ (outer loop, resident in SRAM across the inner loop), iterate over key/value blocks $j$, maintaining running statistics $m_i, \ell_i$ and partial output $O_i$:

```python
# FlashAttention forward pass (schematic, after Dao et al. [1], Alg. 1)
O = zeros(N, d);  l = zeros(N);  m = full(N, -inf)   # HBM
for i in range(Tr):                                   # query blocks
    Qi = load(Q[i*Br:(i+1)*Br, :])                    # HBM -> SRAM
    Oi = zeros(Br, d);  mi = full(Br, -inf);  li = zeros(Br)
    for j in range(Tc):                               # key/value blocks
        Kj = load(K[j*Bc:(j+1)*Bc, :])                # HBM -> SRAM
        Vj = load(V[j*Bc:(j+1)*Bc, :])                # HBM -> SRAM
        Sij = Qi @ Kj.T / sqrt(d)                     # SRAM GEMM
        mij_prev = mi
        mi  = maximum(mi, rowmax(Sij))                 # online update
        Pij = exp(Sij - mi[:, None])                   # safe softmax
        li  = exp(mij_prev - mi) * li + rowsum(Pij)
        Oi  = diag(exp(mij_prev - mi)) @ Oi + Pij @ Vj
    O[i*Br:(i+1)*Br, :] = Oi;  l[...] = li;  m[...] = mi  # -> HBM
return O, l, m
```

$S_{ij}$ and $P_{ij}$ exist **only in SRAM**, never touching HBM. On completion, $O_i = \mathrm{softmax}(Q_i K^\top/\sqrt{d})\,V$ exactly — the rescaling $\mathrm{diag}(e^{m^{\mathrm{prev}} - m})$ retroactively corrects the accumulator whenever a larger row maximum appears. This is exact attention: the online softmax decomposition is an algebraic identity, not an approximation.

### 3.2 IO Complexity Analysis

The outer loop loads each $Q$ block once ($O(Nd)$). The inner loop loads each $K$ and $V$ block once *per query block*: $T_r T_c B_c d = \Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ elements, since $T_r T_c B_c = N^2/B_r$ and $B_r = \Theta(M/d)$. Writing $O, \ell, m$ costs $O(Nd)$.

> **Theorem:** (IO complexity of tiled exact attention [1]). For SRAM size $M$ with $d \le M \le Nd$, FlashAttention requires $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ HBM accesses, versus $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ for standard attention. Moreover, $\Omega(N^2 d^2/M)$ is a lower bound for *any* exact attention algorithm in this regime, so FlashAttention is asymptotically optimal.

*Proof sketch.* The lower bound follows from the red–blue pebble game on the attention DAG: each of the $N^2$ scores $S_{ij} = \langle q_i, k_j \rangle$ is a $d$-dimensional dot product, and a dominator-set argument shows any schedule with $M$ fast-memory slots moves $\Omega(N^2 d^2/M)$ elements across the HBM–SRAM boundary when $d \le M \le Nd$ [1, Thm. 1]. The tiled schedule attains it. ∎

The condition $M \ge d$ is easily satisfied ($d = 64$–$128$ versus hundreds of kilobytes of SRAM), and $M \le Nd$ holds for realistic sequences. With $M \approx 100$ KB and $d = 64$, tiling moves roughly an order of magnitude fewer bytes than naive materialization.

### 3.3 The Backward Pass via Recomputation

Backpropagation needs, per row, the probability row $p_i$ and $dO_i = \partial L / \partial O_i$. Instead of storing $P \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, FlashAttention stores only the $O(N)$ statistics $m, \ell$ and **recomputes** $P_{ij}$ block-by-block in SRAM. With $dP = dO\,V^\top$:

$$dV = P^\top dO, \quad dS = P \circ (dP - \mathrm{rowsum}(dP \circ P)\mathbf{1}^\top), \quad dQ = dS\, K, \quad dK = dS^\top Q,$$

each evaluated in tiled fashion. The backward pass inherits $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$ IO complexity with $O(Nd)$ footprint — a **20$\times$ memory reduction** over baselines at long sequences [1]. The extra compute is roughly one additional forward pass, but HBM traffic — the binding constraint — drops by an order of magnitude.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Online Softmax as a Streaming Reduction

The online softmax is the single mathematical fact unlocking the construction. For a processed prefix with statistics $(m, \ell)$, a new block $x^{(k)}$ with maximum $m^{(k)}$ and normalizer $\ell^{(k)}$ updates via

$$m^{\mathrm{new}} = \max(m, m^{(k)}), \qquad \ell^{\mathrm{new}} = \ell\, e^{m - m^{\mathrm{new}}} + \ell^{(k)} e^{m^{(k)} - m^{\mathrm{new}}},$$

in $O(1)$ per row per block. Three consequences: (i) **no block-ordering constraints** — blocks may be processed in any order, enabling FlashAttention-2's parallel schedules; (ii) **inherited numerical stability** — every exponent is relative to the running maximum, so overflow is impossible in any precision; (iii) **generality** — the same streaming pattern underlies FA3's FP8 kernels and online formulations of attention variants. The abstraction: *softmax normalization is a monoid over row-blocks*, and FlashAttention is its tiled fold.

### 4.2 Tiling Schedules and the SRAM Budget

With $M \approx 50$K FP16 elements (100 KB) and $d = 64$, $B_c = \lceil M/4d \rceil \approx 195$, rounded to a Tensor Core multiple (128/64 on Ampere). Larger $B_c$ amortizes kernel overhead and improves GEMM arithmetic intensity but coarsens load balance at small $N$. The outer loop is over query blocks because $O_i, m_i, \ell_i$ are accumulators staying SRAM-resident across the inner sweep — each $Q$ element loads exactly once, while $K, V$ reload $T_r$ times, the source of the $N^2 d^2/M$ term.

Production kernels pipeline HBM→SRAM copies of $K_{j+1}, V_{j+1}$ (via `cp.async` on Ampere) with the GEMM on $K_j, V_j$, hiding copy latency behind compute. The IO model counts *volume*, but wall-clock depends on *overlap* — a theme FlashAttention-3 elevates to a first-class design principle.

### 4.3 The Backward Pass Without Materialization

The backward pass is where IO-awareness pays most, since training — not inference — is where the $O(N^2)$ memory wall bites. One subtlety: $dK$ and $dV$ accumulate over *query* blocks, so updates for the same $j$ contend across thread blocks processing different $i$. Implementations use atomics or transpose the loop nest for the $dK/dV$ phase. FlashAttention-2 resolves this structurally by parallelizing over the sequence dimension — one thread block per (head, query-block) pair — a key source of its speedup.

### 4.4 Beyond FlashAttention: FA2, FA3, and the Hopper Generation

**FlashAttention-2** [2] keeps the IO-optimal tiling but re-engineers parallelism: sequence-dimension parallelism (one CTA per query block) instead of batch×heads, fewer non-GEMM FLOPs in the inner loop, and balanced partitioning of the causal-mask triangular workload. Result: ~**2$\times$ FlashAttention**, ~230 TFLOPs/s on A100 (~70% of FP16 peak), up to 9$\times$ a PyTorch baseline.

**FlashAttention-3** [3] targets Hopper: *warp specialization* (producer warps issuing TMA loads while consumer warps drive WGMMA), multi-stage pipelining, and FP8 with block-wise quantization plus incoherent processing. FA3 reaches ~**75% of peak FLOPs** on H100, 1.5–2$\times$ over FA2. The arc is clear: each generation extracts performance from the *schedule* — parallelism, overlap, asynchrony — while asymptotic HBM complexity stays fixed. The same discipline (tile to SRAM, stream reductions online, recompute rather than store) now underpins fused MLPs, state-space scans, and KV-cache management: IO-awareness is a methodology for the memory-wall era, not an attention trick.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

### 5.1 Complexity Comparison

| Algorithm | HBM accesses | Footprint | Exact? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard attention | $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ | $\Theta(N^2)$ | Yes |
| FlashAttention [1] | $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$ | $\Theta(Nd)$ | Yes |
| FlashAttention-2 [2] | $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$ | $\Theta(Nd)$ | Yes |
| Block-sparse FlashAttention [1] | pattern-dependent $O(N\sqrt{N}\,d^2/M)$ | $\Theta(Nd)$ | Approximate |

### 5.2 Wall-Clock and Memory Measurements

On A100 GPUs, Dao et al. [1] report: **15% end-to-end speedup on BERT-large** (512) versus the MLPerf 1.1 record; **3$\times$ on GPT-2** (1K); **2.4$\times$ on Long-Range Arena** (1K–4K). Memory grows linearly with $N$, up to **20$\times$ smaller** than exact baselines; all baselines except Linformer exhaust A100 memory before $N = 64$K. Longer contexts enabled new capabilities: first Transformers beating chance on Path-X ($N = 16$K, 61.4%) and Path-256 ($N = 64$K, 63.1%), 0.7 better GPT-2 perplexity, and 6.4 points of lift on long-document classification [1].

FlashAttention-2 [2] doubles FlashAttention's throughput (~230 TFLOPs/s FP16 on A100); FlashAttention-3 [3] reaches ~75% of H100 peak FLOPs. That asymptotic HBM complexity has been unchanged since [1] while throughput keeps climbing is itself evidence for this thesis: once HBM traffic is minimized, gains come from refining the IO-aware cost model, not the FLOP count.

### 5.3 Optimality

The red–blue pebble argument [1, §3.2][5] gives an $\Omega(N^2 d^2/M)$ lower bound for exact attention when $d \le M \le Nd$ — containing every practical deployment ($d \in \{64,128\}$, SRAM in the hundreds of KB). Tiled attention is thus *asymptotically IO-optimal*. Few deep-learning primitives enjoy matching upper and lower bounds; attention, reformulated as an IO problem, is one of them.

## 6. Limitations

- **Quadratic compute remains.** IO-awareness cuts HBM traffic, not the $\Theta(N^2 d)$ FLOPs. At $N \gg 100$K, compute reasserts itself and subquadratic approximations regain relevance.
- **The decode regime differs.** Batch-1 autoregressive decoding is dominated by KV-cache and weight loading, not attention GEMMs; paged KV-caches and speculative decoding address a different bottleneck.
- **Hardware specificity.** Tiling parameters, pipelining, and parallel decomposition are co-designed with specific GPUs (Ampere `cp.async`, Hopper TMA/WGMMA). Porting performance to other accelerators requires re-deriving the schedule.
- **Precision and head-dimension pressure.** Large $d$ shrinks $B_c = \lceil M/4d \rceil$, eroding Tensor Core efficiency; FP8 variants [3] recover throughput but need careful block-wise quantization. Causal masking and grouped-query attention each perturb the ideal schedule.
- **Linear, not constant, memory.** $\Theta(Nd)$ storage plus the KV-cache still grows with context; million-token training additionally needs sequence parallelism and offloading.

---

## 7. Conclusion

FlashAttention reframed attention optimization from *FLOP minimization* to *IO minimization*, resolving the paradox of asymptotically superior yet slower approximate methods. The construction rests on three durable ideas: **tiling**, confining hot data to SRAM; **online softmax**, turning global normalization into a streaming monoid over row-blocks; and **recomputation**, converting quadratic memory into linear memory with FLOPs the hardware has in abundance. The Hong–Kung analysis certifies the schedule as IO-optimal across the practical SRAM regime.

The subsequent evolution — FA2's work partitioning, FA3's asynchronous warp-specialized Hopper execution — shows the IO-aware viewpoint has generative power: each generation found performance in the *schedule* while asymptotic HBM complexity stood fixed. As accelerators widen the compute–bandwidth gap (H100: ~3.35 TB/s HBM3 against soaring Tensor Core FLOPs [8]), IO-awareness only grows in importance. The fastest algorithm, in the memory-wall era, is the one that moves the least data.
