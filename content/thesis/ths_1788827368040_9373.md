# Compositional Deep Learning via Applied Category Theory: Monoidal Functors, String Diagrams, Parametric Lenses, Reverse Derivative Categories, and Sheaf Neural Networks

## Abstract

Modern deep learning composes with exquisite practical reliability, yet the mathematics of that composition lags behind the engineering. This thesis argues that applied category theory supplies the missing foundation. Supervised learning admits a genuinely compositional semantics: gradient descent defines a monoidal functor from parametrized functions to update rules, so training a composite network is the composite of its parts' training procedures [1]. We extend this through the 2-category **Para** and the ICML position that monads valued in parametric maps subsume the architectural zoo, from RNNs to transformers [3]. Backpropagation becomes the *reverse derivative* of a Cartesian reverse differential category [4], packaged with its forward map as a *lens* — a bidirectional optic [2,7] — recovering Adam, AdaGrad, and Nesterov momentum in one uniform framework. Finally we treat sheaf neural networks [5]: cellular sheaves on graphs give stalks, restriction maps, and a sheaf Laplacian whose diffusion provably defeats oversmoothing and heterophily. We close with a research program for compositional machine intelligence.

## 1 Introduction

Deep neural networks are among the most compositional artifacts ever engineered. Practitioners routinely *fuse* layers, *stack* residual blocks, *branch* attention heads in parallel, *share* parameters across time steps, and *compose* entire pretrained backbones with new heads — and these operations behave predictably. Yet the dominant mathematical account of a neural network is an unstructured tuple of weight matrices, for which composition is an accident of linear algebra rather than a governed law. Category theory is the mathematics of *things that compose* — objects, morphisms, and the laws of their composition — and the applied category theory program asks whether that discipline can govern engineering artifacts [1].

The thesis of this thesis is affirmative for deep learning. Our contributions are as follows:

1. We present **supervised learning as functorial**: gradient descent defines a *monoidal functor* **Para(Euc) → Learn**, making backpropagation's compositionality a theorem rather than a heuristic [1].
2. We develop the **2-categorical Para construction** and the **algebraic theory of architectures** [3]: the universal algebra of monads valued in parametric maps encodes geometric deep learning constraints and RNN implementations alike.
3. We give the **lens-theoretic account of backpropagation**: each differentiable map carries a forward map and a *reverse derivative* [4]; together they form a lens [2], and Adam, AdaGrad, and Nesterov momentum emerge as lenses on parameter space [7].
4. We treat **sheaf neural networks** [5]: stalks, restriction maps, and the sheaf Laplacian give message passing a principled *transport* geometry that defeats oversmoothing and heterophily.
5. We formalize the **string-diagram calculus**: layers are boxes, data are wires, and batching, branching, and depth are tensor products and compositions in a symmetric monoidal category.

> **Thesis statement:** A neural network is not a list of matrices; it is a morphism in a well-chosen category, its training procedure is a functor, and its diagrams are a proof calculus. Compositionality is not a style — it is the structure.

Throughout, we distinguish this work from the parallel application of category theory to *databases*: functorial data migration [cf. the companion post in this series] concerns schema functors and migration adjunctions between instance categories. Here, the objects are Euclidean spaces and parameter spaces, the morphisms are parametrized smooth maps and their reverse derivatives, and the diagrams are string diagrams of *learning machines*, not of schemas.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Categories, functors, and monoidal structure

A **category** consists of objects, morphisms between objects, an identity morphism on each object, and associative composition. A **functor** maps objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving identities and composition. A **symmetric monoidal category** additionally carries a tensor product ⊗ with unit object *I*, letting us run processes in parallel; the coherence theorems of Mac Lane justify **string diagrams**, the graphical calculus in which boxes are morphisms, wires are objects, horizontal juxtaposition is composition, and vertical juxtaposition is tensor product.

### 2.2 The Para construction

Given a symmetric monoidal category **C** and a symmetric monoidal subcategory **M** of "parameter spaces" acting on **C**, the **bicategory Para(M, C)** has the same objects as **C**, but a 1-morphism *X → Y* is a pair *(P, f)* with *f : P ⊗ X → Y* [1]. Two-morphisms are reparameterizations: a morphism *r : P' → P* in **M** satisfying the evident triangle law. Composition of *(P, f : P ⊗ X → Y)* with *(Q, g : Q ⊗ Y → Z)* is *(Q ⊗ P, h)* where *h* runs *f* and then *g*, re-associating the parameters.

This is the natural home of neural networks: each layer is a map from parameters ⊗ inputs to outputs, and a whole network is a single 1-morphism whose parameter space is the tensor of the layer parameter spaces. **Para** is in fact a 2-category (under mild conditions), and its richer 2-categorical structure — reparameterizations as 2-cells — models weight sharing and transfer learning [3].

### 2.3 Reverse derivatives and lenses

Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras introduced a category **Learn** whose morphisms are *update rules*: a morphism *P → P'* consists of a parameter space, an implementation function, an update function, and crucially a **request function** *r : P × B → A* that, given the current parameter and a desired output, computes the input that should have been supplied — the categorical essence of the backward pass [1].

A cleaner formulation replaces the request function by the **reverse derivative**. In a Cartesian reverse differential category (CRDC), every map *f : A → B* carries *R[f] : A × B → A*, sending *(x, v)* to *J[f]ᵀ(x)·v*: the transposed Jacobian applied to a cotangent vector [4]. The pair *(f, R[f])* is exactly a **lens**: a forward map *get* and a backward map *put* threading a residual [2,7]. Lenses are the Cartesian case of **optics**, the general bidirectional accessor construction of Riley [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is axiomatic and diagrammatic. We identify, for each deep learning phenomenon, the minimal categorical structure that governs it, state the structure as definitions and axioms, prove that the phenomenon instantiates them, and then *use* the abstract structure to derive consequences — new optimizers, new architectures, new guarantees. Concretely:

1. **Axiomatize the forward pass.** Architectures live in **Para(M, C)**; the tensor product governs parallel structure (attention heads, branching), composition governs depth, and 2-cells govern reparameterization (weight tying).
2. **Axiomatize the backward pass.** Differentiation lives in a Cartesian reverse differential category [4]; the backward pass is a lens; optimizers are lenses on parameter space composed with model lenses [2].
3. **Axiomatize transport on graphs.** Message passing lives in the category of cellular sheaves on a graph; restriction maps transport features between stalks; diffusion is governed by the sheaf Laplacian [5].
4. **Verify empirically.** We cite the experimental record — from the Python implementation of categorical learners in Cruttwell et al. [2] to the heterophily benchmarks of neural sheaf diffusion [5] — and sketch how the abstractions compile to runnable code.
5. **Bound the claims.** Section 6 lists what the categorical picture does *not* yet explain: generalization, scaling laws, and optimization dynamics remain substantially outside the compositional frame.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Backpropagation as a monoidal functor

The founding result of the field is due to Fong, Spivak, and Tuyéras [1]. They define two categories:

| Category | Objects | Morphisms |
|---|---|---|
| **Para(Euc)** | Euclidean spaces | Parametrized functions *(P, f : P × A → B)* |
| **Learn** | Parameter spaces | Learners: *(P, I, U, r)* — implementation, update, request |

> **Theorem:** *Gradient descent — with respect to a fixed step size and an error function satisfying a certain property (a quadratic error, in the paper's main case) — defines a monoidal functor from a category of parametrized functions to this category of update rules* [1].

The functoriality equations are the whole story:

- *F(g ∘ f) = F(g) ∘ F(f)* — training the composite equals composing the training procedures;
- *F(id) = id*;
- *F(f ⊗ g) = F(f) ⊗ F(g)* — training parallel branches in parallel equals training them separately and tensoring.

In string-diagram language, the network diagram *is* the training diagram: the same boxes and wires, transported by the functor into the world of learners. This is not a metaphor. It is why backpropagation "just works" through arbitrary architectures — the chain rule is the functor's composition-preservation law.

### 4.2 The algebraic theory of all architectures

Gavranović, Lessard, Dudzik, von Glehn, Araújo, and Veličković advanced a striking position at ICML 2024: *categorical deep learning is an algebraic theory of all architectures* [3]. Their proposal is to apply the universal algebra of **monads**, but valued in the 2-category **Para** rather than in **Set**.

The architecture of the argument is:

1. **Constraints as monad algebras.** Geometric deep learning [6] specifies architectures by *equivariance constraints*: a CNN is a map equivariant to translations. Here, an equivariance constraint is an algebra for a monad (e.g., a group-action monad), and an architecture satisfying it is an *algebra homomorphism* [3].
2. **Implementations as (co)algebras.** RNNs are *algebras and coalgebras* for endofunctors in **Para**: folding RNNs are algebras for *1 + A × −*, unfolding RNNs are coalgebras, and Mealy/Moore machines appear as coalgebras for richer functors [3].
3. **One bridge.** Because constraints and implementations live in the same 2-categorical world, there is finally a *coherent bridge between specifying what a model must satisfy and how it is implemented* — the elusive quest the paper's title names [3].

This subsumes geometric deep learning rather than replacing it: the GDL blueprint [6] — grids, groups, graphs, geodesics, gauges — becomes the study of which monads induce which equivariances, and which architectures are their algebras. The practical upshot is a *specification language* in which illegal designs (e.g., a supposedly translation-equivariant layer that secretly breaks equivariance) are *type errors* [3].

### 4.3 Lenses, optics, and the categorical semantics of optimizers

Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson, and Zanasi gave the field its most complete working semantics [2]. Their framework rests on three pillars:

- **Parametrized maps** (the **Para** construction) for models;
- **Lenses** for the forward/backward pair;
- **Cartesian reverse derivative categories** for differentiation [4].

A *learner* in their sense is a **parametric lens**: a lens *(f, R[f])* equipped with a parameter space, so that the forward map has type *P × A → B* and the backward map threads the residual through *R[f]*. Composition of parametric lenses is composition of the underlying lenses; the chain rule is the lens composition law.

The payoff is a **uniform taxonomy of optimizers**. Gradient descent, Nesterov momentum, AdaGrad, and Adam are all *lenses on parameter space*: they differ only in their *state* (AdaGrad and Adam carry running statistics as lens state) and their update maps. Because optimizers are lenses and models are lenses, *model ⊕ optimizer* is just lens composition — and the framework proves, once, that the composite behaves correctly, rather than once per optimizer [2]. The authors further demonstrate the framework beyond the continuous world: their semantics also instantiates in the category of **boolean circuits** (the *POLY(Z₂)* example), giving a compositional account of learning with discrete parameters [2].

In Haskell-flavored pseudocode, the pattern is:

```haskell
-- A lens: forward map plus backward (reverse-derivative) map
data Lens a b s t = Lens
  { get :: a -> b        -- forward pass
  , put :: a -> t -> s   -- backward pass: input, output-cotangent -> input-cotangent
  }

-- A parametric lens adds a parameter space P
data ParaLens p a b = ParaLens
  { params :: p
  , lens   :: Lens (p, a) (p, b) b a
  }

-- Composition of learners is composition of lenses: the chain rule, categorically
composeLearners :: ParaLens p a b -> ParaLens q b c -> ParaLens (q, p) a c
composeLearners l1 l2 = ParaLens (params l2, params l1) (lens l2 `compose` lens l1)
```

Optimizers slot into the same interface: an optimizer is a lens whose "forward" map is the identity on parameters and whose "backward" map performs the parameter update, possibly maintaining momentum or second-moment state in the lens residual.

### 4.4 Sheaf neural networks: stalks, restriction maps, and the sheaf Laplacian

Graph neural networks suffer from two characteristic pathologies. **Oversmoothing**: repeated message passing collapses node representations toward a constant, so depth destroys information. **Heterophily**: when neighbors tend to belong to *different* classes, averaging neighbor features is actively harmful. Both stem from the same modeling choice: the graph Laplacian diffuses features as if every edge transported opinions unchanged and symmetrically.

**Cellular sheaf theory** replaces this with geometry. A cellular sheaf *F* on a graph *G = (V, E)* assigns [5]:

- to each vertex *v*, a **stalk** *F(v)* — a vector space of "private opinions";
- to each edge *e*, a **stalk** *F(e)* — a "public discourse space";
- to each incident pair *v ◁ e*, a linear **restriction map** *F_{v◁e} : F(v) → F(e)* translating private opinion into public discourse.

The **coboundary** *δ : C⁰(G, F) → C¹(G, F)* measures disagreement across edges; the **sheaf Laplacian** *Δ_F = δᵀδ* generalizes the graph Laplacian, reducing to it when all stalks are ℝ with identity restriction maps [5]. Diffusion under the sheaf heat equation *∂X/∂t = −Δ_F X* converges to the **harmonic space** *H⁰(G, F)* — which, unlike the constant signals of graph diffusion, can be high-dimensional and class-separating. Bodnar et al. proved that with *O(d)*-valued restriction maps, sheaf diffusion **linearly separates classes in heterophilic regimes** where graph diffusion provably oversmooths, and that with sufficient capacity an MLP can *learn* the sheaf's restriction maps end-to-end from data [5].

The categorical moral is sharp: a GNN layer is not "aggregate then transform" but **transport along restriction maps, then diffuse under the sheaf Laplacian**. The architecture's inductive bias is literally the sheaf's geometry — a functor from the graph's incidence category into vector spaces, with learning as the search for the right functor.

### 4.5 String diagrams as a proof calculus for architectures

String diagrams in a symmetric monoidal category are not mere illustrations; by the coherence theorems they are a **sound and complete graphical calculus**: two diagrams denote the same morphism iff they are related by planar isotopy (plus the monoidal coherences) [1]. For deep learning this yields:

1. **Architecture search as diagram rewriting.** Neural architecture search becomes the enumeration of string diagrams in **Para**; validity is well-typedness, and equivalences (e.g., fusing batch-norm into convolution) are diagram equations.
2. **Automatic differentiation as diagram transformation.** The reverse derivative *R[−]* functorially assigns a backward diagram to each forward diagram [4]; autodiff engines implement this assignment.
3. **Parallelism as tensor product.** Data parallelism, model parallelism, and multi-head attention are all instances of ⊗; the interchange law *(g ∘ f) ⊗ (k ∘ h) = (g ⊗ k) ∘ (f ⊗ h)* states that independent computations commute — the basis of correct parallel training [1].

In Rust-flavored pseudocode, the **Para** composition at the heart of every framework:

```rust
// A parametric morphism: parameter space P, map P ⊗ X -> Y
struct Para<P, X, Y> {
    params: P,
    map: Box<dyn Fn(P, X) -> Y>,
}

// Sequential composition tensors the parameter spaces
fn compose<P, Q, X, Y, Z>(f: Para<P, X, Y>, g: Para<Q, Y, Z>) -> Para<(Q, P), X, Z> {
    Para {
        params: (g.params, f.params),
        map: Box::new(move |(q, p), x| (g.map)(q, (f.map)(p, x))),
    }
}
```

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The categorical program is unusual in machine learning for proving theorems first and benchmarking second. We summarize both.

**Theorem (Functorial backpropagation)** [1]. *With fixed step size and a quadratic error function, gradient descent is a monoidal functor **Para(Euc) → Learn**.* Consequence: backpropagation through *any* architecture built from the monoidal operations is correct by construction — including architectures not yet invented.

**Theorem (Reverse derivatives)** [4]. *In a Cartesian reverse differential category, the reverse derivative satisfies seven axioms (RD.1–RD.7) governing additivity, linearity, the chain rule, and symmetry of mixed partials; moreover, a reverse derivative induces a forward derivative, and is equivalent to a forward derivative plus a dagger structure on linear maps.* Consequence: reverse-mode AD — the engine of deep learning — has a complete axiomatics; any implementation satisfying the axioms computes correct gradients.

**Theorem (Sheaf separation power)** [5]. *For connected graphs with at most 2d classes, the class of O(d)-bundles (orthogonal restriction maps) has linear separation power: sheaf diffusion separates classes where graph diffusion collapses.* Consequence: heterophily and oversmoothing are artifacts of the *trivial sheaf*, not of message passing; learning the sheaf cures them, as Bodnar et al. verify on heterophilic benchmarks [5].

**Empirical record** [2]. Cruttwell et al. implement their categorical learners in Python, demonstrating that the lens-based semantics executes: the same abstract code instantiates gradient descent, Adam, and boolean-circuit learning by changing only the base CRDC [2].

**Empirical record** [3]. The algebraic theory is validated by reconstruction: the paper exhibits RNNs, recursive networks, Mealy/Moore machines, and GDL-equivariant maps as instances of the monad/**Para** formalism, with Haskell/Idris-style specifications in the appendices [3].

The functoriality laws any implementation of categorical learners must satisfy are: *F(g ∘ f) = F(g) ∘ F(f)*, *F(id) = id*, and *F(f ⊗ g) = F(f) ⊗ F(g)* — composition, identity, and monoidality preservation.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires stating what the categorical picture does not yet deliver.

1. **Generalization is outside the frame.** Functoriality governs how training *composes*, not what functions generalize. PAC-Bayes bounds, double descent, and scaling laws have no categorical account in this literature; the framework is about the *mechanics* of learning, not its *statistics*.
2. **Optimization dynamics are coarse.** The functorial treatment fixes the step size and treats one update step; the rich dynamical phenomena of deep learning — sharp vs. flat minima, edge of stability, grokking — live in the *iteration* of the functor, which the current theory barely studies.
3. **Strictness vs. practice.** Real frameworks implement **Para** only up to isomorphism (parameter tuples are flattened, reparameterizations are implicit). The 2-categorical niceties of [3] have no production implementation; the gap between the 2-category and PyTorch is real engineering work.
4. **Sheaf learning is expensive.** Learning *d × d* restriction maps per edge multiplies parameters and the sheaf Laplacian must be renormalized per step [5]; on large graphs this is prohibitive without the diagonal or orthogonal parametrizations, which trade expressivity for tractability.
5. **The reverse-derivative axioms assume smoothness.** Nonsmooth activations (ReLU at zero), stochastic layers (dropout), and discrete sampling break the CRDC axioms strictly speaking; the theory covers them only via smoothings or the boolean-circuit instance [2,4].
6. **No account of data.** The entire program takes the dataset as given. Functorial *data* migration is a separate categorical story [cf. companion post]; unifying the data category with the learner category — learning as a functor *between* them — remains open.

## 7 Conclusion

We have surveyed the categorical foundations of deep learning: backpropagation as a monoidal functor [1], architectures as monad algebras in **Para** [3], differentiation as reverse derivatives [4] packaged into lenses and optics [2,7], and message passing as sheaf diffusion [5]. The through-line is **compositionality made mathematical**: stacking, branching, sharing, and transporting are the operations of monoidal categories, 2-categories, and sheaf categories, and their correctness follows from functoriality rather than from luck.

The research program this opens is concrete:

- **Certified autodiff**: implement reverse-derivative categories in proof assistants and extract gradient code with machine-checked correctness.
- **Architecture type systems**: make equivariance violations and shape mismatches *type errors* via the algebraic theory [3].
- **Learned geometry**: extend neural sheaf diffusion [5] to hypergraphs and cell complexes, making the transport geometry itself a learned functor.
- **Categorical optimizers**: design new lenses on parameter space with provable compositional behavior, rather than update rules validated only by benchmark.

Deep learning grew up as alchemy; category theory is one of the instruments turning it into chemistry. The diagrams were always there, drawn on whiteboards in every lab — we are finally learning to read them as what they are: proofs.
