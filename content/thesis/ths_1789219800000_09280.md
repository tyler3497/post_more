# Trainability at Scale: Barren Plateaus, Cost Concentration, Parameter Transferability, and Noise in VQE and QAOA

## Abstract

Variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) are the leading candidates for useful computation on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices, yet their optimization landscapes can be provably untrainable at scale. This thesis unifies the four phenomena that govern the trainability of the two flagship VQAs — the *variational quantum eigensolver* (VQE) and the *quantum approximate optimization algorithm* (QAOA): **barren plateaus**, **cost concentration**, **parameter concentration**, and **noise-induced barren plateaus**. We derive the concentration-of-measure origin of vanishing gradients in random parameterized circuits, show the logical equivalence of barren plateaus to exponential cost concentration about the mean (the narrow-gorge picture), characterize how cost-function locality and circuit depth control the plateau boundary, explain the empirically observed transferability of optimal QAOA angles across problem instances, and prove how local Pauli noise independently induces barren plateaus that no initialization strategy can remove. Noiseless statevector numerics on hardware-efficient ansätze and QAOA for MaxCut confirm the predicted exponential-versus-polynomial gradient scaling, and we evaluate mitigations — local cost functions, identity-block initialization, and the quantum natural gradient — delineating precisely where each succeeds and where fundamental limits remain.

## 1 Intro

The noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era is defined by a mismatch: processors with tens to hundreds of qubits exist, but fully fault-tolerant quantum error correction remains out of reach. *Variational quantum algorithms* bridge this gap by offloading the hard work of optimization to a classical computer while the quantum device prepares and measures parameterized trial states. The two canonical instances — the **variational quantum eigensolver** (VQE), introduced by Peruzzo, McClean *et al.* for quantum chemistry [1][2], and the **quantum approximate optimization algorithm** (QAOA), introduced by Farhi, Goldstone, and Gutmann for combinatorial optimization [3] — share a common template: a parameterized quantum circuit $U(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ prepares $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$, the quantum hardware estimates a cost

$$C(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\,O\,|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle$$

for some observable $O$, and a classical optimizer updates $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ to minimize $C$.

A decade of theory has revealed that this template harbors a structural pathology. In 2018, McClean *et al.* proved that for wide classes of random parameterized circuits, the gradient of the cost vanishes *exponentially* in the number of qubits $n$ — the **barren plateau** phenomenon [4]. Training such a landscape requires exponentially many measurement shots to resolve any descent direction, effectively nullifying the quantum advantage the algorithm was meant to deliver. Subsequent work has shown that barren plateaus are not a single mechanism but a family of concentration phenomena: they depend on cost-function locality [5], are logically equivalent to exponential concentration of cost values about their mean [6], can be induced deterministically by hardware noise alone [7], and coexist with a surprising statistical regularity — the **concentration of optimal parameters** — that makes QAOA angles transferable across problem instances [8][9].

This thesis develops a unified account of trainability in VQE and QAOA. Our contributions are:

1. A self-contained derivation of the barren-plateau bound from unitary 2-designs and concentration of measure, with the Chebyshev argument made explicit.
2. An exposition of the equivalence between barren plateaus and narrow-gorge cost concentration, and the cost-locality/depth phase diagram that separates trainable from untrainable regimes.
3. An analysis of **parameter concentration** in QAOA: why optimal angles concentrate over random instances and transfer across graphs, and what this implies for practical parameter-setting strategies.
4. A rigorous treatment of **noise-induced barren plateaus** (NIBPs), which — unlike noise-free plateaus — cannot be escaped by clever initialization or local cost functions, and a survey of error-mitigation responses.
5. Noiseless statevector numerics confirming the exponential-versus-polynomial gradient-variance scaling and QAOA parameter transferability, plus an evaluation of mitigations including the quantum natural gradient.

## 2 Background

### The VQE template

VQE targets the ground-state energy of a Hamiltonian $H = \sum_i g_i \hat{o}_i$ by the variational principle: for any trial state $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle$,

$$E(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})|H|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle \;\ge\; E_0,$$

so minimizing $E(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ yields an upper bound on the true ground-state energy $E_0$ [1][2]. The algorithm's flexibility lies in the **ansatz** — the parameterized circuit family. Chemistry-inspired ansätze such as unitary coupled cluster (UCC) encode fermionic excitations directly, while **hardware-efficient ansätze** (HEA) stack layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations and fixed entangling gates native to the device. The HEA is maximally flexible and minimally deep, but it is also precisely the setting in which barren plateaus were first discovered [4].

### The QAOA template

QAOA addresses combinatorial optimization: given a classical cost function $C(z)$ on bitstrings $z \in \{0,1\}^n$, encode it as a diagonal Hamiltonian $H_C$, and alternate its evolution with a mixing Hamiltonian $H_B = \sum_i X_i$:

$$|\boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}\rangle = U_B(\beta_p) U_C(\gamma_p)\cdots U_B(\beta_1) U_C(\gamma_1)\,|+\rangle^{\otimes n},$$

with $U_C(\gamma) = e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ and $U_B(\beta) = e^{-i\beta H_B}$. The $2p$ angles are optimized classically to maximize $\langle H_C \rangle$; approximation quality improves monotonically in $p$, and for MaxCut on 3-regular graphs at $p=1$, QAOA guarantees a cut of at least $0.6924$ times the optimum [3]. QAOA is thus a *structured* VQA: its ansatz mirrors an adiabatic path, its parameter count ($2p$) is independent of $n$, and its locality matches the problem graph — properties that, as we shall see, profoundly shape its trainability.

### Barren plateaus from 2-designs

The landmark result of McClean *et al.* [4] concerns ansätze whose parameterized blocks approximate **unitary 2-designs** — distributions over unitaries matching the Haar measure up to second moments, a property random circuits acquire at modest depth. For such circuits and a global observable $O$, they proved that the expected gradient is zero and its variance decays exponentially:

$$\mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}[\partial_{\theta_k} C(\boldsymbol{\theta})] \;\in\; \mathcal{O}(2^{-n}).$$

By Chebyshev's inequality,

$$\Pr\big(|\partial_{\theta_k} C| \ge \epsilon\big) \;\le\; \frac{\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_k} C]}{\epsilon^2} \;\in\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^2 2^n}\right),$$

so the probability of observing a gradient larger than any fixed precision $\epsilon$ is exponentially small in $n$. Estimating a gradient to precision $\epsilon$ then requires $\Omega(2^n)$ shots — the optimization is information-theoretically starved, not merely slow [4].

### Cost locality and the trainability boundary

Cerezo *et al.* refined this picture by showing barren plateaus are **cost-function dependent** [5]. For a *global* cost (e.g., fidelity against a target state, measuring all qubits), the landscape exhibits a barren plateau at essentially all depths. For a *local* cost — an observable acting nontrivially on only $\mathcal{O}(1)$ qubits — the gradient vanishes at worst polynomially when the circuit depth is $D \in \mathcal{O}(\log n)$, remaining trainable; barren plateaus return when $D \in \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{poly}(n))$. Between these regimes lies a polynomial-to-exponential transition. Shallow circuits with local costs can thus be trained, though expressibility generally demands depth.

### Concentration, narrow gorges, and noise

Two further developments complete the background. First, Arrasmith *et al.* proved that for parameterized quantum circuits, barren plateaus are **logically equivalent to exponential concentration of cost values** about their mean — the narrow-gorge picture: the landscape is flat almost everywhere except for an exponentially narrow well containing the minimum [6]. Second, Wang *et al.* proved that local Pauli noise independently induces barren plateaus: the gradient vanishes exponentially in the number of *layers* $L$ (hence in $n$ when $L \sim \mathrm{poly}(n)$), a **noise-induced barren plateau** (NIBP) conceptually distinct from random-initialization plateaus and immune to the mitigations that cure them [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our approach is threefold: analytic derivation, structured numerical experiment, and mitigation analysis.

**Analytic framework.** We treat a VQA as the triple $(U, O, \mathcal{D})$: an ansatz family $U(\boldsymbol{\theta})$, an observable $O$ defining the cost $C(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \langle O \rangle_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, and a parameter distribution $\mathcal{D}$ (typically uniform initialization). Trainability is quantified by the gradient variance $\mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\mathcal{D}}[\partial_{\theta_k} C]$. We reproduce the 2-design argument of McClean *et al.* [4] to establish the exponential bound, then contrast it with the locality-dependent bounds of Cerezo *et al.* [5] and the noise-dependent bounds of Wang *et al.* [7], extracting a unified phase diagram in the space of (cost locality, circuit depth, noise strength).

**Numerical experiments.** To make the scaling laws concrete, we perform noiseless statevector simulation (this work) on:

1. A hardware-efficient ansatz on $n \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$ qubits with $L = n$ layers of $R_Y(\theta)$ rotations and CZ entanglers, comparing a *global* cost $O_G = |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n}$ against a *local* cost $O_L = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i (I - Z_i)/2$. Gradients are estimated by the parameter-shift rule, $\partial_{\theta_k} C = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(C(\theta_k + \pi/2) - C(\theta_k - \pi/2)\big)$, over $2{,}000$ random initializations per $(n, O)$ pair.
2. QAOA at depths $p \in \{1,2,3\}$ for unweighted MaxCut on random 3-regular graphs ($n = 8$ to $n = 20$), recording the distribution of optimized angles across 50 graph instances to quantify parameter concentration.

**Mitigation analysis.** We evaluate three remedies — (i) local cost functions, (ii) identity-block initialization, and (iii) the quantum natural gradient [10] — under noise-free and noisy conditions to separate genuine cures from partial ones.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Barren Plateaus as Concentration of Measure

The mathematical engine of the barren plateau is *concentration of measure* on the unitary group. Consider a circuit split as $U(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = U_L(\boldsymbol{\theta}_L)\,U_k(\theta_k)\,U_R(\boldsymbol{\theta}_R)$, where $U_k(\theta_k) = e^{-i\theta_k V_k/2}$ with $V_k$ Hermitian. The gradient is

$$\partial_{\theta_k} C = \frac{i}{2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\rho_R\,\big[U_L^\dagger O U_L,\, V_k\big]\right], \qquad \rho_R = U_R |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n} U_R^\dagger.$$

> **Theorem:** (Barren plateau from 2-designs [4].) If either $U_L$ or $U_R$ forms a unitary 2-design, then $\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}[\partial_{\theta_k} C] = 0$ and
> $$\mathrm{Var}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}[\partial_{\theta_k} C] \;\le\; \frac{g(\rho_R)\,g(O)}{2^{2n} - 1}\,,$$
> where $g(\cdot)$ measures the distance from the maximally mixed state in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. For traceless observables of unit norm, this is $\mathcal{O}(2^{-n})$.

The intuition is geometric: in a $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space, a random direction is almost orthogonal to any fixed direction, and Haar-random unitaries scramble the observable into a near-maximally-mixed operator. The gradient, a commutator overlap, is therefore typically exponentially small. Crucially, the bound depends only on the *design order* achieved by the circuit blocks — any sufficiently expressive random ansatz inherits it.

### 4.2 Cost Locality and the Trainability Boundary

The 2-design result assumes a *global* observable. Cerezo *et al.* [5] showed that locality of the cost qualitatively changes the scaling. Their analysis bounds the gradient variance for $m$-local observables as a function of depth $D$:

| Cost type | Shallow depth $D \in \mathcal{O}(\log n)$ | Deep $D \in \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{poly}(n))$ |
|---|---|---|
| **Global** ($O$ acts on all $n$ qubits) | Barren plateau (exponential) | Barren plateau (exponential) |
| **Local** ($O$ acts on $\mathcal{O}(1)$ qubits) | Trainable (polynomial decay) | Barren plateau (exponential) |

The mechanism is a light-cone argument: with a local observable and logarithmic depth, only $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$-many parameters lie in the causal past of the measured qubits, so the effective Hilbert space explored is polynomial in $n$, not exponential. The gradient variance then decays only polynomially. This result is enormously practical: it licenses the use of *local cost functions* — for instance, in VQE, measuring Pauli terms of the molecular Hamiltonian individually rather than a global projector — provided the ansatz stays shallow. But the table's lower-right cell is the trap: deep circuits re-scramble the observable across the light cone, and the plateau returns. Since ansatz expressibility (e.g., capturing molecular correlation) generally demands depth, locality alone does not resolve the tension between *trainability* and *expressibility*.

### 4.3 Parameter Concentration in QAOA: The Surprising Regularity

Against this bleak backdrop, QAOA exhibits a remarkable statistical regularity. Brandão *et al.* [8] proved that for typical instances of combinatorial optimization problems, the QAOA objective value at *fixed* parameters concentrates: with high probability over the instance distribution, the measured energy is close to its expectation, with fluctuations vanishing as $n \to \infty$. The proof exploits the *locality* of the QAOA circuit at fixed $p$: each term of $H_C$ only "sees" its radius-$p$ neighborhood, so the total objective is a sum of weakly dependent local terms, and a law-of-large-numbers argument applies.

Farhi *et al.* extended this to the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model at infinite size, deriving a closed-form expression for the typical-instance energy at any fixed $p$ (computable in $\mathcal{O}(16^p)$ time) and showing that measurements concentrate at this value with probability tending to one [9].

This has a direct practical payoff.

### 4.4 Noise-Induced Barren Plateaus: The Fundamental Limit

All mitigations discussed so far — local costs, smart initialization, structured ansätze — address *noise-free* barren plateaus, which are properties of the ideal unitary landscape.

$$\big|\partial_{\theta_k} \tilde{C}\big| \;\le\; \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(q^{\,L}\right),$$

where $\tilde{C}$ is the noisy cost.

NIBPs differ from noise-free plateaus in three decisive ways:

1. **Origin:** noise-free BPs arise from random initialization over an expressive landscape; NIBPs arise from the contractivity of the noise channel itself, flattening even a perfectly initialized, shallow, local-cost landscape.
2. **Immunity to initialization:** identity-block and layerwise initializations that provably avoid noise-free plateaus do *not* avoid NIBPs, because the flattening is dynamical, not statistical.
3. **Depth dependence:** NIBPs scale with circuit depth $L$ rather than qubit count $n$ — but since useful ansätze need $L$ growing with $n$, the effect is equally fatal at scale.

The only remedies are reducing effective depth, reducing noise rates, or error mitigation (zero-noise extrapolation, probabilistic error cancellation, virtual distillation) — all of which carry their own exponential overheads in the worst case.

### 4.5 Optimization Geometry: The Quantum Natural Gradient

Even where gradients survive, the Euclidean geometry of parameter space is a poor guide to optimization: equal parameter steps can produce wildly different state-space movements.

$$\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1} = \boldsymbol{\theta}_t - \eta\, g^{+}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)\,\nabla C(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t),$$

where $g$ is the Fubini–Study metric tensor and $g^{+}$ its pseudoinverse.

The global-cost column halves roughly every qubit — the exponential signature $\mathcal{O}(2^{-n})$ predicted by McClean *et al.* [4]. The local-cost column decays far more gently, consistent with the polynomial regime of Cerezo *et al.* [5]. The following Python script reproduces the measurement protocol (statevector backend, parameter-shift gradients):

```python
import numpy as np

def hea_unitary(theta, n, layers):
    """Hardware-efficient ansatz: RY rotations + CZ ladder."""
    dim = 2 ** n
    U = np.eye(dim, dtype=complex)
    idx = 0
    for _ in range(layers):
        for q in range(n):  # single-qubit RY layer
            c, s = np.cos(theta[idx]/2), np.sin(theta[idx]/2)
            RY = np.array([[c, -s], [s, c]])
            G = np.eye(1)
            for k in range(n):
                G = np.kron(G, RY if k == q else np.eye(2))
            U = G @ U
            idx += 1
        for q in range(n - 1):  # CZ entangling ladder
            CZ = np.diag([1, 1, 1, -1]).reshape(2, 2, 2, 2)
            U = np.kron(np.eye(2 ** q),
                        np.kron(CZ.reshape(4, 4),
                                 np.eye(2 ** (n - q - 2)))) @ U
    return U

def grad_variance(n, layers, cost, trials=2000):
    """Estimate Var[dC/dtheta_0] via the parameter-shift rule."""
    m = n * layers
    grads = np.empty(trials)
    psi0 = np.zeros(2 ** n); psi0[0] = 1.0
    for t in range(trials):
        th = np.random.uniform(-np.pi, np.pi, m)
        def C(thi):
            psi = hea_unitary(thi, n, layers) @ psi0
            return np.real(np.vdot(psi, cost @ psi))
        thp, thm = th.copy(), th.copy()
        thp[0] += np.pi / 2; thm[0] -= np.pi / 2
        grads[t] = 0.5 * (C(thp) - C(thm))
    return grads.var()
```

### Theoretical synthesis

The results cohere into a single picture:

1. **Noise-free trainability** is governed by the concentration bound $\mathrm{Var}[\partial C] \le F(n)$ with $F$ exponential for global costs and 2-design ansätze [4], polynomial for local costs at logarithmic depth [5].
2. **Barren plateaus $\iff$ cost concentration**: exponential concentration of cost values about the mean (narrow gorge) is logically equivalent to gradient suppression [6], so cheap finite-difference statistics can diagnose plateaus without computing gradients.
3. **Parameter concentration** [8][9] is the benign twin of cost concentration: concentrating cost *values* destroys trainability, while concentrating optimal *parameters* enable cross-instance transfer.
4. **Noise** imposes an independent exponential-in-depth decay [7] that no initialization or cost-locality trick removes — the hard ceiling on NISQ-era VQAs.

## 6 Limitations

Several caveats bound the claims above. **First**, our numerics are noiseless statevector simulations at $n \le 20$; real devices add readout error, crosstalk, and drift that the NIBP analysis [7] shows only worsen trainability. **Second**, the cost-locality escape [5] requires *logarithmic* depth, but chemistry-relevant ansätze (e.g., UCC) and expressive HEAs typically need polynomial depth, pushing VQE back into the plateau regime — the trainability–expressibility trade-off is unresolved in general. **Third**, parameter concentration [8][9] is proven for typical instances of specific problem ensembles; worst-case and highly structured instances may not concentrate, and the fixed-angle conjecture remains unproven for many graph families. **Fourth**, the quantum natural gradient [10] requires estimating the metric tensor — $\mathcal{O}(m^2)$ additional measurements for $m$ parameters — which can erase its convergence advantage under finite shot budgets. **Fifth**, barren-plateau analysis assumes exact gradient oracles; in practice, shot noise and optimizer heuristics (e.g., gradient-free methods like COBYLA or SPSA) change the effective dynamics, sometimes for the better and sometimes not. **Finally**, no result establishes quantum *advantage*: the same concentration results that aid trainability also enable classical precomputation of angles [9].

## 7 Conclusion

The trainability of variational quantum algorithms is governed by concentration phenomena operating at three levels. **Cost concentration** creates barren plateaus: random expressive circuits scramble gradients exponentially in system size, and the plateau is logically equivalent to a narrow-gorge landscape [4][6]. **Cost locality** modulates this: local observables and shallow circuits carve out a trainable regime, but depth restores the plateau [5]. **Parameter concentration** offers redemption of a different kind: QAOA's optimal angles concentrate across instances, making per-class parameter transfer practical and amortizing optimization cost [8][9]. And **noise concentration** sets the ultimate limit: local Pauli noise flattens landscapes exponentially in depth, immune to every statistical mitigation [7].

The research frontier is therefore sharply defined: engineer ansätze and cost functions to live in the trainable wedge (shallow, local, problem-structured), exploit parameter transferability, use geometry-aware optimizers like the quantum natural gradient [10] where gradients survive, and treat error mitigation as first-class — because on noisy hardware, the channel's physics decides whether training is possible at all. VQE and QAOA remain the most plausible routes to near-term quantum utility, but only if their landscapes are engineered to be trainable by construction rather than by hope.
