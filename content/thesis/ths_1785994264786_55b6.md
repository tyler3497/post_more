# Causal Inference and Double Machine Learning: Potential Outcomes, Instrumental Variables, and Heterogeneous Treatment Effects via Orthogonal Scores

## Abstract
> ***Double Machine Learning*** resolves the fundamental tension between flexible machine learning for confounder adjustment and valid causal inference by leveraging *Neyman orthogonal scores* and *cross-fitting* to eliminate regularization bias and overfitting [1][2]. This thesis provides a unified treatment of modern causal inference: starting from the **Rubin Potential Outcomes Model**, through **propensity scores** and **inverse propensity weighting (IPTW)**, to **instrumental variables (IV)** for **Local Average Treatment Effects (LATE)**, and culminating in **heterogeneous treatment effects (CATE)** via the **R-learner** [2], **causal forests** [3], and **DR-learner**. We prove root-n consistency of Double ML estimators even when nuisance functions converge at slow $o(n^{-1/4})$ rates, a regime where naive plug-in fails. This synthesis emphasizes *orthogonality*, *efficiency*, and *honest* heterogeneity search.

**Keywords:** *causal inference, potential outcomes, propensity score, double machine learning, Neyman orthogonality, cross-fitting, instrumental variables, LATE, CATE, R-learner, causal forest, semiparametric efficiency*

---

## 1. Introduction

Causal inference seeks to answer *what-if* questions that prediction cannot: ***What is the effect of a treatment $D$ on outcome $Y$?*** Unlike supervised learning where ground truth $Y$ is observed, the fundamental problem of causal inference is that for unit $i$ we observe only one of two potential outcomes $Y_i(1)$ or $Y_i(0)$, never both [4]. The **Rubin Causal Model (RCM)** formalizes this missing data problem, making explicit the assumptions needed for identification.

Simultaneously, modern applications — digital platforms, genomics, economics, healthcare — provide high-dimensional covariates $W \in \mathbb{R}^p$ where $p \gg n$ or $p \sim n^{c}$. Flexible machine learning (random forests, boosting, neural nets) can approximate nuisance functions like $g_0(W)=\mathbb{E}[Y|W]$ and $m_0(W)=\mathbb{E}[D|W]$ with excellent predictive performance, but naively plugging them into causal estimators induces **regularization bias** and **overfitting bias** that breaks $\sqrt{n}$ asymptotics.

> **Theorem 1.1 (Failure of Naive ML Plug-in):** Let $\theta_0$ be the Average Treatment Effect (ATE). Let $\hat{g},\hat{m}$ be $L_2$-consistent ML estimators with bias $O(n^{-\varphi})$ where $\varphi < 1/2$. Then plug-in estimator $\hat{\theta}_{plug} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \hat{g}(1,W_i)-\hat{g}(0,W_i)$ has bias of order $n^{-\varphi}$ dominating variance $n^{-1/2}$, violating asymptotic normality and invalidating confidence intervals [1].

This thesis presents **Double Machine Learning (DML)** [1] as the principled remedy:

1.  **Neyman Orthogonal Scores:** Construct moment $\psi(W;\theta,\eta)$ with vanishing Gateaux derivative w.r.t. nuisance $\eta$ at truth.
2.  **Cross-Fitting (Sample Splitting):** Estimate nuisance $\hat{\eta}$ on $K-1$ folds, evaluate score on held-out fold to remove own-observation bias.
3.  **Heterogeneity Orthogonalization:** Extend to CATE $\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|X=x]$ via R-learner loss that is orthogonalized against baseline $m$ and $e$ [2][3].

### Contributions
- A self-contained derivation of identification under **unconfoundedness** and **overlap**, **propensity theorem**, IPTW and **doubly robust AIPW**.
- Unified view of **IV, Wald, compliance types**, and LATE identification under **exclusion, independence, monotonicity** [4][5].
- Full construction of DML for Partially Linear Model and Interactive Model, with proof sketch of $\sqrt{n}$ normality under $n^{-1/4}$ rates.
- Characterization of **CATE meta-learners**: S-learner, T-learner, X-learner, **R-learner** [2], **DR-learner**, and **causal forests** [3] as generalized random forests solving locally weighted orthogonal moments.
- Empirical considerations: implementation, honest splitting, and practical diagnostics.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Rubin Potential Outcomes

Following Neyman (1923) and Rubin (1974) [4], define for each unit potential outcomes mapping:

- $Y_i(d) \in \mathbb{R}$ outcome under treatment $D_i = d$, $d\in\{0,1\}$
- $D_i \in \{0,1\}$ treatment assignment
- $W_i \in \mathcal{W}$ pre-treatment covariates

Observed outcome: $Y_i = D_i Y_i(1) + (1-D_i) Y_i(0)$ (*consistency / SUTVA*).

Target parameters:

| Parameter | Definition | Identification Need |
|-----------|------------|---------------------|
| **ATE** | $\theta_0^{ATE}= \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]$ | Population mean difference |
| **ATT** | $\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|D=1]$ | Effect on treated |
| **CATE** | $\tau_0(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|X=x]$ | Heterogeneity |
| **LATE** | $\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|\text{Complier}]$ | IV local |

**Fundamental Problem:** $(Y_i(0), Y_i(1))$ never jointly observed → causal estimand is *missing data* functional.

### 2.2 Identification: Unconfoundedness and Overlap

> **Definition 2.1 (Strong Ignorability [Rosenbaum & Rubin 1983]):** $(Y(0),Y(1)) \perp\!\!\!\perp D \mid W$ and $0 < P(D=1\mid W=w) <1$ a.s.

Under Definition 2.1:

$$\theta_0 = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y|D=1,W]-\mathbb{E}[Y|D=0,W]]$$

Proof follows from iterated expectations and ignorability: $\mathbb{E}[Y(d)|W]=\mathbb{E}[Y(d)|D=d,W]=\mathbb{E}[Y|D=d,W]$.

*Unconfoundedness* is *unverifiable* and requires domain knowledge encoded in **Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)** [Figure: DAG potential outcomes]. Confounder $W$ blocks back-door path $D \leftarrow W \rightarrow Y$. Unobserved $U$ that affects both $D$ and $Y$ violates identification.

### 2.3 Propensity Score and Balancing

Define $e_0(W)=P(D=1|W)$ (*propensity*) [Rosenbaum & Rubin]. 

> **Theorem 2.2 (Balancing):** If strong ignorability holds conditional on $W$, then it holds conditional on $e_0(W)$: $(Y(0),Y(1))\perp\!\!\!\perp D \mid e_0(W)$. Moreover, $W \perp\!\!\!\perp D \mid e_0(W)$.

Thus dimension reduction from $\mathbb{R}^p$ to $[0,1]$ suffices for confounding control, though direct ML on $W$ often outperforms propensity trimming alone.

Inverse propensity estimators:
$$\hat{\theta}_{IPTW}= \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \frac{D_i Y_i}{\hat{e}(W_i)} - \frac{(1-D_i)Y_i}{1-\hat{e}(W_i)}$$

IPTW is unbiased if $\hat{e}=e_0$ but high variance when $\hat{e}\approx 0,1$ (overlap violation). Stabilized weights and trimming at $[0.05,0.95]$ are common.

### 2.4 Semiparametric Theory Primer

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be nonparametric model, functional $\theta(P)$. Efficient influence function $\varphi(P)$ satisfies pathwise derivative condition. Estimator achieving variance equal to $\text{Var}_P(\varphi)$ is *semiparametrically efficient*: Cramér-Rao analog for infinite-dimensional nuisance.

**Neyman Orthogonality** is first-order insensitivity: $\partial_\eta \mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)][\eta-\eta_0]=0$. This severs link between slow nuisance learning and fast parameter estimation [1].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 The Double ML Framework [1]

Chernozhukov et al. (Econometrica 2018) [1] consider general score $\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)]=0$ with $\theta_0\in\mathbb{R}^{d_\theta}$ low-dimensional target, $\eta_0\in\mathcal{T}$ nuisance infinite-dimensional.

**DML1 Algorithm:**

```python
# Python sketch – DML1 for Partially Linear Model
import numpy as np
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold

def dml_plr(Y, D, W, K=5):
    n = len(Y)
    kf = KFold(n_splits=K, shuffle=True)
    thetas = []
    for train_idx, test_idx in kf.split(W):
        # 1. nuisance on train
        g_hat = RandomForestRegressor().fit(W[train_idx], Y[train_idx])
        m_hat = RandomForestRegressor().fit(W[train_idx], D[train_idx])
        # 2. residuals on test
        Y_res = Y[test_idx] - g_hat.predict(W[test_idx])
        D_res = D[test_idx] - m_hat.predict(W[test_idx])
        # orthogonal moment: (Y - g - theta*(D - m))*(D - m)=0
        theta_fold = np.sum(D_res*Y_res) / np.sum(D_res**2)
        thetas.append(theta_fold)
    # 3. aggregation: median or mean
    return np.mean(thetas)
```

> **Theorem 3.1 (DML):** Assume $\psi$ is Neyman orthogonal, nuisance estimators $\hat{\eta}_k$ satisfy $\|\hat{\eta}_k-\eta_0\|_{L_2}=o_p(n^{-1/4})$ and cross-fitted. Then $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\sigma^2)$ where $\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[\psi^2]/(\mathbb{E}[\partial_\theta\psi])^2$.

Key insight: orthogonalization kills term $\partial_\eta \psi \cdot (\hat{\eta}-\eta_0) = 0$ leaving second-order remainder $O(\|\hat{\eta}-\eta_0\|^2)=o_p(n^{-1/2})$ when rate $> n^{-1/4}$ [1].

### 3.2 Two Canonical Models

**Partially Linear Regression (PLR):**
$$ Y = \theta_0 D + g_0(W) + \varepsilon, \quad \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon|D,W]=0$$
$$ D = m_0(W) + V, \quad \mathbb{E}[V|W]=0$$

Nuisance $\eta_0=(g_0,m_0)$, orthogonal score:
$$\psi(W;\theta,\eta)= (Y - g(W) - \theta(D - m(W)))(D - m(W))$$

**Interactive Model (IRM, heterogeneous ATE):**
$$ Y = g_0(D,W)+\varepsilon, \quad D|W \sim \text{Bernoulli}(e_0(W))$$
Score (doubly robust):
$$\psi = g(1,W)-g(0,W) + \frac{D(Y-g(1,W))}{e(W)} - \frac{(1-D)(Y-g(0,W))}{1-e(W)} - \theta$$

This is **AIPW** (Augmented IPTW), doubly robust: $\mathbb{E}[\psi]=0$ if either $g=g_0$ OR $e=e_0$.

| Estimator | Needs $e$ correct? | Needs $g$ correct? | Orthogonal? | Rate requirement |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------|------------------|
| IPTW | Yes | No | No | $n^{-1/2}$ |
| Outcome Regression | No | Yes | No | $n^{-1/2}$ |
| **AIPW / DML** | One of two | One of two | **Yes** | $n^{-1/4}$ product condition |

This *double robustness* plus orthogonality is the workhorse.

### 3.3 Instrumental Variables and LATE

When unconfoundedness fails due to unobserved $U$, instrument $Z$ provides exogenous variation [5].

Assumptions (Imbens & Angrist 1994):

- **Relevance:** $Z \not\perp\!\!\!\perp D$
- **Independence:** $Z \perp\!\!\!\perp (Y(d),D(z)) \mid W$
- **Exclusion:** $Y(d,z)=Y(d)$ — $Z$ affects $Y$ only via $D$
- **Monotonicity:** $D_i(1) \ge D_i(0)$ — no defiers

Compliance types:

- **Complier:** $D(1)=1,D(0)=0$
- **Always-taker:** $D(1)=D(0)=1$
- **Never-taker:** $D(1)=D(0)=0$
- **Defier:** $D(1)=0,D(0)=1$ — ruled out

Wald estimand:

$$\theta_{LATE}= \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=0]}{\mathbb{E}[D|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[D|Z=0]} = \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|\text{Complier}]$$

DML-IV extension [1 Sec.5] orthogonal score for PLR-IV:

$$ Y - D\theta - g(W) =0, \quad Z \perp \varepsilon$$

Score: $\psi = (Y - D\theta - g(W))(Z - m(W))$ where $m(W)=\mathbb{E}[Z|W]$.

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch: orthogonal score type
type Nuisance = (W -> Double, W -> Double) -- (g, m)
psi :: Double -> Nuisance -> Sample -> Double
psi theta (g,m) (y,d,w,z) = (y - g w - theta*d)*(z - m w)
-- Neyman orthogonality: D_eta psi [h] =0 at truth
```

Instrument strength diagnostic: $F=\frac{(\hat{\mathbb{E}}[D|Z=1]-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[D|Z=0])^2}{\widehat{Var}}$; $F<10$ weak instrument inflates variance catastrophically.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Neyman Orthogonality and R-learner Decomposition

Robinson (1988) decomposition for PLR:

$$Y - m_Y(W) = \theta_0(W)(D - m_D(W)) + \varepsilon$$

Where $m_Y(W)=\mathbb{E}[Y|W]$, $m_D(W)=\mathbb{E}[D|W]$. For CATE where $\theta_0(W)=\tau_0(X)$ depends on heterogeneity features $X\subset W$:

$$Y_i - m(W_i) = \tau_0(X_i)(D_i - e(W_i)) + \epsilon_i$$

Nie & Wager 2021 [2] define **R-learner loss**:

$$\mathcal{L}_n(\tau)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \big[(Y_i-\hat{m}(W_i)) - \tau(X_i)(D_i-\hat{e}(W_i))\big]^2 + \Lambda_n(\tau)$$

Where $\Lambda_n$ is regularizer (e.g., RKHS norm, tree depth). Minimizing this *R-loss* is equivalent to orthogonal estimation of $\tau$. Why R? Residualization.

> **Theorem 4.1 (R-learner Quasioracle):** If $\hat{m},\hat{e}$ converge at $o_p(n^{-1/4})$, and $\Lambda_n$ tuned appropriately, then $\|\hat{\tau}_R - \tau_0\|_{L_2}^2 = O_p\left( \|\tilde{\tau}-\tau_0\|^2 + r_n^2\right)$ where $\tilde{\tau}$ is oracle learner with known $m,e$, and $r_n$ second-order nuisance error. Thus R-learner achieves *quasi-oracle* rate: as good as if we knew nuisances [2].

Contrast with S-learner $\hat{\mu}(W,D)$ and $\hat{\tau}_S=\hat{\mu}(W,1)-\hat{\mu}(W,0)$ which biases heterogeneity toward zero due to regularization favoring $D$-ignorant fit; T-learner $\hat{\mu}_1-\hat{\mu}_0$ overfits when treatment groups unbalanced. R-learner balances both via orthogonalization.

```python
# R-learner practical via lasso / XGBoost
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_predict
m_hat = cross_val_predict(GradientBoostingRegressor(), W, Y, cv=5)
e_hat = cross_val_predict(GradientBoostingClassifier(), W, D, cv=5, method='predict_proba')[:,1]
Y_res = Y - m_hat
D_res = D - e_hat
# Weighted regression: sample weight = D_res^2
# target = Y_res / D_res
import xgboost as xgb
z = Y_res / np.clip(D_res, -5,5)
w = D_res**2
model_tau = xgb.XGBRegressor().fit(X, z, sample_weight=w)
```

### 4.2 Causal Forests and Generalized Random Forests [3][6]

Athey et al. (Annals 2019) [3] reframe random forests as *adaptive nearest neighbor* with *honesty*.

**Honest Tree:** Split data into $J_1$ for structure (splits) and $J_2$ for leaf estimation. This removes optimism same as cross-fitting: tree structure $\mathcal{T}$ independent of leaf responses conditional on $J_1$.

**Causal Tree Splitting Criterion:** Maximize treatment effect heterogeneity, not prediction error:

$$\Delta(C_1,C_2) = \frac{n_{C_1}n_{C_2}}{n_P^2}(\hat{\tau}_{C_1}-\hat{\tau}_{C_2})^2$$

Where $C_1,C_2$ children of parent $P$. This rewards splits that increase EMSE via gradient approximation of $\tau$ variance.

**Generalized Random Forest** [3] solves at each leaf weighted estimating equations:

$$\sum_i \alpha_i(x) \psi_{\theta,\nu}(O_i)=0$$

Where $\alpha_i(x)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_b \frac{\mathbf{1}\{X_i\in L_b(x)\}}{|L_b(x)|}$ are forest kernels, $\psi$ orthogonal score for ATE at leaf. For CATE, $\psi = [Y-\mu(X)- \tau(X)(D-e(X))]$. Solving yields $\hat{\tau}(x)$ pointwise with asymptotic normality and variance estimates via infinitesimal jackknife — "little bags" [6].

*Example forest visualization*: root splits on $X_1>0.5$, left child splits on $X_2$, leaves report $\hat{\tau}\pm SE$.

### 4.3 Cross-Fitting and Honesty: Why Sample Splitting is Non-Negotiable

Traditional overfitting bias: $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{g}(W_i)-g_0(W_i))\cdot V_i] \neq 0$ when $i$ used for training $\hat{g}$.

**K-fold Cross-fitting** [1] ensures: $\hat{\eta}_{-k} \perp O_i$ for $i\in I_k$. Then:

$$\mathbb{E}_{I_k}[(\hat{g}_{-k}-g_0)(D-\hat{m})] = \mathbb{E}[\hat{g}_{-k}-g_0]\cdot \mathbb{E}[D-m_0] + o_p$$

Which vanishes due to orthogonality and independence. Without cross-fitting, even orthogonal score has bias $O(K/\sqrt{n})$ where $K$ = effective degrees freedom of ML; with Lasso $K=s\log p$, bias non-negligible [1 Lemma 3].

*Honest estimation* in causal forests is cross-fitting within tree: structure fold vs estimation fold.

Procedure (pseudocode outlined in Algorithm 1 Sec. 3.1) yields *uniform* validity over $P\in\mathcal{P}$ classes with entropy condition $\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal{F})\le C\epsilon^{-2}$ typical for RF / NN.

### 4.4 From LATE to MTE and Continuous IV

Beyond binary $Z$, marginal treatment effect framework:

$$MTE(u)= \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|U_D = u]$$

Where $U_D\sim Uniform[0,1]$ latent resistance to treatment in selection equation $D=\mathbf{1}\{P(Z) \ge U_D\}$, $P(Z)=P(D=1|Z)$. Under monotonicity, LATE = $\frac{1}{p_1-p_0}\int_{p_0}^{p_1} MTE(u)du$.

DML extends to continuous treatment dose-response via *double debiased* generalized propensity score. Score:

$$\psi_{DR}(Y,D,W;\theta,\eta)= K_h(D-d_0)\frac{Y-g(D,W)}{f(D|W)} + \tilde{g}(d_0,W) - \theta$$

Kernel $K_h$ localizes, $f(D|W)$ generalized propensity. Neyman orthogonality holds pointwise in $d_0$ [1 Sec. 6].

Practical diagnostic table:

| Violation | Symptom | Test |
|-----------|---------|------|
| Overlap $e(W)\approx0/1$ | Extreme weights, variance $\uparrow$ | Plot $e$ histogram, ESS = $(\sum w)^2/\sum w^2$ |
| Weak IV $Cov(D,Z|W)\approx0$ | Huge SE, estimate unstable | First-stage F <10, Anderson-Rubin CI |
| Unconfoundedness | Unobserved $U$ suspicion | Sensitivity Rosenbaum $\Gamma$, negative control |
| Honesty failure | CATE spuriously heterogeneous | Best linear predictor test: $\beta_2$ in $Y = ... + \beta \hat{\tau}$ |

## 5. Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Root-n Normality Sketch for DML-PLR

We want to prove:

$$\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta_0)= \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_i \phi_0(W_i) + o_p(1)$$

Where influence $\phi_0 = -J_0^{-1}\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)$, $J_0=\mathbb{E}[\partial_\theta\psi]$.

*Step 1 – Decomposition:*

$$0 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i\in I_k} \psi(W_i;\hat{\theta},\hat{\eta}_{-k})$$

Taylor expand in $\theta$:

$$\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta_0)= -\hat{J}^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k,i}\psi(W_i;\theta_0,\hat{\eta}_{-k})$$

*Step 2 – Orthogonality Expansion:*

$$\psi(\theta_0,\hat{\eta}) = \psi(\theta_0,\eta_0) + \partial_\eta \psi(\eta_0)[\hat{\eta}-\eta_0] + \frac12 \partial^2_{\eta\eta}\psi(\tilde{\eta})[\hat{\eta}-\eta_0]^2$$

First derivative term has mean zero due to orthogonality + cross-fitting: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{I_k} \partial_\eta\psi \cdot (\hat{\eta}_{-k}-\eta_0) = o_p(1)$ by conditioning on fold complement and Chebyshev, using $\|\hat{\eta}-\eta_0\|_{L_2}=o(n^{-1/4})$ and stochastic equicontinuity [1 Thm 3.1].

Remainder second order: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum \|\hat{\eta}-\eta_0\|^2 = \sqrt{n} O_p(n^{-1/2}) = o_p(1)$ when product rate $\le o(n^{-1/2})$. Requires $\|\hat{m}-m_0\|_2 \|\hat{g}-g_0\|_2 = o_p(n^{-1/2})$.

Hence asymptotic linear representation holds, CLT yields Gaussian.

### 5.3 IV LATE Proof Under Monotonicity

We prove Wald = LATE.

$$ \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=0]}{\mathbb{E}[D|Z=1]-\mathbb{E}[D|Z=0]} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))]}{\mathbb{E}[D(1)-D(0)]}$$

By independence, $\mathbb{E}[Y|Z=z]=\mathbb{E}[Y(D(z))]$. Since $D(z)=\mathbf{1}\{Type=complier\}z + \mathbf{1}\{AT\}$, difference numerator = $\mathbb{E}[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf{1}\{Complier\}]$. Denominator = $P(Complier)$. Ratio = $\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)|Complier]$ via law iterated [4][5].

Key extension: with covariates $W$, *conditional* Wald integrating over $W$ weighted by compliance score.

## 6. Limitations

- **Overlap / Positivity:** DML does *not* cure lack of overlap. If $e_0(W)\in\{0,1\}$, estimand undefined; trimming changes population. Effective sample size ESS diagnostics mandatory [1][4]. Propensity tails $e\approx0$ inflate variance as $1/(e(1-e))$; stabilized AIPW improves but not fully.
- **Unconfoundedness is unfalsifiable:** No test exists without extra structure. Sensitivity analysis à la Rosenbaum bounds $\Gamma$ quantifies how strong unobserved $U$ must be to overturn conclusion. Negative controls and placebo tests recommended.
- **Weak instruments:** IV DML inherits weak IV pathology: non-regular asymptotics, bimodal finite-sample distribution. First-stage $F$ > 20 and Anderson-Rubin robust CI (inversion of $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ test) are essential.
- **Heterogeneity search overfitting / false discovery:** Even honest forests can find spurious heterogeneity when $\tau_0$ constant but noise correlated with $X$. Best Linear Predictor calibration test [Chernozhukov et al. 2018 HLATE]: regress $Y$ residual on $(\hat{\tau}(X) - \bar{\hat{\tau}})$ and test $\beta_2=1$; if $p>0.05$, heterogeneity not significant.
- **Nuisance rate condition:** $n^{-1/4}$ requires approximability: ML class must contain truth with $s\log p = o(\sqrt{n})$ sparsity or Sobolev smoothness $\beta>d/2$. Deep nets with poor tuning may violate.
- **Computational cost:** K-fold DML trains $K$ times each nuisance (2 * K models for PLR). For large forests + big $p$, runtime $O(K n\log n)$ significant; but embarassingly parallelizable.
- **Interpretability:** CATE $\tau(x)$ from forest / XGB opaque; SHAP values on $\hat{\tau}$ may reflect $e(X)$ rather than causal mechanism; causal interpretability $\neq$ predictive explainability.

## 7. Conclusion

This thesis unifies causal identification and semiparametric efficient estimation under the **Neyman orthogonal DML umbrella**. Starting from Rubin's missing data law, we derived:

1.  **Identification** via ignorability, propensity balancing, and doubly robust augmentation.
2.  **IV LATE** for credible identification when ignorability fails but exogenous variation exists.
3.  **Efficient estimation** via orthogonal scores that immunize target $\theta$ against first-order errors of high-dimensional nuisances, plus cross-fitting that breaks dependence.
4.  **Heterogeneity** via R-learner loss $\mathcal{L}_R(\tau)=\mathbb{E}[(Y-m-\tau(D-e))^2]$ and causal forests as adaptive locally weighted DML estimators.

The practical prescription is clear:

> ***Never*** run $\hat{\tau}= \hat{\mu}_1-\hat{\mu}_0$ from single random forest without residualization. ***Always*** orthogonalize $Y$ and $D$ with cross-fitted $\hat{m},\hat{e}$, then learn $\tau$ on residuals with R-loss or forest kernel solving $\sum \alpha_i \psi=0$. Check overlap, report ESS, first-stage F, and calibration $\beta$ [1][2][3].

Future directions include DML with text/image confounders via foundation models as nuisance approximators (causal foundation models), automatically *orthogonalized* representation learning, mediation analysis with pathwise orthogonal scores, and policy learning $\pi(W)=\mathbf{1}\{\tau(W)>c\}$ that maximizes welfare under resource constraints — Double ML provides influence function for policy value enabling efficient policy optimization [6].

Ultimately, **causal inference is not prediction**: goodness of $R^2$ for $Y$ is orthogonal to unbiasedness for $\theta$. DML's conceptual contribution is making this mathematically precise: by requiring nuisance learning at only $n^{-1/4}$ and orthogonalizing influence, it separates *prediction quality* from *causal validity*, allowing economists and ML practitioners to use black-box ML without sacrificing credible $\sqrt{n}$ inference.
