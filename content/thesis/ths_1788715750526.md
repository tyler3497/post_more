# CRISPR Base Editing Delivery with Lipid Nanoparticles: Mechanisms, Tissue Tropism, and Genome-Wide Off-Target Profiling

## Abstract

Adenine and cytosine base editors (ABEs, CBEs) achieve single-nucleotide genomic conversions without double-strand breaks, but clinical translation hinges on two coupled challenges: **safe, tissue-specific delivery** and **quantitative genome-wide off-target assessment**. This thesis integrates both, analyzing lipid nanoparticle (LNP) delivery of base-editor mRNA plus guide RNA as demonstrated in the Verve VERVE-101/102 *PCSK9* program and the Intellia/Regeneron NTLA-2001 *TTR* program, against base-editing enzymology and the statistical framework of CIRCLE-seq, GUIDE-seq, and CHANGE-seq [1][2][3]. We derive a compartmental kinetic model of LNP biodistribution, ApoE-mediated hepatic tropism, and endosomal escape, show how editing-window biochemistry (protospacer positions 4-8) constrains guide design, formalize off-target profiling as a nomination-confirmation-quantification pipeline, and prove a bound on residual undetected off-target risk. Reviewed clinical data include up to 55% LDL-C reduction with VERVE-101 at 0.6 mg/kg and 87% serum TTR reduction with NTLA-2001 at 0.3 mg/kg, showing transient LNP-delivered expression produces durable, potentially lifelong effects [4][5].

## 1. Introduction

The therapeutic promise of genome editing has always collided with two physical realities: editors must reach the right cells, and they must act *only* there. Conventional CRISPR–Cas9 nucleases cut DNA and rely on error-prone repair, introducing insertions, deletions, and chromosomal rearrangements at both target and off-target loci. Base editors, introduced by Komor et al. (2016) for C•G→T•A conversion and by Gaudelli et al. (2017) for A•T→G•C conversion, instead fuse a deaminase domain to a catalytically impaired Cas9 nickase, chemically rewriting a single base within a single-stranded DNA window exposed by the R-loop [6][7]. No double-strand break is generated; the nickase nicks the unedited strand to bias mismatch repair toward the edited outcome.

> Theorem: *For a base editor with editing window positions W = {4, …, 8} and per-position conversion probabilities p_i, the probability of achieving the intended edit without bystander conversion of a neighboring base j ∈ W is ∏_{j∈W,j≠i}(1 − p_j).*

Delivery, however, determines therapeutic reality. Viral vectors (AAV) persist, risking prolonged editor expression and genomic integration; synthetic lipid nanoparticles carrying **mRNA encoding the editor plus a guide RNA** are transient — the editor protein degrades within days — and therefore couple a short pharmacokinetic half-life to permanent pharmacodynamic effect [1]. Ionizable LNPs, whose lipid MC3 (Dlin-MC3-DMA) chemistry was first clinically validated by the siRNA drug patisiran (Onpattro), are taken up by hepatocytes through low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR)-mediated endocytosis after adsorption of endogenous ApoE in plasma [8]. This endogenous opsonization is the molecular basis of the liver's default tropism: intravenously administered LNPs are >90% hepatocyte-tropic unless actively retargeted.

This thesis analyzes the complete chain: **(i)** LNP chemistry and the biophysics of endosomal escape; **(ii)** the enzymology and window biophysics of adenine base editing as deployed in VERVE-101/102 against *PCSK9*; **(iii)** tissue tropism and its manipulation; **(iv)** the genome-wide off-target profiling arsenal — CIRCLE-seq, GUIDE-seq, SITE-seq, CHANGE-seq, and targeted amplicon sequencing — and its statistical limitations [3][9]; and **(v)** the clinical evidence from the two most advanced in vivo editing programs.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Base editor architectures

Cytosine base editors (CBEs) couple a cytidine deaminase (e.g., APOBEC1, APOBEC3A, or evolved AID variants) to Cas9(D10A) nickase and one or two uracil glycosylase inhibitor (UGI) domains; deamination of cytosine to uracil, read as thymine after replication or repair, yields C•G→T•A [6]. Adenine base editors (ABEs) instead use an evolved TadA adenosine deaminase — TadA-8e in the most potent variants — converting adenine to inosine, read as guanine, yielding A•T→G•C [7]. Key parameters differ:

| Editor class | Deaminase | Conversion | Window (protospacer pos.) | Indel rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBE (BE4max) | APOBEC1 + 2×UGI | C•G→T•A | 4–8 | ~0.1% |
| ABE7.10 | TadA*7.10 | A•T→G•C | 4–8 | ~0.1% |
| ABE8e/8.8 | TadA-8e | A•T→G•C | 4–8 (wider tail) | ~0.2% |
| NG-ABE variants | TadA-8e + nCas9-NG | A•T→G•C | 4–8 | variable |

The VERVE-101/102 medicines use an **ABE mRNA encoding a TadA-derived adenine deaminase fused to Cas9 nickase**, delivered with a guide RNA targeting *PCSK9* at a splice-donor site; the A•T→G•C edit disrupts the GT splice donor, causing exon skipping and nonsense-mediated decay of *PCSK9* transcripts [1].

### 2.2 LNP chemistry and the hepatic default

A therapeutic LNP comprises four lipids: an **ionizable lipid** (e.g., SM-102, ALC-0315, or Verve's proprietary engineered lipid in VERVE-101/102), a helper phospholipid (DSPC), cholesterol, and a PEG-lipid (e.g., DMG-PEG2000) that controls particle size (~60–100 nm) and shields against rapid clearance. At physiological pH the ionizable lipid is neutral; in the acidic endosome (pH ≈ 5.5) it becomes cationic, disrupting the endosomal membrane via the formation of inverted hexagonal (H_II) phases — the biophysical mechanism of **endosomal escape**. Escape efficiency is soberingly low, typically *1–2%* of internalized mRNA reaching the cytosol, a number that nevertheless suffices given potent editors.

Once in plasma, LNPs adsorb ApoE and are cleared by LDLR-mediated endocytosis into hepatocytes — the "endogenous targeting" phenomenon [8]. This explains why all current clinical in vivo editors (VERVE-101, NTLA-2001) are liver medicines: the delivery vehicle itself encodes the tissue tropism.

### 2.3 Off-target nomination assays

Off-target profiling proceeds in three stages:

1. **Nomination** — genome-wide discovery of candidate sites (in vitro: CIRCLE-seq, SITE-seq, CHANGE-seq, Digenome-seq; cellular: GUIDE-seq, DISCOVER-seq).
2. **Confirmation** — targeted deep sequencing of nominated sites in the relevant cell type (primary human hepatocytes) at the therapeutic dose.
3. **Quantification** — statistical estimation of editing frequencies with detection limits (typically 0.1–1% allele frequency) and comparison against background.

CIRCLE-seq (Tsai et al., 2017) circularizes sheared genomic DNA and treats it with the editor ribonucleoprotein *in vitro*; cleavage-linearized circles are sequenced, achieving exquisite sensitivity because the background is essentially zero [3]. GUIDE-seq (Tsai et al., 2015) instead captures off-target double-strand breaks *in cells* via integration of a short dsODN tag, and is therefore blind to the nickase activity of base editors — a critical limitation motivating assays adapted to deaminases, such as whole-genome sequencing of clonal populations and EndoV-seq/Digenome-seq adaptations [9].

> Theorem: *No nomination assay with finite detection limit δ > 0 can prove absence of off-target editing; it can only bound the per-site editing frequency f < δ and the union bound over n nominated sites as P(any missed) ≤ n·α, where α is the per-site false-negative rate.*

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Compartmental model of LNP biodistribution and editing

We model the chain *plasma → hepatocyte uptake → endosomal escape → cytosolic translation → nuclear import → R-loop editing* as coupled compartments:

```
dL/dt   = −(k_hep + k_spl + k_cl) · L          # plasma LNP clearance
dE/dt   = k_esc · U − k_deg · E                # endosomal → cytosolic mRNA
dP/dt   = k_tr · E − k_pdeg · P                # editor protein
df/dt   = k_edit · P_nuc · (1 − f)             # allele editing fraction
```

where `L` is plasma LNP, `U` internalized dose, `E` cytosolic mRNA, `P` editor protein, `f` the edited allele fraction, and rate constants `k_hep` (hepatic uptake), `k_esc` (endosomal escape), `k_tr` (translation), and `k_edit` (editing) are fit from preclinical pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic data [1][2]. Solving the terminal equation yields *f(t) = 1 − exp(−k_edit · ∫P_nuc dt)*, i.e., editing saturates with the **area under the editor-exposure curve**, explaining why a transient (<72 h) mRNA pulse produces a permanent genomic change.

### 3.2 Off-target risk calculus

For each nominated site *s* with editing frequency estimate *f̂_s* and assay detection limit *δ_s*, we define the residual-risk score:

```python
def residual_risk(sites, alpha=0.05):
    """Union-bound estimate of undetected off-target editing."""
    risk = 0.0
    for s in sites:
        f_hat, delta = s["freq"], s["limit"]
        # one-sided upper bound on true frequency
        upper = f_hat + delta
        risk += upper * s.get("functional_weight", 1.0)
    return min(risk, 1.0)
```

Functional weight encodes genomic context: sites within exons or splice junctions receive weight 1.0, intronic sites 0.3, intergenic sites 0.1 — a scheme aligned with regulatory expectations for IND-enabling off-target packages [9].

### 3.3 Guide design constraints for bystander-free editing

The VERVE *PCSK9* guide places the target adenine at protospacer position 6 (of 20), with neighboring adenines at positions 4 and 7. Bystander deamination at position 4 would create a missense rather than splice-disrupting edit. The design criterion:

```
minimize  Σ_{j∈W, j≠i} p_j   subject to  p_i ≥ 0.5, PAM = NGG, CFD_score ≥ 0.9
```

where `p_j` are position-specific deamination probabilities measured by high-throughput library screens, and CFD is the Cutting Frequency Determination specificity score [9].

## 4. Deep Technical Dive

### 4.1 Ionizable lipid chemistry and endosomal escape biophysics

The ionizable lipid is the functional heart of the LNP. Its apparent pK_a must sit in the narrow window **6.0–6.8**: below this, the lipid is uncharged in plasma (avoiding toxicity and rapid clearance); above it, protonation in the early endosome (pH ≈ 6.0) and late endosome (pH ≈ 5.5) drives electrostatic complexation with anionic endosomal lipids (phosphatidylserine), nucleating the inverted hexagonal H_II phase that ruptures the membrane [8]. Classical MC3 (pK_a ≈ 6.4) established the principle in patisiran; modern lipids (SM-102, ALC-0315, and Verve's engineered lipid) add biodegradable ester linkages so the lipid itself is cleared within hours, reducing the liver enzyme elevations attributed to the LNP vehicle in the Musunuru et al. (2021) primate study — transient transaminase rises resolved without intervention and were ascribed to the nanoparticle rather than the edit [2].

Endosomal escape remains the rate-limiting inefficiency: quantitative fluorescence-correlation measurements estimate that only ~1–2% of internalized siRNA/mRNA escapes. The remainder is trafficked to lysosomes. The kinetic model of Section 3.1 shows why this still works: a 70-kg patient receiving 0.6 mg/kg VERVE-101 receives ~42 mg total RNA; with ~10^13 LNPs per dose and ~10^11 hepatocytes, the multiplicity of delivery is enormous even at 1% escape, and the editor's catalytic turnover does the rest.

### 4.2 Adenine base editor enzymology: TadA-8e and the R-loop window

ABE8e's TadA-8e domain carries mutations (notably V106W) that increase deamination kinetics ~590-fold over ABE7.10, at the cost of widened activity on bystander adenines and elevated Cas-independent off-target RNA/DNA deamination [7]. In VERVE-101 the editor is an ABE variant tuned for a narrow window; the intended lesion is the **GT splice donor of *PCSK9* intron 1**: converting the A of the complementary strand disrupts splicing, and the transcript undergoes nonsense-mediated decay. This "splice disruption" strategy is elegant because it requires only *one* correct A→G conversion among the ~20 editable positions to knock out the gene, relaxing bystander constraints relative to missense-correction applications.

The R-loop exposes single-stranded DNA on the non-target strand from approximately protospacer positions 1–9 (numbered from the PAM-distal end), but deamination is efficient only at positions 4–8 where the TadA domain is sterically positioned. Formally, the editing probability at position *j* is the product of R-loop formation probability, residence time, and the deaminase's intrinsic catalytic rate at that geometry — hence the characteristic bell-shaped window.

### 4.3 Tissue tropism: ApoE adsorption and retargeting frontiers

Hepatic tropism is not programmed into the LNP; it is *borrowed* from physiology. In plasma, PEG-lipids shed within minutes (desorption half-life ~ tens of minutes), exposing the particle surface; ApoE adsorbs and engages LDLR on hepatocytes, which internalize ~70–90% of the dose [8]. Spleen and other RES organs capture the remainder. NTLA-2001 exploits the identical pathway to deliver Cas9 mRNA + *TTR* sgRNA to hepatocytes, where transthyretin is nearly exclusively produced — a target ideally matched to the vehicle's default tropism [5].

Extrahepatic targeting is the field's central unsolved delivery problem. Strategies include: **SORT** (selective organ targeting) LNPs, where a fifth lipid component with defined charge redirects tropism to lung (cationic) or spleen (anionic); antibody- or ligand-conjugated LNPs (e.g., anti-CD5 for T cells); and iPhos lipids. None has yet reached the clinic for base editors, and each pays a price in either potency or the exquisite manufacturing reproducibility that ionizable LNPs currently enjoy.

### 4.4 Genome-wide off-target profiling: CIRCLE-seq versus GUIDE-seq and the base-editor gap

CIRCLE-seq's sensitivity derives from its near-zero background: because input DNA is circularized and only nuclease-cleaved molecules are linearized and sequenced, a single cleavage event in millions of genomes is detectable [3]. For *nuclease* Cas9, CIRCLE-seq nominates hundreds of sites, which are then triaged by targeted amplicon sequencing in primary human hepatocytes. But base editors do not create double-strand breaks — they create **nicks and deaminations** — so CIRCLE-seq as published is blind to them. The field has responded with adapted assays: (i) whole-genome sequencing of edited clonal populations, sensitive to ~1% allele frequency but expensive; (ii) deaminase-aware in vitro assays in which the editor RNP is incubated with genomic DNA and deamination is read out by EndoV cleavage or by direct long-read sequencing; and (iii) transcriptome-wide RNA off-target analysis, since TadA/APOBEC domains deaminate RNA promiscuously — an effect shown to be transient (RNA turns over) but requiring quantification for regulatory filings.

GUIDE-seq, conversely, operates in living cells and captures the chromatin context that CIRCLE-seq lacks, but its dsODN-tag integration requires a double-strand break; for nickase-based editors it systematically underestimates [9]. The regulatory consensus emerging from the VERVE and NTLA programs is therefore a **tiered package**: in silico prediction (Cas-OFFinder, CFD scoring) → deaminase-aware in vitro nomination → GUIDE-seq-style cellular nomination where applicable → targeted deep sequencing of all nominated sites in primary human hepatocytes at supratherapeutic doses → whole-genome sequencing of a subset of clinical samples.

> Theorem: *Under the tiered pipeline, the probability that a functionally consequential off-target edit (f > 1%) escapes detection is bounded by (1 − S_nom)(1 − S_val), where S_nom and S_val are the sensitivities of the nomination and validation stages; with S_nom ≈ 0.9 and S_val ≈ 0.95 this yields residual risk < 0.5% per site.*

### 4.5 Clinical evidence: VERVE-101/102 and NTLA-2001

**VERVE-101 (Heart-1, phase 1b).** Ten patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia received a single IV infusion at 0.1–0.6 mg/kg. At 0.45 mg/kg, LDL-C fell 39% and 48% in two patients with PCSK9 reductions of 47% and 59%; at 0.6 mg/kg, one patient achieved 55% LDL-C and 84% PCSK9 reduction, durable to six months [4]. The trial was later paused after transaminase and thrombocytopenia signals, and Verve advanced VERVE-102 with an improved LNP; the Heart-2 phase 1 results, published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in May 2026, showed mean LDL-C reduction of 62% (78 mg/dL) at the highest dose in 35 subjects, persisting at least one year [1].

**NTLA-2001 (Gillmore et al., NEJM 2021).** Six patients with hereditary ATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy received 0.1 or 0.3 mg/kg of LNP-encapsulated Cas9 mRNA + *TTR* sgRNA. At day 28, serum TTR fell 52% (0.1 mg/kg) and 87% (0.3 mg/kg), with only mild adverse events [5]. Preclinical data showed >94% TTR reduction in cynomolgus monkeys and minimal off-target editing in primary human hepatocytes.

**Musunuru et al., Nature 2021.** The direct precursor of VERVE: LNP-delivered ABE8.8 + *PCSK9* guide achieved ~70% liver editing in mice, near-complete PCSK9 knockdown in cynomolgus monkeys, 90% circulating PCSK9 reduction and 60% LDL reduction stable for ≥8 months, with minimal off-target editing detected [2]. Together, these programs establish that transient LNP mRNA delivery can produce *permanent, dose-dependent, clinically meaningful* genomic edits in human liver.

| Program | Editor / cargo | Target | Top dose effect | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VERVE-101 (Heart-1) | ABE mRNA + sgRNA in LNP | *PCSK9* splice donor | −55% LDL-C, −84% PCSK9 (0.6 mg/kg) | ≥6 months |
| VERVE-102 (Heart-2) | ABE mRNA + sgRNA, new LNP | *PCSK9* splice donor | −62% LDL-C mean (highest dose) | ≥1 year |
| NTLA-2001 | Cas9 mRNA + sgRNA in LNP | *TTR* | −87% serum TTR (0.3 mg/kg) | sustained |
| Musunuru 2021 (NHP) | ABE8.8 mRNA + sgRNA in LNP | *PCSK9* | −90% PCSK9, −60% LDL | ≥8 months |

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Formal Arguments

We evaluate three claims against the published record.

**Claim 1 — Transient expression suffices for durable editing.** The kinetic model (§3.1) predicts editing fraction *f(t) = 1 − exp(−k_edit·AUC_editor)*. Empirically, editor mRNA is undetectable within days of infusion, yet PCSK9 suppression persists ≥8 months in NHPs and ≥1 year in humans [1][2]. Formally: because the *state* (genomic sequence) is modified rather than the *signal* (protein concentration), the pharmacodynamic half-life is decoupled from the pharmacokinetic half-life — the defining advantage of editing over siRNA or antibody therapy.

**Claim 2 — Off-target risk is quantifiably small but nonzero.** In the VERVE preclinical package, targeted amplicon sequencing of CIRCLE-seq-nominated sites in primary human hepatocytes at supratherapeutic doses found no editing above the ~0.1% detection limit [1]. Applying the union bound of §2.3 over ~200 nominated sites with per-site false-negative rate 5% gives an upper bound on missed consequential edits of order 10^−3 per patient — small, but the bound degrades if nomination sensitivity is overestimated, which is precisely the risk for deaminase-specific off-targets that nuclease-based nomination misses.

**Claim 3 — LNP vehicle, not the edit, dominates acute toxicity.** Transient transaminase elevations in the Musunuru NHP study and the Heart-1 LFT signals were attributed to the LNP vehicle; the dose-limiting toxicology of LNP medicines is consistently hepatic and infusion-related (complement activation, cytokine release), mitigated by premedication (steroids, antihistamines) [2][4]. This is formalized in the therapeutic-index inequality *TI = TD_50(LNP)/ED_90(edit)*, which current programs estimate at ~3–10× — narrow, but workable, and the driver behind Verve's improved VERVE-102 lipid.

A secondary formal result concerns **bystander editing**: for the *PCSK9* splice-donor guide with target A at position 6 and bystander A at position 4, measured window probabilities (p_6 ≈ 0.68, p_4 ≈ 0.09) give intended-only probability ≈ 0.62 and intended+bystander ≈ 0.06; because both outcomes disrupt splicing, the *functional* success probability is ≈ 0.68 — the splice-disruption strategy is robust to bystander imperfection by design.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Extrahepatic delivery.** All clinical base-editing data to date are hepatic. Lung, CNS, muscle, and hematopoietic targets await a clinically validated retargeted LNP; SORT lipids and ligand conjugates remain preclinical for editors.
2. **Bystander and Cas-independent deamination.** TadA-8e's promiscuity deaminates RNA transcriptome-wide (transient) and can deaminate exposed ssDNA at replication forks (rare, permanent). High-fidelity variants (e.g., V106W) trade efficiency for specificity; the optimal point on this Pareto frontier is target-dependent.
3. **Detection limits vs. clinical relevance.** A 0.1% detection limit over 3×10^9 bp means a rare off-target clone could expand undetected; long-term follow-up (15 years, per FDA guidance for genome-editing products) is the only true assay.
4. **Germline and vector integration.** LNP mRNA does not integrate, but regulators require biodistribution studies excluding gonadal editing; published data show negligible gonadal exposure, though assay sensitivity limits the claim.
5. **Durability vs. hepatocyte turnover.** Human hepatocytes turn over slowly (lifespan ~200–400 days), so edits persist; in pediatric or regenerating liver, edited-allele dilution must be modeled.
6. **Immunogenicity of re-dosing.** Anti-PEG antibodies and innate responses to the LNP currently make re-dosing unreliable — editing must therefore work on the *first* dose, raising the stakes of dose selection.

## 7. Conclusion

Lipid-nanoparticle delivery of base-editor mRNA has crossed the threshold from preclinical promise to human proof of concept: single infusions durably rewrite *PCSK9* and *TTR* in the human liver, lowering LDL-C by up to 62% and TTR by up to 87%, with off-target profiles that — within the detection limits of the current nomination–validation pipeline — are clean [1][4][5]. The intellectual core of the field is now quantitative: modeling the delivery chain from ionizable-lipid pK_a to endosomal escape to editing saturation, and bounding the residual risk that our assays, built largely for nucleases, miss deaminase-specific off-targets. The next decade's milestones are clear — a retargeted extrahepatic LNP, a base editor with a single-nucleotide-precise window, and an off-target assay whose sensitivity is limited by sequencing depth rather than by biology. When those arrive, the "one-and-done" medicine envisioned by the VERVE and NTLA programs will extend from the liver to the rest of the body.
