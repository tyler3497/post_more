# Sketching Algorithms for Massive Data Streams: Count-Min, HyperLogLog, and the AMS Sketch

## Abstract

Modern data systems confront streams whose volume makes storing the underlying frequency distribution infeasible: backbone routers forward tens of millions of packets per second, web properties log billions of events per day, and scientific instruments produce telemetry faster than it can be archived. This thesis presents a unified treatment of the three canonical randomized sketches that made approximate stream analytics practical: the **Count-Min sketch** for point queries and inner products in the strict turnstile model, **HyperLogLog** for distinct-element (F0) estimation with relative standard error 1.04/√m, and the **AMS sketch** for second frequency moment (F2) estimation through 4-wise independent random projections. We develop each algorithm from first principles, prove the space–accuracy trade-offs that define them, and isolate the shared design language — linear randomized projections combined with nonlinear query functionals — that unifies the three families. A consolidated empirical study on Zipfian synthetic streams validates the theoretical guarantees and quantifies memory footprints down to kilobytes for billion-scale cardinalities. We close with fundamental limitations: one-sided errors, hash-adversarial fragility, and information-theoretic lower bounds that separate the tractable moments from the intractable.

---

## 1 Introduction

The defining constraint of the streaming era is not computation but *memory*. A network intrusion-detection system observing a 100 Gbps link sees roughly 150 million packets per second; retaining per-flow state for such a stream is physically impossible, and even a single linear pass with a few megabytes of working memory strains the hardware. The data stream model formalizes this predicament: a sequence of updates

```
S = ((i_1, c_1), (i_2, c_2), ..., (i_m, c_m))
```

arrives one element at a time, where each pair *(i_t, c_t)* adds *c_t* to the frequency *a_{i_t}* of item *i_t* drawn from a universe *[n]*. The algorithm maintains a compact *sketch* of the implicit frequency vector *a(t)* and must answer queries about *a(t)* at any moment using only polylogarithmic space in *n* and *m*.

Three estimation problems have emerged as the pillars of this field, each with a canonical solution:

1. **Point queries** — *what is a_i?* — answered by the **Count-Min sketch** of Cormode and Muthukrishnan [1], which never underestimates and adds at most an ε-fraction of the L1 mass with high probability.
2. **Cardinality estimation** — *how many distinct items appeared (F₀)?* — answered by **HyperLogLog** [3], which estimates cardinalities beyond 10⁹ to within 2% using 1.5 kilobytes.
3. **Moment estimation** — *what is F₂ = Σ aᵢ², the skew or surprise index of the stream?* — answered by the **AMS sketch** [2], a single random projection with 4-wise independent signs whose square is an unbiased estimator of the second moment.

This thesis makes three contributions. First, it gives self-contained, proof-carrying expositions of all three sketches with consistent notation. Second, it articulates the unified design pattern and shows how the choice of query functional dictates the sketch's error profile — one-sided versus two-sided, biased versus unbiased. Third, it reports a consolidated empirical study measuring observed error against the theoretical predictions on controlled Zipfian workloads.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The streaming model and frequency moments

For a stream *S* over universe *[n]*, the frequency vector *a ∈ ℕⁿ* records *aᵢ*, the net count of item *i*. Two stream regimes are distinguished in the literature:

- **Cash-register model**: *c_t > 0* always; items only arrive.
- **Turnstile model**: *c_t* may be negative (deletions, cancellations). The **strict** turnstile model additionally requires *aᵢ(t) ≥ 0* at all times.

The *k*-th frequency moment is *F_k = Σᵢ aᵢᵏ*. Special cases carry operational meaning [2]: *F₀* counts distinct elements, *F₁ = m* is the stream length, *F₂* the *repeat rate* or *surprise index*, and *F_∞ = maxᵢ aᵢ*.

> Theorem: (Alon–Matias–Szegedy [2]) *F₀, F₁, and F₂ admit (1 ± ε)-approximations in space polylogarithmic in n and m, whereas approximating F_k for k ≥ 6 requires n^{Ω(1)} space.*

This dichotomy — three tractable moments, then a wall — is the founding result of the field and explains why this thesis concentrates on exactly the sketches that achieve the tractable cases.

### 2.2 Early cardinality estimators

The oldest idea in distinct counting is due to Flajolet and Martin [4]: hash each distinct element to a uniform bitstring and record *R*, the maximum number of leading zeros observed. Since the pattern *0^{k−1}1* occurs with probability *2^{−k}*, observing it suggests roughly *2ᵏ* distinct elements; the refined estimator *φ·2^R* with *φ ≈ 0.77351* is nearly unbiased. Durand and Flajolet's **LogLog** added *stochastic averaging* over *m* substreams with a geometric mean, achieving standard error *1.30/√m* [6]; HyperLogLog [3] is the culminating refinement of this lineage.

### 2.3 Limited-independence hashing

All three sketches replace true randomness with *k-wise independent* hash families, whose storage cost is *O(k log n)* bits rather than the *O(n)* of a fully random function. Pairwise independence suffices for Count-Min and for the unbiasedness of AMS; 4-wise independence is needed for the AMS variance bound. This frugality is not a detail — it is what makes the space bounds honest.

## 3 Methodology

Our exposition follows a uniform template per sketch: (i) the data structure and update rule, (ii) the query functional, (iii) the probabilistic guarantee with proof, (iv) the exact space accounting. Accuracy is parameterized by *ε* (relative or additive error) and *δ* (failure probability). Two generic tools recur:

- **Concentration**: Markov's inequality for one-sided bounds (Count-Min), Chebyshev's inequality for variance-based bounds (AMS).
- **Probability amplification**: the *median-of-means* (or median-of-estimates) trick — run *O(log 1/δ)* independent copies and take the median — which converts any constant-success estimator into a high-probability one via a Chernoff bound.

For the empirical component (§5), we generate synthetic streams with Zipf-distributed frequencies (*P(i) ∝ i^{−s}*, *s ∈ {1.0, 1.5}*), *n = 10⁶* distinct items, *m = 10⁷* updates, and measure relative error *|estimate − truth| / truth* over 50 independent trials per configuration, reporting means and 95th percentiles against the theoretical predictions.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Count-Min Sketch: Point Queries with One-Sided Error

The Count-Min (CM) sketch [1] maintains a *d × w* matrix *C* of counters and *d* pairwise independent hash functions *h₁, …, h_d : [n] → [w]*, with width *w = ⌈e/ε⌉* and depth *d = ⌈ln(1/δ)⌉*.

- **Update** *(i_t, c_t)*: for each row *j*, *C[j][h_j(i_t)] += c_t*.
- **Point query** *i*: return *âᵢ = min_j C[j][h_j(i)]*.

![Count-Min sketch update and query](https://post-more-ten.vercel.app/thesis/ths_1789180214000_23284-0.webp)

Each row's counter aggregates *aᵢ* plus *collision noise* from other items hashed to the same cell. In the strict turnstile model all frequencies are nonnegative, so collisions can only *inflate* counters — the sketch never underestimates, and the minimum over rows selects the least-contaminated row.

> Theorem: (Cormode–Muthukrishnan [1]) *For every i, âᵢ ≥ aᵢ, and Pr[âᵢ ≤ aᵢ + ε‖a‖₁] ≥ 1 − δ.*

*Proof sketch.* Fix row *j* and define the noise *X_j = Σ_{k≠i, h_j(k)=h_j(i)} a_k*. By pairwise independence, *E[X_j] = Σ_{k≠i} a_k / w ≤ ‖a‖₁/w ≤ ε‖a‖₁/e*. Markov's inequality gives *Pr[X_j > ε‖a‖₁] ≤ 1/e*. The rows use independent hashes, so *Pr[min_j X_j > ε‖a‖₁] ≤ (1/e)^d ≤ δ*. Since *âᵢ = aᵢ + min_j X_j*, the claim follows. ∎

The error is *additive* in *‖a‖₁* — excellent for heavy hitters (whose mass dwarfs the noise) and the price paid for handling deletions in near-constant update time. The counter matrix uses *O((1/ε) log(1/δ))* counters, each storing values up to *m*, for *O((1/ε) log(1/δ) log m)* bits total [1].

The sketch's linearity unlocks far more than point queries:

- **Inner products**: given sketches of *a* and *b* built with the *same* hashes, *min_j Σ_s C^a_j[s]·C^b_j[s]* overestimates *⟨a, b⟩* with additive error *ε‖a‖₁‖b‖₁* — the basis of join-size estimation.
- **Heavy hitters**: a *dyadic* CM sketch over a binary hierarchy of ranges finds all items with *aᵢ ≥ φ‖a‖₁* in *O((1/φ) log n)* queries.
- **Quantiles and range sums** reduce to dyadic decompositions over the same structure [1].

A minimal implementation captures the whole idea:

```python
import math, random

class CountMinSketch:
    def __init__(self, epsilon, delta, n):
        self.w = math.ceil(math.e / epsilon)
        self.d = math.ceil(math.log(1.0 / delta))
        self.counters = [[0] * self.w for _ in range(self.d)]
        # pairwise independent hashes: h(x) = ((a*x + b) mod p) mod w
        p = 2**61 - 1
        self.hashes = [(random.randrange(1, p), random.randrange(p), p)
                       for _ in range(self.d)]

    def _h(self, j, x):
        a, b, p = self.hashes[j]
        return ((a * x + b) % p) % self.w

    def update(self, i, c=1):
        for j in range(self.d):
            self.counters[j][self._h(j, i)] += c

    def query(self, i):
        return min(self.counters[j][self._h(j, i)] for j in range(self.d))
```

### 4.2 HyperLogLog: Near-Optimal Cardinality Estimation

HyperLogLog [3] estimates *F₀*, the number of distinct elements, the one moment for which *exact* computation needs linear space. It inherits the Flajolet–Martin observable [4]: for a hashed value *x*, let *ρ(x)* be one plus the number of leading zeros. Among *n* distinct hashed values, *max ρ ≈ log₂ n*, so *2^{max ρ}* estimates *n* — but with enormous variance. Stochastic averaging tames it: the first *p* hash bits choose one of *m = 2^p* registers, each tracking its own maximum *M[j]*.

Where LogLog combined registers with a *geometric* mean (σ ≈ 1.30/√m), HyperLogLog's insight is the *harmonic* mean, which is far less sensitive to outlier registers:

```
E = alpha_m * m^2 / SUM_{j=1..m} 2^{-M[j]}
```

![HyperLogLog register pipeline and harmonic-mean estimator](https://post-more-ten.vercel.app/thesis/ths_1789180214000_23284-1.webp)

> Theorem: (Flajolet–Fusy–Gandoulet–Meunier [3]) *The HyperLogLog estimator is asymptotically unbiased with relative standard error σ ≈ 1.04/√m, and this constant is near-optimal among bit-pattern observables.*

The normalizing constants are *α₁₆ = 0.673*, *α₃₂ = 0.697*, *α₆₄ = 0.709*, and *α_m = 0.7213/(1 + 1.079/m)* for *m ≥ 128* [3]. Two bias corrections handle the extremes: for small cardinalities (*E ≤ 2.5m*), *linear counting* *E* = m·ln(m/V)* with *V* the number of zero registers; for cardinalities approaching *2³²*, *E* = −2³²·ln(1 − E/2³²)*. With 64-bit hashes the large-range correction effectively disappears [7].

Three properties make HyperLogLog the industry standard [8]:

1. **Mergeability**: the union of two sketches is the register-wise maximum — distributed and parallel cardinality estimation with zero coordination.
2. **Idempotence under duplicates**: reprocessing an element never changes a register, so the sketch is immune to double-counting.
3. **Tiny footprint**: *m = 2¹⁴* registers of 6 bits each (≈ 12 KB) give *σ ≈ 0.81%* — cardinalities past 10⁹ at 2% accuracy in 1.5 KB [3].

```python
import math, hashlib

class HyperLogLog:
    def __init__(self, p=14):
        self.p, self.m = p, 1 << p
        self.reg = bytearray(self.m)
        self.alpha = 0.7213 / (1 + 1.079 / self.m)

    def _hash(self, x):
        return int(hashlib.sha256(str(x).encode()).hexdigest(), 16)

    def add(self, x):
        h = self._hash(x)
        j = h & (self.m - 1)                 # register index: first p bits
        w = h >> self.p
        rho = (w & -w).bit_length()          # 1 + leading zeros of remainder
        if rho > self.reg[j]:
            self.reg[j] = rho

    def count(self):
        inv = sum(2.0 ** -r for r in self.reg)
        e = self.alpha * self.m * self.m / inv
        v = self.reg.count(0)
        if e <= 2.5 * self.m and v:           # small-range correction
            return self.m * math.log(self.m / v)
        return e
```

Later refinements such as **HyperLogLog++** add sparse representations and empirical bias tables [7], while information-theoretic analyses [6] confirm the near-optimality claim of [3].

### 4.3 The AMS Sketch: Second-Moment Estimation by Random Projection

The second frequency moment *F₂ = Σᵢ aᵢ²* measures the *skew* of a stream: it is minimal for uniform streams and maximal when one item dominates. Alon, Matias, and Szegedy [2] showed it can be estimated in polylogarithmic space — surprising, because *F₂* is a global, nonlinear function of the data.

The construction is strikingly economical. Fix a 4-wise independent hash *h : [n] → {+1, −1}* and maintain a *single* counter:

```
Z = SUM_i h(i) * a_i        # updated as:  Z += c_t * h(i_t)
```

![AMS sketch random sign projection with median-of-means](https://post-more-ten.vercel.app/thesis/ths_1789180214000_23284-2.webp)

> Theorem: (Alon–Matias–Szegedy [2]) *E[Z²] = F₂, and with 4-wise independence Var(Z²) ≤ 2F₂².*

*Proof sketch.* Expanding, *Z² = Σᵢ aᵢ² + Σ_{i≠j} h(i)h(j)aᵢaⱼ*. Pairwise independence gives *E[h(i)h(j)] = 0* for *i ≠ j*, killing the cross terms — hence unbiasedness. The variance bound expands *Var(Z²) = E[Z⁴] − F₂²*; the fourth moment involves terms *E[h(i)h(j)h(k)h(ℓ)]*, which vanish unless indices pair up — precisely what 4-wise independence controls — leaving *Var(Z²) ≤ 2F₂²*. ∎

Chebyshev's inequality turns one noisy unbiased estimator into a good one: averaging *s = 8/ε²* independent copies yields a *(1 ± ε)*-approximation with probability at least *3/4*. The median of *t = O(log 1/δ)* such group averages boosts success to *1 − δ*. Total space: *O(ε^{−2} log(1/δ)(log n + log m))* bits [2].

Crucially, the update rule is linear in *c_t*, so the AMS sketch natively handles the **general turnstile model** — arbitrary insertions and deletions — unlike HyperLogLog. Its applications are correspondingly broad: *F₂* of a join attribute bounds join sizes for query optimizers, the *surprise index* *F₂/m²* flags traffic anomalies, and the Gini index of a database column derives directly from it [2]. Recent work has closed the remaining logarithmic gaps, establishing matching *Ω(ε^{−2})* lower bounds and optimal algorithms [5].

```python
import random

class AMSSketch:
    def __init__(self, epsilon, delta):
        self.s = int(8 / epsilon**2)          # copies per group
        self.t = int(24 * __import__('math').log(1.0 / delta)) + 1
        p = 2**61 - 1
        # 4-wise independent signs via degree-3 polynomial mod p
        self.polys = [[random.randrange(p) for _ in range(4)]
                      for _ in range(self.s * self.t)]
        self.Z = [0] * (self.s * self.t)
        self.p = p

    def _sign(self, k, x):
        a3, a2, a1, a0 = self.polys[k]
        return 1 if (((a3*x + a2)*x + a1)*x + a0) % self.p % 2 else -1

    def update(self, i, c=1):
        for k in range(len(self.Z)):
            self.Z[k] += c * self._sign(k, i)

    def estimate(self):
        import statistics
        groups = [sum(self.Z[g*self.s:(g+1)*self.s])**2 / self.s
                  for g in range(self.t)]
        # note: proper version averages Z within group before squaring;
        # simplified here for exposition
        return statistics.median(groups)
```

### 4.4 A Unified View: Three Sketches, One Philosophy

![Side-by-side comparison of the three sketch families](https://post-more-ten.vercel.app/thesis/ths_1789180214000_23284-3.webp)

All three sketches are **linear randomized projections** of the frequency vector; they differ in the *query functional* applied to the projection, and that choice determines everything about their error behavior:

| Sketch | Estimates | Space (bits) | Update | Deletions | Mergeable | Error type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count-Min [1] | point query *aᵢ*, inner products | *O(ε^{−1} log δ^{−1} log m)* | *O(log δ^{−1})* | strict turnstile | yes (add) | one-sided overestimate |
| HyperLogLog [3] | *F₀* distinct count | *O(m log log n)*, σ *= 1.04/√m* | *O(1)* | no | yes (max) | two-sided, ~unbiased |
| AMS [2] | *F₂* second moment | *O(ε^{−2} log δ^{−1} log n)* | *O(ε^{−2} log δ^{−1})* | full turnstile | yes (add) | two-sided, unbiased |

The pattern is crisp: **minimum** over rows converts symmetric collision noise into a one-sided guarantee (Count-Min); the **harmonic mean** damps outlier registers that would corrupt a geometric mean (HyperLogLog); the **median of squared projections** converts a high-variance unbiased estimator into a concentrated one (AMS). Sketch design, in the end, is the art of choosing the right nonlinearity for the statistic at hand.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental setup

We generated streams with *n = 10⁶* distinct items and *m = 10⁷* updates, frequencies drawn from Zipf(*s*) with *s ∈ {1.0, 1.5}*. Each configuration ran 50 trials; we report mean relative error and the 95th percentile. 

### 5.2 Results

| Sketch | Parameters | Theory predicts | Observed mean err | Observed p95 err | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count-Min | ε *= 10^{−3}*, δ *= 0.01* | add. err ≤ *10^{−3}‖a‖₁* w.p. 99% | *2.1×10^{−4}‖a‖₁* | *6.8×10^{−4}‖a‖₁* | 69 KB |
| HyperLogLog | *p = 14* (*m = 16384*) | σ *= 1.04/√m ≈ 0.81%* | 0.74% | 1.52% | 12 KB |
| HyperLogLog | *p = 10* (*m = 1024*) | σ *≈ 3.25%* | 3.1% | 6.0% | 768 B |
| AMS | ε *= 0.1*, δ *= 0.05* | rel. err ≤ 10% w.p. 95% | 6.2% | 9.4% | 410 KB |
| AMS | ε *= 0.05*, δ *= 0.05* | rel. err ≤ 5% w.p. 95% | 3.4% | 4.9% | 1.6 MB |

Three observations stand out. First, observed errors sit comfortably *below* the theoretical bounds — the bounds are worst-case over adversarial frequency vectors, while Zipfian data is comparatively benign. Second, HyperLogLog's measured standard error tracks *1.04/√m* to within a few percent, confirming the sharpness of the analysis in [3]; the experimental survey of [8] reports the same agreement across real datasets. ### 5.3 Mergeability stress test

We split each stream across 16 shards, sketched independently, and merged (addition for CM/AMS, register-wise max for HLL). Merged estimates matched single-sketch estimates to within *10^{−6}* relative difference — exact mergeability, as the linearity/max-structure predicts. This is the property that lets HyperLogLog count distinct users across data centers with no coordination [7].

### 5.4 Proof consolidation: why limited independence suffices

A unifying proof-theoretic point: none of the three sketches needs full randomness. Count-Min's analysis uses only pairwise independence (to bound *E[X_j]*) plus independence *across rows* (to multiply the *1/e* failure probabilities). AMS needs pairwise independence for unbiasedness but 4-wise independence for the variance bound — the fourth moment is where the analysis lives. HyperLogLog's analysis [3] is the subtlest, requiring Mellin-transform asymptotics of the harmonic-mean functional, yet its *implementation* needs only a good hash function. The lesson for practitioners: invest in hash quality (e.g., MurmurHash, xxHash), and the theory follows.

## 6 Limitations

**Count-Min.** The guarantee is one-sided: estimates never underestimate, but on streams where *‖a‖₁* is dominated by many small frequencies, the additive *ε‖a‖₁* noise can swamp the point queries of rare items. The strict turnstile requirement is real — if frequencies can go negative mid-stream, collisions can *cancel* and the one-sidedness collapses. Heavy-hitter recovery needs the dyadic extension; the vanilla sketch answers point queries only.

**HyperLogLog.** Insert-only: deletions are fundamentally unsupported, since a register maximum cannot be un-taken. Accuracy depends entirely on hash uniformity — adversarial or pathological inputs (e.g., sequential keys under a weak hash) break the analysis, a fragility shared by all bit-pattern sketches. The relative error constant *1.04* cannot be driven below *~1/√m* without more registers, and information-theoretic bounds [6] show this scaling is essentially optimal.

**AMS.** The *O(1/ε²)* dependence is tight [5] but painful — 1% error needs ~10⁴ counters — and AMS estimates only *F₂*; higher moments face *Ω(n^{1−2/k})* lower bounds for *k > 2*.

**Fundamental walls.** The AMS dichotomy [2] — polynomial space required for *F_k*, *k ≥ 6* — is not a failure of imagination but a theorem. Recent optimality results [5] further show that even for *F₂*, the classical *O(ε^{−2})* sketch is optimal up to constants. Finally, all guarantees in this thesis assume an *oblivious* adversary: against an *adaptive* adversary that observes sketch outputs and crafts future updates accordingly, randomized sketches can be driven to arbitrary error without additional robustification machinery.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Count-Min, HyperLogLog, and the AMS sketch jointly cover the three tractable pillars of stream summarization — point queries, cardinality, and the second moment — each in polylogarithmic space, each with a proof that its error does what it claims. Their shared architecture — a linear randomized projection paired with a statistic-specific nonlinearity — remains the most successful design pattern in streaming algorithms, still yielding optimal constants [5] and information-theoretic limits [6].

The deeper message is one of *principled approximation*. When data cannot be stored, the question is never whether to approximate but how to bound the damage — and these three sketches show that with the right randomization and the right query functional, the damage can be quantified, tiny, and fit in kilobytes.
