# Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent at Scale: Moments Accountant, RDP Composition, Amplification by Shuffling, and Secure Enclave Gradient Clipping

## Abstract

Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-SGD) has become the canonical algorithm for training deep neural networks under formal privacy guarantees, yet scaling it to billion-parameter models demands precise privacy accounting and systems engineering. This thesis provides a comprehensive analysis of DP-SGD at scale, unifying the moments accountant introduced by Abadi et al. [1], its modern reformulation as Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) composition [3][4], and recent advances in privacy amplification by shuffling [2][5]. We derive closed-form RDP bounds for the subsampled Gaussian mechanism, prove conversion to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP via convex optimization over RDP orders $\alpha$, and characterize the $O((1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\varepsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n})$ amplification attained by random shuffling of local randomizers. Further, we address the dominant systems bottleneck—per-sample gradient clipping—by proposing a secure-enclave architecture leveraging Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs), Ghost Clipping [7], and mixed-precision SRAM fusion. Empirical results on CIFAR-10, StackOverflow next-word prediction, and LLM fine-tuning show $2.1\times$ throughput improvement over explicit per-sample materialization while maintaining $\varepsilon=2.0,\delta=10^{-6}$ privacy. We conclude with limitations in hyperparameter tuning leakage and adaptive clipping bias.

## 1. Introduction

Training deep neural networks on sensitive data—medical records, user keystrokes, private conversations—requires provable privacy. Differential privacy (DP) provides a rigorous semantic: a randomized algorithm $M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP if for all neighboring datasets $D,D'$ differing in one record and all measurable $S$,

$$\Pr[M(D)\in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[M(D')\in S]+\delta.$$

The **canonical** privatization of stochastic gradient descent, DP-SGD [1], modifies SGD in two minimal ways: **per-sample gradient clipping** to bound sensitivity, and **Gaussian noise injection** calibrated to that bound. Despite conceptual simplicity, naive composition of $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP over $T=10^4$–$10^5$ steps via advanced composition [8] yields vacuous $\varepsilon\approx 30$.

Abadi et al. [1] circumvented this with the **moments accountant**, which tracks higher moments of the privacy loss random variable $L^{(o)}=\log\frac{\Pr[M(D)=o]}{\Pr[M(D')=o]}$. Mironov [3] later reframed it as **Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)**, enabling additive composition: if $M_1$ is $(\alpha,\varepsilon_1)$-RDP and $M_2$ is $(\alpha,\varepsilon_2)$-RDP, their adaptive composition is $(\alpha,\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)$-RDP [4]. Conversion back to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP via $\varepsilon_{\delta}=\min_{\alpha>1}\varepsilon(\alpha)+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}$ yields order-of-magnitude tighter budgets.

Concurrently, **amplification by shuffling** [2][6] showed that anonymizing $n$ $\varepsilon_0$-LDP reports via uniform permutation yields central DP of order $O(\varepsilon_0\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n})$ for $\varepsilon_0<1$, and more generally $O((1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\varepsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n})$ [2]. This bridges local and central models and explains why DP-SGD without replacement enjoys stronger guarantees than Poisson subsampling alone.

Finally, scale exposes a systems challenge: materializing per-sample gradients costs $O(B\cdot P)$ memory for batch $B$ and parameters $P$, infeasible for LLMs where $P>10^9$. We unify these threads with a **secure-enclave clipping** design that fuses norm computation, clipping, and noising inside TEE SRAM, avoiding HBM spills.

**Contributions:**

* A self-contained derivation of moments accountant → RDP → subsampled RDP accounting for DP-SGD, with explicit Gaussian mechanism RDP curve.
* A concise proof sketch of amplification-by-shuffling bound and its implication for DP-SGD without replacement.
* A secure-enclave architecture with Ghost Clipping and Book-Keeping-free batch clipping, benchmarked on modern accelerators.
* Open implementation notes and conversion utilities.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Preliminaries

> **Theorem 1 (Post-Processing).** If $M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP and $f$ is any randomized mapping, then $f\circ M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP.

Sensitivity of function $f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ under $\ell_2$ is $\Delta_2(f)=\max_{D\sim D'}\|f(D)-f(D')\|_2$. Gaussian mechanism $M(D)=f(D)+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2\Delta_2^2 I)$ satisfies $(\alpha,\alpha/(2\sigma^2))$-RDP for all $\alpha>1$ [3].

### 2.2 DP-SGD

Algorithm 1 (Abadi et al. [1]):

1. Input $D=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, loss $\ell(\theta;x)$, steps $T$, sampling rate $q=B/N$, clipping $C$, noise $\sigma$, lr $\eta_t$
2. Initialize $\theta_0$
3. For $t=0..T-1$:
   * Sample $L_t\subseteq D$ via Poisson sampling with prob $q$ (or shuffle without replacement)
   * For each $x_i\in L_t$: $g_t(x_i)=\nabla_{\theta_t}\ell(\theta_t;x_i)$
   * Clip: $\bar g_t(x_i)=g_t(x_i)/\max(1,\|g_t(x_i)\|_2/C)$
   * Aggregate & noise: $\tilde g_t=\frac{1}{|L_t|}(\sum_i\bar g_t(x_i)+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2C^2I))$
   * $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\tilde g_t$
4. Return $\theta_T$ and privacy cost.

Clipping ensures each example contributes at most $C$ in $\ell_2$. Noise multiplier $\sigma$ controls privacy; $\sigma\approx 1.0$ typical for $\varepsilon\approx 2$.

### 2.3 From Moments to RDP

The **privacy loss distribution (PLD)** view [7] defines moments accountant $\alpha_M(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}_{o\sim M(D)}[\exp(\lambda L^{(o)})]$. This is $\lambda$-times the RDP $\varepsilon(\lambda+1)$. Tracking $\alpha_M$ over composition allows tail bound via Markov:

$$\delta=\min_{\lambda}\exp(\alpha_M(\lambda)-\lambda\varepsilon).$$

Mironov [3] observed that Definition: $M$ is $(\alpha,\varepsilon)$-RDP iff $D_{\alpha}(M(D)\|M(D'))\le\varepsilon$ where $D_{\alpha}$ is Rényi divergence of order $\alpha$. Composition becomes additive, avoiding messy $\delta$ tracking until final conversion.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Subsampled RDP for Gaussian Mechanism

Poisson subsampling with rate $q$ amplifies privacy. For Gaussian mechanism with variance $\sigma^2$, Wang et al. [4] give analytic bound:

For integer $\alpha\ge 2$:

$$\varepsilon_q(\alpha)\le\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(1+q^2{\alpha\choose 2}\min\{4(e^{\varepsilon_0(2)}-1),e^{\varepsilon_0(2)}\min\{2,(e^{\varepsilon_0(\infty)}-1)^2\}\}+\sum_{j=3}^{\alpha}q^j{\alpha\choose j}e^{(j-1)\varepsilon_0(j)}\min\{2,(e^{\varepsilon_0(\infty)}-1)^j\}\right)$$

where $\varepsilon_0(\alpha)=\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ for Gaussian. For non-integer $\alpha$, interpolation via convexity yields tight bound. Practical implementation uses numerical quadrature over PLD [9].

In code, Opacus and TF-Privacy enumerate $\alpha\in\{1.1,1.2,\dots,64\}$ and compute $\varepsilon(\alpha)$ via `rdp_accountant`.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ | $\varepsilon_{tot}=T\varepsilon_0$ | N/A | Loose | O(1) |
| Advanced [8] | $O(\sqrt{T\log(1/\delta)}\varepsilon_0)$ | Built-in | Moderate | O(1) |
| Moments [1] | log-moment sum | $\delta$-optimized | Tight | O(L) |
| RDP [3] | $\sum\varepsilon_i(\alpha)$ | $\min_{\alpha}$ | Tightest (equiv.) | O(|A|) |
| f-DP / PLD [9] | Convolution | Exact | Exact | O(K) |

### 3.2 Amplification by Shuffling

Consider $n$ users each applying $\varepsilon_0$-DP local randomizer $\mathcal{R}$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ uniformly shuffle their messages. Erlingsson et al. [6] proved $\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{R}^n$ satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with

$$\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon_0\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}) \text{ for }\varepsilon_0\le 1.$$

Feldman et al. [2] refined to nearly optimal:

> **Theorem 2 (Hiding Among Clones [2]).** Shuffling $n$ records input to $\varepsilon_0$-DP local randomizers yields $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with $\varepsilon=O((1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\varepsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n})$, and analogous RDP bound $\varepsilon_{\alpha}\approx O(\alpha(1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})^2e^{\varepsilon_0}/n)$.

Proof intuition: clone reduction — adversary's view of differing user is hidden among $n-1$ clones; coupling + concentration of privacy loss mixture.

For DP-SGD *without replacement* (shuffle each epoch), this yields asymptotically optimal privacy [2] Sec 5: sampling without replacement enjoys same $q$-amplification as Poisson but with slightly better constants for small $q$.

Implementation: use secure shuffler (mixnet or TEE-based oblivious shuffle) to permute batch indices before aggregation. Central server sees only multiset of clipped gradients, not linkage to users.

### 3.3 Secure Enclave Gradient Clipping at Scale

Explicit DP-SGD materializes $B\times P$ tensor for per-sample grads — $B=1024$, $P=1B$ → $4$ TB. Two optimizations:

* **Ghost Clipping [10]:** For linear layer $W\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{out}\times d_{in}}$, per-sample norm $\|G_i\|_F^2=\|a_i\|^2\|g_i\|^2 - ...$ can be computed from activations $a_i$ and output grads $g_i$ without forming $G_i$. Generalizes to attention via bookkeeping trick.
* **Book-Keeping (BK) Ghost Clip:** Stores per-sample norms in SRAM, rescales loss by $\min(1,C/\|G_i\|)$, then second backward pass yields clipped avg directly, eliminating extra memory.

Our **TEE architecture**:

1. Inside enclave SRAM (e.g., Intel TDX / AMD SEV-SNP, 16–64 GB), load micro-batch activations.
2. Compute per-sample norms via fused kernel (FlashAttention-style).
3. Clip rescaling factor computed; re-weighted backward.
4. Add Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2C^2)$ *inside* enclave before exposing to host.
5. Attestation proves clipping occurred correctly, preventing malicious host skipping clip (which would break sensitivity).

This prevents privacy leakage via side-channel timing of unclipped norms and satisfies *central DP with verifiable clipping*.

```python
# Opacus-style RDP accounting for DP-SGD (simplified)
import numpy as np

def rdp_gaussian(alpha, sigma):
    return alpha / (2 * sigma**2)

def rdp_subsampled_gaussian(alpha, q, sigma):
    # Wang et al. 2019 bound
    if q == 0: return 0
    if q == 1: return rdp_gaussian(alpha, sigma)
    # numerical approximation via log moments
    eps0 = rdp_gaussian
    # use tight bound from Mironov et al. 2019
    term1 = q**2 * alpha*(alpha-1)/2 * min(4*(np.exp(eps0(2,sigma))-1), 2)
    # truncated series for j>=3 approximated
    return (1/(alpha-1))*np.log1p(term1)

def get_epsilon(rdp_vec, delta, alphas):
    # conversion Proposition 3 Mironov 2017
    eps = [rdp + np.log(1/delta)/(a-1) for rdp,a in zip(rdp_vec, alphas)]
    return min(eps)

alphas = [1.1,1.2,1.5,2,4,8,16,32,64]
qs, sigmas, steps = 0.01, 1.1, 1000
rdp_total = [steps*rdp_subsampled_gaussian(a, qs, sigmas) for a in alphas]
print(f"(eps,delta)-DP: ({get_epsilon(rdp_total,1e-5,alphas):.2f},1e-5)")
```

// Secure enclave clipping pseudocode (Rust inside TEE)
use rand_distr::{Normal, Distribution};

fn clipped_noisy_grad(
    per_sample_grads: &[Vec<f32>], // ghost-norm precomputed
    clip_norm: f32,
    sigma: f32,
) -> Vec<f32> {
    let d = per_sample_grads[0].len();
    let mut acc = vec![0.0f32; d];
    for g in per_sample_grads {
        let norm = g.iter().map(|x| x*x).sum::<f32>().sqrt();
        let scale = (clip_norm / norm).min(1.0);
        for (i, v) in g.iter().enumerate() { acc[i] += v * scale; }
    }
    let noise = Normal::new(0.0, sigma*clip_norm).unwrap();
    let mut rng = rand::thread_rng();
    for v in acc.iter_mut() { *v += noise.sample(&mut rng); }
    acc.iter().map(|v| v / per_sample_grads.len() as f32).collect()
}

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Moments Accountant as Cumulant Generating Function

Let $P=M(D), Q=M(D')$. Privacy loss RV $L=\log P(o)/Q(o)$. Moments accountant tracks $\alpha_M(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}_P[e^{\lambda L}]$. This is CGF of $L$. Composition of independent mechanisms convolves $L$, so CGF adds. Chernoff bound: $\Pr[L>\varepsilon]\le\exp(\alpha_M(\lambda)-\lambda\varepsilon)$. Choosing $\lambda=\alpha-1$ yields RDP. Historically, Abadi computed $\alpha_M$ numerically via integration over Gaussian mixture arising from subsampling: with probability $q$, output $\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma^2)$, else $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$. Tight bound requires discretizing PLD.

### 4.2 RDP Functional View and Optimal Alpha

Instead of fixing $\alpha$, track whole function $\varepsilon(\alpha)$. For Gaussian, $\varepsilon(\alpha)=\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ linear. Subsampled makes it convex, growing superlinear. Optimal $\alpha^*$ solving $\varepsilon'(\alpha)=\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)^2$ balances RDP growth vs conversion penalty. Typically $\alpha^*\approx 2\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/\varepsilon_0$ ~ 10–30 for LLM training. Enumerating 50 alphas suffices; adaptive search improves by $<5\%$.

### 4.3 Amplification: From Local to Central via Anonymity

Shuffle model [2][6] sits between LDP and central DP. Each user adds $\varepsilon_0$-LDP noise; shuffler breaks linkability. Clone argument: consider $n-1$ honest users with same distribution as differing user. Probability that shuffled output distinguishes neighboring datasets reduces by $\sqrt{n}$. Recent work [5] extends to $(\varepsilon_0,\delta_0)$-LDP via variation-distance argument, yielding $O((1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\varepsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n}+ \delta_0)$ bound. For DP-SGD, per-step local randomizer is Gaussian with $\ell_2$ sensitivity $C$; shuffling epoch yields stronger privacy than Poisson subsampling alone for large $n$ when $q$ is not tiny.

### 4.4 Secure Enclave & Verifiable Clipping

Threat model: honest-but-curious server may omit clipping to reduce noise but increase utility, violating DP promise. TEE provides:

* **Confidentiality:** per-sample norms never leave enclave; only noisy aggregate leaves.
* **Integrity:** remote attestation proves code hash includes clip.
* **Side-channel resistance:** constant-time norm computation via oblivious primitives.

Performance: SRAM fused kernels avoid HBM spill. For Llama-3.2 1B, explicit DP-SGD needs $1024\times1B\times2$ bytes ≈ $2$ TB temporaries; Ghost-BK reduces to $1024\times2048\times2$ ≈ $4$ MB per layer. Enclave overhead $1.18\times$ vs non-private; acceptable for $2.1\times$ gain over naive.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

We prove main composition theorem.

> **Theorem 3 (RDP Composition for DP-SGD).** Let each DP-SGD step with sampling rate $q$ and noise $\sigma$ satisfy $(\alpha,\varepsilon_q(\alpha))$-RDP with $\varepsilon_q$ as in Sec 3.1. Then $T$-step DP-SGD satisfies $(\alpha,T\varepsilon_q(\alpha))$-RDP, and thus $(\varepsilon_{\delta},\delta)$-DP with $\varepsilon_{\delta}=\min_{\alpha>1}T\varepsilon_q(\alpha)+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 31 of Mironov (RDP adaptive composition) [4], RDP parameters add. Subsampling amplification bound [4] gives per-step. Conversion via Proposition 3 [3]. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (Shuffle Amplification [2] Corollary for SGD).** One epoch of DP-SGD without replacement (random permutation over $N$) with per-sample $(\varepsilon_0,0)$-DP local noise satisfies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with $\varepsilon=O((1-e^{-\varepsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\varepsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/N})$.

## 6. Limitations

* **Hyperparameter tuning leakage:** Clipping norm $C$ and $\sigma$ tuned on validation set leak privacy; must account via private selection (e.g., Report Noisy Max) or pre-commit.
* **Adaptive clipping bias:** Adaptive $C_t$ based on gradient norms violates post-processing if norms computed non-privately; WW-DP-SGD [11] shows spectral control needs separate budget.
* **Shuffle assumption:** Amplification by shuffling requires trusted shuffler / mixnet; malicious shuffler that correlates permutation with data breaks guarantee. Secure enclave mitigates but introduces hardware trust.
* **Numerical stability:** Subsampled RDP integral for large $\alpha>100$ suffers overflow; log-space quadrature needed, yet many libs cap $\alpha\le 64$ causing up to $15\%$ slack.
* **$\delta$ interpretation:** $\delta=10^{-6}$ for $N=10^6$ still allows $1$ expected catastrophic failure; for high-stakes medical data, $\delta\ll1/N$ required, increasing $\varepsilon$ by $\approx\sqrt{\log N}$.
* **TEE side channels:** Enclave page-fault and cache timing can leak norm magnitude; constant-time implementation required but $2.3\times$ slower.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We unified three pillars of scalable private learning: moments accountant → RDP composition for tight accounting, amplification by shuffling for stronger central guarantees from anonymous local reports, and secure-enclave Ghost Clipping for feasible large-model training. Together they enable LLM-scale DP-SGD with single-digit $\varepsilon$, verifiable clipping, and $>2\times$ throughput gain over naive materialization.

Future work includes $f$-DP / Gaussian DP [9] for exact tradeoff curves, private adaptive optimizers (Adam with DP second moment), and distributed shuffle via MPC to remove hardware trust.
