# Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search at Billion Scale: HNSW Graphs, IVF-PQ Inverted Indexes, and DiskANN

## Abstract

Given a database X of N points in R^d and a query q, approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search returns points x in X minimizing d(q, x) with bounded error while avoiding the linear scan. At N = 10^9, memory footprints of full-precision vectors exceed single-node RAM budgets, forcing compression, partitioning, and secondary-storage-aware index design. This thesis surveys and formalizes the three dominant architectures: inverted file systems with product quantization (IVF-PQ), which compress each vector to tens of bytes and prune the search space through coarse quantization; hierarchical navigable small world (HNSW) graphs, which route queries through a probabilistic multi-layer proximity graph with logarithmic hop complexity; and DiskANN, which replaces the hierarchy with a single Vamana graph whose robust-prune construction minimizes SSD page faults. We develop the distortion theory of product quantizers, the recall-complexity trade-offs of efSearch/efConstruction, and the angular-diversity pruning rule underlying Vamana. Empirical results on SIFT1B and DEEP1B demonstrate that graph indices dominate the recall-QPS Pareto frontier in RAM-resident regimes while DiskANN achieves 95%+ recall@1 at 5,000 QPS on a single 64 GB machine [3]. We conclude with fundamental limits: space-partitioning lower bounds, the quantization-graph duality, and open problems in dynamic updates.

## 1. Introduction

Nearest neighbor search is the computational backbone of retrieval-augmented generation, image and video search, recommendation, and dense passage retrieval. The exact problem is trivial to state and brutally expensive to solve: for a query vector q in R^d, compute argmin over a database of N points. The brute-force scan costs O(Nd) per query, which for N = 10^9 and d = 128 translates to roughly 128 billion floating point operations --- far beyond any latency budget.

The *curse of dimensionality* defeats classical space-partitioning structures: k-d trees, R-trees, and vantage-point trees all degrade to linear scan once d exceeds a few dozen dimensions, because the volume of an epsilon-neighborhood relative to the unit cube vanishes exponentially in d. *Approximate* nearest neighbor search concedes exactness in exchange for sub-linear cost. The standard quality measure is **recall@k**: the fraction of the true k nearest neighbors returned by the index. The universal visualization of the field is the **recall versus queries-per-second (QPS) Pareto frontier** [5]: an algorithm dominates another if it achieves higher recall at the same QPS, or higher QPS at the same recall.

Three architectures define the modern state of the art at billion scale. *Inverted file with product quantization* (IVF-PQ), pioneered by Jégou, Douze, and Schmid [1] and industrialized in FAISS [4], partitions space with a coarse quantizer and compresses residual vectors to compact codes of 8--64 bytes. *Hierarchical navigable small world* (HNSW) graphs, due to Malkov and Yashunin [2], build a multi-layer proximity graph whose greedy routing achieves logarithmic search complexity in the in-memory regime. *DiskANN*, with its Vamana graph construction [3], discards the multi-layer hierarchy in favor of a single SSD-resident graph engineered to minimize random I/O. Together they span the three fundamental resources --- memory, computation, and I/O --- whose balance determines which index wins for a given deployment.

This thesis provides a unified formal treatment: the distortion theory of product quantization in §4.1, the pruning geometry of inverted indexes in §4.2, the skip-list-like probabilistic analysis of HNSW in §4.3, and the angular-diversity construction of Vamana in §4.4.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The ANN problem and its relaxations

Let (M, d) be a metric space and X = {x_1, ..., x_N} a finite subset. The *exact* k-nearest-neighbor query returns the k points minimizing d(q, x_i). Two standard relaxations exist. The **c-approximate** variant accepts any point within factor c of the true nearest distance. The **recall-oriented** variant, dominant in practice, returns exactly k candidates and is scored by recall@k against ground truth computed offline. All three architectures in this thesis target the recall-oriented formulation under Euclidean distance or maximum inner product.

> **Theorem:** *(Cell-probe lower bound sketch.)* Any deterministic data structure answering exact nearest neighbor queries in R^d with polynomial preprocessing and polylogarithmic query time requires super-polynomial space for d = ω(log N). Informally, approximation or exponential space is unavoidable in high dimensions.

### 2.2 Vector quantization primitives

A *quantizer* q maps R^d to a finite reproduction alphabet C = {c_1, ..., c_K}. The Lloyd optimality conditions require that each reproduction value be the conditional expectation of its cell, and each cell be the Voronoi region of its reproduction value. K-means approximates these conditions iteratively. A **product quantizer** factorizes the space into m orthogonal subspaces and learns an independent sub-quantizer per subspace, yielding K = (k*)^m effective centroids while storing only m * k* * (d/m) floats [1]. This exponential leverage is the engine of all compression-based ANN.

### 2.3 Proximity graphs and navigability

A *proximity graph* G = (V, E) places one vertex per database point and connects geometrically nearby points. Query answering becomes *graph routing*: start at an entry point and repeatedly move to the neighbor closest to q. Kleinberg's small-world analysis shows that with the right mixture of short (local) and long (shortcut) edges, greedy routing succeeds in O(log^2 N) hops. The NSW and later HNSW line of work [2] converts this insight into a practical, incrementally built index.

## 3. Methodology

We compare the three architectures along four axes: **index construction cost**, **memory footprint**, **query-time operation count**, and **I/O complexity**. Notation is summarized below.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| N | database cardinality (10^6 -- 10^9) |
| d | vector dimensionality |
| m | number of PQ sub-quantizers |
| k* | centroids per sub-quantizer (typically 256) |
| nlist | number of IVF coarse centroids |
| nprobe | number of IVF cells scanned per query |
| M | HNSW maximum out-degree per layer |
| efC | HNSW efConstruction (candidate list size at build) |
| efS | HNSW efSearch (candidate list size at query) |
| R | Vamana maximum out-degree |
| L | DiskANN beam width at search |

The *methodological core* of this thesis is to show that all three systems are instances of a single pattern --- **candidate generation followed by refinement** --- differing only in how candidates are generated (coarse cells, graph hops, or SSD-aware beam search) and how distances are estimated (compressed codes, exact vectors, or a hybrid).

```python
# Unified ANN query skeleton shared by IVF-PQ, HNSW, and DiskANN
def ann_query(q, index, k):
    candidates = index.generate_candidates(q)   # IVF cells / graph hops / beam search
    scores = index.score(q, candidates)         # ADC lookups / exact dist / PQ + rerank
    return top_k(candidates, scores, k)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Product Quantization and Asymmetric Distance Computation

Product quantization decomposes x in R^d into m subvectors x = (x^1, ..., x^m), each in R^{d/m}, and quantizes each subvector with its own codebook C_j of k* centroids learned by k-means on the corresponding subspace [1]. The encoding of x is the tuple of indices (i_1, ..., i_m), stored in m * log2(k*) bits --- for the canonical PQ8x8 configuration, 8 sub-quantizers with 256 centroids each yield a **64-bit code**: the entire database point in 8 bytes. The reproduction x̂ is the concatenation of the selected centroids.

The key algorithmic contribution of [1] is **asymmetric distance computation (ADC)**. Instead of quantizing the query (which would compound distortion), the query remains in full precision. At query time, for each subspace j, a lookup table T_j[t] = ||q^j - c_{j,t}||^2 is precomputed for all k* centroids --- O(m * k* * d/m) = O(d * k*) work. The approximate distance to any database point is then a sum of m table lookups:

```
d̂(q, x)^2 = Σ_j T_j[i_j]          # m additions, no multiplications
```

> **Theorem:** *(ADC unbiasedness under isotropy.)* If the quantization residuals r_j = x^j - c_{j,i_j} are isotropic and independent of q, then E[d̂(q,x)^2] = d(q,x)^2 + Σ_j E[||r_j||^2]. The bias is query-independent, so *ranking* is asymptotically unbiased even though absolute distances are inflated.

Two refinements dominate practice. **Residual quantization** (IVFADC+R) encodes the residual x - q_c(x) relative to the coarse centroid rather than x itself, dramatically reducing distortion because residuals live in a smaller, better-behaved ball [4]. **Optimized product quantization (OPQ)** learns an orthonormal rotation R minimizing Σ ||Rx - q(Rx)||^2, decorrelating subspaces so that the Cartesian product assumption holds more tightly. Anisotropic extensions reweight the loss to penalize error parallel to the datapoint more than orthogonal error, which is provably superior for maximum inner product search [6].

### 4.2 IVF: The Coarse Quantizer and Inverted File

The inverted file adds a *partitioning* stage before compression. A **coarse quantizer** with nlist centroids (typically nlist ~ 4*sqrt(N), e.g., 65,536 for N = 10^9) assigns each database vector to its nearest centroid; each centroid owns an **inverted list** of the (id, PQ code) pairs of its members. Query answering probes the nprobe nearest coarse centroids and scans only those lists, examining roughly N * nprobe / nlist candidates [4].

The design space is governed by a clean trade-off. Larger nlist yields finer cells and fewer distance computations per query, but increases the coarse assignment cost O(d * nlist) and risks empty or tiny cells. Larger nprobe raises recall (the true neighbor's cell may not be the query's nearest cell) at linear cost in scanned codes. FAISS [4] implements the full pipeline --- k-means training on a sample, inverted list construction, ADC scanning with SIMD, and optional GPU kernels achieving 8.5x speedups over prior GPU state of the art [4].

| Configuration | Bytes/vector | nprobe | Recall@1 (SIFT1M) | QPS (1 thread) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IVFFlat, nlist=1024 | 512 | 8 | ~0.99 | ~800 |
| IVFPQ8x8, nlist=4096 | 8 + id | 16 | ~0.65 | ~25,000 |
| IVFPQ16x8, nlist=16384 | 16 + id | 32 | ~0.85 | ~9,000 |
| IVFPQ64x8 + residuals | 64 + id | 64 | ~0.97 | ~2,500 |

*Illustrative figures consistent with FAISS/ANN-Benchmarks reports [4][5]; exact values depend on hardware and build parameters.*

### 4.3 HNSW: Layered Navigable Small Worlds

HNSW [2] constructs, for nested random subsets of the data, a hierarchy of proximity graphs resembling a **probabilistic skip list**. Each element draws its maximum layer l from an exponentially decaying distribution P(l) = (1 - m_L) * m_L^l (with normalization parameter m_L = 1/ln(M)), so the top layer contains O(1) nodes and layer sizes grow geometrically downward, with layer 0 containing all N points. Insertion proceeds top-down: a greedy search (Algorithm SEARCH-LAYER) finds the local minimum at each layer, which becomes the entry point for the layer below; neighbors at each layer are selected from an efC-sized candidate pool.

The critical innovation beyond flat NSW is the **neighbor selection heuristic** (SELECT-NEIGHBORS-HEURISTIC). Rather than connecting to the M nearest candidates, the heuristic enforces *directional diversity*: a candidate e is kept only if it is closer to the new node than to any already-selected neighbor. This prunes redundant edges pointing in the same direction --- the same geometric intuition that later resurfaces in Vamana's RobustPrune [3] --- and is responsible for HNSW's robustness on highly clustered data [2].

Search mirrors insertion: greedy descent through layers 1..L_c, then a best-first beam search with candidate list size efS at layer 0. The parameter efS is the *recall knob*: larger efS explores more of the graph, monotonically increasing recall at linear cost.

```python
def hnsw_search(q, entry, k, efS):
    # Phase 1: greedy descent through upper layers
    cur = entry
    for layer in reversed(range(1, Lmax + 1)):
        cur = greedy_search_layer(q, cur, layer)   # move to local minimum
    # Phase 2: beam search at layer 0 with dynamic candidate list
    return beam_search_layer(q, cur, layer=0, ef=efS, k=k)
```

> **Theorem:** *(Logarithmic scaling, Malkov–Yashunin [2].)* Under the skip-list analogy, the expected number of hops per layer is O(1) and the expected number of layers is O(log N), giving expected query complexity O(log N) distance computations times the efS beam factor. Empirically, HNSW dominates the in-memory recall–QPS Pareto frontier on benchmarks such as ANN-Benchmarks [5].

The price is memory: with M = 16--32 bidirectional edges per layer plus full-precision vectors, HNSW consumes roughly (M * layers + d) floats per point --- hundreds of bytes to kilobytes per vector, which at N = 10^9 exceeds any single machine's RAM. This memory wall motivates DiskANN.

### 4.4 DiskANN: The Vamana Graph on SSD

DiskANN [3] starts from the observation that at billion scale, the graph and the full-precision vectors cannot live in RAM, but they *can* live on NVMe SSD --- provided the number of random 4KB page reads per query stays small (tens, not thousands). The entire design minimizes **I/O complexity** rather than distance computations.

**Vamana construction.** The graph is built by the iterative **RobustPrune** procedure: for each node p, run a greedy search to collect a candidate pool, then prune it with an angular-diversity rule parameterized by α ≥ 1: a candidate v is discarded if there exists an already-kept neighbor u with α * d(u, v) < d(p, v). Setting α > 1 (typically 1.2) keeps *long-range* edges that pure nearest-neighbor pruning would delete, shrinking the graph diameter and hence the number of SSD hops. Unlike HNSW, Vamana is a **single flat graph** of max degree R (typically 64--128); the hierarchy is unnecessary because the scarce resource is I/O, not distance computations, and one well-pruned layer minimizes hops per byte read.

**SSD-aware serving.** DiskANN stores full-precision vectors and adjacency lists on SSD in a layout where each node's vector and its neighbor list share flash pages. A **compressed copy of all vectors** (product-quantized, ~tens of bytes each) resides in RAM [3]. Query answering is a **beam search of width L** guided entirely by in-memory PQ distances; SSD reads are issued only for the final candidate set, whose full-precision distances determine the re-ranked top-k. This two-phase design means a query touching 10^9 points issues only O(tens) of SSD reads.

The headline result of [3]: on SIFT1B (10^9 × 128-dim), a single 16-core machine with 64 GB RAM and one SSD serves **5,000+ QPS at <3 ms mean latency with 95%+ recall@1**. The follow-up FreshDiskANN extends the design to streaming inserts and deletes via the FreshVamana update rules and a write-efficient two-pass StreamingMerge [7].

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Analysis

### 5.1 The Pareto frontier

ANN-Benchmarks [5] established the methodology the field still uses: Docker-isolated implementations, fixed datasets (SIFT1M, GIST1M, GloVe, DEEP1B), and recall@k versus QPS Pareto plots with build time and index size as secondary axes. Its central empirical finding --- that *"very different approaches to k-NN search yield comparable quality-performance trade-offs"* [5] --- has been refined but not overturned: graph methods (HNSW, NSG, Vamana) now strictly dominate tree, hashing, and pure-quantization methods in the high-recall regime, while IVF-PQ remains competitive at extreme QPS with modest recall.

### 5.2 Billion-scale numbers

| System | Dataset | Recall@1 | QPS | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FAISS IVFPQ (GPU) [4] | SIFT1B | ~0.75 | ~10^5 (batch) | 4× Maxwell Titan X |
| HNSW [2] | SIFT1M | 0.99 | ~3,000 | 1 CPU thread |
| DiskANN [3] | SIFT1B | 0.95+ | 5,000+ | 16-core, 64 GB RAM, 1 SSD |
| ScaNN (anisotropic) [6] | GloVe-100 | SOTA MIPS | SOTA | CPU |

### 5.3 Proof sketch: why angular diversity works

Consider greedy routing on a proximity graph. A *local minimum* with respect to q traps the search. Long-range edges are the escape mechanism; a graph whose edges all point to the nearest cluster is a collection of disconnected basins. The Vamana/HNSW pruning rule keeps an edge (p, v) only if v is not "shadowed" by another neighbor u, i.e., it preserves edges spanning *distinct directions*. Formally, the pruned graph approximates a **relative neighborhood graph**, which is known to contain the Euclidean minimum spanning tree and hence to be connected with small stretch. This is why α > 1 RobustPrune simultaneously bounds degree and diameter [3].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Quantization distortion floor.** PQ with m sub-quantizers has irreducible distortion Σ_j E[||r_j||^2]; below ~8 bytes/vector, recall collapses regardless of index structure. Anisotropic losses [6] shift but do not remove this floor.
2. **Graph build cost.** HNSW construction is O(N log N * efC) distance computations; Vamana's iterative pruning passes are similarly expensive. Billion-scale builds take hours even on multi-GPU setups [4].
3. **Dynamic updates.** All three structures assume a static database. Deletions in HNSW leave dangling edges; IVF coarse centroids drift. FreshDiskANN [7] is the first principled streaming solution, at the cost of merge complexity.
4. **Distribution shift.** Codebooks, coarse centroids, and graph neighborhoods are all fit to training data. Under query-distribution shift (e.g., out-of-distribution embeddings), recall degrades silently --- the index has no uncertainty estimate.
5. **Theoretical gaps.** No tight lower bound is known for the recall–QPS trade-off of graph routing; the logarithmic analysis of HNSW [2] is heuristic outside the skip-list analogy, and Vamana's diameter bounds are empirical.

---

## 7. Conclusion

IVF-PQ, HNSW, and DiskANN are three answers to one question: *where should the index spend its budget?* IVF-PQ spends it on **compression**, shrinking each vector until a billion of them fit in RAM. HNSW spends it on **graph connectivity**, buying logarithmic routing at the cost of hundreds of bytes per point. DiskANN spends it on **I/O discipline**, accepting SSD latency in exchange for single-machine billion-scale serving. The unifying lesson is the candidate-generation/refinement decomposition of §3: coarse cells, graph hops, and beam searches are interchangeable candidate generators, and ADC lookups, exact distances, and PQ-guided re-ranking are interchangeable scorers. Future work lies at the intersections --- learned coarse quantizers, GPU-built Vamana graphs, and streaming-robust pruning --- and in closing the theoretical gap between the empirical Pareto frontier and provable lower bounds.
