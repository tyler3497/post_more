# Neuromorphic Continual Learning with Loihi 2: E-prop Surrogate Gradients, STDP Meta-Plasticity, Sparse Spiking Replay, and Catastrophic Forgetting Bounds

## Abstract
We present a hardware-aware continual-learning stack for Intel Loihi 2 that combines e-prop style local credit, surrogate gradients, three-factor STDP, metaplastic consolidation, and sparse spiking replay under explicit spike and energy budgets. Loihi 2 provides 128 neuromorphic cores, approximately 1,048,576 neurons, 120 million synapses, up to 8,192 neurons per core, six x86 management cores, programmable microcode neurons, graded integer spike payloads, and three-factor learning in 31 mm² on Intel 4 [1][2]. Lava offers an open, modular, heterogeneous execution model for conventional and neuromorphic targets [3]. We show how eligibility traces factor temporal credit into local and broadcast terms, why surrogate derivatives are not conservative gradients, and how metaplasticity analogous but not identical to EWC [7] reduces plasticity. We propose an original analytical forgetting bound with three additive error sources and validate its assumptions for fixed-point, quantized Loihi deployment. The bound is explicitly proposed, not an established Loihi theorem.

## 1 Introduction
**Neuromorphic continual learning** must reconcile local learning, sparse communication, and stability-plasticity without dense backpropagation through time (BPTT). *Intel Loihi 2* departs from von Neumann separation by colocating memory and compute, using asynchronous, event-driven spikes and barrier-synchronized algorithmic timesteps [1][2]. Each of its 128 cores stores up to 8,192 neurons with 192 KB SRAM, programmable partitioning, and microcode-defined dynamics, linked by a 3D mesh network-on-chip that carries binary or 32-bit graded spike payloads [1][2][5].

Three practical questions motivate this thesis:

- **Can online credit be local?** E-prop factorizes BPTT gradients into synapse-local eligibility traces $e_{ji}^t$ and top-down learning signals $L_j^t$, such that $\Delta w_{ji} \propto -\sum_t L_j^t e_{ji}^t$ [4]. This avoids storing the full unroll and maps naturally to Loihi 2's learning engine [5].
- **Are surrogate gradients sound?** Surrogate derivatives replace $\partial \Theta/\partial v$ with a pseudo-derivative $\psi(v)$ near threshold to enable gradient flow through hard spikes, but the resulting vector field is generally not the gradient of any surrogate loss [6].
- **How to forget gracefully?** Biological solutions include metaplasticity that stabilizes memories, neurogenesis that expands capacity, and replay that consolidates [5]. CLP-SNN translates these to spatiotemporally sparse local learning, self-normalizing three-factor rules, neurogenesis, and metaplasticity, reporting in its revised OpenLORIS evaluation **0.33 ms, 0.05 mJ, 113× lower latency and 6,600× lower energy** against its specified edge-GPU baseline [5]. We emphasize this is a benchmark-specific result, not a universal Loihi 2 advantage.

**Contributions:**

1. A complete mapping from e-prop/surrogate/three-factor STDP to Loihi 2 microcode and Lava processes [3][4][5].
2. A metaplastic consolidation rule that scales learning rate by inverse Fisher importance and age, analogous but not identical to Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) [7].
3. A bounded-reservoir sparse replay protocol under explicit spike budgets.
4. A **proposed** local forgetting bound with Fisher drift, replay estimation error, and e-prop/surrogate approximation error, stated under explicit assumptions.

## 2 Background
### Loihi 2 Architecture
Loihi 2 is the second-generation neuromorphic research chip fabricated on pre-production Intel 4, 31 mm², 2.3 billion transistors, 1,048,576 neurons, 120 million synapses, 128 neuromorphic cores plus six Lakemont x86 cores for management, 3D mesh scaling, gigabit Ethernet, and 32-bit integer graded spikes [1][2]. Each core supports up to 8,192 neurons at 0.21 mm², 15× density over Loihi 1 [2]. The chip supports programmable neurons with up to 4,096 states, rich instruction sets, and microcode learning engine for on-chip SNN training [2]. Barrier synchronization ensures deterministic timestep semantics while allowing asynchronous spike delivery [5].

### Lava Framework
Lava is Intel's open-source, BSD-licensed, modular and extensible framework for neuromorphic computing, supporting heterogeneous execution across CPUs, GPUs, and Loihi 2 [3]. Processes communicate via channels; compilation lowers Lava to Loihi 2 neurocores when possible, otherwise to conventional hosts. This enables simulation in floating-point and INT8, followed by deployment to Oheo Gulch single-chip PCIe and Kapoho Point 8-chip systems [2][5].

### E-prop and Eligibility Traces
E-prop replaces BPTT's non-local temporal dependencies with forward eligibility traces [4]. For recurrent SNNs:

- Neuron dynamics produce hidden state $h_j^t$ and spikes $z_j^t = \Theta(v_j^t - \vartheta)$.
- Eligibility $e_{ji}^t = \partial h_j^t / \partial w_{ji}$ evolves locally via low-pass filtering of pre/post activity.
- Learning signal $L_j^t$ broadcasts error information, often derived from output loss or modulatory third factor.

Update: $\Delta w_{ji} \propto -\sum_t L_j^t e_{ji}^t$ [4]. This factorization is critical for Loihi 2 because $e_{ji}^t$ can be maintained in synaptic state and $L_j^t$ can be carried as a graded payload.

### Surrogate Gradients
The hard threshold has zero derivative almost everywhere. Surrogate methods replace $\partial z_j^t / \partial v_j^t$ with $\psi(v_j^t)$ e.g., triangular, exponential, or fast sigmoid derivative, enabling gradient flow. Elucidation work shows surrogate fields are generally **not conservative** and thus not gradients of any scalar surrogate loss [6]. Consequences for Loihi 2 include bias and lack of descent guarantees without careful scheduling.

### STDP, Three-Factor Learning, and Metaplasticity
Classical STDP adjusts weights based on pre/post timing [8]. Three-factor rules multiply local timing with a modulatory signal $M^t$ (reward, error, novelty):

$$\Delta w_{ji} \propto M^t \cdot f_{\text{pre}}(t_{\text{pre}}) \cdot f_{\text{post}}(t_{\text{post}})$$

This matches Loihi 2's programmable three-factor learning [1][5]. Metaplasticity modulates plasticity over time from very plastic to consolidated, reducing learning rate for important or old prototypes [5]. EWC formalizes consolidation as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{new}} + \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_i F_i(\theta_i - \theta_i^*)^2$$ [7]

where $F_i$ is diagonal Fisher information estimating parameter importance. Our Loihi variant uses an online Fisher accumulator in fixed-point, explicitly an approximation.

### Sparse Replay and Budgets
Replay mitigates forgetting by revisiting old examples or prototypes. In spiking, sparse replay stores compressed spike events or prototypes under a bounded reservoir, replayed with low duty cycle to respect spike/energy budgets. Temporal learning sparsity dominates efficiency on Loihi 2 [5].

## 3 Methodology
We co-design algorithm and hardware mapping to satisfy Loihi 2 constraints: local state, integer arithmetic, bounded memory, event-driven updates.

**Neuron Model:** Leaky integrate-and-fire with adaptive threshold and 2-3 state variables to support eligibility traces. Implemented as Loihi 2 microcode program with graded spike output encoding membrane surplus.

**E-prop-like Credit:** Each synapse maintains $e_{ji}^t$ as filtered product of pre-synaptic spike train and pseudo-derivative $\psi(v_j^t)$. Postsynaptic learning signal $L_j^t$ arrives as third-factor graded spike, possibly computed by a separate loss-core or x86 Lakemont helper.

**Surrogate Choice:** Fast sigmoid $\psi(v)= (1+|v-\vartheta|)^{-2}$ scaled to Loihi integer range, with explicit note that this does not define a conservative field [6].

**Three-Factor Update:** Triggered only when a spike occurs and $M^t \neq 0$, ensuring spatiotemporal sparsity. Weight update path uses Loihi 2's learning engine integer arithmetic with stochastic rounding.

**Metaplastic Consolidation:** Each prototype or synapse stores age $a_i$ and Fisher accumulator $\hat{F}_i$. Plasticity $\eta_i^t = \eta_0 / (1 + \alpha \hat{F}_i + \beta a_i)$, analogous but not identical to EWC [7]. $\hat{F}_i$ decays slowly to allow re-plasticity if task drift persists.

**Sparse Replay:** Reservoir of $K$ prototypes per class, each compressed as mean spike count vector and covariance diagonal. Replay sampling probability $p_{\text{replay}} \ll 1$, interleaved with online stream, weight updates limited to $B_{\text{spike}}$ spikes per timestep.

**Budget Enforcement:** Per-timestep checks on spike count, SynOps, and graded payload bandwidth; learning skipped if budget exceeded, introducing a controlled approximation error term.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 E-prop on Loihi 2 Microcode
Implementing $e_{ji}^t$ locally requires:

- Pre-trace: $ \bar{z}_i^t = \kappa \bar{z}_i^{t-1} + z_i^t$
- Pseudo-derivative: $\psi_j^t = \text{surrogate}(v_j^t)$
- Eligibility: $e_{ji}^t = \psi_j^t \cdot \bar{z}_i^t$ with low-pass

Loihi 2's programmable neuron allows custom state updates in microcode assembly, while synaptic learning engine computes $\Delta w_{ji}$ only on third-factor arrival. This avoids dense BPTT storage.

```python
# E-prop-like update in Lava-style pseudocode (float sim, maps to Loihi int)
def loihi_eprop_step(v, z_pre, e_trace, L_signal, w, eta=1e-3, kappa=0.9):
    # surrogate derivative - not a true gradient [6]
    psi = 1.0 / (1.0 + abs(v - 1.0))**2
    e_trace = kappa * e_trace + psi * z_pre
    # three-factor: L_signal carries error/modulation as graded spike
    dw = -eta * L_signal * e_trace
    # metaplastic scaling by Fisher importance F_hat and age
    F_hat = 0.9  # placeholder accumulated Fisher
    age = 5.0
    eta_eff = eta / (1 + 0.5*F_hat + 0.1*age)  # analogous to EWC [7] but not identical
    return w + eta_eff * dw / eta, e_trace
```

Key properties: local, event-driven, integer-friendly, respects graded payload limits.

### 4.2 Surrogate Gradients and Non-Conservative Fields
Surrogates enable backprop through spikes but break gradient semantics. Theorem 2 in [6] shows for LIF networks with hard threshold, there exists no scalar loss $\tilde{\mathcal{L}}$ such that $\nabla \tilde{\mathcal{L}}$ equals the surrogate field in general. Implications:

- Learning may not monotonically decrease any loss.
- Oscillations possible near threshold.
- Requires small learning rates and eligibility trace smoothing.

We mitigate by using eligibility traces to smear credit and by bounding $\psi$ magnitude in fixed-point to avoid overflow.

### 4.3 Three-Factor STDP and Self-Normalizing Rules
CLP-SNN's self-normalizing three-factor rule maintains weight normalization without explicit global operations [5]. Our implementation:

- Pre factor: $\text{STDP}_{\text{pre}} = A_+ \exp(-\Delta t/\tau_+)$ if $\Delta t>0$ else $-A_- \exp(\Delta t/\tau_-)$ [8]
- Post factor: filtered post spike
- Third factor: error-driven $M^t = \text{sign}(y - \hat{y})$ or novelty score, delivered as graded spike

Update only when all three present, yielding spatiotemporal sparsity: updates are both sparse in space (only active synapses) and time (only when $M^t\neq0$). This dominates efficiency on Loihi 2 [5].

|---------|---------|---------|------------------------------|
| Neurons per chip | 131,072 | 1,048,576 | 8× capacity for prototype neurogenesis |
| Synapses per chip | 130M | 120M | Slight reduction, compensated by graded payloads |
| Neurons per core | 1,024 | 8,192 | Better core utilization, 0.21 mm² vs 0.41 mm² |
| Spike payload | binary | up to 32-bit graded | Carries $L_j^t$ and $M^t$ without extra channels |
| Learning rule | 2-factor | 3-factor programmable | Enables e-prop and metaplasticity natively |
| Process | Intel 14 nm | Intel 4 | 2× neuron update, up to 10× spike gen speedup |

### 4.4 Metaplasticity and Consolidation
Metaplasticity modulates plasticity from very plastic to consolidated to address catastrophic forgetting [5]. Our Loihi-compatible variant:

- Maintains $\hat{F}_i$ online via squared third-factor-modulated gradients (diagonal Fisher approximation).
- Increments age $a_i$ per timestep where prototype wins.
- Reduces $\eta_i$ as function of $\hat{F}_i$ and $a_i$.

Unlike full EWC which uses Fisher at task boundary [7], we use running estimate to remain online. **Limitations:** diagonal approximation ignores correlations; local quadratic assumption may fail under task shift; quantization adds error.

```rust
// Rust sketch of metaplastic scaling on Loihi Lakemont helper (INT8 sim)
fn effective_lr(eta0: f32, fisher: f32, age: u32, alpha: f32, beta: f32) -> f32 {
    let denom = 1.0 + alpha * fisher + beta * (age as f32);
    eta0 / denom
}

fn consolidate(fisher_accum: &mut [f32], grad: f32, decay: f32) {
    // diagonal Fisher proxy
    for f in fisher_accum.iter_mut() {
        *f = decay * *f + (1.0 - decay) * grad * grad;
    }
}
```

### 4.5 Sparse Spiking Replay
Replay stores prototypes as compressed spike-rate means, not raw event streams, to respect Loihi 2 SRAM limits. Reservoir sampling ensures bounded $K$ per class. Replay is *rehearsal-free* in CLP-SNN's sense when using prototypes only [5], but our optional sparse replay variant adds bounded rehearsal for improved retention.

Replay gradient estimation error arises from sampling finite prototypes vs true old distribution. We bound this error by Hoeffding under assumption of bounded loss, introducing term $\epsilon_{\text{replay}}$ in our forgetting analysis.

---

> **Proposed local forgetting bound.** If old-task loss is locally smooth around its consolidated parameters, Fisher-weighted update energy is bounded, replay estimates are sufficiently accurate, and local e-prop/surrogate errors are bounded, then accumulated old-task loss increase is upper-bounded by terms for Fisher-weighted parameter drift, replay sampling error, and local-gradient approximation error. This is an original analytical proposal under explicit assumptions, not an established Loihi 2 theorem.

Formally, under assumptions A1-A6 below, with $\theta^*$ old-task consolidated parameters, $\theta = \theta^* + \Delta\theta$, $F$ diagonal Fisher, $L_{\text{old}}$ old loss:

$$L_{\text{old}}(\theta) - L_{\text{old}}(\theta^*) \lesssim \frac{\lambda}{2} \|\Delta\theta\|_{F}^2 + |\epsilon_{\text{replay}}| \cdot \|\Delta\theta\| + \|\epsilon_{\text{eprop}}\| \cdot \|\Delta\theta\| + O(\|\Delta\theta\|^2)$$

where $\|\Delta\theta\|_{F}^2 = \sum_i F_i \Delta\theta_i^2$. Terms correspond to consolidation, replay, and local-credit approximation.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
### Proposed Analysis, Not Hardware Theorem
We stress the bound in Section 4.5 is **proposed**, requiring explicit assumptions:

**A1** Local smoothness: $L_{\text{old}}$ is $L$-smooth in ball $B(\theta^*, R)$.
**A2** Diagonal Fisher approximates Hessian: $\nabla^2 L_{\text{old}}(\theta^*) \approx \text{diag}(F)$ within factor $c$.
**A3** Bounded traces: $e_{ji}^t \in [-E_{\max}, E_{\max}]$, $\psi \in [0,1]$.
**A4** Task drift bounded: $\| \nabla L_{\text{new}} \| \le G$.
**A5** Quantization error bounded: fixed-point rounding adds $\epsilon_q$ with $\|\epsilon_q\| \le \delta_q$.
**A6** Replay accuracy: replay gradient estimator satisfies $\| \hat{g}_{\text{old}} - \nabla L_{\text{old}}\| \le \epsilon_{\text{replay}}$ with high probability.

Under A1, Taylor expansion yields quadratic term. A2 replaces Hessian with Fisher, as in EWC [7] but online. A3-A5 control e-prop/surrogate error. A6 controls sampling.

**Sketch Proof:**

1. Expand $L_{\text{old}}(\theta^*+\Delta\theta)$ to second order, remainder bounded by smoothness.
2. Replace Hessian term by Fisher-weighted norm using A2, adding constant factor slack.
3. Decompose actual Loihi update $\Delta\theta$ into ideal consolidation step plus replay error plus e-prop/surrogate error plus quantization.
4. Apply Cauchy-Schwarz to cross terms to obtain additive error terms.
5. Bound accumulation over $T$ continual steps by telescoping sum, assuming bounded drift (A4).

This yields the proposed bound. It is **not** a proven Loihi 2 guarantee; hardware nondeterminism, temperature-dependent leakage, and mesh contention violate A1-A3 in practice, requiring empirical validation.

### Empirical Context
CLP-SNN's OpenLORIS few-shot evaluation reports competitive accuracy to replay methods while being rehearsal-free in its primary mode, with revised measurements of **0.33 ms, 0.05 mJ, 113× lower latency and 6,600× lower energy** against its specified edge-GPU baseline [5]. This is the specific baseline described in that work's Section 4 (Jetson Orin benchmarking scripts), not a universal claim about Loihi 2 versus all GPUs. Earlier preprint versions reported 70×/5,600× on a different baseline [5]; we cite the revised numbers explicitly to avoid generalization.

Efficiency determinants on Loihi 2 include temporal learning sparsity as dominant lever, breaking super-linear energy-latency scaling observed on conventional hardware [5]. Our stack inherits this: because three-factor updates trigger only on spikes and modulatory events, SynOps scale with activity, not network size.

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch: forgetting bound components (proposed, not proven hardware law)
type Param = Double
type Fisher = Double

forgettingBound :: [Param] -> [Param] -> [Fisher] -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
forgettingBound deltaTheta fisher epsReplay epsEprop lambda =
  let fisherTerm = lambda/2 * sum (zipWith (*) fisher (map (^2) deltaTheta))
      replayTerm = epsReplay * sqrt (sum (map (^2) deltaTheta))
      epropTerm  = epsEprop  * sqrt (sum (map (^2) deltaTheta))
  in fisherTerm + replayTerm + epropTerm
  -- assumes A1-A6, diagonal Fisher, bounded errors; proposed only
```

## 6 Limitations
- **Diagonal Fisher:** Ignores correlations; underestimates forgetting when parameters co-adapt. Full Fisher infeasible in Loihi SRAM.
- **Local Quadratic:** Continual drift may exit smooth ball, invalidating Taylor approximation; requires periodic re-anchoring.
- **Bounded Traces:** Real traces may saturate or underflow in INT8/INT16; stochastic rounding helps but adds variance.
- **Task Drift:** Unbounded drift breaks consolidation; neurogenesis [5] mitigates but increases neuron count pressure on 1M budget.
- **Quantization and Fixed-Point:** Loihi 2's integer graded spikes and fixed-point learning introduce $\epsilon_q$ not present in float analysis; we bound but do not eliminate.
- **Non-Conservative Surrogate:** No scalar loss decreases monotonically [6]; convergence requires careful $\eta$ scheduling and trace damping.
- **Replay Staleness:** Compressed prototypes lose temporal structure; reservoir may become stale under non-stationary streams.
- **Benchmark Specificity:** Efficiency gains of 0.33 ms, 0.05 mJ, 113×, 6,600× are tied to CLP-SNN's OpenLORIS setup and specified edge-GPU baseline [5]; different models, datasets, or batch sizes change ratios.
- **Hardware Variance:** Temperature, voltage (0.5-1.25 V), and mesh contention affect spike timing and learning determinism [2].
- **Safety and Leakage:** Side-channel leakage via spike timing not modeled; requires additional threat analysis.

## 7 Conclusion
We synthesized a Loihi-compatible continual-learning stack that respects hardware realities: 128 cores, ~1M neurons, 120M synapses, programmable neurons, graded payloads, three-factor learning, and Lava's heterogeneous execution [1][2][3]. By combining **e-prop eligibility traces** $\Delta w_{ji} \propto -\sum_t L_j^t e_{ji}^t$ [4], **surrogate derivatives** with explicit non-conservative caveat [6], **three-factor STDP** [8][5], **metaplastic consolidation** analogous but not identical to EWC [7], and **sparse spiking replay** under spike budgets, we achieve local, event-driven, and efficient updates. Our proposed forgetting bound isolates three error sources—Fisher-weighted drift, replay estimation, and e-prop/surrogate approximation—under explicit assumptions A1-A6, intended as an analytical tool, not an established Loihi 2 theorem. Future work includes on-chip Fisher validation, neurogenesis policies that respect 8,192 neurons/core limits, and empirical measurement of $\epsilon_{\text{replay}}$ on physical Oheo Gulch and Kapoho Point systems [2][5]. The combination suggests brain-inspired co-design can break traditional accuracy-efficiency trade-offs for edge AI when assumptions are stated and respected.
