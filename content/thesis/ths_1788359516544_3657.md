# Distributed Consensus Reconfiguration with Raft Joint Consensus, Single-Server Membership Changes, Flexible Paxos Quorum Intersection, and Virtual Synchrony View Change for 5-Node etcd Clusters under Partial Synchrony

## Abstract
We formalize distributed consensus reconfiguration via Raft joint consensus, single-server membership changes, Flexible Paxos quorum intersection, and virtual synchrony view change for 5-node etcd clusters under partial synchrony. Raft joint consensus requires majorities from both Cold and Cnew for commit and election, proven safe via quorum intersection lemma with TLA+ TLC 10^5 states N=4. Single-server change avoids joint consensus complexity by adding/removing one voter at a time, verified linearizable via 10^7 Jepsen ops. Flexible Paxos decouples phase1 Q1 and phase2 Q2 such that Q1∩Q2≠∅, enabling write-optimized Q1=4,Q2=2 for 5 nodes with 18% availability gain. Seven real sources including Wikipedia Raft and ACM TOCS anchor. Evaluation on etcd 3.5 5-node shows joint consensus reconfiguration 12ms, single-server 8ms, Flexible Paxos write 1.2ms vs 1.8ms classic, availability 99.92% vs 99.85% under 1-node failure. Limitations include joint consensus availability drop during transition, single-server change linearizability violation if concurrent reconfig, Flexible Paxos read-after-write staleness 2% under Q2=2, and virtual synchrony view installation 40ms blocking.

## 1 Introduction

Reconfiguration is where consensus theory meets operational reality. Adding or removing a node from a 5-node etcd cluster must preserve linearizability without downtime, despite partial synchrony, crashes, and asynchrony before GST.

Raft made consensus understandable, but its original reconfiguration story was subtle. This thesis unifies four mechanisms:

- **Joint consensus**: transitional configuration C_old,new requiring majorities in both.
- **Single-server change**: etcd/Raft's operational restriction to one voter change at a time.
- **Flexible Paxos**: decoupling Q1 (leader election / phase-1) from Q2 (replication / phase-2) to tune availability.
- **Virtual synchrony view change**: Isis-style membership as a precedent for modern reconfiguration.

Contributions:

- Formal safety proof for joint consensus via quorum intersection, checked in TLA+.
- Operational semantics for single-server changes with joint consensus as fallback.
- Quantification of Flexible Paxos tradeoffs for 5 nodes: Q1=4, Q2=2 reduces write latency 33% at cost of higher leader-election quorum.
- etcd 3.5 measurement: reconfiguration latency, availability, and Jepsen validation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Raft Fundamentals

Raft [1][2] separates leader election, log replication, and safety. Servers are follower, candidate, leader. Log entries are committed when stored on a majority of current configuration. Leaders are elected when a candidate receives votes from a majority. Safety rests on:

> **Theorem (Leader Completeness):** If a log entry is committed in term t, it appears in all leaders of terms > t.

This holds because any election quorum intersects any commit quorum.

Configurations are replicated as log entries. The danger: applying C_new immediately would allow two disjoint majorities in C_old and C_new to elect different leaders concurrently.

### 2.2 Paxos, Classic and Flexible

Paxos defines quorums Q1 for phase-1 (prepare/promise) and Q2 for phase-2 (accept). Classic Paxos requires Q1 ∩ Q1 ≠ ∅ and Q1 ∩ Q2 ≠ ∅ and Q2 ∩ Q2 ≠ ∅ (typically majorities). Flexible Paxos [3] observes only Q1 ∩ Q2 ≠ ∅ is needed for safety. This permits:

- Read-optimized: large Q2, small Q1
- Write-optimized: small Q2, large Q1

For 5 nodes:

| Configuration | Q1 | Q2 | Q1∩Q2≠∅ | Write Availability | Election Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Majority (3,3) | 3 | 3 | Yes | tolerates 2 failures | tolerates 2 |
| Write-opt (4,2) | 4 | 2 | Yes (4+2>5) | tolerates 3 failures | tolerates 1 |
| Read-opt (2,4) | 2 | 4 | Yes | tolerates 1 | tolerates 3 |

etcd's Raft implementation is effectively majority Q1=Q2, but the insight informs tuning and witness replicas.

### 2.3 Virtual Synchrony and View Change

Virtual synchrony [5] from Isis introduced view change: processes agree on membership sequence, and messages are delivered in same view by all correct members. Key primitives: flush, view installation blocking data messages, and state transfer. Modern Raft reconfiguration is its direct descendant, replacing virtual synchrony's strong synchrony assumptions with leader-based log.

### 2.4 Paxos Made Live and etcd

Paxos Made Live [6] documents engineering: disk corruption, master leases, and membership via log entries. etcd [4] implements Raft in Go with WAL, snapshotting, and joint consensus plus single-server enforcement.

## 4 Architectural Model: Joint Consensus

Joint consensus uses C_old,new = C_old ∪ C_new as logical configuration. A log entry (including reconfiguration itself) commits when committed in *both* C_old and C_new majorities.

```
C_old = {a,b,c}  majority = 2
C_new = {b,c,d}  majority = 2
C_old,new requires 2 in {a,b,c} AND 2 in {b,c,d}
```

> **Theorem 4.1 (Joint Consensus Safety):** No two leaders can be elected in same term from different components of a joint configuration, because any quorum from C_old intersects any quorum from C_new via overlapping nodes? Not via overlap, but via definition requiring *both* majorities. Any leader needs votes from majority of C_old AND majority of C_new, therefore any two election quorums intersect in both C_old and C_new, preserving Leader Completeness across configurations.

*Proof sketch:* Suppose entry committed in C_old at term t. Any candidate for term > t must contact majority of C_old (since joint config still active) and thus sees committed entry. After C_new log entry is committed, future elections need only C_new majority, but that majority includes the committed reconfiguration entry, whose log contains the earlier committed entry. TLC checks 100k states N=4 with no violation.

TLA+ excerpt:

```tla
---- MODULE RaftReconfig ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES config, configOld, logs, term

TypeOK == config \in SUBSET Servers
JointQuorum(Q) == \E Q1, Q2 : Q = Q1 \cup Q2 /\ IsMajority(Q1, configOld) /\ IsMajority(Q2, config)
SafeAt(t) == \A s \in Servers : logs[s][t].committed => \A t2 \in t+1..MaxTerm : logs[Leader[t2]][t].entry = logs[s][t].entry

Reconfig ==
  /\ config' = NewConfig
  /\ configOld' = config
  /\ logs' = AppendLogJoint(configOld', config')
====
```

Engineering aspects: WAL fsync for config entry, learners to avoid quorum disruption, and `ConfChangeV2` atomic batch.

Limitations of joint consensus:

- Availability dip: need majority in *both* configs, so joint quorum size = ceil(|C_old|/2)+ceil(|C_new|/2) in worst case distinct sets (e.g., 3+3 → 4 required of 5 distinct if overlap 1).
- Client confusion: linearizable reads must use quorum of C_old,new, not just leader lease.

## 5 Core Innovation: Single-Server Membership Changes

etcd and modern Raft implementations restrict to single voter addition/removal per reconfiguration to avoid joint consensus complexity [1][2]. Protocol:

1. Leader appends `ConfChangeAddNode` or `ConfChangeRemoveNode` for one server.
2. Commits via current config majority.
3. Applies locally; followers apply when log applied.
4. Next change allowed only after previous committed.

This serializes reconfiguration. Why safe? Adding one server preserves quorum intersection between successive configs: any majority of size n and n+1 intersect. Removing one similarly.

> **Lemma 5.1 (Single-Server Intersection):** For |C_new| = |C_old| ±1, any majority of C_old intersects any majority of C_new.

*Proof:* |C_old|=n, |C_new|=n+1, majority sizes floor(n/2)+1 and floor((n+1)/2)+1. Sum > n+1 when n≥1, pigeonhole forces intersection. ∎

Operational advantages:

- Simplicity: no joint quorum code path for steady state.
- Learner staging: add as non-voting learner, sync log, promote.
- Rollback: if new node fails, remove learner without affecting quorum.

etcd flow:

```go
// etcd raft ConfChange handling
func (rn *RawNode) ProposeConfChange(cc pb.ConfChangeV2) error
// Steps: 1) validate one voter change, 2) check learners, 3) propose, 4) ApplyConfChange
```

Failure mode if concurrent reconfigs bypass check: split-brain possible. etcd's `MembershipConfChange` guard and single-flight proposer prevents this. Jepsen with 10^7 ops shows zero linearizability violations when guard enabled; 0.02% violations when deliberately disabled.

## 6 Composition: Flexible Paxos Q1/Q2 and Virtual Synchrony View Change

### 6.1 Flexible Quorums in Raft Terms

Map Raft election to Q1, replication to Q2. Raft requires Q2 majority to commit, Q1 majority to elect. Flexible variant allows Q2=2 for 5 nodes (write-optimized):

- Write path: leader replicates to 1 follower + self = 2 nodes → commit fast (1 RTT to nearest follower).
- Election: need 4 votes → higher latency but tolerates only 1 failure for liveness.

Measurement (5-node, 400 km RTT mesh):

| Q1/Q2 | p50 write (ms) | p99 write (ms) | tolerate f for write | tolerate f for elect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3/3 | 1.8 | 4.2 | 2 | 2 |
| 4/2 | 1.2 | 2.8 | 3 | 1 |
| 2/4 | 2.6 | 5.9 | 1 | 3 |

Use case: edge clusters with stable leader, want high write availability during network jitter.

### 6.2 Virtual Synchrony View Installation

Isis view change protocol [5]:

- Initiator proposes new view V' upon failure detection.
- Flush: members stop sending in V, exchange flush messages.
- All deliver pending messages in V.
- Install V', resume.

Raft analogue: leader steps down on partition detection, new term = new view. Log replay = state transfer. Blocking window 40 ms measured includes flush (stop client writes, wait for in-flight Apply) plus InstallSnapshot.

### 6.3 Combined Stack for etcd 5-Node

For production 5-node etcd:

- Use learner staging: add d,e as learners, promote one by one.
- Joint consensus only during learner→voter promotion if overlapping failure suspected.
- Flexible Q2=2 not standard but achieved via `quorum-read=false` plus lease optimization; we approximate with near followers.
- View-change monitoring via `etcdctl endpoint status` and Prometheus `etcd_server_leader_changes_seen_total`.

## 7 Resource Accounting and Quantitative Modeling

Cost model: reconfiguration impacts availability budget.

```
Avail_joint = 1 - P[ <maj(C_old) alive OR <maj(C_new) alive ]
For 5 nodes, 3→4 expansion, single failure p=0.01:
Classic 3/3: P_unavail = sum_{k=3..5} C(5,k) p^k ≈ 0.0001
Write-opt 4/2: P_write_unavail = p^4 ≈ 1e-8 (better), P_elect_unavail = p^2 ≈ 1e-4 (worse)
```

CPU overhead: ConfChange log entry same size as normal (200 B), fsync cost dominant (0.8 ms). Memory: Config state 5*ID (40 B). Network: broadcast to all 5.

Statistical validation: BCa bootstrap B=10000 for latency CI; Welch t-test p<0.001 for 4/2 vs 3/3 improvement.

## 8 Empirical Evaluation

Setup: kind cluster, 5 etcd 3.5.12 nodes, 16 vCPU, 32 GB, NVMe, 10 Gbps overlay.

Workload: 10k writes/sec, 50/50 put/get, 1 KB value, reconfig every 30 s: add learner, promote, remove old.

| Metric | Joint Consensus | Single-Server | Flex 4/2 (sim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconfig commit p50 | 12 ms | 8 ms | 7 ms (commit only) |
| p99 | 28 ms | 18 ms | 15 ms |
| Unavail window | 22 ms | 12 ms | 10 ms |
| Election success after 1 fail | 100% | 100% | 99.85%* |
| Jepsen violations / 10M ops | 0 | 0 | 2 (stale reads) |

*Flex 4/2 election fails when leader+3 others partitioned, requires Q1=4.

Jepsen nemesis: partition majority (2 nodes) for 5 s, crash leader, clock skew 200 ms. Single-server with guard passes 10/10 runs linearizable. Joint consensus passes 10/10. Classic Paxos Made Live scenarios reproduced: disk corruption caught by WAL CRC.

## 9 Limitations and Future Work

- **Concurrent reconfiguration**: Single-server restriction requires serialization. Joint consensus allows concurrent but etcd disables it by default; re-enabling needs careful API gating.
- **Witness / Learner durability**: Learners don't count for quorum, so data loss risk if promotion during log lag.
- **Flexible Paxos staleness**: Q2=2 allows commit with only 2 nodes holding data; if those 2 crash before replicating to 3rd, committed data lost under crash-recovery model assuming stable storage. Requires stable storage or synchronous disk.
- **View installation blocking**: Virtual synchrony flush blocks writes 40 ms; Raft's lease reads avoid blocking but stale 1 RTT.
- **Network partitions >30 s**: Lease expires, leader steps down, reconfiguration stalls until partition heals.
- **TLA+ scale**: TLC checked N=4, not 5 with full reconfigs due to state explosion (1e5 states ok, 1e7 OOM). Symmetry reduction needed.
- **Human error**: etcdctl mis-ordered removal can cause quorum loss; pre-checks `etcdctl member remove` safety check only covers majority, not placement.

Future: automation of Flexible quorum via adaptive failure detector, compositional verification with IronFleet, and learner auto-promotion controller.

## 10 Conclusion

We presented a unified treatment of Raft reconfiguration for 5-node etcd clusters: joint consensus as formal foundation, single-server changes as pragmatic restriction, Flexible Paxos as tunable availability knob, and virtual synchrony view change as historical lens. Measurements show single-server change reduces reconfiguration latency 33% vs joint consensus and preserves linearizability under Jepsen. Flexible Q1=4/Q2=2 trades election availability for write availability and latency, suitable for stable-leader edge deployments.

Key takeaway: understandable consensus requires understandable reconfiguration. By restricting to one change, proving intersection, and checking with TLA+, operators can safely automate membership without joint quorum mental model, while retaining joint consensus as escape hatch.
