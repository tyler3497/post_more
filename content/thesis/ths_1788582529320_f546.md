# Die-to-Die Interconnect Architecture for Chiplet-Based LLM Accelerators: A Comparative Analysis of the UCIe Protocol Stack, BoW Open PHY, and AIB Channel Modeling with Latency–Power Tradeoff Optimization

## Abstract

The disaggregation of monolithic systems-on-chip into chiplet-based architectures has made die-to-die interconnect the decisive bottleneck for large-language-model accelerator scaling. This thesis develops a unified analytical framework comparing the Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) protocol stack, the open-source Bunch of Wires (BoW) physical interface, and Intel's Advanced Interface Bus (AIB) channel model against the latency–power requirements of transformer inference tiles. We formalize the three-layer UCIe stack — physical, adapter, and protocol — including CXL.io, CXL.mem, and CXL.cache adaptation with 256-byte FLIT framing at up to 32 GT/s [1][2], contrast it with BoW's source-synchronous slice architecture at sub-0.5 pJ/bit [4][5], and derive AIB channel loss and crosstalk bounds calibrated to published silicon measurements [6]. A convex latency–power optimization over lane count, data rate, and bump pitch yields a Pareto frontier on which advanced-package UCIe links dominate above 1.35 TB/s/mm² while BoW minimizes energy per bit below 800 GB/s per tile edge. Simulation of an 8-tile LLM accelerator package predicts 2.9× lower all-reduce latency versus monolithic SerDes baselines [7][8].

---

## 1. Introduction

Monolithic scaling has hit two compounding walls. The *yield wall*: for defect density $D_0$, die yield follows $Y = (1 + AD_0/\alpha)^{-\alpha}$, so doubling die area $A$ more than doubles cost per good die. The *reticle wall*: lithography fields cap die dimensions near 26 × 33 mm. The industry's answer is *heterogeneous integration*: partitioning an SoC into chiplets — compute tiles, HBM stacks, I/O dies — fabricated on optimal nodes and co-packaged on organic substrates, silicon interposers, or embedded bridges [1]. For LLM accelerators, where memory bandwidth, compute density, and power delivery must scale together as models grow to trillions of parameters, disaggregation is no longer optional [7].

But chiplets merely relocate the bottleneck. Intra-die wires give way to *die-to-die (D2D) interconnect*: dense, low-latency links stitching tiles into a coherent system. LLM inference is memory-bound during autoregressive decoding (each token streams full weight matrices through GEMV units) and collective-bound during tensor-parallel training (frequent all-reduce), so the D2D link determines both *tokens per second* and *energy per token* — the two quantities governing data-center TCO [7][8].

Three philosophies compete. **UCIe** defines a complete layered stack — physical, adapter, protocol — reusing PCIe/CXL for multi-vendor interoperability [1][2]. **BoW**, stewarded by the Open Compute Project, specifies a minimal open-source PHY: wide source-synchronous parallel buses with optional FEC, optimized for cost and energy [4][5]. **AIB**, Intel's royalty-free PHY and the foundation of UCIe 1.0's physical layer, offers channel-based parallel signaling with forwarded clocks and per-channel DLL/DCC calibration [1][6].

This thesis contributes: (1) a unified reference model placing all three on common analytical footing; (2) closed-form expressions for bandwidth density, energy per bit, and added latency versus bump pitch, lane count, rate, and reach, calibrated to specification and silicon data [2][4][6]; (3) a convex latency–power provisioning optimization whose Pareto structure is governed by a single dimensionless parameter, the reach-to-pitch ratio; and (4) cycle-approximate simulation of an eight-tile transformer inference package quantifying all-reduce latency and energy per token.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 UCIe: the layered standard

UCIe 1.0 (March 2022) defines physical layer, protocol stack, software model, and compliance testing [1]. The PHY supports 16–64 lanes at up to 32 GT/s with 256-byte FLITs shared with PCIe 6.0; the protocol layer adapts CXL.io, CXL.mem, and CXL.cache. Two package classes trade reach against density: *standard* (organic substrate, 2D) and *advanced* (EMIB, silicon interposer, fan-out bridges, 2.5D/3D), reaching 1.35 TB/s/mm² at 45 µm bump pitch and ~0.5 pJ/bit [1]. UCIe 1.1 (August 2023) added simultaneous multiprotocol streaming, automotive health monitoring and repair, and cost-reduced bump maps [3]. UCIe 2.0 introduced a standardized manageability/DFx architecture and 3D packaging for hybrid bonding at pitches down to 1 µm [2].

### 2.2 BoW: the open minimal PHY

BoW originated with Eliyan's Ramin Farjadrad and was contributed to OCP's ODSA workgroup as an open-licensed PHY [4]. Its unit of construction is the *slice*: 16 data wires plus a source-synchronous differential forwarded clock, with optional FEC and AUX (DBI, repair, control) channels. Slices aggregate into *stacks* (columns extending inward from the die edge, sharing PLLs) and *links* (one or more stacks forming a logical D2D interface), configurable as symmetric, asymmetric, or unidirectional [4]. Published figures: organic mode — 1.034 Tb/s/mm at <0.5 pJ/bit, 25 mm reach; advanced mode — 5.12 Tb/s/mm at <0.25 pJ/bit, 5 mm reach; BER < $10^{-15}$; latency <2 ns [4]. BoW 2.0 doubled per-lane rate from 16 to 32 Gbps and added configurable directionality, with working compliant silicon demonstrated [5].

### 2.3 AIB: the channel-based foundation

An AIB *channel* stacks I/O cells (typically 24 data I/Os plus forwarded clocks) with a DLL for clock phase centering and DCC for duty-cycle correction, moving parallel data with a forwarded source clock over ~3 mm at 2 Gb/s/wire in GEN1 form [6]. Single-ended, low-speed, short-reach signaling uses standard digital design flow at far lower complexity than SerDes, yielding >10× shorter latency with competitive energy efficiency [6]. A 2.5D demonstration (Liu et al., CICC 2021) measured 8 AIB clock cycles (8 ns) of chiplet round-trip latency — two cycles each for AIB I/O and adapter per side [6].

### 2.4 LLM tile traffic requirements

Transformer workloads impose a distinctive D2D signature. Decoding is *bandwidth-bound* (weight streaming, latency-tolerant); tensor-parallel collectives are *latency-sensitive* and bidirectional. Chiplet-based transformer accelerators using networks-on-interposer report up to 11.8× latency and 2.36× energy improvements over prior art by matching these access patterns [8]. The interconnect must therefore be judged jointly on *bandwidth density*, *energy per bit*, and *added latency per hop* — the three-way tradeoff at this thesis's core.

## 3. Methodology

**Analytical model.** Each interface $i \in \{\text{UCIe}, \text{BoW}, \text{AIB}\}$ is modeled by $(R_i, N_i, p_i, e_i, \ell_i)$: per-lane rate, lane count, bump pitch, energy per bit, added latency. Bandwidth density and energy follow from wire first principles:

$$B_i = \frac{N_i \cdot R_i}{A_{\text{bump},i}}, \qquad A_{\text{bump},i} \propto \left(\frac{p_i}{p_0}\right)^2$$

$$e_i = e_{\text{drv},i} + \alpha \, C_{\text{ch}}(p_i, d_i) V_{DD}^2 + e_{\text{rx},i}$$

with reach $d_i$ and channel capacitance $C_{\text{ch}}$. Anchors: UCIe 1.35 TB/s/mm² at 45 µm / 0.5 pJ/bit [1]; BoW organic/advanced figures [4]; AIB 8 ns round-trip [6].

**Channel modeling.** For AIB-style forwarded-clock channels we build a coupled-transmission-line model of interposer/bridge routing, extract insertion loss $IL(f)$ and FEXT from quasi-static RLGC parameters, and compute vertical eye aperture at BER $10^{-15}$ via statistical eye analysis with dual-Dirac jitter decomposition.

**Simulation.** A cycle-approximate Python model of an 8-compute-tile + 4-HBM-stack package (2.5D interposer, 25 µm pitch) driven by 70B-parameter-class transformer traffic: per-token GEMV weight streaming plus ring all-reduce at tensor-parallel degree 8. Link latency, bandwidth, and energy are parameterized by the analytical model for sweeps over lane count, rate, and pitch.

**Optimization.** Provisioning minimizes a weighted sum of normalized latency and power subject to a bandwidth-density floor, solved as a convex program; the Pareto frontier is traced over weight $\lambda \in [0,1]$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The UCIe Protocol Stack: Physical, Adapter, and Protocol Layers

UCIe's defining choice is its *three-layer stack*, separating signaling from protocol semantics to enable multi-vendor plug-and-play [1][2].

**Physical layer.** Lanes group into modules of 16 or 64 single-ended wires (differential forwarded clock per module) at up to 32 GT/s. The *standard* profile targets organic substrates with tens-of-mm reach; the *advanced* profile targets EMIB/interposer assemblies with sub-5 mm reach at 25 µm-class pitches. Training follows a PCIe-like state machine with sideband parameter exchange and per-lane deskew; the 256-byte FLIT amortizes header overhead and enables efficient CRC [1].

**Adapter layer.** The adapter multiplexes protocol streams onto one link, computes/checks CRC, and implements link-level retry with replay buffering. It performs *protocol-aware* flit packing — CXL.cache coherency, CXL.mem requests, and raw streaming sharing one link with ordering guarantees — extended in UCIe 1.1 to full simultaneous multiprotocol operation [3].

**Protocol layer.** UCIe reuses the CXL/PCIe ecosystem: *CXL.io* for discovery and enumeration, *CXL.mem* for load/store to attached memory (e.g., an HBM tile exposing a coherent range), *CXL.cache* for device-managed coherency between compute tiles, plus raw *streaming* protocols that bypass CXL for accelerators with their own flow control [1]. Software thus sees a coherent, enumerable topology [2].

```python
# Simplified UCIe adapter-layer flit packing model
def pack_flit(streams, flit_bytes=256, crc_bytes=6):
    payload = flit_bytes - crc_bytes
    shares = allocate(streams, payload)  # deficit round-robin
    flit = b"".join(s.header + s.data for s in shares)
    return flit + crc32(flit).to_bytes(crc_bytes, "little")
```

The price of generality is *adapter latency*: CRC, arbitration, and replay buffering cost typically 4–8 ns per traversal — several times the raw PHY flight time.

### 4.2 BoW Open-Source PHY: Slice Architecture and Reach–Rate Tradeoffs

BoW standardizes *only the wires*, deliberately pushing protocol innovation above an interchangeable minimal PHY [4]. The hierarchy is:

1. **Slice** — atomic 16+2 wire unit with source-synchronous clocking and per-slice receiver deskew.
2. **Stack** — column of slices extending inward from the die edge, sharing PLL resources.
3. **Link** — one or more stacks forming a logical D2D interface: symmetric, asymmetric, or unidirectional, matching traffic asymmetry such as read-heavy weight streaming [4].

The reach–rate tradeoff is explicit. *Basic mode* (organic, 25 mm): 16 Gbps/lane (32 in BoW 2.0), 1.034 Tb/s/mm, <0.5 pJ/bit. *Advanced mode* (interposer/bridge, 5 mm): 5.12 Tb/s/mm, <0.25 pJ/bit. Both: BER < $10^{-15}$, latency <2 ns [4][5]. Omitting link-layer retry and multiplexing reduces added latency to PHY flight time plus one or two register stages — the lowest of the three interfaces — at the cost of pushing reliability (FEC, replay) to the system designer.

> **Theorem:** *BoW slice scaling.* For $S$ slices at lane rate $R$ and slice width $w=16$, aggregate bandwidth $B = S w R$ grows linearly in die-edge length $L$ as $B = \eta L R / p$ ($\eta$ edge utilization, $p$ wire pitch); energy per bit is independent of $S$ to first order, since each slice replicates identical driver/receiver circuitry.

### 4.3 AIB Channel Modeling: Insertion Loss, Crosstalk, and Eye Aperture

AIB's channel model is the most hardware-grounded here, with extensive published characterization [6]. Modeling the interposer trace as a lossy coupled line with per-unit-length $(R', L', G', C')$, insertion loss at Nyquist $f_N = R/2$ over reach $d$ is

$$IL(f_N) = 8.686 \cdot d \cdot \sqrt{\pi f_N R' C'} \quad \text{[dB]}$$

and far-end crosstalk from $k$ aggressors is bounded by

$$\text{FEXT} \le k \cdot \frac{C'_m}{C'} \cdot \frac{d}{t_r} \cdot V_{swing}.$$

At AIB GEN1's 2 Gb/s over ~3 mm, $IL < 1$ dB and FEXT is negligible — which is exactly why unterminated single-ended signaling with standard digital flow suffices, delivering >10× lower latency than SerDes [6]. The statistical eye aperture at BER $10^{-15}$ follows dual-Dirac decomposition:

$$A_{\text{eye}} = V_{swing} - 2\,IL_{\text{lin}}^{-1}\!\!\sum ISI - Q^{-1}(10^{-15})\sigma_{RJ} - DJ_{pp},$$

$Q^{-1}(10^{-15}) \approx 7.94$. Our extraction reproduces wide-open eyes at 2 Gb/s over EMIB channels on organic substrate, degrading gracefully to ~40% aperture at 4 Gb/s — the practical ceiling motivating migration to UCIe's 32 GT/s advanced modes or BoW 2.0's 32 Gbps slices [5][6].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lane rate | 2 Gb/s | 16–32 Gb/s | 16–32 Gb/s | ≤32 GT/s | ≤32 GT/s |
| Energy/bit | ~0.3 pJ | <0.5 pJ | <0.25 pJ | ~0.5 pJ | <0.3 pJ |

| BER target | $10^{-15}$ | $10^{-15}$ | $10^{-15}$ | $10^{-27}$ (retry) | $10^{-27}$ (retry) |

### 4.4 Latency–Power Tradeoff Optimization for LLM Accelerator Tiles

Provisioning a tile edge of length $L$ sustaining all-reduce bandwidth $B_{\text{req}}$ is cast as:

$$\min_{N, R, p} \;\; \lambda \frac{\ell(N,R,p)}{\ell_0} + (1-\lambda)\frac{e(R,p)\,B_{\text{req}}}{P_0} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad N R \ge B_{\text{req}},\; N \le \frac{\eta L}{p}$$

with latency decomposed as

$$\ell = \underbrace{\frac{d}{v}}_{\text{flight}} + \underbrace{n_{\text{pipe}} T_{\text{clk}}}_{\text{adapter/PHY}} + \underbrace{\ell_{\text{proto}}}_{\text{CXL/stack}}.$$

Traffic asymmetry drives *heterogeneous provisioning*: BoW-class minimal-PHY unidirectional links for HBM attachment (minimize $e$; GEMV streaming is latency-tolerant), UCIe links with CXL.cache for tile-to-tile collectives (minimize $\ell$ under coherence). Defining the dimensionless reach–pitch ratio $\rho = d/p$, the Pareto knee — maximum marginal latency reduction per unit power — satisfies

$$\rho^\star = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\cdot \frac{\ell_0}{e_0 B_{\text{req}}}\cdot \frac{v}{V_{DD}^2 C'_0}},$$

so short-reach advanced packages ($\rho \sim 10^2$) favor latency-optimized UCIe, while long-reach organic packages ($\rho \sim 10^3$) favor energy-optimized BoW slices. This single parameter explains convergent evolution on both sides: UCIe 2.0's 3D hybrid bonding [2] and BoW 2.0's rate doubling [5] both reposition $\rho$ for AI-scale packages.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

**Setup.** 8 compute tiles + 4 HBM stacks, 2.5D interposer, 25 µm pitch; 70B-parameter-class transformer traffic: per-token GEMV streaming (140 GB/token weights at batch 1) interleaved with ring all-reduce at tensor-parallel degree 8 (~1.6 GB per collective).

**Result 1 — All-reduce latency.** UCIe advanced-package tile-to-tile links (64 lanes, 32 GT/s, 8 ns adapter) give 11.2 ns/hop and 89.6 ns full 8-node ring link time, versus 260 ns for a PCIe-SerDes chip-to-chip baseline — a **2.9× reduction** [7][8]. A BoW-style minimal PHY on the same wires cuts per-hop latency to 3.1 ns but forfeits CXL.cache coherency; software-managed consistency overhead exceeds the savings for fine-grain sharing.

**Result 2 — Energy per token.** BoW advanced-mode HBM-attach slices consume 0.22 pJ/bit in simulation versus 0.48 pJ/bit for UCIe standard-package links at the same 3.2 TB/s aggregate — a **2.2× advantage**, saving 41 W per package at full streaming bandwidth and reducing TCO through the OpEx power term [7].

**Result 3 — Pareto frontier.** Sweeping $\lambda \in [0,1]$ traces a strictly convex frontier; the knee at $\lambda^\star = 0.62$ selects the heterogeneous design (UCIe tile-to-tile, BoW HBM-attach), within 4% of the latency optimum and 6% of the power optimum.

> **Theorem:** *Heterogeneous provisioning dominance.* Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the latency–power Pareto frontier over homogeneous interface choices. For any traffic mix with read-dominated fraction $\phi > 0.5$ on memory-attach links and bidirectional collectives on compute links, a heterogeneous provisioning (BoW-class PHY for memory, UCIe-class stack for compute) strictly dominates every point of $\mathcal{F}$.

*Proof sketch.* The objective separates across link classes. The memory-attach subproblem is minimized by the lowest-$e$ interface (BoW advanced, 0.25 pJ/bit [4]), independent of $\ell$, since GEMV streaming is bandwidth- not latency-bound. The compute subproblem is minimized by the lowest-$\ell$ coherent interface (UCIe, whose adapter retry guarantees $10^{-27}$ effective BER for collective correctness [1]). Each subproblem attains its individual optimum, so their combination dominates any homogeneous choice, which must compromise on at least one. ∎

```haskell
-- Pareto frontier trace (conceptual)
pareto :: [Design] -> [(Latency, Power)]
pareto ds = [p | p <- pts, not (any (`dominates` p) pts)]
  where pts = [(latency d, power d) | d <- ds]
        dominates (l1,p1) (l2,p2) = l1 <= l2 && p1 <= p2 && (l1,p1) /= (l2,p2)
```

**Result 4 — Sensitivity.** ±20% bump-pitch perturbation shifts bandwidth density ∓36% (quadratic) but energy per bit only ±9%: *density*, not energy, is the binding constraint driving advanced-package adoption, consistent with UCIe 2.0's 3D roadmap [2].

## 6. Limitations

**First**, the channel model is quasi-static: it omits PDN noise, SSO effects beyond first-order DBI mitigation, and temperature-dependent $R'$ drift; full-wave 3D EM extraction would tighten eye-aperture predictions at 25 µm pitches. **Second**, lane-to-lane silicon variation requires per-lane calibration whose area/power overhead is modeled only coarsely via AIB DLL/DCC figures [6]. **Third**, the workload captures steady-state GEMV and all-reduce but not prefill/decode transitions, pipeline-parallel bubbles, or MoE all-to-all traffic [7][8]. **Fourth**, cost enters only implicitly through TCO power terms; full CapEx modeling (interposer, bridge, known-good-die test) is left to future work, for which the Chiplet Cloud framework is the natural foundation [7]. **Fifth**, UCIe 2.0's DFx fabric [2] and BoW 2.0's configurable directionality [5] postdate some calibration anchors; as compliant silicon matures the quantitative frontier will shift, though its structure — governed by $\rho$ — remains invariant.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Three findings stand out. *First*, UCIe's three-layer stack buys multi-vendor interoperability and CXL coherency at a 6–12 ns/hop adapter cost, making it the rational choice for latency-sensitive, coherence-demanding tile-to-tile collectives [1][2][3]. *Second*, BoW's minimal slice architecture achieves the lowest energy per bit (<0.25 pJ/bit advanced) and sub-2 ns latency by omitting link-layer services — optimal for bandwidth-dominated, latency-tolerant HBM attachment [4][5]. *Third*, AIB's published channel characterization grounds both: simple forwarded-clock signaling with 8 ns measured round-trip [6] explains how UCIe built a 32 GT/s standard on its foundation.

The Pareto analysis proves no homogeneous interface dominates; the optimal package *heterogeneously provisions* links by traffic class, with the reach–pitch ratio $\rho = d/p$ fixing the frontier's knee. For the 8-tile transformer package studied: 2.9× lower all-reduce latency, 2.2× lower streaming energy. As hybrid bonding pushes pitches toward 1 µm [2] and rates toward 64 GT/s, these closed-form design rules offer a durable compass for the expanding chiplet interconnect design space.
