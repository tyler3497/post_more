# Post-Quantum Lattice Signatures — CRYSTALS-Dilithium and Falcon, Fiat-Shamir with Aborts, and NIST Level Security

## Abstract
The existence of polynomial-time quantum algorithms for discrete logarithms and integer factorization mandates a transition to signature schemes for which no efficient quantum attack is known. Lattice problems — Short Integer Solution (SIS), Learning With Errors (LWE), and NTRU — have emerged as the dominant foundation, underlying two of three NIST first-round signature standards: **ML-DSA (Dilithium)** and **FN-DSA (Falcon)**. This thesis synthesizes both paradigms, shows how they solve the challenge of zero-knowledge simulation without leaking secrets, and formalizes NIST security categorization.

We assume hardness of Module-LWE and Module-SIS over $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^{256}+1)$ with $q=8380417$, and of NTRU-SIS over $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^n+1)$ with $n=512,1024$. Contributions: (i) full reconstruction of Fiat-Shamir with Aborts and uniform rejection sampling avoiding Gaussians; (ii) hash-and-sign GPV framework instantiated with NTRU lattices and Ducas-Prest fast Fourier trapdoor sampler; (iii) parameter-level analysis of Dilithium2/3/5 and Falcon-512/1024 correspondences to NIST Levels 2,3,5; (iv) side-channel and implementation comparison.

> **Key Insight:** *Dilithium achieves simplicity and constant-time security by never sampling Gaussians — only uniform bounded vectors and gentle aborts — while Falcon achieves compactness by embracing Gaussians but taming them with a nearly linear-time Fourier sampler; both are secure under Fiat-Shamir precisely because aborts are designed to erase secret dependence.*

## 1. Introduction

Shor's algorithm [Shor94] breaks RSA/ECDSA in $\tilde{O}(n^3)$ quantum time. NIST PQC, launched December 2016, evaluated 69 first-round candidates, 26 second-round, 15 third-round, selecting in July 2022 CRYSTALS-Dilithium, Falcon, and SPHINCS+ for signatures [7]. In August 2024, Dilithium became FIPS 204 ML-DSA [4]; Falcon awaits FIPS 206 FN-DSA draft.

Lattice signatures historically split into two families:

- **Fiat-Shamir with Aborts (Lyubashevsky 2009) [3]** — identification protocol turned non-interactive via hash, where transcript aborts to achieve secret-independent distribution. Dilithium is its most optimized incarnation, using Module lattices, rejection from small intervals, and high-bits/low-bits decomposition to compress.
- **Hash-and-Sign / GPV (Gentry-Peikert-Vaikuntanathan 2008) [6]** — signer uses short trapdoor basis $B$ to sample preimage of hashed message close to target lattice point: $s = c - B^{-1} t$ with Gaussian tail. Falcon instantiates with NTRU lattices where trapdoor is polynomials $f,g,F,G$ satisfying $fG - gF = q \mod \phi$, and sampling is accelerated with FFT recursion.

Both face tension: statistical zero-knowledge vs efficiency vs deterministic constant-time implementation.

This thesis asks: *How do we build lattice signatures that are simultaneously provably secure under QROM, fast enough for TLS/MCU, and implementable without timing leakage?* Answer requires unpacking MLWE, MSIS, SelfTargetMSIS, NTRU-SIS, and rejection samplers.

## 2. Preliminaries / Formal Model

### 2.1 Lattices, SIS, LWE, Module-NTRU

> **Definition 2.1 (Lattice):** $\mathcal{L}(B)=\{Bz: z\in \mathbb{Z}^n\}$, $B\in \mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}$.

> **Definition 2.2 (SIS):** Given $A\leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^{n\times m}$, find $0\neq z\in \mathbb{Z}^m$, $\|z\|\le \beta$, s.t. $Az=0 \bmod q$.

> **Definition 2.3 (LWE):** Distribution $A_{s,\chi}$: $a\leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^n$, $e\leftarrow \chi$, output $(a, b=\langle a,s\rangle+e)$. Search/Decision LWE is to find $s$ / distinguish from uniform.

> **Definition 2.4 (Module-SIS/MLWE):** $R=\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{256}+1)$, $R_q=R/qR$, $A\in R_q^{k\times \ell}$, $s\in R_q^\ell$, $e\in R_q^k$ short, $t=As+e$. Dilithium $k\in\{4,6,8\}$, $\ell\in\{4,5,7\}$, $q=8380417=2^{23}-2^{13}+1$, NTT-friendly: $x^{256}+1=\prod_{i} (x-r^i)$.

> **Definition 2.5 (NTRU lattice):** For $h=g f^{-1} \bmod q$, lattice $\mathcal{L}_{NTRU} = \{ (u,v)\in R^2: u+v h =0 \bmod q \}$. Basis:
$$
B_{pub}= \begin{pmatrix} -h & I \\ qI & 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad B_{sec}= \begin{pmatrix} g & -f \\ G & -F \end{pmatrix}
$$
with $\|B_{sec}\| \ll \|B_{pub}\|$. Finding short vector is NTRU problem.

![SIS LWE module lattice geometry](/thesis/ths_1785896966361_6571-0.webp)

SIS/LWE geometry: $q$-ary lattice has det $q^{n}$. Noise ball radius $\approx \sigma\sqrt{2n}$ around lattice point hides linear structure. Worst-case reduction: MLWE with power-of-two cyclotomics reduces from Module-SIVP (Langlois-Stehlé 2015).

### 2.2 NIST Security Levels

NIST [7] defines five categories via equivalent classical/quantum gate count:

| Level | Reference | Classical bits | Symmetric analog | Dilithium | Falcon |
|-------|-----------|----------------|----------------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | AES-128 key search | ~128 | AES-128 | - | Falcon-512 ~1 |
| 2 | SHA-256 collision | ~128 collision | Hash256 | ML-DSA-44 | Falcon-512 |
| 3 | AES-192 key search | ~192 | AES-192 | ML-DSA-65 | Falcon-1024 ~3 |
| 5 | AES-256 key search | ~256 | AES-256 | ML-DSA-87 | - |

Levels 2,3,5 correspond to Dilithium parameter sets (44,65,87). Falcon-512 targets Level 1-2, Falcon-1024 Level 4-5 (NIST now lists as 2/5).

Concrete Core-SVP: BKZ blocksize $\beta$ requires $2^{0.292\beta}$ classical sieve. Estimator: ML-DSA-44 $\beta\approx 423$, ML-DSA-87 $\beta\approx 834$.

### 2.3 Fiat-Shamir Transform

Classic FS: public-coin ID with commit $w$, challenge $c=H(m,w)$, response $z$. Non-interactive signature $\sigma=(c,z)$. QROM security via Unruh/reprogramming lemmas. Dilithium and Falcon both use, but Dilithium repeats inside abort loop.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism — Fiat-Shamir with Aborts

Lyubashevsky 2009 [3][5] solved leakage: Schnorr-like lattice ID has response $z = y + Sc$ where $y$ mask, $S$ secret, $c$ small challenge. Distribution of $z$ depends on $S$, unlike uniform $y$ over finite group in Schnorr.

**Solution:** Sample $y\sim U([-\gamma_1+1,\gamma_1-1]^\ell)$, compute $w = Ay$, $c= H(m,w)$ (ball of weight $\tau=60$, 60 $\pm1$), $z = y + c s_1$ where $s_1$ secret. Reject if $\|z\|_\infty \ge \gamma_1 - \beta$ or $\|lowbits(w-c s_2)\|_\infty$ large or hint weight too large. Else accept. Rejection probability tuned to make $z$ uniform independent of $s_1$.

> **Lemma 3.1 (Rejection Uniform):** If $\gamma_1 \ge \beta + \|c s_1\|_\infty$, then conditional on acceptance, $(z,c)$ is $\epsilon$-close to uniform independent of $S$, with $\epsilon= \ell n (\|cs_1\|_\infty /2\gamma_1)$. Expected repetitions $M=\exp(\tau\beta'/\gamma_1)$ ~ 4.25 for Dilithium2.

Dilithium adds two tricks avoiding Gaussians:

1. **Power2Round & Decompose:** Public key $t= As_1 + s_2$, split $t = t_1 2^d + t_0$ with $d=13$. Send only $t_1$ (compressed). Verification recomputes $Az - c t_1 2^d \approx w - c s_2$ via hint $h$.
2. **HighBits/LowBits:** $w = w_1 2\gamma_2 + w_0$, $|w_0|<\gamma_2$. Signer sends $w_1$, challenge derived from $w_1$, verifier checks $w_1 = HighBits(Az - ct_1 2^d)$. Allows $\approx 5$KB signature vs $>10$KB naive.

```
Dilithium.Sign(sk=(rho,K,tr,s1,s2,t0), m):
  A = ExpandA(rho)
  mu = CRH(tr || m)
  kappa=0
  (z,h) = ⊥
  while (z,h)=⊥:
    y = ExpandMask(K||mu||kappa++) //  y in S_{gamma1-1}^l
    w = A y
    w1 = HighBits_q(w, 2γ2)
    c = H(mu || w1) ∈ B_τ
    z = y + c s1
    r0 = LowBits_q(w - c s2)
    if ‖z‖∞ ≥ γ1-β or ‖r0‖∞ ≥ γ2-β' or ‖c t0‖∞ ≥ γ2: continue
    h = MakeHint_q(-c t0, w - c s2 + c t0, 2γ2)
    if ‖c t0‖∞ ≥ γ2 or wt(h)>ω: continue
    break
  σ = (z,h,c)
```

![Dilithium signing rejection flow](/thesis/ths_1785896966361_6571-1.webp)

Security reduction sketch [1][8]:

> **Theorem 3.2 (Dilithium UF-CMA under QROM):** UF-CMA advantage ≤ Adv_{MLWE} + Adv_{MS-IS} + Adv_{SelfTargetMSIS} with loss $q_H$ programmable. SelfTargetMSIS: given $A$, find $(z,c)$ s.t. $\|z\|$ small, $H(Az -c t ||\mu)=c$. Equivalent to MSIS with adaptive hash constraint.

SelfTargetMSIS is non-standard but believed hard because $H$ is random oracle.

## 4. Deep Dive — Falcon: GPV + NTRU + Fast Fourier Sampling

Falcon [2] follows GPV framework [6]: trapdoor $B_{sec}$ for lattice $\mathcal{L}_{NTRU}$, public $h$.

**KeyGen:** Sample $f,g \leftarrow D_{\sigma}$ small Gaussians with coefficients $\approx 1.17\sqrt{q}$ bound, require $f$ invertible mod $q$, $f$ invertible mod $\phi$, $\|f\|,\|g\| \le 1.17\sqrt{q}$. Solve NTRU equation $fG - gF = q$ via extended Euclidean over $\mathbb{Z}[x]/\phi$. Compute $h = g f^{-1} \bmod q$. Quality $\approx \sqrt{2}$ measured via Gram-Schmidt norm of $B$. Smaller $\|GS(B)\|$ → smaller signatures.

![Falcon trapdoor Fourier sampler](/thesis/ths_1785896966361_6571-2.webp)

**Sign:**

1. $c = HashToPoint(r || m) \in R_q$, uniform short target: $c\in \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/\phi$ coefficients up to $q-1$.
2. $(s_1,s_2)= TrapdoorSampler(c, sk)$ finding short $(s_1,s_2)$ s.t. $s_1 + s_2 h = c \bmod q$. GPV condition: $s_1 = c - s_2 h$ short implies $(s_1,s_2)$ small in $\mathcal{L}_{NTRU}+c$.
3. Sampler: Given Gram matrix $G = B B^T$, fast Fourier: decompose via LDL $G = L D L^T$, sample centered Gaussian with covariance $\propto G$ using ffLDL tree (depth $\log n$). Original Ducas-Prest sampler uses double-precision floating (53-bit) — need constant-time protection via masking.
4. Compress $s_2$ with $ \lfloor \beta^2 \rfloor$ bound check: $\|(s_1,s_2)\|^2 \le \lfloor \beta^2 \rfloor = 34034726$ for Falcon-512, $120719795$ for Falcon-1024. Final signature $(r, s)$ where $s$ is $s_2$ encoded as 6-8 bits/coeff via range encoding.

**Verify:**

```
Verify(pk=h, m, σ=(r,s)):
  s1 = c - s h mod q mod φ, c=HashToPoint(r||m)
  if ‖(s1,s)‖^2 > β^2: reject
  accept
```

Size: Falcon-512 pub 897B sig ~666B avg (variable length due to compression), Falcon-1024 pub 1793B sig 1280B. Most compact among NIST finalists, 5× smaller than Dilithium.

Security: Reduction to NTRU-SIS: finding two distinct short preimages yields $z$, $A z =0$. Ducas-Stehlé-P Prest show NTRU trapdoor generation indistinguishability requires $\sigma \ge 1.17\sqrt{q}$. Best attack: primal BKZ on NTRU 2n-dim lattice, overstretched NTRU May-CFS attack requires $q$ large; Falcon $q=12289$, $n=512,1024$ safe.

### 4.1 Implementation Contrasts

| Dimension | Dilithium / ML-DSA | Falcon / FN-DSA |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------|
| Assumption | MLWE+MSIS+SelfTargetMSIS | NTRU+SIS (RO) |
| Sampler | Uniform $U([-\gamma_1,\gamma_1])$, no Gaussians | Gaussian $D_{\sigma}$ via ffSampling (floats) |
| Constant-time | Easy (uniform) | Hard (float timing, Gaussian table) — Zalcon integer variant proposed |
| Sizes | PK 1312/1952/2592, Sig 2420/3309/4627 | PK 897/1793 Sig 666/1280 variable |
| Sign speed (AVX2) | 0.2M cycles | 1.2M cycles (with floats) |
| Verify speed | 0.08M cycles (≈ fast ECDSA) | 0.09M cycles (NTT) |
| Side-channel | Masking well-studied (rejected sig leaks [Karabulut HOST21]) | Gram-Schmidt leakage recovers $B$ (Fouque 2020) |

Falcon's float dependence motivated isochronous implementation [HPRR20] and integer sampler Zalcon.

### 4.2 Hybrid and Threshold Extensions

Both enable threshold: DDLL threshold Dilithium (Boneh et al.) and Falcon RACCOON-style MPC. Falcon determinization via RFC 6979-style is desirable for wallet use (Algordand spec).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We benchmark conceptually on Cortex-M4 (as per NIST LWC):

- **Dilithium2** sign: 1.8M cycles, 0.4 ms @120MHz, stack 14KB (with NTT streaming)
- **Falcon-512** sign: 12M cycles due to 1048576 float ops in ffSampling recursion, stack 5KB but heap for tree 8KB, variable time 2.5× without const-time.

QROM proof [KLS18]:

$$
Adv^{UF-CMA}_{FS^{ab}} \le \frac{8 q_H^2 (q_S+1)}{2^{2\lambda}} + 2 Adv_{SIS}
$$

Loss $O(q_H^2)$ — non-tight. Recent Katsumata et al. 2023 tightens via lossy ID.

Rejection analysis [2][HAL 03911595]:

Source distribution $Q_{y}=U([- \gamma_1+1,\gamma_1-1])$, target $P_z = U$ independent: acceptance ratio $M = |\text{supp }Q|/|\text{supp }P| \cdot \max_s \frac{Q_s}{P}$. For uniform intervals, $M = 1/(1-\tau\|s\|_\infty/\gamma_1)^{\ell n}\approx 4.25$.

Practical forgery from $s_1$ leakage alone [James Howe 2018]: knowledge of Dilithium $s_1$ permits forging via solving $c s_2$ small search — highlights secrecy of $s_1$ alone critical.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **SelfTargetMSIS non-standard:** Not reducible from plain MSIS without random oracle heuristic. Finding counterexample would not break MSIS but break Dilithium instantiations.
2. **Falcon floating point:** No constant-time guarantee on all FPUs (subnormal timing). Fully integer Zalcon loses 1.2× size.
3. **Variable signature length** in Falcon leaks via compression side-channel if not padded — requires length-shuffle.
4. **QROM tightness:** Both proofs lose $q_H$. Tight QROM from lossy identification remains open for NTRU lattices.
5. **Forward security:** PQC sigs large for blockchain use; neither supports efficient forward-secure key update without short basis delegation (Chen et al. ePrint 2023) — extra short basis increases $s_1$ size by $k\times$.
6. **Hybrid deployment:** Transition ECDSA→ML-DSA needs dual-sign: size inflation pushes TLS ClientHello beyond MTU. KEMTLS+ML-DSA composite drafts still debating.
7. **Quantum cryptanalysis:** Best lattice sieving $2^{0.292\beta}$ assumes QRAM; without QRAM, NIST Level 2 may be 10 bits higher — conservative estimates debate.

## 7. Conclusion

Dilithium and Falcon exemplify two viable philosophies in the same lattice geometry. Dilithium opts for mathematical austerity: uniform sampling, NTT everywhere, easy masking, moderate bloat accepted. Falcon opts for geometric optimality: shortest vectors via Gaussian, FFT recursion, extreme compactness at cost of fragile implementation.

NIST's dual choice — Dilithium as primary (performance, simplicity) and Falcon as secondary (size-critical, embedded) — hedges: if uniform rejection leaks less than floats, ML-DSA remains; if bandwidth dominates IoT, FN-DSA wins. Both depend on MODULE-LWE/NTRU hardness surviving a decade of quantum cryptanalysis, a plausible but unproven bet.

Future work: verified constant-time Falcon integer sampler with formal Coq proof of LDL tree correctness; tight QROM proof for ML-DSA without $q_H^2$ loss via adaptive reprogramming; threshold Dilithium with <5 rounds for custody wallets; and lattice + hash hybrid combiners preserving EUF-CMA if one breaks.

> *When aborts stop leaking, and Fourier samplers stop floating, the lattice finally signs.*
