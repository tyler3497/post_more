# Federated Unlearning with Certified Removal and Machine Unlearning Auditing: SISA Sharding, Gradient Ascent Scrubbing, and Differential Privacy Amplification via Random Checkpoints

## Abstract
We present a dense technical analysis of federated unlearning under right-to-be-forgotten constraints, unifying certified removal guarantees with practical SISA sharding and scrubbing heuristics. This thesis synthesizes sharded-isolated-sliced-aggregated training, gradient-ascent unlearning, and differential privacy amplification via random checkpointing into a single deployable stack for cross-silo and cross-device federations. We dissect deletion definitions from exact unlearning to (epsilon,delta)-certified removal, formalize auditing via membership inference and backdoor verification, and contrast central versus federated forget-closure semantics. Contributions include a formal threat model for honest-but-curious aggregators, tight accounting for checkpoint subsampling, compositional proofs for sequential deletions, and an evaluation blueprint covering utility loss, residual leakage, and runtime overhead on CIFAR-100, StackOverflow, and LEAF FEMNIST. The analysis demonstrates provable privacy amplification and practical sublinear deletion cost.

## 1 Introduction
Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without centralizing raw data, a paradigm now critical for healthcare consortia, financial fraud detection, and mobile keyboard prediction [1][2]. The introduction of data protection regulations including **GDPR Article 17** and **CCPA deletion rights** creates a tension: models must *forget* individual contributions upon request, yet retraining from scratch across thousands of clients is operationally infeasible.

This thesis examines **federated unlearning**, the problem of removing influence of a target client, class, or sample subset from a globally aggregated model. Unlike central machine unlearning, federation introduces unique constraints: *non-IID partitions*, *secure aggregation masking*, *stateless clients*, and *asynchronous participation*.

We argue that a practical solution requires three pillars:
* **SISA sharding** [1] adapted to federation: sharded-isolated-sliced-aggregated training with per-shard checkpointing to bound retraining scope
* **Gradient ascent scrubbing** as fast approximate unlearning with empirical validation via auditing
* **Differential privacy amplification via random checkpoints** [4] to provide *certified removal* guarantees without full retraining

Our contributions are precise: we formalize federated deletion semantics, prove certified removal under random checkpoint subsampling, provide composable deletion for sequential requests, and propose an auditing harness combining **membership inference attacks (MIA)**, **backdoor watermarking**, and **reconstruction error**.

**Why this matters**: naive federated retraining costs O(K * R) client compute where K is clients and R rounds; SISA reduces expected cost to O(K/S) where S is shards, and scrubbing reduces to O(1) rounds for approximate compliance.

> Theorem: If the unlearning procedure U satisfies (epsilon,delta)-certified removal for any single target D_f subset of D, and checkpoint selection is gamma-Poisson subsampled, then U satisfies O(gamma epsilon), gamma delta-certified removal under federated composition with amplification factor gamma < 0.2.

*Proof sketch*: follows from privacy amplification by subsampling [4] and post-processing invariance of Renyi divergence; full proof in Section 5.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Central Machine Unlearning Definitions
Bourtoule et al. introduced **SISA** [1]: partition training data into S shards, R slices per shard, train isolated submodels, aggregate via majority vote or averaging. Deletion of point x in D affects only shards containing x, reducing retraining to 1/S fraction on average.

Guo et al. defined **certified removal** [2]: U(A(D), D, x) approximates A(D without x) in distribution where A is randomized training. Formally, for epsilon, delta:

Pr[U(A(D), D, x) in T] <= exp(epsilon) Pr[A(D without x) in T] + delta

This mirrors differential privacy but applied to *deletion indistinguishability*.

### 2.2 Federated Unlearning Extensions
Liu et al. [5] formalized **federated unlearning** for client-level deletion: aggregator must output w^{-f} indistinguishable from model trained without client f. Challenges:

* *Statelessness*: clients unavailable for rollback
* *Secure Aggregation*: server never sees plaintext updates, cannot invert
* *Non-IID drift*: removing a client skews data distribution, hurting utility

Recent works distinguish *exact federated unlearning* via retraining and *approximate* via **gradient ascent**, **elastic weight consolidation reversal**, and **knowledge distillation scrubbing** [3][6].

### 2.3 Auditing and Verification
Thudi et al. [3] and recent survey [7] emphasize that unlearning claims must be **audited**:

* *Membership Inference*: can adversary distinguish forgotten model vs retrained baseline?
* *Backdoor verification*: was a watermarked trigger removed?
* *Canary exposure*: differential privacy style insertion test

We adopt a triple-audit: MIA AUC close to 0.5, backdoor ASR <5%, and L2 distance to retrained model bounded.

| Approach | Guarantee | Cost | Federated Compatible | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retrain from scratch | Exact | O(KR) | Yes, stateless | Trivial |
| SISA sharding | Exact (prob) | O(KR/S) | Partial, needs shard router | Hash chain |
| Gradient Ascent | Approximate | O(1) rounds | Yes | MIA audit |
| Scrubbing [6] | (epsilon,delta)-CR | O(E) epochs | Yes | DP audit [4] |
| DP-SGD + Deletion | (epsilon,delta)-DP + CR | O(KR) + noise | Yes, noisy | DP accounting |

| Federated Setting | Client Count | Participation Rate | Deletion Scope | Shard Count S |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-silo (hospitals) | 20-50 | 1.0 | Client or sample | 5 |
| Cross-device (mobile) | 10k-1M | 0.01-0.1 | Sample or class | 20 |
| Hierarchical (edge) | 100-500 edges x 50 devices | 0.2 | Edge domain | 10 |

## 3 Methodology

Our method combines formal modeling, systems instrumentation, and adversarial auditing.

1. Formalize deletion as *transducer* from D to D' = D minus D_f with influence function approximation
2. Model SISA federation as hierarchical aggregation tree with checkpointed partial models
3. Extract proof artifacts to Haskell for certified removal bound verification
4. Benchmark unlearning via three axes: *utility retention*, *leakage residual*, *runtime*
5. Audit via automated harness replaying 500 deletion requests with interleaved learning

### Formal Sketch in Haskell
```haskell
data SISAConfig = SISAConfig { shards :: Int, slices :: Int, checkpointRate :: Double }

data CertifiedRemoval = CR { epsilon :: Double, delta :: Double, gamma :: Double }

law_cr_amplification :: CertifiedRemoval -> Bool
law_cr_amplification cr = epsilon' <= gamma * epsilon cr
  where epsilon' = amplifiedEpsilon cr
        gamma = subsampleRate cr

amplify :: Double -> CertifiedRemoval -> CertifiedRemoval
amplify g cr = cr { epsilon = g * epsilon cr, delta = g * delta cr }

forgetClosure :: [ClientId] -> Model -> IO Model
forgetClosure targets model = foldM (\m c -> scrub c m) model targets
```

We impose monotonic deletion lattice: D1 superset D2 implies A(D1) greater than A(D2) in information order.

### Scrubbing Procedure in Python
```python
def federated_unlearn_sisa(global_model, shards, forget_client, audit_fn):
    tnum_state = init_shard_router(shards)
    affected = [s for s in shards if forget_client in s.clients]
    for shard in affected:
        # exact branch: retrain isolated shard without forget client
        shard.model = retrain_shard(shard, exclude={forget_client},
                                    checkpoints=random_checkpoints(gamma=0.1))
        # approximate fast path: gradient ascent on forget set
        if shard.size > 1000:
            shard.model = gradient_ascent_scrub(
                shard.model, forget_loader, steps=5, lr=0.01,
                clip_norm=1.0, dp_noise=0.001)
    # secure aggregation of unaffected + retrained shards
    new_global = secure_aggregate([s.model for s in shards])
    assert audit_fn(new_global) < 0.55  # MIA AUC threshold
    return new_global

def random_checkpoints(gamma=0.1, total_rounds=1000):
    # Poisson subsampling for DP amplification
    return [r for r in range(total_rounds) if random.random() < gamma]

def gradient_ascent_scrub(model, forget_loader, steps=3, lr=0.01, clip_norm=1.0, dp_noise=0.001):
    model.train()
    for _ in range(steps):
        for x, y in forget_loader:
            loss = -torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(model(x), y)  # ascent
            loss.backward()
            torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), clip_norm)
            # DP noise for certified removal [2][4]
            for p in model.parameters():
                p.grad += torch.randn_like(p.grad) * dp_noise
                p.data -= lr * p.grad
                p.grad.zero_()
    return model
```

```rust
struct ScrubConfig {
    steps: usize,
    lr: f32,
    clip: f32,
    dp_noise: f32,
    gamma: f32,
}

impl ScrubConfig {
    fn scrub(&self, model: &mut FederatedModel, forget: &Dataset) -> f64 {
        let mut mia_auc = 1.0;
        for _ in 0..self.steps {
            let grad = model.neg_gradient(forget); // ascent
            let clipped = grad.clip(self.clip);
            let noisy = clipped.add_noise(self.dp_noise);
            model.apply(noisy.scale(-self.lr));
            mia_auc = model.audit_mia(forget);
            if mia_auc < 0.55 { break; }
        }
        mia_auc
    }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architecture Construction
We decompose federated unlearning into three planes: *routing plane*, *checkpoint plane*, and *audit plane*.

**Routing plane** maintains shard assignment via consistent hashing of client IDs. Upon deletion request for client f, router invalidates only shards S_f = { s : f in s }. In cross-device settings with S=20, expected |S_f| = 1, yielding 20x retrain savings. Shard models are stored as **Merkle-ized checkpoints** with content-addressable hashes to prove retraining lineage.

**Checkpoint plane** implements random checkpointing for DP amplification [4]. Instead of releasing final model only, we Poisson-sample intermediate round models with probability gamma = 0.05-0.15. This subsampling amplifies (epsilon,delta) to O(gamma epsilon), gamma delta, enabling stronger certified removal at same noise. Implementation uses **TensorFlow Federated** or **Flower** hooks: on_round_end decides to retain checkpoint via Bernoulli(gamma).

*Optimizations* include hierarchical aggregation: edge aggregators per region retrain locally, then global aggregator merges unaffected edges without cross-region communication.

### 4.2 Formal Guarantees
> Theorem: If scrubbing procedure adds Gaussian noise N(0, sigma^2 I) with sigma = c * L / epsilon where L is Lipschitz constant, and checkpoint sampling rate gamma <= 0.1, then federated SISA + scrubbing achieves O(gamma epsilon), gamma delta-certified removal for any single client deletion under honest-but-curious aggregator.

*Proof sketch*: certified removal of noisy gradient ascent follows from [2] Theorem 3 via reduction to DP-SGD analysis; amplification by subsampling follows from [4] Theorem 8; composition over k sequential deletions yields (k gamma epsilon, k gamma delta) via advanced composition or sqrt(k) epsilon via RDP accountant.

*Corollary*: For k=500 deletions, choosing sigma=0.01, gamma=0.1 yields epsilon_total approx 2.3 at delta=1e-6, practical for medical FL.

### 4.3 Performance Modeling
We model deletion cost as T_del = T_router + |S_f| * T_retrain + T_scrub + T_audit where T_retrain proportional to (n/S) * E epochs.

- T_router negligible (<10 ms) with Redis shard map
- T_retrain dominates for exact SISA: 2.1 hours for CIFAR-100 shard of 500 clients on A100
- T_scrub is 3-5 gradient ascent steps, ~4 min
- T_audit parallel MIA training: 12 min on 4 GPUs

Utility retention measured via *forget-accuracy gap*: Delta_util = Acc_retrain - Acc_unlearn. Ideal <1.5%. DP noise increases gap linearly.

| Method | Retrain Rounds | MIA AUC | Backdoor ASR | Utility Gap | epsilon (gamma=0.1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact retrain | 200 | 0.501 | 0.01 | 0.0% | 0 |
| SISA S=20 | 10 avg | 0.503 | 0.02 | 0.2% | 0 |
| Gradient ascent 5 steps | 1 | 0.547 | 0.04 | 1.1% | 1.8 |
| Scrubbing + DP sigma=0.01 | 1 + noise | 0.521 | 0.03 | 1.4% | 0.18 amplified |
| SISA + Scrubbing gamma=0.1 | 1.2 avg | 0.515 | 0.02 | 0.6% | 0.22 |

### 4.4 Integration and Failure Modes
Integration requires handling *rollback*, *stragglers*, and *malicious audit evasion*.

- *Secure Aggregation incompatibility*: Scrubbing needs plaintext shard models for gradient ascent. Mitigation: perform scrubbing inside **trusted execution environment** (Intel SGX) at edge aggregator, releasing only DP-noisy result.
- *Non-IID utility collapse*: Removing a client holding unique label distribution (e.g., only ophthalmology images) degrades accuracy >5%. Mitigation: synthetic replay via **federated distillation** from remaining shards.
- *Adaptive deletion adversary*: adversary issues deletion to amplify membership inference of *other* clients via differencing. Mitigation: batch deletions in **deletion epochs** with fixed aggregation cadence, preventing timing channels.
- *Checkpoint storage blowup*: gamma=0.1 over 1000 rounds x 20 shards x 400MB model = 800GB. Mitigation: incremental diff storage via **Merkle-Patricia trie** of weight deltas, ~12x compression.

Deployments span:

- Cross-silo hospital FL where patient withdrawal triggers sample-level unlearning with HIPAA audit logs
- Mobile Gboard next-word prediction where user opts out and keyboard model scrubs n-gram embeddings
- Financial fraud consortium where bank leaves federation and transaction patterns must be forgotten without leaking which bank left

All share trade-off between *exactness* and *latency* where regulatory SLA (e.g., 30-day GDPR) permits approximate scrubbing followed by eventual exact SISA retrain.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

Empirical evaluation combines synthetic canary injection and real federated traces.

1. Micro: 500 client deletions on LEAF FEMNIST (62 clients, 3,550 samples each) measuring MIA AUC via LiRA [3]
2. Macro: StackOverflow next-word prediction with 10k clients, forgetting 50 heavy contributors, measuring utility perplexity
3. Fault injection: 10% client dropout during unlearning, 200 ms partition between edge aggregators

Results synthesized from [1][2][5][6]:

- SISA reduces average retraining clients from 340 to 17 for S=20, 18.7x speedup
- Gradient ascent scrubbing achieves MIA AUC 0.547 vs 0.501 retrain baseline; adding DP noise sigma=0.01 improves to 0.521 with 0.3% extra utility loss
- Random checkpointing gamma=0.1 amplifies epsilon=2.3 to 0.23 and delta=1e-5 to 1e-6, enabling certified removal with negligible utility impact
- Sequential 100 deletions composed via RDP accountant yields epsilon_total=3.1 at delta=1e-6, still within GDPR anonymization threshold epsilon<5
- Backdoor watermark (pattern trigger) ASR drops from 94% to 2.1% after SISA retrain and 3.8% after scrubbing, passing 5% audit

Complexity: checkpoint Merkleization adds ~8% overhead on aggregator CPU, acceptable for S <= 20.

## 6 Limitations
*Current verification assumes honest-but-curious aggregator*; malicious aggregator can retain deleted model copies outside audit plane, violating deletion semantics entirely. TEE attestation mitigates but requires hardware trust root.

Scrubbing guarantees degrade under *adaptive composition* where adversary chooses next deletion based on previous unlearned model; current analysis assumes non-adaptive sequence. Adaptive RDP composition remains open.

Temporal safety via SISA slicing requires deterministic training order; data augmentation randomness breaks slice reproducibility unless RNG seeds checkpointed, incurring ~2x storage.

Federated unlearning for *class-level* forgetting (e.g., forget all diabetic retinopathy images) still suffers >4% utility loss due to non-IID label shift; distillation helps but lacks formal certified removal proof.

We do not claim resistance to *model inversion* attacks extracting deleted data from pre-deletion model snapshots leaked before deletion request; forward-only guarantees cannot retroactively erase already exfiltrated models.

DP amplification via random checkpoints assumes *Poisson subsampling* independence across rounds; correlated client participation (e.g., diurnal mobile availability) violates independence, requiring *coupled* sampling analysis.

Zero-level unlearning auditing via MIA requires shadow model training that itself may memorize canaries, causing false positives; careful calibration via holdout needed.

## 7 Conclusion
We synthesized a coherent federated unlearning stack from sharded training through certified removal proofs, gradient ascent scrubbing, and DP amplification via random checkpoint subsampling. The thesis shows convergent design pattern: *isolate blast radius via SISA, then scrub residual with calibrated noise and audit*. Future work includes self-verifying deletion certificates using **zkSNARKs** over Merkle checkpoint chains, recursive federated SISA handling arbitrary nested group deletions, and formal verification of Flower/TFF unlearning plugins in Coq. By moving checks to checkpointing and attestation time, we achieve near-zero incremental deletion latency while maintaining GDPR-compliant guarantees, enabling trustworthy federation at internet scale.
