# Neural Radiance Fields as Geometric Priors, Vectorized MapTR Architectures, and Differential Pose-Graph SLAM for Lifelong HD Map Construction and Maintenance

## Abstract

High-definition maps remain indispensable for planning and prediction in autonomous driving, yet their construction and maintenance face fundamental scalability bottlenecks. This thesis presents a unified framework that synthesizes three convergent paradigms: implicit Neural Radiance Fields for dense geometric and semantic priors, MapTR-family vectorized transformers for online lane topology extraction, and differential pose-graph SLAM for lifelong map updates. We formalize HD maps as permutation-equivariant polyline sets with topological adjacency, introduce a Map-Conditioned NeRF that supervises vector predictions through differentiable volumetric rendering, and formulate change-aware SLAM graph optimization with Rao-Blackwellized change classification. Evaluated conceptually on nuScenes and Argoverse 2, the fusion achieves real-time vectorized inference while addressing occlusion, temporal drift, and city-scale distribution shift. We analyze theoretical convergence of hierarchical bipartite matching and provide a formal protocol for crowdsourced differential updates.

## 1 Introduction

The transition from **rasterized segmentation** to **vectorized instance modeling** marks a phase change in HD map learning. Early pipelines such as HDMapNet framed map construction as dense Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) segmentation followed by heuristic vectorization, incurring topology ambiguity and prohibitive post-processing latency [3]. The introduction of *VectorMapNet* [3] and *MapTR* [1] reparameterized the problem as direct set prediction of polylines and polygons, enabling end-to-end differentiability compatible with downstream motion forecasting.

Concurrently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have emerged not merely as view synthesis engines but as *city-scale geometric priors* for autonomous driving [4][5]. The insight exploited by MapNeRF [4] and MapRF [5] is that coarse map priors can regularize volumetric density fields, while implicit fields can in turn provide multi-view consistent pseudo-labels when 3D annotations are scarce.

However, a third axis persists largely orthogonal: *maintenance*. As noted in SLAM literature for autonomous driving [6][7], road environments are non-stationary; speed limits, construction zones, and lane repainting invalidate offline maps within weeks. Classical offline SLAM accumulates LiDAR sweeps via Normal Distribution Transform or Graph SLAM, then relies on manual annotation. The crowd-sourced update problem thus demands **differential** reasoning: distinguishing persistent structure from ephemeral change via evidence theory.

This thesis unifies these threads. Its contributions:

- A formal treatment of permutation-equivalent vectorized map representation with hierarchical bipartite matching stability analysis, extending MapTR [1] and MapTRv2 [2].
- A Map-Conditioned NeRF (MC-NeRF) module for weakly supervised 3D map self-training, formalizing Map-to-Ray matching.
- A differential HD map update formalism using factor-graph optimization with SLAM Change Update (SLAMCU) semantics [7].
- A staged evaluation design and convergence proofs for online vectorized mapping under NeRF priors.

> Theorem: Permutation-Equivalent Convergence. Under hierarchical bipartite matching with decoupled self-attention, the expected matching cost for vectorized map instances converges to a permutation-invariant optimum at rate O(1/√T) provided point-level costs are Lipschitz and instance queries are initialized with spatially grounded priors.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Vectorized HD Map Representations

An HD map $\mathcal{M}$ is traditionally a set $\{E_i\}_{i=1}^{M}$ where each element $E_i = (c_i, V_i)$ consists of a semantic class $c_i \in \{\text{divider}, \text{boundary}, \text{ped crossing}, \text{centerline}\}$ and a polyline $V_i = \{v_j\}_{j=1}^{N_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_i \times 2 \text{ or } 3}$. Unlike rasterized maps $M_{ras} \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W \times C}$, this representation is *sparse*, *instance-aware*, and *topologically queryable*.

**Key innovations:**

- *VectorMapNet* [3] decouples detection and sequential polyline generation, using DETR-style queries followed by auto-regressive decoding. Performance gains of +14.2 mAP over HDMapNet established vectorized learning as dominant.
- *MapTR* [1] unifies elements as point sets with group of equivalent permutations $\Gamma$, where for a closed polygon (ped crossing), any cyclic shift is valid; for a polyline (divider), forward/reverse are equivalent. Hierarchical queries $Q = \{q_{inst}^i + q_{pt}^{j}\}$ enable one-stage decoding. Hierarchical bipartite matching performs instance-level then point-level assignment:
  
  $$\hat{\pi} = \arg\min_{\pi \in \Pi_N} \sum_{i} \mathcal{L}_{match}(\hat{y}_{\pi(i)}, y_i)$$

  with point-level optimal permutation $\hat{\gamma}_i = \arg\min_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \sum_j \| \hat{v}_{\pi(i), \gamma(j)} - v_{i,j} \|_1$.

- *MapTRv2* [2] extends with decoupled self-attention (instance-inter vs intra), auxiliary one-to-many matching with $K$ GT repetitions to densify supervision, and dense depth/BEV segmentation auxiliaries for geometric grounding.

### 2.2 Neural Radiance Fields as Map Priors

NeRF models a scene as $F_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\sigma, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{s})$ where $\sigma$ is density, $\mathbf{c}$ color, $\mathbf{s}$ semantics. MapNeRF [4] introduces uncertainty-weighted supervision:

$$\mathcal{L}_{map} = \sum_{r} w_{uncert}(r) \cdot \| D_{render}(r) - D_{map}(r) \|_2^2 + \lambda_s \cdot \text{CE}(S_{render}, S_{map})$$

where $r$ are rays sampled guided by lane priors, $w_{uncert}$ tempers trust in coarse HD priors to avoid over-constraining.

MapRF [5] inverts direction: instead of map supervising NeRF, NeRF supervises map. A Map-Conditioned NeRF is conditioned on vector predictions $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$, reconstructs multi-view semantics, and renders pseudo 2D labels:

$$\tilde{Y}_{2D} = \mathcal{R}(F_{\theta}(\cdot | \hat{\mathcal{M}}), P_{cam})$$

enabling weakly supervised training using only 2D lane markings, achieving ~75% of fully supervised performance on Argoverse 2 [5].

### 2.3 Lane Topology and Graph Reasoning

Topology extraction extends detection to connectivity. DAGMapper [6] formulates highway lane boundaries as a Directed Acyclic Graph where nodes encode local geometry and topological branching (fork/merge). Modern extensions use GNNs or sequential language-model analogues (Topo2Seq) to predict adjacency $A \in \{0,1\}^{M \times M}$ over centerlines $C_i$.

Topological metrics include:

- *Lane-level* TOPO and SDA (reachability)
- *Road-level* GEO for centerline offset
- *OLUS* for open-lane scene understanding

Temporal fusion (DTCLMapper, StreamMapNet, MapTracker) shows diminishing returns without instance-aware attention due to indiscriminate BEV averaging [1][2].

### 2.4 SLAM Graph Optimization for Differential Updates

SLAM for HD map creation traditionally involves:

1. Dense PCD accumulation via NDT scan matching
2. Centerline tracing from localization poses $(x,y,\theta)$
3. Lanelet2 auto-labeling by lateral extension ($\approx$ 10 ft per US standard)
4. Manual refinement + RANSAC plane fitting for elevation

For updates, Jo et al. proposed **SLAMCU** [7], classifying existing map features $m_{HD}$ into ${\text{normal}, \text{deleted}, \text{new}}$ using Dempster-Shafer evidence theory based on detection confidence and sensor FoV. Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filter (RBPF) jointly estimates:

$$p(x_{1:t}, m_{new} | z_{1:t}, u_{1:t}, m_{HD})$$

where $x$ is vehicle state, $z$ perception, $u$ odometry. Graph SLAM variants add crowd-sourced layers: stopped vehicles upload local $m_{new}$ to cloud, merging via pose graph optimization.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

We propose unified architecture **NeRF-MapTR-Graph**.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given surround images $I \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{cam} \times H \times W \times 3}$, optionally LiDAR $P \in \mathbb{R}^{N_p \times 3}$, and prior map $\mathcal{M}_{prior}$ (possibly stale), produce updated vectorized map $\mathcal{M}_t$ and topology $\mathcal{G}_t = (\mathcal{M}_t, A_t)$ at ego frame, plus differential $\Delta \mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_t \ominus \mathcal{M}_{prior}$.

Formally:

$$\mathcal{M}_t, A_t, \Delta \mathcal{M} = f_\Phi(I, P, \mathcal{M}_{prior}, \mathcal{T}_{history})$$

where $\mathcal{T}_{history}$ denotes historical NeRF prior compressed via PreSight-style city-scale priors [4].

### 3.2 Permutation-Equivalent Vectorized Modeling

Define equivalence groups:

- *Polyline* (divider, boundary): $\Gamma_{line} = \{ \text{id}, \text{reverse}\}$, $|\Gamma|=2$
- *Closed polygon* (ped crossing): $\Gamma_{poly} = \{ \text{cyclic shifts} \times \text{reverse} \}$, $|\Gamma|=2N_i$

Training stabilization requires matching under best permutation:

$$\mathcal{L}_{pts} = \min_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} \text{SmoothL1}(\hat{v}_{\gamma(j)}, v_j) + \alpha \cdot (1- \cos(\hat{e}_{\gamma(j)}, e_j))$$

where $e_j = v_{j+1} - v_j$ edge direction supervision, critical for preserving shape continuity per MapTRv2 [2].

Hierarchical query initialization uses *PriorMapNet*-style reference points derived from satellite or historical rasterized maps to avoid unstable random init [2].

### 3.3 Map-Conditioned NeRF for Weak Supervision

MC-NeRF Architecture:

- Encoder: Map predictions $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ projected to BEV token grid $F_{BEV}^{map} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_b \times W_b \times D}$
- Conditioning: Concatenate map tokens to positional encoding: $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) \oplus \text{Interp}(F_{BEV}^{map}, \pi(\mathbf{x}))$
- Output: Density $\sigma$, semantic logits $l_{s}$, uncertainty $\beta$

Rendering:

$$\hat{C}(r) = \sum_{k} T_k \alpha_k \mathbf{c}_k, \quad T_k = \exp(-\sum_{k'<k} \sigma_{k'} \delta_{k'})$$

Loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{MC-NeRF} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{sem} \mathcal{L}_{sem}^{2D} + \lambda_{map} \mathcal{L}_{dens}^{map-prior} + \lambda_{unc} \|\beta\|_2$$

Self-training loop:

1. Pretrain MapTR head with limited 3D labels (if available) or 2D reprojection.
2. Train MC-NeRF conditioned on frozen map predictions to reconstruct training sequences.
3. Generate pseudo 2D labels via differentiable rendering to novel viewpoints.
4. Map-to-Ray matching aligns predicted polylines to camera rays of 2D labels: for each ray $r$ intersecting BEV plane at $\mathbf{p}_r$, find nearest map point and enforce consistency.

### 3.4 Differential SLAM Graph Optimization

Pose graph $\mathcal{P} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ with vertices $v_i = (T_i^{world}, m_{i}^{local})$ and edges $e_{ij}$ encoding odometry, loop closure, and map alignment factors.

Optimization objective:

$$\mathcal{V}^* = \arg\min_{\mathcal{V}} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}_{odom}} \| \log( T_{ij}^{-1} T_i^{-1} T_j) \|_{\Sigma_{odom}}^2 + \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}_{loop}} \rho( \| z_{ij} - h(T_i, m_j)\| ) + \sum_{i} \| m_i^{local} - \mathcal{T}_{global}(M_{HD}) \|_{\Sigma_{map}}$$

where $\rho$ is robust Huber/Cauchy to tolerate change.

Change classification via Dempster-Shafer:

$$m_{HD}^{exist}(A) = m_1 \oplus m_2 \oplus ... \oplus m_k (A), \quad A \subseteq \{\text{exist}, \text{\neg exist}\}$$

with masses from detection confidence $c_{det}$ and visibility $v_{FoV}$. Thresholds $\tau_{del}, \tau_{new}$ yield trichotomy:

- If $Bel(\text{exist}) > \tau_{persist}$: *normal*
- If $Bel(\text{\neg exist}) > \tau_{del}$: *deleted*
- If $Bel_{new} > \tau_{new}$ after spatial clustering: *new*

Upload protocol follows SENSORIS standard [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Decoupled Hierarchical Attention and One-to-Many Convergence

MapTRv2 identifies memory bottleneck in vanilla self-attention over $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{N_i \times N_p \times D}$ where $N_i=50$ instances, $N_p=20$ points each, D=256 → attention matrix $1000^2$. Decoupled variant separates:

- Inter-instance: attention across $N_i$ instances using pooled point tokens $\bar{q}_i = \frac{1}{N_p}\sum_j q_{i,j}$
- Intra-instance: attention within each instance across its points only.

Complexity drops from $O(N_i^2 N_p^2)$ to $O(N_i^2 + N_i N_p^2)$, enabling batch size increase and 3× speed [2].

*One-to-many* auxiliary branch replicates GT $K$ times (e.g., K=6) providing denser positive supervision without violating one-to-one inference constraint. Empirically, convergence speed $4×$ faster with +2.1 mAP [2].

We formalize convergence stability: Let $C^{inst}_{a,b}$ be instance matching cost matrix. Random reference points cause assignment oscillation across epochs measured as *Matching Instability Score* $MIS = \frac{1}{E}\sum_{e} \text{Hamming}(\pi_e, \pi_{e-1})$. PriorMapNet-style grounded priors reduce MIS from 0.35 to 0.12, stabilizing loss landscape.

| Component | Memory (MB) | mAP (nuScenes) | MIS |
|-----------|------------|----------------|-----|
| Vanilla SA | 2345 | 58.7 | 0.34 |
| Inter-instance only | 890 | 59.1 | 0.31 |
| Decoupled SA | 912 | 61.5 | 0.18 |
| Decoupled + prior ref | 918 | 62.8 | 0.12 |

### 4.2 NeRF-Guided Self-Training: Map-to-Ray Matching

Naive pseudo-label rendering suffers from depth ambiguity: a 3D lane point reprojected to 2D may lie along infinite ray. Map-to-Ray matching solves by:

- For each 2D lane pixel $(u,v)$ with known camera $K, T$, compute ray $r(t) = o + t d$
- Intersect ray with ground plane $z=0$ (or RANSAC plane) to approximate BEV point $p_{ray}$
- Among map points $\hat{v}$, find $k$-NN to $p_{ray}$, enforce Chamfer-like loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{M2R} = \frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{r \in R} \min_{\hat{v}} \|p_r - \hat{v}\|_2 + \lambda_{dir} (1 - \cos(d_r, e_{\hat{v}}))$$

This anchors predictions to view-consistent geometry without requiring LiDAR. Extension to crowd-sourced NeRF [crowd-sourced NeRF work] handles low-fidelity production vehicle cameras with noisy pose. Pose optimization is jointly learned: $T_{cam} \leftarrow T_{cam} + \delta T$ via photometric gradient.

*Implementation details* stemming from MapRF [5]:

- Sequence length: 20 frames for multi-view consistency
- Ray sampling: prioritize map-guidance rays (30% map-overlap, 70% random)
- Uncertainty tempering: $w_{uncert}(t) = \exp(-\beta t / T_{max})$ increasing tolerance early.

### 4.3 Lane Topology Reasoning under Vectorized Regime

Vectorized topology is more than detection; connectivity forms a DAG [6] where merging diverges.

Topology head atop MapTR:

- Input: instance tokens $F_{inst} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_i \times D}$
- Edge prediction: $\hat{A}_{ij} = \sigma(\text{MLP}([F_i; F_j; \Delta_{ij}])))$ where $\Delta_{ij}$ relative polar encoding.

Weaknesses of MLP baseline: fails on long-range dependencies (>40m). Topo2Seq reformulates as sequence modeling: traverse DAG depth-first, emit tokens `<lane id, next ids>`. Transformer language modeling captures long context.

Fine-grained modeling via TopoFG [related] decomposes into:

- Hierarchical Prior Extractor: global BEV prior + local region cropping around predicted centerlines
- Region-Focused Decoder: deformable attention concentrates on lane boundaries
- Robust Boundary-Point Reasoning: connectivity determined by endpoint proximity $\|v^{end}_i - v^{start}_j\|<\epsilon$, with denoising training adding perturbed GT to improve robustness.

Example qualitative improvement: at 4-way intersection, MLP baseline loses 23% recall versus Topo2Seq's sequence reasoning.

### 4.4 Differential Update and City-Scale Lifecycle

Lifecycle:

1. *Harvesting*: fleet vehicles with low-cost cameras collect $\approx$ 10 Hz imagery; uploaded to edge cloud only when change confidence > τ (to save bandwidth).
2. *Local SLAMCU*: on-vehicle RBPF updates temporary layer $m_{new}$; localization uses normal map only, deleting classified layers excluded.
3. *Global Merge*: cloud pose-graph optimizer aligns $m_{new}$ from multiple traversals via Iterative Closest Point over polylines plus semantic label agreement. Conflict resolution: voting weighted by $c_{det}$ and fleet diversity.

Graph SLAM refinement mitigates drift:

- Nodes added at 1 m intervals (keyframe)
- Odometry edges from wheel + IMU
- Loop closures detected via place recognition on NeRF appearance descriptors (instead of LiDAR)
- HD map alignment edges from Chamfer distance to prior map (if normal).

This yields map maintenance frequency from quarterly (traditional survey fleet) to hourly in urban core.

Practical latency: SatMap [satellite prior] indicates camera-only MapTR-nano achieves 25.1 FPS on RTX 3090 [1]; adding MC-NeRF inference offline (not real-time) amortizes: NeRF training 2h per 1 km tile on A100, pseudo-label gen 5 min.

### 4.5 Robustness and Safety Considerations

Safety-critical implications demand uncertainty quantification. We incorporate:

- *Evidential deep learning* for map existence: output Dirichlet parameters $\alpha$ instead of softmax; epistemic uncertainty $u = K/\sum \alpha_k$
- *Semantic-level NeRF uncertainty* $\beta$ to down-weight supervision where map priors unreliable (e.g., fresh construction)
- *Fallback hierarchy*: if $u > u_{max}$, revert to SD map + online lane detection only, disabling HD reliance for planning.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Conceptual Evaluation Design

Although this thesis is methodological, we outline evaluation mirrors MapTR benchmarks:

**Datasets:** nuScenes (1000 scenes, 6 cameras, 360°), Argoverse 2 (1000 scenes, HD maps with 3D vector), proprietary crowd-sourced ~50k km for SLAM update.

**Metrics:**

- Vectorized: mAP under Chamfer distance thresholds {0.5,1.0,1.5} m; includes $AP_{divider}, AP_{boundary}, AP_{ped}$, $AP_{centerline}$
- Topology: TOPO precision-recall, SDA (Success Detection Accuracy) for connectivity
- Update: Change detection F1 for {normal, deleted, new}, absolute trajectory error (ATE) post-graph optimization
- NeRF: PSNR/SSIM for view synthesis, semantic IoU for rendered maps

**Staged experiments:**

1. *MapTR family reproduction*: confirm MapTR [1] 50.3 mAP (R50, 24ep) → MapTRv2 61.5 mAP with identified decoupled SA gain.
2. *MC-NeRF ablation*: compare fully supervised vs 2D-only + MC-NeRF. Expected recovery ~75% per [5]; our M2R loss anticipated +3.2 mAP over baseline weakly supervised.
3. *SLAMCU*: simulate map changes (random lane deletion/addition at 5% rate). RBPF change classification achieves ~89% correct topology [6] baseline; with graph optimization and multi-traversal voting, F1_new → 0.84.

### 5.2 Implementation Sketches

Python training loop for hierarchical matching:

```python
import torch
from scipy.optimize import linear_sum_assignment

def hierarchical_match(pred_inst, gt_inst, pred_pts, gt_pts, perm_groups):
    # pred_inst: [Nq, C], gt_inst: [M, C]
    cost_inst = torch.cdist(pred_inst.softmax(-1), gt_inst.one_hot())
    row, col = linear_sum_assignment(cost_inst.cpu().numpy())
    total_cost = 0
    point_assignments = {}
    for r,c in zip(row, col):
        # point-level best permutation
        best_perm, min_cost = None, float('inf')
        for perm in perm_groups[gt_inst[c].type]:  # e.g., 2 for line
            cost = torch.nn.functional.l1_loss(
                pred_pts[r, perm], gt_pts[c], reduction='none').mean()
            if cost < min_cost:
                min_cost, best_perm = cost, perm
        total_cost += cost_inst[r,c] + min_cost
        point_assignments[(r,c)] = best_perm
    return total_cost, point_assignments
```

Haskell-style functional specification for differential merge:

```haskell
-- | Differential HD Map merge with evidence theory
type Confidence = Double
type MapFeature = Polyline
data Existence = Normal | Deleted | New deriving (Eq, Show)

data LabeledFeature = Labeled
  { feature    :: MapFeature
  , belief     :: Existence
  , mass       :: Confidence
  , sourceId   :: Int
  }

dempsterCombine :: LabeledFeature -> LabeledFeature -> LabeledFeature
dempsterCombine a b = Labeled
  { feature  = if mass a > mass b then feature a else feature b
  , belief   = combineBelief (belief a) (belief b)
  , mass     = mass a * mass b / (1 - conflict)
  , sourceId = sourceId a -- provenance preserved
  }
  where
    conflict = if belief a /= belief b then 0.2 else 0.0
    combineBelief Normal Normal = Normal
    combineBelief _ New = New
    combineBelief _ Deleted = Deleted
    combineBelief _ _ = Normal
```

Rust core for pose-graph edge optimization (g2o-like):

```rust
use nalgebra as na;

struct PoseEdge {
    from: usize,
    to: usize,
    meas: na::Isometry3<f64>,
    info: na::Matrix6<f64>,
}

impl PoseEdge {
    fn error(&self, poses: &[na::Isometry3<f64>]) -> na::Vector6<f64> {
        let t_ij = poses[self.from].inv() * poses[self.to];
        let err_isom = self.meas.inv() * t_ij;
        // log map to se3
        let mut err: na::Vector6<f64> = na::Vector6::zeros();
        err.fixed_rows_mut::<3>(0).copy_from(&err_isom.translation.vector);
        err.fixed_rows_mut::<3>(3).copy_from(&err_isom.rotation.scaled_axis());
        err
    }
    fn robustify(&self, err: &na::Vector6<f64>) -> f64 {
        let chi2 = (err.transpose() * self.info * err)[(0,0)];
        // Huber
        if chi2.sqrt() < 1.345 { chi2 } else { 2.0*1.345*chi2.sqrt() - 1.345*1.345 }
    }
}
```

### 5.3 Theoretical Argument

> Theorem: One-to-Many Acceleration. Let $L_{one2one}$ be loss under Hungarian matching with single GT copy. Replicating GT $K$ times reduces variance of gradient estimator $g$ by factor $1/K$, accelerating SGD convergence by $\mathbb{E}[\|w_t - w^*\|^2] \le O(1/(K t))$.

*Proof sketch.* Follows from increasing positive match count per batch; each query gets matched at least once with high probability, eliminating dead queries. Dense supervision ensures loss surface smoother (Lipschitz constant reduced). Empirically, MapTRv2 reports 4× faster convergence [2].

## 6 Limitations

1. **NeRF scalability**: City-scale NeRF priors compressed via PreSight [2] still require $O(N_{tiles})$ storage; 1 km² tile ~85 MB implicit field. Crowd-sourced NeRF must handle unbounded exposure variance and transient occlusion (pedestrians). Joint pose-NeRF optimization may converge to local minima in tunnel-like degenerate geometry.

2. **Permutation ambiguity under occlusion**: Hierarchical bipartite matching assumes complete visibility; partial occlusion (e.g., divider behind truck) yields truncated polylines where equivalence group no longer valid. PriorMapNet hints that spatial priors partially mitigate but not solve.

3. **SLAM graph drift without LiDAR**: Vision-only pose graph relying on NeRF descriptors degrades under rain/night where photometric consistency breaks. Multi-modal fusion (camera+LiDAR) in MapTR shows +13.5 mAP gain [1]; maintaining LiDAR-free update sacrifices robustness.

4. **Topology long-tail**: OpenLane-V2 benchmark reveals long-tail branching (roundabouts, U-turn pockets) where TOPO recall < 40% even with sequence models. Graph representation assumes planarity; overpasses violate DAG acyclicity.

5. **Evidence theory calibration**: Dempster-Shafer masses require hand-tuned FoV models and confidence calibration; deep evidential learning improves but remains sensitive to out-of-distribution detector scores.

6. **Compute and latency trade-off**: Real-time inference 25.1 FPS nano vs 14.1 FPS tiny vs offline NeRF training 2h/tile reveals two-tier system complexity, complicating deployment orchestration.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a comprehensive synthesis uniting *explicitly structured vectorized mapping* exemplified by MapTR [1] and MapTRv2 [2], *implicit neural fields* for geometric and weakly supervised priors exemplified by MapNeRF [4] and MapRF [5], and *differential graph-theoretic maintenance* via SLAM Change Update [7] and graph SLAM. By formalizing permutation-equivalent polylines, Map-Conditioned NeRF with Map-to-Ray matching, and evidential change classification over pose graphs, the framework aims for lifelong HD maps that are accurate, real-time, and sustainably updatable.

Future work includes integrating 4D NeRF (time-aware) for dynamic element filtering, learning uncertainty-calibrated evidential fusion end-to-end, extending topology reasoning to 3D overpasses via layered graphs, and deploying crowd-sourcedcity-scale evaluation with privacy-preserving federated NeRF updates.

Practically, the convergence of transformer-based vectorized HD map construction with NeRF priors marks the path from costly offline annotation to scalable autonomy. Maintenance, however, remains the unsolved frontier—requiring not only learning but *estimation theory* and robust graph optimization.
