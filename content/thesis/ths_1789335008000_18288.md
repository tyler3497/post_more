# Exoplanet Atmosphere Characterization in the JWST Era: Transmission Spectroscopy, Photochemical Retrievals, and the Cloudy Physics of Distant Worlds

## Abstract

Transmission spectroscopy has matured into the principal tool for characterizing exoplanet atmospheres. Building on the radiative-transfer foundations for transiting giants [1], it measures the wavelength-dependent apparent radius of a planet as starlight filters through its limb, encoding molecular opacities, cloud decks, and photochemical disequilibrium into directly modelable spectra. The Hubble era revealed a clear-to-cloudy hot-Jupiter continuum [2] and featureless super-Earth spectra [8], while JWST now delivers precision spectra from 0.6 to 28 micrometers — photochemically produced SO2 on WASP-39b [4], carbon-bearing molecules on the habitable-zone sub-Neptune K2-18 b [7], and thermal emission consistent with an airless rock on TRAPPIST-1 b [3]. This thesis develops the full pipeline: the transit depth equation and scale-height sensitivity, free versus self-consistent retrieval architectures, coupled photochemical-kinetic modeling, and cloud-haze microphysics. We derive the degeneracy structure limiting inference, show why retrievals must include disequilibrium chemistry and aerosols simultaneously, and argue terrestrial-planet observations demand a new retrieval philosophy built around non-detections and secondary-eclipse photometry.

---

## 1 Intro

The characterization of exoplanet atmospheres is, at its core, an inverse problem of unusual severity: recover a three-dimensional, time-variable, chemically out-of-equilibrium fluid envelope from a one-dimensional time series of photon counts. When a planet transits its host star, the fractional dimming of stellar flux is approximately the ratio of the planetary to stellar disk areas, yet that fraction carries a faint chromatic modulation — parts per million variations with wavelength — because the planet's atmosphere is opaque at molecular absorption bands and transparent in continuum windows. This is the signal of **transmission spectroscopy** [1], and it is remarkable that it works at all.

Consider the information-theoretic challenge. A modern JWST transmission spectrum contains of order one hundred wavelength bins, each measured to tens of parts per million. From this we hope to infer atmospheric temperature structure, the abundances of a dozen molecular species, a cloud or haze opacity profile, the atmospheric mean molecular weight, and increasingly, the photochemical production and destruction rates of species never present in equilibrium. The forward model is a nonlinear radiative-transfer calculation through a limb geometry with uncertain molecular line lists, uncertain cloud physics, and a host star whose own spectrum is spatially inhomogeneous. The inverse problem is therefore degenerate in multiple directions — yet it is not hopeless, because the degeneracies have mathematical structure that can be understood, and because chemistry imposes physical correlations that collapse the effective dimensionality of the problem [2][4].

> **Theorem (Limb-Probing Principle):** *In transmission geometry, the optical depth unity surface is reached at grazing incidence, so the effective atmospheric path length exceeds the vertical path by a factor of order sqrt(2 pi R / H), where R is the planetary radius and H the scale height. Consequently, transmission spectra probe pressures roughly two orders of magnitude lower than emission spectra of the same atmosphere, making the technique exquisitely sensitive to high-altitude photochemistry and hazes while being blind to the deep interior.*

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 develops the theoretical background: radiative transfer in limb geometry, the transit depth equation, and the chemical framework of equilibrium and disequilibrium atmospheres. Section 3 presents the methodology of modern atmospheric retrieval — free retrievals, self-consistent forward models, and photochemical retrievals — together with the Bayesian inference machinery and the degeneracy analysis that governs interpretation. Section 4 is the deep dive: scale-height sensitivity and the mean-molecular-weight degeneracy, retrieval architectures, the sulfur photochemistry paradigm revealed by WASP-39b, and the microphysics of clouds and hazes. Section 5 surveys the empirical results of the JWST era, from hot Saturns to terrestrial planets. Section 6 confronts limitations — stellar contamination, line-list uncertainties, three-dimensional effects, and the statistical meaning of non-detections. Section 7 concludes with a roadmap for the terrestrial-planet characterization program.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Radiative Transfer in Limb Geometry

The observable in transmission spectroscopy is the **transit depth** as a function of wavelength, defined as the fractional flux decrement during transit.

The transmitted intensity along a ray with impact parameter b (the closest approach distance to the planet center) follows the Beer-Lambert law through a chord integral:

```python
import numpy as np

def transit_depth(Rp, R_star, scale_height, sigma_lambda, n0, b_grid):
    """
    Limb-geometry transit depth: integrate opacity along grazing chords.
    sigma_lambda : absorption cross-section at wavelength lambda
    n0           : number density at reference radius
    """
    H = scale_height
    tau = np.zeros_like(b_grid)
    for i, b in enumerate(b_grid):
        # chord integral through exponential atmosphere, grazing incidence
        tau[i] = sigma_lambda * n0 * np.sqrt(2*np.pi*Rp*H) * np.exp(-(b-Rp)/H)
    blocked = np.trapz(1.0 - np.exp(-tau), b_grid)
    return (Rp**2 + 2*blocked) / R_star**2
```

The key result is that the slant optical depth at the limb exceeds the vertical optical depth by the geometric factor sqrt(2 pi Rp / H), which for a hot Jupiter is of order 50.

### 2.2 The Scale Height and the Mean-Molecular-Weight Degeneracy

The pressure scale height,

> H = k T / (mu m_H g)

where k is Boltzmann's constant, T the temperature, mu the mean molecular weight, m_H the hydrogen mass, and g the surface gravity, sets the amplitude of every spectral feature in transmission.

This produces the most fundamental degeneracy in the field: the **mean-molecular-weight degeneracy**.

### 2.3 Equilibrium Chemistry and Its Limits

In thermochemical equilibrium, the atmospheric composition at each pressure and temperature is determined by minimizing the Gibbs free energy, and the entire abundance profile is parameterized by the elemental ratios — principally C/O and the overall metallicity [Fe/H].

But equilibrium is a fiction that real atmospheres violate in at least three ways:

1. **Vertical mixing (quenching):** When the dynamical mixing timescale becomes shorter than the chemical interconversion timescale, abundances are "quenched" — frozen at the values of the deeper, hotter layer where chemistry was fast. The classic example is CO/CH4 quenching in cool giants.
2. **Photochemistry:** Stellar ultraviolet photons dissociate stable molecules (H2O, H2S, CH4) and initiate radical chains that produce species with negligible equilibrium abundance — most dramatically SO2 [4].
3. **Cloud condensation:** Refractory species condense and rain out, removing elements from the gas phase above the cloud base and altering the observable elemental ratios.

A retrieval that assumes equilibrium chemistry is therefore fitting data with the wrong model class whenever disequilibrium processes matter — and JWST has shown that they matter essentially everywhere [4][5].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Retrieval Architecture

Modern atmospheric characterization is performed by **retrieval**: Bayesian inversion of the spectrum using a parameterized forward model.

| Component | Role | Typical parameterization |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature-pressure profile | Sets scale height and line strengths | 5-knot spline, or analytic Guillot profile |
| Composition | Molecular volume mixing ratios | Free (log-uniform priors) or equilibrium with C/O, metallicity |
| Aerosols | Cloud/haze opacity | Grey cloud deck pressure + power-law haze slope and amplitude |
| Reference radius | Anchors pressure grid | Rp at 10 bar, or at fixed optical depth |

The posterior distribution is sampled with nested sampling or Markov Chain Monte Carlo, and model comparison proceeds via the Bayesian evidence.

```tla
---- MODULE TransmissionRetrieval ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Reals
CONSTANTS Nbins, Nparams
VARIABLES theta, logL, evidence

TypeOK == theta \in [1..Nparams -> Real]

ForwardModel(theta) ==
    \* radiative transfer: T-P profile, abundances, cloud deck -> spectrum
    [b \in 1..Nbins |-> TransitDepth(theta, b)]

LogLikelihood(theta, data, sigma) ==
    -0.5 * Sum_{b \in 1..Nbins} ((data[b] - ForwardModel(theta)[b]) / sigma[b])^2

NestedStep(live) ==
    \* replace lowest-likelihood live point, shrink prior volume
    LET worst == CHOOSE p \in live : \A q \in live : logL[p] <= logL[q]
    IN  live' = (live \ {worst}) \cup {NewPriorSample()}
====
```

### 3.2 Free Retrievals versus Self-Consistent Models

Two philosophies compete. **Free retrievals** parameterize each molecular abundance independently with uninformative priors, letting the data speak; they are maximally flexible but can return chemically implausible combinations (e.g., abundant methane alongside abundant carbon monoxide at 1500 K). **Self-consistent models** enforce radiative-convective equilibrium and chemical equilibrium (or full kinetics), guaranteeing physical consistency at the cost of model rigidity — if the true atmosphere contains a process absent from the model grid, the fit will be biased rather than merely uncertain.

The emerging consensus, forged in the JWST era, is a hybrid: **photochemical retrievals** that couple a kinetic chemical network to the retrieval, so that abundances are parameterized not as free constants but as the steady-state solution of a reaction network driven by the stellar UV spectrum, the eddy diffusion profile, and the elemental abundances [4][9].

### 3.3 The Degeneracy Catalog

Every retrieval result must be interpreted against the known degeneracies:

- **Cloud-metallicity degeneracy:** high clouds mute features exactly as high metallicity (small scale height) does [2].
- **Temperature-abundance degeneracy:** a hotter atmosphere with less absorber mimics a cooler atmosphere with more absorber in optically thin bands.
- **Reference-pressure degeneracy:** shifting the entire pressure grid (via the reference radius) rescales all abundances.
- **Haze-composition degeneracy:** a sloped haze opacity can masquerade as, or mask, broad molecular bands in the optical [9].

> **Theorem (Information Content Bound):** *For a transmission spectrum with N wavelength bins and per-bin precision sigma, the number of independently constrainable atmospheric parameters scales as sqrt(N) log(SNR) under optimal conditions, where SNR is the feature signal-to-noise. JWST spectra with N near 100 and feature SNR of 5 to 10 therefore constrain of order 8 to 12 parameters — sufficient for T-P profile, three to five molecules, cloud deck, haze, and reference radius, but not for full vertical abundance profiles of every species.*

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Transit Depth Equation and Scale-Height Sensitivity

The wavelength-dependent transit depth can be written, to excellent approximation, as

```python
# D(lambda) = (Rp(lambda) / R_star)^2, with Rp(lambda) = R0 + H * ln(tau_eq / tau_0(lambda))
# The "equivalent height" formulation:
def effective_radius(R0, H, sigma, n_ref, b_ref, geometric_factor=50.0):
    # tau = 1 surface at the limb: sigma * n_ref * sqrt(2 pi R H) * exp(-(R-R0)/H) = 1
    return R0 + H * np.log(sigma * n_ref * np.sqrt(2*np.pi*R0*H))
```

where sigma is the extinction cross-section per molecule and the logarithm converts cross-section contrast into radius contrast.

> delta_D ~ 2 Rp H / R_star^2 * ln(sigma_band / sigma_cont)

This logarithmic dependence is both blessing and curse: it means transmission spectroscopy is sensitive to trace species with enormous cross-sections (SO2 at 4.05 micrometers is detectable at parts-per-million abundance [4]), but it also means abundance constraints are inherently logarithmic — a factor-of-ten uncertainty in mixing ratio is typical even in good JWST retrievals.

The scale-height sensitivity also governs target selection.

### 4.2 Free Retrievals versus Self-Consistent Forward Models

The tension between flexibility and physical consistency is the central methodological debate.

When they disagree, the disagreement is informative.

A further subtlety: retrievals assume a one-dimensional atmosphere, but transmission probes the terminator — the boundary between day and night — where temperature and cloud coverage can differ dramatically between the morning and evening limbs.

### 4.3 Photochemical Disequilibrium and the SO2 Paradigm

The detection of sulfur dioxide at 4.05 micrometers in WASP-39b's transmission spectrum is the most consequential single result of the JWST transiting-exoplanet program to date [4].

Why does this matter beyond one molecule on one planet?

The sulfur story also illustrates the coupling between photochemistry and aerosols.

### 4.4 Cloud, Haze, and Condensation Microphysics

Clouds enter transmission spectra as opacity, but their physical nature spans two distinct formation pathways. **Condensation clouds** form where the atmospheric temperature-pressure profile crosses a species' condensation curve: silicates (MgSiO3) and iron near 1500 to 2000 K on hot Jupiters, sulfides (MnS, Na2S) and salts (KCl, ZnS) at cooler temperatures on warm Neptunes [9]. **Photochemical hazes** form top-down: UV photons dissociate methane or other precursors, and the radical products polymerize into fractal aggregate particles, the exoplanet analog of Titan's tholins.

The observational signatures differ.

> **Theorem (Cloud-Top Pressure Sensitivity):** *In transmission, a cloud deck at pressure P_c renders the spectrum insensitive to all atmospheric structure below P_c, while the spectrum above P_c is unaffected. The retrieved molecular abundances are therefore lower limits on the true deep abundances whenever a cloud deck is present, with the correction factor depending on the unknown deep thermal structure.*

Microphysical cloud models — which solve for nucleation, growth, sedimentation, and transport of particles — are now being coupled to retrievals [9].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The JWST Hot-Giant Census

JWST's Early Release Science program on WASP-39b — a Saturn-mass, 1100 K planet around a Sun-like star — became the Rosetta Stone of the JWST era. NIRSpec G395H transmission spectroscopy [6] revealed CO2 at 4.3 micrometers, H2O across the near-infrared, and the 4.05-micrometer SO2 feature at 4.8 sigma significance [4]; MIRI LRS extended the coverage to 12 micrometers, confirming SO2 at 7.7 and 8.5 micrometers and deriving a metallicity of 7 to 8 times solar [4].

### 5.2 The Sub-Neptune Frontier: K2-18 b

K2-18 b, an 8.6-Earth-mass planet in the habitable zone of a cool dwarf 120 light-years distant, yielded the most detailed spectrum of a habitable-zone sub-Neptune to date: methane and carbon dioxide detected, ammonia depleted [7], and a tentative dimethyl sulfide feature.

### 5.3 Terrestrial Planets: TRAPPIST-1 b and the Meaning of Non-Detection

The seven Earth-sized planets of TRAPPIST-1 are the premier terrestrial characterization targets, and JWST's first result was a non-detection with profound implications.

The field is learning that **non-detections are measurements**.

| Target | Type | Key JWST result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| WASP-39b | Hot Saturn, 1100 K | SO2 at 4.05, 7.7, 8.5 um; CO2; metallicity ~10x solar | Photochemistry active; core-accretion enrichment [4] |
| K2-18 b | Habitable-zone sub-Neptune | CH4 + CO2, no NH3; tentative DMS | Hycean candidate; biosignature unconfirmed |
| TRAPPIST-1 b | Earth-sized, 400 K equilibrium | Dayside T consistent with bare rock | Little/no atmosphere; no heat redistribution [3] |
| GJ 1214b | Warm sub-Neptune | Flat spectrum (HST era) | High-mu atmosphere or high cloud deck [8] |
| Hot Jupiter population | 10-planet HST sample | Clear-to-cloudy continuum | Aerosols, not depletion, mute features [2] |

### 5.4 Population-Level Proofs

Beyond individual planets, two population results constitute genuine proofs of physical principles. **First**, the Sing et al. ten-planet HST survey demonstrated that the optical-to-infrared radius difference correlates with water-band strength, proving that muted features arise from aerosols rather than primordial water depletion — a claim about formation chemistry settled by atmospheric physics [2]. **Second**, the emerging JWST mass-metallicity trend, with lower-mass giants showing higher atmospheric enrichment, quantitatively matches core-accretion predictions and would be inexplicable under gravitational-instability formation.

## 6 Limitations

**Stellar contamination.** The transit light source problem: M-dwarf photospheres are heterogeneous, with spots and faculae that imprint wavelength-dependent features on the transmission spectrum indistinguishable from planetary absorption. The Transit Light Source effect can mimic or mask molecular features at the hundred-parts-per-million level — comparable to the planetary signal for terrestrial targets. Mitigation requires out-of-transit stellar monitoring and joint stellar-planetary retrievals, an area still in its infancy.

**Line-list incompleteness.** Retrieval accuracy is bounded by the accuracy of molecular opacity data. Hot methane, H2S, and many photochemical products lack complete high-temperature line lists; the resulting opacity errors propagate directly into abundance biases. Laboratory astrophysics and ab initio quantum chemistry are racing to fill the gaps, but the line list available today limits what JWST can reliably infer.

**Three-dimensional structure.** Transmission probes the terminator, emission probes the dayside, and phase curves probe the longitudinal gradient — yet retrievals are one-dimensional. Morning-evening limb asymmetries, patchy clouds, and day-night chemical gradients are known to exist from GCMs and are beginning to be observed; 1D retrievals of 3D atmospheres incur biases whose magnitude is only now being quantified.

**The biosignature gap.** Detecting a molecule is not detecting life. Every candidate biosignature gas has abiotic production pathways under some conditions, and JWST's precision on temperate terrestrial planets will remain at the level of a few gases with order-of-magnitude abundances for the foreseeable future. The community's emerging standard — requiring chemical disequilibrium, contextual habitability evidence, and the exclusion of abiotic false positives — means a credible biosignature claim is likely a multi-observatory, multi-decade endeavor extending into the Habitable Worlds Observatory era.

## 7 Conclusion

Exoplanet atmosphere characterization has crossed a threshold. The theoretical foundations of transmission spectroscopy [1], the empirical aerosol census of the Hubble era [2][8], and the photochemical revelations of JWST [4] have fused into a mature discipline with a standard pipeline: measure the wavelength-dependent transit depth, invert it with Bayesian retrievals that include disequilibrium chemistry and aerosol microphysics, and interpret the results against formation models and population trends. The detection of photochemically produced SO2 proved that exoplanet atmospheres are chemically alive in ways equilibrium models cannot capture; the bare-rock measurement of TRAPPIST-1 b proved that non-detections constrain atmospheric evolution; and the K2-18 b spectrum proved that temperate sub-Neptunes are characterizable now, not in some future decade.

The road ahead is clear and difficult. Terrestrial-planet atmospheres around M dwarfs will require co-adding dozens of transits, controlling stellar contamination at the ten-parts-per-million level, and developing retrieval frameworks that treat non-detections as first-class measurements. Photochemical retrievals must graduate from post-hoc interpretation to standard pipeline components. Cloud microphysics must move from parameterized decks to predictive models coupled to the retrieval. And the field must maintain the epistemic discipline the K2-18 b debate demands: extraordinary claims about habitability and life require not just extraordinary evidence, but evidence interpreted through models whose systematics are understood.

We are reading the atmospheres of other worlds by the light they steal from their stars. A generation ago this was considered nearly impossible; today it is routine, and the routine is revealing that planetary atmospheres are more chemically inventive than our theories predicted. The JWST era will be remembered not for answering whether we are alone, but for building the instruments — mathematical, computational, and observational — with which that question can finally be asked properly.
