## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of hardware-software co-design for sparse attention in long-context llms, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of hardware-software co-design for sparse attention in long-context llms and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early hardware-software constructions to state-of-the-art FlashAttention-2 deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show hardware-software co-design for sparse attention in long-context llms achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
