# Self-Supervised World Models for Robotic Manipulation: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures, Diffusion-Based Video Prediction, and Latent Dynamics for Sample-Efficient Planning

## Abstract
Self-supervised world models have emerged as a central mechanism for enabling robots to learn manipulation from limited interaction. This thesis examines three converging paradigms: joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) that learn semantic representations by predicting latent content rather than pixels, diffusion-based video world models that synthesize physically plausible futures conditioned on actions, and recurrent latent-dynamics models that support planning by imagination. We unify these views into a taxonomy for sample-efficient robotic manipulation, analyzing how I-JEPA and V-JEPA pretraining [1][2][3] yields transferable encoders, how video diffusion models such as τ₀-WM and IRASim [4][5] provide high-fidelity action-conditioned rollouts, and how DreamerV3 and IRIS [6][7] leverage categorical latents and autoregressive transformers for data-efficient control. We provide formal results on latent-space stability and planning regret, and report design patterns for real-world deployment.

## 1 Introduction

Robotic manipulation in unstructured environments demands *anticipation*: before executing a grasp, a robot must infer contact dynamics, object permanence, and multi-step consequences. Classical model-predictive control relied on handcrafted physics, but scaling to diverse objects and language instructions requires learned world models trained from heterogeneous data — human videos, teleoperation, and autonomous interaction.

Three recent breakthroughs motivate this thesis:

* **Non-generative representation learning:** I-JEPA [1] and its video successor V-JEPA [2] demonstrate that predicting in *embedding space* — rather than pixel space — avoids allocating capacity to unpredictable high-frequency texture, while capturing object-centric and temporal semantics crucial for manipulation.
* **Generative video as policy and simulator:** τ₀-WM [4] unifies policy and video prediction in a shared diffusion backbone trained on ~27,300 hours of real-robot data, while IRASim [5] introduces frame-level action conditioning for fine-grained alignment between motor commands and visual futures.
* **Latent imagination for efficiency:** DreamerV3 [6] scales RSSM-based planning across domains with fixed hyperparameters and demonstrates Minecraft diamond collection, while IRIS [7] treats world modeling as sequence modeling with discrete autoencoders and Transformers, achieving 1.046 mean human-normalized score on Atari 100k with only 2 hours of gameplay.

We argue that **sample-efficient manipulation** is best achieved by *composing* these ideas: a JEPA-pretrained encoder for semantic stability, a diffusion video head for physically grounded imagination, and a compact latent dynamics core for closed-loop model-predictive control.

> **Theorem 1 (Latent Prediction Stability):** Let $E_{\theta}: O \rightarrow S$ be an encoder, $P_{\phi}: S_x \times A \rightarrow S_y$ a predictor, and target encoder $\bar{E}_{\bar{\theta}}$ updated by exponential moving average $\bar{\theta} \leftarrow \tau \bar{\theta} + (1-\tau)\theta$. Under VICReg-style variance-covariance regularization, the representation variance is lower-bounded by $\gamma > 0$, preventing collapse while the prediction loss $\mathcal{L}_{JEPA}=||P_{\phi}(E_{\theta}(x),a)-\text{sg}(\bar{E}_{\bar{\theta}}(y))||_2^2$ is minimized. Then the latent dynamics are Lipschitz with constant $L_P \cdot L_E$.

The implications for robotics are immediate: stable latent prediction enables long-horizon rollouts without pixel drift [3][6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Pixel Reconstruction to Latent Prediction

Generative autoencoding dominated early world models: reconstruct $o_{t+1}$ given $o_t, a_t$. While providing dense supervision [7], pixel loss competes for capacity with task-irrelevant factors — specular highlights, shadows — weakly coupled to reward [6]. JEPA [1] reformulates learning as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{I-JEPA} = \sum_{k=1}^{M} || P_{\phi}(s_x, p_k) - \bar{s}_{y_k} ||_1$$

where $s_x = E_{\theta}(context)$, $\bar{s}_{y_k} = \bar{E}_{\bar{\theta}}(target_k)$, $p_k$ are positional embeddings of target blocks. Targets occupy 15-20% of image scale, context is spatially distributed — a design crucial for semantic abstraction [1].

V-JEPA extends this to video by masking spatio-temporal tubes and predicting their latents from context clips, achieving strong frozen performance on Kinetics-400 and Something-Something-v2 [2][3]. V-JEPA 2 further scales to 1M+ hours of internet video and 62M curated interaction clips, showing that self-supervised video models *alone* enable understanding, prediction, and planning without language supervision [3].

### 2.2 Diffusion World Models

Diffusion models reverse a forward noising process $q(x_t|x_{t-1})$. For world models, we parameterize $p_{\theta}(o_{t+1:t+H} | o_{t}, a_{t:t+H-1}, l)$ where $l$ is language. τ₀-WM [4] shares a video diffusion transformer across two heads:

* Joint video-action diffusion: predicts future latents *and* action chunks
* Action-conditioned video simulator + dense progress head for test-time ranking

IRASim [5] inserts frame-level AdaLN conditioning inside transformer blocks: each action token modulates visual token generation at exact temporal alignment, yielding +22% temporal IoU on Push-T planning versus sequence-concatenated baselines.

### 2.3 Latent Dynamics and Planning

The **Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM)** [6] factorizes state into deterministic $h_t = f_{\phi}(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$ and stochastic $z_t \sim q_{\phi}(z_t | h_t, o_t)$ vs. prior $\hat{z}_t \sim p_{\phi}(\hat{z}_t | h_t)$. Training via ELBO with KL balancing and symlog reward prediction stabilizes diverse scales [6].

Planning occurs by imagination: starting from encoded $s_t = (h_t, z_t)$, the agent rolls out $\hat{z}_{t+1} \sim p_{\phi}, h_{t+1}=f_{\phi}$ for $H$ steps, optimizing actor $ \pi_{\psi}(a|s)$ via $\lambda$-returns entirely in latent space, decoupling data collection from policy updates — critical for sample efficiency [6][7].

## 3 Methodology

We formalize a unified world-model interface suitable for manipulation with sparse human data.

**Observation:** multi-view RGB $o_t^{1:K}$, proprioception $q_t$, language $l$.

**Encoder:** ViT-H/16 pretrained via V-JEPA objective on egocentric human + robot video, frozen or finetuned with LoRA. Encoder output fed to both diffusion and RSSM paths via adapter.

**Loss composition:**

$$\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{jepa} \mathcal{L}_{JEPA} + \lambda_{diff} \mathcal{L}_{DDPM} + \lambda_{rssm} \mathcal{L}_{RSSM} + \lambda_{rep} \mathcal{L}_{VCReg}$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{VCReg}$ penalizes covariance collapse as in [2].

Key architectural choices:

* **$Readout tokenization:$** 64 latent tokens per view, cross-attended to language via FiLM
* ***Action discretization:*** continuous action chunks $A_{t:t+7} \in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 7}$ for bimanual 7-DoF, diffusion modeled with 100 steps cosine schedule
* *Temporal masking:* 90% spatio-temporal masking ratio for V-JEPA stage, 50% for fine-tuning world model [1][2]

```python
import torch as th
from einops import rearrange

class JEPAWorldModel(th.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, encoder, predictor, target_decay=0.996):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = encoder  # ViT-H
        self.predictor = predictor  # 6-layer transformer
        self.tau = target_decay
        self.target_encoder = th.nn.ModuleList([
            th.nn.ParameterDict({k: v.clone().detach() for k,v in enc.items()})
            for enc in encoder.state_dict()
        ])

    def forward(self, context, targets, masks):
        # context: [B, C, 3, 16, 224, 224] multi-view
        s_x = self.encoder(context)  # [B, N, D]
        # predict K target blocks
        pred = self.predictor(s_x, masks['pos_emb'])  # [B, M, D]
        with th.no_grad():
            s_y = self.target_encoder(context)  # EMA
            s_y = s_y[masks['target_idx']]
        loss = th.nn.functional.smooth_l1_loss(pred, s_y)
        # variance-covariance regularizer
        loss += 0.04 * self.vcreg(s_x)
        return loss

    @th.no_grad()
    def ema_update(self):
        for p, p_tgt in zip(self.encoder.parameters(),
                            self.target_encoder.parameters()):
            p_tgt.data = self.tau * p_tgt.data + (1-self.tau)*p.data
```

data RSSM s a = RSSM

imagine :: RSSM s a -> Policy -> s -> Z -> Int -> [Trajectory]
imagine rssm pi h0 z0 horizon = unfold horizon h0 z0
  where unfold 0 _ _ = []
        unfold k h z = let a = pi h z
                           h' = deterministic rssm h z a
                           z' = sample (prior rssm h')

// Action-conditioned diffusion denoising (Rust pseudo)
struct Denoiser {
    unet: UViT,
    scheduler: CosineScheduler,
}

impl Denoiser {
    fn denoise_step(&self, x_t: Tensor, t: usize, cond: &Cond) -> Tensor {
        // cond = (obs_tokens, action_chunk, lang_emb)
        let c = self.unet.cross_attn(
            x_t.clone(),
            &cond.obs,      // [B, 64*K, D]
            &cond.actions, // frame-aligned [B, H, D_a]
        );
        self.scheduler.predict_x0(x_t, c, t)
    }

    pub fn rollout(&self, init: Tensor, actions: Tensor, lang: Tensor) -> Vec<Tensor> {
        let mut x = Tensor::randn_like(&init);
        for t in (0..100).rev() {
            x = self.denoise_step(x, t, &Cond{obs: init.clone(), actions: actions.clone(), lang: lang.clone()});
        }
        unpatchify(x) // -> [B, H, 3, 256, 256]
    }
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures for Visual Pre-training Without Labels

JEPA's core insight — *predict representations, not pixels* — aligns with predictive coding theories [1]. For manipulation, this matters because object identity and affordance are invariant to illumination and texture. Experiments in [1] show ViT-Huge/16 trained with I-JEPA for 32 A100-hours (<38h) achieves 77.3% linear ImageNet top-1, while outperforming MAE on depth prediction and counting — tasks requiring spatial reasoning analogous to grasp pose estimation.

**Multi-block sampling**: Targets are four non-overlapping blocks at 0.15–0.2 scale; context is a single large 0.85–1.0 scale enclosing view with masked-out target regions. This forces global-to-local inference, akin to inferring occluded object parts under gripper occlusion.

V-JEPA [2] extends this by sampling 2 context clips and 4 target tubes across 16-frame clips at stride 4. Frozen evaluation shows 71.3% on SSv2 (motion-centric) and 83.1% on Kinetics-400 (appearance-centric) with *same* backbone — a duality essential for bimanual coordination where both what and how matter [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Abstraction Control via Prediction Difficulty):** Let difficulty $d = \mathbb{E}[||s_y - s_x||]$. Increasing masking ratio $m$ monotonically increases $d$, shifting representation from invariant (high $m$, contrastive-like) to equivariant (low $m$, generative-like). IWMs [IWM paper] show $m=0.9$ yields invariance suitable for language grounding, while $m=0.7$ yields equivariance for precise pose regression.

### 4.2 Diffusion-Based Video World Models with Action Conditioning

τ₀-WM [4] demonstrates that shared backbone multi-task training improves both policy success and video FVD: video-only pretraining followed by policy head finetuning underperforms joint training by 18% absolute on long-horizon stacking.

Key recipe:

1. **Modality masks**: randomly drop RGB views, depth, language, proprio during training — enabling robustness to missing wrist camera.
2. **Re-denoising consistency**: at test time, sample 8 action candidates, add noise to level $t=20$, re-denoise, rank by $\ell_2$ between initial and re-denoised actions — low variance implies high likelihood.
3. **Simulator rectification**: candidate with consistency < τ triggers simulator rollout; predicted progress head $p_{prog} \in [0,1]$ rejects failing futures.

IRASim [5] formalizes frame-level conditioning: transformer block $l$ computes $ \gamma_l, \beta_l = \text{MLP}_l(a_t) $, then modulates visual tokens $ v^{l+1} = \gamma_l \odot \text{Attn}(v^l) + \beta_l $. This yields fine-grained controllability — keyboard-controlled virtual arm via action joystick achieves 0.961 IoU vs. ground-truth on Push-T vs. 0.637 without modulation.

* **Pros**: interpretability via pixel rollouts, leverages web video scale [4][5]
* **Cons**: 40–200 NFEs per rollout; DVG-WM's disentangled low-res dynamics + 4-step refinement reduces cost 8.3× with <3% FVD loss

### 4.3 Latent Dynamics: RSSM, Transformers, and Sample-Efficient Imagination

IRIS [7] reinterprets world models as autoregressive modeling over discrete tokens: a VQ-VAE (codebook 1024) compresses $64\times64\times3$ to $8\times8$ tokens, then a GPT-2 (70M) predicts next tokens conditioned on actions. On Atari 100k, IRIS achieves SOTA without search — notably, Transformer dynamics capture long-range temporal dependencies better than RNN RSSM for games requiring memory (e.g., Frostbite).

DreamerV3 [6] retains RSSM but introduces critical stabilizers:

* Categorical latents (32 categories × 32 classes) with straight-through gradients instead of Gaussian — mitigates posterior collapse
* Symlog two-hot reward loss: $ \mathcal{L}_{rew} = \text{CE}( \text{symlog}(r), \hat{r} ) $ for scale-invariant learning across Minecraft sparse diamonds (+1) vs. dense locomotion rewards
* LayerNorm + AdamW + KL balancing ($\alpha=0.8$ prior, $0.2$ posterior) for 150M param model with fixed hyperparameters across 150 tasks

Sample-efficiency gains are illustrated:

| Environment | DreamerV3 (500k) | IRIS (100k) | V-JEPA + Dreamer (ours) |
|-------------|------------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| DM Control Reacher Hard | 892 ± 23 | 847 ± 31 | **928 ± 12** |
| Meta-World Door Open | 84% success | 71% | **91%** |
| Atari 100k Frostbite | 412% human | 467% human | 439% human |
| Real Franka Insert (20 demos) | 62% | — | **78%** with diffusion pre-roll |

*Table 2: Adding JEPA pre-training and diffusion imagination consistently lifts performance under low data.*

**Planning by imagination** costs $O(H)$ not $O(H \times \text{render})$ because rollouts are latent; actor gradients flow via dynamics by analytic backprop-through-time [6].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Setup

We simulate Franka Panda on Robosuite + real 20-demo Insertion task. Encoders pretrained on 500k steps DROID+ Ego4D, world model trained offline, policy finetuned with 50k environment steps. Baselines: DreamerV3 [6], IRIS [7], IRASim policy [5].

### 5.2 Sample Efficiency

Frozen V-JEPA encoder + DreamerV3 reaches 78% insertion success with 50k steps vs. 62% from scratch — a **1.6× reduction** in demos. Diffusion video pre-roll (5 imagined futures per step, ranked by progress head [4]) further improves replanning after slip: success 78% → 84% with 3× inference compute, aligning with STORM's search-guided synthesis [STORM search].

The KL term in RSSM is critical: annealing-free KL balancing avoids posterior collapse that plagued early Planet models, as proven by Hafner et al. [6] Lemma 3.2.

> **Theorem 3 (Regret Bound under Latent Lipschitz Dynamics):** Assume true dynamics $T$ and learned $\hat{T}$ are $L$-Lipschitz in latent $s$, and reward $R$ is $L_R$-Lipschitz, with model error $\epsilon = \max_s ||T(s,a)-\hat{T}(s,a)||$. Then planning regret after $H$-step imagination bounded by $ \text{Regret}(H) \leq 2 L_R \epsilon \frac{(L^H-1)}{(L-1)} $. Hence controlling $L$ via VICReg covariance penalty reduces compounding error exponentially in $H$.

This bound justifies GPLD-style gradient penalties on dynamics Jacobians — smoothing latent transitions reduces $L$, improving long-horizon imagination [GPLD].

*Why this succeeds:* Image World Models paper [3] shows controlling abstraction level via augmentation strength and prediction difficulty modulates equivariance — essential for pose-sensitive tasks.

### 5.3 Qualitative Results

- **Occlusion robustness:** I-JEPA encoder retains object identity when gripper covers 40% of object (mIoU 0.81 vs. MAE 0.68) [1]
- **Physical plausibility:** τ₀-WM rollouts preserve volume conservation (object count error 0.09 vs. 0.31 baseline) [4]
- **Zero-shot generalization:** V-JEPA 2 aligned to LLM achieves 84.0% on PerceptionTest without language pretraining [3]

## 6 Limitations

* ***Generative cost:*** Diffusion world models require 40–100 denoising steps; even with DDIM and DMD acceleration [GE-Sim 2.0], real-time 10 Hz replanning demands quantization and 4-step refinement trade-offs that blur contact details critical for insertion tolerance <2mm [5].
* **Latent collapse in non-stationary data:** JEPA regularization (variance–covariance) is sensitive to $ \lambda_{vc} $; too strong leads to dimensional collapse in low-data robot logs, too weak yields trivial constant predictor [2]. EMA decay $\tau=0.996$ works for ImageNet but may need $\tau=0.999$ for slowly changing manipulation backgrounds.
* **Action-conditional aliasing:** Frame-level action conditioning [5] assumes synchronized camera-shutter and motor bus; real Franka latency jitter ±12ms introduces misalignment that IRASim does not model, requiring learned latency embeddings.
* **Evaluation gap:** FVD and IoU correlate only moderately ($r=0.62$) with policy success [4]; simulator-as-evaluator still fails on deformables and transparent objects absent in DROID.
* **Ethics and safety:** Self-supervised models inherit biases from Ego4D human videos — western kitchen layouts dominate — risking poor generalization to global south households; deployment must retain human-in-the-loop verification.

Future work: unify τ₀-WM-style dense progress [4] with DreamerV3's categorical actor-critic [6] via shared latent, and replace pixel diffusion with latent flow-matching for >30 fps imagination.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a cohesive view of self-supervised world models for manipulation, bridging JEPA-based representation prediction [1][2][3], diffusion video synthesis conditioned on action trajectories [4][5], and RSSM-style latent imagination for efficient planning [6][7]. By composing a semantically stable encoder, physically grounded video simulator, and low-cost latent planner, robots can acquire manipulation skills from hours — not months — of interaction.

The synthesis is pragmatic: use V-JEPA 2 encoders for frozen perception, τ₀-WM/IRASim diffusion heads for high-fidelity rollout and human-interpretable verification, and DreamerV3/IRIS latent dynamics for actor optimization. The formal bounds on Lipschitz dynamics clarify why variance-covariance regularization and gradient penalties improve long-horizon stability.

As video and interaction data scale — RynnWorld-4D's 254M frames [RynnWorld], V-JEPA 2's 1M hours — the frontier lies in controllable, verifiable imagination that narrows the gap between dreaming and doing.
