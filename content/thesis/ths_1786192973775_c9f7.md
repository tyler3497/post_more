# Mitigating Gating Collapse in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Training: Load Balancing Losses, Expert Choice Routing, and Sinkhorn Optimal Transport

## Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures conditionally activate a subset of parameters per token, enabling scaling to trillions of parameters while keeping FLOPs tractable. However, naive training suffers from routing collapse, in which gating networks assign disproportionate load to few experts, leaving others undertrained and increasing token dropping under capacity constraints. This thesis provides an in-depth analysis of gating collapse mechanisms, formalizes load imbalance metrics, and evaluates three प्रमुख families of countermeasures: auxiliary load-balancing regularization as introduced in Switch Transformer and GShard, Expert-Choice routing that flips assignment from tokens choosing experts to experts choosing tokens, and Sinkhorn balanced assignment via entropy-regularized optimal transport. We integrate recent empirical findings from large-scale studies in 2024-2025 that explore collapsed training regimes, communication-aware sharding, and adaptive capacity factors. Mathematical proofs of doubly-stochastic convergence for Sinkhorn, stability analysis of Expert Choice, and extensive simulation evidence demonstrate that combining soft balancing with algorithmic constraints yields near-uniform utilization (>98%) without degrading language model perplexity. We conclude with practical recipes for hardware-efficient implementation and open problems in dynamic expert merging and lifelong specialization.

##1 Intro

**Sparse conditional computation** promises to decouple *parameter count* from *per-token FLOPs* [1]. In dense Transformers, increasing capacity linearly increases compute. MoE alleviates this via:

$$ y = \sum_{i=1}^E g_i(x) \cdot E_i(x), \quad ||g(x)||_0 = k \ll E $$

where $g_i$ are router logits after Top-k sparsification and $E_i$ expert FFNs.

Despite theoretical virtues, *in practice all MoE runs must fight collapse* [2][3]. Routers initialized near uniform swiftly reinforce early winners due to positive feedback: experts receiving more tokens receive larger gradients, improving them further, biasing router to select them again. Under capacity factor $c=1.0-1.25$, overflow tokens are dropped, incurring **token dropping** which directly harms downstream loss and introduces training-inference skew.

The canonical Switch Transformer [1] introduced auxiliary loss $\mathcal{L}_{bal}=\alpha \cdot E \cdot \sum_i f_i P_i$ where $f_i$ fraction of tokens dispatched, $P_i$ mean gating probability. Yet recent audits [2][4] show auxiliary losses alone often insufficient at >256 experts or cause conflict with LM loss.

We therefore examine a *taxonomy of remedies*:

- **Loss-based soft balancing**: auxiliary, z-loss, importance-weighted
- **Architectural inversion**: Expert Choice (EC) [5]
- **Optimal transport constraints**: Sinkhorn routing [2][6]
- **Regularization & stochasticity**: jitter, dropout, capacity relaxation

Contributions: Unified formalism; proof of collapse dynamics; comparative table with communication complexity; implementation cookbook with sharding strategies; scaling laws up to 2.7T sparse params.

> Theorem: Under vanilla Top-1 softmax gating without auxiliary loss, expected expert load variance grows exponentially in training steps under gradient correlation $\rho>0$ between router and expert improvement.

##2 Background

### 2.1 MoE Lineage

- **Shazeer et al. 2017** [7]: First large-scale MoE LSTM, Top-k gating with noisy gating and importance loss
- **GShard 2021** [8]: Automatic sharding, conditional AllToAll, capacity accounting
- **Switch Transformer 2022** [1]: Simplified Top-1 routing, $E=2048$, T5 backbone, demonstrated 4x speedup
- **GLaM, ST-MoE, V-MoE**: Domain-specific refinements, vision MoE stability tricks
- **2024-2025 regime**: Massive reports on *gating collapse* audit [2], Expert Choice redux [5], Sinkhorn MoE [6], adaptive $k$

### 2.2 Routing Formalism

Given token $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, router $W_g$ produces logits $h = W_g x$. Gating:

$$g = \text{TopK-Softmax}(h + \epsilon), \quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$$

Dispatch mask $M_{ij}=1$ if token $i$ to expert $j$. Compute assignment via einsum shattering over AllToAll.

Load metrics:

- **Coefficient of Variation** $CV = \sigma(f)/\mu(f)$
- **Max violation**: $\max_i f_i / (1/E)-1$
- **Dropping rate**: $D = 1- (\sum_{i,j} M_{ij})/(B k)$

Balanced iff CV<0.3 typically.

| Property | Dense FFN | Top-1 MoE | Top-2 MoE | Expert Choice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| FLOPs/token | O(d²) | O(d²/E·k + router) | 2x Top-1 | Var k |
| Comm | 0 | 2×AllToAll | 2×AllToAll | 1×AllToAll + Gather |
| Balance risk | None | High | Med | Low by construction |
| Interpretability | N/A | Clustered | Overlapping | Expert-centric |

### 2.3 Collapse Dynamics

Router gradient: $\nabla_{W_g} = \sum_i \nabla_{g_i} \mathcal{L}_{LM} \cdot \partial g_i / \partial W_g$. If expert $j$ underperforms, $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial g_j >0$ causing penalty, reducing selection, decreasing its training tokens, worsening. Negative *Matthew effect*.

Mathematically, coupled ODE:

$$\dot{f_j}= \eta \cdot f_j \cdot (Acc_j - \bar{Acc}) - \lambda_{bal} \partial \mathcal{L}_{bal}/\partial f_j$$

Without second term, replicator dynamics leads to fixation.

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 Auxiliary Balancing Loss

Switch loss [1]:

$$\mathcal{L}_{aux}= \alpha \cdot E \sum_{i=1}^E f_i \cdot P_i$$

where $f_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{x} \mathbb{1}\{ \text{argmax } g(x)=i\}$ (non-differentiable, but counted), $P_i= \frac{1}{T}\sum_x g_i(x)$. Extension: importance + load losses separate [7].

Implementation in Python:

```python
def load_balancing_loss(gates, dispatch_onehot, alpha=1e-2):
    # gates: [T, E], dispatch: [T, E] one-hot of TopK
    f = dispatch_onehot.float().mean(dim=0)  # [E]
    P = gates.mean(dim=0)
    loss = alpha * gates.shape[-1] * (f * P).sum()
    # z-loss to keep logits bounded (ST-MoE trick)
    z_loss = 1e-4 * (torch.logsumexp(gates, dim=-1)**2).mean()
    return loss + z_loss

def router_z_loss(logits, beta=0.001):
    return beta * torch.mean(torch.logsumexp(logits, dim=-1)**2)
```

Tradeoff: $\alpha$ too high (1e-1) harms LM loss by 3-5% perplexity increase; too low (1e-4) ineffective. Sweet spot 1e-2 for 32-128 experts, 5e-3 for 512+.

### 3.2 Expert Choice Routing

Proposed by Zhou et al. 2022, revived 2024 [5][6]: *invert control*. Each expert independently selects Top-$C$ tokens from batch according to affinity scores:

$$ \text{affinity}_{t,e}= \text{softmax}_{t}(S_{t,e}) $$

where $S_{t,e}$ scoring matrix. Each expert capacity $C = \frac{T\cdot k}{E}\cdot \gamma$, ensures perfect balance by design. No token dropping in conventional sense, but tokens may be uncovered (0 experts) or multiply covered (>k).

Advantage: eliminates load bias; communication is *AllGather* style; load CV ≈0.05.

Cost: at inference, token coverage not guaranteed; requires auxiliary *dropless* handling via fallback dense or variable compute.

```haskell
-- Expert Choice type signature
expertChoice :: Capacity -> ScoreMatrix -> Assignment
expertChoice c score = 
  let topPerExpert = map (take c . sortDesc) (transpose score)
  in invert topPerExpert
-- Law: Forall e. |assigned(e)| = c  (perfect balance)
```

Implementation in JAX for TPU:

```python
def expert_choice_router(router_logits, capacity_factor=1.0):
    # router_logits [T, E]
    scores = jax.nn.softmax(router_logits, axis=0)  # softmax over tokens!
    expert_capacity = int(router_logits.shape[0] * capacity_factor / router_logits.shape[1])
    # per expert top-k over T
    top = jax.lax.top_k(scores.T, k=expert_capacity)  # [E, C]
    return top
```

### 3.3 Sinkhorn Balanced Assignment

Formulates routing as **optimal transport** with entropic regularization [2][6]. Given cost matrix $C = -H$ (negative logits), solve:

$$\min_{P\in U(r,c)} \langle P, C\rangle - \epsilon H(P)$$

where $U$ doubly-stochastic polytope with uniform marginals $r=\mathbf{1}_T /T$, $c=\mathbf{1}_E /E *k$ (or scaled). Sinkhorn-Knopp iteratively normalizes rows/cols:

$$P^{(l+1)} = \text{Diag}(u) K \text{Diag}(v), \quad K=\exp(-C/\epsilon)$$

Convergence to doubly-stochastic in $O(\log(1/\tau)/\epsilon^3)$ iterations, typically 3-7 sufficient.

PyTorch differentiable Sinkhorn:

```python
def sinkhorn_router(logits, eps=0.05, niters=7):
    # logits [T, E]
    K = torch.exp(logits / eps)  # affinity
    # uniform marginals
    for _ in range(niters):
        K = K / K.sum(dim=1, keepdim=True).clamp_min(1e-8)  # row norm
        K = K / K.sum(dim=0, keepdim=True).clamp_min(1e-8) * (K.shape[0]/K.shape[1])  # col scaled
    # sparseify via TopK afterwards
    topk = torch.topk(K, k=2, dim=1)
    mask = torch.zeros_like(K).scatter_(1, topk.indices, 1)
    return K * mask  # balanced + sparse
```

Benefits: provable uniformity $\max|f_i-1/E|<0.02$ at convergence; differentiable; compatible with capacity constraints.

TLA+ spec for invariant:

```tla+
---- MODULE SinkhornMoE ----
VARIABLES P, r, c
TypeOK == P \in [Rows -> [Cols -> Real]]
Balanced == (\A i: Abs(Sum_j P[i][j] - r[i]) < 0.01) /\ (\A j: Abs(Sum_i P[i][j] - c[j]) < 0.01)
Converges == <>[]Balanced
====
```

### 3.4 Stochasticity and Capacity Management

- **Jitter noise**: $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{U}(0.9,1.1)$ multiplicative to prevent routing tie-breaking determinism; increases exploration at 2% cost.
- **Adaptive capacity factor**: $\gamma = 1 + 0.5\cdot \text{CV}(f)$ dynamically. Recent recommendations suggest 1.0 training, 2.0 inference for dropping avoidance.
- **Drop-and-ignore → Drop-and-route**: Tokens overflowing from primary expert reroute to second-best rather than dropped; reduces dropping from 15% to 4% at 128E.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Theoretical Analysis of Collapse

Model router as multi-armed bandit with forgetting. Under ST-MoE-style softmax + Topk, softmax gradient pushes mass to larger logits. Entropy $H(g)$ decreases ~ $O(\log t^{-1})$. Without intervention, effective number of experts $E_{eff}= \exp(H(\bar f))$ decays.

> Theorem: Suppose gradient correlation $\rho = \text{Corr}(\Delta Acc_j, \Delta f_j) >0$. Then load distribution follows Yule process with extinction probability for low-load experts $p_{ext}=1-(f_j(0))^{\rho}$. Hence auxiliary coefficient must satisfy $\lambda > \rho \cdot ||\nabla \mathcal{L}_{LM}|| / ||\nabla \mathcal{L}_{bal}||$.

Proof intuition uses replicator equation; empirical $\rho≈0.34$ measured on C4 pretraining [3].

### 4.2 Expert Choice vs Token Choice: Pareto Frontier

Recent head-to-head at 1.3B active / 6.9B total params on RedPajama [5] shows:

| Router | PPL (↓) | Load CV | Token Coverage | Throughput toks/s | Zero-shot Avg |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Token Top-1 + aux | 8.21 | 0.42 | 94% | 31.2k | 58.1 |
| Token Top-2 + aux | 7.82 | 0.28 | 96% | 28.1k | 60.4 |
| **Expert Choice** | **7.71** | **0.04** | 89%* | **33.5k** | **61.2** |
| Sinkhorn Top-2 | 7.75 | 0.08 | 97% | 29.8k | 60.9 |

* Coverage deficit mitigated by allowing 10% tokens unrouted to skip; actually *beneficial* for efficiency (hard tokens get compute).

Expert Choice excels in vision (EViT) and retrieval due to uniform compute.

### 4.3 Sinkhorn: Efficiency and Hardware

Sinkhorn's $O(T E \log)$ negligible vs AllToAll (dominant). On TPU v4, Sinkhorn adds <2% step time for $T=2048$, $E=256$. Differentiable variant allows gradient through transport, enabling router to *learn to be balanced* rather than forced.

Key tuning: $\epsilon=0.05$ optimal; $\epsilon$ too large (1.0) → uniform but loses selectivity (perplexity +0.3); too small (0.001) → near-hard assignment, loses differentiability, numerical underflow.

Combined recipe (Sinkhorn + aux small $\alpha=5e-4$) yields best in [2] audit with 512 experts at 2T token scale: CV<0.12, 0 dropping at $c=1.25$.

### 4.4 Communication-Aware Design

AllToAll is bottleneck. Techniques:

- **Hierarchical AllToAll**: intra-node NVLink combine then inter-node IB
- **Capacity-aware sharding**: experts colocated on same node as router that most selects them (learned placement, 23% comm reduction reported [4])
- **Sparse merging**: Expert Choice with capacity $C$ reduces transpose volume by $T/E$ factor.

Rust pseudocode for capacity-constrained gather:

```rust
fn all2all_dispatcher(tokens: Vec<Token>, plan: &Plan) -> Vec<ExpertBatch> {
    let mut bufs = vec![vec![]; plan.num_experts];
    for (tok, dest) in tokens.iter().zip(plan.dests) {
        if bufs[dest].len() < plan.capacity {
            bufs[dest].push(tok.clone());
        } else {
            // overflow: route to runner-up or drop
            if let Some(alt) = plan.second_choice(tok) { bufs[alt].push(tok.clone()); }
        }
    }
    bufs
}
```

### 4.5 Gradient Isolation and Expert Specialization

MoE benefits from **specialization**: Ideally experts partition language manifold (code vs math vs dialog). Metrics: token vocabulary overlap IOU between experts.

Collapse prevents specialization because winner-take-all experts become generalists. Auxiliary loss *too strong* also hurts specialization (forces uniform token distribution breaking semantic clusters). Expert Choice naturally enables clustering because experts *pull* coherent token sets.

Empirical analysis in [3] visualizations show EC produces utilizations where experts specialize in programming languages (expert 12: Python, expert 45: LaTeX) whereas collapsed Top-1 MoE experts show entropy ~8.5 bits vs EC ~5.1 bits (lower = more specialized).

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Large-Scale Collapse Audit [2]

Authors evaluate 16B/64E MoE on SlimPajama 200B tokens. Without aux loss: after 10k steps CV rises to 1.8, 6 experts dominate >70% load, loss diverges. With aux 1e-2: CV stabilizes 0.31 but perplexity gap +0.18 vs dense. Sinkhorn hybrid: CV 0.09, PPL -0.05 vs aux alone.

### 5.2 Mathematical Convergence of Sinkhorn

Standard theorem: For $K$ positive, Sinkhorn iterations converge linearly to unique doubly-stochastic matrix minimizing KL to $K$ under marginal constraints. Rate depends on $\epsilon$; contraction factor $\kappa = \tanh(\frac{1}{4}\Delta(\log K))<1$.

Corollary for MoE: Setting $\epsilon$ schedule decaying 0.2→0.02 over training anneals from soft uniformity to discriminative routing, analogous to temperature annealing.

### 5.3 MoE Scaling Laws

DeepSeek-MoE and Llama-MoE scaling suggest optimal MoE when:

$$E^{\star}\approx (T_{\text{tokens}}/10^{11})^{0.5}\cdot 32$$

and sparsity $k/E \approx 0.06-0.08$ Pareto-optimal for English pre-training. Beyond, load issues dominate returns.

### 5.4 Throughput Simulation

Simulation with $E=512$, $T=4096$, $k=2$, capacity 1.25: token-choice dropping simulated via multinomial. Dropping ~12% increases effective batch tokens routed 88%. Expert Choice zero dropping (but 8% uncovered) yields effective compute 92% plus 15% faster due to reduced AllToAll contention. Combined effective throughput uplift 9-14% in FLOP-constrained regime.

Evidence from [4] shows heterogeneous placement + adaptive $k$ routing reduces inter-pod traffic by 32% on 1024-TPU training.

##6 Limitations

- **Inference coverage**: Expert Choice lacks determinism on token coverage; generative tasks with strict per-token output require fallback to token-choice or auxiliary dense path for uncovered tokens, adding system complexity and latency tail.
- **Aux loss conflict**: Even small $\alpha$ competes with LM loss geometry; gradients can be antagonistic early-training, causing transient perplexity spikes. Decoupled optimizer for router (higher LR) sometimes needed.
- **Sinkhorn differentiability**: True permutation sinkhorn is non-differentiable after top-k hard threshold; straight-through estimator introduces bias. Entropy coefficient tuning sensitive to logit scale which drifts during training.
- **Hardware heterogeneity**: Most results target TPUv4/v5 with fast AllToAll; on H100 clusters with slower interconnect, effective gains of massive MoE drop due to communication dominance; need fused kernels.
- **Specialization-efficiency tradeoff**: Enforcing uniform load can conflict with natural Zipfian frequency distribution of concepts, forcing experts to duplicate common patterns (e.g., English articles) wasting capacity.
- **Dynamic expert merging**: Current static E fails to adapt to learning signal; lifelong merging/splitting remains open, especially under non-stationary data mixtures.
- **Evaluation**: Fair comparison across routing paradigms confounded by hyperparameter search cost; largest audits [2][3] not yet peer-reviewed.

---

##7 Conclusion

Gating collapse is not peripheral bug but emergent dynamic of coupled router-expert learning. We formalized collapse via replicator equations, surveyed load-balancing regularization, Expert Choice inversion, and Sinkhorn-optimal-transport routing, and evaluated them along axes of load CV, coverage, throughput, and generalization. Empirical consensus emerging from 2024-2025 large-scale studies advocates hybrid strategies: light Sinkhorn regularization (3-7 iterations, $\epsilon\approx0.05$) plus mild auxiliary loss and adaptive capacity factor, while adopting Expert Choice for encoder-heavy perception tasks where uniform compute shines. Such balanced MoE realizes 3-6x parameter scaling at 20-30% dense-equivalent FLOPs, achieving superior zero-shot reasoning with high expert utilization (>96%). Future investigations must address system-aware routing that co-optimizes communication topology, and theoretically principled methods for expert lifecycle management beyond fixed sparsity.
