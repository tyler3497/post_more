# Homotopy Type Theory: Univalence, Higher Inductive Types, and the Computational Content of Paths

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf's identity types as *path spaces*, making dependent type theory a synthetic language for homotopy theory. This thesis surveys the three pillars of the subject: the univalence axiom of Voevodsky, which identifies equality of types with homotopy equivalence and thereby internalizes the principle that isomorphic structures may be identified [1][2]; higher inductive types (HITs), which extend ordinary inductive definitions with *path constructors* and thereby provide direct logical descriptions of spheres, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts [4]; and cubical type theory, which gives univalence computational content by interpreting identity as maps out of a formal interval equipped with Kan composition operations [3][5]. We develop the circle, suspensions, and truncations as HITs, present the Glue-type construction that renders univalence provable with computation rules, and survey synthetic homotopy theory: the encode-decode computation of π₁(S¹), the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the Blakers–Massey theorem, and Brunerie's synthetic proof that π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ [7], now verified in Cubical Agda [8]. We close with limitations — decidability and universes — and argue that HoTT with computational univalence is a serious alternative foundation of mathematics.

## 1 Introduction

The foundational crisis of the twenty-first century is not about consistency but about *computability of proofs*. Classical set-theoretic foundations record the truth of mathematical statements while discarding the computational content of their proofs; Martin-Löf type theory (MLTT) retains that content but could not, until recently, express even elementary homotopy theory [1]. The discovery that the *identity types* of MLTT carry an intrinsic homotopical structure — types as spaces, terms as points, identity proofs as paths — changed the field's ambitions entirely.

Three innovations made the change effective. First, Voevodsky's **univalence axiom** imposes that the canonical map `idtoeqv : (A =ᵤ B) → (A ≃ B)` is itself an equivalence, so that paths in a universe correspond exactly to homotopy equivalences between its points [2]. This turns the informal mathematical practice of treating isomorphic objects as interchangeable into a theorem-bearing, machine-checkable axiom. Second, **higher inductive types** generalize inductive definitions by permitting constructors that produce *paths*, not merely points, so that the circle S¹, suspensions ΣA, and truncations ∥A∥ₙ can be declared directly rather than encoded [4]. Third, **cubical type theory** replaces the axiomatic treatment of univalence with a constructive one: paths are functions out of a formal interval 𝕀, and a composition operation yields transport, function extensionality, and univalence as *theorems with computational behavior* [3][5][6].

This thesis presents these three pillars with proofs, contrasts the axiomatic and cubical approaches to computation, and surveys the synthetic homotopy theory that the framework has produced — culminating in the computation of π₄(S³), a result whose synthetic proof is fully constructive and whose complete formalization in Cubical Agda only recently closed [8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf identity types

In MLTT every type `A` admits an *identity type* family `Id_A : A → A → U`, generated by reflexivity:

```agda
data _≡_ {A : Type} : A → A → Type where
  refl : (x : A) → x ≡ x
```

The eliminator `J` (path induction) states that to prove `C : Π (x y : A), x ≡ y → U` it suffices to handle the reflexivity case:

```agda
J : (C : (x y : A) → x ≡ y → Type)
  → ((x : A) → C x x (refl x))
  → (x y : A) (p : x ≡ y) → C x y p
```

with the computation rule `J C c x x (refl x) ≡ c x`. This is *intensional*: from `p : x ≡ y` one cannot conclude `x` and `y` are definitionally equal, and the uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP) is not derivable [1]. The *loop space* `Ω(A, a) = (a ≡ a)` already suggests homotopical content: in the homotopy interpretation, `x ≡ y` is the space of paths from `x` to `y`.

> **Theorem (Transport):** For `P : A → U` and `p : x ≡ y`, there is `transport p : P x → P y`, defined by path induction sending `refl x` to the identity function. Consequently, every definable operation respects equality — a property univalence will extend to types themselves [1][2].

### 2.2 The homotopy interpretation

Voevodsky's model of MLTT in Kan simplicial sets interprets types as spaces, terms as points, and identity proofs as paths [2]. Under this interpretation, `Id_A(x,y)` is the path space, `Ωⁿ(A,a)` is the `n`-fold loop space, and the type of *equivalences* `A ≃ B` corresponds to homotopy equivalences. Crucially, the universes `Uᵢ` themselves become objects of study: their identity types `A =ᵤ B` are *path spaces of spaces*, i.e., spaces of identifications between spaces. The univalence axiom asserts that this path space coincides with the space of equivalences.

### 2.3 Intensionality vs. extensionality

Intensional type theory keeps equality of functions proof-relevant in a weak sense: function extensionality (`funext : (f ∼ g) → f ≡ g`) is not provable in bare MLTT, and neither is propositional extensionality. Univalence implies both — and in cubical type theory, function extensionality is provable *directly*, without any axiom [3].

| Principle | MLTT (axiomatic) | Cubical TT |
|---|---|---|
| Function extensionality | Unprovable; follows from univalence | Provable directly [3] |
| Propositional extensionality | Unprovable; follows from univalence | Provable directly |
| Univalence | Added as axiom | *Theorem* via `Glue` [3] |
| Canonicity with univalence | Lost (axioms block computation) | Recovered: closed naturals reduce to numerals (Huber) [5] |
| HITs | Postulated with propositional computation | Judgmental computation rules for all constructors [4] |

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *synthetic*: we work inside type theory as the ambient logic and develop homotopy theory axiomatically, never referring to topological spaces, simplices, or model categories in definitions or proofs. Results are then justified *semantically* by models — the simplicial model of Voevodsky–Kapulkin–Lumsdaine and the cubical models of Bezem–Coquand–Huber [6] and Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg [3] — and *computationally* by the operational semantics of cubical type theory. This dual justification is essential: synthetic proofs are transported automatically across models, while cubical semantics guarantees that closed terms compute.

Concretely, we (i) define the circle and suspensions as HITs and derive their induction principles; (ii) state univalence and derive the structure identity principle; (iii) present the cubical primitives (interval, composition, Glue) and show how they compute univalence; (iv) survey synthetic results (π₁(S¹), Freudenthal, Blakers–Massey, Brunerie's π₄(S³)) that validate the methodology.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Identity types as path spaces

Path induction makes paths behave like *based path spaces are contractible*: the total space `Σ (y : A), x ≡ y` is contractible, with center `(x, refl x)`. This single fact generates the groupoid structure of every type:

- **Inversion:** `p⁻¹ : y ≡ x` from `p : x ≡ y`.
- **Concatenation:** `p ∙ q : x ≡ z` from `p : x ≡ y`, `q : y ≡ z`.
- **Coherence:** associativity, unit, and inverse laws hold *propositionally* — as higher paths — not definitionally. The tower of coherences (paths between paths between paths…) is exactly what makes types into *∞-groupoids* [1].

Homotopy levels organize types by the complexity of their path spaces. A type is a *mere proposition* (h-level −1) if all its paths are equal; a *set* (h-level 0) if its path spaces are propositions; a *1-type* if its path spaces are sets, and so on. Universes are not sets: `U` contains types with nontrivial higher structure, and univalence will show its path spaces are as rich as equivalence types.

> **Theorem (Encode–decode pattern):** To characterize `x ≡ y` in a HIT, define a *code* family `code : A → A → U` and exhibit `encode : x ≡ y → code x y` and `decode : code x y → x ≡ y` that are mutually inverse. This pattern computes π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ and underlies the Blakers–Massey proof [1].

### 4.2 The univalence axiom

Let `A ≃ B = Σ (f : A → B), isEquiv f`, where `isEquiv f` is the proposition that every fiber of `f` is contractible. Path induction on `refl A` defines

```agda
idtoeqv : (A =ᵤ B) → (A ≃ B)
idtoeqv (refl A) = (id , idIsEquiv)
```

> **Axiom (Univalence, Voevodsky):** The map `idtoeqv : (A =ᵤ B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence. Equivalently, `(A =ᵤ B) ≃ (A ≃ B)` [1][2].

Consequences are far-reaching:

1. **Function extensionality** and **propositional extensionality** become theorems.
2. **Transport of structure:** for any type family `P : U → U` and equivalence `e : A ≃ B`, one obtains `P A → P B` by transporting along `ua e : A =ᵤ B`, where `ua` is the inverse of `idtoeqv`. Mathematical structures automatically transfer across equivalences — the formal content of the informal practice of "identifying isomorphic objects" [2].
3. **Structure identity principle:** for structured types (groups, categories, …), identifications coincide with structure-preserving isomorphisms [1].

The price of the axiom in Book HoTT is *canonicity*: a closed term of type `ℕ` built using `ua` is stuck, since the axiom has no computation rule. Cubical type theory pays this debt.

### 4.3 Higher inductive types: the circle, suspensions, truncations

Higher inductive types extend inductive definitions with *path constructors* — generators for the path spaces, not just the points [4]:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator says that maps out of `S¹` are determined by a point and a loop:

```agda
S¹-rec : (B : Type) (b : B) (l : b ≡ b) → S¹ → B
S¹-rec B b l base      = b
S¹-rec B b l (loop i)  = l i        -- in cubical syntax
```

In Book HoTT the second computation rule holds only propositionally; in cubical type theory it holds *judgmentally* [4]. Other HITs follow the same schema:

- **Suspension** `ΣA`: points `north, south : ΣA` and `merid : A → north ≡ south`. Spheres arise by iteration: `S⁰ = 𝟚`, `Sⁿ⁺¹ = Σ(Sⁿ)`; indeed `S¹ ≃ Σ𝟚` [1][4].
- **Propositional truncation** `∥A∥`: constructor `|–| : A → ∥A∥` plus `sq : Π (x y : ∥A∥), x ≡ y`. It internalizes *mere existence*: `∃ (x : A), P x = ∥ Σ (x : A), P x ∥`, and defines homotopy groups `πₙ(A) = ∥ Ωⁿ(A) ∥₀` [1].
- **Pushouts**, **joins**, and **n-truncations** `∥A∥ₙ` complete the toolkit; all are given judgmental computation rules in the cubical setting [4].

```haskell
-- conceptual rendering of the truncation eliminator
truncRec :: IsProp B => (A -> B) -> (Trunc A -> B)
truncRec f (TruncIntro a) = f a
-- well-defined because B is a proposition: all paths are equal
```

### 4.4 Cubical type theory and computational univalence

Cubical type theory (CCHM) internalizes the cubical-sets model [3][6]. Its ingredients:

- A formal **interval** `𝕀` with endpoints `i0, i1` and De Morgan operations `∧, ∨, ¬`.
- **Paths** as functions: `Path A a b` is `p : 𝕀 → A` with `p i0 = a`, `p i1 = b`. Identity becomes a special case of function type, so *function extensionality is immediate*.
- **Composition** `comp`: given a partial cube (an open box), fill it — this yields transport, concatenation, and inverses with definitional behavior.
- **Glue types**: `Glue [φ ↦ (T, e)] A` glues a partial equivalence `e : T ≃ A` onto `A`, constructing a type that is `T` on the face `φ` and `A` elsewhere. Univalence is then *proved*: for `e : A ≃ B`, the path `ua e : Path U A B` is `λ i → Glue [i = i0 ↦ (A, e), i = i1 ↦ (B, idEquiv)] B`-style, with the property that `transport (ua e)` computes as `e` applied [3].

The metatheory is constructive: Huber proved **canonicity** — every closed term of natural-number type reduces to a numeral — so univalence no longer blocks computation [5]. Orton and Pitts axiomatized the requirements on the interval object in an arbitrary topos, clarifying the *uniform Kan filling* at the heart of the construction [5]. Cubical Agda implements this theory with native univalence and HITs, enabling the large-scale formalizations of the next section [8][9].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ — the encode–decode method

Define `code : S¹ → Type` by `code base = ℤ` and `ap code loop = ua succEquiv`, using univalence to turn the successor equivalence on `ℤ` into a path in the universe. Then `transport code loop` acts as successor, and one proves `(base ≡ x) ≃ code x` by circle induction. Setting `x = base` gives `Ω(S¹) ≃ ℤ`, i.e., `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` [1]. The proof is a template: Blakers–Massey and the Freudenthal theorem are its large-scale descendants.

### 5.2 The Hopf fibration and Freudenthal

The Hopf fibration is defined as a HIT-style family over `S²` with fiber `S¹`, and its total space is shown equivalent to `S³`. From it follows `π₂(S²) ≅ ℤ` and, via the James construction, the **Freudenthal suspension theorem**: the suspension map `πₖ(Sⁿ) → πₖ₊₁(Sⁿ⁺¹)` is an isomorphism for `k < 2n − 1` [7]. The **Blakers–Massey theorem** — characterizing the connectivity of pushouts — was likewise proved synthetically, demonstrating that classical connectivity arguments survive translation into path induction [1].

### 5.3 Brunerie's π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ

The landmark result of synthetic homotopy theory is Brunerie's thesis [7]: a fully constructive, purely homotopy-theoretic proof that `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ`. The argument proceeds in stages:

1. **The Brunerie number.** Using the James construction and Freudenthal, Brunerie shows there exists `β : ℕ` with `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/βℤ` — without computing `β`. Remarkably, the proof defines `β` but does not evaluate it; closedness under the univalence axiom (pre-cubical) blocked reduction.
2. **Narrowing.** The Hopf invariant, defined via the cup product on cohomology `H*(–; ℤ)` constructed synthetically from Eilenberg–MacLane spaces `K(ℤ, n) = ∥Sⁿ∥ₙ`, shows `β ∈ {1, 2}` [7].
3. **The Gysin sequence.** Computing the cohomology ring of `ℂP²` via the Gysin exact sequence of the Hopf fibration `S¹ → S⁵ → ℂP²` forces `β = 2`, hence `π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ`, and more generally `πₙ₊₁(Sⁿ) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ` for `n ≥ 3` [7].

Because the proof is constructive, `β` is in principle a *program* whose value is 2. Ljungström and Mörtberg formalized Brunerie's argument in Cubical Agda — where univalence computes — and thereby *evaluated* the Brunerie number, confirming `β = 2` by normalization [8]. This is arguably the deepest computation ever extracted from a synthetic homotopy proof, and it validates the entire cubical program: the axiom became a program.

| Result | Classical proof | Synthetic status |
|---|---|---|
| π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ | Covering spaces | Encode–decode in Book HoTT [1] |
| Freudenthal suspension | Excision / Serre spectral sequence | James construction, synthetic [7] |
| Blakers–Massey | Triad connectivity | Formalized synthetically [1] |
| π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ | Serre (1951), spectral sequences | Brunerie thesis [7]; computed in Cubical Agda [8] |
| Symmetric monoidal smash product | Point-set topology | Fully formalized, 1-coherent [8] |

## 6 Limitations

1. **Decidability and performance.** Cubical type checking is decidable, but composition and Glue-heavy terms can be expensive; large formalizations (e.g., the smash-product coherence [8]) stress current implementations.
2. **Universes.** Univalence is stated per universe; the interaction of cumulativity, universe polymorphism, and Glue remains delicate, and "univalent universes side by side with non-univalent ones" complicates library design [2].
3. **Cubical vs. simplicial.** Cubical sets model the theory constructively, but some classical arguments (e.g., certain spectral-sequence computations) have no known direct synthetic translation; the dictionary between synthetic and analytic homotopy theory is incomplete.
4. **Higher coherences.** HITs with higher path constructors (e.g., the torus with a 2-dimensional coherence, localizations) require careful handling; schema-level general theories of HITs are still being consolidated [4].
5. **Semantics vs. syntax.** The simplicial model justifies consistency but is classical; the cubical model is constructive but its metatheory (normalization, decidability) is technically demanding [5][6].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began as an interpretation and became a foundation. The univalence axiom internalizes the structuralist maxim that equivalent objects are identical [1][2]; higher inductive types give direct logical access to the spaces of homotopy theory [4]; and cubical type theory converts the axiom into a *program*, restoring canonicity and making function extensionality, transport, and univalence compute [3][5][6]. The synthetic proofs — from the encode–decode computation of π₁(S¹) to Brunerie's π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ and its machine-checked evaluation in Cubical Agda [7][8] — demonstrate that this is not merely a philosophical proposal but a working mathematical practice. The remaining challenges are engineering and scope: performance, universes, and the completeness of the synthetic–analytic dictionary. If univalent foundations eventually replace set theory as the implicit foundation of working mathematics [1], it will be because of results like these — proofs that are at once classical in ambition, constructive in content, and computational in execution.
