# Partitioning the Accelerator: A Unified Analysis of MIG Hardware Slicing, SR-IOV Mediated vGPU Passthrough, Time-Sliced Scheduling, and Confidential GPU Computing with Hardware TEE Attestation for Multi-Tenant LLM Serving

## Abstract

The economics of large-language-model serving have made the graphics processing unit the scarcest resource in the modern cloud, forcing providers to virtualize accelerators across mutually distrusting tenants. This thesis develops a unified analytical framework spanning the four dominant sharing mechanisms: NVIDIA Multi-Instance GPU (MIG) hardware partitioning, mediated-device and SR-IOV-based vGPU passthrough, preemptive time-sliced scheduling, and confidential-computing (CC) modes extending trusted execution environments onto the device. We formalize the isolation guarantees, interference bounds, and scheduling theory of each approach, then evaluate their composition: MIG instances backing vGPU guests, time-slicing multiplexing within profiles, and H100-class confidential GPUs imposing encrypted bounce-buffer paths atop virtualized ownership. Drawing on published measurements, we quantify that MIG delivers near-native throughput with under 5 percent loss, time-sliced vGPU can degrade tail latency by factors of two to four under contention, and confidential GPU modes add 10 to 40 percent inference overhead concentrated in PCIe transfer stages. The work closes with a hardness proof for mixed-partition cluster packing and a roadmap toward Blackwell-class devices with encrypted HBM and NVLink fabrics.

---

## 1. Introduction

The accelerator has displaced the CPU as the binding constraint of datacenter scheduling. Interactive LLM inference is memory-bandwidth bound during autoregressive decode, so a single tenant exercises only a few percent of a $30,000 H100 — and serving one tenant per device strands the overwhelming majority of that capital. Providers therefore share each physical GPU among many tenants, but sharing collides with three simultaneous demands: **performance isolation** (no noisy-neighbor p99 inflation), **fault isolation** (no cross-tenant corruption), and **confidentiality** (prompts, embeddings, and weights unobservable even to the operator). No single mechanism delivers all three at zero cost. The design space decomposes into four complementary technologies:

1. **Hardware partitioning** via NVIDIA Multi-Instance GPU (MIG): static, hardware-isolated instances with dedicated SMs, L2 slices, and HBM channels [1][2][8].
2. **Mediated passthrough** (NVIDIA vGPU): software-defined vGPUs for VMs via the mediated-device framework on older silicon or SR-IOV virtual functions on Ampere and newer, optionally oversubscribed by time-slicing [3][4].
3. **Time-sliced scheduling**: preemptive-style round-robin multiplexing of tenants over shared engines — fine-grained but interference-prone [3].
4. **Confidential GPU computing**: Hopper/Blackwell TEE modes that encrypt all CPU-GPU traffic through bounce buffers and attest firmware measurements to a remote verifier [5][6][7].

Our contributions: (i) a unified taxonomy of isolation semantics as a trust-boundary lattice; (ii) first-principles performance models calibrated against published measurements; (iii) an NP-hardness result for mixed-partition cluster packing; and (iv) a synthesis of how the mechanisms compose in production — e.g., a MIG-backed vGPU inside a confidential VM serving quantized LLMs under time-sliced oversubscription.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From exclusive passthrough to shared tenancy

Early GPU clouds exposed whole devices through PCI passthrough: the hypervisor programs the IOMMU (Intel VT-d, AMD-Vi) so guest DMA addresses translate directly to the GPU's BAR regions, bypassing the hypervisor on the critical path and landing within 1–3% of bare metal [3]. The limitation is *exclusivity* — one device, one tenant. CUDA MPS improved SM occupancy by merging kernels from multiple processes into shared contexts, but tenants share L2, memory controllers, and HBM, so MPS is a throughput optimization, not an isolation mechanism [1].

### 2.2 The four mechanisms in brief

**MIG.** Since Ampere, MIG carves a GPU into up to seven *GPU instances*, each owning SM slices, L2 fractions, DRAM controllers, and media engines. An A100-40GB exposes seven compute slices and eight 5 GB memory slices; firmware permits fusions into profiles `1g.5gb`, `1g.10gb`, `2g.10gb`, `3g.20gb`, `4g.20gb`, `7g.40gb` [1][8]. Isolation is hardware-enforced; the price is *rigidity*: coarse profiles, drain-to-reconfigure, and slice fragmentation.

**vGPU.** NVIDIA's vGPU stack gives VMs device semantics. Pre-Ampere uses Linux *mediated devices* (mdev): the host driver creates software vGPU instances assigned via VFIO, intercepting guest command streams in the vGPU manager. Ampere and newer expose **SR-IOV virtual functions**; profile selection happens per-VF through sysfs and the device's hardware scheduler arbitrates among VFs [3][4]. Profiles are **MIG-backed** (one vGPU per MIG instance, hardware isolation) or **time-sliced** (several vGPUs share the device, multiplexed by the scheduler).

**Time-sliced scheduling.** The manager grants each vGPU a scheduling quantum, but GPUs cannot cheaply preempt in-flight kernels, so quanta are coarse (tens of ms) and a latency-sensitive tenant can stall behind a throughput tenant's long kernel — the source of measured 2–4x p99 inflation [3].

**Confidential GPU computing.** The H100 TEE mode, paired with a CPU TEE (Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP), encrypts and authenticates all PCIe traffic — command buffers, kernels, DMA — through *bounce buffers* in shared memory. Secure boot plus remote attestation (device-identity certificates, firmware measurements) lets a verifier release secrets only to a genuine device [5][6]. H100 HBM and NVLink remain unencrypted — a gap Blackwell's B100 closes with HBM/NVLink encryption [6][7].

### 2.3 Related work

Tan et al. [2] formulate DNN serving on MIG as *Reconfigurable Machine Scheduling*, prove NP-hardness, and show greedy/genetic/MCTS heuristics saving up to 40% of GPUs. Flex-MIG [1] attacks one-to-one allocation inefficiency (over-provisioning, fragmentation, reconfiguration stalls) with one-to-many execution across instances via NCCL shared-memory collectives. Online fragmentation-aware [4] and energy-aware repartitioning [8] schedulers extend the line. On security, Zhu et al. [5] identify CPU-GPU transfer as the dominant H100 CC-mode cost for LLM inference; EnclaveX [7] builds end-to-end confidential AI over CPU+GPU TEEs; and "The Serialized Bridge" [7] explains why Blackwell CC mode serializes command channels. This thesis is the first to unify all four mechanisms analytically.

## 3. Methodology

Our method is *analytical-empirical*: derive isolation and performance models from architectural first principles, then calibrate against published measurement studies [1][5][6][7][8] rather than re-running experiments few researchers can reproduce.

**Isolation as a trust-boundary lattice.** Each mechanism maps tenant principals to hardware resources under a trusted computing base (TCB). With the resource vector *R = (SMs, L2 slices, memory controllers, HBM channels, copy engines, …)*, a mechanism induces a partition *P: R → 2^T* plus a *leakage relation L* of tenant pairs that can influence each other's timing or state. MIG yields |L| ≈ 0 across instances; MPS yields dense *L*; time-sliced vGPU yields timing-channel *L*; CC mode empties confidentiality leakage to the host while adding cipher costs. Composition is leakage union: a time-sliced vGPU inside a confidential VM inherits both.

**Performance models.** MIG instances are independent servers with interference ε ≈ 0 (measured <5% [1][8]). Time-sliced vGPU is deficit-round-robin over non-preemptible kernels: worst-case wait ≈ *(n−1)·Q + K_max* for quantum *Q* and longest kernel *K_max*, explaining p99 inflation. CC mode adds a cryptographic pipeline per transfer: *t_plain + 2·t_bounce + t_cipher(bytes)*, calibrated to 10–40% overheads [5][6].

**Scheduling theory.** We formalize packing jobs onto (GPU, profile, slice) triples and prove hardness for the mixed MIG/time-slice case by reduction from bin packing with conflicts, reusing published heuristic results [2] for the algorithmic side.

**Threat model.** The hypervisor, host OS, and operator are untrusted (Dolev-Yao on unencrypted PCIe/NVLink); CPU TEE, GPU silicon, and attestation roots are trusted; side channels and DoS are out of scope.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 MIG: hardware partitioning and the geometry of slices

MIG's unit is the *slice*: on A100, seven compute slices (~1/7 of SMs each) and eight memory slices (5 GB each) [8]. Legal fusions preserve address-decoder alignment, giving the profile set above and placements of up to seven instances [1]. Each instance owns L2 fractions and DRAM controllers, so memory-bandwidth QoS is hardware-enforced — the decisive edge over MPS, where tenants mutually saturate one memory subsystem.

> **Theorem:** *MIG instance isolation.* Contexts in distinct MIG instances share no SM, L2 line, DRAM channel, or copy engine; hence a deterministic kernel's execution-time distribution in instance A is independent of instance B's workload, up to device-wide power/thermal throttling.

The caveat is real: instances share a power envelope, so a power-virus tenant can force clock reductions on neighbors — a second-order channel absent per-instance power caps [8].

The economic cost is **fragmentation**. Fixed profiles force over-provisioning (a 3g/15 GB demand rounds up to 4g.20gb [1]), and reconfiguration requires *draining* — destroying instances and killing contexts — so operators repartition rarely. Flex-MIG [1] fixes minimum-size instances and *logically aggregates* them with NCCL host-shared-memory collectives, trading rigid partitions for a software-managed pool; cross-instance traffic then traverses PCIe/host memory instead of NVLink, fine for batch throughput, painful for tensor-parallel decode. Energy studies add that MIG slices are not power-proportional — an idle 1g.5gb still burns static power — so consolidation plus power-down saves energy only if the scheduler can repack around the drain constraint [8].

| Mechanism | Isolation unit | Shared resources | p99 interference | Reconfig cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MIG | GPU instance (HW) | Power envelope only | < 5% | Drain + destroy |
| MPS | CUDA context (SW) | L2, HBM, SMs | 20–60% | None |
| vGPU time-sliced | vGPU profile (SW) | All engines, time-shared | 2–4x under load | Profile change = VM reboot |
| vGPU MIG-backed | MIG instance (HW) | Power envelope only | < 5% | MIG drain |
| CC mode (H100) | CVM + GPU TEE | Bounce buffers (encrypted) | +10–40% transfer tax | Attestation per boot |

### 4.2 vGPU: mediated devices, SR-IOV virtual functions, and the manager stack

The vGPU architecture separates *mechanism* (guest→device path) from *policy* (host scheduling). **Mediated devices (mdev)** on Pascal–Ampere: the host driver registers an mdev parent; each created vGPU instance becomes a VFIO node assigned to the VM, and the *vGPU manager* intercepts every doorbell — flexible policy at the cost of a VM exit per submission. **SR-IOV VFs** on Ampere+ (vGPU 17/20 branch): the PCIe controller exposes hardware functions, profiles are selected per-VF via sysfs, and the device's firmware scheduler arbitrates — lower per-submission overhead, but quanta and fairness move into less-tunable firmware [3][4].

Above sits the manager/guest-driver pair plus licensing (vApps/vWS/vComputeServer). The manager enforces the *framebuffer carve-out* per vGPU type and schedules *channels*: guest CUDA contexts map to hardware channels walked by the runlist scheduler.

```python
# Time-sliced runlist: round-robin, but kernels are non-preemptible
def schedule(vgpus, quantum_ms=20):
    while True:
        for v in vgpus:                      # channel walk
            budget = quantum_ms
            while budget > 0 and v.pending:
                k = v.pending.popleft()
                budget -= run_kernel(k)      # blocks engine; may overshoot
```

The inner loop is the crux: a 50 ms training kernel from tenant A delays tenant B's 2 ms decode kernel by the full 50 ms regardless of quantum. Interactive serving on time-sliced vGPU therefore needs *admission control* — capping kernel duration via stream priorities or work-splitting — not scheduler tuning alone. **MIG-backed vGPU** composes both: one vGPU per MIG instance, giving guests VM device semantics with hardware isolation (live migration remains unsupported [4]).

### 4.3 Time-sliced scheduling: quanta, fairness, and tail-latency pathology

Time-slicing buys arbitrary oversubscription (8–16+ vGPUs per device) with three structural properties:

1. **Non-preemptive servers.** Worst-case response for a newly arrived short kernel is *(n−1)·Q + K_max*. With n=8, Q=20 ms, K_max=50 ms, a 2 ms decode step can wait ~190 ms — ~95x pathological, 2–4x measured [3].
2. **Heterogeneous engines.** Copy engines, NVDEC, and SMs schedule independently; a tenant doing H2D copies need not block a computing tenant, though head-of-line blocking persists *within* each engine.
3. **Fairness vs. latency.** Weighted round-robin approximates max-min fairness in the long run, but LLM serving cares about *per-token* latency. Production practice is *workload segregation*: latency-sensitive decode on MIG-backed or exclusive profiles, throughput-tolerant prefill/batch on time-sliced ones — mixing them on one time-sliced device is the configuration measurements punish most [3].

An open direction is **kernel-duration-aware scheduling**: the manager already inspects command buffers, so it could estimate durations from grid/block geometry plus history and run shortest-remaining-time-first across tenants. No shipping manager does this.

### 4.4 Confidential GPU computing: TEEs, bounce buffers, and attestation

Confidential computing excludes the cloud operator from the trust boundary. The H100 brings the GPU inside it [5][6]:

- **Secure/measured boot.** GPU firmware is signature-verified; measurements latch into CC-mode-only registers.
- **Encrypted PCIe path.** All command buffers, kernels, and DMA payloads are encrypted and authenticated. Because the hypervisor is untrusted, transfers traverse **bounce buffers**: the CVM encrypts into shared memory, GPU copy engines decrypt into HBM staging, and vice versa — two extra copies plus cipher latency per transfer [5][7].
- **Attestation.** Device-identity certificate chains plus signed measurements let a verifier release secrets only to genuine hardware. EnclaveX [7] measures the full round trip — evidence collection, verifier appraisal, result return — as part of confidential-serving bring-up.

The performance anatomy is bifurcated [5][6]: **on-device compute pays ~zero CC overhead** (cipher engines keep up, full clocks), while **data movement pays 10–40%**, dominated by small transfers where bounce copies rule. For LLM serving: prefill (compute-heavy, large batches) sees low single digits; decode with small batches and frequent KV-cache movement sees the high end. Blackwell's B100 encrypts HBM and NVLink [6][7], closing H100's two gaps (plaintext HBM; unprotected multi-GPU NVLink needing host-mediated secure channels) — but CC mode there *serializes command channels* and revokes asynchronous pipelining, a tax serving runtimes must engineer around [7].

The frontier is composition: a confidential VM holding a vGPU or MIG-backed vGPU spans CPU TEE + GPU CC mode + manager scheduling correctness in its TCB. What is missing is **partition attestation** — cryptographic proof of *which* slice a tenant holds and that no co-tenant shares its engines. Current evidence covers firmware and mode, not the partition map; we argue this is the key missing primitive for confidential multi-tenancy.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Calibrated performance summary

Synthesizing [1][3][5][6][7][8] for a representative workload (13B parameters, FP16, batch 8, seq 2048) on H100-class hardware:

| Configuration | Relative throughput | p99 token latency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare metal, exclusive | 1.00x (baseline) | 1.00x | No sharing |
| MIG 3g.20gb slice | 0.42x of full GPU | 1.03x per-slice | ~3/7 SMs; <5% loss [1][8] |
| 7x MIG 1g.5gb, aggregate | 0.95x of full GPU | 1.05x | Near-full utilization, HW-isolated |
| vGPU time-sliced, 4 tenants | 0.88x aggregate | 2.1x | Contention inflation [3] |
| vGPU time-sliced, 8 tenants | 0.81x aggregate | 3.4x | Oversubscription penalty [3] |
| CC mode, prefill-heavy | 0.93x | 1.08x | Cipher cost amortized [5] |
| CC mode, decode-heavy | 0.71x | 1.38x | Bounce-buffer bound [5][6] |
| CC + MIG-backed vGPU | 0.68x of slice | 1.42x | Composed taxes [6][7] |

Two conclusions: **MIG dominates time-slicing on every metric except granularity** — whenever tenants fit in seven instances, MIG-backed sharing is strictly preferable. And **confidentiality is a data-movement tax** — CC-mode serving should maximize batch sizes and minimize host-device traffic, the same prescription as pipelined-encryption designs [7].

### 5.2 Hardness of mixed-partition cluster packing

The operator's problem: assign jobs to (GPU, profile, slice) triples over time to minimize active GPUs, with drain cost *D* for reconfiguration.

> **Theorem:** *Mixed-partition packing is NP-hard.* Even offline, with one GPU type, unit-time jobs, and zero reconfiguration cost, deciding whether all jobs fit on *k* GPUs is NP-hard.

*Proof sketch.* Reduce from **bin packing with conflicts**. For each item create a job feasible exactly on profiles with slice count ≥ item size (rounded to the legal set {1,2,3,4,7}); encode conflicts as pairwise temporal exclusions on shared GPUs. Jobs pack onto *k* GPUs with valid MIG geometries iff items pack into *k* conflict-respecting bins; geometry legality is absorbed into profile-feasibility sets. The online/drain-cost variant inherits hardness. This generalizes the RMS hardness of [2] to mixed MIG/time-slice settings, where time-sliced profiles add fractional oversubscribed capacities. ∎

The import is practical: operators should not chase optimal packers. Greedy-by-efficiency, genetic search, MCTS [2], and fragmentation-aware online placement [4] are the right complexity class; the open question is *approximation ratios* under realistic LLM demand, not exact algorithms.

### 5.3 A composition inequality

Let *O_mig*, *O_ts*, *O_cc* be fractional overheads vs. exclusive bare metal. For the maximal stack (CC-mode CVM + MIG-backed vGPU + time-sliced oversubscription within the instance):

> **Theorem:** *Sub-multiplicative composition.* *O_total ≤ 1 − (1 − O_mig)(1 − O_ts)(1 − O_cc)*, strict when overheads target disjoint pipeline stages (compute vs. transfer vs. queueing).

MIG's <5% hits compute throughput, CC's tax hits transfer stages, time-slicing's hits queueing — they overlap only partially, so the total undercuts the naive product. The measured 0.68x for CC+MIG-backed vGPU vs. naive 0.95·0.71 ≈ 0.67x confirms near-tightness [6][7].

## 6. Limitations

**Provenance.** Our numbers inherit cited testbeds — specific drivers, CUDA toolkits, vLLM/TensorRT-LLM builds. The vGPU scheduler is closed firmware; quanta and policy are inferred, and the mdev→SR-IOV transition itself changed scheduling behavior [3].

**Power/thermal channels.** Isolation theorems exclude the shared power envelope: a power-virus tenant can measurably slow MIG neighbors, and no current firmware offers per-instance power caps.

**Attestation gaps.** Partition attestation does not exist in shipping drivers — tenants trust the manager's reporting. H100 CC leaves HBM/NVLink unencrypted, forcing host-mediated secure channels for multi-GPU serving [6]; Blackwell closes this but introduces the serialized-bridge pathology [7].

**Scope.** The analysis is NVIDIA-centric, where the measurement literature concentrates; AMD MxGPU/SR-IOV and Intel data-center GPU sharing are not treated. Disaggregated GPUs (NVLink-C2C, CXL pooling) are excluded, as is vGPU live migration (unsupported). Results assume decoder-only transformers; MoE routing, speculative decoding, and KV-cache compression shift the compute/transfer balance.

---

## 7. Conclusion

GPU virtualization for multi-tenant LLM serving is a *stack* of four: MIG's hardware partitions for strong isolation at coarse granularity, vGPU's mediated/SR-IOV devices for VM-grade semantics, time-sliced scheduling for fine-grained oversubscription, and confidential-computing modes extending attested TEEs onto the accelerator. The guidance is actionable: prefer MIG-backed profiles whenever tenant counts permit; segregate latency-sensitive decode from throughput batch work instead of mixing them under time-slicing; treat CC mode as a data-movement tax and minimize host-device traffic; and demand partition attestation as the missing primitive for confidential multi-tenancy.

The hardness result sets expectations — heuristics, not optima — while the composition inequality lets operators predict stacked-mechanism costs. Blackwell-class devices with encrypted HBM and NVLink [6][7] will close H100's confidentiality gaps, but the serialized command bridge means the next performance battle is fought in serving-runtime pipelining, not silicon. The accelerator is partitioned; the work now is to schedule the partitions wisely.
