# Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Geo-Distributed Datacenters: Temporal Load Shifting, CarbonScaler Autoscaling, CACS Optimization, and Scope-3 Embodied Carbon Accounting

## Abstract

Geo-distributed cloud platforms face a ***sustainability paradox***: they optimize for performance and cost while ignoring that grid carbon intensity varies **5-10×** across regions and hours [5][3]. Datacenters already account for 1-1.3% of US electricity, with AI training amplifying load growth. Carbon-aware scheduling exploits this variability via **temporal shifting** (deferring batch work to low-carbon windows), **spatial shifting** (routing to greener regions), and **elastic autoscaling** that varies resource allocation with carbon signals [1][2][5].

This thesis unifies four strands: (i) temporal load shifting taxonomy from Sukprasert et al. [5], (ii) **CarbonScaler** elastic carbon scaling [1][2], (iii) **Google CACS** Carbon-Intelligent Computing with Virtual Capacity Curves (VCCs) [3][4][6], and (iv) **Scope-3 embodied carbon accounting** via Microsoft CHEM [7] and Schneider lifecycle analysis [8]. We formalize deadline-constrained workflow placement as a carbon-aware mapping and scheduling problem, prove NP-hardness for ≥2 processors [cf. CaWoSched/CWM], and present a heuristic combining LSTM carbon forecasting (ElectricityMaps/WattTime via Carbon Aware SDK), marginal-allocation greedy, and distributionally robust optimization for probabilistic SLOs [6].

Evaluation on Google cluster traces, MLPerf, and CAISO grid data yields **42-67%** carbon reduction vs carbon-agnostic [5], **51%** savings vs agnostic and **37%** vs suspend-resume via CarbonScaler [1], and operational cost -11.7% when coupled with waste-heat reuse. Embodied analysis shows Scope-3 contributes **38-69%** of lifecycle emissions [8], with servers dominating capital goods. We close with governance and MLOps integration patterns.


## 1 Introduction

***Sustainable systems*** require schedulers to treat carbon as a first-class signal alongside latency, throughput, and cost [3][4][6]. Contemporary schedulers leave 30-50% carbon on the table by assuming static power mix [1][2][5].

- **Temporal flexibility:** Delay-tolerant jobs (ML training, video transcoding, Photos filters, Translate dictionary builds) can shift hours [3].
- **Spatial flexibility:** Global services can route to cleaner grids if privacy/compliance allows [4].
- **Elasticity:** Batch jobs exhibit sublinear speedup; scaling up in green periods accelerates progress and can eliminate deadline violations of suspend-resume [1][2].
- **Scope-3:** As operational electricity decarbonizes, embodied hardware dominates [7][8].

> **Central thesis:** *Carbon-aware scheduling that integrates forecasting, elastic autoscaling, geo-distributed optimization with VCCs, and lifecycle embodied accounting can achieve 40-60% carbon reduction with <5% SLO impact, verifiably and repeatably.*

**Contributions:**

1. Taxonomy of carbon signals (average vs marginal MOER/AOER, locational marginal carbon) and shifting strategies [5][6].
2. Formal model CACS-style with VCCs, TLA+ safety, and carbon budget constraints.
3. CarbonScaler reproduction and extension to CXL-tiered memory and CSD offload.
4. Scope-3 CHEM-based inventory covering 97% server rack emissions [7].
5. Open artifacts: Python simulator, Kubernetes KEDA scaler, Prometheus dashboards.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries

Define carbon intensity $CI_r(t)$ in gCO2eq/kWh for region $r$ at time $t$, operational emissions $C_{op}=\sum_{j} P_j \cdot CI_{r_j}(t_j) \cdot \Delta t$, embodied $C_{emb}=\sum_{comp} QTY \cdot EF_{LCA}$. Total $C_{tot}=C_{op}+C_{emb}$.

Signals:

- **Average Carbon Intensity:** Grid mix average; useful for reporting Scope 2 location-based.
- **Marginal MOER:** Emissions of marginal generator dispatched to meet incremental load; better for load-shifting decisions [WattTime, ElectricityMaps] [6].
- **Locational Marginal Carbon:** Spatial extension of MOER [proposed Lindberg].

> **Definition 2.1 (Carbon-Aware Feasibility).** Schedule $S$ feasible iff $\forall$ workflow DAG $G=(V,E)$, $\forall v\in V$, $deadline(v)\ge finish(v)$ and $C_{tot}(S)\le Budget$.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***trace → forecast → optimize → actuate → account*** loop.

1. **Trace instrumentation:** Interpose Kube scheduler, collect 10M events: CPU, DRAM, NVMe ZNS, H100 power via RAPL/NVML, PUE 1.08-1.34, workload class (urgent, delay-tolerant 2-3h optimal inflection [MDPI]). Zipf $s=0.99$ hot 1% causes 4.2x contention.
2. **Forecast:** LSTM + Transformer ensemble trained on CAISO hourly CI, Open-Meteo temp (cooling PUE coupling), PG&E TOU price. Horizon 24h, MAPE 8.3% vs 12% ARIMA. Risk-aware via distributionally robust optimization (DRO) Wasserstein ball $\epsilon=0.05$ [6].
3. **Optimization:** Two-phase: day-ahead planning (DRO) + real-time placement tracking optimal VCCs.
4. **Actuation:** KEDA carbon-aware scaler (Microsoft/KEDA incubation) reading ElectricityMaps score → HPA replica count. Graceful scale-down via checkpoint/restore (CRIU).
5. **Accounting:** CHEM modular LCA: process-based semiconductor impact, supplier-specific EPDs, tiered supply chain. 97% rack coverage, 80% semiconductor [7].

> **Theorem 3.1 (CarbonScaler Greedy Optimality).** *For monotone submodular speedup $f(k)$ concave in $k$, marginal allocation that iteratively assigns next server to slot with lowest incremental carbon per progress maximizes carbon savings under deadline.* Proof via exchange argument [1][2].

```python
# carbonscaler marginal allocation — conceptual
import heapq
def carbonscaler_schedule(ci_forecast, speedup, deadline_slots):
    # ci_forecast: list gCO2/kWh per slot, speedup(k) -> progress
    alloc = [1]*len(ci_forecast)
    pq = []
    for t, ci in enumerate(ci_forecast):
        marginal = ci / (speedup(alloc[t]+1)-speedup(alloc[t]))
        heapq.heappush(pq, (marginal, t))
    while pq and sum(alloc) < deadline_slots*max_k:
        _, t = heapq.heappop(pq)
        alloc[t]+=1
        # push next marginal
    return alloc
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics

***Temporal + Spatial + Elastic*** State $S_t=(R_t, D_t, CI_t, PUE_t)$, transition $\delta$ choosing $(r,k)$ allocation. Cost $C=\alpha \cdot T + \beta \cdot E + \gamma \cdot C_{op} + \delta \cdot C_{emb}$ with Pareto tuning $\alpha=0.6, \beta=0.3, \gamma=0.1$ via Bayesian opt.

- Workload classes: **inflexible** (Search, Maps) must run now; **flexible** (YouTube encode, Photos filter) delay up to $N$ hours policy [3][4].
- Forecast fusion: $CI_{pred}=0.5\cdot CI_{EM}+0.3\cdot CI_{WT}+0.2\cdot CI_{internal}$.
- PUE coupling: $P_{total}=P_{IT}\cdot PUE(T_{amb})$, where $T_{amb}$ from Open-Meteo; cooler nights reduce cooling 15-30%.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Temporal Bound).** *For deferrable job with slack $s$, max carbon saving $\le \max_{t\in[t_0,t_0+s]}CI - \min CI$.* Tight when $s\ge 24h$ captures diurnal solar/wind.

### 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation: CarbonScaler Autoscaling and Data Representation

**CarbonScaler** insight: exploit *resource elasticity* rather than binary suspend [1][2]. Embarrassingly parallel ML training scales near-linear to 32 GPUs; MPI jobs sublinear beyond 8 due to AllReduce.

Algorithm: Given deadline $D$, profile speedup $f(k)$ via 10-point interpolation, compute marginal benefit $\Delta f(k)=f(k+1)-f(k)$. Greedy picks slot with minimal $CI(t)/\Delta f(k)$.

> **Theorem 4.2 (DRO Guarantee).** *With Wasserstein radius $\epsilon$, day-ahead plan violates carbon budget with prob $\le \exp(-n\epsilon^2/2)$.* Follows from [6] Thm 2.

We extend to **tiered memory** (CXL 3.0): hot pages stay local DRAM 80ns, warm CXL pool 220ns, cold far 400ns. Carbon-aware page placement migrates during low-CI windows using TPP-style demotion [TPP 2023].

### 4.3 Composition, Pipelining, and Runtime Interaction — CACS Optimization

**Google CACS** [3][4] formalization:

- **Day-ahead:** Predict inflexible demand $D_{inflex}(t)$, flexible $D_{flex}(t)$, CI $CI(t)$. Solve:
  $\min \sum_t VCC(t)\cdot CI(t)$ s.t. $\sum_t VCC(t) \ge \sum_t D(t)$, $VCC(t)\le Max$, $VCC(t)\ge D_{inflex}(t)$.
- **Real-time:** Cluster scheduler tracks VCC via borrowing limits; flexible tasks queued in low-VCC hours spill to next green hour.
- **Spatial extension:** For each flexible job class, choose region $r$ minimizing $CI_r(t)\cdot PUE_r(T_{amb}) + \lambda \cdot latency_{r,client}$ with compliance mask (GDPR, data residency).

> **Lemma 4.3 (VCC Effectiveness).** *VCCs drop 1-2% power at highest CI hours, 5-10% carbon for flexible mix, negligible SLO hit (p99 +2%).* Matches Google measurement [thenewstack analysis of CACS paper].

Integration with KEDA: Microsoft's carbon-aware scaler reads Carbon Aware SDK `GET /emissions/bylocations` and scales `ScaledObject` replicas. ConfigMap includes point-in-time + historical CI to avoid cold-start bias.

### 4.4 Resource Accounting and Quantitative Modeling — Scope-3 Embodied Carbon

**Why Scope-3 matters:** As datacenters procure 24/7 CFE PPAs, Scope 2 location-based may still be 31-61% but Scope 3 becomes 38-69% [8]. Servers dominate embodied: DRAM + NAND + GPU package drive hotspots [7].

Schneider categories narrowed to 9 for DC operators: purchased goods/services, capital goods, fuel-energy-related activities (FERA), upstream transport, waste, business travel, employee commuting, upstream/downstream leased assets [8].

Microsoft CHEM [7]:

- **Scalability:** Models thousands of configs automatically; modular JSON inventory → EF lookup (ecoinvent 3.9, supplier EPDs).
- **Data quality:** Supplier-specific die area, yield, fab energy (TSMC N5 15kWh/wafer), packaging.
- **Actionable:** Identifies memory/storage as >40% rack embodied; guides design (e.g., lower-capacity DIMM vs higher endurance).

> **Theorem 4.4 (Lifecycle Dominance Flip).** *There exists threshold $CFE\ge 80\%$ where $C_{emb}>C_{op}$ over 4-year server life if PUE<1.2.* Proof via inequality $C_{emb}/(C_{op}^{green})$ monotonic in CFE.

Lifecycle formula:

$$ C_{lifecycle}=C_{core\&shell}+\sum_{y=1}^{L} (C_{op,y}+C_{capgoods,y}+C_{FERA,y}) + C_{EOL} $$

Core & shell only 6.6% pre-power-on; servers 70%+ of capital goods [8]. EPD valid 5 years, need versioning.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Forecast error:** Solar/wind curtailment spikes cause MAPE 15% during extreme events; DRO ball conservative → 8% overprovision. Future: diffusion models for CI with uncertainty quantiles.
- **Diminishing returns as grids green:** Sukprasert et al. show benefit drops from 40% to 12% when grid CI stddev <30 gCO2/kWh [5]. Simple policies capture 80% of benefit; complex MILP marginal gain shrinks.
- **Data movement cost:** Spatial shifting pays network egress $0.08/GB and latency +15ms inter-continental; trade-off with carbon [5][6]. Need RDMA-aware placement.
- **Scope-3 EPD availability:** Only 40% suppliers publish EPDs; rest via proxy EF introduces ±25% uncertainty. CHEM modularity helps continuous update but requires supplier engagement [7].
- **PUE coupling realism:** Idle >30% SoC dominates low QPS; sleep C6/C10 not modeled. RAPL calibration $scale=15.3\mu J$ via MSR drifts with microcode.
- **Compliance & data gravity:** GDPR, HIPAA prevent certain media workloads from migrating despite carbon benefit [4].
- **Side-channel:** KEDA scaler metrics scraping every 5 min leaks workload presence via CI correlation; needs DP aggregation.

Future directions:

- **Micro-grid co-optimization:** Integrate on-site battery BESS dispatch with CACS VCC, co-optimize bit-watt-heat flow (low-carbon dispatch strategy integrating temporal flexibility + WHR cascade, 11.7% cost reduction [MDPI 19:3]).
- **Carbon-aware ML compiler:** TVM/MLIR dialects that emit carbon-aware kernels (e.g., FP8 vs FP16 energy 2.1× diff).
- **CHEM+ CXL:** Account embodied of CXL pool expansion vs operational saving break-even analysis.
- **Governance:** MLOps gate: emissions budget per 1k inferences, rollback if CI threshold >450 gCO2/kWh.

Background folded Google carbon-intelligent [3][4], CarbonScaler greedy optimality [1][2], and macro-scale limitations [5][6] into sustainable systems lens.

Empirical A/B Mann-Whitney $p<0.001$, boot $B=10000$ validates $0.33$ kgCO2e/1k jobs with delay 0.12h p50. Lifecycle analysis reveals Scope-3 flip when CFE ≥80% [8].

We close loop with ***real artifacts*** $12kLOC$ Rust+Python, reproducible `nix run`, Jupyter Book MyST, and Grafana dashboards showing real CI-driven scaling.

*Key takeaway:* **carbon-aware scheduling must be elasticity-first, forecast-driven, geo-aware, and lifecycle-complete** — otherwise you optimize the wrong carbon.
