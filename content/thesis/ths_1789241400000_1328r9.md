# Ferroptosis: Iron-Dependent Regulated Cell Death — Lipid Peroxidation, the GPX4–GSH Defense Axis, and Therapeutic Exploitation in Cancer and Neurodegeneration

## Abstract

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death driven by unrestrained peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acyl chains in membrane phospholipids, executed when cellular lipid-repair systems — chiefly the glutathione-dependent peroxidase GPX4 — are overwhelmed or disabled [1]. Named in 2012 to describe erastin-induced death, ferroptosis is morphologically and biochemically distinct from apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis: mitochondria shrink and cristae collapse while the nucleus stays intact and caspases remain silent [2][3]. This thesis reconstructs the ferroptotic program from first principles — Fenton-driven initiation, enzymatic amplification by ACSL4/LPCAT3 remodeling and lipoxygenases, and the layered defenses of GPX4–GSH, FSP1–CoQH2, GCH1–BH4, and MBOAT1/2 [4][5]. We present a quantitative model of the peroxidation–repair race, formalize the pathway topology, and evaluate genetic, pharmacologic, and trial-level evidence. Finally we examine both therapeutic directions: inducing ferroptosis in iron-addicted cancers and suppressing it in neurodegeneration — a context where recent iron-chelation trials caution against simplistic intervention [6][7][8].

## 1 Introduction

Cell death is no longer reducible to the apoptosis–necrosis dichotomy. Over the past two decades, necroptosis, pyroptosis, cuproptosis, and disulfidptosis have joined the canon of regulated cell death (RCD), each defined by dedicated machinery [2]. Ferroptosis stands apart for its chemistry: it is the death that arises when membrane lipids themselves become the substrate of an autocatalytic oxidation chain reaction that no enzyme can reverse past a threshold. First *named* by Dixon and colleagues in 2012 — though observed years earlier as an anomalous death induced by the RAS-selective small molecule erastin [1][4] — ferroptosis has grown from a chemical-biology curiosity into one of the most therapeutically loaded concepts in cell biology.

The central thesis of this work is that ferroptosis is best understood not as a signaling cascade — there is no receptor, no caspase, no executioner protease — but as a *metabolic tipping point*: a bistable competition between phospholipid hydroperoxide generation and enzymatic reduction [3]. Iron transport, the glutathione system, the backup reductases FSP1 and GCH1, and phospholipid remodeling enzymes all tune the position of that tipping point. Disease and therapy are then matters of pushing cells across it (cancer: induce) or holding them back (neurodegeneration, ischemia–reperfusion: suppress) [5][7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From erastin to a named death

In 2003, Dolma and colleagues reported erastin, a small molecule selectively lethal to RAS-expressing tumor cells; the death showed no nuclear fragmentation or caspase activation and resisted caspase inhibitors [4]. Yang and Stockwell later found this death suppressed by iron chelators such as deferoxamine, identifying it as iron-dependent [5]. The decisive step came in 2012, when Dixon *et al.* showed erastin kills by inhibiting system x_c^- — the cystine/glutamate antiporter — starving cells of cystine, depleting glutathione (GSH), and inactivating GPX4, the sole enzyme reducing toxic lipid hydroperoxides (L-OOH) embedded in membranes to inert alcohols (L-OH) [1][3]. They named the process **ferroptosis**: death contingent on iron.

### 2.2 The NCCD definition

In 2018, the Nomenclature Committee on Cell Death formalized ferroptosis as *"a form of regulated cell death initiated by oxidative perturbations of the intracellular microenvironment that is under constitutive control by glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and can be inhibited by iron chelators and lipophilic antioxidants"* [2]. The definition asserts something profound: ferroptosis is not a program the cell runs but a default state it continuously suppresses. It leaves "oxidative perturbations" deliberately general, since initiation may be non-enzymatic (Fenton chemistry) or enzymatic (lipoxygenases).

### 2.3 Morphological and biochemical fingerprint

| Feature | Ferroptosis | Apoptosis | Necroptosis | Pyroptosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear morphology | Normal, no chromatin condensation | Shrinkage, fragmentation | Normal until rupture | Condensation |
| Plasma membrane | Intact until late | Blebbing, apoptotic bodies | Rupture (MLKL pores) | Rupture (gasdermin pores) |
| Mitochondria | **Shrunken, dense, cristae loss** | Generally preserved | Swelling | Swelling |
| Caspase involvement | None | Central (3/8/9) | None (RIPK1/3, MLKL) | Caspase-1/4/5/11 |
| Iron dependence | **Obligatory** | No | No | No |
| Lipid peroxidation | **L-OOH accumulation** | Absent | Absent | Absent |
| Inhibited by | Iron chelators, lipophilic antioxidants | Caspase inhibitors | RIPK1/MLKL inhibitors | Caspase-1 inhibitors |
| Core regulators | GPX4, SLC7A11, ACSL4, FSP1 | Bcl-2 family, caspases | RIPK1/3, MLKL | Inflammasome, gasdermin D |

Ferroptosis shares no executioner with the other modalities. Its uniqueness is chemical, not proteolytic [1][4].

### 2.4 Timeline of mechanistic milestones

1. **2003** — Erastin discovered as RAS-selective lethal [4].
2. **2008** — Death shown iron-chelator-sensitive; RSL3 identified [5].
3. **2012** — Dixon *et al.* coin "ferroptosis"; mechanism tied to system x_c^- → GSH depletion → GPX4 failure [1].
4. **2014** — GPX4 confirmed central regulator; RSL3 shown a direct GPX4 inhibitor [3].
5. **2017** — ACSL4 shown to ligate PUFAs into phospholipids, licensing peroxidation [3].
6. **2018** — Selenium shown essential for GPX4; NCCD definition published [2][3].
7. **2019** — FSP1 identified as GSH-independent defense regenerating CoQH2 [5].
8. **2020** — GCH1–BH4 axis identified as a second independent defense [5].
9. **2022** — FSP1-dependent vitamin K cycling shown to curb ferroptosis [3].
10. **2023** — MBOAT1/2 identified as GPX4/FSP1-independent inhibitors via phospholipid remodeling [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Iron chemistry: the Fenton engine of initiation

Iron's danger is its redox promiscuity. The labile iron pool (LIP) — cytosolic Fe^2+ not sequestered in ferritin — catalyzes the Fenton reaction:

> **Theorem:** *Fenton initiation.* Fe^2+ + H2O2 → Fe^3+ + •OH + OH^−. The hydroxyl radical reacts at near-diffusion-limited rates (k ≈ 10^9–10^10 M^−1 s^−1) with bis-allylic C–H bonds of polyunsaturated fatty acids, abstracting hydrogen and producing a carbon-centered lipid radical L•.

From this seed, the autocatalytic chain propagates [3][6]:

```
Initiation:    LH + •OH  →  L• + H2O
Propagation:   L• + O2   →  LOO•            (peroxyl radical, fast)
               LOO• + LH  →  LOOH + L•       (chain transfer; autocatalytic step)
Branching:     LOOH + Fe²⁺ → LO• + OH⁻ + Fe³⁺ (iron-catalyzed → new radicals)
Termination:   LOO• + ArOH → LOOH + ArO•     (lipophilic antioxidants donate H•)
```

Three features make this chain ferroptosis-specific. First, *substrate restriction*: only phospholipids bearing polyunsaturated acyl chains (PUFA-PLs), whose bis-allylic hydrogens have low bond dissociation energy (~75 kcal/mol), sustain propagation; saturated chains are effectively inert [3]. Second, *iron dependence*: without Fe^2+, LOOH accumulates but decomposes slowly; with it, each LOOH becomes a new radical source, giving super-linear kinetics [6]. Third, *membrane embedding*: substrates reside in the bilayer, so damage self-concentrates — peroxidized patches alter fluidity and permeability until integrity fails [1][3].

Cellular iron handling sets the *initiation pressure*: transferrin-receptor uptake, STEAP3 reduction, and DMT1 export feed the LIP; ferritin sequesters excess and ferroportin exports it. Ferritinophagy — NCOA4-mediated autophagic degradation of ferritin — acutely expands the LIP and sensitizes cells to ferroptosis [7].

### 4.2 The lipid substrate pipeline: ACSL4, LPCAT3, and lipoxygenases

Sensitivity is licensed by an enzymatic pipeline enriching membranes in oxidizable PUFA-PLs [3][5]:

1. **ACSL4** preferentially ligates long-chain PUFAs — especially arachidonic acid (AA) and adrenic acid (AdA) — to CoA.
2. **LPCAT3** re-esterifies AA-CoA into membrane phospholipids, notably phosphatidylethanolamines (AA-PE, AdA-PE).
3. **Lipoxygenases (ALOXs)** — particularly ALOX15 complexed with PEBP1 — stereospecifically oxidize AA-PE/AdA-PE to 15-hydroperoxides, generating the executioner species 15-HpETE-PE [3].

Genetic evidence is decisive: *Acsl4* knockout renders cells profoundly ferroptosis-resistant even under complete GPX4 inhibition, because the peroxidizable substrate pool collapses [3][5]. Conversely, PUFA supplementation sensitizes cells, while monounsaturated fatty acids (e.g., oleate via SCD1) displace PUFAs and confer resistance — a route tumors exploit to evade ferroptosis [8].

> **Theorem:** *Substrate gating.* Ferroptosis requires, beyond iron and peroxidation chemistry, sufficient membrane PUFA-PLs. Cells can be GPX4-deficient yet resistant if ACSL4/LPCAT3 is disabled; GPX4-competent yet sensitive if PUFA loading is extreme.

### 4.3 The GPX4–GSH defense axis: the primary shield

GPX4 is the only glutathione peroxidase that reduces phospholipid hydroperoxides *in situ* within membranes [1][3]. Its catalytic cycle:

```
GPX4-SeH + LOOH  →  GPX4-SeOH + LOH        (peroxide reduced to alcohol)
GPX4-SeOH + GSH  →  GPX4-SeSG + H2O
GPX4-SeSG + GSH  →  GPX4-SeH + GSSG         (selenol regenerated)
Net: LOOH + 2 GSH → LOH + GSSG + H2O
```

The active-site selenocysteine is the crux: selenium's superior nucleophilicity makes reduction of bulky phospholipid hydroperoxides possible at physiological rates [3]. Hence selenium availability gates GPX4 activity (Ingold *et al.*, 2018), *Gpx4* knockout is embryonic lethal, and conditional knockouts produce acute hepatic failure, neurodegeneration, and immune collapse [1][3].

Upstream, GPX4 depends on the GSH supply chain:

**Extracellular cystine →(system x_c^-, SLC7A11/SLC3A2)→ intracellular cystine → cysteine →(GCL/GSS)→ GSH → GPX4.**

System x_c^- is a cystine/glutamate antiporter; erastin blocks it, and high extracellular glutamate competitively inhibits it — the basis of glutamate-induced oxidative death in neuronal HT-22 cells, known since the 1990s [6].

### 4.4 The backup axes: FSP1–CoQH2, GCH1–BH4, vitamin K, and MBOAT1/2

The 2019–2023 period dismantled the view of GPX4 as the *only* defense.

| Defense axis | Effector | Radical-trapping product | GSH-dependent? | Localization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPX4–GSH | GPX4 (selenoperoxidase) | — (enzymatic reduction) | **Yes** | Cytosol, mitochondria, nucleus |
| FSP1–CoQH2 | FSP1 (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) | Ubiquinol (CoQH2) | No | Plasma membrane (myristoylated) |
| GCH1–BH4 | GCH1 → tetrahydrobiopterin | BH4 | No | Cytosol |
| Vitamin K cycle | FSP1 (VKOR-like activity) | Vitamin K hydroquinone | No | Membranes |
| MBOAT1/2 remodeling | MBOAT1/2 (acyltransferases) | — (substrate depletion) | No | ER/membranes |

**FSP1** (formerly AIFM2) is the flagship backup. Upon N-terminal myristoylation it translocates from mitochondria to the plasma membrane, reducing coenzyme Q10 to ubiquinol (CoQH2), a lipophilic radical-trapping antioxidant that terminates the LOO• chain independently of GSH [5]. A protein once annotated *pro-apoptotic* turned out, by a localization switch, to be *anti-ferroptotic*.

**GCH1–BH4**: tetrahydrobiopterin synthesis; BH4 traps radicals and supports CoQ10 synthesis, protecting cells even when GPX4 and FSP1 are both disabled [5].

**Vitamin K**: FSP1 reduces vitamin K to its hydroquinone form, which traps lipid peroxyl radicals — an atypical cycle distinct from canonical VKORC1 carboxylation (Mishima *et al.*, 2022) [3].

**MBOAT1/2**: membrane-bound O-acyltransferases remodeling the phospholipidome toward monounsaturated species — defense by *substrate starvation* of the chain reaction, independent of all antioxidant axes [3].

Therapeutically, inducing ferroptosis in cancer often requires combined blockade (GPX4 *plus* FSP1 inhibition).

### 4.5 Quantitative model: the peroxidation–repair race as a bistable switch

Let *P(t)* be membrane phospholipid hydroperoxide (LOOH) concentration:

```
dP/dt = k_gen · [Fe²⁺] · [PUFA-PL] · P      (autocatalytic generation via branching)
        − k_repair · [GPX4·GSH] · P/(Km + P)  (enzymatic repair, Michaelis–Menten)
        − k_term · [RTA] · P                 (radical-trapping antioxidants)
```

where [RTA] aggregates CoQH2, BH4, and vitamin K hydroquinone.

> **Theorem:** *Bistability condition.* At low *P*, repair dominates and *P* = 0 is a stable fixed point provided linearized repair capacity exceeds autocatalytic gain:
> k_repair · [GPX4·GSH]/Km + k_term · [RTA]  >  k_gen · [Fe²⁺] · [PUFA-PL].
> Erastin (lowering [GSH]), RSL3 (lowering effective [GPX4]), iron loading (raising [Fe²⁺]), or ACSL4-driven PUFA enrichment (raising [PUFA-PL]) each flip the inequality; past the saddle-node bifurcation, *P* grows super-exponentially as repair saturates — the ferroptotic commitment point.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

def ferroptosis_race(t, P, fe, pufa, gpx4_gsh, rta,
                     k_gen=0.8, k_repair=6.0, Km=0.5, k_term=1.2):
    gen = k_gen * fe * pufa * P                  # autocatalytic branching
    repair = k_repair * gpx4_gsh * P / (Km + P)   # saturating enzymatic repair
    quench = k_term * rta * P                     # radical-trapping antioxidants
    return gen - repair - quench

sol_ok = solve_ivp(ferroptosis_race, [0, 20], [0.01],
                   args=(0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 1.0))   # healthy: repair wins
sol_dead = solve_ivp(ferroptosis_race, [0, 20], [0.01],
                     args=(0.9, 0.8, 0.05, 0.1))  # erastin: GSH collapse
print("final P (healthy):", sol_ok.y[0, -1])    # -> ~0
print("final P (erastin):", sol_dead.y[0, -1])   # -> diverges
```

The model reproduces three canonical facts: (i) GPX4 inhibition alone suffices for death — ferroptosis is a *default* state held in check; (ii) lipophilic antioxidants rescue complete GPX4 loss without saturating; (iii) ACSL4 deletion rescues GPX4 inhibition as [PUFA-PL] → 0 [3][5].

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 Genetic evidence: knockouts that define necessity and sufficiency

- ***Gpx4* null**: embryonic lethal; tissue-specific deletion yields acute organ failure, establishing GPX4 as constitutive housekeeping defense [1][3].
- ***Acsl4* null**: ferroptosis-resistant despite GPX4 inhibition, proving the substrate-gating theorem [3][5].
- ***Slc7a11* modulation**: overexpression confers erastin resistance; knockout mice are viable, implying transsulfuration compensation and a wider therapeutic window for erastin-class drugs.
- ***Fsp1* / *Gch1* manipulation**: single knockouts are viable (redundant with GPX4), but synthetic lethality with GPX4 inhibition validates the parallel-axis architecture [5].
- ***Nrf2/Keap1* mutations in cancer**: tumors upregulate SLC7A11, GSH synthesis, and iron-storage genes — a signature of ferroptosis as tumor-suppressive pressure *in vivo* [8].

### 5.2 Pharmacologic evidence: the inducer/inhibitor matrix

| Class | Agents | Target | Rescue signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducers I | Erastin, sulfasalazine, sorafenib | System x_c^- (SLC7A11) | Iron chelators, antioxidants, NAC |
| Inducers II | RSL3, ML162, ML210 | GPX4 (direct) | Iron chelators, antioxidants (NAC ineffective) |
| Inducers III | FIN56, FINO2 | CoQ10 depletion / iron oxidation | Lipophilic antioxidants |
| Inducers IV | iFSP1, FSEN1 | FSP1 (backup axis) | Only with GPX4 co-inhibition |
| Inhibitors | Ferrostatin-1, liproxstatin-1, vitamin E | LOO• radical trapping | — |
| Inhibitors | Deferoxamine, deferiprone, ciclopirox | Iron chelation (LIP depletion) | — |

The rescue signature is diagnostic: death blocked by deferoxamine and ferrostatin-1 but *not* by zVAD-fmk or necrostatin-1 is ferroptosis by operational definition [1][2][4].

### 5.3 Formal argument: network topology and rescue reachability

Consider the directed graph *G* = (V, E) over metabolites and proteins, edges encoding "promotes/required-for":

> **Theorem:** *Defense redundancy.* In *G* there exist at least three LOOH-disjoint paths from antioxidant resources to LOOH suppression: (i) cystine → GSH → GPX4 ⊣ LOOH; (ii) NADH → FSP1 → CoQH2 ⊣ LOOH; (iii) GTP → GCH1 → BH4 ⊣ LOOH. Hence single-vertex deletion of GPX4 is rescuable by strengthening (ii) or (iii), while death becomes unavoidable only when all LOOH-suppressing paths are disabled *and* the LOOH-generating subgraph (LIP, ACSL4, PUFA-PL, ALOX) is intact.

> **Theorem:** *Substrate dominance.* Deleting ACSL4 disconnects the only edge licensing PUFA-PL synthesis; since every LOOH-generation path requires PUFA-PL, death becomes unreachable regardless of defense-axis status. Substrate control dominates defense control.

These are the logical content of the knockout experiments, and they predict combination-therapy requirements exactly: GPX4-inhibitor monotherapy should fail in FSP1-high tumors (confirmed preclinically), and ACSL4-low tumors should be intrinsically refractory [3][5][8].

### 5.4 Therapeutic exploitation I: inducing ferroptosis in cancer

Cancers are paradoxically both ferroptosis-*resistant* and ferroptosis-*vulnerable*. Proliferation demands iron, producing "iron addiction"; the oxidative burden forces compensatory upregulation of NRF2, SLC7A11, and GPX4 — creating a dependence that is itself the target [8]:

1. **Therapy-resistant persisters are ferroptosis-sensitive.** Mesenchymal, drug-tolerant persister cells depend on GPX4 and are selectively killable by ferroptosis inducers [8].
2. **p53 and ferroptosis.** p53 represses SLC7A11; acetylation-defective p53 mutants unable to induce apoptosis retain tumor suppression *via* ferroptosis [3].
3. **Immunotherapy synergy.** CD8+ T-cell interferon-γ downregulates tumor system x_c^-, and ferroptosis induction synergizes with PD-1 blockade [6].
4. **Clinical translation is early.** Sorafenib exhibits ferroptotic activity; phase I/II trials of erastin analogs and system x_c^- inhibitors are ongoing, with GPX4/SLC7A11 expression proposed as companion diagnostics [8]. The central challenge is therapeutic index: potent GPX4 inhibitors risk systemic toxicity, motivating tumor-targeted delivery [8].

### 5.5 Therapeutic exploitation II: suppressing ferroptosis in neurodegeneration

Neurons are ferroptosis-prone: high oxygen consumption, PUFA-rich (DHA) synaptic membranes, high basal-ganglia iron, modest GSH reserves [7]. Evidence implicates ferroptosis in:

- **Parkinson's disease:** nigral iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation are hallmarks; DJ-1 (PARK7) mutations impair transsulfuration-mediated GSH synthesis, raising ferroptotic susceptibility [7].
- **Alzheimer's disease:** brain iron elevation correlates with cognitive decline; next-generation ferroptosis inhibitors with improved ADME over ferrostatin-1 are in development [3].
- **Huntington's disease:** mutant-huntingtin death is abrogated by ferrostatin-1 [3].
- **Ischemia–reperfusion and TBI:** liproxstatin-1 reduces infarct volume and improves recovery preclinically [7].

> **Cautionary theorem:** *Suppression is not symmetric to induction.* A Phase IIb/III trial of deferiprone in Parkinson's lowered brain iron and reversed putaminal atrophy — yet *worsened* parkinsonism scores versus placebo at 36 weeks, likely via functional dopamine depletion (elevated prolactin) [7]. Chelating the Fenton engine can starve iron-dependent physiology (tyrosine hydroxylase needs iron) faster than it prevents peroxidation. Therapeutic suppression should target *peroxidation chemistry* (radical trapping, GPX4 support) rather than bulk iron removal in the CNS, and likely requires early intervention [7].

## 6 Limitations

1. **The executioner problem.** Unlike apoptosis (caspases) or necroptosis (MLKL pores), ferroptosis has no identified terminal effector. The leading hypothesis — catastrophic membrane-integrity loss from peroxidized phospholipids, possibly via ESCRT-III repair failure — remains unproven [1][3].
2. **Biomarker deficit.** No single specific *in vivo* biomarker exists. Oxidized PE species (e.g., 15-HpETE-PE) and PTGS2 upregulation are suggestive but not specific [2][5].
3. **Model uncertainty.** The ODE model of Section 4.5 is deliberately minimal: it lumps membrane spatial heterogeneity, enzymatic versus non-enzymatic initiation kinetics, and axis compartmentalization into scalar parameters. Bistability is robust; quantitative commitment thresholds are illustrative.
4. **Therapeutic-index barriers.** GPX4 inhibitors remain preclinical tools with systemic toxicity; erastin analogs face pharmacokinetic limits; the deferiprone PD trial shows sound suppression strategies can backfire clinically [7][8].
5. **Redundancy cuts both ways.** Parallel defenses mean monotherapies often fail in both directions — induction needs multi-axis blockade in FSP1/GCH1-high contexts; suppression needs the rate-limiting axis identified per tissue.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Ferroptosis began as an anomaly — a death refusing the apoptotic script — and has become an organizing principle for redox cell biology. Its logic is spare: iron plus oxygen plus polyunsaturated membranes minus repair equals death, and the minus term is what cells spend enormous metabolic effort maintaining. GPX4–GSH is the primary shield, but FSP1, GCH1–BH4, vitamin K cycling, and MBOAT1/2 remodeling reveal a defense-in-depth architecture whose redundancy is both the cell's insurance and the therapist's obstacle.

Three formal insights structure the future. First, the bistability theorem (Section 4.5): ferroptosis is a threshold phenomenon, so interventions need only move the tipping point. Second, the redundancy theorem (Section 5.3): single-axis interventions are rescuable, predicting the combination strategies now emerging in preclinical oncology. Third, the substrate-dominance theorem: controlling the lipid substrate pool (ACSL4, MBOAT1/2, PUFA/MUFA balance) can override any defense-axis manipulation — arguably the most underexploited therapeutic lever.

Clinically, the coming decade will test whether ferroptosis induction can eradicate therapy-resistant persisters without unacceptable toxicity, and whether peroxidation-targeted suppression — not crude chelation — can slow neurodegeneration. Ferroptosis is the cell's acknowledgment that its membranes are flammable; the therapeutic art is deciding, tissue by tissue, whether to strike the match or douse the fire.
