# Capacity-Achieving Codes in Practice: LDPC Belief Propagation, Polar Code Channel Polarization, and the 5G NR Coding Chain

## Abstract

Since Shannon's 1948 proof that reliable communication is possible at any rate below channel capacity, the central pursuit of coding theory has been the construction of explicit code families that achieve capacity with feasible decoding complexity. This thesis unifies the two great triumphs of that pursuit: low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes decoded by iterative belief propagation on Tanner graphs, and polar codes built upon Arıkan's channel polarization phenomenon. We develop the sum-product message-passing formalism in the log-likelihood-ratio domain, derive the recursive channel combining and splitting transforms $W \mapsto (W^-, W^+)$, and prove the polarization of the mutual informations $I(W_N^{(i)})$ and Bhattacharyya parameters $Z(W_N^{(i)})$ of the synthesized channels. We then trace how 3GPP fused both families into the 5G New Radio coding chain — quasi-cyclic LDPC codes drawn from base graphs BG1 and BG2 with circular-buffer rate matching and incremental-redundancy HARQ for data channels, and CRC-aided polar codes for control channels. Density-evolution thresholds, finite-length scaling laws, and error-floor mechanisms are analyzed quantitatively, and the remaining gaps between asymptotic guarantees and deployed practice are delineated precisely.

## 1 Introduction

In 1948, Shannon demonstrated that every noisy channel possesses a *capacity* $C$, and that error probability can be driven to zero at any rate $R < C$ — but his proof was non-constructive, relying on random coding arguments with maximum-likelihood decoding of exponential complexity [5]. For nearly five decades, the central embarrassment of coding theory was the gap between this existence proof and any practical scheme: algebraic codes such as Reed–Solomon and BCH offered structure but fell several decibels short of capacity, while the random-like codes Shannon's proof required appeared undecodable.

The modern era began with two disruptions. First, Berrou's turbo codes (1993) showed iterative decoding of concatenated constituents could approach capacity, rekindling interest in Gallager's long-neglected 1962 LDPC codes; their mid-1990s rediscovery revealed sparse-graph codes with *belief propagation* decoding operating within a fraction of a decibel of the Shannon limit [5]. Second, in 2009, Arıkan introduced *channel polarization* — the first deterministic, explicit construction provably achieving the symmetric capacity of any binary-input discrete memoryless channel, with encoding and decoding complexity $O(N \log N)$ [1]. The elegance of the polarization proof, requiring no ensemble averaging and no unproven convergence assumptions, resolved the foundational question Shannon had left open.

Practical vindication arrived in 2016, when 3GPP selected *both* families for 5G New Radio — LDPC for data channels (DL-SCH, UL-SCH), polar for control channels (PDCCH, PUCCH, PBCH) — as specified in TS 38.212 [3][4]. This thesis develops the full arc: the graphical and probabilistic machinery of belief propagation, the martingale mathematics of polarization, the decoding algorithms that make each family practical, and the industrial coding chain that interleaves CRC attachment, code-block segmentation, base-graph selection, rate matching, and hybrid ARQ into a working cellular standard.

## 2 Background

### Channel models and symmetric capacity

We consider a binary-input discrete memoryless channel (B-DMC) $W: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ with $\mathcal{X} = \{0,1\}$, transition probabilities $W(y|x)$, and *symmetric capacity*

$$I(W) = \sum_{y} \sum_{x \in \{0,1\}} \tfrac{1}{2} W(y|x) \log_2 \frac{W(y|x)}{\tfrac{1}{2}W(y|0) + \tfrac{1}{2}W(y|1)},$$

the mutual information under uniform inputs — the highest rate achievable with equiprobable signaling. The Bhattacharyya parameter

$$Z(W) = \sum_y \sqrt{W(y|0)W(y|1)} \in [0,1]$$

upper-bounds the maximum-likelihood decision error for a single use and serves as the reliability measure driving polar code construction [1].

### LDPC codes and Tanner graphs

A binary LDPC code is the null space of a *sparse* parity-check matrix $H$ of size $M \times N$: $\mathcal{C} = \{x \in \{0,1\}^N : Hx = 0\}$. Tanner (1981) introduced the bipartite graphical representation now bearing his name: $N$ *variable nodes* (one per code bit) connected by edges to $M$ *check nodes* (one per parity equation), with an edge $(v_i, c_j)$ iff $H_{ji} = 1$ [5]. A $(\lambda, \rho)$-*irregular* ensemble is specified by edge-perspective degree distributions $\lambda(x)$ and $\rho(x)$, with design rate $R = 1 - \int_0^1 \rho / \int_0^1 \lambda$. Gallager's original *probabilistic decoding* — the first form of belief propagation — was already present in his 1962 thesis, decades before Pearl formalized BP for Bayesian networks in 1988 [5].

### Polar codes and the Arıkan transform

Polar codes arise from the $2 \times 2$ kernel $F = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 \ 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$ and its $n$-fold Kronecker power $G_N = F^{\otimes n}$ (up to bit-reversal), mapping $u_1^N \mapsto x_1^N = u_1^N G_N$ with $N = 2^n$. Applying the transform recursively to $N$ copies of $W$ synthesizes $N$ new binary-input channels $\{W_N^{(i)} : 1 \le i \le N\}$: the $i$-th sees $u_i$ given the channel outputs $y_1^N$ and the previous decisions $u_1^{i-1}$. The *frozen set* $\mathcal{F}$ collects indices with $Z(W_N^{(i)})$ near 1 (inputs fixed to known values, typically zero); the *information set* $\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{F}^c$ carries data at rate $K/N$ [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds along three methodological axes, each matched to the mathematical character of its object.

**Message-passing analysis for LDPC codes.** Decoding is studied as *belief propagation* (the sum-product algorithm) on the Tanner graph: variable and check nodes exchange extrinsic log-likelihood-ratio (LLR) messages along edges. Asymptotic performance is predicted by *density evolution*, which tracks the probability density of messages through iterations under the cycle-free (tree) assumption and yields a *threshold* — the worst channel parameter at which the error probability converges to zero. Gaussian approximation (DE-GA) and EXIT charts provide tractable surrogates used in industrial code design [5].

**Polarization analysis for polar codes.** The methodology is martingale-theoretic: the channel combining/splitting recursion defines a stochastic process on Bhattacharyya parameters whose almost-sure convergence to $\{0,1\}$ *is* the polarization theorem. Finite-length behavior is quantified through the *rate of polarization* — the scaling of $P_e$ as $2^{-N^\beta}$ — and through explicit recursive LLR formulas enabling $O(N \log N)$ successive-cancellation decoding [1][2].

**Standards-driven systems analysis for 5G NR.** The coding chain is reconstructed from 3GPP TS 38.212 [3]: transport-block CRC attachment, code-block segmentation, base-graph selection, quasi-cyclic LDPC encoding with lifting, circular-buffer rate matching, and HARQ for data; CRC attachment, interleaving, polar encoding, sub-block interleaving, and puncturing/shortening/repetition rate matching for control. Each stage is examined for its information-theoretic rationale and its implementation cost.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Belief Propagation on Tanner Graphs

Belief propagation decodes by treating the Tanner graph as a factor graph and computing approximate marginal posteriors through local message exchange. Let $L_v = \log \frac{P(x_v=0|y_v)}{P(x_v=1|y_v)}$ be the channel LLR at variable node $v$. Messages are *extrinsic*: each outgoing message excludes the information that arrived on the same edge, preventing immediate positive feedback.

1. **Variable-to-check update.** The message from $v$ to $c$ sums all incoming information except that from $c$ itself:
$$m_{v \to c} = L_v + \sum_{c' \in \partial v \setminus \{c\}} m_{c' \to v}.$$
2. **Check-to-variable update.** The message from $c$ to $v$ enforces the parity constraint over all *other* neighbors via the hyperbolic-tangent rule:
$$m_{c \to v} = 2\,\mathrm{atanh}\!\left(\prod_{v' \in \partial c \setminus \{v\}} \tanh\frac{m_{v' \to c}}{2}\right).$$
3. **Decision.** After each iteration, the *a posteriori* LLR $L_v^{\mathrm{APP}} = L_v + \sum_{c \in \partial v} m_{c \to v}$ is thresholded at zero; decoding halts when $H\hat{x} = 0$ or a maximum iteration count is reached.

The check-node update admits a hardware-friendly simplification. Since $\tanh$ is compressive, the product is dominated by the least reliable input, yielding the *min-sum* approximation $m_{c\to v} \approx \bigl(\prod_{v'} \mathrm{sgn}(m_{v'\to c})\bigr)\cdot \min_{v'} |m_{v'\to c}|$, typically corrected by a normalization factor ($\approx 0.75$) or offset to recover most of the $\sim$0.3–0.5 dB loss versus full sum-product [5].

```python
import numpy as np

def check_node_update(incoming):
    """Sum-product check-node update in the LLR domain (tanh rule)."""
    t = np.tanh(np.asarray(incoming, dtype=float) / 2.0)
    prod = np.prod(t)
    # extrinsic principle: divide out each edge's own contribution
    extrinsic = prod / np.where(t != 0, t, 1.0)
    return 2.0 * np.arctanh(np.clip(extrinsic, -1 + 1e-12, 1 - 1e-12))

def min_sum_update(incoming):
    """Hardware-friendly min-sum approximation (per-edge exclusion omitted)."""
    s = np.sign(incoming)
    return np.prod(s[s != 0]) * np.min(np.abs(incoming))
```

> **Theorem:** (Exactness of BP on trees.) If the Tanner graph of a parity-check code is acyclic, belief propagation computes the *exact* bitwise a posteriori probabilities, and thresholding them yields the bit-optimal decoder [5].

Real LDPC graphs contain cycles, so BP is only approximate — yet density evolution proves the approximation asymptotically exact on cycle-free computation trees. The practical penalty of cycles appears as *trapping sets* $TS(a,b)$: subgraphs with $a$ variable nodes and $b$ unsatisfied checks that sustain *error floors* at low BER [5]. *Layered* scheduling — updating check nodes serially with fresh messages — roughly doubles convergence speed and dominates hardware.

### 4.2 Channel Polarization: From $W$ to $(W^-, W^+)$

The engine of polar coding is a single recursive step. Given two independent copies of a B-DMC $W$, combine inputs as $(x_1, x_2) = (u_1 \oplus u_2, u_2)$ and split the resulting super-channel into two synthesized channels [1][6]:

- $W^-: u_1 \mapsto (y_1, y_2)$ — the *worse* channel (decodes $u_1$ treating $u_2$ as noise);
- $W^+: u_2 \mapsto (y_1, y_2, u_1)$ — the *better* channel (decodes $u_2$ with $u_1$ known).

Capacity is conserved while reliability spreads apart:

$$I(W^-) + I(W^+) = 2\,I(W), \qquad I(W^+) \ge I(W) \ge I(W^-),$$

with strict inequality unless $W$ is already perfect or useless. The Bhattacharyya parameters obey the clean recursion $Z(W^+) = Z(W)^2$ and $Z(W^-) \le 2Z(W) - Z(W)^2$, with equality on the binary erasure channel [1]. Iterating this combining/splitting operation $n$ times yields the $N = 2^n$ channels $W_N^{(i)}$; $\{Z_n\}$ is a bounded super-martingale converging a.s. to $\{0,1\}$, and capacity conservation forces $\Pr[Z_\infty = 0] = I(W)$. This is *polarization*: a vanishing fraction of channels remain partially noisy, while fractions $I(W)$ and $1 - I(W)$ become essentially perfect and essentially useless, respectively.

> **Theorem:** (Polarization, Arıkan [1].) Let $W$ be any symmetric B-DMC with $I(W) > 0$ and fix $\beta < \tfrac{1}{2}$. Then $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{2^n}\,\#\{i : Z(W_{2^n}^{(i)}) < 2^{-2^{\beta n}}\} = I(W)$. Hence polar codes of rate $R < I(W)$ achieve block error $P_e \le O(N^{-1/4})$ — refinable to $2^{-N^\beta}$ — under $O(N \log N)$ successive-cancellation decoding.

The exponent $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is optimal for the $2 \times 2$ kernel; larger kernels can improve it, with BCH-based constructions approaching exponent 1 asymptotically [2]. On the BEC the recursion is numerically exact, and a few lines of code exhibit polarization directly:

```python
def polarize_bec(eps, n):
    """BEC Bhattacharyya recursion: Z(W^-) = 2Z - Z^2, Z(W^+) = Z^2."""
    z = [eps]
    for _ in range(n):
        nxt = []
        for v in z:
            nxt += [2 * v - v * v, v * v]  # (W^-, W^+) pair
        z = nxt
    return z  # Z(W_N^{(i)}); #{z_i < 1/2} / N -> 1 - eps (capacity)

def polar_transform(u):
    """Arikan transform x = u G_N, G_N = F^{⊗n}; in-place butterflies, O(N log N)."""
    x = [int(b) for b in u]
    n, step = len(x), 1
    while step < n:
        for i in range(0, n, 2 * step):
            for j in range(step):
                x[i + j] ^= x[i + step + j]  # (u1, u2) -> (u1⊕u2, u2)
        step *= 2
    return x
```

Running `polarize_bec(0.5, 10)` returns 1024 values of which 512 cluster below $10^{-3}$ and 512 above $1 - 10^{-3}$ — the capacity $1 - \epsilon = 0.5$ emerging as a count of good channels, with the transition sharpening as $n$ grows.

### 4.3 Decoding Polar Codes: Successive Cancellation and List Decoding

Successive-cancellation (SC) decoding mirrors the splitting recursion in the LLR domain. For a node combining LLRs $a, b$ from its children [1]:

- **$f$-operation** (for $W^-$, i.e., $u_1$): $f(a,b) = 2\,\mathrm{atanh}(\tanh(a/2)\tanh(b/2)) \approx \mathrm{sgn}(a)\mathrm{sgn}(b)\min(|a|,|b|)$;
- **$g$-operation** (for $W^+$, i.e., $u_2$ given $\hat{u}_1$): $g(a,b,\hat{u}_1) = b + (1 - 2\hat{u}_1)\,a$.

Bits are decided sequentially — frozen indices to known values, information indices by LLR sign — visiting $N \log_2 N$ nodes. But SC is *greedy*: early errors propagate, and at short blocklengths polarization is incomplete, so SC trails LDPC belief propagation by 1–2 dB at $N = 1024$.

*Successive-cancellation list* (SCL) decoding retains the $L$ most likely partial paths under an LLR-based path metric, approaching maximum-likelihood performance for $L = 8$–$32$; selecting the surviving path that passes an outer CRC (*CRC-aided* SCL, CA-SCL) yields near-ML block error rates at short lengths. This crossover — CA-SCL *outperforming* turbo and LDPC codes for $N \lesssim 1024$ — was the decisive technical argument for 3GPP's 2016 adoption of polar codes for control channels [4].

| Decoder | Complexity | Capacity-achieving | Practical character |
|---|---|---|---|
| BP (flooding) on LDPC | $O(I \cdot E)$ | Ensemble thresholds approach capacity | Massively parallel; layered variants dominate silicon |
| Min-sum BP | $O(I \cdot E)$ | Threshold slightly degraded | Hardware standard; ~0.3 dB loss vs. sum-product |
| SC on polar | $O(N \log N)$ | Yes, proven for all symmetric B-DMCs | Elegant but weak at short $N$ |
| SCL ($L$ paths) | $O(LN \log N)$ | Yes | Near-ML for $L \ge 8$ at short $N$ |
| CA-SCL | $O(LN \log N)$ | Yes | 3GPP control-channel decoder of choice |

### 4.4 The 5G NR Coding Chain: LDPC and Polar in Concert

TS 38.212 [3] assigns each family where its strengths dominate: LDPC's high-throughput iterative decoding for long data blocks, polar's superior short-block performance for control.

**Data path (DL-SCH / UL-SCH).** A transport block of $A$ bits receives CRC attachment ($g_{\mathrm{CRC24A}}$, $L=24$, if $A > 3824$, else $L=16$), then *code-block segmentation* splits oversized blocks, each gaining its own 24-bit CRC ($g_{\mathrm{CRC24B}}$). The *base-graph selection* rule, verified against the specification text, is [3]:

- use **BG2** if $A \le 292$, or if $A \le 3824$ and $R \le 0.67$, or if $R \le 0.25$;
- otherwise use **BG1**,

where BG1 ($46 \times 68$, max $K = 8448$) targets large blocks and high rates, and BG2 ($42 \times 52$, max $K = 3840$) targets small blocks and low rates. Each code block is encoded by the quasi-cyclic LDPC code from *lifting* the base graph with circulant size $Z_c$; the first $2Z_c$ systematic bits are punctured (high-degree nodes whose puncturing *improves* the threshold). Rate matching writes the codeword to a *circular buffer* and reads $E$ bits from a redundancy-version offset ($rv \in \{0,1,2,3\}$), enabling *incremental-redundancy HARQ* with fresh parity per retransmission.

**Control path (PDCCH, PBCH, PUCCH).** Downlink control information receives a 24-bit CRC masked with the RNTI, is interleaved, polar-encoded at $N = 2^n \in [32,512]$, sub-block interleaved, and rate-matched by puncturing, shortening, or repetition [3]. The PBCH maps a 32-bit payload plus timing bits through an $N=512$ polar code to $M=864$ coded bits; PUCCH formats 2/3/4 above 11 UCI bits use polar coding with *distributed* CRC. The chain's asymmetry reflects deliberate engineering: no single code is optimal across 5G's three-orders-of-magnitude payload span [4][7].

| Channel | Code family | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|
| DL-SCH / UL-SCH | QC-LDPC (BG1/BG2) | CB $\le 8448$ (BG1) / $3840$ (BG2); RV 0–3; IR-HARQ |
| PDCCH (DCI) | Polar + CRC24, RNTI-masked | $N = 32 \ldots 512$; puncture/shorten/repeat |
| PBCH | Polar + CRC | $N = 512 \to M = 864$ |
| PUCCH fmt 2/3/4 ($> 11$ bits) | Polar + distributed CRC | Short-block control regime |

## 5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

Theory and measurement converge on a consistent picture. On the guarantee side, Arıkan's original $P_e \le O(N^{-1/4})$ bound [1] was sharpened by Arıkan–Telatar and Korada–Şaşoğlu–Urbanke to the scaling $P_e \approx 2^{-N^\beta}$ for any $\beta < \tfrac{1}{2}$, with $\tfrac{1}{2}$ the optimal exponent of the $2 \times 2$ kernel [2][7]. For LDPC codes, density evolution gives *exact* asymptotic thresholds: the regular $(3,6)$ ensemble on the AWGN channel has threshold $\sigma^* \approx 0.88$ (about $1.1$ dB $E_b/N_0$), while optimized *irregular* ensembles achieve thresholds within $0.0045$ dB of the Shannon limit at rate $1/2$ under belief propagation — the closest any iterative scheme has come to capacity [5].

At finite lengths, DVB-S2 $N = 64800$ LDPC codes operate within roughly $0.7$ dB of capacity at $\mathrm{BER} = 10^{-6}$ before error floors intrude [5]. In 5G silicon, layered min-sum LDPC decoders exceed $10$ Gb/s with syndrome-based early termination, while incremental-redundancy HARQ closes the residual gap across retransmissions [3]. For polar codes, CA-SCL with $L = 32$ at $N = 1024$, $R = 1/2$ nearly closes the SC-to-ML gap and outperforms LTE turbo codes at short blocks — the measured result that carried the 3GPP vote [4].

| Result | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Polarization theorem | $R \to I(W)$, $P_e \le O(N^{-1/4})$, $O(N \log N)$ | [1] |
| Rate of polarization | $P_e \approx 2^{-\sqrt{N}}$; exponent $\tfrac12$ optimal for $2\times2$ | [2] |
| Irregular LDPC thresholds | Within $0.0045$ dB of Shannon limit ($R = 1/2$, AWGN, BP) | [5] |
| 5G standardization | Polar for control, LDPC for data (Rel-15, 2016 decision) | [3][4] |

## 6 Limitations

Neither family is without structural weaknesses, and the standard reflects them. **LDPC limitations:** belief propagation is optimal only on trees — on practical graphs with cycles it is a principled heuristic whose fixed points need not be global optima; *trapping sets* induce error floors that no increase in SNR removes, forcing careful code design (high girth, structured QC constructions) rather than pure ensemble optimization; density-evolution thresholds describe *ensembles*, not individual codes; and naive LDPC encoding is $O(N^2)$, tamed in 5G only by the quasi-cyclic structure enabling linear-time encoding via the Richardson–Urbanke transform [5].

**Polar limitations:** SC decoding is mediocre at finite lengths because polarization is slow — at $N = 1024$ many synthetic channels remain mediocre, and only list decoding rescues the scheme at $O(LN \log N)$ complexity plus memory for $L$ path states; code *construction* is channel-dependent, with mismatched constructions under fading costing decibels; CA-SCL's CRC overhead is proportionally large for very short payloads; and at long blocks BP's parallelism wins on throughput per unit silicon.

**Systems limitations:** 5G pays for two code families in duplicated decoder hardware; circular-buffer rate matching with puncturing incurs a measurable threshold loss versus mother-code performance; and HARQ buffer memory scales with the number of processes and transport-block size, a genuine cost driver in modem design [3].

## 7 Conclusion

LDPC codes and polar codes represent the two complete answers to Shannon's challenge: the former achieving capacity *operationally*, through sparse graphs and iterative inference whose thresholds can be driven arbitrarily close to the limit; the latter *constructively*, through a deterministic polarization transform with a rigorous finite-complexity proof [1][5]. Belief propagation's local message updates and polarization's recursive channel splitting are, at depth, the same idea seen from opposite ends — one distributes computation across a graph, the other concentrates reliability across synthesized channels. Their joint deployment in 5G NR [3], with LDPC carrying data and polar codes carrying control, is not a historical accident but an engineering optimum matched to blocklength: long blocks favor parallel iterative decoding, short blocks favor near-ML list decoding. The remaining frontier is finite-length fundamental limits — closing the gap between normal-approximation bounds and practical decoders at $N \sim 10^2$–$10^3$, and unifying the two families' analyses — work for which the tools developed here, density evolution and polarization martingales, remain the indispensable foundation.
