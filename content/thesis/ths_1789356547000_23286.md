# Homotopy Type Theory: Univalence, Higher Inductive Types, and the Future of Formalized Mathematics

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf's identity types as path spaces, wedding constructive type theory to classical homotopy theory in a single foundation for mathematics. This thesis presents the core architecture of HoTT: Vladimir Voevodsky's *univalence axiom*, which identifies equivalences between types with identities in the universe, and *higher inductive types* (HITs), which freely generate types by both point and path constructors. We develop the homotopy-level hierarchy of truncated types, the encode–decode method for computing path spaces of inductive types, and the transport of structure along equivalences that univalence legitimizes. We then survey the computational response to the canonicity problem posed by axiomatic univalence and HITs — cubical type theory, in which univalence is a theorem with computational content — and the state of formalization in proof assistants such as Coq, Lean, and Agda. The article closes with open problems: semisimplicial types, a general schema for HITs, and the search for a fully computational foundation [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1 Intro

For most of the twentieth century, the default foundation of mathematics was Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory: everything is a set, equality is extensional, and proofs are informal narratives checked by human referees. This arrangement worked, but it was never *computational*. A proof in ZFC cannot, in general, be executed; it carries no algorithmic content beyond what the mathematician happened to put into it. The rise of proof assistants — Coq, Agda, Lean, Isabelle — changed the stakes. Suddenly the question was not only *what* mathematics is true, but *in what formal system* it can be checked by machine, and whether that system admits a faithful computational reading of its proofs [1].

Martin-Löf's dependent type theory offered a compelling answer: propositions as types, proofs as programs, and a notion of definitional equality strong enough to drive computation. Yet intensional Martin-Löf type theory (MLTT) had a notorious wart. Its *identity type* `a = b`, introduced by Martin-Löf in 1973, came with an eliminator — path induction, the famous `J` rule — that was far weaker than mathematicians' informal notion of equality, yet far stranger than set-theoretic equality: Hofmann and Streicher showed that in intensional type theory, identity proofs need not be unique, and types behave like groupoids, with identity proofs as morphisms, higher identities as 2-morphisms, and so on ad infinitum [1]. What looked like a defect was, in retrospect, a discovery.

Vladimir Voevodsky's insight, beginning around 2006 and crystallized during the 2012–2013 Special Year on Univalent Foundations at the Institute for Advanced Study, was to take the groupoid structure seriously as *homotopy* structure: types are spaces, terms are points, identity proofs are paths, and identities between identities are homotopies between paths [1]. The missing piece was an axiom governing the universe of types itself. Voevodsky's *univalence axiom* asserts that the canonical map from identities between types to equivalences between types is itself an equivalence — informally, "equivalent types are identical." Combined with *higher inductive types*, which allow type constructors to generate not only points but also paths, the result is a foundation in which one can do synthetic homotopy theory: define the circle as an inductive type, prove that its loop space is the integers, and have the proof assistant check every step [1][2][3].

This thesis is a systematic exposition of that edifice. We assume familiarity with basic logic and some topology, but no prior acquaintance with dependent type theory. Our aims are threefold: to explain *why* univalence is the right axiom about equality of structures; to show *how* higher inductive types make spaces directly definable in logic; and to assess *where* the program stands — its triumphs in formalization, its computational rehabilitation through cubical type theory, and its open problems [1][4][5][6][7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and the identity type

In Martin-Löf's dependent type theory, every term has a type, and types may depend on terms. The propositions-as-types correspondence reads a proposition as the type of its proofs: to prove `A ∧ B` is to construct a pair, to prove `A → B` is to construct a function. Equality, too, becomes a type former. For a type `A` and terms `a b : A`, there is a type `a =_A b`, the *identity type*, whose canonical inhabitant is `refl_a : a = a`. The elimination rule — *path induction*, or `J` — states that to prove a property `P` of all identifications, it suffices to prove it for reflexivity:

```agda
J : (P : (x y : A) → x ＝ y → Type)
  → ((x : A) → P x x refl)
  → (x y : A) → (p : x ＝ y) → P x y p
```

In words: to reason about all paths it suffices to handle reflexivity. Path induction yields transport, composition, and inversion of paths — yet it does *not* imply uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP): there can be many distinct proofs of `a = b`, just as there can be many distinct paths between two points of a space [1].

### 2.2 The homotopical interpretation

Hofmann and Streicher's 1994 groupoid model made the higher structure explicit: types interpret as groupoids, terms as objects, identity proofs as morphisms. Voevodsky extended the ladder all the way up: types interpret as *Kan complexes* — simplicial sets in which every horn has a filler — i.e., spaces up to homotopy, and the hierarchy of identity types interprets as the hierarchy of path spaces. This *simplicial model*, developed with Kapulkin and Lumsdaine, validates the univalence axiom and shows the theory consistent relative to ZFC with suitable universes [1].

The conceptual payoff is a stratification of types by *homotopy level* (h-level), due to Voevodsky:

| h-level | Name | Characterization |
|---|---|---|
| −2 | Contractible | `Σ(x : A). Π(y : A). x = y` |
| −1 | Mere proposition | `Π(x y : A). x = y` |
| 0 | Set | `Π(x y : A). isProp (x = y)` |
| 1 | Groupoid | identity types are sets |
| n+1 | n-groupoid | identity types are n-types |

Everyday mathematics lives at levels −1 and 0: propositions and sets. But the universe `Type` itself sits at no finite level — it is an ∞-groupoid — and univalence is a statement about its path structure [1].

### 2.3 The problem with equality of structures

Classically, isomorphic groups are "the same for all mathematical purposes," yet in ZFC an isomorphism is not an identity: transporting a theorem across an isomorphism requires manual re-proof, and proof assistants demand it be done explicitly. Category theorists learned to work invariantly, but the *foundation* did not enforce invariance. Voevodsky's diagnosis was that foundations should make structural identity *automatic*: univalence turns "equivalent structures can be identified" from a slogan into a theorem-proving principle [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *synthetic*: rather than defining spaces as sets of points with extra structure, we axiomatize their homotopical behavior directly in type theory. Concretely, this means:

1. **Types as spaces.** We reason about a type `A` the way a homotopy theorist reasons about a space: its points are terms `a : A`, its paths are identifications `p : a = b`, its homotopies are identifications of paths `α : p = q`, and so on.
2. **Path induction as the universal tool.** Nearly every construction on paths is defined by `J`, reducing to the reflexivity case. This disciplines proofs: if a definition does not obviously respect `refl`, it is probably wrong.
3. **The encode–decode method.** To characterize a path space `x = y` in an inductive type, we define a *code* family `code : A → A → Type` by recursion, exhibit maps back and forth between `x = y` and `code x y`, and prove they are mutually inverse. This technique computes loop spaces of higher inductive types from first principles [1][3].
4. **Univalence for transport.** Once `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B)` turns equivalences into identities, any construction or theorem transports along equivalences for free. Invariance under equivalence becomes a metatheorem rather than a discipline [1].
5. **Truncation to control level.** When classical reasoning demands propositions or sets, we apply *propositional truncation* `∥A∥` or *set truncation* `∥A∥₀`, collapsing higher structure and recovering ordinary logic inside the theory [1][3].

This methodology is validated empirically — in the proof-theoretic sense — by large formalizations: the HoTT libraries in Coq and Lean, and the Cubical Agda library, which together check thousands of lemmas of synthetic homotopy theory by machine [2][3][6][7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The univalence axiom

Let `U` be a universe of types. For `A B : U` there is a canonical map

```lean
idtoeqv : (A = B) → (A ≃ B)
```

defined by path induction, sending `refl_A` to the identity equivalence. Voevodsky's *univalence axiom* asserts that `idtoeqv` is itself an equivalence:

> **Theorem:** *(Univalence)* For a universe `U`, the map `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence, so that `(A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B)` [1].

Equivalently, there is a map `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B)` inverse to `idtoeqv`. The consequences are immediate and profound:

- **Function extensionality.** Two functions that agree pointwise are equal — a principle independent of MLTT but provable from univalence. The proof goes through the equivalence between `f = g` and homotopies `f ~ g` [1].
- **Propositional extensionality.** Logically equivalent mere propositions are equal.
- **Transport of structure.** Given `e : A ≃ B` and any family `P : U → Type`, we obtain `transport^P (ua e) : P A → P B`. A theorem proved about one group applies, *by computation of the proof term*, to every isomorphic group. No manual transfer lemmas are needed.
- **The structure identity principle.** For structured types — groups, rings, categories — identities correspond exactly to structure-preserving isomorphisms: the precise form of "isomorphic structures can be identified" [1].

Univalence declares that the *paths* in `U` are exactly the equivalences — the homotopically correct notion of sameness for types-as-spaces.

### 4.2 Higher inductive types

Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point constructors*: the natural numbers by `0` and `succ`, lists by `nil` and `cons`. A *higher inductive type* (HIT) additionally permits *path constructors* — identifications imposed between terms — and the elimination principle must then supply coherence data for those paths [1][3][7].

The paradigmatic example is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ＝ base
```

`S¹` is *freely* generated by one point and one loop: its induction principle says that to define `f : (x : S¹) → P x`, it suffices to give `b : P base` and a path `l : transport^P loop b ＝ b` lying over `loop`. No other paths exist — the type is the homotopy pushout of the schema, the initial algebra for the signature [3][7].

From this sparse definition, the entire theory of the circle unfolds synthetically. The *encode–decode* argument defines a family `code : S¹ → Type` with `code base ≡ ℤ` and `transport^code loop` acting as successor; one then proves `(base = x) ≃ code x` for all `x`, and in particular:

> **Theorem:** *The loop space of the circle is the integers: `(base =_{S¹} base) ≃ ℤ`, and the fundamental group `π₁(S¹)` is infinite cyclic* [1][3].

Other HITs recover the standard toolkit internally: *suspensions* `ΣA`, *pushouts*, *quotients* `A/R`, and the *truncations*. Van Doorn's dissertation develops this machinery at scale, formalizing the Serre and Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences for cohomology in the Lean HoTT library [3]. Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg give the syntax and semantics of spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts in cubical type theory, with judgmental computation rules for all constructors including the higher-dimensional ones [7].

### 4.3 The homotopy hierarchy: h-levels and truncation

Not every type needs infinite homotopical structure. Voevodsky's h-levels measure how much identity structure a type carries, and *truncation* operations collapse types to a prescribed level:

- **Mere propositions** (`isProp A ≔ Π(x y : A). x = y`) are the types with at most one inhabitant — the internal logic of the theory. Classical connectives are recovered by truncation: `A ∨ B ≔ ∥A + B∥`, `∃(x:A). P x ≔ ∥Σ(x:A). P x∥`.
- **Sets** (`isSet A ≔ Π(x y : A). isProp (x = y)`) are the types with unique identity proofs — the internal set theory, in which ordinary algebra and analysis are developed.
- **Propositional truncation** `∥A∥₋₁` universally collapses `A` to a proposition; **set truncation** `∥A∥₀` collapses it to a set. Every map from `A` into an n-type factors uniquely through `∥A∥ₙ` [1][3].

This hierarchy resolves a century-old tension: logic (proof-irrelevant propositions) and mathematics (proof-relevant sets) coexist in one theory, with truncation mediating. Univalence refutes *global* choice — choice cannot respect all equivalences — while choice for sets remains consistent [1].

### 4.4 Cubical type theory and the computational interpretation

In "Book HoTT," univalence and HITs are *axioms*: they postulate new canonical terms without explaining how they compute. The theory therefore lacks *canonicity* — a closed term of type `ℕ` may be stuck on `ua` or a path constructor instead of reducing to a numeral. For a foundation advertised as computational, this is a serious defect [4].

The *cubical* response, due to Bezem, Coquand, and Huber and developed into a full type theory by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, replaces axioms with geometry [5][6]:

1. **Paths as maps out of an interval.** Introduce a formal interval object `I` with endpoints; a path in `A` is a term of `Path A a b ≔ (i : I) → A` with the right endpoints. Path algebra becomes function application.
2. **Composition and filling.** A Kan-like *composition* operation fills open boxes, giving transport, composition, and inversion of paths definitionally.
3. **Glue types.** Voevodsky's univalence is proved via the *Glue* construction, which glues a partial equivalence-defined family onto a total type along a cofibration. The resulting `ua` *computes*: transporting along `ua e` applies the equivalence `e` [5].
4. **Canonicity restored.** Huber proved canonicity for cubical type theory: every closed term of natural-number type reduces to a numeral, via a computability argument over a presheaf semantics [6]. Angiuli and collaborators extended the computational interpretation to cumulative univalent universes and a schema of *cubical inductive types* covering circles, tori, pushouts, and localizations [4].

Cubical type theory is therefore the leading candidate for a *computational* univalent foundation — one in which proofs of equality compute, and proof assistants such as Cubical Agda and `cubicaltt` can execute them [5][6][7].

### 4.5 Formalization in proof assistants

The univalent program has always been inseparable from machine checking; indeed, many proofs in the HoTT Book were discovered *through* formalization [1]. The landscape:

| System | Library / mode | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Coq | HoTT/HoTT library | Large corpus: equivalences, HITs as axioms, synthetic homotopy theory |
| Lean 2 | HoTT/Lean library | Van Doorn–von Raumer–Buchholtz: quotients and truncations as primitives, cubical methods; `πₙ(Sⁿ) ≃ ℤ`, Hopf fibrations, Freudenthal [2][3] |
| Agda | Cubical Agda | Native cubical mode: univalence provable, HITs compute |
| cubicaltt | Experimental checker | Cohen et al.'s reference implementation [5] |

Van Doorn, von Raumer, and Buchholtz report that nearly all of Chapter 8 of the HoTT Book — connectedness, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the complex and quaternionic Hopf fibrations, the long exact sequence of homotopy groups — has been formalized in Lean, including the computation `π₃(S²) ≃ ℤ` [2]. Van Doorn's dissertation pushes further, formalizing the Serre and Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences for cohomology entirely inside HoTT [3]. These are not toy results: spectral sequences are among the most technically demanding tools of twentieth-century algebraic topology, and their synthetic formalization is evidence that univalent foundations scale to real mathematics.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We collect the landmark synthetic results — the "experiments" by which the theory proves its worth. Each is a machine-checked theorem in at least one assistant [1][2][3].

> **Theorem:** *(Fundamental group of the circle)* `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ` as groups. *Proof sketch.* Encode–decode: define `code : S¹ → Type` by circle recursion with `code base ≔ ℤ` and `transport loop ≔ succ`; show `Σ(x : S¹). code x` is contractible, whence `(base = x) ≃ code x` for all `x`. Truncating gives the group isomorphism [1].

> **Theorem:** *(Eckmann–Hilton)* For `α β : refl_a = refl_a` in `Ω²(A, a)`, `α ∙ β = β ∙ α`. The second loop space is always abelian — proved by a 2-dimensional path induction, the *syllepsis*, with no recourse to topology [1].

> **Theorem:** *(Function extensionality from univalence)* `ua` implies `(f ~ g) → (f = g)` for dependent functions. The proof factors through equivalences of fibers; it is the reason univalence "repairs" intensional type theory's most-missed principle [1].

> **Theorem:** *(Freudenthal suspension)* If `A` is n-connected, the canonical map `A → ΩΣA` is 2n-connected. Formalized in Lean with applications to the stability of homotopy groups of spheres [1][2].

> **Theorem:** *(Brunerie)* `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ` — a synthetic computation carried out entirely inside HoTT, showing the theory handles the delicate arguments of classical homotopy theory without simplicial sets [1].

These results share a methodology: state the classical theorem, find its synthetic proof via path induction, encode–decode, and univalence, and let the kernel check every coherence. Invariance under equivalence eliminates the bookkeeping of transferring structures across isomorphisms [1][2][3].

## 6 Limitations

Enthusiasm must be tempered. The program faces genuine mathematical and engineering obstacles [1][4][5][6][7]:

1. **Canonicity for Book HoTT is open.** Cubical type theory enjoys canonicity [6], but the original axiomatic formulation — univalence plus HITs as axioms atop MLTT — still lacks a canonicity or normalization proof. Whether every closed natural-number term of Book HoTT computes to a numeral remains unknown.
2. **No general schema for HITs (yet).** We have schemas for large classes — cubical inductive types [4], and the syntax of Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg for spheres, suspensions, truncations, pushouts [7] — but no accepted general schema covering *all* HITs with proved metatheory. Inductive-inductive and higher inductive-inductive types remain especially delicate.
3. **Semisimplicial types are not internalizable.** The type of n-truncated semisimplicial types cannot be defined uniformly in n inside HoTT — a rare example of a natural construction that resists the theory, and an active research target [1].
4. **Performance of cubical implementations.** Cubical Agda's path and composition machinery carries real computational overhead; large developments can be significantly slower to check than their axiomatic counterparts, and the ergonomics of higher-dimensional goal states remain rough [5][7].
5. **Classical principles need care.** Univalence refutes global choice, and combining univalence with excluded middle requires restricting LEM to mere propositions. Mathematicians accustomed to unrestricted classical reasoning must learn where the guardrails are [1].
6. **The universe is wild.** `U` is not truncated at any finite level, and internal characterizations of its homotopy type are essentially unexplored.

None of these is a refutation; several are active frontiers. But they mark the distance between a promising foundation and a finished one.

## 7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory proposes that equality was never about sameness of *presentation* but about existence of *paths* — and that the paths between types are equivalences. From this single reorientation flow the univalence axiom, the synthetic treatment of spaces via higher inductive types, a principled hierarchy of truncated types recovering logic and set theory, and a computational rehabilitation through cubical methods that makes univalence compute [1][4][5][6].

The empirical record is strong: the fundamental group of the circle, Eckmann–Hilton, Freudenthal, the Hopf fibrations, spectral sequences — all formalized, all machine-checked, several first discovered through the formalization itself [1][2][3]. The cubical turn answers the canonicity objection that shadowed the axiomatic theory, giving a foundation in which proofs of equality are programs that run [4][5][6][7].

What remains is consolidation: a general HIT schema with full metatheory, canonicity for the book theory or a proof that the cubical theory suffices, internal semisimplicial types, and proof assistants fast enough for working mathematicians. Voevodsky's gamble was that foundations should be *invariant* — that mathematics done in them should automatically respect equivalence. A decade of synthetic homotopy theory suggests the gamble is paying off [1].
