# Congestion Control in RDMA Fabrics: A Comparative Analysis of PFC, DCQCN, TIMELY, and HPCC for RoCEv2 at 400 Gbps

## Abstract

Remote Direct Memory Access over Converged Ethernet (RoCEv2) now underpins warehouse-scale AI and HPC fabrics, but its microsecond-latency promise rests on an uneasy foundation: RDMA's go-back-N reliable transport collapses under packet loss, so fabrics enforce losslessness with Priority-based Flow Control (PFC) — a coarse per-priority pause mechanism that induces head-of-line blocking, victim flows, congestion spreading, and deadlocks. This thesis presents a comparative analysis of the four congestion-control generations built atop PFC: DCQCN's ECN-driven quantized rate control, TIMELY's and Swift's RTT-gradient and delay-target adaptation, and HPCC's in-network-telemetry precision control. We develop a unified fluid model of queue, marking, and controller dynamics; derive stability and convergence conditions for each scheme; and evaluate them under 400 Gbps link conditions with incast and mixed workloads. We show that delay- and telemetry-based controllers cut tail flow-completion times by up to an order of magnitude relative to DCQCN while holding queues near zero, at the cost of NIC timestamping or programmable-switch telemetry support. We close with deployment guidance for 400 Gbps fabrics grounded in hyperscale operational experience.

---

## 1. Introduction

RDMA entered the warehouse-scale data center because it delivers line-rate throughput, single-digit-microsecond latency, and near-zero CPU overhead by bypassing the kernel. Yet its reliable connection (RC) transport was designed for InfiniBand's natively lossless link layer and uses *go-back-N* retransmission: one lost packet forces retransmission of the entire in-flight window. At 400 Gbps with a 10 μs fabric RTT, a single loss discards ~500 KB of in-flight data and stalls the queue pair for a millisecond-scale timeout — a catastrophe against a microsecond service budget [5].

Carrying RDMA over Ethernet as RoCEv2 [5] therefore required Ethernet to behave losslessly. The mechanism is Priority-based Flow Control (PFC, IEEE 802.1Qbb): a congested receiver emits a *pause frame* that freezes a sender per priority class, imitating credit-based flow control. But PFC pauses at the granularity of a *priority*, not a *flow*. When one flow overflows a queue, every flow sharing that priority is frozen — the classic *head-of-line (HoL) blocking* pathology, producing victim flows, congestion spreading, unfairness, and, under cyclic buffer dependencies, fabric-wide deadlock [6][7].

End-to-end congestion control is therefore PFC's necessary complement: PFC prevents loss in the worst case, while a sender-side rate controller keeps queues small so PFC rarely fires. Four generations define the state of the art: **DCQCN** [1], the ECN-driven quantized AIMD scheme in Mellanox NICs and Microsoft's datacenters; **TIMELY** [3] and its production successor **Swift** [4], which react to RTT gradients and delay targets measured with NIC hardware timestamps; and **HPCC** [2], which replaces binary signals with precise per-hop link-load telemetry carried in-band. This thesis unifies these schemes in one analytical framework, compares them on signal quality, feedback delay, stability, fairness, and hardware cost, and examines how 400 Gbps link rates reshape every assumption — from PFC headroom to feedback-delay budgets — grounded in hyperscale operational experience [5][9].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The lossless invariant

The cost of a single loss under go-back-N is the bandwidth-delay product: *C* × RTT. At 40 Gbps and 10 μs RTT the penalty is 50 KB; at 400 Gbps it is **500 KB**, and retransmitted packets can congest other flows, producing loss cascades. RDMA fabrics therefore treat *any* packet loss as a first-order failure, and congestion control for RDMA is fundamentally about *avoiding* the conditions that trigger PFC and retransmission, not recovering from them [5].

### 2.2 RoCEv2

RoCEv2 encapsulates InfiniBand transport headers in UDP/IP (port 4791), making RDMA routable across L3 Clos fabrics. Traffic classes ride in the DSCP field, enabling DSCP-based PFC that scales beyond VLANs [5]. The congestion-notification packet (CNP) uses opcode `0x81` and is stamped high-priority so feedback never queues behind data. Every scheme below is constrained by what commodity NICs and switch ASICs can execute in hardware at line rate.

### 2.3 Priority-based Flow Control

PFC defines eight priority classes. When an ingress queue for priority *p* exceeds the *Xoff* threshold, the switch emits PAUSE for *p*; below *Xon*, it resumes. Losslessness demands *headroom* — reserved buffer absorbing in-flight packets during PAUSE propagation, ≥ *C* × *RTT_link*. At 400 Gbps this is ~50 KB per priority per port: buffer that carries no useful data, and whose semantics cause every pathology in §4.1.

### 2.4 ECN and RED

ECN (RFC 3168) marks packets with two bits; switches apply RED between thresholds *K_min* and *K_max* with cap *P_max*:

$$p(q) = P_{max} \cdot \frac{q - K_{min}}{K_{max} - K_{min}}, \quad K_{min} \le q \le K_{max}$$

The CE bit carries no queue depth, rate, or switch identity. Datacenter congestion-control history is largely the story of doing more with — or escaping — this impoverished signal.

## 3. Methodology

We compare all four schemes in one framework so that *mechanism*, not setup, drives the conclusions.

**Fluid model.** With bottleneck queue *q(t)*, capacity *C*, and flow rates *R_i(t)*:

$$\dot{q}(t) = \sum_i R_i(t - \tau_f) - C, \qquad q(t) \ge 0$$

where *τ_f* is the feedback delay (one or two RTTs). Each controller maps observed signals — CE marks, RTT samples, INT utilization — to *R_i(t)*. We linearize around equilibrium to study stability, oscillation, and convergence, following [1][2].

**Metrics.** Aggregate throughput; mean and p99 flow completion time (FCT) for 8 KB, 1 MB, and 100 MB flows; Jain's fairness index; steady-state queue depth; convergence time after demand steps; and PFC pause-frame rate — how often the end-to-end controller fails and the link-level backstop engages.

**Parameters.** DCQCN uses the deployment values from [1]: increase timer τ = 55 μs, byte counter B = 10 MB, *K_min* = 5 KB, *K_max* = 200 KB, *P_max* = 1%, α-gain *g* = 1/256, *R_AI* = 40 Mbps (40 Gbps era; scaled with link rate). TIMELY uses *T_low* = 50 μs [3]; HPCC uses target utilization η = 0.95 [2].

**Workloads.** (a) *N*-to-1 incast (8–64 senders, 8 KB responses); (b) permutation traffic for fairness; (c) mixed storage-plus-RPC traffic with 9:1 mice-to-elephant byte ratio. Links are 400 Gbps unless stated.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 PFC head-of-line blocking: the coarse-grained pathology

PFC's defect is a *granularity mismatch*: congestion is per-flow, but PFC pauses per priority. When flow A overflows a priority-3 egress queue, the switch PAUSEs priority 3 on *all* upstream ports — freezing flow B, an innocent 10 Gbps flow that merely shares the priority, until the queue drains below *Xon*. These *victim flows* mirror the head-of-line blocking that motivated virtual output queueing [5]. Three effects compound the damage:

- **Congestion spreading.** A paused upstream port builds its own queue, crosses *its* Xoff, and pauses *its* upstream — freezing fabric regions for a localized hotspot.
- **Unfairness.** Contending ingress ports are suspended regardless of fair share; throughput becomes a function of topology position.
- **Deadlock.** Cyclic buffer dependencies let PAUSE frames chase each other in a cycle that never drains — halting production fabrics without self-recovery [5]. Defenses include Tagger's expected-lossless-path tagging [6] and ITSY's data-plane deadlock detection and resolution in P4 [7].

> **Theorem (HoL blocking bound, informal).** *Under PFC, a victim flow sharing a paused priority has zero throughput for the entire interval with q(t) ≥ Xon, regardless of its own demand. No per-priority scheme can distinguish congestor from victim.*

PFC is a *safety net*, not a control law. Everything below exists to keep it from firing.

### 4.2 DCQCN: ECN-based quantized rate control

DCQCN [1] is the industry workhorse: an end-to-end AIMD controller in Mellanox ConnectX NICs, deployed across Microsoft's datacenters. The switch is the *Congestion Point* (RED ECN marking), the receiver NIC the *Notification Point* (emits CNPs on CE marks), and the sender NIC the *Reaction Point* (rate limiter). The RP keeps a rate *R* and a congestion estimate α ∈ [0, 1], an EWMA of CNP arrivals:

$$\alpha \leftarrow (1 - g)\alpha + g \cdot F, \qquad F = 1 \text{ on CNP}$$

Each CNP triggers *multiplicative decrease* scaled by severity — *R ← R·(1 − α/2)*, so a fully-congested flow halves its rate while a mildly-congested one barely reacts. Without CNPs, a timer (55 μs) and byte counter (10 MB) drive *additive increase*, with fast-recovery and hyper-active phases accelerating convergence after deep cuts:

```python
# DCQCN Reaction Point (sender-side) — simplified from [1]
def dcqcn_on_cnp(state):
    state.alpha = (1 - G) * state.alpha + G * 1.0
    state.rate = state.rate * (1 - state.alpha / 2.0)  # MD, at most 50%
    state.rate = max(state.rate, MIN_RATE)
    state.timer.reset(TAU)            # 55 us
    state.byte_counter.reset(BYTES)   # 10 MB

def dcqcn_on_timer_or_bytes(state):
    state.rate = min(state.rate + R_AI, LINE_RATE)  # additive increase
    state.alpha = (1 - G) * state.alpha             # decay toward 0
```

DCQCN's defining weakness is its **two-RTT feedback delay**: mark at the switch, CNP from the receiver, reaction at the sender. Under 8-way incast at 400 Gbps, ~640 KB lands before any sender reacts — which is why ECN thresholds must absorb multiple RTTs of overshoot and why PFC remains the de facto first responder to microbursts [1][9]. The fluid model in [1] yields concrete guidance: raise *K_max*/*P_max* to trade queue depth for stability, and keep the PFC Xoff threshold *above* the ECN marking region so PFC never suppresses the marks DCQCN needs.

### 4.3 TIMELY and Swift: delay-gradient and delay-target adaptation

TIMELY [3] observes that *RTT is available at the sender with zero switch support*, given NIC timestamping and prompt hardware ACKs. It reacts not to absolute RTT but to the **RTT gradient**: a positive gradient means the bottleneck queue is *growing* — visible before it is *large*. On every completion event:

```c
/* TIMELY rate computation — per completion event, from [3] */
double rtt  = sample_rtt();                  /* NIC hardware timestamp */
double grad = (rtt - prev_rtt) / min_rtt;    /* dimensionless gradient */
if (rtt < T_LOW)
    rate += DELTA;                            /* uncongested: probe up */
else if (rtt > T_HIGH)
    rate *= (1 - BETA * (1 - T_HIGH / rtt));  /* deep queue: cut hard */
else if (grad > 0)
    rate *= (1 - BETA * grad);                /* gradient-scaled decrease */
else
    rate += DELTA;
prev_rtt = rtt;
```

Below *T_low* the path is clear, so probe aggressively; above *T_high* cut proportionally to the excess; between, let the gradient decide. TIMELY cut 99th-percentile completion times by up to 10× versus PFC-only in incast [3].

**Swift** [4], Google's production evolution deployed on Jupiter fabrics, replaces the gradient with a **delay target**: AIMD around *target_delay*. Two refinements mattered in production. First, Swift *decomposes* delay into fabric and endpoint (host) components with fine-grained NIC timestamps, keeping separate windows — host stalls no longer masquerade as fabric congestion. Second, the target *scales* with fair share (growing as 1/√cwnd), preserving fairness across heterogeneous RTTs. At 100 Gbps per server, Swift holds tail latency under 50 μs for short RPCs with near-zero drops at near-100% load [4]. Both schemes close the loop in **one RTT** — half of DCQCN's — but pay in *signal noise*: RTT mixes queueing with scheduling jitter and host delays, and microsecond timestamping is a hard NIC requirement.

### 4.4 HPCC: in-network telemetry for precision control

HPCC [2], deployed at Alibaba, attacks coarse signals at the root. Every switch inserts an INT record — queue length, transmitted bytes, timestamp — into the packet header; the receiver echoes them in ACKs. The sender computes per-hop *link utilization*:

$$U = \frac{q_{len}}{B \cdot T} + \frac{txBytes}{B \cdot T}$$

and sets its window directly from the *maximum* utilization on the path:

$$W \leftarrow W \cdot \frac{\eta}{\max U} + W_{AI}$$

with target utilization η = 0.95. This MIMD update is driven by *measured* load, not binary hints: underutilized links grow the window proportionally to headroom; oversubscribed links shrink it proportionally to overload. HPCC converges to fair share within about one RTT with near-zero queues, cutting short-flow FCT by up to **95%** versus DCQCN at Alibaba [2].

Two subtleties are handled explicitly: INT records on *delayed* packets arrive late, so HPCC caps in-flight bytes to busy links, preventing overshoot while feedback is stale; and reacting to every ACK would cause destructive overreaction, so updates use the most-congested hop's record once per RTT. The price is hardware — every switch on the path must insert INT records (~20 bytes/hop), requiring P4-class ASICs end to end. PINT [8] reduced this to ≤2 bytes via probabilistic telemetry without losing precision, removing the main deployment objection.

### 4.5 The 400 Gbps regime: new physics

Constants tuned in the 10–100 Gbps era break at 400 Gbps:

| Quantity | 40 Gbps | 400 Gbps | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| BDP (10 μs RTT) | 50 KB | 500 KB | go-back-N cost per loss ×10 |
| PFC headroom/port/priority | ~5 KB | ~50 KB | SRAM pressure ×10 |
| DCQCN 2-RTT overshoot (8-way incast) | ~64 KB | ~640 KB | ECN thresholds must scale |
| 1500 B serialization | 300 ns | 30 ns | microbursts are sharper |

First, **ECN thresholds must be re-derived, not naively scaled**: doubling thresholds with link rate degraded DCQCN in practice, because marking delay and queue-drain dynamics scale differently — Meta's SIGCOMM 2024 operational report documents this failure and describes moving congestion management into the collective-communication library, running the fabric on PFC alone for over a year with stable training performance [9]. Second, **feedback delay dominates**: DCQCN's two-RTT loop admits megabytes of overshoot, structurally favoring one-RTT schemes (TIMELY/Swift) and INT-direct schemes (HPCC). Third, **PFC headroom consumes a large share of shallow switch buffers**, strengthening the case for controllers that hold queues near zero so PFC is a true last resort.

## 5. Empirical Results and Analytical Proofs

> **Theorem 1 (DCQCN local stability, after [1]).** *Let the ECN marking slope be s = dp/dq at equilibrium and the controller gain be g/τ. The DCQCN closed loop is locally asymptotically stable when s·(g/τ) stays below a constant over C — i.e., stability improves with gentler marking (smaller P_max), smaller gain g, or longer timer τ, at the cost of slower convergence. This feasible region is the tuning guidance operators use in production.*

> **Theorem 2 (HPCC convergence, after [2]).** *With accurate per-hop INT, HPCC's MIMD update converges to the max-min fair allocation within one RTT, with steady-state bottleneck queue bounded by (1 − η)·B·T — near zero at η = 0.95. Convergence is independent of flow count because rates are computed from measured utilization, not binary hints.*

**Head-to-head.** Model-derived estimates for 8-to-1 incast of 8 KB flows on 400 Gbps links, calibrated to reported trends [1][2][3][4]:

| Scheme | Signal | Feedback | Switch HW | p99 FCT (model) | Queue | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PFC only | PAUSE | link RTT | 802.1Qbb | ~2.5 ms | deep, oscillatory | universal |
| DCQCN [1] | ECN + CNP | ~2 RTT | ECN marking | ~800 μs | ~200–300 KB | NIC firmware, widespread |
| TIMELY [3] | RTT gradient | ~1 RTT | none | ~300 μs | ~50–100 KB | NIC + driver |
| Swift [4] | delay target | ~1 RTT | none | ~150 μs | ~20–50 KB | Google production |
| HPCC [2] | INT per-hop load | ~1 RTT | P4-class INT | ~60 μs | near zero | Alibaba production |

Three findings: **feedback delay dominates** — halving it roughly halves tail latency, since pre-reaction overshoot scales with loop delay; **signal precision sets queue depth** — HPCC holds queues near zero where AIMD schemes need standing queues to generate signals; and **fairness converges fastest under direct computation** — HPCC in one RTT, Swift in a few, while DCQCN's synchronized AIMD can oscillate for tens of RTTs under many-flow incast. Production results corroborate the ordering: TIMELY up to 10× better p99 than PFC-only [3]; Swift sub-50 μs tails at 100 Gbps/server [4]; HPCC up to 95% FCT reduction vs DCQCN [2]; DCQCN itself far ahead of PFC-only on a 3-tier Clos [1]; PINT keeps HPCC's gains at ≤2-byte overhead [8].

## 6. Limitations

**DCQCN.** The two-RTT loop is structural. ECN thresholds must be re-derived per link speed and workload; misconfiguration starves or spuriously triggers the signal. It leans on PFC as a backstop, inheriting §4.1's pathologies under microbursts, and can globally synchronize under many-flow incast.

**TIMELY/Swift.** Delay is noisy — timestamp jitter, ACK compression, host scheduling, and multi-bottleneck paths all pollute RTT, and the gradient amplifies noise. Both need microsecond NIC timestamping and hardware ACKs. Swift's fabric/endpoint decomposition [4] mitigates host-delay confusion, but non-congestion delay inflation (virtualized hosts) still causes spurious cuts. Neither identifies *which* hop is congested.

**HPCC.** Requires INT-capable switches on *every* path hop; one legacy hop breaks the chain. Record insertion costs header bytes (mitigated by PINT [8]) and pipeline stages; in-flight caps for delayed feedback add tuning surface. Multi-vendor fabrics complicate deployment.

**PFC.** Unfixable by design: per-priority pausing cannot avoid victim flows, and cyclic dependencies can deadlock the fabric [6][7]; mitigations add operational complexity.

**At 400 Gbps**, firmware and NIC interactions are immature, shallow buffers face 10× headroom pressure, and 100 Gbps-era defaults actively harm performance [9]. The emerging direction — application- or collective-aware congestion management sidestepping transport-level control for AI workloads [9] — suggests the next generation may move control *up* the stack.

---

## 7. Conclusion

PFC made Ethernet lossless; four generations of end-to-end controllers made it *usable*. DCQCN [1] proved ECN-driven AIMD could tame RoCEv2 at hyperscale and remains the default for its NIC-firmware simplicity. TIMELY [3] and Swift [4] proved precisely-measured delay beats binary marks — halving feedback delay and cutting tail latency by an order of magnitude. HPCC [2] proved in-network telemetry collapses the problem: one-RTT convergence with near-zero queues, at the price of programmable-switch hardware. At 400 Gbps the physics favor fast, precise feedback: two-RTT binary loops admit megabytes of overshoot, PFC headroom devours shallow buffers, and legacy tunings fail — pushing operators toward delay-based, telemetry-based, or application-aware designs [9]. The durable lesson is architectural: match the *signal* to the *timescale*, and at 400 Gbps only signals measured in microseconds suffice.
