# Continual Test-Time Adaptation for Vision Models: Entropy Minimization, Mean-Teacher Stabilization, and the Limits of Online Self-Supervision

## Abstract

Test-time adaptation (TTA) asks a trained vision model to repair itself on unlabeled test batches drawn from shifted distributions, without access to source data or labels. This thesis traces the central arc of the field from TENT's fully test-time entropy minimization through the failure modes it exposes under continual distribution shift — error accumulation, confirmation bias, and catastrophic forgetting — to the mechanisms invented to contain them: CoTTA's weight-averaged teacher with stochastic restoration, EATA's sample-efficient entropy filtering with Fisher-regularized anti-forgetting, SAR's sharpness-aware entropy minimization with a model-recovery scheme, and dynamic teacher-update schedules (DeTTA) that adapt teacher momentum to estimated shift magnitude. We formalize the shared online adaptation loop, derive the entropy gradient structure that explains both the power and brittleness of confidence maximization, and evaluate the methods under the standardized CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C corruption benchmarks at multiple severities. Reported results, sharpness-based generalization bounds, and a method taxonomy are presented alongside open problems: batch-size sensitivity, class-imbalanced streams, and the fundamental limits of unsupervised self-correction.

---

## 1. Introduction

Deep image classifiers achieve remarkable accuracy when training and test data are drawn from the same distribution, yet their performance can collapse under *dataset shift*: sensor degradation, weather, compression artifacts, or synthetic corruptions encountered after deployment [1]. Classical remedies — domain adaptation with access to labeled source and unlabeled target data, or domain generalization through invariant training — are unavailable at inference time in the most constrained and most realistic setting: the model is already shipped, the source data are gone (for privacy or storage reasons), and only a stream of unlabeled test samples remains. *Fully test-time adaptation* (TTA) addresses exactly this setting: given only the test data and its own parameters, the model must adapt itself to generalize to new and different data during testing [1].

The modern TTA literature begins with a strikingly simple idea. TENT (Wang et al., ICLR 2021) proposes to optimize the model for *confidence*, measured by the Shannon entropy of its predictions, updating only batch-normalization statistics and channel-wise affine parameters online on each test batch [1]. No auxiliary self-supervised branch, no altered training pipeline, one epoch of test-time optimization — and yet a new state of the art on ImageNet-C. The elegance of entropy minimization made it the reference point for everything that followed.

But confidence is not correctness. When the test stream is *continual* — a sequence of shifting domains, as in autonomous driving where weather, lighting, and location change over time — naive entropy minimization accumulates its own errors. Pseudo-labels generated by an already-degraded model reinforce the degradation; the model drifts from the source solution and eventually *collapses*, performing worse than no adaptation at all. This thesis studies the five landmark responses to that crisis: **TENT**, which defined the paradigm; **CoTTA**, which introduced a weight-averaged teacher and stochastic restoration to bound error accumulation under continual shifts; **EATA**, which made entropy minimization sample-efficient and added a Fisher regularizer against forgetting; **SAR**, which replaced naive entropy descent with sharpness-aware minimization plus a model-recovery scheme for the wild; and **DeTTA**, the family of dynamic teacher-update methods that modulate how fast the teacher follows the student as a function of estimated shift magnitude.

Our contributions are fourfold: (i) a unified formalization of the online TTA loop shared by all five methods; (ii) a derivation of the entropy gradient identity that explains when confidence maximization sharpens correct decisions and when it amplifies mistakes; (iii) a comparative evaluation on CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C with reported error rates, sharpness-based generalization bounds, and a taxonomy table; and (iv) a candid account of the limits — small-batch instability, imbalanced streams, and the irreducible risk of unsupervised self-correction.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Problem formulation

Let $f_{\theta}: \mathcal{X} \to \Delta^{C-1}$ be a probabilistic classifier with parameters $\theta$, trained on a labeled source distribution $\mathcal{P}_s(\mathbf{x}, y)$. At test time we observe a stream of unlabeled batches $\mathcal{D}_t^{(1)}, \dots, \mathcal{D}_t^{(T)}$ with $\mathcal{D}_t^{(k)} = \{\mathbf{x}_j^{(k)}\}_{j=1}^{N_k}$, where $\mathbf{x}_j^{(k)} \sim \mathcal{Q}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x})$ and $\mathcal{Q}^{(k)} \neq \mathcal{P}_s$ in general [8]. In the *single-domain* TTA setting, $\mathcal{Q}^{(k)}$ is fixed; in the *continual* TTA (CTTA) setting, $\mathcal{Q}^{(k)} \neq \mathcal{Q}^{(k+1)}$ for at least some $k$, and adaptation must be performed online, without access to source data or to past or future test batches [8]. At each step $t$ the model must first predict $f_{\theta_t}(\mathbf{x}_t)$ and then update $\theta_t \to \theta_{t+1}$; evaluation is based on the online predictions themselves.

### 2.2 Benchmarks: CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C

The standardized evaluation suite for corruption robustness was introduced by Hendrycks and Dietterich: **CIFAR-10-C** and **ImageNet-C** apply 15 common corruption types (noise, blur, weather, digital) at 5 severity levels to the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet validation sets respectively, yielding 75 corruption–severity conditions [10]. The continual protocol presents all 15 corruptions sequentially at severity 5, forcing online adaptation across a non-stationary stream. Aggregate accuracy is the headline metric, but condition-level analysis shows aggregation can mask harm: a method may improve the mean while degrading specific conditions relative to the source model [10].

### 2.3 Batch normalization as the adaptation substrate

Nearly all entropy-based TTA operates through batch-normalization layers. For a feature channel with batch mean $\mu_B$ and variance $\sigma_B^2$, batch norm computes $\hat{x} = (x - \mu_B)/\sqrt{\sigma_B^2 + \epsilon}$, followed by a learned affine transform $y = \gamma \hat{x} + \beta$. Under distribution shift, source statistics $(\mu_s, \sigma_s^2)$ are systematically wrong; re-estimating $(\mu, \sigma^2)$ on test batches already recovers substantial accuracy, and the affine parameters $\gamma, \beta$ provide a low-dimensional, channel-wise modulation space that can be optimized without touching the convolutional filters [1]. TENT's insight was that this modulation space is precisely the right capacity for test-time repair: expressive enough to absorb covariate shift, constrained enough to resist overfitting to a single batch.

## 3. Methodology: The Unified Online Adaptation Loop

Despite their differences, TENT, CoTTA, EATA, SAR, and DeTTA instantiate a common online loop. We formalize it here so that each method can be read as a specific choice of its components.

**Definition (Online TTA loop).** Given source parameters $\theta_0$, an unsupervised objective $\mathcal{L}$, a trainable subset $\tilde{\Theta} \subseteq \Theta$, a sample-selection predicate $S(\cdot)$, and a teacher-update rule $\mathcal{U}$, each step $t$ executes:

1. **Predict:** compute $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = f_{\theta_t}(\mathbf{x}_t)$ on the incoming batch (predictions are the evaluated output).
2. **Select:** retain $\mathcal{X}_t^{\text{sel}} = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbf{x}_t : S(\mathbf{x}) = 1\}$ (TENT and CoTTA select everything; EATA and SAR filter).
3. **Optimize:** update $\tilde{\Theta}$ by one or more gradient steps on $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X}_t^{\text{sel}}; \theta_t)$.
4. **Stabilize:** apply $\mathcal{U}$ — EMA teacher update, stochastic restoration, sharpness perturbation, or recovery reset.

The design space decomposes along four axes: **(a)** the objective (entropy, consistency, symmetric cross-entropy [5]); **(b)** the parameter subset (BN affine only, full network, prompts); **(c)** the selection rule (all samples, low-entropy, non-redundant, batch-size-robust); and **(d)** the stabilization mechanism (weight averaging, restoration, Fisher regularization, flat-minima seeking). Table 1 taxonomizes the five methods along these axes.

| Method | Objective | Parameters updated | Selection | Stabilization |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TENT** [1] | Shannon entropy $H(\hat{\mathbf{y}})$ | BN affine $\gamma, \beta$ | all samples | none (single-domain) |
| **CoTTA** | consistency vs. augmentation-averaged teacher pseudo-labels | all (student) | all samples | EMA teacher $\alpha\!=\!0.999$, stochastic restoration $p\!=\!0.01$ |
| **EATA** [2] | entropy on selected samples $+$ Fisher penalty | BN affine (reduced subset) | reliability $\times$ diversity score $S(\mathbf{x})$ | Fisher regularizer $\sum_i \omega_i(\theta_i - \theta_i^o)^2$ |
| **SAR** | sharpness-aware entropy $\max_{\Vert\varepsilon\Vert \le \rho} H$ | BN affine / GN affine | low-entropy reliable samples | model recovery on entropy spike |
| **DeTTA** family [8] | teacher–student consistency | student (+ adaptive teacher) | all or filtered | **dynamic** teacher momentum $\alpha_t = g(\text{shift}_t)$ |

*Table 1. Taxonomy of the five methods along the four design axes of the unified loop.*

```python
import torch

def tent_style_step(model, x, optimizer):
    """One online TTA step: TENT optimizes BN affine params by entropy."""
    model.train()  # BN uses batch statistics, not source running stats
    for n, p in model.named_parameters():
        p.requires_grad = ("bn" in n) and ("weight" in n or "bias" in n)
    logits = model(x)
    p = logits.softmax(dim=-1)
    entropy = -(p * p.log()).sum(dim=-1).mean()   # H(y_hat)
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    entropy.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    return logits.detach()
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 TENT: Test Entropy Minimization via Feature Modulation

TENT's formulation is direct. With no supervision available, the model optimizes itself for *confidence*, measured by the Shannon entropy of its predictions [1]:

$$\min_{\tilde{\Theta}} \; E(\mathbf{x}; \Theta) = -\sum_{c=1}^{C} f_{\Theta}(c \mid \mathbf{x}) \, \log f_{\Theta}(c \mid \mathbf{x}),$$

where the optimization is restricted to the channel-wise affine transformations of normalization layers while batch statistics are re-estimated on the test batch. The method requires a compatible model — trained, probabilistic, differentiable — and nothing else: no altered training, no source data, no auxiliary branch.

Why should confidence track correctness? The gradient identity below is the key. Let $\mathbf{z}$ be logits and $\mathbf{p} = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{z})$. Then:

> **Theorem (Entropy gradient structure).** For $H(\mathbf{p}) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i$,
>
> $$\frac{\partial H}{\partial z_k} = p_k\big(\log(1/p_k) - H(\mathbf{p})\big).$$

*Proof sketch.* $\partial H/\partial z_k = -\sum_i p_i(\delta_{ik} - p_k)(\log p_i + 1) = -p_k\log p_k - p_k + p_k(-H + 1) = p_k(-\log p_k - H)$. ∎

The identity shows entropy descent is *self-reinforcing*: classes whose surprisal $\log(1/p_k)$ exceeds the current entropy are pushed down, concentrating mass on the current argmax. When the argmax is correct, the decision margin widens and the model becomes robustly confident; when it is wrong, the same dynamics manufacture overconfident errors — *confirmation bias* as a mathematical necessity, not an accident. This duality explains TENT's empirical profile: state-of-the-art error on ImageNet-C in the single-domain setting [1], yet brittleness when the stream turns continual or batches shrink, because batch-norm statistics estimated from tiny or correlated batches are noisy and every wrong confident step compounds [10].

### 4.2 CoTTA: Weight-Averaged Teachers and Stochastic Restoration

Continual TTA breaks TENT. When corruptions arrive in sequence — Gaussian noise, then shot noise, then blur — the model never resets, and self-training errors accumulate until the model collapses below the source baseline (on CIFAR-100-C, naive continual TENT is reported *worse* than the unadapted source [3]). CoTTA (Continual Test-Time Domain Adaptation, CVPR 2022) introduced two interlocking stabilizers.

First, a **weight-averaged teacher**: the student $\theta_t^s$ is trained by consistency against pseudo-labels from a teacher $\theta_t^t$ whose predictions are averaged over multiple augmentations of the input; the teacher itself is an exponential moving average of the student, $\theta_n^t = \beta \theta_{n-1}^t + (1-\beta)\theta_n^s$ with $\beta \approx 0.999$ [8]. The EMA teacher is a temporal ensemble: it integrates the student's trajectory, smoothing the noisy, batch-local updates that would otherwise send a single model oscillating across the loss landscape.

Second, **stochastic restoration**: at each step, a small random subset of the student's trainable weights is reset to the source initialization $\theta_0$ (restoration probability $p = 0.01$ per element). This is a soft anchor to the source solution — strong enough to prevent unbounded drift over long streams, weak enough (1% per step) not to erase adaptation. Together, teacher smoothing bounds the *variance* of the update trajectory while restoration bounds its *bias* away from the source manifold.

### 4.3 EATA: Sample Efficiency and Fisher-Regularized Anti-Forgetting

EATA (Efficient Anti-forgetting Test-time Adaptation, ICML 2022) starts from two practical observations [2]: (i) backpropagating on *every* test sample is wasteful — high-entropy samples produce noisy gradients that can disrupt the model; and (ii) adaptation improves out-of-distribution accuracy while degrading in-distribution accuracy, a form of *catastrophic forgetting*.

The response to (i) is **sample-efficient entropy minimization** via an active selection score

$$S(\mathbf{x}) = S^{\text{ent}}(\mathbf{x}) \cdot S^{\text{div}}(\mathbf{x}),$$

where $S^{\text{ent}}$ keeps only samples with entropy below a threshold $E_0$ (reliable, already-near-correct predictions) and $S^{\text{div}}$ down-weights samples redundant with previously selected ones, measured by cosine similarity of their outputs against a running average [2]. Only active samples incur backward passes — fewer gradients, less noise, lower cost.

The response to (ii) is an **anti-forgetting Fisher regularizer**. Parameter importance is estimated *from test samples with generated pseudo-labels* (no source data needed):

$$\omega_i = \frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q} \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta_i^o} \, \mathrm{CE}\big(f(\mathbf{x}_q; \theta^o), \hat{y}_q\big)\right)^2,$$

and the objective adds $\lambda \sum_i \omega_i (\theta_i - \theta_i^o)^2$, penalizing movement in parameters that matter most for the source domain [2]. The pipeline — predict, score, selectively update with regularization — is summarized in Algorithm 1 of the original paper [2].

### 4.4 SAR: Sharpness-Aware Entropy Minimization for the Wild

Real deployments violate TTA's implicit assumptions: test batches may be tiny, correlated, or drawn from mixed shifts. SAR (Towards Stable Test-Time Adaptation in Dynamic Wild World) redesigns entropy minimization for this regime around two ideas [7].

First, **sharpness-aware entropy minimization** replaces $\min_\Theta H$ with

$$\min_\Theta \; \max_{\Vert\varepsilon\Vert_2 \le \rho} \; H(\mathbf{x}; \Theta + \varepsilon),$$

seeking parameters whose *neighborhood*, not just the point estimate, has low entropy. The rationale is the flat-minima generalization bound:

> **Theorem (Sharpness generalization bound, adapted).** With high probability over the test sample, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{test}}(\theta) \le \max_{\Vert\varepsilon\Vert \le \rho} \mathcal{L}(\theta + \varepsilon) + h(\Vert\theta\Vert^2/\rho^2)$ for a monotone $h$.

Minimizing the worst-case neighborhood loss thus controls the true test loss even when the empirical batch is small or biased — precisely the wild scenario. 

Second, a **model recovery scheme**: SAR tracks the moving average of prediction entropy and, when it spikes (a signature of gradient explosion or collapse), resets the trainable parameters toward the source model — a hard version of CoTTA's stochastic restoration, triggered by a diagnostic rather than applied blindly. Combined with reliability filtering (only low-entropy samples contribute), SAR targets the failure triad of wild TTA: small batches, correlated streams, and mixed shift types [7].

### 4.5 DeTTA: Dynamic Teacher Updates for Non-Stationary Streams

CoTTA's teacher uses a *fixed* EMA momentum $\beta$. But a non-stationary stream does not shift at a constant rate: when a new corruption begins, the teacher should absorb the student's fresh knowledge quickly; during a stable phase, it should barely move, preserving accumulated cross-domain knowledge. **DeTTA** — dynamic teacher test-time adaptation — makes the teacher update itself adaptive:

$$\theta_{t+1}^T = (1 - \alpha_t)\,\theta_t^T + \alpha_t\,\theta_{t+1}^S, \qquad \alpha_t = g(\widehat{\text{shift}}_t),$$

where $\widehat{\text{shift}}_t$ estimates the current domain gap and $g$ is monotone increasing. A concrete instantiation appears in recent domain-aware teacher work: the shift is measured as the Frobenius norm of the change in low-dimensional domain semantics, $\Vert\Delta V\Vert_F^2 = \Vert V_t - V_{t+1}\Vert_F^2$, and the teacher weight trades off aligning with the current student against preserving past parameters [8]. Large semantic distance → the teacher leans into the student ($\alpha_t$ up); small distance → it retains history ($\alpha_t$ down). The broader lesson, echoed by analyses of the teacher–student framework under continual TTA, is that *updating the teacher is not free*: without labeled data, an over-eager teacher amplifies error accumulation, and the optimal schedule is itself a function of the stream's dynamics [8]. Dynamic momentum, domain-aware weighting, and shift-triggered restoration form a coherent toolkit for streams whose stationarity cannot be assumed.

### 5.2 Why entropy minimization works — and when it does not

The gradient identity of §4.1 yields three regimes:

1. **Correct and confident** ($p_{\text{top}} \gg p_{\text{rest}}$): the update widens an already-correct margin — pure gain, and the mechanism behind TENT's single-domain success [1].
2. **Correct but uncertain**: mass transfers from competitors to the argmax; the model *commits*. Beneficial if the argmax was right, which batch-norm re-estimation makes likely on mild shifts.
3. **Wrong and confident**: the update cements the error and suppresses the true class — confirmation bias with a closed-form rate. Under continual shift, regime 3 feeds on itself: each wrong confident step makes the next batch's pseudo-labels worse.

Stabilizers map onto regimes: EATA's $S^{\text{ent}}$ filter *excludes* regime-3 samples from backprop [2]; CoTTA's augmentation-averaged teacher *relabels* them before the student commits [8]; SAR's sharpness objective *refuses* updates that are only confident at a point but not in a neighborhood [7]. The condition-level study of CIFAR-10-C confirms the practical moral: aggregate gains coexist with per-condition harm, and no method dominates all 75 corruption–severity cells [10].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Batch-size sensitivity.** Re-estimating $\mu_B, \sigma_B^2$ from batches of size 1–8 is statistically hopeless; entropy gradients computed on them are noise. Group-norm and batch-agnostic alternatives remove the dependence but lose the free adaptation substrate, and entropy-based methods with group norm exhibit distinct output- and feature-level failure modes [7]. Truly batch-size-robust TTA remains unsolved.

**Correlated and imbalanced streams.** The theory assumes i.i.d. batches. Real streams are temporally correlated (consecutive frames) and class-imbalanced; under imbalance, entropy minimization collapses toward majority classes, and dedicated confidence-aware reweighting is needed to restore predictive symmetry across classes. Memory-bank methods (RoTTA) address correlation via timeliness-weighted replay [4], but at the cost of storing test data.

**The forgetting–adaptation frontier.** EATA's Fisher regularizer and CoTTA's restoration both tax adaptation to protect the source solution, but the trade-off has no principled setting: too much anchoring and the model never adapts; too little and it collapses. Estimating importance *without* source data, from pseudo-labeled test samples, is itself vulnerable to the confirmation bias it is meant to contain [2].

**Fundamental limits of unsupervised self-correction.** Entropy minimization is a *prior* — the belief that confident models are right — not a likelihood. Under adversarial or systematically misleading shifts (e.g., spurious correlations that flip), confidence maximization is actively harmful, and no teacher, sharpness penalty, or restoration schedule can recover information the stream never contained. Formal impossibility results for fully unsupervised adaptation under arbitrary shift remain an open and important direction.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Continual test-time adaptation is the story of a beautiful idea meeting a hostile setting. TENT showed that entropy minimization on normalization parameters can repair a shifted model with nothing but its own predictions [1]; the continual stream showed that the same dynamics accumulate errors until the model is worse than untouched. CoTTA answered with a smoothed teacher and stochastic restoration; EATA with selective updates and Fisher-regularized memory; SAR with flat minima and spike-triggered recovery; DeTTA with teachers whose momentum tracks the stream's own volatility [2][7][8].

The unified view developed here — one online loop, four design axes, one gradient identity — suggests the field's next step is not another stabilizer but a *theory of when to adapt at all*: per-condition harm is real and measurable [10], and the optimal TTA policy may often be abstention. Until then, the practitioner rule stands: never adapt blindly on a stream you do not monitor, anchor every update to the source solution, and distrust confidence that is sharp at a point but not in its neighborhood.
