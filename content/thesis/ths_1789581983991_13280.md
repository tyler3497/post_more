# Univalent Foundations: Homotopy Type Theory, the Univalence Axiom, and Cubical Computational Interpretations

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf dependent type theory through the lens of homotopy theory: types are spaces, terms are points, and identity types are path spaces. At its center stands Vladimir Voevodsky's univalence axiom, which identifies the identity type of a universe with the type of homotopy equivalences, thereby formalizing the structuralist practice of identifying isomorphic mathematical structures. This thesis surveys the univalent foundations program from three angles: the syntactic theory of dependent types with higher-dimensional identity structure; the semantic models in simplicial and cubical sets that establish relative consistency; and the cubical computational interpretations that restore canonicity, making univalence compute rather than merely postulate. We develop path induction and the transport principle, construct the equivalence-based formulation of univalence, examine higher inductive types as synthetic homotopy colimits, and present the Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg cubical type theory in which univalence becomes a theorem with explicit computational behavior via the Glue type former and Kan filling operations. The result is a coherent picture of a foundation of mathematics that is simultaneously synthetic, homotopical, and implementable.

##1 Introduction

The discovery, in the early 2010s, that intensional Martin-Löf type theory admits a sound and faithful interpretation in homotopy theory ranks among the most consequential foundational events of recent decades. Where set theory interprets types as sets and equality as the diagonal, homotopy type theory interprets types as *spaces*, elements as *points*, dependent types as *fibrations*, and — most radically — identity types as *spaces of paths* [1]. Under this reading, the long-standing embarrassment of intensional identity types, whose proofs of equality were traditionally regarded as proof-irrelevant noise, becomes a virtue: the higher structure of identity proofs encodes exactly the higher homotopical content of the space a type denotes.

The catalyst for this synthesis was Vladimir Voevodsky's *univalence axiom* (circa 2009–2010). Classical mathematics is replete with identifications of isomorphic structures: a group theorist freely identifies isomorphic groups, an analyst identifies canonically isomorphic vector spaces, and a topologist identifies homotopy equivalent spaces. In set-theoretic foundations, such identifications are informal conveniences without logical force. Univalence internalizes them: for types $A, B$ in a universe $\mathcal{U}$, the canonical map $\mathrm{idtoeqv} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$ from identifications to equivalences is itself an equivalence [2]. Isomorphic structures can therefore be *identified* with the full force of the identity type, and every construction in the theory respects this identification automatically by Leibniz transport.

This thesis has three aims. First, to present the syntactic core of homotopy type theory — path induction, transport, equivalences, and the truncation hierarchy — with the precision the subject demands. Second, to survey the semantic landscape: Voevodsky's simplicial model, which established consistency relative to ZFC with inaccessibles [2], and the cubical models of Bezem, Coquand, and Huber [4]. Third, and most centrally, to explain how *cubical type theory* (CCHM) turned univalence from an axiom that blocks computation into a theorem with explicit computational behavior, restoring canonicity for closed terms of natural number type [3]. Along the way we treat higher inductive types (HITs), the synthetic counterparts of homotopy colimits, which cubical methods also make computational [5].

##2 Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory

Dependent type theory organizes mathematics around judgments $\Gamma \vdash a : A$, read as the term $a$ having type $A$ in context $\Gamma$. The principal type formers are dependent products $\Pi_{(x:A)} B(x)$ and dependent sums $\Sigma_{(x:A)} B(x)$, together with the natural numbers, the empty and unit types, disjoint sums, and a cumulative hierarchy of universes $\mathcal{U}_0 : \mathcal{U}_1 : \cdots$. Every construction respects *definitional equality* (judgmental conversion), while the *identity type* $\mathrm{Id}_A(a, b)$, written $a =_A b$, internalizes propositional equality as a type whose inhabitants are proofs.

The elimination rule for identity, **path induction** (the J-rule), states that to prove $P(x, y, p)$ for all $x, y : A$ and $p : x =_A y$, it suffices to prove $P(x, x, \mathrm{refl}_x)$. This induction principle generates all of the familiar groupoid structure: symmetry, transitivity, congruence of functions, and transport. Historically, *uniqueness of identity proofs* (UIP) was often assumed, collapsing every identity type to a mere proposition. HoTT begins precisely by refusing this assumption.

### 2.2 The homotopy interpretation

The homotopy interpretation assigns to each syntactic construct a homotopical meaning, summarized in the table below [1][7].

| Type theory | Logic | Sets | Homotopy |
|---|---|---|---|
| $A$ | proposition | set | space |
| $a : A$ | proof | element | point |
| $B(x), x : A$ | predicate | family of sets | fibration |
| $a =_A b$ | equality | diagonal relation | path space |
| $\Pi_{(x:A)} B(x)$ | $\forall x.\, B(x)$ | product | space of sections |
| $\Sigma_{(x:A)} B(x)$ | $\exists x.\, B(x)$ | disjoint sum | total space |
| $\mathcal{U}$ | — | — | object classifier |

Under this reading, $p : a =_A b$ is a *path* from $a$ to $b$; $q : p =_{a=_A b} r$ is a *homotopy* between paths; and so on, yielding the full infinite tower of higher paths. Path induction corresponds to the fact that the based path space $\Sigma_{(y:A)} (a =_A y)$ is contractible: every path can be continuously deformed to reflexivity. The *truncation levels* stratify types by homotopical complexity: contractible types (level $-2$), propositions (level $-1$), sets (level $0$), groupoids (level $1$), and so forth.

### 2.3 Equivalences

A function $f : A \to B$ is an *equivalence*, written $A \simeq B$ when $f$ is an equivalence, if it has contractible fibers: $\mathrm{isequiv}(f) :\equiv \Pi_{(y:B)} \mathrm{iscontr}(\mathrm{fib}_f(y))$, where $\mathrm{fib}_f(y) :\equiv \Sigma_{(x:A)} (f(x) =_B y)$. Equivalently — and this formulation matters for computation — $f$ is an equivalence when it admits both a left and a right homotopy inverse (the *bi-invertibility* criterion). Every equivalence induces, by transport along the identity type, identifications between types; univalence will assert the converse.

##3 Methodology

This thesis proceeds by *synthetic* exposition: we work inside the type theory, treating types as spaces and proving theorems about them using path induction and univalence, rather than by analytic manipulation of topological spaces. This follows the methodological stance of the HoTT Book [1] and the introductory literature [7]: the goal is to demonstrate that substantial homotopy theory — the fundamental group of the circle, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, the Blakers–Massey theorem — can be developed synthetically, with machine-checked formalizations.

Methodologically, we distinguish three layers throughout:

1. **Syntax.** The rules of intensional type theory plus the univalence axiom and higher inductive types. This layer is what the working mathematician and the proof assistant manipulate.
2. **Semantics.** Models of the syntax in categories of spaces — simplicial sets [2], cubical sets [4] — which establish consistency and calibrate the theory against classical homotopy theory.
3. **Computation.** Cubical type theories [3][6] that refine the syntax so that every closed term computes, with univalence realized by explicit Glue types and Kan composition operations.

The tension between layers 1 and 3 drives the narrative: the simplicial model validates univalence only at the cost of classical reasoning (it uses choice-like principles to construct fillers), and as an *axiom* univalence blocks the canonicity property — a closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ built using univalence need not reduce to a numeral. Cubical type theory resolves this by making the geometry of paths and fillers part of the syntax itself.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Path induction and the groupoid laws

Path induction is the engine of all higher-dimensional reasoning. From it we derive:

- **Transport.** For $P : A \to \mathcal{U}$ and $p : a =_A b$, a function $\mathrm{transport}^P(p) : P(a) \to P(b)$, with $\mathrm{transport}^P(\mathrm{refl}_a) \equiv \mathrm{id}$.
- **Congruence.** For $f : A \to B$, $\mathrm{ap}_f : (a =_A b) \to (f(a) =_B f(b))$.
- **Composition and inverses.** Paths compose, $p \cdot q$, and invert, $p^{-1}$, satisfying the groupoid laws *up to higher paths*: e.g., $(p \cdot q) \cdot r = p \cdot (q \cdot r)$ holds propositionally, with the witnessing 2-path itself subject to further coherence.

> **Theorem 1 (Contractibility of singletons).** For any $a : A$, the type $\Sigma_{(x:A)} (a =_A x)$ is contractible, with center $(a, \mathrm{refl}_a)$.

> *Proof.* By path induction on the path component: for $(x, p)$ with $p : a =_A x$, induction on $p$ reduces to $(a, \mathrm{refl}_a)$. ∎

This theorem is the semantic content of path induction and the reason the based path fibration is a homotopy equivalence.

### 4.2 The univalence axiom

Univalence concerns the universe $\mathcal{U}$ itself. For $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, there is a canonical map

$$\mathrm{idtoeqv} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$$

defined by path induction: $\mathrm{idtoeqv}(\mathrm{refl}_A) :\equiv \mathrm{id}_A$. The **univalence axiom** asserts that $\mathrm{idtoeqv}$ is an equivalence, so that

$$(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B).$$

Equivalently, the type $\Sigma_{(X:\mathcal{U})} (A \simeq X)$ is contractible: $A$ is the homotopy center of its equivalence class. A universe satisfying this is called *univalent*.

The consequences are sweeping. **Function extensionality** — that pointwise equal functions are equal, $\mathrm{funext} : (\Pi_{(x:A)} f(x) = g(x)) \to (f = g)$ — follows from univalence by a standard argument. So does **propositional extensionality** for mere propositions. More profoundly, univalence makes *equivalence-invariant* reasoning automatic: any type family $P : \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{U}$ satisfies $P(A) \simeq P(B)$ whenever $A \simeq B$, by transporting along the univalent identification. This is Voevodsky's formalization of the mathematical practice of identifying isomorphic structures [2].

### 4.3 Higher inductive types

Ordinary inductive types are generated by point constructors. **Higher inductive types** additionally admit *path constructors*: constructors whose codomain is an identity type. The paradigmatic example is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The recursion principle for $S^1$ states that to define $f : S^1 \to C$ it suffices to give $c : C$ and a loop $l : c =_C c$; the computation rules send $\mathrm{base} \mapsto c$ and $\mathrm{loop} \mapsto l$. From this alone one proves synthetically that the loop space $\Omega(S^1, \mathrm{base})$ is equivalent to the integers — the fundamental group of the circle computed by pure type theory [1].

HITs provide synthetic versions of homotopy colimits: suspensions, pushouts, truncations, and quotients all arise as HITs. The *propositional truncation* $\lVert A \rVert$ and the *set truncation* $\lVert A \rVert_0$ force types down the truncation hierarchy, recovering classical logic and set-level mathematics inside the univalent setting. In cubical type theory, HITs acquire judgmental computation rules even for higher constructors [5].

```haskell
-- Circle as a higher inductive type (Agda-style pseudo-syntax)
data S1 : Type where
  base : S1
  loop : base ≡ base

-- Recursor: a map out of S1 is determined by its values on constructors
recS1 : {C : Type} -> (c : C) -> (l : c ≡ c) -> S1 -> C
recS1 c l base = c
recS1 c l loop = l   -- definitional on the higher constructor (cubical)
```

### 4.4 Cubical computational interpretations

The original formulation of HoTT adds univalence as an *axiom*: a postulated constant $\mathrm{ua} : (A \simeq B) \to (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B)$ with no computation rule. Consequently, closed terms of base type can get stuck on $\mathrm{ua}$, and *canonicity* fails — a closed $n : \mathbb{N}$ need not evaluate to a numeral. Worse, the simplicial model [2] establishing consistency uses classical principles, giving no account of how univalence *computes*.

**Cubical type theory** (Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg, 2016) solves this by internalizing cubes [3]. Contexts carry *dimension variables* $i, j$ ranging over an abstract interval $\mathbb{I}$ with endpoints $0$ and $1$; a term $p$ with a free dimension variable is a *path*, a term with two is a *square*, and so on. The identity type is replaced by the *path type* $\mathrm{Path}_A(a, b)$, whose inhabitants are functions out of $\mathbb{I}$ — paths with genuine computational content, evaluated by substituting endpoints.

Two mechanisms carry the weight:

1. **Kan composition.** Every type supports *composition* of open boxes: given a partial cube defined on all faces except one, composition fills the missing face. This is the constructive analogue of Kan filling in cubical sets and provides the computational content of transport.
2. **Glue types.** The univalence principle is realized by $\mathrm{Glue}$, a type former that glues a partial equivalence-defined family onto a total type along a cofibration (a face formula). For an equivalence $e : A \simeq B$, the path $\mathrm{ua}(e) : \mathrm{Path}_{\mathcal{U}}(A, B)$ is *defined* as a Glue type varying over the interval; transporting along it computes by the equivalence's forward map at one end and its inverse structure at the other. Univalence is thus a *theorem with a program*, not an axiom.

A cartesian variant of the theory, with a structural interval and diagonals, simplifies the presentation and is the basis of textbook accounts [6]. The cubical approach also validates univalence in a *constructive metatheory* [4], answering the objection that the original consistency proof was essentially classical.

The following TLA+-style sketch captures the filling discipline abstractly (an open box with one missing face always admits a filler):

```tla
------------------------------ MODULE KanFilling ------------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANTS Faces          \* the 2n faces of an n-cube
VARIABLES partialCube    \* defined on all but one face

FillerExists ==
  \A missing \in Faces :
    \E filler : filler \in [Faces -> Term]
      /\ \A f \in Faces \ {missing} : filler[f] = partialCube[f]
      /\ filler[missing] \in Term   \* the composite face

THEOREM Kan == FillerExists
=============================================================================
```

##5 Empirical Results / Proofs

We now record the principal theorems, with proof sketches emphasizing computational content.

> **Theorem 2 (Univalence implies function extensionality).** For $f, g : \Pi_{(x:A)} B(x)$, if $h : \Pi_{(x:A)} (f(x) = g(x))$ then $f = g$.

> *Proof.* Each $h(x)$ yields an equivalence on singleton fibers; assembling these gives an equivalence between the total spaces $\Sigma_{(x:A)} (f(x) = -)$ and the graph of $g$. Univalence turns this fiberwise equivalence into an identification of the families, and hence of $f$ and $g$. ∎

> **Theorem 3 (Glue computes univalence).** In cubical type theory, for $e : A \simeq B$ with underlying map $f$, transporting along $\mathrm{ua}(e) : \mathrm{Path}_{\mathcal{U}}(A, B)$ satisfies $\mathrm{transport}^{\mathrm{id}}(\mathrm{ua}(e)) \equiv f$ definitionally.

> *Proof sketch.* The Glue type $\mathrm{Glue}[\varphi \mapsto (B, e)]\, A$ is defined so that its elements are pairs $(a, b)$ with a coherence condition; at endpoint $0$ the cofibration $\varphi$ is false and the type reduces to $A$, at endpoint $1$ it is true and reduces to $B$. Composition along the path therefore applies $f$ on the nose. This is the content of the CCHM construction [3]. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (Canonicity).** Every closed term $n : \mathbb{N}$ in cubical type theory evaluates to a numeral $\mathrm{succ}^k(0)$.

> *Proof sketch.* Huber's canonicity proof proceeds by a sconing (glued) model: a cubical sets model equipped with a computability predicate over closed terms, in which the interpretation of $\mathbb{N}$ consists exactly of numerals. Since every syntactic term has a computable denotation, closed naturals reduce to numerals [3]. ∎

Empirically, these results are not merely paper theorems. The **Cubical Agda** implementation type-checks the univalence axiom with computational behavior: `transport (ua e) x` reduces, and large synthetic homotopy developments — including the Brunerie number computing $\pi_4(S^3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ — have been formalized. The table below summarizes the three generations of models.

| Model | Base category | Univalence status | Constructive | Canonicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simplicial (KLV) [2] | Kan simplicial sets | axiom, validated classically | No | Fails |
| Cubical sets (BCH) [4] | symmetric cubical sets | holds in model | Yes | Not syntactic |
| Cubical TT (CCHM) [3] | de Morgan cubes, syntactic | theorem (Glue) | Yes | Proved |

##6 Limitations

Several limitations temper the picture. First, the *computational cost* of cubical univalence is real: Glue types and Kan composition introduce significant definitional unfolding, and proof assistants must implement sophisticated cubical reduction to keep type-checking tractable. Naive use of univalence can produce terms whose normalization is exponentially worse than their axiomatic counterparts.

Second, **higher inductive types** remain partially schematic. While spheres, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts have full computational semantics in cubical type theory [5], a general schema for arbitrary HITs with judgmental computation rules on all constructors is still an active research area; implementations restrict to well-behaved classes.

Third, the *two-level* tension persists: univalent mathematics naturally wants both strict (judgmental) equality for computation and weak (propositional) equality for homotopy, and reconciling these in a single system without confusing the levels requires care — the subject of two-level type theory.

Fourth, *semantic completeness* questions remain open. While every Grothendieck $\infty$-topos models HoTT, the converse — whether the syntax is complete for the intended homotopical semantics — is subtle, and the relationship between cubical syntax and the full range of higher-categorical models continues to be investigated.

Finally, adoption barriers are sociological as much as technical: the working mathematician's toolkit (choice, excluded middle, impredicative encodings) must be re-examined proposition by proposition in the univalent setting, and the library of formalized univalent mathematics, while growing, remains far smaller than classical corpora.

##7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory, centered on the univalence axiom, offers a foundation of mathematics that is at once *synthetic* — reasoning directly about spaces via their universal properties — and *computational* — every proof a program that can be executed. Voevodsky's simplicial model secured consistency [2]; the cubical revolution of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg secured computation, turning univalence from a postulated axiom into a theorem with explicit operational behavior via Glue types and Kan filling [3][4]. Higher inductive types extend this computational synthetic homotopy theory to colimits [5], and cartesian reformulations are making the ideas accessible to a broader mathematical audience [6].

The univalent foundations program thus stands as a rare achievement: a proposed foundation of mathematics that was conceived with machine implementation as a first-class requirement, validated by models, and refined until its central axiom computes. Whether it ultimately displaces set-theoretic foundations is a question for the coming decades; that it has permanently changed how we understand the relationship between logic, geometry, and computation is already beyond dispute.
