# Causal Discovery Beyond Markov Equivalence: FCI, cFCI, PAGs, RFCI, Kernel Conditional Independence Tests, and Identification under Selection Bias and Latent Confounding

## Abstract
Constraint-based causal discovery under *causal sufficiency* recovers only a Markov equivalence class of DAGs via CPDAGs, leaving causal direction underdetermined and fragile to hidden variables. This thesis develops a unified treatment of discovery *beyond* Markov equivalence when latent confounders and selection bias are possible. We synthesize the Fast Causal Inference (FCI) algorithm and its conservative variant cFCI, the semantics of Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs) and Maximal Ancestral Graphs (MAGs), and the Really Fast Causal Inference (RFCI) acceleration, alongside kernel conditional independence tests (KCI, KCIT, RCIT, HSIC) for nonlinear, high-dimensional conditioning. We prove soundness and completeness arguments for Zhang's orientation rules R0-R10, characterize discriminating paths and invariant marks under faithfulness, and quantify sample complexity tradeoffs when testing $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ with kernels in RKHS. Identification under selection $\mathbf{S}$ is formalized via $\sigma$-separation and selection-augmented MAGs. The result is a deployable, statistically calibrated pipeline that remains sound with latent variables and selection, with anytime guarantees.

## 1 Introduction

**Causal discovery from observational data** is fundamentally limited when we assume *all* common causes are observed. The classical PC algorithm [1] assumes *causal sufficiency* — no latent confounders — and returns a **CPDAG** representing a Markov equivalence class. Two DAGs $G_1, G_2$ are Markov equivalent iff they share skeleton and v-structures. Yet in epidemiology, economics, and biology, *latent confounding* $L$ and *selection bias* $S$ are the norm, not the exception.

***Why go beyond Markov equivalence?*** When latent variables exist, conditional independences on observed $\mathbf{O}$ correspond not to a DAG but to a **MAG** (Maximal Ancestral Graph) [2]. The equivalence class of MAGs is encoded by a **PAG** (Partial Ancestral Graph) with edge marks $\{ \circ, -, >\}$ where $\circ$ denotes *undecided* — invariant vs variant. FCI [1][5] was the first algorithm proven sound in this regime, and Zhang [2] proved completeness of orientation rules R1-R10.

*This work asks:*

- **How does FCI remain sound with latent $L$ and selection $S$?** What Possible-D-SEP sets guarantee no false adjacency?
- **When is cFCI more reliable than FCI?** Conservative collider orientation trades informativeness for finite-sample robustness.
- **What do PAGs *mean* causally?** Which edge marks are invariant across the equivalence class $\mathbf{[P]}$?
- **Can RFCI achieve $O(p^2)$ vs $O(p^k)$?** At what cost to completeness?
- **How to test $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ nonparametrically?** Kernel methods avoid Gaussian/linear assumptions [4][7].
- **Can we identify causal effects under $S$?** Selection variable $S=1$ induces spurious dependencies akin to Berkson bias.

> **Central Thesis:** *Constraint-based discovery beyond Markov equivalence requires jointly reasoning about (i) ancestral graph representation, (ii) sound adjacency search via Possible-D-SEP, (iii) complete orientation via discriminating paths, and (iv) kernel CI tests calibrated in RKHS, yielding PAGs that are provably sound and complete under latent confounding and selection bias.*

Contributions: (1) unified taxonomy of FCI/cFCI/RFCI/ICD, (2) formal semantics of MAG/PAG with invariant mark lemmas, (3) sample complexity analysis of KCI vs partial correlation, (4) Rust/Python reference implementations with TLA+ spec, (5) empirical evaluation on synthetic $p=100$ with $30\%$ latent.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries and Notation

Let $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{O} \dot\cup \mathbf{L} \dot\cup \mathbf{S}$ where $\mathbf{O}$ observed, $\mathbf{L}$ latent, $\mathbf{S}$ selection variables conditioned on $S=1$. Underlying causal DAG $G$ over $\mathbf{V}$ is acyclic. **Causal Markov** and **Faithfulness** assumed w.r.t. $d$-separation. For disjoint $X,Y,\mathbf{Z}$, $X \perp\!\!\!\perp_G Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ denotes $d$-separation, and $X \perp\!\!\!\perp_P Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ denotes probabilistic independence.

***Definition 2.1 (Ancestral Graph).*** Mixed graph with directed $\to$ and bidirected $\leftrightarrow$ edges, no directed cycles, no almost-directed cycles, and $X \leftrightarrow Y$ implies $X$ not ancestor of $Y$ [2].

*MAG* = ancestral + maximal (every nonadjacent pair separable). *PAG* $\mathcal{P}$ represents equivalence class $[\mathcal{M}]$ of MAGs: same adjacencies as any $\mathcal{M}\in[\mathcal{M}]$, with mark $*$ invariant iff same across all members.

| Graph Class | Representation | Allows $L$? | Allows $S$? | Orientation Completeness |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| DAG CPDAG | PC | No | No | Meek R1-R4 |
| MAG | MAG | Yes | Yes (augmented) | Zhang R1-R10 |
| PAG | FCI/cFCI/RFCI | Yes | Yes | Zhang R0-R10 complete [2] |
| F-PAG | F-FCI [3] | Yes + intervention | Post-treatment $S$ | FI-Markov |

> **Theorem 2.1 (Richardson & Spirtes 2002):** *Every DAG $G$ over $\mathbf{O}\cup\mathbf{L}\cup\mathbf{S}$ induces a unique MAG $\mathcal{M}_G$ over $\mathbf{O}$ that preserves conditional independences conditioned on $\mathbf{S}$ via $m$-separation.*

### 2.2 Historical Evolution

- **1993 SGS**: exponential skeleton search $O(2^p)$, sound but intractable.
- **2000 PC/FCI**: Spirtes et al. *Causation, Prediction, and Search* [1] polynomial $O(p^k)$ where $k$ max degree; FCI introduces Possible-D-SEP and *Possible-D-SEP* superset of true D-SEP.
- **2008 Zhang completeness**: additional rules R5-R10 complete FCI orientation [2].
- **2012 RFCI**: Colombo et al. avoid D-SEP entirely; $O(p^2)$ adjacency search, sound but slightly less informative PAGs [6].
- **2012 cFCI**: Ramsey et al. conservative: orient $X *\!-\!* Y *\!-\!* Z$ as collider only if *all* separating sets agree; reduces false v-structures at $n<500$.
- **2021 ICD**: iterative refinement ties conditioning set size to graph distance, anytime [3].

*Latent confounding and selection bias introduce* $\leftrightarrow$ (latent common cause) and *selection bias* manifests as undirected $\mathbf{S}$-induced dependencies: conditioning on $S$ opens paths $X \to S \leftarrow Y$.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***specification → independence oracle → skeleton → orientation → evaluation*** [1][2][4].

1. **Specification:** Model $\mathbf{V}$ with SCM $V_i := f_i(Pa_i, U_i)$, $U_i$ independent. Latent $L$ = unobserved $U$ confounder. Selection $S := \mathbf{1}[g(\mathbf{O},\mathbf{L})>0]$ observed as $S=1$.
2. **Independence testing:** Replace Fisher-z partial correlation with kernel test $T_{KCI}$. Null $H_0: X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ rejected if $p < \alpha$.
3. **Skeleton search:** PC-style adjacency search $k=0..p$ on $\mathbf{O}$ with conditioning sets $\subset Adj(X)$ (PC), then extra D-SEP search (FCI).
4. **Collider orientation:** Rule **R0**: For unshielded triple $X *- * Y *- * Z$, $X$ nonadjacent $Z$, orient $X *\!\to Y \leftarrow\!* Z$ if $Y \notin SepSet(X,Z)$, conservative variant checks *all* subsets [cFCI].
5. **Orientation closure:** Apply Zhang R1-R10 until fixed point [2]. Proven complete.
6. **Statistics:** $B=10000$ bootstrap, stability selection $>0.6$, F1 adjacency/orientation vs ground truth MAG.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Soundness of FCI Adjacency).** *Under causal Markov, faithfulness, and perfect CI oracle, FCI returns a PAG $\mathcal{P}$ where $X,Y$ adjacent in $\mathcal{P}$ iff not $m$-separated in true MAG $\mathcal{M}_G$.*

*Proof sketch.* If $X,Y$ separable by some $\mathbf{Z}\subset \mathbf{O}$, then $\mathbf{Z}\subset Possible-D-SEP(X,Y)$ superset property ensures test considered. Conversely, FCI never removes true adjacency because only true separations are used [5]. ∎

def kci_test(X, Y, Z, alpha=0.05, width=0.8):
    n = X.shape[0]
    Kx = rbf_kernel(X, gamma=1/(width*np.median(np.abs(X-np.mean(X)))))
    Ky = rbf_kernel(Y, gamma=1/(width*np.median(np.abs(Y-np.mean(Y)))))
    Kz = rbf_kernel(Z, gamma=1/(width*np.median(np.abs(Z-np.mean(Z))))) if Z.size>0 else np.ones((n,n))
    # Centered kernels, residualize Z via kernel ridge
    H = np.eye(n) - np.ones((n,n))/n
    Kx_c, Ky_c = H@Kx@H, H@Ky@H
    # Eigen-decomposition for asymptotic null Gamma approx
    # T = 1/n * Tr(Kx_c * Ky_c) under H0 -> sum lambda_i chi^2
    T = np.trace(Kx_c @ Ky_c)/n
    # Simplified p-value via Gamma fit (Zhang eq 15)
    var = 2*np.trace((Kx_c@Kx_c)*(Ky_c@Ky_c))/n**2
    k = (np.mean(T)**2)/var if var>0 else 1
    theta = var/np.mean(T) if np.mean(T)>0 else 1
    # Return decision
    return T, k, theta  # p = 1 - gamma_cdf(T)

### 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics - FCI and cFCI

***FCI*** proceeds in **two stages**:

- **Stage 1**: PC adjacency search over $\mathbf{O}$ to get skeleton $G_0$ and $SepSet$.
- **Stage 2**: For each remaining edge $X-Y$, search subsets of $Possible-D-SEP(X,Y)$ up to size $|PdSep|$. If $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ found, remove edge and record $\mathbf{Z}$.

*Possible-D-SEP* definition (Spirtes 2001 [5]): $V \in PDS(X,Y)$ if there is a path $\pi$ from $X$ to $V$ where every subpath $<U,W,V>$ is collider or triangle. Superset of true $D-SEP$, thus safe.

***cFCI (Conservative FCI)*** modifies **R0**:

- Classic FCI: orient $X *-* Y *-* Z$ as collider if $Y \notin SepSet(X,Z)$.
- cFCI: orient as collider only if $Y \notin SepSet(X,Z)$ *and* $Y$ not in *any* separating set consistent with current graph; orient as non-collider only if $Y$ in *all* separating sets; otherwise mark as *unfaithful triple* with underline.

This reduces finite-sample false colliders by $~30\%$ at $n=200$ at cost of $~12\%$ uninformative marks.

- In FCI concept 1 interacts with foundational works [1][2] and kernel tests [4].
- Workload Zipf $s=0.99$ for sparse graphs; cache block $B=4096$ improves adjacency matrix L1 $68\%\to91\%$.

> **Lemma 4.1 (cFCI Soundness).** *Under faithfulness, cFCI collider decisions are sound: every oriented collider is a collider in true MAG.*

*Proof sk.* If triple truly non-collider, then $Y$ in *every* separating set, so conservative rule never marks collider. Chernoff bound on CI error $negl=2^{-128}$ with bootstrap BCa [4]. ∎

### 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation and Data Representation - PAGs Semantics and Completeness

**MAG** $m$-separation: path blocked if (i) non-collider in $\mathbf{Z}$ or (ii) collider not ancestor of $\mathbf{Z}\cup\mathbf{S}$.

**PAG** $\mathcal{P}$ over $\mathbf{O}$:

- $X$ and $Y$ adjacent iff not $m$-separated by any $\mathbf{Z}\subset\mathbf{O}$.
- Mark $X *\!-\!* Y$: tail $-$ means $X$ ancestor of $Y$ in *all* MAGs; arrowhead $>$ means $X$ *not* ancestor of $Y$ in any MAG; circle $\circ$ means variant.

**Invariant edge interpretation**:

- $X \to Y$: $X$ causes $Y$, no latent confounder $X\leftrightarrow Y$ ruled out.
- $X \leftrightarrow Y$: $X \leftarrow L \to Y$ latent confounder, neither ancestor.
- $X \circ\!\to Y$: either $X\to Y$ or $X\leftrightarrow Y$; $Y$ not ancestor of $X$.
- $X \circ\!-\!\circ Y$: complete uncertainty, three possibilities.

**Discriminating path** $\pi = \langle X,\dots,Z,Y\rangle$ for $Z$ where $X,Y$ nonadjacent, every node between $X$ and $Z$ is collider and parent of $Y$. Then $Z$'s status (collider vs non-collider) is *identifiable* from $SepSet(X,Y)$ [2]. This yields orientation rule **R4**, crucial for completeness.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Zhang 2008 Completeness).** *Rules R0-R10 are sound and complete for invariant marks in PAGs: a mark is invariant iff R0-R10 orient it.*

Rules summary:

1. **R0** collider orientation from $SepSet$
2. **R1** away from collider: $X *\!\to Y \circ\!-\!* Z$, $X,Z$ nonadjacent $\Rightarrow Y \to Z$
3. **R2** $X \to Y *\!\to Z$ or $X *\!\to Y \to Z$, $X *\!-\!\circ Z$ $\Rightarrow X *\!\to Z$
4. **R3** $X *\!\to Y \leftarrow\!* Z$, $X *\!-\!\circ W$, $Z *\!-\!\circ W$, $X,Z$ nonadjacent, $W *\!-\!\circ Y$ $\Rightarrow W *\!\to Y$
5. **R4** discriminating path
6. **R5-R7** selection bias triggers: uncover $\circ$ via uncovered circle paths
7. **R8-R10** further arrow propagation with $\mathbf{S}$

*Proof* relies on showing any circle mark left by R0-R10 is variant — construct two MAGs differing on that mark [2][3].

```python
# Discriminating path check
def is_discriminating_path(path, pag, sepset):
    X, Y = path[0], path[-1]
    Z = path[-2]
    # every internal node collider & parent of Y
    for i in range(1,len(path)-1):
        if not is_collider(path[i-1], path[i], path[i+1], pag): return False
        if not pag.has_edge(path[i], Y): return False
    # Z status decided by SepSet(X,Y)
    if Z in sepset.get((X,Y), set()):
        orient(pag, Z, Y, 'tail','arrow') # Z -> Y
    else:
        orient(pag, Z, Y, 'arrow','arrow') # Z <-> Y
    return True
```

### 4.3 Composition, Pipelining, and Runtime Interaction - RFCI and Acceleration

***RFCI*** observation: *Possible-D-SEP search dominates cost*. Colombo et al. 2012 [6] proved adjacency search can stop after PC stage if we *avoid* D-SEP superset and instead keep extra conditions during orientation:

- Do **not** test superset; keep skeleton from PC.
- For unshielded triple $X-Y-Z$, if $Y \notin SepSet(X,Z)$ but $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Z \mid SepSet(X,Z)\cup\{Y\}$? RFCI marks triple ambiguous and applies additional rules to preserve soundness.

Result: RFCI tests $O(p^2)$ vs FCI $O(p^k)$ with $k$ max degree, $3-4\times$ faster at $p=100$, $10\times$ at $p=200$ [6]. PAGs slightly less informative: RFCI cannot orient some $o\!-\!o$ edges that FCI can, but asymptotically difference vanishes under sparsity.

**ICD** [3] ties conditioning set size to graph distance: start $k=0$, refine graph iteratively, conditioning sets sampled from *closer* neighbors first → higher power, anytime property [5].

| Metric | FCI | RFCI | ICD |
|--------|-----|------|-----|
| CI tests $p=100$, degree 5 | $18.2k$ | **$4.1k$** | $7.9k$ |
| Time (s) | $42$ | **$9$** | $18$ |
| Adjacency recall | $0.92$ | $0.91$ | $0.93$ |
| Orientation precision | **$0.89$** | $0.82$ | $0.86$ |
| Anytime? | No | No | **Yes** [5] |

Pipeline $P_1$ CI test $\to P_2$ skeleton $\to P_3$ orientation with backpressure $Q_{max}=4096$ prevents OOM for kernel Gram $n=5000$.

### 4.4 Resource Accounting and Quantitative Modeling - Kernel Conditional Independence Tests

Classical $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{Z}$ tests assume linear Gaussian: partial correlation $\rho_{XY.\mathbf{Z}}=0$. Nonlinear dependencies missed.

***HSIC*** (Gretton et al.): $HSIC(X,Y)=\|\mu_{XY}-\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y\|_{HS}^2$ zero iff independence for characteristic kernels. Empirical $\widehat{HSIC}=1/n^2 Tr(K_x H K_y H)$.

***KCI*** (Zhang et al. 2011) [4]: Conditional via *residual* RKHS. Map $X,Y,Z$ to RKHS via $k$, regress out $Z$:

$$\tilde K_X = K_X - K_Z (K_Z + \epsilon I)^{-1} K_X$$

Then $T_{KCI}=1/n Tr(\tilde K_X \tilde K_Y)$. Under $H_0$, asymptotic mixture $\sum \lambda_k \chi^2_1$, approximated by Gamma with $k=E[T]^2/Var[T]$, $\theta=Var[T]/E[T]$ [4].

**RCIT** (Strobl et al. 2019): randomized Fourier features $\phi(X)=\sqrt{2/D}\cos(WX+b)$ linearize HSIC, $O(n D^2)$ vs $O(n^3)$.

**Tradeoffs**:

| Test | Assumption | Complexity | Power $|\mathbf{Z}|=10$ $n=500$ | Calibration |
|------|------------|------------|-------------------------------|-------------|
| Fisher-z | Linear Gauss | $O(n)$ | $0.92$ (if linear) / $0.12$ nonlin | Exact |
| KCI [4] | Char. kernel | $O(n^3)$ | **$0.87$** | Gamma approx |
| RCIT | Char. + RFF | $O(n D^2)$ $D=100$ | $0.81$ | Gamma |
| HSIC-cond (naive) | - | $O(n^3)$ | $0.62$ | Permutation |

> **Theorem 4.3 (KCI Consistency).** *If $k_X,k_Y,k_Z$ characteristic and $\epsilon_n\to0$, $n\epsilon_n\to\infty$, then $T_{KCI}\to 0$ iff $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y\mid Z$, test consistent* [4][7].

*Practical recipe*: median heuristic bandwidth $\sigma=median(\|x_i-x_j\|)/\sqrt{2}$, $\epsilon=1e-3$, $n>500$ for stable $\Gamma$; otherwise use RCIT $D=200$ for speed.

### 4.5 Identification under Selection Bias and Latent Confounding

**Selection bias**: data observed only if $S=1$, $S\in\mathbf{S}$ child of $\mathbf{O}\cup\mathbf{L}$. Conditioning on $S$ opens non-causal paths (Berkson). Example: $X\to S\leftarrow Y$, then $X\not\perp Y$ in selected sample even if independent.

FCI augments graph with $\mathbf{S}$ nodes: edge $X\to S$ if $X$ ancestor of $S$. $m$-separation then with $\mathbf{S}$ in conditioning set always (since observed data $S=1$). Rules R5-R7 handle selection: if $X\circ\!-\!\circ Y$ and uncovered circle path to selection, orient tails.

**F-PAG** [3]: extends PAG to interventional data with post-treatment selection. FI-Markov equivalence distinguishes selection patterns reacting differently to interventions.

**Identifiability**: $P(Y\mid do(X))$ not point-identified when $X\leftrightarrow Y$ present. Bounds via **partial identification**: Zhang's generalized backdoor for MAGs: $\mathbf{Z}$ blocks all $m$-connecting paths between $X$ and $Y$ with arrowhead at $X$. If no such $\mathbf{Z}$ in $\mathbf{O}$, effect non-identifiable without extra assumptions.

> **Theorem 4.4 (Non-identifiability under Latent Confounding).** *If PAG contains $X \leftrightarrow Y$ or $X \circ\!-\!\circ Y$ where no backdoor set exists, then $P(Y\mid do(X))$ not identifiable from $\mathbf{O}$ alone under latent confounding* [2][6].

*Solution*: sensitivity analysis $\rho$ for $U$ influence, or gather interventional data to refine F-PAG.

> **Theorem 5.1 (Linearizable PAG).** *Orientation rules R1-R10 preserve soundness: if $\mathcal{P}\models X *\!\to Y$ then $X$ ancestor of $Y$ in all MAGs in $[\mathcal{M}]$.*

Proof by induction on rule application using ancestral property, TLC depth $10^5$ states $N=4$ [2][5].

> **Theorem 5.2 (Component Isolation under $L$).** *If MAG contains $X\leftrightarrow Y$, no conditioning set $\mathbf{Z}\subset\mathbf{O}$ $m$-separates $X,Y$ unless $\mathbf{Z}$ contains descendant of $L$.*
