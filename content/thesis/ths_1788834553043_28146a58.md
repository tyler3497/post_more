# Partition Trees, Directional Intra Prediction, In-Loop Restoration, and Grain Synthesis in AV1: An Analysis of Superblock Coding, CDEF, and SVT-AV1 Parallel Encoding Architectures

![Superblock partition tree](ths_1788834553043_28146a58-0.webp)

## Abstract

The AV1 video codec achieves approximately 30% bitrate savings over its predecessor VP9 [1] through a constellation of coding tools whose interactions are rarely analyzed jointly. This thesis presents a rigorous dissection of AV1's core spatial coding machinery: the ten-way superblock partition tree spanning 128x128 down to 4x4 coding blocks, the fifty-six-direction intra prediction framework including chroma-from-luma (CFL) and Paeth predictors, and the in-loop restoration pipeline comprising deblocking, the constrained directional enhancement filter (CDEF), and dual-mode Wiener/self-guided loop restoration [2][3]. We further derive the film grain synthesis model as a normative post-process outside the prediction loop [4], and characterize the SVT-AV1 encoder's multi-stage parallel architecture with lookahead analysis, demonstrating how its thread-pool design exposes rate-distortion tradeoffs across speed presets [5][6]. Rate-distortion Lagrangian analysis of the encoder mode-decision loop is provided alongside empirical complexity measurements, establishing that encoder parameter tuning (e.g., fast-decode levels) can reduce decode cycles by over 20% at modest compression cost [7].

## 1 Introduction

Modern video compression is governed by a single tension: *fidelity* against *bitrate*, mediated by computational complexity. The AV1 codec, finalized by the Alliance for Open Media in 2018, represents the culmination of decades of hybrid block-based coding research [1][2]. Unlike its predecessors, AV1 introduces a radical expansion of the fundamental coding unit hierarchy, permitting superblocks of 128x128 samples subdivided recursively down to 4x4 blocks through a ten-way partition tree.

This expansion is not cosmetic. Each split decision is a **rate-distortion optimization (RDO)** problem whose search space grows combinatorially. The encoder must jointly decide:

- *Partition structure*: how to subdivide each superblock across 10 partition types.
- *Prediction modes*: which of 56 directional intra angles (plus non-directional modes) best fits each block.
- *Transform coding*: which of the separable DCT/DST/ADST combinations to apply to residuals.
- *In-loop filtering*: per-frame and per-block CDEF and loop-restoration parameters.
- *Grain synthesis*: stochastic texture parameters transmitted outside the prediction loop.

The analytical challenge is that these decisions are **coupled** A partition split changes the reference samples available to intra prediction; intra prediction determines residual statistics; residuals determine transform and quantization behavior; and quantization artifacts determine the gains achievable by in-loop filters. This thesis treats the codec as an integrated system rather than a bag of tools.

Our contributions are:

1. A formal treatment of the ten-way partition tree and its combinatorial properties.
2. A mathematical derivation of the directional intra prediction framework, CFL, and Paeth predictors.
3. An analysis of the three-stage in-loop filtering pipeline (deblocking, CDEF, loop restoration).
4. The film grain synthesis model as normatively specified.
5. The SVT-AV1 parallel encoding architecture and its rate-distortion tradeoffs.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Hybrid Block-Based Coding

AV1 follows the classical hybrid paradigm — **prediction + transform + quantization + entropy coding** per block [2] — with residuals entropy-coded by a multi-symbol arithmetic coder derived from Daala [1].

> Theorem: For a stationary Gaussian source with covariance C, the optimal linear predictor minimizing mean-square error is given by the Wiener-Hopf solution w = C_xx^{-1} c_xy, where C_xx is the autocorrelation matrix of observed samples and c_xy is the cross-correlation vector.

This classical result motivates both intra prediction (exploiting spatial correlation) and loop restoration (exploiting correlation between reconstruction and original) [3].

### 2.2 Coding Block Sizes

AV1 supports block widths and heights from 4 to 128, with rectangular variants at 1:2, 2:1, 1:4, and 4:1 aspect ratios [2][8]:

| Block type | Dimensions | MI grid units | Min/Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| BLOCK_128X128 | 128 x 128 | 32 x 32 | Superblock root |
| BLOCK_64X64 | 64 x 64 | 16 x 16 | — |
| BLOCK_32X32 | 32 x 32 | 8 x 8 | — |
| BLOCK_16X16 | 16 x 16 | 4 x 4 | — |
| BLOCK_8X8 | 8 x 8 | 2 x 2 | — |
| BLOCK_4X4 | 4 x 4 | 1 x 1 | Minimum leaf |
| BLOCK_4X8 / 8X4 | rectangular | 1 x 2 / 2 x 1 | Rectangular |
| BLOCK_4X16 / 16X4 | rectangular | 1 x 4 / 4 x 1 | 1:4 ratio |

In 4:2:0, chroma block dimensions halve relative to luma, and sub-8x8 chroma blocks may merge into joint prediction regions [1].

### 2.3 Related Work

Comprehensive overviews of AV1 coding tools appear in [2] and [3]. The AV1 specification itself is the normative reference for all processes described here [1]. SVT-AV1's architecture and complexity modeling are treated in [5][6], and decoder-side complexity reduction through encoder tuning is empirically studied in [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds through three complementary lenses:

1. **Normative specification analysis**: We trace the decoding processes in [1] to establish exact behavior of partitioning (section 5), intra prediction (section 7.11), CDEF (section 7.15), loop restoration (section 7.16), and film grain (section 7.18).
2. **Information-theoretic modeling**: We model partition and mode decisions as **Lagrangian rate-distortion optimization**, J = D + lambda * R, where D is distortion, R is rate, and lambda is the Lagrange multiplier derived from the quantization parameter.
3. **Architectural analysis**: We examine the SVT-AV1 encoder pipeline as documented in [5][6], characterizing stage-level parallelism and its effect on the achievable R-D operating point.

The following Python model formalizes the partition tree's combinatorial structure:

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import List, Optional

# AV1 partition types (spec section 5.x), [1]
PARTITION_TYPES = [
    "NONE", "HORZ", "VERT", "SPLIT",
    "HORZ_A", "HORZ_B", "VERT_A", "VERT_B",
    "HORZ_4", "VERT_4",
]

@dataclass
class Block:
    w: int
    h: int

def children(part: str, b: Block) -> List[Block]:
    w, h = b.w, b.h
    if part == "NONE":   return [b]
    if part == "HORZ":   return [Block(w, h//2), Block(w, h//2)]
    if part == "VERT":   return [Block(w//2, h), Block(w//2, h)]
    if part == "SPLIT":  return [Block(w//2, h//2)] * 4
    if part == "HORZ_A": return [Block(w, h//4), Block(w, h//4), Block(w, h//2)]
    if part == "HORZ_B": return [Block(w, h//2), Block(w, h//4), Block(w, h//4)]
    if part == "VERT_A": return [Block(w//4, h), Block(w//4, h), Block(w//2, h)]
    if part == "VERT_B": return [Block(w//2, h), Block(w//4, h), Block(w//4, h)]
    if part == "HORZ_4": return [Block(w, h//4)] * 4
    if part == "VERT_4": return [Block(w//4, h)] * 4
    raise ValueError(part)

def leaves(b: Block, parts: List[str]) -> List[Block]:
    # count leaf blocks for a partition sequence; min leaf 4x4 [1]
    out = [b]
    for p in parts:
        out = [c for x in out for c in children(p, x)]
    return out

# A 128x128 superblock fully split to 4x4 yields 1024 leaves
print(len(leaves(Block(128,128), ["SPLIT"]*5)))  # 1024
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Ten-Way Superblock Partition Tree

The fundamental coding structure in AV1 is the **superblock**, either 64x64 or 128x128 luma samples [1][2]. Each superblock is recursively partitioned using one of ten partition types at each node:

1. **PARTITION_NONE**: no split; the block is coded as a single unit.
2. **PARTITION_HORZ**: split horizontally into two equal halves.
3. **PARTITION_VERT**: split vertically into two equal halves.
4. **PARTITION_SPLIT**: quad-tree split into four equal quadrants.
5. **PARTITION_HORZ_A**: three-way horizontal split, 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/2 (top-heavy).
6. **PARTITION_HORZ_B**: three-way horizontal split, 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 (bottom-heavy).
7. **PARTITION_VERT_A**: three-way vertical split, 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/2.
8. **PARTITION_VERT_B**: three-way vertical split, 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4.
9. **PARTITION_HORZ_4**: four-way horizontal split into quarters.
10. **PARTITION_VERT_4**: four-way vertical split into quarters.

The asymmetric *A/B* three-way splits are critical: they allow the encoder to isolate a thin feature (an edge, a text line, a subtitle band) without forcing a full quad-tree split of the entire block [2]. This reduces signaling overhead dramatically for mixed-content frames.

The minimum block size is 4x4, so a 128x128 superblock supports up to 5 levels of quad-tree splitting. Partition signaling uses context-adaptive CDFs conditioned on neighboring block sizes, exploiting the strong spatial correlation of partition decisions [1].

The encoder's partition search is a **top-down recursive RDO process**: at each node it compares the leaf cost against the summed child costs, and SVT-AV1 accelerates this with *partition pruning heuristics* from lookahead statistics [6].

### 4.2 Directional Intra Prediction: 56 Angles, CFL, and Paeth

![Intra prediction directional modes](ths_1788834553043_28146a58-1.webp)

AV1's intra prediction extends the 8 nominal directions of VP9 into **56 directional modes** via per-mode angle deltas [2]. Each nominal direction (e.g., V_PRED at 90 degrees) admits up to 7 delta angles, yielding fine-grained prediction:

- Nominal directions: 8 (vertical, horizontal, 6 diagonal modes)
- Angle delta: 3 bits, mapped to offsets of -3..+3 steps of ~3 degrees
- Total: 8 x 7 = 56 directional modes

The prediction process projects each sample position back along the angle vector to the reference row/column, applying a two-tap bilinear interpolation filter [1]. For angles beyond 90 degrees (the "extended" zone), the reference samples come from the *opposite* edge — e.g., a steep diagonal may reference the top row for samples near the left column — which the spec handles through a unified projection with the `upsample` logic [1].

Three non-directional luma modes complement the directional set:

- **DC_PRED**: the block is filled with the average of above and left reference samples (with a 128 shift for smoothness at high bit depth).
- **SMOOTH_PRED / SMOOTH_V_PRED / SMOOTH_H_PRED**: bilinear interpolation between corner references, modeling gradual gradients.
- **PAETH_PRED**: the Paeth predictor selects, per sample, whichever of the top, left, or top-left reference is closest to the Paeth base `p = top + left - top_left`. This is *edge-preserving*: unlike averaging predictors, Paeth tracks the dominant edge direction sample-by-sample [2].

For chroma, AV1 introduces **CFL (chroma-from-luma)**: the chroma block is predicted as a linear function of the co-located reconstructed luma,

> Theorem: Under the assumption that chroma and luma share edge structure (justified by the statistics of natural images), the minimum-MSE linear predictor of chroma C from luma L is C_hat = alpha * (L - mean(L)) + beta, with alpha = cov(L,C)/var(L) and beta = mean(C).

CFL transmits only the scaling factors *alpha_u*, *alpha_v* (signed, 3 bits each) plus a joint sign, and derives beta from DC prediction [2]. This exploits the high luma-chroma correlation and is particularly effective on 4:2:0 content where chroma blocks are coarser.

Mode decision is RDO-driven: candidates pruned by SATD pre-search, selecting the minimizer of D + lambda*R including mode signaling cost [1][6].

### 4.3 In-Loop Filtering: Deblocking, CDEF, and Loop Restoration

![In-loop filter pipeline](ths_1788834553043_28146a58-2.webp)

AV1 applies three sequential in-loop filters before the frame enters the reference buffer [1][3]. This ordering is normative and interacts with super-resolution: CDEF operates at coded resolution, while loop restoration may operate after horizontal upscaling [3].

**Stage 1: Deblocking.** Operates on 4x4/8x8+ boundaries with strength from loop filter level, edge type, and transform size [1]; up to 14 taps on strong edges, skipped where coefficients indicate a genuine image edge.

**Stage 2: CDEF (Constrained Directional Enhancement Filter).** CDEF is AV1's signature restoration tool, derived from Daala [2][3]. Its operation per 8x8 block:

1. **Direction detection**: For each of 8 candidate directions, compute the variance of pixel values along lines perpendicular to the direction; select the direction of *minimum* variance (strongest edge direction).
2. **Non-linear filtering**: Along the detected direction, apply a constrained low-pass filter:
   ```
   y = x + sum over taps w_k * constrain(x_k - x, strength, damping)
   ```
   where `constrain(diff, strength, damping) = sign(diff) * min(|diff|, max(0, strength - |diff| >> damping))`. The non-linearity prevents blurring across genuine edges while smoothing ringing artifacts along them [3].

CDEF parameters (strength, damping) are signaled per 64x64 *filter block* and searched via a dedicated RDO loop — one of the costliest encoder operations [6][7].

**Stage 3: Loop restoration.** Applied per 64x64 *restoration unit*, the encoder selects among three modes per plane [1][3]:

- **RESTORE_NONE**: bypass.
- **RESTORE_WIENER**: separable 7-tap Wiener filter, with coefficients solved per unit by least-squares against the source (in the encoder) and transmitted in the bitstream.
- **RESTORE_SGRPROJ**: self-guided projection — a guided-filter variant where the filter weights are derived from the local statistics of the degraded image itself, requiring only two transmitted parameters (xqd0, xqd1 indices into a codebook).

The Wiener coefficients are computed as follows. Let **A** be the matrix of degraded neighborhood samples and **b** the source samples; the optimal filter **h** solves the normal equations **(A^T A) h = A^T b**. Because this is done per 64x64 unit per plane, the encoder cost is significant, and SVT-AV1 prunes restoration search aggressively at higher speed presets [6].

### 4.4 Film Grain Synthesis: Normative Stochastic Post-Processing

Film grain is part of creative intent in cinema content, yet its stochastic nature makes it nearly incompressible with transform coding [3][4]. AV1's solution is radical: **remove the grain before encoding, estimate its parameters, transmit them, and re-synthesize it at the decoder** — outside the prediction loop [1][4].

The film grain parameters per frame include [1]:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `apply_grain` | enable flag |
| `grain_seed` | PRNG seed for the frame |
| `num_y_points`, `point_y_value[]`, `point_y_scaling[]` | luma scaling LUT control points |
| `num_cb_points`, `num_cr_points`, ... | chroma scaling LUTs |
| `ar_coeff_lag`, `ar_coeffs_y[][]` | autoregressive model order and coefficients |
| `grain_scale_shift` | output scaling shift |
| `cb_mult`, `cb_luma_mult`, `cr_mult`, `cr_luma_mult` | chroma cross-correlation multipliers |
| `overlap_flag`, `clip_to_restricted_range` | blending behavior |

Grain is generated by a **24-coefficient autoregressive model** over a 13x13 window, scaled by intensity LUTs, and blended into 32x32 blocks [1][4]. Because synthesis is normative — PRNG and all rounding fixed in the spec — every conformant decoder produces *bit-identical* grain [4].

The encoder-side estimation (in `aomenc` and SVT-AV1) analyzes the source's high-frequency residual after denoising, fits the AR model via Yule-Walker equations, and derives the scaling LUTs from local variance statistics [4].

### 4.5 SVT-AV1: Parallel Encoding Architecture and Rate-Distortion Tradeoffs

![SVT-AV1 parallel pipeline](ths_1788834553043_28146a58-3.webp)

SVT-AV1 (Scalable Video Technology for AV1), originally developed at Intel/Netflix, restructures the encoder as a **multi-stage parallel pipeline** rather than a monolithic frame loop [5][6]. The key stages:

1. **Picture analysis / lookahead**: A downscaled copy of upcoming frames is analyzed for spatial/temporal complexity (variance, motion activity, edge density). This produces *complexity features* consumed by all downstream stages.
2. **Motion estimation (ME)**: Hierarchical ME (HME) searches a motion vector pyramid; the lookahead stage provides search-center hints.
3. **Mode decision (MD)**: Per-superblock RDO over partition trees, intra/inter modes, and transforms — the dominant compute stage.
4. **Entropy coding (EC)**: Multi-symbol arithmetic coding of the decided syntax; serialized per tile-group but parallel across tiles.

Parallelism is exposed at frame level (multiple frames in flight), tile/superblock-row level (wavefront mode decision), and stage level (ME of frame N+1 overlaps MD of N and EC of N-1), communicating via lock-free FIFO packet queues [6]. This lets SVT-AV1 scale to dozens of cores where `libaom`'s serial design saturates [5].

**Rate-distortion tradeoffs** are exposed through 13 speed presets (0 = slowest/best, 13 = fastest) [5][6]. Each preset gates a bundle of tools:

| Preset range | Disabled/pruned tools | Approx. BD-rate penalty |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Full RDO, full partition search, full CDEF/restoration search | baseline |
| 3-6 | Pruned partition candidates, reduced intra angle search | 1-3% |
| 7-9 | Simplified ME, capped transform search, restoration bypass on easy units | 3-8% |
| 10-13 | No CDEF search, no restoration, minimal lookahead | 8-20% |

Empirical modeling shows encoding time scales with content complexity (spatial/temporal information) and preset approximately as a power law, enabling energy-aware preset selection [5]. Speed-adaptive preset switching can enforce a wall-clock budget with ~9% precision by dynamically promoting/demoting presets mid-encode [6].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Partition Tree Combinatorics

> Theorem: The number of distinct partitionings of a 128x128 superblock down to 4x4 leaves is finite but exceeds 10^9; exact enumeration is intractable, which is why encoders use top-down greedy RDO with lookahead pruning.

*Proof sketch.* Even quad-tree-only splits admit a 5-level tree whose pruned-tree count grows super-exponentially; adding the six asymmetric splits multiplies branching further. A dynamic-programming count over all legal block sizes yields > 10^9 configurations, so encoders evaluate the pruning identity J(node) = min(J_leaf, sum J(children)) top-down instead of enumerating [1][6].

```haskell
-- Partition cost model: Lagrangian RDO over the tree, cf. [1]
data Part = None | Horz | Vert | Split
          | HorzA | HorzB | VertA | VertB | Horz4 | Vert4
          deriving (Show, Eq)

type Cost = Double
rdCost :: Cost -> Cost -> Double -> Cost
rdCost dist rate lambda = dist + lambda * rate
-- Encoder prunes: keep split only if
--   sum (rdCost children) < rdCost leaf - epsilon
```

### 5.2 Intra Prediction Gain

For a first-order Markov image model with edge-direction correlation rho, prediction MSE scales as sigma^2*(1-rho)*delta_theta^2; refining the angular step from ~22.5 degrees (VP9) to ~3 degrees (AV1) thus yields a quadratic reduction in angular error, measured at 2-4% BD-rate on natural content [2].

```rust
// Paeth predictor per sample, AV1 spec section 7.11.2.4 [1]
fn paeth_predictor(a: u16, b: u16, c: u16) -> u16 {
    // a = left, b = top, c = top-left
    let p = a as i32 + b as i32 - c as i32;
    let (pa, pb, pc) = (
        (p - a as i32).abs(),
        (p - b as i32).abs(),
        (p - c as i32).abs(),
    );
    if pa <= pb && pa <= pc { a }
    else if pb <= pc { b } else { c }
}
```

### 5.3 Filter Pipeline Gains

The in-loop filter chain's contributions have been measured in the AOM common test conditions [2][3]:

- Deblocking: 1-2% BD-rate.
- CDEF: 2-5% BD-rate (larger on high-detail content with ringing).
- Loop restoration (Wiener + SGRPROJ): 3-7% BD-rate (larger at low bitrates where quantization noise dominates).
- Film grain synthesis: *negative* BD-rate in PSNR terms (it adds noise), but large *perceptual* gains; VMAF-based evaluations show 5-15 point improvements on grainy film content at equal bitrate [4].

### 5.4 SVT-AV1 Complexity Scaling

Encoding time models fit to SVT-AV1 measurements show [5]:

```
t_enc ~ C_content * f(preset) * (W*H*n_frames) / n_threads^alpha
```

with alpha < 1 due to pipeline fill/drain and serial EC sections (Amdahl-limited). Speed-adaptive preset switching tracks a time budget with mean precision 8.9% [6]. Decoder-side, disabling CDEF via encoder tuning cuts decode cycles by ~10%, and SVT-AV1's `fast-decode=2` level cuts ~24% of decode cycles with small compression loss [7].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Partition search optimality**: Top-down greedy RDO is not globally optimal; lookahead heuristics can misfire on scene cuts and fades.
2. **Angular resolution vs. signaling**: 56 directions cost signaling bits; on smooth content the delta angles are pure overhead.
3. **CDEF cost**: 8x8 direction detection and per-filter-block search is among the most expensive encoder operations; disabled at high presets, surrendering 2-5% BD-rate [6][7].
4. **Restoration granularity**: 64x64 units cannot adapt to fine detail; per-unit Wiener coefficients are a compromise and SGRPROJ's two-parameter model is coarse.
5. **Grain estimation**: Encoder-side grain estimation is non-normative and heuristic; poor estimates yield visible mismatch [4].
6. **Parallelism limits**: SVT-AV1 scaling is Amdahl-limited by entropy coding and reference dependencies; zero-lookahead low-delay modes sacrifice pipeline parallelism.
7. **Content dependence**: All BD-rate figures are content-dependent; screen content and animation deviate from natural-video averages.

---

## 7 Conclusion

AV1's compression efficiency emerges not from any single tool but from the **joint design** of its spatial coding pipeline: a ten-way partition tree that adapts block geometry to content, a 56-direction intra framework with CFL and Paeth predictors that exploit fine-grained spatial correlation, a three-stage in-loop restoration chain (deblocking, CDEF, Wiener/SGRPROJ) that attacks distinct artifact classes, and a normative film grain synthesizer that moves stochastic texture outside the rate-distortion loop. SVT-AV1 demonstrates that this toolset can be mapped onto a multi-stage parallel architecture with explicit, preset-controlled rate-distortion-complexity tradeoffs [5][6][7].

Architecturally, AV1 treats the encoder as a *search engine over a combinatorial space* — partitions, modes, filters, grain parameters — with gains from making search richer and cheaper. Future work: learned pruning policies, content-adaptive preset control [6], and perceptual metrics inside the Lagrangian.
