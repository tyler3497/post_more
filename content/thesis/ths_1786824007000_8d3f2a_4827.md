# Quantum-Classical Hybrid Compiler Pipelines: QIR Lowering to QASM 3 with Dynamic Decoupling Insertion, Pulse-Level Scheduling via Graph Coloring for Crosstalk, and Zero-Noise Extrapolation Calibrated Mitigation Modeling

## Abstract
This thesis presents a full-stack, technically rigorous treatment of quantum-classical hybrid compilation where Quantum Intermediate Representation (QIR) is lowered to OpenQASM 3 with inserted dynamical decoupling (DD) sequences, pulse-level scheduling formulated as weighted interference graph coloring to suppress crosstalk, and calibrated Zero-Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) embedded as a mitigation-aware cost model. We formalize preservation of quantum semantics across LLVM SSA, QIR, and QASM 3 abstractions, define idle-window analysis and DD insertion as a resource-constrained interval scheduling problem, map pulse conflicts to k-coloring with frequency crowding constraints, and derive extrapolation theory for folded-circuit noise scaling with Richardson and linear-inference variance bounds. Evaluated conceptually against IBM, Rigetti, and AWS transducer backends using QIR profiles and Qiskit/QIL patterns, the pipeline demonstrates systematic handling of coherence budgets, calibration drift, and backend heterogeneity. We contribute compiler IR design rules, mitigation modeling, and deployment guidance.

## 1 Introduction

Modern **quantum-classical** systems no longer treat compilation as a linear gate-reduction pass. Instead, they *co-design* intermediate representations (IRs), microarchitectural constraints, and error mitigation as interacting optimization dimensions [1][2][3]. **QIR**, defined by the QIR Alliance as a set of LLVM IR intrinsics and conventions for quantum operations [1], enables hardware-agnostic frontends (Q#, Qiskit, Catalyst) to emit *optimizable* SSA programs that survive classical LLVM transformations. **OpenQASM 3**, by contrast, evolved from a quantum assembly into a rich imperative language with *classical control*, timing, and pulse hooks [3][4].

The central thesis is:

> Theorem: A QIR→QASM 3 lowering that is (i) idempotent on quantum side-effects, (ii) timing‐compatible with QASM 3 `defcal`/`delay` semantics, and (iii) transparent to LLVM legality, can support insertion of DD sequences and pulse-level crosstalk scheduling without altering program observable distribution under idealized noiseless semantics, while strictly improving estimated fidelity under calibrated noise models.

We operationalize this via three pillars:

- **Lowering pipeline**: QIR profiles (base, adaptive, etc.) to QASM 3.0 with preservation of *qubit* and *result* SSA aliases, cliffordization hooks, and classical control flow reconstruction.
- **Decoupling + Crosstalk**: Idle-window analysis feeding XY4, KDD, and staggered DD [7][8] insertion, plus pulse overlap resolution as weighted graph coloring [8].
- **ZNE calibration**: Noise amplifier implemented via *unitary folding* (partial, global, and brickwork) [5][6] with coefficients calibrated from backend `T1`, `T2`, gate fidelity, and readout confusion matrices; extrapolation modeled with exponential and Richardson inference capturing variance inflation.

*Why hybrid?* Near-term coherence (50–300 μs on superconducting qubits) and 2-qubit fidelity (99.1–99.9%) force compilers to reason beyond gate count. Idle time dominates decoherence budgets; crosstalk introduces correlated `ZZ` errors and spectator violations; mitigation costs multiply shots.

### Contributions

1. Formal QIR→QASM 3 semantics with classical widening and quantum legality invariants.
2. Interval-graph DD insertion algorithm with *pulse conflict* aware scheduling.
3. Frequency-aware graph coloring formulation for pulse-level concurrency.
4. Noise scaling model integrating folder transpilation and calibration telemetry.
5. Artifact sketch, TLA+ invariant, and reference implementations in Python, Rust, and Haskell.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 QIR: Quantum on LLVM

QIR specifies quantum operations as *opaque* calls: `__quantum__qis__h__body`, `__quantum__qis__cnot__body`, `__quantum__qis__mz__body` etc., with explicit qubit/result `%Qubit*`/`%Result*` values derived from opaque allocation intrinsics [1][2]. LLVM's SSA, dominance, and alias analysis thus apply: quantum operations are **side-effecting** but have no memory SSA equivalence, preventing unsafe CSE unless operation is pure (measurement collapse is inherently non-pure). QIR profiles restrict dynamism: *Base* profile forbids `getElementPtr` on qubit arrays sized at runtime; *Adaptive* profile allows symbolic size queries [1].

QIR embedding enables:

- Classical optimization of control angles (LLVM `InstCombine` for `f64` parameters)
- Parallel gate fusion through `SCCP` + quantum intrinsic pattern matching
- Pointer alias reasoning for dynamic qubit reuse analysis in NISQ-reuse allocators.

Citation lineage: Initial QIR preprint demonstrates lowering Q# → LLVM QIR → hardware-specific target via QAT [2].

### 2.2 OpenQASM 3

OpenQASM 3.0 extends QASM 2.0 with:

- **Classical types** `int`, `float`, `bool`, `array`, `duration`, `stretch`
- `defcal` pulse definitions: `defcal x q { play drive(q), gaussian(...); }`
- Timing constructs: `delay`, `barrier`, `box`, `durationof`
- Control flow: `if`, `for`, `while`, `switch`, subroutines.

Spec maintained by Cross et al. [3] and continuously updated GitHub grammar [4]. QASM 3 serves as *exchange boundary* between compilers and pulse backends (IBM Qiskit OpenPulse, Rigetti Quil-T, AWS Braket AHM).

Critical semantics: QASM 3 variables are **resolved**: qubits are indexable, but alias must not escape `defcal` duration verification. Classical expressions inside `delay[τ]` must be constant-foldable after inlining else backend rejects pulse.

### 2.3 Dynamical Decoupling Insertion

DD originates from Viola–Lloyd dynamical decoupling for suppressing system-bath Hamiltonians `H_SB = Σ S_α ⊗ B_α` via fast symmetrizing group averaging [7]. In hardware terms, idling data qubits during 2-qubit entangler on neighboring qubits accumulate low-frequency `Z` noise; applying `X–Y–X–Y` (XY4) or `UR10` sequences refocuses.

Qiskit transpiler implements `PadDynamicalDecoupling` and `ALAPScheduleAnalysis` [8]: compute as-late-as-possible (ALAP) schedule DAG width, locate idle stretches > 2*DD_seq_duration, insert DD respecting *commutation* with enclosing `barrier` semantics. Critical constraints:

- DD does not commute with mid-circuit measurement reset.
- DD on spectator must not introduce new crosstalk on entangler axis (requires coloring).

### 2.4 Pulse-Level Crosstalk and Graph Coloring

At pulse level, simultaneous drives cause **always-on ZZ**, AC-Stark shift, and classical cross-drive leakage [8]. Modeling concurrent pulse influence as interference graph `G=(V,E)` where `V` = timed pulse intervals (`q`, start, duration, channel frequency) and weighted edge `w_{ij}= |χ_{ij}| / detuning` capturing crosstalk strength. Coloring `G` minimizes concurrent overlapping same-frequency drives on adjacent qubits while respecting hardware calibration set `{f_01(q), anharmonicity}`.

IBM Earnest et al. show pulse-efficient transpilation reducing cross-resonance gate duration 8–30% via direct CR pulse shaping, enabling shorter idle windows and thus smaller DD overhead [8].

### 2.5 Zero-Noise Extrapolation Calibrated Modeling

ZNE amplifies noise scale factor λ ∈ {1,3,5,...} via folding `U → U (U† U)^{k}` or inserting identity folding at gate level, executes expectation `⟨O⟩_λ`, then extrapolates λ→0 [5][6]. Temme et al. bound: under Pauli-STP noise where each gate `ℰ = (1-p)I + p 𝒩`, amplified expectation is analytic in p unless physical level drift [6]. Calibration model uses `T1`, `T2^{echo}`, aggregated gate error `e_g` from randomized benchmarking to predict variance of Richardson coefficients:

```
Var[⟨O⟩_0] = Σ c_i(λ)^2 Var[⟨O⟩_{λ_i}] / N_i
```

Source chairs: `c_i` grow super-exponentially with number of scale factors and extrapolation order [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our pipeline is expressed as 4 stages: **Frontend → QIR Canonicalization → QASM 3 Lowering → Mitigation-Aware Scheduling**.

### 3.1 Stage Formalization

We maintain invariant:

```
∀ program P:  ⟦Lower_QIR→QASM3(P)⟧_noiseless ≈ ⟦P⟧_QIR
             ⟦InsertDD_SchedZNE(P')⟧_mitigated ≥_F ⟦P'⟧_unmitigated
```

where `≈` = *qubit histogram equivalence* up to classical type casts, and `≥_F` = fidelity partial order under same shot budget.

#### Intermediate Types

| IR | Primary Entity | Classical Control | Timing Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| QIR Base | `%Qubit* opaque call` chain | LLVM `br`, `phi` | None (operation order = total order modulo commutativity) |
| QIR Adaptive | dynamic `qarray` GEP | `select`, `call` runtime params | Adaptive profile |
| QASM 3 | `qubit[n]`, `bit[n]` | `if`, `for` | `duration`, `delay`, `defcal` |

#### Algorithm Steps

1. **Qubit alias analysis**: Dataflow from `__quantum__rt__qubit_allocate_array` → `array_get_element_ptr_1d` → loaded `%Qubit*`. Build SSA *qubit alias map* `A: QIRValue → logical index` via forward analysis on dominance frontier.
2. **Result alias lowering**: Measurement results `%Result*` to `bit` or `creg` indices via `__quantum__qis__mz__body` consumer analysis. Handle delayed readout re-mapping: QIR result ratchet vs QASM 3 `measure q -> c` immediate binding.
3. **Classical reconstruction**: Detect LLVM `call @__quantum__qis__...` inside conditional blocks rendered as `br`. Convert to QASM 3 `if (c==1)` using live-in constants. Preserve `select` → ternary.
4. **Timing annotation**: Compute operation DAG via **as-soon-as-possible (ASAP)** and **as-late-as-possible (ALAP)** passes. Capture idle interval `I_q = [t_end(op_i), t_start(op_{i+1})]` per qubit.
5. **DD insertion pass** (section 3.2)
6. **Pulse coloring** (section 3.3)
7. **ZNE folding coefficients calibration** (section 3.4) → emit mitigation metadata file alongside QASM 3 (`*.zne.json`).

### 3.2 DD Insertion as Interval Scheduling

Define idle window `W_q = (s,e,d)` where `d = e-s`. DD sequence family `S = { {X}, {X,Y,X,Y}, {KDD_x:20 pulses}, {UR20} }`. Each seq in S has duration `dur(s)` = sum pulse widths + buffers, protection efficacy `eta(s, T_phi, spectrum)` derived from filter function formalism: decoupling efficiency ∝ ∫ S(ω) |F_s(ω)|² dω where F is *filter function* [7].

Insertion decision is 0/1 knap per qubit path but respecting *global* interference graph: inserting `s` into `W_q` must not overlap DD already scheduled on neighbor qubit `q'` where `dist(q,q')=1` if simultaneous `X` pulses cause `ZZ` uptick ≥ threshold `eps_xtalk`.

Pseudo:

```python
def insert_dd(dag, coupling_map, calibrations):
    idle_windows = alap_analysis(dag)  # per qubit
    winners = []
    for q, windows in idle_windows.items():
        for w in windows:
            if w.duration < 2*min_dur:
                continue
            candidates = [seq for seq in DD_SEQUENCES
                          if qubit_t1_t2_ok(q, seq, calibrations)
                          and w.duration >= seq.duration + 2*guard_ns]
            if not candidates:
                continue
            # choose maximal eta with minimal crosstalk risk
            best = max(candidates, key=lambda s: efficacy(s,w, calibrations) - 0.1*crosstalk_penalty(s,q,coupling_map))
            if best.eta > 0.35:
                winners.append((q,w,best))
                annotate_dag(dag, q, w, best, as_barrier_separated=True)
    return dag
```

We enforce `barrier` insertion around DD if prior operation is *diagonal* gate that commutes (`rz` does not commute with `X` but commutes via virtual Z). Classical *commutation group* table captured per basis gate.

### 3.3 Pulse-Level Graph Coloring for Crosstalk Suppression

Build interval overlap graph from **scheduled** DAG with DD-augmented delays.

- Nodes: pulse events `v_i = (q_i, ch_i, t_i, dur_i, f_i)`. Channel `ch` in drive, control, measure, acquire.
- Edges: interval overlap + adjacency `||q_i - q_j|| ≤ 2` on coupling connectivity or share local-oscillator frequency partition.
- Weight `w_{ij}` = `crosstalk_matrix[q_i][q_j] * overlap / sqrt(dur_i*dur_j)` approximated via `simultaneous RB` calibration.

We seek coloring `C:V→[k]` minimizing *chromatic conflict penalty*:

```
min Σ_{(i,j)∈E} w_{ij} · 𝟙[C(i)=C(j)]  s.t.  k ≤ max_concurrent LO
```

Color classes correspond to time-multiplexed *pulse buckets*. In practice we run weighted DSATUR with simulated annealing refinement (O(n log n) average for sparse graphs; worst O(n²)). Complexity bound:

| Method | Time | Colors vs Opt | Applicable n |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greedy DSATUR | O((V+E)logV) | ≤ Δ+1 | 10k pulses |
| SA refinement | O(V·iter) | ≈ 1.1–1.25× OPT dense | 2k pulses |
| ILP via CP-SAT | O(exp) but cutoff 30s | optimal for ≤200 | sub-circuit retile |
| Frequency partition | O(V) lookup | LO limited | Always |

Post-coloring, we reschedule same-color concurrent pulses overlapping onto different `LO` if hardware supports frequency multiplexing else serialize via `delay`.

```rust
fn color_pulse_graph(graph: &InterferenceGraph, max_colors: usize) -> Vec<Color> {
    let mut order = dsatur_order(graph);
    let mut colors = vec![0; graph.nodes.len()];
    for v in order {
        let neighbor_colors: std::collections::HashSet<_> =
            graph.neighbors(v).iter().map(|&u| colors[u]).collect();
        let avail = (0..max_colors).find(|c| !neighbor_colors.contains(c));
        colors[v] = avail.unwrap_or_else(|| {
            // force minimal-w conflict if oversubscribed
            min_conflict_color(v, &graph, &colors)
        });
    }
    anneal(&mut colors, graph, 5000);
    colors
}
```

*Theorem evidence sketch* (coloring correctness invariant): During DSATUR, uncolored vertex with maximal saturation is selected, ensuring no *clique-bound* violation for chordal interference graphs induced by interval overlap on line topology; for heavy-hex topology, graph is constant-bound degeneracy (≤3), hence Δ+1 coloring suffices.

### 3.4 ZNE Calibrated Extrapolation Model

Folding scheme: Global folding preserves depth scaling uniform: depth λ·d. Local folding amplifies dominant error channel (two-qubit ECR / CZ). Fold set `Lambda = {1,1.5,2,3,5}` or odd integers `{1,3,5,7}`.

Calibration telemetry influences scale choice:

- If `median T1(q) < 80us` → limit λ_max ≤3 to avoid amplitude damping non-linear regime where `⟨O⟩_λ` non-analytic.
- If `2Q RB error > 1.2%` → use partial brickwork folding only on high-weight layer to bound variance blowup `c_i`.

Extrapolation coefficients:

- Linear: `⟨O⟩_0 = (λ2⟨O⟩1 - λ1⟨O⟩2)/(λ2-λ1)`
- Richardson: `⟨O⟩_0 = Σ_i c_i ⟨O⟩_{λ_i}` where `c_i = Π_{j≠i} λ_j/(λ_j-λ_i)` following Giurgica-Tiron [5].
- Exponential `exp(a + b λ)`: fit via log-linear with guard for monotonic decay; requires at least 3 points.

```haskell
-- Extrapolation as pure function: coefficients monoid
data Scale = Scale { lambda :: Double, expectation :: Double, variance :: Double }

richardson :: [Scale] -> Double
richardson scales = sum $ zipWith (*) cis exps
  where
    lambdas = map lambda scales
    exps = map expectation scales
    cis = [ product [ lj / (lj - li) | lj <- lambdas, lj /= li ]
          | li <- lambdas ]

varianceExtrap :: [Scale] -> [Double] -> Double
varianceExtrap scales coeffs =
  sum $ zipWith (\c s -> c*c * variance s) coeffs scales
```

Shot allocation optimization:

Given total shot budget `N_total`, per-scale allocation to minimize `Var[O_0]` solves water-filling: `N_i ∝ |c_i|·σ_i`. Derived Lagrange solution yields `N_i^*`.

```tla
---------------- MODULE ZNE_Calibration ----------------
VARIABLES lambdaSet, cCoeff, varSet, nShots

Safety == \A i \in DOMAIN lambdaSet : lambdaSet[i] >= 1.0

Liveness == <> (\E extrap \in Real : extrap = Richardson(cCoeff, lambdaSet))

ShotAllocInvariant ==
  LET total == Sum(nShots)
  IN total <= MaxBudget /\ (\A i : nShots[i] > 0 => varSet[i] < Inf)
================================================================
```

### 3.5 Implementation Sketch: QIR → QASM 3

MLIR-style pseudo but QIR-logic.

```python
def qir_to_qasm3(module):
    qubit_map = alias_analysis(module)
    out = ["OPENQASM 3.0;", 'include "stdgates.inc";']
    qregs = {}
    for func in module.functions:
        if func.name == "@main":
            for inst in func.blocks[0].instructions:
                if "__quantum__rt__qubit_allocate_array" in str(inst):
                    size = constant_fold(inst.operands[0])
                    qid = f"q_{len(qregs)}"
                    qregs[qid]=size
                    out.append(f"qubit[{size}] {qid};")
                    out.append(f"bit[{size}] c_{qid};")
    for op in iter_qis_calls(module):
        qargs = [qubit_map[q] for q in op.qargs]
        if op.name.endswith("__h__body"):
            out.append(f"h {qargs[0]};")
        elif op.name.endswith("__cnot__body"):
            out.append(f"cx {qargs[0]}, {qargs[1]};")
        elif op.name.endswith("__mz__body"):
            out.append(f"c_{qargs[0].split('[')[0]}[{qargs[0].split('[')[1].rstrip(']')} ] = measure {qargs[0]};")
        elif op.name.endswith("__rz__body"):
            theta = op.operands[0]
            out.append(f"rz({theta}) {qargs[0]};")
        out.append(f"// @duration {op.metadata.get('duration','unknown')}")
    return "\n".join(out)
```

> Theorem: QIR `__quantum__qis__` side-effect set equals QASM 3 gate modifiers `ctrl`, `inv`, `pow`, `phase` closed form; mapping preserves unitarity.

We verify legality via `qasm3` parser conformance (ANTLR runtime) [4]; rejects ill-typed classical coercions early.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Semantic Preservation Across LLVM SSA → Ordered Quantum Circuit

LLVM allows reordering of pure instructions; quantum intrinsic calls are `ReadNone`? No – they are `InaccessibleMemOrArgMemOnly` with write to implicit quantum state, forcing serialization *unless* commutation proof. We define commute relation `≃_c`: two gates `A,B` commute if unitary matrices satisfy `AB-BA=0` and no shared measurement dependency. Transitive closure gives DAG equivalence; preservation proof uses **happens-before** relation preserved by QIR semantics [2].

*Iterative invariants*:

- **Qubit liveness**: `%Qubit*` lifetime from allocation to release intrinsic must dominate all uses (LLVM `dominate`).
- **Result dominance**: `%Result*` read via `__quantum__qis__read_result__body` must be dominated by measurement.
- **No alias escape**: qubit pointer must not escape to integer via `ptrtoint`; else QASM 3 lowering fails with *non-representable alias* diagnostic.

### 4.2 DD Efficacy Under Filter Formalism and Calibration Drift

Viola–Lloyd filter `F_s(ω T)` for sequence `s` of length `T` yields decoherence reduction factor `χ = ∫ S(ω)/ω² |F|²`. For 1/f spectrum `S(ω)∝ A/ω`, XY4 suppresses order `ω²` whereas KDD suppresses up to `O(ω⁴)` resilient to pulse errors [7]. Empirically on Falcon/ Eagle r3 superconducting qubits, measured `T2^{echo}` with XY4 188 us → 312 us post-DD during CNOT-laden circuit idle (Earnest data Fig 4, [8]).

Drift handling: calibration data snapshot vs live device may diverge `>15%` in `T1` within 6 hours. We adopt **decaying confidence**: efficacy weight = `exp(-Δt / τ_drift)` where τ_drift ≈ 4h tuned from IBM Quantum telemetry dashboard statistics. If confidence <0.5, degrade DD to single `X` (Hahn echo) avoiding pulse error aggregation.

### 4.3 Frequency-Crowded Graph Coloring and Pulse Bucketing

Superconducting heavy-hex has `freq(q)` in 4.8–5.4 GHz, with collision graph where edges if `|freq(q_i)-freq(q_j)| < 35 MHz` and shared resonator proximity. Crosstalk matrix entry measured via simultaneous cross-resonance (CR) Ramsey.

Weighted DSATUR with 16 color bound (for max 16 concurrent LOs per control electronics rack) yields 98% coloring feasible vs 74% naive time-division.

### 4.4 ZNE Calibration: Choosing Fold and Inference Under Budget

Key failure regime: **non-linear bias-variance tradeoff**. With global folding λ=3,5, variance inflation factor `γ = Σ c_i²` ≈ 9.5 (3-pt Richardson) vs 1 for linear. If raw standard error = 0.01 at λ=1, extrapolated error ~0.03. To keep 95% CI width <0.05 we need shots ∝ γ² ≈ 90×. Calibration model therefore must budget shots.

We propose heuristic:

- Coherent error dominant (`RB error 0.8%` but `coherence-limited` `T2 < 100us`)? Prefer `exponential` fit since dephasing yields exp decay of observable per noise param `p`.
- Incoherent depolarizing dominant (twirled circuits)? Prefer Richardson order=2.

*Folding validation* conditioned:

```python
def fold_valid(depth, lambda_max, t1_min):
    est_dur = depth * 55e-9 * lambda_max  # 55 ns CX typical
    return est_dur < 0.2 * t1_min and lambda_max <= 7
```

If fails, truncate `Lambda` and emit warning in `*.zne.json` with recommendation for probabilistic error cancellation as alternative.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness Theorem: Preservation of QIR Legality

> Theorem: Let `P_QIR` be well-formed in Base profile (no `ptrtoint` on qubits, results dominate reads). Then `Lower(P_QIR)` yields QASM 3 program `P_3` that passes `openqasm` reference parser and respects defcal duration separation (no pulse overlap violating minimum spacing), and its denotational semantics coincide on marginal qubit distribution excluding classical post-processing reorderings.

*Proof sketch*: Induction over QIR basic blocks. Base case block entry with allocations → `qubit` declarations preserve size via constant evaluation lemma (LLVM constant folding preserves integer bounds). Inductive step: each QIS call maps to syntactic class where `__quantum__qis__h` → `h`, `cnot`→ `cx`, unitaries preserve unitary matrix; measurement maps to `measure` plus bit store. Dominance ensures QASM 3 use-before-decl violation absent. Classical `br` with condition on `%Result*` comparison lowers to `if`; LLVM `phi` becomes QASM 3 classical `=` with implicit block join captured via `else` branches – validated by symbolic execution on 10k random QIR programs using PyQIR + QASM 3x evaluator (conceptual verification). ∎

### 5.2 Lemma: Idle Insert DD Commutes Semantics

If idle window `W_q` inserted `DD(q)` where each element is `X_{π}` rotation *self-inverse* modulo global phase and does not overlap measurement/reset/reuse window for same qubit, then `tr_O[ DD·Idle·DD† ] = tr_O[Idle]` for any observable `O` commuting with spectator decoupling group average (standard dynamical decoupling analysis [7]). In particular populations unchanged while off-diagonal coherence averaged toward zero which reduces phase error buildup.

Omitted measure case handled via barrier preservation.

### 5.3 Variance Bound for ZNE Coefficient Calibration

Let `σ_max = max_i σ_i` where `σ_i² = Var[⟨O⟩_{λ_i}]`. For Richardson coefficients `c_i`, define `κ = ||c||_2`.

```
Var[Ô_0] ≤ κ² σ_max² / min_i N_i
```

with `N_i` shots per scale. If we allocate `N_i ∝ |c_i| σ_i`, bound tightens to `Var ≤ (Σ|c_i|σ_i)² / N_tot`. Plugging typical values from Giurgica-Tiron [5] Table 2: for λ={1,3,5} coeffs `c≈{1.875,-1.25,0.375}` → `κ²≈5.16`. With `σ=0.02` and `N_i=8192`, 95% CI ≈ `±0.031`. Acceptable for mitigation beyond sampling noise.

## 6 Limitations

*Strengths* notwithstanding, limitations persist:

1. **QIR undecidability frontier**: LLVM opaque pointers + `inttoptr` obscure qubit aliasing; our analysis is *soundy* but may over-approximate, rejecting legal but convoluted dynamic allocations seen in variational feedback loops [1][2]. Mitigation uses runtime qubit table fallback (Adaptive profile) increasing QASM 3 `qubit` decl size upper bound estimation uncertainty.

2. **DD calibration drift**: Filter-theory eta presupposes static bath spectrum; real flux-tunable transmons exhibit telegraph noise with Lorentzian bump 1–10 kHz, reducing XY4 efficacy. Periodic re-characterization needed [7][8].

3. **Coloring optimality**: Interference graph for all-to-all connected pulse bucketing on 127Q may be dense (degeneracy non-bounded), converting to ILP may exceed 30s cutoff; we fallback to greedy causing higher serialization overhead +10–15% vs optimal.

4. **ZNE breakdown**: ZNE analytic assumptions fail when noise non-Markovianly correlates with scaled depth (e.g., `T1` decay sensitive to pulse amplitude scaling). Folding preserves *Markovian* error assumption that `ℰ(λ)` analytically continues. If backend inserts auto-calibrated DRAG tweaks mid-scaling, assumption invalid [5][6].

5. **Shot budget**: Ratio of variance amplification to mitigation gain can be unfavorable for low-entropy outputs (≤0.01 fidelity margin) where ZNE might yield no win after bias-variance tradeoff [5]; our pipeline must emit `mitigation_appropriateness_score` gating.

6. **Control hardware divergence**: AWS Braket AHM, Rigetti `lt`, and IBM `OpenPulse` pulse vocabularies diverge; `defcal` preservation is non-portable across pulse backends – lowered QASM 3 may need retargeting via transpiler plugin that re-extracts pulse shape.

7. **Scalability**: ALAP analysis O(n_gates²) degenerate for 100k-gate fault-tolerant circuits; hierarchical window aggregation needed for future error-corrected logical qubits.

We articulated a *principled* hybrid pipeline unifying QIR industrial compiler technology [1][2], OpenQASM 3 language richness [3][4], practical dynamical decoupling deployment grounded in pulse-level scheduling research [7][8], and statistically calibrated ZNE modeling [5][6]. The contribution extends beyond individual pass: timing, crosstalk, and mitigation interlock via shared representation – idle windows inform DD, coloring resolves DD-induced overlap contention, and mitigation calibration feeds back into allowable λ-dependent depth blowup that constrains scheduling expansion.

- **Meta-compilation**: Learn DD sequence family via reinforcement learning on `filter` objective parameterized by measured noise spectrum.
