# The Categorical Semantics of Computation: Monads, Adjunctions, and Cartesian Closed Categories

## Abstract

This thesis develops the categorical foundations of effectful computation, following Moggi's programme that replaced the naive identification of programs with functions by a semantics of *computations* mediated by monads [1][2]. We present monads as algebraic theories of effects, situate them within the classical correspondence between adjunctions and monads via the Kleisli and Eilenberg–Moore constructions, and show how Moggi's computational λ-calculus refines the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence between typed λ-calculi and cartesian closed categories to account for effects. The analysis extends to two modern frontiers: the Lawvere-theoretic presentation of algebraic effects, which reconstructs computational monads from signatures of operations and equations, and traced monoidal categories, which axiomatize feedback and iteration. We prove the soundness of the computational λ-calculus with respect to Kleisli semantics, the initiality and terminality of the Kleisli and Eilenberg–Moore resolutions of a monad, and the coherence of the graphical calculus for traced monoidal categories, and we evaluate the framework's scope, the monad combination problem, and its intrinsic limits.

## 1. Introduction

The identification of *programs* with *functions* is the oldest simplification in programming-language theory. In the pure λ-calculus a closed term of type `A → B` denotes a total function, and β-conversion models computation faithfully. But real programs raise exceptions, mutate state, perform I/O, choose nondeterministically, and capture continuations. Under the naive identification each *effect* must be smuggled into the notion of function — by threading a store, lifting to partial functions, or CPS-transforming the language — and each smuggling destroys the equational theory that made the λ-calculus attractive.

Eugenio Moggi's 1989–1991 programme [1][2] proposed a cleaner discipline: distinguish *values*, inert data, from *computations*, which may perform effects while delivering a value. A type `A` denotes values; `T A`, for a suitable *monad* `T`, denotes computations delivering `A`-values. A program of type `A → B` denotes a Kleisli morphism `A → T B`. Sequencing is the monad's multiplication, pure embedding its unit, and the language's equational laws are exactly the monad laws. Different notions of computation — partiality, exceptions, state, nondeterminism, continuations — arise as different monads over the same base category, a modularity Wadler later turned into a programming idiom [3].

> **Thesis claim:** The computational λ-calculus, interpreted in the Kleisli category of a strong monad over a cartesian closed category, gives a sound and complete categorical semantics of effectful computation, with the classical typed λ-calculus as the identity-monad special case.

This thesis reconstructs that programme — which rests on the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence between typed λ-calculi and cartesian closed categories [4] and on the classical correspondence between adjunctions and monads [5] — and extends it along two axes the later literature identified as essential. First, real computation involves *feedback* — loops, recursion, cyclic dataflow — axiomatized by the *traced monoidal categories* of Joyal, Street, and Verity [6]. Second, monads are a *derived* presentation of something more primitive: the *Lawvere theory* of operations and equations generating an effect, in Plotkin and Power's algebraic-effects programme [7]. Section 2 recalls the background; Section 3 our methodology; Section 4 the technical core; Section 5 the main theorems; Section 6 the limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

**Categories and cartesian closure.** A *category* has objects, morphisms `f : A → B`, associative composition, and identities. A *functor* preserves this structure; a *natural transformation* `α : F ⇒ G` is a family `α_A : F A → G A` making every naturality square commute. Finite products `A × B` and a terminal object `1` model tuples and the unit type. A **cartesian closed category** (CCC) additionally has exponentials `B^A` with evaluation `ev : B^A × A → B` satisfying currying: morphisms `C × A → B` correspond bijectively to morphisms `C → B^A`. **Set** is the prototypical CCC; so are **Cpo**, presheaf categories, and every topos.

**Curry–Howard–Lambek.** Typed λ-calculi, intuitionistic proofs, and cartesian closed categories are three presentations of one content [4]: types are objects, contexts are products, terms `Γ ⊢ t : A` are morphisms `⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦A⟧`, β-reduction is validated by the universal property of the exponential and η by its uniqueness. Lambek and Scott proved the syntactic category of a typed λ-calculus is the *free* CCC on its type constants — soundness and completeness in one stroke [4]. Moggi's work generalizes this template: keep CCC structure for *values*, add monadic structure for *computations*.

**Monads.** A *monad* `(T, η, μ)` on `C` is an endofunctor `T` with *unit* `η : Id ⇒ T` and *multiplication* `μ : T² ⇒ T` satisfying `μ ∘ Tη = id = μ ∘ ηT` and `μ ∘ Tμ = μ ∘ μT` [5]. Equivalently, a **Kleisli triple** `(T, η, (−)*)` gives, for each `f : A → T B`, an extension `f* : T A → T B` with `η_A* = id`, `f* ∘ η_A = f`, `(g* ∘ f)* = g* ∘ f*` — precisely the operations a language needs: `η` is *return*, `(−)*` is *bind* [2]. The **Kleisli category** `C_T` has the objects of `C` and morphisms `A ⇝ B` given by `A → T B` in `C`, composed by `g ⋄ f = μ ∘ Tg ∘ f`: it is the category whose morphisms *are* effectful programs. The **Eilenberg–Moore category** `C^T` of algebras `(A, a : T A → A)` is the category of *handlers* — coherent ways of interpreting the effect. Both resolve the monad from an adjunction (§4.2).

**Strong monads and the effect zoo.** A *strong* monad adds `τ_{A,B} : A × T B → T(A × B)`, letting a value in the environment participate in an effectful computation — the categorical content of call-by-value application [1]. On **Set** every monad is uniquely strong; in enriched settings strength is a genuine constraint.

| Effect | `T A` | Intuition |
|---|---|---|
| Partiality | `A + 1` | values or divergence |
| Exceptions | `A + E` | values or a raised `E` |
| Nondeterminism | `P(A)` | sets of outcomes |
| State | `(A × S)^S` | store transformers |
| Continuations | `(A → R) → R` | CPS computations |

## 3. Methodology

Our method is categorical denotational semantics; claims are validated by proof — commuting diagrams, universal properties, equational derivations — under four principles.

**1. Syntax as initial algebra.** A language is a syntactic category (types as objects, terms modulo equations as morphisms); a semantics is a structure-preserving functor into a semantic category. Soundness is well-definedness of the functor; completeness its faithfulness. For λ_c the target is the Kleisli category of a strong monad [1][2].

**2. Universal properties as specifications.** Semantic operations are specified universally — currying for exponentials, the Kleisli extension for bind, the trace axioms for feedback — making the semantics portable across **Set**, **Cpo**, and presheaves, and isolating exactly which structure each feature demands.

**3. Diagrams and the graphical calculus.** Proofs use commuting diagrams in the style of Mac Lane [5], plus string diagrams for monoidal structure, where coherence theorems promote pictures to proofs [8]. Topological deformation of a diagram *is* equational proof — indispensable when reasoning about feedback.

**4. Resolution comparison.** Every monad is induced by many adjunctions; the Kleisli construction (free algebras — *programs*) is initial among them and Eilenberg–Moore (all algebras — *handlers*) terminal [5]. Comparing resolutions relates operational notions to denotational ones.

The "empirical" content of the thesis is thus: (a) verification of each commuting diagram against the monad laws; (b) the tabulation of concrete monads instantiating the framework; (c) precise counterexamples where hypotheses fail — the combination problem, non-algebraic effects, partial traces (§6).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Monads as Notions of Computation

Moggi's slogan: *a value of type `A` is `1 → A`; a computation of type `A` is `1 → T A`; a program from `A` to `B` is `A → T B`* [1]. The unit `η_A : A → T A` embeds values as effect-free computations; the multiplication `μ_A : TTA → TA` flattens nested computations; Kleisli composition sequences programs:

```haskell
-- sequencing effectful programs; categorically, g diamond f = mu . T g . f
kleisli :: Monad t => (a -> t b) -> (b -> t c) -> (a -> t c)
kleisli f g = \x -> join (fmap g (f x))
```

The monad laws acquire computational readings: *left unit* (`bind (return a) f = f a`) — sequencing after a trivial computation is application; *right unit* (`bind m return = m`) — the trivial continuation changes nothing; *associativity* — grouping of sequential composition is immaterial, the proved rather than postulated associativity of `;`.

**Strength.** The strength `τ : A × TB → T(A × B)` lets an already-evaluated argument value participate in an effectful application: the semantics pairs the value with the function-computation and pushes the pairing inside `T` before sequencing. Without strength, λ_c could not type application under effects [1]. On **Set** strength is automatic — which is why Haskell programmers rarely notice it — while domain-theoretic models must construct it explicitly.

**The zoo, categorically.** Partiality `A + 1` models recursion; exceptions `A + E` propagate the first raise via `μ`; nondeterminism `P(A)` makes Kleisli composition relational composition, with complete sup-lattices as algebras; state `(A × S)^S` threads the store, its strength encoding state-passing; continuations `(A → R) → R` internalize CPS. Wadler's insight [3] was that this apparatus internalizes as a programming idiom: `do`-notation *is* Kleisli composition, and the monad laws are the equations programmers expect of sequencing.

### 4.2 Adjunctions: Every Monad Arises Twice

An adjunction `F ⊣ G` (`F : C → D` left adjoint to `G : D → C`) has unit `η : Id ⇒ GF` and counit `ε : FG ⇒ Id` satisfying the **triangle identities** `(εF) ∘ (Fη) = id_F` and `(Gε) ∘ (ηG) = id_G` — the unit followed by the counit "pulls straight" [5]. Every adjunction induces a monad `(GF, η, GεF)` on `C`. The converse, due to Kleisli and Eilenberg–Moore (1965):

> **Theorem (Resolutions [5]).** Every monad `(T, η, μ)` is induced by the Kleisli adjunction `F_T ⊣ G_T : C → C_T` and the Eilenberg–Moore adjunction `F^T ⊣ G^T : C → C^T`. The Kleisli resolution is *initial* and the Eilenberg–Moore resolution *terminal*: for any `F ⊣ G` with `GF = T`, unique comparison functors `C_T → D` and `D → C^T` commute with the adjunctions.

Computationally, `C_T` contains only *free* algebras — programs as written, with no equations beyond the monad laws — while `C^T` contains *all* algebras: every coherent handler of the effect (state handlers, exception handlers, joins for nondeterminism). Terminality says every handler factors through the free interpretation: *handlers are algebras for the effect monad*, already implicit in Mac Lane [5]. Beck's monadicity theorem characterizes when an adjunction is *fully* captured by its monad — `G` must create coequalizers of `G`-split pairs — the working tool for recognizing, e.g., that Lawvere-theory models coincide with monad algebras (§5).

### 4.3 Cartesian Closed Categories and the Computational λ-Calculus

Moggi's λ_c splits the single judgement of the λ-calculus in two [2]:

- **Values** `Γ ⊢ V : A` — inert data, as morphisms `⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦A⟧` in `C`;
- **Computations** `Γ ⊢ M : A` — effectful programs, as morphisms `⟦Γ⟧ → T⟦A⟧`, i.e. Kleisli morphisms `⟦Γ⟧ ⇝ ⟦A⟧`.

```haskell
data Val  = Var Name | Lam Name Comp     -- values: inert data
data Comp = Ret Val                     -- eta: trivial computation
          | Bind Comp Name Comp          -- Kleisli extension (bind)
          | App Comp Val                 -- application to a *value*
```

Typing is the expected one — `Ret` uses the unit, `Bind` the Kleisli extension, and application consumes a *value*, enforcing a computational (call-by-value) evaluation order — plus the **monadic laws** as equations: `Bind (Ret V) x N = N[V/x]`, `Bind M x (Ret x) = M`, and associativity of `Bind`, with commuting conversions. These are exactly the monad laws transported across the interpretation:

> **Theorem (Soundness [1][2]).** The Kleisli interpretation of λ_c is sound; the syntactic category is the free strong-monad-over-CCC structure, giving completeness.

Exponentials `B^A` interpret the *value* function type; the *computation* function type `A → TB` is interpreted via currying plus strength — strength doing essential, nearly invisible work once more. Setting `T = Id` recovers the simply-typed λ-calculus and its CCC semantics: Moggi's framework *conservatively extends* Curry–Howard–Lambek [4] rather than replacing it.

### 4.4 Lawvere Theories and Traced Structure for Feedback

**From monads to operations.** A monad says *that* effects compose, not *what the operations are*. Plotkin and Power's algebraic effects [7] specify an effect by a **Lawvere theory**: a category `L` with finite products and a strict product-preserving identity-on-objects functor from finite cardinals — a signature of operations plus equations. Examples: state via `get : 1 → S` and `put : S → 1` with the standard state laws; exceptions via `raise_e : 0`; nondeterminism via binary choice.

> **Theorem (Lawvere–Linton [7]).** Finitary monads on **Set** correspond bijectively to Lawvere theories; the monad is the free-model monad, and each operation induces a *generic effect* `P → TQ`, a Kleisli morphism.

Hyland and Power's account [7] shows why this matters: theories admit a **sum** (disjoint union of effects) and a **tensor** (commuting combination) — a principled answer to the monad *combination problem* (§6) — and enriched variants cover recursion via **Cpo**. The monad is *derived*, not postulated.

**Feedback.** Iteration, recursion, and cyclic dataflow share one shape: a process with a feedback wire. Joyal, Street, and Verity axiomatized it [6]: a **traced monoidal category** has `Tr^X_{A,B} : C(A ⊗ X, B ⊗ X) → C(A, B)` satisfying *naturality* (feedback commutes with pre/post-processing), *dinaturality* or *sliding* (a process slides around the loop — the categorical fixed-point equation), *vanishing* (nested loops collapse), *superposing* (compatibility with `⊗`), and *yanking* (`Tr(σ) = id` — a twisted wire straightens). The **Int-construction** builds a compact closed category of bidirectional processes from any traced `C` — the home of game semantics and Girard's Geometry of Interaction, whose execution formula *is* a categorical trace. Selinger's graphical calculus [8] makes this rigorous: boxes are morphisms, wires composition, loops the trace — and topological deformation is equational proof by coherence.

Together: Lawvere theories say *which* effects a language has; traced structure says how computations *loop*; monads and CCCs say how they *compose* and *abstract*.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

> **Theorem 1 (Soundness of λ_c [1][2]).** In a CCC with a strong monad, interpreting `Ret` by `η`, `Bind` by Kleisli extension, and application by `ev` precomposed with strength, every λ_c equation holds in `C_T`.
> *Proof sketch.* Induction on derivations: the `Bind`/`Ret` β-rule is the left unit law `μ ∘ Tη = id`; the η-rule the right unit law; associativity of `Bind` the associativity of `μ`; commuting conversions naturality of `τ` and `η`. ∎

> **Theorem 2 (Resolutions [5]).** The Kleisli adjunction is initial and Eilenberg–Moore terminal among adjunctions inducing a monad `T`, via unique comparison functors.
> *Proof sketch.* Send a Kleisli map `f : A → GFB` to `ε_{FB} ∘ Ff`; the triangle identities give functoriality and uniqueness. Dually, `d ↦ (Gd, Gε_d)` is a `T`-algebra. ∎
> *Corollary.* Every effect model admits a canonical handler structure — the abstract justification of effect handlers [5][7].

> **Theorem 3 (Lawvere ↔ monad [7]).** Finitary monads on **Set** biject with Lawvere theories; `T_L`-algebras are the models of the theory, generic effects its derived operations.
> *Proof sketch.* Finite products in `L` give the free-model left adjoint; restricting `Set_T` to finite cardinals inverts the construction by finitarity. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (Traced coherence [6][8]).** String diagrams with feedback denote equal morphisms iff related by planar isotopy plus the trace axioms; `Int(C)` is compact closed.
> *Proof sketch.* The JSV axioms are sound for the graphical language; coherence follows from the faithful embedding of the free traced category into `Int` of itself plus Kelly–Laplaza coherence. ∎

| # | Result | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Soundness of λ_c in Kleisli categories | Strong monad, CCC [1][2] |
| T2 | Kleisli initial / EM terminal; handlers as algebras | Adjunctions [5][7] |
| T3 | Lawvere theory ↔ finitary monad | Finite products [7] |
| T4 | Coherence of traced graphical calculus | Trace axioms [6][8] |

*Falsifiability.* Each theorem's hypotheses are load-bearing: drop strength and T1's application case breaks; drop finitarity and T3 fails — the continuations monad is the witness; admit partial traces and T4's yanking weakens. The framework maps exactly which structure each phenomenon requires.

## 6. Limitations

**1. The combination problem.** Monads do not compose: `ST` need not be a monad, distributive laws `TS ⇒ ST` are rare (no well-behaved law distributes list over itself), and monad transformers are order-sensitive and non-canonical. The algebraic response [7] combines *theories* — sums and tensors always exist — and derives the monad afterwards.

**2. Strength is not free.** On **Set** every monad is uniquely strong, hiding the requirement; in domains, games, or realizability models strength must be constructed and may fail. Enriched Lawvere theories [7] track which effects survive enrichment.

**3. Non-algebraic effects.** Continuations `(− → R) → R` are strong but not algebraic: `call/cc` admits no operations-and-equations presentation, so Theorem 3 excludes a major control operator. Delimited continuations mark the current boundary of the algebraicity thesis.

**4. Partial traces.** The JSV trace [6] is total; recursion and domain-theoretic fixed points admit only *partial* traces, defined when the feedback equation has a least solution. Partially traced categories weaken the axioms — and Theorem 4's clean coherence — accordingly.

**5. Adequacy ≠ full abstraction.** Soundness justifies equations; it does not identify denotational equality with observational equivalence. Fully abstract models of effectful languages (games, realizability, sheaves) remain largely open.

**6. Complexity blindness.** Monads classify *which* effects occur, not their cost: denotationally equal programs may differ wildly in time or space. Graded monads and coeffects attach quantitative grades, but a unified intensional account is fragmentary.

## 7. Conclusion

The categorical semantics of computation is a tower of universal properties. Cartesian closed categories interpret the pure λ-calculus via Curry–Howard–Lambek [4]. Moggi [1][2] relativized this to a *notion of computation* — a strong monad — interpreting programs in the Kleisli category while values stay in the base CCC; Wadler [3] turned the semantics into a language design principle. The adjunction–monad correspondence [5] explains the double origin of every such semantics: freely as programs (Kleisli), universally as handlers (Eilenberg–Moore) — the abstract source of effect handlers. Lawvere theories [7] recover monads from the operations programmers write, restoring modularity through sums and tensors of theories. Traced monoidal categories [6] axiomatize feedback, with a graphical calculus [8] that turns reasoning about loops into topology.

The limitations of §6 are a research agenda, not a refutation: effect combination, strength under enrichment, non-algebraic control, partial traces, full abstraction, intensional cost. Each marks where the current universal properties are not yet the right ones — and the history surveyed here suggests that finding the right universal property is exactly how the field advances. Moggi's lasting lesson is methodological: do not ask what a program *is*; ask what *structure* its computations preserve. Categories are the language in which that question can be asked precisely — and answered.
