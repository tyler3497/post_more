# Flow Matching and Rectified Flow: Simulation-Free Generative Modeling via Continuous Normalizing Flows

## Abstract

Flow matching has displaced score-based diffusion as the dominant paradigm for large-scale generative modeling, powering Stable Diffusion 3, Flux, and Sora [1][6]. This thesis develops the theory from first principles: continuous normalizing flows [7] and the simulation bottleneck of maximum-likelihood training; conditional flow matching [1], whose per-example conditional loss provably shares gradients with the intractable marginal objective; the optimal-transport displacement interpolation yielding the simple target velocity u_t = x_1 - x_0; rectified flow and reflow [3][4], which provably reduce transport cost while preserving marginals and straighten trajectories toward exact single-step integration; a precise comparison showing diffusion's probability-flow ODE is a special case of flow matching [1]; and quantitative analysis of straightness versus ODE-solver discretization error. We close with empirical results -- state-of-the-art ImageNet likelihoods and FIDs [1][3], minibatch OT-CFM gains [5] -- and limitations: reflow mode dropout, latent-space coupling quality, and likelihood-evaluation cost.

## 1 Introduction

Deep generative models learn to sample from a data distribution $q(x_1)$ over $\mathbb{R}^d$. The last decade's dominant approaches — GANs, VAEs, autoregressive models, and diffusion models — each made a different trade between sample quality, training stability, and sampling cost. *Score-based diffusion models* [8][10] achieved remarkable sample quality by learning to reverse a stochastic noising process, but at the price of hundreds to thousands of sequential denoising steps at sampling time, a fixed noise schedule, and a somewhat baroque training procedure (noise prediction, $\bar\alpha_t$ schedules, Tweedie corrections).

*Flow matching*, introduced by Lipman et al. [1], asks a disarmingly simple question: if all we need is a *velocity field* whose flow carries noise to data, why route through stochastic processes at all? Why not learn the velocity field *directly*, and choose the nicest possible transport paths? The answer is a simulation-free training objective of striking simplicity — plain least-squares regression of a neural velocity field onto target velocities along interpolated paths — that subsumes diffusion as a special case [1].

*Rectified flow*, introduced by Liu et al. [3][4], takes the second step: it observes that any learned flow induces a deterministic coupling between noise and data samples, and that retraining on this induced coupling ("reflow") monotonically reduces the transport cost and straightens trajectories. Perfectly straight flows can be integrated *exactly* in a single Euler step, which is the theoretical engine behind modern few-step generators [3][6].

This thesis gives a rigorous, self-contained account: CNFs and their training bottleneck (§2–3), the conditional flow matching theorem (§4.1), OT paths and the linear-interpolation miracle (§4.2), rectified flow and reflow (§4.3), the diffusion comparison (§4.4), straightness and ODE solvers (§4.5), empirical results (§5), and limitations (§6).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Continuous Normalizing Flows

A *continuous normalizing flow* [7] defines a time-dependent diffeomorphism via the ODE

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = v_\theta(x, t), \qquad x(0) \sim p_0, \quad t \in [0,1],$$

where $v_\theta : \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is a neural velocity field and $p_0$ is a simple base distribution (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$). Pushing $p_0$ through the flow yields the model distribution $p_1 = [\phi_1]_\# p_0$, where $\phi_t$ is the flow map. Densities evolve by the *instantaneous change of variables* [7]:

$$\log p_1(x_1) = \log p_0(x_0) - \int_0^1 \nabla \cdot v_\theta(x_t, t)\, dt,$$

so exact likelihoods are available — unlike GANs or VAEs. The catch is computational: maximum-likelihood training requires *simulating the ODE* (and its divergence integral) for every training example at every gradient step. This simulation cost is what kept CNFs a niche tool until flow matching removed it [1].

### 2.2 Score-Based Diffusion as a Point of Departure

Score-based models [8][10] define a forward SDE $dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw$ that diffuses data into noise, then learn the *score* $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ to reverse it. Sampling integrates the reverse SDE or its deterministic *probability-flow ODE* [10]:

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x) =: u_t(x).$$

Two observations matter for what follows. First, diffusion already *is* a flow: the probability-flow ODE transports noise to data deterministically [10]. Second, training is indirect — the network predicts *noise* added at time $t$, and the velocity field is reconstructed through schedule-dependent algebra. Flow matching [1] keeps the first observation and discards the second: learn $u_t$ directly.

### 2.3 Couplings, Transport Cost, and Straightness

Let $\pi_0 = p_0$ (noise) and $\pi_1 = q$ (data). A *coupling* $\pi \in \Pi(\pi_0, \pi_1)$ is a joint distribution with the correct marginals — a probabilistic pairing of noise samples with data samples. The *transport cost* of a coupling under squared Euclidean cost is $\mathbb{E}_{(X_0,X_1)\sim\pi}[\|X_1 - X_0\|^2]$; the *2-Wasserstein distance* $W_2^2(\pi_0,\pi_1)$ is its infimum [4].

> **Definition (Straightness):** A flow $\{X_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}$ is *straight* if trajectories are constant-speed line segments: $X_t = (1-t)X_0 + tX_1$ a.s. Equivalently, the velocity field satisfies $v(X_t, t) = X_1 - X_0$ along trajectories.

Straightness is the property that makes sampling cheap: a straight flow is integrated *exactly* by a single Euler step, so discretization error vanishes and few-step sampling is principled rather than heuristic [3].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Flow Matching Objective — and Why It Is Intractable

Suppose we are given a *target* probability path $p_t(x)$ interpolating $p_0 \to q$ and a *target* velocity field $u_t(x)$ generating it (i.e., satisfying the continuity equation $\partial_t p_t + \nabla\cdot(p_t u_t) = 0$). The *flow matching* loss [1] regresses the neural field onto the target:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1],\, x \sim p_t}\big[\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x)\|^2\big].$$

The problem: for any nontrivial $p_t$, the field $u_t(x)$ has no closed form — computing it requires solving a high-dimensional PDE or simulating the process that defines $p_t$. Direct FM is therefore intractable in exactly the cases of interest.

### 3.2 Conditional Flow Matching: The Key Theorem

The breakthrough of [1] is to condition on the *data point*. For each $x_1 \sim q$, choose a *conditional* path $p_t(x \mid x_1)$ and conditional velocity $u_t(x \mid x_1)$ that are *tractable by construction*, and define:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\, x_1 \sim q,\, x \sim p_t(\cdot \mid x_1)}\big[\|v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x \mid x_1)\|^2\big].$$

> **Theorem (Lipman et al. [1]):** Let $p_t(x) = \int p_t(x \mid x_1)\, q(x_1)\, dx_1$ be the marginal path and $u_t(x)$ the marginal velocity field it generates. Then $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)$. In particular, the two objectives have identical minimizers.

*Proof sketch.* Expand the FM loss: $\|v_\theta - u_t\|^2 = \|v_\theta\|^2 - 2\langle v_\theta, u_t\rangle + \|u_t\|^2$. The last term is $\theta$-independent. The cross term, by the tower property, satisfies $\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_t}[\langle v_\theta(x,t), u_t(x)\rangle] = \mathbb{E}_{x_1\sim q}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_t(\cdot\mid x_1)}[\langle v_\theta(x,t), u_t(x\mid x_1)\rangle]$, because the marginal velocity is exactly the conditional expectation $u_t(x) = \mathbb{E}[u_t(x\mid X_1)\mid X_t = x]$ [1]. The same identity applied to $\|v_\theta\|^2$ completes the argument. ∎

This is the simulation-free miracle: we train on *conditional* targets we can write down in closed form, and gradient descent provably optimizes the *marginal* objective we actually care about.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Optimal-Transport Conditional Path

The canonical choice in [1] is the *OT displacement interpolation*. Given a coupling $(X_0, X_1) \sim \pi$ (in the simplest case, the *independent* coupling $\pi_0 \otimes q$), define:

$$X_t = (1-t) X_0 + t X_1, \qquad p_t(\cdot \mid x_1) = \text{Law}(X_t \mid X_1 = x_1).$$

Differentiating, the conditional velocity is constant along each segment:

$$u_t(x \mid x_1) = X_1 - X_0 \quad \text{(evaluated at the preimage of } x).$$

The CFM objective collapses to beautifully simple least squares [1]:

```python
# Conditional Flow Matching with OT displacement interpolation (Lipman et al. 2023)
t  = rand(batch)[:, None]              # t ~ Uniform[0,1]
x0 = randn(batch, dim)                 # noise  ~ pi_0
x1 = data_batch                        # data   ~ q
xt = (1 - t) * x0 + t * x1             # linear interpolation
u  = x1 - x0                           # target velocity (constant along segment)
loss = ((v_theta(xt, t) - u) ** 2).mean()
```

No noise schedule, no $\bar\alpha_t$, no Tweedie formula — just interpolation and regression. Lipman et al. [1] further show that choosing the *diffusion* conditional path recovers score-based diffusion exactly: diffusion is a special case of flow matching with curved conditional paths, and its probability-flow ODE is the marginal field of that choice.

### 4.2 Minibatch OT Couplings: OT-CFM

The independent coupling pairs noise with data at random, producing *crossing* trajectories that the network must average over — a harder regression problem with curved marginal flows. Tong et al. [5] replace it with a *minibatch optimal-transport* coupling: within each training batch, solve the discrete OT assignment between noise and data samples, then interpolate along the matched pairs. This *OT-CFM* objective:

- provably approximates *dynamic* optimal transport as batch size grows [5],
- yields straighter, non-crossing conditional paths and more stable training,
- improves both likelihood and sample quality with no extra simulation [5].

The cost is one small linear-assignment solve per batch (Hungarian algorithm, $O(B^3)$ for batch size $B$) — negligible next to the network forward pass.

### 4.3 Rectified Flow and Reflow

Liu et al. [3][4] add the crucial second idea. Train a flow $v^{(0)}$ on *any* coupling (e.g., independent). Simulating its ODE from $X_0 \sim \pi_0$ produces $X_1 = \phi_1(X_0)$ — a *deterministic coupling* $\pi^{(1)} = \mathrm{Law}(X_0, \phi_1(X_0))$ of noise and (generated) data. Now *retrain* a new flow $v^{(1)}$ on straight-line interpolations of *this* coupling. Repeat: this is **reflow** [3].

> **Theorem (Rectification reduces transport cost [4]):** Let $\pi$ be a coupling of $(\pi_0, \pi_1)$ and let $\pi'$ be the rectified coupling induced by the flow trained on $\pi$. Then $\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}[\|X_1 - X_0\|^2] \le \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\|X_1 - X_0\|^2]$, with equality iff the flow is already straight. Moreover the marginals are preserved: $\pi' \in \Pi(\pi_0, \pi_1)$.

*Intuition.* The rectified coupling replaces each conditional path by the flow's own trajectory, and the new target velocity $X_1 - X_0$ is the *conditional expectation* of the old velocity along trajectories — a Rao–Blackwellization that cannot increase squared cost (Jensen). Iterating drives the coupling toward the straight, cost-minimizing one; in the limit of perfect reflow, trajectories become straight lines and the flow is *1-rectified* [3][4].

The practical consequence is dramatic: a 1-rectified flow can be sampled with a *single Euler step* with no discretization error, and a few steps suffice even for imperfectly straight flows [3]. Reflow is also the theoretical ancestor of *distillation* methods used in production systems [6].

```haskell
-- Reflow, conceptually (Liu et al. 2022)
reflow :: Coupling -> Flow -> Coupling
reflow pi v = let phi = solveODE v               -- simulate learned ODE
              in pushforward (\(x0,_) -> (x0, phi x0)) pi
-- iterate: pi_{k+1} = reflow pi_k v_k ; cost is nonincreasing, marginals preserved
```

### 4.4 Flow Matching vs. Score-Based Diffusion: A Precise Comparison

| Aspect | Score-based diffusion [8][10] | Flow matching [1] / Rectified flow [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Learned object | Score $\nabla_x\log p_t$ (or noise $\epsilon$) | Velocity field $v_\theta(x,t)$ directly |
| Training paths | Curved SDE sample paths | Straight-line interpolations (OT-CFM [5]) |
| Noise schedule | Required ($\beta_t$, $\bar\alpha_t$) | None — $t \in [0,1]$ uniform |
| Sampling | Reverse SDE / probability-flow ODE, 100s of steps | ODE solve; few Euler steps suffice when straight |
| Simulation-free | Yes (denoising score matching) | Yes (CFM theorem [1]) |
| Likelihood | Via probability-flow ODE + divergence integral | Same machinery, often better NLL [1] |
| Relation | — | Diffusion is FM with diffusion conditional paths [1] |

The "diffusion is a special case" claim [1] is exact, not rhetorical: choose $p_t(x\mid x_1)$ as the diffusion perturbation kernel and $u_t(x\mid x_1)$ as its associated conditional velocity, and CFM *is* denoising score matching up to the probability-flow ODE change of variables.

### 4.5 Straightness, Discretization Error, and ODE Solvers

Sampling a CNF means numerically integrating $dx/dt = v_\theta(x,t)$. For Euler's method with $N$ steps on a general field, global error is $O(1/N)$ — hence diffusion's hundreds of steps. But for a *straight* flow, $v(x_t,t) = x_1 - x_0$ is constant along each trajectory, and Euler with $N=1$ is *exact* [3]. More generally, the local truncation error scales with trajectory curvature, so straighter flows tolerate coarser steps. This is why:

- OT-CFM [5] (straighter conditional paths) samples faster than vanilla CFM,
- reflowed models [3] generate in 1–10 steps,
- production systems (Stable Diffusion 3, Flux [6]) use flow matching with distilled few-step samplers.

Higher-order solvers (Heun, Dormand–Prince) further reduce steps for moderately curved flows, at the cost of extra function evaluations per step — the standard speed/accuracy trade-off now operating on much friendlier vector fields.

## 5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

- **ImageNet.** Lipman et al. [1] report that OT-path flow matching achieves state-of-the-art likelihood *and* sample quality on ImageNet-32/64, outperforming contemporary diffusion baselines on both NLL and FID — the first method to lead on both metrics simultaneously.
- **Rectified flow generation.** Liu et al. [3] demonstrate high-quality CIFAR-10 and ImageNet generation with as few as 1–10 Euler steps after one round of reflow, versus hundreds for diffusion baselines at matched quality.
- **Minibatch OT.** Tong et al. [5] show OT-CFM and Schrödinger-bridge CFM variants improve FID and NLL over independent-coupling CFM across image benchmarks, with the gains growing as batch size increases toward the true OT coupling.
- **Theory.** The CFM gradient identity [1], the rectification cost-reduction theorem [4], and the characterization of diffusion as a CFM special case [1] are exact results, not heuristics. The stochastic-interpolant framework [9] further unifies flows, diffusions, and probability-flow ODEs under one umbrella, showing that the *choice of interpolant* $I_t(x_0,x_1)$ and noise schedule is the only real degree of freedom.
- **Industry adoption.** Stable Diffusion 3, Flux, and Sora all train with flow-matching objectives [6], marking a paradigm shift: the field's frontier models are now CNFs trained simulation-free.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Reflow and mode coverage.** Reflow trains on *generated* data; if the first flow drops modes, the rectified coupling inherits the dropout and reflow can amplify it. Careful monitoring of recall/diversity across reflow rounds is required [3].
2. **Likelihood evaluation still simulates.** Training is simulation-free, but computing $\log p_1(x)$ still requires integrating the divergence — with Hutchinson trace estimation and many solver steps. Flow matching improves the field but not the change-of-variables formula.
3. **Coupling quality in high dimensions.** Minibatch OT approximates true OT well only for large batches; in very high dimensions (e.g., $256\times256$ images in pixel space) the assignment is noisy, which is why production systems apply FM in *latent* space [6].
4. **Straightness is asymptotic.** One reflow round rarely achieves perfect straightness; the "single-step" claim is exact only for 1-rectified flows, and residual curvature reintroduces discretization error.
5. **Conditional generation overhead.** Classifier-free guidance ports naturally to velocity fields ($\tilde v = v_\varnothing + w(v_c - v_\varnothing)$), but doubles function evaluations — a real cost in few-step regimes where each evaluation dominates.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Flow matching [1] resolved the central dilemma of continuous normalizing flows [7] — exact likelihoods and deterministic transport at the price of simulation-based training — by proving that a tractable *conditional* regression objective has gradients identical to the intractable marginal one. The optimal-transport interpolation makes the target velocity trivial ($x_1 - x_0$), minibatch OT couplings [5] straighten conditional paths, and rectified flow with reflow [3][4] provably reduces transport cost while preserving marginals, driving trajectories toward the straight lines that admit exact single-step integration. Diffusion models survive inside this framework as a special case [1][9], but the general theory is simpler, faster to sample from, and empirically stronger — which is why the frontier has moved. Open problems remain: mode-preserving reflow, simulation-free likelihoods, and a deeper understanding of straightness in latent spaces.
