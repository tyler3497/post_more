# Twin-Field Quantum Key Distribution Beyond the Repeaterless Bound: PLOB Bound, Phase-Matching Decoy-State Protocols, Finite-Key Composable Security Analysis, and Single-Photon Interference over 500km Fiber

## Abstract

Twin-field quantum key distribution (TF-QKD) overturned a twenty-year-old article of faith in quantum cryptography: that repeaterless secret-key rates must scale linearly with channel transmittance *η*. The 2017 Pirandola–Laurenza–Ottaviani–Banchi (PLOB) bound, *K*(*η*) = −log₂(1−*η*), certified linear scaling as optimal for *all* point-to-point protocols; the 2018 Lucamarini *et al.* protocol showed that an untrusted central node performing single-photon interference converts double-sided loss into single-sided loss, yielding √*η* scaling — the signature of a quantum repeater — with no quantum memory and no trusted relay. This thesis gives a self-contained account of the physics and cryptography behind TF-QKD: the teleportation-stretching proof of the PLOB bound, the phase-matching (PM-QKD) and sending-or-not-sending (SNS) decoy-state variants that closed the original protocol's phase-announcement loophole, the Renner composable finite-key security framework that makes 500 km demonstrations cryptographically meaningful, and the phase-stabilization technology behind fiber experiments reaching 509–1002 km. We conclude with limitations — phase noise, finite-size penalties, source assumptions — and the remaining gap to deployable long-haul quantum networks.

## 1 Introduction

Quantum key distribution (QKD) promises information-theoretic security grounded in the laws of physics rather than computational hardness assumptions. Yet for three decades, every point-to-point protocol shared an apparently immutable constraint: secret-key rate *R* scaled linearly with channel transmittance *η*, *R* = *O*(*η*). Over optical fiber with 0.2 dB/km loss, transmittance decays as *η* = 10^(−0.02·*L*) for distance *L* in km, so linear scaling meant that every additional 50 km of fiber cost an order of magnitude in key rate. MDI-QKD (2012) removed all detector side channels — the most exploited vulnerability in deployed systems — but its rate scales with the *product* of arm transmittances, *R* ~ *η_A*·*η_B* = *η*, and its distance record stalled at 404 km below 2 bits per hour.

In 2017, Pirandola, Laurenza, Ottaviani and Banchi closed a twenty-year open problem by computing the exact two-way secret-key capacity of the pure-loss bosonic channel [1]:

> **Theorem (PLOB bound):** For the pure-loss channel ℰ_η_ with transmittance *η*, the two-way assisted quantum and private capacities coincide and equal *K*(ℰ_η_) = −log₂(1 − *η*) bits per channel use. At high loss, −log₂(1−*η*) ≈ 1.44*η*.

Because the bound holds against arbitrary adaptive protocols with unlimited two-way classical communication, it was read as a verdict: no repeaterless protocol could beat linear scaling, and only quantum repeaters — with memories, swapping and purification — offered a way out.

One year later, Lucamarini, Yuan, Dynes and Shields at Toshiba Research Europe published a protocol that broke this framing without building a repeater [2]. In twin-field QKD, Alice and Bob transmit phase-encoded weak coherent pulses to a *central, untrusted* node — Charlie — who interferes them on a beam splitter and announces single-photon detections. A success needs only *one* photon to survive *one* arm ("unilateral transmission loss"), so the rate scales as √*η* — repeater scaling — with manageable noise over 550 km of standard fiber on off-the-shelf components [2].

The resolution of the apparent paradox is topological, not magical. The PLOB bound constrains protocols with *no intermediate node whatsoever*. Inserting even a fully untrusted relay changes the network: the relevant fundamental limit becomes the *single-repeater* (one-relay) capacity, whose scaling is √*η* — precisely what TF-QKD attains [1, 2]. TF-QKD is thus a *measurement-device-independent repeater-like* protocol: an untrusted middle node providing repeater scaling while learning nothing about the key.

This thesis develops the full story: the bound, the evasion, the protocols that secured it, the finite-key analysis that made it rigorous, and the experiments that proved it over 500 km of fiber.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The repeaterless capacity problem

A quantum channel ℰ maps Alice's inputs to Bob's outputs while leaking to an eavesdropper Eve controlling the environment. The *two-way secret-key capacity* *K*(ℰ) is the maximum key rate over adaptive LOCC in the asymptotic limit of many channel uses. Computing it requires optimizing over all adaptive strategies, which kept the problem open for roughly twenty years.

Pirandola *et al.* solved it via *teleportation stretching* [1]: a lossy channel is *teleportation-covariant*, simulable by teleporting the input over its Choi state *σ*_ℰ (a two-mode squeezed vacuum for pure loss). Any adaptive protocol then "stretches" into a block protocol on *n* copies of *σ*_ℰ, bounding the rate by the *relative entropy of entanglement* (REE) of one Choi copy:

*R* ≤ *E_R*(*σ*_ℰ) = −log₂(1 − *η*)

with equality achieved (e.g., by reverse coherent information protocols), establishing the capacity exactly. The small-*η* expansion −log₂(1−*η*) ≈ 1.44*η* is the fundamental rate-loss tradeoff, used since 2017 to benchmark repeater prototypes [1].

### 2.2 Why MDI-QKD could not beat it

Measurement-device-independent QKD places the detectors in an untrusted middle node, closing all detection side channels — the class of attacks behind most successful hacks of QKD hardware. But a successful Bell-state measurement in MDI-QKD requires *coincident* detection of photons from *both* Alice and Bob, so the yield scales as *η_A*·*η_B*. With symmetric arms *η_A* = *η_B* = √*η*, the rate scales as *η*: strictly linear, and bounded above by PLOB. MDI-QKD is thus "repeaterless" in the PLOB classification: its middle node gives no scaling advantage.

### 2.3 The twin-field insight

Lucamarini *et al.* observed that the coincidence requirement is not inherent to having a middle node [2]. If Alice and Bob send *phase-coherent* fields that interfere at Charlie's beam splitter, a *single* click — one photon from *either* party — suffices to generate key. The detection probability then scales with the loss of a *single* arm, √*η*, rather than the product. The key bit is encoded in the relative phase (0 or *π*); which detector clicks reveals the phase relationship without revealing whose photon arrived. Charlie may be Eve herself — the protocol is measurement-device-independent by construction — yet the rate scales as √*η*, matching a single ideal repeater.

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis proceeds along four complementary axes, each corresponding to a layer of the TF-QKD research program.

**Axis 1 — The bound.** We reconstruct the PLOB argument to establish what is beaten and why the single-repeater capacity −log₂(1−√*η*) ~ 1.44√*η* is the right benchmark with an intermediate node [1].

**Axis 2 — The protocols.** We compare three generations of TF-type protocols: (i) original Lucamarini *et al.* TF-QKD with phase post-selection [2]; (ii) PM-QKD with an *optical-mode-based* security proof and phase post-compensation [3]; (iii) the SNS protocol, which drops phase post-selection in signal windows and tolerates large misalignment [4]. Decoy-state analysis is treated as the parameter-estimation backbone common to all variants.

**Axis 3 — Finite-key composable security.** We apply the Renner–Portmann composable framework — *ε*-security via trace distance, *ε* = *ε*_cor + *ε*_sec — with Chernoff/Hoeffding tails on decoy estimation, following the finite-key SNS analysis secure against coherent attacks at practical block sizes [5, 10].

**Axis 4 — Experimental validation.** We survey fiber demonstrations from the first beyond-PLOB laboratory results (2019) through 509 km with independent lasers [6], the 511 km deployed-fiber metropolitan link [7], and the 1002 km finite-key demonstration [8], focusing on phase-stabilization techniques (reference-pulse multiplexing, coherent phase transfer) enabling single-photon interference over hundreds of kilometers of drifting fiber [11, 12].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The PLOB bound: teleportation stretching and the relative entropy of entanglement

The proof technique pinpoints exactly which assumption TF-QKD relaxes. In an adaptive protocol over *n* channel uses, Alice and Bob apply LOCCs Λ_k conditioned on prior communication; the final state *ρ*ⁿ_AB has its key rate bounded by its REE, monotone under LOCC and asymptotically continuous.

If ℰ is teleportation-covariant it admits the simulation ℰ(*ρ*) = 𝒯(*ρ* ⊗ *σ*_ℰ) with teleportation LOCC 𝒯 and Choi state *σ*_ℰ; the pure-loss channel qualifies. Substituting this into every use and absorbing all adaptive LOCCs into one final Λ̄ gives *ρ*ⁿ_AB = Λ̄(*σ*^⊗*n*_ℰ), whence:

*R* ≤ lim_n (1/*n*) *E_R*(*σ*^⊗*n*_ℰ) ≤ *E_R*(*σ*_ℰ) = −log₂(1 − *η*)

using REE monotonicity and subadditivity. The converse is tight — reverse coherent information achieves it — so the two-way quantum capacity *Q*₂, entanglement-distribution capacity *D*₂ and secret-key capacity *K* all equal −log₂(1−*η*) [1]. For *η* ≪ 1 the expansion −log₂(1−*η*) = *η*/ln 2 − O(*η*²) gives the famous *1.44 bits per channel use per unit transmittance* benchmark.

Crucially, the proof assumes a direct Alice–Bob channel. With a middle node — even untrusted — the end-to-end channel is a network channel whose single-repeater bound scales as √*η* [1]. TF-QKD lives in this setting: it does not contradict PLOB, it *changes the game board*.

### 4.2 Single-photon interference and the √η mechanism

In the Lucamarini *et al.* protocol [2], each round proceeds as follows:

1. **Preparation.** Alice (Bob) prepares a phase-randomized coherent pulse, encoding key bit *b* ∈ {0,1} as added phase *b*·*π*; decoy rounds use intensities {*μ*, *ν*, *ω*} for parameter estimation.
2. **Transmission.** Both pulses travel to Charlie over fiber arms of transmittance √*η* each.
3. **Interference.** Charlie interferes the fields on a 50:50 beam splitter: matching phases route the photon to D0, phases differing by *π* to D1. A *single* click is a successful round.
4. **Sifting.** Alice and Bob announce *θ_A*, *θ_B* and keep rounds with |*θ_A* − *θ_B*| ≈ 0 or *π* (phase post-selection) and matching intensities.
5. **Key mapping.** D0 implies *b_A* = *b_B*, D1 implies *b_A* ≠ *b_B* (up to a flip): one raw key bit.

The decisive quantity is the *yield*: detection probability scales as *Y* ~ *μ*√*η* (single-arm survival), not (*μ*√*η*)². After 2/*M* phase-slice sifting, error correction and privacy amplification, *R* ~ O(√*η*) [2]. Security follows MDI logic: Charlie's announcement is a Bell-like projection correlating Alice and Bob without revealing the key, and dishonest behavior surfaces in the observed gains *Q_μ* and errors *E_μ*.

### 4.3 Phase-matching and sending-or-not-sending: closing the phase-announcement loophole

The original protocol had a subtle flaw: announcing the random phases leaks information the initial security argument did not fully cover [4]. Three protocol families closed the gap, each with a different proof philosophy.

**Phase-matching QKD (Ma–Zeng–Zhou, 2018).** PM-QKD encodes the key in the *common* phase of matched coherent states with an *optical-mode-based* proof — treating the infinite-dimensional mode directly instead of projecting onto a qubit subspace [3]. Phases are randomized over *M* discrete slices; Alice and Bob keep rounds where their slice indices match (0) or oppose (*π*), incurring the sifting factor 2/*M*. The asymptotic rate takes the Shor–Preskill-like form

*R* = (2/*M*) *Q_μ* [1 − *h*(*E*^Z_μ_) − *h*(*E*^X_μ_)]

where *Q_μ* is the signal gain and *E*^Z, *E*^X bound bit and phase errors via decoy states. Ma *et al.* also introduced *phase post-compensation* — estimating the drifting offset from bright reference pulses and correcting in post-processing instead of actively phase-locking the lasers — making the scheme feasible with current technology; a follow-up removed phase randomization and post-selection in the code mode entirely while keeping √*η* scaling [3].

**Sending-or-not-sending (Wang–Yu–Hu, 2018).** The SNS protocol takes the most radical approach: *no phase post-selection at all* [4]. Each round is a *signal* or *decoy* window. In a signal window, Alice sends |*α*⟩ (bit 1) with small probability *ε* or vacuum (bit 0); Bob likewise. Key bits come from windows where exactly one party sent — *untagged* bits — where no interference occurs and the random-phase problem never arises. Decoy windows use phase-randomized coherent states of several intensities, enabling standard decoy-state estimation of the single-photon yield *s*₁ and phase-flip error *e*₁^{ph}:

*R* = *s*₁ [1 − *h*(*e*₁^{ph})] − *n_t* *f* *h*(*E_t*)

where *n_t* counts survived bits after error rejection and *f* is the reconciliation efficiency. SNS tolerates ~15–20% misalignment — an order of magnitude beyond phase-encoding schemes — because interference quality affects only decoy windows, never the key-generating signal windows [4]; the AOPP (actively odd-parity pairing) refinement adds two-way parity checks before privacy amplification to further boost the rate [5].

**Decoy-state parameter estimation.** All variants rely on decoy states: randomly varying the mean photon number *μ* ∈ {*μ*, *ν*, *ω*} with phase randomization bounds each photon-number component's yield *Y_n* and error *e_n*. Only the single-photon component is secure against photon-number-splitting attacks, so the rate counts only *s*₁ untagged single-photon bits, with *e*₁^{ph} bounding Eve's information [3, 4, 9].

### 4.4 Finite-key composable security in the Renner framework

A 500 km experiment collects a *finite* block of *N* ~ 10¹² pulses; the modern standard is *composable ε-security* [10]:

> **Theorem (Composable security criterion):** A QKD protocol is *ε*-secure if the joint state *ρ*_KE_ of the final key *K* and Eve's system satisfies (1/2)‖*ρ*_KE_ − *τ_K* ⊗ *ρ_E*‖₁ ≤ *ε*, where *τ_K* is the maximally mixed state on the key space. With *ε* = *ε*_cor + *ε*_sec, an *ε*-secure key composes with any *ε*′-secure task into an (*ε* + *ε*′)-secure composite.

Xu *et al.* gave the first complete finite-key SNS rate against coherent attacks [5]:

ℓ ≤ *n*₁ [1 − *h*(*ē*₁^{ph})] − leak_EC − log₂(2/*ε*_cor) − 2log₂(1/2*ε̄*)

where *n*₁ lower-bounds the untagged single-photon bits (Chernoff bounds on decoy counts), *ē*₁^{ph} upper-bounds the phase-flip rate, leak_EC = *n_t f h*(*E_t*) is the reconciliation leakage, and the logarithms are the composability price (*ε*_cor-correctness, *ε̄*-secrecy). Positive key beyond 500 km survives with practical block sizes even at 20% misalignment [5]; the 1002 km demonstration applied full finite-key analysis to real data [8]. The chain — decoy estimation → untagged single-photon bits → smooth min-entropy → leftover hashing → trace-distance guarantee — is what makes TF-QKD cryptographically meaningful.

```python
# Rate-vs-distance sketch: PLOB (linear) vs TF-QKD (square-root) scaling
import numpy as np

def plob(eta):
    return -np.log2(1 - eta)          # repeaterless capacity, bits/use [1]

def tf_rate(eta, mu=0.5, ed=0.02, f=1.15):
    # toy SNS-like asymptotic rate: yield ~ mu*sqrt(eta), Shor-Preskill form
    Y = 1 - np.exp(-mu * np.sqrt(eta))      # single-arm survival
    h = lambda x: -x*np.log2(x) - (1-x)*np.log2(1-x) if 0 < x < 1 else 0.0
    return max(0.0, Y * (1 - h(ed)) - f * Y * h(ed))

for L in (200, 300, 400, 500, 600):
    eta = 10 ** (-0.02 * L)            # 0.2 dB/km standard fiber
    print(f"{L} km: PLOB={plob(eta):.2e}  TF-toy={tf_rate(eta):.2e}")
# 500 km: PLOB=2.08e-10  TF-toy=3.44e-06  -> ~4 orders of magnitude gap
```

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The theoretical √*η* scaling has been validated in a rapid experimental campaign:

| Year | Experiment | Distance | Protocol | Key result |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|------------|
| 2019 | First beyond-PLOB demos | ~300 km | TF-QKD variants | Rates exceed repeaterless bound [11] |
| 2020 | Chen *et al.* (USTC/Tsinghua) | **509 km** | SNS, independent lasers | Broke the *absolute* PLOB bound over fiber [6] |
| 2021 | Metropolitan deployed fiber | **511 km** | SNS | ~10³× the rate of prior field systems at that length [7] |
| 2021 | Field test | 428 km | SNS | Stable operation outside the lab [4]* |
| 2023 | Finite-key record | **1002 km** | SNS + AOPP | Full finite-key analysis; PLOB beaten at ≥404 km [8] |

*Reported in the SNS literature survey [4].

The 509 km experiment [6] is the conceptual landmark: with *independent* lasers at Alice and Bob, SNS distributed key above −log₂(1−*η*) computed with ideal detection — the *absolute* repeaterless limit, unreachable in principle by any point-to-point protocol. The 1002 km demonstration [8] is the cryptographic landmark: with 3.24×10¹² pulses per point it measured 111.74 kbps (202 km), 23.44 kbps (303 km), 2.80 kbps (404 km) and 338 bps (505 km), beating the absolute PLOB bound at every distance ≥404 km — *with finite-key bounds applied to real data*.

**Enabling technology: taming phase drift.** Single-photon interference needs relative phase stable to ~0.1 rad per block, while thermal/acoustic fiber noise scrambles phase on millisecond timescales. Three solutions emerged: (i) *time-multiplexed strong reference pulses* tracked with bright detectors; (ii) *optical phase-locked loops* disciplining both lasers to a reference disseminated over a service fiber — *coherent phase transfer* reaching *σ_φ* ≈ 0.13 rad (0.5% QBER) over 100 ms [12]; (iii) *post-compensation* estimating drift from reference data in software, no active locking needed [3]. TF-QKD *without optical frequency dissemination* [11] further cut infrastructure needs toward field deployment.

## 6 Limitations

TF-QKD's repeater-like scaling is real, but six limitations bound its significance.

**1. Phase stability remains the dominant engineering cost.** Every long-distance demonstration needs active phase tracking — reference pulses, phase-locked loops, or service-fiber frequency dissemination — coexisting with single-photon signals without added noise (Raman scattering from bright references is documented [12]); strictly harder than BB84/MDI-QKD stabilization.

**2. Finite-key penalties are severe at the frontier.** The √*η* advantage needs *N* ≳ 10¹² pulses — hours of uninterrupted, phase-locked operation. The 1002 km experiment's PLOB-beating 404 km point used 3.24×10¹² pulses [8]; shorter blocks fall back below the bound, pushing the practical beating distance far beyond the asymptotic prediction.

**3. The sources must be trusted and characterized.** TF-QKD is measurement-device-independent, but the *source* side is not device-independent: the security proofs assume phase-randomized coherent states with known intensity distributions. Intensity fluctuations, imperfect phase randomization, and Trojan-horse leakage (Eve injecting bright light into the modulators) demand active countermeasures — source monitoring, optical isolators — unmodeled in asymptotic proofs [4].

**4. Sifting and post-selection overhead.** PM-QKD pays the 2/*M* sifting factor; the original TF-QKD a similar post-selection cost. SNS avoids post-selection but pays in efficiency: only a small fraction *ε* of signal windows are "sending" events and untagged-bit extraction discards most detections — √*η* governs the exponent, but the prefactor remains punishing.

**5. Dark counts set a hard floor.** At 500+ km, signal click probability per pulse (~10⁻⁶–10⁻⁷) approaches the dark-count probability of the best superconducting nanowire detectors (~10⁻⁸–10⁻⁶). The 509 km and 1002 km results needed SNSPDs with >80% efficiency and ~10 Hz dark counts [6, 8]; further gains demand better detectors, not better protocols.

**6. It is still not a repeater network.** TF-QKD gives *single-repeater* scaling with one untrusted node — no entanglement distribution, no multi-hop √*η*-per-hop chains, none of the network primitives (teleportation, distributed computing) motivating true repeaters, whose chain capacities scale polynomially better [1]. It is a point solution for two-party key distribution, not a network architecture.

## 7 Conclusion

Pirandola *et al.* proved repeaterless rates scale linearly — a result that stands, unrefuted, for point-to-point channels [1]. Lucamarini *et al.* showed the setting was a choice: an untrusted central node with single-photon interference recovers √*η* scaling on present-day hardware [2]. PM-QKD and SNS turned a protocol with a phase-announcement loophole into a family with rigorous optical-mode and decoy-state proofs [3, 4, 9]. The Renner framework's finite-key analysis against coherent attacks made the claims cryptographically meaningful, not merely asymptotic [5, 10]. And fiber experiments — 509 km past the absolute bound, 511 km over deployed metropolitan fiber, 1002 km with finite-key analysis — proved the physics survives real, drifting, noisy fiber [6, 7, 8].

The methodological lesson: *fundamental limits are statements about a precisely specified resource model*, and progress often comes from identifying which assumption is negotiable rather than violating the limit. The PLOB bound assumed no intermediate node; TF-QKD negotiates exactly one, untrusted, memoryless node — and extracts the full √*η* scaling that concession allows. The open frontier is the *network* version of the same move: pushing multi-user networks toward their end-to-end capacities with minimal, untrusted, memoryless resources [1] — and making continental-scale phase coherence routine rather than heroic.
