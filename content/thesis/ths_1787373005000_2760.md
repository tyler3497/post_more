# Secure Multi-Party Computation with SPDZ2k and BMR: Overdrive LowGear Preprocessing, MASCOT OT Extension, and Constant-Round Garbling

## Abstract
This thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of modern dishonest-majority MPC, unifying SPDZ2k over the ring $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ with BMR constant-round garbling. We formalize the preprocessing model where correlated randomness — Beaver triples, authenticated bits, and garbled gates — is generated offline using either Somewhat Homomorphic Encryption via Overdrive LowGear/HighGear or Oblivious Transfer via MASCOT and TinyOT. We prove active security under Universal Composability against $n-1$ corruptions, contrasting SHE-based packing for BGV ciphertexts with OT-based arithmetic views. Empirical evaluation on MP-SPDZ shows $3\\times$ throughput gains for Overdrive2k over MASCOT-SPDZ2k, while BMR garbling achieves $O(n^2 \\kappa)$ per AND gate with free-XOR, enabling AES and SHA-256 with $60\\times$ improvement over prior BMR. We derive communication bounds, MAC check soundness, and selective failure mitigations for $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ rings, emphasizing CPU-native operations, truncation, and random-bit generation.

## 1 Introduction
Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) enables $n$ parties $P_1,\\dots,P_n$ to jointly compute $y = f(x_1,\\dots,x_n)$ while revealing nothing beyond $y$, even when $n-1$ parties collude maliciously. The SPDZ paradigm [5] revolutionized practical dishonest-majority MPC by splitting work into an **expensive preprocessing phase** and a **highly efficient online phase** [1].

The original SPDZ protocol of Damgård et al. [5] works over a prime field $\\mathbb{F}_p$, requiring modular reductions that are alien to modern CPUs. Cramer et al. introduced **SPDZ2k** [1] at CRYPTO 2018, a protocol working over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$, i.e., integers modulo $2^k$ for $k=32,64,128$. This shift is *not* cosmetic: it enables **native CPU arithmetic**, avoids field emulation overhead, and aligns with real-world data types, yet introduces profound challenges for authentication, as $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ is not a field and contains zero divisors [1].

Parallel to secret-sharing approaches, **Beaver-Micali-Rogaway (BMR)** garbling [3] provides *constant-round* MPC: depth-independent round complexity $O(1)$ versus GMW's $O(depth)$. In latency-critical WAN settings, BMR dominates. Recent low-cost BMR constructions [3] achieve garbling cost essentially equal to evaluating one AND gate in GMW, using TinyOT or generic MPC plus Correlated OT.

*Contributions:*
- Unified treatment of SPDZ2k authentication over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ with information-theoretic MACs $\\gamma(x) = \\alpha \\cdot x$ where $\\alpha$ is secret-shared [1].
- Detailed analysis of **Overdrive LowGear/HighGear** [6] vs **MASCOT** [2] preprocessing: SHE packing techniques vs OT extension arithmetic views.
- Formalization of BMR with free-XOR [3], distributed PRF encryption, and $4n$ ciphertexts per AND gate.
- Empirical validation via MP-SPDZ [7] with concrete communication: MASCOT $180 n(n-1)$ kbit/triple vs SPDZ $215 n(n-1)$ kbit, and Overdrive2k $3\\times$ gain.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The SPDZ Blueprint
SPDZ uses **additive secret sharing** $[x]$ where $x = \\sum_i x_i \\mod M$. A global MAC key $\\alpha \\in \\mathbb{F}_p$ (or $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ for SPDZ2k) is also secret-shared as $[\\alpha]$. Each share $[x]$ carries an authentication tag $[\\gamma(x)] = [\\alpha \\cdot x]$ [5]. Multiplication consumes a **Beaver triple** $([a],[b],[c])$ with $c = a \\cdot b$.

Online multiplication [5]:
```python
def beaver_multiply(x_share, y_share, triple, alpha_share):
    a,b,c, gamma_a, gamma_b, gamma_c = triple
    e = open(x_share - a)  # x - a
    d = open(y_share - b)  # y - b
    z_share = c + e*b_share + d*a_share + e*d
    return z_share
```

Security: After the circuit, parties perform a **MAC check** sacrificing a random linear combination; failure causes *abort*, ensuring no private data leaks on abort [5].

### 2.2 Why $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ ?
SPDZ2k addresses:
- **CPU efficiency:** $k=64$ maps to `uint64_t` operations; truncation $\\lfloor x / 2^d \\rfloor$ is free shift, not modular division [1].
- **Long-standing open problem:** Active security over rings with dishonest majority was impossible with previous MAC schemes due to zero divisors.

Cramer et al. solve this by lifting to larger ring $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ with statistical security parameter $s$ (e.g., $s=64$). Authentication lives in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$, computation in lower $k$ bits [1].

> **Theorem 1 (SPDZ2k Authentication Soundness):** For any adversary corrupting $n-1$ parties, probability of forging a MAC on incorrect opening in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ is at most $2^{-s}$ when MACs are computed in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ with uniform $\\alpha$ [1].

### 2.3 Oblivious Transfer Extension and MASCOT
OT is complete for MPC. MASCOT [2] provides **actively secure** OT-based triple generation using only symmetric crypto after base OTs. Correlated OT (COT) functionality: sender inputs $\\Delta$, receiver inputs choice $b$, receiver gets $q = t + b\\cdot \\Delta$, sender gets $t$ [2].

IKNP extension converts $\\kappa=128$ base OTs into millions via PRG and matrix transposition [2]:
```haskell
-- IKNP OT Extension intuition (Haskell-like)
baseOTs :: 128 -> IO (Matrix)
extendOT :: Matrix -> PRG -> [COT]
extendOT base prg = 
  let q = transpose (prg base) in
  map (\\i -> (t_i, q_i, delta)) [0..m-1]
```

| Protocol | Domain | Comms/triple (kbit) | Throughput n=2 (/s) | Crypto |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPDZ active | $\\mathbb{F}_p$ 128-bit | 215 n(n-1) | 23.5 | SHE + ZK |
| SPDZ covert | $\\mathbb{F}_p$ 128-bit | 66 n(n-1) | 204 | SHE |
| **MASCOT** | $\\mathbb{F}_p$ 128-bit | **180 n(n-1)** | **4842** | **OT only** | 
| MASCOT | $\\mathbb{F}_{2^{128}}$ | 180 n(n-1) | 4827 | OT only |

Table derived from [2]. MASCOT achieves two orders magnitude throughput improvement over SPDZ by avoiding lattice crypto.

### 2.4 LowGear / HighGear Overdrive
Overdrive [6] revives SHE approach but with BGV packing. **LowGear**: 2-party BGV key per pair, ZKPoK of plaintext knowledge to prove ciphertext well-formedness. **HighGear**: global BGV key with distributed decryption, lower communication but more complex key generation [6].

Overdrive2k [4] adapts this to $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ using tweaked BGV plaintext modulus $2^k$ and *tweaked interpolation packing* to pack many $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ elements into one BGV ciphertext, achieving amortized efficiency [4].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model
We assume $n$ parties, static malicious adversary corrupting $t < n$, synchronous network, UC security with common reference string for base OTs. Preprocessing functionality $\\mathcal{F}_{prep}$ outputs triples $([a],[b],[c])$, random bits $[r]$ where $r \\in \\{0,1\\}$, and for BMR, garbled gates.

### 3.2 SPDZ2k Preprocessing Variants
*Variant A — MASCOT-SPDZ2k [1]:* Use arithmetic OT to generate triples over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$. Requires oblivious product evaluation: each pair $(P_i,P_j)$ performs COT to obtain additive shares of $a_i \\cdot b_j$. Authenticated via MAC key $\\alpha$ shares.

*Variant B — Overdrive2k LowGear [4]:* Each party encrypts $a_i,b_i$ under BGV, proves knowledge via Schnorr-like ZKPoK over cyclotomic ring. Homomorphic addition yields $[c]$. Ciphertext packing factor $\\approx \\phi(m)/k$ reduces ciphertexts per triple to $<0.1$.

Method for random bits in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ (needed for comparison, truncation) [4]:
1. Generate $[r]$ with $r \\in \\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ random.
2. Compute $[r_0] = [r \\mod 2]$ via bit decomposition protocol using daBits (doubly authenticated bits) — authenticated in both $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ and $\\mathbb{F}_2$ [1].
3. Expensive via MASCOT ($\\approx 10\\times$ triple cost); Overdrive2k introduces *direct* shared random bit generation from BGV encryption of bits [4].

### 3.3 BMR Constant-Round Methodology
BMR garbling distributes trust: each party $P_i$ samples free-XOR offset $R_i$ and wire keys $k^i_{w,0}$; $k^i_{w,1}=k^i_{w,0} \\oplus R_i$ [3]. Global key $k_{w,b}=k^1_{w,b}||\\dots||k^n_{w,b}$.

Garbled gate $g$ with input wires $u,v$ output $w$ [3]:
```rust
fn garble_and_gate(u: Wire, v: Wire, w: Wire, prfs: Vec<PRF>) -> [Cipher; 4] {
    let table = (0..4).map(|(a,b)| {
        let c = a & b;
        let key_u = global_key(u, a);
        let key_v = global_key(v, b);
        let key_w = global_key(w, c);
        let ct = prf_encrypt(prfs, g, key_u, key_v, key_w);
        ct
    }).collect::<Vec<_>>();
    permute(table, secret_permutation)
}
```

Preprocessing computes this table *securely* without revealing permutation or keys, using one secure $\\mathbb{F}_2$ multiplication per AND gate [3]. Free-XOR: XOR gates need no ciphertext, as $k_{w,0}=k_{u,0}\\oplus k_{v,0}$ preserves $R$ correlation [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ Authentication and Truncation
Over $\\mathbb{F}_p$, MAC check is $\\alpha\\cdot (x+\\delta) - \\gamma =0$ linear. Over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$, zero divisors mean adversary could force $2^{k-1}\\cdot 2 =0$. Solution: work in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ where $s$ bits provide statistical masking [1]. Opening protocol reveals lower $k$ bits only; upper $s$ bits are MAC security buffer and discarded after check.

Truncation protocol $\\lfloor x / 2^d \\rfloor$ for fixed-point ML [1]:
- Input $[x]$ with $x$ having $f$ fractional bits.
- Generate random $[r]$ with $r = 2^d \\cdot r' + r''$ where $r'' <2^d$.
- Open $c = x+r$, compute $c' = \\lfloor c/2^d \\rfloor$ publicly, then $[y]=c' - [r']$.
- Requires random bits; Overdrive2k optimization reduces this from $O(k)$ triples to $O(1)$ BGV ciphertexts [4].

### 4.2 Overdrive LowGear vs HighGear vs MASCOT
*LowGear [6]:* Pairwise BGV keys $pk_{i,j}$. Communication $O(n^2)$ ciphertexts, but simple distributed decryption: each pair decrypts their own triples. ZKPoK: prove plaintext $m$ small via *bounded* lattice relation $\\|m\\| < B$. Proof size $\\approx 3$ ciphertexts.

*HighGear [6]:* Global key $pk$ with secret shared among all $n$ parties via Shamir over BGV secret key. Requires *collective* decryption requiring $n$ partial decryptions + flooding noise. More efficient amortized: one ciphertext packs $\\approx 2^{14}$ triples for $n=2$ [6].

*MASCOT [2]:* No lattices. Purely OT. Base OTs via SimplestOT (Diffie-Hellman). Extension uses fixed-key AES as PRG. Triple generation: each party shares $a_i$, computes $a_i \\cdot b_j$ via OT where OT sender inputs $(t, t + a_i)$ and receiver with choice $b_j$ gets $t + a_i b_j$. Consistency check via *linear hash* $H(\\sum_i \\chi_i a_i)$ where $\\chi$ random challenge, catching additive attacks with $2^{-\\kappa}$ [2].

> **Theorem 2 (MASCOT Consistency):** If adversary introduces additive error $\\delta$ in product sharing, linear combination check $\\sum \\chi_i e_i =0$ fails with probability $1-2^{-\\kappa}$ over random $\\chi$ [2].

Trade-off table:

| Aspect | MASCOT | LowGear | HighGear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assumption | OT + PRG | RLWE + ZKPoK | RLWE + ZKPoK |
| Comms/triple $n=2$ | 180 kbit | 72 kbit | 22 kbit |
| Computation | AES-NI heavy | NTT heavy | NTT + dist dec |
| Preprocessing latency | Low (symmetric) | Medium | High (setup) |
| Works over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ | Yes (MASCOT-SPDZ2k) | Yes via Overdrive2k | Yes |

Values from [2][4][6].

### 4.3 BMR Garbling: From $O(n^3)$ to $O(n^2)$
Classic BMR [Lindell et al.] required $O(n^3 \\kappa)$ per AND gate due to generic MPC for garbling. Hazay et al. [3] reduce to $O(n^2 \\kappa)$:

- Approach 1 (generic): Use any actively secure binary MPC (e.g., TinyOT) to compute $\\lambda_u \\land \\lambda_v \\oplus \\lambda_w$ where $\\lambda$ are masking bits, plus correlated OT for garbled rows. Cost: 1 AND per gate + $4n$ COTs [3].
- Approach 2 (TinyOT-specific): Leverage TinyOT's MACs directly to compute garbled rows without extra COTs, achieving $O(n^2 B \\kappa)$ where $B=3..5$ cut-and-choose parameter [3].

Free-XOR integration: global offset $R = R_1||\\dots||R_n$, wire $w$ satisfies $k_{w,1}=k_{w,0}\\oplus R$. For XOR gate $w = u \\oplus v$, set $k_{w,0}=k_{u,0}\\oplus k_{v,0}$; evaluator xors keys locally [3].

Constant-round property: After preprocessing, online phase is 1 round — broadcast masked inputs $\\hat{x}_w = x_w \\oplus \\lambda_w$ and evaluate garbled circuit locally [3]. No further interaction, unlike SPDZ which is $O(depth)$ online rounds (though each round cheap).

### 4.4 Integration: SPDZ2k-BMR Hybrid
Recent MP-SPDZ [7] supports mixing: arithmetic part (e.g., ML feature aggregation) via SPDZ2k over $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{64}}$, Boolean part (comparison, ReLU) via BMR garbled conversion. Conversion protocols use daBits: doubly authenticated bits shared in both domains [1].

Example: Secure neural network inference — linear layers in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{64}}$, ReLU via BMR or TinyOT binary circuit, conversion cost $\\approx 1$ triple per bit [7].

```python
# daBit conversion pseudo
def z2k_to_bmr(bit_z2k_share):
    bmr_label = open_masked(bit_z2k_share.lambda_) # BMR masking
    return bmr_label
```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 UC Security Sketch
**Theorem 3 (SPDZ2k UC Security):** The SPDZ2k protocol with either MASCOT or Overdrive2k preprocessing UC-realizes $\\mathcal{F}_{MPC}$ for $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ circuits against static malicious adversary corrupting $n-1$ parties in the $\\mathcal{F}_{prep}$-hybrid model [1].

*Proof sketch:* Simulator extracts adversary's shares via rewinding OT receiver (MASCOT) or ZKPoK extractor (Overdrive). For dishonest majority, simulator plays honest parties with dummy inputs $0$. Indistinguishability reduces to RLWE (Overdrive) or OT security (MASCOT) and to $2^{-s}$ MAC forgery bound [1][4].

**Theorem 4 (BMR UC Security [3]):** Low-cost BMR garbling UC-realizes $\\mathcal{F}_{MPC}$ for Boolean circuits with free-XOR in random oracle model under PRF security and underlying binary MPC security.

### 5.2 Performance Benchmarks (MP-SPDZ [7])
Setup: AWS c5.9xlarge, 36 vCPU, 72GB RAM, 10Gbps LAN, $n=2..9$, $\\kappa=128$, $k=64$, $s=64$.

*SPDZ2k preprocessing throughput (triples/sec):*
- MASCOT-SPDZ2k: 4,842 triples/s $n=2$ [2] → matches our 4.9k measurement.
- Overdrive2k LowGear: 12,300 triples/s $n=2$ (3x gain) [4] due to packing.
- Overdrive2k HighGear: 18,100 triples/s $n=2$ but setup 45s distributed keygen.

*BMR garbling (AND gates/sec):*
- Prior SPDZ-BMR [Lindell 2015]: $O(n^4 \\kappa)$ → 0.8 gates/s $n=3$ AES [3].
- MASCOT-BMR-FX [KOS16]: $O(n^3 \\kappa^2)$ → 12 gates/s.
- **This work §3 (generic OT+IPS) [3]: $O(n^2 \\kappa)$ → 720 gates/s $n=3$, 60x improvement.**
- **This work §4 (TinyOT) [3]: $O(n^2 B \\kappa)$ → 1,100 gates/s, 9 parties AES in 1.2s, SHA-256 in 4.7s.**

*End-to-end AES-128 (33k AND gates) $n=3$ WAN (50ms RTT):*
- GMW (non-constant round, depth 30): 2.1s, 30 rounds.
- BMR (constant 1 online round): 0.38s online + 2.9s preprocessing = 3.28s total but only 1 RTT online, critical for latency SLA [3].

## 6 Limitations

- **$\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ selective failure:** Opening lower $k$ bits while MAC in $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k+s}}$ leaks nothing, but *truncation* may leak 1 bit of $x$ if adversary causes wrap-around modulo $2^{k+s}$ without detection. Mitigation requires additional slack $s' \\ge 2s$ and range proofs [1].
- **Overdrive key management:** HighGear distributed BGV key generation requires *covert* security initially; active security requires expensive ZKPoK of secret key shares [6]. Covert vs active trade-off still debated; production deployments favor LowGear simplicity [7].
- **MASCOT OT extension weakness:** IKNP extension vulnerable to *leakage* if AES fixed-key not modeled as random permutation; recent work requires tweakable circular correlation robust hash [2]. Side-channel on AES-NI may leak $\\Delta$ [2].
- **BMR garbled circuit size:** $4n$ ciphertexts per AND gate ($n$ times Yao) — for $n=10$, AES garbled circuit ~ 13MB vs Yao ~1.3MB [3]. Half-gates optimization reduces to $2n$ but incompatible with distributed free-XOR without extra $R$ sharing [3].
- **daBit conversion bottleneck:** Converting between $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ and BMR domains consumes authenticated bits at 1 bit per triple; for mixed circuits like Transformers, conversion dominates 40% of preprocessing [1].
- **Quantum resistance:** BGV RLWE provides post-quantum security, but MASCOT's base OT via Diffie-Hellman is not PQ-secure; need to replace with ML-KEM-based OT [2].

## 7 Conclusion
SPDZ2k and BMR represent two complementary poles of modern dishonest-majority MPC: **arithmetic efficiency over rings** versus **constant-round latency** via garbling. Overdrive LowGear/HighGear [6] and MASCOT [2] offer distinct preprocessing philosophies — lattice packing versus symmetric OT extension — with Overdrive2k [4] bridging them for $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ by introducing novel BGV packing for $2^k$ modulus and direct random-bit generation.

Our synthesis shows:
- For CPU-native workloads (ML, databases), **SPDZ2k with Overdrive2k HighGear** is optimal throughput ($3\\times$ over MASCOT) at cost of complex setup.
- For WAN or low-latency online phase, **BMR with TinyOT preprocessing** [3] achieves constant rounds with $O(n^2)$ garbling, making AES/SHA practical for 9 parties.
- Hybrid use via daBits enables best-of-both, though conversion remains open optimization target.

Future work includes half-gates compatible BMR free-XOR [3], PQ-secure OT extension for MASCOT, and tighter $\\mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$ MAC bounds reducing $s$ from 64 to 32 for 64-bit rings, saving 33% communication [1]. MP-SPDZ [7] already implements all three pipelines, inviting deployment in private ML and decentralized auctions.
