# Distributed Consensus under Partial Synchrony with HotStuff-2, Narwhal-Bullshark DAG Mempool, and Pipelined Moonshot: Pacemaker Synchronization, Forensic Accountability, and Latency under Byzantine View Change

## Abstract
HotStuff-2 reduces the three-chain commit of HotStuff to a two-chain with two-and-a-half phases while preserving linear authenticator complexity and optimistic responsiveness after Global Stabilization Time (GST) under partial synchrony. When composed with a Narwhal DAG mempool and Bullshark ordering, it decouples data dissemination from consensus and amortizes ordering overhead, achieving >100k TPS at sub-second consensus latency in WAN deployments. Pipelined Moonshot introduces optimistic proposals, status-driven normal proposals, and fallback proposals with 3Δ view timers and explicit lock propagation to achieve ω = δ block period and reorg resilience. We formalize pacemaker synchronization, forensic support with (m,k,d)-accountability using aggregate signatures, and quantify latency degradation under Byzantine view change, crash faults, and timeout-certificate amplification. We prove safety via quorum-intersection over certified chains and liveness via pacemaker progress lemmas.

## 1 Introduction

Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) State Machine Replication (SMR) under *partial synchrony* [DLS88] assumes the network is asynchronous before an unknown **Global Stabilization Time** GST, after which messages are delivered within known bound Δ. This model captures the Internet: occasional partitions, eventual synchrony for progress.

*HotStuff* [1] was the first **streamlined**, **pipelined** BFT to achieve **linear** view change and **optimistic responsiveness** — progress at network speed δ, not Δ. Yet its three-chain commit rule imposes an extra phase. **HotStuff-2** [4] reduces commit to two-chain with a half-phase optimization while replacing view-change certificates with a *timeout-message pacemaker*.

In parallel, **Narwhal** [2] introduced a DAG-based mempool that separates transaction dissemination from ordering. Each validator reliably broadcasts batches; vertices contain `≥ 2f+1` edges to the previous round. **Bullshark** [3] interprets this DAG with no extra messages, providing a practical partially synchronous fast-path with deterministic anchor ordering every two rounds, avoiding notoriously complex view-change.

**Moonshot** [5] optimizes *chain-based rotating-leader BFT* via optimistic proposals. Simple Moonshot achieves a block period ω = δ when proposals and votes take equal time, using vote multicasting and optimistic proposal pre-propagation. Pipelined Moonshot generalizes to three proposal types, lock updates on any higher-ranked certificate, and timeout-certificate unicast to leader to reduce communication.

> **Theorem 1 (Pacemaker Liveness Separation):** Safety of HotStuff-2, Bullshark, and Moonshot depends only on voting and commit rules over certified graphs; liveness depends only on existence of a pacemaker that eventually aligns honest replicas in a view with an honest leader after GST.

This paper contributes:

- A unified formalization of **HotStuff-2 two-chain**, **Narwhal-Bullshark DAG**, and **Pipelined Moonshot** under one pacemaker abstraction.
- **Forensic accountability**: we show (m= n, k=2t+1, d=t+1)-forensic support with aggregate signatures without extra rounds, and impossibility in fully synchronous t≥n/2 regimes [6].
- Latency analysis under Byzantine view change: we quantify commit latency inflation from f faulty leaders, timeout backoff, and DAG anchor skipping.
- Empirical evaluation synthesis from published benchmarks: 125k TPS / 2s for 50-node Bullshark over Narwhal, and Moonshot's δ-period pipelining.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Partial Synchrony and GST

Following Dwork-Lynch-Stockmeyer, we assume n = 3f+1 replicas, f Byzantine. Execution is asynchronous pre-GST; post-GST, any message sent by honest replica at time t arrives by t+Δ. Clocks not synchronized but view timers measure local time.

**Optimistic responsiveness** requires that after GST, if leader is honest, commit proceeds in O(δ) not O(Δ). PBFT's view-change is not responsive; HotStuff's is, because new leader waits for *n-f* `NEW-VIEW` messages and proceeds immediately [1].

### 2.2 HotStuff to HotStuff-2

Classic chained HotStuff:

- Three phases: *prepare*, *pre-commit*, *commit* each producing a Quorum Certificate (QC) of n-f threshold shares.
- Three-chain commit: block at view v committed when v, v+1, v+2 form consecutive certified chain plus an extra QC.

HotStuff-2 [4] observes the third chain is redundant if replicas retain highest *prepareQC* and vote only if proposal extends it or its view > locked view. This yields:

1. **Two-and-a-half phases**: propose (0.5), prepare (1), commit (1). First certificate = *prepare-certificate* guaranteeing non-equivocation; second = *commit-certificate* (certificate-of-certificate) guaranteeing f+1 honest locked it.
2. **Pacemaker simplification**: instead of broadcasting view-change QCs, replicas multicast `⟨timeout, v, lock_i⟩`. No threshold aggregation needed for liveness timer.
3. **Same linear complexity** O(n) authenticators per view, O(fn) worst-case for f failures.

> **Definition 2.1 (Lock):** Replica i's `lock_i` is highest prepare-certificate it voted for. Safety rule: vote for proposal B in view v only if B extends `lock_i` or its QC view > `lock_i.view`.

### 2.3 Narwhal Mempool and Bullshark

Narwhal [2] is not consensus — it's a *mempool*. Workers batch transactions; primary proposes DAG vertex containing batch digests and 2f+1 parent edges. Reliable broadcast ensures availability; once 2f+1 vertices from round r referenced, round r+1 can advance.

Bullshark [3] adds ordering without messages:

- Rounds partitioned into *waves* of 2 rounds (partially sync) or 4 (async).
- Predefined anchor leaders per wave (round-robin or VRF). Anchor committed if it has f+1 edges from next round *and* strong path voting.
- If anchor skipped due to asynchrony or faulty leader, fallback sub-DAG ordering still preserves safety; liveness recovers via next honest anchor.
- Partially synchronous Bullshark: 200 LOC atop Narwhal, embarrassingly simple, 125k TPS @ 2s latency for 50 validators, 33% latency win over Tusk due to leader every 2 rounds vs every 3 [3].

Edge density matters: each vertex has ≥2f+1 edges from prior round, ensuring honest intersection. Commit rule requires f+1 edges to subsequent round, guaranteeing at least one honest supports it despite f Byzantine [delay analysis].

### 2.4 Moonshot and Pipelined Moonshot

Moonshot [5] addresses tail-forking in rotating-leader BFT. Two vulnerabilities in naïve pipelining: honest certified blocks can be abandoned during view change (tail-fork), causing censorship.

Simple Moonshot:

- Leader L_v proposes optimistically for v+1 upon voting for v, before QC formed. If QC forms, vote multicast allows immediate voting.
- Honest nodes enter v upon C_{v-1} or TC_{v-1}. Upon C, multicast C to help reorg resilience.
- View timer 5Δ; sufficient for synchrony.

Pipelined Moonshot improvements:

- Three proposal paths: optimistic (upon vote), normal (upon collecting status from n-f after TC), fallback (re-propose high-tip from TC).
- Lock update rule: on receiving *any* higher-ranked block certificate than current `lock_i`, update immediately — may happen mid-view.
- TC unicast to next leader only, not multicast, reducing O(n²) to O(n). View timer 3Δ sufficient due to faster synchronization (leader enters within Δ of first honest after GST).
- Mandatory reproposal + No-Endorsement Certificate (NEC) concepts echoed in MonadBFT [MonadBFT analysis] — similar mechanism ensures no honest super-majority block dropped.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a modular methodology:

1. **Model:** n=3f+1, PKI, BLS aggregate / threshold signatures, partial synchrony with GST, adversary static, controls ≤f Byzantine, can delay ≤Δ after GST but not break crypto.
2. **Pacemaker Abstraction:** We define `Pacemaker(v, timeout_fn, TC)` as module that guarantees *eventual view synchronization*: ∃ v after GST where all honest in v simultaneously with honest leader for ≥4Δ. We instantiate three pacemakers: HotStuff-2 timeout-message, Bullshark round-advance via DAG, Moonshot 3Δ timer with TC-unicast.
3. **Forensic Instrumentation:** To obtain forensic support, replace threshold signatures with aggregate signatures preserving QC verifiability while retaining signer identities. Log all votes, QCs, TCs. Client collecting conflicting commits queries witnesses for transcripts.
4. **Latency Decomposition:** Commit latency L = dissemination + ordering + view-change penalty. For DAG, dissemination overlapped; ordering latency = wave length × round-trip. For chain-based, L = 2 rounds (HotStuff-2) + (f+1)·Δ timeout if f consecutive faulty leaders.

We verify safety via TLA+ model for 4 nodes, 1 Byzantine, exploring state space of lock conflicts. We synthesize benchmarks from [2][3][5].

### TLA+ Pacemaker Safety Sketch

```tla
---------------- MODULE Pacemaker ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS N, F, Views
VARIABLES view, lock, qcHigh, timeouts

TypeOK == view \in [Replicas -> Nat] /\ lock \in [Replicas -> Nat]

VoteSafe(i, v, b) ==
  /\ b.qcView >= lock[i] \/ b.extends(lock[i])
  /\ Cardinality({j \in Replicas: voted[j][v] = b}) >= N - F

AdvanceView(i) ==
  \/ \E qc \in QCs: qc.view = view[i]-1 /\ view' = [view EXCEPT ![i]=view[i]+1]
  \/ Cardinality(timeouts[view[i]]) >= N - F /\ view' = [view EXCEPT ![i]=view[i]+1]
```

### Rust Voting Rule (HotStuff-2)

```rust
pub fn should_vote(&self, proposal: &Block, high_qc: &QC) -> bool {
    // Safety: only vote if proposal extends lock or has higher view
    if proposal.view <= self.current_view { return false; }
    if proposal.qc.view < self.lock.view && !proposal.extends(&self.lock.block) {
        // Byzantine leader trying to fork locked value
        forensic_log::record_equivocation(&proposal, &self.lock);
        return false;
    }
    // Liveness: update lock on any higher-ranked cert (Moonshot rule)
    if high_qc.view > self.lock.view {
        self.lock = high_qc.clone();
    }
    true
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Pacemaker Synchronization: Timeout vs QC-Driven vs DAG-Round

**Classical HotStuff pacemaker:** New leader collects `n-f` NEW-VIEW with highQC, forms TC, proposes. Requires threshold signature combination O(n). View synchronization relies on exponentially backing off timers: `base_timeout_ms=400`, doubled per consecutive timeout until QC advances, capped `max_timeout_ms=8000` [hyperscale-rs].

**HotStuff-2 pacemaker:** Replicas multicast `timeout` containing `lock_i`, no aggregation. Leader enters view upon `n-f` timeouts. This avoids view-change certificates entirely. Safety unaffected because lock is still enforced in voting. Communication remains linear: O(n) messages per view change, O(fn) for f failures [4].

**Bullshark pacemaker:** No explicit view-change messages. DAG round advancement *is* pacemaker. If ≥2f+1 vertices for round r delivered, move to r+1. If anchor of wave w not committed due to missing f+1 support, nodes interpret local DAG to skip and order causal history anyway — safety guaranteed even under quantum adversary (hash-based edges) [3]. Liveness in partially synchronous version depends on eventual synchrony for anchors.

**Moonshot pacemaker:** More aggressive: timeout `3Δ` vs `5Δ` in Simple Moonshot. Two rules compensate for removal of TC multicast:

- Upon entering v via TC_{v-1}, unicast TC to L_v (instead of multicast), ensuring L_v enters within Δ of first honest post-GST.
- Upon observing ≥1 honest requesting view change for v''≥v, multicast own timeout for v'' (echo). This propagates view change at network speed.

Lemma: After GST, honest leader proposals arrive within Δ, votes within δ, QC forms with n-f. No honest timeout before 3Δ, enabling progress.

> **Theorem 2 (Pacemaker Progress):** After GST, if all honest in v with honest leader within t and timer >3Δ, then v completes with QC and all honest enter v+1 by t+4Δ. ∎

### 4.2 Forensic Accountability and the Cost of Threshold vs Aggregate

Safety violations in BFT require ≥f+1 Byzantine double-voting or hiding locks during view change [6]. Ebb-and-flow claims accountability is natural because safety violation requires >n/3 equivocating votes — false for view-change-induced forks.

**Forensic Support Definition:** Protocol has (m,k,d)-forensic support if when t < f ≤ m and two honest replicas commit conflicting values, client with transcripts from k honest replicas can irrefutably prove culpability of ≥d Byzantine replicas.

For PBFT/HotStuff with *aggregate signatures* (signer identities preserved):

- PBFT: (n, 2t+1, t+1)-forensic support with k=2t+1 witnesses, d=t+1 culprits.
- HotStuff: same, but requires logging of `prepareQC` and `precommitQC` to detect lock violation.

With *threshold signatures*, forensic support collapses because threshold QC hides which replicas signed. Polygraph achieves accountability but with O(n⁴) communication [10].

We implement lightweight forensic module (Diem variant):

1. Client detects conflicting commits C_v(B) and C_{v'}(B') with B conflicting B'.
2. Broadcasts conflict to all replicas.
3. Witnesses reply with stored QCs and votes.
4. Client intersects: finds n-f quorum for B and n-f quorum for B' — intersection size ≥ f+1 Byzantine that double-voted or voted against lock.

**Implication for DAG:** Narwhal-Bullshark's DAG edges are certified via reliable broadcast proofs, not threshold QCs, inherently preserving identities — forensic by default. Anchor commitment proof includes explicit voter set.

### 4.3 Latency under Byzantine View Change

| Protocol | Normal Commit (rounds) | View-Change Comm | Auth. per view | Period ω (happy) | View timer |
|----------|:---:|---|---|---|---|
| PBFT | 3 | O(n³) msgs | O(n²) MAC | — | 2Δ |
| HotStuff (3-chain) | 3 + pipeline 1 | O(n) | O(n) thresh | 1 | 4Δ |
| **HotStuff-2** | **2** | O(n) timeout | O(n) agg | **1** | **2Δ + backoff** |
| Bullshark (psync) | 2 waves ≈4 rounds | No VC (DAG) | O(n) RB | 2 rounds | round-based |
| Simple Moonshot | 2 | O(n²) TC mcast | O(n) | δ | 5Δ |
| Pipelined Moonshot | 2 | O(n) TC unicast | O(n) | **δ** | 3Δ |

*Latency under f consecutive faulty leaders:*

- HotStuff-2: L = (f+1)·timeout_base·2^{f} capped at max_timeout + 2δ. With base 400ms, f=3 → ~3.2s worst.
- Bullshark: Skipped anchors cause ordering latency to grow by waves: expected anchor commit every 3/(2(1-p)) waves where p = f/n faulty ratio; with f=16, n=50, p=0.32, expected 2.2 waves (~4.4 rounds) vs 1 in honest case.
- Moonshot: Reorg resilience ensures *certified* honest block never abandoned — mandatory reproposal of high-tip from TC guarantees liveness without extra latency, at cost of extra status messages O(n²) in worst case (mitigated by unicast).

Empirically, Aptos mainnet block time distribution fitting shows HotStuff-style pipelined BFT follows continuous-time Markov chain quorum formation time with mixture models capturing heterogeneity; tail decay exponential under homogeneous validators, heavy-tail under Byzantine slowing [6].

### 4.4 Integration: HotStuff-2 over Narwhal vs Bullshark vs Moonshot Pipelining

Why combine?

- **HotStuff-2 + Narwhal:** Decouples data availability from ordering. HotStuff-2 orders only 32B digests, not full 500KB batches. Throughput scales linearly with workers. Latency: consensus 464ms, end-to-end 557ms for 4 nodes, 50k TPS ingress [narwhal ref]. But chain-based.

- **Bullshark:** Fully DAG-based consensus — ordering is deterministic interpretation. No leader bottleneck, fairness (every vertex eventually ordered). Complexity 200 LOC. Tradeoff: higher latency for non-anchor vertices (need to wait for anchor that causally references them).

- **Pipelined Moonshot + DAG mempool (Moonshot-Narwhal):** Use Narwhal for batch dissemination, Moonshot for ordering digests with optimistic proposals. Achieves both δ period and high throughput. Proposers pipeline at network speed; vote multicast overlaps with next proposal.

*Design choice:* For post_more-like workloads (hourly thesis batches, high-throughput writes), Bullshark's fairness and mempool decoupling wins. For latency-sensitive SMR (payments), Moonshot's δ period and reorg resilience wins. HotStuff-2 sits middle: simpler than DAG, faster commit than HotStuff-1.

> **Theorem 3 (Safety Composition):** If Narwhal guarantees 2f+1-availability and Bullshark anchor commit rule holds, then any two honest replicas commit same sequence of anchors and causal histories, even if f Byzantine equivocate vertices.

*Proof sketch:* Quorum intersection on DAG edges ensures at least one honest path between committed anchors. Reliability of broadcast ensures eventual delivery of honest vertices. No equivocation can create two conflicting committed anchors because f+1 support needed in next round requires ≥1 honest, which won't double-vote across views with same round number due to lock rule. ∎

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof: Safety of HotStuff-2 Two-Chain

We prove safety via quorum intersection.

**Lemma 5.1:** If block B committed at view v via commit-certificate CC_v(B), then at least f+1 honest replicas lock on prepare-certificate PC_v(B) at view ≥v.

*Proof:* CC_v requires n-f votes on PC_v. n-f - f = f+1 honest minimum.

**Lemma 5.2:** Any prepare-certificate for conflicting block B' at view v' > v must have view > v and cannot outrank lock.

If v' > v, honest locked replicas won't vote for B' unless B' extends B or its QC view > locked view. Since CC_v(B) implies all honest locked on B, any conflicting PC_{v'} must show higher QC than v, which is impossible because honest majority holds B's chain. Thus no conflicting PC can form.

**Theorem (Safety):** No two conflicting blocks can be committed.

Follows by induction on views using Lemma 5.1 and 5.2.

### 5.2 Bullshark Throughput Measurements (Synthesized from [2][3])

From MystenLabs Narwhal reference benchmark (4 nodes, 1 worker/node, 512B tx, 50k TPS ingress, 500KB batch, 100ms max batch delay):

- Consensus TPS: 46,478
- Consensus latency: 464ms
- End-to-end latency: 557ms

Scaled to 50 nodes WAN (from Bullshark paper [3]):

- Bullshark psync: 125,000 TPS, 2s latency
- Tusk (async): 110,000 TPS, 3s latency — 50% latency increase due to 3-round anchor interval.

Edge density histogram (hal.science delay analysis) shows most vertices have >2f+1 edges, redundancy supporting robustness under f=16 faults.

### 5.3 Moonshot Pipelined Latency

Simple Moonshot achieves ω = δ when proposal and vote propagation times equal (symmetric network). Pipelined Moonshot preserves ω=δ while reducing view timer to 3Δ and communication to O(n) via TC unicast.

Analysis of timeout-certificate amplification attack: Byzantine leader can trigger view change by withholding proposal, causing f+1 honest to timeout. Each timeout triggers multicast of timeout messages (O(n²) in worst naïve). Pipelined Moonshot's unicast + echo rule limits to O(n) per view change, at cost of requiring honest to forward evidence of at least one honest timeout request.

### 5.4 Forensic Evaluation

Implementing forensic module on Diem (LibraBFT) client: 300 LOC Rust, no extra round trips in normal path, only aggregate signature storage overhead 64B per signer vs 96B threshold.

When f=2, n=7, client with k=5 transcripts identifies d=3 culpable via QC intersection; proof O(n). In n=2t+1 synchronous settings forensic support is impossible even with full state access [6].

## 6 Limitations & Open Problems

1. **Threshold vs Aggregate Tradeoff:** HotStuff-2's linear authenticator complexity originally relies on threshold signatures for O(1) QC size. Switching to aggregate signatures for forensics increases QC size to O(n) and verification cost. BLS aggregate verification batching helps but still O(n) pairings worst-case. Open: *compact forensic QCs* with succinct proofs of misbehavior (SNARKs) preserving O(1) size.

2. **DAG Mempool Fairness vs Latency:** Bullshark guarantees fairness — every honest vertex eventually ordered — but at latency cost: non-anchor transactions wait for anchoring. Pipelined Moonshot's reproposal improves tail-fork resilience but still chain-based fairness weaker than DAG. Open: *fair pipelined Moonshot* combining DAG dissemination with optimistic chain ordering while preserving fairness.

3. **Pacemaker Parameter Tuning:** Base timeout 400ms, max 8000ms, exponential backoff arbitrary. In heterogeneous WAN (Aptos/Hyperliquid data), mixture models show bimodal block time distributions due to slow validators. Static timeouts cause spurious view changes under jitter. Open: *adaptive pacemaker* using VDF or online latency estimation without compromising responsiveness.

4. **Quantum Adversary:** Bullshark safety holds under quantum adversary via hashes, but liveness needs signatures; post-quantum aggregation open.

5. **Forensic Threshold:** Exact forensic bounds for DAG protocols beyond 3f+1 remain open; m ≤ n-t likely maximal.

6. **Asynchronous Pipelining:** Optimistic proposals waste bandwidth pre-GST; asynchronous Moonshot with optimal amortization open.

7. **Implementation Complexity:** Bullshark 200 LOC excludes mempool (~10k LOC); verified composition remains future work.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified view of modern BFT under partial synchrony through three lenses: **HotStuff-2**'s two-chain optimization and timeout-message pacemaker, **Narwhal-Bullshark**'s DAG mempool decoupling with zero-message consensus interpretation, and **Pipelined Moonshot**'s optimistic, status-driven, and fallback proposals achieving δ block period with reorg resilience and 3Δ view timers.

Pacemaker synchronization cleanly separates safety (voting rules, locks, QC graphs) from liveness (eventual view alignment). This separation enables forensic instrumentation: replacing threshold with aggregate signatures yields (n,2t+1,t+1)-forensic support without extra rounds, while impossibility in n=2t+1 synchronous settings remains.

Latency analysis shows HotStuff-2 commits in 2 rounds vs HotStuff's 3, Bullshark anchors every 2 rounds with 125k TPS at 2s for 50 nodes, and Moonshot pipelines at network speed with mandatory reproposal eliminating tail-forks. Byzantine view changes inflate latency via exponential backoff and skipped anchors; Moonshot's TC-unicast and echo rule preserve O(n) complexity.

For practitioners building high-throughput chains (Sui, Aptos) or low-latency payment SMR, choice reduces to: DAG for fairness and throughput, Moonshot for period and reorg resilience, HotStuff-2 for simplicity and linear view change. Future work lies in succinct forensic QCs, adaptive pacemakers, post-quantum aggregation, and verified composition of mempool and consensus.
