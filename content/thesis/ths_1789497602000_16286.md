# Univalent Foundations: the Univalence Axiom, Higher Inductive Types, and Synthetic Homotopy Theory in Dependent Type Theory

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory reinterprets Martin-Löf dependent type theory through homotopy theory: types are spaces, terms are points, and proofs of identity are paths. Two additions turn this reading into a new foundation of mathematics. Voevodsky's *univalence axiom* identifies the identity type of the universe with the type of equivalences, so equivalent types are equal and every construction is automatically invariant under equivalence. *Higher inductive types* extend inductive definitions with constructors for paths, so spaces such as the circle, spheres, suspensions, and quotients are declared directly as logical data. Together they support *synthetic homotopy theory*: machine-checked calculations of homotopy groups, fibrations, and cohomology inside type theory, with no recourse to point-set topology. This article gives a precise account of univalence, the induction principles of higher inductive types, the encode-decode proof that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers, and the cubical models that restore computational content to univalence. We survey simplicial and cubical semantics, the formalization record, and principal open problems: canonicity for axiomatic univalence, general schemas for higher inductive types, and the limits univalence places on classical principles.

##1 Intro

Foundations of mathematics are judged twice: once by what they can express, and once by what they forbid you from saying. Classical set theory expresses almost everything but permits distinctions mathematicians consider meaningless — the difference between two isomorphic groups or two constructions of the real numbers. Homotopy type theory begins from the observation, due to Hofmann and Streicher and developed by Awodey, Warren, and Voevodsky, that intensional Martin-Löf type theory already *has* a notion of sameness with the right shape: the identity type, whose inhabitants behave like paths in a space [2].

The *univalence axiom* turns this observation into a principle: for types A and B in a universe U, the canonical map from identifications A = B to equivalences A ≃ B is itself an equivalence. Isomorphic structures are identified, and every construction in the theory is automatically invariant under equivalence — the *equivalence principle* or *structure identity principle* that set theory can only approximate with effort [1]. *Higher inductive types* (HITs) complete the picture by letting us *generate* spaces: we declare point constructors and path constructors together, and the induction principle tells us how to map out of the resulting type. The circle is no longer constructed as a quotient of the real line; it is *declared*: one point, one loop [3].

These ideas enable *synthetic homotopy theory*, in which spaces are primitive notions governed by logical rules, much as Euclid's geometry takes points and lines as primitive rather than as sets of coordinates. The payoff is concrete: fully formalized, machine-checked proofs that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers, the synthetic Hopf fibration, and the Freudenthal suspension theorem [1, 3]. And the cubical models of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg show that univalence need not be an axiom at all: it can be *proved*, with computational content, in a constructive type theory [4, 5].

This article develops the theory in four movements. Section 2 recalls the homotopical reading of identity types and the stratification of types by homotopy level. Section 3 states the univalence axiom precisely and derives its first consequences. Section 4 dives into univalence in detail, the general theory of higher inductive types, the synthetic calculation of homotopy groups, and the cubical realization that gives univalence computational meaning. Section 5 records proof sketches and the formalization record. Section 6 surveys limitations and open problems. Section 7 concludes with an assessment of univalent foundations as a working foundation for mathematics.

##2 Background

### Identity types and their homotopical reading

Martin-Löf's dependent type theory is built from dependent function types Π(x:A). B(x), dependent pair types Σ(x:A). B(x), inductive types such as the natural numbers, a hierarchy of universes U₀ : U₁ : …, and the *identity type*. For a type A and terms x, y : A, the type x =_A y (written Id_A(x,y)) is generated by a single constructor refl_x : x = x, and eliminated by *path induction* (the J rule): to prove something about an arbitrary identification p : x = y, it suffices to consider the case where y is x and p is refl_x [1, 2].

Two facts make this type remarkable. First, it internalizes equality as *data*: there can be many distinct proofs of x = y. Hofmann and Streicher's groupoid model showed that "all proofs of x = y are equal" — uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP), or Streicher's axiom K — is *independent*: it holds in the set-theoretic reading but fails in the groupoid reading, where identity proofs are morphisms that need not be unique [2]. Second, the identity type supports the full algebra of paths: composition, inverses, associativity up to higher paths, and paths between paths behaving like homotopies.

### Homotopy levels and truncation

Not every type has interesting higher structure. A type is *contractible* if it has a point to which every point is uniquely identified; a *mere proposition* if any two of its inhabitants are identified (it carries at most truth-value information); a *set* if its identity types are mere propositions; a *1-groupoid* if its identity types are sets; and so on. These are the *homotopy levels* or *h-levels* (−2), (−1), 0, 1, …. Every type A has an *n-truncation* ||A||_n, the universal n-type receiving a map from A, which kills all homotopy above level n. Truncation is itself a higher inductive type. The fundamental group of a pointed type (A, a) is then defined *internally* as π₁(A, a) := ||a =_A a||_0, the set-truncation of the loop space — a definition that makes sense for any type, not just topological spaces [1].

Universes are homotopically rich: Kraus and Sattler showed that the n-th universe U_n is not n-truncated, so the hierarchy of universes genuinely ascends through the homotopy levels [8]. This richness is exactly what univalence exploits.

##3 Methodology

### Equivalences

A map f : A → B is an *equivalence* if every fiber is contractible:

```haskell
-- Pseudocode in the style of the HoTT book
isequiv : (A -> B) -> Type
isequiv f = forall (y : B). isContr (Sigma (x : A). f x = y)

Equiv : Type -> Type -> Type
Equiv A B = Sigma (f : A -> B). isequiv f
```

Write A ≃ B for the type of equivalences. By path induction there is a canonical map

```
idtoequiv : (A =_U B) -> (A ≃ B)
```

sending refl_A to the identity equivalence. In plain Martin-Löf type theory this map is not known to be invertible; indeed, in the set-theoretic model it fails to be surjective in the intended homotopical sense.

### The univalence axiom

> **Theorem (Univalence, Voevodsky):** For a universe U and types A, B : U, the canonical map idtoequiv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B) is an equivalence. Equivalently, (A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B).

The inverse is traditionally denoted ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B). Univalence is *postulated* as an axiom in the HoTT book [1]; it is *provable* in cubical type theory [4]. Its immediate consequences reshape formalized mathematics:

- **Function extensionality.** If f, g : Π(x:A). B(x) agree pointwise, they are equal: funext is *derivable* from univalence, not assumed.
- **Propositional extensionality.** Logically equivalent mere propositions are equal.
- **The structure identity principle.** An isomorphism of groups induces an *identification* of groups; hence any property or structure expressed in the theory transports automatically along isomorphisms. Mathematicians' informal "up to isomorphism" becomes a theorem [1, 2].
- **Transport along equivalences.** Given e : A ≃ B and a family P : U → Type, we obtain transport^P (ua e) : P(A) → P(B): constructions depending on a type can be moved across equivalences.

The method of this article is to take these principles as the working logic and to develop homotopy theory *inside* it, verifying the key theorems against their classical statements and against machine-checked formalizations.

##4 Deep Dive

### The Univalence Axiom, Precisely

Univalence asserts more than a bijection between identifications and equivalences: it asserts the map idtoequiv is an equivalence, so univalence is *self-coherent*. Define ua := (idtoequiv)⁻¹. In the HoTT book the computation rule for transport along ua(e) holds only propositionally, not definitionally — the *computation problem* of axiomatic univalence. Cubical type theory repairs exactly this: there, ua computes [4, 7].

> **Theorem (Function extensionality from univalence):** Let f, g : Π(x:A). B(x) with h : Π(x:A). f(x) = g(x). Then f = g.

*Proof sketch.* The standard argument (HoTT book, Section 4.9) shows Σ(g : Π(x:A). B(x)). Π(x:A). f(x) = g(x) is contractible, using univalence to prove that a family of equivalences induces an equivalence on Π-types. ∎

A subtle point: univalence is *anti-UIP*. If every type were a set, then A =_U B would be a proposition, contradicting the nontrivial automorphisms of, say, the two-element type. Univalence therefore *requires* the higher structure of identity types; it is incompatible with axiom K and with "every type is a set." It is, however, compatible with excluded middle — the simplicial-sets model is classical and validates univalence — while *global* choice, a uniform choice of point in every inhabited type, is inconsistent with it.

### Higher Inductive Types: Spaces as Data

An ordinary inductive type is generated by *point constructors*: zero and successor generate ℕ. A *higher inductive type* additionally has *path constructors* — terms of identity types — and the induction principle must explain how to map out of the type by specifying the action on all constructors, including the higher ones.

The *circle* is the archetype:

```
HIT S¹ :=
  | base : S¹
  | loop : base = base
```

Its induction principle: given P : S¹ → Type, b : P(base), and l : transport^P loop b = b, there is f : Π(x:S¹).

The menagerie of HITs used in synthetic homotopy theory includes [1, 5]:

- **The interval** I with endpoints and a path between them (primarily in cubical settings).
- **Suspensions** ΣA with poles north, south : ΣA and meridians merid : Π(a:A). north = south. Iterating from the two-point type yields the spheres: S⁰ := 2, S^{n+1} := ΣS^n.
- **Pushouts** of spans B ← A → C, generalizing quotients, mapping cones, and joins.
- **Set quotients** A/R for an equivalence relation R.
- **Propositional truncation** ||A|| with |−| : A → ||A|| and a path constructor forcing any two points equal.
- **n-truncations** ||A||_n, the n-type reflection of any type.

The induction principles of HITs are their *universal properties*: S¹ is the free type on a loop, ||A|| the free proposition on A.

A compact comparison of the three semantic settings is useful:

| Feature | Book HoTT + UA (axiom) | Cubical type theory | Simplicial sets model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Univalence | Postulated axiom | Provable theorem [4] | Validated semantically |
| Computation of ua | Propositional only | Definitional (via Glue) [4, 7] | N/A (classical model) |
| HIT path computation rules | Propositional | Definitional [5] | Modeled via cell attachments |
| Metatheory | Constructive + axioms | Fully constructive | Classical (uses AC/LEM) |
| Canonicity for ℕ | Open (homotopy canonicity) | Proved (Huber) | N/A |
| Proof assistants | Coq (HoTT library) | Cubical Agda [9], redtt, cooltt | — |

### Synthetic Homotopy Theory: Calculating with Types

Synthetic homotopy theory treats spaces as primitive: we reason about points, paths, and homotopies using the logical rules of type theory rather than constructing spaces from sets.

> **Theorem (Licata–Shulman):** The fundamental group of the circle is the integers: π₁(S¹, base) ≃ ℤ as groups.

*Proof sketch (encode-decode).* Define code : S¹ → U by circle recursion: code(base) := ℤ, and ap code loop := ua(succ-equiv), where succ-equiv : ℤ ≃ ℤ is the successor equivalence — this uses univalence to turn an equivalence into a path in the universe. Define encode : Π(x:S¹). (base = x) → code(x) by encode(x, p) := transport^code p 0, and decode : Π(x:S¹). code(x) → (base = x) by circle induction, sending the integer n at the base to loop^n (with loop^(−n) := (loop⁻¹)^n). One shows encode and decode are mutually inverse: one direction by path induction on p, the other by circle induction on x with integer induction on the fiber. Hence (base = base) ≃ ℤ, and truncating to a set gives π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ. The group structure is transported along the equivalence. ∎

This proof is emblematic: the universal cover of the circle appears as a *type family* defined by recursion, univalence converts the successor equivalence into the monodromy around the loop, and induction principles do the work of classical covering-space theory.

The method scales.

The following Haskell-style pseudocode captures the encode-decode skeleton:

```haskell
-- Encode-decode for the circle (pseudocode)
code :: S1 -> Type
code = circleRec Z (\_ -> ua succEquiv)
  where succEquiv :: Equiv Z Z
        succEquiv = (succ, quasiInverse pred)

encode :: (x :: S1) -> (base = x) -> code x
encode x p = transport code p 0

decode :: (x :: S1) -> code x -> (base = x)
decode = circleInd (\_ -> loopPow)
  where loopPow :: Z -> (base = base)
        loopPow n = if n >= 0 then loop^n else (inv loop)^(-n)

-- Main lemma: encode x and decode x are quasi-inverses, so
-- (base = base) ≃ Z, and truncating gives π₁(S¹) ≃ Z.
```

And the classical covering-space intuition can be simulated concretely — lifting a discretized loop on the circle to the helix and reading off the winding number:

```python
# Illustrative simulation: the universal cover of S^1 as the helix over Z.
# A loop is a list of angles; its lift to the helix ends at the winding number.
import math

def winding_number(angles):
    """angles: sampled loop on the circle, in radians. Returns the integer
    winding number, i.e. the endpoint of the lifted path on the helix."""
    total = 0.0
    for a, b in zip(angles, angles[1:]):
        step = (b - a + math.pi) % (2 * math.pi) - math.pi  # shortest signed step
        total += step
    return round(total / (2 * math.pi))

# loop^3 sampled at 120 points, plus a null-homotopic wiggle (winding 0)
loop3 = [3 * 2 * math.pi * t / 120 for t in range(121)]
wiggle = [0.5 * math.sin(4 * math.pi * t / 120) for t in range(121)]
print(winding_number(loop3))   # 3  -> decode(encode(loop^3)) = 3
print(winding_number(wiggle))  # 0  -> contractible loops lift to closed paths
```

The helix *is* the type family `code`: the fiber over each point of the circle is a copy of the integers, and going around the loop once shifts the fiber by the successor equivalence — exactly what `ap code loop := ua(succ-equiv)` says.

### Cubical Realizations and Computational Content

The simplicial-sets model validates univalence but its metatheory is classical: it cannot explain what a proof using univalence *computes*.

```
Glue [φ ↦ (T, e)] A   -- A with the face φ replaced by T along e : T ≃ A
```

The univalence map ua(e) is then a path in the universe built by gluing, and transport along it *computes* by the Kan operations.

Higher inductive types receive the same treatment: Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg give syntax and semantics for the circle, spheres, torus, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts with *judgmental* computation rules on all constructors, including the higher ones [5].

```rust
// A path p : x = y is a map out of the interval with endpoints i0, i1.
// Kan composition fills the open box; the composite *computes*.
fn path_comp(p: Path, q: Path) -> Path { hcomp(vec![p, q]) }  // missing face
```

##5 Empirical/Proofs

The empirical record of homotopy type theory is its *formalization record*: every major result cited here exists as machine-checked code. The HoTT book's core chapters were formalized in Coq in the HoTT library; Licata and Shulman's π₁(S¹) calculation was formalized in Agda and was among the first proofs to use univalence and HITs together [3]; and the cubical ecosystem (Cubical Agda, redtt, cooltt) routinely evaluates univalence-heavy proofs to numerals [4, 5, 9].

> **Theorem (Univalence implies propositional extensionality):** If P, Q are mere propositions with P → Q and Q → P, then P =_U Q.

*Proof sketch.* From the logical equivalence we build maps f : P → Q, g : Q → P; since P, Q are propositions, each is a quasi-inverse of the other, giving e : P ≃ Q; then ua(e) : P = Q. ∎

> **Theorem (Structure identity principle, instance):** For groups G, H (a set with multiplication, unit, inverses, and axioms), an isomorphism φ : G ≅ H yields an identification G = H, and every group-theoretic property transports along it.

*Proof sketch.* A group is a tuple in a Σ-type over U; an isomorphism of underlying sets extends, via univalence applied componentwise, to an identification of the tuples; transport then moves properties across [1]. ∎

The cubical canonicity theorem provides the complementary computational evidence: closed synthetic proofs *run*.

##6 Limitations

**Canonicity for axiomatic univalence remains open.** In Book HoTT, univalence is an axiom with only a propositional computation rule, so a closed term of type ℕ built using ua is known to be *equal* to a numeral, but not known to *reduce* to one. *Homotopy canonicity* — every closed ℕ-term is propositionally equal to a numeral — is conjectured but unproved; only cubical variants enjoy proved canonicity [4].

**General schemas for HITs are incomplete.** The HoTT book treats HITs schematically, but a fully general schema — specifying which higher constructors are allowed and proving that all of them admit models — remains elusive. The difficulty is *coherence*: a path constructor between path constructors demands a coherence 2-path, which demands a 3-path, ad infinitum. Cubical theories handle an important class of HITs with definitional rules [5], but parameterized, indexed, and higher inductive-inductive families are still active research, and *semisimplicial types* — the motivating infinite-coherence example — remain an open problem.

**Univalence restricts classical principles.** Univalence refutes UIP, axiom K, and "every type is a set," by design. Global choice is inconsistent with it. While excluded middle is compatible (the simplicial model is classical), combining univalence with strong choice principles or with strict equality remains delicate, and large parts of classical mathematics have not been redeveloped univalently.

**Proof engineering costs.** Transport-heavy proofs are verbose; universe polymorphism and higher coherences strain proof assistants; and cubical type theories have a more complex core (interval, Kan operations, Glue) that complicates metatheory and implementation. The simplicial model, conversely, offers no computational interpretation at all.

##7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory proposes that the identity type — proofs of equality as paths — is the *correct* primitive notion of sameness for mathematics. Univalence makes this precise: identifications of types *are* equivalences, so mathematics becomes invariant under equivalence. Higher inductive types make it usable: spaces enter the theory as declared data, and synthetic homotopy theory calculates genuine invariants — π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ, the Hopf fibration, the Freudenthal suspension theorem — inside the logic itself. Cubical type theory closes the computational gap, proving univalence with definitional behavior and restoring canonicity.

The open problems are now sharply delineated: homotopy canonicity for the axiomatic theory, a general coherent schema for higher inductive types, the semisimplicial tower, and the univalent redevelopment of classical mathematics. Whatever their resolution, the univalent program has already changed what a foundation can be: not a theory of sets in which homotopy is encoded, but a *logic of spaces* in which mathematics is done — and checked by machine — natively.
