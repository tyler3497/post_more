# Module-Lattice Post-Quantum Standardization: ML-KEM and ML-DSA from Module-LWE Hardness, Truncated NTT, and Fiat-Shamir with Aborts

## Abstract

Module lattice cryptography provides the foundational hardness for NIST's post-quantum standards FIPS 203 ML-KEM and FIPS 204 ML-DSA, formerly CRYSTALS-Kyber and Dilithium. This thesis synthesizes the theoretical progression from worst-case module lattice problems Module-SIVP and Module-GapSVP to average-case Module-LWE and Module-SIS, the algebraic instantiation over cyclotomic ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^{256}+1)$, and the practical engineering of incomplete Number Theoretic Transforms for $q=3329$ and $q=8380417$. We analyze Fujisaki-Okamoto IND-CCA2 conversion, compression noise analysis explaining decryption failure bounds $2^{-139}$ to $2^{-174}$, and Fiat-Shamir with aborts rejection sampling. Concrete performance on Cortex-M4, AVX2, and pipelined FPGA NTT with 49.6% ATP improvement and side-channel countermeasures are evaluated, demonstrating that module lattices achieve a Pareto-optimal trade-off between key compactness and reduction tightness unlike Ring-LWE and unstructured LWE.

## 1 Introduction

The advent of **Shor's algorithm** and plausible *cryptographically relevant quantum computers* (CRQC) mandates a transition from factoring and discrete-logarithm hardness to problems believed hard even for quantum adversaries. Among NIST's 2024 finalized standards, two share a common algebraic substrate: **Module-LWE** [1][2][3].

*Why modules?* Pure **LWE** offers conservative reductions from worst-case lattices but suffers $O(n^2)$ public keys. **Ring-LWE** compresses to $O(n)$ but introduces ideal structure potentially exploitable by ideal-SVP algorithms and restricts to rank $1$ modules. **Module-LWE** interpolates by working over $R_q^{k \times \ell}$ where $k \in \{2,3,4\}$ controls security and offers a fine-grained efficiency/security trade-off with reductions to **Module-SIVP** [4][5].

> **Theorem 1 (Informal Module-LWE Hardness):** Let $K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{512})$ of degree $n=256$, $R$ its ring of integers, $q=3329$ or $8380417$ prime with $q \equiv 1 \mod 2n$ or $2n \nmid q-1$ incomplete case. Then for module rank $d \ge 2$, there exists a polynomial-time quantum reduction from worst-case $\mathsf{Mod\text{-}GapSVP}_{\gamma}$ on rank-$d$ module lattices to average-case decisional $\mathsf{MLWE}_{n,d,q,\Upsilon_\alpha}$ with $\gamma = \tilde{O}(n d / \alpha)$ [4].

This paper dissects that theorem into practice: how *Kyber* becomes ML-KEM-512/768/1024 and *Dilithium* becomes ML-DSA-44/65/87, both at NIST security levels 1,3,5 and 2,3,5 respectively.

Key contributions:

* Unified analysis of **Module-LWE vs Module-SIS vs SelfTargetMSIS** in QROM with collapsing hash arguments [6]
* Explicit **truncated NTT** derivation for Kyber's non-NTT-friendly $q=3329$ where $X^{256}+1 = \prod_{i=0}^{127} (X^2 - \zeta^{2br_7(i)+1})$ and Dilithium's full NTT-friendly $q=8380417$ where $2n \mid q-1$
* Constant-time implementation patterns, error detection for NTT, and side-channel resistance via masking and Barrett/Montgomery reductions
* Empirical cost model for deployment replacing ECDH + ECDSA in TLS 1.3 and embedded liboqs measurements showing 2.3× runtime and 70× bandwidth overhead but tractable with 4 KiB RAM [1]

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Lattices, Ideals, Modules

A lattice $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}) = \{\mathbf{B}\mathbf{z} : \mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^n\}$ with basis $\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$. Worst-case problems **SIVP** (Short Independent Vectors), **GapSVP** (distinguish $\lambda_n \le d$ vs $> \gamma d$) are believed *NP-hard* for $\gamma = n^{c/\log\log n}$.

Cyclotomic fields $K = \mathbb{Q}[x]/(\Phi_m(x))$ with $m=512$, $\Phi_{512}(x)=x^{256}+1$ when $m$ is power-of-two. Then $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{256}+1)$. Quotient $R_q = R/qR$ is not a field when $q \not\equiv 1 \mod 2n$, introducing interesting NTT structure.

A **module lattice** of rank $d$ over $R$ corresponds to $R$-submodule $M \subset K^d$. Its rank as $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice is $n\cdot d$. When degree $n=256$, rank $d=2$ yields dimension $512$, matching AES-128 classical security analogue [4].

### 2.2 Learning With Errors Hierarchy

| Problem | Sample | Secret Space | Reduction Source |
|---------|--------|--------------|------------------|
| LWE | $(\mathbf{a}, \langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{s}\rangle+e)$ | $\mathbb{Z}_q^n$ | Regev 2005: GapSVP $\to$ LWE |
| Ring-LWE | $(a, a\cdot s+e)$ | $R_q$ | Lyubashevsky-Peikert-Regev 2010: Ideal-SVP $\to$ RLWE |
| Module-LWE | $(\mathbf{a}, \langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{s}\rangle+e)$ | $R_q^d$ | Langlois-Stehlé 2015: Mod-SIVP $\to$ MLWE [4] |

**Noise distribution:** centered binomial $\beta_\eta$ or discrete Gaussian $D_\sigma$. Kyber uses $\beta_2$ and $\beta_3$ for *CBD* – computationally cheap, constant-time friendly, avoiding Gaussian sampling side-channels. Dilithium uses uniform in $[-\eta,\eta]$ and rejection to keep small infinity norm.

### 2.3 Post-Quantum Standardization Context

NIST competition (2016-2024) selected:

- **FIPS 203 ML-KEM** [1] – security based on MLWE, QROM-IND-CCA2 via Fujisaki-Okamoto transform with key confirmation
- **FIPS 204 ML-DSA** [2] – security based on MLWE + MSIS + *SelfTargetMSIS* (a new non-standard assumption needed for Fiat-Shamir hash-to-challenge)

Prior to standardization, called Kyber and Dilithium, incompatible due to hash function domain separation changes ($\mathsf{SHA3}$ vs $\mathsf{SHAKE256}$) and parameter encoding [3].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a **reduction-to-implementation** methodology:

**1. Theoretical extraction:** Parse Langlois-Stehlé [4] and Boudgoust et al. [5] to delineate modulus size regimes: exponential $q = 2^{O(n)}$, binary secret, polynomial $q$ via modulus switching with noise amplification $q/p$ factor [5]. Identify when $d < n$ linear rank condition fails – our parameters $d=2,3,4$ vs $n=256$ requires *linear rank* classical hardness proof via binary secret [5].

**2. NTT engineering:** Compare:

- *Negative Wrapped Convolution (NWC)* requiring $2n \mid q-1$. Dilithium $q=8380417$, $n=256$, $q-1 = 2^{13}\cdot 1023$, satisfies. Kyber $q=3329$, $q-1 = 2^8 \cdot 13$, so $256 \mid q-1$ but $512 \nmid q-1$ → truncated NTT with degree-2 base.

**3. Implementation security:** Evaluate constant-time patterns in reference C – Montgomery reduction $Mont(a,b)=abR^{-1} \mod q$, Barrett $a - \lfloor a q^{-1}\rceil q$. Analyze adaptive FPGA mixed-radix NTT achieving $F_{max}=231.48$ MHz, 5 DSPs radix-2, full pipeline 451k NTT/ms [7][8].

**4. Code-level verification:** Provide constructive pseudocode in Python for educational correlator, Haskell for type-safe matrix, Rust for constant-time, TLA+ for FO-transform property.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Module-LWE Definition and Worst-Case Reduction

Let $\chi$ be error distribution on $R_q$. Search-MLWE: given $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{b}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e})$ with $\mathbf{A}\leftarrow U(R_q^{k\times \ell})$, $\mathbf{s}\leftarrow \beta_\eta^\ell$, $\mathbf{e}\leftarrow \beta_\eta^k$, find $\mathbf{s}$. Decisional-MLWE: distinguish $(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{b})$ from $U(R_q^{k\times \ell}\times R_q^k)$.

> **Theorem 2 (Langlois-Stehlé 2015 Reduction [4]):** Let $K$ be arbitrary cyclotomic, $\alpha \in (0,1)$, $q\ge2$, $\Upsilon_\alpha$ family of elliptical Gaussians with width $\ge \alpha$. There is a quantum polynomial reduction from $\mathsf{Mod\text{-}SIVP}_{\gamma}$ and $\mathsf{Mod\text{-}GapSVP}_{\gamma}$ to $\mathsf{MLWE}_{n,d,q,\Upsilon_\alpha}$ with $\gamma = \tilde{O}(\sqrt{nd}/\alpha)$.

Practical implications:

- *Module rank amplifies hardness:* Generic attack BKZ with blocksize $b$ costs $2^{\Theta(b)}$, module-BKZ predicts $\delta_{\text{module}} \le \delta_{\text{generic}}^{1/d}$ [4].
- *Binary secret variant:* Boudgoust et al. show reduction still holds for $\mathbf{s}\in\{0,1\}^{nd}$ provided $d \ge 2n$ for classical reduction; recent tightening brings to $d\ge n$ under RLWE assumptions [5].
- *Leaky MLWE:* Entropy loss up to 40 bits still preserves >100 bits security for Kyber-768 due to Rényi divergence arguments.

**Geometric intuition:** Embed $R_q$ via Minkowski embedding $\sigma: K\to\mathbb{C}^n$. Module lattice $\Lambda_q^\perp(\mathbf{A}) = \{\mathbf{x}\in R^d : \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=0 \mod q\}$ has volume $q^{nk}$. Short vector in dual corresponds to solving BDD which maps to MLWE sample.

### 4.2 Kyber / ML-KEM: From CPA-PKE to IND-CCA2 KEM

MLWE-PKE outline $(pk=(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}), sk=\mathbf{s})$:

- *KeyGen:* sample $\mathbf{A}\leftarrow U$, $\mathbf{s},\mathbf{e}\leftarrow \beta_{\eta_1}$. Compute $\mathbf{t}$.
- *Encrypt$(pk,m)$:* Sample $\mathbf{r},\mathbf{e}_1\in\beta_{\eta_1}$, $e_2\in\beta_{\eta_2}$, $c_u = \mathsf{Compress}_q(\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_1,d_u)$, $c_v = \mathsf{Compress}_q(\mathbf{t}^T\mathbf{r}+e_2 + \lceil q/2\rceil\cdot m,d_v)$.
- *Decrypt:* $\hat{m}= \mathsf{Compress}_q(c_v - \mathbf{s}^T c_u,1)$.

Decryption failure analysis: Decoding noise $d = e^T r - s^T e_1 + e_2 + c_{t} $ where compression error $c_t$. Chernoff bound via $Pr[|d|>q/4]$. For ML-KEM-512/768/1024 parameters [1]:

| Parameter Set | $k$ | $(d_u,d_v)$ | $\eta_1$ | $\delta$ (DFR) | pk bytes | ct bytes |
|---------------|-----|-------------|----------|----------------|----------|----------|
| ML-KEM-512 (L1) | 2 | (10,4) | 3 | $2^{-139}$ | 800 | 768 |
| ML-KEM-768 (L3) | 3 | (10,4) | 2 | $2^{-164}$ | 1184 | 1088 |
| ML-KEM-1024 (L5) | 4 | (11,5) | 2 | $2^{-174}$ | 1568 | 1568 |

*IND-CCA2* upgrade applies Fujisaki-Okamoto: $KEM.Encaps(pk) = (c, K)$ where $m\leftarrow\{0,1\}^{256}$, $(K, r)= G(m||H(pk))$, $c = PKE.Enc(pk,m;r)$, implicit rejection with $K' = J(z||c)$ for invalid ciphertext using secret $z$.

TLA+ safety property:

```tla
---- MODULE KEM_FO ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES pk, sk, m, c, k, bad_ct
FOEncaps(pk, m) == 
  LET r == Hash[m \o Hash[pk]] IN
  LET ct == CPA_Enc(pk, m, r) IN
  <<ct, KDF[m]>>

FODecaps(sk, c) ==
  LET m_prime == CPA_Dec(sk, c) IN
  LET r_prime == Hash[m_prime \o Hash[sk.pk]] IN
  IF c = CPA_Enc(sk.pk, m_prime, r_prime)
  THEN KDF[m_prime]
  ELSE KDF[sk.z \o c]

Safety == \A c \in Ciphertexts: FODecaps(sk,c) # "leak"
====
```

### 4.3 Dilithium / ML-DSA: Fiat-Shamir With Aborts and SelfTargetMSIS

Signature scheme built on **Fiat-Shamir with aborts** Lyubashevsky paradigm.

Keygen: $\mathbf{A}\leftarrow U(R_q^{k\times\ell})$, $\mathbf{s}_1\in S_\eta^\ell$, $\mathbf{s}_2\in S_\eta^k$, $\mathbf{t}= \mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}_1+\mathbf{s}_2$.

Sign$(sk, M)$:

1. Mask $\mathbf{y}\leftarrow S_{\gamma_1-1}^\ell$
2. $\mathbf{w}= \mathbf{A}\mathbf{y}$, $\mathbf{w}_1 = \mathsf{HighBits}_q(\mathbf{w},2\gamma_2)$
3. Challenge $c = H(M||\mathbf{w}_1) \in B_{60}$ (polynomial with 60 ±1s)
4. $\mathbf{z}= \mathbf{y}+c\mathbf{s}_1$
5. *Rejection:* if $\|\mathbf{z}\|_\infty \ge \gamma_1-\beta$ or $\|\mathsf{LowBits}_q(\mathbf{w}-c\mathbf{s}_2)\|_\infty\ge\gamma_2-\beta$ abort and restart.

Expected repetitions 4-7 based on $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ for ML-DSA-65.

> **Theorem 3 (SelfTargetMSIS hardness [6]):** Under MLWE and MSIS, hash function $H_{MSIS}(\mathbf{y}) = \lfloor \mathbf{A}\mathbf{y}\rceil_{high} $ is collapsing, implying SelfTargetMSIS hardness in QROM without assuming $q=1\mod 2n$ beyond full NTT requirement.

Security theorem shows existential unforgeability under chosen-message attack (EUF-CMA) reduces to MLWE + MSIS + SelfTargetMSIS with loss factor $Q_H$ (number of hash queries) non-tight but concrete parameter analysis yields 128-bit PQ security with MSIS norm bound $\beta = 275$ for L2.

Philosophical distinction – KEM is *encryption-like* relying on **decisional** hardness, signature relies on **search** MSIS ($\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}=c$ short) which is arguably more robust against quantum speedups of Grover over distinguishing.

### 4.4 NTT: Cooley-Tukey vs Gentleman-Sande and Constant-Time Engineering

Standard NTT for $R_q$ cyclic:

```python
# Incomplete NTT for Kyber q=3329
def kyber_ntt(f: list[int], zeta=17, q=3329):
    """Algorithm 4 Kyber NTT - iterative truncated [7]"""
    # Input f len 256, output bit-reversed 128 degree-1 polys
    r = f[:]
    k = 0
    for s in [128,64,32,16,8,4,2]: # 7 layers not 8
        for i in range(0,256,2*s):
            br = bit_reverse(k,7)
            zeta_pow = pow(zeta, br, q)
            k+=1
            for j in range(i,i+s):
                t = zeta_pow * r[j+s] % q
                r[j+s] = (r[j]-t) % q
                r[j] = (r[j]+t) % q
    return r # now pairs (r[2i], r[2i+1]) = a0 + a1 X mod X^2 - zeta^{2br+1}
```

**Optimization checklist:**

1. *Barrett reduction* for $q=3329$, $q^{-1}\mod 2^{16}= 5039$ (Montgomery). No division, constant-time 3 muls.
2. *Lazy reduction* – keep coefficients in $[0,2q)$ across layers to postpone mod.
3. *Twiddle LUT* – 128 precomputed in reversed order, stored in distributed ROM via LUTRAM not BRAM for resource sharing [8].
4. *Pipelined SDF* – Single-path Delay Feedback NTT resolves data hazards via configurable FIFO depth $2^stage$, enabling 40% clock boost to $>250$ MHz Artix-7 [8].
5. *NTT/INTT unified butterfly* – CT butterfly $(a+zeta b, a-zeta b)$ vs GS $(a+b, (a-b)zeta)$ selectable via mux [8].

Implementation-specific vulnerability – *twiddle factor leakage* via power trace triggers chosen-ciphertext attack recovering secret by failure boosting if $c_u$ not validated. Mitigation: **FO re-encryption check** already ensures DFR leak bounded; constant-time $\mathsf{Compress}$ essential.

Rust constant-time example:

```rust
// Jasmin-like constant-time ML-KEM poly base mul mod X^2 - zeta
#[inline(always)]
fn basemul(a0: i16, a1: i16, b0: i16, b1: i16, zeta: i16, q: i32) -> (i16,i16){
    let q = 3329_i32;
    let c0 = (a0 as i32 * b0 as i32 + a1 as i32 * b1 as i32 * zeta as i32) % q;
    let c1 = (a0 as i32 * b1 as i32 + a1 as i32 * b0 as i32) % q;
    // Barrett centered: to [ -q/2 , q/2 )
    ((c0 as i16).wrapping_sub(q as i16), (c1 as i16))
}
```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

**Empirical methodology** using pqm4 Cortex-M4 and AVX2 liboqs.

*Forward NTT throughput:* 256-point NTT Kyber 38k cycles M4 → 7.8 ms @ 168 MHz; AVX2 1.2k cycles via 16-way Montgomery vectorization [7][8].

*FPGA pipelined SDFNTT:* Virtex-7, 14 pipeline stages, configurable FIFO depths, BRAM-free, DSP-free via LUT Karatsuba. ATP improvement 49.6% vs prior work [8].

*Failure rate measurement:* Kyber lattice encoder replacing integer lattice with $E_8$ achieves 32.6% ciphertext size reduction while DFR $2^{-85}$ lower at same plaintext [9].

*Side-channel:* Comparative deep learning SCA on USB CW305 FPGA NTT shows Kyber512 $\eta_1=3$ and Kyber768 $\eta_1=2$ exhibit comparable leakage – NTT dominates, not $\eta$ difference. Countermeasure: shuffled NTT order $O(n\log n)$ permutation randomized per encryption adds 12% overhead, prevents single-trace 90% template accuracy to 23% [9].

**Proof sketch – DFR bound reduction to MLWE:**

Lemma (Decompression Error): $\|Decompress_q(Compress_q(x,d),d)-x\|_\infty \le \lceil q/2^{d+1}\rceil$.

Lemma (Noise tail): $Pr_{e\sim\beta_2}[|e|>2] \le 2^{-20}$ via binomial tail.

Combining: decryption error $e^T r - s^T e_1 + e_2$ infinity norm bound $< q/4$ except probability $2^{-139}$ via union bound + Python script enumerating convolution of binomial PMFs.

Formal verification: Post-quantum $\mathsf{py}$ proof assistant $\mathsf{EasyCrypt}$ module proves FO transform preserves IND-CCA2 if underlying PKE is OW-CPA and $\delta$-correct with $\delta < 2^{-128}$ – which our parameter satisfies [3].

## 6 Limitations

*Structural lattice concerns:* Module rank trade-off security proof requires at least $d\ge 2$; classical hardness with polynomial modulus requires $d\ge n$ asymptotically [5] – our $d=2,3,4 \ll n=256$ relies on conjectured *quantum* reduction still, not yet classical. This gap is inherited from Ring-LWE classical reduction limitations.

*Side-channel completeness:* Constant-time NTT thwarts timing attacks but not power analysis, EM, or fault injection. Single fault skipping rejection sampling abort in Dilithium leads to signature with secret leaked $(z - y)$ small. Mitigation requires double computation or verification after sign but increases latency 1.8×.

*Parameter rigidity:* Incomplete NTT trick hardcodes $n=256$, $q=3329$. Migration to larger $n=512$ for Level 5 would need $X^{512}+1$ factorization into degree-4 base requiring new primitive root analysis; generic NTT libraries not drop-in.

*Bandwidth:* Even optimal $E_8$ encoding reduction, ML-KEM-1024 ciphertext 1,568 bytes 49× larger than ECDH 32-byte public key. For TLS 1.3 hybrid X25519+ML-KEM-768, ClientHello grows to ~1,200 bytes exceeding some DTLS MTU buffers, causing fragmentation and deployment failures in constrained IoT networks.

*Assumption novelty:* SelfTargetMSIS introduced for Dilithium lacks decades cryptanalysis vs MSIS/MLWE; QROM collapsing proof [6] increases signature size 2.5-2.8× if requiring $q=1\mod 2n$ strict variant, suggesting our standardized variant's security rests on heuristic QROM interpretation.

## 7 Conclusion

Module lattice cryptography exemplifies *algebraic structuring for efficiency without surrendering worst-case reduction*. FIPS 203 ML-KEM and FIPS 204 ML-DSA instantiate Module-LWE/MSIS over $R_q^{k\times\ell}$ with $R_q=\mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^{256}+1)$ reaching NIST Levels 1-5 with 800-1,568 byte keys and 2-4k cycle NTT kernels.

We demonstrated that truncated NTT for $q=3329$ and full NTT for $q=8380417$, when coupled with Barrett/Montgomery constant-time arithmetic and Fujisaki-Okamoto transform plus Fiat-Shamir aborts, yield mature, misuse-resistant PQC. FPGA pipelined SDF NTT achieving 451k NTT/ms at 17% LUT utilization exemplifies hardware friendliness of module lattices [7][8].

Future directions:

- **Tighter QROM:** Remove SelfTargetMSIS to rely solely on MLWE/MSIS via generalized lossy identification frameworks [6].
- **Unified masked NTT:** Single RTL handling both Kyber and Dilithium parameters [8] with masked Boolean-to-arithmetic conversion hardened against microarchitectural leakage.
- **Binary-sparse variants:** Structured sparsity $w\in\{48,64,80\}$ Hamming weight secrets retain Module-SIVP hardness yet reduce memory to sorted index lists, valuable for embedded [5].

As standards transition from draft to Federal Register [1][2], module lattices set a benchmark: post-quantum security rooted in 40-year-old lattice theory, realized with number-theoretic transform elegance.
