# Neural Video Codecs at the Standardization Threshold: Conditional Coding, Rate–Distortion–Perception Tradeoffs, and Video Coding for Machines

## Abstract

This thesis presents a rigorous treatment of the contemporary frontier in video compression: end-to-end learned (neural) video codecs, their convergence toward standardized practice, and the information-theoretic frameworks that now govern their design. We trace the architectural evolution from the first joint-optimization residual coders, exemplified by DVC, through the paradigm shift to conditional coding inaugurated by DCVC and refined through the DCVC-TCM, DCVC-HEM, DCVC-DC, and DCVC-FM lineage, including the bi-directional DCVC-B extension. We formalize the rate–distortion–perception (RDP) tradeoff of Blau and Michaeli, derive its Lagrangian operationalization in modern perceptual neural codecs, and examine Video Coding for Machines (VCM) as a multi-objective generalization in which decoded representations serve downstream vision tasks rather than human observers. We contextualize learned codecs against the newly finalized AV2 specification (released May 2026) and the JPEG AI learning-based image coding standard (ITU-T T.840.1 | ISO/IEC 6048-1), present BD-rate evidence placing recent neural codecs at or beyond VVC (VTM reference) performance on select test sets, and conclude with the structural limitations — decode complexity, generalization, and intellectual property — that will decide whether neural codecs displace or merely augment classical toolchains.

## 1 Introduction

Video data constitutes the dominant share of global network traffic, and the economic value of each percentage point of compression gain is measured in billions of dollars of transit and storage cost. For three decades, compression progress arrived through hand-crafted codec generations — H.264/AVC, HEVC, VVC, and the royalty-free AV1 — each delivering roughly 30–50% bitrate reduction over its predecessor through increasingly sophisticated block partitioning, intra/inter prediction, transforms, and entropy coding [1][7]. These designs are the product of expert-driven trial and error: every tool is specified mathematically, and encoder search heuristics explore a combinatorial space that has grown almost beyond human intuition.

A parallel line of research, maturing over the last decade, replaces this hand-crafted pipeline with *learned* compression: convolutional and transformer architectures trained end-to-end to minimize a Lagrangian cost *R + λD*, where *R* is the estimated bitrate of a learned latent representation and *D* is distortion. The promise is twofold. First, gradient-based optimization over real data distributions can discover coding strategies that experts never enumerated — anisotropic, content-adaptive, and globally coordinated transforms. Second, the same machinery can be re-targeted to entirely new objectives: perceptual realism rather than pixel fidelity, and task utility for machine consumers rather than human viewers [3][5].

This thesis argues that neural video compression has reached a threshold of maturity where three formerly separate discourses — codec architecture, information-theoretic limits, and standardization — must be analyzed jointly:

1. **Architecture.** The field has moved decisively from *residual coding* (predict, subtract, code the residue) to *conditional coding* (code the frame conditioned on learned temporal context), with each generation of the DCVC family cutting roughly 15–25% of bitrate over the last.
2. **Theory.** The classical rate–distortion framework is insufficient. Practitioners increasingly operate on the rate–distortion–perception surface, and the machine-consumption regime demands a task-aware generalization formalized by VCM.
3. **Standardization.** JPEG AI (Part 1 published 2025 as ISO/IEC 6048-1) is the first ISO standard built on learned coding, and the Alliance for Open Media released the AV2 specification in May 2026 with ~30% gains over AV1 — the classical counterweight against which every neural codec must now be benchmarked.

The remainder of this thesis develops each thread, evaluates empirical evidence via BD-rate analysis, and identifies the unresolved obstacles.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Classical hybrid video coding

All modern standards follow the hybrid predictive–transform architecture: intra-frame prediction exploits spatial redundancy, motion-compensated inter-frame prediction exploits temporal redundancy, and the residual is transformed (DCT/DST variants), quantized, and entropy-coded (CABAC/CAVLC). Rate–distortion optimization selects coding modes by minimizing *J = D + λR* at each block. VVC (H.266) adds tools such as affine motion compensation, multi-transform selection, and improved loop filtering, achieving roughly 40–50% bitrate savings over HEVC at the cost of an order-of-magnitude increase in encoder complexity [1]. The AV2 specification, finalized in May 2026, delivers approximately 30% bitrate reduction over AV1 under a royalty-free patent policy, with extended recursive partitioning, semi-decoupled luma/chroma partitioning, chroma-from-luma intra prediction, and multi-view/multi-stream support [7].

> **Theorem 1.** *(Shannon's rate–distortion bound.)* For a stationary memoryless source *X* with distortion measure Δ, the information rate–distortion function *R(D) = min_{p(Ŷ|X): E[Δ(X,X̂)] ≤ D} I(X; X̂)* is the fundamental lower bound on the achievable rate of any block code. Neural codecs trained with *R + λD* objectives are, in the information-theoretic sense, variational approximations to this bound computed over empirical data distributions rather than analytic source models.

### 2.2 Learned image compression as the substrate

Neural video codecs inherit their entropy machinery from learned image compression. A variational autoencoder (VAE) maps an image *x* to a latent *y = g_a(x; φ)*, quantizes to *ŷ*, and reconstructs *x̂ = g_s(ŷ; θ)*. The rate is estimated with a learned entropy model — factorized, then hyperprior-augmented *p(ŷ|ẑ)* with side information *ẑ = h_a(y)* — and training minimizes

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{x}[-\log_2 p_{\hat{y}}(\hat{y} \mid \hat{z})] + \lambda \cdot \mathbb{E}_{x}[\Delta(x, \hat{x})].$$

Key developments include scale hyperpriors, autoregressive context models, channel-conditional entropy coding, and Gaussian mixture likelihoods. CompressAI and TensorFlow Compression standardized this infrastructure, enabling reproducible RD curves across papers.

### 2.3 BD-rate methodology

The Bjøntegaard delta rate (BD-rate) integrates two RD curves over their overlapping quality range, reporting the average percentage bitrate difference at equal distortion. Negative values indicate savings relative to the anchor (e.g., VTM for VVC, x265 for HEVC). BD-rate is the lingua franca of codec evaluation, though it is sensitive to curve fitting (cubic interpolation), quality-range overlap, and the anchor's configuration — caveats that loom large when comparing neural codecs against reference software under low-delay vs. random-access conditions.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 End-to-end formulation

A learned video codec encodes a sequence *{x_t}* with access to previously decoded frames *{x̂_{<t}}*. The canonical residual-coding formulation, introduced by DVC [1], factorizes each inter frame into:

- **Motion path:** estimate optical flow *m_t* between *x_{t-1}* (or *x̂_{t-1}*) and *x_t*, compress the flow with an autoencoder, and warp the reference frame to obtain the prediction *x̄_t*.
- **Residual path:** compress *r_t = x_t − x̄_t* with a second autoencoder.
- **Joint training:** all modules — flow estimator, motion codec, residual codec, and hyperprior entropy models — are optimized under a single *R + λD* loss.

DVC demonstrated that this formulation could outperform H.264 in PSNR and match H.265 in MS-SSIM, establishing the viability of the end-to-end approach [1].

### 3.2 From residual to conditional coding

Residual coding is provably suboptimal: subtracting a pixel-domain prediction discards the structure that a decoder could exploit, forcing the network to re-learn correlations from the residue. DCVC [2] replaced subtraction with *conditional coding*: the encoder and decoder receive the learned temporal context *C_t* — high-dimensional feature maps propagated from the reference frame — as side information, and the current frame is coded as *x̂_t = f_dec(ŷ_t | C_t)* where *ŷ_t* is the quantized latent of *x_t* conditioned on *C_t*.

> **Theorem 2.** *(Conditional coding dominance, Ladune et al. [6].)* Under the same entropy model capacity, conditional coding *H(X_t | C_t)* cannot exceed the residual-coding cost *H(X_t − X̄_t)* when the context *C_t* is a sufficient statistic of the reference for predicting *X_t*, and is strictly lower whenever *C_t* carries information about the prediction error's structure that subtraction destroys.

This shift was validated empirically: DCVC achieved 26.0% bitrate savings over x265 (veryslow) on 1080p content, decisively outperforming DVCPro [2]. Conditional coding is now the default design pattern.

### 3.3 Entropy modeling: the real engine of progress

Within conditional coding, successive gains have come primarily from the entropy model rather than the transform:

- **DCVC-TCM** (temporal context mining): multi-scale temporal context refinement.
- **DCVC-HEM** (hybrid spatial-temporal entropy modeling): dual spatial prior with parallel-friendly checkerboard context.
- **DCVC-DC** (diverse contexts): richer context aggregation; OpenDCVCs reports −59.93% average BD-rate vs. DCVC-official on 1080p sets.
- **DCVC-FM** (feature modulation): variable-rate coding within a single model via feature-wise modulation, eliminating the need for one model per λ.
- **DCVC-B**: bi-directional conditional coding with motion-difference context propagation, achieving −26.6% BD-rate vs. HEVC reference under random access and surpassing VVC reference on some datasets [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The DVC architecture in detail

DVC's pipeline comprises four learned modules: (i) a pre-trained optical flow network (FlowNet/PWC-Net style) estimating *m_t*; (ii) a motion-vector autoencoder with hyperprior compressing *m_t*; (iii) a motion-compensation warping module with a refinement network producing *x̄_t*; (iv) a residual autoencoder compressing *r_t = x_t − x̄_t*. All are trained jointly with *R_motion + R_residual + λ·D(x_t, x̂_t)*. The design's elegance lies in preserving the classical hybrid structure while making every component differentiable. Its limitation is structural: pixel-domain warping and subtraction bottleneck the information flow, and the motion path's bitrate grows in high-motion content where flow fields are chaotic [1].

```python
# Pseudocode: DVC-style joint rate-distortion training step
def train_step(x_t, x_hat_prev, model, lmbda):
    flow = model.flow_net(x_hat_prev, x_t)          # optical flow
    flow_hat, rate_flow = model.mv_codec(flow)      # compress motion
    x_bar = model.mc_net(warp(x_hat_prev, flow_hat))# motion compensation
    resid = x_t - x_bar
    r_hat, rate_resid = model.res_codec(resid)      # compress residual
    x_hat = x_bar + r_hat
    loss = rate_flow + rate_resid + lmbda * mse(x_t, x_hat)
    return loss, x_hat
```

### 4.2 The DCVC conditional framework

DCVC's innovation is the *contextual* formulation. Rather than a pixel prediction *x̄_t*, a context generation network produces multi-scale features *{C_t^s}* from the propagated reference feature *F_{t-1}*. The contextual encoder computes *y_t = g_a(x_t, {C_t^s})*, and the contextual decoder reconstructs *x̂_t = g_s(ŷ_t, {C_t^s})*, with the entropy model *p(ŷ_t | C_t, ẑ_t)* exploiting temporal context for both arithmetic coding and feature reconstruction. Because the context lives in feature space with hundreds of channels, it carries an order of magnitude more information than a 3-channel predicted frame — particularly high-frequency detail that subtraction-based methods lose. The framework is extensible: any conditioning signal (multi-reference, bidirectional, scale-space) can be substituted [2].

| Generation | Representative | Paradigm | Key mechanism | Relative gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | DVC [1] | Residual | Flow + dual autoencoders | ~HEVC parity (MS-SSIM) |
| 2020 | Scale-space flow | Residual | Scale-space motion | +10–15% over DVC |
| 2021 | DCVC [2] | Conditional | Feature-domain context | −26% vs. x265 (1080p) |
| 2022 | DCVC-TCM/HEM | Conditional | Temporal mining; hybrid entropy | −39 to −54% vs. DCVC |
| 2023 | DCVC-DC | Conditional | Diverse contexts | −60% vs. DCVC |
| 2024 | DCVC-FM / DCVC-B | Conditional | Variable-rate modulation; B-frames | RA: −26.6% vs. HEVC, ≈VVC |

### 4.3 The rate–distortion–perception tradeoff

Classical RD theory is blind to perceptual quality: minimizing MSE produces blurry reconstructions that are distributionally far from natural images. Blau and Michaeli formalized perception as a divergence *d(p_X, p_X̂)* between source and reconstruction distributions and defined the *information RDP function*:

$$R(D, P) = \min_{p_{\hat{X}|X}} I(X; \hat{X}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}[\Delta(X,\hat{X})] \le D, \;\; d(p_X, p_{\hat{X}}) \le P. \tag{1}$$

> **Theorem 3.** *(Blau & Michaeli [3].)* The RDP function *R(D, P)* is non-increasing in both *D* and *P*, convex in *(D, P)*, and satisfies *R(D, ∞) = R(D)* (classical RD) and *R(0, 0) = H(X)*. For perfect perceptual quality (*P = 0*), the rate generally exceeds the classical RD bound: *R(D, 0) ≥ R(D)*, with equality only in degenerate cases. The gap quantifies the bit cost of realism.

Consequences for neural video codecs are direct. GAN-based and diffusion-based compression codecs trade PSNR for perceptual scores (LPIPS, FID, DISTS): at fixed rate, perfect realism demands roughly double the MSE of the distortion-optimal reconstruction in typical regimes. Recent work on *universal* RDP representations shows a single encoder can span the tradeoff with varied decoders, suggesting future codecs may expose perception as a decoder-side dial rather than an encoder-side commitment [3]. For video, temporal perception adds flicker and motion-consistency constraints largely absent from the image literature — an open theoretical gap.

```haskell
-- Conceptual: RDP Lagrangian training objective (Blau–Michaeli)
rdpLoss :: Rate -> Distortion -> Perception -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
rdpLoss r d p lambdaR lambdaD lambdaP =
    lambdaR * r          -- bits: cross-entropy of latents
  + lambdaD * d          -- fidelity: MSE / MS-SSIM
  + lambdaP * p          -- realism: adversarial / divergence term
-- Varying (lambdaD, lambdaP) traces the Pareto surface R(D,P)
```

### 4.4 Video Coding for Machines

VCM reframes compression for the regime where the consumer is not a human but a vision model. Duan et al. [5] formalized VCM as a collaborative optimization over feature hierarchies *F = {F^0, …, F^z}*, where *F^z* is the pixel reconstruction for humans and *F^i* (*i < z*) are increasingly abstract features serving analytics tasks:

$$\arg\max_{\Theta} \sum_i \omega^i q^i \quad \text{s.t.} \quad S(R_{F^0}) + \sum_{i>0} \min_j S(R_{F_{i\to j}}) + S(R_M) + S(\Theta) \le S_T, \tag{2}$$

with *q^i = Φ^i(F̂^i)* the task-*i* performance and *S(·)* the resource cost [5]. Five solution families exist: feature-assisted coding, scalable layered coding, intermediate feature compression, task-targeted codecs, and — most recently — end-to-end learned ICM (image coding for machines) where the training loss replaces visual distortion with task loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{total} = w_{rate}\mathcal{L}_{rate} + w_{mse}\mathcal{L}_{mse} + w_{task}\mathcal{L}_{task}. \tag{3}$$

Strikingly, JPEG AI's normative design already embeds this duality: its compressed-domain latent is explicitly engineered to be machine-analyzable without full decoding, delivering ~30% rate savings over conventional codecs at equal subjective quality *and* a representation natively suited to vision workflows [8]. VCM is thus not a future add-on but a co-equal design axis alongside human fidelity.

### 4.5 Standardization: AV2 and JPEG AI

Two standardization milestones reframe the competitive landscape:

- **JPEG AI (ITU-T T.840.1 | ISO/IEC 6048-1:2025).** The first ISO image coding standard built on learned technology: a variational-autoencoder core with hyperprior entropy model, normative decoder specification, five-part structure (core system, profiling, reference software, conformance, file format), and profiles (Main@Simple/Base/High) constraining decoder complexity [8]. Its existence proves that learned codecs can survive the rigor of international standardization — conformance suites, interoperability, and royalty frameworks — providing a template for learned *video* standardization.
- **AV2 (AOMedia, v1.0.0, 28 May 2026).** The royalty-free successor to AV1, delivering ~30% bitrate reduction over AV1 with extended recursive partitioning, semi-decoupled luma/chroma, chroma-from-luma intra prediction, enhanced inter modes, and multi-view/multi-stream support; real-time software decode was demonstrated at CES 2026 and hardware IP (e.g., Chips&Media WAVE series) is in development [7]. AV2 sets the classical bar that neural codecs must clear for commercial relevance — and its royalty-free status removes the licensing argument that once favored learned approaches.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

We synthesize BD-rate evidence reported across the DCVC lineage and independent reproductions (notably OpenDCVCs). Anchors and configurations matter enormously: results below use PSNR unless noted, low-delay P (LDB) configurations, and 96-frame sequences with intra-period 32.

| DCVC-B [4] | HM (HEVC ref), RA | −26.6% avg; surpasses VTM on select sets | | | | — |

Three conclusions follow. **First**, the best learned codecs now meet or exceed VVC reference software on 1080p content under matched low-delay conditions — a crossing point the field predicted for years but only recently achieved. **Second**, gains are content-dependent: DCVC-DC's −25.0% on HEVC Class D versus −9.0% on Class B reflects the learned prior's strength on high-frequency textures and weakness on smooth, low-motion content where classical tools excel. **Third**, real-time variants (DCVC-RT, UI2C) trade 20–40 percentage points of BD-rate for 50+ fps decoding, exposing the complexity–efficiency frontier as the binding commercial constraint [4].

A methodological caution: BD-rate comparisons between neural codecs and reference software are confounded by differing intra periods, rate-control modes (constant-QP vs. constant-rate-factor), YUV420 vs. RGB pipelines, and the neural models' training-distribution overlap with test sets (Vimeo-90K-trained models evaluated on UVG share scene statistics). The EHVC and Uni-LVC reproductions show 5–15 point swings under re-implementation, underscoring the need for standardized neural-codec test conditions analogous to JVET CTC.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Decode complexity.** DCVC-DC requires ~910 kMACs/pixel at the decoder — roughly three orders of magnitude above VVC's optimized software decoders and far beyond mobile power budgets. Autoregressive entropy models serialize decoding; parallel context models (checkerboard, channel-wise) recover throughput at modest RD cost but remain heavy. Hardware acceleration for neural codecs is nascent; AV2, by contrast, ships with committed hardware IP [7].

2. **Generalization and domain shift.** Learned codecs are trained on Vimeo-90K and similar corpora; performance degrades on out-of-distribution content (screen content, animation, medical video) where classical codecs' content-agnostic tools are robust. No learned codec yet matches VVC's all-intra or screen-content coding extensions across domains.

3. **Rate control and random access.** Classical codecs offer fine-grained rate control, hierarchical B-frame structures, and scene-cut adaptation refined over decades. Learned codecs predominantly operate in low-delay P configurations; DCVC-B's hierarchical B-frame training is a first step, but random-access parity with VVC remains unproven at scale [4].

4. **Perceptual-temporal theory gap.** The RDP framework is developed for images; video adds temporal consistency, flicker, and motion realism that lack formal treatment. Perceptual video metrics (VMAF aside) are immature, making the *P* axis of *R(D,P)* difficult to measure, let alone optimize.

5. **Intellectual property and standardization inertia.** Thousands of patents encumber neural compression techniques, and no learned video codec has entered formal standardization. JPEG AI's precedent [8] suggests the barrier is surmountable, but the MPEG VCM exploration and AOM's classical roadmap currently absorb institutional attention.

6. **Evaluation reproducibility.** Reported BD-rate numbers vary by 5–15 points across reproductions due to training stochasticity, test-condition mismatches, and undisclosed hyperparameter tuning — a credibility problem the field must address with frozen checkpoints and common test conditions.

## 7 Conclusion

Neural video compression has traversed, in under a decade, the path from proof of concept (DVC) to VVC-competitive performance (DCVC-DC, DCVC-B) via the decisive architectural insight of conditional coding. The field's theoretical scaffolding has kept pace: the rate–distortion–perception tradeoff explains why realism costs bits, and Video Coding for Machines extends compression's objective from human fidelity to task utility. Standardization has begun to absorb the paradigm — JPEG AI as the learned-coding precedent, AV2 as the classical benchmark to beat.

The remaining obstacles are engineering and institutional rather than conceptual: decoder complexity must fall by two orders of magnitude, evaluation must be standardized, and the patent thicket must be navigated. The most probable near-term future is hybrid: classical codecs (AV2, VVC) carrying the base layer with neural enhancement layers and neural post-filters, and VCM-style task-aware bitstreams serving the growing machine-consumer segment. The longer-term question — whether an end-to-end learned codec displaces the hybrid architecture entirely — will be decided not by RD curves alone, but by the unforgiving arithmetic of silicon, watts, and standards politics.
