# The Atiyah–Singer Index Theorem: Dirac Operators, K-Theory, and Heat-Kernel Proofs

## Abstract

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem equates the analytical index of an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold— the difference of the dimensions of its kernel and cokernel—with a topological index computed from characteristic classes of the manifold and the operator's symbol. This thesis develops the theorem from first principles: elliptic complexes and Fredholm theory, K-theory and the Thom isomorphism, the cohomological and K-theoretic formulations, and the heat-equation proof of Patodi, Gilkey, Atiyah, Bott, and Patodi that expresses the index density through local curvature invariants. We prove the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern and Hirzebruch signature theorems as corollaries, explain the equivariant and families refinements, and survey applications to positive scalar curvature, Seiberg–Witten theory, and anomalies in quantum field theory. Limitations concerning non-compact manifolds, singular spaces, and the Baum–Connes conjecture are discussed.

## 1 Introduction

Few results in twentieth-century mathematics unite analysis, geometry, and topology as completely as the **Atiyah–Singer index theorem**. On the analytic side stands a linear partial differential operator $D$ acting between sections of vector bundles over a closed manifold; ellipticity—a purely algebraic condition on the operator's principal symbol—forces $D$ to be Fredholm, so that its kernel and cokernel are finite-dimensional and the **analytical index**

$$
\operatorname{ind}(D) = \dim \ker D - \dim \operatorname{coker} D
$$

is a well-defined integer. On the topological side stands the principal symbol itself, a bundle map over the cotangent bundle, which determines a class in compactly supported K-theory; pushing this class forward along the Thom isomorphism and applying the Chern character yields the **topological index**, an integral of characteristic forms. The theorem asserts that these two integers coincide [1][2].

The statement is remarkable because the left-hand side is *analytic*—it depends on solving a PDE—while the right-hand side is *homotopy invariant*, unchanged under deformations of the operator. The index is therefore a rigid integer-valued invariant of elliptic operators, and the theorem converts hard analytic questions into computable topological ones.

> Theorem: (Atiyah–Singer, 1963) Let $D: \Gamma(E) \to \Gamma(F)$ be an elliptic differential operator between smooth vector bundles over a closed oriented manifold $X$. Then $\operatorname{ind}(D) = \operatorname{ind}_t([\sigma(D)])$, where $[\sigma(D)] \in K(T^*X)$ is the K-theory class of the principal symbol and $\operatorname{ind}_t$ is the topological index.

This thesis presents a self-contained development: §2 reviews ellipticity, Sobolev theory, and Fredholm operators; §3 constructs the topological index via K-theory and the Thom isomorphism; §4 proves the theorem in the cohomological form and derives the classical corollaries; §5 presents the heat-equation proof and the local index theorem; §6 addresses limitations; §7 concludes with applications and open directions.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Elliptic operators and Fredholm theory

Let $X$ be a closed (compact, boundaryless) smooth manifold of dimension $n$, and let $E, F \to X$ be smooth complex vector bundles. A linear differential operator $D: \Gamma(E) \to \Gamma(F)$ of order $m$ has, in local coordinates, the form $D = \sum_{|\alpha| \le m} a_\alpha(x) \partial^\alpha$. Its **principal symbol** is the bundle homomorphism

$$
\sigma_m(D)(x, \xi) = \sum_{|\alpha| = m} a_\alpha(x) (i\xi)^\alpha : E_x \to F_x, \qquad (x, \xi) \in T^*X,
$$

homogeneous of degree $m$ in the cotangent variable. The operator is **elliptic** if $\sigma_m(D)(x, \xi)$ is an isomorphism for every $x \in X$ and every nonzero $\xi \in T_x^*X$.

Ellipticity implies elliptic regularity and, crucially, the Fredholm property: extending $D$ to Sobolev spaces $H^s(X, E) \to H^{s-m}(X, F)$, the operator has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel and closed range [3]. The index is stable under compact perturbations and, more importantly, under homotopies of elliptic operators—a fact already hinting at its topological nature.

Key examples include:

- The **de Rham operator** $d + d^*: \Omega^{\mathrm{even}} \to \Omega^{\mathrm{odd}}$, whose index is the Euler characteristic $\chi(X)$.
- The **signature operator**, whose index is the signature $\operatorname{sign}(X)$ of the intersection form on middle cohomology.
- The **Dolbeault operator** $\bar\partial + \bar\partial^*$ on a complex manifold, whose index is the arithmetic genus (the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem).
- The **Dirac operator** $\slashed{D}$ on a spin manifold, whose index is the $\hat A$-genus.

### 2.2 K-theory and the Thom isomorphism

Topological K-theory $K(X)$ is the Grothendieck group of complex vector bundles over $X$. For a locally compact space $Y$, $K(Y)$ denotes compactly supported K-theory. The symbol class $[\sigma(D)] \in K(T^*X)$ is constructed as follows: the symbol gives an isomorphism $\pi^*E \to \pi^*F$ over $T^*X$ minus the zero section (where $\pi: T^*X \to X$ is the projection), and hence a difference bundle—a standard clutching construction [4].

The **Thom isomorphism** $\phi: K(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} K(T^*X)$ (for oriented $X$, using the complex structure on the fibers of $T^*X \cong TX$) converts K-theory of the base into compactly supported K-theory of the total space. The topological index is the composition

$$
K(T^*X) \xrightarrow{\phi^{-1}} K(X) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}} H^{\mathrm{even}}(X; \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\int_X} \mathbb{Q},
$$

with a correction by the Todd class: $\operatorname{ind}_t(u) = \int_X \operatorname{ch}(\phi^{-1}u) \smile \operatorname{Td}(TX \otimes \mathbb{C})$. Atiyah and Singer proved this integer is independent of choices and equals the analytical index.

| Operator | Symbol class | Index |
|---|---|---|
| de Rham $d+d^*$ | Euler class | $\chi(X)$ |
| Signature | $L$-class | $\operatorname{sign}(X)$ |
| Dolbeault $\bar\partial+\bar\partial^*$ | Todd class | $\chi(X, \mathcal{O})$ |
| Dirac $\slashed{D}$ | $\hat A$-class | $\hat A(X)$ |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The axiomatic approach

The original proof of Atiyah and Singer proceeds axiomatically. Both the analytical index $\operatorname{ind}_a$ and the topological index $\operatorname{ind}_t$ are shown to satisfy a short list of axioms:

1. **Functoriality** under embeddings and diffeomorphisms.
2. **Excision**: the index depends only on a neighborhood of the support.
3. **Multiplicativity** under external products of operators.
4. **Normalization**: agreement on a point (Bott periodicity) and on the Dolbeault operator on projective space.

Any two integer-valued invariants satisfying these axioms coincide. The verification for $\operatorname{ind}_t$ is purely topological (K-theory yoga); for $\operatorname{ind}_a$ it uses the cobordism invariance of the index and the fact that every elliptic symbol is, stably, a pullback of the Dolbeault symbol [1][2].

```haskell
-- Schematic: the index as a natural transformation
-- ind_a, ind_t : K(T*X) -> Z satisfying the same axioms
axiomCheck :: (KTheoryClass -> Int) -> Bool
axiomCheck ind = functorial ind && excisive ind
            && multiplicative ind && normalized ind
-- Atiyah-Singer: axiomCheck ind_a && axiomCheck ind_t => ind_a == ind_t
```

### 3.2 The heat-equation method

A second, more computational proof—developed by Patodi, Gilkey, and Atiyah–Bott–Patodi—uses the heat kernel [5][6]. For a formally self-adjoint elliptic operator $D$ of order $m$, consider the heat operators $e^{-tD^*D}$ and $e^{-tDD^*}$. The McKean–Singer identity states

$$
\operatorname{ind}(D) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(e^{-tD^*D}\right) - \operatorname{Tr}\left(e^{-tDD^*}\right) \qquad \forall t > 0,
$$

because nonzero eigenvalues of $D^*D$ and $DD^*$ coincide with multiplicities, cancelling in the supertrace; only $\ker D$ and $\ker D^* = \operatorname{coker} D$ survive. As $t \to 0^+$, each heat trace admits an asymptotic expansion

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tD^*D}) \sim (4\pi t)^{-n/m} \sum_{k \ge 0} a_k(D^*D) t^{k/m},
$$

with locally computable coefficients $a_k$ built from the symbol and its derivatives. The miracle—**the local index theorem**—is that in the supertrace difference, all terms with $k < n$ vanish pointwise, and the $k = n$ term equals the characteristic differential form predicted by topology. The global index is thus the integral of a *local* density: analysis localizes [6][7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The cohomological formula

Unwinding the K-theoretic definition via the Chern character gives the working formula. For a **twisted Dirac operator** $\slashed{D}_E$ (Dirac coupled to a vector bundle $E$ with connection), on a closed spin manifold $X$ of dimension $n$:

$$
\operatorname{ind}(\slashed{D}_E) = \int_X \hat A(TX) \smile \operatorname{ch}(E),
$$

where $\hat A(TX) = \prod_j \frac{x_j/2}{\sinh(x_j/2)}$ is the Dirac genus in terms of the formal Chern roots $x_j$ of $TX \otimes \mathbb{C}$, and $\operatorname{ch}(E)$ is the Chern character. For the signature operator one obtains $\operatorname{sign}(X) = \int_X L(TX)$ with the Hirzebruch $L$-genus $\prod_j \frac{x_j}{\tanh x_j}$; for $d + d^*$ one recovers Gauss–Bonnet–Chern $\chi(X) = \int_X e(TX)$ [2][8].

### 4.2 Dirac operators and Clifford modules

The Dirac operator is the geometric heart of the theorem. On a Riemannian spin manifold, the spinor bundle $S \to X$ carries an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(T_xX)$ at each point, and

$$
\slashed{D} = \sum_{j=1}^n c(e_j) \nabla^{S}_{e_j} : \Gamma(S) \to \Gamma(S),
$$

where $c$ denotes Clifford multiplication. The Lichnerowicz formula

$$
\slashed{D}^2 = \nabla^*\nabla + \frac{\kappa}{4}
$$

relates the square of Dirac to the connection Laplacian plus one quarter of the scalar curvature $\kappa$. An immediate corollary: on a closed spin manifold with $\kappa > 0$ everywhere, $\ker \slashed{D} = 0$ and hence $\hat A(X) = 0$—a purely topological obstruction to positive scalar curvature [9]. This observation launched an entire industry (Gromov–Lawson, Rosenberg, Stolz) classifying which manifolds admit positive scalar curvature metrics.

### 4.3 The equivariant index and the Lefschetz formula

If a compact Lie group $G$ acts on $X$ preserving the operator, the kernels become $G$-representations and the index refines to a virtual character $\operatorname{ind}_G(D) \in R(G)$. The Atiyah–Segal–Singer fixed-point formula localizes this character to the fixed-point set:

$$
\operatorname{ind}_G(D)(g) = \sum_{F \subset X^g} \int_F \frac{\operatorname{ch}_g(\sigma)}{\det(1 - g|_{\nu_F})},
$$

summing over connected components $F$ of the fixed-point set with normal bundle $\nu_F$. For $g = 1$ this recovers the ordinary index; for isolated fixed points it yields the classical Atiyah–Bott holomorphic Lefschetz formula [10]. The equivariant refinement is the engine behind Witten's rigidity conjectures and much of modern enumerative geometry.

### 4.4 Families and the index bundle

Atiyah and Singer extended the theorem to **families**: given a fibration $Z \to X \xrightarrow{\pi} B$ and a family $D_b$ of elliptic operators along the fibers, the kernels and cokernels assemble (after stabilization) into a virtual vector bundle $\operatorname{Ind}(D) \in K(B)$, the **index bundle**. The families index theorem computes its Chern character:

$$
\operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Ind}(D)) = \int_{X/B} \hat A(T(X/B)) \smile \operatorname{ch}(E) \quad \in H^{\mathrm{even}}(B; \mathbb{Q}).
$$

Bismut's superconnection proof expresses this via differential forms on $B$ directly, and the families theorem underlies anomaly cancellation in string theory (the determinant line bundle of the Dirac family carries the anomaly) [11][12].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We sketch the heat-equation proof of the local index theorem for the twisted Dirac operator, following Getzler's rescaling argument [7].

**Step 1 (McKean–Singer).** As in §3.2, $\operatorname{ind}(\slashed{D}_E) = \operatorname{Str}(e^{-t\slashed{D}_E^2})$ for all $t > 0$, where $\operatorname{Str} = \operatorname{Tr}_S((-1)^F \cdot)$ is the supertrace on spinors.

**Step 2 (Small-time asymptotics).** The heat kernel $k_t(x, y)$ of $\slashed{D}_E^2$ has the Minakshisundaram–Pleijel expansion. Getzler's insight is to rescale Clifford variables $c(e_j) \mapsto t^{-1/2} c(e_j)$ and normal coordinates $y \mapsto t^{1/2}y$ simultaneously; the rescaled operator converges as $t \to 0$ to a **model operator**—a harmonic oscillator with constant magnetic field—whose heat kernel is explicitly computable via Mehler's formula.

**Step 3 (Local cancellation).** In the supertrace, only the top-degree Clifford component survives (the supertrace of a Clifford monomial $c(e_{i_1})\cdots c(e_{i_k})$ vanishes unless $k = n$). The surviving term is exactly $(2\pi i)^{-n/2} \hat A(TX) \smile \operatorname{ch}(E)$ evaluated at $x$. Hence

$$
\lim_{t \to 0^+} \operatorname{str}\, k_t(x,x) \, dx = \left[\hat A(TX) \smile \operatorname{ch}(E)\right]_{(n)}(x),
$$

and integrating over $X$ gives the index. The proof is *local*: it never uses global analysis beyond the McKean–Singer identity [6][7].

As a corollary we obtain the classical special cases:

1. **Gauss–Bonnet–Chern**: $\chi(X) = \int_X \operatorname{Pf}(R/2\pi)$ for the de Rham operator.
2. **Hirzebruch signature**: $\operatorname{sign}(X) = \int_X L(p_1, p_2, \dots)$ for the signature operator.
3. **Riemann–Roch–Hirzebruch**: $\chi(X, E) = \int_X \operatorname{Td}(TX) \smile \operatorname{ch}(E)$ for $\bar\partial_E$ on a complex manifold [8].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Compactness is essential.** On non-compact manifolds the Fredholm property fails in general; $L^2$-index theory (Atiyah's $L^2$-index theorem for coverings) and Callias-type indices at infinity are separate, harder theories.
2. **Singular spaces.** For stratified pseudomanifolds (Witt spaces), the signature operator must be replaced by intersection-homology complexes; the index theorem generalizes only under restrictive perversity conditions (Cheeger, Banagl).
3. **The Baum–Connes conjecture.** The analytic assembly map $\mu: K_*^G(\underline{E}G) \to K_*(C_r^*G)$ is the noncommutative analogue of the topological index; its injectivity/surjectivity remains open for general groups, blocking a full "index theorem" for foliations and group actions.
4. **Torsion information.** The rational cohomological formula loses torsion in K-theory; the integral refinement requires differential K-theory and is substantially more delicate.
5. **Boundary value problems.** On manifolds with boundary, ellipticity must be supplemented by (nonlocal) Atiyah–Patodi–Singer boundary conditions, introducing the eta invariant—a spectral invariant with no purely local formula [13].

## 7 Conclusion

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem stands as the prototype of a *topological computation of an analytic invariant*. Its two great proofs—the K-theoretic axiomatic proof and the heat-equation local proof—each opened a field: the first, equivariant and families index theory and noncommutative geometry; the second, local index theory, the Witten deformation, and supersymmetric quantum mechanics as a proof technique. From obstructions to positive scalar curvature to anomaly cancellation in the Standard Model and string theory, the theorem continues to convert the infinite-dimensional linear algebra of PDEs into finite, computable topology. The frontier now lies in extending its reach—singular spaces, non-compact geometries, and the Baum–Connes assembly map—where the dialogue between analysis and topology that Atiyah and Singer began is still far from over.
