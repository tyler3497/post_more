# Selective State Space Models for Linear-Time Sequence Modeling: HiPPO Initialization, Zero-Order-Hold Discretization, Hardware-Aware Parallel Scan, and the Mamba S6 Architecture

## Abstract

State space models (SSMs) reframe sequence modeling as simulation of a linear dynamical system, a principled alternative to attention with linear rather than quadratic cost in sequence length. This thesis develops the full stack of selective SSMs: the HiPPO theory of optimal polynomial memory, zero-order-hold discretization of continuous-time dynamics, and Mamba's selective S6 mechanism, which makes system matrices input-dependent while preserving an associative structure for a hardware-aware parallel scan. We prove associativity of the scan operator, derive the convolutional dual of the time-invariant system, and analyze the SRAM-tiled kernel design that makes Mamba competitive with optimized attention. Evidence from the Long Range Arena, 2.8B-parameter language modeling, and structured state space duality shows selective SSMs matching Transformers while scaling to million-token contexts. We close on finite-state compression limits in associative recall and open problems in hybrids and selective-memory theory.

## 1. Introduction

The Transformer [1] derives its power from *content-based* pairwise interactions — every token attends to every other token. This quadratic dependence on sequence length $L$ is simultaneously the architecture's strength and its binding constraint: as contexts grow from thousands to millions of tokens, the $O(L^2)$ attention matrix dominates memory and compute, motivating architectures with *linear* scaling and no loss of modeling capacity.

State space models (SSMs) are the most theoretically grounded answer. An SSM posits that observations are generated by a latent dynamical system in continuous time:

$$x'(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t), \qquad y(t) = Cx(t) + Du(t),$$

with input $u(t)$, latent state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$, and output $y(t)$. Classical in control theory, this formulation required three innovations to become a deep sequence model:

1. **HiPPO initialization** [3]: the state matrix $A$ is derived from *optimal polynomial projections* of the input history, giving the recurrence provably optimal memory and curing the vanishing-gradient pathology of classical RNNs.
2. **Structured discretization**: a zero-order-hold (ZOH) rule maps the continuous system to $\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)$, $\bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}(\exp(\Delta A) - I)\,\Delta B$, and a normal-plus-low-rank (NPLR) parameterization of $A$ makes the convolution kernel computable in $O(L \log L)$.
3. **Selectivity and hardware-aware scanning** [1]: Mamba's S6 layer makes $\Delta_t, B_t, C_t$ *input-dependent* — a time-varying system that selectively propagates or forgets — while preserving an associative operator admitting a work-efficient parallel scan fused into SRAM-resident GPU kernels.

The unifying claim of this thesis: **memory theory determines the parameterization, discretization determines the computation, and selectivity determines the expressivity**, with the parallel scan as the algorithmic glue making the time-varying system trainable at Transformer scale.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Classical recurrences and gating

RNNs update $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$ with a fixed-size state, suffering *vanishing and exploding gradients* over long horizons. LSTMs and GRUs answered with multiplicative gates — an early form of *selectivity*, learning when to remember and forget. Mamba's selection is the continuous-time, linear-algebraic descendant of gating, except the gate is derived from the discretization step of a principled dynamical system rather than heuristic sigmoids.

### 2.2 Linear attention as a degenerate SSM

Linear attention replaces softmax with a kernel feature map, $\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top)V \approx \phi(Q)(\phi(K)^\top V)$, reaching $O(L)$ complexity. But these models are linear recurrences in disguise: $S_t = S_{t-1} + \phi(k_t)v_t^\top$ is an SSM with $A = I$. With $A$ frozen at identity, the state can only accumulate — never decay or oscillate — so memory management is impoverished. Selective SSMs generalize this by making the dynamics data-dependent, recovering the full power of a time-varying linear system.

### 2.3 Structured state spaces: S4

S4 [2] made deep SSMs competitive through three contributions: the **HiPPO-LegS** matrix initializing $A$ for optimal online history approximation [3]; the **NPLR parameterization** $A = V\Lambda V^* - PQ^\top$ enabling stable diagonalization and kernel computation via the Cauchy-kernel trick in $\tilde{O}(L+N)$; and a **convolutional training mode** $y = \bar{K} * u$ with $\bar{K} = (C\bar{B}, C\bar{A}\bar{B}, \dots)$, parallelizable on GPUs. S4 averaged 80.48% on the Long Range Arena [5], solving Path-X and far exceeding efficient-Transformer baselines.

### 2.4 Mamba and structured state space duality

Mamba [1] observed that time-invariance is a *modeling* limitation: an LTI system treats all tokens identically and cannot do content-aware reasoning such as selective copying. S6 makes $\Delta$, $B$, $C$ input-dependent and replaces convolution with a **parallel associative scan**. Mamba-2 [4] then unified SSMs and attention via *structured state space duality* (SSD): the selective scan equals multiplication by a 1-semiseparable matrix, enabling block-decomposed algorithms that exploit tensor cores.

## 3. Methodology

1. **Continuous-time formulation.** We study the SSM $x'(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t)$, $y(t) = Cx(t)$ as *online history compression*: maintaining in $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the best approximation of the full input function $u|_{[0,t]}$.
2. **HiPPO initialization.** Under the LegS measure $\mu^{(t)}(x) = \frac{1}{t}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(x)$, the state dynamics implement orthogonal projection of the history onto scaled Legendre polynomials, with an optimality guarantee (Theorem 4.1).
3. **ZOH discretization and selectivity.** ZOH yields $x_t = \bar{A}x_{t-1} + \bar{B}u_t$; selectivity introduces $\Delta_t = \mathrm{softplus}(s_\Delta(x_t))$, $B_t = s_B(x_t)$, $C_t = s_C(x_t)$.
4. **Hardware-aware parallel scan.** We prove an associative binary operator on state-transition pairs, enabling a Blelloch scan, then tile it in SRAM so HBM traffic drops from $O(BLDN)$ to $O(BLD + ND)$.

We evaluate on the Long Range Arena [5], Pile perplexity and zero-shot benchmarks versus Transformers, and ablations of selectivity, state size $N$, and scan implementation.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The HiPPO theory of optimal online memory

The foundational question — *what should the hidden state remember?* — is answered by HiPPO [3] with approximation theory. Given a measure $\mu^{(t)}$ on the history $[0,t]$, the optimal $N$-dimensional summary of $u|_{[0,t]}$ is its projection onto the first $N$ orthonormal polynomials:

$$x_n(t) = \int_0^t u(s)\, g_n^{(t)}(s)\,\omega^{(t)}(s)\,ds, \qquad n = 0, \dots, N-1.$$

Differentiating in $t$ yields a *linear ODE* for $x(t)$. For the LegS measure the HiPPO matrices are:

$$A_{nk} = -\begin{cases}
(2n+1)^{1/2}(2k+1)^{1/2}, & n > k, \\
n + 1, & n = k, \\
0, & n < k,
\end{cases} \qquad B_n = (2n+1)^{1/2}.$$

> **Theorem (HiPPO-LegS optimality).** Let $u \in L^2[0,T]$ and let $x(t)$ evolve under $\dot{x} = \frac{1}{t}Ax + \frac{1}{t}Bu$ with the LegS matrices. Then $x(t)$ holds exactly the coefficients of the $L^2(\mu^{(t)})$-optimal degree-$(N-1)$ polynomial approximation of $u|_{[0,t]}$, and for $u$ with bounded $k$-th derivative, $\|u - \hat{u}_N\|_{\mu^{(t)}} = O(N^{-k})$ uniformly in $t$.

The timescale $\Delta$ sets the effective window: small $\Delta$ resolves fine detail, large $\Delta$ compresses long histories. Crucially, the LegS matrix is **normal plus low-rank** ($A = V\Lambda V^* - PQ^\top$), which is what makes S4's fast kernel computation possible [2].

### 4.2 Zero-order-hold discretization

With step $\Delta > 0$ and input held constant on $[t, t+\Delta)$ (the *zero-order hold*), the ODE's exact one-step solution is:

$$x(t+\Delta) = e^{\Delta A}x(t) + \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{A\tau}\,d\tau\right) B\, u(t),$$

giving the discrete parameters:

$$\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}\big(\exp(\Delta A) - I\big)\,\Delta B, \qquad \bar{C} = C,$$

and the recurrence $x_t = \bar{A}x_{t-1} + \bar{B}u_t$, $y_t = \bar{C}x_t$. Three properties matter:

- **Exactness.** ZOH is the *exact* discretization under the piecewise-constant assumption — unlike Euler or Tustin, it introduces no error beyond the hold.
- **Stability.** HiPPO matrices are Hurwitz, so $\rho(\bar{A}) < 1$ for all $\Delta > 0$: unconditional stability, and gradients cannot explode through the recurrence.
- **Timescale semantics.** As $\Delta \to 0$, $\bar{A} \to I + \Delta A$ (fine resolution, inputs pass through); as $\Delta \to \infty$, $\bar{A} \to 0$ (state resets each step). Mamba's selectivity exploits exactly this: input-dependent $\Delta_t$ learns *per-token timescales* — small $\Delta_t$ ignores a token, large $\Delta_t$ attends to it.

For LTI systems, unrolling gives the convolutional dual $y_t = \sum_{k=0}^{t} C\bar{A}^{\,t-k}\bar{B}u_k$, i.e. $y = \bar{K} * u$ with $\bar{K}_k = C\bar{A}^{\,k}\bar{B}$. S4 computes $\bar{K}$ in $\tilde{O}(L+N)$ via NPLR structure and convolves by FFT in $O(L \log L)$ [2] — *recurrent for inference, convolutional for training*.

### 4.3 The selective S6 mechanism

Mamba [1] makes three quantities input-dependent (per channel $d$ of $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$):

$$\Delta_t = \mathrm{softplus}\big(s_\Delta(x_t)\big) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}, \qquad B_t = s_B(x_t) \in \mathbb{R}^N, \qquad C_t = s_C(x_t) \in \mathbb{R}^N,$$

with learned linear $s_\Delta, s_B, s_C$. The matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^N$ stays input-independent (diagonal, HiPPO-initialized), since input-dependent $B_t, C_t$ already supply full selective power. The recurrence is:

$$h_t = \bar{A}_t \odot h_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t\, x_{t,d}, \qquad y_{t,d} = C_t^\top h_t,$$

with $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t \odot A)$. The gating reading is direct: since $A_n < 0$, small $\Delta_t$ gives $\bar{A}_t \approx \mathbf{1}$ (state persists, input *ignored*); large $\Delta_t$ gives $\bar{A}_t \approx 0$ (state *overwritten*). Meanwhile $B_t$ gates *what enters* the state and $C_t$ gates *what is read out* — content-based addressing inside a linear recurrence.

> **Theorem (Selectivity strictly generalizes LTI systems).** The selective recurrence with input-dependent $(\Delta_t, B_t, C_t)$ realizes every LTI SSM (take projections constant) and additionally the *selective copy* task — copying a sparse marked subset of tokens from a long context — which no LTI system with $N$ smaller than the context length can solve. The selective class is strictly more expressive at fixed $N$.

Selectivity kills the convolutional mode: with time-varying $(\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t)$ no fixed kernel exists, and naive training is $O(L)$ *sequential*. The parallel scan recovers parallelism.

### 4.4 The hardware-aware parallel scan

First-order linear recurrences admit an **associative** operator, hence a parallel prefix scan. With $e_t = (\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t x_t)$ define:

$$(a_2, b_2) \bullet (a_1, b_1) \;=\; \big(a_2 a_1,\; a_2 b_1 + b_2\big).$$

> **Theorem (Associativity of the scan operator).** $\bullet$ is associative: $(e_3 \bullet e_2) \bullet e_1 = e_3 \bullet (e_2 \bullet e_1)$. The prefix reductions $p_t = e_t \bullet \cdots \bullet e_1 = (\Phi_t, \Psi_t)$ satisfy $h_t = \Phi_t h_0 + \Psi_t$ — the scan computes *exactly* the sequential recurrence in $O(\log L)$ depth and $O(L)$ work.

*Proof sketch.* Both bracketings give first component $a_3a_2a_1$ and second component $a_3a_2b_1 + a_3b_2 + b_3$, by associativity of multiplication and distributivity; induction on $t$ gives the recurrence. ∎

Algebra alone is not enough: naively materializing the $N$-dimensional state in HBM costs $O(BLDN)$ traffic. Mamba's **hardware-aware** kernel [1] instead (i) loads $(\Delta, A, B, C)$ to SRAM — $O(BLD + ND)$; (ii) discretizes $(\bar{A}, \bar{B})$ *on-chip*; (iii) scans with the state resident in SRAM; (iv) multiplies by $C$ and writes only the $D$-dimensional output to HBM. Backward-pass states are *recomputed*, not stored. Training FLOPs match optimized attention while memory stays $O(L)$, enabling million-token contexts on one GPU. See the official `mamba-ssm` implementation [6].

```python
# Associative parallel scan for the selective recurrence.
# e_t = (A_bar[t], B_bar[t]*x[t]); (a2,b2).(a1,b1) = (a2*a1, a2*b1 + b2)
import torch

def ssm_scan(A_bar, Bx, h0=None):
    L, N = A_bar.shape
    a, b = A_bar.clone(), Bx.clone()
    stride = 1
    while stride < L:                       # Blelloch up-sweep, O(log L) depth
        a_next, b_next = a.clone(), b.clone()
        idx = torch.arange(stride, L, 2 * stride)
        a_next[idx] = a[idx] * a[idx - stride]
        b_next[idx] = a[idx] * b[idx - stride] + b[idx]
        a, b, stride = a_next, b_next, stride * 2
    return b if h0 is None else a * h0 + b   # exact sequential states
```

```rust
// Fused SRAM-tiled kernel: h[N] never leaves on-chip memory.
fn ssm_kernel_fused(delta: &[f32], a: &[f32], b: &[f32], c: &[f32], x: &[f32]) -> Vec<f32> {
    let (l, n) = (delta.len(), a.len());
    let mut h = vec![0.0f32; n];            // resident in SRAM
    let mut y = Vec::with_capacity(l);
    for t in 0..l {
        for i in 0..n {
            let a_bar = (delta[t] * a[i]).exp();   // ZOH fused on-chip
            h[i] = a_bar * h[i] + delta[t] * b[i] * x[t];
        }
        y.push(h.iter().zip(c.iter()).map(|(hi, ci)| hi * ci).sum());
    }
    y
}
```

### 4.5 Structured state space duality (Mamba-2)

Mamba-2 [4] showed selective SSMs and attention are two views of one object: unrolling gives $y = Mx$ with $M$ a **1-semiseparable** matrix, $M_{ji} = C_j^\top (\prod_{k=i+1}^{j} \bar{A}_k) \bar{B}_i$ for $j \ge i$. The SSD algorithm computes $y = Mx$ by block decomposition, exploiting rank-$N$ off-diagonal blocks and dispatching dense blocks to tensor cores. Linear-time modeling and quadratic attention thus differ only in the structure imposed on the mixing matrix.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Analysis

| Model | Training | Inference / token | Memory | Selective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer [1] | $O(L^2 D)$ | $O(LD)$ (KV cache) | $O(L^2)$ | Yes |
| Linear attention | $O(LD^2)$ | $O(D^2)$ | $O(L)$ | Weak |
| S4 (conv. mode) [2] | $O(L \log L \cdot D)$ | $O(ND)$ | $O(L)$ | No |
| Mamba S6 (scan) [1] | $O(LDN)$ | $O(ND)$ | $O(L)$ | Yes |
| Mamba-2 SSD [4] | $O(LDN)$ (tensor-core) | $O(ND)$ | $O(L)$ | Yes |

Mamba thus attains selectivity at the asymptotic cost of the efficient models.

**Long Range Arena.** S4 [2] averaged **80.48%** across the six LRA tasks [5] (lengths 1K–16K), the first model to solve Path-X, versus 62.5% for the best efficient Transformer — validating the HiPPO hypothesis that principled memory initialization, not scale, was the missing ingredient. Mamba-2 [4] adds 2–8× throughput over Mamba-1 at matched quality via SSD.

**Language modeling.** Gu and Dao [1] trained Mamba from 130M to 2.8B parameters on the Pile: (1) perplexity matches or beats strong Transformer baselines at every scale, with the gap *widening* at longer contexts; (2) generation throughput is ~5× higher, since inference needs only the $O(ND)$ recurrent state instead of a growing KV cache; (3) models trained on 2K–8K tokens generate coherently at 100K+ tokens — regimes where attention is infeasible.

**Theory versus practice.** The theory predicts *memory* (HiPPO error bounds, stability for all $\Delta > 0$) and *compute* (scan work/depth, HBM traffic) cleanly. It does not yet predict *optimization*: why selective SSMs train stably, how input-dependent $\Delta_t$ interacts with adaptive optimizers, or the joint scaling laws in $(N, D, L)$ — all empirical observations awaiting theory.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**The state compression bottleneck.** Every SSM funnels history through a fixed $N$-dimensional state — a *lossy* channel of capacity $O(N)$:

- **Associative recall.** Exact retrieval of many arbitrary earlier tokens degrades once key-value pairs exceed effective state capacity. Hybrids (Mamba + sparse attention) are the pragmatic fix, but the capacity/selectivity trade-off lacks theory.
- **Copying and counting.** Exact verbatim copying of long spans and precise counting stay weaker than attention's, since the compressed state shares capacity between content and positional addressing.

**Open problems:**

1. *Selective optimization theory.* No convergence or generalization results exist for input-dependent discretization or its interaction with Adam-style preconditioning.
2. *State dimension scaling.* $N$ sits at 16–64 while $D$ scales to thousands; whether $N$ should grow with task complexity — and at what cost to SRAM tiling — is unresolved.
3. *Hardware portability.* The scan is hand-tuned for NVIDIA SRAM hierarchies; mapping it (and SSD's tensor-core demands) to other accelerators remains open engineering.
4. *Hybrid design principles.* Empirics favor interleaved SSM/attention layers, but there is no principled account of *which* layers should be which [4].
5. *Beyond 1-semiseparable structure.* The SSD framework [4] suggests higher-order semiseparable and quasiseparable mixing matrices; the space is largely unmapped.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Selective SSMs fuse three advances into one architecture. **HiPPO** [3] supplies the memory theory: the LegS ODE keeps a provably optimal polynomial projection of history, with NPLR structure making it computable. **ZOH discretization** supplies the computational bridge: exact, unconditionally stable, with a step size $\Delta$ whose resolution-versus-compression semantics Mamba turns into a learnable selection gate. **The parallel associative scan** supplies the algorithm: $O(\log L)$-depth evaluation of the time-varying recurrence, fused into SRAM kernels holding HBM traffic at $O(BLD + ND)$. The result is S6 [1] — linear-time training, constant-state inference, content-aware memory — extended by Mamba-2's duality [4] into a unified theory of structured sequence mixing. The defining tension — finite-state compression buys linear efficiency at the price of a recall ceiling — remains the central open problem of the post-attention era.
