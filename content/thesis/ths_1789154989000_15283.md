# Privacy, Communication, and Utility in Federated Learning at Scale: Secure Aggregation, Differential-Privacy Accounting, and Personalized Optimization

## Abstract

Federated learning (FL) trains machine learning models across fleets of distributed clients — phones, hospitals, edge devices — without centralizing raw data, yet scaling this paradigm exposes a tripartite tension between *communication efficiency*, *privacy*, and *model utility* under pervasive client heterogeneity. This thesis provides a unified treatment of that tradeoff frontier. We dissect the Bonawitz et al. secure aggregation protocol — its pairwise-masking construction, Shamir-secret-shared dropout recovery, and per-client communication costs — and characterize how it composes with central differential privacy to hide even the aggregate's per-round sensitivity. We develop the differential-privacy accounting machinery (the moments accountant, Rényi differential privacy, privacy amplification by subsampling) exactly as it applies when clients are heterogeneous, participate intermittently, and hold non-IID data, emphasizing the underappreciated interaction between clipping norms, data skew, and the privacy budget. We then analyze personalized federated optimization — FedAvg, FedProx's proximal regularizer, SCAFFOLD's control variates, and the drift-correction landscape — connecting each algorithm to a distinct point on the communication–privacy–utility frontier. The thesis derives convergence and privacy bounds, tabulates empirical results from the literature, provides reference implementations in Python and TLA+, and closes with open problems: adaptive per-client privacy budgets, Byzantine robustness under secure aggregation, and accounting under correlated or adversarial subsampling.

## 1. Introduction

Federated learning, introduced by McMahan et al. [1], reframed distributed optimization around a single constraint: *the data stays on the device*. Instead of shipping data to a datacenter, the model — or its updates — circulate among a large, unreliable, heterogeneous client population. The canonical protocol, **Federated Averaging (FedAvg)**, samples a cohort of clients per round, lets each run several epochs of local SGD, and averages the resulting models. It cuts communication rounds by 10–100× relative to synchronized SGD [1], and remains the baseline against which everything is measured.

But FedAvg's empirical success masked three structural problems that only appear at scale. First, **heterogeneity**: client devices differ in compute, bandwidth, and availability (systems heterogeneity), and their data are non-identically distributed (statistical heterogeneity). Local objectives disagree; local optima drift apart; the global average of drifted locals need not be a good model [2]. Second, **privacy**: gradients and model deltas leak membership and even enable reconstruction of training samples, so the naive "server sees every update" design is a privacy sieve. Third, **personalization**: a single global model may be the wrong objective entirely when clients' distributions differ radically — per-client models that borrow strength from the population can dominate [3].

> **Theorem:** (Informal trilemma). In cross-device federated learning, no protocol simultaneously achieves (i) O(1) per-round communication, (ii) a *worst-case* per-client privacy bound competitive with central DP, and (iii) convergence rates matching the homogeneous-data regime, once clients are arbitrarily heterogeneous and dropout-prone. Every production system chooses which corner to soften.

This thesis maps the design space that trilemma generates. Our contributions are a comparative analysis of secure aggregation protocols (§4.1), a self-contained derivation of DP accounting under subsampling and heterogeneity (§4.2), a unified convergence analysis of personalized federated optimizers (§4.3), and an empirical synthesis of where the frontier actually lies (§4.4–§5).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The federated optimization problem

Consider $N$ clients with local datasets $D_i$ and local empirical risks $f_i(w) = \frac{1}{|D_i|} \sum_{(x,y) \in D_i} \ell(w; x, y)$. The global objective is the weighted sum

$$F(w) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i f_i(w), \qquad p_i = \frac{|D_i|}{\sum_j |D_j|}.$$

FedAvg [1] proceeds in rounds $t = 1, \dots, T$: the server broadcasts $w^t$ to a sampled cohort $S_t$ of $K$ clients; each client runs $E$ local SGD epochs, producing $w_i^{t+1}$; the server aggregates $w^{t+1} = \sum_{i \in S_t} \frac{p_i}{\sum_{j \in S_t} p_j} w_i^{t+1}$. The two hyperparameters that matter are the *cohort size* $K$ and the *local epoch count* $E$.

The measure of statistical heterogeneity is **gradient dissimilarity**: the extent to which $\nabla f_i(w)$ differs from $\nabla F(w)$. In the bounded-dissimilarity regime,

$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \lVert \nabla f_i(w) - \nabla F(w) \rVert^2 \leq \zeta^2,$$

FedAvg converges at $O(1/\sqrt{KTE}) + O(\zeta^2)$ — the residual $O(\zeta^2)$ term is *client drift*, and it does not vanish with more rounds [4].

### 2.2 Threat models and privacy definitions

We distinguish three privacy postures:

- **Honest-but-curious server**: follows the protocol but inspects every message. Defended by *secure aggregation* (the server learns only the sum).
- **External observer / released model**: an adversary sees only published checkpoints. Defended by *differential privacy*.
- **Malicious server or Byzantine clients**: active deviations. Defended by verifiable/robust aggregation (see §6).

Differential privacy [5] requires, for neighboring datasets $D, D'$ differing in one client's entire contribution (client-level DP) or one record (record-level DP):

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \leq e^{\varepsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta.$$

In FL, **client-level DP** is the natural unit: the adversary should not infer whether a particular device participated. The standard recipe is *DP-FedAvg*: clip each client's update to norm $C$, aggregate, and add Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 C^2)$ scaled to the cohort size.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology combines (i) protocol analysis of secure aggregation, with exact communication accounting; (ii) derivation of privacy composition under Poisson and fixed-size subsampling with Rényi DP; and (iii) a unified convergence framework for drift-corrected federated optimizers, evaluated against the published empirical record. We ground claims in reference implementations: a Python prototype of the masking and accounting machinery, and a TLA+ specification sketch of the aggregation round's dropout handling.

The following Python snippet implements the DP accounting core — clipped per-client updates with subsampled Gaussian mechanism and RDP composition:

```python
import numpy as np

def clip_update(delta: np.ndarray, C: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Clip client update to l2 norm C (per-client sensitivity bound)."""
    norm = np.linalg.norm(delta)
    return delta * min(1.0, C / (norm + 1e-12))

def rdp_gaussian_subsampled(q: float, sigma: float, orders) -> np.ndarray:
    """RDP of subsampled Gaussian mechanism, order-by-order upper bound.
    q: client sampling rate, sigma: noise multiplier."""
    # Mironov et al. bound: epsilon(alpha) <= (1/(alpha-1)) * log A_alpha,
    # A_alpha = E_z[( (1-q) + q*exp((2z-1)/(2 sigma^2)) )^alpha], z ~ N(0,1)
    eps = np.zeros(len(orders))
    for j, a in enumerate(orders):
        z = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(2_000_000)
        A = np.mean(((1 - q) + q * np.exp((2 * z - 1) / (2 * sigma ** 2))) ** a)
        eps[j] = np.log(A) / (a - 1)
    return eps

# Example: q=0.01 cohort sampling, sigma=1.1, T=1000 rounds
orders = np.arange(2, 65)
rdp = T * rdp_gaussian_subsampled(q=0.01, sigma=1.1, orders=orders)
# convert to (eps, delta): eps = min_a rdp[a] + log(1/delta)/(a-1)
```

The snippet illustrates the three levers of the frontier: **$C$** (utility vs. bias), **$\sigma$** (privacy vs. utility), and **$q$** (communication cost vs. privacy amplification).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Secure aggregation protocols: hiding the summands

The Bonawitz et al. protocol [3] lets a server compute $\sum_i x_i$ for high-dimensional client vectors $x_i$ while learning *nothing* about any individual $x_i$ (in the honest-but-curious model). Its construction has three moving parts:

1. **Pairwise masking.** Every pair of clients $(u, v)$ agrees on a shared seed $s_{u,v}$ via Diffie–Hellman key agreement. Client $u$ adds $+\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v})$ and client $v$ adds $-\mathrm{PRG}(s_{u,v})$, so masks cancel exactly in the sum.
2. **Self masking.** Each client adds a private mask $b_u$ to protect against a server that colludes with all-but-one clients; $b_u$ is shared via Shamir secret sharing.
3. **Dropout recovery.** If client $u$ drops after advertising its public key but before submitting, surviving clients reveal shares of $u$'s secrets so the server can subtract $u$'s mask — without revealing $u$'s input.

> **Theorem:** (Bonawitz et al. [3], informal). The protocol is secure against an honest-but-curious server and any coalition of fewer than $t$ malicious clients, tolerating arbitrary dropout patterns, provided at least $t$ honest clients survive to the reconstruction phase. Communication per client is $O(n + d)$ for $n$ clients and dimension $d$ (dominated by the $d$ masked values and $O(n)$ key material).

The measured overhead is modest: for 16-bit values, communication expands by only $1.73\times$ for $2^{10}$ clients with $2^{20}$-dimensional vectors [3]. This matters because it means privacy-against-the-server costs roughly a factor of two in bandwidth — cheap relative to the order-of-magnitude DP noise costs below. Later refinements (SecAgg+, TurboAgg, LightSecAgg) reduce the $O(n^2)$ pairwise key-exchange term, but the qualitative lesson stands: **secure aggregation moves the trust boundary from the server to the aggregate**, enabling *distributed* DP (noise added by clients) or *central* DP (noise added by the server to a sum it cannot inspect per-client).

A minimal TLA+ sketch of the dropout-recovery round structure captures the protocol's phase discipline:

```tla
---- MODULE SecAggRound ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Clients, Threshold
VARIABLES phase, alive, revealed

Init == phase = "advertise" /\ alive = Clients /\ revealed = {}

Advertise == phase = "advertise" /\ phase' = "mask"
    (* every alive client publishes DH public key *)

Drop(c) == c \in alive /\ alive' = alive \ {c} /\ UNCHANGED <<phase, revealed>>

SubmitMasked == phase = "mask" /\ phase' = "recover"
    (* alive clients submit pairwise-masked vectors *)

Recover == phase = "recover" /\ Cardinality(alive) >= Threshold
    /\ revealed' = revealed \cup (Clients \ alive)
    /\ phase' = "done"
    (* server reconstructs masks of dropped clients only *)
====
```

### 4.2 Differential-privacy accounting under client heterogeneity and subsampling

The moments accountant [5] — now standardly expressed as Rényi DP — is what makes DP training of deep models feasible: it composes the subsampled Gaussian mechanism tightly across thousands of rounds. For a single round with sampling rate $q$ and noise multiplier $\sigma$, the RDP of order $\alpha > 1$ satisfies

$$\varepsilon(\alpha) \leq \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \log A_\alpha, \quad A_\alpha = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\left((1-q) + q\, e^{(2z-1)/(2\sigma^2)}\right)^\alpha\right],$$

and over $T$ rounds, RDP composes additively: $\varepsilon_{\text{total}}(\alpha) = T \cdot \varepsilon(\alpha)$, convertible to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP via $\varepsilon = \min_\alpha \left[\varepsilon_{\text{total}}(\alpha) + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1}\right]$.

**Privacy amplification by subsampling** is the engine: with $q = K/N = 0.01$, the effective per-round privacy cost is roughly $O(q^2)$ rather than $O(q)$, a quadratic saving that is precisely why cross-device FL (huge $N$, small $K$) is the *natural* habitat of DP [6].

Heterogeneity warps every parameter of this accounting:

- **Clipping norm $C$ vs. data skew.** Clipping bounds per-client sensitivity but *biases* updates toward the majority distribution: clients with rare data produce large updates that get clipped hardest, so DP noise disproportionately degrades minority clients' influence — a fairness–privacy coupling rarely made explicit.
- **Intermittent participation.** Privacy accounting assumes the sampled cohort is uniform; in practice, available clients are a biased, time-varying subset (diurnal patterns, charging state). Correlated availability *breaks* the amplification argument, and worst-case accounting must fall back to the no-amplification bound — potentially 100× worse at $q = 0.01$.
- **Adaptive clipping.** Rather than fixing $C$, estimating a quantile of update norms each round (itself done privately) recovers utility under skew, at the cost of spending part of the budget on the quantile estimate [6].

| Accounting method | Composition | Subsampling | Heterogeneity-aware? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic/advanced composition | $O(\sqrt{T})$ | no | no |
| Moments accountant [5] | tight numerical | yes | partially (via $C$) |
| RDP + Poisson subsampling | tight analytic | yes | partially |
| DP-SCAFFOLD [6] | RDP + control-variate sensitivity | yes | yes (drift-corrected) |

### 4.3 Personalized federated optimization: FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD

Client drift is the central pathology of heterogeneous FL: with non-IID data, each client's local optimum differs, and averaging $E$ steps of drifted local SGD converges to a *biased* stationary point — or diverges. Three generations of fixes:

**FedAvg [1]** is the uncorrected baseline: local SGD + periodic averaging. Its convergence carries the irreducible $O(\zeta^2)$ drift term (§2.1). It is communication-minimal but heterogeneity-fragile.

**FedProx [2]** adds a proximal term to each client's local objective:

$$\min_w \; f_i(w) + \frac{\mu}{2} \lVert w - w^t \rVert^2,$$

anchoring local updates to the global model. The $\mu$ knob interpolates between FedAvg ($\mu = 0$) and fully conservative local steps; Li et al. prove convergence under a *dissimilarity* assumption strictly weaker than the bounded-gradient assumptions FedAvg analyses required, and report +22% absolute accuracy over FedAvg in highly heterogeneous regimes [2]. FedProx also tolerates *systems* heterogeneity: stragglers perform fewer local epochs (partial work), and the proximal term keeps their partial updates safe to include.

**SCAFFOLD [4]** attacks drift directly with control variates. Each client maintains $c_i$ estimating its local gradient direction at the optimum; the server maintains the average $c$. Local updates become

$$y_i \leftarrow y_i - \eta_\ell \left(\nabla f_i(y_i) - c_i + c\right),$$

so that at the optimum, where $c_i \to \nabla f_i(w^\star)$, the correction $(\nabla f_i - c_i + c)$ has *zero* client variance. Karimireddy et al. prove SCAFFOLD is unaffected by heterogeneity or client sampling, needs strictly fewer communication rounds than FedAvg, and — for quadratics — provably *exploits* client similarity, the first result quantifying the value of local steps [4].

**DP-SCAFFOLD [6]** closes the loop: Noble, Bellet, and Dieuleveut privatize SCAFFOLD, showing control variates can be estimated under DP with a warm-start phase, and deriving RDP accounting whose sensitivity analysis accounts for the variates. The lesson: drift correction and privacy are not orthogonal — correcting drift *reduces* the sensitivity the DP mechanism must cover, buying back utility.

In Haskell-flavored pseudocode, the three algorithms share one skeleton:

```haskell
-- Unified local step: FedAvg = noCorr, FedProx = proxCorr, SCAFFOLD = variateCorr
localStep :: Correction -> Model -> ClientData -> Model
localStep corr w d = foldl update w (batches d)
  where update y b = y - eta * (grad b y + corr y)

data Correction = NoCorr
               | ProxCorr Double Model        -- mu, global model
               | VariateCorr GradVar GradVar  -- c_i, c
```

### 4.4 The communication–privacy–utility frontier

We can now state the frontier quantitatively. Fix target excess risk $\Delta$ and privacy $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ over $T$ rounds with cohort size $K$:

1. **Communication $\propto K \cdot d \cdot T$.** More clients per round and more rounds cost bandwidth; secure aggregation multiplies by ~2× [3].
2. **Privacy noise $\propto \sigma C / K$.** Noise added to the aggregate scales as $1/K$ — larger cohorts are *quieter* — but larger $K$ weakens amplification ($q = K/N$ rises). The optimum $K$ balances the two.
3. **Heterogeneity tax.** Without drift correction, the attainable $\Delta$ floors at $O(\zeta^2)$ regardless of $T$; SCAFFOLD/FedProx convert the tax from a floor into a slower rate.

Empirically (compiled from [1, 2, 4, 6]):

| Method | Handles drift? | DP-compatible | Extra comm/round | Heterogeneous accuracy vs FedAvg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg [1] | no | yes (DP-FedAvg) | 1× | baseline |
| FedProx [2] | partially (proximal) | yes | 1× | +22% (high heterogeneity) |
| SCAFFOLD [4] | yes (variates) | yes (DP-SCAFFOLD [6]) | 2× (variates up/down) | fewer rounds to target |
| + SecAgg [3] | orthogonal | yes | ~1.7–2× | unchanged (exact sum) |

The Pareto frontier's shape: adding secure aggregation costs bandwidth but *no* utility; adding DP costs utility unless cohorts grow; adding drift correction costs bandwidth but buys utility under skew. The system designer's job is to pick the operating point — and the accounting in §4.2 is what prices each choice.

## 5. Empirical Evidence and Proofs

**Convergence sketch (SCAFFOLD, strongly convex).** Under $\mu$-strong convexity and $L$-smoothness of each $f_i$, with bounded stochastic gradient variance $\sigma^2$, SCAFFOLD with appropriate $\eta_\ell, \eta_g$ satisfies

$$\mathbb{E}[F(\bar w^T)] - F(w^\star) \leq \tilde O\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu K T E} + \frac{L}{\mu} e^{-\mu \eta T}\right),$$

with *no* $\zeta^2$ residual — the control variates absorb heterogeneity into a transient term that decays as the variates converge [4]. Contrast FedAvg's $O(\zeta^2)$ floor: this is the precise sense in which drift correction moves the frontier.

**Privacy proof sketch (DP-FedAvg).** Each round: cohort $S_t$ sampled with rate $q$; per-client update clipped to $C$ (sensitivity $C$ per client); Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 C^2 I)$ added to the mean. The subsampled Gaussian mechanism's RDP bound (§4.2) composes over $T$ rounds; converting at the optimal $\alpha$ yields the final $(\varepsilon, \delta)$. Secure aggregation *does not change the accounting* — it only changes who adds the noise (server vs. clients) and what the server observes — but it upgrades the guarantee from "the server is trusted with individual updates" to "the guarantee holds even against the server" [3, 6].

**Representative empirical results** (as reported in the cited works, EMNIST/Shakespeare/StackOverflow-style federated benchmarks):

1. FedAvg reaches target accuracy in 10–100× fewer rounds than large-batch synchronous SGD [1].
2. FedProx improves absolute test accuracy by 22% on average over FedAvg across five heterogeneous datasets, with the gap largest under 90% straggler rates [2].
3. SCAFFOLD halves communication rounds to a fixed accuracy target on non-IID EMNIST relative to FedAvg and is insensitive to client sampling rates [4].
4. DP-SCAFFOLD matches non-private SCAFFOLD utility at $(\varepsilon, \delta) = (3, 10^{-5})$ on heterogeneous benchmarks where DP-FedAvg degrades by >5 points, demonstrating that drift correction and privacy compose synergistically [6].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Accounting assumes honest sampling.** Real client availability is correlated (time zones, charging), which invalidates the i.i.d. subsampling assumption behind privacy amplification. Worst-case bounds without amplification are the only rigorous fallback, and they are devastating at small $q$.
2. **Secure aggregation vs. robustness.** SecAgg hides individual updates — which also blinds the server to *Byzantine* updates. Robust aggregators (Krum, trimmed mean) need per-client inspection; reconciling cryptographic privacy with Byzantine robustness remains largely open, with only early verifiable-aggregation constructions.
3. **Control-variate statefulness.** SCAFFOLD requires per-client state ($c_i$), which breaks down when the client population is effectively infinite and each client participates once — the canonical cross-device regime. Stateless drift correction is an active research area.
4. **Fairness under clipping.** DP clipping disproportionately silences minority-distribution clients (§4.2); the privacy–fairness interaction in FL lacks a satisfactory theoretical treatment.
5. **Personalization objective ambiguity.** This thesis treats personalization as drift-corrected global optimization; fully personalized formulations (per-client models with regularization toward the global) change the objective and are not covered in depth.

## 7. Conclusion

Federated learning at scale is governed by a three-way negotiation between communication, privacy, and utility, mediated by heterogeneity. Secure aggregation [3] removes the server from the trust base at ~2× bandwidth cost; Rényi-DP accounting with subsampling amplification [5, 6] prices privacy tightly but rests on sampling assumptions that deployment routinely violates; and drift-corrected optimizers — FedProx's proximal anchoring [2], SCAFFOLD's control variates [4] — convert heterogeneity from a convergence floor into a manageable rate penalty, while composing synergistically with DP [6]. The frontier is not a single curve but a surface parameterized by cohort size, clipping norm, noise multiplier, and drift-correction strength; navigating it requires the accounting machinery of §4.2 as a matter of engineering routine, not specialist craft. The most pressing open problems — correlated-availability accounting, privacy-preserving Byzantine robustness, and stateless drift correction — are where the next order-of-magnitude improvements will come from.
