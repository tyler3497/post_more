# Geometric Complexity Theory: Representation-Theoretic Obstructions and the Permanent-versus-Determinant Problem

## Abstract

Geometric complexity theory (GCT) [1][3] attacks Valiant's algebraic P-versus-NP problem — whether the permanent admits polynomial-size determinantal representations — by comparing orbit closures of the padded permanent and the determinant under the general linear group. Their coordinate rings are compared as $GL_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$-modules; a partition whose irreducible module occurs in one ring but not the other is an *occurrence obstruction*. We survey the program: its algebro-geometric encoding of VP versus VNP [1][13], reduction of obstructions to Kronecker and plethysm positivity [2][4], the no-go theorems ruling out occurrence obstructions [2][4], the shifted-partial-derivative barrier [10], and the padding-free matrix-powering reformulation [7][8], concluding that GCT has transformed the permanent-versus-determinant problem into a central question at the interface of complexity theory and representation theory.

## 1 Introduction

In 1979, Leslie Valiant introduced algebraic analogues of P and NP — **VP** (polynomial families computable by polynomial-size circuits of polynomial degree) and **VNP** (its nondeterministic counterpart) — and conjectured that the permanent family

$$\mathrm{perm}_m(X) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_m} \prod_{i=1}^{m} x_{i,\sigma(i)}$$

does not belong to VP. The *determinantal complexity* $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m)$ — the smallest $n$ such that $\mathrm{perm}_m$ can be expressed as $\det_n(A(X))$ for a matrix $A$ of affine linear forms — is polynomially related to circuit size: Valiant showed that every polynomial computed by a circuit of size $s$ is a projection of a determinant of size quasipolynomial in $s$, and that the determinant itself is universal for formulas [13]. Valiant's hypothesis may therefore be stated crisply: $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m)$ grows superpolynomially in $m$.

For decades, progress was confined to restricted models — monotone, bounded-depth, and multilinear circuits [13] — whose techniques showed no sign of generalizing. In 2001, Ketan Mulmuley and Milind Sohoni proposed *geometric complexity theory* (GCT), importing algebraic geometry, geometric invariant theory, and representation theory into complexity [1]. Rather than analyzing circuits combinatorially, GCT exploits the *symmetries* of the determinant and permanent, converting the lower bound into a question about decomposing coordinate rings of orbit closures into irreducible $GL$-modules. Mulmuley argued this approach evades the relativization, algebrization, and natural-proofs barriers that stymied Boolean approaches [3].

This thesis presents a systematic account of the GCT program as it applies to the permanent-versus-determinant problem. We describe the orbit-closure formulation and its stabilizer analysis (§2), the obstruction methodology and the "flip" (§3), the representation-theoretic core connecting obstructions to Kronecker and plethysm coefficients (§4), the landscape of proved results and no-go theorems (§5), and the limitations that currently constrain the program (§6). GCT's most celebrated achievements to date are *negative* — rigorous proofs that its own flagship strategy, the search for occurrence obstructions, cannot succeed [2][4][8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Valiant's classes and determinantal complexity

Let $\mathbb{C}$ denote the complex numbers. A *p-family* is a sequence $(f_m)$ of polynomials with $f_m$ in $m^{O(1)}$ variables of degree $m^{O(1)}$. The class **VP** consists of p-families computed by arithmetic circuits of polynomial size; **VNP** consists of p-families $f_m(x) = \sum_{e \in \{0,1\}^{p(m)}} g_m(x, e)$ where $(g_m)$ is in VP. The permanent is *VNP-complete* under p-projections, while the determinant lies in VP — indeed, in the formula-computable subclass $VP_{ws}$.

The *determinantal complexity* $dc(f)$ is the minimal $n$ such that $f = \det_n \circ A$ for an affine linear map $A$. Valiant's hypothesis becomes:

> **Valiant's Hypothesis (VP $\neq$ VNP).** $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m) = m^{\omega(1)}$.

The best unconditional lower bound remains the quadratic $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m) \ge m^2/2$ of Mignon and Ressayre, proved via the Hessian rank at a singular point — a differential-geometric invariant [13]. GCT replaces such analytic invariants with representation-theoretic ones.

### 2.2 Padding and orbit closures

Algebraic geometry prefers homogeneous polynomials, so Mulmuley and Sohoni homogenize via a padding variable $\ell$ [1]:

$$f_{m,n} = \ell^{\,n-m}\,\mathrm{perm}_m \in S^n(\mathbb{C}^{n^2 \oplus 1})^*.$$

Let $V = \mathbb{C}^{n^2}$ and $W = V \oplus \mathbb{C}$. The group $G = GL(W)$ acts on the space of degree-$n$ forms. The *orbit closures*

$$\mathcal{D}et_n = \overline{G \cdot \det_n}, \qquad \mathcal{P}erm_{m,n} = \overline{G \cdot \ell^{n-m}\mathrm{perm}_m}$$

are projective varieties. The fundamental reduction of GCT is:

> **Theorem:** (Mulmuley–Sohoni [1]) If $\ell^{n-m}\mathrm{perm}_m \notin \mathcal{D}et_n$ for $n = m^{O(1)}$, then $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m)$ is superpolynomial in $m$.

Thus a complexity lower bound follows from separating two algebraic varieties — a purely geometric statement. The *border determinantal complexity* $\underline{dc}(\mathrm{perm}_m)$ is the smallest $n$ with $\ell^{n-m}\mathrm{perm}_m \in \mathcal{D}et_n$; the orbit-closure formulation targets precisely this border quantity [14].

### 2.3 Stabilizers and the comparison of coordinate rings

The symmetries of the two polynomials are their stabilizer subgroups. The stabilizer of $\det_n$ in $GL(V)$ is generated by $X \mapsto AXB$ with $\det A \det B = 1$ together with transposition — essentially $(SL_n \times SL_n) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$. The stabilizer of $\mathrm{perm}_m$ is the wreath product $(S_m \times S_m) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$ (row/column permutations plus transpose), extended by a diagonal torus acting by row and column scalings [13][14].

| Invariant | $\det_n$ | $\mathrm{perm}_m$ (padded) |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous stabilizer | $(SL_n \times SL_n) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$ | diagonal torus $\times$ discrete group |
| Discrete symmetry | transpose | $(S_m \times S_m) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$ |
| Degree / variables | $n$ / $n^2$ | $n$ / $m^2 + 1$ (padded) |
| Complexity class | $VP_{ws}$-complete | VNP-complete |

Because the orbit closures are $G$-varieties, their homogeneous coordinate rings decompose into irreducibles $S^\lambda W^*$ indexed by partitions $\lambda$. An *obstruction* is representation-theoretic data distinguishing the two rings: most ambitiously, an *occurrence obstruction* — a partition $\lambda$ with

$$\mathrm{mult}\big(S^\lambda W^*, \mathbb{C}[\mathcal{P}erm_{m,n}]_d\big) > 0 = \mathrm{mult}\big(S^\lambda W^*, \mathbb{C}[\mathcal{D}et_n]_d\big)$$

for some degree $d$ [4]. Such a $\lambda$ witnesses the non-containment $\mathcal{P}erm_{m,n} \not\subseteq \mathcal{D}et_n$ and hence a lower bound on border determinantal complexity.

## 3 Methodology

The GCT methodology proceeds in four stages, translating the problem into increasingly algebraic language [1][3][13].

1. **Geometrization.** Replace the circuit lower bound by the orbit-closure non-containment $\ell^{n-m}\mathrm{perm}_m \notin \overline{End(W)\cdot \det_n}$.
2. **Representation-theoretic reduction.** Separate the coordinate rings by exhibiting an occurrence (or, more generally, *multiplicity*) obstruction: a partition $\lambda$ with differing multiplicities in the two rings.
3. **Combinatorialization.** Express the multiplicities through classical quantities — *plethysm coefficients* for the determinant side, *Kronecker coefficients* for the permanent side — reducing the obstruction search to positivity questions in algebraic combinatorics [2][4].
4. **The flip.** Transform the obstruction from a mere existence statement into an *explicit* family: short (polynomial-size description) and easy to verify (polynomial-time checkable). Mulmuley's "flip theorems" assert that crossing this *complexity barrier* — making obstructions explicit — is equivalent in difficulty to the original lower bound [3].

A crucial claim of the program is *barrier evasion*: because obstructions are defined via group actions and invariant theory rather than via properties of truth tables, the Razborov–Rudich natural-proofs barrier — which defeats "large, constructive" properties — does not apply as stated; the relevant properties are *not* large in the natural-proofs sense, being indexed by the tiny (polynomial-size) set of partitions of bounded size [3]. (Algebraic analogues of natural proofs, developed later by Forbes, Shpilka, and Volk, pose a refined challenge; see §6.)

The multiplicity comparison admits an elegant computational formulation. For the determinant orbit closure, the multiplicity of $S^\lambda W^*$ in degree $d$ is a *plethysm coefficient* $a_\lambda(d,n)$, the multiplicity of the $GL(W)$-module $S^\lambda W$ in the plethysm $S^d(S^n W^*)$. For the padded permanent, the corresponding multiplicities are controlled by *Kronecker coefficients* $g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)$ of the symmetric group — the structure constants of tensor products of Specht modules — for rectangular partitions. The obstruction problem thus becomes [4][6]:

> **Obstruction Problem.** Find a partition $\lambda$ with $a_\lambda(d,n) = 0$ but the corresponding Kronecker-theoretic multiplicity positive.

We may encode partitions and their multiplicities in a dependently typed style to emphasize their combinatorial character:

```haskell
-- Partitions index irreducible GL- and S_n-modules
type Partition = [Int]  -- weakly decreasing, e.g. [4,2,2]

-- Kronecker coefficient: multiplicity of S^λ in S^μ ⊗ S^ν
kronecker :: Partition -> Partition -> Partition -> Integer
kronecker lam mu nu = mult (specht lam) (tensor (specht mu) (specht nu))

-- Plethysm coefficient: multiplicity of S^λW in S^d(S^n W*)
plethysm :: Partition -> Int -> Int -> Integer
plethysm lam d n = mult (schur lam) (compose (schur [d]) (schur [n]))

-- Occurrence obstruction: plethysm vanishes, Kronecker does not
isOccurrenceObstruction :: Partition -> Int -> Int -> Bool
isOccurrenceObstruction lam d n =
  plethysm lam d n == 0 && kronecker lam rect rect > 0
  where rect = replicate (n - m) [n]  -- padding rectangle
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Orbit closures, padding, and why the comparison is hard

The padding factor $\ell^{n-m}$ is both the enabler and the Achilles' heel of classical GCT. Geometrically, padding embeds the $m^2$-variable permanent into the $n^2$-variable ambient space of the determinant, but it distorts the representation theory: the orbit of $\ell^{n-m}\mathrm{perm}_m$ is governed by a *parabolic* subgroup, and its coordinate ring multiplicities become entangled with the combinatorics of the padding rectangle. Kadish and Landsberg showed that padded polynomials exhibit pathologies complicating multiplicity computations [11].

The barriers of Efremenko–Landsberg–Schenck–Weyman against *shifted partial derivatives*, and of Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova against occurrence obstructions, both exploit padding essentially [8][10] — raising the question of whether padding is intrinsic or an artifact.

### 4.2 The matrix-powering reformulation

A breakthrough came from Nisan's classical homogenization: the determinant can be replaced, for the purposes of VP versus VNP, by the *trace of a matrix power*, $\mathrm{tr}(X^n)$. Gesmundo, Ikenmeyer, and Panova developed a homogeneous, padding-free GCT in which the determinant is replaced by this iterated matrix product [7][8]. The new formulation is cleaner — padding disappears entirely — but the representation theory changes radically: unlike the determinant, $\mathrm{tr}(X^n)$ is *not* characterized by its stabilizer, so the stabilizer-based multiplicity analysis must be rebuilt from scratch [8].

Strikingly, the no-go results *strengthen* here: Gesmundo and Ikenmeyer proved that in the homogeneous formulation no orbit occurrence obstructions exist proving even *superlinear* lower bounds on the permanent — the obstruction strategy fails for structural reasons, not a technicality of padding [8].

### 4.3 Kronecker coefficients and the Ikenmeyer–Panova barrier

The permanent side of the obstruction problem reduces to *rectangular Kronecker coefficients*: for partitions $\lambda$ and the rectangle $\mu = \nu = (n^m)$ (in the padded model), the question is whether $g(\lambda, \mu, \mu) = 0$ can occur while the plethysm coefficient vanishes. Kronecker coefficients lack a positive combinatorial formula despite 80 years of effort [6], yet Ikenmeyer and Panova proved the *relevant* rectangular Kronecker coefficients are always positive when the rectangle's side lengths are at least quadratic in the partition's length [2]:

> **Theorem:** (Ikenmeyer–Panova [2]) In the GCT program, the vanishing of rectangular Kronecker coefficients cannot be used to prove superpolynomial determinantal complexity lower bounds for the permanent.

They also showed the saturation of the rectangular Kronecker semigroup is trivial and classified Manivel's rectangular limit Kronecker coefficients — results of independent interest illustrating how complexity questions drive progress in pure combinatorics [2][6].

A computational perspective on these positivity questions can be sketched as follows:

```python
from sympy.combinatorics.tensor import KroneckerProduct  # illustrative only
import itertools

def rectangular_partition(n: int, m: int) -> list[int]:
    """The n x m rectangle indexing the padded permanent's multiplicities."""
    return [n] * m

def obstruction_search(n: int, m: int, max_size: int):
    """Naive search for a vanishing rectangular Kronecker coefficient.
    In practice: #P-hard in general; the GCT-relevant range is the
    stable regime where Ikenmeyer-Panova prove positivity [2]."""
    rect = rectangular_partition(n, m)
    for lam in partitions_upto(max_size):
        if kronecker(lam, rect, rect) == 0:
            return lam  # candidate obstruction partition
    return None
```

### 4.4 No occurrence obstructions: the Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova theorem

The determinant side reduces to *plethysm coefficients*. Bürgisser, Ikenmeyer, and Panova proved positivity of the relevant plethysm coefficients, establishing the landmark negative result [4]:

> **Theorem:** (Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova [4]) There are no occurrence obstructions in geometric complexity theory: for the permanent-versus-determinant problem, no irreducible $GL_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$-module occurs in the coordinate ring of the padded permanent's orbit closure but not in that of the determinant's, for superpolynomial $n(m)$.

> **Proof:** (Sketch) The determinant-side multiplicities are plethysm coefficients $a_\lambda(d,n)$; the permanent-side ones are controlled by symmetric Kronecker coefficients. Via semigroup methods and analysis of the padding rectangle, the authors show that whenever the permanent-side multiplicity is nonzero, the plethysm coefficient is also nonzero in the relevant degree range — the putative "zero" never materializes [4]. ∎

This theorem *disproved* one of the main conjectures of Mulmuley and Sohoni [1][4] and demonstrated that the permanent-versus-determinant problem is harder than the GCT program initially anticipated [6].

The obstruction data can also be viewed through the lens of formal verification, where the orbit-closure separation becomes a reachability-like property:

```tla+
---- MODULE GCTObstruction ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS N, M, D  \* ambient dimension, permanent size, degree
VARIABLES lambda   \* candidate obstruction partition

IsPartition(p) == \A i \in 1..(Len(p)-1) : p[i] >= p[i+1]
PlethysmMult(p) \in Nat      \* a_p(d,n): multiplicity in det orbit closure
KroneckerMult(p) \in Nat     \* multiplicity in padded-perm orbit closure

IsOccurrenceObstruction ==
  /\ IsPartition(lambda)
  /\ PlethysmMult(lambda) = 0
  /\ KroneckerMult(lambda) > 0

\* BIP19 theorem: the obstruction state is unreachable
THEOREM NoObstruction == []~IsOccurrenceObstruction
====
```

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We now consolidate the proved landscape — the "empirical" content of the program, i.e., its theorems — into a single picture.

| Result | Authors | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occurrence obstructions | Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova [4] | None exist for superpolynomial $n(m)$ | Proved (JAMS 2019) |
| Rectangular Kronecker vanishing | Ikenmeyer–Panova [2] | Cannot yield superpolynomial bounds | Proved (Adv. Math. 2017) |
| Homogeneous no-go | Gesmundo–Ikenmeyer [8] | No orbit obstructions even for superlinear bounds | Proved (arXiv 2016) |
| Shifted partials barrier | Efremenko–Landsberg–Schenck–Weyman [10] | Padding defeats shifted-partials method | Proved (2016) |
| Quadratic lower bound | Mignon–Ressayre [13] | $dc(\mathrm{perm}_m) \ge m^2/2$ | Proved |
| Depth-3 chasm | Mulmuley [1] | Exponential bounds reduce to depth-3 circuits | Proved (GCT IV) |
| Border vs. exact complexity | BGG–GCT program [14] | New invariants via minimal free resolutions | Active research |

The pattern is clear: every concrete obstruction strategy has met a rigorous barrier, yet each redirected the program — toward Kronecker positivity theorems [2], padding-free formulations [8], and new invariants such as the Betti numbers of permanental ideals [14].

A systems-level view of the multiplicity pipeline — the computational core of any future obstruction search — can be expressed as:

```rust
/// Multiplicity of S^λ in the degree-d piece of an orbit closure's
/// coordinate ring, dispatched by the underlying polynomial family.
fn multiplicity(lambda: &Partition, family: Family, d: usize, n: usize) -> u64 {
    match family {
        Family::Determinant => plethysm_coefficient(lambda, d, n),
        Family::PaddedPermanent { m } =>
            symmetric_kronecker(lambda, &rectangle(n, m)),
        Family::MatrixPower => {
            // stabilizer not characterizing: fall back to
            // highest-weight-vector enumeration [8]
            highest_weight_multiplicity(lambda, d, n)
        }
    }
}
```

## 6 Limitations

**The no-go theorems are genuine limitations.** The results of [2][4] rule out the *entire class* of occurrence obstructions — the flagship strategy of GCT1 — not merely particular candidates. Future lower bounds need strictly finer invariants: *multiplicity obstructions* (differing positive multiplicities), requiring exact coefficient values rather than mere vanishing, or entirely new geometric invariants.

**Padding remains poorly understood.** The matrix-powering reformulation removes padding [7][8], but its representation theory is in some ways harder — the trace of a matrix power is not determined by its stabilizer — and the no-go results persist. The BGG–GCT syzygy program has so far yielded results only in small cases [14].

**The flip is unproven.** Mulmuley's flip theorems remain conjectural [3]; without the flip, GCT offers no general mechanism converting non-constructive multiplicity arguments into explicit certificates.

**Algebraic natural proofs.** The Forbes–Shpilka–Volk theory shows that many algebraic techniques admit natural-proofs-style barriers; whether GCT's invariants can be made explicit enough to be useful yet inexplicit enough to evade this barrier is the central tension of the program's next phase.

**Scale of the undertaking.** Mulmuley himself estimated the program might take a century to settle P versus NP [9]; the representation-theoretic questions it generates — positivity of Kronecker and plethysm coefficients, the structure of orbit closures — are profound independent of their complexity-theoretic motivation, which is both the program's greatest strength and a sign of its difficulty.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Geometric complexity theory began as an audacious proposal to prove VP $\neq$ VNP using algebraic geometry and representation theory [1][3]. A quarter-century on, the program has not produced the desired lower bound, and its prominent conjectures about occurrence obstructions have been *refuted* by the researchers it inspired [2][4]. Yet GCT translated an intractable combinatorial problem into precise questions about Kronecker coefficients, plethysm coefficients, and orbit closures; answering them — even negatively — generated new theorems [2], new formulations [7][8], new barriers [10], and new invariants [14].

The permanent-versus-determinant problem remains open, but it is no longer merely a question about circuits: it is about the geometry of orbit closures, the combinatorics of partitions, and the limits of explicit construction.
