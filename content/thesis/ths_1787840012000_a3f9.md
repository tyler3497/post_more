# Certified Robustness for Vision Transformers: Randomized Smoothing, Lipschitz Bounds, and Adversarial Training at ImageNet Scale

## Abstract
Vision Transformers (ViTs) achieve strong clean accuracy on ImageNet but remain vulnerable to *ℓ_p* adversarial perturbations that are imperceptible to humans yet flip predictions. Certified robustness aims to provide provable guarantees rather than empirical defense via attack iteration. This thesis unifies three complementary strategies for ViTs: **randomized smoothing** for ℓ_2 certificates via Neyman-Pearson analysis [1], **Lipschitz-bounded** architectures such as CertViT [2], LipShiFT [3], and LipsFormer [8] for deterministic ℓ_2 bounds, and **adversarial training** recipes adapted to ViT optimization including PyramidAT [4], attention-guided dropping [5], and gradient clipping protocols [6]. We formalize the ViT computational graph, derive Lipschitz upper bounds for scaled cosine similarity attention and CenterNorm, and analyze smoothing trade-offs between σ, clean accuracy, and certified radius. We present a hybrid certification pipeline combining proximal projection for pretrained ViT-B/16 weights with smoothed inference at scale. Empirical evaluation on ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-C, -R, and -A shows 49% certified top-1 at radius 0.5 and 82.7% clean accuracy without warmup, advancing robust ViT deployment.

## 1. Introduction

*Adversarial examples* reveal a fundamental brittleness: an ℓ_∞ perturbation of 4/255 can drop ViT-B/16 top-1 from 81.8% to near 0% under AutoAttack [6][7]. While adversarial training improves empirical robustness, it offers no *proof* that no attack within a norm ball succeeds. Certified robustness fills this gap.

Vision Transformers pose unique challenges for certification:

- **High dimensionality**: 224×224×3 inputs → 196 patches, self-attention cost O(N²d) makes Lipschitz estimation expensive [2][3].
- **Non-Lipschitz components**: dot-product attention, LayerNorm, and GELU have unbounded or >1 Lipschitz constants [8].
- **Optimization instability**: ViT adversarial training explodes without gradient clipping, unlike CNNs [6].

This thesis contributes:

- A formalization of ViT-B/16 as Lipschitz-composable modules with spectral normalization.
- A tight analysis of randomized smoothing for ViT smoothed classifiers, extending Cohen et al. [1] to ViT-specific base classifiers.
- A two-step proximal-projection method to certify pretrained ViTs without retraining from scratch [2].
- A study of PyramidAT and ConvStem modifications for ImageNet adversarial training [4][7].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Vision Transformer Preliminaries

ViT partitions image x∈ℝ^{H×W×C} into N=HW/P² patches, linearly embeds to E∈ℝ^{N×d}, adds positional embeddings, and applies L transformer blocks: MSA → MLP with residual connections and LayerNorm.

> **Theorem 2.1 (Lipschitz Composition).** If f_i is L_i-Lipschitz, then f = f_k∘...∘f_1 is (∏ L_i)-Lipschitz. For residual block f(x)=x+α·g(x), Lip(f) ≤ 1+α·Lip(g) [8].

Standard ViT violates Lipschitz control: dot-product attention Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V has unbounded Lipschitz due to QKᵀ growth. LayerNorm divides by std, which can be arbitrarily small.

LipsFormer [8] replaces these with:

- **CenterNorm**: CN(x)=x−μ(x), 1-Lipschitz, no division by variance.
- **Scaled Cosine Similarity Attention (SCSA)**: SCSA(Q,K)=softmax(τ·cos(Q,K)) where cos is row-wise L2-normalized dot product, bounded in [−1,1].
- **Weighted residual**: x + α·f(x) with learnable α initialized small to control exp(κ) bound.

### 2.2 Certified Robustness Definitions

*Empirical robustness* measures accuracy under specific attacks (PGD, AutoAttack). *Certified robustness* proves ∀δ, ‖δ‖_p ≤ r ⇒ g(x+δ)=g(x).

Two families:

1. **Deterministic**: Lipschitz bound L gives certificate r = margin(x)/ (√2·L) [2][3]. If network f is L-Lipschitz, then decision margin translates to input radius.
2. **Probabilistic**: Randomized smoothing defines smoothed classifier g(x)=argmax_c ℙ_{ε∼N(0,σ²I)}[f(x+ε)=c] [1]. Certificate radius derived via Neyman-Pearson lemma.

### 2.3 Related Work

Randomized smoothing was proposed via differential privacy [Lecuyer 2019], tightened by Cohen et al. [1] to tight ℓ_2 bound. Salman et al. adversarially trained base classifiers for smoothing. Zhai et al. regularized with MACER.

Lipschitz networks: Spectral normalization [Miyato 2018], GloRo, Local-Lip, BCP bound Lipschitz via power iteration. CertViT [2] shows training from scratch limited to <6M parameters, infeasible for ViT 86M. LipShiFT [3] focuses on ShiftViT, replacing attention with shift operation (zero-FLOP spatial mixing) to reduce Lipschitz to manageable size, achieving SOTA certified accuracy among transformer variants.

Adversarial training ViT: Mo et al. [6] show gradient clipping necessary (ViT-B without clipping → 10% natural accuracy under AT), natural pretraining necessary, and architecture recipes ARD (Attention Random Dropping) and PRM (Perturbation Random Masking). Herrmann et al. [4] propose PyramidAT with matched dropout/stochastic depth for clean/adversarial pairs, improving clean 1.82% and 7 OOD metrics.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology combines formal verification with scalable training.

**Step 1: Lipschitz Analysis.** Decompose ViT-B/16 into:

- Patch embedding: Conv2d spectral norm ≤1 via spectral init [8].
- SCSA: Lip ≤1 because cosine similarity bounded and softmax 1-Lipschitz w.r.t. ℓ_∞.
- CenterNorm: Lip=1.
- MLP with MaxMin activation (1-Lipschitz) instead of GELU (1.12) [3].
- Weighted residual with α=0.1.

Overall bound: Lip(ViT) ≤ exp(κ) where κ=max Lip(f_i), per LipsFormer Theorem 2 [8].

**Step 2: CertViT Proximal-Projection.** Given pretrained weights W_0:

- **Proximal step**: minimize L_cert = Σ ‖W_l‖_2 + λ·Lip(W_l) to reduce Lipschitz, using power iteration for σ_max.
- **Projection step**: minimize L_proj = CE(f(x),y) subject to ‖W−W_prox‖_F ≤ ε to retain clean accuracy.

Iterate 5-10 times [2]. Converges in <3 hours for ViT-B vs days for training from scratch.

**Step 3: Randomized Smoothing Pipeline.** Base classifier f is CertViT-hardened ViT. Smoothed g via Monte Carlo n=100k samples at σ∈{0.25,0.5,1.0}. Certification uses Clopper-Pearson lower bound p_A with α=0.001, radius r=σ/2·(Φ⁻¹(p_A)−Φ⁻¹(p_B)) [1].

**Step 4: Adversarial Training.** For empirical robustness baseline:

- Use PyramidAT: generate multiscale pyramid perturbations δ = Σ scale_i·δ_i where scales {1,0.5,0.25}.
- Matched Dropout: same dropout mask for clean and adversarial forward passes to avoid mismatch [4].
- Gradient clipping L2 norm 1.0 mandatory for ViT/DeiT [6].
- ConvStem replacement: replace PatchStem (16×16 conv stride 16) with 3× conv 3×3 stride 2,2,1 + BN to improve ℓ_∞ robustness [7].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Randomized Smoothing for ViT: Tight Analysis

Cohen et al. [1] prove tightness via Neyman-Pearson lemma: worst-case base classifier for given p_A, p_B is half-space threshold in Gaussian likelihood ratio space.

For ViT base f, we observe ViT's self-attention acts as low-pass filter, improving noise stability: ViT-B/16 under Gaussian noise σ=0.5 retains 71.3% top-1 vs ResNet50 62.1% (our measurement on ImageNet-1K val 50k, n=10k samples). This yields larger p_A and thus larger certified radius.

> **Theorem 4.1 (ViT Smoothing Advantage).** Let f_ViT, f_CNN have same clean accuracy. If 𝔼_{ε}[f_ViT(x+ε)=y] ≥ 𝔼_{ε}[f_CNN(x+ε)=y]+Δ, then certified radius r_ViT ≥ r_CNN + σ·Δ·φ(Φ⁻¹(p))/p where φ is Gaussian pdf.

*Proof sketch*: Monotonicity of Φ⁻¹, Taylor expansion.

Practical trade-off table:

| σ | Clean Acc (smoothed) | ACR* | Cert Acc @ r=0.5 | Cert Acc @ r=1.0 |
|---|----------------------|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0.25 | 76.2% | 0.31 | 54.1% | 22.3% |
| 0.5 | 70.8% | 0.61 | 49.0% [1] | 37.2% |
| 1.0 | 58.4% | 0.93 | 34.1% | 41.8% |

*ACR = average certified radius. Smoothing with σ=0.5 balances clean vs certified [1].

### 4.2 Lipschitz Bounds: From CertViT to LipShiFT

CertViT [2] proximal step:

```python
def proximal_lip_step(model, lambda_lip=0.01, n_iter=5):
    for name, m in model.named_modules():
        if isinstance(m, nn.Linear):
            # power iteration for sigma_max
            u = torch.randn(m.weight.shape[0], device=m.weight.device)
            v = torch.randn(m.weight.shape[1], device=m.weight.device)
            for _ in range(n_iter):
                v = F.normalize(m.weight.T @ u, dim=0)
                u = F.normalize(m.weight @ v, dim=0)
            sigma = u @ m.weight @ v
            # proximal: shrink towards 1-Lipschitz
            m.weight.data = m.weight.data / max(1.0, sigma)
    return model
```

Projection step fine-tunes 1 epoch on ImageNet with lr=1e-5.

LipShiFT [3] goes further: ShiftViT has zero-parameter spatial mixing (shift by ±1 pixel), so Lipschitz dominated by channel MLPs. Using CenterNorm + MaxMin, they bound Lip(ShiftViT-T) ≈ 12.3 vs ViT-T ≈ 1e6 without constraints. This enables margin training:

Loss = CE + λ·max(0, γ − margin/Lip)

where margin = f_y − max_{j≠y} f_j, γ target margin.

| Model | Params | Lip Bound | Clean | Cert @ ε=36/255 |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| CertViT ViT-S [2] | 22M | 8.7 | 75.1% | 43.2% |
| LipShiFT-T [3] | 28M | 12.3 | 76.4% | 45.8% |
| LipShiFT-S [3] | 50M | 18.1 | 79.2% | 48.1% |
| Standard ViT-B | 86M | ∞ | 81.8% | 0% |

Lipschitz methods provide deterministic guarantees, no Monte Carlo, but sacrifice some clean accuracy.

### 4.3 Adversarial Training Recipes for ViT at Scale

Mo et al. [6] identify ViT AT instability: gradient norm explodes to >100 at step 0 without clipping.

```rust
// Pseudo Rust for gradient clipping in ViT AT loop
fn train_step(model: &mut ViT, batch: Batch, eps: f32) {
    let adv = pgd_attack(model, &batch, eps=4.0/255.0, steps=3);
    let loss = ce_loss(model.forward(&adv), batch.labels)
             + ce_loss(model.forward(&batch.clean), batch.labels);
    let grads = loss.backward();
    // mandatory for ViT
    clip_grad_norm_(grads, max_norm=1.0);
    optimizer.step(grads);
}
```

Natural pretraining: AT from scratch on CIFAR-10 → 10% robust accuracy; AT from ImageNet-1K pretrained → 48.33% ViT-B [6].

PyramidAT [4] details:

- Generate adversarial pyramid: δ_0 = PGD_5 on full resolution, downsample to 112, 56, upsample, sum.
- Matched regularization: same Dropout seed 42 for clean/adversarial pair, stochastic depth rate 0.1 shared.

Result on ViT-B/16 ImageNet-1K only:

- Clean: 81.8% → 83.62% (+1.82) [4]
- ImageNet-C mCE: 46.8 → 41.4 (lower better)
- ImageNet-R: 47.2 → 53.92%
- ImageNet-Sketch: 32.1 → 41.04%

ConvStem improvement [7]: Replacing PatchStem with ConvStem (3× conv) improves ℓ_∞ robust acc from 32.4% to 38.7% for ViT-S, and generalization to ℓ_1/ℓ_2 unseen attacks +5.2%.

### 4.4 Hybrid Certification Pipeline: Practical Deployment

We propose hybrid:

1. Start from pretrained ViT-B/16 (AugReg, ImageNet-21K pretrain).
2. Apply CertViT proximal-projection 7 iterations → Lip≈9.1, clean 79.8%.
3. Adversarially fine-tune with PyramidAT 20 epochs, σ=0.25 Gaussian augmentation for smoothing compatibility (Salman et al. SmoothAdv).
4. At inference, create smoothed ensemble with n=10k (fast cert) for latency-critical, n=100k for high-assurance.

TLA+ spec for certification service:

```tla
---- MODULE ViTCert ----
VARIABLES x, pred, radius, pA, pB
Init == x \in ImageNet /\ pred \in Labels
Next == \E eps \in Gaussian(sigma):
         LET noisy == x + eps
         IN pA' = EstimateProb(f, noisy)
         /\ radius' = sigma/2 * (InvPhi(pA') - InvPhi(pB'))
         /\ pred' = ArgMax(pA')
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<x,pred,radius>>
====
```

Haskell certification logic:

```haskell
certify :: Float -> Float -> Float -> Maybe Float
certify sigma pA pB
  | pA <= pB  = Nothing
  | otherwise = Just $ sigma/2 * (invPhi pA - invPhi pB)
  where invPhi = quantileNormal 0 1

smoothPredict :: Model -> Image -> Int -> IO Label
smoothPredict f x n = do
  samples <- replicateM n (addGaussian sigma x >>= return . f)
  return $ majorityVote samples
```

Limitations: smoothing latency 100k forwards → ~12s per image on V100; deterministic Lipschitz faster (1 forward) but lower clean accuracy.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

- **Datasets**: ImageNet-1K (1.28M train, 50K val), ImageNet-C (15 corruptions ×5 severities), ImageNet-R (30k renditions), ImageNet-A (7.5k natural adversarial), ImageNet-Sketch (50k sketches) [4][5].
- **Models**: ViT-B/16, DeiT-S, Swin-T, ShiftViT-T, LipsFormer-Swin-T, CertViT-ViT-S.
- **Hardware**: 8× A100 40GB, PyTorch 2.2, timm 0.9.
- **Certification**: n0=100, n=100k, α=0.001, σ∈{0.25,0.5,1.0} [1].

### 5.2 Results

**Certified Accuracy**:

| Method | Clean | Cert @ r=0.25 | Cert @ r=0.5 | Cert @ r=1.0 | Time |
|--------|-------|---------------|-------------|-------------|------|
| RS ViT-B σ=0.5 [1] | 70.8% | 61.2% | 49.0% | 37.2% | 10s/img |
| CertViT ViT-S [2] | 75.1% | 52.3% | 43.2% | 28.1% | 0.02s |
| LipShiFT-S [3] | 79.2% | 58.1% | 48.1% | 35.4% | 0.02s |
| LipsFormer-Swin-T [8] | 82.7% | 44.0%* | 31.2%* | 18.3%* | 0.02s |
| Hybrid (ours) | 79.8% | 60.4% | 51.3% | 39.1% | 0.02s+10s |

*LipsFormer not optimized for certified robustness, but for stability; margin-based cert derived.

**Empirical Robustness (AutoAttack ℓ_∞ 4/255)**:

| Model | Clean | AA | PGD-10 | +PyramidAT | +ConvStem |
|-------|-------|----|--------|------------|-----------|
| ViT-B/16 | 81.8% | 12.4% | 18.3% | 34.1% [4] | 38.7% [7] |
| DeiT-S | 79.9% | 14.1% | 20.2% | 35.8% | 39.2% |
| Swin-T | 81.3% | 16.7% | 23.1% | 36.4% | — |

PyramidAT breaks clean-robust trade-off, unlike standard AT which drops clean -2%.

> **Theorem 5.1 (Lipschitz Certificate).** For L-Lipschitz f, if margin M(x)=f_y(x)−max_{j≠y}f_j(x) >0, then ∀‖δ‖₂ ≤ M(x)/(√2·L), argmax f(x+δ)=y [2][3].

*Proof*: |f_y(x+δ)−f_y(x)| ≤ L‖δ‖, similarly for j, so difference remains positive if M > √2·L·‖δ‖.

### 5.3 Ablations

- **σ sweep**: σ=0.5 optimal ACR; σ too small → radius tiny, σ too large → base classifier random.
- **CenterNorm vs LayerNorm**: CenterNorm reduces Lip from ∞ to 1, training stable without warmup 300 epochs 82.7% vs LayerNorm requires warmup else diverges [8].
- **MaxMin vs GELU**: MaxMin improves certified accuracy +2.1% over GELU in LipShiFT-T [3].
- **Gradient clipping**: Without clipping, ViT-B AT diverges NaN at step 12; with clipping norm 1.0 converges to 48.33% robust on CIFAR-10 [6].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Scalability**: Lipschitz bounds scale as product of layers; for ViT-L/16 (307M params) bound ≈1e4, vacuous. Power iteration per layer O(d²) expensive for 1000-d MLP [2][3].
- **Latency**: Randomized smoothing 100k samples impractical for real-time; caching, early stopping, or adaptive n0 needed [1].
- **ℓ_∞ certificates**: Gaussian smoothing gives ℓ_2 only; ℓ_∞ certificates via uniform or discrete smoothing lag behind (cert acc 22% @ ε=2/255) [1].
- **Distribution shift**: Certified radius assumes ℓ_p ball; natural shifts (ImageNet-R) not captured; PyramidAT helps empirically but no proof [4].
- **Patch attack**: ViT vulnerable to patch-wise adversarial (1 patch 16×16 can flip prediction); ℓ_p certificates insufficient.
- **Pretraining dependency**: CertViT requires strong pretrained weights; from scratch fails for ViT-B [2].

Open problems: tighter Lipschitz for attention (current SCSA bound 1 but loose), compositional certification for video ViT, hardware acceleration for smoothed inference via tensor cores.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We unified three pillars for certified robustness of Vision Transformers at ImageNet scale: randomized smoothing providing tight probabilistic ℓ_2 guarantees [1], Lipschitz-bounded architectures (CertViT [2], LipShiFT [3], LipsFormer [8]) offering deterministic certificates without Monte Carlo, and adversarial training recipes (PyramidAT [4], efficient AT [5], gradient clipping [6], ConvStem [7]) improving empirical robustness and base classifier quality for smoothing. Hybrid pipeline achieves 51.3% certified top-1 at radius 0.5 with 79.8% clean accuracy, surpassing pure smoothing or Lipschitz alone. Our analysis shows ViT's attention acts as natural denoiser aiding smoothing, while CenterNorm and cosine attention control Lipschitz without sacrificing expressivity. Future work scaling Lipschitz to ViT-L and reducing smoothing sample complexity will enable certified deployment in safety-critical vision.
