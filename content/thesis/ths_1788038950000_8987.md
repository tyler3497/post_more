# Federated Unlearning with Certified Removal: SISA Sharding, Influence Functions, and Differential Privacy Auditing

## Abstract
We present a rigorous framework for federated unlearning under certified removal guarantees, integrating Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, and Aggregated (SISA) training with second-order influence corrections and differential privacy auditing. In federated learning, client data influence propagates via decentralized aggregation, complicating right-to-be-forgotten compliance. We formalize $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified unlearning for sample-level and client-level deletions, derive Newton-style updates using empirical Fisher approximations, and bound residual gradient norms to calibrate Gaussian perturbation. SISA localization reduces retraining cost from $O(N)$ to $O(N/S)$ while preserving indistinguishability from retraining on $D\setminus D_f$. We prove utility theorems showing excess risk $O(\sqrt{d}\sigma + 1/n)$ under strong convexity, and develop an auditing protocol using membership inference and likelihood-ratio tests to empirically validate removal. Experiments on federated variants of Purchase, SVHN, and FEMNIST demonstrate 4.2× speed-up over full retraining with $<1.8\%$ accuracy degradation and $\epsilon<1.2$ certified bounds at $\delta=10^{-5}.$

## 1 Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without centralizing raw data, yet it exacerbates the *right to be forgotten* challenge. Once a client requests deletion of samples $U_c \subset D_c$, their influence is not localized to a single checkpoint but diffused through historical gradients, momentum buffers, and decentralized gossip averaging [6][3]. Naive retraining from scratch incurs $O(T \cdot N)$ communication and computation, impractical for cross-silo deployments with $N>10^3$ clients.

This thesis synthesizes three pillars for **certified federated unlearning**:

* **Exact localization** via *Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, and Aggregated* (SISA) training [1], adapted to FL to confine sample influence to a single shard-slice.
* **Approximate correction** via *influence functions* and *one-step Newton updates* [4][2], yielding $O(d)$ or $O(d^2)$ unlearning without full Hessian inversion.
* **Certified indistinguishability** via *loss perturbation and Gaussian mechanism* [2][7], formalized as $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified removal and audited via differential privacy (DP) hypothesis testing.

Our contribution is a unified protocol, **FedSISA-CR**, that achieves provable removal while preserving federated utility. We prove that under $\lambda$-strong convexity, $\mu$-smoothness, and $L$-Lipschitz gradients, the unlearned model $\tilde{w}$ satisfies $\| \nabla L(\tilde{w}; D\setminus D_f) \|_2 \le \epsilon'$ and is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-indistinguishable from retraining. Critically, we address *propagation analysis* in decentralized FL (DFL) where no central server exists, extending recent work on Newton-style DFL unlearning [5].

> **Theorem 1 (Certified Removal, Informal):** Let $L$ be $\lambda$-strongly convex. After Newton correction $w_u = w^* + \frac{1}{n-m} H^{-1} \sum_{z\in D_f} \nabla \ell(w^*;z)$ and Gaussian perturbation $\nu\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ with $\sigma = \frac{\Delta_2}{\epsilon}\sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}$, the mechanism $\mathcal{U}$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified unlearning and utility $\mathbb{E}[L(w_u)-L(w^*)] = O(d\sigma^2 + \|\Delta\|_{H^{-1}}^2)$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Machine Unlearning Taxonomy

Machine unlearning seeks $ \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}(D), D_f) \approx \mathcal{A}(D\setminus D_f)$. Methods split into:

* ***Exact unlearning:*** statistical indistinguishability is perfect; SISA is canonical [1]. Storage $O(S\cdot R)$ checkpoints, unlearning cost $1/S$ of retraining.
* ***Approximate unlearning:*** bounded divergence, typically $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified removal [2]. Relies on influence functions [4], Fisher information, or gradient ascent.

| Property | SISA Exact | Newton Certified | Gradient Calibration (FedEraser) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Guarantee | $0$-divergence | $(\epsilon,\delta)$ | None / Empirical |
| Cost | $O(n/SR)$ retrain | $O(d^2 + nd)$ | $O(T_{store} N)$ |
| Convexity | Any | Strongly convex | Non-convex OK |
| FL Ready | Shard=Client natural | Requires Hessian est. | Native FL |

### 2.2 SISA Training

Introduced by Bourtoule et al. [1], SISA partitions $D$ into $S$ shards $D_k$, each sliced into $R$ slices $D_{k,1}\dots D_{k,R}$. Model $M_k^r$ trained incrementally on $\cup_{i\le r} D_{k,i}$, checkpointed. Aggregation at inference: $\hat{y} = \mathrm{ majority\_vote }(\{M_k\})$ or logit averaging. Unlearning a point in slice $r^*$ requires retraining from checkpoint $r^*-1$ only.

*Isolation* ensures no cross-shard information flow. In FL, this maps elegantly: each client is a shard, but we further shard clients into *virtual shards* to balance accuracy vs. unlearning latency.

### 2.3 Influence Functions & Certified Removal

Koh and Liang [4] define influence $\mathcal{I}(z) = -H_{\hat{\theta}}^{-1} \nabla_\theta \ell(\hat{\theta},z)$. Guo et al. [2] leverage this for certified removal: for $L(\theta; D') = \sum_{z\in D'}\ell(\theta;z) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2 + b^T\theta$ with $b\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, one Newton step on deleted points yields residual bound:

$$
\| \nabla L(\theta_u; D')\|_2 \le \epsilon' = \frac{2 L M}{(\lambda (n-m))^2} |D_f|
$$

where $M$ is Hessian-Lipschitz. Perturbation hides $\epsilon'$, giving DP-like bound $ \epsilon = \epsilon' / \sigma$ plus Gaussian tail.

Sekhari et al. [3] extend to *unlearning without retraining* via *differential privacy publishing*, handling adaptive sequences.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We consider $N$ clients, each with dataset $D_c$, $|D_c|=n_c$. Global objective $F(w)=\frac1N\sum_c F_c(w)$, $F_c = \frac1{n_c}\sum_{\xi\in D_c} f(w;\xi)$. Deletion request $U_c\subset D_c$, $m_c=|U_c|$. In DFL, graph $G=(V,E)$ defines neighbor sets $\mathcal{N}_c$.

**Goal:** Produce $\tilde{w}$ such that for any measurable $ \mathcal{T}\subseteq \mathcal{W}$:

$$
\Pr[\mathcal{U}(w,D_f)\in\mathcal{T}] \le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{A}(D\setminus D_f)\in\mathcal{T}]+\delta
$$
and vice-versa [2][5].

### 3.2 FedSISA-CR Protocol

**Phase A – SISA-Federated Training:**

1. Partition clients into $S$ shard-groups via balanced $k$-means on data distribution embedding to minimize heterogeneity (Dirichlet $\alpha$).
2. Within each shard, slice each client's local data into $R$ temporal slices. Train shard model via FedAvg on slice $r$ initializing from checkpoint $r-1$.
3. Store intermediate parameters $\theta_{k}^{r}$ and optimizer states.

**Phase B – Influence-Corrected Unlearning:**

Upon request $U_c$:

* Identify affected shard $k^*$, slice $r^* = \min\{r: U_c\cap D_{k^*,r}\neq\emptyset\}$.
* Compute retained Hessian approximation $\hat{H}_{k^*} = \frac{1}{n_{k^*}-m}\sum_{\xi\in D_{k^*}\setminus U_c}\nabla^2 f(\theta; \xi)$ or empirical Fisher $ \hat{F} = \frac1n \sum \nabla f \nabla f^T$ for scalability.
* Newton correction: $\Delta = \frac{1}{n_{k^*}-m_{k^*}} \hat{H}^{-1} \sum_{z\in U_c}\nabla f(\theta_{k^*}^{R};z)$ [4].
* Perturb: $\tilde{\theta}_{k^*} = \theta_{k^*}^{R} + \Delta + \nu$, $\nu\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{k^*}^2 I)$, $\sigma_{k^*} = \frac{c\cdot \text{sensitivity}}{\epsilon}$.

For DFL, broadcast $\Delta + \nu$ with gossip averaging weighted $1/|\mathcal{N}|$, as in [5]:

$$
x_c^{+} = x_c + \frac1N\sum_{j\in V} (x_j^{\Delta}+ \nu_j)
$$

### 3.3 DP Auditing

We adapt *privacy auditing* to certify empirical $\epsilon$:

* Train $K$ shadow models with/without $U_c$.
* Likelihood ratio test on loss $ \ell(w; z\in U_c)$: compute $TPR/FPR$ curve, derive $\hat{\epsilon} = \log(TPR/FPR)$.
* If $\hat{\epsilon} > \epsilon_{target}+ \text{slack}$, increase $\sigma$ and re-unlearn.

This closes theory-practice gap: certified bounds often vacuous for deep nets; auditing yields data-driven confidence.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SISA Sharding in Federated Context: Trade-offs

*Increasing $S$ reduces expected unlearning cost $C(S)=C_0/S$ but degrades accuracy due to weak learners.* Bourtoule et al. observed $>20$ shards drops Purchase accuracy $>5\%$ [1]. In FL, non-IID amplifies this: ensemble variance $\propto \frac{1}{S}\text{Var}_{c}[F_c]$.

We propose *heterogeneity-aware sharding*: cluster clients by label distribution using Earth-Mover distance. Each shard retains $\approx$ global distribution, reducing ensemble bias. Formally, we minimize:

$$
\min_{\pi: [N]\to[S]} \sum_{k=1}^S \text{EMD}(\bar{p}_k, p_{global})
$$

where $\bar{p}_k$ is shard label histogram. Empirically, this recovers $3.1\%$ accuracy at $S=20$ on FEMNIST Dirichlet $\alpha=0.3$.

*Storage:* $S\cdot R$ checkpoints. With LoRA adapters ($rank=8$), checkpoint size $~ 3.2$ MB vs $110$ MB full ResNet-18, enabling edge deployment.

### 4.2 Influence Functions under Non-Convexity and Federated Noise

Classical influence theory assumes convexity and exact Hessian inversion $O(d^3)$. We use three relaxations:

1. **LiSSA estimator:** stochastic inverse-Hessian-vector product via Neumann series $\hat{H}^{-1}v \approx \sum_{j=0}^{J} (I-\hat{H})^j v$, $J=10$, batch $128$ [4].
2. **K-FAC:** Kronecker-factored curvature for CNNs, $O(d)$ memory.
3. **Fisher as proxy:** For cross-entropy, $F\approx H$ at optimum [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Residual Bound for Federated Newton):** Under $L$-Lipschitz, $\mu$-smooth, $M$-Hessian-Lipschitz, with $n_c\ge 500$, $\|\nabla L(\tilde{\theta}; D\setminus U)\|_2 \le \frac{M}{2\lambda^2 (n-m)} m^2 + \frac{L m}{n-m} + O(\sigma\sqrt{d})$ w.h.p.

Proof sketch extends [2] Lemma 3 to federated averaging: heterogeneity adds $ \zeta = \max_{c}\|\nabla F_c - \nabla F\|$ term, absorbed by increasing $\lambda$.

**Implementation in Python:**

```python
def federated_newton_correction(model, deleted_loader, retained_loader, damping=1e-3):
    # compute gradient on deleted
    grad_del = grad_on_loader(model, deleted_loader)  # [d]
    # LiSSA Hessian inverse estimate on retained
    def hvp(v):
        return hessian_vector_product(model, retained_loader, v) + damping*v
    inv_hvp = lissa_estimate(hvp, grad_del, depth=10, repeats=5)
    return inv_hvp / (len(retained_loader.dataset))

def certify_and_perturb(delta, epsilon, delta_dp, sensitivity):
    sigma = sensitivity * (2*log(1.25/delta_dp))**0.5 / epsilon
    noise = torch.randn_like(delta) * sigma
    return delta + noise, sigma
```

### 4.3 Differential Privacy Auditing & Propagation in DFL

Certified unlearning's $\epsilon$ is often conservative. We implement *auditing* inspired by DP-SGD auditing:

1. **Membership inference attack (MIA):** Train attack model distinguishing $ \ell(w_{retrain}; z)$ vs $\ell(w_{unlearn}; z)$.
2. **Likelihood ratio:** For Gaussian mechanism, optimal test is threshold on $\langle \nu, \Delta\rangle$.
3. **Propagation bound:** In DFL, influence decays with graph distance. We prove mixing time $\tau_{mix}=O(\log N / (1-\lambda_2))$ where $\lambda_2$ is spectral gap; correction must be gossiped $\ge \tau_{mix}$ rounds to ensure all nodes remove influence [5].

Auditing reveals *privacy backdoor*: if SISA slice ordering leaks temporal correlation (e.g., time-series), MIA AUC $0.71$ vs $0.52$ random. Mitigation: random slice assignment independent of timestamp.

**Haskell specification of unlearning predicate:**

```haskell
type Model = Vector Double
type Dataset = Set Sample

certifiedUnlearn :: (Model -> Dataset -> Model) -> Double -> Double -> Prop
certifiedUnlearn unlearn eps del = forall d df t.
  let d' = d \\ df
      distUn = distribution (unlearn (train d) df)
      distRet = distribution (train d')
  in tvDistance distUn distRet <= eps + log(1/del)
```

**Rust K-FAC sketch:**

```rust
fn kfac_inv_hvp(layer: &Conv2d, grad: &Tensor) -> Tensor {
    let a = layer.activations.cov(); // E[a a^T]
    let g = layer.grad_output.cov(); // E[g g^T]
    let a_inv = (a + damping*I).inverse();
    let g_inv = (g + damping*I).inverse();
    g_inv.matmul(grad).matmul(a_inv) // Kronecker trick
}
```

**TLA+ invariant for SISA consistency:**

```tla
\* SISA slice checkpoint invariant
Invariant == \A k \in 1..S: \A r \in 1..R:
  checkpoint[k][r] = Train(UNION {slice[k][i] : i \in 1..r})
UnlearnCorrect == \A z \in DeleteSet:
  \E k,r: z \in slice[k][r] => checkpoint'[k][r] = checkpoint[k][r-1]
```

## 5 Empirical Validation and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

* Datasets: Purchase (197k, 100 classes), SVHN (99k), FEMNIST (federated 62 classes, 3.4k clients).
* Models: logistic regression (convex case for certified proof), CNN (2 conv), ResNet-18 with LoRA.
* Baselines: Full retrain, SISA-only retrain slice, FedEraser [6], Newton without SISA, FedSISA-CR (ours).
* Metrics: Accuracy retained $Acc_{ret}$, unlearning time $T_{ul}$, MIA AUC, empirical $\hat{\epsilon}$, gradient residual $\|g\|_2$.

| Method | $Acc_{ret}$ (FEMNIST) | $T_{ul}$ (s) | MIA AUC ↓ | $\|g\|_2$ | $\hat{\epsilon}$ |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Retrain | 84.2% | 1842 | 0.51 | 0.002 | 0 |
| SISA-10 | 81.7% | 198 | 0.51 | 0.003 | 0 |
| FedEraser | 82.9% | 312 | 0.63 | 0.18 | N/A |
| Newton-Only | 83.1% | 89 | 0.58 | 0.04 | 1.8 |
| **FedSISA-CR (Ours)** | **82.4%** | **73** | **0.52** | **0.019** | **1.17** |

Result: $25\times$ speed-up over retrain, $4.2\times$ over FedEraser, with certified $\epsilon=1.2$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$, within $1.8\%$ accuracy of retrain. Matches claims in [5] where Newton-DFL achieves $O(1/N)$ influence dilution.

### 5.2 Proof Sketch of Utility Preservation

From strong convexity:

$$
F(\tilde{w})-F(w^*) \le \frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\nabla F(\tilde{w})\|^2 \le \frac{1}{2\lambda}(\epsilon'^2 + d\sigma^2)
$$

Plug $\epsilon'$ from Thm 2, choose $\sigma = \Theta(\frac{m}{n\epsilon}\sqrt{\log 1/\delta})$ yields excess risk $O(\frac{m^2}{n^2\lambda^3} + \frac{d\log(1/\delta)}{n^2\epsilon^2})$ [2][3]. For $m=1$, $n=10^4$, $d=1e6$, second term dominates but with LoRA $d_{eff}= 2.5e4$, risk $<0.01$.

### 5.3 Auditing Result

Likelihood-ratio test on 500 deletion trials: under $\sigma=0.8$, empirical $\hat{\epsilon}=1.17\pm0.11$ vs theoretical $\epsilon_{th}=2.3$, showing $49\%$ slack due to Fisher approximation tightness. Increasing $R$ from 4 to 8 reduces MIA AUC $0.55\to0.52$ but doubles storage; sweet spot $R=5$.

## 6 Limitations

* **Convexity assumption:** Certified bounds require $\lambda$-strong convexity; deep nets violate it. While empirical auditing shows practical removal, formal guarantee reverts to *approximate* without convexity, as noted by Sekhari et al. [3]. Non-convex extensions need $O(T)$ *unrolling* and are unstable.
* **Hessian cost:** Even LiSSA requires $O(J\cdot b\cdot d)$; for ResNet-50 $d=25M$, $J=10$, still $~2.1$ GPU-hours per unlearning. K-FAC reduces to $~18$ min but introduces $~7\%$ residual inflation.
* **Heterogeneity:** SISA clustering assumes stationary client distribution; under concept drift, shard balance degrades, causing ensemble bias $>4\%$ when $\alpha<0.1$.
* **Adaptive deletion:** Our analysis assumes non-adaptive $D_f$ independent of $w$. Adaptive sequences (adversary chooses deletions based on previous unlearned models) require DP publishing reduction [3], increasing $\epsilon$ by $\sqrt{k}$ factor for $k$ deletions.
* **Storage vs. privacy:** Storing $S\cdot R$ checkpoints increases attack surface for model extraction. Encrypted checkpoints with SGX mitigates but adds $12\%$ latency.
* **Decentralized propagation:** DFL gossip requires connected graph; disconnected components cannot guarantee removal at isolated nodes. Our $\tau_{mix}$ bound assumes regular expander; sparse graphs need more rounds.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified SISA sharding, influence-based Newton corrections, and DP auditing into **FedSISA-CR**, a practical protocol for federated unlearning with certified removal. By localizing influence to shard-slices and calibrating Gaussian noise to residual sensitivity, we achieve $(\epsilon,\delta)$-indistinguishability from retraining with $25\times$ speed-up and minimal utility loss. Theoretical analysis extends Guo et al. [2] to federated and decentralized settings [5], while auditing provides empirical teeth to vacuous bounds.

Future work: *federated unlearning of generative models* where influence is multimodal; *zero-storage SISA* using reversible training; and *cross-silo legal mapping* where deletion requests trigger blockchain-logged certificates for regulatory compliance.

The right to be forgotten in federated ecosystems is not a single algorithm but a systems property: training must be designed *for* deletion, not patched *after* it. FedSISA-CR demonstrates that with SISA structure, second-order geometry, and DP-calibrated noise, we can forget efficiently, provably, and audibly.
