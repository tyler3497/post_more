# Federated Learning with Differential Privacy: Rényi DP Accounting, Secure Aggregation, and Heterogeneous Client Drift Mitigation via FedProx and SCAFFOLD

## Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) promises collaborative model training without centralizing raw data, yet practical deployments face dual challenges of rigorous privacy and statistical heterogeneity. This thesis develops a unified framework integrating client-level differential privacy with Rényi DP accounting, cryptographically secure aggregation, and drift-resilient optimization. We analyze Gaussian mechanism calibration under subsampled Rényi DP, showing how moments-accountant composition yields tight (ε,δ)-DP guarantees across thousands of rounds. We contrast threat models where the server is honest-but-curious, motivating secure aggregation protocols that mask individual updates while revealing only sums. For non-IID data, we formalize client-drift and evaluate mitigation via FedProx proximal regularization and SCAFFOLD control variates, deriving convergence rates under bounded dissimilarity. Theoretical synthesis demonstrates compatibility of privacy noise, secure aggregation, and variance reduction, while empirical simulations on FEMNIST and Shakespeare illustrate tradeoffs among privacy budget, communication, and accuracy. We conclude with open directions in adaptive clipping, shuffled DP, and Byzantine-robust aggregation.

---

## 1 Introduction

Federated Learning, introduced by McMahan et al. and refined through a series of systems papers, reimagines *distributed optimization* under the constraint that data never leaves the client device [1][2]. A central server orchestrates training by broadcasting a global model, collecting locally computed updates, and aggregating. This paradigm is compelling for mobile keyboards, medical consortia, and financial fraud detection where data silos are legally or ethically immovable.

Yet two tensions define modern FL research:

1. **Privacy leakage via updates.** Gradient inversion attacks [3] demonstrate that even a single gradient can reconstruct training images. FedAvg alone provides no formal guarantee. Formal *differential privacy* (DP) [4] is required, but its naive composition is loose and its noise degrades utility.

2. **Statistical heterogeneity.** Client data is ***non-IID*** by nature. Label skew, feature shift, and quantity imbalance cause **client-drift**, where local optima diverge from the global objective. FedAvg can diverge or require exponentially more rounds [5][6].

This thesis argues that these challenges must be addressed *jointly*. Privacy accounting influences clipping and noise, which interacts with secure aggregation masks, which in turn interacts with drift correction. We present a unified design.

**Contributions:**

- A precise treatment of **Rényi DP (RDP)** accounting for subsampled Gaussian mechanisms in FL, including conversion to (ε,δ)-DP and moments accountant implementation in PyTorch/Opacus.
- A systematization of **secure aggregation (SecAgg)** from Bonawitz et al. [7] through Bell et al. and recent 3-round variants, analyzing dropout resilience and compatibility with distributed DP.
- A comparative analysis of **FedProx** and **SCAFFOLD** for heterogeneous mitigation, with convergence bounds and practical hyperparameter guidance.
- An integrated protocol **DP-SecAgg-SCAFFOLD-Prox** with modular proofs and empirical evaluation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy and Rényi DP

Standard (ε,δ)-DP requires for neighboring datasets D, D' differing by one user's data [4][8]:

> **Definition:** M satisfies (ε,δ)-DP if Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε Pr[M(D') ∈ S] + δ.

Rényi DP, proposed by Mironov [9], refines this via Rényi divergence of order α>1:

$$D_{\alpha}(P\|Q) = \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log \mathbb{E}_{x\sim Q}\left[\left(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right)^{\alpha}\right]$$

Mechanism M is (α, ε_α)-RDP if D_α(M(D)‖M(D')) ≤ ε_α. Key properties:

- *Adaptive composition:* If M1 is (α, ε1)-RDP and M2 is (α, ε2)-RDP, composition is (α, ε1+ε2)-RDP [9][10].
- *Conversion:* (α, ε_α)-RDP implies (ε_α + log(1/δ)/(α-1), δ)-DP for any δ∈(0,1) [9].

> **Theorem:** RDP Composition Tightness. For T iterations of Gaussian mechanism with noise multiplier σ and sampling rate q, the total ε(δ) = min_{α>1} { T·ε_α(q,σ) + log(1/δ)/(α-1) } is strictly tighter than advanced composition by a factor of O(√T).

*Proof sketch:* Follows from Mironov Prop. 1 and 3 and the subsampled RDP bound for Gaussian [11].

This functional view ε(·): α ↦ ε_α enables **accountant** optimization over α grid {2,4,8,16,32,64}.

### 2.2 Threat Model in FL

We consider three adversaries:

- **Honest-but-curious server:** Follows protocol but tries to infer client data from updates. Mitigated by *secure aggregation* and *distributed DP*.
- **External eavesdropper:** Observes model releases. Mitigated by *central DP* accounting.
- **Malicious clients:** May poison. Out of scope for privacy but addressed in limitations via robust aggregation.

Distinction between *item-level* and *user-level* DP matters: In cross-device FL, a user contributes many records; we protect addition/removal of *all* records of a user [12][13].

### 2.3 Heterogeneity Taxonomy

- **Statistical:** P_k(y|x) differs across clients k; Dirichlet α=0.1 induces severe label skew.
- **Systems:** Clients have varying compute; stragglers may perform fewer local epochs.
- **Drift metric:** Let w* be global optimum, w_k* local optimum. Dissimilarity Γ = (1/K)∑‖∇F_k(w*)‖² quantifies heterogeneity [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology integrates literature synthesis, protocol design, and simulation.

**Protocol stack:**

1. **Clipping:** Per-client update Δ_k clipped to L2 norm C: clip(Δ)=Δ·min(1, C/‖Δ‖₂). Bounds sensitivity.
2. **Gaussian noise:** Server adds N(0, σ²C²I) or distributed noise via clients.
3. **RDP accounting:** Using Opacus RDPAccountant, track ε_α across rounds with Poisson subsampling rate q=K_sample/N.
4. **SecAgg:** Mask updates with pairwise additive masks derived from DH key agreement [7][14]. Survives dropout via Shamir secret sharing.
5. **Drift correction:** FedProx adds (μ/2)‖w - w^t‖² proximal term locally; SCAFFOLD maintains control variates c, c_k to correct gradients.

We implement reference stack in Python:

```python
# Federated DP-SCAFFOLD round (simplified)
def client_update(w_global, c_global, c_local, data, mu=0.1, C=1.0, E=5, lr=0.01):
    w = w_global.clone()
    for _ in range(E):
        g = grad(loss(w, data))
        # SCAFFOLD correction
        g_corr = g - c_local + c_global
        # FedProx proximal
        g_corr += mu * (w - w_global)
        w = w - lr * g_corr
    delta = w - w_global
    # clip
    delta = delta * min(1.0, C / delta.norm(p=2))
    # update control variate (option II)
    c_new = c_local + (1/(E*lr))*(w_global - w) - c_global
    return delta, c_new

def rdp_accountant(q, sigma, T, delta=1e-5):
    orders = [1.5,2,4,8,16,32,64]
    eps = []
    for alpha in orders:
        # subsampled Gaussian RDP per Mironov et al.
        rdp = rdp_gaussian_subsampled(q, sigma, alpha)  # [11]
        eps_alpha = T*rdp + math.log(1/delta)/(alpha-1)
        eps.append(eps_alpha)
    return min(eps)
```

In Haskell, RDP composition is a monoid:

```haskell
-- RDP epsilon as a function of alpha
newtype RDP = RDP { unRDP :: Double -> Double }

instance Semigroup RDP where
  RDP f <> RDP g = RDP (\a -> f a + g a)
instance Monoid RDP where mempty = RDP (const 0)

convert :: RDP -> Double -> Double -> Double
convert (RDP eps) delta alpha = eps alpha + log (1/delta) / (alpha - 1)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Rényi DP Accounting in Federated Learning

Standard moments accountant [11] was breakthrough: by tracking log moments of privacy loss random variable, it achieved O(qε√T) rather than O(qεT). RDP formalizes this.

For Gaussian mechanism M(D)= f(D)+N(0,σ²I) with sensitivity Δ₂ ≤ C:

$$ε_α = \frac{α Δ²}{2σ²}$$

With Poisson subsampling rate q, Mironov et al. bound [10][12]:

$$ε_α^{sub} ≤ \frac{1}{α-1}\log\left(1+ q² \binom{α}{2} \min\{4(e^{ε_2}-1),2e^{ε_2}\} + O(q³)\right)$$

Practical accountant steps:

1. Choose σ to target ε budget: For T=1000, q=0.01, δ=1e-5, to achieve ε=3, need σ≈1.1 (no amplification) vs σ≈0.9 with amplification – 20% less noise.
2. Use adaptive clipping [15]: Track quantile of update norms, set C = median norm; reduces bias.
3. User-level DP: Sensitivity is C, not per-example. Clipping per-client update is critical.

| Mechanism | RDP ε_α | Subsampled Amplification | Composition |
|-----------|---------|--------------------------|-------------|
| Gaussian σ=1.0 | α/2 | ~ O(q²α) | Linear in T |
| Laplace b=1/ε | ε (pure DP) | Weaker | Linear |
| Skellam [12] | α·Δ²/(2σ²)+... | Discrete, compatible with SecAgg | Linear |

**Key insight:** *Tight accounting enables more rounds under same budget.* UDP-FL [16] shows harmonizing mechanisms via RDP mediator improves accuracy by 8-12% over Gaussian-only at ε=2.

### 4.2 Secure Aggregation and Distributed DP

Bonawitz et al. CCS 2017 [7] proposed SecAgg for honest-but-curious server. Idea: Each pair (i,j) agrees on random seed s_{i,j} via DH. Client i adds Σ_j s_{i,j} - Σ_j s_{j,i} plus self-mask b_i. Sum of masks cancels.

Dropout handling: Secret-share seeds via Shamir t-out-of-n; if client drops after masking, surviving clients reveal shares to reconstruct its mask and subtract. Overhead: O(n²) key exchanges originally; Bell et al. reduces to O(n log n) via sparse graph.

Compatibility with DP:

- **Central DP:** Server adds noise after SecAgg sum; noise not compatible with mask cancellation? Actually addition commutes: SecAgg reveals sum, then noise added centrally still satisfies DP but server sees sum.
- **Distributed DP:** Each client adds discrete noise (Skellam, Binomial) such that sum of noises = target Gaussian approximation [12][13]. This achieves *trustless* DP without trusted server. Tradeoff: Discretization error.

Recent FSSA [17] reduces rounds from 4 to 3, crucial because extra rounds increase dropout probability. Hyades [18] extends RDP accounting to handle dropout explicitly, setting minimum target noise level even if sampled clients drop.

> **Theorem:** SecAgg + Distributed Discrete Gaussian. If each of n clients adds N_Z(0, σ²/n) discrete Gaussian, the aggregated sum is (α, α·Δ²/(2σ²)+τ)-RDP where τ is discretization error O(α²/σ³). Security holds against honest-but-curious server colluding with <t clients.

### 4.3 FedProx: Proximal Stabilization

FedProx [5] generalizes FedAvg:

$$ \min_w F_k(w) + \frac{μ}{2}‖w - w^t‖² $$

Proximal term μ restricts local drift. Viewed as inexact proximal point method.

**Assumptions:**

- **B-local dissimilarity:** E_k‖∇F_k(w)‖² ≤ B²‖∇f(w)‖² where f is global objective. B=1 is IID, larger B = more heterogeneity.
- **γ-inexact solver:** Client solves proximal problem to accuracy γ.

**Convergence:** Under L-smooth non-convex, FedProx achieves

$$\mathbb{E}‖∇f(\bar w)‖² ≤ O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{KT}} + \frac{B}{\sqrt{T}}\right)$$

with μ tuned ≈ L·B. Empirically, μ∈{0.01,0.1,1} sweep; too large μ stalls personalization.

Advantages: *Systems heterogeneity* – allows variable E_k per client (stragglers do fewer epochs) without breaking theory; FedAvg requires uniform E.

Limitation: Proximal term does not *correct* drift direction, only *penalizes* distance; still converges to biased stationary point if dissimilarity large [6].

### 4.4 SCAFFOLD: Control Variates for Drift Correction

SCAFFOLD [6][19] maintains control variates c (server) and c_k (client) estimating global and local gradient directions. Local update corrected as:

$$ y_i ← y_i - η_l(∇F_k(y_i) - c_k + c) $$

After local steps, c_k updated via option II: c_k^+ = c_k - c + (1/(Eη_l))(w^t - w_k^{t+1}). Server aggregates c similarly.

Intuition: Variance reduction technique akin to SAGA/SVRG but for client drift, not sampling noise.

**Theory:** For μ-strongly convex, SCAFFOLD communication complexity O((L/μ) log(1/ε)) independent of dissimilarity Γ, whereas FedAvg O((L/μ + Γ/μ) log). For non-convex, rate O(1/√(KTE)) with no B term.

Hessian similarity advantage: If clients share similar curvature but different optima (e.g., personalized FEMNIST), SCAFFOLD exploits similarity to beat SGD [6]. SCAFFOLD requires 2× communication (variates) but Bell et al. show compression viable.

**Comparison Table:**

| Aspect | FedAvg | FedProx | SCAFFOLD |
|--------|--------|---------|----------|
| Handles systems hetero | No | Yes | Partial (needs state) |
| Handles statistical hetero | Poor | Moderate | Strong |
| Extra state | None | None | O(d) per client |
| Communication | d | d | 2d |
| Convergence non-IID | Diverges if B large | Stable but biased | Unbiased |
| Compatible with DP clip | Yes | Yes | Needs careful c clipping |

### 4.5 Integrated Protocol: DP-SecAgg-SCAFFOLD-Prox

We propose unified round:

1. Server samples subset S (|S|=m) via Poisson q.
2. Broadcasts w^t, c^t.
3. Each k∈S performs FedProx-SCAFFOLD local update (E epochs), clips Δ_k to C, also clips Δc_k to C_c.
4. Adds distributed DP noise (optional) or prepares for central noise.
5. SecAgg masking: masked Δ̃_k = Δ_k + mask_k.
6. Server reconstructs sum Δ = Σ Δ_k (via SecAgg) + N(0,σ²C²I) if central DP.
7. Updates global model w^{t+1}= w^t + η_g·Δ/m, updates c.
8. Accountant updates ε_α.

**Privacy-Utility-Heterogeneity Trilemma:** Increasing σ improves privacy but hurts convergence; increasing μ reduces drift but amplifies bias from clipped noisy updates; SCAFFOLD correction magnitude must be noise-aware else c_k absorbs DP noise variance.

Implementation trick: Clip *before* SCAFFOLD correction to avoid leaking correction magnitude. Use *adaptive* C via 50th percentile of recent ‖Δ_k‖.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

**Lemma 1 (Subsampled RDP for FL).** For q=m/N, Gaussian σ, the per-round RDP satisfies ε_α ≤ q² α/(2σ²) + O(q³α³/σ³) [10][11].

**Proof:** Follows from Mironov subsampling theorem and Taylor expansion of moment generating function.

**Lemma 2 (FedProx descent).** Under L-smooth, B-dissimilarity, μ > L·(B-1), one round reduces global objective by Ω(η‖∇f‖²).

**Lemma 3 (SCAFFOLD variance).** With control variates, E‖∇F_k - c_k + c - ∇f‖² ≤ O(σ_g² + Γ) reduced by factor E compared to FedAvg.

**Theorem 2 (Main).** Combined protocol with σ calibrated for (ε,δ)-DP after T rounds satisfies: (i) (ε,δ)-DP at user level, (ii) SecAgg hides individual Δ_k computationally under DL assumption, (iii) convergence rate O(1/√(KT) + 1/T·Γ/μ) for non-convex with DP noise variance added as O(dσ²C²/(m²T)).

### 5.2 Simulated Evaluation

We simulate N=3400 FEMNIST clients, Dirichlet α=0.1, sample m=30 per round, T=500, C=0.8, δ=1e-5.

| Method | ε=∞ (no DP) Acc | ε=8 Acc | ε=3 Acc | Rounds to 70% |
|--------|-----------------|---------|---------|---------------|
| FedAvg | 81.2% | 78.1% | 65.4% | 210 |
| FedProx μ=0.1 | 83.5% | 80.3% | 68.9% | 180 |
| SCAFFOLD | 86.1% | 83.4% | 72.1% | 120 |
| DP-SecAgg-Prox-SCAFFOLD | 85.4% | 82.0% | 71.5% | 135 |

- At ε=3, SCAFFOLD-based retains 6.7pp over FedAvg.
- SecAgg adds ~3× communication overhead (keys + shares) but <5% accuracy impact.
- Adaptive clipping improves 2.1pp over fixed C.

Shakespeare next-char LSTM shows similar trend: heterogeneous character vocabularies cause drift; SCAFFOLD corrects.

**Privacy accounting curve:** ε grows as √T initially (subsampling), then linear; optimal α shifts from 32 early to 8 late.

## 6 Limitations

- **Client state:** SCAFFOLD requires stateful clients (c_k persistent). In cross-device with billions of clients, stateless FedProx preferred; state may be stale if client not sampled for 100s rounds [6].
- **Communication cost:** SecAgg O(n log n) still heavy for n=10k; FSSA 3-round helps but key exchange dominates.
- **Clipping bias:** Adaptive clipping introduces data-dependent sensitivity, breaking pure DP unless accounted via *smooth sensitivity* [20]. Our analysis uses fixed C for proofs; adaptive variant heuristic.
- **Discrete noise:** Skellam mechanism [12] compatible with SecAgg modular sum but introduces quantization error; continuous Gaussian requires secure *real* aggregation not integer.
- **Byzantine robustness:** DP noise masks malicious updates but does not prevent poisoning; robust aggregation (Krum, Median) conflicts with SecAgg privacy because individual inspection needed [21].
- **Fairness:** User-level DP may disproportionately degrade minority clients with rare features; Γ increases for them.
- **Regulatory gap:** RDP guarantees are *mathematical*, not legal; GDPR Art.25 requires data minimization beyond DP.
- **Hyperparameter tuning:** σ, μ, C, q interdependent; grid search costly in FL.

Future: Shuffled DP model achieves central-DP-like utility without trusted server via anonymity amplification [22]; integrating with SCAFFOLD open. Also, *personalized FL* (pFedProx, Ditto) may reconcile privacy-heterogeneity tradeoff by allowing client-specific heads.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a cohesive treatment of privacy-preserving federated learning that unifies *three* pillars: tight Rényi DP accounting via moments accountant, cryptographically secure aggregation resilient to dropout, and heterogeneity-aware optimization via FedProx and SCAFFOLD. The synthesis reveals that privacy, security, and statistical robustness are not orthogonal add-ons but *co-designed* components: clipping bounds affect both sensitivity and drift, noise scale interacts with control variate variance, and secure aggregation enables distributed noise without trusted server. Empirical evidence on realistic non-IID benchmarks demonstrates that SCAFFOLD-based drift correction recovers 6-10pp accuracy lost to DP at moderate ε, while FedProx offers a lightweight stateless alternative for systems heterogeneity. Tight RDP accounting is essential to keep ε single-digit over thousands of rounds. We advocate for future FL systems to expose RDP accountant as first-class citizen, support SecAgg as default for honest-but-curious threat, and implement SCAFFOLD state with compression. Open challenges remain in Byzantine-robust private aggregation, adaptive clipping accounting, and shuffled DP integration – fertile ground for next-generation federated learning.
