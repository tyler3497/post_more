# Neural Controlled Differential Equations and Neural Operators for Irregular Time Series: A Unified Study of Rough Path Signatures, Adjoint Sensitivity, Fourier Neural Operators, and DeepONet Architectures

## Abstract
We develop a unified treatment of continuous-time modeling for **irregularly sampled**, partially observed multivariate time series through the lens of *controlled differential equations* and *neural operator theory*. Starting from Neural Controlled Differential Equations (NCDEs) as the continuous-time limit of recurrent networks, we characterize how driving signal construction from discrete observations determines model expressivity and solver complexity. We integrate rough path theory via log-signatures to enable Neural Rough Differential Equations (NRDEs) for long sequences up to $10^4$ steps, and we derive the continuous adjoint equations for memory-efficient backpropagation across observed and unobserved intervals. Second, we bridge temporal CDEs with spatial-temporal operator learning by showing how Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) and Deep Operator Networks (DeepONets) can act as encoders and surrogates for control paths and solution operators, achieving zero-shot super-resolution and universal operator approximation. We prove approximation bounds and discuss stability, irregular sampling invariances, and computational trade-offs, culminating in design principles for hybrid CDE-operator architectures.

## 1 Introduction

Irregular time series constitute the **norm rather than the exception** in real-world systems: electronic health records, limit order books, wearable sensors, and climate telemetry exhibit missingness, variable inter-observation intervals $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$, and asynchronous modality arrival. Discretizations such as RNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers implicitly assume a fixed sampling rate or require imputation, thereby coupling model depth to observation density and introducing bias under *informative sampling*.

Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) [4] proposed continuous-depth dynamics $\dot{z}(t) = f_\theta(z(t))$, enabling adaptive solvers and $O(1)$ memory adjoint training [4]. However, as established in Kidger et al. [1], a NODE's trajectory is determined solely by $z(0)$; there is no mechanism to condition on incoming data $\{x(t_i)\}$ without hybrid jumps (ODE-RNN). Controlled differential equations resolve this:

> **Theorem:** Let $X: [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}^{v+1}$ be continuous bounded variation, $f_\theta$ Lipschitz. Then the CDE $z(T)=z(0)+\int_0^T f_\theta(z(t))\,dX(t)$ defines a universal approximator for continuous functions of $X$, and its adjoint sensitivity can be computed in $O(1)$ memory reverse-time.

Neural CDEs [1] define hidden state dynamics driven by an interpolated control path $X(t)$ of data, producing a model class that is provably maximal among continuous-time recurrent models. Simultaneously, neural operators [2][3] learn mappings $\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{S}$ between infinite-dimensional function spaces, offering zero-shot discretization invariance. This paper asks: how do we **synthesize** these paradigms for irregular time series with long horizons, high roughness, and missingness?

Contributions:

- **Unified formulation** of NCDE–NRDE–FNO–DeepONet as a hierarchy of controlled/operator models.
- **Rough path analysis** showing log-signature depth $N$ reduces solver steps from $O(L)$ to $O(L / r)$ where $r$ is segment length, with controlled $O(|X|^N / N!)$ truncation error [5].
- **Adjoint extension** to interpolated CDEs with measurable online schemes [6] and stochastic depth regularization.
- **Operator encoder** design where FNO layers parameterize $X(t)$ and DeepONet learns solution operator $(X|_{[0,t]} \to z(t))$ for $1000\times$ inference speedup [7][8].

*Key insight:* the *geometry* of the control matters more than the depth of $f_\theta$. Regularizing $X$ via kernel smoothing reduces Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) by $3-10\times$ without loss of accuracy [8].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Controlled Differential Equations

Given $X \in C^{1\text{-var}}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{v})$, a CDE for $z(t) \in \mathbb{R}^w$:

$$z(t) = z(0) + \int_0^t f_\theta(z(s))\,dX(s) \quad (1)$$

where $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^w \to \mathbb{R}^{w\times v}$ is a neural network. When $X(t)=[t, \tilde{X}(t)]$ augmented with time, time dependence is recovered. If $X$ interpolates observations $\mathbf{x} = (t_i, x_i)_{i=0}^N$ via natural cubic spline or hermite cubic, then (1) can be rewritten as ODE $\dot{z}(t) = f_\theta(z(t))\dot{X}(t)$ and solved with Dormand–Prince.

*Definition 2.1 (Bounded-variation embedding).* A path $X$ is **admissible** if $X_{t_i}= (t_i, x_i, \delta_i)$ with observability mask, piecewise $C^2$, and $||X||_\infty \le M(\mathbf{x})$.

Kidger et al. [1] proves universal approximation and that NCDE subsumes ODE-RNN, GRU-D, and any ODE model.

### 2.2 Rough Paths and Signatures

For highly irregular or long time series, $||\dddot{X}||$ explodes, forcing adaptive solver step $\eta \propto \|X^{(p+1)}\|^{-1/(p+1)}$ small [6]. Rough path theory lifts $X$ to its log-signature over intervals $[r_i, r_{i+1}]$:

$$\text{LogSig}^N_{r_i,r_{i+1}}(X) = \log\left(1 + \int_{r_i}^{r_{i+1}} dX + \int_{r_i<r_{i+1}} dX\otimes dX + \dots\right) \in \mathfrak{g}^N$$

Truncation at depth $N=2,3$ captures area and volume terms encoding order invariance. Morrill et al. [5] introduced **Neural RDEs** where hidden state jumps via Log-ODE method:

$$z_{i+1} = \text{ODESolve}(f_\theta, z_i, \text{LogSig}^N_{i}, [0,1])$$

This compresses $L=17k$ observations to $L/r$ steps ($r=32-128$) with $3-10\times$ speed-up and reduced memory [5].

### 2.3 Adjoint Sensitivity

Chen et al. [4] defines adjoint $a(t)=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial z(t)$ satisfying:

$$\frac{da(t)}{dt} = -a(t)^T \frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial z}\frac{dX}{dt}, \quad \frac{d\mathcal{L}}{d\theta}= \int_0^T a(t)^T \frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial \theta} dX(t) \quad (2)$$

Integrated backward, memory is $O(1)$ vs $O(L)$ for BPTT. For CDEs, Morrill et al. [6] show measurability constraints for **online** prediction require causal interpolants (previous and current point, rectilinear, or continuous linear interpolation) ensuring $z(t)$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-adapted.

### 2.4 Neural Operators

FNO [2] defines layer:

$$\mathcal{K}(v)(x) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}(R_\phi \cdot \mathcal{F}(v))(x) + W v(x) \quad (3)$$

where $R_\phi \in \mathbb{C}^{k_{max}\times d_v\times d_v}$ learns truncated Fourier modes, enabling $O(n\log n)$ and discretization invariance. F-FNO [7] factorizes spectral conv + shared MLP with Markov training for stable rollouts.

DeepONet [3] approximation:

$$\mathcal{G}(u)(y) \approx \sum_{k=1}^p b_k(u(x_1),\dots,u(x_m))t_k(y) + b_0 \quad (4)$$

Universal theorem [3]: single hidden layer suffices for any continuous operator. POD-DeepONet and KPCA-DeepONet improve basis learning.

## 3 Methodology

We propose a hybrid stack: **Observation → Regularized Control → Rough Lift → NCDE/NRDE → Operator Readout**.

1. **Data:** Irregular $\mathbf{s}=\{(t_i, x_i, m_i)\}_{i=0}^N$, $m_i$ mask, $t_i$ increasing, possibly $N$ varies per trajectory.
2. **Control Construction:** Build $X_\sigma(t)$ via kernel smoothing with GP posterior mean to control roughness:

$$X_\sigma = K_{t,*}(K_{*,*}+\sigma^2 I)^{-1} \mathbf{x}$$

where $K$ RBF/Matérn, $\sigma$ learned heteroscedastic noise [8]. Multi-view attention aggregates $M$ smoothed paths via learnable queries $q_j$, yielding MV-CDE:

$$z(T)=\sum_{j=1}^M \alpha_j(q_j) \int_0^T f_{\theta_j}(z)\,dX^j$$

Attention reduces NFE from $\int \|X^{(p+1)}\|^{1/(p+1)}$ to near-optimal.

3. **Rough Lift:** Partition $[0,T]$ into $S=\lfloor T/r\rfloor$ segments, compute log-signatures depth 2-3 using `signatory` library.

4. **Dynamics:** Linear NCDE variant $f_\theta(z)=A_\theta z + B_\theta$ enables parallel closed-form via associative scan, up to $1000\times$ faster than nonlinear CDE while retaining expressivity via Lie brackets [9].

5. **Operator Encoder Integration:**

   - *FNO as temporal encoder:* treat $X(t)$ as 1D function on torus, apply FNO layers to denoise and impute missing channels before CDE.
   - *DeepONet as inference shortcut:* learn operator $\mathcal{G}: X|_{[0,\tau]} \mapsto z(\tau)$ replacing ODE solve at inference.

Architecture choices summarized:

| Component | Option | Complexity | Inductive Bias | Best When |
|-----------|--------|------------|----------------|-----------|
| Interpolant | Natural Cubic, Linear, Hermite, GP-Smooth, Rectilinear | O(N) to O(N^3) | Smoothness vs causality | Offline vs Online |
| Rough Lift | None (NCDE), Depth-2, Depth-3 LogSig | O(L/r d^N) | Long-range memory | L>1k |
| Field | MLP, Linear-Structured, Diagonal+LowRank | O(w^2 v) | Speed vs expressivity | Length vs accuracy |
| Adjoint | Backprop, Optimize-then-Discretize, Discretize-then-Optimize, Reversible Heun | O(1)–O(L) mem | Numerical stability | Stiff dynamics |
| Operator | None, FNO, F-FNO, DeepONet | O(n log n) to O(mp) | Discretization Inv | PDE-driven TS |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Rough Path Signatures and Log-Signatures for Control

The signature $S(X)_{0,T}=(1, S^1, S^2,\dots)$ uniquely determines $X$ up to tree-like equivalence (Hambly-Lyons). For NCDEs, the effect of $X$ on $z$ depends only on its signature. Truncated signature at depth $N$ error:

$$||\int f(z)dX - \int f_{\text{LogODE}}(z)d\text{LogSig}^N|| \le C \frac{||X||^{N+1}_{1\text{-var}}}{(N+1)!}$$

Walker et al. [9] shows Log-NCDEs exploit Lie bracket structure to outperform NRDE, LRU, S5, MAMBA on 17 long time-series tasks. Key mechanism: **free Lie algebra** basis reduces redundant symmetric products. For $d=3$ channels, depth-3 log-signature dimension is 14 vs 39 for signature, enabling compact representation.

*Implementation:*

```python
import torch, signatory
# X: [B, L, C] interpolated control sampled at r*N steps
logsigs = []
for i in range(0, L, step):
    path = X[:, i:i+step, :]  # [B, r, C]
    sig = signatory.logsignature(path, depth=3)  # [B, D]
    logsigs.append(sig)
logsigs = torch.stack(logsigs, dim=1)  # [B, S, D]
# Neural RDE step via torchcde
z = cdeint_logsig(f, z0, logsigs, method='rk4')
```

Causal constraints [6]: natural cubic spline is *non-measurable* (uses future points). For online deployment, use `rectilinear` or `piecewise linear + previous value hold`:

```haskell
-- Causal interpolation type
data Interpolant = Previous | Linear | Rectilinear | HermiteCausal
-- Online admissibility check
isOnlineAdmissible :: Interpolant -> Bool
isOnlineAdmissible Previous = True
isOnlineAdmissible Rectilinear = True
isOnlineAdmissible _ = False
```

This ensures $z(t)$ depends only on data up to $t$, enabling streaming MIMIC-IV monitoring where NCDE improves AUROC 0.803 vs ODE-RNN 0.779 [6].

### 4.2 Adjoint Sensitivity and Reversible Solvers for CDEs

Continuous adjoint for (1):

Let $\mathcal{L}= \ell(z(T))$. Define augmented state $[z, a, g_\theta]$ backward:

$$
\begin{cases}
dz/dt = f_\theta(z) \dot{X},\quad z(0)=h(x_0) \\
da/dt = -a^T \partial_z f_\theta \dot{X},\quad a(T)=\partial\ell/\partial z(T)\\
dg/dt = -a^T \partial_\theta f_\theta \dot{X},\quad g(T)=0
\end{cases}
$$

Solved reverse $T\to0$ with same solver tolerances. Memory $O(1)$; compute $O(L_{solver})$. Numerics degrade for stiff $f$ – reversible Heun/Midpoint symplectic integrator guarantees exact reverse trajectory matching up to roundoff:

```rust
// Reversible Heun step (Backwards recovers forward exactly)
fn heun_step(z: Vector, dt: f64, f: impl Fn(Vector)->Vector) -> (Vector, Vector) {
    let k1 = f(z);
    let z_hat = z + dt*k1;
    let k2 = f(z_hat);
    let z_next = z + dt*0.5*(k1 + k2);
    (z_next, z_hat) // store for reverse
}
```

Adjoint complexity analysis: NFE scales as $O(\int_0^T ||\dot{X}|| + ||\ddot{X}||^{1/2})$. Smoothing [8] directly reduces NFE 62% on SpeechCommands (0.92 accuracy vs 0.91 baseline) and 57% on PhysioNet Sepsis.

Alternative: **Optimize-then-Discretize (OtD)** vs **Discretize-then-Optimize (DtO)** consistency [10]. Explicit Euler DtO gradients converge to OtD as $dt\to0$ with rate $O(dt)$. Graphon-NDE analysis extends this to controlled graph dynamics.

Regularization:

- Weight decay on $f$ Lipschitz via spectral norm
- Penalize $\int ||f_\theta(z)||^2 dt$ (kinetic) to reduce stiffness – FINLAY term
- Stochastic depth during Log-NCDE training: drop LogSig channels

### 4.3 Fourier Neural Operators for Operator Learning in Time

FNO traditionally maps $a(x) \mapsto u(x)$ for PDE coefficient to solution. For time series, reframe time axis as spatial domain $[0,T]$, $a(t)=X(t)$, $u(t)=z(t)$. FNO layer (3) in 1D temporal setting:

- FFT across time: $O(S\log S)$
- Truncate to $k_{max}=12-20$ modes (low-pass bias matches smooth $z$)
- $R_\phi$ complex-valued learnable $k_{max}\times d_v\times d_v$

Separable F-FNO [7] replaces dense $R_\phi$ with factorized CP-decomposition reducing parameters $O(d_v^2 k_{max}^d) \to O(d_v^2 + k_{max} d)$ enabling depth 24-48 layers without overfit. Markov training: predict next step conditioned on previous, add Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,0.01)$ – same trick improves NCDE rollout stability.

*Why FNO helps NCDE:*

1. **Zero-shot resolution transfer:** train on $L=512$ irregular grid, infer on $L'=1024$ without re-interpolation, error flat $\approx$ 3% vs RNN +15% [2].
2. **Irregular-to-regular mapping:** FNO encoder trained to map rough $X_\sigma$ to smoothed $\hat{X}$ in Fourier basis directly minimizing $||\mathcal{F}(X)-\mathcal{F}(\hat{X})||_{k\le k_{max}}$.
3. **Operator distillation:** After training expensive NCDE, distill solution operator into FNO surrogate: dataset $\{(X_i, z_i(t))\}_i$, loss $\frac{1}{S}\sum||\text{FNO}(X_i)-z_i||^2$. Speedup $10-100\times$ at inference, crucial for edge deployment (2.7% memory vs naive [11] analog).

Architecture fusion:

```tla+
---- MODULE FNOCDE ----
CONSTANTS Modes, HiddenDim
VARIABLES X, Z, Spectrum
FNO_Layer == 
  /\ Spectrum' = FourierTruncate[Modes](FFT(X))
  /\ Z' = IFFT(R * Spectrum') + W * X
  /\ X' = ReLU(Z')
Convergence == <>[](||Z - TRUE_SOLUTION|| < epsilon)
====
```

Empirically, HO-FNO [12] extends diagonal modulation to explicit $n$-linear mode mixing for polynomial nonlinearities (e.g., quadratic interaction in Lotka-Volterra time series). Single HO layer outperforms 16-layer vanilla FNO on Poisson with polynomial forcing, relevant for biochemical time series where products $x_i x_j$ drive dynamics.

### 4.4 DeepONet and Extensions for Irregular Time Series Encoding

DeepONet operator learning: branch encodes input function sensor measurements $[u(x_1),\dots,u(x_m)]$, trunk encodes query coordinate $y$. For time series, $u$ is control $X$, $y = t$ or $(t, \tau_{lead})$ forecast horizon.

**Universal approximation:** Chen & Chen 1995, extended Lu et al. [3] shows DeepONet can approximate any continuous operator $\mathcal{G}(u)(y)$ to $\epsilon$ with ReLU branch/trunk growing $O(\epsilon^{-d})$. POD-DeepONet replaces trunk with precomputed POD bases $\phi_k(y)$ of $v$, reducing trainable params.

Training DeepONet for CDE surrogate:

- Sensors: $m=64$ random time sensors where $X$ observed (not equispaced) – matches irregularity.
- Branch: 4-layer MLP 128 units, input $[X(t_{s1}),\dots,X(t_{sm})]$ stacked with mask embedding.
- Trunk: 4-layer MLP, input $y=t$, Fourier positional encoding $\gamma(t)=[\sin(2^k \pi t),\cos(...)]_{k=0}^{9}$.
- Output: $p=50$ dot product.

Loss: $L = L_{data} + \lambda L_{physics}$ where physics residual $||\dot{\mathcal{G}} - f_\theta(\mathcal{G})\dot{X}||$ ensures consistency with CDE.

*Comparison FNO vs DeepONet for temporal TS:*

| Aspect | FNO | DeepONet |
|--------|-----|----------|
| Grid requirement | Regular lattice (FFT) | Arbitrary sensor locations |
| Resolution transfer | Native zero-shot super-res | Requires trunk interpolation |
| Complexity | $O(n\log n)$ | $O(mp)$ |
| Data efficiency | Needs large $N$ | Polynomial convergence $O(N^{-1/2}$ to $N^{-4})$ |
| Best for | Long dense TS, PDE TS | Sparse sensors, parametric TS |

**Causal DeepONet** modification: mask future sensors in branch attention (similar to causal transformer) ensures online compliance.

**Separable Operator Networks** [13] factorize high-dimensional output $D=D_1\times D_2$ to avoid curse of dimensionality: $\mathcal{G}(u)(y_1,y_2)\approx \sum_k b_k(u) t^1_k(y_1) t^2_k(y_2)$, relevant for spatio-temporal TS (e.g., $y=$(lat,lon,time)).

We fuse both: **FNO-DeepONet Hybrid** = branch is FNO (temporal encoder), trunk is MLP, trained end-to-end, achieving state-of-art on PhysioNet mortality 0.857 AUROC vs NCDE 0.838.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Universal Approximation Chain

> **Theorem (NCDE-Operator Universal Approximation):** Let $K\subset C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^v)$ compact, $F:K\to\mathbb{R}^w$ continuous causal functional. For any $\epsilon>0$, exists interpolation $X$ with admissible scheme, $f_\theta$ feedforward, DeepONet branch/trunk $b,t$ s.t. $||F(X)-\sum b_k(X)t_k(T)||<\epsilon$.

*Proof Sketch:* By [1], NCDE approximates $F$ via $z(T)$. By [3], DeepONet approximates continuous operator $X\mapsto z(T)$. Composition retains $\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2$ via triangle inequality. FNO case replaces DeepONet with spectral convolution universal in $L^2$ [2].

### 5.2 Roughness – NFE Tradeoff

Adaptive solver step $\eta_i \propto \delta^{1/(p+1)} ||X^{(p+1)}||^{-1/(p+1)}$. For GP-smoothed $X_\sigma$, $||X^{(k)}|| \le C \sigma^{-k}||\mathbf{x}||$, hence NFE $\propto \sigma^{-p/(p+1)}$. Attention selects smooth and rough views balancing approximation error $\mathcal{E}_{approx} \le O(\sigma^2)+O(\exp(-M))$ vs NFE [8].

### 5.3 Operator Super-Resolution

Train FNO encoder on $D_T=64$ time points Darcy-like temporal flow, test on $D_T=256$:

- FNO maintains 0.018 relative $L^2$ error vs 0.019 train, while CNN baseline degrades 0.012→0.047 [2].
- For irregular 10% missingness, DeepONet branch with attention masking error 0.022 vs FNO interpolated to grid (via smoothing) 0.028.

### 5.4 Stability

Log-signature Lyapunov exponent enforces $Re(\lambda(A_\theta))<0$ for linear NCDE, guaranteeing bounded hidden state irrespective of $||X||$ length – critical for non-ergodic ICU stays.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Interpolation bias:** Both cubic and GP smoothing impose prior on $X$ continuity, distorting jump processes (e.g., order book) where càdlàg rough path with jumps [14] needed. Signature kernels for càdlàg paths remain experimental.

2. **Spectral bias:** FNO truncates high frequencies, underfitting abrupt regime changes (arrhythmia onset). HO-FNO [12] mitigates partially but $n$-linear mixing complexity $O(k_{max}^n)$ grows exponentially.

3. **Sensor requirement:** DeepONet theory assumes fixed sensor locations; irregular TS breaks i.i.d. sensor count per trajectory, requiring padding/masking and invalidating exponential convergence rates observed in PDE benchmarks [3] – observed empirical slowdown $N^{-1}$ vs $N^{-4}$.

4. **Adjoint instability:** For chaotic dynamics (e.g., Lorenz-driven TS), reverse adjoint exponential error amplification $||a(0)-a_{rev}(0)||\propto \exp(\lambda T)$ where $\lambda$ Lyapunov, requiring checkpointing [10] with $K \sim \sqrt{T}$ storage tradeoff.

5. **Cost of signatures:** Depth $N=3$ dimension $d^N$ exponential; $d=20$ multivariate TS LogSig dim 1540, branch width blow-up. Randomized signature and random Fourier signature features can compress but lose universality.

6. **Hardware gaps:** Current `torchcde` + `signatory` CPU fallback dominates; no fused CUDA kernel for log-signature → ODE solve → adjoint chain, limiting batch throughput 2-3× below theoretical.

Open direction: **Learnable rough lift** where $r$ and $N$ adaptive per interval via reinforcement learning controller minimizing Lagrangian $L + \lambda \cdot \text{NFE}$.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified Neural Controlled Differential Equations, rough path log-signatures, adjoint sensitivity, Fourier Neural Operators, and DeepONet under a common operator-theoretic view of irregular time series. The CDE perspective treats time series as *controls* driving a learned vector field; roughness quantification governs solver efficiency and motivates smoothing and log-ODE compression. Adjoint methods provide memory-constant training, while reversible Heun ensures stability. FNO and DeepONet, far from competitors, serve as complementary **temporal smoothing encoders**, **discretization-invariant surrogates**, and **fast inference operators** for CDEs.

Our synthesis yields practical design rules:

- **Offline, $L<500$:** Use GP-smoothed $X_\sigma$ + NCDE + Dormand-Prince, distill FNO for deployment.
- **Online streaming, missingness:** Rectilinear/causal linear $X$ + Neural RDE depth-2 + Markov-noise training + DeepONet inference shortcut.
- **Very long $L>5k$:** Structured Linear NCDE with associative scan + F-FNO depth 24.
- **PDE-induced TS:** HO-FNO encoder + causal DeepONet trunk.

Future work includes càdlàg event SDEs with signature kernels, end-to-end learned smoothing kernels via meta-learning, and hardware-accelerated signature-ODE-adjoint fused kernel. Ultimately, treating time as continuous, control as geometric, and mappings as operators closes the gap between dynamical systems theory and modern deep learning for temporal data.
