# Selective State Space Models for Sequence Modeling: From HiPPO Theory to Mamba and State Space Duality

## Abstract

Sequence modeling has long been torn between the expressivity of attention and the efficiency of recurrence. State space models (SSMs) resolve this tension by parameterizing sequence transformations through continuous-time linear dynamical systems with structured state matrices. This thesis develops the full theoretical arc of modern SSMs: the HiPPO framework for online function approximation via optimal polynomial projections [2], the Structured State Space sequence model (S4) and its discretization theory [1], and Mamba's input-dependent selective mechanism that restores content-based reasoning in linear time [3]. We formalize discretization under zero-order hold and bilinear (Tustin) rules, analyze the parallel associative scan that makes selective recurrence trainable, and describe the hardware-aware kernel design behind Mamba's 5x inference throughput advantage. We derive Mamba-2's state space duality (SSD) [4], showing selective SSMs and structured linear attention are two faces of semiseparable matrix multiplication, contrast O(N) recurrent with O(N^2) attention complexity, and survey language-modeling results where Mamba-3B matches transformers twice its size.

## 1. Introduction

The Transformer [1] has dominated sequence modeling since 2017, yet its self-attention mechanism carries a well-known cost: time and memory quadratic in the sequence length N. As foundation models ingest contexts of hundreds of thousands of tokens, this *quadratic bottleneck* has motivated a search for subquadratic architectures—linear attention [7], gated convolutions, recurrent models, and structured state space models (SSMs). For years, these alternatives traded expressivity for efficiency, systematically underperforming attention on language, a modality demanding *content-based reasoning*: the ability to let the content of the sequence determine what is remembered and what is forgotten [3].

Structured state space models began as an attempt to make long-range dependencies tractable. The Linear State Space Layer (LSSL) [5] showed that the classical state space model x′(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t), y(t) = Cx(t) + Du(t) could model sequences up to 16,000 steps, but at prohibitive computational cost. S4 [1] solved the efficiency problem through a new parameterization of the state matrix A with a low-rank correction, reducing the SSM to a Cauchy kernel computable in pseudolinear time, and achieved state-of-the-art on the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark. Yet S4 remained *linear time-invariant* (LTI): its dynamics are fixed regardless of input content, fundamentally limiting its ability to perform selective, content-dependent computation.

Mamba [3] broke this barrier with a simple but radical step: making the SSM parameters functions of the input. By allowing the discretization timescale Δ, input matrix B, and output matrix C to vary with each token, Mamba enables *selective propagation*—gating what enters the hidden state and what is read from it. This selection mechanism sacrifices the convolutional view that made S4 efficient, so Mamba compensates with a hardware-aware parallel associative scan that computes the input-varying recurrence in linear time. The result is an architecture that matches or exceeds transformer quality at small to medium scale while scaling linearly, with 5× higher inference throughput [3].

Mamba-2 [4] deepened this program by uncovering a *state space duality* (SSD): selective SSMs and variants of linear attention are dual views of the same computation—multiplication by a structured semiseparable matrix. This duality both yields a 2–8× faster architecture and unifies the theoretical landscape, revealing transformers and SSMs as two facets of one mathematical object.

This thesis reconstructs that arc in full mathematical detail, from the optimal polynomial projections of HiPPO to the semiseparable decompositions of SSD.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Classical State Space Models

The state space model (SSM) originates in control theory as a compact description of a linear dynamical system. Given an input signal u(t) ∈ ℝ, a latent state x(t) ∈ ℝᴺ, and an output y(t) ∈ ℝ, the continuous-time SSM is defined by

> **Definition (Continuous SSM):** x′(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t), y(t) = Cx(t) + Du(t)

with matrices A ∈ ℝᴺˣᴺ, B ∈ ℝᴺˣ¹, C ∈ ℝ¹ˣᴺ, and scalar feedthrough D. Solving the linear ODE yields the *convolution representation* y = K ∗ u, where the kernel K = (CB, CAB, CA²B, ...) is a Krylov sequence determined by (A, B, C). Three equivalent views coexist: the **continuous** ODE view, the **recurrent** discrete view, and the **convolutional** discrete view [5]. LTI systems admit all three; this equivalence is the foundation on which S4 builds—and the equivalence Mamba deliberately breaks.

### 2.2 The HiPPO Framework: Optimal Online Function Approximation

Long-range memory requires a mechanism for compressing an unbounded history f(≤t) into a fixed-size state. The HiPPO framework [2] formulates this as an *online function approximation* problem: maintain the coefficients c(t) ∈ ℝᴺ of the projection of the history onto a polynomial basis {gₙ} under a time-varying measure μ⁽ᵗ⁾.

> **Theorem (HiPPO ODE):** For measures such as the exponentially-decaying LegS or the uniform LegT, the optimal projection coefficients satisfy dc/dt = A(t)c(t) + B(t)f(t), where (A, B) are explicit structured matrices (e.g., the HiPPO-LegS matrix with Aₙₖ = −(2n+1)¹ᐟ²(2k+1)¹ᐟ² for n > k, −(n+1) for n = k) [2].

HiPPO answers the critical question of *initialization*: it tells us exactly which matrix A endows a state space model with long-range memory. S4 initializes with the HiPPO matrix (in diagonalized or DPLR form), giving the model a theoretically grounded memory of the entire past [1]. Approximation bounds show that projecting onto degree-N polynomials yields error decaying as O(N⁻ᵏ) for k-times differentiable signals [2].

### 2.3 S4 and the Cauchy Kernel

A naive discretization of the HiPPO-initialized SSM is numerically unstable and O(N²L)-expensive. S4's core contribution is a reparameterization: write A = Λ − PQ*, a diagonal matrix Λ plus a low-rank correction PQ* (the DPLR form of the HiPPO matrix) [1]. This enables:

- **Stable diagonalization** of A, reducing the kernel computation to a Cauchy-like matrix product evaluated in O(N + L) time via the Cauchy kernel algorithm.
- **Pseudolinear training** through FFT-based convolution, since LTI recurrence equals convolution by K = (CB, CAB, ...).
- **Strong empirical results**: 91% on sequential CIFAR-10 without augmentation, SoTA on every LRA task including the 16k-length Path-X task that all prior work failed on, and 60× faster generation than transformers on language modeling [1].

S4D later showed that a purely diagonal A (initialized with HiPPO-like structure) recovers S4's performance on most tasks [1], suggesting that diagonalizability, not the low-rank correction per se, is the key structural property.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Discretization of Linear SSMs: ZOH and Bilinear Rules

To use a continuous SSM on discrete data with step size Δ, we must discretize. Two canonical rules govern the map (A, B, C) ↦ (Ā, B̄, C̄) [5]:

| Rule | Ā | B̄ | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero-order hold (ZOH)** | e^(ΔA) | (ΔA)⁻¹(e^(ΔA) − I)·ΔB | Exact for piecewise-constant inputs; preserves stability |
| **Bilinear / Tustin** | (I − ΔA/2)⁻¹(I + ΔA/2) | (I − ΔA/2)⁻¹ΔB | Maps left half-plane to unit disk exactly; second-order accurate |
| **Simplified ZOH (Mamba)** | e^(ΔA) | B·Δ | First-order approximation valid for small Δ‖A‖; used by S6 |

> **Theorem (Stability preservation):** If A is Hurwitz (eigenvalues in the left half-plane), then both ZOH and bilinear discretization produce Schur-stable Ā (spectral radius < 1), guaranteeing the discrete recurrence xₖ = Āxₖ₋₁ + B̄uₖ is bounded-input bounded-output stable [5].

The timescale Δ is not merely a numerical artifact: it controls the *memory resolution* of the model. Large Δ coarsens the discretization (fast forgetting), small Δ preserves fine detail. Mamba exploits this by making Δ a function of the input, turning discretization into a gating mechanism [3].

### 3.2 The Selective Mechanism (S6)

The decisive innovation of Mamba is the *selection* mechanism: the parameters Δ, B, C become functions of the input sequence x ∈ ℝᴸˣᴰ [3]:

```python
# Mamba (S6) selective discretization, per-token
def selective_scan_step(x_t, h, A, W_B, W_C, W_d, tau=softplus):
    delta = tau(W_d @ x_t)            # (N,) input-dependent timescale
    B_t   = W_B @ x_t                 # (N,) input-dependent input matrix
    C_t   = W_C @ x_t                 # (N,) input-dependent output matrix
    A_bar = torch.exp(delta * A)      # ZOH discretization of A
    B_bar = B_t * delta               # simplified ZOH
    h = A_bar * h + B_bar * x_t       # state update (elementwise per head)
    y_t = (C_t * h).sum()             # linear readout
    return y_t, h
```

Because Δₜ now varies with the token, the model can *reset* its state (Δₜ → ∞ collapses memory of hₜ₋₁, letting Bₜxₜ dominate) or *ignore* the current token (Δₜ → 0 freezes the state). The matrices Bₜ and Cₜ provide content-dependent write and read addressing, analogous to the query/key/value mechanism of attention [3]. Crucially, this makes the recurrence **linear time-varying** (LTV): it can no longer be computed as a convolution, and naive sequential evaluation would be too slow for training.

### 3.3 The Mamba Block Architecture

Mamba simplifies prior SSM architectures by fusing the H3 gated block with the transformer's MLP into a single homogeneous block [3]: the input is projected, passed through a depthwise causal convolution and the selective SSM on one branch, gated by a SiLU-activated projection on the other branch, then projected back. Notably, Mamba uses **no attention and no MLP blocks**—the selective SSM is the sole sequence-mixing operator, with most parameters in the linear projections. This design concentrates capacity where selectivity lives, and it is this minimalism that lets the model's quality-per-parameter rival transformers [3].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Parallel Associative Scan: Making Selection Trainable

Training an LTV recurrence sequentially would require O(N) depth and forfeit GPU parallelism. The rescue is an algebraic fact: first-order linear recurrences compose through an *associative* binary operator, enabling the parallel prefix (Blelloch) scan [8].

> **Theorem (Scan decomposition):** Define per-step elements eₖ = (Āₖ, B̄ₖxₖ) and the operator eᵢ ⊗ eⱼ = (ĀⱼĀᵢ, Āⱼ(B̄ᵢxᵢ) + B̄ⱼxⱼ). Then ⊗ is associative, and the scan over (e₁,...,e_L) computes all prefixes (ΠĀ, Σ ΠĀ·B̄x) — i.e., every hidden state hₖ — in O(log L) parallel depth with O(L) total work [8].

In the diagonal case (Mamba's A is diagonal per head), the operator reduces to elementwise multiplies and adds, mapping perfectly onto GPU threads. Smith et al. [6] first applied this to S5's MIMO recurrence; Mamba extends it to the selective, input-varying case. The scan gives the best of both worlds: *linear total work with logarithmic depth*, exactly what training requires.

### 4.2 Hardware-Aware Kernel Design

Even with the scan, a naive implementation would shuttle the state h (size N per head) between slow HBM and the chip for every timestep. Mamba's hardware-aware algorithm [3] exploits the memory hierarchy of modern GPUs:

1. **Kernel fusion**: discretization, scan, and multiplication by C are fused into a single kernel—parameters Δ, B, C are computed on-chip in SRAM from the input projection without materializing in HBM.
2. **Recomputation**: intermediate states of the scan are not saved for the backward pass; they are recomputed on the fly, trading FLOPs for memory bandwidth (the true bottleneck).
3. **Parallel scan in SRAM**: the associative scan executes entirely in fast SRAM across the sequence chunk, with only O(N) state (not O(NL)) ever touching HBM.

This yields the headline result: computation and memory scale *linearly* in L during training, and autoregressive inference requires only **O(1) time per step** with a constant-size state—no KV cache, hence the 5× throughput advantage over transformers [3].

### 4.3 State Space Duality: Mamba-2 and the SSD Framework

Mamba-2 [4] reveals that selective SSMs and linear attention are *dual* computations. Consider the scalar-identity structured SSM used in Mamba-2, where A is a scalar aₜ times the identity (one timescale per head). The output can be written as

> **Theorem (SSD duality):** y = Mx with M a **1-semiseparable matrix** Mⱼᵢ = Cⱼᵀ Aⱼ:ᵢ Bᵢ where Aⱼ:ᵢ = aᵢ₊₁···aⱼ (and 0 for i > j). This same matrix admits two decompositions: a **recurrent (quadratic/linear) form** — the selective scan — and a **dual (quadratic) form** yⱼ = Σᵢ≤ⱼ CⱼᵀBᵢ · (Π a) xᵢ, which is exactly masked linear attention with a data-dependent decay mask [4].

The SSD framework decomposes M into blocks and computes each block in the dual (attention-like, matrix-multiply) form while propagating states between blocks in recurrent form—mirroring how FlashAttention tiles the attention computation. Because the scalar-A structure lets the state be a matrix (N×P) rather than a vector, Mamba-2 expands the state 8× larger than Mamba while remaining 2–8× faster, and its block design permits tensor-parallel sharding like transformer attention heads [4]. Philosophically, SSD dissolves the SSM-vs-attention dichotomy: *transformers are SSMs* with a particular structured kernel, and Mamba-2 is the architecture that lives at their intersection.

### 4.4 Complexity Analysis: O(N) Recurrence vs O(N²) Attention

The asymptotic comparison is stark:

| Architecture | Training FLOPs | Inference memory | Per-step decode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer | O(N²d) | O(Nd) KV cache | O(Nd) |
| S4 (LTI) | O(N log N · d) via FFT | O(Nd) state | O(Nd) |
| Mamba | O(Nd) scan | O(Nd) state | O(d), constant |
| Mamba-2 (SSD) | O(Nd) tiled | O(Nd) state | O(d), constant |

Beyond asymptotics, constants matter: the fused selective scan kernel on an A100 runs up to 3× faster than prior convolution-based SSM implementations [3], and end-to-end Mamba achieves 5× higher generation throughput than an equivalently sized transformer [3]. The practical consequence is that quality improvements from Mamba continue to accrue up to **million-length sequences** [3], a regime where transformers are simply infeasible.

### 4.5 What Selection Buys: Content-Based Reasoning

Why does selection matter so much for language? LTI SSMs like S4 treat every token identically: the kernel K is fixed, so the model cannot *decide* to remember a name introduced in paragraph 1 and forget filler words. The selective copying and induction-heads synthetic tasks [3] isolate this: S4 fails where selective SSMs succeed, because induction heads require binding "A ... B ... A" patterns based on content equality. Making B, C, Δ input-dependent gives the SSM precisely the three roles of attention's Q/K/V: Cₜ selects what to read (query), Bₜ selects what to write (key), and Δₜ gates how much the past is decayed versus refreshed (a learned, per-token forget gate) [3]. In this light, Mamba is best understood not as "an RNN revival" but as *attention with linear complexity*: a mechanism for content-addressable memory whose cost does not explode with context length.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

Mamba's empirical validation spans modalities and scales [3]:

- **Language modeling**: Mamba-3B outperforms transformers of the same size and *matches transformers twice its size* in both pretraining perplexity and downstream zero-shot evaluations. Mamba scales favorably: its advantage grows with sequence length, with gains measured up to 1M tokens.
- **Long Range Arena**: while S4 set the bar with 86.1% average [1], selective SSMs preserve this long-range competence while adding language quality that S4 lacked.
- **Other modalities**: Mamba achieves SoTA on audio (following SaShiMi [1]) and competitive genomics results, supporting the claim of a general sequence backbone.

Mamba-2's results [4] confirm the SSD design: the refined core layer is 2–8× faster than Mamba's selective scan while remaining competitive with transformers on language modeling, and the larger state (SSD permits 8× the state size at equal cost) translates into measurable quality gains on associative-recall-heavy tasks such as multi-query associative recall (MQAR), where Mamba-2 closes the remaining gap to attention.

> **Theorem (Expressivity of selective SSMs, informal):** With input-dependent Δ, B, C and a nonlinear output head, the S6 layer is a universal approximator of regular languages on bounded-length inputs, whereas any fixed LTI SSM with bounded state is not [3, 9]. This formalizes why selectivity is not an engineering convenience but a qualitative expressivity upgrade.

On the theoretical side, the scan's correctness follows from associativity of the pair operator (Section 4.1); SSD's equivalence is an exact algebraic identity of semiseparable matrices, not an approximation [4]; and HiPPO's optimality is exact for polynomial measures under L² projection [2].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Stability of selective discretization.** Mamba's simplified ZOH B̄ = BΔ is only first-order accurate; for large Δ‖A‖ the approximation degrades, and nothing in the selective formulation enforces Schur stability of Āₜ the way the bilinear transform does for LTI systems. Empirically the softplus-bounded Δ keeps training stable, but a rigorous stability theory for input-varying discretization is missing.
2. **State capacity vs. attention.** Even Mamba-2's enlarged state is a fixed-size compression bottleneck; on tasks requiring exact retrieval of many facts (needle-in-haystack at 1M tokens), attention's O(N) cache retains an information-theoretic edge. Hybrid architectures (e.g., interleaving Mamba-2 and attention layers [4]) currently dominate such settings, suggesting the pure-SSM regime still has headroom.
3. **Bidirectionality and non-causal data.** The scan is inherently causal; vision and audio understanding tasks benefit from bidirectional variants whose theory (two scans, fused or separate) is less developed than the unidirectional case.
4. **Theoretical gaps in SSD.** The duality is proven for scalar-identity A; extending SSD to general structured (e.g., diagonal complex) A—recovering S4's richer dynamics—remains open, as does a characterization of which attention variants admit efficient SSM duals and vice versa.
5. **Hardware lottery.** Mamba's speed depends critically on hand-tuned fused kernels; on hardware without fast SRAM hierarchies (or in pure PyTorch), the constant factors erode the asymptotic advantage. Compiler-level abstractions (e.g., Triton-based scans) are closing this gap but are not yet universal.

## 7. Conclusion

From HiPPO's optimal polynomial projections [2] to S4's Cauchy-kernel efficiency [1], to Mamba's selective mechanism and hardware-aware scan [3], to Mamba-2's state space duality unifying SSMs with attention [4], the selective state space program has rewritten the terms of the sequence-modeling debate. The central lesson is that *structure plus selectivity* recovers the content-based reasoning of attention at linear cost: by making discretization timescales and projection matrices functions of the input, SSMs learn what to remember and what to forget; by exploiting associativity and the GPU memory hierarchy, they do so without paying attention's quadratic tax; and by recognizing the semiseparable matrix at the heart of both families, SSD shows the dichotomy was illusory all along. Open problems remain—stability theory for selective discretization, the retrieval capacity of fixed states, and the hardware generality of fused scans—but the trajectory is clear: the future of long-sequence modeling is selective, linear, and increasingly unified with the attention it set out to replace.
