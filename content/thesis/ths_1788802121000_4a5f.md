# Algebraic Verification of BGP Routing Policy Convergence with Batfish: Stable Paths Problem Oscillations, SMT-Encoded Policy Analysis, and Formal Route Selection Models at Cloud Scale

## Abstract

The Border Gateway Protocol selects routes through locally defined policies that need not be globally consistent, and the Stable Paths Problem of Griffin, Shepherd, and Wilfong shows that deciding whether such policies admit any stable routing is NP-complete, while a *dispute wheel* — a cyclic preference dependency among permitted paths — guarantees divergence of the abstract path-vector protocol [3]. This thesis develops an algebraic verification framework for BGP convergence that couples Batfish's declarative configuration analysis [1] with the Minesweeper SMT encoding of stable path states [2], treating BGP policy as an edge-constraint system over permitted paths rather than as simulated message exchange. We formalize the BGP decision process (RFC 4271), including the non-transitive MULTI_EXIT_DISC (MED) comparator that underlies persistent iBGP oscillation under route reflection [4][5], and show how SMT-encoding Griffin-style dispute structures detects oscillations that data-plane snapshot tools miss. We evaluate scaling through Bonsai-style network abstraction [7] and the multilayer graph model of Tiramisu [6], and we report complexity bounds, proof sketches, and measured verification throughput on cloud-scale topologies.

## 1 Introduction

BGP is the only interdomain routing protocol on the Internet, yet unlike RIP or OSPF it does not solve a shortest-paths problem: policy-based metrics are *required* to override distance-based metrics, and autonomous systems define routing policies independently with little global coordination [3]. Convergence — the property every operator assumes — is therefore not guaranteed. Griffin and Wilfong demonstrated persistent oscillation induced by the MULTI_EXIT_DISC attribute [4][5]; the *BGP wedgie* showed a configuration converging to one of several stable states depending on message arrival order [9]; and Griffin, Shepherd, and Wilfong proved that deciding whether a policy configuration admits *any* stable assignment is NP-complete [3].

Operators face three problems. *Detection*: does a configuration contain a policy conflict that prevents convergence? *Diagnosis*: which policies are responsible, and under what failure scenarios do they trigger? *Scale*: can analysis keep pace with cloud networks holding millions of routes? The tooling lineage answers in stages. Batfish [1] parses vendor configurations into a vendor-independent representation and derives data planes declaratively via Network Optimized Datalog, checking properties with an SMT solver. Minesweeper [2] encodes the *stable states themselves* as a logical formula, avoiding message-exchange simulation entirely. Bonsai [7] compresses networks into behavior-preserving abstractions per destination equivalence class, and Tiramisu [6] replaces constraint solving with property-customized graph algorithms over a multilayer model.

This thesis unifies these strands algebraically. Contributions: (i) a formalization of BGP route selection as a lexicographic, partially non-transitive comparator, pinpointing where transitivity fails (MED); (ii) a dispute-wheel detection procedure as an SMT encoding in the Minesweeper style, fed by Batfish-derived policy facts; (iii) proof sketches linking dispute wheels, SPVP divergence, and iBGP oscillation; (iv) an empirical synthesis of scalability evidence from Batfish's evolution study, Minesweeper, and Tiramisu for cloud-scale fabrics.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Stable Paths Problem

Griffin, Shepherd, and Wilfong model interdomain routing as an instance $S = (G, \mathcal{P}, \Lambda)$ of the *Stable Paths Problem* [3]: graph $G$ with origin $0$; per node $v \ne 0$, a set $\mathcal{P}_v$ of *permitted paths* surviving import policy; and ranking functions $\lambda_v$. A *path assignment* $\pi$ is *stable* when each node holds its highest-ranked permitted path extending a neighbor's assigned path. The Simple Path Vector Protocol (SPVP) abstracts BGP dynamics:

> **Theorem:** If SPVP converges to a stable state, the path assignment is a stable SPP solution. If the SPP instance has no solution, SPVP always diverges [3].

> **Theorem:** Deciding SPP solvability is NP-complete (3-SAT reduction) [3].

The *dispute wheel* is the central obstruction: nodes $u_0,\dots,u_{k-1}$, spoke paths $Q_i \in \mathcal{P}_{u_i}$ to the origin, rim paths $R_i$ from $u_i$ to $u_{i+1}$, with $R_i Q_{i+1} \in \mathcal{P}_{u_i}$ and $\lambda_{u_i}(Q_i) < \lambda_{u_i}(R_i Q_{i+1})$ for all $i$ (mod $k$) — each node prefers the *indirect* route through its neighbor's spoke over its *direct* spoke, a circular dependency no assignment can satisfy [3].

> **Theorem:** With no dispute wheel, the SPP instance has a *unique* stable solution, and SPVP converges to it [3].

![Dispute wheel oscillation in a Stable Paths Problem instance: three nodes with spoke paths to the origin and cyclic rim-path preferences](/thesis/ths_1788802121000_4a5f-0.webp)

The converse fails: SPVP can diverge even when a unique stable solution exists (bistable oscillation), so non-convergence in simulation does not imply unsolvability [8]. The Gao–Rexford conditions — prefer customer routes, export only customer routes to providers/peers, acyclic customer–provider digraph — are the practical sufficient conditions: they preclude dispute wheels and guarantee safety [3].

### 2.2 The BGP Decision Process and Where It Breaks

RFC 4271 best-path selection is lexicographic over route attributes:

| Step | Criterion | Direction |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | LOCAL_PREF | highest |
| 2 | AS_PATH length | shortest |
| 3 | ORIGIN type | lowest (IGP < EGP < incomplete) |
| 4 | MED | lowest, *same neighboring AS only* |
| 5 | eBGP over iBGP | prefer eBGP |
| 6 | IGP cost to NEXT_HOP | lowest |
| 7 | Router ID / oldest route | tie-break |

Step 4 is the defect: MED is *non-transitive* — comparable only between routes from the same neighboring AS. In a full-mesh iBGP network every router sees all exit points, but route reflection (RFC 4456) and confederations (RFC 5065) advertise only best paths, so routers see *inconsistent subsets* of exit points [4]. McPherson et al. document the canonical persistent-oscillation topology: MED values $\{10,1,0\}$ through two route-reflector clusters produce a closed loop of best-path recomputation [4]. RFC 7964 formalizes the remedy: reflectors must advertise more than the best path — *all* paths or the *Group Best Paths* (best path per neighboring AS) — restoring the information needed for consistent MED comparison [5]. Route flap damping (RFC 2439) is the blunt instrument: per-flap penalties decay exponentially (defaults: suppress 2000, reuse 750, half-life 15 min), suppressing symptoms without repairing dispute wheels.

### 2.3 From Snapshots to Stable States

Early verification analyzed data-plane snapshots — forwarding tables as observed — which check reachability but cannot explain *why* a state arose or whether a worse stable state exists. Batfish [1] *derives* the data plane from configurations: vendor syntax (Cisco IOS/NX-OS, Juniper JunOS, Arista) becomes a vendor-independent IR; control/data planes are computed declaratively; and properties — no black holes or loops, *multipath consistency* (identical headers treated identically on all paths), *failure consistency* (accepted flows stay accepted under link failures), *destination consistency* (an AS announces only its own addresses) — are checked by translating data-plane relations into Z3 constraints, with concrete violating packets as counterexamples [1]. The *environment* (link up/down status plus external announcements) parameterizes what-if analysis without re-deriving data planes [1].

Minesweeper [2] eliminates simulation: it encodes the *stable states* as a logical formula, exploiting Griffin et al.'s reduction of control planes to edge constraints over stable paths. Satisfying assignments *are* converged control planes; conjoining a property constraint and asking for satisfiability searches *all* stable states at once [2].

## 3 Methodology

Our pipeline has four components.

**1. Vendor-independent policy extraction (Batfish).** Batfish normalizes route-maps, prefix-lists, community filters, and BGP session structure into logical facts over a uniform schema [1]. From this IR we derive, per destination equivalence class, the permitted-path sets $\mathcal{P}_v$ and rankings $\lambda_v$ implied by LOCAL_PREF, AS_PATH, MED, and tie-break configuration.

**2. Stable-state SMT encoding (Minesweeper style).** For each node $v$ and destination $d$, variable $best_v$ ranges over $\mathcal{P}_v \cup \{\epsilon\}$; the formula asserts $best_v$ is the $\lambda_v$-maximal permitted path whose next hop $u$ selects the matching suffix — $v$'s choice consistent with its neighbor's. A dispute wheel surfaces as an unsatisfiable core with cyclic dependency — the solver's unsat core identifies the conflicting policies. Oscillation detection becomes constraint diagnosis, not trace simulation.

**3. Dispute-structure analysis (Griffin-style).** We build the *dispute digraph*: permitted paths as nodes, transmission arcs $(u,v)P \to P$ and dispute arcs for "node $u$ prefers $(u,v)P$ over $P$"; a directed cycle witnesses a dispute wheel [3]. With no dispute wheel, we certify *uniqueness* of the stable solution — the guarantee operators actually want.

**4. Abstraction and acceleration.** Full SMT encoding of million-route fabrics is intractable, so we adopt two scaling strategies. *Bonsai* compresses policy-equivalent routers into canonical representatives per destination equivalence class, preserving forwarding behavior while shrinking analyzed configurations, and parallelizes naturally since classes are disjoint [7]. *Tiramisu* abandons constraint solving for property-customized graph algorithms over a multilayer model (routing-adjacency graph of per-process vertices; traffic-propagation graph of per-RIB ingress/egress vertices), verifying policies under failures up to 600× faster than constraint-based tools [6]. We use SMT for *diagnosis* (counterexamples, unsat cores) and graphs for *screening* (speed).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Dispute Wheel as Cyclic Chasing

An SPP instance $S = (G, \mathcal{P}, \Lambda)$ is *stable* at assignment $\pi$ iff $\pi(v) = \arg\max_{\lambda_v}\{(v,u)\pi(u) : (v,u) \in E\} \cup \{\epsilon\}$ for all $v$. The dispute wheel's force is a chasing argument: $u_i$ adopts $R_i Q_{i+1}$ only after $u_{i+1}$ advertises $Q_{i+1}$, but $u_{i+1}$ then abandons $Q_{i+1}$ for $R_{i+1}Q_{i+2}$, withdrawing the route $u_i$ depends on. Around the wheel, each node's preferred route is perpetually withdrawn by the neighbor it transits. The *disagree gadget* (two nodes, paths $(2,1,0)$ and $(1,2,0)$, each preferring the route through the other) is the $k=2$ case and yields two stable solutions; the $k=3$ wheel yields none [3][8]. Mechanized detection over a Batfish-derived IR is cycle detection in the dispute digraph:

```python
from collections import defaultdict

def dispute_wheel_present(permitted, rank, adj):
    """permitted[v]: paths (tuples) from v to origin.
       rank[v]: path -> int, higher preferred. adj[v]: neighbors."""
    trans, dispute = defaultdict(list), defaultdict(list)
    for v, paths in permitted.items():
        for P in paths:                       # transmission arcs
            if len(P) > 1:
                u, suffix = P[0], P[1:]
                if suffix in permitted.get(u, ()):
                    trans[(v, P)].append((u, suffix))
        for Q in paths:                       # dispute arcs
            for u in adj.get(v, []):
                for P in permitted.get(u, ()):
                    cand = (v, u) + P[1:]
                    if cand in paths and rank[v][cand] > rank[v][Q]:
                        dispute[(v, Q)].append((u, P))
    return has_cycle_with_dispute_arc(trans, dispute)
```

### 4.2 The MED Comparator's Algebraic Defect

Write the decision process as lexicographic over $\tau(r) = (\text{LOCAL\_PREF}, -\lvert\text{AS\_PATH}\rvert, -\text{ORIGIN}, -\text{MED}, \text{is\_eBGP}, -\text{IGP\_cost}, \text{router\_id})$, with the MED coordinate *undefined* across different neighbor ASes. Incomparability propagates silently: tied through ORIGIN with different neighbor ASes, the comparison *skips* MED and falls to eBGP/iBGP and IGP cost — MED fails to discriminate exactly when discrimination matters most. RFC 7964's Group Best Paths repair is algebraic: replace the reflector advertisement $\text{adv}(R) = \{\text{best}(R)\}$ with $\text{adv}(R) = \{\text{best}(R, X) : X \text{ neighboring AS}\}$, making each router's candidate set *closed* under MED comparison and eliminating MED-induced oscillation with far fewer advertisements than full mesh [5].

### 4.3 The Minesweeper Encoding, Precisely

Minesweeper's insight is that distributed message-passing can be replaced by a *combinational* formula [2]. Per router $v$ and destination $d$: variable $n_v$ (stable next hop, $\bot$ = unreachable) and attribute variables $a_v$. The encoding asserts **consistency** ($n_v = u$ implies $u$'s route is what $v$'s import policy maps to $a_v$, with the chain terminating at the originator), **maximality** (no higher-ranked permitted path is available — every better path's first hop fails to select the required suffix or filters the announcement), and **policy fidelity** ($\mathcal{P}_v$, $\lambda_v$ the sets induced by $v$'s route-maps and session topology). A model is a converged control plane; *unsatisfiability* is the algebraic signature of a dispute wheel. Conjoining $\neg\text{Property}$ gives a verifier: SAT yields a violating stable state as counterexample; UNSAT certifies the property across *all* stable states [2]. The TLA+ sketch below captures the fixed-point semantics:

```tla+
---- MODULE SPVP ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS V, Origin, Permitted, Rank
VARIABLES choice
Init == choice = [v \in V |-> <<>>]
NextHop(p) == IF p = <<>> THEN Origin ELSE Head(p)
Suffix(p)  == IF p = <<>> THEN <<>> ELSE Tail(p)
Consistent(v) ==
    \/ choice[v] = <<>>
    \/ /\ NextHop(choice[v]) \in V
       /\ choice[NextHop(choice[v])] = Suffix(choice[v])
Maximal(v) ==
    \A P \in Permitted[v] :
        Rank[v][P] > Rank[v][choice[v]] => ~Available(v, P, choice)
Stable == \A v \in V : Consistent(v) /\ Maximal(v)
====
```

### 4.4 Batfish's Declarative Core

Batfish computes control and data planes as the least fixed point of Datalog rules — the NoD engine [1]: "the best route is the minimum-cost route; the minimum cost aggregates over all candidates" is effectively a declarative shortest-path computation, with analogous rule sets for BGP import/export, redistribution, and FIB construction. The environment (link states, external announcements) is just more input facts, so what-if analysis is re-derivation under modified facts, not re-simulation [1]. Properties become Z3 constraints over data-plane relations; each model is a concrete violating packet plus originating router — the provenance operators need [1]. Generality follows: new protocols are new rule sets, not new simulators.

### 4.5 Screening with Graphs, Diagnosing with SMT

**Bonsai** observes most routers are *policy-equivalent* per destination: identical import/export behavior on a destination's equivalence class compresses them into canonical representatives, shrinking configurations by orders of magnitude for Batfish/Minesweeper, parallelized across disjoint classes [7]. **Tiramisu** models the network as graphs: a *routing adjacency graph* (vertex per routing process, edges per adjacency) and a *traffic propagation graph* (vertices per RIB and per-VLAN ingress/egress, encoding iBGP-over-IGP recursion). Its TPVP algorithm computes the exact path taken under failures on the graph — verifying path-preference and multipath-consistency policies 2–600× faster than SMT tools (<0.08 s small, <2.2 s large networks) [6]. The earlier ARC tool shares the graph niche but models no iBGP, VRFs, VLANs, or BGP attributes like local preference and communities — precisely where dispute wheels hide — so the algebraic SMT layer remains necessary for convergence diagnosis [6].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

**Complexity.** SPP solvability is NP-complete [3]; dispute-wheel detection on an explicit instance is polynomial (dispute-digraph cycle detection), while extracting the instance from vendor configs is the expensive step — Batfish amortizes it by deriving data planes once and checking many properties over them [1]. Minesweeper's formula is linear in router–destination pairs times policy size; the exponential worst case is delegated to the SMT solver, which exploits the structured, mostly-Horn shape of routing constraints [2].

**Proof sketch: no dispute wheel ⇒ unique stable solution.** Suppose distinct stable assignments $\pi \ne \pi'$, and let $v$ be a node where they differ with $\lambda_v(\pi(v)) > \lambda_v(\pi'(v))$. Stability of $\pi'$ forces $\pi(v) = (v,u)P$ with $\pi'(u) = P$ yet $\pi(u) \ne P$ (else $\pi(v)$ would equal $\pi'(v)$). Chasing the disagreement must revisit a node — the graph is finite — yielding $u_0,\dots,u_{k-1}$ with spokes from one assignment and rims from the other, satisfying exactly the dispute-wheel inequalities. Contrapositively, no dispute wheel implies at most one stable assignment; fairness drives SPVP to it [3].

**Proof sketch: MED + route reflection ⇒ oscillation.** In the RFC 3345 topology, reflector $R_a$ alternately selects the MED-1 route (via client $R_c$) and the MED-10 route (via $R_b$), because each selection changes which routes $R_c$, $R_e$ re-advertise, changing $R_a$'s candidate set. The three candidates are never totally ordered at any single router — MED compares only same-neighbor-AS pairs — so no fixed point exists and updates cycle with the BGP scan timers [4]. Group Best Paths breaks the cycle by closing each router's candidate set under MED comparison [5].

**Measured scale.** Batfish's evolution study: a 2,735-node network with ~16M routes (NET11) completes snapshot init, data-plane generation, and 100 serial destination-reachability queries in ~5 minutes; cost depends weakly on size — configuration complexity, not node count, dominates [1]. Minesweeper verifies reachability, isolation, waypointing, black holes, bounded path length, load balancing, router equivalence, and fault tolerance across OSPF/BGP/static mixtures [2]. Doctrine: *screen with graphs, diagnose with SMT* — Tiramisu-class checks in CI; Batfish+Minesweeper on the abstracted network on anomaly.

| Tool | Technique | Convergence diagnosis | Cloud-scale verdict |
|------|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Batfish [1] | NoD Datalog + Z3 | Indirect (derived data planes) | 2.7k nodes / 16M routes, ~5 min |
| Minesweeper [2] | SMT over stable states | Direct (unsat core = dispute) | Protocol-broad; solver-bound |
| Bonsai [7] | Policy-equivalence abstraction | Preserved per destination class | Parallel; 10–100× shrinkage |
| Tiramisu [6] | Multilayer graphs (RAG/TPG) | Path-level, under failures | 2–600× faster; weaker policy coverage |
| ARC [6] | Graph characteristics | Not modeled (no iBGP/BGP attrs) | Fast but misses dispute wheels |

## 6 Limitations

The SPP model abstracts BGP timers, MRAI rate-limiting, and flap-damping dynamics: no dispute wheel means *eventual* stability, but says nothing about *how long* convergence takes. The SMT encoding captures stable states, not transients — mid-convergence loops and black holes relevant to fast reroute are invisible. Bonsai's abstraction is per destination class and compresses poorly on fabrics with per-neighbor route-maps at every edge. External BGP is only an *environment* of announcements [1]; eccentric peer policies — the true source of Internet dispute wheels — lie outside the verified perimeter unless modeled. Finally, MED-oscillation analysis assumes the RFC 4271 decision process; vendor knobs (always-compare-MED, deterministic-MED off) alter the comparator and must be reflected in $\lambda_v$.

## 7 Conclusion

BGP convergence is an algebraic property of routing policy, not a dynamic accident of message timing: the dispute wheel is the structural obstruction, and the MED comparator's non-transitivity is the mechanism of real iBGP oscillation [3][4][5]. Batfish's declarative derivation [1] supplies vendor-independent policy facts; the Minesweeper SMT encoding [2] turns convergence into satisfiability, where an unsatisfiable core *is* the diagnosis; Bonsai [7] and Tiramisu [6] carry the analysis to cloud scale. The practical doctrine is two-tiered: fast graph-based screening over the full fabric, SMT-based algebraic diagnosis over the abstracted network wherever screening flags an anomaly — or wherever convergence itself is the property under test. Operators who verify the *fixed points* of their routing, rather than simulating its transients, are the ones whose networks converge.
