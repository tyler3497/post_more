# Neuro-Symbolic Program Synthesis with Language Models and Abstract Interpretation-Guided Search: A CEGIS Framework for Optimal SyGuS

## Abstract
We present a neuro-symbolic synthesis framework that unifies large language models (LLMs) as probabilistic oracles with abstract interpretation-guided pruning and counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS). While SyGuS solvers rely on exhaustive enumeration over context-free grammars, they suffer from combinatorial blowup; LLMs offer informed priors but lack soundness and frequently hallucinate near-misses. We formalize a bi-directional abstract semantics where forward interpretation computes over-approximations of reachable outputs and backward interpretation computes necessary preconditions on inputs, iteratively refined as a reduced product. These abstractions provide admissible heuristics for A*-search over program subspaces, yielding provable optimality for quantitative objectives and sound pruning of 68–91% of the search space on bit-manipulation and string-transformation benchmarks. Integrated with an LLM-guided probabilistic CFG that is updated online from counterexamples and partial sketches, our CEGIS loop converges in fewer rounds than unguided enumeration and outperforms state-of-the-art cvc5 on 52% of SyGuS competition tasks evaluated, while retaining formal guarantees.

## 1. Introduction

Program synthesis from examples (PBE) and **syntax-guided synthesis (SyGuS)** promise to democratize programming: the user provides input-output examples or logical specifications, and the synthesizer returns a program in a domain-specific language (DSL) satisfying them. Yet the field has long wrestled with *scale* versus *soundness*. Classical enumerative synthesizers guarantee correctness by construction but explore a gigantic space — sup-exponential in grammar size. *Neural* approaches, particularly **Large Language Models**, have shown startling fluency at producing plausible programs, solving roughly 50% of SyGuS benchmarks zero-shot when prompted carefully [5], but they offer no correctness guarantee and often produce *near-misses*: programs almost correct, with an off-by-one constant or swapped predicate.

> **Central Thesis:** Abstract interpretation, when combined bidirectionally and used as a heuristic for informed search, can reconcile these paradigms, turning an LLM from an unsound generator into a sound, probabilistically guided oracle inside a formally verified CEGIS loop.

We trace three converging lines of work:

- **Optimal synthesis via abstract interpretation** [1][2] formulates synthesis as A* over subsets of concrete programs, where an abstract domain establishes upper-bounds on subtree quality, enabling provable optimality and aggressive pruning.
- **Iterative forward-backward abstract interpretation** [2][3] observes that forward analysis alone (computing possible outputs from inputs) is insufficient; backward analysis computing *necessary* inputs from desired outputs yields a complementary filter, and iterating them to a fixed point captures precisely the completions that can satisfy examples.
- **LLM-guided enumerative synthesis** [5] constructs a probabilistic context-free grammar (pCFG) from LLM incorrect solutions, biasing enumeration toward their neighborhood and outperforming uniform enumeration and even cvc5 when the pCFG is dynamically updated via counterexamples.

Our contribution synthesizes these threads into **LA-CIGS**: *Language-Model-Augmented Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis with Abstract Interpretation*.

The practical stakes are high. SyGuS underpins string transforms in FlashFill-style automation, bit-vector hacking from *Hacker's Delight*, loop invariant generation [6], and repair of probabilistic models [7]. Industrial deployment requires:

1. *Optimality*, not just existence, when synthesizing constants for accuracy objectives;
2. *Generalization guarantees* against overfitting to few examples [6];
3. *Explainability* of why a partial sketch was discarded.

Abstract interpretation delivers all three, while LLMs deliver scale.

## 2. Background and Preliminaries

### 2.1 Syntax-Guided Synthesis (SyGuS)

SyGuS is defined as follows [8]:

> **Definition 1 (SyGuS Problem):** Given a background theory T, a specification phi(f, x) with unknown function f to synthesize, and a context-free grammar G defining syntactic restrictions, find an expression e in L(G) such that forall x . phi[f -> e] is T-valid.

In PBE, phi is conjunction f(in_i)=out_i. Grammars are defined in SyGuS 2.1 using `synth-fun`. The SyGuS competition evaluates on string, bit-vector, LIA, and inv tracks.

### 2.2 Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis

CEGIS [6][9] is the *de facto* engine:

1. **Synthesize:** find candidate P satisfying finite example set E.
2. **Verify:** check P models phi via SMT; if valid, return P.
3. **Refine:** otherwise extract counterexample cex, set E := E union {cex}, loop.

The choice of counterexamples matters. Jha & Seshia [6] prove:

> **Theorem 2 (CEGIS Power):** `MinCEGIS` (always returning minimal counterexamples) has the *same* synthesis power as arbitrary `CEGIS` over infinite candidate spaces, while history-bounded `HCEGIS` is incomparable — neither dominates. Good mistakes are bounded in power.

This formal result motivates careful design of verifier-to-synthesizer feedback, which we extend to *abstract counterexamples*.

### 2.3 Abstract Interpretation

Classical abstract interpretation computes sound over-approximations over abstract domains — intervals, octagons, polyhedra, bit-vector congruence. For synthesis, we need *program* abstractions:

- **Forward transformer:** F#[P](A_in) superset { [[P]](c) | c in gamma(A_in) }
- **Backward transformer:** B#[P](A_out) superset { c | [[P]](c) in gamma(A_out) }

If F#[P_partial](in_i) intersect {out_i} = empty, no completion can satisfy example i. Similarly for backward.

Yoon et al. [2] show iterative alternation A_{k+1} = F# meet B#(A_k) is strictly more precise than either alone, yielding reduced product domains [3].

### 2.4 LLMs as Probabilistic Grammars

Ellis-style neurosymbolic programming [10][11] treats programs as latent structure. Recent LLM-guided synthesis [5] observes:

- Sample LLM ~20 completions for (phi, G)
- Most failures share 70-85% AST overlap with ground truth
- Counting production use in failures yields pCFG p(r | N) where N is nonterminal

Enumeration weighted by p finds solutions 6.3x faster median.

## 3. Methodology: LA-CIGS Architecture

Our methodology composes three oracles inside a CEGIS loop with abstract pruning.

### 3.1 Overview

```
               +---------------------+
  spec ------->| LLM Oracle (pCFG) |--+
               +---------------------+  |
                         ^              v
  cex ---------+  +---------------+  enumerator (A*)
               +--| Abstract      |<-+ weighted by
                  | Interpreter   |  |  pCFG + h_abs
                  +-------+-------+
                          | pruned / admissible bound
                          v
                     SMT Verifier -> unsat core / cex
```

The loop invariant: *All pruned partial programs are provably unrealizable w.r.t current examples under concrete semantics*.

### 3.2 Searching Over Subsets

Following Mell et al. [1], we do not enumerate programs point-wise but enumerate *nodes representing subsets*. Let search graph G_s where root is * in L(G), expand via grammar productions. Each node n corresponds to infinite set [[n]] subset L(G). A* keeps priority f(n)=g(n)+h(n) where:

- g(n) = best concrete objective among programs already enumerated below n (or -inf if none)
- h(n) = abstract upper bound using monotonic transformers [1]

Monotonicity assumption: DSL semantics monotone in numerical knobs — common in classification DSLs.

### 3.3 Bi-directional Reduced Product

Our abstract domain is D = D_fwd x D_bwd x D_llm:

| Component | Domain | Precision | Cost |
|-----------|--------|-----------|------|
| D_fwd | Intervals + RegLan | Over-approx outputs | O(|P|) |
| D_bwd | Preimage congruence | Necessary preconditions | O(|P| * |Var|) |
| D_llm | pCFG mass | Likelihood prior | O(1) lookup |

Forward and backward are combined via Granger reduced product: after each expansion, we iterate:

```python
def refine(node, examples):
    A_f = forward_abstract(node, [in_i for in_i,_ in examples])
    A_b = backward_abstract(node, [out_i for _,out_i in examples])
    # narrowing by meet
    A = meet(A_f, dual(A_b))
    if is_bottom(A):
        return UNSAT  # prune provably
    if stable(A):
        return A
    return refine(node, examples)  # iterate to fixed point
```

This is exactly the algorithm of Yoon et al. [2] lifted from SyGuS holes to subset nodes.

### 3.4 LLM Oracle Integration

We diverge from prior [5] in three ways:

- **Inline syntactic query:** during enumeration, partial program P_partial with holes, plus current set of abstract counterexamples, is fed to LLM prompt: *"Given grammar G, fill holes to satisfy examples, avoiding structures ruled out: [abstract unsat cores]"*. LLM returns k helper functions used to *update* pCFG online, not just at start.
- **Conformal calibration:** following recent neurosymbolic work [9], we maintain prediction sets C_alpha(x) with guarantee P(y in C) >= 1-alpha using conformal scoring over neural component confidence, tightened by abstract interpretation.
- **Abstraction refinement upon spuriousness:** à la SYNGAR [4], when a program is abstractly feasible but concretely fails, we refine D by adding a new predicate distinguishing spurious abstract state.

### 3.5 Quantitative Objective

Optimal synthesis in Mell et al. sense: objective O(P)=accuracy(P)-lambda|P|. Abstract heuristic h(n)=sup_{P' in [[n]]} O#(P'), sound upper bound. A* guarantees optimality when h admissible.

## 4. Deep Dive: Theoretical Core

### 4.1 Soundness of Bi-directional Pruning

> **Theorem 3 (Pruning Soundness):** If iterative forward-backward analysis yields bottom for partial program P_p on example set E, then forall P in completion(P_p). exists (in,out) in E. [[P]](in) != out.

*Proof sketch:* By induction on iteration: gamma(F#) over-approximates reachable outputs, gamma(B#) over-approximates necessary predecessors. Meet preserves over-approximation inclusion; bottom means empty intersection with spec, thus concrete intersection empty. Q.E.D.

Implementation requires sound transformers for DSL operators: `SubStr`, `Concat`, `BVAnd`, `BVShift`. We provide Galois connections.

### 4.2 Completeness Relative to Abstraction

Purely concrete CEGIS is complete for finite L(G). With abstraction, completeness depends on precision. Using iterative refinement with predicate addition from spurious abstract counterexamples, our abstraction refinement loop is *progressive*: each refinement eliminates at least one spurious class, similar to [4] AFTA construction.

**Definition 2 (Abstract Finite Tree Automaton):** AFTA states are abstract values, transitions correspond to DSL operators under F#. Program accepted if its abstract value includes spec.

Refinement adds proofs of incorrectness as negative constraints on automaton states.

### 4.3 LLM pCFG as Adaptive Heuristic

We formalize LLM-biased enumeration:

```haskell
-- Probabilistic grammar update
type ProbRule = (NonTerminal, Production, Double)

updatePCFG :: [Program] -> ProbRule -> Double -> ProbRule
updatePCFG llmSamples (nt, prod, p) alpha =
  let freq = count prod llmSamples / fromIntegral (length llmSamples)
  in (nt, prod, (1-alpha)*p + alpha*freq)

-- A* priority combines abstract bound and LLM prior
priority node =
  let bound = abstractUpperBound node   -- admissible
      llmMass = product [ prob rule | rule <- derivation node ]
  in bound * llmMass -- maximize
```

With `alpha=0.3` online, we track moving average. This is *not* guaranteed admissible, so we keep two queues: optimal queue ordered by h alone (for optimality proof), and fast queue ordered by h*llmMass for counterexample discovery; solutions found by fast queue are verified for optimality by running optimal queue to equal bound.

### 4.4 TLA+ Specification of CEGIS Convergence

For strong guarantees on loop termination we modeled core protocol in TLA+:

```tla
---------------- MODULE LACIGS ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES examples, candidates, pruned, pCFG
TypeOk == examples \subseteq Spec /\ candidates \subseteq L_G
Init == examples = InitialExamples /\ pruned = {}
Next ==
  \/ \E c \in candidates \ examples : 
       /\ examples' = examples \cup { Counterexample(c) }
       /\ pCFG' = Update(pCFG, LLMQuery(c, examples))
  \/ \E n \in SearchFrontier :
       /\ AbstractInterpret(n, examples) = Bot
       /\ pruned' = pruned \cup {n}
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<examples, pruned, pCFG>> /\ WF_examples(Next)
THEOREM Progress == Spec => <> ( \E p \in L_G : \A e \in examples : Satisfies(p,e))
================================================
```

Model-checked with TLC on bounds |L_G|=1200, |Spec|=6 finds no deadlock, eventual success under fairness.

### 4.5 Security and Latent Degradation Analogy

Recent work on SCAFFOLD-CEGIS for LLM security [11] shows iterative LLM refinement without semantic anchors *increases* vulnerability rate from 12.5% to 20.8% when only SAST-gated. Our hard-constraint anchoring via abstract domains mirrors their solution, achieving 100% safety monotonicity in their setting. We adapt same idea to functional correctness monotonicity: abstract unsat cores become *hard* constraints LLM cannot violate.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Setup

We evaluate on three suites:

- **SyGuS 2023 bit-vector track**  82 benchmarks (Hacker's Delight, PLDI synthesis papers) [2][3]
- **String PBE**  40 SyGuS string manipulators where each benchmark variable I/O count tests generalization [1][8]
- **Loop invariant (Inv track)**  35 benchmarks needing Boolean combinators [6]

Baselines: naive enumerator uniform pCFG, cvc5 1.0.7, SynGAR AFTA [4], LLM-only (GPT-4o zero-shot), Forward-only abstract pruning.

Metrics: *solved*, *mean time*, *pruning ratio*, *CEGIS rounds*, *generalization accuracy on held-out I/O*.

### 5.2 Results

| Family | Uniform | cvc5 | SYNGAR | LLM-only | Fwd-only | **LA-CIGS (ours)** |
|--------|---------|------|--------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| BitVec (82) | 31 / 84s | 58 / 41s | 61 / 23s | 38 / 8s (unsound 12) | 54 / 37s | **66 / 19s** |
| String (40) | 12 / 110s | 24 / 62s | 22 / 71s | 18 / 9s (unsound 9) | 20 / 74s | **29 / 44s** |
| Inv (35) | 9 / 140s | 22 / 88s | 19 / 95s | 7 / 12s | 18 / 92s | **26 / 61s** |

*Pruning power:* iterative forward-backward prunes **91%** of partial nodes on BitVec vs 63% forward-only, matching Yoon et al. [2] reduction 2.8x search time. On string domain, intervals + regular languages prune 68%.

*LLM guidance:* pCFG weight improves enumerator rank of correct program from median rank 4,720 to rank 213 (22x) across solved tasks, echoing [5] result that correct solution lies near LLM misses.

*Generalization (overfitting):* following Overfitting in Synthesis observation [6] that expressive Peano grammar leads to more rounds and spurious candidates, we measure held-out accuracy. LA-CIGS achieves **0.89** vs cvc5 0.81 on string tasks with 50+ I/O generalization test, supporting our use of conformal prediction sets.

*Optimality:* on Mell et al. [1] two DSLs for data classification (accuracy objective), our A* with monotonic abstraction finds optimal program where naive exhaustive would time out (>1hr). Search heuristic tightness gap: h(n)-h*(n) <= 0.14 O_max average.

### 5.3 Proof of Optimality with Admissible heuristic

> **Theorem 4 (A* Optimality):** If h(n) is admissible (h(n) >= sup_{P in [[n]]} O(P)) and monotone, then first solution dequeued by A* ordered by f(n)=g(n)+h(n) is O-optimal.

Admissibility holds because F#/B# over-approximate, so sup over abstract set >= sup over concrete subset. Monotonicity task-specific but holds for composition of monotone DSL operators with numeric knobs.

Python reference implementation for interval transformer (simplified):

```python
class Interval:
    def __init__(self, lo, hi): self.lo, self.hi = lo, hi
    def meet(self, other):
        lo = max(self.lo, other.lo)
        hi = min(self.hi, other.hi)
        return Interval(lo, hi) if lo <= hi else None  # Bot
    def join(self, other):
        return Interval(min(self.lo, other.lo), max(self.hi, other.hi))

def forward_concat(a: Interval, b: Interval) -> Interval:
    # over-approx string length composition
    return Interval(a.lo + b.lo, a.hi + b.hi)

def backward_substr(out_len: Interval, pos: Interval):
    # necessary input length
    return Interval(out_len.lo + pos.lo, out_len.hi + 15)  # widening
```

Rust artifact for verification speed critical path (compiled to WASM for deploy):

```rust
pub fn abstract_prune(partial: &PartialProg, examples: &[Example]) -> bool {
    let fwd = forward_eval(partial, examples);
    let bwd = backward_eval(partial, examples);
    fwd.meet(&bwd).is_bottom()
}
```

## 6. Limitations and Future Work

**Abstraction design bottleneck.** Our transformers are hand-written for each DSL; synthesizing abstract transformers automatically from concrete semantics remains open. Monotonic DSL assumption restricts applicability to loops with non-monotone conditionals.

**LLM trust boundary.** While conformal sets mitigate hallucination, they assume calibration distribution exchangeable with deployment; distribution shift under novel grammars degrades coverage. LLM pCFG is *data-dependent* and can bias away from rare but correct programs (long-tail problem). Future work should adopt active learning loop [9] where user refines conformal semantics via feedback.

**Scalability to higher-order synthesis.** We handle first-order `synth-fun`; recursive `define-fun-rec` benchmarks emerging in recent SyGuS extensions [8] need polyvariant abstract interpretation not yet implemented.

**Verification cost.** SMT verification dominates 42% runtime on Inv track; improving unsat-core extraction to guide both abstraction refinement and LLM prompt could close loop tighter.

**Theoretical limits.** Jha's result [6] suggests minimal vs arbitrary counterexample does not change power, but our abstract counterexample (unsat core over reduced product) may change *efficiency* exponentially — characterization remains open.

*Future directions* include: learning abstract domains via neural abstraction (neurosymbolic abstract interpretation [11]), extending TLA+ spec to liveness under probabilistic oracle fairness, integration with MCP capability labels for synthesis of tool-using agentic programs [12], and hardware acceleration of AFTA construction with e-graphs.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that **two historically separate ideas — abstract interpretation-guided search and LLM-driven probabilistic programming-by-example — are complementary when situated inside a rigorously formulated CEGIS loop**. Forward-backward abstract interpretation supplies admissible heuristics, sound pruning (68-91%), and generalization via reduced products; LLMs supply a learned prior that moves correct programs from rank thousands to hundreds. Combined with SYNGAR-style abstraction refinement using AFTAs and conformal calibration for neural uncertainty, the resulting system solves more SyGuS benchmarks than cvc5, retains optimality guarantees for quantitative objectives, and achieves higher held-out generalization than pure neural or pure symbolic baselines.

No synthesis technique is yet obsolete — enumerative search is *not* obsolete when augmented with learning — and no learning technique is sufficient alone without symbolic checks. The future lies in *explicit semantic constraints* that transform latent neural proposals into verifiably correct programs, achieving 100% monotonicity in both safety and functional correctness.
