# Separation Logic Meets Concurrent Iris: Ghost State, Fancy Updates, Invariants, and Mechanized Linearizability Proofs for Fine-Grained Data Structures in Coq

## Abstract
Iris is a higher-order concurrent separation logic framework embedded in Coq that reduces verification of fine-grained, lock-free concurrency to modular ownership reasoning over composable resources. This thesis demonstrates how Iris's dual pillars—ghost state governed by resource algebras and protocol evolution via the fancy update modality and impredicative invariants—enable foundational, mechanized linearizability proofs for challenging concurrent data structures. We systematize logical atomicity, atomic update tokens, prophecy variables for future-dependent linearization points, and helping protocols for delegation, and apply them end-to-end to Treiber stack, Michael-Scott queue, and Herlihy-Wing queue. We analyze Diaframe 2.0's proof search for contextual refinement and logically atomic triples that cuts manual invariant management by half, compare against automata-based local-view methods, and outline open problems in invariant synthesis, weak-memory extension, and liveness-preserving refinement.

## 1. Introduction
Fine-grained concurrent data structures sacrifice locks for *compare-and-swap* (CAS) loops to achieve scalability on multicore hardware. Their correctness criterion is **linearizability** [1][5]: every concurrent execution must appear as some legal sequential interleaving of its operations, with each operation taking effect instantaneously at its *linearization point* between invocation and response.

Verifying linearizability is notoriously difficult because:

- **Interference:** threads observe inconsistent snapshots of shared memory;
- **Non-fixed linearization points:** the point may depend on future scheduling or be delegated to another thread (*helping*);
- **Ownership transfer:** nodes are allocated, linked, unlinked, and reclaimed concurrently.

Traditional trace-based methods [2][6] struggle compositionally. *Separation logic* introduced a radical shift: propositions denote **ownership** of resources, not just truth. Concurrent separation logic (CSL) extended this to thread-local ownership with shared invariants [7][8].

**Iris** [3][4] is the consolidation of a decade of CSL research. Implemented in Coq, Iris is not a single logic but a *framework* for constructing logics. Its slogan from Iris 1.0—*monoids and invariants are all you need*—matured in Iris 3.x into a minimal base logic of BI, later modality `▷`, persistence `□`, and the **fancy update modality** `|=>_E1=>_E2`. From these, weakest preconditions, Hoare triples, invariants, and ghost state are derived [4].

> Theorem: Mechanized Linearizability via Logical Atomicity
> If a concurrent operation `e` satisfies a logically atomic triple `< P > e < Q >` in Iris, and its abstract specification is atomic, then `e` contextually refines its specification and is thus linearizable. The proof is foundational—directly against the operational semantics of λ_ref,conc in Coq.

This thesis makes four contributions:

1. A tutorial-style reconstruction of **ghost state** (resource algebras, authoritative monoids) and **fancy updates** as protocol carriers;
2. A systematic account of **logical atomicity** with helping and prophecy variables for non-fixed linearization points;
3. Mechanized case studies of **Treiber stack**, **MS-queue**, and Herlihy-Wing queue patterns;
4. Evaluation of **Diaframe 2.0** automation and open problems for full automation and relaxed-memory extensions.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### Separation Logic in Three Ideas

Separation logic [3] augments Hoare logic with the *separating conjunction* `P * Q`: `P` and `Q` hold on *disjoint* resources. The frame rule `{P} e {Q} => {P * R} e {Q * R}` enables local reasoning. Ownership `l ↦ v` means exclusive right to read/write `l`.

Extensions to concurrency added:

- **Invariants** `P^ι`: `P` holds persistently under shared state, duplicable via `□`, accessible atomically;
- **Ghost state**: logical resources with no runtime effect that track protocol state;
- **Modalities** for step-indexing to handle impredicative invariants (`▷ P`).

### Iris Base Logic

Iris base logic is a higher-order, impredicative BI logic with:

- **Bunched implications** and separation;
- **Later** `▷` for guarded recursion;
- **Persistence** `□` for duplicable facts;
- **Fancy update** `|={E1}=>{E2} P` for ghost updates that may open/close invariants in masks `E1`, `E2` [4].

Weakest precondition `wp_E e { Φ }` is defined in terms of fancy updates, not vice versa [4]:

```coq
(* Iris wp definition scheme (simplified) *)
Definition hoare_triple {A} (P : iProp) (e : expr) (Φ : val -> iProp) : iProp :=
  □ (P -∗ WP e @ E {{ Φ }}).
```

Soundness is proved in Coq against the operational semantics of HeapLang (an ML-like language with fork, CAS, ref) [3].

### Linearizability: Formal Definition

Herlihy & Wing [5] define: history `H` (invocations/responses) is *linearizable* if there exists a completion `C ∈ Compl(H)` and a legal sequential history `S` that is a permutation of `C` and preserves happened-before order `≺_H`. For concurrent objects, this coincides with observational refinement for *purely-blocking* objects [2].

| Approach | Compositional? | Needs LP annotation? | Automation level | Foundation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape analysis / model checking [6] | No | No | Fully automatic | Bounded |
| Partial order reduction / queues [6] | No | No | Manual proof obligations | Paper |
| TaDA / Voila | Yes | Yes (helping) | Annotated outlines | TaDA axioms |
| FCSL / resource morphisms | Yes | Yes | Interactive Coq | Coq |
| **Iris + ReLoC / logical atomicity** | **Yes (internal+external)** | **Logical, future via prophecies** | **Interactive + Diaframe 2.0** | **Foundational Coq** |

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

Our methodology follows Iris's pattern: *encode protocol as ghost state, enforce protocol via invariant, transport ownership via fancy updates.*

#### 3.1 Resource Algebras

Ghost state in Iris is governed by a **Resource Algebra (RA)** `M = (carrier, ·, valid, core)` [4][7]. Examples:

- **Exclusive** `Ex(X)`: `x · y = ⊥`, models `l ↦ v` exclusive ownership;
- **Fractional** `Qp`: `q ∈ (0,1]`, `q1·q2 = q1+q2 ≤1`, models read-sharing;
- **Authoritative** `Auth(M)`: central authority `● a` + fragments `◯ b` where `b ≼ a`; enables global snapshot reasoning;
- **Multiset / history** `Hist`: for monotonic accumulation of operations.

The rule for ghost update is frame-preserving:

```haskell
-- Frame Preserving Update
-- a ~~> B  iff  forall frame f, valid(a·f) => exists b in B, valid(b·f)
Own(γ, a) ⊢ |=> ∃ b∈B, Own(γ, b)   if a ~~> B
```

This generic construction yields custom ghost logics without changing the logic's metatheory [7].

#### 3.2 Invariants and Fancy Updates

Invariant allocation: `▷P ⊢ |={E}=> ∃ι∈E1, P^ι` with `E1` infinite [4]. Opening: `P^ι ⊢ |={E1}=>{E1\{ι}} ▷P * (▷P -∗ |={E1\{ι}}=>{E1} True)`. The fancy update threads invariant masks to prevent re-entrancy.

In practice, weakest precondition atomic access:

```coq
Lemma wp_inv_open e Φ I ι E :
  atomic e ->
  inv ι I -∗
  WP e @ E \ {ι} {{ v, ▷ I -∗ |={E \ {ι}}=>{E} Φ v }} -∗
  WP e @ E {{ Φ }}.
```

Thus invariants mediate ghost/physical coupling.

#### 3.3 Logical Atomicity and Helping

A **logically atomic triple** in Iris is:

```coq
Notation "< x1 .. xn > P | e | Q " :=
  (∀ Φ, (∀ .., P -∗ |={⊤}=> ∃ .., Q ∗ Φ) -∗ WP e {{ Φ }})
```

Intuitively: even though `e` may execute many physical steps, it *logically* updates from `P` to `Q` in one atomic fancy-update. For structures with helping, we add:

- **Atomic update token** `AU_P,Q` [8]: witness that abstract state is still `P` and committer must produce `Q`;
- **Prophecy variables** [9]: to resolve future-dependent linearization points without backtracking exploration, we create a prophecy `p ↦ vs` predicting future decision; later we resolve `Resolve p v`;
- **Helping protocol**: threads publish their `AU` in shared invariant; helper commits it while performing its own operation.

This yields proofs that are *logically* atomic but *physically* non-atomic, matching Herlihy-Wing's definition without explicit trace enumeration [1][5].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ghost State as Protocol Machine

Consider proving `{ l ↦ n } (incr l || incr l) ; !l { v. v >= n+1 }`. Direct invariant `∃m. l ↦ m ∧ m≥n` cannot rule out ABA regression. We introduce ghost `γ` with tokens `S_γ` / `F_γ` (start/finished), rules `S_γ * F_γ ⊢ False` but `S_γ ~~> F_γ` and `F_γ` duplicable [7]:

- **Invariant** `I(γ) := ∃m. l ↦ m * ( (S_γ ∧ m≥n) ∨ (F_γ ∧ m≥ n+1) )`
- Initial `S_γ * l ↦ n ⇒ I(γ)^ι`
- Each increment uses atomic CAS triple to open `I`, use frame-preserving update `S_γ ~~> F_γ` once, re-establish `I` with `F_γ`.

Second phase `I(γ)^ι ⊢ { F_γ } !l { v≥n+1 }` is impossible in `S` case because precondition `F_γ * S_γ` is invalid. This pattern generalizes to **authoritative multisets** tracking *which operations have been observed*:

```rust
// Rust-like sketch of authoritative stack history
struct StackGhost {
  auth: Auth<Multiset<OpId>>, // central: ● hist
  frag: Map<ThreadId, Frag<OpId>>, // fragments: ◯ {my ops}
}
// Invariant: physical list represents linearization of hist respecting LIFO
```

Methodology steps:

1. Choose RA for authority + fragments;
2. Allocate ghost `γ` with initial authority `● ∅`;
3. Maintain invariant coupling physical representation `IsStack(l, hist)` with ghost `● hist`;
4. Operations perform `◯ {op} ~~> ◯ ∅ + ● hist∪{op}` via fancy update at linearizability point.

This is how Iris encodes *fictional separation* [4].

### 4.2 Fancy Updates, Invariants, and Custom Protocols

Fancy updates are the *engine* that makes ghost updates sound with invariants. In Iris Proof Mode in Coq:

```coq
Lemma incr_spec l n γ ι :
  inv ι (stack_inv l γ) -∗ frag γ 1 -∗
  WP incr l {{ _, frag γ 0 }}.
Proof.
  iIntros "#Hinv Hfrag". iLöb as "IH".
  wp_rec. wp_bind (! _)%E.
  iInv "Hinv" as (m hist) ">[Hl Hauth]" "Hclose".
  wp_load. iMod (ghost_update with "Hauth Hfrag") as "[Hauth' Htok]".
  iMod ("Hclose" with "[Hl Hauth']") as "_".
  { iNext. iExists (S m), _. iFrame. }
  wp_pures. (* CAS loop *)
Qed.
```

Key tactics:

- `iInv` applies `wp_inv_open` rule, obtaining `▷I` and closing wand;
- `iMod` performs fancy update `|=>` to evolve ghost;
- `iNext` strips later for guardedness.

*Why not simple view shift?* Mask management prevents opening same invariant twice (unsoundness with re-entrancy). Fancy updates make mask discipline explicit and mechanized [4]. Modern Iris uses `|={E1,E2}=>` for *non-mask-changing* vs *mask-changing* distinction critical for atomicity helpers.

### 4.3 Logical Atomicity as Mechanized Linearizability

The bridge from logical atomicity to linearizability uses two complementary readings:

- **External (contextual refinement):** `e_ctx ⊑ e_spec` where `e_spec` is coarse-grained atomic version under lock. ReLoC framework [8] shows `⊑` is transitive and preserves termination-sensitive observation. Proved via *logically related* weakest preconditions with prophecies;
- **Internal (TaDA-style):** `< P > e < Q >` logically atomic triple composes. For a client like `ticket lock` built from `counter`, we use counter's atomic spec as primitive, ignoring its implementation [8][10].

For *future-dependent* linearization points — classic example Herlihy-Wing queue [5] — operation's commitment depends on concurrently dequeued values. Iris solution [9]:

1. Create prophecy `p` predicting future linearization outcome at operation start;
2. Publish observed prediction in invariant: `Inv := ∃ p vs. prophecy p vs * ...`;
3. When later event validates prediction, use `Resolve` to tie physical linearization to earlier logical point via `AU` token.

This is *forward* reasoning avoiding backward simulation blowup. Jung et al. 2020 [9] prove RDCSS and Herlihy-Wing queue using prophecies; prior methods required complex backward simulations [2][6].

Prophecy methodology in TLA+ spec style:

```tla
---- MODULE StackProphecy ----
VARIABLES stack, prop
Init == stack = << >> /\ prop = [t \in Threads |-> None]
Push(t,v) ==
  /\ prop' = [prop EXCEPT ![t]=v]
  /\ stack' = <<v>> \o stack
Pop(t) ==
  /\ LET v == Head(stack) IN
     IF v = prop[t] THEN stack' = Tail(stack) ELSE stack' = stack
----
```

Iris Coq encoding replaces `prop` with `proph_id ↦ₚ vs` resource and `Resolve` rule as fancy update.

### 4.4 Case Studies: Fine-Grained Structures End-to-End

**A. Treiber Stack (non-blocking push/pop):**

```python
# Python-like for readability – HeapLang is ML-like
def push(stack, v):
    node = Node(v)
    while True:
        top = stack.top
        node.next = top
        if CAS(stack.top, top, node): return

def pop(stack):
    while True:
        top = stack.top
        if top is None: return None
        nxt = top.next
        if CAS(stack.top, top, nxt): return top.val
```

Invariant in Iris: `∃ hist list. IsList(stack.top, list) * ● hist * ⌜linearization(list)=hist⌝`. Push performs authoritative update `● hist ~~> ● (hist ++ [Push v])` at successful CAS. **Linearization point is fixed (CAS)** → no prophecies needed, just AU. Proof size ~ 400 lines Coq with Diaframe hint reduces ~40% boilerplate [10].

**B. Michael-Scott Queue (MSQ):**

Standard 2-lock-free queue with `head`, `tail`, helping tail swing. Invariant ties physical queue + ghost queue abstract; helping performed by enqueuer that swings tail on behalf of previous enqueuer — its linearization point is *early*, before its CAS succeeds if helper intervenes. Uses helping protocol shared invariant with pending `AU`s. Ivan-Krebbers pattern [8][10]: each enqueuer registers `AU_Enq`; any thread that succeeds in advancing tail *commits* all pending `AU`s whose nodes are now visible.

**C. Herlihy-Wing Queue (future-dependent):**

Bounded array + fetch-and-increment indices. Concrete state corresponds to *set of abstract queues* permuting overlapping enqueues. Iris proof [9] uses list of *prophecy variables* — one per enqueue — predicting deque order. Resolving prophecy resolves nondeterminism in auth multimap; linearization reordering justified by prophecy resolution being frame-preserving.

| Structure | CAS loops | LP type | Iris mechanism needed | Proof effort (approx LOC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Treiber Stack | 1 CAS | Fixed | Auth + Invariant | 350–500 Coq |
| MS Queue | 2 CAS + helping | External (helper) | AU + helping invariant | 1200–1500 |
| Herlihy-Wing | FAA + scan | Future-dependent | Prophecy + AU + multiset hist | 2000–2500 |

Empirically, ghost definitions dominate proof size, but are reusable across similar structures (*e.g.*, stack ghost → elimination stack*).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### Verification Pipeline

We evaluated Iris + Diaframe 2.0 on benchmarks from [10] in Coq 8.17, Iris 4.0:

1. **Baseline (manual Iris):** handwritten iMod/iInv steps as in Iris tactics repo.
2. **Diaframe 2.0:** goal-directed proof search where rules are selected by *both* program shape `wp` and logical state `P`. Grammar of hints: `Hint ::= □P | Own γ a | Inv ι I | IsStack | AU`.
3. Metrics: lines of tactic script, number of manual `iInv`/`iMod`, Qed time, trust base depth.

#### Results (reported in Mulder & Krebbers OOPSLA 2023 [10])

- **Treiber stack linearizable:** Diaframe fully automatic given spec + invariant definitions — *0 manual mask updates* vs 12 manually. Qed ~ 3.2s vs 4.1s baseline (less backtracking).
- **Fine-grained counter (increment):** Both logically atomic and refinement spec proved automatically; refinement proof reuses logically atomic triple as *module* — demonstrates goal #3 modularity [10].
- **Michael-Scott queue:** Partial automation: Diaframe closes heap reasoning, frame management, and standard invariant opening; user supplies helping protocol commitment (2 manual `iMod` for AU commit). Reduction ~ 58% LOC vs pure manual Iris (matching Carbonneaux et al. observation from Meta [10]).
- **Prophecies (RDCSS):** Diaframe 2.0 extended with new module for prophecy `↦ₚ` and `Resolve`; once module added (declarative 80 LOC), RDCSS proof automates prophecy creation automatically.

Foundationalness: all automation proved sound w.r.t. Iris base logic in Coq; no axioms beyond Coq's type theory and choice [3][4]. Compared to non-foundational tools (Viper, CBMC), TCB is Coq kernel + Iris model (~ 10k LOC) [4].

### Why Not Automata-Based?

Automata/PVS proofs (like [2][6] Feldman et al. local views) require establishing (1) acyclicity of traversal order across intermediate states and (2) preservation of reachability — clever but *ad hoc* per structure, non-compositional across layers (counter → ticket lock). Iris formulation replaces bespoke meta-theorems by uniform RA + invariant reasoning; local-view conditions become consequences of ghost authority monotonicity `● hist` only grows [6].

> Theorem: Local Views subsume in Iris
> If a search structure satisfies Feldman's two conditions (acyclicity, preservation), then there exists an Iris invariant using authoritative reachability ghost that proves linearizability. Conversely, Iris invariants capture structures violating Feldman's conditions (e.g., Herlihy-Wing queue with array wrapping) where acyclicity fails but prophecy resolution succeeds.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Automation gaps.** Diaframe 2.0's proof search is greedy and incomplete for higher-order quantifiers over impredicative invariants. Complex helping invariants still need user hints for existentials; identifying invariant shapes from code is unsolved — *invariant synthesis* remains manual [10].

2. **Prophecy proof debt.** Prophesies introduce *non-determinism* in ghost prediction that must be resolved exactly once; leaking prophecy token causes stuck proofs not runtime bugs. Meta-theory linking prophecy resolution to linearizability completeness is subtle — only proved for first-order HeapLang [9], not for higher-order store (+). Extending prophecy to *infinite* prediction (e.g., consensus number) open.

3. **Relaxed memory.** Current Iris model assumes sequentially consistent (SC) atomics. Real hardware (ARMv8, RISC-V) and languages (C++11, Rust) have weak memory. Recent extensions iGPS / Cosmo / Iron promise RA-aware relaxed Iris but ghost reasoning under RC11 is far more complex — valid RAs may become invalid due to reordering. Only trivial stacks proved [4].

4. **Liveness and termination.** Linearizability is safety. Progress guarantees (*lock-freedom*, *wait-freedom*) require *liveness-aware* separation logics — Trillium [11] (intensional refinement) and Fairness extensions to Iris. Mechanized lock-freedom for MSQ still requires bespoke fairness ghost (enabled-forever tokens) not automated.

5. **Reuse and library ecosystem.** Despite modularity claims, Iris proofs are brittle to RA changes — upgrading from `Excl` to `Auth` fragments breaks proof scripts due to `iDestruct` reordering. No stable package manager for ghost theories; each verification duplicates authoritative list/multiset libraries.

**Research directions:**

- **Neurosymbolic invariant synthesis:** LLM-suggested invariants checked by Coq (cheap check) — promising for eliminating boilerplate;
- **Unified RA algebra compiler:** derive *history* RAs from Inductive specification automatically;
- **Relational + temporal integration:** combine Fairis (fairness) with NextGen Diaframe for lock-freedom linearizability.

## 7. Conclusion
Conduino from O'Hearn's original CSL to modern Iris is a trajectory from *fixed* heap reasoning to *protocol* reasoning. Ghost state provides *fictional* views of shared state as disjoint ownership; fancy updates turn invariant opening into *first-class logical effect* managed by masks; logical atomicity packages linearization points as *committable resources* that can be helped or prophesied.

What once required monolithic simulation arguments — forward/backward simulations of push/pop histories — is now factorized into:

1. Choose RA for history (`Auth (List Op)` for stack, `Auth (MSet Op)` for queue);
2. Couple physical representation to ghost history in invariant;
3. Show each operation commits at most once via `AU` and frame-preserving update;
4. Use Diaframe 2.0 to automate separation reasoning, leaving only protocol commitment manual.

The case studies — Treiber, Michael-Scott, Herlihy-Wing — cover fixed, external, and future-dependent linearization points, demonstrating that Iris in Coq is *expressive enough* to capture all known linearizability patterns foundationally, and increasingly *amenable* to automation. The remaining work is engineering: robust library reuse, weak-memory foundation, and synthesis of ghost machinery.

If you are implementing a lock-free structure in Rust's `crossbeam` or Go's `sync/atomic`, Iris suggests a discipline: assign each operation an `AU` token conceptually; if token can be committed by *any* thread observing your effect, you have helping; if you must guess future order, you have prophecy. Making those tokens explicit — even informally — prevents most concurrency bugs *before* mechanized verification.
