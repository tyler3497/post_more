# Sub-Millisecond Garbage Collection at Scale: ZGC Colored Pointers, Shenandoah Brooks Forwarding Barriers, and Region-Based Concurrent Evacuation

## Abstract

Modern latency-critical services — electronic trading, real-time bidding, interactive stream processing — cannot tolerate stop-the-world pauses that grow with heap size. This thesis presents a comparative architectural study of the two production concurrent compacting collectors in OpenJDK that break that scaling law: ZGC, built on *colored pointers* and JIT-injected *load barriers*, and Shenandoah, built on *Brooks forwarding pointers* and *snapshot-at-the-beginning* write barriers. We dissect how ZGC encodes mark and relocation metadata in the upper bits of every 64-bit reference and heals stale pointers lazily on the load path, and how Shenandoah maintains a to-space invariant through an indirection word in every object header, evacuating regions concurrently via compare-and-swap. We develop a unified barrier cost model, derive pause-time decomposition formulas showing why both collectors' pauses are asymptotically independent of heap size, and compare published evaluation results. We close with limitations — barrier throughput tax, footprint costs, weak-reference handling — and open problems in barrier elision and hardware assistance.

## 1. Introduction

For three decades, the central tension in managed-runtime garbage collection has been *throughput versus latency*. Classical throughput-oriented collectors — mark-sweep-compact and parallel scavengers — amortize collection cost efficiently but impose stop-the-world (STW) pauses proportional to the live set or heap size. On multi-gigabyte and terabyte heaps these pauses stretch into the hundreds of milliseconds or seconds, breaching the service-level agreements of latency-sensitive systems [1][2].

The last decade produced a architectural break: collectors whose maximum pause times are *independent of heap size*. Two such collectors ship in OpenJDK today, and they solve the problem from opposite ends of the pointer-metadata design space:

- **ZGC** stores collection metadata *in the pointer itself* — four color bits in every 64-bit reference — and intercepts mutator *loads* with a fast-path barrier that lazily heals stale references [1][2][3].
- **Shenandoah** stores collection metadata *in the object* — a Brooks forwarding pointer word in every object header — and intercepts mutator loads and stores with read and write barriers that maintain a to-space invariant during concurrent evacuation [4][5][6].

Both are *region-based* and *concurrently compacting*: the heap is partitioned into independently reclaimable regions, and live objects are relocated while application threads keep running. Region-based evacuation is what bounds the unit of copying work; concurrent barriers are what make that copying invisible to the mutator.

This thesis makes four contributions. **First**, it gives a unified mechanical account of colored pointers and Brooks forwarding pointers, including exact bit layouts, barrier fast/slow paths, and phase machines. **Second**, it proposes a barrier taxonomy and an analytical cost model that explains the throughput tax each design pays. **Third**, it derives pause-time decomposition formulas proving, informally but rigorously, why both collectors achieve heap-size-independent pauses, and surveys published empirical results. **Fourth**, it catalogues limitations and open research problems, from barrier elision to hardware-assisted pointer metadata.

The unified barrier cost model in §4.4 quantifies the throughput tax each design pays for pauselessness.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Snapshot-at-the-beginning (SATB) marking.** Concurrent marking must cope with mutator interference: while the collector traces the object graph, the mutator may delete the *last* reference to an object the collector has not yet reached, hiding it forever. Yuasa's snapshot-at-the-beginning discipline solves this with a *pre-write barrier*: before any reference field is overwritten, the overwritten (previous) referent is recorded and treated as logically live [7]. Formally:

> **Theorem 1 (SATB completeness).** Let *G₀* be the object graph at the start of concurrent marking. If every overwritten reference field's previous value is enqueued by the write barrier before the store becomes visible, then every object reachable in *G₀* is marked.

*Proof sketch.* Any path in *G₀* from a root either survives to mark end — in which case the tracer follows it — or has some edge deleted during marking; the deleted edge's target was enqueued by the barrier and is therefore traced. ∎

The price is *floating garbage*: objects that die during marking are retained until the next cycle. Objects allocated *during* marking are conventionally treated as implicitly live (black allocation), trading a little floating garbage for termination simplicity [7][8].

**Brooks forwarding pointers.** Concurrent *copying* is harder than concurrent marking: the mutator may read or write an object mid-relocation. Brooks' indirection technique adds one word to every object header — the *forwarding pointer* — which always designates the current (to-space) copy of the object. Evacuation proceeds as: copy the object, then `CAS` the forwarding word from from-space to to-space address; losers of the race discard their copies. Mutator barriers resolve every access through the forwarding word, preserving the invariant:

> **Lemma 1 (To-space invariant).** During concurrent evacuation, every mutator read observes and every mutator write targets the to-space copy of the object.

which is exactly what concurrent evacuation requires [5].

**Region-based heaps.** The Garbage-First (G1) collector partitioned the heap into equal-sized regions, tracked inter-region references with remembered sets, and evacuated a *collection set* of the regions containing the most garbage first — hence the name [7]. Regions bound the unit of copying: a collector need not move the whole heap to make progress. Both ZGC and Shenandoah inherit this insight while eliminating G1's STW evacuation pauses: ZGC replaces remembered sets with load-barrier-driven remapping, and Shenandoah evacuates regions concurrently under its forwarding barriers [1][4].

**Lineage.** ZGC was proposed as an experimental scalable low-latency collector in JDK 11 [1], declared production-ready in JDK 15 [2], and extended with a generational mode in JDK 21 [3]. Shenandoah was proposed in JDK 12 [4] as a low-pause-time collector led by Christine Flood and Aleksey Shipilёv, with its concurrent-copying design documented in a series of public technical talks [5][8] and the OpenJDK wiki [6].

## 3. Methodology

We conduct a *comparative mechanism analysis*: rather than benchmarking, we reconstruct each collector's invariants from published specifications, design documents, and conference material [1][2][3][4][5][6][8], then analyze those mechanisms with a uniform cost model. Our procedure is:

1. **Extract the metadata encoding** — where collection state lives (pointer bits vs. object header words) and its exact layout.
2. **Extract the barrier protocol** — which mutator operations are intercepted, the fast-path/slow-path split, and the invariant each barrier maintains.
3. **Extract the phase machine** — the sequence of STW and concurrent phases and what state transition each STW pause performs.
4. **Model costs analytically** — expected barrier cost per operation, pause-time decomposition, and asymptotic scaling in heap size *H*, live set *L*, and root set *R*.
5. **Cross-check against published evaluations** — pause-time and throughput figures reported by the collector authors and independent studies, presented as workload-dependent ranges rather than single numbers.

**Assumptions and scope.** We assume a 64-bit address space on x86-64 or AArch64, the platforms where both collectors are production-supported [1][4]. We analyze the non-generational ZGC design in depth and note where generational ZGC [3] differs. For Shenandoah we analyze the default SATB (`normal`) mode [6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Colored Pointers and the Load-Barrier Fast Path

ZGC's radical decision is to store per-reference GC metadata *in the reference*. On 64-bit platforms, virtual addresses use only the low 48 bits, leaving the upper bits free. ZGC partitions the 64-bit pointer as follows [1][2]:

| Bits | Width | Field | Meaning |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| 63–48 | 16 | Unused | Must be zero (canonical form) |
| 47 | 1 | Finalizable | Reference needs finalizer processing |
| 46 | 1 | Remapped | Pointer already forwarded to new location |
| 45 | 1 | Marked1 | Mark color, odd cycles |
| 44 | 1 | Marked0 | Mark color, even cycles |
| 43–0 | 44 | Object address | Byte offset; 2⁴⁴ bytes = **16 TB** max heap |

The four color bits yield the collector's entire phase vocabulary. Marking alternates the *good* color between `Marked0` and `Marked1` each cycle, so the previous cycle's marks invalidate themselves without any sweep. The `Remapped` bit records that a pointer has been forwarded past a relocation. Because colors occupy address bits, ZGC *multi-maps* physical memory: each physical page is mapped once per color into different virtual ranges, so a colored pointer is directly dereferenceable — no masking is required on the hot path [1].

The **load barrier** is the heart of the design. The JIT injects a check on (almost) every reference load from the heap. Conceptually:

```c
// ZGC load barrier (conceptual). Each color is a valid virtual mapping,
// so a good-colored pointer needs no stripping before dereference.
uintptr_t zgc_load_barrier(uintptr_t* field) {
    uintptr_t p = *field;                 // colored pointer
    if (p & BAD_COLOR_MASK) {             // stale color for this phase?
        p = zgc_slow_path(field, p);      // mark / remap / relocate as needed
        *field = p;                       // self-heal: publish healed pointer
    }
    return p;                             // good-colored, directly usable
}
```

The fast path is two to four instructions: load, test against the *bad mask*, branch-predicted not-taken. The slow path performs whatever the current phase requires — marking the referent during concurrent mark, or copying the object during concurrent relocate — and then **self-heals** by storing the healed pointer back through the field address, so each stale reference is repaired at most once per phase. This amortization is crucial: the slow path is *O(number of stale references)* per cycle, not per load.

> **Theorem 2 (Load-barrier convergence).** In any ZGC phase, the set of bad-colored live references is monotonically non-increasing, and every mutator load observes a good-colored reference after its barrier completes.

*Proof sketch.* The slow path replaces the loaded reference's color with the phase's good color and republishes it atomically through the field. Colors advance monotonically through the phase machine (mark colors alternate once per cycle; `Remapped` is terminal within a cycle), so a healed reference cannot regress to a bad color. ∎

ZGC's cycle is a phase machine with three STW pauses, each doing only *O(roots)* work: **Pause Mark Start** (flip the mark color, scan roots), **Concurrent Mark**, **Pause Mark End** (drain the mark stacks to a fixpoint), **Concurrent Prepare for Relocation** (select the relocation set), **Pause Relocate Start** (publish the relocation set), **Concurrent Relocate** (copy objects while load barriers heal mutator references) [1]. All heap-proportional work — marking, copying — happens concurrently.

Generational ZGC [3] refines this picture: a young generation collected frequently with the same machinery plus *store barriers* for old-to-young edges, and a single address mapping instead of multi-mapping.

### 4.2 Brooks Forwarding Pointers and the To-Space Invariant

Shenandoah takes the dual approach: metadata lives *in the object*, not the pointer. Every object header carries an extra word — the **Brooks forwarding pointer** — which at all times designates the object's current copy [5][8]. If the object has not been evacuated, the forwarding pointer points to the object itself; after evacuation it points to the to-space copy.

Mutator access is mediated by two barriers. The **read barrier** resolves every heap reference load through the forwarding word:

```c
// Shenandoah read barrier (conceptual)
inline oop shenandoah_read_barrier(oop obj) {
    oop fwd = *(oop*)((char*)obj + FWD_WORD_OFFSET);
    return (fwd != obj) ? fwd : obj;   // to-space copy if evacuated
}
```

The **write barrier** is two barriers in one: a SATB *pre-write* barrier that enqueues the overwritten referent (preserving Theorem 1), and a *to-space* barrier enforcing Lemma 1 — if the target object lies in the collection set and has not yet been evacuated, the barrier evacuates it *on demand* (copy, then `CAS` the forwarding word; on CAS failure, discard the copy and use the winner's address) before performing the store [5][6].

Shenandoah's cycle reflects its heavier barrier contract with more, but still short, STW pauses: **Init Mark** (STW, scan roots), **Concurrent Mark** (SATB), **Final Mark** (STW, drain SATB queues), **Concurrent Evacuation** (copy collection-set regions), **Init/Concurrent/Final Update References** (rewrite stale heap references through forwarding pointers — the phase ZGC avoids entirely via its load barrier), and **Concurrent Cleanup** [4][6]. Barrier coverage is exhaustive in the default `normal`/`satb` mode: ordinary loads and stores, `acmp`, `CAS`, array copy, and clone all pass through barriers; the experimental `iu` (incremental-update) and diagnostic `passive` modes vary this contract [6].

The engineering trade is visible: Shenandoah pays one header word per object and a read barrier on *every* reference load, but its pointers are plain addresses — no multi-mapping, no color management, and native code interoperates without address-space tricks.

### 4.3 Region-Based Concurrent Evacuation

Both collectors partition the heap into regions so that evacuation proceeds one bounded unit at a time.

**ZGC pages.** ZGC manages the heap as *pages* (its term for regions) of three size classes: small (2 MB), medium (32 MB), and large (multiples of 2 MB for humongous objects) [1]. Allocation is page-local and NUMA-aware; the relocation set is chosen per page by liveness. Because remapping is performed lazily by load barriers, ZGC needs no remembered sets: a stale pointer into an evacuated page is simply healed on next use.

**Shenandoah regions.** Shenandoah divides the heap into ergonomically sized regions (megabyte-scale, sized from the heap capacity) and selects a *collection set* each cycle using a garbage-first heuristic — the regions with the most reclaimable garbage, exactly as in G1 [4][7]. Live objects in the collection set are evacuated concurrently by GC workers while mutators run; afterwards, the concurrent update-references phase rewrites heap references to the to-space copies using the forwarding words, and empty regions are recycled.

The region abstraction is what makes the pause-time argument go through for *both* designs: the unit of copying is a region, regions are processed concurrently, and no STW phase ever scans or copies a heap-proportional amount of memory. The STW pauses in both collectors perform only root scanning, queue draining, and set publication — work proportional to the *root set*, not the heap.

### 4.4 A Unified Barrier Taxonomy

The two collectors — plus G1, the common ancestor — can be compared purely as barrier protocols:

| Collector | Barrier | Trigger | Fast path | Slow path action | Invariant maintained |
|-----------|---------|---------|-----------|------------------|----------------------|
| ZGC | Load barrier | Reference load | Test bad-color mask (~2–4 instr.) | Mark / remap / relocate; self-heal store-back | All observed refs carry the good color (Thm. 2) |
| ZGC (gen) | Store barrier | Reference store (old→young) | Card/dirty check | Record intergenerational edge | Young-gen root completeness |
| Shenandoah | Read barrier | Reference load | Load fwd word, compare | (rare) resolve to to-space copy | Reads observe to-space copy (Lemma 1) |
| Shenandoah | SATB write barrier | Reference store | Check marking flag | Enqueue overwritten referent | SATB completeness (Thm. 1) |
| Shenandoah | To-space write barrier | Reference store | Check region in collection set | Evacuate-on-write via CAS | All writes target to-space copy |
| G1 | SATB write barrier | Reference store | Check marking flag | Enqueue overwritten referent | SATB completeness (Thm. 1) |

The SATB pre-write barrier, shared by Shenandoah and G1, follows the classic Detlefs–et al. form [7]:

```
; SATB pre-write barrier (after Detlefs et al. [7])
if (marking_in_progress) {
    prev = load(obj + offset);      ; value about to be overwritten
    if (prev != null) satb_enqueue(prev);
}
store(obj + offset, new_val);       ; the actual mutator store follows
```

An analytical cost model unifies the throughput discussion. Let *C_fast* be the fast-path instruction cost, *p_slow* the probability a barrier takes the slow path, and *C_slow* the slow-path cost. Expected barrier cost per operation is **E[T] = C_fast + p_slow · C_slow**. For ZGC loads, *C_fast* ≈ 3 instructions and *p_slow* is bounded by (stale references)/(total loads) per cycle — small on steady-state workloads, spiky during relocation. For Shenandoah, *C_fast* is paid on *both* loads and stores, so write-heavy workloads feel its tax more, while ZGC's tax concentrates on load-heavy, pointer-chasing workloads. Neither tax exists in a barrier-free collector, which is precisely the throughput price of pauselessness.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Analysis

**Pause-time decomposition.** The heap-size-independence claim can be stated as a formula. For ZGC, with root set *R*, heap *H*, and live set *L*:

> **T_pause(ZGC) = T_safepoint + T_scan(R) + T_publish**

where *T_scan(R)* covers thread stacks and global roots and *T_publish* flips colors and publishes the relocation set. Marking (*O(L)*) and relocation (*O(moved bytes)*) are concurrent. Hence **T_pause is O(R), independent of H and L** — the defining property of the design [1][2]. Shenandoah's decomposition is analogous with slightly more terms (SATB queue draining, update-reference rendezvous), each bounded by *R* plus queue backlog, never by *H* [4][6].

**Reported results.** The table below gives representative orders of magnitude from public evaluations and author-reported figures. Exact numbers are workload-dependent; the invariant across studies is the *scaling behavior*, not any single millisecond value.

| Collector | Heap range | Reported max pause (representative) | Pause scaling | Throughput vs. G1 (representative) |
|-----------|-----------|--------------------------------------|---------------|-------------------------------------|
| ZGC | 8 MB – 16 TB | Sub-millisecond (design goal [1][2]) | Independent of heap, live set, root set | Within ~5–15% on throughput benchmarks |
| Shenandoah | Up to TB-scale | Low milliseconds, heap-independent [4][6] | Independent of heap size | Within ~5–10% on throughput benchmarks |
| G1 | GB-scale | Tens–hundreds of ms at large heaps [7] | Grows with heap / live set | Baseline |

The crucial column is the third-to-last: on SPECjbb2015-style workloads, ZGC's and Shenandoah's *maximum* pause curves are flat as the heap grows from gigabytes to terabytes, while G1's climbs — the empirical signature of the *O(R)* decomposition above. The throughput gap reflects the barrier tax modeled in §4.4.

**Reproducibility.** A latency-oriented ZGC run is configured as follows:

```java
// Reproducing a low-latency ZGC configuration (JDK 21+)
java -XX:+UseZGC -XX:+ZGenerational -Xmx32g \
     -Xlog:gc*:file=gc.log:time,level,tags \
     -jar latency-workload.jar
```

```java
// Equivalent Shenandoah configuration (default SATB mode)
java -XX:+UseShenandoahGC -Xmx32g \
     -XX:ShenandoahGCMode=normal \
     -Xlog:gc*:file=gc.log:time,level,tags \
     -jar latency-workload.jar
```

`gc.log` then yields the pause-time series from which maxima and percentiles are computed; both collectors' logs confirm pauses clustered in the sub-millisecond to low-millisecond band with no heap-size trend [1][6].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**ZGC's costs.** Colored pointers forgo *compressed oops*: every reference is a full 64-bit word, inflating heap footprint versus compressed-pointer collectors. Multi-mapping multiplies virtual address consumption (one view per color), pressuring virtual memory on constrained platforms — a cost generational ZGC [3] explicitly removed by moving to a single mapping. The load barrier taxes pointer-chasing workloads most, and weak-reference processing, class unloading, and native/JNI transitions remain subtle correctness frontiers. Generational mode adds tuning surface (tenuring thresholds, generation sizing) that the original single-generation design avoided.

**Shenandoah's costs.** The Brooks word costs 8 bytes per object — significant for small-object workloads — and the read barrier fires on *every* reference load, including `acmp` and `CAS`, broadening the throughput tax. Under extreme allocation pressure the pacing mechanism can degenerate to STW fallback collections. Generational Shenandoah remains experimental, and barrier coverage across `Unsafe`, VarHandles, and native code is a perennial maintenance burden [6].

**Open problems.** (1) *Provably safe barrier elision*: removing barriers the JIT can prove redundant, without weakening Theorems 1–2. (2) *Hardware assistance*: pointer-authentication and memory-tagging extensions could carry color bits in hardware, collapsing the barrier fast path. (3) *Formal verification*: machine-checked proofs of the ZGC phase machine and the Shenandoah evacuation protocol remain largely open. (4) *Unifying the designs*: whether one collector can combine colored-pointer remapping with plain addresses is unresolved.

---

## 7. Conclusion

ZGC and Shenandoah demonstrate that sub-millisecond, heap-size-independent pause times are achievable in a production managed runtime — but they achieve them through mirror-image trade-offs. ZGC complicates the *pointer* (colored bits, multi-mapped address space) to simplify the *protocol* (no remembered sets, no update-references phase, lazy self-healing). Shenandoah complicates the *object* (a forwarding word in every header) and the *barrier set* (read, SATB-write, and to-space barriers) to keep pointers plain and the address space simple. Both rest on the same foundation: region-partitioned heaps that bound the unit of copying, and concurrent phases that absorb all heap-proportional work, leaving STW pauses proportional only to the root set.
