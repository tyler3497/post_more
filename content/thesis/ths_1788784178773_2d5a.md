# Lattice QCD at the Exascale: Gauge Configurations, Hybrid Monte Carlo Sampling, and Hadron Spectroscopy

## Abstract

Lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the only known *ab initio* method for computing the nonperturbative dynamics of the strong interaction from first principles. By discretizing spacetime on a four-dimensional Euclidean grid, it recasts the QCD path integral as a statistical-mechanical average over gauge configurations — snapshots of the gluon field — sampled by Markov chain Monte Carlo. This thesis presents a rigorous treatment of the exascale-era lattice QCD pipeline: the construction of gauge configurations through the Hybrid Monte Carlo (HMC) algorithm with pseudofermion determinants, rational approximations, and domain-decomposed multigrid solvers; the continuum, infinite-volume, and chiral extrapolations that convert lattice correlators into physical hadron masses; and the software and hardware co-design that enabled the U.S. Exascale Computing Project (ECP) LatticeQCD application to exceed its 50× stretch goal on Frontier. We derive the HMC acceptance criterion from detailed balance, prove correctness of the pseudofermion representation, quantify critical slowing down and topological freezing, and show how variational spectroscopy extracts excited-state spectra. The result is a self-contained, graduate-level account of how exascale machines turn the QCD Lagrangian into precision hadron physics.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum chromodynamics is defined by a deceptively compact Lagrangian. For quark fields $\psi_f$ of flavor $f$ and gluon fields $A_\mu^a$,

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}} = -\frac{1}{4} F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{a\mu\nu} + \sum_f \bar{\psi}_f \left(i\slashed{D} - m_f\right) \psi_f,
$$

with $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + g f^{abc} A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$. At energies below roughly $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the coupling $\alpha_s$ grows large, perturbation theory fails, and the degrees of freedom we observe — protons, pions, glueballs, exotic $J^{PC}=1^{-+}$ mesons — are bound states whose masses are *generated* by the theory rather than input into it. Lattice QCD, introduced by Wilson in 1974 [1], makes these masses computable: Wick-rotate to Euclidean spacetime, replace the continuum by a hypercubic lattice of spacing $a$, and evaluate the path integral by importance sampling.

The central computational object is the **gauge configuration**: an assignment $U_\mu(n) \in \mathrm{SU}(3)$ to every link of a lattice with $N_s^3 \times N_t$ sites. A typical production lattice ($64^3 \times 128$, $a \approx 0.06\,\mathrm{fm}$) holds on the order of $10^9$ link matrices, and the physical observables are ensemble averages $\langle \mathcal{O} \rangle = Z^{-1} \int \mathcal{D}U\,\mathcal{O}[U]\,e^{-S_g[U]} \prod_f \det(D[U] + m_f)$. The fermion determinant is the computational crux: direct evaluation costs $\mathcal{O}(V^3)$ in the lattice volume $V$, so it is stochastically represented by pseudofermion fields and sampled with the Hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm [2]. Everything downstream — quark propagators, hadron correlators, effective-mass plateaus, variationally resolved excited states — is built on these configurations.

Three developments have reshaped the field over the past decade:

1. **Algorithmic**: rational HMC, Hasenbusch mass preconditioning, domain-decomposed HMC, and adaptive aggregation multigrid (DD-$\alpha$AMG) solvers [3] have tamed the inversion of the ill-conditioned Dirac operator at physical light-quark masses.
2. **Architectural**: the migration to GPU-accelerated nodes (Summit, then Frontier and Aurora) forced a rewrite of the software stack — QUDA, Grid, and SIMULATeQCD — around fine-grained parallelism and mixed precision.
3. **Institutional**: the ECP LatticeQCD project defined figures of merit for six benchmark computations across three fermion actions (HISQ, domain-wall, Wilson-clover), and the team exceeded its 50× speedup stretch goal on Frontier, achieving roughly a 70× average speedup [4].

This thesis walks the full pipeline, from the Wilson plaquette action to variational hadron spectroscopy, with the rigor of a PhD-level treatment: theorems with proofs, complexity analyses, representative Python reference code, and a candid accounting of limitations.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Lattice formulation

The Wilson gauge action is built from the plaquette — the smallest closed loop of links:

$$
S_g[U] = \beta \sum_{n}\sum_{\mu<\nu} \left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,U_{\mu\nu}(n)\right), \qquad \beta = \frac{6}{g_0^2},
$$

where $U_{\mu\nu}(n) = U_\mu(n) U_\nu(n+\hat{\mu}) U_\mu^\dagger(n+\hat{\nu}) U_\nu^\dagger(n)$ approximates $\exp(ia^2 F_{\mu\nu})$. Expanding for small $a$ recovers the continuum Yang–Mills action up to $\mathcal{O}(a^2)$ discretization errors. Fermions live on sites with the Wilson–Dirac operator

$$
D_W(n,m) = \delta_{nm} - \kappa \sum_{\mu} \left[(1-\gamma_\mu)U_\mu(n)\delta_{n+\hat{\mu},m} + (1+\gamma_\mu)U_\mu^\dagger(n-\hat{\mu})\delta_{n-\hat{\mu},m}\right],
$$

where the Wilson term lifts the fifteen unwanted doublers at the price of explicitly breaking chiral symmetry. Modern production actions improve on this baseline: the HISQ (highly improved staggered quark) action used by MILC suppresses taste-symmetry breaking through smeared links [5]; domain-wall fermions realize approximate chiral symmetry via a fictitious fifth dimension at higher cost; and the Wilson-clover action adds the Sheikholeslami–Wohlert term to remove $\mathcal{O}(a)$ errors. The FLAG collaboration's 2021 review [6] and the PDG lattice review [7] document the resulting sub-percent precision on decay constants, quark masses, and the strong coupling.

### 2.2 The sampling problem

The path-integral measure $\mathcal{D}U\,e^{-S_g[U]} \prod_f \det(D[U]+m_f)$ cannot be sampled by local heatbath once dynamical fermions are included, because the determinant couples every link to every other. The pseudofermion trick rewrites, for two degenerate flavors,

$$
\det\big((D+m)^\dagger(D+m)\big) = \int \mathcal{D}\phi^\dagger\mathcal{D}\phi\;\exp\left[-\phi^\dagger \big((D+m)(D+m)^\dagger\big)^{-1}\phi\right],
$$

turning the nonlocal determinant into a Gaussian integral over bosonic fields $\phi$ at the cost of one Dirac inversion per evaluation of the effective action. The Hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm of Duane, Kennedy, Pendleton, and Roweth [2] then evolves the extended system $(U, P, \phi)$ — links, conjugate momenta, pseudofermions — under a fictitious Hamiltonian, and corrects discretization error with a Metropolis accept/reject step.

> **Theorem (Correctness of HMC):** Let $\mathcal{H}(U,P) = S_{\mathrm{eff}}[U] + \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\,P^2$, and let $\Phi_\tau$ be a reversible, area-preserving (symplectic) integrator of the Hamiltonian equations of motion for time $\tau$. Then the Markov chain that (i) refreshes $P \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, (ii) proposes $(U',P') = \Phi_\tau(U,P)$, and (iii) accepts with probability $\min(1, e^{-\Delta\mathcal{H}})$, has stationary distribution proportional to $e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}[U]}$.

> **Proof:** Momentum refreshment is an exact Gibbs step for the Gaussian part. The leapfrog integrator is symplectic, hence volume-preserving, and reversible: $\Phi_{-\tau} = R\Phi_\tau R$ with momentum flip $R$. For a proposal from $x=(U,P)$ to $x'=(U',P')$, the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance $A(x\to x') = \min(1, e^{-\mathcal{H}(x')+\mathcal{H}(x)})$ satisfies detailed balance $\pi(x)T(x\to x') = \pi(x')T(x'\to x)$ with $\pi \propto e^{-\mathcal{H}}$, because reversibility makes the proposal symmetric up to the momentum flip, which is measure-preserving. Integrating out $P$ yields the target distribution in $U$. ∎

### 2.3 Related exascale efforts

The ECP LatticeQCD project adopted HISQ, domain-wall (DWF), and Wilson-clover actions as its challenge problems, with figures of merit measuring both gauge-generation rate and measurement throughput [4]. Performance-critical kernels — the Dirac operator, multigrid smoothers, and force terms — were ported to GPUs in QUDA [8] and the Grid framework, while SIMULATeQCD provides a modern multi-GPU HMC framework [9]. Parallel machine-learning research explores learned trivializing maps and gauge-equivariant flows as changes of variables to reduce $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ violations in HMC [10].

## 3. Methodology

Our methodological spine is the *exact* HMC with multiple timescales, as deployed in production ensemble generation. The methodology has four layers:

**Layer 1 — Action and ensemble design.** Choose a gauge action (e.g., Lüscher–Weisz improved), a fermion discretization, lattice spacings $a \in \{0.15, 0.12, 0.09, 0.06\}\,\mathrm{fm}$, and volumes with $m_\pi L \gtrsim 4$ to control finite-volume effects. MILC's $N_f = 2+1+1$ HISQ ensembles follow exactly this program [5].

**Layer 2 — Configuration generation.** Sample the path-integral measure with HMC using the Sexton–Weingarten multiple-timescale integrator: cheap gauge forces on a fine step, expensive fermion forces on coarser steps. Hasenbusch mass preconditioning splits $\det(D+m) = \det(D+m)\det^{-1}(D+\mu)\,\det(D+\mu)$ with $\mu > m$, so that each factor's force is smaller and better conditioned.

**Layer 3 — Measurement.** On decorrelated configurations, compute quark propagators $S = (D+m)^{-1}\eta$ with adaptive multigrid, contract them into hadron two-point functions $C(t) = \langle \mathcal{O}(t)\,\mathcal{O}^\dagger(0)\rangle$, and extract masses from effective-mass plateaus $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(t) = \ln[C(t)/C(t+1)]$.

**Layer 4 — Extrapolation and scale setting.** Fit $C(t)$ jointly across ensembles, extrapolate $a \to 0$, $m_\pi \to m_\pi^{\mathrm{phys}}$, $L \to \infty$, and set the lattice spacing via gradient-flow scales ($t_0$, $w_0$) or the $\Omega$-baryon mass.

A minimal reference implementation of the HMC accept/reject skeleton in Python illustrates the exactness structure (a pedagogical $U(1)$ analogue; production codes use $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ with even–odd preconditioned solvers):
```python
import numpy as np

def leapfrog(U, P, dS_dU, eps, n_steps):
    """Reversible, volume-preserving integrator for HMC trajectory."""
    P = P - 0.5 * eps * dS_dU(U)          # half-step momentum
    for _ in range(n_steps - 1):
        U = U + eps * P                    # full-step position
        P = P - eps * dS_dU(U)             # full-step momentum
    U = U + eps * P
    P = P - 0.5 * eps * dS_dU(U)
    return U, P

def hmc_step(U, S_eff, dS_dU, eps, n_steps):
    P = np.random.normal(size=U.shape)     # momentum refresh
    H_old = S_eff(U) + 0.5 * np.sum(P**2)
    U_new, P_new = leapfrog(U.copy(), P, dS_dU, eps, n_steps)
    P_new = -P_new                          # momentum flip (reversibility)
    H_new = S_eff(U_new) + 0.5 * np.sum(P_new**2)
    if np.random.rand() < np.exp(H_old - H_new):   # Metropolis correction
        return U_new, True
    return U, False
```

> The Metropolis step is what makes HMC *exact*: any integration error enters only through the acceptance rate, never as a systematic bias. A trajectory of length $\tau = n_{\mathrm{steps}}\,\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ decorrelates configurations; too short a $\tau$ random-walks, too long wastes inversions.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The lattice as a computational object

A $64^3 \times 128$ lattice has $V = 33{,}554{,}432$ sites. Each site carries 4 forward links, each an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ matrix stored as 18 doubles (144 bytes), for roughly $19\,\mathrm{GB}$ per configuration in double precision — before momenta, pseudofermions, and solver workspaces. The Dirac operator couples only nearest neighbors, so the dominant kernel is a sparse stencil with arithmetic intensity of order $1$–$2$ FLOP/byte: a memory-bound problem that maps naturally to GPU high-bandwidth memory. Even–odd (red–black) preconditioning halves the effective volume of the solve, and mixed-precision defect correction (single-precision inner solve, double-precision outer refinement) roughly doubles throughput on tensor-capable hardware.

| Ensemble feature | Coarse ($a\approx0.15$ fm) | Fine ($a\approx0.06$ fm) |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | $32^3\times48$ | $96^3\times192$ |
| Sites | $1.6\times10^6$ | $1.7\times10^8$ |
| Config size (fp64) | $\sim 0.9$ GB | $\sim 98$ GB |
| HMC trajectory cost | $\mathcal{O}(10)$ GPU-h | $\mathcal{O}(10^3)$ GPU-h |
| Dominant cost | Force evaluations | Dirac inversions |

### 4.2 Hybrid Monte Carlo with pseudofermions and rational approximations

The effective action sampled by HMC is

$$
S_{\mathrm{eff}}[U] = S_g[U] + \sum_{k} \phi_k^\dagger\, r_k\big((D+m)(D+m)^\dagger\big)\,\phi_k,
$$

where the rational approximation $r_k(x) \approx x^{-1/2}$ (or $x^{-\alpha}$) handles non-degenerate and odd numbers of flavors — the **rational HMC (RHMC)** of Clark and Kennedy [3]. The molecular-dynamics force on a link is

$$
F_\mu(n) = -\frac{\partial S_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\partial U_\mu(n)},
$$

evaluated via the chain rule through the Dirac inverse: each force term requires solves of $(D+m)^\dagger(D+m)\,x = \phi$. The Sexton–Weingarten scheme nests integrators so the cheap gauge force (no inversions) is integrated with step $\varepsilon_g \ll \varepsilon_f$ used for the fermion force. Hasenbusch preconditioning further splits the determinant into well-conditioned ratios, each with its own timescale — a standard production HMC may use *four or more* nested timescales.

> **Theorem (Pseudofermion exactness):** For positive-definite $M[U] = (D[U]+m)(D[U]+m)^\dagger$, the Gaussian integral $\int \mathcal{D}\phi^\dagger\mathcal{D}\phi\,\exp(-\phi^\dagger M^{-1}\phi)$ equals $\det M$ up to a $U$-independent constant.

> **Proof:** Diagonalize $M = V\Lambda V^\dagger$ with $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$, $\lambda_i > 0$. Changing variables $\eta = V^\dagger\phi$ (unitary, Jacobian 1), the integral factorizes into $\prod_i \int d\eta_i^* d\eta_i\, e^{-|\eta_i|^2/\lambda_i} = \prod_i (\pi\lambda_i) = \pi^N \det M$. The constant $\pi^N$ is absorbed into the partition function normalization. ∎

Critical slowing down is the central scaling law: the integrated autocorrelation time of the topological charge grows as $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}(Q) \propto a^{-z}$ with dynamical exponent $z \approx 5$ for periodic HMC, so that *topological freezing* sets in below $a \lesssim 0.05\,\mathrm{fm}$ [11]. Open boundary conditions in the time direction [12] allow topological charge to flow through the boundary and restore ergodicity, at the cost of boundary artifacts that must be excluded from measurement windows.

### 4.3 Multiscale solvers: from CG to adaptive multigrid

The Dirac operator at physical pion mass has condition number $\kappa \sim 10^5$–$10^6$; plain conjugate gradient on the normal equations needs $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\kappa})$ iterations. The breakthrough was **adaptive aggregation-based domain-decomposition multigrid (DD-$\alpha$AMG)** [3]: the near-null space of $D$ (the low modes responsible for critical slowing down of the solver) is captured by adaptively constructed test vectors, aggregated over $4^4$ blocks, and used to build a coarse-grid correction. The result is a solver whose iteration count is nearly *independent* of the quark mass, delivering order-of-magnitude speedups over deflated CG at the physical point. On GPUs, QUDA's multigrid implementation overlaps the setup and solve phases across MPI ranks with GPU-aware communication, and the ECP benchmarks measured both configuration-generation rate and measurement throughput as figures of merit [4][8].

### 4.4 Hadron spectroscopy from correlation functions

Given a gauge ensemble, spectroscopy proceeds through Euclidean two-point functions. For a nucleon interpolator $\mathcal{N} = \epsilon_{abc}(u_a^T C\gamma_5 d_b)u_c$,

$$
C(t) = \sum_{\mathbf{x}} \langle \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},t)\,\bar{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{0},0)\rangle = \sum_n |Z_n|^2 e^{-E_n t},
$$

a spectral decomposition over states $n$ with the quantum numbers of $\mathcal{N}$. The ground-state mass emerges from the effective mass $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(t+1/2) = \ln[C(t)/C(t+1)] \to E_0$ as $t \to \infty$, but signal-to-noise degrades as $\exp[-(E_0 - \tfrac{3}{2}m_\pi)t]$ for baryons (the Parisi–Lepage argument), so the plateau must be identified before noise swamps the signal — the central tension of baryon spectroscopy.

Excited states require the **variational method**: build a basis of interpolators $\{\mathcal{O}_i\}$, form the matrix $C_{ij}(t)$, and solve the generalized eigenvalue problem

$$
C(t)\,v_n = \lambda_n(t,t_0)\,C(t_0)\,v_n, \qquad \lambda_n(t,t_0) \xrightarrow[t\gg t_0]{} e^{-E_n(t-t_0)}.
$$

The Hadron Spectrum Collaboration used large operator bases with distillation (a low-rank smearing projector) to resolve the excited meson and baryon spectrum, including states with exotic quantum numbers inaccessible to the quark model [13]. Distillation factorizes the problem: eigenvectors of the 3D Laplacian computed once per configuration are reused for all Wick contractions, trading an upfront cost for a dramatically enlarged operator basis.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The empirical record of lattice QCD is a decades-long convergence of independent discretizations onto common continuum values — the strongest evidence that the method controls its systematics:

1. **Light hadron spectrum.** With only $m_\pi$, $m_K$, and $m_\Omega$ (or $f_\pi$) as inputs to fix the bare parameters, lattice QCD *postdicts* the masses of the nucleon, $\Delta$, $\Lambda$, $\Sigma$, $\Xi$, vector mesons, and heavy-light states at the percent level. The PDG review compiles these against experiment [7].
2. **CKM matrix elements.** FLAG averages of $f_K/f_\pi$, $f_+(0)$, $f_D$, $f_{D_s}$, $B$-meson decay constants, and mixing bag parameters deliver sub-percent inputs to CKM unitarity tests [6]; the tension between exclusive and inclusive $|V_{cb}|$ determinations remains an active driver of improved form-factor calculations.
3. **Exascale performance.** The ECP LatticeQCD application demonstrated its figures of merit — gauge-generation rate and measurement throughput for HISQ, DWF, and clover actions — with an average $\sim 70\times$ speedup over the pre-ECP baseline on Frontier, exceeding the 50× stretch goal [4]. Early Summit work had already shown a $9\times$ throughput gain with $8\times$ fewer GPUs via QUDA's adaptive multigrid, a $72\times$ effective speedup [14].
4. **Algorithmic scaling.** DD-$\alpha$AMG reduces the Dirac solve at physical quark mass from thousands of CG iterations to tens of multigrid cycles with mass-independent convergence [3]; RHMC made odd-flavor and non-degenerate simulations exact rather than approximate [3].

> **Theorem (Continuum limit universality):** Different lattice discretizations with the same continuum limit yield identical renormalized observables as $a \to 0$, up to their respective $\mathcal{O}(a^n)$ artifacts.

> **Proof (sketch):** By Symanzik's effective theory, each lattice action is described at long distances by the continuum QCD action plus a tower of higher-dimension operators with coefficients proportional to powers of $a$. Renormalization absorbs the universal divergences; the residual $a$-dependent operators vanish as $a \to 0$ provided the theory is renormalizable and the action is local — conditions satisfied by Wilson, staggered, domain-wall, and clover actions. Agreement of extrapolated results across actions (as documented by FLAG [6]) is the empirical counterpart. ∎

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

Intellectual honesty demands a ledger of what can go wrong:

- **Topological freezing.** At $a \lesssim 0.05\,\mathrm{fm}$ with periodic boundaries, HMC ceases to tunnel between topological sectors within affordable trajectory counts [11]. Open boundaries [12] mitigate this but complicate the transfer-matrix interpretation near the edges; master-field approaches (single huge volumes) are an active alternative.
- **Signal-to-noise.** The Parisi–Lepage scaling is exponential in Euclidean time for baryons; multi-baryon systems (nuclei) are exponentially harder still. Variational and distillation techniques help, but the noise problem is intrinsic to the path integral, not an algorithmic artifact.
- **Scale setting and isospin breaking.** Sub-percent precision requires controlling electromagnetic and $m_u \neq m_d$ effects; most ensembles remain isosymmetric, with QED included only in a growing subset of calculations.
- **Disconnected diagrams.** Flavor-singlet quantities (e.g., the $\eta'$ mass, nucleon sigma terms) need all-to-all propagators estimated stochastically; the stochastic noise can dominate the gauge noise.
- **Exascale software fragility.** Performance portability across Frontier (AMD), Aurora (Intel), and future architectures rests on abstraction layers (Kokkos, SYCL, HIP/CUDA backends) that add maintenance burden; a benchmark figure of merit is not a physics result, and Amdahl bottlenecks in I/O and global reductions persist.
- **Machine-learned samplers.** Flow-based and trivializing-map approaches [10] show reduced $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ on small volumes, but scaling to production volumes, proving ergodicity, and beating highly tuned HMC remain open; a learned sampler that silently under-samples a topological sector would bias physics undetectably.

## 7. Conclusion

Lattice QCD at the exascale is a mature computational science: a theoretically exact sampling algorithm (HMC, with pseudofermion and rational representations proven correct above), multiscale linear solvers that conquered the physical quark mass, a software stack re-engineered for GPU-dense nodes, and a spectroscopy program that converts Euclidean correlators into the hadron spectrum with controlled, extrapolated uncertainties. The ECP's 70× speedup on Frontier was not merely faster arithmetic — it bought finer lattices, larger volumes, and the statistics needed to attack resonances, exotics, and multi-hadron systems that were previously out of reach.

The frontier is now *algorithmic* rather than arithmetic: defeating topological freezing and critical slowing down at $a \to 0$, taming the signal-to-noise problem for nuclei, and incorporating QED and isospin breaking as standard. Whether the next leap comes from open boundaries, master fields, or machine-learned trivializing maps, the standard of evidence remains the one lattice QCD has always held: independent discretizations, blinded analyses, and continuum extrapolations that agree. The strong interaction yields its secrets only to those who count every configuration.
