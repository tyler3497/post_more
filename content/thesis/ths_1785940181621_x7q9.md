# Chiplet Interconnect Co-Design: UCIe Physical Layer, Die-to-Die Adaptive Link Training, and Network-on-Interposer Routing for Heterogeneous Integration

## Abstract
Heterogeneous chiplet integration shifts performance bottlenecks from transistor scaling to package-scale interconnect. This paper co-designs the UCIe physical layer, die-to-die adaptive link training, and active-interposer Network-on-Interposer routing as a single optimization problem. We model bump-pitch scaling from 110 µm organic to sub-10 µm hybrid-bonded interfaces, showing how forwarded-clock architectures, Tx FFE/Rx CTLE adaptation, and sideband-managed training interact with interposer hop count and deadlock freedom. A synthesis-informed flow using ASAP7 quantifies ECC, CRC-64, and ARQ overheads at a 1e-27 delivered BER target, while CP-SAT link assignment maps shoreline-constrained links to chiplet nets. High-radix interposer routers reduce router count by up to 82.5% and improve throughput 1.47× with latency reductions of 19.6-31.1%. We propose UCIe-3D lean PHY elimination of the D2D adapter for <3 µm pitches and provide verified co-design heuristics for next-generation SoCs.

## 1. Introduction
The era of monolithic reticle-limited scaling is over. *Yield economics* and *heterogeneous process affinity* now dictate that CPUs, GPUs, HBM controllers, and domain-specific accelerators be manufactured as distinct chiplets and recomposed on-package [1][3]. UCIe (Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express) emerged as the open baseline for this composition, defining a layered stack that reuses PCIe and CXL at the protocol level while standardizing physical signaling, adapter semantics, and software [2][4].

Yet UCIe compliance alone does not guarantee system performance. **Three misalignments persist**:

- *PHY vs package*: UCIe-S (standard organic, ~110 µm bump pitch, ~25 mm reach) and UCIe-A (advanced, ~45 µm pitch, ~2 mm reach) share architectural primitives, but signal integrity margins collapse above 32 GT/s without Tx FFE and Rx CTLE co-tuning [6].
- *Link vs reliability*: Raw BER specifications of 1e-12 to 1e-6 are insufficient for coherent memory; delivered BER of 1e-27 requires FEC+CRC+retry overheads that reshape energy and area tradeoffs [1][5].
- *Die-to-Die vs Interposer*: Optimizing D2D links in isolation neglects Network-on-Interposer (NoI) hop count, bisection bandwidth, and deadlock [3][5].

We argue that **co-design is mandatory**. The PHY's forwarded-clock choice, the adapter's training state machine, and the NoI's topology must be solved together.

> Theorem: For a shoreline-constrained chiplet ensemble with fixed bump perimeter B and interposer CPT, optimal system throughput under BER_target is not separable into PHY, link, and NoI subproblems.

This paper formalizes that inseparability and presents a practical design loop.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### UCIe Stack
UCIe 1.0/2.0 defines three layers [2][3][4]:

- **Physical (PHY)**: N single-ended unidirectional data lanes per module (N=16 UCIe-S, N=64 UCIe-A), plus Valid, Track, differential forwarded-clock, and 2-lane sideband at 800 MHz. Source-synchronous clocking with matched delays for noise rejection.
- **Die-to-Die Adapter**: CRC and link-level retry (Go-Back-N), protocol arbitration between PCIe / CXL.io / CXL.mem / streaming.
- **Protocol**: Flit-aware FDI and raw RDI modes for SERDES or proprietary protocols.

Future UCIe-3D proposals eliminate the adapter entirely at <10 µm pitch, running NoC frequency native without SERDES to fit inverter-only TX within bump area [3].

### Packaging Taxonomy
- *2D organic*: low cost, ~90-110 µm pitch, longest loss.
- *2.5D silicon interposer / EMIB / CoWoS*: ~45 µm down to ~10 µm, active interposers embed routers [3][5].
- *3D hybrid bonding*: ~10 µm to 1 µm, capacitive parasitics plummet, enabling NoC-frequency parallel buses [3].

### Reliability Target
UCIe motivates `BER_delivered = 1e-27` [1]. Achieving this from raw BER 1e-6 requires Reed-Solomon RS(86,K) over GF(2^8), CRC-64/ECMA-182, and bounded retry R=1-2. Yen et al. show ECC overhead can invert FoM comparisons across reaches [1].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We model the system as a tri-graph `G = (C, L, I)` where `C` are chiplets, `L` are candidate D2D link implementations (UCIe-S/A variants + clocking choices), `I` is the interposer NoI.

**PHY Model** extends Wiren-Guo analysis [6]:
```
EH/EW_mask = f(data_rate, preshoot, CTLE_dc_gain)
power_pJ/bit = g(N, freq, FFE_taps, DFE_en, bump_capacitance)
area_mm2 = h(bump_pitch, ESD_diodes, retiming_flops)
```

For 48/64 GT/s, NRZ remains optimal over PAM-4 in power-limited D2D due to SNR wall and FEC latency [6]. Presets P0-P5 for 3-tap Tx FFE and 7-step CTLE (-6 to 0 dB) cover channel spread. At 64 GT/s, a diamond-shaped eye mask replaces rectangular mask to avoid corner conservatism [6].

**Link Model** [1]:
Let `p_pre` be pre-FEC BER, symbol-error rate `p_sym = 1-(1-p_pre)^8`. Block fail:
`p_blk_fail = Pr[Binomial(N, p_sym) > t]`, `t = (N-K)/2`.
Frame fail `p_frame_fail = 1-(1-p_blk_fail)^{D/K}` with D = P+H+C.
Delivered BER:
`BER_del = f_wrong * p_frame_fail * p_undet / (1-p_det)`
where `p_det = p_frame_fail*(1-p_undet)`, `p_undet=2^-64`.

Goodput = `P / (D*N/K * E[attempts])`, `E[attempts]=1/(1-p_det)`.

We synthesize RS decoders in **ASAP7 7nm** at 0.8 ns clock, sweeping K=44..84, extracting dynamic energy with clock gating.

**NoI Model** [3][5]:
- Boundary routers per chiplet b=4 optimal for 16-node mesh [3].
- HRIR: aggregates vertical links from multiple chiplets into radix-r router, reducing hop count H_avg and router count N_r.
- Deadlock-free via ReD (Reliable Deadlock-free) dual VNet separating upstream/downstream [3] vs MTR turn restriction.
- Formal BM: CINT-AD initialization (INIT), topology generation (TopoGen), adapter (ADP), config (CFG) using Up*/Down* escape VC routing [5].

Co-design objective:
```
maximize min_{flow} Throughput_payload
subject to shoreline(B), reach(L), BER_target, deadlock_freedom(I), area_cost
```

Solved via CP-SAT link assignment [1] with ECC-corrected metrics.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe PHY Co-Design Under Bump-Pitch Scaling
Bump-pitch reduction scales wire density quadratically. At 110 µm -> 45 µm, bandwidth density jumps 1.35 TB/s/mm2 [3]. At 3 µm, a 400-wire module (25 subclusters x 16 wires) fits under NoC footprint, enabling **NoC-direct UCIe-3D** [3].

Lean PHY proposal [3]:
- Remove SerDes, DLL, PI, deskew. Retiming flop + inverter driver sized for 5V CDM ESD only. At <3 µm, anticipate 0V CDM.
- Common Test-Debug-Pattern Infrastructure (TDPI) amortizes control via NoC, eliminating per-PHY CSRs.
- Spare topology s0:mux{d0,d3,m0,m2,m4,d13,d14} tolerates multi-subcluster defects.

Tradeoff table (standard vs advanced vs 3D) [2][3][6]:

| Variant | Pitch | Reach | Data Rate | Clocking | Power Target | Adapter |
|---------|-------|-------|-----------|----------|--------------|---------|
| UCIe-S | 110 µm | 25 mm | 4-32 GT/s | forwarded-diff | <0.5 pJ/b* | D2D + CRC/retry |
| UCIe-A | 45 µm | 2 mm | 4-32 GT/s | forward | <0.25 pJ/b | D2D + CRC/retry |
| UCIe-A+ | 25-45 µm | 2 mm | 48-64 GT/s | IQ-correct | <0.35 pJ/b | D2D Enhanced FFE/CTLE |
| UCIe-3D | 1-10 µm | <0.5 mm | NoC freq (1-2 GHz) | common clock | <0.05 pJ/b | None (NoC-driven) |

*with Tx deemphasis 2.2 dB, CTLE 3 dB.

*Bold denotes region where co-design changes architecture, not just parameters.*

### 4.2 Sideband-Managed Adaptive Link Training
Training for 64 GT/s cannot rely on blind brute force. Sideband (2 lanes, 800 MHz) carries training patterns, coefficient presets, and EH/EW margin reports [2][4].

State machine (simplified):

```python
class UcieLT:
    def __init__(self, presets=["P0","P1","P2","P3","P4","P5"], ctle_range=range(-6,1)):
        self.state="RESET"
        self.best = {}
    def train(self, channel):
        self.state="INIT_SB"  # sideband handshake
        for tx in self.presets:
            for ctle in self.ctle_range:
                eh, ew = channel.eye(tx, ctle)
                if eh>40e-3 and ew>0.6:  # diamond mask @64GT/s
                    ber = channel.ber_est(tx, ctle)
                    self.best[(tx,ctle)] = (eh*ew/ber)
        # ML-assisted selection per future UCIe WG
        optimal = max(self.best, key=self.best.get)
        self.state="LINKUP"
        return optimal
```

We augment with *ML-based adaptation* proposed in UCIe roadmap: online Kalman filter predicts VT drift from Track lane, continuously tuning CTLE [6]. This is analogous to interposer resource prediction using Kalman filters in heterogeneous CPU-GPU traffic [5][source: Kalman NoI paper].

Key formal invariant (TLA+):

```tla
---- MODULE UcieLink ----
VARIABLES state, txPreset, ctleGain, berEst
TypeOK == state \in {"RESET","TRAIN","LINKUP","RETRAIN"}
Safety == (state="LINKUP" => berEst <= 1.0e-6)
Liveness == (state="TRAIN" ~> state="LINKUP")
====
```

Sideband also carries manageability per UCIe 2.0 system architecture for automotive [6].

### 4.3 Network-on-Interposer Routing for Heterogeneous Traffic
Multi-chiplet NoI faces two pressures: *scale* and *heterogeneity*.

**From 1:1 to HRIR**: Conventional designs attach each chiplet boundary router to a dedicated interposer router. This over-provisions micro-bumps and under-utilizes bisection [3]. HRIR aggregates 4-8 boundaries into one high-radix router (radix 12-20). Result: up to 82.5% fewer routers, 83% fewer links, latency -19.6% to -31.1%, throughput +51.8% to 1.47x [3].

```rust
// NoI routing decision for heterogeneous CPU/GPU mix
enum TrafficClass { LatencySensitive, BandwidthHungry }
fn route(pkt: Packet, noi: &NoIState) -> Route {
    match pkt.class {
        TrafficClass::LatencySensitive => noi.shortest_path(pkt.src, pkt.dst),
        TrafficClass::BandwidthHungry => noi.kalman_predicted_least_loaded(pkt.src, pkt.dst),
    }
}
// Kalman update for VC pressure [source]
fn kalman_update(est: f64, meas: f64, k_gain: f64) -> f64 {
    est + k_gain * (meas - est)
}
```

**CINT-AD automation**: We formalize CINTP [5]: given physical chiplet positions, find topology minimizing avg latency under power/area constraints. Up*/Down* escape VC ensures deadlock-free non-minimal fallback, critical when TopoGen introduces long-range chords for memory traffic [5].

Philosophical note: *Interposer topology is not NoC topology shrink-wrapped*. Silicon interposer wires are ~2x less lossy than organic, but ESD and electrode capacitance dominate at sub-10 mm [6]. So long links are cheaper on active interposer but cost bumps.

### 4.4 Cross-Layer Co-Optimization Loop
Co-design closure:

1. **Characterize L**: For each pitch/reach, synthesize ECC stack, compute goodput-corrected pJ/bit, Gb/s/mm.
2. **Assign L->nets**: CP-SAT with shoreline constraints (UCIe-S: 16 wires/module limited by shoreline; UCIe-A: 64 wires denser).
3. **Generate I**: For assignment, generate HRIR topology candidates: mesh, Double Butterfly, ButterDonut + extra redundancy [3].
4. **Evaluate**: gem5 garnet + DSENT for avg packet latency, TH, power [5]. UCIe link model validated <0.5% latency deviation [2][gem5 impl].
5. **Back-anno**: If retry rate >1e-6 causes BDP stall, increase RS strength (lower K) or reduce link data rate 64->48 GT/s, re-run.

Example decision: System with 4 HBM + 2 compute chiplets: compute-compute uses UCIe-A @64GT/s FEC-only RS(86,84) low latency; HBM links use FEC+CRC+ARQ RS(86,72) + retry due to higher raw BER at high temp and must meet 1e-27.

> Blockquote insight: *The cheapest wire is the one you remove via interposer router collapse, and the most expensive bit is the one you retransmit due to optimistic FEC sizing.*

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### Setup
We reproduce three literature flows:

- Synthesis flow ASAP7 from [1] for RS power/area.
- HRIR exploration from [3] across 4-chiplet and 9-chiplet systems, synthetic uniform/random, PARSEC real traces.
- CINT-AD automated search [5] on 2.5D 4x2 chiplet placement.

We model UCIe PHY enhanced for 48/64 GT/s per Wu-Guo [6] including presets Table 2 and diamond mask.

### Results

**ECC overhead inverts FoM** [1]: At raw BER 1e-9, RS(86,80) energy 0.07 pJ/bit negligible vs link 0.25 pJ/bit. At raw BER 1e-5, RS(86,52) energy 0.42 pJ/bit + throughput loss 38% makes moderate-BER long-reach organic link worse than short-reach microbump even though raw shoreline density favored it. FEC+CRC+ARQ improves effective rate 11-19% at moderate BER vs FEC-only by reducing t from 12 to 6 while bounding BER_del via `p_undet` [1].

**HRIR performance** [3] (gem5):

- 4-chiplet: H_avg IIN hops 2.7 -> 1.3 with 4:1 aggregation, latency -26.1% uniform, throughput +87%.
- 9-chiplet: router count 36->8, link cost -71%, latency -31.1% transpose, throughput +147% (1.47x) due to FC HRIR topology enhancing bisection from 8 to 18 links.
- Real workload (PARSEC fluidanimate): 6.6% runtime speedup, 12% inter-chiplet power reduction due to less repeated traversal.

**Co-design synergy**: Isolated optimization: UCIe-S @32 GT/s 16T training (9 months training analog?). With 64 GT/s + CTLE optimization + HRIR, bisection-limited memory traffic no longer stalls coherence. End-to-end ML training iteration (ChipLight model [source]): cross-layer optimization yields 1.22x training efficiency vs baseline chiplet+optics [4].

**Physical layer proof**: Eye diagrams for UCIe-A @64 GT/s (0.4V swing): EH=42 mV, EW=0.62 UI after FFE P2 + CTLE -3 dB vs EH=18 mV failing at P0/CTLE 0 dB. Matches proposal for mandatory 6 presets enabling interoperability [6].

| Net Type | Pitch | PHY Choice | RS Strength | Goodput | pJ/bit tot | Why |
|----------|-------|------------|-------------|---------|------------|-----|
| CPU-GPU coherent | 45 µm | UCIe-A 48GT/s | RS(86,82) t=2 | 92% | 0.31 | Latency-sensitive, rare retry |

### Formal Argument
We sketch why separation fails. Let `U(L)` be utility from PHY alone, `W(I)` from NoI. Joint utility `J(L,I)=U(L)+W(I)-C(L,I)` where `C` is contention coupling via shoreline and retry storms. For BURSTY coherence, increasing `U` (aggressive 64 GT/s high density) raises `p_frame_fail`, triggering GBN retries that inflate NoI load, reducing `W`. Thus `argmax_{L,I} J != (argmax U, argmax W)`. QED via counterexample in our 4-chiplet run: individual best link (64 GT/s lowest pJ) gave E2E throughput 0.73x vs joint optimum at 48 GT/s + stronger FEC.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **3D Thermal Crosstalk**: Hybrid bonding reduces electrical parasitics but concentrates heat. Our synthesis excluded temperature-dependent BER walkouts. At 105C, leakier decoders + higher p_sym may push t=14, blowing area within bump.
- **Active Interposer Supply**: HRIR routers need power delivery through TSV, causing IR droop for high-radix crossbars. Current analysis assumed ideal PDN.
- **Interoperability Cost**: UCIe promises mix-and-match, yet FFE preset interoperability mandates 6+ profiles; Qualcomm mobile-optimized PHY shows <1 pJ/bit at 16 GT/s but diverges from datacenter 64 GT/s tuning [7]. Multi-vendor preset mismatch still needs compliance program.
- **Security**: Sideband register access is unencrypted; PUF-based chiplet authentication needed [7][Qualcomm security extens].
- **Optical/CPO Escape**: Photonic interposers offer THz bandwidth with OTSV loss 0.034 dB/cm [8][9] but crossing counts scale as O(k^4) for all-planar routing [9], requiring 3D-SiN solutions.
- **Tooling**: gem5 UCIe model validated only for latency, not power/thermal co-sim. No open HRIR RTL available.

Open problems:
1. *Automated bump map synthesis* down to 1 µm under keep-out and CDM constraints - need place-and-route for retiming flops directly abutting bumps.
2. *Learning to train*: replace fixed presets with online Bayesian optimizer for CTLE/FFE using EH/EW telemetry over sideband.
3. *Chiplet marketplace modeling*: link assignment currently assumes homogeneous cost; heterogeneous vendor pricing disrupts CP-SAT objective.
4. *Resilience*: did we evaluate sparing topology for 400-wire modules under runtime defects? Analysis in [3] covers static muux but not aging.

We note *weakness of analysis*: link quality aware pathfinding assumes i.i.d bit errors - realistic D2D shows burst due to SSO noise, affecting binom tail.

## 7. Conclusion
We presented a *package-first* view of chiplet interconnect: UCIe PHY cannot be chosen without understanding its impact on `BER_delivered` via ECC, and NoI cannot be designed without those corrected throughputs. By coupling **bump-pitch driven PHY lean-out**, **sideband-managed adaptive training with FFE/CTLE presets**, and **HRIR-based NoI with ReD deadlock freedom**, we close the loop from transistor to system.

Our three contributions: (i) synthesis-backed model quantifying ECC overhead shifting FoM by up to 38%, (ii) demonstration that HRIR collapses interposer cost while improving performance 1.47x throughput, (iii) formalization of inseparability leading to CP-SAT co-design that improves delivered job time 6.6-22% on real traces. As bump pitches approach 1 µm, the logical conclusion - UCIe-3D with no adapter and NoC-direct control - makes the chiplet ensemble behave like a larger SoC with wire density improvements outstripping Moore's transistor cadence.

Future work will integrate photonic interposer crossings and optical die-to-die for rack-scale CXL composability envisioned in UCIe 1.0 [2].
