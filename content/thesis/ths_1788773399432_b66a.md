# Lean 4 Elaboration and Dependent Type Checking: The Elaborator, Metavariable Assignment, Unification up to Reducible Definitional Equality, and Typeclass Resolution as Logic Programming

## Abstract

The Lean 4 elaborator bridges surface syntax and the trusted kernel by inferring and checking terms in dependent type theory while solving unknowns through a sophisticated constraint mechanism. This thesis provides a comprehensive account of that bridge. We describe the elaboration architecture: the bidirectional `TermElabM` framework, preprocessing of syntax, coercion insertion, and error reporting. We analyze metavariables as the central data structure of incomplete information, showing how `Lean.Meta.Basic`'s four intertwined services — weak-head normalization with transparency modes, definitional-equality checking with metavariable assignment, type inference, and typeclass resolution — mutually depend within the `MetaM` monad [3][4]. We dissect unification up to *reducible* definitional equality, explaining transparency lattices, the flex-rigid and flex-flex cases, occurs checks, postponed constraints, and universe-level solving. We then treat typeclass resolution as logic programming: the tabled resolution procedure of Selsam, Ullrich, and de Moura [2], discrimination-tree indexing of instances, and the interplay with unification during proof search. We conclude with empirical observations on elaborator performance, limitations including undecidability of definitional equality and exponential TC search blowups, and directions for more predictable elaboration.

## 1 Introduction

Dependent type theory, in the style of the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC), gives a single language for programming and proving. Yet the terms programmers write are not the terms the kernel checks: programmers write *surface syntax* — omitting implicit arguments, relying on overloading and coercions, leaving holes (`_`) and named binders underspecified. The component that turns this surface text into fully annotated kernel terms is the **elaborator**. In Lean 4, the elaborator is simultaneously the largest, most complex, and least formalized part of the system: roughly half of the Lean source tree lives in `Lean.Elab`, and its behavior determines whether a proof assistant feels cooperative or hostile [5].

The central thesis of this work is that Lean 4 elaboration can be understood as *constraint-driven synthesis with incomplete information*. Every gap in the user's input becomes a **metavariable** — a first-class unknown whose type (its local context) is tracked — and elaboration is the process of filling those unknowns via two mechanisms: **unification up to definitional equality** (`isDefEq`, `t =?= u`), which assigns metavariables when comparing expressions, and **typeclass resolution**, a Prolog-style logic-programming search that constructs proof terms for implicit instance arguments [2]. The four pillars documented in `Lean.Meta.Basic` — WHNF computation, definitional-equality checking with unification, type inference, and typeclass resolution — are mutually recursive: unification needs WHNF, type inference needs unification, and typeclass resolution needs both [4].

Understanding the elaborator matters beyond Lean users. Proof-assistant ergonomics is the difference between formal methods being a research curiosity and a verification infrastructure: every successful large-scale formalization (mathlib4, CompCert, seL4) rests on an elaborator that silently discharges thousands of routine obligations. This thesis reconstructs the design decisions, algorithms, and engineering trade-offs behind one of the most sophisticated elaborators ever built.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Dependent type theory and the kernel boundary

Lean 4's kernel implements a variant of CIC with **universe polymorphism**, **inductive types**, **quotient types**, and **proof irrelevance**. The kernel is small and trusted; everything else — including the elaborator — is untrusted code that must produce terms the kernel accepts. A term is well-typed only if it can be synthesized or checked against its type up to *definitional equality*, the congruence generated by [3][5]:

| Reduction | Description |
|---|---|
| β | `(λ x, b) a → b[x := a]` — function application |
| δ | unfolding of *reducible* or *transparent* definitions |
| ζ | `let x := v; b → b[x := v]` — let-expression inlining |
| ι | reduction of recursors/match on constructor values |
| η | `f ≈ λ x, f x` — eta expansion (definitional in Lean) |

Because definitional equality involves unfolding arbitrary definitions, it is in general **undecidable** in the presence of non-terminating recursion; the elaborator must therefore be a careful heuristic engine rather than a decision procedure.

### 2.2 Prior work on elaboration

The canonical reference is de Moura, Kong, Avigad, van Doorn, and von Raumer's **"Elaboration in Dependent Type Theory"** [1] (arXiv:1505.04324), which describes the Lean 3 elaborator: bidirectional elaboration, the `PreTerm`/`PostTerm` pipeline, coercion elaboration, and the use of choice constraints for overloading. Lean 4 rebuilt the elaborator from scratch (the codebases share no code), but the conceptual core — bidirectional traversal with expected types, metavariable postponement, and the unification engine — survives and is substantially extended [5].

Typeclass resolution in Lean 4 is described by Selsam, Ullrich, and de Moura in **"Tabled Typeclass Resolution"** [2] (arXiv:2001.04301): a memoizing (tabled) Prolog-like search that indexes instances in discrimination trees, avoiding the exponential blowups of naive backtracking.

The metaprogramming book [3] and the module documentation of `Lean.Meta.Basic` [4] document the `MetaM` infrastructure: WHNF with transparency modes and definitional equality with metavariable support.

> **Theorem (soundness boundary):** The kernel accepts a term iff its typing derivation uses only the core rules of CIC. The elaborator's job is totality of *translation*: given surface syntax `s`, produce kernel term `t` and type `T` such that `⊢ t : T` holds definitionally. No completeness guarantee exists — the elaborator may fail on inputs the theory accepts.

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis combines primary-source archaeology (the Lean 4 source tree at `github.com/leanprover/lean4` [5], especially `src/Lean/Elab/Term.lean`, `src/Lean/Meta/Unify.lean`, `src/Lean/Meta/WHNF.lean`, `src/Lean/Meta/Basic.lean`, and `src/Lean/Meta/SynthInstance.lean`), the two canonical papers [1][2], the Lean 4 metaprogramming book [3], and reconstruction of the elaborator's invariants from its documented data structures. We organize the deep dive around the four services of `Lean.Meta.Basic`:

1. **Weak-head normalization** with transparency modes,
2. **Definitional equality checking with metavariable assignment** (unification),
3. **Type inference** (bidirectional elaboration), and
4. **Typeclass resolution** as tabled logic programming.

We treat each algorithmically, give Lean-style pseudocode, and quantify its costs.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The bidirectional elaborator: `TermElabM` and expected types

Elaboration in Lean 4 is **bidirectional**: every elaboration function takes an optional *expected type* and runs in one of two modes [1][3].

```lean
-- Simplified skeleton of term elaboration
def elabTerm (stx : Syntax) (expectedType? : Option Expr) : TermElabM Expr := do
  let e ← match stx with
    | `(fun $x => $b)  => elabLambda x b expectedType?
    | `($f $a)        => elabApp f a expectedType?     -- application elaborator
    | `($_)           => mkFreshExprMVar expectedType? -- hole: fresh metavariable
    | _               => elabTermUsing expectedType?   -- fallback + coercions
  -- Insert coercions: if type of e is not defeq to expectedType?, try ↑e
  ensureHasType expectedType? e
```

The key elaboration strategies are [1][5]:

- **Synthesis mode** (no expected type): elaborate subterms bottom-up, inferring types; e.g., `f x` synthesizes `f : Π a, B a`, then checks `x` against `a`.
- **Checking mode** (expected type given): propagate the type downward; e.g., `fun x => b` against `Π x : A, B` elaborates `b` against `B` with `x : A` in context. This avoids creating unnecessary metavariables and yields better error messages.
- **Application elaboration** (`elabApp`): the most intricate elaborator. It elaborates the function, unfolds its type to a telescope of binders `∀ (x₁ : A₁) ... (xₙ : Aₙ), B`, creates a metavariable for each *implicit* or *instance-implicit* argument, elaborates each explicit argument against its domain type (instantiating previous metavariables), and finally synthesizes pending instance arguments. Named-argument resolution, dot-notation (`x.f` → `Namespace.f x`), and coercion insertion all happen here.
- **Coercion insertion**: after elaboration, if the inferred type `T` is not definitionally equal to the expected type, the elaborator attempts `↑`-coercions — registered `Coe`/`CoeT`/`CoeOut` instances resolved by (you guessed it) typeclass search — and re-checks.

The elaborator never fully *trusts* itself: every produced term is re-checked by the kernel's independent (and much simpler) type checker.

### 4.2 Metavariables: unknowns with local contexts

A **metavariable** `?m` is a placeholder for a term *and* its type is itself possibly unknown. In Lean 4, `Expr.mvar m` carries a unique `MVarId`; the metavariable *declaration* stores:

```haskell
-- Conceptual (Lean: Lean.MetavarContext.MetavarDecl)
data MetavarDecl = MetavarDecl
  { userName  :: Name          -- for error messages and `?_`
  , lctx      :: LocalContext  -- the local context at creation: types of free vars
  , type      :: Expr          -- the expected type (may contain other mvars)
  , depth     :: Nat           -- for postponement ordering
  , kind      :: MetavarKind   -- natural | synthetic | syntheticOpaque
  }
```

There are three kinds of metavariables [4][5]:

1. **Natural** (`?m`): ordinary holes, solved by unification or left for the user/tactics.
2. **Synthetic**: created for typeclass goals (`SynthInstance`) and postponed universe constraints; processed by `synthesizePendingMVars` at the end of elaboration.
3. **Synthetic opaque**: goals that must not be solved by unification at all (e.g., goals postponed across `by` blocks).

Metavariables are assigned by unification, and assignment is *monotonic*: once `?m := v`, the assignment is never undone (no backtracking in the unifier — backtracking lives one level up, in TC resolution and tactic blocks). Each assignment records the *local context* so that `v` may only mention free variables available at `?m`'s creation site; the **occurs check** plus *scope checking* guarantees well-scopedness.

```rust
// Pseudocode: assignment with context validation
fn assign_mvar(m: MVarId, v: Expr) -> MetaM<()> {
    let decl = get_mvar_decl(m);
    // 1. occurs check: m must not occur in v (modulo assignment)
    // 2. abstract v over decl.lctx: every fvar in v must be in lctx
    //    (otherwise the assignment is ill-scoped and unification fails)
    let v_abs = abstract_mvars(v, decl.lctx);
    set_mvar_assignment(m, v_abs);
}
```

> **Theorem (monotonicity):** Metavariable assignment in `MetaM` is append-only. Combined with the occurs check, this guarantees that the substitution applied to a term is always idempotent and well-scoped, so `instantiateMVars` terminates.

### 4.3 Unification up to reducible definitional equality

The heart of the elaborator is `isDefEq : Expr → Expr → MetaM Bool` — unification *modulo* definitional equality [4]. Its two inputs may contain assignable metavariables; on success it may assign them (`t =?= u`). The algorithm [3][5]:

1. **Instantiate** assigned metavariables on both sides; reduce both to **weak-head normal form** (WHNF) at the current *transparency*.
2. **Transparency lattice**: constants carry a *reducibility* attribute — `reducible` > `default` > `irreducible` (opaque). `isDefEq` can run at four transparency modes: `default` (unfold reducible + default), `reducible` (unfold only `[reducible]` constants), `instances` (reducible + typeclass instances), and `none` (unfold nothing). Running at `.reducible` during typeclass search prevents expensive unfolding of ordinary definitions — a critical performance design decision [4].
3. **Case analysis** on the WHNF heads:
   - *rigid–rigid*: same head constructor → unify arguments pairwise; different heads → fail (after trying `isDefEqProjDelta` for projections, and structure eta).
   - *flex–rigid* (`?m =?= t`): if `?m` is assignable and passes the occurs check, assign `?m := t` (with Miller-pattern refinement for higher-order cases). Otherwise **postpone** the constraint.
   - *flex–flex* (`?m =?= ?n`): link them or postpone; postponed constraints are retried after more assignments accrue.
   - *universe levels*: `Level` constraints are collected and solved by a small dedicated solver, with `univApprox` fallback when no progress is made [4].
4. **Postponement**: when a constraint cannot be decided now (stuck on unassigned metavariables), it is pushed onto `State.postponed` keyed by the metavariables it mentions; `synthesizePendingMVars` re-processes the queue at elaboration end. The `synthPendingDepth` counter guards against re-entrant synthesis [4].

```
isDefEq t u:
  t ← instantiateMVars t; u ← instantiateMVars u
  t ← whnf t (transparency); u ← whnf u (transparency)
  match (t, u):
    | (mvar m, _) if assignable m  => assignOrPostpone m u
    | (_, mvar m) if assignable m  => assignOrPostpone m t
    | (app f a, app g b) if f ≈ g  => isDefEq a b   -- congruence
    | (lam .., lam ..)             => isDefEq under binder
    | (const c .., const d ..)     => unfold if reducible enough, else fail
    | _                            => structural mismatch → fail
```

The unifier is deliberately **incomplete** (higher-order unification is undecidable; even first-order unification modulo βιδζ is heuristic), and incompleteness is *by design*: postponement lets later information resolve stuck constraints, and failure produces localized error messages rather than divergence.

### 4.4 Typeclass resolution as tabled logic programming

Instance-implicit arguments `[Add α]` elaborate to synthetic metavariables whose values are *proofs* found by **typeclass resolution** — a logic-programming search over the global instance database [2]. The procedure (`Lean.Meta.SynthInstance`):

1. **Goal**: synthesize `?inst : C t₁ ... tₙ` where `C` is a class.
2. **Index lookup**: instances are indexed in **discrimination trees** keyed by the conclusion's head symbol and argument shapes, so only plausibly-applicable instances are tried — this is what makes mathlib4's ~10⁴ instances tractable [2].
3. **Tabled search**: goals are memoized in a table keyed by the goal up to reducible defeq; if the same goal recurs (diamond inheritance, e.g., `AddCommMonoid → AddMonoid` and `→ CommMonoid`), the cached result (or in-progress marker, for cycle detection) is reused instead of re-searching. This converts exponential naive Prolog search into something near-linear on diamonds [2].
4. **Unification at each step**: matching an instance conclusion against the goal uses `isDefEq` at `.instances` transparency — notably *not* `.default`, and with `isDefEqProjDelta` disabled, because projection unfolding during TC search caused severe performance pathologies [4].
5. **Backtracking**: unlike metavariable assignment, TC search *does* backtrack across candidate instances; the first success commits, and remaining candidates are discarded (Lean uses depth-first search with chronological backtracking, plus the table).

```tla+
---- MODULE TypeclassSearch ----
EXTENDS Naturals
(* Abstract state machine of tabled TC resolution *)
VARIABLES goal, table, stack
TryInstance(inst) ==
  /\ MatchConclusion(inst, goal)       \* isDefEq at .instances transparency
  /\ stack' = Push(stack, inst)
  /\ UNCHANGED <<goal, table>>
MemoHit(g) == g \in DOMAIN table        \* tabled: reuse or detect cycle
Resolve ==
  \/ \E g \in Subgoals(goal) : MemoHit(g) /\ UseCached(g)
  \/ \E inst \in LookupDiscrTree(goal) : TryInstance(inst)
====
```

The discrimination-tree index deserves emphasis: it is a trie over term *patterns* where each node branches on the head symbol at each argument position, with special handling for metavariables and reducible unfolding. Instance lookup is thus sub-linear in the number of instances for well-discriminated goals, and the tabled memoization handles the diamond-heavy algebraic hierarchy that naive search would blow up on [2].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness invariants (proof sketches)

- **WHNF termination**: `whnf` at any fixed transparency terminates because each unfolding step strictly decreases a measure on (transparency-respecting) redex depth, and metavariable instantiation is finite (assignments are monotonic and acyclic by the occurs check) [4].
- **Unifier soundness**: every assignment `?m := v` is validated by `isDefEq`, so the instantiated term remains well-typed up to definitional equality; the kernel re-checks everything, making the elaborator's soundness *defense in depth* rather than a single trusted component [1][5].
- **Tabled TC completeness (relative)**: Selsam et al. prove that tabling preserves the success set of the underlying SLD search for the definite-clause fragment used by instances, while cycle detection converts infinite derivations into failure rather than divergence [2].

### 5.2 Performance characteristics

Empirical behavior observed in large developments (mathlib4) and documented in the sources [2][4][5]:

| Component | Typical cost | Pathological case |
|---|---|---|
| `whnf` at `.reducible` | near-constant | deep `.default` unfolding chains |
| `isDefEq` rigid–rigid | linear in term size | higher-order flex–rigid, postponed storms |
| TC discrimination-tree lookup | sub-linear in #instances | poorly discriminated goals (e.g., `OfNat`) |
| Tabled TC search | near-linear on diamonds | exponential without tabling (pre-2019 Lean 3) |
| Postponed-constraint retry | amortized linear | retry storms on huge `calc`/`simp` terms |

The single most impactful optimization in Lean 4's history was arguably the **tabled resolution** of [2]: before tabling, the algebraic hierarchy's diamonds made instance search exponential; after, mathlib-scale developments became feasible. The second is the **transparency discipline**: running unification at `.reducible` inside hot loops (TC search, `simp` matching) avoids unfolding the thousands of definitions that `.default` would touch.

### 5.3 A worked micro-example

Consider elaborating `f : α → β`, `x : α ⊢ f x`:

1. `elabApp` synthesizes `f : α → β` (metavariables `?α ?β` already solved from the context).
2. It creates no implicit holes; it elaborates `x` against expected type `?α` — checking mode, so `x` is accepted iff `isDefEq (typeOf x) ?α` succeeds, which it does by reflexivity.
3. The result `f x : ?β[x := x]`; no synthetic metavariables remain; `synthesizePendingMVars` finds an empty queue.

With typeclasses — `∑ i ∈ s, f i` requiring `AddCommMonoid β` — step 2 additionally creates a synthetic metavariable `?inst : AddCommMonoid ?β`, and the TC resolver runs its tabled search, typically hitting the discrimination tree on head `AddCommMonoid` and unifying `?β` against instance conclusions, which may in turn assign `?β` itself. This is the *mutual recursion* the architecture is built around: elaboration → unification → TC search → unification → ….

## 6 Limitations

1. **Undecidability**: definitional equality with general recursion is undecidable; `isDefEq` is a heuristic that can both fail spuriously and diverge (mitigated by `maxRecDepth` fuel and caching, but fuel-exhaustion errors are a real user-facing phenomenon) [3][4].
2. **Incompleteness of unification**: higher-order patterns beyond Miller's fragment are postponed or rejected; users must annotate. There is no principal-types property; elaboration order affects which solution is found.
3. **TC search blowups**: despite tabling, goals with many applicable instances (numeric literals via `OfNat`, coercions, `HAdd` heterogenous operators) still produce large search spaces; `inTypeClassResolution` disables expensive defeq features as a band-aid, and the sources admit "a proper solution would require a more sophisticated caching mechanism" [4].
4. **Error messages**: because failure emerges from deep inside backtracking search, the reported error often points at the wrong subterm; the `ref` tracking in postponed entries exists precisely to claw back source locations [4].
5. **No backtracking unifier**: the unifier commits to assignments, so elaboration is order-sensitive — reordering implicit arguments can change success into failure. Tactic-block postponement (`syntheticOpaque`) only partially isolates this.
6. **Opacity vs. performance tension**: marking a definition `irreducible` improves elaborator predictability but can break downstream `simp`/`rw` reasoning that needs to see through it; library design must constantly negotiate this trade-off.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Lean 4's elaborator is best understood not as a type checker with extras but as a **constraint-solving engine over a dependently typed language**: metavariables reify ignorance, `isDefEq` resolves ignorance by unification up to (reducible) definitional equality with transparency-controlled unfolding, and typeclass resolution resolves ignorance by tabled logic-programming search over discrimination-tree-indexed instances. The four services of `Lean.Meta.Basic` — WHNF, defeq-with-unification, inference, and TC resolution — form a mutually recursive knot inside `MetaM` that is the true core of the system [4]. Its design lessons — monotonic metavariable assignment with occurs checks, postponed constraints retried at quiescence, transparency lattices to bound unfolding, and tabling to tame search — generalize to any elaborator for dependent types. The open problems are equally clear: principled caching for TC-driven defeq, order-independent elaboration, and error reporting that survives backtracking. As formal verification scales toward ever-larger developments, the elaborator — not the kernel — will remain the decisive engineering artifact.
