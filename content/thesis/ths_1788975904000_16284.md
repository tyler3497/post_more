# Signal Temporal Logic and Runtime Verification of Cyber-Physical Systems: Robustness Semantics, Falsification, and Online Monitoring

## Abstract

Cyber-physical systems (CPS) intertwine discrete control software with continuous physical dynamics, and their safety-critical nature demands rigorous specification and verification. Signal Temporal Logic (STL), introduced by Maler and Nicković in 2004 for monitoring continuous signals, has become the dominant formalism for expressing real-time requirements over dense-time trajectories. This thesis develops a unified treatment of STL for *runtime verification*: the quantitative robustness semantics that assigns a real-valued satisfaction margin to each trace [1]; the reduction of falsification — the search for violating inputs — to stochastic optimization over that margin, as implemented in tools such as Breach, S-TaLiRo, and its Python successor PSY-TaLiRo [5]; and online monitoring over partial traces, where the verdict is an interval of reachable robustness values rather than a single number [2]. We prove soundness of the robustness semantics, correctness of the falsification reduction, and the monotone-tightening property of robustness intervals, and we evaluate the approach on a synthetic automotive benchmark. The thesis closes with the masking and scale problems of min/max aggregation, recent causation-monitoring extensions [3], and a roadmap toward stochastic and smooth differentiable semantics.

## 1. Introduction

A cyber-physical system is a feedback interconnection of computational elements (controllers, estimators, software) and physical processes governed by differential equations — from adaptive cruise control and autonomous driving to pacemakers, power grids, and robotic manipulators. Verification is intrinsically hard: the state space is continuous and high-dimensional, models are nonlinear, and many systems of interest exist only as *black-box simulators* rather than analyzable closed forms.

Classical model checking, which exhaustively explores finite-state abstractions, struggles in this regime. Two complementary strategies have matured over two decades: **testing and falsification** — generate simulation traces and check for requirement violations, guiding the search with optimization; and **runtime verification (monitoring)** — observe a single execution, offline from a log or online during operation, and decide satisfaction of a specification.

Both need a specification language expressive enough for continuous-time, real-valued behaviors, and quantitative enough to distinguish "barely safe" from "comfortably safe." Signal Temporal Logic provides exactly this.

STL extends propositional logic with temporal operators *timed* by real intervals. An atomic predicate is an inequality $f(x) \geq 0$ over the signal $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$; from the timed until $\varphi\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\psi$ derive eventually $\Diamond_{[a,b]}$ and globally $\Box_{[a,b]}$. For instance, *"whenever speed exceeds 90 km/h, it must return below 90 within 3 seconds"* becomes

$$\Box_{[0,T]}\big((v > 90) \Rightarrow \Diamond_{[0,3]}(v \leq 90)\big).$$

The Boolean satisfaction relation $\mathbf{w} \models \varphi$ tells whether a trace satisfies the formula. But for engineering purposes a Boolean verdict is too coarse: a cruise controller that keeps speed 0.05 units below a threshold and one that keeps 20 units below it are not equally safe. Fainekos and Pappas (2006; journal version 2009) introduced a *quantitative* or *robustness* semantics $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t) \in \mathbb{R}$ measuring the distance of the trace from the satisfaction boundary. Donzé and Maler (2010) extended this spatial robustness with a *temporal* component measuring how far the trace can be shifted in time before the verdict flips [1].

This thesis makes the following contributions:

- **(C1)** A self-contained exposition of the spatiotemporal robustness semantics of STL with a proof of the soundness theorem.
- **(C2)** A formal account of falsification as robustness minimization, covering the optimizer-in-the-loop architecture of Breach, S-TaLiRo, and PSY-TaLiRo [5], plus refinements via specification transformation and valued Booleans [7].
- **(C3)** A theory of online monitoring over partial traces via robustness intervals, culminating in the monotone-tightening theorem, with reference to Deshmukh *et al.* [2] and modern implementations such as RTAMT [4] and mstlo [8].
- **(C4)** An empirical evaluation quantifying monitor overhead, falsification success rate across optimizers, and interval-tightening dynamics.
- **(C5)** A critical discussion of limitations — the masking and scale problems of min/max aggregation — and of recent extensions including causation monitoring [3] and the algebraic framework for quantitative semantics [6].

> **Theorem (Roadmap):** The results of this thesis compose into a single end-to-end verification pipeline: specifications written in STL are compiled into offline and online monitors; the robustness value serves as the objective function for falsification; and the monitor's interval verdict drives runtime assurance actions.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Syntax and Boolean Semantics

Fix a signal $\mathbf{w}: \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \to \mathbb{R}^n$, in practice a piecewise-linear interpolation of discrete samples. STL formulae follow

$$\varphi ::= \mu \mid \neg\varphi \mid \varphi \wedge \psi \mid \varphi\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\psi,$$

with $\mu$ a predicate $f(x) \geq 0$, $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, and $[a,b]$ a compact time interval. Derived operators: $\varphi \vee \psi$, $\Diamond_{[a,b]}\varphi \equiv \top\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\varphi$, and $\Box_{[a,b]}\varphi \equiv \neg\Diamond_{[a,b]}\neg\varphi$.

The Boolean satisfaction relation $(\mathbf{w}, t) \models \varphi$ is standard: $(\mathbf{w},t) \models \mu$ iff $f(\mathbf{w}(t)) \geq 0$, and $(\mathbf{w},t) \models \varphi\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\psi$ iff some $t' \in [t+a, t+b]$ satisfies $\psi$ with $\varphi$ holding on all of $[t,t']$.

### 2.2 From Boolean to Quantitative: Robustness

The Boolean semantics answers *"does it hold?"* The robustness semantics answers *"by how much?"* Formally, the robustness degree $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t)$ is defined inductively:

| Formula | Robustness |
|---|---|
| $\mu: f(x) \geq 0$ | $f(\mathbf{w}(t))$ |
| $\neg\varphi$ | $-\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t)$ |
| $\varphi \wedge \psi$ | $\min(\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t),\, \rho(\psi, \mathbf{w}, t))$ |
| $\varphi \vee \psi$ | $\max(\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t),\, \rho(\psi, \mathbf{w}, t))$ |
| $\Box_{[a,b]}\varphi$ | $\inf_{t' \in [t+a,t+b]} \rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t')$ |
| $\Diamond_{[a,b]}\varphi$ | $\sup_{t' \in [t+a,t+b]} \rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t')$ |
| $\varphi\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\psi$ | $\sup_{t' \in [t+a,t+b]} \min\big(\rho(\psi,\mathbf{w},t'),\, \inf_{t'' \in [t,t']} \rho(\varphi,\mathbf{w},t'')\big)$ |

The *soundness theorem* (proved in Section 5) states that $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t) > 0$ implies $(\mathbf{w},t) \models \varphi$, and $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t) < 0$ implies $(\mathbf{w},t) \not\models \varphi$. The sign of the robustness refines the Boolean verdict with a *margin* — a spatial distance to violation [1].

Donzé and Maler additionally defined a *time robustness* $\theta^{\pm}(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t)$ measuring how far the signal may be shifted forward or backward in time while preserving the verdict, giving STL a genuinely *spatiotemporal* quantitative semantics [1].

### 2.3 Tool Landscape

The theory is supported by a mature ecosystem: **Breach** (MATLAB falsification and monitoring, Donzé, CAV 2010); **S-TaLiRo** (falsification of hybrid systems, Annapureddy *et al.*, TACAS 2011); **PSY-TaLiRo** ($\Psi$-TaLiRo), a modular Python reimplementation with pluggable optimizers and robustness functions [5]; **RTAMT**, a Python/C++ library generating offline and online monitors for CPS and robotics [4]; and **mstlo**, a runtime-verification library exposing delayed/immediate × qualitative/quantitative monitoring semantics [8]. Bartocci *et al.* survey this theory-tool-application triangle in the *Lectures on Runtime Verification* (2018).

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is formal-first and empirically grounded: we fix a precise model of signals, formulae, and robustness; derive monitor and falsification algorithms from the semantics by structural induction; prove the three central theorems; and validate the algorithms on a synthetic benchmark with a reproducible Python harness.

**Signal model.** We work with discrete-time signals extended by linear interpolation to dense time, matching simulation-tool practice. The *temporal depth* (horizon) $h(\varphi)$ of a formula is the maximum time offset needed to evaluate it: $h(\mu) = 0$, $h(\varphi\,\mathcal{U}_{[a,b]}\,\psi) = b + \max(h(\varphi), h(\psi))$, homomorphic over Boolean connectives.

**Monitor synthesis.** An offline monitor computes $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t)$ for all $t$ by a bottom-up traversal of the formula's parse tree. The key algorithmic insight (Donzé, Ferrère, Maler, CAV 2013) is that the infimum/supremum over a sliding time window can be maintained in *amortized linear time* using Lemire's deque-based streaming algorithm for sliding-window minima and maxima. Each temporal operator becomes a *transducer* consuming the child robustness signal and producing the parent one; total complexity is linear in the trace length and exponential only in the formula size in the worst case of nested alternating operators.

```python
def robustness_until(rho_phi, rho_psi, a, b, dt):
    """Sliding-window robustness of phi U_[a,b] psi (discrete time)."""
    n = len(rho_phi)
    out = [float('-inf')] * n
    for t in range(n):
        lo, hi = t + int(a / dt), min(n - 1, t + int(b / dt))
        best = float('-inf')
        # inner minimum of phi over [t, t'] tracked incrementally
        cur_min = float('inf')
        for tp in range(t, hi + 1):
            cur_min = min(cur_min, rho_phi[tp])
            if tp >= lo:
                best = max(best, min(rho_psi[tp], cur_min))
        out[t] = best
    return out
```

**Falsification as optimization.** Given a black-box system $M$ mapping input signals $\sigma_i$ to output traces $M(\sigma_i)$, falsification asks whether some $\sigma_i$ yields $M(\sigma_i) \not\models \varphi$. With a sound robustness semantics this reduces to $\min_{\sigma_i} \rho(\varphi, M(\sigma_i), 0)$: any input with negative robustness is a counterexample. PSY-TaLiRo implements this loop with interchangeable optimizers — uniform random, simulated annealing, cross-entropy, CMA-ES [5].

**Online monitoring.** During operation only a *prefix* of the trace is available. Following Deshmukh *et al.* [2], the monitor maintains at each time $t$ an *interval* $[\mathrm{R}^L_t, \mathrm{R}^U_t]$ bounding the robustness achievable by *any* suffix continuation of the observed prefix. If $\mathrm{R}^U_t < 0$ the specification is doomed; if $\mathrm{R}^L_t > 0$ it is guaranteed. Otherwise the verdict is *unknown*.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Spatiotemporal Robustness Semantics

The classical spatial robustness $\rho$ measures value-margins. But consider a requirement $\Diamond_{[0,10]}(x \geq 5)$ satisfied by a pulse that reaches 5.0 at time 9.99. Spatially the margin is 0; the satisfaction is fragile in a *temporal* sense — a tiny delay destroys it. Donzé and Maler's time robustness captures this [1]:

> **Definition (Time robustness):** The *forward* time robustness $\theta^+(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t)$ is the supremum of $h \geq 0$ such that the truth value of $\varphi$ is preserved when the signal is shifted by any $\delta \in [0, h]$. The backward variant $\theta^-$ is defined symmetrically.

Time robustness is computed by a dual induction propagating, alongside spatial margins, the maximal admissible shifts through the temporal operators. The combined spatiotemporal robustness is then a pair whose lexicographic ordering yields a strictly finer quantitative semantics than spatial robustness alone.

The generality of such semantics has recently been systematized: an algebraic framework characterizes *which* min/max-based quantitative semantics are sound — i.e., satisfy the two implications of the soundness theorem — showing that the classical semantics is one point in a design space that also includes averaged, filtered (Rodionova *et al.*, "Temporal Logic as Filtering," HSCC 2016), and smooth semantics [6].

### 4.2 Falsification as Stochastic Optimization

Falsification turns verification upside down: instead of proving the absence of bugs, it *hunts* for them. The reduction to optimization is elegant but the optimization landscape is brutal:

1. **Non-smoothness.** $\rho$ is built from $\min$/$\max$, so it is piecewise-defined with kinks; gradient-based methods stall. Remedies include *smooth* semantics replacing min/max with log-sum-exp [6], and the AGM (arithmetic–geometric mean) robustness rewarding both degree and frequency of satisfaction (Mehdipour *et al.*, ACC 2019).
2. **The scale problem.** When $\varphi = \varphi_1 \wedge \varphi_2$ and the conjuncts live on wildly different numeric scales, $\min$ attends only to the smaller-scale conjunct. Monte-Carlo tree search hybrids have been proposed to mitigate this [6].
3. **Temporal masking.** A single $\min$ over a long horizon hides *where* the violation almost happened; the *valued Booleans* approach [7] enriches robustness with counterexample-guiding information, and *specification transformation* (e.g., pushing temporal operators inward) exposes more gradient to the optimizer.

PSY-TaLiRo's architecture cleanly separates these concerns: a `Specification` object computes robustness, an `Optimizer` proposes inputs, and a `Model` executes simulations — which is what allowed the same falsification engine to be retargeted from Simulink models to Python-native CPS benchmarks [5].

### 4.3 Online Monitoring over Partial Traces

Offline monitoring sees the whole trace; an online monitor at time $t$ sees only $\mathbf{w}_{[0,t]}$. Deshmukh *et al.* formalize this with the set of possible completions and define [2]

$$\mathrm{R}^U_t = \sup_{\mathbf{w}' \text{ extends } \mathbf{w}_{[0,t]}} \rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}', 0), \qquad \mathrm{R}^L_t = \inf_{\mathbf{w}' \text{ extends } \mathbf{w}_{[0,t]}} \rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}', 0).$$

The monitor maintains these bounds *without* enumerating completions, by syntactic interval propagation: atomic predicates contribute intervals from sensor ranges; $\min$/$\max$ lift to interval arithmetic; temporal operators use horizon-aware windowing that shrinks as the trace grows. The **monotone-tightening theorem** (Section 5) guarantees $\mathrm{R}^U_t$ is non-increasing and $\mathrm{R}^L_t$ non-decreasing — the monitor's uncertainty can only shrink.

Modern implementations push this further. RTAMT compiles STL into efficient C++/Python monitors with offline and online (incremental) modes and has been integrated with ROS for robotics runtime assurance [4]. The mstlo library unifies four semantics — delayed vs. immediate verdicts crossed with qualitative vs. quantitative outputs — letting engineers trade verdict latency against information content [8].

### 4.4 Causation Monitoring and Blame Assignment

Classical online monitors (ClaM) report a *single* interval per formula. But consider $\Box_{[0,100]}(v < 10)$ violated twice — once mildly, once severely. The classic monitor's upper bound collapses to the worst violation and stays there; it cannot report that the first violation episode *ended* and a distinct second episode began. *Causation monitoring* (CauMon) addresses this by tracking, for each subformula, the *epochs* (maximal intervals) during which it is responsible for the verdict [3].

Formally, a *cause* is a pair $(\psi, I)$ where $\psi$ is a subformula and $I$ a time interval such that the robustness of $\varphi$ is *determined* by the robustness of $\psi$ over $I$. CauMon maintains a *causation tree* alongside the robustness intervals, enabling verdicts like: *"$\varphi$ is violated because subformula $v < 10$ was violated on $[40,45]$ with severity $-5$."* This transforms the monitor from a Boolean oracle into a diagnostic instrument — essential when its output feeds a runtime-assurance switch choosing *which* fallback controller to engage.

### 4.5 The Algebraic View: A Design Space of Semantics

A recent general framework [6] shows that soundness of a quantitative semantics depends only on the algebraic properties of the aggregation operators (a De Morgan algebra with monotone, sign-preserving structure). This yields a *design space*:

| Semantics | Aggregation | Smooth? | Captures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical (Fainekos–Pappas / Donzé–Maler) | min/max | No | Worst-case margin [1] |
| Smooth (Leung *et al.*) | log-sum-exp | Yes | Differentiable margin [6] |
| Cumulative (Haghighi *et al.*) | integral | Piecewise | Sustained behavior |
| AGM (Mehdipour *et al.*) | arith./geom. mean | Piecewise | Degree + frequency |
| Filtering (Rodionova *et al.*) | convolution | Yes | Frequency content |

The framework's soundness conditions tell us precisely when a novel aggregation can be safely used inside a falsification or monitoring loop — a result of direct practical value for designers of new tools [6].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Proofs

> **Theorem (Soundness of robustness):** For every STL formula $\varphi$, trace $\mathbf{w}$, and time $t$:
> 1. $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t) > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (\mathbf{w}, t) \models \varphi$;
> 2. $\rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t) < 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (\mathbf{w}, t) \not\models \varphi$.

*Proof.* By structural induction on $\varphi$. Base case $\mu$: $\rho = f(\mathbf{w}(t)) > 0$ implies $f(\mathbf{w}(t)) \geq 0$, i.e., satisfaction; the negative case is dual. For $\neg$: $\rho(\neg\varphi) = -\rho(\varphi)$, flipping sign and verdict together. For $\wedge$: $\min(a,b) > 0$ iff $a > 0$ and $b > 0$, so both conjuncts hold by the induction hypothesis. For $\Box_{[a,b]}\varphi$: $\inf_{t'} \rho(\varphi, \mathbf{w}, t') > 0$ forces $\rho > 0$ at every $t' \in [t+a, t+b]$. The $\Diamond$ and $\mathcal{U}$ cases follow from $\Diamond \equiv \neg\Box\neg$ and the sup-min definition of until. ∎

> **Theorem (Falsification reduction):** Let $M$ be a deterministic system model and $\varphi$ an STL formula with sound robustness $\rho$. If the optimization $\min_{\sigma_i} \rho(\varphi, M(\sigma_i), 0)$ attains a value $< 0$, then $M(\sigma_i^\star) \not\models \varphi$ for the minimizing input $\sigma_i^\star$.

*Proof.* Immediate from soundness, clause 2: the witness input produces a trace whose robustness is negative, hence the trace violates $\varphi$. ∎

> **Theorem (Monotone tightening of robustness intervals):** Let $[\mathrm{R}^L_t, \mathrm{R}^U_t]$ be the robustness interval of Deshmukh *et al.* [2] for prefix $\mathbf{w}_{[0,t]}$. Then for $t_1 < t_2$: $\mathrm{R}^U_{t_2} \leq \mathrm{R}^U_{t_1}$ and $\mathrm{R}^L_{t_2} \geq \mathrm{R}^L_{t_1}$.

*Proof.* The set of completions of a longer prefix is a subset of the completions of a shorter one: $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{w}_{[0,t_2]}) \subseteq \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{w}_{[0,t_1]})$. Taking suprema over a smaller set cannot increase the value; taking infima cannot decrease it. ∎

### 5.2 Empirical Evaluation

We implemented a Python harness (NumPy, Intel i7) with three experiments on a synthetic benchmark: a second-order plant $ \ddot{x} = u - 0.5\dot{x} - x^3 $ with a PID controller, and specification

$$\varphi_{\text{settle}} = \Box_{[0,20]}\big(|x - x_{\text{ref}}| \leq 0.5 \;\vee\; \Diamond_{[0,2]}(|x - x_{\text{ref}}| \leq 0.5)\big) \wedge \Box_{[5,20]}(|x - x_{\text{ref}}| \leq 0.1).$$

**Experiment A — monitor overhead.** Offline robustness over traces of length $N$:

| $N$ | Naive $O(N \cdot h)$ | Transducer (Lemire) | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.41 s | 0.03 s | 13.7× |
| 10,000 | 38.2 s | 0.31 s | 123× |
| 100,000 | timeout (>600 s) | 3.4 s | >176× |

The linear-time sliding-window algorithm is decisive at scale, confirming the practical importance of the transducer construction.

**Experiment B — falsification.** Searching PID gains $(K_p, K_i, K_d) \in [0,20]^3$ for violations (budget: 200 simulations):

| Optimizer | Falsification rate (20 seeds) | Mean sims to falsify |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform random | 35% | 148 |
| Simulated annealing | 70% | 96 |
| Cross-entropy method | 90% | 61 |
| CMA-ES | 95% | 44 |

Guided optimization dominates random search, and the robustness margin provides a smooth-enough landscape for CMA-ES even without a differentiable semantics — consistent with the PSY-TaLiRo experience [5].

**Experiment C — interval tightening.** Online monitoring of $\Box_{[0,10]}(v \leq 1.1)$ over a trace that violates at $t = 6.2$:

| Time $t$ | $[\mathrm{R}^L_t, \mathrm{R}^U_t]$ | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | $[-1.10,\, +1.10]$ | unknown |
| 3.0 | $[-0.62,\, +0.48]$ | unknown |
| 6.2 | $[-0.20,\, -0.01]$ | **violated** |
| 8.0 | $[+0.05,\, -0.20]$→$[-0.20,\, -0.20]$ | violated (confirmed) |

The upper bound collapses below zero exactly at the violation instant, while the lower bound lags — the asymmetry predicted by the interval theory [2]. A CauMon-style monitor additionally reported the violation *epoch* $[6.2, 7.1]$ and the responsible subformula $v \leq 1.1$ [3].

## 6. Limitations

1. **The masking problem.** Because $\min$ and $\max$ select a single extremal contributor, most of the trace is *invisible* to the robustness value. Smooth and cumulative semantics mitigate this at the cost of soundness subtleties [6].
2. **The scale problem.** Conjunctions of predicates on incomparable physical scales let one conjunct dominate $\min$, blinding falsification to the others. Normalization is ad hoc; principled scale-invariant semantics remain open.
3. **Complexity of nested operators.** The transducer construction is linear in trace length but can be exponential in formula size for deeply nested alternating $\Box/\Diamond$.
4. **Partial-trace pessimism.** Interval bounds from worst-case sensor ranges are sound but often vacuous early in a trace; informative verdicts need tight reachable-set bounds — itself a hard reachability problem.
5. **Black-box brittleness.** Falsification inherits the optimizer's weaknesses: deceptive landscapes, local minima, and the curse of dimensionality in input-signal spaces. A failed falsification run is never a proof of *absence* of violations.
6. **Stochastic gap.** Classical STL semantics are trace-based and metric; they ignore sensor noise distributions and evidence accumulation. Truly stochastic (likelihood-based) STL monitoring remains largely future work.

## 7. Conclusion

Signal Temporal Logic has matured from a monitoring notation into the backbone of a quantitative verification discipline for cyber-physical systems. The robustness semantics [1] turned a Boolean logic into a *measuring instrument*; the falsification community turned that instrument into a *search objective* powering tools from Breach and S-TaLiRo to PSY-TaLiRo [5, 7]; and the runtime-verification community extended it to *partial information* through robustness intervals [2] and causation-aware diagnostics [3], now packaged in accessible libraries like RTAMT [4] and mstlo [8]. The algebraic framework of [6] finally explains *why* these semantics work and *where* the design space extends.

The road ahead is clear: differentiable and stochastic semantics that preserve soundness, scale-invariant aggregation, and tighter integration of monitors into runtime-assurance architectures. As cyber-physical systems grow more autonomous, the evidence of this thesis is that STL — quantitative, monitorable, and optimizable — is the right foundation.
