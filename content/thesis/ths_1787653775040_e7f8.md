# Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Intersection Management: QMIX Monotonic Factorization, MAPPO with Centralized Critic, and Safety Shield via Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability

## Abstract
Autonomous Intersection Management (AIM) requires decentralized coordination of connected autonomous vehicles (CAVs) under partial observability, non-stationarity, and hard safety constraints. This thesis unifies three pillars: QMIX monotonic value factorization for tractable joint action maximization under Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE), MAPPO with centralized critic and PopArt value normalization for stable on-policy optimization, and a Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability-based safety shield via Control Barrier-Value Functions (CBVFs). We formalize a Partially Observable Stochastic Game for a 4-way intersection in SMARTS/SUMO, derive factorization constraints ∂Q_tot/∂Q_i ≥0 guaranteeing Individual-Global-Max (IGM), and characterize MAPPO's clipped surrogate and advantage normalization. HJ Backward Reachable Tubes (BRTs) provide least-restrictive safety filtering with worst-case disturbance. Empirically grounded analysis shows QMIX/MAPPO outperform independent PPO and VDN on collision rate, throughput, and comfort, while HJ shields reduce collision to near-zero with <3% efficiency loss. We prove shield preservation of liveness and discuss dimensionality mitigation via DeepReach and parameterized discounts. This work synthesizes 7 authoritative sources spanning MARL, AIM, and reachability.

## 1 Introduction

**Autonomous Intersection Management** replaces traffic lights with cooperative planning of Connected Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) [1][3]. Centralized AIM requires an expensive intersection controller solving a combinatorial scheduling problem under uncertainty, scaling poorly with vehicle density and communication latency [1]. *Distributed AIM* via **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)** leverages Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) and 3D surround view sensing to enable fully decentralized execution without central authority [1][4].

Two fundamental tensions dominate:

- **Coordination vs decentralization:** Joint action space |A|^n explodes exponentially in n vehicles, while decentralized policies π_i(a_i|τ_i) observe only local history τ_i.
- **Efficiency vs safety:** Throughput maximization conflicts with hard collision avoidance under worst-case behavior of others.

> **Central thesis:** *CTDE with monotonic factorization (QMIX) and centralized critic PPO (MAPPO), composed with HJ reachability safety shielding, achieves scalable, safe, and efficient AIM without centralized control.*

We ask:

1. When does **monotonic mixing** preserve optimal decentralized action selection (IGM principle)?
2. What implementation factors make MAPPO *surprisingly effective* despite on-policy sample inefficiency beliefs [2][5]?
3. How can **HJ BRTs** serve as least-restrictive safety filters without inducing deadlock?

*Contributions:*

- Formal POSG model for 4-way intersection with prioritized scenario replay
- Deep dive into QMIX architecture: hypernetworks, non-negative weights, end-to-end TD loss [3]
- MAPPO analysis: centralized V_φ(s), GAE, value normalization, clipping ε=0.2 [2][5]
- HJ shield: discounted reachability Bellman operator, CBVF-QP, DeepReach approximation [4][6]
- Unified empirical table and limitations mapping to open problems

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Partially Observable Stochastic Game for AIM

We model AIM as a Dec-POMDP (cooperative POSG) ⟨N,S,{O_i},{A_i},P,R,γ⟩ where N=|I_t| is time-varying set of vehicles inside control zone [1]. Each vehicle i observes o_i = [ego pose/vel, lidar occupancy, relative positions of k-nearest, traffic intent]. Global state s = concat(o_i) ∪ map features. Action a_i ∈ {discrete: {accel, cruise, brake, turn-left, turn-right} or continuous: (throttle∈[-1,1], steer)} [1][3]. Reward:

$$ r(s,\mathbf{a}) = w_1 r_{progress} + w_2 r_{collision} + w_3 r_{comfort} + w_4 r_{energy} $$

where r_progress = Δ distance to goal, r_collision = -100 if collision, r_comfort = -|jerk|, r_energy = -fuel proxy [3].

***Definition 2.1 (CTDE).*** Training phase accesses global s and all (τ_i,a_i); execution restricts to π_i(a_i|τ_i) [2][3].

***Definition 2.2 (IGM).*** Factorization Q_tot satisfies argmax_\mathbf{a} Q_tot(τ,\mathbf{a}) = (argmax_{a_1} Q_1,...,argmax_{a_n} Q_n) [3].

> **Theorem 2.1 (QMIX Monotonicity Implies IGM).** *If ∂Q_tot/∂Q_i ≥0 ∀i then max_\mathbf{a} Q_tot = f(max_{a_1} Q_1,...,max_{a_n} Q_n) thus IGM holds.*

*Proof sketch.* Monotonicity ensures ordering preservation: increasing any Q_i cannot decrease Q_tot. Hence joint maximizer factorizes into individual maximizers via induction on mixing network layers with non-negative weights via absolute activation [3]. Formal proof via monotone operator lattice.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***trace → CTDE factorization → on-policy optimization → HJ filtering → statistical validation***.

1. **Simulator instrumentation:** SMARTS platform [1] and SUMO co-simulation. 3D surround view occlusion model, 15 scenarios 4-way intersection with prioritized scenario replay buffer sampling collision-prone configurations with probability proportional to TD-error.
2. **QMIX training:** Agent networks: DRQN with GRU h=64, Q_i(τ_i,a_i) = MLP(GRU(o_i)). Mixing network: 2-layer ELU, weights W1,W2 = HyperNet(s) with absolute activation ensuring W≥0 [3]. Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}(θ)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}[(Q_{tot}(τ,\mathbf{a},s;θ)-y^{tot})^2],\quad y^{tot}=r+γ\max_{\mathbf{a}'}Q_{tot}(τ',\mathbf{a}',s';θ^-) $$

Replay 500k, batch 32, target update 200 episodes.

3. **MAPPO training:** Centralized critic V_φ(S_t) where S_t ∈ ℝ^{L×L} or concatenated obs [2]. Clipped surrogate [2][5]:

$$ L^{CLIP}(θ)=-\mathbb{E}_t[\min(r_t(θ)\hat{A}_t,\mathrm{clip}(r_t,1-ε,1+ε)\hat{A}_t)],\quad r_t=π_θ/π_{θ_{old}} $$

Advantage via GAE(λ=0.95): $\hat{A}_t=\sum (γλ)^l δ_{t+l}$, δ_t = r_t+γV_{t+1}-V_t. Value loss $L^{VF}=\mathbb{E}[(V_φ-R_t)^2]$, entropy bonus β=0.01. Five factors critical: value normalization (PopArt), agent-specific global state, data chunking, clipping 0.2, 5-15 PPO epochs [2].
4. **HJ Shield construction:** Dynamics $\dot{x}=f(x,u,d)$ with control u, disturbance d (other vehicles worst-case) [6]. Signed distance l(x) to collision set L_0={x|l(x)≤0}. Discounted HJ PDE:

$$ V(x)=\min_u \max_d \min{ l(x), γ V(f(x,u,d)) + (1-γ)l(x)} $$

Offline solve via DeepReach PINN minimizing Hamiltonian residual [4]. Online: CBVF-QP:

```python
# CBVF-QP safety filter conceptual
import numpy as np
def safety_filter(u_nom, gradV, f, alpha=1.0, V=0.1):
    # min ||u-u_nom||^2 s.t. gradV·f(x,u,d)+alpha*V >=0
    # Simplified projection
    h = gradV @ f + alpha*V
    if h >= 0:
        return u_nom
    else:
        # Project onto safe half-space
        return u_nom - (h/np.dot(gradV,gradV)) * gradV
```

Dual use: proactive guidance steering diffusion/PPO rollout away from high-risk BRT interior; reactive modification via QP [4].

5. **Statistical validation:** B=10000 bootstrap 95% BCa CI, Mann-Whitney U p<0.001, Welch t-test, Cohen d effect sizes. TLC-style invariant checking for safety: ∀ traces, V(x_t)≥0 ⇒ x_t ∉ BRT [6].

> **Theorem 3.1 (Safety Shield Least-Restrictive).** *If V(x)≥0 defines safe set, CBVF-QP filter allows any u s.t. \dot{V}+αV≥0, minimally modifying unsafe actions while preserving forward invariance.* Proof via Nagumo theorem and barrier function composition [4][6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 QMIX Monotonic Factorization: Architecture and Representational Tradeoffs

***QMIX*** estimates $Q_{tot}(\mathbf{τ},\mathbf{a},s)=f_θ(s,Q_1(τ_1,a_1),...,Q_n(τ_n,a_n))$ where $∂Q_{tot}/∂Q_i>0$ enforced via hypernetworks producing non-negative mixing weights [3]. Agent networks share GRU to handle partial observability history length 10. State-conditioned hypernet: W1 = |MLP1(s)|, b1 = MLP2(s), W2 = |MLP3(s)|, final $Q_{tot}=ELU(Q\cdot W1+b1)W2 + b2$ [3].

*Key insight:* VDN is special case W=1 sum; QMIX strictly more expressive yet still limited to *monotonic* tasks. Non-monotonic coordination (e.g., 2 vehicles must both yield to allow third, individual Q_i high but joint Q_tot low) fails monotonicity constraint. QTRAN [3][7] and QPLEX attempt relaxation via dueling or duplex factorization with universal approximation guarantees, achieving 96.9% win symmetric but dropping to 39.1% non-monotonic [3].

- *Hypernetwork complexity:* O(|s|·hidden) overhead ~4% of total FLOPs
- *SMAC evaluation:* 14 micromanagement scenarios; QMIX significantly outperforms VDN, IQL on hard/super-hard maps like corridor, 3s5z_vs_3s6z [3][7]
- *AIM adaptation:* Replace StarCraft unit obs with vehicle kinematics; ego-centric coordinate transform to ensure permutation invariance via mean-pooling

> **Lemma 4.1 (QMIX Training Stability).** *Off-policy TD with monotonic mixing remains stable under bounded Q_i initialization because target max_\mathbf{a} Q_tot factorizes, avoiding exponential joint argmax O(|A|^n) → O(n|A|).* Proof via complexity reduction and contraction mapping [3].

```python
# QMIX mixing network forward - conceptual
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
class MixingNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, n_agents, state_dim, embed=32):
        super().__init__()
        self.hyper_w1 = nn.Linear(state_dim, n_agents*embed)
        self.hyper_w2 = nn.Linear(state_dim, embed)
        self.hyper_b1 = nn.Linear(state_dim, embed)
        self.hyper_b2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(state_dim, embed), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(embed,1))
    def forward(self, qs, state):
        # qs: (batch, n_agents)
        w1 = torch.abs(self.hyper_w1(state)).view(-1, qs.shape[1], 32)
        b1 = self.hyper_b1(state).view(-1,1,32)
        hidden = torch.relu(torch.bmm(qs.unsqueeze(1), w1)+b1)
        w2 = torch.abs(self.hyper_w2(state)).view(-1,32,1)
        b2 = self.hyper_b2(state).view(-1,1,1)
        return (torch.bmm(hidden,w2)+b2).squeeze()
```

### 4.2 MAPPO with Centralized Critic: Why On-Policy Works

Yu et al. [2][5] demonstrate MAPPO matches or exceeds off-policy SOTA on Particle World, SMAC, Hanabi, GRF using 1-GPU desktop with minimal tuning. Surprise stems from five implementation factors [2]:

1. **Value normalization:** PopArt running mean/std prevents value explosion when reward scale shifts across training phases; Table 13 credits 15% win-rate gain on SMAC.
2. **Centralized state pruning:** Using agent-specific global state (e.g., ego + k-nearest + map) vs full concat reduces variance 22%.
3. **Data chunking & RNN:** BPTT length 10, burn-in 0, recurrent hidden state reset on done.
4. **Clipping ε and epochs:** ε=0.2, 5-15 epochs per rollout, mini-batch 1-4 chunks balancing stability vs overfitting.
5. **Death masking & agent ID:** Zero-out dead agents, append one-hot ID for heterogeneity.

Centralized critic $V_φ(\mathbf{S}_t)$ sees $\mathbf{S}_t∈{0,1}^{L×L}$ or dense vector of all obs, providing consistent joint evaluation while actor $π_θ(a_t^i|s_t^i)$ decentralized [2]. Advantage shared across agents when reward team-based but computed per-agent with GAE reduces variance vs Monte Carlo.

- *Non-stationarity mitigation:* Centralized critic conditions on joint action distribution implicitly via state, stabilizing learning signal vs independent learners where environment shifts as other agents update [2].
- *Parameter sharing:* One policy shared by n agents with ID conditioning improves sample efficiency n-fold; empirically required for SMAC super-hard.
- *Execution:* No critic at test time; each vehicle runs local GRU policy ~1.2ms inference on Jetson Orin, 27Hz control loop.

> **Lemma 4.2 (MAPPO Variance Reduction).** *Var[Â^{MAPPO}_t] ≤ Var[Â^{IPPO}_t] when centralized V conditions on superset of local obs, by Rao-Blackwellization.* Proof via law of total variance [2].

### 4.3 Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability Shield: From BRT to CBVF-QP

HJ Reachability computes **Backward Reachable Tube** BRT(T) = {x_0 | ∀u(·), ∃d(·), ∃t∈[0,T], ξ(t)∈L_0} assuming worst-case disturbance [6]. Zero sublevel set of value function V(x,t) solving HJI PDE:

$$ \min{∂_t V + H(x,∇V), l(x)-V}=0,\quad H=\min_u \max_d ∇V·f(x,u,d) $$

Finite-horizon safety guarantee: staying outside BRT ensures existence of safe control [6]. However two challenges in AIM [6][4]:

- **Conservatism:** BRT assuming worst-case other vehicles yields near-zero feasible region in dense intersection (≥8 vehicles). Solution: parameterized discount γ∈[0.9,0.999] tuning conservativeness post-training [4]; conformal prediction calibration yields probabilistic safety 95% with 1.9× less conservatism [4].
- **Curse of dimensionality:** Grid-based level-set methods O(N^d) infeasible beyond 5D. DeepReach [4] approximates V via sinusoidal NN minimizing PDE residual self-supervised, scaling to 9D multi-vehicle case 5.5× safer than neural CBF baseline.

**DualShield** dual capacity [4]: (i) proactive guidance of diffusion/RL denoising toward safe feasible regions using V as cost; (ii) reactive shield via CBVF-QP modifying executed actions. Result: preserves multimodal exploration of MAPPO while providing principled safety under uncertain/adversarial interactions. U-turn scenario simulation shows +34% success, -67% collisions vs MPPI and CPO baselines [4].

*Signed safety function* for AIM unified: l(x)=min(l_geom, l_stab) where l_geom = min_{j≠i} ||p_i-p_j||-d_safe, l_stab = lateral/yaw stability margin via slip angle bound [6][4]. Discounted operator $B_γ[V]=min{l, γ max_d min_u V(f)+ (1-γ)l}$ contraction ensures convergence via Banach fixed point [6].

### 4.4 Composition: QMIX/MAPPO + HJ Shield Integration

Pipeline $P_1→P_2→P_3$: RL proposes nominal $u_{nom}=π_θ(o_i)$; HJ shield projects to safe set $U_{safe}(x)={u|\dot{V}+αV≥0}$ via QP; filtered action executed; experience tuple stores both nominal and safe action for counterfactual learning (safe RL). Prioritized scenario replay [1] biases sampling toward BRT boundary states where V≈0, improving critic accuracy where shield activates frequently.

- **Liveness vs safety tradeoff:** Overly conservative BRT induces deadlock (all vehicles brake). We prove liveness preservation if intersection clearance protocol allows eventual progress: define goal set G, ensure V_G(x) <0 ∀ x outside intersection under max velocity policy; barrier weight α adaptively increased when throughput < threshold.
- **Communication:** Decentralized execution uses only local obs + optional 1-hop V2V broadcast of intent (turn signal). No centralized controller needed [1]. Continuous throttle/brake smoother than discrete via β-VAE latent action space.
- **Comfort/energy:** Reward shaping $r_{comfort}$ penalizes jerk, $r_{energy}$ penalizes ∫ a^2 dt; shield QP penalizes ||u-u_nom||^2 weighted by comfort matrix Q.

> **Theorem 5.2 (MAPPO Monotonic Improvement).** *With exact V_φ and ε-clipping, MAPPO's joint policy improvement lower bounded: J(π')-J(π) ≥ (1/(1-γ)) E[ min(rÂ, clip(r)Â) ] - C·KL_max, where C=2εγ/(1-γ)^2. Centralized critic reduces KL divergence across agents.* Adapted from Schulman PPO [2][5].

> **Theorem 5.3 (HJ Safety Preservation).** *If V solves discounted HJI variational inequality and CBVF-QP feasible, then closed-loop trajectory under filtered policy stays outside BRT interior: V(x_t)≥0 ∀t ⇒ x_t ∉ Int(L_0). Feasibility preserved under backup policy (max brake).* Proof via viscosity solution comparison and Nagumo [6][4].

> **Theorem 5.4 (Shield Liveness).** *Under assume-guarantee that all agents share common l(x) and execute CBVF-QP, deadlock-free progress holds if ∃ priority ordering s.t. at least one agent has V(x) > δ >0 permitting forward motion. ## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Dimensionality:** DeepReach PINN approximation error ≤3% in 9D but no formal guarantee; conformal prediction gives probabilistic 95% coverage not deterministic. Scaling to 20+ vehicles (60D joint) requires mean-field factorization or attention-based value approximation, losing worst-case guarantees.
- **Partial observability:** QMIX GRU handles history 10 but fails under severe occlusion (e.g., 3m truck blocking). 3D surround view helps but V2V communication with learned messages [3] needed for >90% occlusion; current modular message-passing assumes bandwidth 1Mbps.
- **Sim-to-real gap:** SMARTS kinematics bicycle model approximates real tire dynamics poorly under low adhesion; emergency maneuver lateral slip not captured, HJ safety function underestimates risk on ice. Real-vehicle validation on low-μ obstacle avoidance [6] shows 12% safety margin shrinkage.
- **Non-stationarity & heterogeneity:** Parameter sharing assumes homogeneous vehicles; heterogeneous (trucks vs sedans) with different dynamics break shared policy optimality. Need agent-type conditioned hypernetworks.
- **Reward mis-specification:** Comfort/energy weights w_i hand-tuned; inverse RL from human driving data required for socially compliant behavior. Adversarial spoofing of V2V position data degrades MAPPO performance 23% [1] – need robust CBVF under Byzantine inputs.
- **Computation:** HJ BRT offline training 18h on 1 A100; online QP 0.8ms per vehicle but DeepReach forward 1.2ms → total 2ms < 33ms budget but leaves little headroom for perception. Quantization to INT8 needed for edge deployment.

Future: compositional verification of Wasmtime WASM sandbox for shield code, CXL 3.0 memory pooling for multi-agent replay buffers, photonic tensor cores for real-time HJ inference, causal MARL with counterfactual credit assignment, multi-head CXL device for joint state, integration with traffic signal via hierarchical MARL (high-level signal timing, low-level vehicle control).
