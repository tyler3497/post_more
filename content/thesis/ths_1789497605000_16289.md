# Counting Nodal Plane Curves via Tropical Geometry: Amoebas, the Correspondence Theorem, and Node Polynomials

## Abstract

Tropical geometry converts the hard analytic problem of counting algebraic plane curves into a combinatorial problem about piecewise-linear graphs. This article develops the full arc from the complex-analytic origin of the subject — amoebas of plane curves and their Hausdorff limits — through Grigory Mikhalkin's 2005 correspondence theorem, which identifies the classical Gromov–Witten numbers of the projective plane with tropical counts weighted by vertex multiplicities, to the floor-diagram calculus of Brugallé and Mikhalkin that reduces Severi degrees to the enumeration of weighted oriented graphs. We explain the tropical semiring and dual subdivisions of Newton polygons, the balancing condition and the notion of tropical degree and genus, the stretching technique that makes point conditions tractable, and the lattice-path formula. We derive Kontsevich's recurrence computationally, exhibit the node polynomials of Göttsche, Di Francesco–Itzykson, Fomin–Mikhalkin and Block, and survey the parallel real (Welschinger), refined (Block–Göttsche), and quadratically enriched counts. Limitations — superabundant curves, non-toric ambient varieties, and approximation obstructions — are treated honestly.

##1 Intro

How many rational plane curves of degree d pass through 3d − 1 generic points? The answer N_d — the genus-zero Gromov–Witten invariant of the plane — begins 1, 1, 12, 620, 87304, 26312976, governed by Kontsevich's recurrence [9]. The Severi degree N^{d,δ} counts degree-d curves with δ nodes through the appropriate number of points; Di Francesco and Itzykson conjectured it is a degree-2δ polynomial in d for fixed δ and large d [8].

Until the early 2000s these numbers came from intersection theory on moduli spaces of stable maps. Mikhalkin's "Enumerative tropical algebraic geometry in R²" (*J. Amer. Math. Soc.*, 2005) [1] proposed a radical alternative: replace holomorphic curves by **piecewise-linear graphs**, the complex torus by R², and the count by a finite enumeration weighting each graph by the product of integer **multiplicities**. The resulting equality — the *correspondence theorem* — was conjectured by Kontsevich and proved by Mikhalkin [1].

The route to this theorem passes through **amoebas** — images of complex curves under the logarithmic modulus map, introduced by Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevinsky [11] and developed by Mikhalkin and Rullgård [10]. Rescaling the logarithm collapses amoebas in the Hausdorff metric onto piecewise-linear graphs: tropical geometry is the *skeleton* left when the complex structure is degenerated as far as it will go.

We develop the subject in full: the tropical semiring and tropical curves, the passage from a complex enumerative problem to a graph count, Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem with its multiplicity formula, and the **floor-diagram** calculus of Brugallé–Mikhalkin [3] and Fomin–Mikhalkin [4] that proved the Di Francesco–Itzykson conjecture. We close with the real and refined theories (Welschinger, Block–Göttsche, quadratically enriched counts) and an honest account of the method's limits.

> **Theorem (Mikhalkin [1], informal):** The number N_{d,g} of irreducible complex plane curves of degree d and geometric genus g through 3d + g − 1 generic points equals the number N^trop_{d,g} of irreducible tropical plane curves of the same degree and genus through 3d + g − 1 generic points, counted with multiplicity.

##2 Background

### The tropical semiring

On T = R ∪ {−∞}, **tropical addition** x ⊕ y = min(x, y) and **tropical multiplication** x ⊙ y = x + y make (T, ⊕, ⊙) a commutative semiring — the **min-plus** or **tropical semiring** — with additive identity −∞ and multiplicative identity 0. (The max-plus convention is equivalent via negation; we fix min-plus.) A tropical polynomial in n variables,

> f(x) = ⊕_α (c_α ⊙ x^{⊙α}) = min_α (c_α + ⟨α, x⟩),

is a piecewise-linear concave function R^n → R, a minimum of finitely many affine functions. Its **tropical hypersurface** V(f) is the *corner locus*: the set of points where the minimum is attained at least twice, i.e. where f fails to be smooth. In the plane, V(f) is a weighted, balanced, piecewise-linear graph.

The passage from classical to tropical is the **Maslov dequantization**: for t > 1, conjugate ordinary addition and multiplication by the map x ↦ log_t(x); as t → ∞, these operations converge to ⊕ and ⊙. Nothing here is merely formal: the same limit, applied to the modulus map z ↦ log|z| / log t on (C*)^n, sends complex hypersurfaces to tropical hypersurfaces in a precise Hausdorff sense (§4.1).

### Newton polytopes and dual subdivisions

For f(z) = Σ_{α ∈ A} c_α z^α, the **Newton polytope** Δ(f) = conv(A) records the monomials present. Coefficient valuations ν(α) = −val(c_α) lift A to (α, ν(α)); projecting the upper convex hull down yields a **regular subdivision** of Δ(f), dual to the tropical hypersurface:

- a 2-dimensional cell (a lattice polygon) ↔ a vertex of the tropical curve,
- an edge of the subdivision ↔ an edge of the tropical curve, perpendicular to it, with weight equal to the lattice length of the dual edge,
- a vertex of the subdivision ↔ a connected component of the complement (a "chamber").

This is the **duality theorem** of tropical geometry. The fundamental theorem states that the valuation image of the variety, the corner locus of the tropicalized polynomial, and the intersection of tropical hypersurfaces of all polynomials in the ideal all coincide [6].

### Tropical curves

An (abstract) **tropical curve** is a finite connected metric graph Γ with 1-valent vertices removed; a **parameterized plane tropical curve** is a map h: Γ → R², affine-linear with integer slope on each edge. Writing the outgoing primitive integer direction of an edge e at a vertex v as u_{v,e} and its weight as w(e), the **balancing condition** requires

> Σ_e w(e) · u_{v,e} = 0 at every vertex v.

Balancing is the combinatorial shadow of the residue theorem: a holomorphic map has no boundary. The unbounded edges (ends) correspond to amoeba tentacles, with directions given by the outward normals of the Newton polygon. A plane tropical curve has **degree d** when its Newton polygon is the triangle T_d with vertices (0,0), (d,0), (0,d): equivalently, the total weight of ends in each of the three directions (−1,0), (0,−1), (1,1) equals d. Its **genus** is the first Betti number g = b₁(Γ). A curve is **simple** if every vertex is 3-valent and the dual subdivision is a triangulation; every vertex then carries a **multiplicity** equal to twice the Euclidean area of its dual lattice triangle, and the multiplicity of the curve is the product of the vertex multiplicities:

> mult(Γ) = ∏_v 2·Area(Δ_v).

Tropical curves of fixed degree and genus form a polyhedral complex — the moduli space M^trop_{g,n}(R², d) — and point conditions cut it to a finite set, which is what makes enumeration possible [2][6].

##3 Methodology

The tropical attack on curve counting proceeds in five stages, each with a classical analytic counterpart and a tropical combinatorial replacement.

**1. Logarithmic degeneration.** For t > 1, Log_t(z) = (log|z₁|/log t, log|z₂|/log t) sends a complex curve C ⊂ (C*)² to its rescaled **amoeba**; as t → ∞ these converge in the Hausdorff metric to the tropical curve of the tropicalized polynomial — the *tropical limit* [10][1].

**2. Non-Archimedean tropicalization.** Over the Puiseux series field K, the valuation map val: (K*)² → R² sends an algebraic curve to a set whose closure is a tropical curve — its **tropicalization** Trop(C), the corner locus predicted by the valuations of the coefficients [6].

**3. Combinatorial patchworking.** Conversely, every approximable tropical curve is the tropicalization of an actual algebraic curve: Viro's **patchworking** builds, from a regular triangulation of the Newton polygon plus sign choices, an algebraic curve with amoeba arbitrarily close to any prescribed tropical curve [7]. Mikhalkin's proof is a two-way patchworking: curves through stretched points tropicalize to tropical curves through the stretched points, and every such tropical curve lifts.

**4. Stretching the point conditions.** Place the 3d + g − 1 points in **horizontally stretched position** — x-coordinates growing super-exponentially in the y-coordinates. Every tropical curve through them is then *floor-decomposed* into horizontal strips ("floors") joined by vertical "elevators," one marked point per floor; the global count factors into local problems — the origin of the floor-diagram calculus [3][4].

**5. Multiplicity-weighted counting.** The tropical count is Σ_Γ mult(Γ) over tropical curves through the points (for non-simple curves, mult is a lattice-index multiplicity from the dual subdivision); the correspondence theorem asserts this equals the classical number [1].

The table below organizes the principal invariants and their tropical avatars.

| Invariant | Classical meaning | Tropical computation |
|---|---|---|
| N_{d,g} (Gromov–Witten) | Irreducible degree-d, genus-g curves through 3d+g−1 points | Σ over tropical curves of mult(Γ) [1] |
| N^{d,δ} (Severi degree) | Degree-d curves with δ nodes through (d(d+3))/2 − δ points | Σ over (possibly reducible) tropical curves with δ nodes of mult(Γ) [2] |
| W_d (Welschinger) | Real rational curves through 3d−1 real points, signed by (−1)^{solitary nodes} | Σ over tropical curves of (−1)^{m(Γ)}·mult_R(Γ) [7] |
| BG_{d,g}(q) (Block–Göttsche) | q-refined count interpolating complex and real | Σ over tropical curves of ∏_v [mult(v)]_q, the quantum integer [5] |

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Amoebas and the Tropical Limit

For f(z₁, z₂) = Σ c_{ij} z₁^i z₂^j, the **amoeba** A(f) = Log(V(f)) ⊂ R² has finitely many tentacles; its complement's convex components correspond via the **Ronkin function**

> N_f(x) = (1/(2πi)²) ∫_{Log^{−1}(x)} log|f(z)| (dz₁/z₁) ∧ (dz₂/z₂),

whose gradient is constant on each complementary component and takes values in Δ(f) ∩ Z². The amoeba's **spine** — the corner locus of the piecewise-linear extension of N_f — is already a tropical curve [10].

Rescaling, A_t(f) = Log_t(V(f)) with Log_t = Log / log t: as t → ∞, Mikhalkin's limit theorem says A_t(f) converges in the Hausdorff metric to the tropical curve V(trop(f)), where trop(f) = min_{ij}(val(c_{ij}) + ix + jy) [1][10]. Tentacle slopes are the primitive outward normals of the Newton polygon's edges, and tentacle widths encode the tropical weights of the ends. In short: **the tropical curve is what remains after the complex structure is stretched to infinity**, and the amoeba is the finite-stretch intermediary.

Mikhalkin's **approximation theorem** reverses the limit: every *approximable* tropical curve — a condition automatic for simple curves — is the Hausdorff limit of rescaled amoebas of genuine complex curves, via Viro's patchworking [7].

### 4.2 The Correspondence Theorem and Multiplicities

Fix d ≥ 1, g ≥ 0 and a generic configuration P of 3d+g−1 points in R²; let T_{d,g}(P) be the irreducible parameterized plane tropical curves of degree d and genus g through P.

> **Theorem (Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem [1]):** For generic P, the set T_{d,g}(P) is finite, every Γ ∈ T_{d,g}(P) is simple, and
>
> N_{d,g} = Σ_{Γ ∈ T_{d,g}(P)} mult(Γ),
>
> where N_{d,g} is the classical number of irreducible complex plane curves of degree d and geometric genus g through 3d + g − 1 generic points, and mult(Γ) = ∏_v mult(v) is the product of the vertex multiplicities mult(v) = 2·Area(Δ_v) over the trivalent vertices of Γ.

The proof is two-sided. **Algebraic → tropical:** the finitely many complex curves through points in (C*)² lifting P have rescaled amoebas converging to tropical curves through P, shown simple and irreducible of the right genus by a Riemann–Hurwitz dimension count. **Tropical → algebraic:** each simple tropical curve Γ through P lifts to exactly mult(Γ) complex curves, built by patchworking — the multiplicity counts compatible choices of complex phases on the incident edges at each vertex [1].

For the **Severi degrees** N^{d,δ}, reducible curves are allowed: the tropical count runs over all tropical curves of degree d with δ nodes (4-valent vertices with a prescribed local model) through (d(d+3))/2 − δ points, weighted by multiplicity [2]. Mikhalkin's lattice-path formula — a sum over lattice paths in the Newton triangle encoding regular subdivisions — was the first fully effective version [1].

The multiplicity is no fudge factor: at a trivalent vertex it equals |det(w₁u₁, w₂u₂)| — simultaneously twice the dual triangle's area, the number of patchworking phase choices, and a non-Archimedean intersection multiplicity. The coincidence of these three integers is the engine of the theory.

### 4.3 Floor Diagrams and Node Polynomials

Horizontally stretched point configurations force every tropical curve through them into **floor-decomposed** form: d horizontal **floors** (indexed 1 through d) connected by vertical **elevators** of integer weight [3]. Erasing the geometry leaves a **floor diagram**: an oriented weighted graph on vertices {1, …, d}, edges directed upward with positive integer weights, satisfying a divergence condition at each vertex encoding the balancing. Its multiplicity is ∏_e w(e)² — the square because each elevator contributes at both endpoints [3][4].

> **Theorem (Brugallé–Mikhalkin [3]):** The Gromov–Witten invariant N_{d,g} equals the number of (possibly disconnected, appropriately marked) floor diagrams of degree d and genus g, counted with multiplicity ∏_e w(e)².

This made the theory computational: floor diagrams are finite objects a computer can enumerate. Fomin and Mikhalkin introduced **labeled** floor diagrams — vertex bijections with {1, …, d} compatible with the orientation — equinumerous with labeled trees in genus zero, hence counted by Cayley's formula d^{d−2}: the Kontsevich numbers acquire a tree-theoretic interpretation [4].

The deepest application was the **Di Francesco–Itzykson conjecture** (1994): for fixed δ, N^{d,δ} is a polynomial P_δ(d) of degree 2δ for large d [8]. Fomin and Mikhalkin proved this by decomposing floor diagrams of fixed cogenus δ into finitely many **templates** with polynomial contributions [4]; Block implemented the enumeration and computed P_δ through δ = 14, confirming Göttsche's conjectured optimal threshold there [8]. The base case, known to Steiner in 1848, is P_1(d) = 3(d−1)² [8].

Göttsche's conjecture (proved by Tzeng and by Kool–Shende–Thomas) embeds the node polynomials in a universal formula for Severi degrees of arbitrary smooth surfaces, with quasi-modular generating function [8]; the tropical proof of polynomiality was the first and remains the most elementary.

### 4.4 Real, Refined, and Quadratically Enriched Counts

A **Welschinger invariant** W_d counts real rational plane curves through 3d − 1 real points with sign (−1)^s, s the number of solitary real nodes; unlike naive real counts it is configuration-independent [7]. Shustin and Mikhalkin showed W_d equals a tropical sum over the *same* graphs as the complex count, with sign (−1) to a combinatorial exponent times a real multiplicity [7]; Brugallé–Mikhalkin floor diagrams handle conjugate point pairs too [3].

Block and Göttsche introduced a **refined** count: replace each vertex multiplicity m by the *quantum integer*

> [m]_q = (q^{m/2} − q^{−m/2}) / (q^{1/2} − q^{−1/2}) = q^{(m−1)/2} + q^{(m−3)/2} + ⋯ + q^{−(m−1)/2},

with BG_{d,g}(q) = Σ_Γ ∏_v [mult(v)]_q. Then BG_{d,g}(1) = N_{d,g} and BG_{d,g}(−1) = W_{d,g}; the coefficients have no known enumerative interpretation, but Brugallé and Jaramillo Puentes proved they share the node polynomials' polynomiality via floor diagrams [5]. The **quadratically enriched** program unifies real and complex counts over arbitrary fields with Grothendieck–Witt-valued weights — and tropical methods compute those too [12].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### Computing the Kontsevich numbers

Kontsevich's recurrence determines N_d from N_1 = 1 via

> N_d = Σ_{d₁+d₂=d} N_{d₁}N_{d₂} (d₁²d₂²·C(3d−4, 3d₁−2) − d₁³d₂·C(3d−4, 3d₁−1)),

and the following Python implementation reproduces the known values:

```python
from math import comb

def kontsevich_numbers(n: int) -> dict[int, int]:
    """Kontsevich's recurrence for the plane Gromov-Witten invariants N_d."""
    N = {1: 1}
    for d in range(2, n + 1):
        total = 0
        for d1 in range(1, d):
            d2 = d - d1
            term = (d1**2 * d2**2 * comb(3*d - 4, 3*d1 - 2)
                    - d1**3 * d2 * comb(3*d - 4, 3*d1 - 1))
            total += N[d1] * N[d2] * term
        N[d] = total
    return N

for d, n in kontsevich_numbers(7).items():
    print(f"N_{d} = {n}")
# Output (verified by execution): 1, 1, 12, 620, 87304, 26312976, 14616808192
```

N_3 = 12 is the classical benchmark: any correct tropical enumerator, summing vertex multiplicities over tropical cubics through eight stretched points, must return exactly 12 [1][9].

### The tropical semiring in code

The min-plus algebra in Haskell, with tropical polynomials as minima of affine functions:

```haskell
newtype Trop = Trop Double deriving (Eq, Show)   -- min-plus semiring carrier

instance Semigroup Trop where
  Trop x <> Trop y = Trop (min x y)              -- tropical addition

instance Monoid Trop where
  mempty = Trop (1/0)                            -- additive identity

tropMul :: Trop -> Trop -> Trop                  -- tropical multiplication
tropMul (Trop x) (Trop y) = Trop (x + y)

tropPoly :: [(Double, (Int, Int))] -> (Double, Double) -> Trop
tropPoly terms pt = foldr1 (<>)                  -- min over monomials;
  [Trop (c + fromIntegral a * x + fromIntegral b * y)  -- corner locus =
   | (c, (a, b)) <- terms, let (x, y) = pt]      -- the tropical curve
```

### Vertex multiplicities

Vertex multiplicity is the lattice-area determinant |det(w₁u₁, w₂u₂)| — simultaneously twice the dual triangle's area and the number of patchworking phase choices at the vertex.

### Severi degrees and the node polynomial

For nodal counts, the benchmark is Steiner's 1848 formula N^{d,1} = 3(d−1)², the number of 1-nodal degree-d curves through d(d+3)/2 − 1 points. The tropical proof classifies the finitely many cogenus-1 templates — an elevator of weight 2, a "double floor," and so on — whose polynomial contributions sum to 3(d−1)² [4][8]. Block's template machine produced the node polynomials through δ = 14 and verified Göttsche's optimal threshold d ≥ δ + 2 there [8].

### A worked tropical count: cubics

In stretched position the eight constraining points for cubics lie on three floors; the tropical rational cubics through them are indexed by floor diagrams on vertices {1, 2, 3}, and Fomin–Mikhalkin's labeled enumeration returns exactly 12 [4]. The tropical proofs are *bijective*: they exhibit the twelve objects, not merely the number twelve.

##6 Limitations

Tropical enumerative geometry is powerful within its natural domain and misleading outside it; the boundaries are worth stating precisely.

**Superabundance and approximability.** Not every balanced graph lifts to an algebraic curve: a *superabundant* tropical curve has actual deformation dimension below the naive graph count, and Speyer's **well-spacedness** plus Brugallé–Shaw intersection-theoretic obstructions characterize which curves are approximable by amoebas [1][3]. The correspondence theorem sidesteps this — curves through generic stretched points are automatically simple, hence approximable — but tropical counts through non-generic configurations may include phantom curves with no algebraic meaning.

**Beyond toric surfaces.** The clean equality N_{d,g} = N^trop_{d,g} needs a toric ambient variety: amoebas live on the dense torus (C*)² and the toric boundary controls the ends. Beyond the toric world the correct framework is **logarithmic** Gromov–Witten theory (Nishinou–Siebert, Gross–Siebert, Abramovich–Chen), where tropical curves are dual intersection complexes of degenerate targets [2][12].

**Real and refined subtleties.** Tropical Welschinger counts reproduce W_d only in genus zero; in positive genus the real algebraic count is not even deformation-invariant, while the tropical one is — the theories genuinely diverge [7]. The coefficients of the Block–Göttsche q-polynomials have no known enumerative interpretation [5].

**Computational complexity.** Floor-diagram enumeration grows roughly like the Gromov–Witten numbers themselves (super-exponential in d); Block's computation through δ = 14 already needed careful template pruning [8]. Tropical geometry reduces enumerative geometry to combinatorics, not to *easy* combinatorics.

##7 Conclusion

From amoeba to node polynomial: the tentacled amoebas of Gelfand–Kapranov–Zelevinsky [10][11], the Hausdorff limit onto balanced graphs, Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem [1], the computational floor diagrams [3][4], and the node polynomials with the tropical Welschinger and Block–Göttsche theories [5][7][8].

The enduring lesson is that **degeneration is information**: pushing the complex structure to its limit discards the analytic data that made the problem hard while retaining the combinatorial data that determines the answer — the multiplicities being the residue of the discarded analysis, compressed into integers.

Open directions abound: quadratically enriched counts promise unified enumerative geometry over arbitrary fields [12]; the Gross–Siebert program rebuilds mirror symmetry on tropical foundations via scattering diagrams [2]; and the approximability problem — which tropical curves lift — remains the fundamental interface question. Amoebas hinted that complex curves have skeletons; tropical geometry reads the flesh from the bones.
