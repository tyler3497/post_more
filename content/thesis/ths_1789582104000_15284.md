# Minimax Theory in Nonparametric Statistics: Lower Bounds via Fano's Method and Assouad's Lemma

## Abstract

Minimax lower bounds certify that no estimator can outperform a fundamental statistical limit, and in nonparametric estimation they are the principal instrument for proving that a procedure is rate-optimal. This thesis develops the two dominant multi-hypothesis techniques for deriving such bounds. **Fano's method** reduces estimation to multiple hypothesis testing and controls the testing error through mutual information, converting the geometric separation of hypotheses into a concrete risk floor. **Assouad's lemma** instead decomposes the minimax risk across a binary hypercube into a sum of per-coordinate binary testing errors, each bounded by the total variation affinity of neighboring distributions. We present both tools with full rigor — from the information-theoretic Fano inequality through Gilbert–Varshamov packings to the classical divergence inequalities — and apply them to density estimation and Hölder regression, recovering the canonical *n^{-2β/(2β+1)}* rates. We close with the limitations of these methods: non-sharp constants, adaptivity gaps, and modern *f*-divergence generalizations.

##1 Intro

Statistical estimation has two complementary directions. **Upper bounds** are proved by exhibiting a concrete estimator and analyzing its risk; they are existence claims, witnessed by a single procedure. **Lower bounds** are universal claims: they assert that *every* measurable estimator must incur at least a certain risk on at least one member of the model class. The asymmetry is fundamental and is what makes lower bounds technically demanding — one must argue against an uncountable adversary of procedures rather than for a single one [1].

The minimax risk formalizes this adversarial view. For a model *{P_θ : θ ∈ Θ}*, a sample *X* of size *n*, a loss *ℓ*, and a semidistance *d* on Θ,

> **Definition (minimax risk).** *R_n^*(Θ) = inf_{θ̂} sup_{θ ∈ Θ} E_θ ℓ(θ, θ̂),*

where the infimum runs over all estimators *θ̂ = θ̂(X)* and *E_θ* denotes expectation under *P_θ^{⊗n}*. Originating in Wald's decision theory, the minimax criterion asks for the best worst-case performance, and a lower bound on *R_n^** is a certificate of *impossibility*: no procedure, however clever, can beat it [4].

In **nonparametric** problems the parameter space Θ is infinite-dimensional — a class of densities, regression functions, or conditional distributions — and the minimax rate is typically slower than the parametric *1/n*. The classical Hölder regression rate *n^{-2β/(2β+1)}* for smoothness β is the canonical example: it is proved optimal by matching an upper bound (kernel or wavelet estimators) against a lower bound derived from the methods of this thesis [1].

This thesis develops the two dominant **multi-hypothesis** machines for minimax lower bounds:

1. **Fano's method** — an information-theoretic reduction of estimation to *M*-ary hypothesis testing, where the error probability is controlled by mutual information and the Kullback–Leibler divergence [2].
2. **Assouad's lemma** — a combinatorial reduction to *m* coupled *binary* testing problems arranged on a hypercube, where the risk decomposes along coordinates and is controlled by total variation distances between neighbors [4].

The roadmap is as follows. §2 fixes notation and the divergence toolkit. §3 presents both methods as recipes with a comparative table. §4 goes deep: the information-theoretic core of Fano, packing constructions via Gilbert–Varshamov, the full proof architecture of Assouad, and the modern *f*-divergence unification. §5 works through the Hölder regression rate in both frameworks with computational illustrations. §6 discusses limitations, and §7 concludes with guidance on choosing the right tool.

##2 Background

**The statistical decision framework.**

We observe *X = (X_1, …, X_n)* drawn i.i.d. from an unknown *P_θ*, *θ ∈ Θ*. An estimator is any measurable map *θ̂ : X → Θ*. Performance is measured by expected loss, and we focus on losses of the form *ℓ(θ, θ̂) = Φ(d(θ, θ̂))* with *Φ* nondecreasing, typically *Φ(u) = u²* with *d* a metric or semidistance (squared-error risk in *L²* is the running example) [1].

**From estimation to testing.**

The master reduction converts estimation into hypothesis testing via Markov's inequality. For any *δ > 0*,

> *sup_{θ} E_θ d²(θ̂, θ) ≥ δ² · sup_{θ} P_θ(d(θ̂, θ) ≥ δ).*

If hypotheses *θ_1, …, θ_M* are *2δ*-separated, i.e. *d(θ_i, θ_j) ≥ 2δ* for *i ≠ j*, then the minimum-distance test *ψ(X) = argmin_j d(θ̂(X), θ_j)* correctly identifies the index whenever *d(θ̂, θ_j) < δ*. Hence estimation error at scale *δ* implies testing error, and lower-bounding the testing error lower-bounds the minimax risk. The two-point case is Le Cam's lemma:

> **Lemma (Le Cam).** *inf_ψ max(P_0(ψ = 1), P_1(ψ = 0)) ≥ (1 − TV(P_0, P_1)) / 2,*

where *TV* is total variation distance. Fano and Assouad generalize this from 2 hypotheses to *M* [2][4].

**The divergence toolkit.**

| Divergence | Notation | Key property |
|---|---|---|
| Kullback–Leibler | *KL(P, Q)* | Tensorizes: *KL(P^{⊗n}, Q^{⊗n}) = n·KL(P, Q)* |
| Total variation | *TV(P, Q)* | Testing error bound via Le Cam |
| Hellinger | *H(P, Q)* | *H² ≤ TV ≤ √2·H*; tensorizes via affinity |
| Chi-square | *χ²(P, Q)* | *KL ≤ χ²*; often easier to bound for mixtures |

The workhorse inequalities linking them are **Pinsker's inequality** *TV(P,Q) ≤ √(KL(P,Q)/2)* and the Hellinger–TV sandwich *H²(P,Q) ≤ TV(P,Q) ≤ √(2)·H(P,Q)* [1][4]. In Gaussian models, *KL(N(μ_0, σ²I), N(μ_1, σ²I)) = ‖μ_0 − μ_1‖²/(2σ²)*, which makes KL control nearly mechanical once geometric separation is fixed.

**Information theory in one paragraph.**

For a discrete random variable *V*, *H(V)* is its Shannon entropy and *I(V; X) = H(V) − H(V|X)* the mutual information. The **data-processing inequality** states that for a Markov chain *V → X → V̂*, *I(V; V̂) ≤ I(V; X)*: no processing of the data can create information about the parameter. Fano's inequality turns this into an error bound [2].

##3 Methodology

**Recipe 1: Fano's method.**

1. **Pack.** Choose *M* hypotheses *{θ_1, …, θ_M} ⊂ Θ* that are *2δ*-separated in the loss metric *d*.
2. **Control information.** Bound the average pairwise divergence *(1/M²) Σ_{i,j} KL(P_i^n, P_j^n) ≤ α log M* for some *α < 1*.
3. **Apply Fano.** The minimax testing error is at least *1 − α − (log 2)/log M*, so the estimation risk is at least *δ²* times that factor.
4. **Optimize.** Choose the packing scale *δ* (and hence *M*) to maximize the bound; the largest feasible *δ* gives the rate.

> **Theorem (minimax bound via Fano).** *Suppose d(θ_i, θ_j) ≥ 2δ for all i ≠ j, and (1/M²)Σ_{i,j} KL(P_i^n, P_j^n) ≤ α log M with 0 < α < 1. Then R_n^*(Θ) ≥ δ²(1 − α − (log 2)/log M).*

The power of the method is that the packing can be *exponentially* large in the problem's intrinsic dimension, so *log M* grows and the penalty term vanishes [2][6].

**Recipe 2: Assouad's lemma.**

1. **Hypercube.** Embed *{θ_τ : τ ∈ {0,1}^m} ⊂ Θ* such that squared distance scales with Hamming distance: *d²(θ_τ, θ_τ') ≥ c_0 · Σ_j 1{τ_j ≠ τ'_j}*.
2. **Local control.** Bound *TV(P_τ, P_τ') ≤ α < 1* only for pairs at Hamming distance 1 (neighbors).
3. **Decompose.** The minimax risk is at least *(m·c_0/2)(1 − α)*.

> **Theorem (Assouad's lemma).** *Under the separation above, with α = max TV(P_τ, P_τ') over Hamming-1 neighbors, inf_{θ̂} sup_τ E_τ d²(θ̂, θ_τ) ≥ (m·c_0/2)(1 − α).*

Assouad's advantage is *locality*: only neighboring distributions must be close, which is far easier than controlling all *M²* pairs [4].

**Comparative anatomy.**

| Method | # Hypotheses | Key inequality | Divergence to control | Canonical application |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Le Cam (two-point) | 2 | error ≥ *(1 − TV)/2* | TV or KL of the pair | parametric sanity checks |
| Fano | *M* large (packing) | *P_e ≥ 1 − (I + log 2)/log M* | average pairwise KL | Hölder regression, density estimation |
| Assouad | *2^m* (hypercube) | Σ coordinate errors ≥ *(m/2)(1 − TV)* | TV between Hamming-1 neighbors | sparse vectors, additive models |

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Information-Theoretic Core: Fano's Inequality

> **Theorem (Fano's inequality).** *Let V be uniform on a set of size M ≥ 2 and let V → X → V̂ form a Markov chain. With P_e = P(V̂ ≠ V), we have P_e ≥ 1 − (I(V; X) + log 2)/log M (natural logarithms).*

*Proof sketch.* Write *H(V|X) = H(V) − I(V;X) = log M − I(V;X)*. Introduce the error indicator *E = 1{V̂ ≠ V}*. By the chain rule and data processing, *H(V|X) ≤ H(E) + P_e·log(M − 1) ≤ log 2 + P_e·log M*. Rearranging gives the claim [2].

To convert this into a minimax bound, let *V* be uniform on *{1, …, M}* and *X ∼ P_{θ_V}^{⊗n}*. Given any estimator *θ̂*, the minimum-distance decoder *ψ(X) = argmin_j d(θ̂(X), θ_j)* satisfies *{ψ ≠ V} ⊆ {d(θ̂, θ_V) ≥ δ}* under *2δ*-separation. Hence, with *Φ(u) = u²*,

> *sup_θ E_θ d²(θ̂, θ) ≥ δ² · P(ψ ≠ V) ≥ δ²(1 − (I(V;X) + log 2)/log M).*

It remains to bound *I(V; X)*. By convexity of KL (or a direct computation),

> *I(V; X) ≤ (1/M²) Σ_{i,j} KL(P_i^n, P_j^n),*

so controlling the *average* pairwise KL suffices — no worst-case pair analysis needed. For product measures this tensorizes to *n* times the single-observation KL, which is why the sample size enters linearly [2][4].

A useful corollary avoids even the pairwise average: if *(1/M)Σ_j KL(P_j^n, P_0^n) ≤ α log M* for a reference *P_0* (often the "null" hypothesis *θ_0 = 0*), the same conclusion holds. This *KL-to-center* form is the version in Tsybakov's Theorem 2.5 [1].

### 4.2 Packing Sets and the Gilbert–Varshamov Construction

Fano's method is only as strong as the packing it uses: we want *M* as large as possible at separation *2δ*. The **packing number** *M(ε, Θ, d)* — the maximum cardinality of an *ε*-separated subset — is the fundamental geometric quantity, dual to covering numbers via *M(2ε) ≤ N(ε) ≤ M(ε)* [1].

> **Lemma (Gilbert–Varshamov).** *There exists a subset Ω of the binary hypercube {0,1}^m with |Ω| ≥ 2^{m/8} such that any two distinct codewords differ in at least m/8 coordinates.*

*Proof idea.* Greedy selection: pick a codeword, delete its Hamming ball of radius *m/8*, repeat. A volume bound shows at least *2^m / Σ_{k < m/8} C(m,k) ≥ 2^{m/8}* codewords survive [4].

The standard nonparametric construction instantiates this on **bump functions**. Partition the unit interval into *m* bins of width *h = 1/m*. Let *φ* be a smooth bump supported in a single bin with *‖φ‖_∞ = 1*, and define

> *f_τ = Σ_{j=1}^{m} τ_j · γ·h^β·φ_j,  τ ∈ {0,1}^m,*

where *φ_j* is the bump shifted to bin *j* and *γ* is a small constant. For smoothness index *β* between 0 and 1, choosing *γ* small keeps every *f_τ* inside the Hölder class *Σ(β, L)*. Crucially, *‖f_τ − f_τ'‖_2² = γ²·h^{2β+1}·‖φ‖_2²·d_H(τ, τ')*, so *L²* separation is proportional to Hamming distance. Restricting to a Gilbert–Varshamov subcode gives a packing with *log M ≳ m* and pairwise separation *δ² ≍ m·h^{2β+1}* [1][6].

For Gaussian regression with noise variance *σ²*, tensorization gives *KL(P_τ^n, P_τ'^n) = (n/2σ²)·‖f_τ − f_τ'‖_2² ≲ n·m·h^{2β+1} = n·h^{2β}*. The Fano condition *n·h^{2β} ≲ m ≍ 1/h* yields *h^{2β+1} ≲ 1/n*, i.e. *h ≍ n^{-1/(2β+1)}*, and the risk floor *δ² ≍ h^{2β} ≍ n^{-2β/(2β+1)}* — the classical rate [1].

### 4.3 Assouad's Hypercube: Binary Testing in Every Coordinate

Where Fano argues globally about *M* hypotheses, Assouad argues *coordinate by coordinate*. Fix the hypercube embedding with *d²(θ_τ, θ_τ') ≥ c_0·Σ_j 1{τ_j ≠ τ'_j}*.

*Proof architecture.* For any estimator *θ̂*, define the coordinate-wise decoder *ψ_j(X) = argmin_{b ∈ {0,1}} d(θ̂, θ_{τ^{(j,b)}})*-type projection; more cleanly, write

> *E_τ d²(θ̂, θ_τ) ≥ c_0 · Σ_{j=1}^{m} P_τ(ψ_j ≠ τ_j)*

for a suitable binary test *ψ_j* per coordinate. Pair each *τ* with *τ^{(j)}*, the vertex differing only at coordinate *j*. For this pair, *1{ψ_j ≠ τ_j} + 1{ψ_j ≠ τ^{(j)}_j} ≥ 1* pointwise, so averaging the two error probabilities and applying Le Cam,

> *(P_τ(ψ_j ≠ τ_j) + P_{τ^{(j)}}(ψ_j ≠ τ^{(j)}_j))/2 ≥ (1 − TV(P_τ, P_{τ^{(j)}}))/2 ≥ (1 − α)/2.*

Summing over *j* and averaging over *τ* gives the lemma. Note the proof never compares non-neighboring hypotheses — the *locality* that makes Assouad so convenient [4].

*Why it shines.* In the bump construction, *P_τ* and *P_τ'* for Hamming-1 neighbors differ in a *single* bin, so *KL = (n/2σ²)·γ²h^{2β+1}‖φ‖_2²* and *TV ≤ √(KL/2)* by Pinsker. Setting this KL to a small constant gives *h ≍ n^{-1/(2β+1)}* again, and the risk floor is *(m·c_0/2)(1 − α) ≍ (1/h)·h^{2β+1} = h^{2β} ≍ n^{-2β/(2β+1)}*. Same rate, but the divergence analysis touched only *m* neighboring pairs rather than *M² ≍ 2^{m/4}* pairs [1].

### 4.4 The f-Divergence Unification

Fano's inequality is not an isolated trick: it is the *f(x) = x log x* case of a general *f*-divergence bound. Guntuboyina showed that for any convex *f* with *f(1) = 0*, the minimax testing error over a finite hypothesis set is controlled by the *f*-divergences *D_f(P_θ ‖ P̄)* to the mixture *P̄ = Σ w_θ P_θ* [3]:

> **Theorem (f-divergence lower bound).** *The worst-case testing error β_w satisfies a sharp upper bound in terms of Σ_θ w_θ D_f(P_θ ‖ P̄); taking f(x) = x log x recovers Fano's inequality, and the two-point case recovers the Topsøe–Pinsker family.*

This unification matters practically: the *χ²*-divergence version is often the easiest to evaluate for mixture models (since *χ²* tensorizes nicely and dominates KL), while the Hellinger version gives the sharpest constants for total-variation control [3]. The message is that the *choice of divergence is a degree of freedom* in lower-bound proofs, and the f-divergence lens makes that choice principled rather than ad hoc.

A further modern extension is the **continuous Fano inequality** of Gerchinovitz, Ménard, and Stoltz, which avoids discretization entirely: for Lipschitz regression on the unit interval it directly yields the tight *(σ²L/n)^{2/3}* lower bound with an explicit constant, by applying Fano's inequality to a *random* (rather than finitely supported) prior [5].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

**Worked calculation: Hölder regression via both methods.**

Take *Y_i = f(x_i) + ε_i* with fixed design *x_i = (i−1)/n* on the unit interval, *ε_i ∼ N(0, σ²)* i.i.d., and *f* in the Hölder class *Σ(β, L)* with *β* between 0 and 1 [5]. Both methods give *inf_{f̂} sup_{f ∈ Σ} E‖f̂ − f‖_2² ≳ n^{-2β/(2β+1)}*:

- *Fano route.* GV packing of size *M ≥ 2^{m/8}* with separation *δ² ≍ m·h^{2β+1}* and average KL *≲ n·h^{2β}*; feasibility *n·h^{2β} ≲ m = 1/h* forces *h ≍ n^{-1/(2β+1)}* [1][6].
- *Assouad route.* Hypercube with *c_0 ≍ h^{2β+1}*, neighbor *TV ≤ √(n·h^{2β+1})/const*; setting *TV ≤ 1/2* forces the same *h*, and the floor *(m·c_0/2)(1/2) ≍ h^{2β}* matches [4].

The rate is tight: kernel and wavelet estimators achieve *O(n^{-2β/(2β+1)})*, so the lower bound certifies their optimality [1].

**Canonical rate table.**

| Problem | Class | Minimax squared risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gaussian mean, dimension *d* | parametric | *≍ d/n* |
| Hölder regression, smoothness *β* | *Σ(β, L)* | *≍ n^{-2β/(2β+1)}* |
| Density estimation, Sobolev *β* | *W(β, L)* | *≍ n^{-2β/(2β+1)}* |
| *s*-sparse vectors, ambient dim *p* | *ℓ_0* ball | *≍ (s·log(p/s))/n* |
| Lipschitz regression (continuous Fano) | Lipschitz(*L*) | *≍ (σ²L/n)^{2/3}* |

The last row is the *β = 1* case of the Hölder rate, recovered without discretization by [5]; the sparse-vector row is proved by Fano over GV packings of sparse supports [6].

**Computational illustrations.**

The Fano risk floor for a Gaussian location packing:

```python
import numpy as np

def fano_lower_bound(M: int, avg_kl: float, delta: float) -> float:
    """Minimax squared-error floor from Fano's method (natural logs)."""
    if np.log(M) <= 0:
        return 0.0
    err = 1.0 - (avg_kl + np.log(2.0)) / np.log(M)
    return delta ** 2 * max(err, 0.0)

# d-dimensional Gaussian location model, packing of radius r
d, n, sigma, r = 50, 200, 1.0, 0.5
M = 2 ** (d // 8)                       # Gilbert-Varshamov-style packing size
avg_kl = n * (2 * r) ** 2 / (2 * sigma ** 2)   # worst-case n-sample KL
print("Fano risk floor:", fano_lower_bound(M, avg_kl, delta=r / 2))
```

The Gaussian KL used inside divergence control, in Haskell:

```haskell
-- KL divergence between univariate Gaussians N(m0,s0) and N(m1,s1)
klGaussian :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
klGaussian m0 s0 m1 s1 =
  log (s1 / s0) + (s0 * s0 + (m0 - m1) ^ 2) / (2 * s1 * s1) - 0.5

-- Fano testing-error floor from a mutual-information bound
fanoError :: Double -> Int -> Double
fanoError mi m = 1.0 - (mi + log 2.0) / log (fromIntegral m)
```

The Assouad floor evaluator, in Rust:

```rust
/// Assouad lower bound: m coordinates, per-coordinate separation c0,
/// max total variation between Hamming-1 neighbors tv_max.
fn assouad_lower_bound(m: usize, c0: f64, tv_max: f64) -> f64 {
    (m as f64) * c0 * (1.0 - tv_max) / 2.0
}

fn main() {
    // Holder regression sketch: h ~ n^{-1/(2b+1)}, m = 1/h bins
    let (n, beta) = (10_000.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
    let h = n.powf(-1.0 / (2.0 * beta + 1.0));
    let m = (1.0 / h) as usize;
    let floor = assouad_lower_bound(m, h.powf(2.0 * beta + 1.0), 0.5);
    println!("Assouad risk floor ~ {:.5}", floor);
}
```

##6 Limitations

- **Constants are not sharp.** Fano and Assouad deliver *order*-optimal rates (*≍*), not exact constants. Pinning the precise constant — e.g. Pinsker's theorem for Sobolev ellipsoids — requires refined techniques such as hardest rectangular subproblems or Bayesian Cramér–Rao arguments [1].
- **Logarithmic gaps.** Packing constructions via Gilbert–Varshamov lose constant factors in the exponent (*2^{m/8}* vs. the true packing number), which can surface as missing log factors in high-dimensional settings; the Yang–Barron mixture refinement closes some of these gaps [6].
- **Divergence control can fail.** Both methods need *KL* or *TV* between hypotheses to stay small while geometric separation stays large. Heavy-tailed noise, unbounded likelihood ratios, or irregular models can break the tensorization bookkeeping [3].
- **Non-adaptivity.** The constructions are tuned to a *known* smoothness *β*; they say nothing about adaptation (e.g. Lepski's method). Lower bounds for *adaptive* minimax risk need a different, more delicate apparatus [1].
- **Structural scope.** Assouad's lemma needs product-like local structure; Fano's method needs large packings (metric entropy), which may be intractable for exotic classes. Neither captures *computational* lower bounds — the statistical-computational gaps of sparse PCA or planted clique lie outside this framework entirely.
- **Worst-case pessimism.** Minimaxity is a worst-case certificate over the class; it does not imply that the bound is informative for the "typical" members practitioners encounter.

##7 Conclusion

Minimax lower bounds turn the tables on the statistician: instead of analyzing one estimator, one must defeat all of them, and the standard weapon is **reduction to hypothesis testing**. Fano's method and Assouad's lemma are the two great multi-hypothesis forms of this reduction, differing in geometry rather than in spirit:

- **Fano** argues *globally*: pack exponentially many well-separated hypotheses, bound the *average* KL, and let mutual information cap the testing success rate. It is the method of choice when metric entropy is understood [2].
- **Assouad** argues *locally*: embed a hypercube, decompose risk coordinate by coordinate, and bound only *neighboring* total variations. It is the method of choice for additive, sparse, or otherwise decomposable structure [4].

Together with Le Cam's two-point method for quick parametric checks and the modern *f*-divergence unification [3], they form a complete toolkit: essentially every classical nonparametric rate — Hölder, Sobolev, sparse, Lipschitz — is proved optimal by one of these routes [1][5][6]. The practitioner's rule is simple: if you can count packings, use Fano; if you can localize perturbations, use Assouad; if neither, the rate you seek may not exist.
