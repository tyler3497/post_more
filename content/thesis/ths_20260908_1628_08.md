# Formal Verification of Neural Networks: Reluplex, Zonotope Abstract Interpretation, and MILP Encodings for Robustness Certification

## Abstract

## 1 Introduction

Deep neural networks are now deployed in safety-critical settings — collision-avoidance advisories, medical triage, and autonomous perception — yet they resist the classical guarantees of software verification. **Formal verification of neural networks** addresses this: given a network $f: \mathbb{R}^{n_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{n_L}$, an input region $\mathcal{X}$, and a property $\varphi$, a verifier either proves $\forall x \in \mathcal{X}.\ \varphi(f(x))$ or produces a counterexample. The canonical property is *local adversarial robustness*: for a correctly classified input $x_0$ with label $y_0 = \arg\max_j f_j(x_0)$ and radius $\varepsilon > 0$,

$$\forall x \in B_\varepsilon(x_0).\ \arg\max\nolimits_j f_j(x) = y_0, \qquad B_\varepsilon(x_0) = \{x : \|x - x_0\|_\infty \le \varepsilon\}.$$

Verifying this property is **NP-complete** for ReLU networks [1]: the $2^N$ activation patterns of $N$ rectified units induce an exponential case analysis, and the reduction from 3-SAT is direct. Yet hardness results have not stopped progress. Three intellectual traditions have converged on the problem, each with distinct strengths:

1. **Exact logical methods**, exemplified by *Reluplex* [1], extend decision procedures from satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) to reason natively about ReLU constraints, achieving completeness at the cost of worst-case exponential search.
2. **Abstract interpretation**, in the AI2 [7], DeepZ [3], and DeepPoly [4] systems, sacrifices completeness for scalability, propagating sound geometric over-approximations — *zonotopes* and restricted polyhedra — through the network in polynomial time.
3. **Optimization-based methods**, notably the MILP formulation of Tjeng, Xiao, and Tedrake [2], encode verification as mixed-integer programming, exploiting industrial solvers and tight relaxations to certify networks two to three orders of magnitude faster than prior complete verifiers.

This thesis presents all three in depth, proves their key guarantees, and shows — following the unifying analysis of Bunel et al. [5, 6] — that they are facets of a single branch-and-bound architecture parameterized by its bounding and branching choices.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Piecewise-Linear Networks

We consider feedforward networks where layer $k$ computes $z^{(k)} = W^{(k)} a^{(k-1)} + b^{(k)}$ and $a^{(k)} = \sigma(z^{(k)})$. The **rectified linear unit** (ReLU), $\sigma(z) = \max(0, z)$, induces a *piecewise-linear* function: the network partitions input space into finitely many polyhedral regions, on each of which $f$ is affine. Smooth activations (sigmoid, tanh) admit only approximate encodings, which is why the verification literature orbits ReLU.

### 2.2 The Verification Query

Robustness verification is naturally phrased as a *satisfiability* problem. To prove local robustness, we ask whether there *exists* an adversarial example:

$$\exists x \in B_\varepsilon(x_0).\ \bigvee_{j \ne y_0} \big(f_j(x) - f_{y_0}(x) \ge 0\big).$$

If this formula is **unsatisfiable**, the network is certified robust on $B_\varepsilon(x_0)$; if **satisfiable**, the witness is an adversarial example. The verification problem thus becomes: decide satisfiability of a conjunction of linear constraints plus ReLU constraints of the form $y = \max(0, x)$. The difficulty is entirely in the ReLUs: without them, the query is a linear program, solvable in polynomial time.

> **Theorem (Hardness).** *Deciding whether a feedforward ReLU network admits an input in a given box whose output satisfies a linear inequality is NP-complete* [1].

The proof reduces 3-SAT to ReLU satisfiability by encoding Boolean variables as bounded reals and clauses as ReLU gadgets. Complete verifiers must therefore do exponential work in the worst case; practical success depends on *which* instances are hard and on the engineering of the search.

### 2.3 Abstract Interpretation in Brief

Abstract interpretation [7] reasons about *sets* of concrete states via an abstract domain related to the concrete powerset domain by a Galois connection. An abstract transformer $F^\sharp$ for a concrete operation $F$ is *sound* if $F(\gamma(a)) \subseteq \gamma(F^\sharp(a))$: the abstraction over-approximates. If the over-approximated output already satisfies the property, the property holds for the true network. Precision — how little is over-approximated — is the central design variable.

### 2.4 Optimization Preliminaries

A mixed-integer linear program minimizes a linear objective over variables constrained by linear inequalities, some restricted to integer values. Binary variables $\delta \in \{0, 1\}$ enable *disjunctive* modeling: with a sufficiently large constant $M$ (a *big-M*), the ReLU $y = \max(0, x)$ with $x \in [l, u]$ is encoded exactly as

$$y \ge 0,\quad y \ge x,\quad y \le x - l(1-\delta),\quad y \le u\,\delta,\quad \delta \in \{0,1\}.$$

When $\delta = 1$ (active), the constraints force $y = x$; when $\delta = 0$ (inactive), they force $y = 0$. The LP *relaxation* ($\delta \in [0,1]$) yields the well-known triangle (or *Planet*) relaxation [8], the convex hull of the ReLU graph over $[l, u]$ — the tightest possible convex relaxation of a single neuron.

## 3 Methodology

This thesis studies verification through three lenses, unified by a common discipline:

- **Exact logical search (Reluplex).** The simplex extension of Katz et al. [1]: algorithm, ACAS Xu results, and the completeness argument.
- **Sound abstraction (zonotopes).** Zonotope transformers derived from first principles, in the AI2 [7] → DeepZ [3] → DeepPoly [4] lineage, with the precision–cost trade-off across domains.
- **Exact optimization (MILP).** The big-M encoding, interval-arithmetic presolve [2], and the measurement of *exact* adversarial accuracy — all embedded in the branch-and-bound framework of Bunel et al. [5, 6].

Throughout, we distinguish **complete** verifiers (always terminate with a correct SAT/UNSAT verdict) from **incomplete** verifiers (may answer "unknown" but never lie). Completeness costs exponential worst-case work; incompleteness risks failing to certify a truly robust network.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Reluplex: Extending Simplex with Native ReLU Reasoning

Reluplex [1] is the landmark exact verifier. Its insight is that the simplex method — the workhorse of linear programming — can be *extended* rather than merely *applied* to neural network verification, by handling ReLU constraints natively inside the solver instead of encoding them away.

**Simplex background.** The simplex algorithm decides conjunctions of linear equalities over real variables with bounds. It maintains a *tableau* expressing basic variables as linear combinations of non-basic ones, plus an assignment to all variables. Pivot operations repair violated equalities; the algorithm terminates with either a satisfying assignment or a proof of unsatisfiability (via Farkas' lemma). Classical SMT solvers for linear real arithmetic (e.g., the Dutertre–de Moura procedure) build on this.

**ReLU pairs.** For each ReLU $y = \max(0, x)$, Reluplex introduces a *ReLU pair* $\langle x^b, x^f \rangle$: the backward-facing pre-activation $x^b$ and forward-facing post-activation $x^f$. The solver maintains an assignment to *all* variables at all times, even one violating some constraints. Each iteration prioritizes repairing *linear* violations with simplex pivots, tolerating temporarily violated ReLUs. When linear constraints hold but some pair is broken ($x^f \neq \max(0, x^b)$), Reluplex applies a **ReLU correction**, adjusting the assignment and possibly re-breaking linear constraints for subsequent pivots.

**Splitting on demand.** This repair loop need not converge — a ReLU can be "fixed" infinitely often. Reluplex therefore adopts the *splitting-on-demand* framework: a ReLU corrected too many times is **split** into its two linear phases, $x^b \le 0 \wedge x^f = 0$ (inactive) and $x^b \ge 0 \wedge x^f = x^b$ (active), generating two subproblems explored depth-first. In the worst case the search enumerates all $2^N$ activation patterns, at which point every subproblem is a pure LP — guaranteeing termination and completeness.

> **Theorem (Reluplex soundness and completeness).** *Reluplex returns SAT iff the conjunction of linear and ReLU constraints is satisfiable, and always terminates* [1].

**Engineering.** Bound derivation tightens variable bounds from the tableau (e.g., deducing $x^b > 0$ and fixing a ReLU's phase without splitting), and conflict analysis learns from unsatisfiable subproblems. On ACAS Xu — 45 networks with 6 hidden layers of 50 ReLUs each — Reluplex verified 10 safety properties (e.g., "if the intruder is distant and slower, the advisory is never a strong turn"), on networks an order of magnitude larger than prior methods could handle.

### 4.2 Zonotope Abstract Interpretation: The AI2 → DeepZ → DeepPoly Lineage

Where Reluplex searches exactly, abstract interpretation *over-approximates* — trading completeness for polynomial-time certification that scales to networks with hundreds of thousands of neurons.

**Zonotopes.** A *zonotope* is a centrally symmetric convex polytope parameterized by a center $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and generators $g_1, \dots, g_m$:

$$Z = \left\{ c + \sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha_i g_i \;\middle|\; \alpha_i \in [-1, 1] \right\}.$$

Zonotopes are closed under affine maps *exactly*: $WZ + b$ has center $Wc + b$ and generators $Wg_i$. No precision is lost at affine layers $z^{(k)} = W^{(k)} a^{(k-1)} + b^{(k)}$.

**The ReLU transformer.** The ReLU is not zonotope-closed, so an abstract transformer must over-approximate. The AI2 framework [7] pioneered this: given an input zonotope, compute for each neuron its interval bounds $[l, u]$ (cheaply, from the zonotope's support function), then apply a case analysis:

- If $u \le 0$: the ReLU is *definitely inactive*; output the degenerate zonotope $\{0\}$.
- If $l \ge 0$: the ReLU is *definitely active*; output the input zonotope unchanged.
- If $l < 0 < u$: the ReLU is *unstable*; the transformer must enclose the "kinked" image in a new zonotope, introducing a fresh generator to absorb the over-approximation. DeepZ [3] designed this transformer to minimize the volume of the enclosing zonotope while remaining sound with respect to floating-point arithmetic — a subtle but essential requirement, since unsound rounding in the abstract domain voids the certificate.

The following Python sketch captures the essence of zonotope propagation through one layer:

```python
import numpy as np

class Zonotope:
    def __init__(self, center, generators):
        self.c = np.asarray(center, float)
        self.G = np.asarray(generators, float)

    def affine(self, W, b):
        return Zonotope(W @ self.c + b, W @ self.G)  # exact

    def relu(self):
        lo = self.c - np.abs(self.G).sum(axis=1)
        hi = self.c + np.abs(self.G).sum(axis=1)
        c_new, G_new, extra = self.c.copy(), self.G.copy(), np.zeros_like(self.c)
        for i in range(len(self.c)):
            if hi[i] <= 0:                            # definitely inactive
                c_new[i] = 0.0; G_new[i, :] = 0.0
            elif lo[i] < 0:                           # unstable: hull
                lam = hi[i] / (hi[i] - lo[i])
                c_new[i] = lam * (self.c[i] - lo[i]) / 2.0
                G_new[i, :] = lam * self.G[i, :] / 2.0
                extra[i] = lam * (-lo[i]) / 2.0       # fresh noise symbol
        if np.any(extra):
            G_new = np.hstack([G_new, np.diag(extra)])
        return Zonotope(c_new, G_new)
```

The unstable case encloses the ReLU's graph over $[l, u]$ in the parallelogram with vertices $(l, 0)$, $(0,0)$, $(u, u)$, $(l, \lambda(l-u))$ — the tightest zonotope hull, with $\lambda = u/(u-l)$ the slope of the upper bounding line.

**The lineage.** AI2 [7] established the paradigm with box, zonotope, and polyhedra domains, certifying convolutional networks with 53,000 neurons in minutes where Reluplex needed hours. DeepZ [3] refined the zonotope transformers (handling ReLU, tanh, sigmoid), reaching 97% verification accuracy on an 88,500-hidden-unit network under $\ell_\infty$ attack with $\varepsilon = 0.1$ in 133 seconds average. DeepPoly [4] moved to a richer per-neuron polyhedral domain — strictly more precise than zonotopes — and combined it with trace partitioning to certify robustness under geometric perturbations like rotation for the first time. Each step trades polynomial-time cost against precision; none is complete.

| Domain | Affine exact? | ReLU precision | Cost per layer | Complete? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Box (intervals) | Yes | Coarse | $O(n)$ | No |
| Zonotope (AI2/DeepZ) | Yes | Medium (hull) | $O(n \cdot m)$ | No |
| DeepPoly | Yes | Fine (per-neuron) | $O(n^2)$ | No |
| Polyhedra (full) | Yes | Exact | Exponential | In principle |

### 4.3 MILP Encodings: Big-M Formulations, Tight Relaxations, and Presolve

Tjeng, Xiao, and Tedrake [2] took a third path: encode the *entire verification query* as a mixed-integer linear program and hand it to industrial solvers (e.g., Gurobi), whose decades of branch-and-cut engineering then do the heavy lifting.

**The encoding.** Each ReLU $y = \max(0,x)$ with bounds $l \le x \le u$ becomes four linear constraints plus one binary variable (§2.4); provably *stable* neurons need none. The robustness query becomes: find $x \in B_\varepsilon(x_0)$ subject to the network constraints and $f_j(x) - f_{y_0}(x) \ge 0$ for some $j \ne y_0$. Minimizing $\|x - x_0\|_\infty$ instead yields the *minimum adversarial distortion* — the exact distance to the decision boundary. Infeasibility of the MILP is a *proof* of robustness.

**Why it works.** Two ingredients separate MIPVerify from naïve encodings. First, the *tight formulation*: per-neuron bounds $[l, u]$ from interval arithmetic make each ReLU's LP relaxation exactly the Planet triangle — the strongest single-neuron convex relaxation [8]. Second, the *presolve*: interval bounds propagate forward, then backward through the property constraints, fixing stable ReLUs and tightening bounds before the solver starts. The combined effect is 2–3 orders of magnitude faster than Reluplex on minimum-distortion queries, scaling to convolutional and residual networks with over 100,000 ReLUs.

**Exact adversarial accuracy.** The headline result of [2] is methodological: for the first time, the *exact* adversarial accuracy of an MNIST classifier at $\varepsilon = 0.1$ was determined — 4.38% of test samples admit an adversarial example, and the remaining 95.62% are *certified* robust. No attack (which lower-bounds vulnerability) and no incomplete verifier (which upper-bounds it) can produce this number; only a complete verifier closes the gap from both sides.

### 4.4 Branch and Bound as a Unifying Framework

Bunel et al. [5, 6] revealed that these apparently disparate methods are instances of one architecture: **branch and bound (BaB)** over the ReLU activation patterns.

- *Bounding* computes a lower bound on the verification objective (or proves UNSAT of a subproblem) using a convex relaxation — interval bounds, the Planet relaxation, zonotope propagation, or Lagrangian duals [9].
- *Branching* splits a subproblem to tighten the relaxation: on a ReLU's phase (as in Reluplex and MIPVerify) or on an input dimension (splitting $B_\varepsilon$ itself, which sharpens all downstream bounds).

Under this lens, Reluplex is BaB with simplex-based bounding and ReLU-phase branching; MIPVerify is BaB with LP-relaxation bounding and solver-managed branching; Planet-based verifiers [8] are BaB with triangle-relaxation bounding. The framework's power is combinatorial: *any* bounding method pairs with *any* branching strategy. Bunel et al. showed well-chosen combinations — e.g., tight dual bounds with ReLU branching heuristics prioritizing unstable neurons with large bound gaps — achieve two orders of magnitude speedup over the prior state of the art [5]. The JMLR treatment [6] adds convolutional branching strategies and the TwinStream synthetic benchmark, showing verification hardness depends as much on $\varepsilon$ and bound quality as on network size.

> **Theorem (BaB correctness).** *If the bounding procedure is sound (never prunes a subproblem containing a counterexample) and branching partitions the search space exhaustively, BaB returns SAT iff a counterexample exists and terminates* [6].

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

### 5.1 Soundness Arguments

Completeness — the converse direction — holds for Reluplex and MILP (both explore the full activation-pattern space in the worst case) and fails by design for abstract interpretation, where the abstraction may be too coarse to certify a robust network. This asymmetry is fundamental, not incidental: any polynomial-time verifier for an NP-complete problem must be incomplete unless P = NP.

## 6 Limitations

**Scalability walls.** Complete verifiers remain confined to networks far smaller than production models. MIPVerify's 100,000-ReLU milestone is two orders of magnitude below a ResNet-50, and worst-case exponential behavior is not merely theoretical. The annual VNN-COMP competition consistently shows complete methods timing out on deep convolutional benchmarks.

**Activation generality.** The edifice rests on piecewise linearity. Sigmoid, tanh, softmax, and attention nonlinearities admit only *sound over-approximations*, never exact encodings; certifying a transformer with ReLU-level rigor remains out of reach, and the approximation gap grows with depth.

**Specification poverty.** Local $\ell_\infty$ robustness is mathematically convenient, not necessarily the right property. Real safety requirements — "the aircraft never advises descent into terrain," "the classifier is invariant to lighting" — involve semantic perturbations that current encodings capture only crudely. The ACAS Xu properties [1] are a rare example of domain-meaningful specifications; most benchmarks still certify norm balls.

**The relaxation barrier.** Single-neuron convex relaxations (Planet, zonotope hulls) are provably optimal *per neuron* but jointly loose: they ignore correlations between neurons. Multi-neuron relaxations [9] tighten this at steep computational cost, and the fundamental trade-off — precision versus tractability of the relaxation — is the field's central open quantitative question.

**Soundness engineering.** Certificates are only as trustworthy as their implementation. Floating-point unsoundness has historically invalidated verifiers; DeepZ's floating-point-sound design [3] is the exception that proves the rule. Proof-producing verifiers that emit independently checkable certificates remain rare.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Formal verification of neural networks has matured from a theoretical curiosity into an engineering discipline with real artifacts: Reluplex proved safety properties of a collision-avoidance system [1]; zonotope abstract interpretation certifies hundred-thousand-neuron classifiers in minutes [3, 7]; MILP encodings measure *exact* adversarial accuracy, closing the gap between attack and defense [2]; and the branch-and-bound framework [5, 6] reveals these as coordinated choices in a single design space rather than rival paradigms.

The intellectual core is now clear. Verification is search over ReLU activation patterns, and progress comes from *tighter relaxations* (to prune the search) and *smarter branching* (to divide it). NP-completeness guarantees no silver bullet exists; the empirical record shows careful engineering steadily moves the frontier outward. The next decade's challenges — scaling to foundation models, handling smooth nonlinearities exactly, and expressing specifications that capture what safety actually means — will demand the same combination of logical rigor and pragmatic algorithmics that produced the methods surveyed here.
