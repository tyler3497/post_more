# Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations: Denoising Score Matching, Reverse-Time SDE Sampling, and Probability Flow ODEs

## Abstract

Score-based generative modeling reframes generative learning as estimation of the *score function* — the gradient of the log-density $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x})$ — of data progressively corrupted by a forward stochastic differential equation, followed by synthesis through the *reverse-time* SDE of Anderson or its deterministic probability-flow ODE counterpart [1]. This thesis unifies noise-conditional score networks [3] and denoising diffusion probabilistic models [2] within the continuous-time SDE framework of Song et al. [1], derives the denoising score matching objective from first principles, characterizes the variance-exploding (VE), variance-preserving (VP), and sub-VP processes, and analyzes predictor–corrector sampling, DDIM as an ODE discretization [4], noise-schedule design [6], and exact likelihood computation via the instantaneous change of variables. We review empirical evidence that diffusion models surpass GANs in sample quality at scale [7] while quantifying the persistent sampling-speed versus likelihood-weighting tradeoff, and we identify open problems in score estimation under low-density regions, discretization error, and mode coverage. The SDE viewpoint is argued to be the definitive mathematical foundation of modern diffusion models.

## 1. Introduction

Generative modeling seeks to learn a distribution $p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})$ over high-dimensional data $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and to draw novel samples from it. Classical approaches impose structural compromises: normalizing flows demand invertible architectures with tractable Jacobians, GANs sacrifice likelihood evaluation and training stability, and autoregressive models impose an arbitrary variable ordering. *Score-based* generative modeling takes a radically different route: rather than modeling the density $p(\mathbf{x})$ itself, it models its *score function*

$$\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x}) \triangleq \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x}),$$

a vector field pointing in the direction of increasing log-probability at every point in space [5]. The score is sufficient for sampling via *Langevin dynamics*, which needs only log-density gradients — sidestepping the central intractability of energy-based modeling.

The lineage begins with Hyvärinen's score matching [5], which fits $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ by minimizing the Fisher divergence without ever evaluating the true score. Song and Ermon [3] scaled this to deep networks by perturbing data with multiple noise levels, training a *noise-conditional score network* (NCSN), and sampling with annealed Langevin dynamics. Independently, Ho, Jain, and Abbeel [2] introduced *denoising diffusion probabilistic models* (DDPMs) — a variational model over a discrete Gaussian noising chain trained with a simple denoising objective later recognized as denoising score matching across noise scales.

Song et al. [1] unified both: NCSNs and DDPMs discretize a single continuous-time *forward stochastic differential equation*

$$d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}, \qquad t \in [0, T],$$

and that sampling corresponds to simulating the *reverse-time SDE* (Anderson, 1982), or alternatively a deterministic *probability flow ordinary differential equation* sharing the same marginals. This thesis develops that framework in full: forward processes, denoising score matching, reverse-time sampling, the probability flow ODE and exact likelihoods, DDIM [4], schedule design [6], the empirical record [7], and the limitations.

> **Thesis claim.** The SDE formulation is not merely a notational convenience: it exposes the design space of diffusion models (drift, diffusion coefficient, weighting, solver) as independent, composable choices, and it is the lens through which likelihood computation, fast sampling, and theoretical analysis become possible.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Score matching

Hyvärinen [5] introduced *score matching* to fit non-normalized models $p_	heta = 	ilde{p}_	heta/Z_	heta$ by matching scores, which are independent of $Z_	heta$. The *explicit* score matching objective,

$$J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_2^2\right],$$

is intractable but equals — up to a constant — the *implicit* form

$$J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})}\left[\operatorname{tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})) + \tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x})\|_2^2\right] + \text{const},$$

which needs no data score [5]. The trace term is expensive in high dimensions, motivating *sliced* and *denoising* variants that avoid it.

### 2.2 Noise-conditional score networks and DDPMs

Song and Ermon [3] observed that score estimation fails in low-density regions where data are scarce, and in the manifold setting where $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_{\text{data}}$ is undefined off the manifold. Their remedy: perturb data with noise scales $\{\sigma_i\}$, train one network $\mathbf{s}_	heta(\mathbf{x}, \sigma)$ on all scales via *denoising score matching*, and sample by *annealed Langevin dynamics*.

Ho et al. [2] defined a *forward* Markov chain $q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T} \mid \mathbf{x}_0) = \prod_t q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1})$ with Gaussian transitions $q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I})$, and learned a *reverse* chain $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ by optimizing a variational bound. Their key simplification — predicting the added noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ — yields

$$L_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon},\, t)\right\|^2\right],$$

which Ho et al. found superior to the full variational bound, achieving FID 3.17 on CIFAR-10 [2]. Song et al. [1] later proved this objective is exactly a weighted denoising score matching loss, cementing the NCSN–DDPM equivalence.

### 2.3 Unification and extensions

Song et al. [1] generalized both models to continuous-time SDEs, introducing the probability flow ODE, predictor–corrector samplers, and exact likelihood computation. Song, Meng, and Ermon [4] introduced *denoising diffusion implicit models* (DDIMs), a non-Markovian generalization of DDPM permitting deterministic sampling in far fewer steps — in the SDE view, an Euler discretization of the probability flow ODE. Karras et al. [6] *elucidated the design space*, and achieved record FIDs (1.97 on CIFAR-10 unconditional). Dhariwal and Nichol [7] demonstrated that a well-tuned diffusion model (*ADM*) with classifier guidance beats BigGAN-deep on ImageNet 128×128 (FID 2.97 vs. 6.02), ending GAN dominance of the sample-quality frontier.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The forward SDE

Let $p_0$ denote the data distribution and consider the Itô SDE [1]

$$d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}, \qquad t \in [0, T],$$

where $\mathbf{f}$ is the drift coefficient, $g$ the diffusion coefficient, and $\mathbf{w}$ a standard Wiener process. If $\mathbf{f}$ is affine, the *perturbation kernel* $p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}(t) \mid \mathbf{x}(0))$ is Gaussian in closed form, and $p_T$ approaches a tractable prior as $T \to \infty$.

Three canonical SDEs, corresponding to the discrete predecessors, are:

| SDE | Drift $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | Discrete analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VE** (variance exploding) | $\mathbf{0}$ | $\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{dt}\sigma^2(t)}$ | NCSN / SMLD [3] |
| **VP** (variance preserving) | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | DDPM [2] |
| **sub-VP** | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\mathbf{x}$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)\,(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t \beta(s)\,ds})}$ | Improved DDPM variant [1] |

The VE SDE explodes variance while preserving the mean; the VP SDE preserves unit variance, mirroring DDPM's $\bar{\alpha}_t$ schedule; the sub-VP SDE bounds its variance strictly below the VP variance, which [1] found improves likelihoods.

### 3.2 Denoising score matching in continuous time

We train a time-dependent score network $\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ to approximate $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ by minimizing a weighted Fisher divergence integrated over time:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,T]}\left[\lambda(t)\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}(0)}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}(t)\mid\mathbf{x}(0)}\left[\left\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}(t), t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}(t)}\log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}(t)\mid\mathbf{x}(0))\right\|_2^2\right]\right].$$

The Gaussian kernel has closed-form score, reducing training to *denoising* regression. The weighting $\lambda(t)$ is critical:

- $\lambda(t) \propto 1/\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \log p_{0t}\|^2]$ balances the loss across noise scales (the NCSN choice [3]);
- $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ corresponds to the DDPM $L_{\text{simple}}$ [2].

Crucially, Song et al. [1] show that $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$ makes $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ equal — up to a constant — to a *weighted evidence lower bound*, linking score matching to maximum likelihood.

### 3.3 The reverse-time SDE

> **Theorem (Anderson, 1982; reverse-time diffusion).** Let $\mathbf{x}(t)$ solve $d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w}$ on $[0,T]$ with marginals $p_t$. Then the time-reversed process $\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t) \coloneqq \mathbf{x}(T - t)$ satisfies
>
> $$d\bar{\mathbf{x}} = \left[\mathbf{f}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right] dt + g(t)\,d\bar{\mathbf{w}},$$
>
> where $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ is a reverse-time Wiener process. Simulating this SDE backward from $\mathbf{x}(T) \sim p_T$ with the true score recovers $p_0$ exactly.

Replacing the true score with $\mathbf{s}_	heta$ yields a generative sampler. Song et al. [1] advocate *predictor–corrector* samplers interleaving an SDE step (predictor) with Langevin MCMC corrections (corrector), improving sample quality at fixed compute.

### 3.4 The probability flow ODE

> **Theorem (Probability flow ODE; Song et al. [1]).** For the forward SDE above, there exists a deterministic process whose marginals $\{p_t\}_{t=0}^T$ coincide exactly with those of the SDE, given by
>
> $$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}) \;\triangleq\; \tilde{\mathbf{f}}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t).$$

The probability flow ODE unlocks: (i) *fast deterministic sampling* with adaptive ODE solvers; (ii) *exact likelihood computation* via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula; and (iii) an invertible encoding $\mathbf{x}(0) \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}(T)$ enabling latent interpolation. DDIM [4] is, in this light, an Euler discretization of the probability flow ODE for the VP SDE.

### 3.5 Predictor–corrector sampling algorithm

```python
def pc_sampler(score_fn, prior_sample, ts, f, g, snr=0.16):
    """Predictor-Corrector sampler for the reverse-time SDE."""
    x = prior_sample()                      # x(T) ~ p_T
    for t, t_prev in zip(ts[:-1], ts[1:]):
        dt = t_prev - t                     # negative step
        # --- Predictor: Euler-Maruyama on reverse SDE ---
        drift = f(x, t) - g(t)**2 * score_fn(x, t)
        x = x + drift * dt + g(t) * sqrt(-dt) * randn_like(x)
        # --- Corrector: one annealed Langevin step ---
        grad = score_fn(x, t_prev)
        step = snr * norm(randn_like(x)) / norm(grad)
        x = x + step * grad + sqrt(2 * step) * randn_like(x)
    return x                                # approx. sample from p_0
```

An identity corrector recovers plain SDE solvers; DDIM [4] is the deterministic special case with a strided time grid.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why gradients of log-density suffice

The score is invariant to the normalizing constant, since $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log (Z^{-1}\tilde{p}) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log \tilde{p}$ — which is why score-based models represent unnormalized densities yet train without estimating $Z_\theta$ [5]. Two pathologies motivate noise perturbation: (i) where $p_{	ext{data}}$ is near zero, samples are scarce and Langevin chains mix poorly; (ii) on low-dimensional manifolds the score is undefined in ambient directions. Convolving with Gaussian noise yields $p_\sigma = p_{\text{data}} * \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$, whose score is well-defined everywhere at the price of blurring fine detail — hence annealing from large to small $\sigma$ in [3], and its continuous-time analogue in [1].

### 4.2 From intractable Fisher divergence to tractable regression

The conceptual core of the framework is the identity

$$\mathbb{E}_{p_t}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t\|^2\right] = \mathbb{E}_{p_0 p_{0t}}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_{0t}(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0)\|^2\right] + C.$$

The left side is intractable; the right side regresses against the *known* Gaussian kernel score. For the VP SDE, the target is $-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}/\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}$, so denoising score matching with $\lambda(t) = 1-\bar{\alpha}_t$ is *exactly* noise prediction — the DDPM objective [2]. This explains why $L_{\text{simple}}$ beats the variational bound: it reweights a score matching loss toward noise scales where the score is hardest to estimate. The choice of $\lambda(t)$ thus mediates a *sample-quality versus likelihood* tradeoff recurring throughout [1] and [6].

### 4.3 The reverse-time SDE and predictor–corrector methods

Anderson's time-reversal is the generative engine of the framework: reversing time turns noise injection into *denoising drift*, as $-g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ pushes trajectories uphill on the log-density landscape. Euler–Maruyama discretization on the VP process recovers DDPM ancestral sampling [2]; on the VE process it recovers annealed Langevin dynamics [3] — one method viewed through the SDE lens [1]. *Corrector* steps are justified because, at fixed $t$, Langevin dynamics with the learned score converges to $p_t$; interleaving them with predictor steps controls discretization error. Empirically, PC samplers dominate pure SDE solvers on FID at equal function evaluations [1], and stochastic samplers often beat ODE samplers at large step counts because injected noise *corrects* score errors while ODEs compound them [6].

### 4.4 Probability flow ODEs, DDIM, and exact likelihoods

Eliminating the Wiener process while preserving marginals yields the probability flow ODE — a *neural ODE* parameterized by the score network. Three consequences matter. First, deterministic sampling permits adaptive-step solvers and the strided schedules of DDIM [4], cutting sampling from 1000 network evaluations to 20–50 with modest quality loss. Second, the change-of-variables formula gives *exact* likelihoods (up to solver and score error), enabling bits-per-dimension reporting. Third, invertibility yields a genuine latent space: $\mathbf{x}(0) \mapsto \mathbf{x}(T)$ encoding is cycle-consistent, supporting interpolation.

### 4.5 Noise schedule design and the EDM framework

The functions $\mathbf{f}, g$ — equivalently, the noise schedule $\sigma(t)$ — profoundly affect training dynamics and sample quality. Karras et al. [6] *elucidated the design space* by separating four concerns:

1. **Schedule** $\sigma(t)$: concentrate capacity at perceptually relevant noise levels.
2. **Preconditioning**: $D_	heta(\mathbf{x};\sigma) = c_{	ext{skip}}(\sigma)\mathbf{x} + c_{	ext{out}}(\sigma)F_	heta(c_{	ext{in}}(\sigma)\mathbf{x}; c_{	ext{noise}}(\sigma))$ keeps inputs/targets unit-variance across $\sigma$.
3. **Loss weighting** $\lambda(\sigma)$ emphasizing mid-range noise.
4. **Sampler**: their second-order Heun sampler on the probability flow ODE reaches FID 1.97 on CIFAR-10 with 35 evaluations.

A key insight of [6]: much progress attributed to "better models" was in fact better *conditioning and schedules* — with these fixed, VE and VP perform comparably, and remaining differences trace to the weighting function's implicit prioritization of sample quality versus likelihood.

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Analysis

### 5.1 Sample quality: the fall of GAN dominance

The empirical headline of the SDE era is that diffusion models surpassed GANs on the canonical image benchmarks:

| Model | Dataset | FID ↓ | NFE | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM | CIFAR-10 | 3.17 | 1000 | [2] |
| NCSN++ (VE SDE, PC sampler) | CIFAR-10 | 2.20 | 2000 | [1] |
| DDIM (VP ODE, strided) | CIFAR-10 | 4.67 | 50 | [4] |
| ADM + classifier guidance | ImageNet 128² | 2.97 | 250 | [7] |
| BigGAN-deep (best GAN) | ImageNet 128² | 6.02 | 1 | [7] |
| EDM (Heun sampler) | CIFAR-10 | 1.97 | 35 | [6] |

Dhariwal and Nichol [7] introduced *classifier guidance* — perturbing the score with $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(y\mid\mathbf{x})$ — so ADM beat BigGAN-deep on nearly every metric at 128×128: the first decisive diffusion victory over GANs at scale.

### 5.2 Likelihoods and the weighting tradeoff

With $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$, the score matching loss bounds the negative log-likelihood, and the probability flow ODE permits estimation: [1] reports ≤ 2.99 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 with the sub-VP SDE, competitive with the best autoregressive models, while the DDPM weighting achieves superior FID at worse likelihoods. This *likelihood–sample-quality tradeoff* is fundamental: likelihood weighting emphasizes small $t$ (fine detail), quality weightings the mid-noise regime (global structure) [1][6].

### 5.3 Theoretical guarantees

Under Lipschitz score regularity and $L^2$-accurate score estimation, discretization analyses bound reverse-SDE/ODE sampling error in total variation and Wasserstein distance, polynomial in dimension and the log-Sobolev constant of $p_t$. The deepest contribution is conceptual: [1] shows *score matching with likelihood weighting equals maximizing a variational lower bound*, unifying the Fisher-divergence and KL-divergence perspectives separate since [5].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Sampling speed.** The reverse SDE typically requires hundreds to thousands of network evaluations per sample — far slower than a GAN forward pass. DDIM [4] and EDM's Heun sampler [6] reduce this to tens of evaluations, but a quality gap persists.

**Score estimation in low-density regions.** Denoising score matching constrains the learned score only where $p_{0t}$ has mass; in the tails the network extrapolates, producing occasional artifacts.

**Likelihood–quality tradeoff.** No canonical $\lambda(t)$ serves both [1][6]; whether one training run can achieve both remains open.

**Mode coverage and the manifold hypothesis.** Diffusion models cover modes better than GANs empirically [7], but quantitative guarantees are absent, and the forward SDE's interaction with low-dimensional-manifold data — where scores blow up near the manifold — is only partially understood.

**Discretization error.** All samplers discretize continuous-time objects, yet error analyses assume score accuracy networks only approximately satisfy.

**Guidance.** Classifier and classifier-free guidance lack a clean SDE-theoretic justification — they sample from a debated *tilted* distribution — yet drive most state-of-the-art results [7].

## 7. Conclusion

Score-based generative modeling through SDEs [1] unified denoising diffusion [2] and noise-conditional score models [3]: a forward SDE corrupts data to noise, denoising score matching learns the score field, and generation proceeds via the reverse-time SDE or the probability flow ODE — with DDIM [4] as an ODE discretization and exact likelihoods as a corollary. Careful schedule, preconditioning, and sampler design [6] carried the framework past GANs on ImageNet [7], establishing diffusion as the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity synthesis. Yet sampling cost, the likelihood–quality tradeoff, tail reliability, and the foundations of guidance remain open. The SDE viewpoint states these problems precisely enough to be solved — the proper function of a mathematical foundation.
