# Grothendieck Topoi, Sheaf Semantics, and Homotopy Type Theory: A Categorical Foundation for Synthetic Mathematics and Higher Sheaf Cohesion

## Abstract
This thesis develops a unified categorical perspective connecting Grothendieck topoi, sheaf semantics, and homotopy type theory (HoTT) as foundations for synthetic mathematics. We review elementary and Grothendieck topoi, Lawvere-Tierney topologies, and sheafification as a lex left exact modality, and show how the internal Mitchell-Bénabou language yields Kripke-Joyal forcing. We then embed homotopy type theory with univalence into higher topos theory via models in simplicial presheaves and cubical sets, explicating the univalence axiom as object classifier universality. Finally, we examine cohesive (∞,1)-toposes through Lawvere's axiomatic cohesion — the adjoint quadruple ʃ ⊣ ♭ ⊣ ♯ — and Schreiber-Shulman cohesive HoTT, demonstrating synthetic differential geometry and synthetic homotopy theory as internal languages. By comparing 1-topos logic, HoTT, and cohesive modalities, we argue that topos-theoretic foundations provide a robust, verifiable alternative to set-theoretic foundations for modern geometry.

## 1. Introduction

The search for foundations of mathematics that faithfully captures *geometric* and *cohesive* intuition has led categorically from **Set** to **Topos**. While Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory treats all mathematical objects as discrete collections of points, **topos theory** — initiated by Grothendieck and Verdier in SGA4 [1] and axiomatized by Lawvere and Tierney [2,3] — treats a universe of variable sets.

This thesis claims that three historical threads converge:

1.  **Grothendieck topoi** as generalized spaces, defined via sites and sheaves.
2.  **Elementary topos logic**, with its intuitionistic internal language and sheaf semantics (Kripke-Joyal).
3.  **Homotopy Type Theory** (HoTT) with Voevodsky's *univalence*, as the internal language of **(∞,1)-topoi** [4,5].

The synthesis is *cohesive homotopy type theory*, developed by Lawvere [6], Schreiber, and Shulman [7], which internalizes notions like *shape*, *flat*, and *sharp* as modalities inside type theory.

> **Central Thesis:** A Grothendieck topos is not merely a category of sheaves, but a *mathematical universe* with its own logic; an elementary (∞,1)-topos internalizes homotopy; and a cohesive topos reintroduces cohesion that Set forgets. Their internal languages — higher-order intuitionistic logic, HoTT, and cohesive HoTT — provide synthetic axiom systems for differential geometry, gauge theory, and homotopy theory.

We assume graduate-level familiarity with category theory, but recall key definitions for completeness.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Elementary Topos

> **Definition 2.1 (Elementary Topos):** A category $\mathcal{E}$ is an elementary topos if it has finite limits, exponentials $B^A$, and a *subobject classifier* $\Omega$ with a universal monomorphism $\top : 1 \to \Omega$ such that for every mono $m: S \hookrightarrow A$ there exists a unique characteristic map $\chi_m : A \to \Omega$ making a pullback square.

This yields a rich logical structure: $\mathcal{E}$ is **cartesian closed**, **finitely complete and cocomplete**, and has an internal Heyting algebra $\Omega$ representing intuitionistic higher-order logic.

Examples of elementary toposes include:

*   $\mathbf{Set}$
*   $\mathbf{Sh}(X)$ for a topological space $X$
*   $\mathbf{Set}^{C^{op}}$ — presheaves on small $C$
*   The effective topos $\mathcal{Eff}$

Crucially, every elementary topos has an internal *Mitchell-Bénabou language* [8] — a typed higher-order intuitionistic logic.

### 2.2 Grothendieck Topologies and Sites

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be small. A **Grothendieck topology** $J$ is an assignment for each $c \in \mathcal{C}$ of a collection $J(c)$ of sieves $S \subseteq y(c)$ satisfying:

1.  Maximal sieve $\in J(c)$
2.  Stability under pullback
3.  Transitivity (local character)

A pair $(\mathcal{C}, J)$ is a *site*. A more computational presentation uses *covering families* $\{f_i: c_i \to c\}$.

A **presheaf** $F: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Set}$ is a **sheaf** for $J$ if for every covering sieve $S \in J(c)$, the canonical map:

$$ F(c) \longrightarrow \lim_{f: d \to c \in S} F(d) $$

is an isomorphism. Equivalently:

$$ 0 \to F(c) \to \prod_i F(c_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} F(c_i \times_c c_j) $$

is an equalizer.

The inclusion $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C},J) \hookrightarrow \mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ admits a **left exact left adjoint** $a : \mathbf{PSh} \to \mathbf{Sh}$, sheafification. Left exactness preserves finite limits, thus logical structure.

> **Theorem 2.2 (Giraud):** A category $\mathcal{G}$ is a Grothendieck topos iff it satisfies Giraud's axioms: cocomplete, has a small generating set, colimits are stable under pullback and disjoint, and equivalence relations are effective.

### 2.3 Internal Logic and Kripke-Joyal Semantics

For $\mathcal{E}$ topos, the internal language is interpreted via:

*   Types as objects of $\mathcal{E}$
*   Terms $c: A$ as morphisms $c: 1 \to A$ in context
*   Formulas $\phi \subseteq A$ as subobjects $\{\!|x:\phi|\!\} \hookrightarrow A$ or maps $A \to \Omega$
*   $\forall, \exists, \Rightarrow$ interpreted via adjoints to pullback: $\Pi_f \dashv f^* \dashv \Sigma_f$

Kripke-Joyal forcing $c \Vdash \phi(x)$ defines when a generalized element $c: C \to A$ satisfies $\phi$:

*   $c \Vdash \phi \land \psi$ iff $c \Vdash \phi$ and $c \Vdash \psi$
*   $c \Vdash \exists y.\phi$ iff there is epic $p: D \twoheadrightarrow C$ and $d: D \to B$ with $(c\circ p, d) \Vdash \phi$
*   $c \Vdash \phi \Rightarrow \psi$ iff for all $f: D\to C$, $c f \Vdash \phi$ implies $c f \Vdash \psi$

Thus sheaf topoi internalize *locality* as logical modality.

### 2.4 Homotopy Type Theory in One Page

Martin-Löf dependent type theory with identity types $\text{Id}_A(x,y)$ interprets in any locally cartesian closed category. Awodey-Warren and Voevodsky showed that taking fibrations as types models $\text{Id}$ as path spaces [9]. HoTT adds:

*   **Univalence**: $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B)$ — equivalent types are identical.
*   **Higher Inductive Types (HITs)**: inductive definitions with path constructors, e.g. $S^1$ generated by $\mathsf{base}$ and $\mathsf{loop}: \mathsf{base}= \mathsf{base}$.

We work in HoTT as formal system suitable for cubical implementations (Coq, Agda, Cubical Agda).

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

Our methodology is *synthetic-logical* rather than analytic. We compare foundations via universal properties and internal language correspondences.

### 3.1 The Topos Trichotomy

| Foundation | Category | Internal Logic | Logic Type | Universe Object |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Set-theoretic | $\mathbf{Set}$ | ZFC | Classical Boolean | N/A |
| 1-topos | Grothendieck $\mathcal{E}$ | Mitchell-Bénabou HOL | Intuitionistic HOL | $\Omega$ |
| Homotopy | $(\infty,1)$-topos $\mathcal{H}$ | HoTT + Univalence | Intuitionistic + Homotopy | $\mathcal{U}$ , Object Classifier |
| Cohesive | Cohesive $\infty$-topos | Cohesive HoTT | Modal + Differential | $\mathbf{B}G$ classifiers |

Semantic correspondence:

$$ \text{Theory} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \text{Categorical model} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \text{Internal language model construction} $$

We prove correspondences via **Diaconescu's Theorem**: geometric morphisms $p : \mathcal{F} \to \mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ correspond to $J$-continuous flat functors $\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{F}$, and its $\infty$-analogue due to Lurie [4, Thm 6.1.5.2].

### 3.2 Lawvere-Tierney Sheafification as Modality

A **Lawvere-Tierney topology** is $j: \Omega \to \Omega$ with $j\circ j = j$, $j\circ \top = \top$, $j\circ \land = \land \circ (j \times j)$. Sheaves are $j$-closed subpresheaves. Define nucleus $j$ giving left exact modality $\bigcirc_j : \Omega \to \Omega$.

In HoTT, this becomes a **lex modality**: $\bigcirc : \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{U}$ with unit $\eta_A: A \to \bigcirc A$ s.t. $\bigcirc$-modal types closed under $\Sigma$, $\Pi$, $=$, $\bigcirc \bigcirc A \simeq \bigcirc A$.

This unifies 1-sheafification and $(\infty,1)$-localization.

### 3.3 From 1-Topos to $\infty$-Topos

Lurie defines $\infty$-topos as left exact localization of presheaf $\infty$-category $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}) = \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{op}, \mathcal{S})$, where $\mathcal{S}$ is ∞-category of spaces. Universe $\mathcal{U}$ is *object classifier*: for $A$ small, maps $X \to \mathcal{U}$ classify small fibrations.

**Voevodsky's Univalence** asserts $\mathsf{idtoeqv}: (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \xrightarrow{\simeq} (A \simeq B)$ is equivalence. In Lurie's model, this is precisely that $\mathcal{U}$ classifies objects up to homotopy equivalence — a homotopical subobject classifier.

Method: We construct explicit models in $\mathbf{sSet}$-valued presheaves with injective model structure, then Quillen equivalence to cubical sets $\mathbf{cSet}$ with Kan composition structure [10].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Site Coverage, Sieves, and Étale Geometry

Fix site $(\mathcal{C},J)$. A *sieve* $S$ on $c$ is subfunctor $S \subset y(c)$. Covering sieves axiomatize "cover" without points.

Consider $X$ topological, $\mathcal{O}(X)$ poset of opens. Covers $\{U_i \hookrightarrow U\}$ correspond to sieve $S = \{V \hookrightarrow U \mid \exists i: V \subseteq U_i\}$. Sheaf condition: matching families glue uniquely.

> **Theorem (Grothendieck):** $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C},J)$ has enough injectives iff $J$ subcanonical? Actually every Grothendieck topos has enough injectives.

For algebraic geometry, étale site $Et(X)$: objects étale $U \to X$, covers jointly surjective families. Sheaves encode Galois descent. This yields *étale topos* $X_{et}$, cohomology $H^i_{et}$ — essential for Deligne proof of Weil conjectures.

Key insight: *Grothendieck topos separates geometric notion of cover from topological points.* This enables *synthetic algebraic geometry*: define schemes as sheaves on $Ring^{op}$ with Zariski topology.

### 4.2 Elementary Topos Logic and Classifying Topoi

Every geometric theory $\mathbb{T}$ classifiable by a Grothendieck topos $\mathcal{B}[\mathbb{T}]$ — classifying topos. Models of $\mathbb{T}$ in $\mathcal{E}$ $\simeq$ geometric morphisms $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{B}[\mathbb{T}]$.

Example: theory of rings classified by Zariski topos $\mathrm{Zar} = \mathbf{Sh}(Ring^{op}_{fp}, Zar)$. Generic ring $U$ in $\mathcal{B}$. Local maps correspond to local rings.

Thus topos logic is not auxiliary — it *is* generalized algebraic geometry via functor of points.

Internal language practical example in Haskell-style internal language transpilation:

```haskell
-- Internal language of a topos with NNO
type Formula a = Subobject a

-- Sheaf quantifiers via Kripke-Joyal
kripkeForall :: (a -> Formula b) -> Formula a
kripkeForall f = rightAdjoint pi f  -- Π_f ⊣ f*

-- Power object power of sheaf of sets
powerObject :: Topos e => e obj -> e (Power obj)
powerObject a = exponentialOmega a  -- Ω^A

-- Propositional truncation in sheaf topos
sheafTrunc :: Sheaf a -> PropSheaf
sheafTrunc a = image (a -> 1 -> Omega)
```

In Agda cubical form, internal glue modality:

```agda
record LawvereTierneyTopos (ℓ : Level) : Type (lsuc ℓ) where
  Ω : Type ℓ
  j : Ω → Ω
  j-idem : ∀ o → j (j o) ≡ j o
  j-top  : j ⊤ ≡ ⊤
  j-meet : ∀ p q → j (p ∧ q) ≡ j p ∧ j q

record Sheaf (C : Category) (J : Coverage C) : Type where
  F : Presheaf C
```

### 4.3 Homotopy Interpretation and Univalence as Classifier

Martin-Löf's intensional identity elimination $J$ mirrors path induction for spaces. Model in Kan complexes: type $A$ as Kan complex, term $a:A$ as vertex, $p: \mathsf{Id}_A(x,y)$ as 1-simplex.

Awodey's observation [11]: extensional type theory is internal language of 1-topoi, thus *intensional* theory ought to be internal of *higher* topoi. Univalent axiom distinguishes HoTT from bare MLTT.

> **Theorem (Voevodsky):** In simplicial model $\mathcal{U}_{Kan} = \coprod_{[n]} B(\mathrm{Aut}(Δ^n))$, canonical map $\mathsf{idtoeqv}$ is a weak equivalence.

Proof sketch: universal Kan fibration $\tilde{\mathcal{U}} \to \mathcal{U}$ is univalent because simplicial presheaf classifying space for small kan fibrations is object representing equivalences. Simplicially, $Eq(\tilde{\mathcal{U}})$ classifies pairs of embeddings with homotopy equivalence.

Implications:

*   *Function extensionality* holds propositionally (proves dependent function extensionality from univalence)
*   Mathematics invariant under equivalence — *structuralism* formalized, not ad hoc transport

In code, univalence in Cubical Agda is theorem:

```agda
-- Cubical Agda: univalence provable, not axiom
ua : {A B : Type ℓ} → A ≃ B → A ≡ B
ua = Glue -- Kan composition structure, cubical

uaβ : {A B : Type ℓ}(e : A ≃ B) → transport (ua e) ≡ equivFun e
uaβ e i = transp ... -- computational univalence
```

### 4.4 Cohesive Toposes and Modal Synthetic Mathematics

Lawvere axiomatic cohesion [6] captures category of *spaces* versus sets. A Grothendieck topos $\mathbf{H}$ is *cohesive* over $\mathbf{Set}$ if exists adjoint string:

$$ \Pi_0 = ∫ \dashv \flat = Disc \dashv \Gamma = pts \dashv Sharp = coDisc $$

with $∫$ preserves finite products, $Disc$ and $coDisc$ fully faithful, $∫ \dashv \flat \dashv \Gamma \dashv \sharp$, and $∫$ product-preserving.

Modalities:

*   $\flat = \flat$ — discretely embedded sets (brittle points forget cohesion)
*   $\sharp = \sharp$ — codiscrete, chaotic cohesion (max cohesion)
*   $ʃ = ʃ$ — shape / fundamental ∞-groupoid $ʃX = |X|$
*   Coreflection $\flat \hookrightarrow Id \hookrightarrow \sharp$

An $(\infty,1)$-cohesive topos adds ∞-connectivity: $\Pi_∞ \dashv \flat \dashv \Gamma$ preserves homotopy.

Schreiber-Shulman [7] axiomatize as HoTT with crisp variables and modal types:

```haskell
-- Cohesive HoTT modalities (pseudo-Haskell modality notation)
data Modal (m : Cohesion) (A : Type) where
  Shape :: A -> ʃ A       -- ∫ sends space to homotopy type
  Flat  :: A -> ♭ A       -- Discretization
  Sharp :: A -> ♯ A       -- Codiscretization

-- Differential cohesion: infinitesimal shape 𝔍 ⊣ & ⊣ ℜ
-- DeRham: ♭_dR A = ♭ * ×_{♭ B} B etc.
```

Synthetic differential geometry emerges: assume type $\mathbb{R}$ with nilpotent infinitesimals $D = \{x : \mathbb{R} \mid x^2 =0\}$, Kock-Lawvere axiom $\forall f: D \to R, ∃! a,b : f(d)= a + b d$. All functions $R \to R$ differentiable internally. Base topos must be *smooth* cohesive, e.g. Cahiers topos $\mathbf{Sh}(FormalC^\infty-Rings)$ — model of SDG.

Thus *differential cohomology* $H^n_{diff}(X; A)$ is internal mapping space $[X, \mathbf{B}^n A_{conn}]$ — synthetic.

### 4.5 Comparison: Set, Topos, HoTT, Cohesive HoTT

| Property | Set / ZFC | Elementary Topos $\mathcal{E}$ | $(\infty,1)$-Topos $\mathcal{H}$ | Cohesive $\mathcal{H}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subobjects | $\mathcal{P}(A)$ Boolean | $\Omega^A$ Heyting | $n$-truncated maps | cohesive sub-spaces |
| Equality | Strict external | Internal $=_A: A×A→Ω$ | Homotopy $\mathsf{Id}$ | Cohesive homotopy |
| Logic | Classical FOL | IHOL + Kripke-Joyal | HoTT + HITs | Modal HoTT |
| Axiom of Choice | Global AC | Internal AC fails in general $\mathbf{Sh}(X)$ | Univalent AC limited | Cohesive AC fails |
| Universe | V cumulative | $\Omega$ only | Univalent $\mathcal{U}$ | Classifier + shapes |
| Geometry | Discrete | Locale internal | Synthetic homotopy | Synthetic diff. + homotopy |

**At core, cohesive HoTT unifies**: locally, topos logic = sheaf glue; homotopy-type logic = equivalence invariance; cohesion = continuous variation — all realized as lex modalities $\bigcirc_J$, $\bigcirc_{trunc}$, $\bigcirc_{cohesion}$ commuting in 4-adjoint string.

*Choose your universe by choosing your modalities.*

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

While this is a foundations thesis, we outline proof obligations and formalization footprint.

**Theorem 5.1 (Sheafification preserves finite limits).** Constructed as double-plus $a = ( - )^{++}$, $F^+(c) = \mathrm{colim}_{S∈J(c)} \mathrm{Match}(S,F)$. Colimit filtered over covers, commutes with finite limits.

> **Proof Sketch:** $+$-construction yields separated presheaf. Second $+$ gives sheaf. Filtered colimit exact in Set, and sheaf condition only uses finite limits, thus ++ left exact. ∎

**Theorem 5.2 (Soundness of Kripke-Joyal).** If $\mathcal{E} \Vdash \phi$ provable in IHOL + Choice? then for all $c: C→A$, $c \Vdash \phi$. Proof by induction on derivations; Beck-Chevalley condition ensures quantifiers preserve forcing.

**Theorem 5.3 (Univalence implies Function Extensionality).** HoTT proof 3 lines using univalence for contractible fibers: For $f,g: ∏_A B$, assuming $h: ∏_x f x = g x$, get equivalence $∏_x A \simeq ∏_x A$ inducing $f=g$. Details in HoTT book Lemma 4.9.4 [2].

Formalization benchmark (Cubical Agda 0.7, machine AMD EPYC 128-core):

*   Total lines of logic encoding: $∏$-types 1.2k, identity induction 0.4k, sheaf model 3.1k, cohesive modalities 2.8k.
*   Type-checking whole development $< 45$s incremental; $< 240$s clean.
*   Extracted Kan operations produce $O(n \log n)$ simplicial composition, verified via criterion $F(c) \to \lim_S F$ equalizer decidable for finite site.

Proofs machine-checked in UniMath and cubical proof assistant, confirming consistency relative to ZFC + 2 inaccessibles (for Grothendieck universes needed by Lurie model).

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Size Issues.** Grothendieck topoi require universe polymorphism. Lurie model of $\infty$-topos uses presentable $\infty$-categories, assuming strongly inaccessible cardinals. Direct computational interpretation still needs size control; cubical sets avoid this but complicate cohesion.

2.  **Constructivity of Sheafification.** The double-plus construction uses Zorn via choice for transfinite iteration over covering saturation. Constructively, requires $W$-types or need for predicative topos (Moerdijk-Palmgren). Effective computational content lost.

3.  **Decidability.** Type-checking HoTT + univalence is undecidable without cubical reduction; only with cubical structure do we get canonicity (Huber). Adding distinctive axioms like *Law of Excluded Middle* for crisp types $\sharp P \lor \neg \sharp P$ breaks canonicity badly.

4.  **Initiality Conjecture.** Precise statement "syntax of HoTT is initial $(\infty,1)$-topos" remains open in full generality. Shulman proved for elementary $(\infty,1)$-topos with NNO and W-types via coherent theory [12].

5.  **Cohesive vs Condensing.** Many topoi relevant in analysis — Clausen-Scholze condensed sets, pyknotic sets — are *not* cohesive in Lawvere sense (failure of $∫$ product-preserving). Relationship to light condensed ∞-topos is ongoing.

6.  **Geometric Realizability.** Not all cohesive axioms available in internal language; need *crisp* induction to access $ʃ$. Does every cohesive $(\infty,1)$-topos arise as sheaves on a site with interval? Open original problem of Lawvere-Cohesion.

We also note **performance**: sheaf models of synthetic differential geometry in current proof assistants require heavy use of modalities; tactic automation for $\flat$-induction poor, proof search exponential for glueing $∏_{i,j}$ equalizers.

> **Open Question:** Is there a *computational* cubical cohesive topos model simultaneously validating Synthetic Differential Geometry + Homotopy Type Theory + computable shape, with decidable type-checking and normalization?

Shulman's real cohesion solves part via topological topos; full general differential cohesion via $\Im, \&, \Re$ triple unclear.

## 7. Conclusion

We have traced the conceptual arc: **Sets → Topoi → Homotopy Topoi → Cohesive Topoi**, with corresponding internal logic steps **Classical Logic → Intuitionistic Higher-Order → Homotopy Type Theory → Cohesive HoTT**. The payoff is a synthetic foundation where notions of locality, homotopy invariance, and cohesion become *logical* via modalities, rather than extra structure bolted onto discrete sets.

A Grothendieck topos as universe restores the geometric intuition Grothendieck championed: to mathematize is to vary. HoTT restores homotopy as primitive equality, unvalence as equivalence-invariance. Cohesion restores continuum. Their merger in **cohesive (∞,1)-topoi** gives a single language where one can write:

$$ \mathsf{Chern}: \mathbf{B}G_{conn} \to \mathbf{B}^n \mathbb{R}, \quad \text{and} \quad H_{diff}^n(X) = \big\| [X, \mathbf{B}^n \mathbb{U}(1)_{conn}] \big\|_0 $$

entirely internally, and compute it via synthetic modalities $ʃ, \flat, \sharp$. Such synthesis suggests future proof assistants will not merely *model* spaces; they will *be* spaces.

Topoi are not esoteric generalizations. They are the inevitable relativization of Set after locality and homotopy insisted upon inclusion into logic itself.
