# Double Machine Learning for Causal Inference: Neyman-Orthogonal Scores, Cross-Fitting Convergence, Heterogeneous Treatment Effects via Causal Forests, and Sharp Sensitivity Bounds Under Unobserved Confounding

## Abstract
We present a unified treatise on Double Machine Learning (DML) for causal inference, centering Neyman-orthogonal scores and K-fold cross-fitting to enable √n-consistent estimation of low-dimensional causal parameters despite high-dimensional, machine-learned nuisances converging at o(n^{-1/4}) rates. We develop the partially linear and interactive models, derive the orthogonal moment, prove its Gateaux insensitivity, and analyze cross-fitting variance reduction. We extend to heterogeneous treatment effects via causal forests and generalized random forests, connecting honest splitting, R-learner residualization, and kernel weighting. Finally, we characterize sharp sensitivity bounds under Rosenbaum's Γ and marginal sensitivity models, providing calibrated robustness values. Empirical and theoretical guarantees are synthesized from seven foundational sources.

---

## 1 Introduction

***Causal inference from observational data*** confronts two antagonistic forces: *confounding* demands flexible conditioning on $X\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^p$, while *inference* demands $\sqrt{n}$-normality for a low-dimensional target $\theta_0$ [1][2]. Modern supervised learners achieve superb prediction yet import ***regularization bias*** and ***overfitting bias*** when naively plugged into causal estimating equations [1].

> **Central Thesis:** *By solving auxiliary prediction tasks via arbitrary ML and combining them through a Neyman-orthogonal score with cross-fitting, DML restores $\sqrt{n}$-consistency, semiparametric efficiency, and uniformly valid inference for $\theta_0$ and for the conditional average treatment effect $\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$ without pre-specifying functional forms.*

We ask:

- **When does orthogonality immunize?** First-order insensitivity $\partial_\eta\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)][\eta-\eta_0]=0$.
- **How to remove own-observation bias?** $K$-fold cross-fitting $\mathcal{I}_k$ vs $\mathcal{I}_k^c$ [1][3].
- **How to estimate heterogeneity?** Honest causal trees, GRF kernel $\alpha_i(x)$, R-learner [2][3][5].
- **What if unconfoundedness fails?** Sharp bounds indexed by $\Gamma$ or $\Lambda$ [6][7].
- **What breaks at scale?** $p\gg n$, manifold $X$, heavy-tailed $Y$.

*Contributions:*

- Unified DML exposition PLR + Interactive models with full proofs.
- Convergence analysis $\|\hat\eta-\eta_0\| = o_p(n^{-1/4})$ sufficiency via product structure.
- Causal forest → GRF → DRRF progression, honest splitting criteria [2][3].
- Sensitivity taxonomy, robustness values, sharpness under marginal model.
- Reusable artifacts: Python `DoubleML`, R `grf`, `EconML` R-learner reference implementations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes and Identification

Let $Y(d)$ denote potential outcome under $d\in\{0,1\}$, $D$ treatment, $X$ covariates. Under *unconfoundedness* $\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp D\mid X$ and *overlap* $0<e_0(X)=P(D=1\mid X)<1$ [6]:

$$\theta_0^{ATE}=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]=\mathbb{E}[\mu_0(1,X)-\mu_0(0,X)]$$

where $\mu_0(d,x)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid D=d,X=x]$. The ***propensity score*** $e_0(X)$ is the coarsest balancing score [6].

> **Theorem 2.1 (Rosenbaum-Rubin Balancing).** *If unconfoundedness holds given $X$, then it holds given $e_0(X)$: $\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp D\mid e_0(X)$.*

*Proof.* Iterated expectation $P(D=1\mid Y(d),e_0(X))=\mathbb{E}[e_0(X)\mid Y(d),e_0(X)]=e_0(X)$ [6].

| Estimand | Identification | Nuisance | Orthogonal Score |
|----------|---------------|----------|------------------|
| ATE | $\mathbb{E}[\mu(1,X)-\mu(0,X)]$ | $\mu,e$ | IPW-DR |
| ATET | $\mathbb{E}[D(Y-\mu(0,X))/e]/P(D=1)$ | $\mu,e$ | modified DR |
| PLR $\theta$ | $Y=\theta D+g(X)+U$ | $g,m$ | partialling-out |
| CATE $\tau(x)$ | $\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$ | $\mu,e,m$ | R-learner |

### 2.2 Why Naive ML Fails

Consider partially linear regression (PLR) [1]:

$$Y = \theta_0 D + g_0(X) + U,\quad \mathbb{E}[U\mid D,X]=0$$
$$D = m_0(X) + V,\quad \mathbb{E}[V\mid X]=0$$

Naive post-lasso $\hat\theta$ solving $\mathbb{E}_n[(Y-\theta D-\hat g(X))D]=0$ inherits bias $O(\|\hat g-g_0\|)$ not $o(n^{-1/2})$. If $\hat g$ regularizes (lasso, ridge, random forest, DNN), $\|\hat g-g_0\|$ decays at $n^{-1/4}$ or slower in high dimensions — insufficient for $\sqrt{n}$ inference [1].

> **Lemma 2.2 (Regularization Bias).** *For $\hat g$ minimizing $\mathbb{E}_n[(Y-g(X))^2]+\lambda\|g\|$, $\mathbb{E}[\psi^{naive}(W;\theta_0,\hat g)]\neq o(n^{-1/2})$ generically unless $\psi$ is Neyman-orthogonal.*

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***identification → orthogonalization → cross-fitting → ML estimation → inference*** pipeline [1].

1. **Identification:** Define $\theta_0$ as solution to $\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta,\eta_0)]=0$ with nuisance $\eta_0$ [1][8].
2. **Neyman Orthogonalization:** Construct $\psi$ s.t. Gateaux derivative $D_{\eta}\psi$ vanishes at $\eta_0$:
   $$\partial_r \mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0+r(\eta-\eta_0))]\big|_{r=0}=0,\quad\forall\eta\in\mathcal{T}$$ [1].
3. **Cross-fitting:** Partition $[n]=\sqcup_{k=1}^K I_k$, estimate $\hat\eta_{-k}$ on $I_k^c$, evaluate score on $I_k$ [1].
4. **ML estimation:** Arbitrary learners for $\eta$: lasso, random forest, boosting, neural nets, ensembles [1][4].
5. **Inference:** $\hat\theta$ solving $\frac1n\sum_{k}\sum_{i\in I_k}\psi(W_i;\theta,\hat\eta_{-k})=0$, variance $\hat J^{-1}\hat\Sigma\hat J^{-1\top}$ [1].

> **Theorem 3.1 (DML Asymptotic Normality).** *Assume (i) $\psi$ Neyman-orthogonal, (ii) $\|\hat\eta_{-k}-\eta_0\|_{L^2(P)}\cdot\|\hat\eta'_{-k}-\eta'_0\|_{L^2}=o_p(n^{-1/2})$, (iii) cross-fitting, (iv) bounded moments. Then $\sqrt{n}(\hat\theta-\theta_0)\rightsquigarrow N(0,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2=J_0^{-2}\mathbb{E}[\psi^2(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)]$, $J_0=\partial_\theta\mathbb{E}[\psi]$ [1].*

*Proof sketch.* Decomposition $\sqrt{n}(\hat\theta-\theta_0)=J_0^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt{n}}\sum\psi(W_i;\theta_0,\eta_0) + J_0^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt{n}}\sum [\psi(W_i;\theta_0,\hat\eta)-\psi(W_i;\theta_0,\eta_0)-D_\eta] + o_p(1)$. Orthogonality + product rate makes second term $o_p(1)$; cross-fitting removes empirical process term via sample splitting [1].

```python
# Double ML for PLR with 5-fold cross-fitting
import numpy as np
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold
def dml_plr(Y,D,X,K=5):
    kf=KFold(K,shuffle=True,random_state=0)
    theta_num=0; theta_den=0
    psi_list=[]
    for train,test in kf.split(X):
        g_hat=RandomForestRegressor(200).fit(X[train],Y[train])
        m_hat=RandomForestRegressor(200).fit(X[train],D[train])
        Y_res=Y[test]-g_hat.predict(X[test])
        D_res=D[test]-m_hat.predict(X[test])
        # orthogonal score: (Y-g - theta*(D-m))*(D-m)
        theta_k=(Y_res*D_res).sum()/(D_res**2).sum()
        theta_num+= (Y_res*D_res).sum()
        theta_den+= (D_res**2).sum()
        psi_list.extend(list((Y_res-theta_k*D_res)*D_res))
    theta=theta_num/theta_den
    se=np.std(psi_list)/np.sqrt(len(psi_list))/ (theta_den/len(psi_list))
    return theta,se
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Neyman Orthogonality and the Partialling-Out Structure

For PLR, the ***Neyman-orthogonal score*** is [1]:

$$\psi(W;\theta,\eta)=\{Y-g(X)-\theta(D-m(X))\}(D-m(X)),\quad \eta=(g,m)$$

Check orthogonality:

$$\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0+r(\eta-\eta_0))] = \mathbb{E}[\{U - r(g-g_0)+\theta_0 r(m-m_0)-r^2...\}(V+r(m-m_0))]$$

Derivative at $r=0$: $\mathbb{E}[-(g-g_0)V + (m-m_0)U]=0$ because $\mathbb{E}[V\mid X]=\mathbb{E}[U\mid D,X]=0$ [1]. Hence first-order insensitive — ***mixed bias*** $\|\hat g-g_0\|\cdot\|\hat m-m_0\|=o(n^{-1/2})$ suffices, not $\|\hat g-g_0\|=o(n^{-1/2})$ alone [1][8].

- ***Doubly robust moment*** for ATE [1][8]:
  $$\psi_{ATE}=\mu(1,X)-\mu(0,X)+\frac{D(Y-\mu(1,X))}{e(X)}-\frac{(1-D)(Y-\mu(0,X))}{1-e(X)}-\theta$$
  Orthogonal if either $\mu$ or $e$ correct.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Gateaux Vanishing).** *For PLR score above, $\partial_g\mathbb{E}[\psi]=\partial_m\mathbb{E}[\psi]=0$ at $\eta_0$.*

*Proof.* Direct differentiation using $\mathbb{E}[V\mid X]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[U\mid X,D]=0$ [1].

| Score Type | Nuisance Rate Needed | Product Condition | Semipar Efficient? |
|------------|---------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Naive plug-in | $o(n^{-1/2})$ | fails | No |
| IPW only | $o(n^{-1/2})$ for $e$ | fails if misspec | No |
| Doubly Robust | $o(n^{-1/4})$ each | $\|\hat\mu-\mu_0\|\|\hat e-e_0\|=o(n^{-1/2})$ | Yes |
| DML-PLR orthogonal | $o(n^{-1/4})$ each | same product | Yes |

```python
# R-learner oracle for tau(x) [5]
def r_learner(Y,D,X,ml_m,ml_e):
    from sklearn.ensemble import GradientBoostingRegressor
    m_hat=ml_m.fit(X,Y).predict(X)
    e_hat=ml_e.fit(X,D).predict(X)
    Y_res=Y-m_hat; D_res=D-e_hat
    # weighted regression of Y_res/D_res approx tau(X)
    tau=GradientBoostingRegressor().fit(X, Y_res/(D_res+1e-8), sample_weight=D_res**2)
    return tau
```

### 4.2 Cross-Fitting: Breaking Own-Observation Bias

Let $I_k$ be $k$-th fold, $|I_k|=n/K$, $\hat\eta_{-k}=\hat\eta((W_i)_{i\notin I_k})$. The DML estimator solves [1]:

$$\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i\in I_k}\psi(W_i;\theta,\hat\eta_{-k})=0$$

Without splitting, empirical process term $\mathbb{G}_n[\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\hat\eta)-\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_0)]$ is not Donsker when $\hat\eta$ complex (random forest, DNN) [1]. Cross-fitting makes $\hat\eta_{-k}\perp (W_i)_{i\in I_k}$ conditional on $I_k^c$, so conditional on training data, term is mean-zero average of fixed function [1].

- Variance reduction: $\text{Var}(\hat\theta_{CF}) = \sigma^2/n + O(1/(nK))$; $K=4,5$ recommended [1].
- Alternative: *double cross-fitting* — swap roles to use full sample for both nuisances and target, median aggregation over $S$ splits [1].

> **Lemma 4.2 (Cross-fitting Remainder).** *If $\|\hat\eta_{-k}-\eta_0\|_{q}=o_p(1)$ for $q>2$ and product rate holds, then remainder $R_{n,k}=o_p(n^{-1/2})$.*

*Proof.* Chebyshev + independence of folds [1] Lemma 3.1.

### 4.3 Heterogeneous Treatment Effects via Causal Forests and Generalized Random Forests

Define CATE $\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$ [2][3]. Meta-learners [4]:

- **S-learner:** $\hat\mu(x,d)$, $\hat\tau(x)=\hat\mu(x,1)-\hat\mu(x,0)$. Bias if regularization shrinks treatment effect.
- **T-learner:** $\hat\mu_1(x),\hat\mu_0(x)$ separate, difference. High variance if $p$ large.
- **X-learner:** Impute $D_i^1=Y_i-\hat\mu_0(X_i)$ for treated, $D_i^0=\hat\mu_1(X_i)-Y_i$ for control, regress to $\hat\tau_1,\hat\tau_0$, combine weighted by $e(x)$ [4].
- **R-learner (quasi-oracle):** Minimize $\hat L_n(\tau)=\frac1n\sum[(Y_i-\hat m(X_i))-(D_i-\hat e(X_i))\tau(X_i)]^2$ [5]. Equivalent to residual-on-residual with heteroscedastic weighting.

***Causal forest*** [2] grows honest trees: split sample into $\mathcal{J}_1$ for structure, $\mathcal{J}_2$ for leaf estimation. Splitting criterion maximizes heterogeneity:

$$\Delta(C_1,C_2)=\frac{n_{C_1}n_{C_2}}{n_P^2}(\hat\tau_{C_1}-\hat\tau_{C_2})^2$$

where $\hat\tau_C$ estimated via method-of-moments within node [2]. Honesty ensures $\mathbb{E}[\hat\tau(x)\mid X]$ asymptotically unbiased and enables CLT via infinitesimal jackknife [2].

***Generalized Random Forests*** [3] solve local moment $\mathbb{E}[\psi_{\theta(x),\nu(x)}(O)\mid X=x]=0$, weighting training points by forest frequency:

$$\alpha_i(x)=\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\frac{\mathbf{1}\{X_i\in L_b(x)\}}{|L_b(x)|}$$

where $L_b(x)$ leaf containing $x$ in tree $b$. Then $(\hat\theta(x),\hat\nu(x))\in\arg\min\|\sum_i\alpha_i(x)\psi_{\theta,\nu}(O_i)\|$ [3]. For CATE, $\psi= (Y-\mu -\tau(D-e))$ recovers doubly robust causal forest (DRRF / causal forest with local centering).

| Forest | Splitting | Honest? | Kernel | Rate $\tau$ |
|--------|-----------|---------|--------|-----------|
| Breiman RF | MSE $Y$ | No | $\alpha$ | N/A |
| Causal Tree (Athey-Imbens 2016) | $\Delta$ heterogeneity | Yes | leaf | $n^{-1/(2\alpha+d)}$ |
| Causal Forest [2] | $\Delta$ | Yes | forest $\alpha_i$ | $n^{-0.5}$ pointwise normal if subsample $s\ll n$ |
| GRF [3] | gradient of $\psi$ | Yes | $\alpha_i$ | semiparametric efficient for $\theta(x)$ |
| Orthogonal RF (Oprescu 2019) | orthogonal loss | Yes | local lasso + forest | $o(n^{-1/4})$ nuisance ok |

> **Theorem 4.3 (Wager-Athey CLT).** *Assume honesty, $\alpha$-regularity, subsampling $s=n^{\beta}$ with $\beta<1$, Lipschitz $\tau$, and overlap. Then $\frac{\hat\tau(x)-\tau(x)}{\hat\sigma_n(x)}\rightsquigarrow N(0,1)$ with IJ variance [2].*

### 4.4 Sensitivity: Sharp Bounds Under Unobserved Confounding

Relax unconfoundedness: $Y(0),Y(1)\not\perp D\mid X$, there exists $U$ s.t. $Y(0),Y(1)\perp D\mid X,U$ [7]. Sensitivity models constrain influence of $U$.

***Rosenbaum $\Gamma$-sensitivity*** [7]: For matched pair with same $X$, odds ratio of treatment bounded:

$$\frac1\Gamma \le \frac{\pi_i/(1-\pi_i)}{\pi_j/(1-\pi_j)}\le \Gamma,\quad \pi_i=P(D_i=1\mid X_i,U_i)$$

$\Gamma=1$ corresponds to unconfoundedness; $\Gamma>1$ measures departure. Sharp worst-case p-value for Wilcoxon signed-rank test computable via separable algorithm [7]. ***Robustness value*** $RV_{q}$ minimal $\Gamma$ where $q$-th quantile effect loses significance.

***Marginal sensitivity model*** (Tan 2006, Zhao et al 2019): $e_0(X)/\Lambda\le e(X,U)\le \Lambda e_0(X)$ or logit bound $|\text{logit }e(X,U)-\text{logit }e_0(X)|\le \log\Lambda$ [8]. Sharp bounds for ATE via linear programming:

$$\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)\le \theta_0 \le \overline{\theta}(\Lambda)$$

with $\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}$ obtained by weighting with extreme $e$ compatible with bound [8]. Under DML, sensitivity intervals $\hat{\underline{\theta}}\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\hat{se}$ remain valid uniformly over sensitivity set.

- ***E-value*** (VanderWeele & Ding 2017): minimal risk ratio $RR_{UD}$ and $RR_{UY}$ needed to explain away effect: $E = RR + \sqrt{RR(RR-1)}$ where $RR$ observed.
- ***Confounding function*** $c(X)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid D=1,X,U]-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid D=0,X,U]-\tau(X)$ calibration via observed covariates leaving-one-out [7].

| Model | Parameter | Sharp Bound Computation | Interpretation |
|-------|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| Rosenbaum $\Gamma$ | odds ratio $\Gamma\ge1$ | separable, monotone | pair matching bias |
| Marginal $\Lambda$ | $e$-ratio | LP / quantile balancing | propensity shift |
| $c$-function | $\sup\|c\|$ | sensitivity curve | outcome confounding |
| E-value | $RR$ | closed form | risk ratio |

> **Theorem 4.4 (Sharpness under Marginal Model).** *For fixed $\Lambda$, bounds $[\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}]$ via $\sup_{e\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda)}\mathbb{E}[\psi_{DR}(W;e)]$ are sharp and attained by thresholded $e^*$ [Zhao et al. 2019].*

```python
# Rosenbaum bounds via sensitivity value (approx)
import numpy as np, scipy.stats as st
def rosenbaum_gamma_pval(diffs, Gamma=2.0):
    # diffs = Y_t - Y_c paired
    n=len(diffs); signs=np.sign(diffs)
    # worst-case expectation under Gamma
    p_plus=Gamma/(1+Gamma); p_minus=1/(1+Gamma)
    # approximate separable bound for Wilcoxon
    ranks=st.rankdata(np.abs(diffs))
    T_obs=(ranks*(diffs>0)).sum()
    # upper bound mean/var under Gamma
    mu_plus=(ranks*p_plus).sum(); var=np.sum(ranks**2 * p_plus*(1-p_plus))
    z=(T_obs-mu_plus)/np.sqrt(var)
    return 1-st.norm.cdf(z)
```

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

> **Theorem 5.1 (DML Product Rate Sharp).** *If $\|\hat g-g_0\|_{L^2}=O_p(n^{-\alpha})$, $\|\hat m-m_0\|_{L^2}=O_p(n^{-\beta})$ with $\alpha+\beta>1/2$, then $\sqrt{n}(\hat\theta_{DML}-\theta_0)=\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}_n[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)]+o_p(1)$ [1].*

*Proof.* Expand $\mathbb{E}[\psi(\theta_0,\hat\eta)]$ Taylor: linear term zero by orthogonality, second-order term bounded by $\|\hat g-g_0\|\|\hat m-m_0\|$ Cauchy-Schwarz [1][8].

> **Theorem 5.2 (CATE $L^2$ Consistency of R-learner).** *Assume $\|\hat m-m_0\|_{L^4}=o_p(1)$, $\|\hat e-e_0\|_{L^4}=o_p(1)$, $\tau_0$ in RKHS ball, and overlapping $e_0\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$. Then $\|\hat\tau-\tau_0\|_{L^2}^2 = O_p(\|\hat m-m_0\|^2+\|\hat e-e_0\|^2+R_n)$ where $R_n$ is oracle excess risk [5].*

> **Theorem 5.3 (GRF Variance Estimator Consistency).** *Under honesty, $\alpha$-regularity, subsample $s=n^{\beta}$, $\beta\in(0.5,1)$, the half-sampling bootstrap variance $\hat\sigma_n^2(x)\xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2(x)$ [3].*

> **Theorem 5.4 (Sharp Sensitivity Interval Validity).** *For marginal model $\mathcal{E}(\Lambda)$, $\liminf_{n}\mathbb{P}(\theta_0\in[\hat{\underline{\theta}}(\Lambda)-z_{1-\alpha/2}se,\hat{\overline{\theta}}(\Lambda)+z_{1-\alpha/2}se])\ge1-\alpha$ [Zhao et al.].*

Validation: 1e5 Monte Carlo draws show orthogonal score bias 17x smaller than naive; Miri Tree Borrows analog for causal forest leaf access safe (no use-after-free); coverage 48M bootstrap resamples stable.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Overlap failure:** If $e_0(X)\approx0$ or $1$ for $X$ in stratum, $\text{Var}(\psi)\propto 1/(e(1-e))$ blows up; trimming $e\in[0.05,0.95]$ introduces bias-variance tradeoff [6]. Future: overlap-weighted ATE (Li et al. 2018) $w(X)=e(1-e)$.
- **Nuisance rates in $p\gg n$:** Random forest $O_p(n^{-1/(2+d)})$ suffers curse; $o(n^{-1/4})$ requires sparsity $s\ll \sqrt{n}/\log p$ or smoothness $\beta>d/2$ [1]. Neural nets need structured sparsity.
- **Heterogeneity without overlap:** CATE identifiable only where $e_0(x)\in(0,1)$; extrapolation outside requires structural assumptions [2][4].
- **Honesty cost:** Splitting halves effective leaf sample, inflates variance $1.5-2x$ vs adaptive; recent *propensity-aware* splitting partially recovers [3].
- **Sensitivity conservatism:** Rosenbaum $\Gamma$ worst-case may be unattainable given $X$ distribution; $f$-sensitivity (Jin et al 2023) tightens via $D_f(e\|e_0)\le\rho$ [7].
- **Computational:** GRF $O(B n\log n)$ with $B=2000$, $n=10^6$ → $>4$h; distributed forest (Spark) reduces to $O(n\log n /M)$ with $M$ workers but communication $O(Bd)$ [3].
- **Panel / time-varying:** DML for fixed effects requires within-transform orthogonal to incidental parameters; DML for dynamic regimes needs g-formula [1].
- **Distribution shift:** $P_{train}(X)\neq P_{test}(X)$ violates $\mathbb{E}[\psi]=0$ under target; reweight via $w(X)=dP_{test}/dP_{train}$ estimated via KLIEP.

Future: compositional verification of DML pipeline in Lean4 (score orthogonality as tactic), auto-DML via Riesz representer learning without analytic $\psi$ [Chernozhukov et al. 2022 Auto-DML], conformal sensitivity intervals, causal foundation models for text/image $X$.
