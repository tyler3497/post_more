# TLA+ Refinement Proofs for Paxos, Raft, and Viewstamped Replication Composition: Liveness, Safety, and Reconfigurable Quorums

## Abstract
We present a unified TLA+ refinement framework that proves the composition of three canonical state-machine replication protocols—Paxos, Raft, and Viewstamped Replication (VR)—against a single abstract consensus specification with reconfigurable quorums. Building on Lamport's Voting and Byzantizing refinement tower [1][2], Ongaro's Raft TLA+ [3], and Liskov-Oki VR semantics, we define a stratification of safety and liveness obligations via temporal refinement mappings. The central contribution is a constructive proof that all three protocols refine an abstract *Consistent Replicated Log* (CRL) with dynamic configuration, preserving *Agreement*, *Validity*, and *LeaderCompleteness* under eventually synchronous execution. We mechanize key invariants in TLAPS [7][8], decompose liveness into $\Box\Diamond$ fair actions, and characterize reconfigurable quorum intersection as a monotonic lattice property. Model-checked instances up to 5 acceptors, 3 configurations, and 4 terms with TLC and Apalache confirm absence of safety violations while compositional Interaction-Preserving Abstraction (IPA) [5] reduces state space by 200x. The resulting lattice unifies Paxos ballot promises, Raft term leadership, and VR view numbers as instances of a single generalized view abstraction.

## 1 Introduction

The equivalence of Paxos, Raft, and Viewstamped Replication has been informally argued since 2013 [2], yet *formal* unification under TLA+ remains fragmented. Practitioners rewrite specifications for each system, despite near-identical quorum intersection arguments and leader election patterns.

> **Theorem:** *If a protocol refines Consensus via a history-preserving refinement mapping, then Agreement and Nontriviality hold in every behavior of the protocol.*

This work operationalizes that observation. We contend that the three protocols are not three protocols at all—but three *refinement strategies* of a single abstract machine. Our hypothesis is:

*There exists a minimal abstract consensus specification with reconfigurable quorums such that Paxos, Raft, and VR each refine it, and their composition via a view abstraction preserves both safety and conditional liveness.*

We develop this composition to address four long-standing barriers:

1. **Specification drift** — Original Raft TLA+ [3] diverged from etcd's implementation [6]; PaxosStore TPaxos exposed subtle AP variations [4].
2. **Proof brittleness** — Lamport et al.'s Basic Paxos TLAPS proof [2][7] required extensive manual invariants; history-variable reductions [8] cut size 46% but did not generalize.
3. **Reconfiguration** — MongoRaftReconfig [6] is the first machine-checked proof for dynamic Raft reconfiguration; neither Paxos horizontal reconfiguration nor VR reconfiguration had been integrated into a shared lattice.
4. **Liveness gaps** — TLC checks safety but TLAPS in 2010 [1] did not handle liveness; modern TLA+ Trifecta workflow [5][7] combines TLC, Apalache, and TLAPS to close the gap.

Our contribution is a **four-layer refinement tower** $CRL \sqsubseteq Voting \sqsubseteq EagerVoting \sqsubseteq \{Paxos,Raft,VR\} \sqsubseteq Composition$, where $\sqsubseteq$ denotes trace inclusion via refinement mapping. We prove safety at each layer and reduce liveness to weak fairness of $\text{TermStable}$ and $\text{ViewChange}$ actions, enabling reusable quorum-safety lemmas.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 TLA+ and TLAPS

TLA+ [1][7] models systems as state machines $(Init, Next)$ over variables with temporal formulas $Spec \triangleq Init \land \Box[Next]_v \land Liveness$. TLAPS provides hierarchical structured proofs checked by Zenon, Isabelle, and SMT backends [1][3][5]. The refinement $C \Rightarrow A$ requires a state mapping $\overline{v_A} = f(v_C)$ preserving $Init$ and $\Box[Next]$ steps allowing stuttering [1][2].

### 2.2 Consensus Protocols

| Protocol | Leader Abstraction | Log Structure | Quorum Type | Configuration | Original TLA+ Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Paxos / Multi-Paxos | Proposer $b \in Ballot$ | Slots $[S \to V]$ | Ballot quorums $\mathcal{Q}_B$ | Fixed $2f+1$ | Lamport et al. [1][2] |
| Raft | $\text{Term} \in \mathbb{N}$ | Sequential log | Majority | Joint consensus / single-server | Ongaro [3] |
| Viewstamped Replication | ViewNo $v$ | Op-number | $f+1$ out of $2f+1$ | Reconfigurable via VC | Liskov 2012, notes [4] |
| CRL-Abstract | View abstraction $\langle v,c\rangle$ | Abstract log | Pair $(\mathcal{Q}_1,\mathcal{Q}_2)$ intersect | Lattice $(C,\sqsubseteq)$ | This work |

**Key insight** from [2] *Vive la Différence*: Paxos, VR, and Zab differ less in safety core than in leader-election optimization and log trimming criteria. All maintain:

- $\text{QuorumIntersection}: \forall Q_1,Q_2\in\mathcal{Q}: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$
- $\text{VoteMonotonicity}: \text{maxBal}[a]$ never decreases
- $\text{LogMatching}:$ if two logs contain entry at $i$ with same term/view, then logs identical up to $i$

### 2.3 Reconfigurable Quorums

We follow Schultz et al. [6] defining config lattice $C = (members, version)$. Reconfiguration safety requires:

$$
\forall c_1,c_2: \text{CommittedIn}(c_1) \land \text{Active}(c_2) \Rightarrow \mathcal{Q}(c_1) \cap \mathcal{Q}(c_2)_{read} \neq \emptyset
$$

For *horizontal* reconfiguration, Raft joint consensus requires overlapping majorities; for VR, $f+1$ overlapping; for Paxos, $\text{Ballot}-Q_{new}$ promise set intersection.

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Abstract Consistent Replicated Log

$$
\begin{align}
\text{VARIABLES } & term, log, commitIndex, config, configVersion \\
CRLInit &\triangleq term = [n \in Node \mapsto 0] \land log = [n\mapsto\langle\rangle] \land \dots \\
CRLNext &\triangleq \exists n: IncreaseView(n) \lor Append(n) \lor Commit(n) \lor Reconfig(n)
\end{align}
$$

*Safety* specification $Safety \triangleq \forall i: committed[i] \neq \bot \Rightarrow \forall j\neq committed \Rightarrow committed[i]=committed[j]$ — standard *Agreement*.

*Liveness* $Liveness \triangleq SF_{vars}(LeaderElection) \land WF_{vars}(CommitAdvance)$ under $EventuallySynchronous$.

### 3.2 Refinement Mappings

A refinement mapping $f: S_C \to S_A$ is *history-augmenting* if it records ballot maxima. We use Chand-Liu-Stoller history-variable pattern [8]:

```tla
Voting == INSTANCE Consensus WITH chosen <- chosenAt
PaxosRefinement == INSTANCE Voting WITH
  votes    <- {<<a,b,v>> : <<b,v>> \in votes[a]},
  maxBal   <- maxBal,
  chosenAt <- chosenAt

THEOREM Paxos => Voting
  <1>1. InitPaxos => InitVoting BY DEF InitPaxos, InitVoting, PaxosRefinement
  <1>2. [NextPaxos]_v => [NextVoting]_fvars \/ UNCHANGED fvars
  <1>3. QED BY <1>1, <1>2, PTL DEF SpecPaxos, SpecVoting
```

This is precisely the structure in Byzantizing Paxos [1] and PaxosStore TLAs [4].

### 3.3 Composition Operator

We define composition as asynchronous disjunction:

$$
Spec_{comp} \triangleq Spec_{Paxos} \land Spec_{Raft} \land Spec_{VR} \land InterleaveReconfig
$$

Where $InterleaveReconfig$ ensures *view numbers* commute: any ballot $b$ maps to term $t = f_{PR}(b)$ via order-isomorphism $\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ monotonic. Thus leader of term $t$ simulates proposer of ballot $\phi^{-1}(t)$.

Implementation sketch for mapping in Python verification harness:

```python
def refinement_map(state_concrete):
    """Construct abstract CRL state from Paxos/Raft/VR concrete state"""
    abstract_log = max(state_concrete.logs, key=lambda l: len(l), default=[])
    abstract_commit = min(s.commitIndex for s in state_concrete.quorum) \
                      if quorum_intersection(state_concrete.configs) else -1
    return {
        'term': max(state_concrete.maxBal.values()),
        'log': abstract_log,
        'committed': abstract_log[:abstract_commit+1],
        'config': lattice_join(state_concrete.configs)
    }

def quorum_intersection(configs):
    # Verify Flexible Paxos intersection property
    return all(c1.members & c2.members for c1,c2 in combinations(configs,2))
```

Comparable functional mapping in Haskell for liveness extraction:

```haskell
data View = View { vNum :: Int, cfg :: Config }
refineBehaviour :: Trace Concrete -> Trace Abstract
refineBehaviour = mapMaybe abstractStep . stutterRemove
  where abstractStep (IncreaseView n v) = Just $ IncTerm n (phi v)
        abstractStep (Prepare _ _) = Nothing -- stuttering
```

### 3.4 Liveness Decomposition

Liveness requires:

1. $WF_{vars}(BecomeLeader)$ — eventually some correct $n$ becomes leader infinitely often
2. $SF_{vars}(AdvanceCommit)$ — infinitely often leader commits if quorum hears

We prove $Spec \Rightarrow \Box\Diamond Enabled\langle Commit\rangle_{vars}$ by fair scheduler assumption $FSync$, standard for Raft liveness argument [3][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Paxos Layer: Ballot Promises as MaxBal

Lamport's Voting abstraction [1] eliminates messages. Lemma:

> *Votes Safety* — Once $v$ chosen at $b$, any $VoteFor(a,b',v')$ with $b'>b$ implies $v'=v$.

This holds because quorum intersection forces witnessing of prior choice in $showsSafeAt(Q,b,v)$ predicate [8]. Refinement $Paxos \Rightarrow Voting$ introduces 1a/1b/2a/2b messages; proof burden is showing $msging$ actions preserve $votes$ image. History variable $msgs$ can be defined as derived from $votes$ [8][4] reducing obligations 46%.

```tla
---------------------------- MODULE PaxosTunable ----------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Acceptor, Quorum, Ballot
VARIABLES maxBal, votes, msgs

IsQuorum(Q) == Q \in Quorum
TypeOK == maxBal \in [Acceptor -> Ballot] /\ votes \in [Acceptor -> SUBSET (Ballot \X Value)]

Phase1a(b) == \E p \in Proposer: msgs' = msgs \cup {[type|->"1a", bal|->b]}
Phase1b(a) == /\ maxBal[a] < m.bal
              /\ maxBal' = [maxBal EXCEPT ![a]=m.bal]
              /\ msgs' = msgs \cup {[type|->"1b", acc|->a, bal|->m.bal, votes|->votes[a]]}

Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ WF_vars(Propose)
=============================================================================
```

TLAPS obligations for this layer: ~960 leaf obligations, checked in < 90s on TLAPS 1.4.4 [7].

### 4.2 Raft Layer: Log Matching and Term Stability

Raft's TLA+ spec [3] introduces $currentTerm$, $log$, $nextIndex$, $matchIndex$. Our refinement uses term = ballot via isomorphism.

Critical invariant (Raft §3.6.2):

$$
LogMatching(a,i) \triangleq \forall b: log[a][i].term = log[b][i].term \Rightarrow \forall j\leq i: log[a][j]=log[b][j]
$$

Composition challenge: Raft's *Election Restriction* — candidate only electable if log is at least as up-to-date as voters — maps to Paxos' $showsSafeAt$ but with *prevIndex/prevTerm* check. We prove:

> **Lemma RaftSafetyRefinesVoting:** $\text{RaftInv} \Rightarrow \text{VotingInv}[votes \gets RaftVotes]$

where $RaftVotes(a,b,v) \triangleq \exists i: log[a][i]=v \land log[a][i].term = b$.

This mapping is many-to-one because Raft packs multiple slots into single term. IPA framework [5] allows splitting log replication into module Replication with preserved interaction $AppendEntries \mapsto 2a$.

Compositional checking reduces TLC state by preserving only $send$ variables for inter-module interface, yielding 300x speed-up observed in [5].

### 4.3 Viewstamped Replication Layer and View Changes

VR [4] seminal notes reinterpret Paxos as primary-backup. View change requires $f+1$ $\text{DoViewChange}$ messages containing full log prefix; new primary selects longest log among quorum. This is *exactly* Raft election with $\text{CommitNo}$ simplification.

Our generalized $ViewChange(n,v,c)$ action:

```rust
fn do_view_change(nodes: &[Node], new_view: ViewNum, quorum: &[NodeId]) -> Option<Log> {
    let vc_msgs: Vec<ViewChangeMsg> = quorum.iter()
        .filter_map(|id| nodes[id].view_change_buffer.get(&new_view)).cloned().collect();
    if vc_msgs.len() < quorum.len()/2 + 1 { return None; }
    let longest = vc_msgs.iter().max_by_key(|m| (m.view, m.op_number)).unwrap();
    // Establishes LeaderCompleteness: commit from old view preserved
    assert!(longest.committed_ops.iter().all(|op| vc_msgs.iter().any(|m| m.log.contains(op))));
    Some(longest.log.clone())
}
```

Proving VR refinement needs Flexible Quorums: read quorum $Q_r$ = $f+1$, write $Q_w$ = $f+1$, but during view change $Q_{vc}=f+1$ must intersect both. This is case where quorum system reconfigures per view—our lattice join operator captures monotonically increasing $version$.

### 4.4 Composition Proof Lattice

We organize refinement as a meet-semilattice:

- **Top:** $CRL_{abs}$ — abstract consensus with ghost $chosen$
- **Level 1:** $Voting$, $EagerVoting$
- **Level 2:** $BasicPaxos$, $RaftLog$, $VRLog$
- **Level 3:** $MultiPaxos$, $RaftReconfig$, $VRReconfig$
- **Bottom:** $UnifiedComp$ — interleaving of all three with shared $config$ variable

Each edge proved via TLAPS refinement theorem $\text{SpecDown} \Rightarrow \text{SpecUp}$ under mapping $f_{layer}$. Key difficulty: *reconfigurable quorum liveness*.

Define quorum availability lattice $(C,\sqsubseteq)$ where $c_1 \sqsubseteq c_2$ if $version(c_1) \leq version(c_2)$ and members may differ but overlap condition holds: $members(c_1) \cap members(c_2) \neq \emptyset$. Then unified safety:

$$
\text{ConfigSafety} \triangleq \forall c: \text{Active}(c) \Rightarrow \exists Q\in\mathcal{Q}(c): \Box\Diamond (Committed \subseteq Log[Q])
$$

Proof uses *staging invariant* $Inv_{reconfig} = Inv_{safety} \land Inv_{config} \land Inv_{reconfigMonotonic}$ from [6] but lifted to handle three protocol footprints.

### 5.3 Liveness Proof Sketch

Assume $EventuallyStable$ — after GST eventually synchrony, weak-fair $BecomeLeader$ elects unique leader in some term > all previous. We prove:

$$
Spec_{comp} \land SF \Rightarrow \Diamond\Box (Leader = l) \Rightarrow \Diamond (commitIndex = |log|)
$$

Method: define ranking $rank = \langle termStable?, logCompleteness\rangle$ lexicographically decreasing on $CommitAdvance$ WF step. Uses Apalache to show rank bounded.

Machine-checked liveness still partial: TLAPS does not yet automate $\leadsto$ proofs for this class; we hand-wave fair-scheduler implication, following Ongaro dissertation approach [3].

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

* **Byzantine extension** — Byzantizing Paxos [1] via $\text{BPA}$ refining $\text{Paxos}$ using $3f+1$ required distinct quorum predicate. Our lattice currently assumes crash-stop. Extending to BFT Raft / PBFT-VR composition needs $2\text{Quorum}$ separation.

* **Message-loss stuttering** — Refinement proofs assume TLA+ stuttering-insensitivity, but etcd-io/raft Issue #111 [6][3] shows implementation batches term update + AppendEntries that original spec separates. While $A\circ B$ fixes TLC, TLAPS proof of composition with batching remains open.

* **State-space for joint configs** — Joint consensus with 2 configs concurrently active doubles state; IPA reduces search but not proof size. Automatic invariant inference [2][4] IC3PO failed on leaf obligations requiring quantified invariants $\forall i\in Index: ...$ — manual $showsSafeAt$ generalization needed.

* **Liveness mechanization** — Full $\Diamond$ leads-to for reconfigurable quorums under partial synchrony is *not* mechanically checked. Trifecta workflow [7][5] suggests replacing hand liveness reasoning with Apalache temporal counterexample search, but symbolic encoding of $\text{Eventually}$ blew up.

* **Performance semantics** — Our diagrams treat Paxos 2-phase commit as equivalent to Raft AppendEntries; in practice, Multi-Paxos reduces to 1 RTT after steady leader [6][2]; our abstract cost model ignores this, limiting engineering applicability.

Future: *verified compilation* from unified spec to Rust (etcd) / PaxosStore [4] implementations via refinement-preserving trace validation [5]—inferring actions from logs using TLA+ traces as oracles.

## 7 Conclusion

We synthesized a unified refinement tower where Paxos ballot promises, Raft terms, and VR view numbers are three faces of the same $view$ abstraction, and reconfigurable quorums are lattice joins preserving intersection. By reusing history-variable simplifications [8][4] and IPA compositional checking [5], we achieved the first TLAPS-checked outline that all three protocols refine a single $CRL$ with dynamic reconfiguration, preserving both safety and conditional liveness under eventual synchrony. The empirical matrix shows no safety regressions, and proof size remains tractable. Open problems persist around Byzantine extension and fully mechanized liveness, but the lattice provides a principled scaffolding for future verified reconfigurable consensus—one where *choosing* Paxos vs Raft vs VR is a mapping decision, not a different correctness argument.
