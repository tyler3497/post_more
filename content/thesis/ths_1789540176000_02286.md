# The Standard Model Effective Field Theory at Dimension Six: Warsaw Basis, Renormalization Group Evolution, Ultraviolet Matching, and Global Fits

## Abstract

The Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) is the model-independent language for interpreting precision measurements as indirect probes of new physics above the electroweak scale. This thesis develops the dimension-six SMEFT as a complete quantitative framework: the 59-operator Warsaw basis as the canonical nonredundant operator set, the one-loop renormalization group evolution that mixes operators between the matching scale and the electroweak scale, systematic matching of ultraviolet completions onto Wilson coefficients via tree-level dictionaries and functional methods, and global fits that constrain the coefficient space with electroweak precision, Higgs, diboson, top-quark, and flavor data. We explain how equations of motion, integration by parts, and Fierz identities remove operator redundancies, how the full 59-by-59 anomalous dimension matrix computed by Jenkins, Manohar, and Trott organizes mixing, and how modern global analyses such as SMEFiT constrain dozens of independent directions simultaneously. The program yields a consistent picture: per-mille-level electroweak constraints coexist with percent-level Higgs constraints, no significant deviation from the Standard Model has emerged, and the formalism is falsifiable through correlated predictions across seemingly unrelated observables.

---

##1 Intro

The absence of new particles at the Large Hadron Collider, despite direct searches reaching multi-TeV energies, has shifted the experimental program toward *precision*. Deviations from the Standard Model (SM) may appear first not as resonances but as subtle deformations of the SM's couplings — shifts in the Higgs couplings, anomalous triple gauge couplings, or loop-suppressed flavor transitions. The theoretical framework that organizes these deformations is effective field theory (EFT).

The **Standard Model Effective Field Theory** extends the SM Lagrangian with all local operators compatible with the SM gauge symmetry SU(3)_c x SU(2)_L x U(1)_Y and Lorentz invariance, organized in powers of inverse mass dimension [1]:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SMEFT}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{SM}} + \sum_{d > 4} \sum_i \frac{C_i^{(d)}}{\Lambda^{d-4}} \, Q_i^{(d)}$$

Here $\Lambda$ is the scale of new physics, $Q_i^{(d)}$ are operators of canonical dimension $d$ built from SM fields, and $C_i^{(d)}$ are dimensionless Wilson coefficients. The program has four pillars. First, **basis construction**: enumerating a complete, nonredundant set of operators — the Warsaw basis of 59 flavor-distinguished dimension-six operators [1]. Second, **renormalization group evolution**: operators mix under quantum corrections, so a Wilson coefficient generated at the matching scale $\Lambda$ becomes a linear combination of coefficients at the electroweak scale, governed by a computed one-loop anomalous dimension matrix [3][4]. Third, **ultraviolet matching**: translating concrete models — leptoquarks, vector-like fermions, extended Higgs sectors — into SMEFT coefficients via tree-level dictionaries and functional matching techniques [5]. Fourth, **global fits**: simultaneously constraining dozens of coefficients against electroweak precision data, Higgs measurements, diboson production, and top-quark observables [6][7][8].

> **Theorem:** *Any amplitude computed to order $\Lambda^{-2}$ in the SMEFT is independent of the choice of operator basis; a complete nonredundant basis such as the Warsaw basis spans the same physical deformations as any alternative basis, and basis changes correspond to field redefinitions.*

##2 Background

### The operator expansion

The SM is a renormalizable theory with operators of dimension at most four. New physics at a scale $\Lambda \gg v$, where $v \approx 246$ GeV is the electroweak scale, contributes operators of higher dimension. At dimension five there is a *unique* operator (up to flavor structure): the Weinberg operator generating Majorana neutrino masses,

$$Q_{\nu\nu} = (\tilde{H}^\dagger l)^T C (\tilde{H}^\dagger l),$$

which is phenomenologically fixed by neutrino masses and decoupled from collider phenomenology. The leading phenomenologically relevant corrections are therefore **dimension six**, suppressed by $\Lambda^{-2}$ [1][2].

The modern canonical basis, the **Warsaw basis** of Grzadkowski, Iskrzyński, Misiak, and Rosiek, contains 59 independent operators when flavor indices and Hermitian conjugates are not distinguished — expanding to 2499 independent parameters once the full three-generation flavor structure is restored [1].

### The Warsaw basis: anatomy

The 59 operators fall into eight classes by field content:

| Class | Field content | Example | Count (non-Hermitian) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $X^3$ | three field strengths | $Q_G = f^{ABC} G_\mu^{A\nu} G_\nu^{B\rho} G_\rho^{C\mu}$ | 4 |
| $H^6$ | six Higgs fields | $Q_H = (H^\dagger H)^3$ | 1 |
| $H^4 D^2$ | Higgs + derivatives | $Q_{H\Box} = (H^\dagger H)\Box(H^\dagger H)$ | 2 |
| $X^2 H^2$ | field strengths + Higgs | $Q_{HG} = H^\dagger H \, G_{\mu\nu}^A G^{A\mu\nu}$ | 8 |
| $\psi^2 H^3$ | Yukawa-like | $Q_{uH} = (H^\dagger H)(\bar{q}_p u_r \tilde{H})$ | 3 |
| $\psi^2 X H$ | dipole operators | $Q_{uW} = (\bar{q}_p \sigma^{\mu\nu} u_r)\tau^I \tilde{H} W_{\mu\nu}^I$ | 8 |
| $\psi^2 H^2 D$ | current operators | $Q_{Hq}^{(1)} = (H^\dagger i\overleftrightarrow{D}_\mu H)(\bar{q}_p \gamma^\mu q_r)$ | 8 |
| $\psi^4$ | four-fermion operators | $Q_{ll} = (\bar{l}_p \gamma_\mu l_r)(\bar{l}_s \gamma^\mu l_t)$ | 25 |

Here $p, r, s, t$ are flavor indices. The four-fermion sector dominates numerically (25 operators), reflecting the richness of fermionic contact interactions [1].

### Redundancy: equations of motion, integration by parts, Fierz identities

A naive enumeration overcounts: many operators are equivalent *on-shell*. Three tools eliminate redundancy [2]:

1. **Equations of motion (EOM):** operators proportional to the SM equations of motion can be removed by field redefinitions, shifting their effects into remaining operators.
2. **Integration by parts (IBP):** total derivatives drop out of the action, relating operators with different derivative placements.
3. **Fierz identities:** four-fermion operators in different contraction channels are linearly related.

> **Theorem:** *S-matrix elements computed at order $\Lambda^{-2}$ are invariant under field redefinitions, so two operators related by the SM equations of motion contribute identically to all on-shell observables.*

##3 Methodology

The SMEFT workflow proceeds from operator construction through phenomenology:

1. **Enumerate** the operator set with the Hilbert series, which counts invariants of the SM gauge group and automatically implements IBP and EOM redundancies [2].
2. **Derive Feynman rules** in the Warsaw basis after electroweak symmetry breaking, rotating to the mass eigenbasis.
3. **Compute the anomalous dimension matrix** at one loop; resum large logarithms $\ln(\Lambda/v)$ via the RG equations [3][4].
4. **Match** candidate UV models onto Wilson coefficients at the scale $\Lambda$ using covariant derivative expansion techniques [5].
5. **Fit** coefficients to data with profiled likelihoods or Bayesian posteriors, profiling or marginalizing over nuisance parameters [6][7].

Operator running is implemented numerically as a linear differential system. The following illustrates the RG evolution of Wilson coefficients in Python:

```python
import numpy as np

def rge_evolve(C_lambda, gamma, Lambda, v, order="LL"):
    '''
    Evolve Wilson coefficients from matching scale Lambda to the
    electroweak scale v at leading logarithmic order.
    C_lambda : array of Wilson coefficients C_i(Lambda)
    gamma    : one-loop anomalous dimension matrix (59 x 59)
    '''
    t = np.log(Lambda / v)    alpha = 1.0 / (4.0 * np.pi)**2    M = np.eye(len(C_lambda)) - alpha * gamma * t
    return M @ C_lambda

# Example: mixing of C_HWB into the oblique sector at one loop
C_in = np.zeros(59)
C_in[7] = 1.0gamma = np.loadtxt("adm_59x59.dat")C_out = rge_evolve(C_in, gamma, Lambda=10_000.0, v=246.0)
```

##4 Deep Dive

###4.1 Anatomy of the Warsaw basis: classes, counting, and canonical choices

The 59 operators of the Warsaw basis are not an arbitrary list: they form the complete set of dimension-six invariants modulo the EOM/IBP/Fierz equivalence relations [1]. Several structural facts deserve emphasis.

First, the basis is organized so that **each operator has a distinct physical fingerprint** where possible. The operator $Q_{HWB} = H^\dagger \tau^I H \, W_{\mu\nu}^I B^{\mu\nu}$ contributes to the oblique $S$ parameter at tree level; $Q_{HD} = |H^\dagger D_\mu H|^2$ contributes to the $T$ parameter; $Q_{ll}$ shifts the Fermi constant extracted from muon decay, redefining all electroweak inputs. This fingerprint structure is what makes global fits interpretable: a deviation in $S$ points at a specific direction in coefficient space [8].

Second, the counting 59 assumes *one* generation and drops Hermitian conjugates as independent. Restoring three generations, the Yukawa-like operators $Q_{uH}, Q_{dH}, Q_{eH}$ carry two flavor indices each, the four-fermion operators carry up to four, and the total parameter count reaches 2499. Global fits therefore always impose a **flavor hypothesis** — flavor universality $U(3)^5$, minimal flavor violation, or a top-specific scenario — reducing the fit to a manageable subset [6][7].

Third, the CP-odd sector generates CP-violating Higgs couplings constrained by electric dipole moments and Higgs-decay angular analyses [1].

###4.2 Renormalization group evolution and operator mixing

A Wilson coefficient defined at the matching scale $\Lambda$ is not the coefficient that enters low-energy observables. Under RG evolution,

$$16\pi^2 \, \mu \frac{d}{d\mu} C_i = \gamma_{ij} C_j,$$

where $\gamma_{ij}$ is the one-loop anomalous dimension matrix — a dense $59 \times 59$ matrix computed by Jenkins, Manohar, and Trott in a landmark three-paper series covering the Higgs self-coupling and Yukawa dependence, and the full gauge-coupling dependence [3][4]. The matrix has a block structure dictated by field content and by nonrenormalization theorems:

- **Gauge-current operators** $\psi^2 H^2 D$ do not renormalize at one loop proportional to gauge couplings alone in certain flavor channels — a consequence of current conservation.
- **Dipole operators** $\psi^2 X H$ mix strongly with the Yukawa-like operators $\psi^2 H^3$, since both flip chirality.
- **Four-fermion operators** renormalize among themselves through gauge-boson exchange, with QCD driving the running of color-octet operators.
- **Bosonic operators** $X^2 H^2$ mix into each other and feed the running of the SM gauge couplings themselves: dimension-six operators modify the $\beta$-functions of the SM parameters [4].

The phenomenological consequence is **indirect sensitivity**: an operator generated at $\Lambda$ that is invisible to electroweak precision data can mix, via RG running, into operators that are exquisitely constrained. The classic example is $Q_{HWB}$ mixing into the dipole operators, and four-top operators mixing into Higgs couplings — quantum corrections connect the UV fingerprint to the IR fingerprint across sectors that appear unrelated at tree level [4].

Resummation matters quantitatively: for $\Lambda$ in the multi-TeV range, $\ln(\Lambda/v)$ is of order a few, and leading-log resummation shifts constraints at the 10-percent level — comparable to the precision of Higgs coupling measurements [3].

###4.3 Matching ultraviolet models to the EFT

Given a UV model — a leptoquark, a $Z'$ boson, vector-like quarks, a second Higgs doublet — the task is to compute the Wilson coefficients it generates. At **tree level**, the complete dictionary was established by de Blas, Criado, Pérez-Victoria, and Santiago: integrating out general scalar, fermion, and vector fields with arbitrary renormalizable interactions yields every possible tree-level contribution to the dimension-six coefficients [5]. The dictionary is organized by the quantum numbers of the heavy field; a single heavy multiplet typically generates a *pattern* of correlated coefficients, not an isolated one.

At **one loop**, matching requires evaluating the heavy-particle contributions to the EFT functional determinant. Modern functional methods — the covariant derivative expansion (CDE) and universal one-loop effective action (UOLEA) — reduce this to algebraic manipulations of the second-variation operator. Packages such as **CoDEx** and **MatchMakerEFT** automate one-loop matching for arbitrary models [5].

Global fits can therefore test *models*, not just coefficients, by checking whether the fitted coefficients lie along the model's predicted ray in coefficient space [7].

> **Theorem:** *In a weakly-coupled, renormalizable UV completion, the Wilson coefficients of operators generated only at loop level carry an additional suppression factor relative to tree-generated coefficients; this organizes the EFT power counting beyond naive dimensional analysis.*

###4.4 Global fits: electroweak, Higgs, diboson, top, and flavor

The SMEFT program culminates in **global fits** that confront the full coefficient space with data. Three analysis generations illustrate the state of the art.

*Electroweak precision and the oblique parameters.* The LEP legacy — the $Z$-pole observables, $M_W$, and asymmetries — constrains the oblique parameters $S$ and $T$ at the per-mille level. In SMEFT language,

$$S \propto \frac{v^2}{\Lambda^2} C_{HWB}, \qquad T \propto -\frac{v^2}{\Lambda^2} C_{HD},$$

with additional contributions from the current operators $Q_{Hl}^{(3)}$, $Q_{Hq}^{(3)}$, and the four-lepton operator $Q_{ll}$ that shift the input parameters $G_F$, $M_Z$, $\alpha$ [8]. The celebrated Ellis et al. global fit to Higgs, diboson, and electroweak data demonstrated that LHC Run 2 Higgs and diboson measurements substantially improve on the LEP-only constraints, particularly through the STXS (simplified template cross-section) kinematic information [8].

*Higgs and diboson.* The operators $Q_{HG}, Q_{HW}, Q_{HB}, Q_{HWB}$ modify Higgs production (gluon fusion) and decay ($h \to \gamma\gamma$, $h \to ZZ^*$), while $Q_W = \epsilon^{IJK} W_\mu^{I\nu} W_\nu^{J\rho} W_\rho^{K\mu}$ shifts triple gauge couplings probed in diboson production. High-$p_T$ diboson tails grow with energy as $E^2/\Lambda^2$, giving the LHC unique sensitivity to operators that LEP could barely access [8].

*Top quark.* The top sector — $Q_{uG}$ (chromomagnetic dipole), $Q_{qq}^{(1,3)}$, $Q_{uu}$ — is probed by $t\bar{t}$ production cross sections and differential distributions. The **SMEFiT** collaboration performed the first combined fit of Higgs, diboson, and top data, constraining 36 independent directions simultaneously and including both linear and quadratic ($\Lambda^{-4}$) corrections, demonstrating that NLO QCD and quadratic effects are instrumental to accurate posterior mapping [6]. The **Fitmaker** framework of Ellis, Murphy, Sanz, and You extended the global program to include top production together with electroweak and diboson data, and used the results to constrain single-field UV extensions and light stops [7].

##5 Empirical Results or Proofs

1. **No significant deviation from the SM has been found.** Across SMEFiT's 36 fitted directions and Fitmaker's top-plus-electroweak analysis, all marginalized posteriors are consistent with vanishing Wilson coefficients; $\chi^2$ comparisons show no preference for the SMEFT over the SM [6][7].
2. **Electroweak precision sets the tightest bounds.** Coefficients entering $S$ and $T$ — $C_{HWB}$, $C_{HD}$ — are constrained at the level of $|C|v^2/\Lambda^2 \lesssim 10^{-3}$, corresponding to $\Lambda \gtrsim 10$ TeV for order-one coefficients [8].
3. **Higgs couplings are constrained at the 5–10% level.** The operators $Q_{HG}$, $Q_{HW}$, $Q_{HB}$ map onto Higgs coupling modifiers $\kappa_g$, $\kappa_\gamma$, $\kappa_V$; current bounds translate to $\Lambda \gtrsim 1$–$3$ TeV for order-one coefficients [6][8].
4. **Quadratic terms matter.** SMEFiT showed that truncating at linear order in $\Lambda^{-2}$ can misidentify the posterior shape; including $\Lambda^{-4}$ pieces (dimension-six squared, and in principle dimension-eight interference) changes confidence intervals for operators probed only through energy-growing tails [6].
5. **Correlations are the physics.** Individual-operator bounds are misleading: the global fit exhibits strong correlations (e.g., between $C_{HW}$, $C_{HB}$, and $C_{HWB}$ in the $h \to \gamma\gamma$ rate), and it is the correlated combination that a UV model must reproduce [6][7][8].

The falsifiability of the framework is not an abstract virtue. SMEFT predicts **correlations between multi-Higgs couplings**: at order $\Lambda^{-2}$, the double-Higgs and triple-Higgs couplings are not independent but locked to the single-Higgs coupling shifts — a prediction that di-Higgs and triple-Higgs measurements can falsify even with no new light particles [9].

| Observable class | Representative operators | Bound on $\|C\|v^2/\Lambda^2$ | Implied scale ($\Lambda$, $C \sim 1$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $Z$-pole / $M_W$ | $Q_{HWB}$, $Q_{HD}$, $Q_{Hl}^{(3)}$ | $\sim 10^{-3}$ | $\gtrsim 8$ TeV |
| Higgs signal strengths | $Q_{HG}$, $Q_{HW}$, $Q_{HB}$ | $\sim 10^{-1}$ | $\gtrsim 1$ TeV |
| Triple gauge couplings | $Q_W$, $Q_{HWB}$ | $\sim 10^{-1}$ | $\gtrsim 1$ TeV |
| Top pair production | $Q_{uG}$, $Q_{qq}^{(1)}$ | $\sim 10^{-1}$ | $\gtrsim 1$ TeV |
| Four-fermion contact | $Q_{ll}$, $Q_{qq}$ | $\sim 10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$ | $\gtrsim 3$–$10$ TeV |

##6 Limitations

The SMEFT framework, for all its power, carries structural limitations that must be stated plainly.

- **Truncation uncertainty.** Working at order $\Lambda^{-2}$ neglects dimension-eight interference, which contributes at the same $\Lambda^{-4}$ order as dimension-six squared. For operators constrained only through energy-growing effects, the truncation error can rival the experimental uncertainty; SMEFiT's inclusion of quadratic terms mitigates but does not eliminate this [6].
- **Validity at high energies.** The EFT expansion requires $E \ll \Lambda$. Differential distributions extending to partonic energies approaching the fitted $\Lambda$ bound probe the region where the expansion breaks down; "clipping" analyses that discard high-energy bins test the robustness of bounds against this breakdown [6].
- **Flavor assumptions.** With 2499 parameters, every global fit imposes a flavor symmetry (universality, MFV, top-specific). A genuine flavor-nonuniversal signal — as hinted historically by $B$-decay anomalies — would be misattributed or missed under an overly restrictive hypothesis [6][7].
- **Flat and weakly-constrained directions.** Even combined fits leave approximate flat directions — linear combinations of coefficients to which no current observable is sensitive. Claims of "model-independent" bounds must be qualified by the chosen flavor and truncation scheme [7].
- **SMEFT versus HEFT.** If electroweak symmetry is nonlinearly realized (no doublet Higgs in the UV), the correct EFT is HEFT, not SMEFT; the two are distinguished by correlations among multi-Higgs couplings that SMEFT predicts and HEFT relaxes [9].
- **Basis-dependent presentation.** While physics is basis-independent, the *presentation* of bounds is not: individual-coefficient limits depend on which operators are profiled versus fixed, and comparisons across analyses require care with basis translations [2][8].

##7 Conclusion

The Standard Model Effective Field Theory at dimension six is the mature, quantitative language of indirect new-physics searches. The Warsaw basis provides the canonical operator set [1]; the one-loop anomalous dimension matrix of Jenkins, Manohar, and Trott governs the RG mixing that connects UV fingerprints to IR observables [3][4]; tree-level dictionaries and functional matching translate concrete models into coefficient patterns [5]; and global fits — SMEFiT, Fitmaker, and the electroweak-Higgs-diboson legacy analyses — constrain dozens of directions simultaneously with no significant deviation from the Standard Model [6][7][8].

Three structural insights define the program's enduring value. First, **redundancy is not ambiguity**: EOM, IBP, and Fierz relations guarantee that a minimal basis spans all physical deformations, making basis choice a matter of convenience. Second, **mixing is discovery**: RG evolution lets precision electroweak data probe operators generated at scales far above direct reach. Third, **correlation is falsifiability**: SMEFT predicts relations among observables — from oblique parameters to multi-Higgs couplings — that experiments can confirm or kill [9].

As the LHC accumulates luminosity and future colliders sharpen Higgs and electroweak precision further, the SMEFT will remain the sieve through which every candidate theory of physics beyond the Standard Model must pass.
