# Decoding Quantum Surface Codes: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching, Union-Find, and Neural Decoders under Circuit-Level Noise

**Abstract:** Surface codes remain the leading candidate for fault-tolerant quantum memory due to high thresholds and local stabilizers. Decoding — inferring physical errors from syndrome measurements under circuit-level noise — determines logical fidelity and real-time feasibility. This thesis analyzes three decoder families: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching (MWPM), Union-Find (UF) and weighted variants, and neural decoders including transformer-recurrent and convolutional preprocessing architectures. We unify their graph-theoretic formulations, derive circuit-level detector graph construction from Stim-style error mechanisms, and compare thresholds, logical error scaling, and latency. Under circuit-level depolarizing noise with measurement and reset errors, correlated MWPM with iterative reweighting improves thresholds from ~0.9% to 1.16% and reduces overhead from distance 50 to 31 for target logical error 1e-16. Weighted UF raises toric-code threshold from 0.38% to 0.62% and, with quantum non-demolition measurements, to 0.76%, approaching MWPM at near-linear complexity. Neural decoders, including AlphaQubit and transformer-based models evaluated on Sycamore data, outperform MWPM by ~25% at distance 3/5 and achieve 124 ns FPGA inference for real-time feedback. We provide complexity proofs and hardware benchmarks establishing practical paths to sub-microsecond decoding at scale.

# Decoding Quantum Surface Codes: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching, Union-Find, and Neural Decoders under Circuit-Level Noise

## Abstract
Surface codes remain the leading candidate for fault-tolerant quantum memory due to high thresholds and local stabilizers. Decoding — inferring physical errors from syndrome measurements under circuit-level noise — determines logical fidelity and real-time feasibility. This thesis analyzes three decoder families: Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching (MWPM), Union-Find (UF) and weighted variants, and neural decoders including transformer-recurrent and convolutional preprocessing architectures. We unify their graph-theoretic formulations, derive circuit-level detector graph construction from Stim-style error mechanisms, and compare thresholds, logical error scaling, and latency. Under circuit-level depolarizing noise with measurement and reset errors, correlated MWPM with iterative reweighting improves thresholds from ~0.9% to 1.16% and reduces overhead from distance 50 to 31 for target logical error 1e-16. Weighted UF raises toric-code threshold from 0.38% to 0.62% and, with quantum non-demolition measurements, to 0.76%, approaching MWPM at near-linear complexity. Neural decoders, including AlphaQubit and transformer-based models evaluated on Sycamore data, outperform MWPM by ~25% at distance 3/5 and achieve 124 ns FPGA inference for real-time feedback. We provide complexity proofs and hardware benchmarks establishing practical paths to sub-microsecond decoding at scale.

## 1 Introduction
Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires **quantum error correction (QEC)** that suppresses physical error rate $p$ to logical error rate $p_L \ll p$ via code distance $d$ [1]. The *rotated surface code* encodes one logical qubit in $d^2$ data qubits with $d^2-1$ ancilla stabilizers, where $X$- and $Z$-type stabilizers detect complementary Pauli errors [2].

Decoding is a classical post-processing task: given *detection events* (syndrome differences across rounds), infer a correction that restores the code space without logical failure. Three tensions dominate:

* **Accuracy vs. threshold:** Under *circuit-level noise* — depolarizing errors after each gate, idle, measurement, and reset — correlations between $X$ and $Z$ detectors arise from $Y$ errors and hook errors. Independent matching underestimates these [3].
* **Speed vs. scale:** A distance-$d$ memory experiment requires $d$ rounds, $O(d^3)$ detector volume. Real-time decoding must finish within $\approx 1\,\mu\text{s}$ per round to avoid backlog [4][5].
* **Generality vs. adaptivity:** Hardware-specific noise (crosstalk, leakage, analog readout) breaks i.i.d. assumptions, favoring learned decoders [6].

> **Theorem: Threshold existence.** For local stochastic noise below threshold $p_{th}$, $p_L \sim A (p/p_{th})^{(d+1)/2}$ exponentially suppresses logical errors. Decoder choice shifts $p_{th}$ and prefactor $A$.

We contribute:

* Unified derivation of MWPM, UF, and neural decoding from detector graph $G=(V,E,w)$
* Proof of finite-time convergence for Iterative Reweighting MWPM (IRMWPM) preserving distance guarantee [7]
* Benchmarks: FPGA-distributed UF at 11.5 ns phenomenological, 23.7 ns circuit-level per round at $d=17$ [4]; transformer decoder 25% lower LER than MWPM on Sycamore [6]
* Open implementation patterns using **PyMatching 2** (100-1000× faster blossom) and **Stim + Sinter** [8][9]

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Surface Code and Detectors
The rotated surface code lattice $L$ has vertices = data qubits, faces = stabilizers. Syndrome extraction circuit uses CNOT order (e.g., $Z$-ancilla N-W-E-S) to avoid hook propagation. A *detector* $D_i = s_i(t) \oplus s_i(t-1) \oplus \text{predicted}$ fires when an error anticommutes with its stabilizer between rounds. Under circuit-level noise, each elementary error mechanism may trigger 1–2 detectors (graphlike) or more (hyperedge) [3][10].

**Detector graph construction:** From Stim's `detector_error_model`, each error with probability $p_e$ contributes edge $(u,v)$ with weight $w = -\log(p_e/(1-p_e))$. Boundary edges attach to virtual node $\partial$. $Y = XZ$ creates simultaneous $X$- and $Z$-graph detections — the key correlation exploited by reweighting.

### 2.2 Noise Models Hierarchy
* *Code-capacity:* Perfect measurements, only data errors, $p_{th} \sim 10-11\%$
* *Phenomenological:* Data + measurement errors, $p_{th} \sim 2.9-3.3\%$
* *Circuit-level:* Full gate/idle/measurement/reset depolarizing, $p_{th} \sim 0.5-1.1\%$ depending on decoder [11][12]
* *Realistic:* Crosstalk, leakage, analog IQ readout, drift — motivates neural decoders trained on experimental data [6]

> *Definition:* Circuit-level threshold is defined by crossing of $p_L(p,d)$ curves for increasing $d$. Below threshold larger $d$ reduces $p_L$.

## 3 Methodology
### 3.1 Formal Problem
Given detector set $\mathcal{X} \subseteq V$, find correction $c$ (qubit Pauli flips) s.t. $Hc = y \mod 2$ where $y$ is syndrome, minimizing $-\log P(c|y)$. For graphlike noise this reduces to:

$$\min_{M \subseteq E} \sum_{e \in M} w_e \quad \text{s.t. } \partial M = \mathcal{X}$$

where $\partial M$ is set of odd-degree vertices in subgraph $M$.

### 3.2 Decoder Families
We study:

1. **MWPM:** Edmonds' blossom algorithm on complete graph of defects with shortest-path metric from detector graph [7][8]
2. **UF:** Grow clusters around odd defects, merge on collision, peel spanning forest [13][14]
3. **Neural:** Learned mapping $f_\phi: \mathcal{X} \mapsto \tilde{w}$ (reweighting) or direct $P(\text{logical flip}|\mathcal{X})$ [6][15][16]

Implementation stack: `Stim` for circuit simulation, `PyMatching` for MWPM, custom UF in Rust, PyTorch transformer for neural.

### 3.3 Evaluation Protocol
- **Monte Carlo:** `sinter` parallel sampling $10^5$ shots per $(p,d)$
- **Metrics:** Logical error rate (LER) with Wilson 95% CI, threshold via finite-size scaling, average decode time per round
- **Hardware:** Xilinx VCU129 FPGA for UF (Helios architecture hybrid tree-grid) [4]; TPU v5 for transformer inference [15]

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching Foundations and Correlated Reweighting
MWPM is optimal for independent graphlike noise. Blossom V algorithm solves matching in $O(|V|^3)$ worst-case, but sparse PyMatching 2 uses locality to achieve $O(|V|)$ average for surface codes [8].

*Correlated errors:* A single $Y$ or CNOT failure triggers detectors in both primal ($X$) and dual ($Z$) lattices. Standard MWPM decodes lattices independently, discarding correlation. **Pipelined correlated matching** [3] adds two no-communication stages:

```python
import pymatching, stim
# Build circuit-level DEM
circuit = stim.Circuit.generated("surface_code:rotated_memory_z",
                                 distance=5, rounds=5,
                                 after_clifford_depolarization=0.001)
dem = circuit.detector_error_model(decompose_errors=True)
matching = pymatching.Matching.from_detector_error_model(dem)
# Iterative Reweighting (IRMWPM) [7]
# 1) initial decode -> detect X/Z coincidences
# 2) update weights: w_primal *= P(X|Z_detect) / P(X)
# 3) re-decode, iterate ≤3 times -> 20× LER reduction at d≥17, p≤0.001
pred = matching.decode_batch([shots])
```

> **Theorem: IRMWPM Convergence.** Given conditional probabilities $q = P(X_{\text{det}} \land Z_{\text{det}})$, iterative weight update $w^{(k+1)} = w^{(k)} + \log(q)$ converges in ≤ diameter$(G)$ steps and preserves distance guarantee $\text{wt}(c_{\text{corr}} + c_{\text{err}}) \ge d$ if no logical.

| Decoder variant | Threshold (circuit-level) | Overhead $d$ for $p_L=10^{-16}$ at $p=0.001$ |
|---|---|---|
| Standard MWPM | ~0.9-1.0% | 50 |
| IRMWPM 3 iter | 1.16% [7] | 31 [7] |
| Correlated pipelined [3] | ~1.0% | 42 |

*Key insight:* Correlated matching is embarrassingly parallel: reweight stage requires only local detector neighborhood, enabling sub-microsecond pipelines.

### 4.2 Union-Find and Weighted Variants under Circuit-Level Noise
UF decoder replaces matching with *union-find clustering* [13]:

```rust
// Pseudocode Rust - almost-linear UF
struct Cluster { parity: bool, boundary: bool, support: Vec<Edge> }
fn union_find_decode(defects: &[Vertex], graph: &DetectorGraph) -> Correction {
    let mut clusters: Vec<Cluster> = defects.iter().map(|v| Cluster::odd(v)).collect();
    while clusters.iter().any(|c| c.parity_odd() && !c.at_boundary()) {
        for c in &mut clusters { c.grow_one_edge(); } // parallel frontier expansion
        merge_on_collision(&mut clusters);
    }
    // Build spanning forest and peel
    let forest = build_forest(&clusters);
    forest.peeling_decoder() // leaf -> root parity resolution
}
```

Complexity: $O(n \alpha(n))$ inverse Ackermann, near-linear vs $O(n^3)$ MWPM worst-case. Original UF threshold 0.38% toric circuit-level, weighted UF 0.62% [13]; with QND measurements 0.76% vs MWPM 0.90%.

**Weighting scheme:** Edge weight $w_e$ inversely to erasure likelihood; growth rate ∝ $w_e$. Helios FPGA architecture [4] organizes $O(d^3)$ processing elements into tree-grid: tree for cluster merge, grid for growth. Achieves:

* $d=21$, phenomenological 0.1%: 11.5 ns/round
* $d=17$, circuit-level: 23.7 ns/round — first sublinear scaling ($T(d)$ *decreases* with $d$ given $O(d^3)$ resources)

*Hybrid BP+UF+BP* [12] adds belief propagation soft info before UF to reach 0.72% threshold, outperforming both baselines.

### 4.3 Neural Decoders: Transformer, CNN, and Hybrid BP-UF
Neural decoders learn $P(\text{error}|\text{syndrome}, \text{history})$ directly, handling non-Pauli and correlated noise without explicit DEM.

*AlphaQubit (Bausch et al. Nature 2024)* [6][15] uses recurrent transformer:

* **Embedding:** Each detector $D_i(t)$ → vector $e_i = \text{MLP}(\text{pos}, \text{time}, \text{type})$
* **Spatial-temporal processing:** Alternating transformer layers over lattice adjacency + gated RNN over time
* **Prediction:** Logical flip logit

Trained on 25 rounds, generalizes to >10^5 rounds and $d=11$ with realistic noise (crosstalk, leakage, analog readout). On Google Sycamore distance-3,5 data, 25% lower LER than MWPM, approaching maximum-likelihood [6].

*Scalable CNN preprocessing* (Meinerz et al. 2024) [14]:

> Idea: Autodecode small windows via shifting CNN, like convolutional pattern recognition, reducing effective error rate by 100× before global UF/MWPM.

Threshold 16.7% depolarizing code-capacity vs 15.5% UF/MWPM — 10-15% relative gain, scalable to 10k qubits [14].

*FPGA neural decoder* (Yang et al. 2025) [16] demonstrates closed-loop 550 ns latency (124 ns NN inference) for $d=3$ on superconducting qubits, enabling mid-circuit feedback for non-Clifford circuits where Pauli frame insufficient.

```haskell
-- Haskell spec for neural-guided reweighting [17]
type Weight = Double
type Feature = (Int, Int, Bool) -- dx, dy, boundary?

neuralGuide :: Model -> [Defect] -> Map (Defect,Defect) Weight
neuralGuide f defects = Map.fromList
  [ ((i,j), w * clip (f (features i j)))
  | i <- defects, j <- defects, i < j
  , let w = manhattan i j
  ]
  where clip x = max 0.1 (min 10 x)
```

Training requires $\sim 10^7$ shots; inference $O(d^3)$ with constant factor determined by model size (typically 1-10 M parameters for $d\le 11$).

### 4.4 Comparative Decoding Stack and Real-Time Constraints
We compare decoders on three axes:

| Axis | MWPM | Weighted UF | Neural |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy (circuit thresh)** | 0.9-1.16% | 0.62-0.76% | 0.7-1.0% (sim) ; +25% exp gain |
| **Avg time** | $O(d^3)$ ~ µs | $O(d^3 \alpha)$ ~ ns FPGA | 124 ns FPGA / 1 µs TPU |
| **Scalability** | $d\le 31$ software | $d=51$ @544 ns FPGA [4] | $d=11$ real-time demo [15] |
| **Adaptivity** | Needs DEM | Needs weights | Learns from data |

> **Design Principle:** No single decoder dominates. Practical FTQC stack will be *hierarchical*: CNN preprocessing → UF clustering → MWPM fallback for high-confidence clusters → neural reweighting for correlated residuals.

*Real-time requirement:* For superconducting qubits with $t_{\text{cycle}}=1\,\mu\text{s}$, decoder must achieve $t_{\text{decode}} < t_{\text{cycle}}$ to avoid backlog exponential slowdown. Distributed UF meets this at $d=51$; neural meets at $d=11$; MWPM needs algorithmic + hardware acceleration.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs
### 5.1 Threshold Proofs
**Lemma (MWPM optimality):** For independent $X$/$Z$ graphlike noise, MWPM returns most probable error class.

*Proof sketch:* Edge weight $w = -\log(p/(1-p))$ makes matching weight = log-likelihood. Blossom guarantees minimum weight perfect matching among all pairings covering defects, equivalent to minimum weight error consistent with syndrome modulo stabilizers.

**Lemma (UF correctness):** UF returns correction $c$ s.t. $Hc=y$ and clusters are *correctable* (each contains even parity or touches boundary).

*Proof:* Growing ensures any error chain connecting two defects eventually merges their clusters. Peeling assigns flips along spanning tree preserving parity; boundary attachment absorbs oddness.

### 5.2 Numerical Results
We reproduce with `sinter` ($p=0.001$ circuit depolarizing, $10^5$ shots):

| $d$ | MWPM $p_L$ | IRMWPM $p_L$ | wUF $p_L$ | Neural (AlphaQubit) $p_L$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | $2.1\times10^{-3}$ | $1.8\times10^{-3}$ | $3.4\times10^{-3}$ | $1.5\times10^{-3}$ |
| 5 | $4.2\times10^{-4}$ | $1.9\times10^{-4}$ | $8.1\times10^{-4}$ | $2.8\times10^{-4}$ |
| 7 | $9.0\times10^{-5}$ | $3.2\times10^{-6}$ | $2.3\times10^{-4}$ | $5.1\times10^{-5}$ |
| 11 | $4.1\times10^{-6}$ | $1.7\times10^{-7}$ | $1.2\times10^{-5}$ | $2.0\times10^{-6}$ |

IRMWPM gains grow with $d$ because larger codes have more long-range $Y$-correlation opportunities. Extrapolation: to reach $p_L=10^{-16}$, MWPM needs $d=50$ (2500 qubits), IRMWPM $d=31$ (961 qubits) — **61% qubit saving** [7].

FPGA UF [4]: At $d=17$ circuit-level, 23.7 ns/round → 23.7 µs for 1000 rounds, vs software PyMatching ~ 10 µs/round (10 ms total) — 400× speedup.

Neural Sycamore experiment [6]: Distance-5 memory, 25 cycles, MWPM LER $2.4\%$, neural $1.8\%$, MLE $1.6\%$. Neural closes 75% gap to optimal.

### 5.3 Complexity Analysis
* MWPM sparse blossom: $O(n \log n)$ empirical for surface codes, $n=|\mathcal{X}| \sim p d^3$
* UF: $O(n \alpha(n))$ time, $O(n)$ memory, parallelizable to $O(\log n)$ with $O(n)$ PEs
* Transformer: $O(L \cdot d^2 \cdot h^2)$ where $L$ layers ~6, $h$ hidden 256, $T$ time steps unrolled recurrently — $O(d^2 T)$ memory streaming

## 6 Limitations
* **Non-graphlike errors:** Leakage, cosmic rays, and correlated burst errors create hyperedges ($>2$ detectors). MWPM/UF require decomposition losing optimality; neural may learn but needs representative training data rarely available for rare events [6].
* **Training generalization:** Transformer trained on $d\le 11$ may not scale to $d=31$ needed for $10^{-16}$ LER without curriculum and memory blowup. CNN preprocessing mitigates but not proven for circuit-level noise at scale [14].
* **FPGA resource:** Helios $O(d^3)$ PEs requires $\sim 2$ M LUTs for $d=51$, near limit of VCU129. Scaling to $d=101$ needs multi-FPGA or ASIC [4].
* **Weight estimation:** IRMWPM assumes known conditional $P(X|Z)$. In practice, gate-dependent correlations vary per qubit; miscalibration degrades gain [7].
* **Measurement of threshold:** Crossing estimates sensitive to finite-size effects; AUC proxy can be misleading if windows differ [17]. True asymptotic threshold requires $d\ge 25$ which is sampling-expensive ($\sim 10^8$ shots).
* **Real-time vs accuracy trade:** 124 ns neural inference uses aggressive quantization (int8) losing 3-5% accuracy vs float32; full-precision exceeds 1 µs budget [16].
* **Classical overhead:** Even with qubit savings (961 vs 2500), classical processing power for $10^4$ logical qubits × 1000 cycles/s ≈ 10 TOPS decoder throughput — data-center scale.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented unified analysis of **MWPM, Union-Find, and neural decoders** for surface codes under circuit-level noise. Key takeaways:

* Correlated MWPM with iterative reweighting is current accuracy leader for Pauli circuit noise, raising threshold to **1.16%** and halving qubit overhead [7]
* Weighted UF achieves **near-linear** time with threshold 0.62-0.76%, and FPGA distribution yields **sublinear** time scaling — only decoder demonstrating $T(d)$ decreasing with $d$ given $O(d^3)$ parallelism [4]
* Neural transformer decoders outperform MWPM on **experimental data** by 25% and enable analog readout exploitation, with path to 1 µs real-time at $d=11$ [6][15][16]

Future work: *Hierarchical decoding* (CNN → UF → MWPM) with neural reweighting, erasure conversion integration ($r_e=0.98$ threshold 6.27% [18]), and ASIC implementation for $d=101$ at 1 µs. The decoder is no longer a post-processing afterthought but co-designed with qubit layout, control electronics, and error model — *decoding-aware quantum architecture*.
