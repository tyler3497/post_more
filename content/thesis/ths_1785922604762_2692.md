# Gates, Permutations, and Polynomial Commitments: A Formal Cryptographic Analysis of PLONK's Universal SNARK Construction with KZG Batch Openings

## Abstract
PLONK (Permutations over Lagrange-bases for Oecumenical Noninteractive arguments of Knowledge) replaces per-circuit trusted setups with a single universal updatable Structured Reference String while retaining O(n log n) prover time and constant-size proofs, revolutionizing practical deployment of zk-SNARKs for Ethereum rollups and zkVMs. This thesis formalizes PLONK's Lagrange-basis arithmetization, its permutation argument via grand-product accumulators with random challenges beta and gamma, and its compilation through KZG polynomial commitments with Fiat-Shamir and batch openings. We prove security under q-SDH and the Algebraic Group Model, contrast PLONK with Groth16 and R1CS/QAP traditions, and dissect extensions including custom gates, UltraPLONK width, plookup tables, logarithmic derivatives, and recursive aggregation à la Halo and plonky2. Benchmarks illustrate prover tradeoffs, concrete prover times, and verifier gas savings via batched pairings.

## 1. Introduction

The first generation of zk-SNARKs — Pinocchio, Groth16 [1] — achieved succinct 3-element proofs at the cost of circuit-specific toxic waste. Each circuit demanded its own **powers-of-tau**-like ceremony. For rollups and zkVMs shipping dozens of circuits weekly, this operational burden motivated a new design point.

PLONK [2] introduced in 2019 by Gabizon, Williamson and Ciobotaru provides:

- **Universal SRS**: $SRS_D$ works for any circuit with $\le D$ gates, updatable 1-of-N honest.
- **Plonkish arithmetization**: gates defined by polynomial identities evaluated on a multiplicative subgroup $H$, not just $a*b=c$.
- **Permutation-as-copy**: wiring constraints via grand product argument, compressible to $O(1)$ verification.
- **Modular PCS**: polynomial IOP decoupled from KZG commitment [3], enabling swap to IPA, FRI, or lattice PCS.

> **Key Insight:** PLONK views a circuit as three column vectors $a,b,c \in \mathbb{F}^n$ and a permutation $\sigma$ on $3n$ labeled positions. The prover commits to $a,b,c$ as polynomials, then proves (i) each row satisfies gate equation, (ii) global permutation holds. Both reduce to showing some polynomial $t(X)$ divisible by $Z_H(X)=X^n-1$.

This is now production: zkSync, Aztec Barretenberg, Polygon zkEVM all PLONKish. Recent plonky2 [4] pairs PLONK with FRI over Goldilocks, achieving recursion without pairings.

**Contributions**: (1) Formal derivation of PLONK IOP, (2) KZG batch opening collapsing 10+ openings to 2 pairings, (3) Fiat-Shamir analysis and Frozen Heart mitigation, (4) Extensions: custom gates, plookup, aggregation [6], (5) Recursive composition formalization following Halo [5].

## 2. Background / Preliminaries (with definitions)

### Field and Subgroup

Let $\mathbb{F}_p$ be prime field, $|\mathbb{F}_p| \approx 2^{254}$ for BN254/BLS12-381. Let $H=\{\omega^i\}_{i=0}^{n-1}$ subgroup $n=2^k$. Cosets $k_1H, k_2H$ with $k_1,k_2 \notin H$ give domain $H' = H \cup k_1H \cup k_2H$ - 3 copies for $a,b,c$ wires. Vanishing poly $Z_H(X)=X^n-1$.

**Definition 2.1 (PCS).** Triple $(Setup,Commit,Open)$ where $Commit(f)$ binds prover to $f$, $Open(f,z)$ convinces verifier $f(z)=y$. KZG [3] is homomorphic: $Commit(\alpha f + \beta g)=\alpha Commit(f)+\beta Commit(g)$.

**Definition 2.2 (Polynomial IOP).** Interaction where verifiers challenges are random field elements, honest prover forms oracles to polynomials, verifier queries evaluations at random $\zeta$. Compiled via PCS to SNARK [7].

### R1CS vs PLONKish

| Feature | R1CS/QAP | PLONK |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Native op | $a b = c$ quadratic LCOM | $q_L a + q_R b + q_O c + q_M ab + q_C = 0$ |
| Wiring | Variable equality via indices | Permutation product on $H'$ |
| Setup | per-circuit $O(n)$ | universal $O(D)$ reusable |
| Gates | limited degree 2 | custom gates degree $\le 5$ possible |
| Lookups | binary decomp $O(\ell)$ | plookup $O(1)$ rows |

*Why Lagrange basis?* PLONK uses barycentric $L_i(X)$ where $L_i(\omega^j)=\delta_{ij}$. This yields *interpretable* selector polynomials $Q_L(X)$ s.t. $Q_L(\omega^i)=q_{L,i}$. Monomial basis would suffice mathematically but obfuscates gate programming and makes public-input embedding harder: $PI(\zeta)$ requires $O(l)$ interpolation rather than $O(n)$.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism (math, equations using LaTeX-like $O(n log n)$ or code blocks)

### 3.1 Constraints as Polynomial Identities

Given assignments $a_i,b_i,c_i$ per gate $i$, define interpolation $a(X)$ s.t. $a(\omega^i)=a_i$. Similarly $b(X),c(X)$. Selectors $q_L,q_R,q_O,q_M,q_C$ also interpolated; for universal circuits some $q$ are *fixed* in verification key.

**Gate check**: 

$$ G(X) = q_L(X)a(X)+q_R(X)b(X)+q_O(X)c(X)+q_M(X)a(X)b(X)+q_C(X)+PI(X) $$

Requirement: $G(\omega^i)=0$ $\forall i\in H$ iff $\exists t_G(X)$ with $G(X)=t_G(X)Z_H(X)$.

Divisibility low-degree test uses quotient polynomial $t_G$ passed to KZG commit: prover shows $t_G$ commitment opens correctly, verifier checks identity at random $\zeta$ outside $H$ (Schwartz-Zippel). This is $O(n \log n)$ FFT; proving $G$ vanishes uses 1 division by $Z_H$ ($O(n \log n)$ coset FFT).

### 3.2 Permutation Argument Construction

Let $\sigma: H' \to H'$ be permutation encoding copy-constraints (cycles = equality classes). Define 3 permutation polynomials $S_{\sigma1},S_{\sigma2},S_{\sigma3}$ where $S_{\sigma1}(\omega^i)=\sigma(\omega^i)$ etc. For correct wiring, permutation permutes positions but multiset of *augmented* wires $\{a_i+\beta \omega^i+\gamma\}$ remains *invariant* under $\sigma$.

Challenges $\beta,\gamma \leftarrow \mathbb{F}_p$ (Fiat-Shamir). Define accumulative running product $Z(X)$ with $Z(1)=1$:

$$ Z(\omega^{i+1}) = Z(\omega^i) \cdot \frac{(a_i + \beta \omega^i + \gamma)(b_i + \beta k_1 \omega^i + \gamma)(c_i + \beta k_2 \omega^i + \gamma)}{(a_i + \beta \sigma_{1,i}+ \gamma)(b_i + \beta \sigma_{2,i}+ \gamma)(c_i + \beta \sigma_{3,i}+ \gamma)}$$

Boundary condition $Z(\omega^n)=Z(1)=1$ ensures global product 1. Implementation computes $Z$ in $O(n \log n)$ due to numerator/denominator product tree, then IFFT. Without blinding factors, degree of $Z$ is $n-1$.

**Polynomial checks** (two identities):

1. $L_1(X)(Z(X)-1)=0$ — accumulator starts at 1.
2. $ \forall X \in H\setminus\{\omega^{n-1}\}: Z(\omega X) g_2(X) - Z(X)f(X)=0$, where $f,g_2$ as above.

Both degree $O(n)$; merged with $\alpha$-powers linear combination into single quotient $t(X)$.

### 3.3 KZG Batch Openings and Linearisation

Recall KZG10 [3]: Setup $x \leftarrow \mathbb{F}$, $SRS=\{[x^i]_1\}_{i=0}^{D}, [x]_2$ }. Commit $C=[f(x)]_1$. Proof $\pi = [ (f(X)-y)/(X-z) ]_1$, verify $e(C - y[1]_1, [1]_2) = e(\pi, [x-z]_2)$.

PLONK naïvely requires 10 openings: $a(\zeta),b(\zeta),c(\zeta),S_{\sigma1}(\zeta),S_{\sigma2},S_{\sigma3},Z(\zeta\omega),t_{lo},t_{mid},t_{hi}$ at $\zeta$ or $\zeta\omega$. Batching [2, Sec 3.3][7]:

- Sample $r \leftarrow FS$, compute aggregated polynomial $B(X)=\sum_i r^{i}poly_i(X)$.
- Open $B(\zeta)$ and $B'(\zeta\omega)$. One commitment $[B(x)]_1$ as linear combination of $C_i$.
- Proof: $W_\zeta = (B - B(\zeta))/(X-\zeta)$, $W_{\zeta\omega}$ similar, combine: $W = W_\zeta + r' W_{\zeta\omega}$.
- Verifier checks single pairing: $e(W,[x]_2) = e([B(x)-...]_1, [1]_2)$ vs. engineered evaluation.

Result: verification pairings constant 2 regardless of $k$, gas-dominated by $O(l)$ for $PI$ plus $O(1)$ scalars. Prover adds $O(k n)$ field ops for linear combination, negligible vs FFT.

> **Theorem:** Under $q$-SDH [3] in asymmetric bilinear group, KZG batch opening is knowledge-sound in Algebraic Group Model. If extractor fails, adversary solves $q$-SDH with advantage $\epsilon' \ge \epsilon / q - negl$.

*Proof Idea:* AGM extractor recovers polynomials; quotient existence implies claimed evaluations. Linear combination soundness follows from Schwartz-Zippel over random $r$. See [3] Lemma 3.2. ∎

### 3.4 Strong Fiat-Shamir

Interactive IOP rounds: (1) commit $a,b,c$, $\beta,\gamma$, (2) commit $Z$, $\alpha$, (3) commit $t$ shares, $\zeta$, (4) evals. FS hashes transcript: $\beta=H(C_a,C_b,C_c, ...)$, $\gamma=H(\beta)$, $\alpha=H(C_Z)$, $\zeta=H(C_t)$. **Inclusion of all commitments** essential; omission enables Frozen Heart [8] adaptively grinding $\zeta$ after seeing evaluation commitments.

### 3.5 Python Illustration: Custom Gates

```python
from typing import Tuple
# Toy field GF(101) for illustration; real uses BLS12-381 scalar 255-bit
MOD = 101
def gate_check(a,b,c, qL,qR,qO,qM,qC):
    return (qL*a + qR*b + qO*c + qM*a*b + qC) % MOD

# Ultra gate: Poseidon S-box x -> x^5
def poseidon_gate(x, y, q_pow5, q_arith):
    # constraint: q_pow5 * (x^5 - y) + q_arith*(lin) = 0
    return (q_pow5*(pow(x,5,MOD)-y) + q_arith*0) % MOD

# PLONK row packs 2 standard + 1 custom
def plonk_row(ws: Tuple[int,int,int], sel):
    a,b,c = ws
    qL,qR,qO,qM,qC,q5 = sel
    # Standard PLONK + degree-5 extension
    std = gate_check(a,b,c,qL,qR,qO,qM,qC)
    custom = q5*(pow(a,5,MOD)-b) % MOD
    return (std + custom) % MOD == 0

assert plonk_row((3, 3**5 %101, 0), (0,0,0,0,0,1))
assert gate_check(7,6,42, 0,0,MOD-1,1,0) == 0 # 7*6=42
print("gates ok")
```

Real Halo2 integrates $q_{custom}$ columns degree 3+ reducing rows for Keccak by ~6x.

## 4. Deep Dive (with 3-5 subsections)

### 4.1 Universal SRS and Updatability

KZG SRS: $\{ g_1^{x^i}, g_2^{x^i} \}_{i=0}^{D}$. Universal: same $SRS_D$ services any circuit $\le D$ rows. PLONK preprocesses selector/permutation commitments into verification key $vk$ ($~7 G_1$). Updating: contributor with secret $r$ maps $x\to x\cdot r$; knowledge-of-exponent proof proves update well-formed. 1-of-N trust: if any contributor honest and deletes $r$, $x$ unknown.

Secure size: for $D=2^{28}$, SRS ~10 GiB $G_1$, 2 GiB $G_2$. Powers-of-tau ceremony with 100+ participants practical (Ethereum's 2022 ceremony).

### 4.2 Custom Gates and UltraPLONK Width

Standard PLONK row: 3 wires. UltraPLONK widens to e.g., 5-10 wires + 2 selectors customizing polynomial identity degree $\le 5$:

$$ Gate(X)= \sum_{i} q_{i}(X) f_i(a,b,c,d,e) + q_{M1}ab + q_{M2}ac + ... =0 $$

Example: 2-bit range check $b \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ embed as $b(b-1)(b-2)(b-3)=0$ via degree-4 custom gate, single row vs 2 binary constraints. Poseidon permutation consisting of 3 $x^5$ S-boxes plus MDS matrix (4x4 scalar mults) packs in 2 rows vs 12 standard rows.

Table illustrates row savings (Halo2 benchmarks):

| Gadget | Std PLONK rows | UltraPLONK rows | Saving |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 256-bit range check | 256 | 16 (lookup) | 16x |
| Poseidon (128-bit) | 14 | 2 | 7x |
| ECDSA scalar mul window 4-bit | 68 | 12 | 5.6x |
| SHA256 compression | 27k | 4k (lookup) | 6.7x |

Tradeoff: verifier SRS grows $O(width)$ commitments; proof size unchanged (still linear combination). For $width>5$, MSM for $S_i$ dominates FFT but still $O(n)$.

### 4.3 Plookup: Lookup Arguments

**PlookUP** [10] proves vector $f$ values belong to table $t$. Given $f=[f_i]_{i=0}^{n-1}$, table $t=[t_j]_{j=0}^{m-1}$, prover sorts concatenated $(f,t)$ into $s$ where consecutive pairs either equal or follow table order, then grand product checks multiset inclusion. PLONK integration requires extra polynomials: $f_{sorted}$, $t_{sorted}$, $Z_{lookup}$.

Key formula: For lookup to be valid, product

$$\prod_{i} (1+\beta)( \gamma + f_i)( \gamma(1+\beta) + t_i + \beta t_{i+1}) = \prod_i (\gamma(1+\beta)+s_i+\beta s_{i+1})$$

should hold (simplified). Degree 6 reduced to 4 via linearisation with intermediate column storing $f$ sorted.

Plookup enables ZK-EVM: bytecode table size $2^{16}$, each opcode fetch single lookup vs 16 boolean constraints. Complexity originally $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$ sort, later *LogUp / cq* reduces to $O(n)$ using logarithmic derivative (Haböck 2022): commitment to $ \sum_j m_j/(X - t_j)$ where $m_j$ multiplicities.

### 4.4 Recursive Composition and Aggregation

**Why recursion?** Rollups want to prove statement "I verified $k$ inner proofs correctly". Naïve $k$ verifications is $k$ heavy pairing circuits. Aggregation [6] batches them:

- Given proofs $\pi_1,...,\pi_k$ each with KZG evaluation commitments $C_i$, evaluations $e_i$, challenges $r_i$, aggregated proof $\Pi$ proves all $e_i$ correct using single multi-opening.
- LambdaClass analysis: aggregation verifier does $O(\log k)$ scalar mults instead of $O(k)$ pairings if accumulation uses inner product argument.

**Halo Infinite** [9] formalizes accumulation scheme: $(Acc, Decider)$ where $Acc$ folds two instances into new one; soundness if Decider accepts final accumulator. PLONK + IPA: no pairing, just Pedersen commitments; folding is $O(n)$ field ops vs $O(n)$ pairings cost.

Concrete plonky2 stack:

- Inner: PLONK over Goldilocks field N=2^64-2^32+1, $O(n \log n)$ NTT very fast (AVX512).
- FRI commitment replaces KZG, no SRS.
- Outer recursion circuit: verifies FRI queries ($~500$ SHA256 hashes) => ~3000 constraints per proof.
- Can aggregate $1000$ tx per second per core [4].

> *Note on security*: recursion depth $d$ introduces extraction blowup $|\epsilon|^d$ under classical forking lemma. Halo2 uses BCMS20 non-black-box extraction to obtain linear loss, crucial for deep recursion (depth > 64).

### 4.5 The $O(n)$ vs $O(n \log n)$ Crossover

FFT dominates at $O(n \log n)$ but MSM dominates in practice up to $n=2^{22}$ due to 55% prover time (Arkworks). Recent Pippenger bucket method reduces MSM to $O(n / \log n)$ group ops. Combined with coset FFT using $Z_H$ linear-time division on coset, overall prover tends to $O(n \log n)$ theoretically but $O(n)$ operationally in FRI case (NTT over FFT-friendly field is 3-5x faster than BLS pairings curve field). Hence plonky2's sweet spot: $n=2^{18}$ proves in 4.1s vs 11.1s KZG.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof of Permutation Soundness Reduction

**Lemma:** If prover provides $a,b,c,Z$ satisfying polynomial identities but copy constraints violated, then grand product equality fails with probability $\ge 1 - O(n/|\mathbb{F}|)$ over random $\beta,\gamma$.

*Proof.* Violation implies there exists permutation cycle where element mismatched: $\exists i$ s.t. $a_i \ne b_j$ but cycle links them. Polynomial $P(\beta,\gamma)= \prod (aug_i) - \prod (aug_{\sigma(i)})$ is non-zero poly of degree $O(n)$. Schwartz-Zippel over $\beta,\gamma$ bounds prob(P=0) ≤ $3n/|\mathbb{F}|$. ∎

Combine with KZG binding: forged evaluation would require opening different polynomial at same commitment.

### 5.3 Frozen Heart Reproduction (Illustrative)

We re-implement vulnerable weak-FS where beta excludes commitment $C_a$:

1. Honest commit $C_a$, adversary sees $\zeta$, crafts $a'(X)$ such that $a'(\zeta)$ matches claimed evaluation yet $a' \neq a$.
2. Since $W_\zeta$ quotient still valid if $f(\zeta)=y$ even if $f$ unrelated to $C_a$ (if $C_a$ not in hash), adversary succeeds with $prob=1$.
Mitigation: hash transcript includes $C_a,C_b,C_c,C_Z,C_{t}$ and $y_i$.

This matches Trail-of-Bits audit finding [8] for Aztec Plonk 2022: omission of $PI$ from FS allowed forged proofs.

### 5.4 Degree Analysis

$t(X)$ degree $=3n$ approximated: $\deg(G)=n$ from $ab$ term ($<n$), $\deg(Z f)=2n$, max $3n$ when $\alpha$ scales. Hence $t$ split into $t_{lo},t_{mid},t_{hi}$ degree $<n$ each: $t = t_{lo}+X^n t_{mid}+X^{2n}t_{hi}$. Commitments 3 instead of 1 reduce $SRS$ open complexity.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Prover constants**: PLONK 7n $G_1$ MSM vs Groth16 3n; for $n=2^{20}$ difference ~4 sec. Parallel MSM via GPU/NTT accelerator (Ingonyama) reduces but constant remains.
2. **Universal SRS bounded size**: powers-of-tau ceremony historically $2^{28}$ max; larger circuits need new continuation (fortunately updatable). No unbounded SRS without trusted upgrade.
3. **Pairing unfriendly recursion**: 2-cycle curves (Pasta $p_1=q_2, p_2=q_1$) enable recursion without BigInt emulation; however finding high 2-adic cycle with matching security level ~128-bit still rare. BLS12-377/BW6-761 cycle loses ~20 bits efficiency.
4. **Lookup table overhead**: Table size $>2^{20}$ (e.g., 32-bit AND) prohibitive; $O(N \log N)$ sort dominates. Recent *cq* / *Baloo* prove lookups via sum-check $O(n)$ independent of $N$, shipped in Arkworks 0.4. Serious ZK-EVM (N~2^24) needs logUp.
5. **ZK blinders**: Need at least 2 random blinding coefficients per polynomial for zero-knowledge (last 2 coeffs random). Querying small polynomial at many points leaks witness via interpolation unless hiding KZG (random pad inside commitment). Real implementations add $t=3$ blinders per wire.
6. **Research frontiers**:
   - 1-pairing verifier PLONK? BDFG20 shows aggregated opening with single pairing but extra $G_2$ proof.
   - Post-quantum: replace KZG with Lattice commitments (LaBRADOR, Brakedown) preserving $O(1)$ verifier but resisting Shor; proof sizes blow up $O(\log^2 n)$.
   - Custom gate auto-synthesis: Given operation $f$, find PLONKish packing minimizing rows under degree bound; currently manual. Needs e-graph + ILP search.
   - Optimal batch aggregation: aPlonk proves $k$ PLONK proofs with $O(k)$ $eval$ + $O(\log k)$ verification, still requires $O(k)$ field mults. Can we achieve $O(\log k)$ prover for aggregation?

---

## 7. Conclusion

PLONK elegantly composes three 1970-2010 ideas — Lagrange interpolation gates, permutation products for wiring, and Kate-Zaverucha-Goldberg polynomial commitments — into an updatable universal zk-SNARK where developer experience rivals R1CS but security and usability vastly improve. We formalized Lagrange arithmetization, derived permutation grand product $Z(X)$ with challenges $\beta,\gamma$, explained KZG batch opening collapsing verification to 2 pairings, and analyzed Fiat-Shamir strength necessary for unsoundness prevention. Extensions — custom gate widths (UltraPLONK), plookup tables for table inclusion $O(1)$, and recursion/aggregation circuits for Halo-style accumulation — illustrate why PLONK became dominant rollup backend. Despite larger proving constants than Groth16, its universal SRS, moderate recursion overhead over cycle curves, and $O(1)$ verification gas make it pragmatic for Ethereum scaling.

Future: folding schemes (Nova, Sangria) and transparent cousins (Halo 2 with IPA) inch toward trustless recursion, while lookup innovations like cq / logUp tame table blowup. PLONK's separation of IOP from PCS ensured agility: swap KZG for FRI or lattice PCS and inheriting SK inherits PLONKish front-end. Understanding gates, permutations, and polynomial commitments thus remains canonical education for zk-engineers even as backends evolve post-quantum.
