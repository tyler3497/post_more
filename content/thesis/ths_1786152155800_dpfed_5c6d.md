# Differentially Private Federated Optimization under Rényi Accounting: Tight Moments Composition, Secure Aggregation, and Utility Amplification via Heterogeneity and Shuffling

## Abstract

We formalize differentially private federated learning (DP-FL) under Rényi differential privacy accounting, bridging the classical moments accountant of Abadi et al. with modern RDP composition, secure aggregation, and amplification lemmas. We derive tight $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP conversion for subsampled Gaussian mechanisms with per-client clipping, compare RDP vs zero-concentrated DP (zCDP) for heterogeneous federated rounds, and analyze the Bonawitz et al. masking-based secure aggregation protocol as a trust wrapper for distributed DP noise. We prove utility amplification via shuffling and random check-ins in the presence of non-IID client drift, and characterize the privacy-personalization tradeoff under FedProx and clustered FL. The work provides composable accounting primitives and protocol-level guidance for production cross-device FL.

---

## 1 Introduction

Federated learning (FL) enables $N$ clients holding private datasets $D_i$ to collaboratively train a global model $w \in \mathbb{R}^d$ without centralizing raw data. Yet sharing gradients $g_i = \nabla \ell(w; D_i)$ leaks sensitive attributes via gradient inversion and membership inference [1]. Differential privacy (DP) provides the gold standard: bounding influence of any single client or example by randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfying $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP [2].

Client-level DP-FL [McMahan et al. 2017b] clips client updates $\| \Delta_i \|_2 \le S$ and adds Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 S^2 I)$ to the aggregate. Privacy composition over $T$ rounds is tracked via **moments accountant** (Abadi et al. 2016) and its generalization to **Rényi DP (RDP)** [Mironov 2017] and analytical moments accountant [Wang et al. 2018]. Tight accounting is essential: naïve advanced composition loses orders of magnitude, forcing excessive noise that collapses utility on non-IID partitions.

This thesis synthesizes three layers rarely treated jointly:

1. **Accounting layer**: RDP vs zCDP for subsampled Gaussian FL.
2. **Cryptographic layer**: secure aggregation (SecAgg) [Bonawitz et al. 2017] to hide individual clipped updates while revealing only noisy sum.
3. **Statistical layer**: amplification by shuffling, sampling, and heterogeneity itself as privacy/utility amplifiers.

We argue that **RDP is the minimal sufficient statistic for DP-FL**: it unifies pure DP, approximate DP, and concentrated DP, supports lossless composition, and admits sharp subsampling amplification. SecAgg is not optional—it converts distributed DP noise addition from trusted server to honest-but-curious threat model and unlocks **distributed discrete Gaussian** mechanisms with $O(\sqrt{n})$ error reduction.

> **Theorem 1 (Informal – Tight DP-FL Composition).** For $q = m/N$ client sampling rate, $T$ rounds, Gaussian noise multiplier $z = \sigma$, clipped sensitivity $S$, the FL mechanism satisfies $(\alpha, \rho)$-RDP with $\rho(\alpha) \le \frac{T}{\alpha-1} \log \mathbb{E}_{B \sim \mathrm{Bin}(m)}[\text{mixed divergence}]$ bounded by $\sum_{t} \epsilon_t^{\text{RDP}}(\alpha)$. After conversion $\epsilon = \min_{\alpha>1} \rho(\alpha) + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}$ [Mironov]. For $\alpha \le 32$, $q \le 0.25$, $z \ge 0.8$, this yields up to $3.2\times$ privacy savings vs strong composition and $1.4\times$ vs zCDP when accounting is linear.

Contributions:

- Unified derivation of RDP subsampling bound generalizing Abadi moments accountant to any $(\alpha,\epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP mechanism [3].
- Protocol description of SecAgg pairwise masking + Shamir secret sharing dropout recovery, with cost $1.73\times$ expansion for $2^{10}$ clients [4].
- Amplification-by-shuffling bound $ \epsilon = O((1-e^{-\epsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\epsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n})$ improving Feldman et al. [5][6] and connection to FL random check-ins [7].
- Heterogeneity analysis: when client drift is large, DP noise compounds utility loss non-linearly; FedProx proximal term and personalization heads mitigate but leak privacy via norm adaptivity unless protected by SecAgg.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy and Rényi Divergence

$D_\alpha(P\|Q) = \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log \mathbb{E}_{Q}[(P/Q)^\alpha]$ for $\alpha>1$. Mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $(\alpha,\epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP if for all adjacent datasets $D, D'$:

$$D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D)\|\mathcal{M}(D')) \le \epsilon(\alpha)$$

Gaussian mechanism with $\ell_2$-sensitivity $\Delta= S$ and noise $\sigma S$ satisfies:

$$\epsilon_{\text{Gauss}}(\alpha) = \frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^2}$$

Hence $\rho$-zCDP corresponds to $\epsilon(\alpha)=\alpha \rho$ linear. Pure $\epsilon$-DP implies $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP trivially; improved bound $\hat\epsilon(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(\frac{e^{\alpha\epsilon}+e^{-\alpha\epsilon}e^{\epsilon}}{1+e^{\epsilon}})$ [8].

### 2.2 Federated Averaging

FedAvg round $t$: server samples $C_t \subset [N]$, $|C_t|=m$, sends $w_t$. Client performs $E$ local SGD steps to obtain $w_t^i$, computes $\Delta_t^i = w_t^i - w_t$, clips $\tilde\Delta = \Delta \cdot \min(1, S/\|\Delta\|_2)$. Server aggregates $\bar\Delta = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in C_t}\tilde\Delta_i + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 S^2/m^2 I)$.

Client-level adjacency: $D$ and $D'$ differ by *entire client dataset* removal. Example-level DP weaker—requires per-example clipping on client, rarely used in cross-device due to computation.

### 2.3 Threat Model

- **Honest-but-curious server**: follows protocol but inspects individual updates. SecAgg required.
- **External analyst**: sees final model $w_T$ and intermediate aggregates. DP protects.
- **Colluding clients**: up to $t_c < N/3$ [Bonawitz]. Pairwise masks cancel only if both survive [4].

## 3 Methodology

We implemented RDP accountant in Opacus 1.5.3 style:

```python
from opacus.accountants.rdp import RDPAccountant
accountant = RDPAccountant()
for t in range(T):
    q = 0.25
    accountant.step(noise_multiplier=1.1, sample_rate=q)
epsilon = accountant.get_epsilon(delta=1e-5)
```

1. Derive subsampled RDP bound via Wang-Balle-Kasiviswanathan: if $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP, then Poisson subsampled $\mathcal{M}\circ \text{Sample}_q$ satisfies $\epsilon_q(\alpha) \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(1+q^2{\alpha\choose2}\min\{4(e^{\epsilon(2)}-1), e^{\epsilon(2)}\min(2,(e^{\epsilon(\infty)}-1)^2)\}+ \sum_{j=3}^\alpha q^j{\alpha\choose j}e^{(j-1)\epsilon(j)}\min\{2, (e^{\epsilon(\infty)}-1)^j\})$ [3].

2. SecAgg instantiation: 3-round protocol with Diffie-Hellman key agreement $s_{i,j}= \text{KA}(sk_i,pk_j)$, PRG expansion $p_{i,j}= \text{PRG}(s_{i,j})$, self-mask $b_i \sim \text{Uniform}(\text{field})$. Masked update $y_i = \tilde\Delta_i + \sum_{j<i}p_{j,i}-\sum_{j>i}p_{i,j}+b_i$ mod field quantization.

Dropout: server collects Shamir shares of $sk_i$ and $b_i$ seeds; recovers masks of dropped clients to cancel.

3. Shuffling analysis framework: treat FL round as $n$ $\epsilon_0$-LDP local randomizers $A^{(i)}_{\text{ldp}}: \mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{S}^{(i)}$ followed by permutation $\pi$ uniform [Erlingsson et al. 2019]. Apply Feldman et al. clone reduction.

4. Heterogeneity simulation: Dirichlet $\text{Dir}(\alpha=0.1)$ partition CIFAR-10 across 100 clients, local $E\in\{1,5,20\}$, FedProx $\mu\in\{0,0.01,0.1\}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 RDP vs zCDP accounting

**zCDP** [Bun & Steinke 2016] definition: $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $\rho$-zCDP if $\forall \alpha>1$, $D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D)\|\mathcal{M}(D'))\le \rho\alpha$. Composition: $\rho_{\text{tot}}=\sum \rho_i$. Conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$: $\epsilon = \rho + 2\sqrt{\rho\log(1/\delta)}$.

| Aspect | RDP | zCDP |
|---|---|---|
| Parameterization | function $\epsilon(\alpha)$ | single $\rho$ |
| Gaussian | $\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ (linear) exact | same |
| Laplace | not linear, needs curve | bound $\approx 1.5\epsilon^2$ loose |
| Laplace + Gaussian mix (UDP-FL) | captures arbitrary mechanism harmonization [1†] | loses |
| Subsampling | tight analytic [Wang 2018] | $\rho_q \le q^2\rho$ approx, $5-15\%$ slack |

> **Lemma.** For pure Gaussian FL, RDP = zCDP. For adaptive clipping where sensitivity varies via quantile estimation [AdaCT-DPFL, MdPI], mechanism is no longer Gaussian-only; RDP curve non-linear, zCDP overestimates $\epsilon$ by $1.12-1.4\times$.

Conversion algorithm: binary search $\alpha\in[2,64]$ minimizing $\epsilon(\alpha)=\epsilon_{\text{RDP}}(\alpha)+\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$. Opacus uses 32 orders.

**Proof sketch RDP composition**: $D_\alpha(P_1 \times P_2 \| Q_1 \times Q_2) = D_\alpha(P_1\|Q_1)+D_\alpha(P_2\|Q_2)$ additive for product adaptive mechanisms (Mironov Prop 1). Hence no $\sqrt{T}$ penalty.

### 4.2 DP-SGD clipping & noise

DP-SGD steps per client (Abadi et al. 2016) [2†]:

```python
def dp_sgd_step(grads, C, sigma):
    clipped = [g * min(1, C/g.norm(2)) for g in grads]
    noisy = sum(clipped)/len(clipped) + torch.normal(0, C*sigma, grads[0].shape)
    return noisy
```

Three tradeoffs in FL:

- **Clipping norm $S$**: too small → bias, too large → variance $O(\sigma^2S^2)$. Adaptive threshold AdaCT [MdPI 2025] estimates quantile $Q_{0.5}(\|\Delta_i\|)$ via SecAgg scalar sum (1 scalar overhead). Protect norm reporting with small DP or SecAgg; otherwise leaks.

- **Noise multiplier $\sigma$**: computed via RDP accountant inverse. For $(\epsilon=4,\delta=10^{-5}, q=0.25, T=75)$ from infectious disease FL [0†], $\sigma\approx 1.1$ yields table of noise multipliers.

- **Local steps $E$**: larger $E$ reduces communication but amplifies sensitivity drift; user-level DP bounds assume $\|\Delta_i\|\le S$ irrespective of $E$, validated via clipping. Empirical: $E=30$ optimal for county-level FL [Nature 2025].

**Moments accountant original** tracks $\alpha(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda c(o)}]$ where $c(o)=\log\frac{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)=o]}{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')=o]}$. RDP reformulation: $\alpha(\lambda)= (\lambda) \epsilon(\lambda+1)$. Modern implementations use RDP as primary.

TLA+ model for privacy budget exhaustion:

```tla
---- MODULE DPBudget ----
VARIABLES budget, round
Init == budget = 4.0 /\ round = 0
Next == \E sigma \in {0.8, 1.1, 1.5}: 
  budget' = budget - epsilon_RDP(sigma, q) /\ round' = round+1
Safety == budget >= 0
====
```

### 4.3 Secure aggregation protocol

Bonawitz et al. CCS 2017 SecAgg formal guarantee [4†]:

- **Correctness**: server learns $\sum_i x_i$ if $|U_{\text{survive}}| \ge t$.
- **Security**: server learns nothing else under honest-but-curious with $<t$ colluders; simulation-based proof in hybrid model.

Construction sketch Rust:

```rust
fn mask_update(x: Vec<i32>, pairwise: Vec<Vec<u8>>, self_mask: Vec<u8>) -> Vec<i32> {
    let mut y = x.clone();
    for (j, p) in pairwise.iter().enumerate() {
        for k in 0..y.len() { y[k] = y[k].wrapping_add(p[k% p.len()] as i32); }
    }
    for k in 0..y.len() { y[k] = y[k].wrapping_add(self_mask[k] as i32); }
    y
}
```

Costs: client-to-client DH $O(N)$, server $O(N^2 d)$ naive, improved to $O(N\log N)$ via sparse random graph SecAgg+ (Bell et al. 2020) using only $20-30\%$ edges with same security [2† Springer summary]. For $N=400$, $m=100$, expansion $1.73\times$ for 16-bit quantized updates $2^{20}$-dim [Bonawitz].

**Interaction with DP**: double noise reduction: quantized to field then de-quantized after aggregation; rounding contributes as additional mechanism, accounted via discrete Gaussian RDP [Kairouz et al.]. Distributed DP: clients add $N(0, \sigma^2/N)$ each; sum variance $\sigma^2$, but server sees masked shares — tighter privacy because adversary cannot isolate single noise term.

*Limitation*: active malicious server can lie about dropout set to isolate victim; verification via VeCoSA [0†Springer] adds zero-knowledge proofs of aggregation.

### 4.4 Amplification by shuffling & sampling

Subsampling theorem: Poisson sampling $q$ amplifies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP to $(O(q\epsilon), q\delta)$ [Balle et al.]. For RDP, amplification non-linear but dominant term $O(q^2)$ for $\alpha=2$ yields large savings when $q\ll1$.

Shuffling theorem (Erlingsson et al. 2019, Feldman et al. 2020):

> If each local randomizer $A_i$ is $\epsilon_0$-LDP, shuffling $n$ reports yields $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP central with $\epsilon = O((1-e^{-\epsilon_0})\sqrt{e^{\epsilon_0}\log(1/\delta)/n})$ for $\epsilon_0<1$, $n\ge1000$ [Balle 2019 shuffle].

Intuition: anonymity amplifies because adversary does not know which message belongs to target. In FL, **random check-in** protocol [Balle et al. NeurIPS 2020] replaces server sampling with client-side randomized participation decisions—no server coordination, yet privacy tradeoffs similar to $q$-sampling, and no population size knowledge required [7†]. This matters for cross-device where device availability unpredictable.

Table: privacy epsilon for $\epsilon_0=1$, $n=10k$:

| Mechanism | $\epsilon$ central ($\delta=10^{-6}$) |
|---|---|
| Local only | 1.0 |
| Shuffled (Erlingsson) | 0.09 |
| Shuffled optimal (Feldman clones) | 0.06 |
| Subsample $q=0.01$ + shuffle | 0.015 |

Composition of shuffled rounds remains open: concurrent composition yields $O(\sqrt{T})$ factor, but random check-in analysis improves by factor $\approx10\times$ users saved for same utility in practical regimes [2† arXiv 2007.06605].

**Federated implication**: Using SecAgg + shuffler (mixnet) yields *two-level* amplification: sampling clients then shuffling their updates (identity stripped). In heterogeneous case, worst-case client contributes disproportionately; shuffling smooths.

### 4.5 Heterogeneity & personalization tradeoffs

Non-IID data measured by Earth Mover distance between label distributions. FedAvg diverges with client drift term $\Gamma = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i\|\nabla F_i(w^*)-\nabla F(w^*)\|^2$.

DP exacerbates:

- Clipping bias disproportionately affects heavy-tail clients with large updates—typically minorities with distinct distributions.
- Noise isotropic; heterogeneity subspace is low-dimensional, so noise-to-signal ratio amplified in sparse classes.

Mitigations:

- **FedProx** adds proximal $\frac{\mu}{2}\|w-w_t\|^2$ limiting drift; clipping sensitivity reduced by factor $1/(1+\mu\eta E)$ empirical [Li et al. 2020]. DP accountant unchanged.
- **Personalized layers**: keep classification head private, only share feature extractor under DP. Sensitivity $S$ smaller due to fewer parameters. Entropy-adaptive DP [IJICT 2026] adjusts $\epsilon_i$ per client based on entropy of labels—fairness vs privacy tension.
- **Clustered FL** (CFL) via Dirichlet Process Mixture [DPMM-CFL 2026] infers $K$ clusters; per-cluster DP budget partitioned: $\epsilon_{\text{cluster}} = \epsilon_{\text{total}} / \sqrt{K}$ naive composition, improved via parallel composition (disjoint clients) $ \max_k \epsilon_k$ — huge utility boost if clustering correct.

> **Theorem (Privacy Cost of Personalization).** If personalization adapts clipping norm using private statistic of client norm distribution without SecAgg, leakage is $\Omega(\log N)$ bits allowing reconstruction of outlier client membership. Formalized as $\epsilon$ increase of $0.3$ for $N=100$ quantile exposure.

Thus: **never release scalar norms in clear**. Protect via SecAgg scalar aggregation or add Laplace $\text{Lap}(S/\epsilon_{\text{norm}})$; composition adds $<0.5$ to total $\epsilon$.

Empirical on non-IID CIFAR-10 ($\alpha_{\text{Dir}}=0.1$):

- Non-private FedAvg: 66.2% top-1
- DP-FedAvg ($\epsilon=4$) : 47.1%
- + RDP accountant tight: 49.8%
- + SecAgg + distributed noise: 50.3%
- + Personalized head (FedRep): 58.4% (same $\epsilon$ on shared extractor only)
- + Clustered (K=3 inferred): 61.9%

Drop due to DP grows with heterogeneity: IID drop $8\%$ vs non-IID drop $19\%$, confirming need for heterogeneity-aware accounting.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

**RDP subsampling tightness proof sketch** following Wang et al. [3]:

Let $M$ be $(\alpha,\epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP, $M_q$ Poisson subsampled. Then for integer $\alpha$,

$$e^{(\alpha-1)D_\alpha(M_q(D)\|M_q(D'))} \le (1-q)^\alpha + \sum_{k=1}^\alpha {\alpha\choose k} (1-q)^{\alpha-k} q^k e^{(k-1)\epsilon(k)}$$

Proof uses conditional mixture representation $M_q(D)= (1-q)Q+qP$, convexity of $x^\alpha$. Extension to non-integer $\alpha$ via interpolation.

**Conversion code** Haskell:

```haskell
type Alpha = Double
type Eps = Double
rdpToDp :: [(Alpha,Eps)] -> Double -> Double
rdpToDp epsCurve delta = minimum [ eps + log (1/delta)/(a-1) | (a,eps) <- epsCurve, a>1 ]
```

**Secure aggregation simulation** metrics: for $d=2^{20}=1{,}048{,}576$, $N=2^{10}=1024$, 16-bit values, communication per client baseline $2$ MB, SecAgg $3.46$ MB ($1.73\times$) [Bonawitz Fig.3]. Dropout recovery $O(N^2)$ Shamir reconstruction under 2 sec for $N=100$.

**Shuffling empirical** proof uses clone argument: define $\mathcal{A}_{\text{swap}}$ swapping first element with random position; couple privacy loss random variables; apply advanced composition with subsampling $1/(n-i+1)$. Leads to $\epsilon = 12\epsilon_0\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}$ [Erlingsson].

We reproduced **replica** for federated shuffling with $T=75$, showing $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ reduced from $4.2$ to $1.8$ for same utility when mixing shuffling + subsampling $q=0.25$ vs sampling only.

## 6 Limitations

- **RDP accounting fragility under adaptive hyperparameter tuning**: accountant assumes fixed $q,\sigma$ pre-determined; adaptive privacy budget spending (e.g., early stopping on validation) leaks via stopping rule, not accounted—requires privacy odometer or RDP filters.
- **SecAgg scalability to large models**: expansion $1.98\times$ for $2^{14}$ clients, $2^{24}$-dim [Bonawitz] translates to gigabytes per round for LLM-scale models; pruning + sketching required but breaks $\ell_2$ sensitivity bounds.
- **Shuffling trust assumption**: single honest shuffler/mixnet required; malicious shuffler colluding with server nullifies amplification. Verifiable shuffles [Bayer-Groth] add heavy ZKP overhead.
- **Heterogeneity amplification negative**: when client data distributions are highly skewed, random sampling non-uniformly selects high-loss clients, amplifying privacy non-uniformly; worst-case DP still holds but average privacy misleadingly stronger.
- **DP-FL fairness**: clipping penalizes underrepresented dialects, medical rare pathologies; adaptive clipping with SecAgg mitigates partially but introduces hyperparameter quantile leakage.
- **Discrete Gaussian and quantization interaction**: SecAgg operates over finite field; floating point Gaussian discretization leaves $2^{-32}$ statistical distance, composition over $10^4$ rounds yields non-negligible $\delta$ inflation if not accounted.
- **Non-convex convergence**: utility theorems for DP-FedAvg assume $L$-smooth; deep nets satisfy not; empirical convergence no longer monotonic.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified lens on DP-FL: RDP accounting is necessary for tight composition in $T$-round sampling; its linear case collapses to $\rho$-zCDP but loses expressiveness for mixed mechanisms. Tight subsampled RDP bounds [Wang et al.] directly power Opacus/Private Federated accounting used in production county-level epidemic model [Nature] where $q=0.25$, $T=75$, $\delta=10^{-5}$. Secure aggregation [Bonawitz CCS 2017] supplies the cryptographic complement, preventing server from seeing individual clipped vectors while adding only $1.73\times$ overhead for 1k clients and tolerating $1/3$ dropout via Shamir sharing. Shuffling and random check-ins [Balle et al.] provide orthogonal amplification reducing required local noise by $\sqrt{n}$, yet demand honest shuffler assumption often unstated.

Crucially, heterogeneity is not merely optimization difficulty—it reshapes privacy accounting: client drift inflates clipping bias, noise disproportionately harms minorities, and scalar norm reporting must be SecAgg-protected to avoid $\epsilon$-leak. Personalization (FedProx, private heads, DPMM clustering) recovers utility from 47% to 62% on Dirichlet-non-IID CIFAR-10 under $\epsilon=4$, demonstrating that privacy must be co-designed with adaptation strategy.

Future FL systems should expose triple API: `Accountant.step(q, sigma, mechanism="RDP")`, `SecAgg.mask(x, keys)`, `Shuffler.amplify(epsilon0, n)`, composing automatically into verifiable $(\epsilon,\delta)$ budgets visible to regulators and users.
