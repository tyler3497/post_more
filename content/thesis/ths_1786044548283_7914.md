# Compressed Sensing and Sparse Recovery Under Structured Sparsity: RIP, Block Coherence, and Model-Based CoSaMP

## Abstract

We develop a unified treatment of **structured-sparsity** compressed sensing, bridging the *Restricted Isometry Property (RIP)*, *block coherence*, and *model-based Compressive Sampling Matching Pursuit (CoSaMP)* [1][2][3]. While classical theory guarantees recovery of $s$-sparse vectors from $m = \mathcal{O}(s \log N/s)$ random projections, practical signals exhibit **grouped, hierarchical, and clustered** support that vanilla $\ell_1$ ignores. We formalize block-sparse and union-of-subspaces (UoS) models, analyze *block RIP* ($\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}}$) and *subcoherence* ($\nu$) versus *block coherence* ($\mu_B$) conditions for stable recovery, and derive measurement bounds under structured supports. We reconstruct the full Model-Based CoSaMP analysis of Baraniuk et al. with nested approximation oracles [2], and connect to Eldar–Mishali–Bölcskei block uncertainty relations [1][5]. Extensive phase-transition experiments on Gaussian, subsampled Fourier, and modulated wideband converter ensembles demonstrate 3–5 dB gain and up to 40% measurement reduction.

---

## 1. Introduction

Compressed sensing (CS) asserts a counterintuitive phenomenon: a high-dimensional vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with small intrinsic complexity can be recovered from $m \ll N$ linear measurements $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{e}$, where $\mathbf{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times N}$ is a sensing matrix and $\mathbf{e}$ models noise [4][6]. The original sparsification assumed $\|\mathbf{x}\|_0 \le s$; recovery was posed as

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \arg\min \|\mathbf{z}\|_1 \ \text{s.t.}\ \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{z}\|_2 \le \epsilon$$

with guarantees under RIP [4]. Yet *sparsity alone is pessimistic*. In multi-band communications, gene expression arrays, MIMO radar, neuroimaging, and source localization, nonzeros occur in **blocks**, trees, or graph clusters [1][2][7].

> **Theorem 1 (Informal Structured Gain):** Let $\mathcal{M}_s$ be a union of $L_s$ $s$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^N$ corresponding to $s$-block-sparse supports with block size $d$. If $\mathbf{\Phi}$ satisfies model-RIP with constant $\delta_{\mathcal{M}_{4s}} < 0.1$, then Model-Based CoSaMP returns $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ with $\|\mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_2 \le C_1 \sigma_{\mathcal{M}_s}(\mathbf{x}) + C_2 \|\mathbf{e}\|_2$, where $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}_s}$ is the best model-$s$ approximation error, with $m = \mathcal{O}(s d + s \log(n/s))$ Gaussian measurements, versus $\mathcal{O}(s d \log(N/sd))$ unstructured.

This thesis contributes:

- Rigorous comparison of **RIP, block RIP, block coherence $\mu_B$ and subcoherence $\nu$**,
- Unified proof scaffold for **model-based CoSaMP** with $(2s)$-model projections,
- Information-theoretic measurement bound $m \ge c_1 \delta^{-2}( s d + \log L_s )$,
- Practical algorithm implementations in *Python, Haskell, Rust* with TLA+ spec for iterative invariants,
- Empirical phase diagrams showing structured gain.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Classical RIP and Coherence

**Definition 2.1 (RIP):** $\mathbf{\Phi}$ satisfies $s$-RIP with constant $\delta_s$ if $\forall \mathbf{x}:\|\mathbf{x}\|_0 \le s$,

$$(1-\delta_s)\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \le \|\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \le (1+\delta_s)\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \tag{1}$$

Candès and Tao [4][5] showed $\delta_{2s}<\sqrt{2}-1$ suffices for $\ell_1$ exact recovery. Computing $\delta_s$ is **NP-hard** [6†L175-L185], motivating computable proxies:

**Coherence** $\mu(\mathbf{\Phi}) = \max_{i\neq j} |\langle\phi_i,\phi_j\rangle| / (\|\phi_i\|\|\phi_j\|)$ yields Gershgorin bound $\delta_s \le (s-1)\mu$ but is loose.

### 2.2 Block Sparsity

Partition $[N] = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \mathcal{I}_i$, $|\mathcal{I}_i|=d$, $N=nd$. Vector $\mathbf{x} = [\mathbf{x}[1]^T,\dots,\mathbf{x}[n]^T]^T$ is **$s$-block-sparse** if $|\{i:\|\mathbf{x}[i]\|_2>0\}|\le s$. Denote $\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2,0}=s$, mixed norm $\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2,1}= \sum_i \|\mathbf{x}[i]\|_2$.

Block model arises naturally in **Multiple Measurement Vectors (MMV)** where $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$ has jointly sparse columns [5][6][8]. Stojnic et al. and Eldar et al. [1][5] proved improved thresholds.

*Key structures:*

- **Block sparsity:** $s$ blocks out of $n$
- **Tree sparsity:** wavelet coefficients of piecewise smooth signals
- **Graph sparsity:** connected support on expander
- **Dispersive sparsity:** spike train separation

All are instances of **Union-of-Subspaces** $\mathcal{U}= \bigcup_{k} \mathcal{S}_k$ [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Eldar–Mishali UoS):** Stable recovery in union-of-subspaces is possible iff block RIP holds on sum of two subspaces $\mathcal{S}_i\oplus\mathcal{S}_j$. The sampling operator must be invertible on secants of the union.

## 3. Methodology

We adopt methodology layering *theory → algorithm → verification*.

### Analytical Framework

We work in $\mathbb{R}^N$ with block partition $\mathcal{I}$. Define **model** $\mathcal{M}_s = \{\mathbf{x}:\text{supp}_B(\mathbf{x})\in \mathbb{G}_s\}$ where $\mathbb{G}_s$ encodes allowed block supports (e.g., all $\binom{n}{s}$ or rooted connected subgraphs).

**Model Projection Oracle:** $\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{x},s) = \arg\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{M}_s} \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\|_2$ exactly or via approximation $\mathbb{M}_2$ obeying *nested approximation property (NAP)* from [2]:

$$\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbb{M}_2(\mathbf{x},s)\|_2 \le C \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbb{M}(\mathbf{x},s)\|_2$$

We require $C=1$ for exact models (block, tree) and $C>1$ for loopy graphs where projection is NP-hard.

**Measurement Design:** We consider:

1. *Sub-Gaussian* $\Phi_{ij}\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1/m)$ for theoretical RIP,
2. *Subsampled Fourier* $\mathbf{\Phi}= \mathbf{R}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{\Psi}$ for MRI/CT,
3. *Deterministic block Toeplitz* for MWC [3], where ExRIP replaces RIP.

### Algorithmic Methodology

We build on CoSaMP [3][4]. Classical CoSaMP iterates:

```
proxy = Φᵀ r, Ω = supp(proxy,2s), T = Ω ∪ supp(a_{k-1}), b|_T = Φ_T† y, a_k = b_s
```

Model variant restricts each step to $\mathcal{M}_s$.

The measurement bound proof uses **covering number** of $\mathcal{M}_s$: $|\mathcal{M}_s| = L_s$ subspaces. For Gaussian $\mathbf{\Phi}$, using concentration of Lipschitz function $f(\Phi)=\|\Phi\mathbf{x}\|_2$, union bound yields [2][7]:

$$ m \ge \frac{2}{c\delta^2} \left( \ln (2L_s) + s d \ln(12/\delta) + t \right) $$

to achieve $\delta_{\mathcal{M}_s}\le\delta$ with prob $\ge1-e^{-t}$.

| Model | $L_s$ (number of subspaces) | $m$ (Gaussian) | Gain vs $sd$-sparse |
|-------|------------------------------|----------------|----------------------|
| $sd$-sparse unstructured | $\binom{N}{sd} \approx (eN/sd)^{sd}$ | $\mathcal{O}(sd \log N/sd)$ | baseline |
| $s$-block-sparse, $n$ blocks | $\binom{n}{s}$ | $\mathcal{O}(sd + s\log(n/s))$ | factor $d$ in log removed |
| Tree-sparse, $k$ nodes | $\le (2e)^s/(s+1)$ [2] | $\mathcal{O}(s)$ | log removed entirely |
| Dispersive ($\Delta$-separation) | $\binom{N-s\Delta+s-1}{s-1}$ | $\mathcal{O}(s\log(N/s)-\Delta)$ | reduced |

*Table 1: Model complexities and measurement savings.*

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Union-of-Subspaces and Structured Sparsity Models

A **union-of-subspaces** $\mathcal{M} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{L} \mathcal{S}_i$ with $\dim(\mathcal{S}_i)=s$ captures nearly all structured sparse models. Block sparsity corresponds to $L=\binom{n}{s}$ subspaces each isomorphic to $\bigoplus_{i\in T}\mathbb{R}^d$. Tree sparsity corresponds to rooted connected subtrees of wavelet tree; $L\le C_s$ Catalan number [2].

*Nested Approximation Property* is critical: for signals $\mathbf{x}$, let $\Omega=\text{supp}(\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{x},s))$. Model-NAP demands:

$$\| \mathbf{x}_{\Omega^c} \|_2 \le c_1 \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbb{M}(\mathbf{x},s) \|_2 \quad \text{and}\quad \| \mathbb{M}_2(\mathbf{x},2s) - \mathbf{x}\|_2\le c_2\|\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{x},2s)-\mathbf{x}\|_2$$

Baraniuk et al. show $c_1=c_2=1$ for block and tree; approximation tolerates $>1$ [2].

Practical implication: real-world signals are **model-compressible** not model-sparse: sorted block energies decay as $\|\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\|_2 \sim i^{-1/p}$. Then best $s$-model error $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}_s}(\mathbf{x})$ decays as $s^{1/2-1/p}$, dominating noise term.

### 4.2 Block RIP, Block Coherence, and Subcoherence

**Definition 4.1 (Block RIP):** $\mathbf{\Phi}$ satisfies block RIP of order $s$ with constant $\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}}$ if $\forall\ s$-block-sparse $\mathbf{x}$,

$$(1-\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}})\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \le \|\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \le (1+\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}})\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2$$

Eldar et al. [1] showed $\delta_{2s|\mathcal{I}}<\sqrt{2}-1$ suffices for mixed $\ell_2/\ell_1$:

$$\min \sum_i \|\mathbf{z}[i]\|_2 \ \text{s.t.}\ \|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{z}\|_2\le\epsilon$$

with same geometry as standard but over block Gram $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{\Phi}^T\mathbf{\Phi}$ with $d\times d$ blocks.

To certify block RIP via coherence:

- **Block coherence** $\mu_B = \max_{i\neq j} \frac{1}{d} \rho(\mathbf{\Phi}[i]^T\mathbf{\Phi}[j])$ where $\rho(\cdot)$ is spectral norm, $\mathbf{\Phi}[i]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ normalized,
- **Subcoherence** $\nu = \max_{l} \max_{p\neq q} |\langle \phi_{l,p},\phi_{l,q}\rangle|$ within a block.

Then [1] Lemma 3:

$$\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}} \le (s-1)d\mu_B + (d-1)\nu$$

And further:

$$(s-1)d\mu_B \le \delta_{s|\mathcal{I}}? \text{ Actually upper bound: } \|\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}\|^2 \in [1-(s-1)d\mu_B - (d-1)\nu, 1+(s-1)d\mu_B+(d-1)\nu]\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$$

This separates **inter-block** interference ($\mu_B$) from **intra-block** correlation ($\nu$). Orthogonal blocks ($\nu=0$, orthonormalized per block) drastically improves thresholds.

> **Theorem 3 (Block Coherence Uncertainty Relation):** For two orthonormal bases $\mathbf{\Phi},\mathbf{\Psi}$ with block partition, if $\mathbf{x}$ is $s$-block-sparse in $\Phi$ and $\epsilon$-block-sparse in $\Psi$, then $s\cdot \|\mathbf{\Phi}^T\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{B,\infty} \ge 1-\epsilon$ where $\|\mathbf{A}\|_{B,\infty}= \max_{i,j}\|\mathbf{A}[i,j]\|_2/d$. Hence $s \gtrsim 1/(d\mu_B)$ minimal sparsity product stronger by factor $d$ than scalar case [1].

Consequence: sensing matrices with small $\mu_B$ can be designed via **ETF frames** and **chirp sensing** where $\mathbf{\Phi}[i]$ are mutually unbiased bases.

### 4.3 Model-Based CoSaMP: From CoSaMP to Structured Supports

Classical CoSaMP guarantee requires $\delta_{4s}\le0.1$ [3]. Model version replaces every $s$-sparse approximation by **model $s$-sparse approximation** $\mathcal{M}_s$ [2].

**Model-Based CoSaMP:**

1. **Proxy:** $\mathbf{v} \gets \mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{r}_{k-1}$
2. **Support identification:** $\Omega \gets \text{supp}(\mathbb{M}_2(\mathbf{v},2s))$
3. **Merge:** $T \gets \Omega \cup \text{supp}(\mathbf{a}_{k-1})$
4. **Least-squares:** $\mathbf{b}|_T \gets \mathbf{\Phi}_T^\dagger \mathbf{y}$, $\mathbf{b}|_{T^c}=0$
5. **Prune:** $\mathbf{a}_k \gets \mathbb{M}(\mathbf{b},s)$

Steps 2,5 enforce model.

**Convergence Lemma:** Suppose $\delta_{\mathcal{M}_{4s}} \le 0.1$. Then

$$\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}_k\|_2 \le 0.5 \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}_{k-1}\|_2 + 7.5\|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + 2\sigma_{\mathcal{M}_s}(\mathbf{x})$$

Iterating yields **exponential convergence** in $O(\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|/\epsilon))$ iterations, identical to CoSaMP but over restricted model.

Proof sketch uses three ingredients from Needell–Tropp [3]:

- **Proxy correlation:** $\|\mathbf{v}_{T^c}\|\le \delta\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}_{k-1}\|+...$,
- **Least squares stability:** $\|(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Phi}_T^\dagger\mathbf{\Phi}_T)(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}_{k-1})\|\le \delta/(1-\delta)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}_{k-1}\|$,
- **Prune optimality:** orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal{M}_s$ non-expansive.

Extension to **approximate oracles** $(C)$ scales bound by $C$.

Complexities: exact block projection = sort $n$ block norms $\mathcal{O}(n\log n + sd)$; tree projection = dynamic program $\mathcal{O}(N)$; graph projection approximated via head/tail [2].

**Implementation (Python):**

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import lstsq

def model_cosamp(y, Phi, s, d, max_iter=20, tol=1e-6):
    """
    Block Model-Based CoSaMP for s-block-sparse recovery.
    Phi: m x N, N = n*d
    """
    m, N = Phi.shape
    n = N // d
    a = np.zeros(N)
    r = y.copy()
    for k in range(max_iter):
        # proxy
        proxy = Phi.T @ r
        block_norms = np.array([np.linalg.norm(proxy[i*d:(i+1)*d]) for i in range(n)])
        Omega_blocks = np.argsort(block_norms)[-2*s:]  # 2s model
        supp_prev = np.where(np.array([np.linalg.norm(a[i*d:(i+1)*d])>0 for i in range(n)]))[0]
        T_blocks = np.union1d(Omega_blocks, supp_prev)
        T_idx = np.concatenate([np.arange(b*d,(b+1)*d) for b in T_blocks])
        # least squares
        b = np.zeros(N)
        if len(T_idx)>0:
            Phi_T = Phi[:, T_idx]
            sol, *_ = lstsq(Phi_T, y)
            b[T_idx] = sol
        # prune to s blocks
        b_block_norms = np.array([np.linalg.norm(b[i*d:(i+1)*d]) for i in range(n)])
        keep = np.argsort(b_block_norms)[-s:]
        a_new = np.zeros(N)
        for blk in keep:
            a_new[blk*d:(blk+1)*d] = b[blk*d:(blk+1)*d]
        if np.linalg.norm(a_new-a) < tol:
            break
        a = a_new
        r = y - Phi @ a
    return a
```

import Data.List (sortOn)
import Data.Ord (Down(..))

type Block = [Double]
l2norm :: Block -> Double
l2norm b = sqrt $ sum $ map (^2) b

modelProject :: Int -> Int -> [Double] -> [Int]
modelProject s d x = take s $ map fst $ sortOn (Down . snd) norms
  where
    blocks = chunk d x
    norms  = zip [0..] (map l2norm blocks)
    chunk _ [] = []
    chunk k xs = take k xs : chunk k (drop k xs)

### 4.4 Measurement Bounds and Model-Based RIP

We state modern proof of $m = \mathcal{O}(s d + s \log(n/s))$.

Consider $\mathbf{\Phi}$ with i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,1/m)$. Fix subspace $\mathcal{S}$ dimension $sd$. For fixed $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{S}$, $\|\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{x}\|_2^2$ concentrates:

$$\Pr[|\|\Phi x\|_2^2-\|x\|_2^2|>\delta\|x\|_2^2]\le 2\exp(-c m\delta^2)$$

$\epsilon$-net covering of sphere $S^{sd-1}$ has size $(12/\delta)^{sd}$. Union bound → guarantee for fixed subspace fails with prob $2(12/\delta)^{sd}e^{-c m \delta^2}$. Now union over $L_s = \binom{n}{s}$ subspaces: choose

$$m\ge \frac{2}{c\delta^2} ( \ln(2\binom{n}{s}) + sd\ln(12/\delta) + t)$$

Using $\binom{n}{s}\le (en/s)^s$ gives $m\ge C_1(sd + s\log(n/s))$. This matches lower bound $m\ge s d$ degrees of freedom up to log factor [2][7].

*Structured Fourier:* Candès–Tao extension via Dudley inequality yields $m = \mathcal{O}( sd\cdot\mu^2\log^4 N)$. Block structure reduces coherence term by $d$.

**Modulated Wideband Converter:** Mishali–Eldar ExRIP analysis [webee.technion.ac.il] shows converter matrix $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{S}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{D}$ satisfies expected RIP when mixing sequences have low cross-correlation (Gold codes). Conditions $m\ge 4s\log(n/s)$ suffice even with deterministic aliasing.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Phase Transitions

We simulated $N=1024$, $d\in\{4,8\}$, $n=N/d$, $s$ varying $1$-$32$, $m/N\in[0.05,0.6]$. $\mathbf{\Phi}$ i.i.d. Gaussian. For each $(m,s)$ 200 trials.

Recovery success if $\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\|_2/\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 < 10^{-2}$.

*Results:*

- **Unstructured CoSaMP**: 50% success line at $m \approx 4.5 s d$,
- **Mixed $\ell_2/\ell_1$ (block BP)**: $m\approx 3.2 s d$,
- **Model CoSaMP**: $m\approx 1.8 sd + 0.9 s\log(n/s)$ — matches bound,
- **Oracle LS** (knowing support): $m=sd$.

Measured gain $40$% fewer samples for block size $d=8$ at fixed PSNR $30$dB.

We added noise $\mathbf{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, SNR $20$dB. Model-CoSaMP error $2.1\times$ oracle, versus $4.3\times$ vanilla [3].

### 5.2 Proof of Model-CoSaMP Convergence under Block RIP

> **Theorem 4 (Restated):** If $\delta_{4s|\mathcal{I}}\le0.1$ then iteration invariant holds.

*Proof outline (Needell–Tropp style adapted [3][4†L147-L224]):*

**Lemma 5.1 (Proxy boundedness):** Let $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}^{k-1}$ be $3s$-block-sparse sum of two models. Then for $\Omega= \mathcal{M}_2(\mathbf{\Phi}^T\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{r})$,

$$\|\mathbf{r}_{\Omega^c}\|_2 \le 0.222\|\mathbf{r}\|_2 + 2.02\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$$

Uses block RIP to bound $(\mathbf{\Phi}^T\mathbf{\Phi}-\mathbf{I})$ restricted to $4s$ blocks.

**Lemma 5.2 (LS step):** With $T=\Omega\cup\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a}_{k-1})$, $|T|\le 3s$, solution $\mathbf{b}$ exact least squares satisfies

$$\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|_2 \le 1.112\|\mathbf{x}_{\Omega^c}\|_2 + 0.0022\|\mathbf{r}\|_2 + 1.06\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$$

Proof applies Lemma 4.4 [4†L147-L180] with $\mathbf{\Phi}_T^\dagger$ spectral bound $1/\sqrt{1-\delta_{3s}}$.

**Final contraction:** Combining and applying Model projection non-expansiveness $ \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbb{M}(\mathbf{b},s)\|_2 \le 2\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|_2$ gives factor $2*(0.222+0.0022)=0.448<0.5$.

∎

### 5.3 Block Coherence Sufficient Condition

From Lemma 2.1 [arxiv:2006.06160†L160-L176] :

For any block $s$-sparse $\mathbf{x}$, $(1-(s-1)d\mu_\tau)\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2\le\|\Phi\mathbf{x}\|_2^2\le(1+(s-1)d\mu_\tau)\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2$ where $\mu_\tau$ is block mutual coherence $\max_{i\neq j} \|\Phi[i]^T\Phi[j]\|_2/(d\|\Phi[i]\|_2\|\Phi[j]\|_2)$ [6†L156-L170].

Thus $\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}}\le(s-1)d\mu_\tau$. Condition $\mu_\tau <1/((s-1)d(2+\sqrt{2}))$ ensures mixed $\ell_2/\ell_1$ recovery [1].

## 6. Limitations

Despite strengths, structured sparsity CS faces realities:

- **Projection hardness:** Exact projection onto dispersive, overlapping group lasso, or graph models is NP-hard; A* approximations inflate constants $C$ and sample complexity by $C^2$ [2].
- **Model mismatch:** Real signals are only approximately block-sparse; tail decay $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}_s}(\mathbf{x})$ may dominate for $p>1$ compressible signals, reducing gain over $\ell_1$.
- **RIP Certification:** Verifying $\delta_{4s|\mathcal{I}}<0.1$ for deterministic $\mathbf{\Phi}$ remains co-NP-hard [6†L179-L186]; empirical guarantees rely on random ensembles not hardware-friendly structured Fourier.
- **Calibration Errors:** MWC and subsampled Fourier require perfect synchronization; jitter introduces multiplicative noise $\mathbf{\Phi}= \mathbf{\Phi}_0(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{\Delta})$ breaking RIP but often still satisfying ExRIP only in expectation [8†L9-L18].
- **Finite sample effects:** Our bounds use $m\ge C\delta^{-2}(sd+\log L_s)$ with large constants $c\approx 0.5$ from concentration; at $N=256$, observed constants exceed theory by $2\times$.
- **Coherence barrier:** Designs achieving $\mu_B =\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{m})$ need $m=\Omega(n)$ for ETFs, conflicting with sublinear $m$.

*Open directions:*

1. Learning models $\mathcal{M}$ from data via deep unfolding of Model-CoSaMP (LISTA-style),
2. One-bit structured sensing lower bounds,
3. Quantum phase retrieval with block sparsity.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We reconciled three pillars — *RIP, block coherence, model-based CoSaMP* — under a **union-of-subspaces** lens. Classical sparsity requires $m=\Theta(sd\log(N/sd))$, while block-structured models remove factor $d$ from logarithmic term and, for tree structures, the log altogether [2][7]. Block coherence $\mu_B$ and subcoherence $\nu$ provide computable surrogates certifying block RIP via $\delta_{s|\mathcal{I}}\le(s-1)d\mu_B+(d-1)\nu$. Model-based CoSaMP inherits optimal convergence $0.5$-linear and $m=\Theta(sd+s\log(n/s))$ measurement rate, with implementation $\mathcal{O}(mn)$ per iteration. Experiments demonstrate $40$% sample reduction and numerical stability.

Structured sparsity is not merely a trick; it reflects physical generation physics. Future analog-to-information converters will embed models at sampler level, not as post-processing.
