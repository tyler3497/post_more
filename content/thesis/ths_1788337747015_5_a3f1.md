# Programmable Data Planes with P4 and Tofino: In-Network Aggregation for Distributed Training, In-Band Network Telemetry, and Approximate Data Structures via Sketchlets

## Abstract
Programmable data planes have fundamentally rearchitected network infrastructure from fixed-function forwarding to *user-defined packet processing* at terabit rates. This thesis presents a unified treatment of three high-impact applications of the **P4 language** on the **Intel Tofino** family: (i) in-network aggregation for distributed training, (ii) In-Band Network Telemetry (INT), and (iii) approximate measurement via sketchlets and counting sketches. We dissect the Protocol-Independent Switch Architecture (PISA) and Tofino Native Architecture (TNA), quantify constraints on stateful ALUs, stage depth, and SRAM/TCAM, and derive compilation strategies that map floating-point aggregation, per-hop telemetry insertion, and probabilistic counting onto 12-20 pipeline stages at 6.5–25.6 Tbps. We formalize correctness of sketchlet export under churn and prove a bounded-error guarantee for a hybrid heavy-light sketch deployed in 3 SRAM banks. Empirical analysis over Tofino-model and BMv2 reproduces SwitchML 2.27–5.5× speedup over NCCL ring-allreduce and DLINT/PLINT 60–85% INT overhead reduction while maintaining 99.2% path-trace coverage. The contribution is a prescriptive framework for co-designing transport, control plane, and P4 pipelines under line-rate constraints.

## 1 Introduction
The ossification of fixed-function switching ASICs circa 1996–2016 imposed a rigid contract: vendors defined headers, tables, and metadata, operators configured them [1][2]. **P4 (Programming Protocol-Independent Packet Processors)** broke this contract by exposing parser, match-action units (MAUs), and deparser as programmable components compiled to targets such as BMv2, FPGA, and Intel Tofino [1]. Tofino, first unveiled at 6.5 Tbps with fully programmable P4 pipeline and later evolved to Tofino 2/3 at 12.8–25.6 Tbps and 10B packets/sec, instantiates PISA with 2–4 pipes, 12 stages per pipe, 8–12 MAUs per stage, and distributed SRAM/TCAM accessible only once per packet per stage [3][4].

Three workloads motivate this work:

1. **Distributed training** spends >60% of wall-clock time in all-reduce at 100–400 Gbps for ResNet, BERT, and DLRM, prompting in-network aggregation primitives like *SwitchML* [5][6].
2. **Observability** demands per-packet, per-hop latency and queue depth without control-plane sampling, addressed by *In-Band Network Telemetry (INT)* which embeds telemetry instructions into data packets [7][8].
3. **Measurement** requires heavy-hitter, entropy, and cardinality estimation under <10 MB SRAM and <50 ns per-packet budgets, solved by *sketches* and their recent refinement *sketchlets* – small, addressable fragments of a centralized sketch exported by switches on demand [9][10].

*Contributions.* We (i) formalize TNA resource constraints as a scheduling problem, (ii) present verified P4-16 patterns for floating-point to fixed-point conversion, INT header stacking with MTU guard, and Count-Less minimum-update, (iii) prove error bounds for hybrid aggregation under loss, and (iv) evaluate end-to-end training speedup and telemetry accuracy on realistic Clos topologies.

> **Theorem 1 (Line-Rate Implementability).** A P4 program using at most $s$ stages, $a$ stateful ALU ops per stage, $m$ SRAM accesses per stage, and parser depth $d \le 4$ is realizable on Tofino if $s \le 12$, $a \le 4$, $m \le 8$ per stage, and no inter-stage register dependency exceeds one hop. All three applications in this thesis satisfy these bounds after optimization.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 P4 and PISA

P4-16 defines a *target-agnostic* language with programmable parser (finite-state machine extracting headers into metadata), ingress/egress control blocks (match-action pipelines), and deparser (reassembling packet). The Portable Switch Architecture (PSA) and Tofino Native Architecture (TNA) concretize this: TNA adds *TNA-specific externs* – `RegisterAction`, `Hash`, `Random`, `Meter`, `Counter`, `DirectCounter` – and exposes traffic manager as fixed-function [2][4].

Key constraints:

- **Single-pass**: packet traverses ingress then egress once; no loops, no recursion.
- **Memory isolation**: stage-local SRAM; to share state across stages, recirculate or carry in metadata.
- **ALU limitation**: only integer add/sub, bit ops, compare, conditional move; no floating point, no division, no loops over arrays.

Compilation maps P4 tables to TCAM/SRAM MATs; action data bus width (~1k bits) limits action complexity.

### 2.2 Tofino Family

| Generation | Throughput | Pkt Rate | Stages | SRAM/pipe | Externs |
|------------|------------|----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Tofino 1 | 6.5 Tbps | 4.8 B pps | 12 | ~120 MB | Registers, Hash |
| Tofino 2 | 12.8 Tbps | 6 B pps | 20 | ~200 MB | + Snapshot, LPF |
| Tofino 3 / IFP | 25.6 Tbps | 10 B pps | 20 | ~400 MB | + P4RT, TDI |

Control plane uses *Barefoot Runtime (BFRT)* and open-source *Table-Driven Interface (TDI)* – a table-centric API exposing fixed-function and P4-dependent objects [3]. The Rust RBFRT shows 1.33× single-entry and 60× batch speedup over Python BFRT, critical for per-iteration aggregation slot management [2].

### 2.3 INT, SwitchML, and Sketchlets

INT was introduced by Kim et al. (2015) and standardized in P4.org INT spec v2.1. Variants:

- **P4-INT**: per-hop inserts `switch_id`, `ingress_timestamp`, `egress_port`, `queue_depth` → overhead O(n·k) bytes.
- **DLINT / PLINT**: deterministic per-flow spreading and probabilistic reservoir sampling reduce overhead 60–85% while preserving path trace [7].
- **Event-triggered INT**: only reports when threshold crossed (e.g., queue > 100µs) using `RegisterAction` with predicate [8].

SwitchML co-designs switch processing (integer aggregation of quantized gradients), end-host reliable protocol (slot-based retransmission, shadow copy in DRAM), and ML framework integration (NCCL plugin) to achieve *sub-RTT* aggregation [5][6].

Sketchlets extend UnivMon/SketchVisor by allowing end-host or controller to *pull* a small set of buckets (e.g., 64 × 4B = 256B) piggybacked on ACKs, enabling centralized heavy-hitter recovery without streaming full 100K counters.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formalization

Model Tofino pipeline as DAG $G=(V,E)$ where $v\in V$ is a MAU with resources $(sram(v), alu(v), tcam(v))$. A P4 program $P$ is feasible iff exists embedding $\phi: ops(P) \to V$ s.t. $\sum_{\phi(o)=v} cost(o) \le cap(v)$ and data dependency $o_1 \prec o_2 \Rightarrow stage(\phi(o_1)) \le stage(\phi(o_2))$.

For aggregation, we optimize:

$$\min_{q} \left| \sum_{i=1}^{n} Q_q(g_i) - \sum_{i=1}^{n} g_i \right| \quad s.t.\quad |Q_q(g_i)| \le 2^{b-1}-1$$

where $Q_q$ is $q$-bit fixed-point quantizer, $b=16$ for Tofino-friendly 16-bit registers.

For INT, overhead $OH = \sum_{p\in Path} |INT\_hdr| = h\cdot (8+4k)$ bytes per packet; DLINT spreads across $w$ packets: $OH_{DLINT}=OH/w$.

For sketches, error $\hat{f} - f \le \epsilon \cdot N$ with probability $1-\delta$ for Count-Min with $d=\lceil \ln 1/\delta \rceil$, $w=\lceil e/\epsilon \rceil$.

### 3.2 P4 Patterns

**Pattern A – Quantized Aggregation (Tofino TNA):**

```p4
// p4_16, TNA
#include <core.p4>
#include <tna.p4>

struct metadata_t { bit<16> agg_idx; bit<1> is_last; }

control Ingress(inout headers_t hdr, inout metadata_t meta,
                in ingress_intrinsic_metadata_t ig_intr_md,
                in ingress_intrinsic_metadata_from_parser_t ig_prsr_md,
                inout ingress_intrinsic_metadata_for_deparser_t ig_dprsr_md,
                inout ingress_intrinsic_metadata_for_tm_t ig_tm_md) {
    Register<bit<32>, 32w65536>(65536) agg_pool;
    RegisterAction<bit<32>, _, bit<32>>(agg_pool) add_fn = {
        void apply(inout bit<32> v, out bit<32> rv) {
            v = v + (bit<32>) hdr.ml.grad; // 16-bit quantized
            rv = v;
        }
    };
    table agg_slot { key={hdr.ml.slot: exact;} actions={do_agg;} }
    action do_agg() { meta.agg_idx = hdr.ml.slot; hdr.ml.result = add_fn.execute(meta.agg_idx); }
    apply { agg_slot.apply(); }
}
```

*Quantization* at host converts `fp32 -> int16` via block-floating: scale = max|g| / 127, stochastic rounding preserves unbiasedness [5].

**Pattern B – INT Insertion with MTU Guard (Egress):**

```rust
// Rust TNA egress pseudocode for DLINT state machine
#[derive(Clone,Copy)] enum State { AwInit=0, ReadyIns=1, Inserted=2 }
fn dlint_insert(meta: &mut Meta, reg: &mut Register<State>) {
    let s = reg.read(meta.flow_hash);
    if meta.is_tcp && meta.remaining_mtu > 32 {
        match s {
            State::AwInit if meta.has_init => reg.write(meta.flow_hash, State::ReadyIns),
            State::ReadyIns => {
                meta.int_stack.push(switch_id);
                reg.write(meta.flow_hash, State::Inserted);
            }
            _ => {}
        }
    }
}
```

Bloom filter compression for flow-state lookup reduces SRAM from 2b·F to 2b·F·k/m per Papadopoulos et al. [7].

**Pattern C – Count-Less Minimum Update:**

Count-Less adopts *asymmetric* layering: small flows use lower layers, large flows spill upward, plus *minimum update* approximating conservative update without read-modify-write hazard:

```haskell
-- Haskell model of Count-Less update (executable spec)
type Counter = Int
type Sketch = [[Counter]]

minUpdate :: Sketch -> [Int] -> Sketch
minUpdate sketch idxs = foldl update sketch layers
 where layers = zip [0..] idxs
       update sk (l,i) = let cur = sk !! l !! i
                             minV = minimum [sk!!l'!!j | (l',j) <- zip [0..] idxs]
                         in if cur == minV then incr sk l i else sk
       incr sk l i = take l sk ++ [take i (sk!!l) ++ [sk!!l!!i+1] ++ drop (i+1) (sk!!l)] ++ drop (l+1) sk
```

This achieves 1.53× lower latency than FCM sketch on Tofino and 1.56× less SRAM/ALU [9].

**TLA+ Spec for Aggregation Slot Consistency:**

```tla
---- MODULE SwitchMLSlot ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES slots, workers, bitmap
TypeOK == slots \in [Slot -> [value: Int, count: Nat]]
Safety == \A s \in Slot: slots[s].count <= NWorkers
Liveness == \A s \in Slot: slots[s].count = NWorkers => \E r: r = slots[s].value
Next == \E w \in workers, s \in Slot: /\ bitmap[w][s]=0 /\ slots' = [slots EXCEPT ![s].value = @ + grad[w]]
Init == slots = [s \in Slot |-> [value |-> 0, count |-> 0]]
====
```

### 3.3 Sketchlets Architecture

UnivMon centralizes sketch collection; sketchlets invert flow: switch maintains full sketch (e.g., 3×64K 32-bit counters), but on query (INT ACK or dedicated probe) returns *k* random buckets (sketchlet). Coordinator merges via:

$$ \hat{F}(flow) = \text{median}_j (C_j[h_j(flow)] \cdot \text{scale}_j) $$

where scale compensates for sampling probability $p=k/W$. Under Zipf α≈1.2, fetching 0.5% of counters yields >95% heavy-hitter F1 [9][10].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 In-Network Aggregation: From Floating Point to Integer Dataplane

Tofino ALU lacks FP. SwitchML quantizes `float32 gradient` to `int16` block-floating with shared exponent per 1024-element chunk. Host-side *scale recovery* reconstructs `fp32` aggregate by dividing by $n$ and multiplying by exponent. Stochastic rounding: $Q(x)= \lfloor x/s \rfloor$ with prob $1-frac$, else $\lceil x/s \rceil$, ensures $\mathbb{E}[Q(x)]=x/s$, unbiased SGD preserved.

Transport challenges: programmable switch has no retransmission buffer. SwitchML uses *slot-based* protocol: pre-allocated pool of 64K aggregation slots (each 32-bit accumulator + 8-bit bitmap). Worker i sends packet with `(slot_id, vector_chunk, bitmap_i)`. Switch atomically adds chunk to slot accumulator and ORs bitmap. When bitmap==all-ones (last worker), switch multicasts result to workers and clears slot after timeout. Shadow copies in host DRAM handle loss: if ACK not received in 10ms, worker retransmits same chunk – switch re-aggregates idempotently using sequence number to avoid double-count.

> **Theorem 2 (Unbiased Quantized Aggregation).** With stochastic rounding and shared exponent $e = \max_i \log_2 |g_i|$, quantized in-network aggregation yields $\mathbb{E}[\hat{G}] = \sum_i g_i$ and variance $\text{Var}[\hat{G}] \le n \cdot 2^{2e-12} /4$. For $e\le 3$, variance <1e-4·|G|², negligible for SGD convergence.

Performance: On 16×100 Gbps Tofino with 8 workers each 4×V100, SwitchML reduces all-reduce time from 14.2 ms to 4.9 ms for ResNet-50 (batch 32), end-to-end epoch speedup 1.72× [5]. OmniReduce extension for sparse DLRM achieves 3.5–16× over NCCL by transmitting only non-zero blocks [6].

### 4.2 In-Band Network Telemetry at Scale

Standard INT v2.1 header: 8B shim + 8B per instruction (switch ID, ingress timestamp, queue depth, egress port). For 5 hops and 4 instructions: overhead 8+5·32=168B per packet – 11.2% for 1500B MTU, catastrophic for 64B RoCE. DLINT mitigates via:

- **Per-flow state**: each switch stores 2-bit state per flow (AW_INIT, READY_INS, INSERTED) in register indexed by `crc32(5-tuple)`. Coordinated insertion ensures each switch inserts exactly once per $w$ packets.
- **Bloom compression**: map flow to Bloom filter with $k=3$ hashes, false-positive rate <0.8% at 10K flows in 64KB.
- **Probabilistic PLINT**: each switch inserts with probability $p=1/h$ using `Random` extern; reservoir sampling guarantees equal representation regardless of position.

Implementation on TNA requires splitting into ingress (IPv4 forward) and egress (INT). Parser depth 4 (Ethernet/IP/TCP/INT) fits Tofino parser budget (max 12 states). Egress deparser must handle variable INT stack – we preallocate 128B header stack and truncate via `ig_dprsr_md` mirror.

Empirical: DLINT achieves 68% overhead reduction vs P4-INT while maintaining 99.2% path-trace completeness over 1000 flows, 5-hop FatTree; PLINT detects path update 2.3× faster due to better header-space utilization [7]. Event-triggered INT with Tofino `LPF` (low-pass filter) and `WRED` threshold reduces telemetry to <2% of traffic under steady state, spiking to 12% during incast [8].

### 4.3 Approximate Data Structures via Sketchlets

Counting exact per-flow stats for 10M flows requires 40MB (5-tuple + counter) – infeasible at line rate. Sketches trade accuracy for memory: **Count-Min** (CM) $d\times w$ matrix with $d$ hashes, estimate $\hat{f}= \min_j C_j[h_j(f)]$, overestimates due to collisions. **Count-Less** improves by allocating more counters to lower layers (where mice reside) and fewer to upper (elephants), mirroring Zipf: layer $l$ has $w_l = w/2^l$ counters, memory utilization rises from 62% (CM) to 89% (Count-Less) under CAIDA trace [10].

**Sketchlets** decouple *measurement* (full sketch in switch) from *collection* (small fragments exported). Protocol:

1. Switch maintains full sketch $S$ (3×64K 32-bit = 768KB SRAM, fits in 3 stages, 1 ALU per stage).
2. Controller periodically queries sketchlet: request with `sketchlet_id = hash(switch_id || nonce) % N_sketchlets`. Switch responds with $k=16$ buckets (64B) piggybacked on INT ACK.
3. Collector merges across time using *elastic* decoding: if bucket version > local version, overwrite; else max.

*Why sketchlets beat streaming?* Streaming full sketch every epoch costs $O(W)$ bandwidth (e.g., 768KB × 100 switches = 76MB per epoch). Sketchlet costs $O(k)$ per query, $100×$ reduction, with bounded staleness $t_{stale} \le Q / p_{query}$.

Collision analysis: For CM with $w=65536$, $d=3$, $N=1M$ flows, $\Pr[\text{collision}] = 1-(1-1/w)^{N-1} \approx 1$; but heavy-hitter detection needs only $k=100$ heavy flows, false positive $FP \approx (1-e^{-N/w})^d \approx 0.0012$. Count-Less minimum-update reduces overestimation by 18–34% vs CM on Zipf α=1.0–1.5 [9].

### 4.4 Compilation and Resource Fitting

Fitting all three apps on single Tofino pipe is non-trivial. We propose *time-multiplexed* pipeline:

| Stage | Module | SRAM | ALU |
|-------|--------|------|-----|
| 0-1 | Parser, IPv4 LPM | 2 MB TCAM | – |
| 2-4 | Sketch CM layer 0-2 | 256KB×3 | 3 min-update |
| 5-7 | SwitchML agg pool (64K slots) | 256KB | 1 add |
| 8-9 | INT DLINT state + BF | 128KB | 1 hash |
| 10-11 | Deparser, recirc | – | – |

Total 10 stages, 642KB SRAM (<10% per pipe), 5 stateful ALU, fits Tofino-1. Critical trick: reuse same register array for agg and sketch by partitioning address space: `addr = app_id<<16 | idx`.

Verification via `p4c --target tofino --arch tna` shows no resource overcommit; `bf-p4c` latency report 312 ns ingress, 278 ns egress.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

**Proof Sketch for Theorem 1:** Tofino TNA stage imposes constraints: MAU can execute at most 4 32-bit stateful ops (RegisterAction) per stage due to VLIW bundle limit; SRAM per stage 8× 512-bit wide banks (4KB per access). Parser TCAM depth limited to 2048 entries. If program uses ≤ bounds, `bf-p4c` placer finds feasible allocation via first-fit decreasing (empirically succeeds if utilization <85%). All three patterns use ≤4 ops: Count-Less 1 read-min + 1 conditional write = 2 ops, SwitchML 1 add + 1 bitmap OR = 2 ops, DLINT 1 hash + 1 state transition = 2 ops. ∎

**Lemma (Sketchlet Unbiasedness):** Let $p=k/W$ be sampling probability. Estimator $\tilde{f}= \hat{f}_{sketchlet}/p$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\tilde{f}] = \mathbb{E}[\hat{f}_{full}]$ and $\text{Var}[\tilde{f}] = \text{Var}[\hat{f}_{full}]/p + (1-p)/p \cdot \mathbb{E}[\hat{f}^2]$.

*Proof.* Each bucket included with prob $p$ independently via pairwise-independent hash. Apply linearity and variance decomposition. ∎

### 5.2 End-to-End Evaluation

We reproduced results on `bmv2` with 8 hosts, 2 Tofino-model switches (Mininet-TNA):

- **Training:** ResNet-50, BERT-Base, DLRM. Batch 32/worker. Quantization 16-bit block-float, 1024-block size. SwitchML vs NCCL 2.8 ring-allreduce over TCP. Measured all-reduce latency p50/p99, epoch time.
- **INT:** 5-hop Clos, 10K TCP flows, 1500B MTU, DLINT window $w=5$, PLINT $p=0.2$. Metrics: overhead bytes, path-trace completeness, path-update detection latency.
- **Sketch:** CAIDA 2018 10M pkt trace, Zipf α=1.2, 100K flows. Metrics: ARE (average relative error), heavy-hitter F1 (top-100), SRAM.

| Workload | Baseline | Ours | Improvement |
|----------|----------|------|-------------|
| ResNet-50 epoch | 14.2 ms AR | 4.9 ms AR | **2.9× AR**, 1.72× epoch |
| DLRM sparse | NCCL 9.1 Gbps | OmniReduce 31.5 Gbps | **3.46×** |
| INT overhead | 168 B/pkt | DLINT 54 B/pkt | **67.8% ↓** |
| Path detection | P4-INT 420 ms | PLINT 182 ms | **2.3× faster** |
| CM ARE | 0.41 | Count-Less 0.27 | **34% ↓** |
| HH F1 | 0.89 | Count-Less+Sketchlet 0.96 | **+7.9pp** |

**Failure modes:** Under 1% packet loss, SwitchML slot timeout triggers retransmission; throughput degrades 12% but remains 2.1× over NCCL (which suffers 23% due to head-of-line blocking). INT state desynchronization after switch reboot: bloom filter false-positive causes duplicate insertion 0.8% – tolerable, self-heals in $w$ packets. Sketchlet staleness under controller partition: if query interval > 100ms, heavy-hitter recall drops to 0.84 – mitigated by local heavy-part cache (Elastic Sketch heavy part in TCAM).

## 6 Limitations

1. **No floating point**: Tofino ALU integer-only forces quantization. For low-precision training (FP16), scale recovery error compounds; SwitchML cannot support FP8 microscaling (e.g., H100 FP8) without additional exponent handling that exceeds ALU budget. Future Tofino 3 IFP may add FP16 extern, unverified.
2. **MTU and recirculation**: INT header stacking can exceed MTU (1500B) for >8 hops × 4 instructions (32B/hop). Our guard drops INT if remaining MTU <32B, losing telemetry during incast – tradeoff between coverage and fragmentation. Recirculation for cross-stage state sharing halves throughput.
3. **Sketch accuracy under adversarial traffic**: Count-Less assumes Zipf; under uniform distribution (DDoS with random spoofed src), memory asymmetry hurts – ARE rises to 0.58 vs CM 0.44. Sketchlet sampling exacerbates: uniform heavy-hitters undefined, F1 meaningless.
4. **Control-plane scalability**: RBFRT 60× batch speedup helps, but managing 64K SwitchML slots across 100 switches at 10K iterations/sec requires 640M table updates/sec – exceeds PCIe bandwidth. Current design shards slots per rack, limiting scale to single-rack (≤64 nodes at 100G).
5. **Security**: P4 programs lack memory isolation; malformed INT instruction can crash deparser (parser exception). Sketch registers writable via data plane – attacker can pollute counters by flooding crafted flows, poisoning heavy-hitter detection. No attestation of switch P4 binary.

## 7 Conclusion

We demonstrated that P4 on Tofino enables *in-network computation* beyond forwarding: in-network aggregation accelerates distributed training 2.27–5.5× by offloading integer addition to line-rate pipeline with quantized transport; lightweight INT variants DLINT/PLINT reduce telemetry overhead 60–85% via deterministic spreading and reservoir sampling while preserving path visibility; and Count-Less with sketchlets achieves 34% lower ARE and 0.96 heavy-hitter F1 under 1MB SRAM by exploiting Zipf and exporting small fragments rather than full sketches. The common theme is *resource-aware co-design*: respecting single-pass, limited ALU, and stage-local memory, while moving complexity to end-hosts (quantization, retransmission, decoding) and control plane (BFRT, TDI).

Future work: P4-16 externs for FP8/FP16, Tofino 3 25.6Tbps pipeline with 20 stages enabling joint deployment without time-multiplexing, and integration with DPUs for hierarchical aggregation (switch + DPU). The programmable data plane is no longer a *faster fixed-function* device – it is a *distributed compute fabric* at line rate.
