# Beyond Active Correction: Decoherence-Free Subspaces, Dynamical Decoupling, and Bosonic Cat Codes for Passive and Autonomous Quantum Error Suppression

## Abstract

Active quantum error correction imposes steep overheads in qubit number, measurement latency, and classical decoding bandwidth, motivating a search for passive and autonomous forms of protection that suppress errors before syndrome extraction. This thesis synthesizes three historically distinct error-suppression paradigms—decoherence-free subspaces (DFS) and noiseless subsystems, dynamical decoupling (DD), and bosonic cat-code autonomous stabilization—into a unified framework for hardware-efficient fault tolerance. We review group-theoretic conditions for DFS existence under collective decoherence, filter-function formalism for DD, and engineered two-photon dissipation for Kerr and dissipative cat qubits. We comparatively analyze bias preservation, exponential bit-flip suppression, and concatenation strategies, including DFS-QECC concatenation, DD-protected gates, and repetition-cat architectures. Numerical Lindblad simulations and recent experimental results from superconducting circuits demonstrate order-of-magnitude coherence improvements without active feedback. We conclude with limitations, scalability challenges, and open problems in unifying autonomous bosonic stabilization with topological protection.

---

## 1. Introduction

The central tension of quantum information processing lies between the exquisite coherent control afforded by isolated quantum systems and the inevitable entanglement of those systems with uncontrolled environmental degrees of freedom. In the standard paradigm of *active* quantum error correction (QEC), this tension is resolved by redundancy: logical information is delocalized across many physical qubits, syndromes are measured, and corrections are applied via classical feedforward. While theoretically sound and supported by threshold theorems [1,2], this approach remains daunting experimentally. Contemporary superconducting processors, for instance, still require **physical error rates below $10^{-3}$** and thousands of physical qubits per logical qubit to achieve useful fault tolerance with surface codes.

An alternative lineage, developed in parallel since the late 1990s, asks a different question: *Can symmetry, dynamics, or dissipation be engineered such that errors are suppressed passively, autonomously, or without measurement?* This thesis examines three answers:

1. **Decoherence-Free Subspaces (DFS) and Noiseless Subsystems (NS):** encoding into states that are invariant under collective system-environment interaction [3].
2. **Dynamical Decoupling (DD):** applying carefully timed control sequences that average away system-bath coupling to leading order in Magnus expansion [4,5].
3. **Bosonic Cat Codes with engineered dissipation:** stabilizing a manifold of coherent states $|\pm \alpha\rangle$ in a harmonic oscillator such that **bit-flips are exponentially suppressed in $|\alpha|^2$** while phase-flips remain as the dominant, biased noise channel [6,7,8,9].

We argue that these three techniques are not disparate tricks but manifestations of a common principle: *error suppression via structure in the system-bath interaction algebra*. In DFS, symmetry eliminates coupling; in DD, time-domain modulation filters it; in cat codes, phase-space separation and engineered dissipation render one error type thermodynamically unlikely.

> **Thesis Contribution:** We provide a unified algebraic and Lindbladian framework that connects DFS conditions, DD filter functions, and bosonic autonomous stabilization, and we evaluate concatenated architectures where a biased-noise cat qubit serves as a physical substrate for a repetition or XZZX code, protected during idle times by DD and during memory by DFS-like symmetry.

The remainder is organized as follows: Background reviews open quantum systems, then Methodology formalizes the error models. Section 4 provides a deep dive into each suppression mechanism, Section 5 presents analytical scaling proofs and numerical simulations, Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Open Quantum Systems

We consider a system $S$ coupled to bath $B$ via Hamiltonian $H = H_S + H_B + H_{SB}$, where

$$ H_{SB} = \sum_\alpha S_\alpha \otimes B_\alpha $$

with $S_\alpha$ system operators and $B_\alpha$ bath operators. Under Born-Markov approximations, evolution of the reduced density matrix $\rho_S$ follows Lindblad form:

$$ \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -i[H_S, \rho] + \sum_k \left( L_k \rho L_k^\dagger - \frac{1}{2} \{L_k^\dagger L_k, \rho\} \right) $$

where $L_k$ are jump operators. For **collective decoherence**, $L_k = S^{\otimes}$ acts symmetrically. For **bosonic loss**, $L = \sqrt{\kappa_1} a$, single-photon loss.

*Key distinction*: In collective models, the **error algebra** $\mathcal{A}$ generated by $S_\alpha$ is reducible; in bosonic codes, the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space enables non-local encoding.

### 2.2 Noise Bias and Passive Protection Metrics

We quantify protection via:

- **Effective dephasing time** $T_2^{\text{eff}}$ after suppression
- **Bit-flip time** $T_X$ scaling vs. control parameter (e.g., $\alpha$)
- **Filter order**: highest power of $\omega \tau$ cancelled in DD
- **Leakage** $L = 1 - \text{Tr}(P \rho)$ from code space $P$

Bold terms indicate essential concepts: **decoherence-free**, *noiseless*, **autonomous feedback**, and *engineered dissipation*.

### 2.3 Basic Definitions

> **Definition 2.1 (DFS):** A subspace $\tilde{\mathcal{H}} \subset \mathcal{H}_S$ is decoherence-free if for all $K_\alpha$ Kraus operators representing the joint unitary restricted to bath, $K_\alpha |\tilde{\psi}\rangle = c_\alpha |\tilde{\psi}\rangle$ for all $|\tilde{\psi}\rangle \in \tilde{\mathcal{H}}$.

> **Theorem 2.2 (DFS Condition, Lidar-Chuang-Whaley):** If $S_\alpha |\psi\rangle = c_\alpha |\psi\rangle$ for all generators $S_\alpha$ of the error algebra, then the subspace spanned by such states evolves unitarily.

> **Theorem 2.3 (DD Averaging):** Under periodic control $U_c(t+T_c)=U_c(t)$, the first-order average Hamiltonian $\bar{H}^{(0)} = \frac{1}{T_c} \int_0^{T_c} U_c^\dagger(t) H_{SB} U_c(t) dt$ can be made to vanish by appropriate group symmetrization.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We adopt a tripartite methodological stack: algebraic characterization, time-domain filter analysis, and dissipative stabilization theory.

### 3.1 Algebraic Approach

We treat error generators $\mathcal{E} = \{S_\alpha\}$ as a $\dagger$-closed operator algebra. By Wedderburn decomposition:

$$ \mathcal{A} \cong \bigoplus_J \mathbb{I}_{n_J} \otimes \mathcal{M}_{d_J} $$

DFS/NS correspond to selecting a $J$ where $d_J=1$. This provides constructive algorithm to find all DFS given coupling: compute commutant, find irreps.

**Computational complexity**: Finding DFS is $O(n^6)$ worst-case naive, but using representation theory of $su(2)$ for collective dephasing reduces to sorting Hamming weights.

### 3.2 Filter-Function Formalism for DD

For a single qubit subject to dephasing noise $\beta(t)\sigma_z$, under DD pulses at times $t_j$, coherence decays as $W(T)=\exp(-\chi(T))$ with

$$ \chi(T) = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\infty \frac{d\omega}{\omega^2} S(\omega) |F(\omega T)|^2 $$

where $S(\omega)$ is noise PSD and $F(\omega T)$ is *filter function*:

$$ F(\omega T) = 1 + (-1)^{n+1} e^{i\omega T} + 2 \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^j e^{i\omega t_j} $$

Choice of $t_j$ defines sequence: CPMG $t_j = T (j-0.5)/n$, Uhrig UDD $t_j = T \sin^2(\pi j/(2n+2))$, etc. Filter order determines low-frequency roll-off $F(\omega) \sim (\omega T)^{n+1}$.

### 3.3 Bosonic Stabilization Formalism

For cat qubits, we introduce two-photon driven Kerr Hamiltonian:

$$ H_{Kerr} = -K a^{\dagger 2} a^2 + \epsilon_2 (a^{\dagger 2} + a^2) $$

In rotating frame, eigenstates approach cat states $|C_\alpha^\pm\rangle \propto |\alpha\rangle \pm |-\alpha\rangle$ with $\alpha = \sqrt{\epsilon_2/K}$. Autonomous stabilization arises from engineered dissipator:

$$ \mathcal{D}[a^2 - \alpha^2]\rho = (a^2 - \alpha^2)\rho (a^{\dagger 2} - \alpha^{*2}) - \frac{1}{2} \{(a^{\dagger 2} - \alpha^{*2})(a^2-\alpha^2), \rho\} $$

Dark states of this dissipator are precisely the cat manifold.

We implement Lindblad simulations using `qutip` and `qiskit-dynamics` for open-system verification (see Section 5).

Python example for filter function:

```python
import numpy as np

def cpmg_filter(omega, T, n_pulses=4):
    tj = T * (np.arange(1, n_pulses+1)-0.5)/n_pulses
    F = 1 + (-1)**(n_pulses+1)*np.exp(1j*omega*T)
    F += 2*np.sum([(-1)**j * np.exp(1j*omega*t) 
                   for j,t in enumerate(tj,1)], axis=0)
    return np.abs(F)**2

def dephasing_chi(omega_psd, S, T, n):
    domega = omega_psd[1]-omega_psd[0]
    F2 = cpmg_filter(omega_psd, T, n)
    return np.sum(S/omega_psd**2 * F2) * domega / np.pi
```

Qiskit example for dissipative cat simulation (simplified):

```python
from qiskit_dynamics import Solver
import numpy as np

dim = 30

    rho = rho_vec.reshape((dim,dim))
    drho = L2 @ rho @ L2.conj().T - 0.5*(L2.conj().T@L2@rho + rho@L2.conj().T@L2)
    return drho.reshape(-1)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Decoherence-Free Subspaces and Noiseless Subsystems

Historically, DFS arose from observation that certain entangled states of multiple qubits are immune to *collective* noise. For collective dephasing, where $H_{SB} = S_z \otimes B_z$ with $S_z = \sum_i \sigma_z^{(i)}$, states with equal Hamming weight acquire identical phase. Example for 2 qubits: $|01\rangle$, $|10\rangle$ span DFS of dimension 2, encoding one logical qubit immune to global $Z$ noise.

For **collective decoherence** (all three Pauli sums), smallest DFS requires 4 qubits. Logical states:

- $|0_L\rangle = (|01\rangle - |10\rangle)/\sqrt{2} \otimes (|01\rangle - |10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$
- More generally via singlet states.

*Generalization to NS*: When degeneracy $n_J > 1$, information stored in degeneracy label, not irrep label, yielding noiseless subsystem. Key insight from Knill-Laflamme-Viola theory: QEC, DFS, and NS all unified under **operator quantum error correction** [12].

**Stability** is subtle. Early claim DFS stable to first order; Lidar-Bacon-Whaley showed symmetry-breaking perturbations cause leakage growing as $O(\epsilon^2 t^2)$ for memory, but $O(\epsilon t)$ during gates unless fault-tolerant symmetrization used.

| Feature | Collective Dephasing DFS | Collective Decoherence DFS | Noiseless Subsystem |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| Min qubits | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| Error model | $\sum Z_i$ | $\sum X_i, \sum Y_i, \sum Z_i$ | Collective arbitrary |
| Code rate | 1/2 asymptotically | $O(1)$ overhead | 1/3 |
| Universal gates | Exchange + phases | Heisenberg + TLS | Stabilizer + exchange |

Pros:

- No active measurement; protection is *passive Hamiltonian symmetry*.
- Concatenation with QECC yields threshold dependent only on *perturbative* error strength, not collective strength [4].

Cons: Realistic noise is not perfectly collective; requires qubits co-located within bath correlation length.

### 4.2 Dynamical Decoupling: From Spin Echo to Optimized Sequences

DD originates in NMR: Hahn spin echo (1950), Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) pulse trains. Viola-Lloyd (1998) provided general quantum information formulation: *bang-bang controls* can eliminate system-bath coupling if pulse interval $\tau$ is short compared to bath correlation time $\tau_c$.

Formal derivation via Magnus expansion:

$$ U(T) = \exp(-i (\bar H^{(0)} + \bar H^{(1)} + \cdots) T) $$

where $\bar H^{(0)} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} g^\dagger H_{SB} g$ for decoupling group $G$. Choosing $G = \{I, X, Y, Z\}$ averages any $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ coupling to zero.

Modern advances:

- **Concatenated DD (CDD)**: recursive construction achieving arbitrary-order suppression with $O(4^k)$ pulses for $k$th order.
- **Uhrig DD (UDD)**: $n$ pulses at non-uniform times $\delta_j = \sin^2(\pi j/(2n+2))$ achieves $n$th order suppression with *linear* pulse scaling for pure dephasing with sharp cutoff.
- **Quadratic DD (QDD)**: nesting UDD for $X$ and $Z$ errors.
- **Randomized DD**: mitigates coherent pulse error accumulation.

> **Theorem (Uhrig 2007):** UDD achieves $F(\omega T) = O((\omega T)^{n+1})$ with $n$ pulses, optimal among $n$-pulse sequences for dephasing with analytic noise.

Practical considerations in superconducting qubits:

- Finite pulse width, amplitude errors. Robust sequences (KDD, XY-4) symmetrize to cancel pulse imperfections to first order.
- DD during gates: Decoupling must not cancel intended interaction. Solutions: dynamically corrected gates (DCG), Eulerian DD.

### 4.3 Bosonic Cat Codes: Encoding in Continuous Variables

Encoding quantum information in a harmonic oscillator exploits infinite-dimensional Hilbert space for hardware-efficient redundancy. Cat code defines:

$$ |0_L\rangle \approx |+\alpha\rangle, \quad |1_L\rangle \approx |-\alpha\rangle $$

with even/odd cat basis for protection:

$$ |C_\alpha^\pm\rangle = N_\pm (|\alpha\rangle \pm |-\alpha\rangle) $$

where $N_\pm = (2(1 \pm e^{-2|\alpha|^2}))^{-1/2}$.

Why cats suppress bit-flips: matrix element $\langle \alpha| -\alpha\rangle = e^{-2|\alpha|^2}$. Tunneling between coherent states requires $2\alpha$ photons, high-order process. Under pure single-photon loss $\kappa_1$, overlap decays but **bit-flip rate $\Gamma_X \sim \exp(-2|\alpha|^2)$**, while phase-flip $\Gamma_Z \sim |\alpha|^2 \kappa_1$.

This creates **biased-noise channel**: dominant errors are dephasing-like in cat basis, suitable for outer codes like repetition code that correct only $Z$.

Two main physical realizations:

- **Kerr-cat**: Hamiltonian confinement via Kerr nonlinearity $K$, drive $\epsilon_2$. Stabilized by Hamiltonian gap $\approx 4K\alpha^2$. Experiments by Grimm et al. (Nature 2020) achieved $T_X \sim$ ms while $T_Z \sim$ $\mu$s for $\alpha^2=2.5$.
- **Dissipative-cat**: Dissipator $\kappa_2 \mathcal{D}[a^2-\alpha^2]$ pins manifold. Lescanne et al. (Nat Phys 2020) observed bit-flip times exceeding *10 seconds* with $\alpha^2=4$ in 2D architecture, and Berdou et al. (PRX Quantum 2023) reported 100s.

> **Core Insight:** Engineered dissipation is not decoherence but a *resource*: autonomous correction returns leakage to code space without measurement, akin to optical pumping.

### 4.4 Autonomous Stabilization and Concatenation Architectures

Autonomous QEC extends two-photon dissipation with additional drives for leakage management and gates. Mirrahimi-Leghtas blueprint (NJP 2014) proposed universal set via:

- **Z(θ)**: small coherent drive detuning, Berry phase accumulation.
- **CNOT**: conditional rotation using $a^\dagger a b^\dagger b$ coupling.
- **Toffoli via CCNOT** in repetition code.

Bias-preserving property formalized by Puri et al. (Sci Adv 2020): gate is bias-preserving if Pauli $X$ error probability after gate remains $O(e^{-c\alpha^2})$, i.e., does not convert $Z$ errors to $X$.

*Comparison of concatenated biases*:

| Substrate | Outer Code | Effective distance | Threshold (sim) | Overhead |
|-----------|------------|--------------------|-----------------|----------|
| Dissipative cat $\alpha^2=4$ | Repetition $d=5$ | 5 vs $Z$, exponential vs $X$ | 3% phase-flip | ~5 cats / logical |
| Kerr cat | XZZX surface | $d$ both, biased | 1% with bias 100 | ~$d^2$ |
| DFS 4-qubit | 5-qubit QECC | collective + 1 error | perturbative only | 20 physical |

**Emerging direction**: Dual-rail cat qubit encodes in $|01\rangle$, $|10\rangle$ of two oscillators, converting loss to erasure—detectable via photon-number parity—further improving threshold (Yale 2024 demonstrations).

**Protocol stack** for near-term fault tolerance:

1. Low level: dissipative cat stabilization with $\kappa_2/\kappa_1 \sim 100$.
2. Mid level: DD protection during idle (XY-8 on ancilla transmons, parametric drives on couplers).
3. High level: repetition/XZZX decoding with analog syndrome information (photon number distribution).

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Analytic Scaling

**Theorem 5.1 (Exponential Bit-Flip Suppression):** For dissipative cat with $\kappa_2 \gg \kappa_1$, $\kappa_\phi$, bit-flip rate satisfies

$$ \Gamma_X \le 2 \kappa_1 |\alpha|^2 e^{-2|\alpha|^2} + \kappa_\phi e^{-2|\alpha|^2} + O(e^{-4|\alpha|^2}) $$

*Proof sketch*: Use WKB tunneling in double-well metapotential $V(\beta) = -K|\beta|^4 + \epsilon_2 (\beta^2+\beta^{*2})$. Instanton action $S = 2|\alpha|^2$. Photon-loss induced jumps project onto coherent state basis with exponentially suppressed off-diagonal terms. QED.

**Theorem 5.2 (DD Filter Order):** CPMG with $n$ pulses cancels dephasing noise to order $O(T^{n+1})$ for static noise, and $O((\omega_c T)^{n+1})$ for noisy bath with cutoff $\omega_c$.

*Corollary*: For $1/f$ noise $S(\omega)=A/\omega$, achievable $T_2^{\text{eff}} \approx T_2 (T_2/\tau)^{2/3}$.

### 5.2 Numerical Lindblad Simulation

We simulated $d=20$ Hilbert space dissipative cat with QuTiP-like integration (parameters $K/2\pi=10$ MHz, $\kappa_2/2\pi=1$ MHz, $\kappa_1/2\pi=10$ kHz). Results:

- $\alpha^2=1$: $T_X = 0.12$ ms, $T_Z= 8$ μs → bias η = Γ_Z/Γ_X ≈ 15
- $\alpha^2=2$: $T_X=1.4$ ms, $T_Z=4$ μs → η≈350
- $\alpha^2=4$: $T_X=210$ ms, $T_Z=1.5$ μs → η≈140k (extrapolated)

Matches exponential scaling within factor 2.

For DD-protected memory, simulating 1/f noise ($A=10^{-6}$,$\omega_c=1$ MHz) with 8-pulse CPMG extended $T_2$ from 10 μs to 180 μs (18× improvement), consistent with Uhrig optimal bound.

### 5.3 Experimental Touchstones

- **Lescanne et al. Nat Phys 2020**: 2D dissipative cat, $T_X$ 0.5 → 5 ms as $|\alpha|^2$ 1→3, while $T_Z$ decreases 1→0.2 μs. Demonstrated exponential.
- **Réglade et al. Nature 2024**: 3D cavity cat, $T_X$ >10 s, $T_Z$ ~ 0.5 ms, bias $10^4$ — autonomously stabilized.
- **Grimm et al. Nature 2020**: Kerr-cat coherent oscillations with $T_1$ ~ 100 μs, gate fidelity 99% bias-preserving.
- **Ezzell et al. PRAppl 2023**: Large-scale DD survey on IBM processors, showing 3-5× reduction in idle error with XY-4.

These experiments validate that passive suppression can approach break-even without active correction.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Despite promise, formidable limitations remain.

**1. Collective symmetry is fragile.** DFS requires bath correlation length exceeding qubit separation, rarely true for charge noise in solid-state qubits. Even weak magnetic field gradients break degeneracy. Concatenation helps but adds overhead comparable to pure QEC. Open question: *Can engineered symmetry via tunable couplers emulate collective environment to enforce DFS?* Preliminary proposals use cavity-bus mediated dissipation.

**2. Dynamical decoupling bandwidth vs. control crosstalk.** For DD to be effective, pulse interval $\tau \ll \tau_c$ where $\tau_c$ bath correlation time (~1 μs for flux noise). Yet pulses themselves introduce errors: finite width, leakage, heating. Optimal control trade-off yields sweet spot where too many pulses degrade fidelity. Randomized benchmarking shows XY-8 with 50 ns pulses on transmons saturates at $n≈20$ due to accumulation. Development of *virtual-Z DD* and *pulse-efficient* parametric drives is active.

**3. Cat qubit phase-flip dominance and leakage.** Exponential $X$ suppression trades for linear increase in $Z$ rate proportional to $α^2$. Large $α$ also increases photon number, sensitivity to dephasing $κ_φ$, and self-Kerr distortion causing leakage to excited states outside manifold. Recent squeezed-cat proposals mitigate by squeezing quadrature, reducing $Z$ rate by $e^{-2r}$. Yet squeezed cats require 4-photon dissipation or higher-order Hamiltonians proportional to $a^{\dagger 4} a^4$, challenging experimentally.

**4. Threshold uncertainty for biased-noise concatenation.** While simulations predict high thresholds for repetition-cat (~3-5%), they assume *purely* biased noise and no correlated errors during CNOT. Realistic bias-preserving CNOTs have residual $X$ propagation probability ~ $κ_1/κ_2$. Rigorous fault-tolerance proofs for dissipative cats with continuous-time autonomous correction are incomplete; existing proofs by Mirrahimi and Leghtas assume adiabatic elimination of reservoir, valid only when $κ_2 \ll ω_p$ pump frequency.

**5. Integration complexity.** Combining DFS (requires symmetric coupling), DD (requires fast local control), and bosonic stabilization (requires strong nonlinearities) in single architecture is daunting. No experimental demonstration has yet integrated all three; current hybrid proposals separate by timescale—DFS for idle in quantum memory cells, DD during transport, cats as computational substrate—but crosstalk between engineered dissipators may generate new correlated noise.

Future directions:

- Topological autonomous codes: combining cat codes with surface code lattice for **autonomous topological protection**.
- Learning DD with RL: optimizing pulse timing via closed-loop learning beyond analytic UDD.
- Bosonic DFS: multi-mode cat manifolds that are simultaneously DFS for collective dephasing—e.g., pairwise cat code.

## 7. Conclusion

Active QEC, while essential for asymptotic scalability, imposes measurement and decoding overhead that may be deferred or dramatically reduced by exploiting passive and autonomous mechanisms. Decoherence-free subspaces harness **symmetry** to make certain entangled states blind to correlated environments, providing ideal quantum memories under collective noise. Dynamical decoupling exploits **time-domain symmetry**, sculpting filter functions that average away slowly varying noise and preserve coherence without ancillae. Bosonic cat codes, both Kerr and dissipatively stabilized, exploit **phase-space topology and engineered dissipation** to exponentially suppress bit-flips, converting the error model into a highly biased form amenable to low-overhead outer codes.

> **Unification Perspective:** All three can be seen as enforcing a *Zeno effect via strong symmetry*: frequent symmetrization (DFS group averaging), frequent pulses (DD), or strong dissipation (cat stabilization) project the system into a protected manifold, with leakage suppressed by an energy gap or dissipative gap scaling with control strength.

No single technique substitutes for full QEC under generic uncorrelated noise. Rather, the optimal near-term path is *layered suppression*: cat qubits providing exponentially biased-noise physical qubits, protected during idling by DD and during storage by approximate DFS-like symmetries in multi-cavity modules, with a high-threshold biased outer code completing fault tolerance. Such hardware-efficient fault tolerance, championed by programs at Yale, Alice&Bob, and AWS, may reduce physical qubit overhead by an order of magnitude relative to unbiased surface-code approaches.

The journey *beyond active correction* is not away from correction but toward *physics-aware* correction, where device Hamiltonians are designed to correct themselves. As experiments cross seconds of bit-flip lifetime and pulse sequences extend coherence by an order of magnitude autonomously, this vision edges toward reality, demanding deeper theoretical unification—an effort this thesis initiates and will require sustained, interdisciplinary inquiry at the intersection of group theory, control, and driven-dissipative physics.
