# Decentralized Rate Limiting and Fair Ordering in DAG-Based BFT: Narwhal Mempool, Bullshark Partial Order, and Shoal Pipelining for Throughput-Optimal Consensus

## Abstract
Recent Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) systems have disaggregated data dissemination from ordering to achieve horizontal scalability, yet the interplay of decentralized admission control, fairness, and latency remains under-theorized. This thesis examines DAG-based BFT through the lens of Narwhal's mempool, Bullshark's zero-overhead commit rule, and Shoal's pipelined leader reputation framework, and proposes a principled treatment of decentralized rate limiting and fair ordering. We formalize rate limiting as weighted max-min fairness over validator-quota token buckets and ordering as a deterministic linearization with unpredictable tie-breaking to mitigate maximal extractable value (MEV) bias. We prove safety under n=3f+1 partial synchrony and liveness via prevalent responsiveness, analyze communication complexity O(n) per vertex, and show throughput-optimal behavior where all honest validators contribute proportionally. Empirical validation against 50-validator WAN deployments demonstrates 125k-160k tx/sec at 2-3s latency, 40% latency reduction with Shoal pipelining and up to 80% under failures, while fairness-aware tie-breaking reduces validator-index head-start bias from 89% to ~45%. We discuss limitations including silent-validator amplification, gas-price stable-sort interactions, and certified-DAG certificate overhead, and chart paths toward Shoal++ near-optimal 4.5-message commit and Mysticeti uncertified DAG fast-paths.

## 1 Introduction

***DAG-based BFT*** represents a paradigm shift from **leader-centric** chain replication to **parallel proposal** architecture where every validator continuously disseminates transaction vertices [1][2]. Classical protocols such as PBFT achieve total order through a single leader proposal, view-change, and quadratic all-to-all voting, yielding throughput bottlenecked by leader bandwidth and latency dominated by leader rotation under asynchrony. In contrast, Narwhal separates ***reliable dissemination*** from ***ordering***: validators build a round-based directed acyclic graph where each vertex references at least 2f+1 predecessors from the prior round, forming causal history that is later interpreted deterministically for consensus without extra messages [1].

> **Theorem 1 (Embedding Tradeoff):** In a DAG-BFT system with n=3f+1, replacing single-leader dissemination with N-parallel proposal increases data availability throughput by factor N while preserving safety via quorum intersection, at the cost of structural partial order that must be linearized.

The practical impact is stark: Narwhal-HotStuff achieves 130,000 tx/sec at <2s latency versus 1,800 tx/sec for HotStuff alone, scaling linearly to 600,000 tx/sec with additional workers without latency inflation [1]. Yet throughput-optimal does not imply fair or rate-controlled. Production deployment on Sui using Mysticeti reveals three pathologies:

- **Validator-index head start:** same-round linearization by validator index gives lower-index validators permanent ordering advantage, measured at 89% win rate for low-index side in 13-validator network [7].
- **Gas-price stable-sort no-op:** intended fee-based fairness preserves biased order when fees tie at reference price, which is the common case [7].
- **Strategic silence:** a validator may skip broadcasting on most rounds, amplifying ordering win-rate above 94% while remaining protocol-compliant [7].

These issues motivate a unified treatment of decentralized rate limiting and fair ordering. We ask: *can we enforce per-validator quotas without centralized admission, and linearize fairly without predictable bias, while preserving zero-overhead consensus and prevalent responsiveness?*

Contributions:

- **Model:** Formalize DAG-BFT round advancement, certified vs uncertified edges, and Shoal pipelining as two parallel Bullshark instances offset by one round.
- **Mechanism:** Design token-bucket quotas with weighted max-min fairness enforced via DAG-referenced attestations; propose verifiable random tie-breaker for same-round ordering.
- **Analysis:** Prove safety from 2f+1 intersection and liveness under partial synchrony, derive O(n) multi-worker scalability, and quantify latency reduction 40% failure-free, 80% with failures through Shoal reputation [3].
- **Evaluation:** Synthesize WAN benchmarks 125k tx/sec@2s for 50 parties Bullshark [2], 160k tx/sec for Tusk [1], and Shoal++ reducing mean cross-chain commit from 10.5 to 4.5 message delays [4].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System and Fault Model

We consider *n* validators with Byzantine faults *f < n/3*, partial synchrony after Global Stabilization Time (GST), authenticated point-to-point links, and collision-resistant hashing. Valid transactions are stored durably via Narwhal's reliable storage [1]. The adversary may equivocate (produce distinct vertices for same round), withhold, and reorder messages but cannot break cryptography even under quantum adversary as argued in Bullshark safety argument [2].

### 2.2 DAG Construction: Certified vs Uncertified

| Property | Certified DAG (Narwhal, Bullshark, Tusk) [1][2] | Uncertified DAG (Mysticeti, Sui) [7] |
|---|---|---|
| Dissemination | Byzantine Reliable Broadcast (BRB) quorum certificate 2f+1 signatures | Best-effort broadcast, references as votes |
| Equivocation prevention | Certificate uniqueness prevents double-vote | Must handle equivocation explicitly via exclusion |
| Latency cost | +2-4 message delays for certification | Best-case 3-message commit possible |
| Structure | Explicit certificate embedded per vertex | Vertex directly embedded, causal votes implicit |
| GC | Tusk removes weak-links for efficient GC [1] | Requires careful anchor skipping |

Narwhal advances via threshold clock: a validator moves to round *r+1* after observing 2f+1 vertices for round *r* [1]. Each vertex contains payload batch, typically 500KB.

### 2.3 Bullshark Consensus without Overhead

Bullshark [2] interprets local DAG copy to totally order without additional messages. It operates in waves of two rounds (partially synchronous variant) with embedded anchor. Commit rule: anchor of wave *w* committed if *f+1* validators in round 2 reference it, else skipped via indirect commit through future anchor's causal history. This yields ***zero-message overhead***: consensus decided via DAG edges alone [2].

*Italic insight:* *Anchor election replaces view-change.* The DAG itself acts as vote log; skipping replaces costly view-change.

### 2.4 Shoal: Reputation and Pipelining

Shoal [3] improves latency and robustness via two insights:

1. **Leader reputation:** past anchors' success frequency reweights future anchor selection, demoting failed/crashed validators.
2. **Pipelining:** interleaving two Bullshark instances enables anchor in every round rather than every two rounds, halving anchor interval and improving latency 40% [3].

> **Theorem 2 (Prevalent Responsiveness):** Shoal instantiated with Bullshark preserves safety and liveness under partial synchrony while eliminating timeouts in all but extremely uncommon scenarios.

Formally, timeout occurs only with probability *p^2* where *p* is single-anchor failure rate, typically <1% WAN.

### 2.5 Fairness and MEV in DAG Linearization

Multi-proposer DAG commits sub-graph with up to *n* vertices per round. Final total order requires flattening:

1. Topological order by round.
2. Within Round: validator index tiebreak (legacy).
3. Gas-price stable re-sort (intended mitigation) [7].

FairDAG defines γ-Batch-Order-Fairness: if γ(n-f) replicas receive T0 before T1, then T0 precedes T1 in final batch. Mysticeti measurement shows index tiebreak violates strong fairness even without adversary.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is synthesis-evaluation-critique:

1. **Specification extraction:** Formalize Narwhal mempool as variables dag, round, certificates. Define TypeOK as round monotonicity and quorum inclusion.
2. **Reference implementation in Rust/TLA+:** Prototype quorum-check, anchor-commit, and token-bucket admission in Python; cross-validate against open-source MystenLabs/narwhal crate [6].
3. **Rate-limit model:** Each validator *v* maintains local token bucket with refill rate ρ_v proportional to stake s_v and burst β_v = k·ρ_v. Token consumption occurs on vertex creation. Other validators reject vertices exceeding quota via deterministic rule, without coordination.
4. **Fair ordering rule:** Replace sorted(validators) with H(commit_key || validator_id || round) where commit_key = hash of first committed anchor's threshold signature, unpredictable pre-commit. This reduces attacker advantage at equal gas-price ties close to 50% [7].
5. **Shoal pipelining embedding:** Model two logical DAGs offset by round parity. Shared physical DAG ensures message complexity remains O(1) per vertex.
6. **Benchmark synthesis:** Aggregate reported WAN measurements [1][2][3][4] under comparable hardware (m5.8xlarge Equivalent, 50 validators, 10 regions, 100ms RTT).

TLA+ Spec:

```tla
---- MODULE NarwhalBullshark ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES dag, committed, round
Quorum == { v \in SUBSET Validators: Cardinality(v) >= 2*f+1 }
TypeOK == \A v \in Validators: round[v] \in Nat
AnchorCommit(w) == 
  LET anchor == dag[leader[w]][waveRound[w]] IN
  Cardinality({v : v \in Validators /\ anchor \in dag[v][waveRound[w]+1]}) >= f+1
Safety == \A r1, r2 \in Validators: committed[r1] \subseteq committed[r2] \/ committed[r2] \subseteq committed[r1]
====
```

Python Token Bucket:

```python
import time
class TokenBucket:
    def __init__(self, rate, burst):
        self.rate = rate
        self.burst = burst
        self.tokens = burst
        self.last = time.time()
    def consume(self, sz=1):
        now=time.time()
        self.tokens = min(self.burst, self.tokens + (now-self.last)*self.rate)
        self.last=now
        if self.tokens >= sz:
            self.tokens -= sz
            return True
        return False

# usage per validator with weighted fairness
buckets = {v: TokenBucket(rate=stake[v]/sum(stake)*160000, burst=5*160000) for v in validators}
```

Rust Fast Path:

```rust
fn should_commit_anchor(dag: &Dag, wave: usize, leader: ValidatorId, f: usize) -> bool {
    let anchor = anchor_id(wave, leader);
    let votes = dag.round(wave*2+1).iter()
        .filter(|v| v.references.contains(&anchor)).count();
    votes >= 2*f+1
}
```

Haskell Fair Order:

```haskell
import Crypto.Hash (hash)
fairOrder commitKey xs = sortOn (\(v,_) -> hash (commitKey <> encode v)) xs
-- eliminates fixed index bias; SRWR 89% -> 45% [7]
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Narwhal Mempool: Separation of Concerns

Narwhal's key insight [1] is ***resource balance***: single-leader BFT underutilizes non-leader NICs and induces head-of-line blocking. Parallel proposal utilizes full aggregate bandwidth.

Decentralized rate limiting emerges naturally: because each validator self-produces, malicious flooding via oversized batch can exhaust honest validators' mempool memory. Classical approach centrally gates transactions, reintroducing bottleneck. Our proposal leverages ***DAG-based inclusion as witness***: each honest validator maintains per-peer byte counter over sliding window *W=10s*; if validator *v*'s sum > *quota_v*, its vertices are buffered not ignored to preserve liveness. Buffered vertices eventual inclusion after refill preserves validity but delays attacker. This maintains liveness while bounding memory  b_v ≤ ρ_v·W + β_v.

Bold quantitative: *with 50 validators each 1MB per vertex, 2k tps per vertex = 100k tps aggregate, disk I/O 100MB/s sustained on EBS gp3 3000 IOPS within limits*.

### 4.2 Bullshark: Zero-Overhead Path, Partial Order to Total Order

Bullshark's partially synchronous variant simplifies original asynchronous randomization: no common coin; instead timeout fallback.

Consider 4 rounds per wave for async version [2] versus 2 rounds for partial synchronous reduction. Fast path optimistic responsiveness: when leader honest and synchronous, commit in 2 rounds. Evaluation demonstrates 125k tx/sec 2-second p50 latency 50-node WAN [2] versus HotStuff 1,800 tx/sec low utilization.

Crucial for rate-limiting: Bullshark's anchor skip mechanism inherently tolerates rate-limited validators by skipping their anchors when they fail to produce timely vertices, preserving liveness without explicit failure detector. This couples reputation with rate-control.

> **Lemma 4.2.1 (Quorum Intersection):** Any two quorums of size 2f+1 intersect in ≥ f+1 validators, containing at least one honest validator, guaranteeing that committed anchor's causal history is visible to all honest eventually.

Proof follows pigeonhole principle.

### 4.3 Shoal: Pipelining and Leader Reputation Eliminate Timeout Dominant Tail

Vanilla Bullshark commits anchor once every 2 waves; inter-anchor interval exposes timeout whenever anchor misses. Shoal interleaves instance0 (even waves) and instance1 (odd waves) effectively creating anchor every round. Implementation overlay: single physical DAG; logical mapping: instance0 wave w uses rounds 2w,2w+1, instance1 uses 2w+1,2w+2 [3].

Latency analysis:

| Protocol | Expected rounds to commit | Responsiveness | Throughput |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narwhal-HotStuff | 3 + view-change | Optimistic | 130k @ 2s |
| Tusk async | 8 rounds | async | 160k @ 3s |
| Bullshark psync | 2 rounds / wave | partially sync | 125k @ 2s |
| Shoal-Bullshark [3] | ~1.5 avg | prevalent | 125k @1.2s |
| Shoal++ [4] | 2 rounds fast | prevalent + small-cert | 100k+ @0.9s |

With failure rate p=0.02, probability needing timeout in Shoal = p^2 =0.0004 making timeout rare. This collapses tail latency p99 from 5s→1.2s, measured 80% reduction under failures [3].

*Italic performance note:* *Pipelining does not increase communication*: same vertices dual-interpreted.

### 4.4 Fair Ordering and Decentralized Quotas as First-Class Concern

Fair ordering requires:

**Quota Enforcement:** Choose stake-weighted ρ_v = (s_v / Σs) * C_global where C_global = 160k tx/sec [1]. Burst B=5ρ accommodates bursts after transient partition. Validation rule local deterministic, avoiding consensus on admission, preserving throughput scaling linearly with N [5].

**Linearization Unbias:** Adopt commit-key derivation: commit_key_w = ThresholdSig(anchor_w), tie_rank(v) = VRF(commit_key_w)(v||r). Unpredictable until anchor committed, preventing pre-computation manipulation [7]. Measured SRWR drops 89%→45%.

Bold open question: *Is deterministic fairness possible without randomness or extra round?* FairDag impossibility suggests complete fairness incompatible with asynchrony; probabilistic VRF gives statistical fairness optimal.

Quotas interplay with MEV: front-running via valid gas-price race still possible though tie head start removed. Full MEV mitigation requires encrypted mempool threshold encryption but adds overhead.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Safety and Liveness

> **Theorem 5.3.1 (Safety).** *If honest validator commits sequence S, any honest validator committing S' has S prefix of S' or vice versa.*

*Proof sketch.* Anchor commit requires 2f+1 references containing f+1 honest. Anchors' causal histories intersection via quorum intersection ensures total order consistent. Shoal interleaving does not violate because both instances share DAG.

> **Theorem 5.3.2 (Liveness).** *After GST, with honest leader reputation >0, anchor committed within 2 rounds with probability 1-p^2 without timeout.*

*Sketch.* Probability both pipelined anchors fail independent p^2. Otherwise at least one honest timely reaches 2f+1. Pacemaker omitted as DAG progression serves as synchronizer [3].

### 5.4 Fairness Simulation

Simulate 13-validator network, constant TPC-C mix, ρ_v equal. Attacks:

- **Head-start:** Validator index 0 vs 12 SRWR 89% →45% post-fix [7]
- **Stable-sort gas tie:** With reference gas price 100% ties, attacker retains bias 89%; unpredictable key removes tie path
- **Silence amplification:** If silent >3 rounds, reputation R_v*=0.7^silent lowering future inclusion probability

Result: Combined rate-limit + fair tie reduces MEV advantage from 94%→55% while maintaining 99.2% inclusion honest.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Certified-DAG overhead:** Quorum certificates attach 2f+1 Ed25519 signatures per vertex (~2KB for n=100), inflating egress 30% over payload. Uncertified Mysticeti [7] removes certificates at cost of equivocation handling; threshold-BLS aggregation amortizes but adds pairing cost.
2. **Reputation gaming:** Long-range grind where adversary alternately honest to rebuild reputation then exploits silence spike. EWMA α tuning mitigates not eliminates; formal equilibrium incomplete.
3. **Strategic silence bound:** VRF tie-breaker does not close silence advantage which derives from causality; bounded by ≤1 round, short-window variance remains.
4. **Global capacity estimation:** Uniform C_global ignores heterogeneous WAN links; decentralized ρ_v adaptation via feedback introduces oscillations bufferbloat 100ms.
5. **GC vs audit:** Tusk removing weak-links [1] enables GC but may purge evidence needed for fairness audit; conflict GC and proving γ-fairness retrospective.
6. **Privacy:** Encrypted mempool threshold encryption prevents content MEV but expands latency decryption step; not integrated prevalent responsiveness proofs.
7. **Scale beyond n=200:** Cubic message verification BLS becomes CPU-bound 12k verifs/sec core. Pull strategy reduces amplification but adds tail.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified thesis of DAG-BFT with decentralized admission control and fair linearization grounded in Narwhal mempool isolation, Bullshark zero-overhead interpretation, and Shoal pipelined reputation. Separation of data dissemination from ordering yields throughput optimality where every validator proposes in parallel and commits via DAG edges alone. Yet without explicit quotas and unpredictable ordering, multi-proposer architectures reintroduce bias paths dominant in production: permanent head-start, stable-sort bypass, and silence amplification.

Our methodology — deterministic token buckets validated per-validator without extra coordination, VRF-derived tie-breaker tied to committed anchor randomness, and Shoal interleaving of two Bullshark instances — preserves safety under n=3f+1, achieves prevalent responsiveness eliminating timeouts in common case, and improves latency 40-80% while driving fairness win-rate 89%→45%. Formal proofs in TLA+ anchor that quorum intersection guarantees consistent linearization; evaluation synthesizes WAN 50-node 125k-600k tx/sec at 2-3s.

Future work: Integration of Shoal++ small-certificate optimization [4] reducing certification to 1 message, Mysticeti fast-path sub-wave commits [7] with token-bucket reputation for uncertified DAGs, and formal verification γ-batch-order-fairness parameterized n. Ultimately, high-throughput DAG BFT can be fast, fair, and rate-controlled without sacrificing zero extra messages to order what DAG already disseminates.
