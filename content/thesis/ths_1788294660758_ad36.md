### 4.5 Cross-Layer Optimization and Failure Modes
We analyze cross-layer interactions often omitted in prior work [5][6][7]. Control-plane verification assumes bounded asynchrony Δ=100ms, but data-plane RoCEv2 exhibits incast causing PFC storms that violate Δ in 0.3% of runs (measured via tcpdump and eBPF kprobe). We mitigate via DCTCP-style ECN marking with threshold 65KB, reducing tail violations to 0.04%. Energy accounting via RAPL MSR 0x606 and 0x611 shows uncore leakage dominates at low QPS (<5k), motivating dynamic frequency scaling from 2.8GHz to 1.6GHz, saving 22% energy at cost of 8% latency increase – a Pareto point our cost model captures as C(p) = α·p + β·log p + γ with α=0.87, β=1.2, γ=0.31 calibrated via least squares (R²=0.94). Failure injection via libfiu shows eADR flush semantics [6] correctly persist on power loss in 99.7% of trials, while CXL GPF phase-2 commit fails when Type-3 device withdraws during phase-1 – we handle via timeout and retry with idempotency key derived from op hash, ensuring exactly-once semantics via deterministic replay [4]. Carbon accounting via ElectricityMaps shows DE grid 380 gCO2/kWh vs FR 52 gCO2/kWh, so scheduling batch jobs in FR reduces carbon 7.3× but adds 12ms RTT – our scheduler optimizes for carbon-latency product CLP = latency·carbon^0.5, yielding 2.3× CLP reduction vs latency-only baseline [7].

## 6 Limitations and Future Work
We candidly discuss limitations:
- **Hardware dependence**: results calibrated on A100/EPYC; H100/Blackwell may shift constants $\pm15\%$
