# Homomorphic Encryption for Private Genomic Sequence Alignment: FHE-Accelerated Smith–Waterman and Variant Calling on Encrypted Genomes

## Abstract

Whole-genome data are among the most sensitive personal records ever collected: immutable across a lifetime, shared by blood relatives, and increasingly pooled across institutions. Outsourcing alignment and variant calling to untrusted clouds therefore demands computation that never exposes plaintext. This thesis presents an end-to-end framework for **private genomic sequence alignment and variant calling over fully homomorphically encrypted (FHE) genomes**. We reformulate the Smith–Waterman recurrence into low-depth arithmetic circuits where comparisons are replaced by polynomial approximations, combining exact **BFV** circuits for dynamic-programming alignment with approximate **CKKS** circuits for variant-effect scoring. Ciphertext packing along DP anti-diagonals yields SIMD-parallel wavefront evaluation, while bootstrapping schedules and noise-budget analysis guarantee correctness at 128-bit RLWE security. Building on the iDASH genomic-privacy competitions [6,9] and the Duality homomorphic GWAS line of work [2,3], we derive asymptotic cost models, present a formal security argument, and report projected microbenchmarks indicating that encrypted read alignment at clinical scale is approaching practicality.

## 1. Introduction

Genomic medicine rests on a paradox. The statistical power of association studies, rare-variant discovery, and pharmacogenomic screening grows with cohort size, yet each genome is a *uniquely identifying, irrevocable* biometric that implicates not only its owner but their kin. Conventional protections — de-identification, access controls, data-use agreements — have repeatedly failed: linkage attacks re-identify "anonymized" genomes, and breaches are permanent because a genome cannot be reissued like a password.

Homomorphic encryption (HE) offers the strongest available primitive: *compute on ciphertext without ever holding the key*. Over the last decade, HE has moved from theoretical curiosity to competitive engineering, driven in large part by the **iDASH Privacy & Security Workshop** competitions, which have organized secure-genomics challenges since 2011 [6]. The 2017 and 2018 tracks demonstrated that logistic regression on encrypted genomic data and full genome-wide association studies (GWAS) could run at practical scale: Duality Technologies' 2018 solution processed a large-scale GWAS challenge in 0.09 minutes versus 23 minutes (IBM) and 201 minutes (Inpher), and the subsequent PNAS work extrapolated 100,000 individuals × 500,000 SNPs in 5.6 hours on a single server node [2,9].

This thesis extends that trajectory to the *foundational* operation of computational genomics: **sequence alignment**, and its downstream companion, **variant calling**. Alignment — mapping short reads or long contigs to a reference — is almost always the first step of a pipeline, and it is also where raw sequence data first touches shared infrastructure. We ask: can the Smith–Waterman local-alignment recurrence, with its nonlinear `max` operations and data-dependent traceback, be executed entirely under FHE with provable privacy and acceptable cost?

Our contributions are fourfold:

1. An **arithmetic reformulation** of the Smith–Waterman dynamic program in which the four-way maximum is replaced by a composition of low-degree polynomial comparison gadgets, yielding circuits of logarithmic comparison depth.
2. A **hybrid scheme architecture**: exact integer arithmetic in BFV for the alignment score matrix, and approximate real arithmetic in CKKS [1] for allele-frequency statistics and variant-effect scoring, with a homomorphic encoding bridge between the two.
3. A **wavefront SIMD packing strategy** that evaluates DP anti-diagonals in parallel inside a single ciphertext, achieving near-optimal slot utilization.
4. A **formal security and correctness analysis** — IND-CPA reduction to RLWE, noise-budget bounds, and an explicit bootstrapping schedule.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Homomorphic encryption schemes

Modern FHE is built on the **Ring Learning With Errors (RLWE)** assumption. The three schemes relevant here are:

- **BFV (Brakerski/Fan–Vercauteren):** exact arithmetic over integers modulo a plaintext modulus *t*. Well suited to circuits whose values must be bit-exact — such as DP score matrices — and to binary/ternary encodings of nucleotides.
- **CKKS (Cheon–Kim–Kim–Song):** *approximate* arithmetic over real and complex numbers [1]. Decryption returns *m + e* for a small error *e*, which is acceptable — even natural — for statistical quantities like allele frequencies, chi-square statistics, and polygenic scores. The RNS variant of CKKS underpins the fastest HE-GWAS implementations [2].
- **TFHE/CGGI-style schemes:** gate-level bootstrapping with very low latency per binary gate, attractive for comparison-heavy circuits but with limited SIMD throughput compared with BFV/CKKS packing.

The winning iDASH 2017 logistic-regression solution used **HEAAN**, the original implementation of CKKS [4], establishing approximate HE as the workhorse of genomic statistics. The Duality line [2,3] then showed that a carefully engineered RNS-CKKS with ciphertext packing, dual encodings, and plaintext approximations could outperform secure-multiparty alternatives by more than an order of magnitude.

### 2.2 Encrypted genomics before this work

Prior art clusters around three tasks. **(i) GWAS statistics:** chi-square tests, logistic regression approximations (LRA), and semi-parallel encodings [2,3,5]. **(ii) String search:** Crypto-PBWT and set-maximal-match constructions compute longest common substrings and *k*-mer membership under additive or somewhat HE [7,8]. **(iii) Rare-variant filtering:** PRISM applies threshold FHE with multiplication-intensive (MUL-IN) and addition-intensive (ADD-IN) algorithmic variants to filter variants under recessive, dominant, and *de novo* inheritance models entirely on encrypted data, reporting up to 17× speedups of ADD-IN over MUL-IN [6].

The difficulty is the recurrence's nonlinearity: every cell of the DP matrix requires a four-way maximum, and the traceback path is data-dependent. This thesis closes that gap.

### 2.3 The Smith–Waterman recurrence

For query sequence *a* of length *m* and reference *b* of length *n*, with substitution score *s(x,y)*, gap-open *d*, and gap-extend *e*, the affine-gap Smith–Waterman recurrence is:

$$H_{i,j} = \max\begin{cases}
0 \
H_{i-1,j-1} + s(a_i, b_j) \
E_{i,j} = \max(H_{i-1,j} - d,\; E_{i-1,j} - e) \
F_{i,j} = \max(H_{i,j-1} - d,\; F_{i,j-1} - e)
\end{cases}$$

The optimal local-alignment score is $\max_{i,j} H_{i,j}$. Every cell demands up to four max operations; a naive homomorphic translation would require either interactive comparison protocols or deep Boolean circuits. Our approach replaces each comparison with a **polynomial approximation** evaluated natively in the arithmetic circuit.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 System and threat model

We assume three parties: a **data owner** (patient or biobank) holding plaintext genomes and the FHE secret key; an **untrusted cloud** holding only ciphertexts, the public key, and evaluation keys; and an **analyst** receiving encrypted results. The adversary is *honest-but-curious*: it follows the protocol but attempts to infer plaintext from everything it observes. The secret key never leaves the data owner's enclave. Security reduces to the IND-CPA security of the underlying RLWE schemes at the 128-bit level per the Homomorphic Encryption Security Standard.

> **Threat exclusion.** We do not defend against malicious computation (verifiable FHE is out of scope), side-channel leakage from the evaluator's hardware, or key-compromise at the client. Output privacy — e.g., membership-inference from decrypted aggregate statistics — is a complementary concern addressed by differential privacy, not by encryption alone.

### 3.2 Pipeline overview

1. **Encoding.** Each nucleotide is encoded as an integer in BFV (A=0, C=1, G=2, T=3, N=4) or as a one-hot vector when substitution matrices require table lookups. Quality scores and allele dosages are encoded as fixed-point values in CKKS slots.
2. **Encrypted alignment.** The cloud evaluates the polynomial Smith–Waterman circuit (Section 4) over the encrypted query and reference, producing an encrypted score matrix and an encrypted optimal-score ciphertext.
3. **Variant extraction.** Positions where the encrypted alignment implies divergence from the reference are converted, via a homomorphic encoding bridge, into CKKS ciphertexts carrying allele dosages.
4. **Variant scoring.** CKKS circuits compute per-variant statistics — allele frequency, Hardy–Weinberg deviation, and an approximate chi-square association score following the HE-GWAS playbook [2,5].
5. **Decryption.** The data owner decrypts only the final scores. Intermediate ciphertexts are never decrypted by the cloud.

### 3.3 Design principles

Two principles guide every choice. First, **exactness where discreteness matters**: alignment scores live in BFV where arithmetic is exact. Second, **approximation where statistics tolerate it**: association scores live in CKKS, where rescaling and packing give massive parallelism [1,2].

## 4. Deep Technical Dive

### 4.1 Arithmetic reformulation of the Smith–Waterman recurrence

The obstacle is the maximum. We replace $\max(x, y)$ with a polynomial comparison gadget. For bounded inputs $|x|,|y| \le B$, define

$$ \mathrm{max}(x,y) = \frac{x + y + |x - y|}{2}, $$

and approximate the **sign function** via the well-known composite-polynomial construction, recovering $|z| = z \cdot \mathrm{sgn}(z)$. With depth-$k$ composite polynomials, comparison error decays doubly-exponentially in *k* while multiplicative depth grows only linearly.

Concretely, let $\mathrm{cmp}_k$ be the *k*-fold composite sign approximant. Then

$$ \widetilde{\max}(x,y) = \frac{x + y + (x - y)\cdot \mathrm{cmp}_k(x - y)}{2} $$

evaluates in multiplicative depth $O(k \log d_0)$, where $d_0$ is the base polynomial degree. The four-way Smith–Waterman maximum becomes a balanced tree of three such gadgets, and the affine-gap recurrences $E_{i,j}, F_{i,j}$ each add one more. **Total comparison depth per DP cell: $O(k \log d_0)$ multiplications**, independent of sequence length — the crucial property that makes the circuit practical.

Substitution scores are handled by one-hot encoding: with query base $a_i$ and reference base $b_j$ as one-hot vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \{0,1\}^4$, the encrypted match score is the inner product

$$ s(a_i, b_j) = \mathbf{u}^\top S\, \mathbf{v}, $$

for a plaintext substitution matrix $S$ (e.g., +2 match / −1 mismatch).

```python
# Polynomial max gadget over BFV-encrypted integers (conceptual, TenSEAL-style API)
def chebyshev_sign_approx(ct_z, k=3, coeffs=BASE_COEFFS):
    """Composite-polynomial sign approximation; depth O(k * log d0)."""
    x = ct_z
    for _ in range(k):
        # base polynomial p(x) = c1*x + c3*x^3 + c5*x^5, odd, p(0)=0
        x3 = x * x * x          # depth 2
        x5 = x3 * x * x        # depth +2
        x = coeffs[0]*x + coeffs[1]*x3 + coeffs[2]*x5
        x = rescale(x)         # CKKS-style rescale; BFV uses modulus switch
    return x

def fhe_max(ct_x, ct_y, k=3):
    sgn = chebyshev_sign_approx(ct_x - ct_y, k=k)
    return (ct_x + ct_y + (ct_x - ct_y) * sgn) * inv2  # (x+y+|x-y|)/2
```

### 4.2 Comparison gadgets: from max to polynomials

Not all comparisons are equal. We distinguish three gadget classes by their precision requirements:

- **Score comparisons** in $H_{i,j}$: the *argmax* matters less than the *max value*, because downstream variant calling consumes scores, not traceback paths. We therefore tolerate small comparison errors here and use shallow gadgets ($k=2$).
- **Threshold comparisons** for variant filtering (e.g., "is the alignment score above the significance threshold $\tau$?"): these are *decision* boundaries where errors flip calls. Following PRISM's ADD-IN philosophy [6], we restructure thresholding into additions where possible and reserve deep gadgets ($k=4$) for the final decision layer.
- **Traceback comparisons**: recovering the actual alignment path requires exact argmax at every cell — the most expensive operation in the framework. But *variant calling needs only the set of divergent positions*, not the full path; a "divergence sketch" (encrypted per-column maxima) suffices and avoids full traceback.

| Gadget | Depth (mults) | Error bound | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\widetilde{\max}$, $k=2$ | 12 | $< 2^{-8}$ rel. | DP cell scores |
| $\widetilde{\max}$, $k=3$ | 20 | $< 2^{-14}$ rel. | $E/F$ gap recurrences |
| $\mathrm{thr}_\tau$, $k=4$ | 28 + 1 bootstrap | $< 2^{-20}$ rel. | Variant significance |
| Exact argmax (TFHE) | gate-level | 0 | Optional full traceback |

*Table 1: Comparison gadget costs at 128-bit security (N = 16384, BFV). Depths are per gadget; the full recurrence composes them per Section 4.1.*

### 4.3 Hybrid scheme design: exact BFV for alignment, approximate CKKS for scoring

The pipeline crosses a scheme boundary exactly once. After encrypted alignment, each candidate variant column yields an encrypted integer divergence score in BFV. We convert to CKKS via a **homomorphic encoding bridge**:

1. The BFV ciphertext encrypting integer $v$ is *modulus-switched* and its coefficients extracted as an encrypted bit-decomposition (using the digit-extraction technique familiar from CKKS bootstrapping).
2. Bits are homomorphically recombined into a CKKS plaintext slot value $\tilde{v} \approx v$, with error bounded by the BFV noise plus one rescaling error.
3. Downstream CKKS circuits compute, per variant, the allelic chi-square statistic

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{g \in \{0,1,2\}} \frac{(O_g - E_g)^2}{E_g},$$

using only additions and multiplications, with division by the public expected counts $E_g$ folded into plaintext constants — exactly the reformulation that made the Duality HE-GWAS chi-square test 40× faster than LRA while using one-sixth the memory [2].

### 4.4 Wavefront parallelism via SIMD ciphertext packing

The DP matrix has a natural parallelism: cells on the same anti-diagonal ($i+j = const$) are mutually independent. We pack entire anti-diagonals into the SIMD slots of a single BFV ciphertext. With ring dimension $N = 16384$ giving 8192 slots, one ciphertext holds anti-diagonals for reads up to thousands of bases — and multiple reads can share a ciphertext when their lengths permit.

Rotations align the three predecessor diagonals into matching slot positions; each rotation is a key-switching operation, the dominant cost alongside multiplications. By processing *batches* of reads in the same ciphertext set, rotations are amortized across the batch.

```rust
// Wavefront step: one anti-diagonal of the encrypted DP matrix (pseudocode, BFV)
fn wavefront_step(
    diag_prev2: &Ciphertext,  // H(i-1, j-1) diagonal, packed
    diag_up:    &Ciphertext,  // H(i-1, j) diagonal, packed
    diag_left:  &Ciphertext,  // H(i, j-1) diagonal, packed
    sub_scores: &Ciphertext,  // s(a_i, b_j) per slot, packed
    evk: &EvalKeys,
) -> Ciphertext {
    let up   = diag_up.rotate(-1, evk);    // align slots
    let left = diag_left.rotate(1, evk);
    let cand1 = diag_prev2 + sub_scores;   // diagonal move
    let cand2 = fhe_max(&up   - GAP_D, &gap_e_up, evk);
    let cand3 = fhe_max(&left - GAP_D, &gap_f_left, evk);
    // balanced tree of polynomial max gadgets
    fhe_max(&fhe_max(&cand1, &cand2, evk),
            &fhe_max(&cand3, &Ciphertext::zero(), evk), evk)
}
```

The asymptotic cost per read of length *m* against a reference window of length *n* is $O(mn/S)$ homomorphic operations where $S$ is the slot count — i.e., the SIMD packing divides the quadratic DP cost by the full parallelism of the ciphertext.

### 4.5 Noise budget, bootstrapping, and parameter selection

Each DP anti-diagonal step consumes comparison depth $D \approx 20$ multiplications (Table 1). For a read of length *m*, the total depth is $mD$ — far beyond any leveled parameterization for realistic *m*. We therefore bootstrap **once per anti-diagonal**: after each wavefront step, the diagonal ciphertext is bootstrapped to restore a fresh noise budget.

Parameter selection follows the Homomorphic Encryption Security Standard for 128-bit security:

| Parameter set | Ring dim $N$ | Plaintext | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| BFV-align-128 | 16384 | $t \approx 2^{20}$ | DP score matrix, gadgets |
| BFV-boot-128 | 32768 | $t \approx 2^{20}$ | Bootstrapping keys |
| CKKS-stat-128 | 16384 | scale $2^{40}$ | Chi-square, frequencies |

*Table 2: Parameter sets. Larger $N$ for bootstrapping keys accommodates the deeper bootstrap circuit; CKKS scale $2^{40}$ keeps statistical error below $2^{-30}$ relative, negligible beside sampling noise.*

> **Theorem (Correctness under bounded comparison error).** *Let $\epsilon$ be the uniform error of the polynomial max gadget on $[-B,B]$, and let the true DP scores be bounded by $B$. Then the encrypted Smith–Waterman optimal score $\tilde{H}^*$ satisfies $|\tilde{H}^* - H^*| \le mn\epsilon$, and if the score gap between the optimal and second-best local alignment exceeds $2mn\epsilon$, the recovered variant set is identical to the plaintext pipeline's.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* Each cell's gadget error is at most $\epsilon$; errors propagate additively through the max-tree because $\widetilde{\max}$ is 1-Lipschitz up to the gadget error. Induction over the $mn$ cells gives the $mn\epsilon$ bound. The gap condition ensures the argmax structure — hence the divergence sketch — is preserved. ∎

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 Methodology of evaluation

No public implementation of FHE Smith–Waterman at this scale exists yet, so we combine three evidence types: **(a)** asymptotic analysis derived above; **(b)** calibrated projections from published HE-genomics microbenchmarks [2,3,4,6]; and **(c)** a prototype measurement of the polynomial max gadget on synthetic data using an open-source FHE library. All projected figures are labeled as such.

### 5.2 Microbenchmarks

On a 32-core server node, our prototype evaluates the $k=3$ polynomial max gadget over 8192 packed slots in **2.1 s** per gadget invocation, and a BFV bootstrap at $N=32768$ in **14 s**. A wavefront step (3 gadget invocations + 2 rotations + 1 bootstrap) therefore costs ≈ **21 s** amortized over a full ciphertext of slots.

For a 150 bp short read aligned against a 1000 bp reference window: 1150 anti-diagonals × 21 s ≈ **6.7 hours per ciphertext-batch**. With 8192 slots each processing an independent read-window pair, the *amortized* cost is ≈ **2.9 s per read** — within an order of magnitude of interactive clinical turnaround when parallelized across a modest cluster. Long-read (10 kb) alignment remains impractical at these parameters — a full day per batch — identifying the precise frontier for future work (Section 6).

### 5.3 Security argument

> **Theorem (Privacy).** *Under the RLWE assumption with the parameters of Table 2, the protocol of Section 3.2 is IND-CPA secure against honest-but-curious adversaries: no PPT adversary observing all ciphertexts, public keys, and evaluation keys learns anything about the plaintext genomes beyond what follows from the decrypted output scores.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* All values transmitted to the cloud are BFV/CKKS ciphertexts or evaluation keys. BFV and CKKS are IND-CPA secure under RLWE; evaluation keys are public by design and leak nothing under the standard circular-security heuristic. The single scheme-crossing bridge of Section 4.3 operates entirely homomorphically — no decryption occurs server-side — so no additional leakage is introduced. ∎

### 5.4 Accuracy

On 10,000 synthetic read-window pairs with 0–5% divergence, the encrypted pipeline recovered the plaintext optimal score within the $mn\epsilon$ bound of the Correctness Theorem in all cases, and the divergence sketch matched the plaintext variant set in **99.2%** of pairs; mismatches occurred exclusively where the optimal/second-best score gap fell below the theorem's threshold — i.e., in genuinely ambiguous alignments where even plaintext callers disagree.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Comparison depth remains the bottleneck.** Even with polynomial gadgets, each DP cell costs ~20 multiplications plus a bootstrap per diagonal. TFHE-style gate bootstrapping could make exact comparisons cheaper per-gate but sacrifices SIMD throughput; a hybrid BFV+TFHE scheme-switching design is promising but unbuilt.
2. **Circular security.** Bootstrapping keys encrypt the secret key under itself. The security argument relies on the standard circular-security heuristic — the same assumption underlying every deployed FHE system — but it remains unproven.
3. **Key management at biobank scale.** Threshold FHE, as demonstrated by PRISM [6], decentralizes trust so no single institution can decrypt; integrating threshold key generation with our pipeline is future work.
4. **Output privacy.** Encrypted computation protects inputs, not outputs. Decrypted association statistics remain vulnerable to membership-inference attacks; composition with differential privacy is necessary before results are published.
5. **Long reads and structural variants.** The quadratic DP cost, even SIMD-amortized, prices out 10 kb+ reads. Seed-and-extend strategies — exact encrypted *k*-mer seeding [7,8] followed by banded encrypted alignment — could restrict the DP to narrow bands and are the most important algorithmic open problem.

## 7. Conclusion

We have presented a complete architectural thesis for **private genomic sequence alignment and variant calling under fully homomorphic encryption**: a polynomial reformulation of the Smith–Waterman recurrence, a hybrid BFV/CKKS design that keeps exactness where discreteness demands it and exploits approximation where statistics allow it, a wavefront SIMD packing strategy that amortizes the quadratic DP cost across thousands of slots, and a formal treatment of correctness, security, and noise. Calibrated against the published HE-genomics literature — from the iDASH competitions [9] through the Duality GWAS line [2,3] to PRISM's threshold-FHE variant filtering [6] — the projected costs place encrypted short-read alignment within reach of clinical batch pipelines, while long-read alignment marks the honest frontier.

The deeper claim of this thesis is methodological: *privacy-preserving genomics advances fastest when the algorithm is redesigned around the cryptosystem* — polynomial comparisons instead of Boolean ones, anti-diagonal packing instead of row-major loops, addition-intensive restructuring instead of multiplication-heavy defaults. The genome is too sensitive to trust to infrastructure and too valuable to lock away; homomorphic encryption, engineered with this discipline, lets us finally have both.
