# Verifiable Delay Functions in Proof-of-Stake Consensus: Wesolowski Proofs, Pietrzak Proofs, Sloth SNARGs, and RANDAO Bias Mitigation

## Abstract
Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) enforce sequential computation yet admit polylogarithmic verification, providing unbiasable public randomness for Proof-of-Stake (PoS) leader election, committee sampling, and sharding assignment. This thesis compares the two practical VDFs deployed in production discourse—Wesolowski's succinct proof-of-exponentiation with constant-size proofs and two-exponentiation verification, and Pietrzak's logarithmic halving protocol with O(log T) verification and O(sqrt(T)) prover parallelism—under groups of unknown order, the low-order and adaptive-root assumptions, and Fiat-Shamir non-interactivity via hash-to-prime and hash-to-random. We dissect Sloth, MiMC, MinRoot, and Veedo algebraic candidates and their SNARG/STARK wrapping, proving why naive permutation VDFs fail to achieve hardware-independent sequentiality under parallel cryptanalysis from CRYPTO 2024. We then formalize Ethereum's RANDAO XOR beacon, last-revealer withholding, selfish mixing, and forking manipulation, and demonstrate how VDFs compose with commit-reveal to neutralize bias under rational economic models incorporating MEV, ASIC speedup, and threshold BLS signatures, with EVM gas measurements and prover benchmarks.

## 1 Introduction
A **Verifiable Delay Function** is a triple $(\mathsf{Setup}, \mathsf{Eval}, \mathsf{Verify})$ such that $\mathsf{Eval}(x,T) = y$ requires $T$ *sequential* steps, yet $\mathsf{Verify}(x,y,\pi,T)$ runs in $O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$ time [1][2]. Boneh et al. formalized VDFs as an extension of Rivest-Shamir-Wagner time-lock puzzles with public verifiability and uniqueness [3].

In PoS, randomness beacons select proposers, committees, and shufflings. If the adversary can **predict** or **bias** randomness, it can grind proposer slots, censor, or extract MEV [4][5]. Ethereum's beacon chain originally used **RANDAO**—a simple XOR-based commit-reveal—vulnerable to last-revealer withholding. VDFs were proposed to force a delay $\Delta > \text{network latency}$ before output becomes known, eliminating withholding advantage [6][7].

This thesis unifies:

- *Cryptographic core*: repeated squaring $y = x^{2^T}$ in groups of unknown order
- *Proof systems*: **Wesolowski** $O(1)$ proof vs **Pietrzak** $O(\log T)$ halving
- *Algebraic attempts*: Sloth / MiMC / MinRoot / Veedo and why [8] broke them
- *PoS integration*: RANDAO bias games, optimal manipulation, and VDF composition

> **Theorem 1 (VDF Uniqueness):** In a group $G$ of unknown order with low-order assumption, if $y = x^{2^T}$ then any PPT adversary cannot produce $y' \neq y$ and valid $\pi'$ s.t. $\mathsf{Verify}(x,y',\pi')=1$ except with $\mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Time-Lock Puzzles to VDFs
Rivest et al. defined $y = x^{2^T} \bmod N$ where $N=pq$ RSA. Sequentiality follows from inability to parallelize squaring without $\phi(N)$ [1]. However verification requires $\phi(N)$—not a VDF. Wesolowski [2] and Pietrzak [3] independently made it verifiable in 2018-2019.

Key properties:

- **Sequentiality**: $T$-sequential, even with $\mathrm{poly}(T)$ parallel processors
- **Soundness**: statistically or computationally sound under **adaptive root** and **low-order** assumptions [3]
- **Incrementality**: single $\mathsf{pp}$ supports many $T$

### 2.2 Groups of Unknown Order
Security hinges on:

- *Unknown order*: adversary cannot compute $|G|$ to reduce exponent $2^T \bmod |G|$
- *Low-order assumption*: hard to find $g \neq 1, d < 2^\lambda$ s.t. $g^d=1$

Candidates:

- **RSA group** $(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^*/\{\pm 1\}$ with $N$ product of safe primes [3][9]. Requires trusted setup or **MPC ceremony** to discard $p,q$, or use RSA-2048 challenge modulus [2].
- **Class groups** $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$ of imaginary quadratic order $\Delta = -p$, $p$ prime [2]. Trustless, but 2x-3x slower, compression complex.

RSA quotient removes order-2 element $-1$ [9].

### 2.3 Ethereum RANDAO
Per epoch $E=32$ slots, each proposer contributes $r_i = \mathrm{hash}( \text{reveal}_i)$. Beacon computes:

$$ \mathsf{randao\_mix} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{32} r_i $$

Attacker controlling tail slots can **withhold** to bias next epoch's proposer selection [4][5]. Alpturer-Weinberg proved combinatorial bounds: $25\%$ stake yields $+4.09\%$ proposer advantage optimal vs $+2.99\%$ greedy [5]. Tapolcai et al. introduced **forking** attacks combining reorgs + selfish mixing, choosing among $ \approx 24$ RANDAO outcomes for $\alpha=0.28$ sacrificing $1.1$ slots [5].

VDF mitigation: $ \mathsf{output} = \mathsf{VDF}(\mathsf{randao\_mix}, T)$ where $T$ is set such that evaluation takes $>1$ epoch, so last-revealer cannot know biased result before honest reveals lock.

## 3 Methodology
We compare VDFs via formal analysis + implementation measurement.

**Formal models:**

- Interactive protocol $\to$ non-interactive via Fiat-Shamir random oracle $H: G^2 \to \mathsf{Primes}_\lambda$ or $r \leftarrow H(x,y,\mu)$
- Security reductions to adaptive root game and low-order game [3]
- RANDAO bias as MDP: states are tail length, actions withhold/reveal/fork, reward is future proposer slots [4][5]

**Empirical:**

- EVM gas: Pietrzak verification $O(\log T)$ 2048-bit RSA ~1.8M-2.6M gas for $\tau=20-25$ ($T=2^\tau$) with $\delta=9$ proof-shortening [10]
- Prover: Pietrzak $O(\sqrt{T})$ parallelization vs Wesolowski $O(T)$ but constant proof [3]
- Algebraic VDF cryptanalysis: latency of $x^e$ with $e \approx 2^{40}$ using parallel addition chains [8]

Code references: Rust `vdf` crate, Chia `chiavdf`, Ethereum `minimal VDF beacon` spec [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wesolowski's Succinct Proof-of-Exponentiation

Protocol for $y = x^{2^T}$:

Prover computes $y$ in $T$ squarings.

**Interactive:**

1. Verifier samples random prime $l \xleftarrow{\$} \mathsf{Primes}_\lambda$ where $\lambda \approx 128$ bits
2. Prover computes $\pi = x^{\lfloor 2^T / l \rfloor} \bmod N$
3. Verifier computes $r = 2^T \bmod l$ and checks $y \stackrel{?}{=} \pi^l \cdot x^r \bmod N$ [2]

Non-interactive: $l = H(x,y)$ where $H$ is hash-to-prime [2][3].

> **Theorem 2 (Wesolowski Soundness):** Under adaptive root assumption in $G$, the PoE is sound: if $y \neq x^{2^T}$, then $\Pr_l[ \exists \pi: \pi^l x^r = y ] \le \mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$.

Complexity:

- Proof size: **1 group element** ~ 256 bytes for 2048-bit $N$ → $0.25$ KB vs Pietrzak $10$ KB for same $T$ [2][9]
- Verification: **2 exponentiations** $\pi^l$, $x^r$ with $|l|=\lambda$, $O(\lambda)$ multiplications
- Prover: $O(T)$ squarings + $O(T)$ to compute $\pi$ naively, but Wesolowski shows division algorithm with $O(T)$ using long division in exponent.

**Fiat-Shamir pitfall**: $l$ must be prime, else $\gcd$ attacks. Hash-to-prime costs ~ $ \approx 100$ Miller-Rabin trials. EVM precompile missing → Pietrzak preferred on-chain [10].

**Rust sketch:**

```rust
fn wesolowski_prove(x: &BigInt, t: u64, n: &BigInt) -> (BigInt, BigInt) {
    let mut y = x.clone();
    for _ in 0..t { y = &y * &y % n; } // T sequential
    let l = hash_to_prime(&y); // 128-bit prime
    let q = (BigInt::from(2).pow(t) / &l).to_bigint().unwrap();
    let pi = x.modpow(&q, n);
    (y, pi)
}
fn wesolowski_verify(x: &BigInt, y: &BigInt, pi: &BigInt, t: u64, n: &BigInt, l: &BigInt) -> bool {
    let r = BigInt::from(2).modpow(&BigInt::from(t), l) % l; // 2^T mod l
    let lhs = (pi.modpow(l, n) * x.modpow(&r, n)) % n;
    lhs == *y
}
```

### 4.2 Pietrzak's Recursive Halving Protocol

Pietrzak trades proof size for prover efficiency.

Idea: to prove $x^{2^T}=y$, prover sends midpoint $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$. Verifier samples $r \leftarrow H(x,y,\mu)$ and reduces to smaller claim:

$$ x_1 = x^r \cdot \mu,\quad y_1 = \mu^r \cdot y $$

Claim: $x_1^{2^{T/2}} = y_1$. Recurse $\log T$ times until $T=1$ checkable by one squaring [3].

Full proof: $\{\mu_i\}_{i=1}^{\log T}$; verification $O(\log T)$ exponentiations.

Complexity comparison:

| Scheme | Proof size ($T=2^{40}, \lambda=2048$) | Verification | Prover overhead | Assumption |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wesolowski | 0.25 KB | 2 exp | $O(T)$ | Adaptive root + low-order |
| Pietrzak | ~10 KB | $O(\log T)$ exp ~ 3 RSA exp | $O(\sqrt{T})$ with parallel | Low-order |
| Pietrzak ($\delta=9$) | 5.38-7.25 KB | 1.8M-2.6M gas EVM | same | Low-order |

*Table: measurements from Attias et al. and Kuppe et al. [10][9]*.

**Proof shortening** $\delta$: stop halving early, verify $x^{2^{2^\delta}} \stackrel{?}{=} y$ directly costing $2^\delta$ squarings. Optimal $\delta$ minimizes gas + calldata: $\tau=20,\delta=9$ → 1,817,855 gas, 5.38 KB [10].

**Haskell recursive verifier:**

```haskell
verifyPietrzak :: Integer -> Integer -> Integer -> [Integer] -> Integer -> Bool
verifyPietrzak x y t [] n = powmod x (2^t) n == y
verifyPietrzak x y t (mu:mus) n =
  let r = hashToInt (x,y,mu) `mod` (2^128)
      x' = (powmod x r n * mu) `mod` n
      y' = (powmod mu r n * y) `mod` n
  in verifyPietrzak x' y' (t `div` 2) mus n
```

### 4.3 Sloth, Algebraic VDFs, and SNARG Wrapping

**Sloth** (Lenstra-Wesolowski): $y = x$ iterated over permutation $x \mapsto x^2$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p \equiv 3 \mod 4$, using modular sqrt as inverse. Gap evaluation vs verification is only $O(1)$ constant → useless without ASIC parity [6].

Drake's minimal VDF beacon list [6]:

- Sloth: constant gap, requires VDF ASIC for verifiers if evaluator uses ASIC → centralization
- MiMC: similar but smaller gap, more non-arithmetic ops
- Guralnick-Muller: unstudied, risky
- **Sloth++ with SNARKs**: wrap Sloth iterated squaring in Groth16 STARK, but SNARK prover needs ASIC to keep up
- **Sloth++ with STARKs**: quantum-secure candidate, prover faster, recursive STARKs harder

**Cryptanalysis** [8] by Biryukov et al. (CRYPTO 2024) broke Sloth++, Veedo, MinRoot: exponentiation $x^e$ latency reducible via parallel addition chains, not $\log_2 e$ sequential multiplications as assumed. Paper shows $40\%$ speedup with $2^{12}$ parallelism, invalidating $ \Delta$ claims.

> **Theorem 3 (Algebraic VDF Insecurity):** There exists parallel algorithm computing $x^{2^T + c}$ in $< T$ sequential multiplications using $O(T / \log T)$ processors via windowing, refuting idealized sequentiality for Sloth-like constructions [8].

Hence production VDFs use RSA/class groups, not permutation polynomials.

SNARG approach: even with RSA VDF, one can wrap verification in SNARK to compress Pietrzak logs to 200 bytes for EVM precompile; Chia uses class group VDF + no proof initially, Pietrzak later.

### 4.4 RANDAO + VDF Composition for Ethereum PoS

Design:

```
for each slot:
  proposer contributes randao_reveal = hash(sk)
  randao_mix = xor_accum(reveal)
at epoch boundary:
  vdf_input = hash(randao_mix || epoch)
  vdf_output = VDF_Eval(vdf_input, T=10min)
  seed = hash(vdf_output)
  seed used for next epoch proposer selection
```

Security:

- If $T >$ network + reveal phase ($\approx 6.4$ min for 32 slots *12s), attacker cannot evaluate VDF in time to decide withholding [4][7]
- **Honest liveness**: even if one VDF evaluator exists (e.g., EF-run), output eventually known; others verify in ms
- **Economics**: rational adversary with MEV $V$ will invest in ASIC speedup if $V > \text{cost(ASIC)}$. Model [11] shows threshold $V^*$ linear in $T$; for Ethereum $V \approx \$2600/ETH$ proposer MEV ~ $100-500$, VDF 1024-bit RSA ~ $33-136$ USD verification cost still < attack profit if hardware speedup $>2x$ [10][11]

Mitigations:

- **Longer epochs**: increase combinatorial work for manipulation (E=64 reduces tail power) [5]
- **Decreased proposer boost**: increase stake needed for forking manipulation [5]
- **Single-slot finality**: removes long reorgs enabling RANDAO forking
- **Commit-reveal + VDF + threshold BLS**: Chimera beacon combining $2f+1$ threshold signatures for unpredictability, VDF for bias-resistance, RANDAO for liveness [7]

Python beacon simulation:

```python
import hashlib, time
def randao_mix(reveals: list[bytes]) -> bytes:
    acc = bytes(32)
    for r in reveals:
        acc = bytes(a ^ b for a,b in zip(acc, hashlib.sha256(r).digest()))
    return acc

def vdf_eval(x: int, T: int, N: int) -> int:
    y = x
    for _ in range(T):
        y = pow(y, 2, N)
    return y

def beacon_epoch(reveals, N, T):
    mix = randao_mix(reveals)
    xi = int.from_bytes(hashlib.sha256(mix).digest(), 'big') % N
    start = time.time()
    y = vdf_eval(xi, T, N)  # sequential bottleneck
    return hashlib.sha256(str(y).encode()).digest(), time.time()-start
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Formal Security Proofs

**Adaptive Root Assumption**: Given random $w \in G$ and prime $l$, hard to find $v$ s.t. $v^l = w$. Wesolowski reduction shows soundness violation → break adaptive root [3].

Proof sketch:

Assume adversary produces $(x,y,\pi)$ with $y \neq x^{2^T}$ but $\pi^l x^r = y$. Then $w = y / x^{r'}$ where $r' = 2^T \bmod l$ ... leads to $l$-th root. QED.

**Low-Order Assumption** needed for Pietrzak: if attacker knows $\mu$ of order $d$, can cheat halving with prob $1/d$ [9]. TrailofBits analysis shows $-1$ order 2 breaks RSA without quotient [9].

### 5.2 Gas and Throughput

From Kuppe et al. 2024 EVM implementation [10]:

- $\lambda=2048$, $\tau=20$, $\delta=9$: **1,817,855 gas**, calldata 5.38 KB
- $\tau=25$: 2,603,753 gas, 7.25 KB
- $\lambda=3072$: 3,495,396-4,958,138 gas (7.13-9.63 KB) — $1.35x$ security, $1.9x$ cost

Assuming 5-20 gwei, 1 ETH=$2600, cost $33.9-$136.6 USD per verification at $\tau=25$ [10]. Wesolowski would be ~400k gas if hash-to-prime precompile existed, but current EVM lacks it.

Prover: Chia class group VDF $T=2^{23}$ takes ~100s on i5-7400 CPU, 10s on FPGA ASIC, proof generation extra $0.8$ sec for Pietrzak with $O(\sqrt{T})$ technique (Attias et al.) [9].

### 5.3 RANDAO Manipulation Bounds

Monte Carlo for optimal policy computed via DP on tail length [4]:

- $\alpha=0.25$ stake: honest proposer rate 0.25, optimal manipulation 0.2602 (+4.09% vs honest, +1.1% vs greedy tailmax)
- $\alpha=0.33$: tail len $\ge 2$ probability $0.1089$, forking success $>50\%$ for target slot with $0.5$ honest slot sacrificed [5]
- Economic security: $V > c \cdot T / \text{speedup}$ → invest in ASIC if MEV $> \$10k$ per epoch (approx) [11]

Thus VDF delay must exceed honest network latency + rational speedup factor. Current Ethereum considers $T=1024$ squarings in class group ~10 min, verification 100ms [7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Trusted setup**: RSA groups need MPC to discard factors; if one participant honest, secure, but ceremony complexity high. Class groups trustless but slower and need discriminant $|\Delta| > 2^{512}$ to avoid Beullens attack.
- **Hardware arms race**: VDF ASICs can achieve 10-100x speedup over CPU; if evaluation time set for CPU 10 min, ASIC evaluator finishes in 6 sec, reintroducing predictability unless verification forces waiting [11]. Beacon must use *minimum* evaluation time = ASIC time.
- **Wesolowski hash-to-prime**: no efficient EVM precompile; using SHA256→candidate→Miller-Rabin in Solidity costs >10M gas [10]. Workaround Truebit-style interactive verification adds liveness assumption.
- **Algebraic VDF broken**: Sloth/MiMC not suitable; must not use for consensus critical randomness [8].
- **Network synchrony**: VDF neutrality assumes partial synchrony; during network partition, adversary may delay honest reveals to bias.
- **Quantum**: RSA and class group VDFs broken by Shor; STARK-wrapped Sloth is only quantum-secure candidate but immature [6].
- **Grinding**: if VDF input not bound to epoch (e.g., attacker can tweak input by withholding), can still grind $k$ inputs in parallel if $k \cdot T$ parallelism allowed; need input = hash(RANDAO, epoch) not attacker-controllable.

## 7 Conclusion
Wesolowski and Pietrzak transformed time-lock puzzles into publicly verifiable delay functions via **proofs of exponentiation** in groups of unknown order, with tradeoffs: $O(1)$ proof vs $O(\log T)$ proof, adaptive-root vs low-order, prover $O(T)$ vs $O(\sqrt{T})$. Algebraic permutations like Sloth failed to provide hardware-independent sequentiality, and their SNARG-wrapped variants remain theoretical due to ASIC prover gaps [6][8].

For PoS, RANDAO alone is **biasable** by last-revealer and forking strategies yielding $5$ bits of manipulative power [5]. Composing RANDAO with a VDF—$ \mathsf{seed} = H(\mathsf{VDF}(\bigoplus r_i))$—removes withholding advantage if $T$ exceeds epoch duration and evaluator set is permissionless yet at least one honest. Optimal parameters from EVM gas studies suggest $\tau=20,\delta=9$, 2048-bit RSA, 5-7 KB proofs, ~2M gas, practical for L1 if amortized per epoch [10].

Future work: trustless class group VDF ceremony for Ethereum, EVM precompile for Wesolowski hash-to-prime, single-slot finality to eliminate RANDAO forking, and economic analysis tying MEV $V$ to VDF hardware cost to derive incentive-compatible $T$. VDFs are **necessary** for dishonest-majority coin-flipping [5], making them foundational, not optional, for unbiasable PoS beacons.
