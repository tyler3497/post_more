# Federated Learning: Secure Aggregation, Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent, and Communication-Efficient Optimization

## Abstract

Federated learning trains machine learning models across decentralized devices holding local data, without raw data ever leaving its origin. This thesis develops the three pillars that make the paradigm viable at scale: **secure aggregation** protocols that let an untrusted server learn only sums of client updates; **differentially private stochastic gradient descent**, which bounds the information any single participant can leak through released models; and **communication-efficient optimization**, which reduces the dominant cost of federated training — network transfer — through local computation, compression, and heterogeneity-aware correction. We formalize the federated empirical risk minimization problem, present the four-round masked aggregation protocol and its polylogarithmic successors, derive the clipping-and-noise mechanics of DP-SGD with tight Rényi accounting, and analyze FedAvg-type algorithms under non-IID data. We characterize the three-way privacy–utility–communication trade-off, survey production deployments, and identify open problems in malicious security, adaptive privacy budgeting, and cross-silo deployment.

##1 Introduction

Modern machine learning is hungry for data, yet the richest data now lives on devices its owners will never surrender: phones, hospitals, banks. Centralizing such data invites regulatory sanction — data-minimization principles are explicit in modern privacy law — and invites breach. Federated learning (FL), introduced in its modern optimization form by McMahan et al. [1], inverts the pipeline: the model travels to the data, and only model updates travel back. The deployment of federated next-word prediction on mobile keyboards demonstrated the paradigm at planetary scale [4].

But the naive version of this idea is neither private nor cheap. Gradients and weight deltas are functions of private data, and a long line of reconstruction attacks shows they leak it: an honest-but-curious server can invert gradients to recover training images and text [4]. Meanwhile the dominant cost of federated training is not FLOPs but bits — uplink bandwidth on mobile networks is scarce, metered, and asymmetric [9].

This thesis develops the three mechanisms that jointly make federated learning viable. *Secure aggregation* lets the server learn the sum of client updates while remaining cryptographically blind to each summand [2][8]. *Differentially private SGD* bounds, in a rigorous quantitative sense, what the released model can reveal about any participant [3][7]. *Communication-efficient optimization* shrinks the per-round payload through local computation and compression, so training converges before the bandwidth budget is exhausted [1][5][6][9].

##2 Background

**The federated optimization problem.** There are K clients; client k holds a private dataset D_k of n_k examples drawn from a local distribution P_k. The global objective is the weighted empirical risk

F(w) = sum over k of (n_k / n) F_k(w), where F_k(w) = (1 / n_k) sum over (x,y) in D_k of loss(w; x, y),

with total n = sum over k of n_k. Two heterogeneities distinguish this from datacenter distributed learning. *Statistical heterogeneity*: the distributions P_k differ — data is non-IID, so local optima drift apart. *Systems heterogeneity*: devices differ in compute, bandwidth, and availability; stragglers and mid-round dropouts are the norm, not the exception [4].

**Threat models.** The standard assumption is an *honest-but-curious* server: it follows the protocol but inspects everything it receives. Secure aggregation addresses the honest-but-curious server; it does not, by itself, stop a malicious client from corrupting the sum.

**Differential privacy.** A randomized mechanism M satisfies (epsilon, delta)-differential privacy if for all adjacent datasets D, D' and all output sets S, the probability that M(D) falls in S is at most e^epsilon times the probability that M(D') falls in S, plus delta. Rényi DP [7] reframes the guarantee through the Rényi divergence of order alpha, yielding (alpha, epsilon)-RDP; its clean composition algebra and tight conversion back to (epsilon, delta)-DP make it the workhorse of modern privacy accounting.

**Cryptographic primitives.** Pairwise additive masking needs only a key-agreement protocol (Diffie–Hellman) and a pseudorandom generator: two clients derive a shared seed, expand it into a mask, and add or subtract it so the masks cancel in the aggregate. Shamir secret sharing splits each client's self-mask seed into shares distributed to peers, so the server can reconstruct masks of dropped clients without ever seeing an honest client's update [2].

##3 Methodology

**Federated averaging.** The canonical algorithm is FedAvg [1]. In round t, the server broadcasts w_t to a cohort S_t of m clients. Each client runs E epochs of mini-batch SGD locally, producing w_t^k, and returns the delta Δ_t^k = w_t^k − w_t. The server updates w_{t+1} = w_t + sum over k in S_t of (n_k / n_{S_t}) Δ_t^k.

```python
def federated_averaging(clients, w0, rounds, cohort_frac, epochs, lr):
    w = w0
    m = max(int(cohort_frac * len(clients)), 1)
    for t in range(rounds):
        cohort = sample(clients, m)          # without replacement
        weighted_deltas, total_n = [], 0
        for k in cohort:
            w_k = client_update(k, w, epochs, lr)   # E local SGD epochs
            weighted_deltas.append((k.n, w_k - w))
            total_n += k.n
        w = w + sum((n / total_n) * d for n, d in weighted_deltas)
    return w
```

**Secure aggregation protocol.** Bonawitz et al. [2] run four rounds. (1) *AdvertiseKeys*: clients exchange Diffie–Hellman public keys through the server. (2) *ShareKeys*: each client secret-shares its self-seed b_u and DH private key with peers. (3) *MaskedInput*: client u sends y_u = x_u + PRG(b_u) + sum_{v: u<v} PRG(s_{u,v}) − sum_{v: u>v} PRG(s_{v,u}), where s_{u,v} is the pairwise seed and arithmetic is modulo a large R. (4) *Unmasking*: for survivors the server collects shares of b_u to strip self-masks; for dropouts it collects shares of DH keys to reconstruct and strip their pairwise masks. Summation cancels every pairwise mask exactly, leaving the sum of the surviving inputs.

**DP-SGD mechanics.** Abadi et al. [3] modify each SGD step: sample a batch with probability q, compute per-example gradients, clip each to l2 norm C so a single example's influence is bounded, then add Gaussian noise scaled to the clipping norm:

```python
def dp_sgd_step(params, batch, lr, clip_norm, noise_multiplier):
    per_ex = [grad(loss(params, x), params) for x in batch]
    clipped = [g * min(1.0, clip_norm / l2_norm(g)) for g in per_ex]
    noise = gaussian(0.0, (noise_multiplier * clip_norm) ** 2, shape=params.shape)
    private_grad = (sum(clipped) + noise) / len(batch)
    return params - lr * private_grad
```

Privacy is tracked by the *moments accountant*, which bounds the log moments of the privacy-loss random variable and converts them into a tight (epsilon, delta) guarantee under composition; Rényi DP [7] gives the same accounting a cleaner closed form, with the Gaussian mechanism satisfying (alpha, alpha/(2 sigma^2))-RDP.

```haskell
-- Rényi DP composes additively: k mechanisms, each (alpha, eps)-RDP,
-- yield (alpha, k*eps)-RDP, converted once to (eps', delta)-DP at the end.
gaussianRDP :: Double -> Double -> Double   -- order alpha, noise scale sigma
gaussianRDP alpha sigma = alpha / (2 * sigma ^ (2 :: Int))

rdpCompose :: Int -> Double -> Double       -- k mechanisms
rdpCompose k eps = fromIntegral k * eps
```

**Compression operators.** A compressor C reduces update size: *quantization* maps floats to few-bit codes (possibly randomized and unbiased), *sparsification* transmits only the top-k coordinates by magnitude, and *structured updates* constrain the update to a low-rank or sparse-support subspace during training itself [9].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Secure Aggregation: From Pairwise Masking to Polylogarithmic Protocols

The Bonawitz protocol's elegance is also its bottleneck: every client negotiates pairwise seeds with every other client, an O(n^2) key-exchange mesh per round, and dropout recovery costs server-side secret-share reconstructions linear in the cohort. For cohorts in the thousands this is practical — at n = 1024 the scheme inflates communication by only about 1.73x over plaintext — but it does not scale to the hundred-thousand-device cohorts FL aspires to [2].

Bell et al. [8] observed that the full mesh is overkill: masking over a sparse k-regular communication graph of logarithmic degree preserves the cancellation argument while cutting each client's peer set to O(log n). Per-client communication and computation drop from linear to polylogarithmic in n, and the protocol retains dropout resilience through subgroup reconstruction.

Two caveats deserve emphasis. First, secure aggregation is *input* privacy, not *output* privacy: the exact sum is revealed, and exact sums of high-dimensional updates still permit reconstruction attacks against individuals — which is why aggregation must be composed with differential privacy, not substituted for it. Second, the protocol authenticates nothing: a malicious client can submit an arbitrary vector, and the server cannot inspect it.

### 4.2 Differentially Private SGD: Mechanisms and Accounting

DP-SGD's power comes from a single analytic insight: clipping bounds *sensitivity*, and bounded sensitivity plus Gaussian noise yields privacy whose cost can be tracked tightly across thousands of steps. The moments accountant [3] replaced loose strong-composition bounds (which grow like epsilon times sqrt(T log(1/delta))) with a numerical bound exploiting *privacy amplification by sampling*: because each example participates with probability q per step, the per-step Rényi divergence shrinks roughly by q^2, a decisive gain at the small sampling rates of deep learning.

Rényi DP [7] streamlined the analysis into algebra: each noisy step is (alpha, alpha/(2 sigma^2))-RDP, composition is addition, and a final conversion yields (epsilon, delta)-DP. Federated learning changes the adjacency relation. In DP-FedAvg [12], the unit of protection is the *user*: each client's whole update is clipped to a global norm before aggregation, so the mechanism hides any single client's entire contribution. DP-FTRL [10] removes the sampling assumption altogether. In cross-device FL the server cannot guarantee uniform random sampling of clients — devices are available when they are available. DP-FTRL replaces sampling amplification with *tree aggregation*: noise is correlated across rounds through a binary tree over the update stream, so that any prefix sum carries bounded noise while the privacy analysis needs no randomness in client participation. It matches or beats amplified DP-SGD's utility without amplification, a crucial property for production FL.

### 4.3 Communication-Efficient Optimization

Communication efficiency in FL has two levers: *fewer rounds* and *fewer bits per round*.

Fewer rounds come from local computation. FedAvg's E local epochs already embody this, but naive local SGD drifts: on non-IID data, each client's local optimum differs, and averaging drifted models slows or breaks convergence. FedProx [11] adds a proximal penalty (mu/2) times ||w − w_t||^2 to each client's objective, tethering local optimization to the global model. SCAFFOLD [5] goes further with control variates: each client maintains c_i and the server c, correcting the local gradient by (c − c_i) so the update direction estimates the *global* gradient even when local data is arbitrarily heterogeneous.

Server-side adaptivity is the second algorithmic lever. FedAdam, FedYogi, and FedAdagrad [6] treat the aggregated client delta as a pseudo-gradient and apply adaptive optimizers at the server. Across EMNIST, CIFAR-100, Shakespeare, and Stack Overflow benchmarks, these methods consistently outperform FedAvg, particularly on sparse-gradient language tasks where per-coordinate adaptivity matters.

Fewer bits per round come from compression. Konečný et al. [9] introduced the dichotomy still in use: *structured updates* (train directly in a restricted subspace — low-rank or random-mask) versus *sketched updates* (train fully, then compress via quantization, random rotations, and subsampling). Their best configurations cut total upload by two orders of magnitude with only slight convergence degradation.

### 4.4 Convergence Theory Under Statistical Heterogeneity

The analytic core of federated optimization is controlling *client drift*. Let each F_k be L-smooth, stochastic gradients have variance bounded by sigma^2, and gradient dissimilarity bounded by sup over w of (1/K) sum over k of ||grad F_k(w) − grad F(w)||^2 <= zeta^2. Then FedAvg with E local steps and m participating clients per round attains, informally:

> **Theorem (FedAvg under heterogeneity, informal [4]).** Under smoothness, bounded variance, and bounded dissimilarity, FedAvg reaches an epsilon-stationary point in O(sigma^2/(m E epsilon^2) + zeta^2 E^2/epsilon^2 + 1/epsilon^2) gradient evaluations. The zeta^2 E^2 term is client drift: it grows with local epochs and heterogeneity, and vanishes only for IID data (zeta = 0).

The bound explains the practitioner folklore: raise E while data is IID and convergence accelerates; raise E under strong heterogeneity and the drift term dominates. SCAFFOLD [5] eliminates the zeta^2 E^2 term entirely through its variate correction, recovering the centralized rate up to constants — at the cost of transmitting control variates.

Partial participation adds a second variance source: sampling m of K clients injects O(1/m) variance independent of E.

### 4.5 The Privacy–Utility–Communication Trilemma

The three pillars interact, and the interactions are where real systems are won or lost.

*Secure aggregation enables distributed DP.* If clients add DP noise locally, the aggregate carries m independent noise draws — utility degrades with cohort size. If instead the *sum* is noised once, utility is cohort-independent. Secure aggregation makes the second option possible without trusting the server: clients submit masked updates, the server learns only the sum, and a single noise draw protects the aggregate [4][13]. This is the architecture behind production private FL.

*Compression meets noise.* Quantization and DP noise are both perturbations, and naive stacking double-pays. cpSGD [13] treats them jointly: binomial noise from randomized quantization itself contributes to the privacy budget, so fewer explicit noise draws are needed. Discrete variants — discrete Gaussian, Skellam noise — are designed to survive the finite-field arithmetic that secure aggregation requires, since continuous Gaussian noise cannot be represented modulo R.

The trilemma resolves into a design rule: spend the communication budget on cohort size (which buys both statistical efficiency and DP utility), spend the cryptographic budget on masking (which buys distributed DP), and spend the privacy budget once, on the aggregate — never per client.

##5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

> **Theorem 1 (Secure aggregation correctness [2]).** If all surviving clients follow the masking protocol and at least the reconstruction threshold remain online through unmasking, the server recovers the exact sum of the surviving inputs. Under the honest-but-curious model with a secure PRG and Diffie–Hellman key agreement, the server's view is computationally indistinguishable from the sum alone.

> **Theorem 2 (DP-SGD privacy [3]).** There exist constants such that DP-SGD with sampling probability q, noise multiplier sigma, and T steps satisfies (O(q epsilon_0 sqrt(T)), delta)-DP for the epsilon_0 of a single Gaussian step — and the moments accountant computes the exact constant numerically, yielding single-digit epsilon at thousands of steps for the sigma used in practice.

> **Theorem 3 (Rényi composition [7]).** If each of T mechanisms is (alpha, epsilon)-RDP, their composition is (alpha, T epsilon)-RDP, convertible to (T epsilon + log(1/delta)/(alpha − 1), delta)-DP for any delta strictly between zero and one. The Gaussian mechanism with noise scale sigma is (alpha, alpha/(2 sigma^2))-RDP for every alpha > 1.

Protocol comparison:

| Protocol | Rounds | Per-client cost | Dropout resilience | Server view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plaintext FedAvg [1] | 1 | O(d) | full | every update |
| Bonawitz et al. [2] | 4 | O(n + d) | threshold t | sum only |
| Bell et al. [8] | O(log n) groups | polylog(n) + O(d) | subgroup recovery | sum only |

Accounting comparison:

| Method | Composition law | Needs sampling | Tightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong composition | O(epsilon sqrt(T log(1/delta))) | no | loose |
| Moments accountant [3] | numerical | benefits | tight |
| Rényi DP [7] | additive in epsilon(alpha) | benefits | tight, closed form |

Optimizer comparison:

| Algorithm | Local correction | Drift-free rate | Server adaptivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg [1] | none | no | no |
| FedProx [11] | proximal term | no | no |
| SCAFFOLD [5] | control variates | yes | no |
| FedAdam [6] | none | no | yes |

Empirically, the literature reports a consistent picture. Adaptive federated optimizers outperform FedAvg across the standard FL benchmarks — EMNIST, CIFAR-100, Shakespeare, and Stack Overflow — with the largest gains on language tasks [6]. DP-FTRL achieves utility competitive with non-private training on Stack Overflow next-word prediction under formal user-level (epsilon, delta)-DP, without any sampling assumption [10]. DP-FedAvg demonstrated the first user-level-DP recurrent language model, establishing that per-user clipping plus aggregate noise is practical [12].

##6 Limitations

Each pillar carries failure modes the others cannot fix.

*Secure aggregation* guarantees confidentiality of inputs, not integrity: it is fully compatible with model poisoning, and a single malicious client can shift the sum arbitrarily while remaining hidden. its bandwidth is wasted.

*Differential privacy* taxes utility, and the tax is regressive: small cohorts, rare classes, and underrepresented clients suffer disproportionately, raising fairness concerns. Privacy accounting rests on idealized sampling; when participation is diurnal or adversarial, quoted epsilon values are optimistic.

*Communication efficiency* has not kept pace with model scale: the techniques above were validated on models of millions of parameters, while contemporary language models are three orders of magnitude larger, making even one full-model upload per client prohibitive. Compression interacts adversarially with heterogeneity — aggressive sparsification discards precisely the rare coordinates that minority clients need. And systems heterogeneity remains unsolved in theory: most convergence proofs assume bounded straggling, while real fleets exhibit heavy-tailed availability.

Finally, the composition of all three is under-theorized: we lack tight end-to-end guarantees for quantized, differentially private updates aggregated under masking with client dropout — the exact configuration production systems run.

##7 Conclusion

Federated learning's promise — training on the world's data without collecting it — rests on three interlocking mechanisms. Secure aggregation removes the server's view of individuals [2][8]; differentially private SGD bounds what the released model reveals, with accounting tight enough to be practical [3][7][10]; communication-efficient optimization — local steps, drift correction, adaptivity, compression — makes the whole enterprise affordable in bits [1][5][6][9][11]. Their deepest synergy is architectural: masking enables distributed differential privacy, where noise is paid once on the aggregate rather than once per client [13].

The field's trajectory — from FedAvg to SCAFFOLD to adaptive server optimizers, from pairwise masking to polylogarithmic graphs, from moments accounting to amplification-free DP-FTRL — suggests these will yield to the same strategy that created them: formalize the exact cost being paid, then design the mechanism that pays it once.
