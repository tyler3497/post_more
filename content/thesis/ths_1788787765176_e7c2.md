# Gravitational-Wave Data Analysis: Matched Filtering with Template Banks, χ² Signal Vetoes, Glitch Mitigation, and Bayesian Parameter Estimation via Nested Sampling

## Abstract

Extracting gravitational-wave signals of strain amplitude 10^-21 from the non-stationary, non-Gaussian noise of kilometer-scale interferometers is the central computational challenge of gravitational-wave astronomy. This thesis develops the complete analysis chain for compact binary coalescence signals. We derive the matched filter as the Neyman-Pearson optimal linear detector for stationary Gaussian noise and describe discrete template-bank construction via geometric metric placement with minimal match 0.97. We present Allen's chi-squared time-frequency discriminator, which partitions each template into frequency bands of equal expected signal-to-noise ratio and rejects glitches whose band-resolved power is inconsistent with a true chirp, together with the re-weighted detection statistic adopted by the PyCBC search. We survey glitch-mitigation methods — gating, data-quality vetoes, and Q-transform morphology — and the time-slide technique for empirical false-alarm-rate estimation. Finally, we formulate Bayesian parameter estimation through nested sampling, which jointly computes posterior distributions and the model evidence. A GW150914-style analysis validates the chain: network signal-to-noise ratio 24, component masses near 36 and 29 solar masses, and a false-alarm rate below one per two hundred thousand years.

## 1. Introduction

The direct detection of gravitational waves by Advanced LIGO in September 2015 inaugurated observational gravitational-wave astronomy [1]. The fundamental difficulty of the field is one of *extraction*: astrophysical strains of order 10^-21 must be recovered from interferometer data whose root-mean-square noise amplitude exceeds any plausible signal by many orders of magnitude, and whose statistics are neither stationary over long timescales nor Gaussian in their tails. Data analysis is therefore not an accessory to gravitational-wave astronomy; it is the final optical element of the instrument itself.

This thesis presents a unified treatment of the canonical analysis chain for **compact binary coalescence** (CBC) signals — the inspiral, merger, and ringdown of binary black holes and binary neutron stars. The chain rests on four pillars:

1. **Matched filtering with discrete template banks**, the optimal detection strategy for known waveforms in Gaussian noise, discretized over the astrophysical parameter space [4];
2. **The χ² signal-consistency veto and glitch mitigation**, which protect the search against the non-Gaussian transient artifacts that dominate real detector data [5];
3. **Empirical background estimation**, which measures detection significance by time slides rather than by analytic noise models [2];
4. **Bayesian parameter estimation via nested sampling**, which converts a confident detection into astrophysical knowledge — masses, spins, distance, and sky location — with full uncertainty quantification [3][6].

We emphasize the interplay among these stages. Detection statistics derived under Gaussian assumptions are made robust by vetoes that exploit the *broadband morphology* of true chirps; candidate significance is calibrated empirically against the measured glitch population; and astrophysical inference is performed with fully Bayesian machinery that propagates waveform and calibration uncertainty into the final posteriors. Each stage is developed from first principles, and the complete chain is validated against a GW150914-style analysis in Section 5.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The interferometer data stream

A ground-based interferometer records a strain time series

s(t) = h(t) + n(t),

where h(t) is the astrophysical gravitational-wave strain projected onto the detector and n(t) is instrumental noise. The noise is characterized by its one-sided power spectral density S_n(f), conventionally displayed as the amplitude spectral density √S_n(f). At design sensitivity, Advanced LIGO reaches ~10^-23 Hz^-1/2 near 100 Hz, limited at low frequencies by *seismic and Newtonian noise*, at mid frequencies by *coating and suspension thermal noise*, and at high frequencies by *photon shot noise*.

Two idealizations structure all subsequent analysis:

- **Stationarity over short segments.** Over minutes, the noise is approximately wide-sense stationary, so S_n(f) can be estimated from neighboring data (typically via median-mean Welch averaging).
- **Whitening.** Dividing the Fourier-domain data by √S_n(f) produces a whitened series whose idealized spectrum is flat; matched filtering is performed in this whitened domain.

Under the further idealization of *Gaussian* noise, the log-likelihood of the data given a waveform model h(θ) is

log L(d|θ) = -½ ⟨d − h(θ) | d − h(θ)⟩ + const,

with the noise-weighted inner product

⟨a|b⟩ = 4 Re ∫_0^∞ [ã(f) b̃*(f) / S_n(f)] df.

This single inner product is the workhorse of the entire field: it defines the matched filter, the χ² veto, and the Bayesian likelihood alike.

### 2.2 Compact binary waveforms

A quasi-circular binary black hole is described by **15 parameters**: eight intrinsic (component masses m_1, m_2; spin magnitudes a_1, a_2; spin tilt angles; two azimuthal spin angles) and seven extrinsic (luminosity distance, two sky angles, inclination, polarization angle, coalescence time t_c, coalescence phase). The waveform sweeps upward in frequency — the *chirp* — with instantaneous evolution

ḟ ∝ M_c^{5/3} f^{11/3},

where the chirp mass M_c = (m_1 m_2)^{3/5}/(m_1 + m_2)^{1/5} sets the leading-order phasing. Accurate templates combine post-Newtonian inspiral, numerical-relativity-tuned merger (e.g., IMRPhenom and SEOBNR families), and quasi-normal-mode ringdown. For neutron-star binaries, tidal deformability adds further intrinsic parameters.

### 2.3 The non-Gaussian reality

Real detector data contain *glitches*: short, loud, non-astrophysical transients of instrumental or environmental origin. Glitches produce large matched-filter outputs and would overwhelm any naive search — a single loud glitch can outrank a genuine signal in raw signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). The entire veto and background-estimation apparatus of Sections 3–4 exists because the Gaussian idealization fails precisely where it matters most: in the tails.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The matched filter

For a known template h(t) in stationary Gaussian noise, the detection problem is a binary hypothesis test: H_0 (noise only) versus H_1 (noise plus signal). The Neyman–Pearson lemma yields:

> **Theorem (matched-filter optimality).** For a known waveform in additive stationary Gaussian noise, the linear filter maximizing detection probability at fixed false-alarm probability correlates the data against the noise-weighted template. The optimal statistic is the signal-to-noise ratio ρ = ⟨s|h⟩ / √⟨h|h⟩ [4].

The squared optimal SNR accumulates as

ρ_opt² = ⟨h|h⟩ = 4 ∫_0^∞ [|h̃(f)|² / S_n(f)] df,

so detectability is set by the noise-weighted signal power. Because the coalescence time and phase are unknown, the filter is evaluated as a *time series* via fast Fourier transforms, maximizing efficiently over t_c and analytically over phase. The FINDCHIRP algorithm [4] implements this with careful PSD estimation — including *inverse-spectrum truncation*, which limits the time-domain corruption introduced by whitening — and is the direct ancestor of modern search pipelines.

```python
import numpy as np

def matched_filter_snr(strain_fd, template_fd, psd, df):
    # Noise-weighted correlation <s|h> evaluated at all arrival times
    corr_fd = 4.0 * np.conj(template_fd) * strain_fd / psd
    snr_series = np.fft.irfft(corr_fd) * df
    norm = np.sqrt(4.0 * np.sum(np.abs(template_fd)**2 / psd) * df)
    return snr_series / norm

rho = np.max(np.abs(matched_filter_snr(s_fd, h_fd, Sn, df)))
```

### 3.2 Template banks

The signal parameters are unknown, so the data are filtered against a **bank** of discrete templates spanning the astrophysical space. Bank construction uses the parameter-space metric

g_ij = -½ ∂²(match)/∂θ^i∂θ^j,

placing templates so that the worst-case fractional SNR loss — the *mismatch* — is bounded. The PyCBC search [2] adopts a **minimal match of 0.97**, guaranteeing that discretization costs no more than 3% of SNR, and covers circular binaries with aligned spins using on the order of 2.5×10^5 templates for a broad mass search. Stochastic placement algorithms achieve this density near-optimally, and a single time-averaged PSD is used to place one bank applied coherently to every detector in the network.

### 3.3 The χ² veto and re-weighted SNR

Each single-detector trigger with ρ above threshold (5.5 in PyCBC) is subjected to Allen's χ² time-frequency discriminator [5]. The template is partitioned into *p* frequency bands chosen so each band carries equal expected SNR, (u,u)_ℓ = 1/p, and the statistic

χ² = p Σ_{ℓ=1}^{p} |ρ_ℓ − ρ/p|²

measures whether the SNR accumulates across frequency as a true chirp demands. For Gaussian noise, χ² follows a chi-squared distribution with 2p−2 degrees of freedom; for a genuine signal it remains small, while a glitch — whose power concentrates in the wrong bands — yields an enormous value. The search then *re-weights* the SNR:

- ρ̂ = ρ, if χ²_r ≤ 1,
- ρ̂ = ρ / [(1 + (χ²_r)³)/2]^{1/6}, if χ²_r > 1,

with the reduced statistic χ²_r = χ²/(2p−2) [2]. Loud glitches are thereby demoted by orders of magnitude while true signals pass through essentially unscathed.

### 3.4 Coincidence and empirical significance

Triggers surviving single-detector vetoes are tested for *coincidence* across the network within a time window set by the light-travel time between sites plus calibration margin. The surviving candidates are ranked by a network detection statistic combining the re-weighted SNRs. Significance is measured **empirically**: the data streams are *time-shifted* relative to one another by offsets far exceeding any physical travel time, destroying true coincidences while preserving the glitch population, and the analysis is repeated to build a background distribution. The false-alarm rate (FAR) of a candidate is the rate at which background coincidences exceed its ranking statistic [2]. This procedure makes no Gaussian assumptions whatsoever — it measures the noise as it actually behaves.

| Stage | Operation | Key statistic |
|---|---|---|
| Conditioning | Downsampling, high-pass filtering, gating of loud transients | — |
| PSD estimation | Median-mean Welch spectrum, inverse-spectrum truncation | S_n(f) |
| Bank filtering | FFT matched filter over ~2.5×10^5 templates | ρ(t) |
| Triggering | Peak finding above ρ = 5.5 | single-detector triggers |
| Signal veto | χ² time-frequency consistency | χ²_r, ρ̂ |
| Coincidence | Multi-detector time coincidence | network ρ̂_c |
| Background | Unphysical time slides | FAR |

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The χ² discriminator in detail

Allen's construction [5] deserves close study because it is the single most effective glitch-rejection tool in CBC searches. Write the complex matched-filter output as ρ = (s,u) in the notation of the FINDCHIRP inner product. Partitioning the template into *p* bands gives per-band outputs ρ_ℓ with Σ_ℓ ρ_ℓ = ρ. For a signal exactly matching the template, each ρ_ℓ = ρ/p plus Gaussian fluctuations; for a glitch, the ρ_ℓ scatter wildly. The statistic χ² = Σ_ℓ |ρ_ℓ − ρ/p|²/(1/p) therefore has expectation 2p−2 under H_0 and stays near that value for true signals.

> **Remark (mismatch bound).** If the true signal differs from the template by mismatch δ = 1 − |(v,u)|, the χ² statistic acquires an offset bounded by 2ρ²δ. Template-bank discreteness therefore cannot veto loud real signals: with δ ≤ 0.03, even ρ = 30 gives χ² ≲ 54 against a threshold-scaled background [5].

In practice PyCBC augments this with a **sine-Gaussian χ²** test that tiles the time–frequency plane above the template's expected power, catching short-duration *blip* glitches whose excess power sits at frequencies the chirp never reaches. The combination of the two tests suppresses the single-detector glitch background by several orders of magnitude [2].

### 4.2 Glitch mitigation: gating, vetoes, and morphology

Before filtering even begins, the loudest transients are *gated*: the strain is smoothly windowed to zero around samples exceeding a threshold, preventing a single instrumental pop from ringing through the entire template bank via the whitening filter. *Data-quality vetoes* flag intervals of known instrumental misbehavior — e.g., photodiode saturations or seismic upsets — using thousands of auxiliary channels that witness the interferometer's state but are insensitive to gravitational waves.

Morphological classification complements these deterministic cuts. The **Q-transform** projects the data onto windowed sinusoids of quality factor Q = f/δf, producing time–frequency maps of normalized energy. A binary chirp traces a distinctive upward-sweeping track; glitches trace *blips* (symmetric teardrops), *scattered-light* arches, *whistles*, and *koi-fish* bursts. Human and machine classifiers (notably the Gravity Spy citizen-science project) label these morphologies, enabling targeted vetoes and informing the priors of unmodeled-burst searches.

| Glitch class | Time–frequency morphology | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Blip | Teardrop, ~0.1 s, broadband | χ² veto, sine-Gaussian χ², gating |
| Scattered light | Long arches at a few Hz | Auxiliary-channel vetoes |
| Whistle | Narrowband descending tone | Frequency notching |
| Koi fish | Broadband burst with fins | Data-quality vetoes |
| Power mains | Persistent 60 Hz harmonics | Line subtraction |

### 4.3 Background estimation and detection significance

The time-slide method converts the unknowable glitch distribution into a measured quantity. Shifting one detector's triggers by multiples of 0.1 s (far beyond the ~10 ms inter-site light-travel time) generates of order 10^7 independent background realizations from months of data. The loudest background coincidences set the scale: PyCBC demonstrated FAR measurements as low as **one per million years** with no additional computational cost over the foreground search [2]. Because background and foreground share identical vetoes, thresholds, and coincidence logic, the comparison is apples-to-apples — the method is robust precisely because it never models the glitches, only counts them.

A subtlety is the *trials factor*: searching hundreds of thousands of templates over months of data constitutes an enormous number of effective trials, which the time-slide background absorbs automatically. Reported significances (e.g., >5.1σ for GW150914) are thus statements about the empirical tail of the instrument's own behavior, not about a Gaussian idealization.

### 4.4 Nested sampling for Bayesian parameter estimation

Detection answers *whether*; parameter estimation answers *what*. Given data d, the posterior over source parameters θ is

p(θ|d) = L(d|θ) π(θ) / Z,

with the Gaussian-noise likelihood of Section 2 and astrophysically motivated priors π(θ) (uniform in component masses, isotropic in spin orientations and sky location, uniform in comoving volume for distance). The evidence

Z = ∫ L(d|θ) π(θ) dθ

normalizes the posterior and, crucially, enables *model selection* — e.g., weighing spinning versus non-spinning hypotheses.

**Nested sampling** [6] computes Z and the posterior simultaneously. Define the prior mass above likelihood threshold λ as X(λ) = ∫_{L(θ)>λ} π(θ) dθ; then Z = ∫_0^1 L(X) dX, a one-dimensional integral. The algorithm maintains *N* live points drawn from the prior, iteratively discards the lowest-likelihood point, and replaces it with a fresh prior sample constrained to L > L_min. The discarded points form nested shells of shrinking prior volume X_i ≈ e^{−i/N}, with weights w_i = X_{i−1} − X_i; accumulating Z += L_i w_i yields the evidence, while the weighted discarded points yield posterior samples as a by-product. The method's hard likelihood constraint — as opposed to the softened likelihoods of annealing — makes it robust to the multimodal, curving degeneracies (notably distance–inclination) characteristic of GW posteriors [6].

The **Bilby** library [3] packages this machinery for gravitational-wave astronomy, interfacing nested samplers (dynesty, cpnest, pymultinest) and MCMC samplers with production waveform approximants and detector calibration models. Its GW150914 reproduction is canonical:

```python
import bilby

ifos = bilby.gw.detector.get_event_data('GW150914')
prior = bilby.gw.prior.BBHPriorDict(filename='GW150914.prior')
likelihood = bilby.gw.likelihood.get_binary_black_hole_likelihood(ifos)
result = bilby.run_sampler(likelihood, prior, sampler='dynesty',
                           nlive=1000, outdir='gw150914_pe')
result.plot_corner()
```

A full 15-dimensional binary-black-hole analysis evaluates the likelihood of order 10^7 times; Bilby's marginalization over coalescence time, phase, and distance reduces the effective dimensionality and cost substantially [3].

## 5. Empirical Evaluation

We validate the chain against the first detection, GW150914 [1], as analyzed by the PyCBC search [2] and followed up with Bayesian parameter estimation [3].

| Quantity | Measured value |
|---|---|
| Network matched-filter SNR | 24 |
| Component masses | 36^{+5}_{−4} M⊙, 29^{+4}_{−4} M⊙ |
| Chirp mass (detector frame) | ≈ 28 M⊙ |
| Luminosity distance | 410^{+160}_{−180} Mpc (z ≈ 0.09) |
| Final black-hole mass / spin | 62^{+4}_{−4} M⊙, 0.67^{+0.05}_{−0.07} |
| Radiated energy | ≈ 3.0 M⊙c² |
| H1–L1 arrival delay | 6.9 ms |
| False-alarm rate | < 1 per 203,000 years (> 5.1σ) |

Several features confirm the methodology. First, the event's **χ²_r was near unity** in both detectors: the SNR accumulated across frequency bands exactly as a chirp demands, while the loudest background glitches in the time-slide analysis carried χ²_r values larger by orders of magnitude and were correspondingly down-weighted. Second, **subtracting the maximum-likelihood waveform left Gaussian residuals**, confirming that the signal model captured the coherent power. Third, independent re-analysis with fully spin-precessing waveforms [3] shifted the masses only modestly (35^{+5}_{−3} and 30^{+3}_{−4} M⊙, distance 440^{+160}_{−180} Mpc), demonstrating that systematic waveform uncertainty is subdominant to statistical uncertainty at this SNR — the expected regime when the detection statistic is well calibrated.

The search that found it [2] simultaneously demonstrated a **~30% increase in sensitive volume** for binary neutron stars over previous pipelines on archival data, attributable directly to the improved χ² handling and coincidence logic — a clean experimental isolation of the veto's value.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Waveform systematics.** Template banks and PE likelihoods assume general relativity and quasi-circularity. Eccentric, precessing, or beyond-GR signals suffer mismatch; the χ² bound of Section 4.1 quantifies the resulting veto risk. Numerical-relativity surrogate coverage remains sparse at high mass ratio.
2. **Calibration uncertainty.** Amplitude and phase calibration errors (percent-level in O1–O3) propagate directly into distance and sky-location posteriors; Bilby-class analyses now marginalize over calibration envelopes [3], but residual systematics persist.
3. **Non-stationary PSD estimation.** The matched filter assumes S_n(f) measured off-source describes the noise at the event time. Slow drifts are tracked by neighboring estimates, but rapid non-stationarity (e.g., during scattered-light episodes) violates the assumption and degrades both SNR and χ² calibration.
4. **Computational scaling.** A 15-dimensional nested-sampling analysis costs hours to days per event at O(10^7) likelihood evaluations. Next-generation detectors expect *daily* detections, motivating accelerated methods — reduced-order quadrature, relative binning, and neural posterior estimation — that trade exactness for speed.
5. **Overlapping signals.** In third-generation observatories, multiple in-band signals will routinely overlap, breaking the single-signal likelihood and the time-slide background model. Joint multi-signal inference remains an open research frontier.
6. **Glitch novelty.** Vetoes are tuned against *known* glitch morphologies; novel instrumental pathologies can and do evade them, which is why empirical background estimation — rather than any fixed veto — remains the ultimate arbiter of significance.

## 7. Conclusion

Gravitational-wave data analysis is a layered defense against an unforgiving noise environment. The matched filter provides the optimal linear statistic under Gaussian assumptions [4]; discrete template banks extend it across the astrophysical parameter space at bounded SNR cost [2]; the χ² discriminator and glitch-mitigation infrastructure restore near-Gaussian performance in the presence of real instrumental transients [5]; time-slide background estimation converts significance into an empirical measurement [2]; and nested sampling transforms detections into astrophysical posteriors and model evidences with full uncertainty accounting [3][6]. The GW150914 analysis — SNR 24, FAR below one per 203,000 years, masses and spins recovered to tens of percent — demonstrates the chain operating as designed. As detector sensitivity and event rates grow, the same four pillars will carry the field, augmented by faster inference and richer waveform models, from dozens of detections toward precision gravitational-wave astronomy.
