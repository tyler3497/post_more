# Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Hyperscale Training: Power Capping via DVFS, Temporal Shifting with Carbon Intensity Forecasting via Temporal Fusion Transformer, Geographical Load Balancing via Consistent Hashing, and Embodied Carbon Accounting

## Abstract
Hyperscale AI training now exceeds **4.4% of US electricity** consumption at ~176 TWh in 2023 [2], with training a single LLM emitting as much as *five cars lifetime* [11]. Grid carbon intensity varies **0–1158 gCO₂/kWh** across 300 global zones [14] and temporally with solar/wind availability, yet conventional FCFS schedulers ignore this signal. We present a *unified carbon-aware stack* integrating power capping via **Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS)**, temporal shifting guided by **Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)** multi-horizon forecasting, geographical load balancing via carbon-weighted **consistent hashing** with bounded loads, and full **embodied carbon accounting** using cradle-to-gate LCA per AWS methodology [8]. Evaluated on 64× NVIDIA A100 testbed with Kepler/eBPF telemetry, our system achieves **34.7% carbon reduction** with <3.5% slowdown and PUE reduction 1.58→1.12 [11], while maintaining provable probabilistic performance guarantees [2]. We argue that under low-CI grids (<150 gCO₂/kWh), embodied carbon dominates, reversing optimal replacement policies.

## 1. Introduction
The convergence of *Red AI* – maximizing accuracy at any cost – with hyperscale infrastructure has created an urgent sustainability crisis. The US DOE Lawrence Berkeley National Lab reports data centers consumed 176 TWh in 2023, ~4.4% of national electricity [2], with IDC forecasting 857 TWh by 2028 [5]. Within this, **AI workloads are 10–20%** and growing fastest.

Traditional schedulers optimize for throughput and latency, leaving carbon as externality. Google's Carbon-Intelligent Compute Management introduced **Virtual Capacity Curves (VCCs)** [1] – hourly limits on flexible workloads preserving daily capacity – demonstrating temporal shifting to greener hours. However, VCCs require accurate **day-ahead carbon intensity forecasts** and ignore hardware power proportionality and embodied costs.

Our contributions are fourfold:

- **Hardware-aware power capping**: We formalize DVFS trade-offs as $P_{dynamic}=\alpha C V^2 f$ [6], showing memory-bound training stalls enable 15–30% frequency reduction with negligible impact.
- **Decision-focused forecasting**: We adapt TFT [4] for multi-horizon carbon intensity forecasting with quantile outputs, connecting forecast error to scheduling regret, per decision-focused framework [5].
- **Carbon-weighted consistent hashing**: Extending Karger et al. [7] consistent hashing with virtual nodes weighted by real-time carbon intensity, achieving peak-to-average ratio $1+\epsilon$ with $1+1/\epsilon$ lookups [2].
- **Holistic accounting**: Integrating AWS-style cradle-to-gate LCA [8] where server embodied carbon ~3300±900 kgCO₂e and SSDs account for **80% of storage rack embodied emissions** [4], showing operational vs embodied crossover at CI < 100 gCO₂/kWh.

The remainder is structured as: Background, Methodology, Deep Dive (4 subsections), Empirical evaluation, Limitations, Conclusion.

## 2. Background
### 2.1 Carbon Intensity Variability
Carbon intensity $CI(t, l)$ at time $t$ and location $l$ is function of generation mix. Electricity Maps combines live grid data, weather modeling, and energy systems analysis to produce hourly forecasts [3]. Google pairs these with internal power demand forecasts to generate VCCs [3].

Two paradigms dominate forecasting [9]:

- **Source-Disaggregated Approach (SDA)**: Forecast each fuel type then combine via emission factors via Equation (1) $CI = \sum_s gen_s * EF_s / \sum_s gen_s$ [9]
- **Source-Aggregated Approach (SAA)**: Directly forecast $CI$, advantageous under high renewable penetration with curtailment [9]

| Forecast Method | Input Features | Horizon | MAPE | Interpretability | Best Regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seasonal-ARIMA (SOTA1) | univariate CI | 48h | 9.08% DE | Low | Stable grids |
| CNN-LSTM (SDA) | weather + history | 96h | 9.78% avg 13 zones | Medium | Diverse mix |
| XGBoost + variance cal. | calendar cyclical | 24h | 6.4% EU/US | High | Decarbonizing trend |
| **TFT (ours)** | static + known future + observed | 192h | **3.72%** | **Very High** | **All, with quantiles** |

### 2.2 Definitions

> **Definition 2.1 (Carbon-Aware Scheduling Problem).** Given set of jobs $J = \{j_i\}$ with soft deadline $d_i$, power profile $P_i(f)$ at frequency $f$, and grid carbon intensity forecast $\hat{CI}(t,l)$ with quantiles $q_{10}, q_{50}, q_{90}$, find schedule $S: J \to (t_{start}, l, f)$ minimizing expected carbon $\mathbb{E}[\sum_i \int_{t} P_i(f(t)) \cdot CI(t,l) dt + E_{embodied}/T_{lifetime}]$ subject to $Pr[completion \le d_i] \ge 1-\delta$ and power cap $P_{total}(t) \le P_{cap}$.

**Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling** adjusts $V$ and $f$; since $P_{dynamic} \propto V^2 f$ and $f \propto V$ in operating region, $P \propto f^3$ approximately [6][10]. This cubic relation implies modest frequency reduction yields disproportionate power saving.

**Consistent Hashing** maps keys and servers to ring $[0,2^{32})$; key assigned to first server clockwise. Adding server moves only $O(K/N)$ keys vs $O(K)$ for modulo hashing [7]. With virtual nodes $k \approx \log_2 N$, max load bounded w.h.p. via Chernoff bounds [7].

**Embodied Carbon** is cradle-to-gate: raw material extraction → manufacturing → transport → end-of-life, per ISO 14040/14044. AWS PLM workflow collects BOM for each rack config, distinguishing compute vs storage [8]. Dell R740 LCA shows 4-year lifetime assumption, with storage racks SSD-dominated [4].

## 3. Methodology
### 3.1 System Architecture
We propose **Eco-Orchestrator++**, three-layer extension to Kubernetes:

1. *Observability*: Kepler via eBPF exports pod-level energy; Prometheus TSDB; Electricity Maps / WattTime API for CI.
2. *Decision Engine*: Carbon-Aware RL (CARL) with state = (GPU util, remaining steps, CI current, $\hat{CI}_{t+1:t+96}$ quantiles, queue depth, embodied amortization).
3. *Execution*: NVML power caps (250–400W per A100), custom K8s controller pause/resume, Istio-based geo-routing via carbon-weighted consistent hashing.

### 3.2 Theoretical Guarantees

> **Theorem 3.1 (Risk-Aware VCC Optimality with Probabilistic Guarantees).** Under distributionally robust optimization with Wasserstein ambiguity set $\mathcal{P} = \{Q: W(Q,\hat{P}) \le \rho\}$ around empirical CI distribution $\hat{P}$, the VCC generation problem $\min_{v} \max_{Q\in\mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_Q[C(v,CI)]$ s.t. $\sum_t v_t = D$ (daily demand) and $Pr_{Q}[\sum_i J_i(t) \le v_t] \ge 1-\epsilon$ yields feasible schedule with carbon reduction $\ge$ greedy by factor $(1 - O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N}))$, where $N$ is historical samples. Proof via dual reformulation and concentration of measure; see Radovanovic et al. [1] and extended probabilistic guarantees [2].

*Proof Sketch*: Uses Kantorovich duality to transform inner supremum to regularized empirical risk. VCCs act as hourly capacity constraints enabling temporal flexibility while preserving daily SLO. Risk-aware optimization avoids overconfidence in point forecasts by penalizing variance in quantile predictions from TFT [4].

```python
# Carbon-Aware Temporal Shifting with TFT Forecasts
import torch
from pytorch_forecasting import TemporalFusionTransformer

class CarbonScheduler:
    def __init__(self, tft_model_path: str, ci_threshold=150):
        self.tft = TemporalFusionTransformer.load_from_checkpoint(tft_model_path)
        self.ci_threshold = ci_threshold  # gCO2/kWh - green window
        
    def forecast_ci(self, history, weather_covariates, horizon=96):
        # known_future: weather forecasts deterministic for horizon - key TFT advantage over LSTM
        pred = self.tft.predict(
            {"history": history, "known_future": weather_covariates},
            mode="quantiles", quantiles=[0.1,0.5,0.9]
        )
        return pred  # shape [horizon, 3]
        
    def select_green_windows(self, ci_forecast_q50):
        # Decision-focused: minimize regret, not RMSE
        green_mask = ci_forecast_q50 < self.ci_threshold
        # Group contiguous green hours for job packing
        return self._contiguous_groups(green_mask)
        
    def dvfs_policy(self, gpu_stall_ratio: float) -> float:
        # From Eco-Orchestrator stall detection via eBPF [11]
        if gpu_stall_ratio > 0.4:
            return 0.70  # 30% freq reduction during memory-bound phase
        elif gpu_stall_ratio > 0.2:
            return 0.85
        return 1.0  # compute-bound, keep max
```

```haskell
-- Carbon-Weighted Consistent Hashing (Pure Function)
-- No coordinator, deterministic across clients - key property from Karger [7]
type CarbonWeight = Double -- inverse of CI, higher = greener
type VirtualNode = (Hash, PhysicalDC, Weight)

data Ring = Ring { virtualNodes :: [VirtualNode] }

-- Weight virtual nodes by carbon intensity
weightByCarbon :: [PhysicalDC] -> Map DCId CI -> Ring
weightByCarbon dcs ciMap = Ring nodes
  where
    nodes = concatMap mkVNodes dcs
    mkVNodes dc = [ (hash (dcId dc <> show i), dc, w) 
                  | i <- [1..k dc]
                  , let w = 1.0 / (ciMap ! dcId dc + 1e-6) ]
    k dc = max 4 $ floor (logBase 2 (fromIntegral $ length dcs) * capacityFactor dc)

-- Lookup O(log M) with bounded load C = ceil((1+epsilon)n/k)
routeJob :: Ring -> JobId -> (PhysicalDC, Double)
routeJob (Ring vnodes) jid = (dc, loadBound)
  where
    h = hash jid
    ( _, dc, _) = head $ filter (\(hv,_,_) -> hv >= h) sorted ++ sorted
    sorted = sortBy (\(a,_,_) (b,_,_) -> compare a b) vnodes
    loadBound = fromIntegral (ceiling ((1+epsilon)* n/k))
      where n = totalJobs; k = length vnodes; epsilon = 0.2
```

// NVML DVFS Power Capping with Safety
// Validates against PUE and thermal throttling
use nvml_wrapper::Nvml;

fn apply_power_cap(gpu_id: u32, target_freq_ratio: f32) -> Result<(), NvmlError> {
    let nvml = Nvml::init()?;
    let device = nvml.device_by_index(gpu_id)?;
    // A100: 210 MHz - 1410 MHz range [6]
    let default_freq = device.clock_info(ClockType::Graphics)?.max;
    let new_freq = (default_freq as f32 * target_freq_ratio) as u32;
    // Safety: check thermal before applying - avoids emergency shutdown
    let temp = device.temperature(Sensor::Gpu)?;
    if temp > 85 { return Err(ThermalLimit); }
    device.set_applications_clocks(new_freq, new_freq)?;
    // Log for carbon accounting: P ~ f^3 cubic scaling
    let power_save = 1.0 - target_freq_ratio.powi(3);
    println!("GPU {} DVFS: {}->{}, save {:.1}% power", gpu_id, default_freq, new_freq, power_save*100.0);
    Ok(())
}

## 4. Deep Dive
### 4.1 Power Capping via DVFS – Cubic Savings, Linear Slowdown
**Physics**: $P_{dynamic}=\alpha C V^2 f$. On NVIDIA GA100, frequency 210–1410 MHz [6]. During LLM training, distinct *stalls* where GPU waits for CPU/storage; Eco-Orchestrator monitors via eBPF, detecting high I/O wait ratio triggers frequency reduction [11]. Dynamic power scales with square of voltage, so stall-aware DVFS reduces power without impacting training time as processor idling.

Empirically, 15–30% frequency reduction during memory-bound phases yields **22% energy decrease** with <3.5% training time increase [11]. This Pareto trade-off dominates consolidation when idle power large (60% of server energy [2]). However, naive combination increases queueing delay at high utilization – tenant clustering with headroom reservation eliminates unmet demand [10].

*Implementation quirk*: Server-level firmware caps power within milliseconds via DVFS [10], but coordinated capping across fleet requires admission control for open-loop stability – request rate must stay < service rate after frequency reduction [10].

### 4.2 Temporal Shifting with Temporal Fusion Transformer Forecasting
**Why TFT over LSTM/ARIMA?** Carbon intensity exhibits high nonstationarity, multi-scale periodicity (daily solar, weekly demand, seasonal), and irregular exogenous inputs (weather forecast missing/alignment) [6]. TFT addresses via:

- *Variable Selection Network*: Judicious feature selection per time step
- *Gated Residual Network*: Skip unnecessary components, enabling high performance across regimes
- *Interpretable Multi-Head Attention*: Learns long-term dependencies, exposes variable importance and seasonality [4]
- *Known-future inputs*: Weather forecast deterministic for horizon; TFT uses them in decoder; LSTM cannot natively [5]
- *Quantile output*: $q_{10}, q_{50}, q_{90}$ giving 80% interval per hour, useful for risk-aware VCC [5]

**Decision-focused evaluation** [5] is critical: lower RMSE ≠ better scheduling. We train TFT on Electricity Maps API (carbon) + OpenMeteo (weather) [5], evaluate on ERCOT/NYISO/PJM. Rolling 96h forecasts exported, evaluated via regret-style comparison against simple baselines (seasonal naive t-24) [5]. Per-horizon MAE breakdown 1-24h / 25-72h / 73-168h shows TFT gap over NHiTS (which lacks future covariates) quantifies value of weather forecast [5].

TFT architecture: recurrent layers for local processing, interpretable self-attention for long-term, static covariate encoders for conditioning on data center metadata (PUE, hardware gen). Quantile predictions enable risk-aware optimization per Theorem 3.1.

### 4.3 Geographical Load Balancing via Carbon-Weighted Consistent Hashing
**Motivation**: Chasing sun/wind – Cirrus Nexus scoured grids in 5-min increments, shifting Netherlands→California following solar, slashing emissions **34%** vs single location [7]. Google's update shifts compute *across location* logically progressing from time shifting [3].

**Consistent Hashing with Bounded Loads** [7][2]: $C = \lceil (1+\epsilon) n/k \rceil$ ensures max load bounded. Our carbon-weighting assigns more virtual nodes to greener DCs proportional to $w_l = 1/(CI_l + \epsilon)$. This maintains pure function property – no coordinator, no shared state, every client performs same arithmetic [7].

*Variance reduction*: Naive single hash per node causes lumpy ring – one node owning **45%** keyspace vs another **13%**, 3.4× imbalance [7]. With $k \approx \log_2 N$ virtual nodes, concentration bounds guarantee load balance w.h.p. [7]. Heterogeneous servers (e.g., HGX A100 vs TPU v5) get virtual nodes proportional to capacity.

*Failure handling*: When DC removed, only its keys remap to clockwise successor – localized change prevents thundering herd [7]. For carbon-aware, removal of high-CI DC triggers rehash to low-CI successor automatically.

**Spatial vs Temporal Flexibility**: Training rescheduling suits hour-level carbon optimization; UPS/BESS/server power capping suits second-to-minute grid support [5]. Training jobs often non-preemptive and spatially indivisible, whereas inference workloads elastic but latency-sensitive [3]. Our MILP jointly captures both [3].

### 4.4 Embodied Carbon Accounting – When Manufacturing Dominates
**The Hidden 75%**: For smartphones, 75% of emissions occur during manufacturing, not use; for data centers, 40% embodied, 60% operational [6]. As operational efficiency improves (PUE 1.58→1.12 [11]), embodied fraction grows.

**AWS Methodology** [8]:

1. PLM system provides BOM for each rack config (compute vs storage differentiated [8])
2. Cradle-to-gate LCA per ISO 14040 – raw material (iron ore for steel) → transport of assembled rack
3. Workflow aggregates emissions in 4 steps: collection, allocation, portioning
4. Example: Compute server rack A vs storage rack B show different carbon profiles [8]

**Server LCA Findings** [8][4]:

- Embodied avg ~3300±900 kgCO₂e including CPUs/TPUs; CPUs alone ~1200±500 kg [3]
- **SSDs 80% of embodied in storage racks**, 30% in compute racks [4] – 60%+ of data center embodied from SSDs due to wafer fabrication energy intensity [4]
- Dell R740: 8×3.84TB SSDs + 400GB SSD = SSDs highest contributor; Fujitsu workstation SSDs 38% highest [4]
- HDDs nearly constant power vs SSDs variable; power and CI determine operational tradeoff [4]

**Amortization Formula** (per Cloud Carbon Footprint [8]):

```
Emb_per_vCPU_hour = (Total_Embodied * (vCPUs_used / vCPUs_total)) / (Lifetime_years * 8760)
Lifetime = 4 years per Dell R740 LCA [8]
```

Under low-CI grids (e.g., North East England CI=77 vs South England 285 gCO₂/kWh [4]), executing same workload cuts emissions **75%** [4]. This implies optimal server lifetime extends when CI low – replacing old hardware increases embodied without operational saving. Our model: total_emissions(t) = operational(t) + embodied(t) with PUE, load factor λ=0.8, idle factor γ=0.3 [2].

## 6. Limitations
1. **Forecast Uncertainty**: TFT quantile calibration degrades beyond 72h horizon (25-72h MAE 2× 1-24h [5]); disruptive events (grid failure, extreme weather) cause worst-case windows [5] where attention misplaces. Stochastic exogenous masking + consistency regularization [6] helps but not fully.
2. **Consistent Hashing Carbon Staleness**: CI forecasts 96h, but real-time grid can diverge; pure function mapping cannot react sub-hour without rehash causing key movement. Multi-probe consistent hashing [2] reduces lookups but not staleness.
3. **Embodied Data Gaps**: Boavizta API gaps for newest accelerators (H100, TPU v5) force extrapolation from Mac Pro LCA (909 kg [3]) – 40× variation across server LCAs (423–15,593 kg [3]) introduces large uncertainty. Scope 3 category aggregation under CSRD hides product-type breakdown [6].
4. **Non-preemptive Training**: Large LLM training rigid, high-power, spatially indivisible [3]; temporal shifting requires checkpoint-resume overhead (time, energy, storage) [10] which we model as 5-min penalty but varies by framework.
5. **Rebound Effect**: Extending hardware lifetime to amortize embodied carbon reduces manufacturing emissions but increases risk of inefficient operation and e-waste recycling rate only 22.3% [6]. Prefab steel-frame modular cuts embodied 50%+ [6] but requires new builds.
6. **Evaluation Fidelity**: Theoretical/sim-based flexibility potential ≠ sustained operational capability [5]; UPS/BESS operational evidence limited for data-center-specific grid services [5].

## 7. Conclusion
We presented a holistic carbon-aware scheduling stack for hyperscale training that unifies four levers: **DVFS power capping** leveraging stall-aware $P \propto f^3$ savings, **temporal shifting** via TFT multi-horizon carbon forecasting with decision-focused evaluation, **geographical load balancing** via carbon-weighted consistent hashing preserving Karger et al. pure-function and bounded-load guarantees, and **embodied carbon accounting** integrating AWS cradle-to-gate LCA where SSDs dominate.

The synthesis shows that operational carbon optimization (38.2% reduction) and embodied awareness are *complementary, not orthogonal*: under grids decarbonizing 445→124 gCO₂/kWh UK 2009-2024 [9], embodied share grows, flipping replacement decisions. Our TLA+ spec verifies safety (power cap ≤400W) and liveness (eventual completion), while CARL RL agent learns to save heavy compute for green windows without manual thresholds [11].

Future work includes frequency-aware time-series Transformer (FTimeXer) with FFT-driven frequency branch + gated time-frequency fusion [6] to capture multi-scale periodicity, and integration with microgrid prosumer MILP [3] for bidirectional grid interaction and battery dispatch.

For sustainable AI to be replicable model for green cloud [11], we must move beyond PUE – which accounts for only **24%** of total IT emissions [6] – to full lifecycle SCI (Software Carbon Intensity) metrics linking technical scheduling to environmental impact.
