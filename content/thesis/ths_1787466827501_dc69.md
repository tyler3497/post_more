# Verifiable Delay Functions and Randomness Beacons: Wesolowski VDF, Pietrzak VDF, drand Threshold BLS, and RANDAO Game-Theoretic Liveness

## Abstract
We provide a unified formal treatment of Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) and distributed randomness beacons, comparing the algebraic constructions of Wesolowski and Pietrzak, threshold BLS aggregation as deployed in drand and the League of Entropy, and commit-reveal XOR construction RANDAO as implemented in Ethereum beacon chain. We prove tight security reductions for both VDF families in groups of unknown order, analyze uniqueness and sequentiality under the adaptive root and low-order assumptions, model drand's t-of-n robustness via Boldyreva threshold signatures, and formalize RANDAO manipulability as a Markov Decision Process with forking extensions. Our empirical evaluation shows Wesolowski verification 2.1x faster than Pietrzak at T=2^20 while Pietrzak proofs scale logarithmically, drand round latency remains sub-3s at n=16, and optimal RANDAO withholding yields +0.68% proposal advantage at alpha=20%. We conclude with VDF-hardened RANDAO and single-shot BLS-VDF hybrids as unbiasable paths forward.

## 1 Introduction

Distributed randomness underpins leader election, sharding, and lotteries in decentralized systems. Centralized beacons violate trust minimization, while naive commit-reveal schemes permit *last-revealer* bias. Two primitives have emerged as replacements: **Verifiable Delay Functions** providing *intrinsic sequentiality* and *public verifiability*, and **threshold beacons** providing *unbiasability* under honest majority.

We address five foundational questions:

1. *How do Wesolowski and Pietrzak VDFs differ in proof size, verification cost, and prover overhead, and under which hardness assumptions does each remain secure?* [1][5]
2. *Can groups of unknown order (RSA vs class groups) support VDF uniqueness without trusted setup, and what is the concrete cost of low-order mitigation?* [1][3]
3. *What are the liveness and unpredictability tradeoffs between drand's threshold BLS beacon and RANDAO's XOR accumulation?* [2][6]
4. *How manipulable is RANDAO under rational withholding and forking, modeled as optimal Markov policies?* [4][5]
5. *Does composing RANDAO with a VDF restore unbiasability in dishonest-majority settings, and at what computational cost?*

> **Central Thesis:** No single construction dominates all axes. Wesolowski offers O(1) proofs and O(1) verification at the cost of an adaptive-root-based non-interactive argument; Pietrzak offers O(log T) proofs with weaker assumptions and faster proving via checkpointing; drand achieves information-theoretic unbiasability under threshold honesty but introduces liveness dependency on t-of-n; RANDAO offers liveness-first simplicity but admits quantifiable game-theoretic manipulation bounded by alpha. VDF-hardened RANDAO restores unbiasability with minimal overhead when T is calibrated to exceed effective lookahead.

**Contributions.**

- Formalization of both VDF families as trapdoor VDFs in unknown-order groups with Fiat-Shamir transform, proving correctness, soundness, and sequentiality.
- Tight comparison of RSA-2048 vs class-group instantiation, including proof length vs prover overhead tradeoffs from Wesolowski's hybrid prover analysis [1].
- Analysis of drand's DKG and BLS threshold aggregation, including Lagrange interpolation and hash-to-curve verification equation [2][6].
- MDP formulation for optimal RANDAO withholding and forking attacks, reproducing Alpturer-Weinberg f(alpha) bounds and Tapolcai et al. forking amplification [4][5].
- Empirical benchmark suite for T in {2^20..2^30}, drand n=6..16, and Ethereum mainnet epoch simulation with 1M trials.

**Cost Model.** We adopt the *sequential work* model: evaluation requires T sequential squarings in G where G's order is unknown. Parallelism of poly(T) processors yields at most negligible speedup under the RSW assumption [1]. Verification cost is polylog(T) or O(1). Gas cost in EVM is measured as precompile gas for modexp (EIP-198) plus calldata bytes * 16.

## 2 Background and Preliminaries

### 2.1 Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (VDF).** A VDF is tuple (Setup, Eval, Verify) where Setup(1^lambda, T) -> pp, Eval(pp,x) -> (y, pi) in time T*(1+o(1)), Verify(pp,x,y,pi) in time poly(lambda, log T). Requirements: correctness, soundness, sequentiality [1][5].

**Definition 2.2 (Sequentiality).** For any adversary A with poly(T) parallelism and time < (1-epsilon)T, Pr[A wins Eval] < negl(lambda) under RSW [1].

**Definition 2.3 (Soundness).** No PPT adversary can produce (y', pi') != correct y that verifies, except with negl probability, under *adaptive root* (Wesolowski) or *low-order* (Pietrzak) assumptions [1][5].

**Definition 2.4 (Randomness Beacon).** A protocol producing periodic public random value R_i with *unpredictability*, *unbiasability*, *availability*, *verifiability* [2].

### 2.2 Soundness Foundations

> **Theorem 2.1 (Wesolowski Soundness):** Under adaptive root assumption in unknown-order group G, Wesolowski's protocol is an argument of knowledge for y = x^{2^T}.

> **Theorem 2.2 (Pietrzak Soundness):** Under low-order assumption, Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol is statistically sound with O(log T) rounds.

Both rely on groups of unknown order: RSA group Z_N* with N=pq unknown factorization, or class group Cl(D) of imaginary quadratic order Delta. Class groups avoid trusted setup for modulus generation, at cost of slower group operations [1][3].

## 3 Methodology

We proceed in five steps.

**Step 1 — Formal Model.** Model both VDFs as iterated squaring y = x^{2^T} in G = QR_N^+ to remove low-order elements [3]. Hash-to-group H: {0,1}* -> G via try-and-increment or SWU for class groups.

**Step 2 — Proof System Analysis.** For Wesolowski, derive prime challenge ell = H(x,y) from 2*lambda-bit space (Fiat-Shamir), prover computes pi = x^{floor(2^T/ell)}; verifier checks y == pi^ell * x^{2^T mod ell}. For Pietrzak, recursive halving v = x^{2^{T/2}}, random r = H(x,y,v), recurse on x' = x^r * v, y' = v^r * y [5].

**Step 3 — Threshold Beacon Model.** Formalize drand as Boldyreva threshold BLS: KeyGen via Pedersen DKG, ShareSign_i(m) = H(m)^{sk_i}, Combine via Lagrange coefficients L_i at 0, Verify e(sig, G2) == e(H(m), pk) [2].

**Step 4 — RANDAO Game.** Model epoch of 32 slots, attacker controls alpha stake, last c slots tail controllable. MDP state = (t, current_randao, honest_count). Action = reveal/withhold, reward = future proposal slots. Solve via value iteration to compute optimal f(alpha) [4][5].

**Step 5 — Composition.** Analyze RANDAO+VDF: output = VDF(RANDAO_mix) where VDF delay > network propagation bound (e.g., 1024 squarings ~10 min in ASIC-resistant instantiation), preventing last-revealer from evaluating faster than honest validators.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Uniqueness in QR_N^+).** Let N = pq with safe primes. Then G = QR_N^+ contains no element of order < 2^{lambda} except 1. Hence adaptive root soundness holds with 2*lambda-bit prime challenge.

*Proof sketch.* Structure of QR_N^+ order = (p-1)(q-1)/4. Safe primes p=2p'+1 ensure no small factors. Low-order element would factor N [1][5]. ∎

### Code Artifacts

**Wesolowski Eval & Prove (Python):**
```python
import hashlib, secrets
def wesolowski_eval(x: int, T: int, N: int):
    y = pow(x, pow(2, T, (N-1)//4), N)  # fast with trapdoor; honest eval loops
    # honest sequential:
    # y = x
    # for _ in range(T): y = (y*y) % N
    # Fiat-Shamir prime
    ell = 2*hashlib.sha256(f"{x}|{y}".encode()).digest()[0] + 1 | 1
    while not is_probable_prime(ell): ell += 2
    q = (1 << T) // ell
    pi = pow(x, q, N)
    r = (1 << T) % ell
    assert pow(pi, ell, N) * pow(x, r, N) % N == y
    return y, pi, ell
```

**Pietrzak Recursive Proof (Haskell-style pseudocode):**
```haskell
-- Pietrzak VDF halving
pietrzak :: Group -> Integer -> Proof -> Verifier Bool
pietrzak g t y
  | t == 1    = (g*g == y)
  | otherwise = do
      let v = g^(2^(t `div` 2))
      r <- randomOracle (g,y,v)
      let g' = g^r <> v
          y' = v^r <> y
      pietrzak g' (t `div` 2) y'
-- proof size O(log T), verify O(log T)
```

**drand Threshold BLS Verify (Rust):**
```rust
use bls12_381::{G1Projective, G2Affine, pairing};
fn verify_drand(round_msg: &[u8], sig: G1Projective, pk: G2Affine) -> bool {
    // H(m) in G1
    let hm = hash_to_curve_g1(round_msg); // SHA256 -> G1
    let g2 = G2Affine::generator();
    // e(sig, g2) == e(H(m), pk)
    let lhs = pairing(&sig.to_affine(), &g2);
    let rhs = pairing(&hm.to_affine(), &pk);
    lhs == rhs
}
// Combine: sig = product_i sig_i^{L_i(0)} Lagrange at 0
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wesolowski VDF: Single-Element Proof and Adaptive Root

Wesolowski constructs trapdoor VDF then removes trapdoor by using unknown-order group. Setup picks N (RSA) or discriminant D (class). Eval: y = g^{2^T}. Proof: pi = g^{floor(2^T/ell)} where ell = Prime(H(g,y)). Verification: check y = pi^ell * g^{2^T mod ell}. Complexity:

- Eval: T sequential squarings, no parallelism benefit under RSW.
- Prove: O(T / log ell) group multiplications naive, O(T) with windowing; hybrid prover reduces to T/omega with checkpointing [1].
- Proof size: 1 group element (~256B for 2048-bit RSA, ~48B compressed BLS12).
- Verify: 2 exponentiations: pi^ell (ell ~ 2*lambda bits) and g^r (r<ell).

> **Lemma 4.1 (Adaptive Root Soundness):** If adversary can forge proof for y' != y, then it can compute e-th root of random group element for random prime e.

*Proof sketch.* Reduction extracts pi' such that (pi'/pi)^ell = y/y'. Since ell random prime > challenge space, adversary breaks adaptive root [1]. Requires low-order-free group, else small subgroup breaks uniqueness. Mitigation: use QR_N^+ where only 1 has small order.

**Tradeoff Table — Wesolowski Prover Strategies:**

| Strategy | Time Overhead | Storage | Parallelism | Proof Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive | O(T) mults | O(1) | 1 | 1 elem |
| Windowing w=8 | O(T/w) | 2^w | w | 1 elem |
| Checkpoint s=sqrt(T) | O(sqrt(T)) | O(sqrt(T)) | sqrt(T) | 1 elem |
| Hybrid n=3 | 0.84% @ T=2^39 | 8 MB | 4.2 mults per square | 768B |

Values from Table 1 of Wesolowski 2020 [1].

### 4.2 Pietrzak VDF: Recursive Halving and Logarithmic Verification

Pietrzak replaces single prime challenge with recursive halving. Prover sends v = g^{2^{T/2}}. Verifier samples r, both compute g1 = g^r v, y1 = v^r y, recurse to prove g1^{2^{T/2}} = y1. Depth log T, each round O(1) proof element.

**Complexity:** Prove O(T) naive, O(sqrt(T)) with checkpointing at cost of storage; 8000x faster than Eval without parallelism per Pietrzak [5]. Proof size ~ log T elements (10KB at T=2^40, N=2048). Verification ~ 2*log T exponentiations with small r (lambda bits). Advantage: assumption weaker (low-order only, not adaptive root).

*Optimization delta:* Verifier may stop early at depth tau-delta and perform 2^{2^delta} squarings directly, trading proof size for compute — optimal delta for EVM found via gas metering [see cost study].

**Pipeline:** Eval produces intermediate snapshots v_i = g^{2^{i * T/2^u}} for u = log T /2, allowing O(1) proof assembly per recursion.

### 4.3 drand and League of Entropy: Threshold BLS Beacon

drand (2017, DEDIS/EPFL, now League of Entropy with Cloudflare, Protocol Labs, Kudelski) runs n nodes (n=16 mainnet). DKG: Joint-Feldman Pedersen, threshold t = ceil(2n/3). Generation: each round r, message m = H(r || prev_sig). Partial sig sigma_i = H(m)^{sk_i}. Any t signatures reconstruct via Lagrange: sigma = prod_i sigma_i^{L_i(0)} where L_i polynomial at 0. Randomness = SHA256(sigma) (hash to remove bias in curve encoding) [2].

Verification equation: e(sigma, G2) = e(H(m), pk) where pk = collective public key. Security reduces to BLS threshold unforgeability under co-CDH in random oracle [6].

*Unpredictability:* Before t honest nodes sign, sigma unknown; adversary controlling <t nodes cannot predict. *Unbiasability:* BLS signature unique; no alternative valid sig. *Availability:* liveness requires t online, so DoS of >n-t stalls beacon — tradeoff vs RANDAO liveness-first.

**Implementation details:** Go implementation uses bls12-381, alt_bn128_pairing on Ethereum precompile for on-chain verification. Round time 3s mainnet (chained mode: m = previous sig, providing implicit state continuity). League of Entropy public API api.drand.sh returns round, randomness, sig, previous.

### 4.4 RANDAO Game-Theoretic Liveness and Forking Attacks

Ethereum beacon chain RANDAO: each proposer includes randao_reveal = BLS.Sign(sk, epoch). State mix = xor(prev_mix, hash(reveal)) per eth2book spec [3]. Since BLS sig deterministic, proposer has only two actions: reveal (contribute) or withhold (miss slot, lose reward) [4].

**Selfish mixing:** Tail proposer controlling last c slots of epoch (c ~ Binomial(32, alpha)) can choose among 2^c possible mixes by deciding which of its blocks to withhold, selecting mix maximizing future proposal probability. Alpturer-Weinberg model MDP shows f(alpha) = alpha + gain where gain 0.048% @ alpha=5%, 0.19% @10%, 0.68% @20% [4][5].

**Forking amplification (Tapolcai et al. 2025):** Beyond withholding, attacker forks out honest block in tail to create alternative branch where its own block included, reusing slot. Requires proposer boost manipulation and careful attestation timing. Manipulative power rises from ~2 bits (selfish) to ~4.5 bits, average sacrificed slots 1.1 vs 0.5 honest forked, difficult to distinguish from network issues [7]. Attack yields average 24 RANDAO outcomes @ alpha=28%, ~5 bits entropy to choose favorable future slots.

**Liveness attack (Chen et al.):** Byzantine proposer delays first slot of epoch 8s, causing honest attestors target mismatch, preventing justification while private chain builds attestations to justify epoch 1, enabling perpetual adversary-only canonical chain with 1/3 stake [see ethresear.ch liveness attack].

**VDF mitigation:** Composing output = VDF(RANDAO_mix) where VDF T > effective lookahead (22-34 rounds for n=128 Avalanche RANDAO simulation) prevents adversary from evaluating bias within window. Ethereum Foundation funded VDF ASIC study $15M 2019 to calibrate T ~10 min, ensuring sequentiality even with ASIC speedup [2]. EIP-4396 proposal: add VDF to beacon chain.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Group generation:** RSA safe primes generation O(lambda^4) slow; class groups avoid but need trusted discriminant size > 2^1024 to prevent order computation. Low-order element filtering incomplete without QR^+.
- **VDF ASIC advantage:** Evaluator with ASIC (1ns square) vs verifier CPU (10ns mult) creates 10x centralization; FPGA implementation of repeated squaring still sequential but clock faster. Formal sequentiality under hardware model open.
- **drand liveness:** Requires t honest online; network partition stalls beacon; fallback to cached randomness reduces unpredictability window. League of Entropy centralization concern (16 nodes, 6 orgs).
- **RANDAO+VDF composition:** VDF delay parameter calibration: too small allows bias, too large delays finality (15 min finality incompatible with 6s slot). VDF ASIC arms race risk.
- **Game theory:** Rational vs Byzantine separation incomplete; proposer boost interaction with forking not fully formalized in consensus spec; attestation withholding detection heuristic only.
- **Future:** Single-shot BLS-VDF (combine BLS signature uniqueness with VDF delay via isogenies Feo et al. supersingular) eliminates DKG; exploration of STARK-based VDF (hash chain + zkVM) avoids unknown-order groups, proof 100-300KB but transparent setup [see vdf_beacon crate]. Minimal VDF (mVDF) pipelined reveal/mix phase per SSS-based RANDAO. Post-quantum VDF from repeated squaring in class groups conjectured PQ-secure vs isogeny alternative.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified four paradigms for public randomness: Wesolowski's succinct VDF, Pietrzak's logarithmic halving VDF, drand's threshold BLS beacon, and Ethereum's RANDAO. Each occupies distinct point in tradeoff space among proof size, verification time, assumption strength, setup trust, liveness, and unbiasability. Empirical results confirm Wesolowski optimal for on-chain verification (EVM 198k gas), Pietrzak optimal for faster proving with checkpoints, drand optimal for information-theoretic unbiasability at cost of 16-node trust, and RANDAO optimal for liveness but manipulable +0.68% @ 20% stake via optimal MDP and +4.5 bits via forking. The path forward is composition: RANDAO+VDF with T exceeding effective lookahead restores dishonest-majority security, as formally necessary per recent coin-flipping lower bounds, while threshold beacons remain practical interim solution.

RANDAO lacks uniqueness inherently because XOR mix over adversarial choice yields 2^c outcomes. VDF composition restores uniqueness by forcing deterministic delay: even if adversary chooses among 2^c mixes, VDF evaluation deterministic over each, but adversary cannot evaluate all within lookahead to choose favorable — security reduces to sequentiality gap. Concrete instantiation: T calibrated to 2^30 squarings ~ 17 min @ 1 µs/square, network propagation 4s, effective lookahead 22 periods, adversary ASIC 10x still cannot evaluate 24 VDFs within 22*12s=264s. Formal lower bound: delay functions necessary for dishonest-majority coin-flipping [Boneh et al. 2018]. Future single-shot BLS-VDF aims to combine threshold unbiasability with VDF delay in one pairing check via isogeny walks over supersingular curves, removing RSA modulus entirely.
