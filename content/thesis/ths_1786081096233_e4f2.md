# Private Information Retrieval via Lattice Homomorphic Encryption: From SealPIR Compressed Queries to Spiral FHE Composition, OnionPIR Response Minimization, Cuckoo Hashing Batching, and Sublinear Communication

## Abstract
We analyze the evolution of single-server computational Private Information Retrieval (PIR) built on lattice-based somewhat and fully homomorphic encryption. Starting from Kushilevitz-Ostrovsky foundations, we trace the lineage through XPIR ciphertext compression [5], SealPIR dimension-wise expansion and selection vectors [1], MulPIR and OnionPIR layered encryption composition [3], and Spiral's Regev-to-GSW ciphertext translation regime [2]. We formalize cPIR correctness and privacy under Ring Learning With Errors, derive asymptotic communication $O(d \cdot \sqrt[d]{N})$ bounds for $d$-dimensional databases, and contrast amortized versus stateful models. A dedicated treatment of batch PIR via reverse Cuckoo hashing with $k$-ary eviction establishes load-balancing guarantees and oblivious allocation. We present empirical throughput estimates calibrated against SealPIR $105$ MB/s and SpiralStreamPack $1.5$ GB/s reports [4][6], prove privacy preservation under RLWE distinguishing, and discuss rate versus noise trade-offs, modulus switching, and expansion factor $F$ blowup. Limitations including malicious security, hint size, and quantum transition are enumerated.

## 1 Intro
Single-server PIR captures the canonical tension between *privacy* and *efficiency* in outsourced data access. A client wishes to retrieve record $\mathsf{DB}[i^*]$ from a public $N$-record database replicated on an untrusted server without leaking $i^*$. The trivial solution of downloading the entire database achieves perfect privacy but linear communication; computationally private PIR achieves *sublinear* communication under cryptographic assumptions.

> Theorem: Under the (decision) RLWE assumption with polynomial degree $N=2^{12}$ and modulus $q \approx 2^{50+}$, there exists a single-server cPIR protocol with communication $O(\sqrt{N})$ and server computation $O(N)$ scalar multiplications.

Early constructions due to Kushilevitz and Ostrovsky [KO97] used additively homomorphic encryption over quadratic residuosity. Their $d$-dimensional hypercube encoding yields communication $O(N^{1/d})$ ciphertexts but expansion factor $F^{d-1}$ blowup where $F$ is ciphertext-to-plaintext size ratio. Lattice-based additively and somewhat homomorphic encryption unlocked **compress-then-expand** paradigms: XPIR demonstrated query compression by encrypting index bits into BFV ciphertexts, SealPIR instantiated FV with modulus-switching and ciphertext compression hints [1], and MulPIR extended to multiplicative homomorphism with relinearization.

The research trajectory culminates in two orthogonal advances:

* **FHE composition:** Spiral [2] composes low-rate Regev matrix encryptions (small query) with high-rate GSW Regev-style encodings for response packing, plus blaze-fast Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) polynomial arithmetic.
* **Response optimization:** OnionPIR [3] layers several BGV/BFV ciphertext moduli in *onion* fashion, where outer encryption wraps inner ciphertexts, shedding noise and shrinking final response to $4.2\times$ baseline versus SealPIR $100\times$.

In parallel, *batch PIR* protocols convert $k$ simultaneous queries into load-balanced bucket queries using **Cuckoo hashing** and **probabilistic batch codes** [6]. The inverse allocation—client as simulated server—ensures oblivious distribution while server replicates each entry into $d$ candidate positions, yielding statistical $1/N$ failure.

We contribute a unified synthesis: precise definitions, security reductions to $\mathbf{RLWE}_{n,q,\chi}$, hypercube indexing math, FV/BGV noise analysis, translation circuits, failure probability proofs for Cuckoo, and throughput modelling.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Computational PIR Definitions

Let $\lambda$ be security parameter, $\mathsf{DB} \in \{0,1\}^{N \times \ell}$. A cPIR scheme is tuple $(\mathsf{Query},\mathsf{Answer},\mathsf{Extract})$.

- **Correctness:** $\Pr[\mathsf{Extract}(\mathsf{Answer}(\mathsf{DB}, q), sk)= \mathsf{DB}[i^*]] \ge 1-\mathsf{negl}(\lambda)$.
- **Privacy:** For any PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}$ controlling server, $|\Pr[\mathcal{A}(q(i_0))=1]-\Pr[\mathcal{A}(q(i_1))=1]| \le \mathsf{negl}(\lambda)$.

We work in *honest-but-curious* model; extensions to malicious via verifiable PIR are out of scope.

### 2.2 Lattice Preliminaries: RLWE, BFV, BGV, GSW

*Ring Learning With Errors* [4] defines $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^N+1)$. Sample $a \leftarrow R_q$, $s,e \leftarrow \chi$. Distinguishing $(a, a s + e)$ from uniform is hard. BFV encrypts $m \in R_t$ as $(c_0 = -a s + e + \Delta m, c_1=a)$ with $\Delta = \lfloor q/t \rfloor$.

**Noise growth:** Addition: $\|e_1+e_2\|$, Multiplication: fractional decomposition and relinearization introduces $\approx N \cdot B$ expansion. Modulus switching $q \to q'$ reduces noise by $q'/q$ at cost of depth.

GSW encrypts bits as matrix $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{R} + m \mathbf{G}$, enabling *asymmetric* multiplication where one operand can be large plaintext.

> Theorem: BFV with $N=8192$, $\log q=180$, $t=2^{20}$ supports depth-4 exact evaluations at $128$-bit security.

### 2.3 SealPIR Architecture

SealPIR [1] packs database as $d=2$ dimensions: $\mathsf{DB}$ shaped $m_0 \times m_1$ pages. Client sends $d$ compressed query ciphertexts encrypting unit vector $e_{i_0}$ folded via *selection vector* $s \in \{0,1\}^{m}$. Server **expands** via oblivious substitution (multiply by $x^{2^k}$ and automorph) producing $m$ ciphertexts encoding each component of $s$.

Process:

1. Compress: Encrypt polynomials encoding $i$ in base-$B$.
2. Expand: $\mathsf{Expand}(ct) \to \{ct_j\}$ such that $ct_j$ decrypts to $1$ iff $j=i_0$.
3. First dimension multiply: $c'_{k} = \sum_j \mathsf{DB}[j,k] \cdot ct_j$.
4. Second dimension folding via plaintext multiplication, final modulus switch, reply.

Communication: $|q|=O(\sqrt N)$ for $d=2$, $|resp|=F^{d-1}\cdot \ell$ ciphertexts. SealPIR optimal $d=2$ for large $\ell$, deeper $d$ reduces query but explodes response.

### 2.4 Spiral Family and Ciphertext Translation

Spiral [2] introduces two embeddings:

- **Regev-style query:** small, $14$ KB for $2^{20}$ entries.
- **GSW streaming:** high-rate packing: $p$ scalar Regev become matrix Regev, then GSW with rate $>0.8$.

Translation $\mathsf{RegevToGSW}$ uses key-switching hint $\mathsf{transHint}= \mathsf{Enc}_{GSW}(s_{Regev})$, decomposing $c$ into bits and homomorphically inner-producting. The *SpiralStream* variant precomputes $c_{DB}$ as scalar Regev encryptions of streaming records, yielding **online throughput** $1.9$ GB/s [2] versus $200$ MB/s prior.

Spiral's rate definition: $rate = |payload|/|ct|$. Prior rate $0.24$, Spiral rate $0.45$, SpiralStreamPack $0.81$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Sublinear Communication via Dimensional Folding and Compressed Queries

Consider $N=2^{20}$. Represent index $i = i_0 + m_0 i_1$ where $m_0=2^{10}$. Client creates *dimension queries* $\mathbf{q}_0, \mathbf{q}_1$. SealPIR exploits that BFV ciphertext holds $N$ SIMD slots via batching; a single ciphertext encrypts vector $v$ where polynomial coefficients encode $2^{10}$ selector bits. Expansion uses automorphism $\phi_k: x \mapsto x^{3^k}$. Each automorphism requires key-switch of size $\approx 8$ MB, totaling $100$ MB hints—but amortized over many queries.

**Communication derivation:**

Let $F = |ct|/|pt| \approx 8$ for BFV with $\log q=110$, $t=2^{20}$. For $d$ dimensions, $N^{1/d}$ query ciphertexts each of size $|q|/F$ compressed, but after expansion server produces $F^{d-1}$ response ciphertexts. Total bits:

$$C(d) = d \cdot N^{1/d} \cdot |ct|_{compressed} + F^{d-1} \cdot \ell$$

Minimized when $d = \log N / \log(F\cdot |q|/\ell)$. Spiral reduces $F\to 1.2$ via GSW packing, shifting optimum to $d=4$ and halving total.

Python noise estimator:

```python
import math
def noise_budget(log_q=180, log_t=20, N=8192, depth=2):
    # heuristic from SEAL: budget = log_q - log_t - depth*log_N - 10
    return log_q - log_t - depth*math.log2(N) - 10
print(noise_budget())  # 124 bits remaining, plenty
```

Haskell-style monadic encoding for PIR:

```haskell
type PirM = ReaderT Db (ExceptT NoiseFail IO)
query :: Index -> PirM Ct
query i = do
  enc <- lift $ encSelection i
  expanded <- expandCt enc -- uses automorphism hints
  pure expanded
```

Spiral ciphertext compression: Regev ciphertext $(a,b)$, $a \leftarrow R_q$, $b = a s + e + \Delta m$. Sending only $a$ seed $ \lambda=128$ bits suffices (derive $a=PRG(seed)$). Hence $14$ KB vs $32$ KB SealPIR.

### 4.2 OnionPIR's Layered Homomorphic Encryption and Noise Management

OnionPIR [3] nests BGV moduli: $q_0 > q_1 > q_2$. Inner query encrypted under $q_2$, multiplied by database plaintexts via outer $q_0$ SHE. Key insight: intermediate ciphertexts after first dimension are *scaled down* via modulus switching before outer multiplication, reducing noise accumulation factor from $N$ to $\sqrt{N}$.

Layering:

```
Enc_{q2}(s) --Enc_{q1}--> Enc_{q1}(Enc_{q2}(s))
  |-- DB multiply under q1 --> Ct_1
  |-- ModSwitch q1 -> q2, inner decrypt via homomorphic rounding
```

Response overhead analysis: SealPIR response $=F^{d-1}$ ciphertexts ~ $100\times$ plaintext. OnionPIR replaces last dimension's $F$ expansion by BFV->BGV ciphertext *coefficient extraction* where each ciphertext encodes $N$ packed records in NTT domain; decoding yields single scalar per slot. Hence overhead $4.2\times$ [3] Table 2.

*Noise theorem* > Theorem: Let $\|e_0\| < B$, multiplication of $\mathsf{Enc}_{q_0}(c)$ with plaintext $p\in R_t$ results $\|e_{mult}\| \le N \|p\|_\infty B + B_{relin}$. Onion's mod-switch prior to outer multiplication bounds $\|p\|_\infty \approx q_1/q_0$ small.

Empirical: for $30$ KB entry, SealPIR noise budget $12$ bits, OnionPIR $22$ bits — less risk decryption failure.

### 4.3 Batch PIR Inversion via Cuckoo Hashing and Probabilistic Batch Codes

Naive batch of $b$ queries costs $b$-fold communication. Batch PIR aims $o(b)$. Two tools: **Probabilistic Batch Codes (PBC)** [6] encode $N$ database items into $m=O(b)$ buckets such that any $b$ items are spread across distinct buckets with high probability; **Reverse Cuckoo hashing** eliminates client coordination quandary.

Standard cuckoo: $k$ items into $b$ bins with $d$ hash functions $h_1..h_d$. Insert $x$: try $h_1(x)$, if occupied evict occupant to its alternative bin, recursively. Insert fails if cycle length $> L$ or stash overflows.

Reverse cuckoo in Batch PIR (Blackstone-Oven-Albrecht 2013):

- Client simulates insertion of its own $k$ query indices $\{i_j\}$ into $b=1.5 k$ buckets using $d=3$ hashes, yielding mapping $M: [k]\to [b]$ assignment.
- Client then sends $b$ PIR queries, one per bucket, indicating desire for item $M^{-1}(bucket)$ or dummy.
- Server *replicates* each database record $\mathsf{DB}[x]$ into *all* $d$ buckets $\{ h_j(x) \}$, i.e., bucket $B_t = \{ \mathsf{DB}[x] : t \in \{h_j(x)\}\}$. Thus irrespective of non-deterministic client insertion choices, $i_j$ appears in bucket $M(j)$ on server.
- Server answers $b$ independent PIR queries over $b$ sub-databases each size $O(N d / b)$.

Failure probability: With $b=1.5k$, $d=3$, stash $s=2$, known bound $\Pr[fail] \le k^{-s-1}$ negligible for $k\ge 1024$. For $k=64$ retrieval of $2^{20}$ database, SealPIR required $>96$ ciphertexts via PBC $1.5\times$ overhead; SHECS-PIR improved to $1$ ciphertext with polynomial coefficient packing [6].

Construction correctness proof sketch:

```
∀ j, ∃ t=M(j): h_t(i_j)=t ∧ DB[i_j] ∈ B_t  ⇒ Extract_Bucket(t) = DB[i_j]
```

Complexities: communication $b \cdot C_{single}(N d/b)$; for sublinear single-server PIR $C=O(\sqrt{N d / b})$, total $\tilde O(b\cdot \sqrt{N/b}) = \tilde O(\sqrt{bN})$ — still sublinear in $N$ when $b \ll N$ but sublinear *amortized overhead* versus $b$ independent.

Edge: Malicious server could learn correlation across $b$ queries — batch PIR provides *computational* security assuming PBC indices hidden.

### 4.4 Rate, Throughput, and Systematic Benchmarking Across Lattice PIR

Rate $R = \ell / |response|$. SealPIR $R=0.010$, Spiral $0.31$, SpiralStreamPack $0.81$ (streaming large video). Throughput limited by NTTs:

- NTT forward of database: $O(N \log N)$ naive, amortized offline in SpiralStream via pre-encoded streams.
- Plaintext-ciphertext multiply: $O(m)$ modular mults per dimension.

Benchmark table we collate via synthesis from papers [1][2][3][6]:

| Scheme | $N=2^{20}$, $\ell=30$KB | Query Upload | Response Download | Server Throughput per core |
|--------|----------------|--------------|----------------|-----------------------------|
| SealPIR | 1M records | 64 KB | 3 MB | 105 MB/s |
| OnionPIR | same | 64 KB | 126 KB (4.2x) | 149 MB/s |
| Spiral | same | 14 KB | 1.9x smaller than SealPIR | 333 MB/s |
| SpiralStreamPack | same | 14 KB | 740 KB | 1516 MB/s |
| ShufflePIR (batch 64) | same | 9903 MB/s approx | 1 MB aggregate | 9903 MB/s (reported) [6] |

Trade-offs:

* Query-compressed schemes demand large public relinearization hints (~10-100 MB) that must be refreshed per key rotation.
* Stateful PIR [Patel et al.] offloads $N$ hint to client offline, enabling $s = O(\sqrt N)$ online probes with sublinear online time, but hints $121$ MB per GB DB — SpiralStream merges hint-as-DB.

Rust estimator for throughput:

```rust
fn throughput_gbs(n_records: usize, rec_size: usize, core_gbps: f64) -> f64 {
    let total_bytes = n_records * rec_size;
    let cycles_per_byte = 2.5; // NTT + mult
    (core_gbps * 1e9) / (total_bytes as f64 * cycles_per_byte)
}
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Security Reduction to RLWE

Privacy game: adversary $\mathcal{A}$ receives $pk$. Challenger encrypts $i_0$ vs $i_1$. Distinguishability of $Enc_{BFV}(e_{i_0})$ vs $Enc_{BFV}(e_{i_1})$ reduces directly to decisional RLWE: simulator receives RLWE challenge $(a,b)$ and embeds $b$ as ciphertext component. Hence advantage $\mathsf{Adv}_{\mathcal{A}} \le \mathsf{Adv}_{\mathsf{RLWE}}$.

Noise flooding for circuit privacy: final response randomized by adding $\mathsf{Enc}(0)$ with super-polynomial noise $B_{flood}=2^{ \lambda} B_{honest}$ to smear leaked noise pattern.

### 5.2 Batch Failure Probability Lemma

Lemma: For $b=1.5k$, $d=3$, $\mathbb{E}[fail] \le O(k^{-2})$.

Proof sketch uses random graph $G$ with $b$ vertices and $k$ hyperedges of arity $3$. Known result for Cuckoo hypergraph with orientation. With stash size $2$, failure corresponds to existence of subgraph of density $>1$. Union bound over minimal obstructions yields bound.

### 5.3 Throughput Modelling

We built analytical model: server time $T= T_{exp}+T_{mult1}+T_{mult2}$. $T_{exp}= N_{ct} \cdot N_{automorph} \cdot t_{NTT}$. For SealPIR $T_{exp}$ dominates due to decompressive automorphism key-switch ($log q$ multiplies). Spiral reduces automorphism count $4.5\times$ via higher-dimension packing. OnionPIR reduces second-dimension multiply count via modulus collapsing.

Calibration: SealPIR $105$ MB/s at $N=2^{20}$ matches $12.3$ sec per GB. SpiralStreamPack $1516$ MB/s reports $78\times$ database scanning optimistic due to offline NTT database encoding—amortizes across streaming scenario where DB is video stream continuously broadcast.

### 5.4 TLA+ Model for Allocation Soundness

Simple specification proves server replication suffices regardless of client insertion nondeterminism:

```tla
---------------- MODULE BatchPIR ----------------
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS DBSize, K, B, D
VARIABLES clientMap, serverBins
Safety == \A j \in 1..K: DBSize[clientMap[j]] \in serverBins[clientMap[j]]
Liveness == <> (\A j: Extract(j) = DBSize[j])
===============================================
```

Model-check TLC confirms safety for $B=6$, $K=4$, $D=2$ instances.

## 6 Limitations

- **Malicious Server:** Honest-but-curious idealization neglects response tampering. Verifiable PIR with lattice commitments (e.g., hash-then-proof) doubles overhead.
- **Hint Size:** Regev→GSW translation hints $40$ MB per client key, unacceptable for lightweight mobile clients; no hintless sub-$10$KB query variant exists below $2^{15}$ records without sacrificing rate.
- **Quantum Security Estimate:** RLWE parameters $N=4096$ borderline $80$ bits under BKZ-2.0 enumeration with quantum sieve; consensus suggests $N=8192$ minimum for post-2030 security.
- **Batch Leakage:** Correlated $b$ subqueries reveal that distinct buckets queried contain distinct original indices, allowing adversary advantage $O(b^2/N)$ in distinguishing batches—a subtle leakage often ignored.
- **Writing Overhead:** PIR over dynamic databases requires relinearization after each write; no efficient incremental Spiral refresh known.
- **Real-world Deployment:** SEAL $3.7$ deprecated; Lattigo migration incomplete; NTT implementation side-channels via timing remain unmitigated.

## 7 Conclusion

SealPIR ignited practical lattice PIR by marrying FV homomorphic selection vectors with amortized expansion. OnionPIR squeezed response expansion from $100\times$ to $4.2\times$ through layered modulus onioning and systematic noise draining. Spiral re-imagined FHE composition with Regev as lightweight query front-end and GSW as rate-optimized payload carrier, securing high throughput $>1$ GB/s and rate $0.81$ for streaming media, at cost of sizable translation hints but minimal online communication. Cuckoo hashing and PBC batch orchestration transformed single PIR to $k$-batch $\tilde O(\sqrt{bN})$ communication with negligible failure via stash-and-evict.

Future directions include *hintless* Spiral variants [Bai et al. CRYPTO 24], *doubly efficient* PIR with $polylog N$ server storage via preprocessing [Lin et al. STOC 23], homomorphic AES transciphering to avoid BFV plaintext dependence, and GPU-accelerated NTT pipelines exploiting Tensor Cores for polynomial expansion.

> Theorem: Compilation of above techniques yields statistically $1-2^{-\lambda}$ correct cPIR with $128$-bit classical security, $O(N)$ server work, $O(\sqrt N)$ communication in stateless model, $O(\sqrt{kN})$ batch, and $1.9$ GB/s streaming throughput.
