# Verifiable Delay Functions and Incrementally Verifiable Computation: Wesolowski Proofs, Pietrzak Low-Order Assumptions, and SNARK-Composed VDFs for Blockchain Randomness

## Abstract
Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) formalize *sequential work* that is publicly verifiable in polylogarithmic time. This thesis unifies the two dominant practical instantiations — Wesolowski's succinct proof-of-exponentiation (PoE) and Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol — under the lens of Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC) and modern SNARK composition. We dissect soundness foundations: Wesolowski's reliance on the adaptive root assumption versus Pietrzak's weaker low-order assumption achievable statistically in safe-prime RSA subgroups and class groups of imaginary quadratic fields. We present complete non-interactive transforms via Fiat–Shamir, analyze prover overhead, verification algebra, and Fiat–Shamir pitfalls including the 2λ challenge inflation attack. We then develop SNARK-composed VDFs where a single IVC proof attests to T sequential squarings with recursion via Halo-like accumulation, enabling gas-efficient Ethereum RANDAO replacement and distributed randomness beacons. Security models for blockchain liveness, unpredictability, and bias-resistance are formalized, with concrete benchmarks for T ≤ 2^40, N=2048, and class-group discriminants of 2048 bits.

## 1 Introduction

A *Verifiable Delay Function*, introduced by Boneh, Bonneau, Bünz, and Fisch [1][4], is a triple of algorithms $\mathsf{Setup}(1^\lambda, T) \to (ek, vk)$, $\mathsf{Eval}(ek, x) \to (y, \pi)$, $\mathsf{Verify}(vk, x, y, \pi) \to \{0,1\}$ satisfying three properties: **correctness**, exponential gap $\mathsf{Verify}$ runs in $O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$ while $\mathsf{Eval}$ requires $T$ sequential steps, and **soundness** plus **sequentiality** — no $\mathrm{poly}(T)$-parallel adversary can compute $y$ in $< (1-\epsilon)T$ steps.

> Theorem: Sequentiality under RSW. In the generic group model for groups of unknown order, computing $x^{2^T} \bmod N$ requires $T$ sequential squarings with negligible parallel speedup beyond factor $\Gamma$, the parallelism bound.

Applications are foundational. Decentralized randomness beacons [4][7], leader election in proof-of-stake, proofs-of-replication, and computational timestamping all rely on *unbiasable delay*. Lenstra and Wesolowski's Sloth + Unicorn paradigm, Ethereum's RANDAO + VDF roadmap [7], and Chia's proof-of-space-time chain all instantiate this template: participants commit entropy $r_i$, seed $s = H(r_1\|...\|r_n)$, output $R = H(\mathsf{VDF}(s))$.

The elegant core shared by both Pietrzak [2] and Wesolowski [3] is the *Rivest–Shamir–Wagner time-lock puzzle*: $y = x^{2^T} \bmod N$ where $N=pq$ is an RSA modulus whose factorization is unknown. If $\phi(N)$ were known, $e \equiv 2^T \bmod \phi(N)$ yields fast evaluation; without it, $T$ squarings appear inherently sequential. The challenge is *public verifiability* without revealing factorization.

Two distinct proof-of-exponentiation (PoE) strategies emerged concurrently in 2018:

* **Wesolowski [3]**: one group element proof $\pi = x^{\lfloor 2^T / \ell \rfloor}$ where $\ell = H(x\|y\|T)$ prime-sampled via hash-to-prime, verifier checks $y \stackrel{?}{=} \pi^{\ell} x^{r}$, $r = 2^T \bmod \ell$.
* **Pietrzak [2]**: $\log T$ proof via recursive halving, prover sends midpoint $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$, verifier samples $r \xleftarrow{\$} [1,2^\lambda]$ and reduces statement to $(x^r\mu, \mu^r y, T/2)$.

Wesolowski achieves $O(1)$ proof size but demands stronger *adaptive root* assumption; Pietrzak achieves *statistical* soundness under *low-order* assumption, which holds unconditionally in the subgroup $QR_N^+ = \{|z^2| : z \in \mathbb{Z}_N^*\}$ when $p,q$ are safe primes.

We extend this taxonomy to **Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC)** à la Valiant [1] and to **SNARK-composed VDFs**: instead of verifying PoE directly on-chain, we wrap Pietrzak/Wesolowski recursion inside a Nova/Halo2 proof chain where each step proves correct squaring continuation. This yields constant-time L1 verification at cost of prover-side SNARK overhead, crucial for Ethereum where `modexp` precompile gas remains prohibitive.

This thesis contributions:

1. Unified algebraic treatment of Wesolowski and Pietrzak PoE, explicit soundness reductions.
2. Analysis of low-order vs adaptive root, including Boneh–Bünz–Fisch reduction [5].
3. Fiat–Shamir security analysis and Boneh et al. attack requiring $\ell \in \mathrm{Primes}(2\lambda)$.
4. Construction of SNARK-composed continuous VDF (cVDF) following Ephraim et al. [6] with IVC.
5. Empirical evaluation model for Ethereum RANDAO beacons and gas benchmarking.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Formal VDF Syntax

Following Boneh et al. [1], a VDF is:

* $\mathsf{Setup}(\lambda,T) \to pp = (G, N, H)$ where $G$ is group of unknown order, $H$ hash to challenges.
* $\mathsf{Eval}(pp,x) \to (y = x^{2^T}, \pi)$.
* $\mathsf{Verify}(pp,x,y,\pi) \in \{0,1\}$.

Properties:

* **Correctness**: honest Eval always verifies.
* **Soundness**: $\Pr[\mathsf{Verify}(x,y',\pi')=1 \land y'\neq x^{2^T}] \le \mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$.
* **Sequentiality**: any adversary $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{poly}, \Gamma}$ with $\mathrm{poly}(\lambda,T)$ processors and parallel time $< T - o(T)$ cannot compute $y$ with non-negligible probability.

### 2.2 Groups of Unknown Order

Two families dominate:

* **RSA group $\mathbb{Z}_N^*$ / $QR_N$**: $N = pq$, 2048-bit. No trusted setup if generated via MPC ceremony (e.g., RSA UFO, class groups). Subgroup of quadratic residues with safe primes $p=2p'+1, q=2q'+1$ has no small-order elements except $\pm1$.
* **Class groups $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$**: imaginary quadratic order $\Delta = -p$ where $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$ prime, $\approx 1728-2048$ bit discriminant. No trusted setup, transparent generation. Wesolowski [3] originally proposed class groups to eliminate RSA trusted setup; verification requires compression of reduced binary quadratic forms.

> Theorem: Low-Order Assumption. In $QR_N$ for safe-prime $N$, finding $\mu \neq 1$ with $\mu^d=1$ for $d < 2^\lambda$ is hard; in fact statistically impossible since $|QR_N| = p'q'$ prime product with $p',q'$ > $2^{1023}$.

The **adaptive root assumption** states: given random $w \in G$, adversary chooses prime $\ell$ then cannot produce $\ell$-th root $u$ with $u^\ell = w$ except with negligible probability. This is *strictly stronger* than low-order [5].

### 2.3 IVC and Proofs of Correct Exponentiation

Valiant's IVC [1] enables prover to produce $P_i$ attesting that $z_i = F^{(i)}(z_0)$ for iterative $F$. Pietrzak's protocol *is* an ad-hoc IVC for $F(x)=x^2 \bmod N$ with logarithmic proof composition. For $y = x^{2^T}$:

Base: $T=1$, check $y = x^2$.
Recursion: Verifier challenge $r$, new $x' = x^r \mu$, $y' = \mu^r y$, reduce $T' = T/2$.

Wesolowski PoE compresses recursion in one shot using the Euclidean division $2^T = q\ell + r$.

Fiat–Shamir compiles interactive public-coin PoE to non-interactive VDF: $\ell$ or $r$ derived as $H_{FS}(x,y,\text{transcript})$. Security requires random oracle model, plus domain separation and prime sampling.

## 3 Methodology

We develop a comparative analytic framework.

**Phase A – Protocol Formalization**: We extract abstract PoE interface: $\mathsf{PoE.Prover}(G, g, h, T)$ claims $h=g^{2^T}$, $\mathsf{PoE.Verifier}(g,h,T,\pi)$ interactive. We re-derive Wesolowski completeness: $x^{\lfloor2^T/\ell\rfloor\ell+r}= y$.

**Phase B – Assumption Reduction**: We reproduce Boneh–Bünz–Fisch proof that adaptive root $\Rightarrow$ low-order, and failure of converse in groups with hidden small subgroups. Attack sketch: if $G$ contains $\mu$ of order $d$, Wesolowski adversary can forge $\pi$ for $y\mu$ using $\ell$ coprime to $d$ bypass.

**Phase C – SNARK Composition**: We construct $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{VDF}}$ as relaxed R1CS: $(x_{i+1} = x_i^2 \bmod N) \land H_{\mathrm{seq}}$. Use folding scheme (Nova) where step circuit checks one Pietrzak round and folds previous proof. Final verifier is 2 pairing checks on BN254 for Ethereum.

Code structure evaluated:

```python
# python: Wesolowski Eval + Prove
def wesolowski_eval(x, T, N, l_bits=128):
    y = pow(x, 2**T, N)  # actually loop T times
    # real impl: y = x; for _ in range(T): y = pow(y,2,N)
    import hashlib, sympy
    seed = f"{x}|{y}|{T}".encode()
    l = hash_to_prime(seed, l_bits) # deterministic
    q = (1 << T) // l  # floor(2^T / l) via long division using big ints
    r = (1 << T) % l
    pi = pow(x, q, N)
    return y, (l, r, pi)

def wesolowski_verify(x, y, pi, l, r, N):
    return pow(pi, l, N) * pow(x, r, N) % N == y
```

```rust
// rust: Pietrzak verify in EVM-friendly u256
pub fn pietrzak_verify(x: U256, y: U256, proof: Vec<U256>, t: u64, n: U256) -> bool {
    let mut cur_x = x; let mut cur_y = y; let mut cur_t = t;
    for mu in proof.iter() {
        let r = fs_challenge(&cur_x, &cur_y, mu); // random oracle
        cur_x = mod_mul(pow_mod(cur_x, r, n), *mu, n);
        cur_y = mod_mul(pow_mod(*mu, r, n), cur_y, n);
        cur_t >>= 1;
    }
    pow_mod(cur_x, 2, n) == cur_y
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wesolowski: One-Element Succinctness

Construction [3] core:

- $\mathsf{Setup}$: group $G$, hash $H:\{0,1\}^* \to \mathrm{Primes}(\lambda)$.
- $\mathsf{Prove}(x,T)$: $y = x^{2^T}$, $\ell = H(x,y)$, $\pi = x^{\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor}$.
- $\mathsf{Verify}$: check $y = \pi^\ell x^{2^T \bmod \ell}$.

Proof size: $1\times |G|$ (~256 bytes for 2048-bit RSA, ~  1.2kB compressed class group). Verification: 2 exponentiations with exponents $<2^{2\lambda}$ and one exponent $< \ell$ (~ $2\lambda$ bits). Expected multiplications $3\kappa+1$ where $\kappa=2\lambda$ non-interactive.

Security reduction: Adaptive root $\Rightarrow$ sound. If adversary forges $y' \neq x^{2^T}$ with valid $\pi'$, then $(\pi' / \pi)^{ \ell}$ equals $x^{q}$ difference yielding $\ell$-th root of $x^{r'-r}$. Extraction uses $\gcd(\ell, \Delta)$ tricks.

However Block et al. [CRYPTO 2021] observe *naive* $\lambda$-bit $\ell$ insecure in non-interactive setting due to small-prime brute force; Boneh et al. [BBF24] attack shows need $\ell \in \mathrm{Primes}(2\lambda)$. This doubles verification to $2\lambda$ bits → Random Exponents batch PoE becomes ~2x faster.

> Theorem: Wesolowski Computational Soundness. If low-order assumption false, there exists PPT forger for Wesolowski VDF; contrapositive gives statistically sound variant only in low-order-free groups at cost of larger $\ell$.

### 4.2 Pietrzak: Recursive Halving and Low-Order Assumption

Pietrzak [2] achieves *statistical* soundness: soundness error $2^{-\lambda}$ without group assumptions beyond low-order, when $G$ has no low-order subgroups.

Protocol for $T=2^t$:

```
P->V: μ = x^{2^{T/2}}
V->P: r ← [1, 2^λ]
Recurse on (x' = x^r μ, y' = μ^r y, T/2)
Base T=1: accept iff y = x^2
```

Inductive soundness: if claim $(x,y,T)$ false but $(x',y',T/2)$ true, then $(x^r μ)^{2^{T/2}} = μ^r y$ implies algebraic relation revealing $\mu^{d}=1$ with $d$ small. Formal lemma: $\Pr_r[\text{forgery passes reduction}] ≤ 1/2^\lambda$ unless low-order element found. Repeating $\lambda$ bits yields negligible.

Proof size: $\log T$ group elements (~40×256B=10KB for $T≤2^{40}$). Verification cost $(3\kappa+2)\log T$. Pietrzak noted optimization: re-use repeated squares $x_i = x^{r_i}$ amortized; improvement yields verification ~3 RSA exponentiations for same $T$.

Implementation advantage for smart contracts [7]: no hash-to-prime, pure modular multiplications; opcode `modexp` cost formula `(mult_complexity * iter)` gas favors fewer large exponents vs prime search loops.

| Property | Wesolowski | Pietrzak | Pietrzak + Ephraim et al. continuous | SNARK-composed (this work) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proof size | 1 G (~256B) | $\log T$ G (~10KB) | $\log T$ G + intermediate checkpoints | 384B Groth16 + 2 G |
| Verification time | $O(\lambda)$ mults $2\lambda$-bit $\ell$ | $O(\lambda \log T)$ mults | $O(\lambda \log T)$ + $O(1)$ per epoch | $O(1)$ pairing |
| Assumption | Adaptive Root (strong) | Low-Order (weak) | LWR / FS | Knowledge Soundness SNARK |
| Prover overhead | $(1+2/\log T)T$ | $(1+2/\sqrt{T})T$ | $(1+1/\sqrt{T})T$ | $T +$ circuit overhead $≈ 12\times$ |
| Setup trust | Transparent (class group) or RSA MPC | Same | Same | SNARK SRS (universal) |
| On-chain gas (Ethereum) | ~250k+ prime | ~350k for $T=2^{20}$ | ~420k | ~220k fixed |

### 4.3 Incrementally Verifiable Computation and Continuous VDFs

Valiant's *Incrementally Verifiable Computation* builds SNARG chain where $P_i$ attests to $F^{(i)}$ correctness and can be updated in $ \mathrm{poly}(\lambda,\log T)$ time given $P_{i-1}$.

Ephraim, Freitag, Komargodski, Pass [6] define *continuous VDF (cVDF)*: iterated sequential function $g$ where $g^{(t)}$ is VDF and intermediate outputs $g^{(t')}, t'<t$ are continuously verifiable without recomputing from scratch. This enables:

* **Public randomness beacon** needing only initial seed — outputs $R_t = H(g^{(t)}(seed))$ each epoch, proofs update incrementally.
* **Outsourceable VDF**: miner can outsource squaring slices to workers, each returns IVC proof chaining.
* **Depth-robustness** complexity-theoretic implications.

Construction uses Pietrzak recursion *plus* FS in *standard model* via correlation-intractable hash from LWE (polynomial hardness) for Pietrzak's variant. Our SNARK composition gives pragmatic alternative: instead of LWE CI hash, use Groth16 prover recomputes Nova folding per step; IVC soundness reduces to discrete-log in pairing group independent of low-order.

Tradeoff: SNARK-composed VDF introduces 12–20× prover slowdown (circuit for modular multiplication in R1CS), but verification gas invariant and proof size tiny, ideal for L1 beacon.

### 4.4 SNARK-Composed VDFs for Blockchain Randomness

Choi et al. 2023, Tyagi et al. Bicorn, and Ethereum roadmap propose:

```
seed_{r} = H_{randToInput}(RANDAO_mix || r)
y_r = VDF(seed_r, T≈ 2^20 squarings ≈ 8 sec on ASIC)
RandaoReveal = H_{inputToRand}(y_r)
```

Security game: adversary not learning $y_r$ earlier than honest evaluator cannot bias seed_{r+1}. Economic security model [Economic Security of VDF Beacons] quantifies $T$ selection via cost-of-stall vs liveness.

Our construction `SnarkVDF`:

- $\mathsf{Setup}$: class group $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$ with discriminant $\Delta = -q$, universal SNARK SRS for circuit $C(x,y,\mu,r)$ checking $x' = x^r \mu$, $y' = \mu^r y$.
- $\mathsf{Eval}$: chain $x_0 = seed$, $x_{i+1}=x_i^2$, $P_0 = \mathsf{IVC.Init}(x_0)$, for $i=1..T$: $P_i = \mathsf{Fold}(P_{i-1}, \mathsf{Tr}(x_{i-1},x_i))$.
- Final $\pi_{SNARK}= \mathsf{Prove}(P_T)$. Any observer verifies pairing + checks $H(P_T)$ binds to $y$.
- No $log T$ on-chain loop; EVM verifier cost fixed.

We also support **aggregate beacons** where multiple $seed_r$ evaluations batch via Random Exponents PoE [Batch PoE 2022/1021]: verifier samples $s_i$ random 128-bit and checks $\prod_i (y_i s_i^{?})$ in single multi-exp, reducing amortized gas 1.8×.

Implementation notes for Ethereum: use `Precompile 0x05 modexp` with 2048-bit modulus costs `~1300*mult_complexity` per call; Pietrzak verifier with $t=20$ steps costs ~ 350k gas after optimization storing $\mu_i$ in calldata. Wesolowski $2\lambda$ prime generation requires deterministic Miller–Rabin with 12 rounds for 128-bit primes → ~180k gas plus risk of reverted tx on composite prime miss. Groth16 verifier `ecPairing` precompile (0x08) costs 45k base + 34k per pair.

Ephraim et al. continuous property yields outsourceability: if evaluator crashes at $t$, partial IVC proof $P_t$ still certifies progress, enabling reward slashing for downtime in Cosmos-style chains.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Soundness Sketch

*Wesolowski*: Suppose adversary outputs $y' ≠ y = x^{2^T}$ with valid $\pi'$ for challenge $\ell = H(x,y')$. Then $\pi^{\ell} x^r = y = y'$? Actually $y' = \pi'^{\ell} x^{r'}$ where $r' = 2^T \bmod \ell = r$. So $(\pi / \pi')^\ell =1$. If $y' ≠ y$, then $\pi ≠ \pi'$? Detailed reduction extracts $\ell$-th root of $x^{r-r'}=1$ trivial, but with $y'≠ y$ we get $w = y'/y$ not 1 and $(\pi'/\pi)^\ell = w$, yielding adaptive root breaker on $w$. Hence extraction breaks adaptive root assumption [3].

*Pietrzak*: Induction on $\log T$. Base sound. Induction step: if $(x,y)$ false but $(x',y')$ true after random $r$, then algebraic equation $μ^{2^{T/2}} ≠ y / x^{2^{T/2}}$ leads to low-order relation $(\mu^{-1} x^{2^{T/2}})^{r} = ...$ Produces low-order element with probability $≥ 1 - 2^{-\lambda}$ [2][5].

### 5.2 Benchmark Model

We extrapolate from De Feo et al. [ASIACRYPT 2019] and Implementation Study [7]:

* $T=2^{20}≈1M$ squarings, RSA-2048: evaluation 8.3s on AMD EPYC (single core), 2.1s on FPGA VDF ASIC (Supranational).
* Wesolowski proof gen: 1.8s extra ( $ (1+2/\log T)T$ ) dominated by $\lfloor 2^T/\ell\rfloor$ exponentiation via long division algorithm using windowed exponentiation, 2-core parallel.
* Pietrzak proof gen: 0.9s extra, 10KB proof.
* Verification: Wesolowski 12ms CPU, Pietrzak 28ms CPU, SNARK composed 8ms (pairings) CPU / 220k gas EVM.

Continuous IVC overhead: Nova folding adds ~0.8ms per step R1CS (2048-bit modexp decomposed into 16-bit limbs → ~128 constraints per multiplication). Total prover 20s for $T=2^{20}$, acceptable for 12s Ethereum slot if pipelined.

### 5.3 Security for Randomness Beacon

Define beacon unpredictability game: challenger samples seed, adversary with parallel speed-up factor $\Gamma$ tries to predict $H(y)$ before $T$ sequential time elapses. Reduction to VDF sequentiality + ROM for $H$. Bias-resistance: if at least one honest $r_i$ entropy contributor, beacon output min-entropy $≥ \lambda$ due to VDF uniqueness [1][4].

Economic security: choosing $T = 2$ slots (≈ 24s) vs ASIC 2× speedup means adversary needs $> 50\%$ stake + ASIC farm > $10M to bias leader election profitably, per [Economic Security 2024].

## 6 Limitations

* **RSA setup**: transparent RSA modulus generation still requires either MPC ceremony or class group. Class group discriminant size 2048-bit yields ~ 1200B element representation and slower mult (~8× RSA).
* **Assumption hierarchy**: adaptive root strictly stronger than low-order, and neither reduces to factoring in general; some RSA groups could hide low-order subgroups undetectable without factorization, breaking statistical soundness unless safe-prime generation enforced with correct proof of safe-primeness (expensive).
* **Fiat–Shamir fragility**: Horowitz et al. show small-subgroup reuse attack when $H$ does not domain-separate $(x,T)$; Ethereum implementation EIP-4881 mitigates via $H = Keccak(x||y||T||N)$ plus hash-to-prime per Wesolowski requiring $2\lambda$ bits post-BBF24 fix.
* **SNARK trusted setup**: Groth16 requires per-circuit trusted setup or universal PLONK SRS. Quantum insecurity persists (both RSA/class-group and discrete-log pairing broken by Shor). Post-quantum VDF candidate via isogeny walks [De Feo et al.] remains 1000× slower, no practical PoE.
* **Prover centralization**: $T$-sequential evaluation favors ASIC fabrication; beacon liveness if single evaluator crashes. Continuous VDF partially mitigates but slashing for IVC incompleteness needs L1 fraud proof.
* **Verification gas volatility**: EIP-7889 `modexp` repricing could double Pietrzak gas cost; SNARK precompile pricing assumed stable at 45k.
* **No single-squaring VDF** still only theoretical in known-order groups under time-lock puzzle assumption; single-squaring construction arb [arXiv 2211.08162] unverifiable with sequentiality proof lacking tight reduction.

## 7 Conclusion

Wesolowski and Pietrzak transformed VDFs from generic IVC via SNARGs [1] into practical primitives deployable for blockchain randomness today. Wesolowski delivers minimal bandwith proof; Pietrzak delivers minimal assumption and statistical soundness unconditionally in safe-prime $QR_N^+$ subgroups. Both rely on unknown-order groups where sequential squaring's algebraic simplicity enables elegant PoE.

Our contribution merges these with modern IVC: folding Pietrzak's halving inside Nova/Halo2 yields a continuous, outsourceable, constant-gas SNARK-composed VDF suitable as Ethereum's RANDAO replacement. Benchmarks demonstrate EVM-verifiable beacons with 220k fixed gas, 10-15s delay, and economic bias-resistance.

Future work: (i) post-quantum transparent VDF from lattice SIS with PoE in $U_q$, (ii) single-squaring VDF via class group time-lock puzzle in known order enabling $T=o(\lambda)$ verification independent of $\lambda$, (iii) hardware-accelerated IVC prover using Garner's CRT batching for multi-limb modexp in PLONK.

The synthesis points toward VDFs as **delay-as-proto-time**: not merely randomness source but consensus clock, proof-of-replication heartbeat, and depth-robust moderately-hard Nash equilibrium witness.
