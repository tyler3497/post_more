# Bootstrapping Leveled FHE to Unbounded Depth: Asymmetric Noise Growth in GSW, RGSW, and TFHE Circuit Bootstrapping via Blind Rotation and Modulus Switching ## Abstract This thesis formalizes the transition from leveled fully homomorphic encryption to unbounded homomorphic computation via **GSW-type bootstrapping**, with emphasis on *asymmetric noise growth*, gadget decomposition, and programmable blind rotation. We dissect LWE and RLWE foundations, compare BGV/BFV relinearization-based multiplication against GSW approximate eigenvector multiplication, and prove why GSW noise grows linearly in the left factor and only polynomially in lattice dimension. We then reconstruct the full bootstrapping pipeline: modulus switching, blind rotation as a homomorphic accumulator on the torus, sample extraction, and RGSW external product key-switching. Finally we explain TFHE functional bootstrapping via test-vector polynomial LUTs, enabling arbitrary univariate function evaluation during noise refresh. > **THESIS / EDUCATIONAL** — This post is a scholarly synthesis for educational purposes only. --- ## 1. Introduction The search for *unbounded* homomorphic computation may be stated simply: **given ciphertexts that carry small noise, can we keep the noise small forever?** Gentry's 2009 breakthrough answered affirmatively with *bootstrapping*: homomorphically evaluate decryption to refresh noise. Yet first-generation bootstrapping required hours per gate. **Second generation** — Brakerski-Gentry-Vaikuntanathan (BGV) and Brakerski-Fan-Vercauteren (BFV) — avoided bootstrapping for *a priori* bounded depth $L$ via *leveled* modes: choose modulus chain $q_0 > q_1 > ... > q_L$ with modulus switching to control noise. This yields $O(\lambda \cdot L^3)$ per gate, quasi-linear in security parameter $\lambda$, but depth is fixed at keygen time. Unbounded depth requires still bootstrapping. **Third generation** — Gentry-Sahai-Waters (GSW) introduces the *approximate eigenvector* paradigm: ciphertext $C$ satisfies $C v \approx m v$, where $v$ is secret key vector and $m$ is message. Multiplication becomes matrix multiplication $C_1 \cdot C_2$ with noise growing *asymmetrically*. This asymmetry powers the Barrington-inspired branching-program bootstrapping of Alperin-Sheriff-Peikert (AP14) and its practical ring incarnation **FHEW** and **TFHE**. Our contributions: - **Noise asymmetry theorem** — formal proof that $||E_{mult}||_\infty \le O(m_1 ||E_2|| + ||E_1|| \cdot ||G^{-1}(C_2)||)$ and why flattening $G^{-1}(\cdot)$ limits growth to poly$(n)$. - **Taxonomy** of BGV/BFV vs GSW noise, relinearization vs flattening, NAND-circuit vs matrix-circuit views. - **Full bootstrapping pipeline** decomposition: LWE $\xrightarrow{\text{modswitch}}$ RLWE torus $\xrightarrow{\text{blind rotate}}$ TRLWE accumulator $\xrightarrow{\text{sample extract}}$ refreshed LWE, with complexity $O(n N log Q)$. - **TFHE programmable bootstrapping** — how negacyclic polynomial test-vector $TV = \sum f(i) X^i$ enables arbitrary function $f$ during boot at no extra cost. - Reference implementations in Python and Rust for gadget decomposition and blind rotation. --- ## 2. Background / Preliminaries ### 2.1 LWE and RLWE > **Definition 2.1 (LWE Search).** Let $n,q,\chi$ be parameters, $s \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^n$ uniform, $A \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^{m \times n}$ uniform, $e \leftarrow \chi^m$. Given $(A, b = A s + e \mod q)$, find $s$. Decisional LWE asks to distinguish $(A,b)$ from uniform. Regev's worst-case to average-case reduction shows LWE hardness implies hardness of $\tilde{O}(n^{1.5})$-approximate GapSVP and SIVP. Typically $n \approx 630-1024$, $q \approx 2^{32}$, $\chi$ discrete Gaussian with $\sigma \approx 3.2$ yields 128-bit security per LWE Estimator. > **Definition 2.2 (RLWE).** Let $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^N+1)$ with $N=2^k$. Secret $s \leftarrow R_q$, $a \leftarrow R_q$ uniform, $e \leftarrow \chi$, sample $(a, b=a s + e)$. Decision advantage negligible under ideal-lattice SIVP. Ring structure enables NTT multiplication $O(N log N)$ and SIMD packing of $N$ slots. **LWE symmetric encryption (Regev):** - Secret $s \in \mathbb{Z}_q^n$ - Enc($m \in \{0,1\}$): sample $a$ uniform, $e$, $b = \langle a,s\rangle + e + \lfloor q/2 \rfloor m$, ciphertext $c=(a,b) \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{n+1}$. - Dec: compute $\phi = b - \langle a,s\rangle = e + \lfloor q/2 \rfloor m$, round. Error bound $|e| < q/4$ ensures correctness. Torus variant TLWE works over $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ with modulus $1$. ### 2.2 Leveled FHE: BGV/BFV BGV encodes $m \in R_t$, chooses chain $q_0 \gg ... \gg q_L$. Fresh ct: $(c_0,c_1)$ with $c_0 + c_1 s = \Delta m + e \mod q_L$, $\Delta = \lfloor q/t \rfloor$. Homomorphic add: componentwise. Mul: tensor to degree-2: $(d_0=c_0 c_0', d_1=c_0 c_1' + c_1 c_0', d_2=c_1 c_1')$ then *relinearize* via evaluation key $evk=\text{Enc}(s^2)$: $c_0' = d_0 + \langle g^{-1}(d_2), evk_0\rangle$, $c_1' = d_1 + \langle g^{-1}(d_2), evk_1\rangle$. Noise growth: $e_{mult} \approx t (e_1 m_2 + e_2 m_1) + e_1 e_2$ — *quadratic* in $||e||$. Modulus switching: $ct^{(l+1)} = \lfloor (q_{l+1}/q_l) ct^{(l)} \rceil$ scales $e \leftarrow (q_{l+1}/q_l) e + e_{round}$ where $||e_{round}|| \le O(\sqrt{N})$. Thus depth limited by $L$ such that final noise $< q_L/2$. No bootstrapping needed if $L$ sufficient — hence *leveled*. ### 2.3 GSW: Approximate Eigenvector Gentry-Sahai-Waters original scheme is construction, asymmetric noise proof, attribute-based FHE. Let gadget matrix $G = I_n \otimes g^T$ where $g = (1,2,4,...,2^{\ell-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{\ell}$, $\ell = \lceil \log_2 q \rceil$. Define $G^{-1}: \mathbb{Z}_q^{n \times n\ell} \to \{0,1\}^{n\ell \times n\ell}$ deterministic binary decomposition: $G \cdot G^{-1}(A) = A$ and $||G^{-1}(A)||_\infty \le 1$. KeyGen: secret $s \in \{0,1\}^n$, choose $A$, set $v = (-s,1)^T$ (dimension $k=n+1$). Construct public matrix $P$ such that $P v = e \approx 0$. Enc($m$): Sample random $R \in \{0,1\}^{k\ell \times k}$, output $C = R \cdot P + m \cdot G$ where $C \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{k \times k\ell}$ satisfied $C v = R e + m G v = e' + m \beta v$? Actually approximate eigenvector: $C \cdot v = m \cdot v \cdot g + e'$ up to $G$ structure, more precisely $C v = m G v + e_{small}$. Dec: Take last column, compute $\langle C_{k-1}, v\rangle / g_{\ell-1}$. Crucial: **Homomorphic ops are matrix add and mult**: - $C_{add}=C_1 + C_2 \approx (m_1+m_2) G v$ - $C_{mult}=C_1 \cdot G^{-1}(C_2) \approx m_1 m_2 G v$ with asymmetric noise. --- ## 3. Methodology / Formalism ### 3.1 Asymmetric Noise Growth and Flattening > **Theorem 3.1 (GSW Asymmetric Noise).** Let $C_1 v = m_1 v + e_1$, $C_2 v = m_2 v + e_2$ with $||e_i||_\infty \le E_i$, $||G^{-1}(C_2)||_\infty \le 1$, $|m_1| \le B_1$. Then for $C_\times = C_1 G^{-1}(C_2)$: $$ C_\times v = m_1 m_2 v + m_1 e_2 + C_1 e_{decomp} $$ > where $e_{decomp}=0$ exact if $G G^{-1}=I$ and more generally $||C_\times v - m_1 m_2 v|| \le O(B_1 E_2 + E_1 \cdot n\ell)$. *Proof.* Use decomposition: $C_2 = G G^{-1}(C_2)$, so $C_2 v = G G^{-1}(C_2) v = m_2 v + e_2$. Multiply by $C_1$: noise bound via $||C_1||$ small. Detailed bound in GSW Sec 3.3. $\square$ **Interpretation:** noise multiplier $B_1 = |m_1|$ is small ($\in \{0,1\}$ for bits) for one factor, while other factor contributes through low-norm decomposition. Thus $E_{mult} \approx (n \ell) E_1 + B_1 E_2$ — *linear* in $E$ if multiplying many ciphertexts sequentially keeping small message on right. Contrast BGV where both factors symmetric and noise quadratic: $E_{BGV} \approx O(E^2)$. Flattening operation $Flat(a) = G^{-1}(G a)$ reduces coefficients to binary, preventing blow-up. GSW defines $C = Flat(m I + BitDecomp(C'))$ maintaining invariant $||C||_\infty \le 1$. **NAND vs Matrix View:** - BGV/BFV think in *arithmetic circuits*: $+$ and $\times$ over $\mathbb{Z}_t$. - GSW thinks in *Boolean circuits*: NAND gate $ \overline{x_1 \land x_2} = 1 - x_1 x_2$ suffices for universality, AND-OR depth corresponds to multiplicative depth but messages $\{0,1\}$ keep $B_1=1$. Hence leveled GSW can evaluate depth-$L$ Boolean circuit with noise $O((n\ell)^L)$ vs BGV noise $O((n)^{2^L})$ doubly exponential? Actually GSW polynomial in $L$, BGV exponential without modulus switching. ### 3.2 Complexity Comparison Table *We use quantifiers $n$ LWE dimension, $N$ ring dimension, $\ell=log q$, $Q$ big modulus.* | Scheme | Ciphertext form | Mult cost | Noise mult bound | Relinearization? | Leveled depth bound | |--------|-----------------|-----------|------------------|------------------|---------------------| | **BGV** | $(c_0,c_1)\in R_q^2$ | $O(N log N)$ NTT + reline $O(N \ell)$ | $E' \le O(t (E_1 B_2 + E_2 B_1) + E_1 E_2)$ quadratic | Yes, evk size $O(N \ell)$ | $L \le O(log q / log tB)$ mod-switch needed | | **BFV** | same | same | $E' \le O(\Delta(E_1 B_2 +...) + ...)$ | Yes | similar | | **GSW** | $C \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{(n+1)\times (n+1)\ell}$ | $O(n^3 \ell^2)$ matrix $\times$ binary $O(n^2 \ell)$ | $E' \le O(B_1 E_2 + n\ell E_1)$ *asymmetric linear* | No — uses $G^{-1}$ flattening | $L \le q / poly(n)^L$ polynomial growth, no reline | | **RGSW** | $2\ell$ RLWE samples | $O(N log N \ell^2)$ external prod | $E'_{RGSW \times RLWE} \le B_{RLWE} E_{RGSW} + E_{RLWE}$ | No | Ternary gate friendly | | **TFHE** | LWE + RGSW boot key $BK_i \in RGSW(s_i)$ | $O(n N log Q)$ blind rotate | refreshed $Var\approx n N \vartheta_{BK}$ independent of depth | key-switch $O(N log Q)$ | unbounded via boot per gate, $<0.1$s | Takeaway: *BGV pays relinearization and symmetric quadratic blow-up; GSW pays larger ciphertext $O(n^2 \ell)$ but eliminates relinearization and yields asymmetric slow growth, which AP/FHEW bootstrapping exploits to keep decryption circuit low-depth.* ### 3.3 Bootstrapping Complexity Overview Bootstrapping = homomorphic decryption. For LWE $b - \langle a,s\rangle = \lfloor q/2\rfloor m + e$, decryption function $f_{s}(a,b) = \text{round}(b - \sum a_i s_i)$. Its *depth* determines if leveled scheme can bootstrap itself. Brakerski-Vaikuntanathan applied Barrington's theorem: any depth-$d$ NAND circuit can be represented as branching program length $4^d$. Under LWE, branching program evaluation consists of repeated matrix multiplies $P = \prod M_{i, select(s_i)}$ where $M \in \{0,1\}^{w\times w}$ small width $w=5$. Noise grows as $O(w \cdot \ell \cdot 4^d)$. For decryption depth $O(log q + log n)$, they bound $L_{dec} = O(\lambda)$ quasi-polynomial noise — polynomial if $q=poly(n)$. Alperin-Sheriff-Peikert (AP14) improved by viewing decryption as *rounded inner product* rather than Boolean circuit: map to group $ \mathbb{Z}_{2n}$ and compute $ \sum a_i s_i$ in exponent. Bottleneck is *homomorphic accumulator*: $$ ACC_0 = g^{\lfloor q/2\rfloor} ; \quad ACC_{i+1}= ACC_i \cdot g^{a_i s_i} $$ using group homomorphism $i \mapsto X^i$ into cyclotomic ring $\mathcal{R}= \mathbb{Z}_Q[X]/(X^N+1)$ where $X^{2N}= -1$. Then rounding extracted by test polynomial. FHEW implemented this over rings: $ACC \in RGSW$, initialized $ACC \leftarrow X^{b + Q/8} \cdot G$, iterated $ACC \leftarrow ACC \boxdot BK_i$ where $\boxdot$ = external product $RLWE \times RGSW \to RLWE$. Final $ACC$ coefficient holds $m$. Complexity per boot: $n$ CMux (approx $n$ external prods) each $O(N log N \ell)$, so $O(n N log N)$. With $n=630$, $N=1024$, $<1$s on single core; TFHE optimization reduces to $<0.1$s via FFT over $\mathbb{T}$, approximate decomposition, and key unrolling. --- ## 4. Deep Dive ### 4.1 LWE to RGSW: External Product and Key Switching TFHE distinguishes three levels: - **LWE** over $\mathbb{Z}_q$: $(a,b) \in \mathbb{T}^n \times \mathbb{T}$, encrypt $m \in \{0,1\}$, phase $\phi = b - \langle a,s\rangle$ - **RLWE** over $\mathbb{T}_N = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$: $(\mathbf{a},b)$, $b = \langle a,s\rangle + e + \mu$, $\mu = m/4$ encoding. - **RGSW** encrypting bit $s_i$: pair of RLWE samples forming matrix s.t. $C_{RGSW} \times RLWE = Enc(m_{RLWE} \cdot m_{RGSW})$ external product. External product definition: $$ \boxdot : RLWE(\mu_1) \times RGSW(\mu_2) \to RLWE(\mu_1 \mu_2) $$ Implemented as $ \bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell} Decomp_k(c) \cdot C_k $ where $Decomp$ is base-$B_g$ decomposition $B_g = 2^7$ typical, $\ell \approx 3$. **Key switching / Sample extraction:** After blind rotation we have $RLWE(\mu)$ where $\mu(X) = X^{m'}$ rotation. *SampleExtract* converts RLWE to LWE by extracting coefficients: $LWE_i = (coeff_i(a), coeff_i(b))$ encrypts $\mu_i$ under expanded secret $s' = coeffs of RLWE key. This requires $KSK$ to switch back to original LWE key: $KS_{s' \to s}(c) = c + \sum g^{-1}(a_i) \cdot KSK_i$. This pipeline completes one boot cycle, reducing error from $O(q)$ to $Var \approx 2^{-25}$ independent of input noise, provided $||e|| < Q/4$. #### Python Reference: Gadget Decomposition ```python import math def gadget_decomp(a, Q, Bg, l): # Decompose a in [0,Q) into base Bg digits in [-Bg/2, Bg/2] decomp = [] for _ in range(l): a_i = a % Bg if a_i > Bg//2: a_i -= Bg a = (a + Bg - (a_i % Bg)) // 1 else: a = a // Bg decomp.append(a_i) if a == 0: break return decomp def gsw_mult(C1, C2, G_inv): # C1: (n+1) x (n+1)l, C2: same, G_inv: flatten C2_bindec = G_inv(C2) # (n+1)l x (n+1)l binary return C1 @ C2_bindec # O(n^3 l^2) naive # RLWE external product sketch def external_product(rlwe, rgsw, Bg, l): Q = 2**64 decomp = [gadget_decomp(c, Q, Bg, l) for c in rlwe] acc = [0]*2 for k in range(l): acc[0] += decomp[0][k]*rgsw[k][0] acc[1] += decomp[1][k]*rgsw[k][1] return acc ``` #### Rust Reference: Blind Rotation Loop ```rust // TFHE-rs inspired blind rotation fn blind_rotate( acc: &mut GlweCiphertext, bsk: &[GgswCiphertext], lwe_a: &[Scalar], mod_switch_delta: usize, ) { for (i, bski) in bsk.iter().enumerate() { let a_i = modulus_switch(lwe_a[i], 2*acc.poly_size()); Construction: 1. Encode plaintext space $\mathcal{M} = \{0,...,p-1\}$ via $ \tilde{m}= \lfloor 2N m / p \rfloor$ mapping to exponent domain $[0,2N)$. 2. Build negacyclic polynomial $TV_f(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f(\lfloor p i /2N\rfloor) X^i \in \mathbb{T}_N$. Due to negacyclicity $X^{N+i} = -X^i$, function must satisfy anti-periodicity $f(x+p/2) = -f(x)$ for torus encoding; padding tricks lift to even dimension with $1$ extra bit loss. 3. Blind rotation: $ACC = X^{- \tilde{b} + \sum \tilde{a_i} s_i} \cdot TV_f$ rotates $TV_f$ so coefficient 0 = $f(m)$. 4. Extract constant term yields LWE encrypting $f(m)$ with fresh noise $\approx 2^{-20}$. Thus PBS evaluates *any* $f: \mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ (or $\mathbb{T}$) during boot, at same cost as identity boot. Composing multiple PBS allows evaluation of arbitrary arithmetic circuit of gates $f_i$ with depth unlimited: each PBS resets noise. Applications: 6-bit precision int operations via CRT packing, ReLU via $f(x)=\max(0,x)$, sigmoid via $e^{-x}$ LUT, homomorphic comparison via sign function $f(x)=1_{x>0}$. Constraints: precision loss — e.g., $N=1024$ offers $\approx 6-8$ bits clean payload before rounding error due to polynomial approximation of rotation modulo $2N$. Larger $N=2048$ gives $8-11$ bits at cost $O(N log N)$. Multi-output PBS uses same accumulator to batch $k$ LUTs $TV_{f_1},...,TV_{f_k}$ sharing blind rotation, amortizing $O(n N log N + k N)$. TFHE-rs parameters concrete: $n_{LWE}=742$, $N_{GLWE}=1024$, $Q\approx 2^{64}$, base $B_g=2^{23}$, levels $\ell=1$ for bootstrapping key, failure prob $2^{-64}$, boot latency $6-12$ ms (CPU), $0.3$ ms (GPU). --- ### 4.3 Modulus Switching and Circuit Bootstrapping *Modulus switching* is the glue between worlds: - LWE $q=2^{32}$ lives large for security, but blind rotation requires exponent $2N$ where $N \in [1024,2048]$ to keep $X^{a_i}$ small. So map $a_i \mapsto \tilde{a_i} = \lfloor 2N a_i / q \rceil$. - When moving RLWE $\to$ LWE via sample extract, key-switching adds error $E_{KS} \approx N \cdot \ell_{KS} \cdot \vartheta_{KSK}$ where $\vartheta_{KSK}$ variance of key-switching key noise. **Circuit bootstrapping** lifts LWE to RGSW: given LWE $c$, produce RGSW($m$) consisting of $\ell$ RLWE encryptions of $m \cdot B_g^{-k}$. This allows leveled gates to output RGSW, which can be fed into PBS external product without further boot. Alg: run $ \ell$ programmable boots with $TV_{k}(x)= x \cdot B_g^{-k}$ LUTs, then assemble. Thus full TFHE circuit evaluates arbitrary Boolean or arithmetic gates: LWE ciphertexts represent bits or small ints, gates are PBS, multiplication uses RGSW $\boxdot$ RLWE leveled ($O(N log N)$), final refresh via circuit boot when noise accumulates. **Negative Example:** naive boot after every NAND multiplies keys $BK_i$ of size $n \times N \log Q \approx 24$ MB vs AP original $1$ GB via torus optimization and approximate gadget decomp with error bounded by $E_{appr} \approx Q / B_g^{\ell+1}$ tolerated. --- ## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs ### 5.1 Theoretical Proof of Unbounded Depth via Bootstrapping > **Theorem 5.1 (Gentry).** If E is *bootstrappable*: its decryption circuit $D_s$ plus one NAND is in supported circuit family $\mathcal{C}_E$, then E can be transformed to fully homomorphic scheme E* supporting unbounded depth, assuming circular security of Enc(s). *Proof Sketch.* Let $pk,sk$ base leveled with $L \ge depth(D)+1$. Publish $bk = Enc_{pk}(sk)$. Given ct tired (noise near threshold), compute $ct^* = Eval_{pk}(D_{(.), ct}, bk)$ homomorphically evaluating $Dec_{sk}(ct)$ inside encryption. Since $Dec$ outputs plaintext bit, its homomorphic evaluation yields fresh encryption of same plaintext with reduced noise $E_{fresh} \ll Q/4$ because evaluation done on mostly-fresh encryptions of $sk$ bits. Composition maintains correctness if $E_{fresh}+E_{eval}(D) < Q/4$. Iterated => arbitrary composition. $\square$ GSW family fulfills premise because its decryption is shallow dot product $\langle a,s\rangle$ (inner product of dimension $n=630$, $log q$ bit numbers) followed by rounding — depth $O(log n + log log q)$ representable as width-5 branching program, hence in GSW-boostrappable class per AP14 theorem. ### 5.2 Microbenchmarks We synthesize measurements from Ducas-Micciancio prototype, TFHE lib, and OpenFHE/TFHE-rs 2024. | Stage | Operation count per boot | Latency CPU | Noise output Var | Key size | Precision | |-------|--------------------------|-------------|-----------------|----------|-----------| | FHEW 2015 $n=630,N=1024$ | $630$ CMux + $1$ rounding | $~600ms$ | $2^{-15}$ | $1$ GB boot key | 1 bit | | TFHE 2016 torus fast boot | $630$ external prods $B_g=1024, \ell=3$, $N=1024$ FFT 64-bit doubles | $~13ms$ 1-core | $2^{-24}$ | $24$ MB | 1 bit + 1 bit negacyclic loss | | TFHE-rs 2024 programmable | $742$ CMux + testvec NTT 2048 $B_g=2^{23}, \ell=1$ | $6.5$ ms PBS | $2^{-20}$ | $16$ MB BK + $12$ MB KSK | $8$-bit LUT | | BGV/TFHE hybrid BTFHE | LWE->RGSW via $4$ PBS | $~80$ ms circuit boot | $2^{-18}$ | $~80$ MB | 4-bit to RGSW | | Free module decoupling $q,N$ | $\tau=4$ module rank | $~40\%$ faster PBS | $2^{-32}$ 32-bit prec | $40$ MB | 32-bit claimed | Interpretation: third-gen GSW/TFHE boot achieves $50\times$ speed-up over HElib bootstrapping which required $~6$ minutes per batch, via *gate bootstrapping* paradigm where each binary gate costs one boot but boot itself cheap, vs *amortized* BGV/BFV where boot expensive but handles $N$ SIMD slots. ### 5.3 Advantages and Trade-offs - **Pros for GSW/TFHE bootstrapping:** - *Unbounded depth without parameter tuning*: any circuit's depth can be accommodated by inserting boots. - *Functional bootstrap free*: evaluate arbitrary $f$ during refresh;
