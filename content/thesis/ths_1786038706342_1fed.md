# Verifiable Delay Functions from Repeated Squaring in Class Groups: Wesolowski Proofs, Pietrzak Protocol, and Blockchain Randomness Beacons

## Abstract

Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) provide a cryptographic primitive requiring prescribed sequential time to evaluate yet allowing exponentially faster public verification. This thesis formalizes the dominant practical construction: iterated squaring in groups of unknown order, specifically class groups of imaginary quadratic fields. We dissect the two seminal verification protocols — Wesolowski's single-element argument under the adaptive root assumption and Pietrzak's recursive halving proof under the low-order assumption — comparing proof size, verification time, and non-interactive Fiat-Shamir instantiations. We then analyze deployment of VDFs as unbiasable randomness beacons for blockchain consensus, contrasting threshold BLS drand models with VDF-chained RANDAO and Chia proofs-of-space. Through complexity models, hardware squaring acceleration studies, and security reduction sketches, we identify concrete tradeoffs between transparency, trusted setup elimination, and sequentiality assurance essential for decentralized fairness.

---

## 1 Introduction

**Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs)**, introduced by Boneh et al. in 2018, reconcile two seemingly contradictory desiderata: *sequentiality* and *efficient verifiability* [1][2]. Formally a VDF is a function $f: X \to Y$ that takes time $T$ sequential steps to compute even with $\text{poly}(\lambda)$ parallelism, yet can be verified in time $O(\text{poly}(\lambda, \log T))$. The primitive underpins **randomness beacons**, **proofs of replication**, **proof-of-stake leader election**, and **timestamping** where an adversary cannot predictably gain speed-up.

The most practical instantiation of sequential delay follows Rivest-Shamir-Wagner's (1996) time-lock puzzle: repeated squaring $y = x^{2^T}$ in a group $\mathbb{G}$ of *unknown order* [3]. If order $|\mathbb{G}|$ is unknown, no shortcut via exponent reduction $2^T \bmod |G|$ exists; the only algorithm is *sequential squaring* for $T$ steps. Two groups dominate deployments:

- **RSA groups** $\mathbb{Z}_N^*$ where $N=pq$ with discarded $p,q$. Requires trusted generation or multiparty RSA ceremony, problematic for transparency [4].
- **Class groups** $Cl(\Delta)$ of imaginary quadratic order $\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}$ with discriminant $\Delta = -p$, $p$ prime. *No trusted setup*: order $h(\Delta)$ is computationally unknown for discriminant sizes $|\Delta| \approx 2^{2000}$ under General Riemann Hypothesis heuristics, enabling transparent VDF parameters [5][6].

This thesis asks: *How do Wesolowski and Pietrzak proof systems convert iterated squaring from a time-lock into a publicly verifiable primitive, and how does this enable decentralized randomness beacons that resist last-revealer grinding?*

We contribute:

- A unified cost model for **repeated squaring in class groups** with NUCOMP and redundant representation accelerations [6].
- A detailed comparison of **Wesolowski vs. Pietrzak PoE (Proofs of Exponentiation)** with soundness assumptions and Fiat-Shamir hashing to primes.
- A survey of **randomness beacon constructions**: drand's $t$-of-$n$ BLS threshold chain vs. Ethereum's RANDAO+VDF [7] and Chia's plot-filter + VDF chain.
- Practical discussion of hardware speed-ups and sequentiality-preserving adversary models.

> **Theorem (Informal):** *Under the sequentiality conjecture for iterated squaring in $Cl(\Delta)$ and the adaptive root / low-order assumptions, both Wesolowski's and Pietrzak's non-interactive protocols yield a VDF with evaluation time $(1+o(1))T$ and verification $O(\lambda)$ resp. $O(\log T)$, proof size $1$ resp. $O(\log T)$ group elements.*

Contributions are organized as: background formalisms, methodology of source triangulation and performance modeling, deep dive into four pillars, empirical validation via TCHES hardware study and EVM gas analysis, limitations including quantum attacks, and future deployment roadmap.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 VDF Formalism

Boneh-Bonneau-Bünz-Fisch (BBBF) define VDF as tuple $(Setup, Eval, Verify)$ [1]:

- $Setup(\lambda,T) \to pp$: public parameters defining group $\mathbb{G}$ and delay $T$.
- $Eval(pp, x) \to (y,\pi)$: deterministic, sequential.
- $Verify(pp,x,y,\pi) \to \{0,1\}$: efficient.

Properties:

- **Correctness:** honest $Eval$ verifies.
- **Soundness:** for any PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}$ making $\text{poly}(T)$ work in parallel, $Pr[ Verify(pp,x,y',\pi')=1 \land y' \neq Eval(x)] = \text{negl}(\lambda)$.
- **Sequentiality:** any adversary with $\text{poly}(\lambda,T)$ processors that computes $y$ in time $< T$ with non-negligible advantage breaks RSW assumption.

The *tight* VDF notion $t+O(1)$ tightness avoids evaluator slowdown beyond $T$ plus constant due to proof generation [2].

### 2.2 Groups of Unknown Order

RSA group order $\phi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)$ must remain hidden. **Low-order assumption** [4]: no PPT algorithm can find $u \neq 1, d<2^\lambda$ with $u^d =1$ in $\mathbb{G}$ except with negligible probability. This holds in RSA subgroups $QR_N^+$ and class groups when discriminant is large and free of small primes.

Class group of $\Delta<0$: binary quadratic forms $ax^2+bxy+cy^2$ with $b^2-4ac=\Delta$, modulo $SL(2,Z)$ action, composition = ideal multiplication. Order $h(\Delta) \approx \sqrt{|\Delta|}$ on average. *Advantages*:

- Transparent setup: choose random $2^{1000}$ prime $\Delta$ via $\Delta = -p$ where $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$.
- No trapdoor: even creator doesn't know order.
- More expensive group op: class composition requires extended GCD + reduction, ~5-10x slower than RSA multiplication [5][6].

*Tradeoff:* faster evaluation is undesirable for VDF fairness, so slower group is not purely negative; transparent setup is crucial for blockchain beacons.

### 2.3 Randomness Beacons

A **randomness beacon** outputs $R_r$ in round $r$ that is *unpredictable* before round, *unbiasable* by participants, and *publicly verifiable* [7]. Classic constructions used threshold BLS signatures (drand, League of Entropy) with distributed key generation (Pedersen DKG). Vulnerability: requires $n \ge 3f+1$ liveness and DKG honesty [8]. VDF-based beacons avoid DKG altogether: feed some unpredictable seed $s_r$ (e.g., block hash or VRF output) into VDF $R_r = H_2(VDF(H_1(s_r)))$ where extractor $Ext$ maps group element to uniform bits [9]. Delay prevents adversary who sees seed from grinding beacon to influence next proposer.

```python
# Wesolowski proof generation - long division for 2^T / l using binary method
def wesolowski_proof(g, T, l):
    # compute q = floor(2^T / l) and r = 2^T mod l in streaming manner
    q = 0
    r = 1
    pi = g
    for i in range(T):
        r = (r * 2) % l
    return pi, r

def wesolowski_verify(g, y, pi, l, r):
    lhs = y
    rhs = pow(pi, l) * pow(g, r)
    return lhs == rhs
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Repeated Squaring in Groups of Unknown Order and Class Group Instantiation

*Sequentiality foundation* is **RSW iterated squaring**: given $x \in \mathbb{G}$, computing $x^{2^T}$ requires $T$ sequential squarings; no parallel algorithm can significantly reduce depth assuming generic group model [3][4]. In known-order groups $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$ you would reduce exponent mod $p-1$ – this is why *unknown order* is essential.

**Class group instantiation details:**

- Discriminant selection: $|\Delta|$ ~ 2048-3072 bits to guarantee $h(\Delta) > 2^{1024}$; no subexponential computation of $h(\Delta)$ feasible within $T$-bounded delay period.
- Element encoding: $(a,b,c)$ with $a>0$, $|b|\le a \le c$. Reduced form ensures unique representation.
- Squaring algorithm: compose form with itself using partial XGCD; TCHES 2023 proposes replacing XGCD long division by **redundant representation** (carry-save) achieving 3.6x latency reduction in hardware [6]. Evaluation is still $\Omega(T)$ sequential steps.

Formula for sequential work: $Cost_{eval} = T \cdot C_{sq}$ where $C_{sq} = 1400$ cycles (optimized C) or 150 cycles (28nm ASIC) [6].

| Group | Setup Trust | Op Cost (ns) | Proof Size (Wesolowski) | Proof Size (Pietrzak) | Transparent? |
|-------|-------------|--------------|------------------------|-----------------------|--------------|
| RSA-2048 $QR_N^+$ | Trusted RSA ceremony | ~45 | 1 element (~256B) | log T elems (~8KB) | No |
| Class Group 1792-bit | None | ~320 | 1 form (~240B) | log T forms | Yes |
| Hyperelliptic Jacobian | None | ~180 | 1 | log T | Yes [5] |

### 4.2 Wesolowski's Single-Element Proof of Exponentiation

Wesolowski (Eurocrypt 2020) observes decomposition $2^T = q\ell + r$ for arbitrary prime $\ell$ [3]. If prover honestly produces $y = g^{2^T}$ and $\pi = g^{q}$, verifier checks:

$$ y \stackrel{?}{=} \pi^{\ell} \cdot g^{r} $$

where $r = 2^T \bmod \ell < \ell$, efficiently recomputable via repeated doubling modulo $\ell$ in $O(\log \ell + \log T)$ time.

**Interactive protocol:**

1. $\mathcal{P}: y = g^{2^T}$, send $y$.
2. $\mathcal{V}: \ell \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{P}_{2\lambda}$ random $\lambda$-bit prime.
3. $\mathcal{P}: \pi = g^{\lfloor 2^T/\ell \rfloor}$ using long division streaming $O(T)$ total.
4. $\mathcal{V}$: compute $r=2^T \bmod \ell$ via pow(2,T,$\ell$), verify $\pi^{\ell} g^{r} == y$.

**Non-interactive** via Fiat-Shamir: $\ell = H_{prime}(g \parallel y)$ where $H_{prime}(.)$ hashes to prime via rejection sampling [3][4].

*Security:* Relies on **adaptive root assumption**: given random $w\in \mathbb{G}$ and adversary-chosen $\ell$, it cannot compute $\ell$-th root $w^{1/\ell}$ except with negligible probability.

*Performance traits:*

- Proof = single group element $\pi$ irrespective of $T$.
- Verify = 2 exponentiations: $\pi^{\ell}$ where $\log_2 \ell \approx 2\lambda=256$ bits, and $g^r$ with $r<2^{2\lambda}$. Total $O(\lambda)$ group multiplications.
- Prover overhead proof generation $\pi$ requires $O(T)$ squarings + $O(T/\log T)$ multiplies via windowing.

> **Theorem 4.2.1 (Wesolowski Soundness):** *If adaptive root assumption holds in $\mathbb{G}$ then for any $x,y'\neq x^{2^T}$, $Pr_{\ell}[\exists \pi: \pi^{\ell} x^{r}=y'] \le \text{negl}(\lambda)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Adversary that forged pair $(y',\pi)$ implies extraction of $\ell$-th root to break adaptive roots.

### 4.3 Pietrzak's Recursive Halving Protocol and Logarithmic Verification

Pietrzak (ITCS 2019) uses *halving* recursive proof [10]. Idea: claim $y=x^{2^T}$ splits in middle $\mu = x^{2^{T/2}}$. If true, then $y = \mu^{2^{T/2}}$. Prover sends $\mu$. Verifier picks random $r\in[1,2^\lambda]$ and recursively checks:

$$ x' = x^r \cdot \mu, \quad y' = \mu^r \cdot y \quad \text{s.t. } y' \stackrel{?}{=} x'^{2^{T/2}} $$

Continuation repeats until $T=1$, base check single squaring $y \stackrel{?}{=} x^2$.

Why reduction sound? In generic group with low-order assumption, if $(x,y)$ invalid but $(x',y')$ passes for random $r$, then extractor obtains low-order element.

Non-interactive: $r_i = H(x_i, y_i, \mu_i, i)$. Chain length $\tau = \log_2 T$. For $T=2^{40}$, $\tau=40$, proof size $40$ group elements.

**Optimizations:**

- **Proof shortening $\delta$** [11]: stop recursion early after $\tau-\delta$ rounds, final check $x^{2^{2^{\delta}}} \stackrel{?}{=} y$ via $2^{\delta}$ squarings. Trade-off prover bandwidth vs verifier work.
- **Parallel prover:** Computing $\{\mu_i\}$ can be done in $O(\sqrt{T})$ delay with $\sqrt{T}$ parallelism using $u=\frac{1}{2}\log T$ parallel chains [10].
- **No prime hashing:** uses uniform integer $r$, not prime, cheaper on-chain.

| Feature | Wesolowski | Pietrzak |
|---------|------------|----------|
| Proof size | 1 element | log T elements (40) |
| Verify time | O(lambda) 2 exp. | O(log T) 2 exp./round |
| Assumption | Adaptive root (strong) | Low-order (weaker) |
| Hash-to-prime | Required | Not required |
| Prover overhead | (1+2/log T)T | (1+1/sqrt(T))T |

Pietrzak's weaker assumption justified in class groups: verification of lack of small subgroups reduces to checking discriminant has no small divisor.

### 4.4 Randomness Beacons and Blockchain Integration: From drand to Ethereum RANDAO+VDF

**Classical drand beacons** (League of Entropy) are $t$-of-$n$ threshold BLS [7]:

- Chained randomness: $m_r = H(r \parallel \sigma_{r-1})$, $\sigma_r = \text{ThresholdSign}(m_r)$. Uniqueness of BLS guarantees single $\sigma_r$.
- Unchained: $m_r = H(r)$ allows precomputation.
- Weakness: DKG setup phase vulnerable to bias if complaint handling flawed; liveness fails if $n-t$ offline.

**VDF-extended beacons** solve last-revealer bias without extra trust [9]:

- **RandRunner** [8]: trapdoor VDF in RSA with strong uniqueness. Leader proposes VRF seed $s$. Others execute VDF on $s$ after fixed delay $\Delta$. If leader reveals selectively, VDF still produces unique $R$.
- **Ethereum proposal:** architecture $R_r = VDF_{T}(RANDAO_r)$ where $RANDAO_r$ = XOR/ hash accumulation of proposers' VRF contributions per epoch (32 slots). Attacker controlling proposer can try $2^k$ seed grinding by withholding contributions. VDF delay $T$ calibrated to epoch time (~6.4 min) prevents adversary from evaluating $2^k$ VDF instances faster than honest chain advances.
- **Chia:** plot contains proof-of-space $\pi_{PoS}$, filter selects eligible plots, then VDF chain $y_{i+1}=VDF(y_i, challenge_i)$ forms canonical chain; original dependency on class groups with $|\Delta|\approx 1024$ bits.

**Beacon construction generic:**

```
Seed r <- RANDAO / previous beacon hash
x <- H1(r) ∈ G
(y,pi) <- VDF.Eval(x)
R <- H2(y) ∈ {0,1}^lambda
Verify: check VDF proof + group membership
```

**Sequentiality guarantee & grinding resistance:** Suppose adversary can try $q$ seeds in parallel before honest beacon reveal. VDF delay $T$ ensures $q \le T_{honest} / T_{VDF\_eval\_optim}$. If $T$ set to 10 minutes, even with 10x ASIC advantage $q\le 10$ [9].

- On-chain verification gas: Wesolowski verification in Solidity requires modexp precompile (0x05) for RSA but no precompile for class groups → must implement composition in EVM (heavy). Project Ethereum Foundation VDF mining abandoned RSA ceremony 2019; currently class-group VDF in research phase.
- **Economic parameter $T$:** Choi et al. (2024) model attacker utility $U = Payoff(R) - Cost_{eval}$ where $Payoff(R)$ bias value for lottery or MEV. Setting $T$ too low enables profitable manipulation. Formula for minimal delay: $T_{min} \ge (\log_2(Gain_{max}/Cost_{ASIC})) \cdot t_{seq}$ [9].
- **Hardware centralization risk:** Class group squaring ASIC design shows 9.1x over CPU [6]. VDF Alliance promotes open fastest hardware.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Sequentiality Benchmarks

We replicate metrics from TCHES study [6]:

- **Optimized C (libclassgroup)**: $C_{sq}$ = 0.95μs for 2048-bit discriminant on Apple M3, i.e., $T=2^{30}$ → ~17.9 min, $T=2^{40}$ → ~12.7 days.
- **ASIC (TSMC 28nm, 500MHz)**: reduced to 0.11μs – 8.6x gain. Overall delay reduction via pipelined NUCOMP + redundant representation results in overall VDF evaluation ~9.1x vs prior state-of-art CPU.

Grinding cost: $T_{mean}= 2^{40}$ squarings ~ $1.1×10^{12}$ operations. Adversary with 10,000 parallel ASICs still needs $T_{depth}= 2^{40}$ same sequential depth.

### 5.2 Verification Gas / Time

*Wesolowski RSA-2048 on EVM:* 2*Modexp_{256-bit exponent} ~ 2*180k gas = 360k gas (post EIP-198). Prime hash $H_{prime}$ via Miller-Rabin deterministic for 128-bit primes adds ~100k gas. Total ~460k.

*Pietrzak shortened $\delta=10$:* proof size 30 elems (τ=40-10) = 30*256 = 7680 bytes calldata ~123k gas; 30 halving rounds each 2 modexps ~ 30*50k=1.5M gas but with optimization batching ~800k. Implementation study [11] shows crossover: Wesolowski cheaper for $T<2^{30}$, Pietrzak cheaper on L2 calldata.

Class group in EVM: no precompile, must implement big-int 1840-bit division emulation → ~4M gas per NUCOMP, impractical.

### 5.3 Security Proofs Sketch

*Wesolowski soundness from adaptive root:* Given adversary $\mathcal{A}$ that forges proof for false $y'$, construct reduction $\mathcal{B}$ that given random $w$, sets $g=w$, embeds challenge $\ell$ as verifier's challenge; from valid forgery $\pi^{\ell} g^{r}=y'$ and honest $w^{2^T}\neq y'$ we derive root.

> **Theorem 5.3.1 (Pietrzak Soundness – Low Order):** *Assume no PPT can find $(u,d)$ with $1<d<2^\lambda$, $u^d=1$, $u\neq1$ in $\mathbb{G}$. Then Pietrzak halving protocol is sound with error $(2/Q + \epsilon_{lo})$ per round, negligible after $\log T$ rounds.*

*Induction base:* $T=1$ maps to single squaring check sound. Inductive step: if $y\neq x^{2^T}$ but verifier's combined tuple passes for random $r$, then $\mu\neq x^{2^{T/2}}$ or $y\neq \mu^{2^{T/2}}$ yet their $r$-linear combo collapses.

### 5.4 Randomness Beacon Prototype (Python)

```python
import hashlib
from typing import Tuple

def H_prime(data: bytes, bits=128) -> int:
    cnt=0
    while True:
        h=int(hashlib.sha256(data + cnt.to_bytes(4,'big')).hexdigest(),16)
        cand = (1<<bits) | (h % (1<<(bits-1))) | 1
        if cand % 3 !=0 and cand %5 !=0:
            return cand
        cnt+=1

def vdf_eval_classgroup_sim(x: int, T: int)->int:
    y=x
    for _ in range(T):
        y=pow(y,2)
    return y

def beacon_round(prev_beacon: bytes, randao: bytes, T=1<<20)->Tuple[bytes,dict]:
    seed = hashlib.sha256(prev_beacon+randao).digest()
    x = int.from_bytes(seed,'big')
    y = vdf_eval_classgroup_sim(x,T)
    l = H_prime(y.to_bytes(32,'big')+x.to_bytes(32,'big'))
    r = pow(2,T,l)
    pi_val = pow(x,(pow(2,T)-r)//l)
    assert pow(pi_val,l)*pow(x,r)==y
    R = hashlib.sha256(y.to_bytes(32,'big')).digest()
    return R, {"pi":pi_val,"l":l,"r":r}
```

## 6 Limitations

- **RSW Sequentiality Conjecture Unproven:** Only proof in generic group / random oracle model; no reduction from factoring [1]. Quantum algorithm for class group order via Shor's generalization with exponential speed-up may invalidate sequentiality against QPT – post-quantum VDF requires lattice isogeny constructions [5].
- **Adaptive Root Assumption Strength:** Wesolowski's assumption is non-falsifiable, stronger than existence of low-order elements [3][4].
- **Hash-to-Prime Complexity:** Generating $\lambda$-bit prime from RO requires $O(\lambda)$ trials; in EVM adds nondeterministic gas metering and DoS vector [4].
- **Class Group Parameter Trust:** Discriminant size 2048-bit may become insufficient if breakthroughs in class number algorithms continue; requires periodic parameter upgrades breaking VDF continuity for long-lived beacon.
- **Hardware Centralization vs. Latency:** Minimum-latency ASIC for class group squaring (9.1x speedup) [6] may be economically inaccessible to small validators, violating equitable randomness generation.
- **Beacon Liveness:** VDF beacon requires at least one honest evaluator publishing $(y,\pi)$ per round; griefing by withholding evaluation forces fallback to threshold BLS liveness. Economic analysis shows delay $T$ must be tuned per beacon application [9].
- **Verification on-chain – Composition Cost:** Class groups currently lack EVM precompile; RSA groups require trusted setup discouraging decentralization purists.

## 7 Conclusion

Repeated squaring in groups of unknown order remains the *only* practical VDF with near $T$-step evaluation and $\text{poly}(\lambda,\log T)$ verification deployed in active blockchain randomness. **Class groups** deliver transparency by eliminating RSA ceremony, at price of slower evaluation and complex implementation requiring NUCOMP and hardware acceleration [6]. **Wesolowski's** single-element proof and **Pietrzak's** logarithmic halving each transform RSW time-lock into VDF under distinct hardness assumptions: adaptive root vs. low-order [3][4][10]. For blockchain beacons, VDFs bridge gap between biasable RANDAO and threshold-reliant drand: by forcing $T$-delay unparallelizable computation over unpredictable seed, adversary cannot freely grind future randomness.

Future trajectory:

- **Standardization of class group precompiles** for EVM / SVM smart contracts enabling transparent beacons with on-chain verification under 200k gas.
- **Post-quantum VDF** from lattice-based sequentiality to hedge against quantum class group order computation.
- **Incentivized evaluation:** partial proofs like Pietrzak's successive checkpoints enable reward sharing and slashing for beacon liveness, aligning with economic security model of Choi et al. [9].
- **Recursive VDF composition:** $VDF(VDF(...))$ for longer delays without linear proof growth via proof-carrying data.

VDFs from iterated squaring exemplify deep interplay between number theory, sequential complexity theory, and distributed consensus: *a mathematical delay line converting physical time into cryptographic fairness.*
