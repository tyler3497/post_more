# FlashAttention and IO-Aware Exact Attention: Online Softmax, SRAM/HBM Tiling, and the Memory-Wall Economics of Transformer Training

## Abstract

Self-attention's quadratic cost is usually narrated as a compute problem; on modern accelerators it is, above all, a *data-movement* problem. Standard attention materializes an $N \times N$ score matrix in high-bandwidth memory (HBM), and the resulting read/write traffic — not floating-point throughput — dominates wall-clock time. This thesis develops a unified account of IO-aware exact attention. We derive the online-softmax recurrences of Milakov and Gimelshein that make stable softmax computable in a single streaming pass; we show how FlashAttention fuses the score GEMM, softmax, and value GEMM into one tiled kernel that never writes the $N \times N$ matrix to HBM, cutting memory from quadratic to linear with exact equivalence; we analyze its HBM traffic of $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$ and prove it optimal for on-chip SRAM size $M \le d^2$; we trace FlashAttention-2's scheduling refinements — sequence-dimension parallelism, delayed rescaling, warp re-partitioning — lifting A100 utilization from 25–40% to 50–73% of peak; and we examine FlashAttention-3's Hopper asynchrony: warp specialization, TMA overlap, and FP8 incoherent processing reaching 740 TFLOPs/s. We close with sequence-parallel extensions and the limits of the IO-aware paradigm.

## 1. Introduction

Transformer training budgets are dominated by a single operator. In a decoder-only language model, self-attention consumes a growing fraction of both memory and time as context length $N$ increases, because its arithmetic and — more importantly — its memory traffic scale quadratically in $N$ [1]. The standard narrative blames the $\Theta(N^2 d)$ floating-point operations. That narrative is incomplete. On an NVIDIA A100, the on-chip SRAM offers roughly **19 TB/s** of bandwidth while HBM offers roughly **2 TB/s** — an order-of-magnitude asymmetry — and on-chip SRAM capacity is only about **20 MB** against **40–80 GB** of HBM [1]. An operator that repeatedly streams large intermediate tensors through HBM will therefore saturate the memory subsystem long before it saturates the tensor cores. *Attention is memory-bound before it is compute-bound.*

The IO-aware program, articulated by Dao et al., reframes kernel design around a simple principle: **count bytes, not FLOPs** [1]. The $N \times N$ matrices $S = QK^\top / \sqrt{d}$ and $P = \mathrm{softmax}(S)$ are never materialized in HBM at all. Instead, $Q$, $K$, $V$ are partitioned into blocks small enough to reside in SRAM; each $K/V$ block is loaded once per $Q$-block row group; and a streaming softmax statistic — a running row-maximum $m$ and running normalizer $\ell$ — absorbs each new score tile and rescales the partial output in place. The technique that makes this possible is the *online normalizer* of Milakov and Gimelshein, which collapses the three memory passes of a numerically stable softmax into one [4]. Fused with recomputation in the backward pass, the design reduces memory from $\Theta(N^2)$ to $\Theta(N)$, and it does so with **zero approximation**: the output is bit-for-bit (up to floating-point reassociation) identical to naive attention.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the GPU memory hierarchy and the roofline model, and derives the IO cost of standard attention. Section 3 presents the methodology: online softmax as a streaming reduction, the tiled forward kernel, and the recomputation-based backward pass. Section 4 deep-dives into four topics: the IO-complexity lower bound; the scheduling evolution from FlashAttention through FlashAttention-2 to FlashAttention-3; Hopper-specific asynchrony; and sequence-parallel attention across devices. Section 5 gives the empirical record and proof sketches. Section 6 states the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The GPU memory hierarchy

A modern GPU exposes a steeply asymmetric hierarchy, summarized below for the two reference platforms of this thesis.

| Level | A100 (Ampere) | H100 (Hopper) | Bandwidth (order) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registers / tensor-core fragments | per-thread | per-thread | tens of TB/s |
| Shared memory (SRAM), per SM | 192 KB | 228 KB | — |
| Aggregate on-chip SRAM | ~20 MB | ~50 MB | ~19 TB/s (A100) |
| L2 cache | 40 MB | 50 MB | ~5–7 TB/s |
| HBM | 40/80 GB | 80 GB HBM3 | ~2 TB/s (A100), ~3 TB/s (H100) |

The crucial ratios are *bandwidth* ($\approx$ 10:1 SRAM:HBM) and *capacity* ($\approx$ 1:4000 SRAM:HBM). An algorithm that moves $\Theta(N^2)$ words through HBM will be throttled by the 2 TB/s pipe. The IO-complexity literature (Hong–Kung red–blue pebbling and its descendants) teaches that the right cost model counts *words moved between slow and fast memory*, not arithmetic operations [1][6].

### 2.2 Standard attention and its IO bill

For one head, let $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$. Standard attention computes

$$S = \frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}, \qquad P = \mathrm{softmax}(S), \qquad O = PV.$$

A framework-level implementation launches three kernels and materializes $S$ and $P$ ($2N^2$ elements) in HBM. The HBM traffic is $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ words: reads of $Q, K, V$, writes and re-reads of $S$ and $P$, and the write of $O$ [1][6]. For $N = 16{,}384$ and $d = 128$, the $S$ matrix alone is $\approx$ 537 MB per head per layer, streamed through HBM multiple times. The softmax and pointwise phases have arithmetic intensity near 1 FLOP/byte — deep in the memory-bound region of the roofline.

### 2.3 Why approximation is not the answer (here)

A large literature — sparse, low-rank, and kernelized attention — reduces asymptotic FLOPs by changing the *function* being computed, often at some cost in model quality and frequently without wall-clock speedup, because sparse access patterns defeat the memory hierarchy [1]. FlashAttention takes the opposite path: it computes **exactly** the same function, faster, by restructuring *where* intermediate values live. Exactness matters practically: it is a drop-in replacement requiring no retraining and no quality validation [2].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Online softmax: a streaming reduction

Numerically stable softmax is usually computed in three passes over a row $x \in \mathbb{R}^B$: find $m = \max_i x_i$; compute $\ell = \sum_i e^{x_i - m}$; write $e^{x_i - m}/\ell$. Milakov and Gimelshein observed that the maximum and the normalizer can be maintained *online* in a single pass [4]. When a new element (or block) $x$ arrives with current state $(m, \ell)$:

> **Theorem (Online normalizer update):** Let $m' = \max(m, x)$ and $\ell' = e^{m - m'}\ell + e^{x - m'}$. Then $(m', \ell')$ is exactly the (max, normalizer) pair of the concatenation of the previous prefix and $x$, and the rescaling factor $e^{m - m'}$ keeps every accumulated exponential numerically bounded.

*Proof sketch.* $e^{x_i - m'} = e^{m - m'} \cdot e^{x_i - m}$ for all previous $i$, so multiplying the old sum by $e^{m-m'}$ converts it to the new reference maximum. Since $m' \ge m$, every exponent $x_i - m' \le 0$ and the multiplier lies in $(0,1]$: no overflow is possible [4][5].

For attention we extend the state with the *unnormalized output accumulator* $A = \sum_j e^{S_{ij} - m} V_j$. The block update becomes

$$m^{(k+1)} = \max\!\left(m^{(k)}, \mathrm{rowmax}(S^{(k)})\right),$$
$$\ell^{(k+1)} = \ell^{(k)} e^{m^{(k)} - m^{(k+1)}} + \mathrm{rowsum}\!\left(e^{S^{(k)} - m^{(k+1)}}\right),$$
$$A^{(k+1)} = A^{(k)} e^{m^{(k)} - m^{(k+1)}} + e^{S^{(k)} - m^{(k+1)}} V^{(k)},$$

with $O_i = A^{(T)}/\ell^{(T)}$ and the stored log-sum-exp $\mathrm{LSE}_i = m^{(T)} + \log \ell^{(T)}$ [1][5]. The reference implementation below is the executable specification against which tiled kernels are validated:

```python
import numpy as np

def online_softmax_attention(Q, K, V, Br=128, Bc=128):
    N, d = Q.shape
    O = np.zeros((N, d)); m = np.full(N, -np.inf); l = np.zeros(N)
    for i in range(0, N, Br):
        Qi = Q[i:i+Br]
        mi = m[i:i+Br].copy(); li = l[i:i+Br].copy()
        Ai = np.zeros((Br, d))
        for j in range(0, N, Bc):
            Sij = Qi @ K[j:j+Bc].T / np.sqrt(d)          # stays in SRAM
            mij_new = np.maximum(mi, Sij.max(axis=1))
            scale = np.exp(mi - mij_new)[:, None]
            Pij = np.exp(Sij - mij_new[:, None])
            li = li * np.exp(mi - mij_new) + Pij.sum(axis=1)
            Ai = Ai * scale + Pij @ V[j:j+Bc]
            mi = mij_new
        O[i:i+Br] = Ai / li[:, None]
        m[i:i+Br], l[i:i+Br] = mi, li
    return O, m + np.log(l)   # O and per-row LSE; S, P never materialized
```

The same rescaling algebra, stated as pure functions, supports equational reasoning about kernel fusion:

```haskell
-- Streaming softmax state: (running max, running normalizer, accumulator)
type OnlineState v = (Double, Double, v)

rescale :: Floating v => OnlineState v -> Double -> OnlineState v
rescale (m, l, acc) m' =
  let c = exp (m - m') in (m', l * c, fmap (* c) acc)

absorbBlock :: (Floating v, Num v) => OnlineState v -> [Double] -> [v] -> OnlineState v
absorbBlock (m, l, acc) xs vs =
  let m'  = maximum (m : xs)
      (m'', l', acc') = rescale (m, l, acc) m'
      w   = map (\x -> exp (x - m'')) xs
  in (m'', l' + sum w, acc' `addScaled` zipWith scale w vs)
```

### 3.2 The tiled forward kernel

The forward kernel partitions $Q$ into $T_r = \lceil N/B_r \rceil$ row-blocks and $K, V$ into $T_c = \lceil N/B_c \rceil$ column-blocks, with $B_c = \lceil M/4d \rceil$ and $B_r = \min(\lceil M/4d \rceil, d)$ chosen so that the $Q$-, $K$-, $V$-blocks and the $B_r \times B_c$ score tile all fit in SRAM simultaneously [1]. The outer loop over $Q$-blocks maps to thread blocks; the inner loop streams $K/V$ blocks from HBM into SRAM; only $O \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ and $m, \ell \in \mathbb{R}^N$ are written back — memory $\Theta(N)$ instead of $\Theta(N^2)$:

```rust
// Tiled IO-aware attention forward. SRAM-resident tiles are annotated.
fn flash_forward(Q: &[f16], K: &[f16], V: &[f16], O: &mut [f16],
                N: usize, d: usize, Br: usize, Bc: usize) {
    for i in (0..N).step_by(Br) {
        let q: Tile<Br, D> = hbm_load(&Q[i*d..]);          // SRAM
        let (mut m, mut l, mut acc) = init_stats(Br);      // SRAM
        for j in (0..N).step_by(Bc) {
            let k: Tile<Bc, D> = hbm_load(&K[j*d..]);      // SRAM
            let v: Tile<Bc, D> = hbm_load(&V[j*d..]);      // SRAM
            let s: Tile<Br, Bc> = matmul(&q, &k.t()) / sqrt(d as f32);
            let m_new = rowmax(&m, &s);
            let p: Tile<Br, Bc> = exp(s - m_new);          // SRAM only
            let scale = exp(m - m_new);
            l = l * scale + rowsum(&p);
            acc = acc * scale + matmul(&p, &v);
            m = m_new;
        }
        hbm_store(&mut O[i*d..], &(acc / l));             // O only
        hbm_store_lse(i, m + log(l));                     // for backward
    }
}
```

### 3.3 The backward pass: recomputation instead of storage

Storing $P$ for the backward pass would reintroduce $\Theta(N^2)$ memory. FlashAttention instead stores only $O$, $m$, $\ell$ — $\Theta(Nd)$ — and *recomputes* each $S_{ij}, P_{ij}$ tile on demand from reloaded $Q, K$ blocks [1]. Given $dO$:

$$dV = P^\top dO, \quad dP = dO\,V^\top, \quad dS = P \circ \big(dP - \mathrm{rowsum}(dP \circ P)\big), \quad dQ = dS\,K, \quad dK = dS^\top Q,$$

where the $dS$ formula is the Jacobian of row-wise softmax. The backward kernel loops over $K/V$-blocks $j$ in the outer loop: it loads $K_j, V_j$ once, then iterates over $Q$-blocks $i$, reloading $Q_i, O_i, dO_i, \mathrm{LSE}_i$ and accumulating $dQ_i$ in SRAM while atomically adding $dK_j, dV_j$ in HBM [1][2]. Recomputation raises arithmetic cost by a constant factor but keeps HBM traffic at $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ — a trade the memory wall makes profitable.

The streaming invariant can be stated precisely enough for machine checking:

```tla
---- MODULE OnlineSoftmax ----
EXTENDS Reals, Sequences, TLC
CONSTANTS N, Blocks
VARIABLES m, ell, acc, k          \* running max, normalizer, accumulator, blocks consumed

Init == m = -1e30 /\ ell = 0 /\ acc = 0 /\ k = 0

Absorb(b) ==  \* absorb one score block b \in Seq(Real)
  LET mNew == Max({m} \cup {b[i] : i \in DOMAIN b}) IN
  /\ m'   = mNew
  /\ ell' = ell * Exp(m - mNew) + Sum({Exp(b[i] - mNew) : i \in DOMAIN b})
  /\ acc' = acc * Exp(m - mNew) + Sum({Exp(b[i] - mNew) * V[b, i] : i \in DOMAIN b})
  /\ k'   = k + 1

Invariant == k <= Blocks /\ m = MaxSeen(k) /\ ell = Normalizer(k, m)

Spec == Init /\ [][\E b \in ScoreBlocks : Absorb(b)]_<<m, ell, acc, k>>
====
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 IO complexity: counting bytes, not FLOPs

**Standard attention** performs $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ HBM accesses: the $N \times N$ matrices $S$ and $P$ are each written and re-read [1]. **FlashAttention** loads each $K$-block and $V$-block $T_r = \Theta(Nd/M)$ times (once per $Q$-block), for $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ total HBM accesses. Dao et al. prove a matching lower bound: *any* exact-attention algorithm with $M$ words of fast memory needs $\Omega(N^2 d^2 / M)$ HBM accesses when $M \le d^2$, via a red–blue pebbling argument on the attention DAG [1]. FlashAttention is therefore **IO-optimal** in the practical regime. The roofline consequence: the algorithm's arithmetic intensity rises above the machine balance, moving attention from the memory-bound slope toward the compute-bound plateau [2][6].

### 4.2 Kernel scheduling: the FlashAttention generations

*FlashAttention (2022)* [1] parallelizes only over batch and heads: the CUDA grid is $(\text{batch}, \text{heads})$. For long sequences with small batch — the LLM inference and long-context training regime — this leaves most of the 108 A100 SMs idle. It also splits $Q$ across warps within a block, forcing every warp to touch all of $K$ and $V$ and generating shared-memory chatter, particularly in the backward pass.

*FlashAttention-2 (2023)* [2] makes three surgical changes: **(i)** it restructures the inner loop so the running output is rescaled once rather than after every $K/V$ block — critical because tensor-core matmul throughput exceeds vector throughput by roughly 16:1 on Ampere; **(ii)** it parallelizes over the sequence dimension, expanding the grid to $(\text{batch}, \text{heads}, \text{num\_q\_blocks})$ (e.g., batch=1, heads=8, $N$=4096, $B_r$=128 yields $8 \times 32 = 256$ blocks instead of 8); **(iii)** within a block it partitions work across warps by splitting $K/V$ rather than $Q$, eliminating inter-warp shared-memory communication in the backward pass [2]. The result is roughly **2× faster** than FlashAttention, reaching **50–73% of A100 peak** and **225 TFLOPs/s (72% MFU)** end-to-end training GPT-style models.

*FlashAttention-3 (2024)* [3] targets Hopper, where FlashAttention-2 attains only ~35% utilization. Three techniques close the gap: **warp specialization** — producer warps issue asynchronous TMA copies while consumer warps drive tensor cores, overlapping data movement with computation; **interleaved blockwise matmul and softmax**, pipelining the two GEMMs with the exponential/normalization between them so neither tensor cores nor vector units idle; and **FP8 with block quantization plus incoherent processing** (a randomized orthogonal transform taming FP8's narrow dynamic range), achieving **2.6× lower numerical error** than baseline FP8 attention [3]. Reported throughput: up to **740 TFLOPs/s (75% utilization) at FP16** and close to **1.2 PFLOPs/s at FP8** — a 1.5–2.0× speedup over FlashAttention-2 on H100 [3][8].

| Generation | Key scheduling idea | A100/H100 utilization | Representative result |
|---|---|---|---|
| FlashAttention [1] | Tiling + online softmax; grid over (batch, heads) | 25–40% of A100 peak | 3× GPT-2 speedup; 15% MLPerf BERT-large gain |
| FlashAttention-2 [2] | Sequence-dim parallelism; delayed rescaling; $K/V$ warp split | 50–73% of A100 peak | 2× over FA1; 225 TFLOPs/s GPT training |
| FlashAttention-3 [3] | Warp specialization; TMA overlap; FP8 incoherent processing | 75% of H100 peak (FP16) | 740 TFLOPs/s FP16; ~1.2 PFLOPs/s FP8 |

### 4.3 Asynchrony on Hopper: why overlap is the new tiling

Hopper's execution model rewards *asynchronous* producer–consumer structure: TMA descriptors move tiles between HBM and shared memory without involving the SM's load/store units, and WGMMA instructions run asynchronously on tensor cores. FlashAttention-3 assigns whole warpgroups to roles — some only move data, others only compute — connected by mbarrier pipelines [3]. This is the natural successor to tiling: FlashAttention-1 minimized *how many* bytes cross the HBM/SRAM boundary; FlashAttention-3 additionally hides the *latency* of the bytes that must cross.

### 4.4 Sequence-parallel attention: beyond one device

**Ring attention** arranges devices in a logical ring: each device holds a $Q$-block and circulates $K/V$-blocks around the ring, applying exactly the online-softmax rescaling of Section 3.1 to merge each arriving remote block into its local $(m, \ell, A)$ state, overlapping communication with the local GEMMs. **DeepSpeed Ulysses** instead uses all-to-all collectives to transpose between sequence-sharded and head-sharded layouts, letting each device run an ordinary (FlashAttention) kernel on its assigned heads. Both preserve exactness; both inherit the online-normalizer trick, now applied across a network rather than across SRAM tiles. The unifying view: *online softmax is the algebraic primitive that makes attention decomposable over any partition of the key/value sequence* — SRAM blocks, thread blocks, or machines.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

**Correctness.** Exactness follows from the online-normalizer theorem of Section 3.1 (induction on blocks, following [4]) plus the observation that the tiled kernel performs the same floating-point operations as the reference up to reassociation of reductions. Implementations validate against naive attention across shapes, dtypes, and causal masks [5][8].

**IO optimality (proof sketch).** Model the computation as a DAG over the $N^2$ score entries and $Nd$ outputs. Any schedule with $M$ fast-memory words must, by the Hong–Kung argument adapted in [1], move $\Omega(N^2 d^2 / M)$ words between HBM and SRAM when $M \le d^2$: each $K/V$ tile of $\Theta(M/d)$ rows can serve only $\Theta(M/d)$ query rows before eviction, forcing $\Theta(Nd/M)$ reloads of $\Theta(Nd)$ data. FlashAttention attains $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$, hence is optimal in this regime [1].

**Wall-clock record.** The published measurements tell a consistent story [1][2][3]:

- FlashAttention: **15% end-to-end speedup on BERT-large**; **3× on GPT-2**; **2.4× on Long Range Arena**; first better-than-chance Transformers on **Path-X (16K, 61.4%)** and **Path-256 (64K, 63.1%)** [1].
- FlashAttention-2: **~2× over FlashAttention**, 50–73% of A100 peak, **225 TFLOPs/s** per GPU training GPT-style models (72% MFU) [2].
- FlashAttention-3: **1.5–2.0× over FlashAttention-2** on H100; **740 TFLOPs/s FP16 (75%)**, **~1.2 PFLOPs/s FP8**; FP8 error **2.6× lower** than baseline FP8 attention [3].
- Independent IO analyses corroborate the mechanism: tiling removes the $\Theta(N^2)$ HBM traffic, and the remaining bottleneck is vector-unit throughput for rescaling — precisely what FlashAttention-2's delayed rescaling and FlashAttention-3's interleaving attack [6][7].

## 6. Limitations

The IO-aware program has sharp boundaries, and they should be stated plainly.

1. **Quadratic arithmetic remains.** FlashAttention reduces memory to $\Theta(N)$ and HBM traffic to $\Theta(N^2 d^2/M)$, but FLOPs are still $\Theta(N^2 d)$. For $N$ in the millions, exact attention is infeasible regardless of tiling [1].
2. **Backward recomputation trades compute for memory.** Recomputing $S, P$ tiles roughly doubles the attention FLOPs of training — the right trade where FLOPs are cheap relative to bandwidth, but the calculus could flip on future hardware with relatively cheaper HBM [1][2].
3. **Hardware specificity.** Each generation is hand-tuned to an architecture's quirks — Ampere's async copies, Hopper's TMA and WGMMA, FP8 formats. Porting to AMD, TPUs, or NPUs requires re-engineering the schedule, and the optimality proof covers HBM traffic, not the full hardware cost model [3][8].
4. **Numerical subtleties at low precision.** FP8 attention needs incoherent processing and block quantization to stay accurate [3]; INT8 variants remain limited (per-token quantization of $V$ is still an open implementation challenge [7]).
5. **Kernel-launch and occupancy cliffs.** Very short sequences underutilize the wide grids of FlashAttention-2/3, and small or odd head dimensions fall off the tuned tile configurations [2][8].
6. **Framework friction.** Fused kernels bypass autograd's operator graph, complicating selective activation checkpointing and parallelism strategies that expect materialized intermediates.

---

## 7. Conclusion

FlashAttention's enduring lesson is methodological: **the scarce resource in deep learning systems is not arithmetic but data movement**, and algorithms should be designed — and lower-bounded — in the IO model first. The online softmax recurrence turned a three-pass, HBM-resident normalization into a single streaming reduction; tiling turned the $N \times N$ score matrix from a stored object into an ephemeral SRAM tile; recomputation turned the backward pass's memory bill into a compute bill the hardware could afford. Successive generations then re-examined the new bottleneck — first occupancy and warp-level communication [2], then asynchronous overlap and low-precision arithmetic [3] — and restructured the schedule around it. The trajectory from 25% to 75% of peak throughput was not won by changing what attention *computes*, but by changing where each byte *lives* and when each byte *moves*.
