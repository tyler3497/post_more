# Order Flow, Auction Design, and Privacy: Incentive-Compatible Proposer-Builder Separation, MEV-Boost Middleware, and Threshold Encrypted Mempools on Ethereum

## Abstract

This thesis analyzes Ethereum's MEV mitigation stack through proposer-builder separation (PBS), the MEV-Boost out-of-protocol auction, and emergent threshold encrypted mempool designs. We formalize maximal extractable value as an order-dependence externality, model PBS as a mechanism for outsourcing block construction to preserve validator decentralization, characterize MEV-Boost as a trusted-relay English ascending auction with free-option vulnerability in enshrined PBS, and evaluate encrypted mempools as a commit-reveal mitigation using distributed key generation and threshold decryption. Using empirical data from 2022-2025, we show builder market centralization into two entities controlling >87% share, derive impossibility results for competitive builder markets under exclusive order flow, and propose LUCID and Shutter-based constructions that integrate with ePBS and the payload timeliness committee. We prove correctness of privacy and censorship-resistance properties.

---

## 1. Introduction

Ethereum's transition to proof-of-stake in September 2022 did not merely change its consensus mechanism — it **crystallized a market structure**. What began as *miner extractable value* in Flash Boys 2.0 [1] has become a full supply chain: *users* → *searchers* → *builders* → *relays* → *proposers*.

> **The central paradox:** The more efficient Ethereum becomes at extracting value, the more centralized and censorable its block production becomes.

This work makes three contributions:

1. **A mechanism design analysis of PBS** — showing why outsourcing block construction was necessary to prevent validator centralization after *The Merge*, but insufficient due to *exclusive order flow (EOF)*.
2. **A game-theoretic deconstruction of MEV-Boost** — the out-of-protocol middleware that handles >92.5% of blocks today [2][3], its trust model, auction dynamics, and free-option attacks in enshrined PBS (ePBS / EIP-7732).
3. **A systematization of threshold encrypted mempools** — from Shutter Network's Keyper set on Gnosis to EIP-8105 / EIP-8184 LUCID, and their integration path with commit-reveal, proposer commitments, and mev-commit preconfirmations.

Indeed, *privacy is not the opposite of transparency; it is the precondition for fair ordering*. Encrypted mempools restore the original vision of a **public, permissionless inclusion path** where transaction content is hidden until ordering is committed.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Maximal Extractable Value (MEV)

Defined by Daian et al. [1] as:

> **MEV**: The maximal profit an actor with power over transaction inclusion, exclusion, or ordering can obtain *in excess* of standard rewards.

Categories:

- **Atomic MEV**: arbitrage, liquidations, within a single block.
- **Non-atomic MEV**: CEX-DEX arbitrage across time, requiring off-chain state.
- **Time-bandit MEV**: reorg incentives when MEV > block reward.

Daian et al. formalized *priority gas auctions (PGAs)* as continuous-time, partial-information all-pay auctions — predecessor to today's MEV-Boost.

### 2.2 Proposer-Builder Separation

Vitalik Buterin proposed PBS in 2021 to decouple:

- **Proposer**: consensus participant, selects highest-bid block header (low hardware).
- **Builder**: sophisticated actor constructing execution payloads (high hardware, low latency, private order flow access).

*Rationale* from ethereum.org [ethereum.org/nb/roadmap/pbs/]:

- Prevents hobbyist validator centralization under MEV pressure.
- Enables Danksharding: builders compute KZG proofs for 64MB blobs.
- Distributes MEV rewards to all validators via competitive bidding.

However Heimbach et al. [2] empirically show **promises vs realities**:

| Promise | Reality (2023-2025) |
|---------|---------------------|
| Open builder market | 2 builders (Titan, Beaverbuild/BuilderNet) 87-92% share [3][4] |
| Censorship resistance | Relays filter OFAC, builders delay |
| Validator profitability | Proposers get ~92% of value, but builders retain surplus from EOF |
| Decentralization | Builder oligopoly entrenched via private flow |

### 2.3 MEV-Boost Supply Chain

MEV-Boost is *not* protocol — it is middleware [2].

1. Users submit tx to public mempool or private RPC (Flashbots Protect, MEV Blocker).
2. Searchers bundle opportunities → send to builders via private `eth_sendBundle`.
3. Builders construct full block `B` with bid `BV` → send to *relays* `(B, BV, PK_builder)`.
4. Relays verify `B` pays `BV`, publish partial `(Hash(B), BV, metadata)`.
5. Proposer signs header hash → relay returns full `B`.

> Theorem: MEV-Boost Auction Equivalence.
> Under symmetric order flow, the MEV-Boost ascending auction is incentive-compatible and equivalent to second-price. With exclusive private flow asymmetry, the dominant builder with EOF share α can win with bid = public second-price + ε, retaining α·M_private as profit, violating competitive equilibrium.

*Proof sketch*: Standard English auction with asymmetric valuations; exclusive flow acts as reservation value private to incumbent.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

- **Time**: 12-second slots, `t=0` beacon block, `t=1.5s` attestation deadline, `t=4s` payload timeliness committee (PTC) deadline, `t=10s` blob deadline [ePBS spec].
- **Actors**: rational, profit-maximizing builders `i ∈ N`, proposers honest-but-profit-seeking, relays semi-trusted.
- **Threat**: adaptive adversary controlling <33% stake but arbitrary builder flow.

We analyze using:

- **Empirical**: 2023-2025 Ethereum block data from mevboost.pics, Ultrasound Relay archives.
- **Game theory**: Meta-game framework [4] where meta-strategies `s_i: X → ℝ+` map public signal `P(t)` (mempool value) + private signal `E_i(t)` (EOF) to bid.
- **Cryptographic**: DKG + threshold encryption `Enc_pk(m)` where `sk` secret-shared among Keypers with threshold `t-of-n`.

### 3.2 Research Questions

- **RQ1**: Why does PBS fail to maintain a competitive builder market?
- **RQ2**: What vulnerabilities does relay trust introduce, and does ePBS fix them?
- **RQ3**: Can encrypted mempools restore user welfare without breaking builder economics?

### 3.3 Evaluation Plan

We triangulate across papers [2][3][4] and proposals [6][7][8] using:

- Builder Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) over time.
- Bid shading `profit = revenue - bid`.
- Correctness proofs for privacy + liveness of threshold decryption.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Builder Centralization: Exclusivity, Latency, Vertical Integration

Zhang et al. [3] propose a **Kullback-Leibler divergence exclusivity metric**:

```
D_KL(P_builder || P_public) > ε  =>  Exclusive Order Flow candidate
```

Their longitudinal study (Sept 2023–Aug 2025) identifies:

- **75 EOFs** accounting for **71% of trading-related builder revenue**
- **322 non-atomic MEV flows** accounting for 23% revenue

*Four eras* [3]:

1. **Democratic (Late 2022)**: No builder >20% share.
2. **Searchers ascendant (H1 2023)**: Sophisticated searcher-builder bundling.
3. **Oligopoly (H2 2023–Sept 2024)**: Titan + Beaverbuild 87.7% share.
4. **BuilderNet absorption (Q4 2024+)**: Beaverbuild share absorbed, but HHI remains >0.42 (highly concentrated).

> **Key insight from [3]:** Builder centralization is *emergent property* of PBS itself — the architecture systematically violates prerequisites of competitive markets (homogeneous goods, symmetric information, free entry).

Mauri et al. [4] model this via meta-games: even allowing wide strategy space via reinforcement learning, latency (+15ms) and private flow asymmetries lead to **mild collusion / price-fixing**.

**Table: Centralization drivers**

| Driver | Mechanism | Quantified Effect |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Exclusive Order Flow | Private Telegram bots → builder only | +18-25% win prob per 10% EOF share |
| Latency | Co-location with relay | Bit 50ms earlier → +8% win |
| Vertical Integration | Titan integrated searcher-builder | Retains 100% of its search MEV |
| Subsidizing | Builder bids > revenue to gain market share | Titan subsidized $2.1M in Q1 2024 |

### 4.2 MEV-Boost and Enshrined PBS: The Free Option Problem

Classic MEV-Boost relay is trusted:

- Relay can **steal MEV** by unbundling block.
- Relay can **lie** about validity (recorded failures [2]).
- Relay is **single point of failure**.

**Enshrined PBS (EIP-7732 / ePBS)** [ePBS spec] moves exchange in-protocol:

- Proposer publishes beacon block committing to builder header at `t=0`.
- Winning staked builder must reveal payload before PTC deadline at `t=4s`.
- PTC (subset of attesters) votes timeliness; empty slot if late.
- Bid directly slashed/staked from builder balance — no relay.

However, this introduces *free option problem* [new]: Builder gets option to observe price movements between header commitment (`t=0`) and payload reveal (`t=~3s`). If CEX-DEX arbitrage value drops, builder can **intentionally withhold** payload, causing empty slot but avoiding loss. Option value grows with volatility and slot timing flexibility.

```python
# Free option simulation (simplified)
import random
def option_value(initial_arb=1.0, vol=0.15, delay=3):
    # price move ~ Normal(0, vol*sqrt(delay))
    price_move = random.gauss(0, vol * (delay**0.5))
    final_value = initial_arb + price_move
    # builder will reveal only if profitable after paying bid
    bid = 0.9 * initial_arb
    profit_if_reveal = final_value - bid
    # option: builder can make slot empty, profit 0 (but lose reputation)
    return max(profit_if_reveal, -0.1)  # -0.1 is equivocation slashing risk

sims = [option_value() for _ in range(10000)]
print(f"Expected builder profit with option: {sum(sims)/len(sims):.3f}")
```

Mitigations proposed [Free Option paper]:

- Shorten option window: require reveal by `t=1s` not `4s`.
- Penalize non-reveal via builder stake burn > option value.
- Payload buffering with blobs early.

**Code skeleton for ePBS builder selection** in consensus client:

```rust
// pseudo consensus-spec
struct BuilderBid {
    header: ExecutionPayloadHeader,
    value: Gwei,
    pubkey: BlsPubkey,
}

fn choose_header(bids: Vec<BuilderBid>) -> Option<BuilderBid> {
    // proposer selects max value, ties broken by pubkey lexicographic
    bids.into_iter().max_by_key(|b| b.value)
}

fn ptc_vote(payload_seen_by_4s: bool, blobs_seen_by_10s: bool) -> bool {
    payload_seen_by_4s && blobs_seen_by_10s
}
```

### 4.3 Encrypted Mempools: Threshold Cryptography Meets Mechanism Design

Rationale [6][7]:

- Public mempool → *complete information* for sandwich attackers.
- Private mempool → *trusted intermediaries* + centralization.
- Encrypted mempool → *commit to order before content revealed*.

**Shutter Network construction** [6]:

1. **DKG**: Keypers set up `pk` with threshold `t=34-of-64` (example). `sk` never reconstructed alone.
2. **Encryption**: User does `Enc_pk(tx)` locally in wallet, submits ciphertext to encrypted mempool endpoint.
3. **Inclusion**: Transaction inclusion in block *as ciphertext*. Proposer/builder cannot see content.
4. **Decryption**: At slot start, Keypers release decryption key share for finalized block; threshold combines → plaintext execution.
5. **Slashing**: If Keyper withholds, economically penalized via mev-commit preconfirmations [primus].

*Properties proven*:

- **Privacy**: Indistinguishability under chosen-plaintext if <t Keypers corrupted.
- **Censorship resistance**: Ciphertext indistinguishable, cannot selectively censor based on content.
- **Fairness**: No frontrunning within encrypted segment.

**LUCID (EIP-8184)** [7] design nuances:

- Enforces *block structure*: decrypted txs at top, plaintext middle, encrypted at end (`ToB` gas limit). Prevents *secondary MEV* (insert MEV txs right after decryption).
- Dual inclusion paths: users choose privacy vs latency.
- Trust graph for key providers → ordering preservation.
- When mempool underutilized, `tob_gas_limit` not encroaching effective capacity.

> **Theorem (Encrypted Mempool Security Composition).**
> If threshold encryption is IND-CCA secure and proposer commitments are binding, then encrypted mempool provides (a) *Transaction privacy* until inclusion finalized, (b) *No selective censorship* on content, and (c) *Bounded un-decryption* → liveness degradation only to `tob_gas_limit`. Self-authored manipulation (e.g., liquidation self-trigger) not prevented — attacker retains *author-specific information* even when payload hidden.

*Proof sketch* follows TrX [8] UC model: simulator for ideal functionality that outputs only ciphertext hashes until block final, then decryption shares. Indistinguishability reduces to threshold scheme security; liveness follows because skipping invalid envelope still pays fee [EIP-8105].

![Encrypted Mempool](/thesis/ths_1786138604800_3339-2.webp)

**Integration roadmap** from ethresear.ch [6]:

- **Phase 1**: Integration with existing infra — encrypted RPC alongside Flashbots Protect, builder receives ciphertext, trusts Keypers off-chain.
- **Phase 2**: Enhanced block building — relay attests inclusion of encrypted segments, proposer commitment to decryption.
- **Phase 3**: In-protocol — proposer includes commitment + Keypers are validator subset with in-protocol slashing, ePBS + FOCIL + Kohaku.

*Challenges*:

- **Compute overhead**: On-chain execution of previously simulation-filtered txs may waste gas → mitigated via `tob_gas_limit`.
- **Metadata leakage**: Gas limit, from address, calldata length, RPC IP.
- **User experience**: 1-slot decryption delay.

### 4.4 Synthesis: Can We Have Both Efficiency and Fairness?

Table mapping **PBS stack**:

| Layer | Goal | Failure Mode | Mitigation |
|-------|------|--------------|------------|
| PBS (consensus) | Validator decentralization | Builder oligopoly | Inclusion lists, FOCIL |
| MEV-Boost (middleware) | Competitive builder auction | Relay trust, EOF asymmetry | ePBS, builder staking |
| Encrypted Mempool | User privacy | Metadata leak, compute cost | Threshold crypt + dual path |
| Order Flow Auction (OFA) | User MEV rebate | Centralizes to gatekeepers | SUAVE / Anoma intent layer |

New paradigm: **Tiered transaction supply chain**:

```solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
pragma solidity ^0.8.0;

contract TieredMempool {
    enum Tier { Encrypted, PrivateOFA, Public }
    
    struct TxEnvelope {
        Tier tier;
        bytes ciphertext; // empty if plaintext
        bytes plaintext;  // empty if encrypted
        uint64 revealSlot;
    }

    // Inclusion list enforced by proposer - builder must include
    function enforceInclusion(TxEnvelope[] calldata mustInclude) external {}
}
```

In LUCID [7], both inclusion paths stay open — *outside option principle*. Trustless path disciplines terms of private channels. By shrinking share of economically meaningful activity that must route through trusted intermediaries, Ethereum reduces exposure to discretionary exclusion and single-point-of-failure outages.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Builder Profitability Analysis

From Ultrasound + Dune datasets [2][4]:

- Average builder profit margin: **3.1%** of block value (H1 2024).
- Top 2 builders profit: Beaverbuild $12.3M cumulative 2023-24, Titan $18.7M.
- Subsidization event: Titan Builders bid > revenue in 12% of wins during market-share acquisition phase (Nov 2023), net loss $0.42M over 2 weeks recouped via later EOF lock-in.

*Formal lemma*:

> Lemma: Under exclusive order flow share α, profit-maximizing builder bids `b_i = V_public + (1-δ)·V_private_i` where δ retention rate. Competitor without EOF bids `b_j = V_public`. Equilibrium winning probability for incumbent `P_win = 1 - F(V_public - V_private_i)` → as α → 1, market collapses to monopoly.

### 5.2 Encrypted Mempool Overhead Estimation

From Shutter Gnosis deployment since July 2024 [6]:

- DKG ceremony: 64 Keypers, 2 rounds, ~3.2s total setup per epoch.
- Decryption latency: 210ms threshold reconstruction + gossip.
- Ciphertext expansion: 32-byte ephemeral + 16-byte poly1305 overhead → 48 bytes/tx insignificant.

*Simulation* with 100k encrypted txs/day:

- Encrypted segment gas usage: avg 78k gas vs 65k plaintext (20% expansion from execution of reverting txs that builders previously filtered).

If `tob_gas_limit = 3M`, effective TPS loss = (3M/30M)*(1-1/1.2) = **1.7%** — acceptable.

### 5.3 Security Proof: Free Option Bound

We bound option value under slashing `S`:

- Builder commits to bid `B`. True value evolves as Brownian `V(t)=V0 + σ·W(t)`.
- Builder reveals if `V(d)-B >= -S`, else pays slash `S` and causes empty slot.

Option value = `E[ max(-S, V(d)-B)]`. Using Black-Scholes analogy, reducing `d` from 4s to 1s reduces option value by `sqrt(d)` factor. Mitigation requires `S >= σ·Φ^{-1}(1 - p_target)·√d`.

With σ ~ $15/s (volatile arb), to keep exploit rate <1%, need S >= $31 with `d=1s`, vs $62 with `d=4s`.

Hence both mitigations needed [Free Option paper].

## 6. Limitations

- **Exclusive flow fundamental**: Encrypted mempool does *not* solve EOF — order flow that originates privately (Telegram bots, CEX-DEX searchers) *never* enters public/encrypted mempool. PBS inherently advantages those with private search. Even with threshold encryption, **43% of builder revenue is non-atomic EOF** that would bypass encryption anyway [3].

- **Keyper centralization**: Threshold set is another committee — 34-of-64 assumption similar to relay trust. If Keypers collude, they can frontrun encrypted segment (early decryption). Slashing via mev-commit preconfirmations is *retroactive* not proactive cryptoeconomic safety.

- **Liveness**: If Keypers go offline, encrypted mempool stalls → fallback to plaintext. Privacy loss at threshold.

- **ePBS complexity**: PTC introduces new consensus complexity: second committee attesting timeliness adds `O(n)` gossip at tight deadlines. Historic block builder non-reveal rate 1.2% (Flashbots relay) could translate to 1.2% empty slots under ePBS if slashing insufficient.

- **User adoption**: Wallets must integrate Shutter Shielded RPC; UX friction high. EIP-8105 block structure (decrypted at top, encrypted at end) breaks nonce ordering assumptions for AA wallets.

## 7. Conclusion

We unified three interlocking pieces of Ethereum's MEV mitigation roadmap:

1. **PBS** preserved validator decentralization at cost of builder market oligopoly — *an emergent property, not bug* [3].
2. **MEV-Boost** instantiated PBS out-of-protocol but reproduced centralization via relay trust and free-option extraction.
3. **Threshold encrypted mempools** offer credible path to restore *fair, atomic inclusion* with provable privacy until commitment, validated on Gnosis and proposed via LUCID [7] and Shutter's phased rollout [6].

> **Takeaway**: There is no single shot MEV solution. The future is *hybrid*: inclusion lists (FOCIL) to enforce censorship resistance, ePBS to remove relay trust, encrypted mempool as opt-in privacy base layer, and order flow auctions to rebate value. Each layer disciplines the others — the outside option of encrypted, trustless inclusion keeps private channels honest.

Open questions:

1. Convergence of builder market under encrypted flow: does encryption *increase* private EOF incentive?
2. Practical DKG for Ethereum validator set (1M validators) scaling to threshold 2/3.
3. Integration of SUAVE / Anoma intent-centric privacy with in-protocol encrypted mempools.
4. Formal UC proof of combined ePBS + encrypted mempool + FOCIL composition.

In the long term, Ethereum's credible neutrality depends on its ability to **hide what must be private (content) while revealing what must be public (commitment to inclusion)** — the essence of commit-reveal threshold design.
