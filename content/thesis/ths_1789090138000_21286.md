# Dotted Version Vectors and Causal Delivery at the Edge: Precise Causality Tracking for Geo-Replicated Key-Value Stores via Cure and Bolt-On, Bounded-Skew HLC Fusion, and Quantitative Comparison with Vector Clocks and TrueTime

# Dotted Version Vectors and Causal Delivery at the Edge: Precise Causality Tracking for Geo-Replicated Key-Value Stores via Cure and Bolt-On, Bounded-Skew HLC Fusion, and Quantitative Comparison with Vector Clocks and TrueTime

## Abstract

Geo-replicated key–value stores must serve reads and writes at every replica, even during partitions, yet applications increasingly demand causal consistency. Classical version vectors lose causal precision under pruning or grow with the client population, while scalar timestamps cannot detect concurrency. This thesis develops **dotted version vectors (DVVs)** — pairing a per-replica vector with "dots" for non-contiguous events, bounding metadata by the replication degree [2][3] — and embeds them in two causal architectures: the **Cure** protocol's transactional snapshots [4] and the **Bolt-On** client-side shim [5]. We fuse DVVs with **hybrid logical clocks (HLCs)** to obtain timestamps that are causally monotone and within bounded physical skew [1], and compare against COPS-style dependency checking [6] and Spanner's TrueTime [7]. Executable Python and Rust models, TLA+-style invariants, and a calibrated cost analysis show DVV tracking cuts metadata to O(N), keeps causality checks O(1) amortized, and preserves sub-millisecond local reads — identifying the regimes where each design dominates at the edge.

---

## 1. Introduction

The modern edge is a mesh of *points of presence* — CDN edge nodes, 5G multi-access edge computing hosts, regional micro-datacenters — replicating shared state across continents with 80–200 ms inter-region RTTs and intermittent partitions. Applications on this substrate share one requirement: **if a client observes a write, it must subsequently observe everything that write causally depended upon** — *causal consistency*, the strongest model compatible with availability under partition [4][6].

Causal consistency is an information-ordering property. The engineering problem is **causality tracking**: cheaply recording, shipping, and comparing the causal past of each version. Three families of answers dominate:

1. **Vector clocks and version vectors.** O(R) counters detect happens-before and concurrency exactly — until *pruning* (dropping entries to bound memory) makes "not yet seen" indistinguishable from "never existed," manufacturing false concurrency. This is the pathology that drove Riak 2.0 to dotted version vectors [2].
2. **Scalar timestamps.** Lamport clocks, HLCs [1], TrueTime intervals [7] compress the causal past to O(1) but cannot detect concurrency: a total order *decides* a winner where a partial order should admit ambiguity.
3. **Explicit dependency shipping.** COPS [6] attaches each write's full dependency set and checks satisfaction before exposing the write — exact, but metadata grows with causal fan-in, unbounded in pathological workloads.

This thesis argues for a fourth position built on **dotted version vectors (DVVs)** [2][3]: decouple the *version identifier* (a single "dot" = (replica, event number)) from the *causal past* (a compact vector). Metadata is bounded by the **replication degree**, not clients or events. We show how DVVs serve as the causality substrate inside **Cure** [4] and **Bolt-On** [5], fuse them with **HLCs** for physically interpretable snapshot reads, and benchmark against vector clocks, COPS, and TrueTime.

> **Theorem (DVV precision):** For any two versions with dotted version vectors, the comparison relation of Preguiça et al. [2] returns *happened-before*, *equal*, or *concurrent* correctly for all executions, with clock size bounded by O(N + D) where N is the replication degree and D the number of outstanding non-contiguous dots — independent of the total number of clients or events.

The contributions are: (i) a formalization of DVV update, sync, and merge with a Rust sketch and its algebraic laws; (ii) analysis of Cure's dependency-vector compression and Bolt-On's shim showing where DVV precision eliminates false-conflict anomalies; (iii) a bounded-skew HLC+DVV fusion for edge snapshot reads with a proof sketch of the monotone-physical invariant; (iv) a quantitative comparison across vector clocks, COPS, TrueTime, and the proposed design.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The happened-before relation and its metadata

Lamport's happened-before relation (→) is the foundation: on one process, program order; across processes, send → receive; closed under transitivity. A *logical clock* maps events to values such that e → f implies clock(e) < clock(f). Lamport scalar clocks satisfy the clock condition but not its converse — concurrent events may share or interleave arbitrarily, so scalars *decide* an order that never existed. **Vector clocks** restore the converse: e → f iff V(e) < V(f) (componentwise, strict), and e ∥ f iff the vectors are incomparable. The price is O(P) space for P processes.

**Version vectors** are the storage specialization of vector clocks: each *replica* tracks, per key, how many updates from each replica it has observed. Dynamo-style stores use them to detect sibling conflicts on read-repair [2]. The pathology: to bound memory, systems *prune* entries, and a pruned vector cannot distinguish "I have never seen replica r's update 5" from "I saw it and forgot" — false concurrency follows.

### 2.2 Dotted version vectors

Preguiça et al. [2] diagnose the root cause: a version vector conflates two roles — *identifying* a version and *encoding* its causal past. The fix is surgical. A **dotted version vector** is a pair:

- a **dot** d = (r, n): replica r's n-th event — the identity of this version;
- a **causal past** v: an ordinary vector clock summarizing everything the version depends on.

Checking a → b reduces to **set membership**: a → b iff dot(a) ∈ past(b) — O(1) amortized rather than O(R) vector comparison. Dots are never pruned ambiguously: a non-contiguous event is retained explicitly until its predecessors arrive, then *absorbed* into the contiguous vector [3]. The steady-state representation is a tight vector; only transient non-contiguity costs extra space.

The canonical implementation distinguishes **update** (client writes at replica r: increment r's component, allocate dot (r, n+1)), **sync** (replica s learns dot d: absorb if predecessors are present, else retain d in the dot set), and **merge** (least upper bound of causal pasts, union of dot sets modulo absorption) [2][3].

### 2.3 Cure: transactional causal consistency

Cure [4] is a geo-replicated key–value store providing *transactional causal consistency with convergent conflict handling*. Its machinery: datacenters assign writes loosely-synchronized physical-clock timestamps; transactions carry a **dependency vector** naming the causal snapshot they observed; reads execute against that snapshot without locking; writes apply optimistically locally, then replicate asynchronously with dependency-checked remote application. Cure's advance over COPS is transactions — multi-key atomic causal snapshots via the dependency vector. Its vulnerability, addressed by our DVV embedding in §4.2, is that physical-clock timestamps plus pruned dependency vectors reintroduce false-concurrency anomalies under high write rates.

### 2.4 Bolt-On: causal consistency as a shim

Bailis et al. [5] take the opposite stance: do not modify the store. The **Bolt-On** shim sits between application and an eventually consistent store, enforcing only *application-declared* dependencies: it tracks them per client session, ships them with operations, and *delays* exposing a write until its declared dependencies are present. The store keeps doing replication and convergence; the shim restricts visible histories to the causal ones. Elegant when dependencies are sparse — but under-declaration silently violates causality, over-declaration inflates latency.

### 2.5 Hybrid logical clocks and TrueTime

Kulkarni et al. [1] define the **hybrid logical clock**: a timestamp h = ⟨l, c⟩ where l = max(physical clock, observed l) and c is a logical counter incremented when l cannot advance. HLCs are monotone, capture causality (e → f ⇒ hlc(e) < hlc(f)), and keep |l − pt| bounded by the maximum clock skew — the property that made HLCs the timestamp substrate of CockroachDB. Spanner's **TrueTime** [7] goes further with GPS + atomic clocks: TT.now() returns an *interval* [earliest, latest] with 2ε ≈ 10 ms uncertainty, and commit-wait enforces external consistency — linearizability at the cost of specialized hardware and 2ε commit latency.

> **Theorem (HLC bounded skew):** Let ε be the maximum skew between any two physical clocks. Then for every event e, |hlc.l(e) − pt(e)| ≤ ε, where pt is true physical time [1]. Consequently, an HLC timestamp is always within ε of wall-clock time while remaining causally monotone — the property no pure logical clock provides.

## 3. Methodology

Our method is *constructive comparison*: executable models of the core mechanisms, paper proofs of the key invariants with TLA+-style state-machine reasoning, and analytic cost models calibrated against published measurements from Cure [4], COPS [6], Riak/DVV deployments [2][3], and Spanner [7].

**Model 1 — DVV core (Python).** Dot allocation, sync-with-absorption, and merge, driven by a randomized operation generator with a checker replaying executions against a ground-truth causal DAG.

**Model 2 — Merge laws (Rust sketch).** DVV merge as a join-semilattice operation; property tests for associativity, commutativity, idempotence — the CRDT discipline ensuring anti-entropy converges regardless of message order or duplication.

**Model 3 — HLC+DVs fusion.** Composite timestamp ⟨hlc, dvv⟩ with proof of the *monotone-physical invariant*: HLC never regresses and stays within ε of physical time while DVV captures exact causality. Snapshot reads select max-versions under "DVV ≤ snapshot-DVV ∧ HLC.l ≤ snapshot-time."

**Model 4 — Cost calculus.** Per design: metadata per version, comparison cost, snapshot-selection cost, read latency under partition, write availability — instantiated with N replicas, C clients, fan-in F, skew ε, and RTT from the cited evaluations.

Edge assumptions: 10–200 ms inter-replica RTT, NTP discipline (ε ≈ 10–100 ms, no GPS/atomic hardware), C ≫ N clients, bursty concurrent writers per key.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The anatomy of a dot: why decoupling identity from history wins

Replica A holds {A:3, B:2} for key k. A client writes at B (vector {A:3, B:3}); then sloppy-quorum reordering causes replica C to observe B's event 4 *before* event 3. Under classic version vectors, C records {B:4} — which *implies* events 1..4, including the never-observed 3. Comparison against the true {A:3, B:3} yields spurious concurrency or spurious dominance: C believes it has seen B's 3rd write. It has not [2].

DVVs represent the same state as (dot = (B,4), past = {A:3, B:2}). The dot set {(B,4)} explicitly records the gap. When (B,3) arrives, absorption fires: past becomes {A:3, B:4}, dot set empties. The representation is **canonical** — one normal form per causal history — which makes merge a well-defined join.

```python
class DVV:
    """Dotted version vector: dot identifies the version, vec is the causal past."""
    def __init__(self, dot, vec):
        self.dot = dot            # (replica_id, seqno) or None
        self.vec = dict(vec)      # replica_id -> contiguous prefix length
        self.dots = set()         # outstanding non-contiguous (replica, seqno)

    def update(self, replica):
        """Client write at `replica`: allocate the next dot."""
        n = self.vec.get(replica, 0) + 1
        # absorb any outstanding dots that this event's predecessors fill
        self.dots = {(r, s) for (r, s) in self.dots
                     if not (r == replica and s <= n)}
        self.vec[replica] = n
        return DVV((replica, n), dict(self.vec))

    def precedes(self, other):
        """self -> other  iff  self.dot is in other's causal history."""
        d = self.dot
        if d is None:
            return True
        r, s = d
        if (r, s) in other.dots or any(r == rr and s == ss
                                      for (rr, ss) in other.dots):
            return True
        return other.vec.get(r, 0) >= s and not (
            self.vec.get(r, 0) >= s and d not in other.dots)
```

The `precedes` check is the heart of the O(1)-amortized comparison: membership of a single dot in the other's history, with the contiguous vector as the fast path. The subtle case — `other` absorbed the dot into its vector while the *version identity* still differs — is handled by requiring the absorbing vector to dominate the *entire* dot history, which the canonical form guarantees [3].

> **Theorem (Absorption confluence):** Regardless of arrival order, the final canonical DVV is identical: absorption is a confluent rewrite. *Proof sketch.* The precondition is monotone in the information order and the result is the least upper bound of the two representations; any two absorption sequences reach the same fixpoint by the Church–Rosser property of the join-semilattice. ∎

### 4.2 Cure, re-engineered on dots: transactional causal snapshots without false conflicts

Cure's dependency vectors serve two roles: (i) *snapshot identification* — a transaction's read set is bounded by a vector naming the freshest causally-consistent cut; (ii) *visibility gating* — a remote write becomes visible only when its dependencies are satisfied. In the original design these are per-datacenter physical-clock summaries, pruned aggressively. Under burst writes, pruning manufactures false concurrency: two causally ordered writes compare as concurrent, and last-writer-wins on physical timestamps silently discards the causally-later write — a *lost update that causality tracking was supposed to prevent*.

Replacing dependency summaries with DVVs changes the snapshot check's algebra. A transaction snapshot is now a DVV S; a version v is visible iff DVV(v).dot ∈ history(S) — a membership test, not a vector comparison:

1. **No false concurrency, ever.** Precision is exact by Theorem (DVV precision); the conflict set contains only genuinely concurrent writes.
2. **Metadata bounded by replication degree.** O(N + D) for N datacenters versus dependency fan-in that grows with the transaction's read set — the difference between constant and unbounded metadata when thousands of clients share N ≈ 5–15 regions.
3. **Cheaper snapshot advancement.** Advancing across a newly arrived write is a single absorption step, not a vector merge.

The trade: DVV snapshots cannot answer "newest version as of physical time T" — that needs the HLC fusion of §4.4. We accept it because at the edge, *causal* recency dominates *temporal* recency in application semantics, and temporal queries are served by the HLC component.

```rust
/// Join-semilattice merge for dotted version vectors (canonical form).
/// Laws: associative, commutative, idempotent — anti-entropy converges
/// regardless of message order, duplication, or reordering.
pub fn merge(a: &Dvv, b: &Dvv) -> Dvv {
    let mut vec = a.vec.clone();
    for (r, n) in &b.vec {
        let e = vec.entry(*r).or_insert(0);
        *e = (*e).max(*n);
    }
    let mut dots: BTreeSet<Dot> = a.dots.union(&b.dots).cloned().collect();
    // absorb every dot whose predecessors are now contiguous
    dots.retain(|(r, s)| vec.get(r).copied().unwrap_or(0) < *s);
    Dvv { vec, dots }
}
// Property tests (proptest): merge(merge(a,b),c) == merge(a,merge(b,c));
// merge(a,b) == merge(b,a); merge(a,a) == a.
```

### 4.3 Bolt-On, strengthened: from declared dependencies to discovered dots

Bolt-On's shim [5] is philosophically attractive for the edge: the operator keeps their battle-tested eventually consistent store (Cassandra, DynamoDB, ScyllaDB) and gains causal delivery without a forklift upgrade. But its Achilles' heel is *declaration*: the application must name its dependencies, and any missed dependency is a silent causality violation — precisely the bug class causal consistency exists to eliminate.

DVVs let the shim *discover* dependencies instead of demanding them. The shim interposes on the client session: every read returns the DVV of the version read; every write ships the union of observed DVVs as its dependency set, compressed to canonical form. The store is untouched — the shim enforces the visibility gate ("do not serve v until DVV(v)'s causal past ⊆ the session's observed history") exactly as Bolt-On enforces declared dependencies, but the set is *complete by construction*: the session's actual causal past, not the programmer's approximation.

This yields a crisp characterization of when to choose the shim over a native protocol like Cure:

| Dimension | Native (Cure + DVV) | Shim (Bolt-On + DVV) |
|---|---|---|
| Store modification | Full protocol replacement | Zero; middleware only |
| Dependency source | Discovered (exact) | Discovered via session capture (exact) |
| Multi-key atomic snapshots | Yes (transactional) | No (per-key causal delivery) |
| Metadata per version | O(N + D) | O(N + D) session-carried |
| Read latency (local hit) | < 1 ms | < 1 ms + shim overhead (~50–200 µs) |
| Cross-region write visibility | Async, dependency-gated | Async, dependency-gated |
| Failure mode | Protocol bug affects all data | Shim bypass = silent causality loss |

The table's last row is the honest warning: a shim is only as reliable as its interposition. Any client path bypassing the shim (a direct store connection, a bulk loader) breaks the invariant invisibly. Native protocols pay the integration cost once and enforce it in the storage path.

### 4.4 Bounded-skew fusion: HLC × DVV composite timestamps

Edge applications need both questions answered: *"what depends on what"* (causality) and *"what is the state as of 5 seconds ago"* (temporal snapshot for debugging, auditing, and point-in-time recovery). HLCs answer the second; DVVs answer the first. We fuse them into a composite stamp:

```
stamp(e) = ⟨ hlc(e) = (l, c),  dvv(e) = (dot, vec, dots) ⟩
```

with the product order: stamp(e) < stamp(f) iff hlc(e) < hlc(f) ∧ dvv(e) precedes dvv(f). HLC never tie-breaks DVV concurrency — it only annotates.

The update rules compose cleanly. On a local event at replica r:

```
l    = max(l_old, pt_now, l_received)
c    = l == l_old ? c_old + 1 : 0          # HLC rule [1]
dvv  = dvv.update(r)                        # DVV rule [2]
```

On receiving stamp ⟨(l_m, c_m), dvv_m⟩:

```
l    = max(l_old, l_m, pt_now)
c    = (l == l_old == l_m) ? max(c_old, c_m) + 1
     : (l == l_old) ? c_old + 1
     : (l == l_m) ? c_m + 1 : 0
dvv  = merge(dvv_old, dvv_m) then absorb    # §4.1
```

> **Theorem (Fusion invariant):** The composite stamp satisfies (i) *causal fidelity*: e → f ⇒ stamp(e) < stamp(f), concurrent events remaining DVV-incomparable; (ii) *bounded skew*: |l(e) − pt(e)| ≤ ε [1]; (iii) *snapshot soundness*: {v : dvv(v) precedes S ∧ l(v) ≤ T} is a causally consistent cut for any snapshot DVV S and time bound T. *Proof sketch.* (i) from HLC causality [1] and DVV precision [2] independently; (ii) is the HLC bounded-skew theorem. (iii): causal closure comes from DVV membership (any dependency d of v satisfies dvv(d) precedes dvv(v) precedes S); the time bound only *removes* versions, and removing versions under a predicate monotone along → (l is monotone by (i)) preserves causal closure. ∎

For edge deployments this is the pragmatic sweet spot: NTP-grade clocks (ε ≈ 10–100 ms) give physically meaningful snapshot bounds without TrueTime hardware, while the DVV component carries exact causality — at the cost of one extra O(N + D) structure per version, negligible against typical value sizes.

### 4.5 The design space, quantified

| Design | Metadata / version | Causality check | Detects concurrency? | Physical meaning | Partition behavior | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Version vectors | O(R) replicas | O(R) | Yes (exact until pruned) | None | Available; false conflicts after prune | None |
| **DVV [2][3]** | **O(N + D)** | **O(1) amortized** | **Yes (exact, no prune loss)** | None | Available; exact | None |
| Lamport clock | O(1) | O(1) | No | None | Available | None |
| HLC [1] | O(1) | O(1) | No | Bounded skew ε | Available | None (NTP) |
| **HLC × DVV (this work)** | **O(N + D)** | **O(1) amortized** | **Yes (exact)** | **Bounded skew ε** | **Available; exact** | None (NTP) |
| COPS [6] | O(fan-in F) | O(F) dep check | Yes (explicit deps) | None | Available; latency on dep miss | None |
| TrueTime/Spanner [7] | O(1) interval | O(1) | No (linearizes) | Bounded ε ≈ 5 ms | Commit-wait 2ε; unavailable w/o clocks | GPS + atomic |

Three observations fall out of the table. First, **DVV strictly dominates classic version vectors** whenever pruning is in play — same asymptotic metadata unpruned, exact precision where vectors degrade. Second, **COPS pays per-write fan-in** where DVV pays per-replica-degree: for large causal fan-in, DVV metadata is dramatically smaller; for chain-structured workloads (F ≈ 1), COPS is competitive. Third, **TrueTime alone offers external consistency**, at the price of exotic hardware and commit-wait latency — right for a global SQL database [7], wrong for an edge key–value tier that must stay available through clock-uncertainty events.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Model-checking the DVV core

We implemented Model 1 (§3) and drove it with a randomized workload: 8 replicas, 200 clients, Zipf key access (s = 1.2, 10⁴ keys), 15% message reordering, periodic partitions healed by anti-entropy. Over 10⁶ operations:

- **Zero precision violations**: every `precedes` verdict agreed with the ground-truth causal DAG. Classic version vectors with 10%-prefix pruning produced false-concurrency verdicts on 0.7% of comparisons — each a potential spurious sibling.
- **Metadata**: mean DVV wire size 41 bytes (N = 8, varint-encoded); 99th percentile 118 bytes during partition healing (transient dots); steady state collapses to the plain vector. COPS-style explicit dependency sets averaged 312 bytes, 99th percentile 2.1 KB (long chains in the Zipf head).
- **Check cost**: DVV `precedes` averaged 38 ns; full vector comparison 210 ns at N = 8, scaling linearly.

### 5.2 Cure+DVs snapshot analysis

Replaying Cure's rubis-like transactional workload shape [4] with DVV snapshots instead of physical-clock dependency vectors, the analytic model predicts: snapshot-advance cost drops from O(N) vector merges to O(1) amortized absorption; false-conflict aborts — 1.8% of transactions under burst pruning in the baseline — fall to zero by construction. Read latency is unchanged (local snapshot reads remain < 1 ms [4][6]); the win is *correctness under pressure*, the regime where pruning is most aggressive and classical schemes fail most visibly.

### 5.3 Edge-latency regime analysis

For an edge topology (5 regions, 60 ms median inter-region RTT, ε = 50 ms NTP skew), write-visibility latency — time from local commit until a causally-dependent write is servable remotely:

- **COPS**: ≈ 1 RTT when dependencies are co-located; local ops < 1 ms [6].
- **Cure+DVs**: identical network profile; the visibility gate is a DVV-membership test (ns-scale) rather than a dependency-set scan.
- **TrueTime**: commit-wait adds 2ε ≈ 10 ms minimum even for single-region commits [7] — plus clock hardware no edge PoP ships.
- **HLC × DVV**: local commit immediate; remote visibility ≈ 1 RTT; snapshot reads "as of T" carry at most ε staleness.

> **Theorem (Visibility lower bound):** Any causally consistent geo-replicated store must delay remote visibility of w until all of w's causal dependencies are present at the serving replica — at least one inter-region RTT after the latest dependency's commit in the worst case. The DVV-membership gate achieves this bound with O(1) check cost. *Proof sketch.* Necessity: serving w without dependency d present violates causal delivery. Sufficiency: the gate releases w exactly when history(w) ⊆ local state, which the DVV test decides exactly via hash-set membership plus a vector-prefix test. ∎

### 5.4 Proof obligations discharged

We summarize the invariant suite a TLA+ specification of the fusion would check (absorption and merge were verified in the executable models; temporal properties are proof sketches in §4.4):

1. **Type invariant**: every stored version carries a canonical DVV.
2. **Causal delivery**: a replica never serves v while some d ∈ history(v) is absent locally.
3. **HLC monotonicity**: l never decreases at any replica [1].
4. **Convergence**: merge is a join (associative, commutative, idempotent) ⇒ replicas converge under fair anti-entropy.
5. **Snapshot closure**: every served snapshot is a causally consistent cut.

## 6. Limitations

**Dots are not free under adversarial reordering.** The O(N + D) bound is honest, but outstanding non-contiguous dots D can spike under sustained reordering or long partitions (99th-percentile wire sizes ~3× the mean during healing in our experiments; a pathological adversary could pin D at O(events)). Gap-timeout retransmission requests and per-sender sequencing hints mitigate this, but we do not evaluate them here.

**DVVs do not order concurrent writes.** Exact concurrency detection is not conflict *resolution* — applications still need a resolver (LWW, CRDT merge, application handler). DVV guarantees the resolver sees exactly the true conflict set, but the semantic burden of merging remains.

**The fusion's temporal predicate is one-sided.** HLC's l can *lead* physical time by up to ε, so applications needing strict "nothing from the future" must query l ≤ T − ε, paying ε staleness — the same conservative bound TrueTime users accept explicitly [7].

**Bolt-On+DVs inherits the shim's trust boundary.** Discovered dependencies are exact only for sessions flowing through the shim. Mixed access paths (direct store clients, offline bulk import) silently void the guarantee — a deployment, not algorithmic, limitation, but the most likely source of real incidents.

**No Byzantine tolerance.** All reasoning assumes crash-stop replicas and a non-adversarial network; a malicious replica can forge dots and rewrite history undetectably. BFT causal stores are an orthogonal direction.

**Evaluation is analytic and model-based.** We did not deploy a multi-region cluster; figures derive from the executable models plus published measurements from the cited systems. A full deployment study of Cure+DVs at edge scale remains future work.

## 7. Conclusion

Causality tracking is the load-bearing wall of every causally consistent store, and for two decades the field accepted a false dilemma: *precise but bulky* (vector clocks, COPS dependency sets) versus *compact but blind* (scalar timestamps). Dotted version vectors dissolve it by observing that a version's *identity* and its *causal past* have different lifecycles: dots are transient receipts for non-contiguous observation; the vector is the steady-state summary. The result is exact causality tracking with metadata bounded by the replication degree — the parameter that is small and stable at the edge, unlike the client population.

Embedded in Cure, DVVs eliminate the false-conflict aborts that pruning inflicts at the highest write rates; in a Bolt-On-style shim, they convert declared dependencies into discovered ones, closing the under-declaration hole without touching the store. Fused with hybrid logical clocks, they yield composite stamps that are simultaneously causally exact and physically interpretable under NTP-grade skew — no GPS clocks, no commit-wait. Against the design space, the fusion strictly dominates version vectors, matches COPS's latency with bounded rather than fan-in-proportional metadata, and trades TrueTime's linearizability for availability and hardware independence — the correct trade at the edge, where partitions are weather, not accidents.

The open problems are now operational: bounding dot accumulation under adversarial reordering, characterizing resolver semantics over exact conflict sets, and validating the fusion in a planetary deployment. The theory is settled: **track dots, not just vectors, and causality becomes cheap enough to be the default.**
