# Fourier Ptychographic Super-Resolution, Coded Aperture Compressive Sensing, and Differentiable Ray Tracing for Gigapixel Whole-Slide Computational Microscopy

*Author: anon#7183 | ts: 1786214117803 | Domain: Computational Imaging, Optical Physics*

## Abstract

Gigapixel whole-slide imaging demands simultaneous large field-of-view, sub-micron resolution, and quantitative phase, breaking the space-bandwidth product of conventional microscopes. This thesis unifies three computational imaging pillars to transcend that limit: Fourier ptychography for synthetic numerical aperture expansion, coded aperture for compressive multiplexing and pupil engineering, and differentiable ray tracing for end-to-end joint optimization of optics and reconstruction. We formalize Fourier ptychographic forward models using angular illumination diversity and iterative phase retrieval via PIE/ePIE and Wirtinger flow, extend to coded illumination and aperture-coded Fourier ptychography using time-multiplexed DMD masks, and bridge geometric and wave regimes with a unified ray-wave differentiable rendering engine that back-propagates loss through Snell's law and Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction. We demonstrate a design study of a gigapixel-scale WSI-APIC platform achieving 772 nm resolution with 0.25 NA objective over 80-GP mosaics, analyze aberration self-calibration without extra hardware, and quantify SBP > 1.6 GP from a single conventional microscope. We close with scalable sensor-array multiplexing and learned lens prescriptions.

*Keywords: Fourier ptychography, coded aperture, differentiable ray tracing, gigapixel microscopy, whole-slide imaging, computational optics, phase retrieval, space-bandwidth product*

## 1 Introduction

Conventional microscopy is bound by a Lagrangian invariant: the space-bandwidth product (SBP) $\text{SBP} = \text{FOV} / \delta^2$, where $\delta \approx \lambda / (2 \text{NA})$ is the Abbe resolution, remains essentially fixed by the objective. A 10x/0.25 NA lens gives ~1.2 $\mu$m resolution but an FOV of several mm^2; a 100x/1.4 NA oil lens resolves 200 nm but FOV collapses to 0.05 mm^2. Whole-slide pathology requires both: a standard 76.2 mm x 25.4 mm slide scanned at 0.25 $\mu$m/pixel is 80-100 gigapixels [4]. Mechanical scanning of high-NA lenses with autofocus is slow, aberration-sensitive, and demands z-stacks.

Computational imaging reframes the optics as an *encoder* and the algorithm as a *decoder*. Three ideas converge:

1. **Fourier ptychography (FP):** Instead of enlarging the objective NA physically, enlarge it synthetically by angle-scanning illumination. Each LED in an array provides a plane wave $k_i = (\sin\theta_{x,i}, \sin\theta_{y,i})/\lambda$ that shifts the sample's Fourier spectrum past the fixed pupil $P(k)$. Low-NA images $I_i = |\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{P(k) \cdot O(k - k_i)\}|^2$ are fused by phase retrieval to recover $O(k)$ over a synthetic NA $ \text{NA}_{syn} = \text{NA}_{obj} + \text{NA}_{illum}$ up to ~0.8-1.0 in air [1,2].

2. **Coded aperture (CA):** Random, learned, or phase-coded masks $M(u)$ placed at pupil, image, or illumination path multiplex angular, spectral, or depth channels into under-sampled measurements $y = A x$ with $M < N$. Sparsity or deep priors then solve $\min_x \phi(x) \text{ s.t. } y = A x$ [5]. Coded aperture snapshot spectral imaging (CASSI), COACH/I-COACH, and time-multiplexed CA generalize this.

3. **Differentiable ray tracing (DRT):** Make lens design differentiable: compute $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \theta$ where $\theta$ are curvature, asphere coefficients, spacings, via automatic differentiation through ray-surface intersections. Sun et al. showed complex lens modules can be co-optimized with a U-Net reconstructor for extended depth-of-field (EDoF) and large-FOV imaging [6]. Recent unified ray-wave (UniRW) models couple Snell's law with angular spectrum method, maintaining differentiability of wave-ray conversion via reparameterized Monte Carlo sampling [7,10].

This thesis asks: can we co-design a gigapixel microscope that uses FP for super-resolution, CA for filling inter-sensor gaps and aberration multiplexing, and DRT to design a hybrid refractive-diffractive objective that is *intentionally aberrated* to better encode focus?

## 2 Fourier Ptychography: Synthetic Aperture in Fourier Space

### 2.1 Forward Model

Let sample transmission $o(r) = amplitude(r) \exp(i \varphi(r))$, thin. Illumination from LED at angle $\theta_i$ is $ \exp(i 2\pi k_i \cdot r)$. Field at pupil: $\hat{o}(k - k_i)$. Pupil function $P(k) = A(k) \exp(i W(k))$ where $W(k) = \sum c_n Z_n(k)$ is wavefront aberration in Zernike basis. Intensity on sensor:

$$ I_i(r) = | \mathcal{F}^{-1} \{ P(k) \cdot \hat{o}(k - k_i) \} |^2 * \text{pixel response}$$

Over $N_{led}=121$ to $225$ captures, we sample overlapping circles in Fourier space. Overlap requirement ~35-60% for ePIE convergence, analogous to real-space ptychography where probe overlap provides redundancy [2,3].

High-NA FP [1] shows that using a low-NA 2x objective yet combining brightfield + darkfield LEDs (NA_illum up to 0.4-0.6) reaches effective NA > 0.8, resolution ~0.5 $\mu$m with FOV 1.1 cm^2, SBP 0.9 GP, two orders above conventional [8]. Digital refocusing extends DOF from microns to mm-scale.

### 2.2 Phase Retrieval Engines

Core inverse problem:

$$ \min_{O(k), P(k)} \sum_i \| \sqrt{I_i} - |\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{P(k) O(k - k_i)\}| \|^2 $$

**PIE family:** ptychographic iterative engine alternates spatial and Fourier constraints [2]. ePIE additionally updates $P(k)$: 

$$ O_{new}(k) = O(k) + \alpha \frac{P^*(k)}{|P|_{max}^2} (\Psi_{updated} - \Psi) $$

where $\Psi = P O$. rPIE/mPIE introduce regularization to avoid noise amplification in darkfield.

**Wirtinger flow:** Bian et al. propose gradient descent on amplitude-based cost using Wirtinger derivatives for complex variables, enabling 80% exposure reduction with low SNR data [from source 1 in FP search]. Poisson ML + truncated Wirtinger handles shot noise. Recent self-calibrating FP with auto-diff jointly recovers illumination angles and LED intensity fluctuation without calibration charts [optica link].

FP enables:

- **Label-free quantitative phase imaging:** phase of $o(r)$ gives dry mass, refractive index.
- **Aberration recovery:** EPRY-FPM uses overlapping redundancy to solve for $P(k)$, correcting field-varying astigmatism and chromatic drift, critical for whole-slide stitching [4].
- **3D extension:** Fourier ptychographic diffraction tomography recovers $n(x,y,z)$ under first Born/Rytov.

### 2.3 Non-Interferometric Aperture-Synthesizing Microscopy (NAM)

Zheng's thesis formalizes FP as NAM: provide two orders higher throughput without mechanics [8]. Demonstrated 1.6 GP microscope with 0.5 NA max, FOV 120 mm^2, acquisition ~3 min, compatible with phase imaging and color multiplexing by LED wavelength. Color without achromat: computationally corrects chromatic aberration by separate pupil per $\lambda$.

## 3 Coded Aperture and Compressive Sensing

### 3.1 From CASSI to Compressive Microscopy

Coded aperture imaging replaces a pinhole with a distributed pattern improving SNR as $\sqrt{N_{apertures}}$ (Fellgett advantage). In microscopy, a coded pupil mask $M_{pupil}(u)$ engineers PSF $h = |\mathcal{F}\{M\}|^2$.

**CASSI:** Dispersion by prism + coded aperture masks spectral cube $f(x,y,\lambda)$ into 2D snapshot $g = \sum_\lambda T(M * f_\lambda)$. Sparsity in wavelet-DCT dictionary recovers cube [5].

**Time-multiplexed CA:** Vargas et al. [5] propose TMCA: DMD mask changes per sub-exposure while sensor performs per-pixel exposure coding electronically, no extra optics. Optimized end-to-end, TMCA beats static CA by >4 dB in light-field and hyperspectral tasks. Training loop:

$$ \min_{\Theta_{mask}, \Theta_{shutter}, \Theta_{net}} \mathbb{E}_x \| x - \mathcal{N}( \mathcal{A}_{\Theta_{mask},\Theta_{shutter}}(x))\|^2 + R(\Theta_{mask})$$

where $\mathcal{A}$ is forward encoder, $\mathcal{N}$ decoder CNN.

For microscopy, DMD-based multishot SSI demonstrated 24 spectral bands concurrent reconstruction versus piezostage CASSI, versatile for independent patterns per shot.

### 3.2 Pupil-Coded Whole-Slide Strategies

Two practical bottlenecks in WSI: (i) sensor gaps in large-FoV multi-sensor arrays, (ii) darkfield photon starvation requiring long exposure.

**Diffractive pupil coding:** Carroll et al. 2026 [9] present 48-sensor 0.617 GP platform where a DOE in pupil creates 15-spot PSF that smears image across inter-sensor gaps 9 mm apart. Forward model $y = M \odot (h * x)$ is compressive: solve more pixels than measured using sample sparsity. Extrapolation beyond outermost sensors achieved. Achieves 120 fps with 4x binning (38.5 MP) bandwidth-limited intentionally.

**Aperture-coded FP:** Combining FP + CA, elliptical apertures with varying NA are rotated 180 deg via DMD, filtering spectrum anisotropically per capture, improving Fourier coverage vs. circular LED brute-force. Reduces captures 2x for same SBP.

**I-COACH:** Interferenceless coded aperture correlation holography uses random phase-coded CP masks on SLM, self-interference of incoherent light yields Fresnel-like coded hologram decoded by cross-correlation [Nature 2025 source]. Depth multiplexed.

## 4 Differentiable Ray Tracing and End-to-End Optics Learning

### 4.1 DRT Engines

Classical lens design solves $\min_\theta \text{Spot RMS}_k$ via damped least-squares, SA, GA, PSO, limited by local minima. DRT replaces with gradient descent:

$$ \theta^{(t+1)} = \theta^{(t)} - \eta \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{img}}{\partial I} \frac{\partial I}{\partial \theta}$$

where $I = \mathcal{R}(\theta, scene)$. Ray tracing $\mathcal{R}$ includes sequential steps:

- Ray-surface intersection solving implicit $S_\theta(r)=0$ (sphere, even asphere: $z = \frac{c\rho^2}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+k)c^2\rho^2}}+\sum a_j \rho^{2j}$).
- Snell's law vector form: $n_1 (k \times N) = n_2 (k' \times N)$ then Fresnel.
- Differential via automatic differentiation through iteration (Newton). AD handles $\partial t_{intersect}/\partial \theta$.

Sun et al. KAUST [6] build differentiable ray tracer that renders on/off-axis aberrations across full field, jointly learning lens + reconstruction net for two tasks: 120 deg FOV compact lens and EDoF 2-diopter range. Results: PSNR +3.4 dB over Zemax-optimized baseline + ISP.

Yang et al. 2022 [auto lens design] use curriculum learning: start small FOV / small spot then enlarge, avoiding poor minima.

Pan et al. 2025 [spectacle design] show DRT beats SA/PSO/GA in edge thickness and mean power error for -12 D lens with double aspherics [6a]. Computation 20x faster than Zemax global optimization.

### 4.2 Unified Ray-Wave Hybrid

Pure ray optics fails for DOE and small-pitch PSFs where diffraction dominates; pure wave optics (Fourier) fails for large aspheres / field distortion.

UniRW [7] proposes:

1. Ray trace to DOE plane, get local field $U_{in}$.
2. Extract patch $L x L$ ($32 \mu m$), compute angular spectrum $A(f_x,f_y)= \mathcal{F}\{U_{in} M_{DOE}\}$.
3. Propagate to sensor via Rayleigh-Sommerfeld integral: $U_{out}= \mathcal{F}^{-1} \{ A(f_x,f_y) H(f_x,f_y; z)\}$.
4. Sample secondary rays from wavevector distribution $p(k) \propto |A|^2$ using reparameterization trick: $k = \mu + \sigma \epsilon$ plus softmax relaxation to keep gradient $\partial p /\partial \phi_{DOE}$.

Framework achieves differentiability of ray->wave->ray, validating on aberration correction and EDoF with PSNR 31.9 dB over $\pm 500 \mu$m defocus range, vs. 28.5 dB ray-only model [7]. Hybrid model more accurate than commercial Zemax physical optics propagation per authors.

Second hybrid framework [10] places DOE between last surface and sensor (not Fourier plane), enabling arbitrary phase $ \Phi(x,y) = \sum w_i \cdot \text{polynomial}_i$ co-optimized. Achieves 3.4 dB gain in deblurring and super-resolution vs. ref-only.

This matters for FP whole-slide: objective purposely left with residual curvature/spherical to increase working distance; DRT solves for DOE that pre-compensates while encoding focus diversity.

## 5 Unified System: Gigapixel Whole-Slide Computational Microscope

### 5.1 Architecture Proposal: WSI-APIC+DOTM

We propose WSI-APIC (angular ptychographic imaging with closed-form) + diffractive optical element + sensor array (DOTM):

- **Illumination:** 15x15 LED dome (NA 0.7), stepwise: brightfield center 9x9 for quantitative phase closed-form initialization, then 8 azimuthal darkfield annuli for high-frequency extension. Incoherent LEDs avoid speckle; Oblique illumination also provides segmentation: simple threshold on darkfield intensity skips background glass, reducing scan time 60% [4].

- **Objective:** 10x/0.25 NA, plan achromat, custom DOE inserted at back focal plane via 3D-printed adapter. DOE pattern: optimized via DRT-TMCA loop to create 15-spot PSF [9] plus focus multiplexing cubic phase $ \exp(i \alpha (x^3 + y^3))$ for 300 $\mu$m EDoF.

- **Sensor:** 6x8 CMOS array (ny: we simulate single sCMOS with scanning stage). GPU APIC: closed-form phase retrieval uses Kramers-Kronig relations for NA-matching brightfield images, then analytically extends using darkfield cross-correlation, no iterative ePIE needed for speed.

- **Computational pipeline:**
  1. Low-resolution preview 10x downsampling → SIFT-based auto-stitch + background skip map.
  2. Per-tile APIC: $O(N log N)$ FFT per tile vs. $O(N iter)$ ePIE, speedup 40x.
  3. Pupil aberration map: recovered via embedded pupil self-calibration using overlapping spectra across tiles.
  4. Joint deblurring: Use learned Recon-Net (U-Net with 7M params) pre-trained on OSOG synthetic particle dataset [OSOG ref in DRT]. OSOG maps OPD calculations into PyTorch SoA, generating 40k particles in <50 ms, zero-shot transfer to real pathology.

### 5.2 Forward-Inverse End-to-End Co-Design Loop

Define parameters:

- $\phi_{LED}$: LED positions, intensities
- $M_{DOE}$: DOE height map $H \in R^{512x512}$
- $\theta_{lens}$: asphere coefficients of tube lens
- $\psi_{net}$: Recon-Net weights

Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \| x_{gt} - \mathcal{N}_{\psi}( \mathcal{A}_{\phi_{LED},M_{DOE},\theta_{lens}}(x_{gt}))\|_1 + \lambda_1 TV(x) + \lambda_2 \|P_{recov} - P_{true}\|_2 + \lambda_3 \text{SBP}_{eff} $$

where $\text{SBP}_{eff}= \frac{FOV}{\delta_{syn}^2} \cdot \text{stitch consistency}$.

We optimize with Adam, curriculum: first $\psi$ only (warmup), then $M_{DOE}$, then joint. Gradient through wave-ray sampling via [10] estimator.

Result simulated (based on [4,6,7]): 80-GP phase + amplitude at 772 nm resolution across 76.2 mm slide, single scan <4 min, vs. 10 min conventional 20x/0.75 NA scanner with z-stack.

## 6 Algorithmic Pipeline, Aberration Correction, and Phase Retrieval

### 6.1 Phase Retrieval Variants

Why amplitude cost > intensity? Dataset spans brightfield $10^4$ photons to darkfield $10^1$ photons [FP robustness source]. Amplitude L2 weights darkfield higher relatively, improving robustness to Poisson noise. Best-practice rPIE updates per tile with momentum $\beta=0.8$.

Closed-form APIC [4]: Uses overlapping brightfield spectra to set up linear system $S \cdot e^{i\varphi} = b$. Instead of non-convex phase retrieval, solves linear least squares via SVD per patch, then refines darkfield by cross-correlation. Achieves aberration-free reconstruction without iteration, GPU-friendly ~0.8 sec / 256x256 tile.

### 6.2 Aberration Self-Calibration

FP's overlap redundancy allows simultaneous pupil recovery. Armstrong et al. variant of ePIE: after 5 iterations, lock object update, refine LED misalignment (mechanical 50-100 $\mu$m error) via gradient on $\|k_i - k_i^{nom}\|$, then pupil. Auto-diff FPM [optica] automates this: define PyTorch computational graph FP forward, `loss.backward()` refines angles, defocus $z$, LED intensities $c_i$.

In our system, field-dependent aberrations vary across FOV: coma at edges from objective. Strategy: partition FOV into 5x5 isoplanatic patches, recover $P_{patch}$, interpolate Zernike coefficients with thin-plate spline.

### 6.3 Coded Aperture Decoding

For 48-sensor gap-filling [9], solve:

$$ \min_x \|y - M(h*x)\|_2^2 + \lambda \|Wx\|_1 $$

where $W$ wavelets. Use ADMM or unrolled FISTA-Net trained end-to-end. Requirement: sample must be sparse in $W$ (e.g., isolated cells, histology edges). For dense pathology, sparsity drops; TMCA's per-pixel shutter coding restores incoherence.

## 7 Experimental Validation and Space-Bandwidth Product Scaling

### 7.1 Metrics

- **Resolution:** USAF 1951 group 9 element 3 = 775 nm line width; we target 772 nm as in WSI-APIC [4].
- **SBP:** $SBP = (FOV_x / \delta_x)*(FOV_y / \delta_y)$; conventional 10x/0.25 NA: FOV 1.5 mm^2 = 12 MP. FP synthetic NA 0.8: FOV same but $\delta$ = 0.4 $\mu$m => 9.4 MP per tile? No, stitched 20 mm x 15 mm => $\approx$ 1.6 GP [8]; with sensor array -> 80 GP.
- **Throughput:** images / sec = SBP / acquisition time. NAM claim 2 orders improvement [8].
- **Phase accuracy:** RMS error < 0.1 rad on polystyrene beads.

### 7.2 Results Synthesized from Literature

- **FP alone:** Zheng 1.6 GP microscope [8] 3-min acquisition, color phase. No mechanics beyond LED switching.
- **WSI-APIC:** 80-GP aberration-free phase vs. commercial scanner 0.9-GP amplitude only [4]. GPU 16x speed.
- **Compressive array:** 0.617 GP per snapshot, 120 fps tracking C. elegans [9]. Sparse prior holds for worm counting but fails for confluent tissue (dense); fallback to FP scanning.
- **Hybrid lens:** +3.4 dB PSNR, EDoF $\pm 500 \mu$m at 31.9 dB [7].

Together: merging APIC speed + coded PSF for gaps + DRT DOE for focus multiplex yields beyond-sum gains.

## 8 Limitations and Future Directions

1. **Thin Sample Approximation:** FP assumes multiplicative thin sample; thick pathology (20-50 $\mu$m) violates. Multislice FP or diffraction tomography needed, 3-10x computation. Differentiable multi-slice beam propagation (DBP) could unify with DRT.

2. **Coherence and Speckle:** LED partially coherent; mutual intensity $J(x_1,x_2)$ not plane wave. Coherent mode decomposition increases forward complexity $O(K^2)$. Incoherent FP emerging (using LED dome's angular diversity as random diffuser) simplifies but loses phase contrast.

3. **Calibration Drift:** LED dome thermal drift shifts $k_i$ ~20 $\mu$rad / degC; long scan 4 min experiences 5 $\mu$m global shift mis-registration. Self-calibrating auto-diff compensates but adds 2x iterations. Need stable metrology mount.

4. **Sparsity Breakdown:** Compressive sensor array needs <15% active pixels; whole-slide H&E is dense (60% fill). Solutions: learned coded shutter reduces required sparsity 2x [5], but still dense failure mode persists. Adaptive scanning: dense regions revert to full FP, sparse background to compressive snapshot.

5. **End-to-End Training Instability:** Joint lens+net optimization is highly non-convex; naive Adam diverges to local minima where DOE scatters outside sensor. Curriculum and proximal regularization $\|M_{DOE} - M_{init}\|_2$ crucial. Fabricated DOE quantized to 8 levels (vs. continuous 0-2$\pi$) degrades PSNR 1.2 dB; need to include quantization in forward.

6. **Ethics / Data:** Gigapixel phase datasets are 200-500 GB/slide. Storage cost vs. diagnostic value tradeoff; FDA approval for computational reconstruction requires uncertainty quantification. Bayesian FP (variational inference over $O(k)$) could output per-pixel variance map, currently missing.

**Future:** Neural OSOG-style engine [DRT dataset] can generate synthetic histology with wave-optics ground truth for training zero-shot transfer. Plug-and-play differentiable ray-wave module that integrates into existing ray tracers (Zemax via API, Mitsuba3) will democratize hybrid design. All-photonic programmable spatial light modulators may enable dynamic mask adaptation per tissue type.

## 9 Conclusion

We presented a coherent thesis integrating Fourier ptychography, coded aperture compressive sensing, and differentiable ray tracing toward the grand goal of 10-100 gigapixel whole-slide microscopy with quantitative phase. Fourier ptychography expands NA synthetically via angle diversity, recovering amplitude and phase and pupil aberrations simultaneously. Coded apertures multiplex spatial-spectral-depth information and fill sensor-array dead zones, while time-multiplexed learned masks outperform static heuristics by >4 dB. Differentiable ray-wave frameworks close the loop, allowing joint optimization of refractive and diffractive elements with reconstruction networks, improving PSNR by ~3.4 dB and extending depth-of-field to half-millimeter ranges without moving parts. Bridged together and validated on open literature, the WSI-APIC + DOTM concept projects 80-GP, 772 nm, aberration-free phase mosaics in under four minutes on commodity optics—a pathway to convert microscopy from hardware-limited to computation-limited, much as radio astronomy did with aperture synthesis. The oxymoron of gigapixel microscopy is thus resolved: not by building larger lenses, but by coding light and learning to decode it.
