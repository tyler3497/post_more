# Flow Matching and Rectified Flow for Generative Modeling: Conditional Vector Fields, Optimal Transport Couplings, and the ODE Paradigm Beyond Score-Based Diffusion

## Abstract

Flow matching and rectified flow have consolidated a *simulation-free ordinary differential equation (ODE) paradigm* for generative modeling that rivals — and in several regimes surpasses — score-based diffusion models. Instead of learning the time-dependent score of a stochastic noising process, these methods regress a neural velocity field against *conditional vector fields* of prescribed probability paths, transporting a tractable source distribution to data along nearly straight trajectories. This thesis develops the machinery of this program: the flow matching (FM) and conditional flow matching (CFM) objectives of Lipman et al. [1] and Tong et al. [2]; *optimal transport couplings* — minibatch OT-CFM and Schrödinger-bridge SB-CFM — that straighten paths and reduce gradient variance [2]; rectified flow's least-squares causalization of linear interpolants, its non-crossing property, and the cost-reducing rectification and reflow procedures of Liu et al. [3]; and modern training refinements [4]. We compare these ideas with score-based diffusion via stochastic differential equations [5] and consistency models [6], present a formal sample-complexity result for rectified flows [7], and close with limitations and open problems.

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling is, at its core, a transport problem: given samples from an unknown data distribution $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, construct a tractable map from a simple source distribution $p_0$ (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$) to $p_{\mathrm{data}}$. For several years the dominant solution has been *score-based diffusion*: corrupt data with a forward stochastic differential equation (SDE), learn the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ of the perturbed marginals by denoising score matching, and sample by simulating the reverse-time SDE or its probability-flow ODE [5]. This framework is extraordinarily expressive, but it carries two structural taxes: training is tied to a *fixed* noising process, and sampling requires simulating a stochastic (or stiff deterministic) differential equation over many discretization steps.

Flow matching (FM), introduced by Lipman et al. [1], and rectified flow, introduced independently by Liu et al. [3], propose a cleaner contract. Both learn a *time-dependent velocity field* $v_t(x)$ defining an ODE

$$\frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_t(x_t), \qquad x_0 \sim p_0,$$

whose pushforward $p_t = [\psi_t]_{\#} p_0$ interpolates from noise to data. The decisive innovation is *simulation-free training*: rather than backpropagating through an ODE solver, the network regresses directly onto analytically tractable *conditional* vector fields of hand-designed probability paths. The celebrated equivalence theorem of flow matching states that this conditional regression has — up to an irrelevant constant — the same gradient as regressing onto the intractable marginal vector field [1, 2].

The subsequent literature has refined every degree of freedom in this template:

- **Probability paths.** Gaussian conditional paths, linear interpolants, optimal-transport displacement interpolations, and Schrödinger bridges [1, 2].
- **Couplings.** The joint distribution $q(z)$ over which paths are conditioned — independent, minibatch-OT, entropic-OT, or deterministic reflow couplings — controls trajectory curvature, training variance, and sampling cost [2, 3, 4].
- **Straightness.** Rectified flow theory shows that iterative *rectification* provably reduces convex transport costs and straightens trajectories until a single Euler step suffices [3].
- **Scale.** Rectified-flow transformers with improved timestep schedules now underpin flagship text-to-image systems [4].

This thesis presents these developments as one coherent theory of *velocity-field regression for distribution transport*, derives the central theorems, compares the ODE paradigm point-by-point with score-based diffusion and consistency models, and evaluates the empirical record honestly — including where the framework still falls short.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Continuous normalizing flows and the simulation bottleneck

A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) [1] models a distribution as the time-$1$ pushforward of a source density under the flow $\psi_t$ of a velocity field $v_t$:

$$p_t(x) = p_0(\psi_t^{-1}(x)) \exp\!\left(-\int_0^t \nabla \cdot v_s(\psi_s(x))\, ds\right).$$

Maximum-likelihood training of CNFs requires integrating the ODE *and* its divergence trace per gradient step — prohibitively expensive at scale. The historical workaround was to train the reverse of a *fixed* diffusion process instead. Flow matching eliminates the workaround: it trains the CNF velocity field directly, with no simulation, by supervising it with conditional targets.

### 2.2 Score-based diffusion models

Song et al. [5] define a forward SDE $dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw$ that perturbs $p_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$ toward a tractable $p_T \approx \pi$. Sampling uses the reverse-time SDE

$$dx = \left[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right] dt + g(t)\, d\bar{w},$$

or the probability-flow (PF) ODE sharing its marginals,

$$dx = \left[f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right] dt.$$

The score network $s_\theta(x,t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ is trained by denoising score matching — itself a conditional regression, $\mathbb{E}[\|s_\theta(x_t,t) - \nabla \log p_t(x_t|x_0)\|^2]$ — which is why the FM/CFM framework can be viewed as its deterministic, path-generalized sibling [1, 2].

### 2.3 Consistency models

Consistency models [6] attack sampling cost from the diffusion side: they learn a *consistency function* $f_\theta(x_t, t) \mapsto x_\epsilon$ mapping every point on a PF-ODE trajectory to its origin, enforcing *self-consistency* $f_\theta(x_t, t) = f_\theta(x_{t'}, t')$. They train either by distilling a pretrained diffusion model or standalone, and achieve one-step generation with FID 3.55 on CIFAR-10 and 6.20 on ImageNet $64{\times}64$ [6]. They are the natural one-step competitor against which straight-flow methods must be measured.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The flow matching objective

Let $u_t(x)$ be a vector field *generating* a target probability path $p_t$, i.e., satisfying the continuity equation $\partial_t p_t + \nabla \cdot (p_t u_t) = 0$ with $p_0$ the source and $p_1 \approx p_{\mathrm{data}}$. The (marginal) flow matching objective is

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1],\, x \sim p_t(x)} \big\| v_\theta(t, x) - u_t(x) \big\|^2.$$

Both $p_t$ and $u_t$ are intractable in general. The CFM construction [1, 2] introduces a latent conditioning variable $z \sim q(z)$ with tractable conditional paths $p_t(x|z)$ and conditional fields $u_t(x|z)$, defining the marginals by mixture,

$$p_t(x) = \int p_t(x|z)\, q(z)\, dz, \qquad u_t(x) = \int u_t(x|z)\, \frac{p_t(x|z) q(z)}{p_t(x)}\, dz,$$

and the *conditional* flow matching objective

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\, z \sim q(z),\, x \sim p_t(x|z)} \big\| v_\theta(t, x) - u_t(x|z) \big\|^2.$$

> **Theorem (CFM equivalence, Lipman et al. [1]; generalized in [2]):** *If $p_t(x) > 0$ for all $t, x$, then $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)$. Hence regressing on the tractable conditional fields is gradient-equivalent to regressing on the intractable marginal field.*

This single theorem is the engine of the entire paradigm: it converts CNF training into ordinary supervised regression.

### 3.2 Canonical conditional paths

The most-used instantiations, following the taxonomy of Tong et al. [2]:

| Variant | Conditioning $z$ | Conditional path $p_t(x\|z)$ | Conditional field $u_t(x\|z)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| FM (Lipman [1]) | $z = x_1 \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$ | $\mathcal{N}(x \mid t x_1,\, \sigma_t^2 I)$, $\sigma_t = 1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t$ | $\dfrac{x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})x}{1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t}$ |
| I-CFM (independent) | $z = (x_0, x_1)$, $x_0 \!\perp\! x_1$ | $\mathcal{N}(x \mid (1-t)x_0 + t x_1,\, \sigma^2 I)$ | $x_1 - x_0$ |
| OT-CFM | $z = (x_0, x_1) \sim \pi_{\mathrm{OT}}$ | $\mathcal{N}(x \mid (1-t)x_0 + t x_1,\, \sigma^2 I)$ | $x_1 - x_0$ |
| SB-CFM | $z = (x_0, x_1) \sim \pi_{2\sigma^2}$ (entropic OT) | $\mathcal{N}(x \mid (1-t)x_0 + t x_1,\, \sigma^2 t(1-t) I)$ | Brownian-bridge drift |

Here $\pi_{\mathrm{OT}}$ denotes a (minibatch) optimal-transport coupling between source and target, and $\pi_{2\sigma^2}$ its entropically regularized counterpart, whose displacement interpolation is the *Schrödinger bridge* [2]. In the limit $\sigma \to 0$, I-CFM and OT-CFM reduce to the *rectified-flow* objective of Liu et al. [3]:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\, (x_0, x_1)} \big\| v_\theta(t, x_t) - (x_1 - x_0) \big\|^2, \qquad x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1.$$

### 3.3 Training and sampling algorithms

Training is a pure regression loop with no ODE solver in sight:

```python
# Conditional flow matching with minibatch OT coupling (PyTorch sketch)
for x1 in dataloader:                       # target batch
    x0 = torch.randn_like(x1)               # source batch
    pi = minibatch_ot_coupling(x0, x1)      # assignment matrix (e.g. via POT)
    x0, x1 = pi.apply(x0, x1)               # OT-CFM pairing
    t = torch.rand(batch, 1)                # t ~ U[0,1], or logit-normal [4]
    xt = (1 - t) * x0 + t * x1 + sigma * torch.randn_like(x1)
    target = x1 - x0                         # conditional vector field
    loss = ((model(xt, t) - target) ** 2).mean()
    loss.backward(); optimizer.step()
```

Sampling integrates the learned field forward from noise:

```python
# Few-step Euler sampling of the learned flow
x = torch.randn(batch, dim)
ts = torch.linspace(0, 1, nfe + 1)
for i in range(nfe):
    dt = ts[i+1] - ts[i]
    x = x + dt * model(x, ts[i])            # straight paths => small nfe
```

When trajectories are straight, $v_\theta(t, x_t) \approx x_1 - x_0$ is nearly constant along each path, so even `nfe = 1` is accurate — the headline property of rectified flow [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why conditional regression is legitimate: the marginal-field identity

The CFM theorem's proof is a short calculation with a long shadow. Expanding $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}$ as quadratics in $v_\theta$, the terms $\|v_\theta\|^2$ coincide after integrating out $z$, and the cross-terms coincide because

$$\mathbb{E}_{z, x \sim p_t(\cdot|z)} \langle v_\theta(t,x), u_t(x|z) \rangle = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_t} \langle v_\theta(t,x), u_t(x) \rangle$$

by the definition of the marginal field $u_t$ as the posterior mean of $u_t(\cdot|z)$. Two consequences follow. First, the *optimal* regressor is the posterior mean $v^\star(t,x) = \mathbb{E}[u_t(x|z) \mid x_t = x]$, e.g. $v^\star(x,t) = \mathbb{E}[X_1 - X_0 \mid X_t = x]$ for rectified flow [3]. Second, the *variance* of the stochastic target $u_t(x|z)$ around this mean is pure gradient noise: it does not bias learning, but it slows it. Tong et al. additionally propose variance-reduction schemes over the conditional targets, formalizing the intuition that *better couplings buy faster training at fixed compute*.

### 4.2 Optimal transport couplings: from independent pairings to Schrödinger bridges

The independent coupling $q(x_0)q(x_1)$ pairs each noise sample with a random data point, so linear interpolants $x_t$ routinely cross large regions of space and the regression target $x_1 - x_0$ has high variance. Optimal transport replaces it with a coupling $\pi$ minimizing $\mathbb{E}_\pi[c(x_0, x_1)]$ (typically quadratic cost), i.e., the displacement interpolation of the $2$-Wasserstein geodesic. In practice, exact OT is infeasible, so OT-CFM computes a *minibatch* OT plan per batch (e.g., with the POT library) and pairs samples accordingly [2]. Empirically this yields straighter learned trajectories, faster loss decay, and better few-step sample quality than independent coupling [2].

SB-CFM goes one step further: with entropic regularization $\pi_{2\sigma^2}$, the conditional path becomes a *Brownian bridge* with variance $\sigma^2 t(1-t)$, and the marginal flow solves the *dynamical Schrödinger bridge* — the entropy-regularized optimal transport problem. This connects flow matching to the classical stochastic-interpolant literature and shows that "noise scale" and "entropic regularization" are two faces of the same object [2].

A subtle point deserves emphasis: *the marginal coupling does not change the marginal path's endpoints*. Whatever $\pi$ is used, $p_0$ and $p_1$ are fixed; the coupling only reshapes *which* conditional paths are mixed and hence the curvature of the learned marginal field.

### 4.3 Rectified flow: causalizing straight lines, non-crossing, and reflow

Liu et al. [3] arrive at the same objective from a different intuition. The linear interpolant $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1$ follows $dx_t = (x_1 - x_0)\,dt$, which is *non-causal*: updating $x_t$ requires knowledge of the future endpoint $x_1$. Rectified flow *causalizes* it by regressing a velocity field onto $x_1 - x_0$; the fitted ODE $dz_t = v(z_t, t)\,dt$ reproduces the interpolant's marginals while being simulable forward in time. The procedure is time-symmetric — swapping $x_0, x_1$ and flipping the sign of $v$ yields the reverse transport — so the same model maps data back to noise.

Two theoretical properties make this more than a heuristic:

1. **Non-crossing.** Trajectories of a well-posed ODE cannot cross. Rectification therefore *rewires* intersecting interpolant paths into a deterministic, non-crossing coupling $(z_0, z_1)$.
2. **Cost reduction.** Let $(z_0, z_1)$ be the coupling induced by the rectified flow of $(x_0, x_1)$. Then for every convex cost $c$,
$$\mathbb{E}[c(z_1 - z_0)] \;\le\; \mathbb{E}[c(x_1 - x_0)].$$
Rectification never increases convex transport cost [3]. Iterating — *reflow*: generate synthetic pairs from the current flow, retrain on them — produces a sequence of couplings with monotonically non-increasing cost and increasingly straight trajectories, until the flow is nearly straight and a single Euler step generates high-quality samples [3].

> **Theorem (informal, Liu et al. [3]):** *If the rectified flow is perfectly straight, i.e. $v(z_t, t) = z_1 - z_0$ for all $t$, then one Euler step from $t=0$ to $t=1$ transports $p_0$ to $p_1$ exactly, with zero discretization error.*

Reflow is conceptually a *deterministic* alternative to minibatch OT: instead of solving an assignment problem, it distills the model's own transport map into the next round's coupling [4].

### 4.4 Systematic comparison: flows versus scores versus consistency

The three modern paradigms differ along every axis that matters in practice:

| Axis | Score-based diffusion [5] | Flow / rectified flow [1, 2, 3] | Consistency models [6] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learned object | Score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ | Velocity field $v_t(x)$ | Consistency fn $f_\theta(x_t, t) \to x_\epsilon$ |
| Dynamics | Reverse SDE / PF-ODE | ODE only | One-step map (multistep optional) |
| Training | Denoising score matching | Conditional velocity regression | Distillation or standalone CT |
| Simulation during training | No | No | Optional (distillation needs teacher) |
| Path design freedom | Fixed by forward SDE | Free: Gaussian, OT, SB, reflow | Inherited from teacher PF-ODE |
| Typical NFE | 50–1000 | 1–50 (1 after reflow) | 1–2 |
| Exact likelihood | Via PF-ODE + trace | Via ODE + trace | Not directly |
| CIFAR-10 FID (reported) | 2.20 [5] | competitive [1, 2, 4] | 3.55 one-step [6] |

Three remarks sharpen the comparison. *First*, flow matching *subsumes* diffusion paths: Lipman et al. show that VP-diffusion probability paths are a special case of the Gaussian CFM family, and that FM training on those paths is a more stable alternative to score matching [1]. *Second*, the velocity target $x_1 - x_0$ is bounded and smooth, whereas scores blow up as $\sigma_t \to 0$; this is the technical reason flow objectives train more stably at low noise levels. *Third*, consistency models and straight flows solve the same *few-step* problem from opposite ends: consistency enforces a functional constraint (every trajectory point maps to the origin) while rectified flow enforces a geometric one (trajectories are straight). Both achieve one-step generation; flows additionally preserve exact invertibility and likelihood computation through the ODE.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Formal Results

**ImageNet-scale flow matching.** Lipman et al. [1] train CNFs with FM on ImageNet and report consistently better performance than diffusion-based alternatives in both likelihood and sample quality, with reliable sampling from off-the-shelf ODE solvers (e.g., dopri5) — a milestone, since CNFs had previously not scaled to this regime.

**OT couplings accelerate and straighten.** Tong et al. [2] demonstrate that OT-CFM and SB-CFM converge faster (in wall-clock and iterations) than independent-coupling CFM on image benchmarks, produce straighter learned trajectories (measured by the straightness functional), and yield better few-step FID — confirming the variance-reduction theory of Section 4.1.

**Single-step rectified flow.** Liu et al. [3] show that 1-rectified flow followed by reflow rounds generates high-quality CIFAR-10 and bedroom images with *one* Euler step, a regime where diffusion models collapse. The cost-reduction theorem is validated empirically: each reflow round lowers the quadratic transport cost of the induced coupling.

**Modern training refinements.** Lee et al. [4] ("Improving the Training of Rectified Flows") systematically ablate the design space — timestep sampling (logit-normal), velocity versus data prediction parametrizations, loss weightings $\omega(t)$ — and show these choices dominate raw compute in determining few-step FID, establishing a recipe adopted by large-scale rectified-flow transformers.

**Formal sample complexity.** A recent analysis [7] proves *order-optimal* sample complexity for learning rectified flows: under standard smoothness assumptions, the estimator attains the minimax rate for the velocity field, and the pushforward of the learned flow converges to the target in Wasserstein distance at the optimal rate up to logarithmic factors. Formally:

> **Theorem (informal, [7]):** *Let $v^\star(x,t) = \mathbb{E}[X_1 - X_0 \mid X_t = x]$ be $L$-Lipschitz and the function class have controlled covering numbers. Then the empirical risk minimizer $\hat v$ of the rectified-flow loss satisfies $\mathbb{E}\|\hat v - v^\star\|_{L^2(p_t)}^2 = \tilde O(n^{-2\beta/(2\beta + d)})$ at smoothness $\beta$, and the induced generative distribution converges to $p_1$ at the corresponding optimal Wasserstein rate.*

This is the first result placing flow-based generators on the same statistical footing as classical nonparametric density estimators — and it is *stronger* than what is known for score-based diffusion, where score blow-up near $t = 0$ complicates the analysis.

**Head-to-head numbers.** For orientation: score-SDE models reach FID 2.20 on CIFAR-10 [5]; consistency models reach 3.55 one-step [6]; rectified-flow variants with reflow and modern schedules are competitive with both at 1–4 NFE while retaining ODE invertibility [3, 4]. The honest summary is that at *many* NFE, well-tuned diffusion remains the quality ceiling; at *few* NFE, straight-flow methods dominate; consistency models trade likelihood tractability for single-step simplicity.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Minibatch OT is a biased proxy.** OT-CFM's theoretical guarantees assume the true OT coupling, but training uses minibatch approximations whose bias grows in high dimensions. Closing the gap between minibatch and population OT — or proving it does not matter — remains open [2].
2. **Reflow is expensive and data-hungry.** Each reflow round requires generating a full synthetic dataset and retraining; on billion-parameter text-to-image models this is a major cost, and synthetic-pair overfitting can degrade diversity [4].
3. **Straightness is not optimality.** A straight flow minimizes discretization error, but the straight coupling is generally *not* the optimal-transport coupling; rectified flow reduces convex costs without converging to the Monge map except in special cases [3]. Characterizing the fixed points of reflow is unresolved.
4. **Likelihood gaps.** Despite ODE-based likelihoods being tractable, flow models still trail autoregressive and well-tuned diffusion models on bits-per-dimension benchmarks — the velocity parametrization seems to pay a density-estimation tax [1, 5].
5. **Conditional and structured generation.** Classifier-free guidance, the workhorse of conditional diffusion, ports awkwardly to velocity fields; principled guidance for flows (beyond score-to-velocity conversion) is an active area.
6. **Theory–practice gap in sampling.** The order-optimality result [7] controls the velocity field in $L^2(p_t)$, but sampling error also depends on ODE discretization and the pushforward's stability — a full end-to-end guarantee for few-step Euler sampling is missing.
7. **Mode coverage diagnostics.** Straight deterministic maps can silently drop modes; unlike SDEs, there is no stochasticity to paper over a poorly covered region, making evaluation of mode coverage more demanding for flows.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Flow matching and rectified flow reframe generative modeling as *supervised regression of velocity fields on designed probability paths*. The CFM equivalence theorem [1, 2] makes CNF training simulation-free; optimal-transport couplings [2] and iterative rectification [3] make the learned trajectories straight; and modern schedules and parametrizations [4] make few-step — even one-step — generation competitive with diffusion at a fraction of the sampling cost. Against score-based diffusion [5], flows trade the rich stochastic-process machinery for geometric simplicity, stability, and design freedom over paths; against consistency models [6], they trade one-step functional constraints for invertible ODEs with exact likelihoods. The emerging statistical theory [7] suggests the framework is not merely a practical hack but a minimax-grounded estimator. The central open challenge is no longer *whether* straight deterministic transport can match diffusion — it can — but how to close the remaining gaps in likelihood, conditional guidance, and end-to-end sampling theory. The ODE paradigm, in short, has earned its place as a first-class citizen of generative modeling.
