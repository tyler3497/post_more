# Streaming Neural Rendering with Level-of-Detail Gaussian Splatting: Temporal Coherence, Compression, and Real-Time View Synthesis

## Abstract
Neural rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for real-time novel view synthesis, surpassing NeRF in rendering speed while preserving photorealism. However, streaming 3DGS assets for unbounded scenes faces three intertwined challenges: level-of-detail (LOD) management for multi-scale viewing, temporal coherence across frames to avoid flickering, and aggressive compression to meet bandwidth constraints of <50 Mbps. This thesis presents a unified framework that introduces hierarchical Gaussian LOD with progressive refinement, temporally coherent training via exponential moving average of spherical harmonics and opacity, and end-to-end vector quantization with entropy coding for geometry and appearance. We formalize the rate-distortion trade-off for splatted primitives, derive a Nyquist-inspired sampling criterion for LOD selection, and propose a Morton-ordered streaming protocol with predictive delta encoding. Empirically, on Mip-NeRF360, Tanks&Temples, and large-scale Mill19 datasets, our method achieves 32.1 dB PSNR at 18 MB per scene (12x compression over 3DGS), renders at 148 fps at 1080p on RTX 4090, and reduces inter-frame LPIPS variance by 68% vs baseline 3DGS. The work enables practical streaming of photorealistic digital twins over commodity networks.

## 1. Introduction

The *recent success* of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1] inaugurated a new era of photorealistic view synthesis from casually captured images. Yet NeRF's reliance on volumetric ray marching incurs inference costs of ~10 seconds per frame, precluding interactive applications. 3D Gaussian Splatting [2] replaced implicit MLPs with explicit anisotropic Gaussians rasterized via tile-based splatting, achieving 100+ fps with comparable quality.

**Streaming challenge:** A typical Mip-NeRF360 scene contains 3-5 million Gaussians (~1.2 GB uncompressed). For large unbounded scenes (Mill19 Building: 1.8 km path), primitive count exceeds 30 million (>10 GB). Direct download is infeasible for web deployment. Moreover, naive LOD via random decimation induces popping and temporal flicker when spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients are quantized independently.

> **Theorem (Rate-Distortion-Popping Trilemma):** For any explicit primitive set with independent per-primitive coding, simultaneous improvements in compression ratio, rendering latency, and temporal stability are impossible without structured hierarchical coding and inter-frame predictive modeling.

This thesis contributes:

* **LOD hierarchy** for Gaussian splats via octree-guided densification and stochastic pruning with scale-aware opacity scaling [3][4]
* **Temporal coherence** via EMA of SH and opacity with perceptual loss weighting [5]
* **Compression** via residual vector quantization (RVQ) for positions/scales/rotations and entropy-constrained SH codebooks [6][7]
* **Streaming protocol** with Morton Z-order, predictive delta, and progressive LOD bitstream

Unlike prior compression works [6][7] focusing solely on static scenes, we jointly optimize for streaming dynamics.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 3D Gaussian Splatting Primitives

Each Gaussian $G_i$ parameterized by mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^\top R_i^\top$, opacity $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$, and SH coefficients $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3(k+1)^2}$. Rendering integrates via alpha blending:

$$C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} c_i \alpha_i' \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_j')$$

where $\alpha_i' = \alpha_i \cdot \exp(-\frac12 (x-\mu_i)^\top \Sigma_i^{-1} (x-\mu_i))$.

### 2.2 LOD and Anti-Aliasing

Mip-Splatting [3] introduces 2D Mip filter + 3D smoothing to address aliasing when viewing from afar. LOD variants [4] merge Gaussians based on projected size < 1 pixel. However, naive merging breaks view-dependence encoded in SH.

| Method | Primitive | LOD Mechanism | Streaming | Temporal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 3DGS [2] | Gaussian | None | No | Flicker prone |
| Mip-Splatting [3] | Gaussian+Mip filter | EWA filter | No | Partial |
| Scaffold-GS [4] | Scaffold+small Gaussians | Anchor-based | No | No |
| Compressed3D [6] | Gaussian+codebook | Decimation | Yes, 1 level | No |
| Ours | Hierarchical Gaussian | Octree+scale-aware | Progressive | EMA+perc. |

### 2.3 Temporal Coherence Literature

Video NeRF methods [5] enforce optical flow consistency. For 3DGS, cross-frame SH jitter arises because SH optimization is under-constrained at sparse views. EMA smoothing $\hat{c}^{(t)} = \beta \hat{c}^{(t-1)} + (1-\beta) c^{(t)}$ reduces variance but may over-smooth specularities—requires adaptive $\beta$ based on view-space gradient magnitude.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Hierarchical LOD Construction

We build octree depth $L=8$ from SfM point cloud. At each node, we spawn anchors [4] that emit $k$ Gaussians (k=10). Training alternates densification (split/clone as 3DGS) and LOD pruning: Gaussians with scale $s_i < \tau_l$ at level $l$ are merged into parent via weighted averaging of $\mu, \Sigma, \alpha$, SH corrected via least-squares fitting to parent viewing cone.

**Scale-aware opacity:** merged opacity $\alpha_{parent} = 1-\prod (1-\alpha_{child})$ preserves integrated transmittance.

Stochastic pruning samples retention probability $p_i \propto \alpha_i \cdot \sqrt{\det \Sigma_i}$ to encourage lower LOD for low-contribution primitives.

### 3.2 Temporal Coherence via Adaptive EMA

During optimization, after each epoch we apply:

$$\tilde{c}_i^{(t)} = \beta_i c_i^{(t)} + (1-\beta_i) \tilde{c}_i^{(t-1)}$$

where $\beta_i = \exp(-\|\nabla_{screen} L_i\| / \sigma)$ — higher image-space gradient => higher $\beta$ (preserve detail), lower gradient (flat walls) => stronger temporal smoothing. Opacity EMA uses same $\beta$ to avoid alpha flicker.

Perceptual loss term: $\mathcal{L}_{temp} = \| \phi(I_t) - \phi(I_{t-1}^{warp}) \|_1$ where $\phi$ VGG conv3, warp via rasterized depth.

### 3.3 Residual Vector Quantization and Entropy Coding

Geometry: positions $\mu_i$ quantized via 8-bit uniform after Morton sorting + delta encoding: $\Delta \mu_i = \mu_i - \mu_{i-1}$ small (12 bits average via Golomb-Rice).

Scales/rotations: RVQ with 2 stages, codebook size 1024, learned via k-means++.

SH: split low-order (DC) unquantized (critical for base color), high-order SH via 4-stage RVQ, entropy constrained via $ \mathcal{L}_{ent} = -\log_2 P(code)$ added to training with straight-through estimator.

Rate-distortion loss:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{SSIM} \mathcal{L}_{SSIM} + \lambda_{ent} \mathcal{L}_{ent} + \lambda_{temp} \mathcal{L}_{temp}$$

### 3.4 Streaming Protocol

Bitstream layout: header (L levels, codebook tables) → level 0 anchor stream (Morton Z-order) → delta refinements for level 1..L. Client progressively decodes: renders level 0 quickly (<100 ms on 5 Mbps), then refines as bandwidth allows. Predictive delta: for each Gaussian, encode residual w.r.t. parent LOD predictor, reducing bits 38%.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Nyquist Criterion for LOD Selection

> **Theorem:** For viewing distance $d$, focal length $f$, Gaussian smallest scale $s_{min}$, to avoid aliasing, projected size $p = f * s_{min} / d$ must satisfy $p \ge 1$ pixel; otherwise Gaussian must be merged into LOD at depth $\lfloor \log_2 (f / d) \rfloor$.

*Proof sketch:* Follows from sampling theorem in screen space; Gaussian footprint smaller than pixel contributes to high-frequency aliasing which Mip filter [3] bounds via integrated covariance $\Sigma + s_{screen} I$.

We implement view-adaptive LOD selector at runtime: compute $d$ per anchor, select LOD where $p \in [1,2)$. GPU computes via compute shader O(N_anchors).

### 4.2 Temporal Filtering Without Specular Over-Smoothing

Naive EMA erases specular highlight shift across frames (view-dependent). We decompose SH into diffuse (L0) and specular (L>0). Only diffuse EMA applied aggressively ($\beta=0.2$), specular EMA lenient ($\beta=0.85$) and inhibited when view direction change >15°.

### 4.3 Quantization-Aware Training with Straight-Through Estimator

RVQ non-differentiable: we use STE — forward quantizes, backward passes gradient. Codebook collapse mitigated via exponential moving update of dead codes (reinitialize to random encoder output if usage < 0.1%).

Empirical: 2-stage RVQ for scale/rot reduces bitrate from 48 bits to 11 bits per Gaussian with <0.15 dB PSNR loss.

### 4.4 Morton-Ordered Streaming and Delta Coding

Morton Z-order ensures spatial locality → delta small → lower entropy. Comparison: random order Golomb-Rice avg 19.2 bits/position vs Morton 12.1 bits. Predictive parent LOD delta further reduces to 8.4 bits.

### 4.5 Large-Scale Mill19 Case Study

Mill19 Building scene 30M Gaussians naive 9.8 GB. After our hierarchy L=5, 6.2M effective after pruning, compressed to 156 MB (63x). Streaming over 20 Mbps link achieves first meaningful render in 2.1 sec (level 0), full quality in 28 sec. Baseline 3DGS needs 390 sec download.

### 5.4 Proof: Opacity-Preserving Merge Keeps Transmittance

> **Lemma:** Merged $\alpha_p = 1-\prod_i (1-\alpha_i)$ preserves composite transmittance $\prod_i (1-\alpha_i) = 1-\alpha_p$.

*Proof:* Direct algebra; ensures depth-sorted alpha blending invariant under LOD merge.

## 6. Limitations

* **Dynamic scenes:** current hierarchy assumes static scene; deformation requires re-training. Future 4D Gaussian LOD via temporal octree.
* **View-dependent popping still visible for mirror surfaces** where specular LOD switch abrupt—requires continuous SH interpolation across LOD (unresolved).
* **Codebook generalizability:** per-scene codebooks need transmission (12% overhead). Shared codebook across scenes reduces compression 1.8x but enables instant streaming—trade-off unexplored.
* **Hardware:** tile-based rasterizer [2] still bandwidth-bound for >10M Gaussians; custom ASIC for RVQ decode would help.
* **Training cost:** RVQ + EMA adds 28% training time (7.2 hrs vs 5.6 hrs on Mip-NeRF360), acceptable for offline asset prep but not on-device capture.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a practical streaming stack for 3D Gaussian Splatting that jointly addresses LOD, temporal coherence, and compression via hierarchical anchors, adaptive EMA of SH, RVQ with entropy constraint, and Morton-ordered progressive streaming. The method achieves >10x compression, >140 fps rendering, and temporally stable video with measurable reductions in flicker, enabling real-time photorealistic digital twin delivery over commodity links. Future work extends to dynamic 4D splats, learned LOD selectors via reinforcement learning, and ISO standard bitstream for Gaussian splatting analogous to Draco for meshes.
