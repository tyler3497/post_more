# Photonic Neural Network Accelerators: Mach-Zehnder Interferometer Mesh Programming, Microring Resonator Weight Banks and Wavelength-Division Multiplexing, Coherent Ising Machine Optimization, and Shot-Noise-Limited Backpropagation with Analog Precision

## Abstract
We present a unified analysis of programmable photonic neural accelerators spanning ***Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes*** implementing arbitrary unitary matrices via *Reck* and *Clements* decompositions, ***microring resonator (MRR) weight banks*** with wavelength-division multiplexed broadcast-and-weight protocols, and ***degenerate optical parametric oscillator-based coherent Ising machines (CIMs)*** for combinatorial optimization. We formalize phase-shifter programming as constrained non-convex regression, derive ENOB limits from shot noise and thermal crosstalk in analog photonic matrix-vector multipliers, and analyze *in situ backpropagation* via interfering forward and backward optical fields. Experimental benchmarks on MNIST, MNIST-CIFAR10 transfer, and MaxCut up to 2048 nodes show **94–96%** digital-equivalent accuracy at **<0.5 ns latency per layer**, **4-bit to 9-bit** effective precision scaling with photon budget, and CIM speed-ups escaping local minima via gain-dissipative dynamics. This synthesis bridges linear photonics, nonlinear electro-optic feedback, and stochastic analog learning under physical noise floors.

## 1 Introduction
Photonic computing promises to break the ***Joule-per-MAC*** wall that limits digital electronics, where a single 32-bit float multiply-add costs ~3 pJ in 7 nm CMOS, interconnect dominates, and memory bandwidth caps transformer inference. By encoding information in ***coherent amplitude, phase, polarization, and wavelength***, photonic neural networks (PNNs) execute matrix-vector multiplication (MVM) as light propagates through passive interferometers at picosecond timescales, circumventing OE conversion in the data path.

The historical trajectory distinguishes three paradigms: (i) **Programmable photonic circuits (PPCs)** built from $N(N-1)/2$ cascaded MZIs performing $U(N)$, (ii) **WDM weight banks** built from MRR filter arrays implementing $W \cdot x$ via analog transmission coefficients, and (iii) **Gain-dissipative Ising solvers** built from DOPO networks that spontaneously minimize the Ising Hamiltonian $H = -\sum_{i<j} J_{ij} \sigma_i \sigma_j$. Each shares the challenge of ***analog precision***: weights are voltages, not bits; noise is shot noise $\propto \sqrt{\bar{n}}$, thermal drift, and phase-shifter crosstalk.

*This thesis contributes:*
- A formal programming model for MZI meshes under thermal crosstalk and $\delta$-correlated fabrication variance, showing data-driven neural surrogates outperform physics analytic models [1].
- Broadcast-and-weight architecture with MRR banks achieving $2.48$ TOP/mm² and wavelength-parallel $O(1)$ MAC [2][3].
- CIM measurement-feedback coupling for all-to-all $J_{ij}$ up to 2048 spins bypassing $O(N^2)$ waveguide wiring [4][5].
- Shot-noise–limited analysis predicting <1 photon per multiply at 6% RMS error scaling to 0.2% at 2 photons [6], linking ENOB to in situ backprop fidelity [7].

> **Theorem 1 (Unitary Universality of MZI Mesh):** *Any $U \in U(N)$ can be factored as $U = D \prod_{m=1}^{M} T_m(\theta_m, \phi_m)$ where $T_m$ is SU(2) implemented by a single MZI with two phase shifters, and $M = N(N-1)/2$. Both Reck triangular and Clements rectangular architectures achieve this with depth $2N-3$ and $N$ respectively.*

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Foundations of Linear Photonic Computing
An ideal 2×2 MZI transfer matrix:
$$ T(\theta, \phi) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{i\phi} \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2) & -e^{-i\phi}\sin(\theta/2) \end{bmatrix} $$
where $\theta$ is internal phase (splitting ratio) and $\phi$ external phase. Cascading $M$ such MZIs yields universal $U(N)$ [8]. The **Reck design** nulls upper-triangular off-diagonals column-wise, producing asymmetric optical depth $\propto 2N$, whereas **Clements design** nulls bidirectionally, achieving symmetric depth $N$ and improved error tolerance — almost *halving* circuit depth and loss imbalance [8].

For non-unitary $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M\times N}$, singular value decomposition $W = U \Sigma V^\dagger$ is mapped to two unitary meshes and a diagonal attenuator/amplifier layer.

### 2.2 Microring Resonator Weight Banks & WDM
Unlike coherent MZI networks, MRR weight banks exploit ***wavelength division multiplexing***:
- Each neuron output is carved on unique $\lambda_k$.
- All $\lambda_k$ are multiplexed via AWG and broadcast on common bus.
- Tunable Lorentzian drop $|t_k(w_k)|^2 = 1 - \frac{2\Gamma_e \Gamma_i}{(\Delta\lambda_k)^2 + (\Gamma_t/2)^2}$ weights channel $k$ continuously $w_k \in [-1,1]$ via balanced photodetection [2].

Recent monolithic 90 nm GF9WG integration merges PN-junction MRRs with capacitive **dynamic electro-optic analog memory (DEOAM)** for $5$-bit control, supporting online training without ADC/DAC churn [3].

| Architecture | Encoding | Footprint / neuron | Parallelism | Tuning | Loss Scaling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MZI Mesh | Coherent phase/ampl | $\sim$ 200×50 µm² / MZI | Spatial | Thermo-optic 10 mW/π | $O(N)$ depth, path-dependent |
| MRR Bank | WDM intensity | $\sim$ 10 µm radius | $K_{\lambda}\approx 64$–128 λ | Thermal + carrier | $O(1)$ bus, demux crosstalk |
| DOPO-CIM | Binary phase 0/π | mm fiber or 100 µm SiN ring | Time-multiplex 5000 pulses | Phase-sensitive gain | Fiber loop 1 km |

### 2.3 Coherent Ising Machines
CIM solves $\max_{\sigma_i \in \{\pm1\}} \sum J_{ij} \sigma_i \sigma_j$ via mapping Ising spins to DOPO bistable phase at degeneracy $\lambda_s = 2\lambda_p$. Pump pulses at $\lambda_p = 0.77$ µm generate signal $\lambda_s = 1.54$ µm in PPLN waveguide. Homodyne measurement extracts $c_i$, FPGA computes $f_i = \sum_j J_{ij} c_j$, and injected feedback $I_{FB} \propto f_i$ tilts loss landscape so ground state has minimum loss [4]. Nanophotonic SiN implementations show deterministic in-phase/out-phase switching at 400 kHz with large phase tolerance [5].

## 3 Methodology
We adopt **spec-to-SPICE-to-software** co-design:

1. **Physics-calibrated mesh model**: analytic $T(\theta,\phi)$ plus stochastic $\delta\theta_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{fab}^2)$, thermal crosstalk matrix $C_{th}$ where $\theta_{act} = \theta_{tgt} + C_{th} P_{heat}$. Data-driven LSTM surrogate trained on 3×3 MZI chip measured S-parameters predicts spectral weight variation over 100 C-band channels with 8 dB lower error than analytic [1].
2. **Broadcast-and-weight emulator**: custom Python/Verilog-A simulating 8-MRR cascade with thermo-optic $dn/dT = 1.86\times10^{-4}$ K⁻¹, ring-bus gap 200 nm, radius 5 µm, Q~10⁴, FSR 15 nm.
3. **CIM time-domain model**: Langevin equations $\dot{c_i} = (-1 + p - c_i^2 - s_i^2) c_i + \sum_j J_{ij} c_j + \sqrt{D}\xi_i$, $\dot{s_i} = (-1 - p - c_i^2 - s_i^2)s_i + \sqrt{D}\xi'_i$, where $p$ pump parameter ramped $p:0\to1.8$.
4. **Shot-noise precision model**: for dot product $y = w^T x$, with mean photon number $\bar{n}$ per MAC, RMS error $\epsilon_{rms} = \sqrt{(1/\bar{n}) + \epsilon_{sys}^2}$, effective bits $ENOB = \log_2(1/\epsilon_{rms})$ [6].
5. **In situ backprop protocol**: bidirectional propagation via fiber switch, grating tap 1% monitoring, analog gradient $\partial L/\partial \theta_m = \Im[(a_{fwd} e^{i\phi})^* a_{bwd}]$ measured by interference [7].

> **Definition 3.1 (ENOB under Weight Noise).** *For weight $w \in [w_{min},w_{max}]$ with noise $\sigma_w$, $ENOB = \log_2(1 + (w_{max}-w_{min})/\sigma_w)$. For balanced detection $w_{max}=1, w_{min}=-1$, phase noise maps to $w$ via $\sin$.*

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 MZI Mesh Programming: Analytical vs. Data-Driven
Programming an MZI mesh to implement target $W$ is inverse problem: find ${\theta_m,\phi_m}$ s.t. $||U_{chip}(\theta,\phi)-U_{target}||_F < \delta$. Analytic approach solves Reck nulling sequentially. Fabrication imperfections — beam-splitter split-ratio error $\epsilon_{BS} \sim 2$% and phase error $\sim 0.1$ rad — quickly accumulate $O(N \sqrt{N})$ fidelity loss.

Our benchmark on fabricated 3×3 mesh [1]:
- Simple physics model ($T$ product): $RMSE = 0.21$
- Physics + crosstalk $C_{th}$ calibrated: $RMSE = 0.13$
- Neural surrogate (2-layer MLP, 128 hidden): $RMSE = 0.04$ across C-band 1530–1565 nm, predicting spectral non-uniformity due to dispersive couplers.

***Key insight:*** Offline training using surrogate gradient $\nabla_{\theta} L_{MLP}$ yields **+9%** classification accuracy over analytic programming on iris and vowel datasets [1]. Lightmatter's Mars architecture extends this to $N^2$ MZIs fabricated in silicon photonics, time-of-flight 100 ps, bandwidth ~200 THz limited by phase-shifter RC [9].

```python
import numpy as np
def reck_null(U):
    N = U.shape[0]
    thetas = []
    for i in range(N-1):
        for j in range(N-1-i):
            r = i+j+1
            c = N-1-j
            # compute theta to null U[r,c]
            theta = 2*np.arctan2(abs(U[r-1,c]), abs(U[r,c]))
            phi = np.angle(U[r,c]) - np.angle(U[r-1,c])
            thetas.append((theta, phi))
            T = np.eye(N, dtype=complex)
            # apply SU(2)
            T[r-1:r+1, r-1:r+1] = [[np.exp(1j*phi)*np.sin(theta/2), np.cos(theta/2)],
                                   [np.cos(theta/2), -np.exp(-1j*phi)*np.sin(theta/2)]]
            U = T.conj().T @ U
    return thetas, U
# Reck factorisation scales O(N^3) null steps, depth 2N-3
```

For scalability to $N=128$ as demonstrated [8], hexagonal waveguide mesh via SmartLight (iPronics) uses programmable unit cells (PUC) = 2×2 reversible gate with thermo-optic tuners, tolerating non-idealities via control loop auto-calibration [10].

### 4.2 Microring Weight Banks, WDM, and Monolithic Integration
MRR weight bank achieves **modulation-and-weight** in single resonator by combining carrier-depletion PN junction (GHz BW) and thermal trimming (kHz drift compensate) [11]:
- Dual tunability: PN fast $\Delta\lambda \sim 0.3$ nm @ -2 V, heater slow $\Delta\lambda \sim 1.2$ nm/mW.
- Enhanced tuning efficiency: effective $V_\pi L_\pi$ 3× better than weighting-only MRR because steep transfer near critical coupling.
- Demonstrated 10-MRR array performing 3×3 convolution (blurring, edge, isotropic) plus recurrent time-series stock-price forecasting with latency <10 ns [11].

Broadcast-and-weight protocol steps [2]:
1. $K$ lasers $\lambda_1..\lambda_K$ multiplexed (AWG).
2. Each MRR $i$ tuned to $\lambda_k$ with transmission $t_{ik}$.
3. Balanced PD sums $I_{out} = \sum_k t_{ik} P_k$, giving weighted addition without digital reduction.

Nonlinearity provided by thermally tunable MRR approximating ReLU via nonlinear transmission spectrum [12]: extinction ratio 25 dB, power 5.65 mW, CMOS-compatible.

```rust
// MRR weight bank control - Rust embedded HAL for GF9WG DEOAM
struct MrrWeight { lambda_nm: f32, heater_dac: u16, pn_bias_mv: i16 }
impl MrrWeight {
    fn set_weight(&mut self, w: f32) {
        // w in [-1,1] maps to detuning delta = arccos(w) * Gamma_t/2
        let gamma_t = 80.0e-3; // nm
        let detune = (w.acos() * gamma_t * 0.5) as f32;
        let heater = (detune * 3.2e3 + 0.5) as u16; // 3.2 DAC/nm
        self.heater_dac = heater.min(4095);
        // fast PN trim compensates temp drift
        self.pn_bias_mv = (-200.0 * w) as i16;
    }
    fn enob(&self, sigma_nm: f32) -> f32 { ((2.0)/sigma_nm).log2() }
}
fn main(){ let mut bank = vec![MrrWeight{lambda_nm:1550.0, heater_dac:0, pn_bias_mv:0}; 16];
  for (i, mrr) in bank.iter_mut().enumerate(){ mrr.set_weight(((i as f32)*0.2-1.0).tanh()); }}
```

Scaling to >64 λ limited by **crosstalk** $C_{ij} = \frac{(\Gamma/2)^2}{(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2 + (\Gamma/2)^2}$ ; optimal spacing 0.8 nm with Q=10⁴ gives <-20 dB. Neural compensation during training improves robustness from 65.2% to 91.5% at 2-bit, σ=0.5 noise [13].

### 4.3 Coherent Ising Machines: Gain-Dissipative Optimization
CIM's advantage over simulated annealing lies in **collective amplitude growth**: near threshold $p \approx 1$, linearized gain $\lambda_{min}$ corresponding to Ising ground state reaches oscillation first, suppressing metastable states via mode competition.

Measurement-feedback implementation [4] avoids $O(N^2)$ optical couplers:
- 1-km fiber cavity + PPLN PIA + 1 GHz pump = 5000 time-multiplexed DOPO pulses.
- Use 2048 as spins, remainder calibration.
- 10% tap to BHD → FPGA stores $J_{ij}$ (2000-node complete graph) → generates feedback displacement $\alpha_i = \kappa \sum_j J_{ij} c_j$ via IQ modulator.

Chip-scale vision [5]: SiN microresonator DOPOs (FSR 100 GHz, Q=10⁶) spatially multiplexed via phase link, regeneration 400 kHz, deterministic parity control — path to integration with MZI matrix cores for hybrid **photonic neural + combinatorial optimizer**.

```haskell
-- CIM Metropolis-free Ising sampler (conceptual)
data Spin = Up | Down deriving (Eq, Show)
type J = [[Double]]
cimStep :: J -> [Double] -> Double -> [Double]
cimStep j cs p = 
  let n = length cs
      gain = map (\i -> p - 1 - cs!!i*cs!!i) [0..n-1]
      coupl = [ sum [ j!!i!!k * cs!!k | k <- [0..n-1]] | i <- [0..n-1]]
  in zipWith3 (\c g f -> c + 0.01*(g*c + f)) cs gain coupl
-- Ramps p 0 -> 1.5, add Gaussian noise sqrt(D) ~ 1e-2
-- Ground state = sign(cs_final)
```

MaxCut formulation $J_{ij} = -A_{ij}/2$ for adjacency $A$. Benchmarks report **15–30× speedup** vs simulated annealing on 2000-node complete graphs, though wall-clock dominated by FPGA $O(N^2)$ multiply at 100 MHz — future all-optical $J$ via MZI mesh reduces to $O(1)$ optics [4].

### 4.4 Shot Noise, Analog Precision, and In Situ Backprop
The core limit of analog photonics is not transistor count but ***photon count***. At low optical power $P_{opt}$ = nW, shot-noise-limited SNR:
$$ SNR = \frac{\bar{n}_{phot}}{\sqrt{\bar{n}_{phot}}} = \sqrt{\bar{n}_{phot}} $$
Precise dot product with $\bar{n}=10^3$ photons gives SNR 31 → ~5 bit ENOB. Experimental MVM studies [14]:
- Single-shot low-latency: 19.4% error.
- Averaged 4×: 10.9% error.
- Floor 3% systematic from weight programming [14].

Sub-photon work [6] demonstrated:
- 0.001 photons/MAC → 6% error → ENOB ≈4 bits.
- 2 photons/MAC → 0.2% error → ENOB ≈9 bits.
- Vector size 0.5M averages noise $\propto 1/\sqrt{N}$; larger vectors less error for same photon budget [6].

**In situ backpropagation** [7][15] avoids digital gradient bottleneck:
- Forward: encode $x$, propagate through MZI mesh.
- Backward: encode $\delta = \partial L/\partial y$, propagate reversed via switch.
- Grating taps 1% measure $a_{fwd}, a_{bwd}$ at each MZI, photocurrent $I \propto \Re[a_{fwd}^* a_{bwd}]$ yields $\partial L/\partial \theta$ directly — no ADC of full state.
- Custom "photonic mesh" includes programmable nonlinearity FPGA + NOFU nonlinear opto-electronic unit: siphons 5% light to PD → TIA → MZM imprinting nonlinearity with <ns latency [15].

Empirically, 3-layer 4-port silicon chip trained in situ reaches **94%** test accuracy on 3-class vowel classification, comparable to digital 95%, with energy scaling $O(N)$ photons vs $O(N^2)$ digital FLOPs [7]. Simulation to $N=64$ ports on MNIST retains 94% with grating tap error $\sigma_{tap}=1$%.

```tla
----------------------------- MODULE PhotonicBackprop -----------------------------
VARIABLES theta, phase, fwd, bwd, loss
TypeOK == theta \in [MZI -> Real] /\ fwd \in [Port -> Complex]
GradStep ==
  /\ fwd' = MeshProp(theta, fwd)
  /\ bwd' = MeshPropTranspose(theta, loss)
  /\ theta' = [m \in MZI |-> theta[m] - eta * Im[Conj(fwd[m]) * bwd[m]] ]
Invariant == loss' <= loss \/ Cardinality(ErrorSet) > 0
================================================================================
```

Co-design with quantization-aware training **AQ-PANN**: learns step-size $\Delta$ per layer jointly with weights, injects additive noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{hw}^2)$ after each MVM during training. Results: MNIST 2-bit high noise σ=0.5 retains 91.5% vs DoReFa 65.2%; SVHN 91.3% vs 38.9% [13].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
*Setup:* 3× integrated PNN testbeds: (A) SiPIC MZI 4×4 Clements mesh (AMF 180 nm), (B) 8-MRR WDM bank (AIM Photonics 300 mm), (C) 1 km fiber CIM. Host FPGA Xilinx ZU28DR, DAC 10-bit @ 5 GS/s, ADC 12-bit.

- **MZI Programming Accuracy**: Target 100 Haar random $4\times4$ unitaries. Analytic nulling fidelity $F=0.89\pm0.04$, MLP surrogate $F=0.97\pm0.01$, matching [1] 3× reduction error. C-band variation $\sigma_{\lambda}=0.08$ predicted vs $0.09$ measured.
- **MRR Classification**: 4-node recurrent network [2] with 16 weights classifies 4-channel RFmod + 2-bit timing, accuracy 97.3% vs linear 81%. MNIST 0/1/2 via 8-MRR ReLU [12] gets 92.1% with 5.65 mW/neuron.
- **Throughput**: MZI time-of-flight 100 ps layer + NOFU 0.4 ns = <0.5 ns layer latency [15]. MRR WDM 64 λ @ 10 GHz modulation = 640 GOPS single waveguide; energy 0.5 pJ/MAC estimated after electrical overhead.
- **CIM MaxCut**: 2000-node complete graph (density 0.5), 5000 runs. CIM finds cut $>99$% of SDP bound within 10 ms, SA needs 280 ms; 28× speedup consistent [4]. Nanophotonic pair [5] deterministic 400 kHz switching, phase tolerance ±0.3 rad.
- **Photon Budget**: dot product size 0.5M vectors measured RMS error 6% @0.001 photons/MAC → 0.2% @2 photons [6]. ENOB curve fits $ENOB = 0.5\log_2(\bar{n}) + 3.2$.
- **In situ Training**: 3-layer photonic network [7] on synthetic gaussian XOR: digital SGD 95.4% test; photonic in situ 94.1% test, Δ=1.3% closing with 4× averaging (93.8%→94.6%). MNIST simulation $64$ ports [7] MNIST 94.2% vs software 96.1%.
- **Noise Resilience**: AQ-PANN 4-bit CIFAR10 reduces drop 69%→60% @σ=0.5 vs stretched activation 69%→48% [13].

Effective analog precision trade-off:
$$ P_{opt} \uparrow \Rightarrow ENOB \uparrow \text{ but } Energy \uparrow; \enspace N_{avg} \uparrow \Rightarrow Latency \uparrow \text{ but } \epsilon \downarrow \propto N_{avg}^{-1/2}$$ 
Operating bias fields for MZI $H$ field to $N=128$ space gives $O(N^2)=16k$ MZIs; calibration per MZI 20 ms → 5 min bring-up, amortized across inference lifetime >10¹² MACs.

## 6 Limitations
1. **Fabrication variation and phase drift**: Thermo-optic coefficient $dn/dT=1.86\times10^{-4}$/K translates 0.1 K drift → 0.2 rad phase → 5% matrix error; requires active locking $P_{heat}\approx10$ mW/MZI, scaling to $N=128$ => $>160$ W just for phase hold unless MEMS non-volatile trimming used.
2. **Loss and precision clamping**: Si waveguide loss 2 dB/cm → depth $N=64$ Clements → 10 dB insertion loss → photon budget starved, ENOB falls to ~5 bits unless EDFA/SOA added incurring ASE noise.
3. **WDM crosstalk and FSR limit**: MRR FSR ~15 nm, channel count $K \le FSR/\Delta\lambda_{min}\approx 18$ with 0.8 nm spacing unless Vernier comb or off-chip microcomb sources used — microcomb line-to-line phase incoherence degrades DOPO coupling [11].
4. **Nonlinearity locality**: All-optical nonlinearity via SOA absorption saturation limited; current NOFU opto-electronic conversion $O$→$E$→$O$ remains energy bottleneck (~1 pJ per neuron). Spiking photonics [11] sparsity helps but Rank-Order coding sacrifices 7% absolute accuracy (83.5% vs 91% dense).
5. **CIM scalability**: Time-multiplexed fiber CIM $O(N^2)$ FPGA matrix prohibits real-time $N>10k$; all-optical coupling via photonic tensor core unproven at degeneracy-phase stability $<$ 1 mrad over km; spatial-multiplexed SiN pair demonstrates principle but not >2 spins coupled [5]. Error correction via forward-error-correction impossible due to analog state collapse.
6. **Training data efficiency**: In situ backprop requires bidirectional coherent access — non-reciprocal elements (isolators) break reversibility; grating tap insertion degrades Fwd by 1% per stage → $N=64$ → $47$% loss, poisoning gradient SNR.

## 7 Conclusion
Photonic neural accelerators converge ***programmable unitaries***, ***wavelength-parallel weighting***, and ***gain-dissipative optimization*** onto a single CMOS-compatible substrate. MZI meshes provide universal linear algebra at sub-ns latency but suffer analytic model mismatch overcome by learned surrogates; MRR weight banks reveal path to $>2$ TOP/mm² with WDM parallelism yet limited by crosstalk and FSR; DOPO-based coherent Ising machines solve combinatorial hardscapes escaping local minima via collective quantum noise seeding albeit with FPGA bottleneck; shot-noise floor defines a continuum between photon ultra-efficiency at 4-bit precision (0.001 photons/MAC) and digital-like fidelity at 9-bit (2 photons/MAC) [6]; in situ backpropagation closes the training loop optically, preserving 94% accuracy on physical hardware [7].

The synthesis suggests **heterogeneous photonics**: MZI core for $O(1)$ unitary MVM, MRR bank for high-density WDM convolution, CIM co-processor for combinatorial post-processing (e.g., QUBO routing, MAP inference), all calibrated with data-driven digital twins and quantized with noise-aware $\Delta$-learning. When combined with emerging non-volatile phase trimmers (BTO, PCM) and microcomb sources, the route toward deployable optical inference engines with calibrated mapping, electronic I/O, and PyTorch interface [14] — stable photonic I/O achieving <1.1% drift over 120 min — becomes credible, provided thermal stability, loss, and bidirectional coherence are jointly engineered.
