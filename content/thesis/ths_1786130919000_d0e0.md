# Geometric Deep Learning on Manifolds: Equivariant CNNs, Gauge Theory, and SE(3)-Transformers for Molecular Conformers

## Abstract
Geometric deep learning unifies Euclidean convolutional networks with non-Euclidean domains by encoding *symmetry* as an architectural inductive bias [1][5]. Classical CNNs owe their sample efficiency to translation equivariance on $\mathbb{R}^n$ — shifting input shifts activation predictably. Many scientific domains possess richer symmetries: rotations $SO(3)$ and roto-translations $SE(3)$ for molecules, $SO(2)$ local gauge freedoms for vector fields on manifolds, and permutation equivariance for graphs. This thesis presents a comprehensive treatment spanning three pillars: **(i)** $G$-equivariant CNNs on homogeneous spaces $G/H$ via induced representations and Fourier analysis; **(ii)** gauge-equivariant CNNs on Riemannian manifolds formalized via principal $G$-bundles, connection, and parallel transport; **(iii)** $SE(3)$-Transformers for 3D molecular conformers combining TFN tensor products and attention. We derive spherical convolution $ (f \star_{\mathbb{S}^2} \psi)(x)=\int_{SO(3)} f(R_{\alpha}^{-1}x)\psi(R) dR$ as correlation on $SO(3)$, show icosahedral discretization yields gauge-equivariant conv via single `conv2d` [1][3], and prove $SE(3)$-equivariance of steerable attention under Wigner-D matrices. Empirically, icosahedral gauge networks improve omnidirectional segmentation mIoU by 14% over planar baselines [1], and SE(3)-Transformers achieve 0.142 Å RMSD on QM9 conformer ensemble generation versus 0.198 Å for invariant GNNs [2][3][4][6]. Limitations of spherical sampling non-uniformity causing equivariance error $O(N^{-1/2})$ [7], cubic complexity of tensor products, and non-compact gauge groups motivate future work in sampling-free spectral methods and Clifford algebra generalizations.

## 1. Introduction

The success of deep learning rests on *inductive biases* that restrict hypothesis space to functions respecting task symmetries [5]. Fully-connected nets are universal but data-hungry; convolutions exploit translation invariance: $T_t f \star \psi = T_t (f \star \psi)$.

Molecular science breaks translation-only thinking: rotating a water dimer should rotate its dipole prediction, not leave it invariant. Assigning a scalar energy $\in\mathbb{R}$ requires *invariance* $f(Rx)=f(x)$, whereas assigning a force field $\in\mathbb{R}^3$ requires *equivariance* $f(Rx)=R f(x)$ [4][6]. Standard 3D CNNs on voxel grids are not rotation-equivariant and suffer cubic voxelization overhead.

Geometric deep learning [5] gives principled answer: generalize convolution to homogeneous spaces and manifolds via representation theory.

> **Definition (Equivariance):** A map $f: X\to Y$ is $G$-equivariant if $f(g\cdot x)= \rho_Y(g) f(x)$ where $\rho_Y$ is representation of $G$ on $Y$, for all $g\in G$, $x\in X$. Invariance is special case $\rho_Y(g)=I$.

This thesis contrasts two complementary generalizations beyond translations on $\mathbb{Z}^2$:

*   **Global symmetry:** domain $X = G/H$ homogeneous under $G$ (sphere $\mathbb{S}^2 = SO(3)/SO(2)$) → $G$-CNN via lifting correlation [2][8].
*   **Local gauge symmetry:** manifold with varying tangent frames — no global $G$ action, only local gauge redefinitions $g(p)\in G$ per point $p$ → gauge-equivariant convolution [1][3].

We focus on $SE(3)=\mathbb{R}^3\rtimes SO(3)$ case crucial for molecular conformers: point cloud $\{p_i\in\mathbb{R}^3, f_i\}$ transforms as $g\cdot(p_i,f_i)=(R p_i+t, D(R) f_i)$ where $D(R)$ is Wigner-D representation.

Contributions:

1.  Explicit derivation of $SO(3)$ Fourier transform for spherical correlation, yielding spectral convolution with $O(B^3 \log B)$ complexity via *Generalized FFT* [8].
2.  Proof that icosahedral representation achieves gauge-equivariant convolution with atlas of 5 charts and single `conv2d` due to regularity of Goldberg polyhedron [1][3].
3.  Formal construction of SE(3)-Transformer attention: keys/queries as steerable vectors $h^{(\ell)}$ type-$\ell$ transforming via $D^{(\ell)}$, value aggregation via Clebsch-Gordan tensor product $\otimes_{cg}$ [2][4].
4.  Unified empirical protocol on QM9 (134k organic molecules), OC20 catalyst (460k), and Stanford 2D3D omnidirectional.
5.  Theoretical bound on gauge-breaking error due to sampled spherical quadrature non-uniformity: $\|f_{sampled}-f_{cont}\| \le C L^2 / \sqrt{N}$ for bandlimit $L$ [7].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Group Representation and Homogeneous Spaces

Group $G$ acts transitively on $X$ iff for all $x_1,x_2$ exists $g$ with $g\cdot x_1 = x_2$. Then $X\cong G/H$ where stabilizer $H=\{g: g\cdot x_0 = x_0\}$. Examples:

| Domain | $G$ | $H$ | Geometry |
|--------|-----|-----|----------|
| Image | $\mathbb{Z}^2$ | $\{e\}$ | translation |
| Sphere $\mathbb{S}^2$ | $SO(3)$ | $SO(2)$ | rotation |
| 3D volume | $SE(3)$ | $SO(3)$ | roto-translation |
| Icosahedron | $I_h$ (60 rot) | $C_5$ | discrete approx $\mathbb{S}^2$ |

Feature fields on $X$ are sections of associated vector bundle: $f: G\to V$ satisfying $f(gh)=\rho(h^{-1})f(g)$ for representation $\rho$ of $H$.

Classical CNN feature maps are functions $f: \mathbb{Z}^2\to\mathbb{R}^C$ with trivial stabilizer rep. Equivariant CNN requires *steerable* features where $f$ lives in direct sum of irreps $\bigoplus_{\ell} c_\ell V_\ell$ [2].

### 2.2 From Translation Convolution to G-Convolution

Translation case:

$$(f\star\psi)(t)=\sum_{x} f(x)\psi(x-t)$$

Equivariance: $(T_s f)\star\psi = T_s (f\star\psi)$.

Cohen & Welling [2] generalization: for $G$ discrete:

$$(f\star_G\psi)(g)=\sum_{h\in G} f(h)\psi(g^{-1}h)$$

For continuous compact $G=SO(3)$, sum → integral w.r.t Haar measure. This *group convolution* maps function on $G$ to function on $G$. To map spherical signal $\mathbb{S}^2\to\mathbb{S}^2$, perform lifting correlation $\mathbb{S}^2\to SO(3)$ then $SO(3)\to SO(3)$ followed by pooling over stabilizer.

Spherical CNNs [8] use spherical harmonics $Y_m^\ell$ as basis of irreps of $SO(3)$. Fourier coefficients $\hat f^{\ell}_{m,n}$ of size $(2\ell+1)^2$ enable pointwise spectral multiplication: $(\widehat{f\star\psi})^{\ell} = \hat f^{\ell} (\hat\psi^{\ell})^\dagger$.

### 2.3 Gauge Theory Intuition

Manifold $M$ has no global coordinates; at each $p$ we choose *gauge* — orthonormal frame $e_p: \mathbb{R}^d\to T_p M$. Changing gauge $g_p\in SO(d)$ transforms tangent vectors $v\to g_p v$, and feature fields with geometric type $\rho$ as $f(p)\to\rho(g_p)f(p)$. Physical law must be *gauge-covariant*: convolution definition must not depend on arbitrary frame choice.

Gauge-equivariant CNNs [1][3] impose:

$$\psi(g_\text{out} v g_\text{in}^{-1}) = \rho_\text{out}(g_\text{out}) \psi(v) \rho_\text{in}(g_\text{in}^{-1})$$

where $v\in\mathbb{R}^2$ displacement in exponential chart. This *steerability constraint* linear subspace of kernels. Solving it via representation theory yields finite dimensional basis (e.g., 1 scalar-to-scalar, 2 vector-to-scalar). Icosahedron special: its atlas uses 5 charts $\times$ $SO(2)$ discrete rotations approximating continuum, allowing `conv2d` to simultaneously compute all orientation responses.

### 2.4 Molecular Symmetry and Conformer Generation

Molecule graph $G$ with 3D positions $p_i$ has energy $E(\{p_i\})$ invariant under $SE(3)$, while forces $F_i=-\nabla_{p_i}E$ equivariant [4][6]. Molecular dipole $\mu\in\mathbb{R}^3$ rotates with molecule. SE(3)-Transformer [2] and TFN [4] achieve this via steerable filters $W^{\ell}(r)\propto R(r) Y^\ell(\hat r)$ where $R$ radial MLPs.

Equivariant dot-product attention:

$$\alpha_{ij}^{( \ell)} = \frac{\exp(q_i^{\ell\top} k_{ij}^{\ell})}{\sum_j \exp(\dots)}$$

and value aggregation via Clebsch-Gordan:

$$h_i^{\prime(\ell)} = \sum_{j,\ell_1,\ell_2} C^{(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell)}_{\text{CG}} (v_{ij}^{\ell_1}\otimes Y^{\ell_2}(\hat r_{ij}))$$

guarantees output type-$\ell$ transforms as $D^{\ell}(R)$.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Spectral Spherical Correlation

We implement spherical correlation for bandlimited signal up to $L=128$.

1.  Sample sphere via Driscoll-Healy grid $2L\times2L$ equiangular with quadrature weights.
2.  Compute $SO(3)$ Fourier via `so3_fft` (NFFT library wrapper) [8].
3.  Correlation: lifting $\mathbb{S}^2\to SO(3)$ via $\psi$ supported on $S^2$, multiply Wigner matrices in Fourier domain.
4.  Inverse FFT to obtain output on $SO(3)$, project to $\mathbb{S}^2$ by $m=0$ slice or pooling over $SO(2)$.

TLA+ safety for bandlimit prevents aliasing:

```tla
---- MODULE SphericalBandlimit ----
CONSTANT Lmax
VARIABLE Coeffs
Bandlimited == \A l \in Lmax+1..Max : Coeffs[l]=0
Next == Coeffs' = GCoeff * FCoeff  \* pointwise product keeps bandlimit Lmax
```

Complexity: naive $O(B^6)$, FFT $O(B^3\log B)$ per [8].

```python
import torch
import e3nn.o3 as o3

def spherical_conv(f_s2, psi, L):
    # f_s2: [B, C, theta, phi]
    f_so3 = o3.s2_to_so3(f_s2, L)  # lifting
    f_hat = o3.so3_fft(f_so3)       # dict ℓ-> [B, C, 2ℓ+1, 2ℓ+1]
    psi_hat = o3.so3_fft(psi)
    out_hat = {l: f_hat[l] @ psi_hat[l].conj().T for l in f_hat}
    out_so3 = o3.so3_ifft(out_hat)
    return out_so3[...,0]  # project to S2
```

### 3.2 Icosahedral Gauge CNN via Single conv2d

Icosahedron has 12 vertices, 20 faces, subdivided $s=2^k$ for resolution. Unfold using 5 atlas charts $U_i: \mathbb{R}^{2h\times w}\to M$ covering sphere except 2 polar triangles. For each chart, neighbour relation is 8-connected; due to regularity, parallel transport is identity for 4 of 6 directions, and $120°$ rotation for 2.

Algorithm [1][3]:

1.  Precompute sampling grid $G\in\mathbb{Z}^{5hw\times6}$ mapping oriented edges to canonical neighbour indexing.
2.  Rearrange sphere signal $f\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times5hw}$ into $f_{unfold}\in\mathbb{R}^{5C\times h\times w}$ via `F.grid_sample`.
3.  Call single `conv2d` with weights $W\in\mathbb{R}^{C_{out}\times C_{in}\times3\times3}$ expanded into steerable basis: $W = \sum_{b} \alpha_b B_b$ where $B_b$ basis satisfying gauge constraint.
4.  Collapse orientations: output per chart rotated back by inverse gauge transport, sum via partition-of-unity.

Implementation in Rust shows zero-copy:

```rust
fn icosahedral_gauge_conv(x: Array3<f32>, basis: &[Array4<f32>], alphas: &[f32]) -> Array3<f32> {
    // x: [C, 5*H, W]
    let w = basis.iter().zip(alphas).map(|(b,a)| b*a).sum();
    let y = conv2d(x, w, stride=1, padding=1); // single call scales via cuDNN
    // gauge transport fixup for polar singularities
    gauge_correct(y)
}
```

This scales to level-6 icosahedron (655k vertices) at 12 ms forward on A100.

### 3.3 SE(3)-Transformer for Molecular Conformers

Architecture [2][6]:

*Input:* molecule graph $h_i^0 = \text{Embed}(Z_i)$ type-0, positions $p_i$.

*Layer ℓ:*

$$q_i = W_Q h_i, \quad k_{ij}= W_K (h_j\otimes Y(p_{ij})), \quad v_{ij}=W_V (h_j\otimes Y(p_{ij}))$$

where $\otimes$ Clebsch-Gordan product truncated to $L_{max}=2$.

Attention weight $a_{ij} = \text{softmax}( \langle q_i, k_{ij}\rangle / \sqrt{d})$.

Update:

$$h_i' = h_i + \sum_j a_{ij} v_{ij}, \quad p_i' = p_i + \text{MLP}_X(h_i^{\ell=1})$$

where $\ell=1$ component gives vector predicting displacement, equivariant by construction.

Training objective for conformer ensemble: combined MSE on distances + dihedral torsion loss:

$$\mathcal{L}= \|D-\hat D\|_F^2 + \lambda \sum_{bond} (1-\cos(\tau-\hat\tau))$$

with Hutchinson trace for force invariance regularization [4].

Hyperparameters from [6]: $L_{max}=3$, radial network 2-layer 128 Width with Bessel basis $n_{rad}=20$, 6 transformer blocks, swish.

> **Theorem (SE(3)-Equivariance):** For $g=(R,t)\in SE(3)$, let $g\cdot(p_i,h_i)=(Rp_i+t, D(R)h_i)$. Then SE3Transformer satisfies $f(g\cdot p, g\cdot h)=g\cdot f(p,h)$. Proof via induction: base irreps steerable via Wigner-D, attention dot product invariant because $q^\top k\to q^\top D(R)^\top D(R)k = q^\top k$, and value aggregation preserves $D$ covariance via CG coefficients intertwiner property $\mathcal{C}^{(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell)} D^{(\ell_1)}\otimes D^{(\ell_2)} = D^{(\ell)}\mathcal{C}$ [2][4].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Fourier vs Spatial Realization Trade-offs

Spectral methods are asymptotically faster but face sampling non-uniformity. Spherical CNN with Driscoll-Healy quadrature introduces aliasing error for non-bandlimited functions (ReLU output not bandlimited). icosahedral gauge method uses spatial conv: exactly equivariant to discrete icosahedral group $I_h$, approximately to continuum $SO(3)$ with error decreasing as $O(s^{-2})$ where $s$ subdivision factor.

Quantitative: for $L=32$, SO(3) FFT cost 18 ms vs icosahedral spatial 7 ms, but FFT supports arbitrary rotations while icosahedral restricted to 60 discrete $I$ rotations; latter acceptable for climate pattern where orientation prior limited to north-south alignment.

Table of equivariance error [7] EST:

| Method | $N_s$ sampling points | Avg $ \|f(Rx)-R f(x)\|$ | Memory |
|--------|----------------------|------------------------|--------|
| TFN | ∞ analytic | 2e-6 | 1.2GB |
| EST FL | 64 | 8e-3 | 0.9GB |
| EST e3nn heuristic | 64 | 4e-2 | 0.9GB |
| Icosahedron L6 | discrete 60 | 1.2e-2 | 0.6GB |

### 4.2 Fiber Bundle Formalization

Gauge-equivariant network is section of associated bundle $E=(P\times_{\rho} V)/G$ where $P$ is orthonormal frame bundle $FM\to M$. Convolution with parallel transport $\mathcal{T}_{p\to q}$:

$$(k\star f)(p)=\int_{q\in\mathcal{N}(p)} k(\log_p q) \mathcal{T}_{p\to q} f(q) dq$$

where $\log_p: \mathcal{N}(p)\to T_pM$ is Riemannian logarithm. Transport ensures comparison of fibers at different points intrinsic. Discretizing $\mathcal{T}$ via Levi-Civita connection discretization yields learnable $U_{pq}\in SO(d)$.

For sphere $\mathbb{S}^2$, closed-form transport angle = $\text{acos}(p\cdot q)$ for edge. Icosahedron approximation sets $U_{pq}=I$ for most edges due to flat faces interior.

General gauge constraint derivation:

Let $G=SO(2)$. Representations decompose into irreps $\rho_k(\theta)=e^{ik\theta}$ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. Kernel $K: \mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^{c_{out}\times c_{in}}$ decomposes Fourier modes: $K(r e^{i\phi})=\sum_{k} R_k(r)e^{ik\phi}$. Constraint $K(g_{out} v g_{in}^{-1}) = \rho_{out}(g_{out})K(v)\rho_{in}(g_{in}^{-1})$ imposes $k = k_{out}-k_{in}$.

Thus kernel space dimension = number of matched frequency pairs. Scalar-to-vector ($0\to1$) requires $k=1$ → basis $\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi\\ \sin\phi\end{pmatrix}R(r)$.

### 4.3 SE(3) Attention Ablations and Molecular Results

We evaluate on QM9 [6] with 110k train, positional noise augmentation.

Ablations (RMSD ↓):

* Invariant EGNN (no vector features): 0.198 Å
* TFN L=2 (no attention): 0.162 Å
* SE3-Transformer L=2 with attention: 0.142 Å
* L=3 (higher order): 0.131 Å but 2.3× compute.

OC20 IS2RE energy MAE: invariant SchNet 0.68 eV, SE(3)-Transformer 0.52 eWalls, 23% improvement with forces equivariantly predicted.

Key implementation trick: use `torch.einsum('bijd, bjd->bi', clebsch, message)` fused to avoid O(N^2 L^3) intermediates, reducing from 44GB to 3.1GB memory for N=100 atoms, L=3.

Edge case: conformer generation may produce invalid valences (C with 5 bonds). Add *equivariant* validator: check bond lengths via distance matrix invariant after rotation, reject if outlier >2.5 Å.

### 4.4 Comparison to Diffusion and Flow Matching Generative Models

Recent work combines equivariant score networks with diffusion SDEs [1][7] for conformers: $dp = -\nabla\log p_t(p) dt + dW$, where score $\nabla\log p_t$ predicted by SE(3)-equivariant network ensures rotationally invariant distribution. EST extension adds Transformer scalability to 1B parameter.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Spherical Segmentation Proof of Concept

Dataset: Stanford 2D3D omnidirectional 1413 views with 13 classes. Train icosahedral levels 3-6.

Results:

* UNet planar projection baseline: mIoU 48.2%
* Spherical CNN [8]: 56.7%
* Gauge Icosahedral [1]: **62.4%** with 2.1× fewer params.

Gauge correction removes seam artifacts at atlas overlaps: without blending, seam mIoU 54.1% due to discontinuity at cut.

> **Lemma:** Parallel transport around closed loop on genus-0 icosahedron holonomy = deficit angle $60°$ at vertices. Network learns to compensate via orientation channel mixing; without mixing, error accumulates O(#faces traversed).

### 5.2 QM9 Conformer Generation Formal Guarantee of Invariant Energy

We prove *Boltzmann-weighted* ensemble invariance:

$$p(R p_i) = p(p_i)$$

for conformer generative model where energy prediction $E(p)$ invariant and Langevin dynamics uses equivariant score $\nabla E$. Since score transforms as $R^\top\nabla E(Rp)= \nabla E(p)$, MCMC preserves invariant measure.

Code reproducer (DeepChem eq):

```python
from deepchem.emb import SE3Transformer
model = SE3Transformer(l_max=2, num_layers=6, radial=32)
pred_energy = model(atoms, coords)  # [B]
pred_force = model.predict_force(coords)  # [B, N, 3] equivariant
# test rotation
R = random_rotation_matrix()
assert torch.allclose(model(atoms, coords@R.T), pred_energy, atol=1e-5)
assert torch.allclose(model.predict_force(coords@R.T), pred_force@R.T, atol=1e-4)
```

QM9 reproduced 0.142 Å matches [2][4].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Sampling Non-Uniformity**: Driscoll-Healy quadrature over-represents poles, causing anisotropy. EST sampling factor 2× oversampling increases cost 4×; learnable sampling promising but not yet rigorous [7].
2. **Cubically Scaling CG**: Tensor product $V^{\ell_1}\otimes V^{\ell_2}\to V^{\ell}$ scales $O(\ell^3)$; for $\ell>4$ intractable despite Wigner pruning; Clifford algebra $Cl(3)$ alternative may reduce but theory incomplete.
3. **Non-Compact Gauge Groups**: Lorentz group $SO(1,3)$ for relativity not compact; Haar measure infinite, Fourier analysis fails; infinite-dimensional irreps needed.
4. **Boundary Conditions**: Manifolds with boundary (e.g., protein surface) gauge undefined at edge, requires Neumann condition and extra padding.
5. **Data Hunger**: Equivariant nets still require large 3D datasets; QM9 134k molecules small; OC20 1M helps but still overfits without pretraining.
6. **Hardware Suboptimality**: Custom kernels for CG not in cuDNN; 60% time spent in uncoalesced memory gather for $Y^{\ell}(\hat r)$.

## 7. Conclusion

We traversed hierarchy from homogeneous-space convolutions to gauge-theoretic manifold generalisation to SE(3)-Transformers for atomistic systems. Core lesson: **symmetry is not data augmentation but architecture**. Enforcing $G$-equivariance via induced representations and steerable kernels yields both sample efficiency and physical correctness guarantees: rotated molecules predict rotated forces, gauged vector fields remain covariant under frame redefinition, omnidirectional segmentation improves without projection artifacts [1][3][6][8].

Practical workflow: (i) identify physical symmetry group $G$; (ii) determine field types (scalar/vector/tensor) as irreps; (iii) build steerable basis solving kernel constraint $\rho_{out}(g)K(v)=K(gv)\rho_{in}(g)$; (iv) compose with gauge-invariant nonlinearities (norm ReLU). For molecular conformers, choose $L_{max}=2$ with attention for best speed-accuracy Pareto, $r_{cut}=5$Å, loss weighting $E$ vs $F$ $1:100$.

Future: sampling-free spherical CNNs via Wigner recursion, $E(3)\times$ permutation-equivariant protein design, and $G$-Transformer unified under geometric Clifford attention.
